Abstract




Conservation laws of mean field games equations

Abstract

Mean field games equations are examined for conservation laws.
The system of mean field games equations consists of two partial differential equations:
the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation for the value function
and the forward Kolmogorov equation for the probability density.
For separable Hamiltonians, this system has a variational structure,
i.e., the equations of the system are Euler-Lagrange equations for some Lagrangian functions.
Therefore, one can use the Noether theorem to derive the conservation laws
using variational and divergence symmetries.
In order to find such symmetries, we find symmetries of the PDE system
and select variational and divergence ones.
The paper considers separable, state-independent Hamiltonians
in one-dimensional state space.
It examines the most general form of the mean field games system
for symmetries and conservation laws
and identifies particular cases of the system
which lead to additional symmetries and conservation laws.

Keywords:

mean field games,
conservation law,
symmetry,
Noether theorem

Introduction

The Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) approach to
Mean Field Games (MFG) theory is a rapidly developing field of research

The theory provides a mathematical framework
for understanding the behaviour of large populations of interacting agents,
where the entire population influences each agent’s behaviour.
A continuum of agents represents such a population

Every again knows the distribution of the other agents
and uses this to implement its strategy.

The mean field game theory has found a broad range of applications in various fields

In particular, MFG theory has been applied to understand the behaviour of large populations of investors

MFG models have also been used to study traffic flow and congestion in transportation systems,
as well as to model the behaviour of crowds in social dynamics

[

,

.
MFG theory has been used to understand the dynamics of learning

[

.
Additionally, MFG theory has been applied to the study of energy systems,

Conservation laws play an essential role in differential equations.
They reflect the intrinsic properties of the equations.
They also help to ensure the consistency and stability of the system.
In the present paper, we analyse the system of mean field games equations for conservation laws.
This system consists of two PDEs:
the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation
for the value function of a typical agent
and the forward Kolmogorov equation (also known as the Fokker-Plank equation)
for the evolution of the density of the agent population.
It should be noted that the first equation is backward in time, and the latter is forward in time.
There are several approaches to derive conservation laws
[
38
]
.
In the paper, we exploit the variational structure of the MFG equations.
For separable Hamiltonians, this system has a variational structure,
i.e., the equations of the system are Euler-Lagrange equations for some Lagrangian functions.
Therefore, one can use symmetries
and the Noether theorem
[
39
]
to derive the conservation laws.

Lie point symmetries provide efficient tools
to analyse nonlinear differential equations

[
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
The symmetries are related to essential properties of the models
represented by the differential equations:

•

Symmetries generate transformations
which transfer solutions of the differential equations into other solutions.
In particular, they can be used to find new solutions from the known ones.

•

Symmetries of PDEs can be used to find particular solutions.
These solutions are called invariant solutions.

•

Symmetries of variational differential equations can be used for Noether’s theorem,
which provides conservation laws.

For the application of the Noether theorem,
one needs variational formulation of the considered differential equations
and variational or divergence symmetries.
Such symmetries are also symmetries of the considered differential equations.
Therefore, the standard approach is to find symmetries of the differential equations,
select those that are also variational or divergence symmetries,
and apply the Noether theorem to obtain conservation laws.
These steps are implemented in the paper.
In particular, we start with the most general case of the differential equations,
for which there are a few symmetries,
and continue with identifying particular cases,
for which there are additional symmetries.
After that, we select symmetries suitable for the Noether theorem
and provide the conservation law.

The paper is organized as follows.
In the next Section we review the mean field games equations
and specify the cases with the variational formulation.
Section
3
describes Lie point symmetries and the Noether theorem.
This section is written for the particular system which is analysed
in Sections
4
and
5
,
where we present the symmetry analysis and the conservation laws.
Section
6
discusses the interpretation of the obtained conservation laws.
Final Section
7
provides concluding remarks.
Some technical details are extracted into the Appendices.

2
Variational MFG equations

The system of mean field games equations consists of two coupled PDEs:
the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation
and the forward Kolmogorov equation
[
37
]
:

\displaystyle-u_{t}-\varepsilon\Delta u+H(x,\nabla u,m)=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\mathbf{R}^{d},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle m_{t}-\varepsilon\Delta m-\mbox{div}(mH_{\nabla u}(x,\nabla u,m))=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(2.1b)

The scalar functions
u(t,x)
and
m(t,x)

stand for the value function
and the probability density.
They depend on time
t\in[0,T]

and spatial independent variable
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

H(x,\nabla u,m)
is the Hamiltonian associated to the game
and
H_{\nabla u}=(H_{u_{x_{1}}},\cdots,H_{u_{x_{d}}})
.
Parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
characterizes the noise.
It should be noted that the HJB equation (
2.1a
)
is backward in time and
the Kolmogorov equation (
2.1b
)
is forward in time.
Thus, the initial-terminal conditions are given as

m(0,x)=m_{0}(x),\qquad u(T,x)=G(x,m(T,x))\qquad\mbox{in}\ \mathbf{R}^{d}.

(2.2)

Since
m(t,x)\geq 0
is the probability density, we have

\int mdx=1.

(2.3)

The solution
(u,m)
of the coupled system (
2.1
)
represents a Nash equilibria of the mean field game.
We refer to
[
37
]

for detailed assumptions
one needs for the existence and uniqueness of the solutions.
A sketch of the derivation of the system
is given in Appendix
A
.

2.1
Separable Hamiltonians

A particular case of the system (
2.1
)

\displaystyle-u_{t}-\varepsilon\Delta u+H(x,\nabla u)-f(x,m)=0,

(2.4a)

\displaystyle m_{t}-\varepsilon\Delta m-\mbox{div}(mH_{\nabla u}(x,\nabla u))=0

(2.4b)

corresponds to separate Hamiltonians

H(x,\nabla u,m)=H(x,\nabla u)-f(x,m)
.
This system has a variational formulation.
It is provided by the Lagrangian function

L=-mu_{t}+\varepsilon\nabla m\cdot\nabla u+mH(x,\nabla u)-F(x,m),

(2.5)

where

F(x,m)=\int f(x,m)dm,

(2.6)

i.e., equations (
2.4
)
are Euler-Lagrange equations

\displaystyle{\delta L\over\delta m}={\partial L\over\partial m}-D_{t}{\partial L\over\partial m_{t}}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{x_{i}}{\partial L\over\partial m_{x_{i}}}=0,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle{\delta L\over\delta u}={\partial L\over\partial u}-D_{t}{\partial L\over\partial u_{t}}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{x_{i}}{\partial L\over\partial u_{x_{i}}}=0

(2.7b)

for Lagrangian (
2.5
).
Here
D_{t}
and
D_{x_{i}}
are total differentiation operators
for time
t
and spatial variables
{x_{i}}
, respectively.

2.2
One-dimensional state space with state-independent Hamiltonians.

In this paper, we examine the simplest case of system (
2.4
):
the case of one-dimensional state space
d=1

with state-independent functions
H=H(u_{x})
and
f=f(m)
.
The equations take the form

\displaystyle F_{1}=-u_{t}-\varepsilon u_{xx}+H(u_{x})-f(m)=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\mathbf{R},

(2.8a)

\displaystyle F_{2}=m_{t}-\varepsilon m_{xx}-m_{x}H^{\prime}(u_{x})-mH^{\prime\prime}(u_{x})u_{xx}=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ [0,T]\times\mathbf{R}.

(2.8b)

We consider second-order MFG systems restricting to
\varepsilon>0
.
Assumptions

H^{\prime\prime}(u_{x}){\not\equiv}0,\qquad f^{\prime}(m){\not\equiv}0;

(2.9)

guarantee that the equations are genuinely coupled,
i.e., none of these equations can be considered independently from the other.

Equations (
2.8
)
are Euler-Lagrange equations for the Lagrangian function

L=-mu_{t}+\varepsilon m_{x}u_{x}+mH(u_{x})-F(m)

(2.10)

with

F(m)=\int f(m)dm.

(2.11)

In Section
4
,
we analyse the system of equations (
2.8
)
for admitted symmetries
and in Section
5

we derive the conservation laws of these equations.

3
Lie point symmetries and the Noether theorem

This section reviews Lie point symmetries and the Noether theorem

[
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
For convenience,
the discussion is adopted to the system of PDEs (
2.8
)
and first-order Lagrangian function (
2.10
),
which are analysed in this paper.

Lie point symmetries of system (
2.8
) are given by generators of the form

X=\xi^{t}{\partial\over\partial t}+\xi^{x}{\partial\over\partial x}+\eta^{u}{\partial\over\partial u}+\eta^{m}{\partial\over\partial m}

(3.1)

with generator coefficients

\xi^{t}=\xi^{t}(t,x,m,u),\qquad\xi^{x}=\xi^{x}(t,x,m,u),\qquad\eta^{u}=\eta^{u}(t,x,m,u),\qquad\eta^{m}=\eta^{m}(t,x,m,u).

For application to differential equations
a generator should be prolonged to all variables
involved in the considered differential equations.
In our case, we get the prolonged generator

X=\xi^{t}{\partial\over\partial t}+\xi^{x}{\partial\over\partial x}+\eta^{u}{\partial\over\partial u}+\eta^{m}{\partial\over\partial m}\\
+\zeta_{t}^{u}{\partial\over\partial u_{t}}+\zeta_{t}^{m}{\partial\over\partial m_{t}}+\zeta_{x}^{u}{\partial\over\partial u_{x}}+\zeta_{x}^{m}{\partial\over\partial m_{x}}+\zeta_{xx}^{u}{\partial\over\partial u_{xx}}+\zeta_{xx}^{m}{\partial\over\partial m_{xx}}.

(3.2)

The coefficients for the derivatives are computed
according to the standard prolongation formulas

[
40
,
41
,
42
]

\zeta_{t}^{u}=D_{t}(\eta^{u})-u_{t}D_{t}(\xi^{t})-u_{x}D_{t}(\xi^{x}),\qquad\zeta_{t}^{m}=D_{t}(\eta^{m})-m_{t}D_{t}(\xi^{t})-m_{x}D_{t}(\xi^{x}),

\zeta_{x}^{u}=D_{x}(\eta^{u})-u_{t}D_{x}(\xi^{t})-u_{x}D_{x}(\xi^{x}),\qquad\zeta_{x}^{m}=D_{x}(\eta^{m})-m_{t}D_{x}(\xi^{t})-m_{x}D_{x}(\xi^{x}),

\zeta_{xx}^{u}=D_{x}(\zeta_{x}^{u})-u_{tx}D_{x}(\xi^{t})-u_{xx}D_{x}(\xi^{x}),\qquad\zeta_{xx}^{m}=D_{x}(\zeta_{x}^{u})-m_{tx}D_{x}(\xi^{t})-m_{xx}D_{x}(\xi^{x}),

where
D_{t}
and
D_{x}
are total differentiation operators for independent variables
t
and
x
,
respectively,
i.e.

D_{t}={\partial\over\partial t}+u_{t}{\partial\over\partial u}+m_{t}{\partial\over\partial m}+\cdots,\qquad D_{x}={\partial\over\partial x}+u_{x}{\partial\over\partial u}+m_{x}{\partial\over\partial m}+\cdots

To find Lie point symmetries admitted by differential equations,
one applies the infinitesimal invariance criterion
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
It requires that
the action of the prolonged generator
on the considered differential equations
provides identities on the solutions of these equations.
For system (
2.8
), the infinitesimal invariance criterion takes the form

\displaystyle E_{1}=\left.X(F_{1})\right|_{F_{1}=0,F_{2}=0}=0,

(3.3a)

\displaystyle E_{2}=\left.X(F_{2})\right|_{F_{1}=0,F_{2}=0}=0.

(3.3b)

These equations are PDEs for the coefficients of the generator (
3.1
).
Solving them, one finds the admitted generators.

Lie point symmetries of the differential equations are needed for the Noether theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(E. Noether
[
39
]
)
Let Lagrangian function (
2.10
) satisfy the equation

XL+L(D_{t}(\xi^{t})+D_{x}(\xi^{x}))=0

(3.4)

for generator
X
given by (
3.1
),
then the generator
X
is a symmetry
of the system of Euler-Lagrange equations (
2.8
)
and there holds the conservation law

D_{t}[T^{t}]+D_{x}[T^{x}]=0,

(3.5)

where

\displaystyle T^{t}=\xi^{t}L+(\eta^{u}-\xi^{t}u_{t}-\xi^{x}u_{x}){\partial L\over\partial u_{t}}+(\eta^{m}-\xi^{t}m_{t}-\xi^{x}m_{x}){\partial L\over\partial m_{t}},

(3.6a)

\displaystyle T^{x}=\xi^{x}L+(\eta^{u}-\xi^{t}u_{t}-\xi^{x}u_{x}){\partial L\over\partial u_{x}}+(\eta^{m}-\xi^{t}m_{t}-\xi^{x}m_{x}){\partial L\over\partial m_{x}}.

(3.6b)

There is a natural extension of the Noether theorem.

Remark 3.2

(
[
43
]
)
If instead of relation (
3.4
), there holds

XL+L(D_{t}(\xi^{t})+D_{x}(\xi^{x}))=D_{t}(V^{t})+D_{x}(V^{x})

(3.7)

with some functions
V^{t}(t,x,u,m)
and
V^{x}(t,x,u,m)
,
then the generator
X
is a symmetry
of the system of Euler-Lagrange equations (
2.8
)
and there holds the conservation law

D_{t}[T^{t}-V^{t}]+D_{x}[T^{x}-V^{x}]=0

(3.8)

with
T^{t}
and
T^{x}
given by (
3.6
).

Symmetries of the variational equations
which satisfy (
3.4
) are called
variational
symmetries
and
symmetries with property (
3.7
) are called
divergence
symmetries.

6
Interpretation of conservation laws

Some conservation laws allow intuitive interpretation.
Finite group transformations

t\rightarrow\bar{t},\qquad x\rightarrow\bar{x},\qquad u\rightarrow\bar{u},\qquad m\rightarrow\bar{m}

for generators (
3.1
)
solve the ODE system
[
40
,
41
]

{d\bar{t}\over da}=\xi^{t}(\bar{t},\bar{x},\bar{u},\bar{m}),\qquad{d\bar{x}\over da}=\xi^{x}(\bar{t},\bar{x},\bar{u},\bar{m}),\qquad{d\bar{u}\over da}=\eta^{u}(\bar{t},\bar{x},\bar{u},\bar{m}),\qquad{d\bar{m}\over da}=\eta^{m}(\bar{t},\bar{x},\bar{u},\bar{m})

with the initial conditions

\bar{t}|_{a=0}=t,\qquad\bar{x}|_{a=0}=x,\qquad\bar{u}|_{a=0}=u,\qquad\bar{m}|_{a=0}=m.

Here
a
is the group parameter.
Its value
a=0
corresponds to the identity transformation.
We remark the solutions can be presented by the exponentiation
[
42
]

t\rightarrow\bar{t}=e^{aX}t,\qquad x\rightarrow\bar{x}=e^{aX}x,\qquad u\rightarrow\bar{u}=e^{aX}u,\qquad m\rightarrow\bar{m}=e^{aX}m.

Specifically, we consider a particular case of the mean field games
described in Appendix
A
.
Let the dynamics of the agents be described by the SDE

dX_{s}=\alpha_{s}ds+\sqrt{2\varepsilon}dB_{s},

(6.1)

where
\alpha_{s}
is the agent’s control.
Brownian motions
B_{s}
for different agents are assumed to be independent.
Each agent minimizes

\mbox{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\left({\alpha_{s}^{2}\over 2}+f(m(s,X_{s}))\right)ds+G(X_{T},m(T,X_{T}))\ |\ X_{0}=x\right].

(6.2)

The running costs have two terms.
The first term
{\alpha_{s}^{2}\over 2}
requires to minimise the state changes.
The second term
f(m)
represents the interaction of the agents.
It can be a willingness to avoid others if
f(m)
is an increasing function
or a willingness to be like others if
f(m)
is a decreasing function.

We obtain the Hamiltonian

H(x,p,m)={p^{2}\over 2}-f(m),

the MFG system

\displaystyle-u_{t}-\varepsilon u_{xx}+{u_{x}^{2}\over 2}=f(m),

(6.3a)

\displaystyle m_{t}-\varepsilon m_{xx}-(mu_{x})_{x}=0.

(6.3b)

and the optimal feedback control

{\alpha_{s}}=-u_{x}(t,x).

(6.4)

System (
6.3
) has four conservation laws.
Conservation law (
5.3
) corresponds to the symmetry
X_{2}
,
which represents the translation of the state variable
x

\bar{t}=t,\qquad\bar{x}=x+a,\qquad\bar{u}=u,\qquad\bar{m}=m.

Taking (
6.4
) into account,
it is possible to consider this conservation law as the conservation of the average control value.
In mechanics, translations of the spatial coordinates lead to the conservation of momenta.

Conservation law (
5.4
) is provided by translations of
u
,

\bar{t}=t,\qquad\bar{x}=x,\qquad\bar{u}=u+a,\qquad\bar{m}=m,

given by the symmetry
X_{3}
.
It has the same interpretation for the quadratic Hamiltonian as for the arbitrary Hamiltonian.
It represents the conservation of the probability measure (
2.3
).
It is analogous to the conservation of mass in mechanical systems.

Conservation law (
5.15
) is derived with the help of the Galileo-type symmetry
X^{c}
.
This symmetry generates the group transformation

\bar{t}=t,\qquad\bar{x}=x+at,\qquad\bar{u}=u-ax-{a^{2}\over 2}t,\qquad\bar{m}=m.

It can be viewed as the law describing the change of the average state value.
In mechanics, the Galileo transformation corresponds to the law of motion of the centre of mass.

There seems to be no natural interpretation for conservation law (
5.2
)
as well as for the additional conservation laws (
5.17
) and (
5.19
),
which exist for quadratic
f(m)
.
These conservation laws correspond to the time translation

\bar{t}=t+a,\qquad\bar{x}=x,\qquad\bar{u}=u,\qquad\bar{m}=m,

the scaling

\bar{t}=e^{2a}t,\qquad\bar{x}=e^{a}x,\qquad\bar{u}=u,\qquad\bar{m}=e^{-a}m,

and the projective transformation in
(t,x)
plane

\bar{t}={t\over 1-at},\qquad\bar{x}={x\over 1-at}

supplemented with the transformation of the dependent variables

\bar{u}=u-{ax^{2}\over 2(1-at)}-\varepsilon\ln(1-at),\qquad\bar{m}=(1-at)m.

The transformations are generated by symmetries

X_{1}
,
Y^{c}_{4}
and
X^{c}_{5}
, respectively.
In mechanics, the time translation provides the conservation of energy.
Conservation laws corresponding to the scaling transformations
and the projective transformations
are known in mechanics, kinetic theory gases, hydrodynamics, etc.
[
44
]
.
They have no specific names.

7
Conclusion

The paper considers system (
2.8
),(
2.9
).
This system is the simplest case of the mean field games system (
2.4
),
which has a variational formulation due to the separable Hamiltonian.
System (
2.8
) corresponds to state-independent Hamiltonians
in one-dimensional state space.
The paper provides Lie point symmetry analysis of the system.
Then, the variational and divergence symmetries are selected.
They are used for the application of the Noether theorem to derive the conservation laws.
The presented approach provides the symmetries and conservation laws
for the general case of the system (
2.8
),(
2.9
)
with arbitrary functions
H(u_{x})
and
f(m)

and identifies the particular cases of
H(u_{x})
and
f(m)

which lead to the additional symmetries and conservation laws.
Some conservation laws have natural interpretations.

Conservation laws of the first-order mean field games systems,
which correspond to parameter
\varepsilon=0
in (
2.1
),
were considered in
[
45
,
46
]
.
To our knowledge, there have been no attempts to investigate
second-order mean field games equations for conservation laws
that makes the results of this paper original.
The study given here can also be performed
for more general MFG systems, such as

•

general system (
2.4
)
for multi-dimensional state space with state-dependent
H
and
f
,

•

system (
2.12
)
for multi-dimensional state space with state-independent
H
and
f
,

•

system (
2.13
)
for one-dimensional state space with state-dependent
H
and
f
.

It is also possible to consider stationary versions of these mean field games equations.

For example, system (
2.4
) with arbitrary
H(x,\nabla u)
and
f(x,m)

admits only two symmetries

X_{1}={\partial\over\partial t},\qquad X_{2}={\partial\over\partial u}.

The generator
X_{1}
is a variational symmetry
and the generator
X_{2}
is a divergence symmetry
of the Lagrangian function (
2.5
).
They provide the conservation laws

D_{t}\left[\varepsilon\nabla m\cdot\nabla u+mH(x,\nabla u)-F(x,m)\right]-\mbox{div}\left[\varepsilon(u_{t}\nabla m+m_{t}\nabla u)+mu_{t}H_{\nabla u}(x,\nabla u)\right]=0

conservation laws (

) and (

which hold for system (
2.8
)
with arbitrary
H(u_{x})
and

Conservation laws reflect fundamental properties of the differential equations,
that helps to understand and analyse the models.
For this reason, it is crucial to preserve them in numerical simulation
[
47
]
.
In particular,
conservation of measure (
2.3

is always respected by numerical methods for the MFG equations

Finally, it is worth mentioning that

Linear instability analysis on compressible Navier-Stokes equations with strong boundary layer
Tong Yang, Zhu Zhang

Abstract
. It is a classical problem in fluid dynamics about the stability and instability of different hydrodynamic patterns in various physical settings, in particular in the high Reynolds number limit of laminar flow with boundary layer. However, there are very few mathematical results on the compressible fluid despite the extensive studies when the fluid is governed by the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. This paper aims to introduce a new approach to study the compressible Navier-Stokes equations in the subsonic and high Reynolds number regime where a subtle quasi-compressible and Stokes iteration is developed. As a byproduct, we show the spectral instability of subsonic boundary layer.



1.
Introduction

One of the most fundamental problems in fluid mechanics is to understand the physical mechanisms that lead to stability or instability of hydrodynamic patterns. With high Reynolds number, most of the laminar flows are unstable so the small perturbation will eventually cascade into turbulence. Under many circumstances, the early stage of such transition is the instability induced by viscous disturbance wave, which is now named as Tollmien-Schlichting or T-S wave. For incompressible flow, the physical description of T-S waves can be found, for instance in the pioneer work by Heisenberg, C.C. Lin, Tollmien and Schlichting, cf.
[
5
,
15
,
21
,
33
]
, and a formal construction was established by Wasow
[
38
]
. Until recently, the rigorous mathematical justification was given by Grenier-Guo-Nguyen
[
7
]
.

From physical point of view, it is important to study the compressible flow with boundary layer that
arises from for instance the flow near the airfoil.
The theoretical investigation can be traced back to Lee-Lin
[
19
]
in which the Rayleigh’s criterion for inviscid flow was extended to the compressible subsonic flow. Later on, the asymptotic expansion used in
[
19
]
near the critical layer was rigorously justified by Morawitz
[
29
]
.
For more investigation from the physical perspectives, we refer to
[
5
,
21
,
20
,
33
]
and the references therein. It is worth noting that the instability mechanisms studied in these literatures are inviscid in nature while the viscous transition mechanisms have not yet been investigated. The aim of this paper is to fill in this gap by justifying the presence of T-S wave in compressible boundary layer rigorously.

1.1.
Problem and main result

Consider the 2D compressible Navier-Stokes equations for isentropic flow in half-space
\{(x,y)\mid x\in\mathbb{T},y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\vec{U})=0,\\
&\rho\partial_{t}\vec{U}+\rho\vec{U}\cdot\nabla\vec{U}+\nabla P(\rho)=\mu\varepsilon\Delta\vec{U}+\lambda\varepsilon\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{U})+\rho\vec{F},\\
&\vec{U}|_{y=0}=\vec{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

In above equations
\rho
,
\vec{U}=(u,v)
and
P(\rho)
stand for the density, velocity field and pressure of the fluid. The vector field
\vec{F}
is a given external force.
The constants
\mu>0,\lambda\geq 0
are rescaled shear and bulk viscosity respectively, while
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is a small parameter which is proportional to the reciprocal of the Reynolds number. For simplicity and without loss of generality, the constant
\mu
is set to 1 throughout the paper.

A shear flow with boundary layer is defined by

(\rho_{s},\vec{\mathbf{U}}_{s})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(1,U_{s}(Y),0),~Y:=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},~\text{with }U_{s}(0)=0,\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}U_{s}(Y)=1.

It is a steady solution to (
1.1
) with external force
\vec{F}=(-\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s},0)
.

In this paper, we study the compressible Navier-Stokes system linearized around above shear flow. Denote the Mach number by
\mathcal{m}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P^{\prime}(1)}}
. The linearization gives

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+U_{s}\partial_{x}\rho+\nabla\cdot\vec{u}=0,~t>0,~(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&\partial_{t}\vec{u}+U_{s}\partial_{x}\vec{u}+\mathcal{m}^{-2}\nabla\rho+v\partial_{y}U_{s}\vec{e}_{1}-{\varepsilon}\Delta\vec{u}-\lambda\varepsilon\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{u})-\rho\vec{F}=0,\\
&\vec{u}|_{y=0}=\vec{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

To study (
1.2
), we use the rescaled variable

\tau=\frac{t}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},~X=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},~Y=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

and then look for solution to the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes system in the following form

(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})(Y)e^{i\alpha(X-c\tau)}.

Plugging this ansatz into (
1.2
) yields to the following system (we replace
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})
by
(\rho,u,v)
for simplicity of notation)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\rho+\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)=0,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}u+\lambda i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)u-(i\alpha\mathcal{m}^{-2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})\rho-v\partial_{Y}U_{s}=0,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}v+\lambda\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)v-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\rho=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

with no-slip boundary condition

\displaystyle u|_{Y=0}=v|_{Y=0}=0.

(1.4)

In (
1.3
),

\Delta_{\alpha}=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})
and
\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)=i\alpha u+\partial_{Y}v
denote the Fourier transform of Laplacian and divergence operators respectively. For convenience, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(\rho,u,v)
the linear operator (
1.3
). If for some
c
with
\text{Im}c>0
and wave number
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the boundary value problem (
1.3
) with (
1.4
) has a non-trivial solution, then the boundary layer profile
(\rho_{s},\vec{\mathbf{U}}_{s})
is spectral unstable. Otherwise, it is spectral stable.

In the following analysis, we focus on a class of shear flows that satisfy the following assumptions:

•

U_{s}\in C^{3}(\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}})
and satisfies

\displaystyle U_{s}(0)=0,~U_{s}(Y)>0,~\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}U_{s}(Y)=1,~\text{and }U_{s}^{\prime}(0)=1.

(1.5)

•

There exist positive constants
s_{0}
,
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
, such that

\displaystyle s_{1}e^{-s_{0}Y}\leq\partial_{Y}U_{s}(Y)\leq s_{2}e^{-s_{0}Y},~\forall Y\geq 0.

(1.6)

•

The boundary layer flow is assumed to be
uniformly subsonic
, i.e.
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
. Moreover, there exists a constant
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}(\mathcal{m})>0
such that for all
Y>0
, it holds

\displaystyle H(Y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{-\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})-2\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}}{|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|+|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}}\geq\sigma_{1}.

(1.7)

•

There exists a constant
\sigma_{2}>0
such that for any
Y>0,
it holds

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial_{Y}^{3}U_{s}}{\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}}\right|+\frac{|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|}{\partial_{Y}U_{s}}+\frac{1-U_{s}}{\partial_{Y}U_{s}}\leq\sigma_{2}.

(1.8)

Note that this class of profiles include the exponential profile
U_{s}(Y)=1-e^{-Y}
with
\sigma_{1}(\mathcal{m})=\frac{1-\mathcal{m}^{2}}{2}
and
s_{0}=s_{1}=s_{2}=\sigma_{2}=1.
Moreover, from (
1.7
), we have

\displaystyle-\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}(Y)\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}}{1-\mathcal{m}^{2}}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}(Y)|^{2},~\forall Y\geq 0.

(1.9)

Hence, the profiles in this class are strictly concave.

The main result in the paper can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the Mach number
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
. There is
K_{0}>1
such that for any sufficiently small
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
and
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
with
K\geq K_{0}
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\alpha\text{Im}c_{\varepsilon}\approx\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}
, such that the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes system (
1.2
) admits a solution
(\rho,u,v)
in the form of

\displaystyle(\rho,u,v)(t,x,y)=e^{\frac{i\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x-c_{\varepsilon}t)}(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})(Y),~Y:=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

(1.10)

where the profile
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times\left(H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\right)^{2}
and satisfies (
1.3
).

Remark 1.2
.

In the following we give several remarks on Theorem
1.1
.

(a)

As shown in the proof, the solution bounds depend on some negative power of
1-\mathcal{m}
that
are uniform when
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
. Therefore, by taking the vanishing Mach number limit,
we have the Tollmien-Schlichting wave solution for the incompressible flow that was analyzed
in

[
7
]
by Grenier-Guo-Nguyen.

(b)

The growing modes obtained are supported in the frequency regime
n=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}=K\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{8}}
and grow like
\exp(K^{-\frac{2}{3}}n^{\frac{2}{3}}t)
with
K\gg 1
.
These parameters indicate a spectral instability in Gevrey
space with index equal to
\frac{3}{2}
. How to justify the stability in Gevrey
\frac{3}{2}
class for the linear problem (
1.2
) that shares the same index as in the incompressible case studied in
[
12
]
will be discussed in our future work.

(c)

By constructing a suitable approximate growing mode, we can also obtain the same results as in Theorem
1.1
for the wave number
\alpha=C\varepsilon^{\beta}
, with
C>0
and
\beta\in(1/12,1/8)
. Here we will not give the detailed analysis in this regime because we focus on the most unstable mode when
\beta=1/8
. We refer to
[
22
]
on the incompressible MHD system for the related discussions and analysis.

1.2.
Relevant literature

Since this paper is motivated by the study of inviscid limit and Prandtl boundary layer expansion for incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, we briefly summarize some related works in this direction. Indeed, there are two main destabilizing mechanisms in the boundary layer that makes inviscid limit problem very challenging. The first one is induced by the inflexion points inside the boundary layer profile. In this case, the linearized Navier-Stokes system exhibits a strong ill-posedness below the analyticity regularity, cf.
[
6
,
8
]
. Therefore, the results of inviscid limit can be obtained only when the data is analytic at least near the boundary, cf.
[
18
,
25
,
30
,
32
,
34
]
. The second one is induced by the small disturbance around a monotone and concave boundary layer profile, called Tollmien-Schlichting instability that has been extensively studied in physical literatures and
was justified rigorously in Grenier-Guo-Nguyen
[
7
]
by constructing a growing mode in Gevrey
3/2
space to linearized incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. The main idea in
[
7
]
is to use the stream function and vorticity, that is, the Orr-Sommerfeld (OS) formulation, and then to solve it via an iteration based on Rayleigh and Airy equation that can be viewed as the inviscid and viscous approximation to the original OS equation.
We also refer to Grenier-Nguyen
[
9
]
for a result of nonlinear instability for small data that depend on viscosity coefficient. On the other hand, this instability result was complemented by the work of Gérard-Varet-Maekawa-Masmoudi
[
12
,
13
]
that establishes the Gevrey stability of Euler plus Prandtl expansion with critical Gevrey index
3/2
; see also
[
2
]
for a result in
L^{\infty}
-setting.
Most recently, the formation of boundary layer is studied by Maekawa
[
26
]
using the Rayleigh profile. For the Sobolev data, the boundary layer expansion is only valid under certain symmetry assumptions or for steady flows, cf.
[
11
,
14
,
16
,
24
,
28
]
and the references therein. Finally, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
10
]
for the instability analysis of boundary layer profile in different settings.

For compressible flow, even though there are many interesting results on
the Navier-Stokes equations at high Reynolds number in different settings, cf.
[
1
,
23
,
31
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
39
,
40
]
and the references therein, to our knowledge, the stability/instability properties of strong boundary layer for compressible Navier-Stokes equations have not yet been investigated. Compared to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, the major difficulty comes from the fact that the Orr-Sommerfeld formulation is no longer available for the compressible case. As a result, Rayleigh-Airy iteration approach used in
[
7
]
can not be applied, either the approaches used in
[
12
,
13
]
. Therefore, the novelty of this paper is to introduce a new iteration approach to study compressible flow in the subsonic and high Reynolds number regime.
We believe that the analytic techniques developed in this work can be used in other related problems for subsonic flows.

In the next subsection, we present the strategy of the proof for better understanding of the
detailed analysis in the following sections.

1.3.
Strategy of proof

The instability analysis is based on the following steps.

Step 1. Construction of the approximate growing mode.
As in the incompressible case
[
7
]
, the T-S instability is driven by the interaction of inviscid and viscous perturbations. Set the approximate growing mode
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}=(\rho_{\text{app}},u_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}})
as

\displaystyle(\rho_{\text{app}},u_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}})=(\rho_{\text{app}}^{s},u^{s}_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}}^{s})-\frac{v^{s}_{\text{app}}(0;c)}{v_{\text{app}}^{f}(0;c)}(\rho_{\text{app}}^{f},u^{f}_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}}^{f}).

(1.11)

Here the slow mode
(\rho_{\text{app}}^{s},u^{s}_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}}^{s})
is an approximate solution to the inviscid system (
2.1
), while the fast mode
(\rho_{\text{app}}^{f},u^{f}_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}}^{f})
is an approximate solution to the full system (
1.3
) which exhibits viscous boundary layer structure near
Y=0
. These solutions will
be given in Sec. 2.1 and 2.2 respectively. Note that the approximate solutions defined in (
1.11
) have zero normal velocity at the boundary, i.e.
v_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)\equiv 0.
Then to recover the no-slip boundary condition, inspired by
[
3
,
4
]
, we analyze the zero point of
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}u_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)-\frac{v^{s}_{\text{app}}(0;c)}{v_{\text{app}}^{f}(0;c)}u_{\text{app}}^{f}(0;c)
by applying
Rouché’s Theorem. Precisely, we study the equation
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)=0
in a family of
\varepsilon
-dependent domains
D_{0}
(see (
2.34
)). Then by Rouché’s Theorem, we can show that
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
has the same number of zero points as a linear function
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)
defined by (
2.41
). In addition, we prove that
|\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)|
has a strictly positive lower bound on
\partial D_{0}
.

Step 2. Stability of the approximate growing mode.
Since the approximate
solution
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}
exhibits the instability already, this step is to show the existence of an exact solution near
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}
.
This is the most difficult and key step. For this, we study the following resolvent problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\alpha(U_{s}-c){\rho}+\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)=0,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}u+\lambda i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)u-v\partial_{Y}U_{s}-(i\alpha\mathcal{m}^{-2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})\rho=f_{u},\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}v+\lambda\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)v-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\rho=f_{v},\\
&v|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.12)

with a given inhomogeneous source term
(f_{u},f_{v})
. Here, we emphasize that in (
1.12
) only normal velocity field
v
is prescribed on the boundary. Even though we relax the boundary constraint on
u
in (
1.12
), it is still difficult for existence because the presence of stretching term
v\partial_{Y}U_{s}
.
This difficulty is overcome by the following three ingredients.

•

Quasi-compressible approximation.

When the inhomogeneous source

(f_{u},f_{v})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
, we introduce the following
quasi-compressible
system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\varrho+i\alpha\mathfrak{u}+\partial_{Y}\mathfrak{v}=0,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho\right]-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{u}-\mathfrak{v}\partial_{Y}U_{s}-i\alpha\mathcal{m}^{-2}\varrho=f_{u},\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}\mathfrak{v}-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{v}-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\varrho=f_{v},\\
&\mathfrak{v}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.13)

that will be denoted by
L_{Q}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})=(0,f_{u},f_{v})
. Note that the inviscid part of the original linear operator
\mathcal{L}
is kept in
L_{Q}
, while the diffusion terms are modified to be divergence free. It turns out that
for Mach number
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
, the system (
1.13
) exhibits a similar stream function-vorticity structure as the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. In fact, if we introduce the stream function
\Psi
associated to the modified velocity variable
(\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho,\mathfrak{v})
satisfying

\partial_{Y}\Psi=\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho,~-i\alpha\Psi=\mathfrak{v},~\Psi|_{Y=0}=0,

then (
1.13
) can be reformulated in terms of
\Psi
as

\displaystyle\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Psi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{i}{n}\Lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi)+(U_{s}-c)\Lambda(\Psi)-\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}U_{s})\Psi

(1.14)

\displaystyle=f_{v}-\frac{1}{i\alpha}\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}f_{u}),

where
n=\alpha/\sqrt{\varepsilon}
,
A(Y)=1-\mathcal{m}^{2}(U_{s}-c)^{2}
and
\Lambda(\Psi)=\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}\Psi)-\alpha^{2}\Psi
. Note that
A(Y)
is invertible at least for
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
and
c
near the origin. When the Mach number
\mathcal{m}=0,
we have
\Lambda=\Delta_{\alpha}
and
A(Y)\equiv 1
. Thus
\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}
is exactly the classical Orr-Sommerfeld operator for incompressible Navier-Stokes system. Therefore,

\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}
can be viewed as the compressible counterpart of the Orr-Sommerfeld equation, which to our best knowledge is for the first time derived in the literatures. This formulation motivates the notion “quasi-compressible” approximation.

We solve (
1.14
) with artificial boundary conditions

\Psi|_{Y=0}=\Lambda(\Psi)|_{Y=0}=0
that allows us to obtain the weighted estimates on
\Lambda(\Psi)
. One can see that when
\mathcal{m}=0
, these boundary conditions are simply the perfect-slip
boundary conditions used in
[
2
,
11
,
13
]
for the study of incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. However, for the problem considered in this paper, the multiplier
\mathcal{w}(Y)=-\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}U_{s})
is not real. Therefore both its leading and first order terms
\mathcal{w}_{0}
,
\mathcal{w}_{1}
(see Lemma
3.3
for the precise definitions) play a role in the energy estimates.
For the bound estimations, we essentially use
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
and the new structural condition (
1.7
) of the profile in order to show that the function
\mathcal{w}_{0}-U_{s}\mathcal{w}_{1}
has a positive lower bound, cf. (
3.29
) in the proof of Lemma
3.4
.

After we obtain
\Psi
that solves (
1.14
), the solution
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
to (
1.13
) can be recovered in terms of
\Psi
, cf. (
3.11
) and (
3.12
). Here we would like to mention that (
1.13
) has a regularizing effect on density. That is,
formally by applying
\text{div}_{\alpha}
to the momentum equation in (
1.13
) and by noting that the diffusion term lies in the kernel of
\text{div}_{\alpha}
, we have
\Delta_{\alpha}\varrho\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
This reveals an elliptic structure for the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equations in the subsonic regime.

•

Stokes approximation.

Note that
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
is not an exact solution to (
1.12
) and its error is

\displaystyle E_{Q}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\mathcal{L}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})-L_{Q}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\left(0,-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}\left[(U_{s}-c)\varrho\right]+\lambda\sqrt{\varepsilon}i\alpha\text{div}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\varrho,\lambda\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\text{div}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\right).

(1.15)

This error term involves a small factor of
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
but lies only in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. This fact prevents us from using the standard fixed point argument to solve (
1.12
). To recover the regularity, we introduce another operator
L_{S}
that we call
Stokes approximation
. It is obtained from
\mathcal{L}
by removing the stretching term, that is,

L_{S}(\xi,\phi,\psi)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\mathcal{L}(\xi,\phi,\psi)+(0,\psi\partial_{Y}U_{s},0).

To eliminate the error
E_{Q}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
, we then take
(\xi,\phi,\psi)
as the solution to

L_{S}(\xi,\phi,\psi)=-E_{Q}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v}),~\partial_{Y}\phi|_{Y=0}=\psi|_{Y=0}=0.

By using the energy approach in the same spirit as Matsumura-Nishida
[
27
]
and Kawashima
[
17
]
, we are able to show
(\xi,\phi,\psi)
is in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
. Thus, the error term
E_{S}(\xi,\phi,\psi):=(0,\psi\partial_{Y}U_{s},0)
is in the weighted space
H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
so that we can treat it as source term of
L_{Q}
. Therefore, we can iterate the above two approximations.

•

Quasi-compressible-Stokes iteration.

Recall that we have the following two decompositions of the linear operator
\mathcal{L}:

\mathcal{L}=L_{Q}+E_{Q}=L_{S}+E_{S}.

The solvability to (
1.12
) can be justified via an iteration scheme that is illustrated as follows. Assume that at the
N
-th step we have an approximate solution in the form of
\sum_{j=0}^{N}\vec{\Xi}_{j}
which satisfies

\mathcal{L}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N}\vec{\Xi}_{j}\right)=(0,f_{u},f_{v})+\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N}.

Here
\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N}
is an error term at this step. Provided that
\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N}
is smooth enough and has zero value at its first component, we can introduce a corrector

\vec{\Xi}_{N+1}=-L_{Q}^{-1}(\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N})+L_{S}^{-1}\circ E_{Q}\circ L_{Q}^{-1}(\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N}),

where
L_{Q}^{-1}
and
L_{S}^{-1}
denote respectively the solution operators to quasi-compressible and Stokes approximate systems. The approximate solution at the
N+1
-step is therefore defined by
\sum_{j=0}^{N+1}\vec{\Xi}_{j}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\vec{\Xi}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=(0,f_{u},f_{v})+\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N+1}

\displaystyle:=(0,f_{u},f_{v})+E_{S}\circ L_{S}^{-1}\circ E_{Q}\circ L_{Q}^{-1}(\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{N}).

A combination of the smallness of
E_{Q}
, the regularizing effect of
L_{S}^{-1}
and the strong decay property of
E_{S}
yields the contraction in
H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
of truncated error operator
E_{S}\circ L_{S}^{-1}\circ E_{Q}\circ L_{Q}^{-1}
so that the convergence of series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\vec{\Xi}_{j}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
follows, cf. the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Step. 3. Recovery of the no-slip boundary condition.
We look for solutions to the original system (
1.3
) with
v|_{Y=0}=0
in the form of
(\rho,u,v)=(\rho_{\text{app}},u_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}})-(\rho_{R},u_{R},v_{R})
. Here the remainder
(\rho_{R},u_{R},v_{R})
solves
\mathcal{L}(\rho_{R},u_{R},v_{R})=E,v_{R}|_{Y=0}=0
, where
E
is the error due to the approximation
(\rho_{\text{app}},u_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}})
. In this step, we need to decompose the error into regular and smallness parts as in (
2.44
). The remainder is divided accordingly into
\vec{\Xi}_{R}=\vec{\Xi}_{\text{re}}+\vec{\Xi}_{\text{sm}}.
The reason for such decomposition is that the regular part
E_{\text{re}}
coming from the rough approximation to Rayleigh equation has a worse bound than the smallness part so that we can
compensate some extra order of
\varepsilon
by the favorable bounds of
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{re}}
due to strong decay and
H^{1}
-regularity of
E_{\text{re}}
, cf. Proposition
3.1
.
Eventually, we can prove that
|u_{R}(0;c)|\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{16}}
on
\partial D_{0}
, which is smaller than
|u_{\text{app}}(0;c)|
. Then we conclude Theorem
1.1

by Rouché’s Theorem.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we will construct the approximate growing mode. In Section 3, we will show
the solvability of the linearized system (
1.3
) with zero normal velocity condition in order to resolve the remainder due to the approximation. The proof is divided into several steps. Firstly, two approximate systems, that is, Quasi-compressible and Stokes approximations will be introduced in Sections 3.1 and 3.2 respectively. Based on these two systems, the iteration scheme will be analyzed in Section 3.3.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in the final section. In the Appendix, we will
give the proof of the invertibility of operator
\Lambda
that is used in the construction of solution to the equation (
3.16
).

2.
Approximate growing mode

In the following three subsections, we will construct the approximate growing mode that satisfies the no-slip boundary condition.
As for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, it is based on the superposition of the slow mode
and the fast mode that represent the interaction of the inviscid and viscous effects near the boundary.

2.1.
Slow mode

In this subsection, we will construct the slow mode to capture the inviscid behavior. For this, we consider the following system denoted by
\mathcal{I}(\rho,u,v)=\vec{0}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\rho+\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)=0,\\
&i\alpha(U_{s}-c)u+\mathcal{m}^{-2}i\alpha\rho+v\partial_{Y}U_{s}=0,\\
&i\alpha(U_{s}-c)v+\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\rho=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

By introducing a new function
\Phi=\frac{i}{\alpha}v
, from
\eqref{2.1.1}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle u=\partial_{Y}\Phi-(U_{s}-c)\rho.

(2.2)

Substituting this into
\eqref{2.1.1}_{2}
yields

\displaystyle-\mathcal{m}^{-2}A(Y)\rho=(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}\Phi-\Phi\partial_{Y}U_{s},

where

\displaystyle A(Y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}1-\mathcal{m}^{2}(U_{s}-c)^{2}.

(2.3)

Note that for the
uniformly subsonic
boundary layer, i.e.
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
, when
|c|\ll 1
,
A(Y)
is invertible so that we can represent
\rho
in terms of
\Phi
by

\displaystyle\rho=-\mathcal{m}^{2}A^{-1}(Y)\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}\Phi-\Phi\partial_{Y}U_{s}\right].

(2.4)

Plugging (
2.4
) into
\eqref{2.1.1}_{3}
, we derive the following equation for
\Phi
, which can be viewed as an analogy of the classical Rayleigh equation in the compressible setting:

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\partial_{Y}\left\{A^{-1}\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}\Phi-\Phi\partial_{Y}U_{s}\right]\right\}-\alpha^{2}(U_{s}-c)\Phi=0.

(2.5)

We remark that the equation (
2.5
) was firstly derived by Lee and Lin in
[
19
]
for the study of stability of shear flow in inviscid fluid. Thus (
2.5
) is sometimes referred to as Lee-Lin equation.

The slow mode will be constructed based on an approximate solution to (
2.5
).
Since (
2.5
) has similar structure of the Rayleigh equation, the construction is similar as
[
7
,
22
]
for incompressible flow. In what follows we sketch
the key steps to make the paper to be self-contained.

Starting from
\alpha=0
, the equation (
2.5
) admits following two independent solutions

\displaystyle\varphi_{+}(Y)=(U_{s}-c),~\varphi_{-}(Y)=(U_{s}-c)\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-c)^{2}}{\rm d}X-\mathcal{m}^{2}(U_{s}-c)Y,~\text{for }\text{Im}c>0.

For
\alpha>0
, to capture the decay property of the solution, we set

\displaystyle\beta\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\alpha A_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}},~\text{where }A_{\infty}=\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}A(Y)=1-\mathcal{m}^{2}(1-c)^{2}=1-\mathcal{m}^{2}+O(1)|c|,~\text{for }|c|\ll 1.

(2.6)

Then we define

\displaystyle\varphi_{+,\alpha}(Y)=e^{-\beta Y}\varphi_{+}(Y),~\varphi_{-,\alpha}(Y)=e^{-\beta Y}\varphi_{-}(Y).

(2.7)

Direct computation yields the following error terms:

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}\left(\varphi_{+,\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\beta A^{-2}\partial_{Y}U_{s}\varphi_{+,\alpha}+A^{-1}(U_{s}-c)(\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}A)\varphi_{+,\alpha},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}\left(\varphi_{-,\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\beta A^{-2}\partial_{Y}U_{s}\varphi_{-,\alpha}+A^{-1}(U_{s}-c)(\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}A)\varphi_{-,\alpha}-2\beta e^{-\beta Y}.

(2.9)

To have a better approximate solution for (
2.5
) up to
O(\alpha^{2})
, the following approximate Green’s function is needed:

G_{\alpha}(X,Y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}-(U_{s}(X)-c)^{-1}\left\{\begin{aligned} &e^{-\beta(Y-X)}\varphi_{+}(Y)\varphi_{-}(X),~X<Y,\\
&e^{-\beta(Y-X)}\varphi_{+}(X)\varphi_{-}(Y),~X>Y.\end{aligned}\right.

Then we define a corrector

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,\alpha}(Y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}2\int_{0}^{\infty}G(X,Y)A^{-2}(X)\partial_{Y}U_{s}(X)\varphi_{+,\alpha}(X){\rm d}X,

(2.10)

and set

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;c)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\varphi_{+,\alpha}+\beta\varphi_{1,\alpha}.

(2.11)

Hence, by (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}\left(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\right)=

\displaystyle-2\beta^{2}A^{-2}U_{s}^{\prime}\varphi_{1,\alpha}+4\beta^{2}e^{-\beta Y}\int_{Y}^{\infty}A^{-2}(X)U_{s}^{\prime}(X)\varphi_{+}(X){\rm d}X

\displaystyle+A^{-1}(U_{s}-c)(\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}A)\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(1)\alpha^{2}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|.

(2.12)

In summary,
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}
is the slow mode with properties given in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let the Mach number
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1).
Then for each
Y\geq 0
,
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;c)
is holomophic in the upper-half plane
\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid\text{Im}c>0\}
. Moreover, there exists
\gamma_{1}\in(0,1)
, such that if
\text{Im}c>0
and
|c|<\gamma_{1}
, we have the expansions:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)

\displaystyle=-c+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O(1)\alpha|c\log\text{Im}c|,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)

\displaystyle=1+O(1)\alpha|\log\text{Im}c|.

(2.14)

Proof.

Since
\text{Im}c>0
,
U_{s}(Y)-c\neq 0,~\forall Y\geq 0
. The analyticity of
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}
follows from the explicit formula (
2.7
), (
2.10
) and (
2.11
). Now we derive the boundary values
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)
and
\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c).

Firstly, note that

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,\alpha}(Y;c)=

\displaystyle-2e^{-\beta Y}\varphi_{+}(Y)\int_{0}^{Y}\varphi_{-}(X)A^{-2}(X)\partial_{Y}U_{s}(X){\rm d}X

\displaystyle-2e^{-\beta Y}\varphi_{-}(Y)\int_{Y}^{\infty}\varphi_{+}(X)A^{-2}(X)\partial_{Y}U_{s}(X){\rm d}X.

(2.15)

Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)

\displaystyle=-2\varphi_{-}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}A^{-2}(X)(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}U_{s}(X){\rm d}X=-\frac{\varphi_{-}(0)}{\mathcal{m}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}X}(A^{-1}){\rm d}X

\displaystyle=-\frac{\varphi_{-}(0)}{\mathcal{m}^{2}}\left(A^{-1}(+\infty)-A^{-1}(0)\right)=\frac{-\varphi_{-}(0)(1-2c)}{[1-\mathcal{m}^{2}(1-c)^{2}][1-\mathcal{m}^{2}c^{2}]}

\displaystyle=-{\varphi_{-}(0)}\left(\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{m}^{2}}+O(1)|c|\right),~\text{for }|c|\ll 1.

(2.16)

Then by using (
2.11
) and the fact that
\beta=\alpha[(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(1)|c|]
,
one has

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)=\varphi_{+,\alpha}(0,c)+\beta\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0,c)=-c-\alpha\varphi_{-}(0)\left(\frac{1}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O(1)|c|\right).

(2.17)

To estimate the boundary value
\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)
, differentiating (
2.1
) yields that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\varphi_{1,\alpha}(Y;c)=

\displaystyle-\beta\varphi_{1,\alpha}(Y;c)+U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)(U_{s}-c)^{-1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}(Y;c)

\displaystyle-2e^{-\beta Y}A(Y)(U_{s}-c)^{-1}\int_{Y}^{\infty}\varphi_{+}(X)A^{-2}(X)\partial_{Y}U_{s}(X){\rm d}X.

Similar to (
2.1
), by using
U_{s}^{\prime}(0)=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)

\displaystyle=-\beta\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)-c^{-1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)+2c^{-1}A(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi_{+}(X)A^{-2}(X)\partial_{Y}U_{s}(X){\rm d}X

\displaystyle=-\beta\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)-c^{-1}\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)+c^{-1}A(0)\left(\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{m}^{2}}+O(1)|c|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})}\left(1+\varphi_{-}(0)\right)+O(1)(1+|\varphi_{-}(0)|).

Here we have used (
2.1
) in the last identity. Consequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)

\displaystyle=\partial_{Y}\varphi_{+,\alpha}(0;c)+\beta\partial_{Y}\varphi_{1,\alpha}(0;c)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha}{c(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(1+\varphi_{-}(0))+O(1)|\alpha|(1+|\varphi_{-}(0)|).

(2.18)

Finally, we have
\varphi_{-}(0)=-1+O(1)|c\log\text{Im}c|,
cf. Lemma 3.1 in
[
22
]
. Then by substituting
this into (
2.17
) and (
2.1
), we obtain (
2.13
) and (
2.14
). The proof of Lemma
2.1
is completed.
∎

With
\Phi^{s}_{\text{app}}
, we can define the slow mode of fluid quantities
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(\rho_{\text{app}}^{s},u_{\text{app}}^{s},v_{\text{app}}^{s})

by using (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) as follows:

\displaystyle v_{\text{app}}^{s}=-i\alpha\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s},~\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}=-\mathcal{m}^{2}A^{-1}\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\partial_{Y}U_{s}\right],~u_{\text{app}}^{s}=\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}-(U_{s}-c)\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}.

(2.19)

One can directly check that
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s})=(0,0,\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s})),

(2.20)

where the error
\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s})
is given in (
2.1
). Therefore,
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}
is an approximate solution to
the inviscid equation (
2.1
) up to
O(\alpha^{2})
.

2.2.
Fast mode

To capture the viscous effect of (
1.3
) in the boundary layer,
we need to construct a boundary sublayer corresponding to the fast mode in the approximate
solution. Let
z\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\delta^{-1}Y
. Here
0<\delta\ll 1
is the scale of boundary sublayer which will be determined later. Now we scale the density and velocity fields in the sublayer by setting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}(z)=\rho(Y),~~\mathcal{U}(z)=u(Y),~~\mathcal{V}(z)=(i\alpha\delta)^{-1}v(Y).

(2.21)

This leads to the following rescaled system associated to (
1.3
):

\displaystyle(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{p}+\mathcal{U}+\partial_{z}\mathcal{V}=0,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{2}\mathcal{U}-in\delta^{2}(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{U}-in\delta^{3}U_{s}^{\prime}\mathcal{V}-(\mathcal{m}^{-2}in\delta^{2}+\delta^{2}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})\mathfrak{p}-\alpha^{2}\delta^{2}\left[(1+\lambda)\mathcal{U}+\lambda\partial_{z}\mathcal{V}\right]=0,

(2.23)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{2}\mathcal{V}-in\delta^{2}(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{V}-\alpha^{2}\delta^{2}\mathcal{V}+\lambda\partial_{z}(\mathcal{U}+\partial_{z}\mathcal{V})+i\mathcal{m}^{-2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{-1}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{p}=0.

(2.24)

Here the constant
n\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
which is the rescaled frequency.
Recalling
U_{s}^{\prime}(0)=1,
we can rewrite
U_{s}(Y)-c
as

\displaystyle U_{s}(Y)-c=U_{s}^{\prime}(0)Y-c+[U_{s}(Y)-U_{s}^{\prime}(0)Y]=\delta(z+z_{0})+O(1)|\delta|^{2}|z|^{2},

(2.25)

where
z_{0}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}-\delta^{-1}c
, and

\displaystyle U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)=U_{s}^{\prime}(0)+U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)-U_{s}^{\prime}(0)=1+O(1)|\delta||z|.

(2.26)

In view of (
2.22
)-(
2.26
), it is natural to set

\delta=e^{-\frac{1}{6}\pi i}n^{-\frac{1}{3}},

so that
in\delta^{3}=1.
Formally, we have the expansion

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{0}+\delta\mathfrak{p}_{1}+\cdots,~~\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{0}+\delta\mathcal{U}_{1}\cdots,~~\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{0}+\delta\mathcal{V}_{1}\cdots.

Inserting this expansion into (
2.22
)-(
2.24
) and taking the leading order, we can derive the following system for
(\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{0}(z)=\mathcal{U}_{0}(z)+\partial_{z}\mathcal{V}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{2}\mathcal{U}_{0}(z)-(z+z_{0})\mathcal{U}_{0}(z)-\mathcal{V}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.28)

where the variable
z
lies in the segment
e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}\mathbb{R}_{+}
. From (
2.27
), we
observe that the leading order terms of the
density and divergence of velocity field vanish in the sublayer.
We also require
(\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})

to concentrate near the boundary, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty,z\in e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})=\vec{0}.

Differentiating (
2.28
), by (
2.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{z}^{4}\mathcal{V}_{0}-(z+z_{0})\partial_{z}^{2}\mathcal{V}_{0}=0.

(2.29)

Therefore, from (
2.27
) and (
2.29
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0}(z)=-\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z+z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})},~\mathcal{V}_{0}(z)=\frac{\text{Ai}(2,z+z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})}.

Here
\text{Ai}(1,z)
and
\text{Ai}(2,z)
are respectively the first and second order primitives of the classical Airy function
\text{Ai}(z)
which is the solution to Airy equation

\partial_{z}^{2}\text{Ai}-z\text{Ai}=0.

\text{Ai}(2,z),

\text{Ai}(1,z)
and
\text{Ai}(z)
all vanish at infinity along
e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}\mathbb{R}_{+}
. They satisfy the relations
\partial_{z}\text{Ai}(k,z)=\text{Ai}(k-1,z)
,
k=1,2,
where
\text{Ai}(0,z)\equiv\text{Ai}(z)
. For the detailed construction of these profiles, we refer to
[
12
]
.

Finally, by rescaling the leading order profile
(\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
via (
2.21
), we define the fast mode as

\displaystyle\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{f}=(\rho_{\text{app}}^{f},u_{\text{app}}^{f},v_{\text{app}}^{f})(Y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(0,\mathcal{U}_{0},i\alpha\delta\mathcal{V}_{0})(\delta^{-1}Y).

(2.30)

Obviously,

\displaystyle u_{\text{app}}^{f}(0;c)=-\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})},~v_{\text{app}}^{f}(0;c)=i\alpha\delta.

(2.31)

2.3.
Approximate growing mode

Based on slow and fast modes constructed in the above two subsections, we
are now ready to construct an approximate growing mode to (
1.3
) with boundary condition (
1.4
). Set

\displaystyle\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}(Y;c)

\displaystyle=(\rho_{\text{app}},u_{\text{app}},v_{\text{app}})(Y;c)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;c)-\frac{v^{s}_{\text{app}}(0;c)}{v^{f}_{\text{app}}(0;c)}\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;c)

\displaystyle=\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;c)+\delta^{-1}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;c),

(2.32)

where
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}
,
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{f}
are defined in (
2.19
), (
2.30
) respectively, and the function
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;c)
is defined in (
2.11
) with boundary data satisfying (
2.13
) and (
2.14
). Thanks to (
2.31
), the normal velocity
v_{\text{app}}
satisfies the zero boundary condition, that is,
v_{\text{app}}(0;c)\equiv 0.
Therefore, the approximate solution (
2.3
) satisfies the full no-slip boundary condition (
1.4
) if and only if
the following function vanishes at some point
c
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}u_{\text{app}}(0;c)=\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)+c\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)-\delta^{-1}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z_{0}(c))}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0}(c))}.

To show the existence of such
c
for
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)=0
, we consider the Mach number
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)

and the wave number
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
with
K\geq 1
being a large but fixed real number. Set

\displaystyle c_{0}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\left(\frac{K}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+K^{-1}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{4}\pi i}\right)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}

(2.33)

and define a disk centered at
c_{0}
by

\displaystyle D_{0}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\left\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid|c-c_{0}|\leq K^{-1-\theta}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\right\},

(2.34)

with some constant
\theta\in(0,1)
. Clearly, for any
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
\tau_{0}>0
(
\tau_{0}\rightarrow 0
as
\mathcal{m}\rightarrow 1
), such that for sufficiently large
K
, the following estimates hold for any
c\in D_{0}
:

\displaystyle\text{Im}c\geq\tau_{0}K^{-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}},~0<\text{arg}c<\tau_{0}K^{-2},~\text{and }\frac{K(1-\tau_{0}K^{-2})}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\leq|c|\leq\frac{K(1+\tau_{0}K^{-2})}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}.

(2.35)

With the above preparation, we will prove the following proposition about the existence
of approximate growing mode.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
. There exists a positive constant
K_{0}>1
, such that if
K\geq K_{0}
, then there exists
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
, such that for
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
c\in D_{0}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
has a unique zero point in
D_{0}
. Moreover, on the circle
\partial D_{0},
it holds that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)|\geq\frac{1}{2}K^{-\theta}.

(2.36)

Proof.

The proof follows the approach used in Proposition 3.2 in the authors’ paper
[
22
]
with Liu on the incompressible MHD system. For completeness, we sketch the main steps as follows. Firstly, we take
K_{0}
sufficiently large
so that (
2.35
) holds in the disk
D_{0}
. Then for
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and any
c\in D_{0},
by (
2.13
), (
2.14
) and (
2.35
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)=-c+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O(1)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}|\log\varepsilon|,~\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)=1+O(1)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|.

(2.37)

Thus from (
2.4
) the expression for
\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{m}^{2}A^{-1}(0)\left(c\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)+U_{s}^{\prime}(0)\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)\right)=\frac{\mathcal{m}^{2}}{1-\mathcal{m}^{2}c^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+O(1)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}|\log\varepsilon|\right)

\displaystyle=O(1)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}.

(2.38)

Next, we consider the ratio
\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})}
. Recall
z_{0}(c)=-\delta^{-1}c=-e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}K^{\frac{1}{3}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{8}}c
. (
2.35
) implies that

\displaystyle|z_{0}|=\frac{K^{\frac{4}{3}}}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(1+\tau_{0}K^{-2}),\text{ and }-\frac{5}{6}\pi<\text{arg}z_{0}<-\frac{5}{6}\pi+\tau_{0}K^{-2},~~\forall c\in D_{0}.

(2.39)

Then using the asymptotic behavior of Airy profile (e.g. cf.
[
12
]
) and by (
2.39
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})}=-z_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(1)|z_{0}|^{-1}=-\frac{K^{\frac{2}{3}}}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}e^{-\frac{5}{12}\pi i}+O(1)K^{-\frac{4}{3}},~~K\gg 1.

(2.40)

Now we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}1+e^{-\frac{1}{4}\pi i}K(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(-c+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right).

(2.41)

On one hand, there exists a unique zero point
c_{0}
in (
2.33
) to the mapping
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)
. And on the boundary
\partial D_{0}
, it holds

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)|=K^{-\theta}.

(2.42)

On the other hand, we can show that
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)
is the leading order of
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
. In fact, by (
2.37
), (
2.3
) and (
2.40
),
we have the following estimate on the difference:

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)-\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|1+e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}K^{\frac{1}{3}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(c-\frac{\alpha}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})}-\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)\right|+C_{K,\mathcal{m}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq\left|1+e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}K^{\frac{1}{3}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(c-\frac{\alpha}{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)z_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)\right|+C_{\mathcal{m}}K^{-2}+C_{K,\mathcal{m}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathcal{m}}K^{-2}+C_{K,\mathcal{m}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|,

where the positive constants
C_{\mathcal{m}}
is independent of
K
and
\varepsilon
and
C_{K,\mathcal{m}}
depends on
K
and Mach number
\mathcal{m}
, but not on
\varepsilon
. Now we take
K_{0}
larger if needed and then take
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
suitably small such that for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
and
K\geq K_{0}
, it holds that

C_{\mathcal{m}}K^{-2}+C_{K,\mathcal{m}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}K^{-\theta}.

Consequently, one obtains

|\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)-\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)|,~\forall c\in\partial D_{0}.

Combining this with (
2.42
) yields (
2.36
). Moreover,
since
\text{Ai}(1,z)
and
\text{Ai}(2,z)
are analytic functions and
\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})\neq 0
due to (
2.40
), both
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
and
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)
are analytic in
D_{0}
. Therefore, by Rouché’s Theorem,
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
and
\mathcal{F}_{\text{ref}}(c)
have the same number of zeros in
D_{0}
. The proof of Proposition
2.2
is then completed.
∎

We now conclude this subsection by summarizing the relations between the parameters
n
(rescaled frequency),
\delta
(scale of sublayer),
\alpha
(wave number) and
\varepsilon
that will be used frequently later:

n=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}};\text{ and }\delta=e^{-\frac{1}{6}\pi i}n^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

If in particular
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
c\in D_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\alpha\approx|c|\approx\text{Im}c\approx|\delta|\approx n^{-\frac{1}{3}}\approx\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}},

(2.43)

where the relations may depend on
K
but not on
\varepsilon
.

2.4.
Estimates on error terms

In this subsection, we will give the detailed estimate on the error of the approximate solution (
2.3
) by using a decomposition that takes the decay and regularity in
Y
into consideration.

“Regular+Smallness” decomposition:
Precisely, the approximate solution
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}
to (
1.3
) has the following error representation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}})=(0,0,E_{v,\text{re}})+(0,E_{u,\text{sm}},E_{v,\text{sm}}).

(2.44)

Here the regular part

\displaystyle E_{v,\text{re}}=\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s})

(2.45)

with
\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s})
defined in (
2.1
). Observe that
E_{v,\text{re}}
has strong decay in
Y
due to the background boundary layer profile. And
the smallness part reads

\displaystyle E_{u,\text{sm}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}u_{\text{app}}^{s}+\lambda i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u_{\text{app}}^{s},v_{\text{app}}^{s})-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}+\eta\sqrt{\varepsilon}\alpha^{2}u_{\text{app}}^{f}

(2.46)

\displaystyle-i\alpha\eta(U_{s}(Y)-U_{s}^{\prime}(0)Y){u}_{\text{app}}^{f}-\eta v_{\text{app}}^{f}(\partial_{Y}U_{s}(Y)-\partial_{Y}U_{s}(0)),

\displaystyle E_{v,\text{sm}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}v_{\text{app}}^{s}+\lambda\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u_{\text{app}}^{s},v_{\text{app}}^{s})+\eta\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}v_{\text{app}}^{f}-i\alpha\eta(U_{s}-c)v_{\text{app}}^{f},

where
\eta\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\delta^{-1}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)
. In the following,
we will show that
E_{u,\text{sm}}
and
E_{v,\text{sm}}
are of higher order in
\varepsilon
than
E_{v,\text{re}}
.

The estimates on these error terms are summarized in the following proposition. Let us first define some weighted Sobolev spaces for later use.

\displaystyle L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\displaystyle\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\bigg|~\|f\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\||\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle H^{N}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\displaystyle\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\left\{f\in H^{N}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\bigg|~\|f\|_{H^{N}_{w}}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\|\partial_{Y}^{j}f\|_{L^{2}_{w}}<\infty\},~N\text{ is a positive integer}\right\}.

(2.47)

Recall
K_{0}\geq 1
and
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
are constants given in Proposition
2.2
. For
K\geq K_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}),
the disk
D_{0}
is defined in (
2.34
). The following proposition gives
the precise error bound estimates.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let the Mach number
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
. There exists
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, such that for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{2})
,
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
c\in D_{0}
, the error terms
E_{v,\text{re}}
,
E_{u,\text{sm}}
and
E_{v,\text{sm}}
satisfy the estimates

\displaystyle\|E_{v,\text{re}}(\cdot~;c)\|_{H^{1}_{w}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{16}},

(2.48)

\displaystyle\|E_{u,\text{sm}}(\cdot~;c)\|_{L^{2}}+\|E_{v,\text{sm}}(\cdot~;c)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K}\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{16}},

(2.49)

where the constant is uniform in
\varepsilon
.

The proof of Proposition
2.3
follows from a series of estimations on the approximate solution. First of all, we show some properties of corrector
\varphi_{1,\alpha}
and approximate solutions
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}
to Rayleigh operator that are defined in (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) respectively. Fix
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
and set
{\beta}_{1}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{1}{2}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\gamma_{1}
be the constant given in Lemma
2.1
. There exists
\gamma_{2}\in(0,\gamma_{1})
, such that for
c\in\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid\text{Im}c>0,~|c|<\gamma_{2}\}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\varphi_{1,\alpha}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}+\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1,

(2.50)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim 1+|\log\text{Im}c|,~\|\partial_{Y}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1,

(2.51)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}+(\text{Im}c)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(\text{Im}c)^{-1},

(2.52)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}+(\text{Im}c)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(\text{Im}c)^{-2}.

(2.53)

Moreover, if in addition
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
c\in D_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{W^{2,\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}+(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Phi^{s}_{\text{app}}\|_{L^{2}}+\text{Im}c\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(2.54)

Proof.

Recall that
\beta=\alpha\left[(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(1)|c|\right]
from (
2.6
) and
A(Y)=1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2}+O(1)|c|
from (
2.3
). Taking
\gamma_{2}\in(0,\gamma_{1})
suitably small, we have

\text{Re}\beta>\beta_{1}
and
|A^{-1}|\leq\frac{1}{2(1-\mathcal{m}^{2})}\lesssim 1
for
|c|<\gamma_{2}
.
Then the proof of (
2.50
)-(
2.53
) follows from an argument
exactly as in Lemma 3.6 in
[
22
]
by using the explicit expression (
2.1
). Hence, we omit it for brevity.

For (
2.54
), we recall (
2.11
) and observe that

\displaystyle\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi_{+,\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}\varphi_{+,\alpha}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\varphi_{+,\alpha}\|_{W^{3,\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

(2.55)

By (
2.43
), we have
\alpha/\text{Im}c\lesssim 1
. Thus putting (
2.50
)-(
2.53
) and (
2.55
) together yields the desired estimate (
2.54
). The proof of the lemma is
then completed.
∎

By Lemma
2.4
, we can immediately obtain the following estimates on the slow mode
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}
given in (

Corollary 2.5
.

If
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and

, the slow mode
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}^{s}
satisfies the following estimates:

\displaystyle\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{H^{1}}+\|u_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{H^{1}}+\|v_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1,

(2.56)

\displaystyle\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{2}u_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\text{Im}c)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.57)

Proof.

The estimate on
v_{\text{app}}^{s}
follows from (
2.54
) directly. For
\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}
, using (
2.54
) gives

\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}U_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1.

Differentiating (
2.4
) with respect to
Y
yields the formulas

\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}=-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}A^{-1}A^{\prime}-A^{-1}(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}+\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}A^{-1}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s},

and

\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}^{2}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}=-2\mathcal{m}^{-2}A^{-1}\partial_{Y}A\partial_{Y}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}-\mathcal{m}^{-2}A^{-1}\partial_{Y}^{2}A\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}-A^{-1}\partial_{Y}\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\right].

Taking

-norm and by (

\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\partial_{Y}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1,

and

\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|(\rho_{\text{app}}^{s},\partial_{Y}\rho_{\text{app}}^{s})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{3}U_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim(\text{Im}c)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

The velocity field
u_{\text{app}}^{s}
can be estimated in the same way so that we omit the details. And
this completes the proof of the corollary.
∎

The following lemma gives some pointwise estimates on the fast mode

Lemma 2.6

The fast mode

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{k}_{Y}u_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;c)\right|

(2.58)

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{k}_{Y}v_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;c)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\frac{k-2}{3}}e^{-\tau_{1}n^{\frac{1}{3}}Y},~k=0,1,2,

(2.59)

for some constant
\tau_{1}>0
which does not depend on
n
.

With the above estimates, we are now ready to prove Proposition
2.3
as follows.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
:
We start with proving (
2.48
) for
E_{v,\text{re}}
. Recall the definition (
2.45
) and explicit formula (
2.1
). By taking
\varepsilon_{2}>0
suitably small, such that

\displaystyle|E_{v,\text{re}}(Y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{2}\partial_{Y}U_{s}(Y)e^{-\beta_{1}Y}\|\varphi_{1,\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}+\alpha^{2}e^{-\beta_{1}Y}(1-U_{s}(Y))+|\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}A|e^{-\beta_{1}Y}\|\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\beta_{1}}}

(2.61)

3.
Solvability of remainder system

In this section, we will construct a solution to the resolvent problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\rho+\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)=0,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}u+\lambda i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)u-v\partial_{Y}U_{s}-(\mathcal{m}^{-2}i\alpha+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})\rho=f_{u},\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}v+\lambda\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)v-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\rho=f_{v},\\
&v|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where
(f_{u},f_{v})
is a given inhomogeneous source term. If
(f_{u},f_{v})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
, we define the operator

\displaystyle\Omega(f_{u},f_{v})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}f_{v}-\frac{1}{i\alpha}\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}f_{u}).

(3.2)

Recall (
2.47
) the weighted function space
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
The following is the main result in this section.

Proposition 3.1

(Solvability of resolvent problem)
.

Let the Mach number
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}).
There exists
\varepsilon_{3}\in(0,1)
, such that

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{3})
,
\alpha=K\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
c\in D_{0}
, then the following two statements hold.

(1) If
(f_{u},f_{v})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
, then there exists a solution
\vec{\Xi}=(\rho,u,v)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to (
3.1
) which satisfies the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{m}^{-1}\rho,u,v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha(\text{Im}c)^{2}}\|(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(u,v)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha(\text{Im}c)^{2}}\|(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}u,\partial_{Y}v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\alpha(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}u,\partial_{Y}^{2}v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{n}{\alpha\text{Im}c}\|(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.6)

(2) If in addition we have
(f_{u},f_{v})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}
with
\|\Omega(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}<\infty,
then there exists a solution
\vec{\Xi}=(\rho,u,v)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\times H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to (
3.1
) which satisfies the following improved estimates

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{m}^{-1}\rho,u,v)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|f_{u}\|+\|f_{v}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}u,\partial_{Y}^{2}v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\Omega(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\|f_{u}\|+\|f_{v}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}}\right).

(3.8)

Moreover, if
(f_{u},f_{v})(\cdot~;c)
is analytic in

with values in

, then the solution
\vec{\Xi}(\cdot~;c)
is analytic with values in

.

Remark 3.2
.

(a)

By Sobolev embedding

(b)

(c)

(d)

As one can see from the proof, the argument also works for a wider regime of parameters:

(3.9)

In fact, the boundedness of wave number

is essentially used in the proof. In addition,
we require

) so that

where

As mentioned in the Introduction, the proof of Proposition
3.1
is based on the following two newly introduced decompositions, i.e., quasi-compressibile approximation and the Stokes approximation.

3.1.
Quasi-compressible approximation

Following the strategy described in the Introduction, we first consider the following approximate problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\varrho+\text{div}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})=0,\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}\left(\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho\right)-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{u}-\mathfrak{v}\partial_{Y}U_{s}-i\alpha\mathcal{m}^{-2}\varrho=s_{1},\\
&\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Delta_{\alpha}\mathfrak{v}-i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{v}-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\varrho=s_{2},\\
&\mathfrak{v}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.10)

with a given inhomogeneous source term
(s_{1},s_{2})
.

By the continuity equation
\eqref{3.1.1}_{1}
, we can define a stream function
\Psi
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\Psi=\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho,~-i\alpha\Psi=\mathfrak{v},~\Psi|_{Y=0}=0.

(3.11)

Then by
\eqref{3.1.1}_{2}
we can express the density
\rho
in terms of
\Psi
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{m}^{-2}\varrho(Y)=-A^{-1}(Y)\left[\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{\alpha}\partial_{Y}\Psi+(U_{s}-c)\partial_{Y}\Psi-\Psi\partial_{Y}U_{s}+(i\alpha)^{-1}s_{1}\right].

(3.12)

Substituting (
3.12
) into
\eqref{3.1.1}_{3}
, we derive the following equation for
\Psi

which can be viewed as the Orr-Sommerfeld equation in the compressible setting:

\displaystyle\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Psi)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{i}{n}\Lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi)+(U_{s}-c)\Lambda(\Psi)-\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}U_{s})\Psi=\Omega(s_{1},s_{2}),~Y>0,

(3.13)

where the definition of operator
\Lambda
is given by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Lambda:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Psi)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}\Psi)-\alpha^{2}\Psi,

and
\Omega
is given in (
3.2
).

In order to solve (
3.13
), we consider the following boundary condition

\displaystyle\Psi|_{Y=0}=\Lambda(\Psi)|_{Y=0}=0.

(3.15)

If
\Psi
solves problem (
3.13
) with boundary conditions (
3.15
), it is straightforward to check that
(\rho,u,v)
defined by (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) is a solution to (
3.10
).

Thus, in the following, we consider
the boundary value problem

\displaystyle\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Psi)=h,~Y>0,~~\Psi|_{Y=0}=\Lambda(\Psi)|_{Y=0}=0,

(3.16)

with a given inhomogeneous source
h\in L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Let us first introduce the multiplier

\displaystyle\mathcal{w}(Y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}-\left(\partial_{Y}\left(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}U_{s}\right)\right)^{-1}.

(3.17)

A straightforward computation yields the properties of
w
stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
and
U_{s}
satisfy (
1.5
)-(
1.8
). There exists
\gamma_{3}>0
, such that if
|c|<\gamma_{3}
,
\mathcal{w}(Y)
has the expansion

\displaystyle\mathcal{w}=\mathcal{w}_{0}+c\mathcal{w}_{1}+O(1)|c|^{2}|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-1}.

(3.18)

Here
\mathcal{w}_{0}
and
\mathcal{w}_{1}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{w}_{0}=\frac{(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{2}}{H\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|\right)},~\mathcal{w}_{1}=\frac{4\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})}{H\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|\right)}-\frac{2\mathcal{m}^{2}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{2}\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}-U_{s}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\right)}{H^{2}\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|\right)^{2}},

(3.19)

where the function
H(Y)
is defined in (
1.7
).
Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{w}_{0}(Y)\approx|\mathcal{w}(Y)|\approx|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}(Y)|^{-1}.

(3.20)

Set the function space

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\left\{\Psi\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),~\Lambda(\Psi)|_{Y=0}=0~\bigg|~\|\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}<\infty\right\}.

For the problem (
3.16
), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4

(A priori estimates)
.

Let
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
and
\Psi\in\mathbb{X}
be a solution to (
3.16
). There exists
\gamma_{4}\in(0,\gamma_{3})
, such that for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
c
lies in

\displaystyle\Sigma_{Q}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid Imc>\min\{\gamma^{-1}_{4}|c|^{2},~\gamma_{4}^{-1}n^{-1}\},~|c|<\gamma_{4}\},

(3.21)

\Psi
has the following estimates

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\text{Im}c}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}},

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|\left(\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi),\alpha\Lambda(\Psi)\right)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

(3.23)

Proof.

Taking inner product of (
3.16
) with the multiplier

leads to

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{w}\Lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi)\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y}_{J_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}-(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{w}|\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}{\rm d}Y}_{J_{2}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}-\Psi\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y}_{J_{3}}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty}h\mathcal{w}\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y}_{J_{4}}=0.

(3.24)

Now we estimate
J_{1}-J_{4}
separately. Let us consider
J_{3}
first. By integrating by parts and using the boundary condition

we obtain

\displaystyle J_{3}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bar{A}^{-1}|\partial_{Y}\Psi|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Psi|^{2}{\rm d}Y.

(3.25)

Recalling (
2.3
) about the definition of

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{-1}=(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{-1}-2\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{-2}\bar{c}+O(1)|c|^{2}.

With this identity, the assumption

, we can

\displaystyle\text{Re}J_{3}\gtrsim\alpha^{2}\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1-O(1)|c|)\|\partial_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\text{Im}J_{3}\gtrsim\text{Im}c\|\mathcal{m}|U_{s}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-O(1)|c|^{2}\|\partial_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.27)

where the constants may depend on
\mathcal{m}
but not on either
\varepsilon
or
c
.

For
J_{2}
, we obtain from the expansion (
3.18
) and bound (
3.20
) that

\displaystyle-(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{w}

\displaystyle=-U_{s}\mathcal{w}_{0}+\left(\mathcal{w}_{0}-U_{s}\mathcal{w}_{1}\right)c+O(1)|c|^{2}|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-1}.

(3.28)

Using the explicit formula (
3.19
) of
\mathcal{w}_{0}
and
\mathcal{w}_{1}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{w}_{0}-U_{s}\mathcal{w}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})}{H^{2}\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|\right)}\left\{(1-5\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})H+\frac{2\mathcal{m}^{2}(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})(U_{s}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}-U_{s}^{2}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})}{|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|}\right\}

(3.29)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})}{H^{2}\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|\right)^{2}}\bigg\{(1-5\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})\left[(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|-2\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})}{H^{2}\left(|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}+|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|\right)^{2}}\bigg\{(1-3\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|+8\mathcal{m}^{4}U_{s}^{3}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}\bigg\}.

Since
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
, we have
(1-3\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})\geq(1-3\mathcal{m}^{2})>0.
Thus by (
1.9
) and (
3.29

\displaystyle C_{1}|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-1}\leq\mathcal{w}_{0}-U_{s}\mathcal{w}_{1}\leq C_{2}|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-1},

(3.30)

where the positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are uniform in
\varepsilon
and
c
. Therefore, taking real and imaginary part of
J_{2}
respectively and using the bounds (
3.20

\displaystyle\left|\text{Re}J_{2}\right|\lesssim C\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2},

(3.32)

For

, we rewrite

\displaystyle J_{1}=\frac{-i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{w}\Delta_{\alpha}\Lambda(\Psi)\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y+\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{w}[\Delta_{\alpha},\Lambda](\Psi)\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y:=J_{11}+J_{12},

\Delta_{\alpha}\left[\Lambda(\Psi)\right]-\Lambda\left[\Delta_{\alpha}(\Psi)\right]

\displaystyle J_{11}=\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{w}\left(|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}\right){\rm d}Y+\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{Y}\mathcal{w}\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y.

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{w}^{2}\partial_{Y}^{2}(A^{-1}\partial_{Y}U_{s})\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{w}\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{Y}U_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\||\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-1}|\partial_{Y}U_{s}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\||\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-1}|\partial_{Y}^{3}U_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\leq C.

Thus the last integral on the right hand side of (

\displaystyle\left|\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{Y}\mathcal{w}\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\overline{\Lambda(\Psi)}{\rm d}Y\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

(3.35)

By taking real and imaginary parts of
J_{11}
respectively and using (
3.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\text{Re}J_{11}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\text{Im}\mathcal{w}|\left(|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}\right){\rm d}Y+\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}},

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|c|}{n}\|(\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi),\alpha\Lambda(\Psi))\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}},

(3.36)

and

\displaystyle\text{Im}J_{11}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\mathcal{w}\left(|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\Lambda(\Psi)|^{2}\right){\rm d}Y-\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

Multiplying estimate (

), we can obtain by Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\|(\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi),\alpha\Lambda(\Psi))\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\text{Im}c\left(\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\left(\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|c|^{2}\left(\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{|c|}{n}\|(\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi),\alpha\Lambda(\Psi))\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left(\frac{1}{2n}+\frac{C|c|}{n}\right)\|(\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi),\alpha\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\text{Im}c\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{C}{n\text{Im}c}+\frac{C|c|^{2}}{\text{Im}c}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{C}{\text{Im}c}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}.

(3.48)

By taking
\gamma_{4}\in(0,\gamma_{3})
suitably small such that

C|c|\leq C\gamma_{4}\leq\frac{1}{4},~\text{and }\frac{C}{n\text{Im}c}+\frac{C|c|^{2}}{\text{Im}c}\leq 2C\gamma_{4}\leq\frac{1}{4},~\forall c\in\Sigma_{Q},

we can absorb the first and second terms on the right hand side of (
3.48
)
by the left hand side. Thus,

\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\text{Im}c}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}},~\text{and }\|(\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi),\alpha\Lambda(\Psi))\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

The above two inequalities immediately imply the estimates (
3.22
) and (
3.23
). The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

With the a priori estimates in Lemma
3.4
, we can prove the existence, uniqueness and analytic dependence on
c
of the solution to the compressible
Orr-Sommerfeld equation (
3.16
) in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5

(Construction of the solution)
.

Let
\mathcal{m}\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
c\in\Sigma_{Q}
. If
\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}<\infty
, there exists a unique solution
\Psi\in\mathbb{X}
to (
3.16
) which satisfies estimates (
3.22
) and (
3.23
). Moreover, if
h(\cdot~;c)
is analytic in
c
in
L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then
\Psi(\cdot~;c)
is analytic in
\mathbb{X}
.

Remark 3.6
.

By elliptic regularity, the solution
\Psi
is in
H^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Proof.

The proof is based on a cascade of approximate process and a continuity argument. First of all, we set
W\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\Lambda(\Psi)
and reformulate (
3.16
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{i}{n}\Lambda(\Delta_{\alpha}\Lambda^{-1}W)+(U_{s}-c)W+\mathcal{w}^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}W=h,~Y>0,\\
&W|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.49)

Here the inverse operator
\Lambda^{-1}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is constructed in Lemma
5.1
. If one can show the solvability of (
3.49
) in
H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then by Lemma
5.1
,
\Psi\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\Lambda^{-1}(W)\in\mathbb{X}
and it solves the equation (
3.16
). Now we elaborate the construction of solution to (
3.49
) in the following three steps.

Step 1.
Fix any parameter
l>0
. We start from an auxiliary problem

\displaystyle T_{l}(W)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{\alpha}W+(U_{s}-c-il)W+\mathcal{w}^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}W=h,~W|_{Y=0}=0.

(3.50)

We claim that there exists
l_{0}>0
, such that if
c\in\Sigma_{Q}
and
\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}<\infty
, then (
3.50
) admits a unique solution
W\in H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and the solution operator
T_{l_{0}}^{-1}:L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\rightarrow H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is analytic in
c
. To prove this claim, we define a sequence of approximate solutions
\{W_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
by the hierarchy of equations

\displaystyle\left[\text{Airy}-(c+il)\right](W_{k+1})=h-\mathcal{w}^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}W_{k},~W_{k+1}\big|_{Y=0}=0,~W_{0}\equiv 0,

(3.51)

where
\text{Airy}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{\alpha}+U_{s}:H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is the Airy operator. For any
c\in\Sigma_{Q}
and
l>0
, by direct energy method, it is straightforward to check that

c+il
lies in the resolvent set of Airy operator. Thus by an inductive argument, we can solve
W_{k}
and establish its analytic dependence on
c
from (
3.51
). In order to take the limit
k\rightarrow\infty,
we need some uniform estimates. Applying the multiplier
(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}\bar{W}_{k+1}
to (
3.51
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}\bar{W}_{k+1}\Delta_{\alpha}W_{k+1}{\rm d}Y+\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}(U_{s}-c-il)|W_{k+1}|^{2}{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}\mathcal{w}^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}(W_{k})\bar{W}_{k+1}{\rm d}Y+\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}h\bar{W}_{k+1}{\rm d}Y.

(3.52)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}h\bar{W}_{k+1}{\rm d}Y\right|\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

(3.53)

By using (
3.20
) and the bound in (
5.1
) for
\Lambda^{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}\mathcal{w}^{-1}\Lambda^{-1}(W_{k})\bar{W}_{k+1}{\rm d}Y\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\||\mathcal{w}|^{-1}|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\Lambda^{-1}(W_{k})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|(1+Y)W_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

(3.54)

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{-1}\bar{W}_{k+1}\Delta_{\alpha}W_{k+1}{\rm d}Y=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{n}\left(\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial_{Y}^{3}U_{s}}{(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{2}}\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\bar{W}_{k+1}{\rm d}Y.

(3.55)

By (
1.8
), the last integral in the above equality is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\frac{i}{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial_{Y}^{3}U_{s}}{(\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s})^{2}}\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\bar{W}_{k+1}{\rm d}Y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\left\|\frac{\partial_{Y}^{3}U_{s}}{\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

(3.56)

By taking the imaginary part of (
3.52
), and using the bounds obtained in (
3.53
)-(
3.1
) with Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}\right)+(\text{Im}c+l)\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\left(\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2n}\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\frac{\text{Im}c+l}{2}\left(1+\frac{C}{n(\text{Im}c+l)}\right)\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\text{Im}c+l}\left(\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\|W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}\right).

(3.57)

We choose
\gamma_{4}>0
smaller if needed so that
\frac{C}{n\text{Im}c}<\frac{1}{2}
, for any
c\in\Sigma_{Q}
. Then (
3.57
) gives

\displaystyle\|W_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{C}{\text{Im}c+l}\left(\|W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\right),~\|(\partial_{Y}W_{k+1},\alpha W_{k+1})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c+l)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\|W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\right).

Now we take the difference
W_{k+1}-W_{k}
. Similar argument gives

\|W_{k+1}-W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{C}{\text{Im}c+l}\|W_{k}-W_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}},~\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}-\partial_{Y}W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c+l)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|W_{k}-W_{k-1}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

By taking
l
suitably large, such that
\frac{C}{\text{Im}c+l}\leq\frac{C}{l}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
\{W_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. This implies the existence of a limit function
W=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}W_{k}
in
H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

that is the solution to (
3.50
). By the elliptic regularity,
W_{k}
converges to
W
in
H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).
Moreover, by induction, each
W_{k}
is analytic in
c
, so is
W
by uniform convergence. This justifies the claim and step 1 is completed.

Step 2.
(Bootstrap from
T_{l_{0}}^{-1}
to
T_{0}^{-1}
). Consider the equation (
3.50
) for any fix
l\in[0,l_{0}]
with
W^{l}
as its solution. Applying the multiplier
-\mathcal{w}\bar{W}^{l}
and using the same argument as in Lemma
3.4
, we can show that
W^{l}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|W^{l}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{C}{\text{Im}c}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}},~\|(\partial_{Y}W^{l},\alpha W^{l})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|h\|_{L^{2}_{w}},~\forall c\in\Sigma_{Q},

(3.58)

where the constant
C
is uniform in
l\in[0,l_{0}]
. Now we take
l_{1}=l_{0}-\lambda
for some fixed constant
0<\lambda<2C^{-1}\gamma_{4}^{-1}n^{-1}
and construct the solution
W^{l_{1}}=T_{l_{1}}^{-1}(h)
through the following iteration

W^{l_{1}}_{k+1}=T_{l_{0}}^{-1}\left(-i\lambda W_{k}^{l_{1}}+h\right),~W_{0}^{l_{1}}\equiv 0.

Applying the a priori estimate (
3.58
) to
W_{k+1}^{l_{1}}-W_{k}^{l_{1}}
yields that

\displaystyle\|W_{k+1}^{l_{1}}-W_{k}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\lambda}{\text{Im}c}\|W_{k}^{l_{1}}-W_{k-1}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{1}{2\gamma_{4}n\text{Im}c}\|W_{k}^{l_{1}}-W_{k-1}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|W_{k}^{l_{1}}-W_{k-1}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}^{l_{1}}-\partial_{Y}W_{k}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\lambda n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|W_{k}^{l_{1}}-W_{k-1}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq C\|W_{k}^{l_{1}}-W_{k-1}^{l_{1}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}},~\forall c\in\Sigma_{Q}.

Hence,
\{W_{k}^{l_{1}}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{1}_{w}
and it has a limit
W^{l_{1}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}W^{l_{1}}_{k}
. It is straightforward to check that
W^{l_{1}}
is in
H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and satisfies (
3.50
) with
l=l_{1}
. Moreover, from the previous step we have already shown that each
W_{k}^{l_{1}}
is analytic in
c
. Thus analyticity of
W^{l_{1}}
follows from the uniform convergence. Thus we have completed the construction solution operator
T^{-1}_{l_{1}}
. Noting that
W^{l_{1}}
satisfies the a priori estimate (
3.58
), we can take
l_{2}=l_{1}-\lambda
and construct the solution operator
T_{l_{2}}^{-1}
in the same way. Repeating the same procedure, we can eventually establish the existence and analytic dependence on
c
of the solution operator
T_{0}^{-1}
.

Step 3.
We now solve the original system
(
3.49
) by using the following iteration

\displaystyle T_{0}(W_{k+1})=h+\frac{i}{n}\left[\Delta_{\alpha},\Lambda\right](\Lambda^{-1}(W_{k})),~Y>0,~W_{k+1}|_{Y=0}=0,~W_{0}(Y)\equiv 0.

By using the bounds in (
3.41
) and (
5.1
)
on the commutator
[\Delta_{\alpha},\Lambda]
and
\Lambda^{-1}
respectively, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left[\Delta_{\alpha},\Lambda\right](\Lambda^{-1}(W_{k}))\right\|_{L^{2}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\partial_{Y}W_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|W_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Lambda^{-1}W_{k},\alpha\Lambda^{-1}W_{k})\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\partial_{Y}W_{k}\|_{L^{2}}+\|W_{k}(1+Y)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C\|W_{k}\|_{H^{1}_{w}}.

Then applying the a priori bound (
3.58
) to
W_{k+1}-W_{k}
gives

\|W_{k+1}-W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{C}{n\text{Im}c}\|W_{k}-W_{k-1}\|_{H^{1}_{w}},~\|\partial_{Y}W_{k+1}-\partial_{Y}W_{k}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|W_{k}-W_{k-1}\|_{H^{1}_{w}}.

By taking
\gamma_{4}>0
smaller if needed such that
\frac{C}{n\text{Im}c}+\frac{C}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{Im}c^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C\gamma_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}})<\frac{1}{2}
for
c\in\Sigma_{Q},
we show that
\{W_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Let
W:=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}W_{k}
. By the elliptic regularity and
H^{1}_{w}
-convergence, it is straightforward to check that
W_{k}
converges to
W
in
H^{2}_{w}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
W
is a solution to (
3.49
). Moreover, since each
W_{k}
is analytic in
c
and the convergence is uniform in
c\in\Sigma_{Q}
, we conclude that
W
is analytic in
c
. The uniqueness of solution follows from the a priori estimates obtained in Lemma
3.4
. Then the proof of the lemma is completed. ∎

Now let
\Psi
be the solution to (
3.16
) with

and

defined in (
3.2

Proposition 3.7

(Solvability of quasi-compressible system)
.

Under the same assumption on
\mathcal{m}
,
\alpha
and
c
as in Lemma
3.5
, if
\vec{s}=(s_{1},s_{2})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}

, there exists a solution
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{3}
to the quasi-compressible approximation system (

(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{u}\|_{H^{1}}+\|(\mathcal{m}^{-2}\varrho,\mathfrak{v})\|_{H^{2}}+\alpha^{-1}\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.59)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\mathfrak{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.60)

Furthermore, if both
\vec{s}(\cdot~;c)

in

and

(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})(\cdot~;c)
is analytic in
c
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Remark 3.8
.

If
\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})=0,
then by (
3.11
), (
3.67
) and regularity of
\Psi
it is easy to deduce that
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}_{+})^{3}
. This reveals the elliptic structure for linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equations around the subsonic boundary layer profile.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
satisfies (
3.10
). The analyticity directly follows from Lemma
3.4
. It remains to show the estimates (
3.59
) and (
3.60
). Firstly, by using bounds given in (
3.22
), (
3.23
) with
h=\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})
and (
3.39
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}},

(3.61)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{Y}\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda(\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{n^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}},

(3.62)

where we have used
n\text{Im}c\gtrsim 1
for
c\in\Sigma_{Q}
. Then by
\mathfrak{v}=-i\alpha\Psi
, (
3.61
) and

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Psi,\alpha\Psi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

(3.63)

Next we estimate
\rho
. Recall (
3.12
) for its representation. Since
\Psi|_{Y=0}=0,
we can use Hardy inequality
\|Y^{-1}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\partial_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}},
and the bounds given in (
3.61
), (
3.62
) to obtain

\displaystyle m^{-2}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n})\|\partial_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Y^{-1}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}\|Y\partial_{Y}U_{s}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\right)\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.64)

For
\partial_{Y}\varrho,
differentiating (
3.12
) yields that

\displaystyle-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\partial_{Y}\varrho

\displaystyle=\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Psi)+\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi+(U_{s}-c)\Psi\right)-\frac{i}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}s_{1})

\displaystyle=\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})+\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi+(U_{s}-c)\Psi\right)-\frac{i}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}(A^{-1}s_{1})

\displaystyle=s_{2}+\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{\alpha}\Psi+(U_{s}-c)\Psi\right),

(3.65)

where we have used the equation (
3.13
) in second identity. Taking
L^{2}
-norm in (
3.1
) and using bound (
3.61
), we can further deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\partial_{Y}\varrho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n}\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha(1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n})\|\alpha\Psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.66)

Now we estimate
\partial_{Y}^{2}\varrho.
By using (
3.12
) and (
3.1

\displaystyle-\mathcal{m}^{-2}\Delta_{\alpha}\varrho=\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})+\alpha^{2}(U_{s}-c)^{2}\varrho+2\alpha^{2}\Psi\partial_{Y}U_{s}.

(3.67)

Then taking
L^{2}
norm leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\varrho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}(1+\mathcal{m}^{-2})\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+\mathcal{m}^{-2}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha\|\Psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\text{div}_{\alpha}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.68)

Here we have used (
3.61
) and (
3.1
) in the last inequality. Therefore,
H^{2}

3.2.

Thus, the improved estimates (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) are proved. And this completes the
proof of the proposition. ∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Finally, in this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
We construct the solution to linearized system (
1.3
) with no-slip boundary condition (
1.4
) in the following form

\displaystyle\vec{\Xi}(Y;c)=\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}(Y;c)-\vec{\Xi}_{\text{sm}}(Y;c)-\vec{\Xi}_{\text{re}}(Y;c),

(4.1)

Here
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{app}}
is the approximate solution obtained in (
2.3
) which satisfies (
2.44
),
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{sm}}=(\rho_{\text{sm}},u_{\text{sm}},v_{\text{sm}})
and
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{re}}=(\rho_{\text{re}},u_{\text{re}},v_{\text{re}})
solve the remainder system

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi}_{\text{sm}})=(0,E_{u,\text{sm}},E_{v,\text{sm}}),~v_{\text{sm}}|_{Y=0}=0,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi}_{\text{re}})=(0,0,E_{v,\text{re}}),~v_{\text{re}}|_{Y=0}=0,

respectively. By Proposition
2.3
and
3.1
, both
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{sm}}
and
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{re}}
are well-defined. Moreover,
it is straightforward to check that
\vec{\Xi}=(\rho,u,v)
satisfies

\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi})=\vec{0},~v|_{Y=0}=0.

To recover the no-slip boundary condition on the tangential component, we introduce the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:D_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},~\mathcal{F}(c)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}u(0;c)=\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)-u_{\text{sm}}(0;c)-u_{\text{re}}(0;c).

On one hand, from Proposition
2.2
,
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
is analytic and has a unique zero point in
D_{0}
. On the other hand, according to Remark
3.2
(a), both
u_{\text{sm}}(0;c)
and
u_{\text{de}}(0;c)
are analytic in
D_{0}
. Then by applying estimates (
3.3
), (
3.5
) to
\vec{\Xi}_{\text{sm}}
with
(f_{u},f_{v})=(E_{u,\text{sm}},E_{v,\text{sm}})
, using the bound in (
2.49
) and the Sobolev inequality,
we deduce that

\displaystyle|u_{\text{sm}}(0;c)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{\text{sm}}(\cdot~;c)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}u_{\text{sm}}(\cdot~;c)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\alpha(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{5}{4}}}\|(E_{u,\text{sm}},E_{v,\text{sm}})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{Cn^{\frac{1}{4}}\varepsilon^{\frac{7}{16}}}{\alpha(\text{Im}c)^{\frac{5}{4}}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{16}},~\forall c\in D_{0}.

(4.2)

Here we have used (
2.43
) in the last inequality. For
u_{\text{re}}(0;c)
, we use the bounds given in (
3.7
) for
\|u_{\text{re}}\|_{H^{1}}
with
(f_{u},f_{v})=(0,E_{v,\text{re}})
and (
2.48
) to have

\displaystyle|u_{\text{re}}(0;c)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{\text{re}}(\cdot~;c)\|_{H^{1}}\leq\frac{C}{\text{Im}c}\|E_{v,\text{re}}\|_{L^{2}_{w}}+\|E_{v,\text{re}}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{\text{Im}c}\right)\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{16}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{16}}.

(4.3)

Thus, by recalling the lower bound of
|\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)|
on the circle
\partial D_{0}

in (
2.36
), and by using the bounds in (
4.2
) and (
4
), it holds that

\forall c\in\partial D_{0},~|\mathcal{F}(c)-\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)|\leq|u_{\text{re}}(0;c)|+|u_{\text{sm}}(0;c)|\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{16}}\leq\frac{1}{4}{K^{-\theta}}\leq\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)|,

by taking
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
suitably small. Therefore, by Rouché’s Theorem,
\mathcal{F}(c)
and
\mathcal{F}_{\text{app}}(c)
have the same number of zero points in
D_{0}
. This justifies the existence of
a unique
c\in D_{0}
such that
\vec{\Xi}(Y;c)
defined in (
4.1
) solves the linear equation (
1.3
) with the no-slip boundary condition (
1.4
). The proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.
∎

5.
Appendix

Recall (
3.14
) the definition of operator
\Lambda
.

Lemma 5.1

(The operator
\Lambda^{-1}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For small
|c|\ll 1
, if
\|h\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
, there exists a unique solution
\psi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to
\Lambda(\psi)=h
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\psi|_{Y=0}=0
which satisfies the following estimates

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\psi,\alpha\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\min\{\alpha^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{2}},\|(1+Y)h\|_{L^{2}}\}.

(5.1)

Moreover, the solution mapping
\Lambda^{-1}(\cdot~;c):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is analytic in
c
.

Proof.

We first establish the a priori estimate (
5.1
). Denote
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the standard inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Taking inner product with
-\bar{\psi}
in the equation
\Lambda(\psi)=h
gives that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}A^{-1}|\partial_{Y}\psi|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\psi|^{2}{\rm d}Y=\langle h,-\bar{\psi}\rangle.

(5.2)

Note that

\displaystyle A^{-1}=(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{-1}+O(1)|c|.

(5.3)

Then for suitably small
|c|
, the real part of (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C|\text{Re}\langle h,\bar{\psi}\rangle|\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\alpha^{-2}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which gives
H^{1}
-estimate of
\psi
. The estimate of
\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi
can be obtained by using
the equation to have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|A^{-1}\partial_{Y}A\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{Y}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\|A\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Ah\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\partial_{Y}\psi,\alpha\psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|h\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\alpha^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.4)

If in addition,
(1+Y)h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we can obtain
|\langle h,\bar{\psi}\rangle|\leq\|Y^{-1}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|Yh\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\partial_{Y}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|Yh\|_{L^{2}}
by using the Hardy inequality for the term
Y^{-1}\psi
. Thus real part of (
5.2
) yields the
H^{1}
-estimate

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\psi,\alpha\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|Yh\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.5)

The estimate on
\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi
then follows from the equation and
H^{1}
-estimate (
5.5
)
similar to the estimation in (
5.4
). Hence the estimate (
5.1
) holds. The uniqueness of solution follows from the a priori estimate (
5.1
).

Set

\Lambda_{0}:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),~\Lambda_{0}(\psi)=\partial_{Y}\left[(1-\mathcal{m}^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{-1}\partial_{Y}\psi\right]-\alpha^{2}\psi.

For
\mathcal{m}\in(0,1)
, the operator
\Lambda_{0}
is clearly uniformly elliptic and invertible in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then by (
5.3
), the existence and analytic dependence on
c
of
\Lambda^{-1}
follow from the standard argument. Hence, we omit the details for brevity. The proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

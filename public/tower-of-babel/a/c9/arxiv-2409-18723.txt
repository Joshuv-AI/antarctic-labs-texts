On the flows of linear vector fields
M. Jotz

Abstract
. This note provides a detailed proof of the fact that a linear vector field on a vector bundle has a flow by vector bundle isomorphisms. It then implies easily the existence of global solutions to linear non-autonomous ODE’s, with a standard time-dependent flow construction. As a further application, a simple proof of the smooth triviality of vector bundles over contractible bases is given. Finally, a detailed elementary proof of the isomorphy (as Lie algebras) of all fibers of the kernel of a transitive Lie algebroid is given.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 53C99.

1.
Introduction

This note shows in detail that the flow of a linear vector field on a vector bundle is a flow by isomorphisms of the vector bundle.
This fact is standard, see e.g.
[
9
, Proposition 3.4.2]
,
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
,
[
4
, Proposition 6.1]
,
but a precise statement like the following (see Theorem
3.1
below) and a detailed proof are not easy to find in the literature.

Theorem 1
.

Let
q\colon E\to M
be a smooth vector bundle and let

\chi\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E)
be a linear vector field
over a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Let
\phi\colon\Omega\to M
be the flow of
X
on its open flow domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times M
containing
\{0\}\times M
. Then the flow
\Phi
of
\chi

is defined on

\widetilde{\Omega}:=\left\{(t,e)\in\mathbb{R}\times E\mid(t,q(e))\in\Omega\right\}

and for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the map

\Phi_{t}\colon q^{-1}(M_{t})\to q^{-1}(M_{-t})

is an isomorphism of vector bundles over the diffeomorphism
\phi_{t}\colon M_{t}\to M_{-t}
, where

M_{t}\subseteq M
is the open subset

M_{t}:=\{x\in M\mid(t,x)\in\Omega\}.

This paper, which is extracted from lecture notes by the author, discusses this theorem and its proof for future reference, in particular because it is needed in
[
3
]
for the proof of the homotopy invariance of twisted Lie algebroid cohomology. While Theorem
1
can be deduced from the existence of global solutions to linear autonomous ordinary differential equations, this paper proves it only using standard results on flows of vector fields, and then deduces from it the existence of global solutions to linear autonomous ODE’s, with a linear time-dependent vector field construction.

The smooth triviality of vector bundles over contractible bases – a folklore fact, see e.g.
[
1
, Lemma 2.5]
,
[
2
, Corollary 15.10]
– follows in a straightforward manner from Theorem

1
, see Corollary
4.1
below and the discussion following it.

The linear flows of the duals of linear derivations and of tensor products of derivations are given as well. Finally, an elementary proof
of the isomorphy (as Lie algebras) of all fibers of the kernel of a transitive Lie algebroid is given (see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 6.5.1]
,
[
11
, Proposition 3.6]
), using the latter results on linear flows of tensor products.

Notation and conventions

All manifolds and vector bundles in this note are smooth and real.
Vector bundle projections are written
q_{E}\colon E\to M
if not stated otherwise, except for
p_{M}\colon TM\to M
, which stands for the projection of the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold
M
. Given a section

\varepsilon
of
E^{*}
, the map
\ell_{\varepsilon}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is
the linear function associated to it, i.e. the function defined by

e_{x}\mapsto\langle\varepsilon(x),e_{x}\rangle
for all
e_{x}\in E\arrowvert_{x}
,
x\in M
.
The set of global sections of a vector bundle
E\to M
is denoted by

\Gamma(E)
and
\mathfrak{X}(M)=\Gamma(TM)
is the space of smooth vector fields on a
smooth manifold
M
.

2.
Linear derivations versus linear vector fields on vector bundles

This section explains in detail the tangent prolongation of a vector bundle, defines linear vector fields on vector bundles and describes their equivalence with linear derivations.

2.1.
The tangent prolongation of a smooth vector bundle

Consider a vector bundle
q:=q_{E}\colon E\to M
with addition

. Then the tangent bundle of

is a vector bundle

The tangent map
T+\colon T(E\times_{M}E)\to TE
is a vector bundle homomorphism over

.

\begin{split}&T_{(e,e^{\prime})}+(v_{e}+_{E}w_{e},u_{e^{\prime}}+_{E}y_{e^{\prime}})=T_{(e,e^{\prime})}+\left((v_{e},u_{e^{\prime}})+_{E\times_{M}E}(w_{e},y_{e^{\prime}})\right)\\

and

.
Hence the two additions of the two vector bundle structures on

. All structure maps of one of the vector bundle structure are vector bundle morphisms with respect to the other one.

Consider the

. By definition, it is a vector subbundle of

of rank

. The intersection

is the space of vectors

. Hence it is the restriction of

M

]
.
Here, the core of

can be identified as follows with the vector bundle

.
The map

is a bijection and a linear map with respect to both additions on

since for

In fact, the whole vector bundle

is isomorphic as a vector bundle over
E
to the pullback

, via the morphism sending

(e,e^{\prime})\in q^{!}E\quad\text{ to }\quad\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}e+te^{\prime},

This isomorphism is easily seen in local coordinates, or in a local trivialisation of

.
As a consequence of the isomorphism above, each section

e^{\uparrow}(e^{\prime})=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}e^{\prime}+te_{q(e^{\prime})},

and since

is simply

\mathbb{R}\times E\to E,\qquad(t,e^{\prime})\mapsto e^{\prime}+te(q(e^{\prime})).

The flows of two such core vector fields commute, and so two core vector fields always have a vanishing Lie bracket.

2.2.
Linear vector fields and linear derivations

A vector field
\chi\colon E\to TE
is called
linear
if it is a morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{TE}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{TM}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Tq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

of vector bundles over a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
In particular, a linear vector field
\chi\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E)
over the zero vector field on
E
has by definition values in the vertical space
T^{q}E
. Hence there is a smooth map
F\colon E\to E
such that
q\circ F=q
and

\chi(e)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}e+tF(e)

for all
e\in E
.
The linearity of
\chi
then forces
F
to be a section of
\operatorname{End}(E)
:

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}e_{1}+e_{2}+tF(e_{1}+e_{2})=\chi(e_{1}+e_{2})=\chi(e_{1})+_{TM}\chi(e_{2})=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}e_{1}+e_{2}+tF(e_{1})+tF(e_{2})

shows that
F(e_{1}+e_{2})=F(e_{1})+F(e_{2})
for
e_{1},e_{2}
in the same fiber of
E
, etc. Hence linear vector fields with values in
T^{q}E
, also called
core-linear vector fields
, are equivalent to sections of
\operatorname{End}(E)
, via the correspondence sending

&=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\arrowvert_{s=0}f(x)=0=\left(\left.\left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\Phi_{t}^{\star}e\right)^{\uparrow}\right\arrowvert_{e^{\prime}}\right)(q^{*}f).\end{split}

Take
\epsilon\in\Gamma(E^{*})
. Then

\begin{split}\left((De)^{\uparrow}\arrowvert_{e^{\prime}}\right)(\ell_{\epsilon})&=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\arrowvert_{s=0}\ell_{\epsilon}\left(e^{\prime}+s\Phi_{-t}(e(\phi_{t}(x)))\right)\\
&=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\arrowvert_{s=0}\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\left(\left\langle\epsilon(x),e^{\prime}\rangle+s\langle\epsilon(x),\Phi_{-t}(e(\phi_{t}(x)))\right\rangle\right)\\
&=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\right\arrowvert_{s=0}s\left\langle\epsilon(x),\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\Phi_{-t}(e(\phi_{t}(x)))\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\epsilon(x),\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\Phi_{-t}(e(\phi_{t}(x)))\right\rangle=\left(\left.\left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\arrowvert_{t=0}\Phi_{t}^{\star}e\right)^{\uparrow}\right\arrowvert_{e^{\prime}}\right)(\ell_{\epsilon}).\qed\end{split}

4.
Applications

This section collects a few further useful applications of Theorem
3.1
.

4.1.
Vector bundles over contractible manifolds are trivial

Theorem
3.1
implies the following corollary, which is standard, but the proof of which is not easy to find in the smooth case in the literature.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold and let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval. Choose
t_{0}\in I
and set
\iota\colon M\to I\times M
to be the smooth inclusion
x\mapsto(t_{0},x)
. Then
each vector bundle
F\to I\times M
is isomorphic to
I\times\iota^{!}F\to I\times M
, via an isomorphism over the identity on
I\times M
.

This implies immediately that
a smooth vector bundle over a contractible manifold is trivial
: consider a contractible smooth manifold
M
and a vector bundle
E\to M
. Take a smooth map
\varphi\colon I\times M\to M
, with
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
an open interval containing
[0,1]
and such that
\varphi_{0}\equiv x_{0}
for some
x_{0}\in M
and
\varphi_{1}=\id_{M}
. Let
\iota_{j}\colon M\to I\times M
be the smooth inclusion
x\mapsto(j,x)
for
j=0,1
. Then
\varphi^{!}E
is a smooth vector bundle over
I\times M
. Hence by Corollary
4.1

\varphi^{!}E\simeq I\times\iota_{0}^{!}(\varphi^{!}E)=I\times\varphi_{0}^{!}E=I\times M\times E_{x_{0}}
over the identity on
I\times M
. As a consequence,
E\simeq\id_{M}^{!}E=\varphi_{1}^{!}E=\iota_{1}^{!}\varphi^{!}E=\iota_{1}^{!}(I\times M\times E_{x_{0}})\simeq M\times E_{x_{0}}
over the identity on
M
.

Proof of Corollary
4.1
.

Consider any linear vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(F)
over the vector field
\partial_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}(I\times M)
. Such a linear vector field exists since any vector field on
I\times M
can be lifted to a linear vector field on
F
, see (
1
).
The flow of
\partial_{t}
is given by

\phi\colon\Omega=\{(t,s,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times I\times M\mid t+s\in I\}\to I\times M,\quad(t,s,x)\mapsto(t+s,x),

and so by Theorem
3.1
the flow of
X
is a map

\Phi\colon\widetilde{\Omega}=\{(t,e_{(s,x)})\in\mathbb{R}\times F\mid t+s\in I\}\to F

such that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F\arrowvert_{((I-t)\cap I)\times M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F\arrowvert_{(I\cap(I+t))\times M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{((I-t)\cap I)\times M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(I\cap(I+t))\times M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{t}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{t}}

is an isomorphism of vector bundles.
The map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{I\times\iota^{!}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{I\times M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{I\times M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id_{I\times M}}

defined
by

\Theta(e_{(t,x)})=\left(t,\Phi(t_{0}-t,e_{(t,x)})\right)

is well-defined, smooth by definition, and covers the identity on
I\times M
. It is a diffeomorphism since it has the smooth inverse

\Theta^{-1}\colon I\times\iota^{!}F\to F,\qquad\Theta^{-1}(t,e_{(t_{0},x)})=\Phi(t-t_{0},e_{(t_{0},x)}).

It remains to show that
\Theta
is fibrewise linear. But this follows immediately from the fact that
\Phi_{t}
is a vector bundle isomorphism for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

4.2.
Flows of derivations on tensor products of vector bundles

Let
E\to M
and
F\to M
be two vector bundles over a same base manifold
M
.
Two linear derivations
D_{E}\colon\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
and
D_{F}\colon\Gamma(F)\to\Gamma(F)
with a same symbol
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
define together a linear derivation
D
on
E\otimes F
via

D(e\otimes f)=(D_{E}e)\otimes f+e\otimes(D_{F}f)

for all
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in\Gamma(F)
. This can be extended to tensor products of arbitrary many vector bundles, for instance to powers of
E^{\otimes k}\otimes(E^{*})^{\otimes l}
for
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
.

This section computes the flow
\Phi^{E^{*}}
of
\widehat{D_{E}^{*}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E^{*})
, where
D_{E}^{*}
is the dual linear derivation to
D_{E}
on
E^{*}
, as well as the flow
\Phi
of
\widehat{D}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E\otimes F)
.
Let
\phi\colon\Omega\to M
be the flow of
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and let
\Phi^{E}\colon\widetilde{\Omega}^{E}\to E
be the flow of
\widehat{D_{E}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E)
. Let
\Phi^{F}\colon\widetilde{\Omega}^{F}\to F
be the flow of
\widehat{D_{F}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(F)
, and let
q_{*}\colon E^{*}\to M
be the vector bundle projection of
E^{*}
.

First choose
x\in M
and
\epsilon\in E^{*}\arrowvert_{x}
. Consider the curve

\gamma\colon I_{x}\to E^{*},\qquad t\mapsto\Phi_{-t}^{*}(\epsilon)=\epsilon\circ\Phi_{-t}\in E^{*}\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},

with
I_{x}:=\Omega\cap(\mathbb{R}\times\{x\})
, the domain of definition of the maximal integral curve of
\phi
through
x
at time
0
.
Then for
e\in\Gamma(E)
(for simplicity a global section) and any
t\in I_{x}

\begin{split}\dot{\gamma}(t)(\ell_{e})&=\frac{d}{dt}\langle\gamma(t),e\arrowvert_{q_{*}(\gamma(t))}\rangle=\frac{d}{dt}\langle\epsilon,\Phi_{-t}(e\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)})\rangle\\
&=\frac{d}{dt}\langle\epsilon,(\Phi_{t}^{\star}e)\arrowvert_{x}\rangle=\langle\epsilon,(\Phi_{t}^{\star}(D_{E}e))\arrowvert_{x}\rangle\\
&=\langle\gamma(t),D_{E}e\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}\rangle=\ell_{D_{E}e}(\gamma(t))=\widehat{D_{E}^{*}}\arrowvert_{\gamma(t)}(\ell_{e})\end{split}

by Corollary
3.3
.
This shows that
\gamma
is an integral curve of
\widehat{D_{E}^{*}}
through
\epsilon
at time
0
. Since the flow domain of
\widehat{D_{E}^{*}}
is

\widetilde{\Omega}^{E^{*}}=\left\{(t,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{R}\times E^{*}\mid(t,q_{*}(\epsilon))\in\Omega\right\},

the curve
\gamma
must be the maximal integral curve of
\widehat{D_{E}^{*}}
through
\epsilon
at time
0
.
The flow
\Phi^{E^{*}}
of
\widehat{D_{E}^{*}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E^{*})
is hence defined by

(6)

\Phi^{E^{*}}\colon\{(t,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{R}\times E^{*}\mid(t,q_{E^{*}}(\epsilon))\in\Omega\}\to E^{*},\qquad(t,\epsilon)\mapsto\epsilon\circ\Phi^{E}_{-t}.

Given
\epsilon\in\Gamma(E^{*})
, the induced curve of sections of
E^{*}
through
\epsilon
at time
0
is then

(\Phi^{E^{*}})_{t}^{\star}\epsilon\in\Gamma_{M_{t}}(E^{*})

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

((\Phi^{E^{*}})_{t}^{\star}\epsilon)_{x}=\Phi^{E^{*}}_{-t}(\epsilon(\phi_{t}(x)))=\epsilon\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}\circ\Phi^{E}_{t}\in E^{*}\arrowvert_{x}

for all
x\in M_{t}
.

Next, since the flow of
\widehat{D}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E\otimes F)
is linear, it is enough to determine it on a basis of
(E\otimes F)_{x}
at each
x\in M
.
Hence it is enough to determine it on elementary tensors.
Choose
x\in M
,
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in\Gamma(F)
global sections. Consider the curve

\gamma\colon I_{x}\to E\otimes F,\qquad t\mapsto\Phi^{E}_{t}(e\arrowvert_{x})\otimes\Phi^{F}_{t}(f\arrowvert_{x})\in(E\otimes F)\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}.

Choose further a (global) section
\epsilon
of
E^{*}
and a (global) section
\kappa
of
F^{*}
and compute

\begin{split}\dot{\gamma}(t)(\ell_{\epsilon\otimes\kappa})&=\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle\epsilon\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{E}_{t}(e\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\kappa\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{F}_{t}(f\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{d}{dt}\left\langle((\Phi^{E^{*}})^{\star}_{t}\epsilon)\arrowvert_{x},e\arrowvert_{x}\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle((\Phi^{F^{*}})^{\star}_{t}\kappa)\arrowvert_{x},f\arrowvert_{x}\right\rangle\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq_d_flow}}{=}\left\langle((\Phi^{E^{*}})^{\star}_{t}(D_{E}^{*}\epsilon))\arrowvert_{x},e\arrowvert_{x}\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle((\Phi^{F^{*}})^{\star}_{t}\kappa)\arrowvert_{x},f\arrowvert_{x}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\left\langle\epsilon\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{E}_{t}(e\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle((\Phi^{F^{*}})^{\star}_{t}(D_{F}^{*}\kappa))\arrowvert_{x},f\arrowvert_{x}\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle(D_{E}^{*}\epsilon)\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{E}_{t}(e\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\kappa\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{F}_{t}(f\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\left\langle\epsilon\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{E}_{t}(e\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle(D_{F}^{*}\kappa)\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)},\,\Phi^{F}_{t}(f\arrowvert_{x})\right\rangle\\
&=\ell_{(D_{E}^{*}\epsilon)\otimes\kappa+\epsilon\otimes(D_{F}^{*}\kappa)}(\gamma(t))=\ell_{D^{*}(\epsilon\otimes\kappa)}(\gamma(t))=\widehat{D}\arrowvert_{\gamma(t)}(\ell_{\epsilon\otimes\kappa})\end{split}

for all
t\in I_{x}
.
Also
\dot{\gamma}(t)(q_{E\otimes F}^{*}g)=X\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}(g)=\widehat{D}\arrowvert_{\gamma(t)}(q_{E\otimes F}^{*}g)

for
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
Hence

\dot{\gamma}(t)=\widehat{D}\arrowvert_{\gamma(t)}

for all
t\in I_{x}
and
the flow of
\widehat{D}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(E\otimes F)
is pointwise the tensor product of the flows of
\widehat{D_{E}}
and
\widehat{D_{F}}
:

(7)

\begin{split}\Phi\colon\{(t,\chi)\in\mathbb{R}\times E\otimes F\mid(t,q_{E\otimes F}(\chi))\in\Omega\}&\to E\otimes F,\\
(t,e\otimes f)&\mapsto(\Phi_{t}^{E}e)\otimes(\Phi_{t}^{F}f)\end{split}

for all elementary tensors
e\otimes f\in E\otimes F
.
Given
e\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in\Gamma(F)
the
induced curve of sections of
E\otimes F
is then

\Phi_{t}^{\star}(e\otimes f)\in\Gamma_{M_{t}}(E\otimes F)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(\Phi_{t}^{\star}(e\otimes f))_{x}=(\Phi_{t}^{\star}e)\arrowvert_{x}\otimes(\Phi_{t}^{\star}f)\arrowvert_{x}

for all
x\in M_{t}
.

4.3.
The kernel of a transitive Lie algebroid

This section now provides an elementary proof of the fact that the kernel of a transitive Lie algebroid (see
[
9
]
and references therein) is a Lie algebra bundle
4
4

4

Note that a bundle of Lie algebras is a Lie algebroid with trivial anchor. Its fibers are then all (isotropy) Lie algebras, which may depend on their base point. A Lie algebra bundle is a Lie algebroid with trivial anchor and isomorphic isotropy Lie algebras.
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A\to M
be a transitive Lie algebroid.

(1)

The kernel

K=\ker(\rho\colon A\to TM)

is a subalgebroid of
A
. Since the anchor vanishes on
K
, it is a bundle of Lie algebras.

(2)

\nabla\colon\Gamma(A)\times\Gamma(K)\to\Gamma(K)

defined by

\nabla_{a}k=[a,k]

for all
a\in\Gamma(A)
and all
k\in\Gamma(K)
is a flat
A
-connection on
K
.

(3)

For all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\Gamma(K)
and
a\in\Gamma(A)

\nabla_{a}[k_{1},k_{2}]=[\nabla_{a}k_{1},k_{2}]+[k_{1},\nabla_{a}k_{2}].

Proof.

The proof is a simple computation which is left to the reader.
∎

The following theorem shows that the kernel
K
of a transitive Lie algebroid
A\to M
is not only a bundle of Lie algebras, but a Lie algebra bundle. This fact is standard, and proofs of it can be found e.g. in
[
9
]
and
[
11
]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A\to M
be a transitive Lie algebroid with anchor
\rho
and assume that
M
is connected. Then the kernel
K
of
\rho
is a Lie algebra bundle.

Proof.

Since the Lie bracket on
K
is defined pointwise, it defines a tensor
B\in\Gamma(K^{*}\otimes K^{*}\otimes K)
,

B(k_{1},k_{2})=[k_{1},k_{2}]

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\Gamma(K)
. The vector bundle
K^{*}\otimes K^{*}\otimes K
is equipped with the
A
-connection inherited from the one on
K
that is found in (2) of Proposition
4.2
:
for
\psi\in\Gamma(K^{*}\otimes K^{*}\otimes K)
and
a\in\Gamma(A)
the tensor
\nabla_{a}\psi
is defined by

\begin{split}(\nabla_{a}\psi)(k_{1},k_{2})&=\nabla_{a}(\psi(k_{1},k_{2}))-\psi(\nabla_{a}k_{1},k_{2})-\psi(k_{1},\nabla_{a}k_{2})\\
&=[a,\psi(k_{1},k_{2})]-\psi[[a,k_{1}],k_{2}]-\psi[k_{1},[a,k_{2}]]\end{split}

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in\Gamma(K)
.
By (3) in Proposition
4.2
,
\nabla_{a}B=0
for all
a\in\Gamma(A)
, i.e.
B
is flat. But by Corollary
3.3
,
\nabla_{a}B=0
just means that the flow
\Phi
of
\widehat{\nabla_{a}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(K^{*}\otimes K^{*}\otimes K)
does

\Phi_{t}^{\star}B=B

on
M_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
By the considerations in Section
4.2
, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
the section
\Phi_{t}^{\star}B
of
K^{*}\otimes K^{*}\otimes K
over
M_{t}
is defined by

(\Phi_{t}^{\star}B)\arrowvert_{x}(k_{1},k_{2})=(\Phi_{-t}(B\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}))(k_{1},k_{2})=\Psi_{-t}(B\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}(\Psi_{t}k_{1},\Psi_{t}k_{2}))

for
x\in M_{t}
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in K\arrowvert_{x}
, where the map
\Psi
on the right-hand side is the flow of
\widehat{\nabla_{a}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}(K)
. Since this is
B\arrowvert_{x}(k_{1},k_{2})
,

[\Psi_{t}k_{1},\Psi_{t}k_{2}]=B\arrowvert_{\phi_{t}(x)}(\Psi_{t}k_{1},\Psi_{t}k_{2})=\Psi_{t}(B\arrowvert_{x}(k_{1},k_{2}))=\Psi_{t}[k_{1},k_{2}]

for all
x\in M_{t}
and all
k_{1},k_{2}\in K\arrowvert_{x}
.

Let
x\in M
and choose a smooth chart
\varphi\colon U^{\varphi}\to\varphi(U^{\varphi})
of
M
centered at
x
and such that
\varphi(U^{\varphi})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a cube centered at
0=\varphi(x)
. Then the smooth vector fields
\partial_{i}^{\varphi}\in\mathfrak{X}(U^{\varphi})
all commute for
i=1,\ldots,m
, where
m
is the dimension of
M
. Since
\rho
is transitive there exist sections
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
of
A
over
U^{\varphi}
, such that
\rho\circ a_{i}=\partial_{i}^{\varphi}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Consider the linear vector fields

\widehat{\nabla_{a_{i}}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{l}\left(q_{K}^{-1}U^{\varphi}\right),\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.

Each
y\in U^{\varphi}
can be written

y=\varphi^{-1}(y_{1}e_{1}+\ldots+y_{m}e_{m})

where
y_{i}=\varphi_{i}(y)
for
i=1,\ldots,m
and
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{m})
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
That is,

y=\phi^{\partial_{m}^{\varphi}}_{y_{m}}\circ\ldots\circ\phi^{\partial_{1}^{\varphi}}_{y_{1}}(x),

where for
i=1,\ldots,m
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
where defined, the map
\phi^{\partial_{i}^{\varphi}}_{t}\colon U^{\varphi}\to U^{\varphi}
is the flow

y\mapsto\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(y)+te_{i})

of the vector field
\partial_{i}^{\varphi}
.
The fiber of
K
over
y
is then isomorphic to
K_{x}
via the composition of flows

\Phi^{\widehat{\nabla_{a_{m}}}}_{y_{m}}\circ\ldots\circ\Phi^{\widehat{\nabla_{a_{1}}}}_{y_{1}}\colon K_{x}\to K_{y}.

The map

\Theta\colon q_{K}^{-1}(U^{\varphi})\to U^{\varphi}\times K_{x},\qquad k_{y}\mapsto\left(y,\phi^{\partial_{1}^{\varphi}}_{-y_{1}}\circ\ldots\circ\phi^{\partial_{m}^{\varphi}}_{-y_{m}}(k_{y})\right)

for
y\in U^{\varphi}
with coordinates
\varphi^{1}(y)=y_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{m}(y)=y_{m}
, is easily seen to be a diffeomorphism
that restricts to an isomorphism of Lie algebras in each fiber. This completes the proof of the fact that
K
is a Lie algebra bundle.
∎

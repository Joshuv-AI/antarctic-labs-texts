Stout smearing and Wilson flow in lattice perturbation theory
Maximilian Ammer, Stephan Dürr

Abstract
We present the expansion of stout smearing and the Wilson flow in lattice perturbation theory to order g 0 3 g_{0}^{3} , which is suitable for one-loop calculations. As the Wilson flow is generated by infinitesimal stout smearing steps, the results are related to each other by taking the appropriate limits. We show how to apply perturbative stout smearing or Wilson flow to the Feynman rules of any lattice fermion action and illustrate them by calculating the self-energy of the clover-improved Wilson fermion.

Introduction

Link smearing is a standard recipe for reducing UV fluctuations in lattice gauge theory calculations, e.g. lattice QCD.
It is particularly useful when applied to the covariant derivative in a lattice fermion action.
Long ago it was noticed that the taste breaking of staggered fermions gets reduced by link
smearing
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, and similarly the additive mass shift
(taken as a measure for the severity of chiral symmetry breaking) of Wilson/clover fermions
gets reduced by link smearing
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

In recent years the “stout smearing” procedure, proposed in Ref.
[
9
]
, has become very popular,
since it yields a smeared link
U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
which depends in a smooth (differentiable) manner on the unsmeared gauge
link
U_{\mu}(x)
and its neighbors forming the “staple” around it.
This allows one to use smeared fermion actions in the HMC/RHMC algorithm
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
,
which crucially relies on the differentiability with respect to the gauge field.

A related recipe is known as “gradient flow” or “Wilson flow”
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
The flow time
t
(with the dimension of a length squared) parametrizes the smooth deformation from the unsmeared
U_{\mu}(x)
to the
smeared/flowed
U_{\mu}(x,t)
, and the recipe was originally formulated as a differential equation (of the diffusion type) in
t
.
In fact, a sequence of stout smearings whose cumulative smearing parameter matches the flow time is the simplest possible integration
scheme (“forward Euler”) to implement the gradient flow – see the discussion around Eq. (
205
) below for details.

The main difference, in contemporary use, between “stout smearing” and “gradient/Wilson flow” concerns what is kept fixed
in the continuum limit (i.e. if the lattice spacing is sent to zero,
a\to 0
, while the box size
L
and the renormalized
quark masses
m_{q}
, all in physical units, remain constant).
With “stout smearing” it is common practice to keep both the smearing parameter
{\varrho}
and the number of smearings
n_{\mathrm{stout}}
fixed.
With “gradient/Wilson flow” it is common practice to keep the flow time
t
(in physical units) constant as the continuum limit is taken,
whereupon the flow time in lattice units
t/a^{2}
(dimensionless) grows in proportion to the cutoff squared.

The rationale behind the former choice is that it results in an ultralocal modification of the action.
Accordingly, a study with, say, seven stout smearings is guaranteed to yield the same continuum limit as with an unsmeared action
(see e.g. Ref.
[
16
]
for an example of this philosophy).
The rationale behind the latter choice is that
t^{-1/2}
introduces a second regulator, independent of the cutoff
a^{-1}
,
which persists (and stays finite) in the continuum limit.
One ends up, with a flow-scale regulated theory (or a perfectly legitimate renormalization scheme and scale), which, contrary to the lattice
regulation, shows universal features
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.
Hence, in the future, one might calculate a light quark mass or
\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}
with staggered fermions at the fixed flow scale
t^{-1/2}=3\,\mathrm{GeV}
.
Upon taking the continuum limit, all memory of the gluon/fermion action combination, which has been used in the computation, is lost, and one ends up with
a genuine physics result in the universal flow scheme.
Hence, no conversion to the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme is needed any more,
the advantage being that the renormalization scheme employed is well defined beyond perturbation theory.
There are encouraging signs that the continuum community is getting aware of the advantages of the flow scheme and provides the necessary
computational tools
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.

So far, we discussed the smoothing of gauge fields.
A second vital ingredient in the field-theoretic setup of many lattice QCD computations is the concept of Symanzik improvement
[
31
]
.
Specifically for Wilson fermions this program offers the opportunity of relegating the leading cutoff effects from
O(a)
to
O(\alpha_{\mathrm{s}}^{k}a)
or
O(a^{2})
,
provided the coefficient
c_{\mathrm{SW}}
in front of the Sheikholeslami-Wohlert term is properly chosen
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
.
This improvement program may be carried out either perturbatively or nonperturbatively, but even in the latter case the perturbative results are vital
for cross-checks and to stabilize the nonperturbative fits
[
34
]
.

In order to combine the merits of link smoothing and Symanzik improvement, a valid framework for carrying out perturbative calculations with smoothed actions is mandatory.
Early steps in this direction have been taken in Refs.
[
5
,
35
,
36
,
8
]
.
More recently, the mutual benefits which come from combining link smearing with clover improvement for undoubled fermion actions
have been emphasized in Ref.
[
37
]
.
What is still missing is a universal framework that allows for an easy derivation of Feynman rules for fermion actions with arbitrary smearing recipes,
i.e. an arbitrary
(\varrho,n_{\mathrm{stout}})
combination or an arbitrary flow time
t/a^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.
In the present paper we try to fill this gap.
In particular we try to convince the reader that a “brute force” approach for deriving the Feynman rules (i.e. inserting the perturbative expansion
of the smeared/flowed variable into the underlying action) is not recommendable for
n_{\mathrm{stout}}>1
.
We advocate a more elegant method, based on an
SU(N_{c})
expansion, which keeps intermediate expressions much shorter.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows.
In Sec. 2 we work out the perturbative expansion of stout smearing, and in Sec. 3 we do the same thing for the gradient/Wilson flow.
As a by-product the perturbative matching between these recipes is discussed, see Eq. (
205
).
In Sec. 4 we discuss the procedure which yields, for any smoothing recipe, the Feynman rules in a manageable form.
As an application of this procedure we study in Sec. 5 the self-energy of a stout smeared clover fermion with variable
\varrho
and
1\leq n_{\mathrm{stout}}\leq 4

or under the gradient flow.
Finally, Section 6 gives a short summary and an outlook.
Some preliminary work on this topic is found in Ref.
[
38
]
.

2
Perturbative expansion of stout smearing

2.1
Definition of stout smearing

Stout smearing is a unitary transformation of the link variable
U_{\mu}(x)
, which depends on the plaquettes containing
U_{\mu}(x)
. Thereby the configuration of link variables is smoothed. Often multiple smearing steps are performed to increase the smoothing effect. We write
U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
for the link variable after
n
smearing steps. The transformation to obtain
U^{(n+1)}_{\mu}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle U^{(n+1)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\exp\big\{i\varrho Q^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)\big\}U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)

(1)

\displaystyle U^{(0)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=U_{\mu}(x),

(2)

with the smearing parameter
\varrho
and the Hermitian operator
Q^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
, which is constructed from link variables of the previous smearing step according to

\displaystyle Q^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\left(W^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[W^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)\right]\right)

(3)

\displaystyle W^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=S^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)U^{(n)\dagger}_{\mu}(x)-U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)S^{(n)\dagger}_{\mu}(x)

(4)

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu\neq\mu}\left(U^{(n)}_{\nu}(x)U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x+\hat{\nu})U^{(n)\dagger}_{\nu}(x+\hat{\mu})+U^{(n)\dagger}_{\nu}(x-\hat{\nu})U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x-\hat{\nu})U^{(n)}_{\nu}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})\right).

(5)

The superscript
(n)
denotes quantities that are constructed from link variables after
n
smearing steps. The quantity
S^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
is referred to as the sum of staples around the link
U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
and
iQ^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)
is constructed as the projection of the product
S^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)U^{(n)\dagger}_{\mu}(x)
onto the algebra
\mathfrak{su}(N_{c})
.

2.2
Stout smearing in perturbation theory

In lattice perturbation theory the group element
U_{\mu}(x)\in SU(N_{c})
is written as the exponential of a linear combination of generators
T^{a}
and gluon fields
A_{\mu}^{a}(x)

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)},

(6)

where the summation over repeated adjoint color indices
a,b,c,\ldots\in\{1,\ldots,N_{c}^{2}-1\}
is implied.
Expanding the action to the desired order in the bare coupling
g_{0}
then gives the Feynman rules. For one-loop calculations we need to expand up to third order in
g_{0}

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}T^{a}T^{b}A_{\mu}^{a}(x)A_{\mu}^{b}(x)-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4}).

(7)

The Fourier transform of the gluon fields is chosen to be

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\mu}(x)=\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i(x+\hat{\mu}/2)k}A^{a}_{\mu}(k)\;.

(8)

The shift
x+\hat{\mu}/2
is needed to eliminate overall phase factors in our results.
In order for
U_{\mu}(x)
to be unitary, the exponent
ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)
has to be anti-Hermitian.
This means the gluon fields
A^{a}_{\mu}(x)
are real (because the generators are Hermitian).
This implies that the momentum space field
A_{\mu}^{a}(k)
fulfils

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\mu}(k)=A^{a}_{\mu}(-k)\,.

(9)

As we are going to be working in momentum space, it is helpful to keep in mind that taking the Hermitian conjugate in position space involves the transformation
k\to-k
in momentum space.

For a link variable after stout smearing things are more complicated.
As the smeared link variable
U_{\mu}^{(n)}(x)
is again a group element of
SU(N_{c})

[
9
]
we expect it to have an exponential representation similar to the unsmeared one

\displaystyle U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=e^{ig_{0}T^{a}\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)}

(10)

\displaystyle=1+ig_{0}T^{a}\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}T^{a}T^{b}\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{(n)a}(g_{0},x)\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{(n)b}(g_{0},x)

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)\tilde{A}^{(n)b}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)\tilde{A}^{(n)c}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)+\mathcal{O}(T^{4}).

(11)

This expansion in terms of generators
T
does, however, no longer coincide with the expansion in the bare coupling
g_{0}
. The new fields
\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)
are themselves functions of
g_{0}
.
By expanding

\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)

in powers of
g_{0}
we will be able to determine its coefficients by comparison with the perturbative expansion of the right-hand side of Eq. (
1
).
This also means that, as we will see below, we will be able to infer the general structure of the coefficients of
\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)
from the perturbative expansion of only the first smearing step

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\exp\big\{i\varrho Q^{(0)}_{\mu}(x)\big\}U^{(0)}_{\mu}(x),

(12)

which in turn allows us to use the following strategy: Expand Eq. (
12
) to the desired order in
g_{0}
and replace
A^{(0)}_{\mu}(x)
by the expansion of

\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)
. This will generate new contributions to higher orders in
g_{0}
and give the results for a general smearing step
n+1>1
.

We start by defining expansions of
S_{\mu}(x)
and
Q_{\mu}(x)
in
g_{0}
(we drop the superscript
(0)
for now):

\displaystyle S_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=6+ig_{0}T^{a}S^{a}_{1\mu}(x)-g_{0}^{2}T^{a}T^{b}S^{ab}_{2\mu}(x)-ig_{0}^{3}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}S^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4})

(13)

\displaystyle Q_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{0}T^{a}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)+ig_{0}^{2}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}-T^{b}T^{a}\Big)Q^{ab}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle+g_{0}^{3}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\Big)Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4})\;.

(14)

Using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula

\displaystyle e^{X}e^{Y}=e^{X+Y+\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]+\frac{1}{12}([X,[X,Y]]+[Y,[Y,X]])+\ldots}\;,

(15)

we therefore get

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\big\{i\varrho Q_{\mu}(x)\big\}\exp\big\{ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)\big\}=\exp\bigg\{ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)+i\varrho g_{0}T^{a}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)-\varrho g_{0}^{2}[T^{a},T^{b}]Q^{[ab]}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle+i\varrho g_{0}^{3}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\Big)Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\varrho g_{0}^{2}[T^{a},T^{b}]Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)-i\varrho g_{0}^{3}[[T^{a},T^{b}],T^{c}]Q^{[ab]}_{2\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12}\Big(-i\varrho^{2}g_{0}^{3}[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)-i\varrho g_{0}^{3}[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)Q^{c}_{1\mu}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4})\bigg\}

(16)

\displaystyle=1+ig_{0}T^{a}\big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)\big)-\frac{1}{2}g_{0}^{2}T^{a}T^{b}\big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)\big)\big(A^{b}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)\big)

\displaystyle-\varrho g_{0}^{2}[T^{a},T^{b}]\Big(\frac{1}{2}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)A_{\mu}^{b}(x)+Q^{ab}_{2\mu}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}}{6}^{3}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\Big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)\Big)\Big(A^{b}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)\Big)\Big(A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{c}_{1\mu}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{2}\{T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]\}\Big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)\Big)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\varrho Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho Q^{bc}_{2\mu}(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle+i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{2}[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]A^{a}_{\mu}(x)\varrho Q^{[bc]}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{12}\Big(-i\varrho^{2}g_{0}^{3}[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)-i\varrho g_{0}^{3}[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)Q^{c}_{1\mu}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle+i\varrho g_{0}^{3}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\Big)Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4})

(17)

where we have used

\displaystyle e^{ax+bx^{2}+cx^{3}}=1+ax+\big(\tfrac{1}{2}a^{2}+b\big)x^{2}+\big(\tfrac{1}{6}a^{3}+\tfrac{1}{2}(ab+ba)+c\big)x^{3}+\mathcal{O}(x^{4})

(18)

in the second step.
Based on this, it is convenient to define fields
A^{(n)a}
,
A^{(n)ab}
,
\bar{A}^{(n)abc}
, and
\hat{A}^{(n)abc}
at each order in
g_{0}
for a general

such that

\displaystyle U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)A^{(n)b}_{\mu}(x)+[T^{a},T^{b}]A_{\mu}^{(n)ab}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)A^{(n)b}_{\mu}(x)A^{(n)c}_{\mu}(x)+\frac{3}{2}\{T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]\}A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)A_{\mu}^{(n)bc}(x)

\displaystyle+[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]\bar{A}^{(n)abc}_{\mu}(x)+\Big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\Big)\hat{A}^{(n)abc}_{\mu}(x)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4})\;.

(19)

We can now also incorporate
\bar{A}
into
\hat{A}
because

2.2.1
Connecting the perturbative and
SU(N_{c})
expansions

In order to relate the smeared gluon field
\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)
to the perturbative functions
A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)
,
A_{\mu}^{(n)ab}(x)
, and
A_{\mu}^{(n)abc}(x)
, we need to expand the products of color matrices such that only terms with a single
T^{a}
remain.
We use

\displaystyle[T^{a},T^{b}]

\displaystyle=if^{abc}T^{c}\;,

\displaystyle f^{abc}

\displaystyle=-2i\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}[T^{b},T^{c}]\big)

(33)

\displaystyle\{T^{a},T^{b}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta^{ab}+d^{abc}T^{c}\;,

\displaystyle d^{abc}

\displaystyle=2\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}\{T^{b},T^{c}\}\big)

(34)

and

\displaystyle T^{a}T^{b}=\frac{1}{2N_{c}}\delta^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\big(d^{abc}+if^{abc}\big)T^{c}

(35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\big]=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}\{T^{b},T^{c}\}+T^{a}[T^{b},T^{c}]\big]=\frac{1}{4}\big(d^{abc}+if^{abc}\big)\,.

(36)

With the above color identities we can now rewrite the following expressions in a way that only one generator
T
remains:

\displaystyle[T^{a},T^{b}]A^{(n)[ab]}_{\mu}(x)=if^{abc}T^{a}A^{(n)[bc]}_{\mu}(x)

(37)

\displaystyle\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\bigg)A^{(n)abc}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\{T^{a},\{T^{b},T^{c}\}\}+[T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]]\Big)-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\bigg)A^{(n)abc}_{\mu}(x)

(38)

\displaystyle=T^{a}\bigg[\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta^{ae}\delta^{bc}+\frac{1}{2}\big(d^{bcd}d^{eda}-f^{bcd}f^{eda}\big)\bigg]A^{(n)ebc}_{\mu}(x)\;.

(39)

Thus we can rearrange our perturbative expansion (
19
) to look like

\displaystyle U^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)=1+ig_{0}T^{a}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}}{2}f^{abc}A^{(n)[bc]}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{6}\bigg[\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta^{ae}\delta^{bc}+\frac{1}{2}\big(d^{bcd}d^{eda}-f^{bcd}f^{eda}\big)\bigg]A^{(n)ebc}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{3})\Bigg\}

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}T^{a}T^{b}\Bigg\{A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)-\frac{g_{0}}{2}\Big(A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)f^{bcd}+f^{acd}A^{(n)b}_{\mu}(x)\Big)A^{(n)[cd]}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{2})\Bigg\}

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\Bigg\{A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)A_{\mu}^{(n)b}(x)A_{\mu}^{(n)c}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0})\Bigg\}+\mathcal{O}(T^{4}).

(40)

We can see that the expressions in the large braces correspond to the first, second, and third order of an expansion of the exponential function, each cut off to give an overall highest order of
g_{0}^{3}
.
Hence we can give the first few orders of the smeared gluon field as

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{(n)a}(g_{0},x)

\displaystyle=A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}}{2}f^{abc}A^{(n)[bc]}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{6}\bigg[\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta^{ae}\delta^{bc}+\frac{1}{2}\big(d^{bcd}d^{eda}-f^{bcd}f^{eda}\big)\bigg]A^{(n)ebc}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{3})\,.

(41)

Thus, we have expressed the new gluon field in terms of the perturbative fields.

2.3
Leading order

As we have seen, at leading order the perturbative and
SU(N_{c})
expansions are identical:
\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)=A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0})
.
The sum of staples at leading order is:

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=6+ig_{0}T^{a}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{4}\Big[A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x)+A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x+\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x+\hat{\mu})-A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x-\hat{\nu})

\displaystyle+A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})\Big]-2A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)\bigg)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{2})

(42)

where the sum now includes the
\nu=\mu
term, which is compensated by the subtraction of
2A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)
. Therefore

\displaystyle Q^{(n)a}_{1\mu}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{4}\Big[A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x)+A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x+\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x+\hat{\mu})-A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x-\hat{\nu})

\displaystyle+A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})-2A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(x)\Big]

(43)

\displaystyle=-6A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)+S^{(n)a}_{1\mu}(x)\,.

(44)

The smeared gluon field in position space can then be written as a convolution
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n+1)a}(x)

\displaystyle=A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho\,Q^{(n)a}_{1\mu}(x)

(45)

\displaystyle=A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)+\varrho\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{4}\sum\limits_{y}g_{\mu\nu}(y)A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x+y)

(46)

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu}\sum\limits_{y}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,y)A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(x+y)

(47)

with

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,y)=\delta_{\mu\nu}+\varrho g_{\mu\nu}(y)

and

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=\delta(y,0)-\delta(y,\hat{\mu})-\delta(y,-\hat{\nu})+\delta(y,\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})+\delta_{\mu\nu}\bigg[\sum\limits_{\tau=1}^{4}\big(\delta(y,\hat{\tau})+\delta(y,-\hat{\tau})\big)-8\delta(y,0)\bigg].

(48)

Performing a Fourier transformation using
\delta(x,y)=\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i(x-y)p}
, we get:

\displaystyle e^{iyp}\Bigg(1-e^{-ip_{\mu}}-e^{ip_{\nu}}+e^{-i(p_{\mu}-p_{\nu})}+\delta_{\mu\nu}\bigg[-8+\sum\limits_{\tau=1}^{4}\big(e^{-ip_{\tau}}+e^{ip_{\tau}}\big)\bigg]\Bigg)

\displaystyle=e^{iyp}e^{-\frac{i}{2}(p_{\mu}-p_{\nu})}\Big(-\delta_{\mu\nu}\hat{p}^{2}+\hat{p}_{\mu}\hat{p}_{\nu}\Big)

(49)

with
\hat{p}_{\mu}=2\sin(\tfrac{1}{2}p_{\mu})
and
\hat{p}^{2}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{4}\hat{p}_{\mu}^{2}
.
Now we can express the Fourier transform of the first order smeared gluon field as

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n+1)a}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu,y}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{iyp}e^{-\frac{i}{2}(p_{\mu}-p_{\nu})}\Big(\delta_{\mu\nu}+\varrho\Big(-\delta_{\mu\nu}\hat{p}^{2}+\hat{p}_{\mu}\hat{p}_{\nu}\Big)\Big)e^{i(x+y+\hat{\nu}/2)k}A^{(n)a}_{\nu}(k)

(50)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i(x+\hat{\mu}/2)k}\Big(\delta_{\mu\nu}+\varrho\Big(-\delta_{\mu\nu}\hat{k}^{2}+\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}\Big)\Big)A^{(n)a}_{\nu}(k)\;,

(51)

where we have used
\sum\limits_{x}e^{-ix(p-q)}=(2\pi)^{4}\delta(p-q)
and performed the integration over
p
.
Thus at leading order the result in momentum space is

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n+1)a}(k)=A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(k)+\varrho\sum\limits_{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}(k)A_{\nu}^{(n)a}(k)

(52)

with

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(k)

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\mu\nu}\hat{k}^{2}+\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}

(53)

Expressing it in terms of the function
f(k)=1-\varrho\hat{k}^{2}
, it becomes easier to iterate

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n+1)a}(k)=\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(f(k)\delta_{\mu\nu}-(f(k)-1)\frac{\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}}{\hat{k}^{2}}\right)A^{(n)a}_{\nu}(k).

(54)

In this form, the form factor retains the same structure after multiple iterations, only the powers of
f
increase
[
5
]
[
37
]
:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n)a}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\bigg(f^{n}(k)\delta_{\mu\nu}-(f^{n}(k)-1)\frac{\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}}{\hat{k}^{2}}\bigg)A^{(0)a}_{\nu}(k)

(55)

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k)A^{(0)a}_{\nu}(k)

(56)

We note that
\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k)
is not exactly the Fourier transform of
\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,y)
but rather fulfils the same purpose in momentum space of relating the newly smeared field to the one of the previous smearing step.

2.4
Next-to-leading order

At higher orders the expansions deviate, and Eqs. (
16
) and (
17
) would need to be modified for a general smearing step
n\to n+1
to include the higher order perturbative fields
A^{(n)[ab]}_{\mu}(x)
,
\bar{A}^{(n)abc}_{\mu}(x)
, and
\hat{A}^{(n)abc}_{\mu}(x)
. This is why we start again by considering the first smearing step.
At order
g_{0}^{2}
, the sum of staples
S^{(0)}_{\mu}(x)
is given by:

\displaystyle S^{(0)ab}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu\neq\mu}\bigg[A_{\nu}^{a}(x)A_{\mu}^{b}(x+\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{a}(x)A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu})-A_{\mu}^{a}(x+\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu})

\displaystyle-A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\mu}^{b}(x-\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})

\displaystyle+A_{\mu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})+\frac{1}{2}\Big\{A_{\nu}^{a}(x)A_{\nu}^{b}(x)+A_{\mu}^{a}(x+\hat{\nu})A_{\mu}^{b}(x+\hat{\nu})

\displaystyle+A_{\nu}^{a}(x+\hat{\mu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu})+A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\mu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\mu}^{b}(x-\hat{\nu})

\displaystyle+A_{\nu}^{a}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})\Big\}\bigg].

(57)

The part in braces
\{\ldots\}
is symmetric in
a\leftrightarrow b
and will therefore disappear in
A^{(1)[ab]}_{\mu}(x)
.
We get

\displaystyle Q^{(0)ab}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(-S^{(0)a}_{1\mu}(x)A^{(0)b}_{\mu}(x)+S^{(0)ab}_{2\mu}(x)\Big)

(58)

which leaves

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(1)[ab]}(x)

\displaystyle=2\varrho\Big(\frac{1}{2}Q^{(0)[a}_{1\mu}(x)A^{(0)b]}_{\mu}(x)+Q^{(0)[ab]}_{2\mu}(x)\Big)=\varrho S^{(0)[ab]}_{2\mu}(x)\;,

(59)

which we can write as a double convolution:

\displaystyle S^{(0)[ab]}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{y,z}\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho=1}^{4}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(y,z)\cdot A^{[a}_{\nu}(x+y)A^{b]}_{\rho}(x+z)

(60)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{y,z}\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho=1}^{4}\frac{1}{2}\big(g_{\mu\nu\rho}(y,z)-g_{\mu\rho\nu}(z,y)\big)\cdot A^{a}_{\nu}(x+y)A^{b}_{\rho}(x+z)

(61)

with

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\rho}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu\nu}\left[\delta(y,-\hat{\rho})\delta(z,\hat{\mu}-\hat{\rho})-\delta(y,\hat{\rho})\delta(z,\hat{\mu})\right]

\displaystyle+\delta_{\mu\rho}\left[\delta(y,0)\delta(z,\hat{\nu})-\delta(y,-\hat{\nu})\delta(z,-\hat{\nu})\right]

\displaystyle-\delta_{\nu\rho}\left[\delta(y,0)\delta(z,\hat{\mu})+\delta(y,-\hat{\nu})\delta(z,\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})\right]\;.

(62)

Fourier transforming gives us

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(1)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=\varrho\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2})\;,

(63)

where

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=2i\Big(\delta_{\mu\rho}c(k_{1\mu})s(2k_{2\nu}+k_{1\nu})-\delta_{\mu\nu}c(k_{2\mu})s(2k_{1\rho}+k_{2\rho})

\displaystyle+\delta_{\nu\rho}s(k_{1\mu}-k_{2\mu})c(k_{1\nu}+k_{2\nu})\Big)\;.

(64)

Here we use the common shorthand for the trigonometric functions

\displaystyle s(k_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\sin\big(\tfrac{1}{2}k_{\mu}\big)

\displaystyle c(k_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\cos\big(\tfrac{1}{2}k_{\mu}\big)

(65)

\displaystyle\bar{s}(k_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\sin(k_{\mu})

\displaystyle\bar{c}(k_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\cos(k_{\mu})

(66)

\displaystyle s^{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mu}s^{2}(k_{\mu})

(67)

Although
g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})
is imaginary (and therefore so is
A_{\mu}^{(1)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})
), it also fulfils
g_{\mu\nu\rho}(-k_{1},-k_{2})=-g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})
, which means that
A_{\mu}^{(1)[ab]}(x)
is real.

If we were to repeat the previous calculation for a smearing step
n\to n+1
, we would not only have to replace the
A^{(0)a}_{\mu}(x)
by
A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)
but the expansion of
S^{(n)ab}_{2\mu}(x)
would also contain terms linear in
A^{(n)[ab]}_{\mu}(x)
.
Instead, it is easier to realize that we can get the same contribution by replacing

\displaystyle T^{a}A^{(0)a}_{\mu}(x)\to T^{a}\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x)=T^{a}A^{(n)a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}}{2i}[T^{a},T^{b}]A^{(n)[ab]}_{\mu}(x)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{2})

(68)

in the
leading order
calculation.
Then we get for a general smearing step

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n+1)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2})A_{\nu}^{(n)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})+\varrho\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(n)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(n)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})

(69)

By iterating once

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(2)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2})\varrho\sum\limits_{\rho\sigma}g_{\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(0)a}_{\rho}(k_{1})A^{(0)b}_{\sigma}(k_{2})

\displaystyle+\varrho\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(1)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(1)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})

(70)

and twice

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(3)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}^{2}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2})\varrho\sum\limits_{\rho\sigma}g_{\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(0)a}_{\rho}(k_{1})A^{(0)b}_{\sigma}(k_{2})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2})\varrho\sum\limits_{\rho\sigma}g_{\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(1)a}_{\rho}(k_{1})A^{(1)b}_{\sigma}(k_{2})

\displaystyle+\varrho\sum\limits_{\nu,\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{(2)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(2)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})\;,

(71)

we can infer the general form

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}\varrho\,g_{\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2})\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2})A^{(m)a}_{\rho}(k_{1})A^{(m)b}_{\sigma}(k_{2})\;.

(72)

Finally, we can also express the first order fields in the sum in terms of the original unsmeared ones

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{(n)[ab]}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma\nu\rho}\varrho\,g_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\Bigg[\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\alpha}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2})\tilde{g}^{m}_{\beta\nu}(\varrho,k_{1})\tilde{g}^{m}_{\gamma\rho}(\varrho,k_{2})\Bigg]A^{(0)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(0)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\mu\nu\rho}(\varrho,k_{1},k_{2})A^{(0)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(0)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})\;.

(73)

2.5
Next-to-next-to-leading order

As before, we start again by considering the first smearing step and generalize later to an arbitrary number of smearing steps. At third order we have defined two perturbative fields
\bar{A}
and
\hat{A}
in Eq. (
19
), where the former can be read off of Eq. (
17
) to be

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{(1)abc}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=-3\varrho A^{a}_{\mu}(x)Q^{[bc]}_{2\mu}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\varrho^{2}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\varrho A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)Q^{c}_{1\mu}(x)

(74)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{(1)[bc]}_{\mu}(x)+\frac{3}{2}\varrho A^{a}_{\mu}(x)Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\varrho^{2}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)Q^{b}_{1\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\varrho A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)Q^{c}_{1\mu}(x)

(75)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{(1)[bc]}_{\mu}(x)+\frac{1}{2}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{(1)b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+\frac{1}{2}A^{(1)a}_{\mu}(x)A^{(1)b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)\,.

(76)

We have dropped all terms that were symmetric in
b\leftrightarrow c
in the last step.
Thus
\bar{A}
is completely given by already known quantities, which is not surprising as it stems only from the commutator terms of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula.

From the third order in the expansion of
Q_{\mu}(x)
we get

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{(1)abc}_{\mu}(x)=-6\varrho Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)

(77)

with

\displaystyle Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{1}{2}S^{a}_{1\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+S^{ab}_{2\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{1}{2}S^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)

(78)

\displaystyle=-2A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{1}{2}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)+S^{ab}_{2\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)-S^{abc}_{3\mu}(x)\;.

(79)

We revisit the sum of staples at second order and define

\displaystyle S^{ab}_{2\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=s_{1\mu}^{ab}(x)+\frac{1}{2}s_{2\mu}^{ab}(x)

(80)

with

\displaystyle s_{1\mu\nu}^{ab}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\Big[A_{\nu}^{a}(x)A_{\mu}^{b}(x+\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{a}(x)A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu})-A_{\mu}^{a}(x+\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu})

\displaystyle-A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\mu}^{b}(x-\hat{\nu})-A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})+A_{\mu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})\Big]

(81)

\displaystyle s_{2\mu}^{ab}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\Big[A_{\nu}^{a}(x)A_{\nu}^{b}(x)+A_{\mu}^{a}(x+\hat{\nu})A_{\mu}^{b}(x+\hat{\nu})+A_{\nu}^{a}(x+\hat{\mu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu})

\displaystyle+A_{\nu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\mu}^{a}(x-\hat{\nu})A_{\mu}^{b}(x-\hat{\nu})+A_{\nu}^{a}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})A_{\nu}^{b}(x+\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})

\displaystyle-2\delta_{\mu\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)\Big]\;.

(82)

Their Fourier transforms are

\displaystyle s_{1\mu}^{ab}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{yz}\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}a_{1\mu\nu\rho}(y,z)A^{a}_{\nu}(x+y)A^{b}_{\rho}(x+z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{1}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{8}}e^{-i(x+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\mu})(k_{1}+k_{2})}\cdot a_{1\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2})

(83)

\displaystyle s_{2\mu}^{ab}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{y}\sum\limits_{\nu}a_{2\mu\nu}(y)A^{a}_{\nu}(x+y)A^{b}_{\nu}(x+y)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{1}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{8}}e^{-i(x+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\mu})(k_{1}+k_{2})}\cdot a_{2\mu\nu}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{b}_{\nu}(k_{2})

(84)

with

\displaystyle a_{1\mu\nu\rho}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu\nu}\left[\delta(y,-\hat{\rho})\delta(z,\hat{\mu}-\hat{\rho})-\delta(y,\hat{\rho})\delta(z,\hat{\mu})\right]

\displaystyle+\delta_{\mu\rho}\left[\delta(y,0)\delta(z,\hat{\nu})-\delta(y,-\hat{\nu})\delta(z,-\hat{\nu})\right]

\displaystyle-\delta_{\nu\rho}\left[\delta(y,0)\delta(z,\hat{\mu})+\delta(y,-\hat{\nu})\delta(z,\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})\right]

(85)

\displaystyle a_{2\mu\nu}(y)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu\nu}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{4}\Big[\delta(y,\hat{\alpha})+\delta(y,-\hat{\alpha})-2\delta(y,0)\Big]+6\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta(y,0)

\displaystyle+\delta(y,0)+\delta(y,\hat{\mu})+\delta(y,-\hat{\nu})+\delta(y,\hat{\mu}-\hat{\nu})

(86)

and

\displaystyle a_{1\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\mu\nu}\left[2i\sin\left(k_{1\rho}+\tfrac{1}{2}k_{2\rho}\right)e^{\frac{i}{2}k_{2\mu}}\right]+\delta_{\mu\rho}\left[2i\sin\left(k_{2\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}k_{1\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{i}{2}k_{1\mu}}\right]

\displaystyle-\delta_{\nu\rho}\left[2\cos\left(\tfrac{1}{2}k_{1\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}k_{2\nu}\right)e^{-\frac{i}{2}(k_{1\mu}-k_{2\mu})}\right]

(87)

\displaystyle=-h_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})+g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})\;.

(88)

Here
h_{\mu\nu\rho}
and
g_{\mu\nu\rho}
are essentially the real and imaginary parts of
a_{1\mu\nu\rho}
, i.e.

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=2i\Big(-\delta_{\mu\nu}c(k_{2\mu})s(2k_{1\rho}+k_{2\rho})+\delta_{\mu\rho}c(k_{1\mu})s(2k_{2\nu}+k_{1\nu})

\displaystyle+\delta_{\nu\rho}s(k_{1\mu}-k_{2\mu})c(k_{1\nu}+k_{2\nu})\Big)

(89)

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=2\Big(-\delta_{\mu\nu}s(k_{2\mu})s(2k_{1\rho}+k_{2\rho})-\delta_{\mu\rho}s(k_{1\mu})s(2k_{2\nu}+k_{1\nu})

\displaystyle+\delta_{\nu\rho}c(k_{1\mu}-k_{2\mu})c(k_{1\nu}+k_{2\nu})\Big)\;.

(90)

We know the antisymmetric
g_{\mu\nu\rho}
already from the second order.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle a_{2\mu\nu}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=6\delta_{\mu\nu}+\delta_{\mu\nu}\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{4}\bigg[2\cos\big(k_{1\alpha}+k_{2\alpha}\big)-2\bigg]+4\cos\big(\tfrac{1}{2}(k_{1\mu}+k_{2\mu})\big)\cos\big(\tfrac{1}{2}(k_{1\nu}+k_{2\nu})\big)

\displaystyle=6\varrho\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\alpha}({\varrho},k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})A^{(m)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(m)[bc]}_{\rho}(k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\varrho^{2}\Big(g_{\alpha\nu}(k_{1})g_{\mu\rho}(k_{2})\delta_{\mu\sigma}-g_{\mu\nu}(k_{1})\delta_{\mu\rho}g_{\mu\sigma}(k_{3})\Big)+\varrho G_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\Bigg)

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\alpha}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})A^{(m)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(m)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})A^{(m)c}_{\sigma}(k_{3})\;.

(130)

Plugging in the relations from leading order and next-to-leading order, we express the end result as a function of the unsmeared fields:

\displaystyle A^{(n)abc}_{\mu}

\displaystyle(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma}6\varrho g_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\alpha}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})\tilde{g}^{m}_{\beta\nu}(\varrho,k_{1})\tilde{g}^{(m)}_{\gamma\rho\sigma}(\varrho,k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\varrho^{2}\Big(g_{\alpha\beta}(k_{1})g_{\alpha\gamma}(k_{2})\delta_{\alpha\delta}-g_{\alpha\beta}(k_{1})\delta_{\alpha\gamma}g_{\alpha\delta}(k_{3})\Big)+\varrho G_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n-1}\tilde{g}^{n-m-1}_{\mu\alpha}(\varrho,k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})\tilde{g}^{m}_{\beta\nu}(\varrho,k_{1})\tilde{g}^{m}_{\gamma\rho}(\varrho,k_{2})\tilde{g}^{m}_{\delta\sigma}(\varrho,k_{3})\Bigg)A^{(0)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(0)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})A^{(0)c}_{\sigma}(k_{3})

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\varrho,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})A^{(0)a}_{\nu}(k_{1})A^{(0)b}_{\rho}(k_{2})A^{(0)c}_{\sigma}(k_{3})\;,

(131)

and we have found the last of the three
\tilde{g}
functions from Eq. (
32
).

3
Perturbative expansion of the Wilson flow

The Wilson flow is defined through

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=iQ_{\mu}(x,t)U_{\mu}(x,t)

(132)

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x,0)

\displaystyle=U_{\mu}(x)

(133)

with the same
Q_{\mu}
as in the definition of stout smearing but made up from the “flowed” fields
U_{\mu}(x,t)
instead of the smeared fields. We already know the perturbative expansion of
Q_{\mu}

\displaystyle Q_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=g_{0}T^{a}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x,t)+ig_{0}^{2}[T^{a},T^{b}]Q^{ab}_{2\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle+g_{0}^{3}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\Big)Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x,t)

(134)

We define the expansion of the flowed link variable in terms of fields
A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)
,
A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x,t)
, and
A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)
such that similarly to the smeared case

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}\Big(T^{a}T^{b}A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)+[T^{a},T^{b}]A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x,t)\Big)

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}\Bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)+\frac{3}{2}\{T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]\}A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle+\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big]\bigg)A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)\Bigg)+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{4})\;.

(135)

They fulfil the initial conditions

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\mu}(x,0)

\displaystyle=A^{a}_{\mu}(x)\;,

\displaystyle A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x,0)

\displaystyle=0\;,

\displaystyle A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,0)

\displaystyle=0\;,

(136)

and we define their Fourier transforms consistently:

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i(x+\hat{\mu}/2)k}A^{a}_{\mu}(k,t)

(137)

\displaystyle A^{ab}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i(x+\hat{\mu}/2)(k_{1}+k_{2})}A^{ab}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)

(138)

\displaystyle A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\limits_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{i(x+\hat{\mu}/2)(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})}A^{abc}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)\;.

(139)

As in the stout smearing case, we can connect the perturbative fields to the flowed gluon field
\tilde{A}^{a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x,t)
in the
SU(N_{c})
expansion such that

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x,t)=e^{ig_{0}T^{a}\tilde{A}^{a}_{\mu}(g_{0},x,t)}

(140)

and

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\mu}^{a}(g_{0},x,t)

\displaystyle=A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)-\frac{g_{0}}{2}f^{abc}A^{[bc]}_{\mu}(x,t)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{6}\bigg[\frac{1}{N_{c}}\delta^{ae}\delta^{bc}+\frac{1}{2}\big(d^{bcd}d^{eda}-f^{bcd}f^{eda}\big)\bigg]A^{ebc}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(g_{0}^{3})\,.

(141)

3.1
Leading order

At leading order the differential equation is

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)=Q_{1\mu}^{a}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{\nu,y}g_{\mu\nu}(y)A^{a}_{\nu}(x+y,t)

(142)

which in momentum space becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{a}_{\mu}(k,t)=\sum\limits_{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}(k)A^{a}_{\nu}(k,t)

(143)

with the same
g_{\mu\nu}
as defined at leading order of the stout smearing.
With

\displaystyle g^{2}_{\mu\nu}(k)=\sum\limits_{\alpha}g_{\mu\alpha}(k)g_{\alpha\nu}(k)=-\hat{k}^{2}g_{\mu\nu}(k)\;,

(144)

the matrix exponential of
g
is

\displaystyle\exp(g(k)t)_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}t+\frac{1}{2}g^{2}_{\mu\nu}t^{2}+\ldots

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{\hat{k}^{2}}\left(e^{-\hat{k}^{2}t}-1\right)g_{\mu\nu}(k)\;.

(145)

With the initial condition for
t=0
, we arrive at:

\displaystyle A^{a}_{\mu}(k,t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}\left(e^{-\hat{k}^{2}t}\delta_{\mu\nu}-\left(e^{-\hat{k}^{2}t}-1\right)\frac{\hat{k}_{\mu}\hat{k}_{\nu}}{\hat{k}^{2}}\right)A^{a}_{\nu}(k,0)

(146)

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu}B_{\mu\nu}(k,t)A^{a}_{\nu}(k,0)\;.

(147)

It is easy to see that for an infinitesimal flow-time
t=\epsilon
, the exponentials become
e^{-\hat{k}^{2}\epsilon}=1-\epsilon\hat{k}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
and the flow transformation becomes the same as a stout smearing with smearing parameter
\varrho=\epsilon
, reiterating the fact that the Wilson flow is generated by infinitesimal stout smearing.

The same result is obtained by starting from
n
stout smearings and taking the limit
n\to\infty
while keeping the product
n\varrho=:t
constant. Then
f(k)^{n}=(1-\tfrac{t}{n}\hat{k}^{2})^{n}\to e^{-\hat{k}^{2}t}

[
38
]
. Moreover, this means for a small flow time
t<1
:

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}(k,t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(t,k)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}(\hat{k}^{2})^{2}g_{\mu\nu}(k)+\mathcal{O}(t^{3})

(148)

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{2}_{\mu\nu}(t/2,k)+\frac{t^{2}}{4}(\hat{k}^{2})^{2}g_{\mu\nu}(k)+\mathcal{O}(t^{3})

(149)

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\nu}(t/n,k)+\frac{t^{2}}{2n}(\hat{k}^{2})^{2}g_{\mu\nu}(k)+\mathcal{O}(t^{3})

(150)

Thus the error we get by approximating the flowed field by the field which has been smeared
n
times with smearing parameter
\varrho=t/n
is always of the order
t^{2}
but decreases with
1/n
.

3.2
Next-to-leading order

At order
g_{0}^{2}
, the differential equation is

\displaystyle T^{a}T^{b}\partial_{t}\big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)\big)+[T^{a},T^{b}]\partial_{t}A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=2T^{a}T^{b}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)+2[T^{a},T^{b}]Q^{ab}_{2\mu}(x,t)\;.

(151)

With

\displaystyle Q^{ab}_{2\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(-S^{a}_{1\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)+S^{ab}_{2\mu}(x,t)-6A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x,t)\Big)

(152)

and the result from the leading order, we arrive at

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=S^{[ab]}_{2\mu}(x,t)-6A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(x,t)

(153)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu,y}g_{\mu\nu}(y)A^{[ab]}_{\nu}(x+y,t)+\sum\limits_{\nu\rho,yz}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(y,z)A^{a}_{\nu}(x+y,t)A^{b}_{\rho}(x+z,t)\;.

(154)

In momentum space, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{ab}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}(k_{1}+k_{2})A^{[ab]}_{\nu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)+\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t)

(155)

with the same
g_{\mu\nu\rho}
we know from stout smearing. Now we have an inhomogenous differential equation and make the ansatz

\displaystyle A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\sum\limits_{\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},t)C^{[ab]}_{\alpha}(k_{1},k_{2},t)

(156)

as we know that
B_{\mu\nu}
solves the homogeneous part. For the inhomogeneous part we need to solve

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},t)\partial_{t}C^{[ab]}_{\alpha}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t)\;,

(157)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\partial_{t}C^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\sum\limits_{\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},-t)\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t)\;,

(158)

because
\big(B_{\mu\nu}(k,t)\big)^{-1}=B_{\mu\nu}(k,-t)
. Thus

\displaystyle C^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)=\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},-t^{\prime})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}

(159)

and

\displaystyle A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\nu\rho}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},t)\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\alpha\beta}(k_{1}+k_{2},-t^{\prime})g_{\beta\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},t-t^{\prime})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\;,

(160)

where we have used
\sum_{\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}(k,t_{1})B_{\alpha\nu}(k,t_{2})=B_{\mu\nu}(k,t_{1}+t_{2})
.
We can also go further and express
A^{[ab]}_{\mu}
in terms of the original gluon fields

\displaystyle A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(k_{1},k_{2})\bigg[\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},t-t^{\prime})B_{\beta\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})B_{\gamma\rho}(k_{2},t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\bigg]

\displaystyle\times A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},0)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},0)

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}B_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2},t)A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},0)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},0)\;.

(161)

Again, we can check that we recover
A^{(1)[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2})
for an infinitesimal flow time
t=\epsilon

\displaystyle A^{[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(k_{1},k_{2})\bigg[\epsilon B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2},0)B_{\beta\nu}(k_{1},0)B_{\gamma\rho}(k_{2},0)\bigg]A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},0)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},0)

(162)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}\epsilon g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},0)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},0)=A^{(1)[ab]}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2})\Big|_{{\varrho}=\epsilon}

(163)

If we expand further (for small
t<1
), we get

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2},t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma}g_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(k_{1},k_{2})\bigg(\delta_{\mu\alpha}\delta_{\beta\nu}\delta_{\gamma\rho}t+\frac{1}{2}\big(g_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2})\delta_{\beta\nu}\delta_{\gamma\rho}+\delta_{\mu\alpha}g_{\beta\nu}(k_{1})\delta_{\gamma\rho}

\displaystyle+\delta_{\mu\alpha}\delta_{\beta\nu}g_{\gamma\rho}(k_{2})\big)t^{2}+\mathcal{O}(t^{3})\bigg)

(164)

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{(1)}_{\mu\nu\rho}(t,k_{1},k_{2})+\frac{t^{2}}{2}(\ldots)+\mathcal{O}(t^{3})

(165)

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\mu\nu\rho}(t/n,k_{1},k_{2})+\frac{t^{2}}{2n}(\ldots)+\mathcal{O}(t^{3})

(166)

which shows the same behavior as the first order fields.

3.3
Next-to-next-to leading order

The differential equation at third order is

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}\Bigg[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\partial_{t}\big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)\big)+\frac{3}{2}\{T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]\}\partial_{t}\big(A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{[bc]}_{\mu}(x,t)\big)

\displaystyle+\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big)\bigg)\partial_{t}A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=-ig_{0}^{3}\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}T^{a}[T^{b},T^{c}]Q^{a}_{1\mu}(x,t)A^{[bc]}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle+[T^{a},T^{b}]T^{c}Q^{ab}_{2\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)-\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big)\bigg)Q^{abc}_{3\mu}(x,t)\Bigg]\;.

(167)

The right-hand side can be rewritten to look like

\displaystyle-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\partial_{t}(A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t))+\frac{3}{2}\{T^{a}[T^{b},T^{c}]\}\partial_{t}(A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{[bc]}_{\mu}(x,t))

\displaystyle-\bigg(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big)\bigg)\Big(-\frac{1}{2}Q_{1\mu}^{a}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle-A^{a}Q_{1\mu}^{b}A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)+3V^{\{ab\}}_{2\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)-3V^{abc}_{3\mu}(x,t)-6A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle+6A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)\Big)\bigg)\;.

(168)

Thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}Q_{1\mu}^{a}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)+A^{a}Q_{1\mu}^{b}A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)-3V^{\{ab\}}_{2\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)

\displaystyle+3V^{abc}_{3\mu}(x,t)+6A^{a}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{b}_{\mu}(x,t)A^{c}_{\mu}(x,t)-6A^{abc}_{\mu}(x,t)\;.

(169)

In momentum space, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}A^{abc}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})A^{abc}_{\nu}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle+6\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{[bc]}_{\rho}(k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}G_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t)A^{c}_{\sigma}(k_{3},t)\;.

(170)

We make again the following ansatz

\displaystyle A^{abc}_{\mu}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)=\sum\limits_{\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t)C_{\alpha}^{abc}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)

(171)

as we know from the leading order that
B_{\mu\nu}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t)
satisfies the homogeneous part of the differential equation. Then we are left with the inhomogeneous part

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t)\partial_{t}C^{abc}_{\alpha}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle=6\sum\limits_{\nu\rho}g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{[bc]}_{\rho}(k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}G_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t)A^{c}_{\sigma}(k_{3},t)\;,

(172)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\partial_{t}C^{abc}_{\alpha}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle=6\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},-t)g_{\alpha\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{[bc]}_{\rho}(k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},-t)G_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})A^{a}_{\nu}(k_{1},t)A^{b}_{\rho}(k_{2},t)A^{c}_{\sigma}(k_{3},t)\;.

(173)

Integrating this and putting everything together, we get

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{abc}

\displaystyle(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho}g_{\alpha\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t-t^{\prime})A_{\nu}^{a}(k_{1},t^{\prime})A_{\rho}^{[bc]}(k_{2},k_{3},t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}G_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t-t^{\prime})A_{\nu}^{a}(k_{1},t^{\prime})A_{\rho}^{b}(k_{2},t^{\prime})A_{\sigma}^{c}(k_{3},t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}

(174)

and we can define a function
B_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)
for the third order of the Wilson flow:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{abc}

\displaystyle(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma}g_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t-t^{\prime})B_{\beta\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})B_{\gamma\rho\sigma}(k_{2},k_{3},t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}G_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\int\limits_{0}^{t}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},t-t^{\prime})B_{\beta\nu}(k_{1},t^{\prime})B_{\gamma\rho}(k_{2},t^{\prime})B_{\delta_{\sigma}}(k_{3},t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\times A_{\nu}^{a}(k_{1},0)A_{\rho}^{b}(k_{2},0)A_{\sigma}^{c}(k_{3},0)

\displaystyle=:\sum\limits_{\nu\rho\sigma}B_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)A_{\nu}^{a}(k_{1},0)A_{\rho}^{b}(k_{2},0)A_{\sigma}^{c}(k_{3},0)\;.

(175)

Again, we can check that the error one makes by approximating the Wilson flow by stout smearing is of the order
t^{2}/(2n)
:

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)=\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(t/n,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})+\frac{t^{2}}{2n}(\ldots)\;.

(176)

Thus we have found three
B
functions that are the Wilson flow equivalent to the
\tilde{g}
functions from the expansion of stout smearing.

3.4
Continuum limit

In the continuum limit
a\to 0
, we replace
\cos(k_{\mu})\to 1
and
\sin(k_{\mu})\to k_{\mu}
, and expand to first order in the momenta.
We get

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}(k,t)

\displaystyle\to\sum\limits_{\nu}\bigg(e^{-k^{2}t}\delta_{\mu\nu}-\left(e^{-k^{2}t}-1\bigg)\frac{k_{\mu}k_{\nu}}{k^{2}}\right)

(177)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2})

4
Feynman rules of fermion actions with stout smearing or Wilson flow

The Feynman rules are obtained by inserting the expansion of the link variables (
182
) into the Fermion action.
This results in expressions for antiquark-quark-
n
-gluon vertices at each order
g_{0}^{n}
.
These vertices are proportional to
a^{n-1}
such that only the
\bar{q}qg
vertex survives in the continuum limit
a\to 0
, as is expected from continuum perturbation theory.
For the purpose of one-loop calculations, we will need the
\bar{q}qg
-,
\bar{q}qgg
- and
\bar{q}qggg
-vertex, i.e. anything up to order
g_{0}^{3}
.

For deriving the Feynman rules of the fermion action including stout smearing or Wilson flow, there are two possible strategies.
One is to simply insert the perturbative expansion of the smeared or flowed link variable into the action and expand to the needed order in
g_{0}
.
This “brute force” approach leads to very large intermediate expressions and involves many steps where indices have to be renamed etc.
This is not feasible beyond one step of stout smearing.
The second much more effective method is based on the
SU(N_{c})
expansion of the link variable.
One simply derives the Feynman rules for generic link variables of the form

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(x)=1+ig_{0}T^{a}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}T^{a}T^{b}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)-i\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}A^{a}_{\mu}(x)A^{b}_{\mu}(x)A^{c}_{\mu}(x)

(182)

which results in fairly simple expressions.
One then couples the stout or flow “form factors” to these generic Feynman rules to obtain the complete results.
This also practically allows us to easily split lengthy expressions into smaller parts for
Mathematica
to work on.
A similar procedure has been described in the context of Feynman rules for the HISQ action in Ref.
[
41
]
.
The following sections show the derivations of the generic Feynman rules and how to couple them to stout smearing or the Wilson flow.

We start with the Wilson quark action

\displaystyle S_{W}=\sum\limits_{x,y}\sum\limits_{\mu=\pm 1}^{\pm 4}\bar{\psi}(x)\frac{1}{2}\bigg[U_{\mu}(x)\delta_{x+\hat{\mu},y}\gamma_{\mu}-r\Big(U_{\mu}(x)\delta_{x+\hat{\mu},y}-\delta_{x,y}\Big)\bigg]\psi(y)\;.

(183)

Links with negative indices are given by
U_{-\mu}(x)=U^{\dagger}_{\mu}(x-\hat{\mu})
and
\gamma_{-\mu}=-\gamma_{\mu}
.
Up to order
g_{0}^{3}
we get the well-known three vertices of a quark antiquark pair coupling to one, two, and three gluons respectively.

\displaystyle V^{a}_{1W\mu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=-g_{0}T^{a}\Big(i\gamma_{\mu}c(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})+rs(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})\Big)

(184)

\displaystyle V^{ab}_{2W\mu\nu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{2}T^{a}T^{b}\delta_{\mu\nu}\Big(i\gamma_{\mu}s(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})-rc(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})\Big)

(185)

\displaystyle V^{abc}_{3W\mu\nu\rho}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{0}^{3}}{6}T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\mu\rho}\Big(i\gamma_{\mu}c(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})+rs(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})\Big)

(186)

These appear in the expansion of the action contracted with the (original) gluon fields

\displaystyle S_{W}

\displaystyle\sim\sum\limits_{\mu}\bar{\psi}(q)T^{a}V_{1\,\mu}(p,q)A^{a}_{\mu}(q-p)\psi(p)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\mu\nu}\bar{\psi}(q)T^{a}T^{b}V_{2\,\mu\nu}(p,q)A^{a}_{\mu}(k_{1})A^{b}_{\nu}(k_{2})\delta(q-p-k_{1}-k_{2})\psi(p)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\mu\nu\rho}\bar{\psi}(q)T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}V_{3\,\mu\nu\rho}(p,q)A^{a}_{\mu}(k_{1})A^{b}_{\nu}(k_{2})A^{c}_{\rho}(k_{3})\delta(q-p-k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{3})\psi(p)

(187)

In order to derive the Feynman rules with smearing or gradient flow, we replace the gluon fields
A^{a}_{\mu}
by
\tilde{A}^{(n)a}_{\mu}
or
\tilde{A}^{a}_{\mu}(t)
respectively. Thereby new terms are added at higher orders of
g_{0}
. For example, replacing the
A^{a}_{\mu}
which couples to
V^{a}_{1\,\mu}
by a smeared or flowed field, provides the new
\bar{q}qg
vertex with a form factor and adds terms to the
\bar{q}qgg
and
\bar{q}qggg
vertices proportional to
A^{[ab]}_{\mu}
and
A^{abc}_{\mu}
respectively.
Specifically, the new stout smeared vertices look like

\displaystyle V^{(n)a}_{1\mu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=T^{a}\sum\limits_{\nu}\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\varrho,q-p)V_{1\nu}(p,q)

(188)

\displaystyle V^{(n)ab}_{2\mu\nu}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=T^{a}T^{b}\sum\limits_{\rho\sigma}\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\rho}(\varrho,k_{1})\tilde{g}^{n}_{\nu\sigma}(\varrho,k_{2})V_{2\rho\sigma}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{0}}{2i}[T^{a},T^{b}]\sum\limits_{\rho}\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\rho\mu\nu}(\varrho,k_{1},k_{2})V_{1\rho}(p,q)

(189)

\displaystyle V^{(n)abc}_{3\mu\nu\rho}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle=T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\alpha}(\varrho,k_{1})\tilde{g}^{n}_{\nu\beta}(\varrho,k_{2})\tilde{g}^{n}_{\rho\gamma}(\varrho,k_{3})V_{3\alpha\beta\gamma}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{0}}{2i}\{T^{a},[T^{b},T^{c}]\}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{g}^{n}_{\mu\alpha}(\varrho,k_{1})\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\beta\nu\rho}(\varrho,k_{2},k_{3})V_{2\alpha\beta}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3})

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{0}^{2}}{6}\bigg[T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}

\displaystyle T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big)\bigg]\sum\limits_{\alpha}\tilde{g}^{(n)}_{\alpha\mu\nu\rho}(\varrho,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})V_{1\alpha}(p,q)

(190)

with the
\tilde{g}
given in Eqs. (
56
),(
73
), and (
131
).
The vertices with Wilson flow are analogously

\displaystyle V^{a}_{1\mu}(p,q,t)

\displaystyle=T^{a}\sum\limits_{\nu}B_{\mu\nu}(q-p,t)V_{1\nu}(p,q)

(191)

\displaystyle V^{ab}_{2\mu\nu}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2},t)

\displaystyle=T^{a}T^{b}\sum\limits_{\rho\sigma}B_{\mu\rho}(k_{1},t)B_{\nu\sigma}(k_{2},t)V_{2\rho\sigma}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{0}}{2i}[T^{a},T^{b}]\sum\limits_{\rho}B_{\rho\mu\nu}(k_{1},k_{2},t)V_{1\rho}(p,q)

(192)

\displaystyle V^{abc}_{3\mu\nu\rho}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)

\displaystyle=T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}\sum\limits_{\alpha\beta\gamma}B_{\mu\alpha}(k_{1},t)B_{\nu\beta}(k_{2},t)B_{\rho\gamma}(k_{3},t)V_{3\alpha\beta\gamma}(p,q,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle T^{b}T^{a}-\frac{1}{N_{c}}\mathrm{Tr}\big(T^{a}T^{b}T^{c}+T^{c}T^{b}T^{a}\big)\bigg]\sum\limits_{\alpha}B_{\alpha\mu\nu\rho}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},t)V_{1\alpha}(p,q)

with the

Any dependence that is still present at tiny flow times (
t/a^{2}\simeq 0.1
) is found
to quickly disappear for moderate flow times (
t/a^{2}\simeq 1
).

In our view, the takeaway message from this section is that there is a perturbative backing
of the well-known benefit that comes from multiple stout smearing. One step reduces the absolute value of

\Sigma_{0}
by about 85%, two steps by 95%, three by 98% and four steps by 99%.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have worked out a systematic approach for deriving Feynman rules for
lattice fermion actions with smeared gauge links (to be used interchangeably in the covariant
derivative, in the species-separating covariant Laplacian and/or a possible clover term).

The basis for such calculations is provided by a perturbative expansion of the stout
smearing recipe of Ref.
[
9
]
and the Wilson variety of the gradient
flow introduced in Refs.
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
;
these expansions have been presented in Sec. 2 and Sec. 3, respectively.

With these results in hand, one may derive the Feynman rules for a fermion action
with an arbitrary parameter combination
(\varrho,n_{\mathrm{stout}})
in case of stout
smearing or an arbitrary flow time
t/a^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
in case of the gradient flow.
In Sec. 4 we argue that the “brute force” approach, i.e. inserting the perturbative
expansion of the smeared/flowed variable into the underlying action, is not
recommendable, since it leads to excessively long intermediate expressions.
We present an approach based on the
SU(N_{c})
expansion of the original (“unsmeared”)
links
U_{\mu}(x)
to the required order in
g_{0}
(typically
g_{0}^{3}
for one-loop
calculations), which keeps intermediate expressions much shorter.
This holds true for both stout smearing and the gradient flow, and confirms, as
a by-product, the perturbative matching (
205
) between these recipes.

To give an immediate application of our recipe, we derive in Sec. 4 the Feynman rules
of a Wilson fermion with clover improvement for arbitrary Wilson parameter
r
and
Sheikholeslami-Wohlert parameter
c_{\mathrm{SW}}
.
With these rules in hand, we calculate in Sec. 5 the self-energy of the clover fermion
for
c_{\mathrm{SW}}=r
, for variable
\varrho
and
1\leq n_{\mathrm{stout}}\leq 4
in case
of stout smearing and the range
0\leq t/a^{2}\leq 1
in case of gradient
flowing.

The cross-check with Ref.
[
37
]
, where numerical results obtained in
the “brute force” approach were presented, proves particularly enlightening.
Of course, reproducing these results felt like a relief, but the difference is in the details.
In Ref.
[
37
]
results for
\Sigma_{0}
were given for a single stout parameter
(
\varrho=0.1
in our terminology), restricted to
n_{\mathrm{stout}}\leq 3
due to the numerical burden.
In our approach we managed to go up to
n_{\mathrm{stout}}=4
and to
evaluate the generic result for arbitrary
\varrho
(using rather modest
computational resources).
This illustrates that the more elegant approach implies not just aesthetic pleasures but actual savings compared to the “brute force” approach.
Still, the bottom line is an affirmation of the finding of Ref.
[
37
]

that combining tree-level clover improvement with stout smearing or gradient flowing with a
cumulative flow time of the order
t/a^{2}\simeq 1
yields an undoubled fermion action with
surprisingly good chiral properties (see the discussion at the end of our Sec. 5).

A logical next step would be to work out the one-loop values of

c_{\mathrm{SW}}
for Wilson and/or Brillouin fermions with a limited number
of stout steps (hopefully again up to four iterations) and/or a few values
of the flow time
t/a^{2}
, for instance
t/a^{2}\in\{0.1,0.2,0.4,0.7,1.0\}
.
This amounts to combining the work we did in Ref.
[
45
]
with the
framework for smeared/flowed Feynman rules presented in this paper, and we hope to
present such results in the not so distant future.
In a further perspective, one might also work out the improvement factors
Z_{S,P,V,A}

to one-loop (and perhaps even two-loop) order in stouted/flowed perturbation theory.

The alert reader might interject that a fully nonperturbative improvement strategy
is more desirable than a perturbative one, and the link smearing may imply only mild
technical complications in the nonperturbative approach.
However, as mentioned in the introduction and detailed in Ref.
[
34
]
, the
nonperturbative approach builds, in practical terms, on existing perturbative results
to one-loop (or even better: two-loop) order, as such results provide essential constraints
in the final step of summarizing the results through a rational function in
g_{0}^{2}
.
To us this indicates that perturbative work continues to be useful in lattice gauge theory.

Quillen metric for singular families of Riemann surfaces with cusps and compact perturbation theorem.
Siarhei Finski

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the behavior of the Quillen metric for the family of Riemann surfaces with cusps when the additional cusps are created by degeneration.

More precisely, in our previous paper, we’ve seen that the renormalization of the Quillen metric associated with a family of Riemann surfaces with cusps extends continuously over the locus of singular curves. The main result of this article shows that, modulo some explicit universal constant, this continuous extension coincides with the Quillen metric of the normalization of singular curves. This result shows that the Quillen metric is compatible with the adjunction of cusps.
When this theorem is applied directly to the moduli space of curves, we obtain the compatibility of the Quillen metric with clutching morphisms in the moduli space of pointed stable curves.

As one application, we obtain the compatibility between our definition of the analytic torsion and the definition of Takhtajan-Zograf using lengths of closed geodesics.

As a consequence of the proof of the main theorem, we get an explicit relation in terms of Bott-Chern forms between the Quillen metric associated with a cusped metric and the Quillen metric associated with a metric on the compactified Riemann surface. This refines relative compact perturbation theorem we obtained before by pinning down the universal constant.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let’s say few words about the general strategy of the proof. First, for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\overline{M},D_{M},g^{TM})
,
g^{TM}
,
g^{T\overline{M}}
,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\overline{M}}
,
h^{\xi}
,
h^{\xi}_{0}
, we define
E_{-n}\in\real
by equality (
1.51
).
Then by using anomaly formula, Theorem
2.3
, we prove that
E_{-n}
, defined by this formula, is independent of
g^{TM}
,
g^{T\overline{M}}
,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\overline{M}}
,
h^{\xi}
,
h^{\xi}_{0}
. Then by using relative compact perturbation theorem, Theorem
2.1
, we prove that
E_{-n}
is independent of
(\overline{M},D_{M})
. Finally, by using Remark
3.3
, we conclude.
Now let’s explain each of this steps in detail.

First, in the proof of independence of
E_{-n}
, defined by (
1.51
), on
g^{TM}
,
g^{T\overline{M}}
,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\overline{M}}
,
h^{\xi}
,
h^{\xi}_{0}
, we will only restrict ourselves to the proof of independence on
g^{T\overline{M}}
, as the other cases are identical.

Now, to show independence on
g^{T\overline{M}}
, by the anomaly formula of Bismut-Gillet-Soulé
[
10
]
(cf. Theorem
2.3
for
m=0
), it is enough to prove that for any two Kähler metrics
g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}
,
g^{T\overline{M}}_{2}
over
\overline{M}
, the following holds

\int_{M}^{\textbf{r}}\Big[\Big(\widetilde{{\rm{Td}}}\big(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1},g^{TM}\big)-\widetilde{{\rm{Td}}}\big(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2},g^{TM}\big)\Big){\rm{ch}}\big(\xi,h^{\xi}_{0}\big){\rm{ch}}\big(\omega_{M}(D)^{n},\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\overline{M}}^{2n}\big)\Big]^{[2]}\\
=\int_{\overline{M}}\Big[\widetilde{{\rm{Td}}}\big(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2}\big){\rm{ch}}\big(\xi,h^{\xi}_{0}\big){\rm{ch}}\big(\omega_{M}(D)^{n},\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}_{\overline{M}}^{2n}\big)\Big]^{[2]}\\
+\frac{{\rm{rk}}(\xi)}{6}\ln\Big(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},1}/\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},2}\Big),

(3.78)

where the norms
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},1}
,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},2}
on the complex line
\otimes_{P\in D_{M}}\omega_{\overline{M}}|_{P}
are induced by
g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}
and
g^{T\overline{M}}_{2}
respectively.
Since the function
\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})
is integrable, it is enough to prove that

\int_{M}^{\textbf{r}}\Big[\Big(\widetilde{{\rm{Td}}}\big(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1},g^{TM}\big)-\widetilde{{\rm{Td}}}\big(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2},g^{TM}\big)\Big)\Big]^{[2]}\\
=\int_{\overline{M}}\Big[\widetilde{{\rm{Td}}}\big(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2}\big)\Big]^{[2]}+\frac{1}{6}\ln\Big(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},1}/\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},2}\Big).

(3.79)

By using (
1.48
) and (
1.49
), we see that it is enough to prove that

\int_{M}^{\textbf{r}}\Big(\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{TM})\big(c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1})+c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{TM})\big)\\
-\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{2}/g^{TM})\big(c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})+c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{TM})\big)\Big)\\
=\int_{\overline{M}}\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})\big(c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1})+c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})\big)+2\ln\Big(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},1}/\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},2}\Big).

(3.80)

Recall the trivial identity

c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{1})=c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})+\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{T\overline{M}}_{2}).

(3.81)

Using (
3.81
), we see that it is enough to prove

\int_{M}^{\textbf{r}}\Big(\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{TM})\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})+\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{TM})\Big)\\
=\int_{\overline{M}}\log(g^{T\overline{M}}_{1}/g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})c_{1}(\omega_{\overline{M}}^{-1},g^{T\overline{M}}_{2})+2\ln\Big(\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},1}/\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert}^{D_{M}}_{\overline{M},2}\Big),

(3.82)

which is a simple application of Green identities.

Now, as we already know that the constant

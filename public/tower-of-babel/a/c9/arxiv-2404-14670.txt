On the sizes of BDDs and ZDDs representing matroids
Hiromi Emoto, Yuni Iwamasa, Shin-ichi Minato

Abstract
Matroids are often represented as oracles since there are no unified and compact representations for general matroids. This paper initiates the study of binary decision diagrams (BDDs) and zero-suppressed binary decision diagrams (ZDDs) as relatively compact data structures for representing matroids in a computer. This study particularly focuses on the sizes of BDDs and ZDDs representing matroids. First, we compare the sizes of different variations of BDDs and ZDDs for a matroid. These comparisons involve concise transformations between specific decision diagrams. Second, we provide upper bounds on the size of BDDs and ZDDs for several classes of matroids. These bounds are closely related to the number of minors of the matroid and depend only on the connectivity function or pathwidth of the matroid, which deeply relates to the classes of matroids called strongly pigeonhole classes. In essence, these results indicate upper bounds on the number of minors for specific classes of matroids and new strongly pigeonhole classes.

3
Structural relations between different representations

There are several variations of BDDs and ZDDs representing matroids.
In addition to the choice between BDDs and ZDDs, the represented collection of independent sets or bases must be chosen.
Moreover, there may be some relations between the BDD/ZDD for a matroid and its dual.
This section compares these three choices and proves
Theorem

1.3

Fix a matroid

\preceq

In this section, we denote by

\mathcal{I}

Before moving to the proof, we introduce clutters.
A set family is called a

We can show that each 1-path corresponds to a member of a clutter one-to-one in a BDD for the clutter.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq 2^{E}
be a clutter on
E
.
In the BDD
#1\lparen\mathcal{C},\lparen E,\preceq\rparen\rparen
, every 1-path traverses exactly
\lvert E\rvert
non-terminal nodes.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a 1-path with redundant nodes.
Let
e\in E
be the label of one of such nodes.
Then, for some
C\subseteq E\setminus\{e\}
, both
C
and
C\cup\{e\}
are members of
\mathcal{C}
; a contraction.
∎

Similarly, we can show that the heads of the corresponding 0-arc and 1-arc are different in a ZDD for a clutter.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq 2^{E}
be a clutter on
E

In the ZDD
#1\lparen\mathcal{C},\lparen E,\preceq\rparen\rparen
, every non-terminal node
\nu
satisfies that
\nu_{0}\neq\nu_{1}

Suppose that there exists a non-terminal node
\nu
such that
\nu_{0}=\nu_{1}
.
Since
(Z-ND)
implies that
\nu_{1}\neq\bot

by
Proposition

Let

be the corresponding set to the 1-path.
Since
\nu_{0}=\nu_{1}

\lparen\nu,\nu_{1}\rparen
in the 1-path with the 0-arc

also results in a 1-path in the ZDD.
Thus

\max\{\min\{i,\rankm\lparen M\rparen\}+\min\{n-i,\rankm\lparen M\rparen\}-\rankm\lparen M\rparen\mathrel{:}i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\}

By

with respect to
\lparen E,\preceq\rparen
is at most
\max\{\confun_{M}\lparen E_{\preceq,i}\rparen+1\mathrel{:}i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\}=\pathwidth\lparen M\rparen+1
.
∎

The pathwidth of a free matroid is clearly zero.
Thus we obtain the following since all free matroids are uniform:

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
M
be a free matroid on the ground set

.
For all total orders
\preceq
on
E
, the width of the BDDs/ZDDs for

with respect to
\lparen E,\preceq\rparen
is at most one.

The last theorem and corollary consider any total order on

.
The following theorems show that a certain total order on

can ensure that the width of BDDs/ZDDs is at most
\pathwidth\lparen M\rparen+1
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let

be a partition matroid on the ground set

.
There exists a total order
\preceq^{\ast}
on

\lparen E,\preceq^{\ast}\rparen

\pathwidth\lparen M\rparen+1

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case when
\rankm\lparen M\rparen\geq 1
.

First, we show the lower bound on the pathwidth of

\preceq

Next, we can define the total order
\preceq^{\ast}
on
E
such that
e_{1,1}\prec^{\ast}\cdots\prec^{\ast}e_{1,n_{1}}\prec^{\ast}e_{2,1}\prec^{\ast}\cdots\prec^{\ast}e_{2,n_{2}}\prec^{\ast}\cdots\prec^{\ast}e_{k,1}\prec^{\ast}\cdots\prec^{\ast}e_{k,n_{k}}

\begin{split}&\max\{\confun_{U_{r_{j},n_{j}}}\lparen\{e_{j,1},\ldots,e_{j,i}\}\rparen+1\mathrel{:}\text{$i\in\{0,\ldots,n_{j}-1\}$ and $j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$}\}\\

Theorem 4.17
.

Let

\trianglelefteq

\lparen E,\trianglelefteq\rparen

(i)

4.3
Application: implementation of a rank oracle

Matroids are often represented as oracles in algorithms on matroids (see
[
25
,
17
]
).
This section shows how to implement matroid oracles, such as a rank oracle, for a matroid
M
by using the property that the 0-child (resp. 1-child) of the ZDD

which has the root with label

represents the collection of independent sets of
M\setminus\{e\}
(resp.
M\mathrel{/}\{e\}
).

One of the matroid oracles is an independence oracle.
The
independence oracle
for

determines whether a given set

is independent in
M
.
Since membership queries to a ZDD can be answered in time proportional to the size of its ground set, the ZDD

can simulate this oracle in
\order\lparen\lvert#1\lparen M\rparen\rvert\rparen
time.
This is one of the reasons why we use BDDs/ZDDs as data structures for representing matroids.

Another oracle is a rank oracle.

Abstract
. Let R ⁡ ( G ) R(G) be the 2 2 -colour Ramsey number of a graph G G . In this note, we prove that for any non-decreasing function n ⩽ f ⁡ ( n ) ⩽ R ⁡ ( K n ) n\leqslant f(n)\leqslant R(K_{n}) , there exists a sequence of connected graphs ( G n ) n ∈ ℕ (G_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}} , with | V ⁡ ( G n ) | = n |V(G_{n})|=n for all n ⩾ 1 n\geqslant 1 , such that R ⁡ ( G n ) = Θ ⁡ ( f ⁡ ( n ) ) R(G_{n})=\Theta(f(n)) . Introduction

For a graph

For

The most notorious open problem here is to determine the Ramsey number of cliques.
The classical bounds on

[
Erd47
]
and Erdős and Szekeres
[

]

, for

,

[

]

In general, if an

, where the lower bound follows from a probabilistic construction and the upper bound was shown by Sudakov
[
Sud11
]
.
Given additional structure on

.
This holds, for instance, for graphs with bounded maximum degree
[

]
,

[

]
,
or bounded degeneracy
[

]
. We recommend
[
CFS15
]
for a survey in the area.

As we have seen, the Ramsey number of an

-vertex graph can vary between linear and exponential in

A natural question is thus to ask which values (between

and

) can be attained as the Ramsey number of some

-vertex graph.
The aim of this note is to study this question, and, in particular, to determine which functions

, are the rate of growth of the Ramsey numbers of some sequence of

-vertex graphs.

It is natural here to restrict our analysis to connected graphs.
Note that after adding

This means that we can obtain values for the Ramsey numbers of

Our first result is that every function, between the appropriate bounds, can be attained as the rate of growth of some sequence of graphs, up to a multiplicative factor.

It is easily seen that Theorem

By the form of the statement, we can assume that

Let

be the constants from Lemma

.

Case 1:

.

Let

For

-graphs, the so-called ‘stepping-up lemma’ of Erdős, Hajnal, and Rado

]
allows us to deduce a tower-type lower bound for the Ramsey number

for every

, namely

(1)

\displaystyle an^{2}\leqslant\log^{(k-2)}_{2}(R(K_{n}^{(k)})),

where

is a constant depending only on

and
\log^{(i)}_{2}(\cdot)
denotes the

[
CFS13
]
for more explicit bounds of this form).

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
k\!\geqslant\!5
.
We find a function
g\colon\mathds{N}\to\mathds{N}
, with
n\!\leqslant\!g(n)\!\leqslant\!R(K_{n}^{(k)})
as follows.
For every
n\in\mathds{N}
, let
I_{n}=[\log_{2}n,\log_{2}R(K_{n}^{(k)})]
be an interval in
\mathds{R}
.
Note that, since

, inequality (
1
) implies that

\log_{2}R(K^{(k)}_{n})-\log_{2}n\geqslant 2^{2^{an}}-\log_{2}n.

Since the number of
k
-graphs on
n
vertices is at most
2^{n^{k}}
, by averaging we find a sub-interval
I_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq I_{n}
which does not contain
\log_{2}R(H)
for any
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
, and such that
I^{\prime}_{n}
has length at least

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2^{an}}-\log_{2}n}{2^{n^{k}}+1}>2n+1,

where we used that

is sufficiently large.
By passing to a sub-interval we may assume that
I^{\prime}_{n}\cap\mathds{N}
has exactly
2n+1
elements.
Let
m_{n}\in I^{\prime}_{n}
be the middle point of
I_{n}^{\prime}\cap\mathds{N}
.
Then, for large
n
and every
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
we have

(2)

\displaystyle\log_{2}R(H)\leqslant m_{n}-n\qquad\text{or}\qquad\log_{2}R(H)\geqslant m_{n}+n.

Let
g:\mathds{N}\to\mathds{N}
be defined by
g(n)=2^{m_{n}}
.
Since

we have

. Then, from (

) we deduce that for every

and every

In particular, for every two positive constants

Note that we could finish the proof here if we were only interested in gaps for hypergraph Ramsey numbers. However, if we insist on having a non-decreasing function, we may define

by setting

∎

We conclude that
R_{3}(G)>N
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Given
n\geqslant 2
, let
t
be the least integer such that
n\leqslant R_{2}(K_{t})
. Note that
t\geqslant 2
.
By choice, we have
R_{2}(K_{t-1})<n
and, by Lemma
6
, we have
R_{2}(K_{t})\leqslant c_{1}R_{2}(K_{t-1})<c_{1}n
.
Let
G_{n}
be the graph obtained from
K_{t}
by adding
n-t
isolated vertices, then
|V(G_{n})|=n
and
R_{2}(G_{n})=\max\{n,R_{2}(K_{t})\}=R_{2}(K_{t})<c_{1}n=O(n)
.
On the other hand, since
n\leqslant R_{2}(K_{t})\leqslant 4^{t}
, we know that
t\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}n
and therefore by Lemma
12
we have
R_{3}(G_{n})=\Omega(n\log n)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
t
be the least integer so that
R_{2}(K_{t})\geqslant n\log_{2}n
and let
G_{n}=J_{n,t}
.
Applying Lemma
10
, we obtain a constant
C>0
such that

R(G_{n})\leqslant\frac{3}{2}(R(K_{t})+(t+1)n)\leqslant Cn\log_{2}n\,,

where we use the bound
R(K_{t})\leqslant 4^{t}
to deduce that
t=\Theta(\log_{2}(n))
.
Since
\chi(G_{n})=\Omega(\log n)
, then, by Lemma
12
and the definition of
t
, we have

R_{3}(G_{n})>(\chi(G_{n})-1)(R_{2}(G_{n})-1)=\Omega(n\log^{2}n),

as required.
∎

5.
Concluding remarks

5.1.
Gaps

For
n\in\mathds{N}
, let us consider the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{R(G)\colon|V(G)|=n\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\{R(G)\colon G\text{ does not contain isolated vertices and }|V(G)|=n\},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}

\displaystyle=\{R(G)\colon G\text{ is connected and }|V(G)|=n\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}\subseteq[n,R(K_{n})]
.
Observe that
n\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
since
R(\overline{K_{n}})=n
, where
\overline{K_{n}}
corresponds to an independent set on
n
vertices.
Furthermore, consider a disjoint union of two stars
\Sigma_{a,b}=K_{1,a}\cup K_{1,b}
.
A result due to Grossman
[
Gro79
]
implies that
R(\Sigma_{a,a-i})=3a-2i
for
i\in\{0,1,2\}
.
Thus, by adding
n-(2a-i+2)
extra isolated vertices to
\Sigma_{a,a-i}
and letting the value of
a
vary from
\lfloor n/3\rfloor
to
\lfloor(n-2)/2\rfloor
, we deduce that
[n,3\lfloor\tfrac{n}{2}\rfloor-3]\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}
for large
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Other families of sparse graphs can also be used to show other inclusions of this kind.

As mentioned in the introduction,
R(G)\geqslant\lceil\tfrac{4}{3}n\rceil-1
holds for every connected graph
G
on
n
vertices, and this bound is tight.
In particular, it implies that

\mathcal{R}^{\mathfrak{c}}_{n}\subseteq\Big[\big\lceil\tfrac{4}{3}n\big\rceil-1,R(K_{n})\Big].

In a similar fashion, Burr and Erdős
[
BE76
]
showed that
R(G)>n+\log n-O(\log\log n)
holds for every
G\in\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\circ}
, which is almost tight as shown by Csákány and Komlós
[
CK99
]
.
It would be interesting to get a better understanding of the structures of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
,
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\circ}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Given a constant
c\!>\!1
, say that a number
a\!\in\![n,R(K_{n})]
is a
c
-gap for
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}
if
[a,ca]\cap\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}=\emptyset
.
As mentioned inside its proof, it is easy to see that Theorem
1
is equivalent to the existence of a constant
c\geqslant 1
for which
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}
has no
c
-gaps for every sufficiently large
n
.

In this direction, a proper (but non-empty) subset of the authors of this paper believes that the answer to the following question should be affirmative.

Question 13
.

Does there exist an
n_{0}\in\mathds{N}
such that for every
n\geqslant n_{0}

\mathcal{R}_{n}=[n,R(K_{n})]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}=\big[\lceil\tfrac{4}{3}n\big\rceil-1,R(K_{n})\big]\,\text{?}

Observe that the first equality would imply that for every function
f\colon\mathds{N}\to\mathds{N}
with
n\leqslant f(n)\leqslant R(K_{n})
there is a sequence of graphs
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
such that
f(n)=R(G_{n})
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathds{N}
, and an analogous statement would hold for connected graphs if the second identity was true. However, in order to show the first equality, one would need to show that
R(K_{n}-e)\in\{R(K_{n})-1,R(K_{n})\}
for sufficiently large
n\in\mathds{N}
, which is likely to be a quite hard problem.
A simpler question, which we still think is interesting is as follows:

Question 14
.

Given
c>1
, it is true that there are no
c
-gaps
a
for
\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\mathfrak{c}}
with
a\geqslant 4n/3
, for all sufficiently large
n
?

5.2.
Chromatic number and connectivity

We observe that the proof of the first case of Theorem
1
can be modified by replacing the rôle of
K_{t,t}
with a complete
k
-partite graph
K_{t,\dots,t}
.
In this way, we may ensure that the graphs in the sequence
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
have large chromatic number for sufficiently large
n
.

Theorem 15
.

For every
k\!\geqslant\!2
, there are positive constants
c
,
C
, and
n_{0}\in\mathds{N}
such that for every function
f:\mathds{N}\to\mathds{N}
, with
n\leqslant f(n)\leqslant R(K_{n})
, there is a sequence of connected graphs
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
with
|V(G_{n})|=n
such that
cf(n)\leqslant R(G_{n})\leqslant Cf(n)
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Moreover,
\chi(G_{n})\geqslant k
for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
.

It would be interesting to ensure other properties for the graphs in this sequence.
In particular, we believe the graphs can also be taken to have large connectivity.

Conjecture 16
.

For every
k\geqslant 2
and for every function
f:\mathds{N}\to\mathds{N}
with
n\leqslant\!f(n)\!\leqslant R(K_{n})
, there is a sequence of graphs
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
with
|V(G_{n})|\!=\!n
such that

R(G_{n})=\Theta(f(n))
and
G_{n}
is
k
-connected for all
n
sufficiently large.

It is also natural to ask if
Theorem

3
holds if we require, in addition, that the graphs
G_{n}
are connected.

Question 17
.

Is there a sequence
\{G_{n}\}_{n\in\mathds{N}}
of connected graphs, with
|V(G_{n})|=n
, such that
R_{2}(G_{n})=O(n)
, but
R_{3}(G_{n})=\omega(n)
?

5.3.
Hypergraphs

We finish by asking what happens with
k
-graphs in the cases not covered by
Theorem

1
and
Theorem

2
, i.e.
k\in\{3,4\}
.
We phrase our question in terms of gaps.

Question 18
.

Given
k\in\{3,4\}
and arbitrary
C>0
, does there exist
n\in\mathds{N}
, and
n\leqslant a\leqslant R(K^{(k)}_{n})
such that no
k
-graph
H
satisfies
a\leqslant R(H)\leqslant Ca
?

Quantum groups, discrete Magnus expansion, pre-Lie & \& tridendriform algebras
Anastasia Doikou

Abstract
. We review the discrete evolution problem and the corresponding solution as a discrete Dyson series in order to rigorously derive a generalized discrete version of the Magnus expansion. We also systematically derive the discrete analogue of the pre-Lie Magnus expansion and express the elements of the discrete Dyson series in terms of a tridendriform algebra action. In the generic discrete case extra significant terms that are absent in the continuous or the linear discrete case appear in both Dyson and Magnus expansions. Based on the rigorous discrete derivation key links between quantum algebras, tridendriform and pre-Lie algebras are then established. This is achieved by examining tensor realizations of quantum groups, such as the Yangian. We show that these realizations can be expressed in terms of tridendriform and pre-Lie algebras actions. The continuous limit as expected provides the corresponding non-local charges of the Yangian as members of the pre-Lie Magnus expansion.

Definition 2.1
.

A Rota-Baxter algebra is a
k
-vector space
{\cal A}
equipped with a linear map
R:{\cal A}\to{\cal A},
such that
\forall a,b\in{\cal A}

(2.1)

R(a)R(b)=R(R(a)b+aR(b)+\theta ab),

where
\theta\in k
is a fixed parameter.

The map
R
is called a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
\theta
.

Definition 2.2
.

A pre-Lie algebra is a
k
-vector space
{\mathrm{A}}
with a binary operation
\triangleright:(a,b)\mapsto a\triangleright b,
such that it satisfies the pre-Lie identity
\forall a,b,c\in A,

(2.2)

(a\triangleright b)\triangleright c-a\triangleright(b\triangleright c)=(b\triangleright a)\triangleright c-b\triangleright(a\triangleright c).

Analogously, a right pre-Lie algebra with a binary operation
\triangleleft
can be defined with a pre-Lie identity:

(2.3)

(a\triangleleft b)\triangleleft c-a\triangleleft(b\triangleleft c)=(a\triangleleft c)\triangleleft b-a\triangleleft(c\triangleleft b).

In the following Proposition it is shown that Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\theta
can be used to construct a pre-Lie algebra action (see
[
5
,
24
]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
R:{\cal A}\to{\cal A}
be a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\theta
and let
\triangleright
be a binary operation:
(a,b)\mapsto a\triangleright b,

\forall a,b\in{\cal A}
such that

(2.4)

a\triangleright b:=\big[R(a),\ b\big]+\theta ab.

Then the binary operation
\triangleright
satisfies the pre-Lie identity (
2.2
).

Proof.

Using definition (
2.4
) and assuming (in accordance to the cases examined here) that

R(a+b)=R(a)+R(b)
we first show that
R(-x)=R(0)-R(x),
indeed

(2.5)

\displaystyle R(x-x)=R(0)\Rightarrow R(x)+R(-x)=R(0)\Rightarrow R(-x)=R(0)-R(x).

We then explicitly compute:

(2.6)

\displaystyle(x\triangleright y)\triangleright z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(R(x)y-yR(x)+\theta xy)z-zR(R(x)y-yR(x)+\theta xy)

(2.7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta\big(R(x)yz-yR(x)z+\theta xyz\big).

Similarly, using (
2.1
):

(2.8)

\displaystyle x\triangleright(y\triangleright z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(R(x)y+xR(y)+\theta xy)z+zR(R(y)x+yR(x)+\theta yx)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta\Big(R(x)yz-yzR(x)+xR(y)z-xzR(y)+\theta xyz\big)-R(x)zR(y)-R(y)zR(x)

And from the two last expressions we conclude that (recall
R(a+b)=R(a)+R(b)
and
R(-x)=R(0)-R(x)
):

(2.9)

\displaystyle(x\triangleright y)\triangleright z-x\triangleright(y\triangleright z)=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle R(R(x)y+xR(y))z-zR(R(x)y+xR(y))

(2.10)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\theta z\big(R(xy)+R(yx)\big)-\theta\big(yR(x)+xR(y)\big)

(2.11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta\big(yzR(x)+xzR(y)\big)+R(x)zR(y)+R(y)zR(x).

The latter expression is symmetric in

Definition 2.7
.

A
tridendriform algebra

{\cal D}
is a
k
-vector space equipped with three binary operations
\prec,

\succ,

\cdot
and the following axioms
[
36
,
37
]
:

(1)

(a\prec b)\prec c=a\prec(b\prec c+b\succ c+b\cdot c)

(2)

(a\prec b)\succ c=a\succ(b\prec c)

(3)

a\succ(b\succ c)=(a\prec b+a\succ b+a\cdot b)\succ c

(4)

a\cdot(b\cdot c)=(a\cdot b)\cdot c

(5)

(a\succ b)\cdot c=a\succ(b\cdot c)

(6)

(a\prec b)\cdot c=a\cdot(b\succ c)

(7)

(a\cdot b)\prec c=a\cdot(b\prec c).

A
dendriform algebra
is defined by setting the product
\cdot
to zero in the above axioms, consequently the rules of a dendriform algebra are given in terms of axioms (1)-(3) without the
\cdot

Remark 2.8
.

We note that the product
x*y=x\prec y+x\succ y+x\cdot y
is associative
[
37
]
, whereas the product
x\triangleright y=x\succ y-y\prec x+x\cdot y
(
x\triangleleft y=x\prec y-y\succ x+x\cdot y
) defines a pre-Lie algebra
{\mathrm{A}},

\forall x,y\in{\cal D}.
In the case of a dendriform algebra the term
x\cdot y
is not present in both products, i.e.
x*y=x\prec y+x\succ y
and
x\triangleright y=x\succ y-y\prec x
(
x\triangleleft y=x\prec y-y\succ x
).

The binary actions of the (tri)dendriform algebra can be defined
\forall x,y\in{\cal A}
in terms of Rota-Baxter operators as

(2.21)

x\succ y:=R(x)y,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ x\prec y:=xR(y),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ a\cdot b=\theta ab,

which leads to the findings of Proposition
2.3
.

Classical Integrability

Connections between (tri)dendriform, pre-Lie algebras and classical integrable systems and the associated
classical deformed algebras are naturally identified given the findings of the previous subsection.
The key point in the description and construction of classical integrable systems from the Hamiltonian point of view is the existence of a classical matrix
r\in\mbox{End}({\mathbb{C}}^{\cal N}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{\cal N})
that satisfies the classical YBE
[
29
,
48
]
,

(4.25)

\big[r_{12}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}),r_{13}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})\big]+\big[r_{12}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})+r_{13}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3}),r_{23}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})\big]=0.

The classical YBE can be seen as a linearization of the quantum YBE, i.e. we set

R=1+\delta r+{\cal O}(\delta^{2}).
Also, the classical Lax operator
{\mathbb{L}}(\lambda)\in\mbox{End}({\mathbb{C}}^{\cal N})
associated to a discrete space intregrable system satisfies the so called Sklyanin’s bracket
[
29
,
49
,
50
]
(i.e. the classical analogue of the fundamental relation (
4.2
)):

(4.26)

\big\{{\mathbb{L}}_{n}(\lambda_{1})\underset{,}{\otimes}{\mathbb{L}}_{m}(\lambda_{2})\big\}=\big[r(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}),\ {\mathbb{L}}_{n}(\lambda_{1})\otimes{\mathbb{L}}_{m}(\lambda_{2})\big]\delta_{n,m}.

As in the quantum case algebraic quantities in involution exist, due the existence of an
r
-matrix. Indeed, the monodromy matrix
T(\lambda)={\mathbb{L}}_{N}(\lambda)\ldots{\mathbb{L}}_{1}(\lambda)\in\mbox{End}({\mathbb{C}}^{\cal N})
also satisfies Sklyanin’s bracket and hence it is shown that
{\mathfrak{t}}(\lambda)=trT(\lambda)
satisfies

\big\{{\mathfrak{t}}(\lambda),{\mathfrak{t}}(\mu)\big\}=0,
which, given that
{\mathfrak{t}}(\lambda)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\lambda^{-m}{\mathfrak{t}}^{(m)},
leads to a family of quantities in involution
\big\{{\mathfrak{t}}^{(m)},{\mathfrak{t}}^{(k)}\big\}=0,
i.e. classical integrability à la Liouville is shown
[
29
]
.

In the case of the classical
\mathfrak{gl}_{\cal N}
Yangian, the
r
-matrix is given as
r(\lambda)={{\cal P}\over\lambda},
where we recall that
{\cal P}
is the
{\cal N}^{2}\times{\cal N}^{2}
permutation operator. Also we recall, as in the quantum case,
{\mathbb{L}}(\lambda)=1+\sum_{m>1}\lambda^{-m}L^{(m)}
and
L^{(m)}=\sum_{a,b=1}^{\cal N}L_{a,b}e_{a,b}.
By substituting
{\mathbb{L}}
in (
4.26
) the classical Yangian relations are recovered, i.e. the classical analogues of (
4.3
). Everything holds as in the quantum case, but
\big[,\big]\mapsto-\big\{,\big\},
i.e.
L^{(m)}_{a,b}
are commutative objects, and are the generators of the classical Yangian.
T_{a,b}^{(m)}
(non-local charges) are the classical analogues of the coproducts
\Delta^{(N)}(L_{a,b}^{(m)})
.

In the continuum case the Lax operator
{\mathbb{A}}(x,\lambda)\in\mbox{End}({\mathbb{C}}^{\cal N})
satisfies a linear Sklyaninn’s bracket (ultra-local algebra):

(4.27)

\big\{{\mathbb{A}}(x,\lambda_{1})\underset{,}{\otimes}{\mathbb{A}}(y,\lambda_{2})\big\}=\big[r(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}),\ {\mathbb{A}}(x,\lambda_{1})\otimes 1+1\otimes{\mathbb{A}}(y,\lambda_{2})\big]\delta(x-y).

In the case of the Yangian specifically
r(\lambda)={{\cal P}\over\lambda}
and
{\mathbb{A}}(x,\lambda)={1\over\lambda}A(x).
The monodromy matrix in this case is defined as T
(x,\alpha)=\overset{\curvearrowleft}{\mathrm{P}}\text{exp}\big(\int_{0}^{x}{\mathbb{A}}(\xi,\alpha)d\xi\big)
(see also (
1.2
) and Remark 3.6) and satisfies the quadratic relation (
4.26
),
[
29
,
39
]
. Also, the expressions for the continuous Magnus expansion (
1.10
), (
1.12
) and (
2.17
) hold (
\alpha=\lambda^{-1}
).

Abstract
. In this paper, we study harmonic analysis on the affine Poincaré group 𝒫 aff {\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}} , which is a non-unimodular group, and obtain pseudo-differential operators with operator valued symbols. More precisely, we study the boundedness properties of pseudo-differential operators on 𝒫 aff {\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}} . We also provide a necessary and sufficient condition on the operator-valued symbols such that the corresponding pseudo-differential operators are in the class of Hilbert–Schmidt operators. Consequently, we obtain a characterization of the trace class pseudo-differential operators on the Poincaré affine group 𝒫 aff {\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}} , and provide a trace formula for these trace class operators. Finally, we study the Wigner transform, and Weyl transform associated with the operator valued symbol on the Poincaré affine group 𝒫 aff {\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}} .

Introduction

One of the most active branches in modern contemporary mathematics is the theory of pseudo-differential operators. These are the generalizations of partial differential operators, which originated as an essential tool in the study of partial differential equations, numerical analysis, and quantum theory.
Let
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\to\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function, where
\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
is dual of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},
the set of all inequivalent irreducible unitary representations. Then the global pseudo-differential operator

associated with

(T_{\sigma}f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(1.1)

for all
f\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, the Schwartz space on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, provided that the integral exists, where
\widehat{f}
is the Euclidean Fourier transform of
f
. The basic tool to define a pseudo-differential operator is the Fourier inversion formula, which is defined by

in which we insert a suitable symbol
\sigma
on the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
in the Fourier inversion formula to get (
1.1
). These ideas have been implemented to construct the pseudo-differential operators on different groups, such as
\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{S}^{1}
, locally compact abelian groups, compact Lie groups, Heisenberg groups, type I non-unimodular locally compact groups, type I unimodular locally compact groups, affine group, polar affine group, Heisenberg motion group by several authors,

2.
The affine Poincaré Goup

The affine Poincaré group
{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}
is a generalization of the affine group, in fact, it is a complexification of the affine group. This group arises in the study of
2
-dimensional wavelet transform. Similar to four parameter similitude group,
{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}
contains the translations
b
in the image plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, global dilations (zooming in and out by
a>0
), but it has hyperbolic rotations around the origin (
{\vartheta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
). The action on the plane is given by

x=(b,a,{\vartheta})y=a\Lambda_{{\vartheta}}y+b,

where

b\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
a>0
, and
\Lambda_{{\vartheta}}
is the
2\times 2
hyperbolic rotation matrix

\Lambda_{{\vartheta}}=\left(\begin{matrix}\cosh{\vartheta}&\sinh{\vartheta}\\
\sinh{\vartheta}&\cosh{\vartheta}\end{matrix}\right).

(2.1)

A convenient representation of the joint transformation
(b,a,{\vartheta})
is in the form of
3\times 3
matrices

(b,a,{\vartheta})=\left(\begin{matrix}a\Lambda_{\vartheta}&b\\
0^{T}&1\end{matrix}\right),~~~0^{T}=(0,0).

(2.2)

Then the matrix multiplications gives the composition of successive transformations and thus the group law is derived as

\displaystyle(b,a,{\vartheta})\ast(b^{\prime},a^{\prime},{\vartheta}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b+a\Lambda_{{\vartheta}}b^{\prime},aa^{\prime},{\vartheta}+{\vartheta}^{\prime}),

With respect to the operation
\ast
,
{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}
is a non-abelian group in which
({0},1,0)
is the identity element and
(\frac{-1}{a}\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}{b},\frac{1}{a},-{\vartheta})
is the inverse of
({b},a,{\vartheta})
in
{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}
. Also, it can be shown that
{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}
is a non-unimodular group as its left and right Haar measures

d\mu_{L}({b},a,{\vartheta})=\dfrac{d{b}dad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}},~~d\mu_{R}({b},a,{\vartheta})=\dfrac{d{b}dad{\vartheta}}{a},

respectively are different, and hence the modular function is given by
\Delta(b,a,{\vartheta})=\dfrac{1}{a^{2}}
.
Moreover, the affine Poincaré group
{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}
has the structure of a semi-direct product:

{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes(\mathbb{R}_{\ast}^{+}\times\mathrm{SO}(1,1)),

where
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the subgroup of the translations,
\mathbb{R}^{\ast}_{+}
that of dilations, and
\mathrm{SO}(1,1)
of hyperbolic rotations. Topologically, one can write

{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is any one of the four cones:

\displaystyle C_{1}^{1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{1}^{2}>x_{2}^{2},+x_{1}>0\}

(2.3)

\displaystyle C_{2}^{1}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{1}^{2}>x_{2}^{2},-x_{1}>0\}

(2.4)

\displaystyle C_{1}^{2}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{1}^{2}<x_{2}^{2},+x_{1}>0\}

(2.5)

\displaystyle C_{2}^{2}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:x_{1}^{2}<x_{2}^{2},-x_{1}>0\}.

(2.6)

Let us define the Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}
and inverse Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
, by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\varphi)(\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{i\langle\xi;x\rangle}\varphi(x)dx

(2.7)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi)(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-i\langle\xi;x\rangle}({\mathcal{F}}{\varphi})(\xi)d\xi,

(2.8)

for all
\varphi\in S({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, where
\langle x;y\rangle=x_{1}y_{1}-x_{2}y_{2}
is the Minkowski inner product. In this article, we denote
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
are the Minkowski-Fourier transform and inverse Minkowski-Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, respectively.

Remark 1
.

The relation between Euclidean Fourier transform and Minkowski-Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is

(\mathcal{F}\varphi)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\widehat{\varphi}(-\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\varphi)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\check{\varphi}(-\xi_{1},\xi_{2}),

where
\widehat{\varphi}
and
\check{\varphi}
are the Euclidean Fourier transform and inverse Euclidean Fourier transform
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, respectively. The reader can easily verify that the Minkowski-Fourier transform carries almost all properties similar to Euclidean Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, like Plancherel formula, Parseval identity.

Harmonic Analysis on the affine Poincaré Group

Let us denote

(\pi_{i}^{j}(b,a,{\vartheta})\varphi)(x)=ae^{i\langle x;b\rangle}\varphi(a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x),i,j=1,2,

Theorem 3.1
.

\{\pi_{i}^{j}:i,j=1,2\}

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

Proof

\displaystyle(\widehat{f}(\pi_{i}^{j})\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}}f(b,a,{\vartheta})\big(\pi_{i}^{j}(b,a,{\vartheta})(D_{i}^{j}\varphi)\big)(x)\frac{dbdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}}f(b,a,{\vartheta})ae^{i\langle x;b\rangle}(D_{i}^{j}\phi)(a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x)\frac{dbdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}}f(b,a,{\vartheta})ae^{i\langle x;b\rangle}|\langle a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x;a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x)\frac{dbdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}}f(b,a,{\vartheta})ae^{i\langle x;b\rangle}|\langle x;x\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x)\frac{dbdad{\vartheta}}{a}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(\mathcal{F}_{1}f)(x,a,{\vartheta})|\langle x;x\rangle|^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(a\Lambda_{-{\vartheta}}x)\frac{dad{\vartheta}}{a}.

Let

{\vartheta}=\cosh^{-1}\Bigg(\dfrac{\langle x;y\rangle}{\langle x;x\rangle}\times\Bigg(\dfrac{\langle x;x\rangle}{\langle y;y\rangle}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg)

(3.3)

for all
(x,y)\in C_{i}^{j}\times C_{i}^{j}
,
i,j=1,2

\displaystyle\int_{C_{i}^{j}}\int_{C_{i}^{j}}|K_{i,j}^{f}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy

where
\varphi_{i}^{j}=\varphi\chi_{C_{i}^{j}},i,j=1,2.

Theorem 3.5

(Plancherel Formula)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}})
. Then for all
\varphi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
, the operator

(\widehat{f}(\pi)\varphi)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}K^{f}(x,y)\varphi(y)dy,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},

where

K^{f}(x,y)=\begin{cases}K_{1,1}^{f}(x,y),(x,y)\in C_{1}^{1}\times C_{1}^{1},\\
K_{2,1}^{f}(x,y),(x,y)\in C_{2}^{1}\times C_{2}^{1},\\
K_{1,2}^{f}(x,y),(x,y)\in C_{1}^{2}\times C_{1}^{2},\\
K_{2,2}^{f}(x,y),(x,y)\in C_{2}^{2}\times C_{2}^{2},\\
0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

, are defined in (

and moreover,

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{{\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}}},S_{2})}=\|\widehat{f}(\pi_{1}^{1})\|^{2}_{S_{2}}+\|\widehat{f}(\pi_{2}^{1})\|^{2}_{S_{2}}+\|\widehat{f}(\pi_{1}^{2})\|^{2}_{S_{2}}+\|\widehat{f}(\pi_{2}^{2})\|^{2}_{S_{2}}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}})}.

Proof.

Using the Plancherel formula for Minkowski-Fourier transform, we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|K^{f}(x,y)\right|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{1}^{1}}\int_{C_{1}^{1}}|K_{1,1}^{f}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy+\int_{C_{2}^{1}}\int_{C_{2}^{1}}|K_{2,1}^{f}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{C_{1}^{2}}\int_{C_{1}^{2}}|K_{1,2}^{f}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy+\int_{C_{2}^{2}}\int_{C_{2}^{2}}|K_{2,2}^{f}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{1}^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|(\mathcal{F}_{1}f)(x,a,{\vartheta})\right|^{2}\dfrac{dxdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}+\int_{C_{2}^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|(\mathcal{F}_{1}f)(x,a,{\vartheta})\right|^{2}\dfrac{dxdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle+\int_{C_{1}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|(\mathcal{F}_{1}f)(x,a,{\vartheta})\right|^{2}\dfrac{dxdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}+\int_{C_{2}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|(\mathcal{F}_{1}f)(x,a,{\vartheta})\right|^{2}\dfrac{dxdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|(\mathcal{F}_{1}f)(x,a,{\vartheta})\right|^{2}\dfrac{dxdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left|f(x,a,{\vartheta})\right|^{2}\dfrac{dxdad{\vartheta}}{a^{3}}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{P_{\textrm{aff}}}})}.

Note that, we can write

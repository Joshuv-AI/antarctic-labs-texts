Singular backward stochastic Volterra integral equations in infinite dimensional spaces
Tianxiao Wang, Mengliang Zheng

Abstract
In this paper, the notion of singular backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (singular BSVIEs for short) in infinite dimensional space is introduced, and the corresponding well-posedness is carefully established. A class of singularity conditions are proposed, which not only cover that of fractional kernel, Volterra Heston model kernel, completely monotone kernels, to mention a few, but also happen to be used in the forward stochastic Volterra integral with new conclusions arising. Motivated by mathematical physics problem such as the viscoelasticity/thermoviscoelasticity of materials, heat conduction in materials with memory, optimal control problems of abstract stochastic Volterra integral equations (including fractional stochastic evolution equations and stochastic evolutionary integral equations) are presented. At last, our BSVIEs are surprisingly used in maximum principle of controlled stochastic delay evolution equations. One advantage of this new standpoint is that the final cost functional can naturally depend on the past state for the first time.



Keywords.
singular kernel, backward stochastic Volterra integral equations, forward stochastic Volterra integral equations, stochastic delay evolution equations, stochastic evolutionary integral equations, maximum principle.

As applications, we study the optimal control problem for singular forward stochastic Volterra integral equations (including fractional stochastic evolution equations and controlled stochastic evolutionary integral equations as special cases)
and use linear singular BSVIEs as the adjoint equation to derive the Pontragin maximum principle. Eventually, we give another interesting and useful applications to optimal control problem of stochastic delay evolution equation where the terminal cost is allowed to depend on the past of the state.

At this moment, let us list the main novelties of the current paper as follows:

•

Singularity: In contrast with the existing literature on BSVIEs, we give some new and deeper treatment on the singularity of the generator. First, we carefully impose different singular assumptions on each element of
g
in (
1.1
) (see (H3.1) and Remark
3.7
) to highlight their differences. Second, the corresponding singular kernels we propose is non-convolution which enable us to cover other Rough, fractional kernels, (see Remark
3.10
). Third, we apply such singularities idea to the forward systems and obtain their well-posedness that seems to be new to our best knowledge.

•

Volterra system feature: To show the advantage of Volterra system, we present one optimal control problem of controlled stochastic delay evolution equations. More precisely, to obtain the maximum principle of optimal controls, we rewrite the variational equation as a forward SVIEs and thus use linear BSVIEs as the adjoint equation instead of anticipated BSDEs. One advantage is that the final cost can easily and naturally depend on the past of the state, which in some sense overcome the limitation of anticipated BSDEs in such general scenario.

•

Infinite dimensionality: The infinite dimensional feature of equations (
1.1
) enables us to apply the current consideration to other various stochastic PDEs arising from mathematical physics (say, the issues of viscoelasticity, thermoviscoelasticity, the incompressible fluids, heat conduction in materials with memory), such as the fractional stochastic evolution equations, stochastic delay evolution equation, stochastic evolutionary integral equations and so on.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects some preliminary results. We give the well-posedness of singular BSVIEs in section 3. As applications, we study two classes of optimal control problems with convex control regions in Section 4, respectively. In Section 5, we give a concluding remark. And at last, some comparison results are presented in the Appendix.

2) for any

, there exists a finite partition
\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{m}
of
\left(0,T\right)
with
0=U_{0}<U_{1}<\cdots<U_{m}=T
such that

\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in\left(U_{i},U_{i+1}\right)}\left(\int_{t}^{U_{i+1}}|f(t,s)|^{2}ds\right)^{1\over 2}<\varepsilon

for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
.

The space
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta,\mathbb{R}_{+})
can be defined similarly. For notation simplicity, in the following we omit the range space
\mathbb{R}_{+}
without confusion, i.e.

L^{2}(\Delta^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}L^{2}(\Delta^{*};\mathbb{R}_{+}),L^{2}(\Delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}L^{2}(\Delta;\mathbb{R}_{+}),\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\mathscr{L}^{2}\left(\Delta^{*};\mathbb{R}_{+}\right),\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta,\mathbb{R}_{+}).

The following example shows the relation between the two conditions in
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*})
, from which we see that neither 1) nor 2) is redundant and can be dropped.

Example 2.1
.

(i) Inspired by Shi and Wang
[
42
]
, let
\displaystyle f(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{T-t}},\quad(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}
. It is easy to see that

\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{t}^{T}f(t,s)^{2}ds=2<\infty.

Therefore, condition 1) in the definition is fulfilled.
However, for any partition
\left\{U_{i}\right\}_{i=0}^{m}
of
\left(0,T\right)
with
0=U_{0}<U_{1}<\cdots<U_{m}=T
, by the choice of
f(\cdot,\cdot)
,

\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in(U_{m-1},T)}\int_{t}^{T}f(t,s)^{2}ds=2,

which shows the condition 2) does not hold any more. In other words, condition 1) can not imply condition 2), and hence condition 2) can not be removed.

(ii) On the other hand, let us look at
f(t,s)=f_{1}(s-t),(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}
, with
f_{1}\in L^{2}(0,\frac{T}{2})\setminus L^{2}(0,T)
. In this case, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
and a partition
\{U_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
of
\left(0,T\right)
with
0=U_{0}<U_{1}<\cdots<U_{m}=T
, whenever
\max\limits_{i\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}}(U_{i+1}-U_{i})<\min\{\delta,\frac{T}{2}\}
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\int_{t}^{U_{i+1}}|f(t,s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\int_{0}^{U_{i+1}-t}|f_{1}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{U_{i+1}-U_{i}}|f_{1}(s)|^{2}ds<\varepsilon,\quad\forall i\in\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\}.

However,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{t}^{T}|f(t,s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{0}^{T-t}|f_{1}(s)|^{2}ds=\int_{0}^{T}|f_{1}(s)|^{2}ds=\infty,

which shows that condition 1) does not hold. In other words, condition 2) does not imply condition 1), and hence condition 1) can not be dropped.

The following gives example in
L^{2}(\Delta^{*})
and
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*})
respectively, from which we can clearly see the difference between these two spaces.

Example 2.2
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)
, let

k^{\alpha,\beta}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\frac{1}{(s-t)^{\alpha}t^{\beta}},\quad(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}.

It is clear that when
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, it holds that

\int_{t}^{T}\Big(\frac{1}{(s-t)^{\alpha}t^{\beta}}\Big)^{2}ds=\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}\frac{(T-t)^{1-2\alpha}}{t^{2\beta}}.

From this, we have that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &k^{\alpha,\beta}\in L^{2}(\Delta^{*}),\qquad\text{ if }\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}),\beta\in[0,\frac{1}{2});\\
&k^{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*}),\qquad\text{ if }\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}),\beta=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In the literature, such
k
is referred to as a doubly singular kernel.

Here and next
C
is a generic positive constant which may be different from line to line.

3
Singular backward stochastic Volterra systems

In this section, we are devoted to the study of singular backward stochastic Volterra integral equations. In Section 3.1, we discuss its well-posedness under quite weak singular conditions. In Section 3.2, we apply the aforementioned singular conditions to the forward stochastic Volterra system. In Section 3.3, we give several different examples to show the usefulness of the previous results.

3.1
Well-posedness of singular BSVIEs

In this section, we investigate the well-posedness of BSVIE (
1.1
).
For readers’ convenience, we rewrite it here,

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,Y(s),Z(t,s),Z(s,t))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T],

(3.1)

where the free term
\psi(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(0,T;H)
and the generator
g
are given. A pair of
\left(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot)\right)\in{\cal H}^{2}[0,T]
(Recalling the definition of
{\cal H}^{2}[0,T]
in Section 2) is referred to as an
adapted
M
-solution
of BSVIE (
3.1
) if (
3.1
) holds in the usual Itô’s sense for almost all
t\in[0,T]
and the following holds:

Y(t)=\mathbb{E}Y(t)+\int_{0}^{t}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad\hbox{\rm a.e.}\;\,t\in[0,T].

We introduce the following assumptions.

(H3.1)
Let
g:\Delta^{*}\times H\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\times\Omega\mapsto H
be measurable such that
s\mapsto g(t,s,y,z_{1},z_{2})
is
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable for all
(t,y,z_{1},z_{2})\in[0,T]\times H\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}
and
g(t,s,0,0,0)=0
. Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle|g(t,s,y,z_{1},z_{2})-g(t,s,\hat{y},\hat{z}_{1},\hat{z}_{2})|_{H}\leqslant

\displaystyle L_{y}(t,s)|y-\hat{y}|_{H}+L_{z_{1}}(t,s)|z_{1}-\hat{z}_{1}|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}+L_{z_{2}}(t,s)|z_{2}-\hat{z}_{2}|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}},

\displaystyle\forall(t,s,y,z_{1},z_{2}),(t,s,\hat{y},\hat{z}_{1},\hat{z}_{2})\in\Delta^{*}\times H\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0},

where
L_{y}\in L^{2}(\Delta^{*}),L_{z_{2}}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*})
and
\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{1}}(t,s)^{2}ds<\infty
.

Remark 3.1
.

In both Anh et al
[
7
]
and Yong
[
61
]
, the authors made similar Lipschitz conditions as that in (H3.1) and assumed that all the coeffcients satisfy simultaneously that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{t}^{T}f(t,s)^{2+\varepsilon}ds<\infty,\quad f\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}L_{y},L_{z_{1}},L_{z_{2}},\text{ for some }\varepsilon>0.

(3.2)

In contrast, we give a deeper exploration of the singularities for the three coefficients, and impose appropriate assumptions among them. In fact, the assumption of
L_{y}
is the weakest while that of
L_{z_{2}}
is the strongest. The condition of
L_{z_{1}}
is in between the previous two cases. We point out that even for the
strongest case, the following example shows that it is weaker than that in Anh et al
[
7
]
and Yong
[
61
]
.
Let

h(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}\ln s},\quad s\in(0,T).

After some basic calculations, we can verify
\displaystyle h(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})\setminus\bigcup_{\varepsilon>0}L^{2+\varepsilon}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}).
Let

L_{y}(t,s)=L_{z_{1}}(t,s)=L_{z_{2}}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}h(s-t),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta^{*}.

Then
f(\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*})\subset L^{2}(\Delta^{*})
for
f\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}L_{y},L_{z_{1}},L_{z_{2}}
, but (
3.2
) does not hold anymore.

Remark 3.2
.

The case of
g(t,s,0,0,0)\neq 0
can be treated as follows. Define a new free term

\tilde{\psi}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,0,0,0)ds,\quad t\in[0,T].

We can prove
\tilde{\psi}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(0,T;H)
if
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|g(t,s,0,0,0)|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt<\infty
is provided.

Now we give the main result in this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(H3.1)
hold. Then for any
\psi(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(0,T;H)
, (
3.1
) admits a unique adapted M-solution in
{\cal H}^{2}[0,T]
. Moreover, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

Let
\bar{g}
also satisfy
(H3.1)
,
\bar{\psi}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(0,T;H)
, and
(\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot,\cdot))\in{\cal H}^{2}[0,T]
be the adapted
M
-solution of (
3.1
) with
g
and
\psi(\cdot)
replaced by
\bar{g}
and
\bar{\psi}(\cdot)
, respectively. Then the following stability estimate holds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}|Y(t)-\bar{Y}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|Z(t,s)-\bar{Z}(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t)-\bar{\psi}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|g(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t))-\bar{g}(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t))|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt\Big\}.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we give some lemmas which will be uesful later. For any
R,S\in[0,T)
, we consider the following
H
-valued stochastic integral equation:

\lambda(t,r)=\psi(t)+\int_{r}^{T}h(t,s,\mu(t,s))ds-\int_{r}^{T}\mu(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[S,T],\;r\in[R,T],

(3.3)

where
h:[S,T]\times[R,T]\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\times\Omega\mapsto H
is given. The unknown process is
(\lambda(\cdot,\cdot),\mu(\cdot,\cdot))
, for which
(\lambda(t,\cdot),\mu(t,\cdot))
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted for all
t\in[S,T]
. We
may regard the above as

\bullet
a family of infinite dimensional BSDEs on
[R,T]
, parameterized by
t\in[S,T]
;

\bullet
a family of infinite dimensional stochastic Fredholm-type integral equations (SFIEs, for short) on
[S,T]
, parameterized by
r\in[R,T]
.

We introduce the following assumptions concerning the generator
h
of (
3.3
).

(H3.2)
Let
R,S\in[0,T)
, and
h:[S,T]\times[R,T]\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\times\Omega\mapsto H
be measurable such that
s\mapsto h(t,s,z)

\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable for all

\int_{S}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{R}^{T}|h(t,s,0)|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt<\infty.

Moreover, the following holds:

|h(t,s,z)-h(t,s,\bar{z})|_{H}\leqslant L(t,s)|z-\bar{z}|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}},\quad(t,s)\in[S,T]\times[R,T],\;z,\bar{z}\in{\cal L}^{0}_{2},\;\hbox{\rm a.s.},

where
L:[S,T]\times[R,T]\to[0,\infty)
is a determinitic function such that

\sup_{t\in[S,T]}\int_{R}^{T}L(t,s)^{2}ds<\infty.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(H3.2)
hold. Then for any
\psi(\cdot)\in L_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{2}(S,T;H)
, (
3.3
) admits a unique adapted solution

(\lambda(t,\cdot),\mu(t,\cdot))\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([R,T];H))\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(R,T;{\cal L}_{2}^{0})

for almost all
t\in[S,T]
. Moreover, if
\bar{h}
also satisfies
(H3.2)
,
\bar{\psi}(\cdot)\in L_{{\cal F}_{T}}^{2}(S,T;H)
, and

(\bar{\lambda}(t,\cdot),\bar{\mu}(t,\cdot))\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega;C([R,T];H))\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(R,T;{\cal L}_{2}^{0})

) with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\sup_{r\in[R,T]}|\lambda(t,r)-\bar{\lambda}(t,r)|_{H}^{2}+\int_{R}^{T}|\mu(t,s)-\bar{\mu}(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi(t)-\bar{\psi}(t)|_{H}^{2}+\Big(\int_{R}^{T}|h(t,s,\mu(t,s))-\bar{h}(t,s,\mu(t,s))|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}\right\},\quad\hbox{\rm a.e.}\;t\in[S,T].

Proof.

Since the proof is similar to the finite dimensional case in
[
61

Next, let us look at two special cases of the above result. First, let

First we define

\left\|(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))\right\|_{{\cal M}^{2}[0,T]}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\Big)dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Step 1: Fix a
(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]
with
S
undertermined, let us consider the following equation:

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}g(t,s,y(s),Z(t,s),z(s,t))ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[S,T],

(3.6)

for any
\psi(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(S,T;H)
. By Corollary
3.6

3.6

M

(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\right\}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|g(t,s,y(s),0,z(s,t))|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}+\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\Big[L_{y}(t,s)|y(s)|_{H}+L_{z_{2}}(t,s)|z(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\Big]ds\Big)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{t}^{T}|y(s)|_{H}^{2}ds+\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{t}^{T}|z(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\right\}.

[S,T]
w.r.t.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{S}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\equiv\left\|(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))\right\|_{{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]}^{2}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant C

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{S}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}dsdt\cdot\int_{S}^{T}|y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\sup_{t\in[S,T]}\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}|z(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C

\displaystyle\Big(\|L_{y}(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta^{*}[S,T])}+\|L_{z_{2}}(\cdot,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*}[S,T])}\Big)\cdot\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\|(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))\|_{{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]}^{2}\Big].

Thus we define a map
\Theta:{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]\mapsto{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]
by

\Theta(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))=(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot)),\qquad\forall(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{2}[S,T].

Now we show that the map
\Theta
is contractive for some
0\leqslant S<T
. Indeed, for another
(\bar{y}(\cdot),\bar{z}(\cdot,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]
and
\Theta(\bar{y}(\cdot),\bar{z}(\cdot,\cdot))=(\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot,\cdot))
. By the stability estimate in Corollary

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}|Y(t)-\bar{Y}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}|Z(t,s)-\bar{Z}(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}|g(t,s,y(s),Z(t,s),z(s,t))-g(t,s,\bar{y}(s),Z(t,s),\bar{z}(s,t))|_{H}ds\Big]^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)|y(s)-\bar{y}(s)|_{H}ds\Big]^{2}dt+C\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)|z(s,t)-\bar{z}(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}ds\Big]^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{t}^{T}|y(s)-\bar{y}(s)|_{H}^{2}ds\Big]dt

\displaystyle+C\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big[\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{t}^{T}|z(s,t)-\bar{z}(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\Big]dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}dsdt\cdot\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}|y(s)-\bar{y}(s)|_{H}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+C\sup_{t\in(S,T)}\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\int_{S}^{t}|z(t,s)-\bar{z}(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big[\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}dsdt+\sup_{t\in(S,T)}\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\Big]\cdot\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}|y(t)-\bar{y}(t)|_{H}^{2}ds.

Recall that
L_{y}\in L^{2}(\Delta^{*})
and
L_{z_{2}}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta^{*})
. With the above
C
, there exists a partition
\{U_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
of
[0,T]
with
0=U_{0}<U_{1}<\cdots<U_{m}=T
such that

C\Big[\int_{U_{i}}^{U_{i+1}}\int_{t}^{U_{i+1}}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}dsdt+\sup_{t\in(U_{i},U_{i+1})}\int_{t}^{U_{i+1}}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{2},\quad\forall i=0,1,\cdots,m-1.

(3.10)

In other words, if we choose
S=U_{m-1}
, then

C\Big[\int_{S}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}dsdt+\sup_{t\in(S,T)}\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\Big]\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, the map
\Theta
admits a unique fixed point
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))\in{\cal M}^{2}[S,T]
which is the unique solution of (
3.1
) over
[U_{m-1},T]
. This step determines the values
(Y(t),Z(t,s))
for
(t,s)\in[U_{m-1},T]\times[U_{m-1},T]
. By (
3.8
) and (
3.10
), we have the following estimate

\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

(3.11)

Step 2: We determine the values
Z(t,s)
of
Z(\cdot,\cdot)
for
(t,s)\in[U_{m-1},T]\times[U_{m-2},U_{m-1}]
by means of martingale representation theorem, i.e.

\mathbb{E}[Y(t)|{\cal F}_{U_{m-1}}]=\mathbb{E}[Y(t)|{\cal F}_{U_{m-2}}]+\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[U_{m-1},T].

So

\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\leqslant\mathbb{E}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2},\quad t\in[U_{m-1},T].

By (
3.11
) and integrating the above inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

(3.12)

Recall that, we have determined
(Y(t),Z(t,s))
for
(t,s)\in[U_{m-1},T]\times[U_{m-2},T]
up to now. Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we get

\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

(3.13)

Step 3: For
(t,s)\in[U_{m-2},U_{m-1}]\times[U_{m-1},T]
, we know that the values
Y(s)
and
Z(s,t)
are determined by Step 2. In this step, we determine the value of
Z(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in[U_{m-2},U_{m-1}]\times[U_{m-1},T]
by solving a stochastic Fredholm integral equation, i.e.

\psi^{U_{m-1}}(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}g^{U_{m-1}}(t,s,Z(t,s))ds-\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[U_{m-2},{U_{m-1}}],

(3.14)

where

g^{U_{m-1}}(t,s,z)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}g(t,s,Y(s),z,Z(s,t)),\quad(t,s,z)\in[U_{m-2},U_{m-1}]\times[U_{m-1},T]\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}.

Thanks to Corollary
3.5
, (
3.14
) has a unique adapted solution

(\psi^{U_{m-1}}(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))\in L_{{\cal F}_{U_{m-1}}}^{2}(U_{m-2},{U_{m-1}};H)\times L^{2}(U_{m-2},{U_{m-1}};L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}({U_{m-1}},T;{\cal L}_{2}^{0})),

and similar to (
3.8
), it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi^{U_{m-1}}|_{H}^{2}+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}+\Big(\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|g(t,s,Y(s),0,Z(s,t))|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{|\psi(t)|^{2}+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Y(s)|_{H}^{2}ds+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2}ds\cdot\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Z(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\right\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}\left\{|\psi^{U_{m-1}}|_{H}^{2}+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\right\}dt

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}\int_{U_{m-1}}^{T}|Z(s,t)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

The last inequality holds due to (
3.13
).

Step 4: So far, we have uniquely determined

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Y(t),\qquad t\in[U_{m-1},T],\\
&Z(t,s),\qquad(t,s)\in([U_{m-1},T]\times[U_{m-2},T])\cup([U_{m-2},U_{m-1}]\times[U_{m-1},T]).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.16)

Now, we consider

Y(t)=\psi^{U_{m-1}}(t)+\int_{t}^{U_{m-1}}g(t,s,Y(s),Z(t,s),Z(s,t))ds-\int_{t}^{U_{m-1}}Z(t,s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[U_{m-2},U_{m-1}].

(3.17)

Since
\psi^{U_{m-1}}(\cdot)
is
{{\cal F}_{U_{m-1}}}
-measurable, (
3.17
) is a BSVIE over
[{U_{m-2}},{U_{m-1}}]
. Hence, recalling the inequality (
3.10
) (by choosing
U_{i}=U_{m-2}
and
U_{i+1}=U_{m-1}
) and by the similar procedures in Step 1, we are able to show that (
3.17
) is solvable on
[{U_{m-2}},{U_{m-1}}]
. Moreover, by (
3.15
), it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{U_{m-1}}|\psi^{U_{m-1}}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

This solvability determines
(Y(t),Z(t,s))
for
(t,s)\in[{U_{m-2}},{U_{m-1}}]\times[{U_{m-2}},{U_{m-1}}]
. As a consequence, we obtain the unique solvability of BSVIE (
3.1
) on
[U_{m-2},T]
and the following estimate holds by combining (
3.13
), (
3.15
), (
3.18
),

\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}|Y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}|Z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{U_{m-2}}^{T}|\psi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt.

By the similar procedures, we can complete the proof by induction.

At last, we give the stability estimate. Suppose that
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
and
(\bar{Y}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot,\cdot))
are adapted
M
-solution of (
3.1
) corresponding to
(g,\psi)
and
(\bar{g},\bar{\psi})
respectively. Denote

\hat{Y}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}Y(t)-\bar{Y}(t),\quad\hat{Z}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}Z(t,s)-\bar{Z}(t,s).

Observe that
\hat{Y}(\cdot)
satisfies the following BSVIE,

\hat{Y}(t)=\hat{\psi}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}\hat{g}(t,s,\hat{Y}(s),\hat{Z}(t,s),\hat{Z}(s,t))ds-\int_{t}^{T}\hat{Z}(t,s)dW(s),

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{\psi}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\psi(t)-\bar{\psi}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}[g(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t))-\bar{g}(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t))]ds,\\
&\hat{g}(t,s,y,z_{1},z_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}g(t,s,y+\bar{Y}(s),z_{1}+\bar{Z}(t,s),z_{2}+\bar{Z}(s,t))-g(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t)).\end{aligned}\right.

It’s easy to check that the generator
\hat{g}
satisfies
assumption
(H3.1)
and the following stability estimate holds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{Y}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{Z}(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt\right\}\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\hat{\psi}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\psi(t)-\bar{\psi}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|g(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t))-\bar{g}(t,s,\bar{Y}(s),\bar{Z}(t,s),\bar{Z}(s,t))|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt\Big\}.

Remark 3.7
.

As to the above proof, we have the following remarks to point out.

(i)

In contrast with
[
61
]
, it is worthy mentioning some technology differences due to the coefficients’ stronger singularity in the current paper (see e.g. Remark
3.1
). While the author in
[
61
]
estimated the Lips. terms in the same way by Hölder inequality, here we considerably relax the coefficients
L_{y},L_{z_{1}},L_{z_{2}}
and treat them differently. In fact, for the
L_{z_{1}}
, we rely on the BSDEs result, while for
L_{y}
,
L_{z_{2}}
, we use the singularities similar to that in Hamaguchi–Wang
[
19
]
.

(ii)

The proof of Theorem
3.3
seems to be lengthy and technical.
One may consider it from a new point by defining an equivalent norm on the solution space as follows (see also
[
42
]
for instance),

\left\|(y(\cdot),z(\cdot,\cdot))\right\|_{{\cal M}^{2,\beta}[0,T]}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\beta t}|y(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\beta t}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}|z(t,s)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\Big)dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

However, in this case, the assumptions on
L_{y},L_{z_{2}}
need to be strengthened, i.e., there exists some
\varepsilon>0
, such that

\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)^{2(1+\varepsilon)}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}}dt+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{t}^{T}L_{z_{2}}(t,s)^{2(1+\varepsilon)}ds<\infty.

This would end up with the essentially same singularity as that in
[
61
]
.

(iii)

In
[
18
]
, the author considered an infinite horizon backward stochastic Volterra integral equations with convolution-type singular kernel.
Even when their system reduces to the finite interval case, it seems that their developed method is not well applicable to our scenario.

3.2
Well-posedness of singular forward SVIEs

In Section 3.1, we have proved the well-posedness of the singular backward stochastic Volterra system (
3.1
) in the sense of adapted M-solution. It is found that the aforementioned singular assumption can be well fit into the forward system as follows:

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}A(t,s,X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}B(t,s,X(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

(3.19)

In this part, we present the solvability of the above (
3.19
) under the following assumptions.

(H3.3)
Let maps
A:\Delta\times H\times\Omega\mapsto H
,
B:\Delta\times H\times\Omega\mapsto{\cal L}_{2}^{0}
be measurable. For each
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times H,s\mapsto\left(A(t,s,x),B(t,s,x)\right)
is
\mathbb{F}
-adapted on
[0,t]
. And,

A(t,s,0)=0,\text{ and }B(t,s,0)=0\text{\quad for \hbox{\rm a.e.}\; $(t,s)\in\Delta,\;\hbox{\rm a.s.}$}

Moreover,
there are
K_{1}\in L^{2}(\Delta)
and
K_{2}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta)
such that for any
(t,s)\in\Delta
and
x,y\in H
, it holds that

\displaystyle|A(t,s,x)-A(t,s,y)|_{H}

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{1}(t,s)|x-y|_{H},

\displaystyle|B(t,s,x)-B(t,s,y)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{2}(t,s)|x-y|_{H}.

Remark 3.8
.

The case with nonzero
A(t,s,0)
and
B(t,s,0)
can be treated as follows. As a matter of fact, suppose that
A
and
B
satisfy the Lipschitz condition in Assumption
(H3.1)
, and

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|A(t,s,0)|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt<\infty,\quad\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}|B(t,s,0)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt<\infty.

We can rewrite (
3.19
) as follows:

\displaystyle X(t)=

\displaystyle\varphi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}A(t,s,0)ds+\int_{0}^{t}B(t,s,0)dW(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{A}(t,s,X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{B}(t,s,X(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

where

\tilde{A}(t,s,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}A(t,s,x)-A(t,s,0),\quad\tilde{B}(t,s,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}B(t,s,x)-B(t,s,0).

Clearly,

\tilde{A}(t,s,0)=0,\quad\tilde{B}(t,s,0)=0,

and
\widetilde{A},\widetilde{B}
satisfy the assumption
(H3.3)
.
Let

\tilde{\varphi}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\varphi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}A(t,s,0)ds+\int_{0}^{t}B(t,s,0)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

be the new free term. Moreover, we can prove
\tilde{\varphi}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
if
\varphi(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(H3.3)
hold. Then for any
\varphi(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
, the equation (
3.19
) admits a unique solution
X(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

\left\|X(\cdot)\right\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)}\leqslant C\left\|\varphi(\cdot)\right\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)}.

(3.20)

Let
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
satisfy
(H3.3)
,
\varphi^{\prime}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
, and
X^{\prime}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
be the solution of SVIE (
3.19
) corresponding to
(\varphi^{\prime},A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|X(t)-X^{\prime}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\Big\{\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[|\varphi(t)-\varphi^{\prime}(t)|_{H}^{2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{t}|A(t,s,X^{\prime}(s))-A^{\prime}(t,s,X^{\prime}(s))|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}|B(t,s,X^{\prime}(s))-B^{\prime}(t,s,X^{\prime}(s))|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}ds\Big]dt\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

We use the Banach fixed point theorem to give the existence and uniqueness of the adapted solution of (
3.19
).

Step 1: For any
S\in(0,T]
and

x(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,S;H)
, we define

{\cal S}[x(\cdot)](t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\varphi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}A(t,s,x(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}B(t,s,x(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[0,S].

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}|{\cal S}[x(\cdot)](t)|_{H}^{2}dt

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leqslant C

\displaystyle\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}|\varphi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)|x(s)|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}|x(s)|_{H}^{2}dsdt\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C

\displaystyle\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}|\varphi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{S}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)^{2}dsdt\cdot\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}|x(s)|_{H}^{2}ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}\int_{s}^{S}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}dt\cdot|x(s)|_{H}^{2}ds\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C

\displaystyle\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}|\varphi(t)|_{H}^{2}dt+\left(\|K_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\Delta)}+\|K_{2}\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta)}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{S}|x(t)|_{H}^{2}dt\Big].

Hence,
{\cal S}
maps
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,S;H)
to itself.

Step 2: Given
T_{1}\in(0,T]
to be determined later.
We prove that the above defined
{\cal S}
is contractive on the interval
[0,T_{1}]
.
For any
x(\cdot),y(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T_{1};H)
, thanks to the Hölder inequality and Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal S}[x(\cdot)](t)-{\cal S}[y(\cdot)](t)\right\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T_{1};H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)|x(s)-y(s)|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}|x(s)-y(s)|_{H}^{2}dsdt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)^{2}dsdt\cdot\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}|x(s)-y(s)|_{H}^{2}ds+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\int_{s}^{T_{1}}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}dt\cdot|x(s)-y(s)|_{H}^{2}ds.

By the definitions of
K_{1},K_{2}
, there exists a partition
\{T_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
of
[0,T]
with
0=T_{0}<T_{1}<\cdots<T_{m}=T
such that

\int_{T_{i}}^{T_{i+1}}\int_{T_{i}}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)^{2}dsdt+\sup_{s\in(T_{i},T_{i+1})}\int_{s}^{T_{i+1}}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}dt\leqslant\frac{1}{4},\quad\forall i=0,1,\cdots,m-1.

(3.22)

In particular, take
i=0
, i.e.

\int_{0}^{T_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t,s)^{2}dsdt+\sup_{s\in(0,T_{1})}\int_{s}^{T_{1}}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}dt\leqslant\frac{1}{4}.

Hence,
{\cal S}
is contractive on
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T_{1};H)
. Therefore (
3.19
) admits a unique solution
X_{1}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T_{1};H)
on
[0,T_{1}]
. Further, by (
3.21
) and noting (
3.22
),
X(\cdot)
satisfies the estimate (
3.20
) with
T
replaced by
T_{1}
.

Step 3:
Next, we rewrite (
3.19
) as follows:

X(t)=\hat{\varphi}(t)+\int_{T_{1}}^{t}A(t,s,X(s))ds+\int_{T_{1}}^{t}B(t,s,X(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[T_{1},T],

where

\hat{\varphi}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\varphi(t)+\int_{0}^{T_{1}}A(t,s,X_{1}(s))ds+\int_{0}^{T_{1}}B(t,s,X_{1}(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[T_{1},T].

It is straightforward to verify
\hat{\varphi}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(T_{1},T;H)
. For any
x(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(T_{1},T_{2};H)
, define

{\cal S}[x(\cdot)](t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\hat{\varphi}(t)+\int_{T_{1}}^{t}A(t,s,x(s))ds+\int_{T_{1}}^{t}B(t,s,x(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[T_{1},T_{2}].

Then by similar procedures in Step 1–2 and noting (
3.22
), we can obtain the unique solution of (
3.19
) on the interval
[T_{1},T_{2}]
.
By repeating the above procedures, we have the solution on the whole interval
[0,T]
. Moreover, the estimate (
3.20
) holds.

Observe that
\bar{X}(\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}X(\cdot)-X^{\prime}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
is the solution of the following SVIE

\bar{X}(t)=\bar{\varphi}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{A}(t,s,\bar{X}(s))\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\bar{B}(t,s,\bar{X}(s))\mathrm{d}W(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

where

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\varphi(t)-\varphi^{\prime}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\left\{A\left(t,s,X^{\prime}(s)\right)-A^{\prime}\left(t,s,X^{\prime}(s)\right)\right\}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\left\{B\left(t,s,X^{\prime}(s)\right)-B^{\prime}\left(t,s,X^{\prime}(s)\right)\right\}\mathrm{d}W(s),

\displaystyle\bar{A}(t,s,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}A\left(t,s,x+X^{\prime}(s)\right)-A\left(t,s,X^{\prime}(s)\right),\quad\bar{B}(t,s,x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}B\left(t,s,x+X^{\prime}(s)\right)-B\left(t,s,X^{\prime}(s)\right).

Note that
\bar{\varphi}\in{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)}
,
\bar{A}
and
\bar{B}
satisfy
(H3.3)
with the Lipschitz functions
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
. Therefore, by estimate (
3.20
), we have

\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\bar{X}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant C\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\bar{\varphi}(t)|_{H}^{2}dt\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies the stability estimate.

The authors in
[
19
]
gave the well-posedness of linear SVIEs by the method of continuation and discrete Gronwall inequality. In contrast, here we prefer to show the proof by induction directly. We also make comparisons with two important papers
[
28
,
63
]
in the Appendix.

Remark 3.10
.

To conclude this subsection, we point out that our singularity on the forward SVIEs can cover several important cases.
Here are some of them.

•

Let us consider the fractional singular kernel of the form

3.3

In this subsection, we present several examples on singular backward stochastic Volterra integral equations. The first one shows a particular case of BSVIE whose singularity

Example 3.11
.

Let us consider BSVIE (

) where the coefficient

takes

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle L_{y}(t,s)=\frac{1}{(s-t)^{1-\beta}},\ \ (t,s)\in\Delta^{*},\ \ \beta\in(0,1).\end{array}

On the one hand, to keep

as in (H3.1),

\beta\in(0,1)
.
In fact, in this case, we can borrow the following Young’s convolution inequality (by slightly modifying the proof of Corollary 2.2 in
[

\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\varphi(s)}{(s-t)^{1-\beta}}ds\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{p}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\Big(\frac{T^{1-r(1-\beta)}}{1-r(1-\beta)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left\|\varphi(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{R})},

where
\beta\in(0,1)
,

,
1\leqslant r<\frac{1}{1-\beta}
satisfies
\frac{1}{p}+1=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
, and
\varphi(\cdot)\in L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
.
Then choosing
p=q=2
,
r=1
, by the same procedure in (
3.8
) and we estimate the

term in (

) by the above inequality instead of Hölder ineqaulity, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}L_{y}(t,s)|y(s)|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt=\mathbb{E}\int_{S}^{T}\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\frac{|y(s)|_{H}}{(s-t)^{1-\beta}}ds\Big)^{2}dt\leqslant\frac{(T-S)^{2\beta}}{\beta^{2}}\|y(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(S,T;H)}.

Similar arguments can be done to prove the contractivity of map
\Theta
. Thus,
we can obtain the well-posedness of (
3.1
) accordingly.

We emphasize that the above particular BSVIEs can be used in treating optimal control problems for forward SVIEs (

,

in (H3.3) taking the following form (see e.g.

For (

The following gives an example of linear BSVIE whose singularity can cover that in rough Heston model and fractional Brownian motion kernel properly.

Example 3.12
.

For any constant
{\cal H}\in(0,1)
, let

B^{\cal H}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{0}^{t}K_{\cal H}(t,s)dB(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

Here

is a 1-dimensional standard Brownian motion and

K_{\cal H}(\cdot,\cdot)

K_{\cal H}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\ell_{1}(t-s)^{{\cal H}-\frac{1}{2}}+\ell_{2}s^{{\cal H}-\frac{1}{2}}F(\frac{t}{s}),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta[0,T],

where

\displaystyle F(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}c_{\cal H}(\frac{1}{2}-{\cal H})\int_{1}^{u}(r-1)^{{\cal H}-\frac{3}{2}}(1-r^{{\cal H}-\frac{1}{2}})dr,
and
\displaystyle c_{\cal H}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\Big(\frac{2{\cal H}\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}-{\cal H})}{\Gamma({\cal H}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(2-2{\cal H})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

denotes the Gamma function,
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
are two constants. In the following, we first look at two important special cases with different
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}

(i)

When
\displaystyle\ell_{1}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(H+\frac{1}{2})}
and
\ell_{2}=0

is called the Riemann-Liouville fractional kernel first introduced by Lévy. At this moment,

. This kind of kernels is closely related to the rough Heston model, in for example
[
8
,
14
]
.

(ii)

When
\ell_{1}=c_{\cal H}
and
\ell_{2}=1
,
\displaystyle K_{\cal H}^{(2)}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}c_{\cal H}(t-s)^{{\cal H}-\frac{1}{2}}+s^{{\cal H}-\frac{1}{2}}F(\frac{t}{s})

is the kernel of fractional Brownian motion

with Hurst parameter
{\cal H}\in(0,1)
. By

K_{\cal H}^{(2)}(t,s)\leqslant Cs^{-|H-\frac{1}{2}|}(t-s)^{-(\frac{1}{2}-H)_{+}},\quad(t,s)\in\Delta,

(3.23)

With the above kernels, let us look at the following linear BSVIE

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\int_{t}^{T}\Big[K_{\cal H}^{(2)}(s,t)M_{1}(t)^{*}Y(s)+K_{\cal H}^{(1)}(s,t)M_{2}(t)^{*}Z(s,t)\Big]ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.24)

) and Example
2.2

3.3
. If we take
further particular form of
M_{1}(\cdot)
,

X(t)=x+\int_{0}^{t}K_{\cal H}^{(2)}(t,s)b(s,X(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}K_{\cal H}^{(1)}(t,s)\sigma(s,X(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

Next, we give one example of infinite-dimensional linear BSVIEs motivated by optimal control problem for fractional stochastic evolution equations.

Example 3.13
.

To begin with, let us make some preparations. Suppose
q\in(\frac{1}{2},1)

{\cal A}

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

Obviously, (

3.1
). Such kind of linear backward Volterra integral equations plays an important role in the optimal control problems for the following fractional stochastic evolution equation with Caputo fractional derivative

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{}^{C}_{0}D^{q}_{t}x(t)={\cal A}x(t)+f(t,x(t))+g(t,x(t))\frac{dW(t)}{dt},\quad a.e.\;t\in[0,T],\\

for appropriate function

4
Two classes of optimal control problems

As applications, in this section we study the optimal control problems for controlled stochastic Volterra integral equation and controlled stochastic delay evolution equation, respectively. We present the maximum principles of optimal control with convex control regions.

4.1
Maximum principle for controlled stochastic Volterra integral equations

In this subsection, motivated by the examples of fractional stochastic evolution equations and stochastic evolutionary integral equations, we discuss an optimal control problem for a stochastic Volterra integral equation with a Lagrange type cost functional. More precisely, consider the following state equation:

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X(s),u(s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,s,X(s),u(s))dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(4.1)

with the cost functional

J(u(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}g(t,X(t),u(t))dt.

(4.2)

Here
u(\cdot)
is the
control process
valued in
U
, with
U
a non-empty convex set of a separable metric space
{\cal O}
, and
X(\cdot)
is the corresponding
state process
valued in
H
. In the above,
\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\mapsto H
is called the
free term
.
b:\Delta\times H\times U\times\Omega\mapsto H
and
\sigma:\Delta\times H\times U\times\Omega\mapsto{\cal L}_{2}^{0}
are called the
drift and diffusion
of the controlled SVIEs, respectively,
g:[0,T]\times H\times U\times\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is called the
running cost
.
We now introduce the following assumptions.

(H4.1)
Suppose
b,\sigma
,
g
are continuously differentiable in the variables
(x,u)
, and the Fréchet derivatives
g_{x}
(over
H
) and
g_{u}
(over
U
) are bounded. Moreover,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|b(t,s,0,u)|_{H}+|\sigma(t,s,0,u)|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\leqslant C,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|b_{x}(t,s,x,u)|_{L(H)}+|b_{u}(t,s,x,u)|_{L(U;H)}\leqslant K_{1}(t,s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle|\sigma_{x}(t,s,x,u)|_{L(H;{\cal L}_{2}^{0})}+|\sigma_{u}(t,s,x,u)|_{L(U;{\cal L}_{2}^{0})}\leqslant K_{2}(t,s),\end{array}

for any
(t,s)\in\Delta,\;x\in H,\;u\in U,
and
K_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Delta)
,
K_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)\in\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta)
.

Let
\mathscr{U}[0,T]\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;U)

be the set of admissible controls. Under
(H4.1)
, by Theorem
3.9
, for any
\varphi(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]
, (
4.1
) admits a unique solution
X(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
. Then the cost functional
J(u(\cdot))
is well-defined and the optimal control problem can be stated as follows:

Problem (C).
Find a
\bar{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]
such that

J(\bar{u}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]}J(u(\cdot)).

(4.3)

In this case,
\bar{u}(\cdot)
is called an
optimal control
, the corresponding state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)
and
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
are called the
optimal state
and
optimal pair
, respectively.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
be an optimal pair. Then the following BSVIE admits a unique adapted
M
-solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
,

\displaystyle Y(t)

\displaystyle=g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(b_{x}(s,t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))^{*}Y(s)

(4.4)

\displaystyle+\sigma_{x}(s,t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))^{*}Z(s,t)\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

such that

\displaystyle\Big\langle g_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))+\mathbb{E}\Big[

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}b_{u}(s,t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))^{*}Y(s)ds

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}(s,t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))^{*}Z(s,t)ds\Big|{\cal F}_{t}\Big],u-\bar{u}(t)\Big\rangle_{U}\geqslant 0,\quad\forall u\in U,\;t\in[0,T],\;\hbox{\rm a.s.}

Proof.

To begin with, we introduce the following abbreviations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &b_{x}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}b_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad b_{u}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}b_{u}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\\
&\sigma_{x}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\sigma_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad\sigma_{u}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\sigma_{u}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\\
&g_{x}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),\qquad g_{u}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}g_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)).\end{aligned}\right.

Let
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
be an optimal pair of Problem (C). For any
v(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[0,T]
and any
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, denote

u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\bar{u}(\cdot)+\varepsilon[v(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)]\in\mathscr{U}[0,T].

Let
X^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
be the solution of (
4.1
) with
u(\cdot)
replaced by
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
. Then

\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\tilde{b}_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+\tilde{b}_{u}(t,s)\varepsilon[v(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)]\Big)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\tilde{\sigma}_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t,s)\varepsilon[v(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)]\Big)dW(s),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{x}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{0}^{1}b_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s)+\alpha[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)],u^{\varepsilon}(s))d\alpha,

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{u}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{0}^{1}b_{u}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)+\theta\varepsilon v(s))d\theta,

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{x}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{0}^{1}\sigma_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s)+\alpha[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)],u^{\varepsilon}(s))d\alpha,

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{0}^{1}\sigma_{u}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)+\theta\varepsilon v(s))d\theta.

By the estimate (
3.20
) in Theorem
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|X^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\bar{X}(\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{b}_{u}(t,s)\varepsilon[v(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)]ds+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\sigma}_{u}(t,s)\varepsilon[v(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)]dW(s)\right|_{H}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}.

Hence,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left\|X^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\bar{X}(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;H)}=O(\varepsilon).

Define

X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\frac{X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)}{\varepsilon},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Then
X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(\cdot)\rightarrow X_{1}(\cdot)
in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
with
X_{1}(\cdot)
satisfying the following equation:

\displaystyle X_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\{b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)+b_{u}(t,s)[v(s)-\bar{u}(s)]\}ds

(4.6)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\{\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)+\sigma_{u}(t,s)[v(s)-\bar{u}(s)]\}dW(s)

\displaystyle\equiv\bar{\varphi}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T],

where

\bar{\varphi}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\int_{0}^{t}b_{u}(t,s)[v(s)-\bar{u}(s)]ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{u}(t,s)[v(s)-\bar{u}(s)]dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

By the optimality of
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(\bar{u}(\cdot))}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\left\langle g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\alpha[X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)],u^{\varepsilon}(t)),\frac{X^{\epsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)}{\varepsilon}\right\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle+\langle g_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)+\theta\varepsilon[v(t)-\bar{u}(t)]),v(t)-\bar{u}(t)\rangle_{U}\Big]dt

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle g_{x}(t),X_{1}(t)\rangle_{H}+\langle g_{u}(t),v(t)-\bar{u}(t)\rangle_{U}\Big]dt

\displaystyle\equiv I_{1}+I_{2},

where
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle I_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{x}(t),X_{1}(t)\rangle_{H}dt
and
\displaystyle I_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{u}(t),v(t)-\bar{u}(t)\rangle_{U}dt
.

To get rid of the term involving
X_{1}(\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\bar{\varphi}(t),Y(t)\rangle_{H}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\left\langle X_{1}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)dW(s),Y(t)\right\rangle_{H}\Big]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\left\langle X_{1}(t),Y(t)\right\rangle_{H}-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s),Y(t)\right\rangle_{H}ds-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s),Z(t,s)\right\rangle_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}ds\Big]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle X_{1}(t),Y(t)\right\rangle_{H}dt-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle X_{1}(t),\int_{t}^{T}b_{x}(s,t)^{*}Y(s)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{*}Z(s,t)ds\right\rangle_{H}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle X_{1}(t),Y(t)-\int_{t}^{T}b_{x}(s,t)^{*}Y(s)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{*}Z(s,t)ds\right\rangle_{H}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle X_{1}(t),g_{x}(t)-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s)\right\rangle_{H}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle X_{1}(t),g_{x}(t)\right\rangle_{H}dt.

The above equality is the so-called duality principle between (
4.4
) and (
4.6
).
The first equality holds because of (
4.6
). By the definitions of
M
-solution and adjoint operator, we get the second and third equalities via Fubini theorem. The fifth equality holds due to (
4.4
). The properties of stochastic integrals imply the last equality. By the definition of
\bar{\varphi}(\cdot)
, we derive that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle g_{x}(t),X_{1}(t)\rangle_{H}dt=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\bar{\varphi}(t),Y(t)\rangle_{H}dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\int_{t}^{T}b_{u}(s,t)^{*}Y(s)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}(s,t)^{*}Z(s,t)ds,v(t)-\bar{u}(t)\right\rangle_{U}dt.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle g_{u}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}b_{u}(s,t)^{*}Y(s)ds+\int_{t}^{T}\sigma_{u}(s,t)^{*}Z(s,t)ds,v(t)-\bar{u}(t)\right\rangle_{U}dt

which implies (
4.5
) since
v(\cdot)
is arbitrary.

When the cost functional (

of (

4.2
Maximum principle for controlled stochastic delay evolution equations

In this part, we study the optimal control problems for controlled stochastic delay evolution equations (SDEEs, for short) by the theory of BSVIEs developed in Section 3.

Given a constant time delay parameter
\delta\in(0,T)
and a constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
U
is a nonempty convex subset of a separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{U}
. In this subsection, we consider the controlled stochastic delay evolution equation of the following form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dx(t)={\cal A}x(t)dt+b\Big(t,x(t),x(t-\delta),\int_{-\delta}^{0}e^{\lambda\theta}x(t+\theta)d\theta,u(t),u(t-\delta)\Big)dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\qquad+\sigma\Big(t,x(t),x(t\negthinspace-\negthinspace\delta),\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{-\delta}^{0}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace e^{\lambda\theta}x(t+\theta)d\theta,\negthinspace u(t),\negthinspace u(t\negthinspace-\negthinspace\delta)\Big)dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle x(t)=\xi(t),\ u(t)=\eta(t),\ t\in[-\delta,0],\end{array}\right.

where

Deterministic continuous function

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}l\Big(t,x(t),x(t-\delta),\int_{-\delta}^{0}e^{\lambda\theta}x(t+\theta)d\theta,u(t),u(t-\delta)\Big)dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+h\Big(x(T),x(T-\delta),\int_{-\delta}^{0}e^{\lambda\theta}x(T+\theta)d\theta\Big)\bigg],\end{array}

4.7

&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{s}^{T}S(t-s)^{*}Z^{0}(t,s)dt+\int_{s+\delta}^{T}S(t-s)^{*}Z^{1}(t,s)dt\cdot{\bf 1}_{[0,T-\delta)}(s),\quad s\in[0,T].\end{aligned}\right.

Then,

J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(u^{*}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\Delta l(s)+\langle\Delta b(s),p(s)\rangle_{H}+\langle\Delta\sigma(s),q(s)\rangle_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\Big]ds+o(\varepsilon).

Next, define a Hamilton function
G:[0,T]\times H\times H\times H\times U\times U\times H\times{\cal L}_{2}^{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

G(t,x,y,z,u,\mu,p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}l(t,x,y,z,u,\mu)+\langle p,b(t,x,y,z,u,\mu)\rangle_{H}+\langle q,\sigma(t,x,y,z,u,\mu)\rangle_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let (H4.2) hold. If
u^{*}(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{ad}
is an optimal control, then the following maximum condition holds:

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\langle G_{u}(t,\Theta(t),p(t),q(t)),v(t)-u^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}+\langle G_{\mu}(t,\Theta(t),p(t),q(t)),v(t-\delta)-u^{*}(t-\delta)\rangle_{U}\Big]dt\geqslant 0,\quad\forall v(\cdot)\in{\cal U}_{ad}.

(4.17)

Proof.

By (
4.13
), when
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(u^{*}(\cdot))}{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\Delta l(s)+\langle\Delta b(s),p(s)\rangle_{H}+\langle\Delta\sigma(s),q(s)\rangle_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\Big]ds,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\langle l_{u}(t,\Theta(t)),v(t)-u^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}+\langle l_{\mu}(t,\Theta(t)),v(t-\delta)-\mu^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}

\displaystyle+\langle b_{u}(t,\Theta(t))^{*}p(t),v(t)-u^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}+\langle b_{\mu}(t,\Theta(t))^{*}p(t),v(t-\delta)-\mu^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}

\displaystyle+\langle\sigma_{u}(t,\Theta(t))^{*}q(t),v(t)-u^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}+\langle\sigma_{\mu}(t,\Theta(t))^{*}q(t),v(t-\delta)-\mu^{*}(t)\rangle_{U}\Big\}dt.

Therefore, by the definition of Hamilton function
G(\cdot)
, we derive the necessary maximum principle condition (
4.17
).

Remark 4.4
.

There are lots of works on the optimal control problem for stochastic delay systems in both finite and infinite dimensional frameworks. We mention a few of them. Chen–Wu
[
10
]
investigated the case of stochastic delay differential equations with pointwise delay in the state and the control. Later Zhang
[
62
]
extended it by allowing move-average delay in the state and control.
As to the case of stochastic delay evolution equations, Meng–Shen
[
32
]
discussed the problem in the weak solution framework where a version of Itô’s formula is applicable. Under the same framework, Li et al.
[
25
]
studied the stochastic maximum principle in the infinite horizon case. We point out that the final cost in the above papers does not depend on the past of state and the anticipated backward stochastic differential/evolution equations are introduced as the dual equations.
Recently Guatteri et al
[
16
]
and Guatteri-Masiero
[
17
]
made some progress by allowing the final cost to depend on the past of the state. They introduced certain anticipated backward stochastic integro-differential system due to the dependence on the past trajectory in the final cost.
However, the diffusion term in
[
16
]
was independent of the state and control.

In contrast with the above papers, we provide a new viewpoint which is inspired by
[
33
]
. The novelties of this method are threefold. First, a new interesting idea via Volterra integral system is introduced for the study of SDEEs. Second, to our best knowledge, it is the first time to show the application of infinite dimensional BSVIEs theory to the optimal control problem for stochastic delayed systems.
Last but not the least, in contrast to previous studies, the final cost functional here is naturally allowed to depend on the past of the state.

5
Concluding remark

In this paper, the notion of singular BSVIEs in infinite-dimensional spaces is introduced and the well-posedness is investigated. We propose a kind of non-convolution singular kernel which can cover many concrete types, and can be well used in treating the forward SVIEs case. To explain the theoretical results, several interesting examples are given in details.
As applications, we study two classes of optimal control problems. For the case of controlled stochastic delay evolution equation, we use BSVIEs instead of antipated BSDEs as the adjoint equations and derive the maximum principle when the terminal cost depends on the past state for the first time.

6.1
Comparison with Lin-Yong
[
28
]

To begin with, we revisit
[
28
]
where the authors considered the finite dimensional valued Volterra equation of the form

y(t)=\eta(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(t,s,y(s))}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}ds,\quad a.e.\;t\in[0,T].

(6.1)

Here
\beta\in(0,1)
. For readers’ convenience, we list the assumptions and the main result in
[
28
]
.

(H6.1) Let
f:\Delta\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable map,
y\mapsto f(t,s,y)
being continuously differentiable. There are nonnegative functions
L_{0}(\cdot),L(\cdot)
with

L_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{(\frac{2}{1+2\beta}\vee 1)+}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}),\qquad L(\cdot)\in L^{(\frac{1}{\beta}\vee 2)+}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}),

such that

|f(t,s,0)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant L_{0}(s),\quad(t,s)\in\Delta,

|f(t,s,y)-f(t,s,y^{\prime})|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant L(s)\cdot|y-y^{\prime}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},\quad(t,s)\in\Delta,\;y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\displaystyle L^{s+}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\bigcup_{r>s}L^{r}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}),(1\leqslant s<\infty).

In
[
28
]
, the authors showed that when (H6.1) holds and
\beta\in(0,1)
, for any
\eta(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, (
6.1
) admits a unique solution
y(\cdot)\equiv y(\cdot;\eta(\cdot))\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Notice that the equation (
6.1
) can be viewed as a special case of (
3.19
), with

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varphi(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\eta(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(t,s,0)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}ds,\\
&A(t,s,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\frac{f(t,s,y(s))-f(t,s,0)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}},\\
&B(t,s,y)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}0.\end{aligned}\right.

Obviously,
\displaystyle A(t,s,0)=0
and

\displaystyle|A(t,s,x)-A(t,s,y)|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\leqslant\frac{L(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}|x-y|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Firstly, we claim that
\varphi(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
In fact, by the assumption on
f(t,s,0)
in (H6.1) and Young’s convolution inequality
[
28
, Corollary 2.2]
,
we choose
p=2
and
q\geqslant 1,\;1\leqslant r<\frac{1}{1-\beta}
satisfying
\frac{3}{2}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
. Then we have

\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f(t,s,0)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}ds\right|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{L_{0}(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}ds\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\Big(\frac{T^{1-r(1-\beta)}}{1-r(1-\beta)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left\|L_{0}(\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Case 1. When
\displaystyle\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\displaystyle L_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{\frac{2}{1+2\beta}+}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we can find
\displaystyle r
close enough to
\frac{1}{1-\beta}
such that

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\uparrow\frac{3}{2}-(1-\beta)=\frac{1+2\beta}{2},

thus
q\downarrow\frac{2}{1+2\beta}
, so that
L_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Case 2. When
\displaystyle\beta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
,
\displaystyle L_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{1+}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we can find
\displaystyle r
close enough to
2
such that

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\uparrow\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}=1,

thus
q\downarrow 1
, so that
L_{0}(\cdot)\in L^{q}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}).

To sum up, in both cases, it follows from the definition of
\varphi
that
\varphi(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Secondly, let
\displaystyle K(t,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\frac{L(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\beta}}
. We claim that

K(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Delta)\text{ when }\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)\text{ and }K(\cdot,\cdot)\notin L^{2}(\Delta)\text{ when }\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}].

Observe that

\displaystyle L(\cdot)\in L^{(\frac{1}{\beta}\vee 2)+}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+}).

In the case
\displaystyle\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, if we choose
L(s)\equiv 1
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}K(t,s)^{2}dtds=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{2(1-\beta)}}dtds=\infty,

which means that
K(\cdot,\cdot)\notin L^{2}(\Delta)
.
On the other hand, if
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, then after basic calculations,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}K(t,s)^{2}dtds=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{L(s)^{2}}{(t-s)^{2(1-\beta)}}dtds=\int_{0}^{T}L(s)^{2}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{2(1-\beta)}}dtds<\infty,

which indicates
K(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Delta)
.

To sum up the above arguments, when our SVIEs reduce to the finite dimensional deterministic system, we have the followig conclusions. The free term in our paper can cover that in
[
28
]
. As to the Lisphcitz function, our
K_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
has more general form. As a tradeoff, we only cover the case
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

6.2
Comparison with Zhang
[
63
]

At first, we review the singularity in
[
63
]
.
Let
\kappa:\triangle\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a measurable function.
Assume that

\int^{\cdot}_{0}\kappa(\cdot,s)ds\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathop{\overline{\rm lim}}_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\left\|\int^{\cdot+\varepsilon}_{\cdot}\kappa(\cdot+\varepsilon,s)ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}_{+})}=0.\end{array}

(6.2)

Denote all the functions satisfying the above two properties by
\mathscr{K}_{0}
. The second condition in the definition of
\mathscr{K}_{0}
is to guarantee the existence of resolvent, from which the author derived a singular Gronwall inequality of Volterra type.
In
[
63
]
, the author investigated the following stochastic Volterra integral equation in infinite dimensional spaces:

X(t)=g(t)+\int^{t}_{0}b(t,s,X(s))ds+\int^{t}_{0}\sigma(t,s,X(s))dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T].

(6.3)

For readers’ convenience, we list the following conditions imposed
on the coefficients in
[
63
]
, and make some slight adjustments in our framework.

(H6.2)
Suppose there exist
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in\mathscr{K}_{0}
, such that

(i) For the free term,

\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\int^{t}_{0}[\kappa_{1}(t,s)+\kappa_{2}(t,s)]\cdot\mathbb{E}\|g(s)\|_{H}^{2}ds<+\infty,

(ii) For the drift term, for all
(t,s)\in\Delta
,

\omega\in\Omega
and
x\in H
,

\|b(t,s,\omega,x)\|_{H}\leqslant\kappa_{1}(t,s)\cdot(\|x\|_{H}+1)

and

\|b(t,s,\omega,x)-b(t,s,\omega,y)\|_{H}\leqslant\kappa_{2}(t,s)\cdot\|x-y\|_{H}.

(iii) For the diffusion term, for all
(t,s)\in\Delta
,

\omega\in\Omega
and
x,y\in H
,

\|\sigma(t,s,\omega,x)\|^{2}_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\leqslant\kappa_{1}(t,s)\cdot(\|x\|_{H}^{2}+1)

and

\|\sigma(t,s,\omega,x)-\sigma(t,s,\omega,y)\|^{2}_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\leqslant\kappa_{2}(t,s)\cdot\|x-y\|_{H}^{2}.

In
[
63
]
, under
(H6.2)
, by Picard iteration and Gronwall inequality, the author proved that there exists a unique adapted process
X(\cdot)
satisfying
(
6.3
).

Next, we compare the assumptions in our paper with that in
[
63
]
.

\bullet
For the free term,
from
(H6.2)
(ii), for all
(t,s)\in\Delta
,

\omega\in\Omega
, we see

\|b(t,s,0)\|_{H}\leqslant\kappa_{1}(t,s),\quad\|\sigma(t,s,0)\|^{2}_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\leqslant\kappa_{1}(t,s).

Since
\kappa_{1}\in\mathscr{K}_{0}
, it is easy to see that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|b(t,s,0)\|_{H}ds\Big)^{2}dt<\infty,\quad\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(t,s,0)\|_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}dsdt<\infty.

Therefore, the free term in our paper is more weaker than that in
[
63
]
by Remark
3.8
.

\bullet
Next we look at the diffusion term. From
(H3.3)
and
(H6.2)
(iii),

\displaystyle|B(t,s,x)-B(t,s,y)|^{2}_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\leqslant K_{2}(t,s)^{2}|x-y|^{2}_{H},

\displaystyle\|\sigma(t,s,x)-\sigma(t,s,y)\|^{2}_{{\cal L}_{2}^{0}}\leqslant\kappa_{2}(t,s)\cdot\|x-y\|_{H}^{2},

where
K_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\kappa_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm esssup}_{s\in[0,T]}\int_{s}^{T}K_{2}(t,s)^{2}dt<\infty,\quad\displaystyle\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{2}(t,s)ds<\infty.

According to Gripenberg
[
15
, Proposition 2.7, pp.231]
,
K_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)^{2}
and
\kappa_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
are called Volterra kernel of type
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
on
[0,T]
, respectively. These two type kernels are two important special cases of Volterra kernels. We believe the reason of using different kernels (at least partially) lies in the different solution spaces. In fact, the solution space in
[
63
]
is
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;H))
for the convenience of large deviation theory, while in our paper the solution belongs to
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)
due to the application to optimal control problem in the sequel.

Another interesting comparison of
K_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\kappa_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
lies in condition 2) of
\mathscr{L}^{2}(\Delta)
and the above condition (
6.2
). In
[
63
]
, the condition (
6.2
) is to guarantee the existence of resolvent, from which a singular Gronwall inequality of Volterra type can be obtained. In our setting, the condition 2) of
K_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
is used to keep the successful induction on
[0,T]
. Mathematically speaking, the
\varepsilon
in (
6.2
) requires the divided subintervals to be larger than a positive constant uniformly, while it is not needed under our condition 2).

\bullet
At last, we discuss the drift term. It seems that the Lipschitz functions of
b
and
\sigma
in
[
63
]
have to satisfy certain structure and keep some consistency by their proof. In contrast, no special relationship is required between the Lipschitz functions of the drift and diffusion terms in our paper.

To sum up the above arguments, both
[
63
]
and ours consider the solutions of the forward singular SVIEs in an abstract framework. The free term in our paper can cover that in
[
63
]
. Due to the different solution spaces, the Lispscitz functions of the diffusion term in
[
63
]
and ours are two different kernels (Volterra kernels of type
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
). For the drift term, the Lipschitz function in
[
63
]
needs to keep consistency with that in the diffusion term, while in our paper, there is no such restriction.

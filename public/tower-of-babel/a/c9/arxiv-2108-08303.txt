Uncertainty principle for the two-sided quaternion windowed linear canonical transform
Wen-Biao Gao, Bing-Zhao Li*

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the (two-sided) quaternion windowed linear canonical transform (QWLCT) and study the uncertainty principles associated with the QWLCT. Firstly, several important properties of the QWLCT such as bounded, shift, modulation, orthogonality relation, are presented based on the spectral representation of the quaternionic linear canonical transform (QLCT). Secondly, Pitt’s inequality and Lieb inequality for the QWLCT are explored. Moreover, we study different kinds of uncertainty principles for the QWLCT, such as Logarithmic uncertainty principle, Entropic uncertainty principle, Lieb uncertainty principle and Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle. Finally, we give a numerical example and a potential application in signal recovery by using Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle associated with the QWLCT.

2.1
Quaternion algebra

The quaternion algebra is an extension of the complex number to 4D algebra. It was first invented by W. R. Hamilton in 1843 and classically denoted by
\mathbb{H}
in his honor. Every element of
\mathbb{H}
has a Cartesian form given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\left\{q|q:=[q]_{0}+\mathbf{i}[q]_{1}+\mathbf{j}[q]_{2}+\mathbf{k}[q]_{3},[q]_{i}\in\mathbb{R},i=0,1,2,3\right\}

(1)

where
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}
are imaginary units obeying Hamilton’s multiplication rules (see
16
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=-1,\\
&\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}=-\mathbf{j}\mathbf{i}=\mathbf{k},\,\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}=-\mathbf{k}\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{i},\,\mathbf{k}\mathbf{i}=-\mathbf{i}\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{j}.\end{split}

(2)

Let
[q]_{0}
and
q=\mathbf{i}[q]_{1}+\mathbf{j}[q]_{2}+\mathbf{k}[q]_{3}
denote the real scalar part and the vector part of quaternion number
q=[q]_{0}+\mathbf{i}[q]_{1}+\mathbf{j}[q]_{2}+\mathbf{k}[q]_{3}
, respectively. Then, from
53
, the
real scalar part has a cyclic multiplication symmetry

\displaystyle\begin{split}[pql]_{0}=[qlp]_{0}=[lpq]_{0},\ \ \forall q,p,l\in\mathbb{H},\end{split}

(3)

the conjugate of a quaternion
q
is defined by
\overline{q}=[q]_{0}-\mathbf{i}[q]_{1}-\mathbf{j}[q]_{2}-\mathbf{k}[q]_{3}
, and the norm of
q\in\mathbb{H}
defined as

\displaystyle\left|q\right|=\sqrt{q\bar{q}}=\sqrt{[q]_{0}^{2}+[q]_{1}^{2}+[q]_{2}^{2}+[q]_{3}^{2}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}.}

(4)

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{pq}=\overline{q}\ \overline{p},|qp|=|q||p|,\ \ \forall q,p\in\mathbb{H}.\end{split}

(5)

If
1\leq s<\infty
, the quaternion modules
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
are defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H}):=\left\{f|f:\mathbb{R}^{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{H},\|f\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{t}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{s}{\rm d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}<\infty\right\}.\end{split}

(6)

For
s=\infty
,
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
is a collection of essentially bounded measurable functions with the norm

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})}=ess\ \ sup|f(\mathbf{x})|,\end{split}

if
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
is continuous then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})}=sup|f(\mathbf{x})|.\end{split}

Now we introduce an inner product of quaternion functions
f,g
defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
given by

\displaystyle(f,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(\mathbf{x})\overline{g(\mathbf{x})}{\rm d}\mathbf{x},

(7)

with symmetric real scalar part

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left\{(f,g)+(g,f)\right\}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[f(\mathbf{x})\overline{g(\mathbf{x})}]_{0}{\rm d}\mathbf{x},

(8)

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
{\rm d}\mathbf{x}={\rm d}x_{1}{\rm d}x_{2}
.

The associated scalar norm of
f(\mathbf{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
is defined by both (7) and (8):

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}^{2}=\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{2}{\rm d}\mathbf{x}<\infty.

(9)

The convolution of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
and
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
, denoted by
f*g
, is defined by

\displaystyle(f*g)(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{t}}f(\mathbf{x})g(\mathbf{y-x})\rm{d}\mathbf{x}.

where
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 1

If
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
, then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds[25]

\displaystyle\left|\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\right|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}^{2}.

(10)

If and only if
f=\lambda g
for some quaternionic parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{H}
, the equality holds.

2.2
The quaternion linear canonical transform

The QLCT is firstly defined by Kou, et., which is a generalization of the LCT in the frame of quaternion algebra
50
;
16
. Due to the non-commutativity of quaternion multiplication, there are three different types of the QLCT: the left-sided QLCT, the right-sided QLCT, and the two-sided QLCT. In this paper, we mainly focus on the two-sided QLCT.

Definition 1

Let

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix parameter satisfying
{\rm det}(A_{i})=1
, for
i=1,2
. The two-sided QLCT of signal
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f\}(\mathbf{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{\mathbf{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})f(\mathbf{x})K_{A_{2}}^{\mathbf{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rm{d}\mathbf{x},

(11)

where
\mathbf{w}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is regarded as the QLCT domain, and the kernel signals
K_{A_{1}}^{\mathbf{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})
,
K_{A_{2}}^{\mathbf{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})
are respectively given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{A_{1}}^{\mathbf{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1}):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{1}}}e^{\mathbf{i}\left(\frac{a_{1}}{2b_{1}}x_{1}^{2}-\frac{x_{1}\omega_{1}}{b_{1}}+\frac{d_{1}}{2b_{1}}\omega_{1}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)},&b_{1}\neq 0\\
\sqrt{d_{1}}e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{c_{1}d_{1}}{2}\omega_{1}^{2}}\delta(x_{1}-d_{1}w_{1}),&b_{1}=0\end{cases}\end{split}

(12)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{A_{2}}^{\mathbf{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2}):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{2}}}e^{\mathbf{j}\left(\frac{a_{2}}{2b_{2}}x_{2}^{2}-\frac{x_{2}\omega_{2}}{b_{2}}+\frac{d_{2}}{2b_{2}}\omega_{2}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)},&b_{2}\neq 0\\
\sqrt{d_{2}}e^{\mathbf{j}\frac{c_{2}d_{2}}{2}\omega_{2}^{2}}\delta(x_{2}-d_{2}w_{2}),&b_{2}=0\end{cases}\end{split}

(13)

where
\delta(x)
representing the Dirac function.

From the above definition, it is noted that for
b_{i}=0,i=1,2
the QLCT of a signal is a kind of scaling and chirp multiplication operations, and it is of no particular interest for our objective in this work. Hence, without loss of generality, we set
b_{i}\neq 0
in the following section unless stated otherwise. Under some suitable conditions, the QLCT above is invertible and the inversion is given in the following section.

Lemma 2

Suppose
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
, then the inversion of the QLCT of
f
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\mathbf{x})&=\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}^{-1}[\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\{f\}](\mathbf{x})\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{-\mathbf{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}\left\{f\right\}(\mathbf{w})K_{A_{2}}^{-\mathbf{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rm{d}\mathbf{w}.\end{split}

(14)

3.1
The definition of 2D QWLCT

This section leads to 2D quaternion window function associated with the QLCT. Due to the noncommutative property of
multiplication of quaternions, there are three different types of
QWLCTs: two-sided QWLCT, left-sided QWLCT and right-sided QWLCT. Alternatively, we use the two-sided QWLCT to define the QWLCT.

Definition 2
(QWLCT)

XF2015
;
37
Let

A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be a matrix parameter satisfying
{\rm det}(A_{i})=1
, for
i=1,2
. Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a quaternion window function. The two-sided QWLCT of a signal
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
with respect to
\phi
is defined by

\displaystyle G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{\mathbf{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\phi(\mathbf{x-u})}K_{A_{2}}^{\mathbf{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})\rm{d}\mathbf{x},

(15)

where
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
K_{A_{1}}^{\mathbf{i}}(x_{1},\omega_{1})
and
K_{A_{2}}^{\mathbf{j}}(x_{2},\omega_{2})
are given by (12) and (13), respectively.

For a fixed
\mathbf{u}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})=\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}{\{f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\phi(\mathbf{x-u})}\}(\mathbf{w})}.\\
\end{split}

(16)

Applying the inverse QLCT to (16), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\phi(\mathbf{x-u})}&=\mathcal{L}_{A_{1},A_{2}}^{-1}\{G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})\}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{-\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\omega_{1})}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})K_{A_{2}}^{-\mathbf{j}(x_{2},\omega_{2})}\rm{d}\mathbf{w},\end{split}

(17)

where
f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})
is called modified signal.

Moreover, we can also obtain the relationship between the QWLCT and the QFT [38]:

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{1}}}e^{\mathbf{i}\left(\frac{d_{1}}{2b_{1}}\omega_{1}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}F_{Q}(h)\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}}{\mathbf{b}},\mathbf{u}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi b_{2}}}e^{\mathbf{j}\left(\frac{d_{2}}{2b_{2}}\omega_{2}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)},\end{split}

(18)

where
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
F_{Q}(f)(\mathbf{w})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-\mathbf{i}x_{1}\omega_{1}}f(\mathbf{x})e^{-\mathbf{j}x_{2}\omega_{2}}\rm{d}\mathbf{x}
is the QFT of the signal
f(\mathbf{x})
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(\mathbf{x,u})&=e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{a_{1}}{2b_{1}}x_{1}^{2}}f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\phi(\mathbf{x-u})}e^{\mathbf{j}\frac{a_{2}}{2b_{2}}x_{2}^{2}}\\
&=e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{a_{1}}{2b_{1}}x_{1}^{2}}f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})e^{\mathbf{j}\frac{a_{2}}{2b_{2}}x_{2}^{2}}.\end{split}

(19)

3.2
Some properties of QWLCT

In this subsection, we present several basic properties of the QWLCT. These properties have been proved in
XF2015
;
37
.

Property 1
(Boundedness)

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a window function and
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}|G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{|b_{1}b_{2}|}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}.\end{split}

(20)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4},\mathbb{H})}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{|b_{1}b_{2}|}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}.\end{split}

(21)

Property 2
(Linearity)

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a window function and
f,g\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
. The QWLCT is a linear operator, namely,

\displaystyle\begin{split}[G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{\lambda f+\mu g\}](\mathbf{w,u})=\lambda G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})+\mu G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{g\}(\mathbf{w,u}),\end{split}

(22)

for arbitrary real constants
\lambda
and
\mu
.

Property 3
(Parity)

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a window function and
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{P\phi}\{Pf\}(\mathbf{w,u})=G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{-w,-u}),\end{split}

(23)

where
P\phi(\mathbf{x})=\phi(-\mathbf{x})
for every window function
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
.

Property 4
(Shift)

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a window function and
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{T_{\mathbf{r}}f\}(\mathbf{w,u})=e^{\mathbf{i}r_{1}\omega_{1}c_{1}}e^{-\mathbf{i}\frac{a_{1}r_{1}^{2}}{2}c_{1}}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{m,n})e^{\mathbf{j}r_{2}\omega_{2}c_{2}}e^{-\mathbf{j}\frac{a_{2}r_{2}^{2}}{2}c_{2}},\end{split}

(24)

where
T_{\mathbf{r}}f(\mathbf{x})=f(\mathbf{x-r}),\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2})
,

\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2})
,
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
m_{i}=w_{i}-a_{i}r_{i}
,
n_{i}=u_{i}-r_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

Property 5
(Modulation)

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a window function and
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
.

\mathbb{M}_{s}f
be modulation operator defined by
\mathbb{M}_{s}f(\mathbf{x})=e^{\mathbf{i}x_{1}s_{1}}f(\mathbf{x})e^{\mathbf{j}x_{2}s_{2}}
with
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{\mathbb{M}_{s}f\}(\mathbf{w,u})&=e^{\mathbf{i}\omega_{1}s_{1}d_{1}}e^{-\mathbf{i}\frac{b_{1}d_{1}s_{1}^{2}}{2}}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{v,u})e^{\mathbf{j}\omega_{2}s_{2}d_{2}}e^{-\mathbf{j}\frac{b_{2}d_{2}s_{2}^{2}}{2}},\end{split}

(25)

where
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

v_{i}=w_{i}-s_{i}b_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

Property 6
(Inversion formula)

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be a window function,
0<\|\phi\|^{2}<\infty
and
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
.
Then we have the inversion formula of the QWLCT,

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\|\phi\|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{A_{1}}^{-\mathbf{i}(x_{1},\omega_{1})}G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})K_{A_{2}}^{-\mathbf{j}(x_{2},\omega_{2})}\phi\mathbf{(x-u)}\rm{d}\mathbf{w}\rm{d}\mathbf{u}.\end{split}

(26)

Property 7
(Parseval’s theorem)

Let
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})\backslash\{0\}
be window functions and
f,g\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H}\right)
.
Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u}),{G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\psi}\{g\}(\mathbf{w,u})}\rangle=[(f,g)(\phi,\psi)]_{0}.\end{split}

(27)

Based on the above theorem, we may conclude the following important consequences.

(i) If
\phi=\psi
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u}),{G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{g\}(\mathbf{w,u})}\rangle=\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\langle f,g\rangle.\end{split}

(28)

(ii) If
f=g
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u}),{G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\psi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})}\rangle=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\langle\phi,\psi\rangle.\end{split}

(29)

(iii) If
f=g
and
\phi=\psi
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u}),{G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})}\rangle&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|G^{A_{1},A_{2}}_{\phi}\{f\}(\mathbf{w,u})|^{2}\rm{d}\mathbf{w}\rm{d}\mathbf{u}\\
&=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}.\end{split}

(30)

3.3
Pitt’s inequality

Lemma 3

(Pitt’s inequality of the QFT
36
)
For
f\in S(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{t}}|\mathbf{w}|^{-\alpha}|F_{Q}(f)(\mathbf{w})|^{2}\rm{d}\mathbf{w}\leq M_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{t}}|\mathbf{x}|^{\alpha}|\textit{f}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\rm{d}\mathbf{x},\end{split}

(31)

where
M_{\alpha}=\pi^{\alpha}\left[\Gamma\left(\frac{2t-\alpha}{4}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{2t+\alpha}{4}\right)\right]
,
0\leq\alpha\leq t
and

S(\mathbb{R}^{t},\mathbb{H})
denotes the Schwartz class and
\Gamma
is Gamma function.

According to the above Lemma, we obtain the Pitt’s inequality of the QWLCT.

Theorem 3.1

(Pitt’s inequality of the QWLCT)
For

4.3
Lieb uncertainty principle

In this subsection, we prove Lieb uncertainty principle of the QWLCT based on the following definition and Lieb inequality.

Definition 4

A function

\epsilon-
concentrated on a measurable set

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\chi_{\Omega^{c}}\textit{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}=\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}|\textit{f}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\rm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})},\end{split}

(52)

(Lieb uncertainty principle for the QWLCT)

(59)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|\mathbf{x}||f(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\rm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\ln|\mathbf{w}||G_{\phi}^{A_{1},A_{2}}\{\textit{f}\}(\mathbf{w,u})|^{2}\rm{d}\mathbf{w}\rm{d}\mathbf{u}\\

(63)

36
, Logarithmic uncertainty principle for the QWLCT implies

Let

-\frac{1}{4}&1\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(\mathbf{x,u})&=e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{a_{1}}{2b_{1}}x_{1}^{2}}f(\mathbf{x})\overline{\phi(\mathbf{x-u})}e^{\mathbf{j}\frac{a_{2}}{2b_{2}}x_{2}^{2}}\\

f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})
is bandlimited to a set
Q
of finite measure for the QLCT.
Assume that the receiver is unable to observe all of
f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})
, a certain subset
T

of
x
-values is unobserved.
Furthermore, the modified signal
f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})
is transmitted and the received signal is corrupted by observational noise

n(\mathbf{x})
. Thus the received signal
r(\mathbf{x})
can be taken to have the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}r(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})+n(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\notin T\\
0,&\mathbf{x}\in T\end{cases}\end{split}

(69)

The receiver’s aim is to reconstruct the transmitted signal
f_{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{x})
as nearly as possible, making use of the bandlimited hypothesis and the received data
r(\mathbf{x})
. The uncertainty principle can make stable signal recovery possible as long as
0<|Q||T|<\frac{2\pi}{|\mathbf{b}|}
.
That is to say, if there exists a linear operator
P
and a constant
\Upsilon
such that
\|f_{\mathbf{u}}-Pr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}\leq\Upsilon\|n\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{H})}
, where
\Upsilon\leq\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{|\mathbf{b}|}{2\pi}}\sqrt{|Q||T|}\right)^{-1}
. Then
f_{\mathbf{u}}
can be stably reconstructed from
r
.
The application of the uncertainty principles for QWLCT in signal recovery is our main research direction in the future.

6
Conclusions

The QWLCT is not only a linear transform, but also has similar properties as the WLCT
8
;
14
.
In this paper, Pitt’s inequality and Lieb inequality for the QWLCT are investigated and different forms of uncertainty principles associated with the QWLCT are proposed.
Firstly, we present some important properties of the QWLCT. These properties have been proved in
37
. Secondly, based on the relationship between the QWLCT and
the QFT, the Pitt’s inequality and Lieb inequality associated with the QWLCT are demonstrated. Thirdly, the uncertainty
principles for the QWLCT such as Logarithmic uncertainty principle, Entropic uncertainty principle, Lieb
uncertainty principle and Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principle are obtained. Finally, we give a numerical example and a potential application in signal recovery by using uncertainty principle. The results can be viewed as a generalized form of other transforms uncertainty relations, such as the QLCT, QWFT and QFT.
Further
investigations on this topic are now under investigation such as the left-sided QWLCT, the
right-sided QWLCT and the QWLCT directional uncertainty principle. Moreover, we will discuss the physical significance and engineering background of this paper in the further. They will be reported in a forthcoming paper.

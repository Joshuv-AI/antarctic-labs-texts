A priori and a posteriori error estimates for discontinuous Galerkin time-discrete methods via maximal regularity Thanks: S. L. was supported by Vetenskapsrådet (VR) through grant no. 2017-04274.
Georgios Akrivis, Stig Larsson

Abstract
. The maximal regularity property of discontinuous Galerkin methods for linear parabolic equations is used together with variational techniques to establish a priori and a posteriori error estimates of optimal order under optimal regularity assumptions. The analysis is set in the maximal regularity framework of UMD Banach spaces. Similar results were proved in an earlier work, based on the consistency analysis of Radau IIA methods. The present error analysis, which is based on variational techniques, is of independent interest, but the main motivation is that it extends to nonlinear parabolic equations; in contrast to the earlier work. Both autonomous and nonautonomous linear equations are considered.

1.
Introduction

We consider the discretization of differential equations satisfying
the maximal parabolic
L^{p}
-regularity property in unconditional
martingale differences (UMD) Banach spaces by discontinuous Galerkin
(dG) methods. We combine the maximal regularity property of the
methods with variational techniques and establish optimal order,
optimal regularity, a priori and a posteriori error estimates.

1.1.
Maximal parabolic regularity

We consider an initial value problem for a linear parabolic equation,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)=f(t),\quad 0<t<T,\\
&u(0)=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

in a UMD Banach space
X
with initial value
u_{0}\in X.
Our
structural assumption is that the closed operator
-A
is the generator
of an analytic semigroup on
X
having
maximal

L^{p}
-regularity
. This means that for vanishing initial value

u_{0}=0
, for any
T\in(0,\infty],
for some, or, as it turns out, for
all
p\in(1,\infty),
and for any
f\in L^{p}((0,T);X)
there exists a
unique solution
u
of (
1.1
) such that
u^{\prime}\in L^{p}((0,T);X)
;
then we also have
Au\in L^{p}((0,T);X).
As a consequence of the closed
graph theorem, the solution
u
of (
1.1
) with
u_{0}=0
satisfies
the stability estimate

(1.2)

\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|Au\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant c_{p,X}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\quad\forall f\in L^{p}((0,T);X)

with a constant
c_{p,X}
independent of
T,
depending only on
p
and
X
;
see, e.g.,
[
5
]
and
[
9
]
.

Since also
\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|Au\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

by the triangle inequality, the norm of the sum
\|u^{\prime}+Au\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

and the sum of the norms
\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|Au\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}
are equivalent
on the Banach space

\big\{v\in W^{1,p}((0,T);X)\cap L^{p}((0,T);{\mathscr{D}}(A)):v(0)=0\big\}

with constants independent of
T,
for
T<\infty
. Here
{\mathscr{D}}(A):=\{v\in X:Av\in X\}

is the domain of the operator
A
.

1.2.
The numerical methods

We consider the discretization of the initial value problem (
1.1
)
by dG methods.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}},k=T/N
be the constant time step,

t_{n}:=nk,n=0,\dotsc,N,
be a uniform partition of the time interval

[0,T],
and
J_{n}:=(t_{n},t_{n+1}].

For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},
we denote by
{\mathbb{P}}(s)
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(s)
the spaces of
polynomials of degree at most
s
with coefficients in
{\mathscr{D}}(A)
and in
the dual
X^{\prime}
of
X
, respectively, i.e., the elements
g
of
{\mathbb{P}}(s)

and of
{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(s)
, respectively, are of the form

g(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}t^{j}w_{j},\quad w_{j}\in{\mathscr{D}}(A)\quad\text{and}\quad w_{j}\in X^{\prime},\quad j=0,\dotsc,s.

With this notation, let
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(s)
and

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s)
be the spaces of continuous and possibly
discontinuous piecewise elements of
{\mathbb{P}}(s)
, respectively,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(s):=\{v\in C\big([0,T];{\mathscr{D}}(A)\big):v|_{J_{n}}\in{\mathbb{P}}(s),\ n=0,\dotsc,N-1\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s):=\{v:(0,T]\to{\mathscr{D}}(A),\ v(0)\in X,\ v|_{J_{n}}\in{\mathbb{P}}(s),\ n=0,\dotsc,N-1\}.

The spaces
{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(s)
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s)
are defined
analogously, with coefficients
w_{j}\in X
. Let us emphasize that we
allow the values at
0
of functions in
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(s)
to belong
to
X.

We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the duality pairing between
X
and
X^{\prime}.

For
q\in{\mathbb{N}},
with starting value
U(0)=U_{0}:=u_{0}\in X
and source term
f\in L^{p}((0,T);X)
,
we consider the discretization of the initial value problem (
1.1
) by the

discontinuous Galerkin method
dG
(q-1)
, i.e., we seek
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
such that

(1.3)

\int_{J_{n}}\big({\langle}U^{\prime},v{\rangle}+{\langle}AU,v{\rangle}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+{\langle}U_{n}^{+}-U_{n},v_{n}^{+}{\rangle}=\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}f,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1)

for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. As usual, we use the notation
v_{n}:=v(t_{n}),

v_{n}^{+}:=\lim_{s\searrow 0}v(t_{n}+s)
.

1.2.1.
A reconstruction operator

With
0<c_{1}<\dotsb<c_{q}=1
the Radau nodes in the interval
[0,1]
,
let
t_{ni}:=t_{n}+c_{i}k,i=1,\dotsc,q,
be the intermediate nodes;
we also use the notation
t_{n0}:=t_{n}.

The
reconstruction operator

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)\to{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q),w\mapsto\widehat{w},

defined via extended interpolation at the Radau nodes, cf.
[
12
]
,

(1.4)

\widehat{w}(t_{nj})=w(t_{nj}),\quad j=0,\dotsc,q\quad(w(t_{n0})=w_{n}),

plays a crucial role in our analysis; notice that

\widehat{w}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q),
provided
w(0)\in{\mathscr{D}}(A).

1.3.
Main results

We establish the following optimal order, optimal regularity, a priori and
a posteriori error estimates.

Theorem 1.1

(A priori error estimates)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and assume that the solution of
(
1.1
) is sufficiently regular,

u\in W^{q,p}\big((0,T);{\mathscr{D}}(A)\big).
Then, the dG approximation

U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
satisfies the estimate

(1.5)

\|A(u-U)\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|Au^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}.

Furthermore, if
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}(A)
and

u\in W^{q+1,p}\big((0,T);X\big),
for the reconstruction

\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q),
we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|(u-\widehat{U})^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(u-\widehat{U})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q}\big(\|u^{(q+1)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|Au^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\big).

The constant
C
depends on
q,p
, and
X
, but it is independent of
the solution
u,
of
T,
and of the time step
k.

Theorem 1.2

(A posteriori error estimate)
.

For initial value
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}(A),
let

R(t):=\widehat{U}^{\prime}(t)+A\widehat{U}(t)-f(t)
be the residual of the
reconstruction
\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
of the dG approximation

U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1).
Then, the following maximal regularity
a posteriori error estimate holds:

(1.7)

\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);X)}\leqslant\|(u-\widehat{U})^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,t);X)}+\|A(u-\widehat{U})\|_{L^{p}((0,t);X)}\leqslant c_{p,X}\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,t);X)}

for all
0<t\leqslant T
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
with the constant

c_{p,X}
from (
1.2
).
Furthermore, the estimator is of optimal asymptotic order of accuracy,

(1.8)

\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\big(\|u^{(q+1)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|Au^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\big),

provided that
u\in W^{q,p}\big((0,T);{\mathscr{D}}(A)\big)\cap W^{q+1,p}\big((0,T);X\big).

Our proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
rely on the
maximal regularity property of the dG methods from
[
2
]

and on variational techniques. The variational technique is applicable
also in the case of nonlinear parabolic equations; see
[
1
]
; this
is our main motivation.

Similar error estimates were recently established in

[
2
]
. The proofs in
[
2
]
rely on properties of
the Radau IIA methods; in particular, the proof of the optimality of

\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}
in
[
2
]
is significantly lengthier
and more involved.

As the proof of Theorem
1.2
is very short, we give it here.

Proof.

Our assumption
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}(A)
ensures that the reconstruction

\widehat{U}
of the dG approximation
U
belongs to

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q).
The residual
R,
the amount by which

\widehat{U}
misses being an exact solution of the differential
equation in (
1.1
), is a computable quantity, depending only on
the numerical solution
\widehat{U}
and the given forcing term
f.

Replacing
f
in the residual by
u^{\prime}+Au,
we see that the error

\hat{e}:=u-\widehat{U}
satisfies the
error equation

(1.9)

\hat{e}^{\prime}(t)+A\hat{e}(t)=-R(t),\quad t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}],\ n=0,\dotsc,N-1;\quad\hat{e}(0)=0.

Now, the triangle inequality and the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
(
1.2
) of the operator
A
applied to the error equation
(
1.9
) yield (
1.7
), i.e.,
the asserted lower and upper a posteriori error estimators.

In view of the representation (
1.9
) of
R,
the
optimality (
1.8
) of the residual is an
immediate consequence of the a priori error estimate
(
1.6
).
∎

We present the a priori error analysis in Section
2
. In
Section
3
we extend these results to the case of
nonautonomous linear parabolic equations. The Appendix contains proofs
of relevant interpolation error estimates.

2.
A priori error estimates

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
A discrete
\ell^{p}(X)
-norm and its equivalence to the continuous
L^{p}(X)
-norm

We introduce the discrete
\ell^{p}(X)
-norm

\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}
on
{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1):=\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1):v(0)=0\}

and on
{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q):=\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q):v(0)=0\}
by

(2.1)

\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}:=\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\Big(k\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|v(t_{\ell i})\|_{X}^{p}\Big)\Big)^{1/p}

and show that it is equivalent to the continuous
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}
-norm.

Let us focus on the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1);
the proof for the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q)

is completely analogous.
First, it is easily seen that the continuous norm is dominated by the discrete norm,

(2.2)

\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant\tilde{c}_{q,p}\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}\quad\forall v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

Indeed, with
\ell_{mi}
the Lagrange polynomials

\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{q-1}
for the Radau points
c_{1},\dotsc,c_{q},
shifted
to the subinterval
J_{m}
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{J_{m}}\|v(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}t&=\int_{J_{m}}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{q}\ell_{mi}(t)v(t_{mi})\Big\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\leqslant k\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|\ell_{mi}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{m})}\|v(t_{mi})\|_{X}\Big)^{p}\\
&\leqslant\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|\ell_{mi}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{\infty}(J_{m})}\Big)^{p/p^{\prime}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{q}k\|v(t_{mi})\|_{X}^{p}\Big)=c_{q,p}k\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|v(t_{mi})\|_{X}^{p}\end{split}

with
p^{\prime}
the dual exponent of
p,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,
and summing over
m,
we obtain (
2.2
);
cf.
[
2
]
.

Next, we prove that the discrete norm is dominated by the continuous norm
in the reference element
[0,1]
for polynomials
v
of degree at most
q-1

with coefficients in
X;
a scaling argument
then shows that this is the case in arbitrary intervals
(0,T).

We use the Lagrange form of
v,

v(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\ell_{i}(t)v(c_{i}),\quad t\in[0,1].

Let
i
be such that

(2.3)

\|v(c_{i})\|_{X}=\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant q}\|v(c_{j})\|_{X}.

We want to show that

\|v(c_{i})\|_{X}\leqslant c\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,1);X)}.

We have

\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,1);X)}^{p}\geqslant\int_{c_{i}-\delta}^{c_{i}}\|v(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=\int_{c_{i}-\delta}^{c_{i}}\Big\|\ell_{i}(t)v(c_{i})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{q}\ell_{j}(t)v(c_{j})\Big\|_{X}^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}t,

whence, in view of (
2.3
),

(2.4)

\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,1);X)}^{p}\geqslant\|v(c_{i})\|_{X}^{p}\int_{c_{i}-\delta}^{c_{i}}\Big(|\ell_{i}(t)|-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{q}|\ell_{j}(t)|\Big)^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Now, for any
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1),
for sufficiently small
\delta,
we have

(2.5)

|\ell_{i}(t)|\geqslant 1-\varepsilon_{1},\quad|\ell_{j}(t)|\leqslant\varepsilon_{2},\quad j\neq i,\quad\forall t\in[c_{i}-\delta,c_{i}],

and (
2.4
) yields

(2.6)

\|v\|_{L^{p}((0,1);X)}^{p}\geqslant\delta\big(1-\varepsilon_{1}-(q-1)\varepsilon_{2}\big)^{p}\|v(c_{i})\|_{X}^{p}

and the desired property follows easily.

The proof for the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q)
is completely analogous.
In this case we shift the Lagrange polynomials
\hat{\ell}_{i}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{q}
for the points
c_{0}=0,c_{1},\dotsc,c_{q},

to a subinterval
J_{m}
, and use the fact that
t_{n,0}:=t_{n}=t_{n-1,q}.

Notice that it is obvious from (
1.4
) that the discrete
\ell^{p}(X)
-norms
of an element
v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
and of its reconstruction
\hat{v}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q)

coincide,

(2.7)

\|\hat{v}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}=\|v\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}\quad\forall v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

Since the equivalence constants of the discrete
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}
- and
continuous
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}
-norms are independent of
T,

the corresponding discrete
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});X)}
- and
continuous
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{n});X)}
-seminorms,
n=1,\dotsc,N,

are also equivalent with constants independent of
n.

Of course, the discrete
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{n});X)}
-seminorm
is the term on the right-hand side of (
2.1
)
with
N
replaced by
n.

2.2.
Maximal parabolic regularity of the dG method

We shall use the notation
\partial_{k}
for the backward difference operator,

\partial_{k}v:=\frac{v(\cdot)-v(\cdot-k)}{k},\quad v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1)\cup{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{c}}(q)

with
v=0
in the interval
[-k,0).

Obviously,
\partial_{k}
commutes with the reconstruction operator,
\partial_{k}\hat{v}=\widehat{\partial_{k}v},v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k,0}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

In the case of vanishing initial value
u_{0}=0,
for
the reconstruction
\widehat{U}
and the dG approximation
U
we have
the following maximal parabolic regularity result

(2.8)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{k}\widehat{U}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}&+\|\widehat{U}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A\widehat{U}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\\
&+\|AU\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant C_{p,X}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},\end{split}

with
C_{p,X}
a method-dependent constant, independent of
N
and
T.

The estimate for the last three terms on the left-hand side of (
2.8
)
is given in
[
2
, (1.9) and the last line on p. 186]
; the proof relies on the interpretation
of dG methods in
[
2
]
as modified Radau IIA methods and on the
maximal regularity property of Radau IIA methods from
[
7
]
.
Notice that
\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
since
u_{0}=0\in{\mathscr{D}}(A).

It remains to prove the estimate for the first term on the left-hand side of (
2.8
).
Now, the nodal values
\widehat{U}_{ni}=U_{ni}=U(t_{ni}),n=0,\dotsc,N-1,i=1,\dotsc,q,

satisfy the Radau IIA equations with the nodal values
f(t_{ni})
of the forcing term replaced
by the averages
f_{ni},

(2.9)

f_{ni}:=\frac{1}{\int_{J_{n}}\ell_{ni}(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s}\int_{J_{n}}\ell_{ni}(s)f(s)\,{\mathrm{d}}s,\quad n=0,\dotsc,N-1,\ i=1,\dotsc,q;

see
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
. Therefore, according to the maximal regularity result
of the Radau IIA methods in
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
, we have

(2.10)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(\partial\widehat{U}_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p}\leqslant C_{p,r}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p};

here,
\widehat{U}_{-1,1}=\dotsb=\widehat{U}_{-1,q}=0,
and for a sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\subset X,
we used the notation

\partial v_{n}:=\frac{v_{n}-v_{n-1}}{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\|(v_{n})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}:=\Big(k\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\|v_{n}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

for the backward difference quotient and for the discrete
\ell^{p}(X)
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}.

Notice that the term on the left-hand side of (
2.10
) coincides with
the first term on the left-hand side of (
2.8
) raised to the power
p.
Furthermore,

(2.11)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|(f_{ni})_{n=0}^{N-1}\|_{\ell^{p}(X)}^{p}\leqslant\gamma\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}^{p}

with a constant
\gamma
depending only on
c_{1}\dotsc,c_{q},
and
p;

see
[
2
, (2.12)]
. Now, (
2.10
) and (
2.11
)
yield

\|\partial_{k}\widehat{U}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant C_{p,X}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

and the proof of (
2.8
) is complete.

Obviously, (
2.8
) is also valid with
T
replaced by
t_{n},n=1,\dotsc,N.

Let us note that the estimate for the first term on the left-hand side of (
2.8
)
is not needed in the error analysis for linear parabolic equations; however, it plays a key
role in the error analysis for nonlinear parabolic equations; see
[
1
]
.
The analogous estimate for Radau IIA methods from
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]

was used there in the error analysis for Radau IIA methods for nonlinear parabolic equations.

Notice also that due to the equivalence of the discrete
\ell^{p}(X)
- and the continuous
L^{p}(X)
-norms
established in section
2.1
,
\|\partial_{k}\widehat{U}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,T);X)}
can be replaced by

\|\partial_{k}\widehat{U}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}
in the maximal regularity estimate (
2.8
).

Logarithmically quasi-maximal parabolic regularity results for dG
methods were earlier established in
[
10
]
in general Banach
spaces for autonomous equations and in
[
11
]
in Hilbert spaces
for nonautonomous equations. These works are not based on the
maximal regularity theory and therefore cover also the cases of
variable time steps as well as the critical exponents
p=1,\infty.

More recently, another approach to the maximal regularity of dG
time discretization was presented in
[
6
]
. This is based on
studying the dG approximation of a temporally regularized Green’s
function. The result allows quasi-uniform meshes but is restricted
to
q\geqslant 2
.

2.3.
An interpolant and its approximation properties

We shall use a standard interpolant
\tilde{u}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
of the solution
u
such that

\tilde{u}(t_{n})=u(t_{n}),n=0,\dotsc,N,
and
u-\tilde{u}
is in each subinterval
J_{n}
orthogonal
to polynomials of degree at most
q-2
(with the second condition being void for
q=1
);
then,
\tilde{u}
is determined in
J_{n}
by the conditions

(2.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tilde{u}(t_{n+1})=u(t_{n+1}),\\
&\int_{J_{n}}\big(u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\big)t^{j}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0,\quad j=0,\dotsc,q-2;\end{aligned}\right.

cf., e.g.,
[
14
, (12.9)]
and
[
13
, §§3.1–3.3]
for the case of Hilbert spaces.

The approximation property (valid for
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
,

q\geqslant 1
)

(2.13)

\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

will play an important role in our analysis.
Furthermore, we will use the following approximation properties of the
reconstruction
\hat{\tilde{u}}
of
\tilde{u}
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|u-\hat{\tilde{u}}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q}\|u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|(u-\hat{\tilde{u}})^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q}\|u^{(q+1)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}.

We shall prove existence and uniqueness of
\tilde{u}
as well as the
approximation properties (
2.13
),
(
2.14
), and (
2.15
)
in the appendix.

Notice that the orthogonality condition in (
2.12
) can be
equivalently written in the form

(2.16)

\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}u(t)-\tilde{u}(t),v(t){\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-2).

2.4.
A priori error estimates

Using the interpolant
\tilde{u},
we decompose the error
e=u-U
in the form

e=\rho+\vartheta\quad\text{with}\quad\rho:=u-\tilde{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta:=\tilde{u}-U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

The desired estimate for the interpolation error
\rho,

(2.17)

\|A\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|Au^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

is obtained by replacing
u
by
Au
in (
2.13
).
Therefore, to prove (
1.5
), it remains to bound
\vartheta.

Since
\rho=u-\tilde{u}
is orthogonal to
v^{\prime}
in
J_{n}
for any

v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1)
, cf. (
2.16
), and
vanishes at the nodes
t_{n},
integration by parts shows that it has
the following crucial property,

(2.18)

\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}\rho^{\prime},v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t+{\langle}\rho_{n}^{+}-\rho_{n},v_{n}^{+}{\rangle}=0\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1);

cf.
[
14
, (12.13)]
.

Subtracting the dG method (
1.3
) from the corresponding relation
for the exact solution, and using the splitting
e=\rho+\vartheta
as
well as relation (
2.18
) for
\rho,
we obtain the following
equation for
\vartheta,

(2.19)

\int_{J_{n}}\big({\langle}\vartheta^{\prime},v{\rangle}+{\langle}A\vartheta,v{\rangle}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+{\langle}\vartheta_{n}^{+}-\vartheta_{n},v_{n}^{+}{\rangle}=\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}A\rho,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1)

for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
. Notice that the reconstruction

\hat{\vartheta}=\hat{\tilde{u}}-\widehat{U}
of
\vartheta
belongs to

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
since
\vartheta(0)=0\in{\mathscr{D}}(A);
in contrast,

\hat{\tilde{u}}
and
\widehat{U}
belong only to
{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
for

u_{0}\in X.

Since
\vartheta(0)=0,
the maximal regularity property
(
2.8
) of the dG method applied to the error equation
(
2.19
) yields

\|\hat{\vartheta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A\hat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant C_{p,X}\|A\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

and thus, in view of (
2.17
),

(2.20)

\|\hat{\vartheta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A\hat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|Au^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}.

Now, (
1.5
) follows immediately from
(
2.17
) and (
2.20
). To prove
(
1.6
), we write

\hat{e}=u-\widehat{U}=(u-\hat{\tilde{u}})+(\hat{\tilde{u}}-\widehat{U})=\hat{\rho}+\hat{\vartheta}

and combine (
2.20
) with the estimates for

\hat{\rho}:=u-\hat{\tilde{u}}
and
\hat{\rho}^{\prime}
from
(
2.14
) and (
2.15
),
namely

\displaystyle\|A\hat{\rho}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|Au^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},\quad\|\hat{\rho}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|u^{(q+1)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}.

3.
Extension to nonautonomous equations

In this section, we extend the maximal parabolic regularity stability
estimates for dG methods to nonautonomous parabolic equations by a
perturbation argument. For similar ideas and results, we refer to

[
11
]
and
[
8
, §3.6]
,
[
3
]
for the dG
method with piecewise constant elements and for Radau IIA methods,
respectively. Furthermore, we establish optimal order a priori and a
posteriori error estimates.

We consider an initial value problem for a nonautonomous linear parabolic equation,

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\prime}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t),\quad 0<t<T,\\
&u(0)=u_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

in a UMD Banach space
X.

Our structural assumptions on
A(t)
are that all operators

A(t),t\in[0,T],
share the same domain
{\mathscr{D}}(A),

A(t)
is the
generator of an analytic semigroup on
X
having maximal

L^{p}
-regularity, for every
t\in[0,T],

A(t)
induce equivalent
norms on
{\mathscr{D}}(A),

(3.2)

\|A(t)v\|_{X}\leqslant c\|A(\tilde{t})v\|_{X}\quad\forall t,\tilde{t}\in[0,T]\ \forall v\in{\mathscr{D}}(A),

and
A(t)\colon{\mathscr{D}}(A)\to X
satisfies the Lipschitz condition with
respect to
t,

(3.3)

\|\big(A(t)-A(\tilde{t})\big)v\|_{X}\leqslant L|t-\tilde{t}|\|A(s)v\|_{X}\quad\forall t,\tilde{t}\in[0,T]\ \forall v\in{\mathscr{D}}(A),

for all
s\in[0,T].

With starting value
U(0)=U_{0}:=u_{0}\in X
and source term

f\in L^{p}((0,T);X)
, we consider the discretization of the initial
value problem (
3.1
) by the dG method dG
(q-1)
, i.e., we seek

U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
such that

(3.4)

\int_{J_{n}}\big({\langle}U^{\prime},v{\rangle}+{\langle}A(t)U,v{\rangle}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+{\langle}U_{n}^{+}-U_{n},v_{n}^{+}{\rangle}=\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}f,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1)

for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
; cf. (
1.3
).

3.1.
Maximal parabolic regularity

Here, for vanishing starting value
U_{0},
we establish maximal parabolic regularity
of dG methods for the nonautonomous parabolic equation (
3.1
) via
a perturbation argument.

For a fixed
t_{m},m\in\{1,\dotsc,N\},
we write (
3.4
) in the form

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}U^{\prime},v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle+\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}A(t_{m})U,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t+{\langle}U_{n}^{+}-U_{n},v_{n}^{+}{\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}(A(t_{m})-A(t))U,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t+\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}f,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1),

n=0,\dotsc,m-1.
Since the time
t
is frozen at
t_{m}
on the
left-hand side of (
3.5
), we can apply the maximal
parabolic regularity estimate (
2.8
) for dG methods for
autonomous equations and obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{k}\widehat{U}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{\ell});X)}&+\|\widehat{U}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{\ell});X)}+\|A(t_{m})\widehat{U}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{\ell});X)}+E_{\ell}\\
&\leqslant C_{p,X}\big(G_{\ell}+\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{\ell});X)}\big),\quad\ell=1,\dotsc,m,\end{split}

with

G_{\ell}:=\|(A(t_{m})-A(\cdot))U\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{\ell});X)},\ E_{\ell}:=\|A(t_{m})U\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{\ell});X)},\ \ell=1,\dotsc,m,

and
E_{0}:=0.

Now, according to (
3.6
),

(3.7)

E_{m}^{p}\leqslant C\big(G_{m}^{p}+\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}^{p}\big).

Furthermore,

G_{m}^{p}=\|(A(t_{m})-A(\cdot))U\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}^{p}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\|(A(t_{m})-A(\cdot))U\|_{L^{p}(J_{\ell};X)}^{p},

and thus, in view of the Lipschitz condition (
3.3
),

G_{m}^{p}\leqslant L^{p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}(t_{m}-t_{\ell})^{p}\|A(t_{m})U\|_{L^{p}(J_{\ell};X)}^{p}=L^{p}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}(t_{m}-t_{\ell})^{p}(E_{\ell+1}^{p}-E_{\ell}^{p}).

Hence, by summation by parts, we have

(3.8)

G_{m}^{p}\leqslant L^{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}a_{\ell}E_{\ell}^{p}=L^{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}a_{\ell}E_{\ell}^{p}+L^{p}k^{p}E_{m}^{p},

with
a_{\ell}:=(t_{m}-t_{\ell-1})^{p}-(t_{m}-t_{\ell})^{p},
and (
3.7
) yields

(3.9)

E_{m}^{p}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}^{p}+C\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}a_{\ell}E_{\ell}^{p}+Ck^{p}E_{m}^{p}

and hence, for
Ck^{p}\leqslant 1/2
,

E_{m}^{p}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}^{p}+C\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}a_{\ell}E_{\ell}^{p}.

Since the sum
\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}a_{\ell}
is uniformly bounded,

\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}a_{\ell}=\big(t_{m}-t_{0})^{p}\leqslant T^{p},

a discrete Gronwall-type argument applied to (
3.9
) leads to

(3.10)

E_{m}^{p}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}^{p}.

Combining (
3.10
) with (
3.8
) and
(
3.6
), we obtain, for sufficiently small
k
, the desired
maximal parabolic regularity stability estimate

(3.11)

\begin{split}\|\partial_{k}\widehat{U}\|_{\ell^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}&+\|\widehat{U}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}+\|A(t_{m})\widehat{U}\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}+\|A(t_{m})U\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)}\\
&\leqslant C_{p,X,T}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,t_{m});X)},\quad m=1,\dotsc,N,\end{split}

with a constant
C_{p,X,T}
independent of
k.
Notice
that, due to the equivalence of norms (
3.2
),
A(t_{m})
can
be replaced by
A(s)
on the left-hand side of (
3.11
), for
arbitrary
s\in[0,T].

3.2.
A priori error estimates

Here, we establish the analogue of Theorem
1.1
in the
nonautonomous case.

Theorem 3.1

(A priori error estimates)
.

Assume that the solution of (
3.1
) is sufficiently regular,

u\in W^{q,p}\big((0,T);{\mathscr{D}}(A)\big).
Then, for sufficiently small

k
, the dG approximation
U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
of
(
3.4
) satisfies the estimate

(3.12)

\|A(s)(u-U)\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|A(s)u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

for any
s\in[0,T].
Furthermore, if
u_{0}\in{\mathscr{D}}(A)
and

u\in W^{q+1,p}\big((0,T);X\big),
for the reconstruction

\widehat{U}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
of
U,
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|(u-\widehat{U})^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(s)(u-\widehat{U})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q}\big(\|u^{(q+1)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(s)u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\big).

The constant
C
depends on
q
,
p
,
L
,
X
, and
T
, but it is
independent of the solution
u
and of the time step
k.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In particular,
we decompose the error
e=u-U
in the form

e=\rho+\vartheta\quad\text{with}\quad\rho:=u-\tilde{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\vartheta:=\tilde{u}-U\in{\mathcal{V}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

The error
\rho
can be estimated as in Section
2.4
; for
instance, the analogue of (
2.17
) reads

(3.14)

\|A(s)(u-\tilde{u})\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant ck^{q}\|A(s)u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

for any
s\in[0,T].

Furthermore, subtracting the dG method (
3.4
) from the
corresponding equation for the exact solution

u
of (
3.1
) and using the identity (
2.18
) for

\rho,
we obtain the following equation for
\vartheta,

(3.15)

\int_{J_{n}}\big({\langle}\vartheta^{\prime},v{\rangle}+{\langle}A(t)\vartheta,v{\rangle}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t+{\langle}\vartheta_{n}^{+}-\vartheta_{n},v_{n}^{+}{\rangle}=\int_{J_{n}}{\langle}A(t)\rho,v{\rangle}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X^{\prime}}(q-1)

for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1
.

Since
\vartheta(0)=0,
the maximal regularity property of the dG
method for nonautonomous equations (see (
3.11
)) applied
to the error equation (
3.15
) yields

\|\hat{\vartheta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(s)\hat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(s)\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant C_{p,X}\|A(\cdot)\rho\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)},

for any
s\in[0,T],
and thus, in view of (
3.14
),

(3.16)

\|\hat{\vartheta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(s)\hat{\vartheta}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}+\|A(s)\vartheta\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}\leqslant Ck^{q}\|A(s)u^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,T);X)}.

The proof can now be completed as in the case of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

3.3.
A posteriori error estimates

Let
R
be the
residual
of the reconstruction
\widehat{U},

R(t):=\widehat{U}^{\prime}(t)+A(t)\widehat{U}(t)-f(t),\quad t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}],\quad n=0,\dotsc,N-1.

Then, the error
\hat{e}:=u-\widehat{U}
satisfies the
error equation

(3.17)

\hat{e}^{\prime}(t)+A(t)\hat{e}(t)=-R(t),\quad t\in(t_{n},t_{n+1}],\ n=0,\dotsc,N-1;\quad\hat{e}(0)=0.

Let us now fix an
s\in(0,T].
To apply the
maximal

L^{p}
-regularity
of the operator
A(s),
frozen at time
s,
we
rewrite (
3.17
) in the form

\hat{e}^{\prime}(t)+A(s)\hat{e}(t)=[A(s)-A(t)]\hat{e}(t)-R(t),\quad t\in(0,s].

Proceeding as in the proof of the maximal regularity property for
nonautonomous parabolic equations in the continuous case, cf., e.g.,

[
3
, §4.2]
for the derivation a posteriori
estimates for Radau IIA methods, we obtain the desired a posteriori
error estimate

\|\hat{e}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,s);X)}+\|A(s)\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,s);X)}\leqslant C\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,s);X)},\quad 0<s\leqslant T,

for any
p\in(1,\infty),
with a constant
C
depending on
p,X,L,

and
T,
but independent of
s.

Furthermore, the triangle inequality applied to (
3.17
)
yields a lower a posteriori error estimator,

\|R\|_{L^{p}((0,s);X)}\leqslant\|\hat{e}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}((0,s);X)}+\|A(\cdot)\hat{e}\|_{L^{p}((0,s);X)},\quad 0<s\leqslant T.

As in the autonomous case, we see that the a posteriori error
estimator is of optimal order as an immediate consequence of the a
priori error estimate (
3.13
).

Appendix A
Interpolation error estimates

We prove error estimates for
\tilde{v}
and
\hat{\tilde{v}}
. We
shall use a standard argument based on the Bramble–Hilbert lemma;
cf.
[
4
, (4.4.4)]
. The key ingredients are reproduction
of polynomials and boundedness with respect to a relevant Sobolev
norm.

A.1.
Existence and uniqueness of
\bm{\tilde{v}}

The interpolant
\tilde{v}
of a function
v\in C([0,T];X)
can be
expressed in terms of the value
v(t_{n+1})
of
v
at
t_{n+1}
and
the Legendre coefficients
v_{0},\dotsc,v_{q-2}\in X
of
v,

(A.1)

v_{i}:=\frac{1}{\|L_{ni}\|_{L^{2}(J_{n})}^{2}}\int_{J_{n}}L_{ni}(t)v(t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

More precisely, for
t\in J_{n}
,

(A.2)

\tilde{v}(t)=(P_{q-2}v)(t)+L_{n,q-1}(t)\Big[v(t_{n+1})-\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}v_{i}\Big],\quad P_{q-2}v=\sum_{i=0}^{q-2}L_{ni}v_{i},

with
P_{q-2}
the piecewise
L^{2}
-projection onto
{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
;
here,
L_{ni}
are the Legendre polynomials
L_{i}
of degree
i
shifted to the interval
J_{n},

L_{ni}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}(t_{n}+t_{n+1}+ks)\big)=L_{i}(s),\quad s\in[-1,1];

see
[
13
, (3.2)]
.
Indeed, the part
P_{q-2}v
of
\tilde{v}
on the right-hand side of (
A.2
)
is due to the orthogonality of
v-\tilde{v}
to the orthogonal polynomials
L_{ni},i=0,\dotsc,q-2.

The coefficient of
L_{n,q-1}
is determined by the interpolation condition

\tilde{v}(t_{n+1})=v(t_{n+1})
and the property
L_{ni}(t_{n+1})=1

of the Legendre polynomials, which yields
(P_{q-2}v)(t_{n+1})=v_{0}+\dotsb+v_{q-2}.

In particular,
\tilde{v}=v
for
v\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1).

A.2.
Proof of the approximation property (
2.13
)

We first consider interpolation of functions on the unit interval

(0,1)
. Here we assume
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
and
q\geqslant 1

and hence Sobolev’s embedding
{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}\subset C([0,1];X)

holds. From (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) it is
clear that the interpolation operator

C([0,1];X)\to{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)},v\mapsto\tilde{v},
is bounded, so
that by Sobolev’s inequality,

(A.3)

\|\tilde{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}\leqslant C\|v\|_{C([0,1];X)}\leqslant C\|v\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}.

Moreover,

(A.4)

\tilde{v}=v\quad\forall v\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X}(q-1).

Hence, by a standard argument based on the Bramble–Hilbert lemma, we
have

(A.5)

\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}\leqslant C|v|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}.

Here
|v|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}=\|v^{(q)}\|_{L^{p}((0,1);X)}
denotes the seminorm.

In fact, by the Bramble–Hilbert lemma there is a Taylor polynomial

\bar{v}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{X}(q-1)
such that

(A.6)

\|v-\bar{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}\leqslant C|v|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}.

Hence, since by (
A.4
)
\tilde{\bar{v}}=\bar{v}
, and by
(
A.3
), (
A.6
),

\displaystyle\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|v-\bar{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}+\|\bar{v}-\tilde{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle=\|v-\bar{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}+\|(\bar{v}-{v})^{\tilde{}}\,\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|v-\bar{v}\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}\leqslant C|v|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)},

which is (
A.5
).

Since
|v|_{W^{j,p}((0,1);X)}\leqslant\|v\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}

and
|v|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);X)}\leqslant C\|v\|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}
,
from (
A.5
) we now conclude

\displaystyle|v-\tilde{v}|_{W^{j,p}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C|v|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)},\quad j=0,\dotsc,q,

\displaystyle\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C|v|_{W^{q,p}((0,1);X)}.

Finally, by a change of variables and with a slight abuse of notation,
we have

\displaystyle|v|_{W^{j,p}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle=k^{j-1/p}|v|_{W^{j,p}(J_{n};X)},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);X)}

\displaystyle=\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{n};X)}.

This proves

(A.7)

\displaystyle|v-\tilde{v}|_{W^{j,p}(J_{n};X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q-j}|v|_{W^{q,p}(J_{n};X)},\quad j=0,\dotsc,q,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\|v-\tilde{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{n};X)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ck^{q-1/p}|v|_{W^{q,p}(J_{n};X)}.

As a consequence of (
A.7
) with
j=0
(or of (
A.8
)) we have
(
2.13
). This is the only case that we need in the
present work; the other cases are included for the sake of
completeness.

The approximation property (
A.8
) for general
p
and for
Banach spaces is an analogue of
[
14
, (12.10)]
for
p=2
and for
Hilbert spaces.

A.3.
Proof of the approximation properties
(
2.14
) and (
2.15
)

We begin by proving that the operator
v\mapsto\hat{\tilde{v}}

reproduces polynomials of degree at most
q
. Again we assume

1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
.

Lemma A.1

(Reproduction property)
.

Assume that
v\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q).
Then, the following
reproduction property holds:

(A.9)

\hat{\tilde{v}}=v.

In particular, if
v\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q-1)
, then

\hat{\tilde{v}}=\tilde{v}=v
.

Proof.

Let us consider the Lagrange interpolant

w\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1),
with
w(0)=v(0),
of
v
at the Radau
nodes,

(A.10)

w(t_{nj})=v(t_{nj}),\quad j=1,\dotsc,q,

for
n=0,\dotsc,N-1.
Then, it follows immediately from the definition
(
1.4
) that
v\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{c}}(q)
is the
reconstruction of
w\in{\mathcal{X}}_{k}^{\text{d}}(q-1)
, that is,
v=\widehat{w}
.

It remains to show that
\tilde{v}=w,
that is, that
w
satisfies the
orthogonality condition

\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\big(v(t)-w(t)\big)t^{j}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0,\quad j=0,\dotsc,q-2.

This can be easily seen by using the Lagrange form of the
interpolation error
v(t)-w(t)=(v_{n}-w_{n}^{+})\ell_{n0}(t),
with

\ell_{n0}
the polynomial of degree
q
vanishing at the nodes

t_{n1},\dotsc,t_{nq}
and taking the value
1
at the node

t_{n0},
and the orthogonality of
\ell_{n0}
to polynomials of
degree at most
q-2

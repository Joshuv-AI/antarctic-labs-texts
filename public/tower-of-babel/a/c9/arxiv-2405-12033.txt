Propagation of equilibrium states in stable families of endomorphisms of ℙ k ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathbb{C}) .
Maxence Brevard, Karim Rakhimov

Abstract
. We prove that, within any holomorphic family of endomorphisms of ℙ k ​ ( ℂ ) \mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathbb{C}) in any dimension k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 and algebraic degree d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , the measurable holomorphic motion associated to dynamical stability in the sense of Berteloot-Bianchi-Dupont preserves the class of equilibrium states associated with weight functions ψ \psi satisfying sup ψ − inf ψ < log ⁡ d \sup\psi-\inf\psi<\log d .



1.
Introduction

Let
f:X\to X
be a continuous map on a compact metric space
X
.
Given a Borel bounded function
\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, an
equilibrium state
with respect to the
weight

\psi
is an
f
-invariant probability measure
\nu
on
X
maximizing the pressure functional

P_{\nu}(\psi):=h_{\nu}(f)+\int\psi\nu
, where
h:\nu\mapsto h_{\nu}(f)
is the measure-theoretic entropy. The existence of equilibrium states follows from a compactness argument, whereas their uniqueness is a strong statistical property. In this paper, we are interested in the persistence of equilibrium states and their uniqueness in
stable families of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}=\mathbb{P}^{k}(\mathbb{C})
, which we now define.

Given a (connected)
complex manifold
M
, a holomorphic family

\{f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}\}_{\lambda\in M}

of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
parametrized by
M
is
a holomorphic map
F:M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}\to M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
of the form
F(\lambda,z)=(\lambda,f_{\lambda}(z))
such that all the maps
f_{\lambda}

are holomorphic endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
of the same algebraic degree
d\geq 2
.
In dimension
k=1
, the notion of stability within such families was introduced by Mañé-Sad-Sullivan
[
33
]

and Lyubich
[
31
]
. A very fruitful
characterization based on potential theory
was later introduced by DeMarco
[
21
]
, see also
[
35
,
37
]
.
The family
\{f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\}_{\lambda\in M}
is said to be
stable
if there exists a
holomorphic motion
of the Julia sets
(i.e., the supports of the measures of maximal entropy of
f_{\lambda}

[
32
]
)
near every
\lambda_{0}\in M
, namely there exists a neighbourhood
U
of
\lambda_{0}
and,
for every
\lambda\in U
, an injective map

\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}:J_{\lambda_{0}}\to J_{\lambda}

depending
holomorphically on
\lambda
. By Hurwitz theorem,
these maps are automatically continuous in
z\in J_{\lambda_{0}}
, hence they
define a topological conjugacy between the systems
(J_{\lambda_{0}},f_{\lambda_{0}})
and
(J_{\lambda},f_{\lambda})
.
In this context, the action of the holomorphic motion over equilibrium states
is straightforward.
If
\nu
is a
f_{\lambda_{0}}
-invariant measure with support in
J_{\lambda_{0}}
, the measure
\nu_{\lambda}:=(\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda})_{*}\nu
inherits all the statistical properties from
\nu
.
In particular, if
\nu
is an equilibrium state with respect to a Hölder continuous weight, since the map
\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}
itself is a Hölder continuous conjugacy (see for instance
[
24
]
), the measure
\nu_{\lambda}
is still an equilibrium state with respect to a Hölder continuous weight. We refer to
[
36
]
as a general reference for the thermodynamical formalism in dimension
1
.

The definition and characterization of stability for families in
any dimension
k\geq 1
are due to Berteloot-Bianchi-Dupont
[
6
,
9
]
,
see also
[
5
]

and
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
34
]
for further characterizations.
As Hurwitz Theorem
does not hold in higher dimension,
the approach
relies on ergodic and pluripotential techniques.
In particular,
the holomorphic motion as above is replaced
by a more adapted measure-theoretic notion.
Consider the space
\mathcal{J}
of
all holomorphic maps
\gamma:M\to\mathbb{P}^{k}
such that
\gamma(\lambda)\in J_{\lambda}:=\supp\mu_{\lambda}
for any parameter
\lambda\in M
, where
\mu_{\lambda}
is the unique ergodic measure of maximal entropy of
f_{\lambda}
, see
[
17
,
23
,
28
]
.
Define the dynamical system
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{J}\to\mathcal{J}
induced on
\mathcal{J}
by

\mathcal{F}(\gamma)(\lambda):=f_{\lambda}(\gamma(\lambda))
, for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{J}
and
\lambda\in M
.
For each parameter
\lambda\in M
, we
also
consider the evaluation map
p_{\lambda}:\mathcal{J}\to J_{\lambda}
defined by
p_{\lambda}(\gamma):=\gamma(\lambda)
, where
\gamma\in\mathcal{J}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix
\lambda_{0}\in M
. Let
\nu
be an
f_{\lambda_{0}}
-invariant probability measure supported on
J_{\lambda_{0}}
. A

(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web

(or
\nu
-web
for brevity) is an

\mathcal{F}
-invariant compactly supported Borel probability measure
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{J}
such that

(p_{\lambda_{0}})_{*}\mathcal{M}=\nu
.

Dynamical stability in the sense of Berteloot-Bianchi-Dupont is equivalent to the existence of a special (
acritical
, see Remark
2.4
)
equilibrium web
, namely a probability measure
\mathcal{M}
which is a
(\lambda,\mu_{\lambda})
-web for every parameter
\lambda\in M
. Such a probability measure
is also called a

measurable holomorphic motion
.
The construction was generalized by
Bianchi and the second author

[
13
]

to a larger class of measures
including, among others, all measures whose measure-theoretic entropy is larger than
(k-1)\log d
.
This class includes, for instance, all equilibrium states with respect to any Hölder continuous function
\psi
with
\max\psi-\min\psi<\log d
, whose existence, uniqueness, and properties were recently proved in
[
10
,
11
,
38
,
40
]
, see also
[
27
]
.

In this context, it is a natural question
whether
the class of equilibrium states is preserved by the measurable holomorphic motions, i.e., if, given
an equilibrium state
\nu

for
f_{\lambda_{0}}
, the measures
\nu_{\lambda}:=(p_{\lambda})_{*}\mathcal{M}
also satisfy the same property, where
\mathcal{M}
is a
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web.
Observe that, if
f_{\lambda_{0}}
is hyperbolic (i.e., uniformly expanding on
J_{\lambda_{0}}
), the measurable holomorphic motion is indeed a holomorphic motion in a neighborhood of
\lambda_{0}
, hence gives a
topological conjugacy on the Julia sets
(see for instance
[
29
]
). On the other hand, unlike in dimension 1 or in specific families
[
1
]
,
we do not know whether the presence of a hyperbolic parameter in a stable family implies that all parameter are hyperbolic, hence the propagation of the equilibrium states in a stable family is not clear even in the presence of hyperbolic parameters.
More generally, the theory of stability in higher dimensions is known to have a number of different features with respect to the one-dimensional counterpart, see for instance
[
2
,
14
,
15
,
25
,
39
]
.

In this note, we give the following answer
to the above problem.

Main Theorem
.

Let
\{f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}\}_{\lambda\in M}
be a stable
family of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
of algebraic degree
d\geq 2
.
Consider a parameter
\lambda_{0}\in M
and a Borel function
\psi:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\sup\psi-\inf\psi<\log d
.
Assume there exists an
(respectively a unique) equilibrium state
\nu
for the system
(J_{\lambda_{0}},f_{\lambda_{0}})
with respect to
\psi

and let

\mathcal{M}
be a

(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web.
Then

(i)

the
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
is unique;

(ii)

for every parameter
\lambda\in M
, there exists a Borel function
\psi_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\nu_{\lambda}:=(p_{\lambda})_{*}\mathcal{M}
is a (resp. the unique) equilibrium state for the system

(J_{\lambda},f_{\lambda})
with respect to
\psi_{\lambda}
.

The condition
\sup\psi-\inf\psi<\log d
implies that the measure-theoretic entropy of
\nu
satisfies
h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})>(k-1)\log d

(hence, in particular, it has strictly positive Lyapunov exponents
[
20
,
26
]
, and falls within the scope of
[
13
]
). The existence of a
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web then follows from
[
13
, Corollary 1.5]
.
The proof of the above result relies
on the existence of a
\mathcal{M}
-full-measure subset of
\mathcal{J}
of elements whose graphs do not intersect
and is given in Section
2
.
In Section
3
we also give an interpretation of
\mathcal{M}
as an equilibrum
state
for the dynamical system
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
, see
Proposition
3.1
.
Following this interpretation, it is also natural to study the distribution of repelling graphs with respect to
\mathcal{M}
, which corresponds to the distribution of repelling points for the systems
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
. This is done in
[
12
]
.

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Given a holomorphic family
\{f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}\}_{\lambda\in M}
of endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
,
observe that for every parameter
\lambda\in M
, the evaluation map
p_{\lambda}:\mathcal{J}\to J_{\lambda}
is continuous, and that
p_{\lambda}\circ\mathcal{F}=f_{\lambda}\circ p_{\lambda}
.
The map
p_{\lambda}
may turn out not to be injective. This is due to the possible
existence of some crossings between graphs in
\mathcal{J}
.
The notion of lamination, introduced in
[
6
]
, will be central in our approach.

Definition 2.1
.

A
lamination
is a Borel subset
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{J}
such that for any maps
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
, we have

\Gamma_{\gamma_{1}}\cap\Gamma_{\gamma_{2}}\neq\emptyset\implies\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2},

where
\Gamma_{\gamma}\subset M\times\mathbb{P}^{k}
denotes the graph of an element
\gamma\in\mathcal{J}
.
If
\mathcal{M}
is a Borel probability measure on
\mathcal{J}
, we say that
\mathcal{L}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-lamination
if, furthermore,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L})=1
.

Given an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant lamination
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{J}
, one can consider the restriction
p_{\lambda}|_{\mathcal{L}}\>:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
of the application
p_{\lambda}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}:=p_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L})
. The map
p_{\lambda}|_{\mathcal{L}}
is automatically
1
-to-
1
. Yet, observe that
p_{\lambda}|_{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}
may not be continuous.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{J}
be a lamination, and fix a parameter
\lambda\in M
. Then,
for any Borel subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{J}
, we have

\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{L}\cap p_{\lambda}^{-1}(p_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A})).

In particular, for any probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, there exists at most one
(\lambda,\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
such that

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L})=1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{J}
be a Borel set.
The direct inclusion is immediate. For the converse one, take
\gamma\in\mathcal{L}

such that
\gamma(\lambda)\in p_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A})
.
Take

\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A}
such that
\gamma(\lambda)=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)
.
Since
\mathcal{L}
is a lamination, we have
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
. In particular, we have
\gamma\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A}
, which proves the desired equality.

Let now
\mathcal{M}
be as in the statement. We have

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L}\cap p_{\lambda}^{-1}(p_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A})))=\nu(p_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A})).

The last expression does not depend on the choice of the
(\lambda,\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
. The proof is complete.
∎

From now on, we will assume that the family
\{f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}\}_{\lambda\in M}
is stable.
Our goal is to propagate the class of equilibrium states. To do this, we will make use of the
\mathcal{M}
-laminations.
Given a lamination
\mathcal{L}
,
for every
\lambda_{0},\lambda\in M
we can consider the bijection
\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}}:=p_{\lambda}\circ p_{\lambda_{0}}|_{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}:\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}}\to\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\{f_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{k}\}_{\lambda\in M}
be a stable
family of holomorphic endomorphisms of
\mathbb{P}^{k}
of
algebraic
degree
d\geq 2
. Let
\lambda_{0}\in M
and
\nu
be an ergodic
f_{\lambda_{0}}
-invariant measure with
h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})>(k-1)\log d
. Then

(i)

there exists a unique
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
is ergodic and for any parameter
\lambda\in M
, we have
h_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{F})=

h_{\nu_{\lambda}}(f_{\lambda})
, where
\nu_{\lambda}:=(p_{\lambda})_{*}\mathcal{M}
;

(ii)

if
\mathcal{L}
is an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant
\mathcal{M}
-lamination and
\psi:J_{\lambda_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Borel measurable function,
then for any parameter
\lambda\in M
, we have

P_{\nu_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})=P_{\nu}(\psi)
, where
\psi_{\lambda}:=\psi\circ(\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The web
\mathcal{M}
in Proposition
2.3
is actually
acritical
(see
[
13
, Definition 1.3]
and
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
). We do not stress the acriticality in this paper, since the existence of an
\mathcal{M}
-lamination guarantees this condition, and we only work with such laminations.

Remark 2.5
.

If we are allowed to shrink
M
to some neighbourhood of
\lambda_{0}
(depending on
\nu
),
the assumption on
\nu

can be weakened as in
[
13
]
, namely it is enough that
\nu
is a measure on the Julia set with
strictly positive Lyapunov exponents and not giving mass to the post-critical set of
f_{\lambda_{0}}
,
see
[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
.
The fact that any
\nu
satisfies these conditions is a consequence of

[
20
,
26
]
and
[
19
,
22
]
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

We first prove (i). By
[
13
, Corollary 1.5]
there exists an ergodic
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
and an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant
\mathcal{M}
-lamination
\mathcal{L}
.
To show the uniqueness property, consider another
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. We have
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\mathcal{L})=\nu(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}})=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L})=1
. Lemma
2.2
then yields
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.
Take
\lambda\in M
.
The application
p_{\lambda}|_{\mathcal{L}}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is continuous, hence Borel measurable. The space
\mathcal{J}
being Polish, as a closed subspace of the space
\mathcal{O}(M,\mathbb{P}^{k})
of all holomorphic functions from
M
to
\mathbb{P}^{k}
, since
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{J}
is a Borel subset, the inverse application
p_{\lambda}|_{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}
is automatically Borel measurable (see for example
[
30
, Theorem 3 §39.V]
).
Since
\nu_{\lambda}=(p_{\lambda})_{*}\mathcal{M}
, the two measurable systems
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{F},\mathcal{M})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},f_{\lambda},\nu_{\lambda})
are then conjugated by
p_{\lambda}
.
The conclusion follows from the fact that
the measure-theoretic entropy is invariant under measurable conjugacy between subsets of full measure.

We now prove (ii). Fix
\lambda\in M
. The above shows that the measurable isomorphism
\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}}
conjugates the two systems
(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}},f_{\lambda_{0}},\nu)
and
(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},f_{\lambda},\nu_{\lambda})
, and that
h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})=h_{\nu_{\lambda}}(f_{\lambda})
. It remains to show that
\int\psi\nu=\int\psi_{\lambda}\nu_{\lambda}
.
Since
(\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}})_{*}\nu_{\lambda}=\nu
and
(\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}})^{*}\psi_{\lambda}=\psi
on
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int\psi_{\lambda}\nu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}}\psi_{\lambda}\nu_{\lambda}=\int_{\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}})}\psi_{\lambda}\nu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}}}(\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}})^{*}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot(\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}})^{*}\nu_{\lambda}=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}}}\psi\nu=\int\psi\nu.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Since the mapping

\Phi_{\lambda_{0},\lambda}^{\mathcal{L}}
is a measurable conjugacy, the proof of Proposition
2.3
shows that the measure
\nu_{\lambda}
inherits the statistical properties that
\nu
may have,
such as (exponential) mixing,
Central Limit Theorem, Almost Sure Invariant Principle or Large Deviation Principle (see
[
10
,
11
,
27
,
38
,
40
]
for large classes of examples of measures satisfying these properties). On the other hand, as the space of Hölder functions is in general not preserved by the measurable conjugacy, the space of observables associated with each
\nu_{\lambda}
will in general depend on
\lambda
.

Proof of the Main Theorem.

Recall that
\mu_{\lambda}
denotes the measure of maximal entropy of the endomorphism
f_{\lambda}
.
Up to adding to
\psi
a constant, for simplicity, we will assume that
\inf\psi=0
.
Then, by the definition of the equilibrium state
\nu
, we have

(2.2)

h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})+\int\psi\nu=P_{\nu}(\psi)\geq P_{\mu_{\lambda_{0}}}(\psi)=k\log d+\int\psi\mu_{\lambda_{0}}\geq k\log d.

By assumption, we have

\int\psi\nu<\log d
. Hence, (
2.2
) yields
h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})>(k-1)\log d
.
Proposition
2.3
then yields a unique
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
, which proves assertion (i).

Denote
\nu_{\lambda}=(p_{\lambda})_{*}\mathcal{M}
. Observe that Proposition
2.3
also gives that
h_{\nu_{\lambda}}(f_{\lambda})>(k-1)\log d
for any parameter
\lambda\in M
.
For every parameter
\lambda\in M
, consider the Borel function
\psi_{\lambda}:\mathbb{P}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
as defined in Proposition
2.3
.
To prove assertion (ii), consider a parameter
\lambda\in M
and assume that
there exists an ergodic
f_{\lambda}
-invariant measure
\omega_{\lambda}
such that

(2.3)

P_{\omega_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})\geq P_{\nu_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda}).

Up to replacing
\omega_{\lambda}
by
\mu_{\lambda}

if

P_{\omega_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})<P_{\mu_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})
,
we can also assume that

h_{\omega_{\lambda}}(f_{\lambda})>(k-1)\log d
.

Proposition
2.3
then yields a
(\lambda,\omega_{\lambda})
-web
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
and
we have

(2.4)

P_{\omega}(\psi)=P_{\omega_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})\geq P_{\nu_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})=P_{\nu}(\psi),

where
\omega:=(p_{\lambda_{0}})_{*}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.
As
\nu
is an equilibrium state with respect to
\psi
, the inequality in (
2.4
)
is actually an equality. Thus,
\nu_{\lambda}
is an equilibrium state with respect to
\psi_{\lambda}
.

Assume now that
\nu
is unique. Then, (
2.4
) shows that
\omega
as above is an equilibrium state with respect to
\psi
as well. By the uniqueness assumption, we have
\omega=\nu
and both
\mathcal{M}
satisfying
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
are
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-webs and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(p_{\lambda_{0}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}})=\omega(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}})=\nu(\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}})=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{L})=1
. Lemma
2.2
then yields
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. In particular, one has
\omega_{\lambda}=\nu_{\lambda}
, which ends the proof.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

As we have seen above, the condition
\sup\psi-\inf\psi<\log d
guarantees that we have
h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})>(k-1)\log d
. This,
thanks to Proposition
2.3
,
is one of the main ingredients in the proof of the Main Theorem. Let us assume that we are just given
an equilibrium state
\nu
with respect to some bounded Borel function
\psi

(with no further conditions)
satisfying
h_{\nu}(f_{\lambda_{0}})>(k-1)\log d
.
Then, with the notations as in the proof of the Main Theorem,
Proposition
2.3
still
implies that
P_{\nu_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})=P_{\nu}(\psi)
. On the other hand, in this case, a priori
\nu_{\lambda}
may not be an equilibrium state of
\psi_{\lambda}
. Indeed, following the proof of the Main Theorem,
let
\omega_{\lambda}
be an
f_{\lambda}
-invariant measure satisfying (
2.3
).
Even if
P_{\omega_{\lambda}}(\psi_{\lambda})\geq k\log d
, since we do not have any condition on
\psi
, the integral
\int\psi_{\lambda}\omega_{\lambda}
can be large enough so that

h_{\omega_{\lambda}}(f_{\lambda})\leq(k-1)\log d
.
Hence, in this case, we cannot apply Proposition
2.3
to obtain (
2.4
).

3.
The web
\mathcal{M}
is an equilibrium state

In this section, we observe that the system
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
itself is well-suited for the thermodynamical formalism. To this purpose, an additional compactness assumption is necessary. Define an
equilibrium state
for the system
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
with respect to a Borel bounded function
\Psi:\mathcal{J}\to\mathbb{R}
as an ergodic
\mathcal{F}
-invariant compactly supported probability measure
\mathcal{M}
on
\mathcal{J}
maximizing the functional
P_{\mathcal{M}}(\Psi)=h_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{F})+\int\Psi\mathcal{M}
.
We have to ensure that the set of ergodic
\mathcal{F}
-invariant compactly supported measures on
\mathcal{J}
is not empty. In general, one may need to shrink
M
so that
\mathcal{J}
becomes compact. Assuming stability, that is simply guaranteed by the existence of an
equilibrium web as in
[
6
]
.

In this framework, given an equilibrium state
\nu
with respect to a weight function
\psi
at a parameter
\lambda_{0}
, we construct a naturally defined weight function
\Psi
on
\mathcal{J}
, compatible with the measurable holomorphic motion, with respect to which the
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
from the Main Theorem is an equilibrium state. We show as well that the uniqueness property holds.
In particular, the equilibrium web of Berteloot-Bianchi-Dupont is the unique measure of maximal entropy for the dynamical system
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the hypothesis of the Main Theorem, there exists a Borel bounded function
\Psi:\mathcal{J}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\Psi=\psi\circ p_{\lambda_{0}}

\mathcal{M}
-a.e. and

P_{\mathcal{M}}(\Psi)=P_{\nu}(\psi)
. Moreover, the
(\lambda_{0},\nu)
-web
\mathcal{M}
is an (resp. the unique) equilibrium state for the system
(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F})
with respect to
\Psi
.

Proof.

As in the proof of the Main Theorem,
up to adding to
\psi
a constant, for simplicity, we will assume that
\inf\psi=0
. As in the proof of Proposition
2.3
, let
\mathcal{L}

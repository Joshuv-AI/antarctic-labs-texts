Conditional Lipschitz shadowing for ordinary differential equations
Lucas Backes, Davor Dragičević, Masakazu Onitsuka, Mihály Pituk

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of conditional Lipschitz shadowing, which does not aim to shadow every pseudo-orbit, but only those which belong to a certain prescribed set. We establish two types of sufficient conditions under which certain nonautonomous ordinary differential equations have such a property. The first criterion applies to a semilinear differential equation provided that its linear part is hyperbolic and the nonlinearity is small in a neighborhood of the prescribed set. The second criterion requires that the logarithmic norm of the derivative of the right-hand side with respect to the state variable is uniformly negative in a neighborhood of the prescribed set. The results are applicable to important classes of model equations including the logistic equation, whose conditional shadowing has recently been studied. Several examples are constructed showing that the obtained conditions are optimal.

1.
Introduction

One of the main properties of chaotic dynamical systems is that different orbits starting close by will move apart as time evolves and
this divergence can be very fast. In particular, whenever performing numerical experiments, small numerical errors, which are inherent to the process due to computer round off errors, tend to grow. Thus, a central question
when performing such numerical experiments is if the resulting behaviour presented by the computer reflects the dynamics of the actual system. It turns out that for some classes of chaotic systems, like the hyperbolic ones, even though a numerical orbit containing round off errors will diverge rapidly from the true orbit with the same initial condition, there exists a different true orbit
which stays near the noisy orbit.
Systems exhibiting this property are said to have the
shadowing property
. In other words, a dynamical system
has the shadowing property if close to its approximate orbits we can find exact ones. The main objective of the present paper is to present sufficient conditions under which a general class of nonautonomous nonlinear ordinary differential equations exhibits a new variant of
shadowing property, the so-called
conditional Lipschitz shadowing property
,
defined below (see Definition
2
).

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denote the
n
-dimensional space of real column vectors and the space of
n\times n
matrices with real entries, respectively. The symbol
|\cdot|
denotes any convenient norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the associated induced norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Consider the ordinary differential equation

(1.1)

x^{\prime}=g(t,x),

where
g\colon[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a continuous. We are interested in the noncontinuable solutions of (
1.1
) starting at
t=0
. It is known that these solutions are defined on intervals of type
[0,\tau)
, where
\tau\in(0,\infty]
may depend on the solution
x
. For this reason, we will consider pseudosolutions (approximate solutions) of (
1.1
) on intervals of the same type in the following sense.
Given
\tau\in(0,\infty]
, by a
pseudosolution of Eq. (
1.1
) on
[0,\tau)
, we mean any continuously differentiable function
y\colon[0,\tau)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1.2)

\sigma_{y}:=\sup_{0\leq t<\tau}|y^{\prime}(t)-g(t,y(t))|<\infty.

The function
e_{y}\colon[0,\tau)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by

(1.3)

e_{y}(t):=|y^{\prime}(t)-g(t,y(t))|\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\tau)$,}

will be called the
error function
and the quantity
\sigma_{y}
is the
maximum error
corresponding to
y
.
Let us recall the definition of the standard Lipschitz shadowing property.

Definition 1
.

We say that Eq. (
1.1
) has the
Lipschitz shadowing property
if there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\kappa>0
with the following property:
if
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
y
is a pseudosolution of (
1.1
) on
[0,\tau)
for some
\tau\in(0,\infty]
such that

\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon
, then Eq. (
1.1
) has a solution
x
on
[0,\tau)
satisfying

(1.4)

\sup_{0\leq t<\tau}|x(t)-y(t)|\leq\kappa\varepsilon.

The concept of Lipschitz shadowing is closely related to the stronger notion of
Hyers–Ulam stability

(
Ulam stability
),
which requires that the condition in the above definition is satisfied for every
\varepsilon>0
. For a related concept in the theory of smooth dynamical systems, see
[
15
, Definition 1.5]
.

Consider the semilinear differential equation

(1.5)

x^{\prime}=A(t)x+f(t,x)

as a perturbation of the linear equation

(1.6)

x^{\prime}=A(t)x,

where
A\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
f\colon[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
are continuous.
The following known result provides a sufficient condition for the Lipschitz shadowing property of (
1.5
) (see
[
2
, Theorem 6]
).

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that the linear equation (
1.6
) has an exponential dichotomy on
[0,\infty)
(see Definition
3
) and there exists
L\geq 0
such that

(1.7)

|f(t,x_{1})-f(t,x_{2})|\leq L|x_{1}-x_{2}|\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$.}

If
L
is sufficiently small, then Eq. (
1.5
) has the Lipschitz shadowing property.

Note that Theorem
1
can be extended to the more general class of delay differential equations (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
). For further related results about shadowing and Hyers–Ulam stability of ordinary differential equations and their discrete counterparts,
see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
and references therein.

Some recent studies (see
[
12
,
19
]
) have been concerned with the shadowing (Hyers–Ulam stability) of the scalar
logistic equation

(1.8)

x^{\prime}=x(ax+b),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},

which is a particular case of Eq. (
1.5
) when
n=1
,
A(t)=b
and

f(t,x)=ax^{2}
. Note that Theorem
1
cannot be applied to Eq. (
1.8
) because
f
does not satisfy the global Lipschitz condition (
1.7
). In
[
19
]
, it has been shown that
Eq. (
1.8
) with
a=-1
and
b=1
is not Hyers–Ulam stable, but certain approximate solutions still can be shadowed by true solutions.

Motivated by this observation, we introduce the notion of conditional Lipschitz shadowing,
which does not require the validity of the Lipschitz shadowing property for all pseudosolutions, but only for those which belong to a given set
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
H
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We say that Eq. (
1.1
) has the
conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
if there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\kappa>0
with the following property: if
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
y
is a pseudosolution of (
1.1
) on
[0,\tau)
for some
\tau\in(0,\infty]
such that

\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon
and
y(t)\in H
for all
t\in[0,\tau)
, then Eq. (
1.1
) has a solution
x
on
[0,\tau)
satisfying (
1.4
).

Evidently, the standard Lipschitz shadowing property is a special case of the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property with
H=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A different concept of conditional shadowing for discrete nonautonomous systems in a Banach space has recently been introduced by Pilyugin
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we establish two types of sufficient conditions under which certain classes of ordinary differential equations have the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in a given set
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In Sec.
2
, we consider the semilinear differential equation (
1.5
). The main result of this part is formulated in Theorem
2
, which is a generalization of Theorem
1
to the case of conditional Lipschitz shadowing. It says that Eq. (
1.5
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in a prescribed set
H
whenever its linear part has an exponential dichotomy and the nonlinearity
f(t,x)
is Lipschitz in
x
in a neighborhood of
H
(uniformly in
t
) with a sufficiently small Lipschitz constant. The smallness condition on the Lipschitz constant can be expressed in terms the dichotomy constants of the linear part. The importance of the obtained sufficient condition will be shown by an application to a scalar logistic equation. In Example
1
, we show that our choice of pseudosolutions is optimal.

In Sec.
3
, we present sufficient conditions for the conditional Lipschitz shadowing of Eq. (
1.1
) in terms of the logarithmic norm of
g_{x}(t,x)
, the partial derivative of
g
with respect to
x
. The
logarithmic norm
(
Lozinskiĭ measure
) of
a square matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is defined by

(1.9)

\mu(A):=\lim_{h\to 0+}\frac{|I+hA|-1}{h}\qquad\text{for $A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$},

where
I
is the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
It should be noted that
\mu
is not a norm, since it can take negative values. In the scalar case (
n=1
), we have that
\mu(A)=A
. The values of
\mu(A)
for the standard norms in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be given explicitly (see Sec.
3
).
The main result of this section, Theorem
5
, says that if
\mu(g_{x}(t,x))
is uniformly negative (bounded away from zero) for all
t\geq 0
and
x
in a neighborhood of the given set
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then Eq. (
1.1
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
.
To the best of our knowledge, this criterion has no previous analogue. It gives a new result even in the case of the standard Lipschitz shadowing. The importance and the sharpness of the assumptions
will be shown in a special case of the Kermack–McKendrick equation from epidemiology.

2.
Conditional Lipschitz shadowing via exponential dichotomy

In this section, we give sufficient conditions for the conditional Lipschitz shadowing of the semilinear equation (
1.5
).

Let
\varPhi
be a fundamental matrix solution of the linear equation (
1.6
) so that its transition matrix
T(t,s)
is given by

T(t,s):=\varPhi(t)\varPhi^{-1}(s)\qquad\text{for $t,s\in[0,\infty)$}.

Definition 3
.

We say that Eq. (
1.6
) has an
exponential dichotomy
on
[0,\infty)
if there exist a family of projections
\left(P(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and constants
N,\lambda>0
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle P(t)T(t,s)=T(t,s)P(s)\qquad\text{for all $t,s\in[0,\infty)$},

(2.2)

\displaystyle|T(t,s)P(s)|\leq Ne^{-\lambda(t-s)}\qquad\text{whenever $t\geq s\geq 0$}

and

(2.3)

|T(t,s)(I-P(s))|\leq Ne^{-\lambda(s-t)}\qquad\text{whenever $0\leq t\leq s$}.

If, in addition,
P(t)=I
(
P(t)=0
) identically for
t\geq 0
, we say that Eq. (
1.6
) admits an
exponential contraction (exponential expansion)
on
[0,\infty)
.

Throughout the paper, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta>0
,
B_{\delta}(x)
will denote the closed
\delta
-neighborhood of
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
B_{\delta}(x):=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|y-x|\leq\delta\,\}
. For
\emptyset\neq H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the closed
\delta
-neighborhood of
H
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H):=\bigcup_{x\in H}B_{\delta}(x).

The main result of this section is the following generalization of Theorem
1
to the case of conditional Lipschitz shadowing.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\emptyset\neq H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential dichotomy on
[0,\infty)
and that there exist
\delta,L>0
such that

(2.4)

|f(t,x_{1})-f(t,x_{2})|\leq L|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(H)$.}

If

(2.5)

L<\frac{\lambda}{2N},

where
N,\lambda>0
are as in Definition
3
,
then Eq. (
1.5
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
.

Proof.

From (
2.5
), it follows that the equation

(2.6)

\frac{2N}{\lambda}(L\kappa+1)=\kappa

has a unique solution
\kappa
given by

\kappa:=\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{2N}-L\biggr)^{-1}=\frac{2N}{\lambda-2NL}>0.

Set
\varepsilon_{0}:=\kappa^{-1}\delta>0
.
Suppose that

0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
y
is a pseudosolution of (
1.5
) on
[0,\tau)
for some
\tau\in(0,\infty]
such that

\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon
and
y(t)\in H
for all
t\in[0,\tau)
. Observe that the transformation

z=x-y
reduces Eq. (
1.5
) to the equation

(2.7)

z^{\prime}=A(t)z+f(t,y(t)+z)-f(t,y(t))+h_{y}(t),

where

(2.8)

h_{y}(t):=A(t)y(t)+f(t,y(t))-y^{\prime}(t)\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\tau)$}.

Clearly,
|h_{y}(t)|=e_{y}(t)
for
t\in[0,\tau)
, where
e_{y}
is the error function corresponding to the pseudosolution
y
of Eq. (
1.5
). Hence

(2.9)

\sup_{0\leq t<\tau}|h_{y}(t)|=\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{b}:=C([0,\tau),\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the Banach space of bounded and continuous functions
z\colon[0,\tau)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the supremum norm,

\|z\|=\sup_{t\in[0,\tau)}|z(t)|,\qquad z\in\mathcal{C}_{b}.

Set

S:=\{\,z\in\mathcal{C}_{b}:\|z\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon\,\}.

Clearly,
S
is a nonempty and closed subset of
\mathcal{C}_{b}
. For
z\in S
and
t\in[0,\tau)
, define

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}z)(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)\left[\,f(s,y(s)+z(s))-f(s,y(s))+h_{y}(s)\,\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{\tau}T(t,s)(I-P(s))\left[\,f(s,y(s)+z(s))-f(s,y(s))+h_{y}(s)\,\right]\,ds.

Take an arbitrary
z\in S
and
s\in[0,\tau)
. Then,

|(y(s)+z(s))-y(s)|=|z(s)|\leq\|z\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon\leq\kappa\varepsilon_{0}=\delta.

Therefore,

(2.10)

y(s)\in H\subset\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H)\qquad\text{and}\qquad y(s)+z(s)\in B_{\delta}(y(s))\subset\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H),

which, together with (
2.4
), implies that

|f(s,y(s)+z(s))-f(s,y(s))|\leq L|z(s)|\leq L\|z\|\leq L\kappa\varepsilon.

This, combined with (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.9
) (see also (
2.6
)) yields that

(2.11)

\begin{split}&|(\mathcal{F}z)(t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|T(t,s)P(s)|\left(\,|f(s,y(s)+z(s))-f(s,y(s))|+|h_{y}(s)|\,\right)\,ds\\
&\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}|T(t,s)(I-P(s))|\left(\,|f(s,y(s)+z(s))-f(s,y(s))|+|h_{y}(s)|\,\right)\,ds\\
&\leq\left(L\kappa\varepsilon+\varepsilon\right)\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}Ne^{-\lambda(t-s)}\,ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}Ne^{-\lambda(s-t)}\,ds\biggr)\\
&\leq\frac{2N}{\lambda}\left(L\kappa+1\right)\varepsilon=\kappa\varepsilon,\end{split}

for
z\in S
and
t\in[0,\tau)
.
We conclude that
\mathcal{F}z
is well-defined and
\mathcal{F}(S)\subset S
.

Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in S
. In view of (
2.10
), we have that
y(s)+z_{j}(s)\subset\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H)
for
s\in[0,\tau)
and
j=1,2
. Hence,

|f(s,y(s)+z_{1}(s))-f(s,y(s)+z_{2}(s))|\leq L|z_{1}(s)-z_{2}(s)|\leq L\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|,

for
s\in[0,\tau)
.
Consequently,

(2.12)

\begin{split}&|(\mathcal{F}z_{1})(t)-(\mathcal{F}z_{2})(t)|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}|T(t,s)P(s)|\left(\,|f(s,y(s)+z_{1}(s))-f(s,y(s)+z_{2}(s))|\,\right)\,ds\\
&\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}|T(t,s)(I-P(s))|\left(\,|f(s,y(s)+z_{1}(s))-f(s,y(s)+z_{2}(s))|\,\right)\,ds\\
&\leq L\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}Ne^{-\lambda(t-s)}\,ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}Ne^{-\lambda(s-t)}\,ds\biggr)\\
&\leq\frac{2N}{\lambda}L\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|,\end{split}

for
t\in[0,\tau)
.
Therefore, for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in S
,

\|\mathcal{F}z_{1}-\mathcal{F}z_{2}\|\leq q\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|\qquad\text{with $q:=\frac{2N}{\lambda}L<1$}.

Thus,
\mathcal{F}\colon S\rightarrow S
is a contraction and it has a unique fixed point
z
in
S
. It follows by differentiation that
z
is a solution of Eq. (
2.7
) on
[0,\tau)
. Moreover,
z\in S
implies that

\sup_{t\in[0,\tau)}|z(t)|=\|z\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon.

Therefore,
x=z+y
is a solution of Eq. (
1.5
) on
[0,\tau)
with the desired property (
1.4
). The proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Remark 2
.

If
D
is a nonempty set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then its

, denoted by

, is the smallest convex set in

which contains

. A sufficient condition for the Lipschitz condition (

) to hold is that

is continuously differentiable and

(2.13)

The following result is an improvement of Theorem
2
in the particular case when Eq. (

) admits an exponential contraction or exponential expansion. It shows that in these settings the smallness condition (

) for the Lipschitz constant

can be weakened.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\emptyset\neq H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential contraction or exponential expansion on
[0,\infty)
and there exist
\delta,L>0
such that (
2.4
) holds.
If

(2.14)

L<\frac{\lambda}{N},

then Eq. (
1.5
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in a similar manner as the proof of Theorem
2
. Take
\kappa>0
such that

\frac{N}{\lambda}(L\kappa+1)=\kappa.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
y
,
S
and
\mathcal{F}
be as in the proof of Theorem

. By arguing as in (
2.11
) (recall that either

or

), we have that

|(\mathcal{F}z)(t)|\leq\frac{N}{\lambda}(L\kappa+1)\varepsilon=\kappa\varepsilon

for

and

. Moreover, by similar estimates as in (

), we conclude that

|(\mathcal{F}z_{1})(t)-(\mathcal{F}z_{2})(t)|\leq\frac{N}{\lambda}L\|z_{1}-z_{2}\|,

for

and

. Now one can complete the proof by the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem

.
∎

2

and

gives sufficient conditions under which Eq. (
1.5
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in a

Corollary 1
.

Let
\rho>0
. Suppose that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential dichotomy on
[0,\infty)
and there exist
\delta,L>0
such that

(2.15)

|f(t,x_{1})-f(t,x_{2})|\leq L|x_{1}-x_{2}|\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{\rho+\delta}(0)$}.

Then, (
2.5
) implies that Eq. (

) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in

. Moreover, if instead of the existence of an exponential dichotomy, we assume that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential contraction or exponential expansion on

, then
the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property of Eq. (
1.5
) in

holds under the weaker condition (
2.14
).

Let
H=B_{\rho}(0)
. Then

and
the conclusion follows from Theorems

and

.

The following theorem gives another reason for the interest in those pseudosolutions of Eq. (
1.5
) which lie in a given ball around the origin.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\rho>0
. Suppose that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential dichotomy on
[0,\infty)
and there exists
L>0
such that

(2.16)

|f(t,x)|\leq L|x|\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x\in B_{\rho}(0)$}.

Then, (
2.5
) implies that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that if
y
is a pseudosolution of Eq. (
1.5
) on
[0,\tau)
for some
\tau\in(0,\infty]
with
\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon
, then Eq. (
1.5
) has a pseudosolution
z
on
[0,\tau)
which lies in
B_{\rho}(0)
and has the same error function as
y
, i.e.

(2.17)

e_{z}(t)=e_{y}(t)\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\tau)$}.

Proof.

In view of (
2.5
), the equation

(2.18)

\frac{2N}{\lambda}(L\rho+\varepsilon)=\rho

has a unique solution
\varepsilon
given by

\varepsilon:=\biggl(\frac{\lambda}{2N}-L\biggr)\rho>0.

Suppose that
y
is a pseudosolution of (
1.5
) on
[0,\tau)
for some
\tau\in(0,\infty]
with
\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon
.
Let
\mathcal{C}:=C([0,\tau),\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the topological vector space of all continuous functions
z\colon[0,\tau)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of
[0,\tau)
. Let

(2.19)

S:=\biggl\{\,z\in\mathcal{C}:\ \text{$\sup_{t\in[0,\tau)}|z(t)|\leq\rho$}\biggr\}.

Clearly,
S
is a nonempty, closed and convex subset of
\mathcal{C}
. For
z\in S
and
t\in[0,\tau)
, set

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}z)(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}T(t,s)P(s)\left[\,f(s,z(s))-h_{y}(s)\,\right]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{t}^{\tau}T(t,s)(I-P(s))\left[\,f(s,z(s))-h_{y}(s)\,\right]\,ds,

with
h_{y}
as in (
2.8
).
In view of (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.16
), we have for
z\in S
and
t\in[0,\tau)
,

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{F}z)(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|T(t,s)P(s)|(\,L|z(s)|+|h_{y}(s)|\,)\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{t}^{\tau}|T(t,s)(I-P(s))|(\,L|z(s)|+|h_{y}(s)|\,)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}Ne^{-\lambda(t-s)}(L\rho+\sigma_{y})\,ds+\int_{t}^{\infty}Ne^{-\lambda(s-t)}(L\rho+\sigma_{y})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2N}{\lambda}(L\rho+\varepsilon)=\rho,

where the last equality follows from (
2.18
).
Thus,
\mathcal{F}z
is well-defined and
\mathcal{F}(S)\subset S
.
It follows in a standard manner that
\mathcal{F}\colon S\rightarrow S
is continuous. In view of (
2.19
), the functions from the image set
\mathcal{F}(S)\subset S
are uniformly bounded on
[0,\tau)
. Take now an arbitrary compact subinterval
I\subset[0,\tau)
and
z\in S
. It follows by differentiation that

(2.20)

(\mathcal{F}z)^{\prime}(t)=A(t)(\mathcal{F}z)(t)+f(t,z(t))-h_{y}(t),\qquad t\in[0,\tau).

Hence,

\sup_{t\in I}|(\mathcal{F}z)^{\prime}(t)|\leq\rho\max_{t\in I}|A(t)|+L\rho+\varepsilon.

We conclude that the derivatives of the functions in
\mathcal{F}(S)
are uniformly bounded on each compact subinterval of
[0,\tau)
,
which implies that the functions in
\mathcal{F}(S)
are equicontinuous on every compact subinterval of
[0,\tau)
. Therefore, the closure of
\mathcal{F}(S)
is compact.
By the application of the Schauder–Tychonoff fixed point theorem (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Chap. I, p. 9]
), we conclude that there exists
z\in S
such that
z=\mathcal{F}z
. From (
2.20
), it follows that

z^{\prime}(t)=A(t)z(t)+f(t,z(t))-h_{y}(t),\qquad t\in[0,\tau).

Hence (see (
2.8
)),
h_{z}=h_{y}
identically on
[0,\tau)
, and hence (
2.17
) holds. Finally, (
2.19
) shows that
z
lies in
B_{\rho}(0)
.
∎

In the following result, we point out another simple consequence of Theorems
2
and
3
.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential dichotomy on
[0,\infty)
.
Assume that
f\colon[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuously differentiable and that there exist
L_{1}\geq 0
and
L_{2}>0
such that

(2.21)

|f_{x}(t,x)|\leq L_{1}+L_{2}|x|\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

If

(2.22)

L_{1}<\frac{\lambda}{2N}

and

(2.23)

0<\rho<\frac{1}{L_{2}}\biggl(\frac{\lambda}{2N}-L_{1}\biggr),

then Eq. (
1.5
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
B_{\rho}(0)
.
Moreover, if instead of the existence of an exponential dichotomy, we assume that Eq. (
1.6
) has an exponential contraction or exponential expansion on
[0,\infty)
, then
the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property of Eq. (
1.5
) in
B_{\rho}(0)
holds under the weaker conditions

(2.24)

L_{1}<\frac{\lambda}{N}

and

(2.25)

0<\rho<\frac{1}{L_{2}}\biggl(\frac{\lambda}{N}-L_{1}\biggr).

Proof.

We give a proof of the first statement of the corollary. The proof of the second statement is similar and hence it is omitted.

In view of (
2.23
), we have that

L_{1}+L_{2}\rho<\frac{\lambda}{2N}.

Choose
\delta>0
such that

L_{1}+L_{2}(\rho+\delta)<\frac{\lambda}{2N}.

From this and (
2.21
), we have
for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in B_{\rho+\delta}(0)
,

|f_{x}(t,x)|\leq L_{1}+L_{2}|x|\leq L_{1}+L_{2}(\rho+\delta)<\frac{\lambda}{2N}.

This implies that condition (
2.15
) of Corollary
1
holds with
L:=L_{1}+L_{2}(\rho+\delta)
. Since
L
satisfies (
2.5
), the Lipschitz shadowing property of Eq. (
1.5
) in
B_{\rho}(0)
follows from the first statement of Corollary
1
.
∎

In the following example, we show the importance and the sharpness of the assumptions of Corollary

.

Example 1
.

Consider the scalar autonomous equation

(2.26)

x^{\prime}=-x-x^{2}-\frac{1}{4}=-\biggl(x+\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{2},

which is a special case of (
1.5
) when
n=1
,
A(t)=-1
and
f(t,x)=-x^{2}-\frac{1}{4}
for
t\in[0,\infty)
and

. Its linear part

admits an exponential contraction with
T(t,s)=e^{-(t-s)}
,
N=1
and

. Moreover,
f
satisfies (
2.21
) with
L_{1}=0
and
L_{2}=2
. Therefore, conditions (
2.24
) and (
2.25
) of Corollary
2
reduce to
0<\rho<1/2
. It follows from Corollary

that (

B_{\rho}(0)=[-\rho,\rho]

We will show the importance of the condition

by proving that the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property for Eq. (

B_{1/2}(0)=[-1/2,1/2]
does not hold. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that (
2.26
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
B_{1/2}(0)
with some constants
\varepsilon_{0},\kappa>0
. Choose

(2.27)

\delta\in\left(0,\min\bigl\{1,\varepsilon_{0}^{1/2},\kappa^{-1}\bigr\}\right)

so that

(2.28)

\varepsilon:=\delta^{2}<\varepsilon_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\kappa\delta^{2}<\delta.

Let

denote the unique solution of the initial value problem

(2.29)

Observe that

(2.30)

y(t)=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1-e^{-2\delta t}}{1+e^{-2\delta t}}\delta\qquad\text{for $t\geq 0$}.

Hence,

(2.31)

\lim_{t\to\infty}y(t)=-\frac{1}{2}+\delta,

while (
2.27
), (
2.28
), (
2.29
) and (
2.30

-\frac{1}{2}\leq y(t)\leq-\frac{1}{2}+\delta<\frac{1}{2}\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\infty)$,}

and

\sigma_{y}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\biggl|\,y^{\prime}(t)+\biggl(y(t)+\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{2}\,\biggr|=\delta^{2}=\varepsilon.

It follows by elementary calculations that if

3.
Conditional Lipschitz shadowing via the logarithmic norm

In this section, we give sufficient conditions for the conditional Lipschitz shadowing of Eq. (
1.1
). These conditions will be formulated in terms of the logarithmic norm
\mu
defined by (
1.9
). Let us recall some useful properties of
\mu
from

[
9
, p. 41]
. For every
\alpha\geq 0
and
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha A)

\displaystyle=\alpha\mu(A),

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\mu(A)|

\displaystyle\leq|A|,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mu(A+B)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A)+\mu(B),

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\mu(A)-\mu(B)|

\displaystyle\leq|A-B|.

The values of
|A|
and
\mu(A)
for the most commonly used norms

|x|_{\infty}=\max_{i}|x_{i}|,\qquad|x|_{1}=\sum_{i}|x_{i}|,\qquad|x|_{2}=\biggl(\sum_{i}|x_{i}|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2},

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given by

|A|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\sum_{k}|a_{ik}|,\qquad|A|_{1}=\max_{k}\sum_{i}|a_{ik}|,\qquad|A|_{2}=\sqrt{s(A^{T}A)},

and

\mu_{\infty}(A)=\max_{i}\biggl(a_{ii}+\sum_{k,\,k\neq i}|a_{ik}|\biggr),\quad\mu_{1}(A)=\max_{k}\biggl(a_{kk}+\sum_{i,\,i\neq k}|a_{ik}|\biggr),\quad\mu_{2}(A)=s\biggl(\frac{A^{T}+A}{2}\biggr),

where
s(A^{T}A)
and
s((A^{T}+A)/2)
are the largest (real) eigenvalue of
A^{T}A
and
(A^{T}+A)/2
, respectively.
We will also need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 1
.

For any continuous map
M\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we have that

(3.5)

\mu\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}M(s)\,ds\biggr)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\mu\left(M(s)\right)\,ds.

Proof.

The existence of the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is a consequence of the continuity of
\mu:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
(see (
3.4
)).
In order to prove (
3.5
), observe that the subadditivity and positive homogeneity of
\mu
(see (
3.1
) and (
3.3
)), applied to the integral sums
\sum_{i=1}^{k}M(s_{i})\Delta s_{i}
,
where
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{k}=1
is a partition of
[0,1]
and
\Delta s_{i}:=s_{i}-s_{i-1}
for
i=1,\dots,k
, imply that

\mu\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}M(s_{i})\Delta s_{i}\biggr)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu\left(M(s_{i})\right)\Delta s_{i}.

Letting
\Delta:=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\Delta s_{i}\rightarrow 0
and using the continuity of
\mu
again, we conclude that (
3.5
) holds.
∎

Now we can state and prove the main result of this section which provides a sufficient condition under which Eq. (
1.1
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in a given set
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\emptyset\neq H\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and suppose that
g\colon[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a continuously differentiable function. If there exist
\delta>0
and
m>0
such that

(3.6)

\mu(g_{x}(t,x))\leq-m\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H)$},

then Eq. (
1.1
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
.

Proof.

We will show that Eq. (
1.1
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
with
\varepsilon_{0}=m\delta
and
\kappa=m^{-1}
. Suppose that
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}=m\delta
and
y
is a pseudosolution of Eq. (
1.1
) on
[0,\tau)
for some
\tau\in(0,\infty]
such that
\sigma_{y}=\sup_{0\leq t<\tau}|y^{\prime}(t)-g(t,y(t))|\leq\varepsilon
and
y(t)\in H
for all
t\in[0,\tau)
. Let
x
be the noncontinuable solution of Eq. (
1.1
) with initial value
x(0)=y(0)
. It is known (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Chap. I (III), p. 16]
) that
x
is defined on
[0,\sigma)
for some
\sigma\in(0,\infty]

and
\sigma=\infty
whenever
x
is bounded. Let
\omega:=\min\{\sigma,\tau\}
. Define

(3.7)

z(t):=x(t)-y(t)\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\omega)$}.

We claim that

(3.8)

|z(t)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{m}\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\omega)$}.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that (
3.8
) does not hold. Since
z(0)=x(0)-y(0)=0
, there exists
t_{1}\in(0,\omega)
such that

(3.9)

|z(t)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{m}\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,t_{1})$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad|z(t_{1})|=\frac{\varepsilon}{m}.

From (
1.1
) and (
3.7
), we find for
t\in[0,\omega)
,

z^{\prime}(t)=g(t,y(t)+z(t))-y^{\prime}(t)=g(t,y(t)+z(t))-g(t,y(t))+k_{y}(t),

where

k_{y}(t):=g(t,y(t))-y^{\prime}(t)\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\omega)$}.

Hence

(3.10)

z^{\prime}(t)=A(t)z(t)+k_{y}(t)\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\omega)$},

where

(3.11)

A(t):=\int_{0}^{1}g_{x}(t,y(t)+sz(t))\,ds\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\omega)$}.

From (
3.5
) and (
3.11
), we obtain

(3.12)

\mu\left(A(t)\right)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\mu(g_{x}(t,y(t)+sz(t)))\,ds\qquad\text{for $t\in[0,\omega)$}.

Let
t\in[0,t_{1}]
be fixed. In view of (
3.9
), for every
s\in[0,1]
, we have

|\left(y(t)+sz(t)\right)-y(t)|=s|z(t)|\leq|z(t)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{m}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{m}=\delta.

Hence, for every
t\in[0,t_{1}]
and
s\in[0,1]
, we have that
y(t)\in H
and

y(t)+sz(t)\in B_{\delta}(y(t))\subset\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H)
.
This, together with (
3.6
) and (
3.12
), yields

(3.13)

\mu\left(A(t)\right)\leq-m\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,t_{1}]$}.

Let
T(t,s)
denote the transition matrix
of the homogeneous linear differential equation (
1.6
), where
A(t)
is given by (
3.11
). Then, for every
s\in[0,\omega)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the solution of Eq. (
1.6
) with initial value
\xi
at
t=s
is given by
x(t)=T(t,s)\xi
for
t\in[0,\omega)
.
By Coppel’s inequality
[
9
, Chap. III, Theorem 3, p. 58]
, we have for
0\leq s\leq t<\omega
,

|T(t,s)\xi|\leq\exp\biggl(\int_{s}^{t}\mu(A(u))\,du\biggr)|\xi|.

Since
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
was arbitrary, this implies that

(3.14)

|T(t,s)|=\sup_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|T(t,s)\xi|}{|\xi|}\leq\exp\biggl(\int_{s}^{t}\mu(A(u))\biggr)\,du\quad\text{whenever $0\leq s\leq t<\omega$}.

Since
z
is a solution of the nonhomogeneous equation (
3.10
) with initial value
z(0)=0
, by the variation of constants formula, we have

z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T(t,s)k_{y}(s)\,ds\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\omega)$}.

From this, (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), and taking into account that
\sup_{0\leq t<\omega}|k_{y}(t)|\leq\sigma_{y}\leq\varepsilon
, we obtain

\displaystyle|z(t_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}|T(t_{1},s)||k_{y}(s)|\,ds\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\exp\biggl(\int_{s}^{t_{1}}\mu(A(u))\biggr)\,du

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t_{1}}e^{-m(t_{1}-s)}\,ds=\frac{\varepsilon}{m}(1-e^{-mt_{1}})<\frac{\varepsilon}{m}.

This contradicts (
3.9
) and hence (
3.8
) holds.

Next we show that
\sigma\geq\tau
. Otherwise,
0<\sigma<\tau
and hence
\omega=\sigma
. This, together with (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), implies that for all
t\in[0,\sigma)
,

|x(t)|=|y(t)+z(t)|\leq|y(t)|+|z(t)|\leq\max_{0\leq t\leq\sigma}|y(t)|+\frac{\varepsilon}{m}.

Consequently,
x
is bounded on
[0,\sigma)
and hence
\sigma=\infty
contradicting the fact that
\sigma<\tau
. Thus,
\sigma\geq\tau
and hence
\omega=\tau
. This, together with (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), implies that condition (
1.4
) is satisfied with
\kappa=m^{-1}
. The proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Corollary 3

Suppose that

\mu(g_{x}(t,x))\leq-m\qquad\text{for all $t\geq 0$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

Now we present a simple corollary of Theorem
5
for the autonomous equation

(3.16)

x^{\prime}=h(x),

where
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuously differentiable.

Corollary 4
.

Let
H
be a nonempty, bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a continuously differentiable function such that

(3.17)

\sup_{x\in H}\mu(h^{\prime}(x))<0,

then Eq. (
3.16
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
H
.

Proof.

Eq. (
3.16
) is a special case of (
1.1
) with
g(t,x):=h(x)
for
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Choose
\varepsilon\in(0,-k)
, where
k:=\sup_{x\in H}\mu(h^{\prime}(x))<0
(see (
3.17
)). Since
H
is bounded, there exists
\rho>0
such that
H\subset B_{\rho}(0)
. The continuity of
\mu
and
h^{\prime}
implies that
\mu\circ h^{\prime}
is uniformly continuous on the compact set
B_{\rho+1}(0)
. Therefore, there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

(3.18)

|\mu(h^{\prime}(x))-\mu(h^{\prime}(\tilde{x}))|\leq\varepsilon\qquad\text{whenever $x,\tilde{x}\in B_{\rho+1}(0)$ and $|x-\tilde{x}|\leq\delta$}.

Let
x\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H)
. Then, there exists
\tilde{x}\in H
such that
|x-\tilde{x}|\leq\delta
.
Hence,
\tilde{x}\in H\subset B_{\rho}(0)
and
|x|\leq|\tilde{x}|+|x-\tilde{x}|\leq\rho+\delta<\rho+1
. Thus, we have that
x,\tilde{x}\in B_{\rho+1}(0)
and
|x-\tilde{x}|\leq\delta
. From (
3.18
) and the definition of
k
, we obtain that

\mu(g_{x}(t,x))=\mu(h^{\prime}(x))\leq\mu(h^{\prime}(\tilde{x}))+\varepsilon\leq k+\varepsilon.

Since
x\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(H)
was arbitrary, condition (
3.6
) is satisfied with
m=-(k+\varepsilon)>0
. The desired conclusion now follows readily from Theorem
5
.
∎

Example 3

Consider the system

which is a special case of the modified
Kermack–McKendrick equation

(S(0),I(0))
from the set

\Gamma:=\left\{(S,I)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:S\geq 0,\,I\geq 0,\,S+I\leq 1\right\}.

For
c\in[0,1)
, define

\Gamma_{c}:=\left\{(S,I)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:S\geq 0,\,I\geq 0,\,S+I\leq 1-c\right\}.

Observe that
\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{c}\subset\Gamma_{0}
for

.
Eq. (
3.19
) is a special case of Eq. (
3.16
) where
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is given by

h(S,I)=(1-IS-S,IS-I)^{T}\qquad\text{for $(S,I)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.

We will show that, for every
c\in(0,1)
, Eq. (
3.19
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
\Gamma_{c}
, but the same property in
\Gamma_{0}
does not hold.
Evidently,
h
is continuously differentiable and

h^{\prime}(S,I)=\begin{pmatrix}-I-1&-S\\
I&S-1\\
\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{for $(S,I)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.

Hence,

\mu_{\infty}(h^{\prime}(S,I))=\max\{\,-I-1+|S|,S-1+|I|\,\}\qquad\text{for $(S,I)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.

From this and the definition of
\Gamma_{c}
, we obtain

(3.20)

\mu_{\infty}(h^{\prime}(S,I))=S-1+I\leq-c\qquad\text{for all $(S,I)^{T}\in\Gamma_{c}$},

where
c\in[0,1)
is arbitrary.
This shows that if
c\in(0,1)
, then condition (
3.17
) of Corollary
4
is satisfied with
\mu=\mu_{\infty}
and
H=\Gamma_{c}
. By the application of
Corollary
4
, we conclude that, for every
c\in(0,1)
, Eq. (
3.19
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
\Gamma_{c}
.

Next we show that the same property in
\Gamma_{0}
does not hold. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that Eq. (
3.19
) has the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
\Gamma_{0}
. Note that the definition of the conditional Lipschitz shadowing is independent of the norm used since all norms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are equivalent. Therefore, we may (and do) use the infinity norm
|\cdot|_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\varepsilon_{0},\kappa>0
be the constants from the definition of the conditional Lipschitz shadowing property in
\Gamma_{0}
. It is easily verified that for every
\varepsilon>0
,

P_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon},\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

is an equilibrium of the system

\begin{split}S^{\prime}&=1-IS-S-\varepsilon,\\[0.0pt]
I^{\prime}&=IS-I+\varepsilon,\end{split}

which is a perturbation of Eq. (
3.19
).
Hence, for
every

\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{\varepsilon_{0},1\})
,
P_{\varepsilon}=(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon},\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{T}
is a constant pseudosolution of (
3.19
) on
[0,\infty)
with maximum error
\sigma_{P_{\varepsilon}}=\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and such that
P_{\varepsilon}\in\Gamma_{0}
. By the definition of the conditional Lipschitz shadowing, this implies that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{\varepsilon_{0},1\})
, Eq. (
3.19
) has a solution
(S_{\varepsilon}(t),I_{\varepsilon}(t))^{T}
on
[0,\infty)
such that

(3.21)

(S_{\varepsilon}(t),I_{\varepsilon}(t))^{T}\in B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon})=B_{\kappa\varepsilon}((1-\sqrt{\varepsilon},\sqrt{\varepsilon})^{T})\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,\infty)$}.

In particular, we have that

(3.22)

\emptyset\neq\omega(S_{\varepsilon},I_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon})\qquad\text{whenever $\varepsilon>0$ is sufficiently small},

where
\omega(S_{\varepsilon},I_{\varepsilon})
denotes the omega-limit set of the solution
(S_{\varepsilon}(t),I_{\varepsilon}(t))^{T}
. If
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small, then
\kappa\varepsilon<\sqrt{\varepsilon}
. Hence,

(3.23)

B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon})\subset G:=(0,1)\times(0,1)\qquad\text{whenever $\varepsilon>0$ is sufficiently small.}

Choose
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that both (
3.21
) and (
3.23
) are satisfied. Define
V\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

V(S,I)=I\qquad\text{for $(S,I)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}$}.

Then
V^{\prime}(S,I)=(0,1)

and for the derivative of
V
along system (
3.19
), we have

\dot{V}_{\eqref{SIspec}}(S,I)=V^{\prime}(S,I)h(S,I)=-I(1-S)\leq 0\qquad\text{for $(S,I)^{T}\in\overline{G}=[0,1]\times[0,1]$}.

Thus,
V
is a Lyapunov function for Eq. (
3.19
) on
\overline{G}

(see
[
10
, Chap. 2, Definition 6.1, p. 30]
) and

(3.24)

E:=\{\,(S,I)^{T}\in\overline{G}\mid\dot{V}_{\eqref{SIspec}}(S,I)=0\,\}=\{\,(S,I)^{T}\in\overline{G}\mid\text{$I=0$ or $S=1$}\,\}.

By the application of LaSalle’s invariance principle (see, e.g.,
[
10
, Chap. 2, Theorem 6.1, p. 30]
), we conclude that

\omega(S_{\varepsilon},I_{\varepsilon})\subset E
. This, combined with (
3.22
), yields

\emptyset\neq\omega(S_{\varepsilon},I_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon})\cap E.

Thus,
B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon})\cap E\neq\emptyset
. On the other hand, (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) imply that

B_{\kappa\varepsilon}(P_{\varepsilon})\cap E=\emptyset
. This contradiction proves that Eq. (
3.19
) does not have the conditional shadowing property in
\Gamma_{0}
. Note that if we take
H=\Gamma_{0}
and
\mu=\mu_{\infty}
, then (
3.20
) with
c=0
implies that

\sup_{(S,I)^{T}\in\Gamma_{0}}\mu_{\infty}(h^{\prime}(S,I))=0,

which shows the importance and the sharpness of condition (
3.17
) in Corollary
4
.

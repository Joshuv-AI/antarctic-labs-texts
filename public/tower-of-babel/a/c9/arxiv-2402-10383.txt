In this paper we will consider quaternionic operators T T , for which at least one ray { t ​ e i ​ ω | t > 0 } \{te^{i\omega}\;|\;t>0\} , ω ∈ [ 0 , π ] \omega\in[0,\pi] , i ∈ 𝕊 i\in\mathbb{S} is contained in the S S -resolvent set, and the inverse operator Q s − 1 ​ ( T ) Q_{s}^{-1}(T) admits certain decay properties on this ray. Introduction

The foundation of quaternionic quantum mechanics, see
[
5
]
, was the initial motivation to develop the spectral theory on the
S
-spectrum, see
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. The central challenge in advancing the spectral theory for both quaternionic and Clifford algebras settings has revolved around defining a suitable concept of spectrum. This definition needs to be applicable for the spectral theorem as well as the hyperholomorphic functional calculus within this framework.

Given the existence of multiple possible definitions of hyperholomorphicity in the hypercomplex setting, the difficulty has been in establishing the most pertinent notion that serves the purpose. The identification of slice hyperholomorphic functions played a pivotal role in determining the
S
-spectrum for both quaternionic and Clifford operators. Importantly, this identification was achieved solely through methods in hypercomplex analysis, rather than relying on physical motivations or operator theory considerations despite the foundational motivation stemming from quaternionic quantum mechanics, see the introduction in
[
10
]
for all the details on the discovery of the
S
-spectrum.

A key distinction between the classical spectral theory in complex Banach spaces
X_{\mathbb{C}}
and the
S
-spectral theory on quaternionic Banach spaces
X
, lies in the structure of the corresponding resolvent operator. While in the complex case the spectrum is related to the invertibility of the operator
\lambda-A:D(A)\subseteq X_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow X_{\mathbb{C}}
, one has to consider the second order polynomial

T^{2}-2s_{0}T+|s|^{2}:D(T^{2})\subseteq X\rightarrow X
, in the quaternionic case, in order to preserve holomorphicity of the resulting resolvent operator. Note, that is essential to consider the domain of
T^{2}
, and the majority of considerations related to the spectral theory on the
S
-spectrum rely on the domain of
T^{2}
and not directly on
T
itself.

In the spectral theory on the
S
-spectrum, there is an additional interest in exploring fine structures on the
S
-spectrum introduced in
[

,
7
,

,

,
14
]
. These investigations involve the functional calculus for sectorial operators associated with axially harmonic functions and axially polyanalytic functions. For an alternative approach, based on monogenic function theory
[

-functional calculus is addressed in

Interpolation spaces have been extensively studied in the literature and classical references include the monographs

The aim of this paper
is to generalize two important results of the well established
K

In order to introduce our first result, we mention that in the complex case the interpolation space between
X_{\mathbb{C}}
and
D(A)
can be characterized by

(1.1)

(X_{\mathbb{C}},D(A))_{\theta,p}=\Set{x\in X_{\mathbb{C}}}{\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{\theta}\varphi_{x}(\lambda)\in L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)},

using the function
\varphi_{x}(\lambda):=\|A(\lambda-A)^{-1}x\|
and the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty):=L^{p}((0,\infty),dt/t)
, see
[
17
, Proposition 3.1.1]
and
[
17
, Proposition 3.1.6]
for a similar result for

. Our result in Theorem

generalizes (

) to the quaternionic
S
-spectral setting, i.e. replaces the resolvent
(\lambda-A)^{-1}
by the
S
-pseudo resolvent
Q_{s}^{-1}(T)=(T^{2}-2s_{0}T+|s|^{2})^{-1}
, and we also consider domains

of arbitrary powers. In particular, we establish the characterization

(X,D(T^{n}))_{\theta,p}=\Set{x\in X}{t\mapsto t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\in L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)},

where it turns out that, due to the second order pseudo
S
- resolvent operator
Q_{s}^{-1}(T)
, the function
\varphi_{x}
has to be replaced by
\psi_{x}
which has to be defined for even and for odd values of
n
differently, namely

\psi_{x}(t):=\begin{cases}\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)x\|,&n\text{ even},\\
\|T^{n+1}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|+t\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|,&n\text{ odd.}\end{cases}

The second goal of this paper deals with intermediate spaces, these are well known in the complex case, but the extension to the quaternionic setting requires more sophisticated proofs. In Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
we prove that for every
n<k<m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the domain

is an intermediate space between
D(T^{n})
and
D(T^{m})
. More precisely, using the Definition

D(T^{k})\in J_{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\big(D(T^{n}),D(T^{m})\big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad D(T^{k})\in K_{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\big(D(T^{n}),D(T^{m})\big).

Explicitly this means that there exist constants
C_{J},C_{K}\geq 0
, such that

\displaystyle\|x\|_{D(T^{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{J}\|x\|_{D(T^{n})}^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{m})}^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}},

\displaystyle x\in D(T^{m}),

\displaystyle K(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}t^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{k})},

\displaystyle x\in D(T^{k}),\,t>0,

where
K(t,x)
is the

D(T^{n})
,
D(T^{m})
.

2.
Interpolation theory of quaternionic Banach spaces

In this section we will give standard results of interpolation theory, which are well known in the complex case, but generalizes also to Banach spaces over the quaternions. We start with a short introduction to quaternionic numbers and quaternionic (right) Banach spaces.

The
quaternionic numbers
are defined as

\mathbb{H}:=\Set{s_{0}+s_{1}e_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}+s_{3}e_{3}}{s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}},

with the three imaginary units
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
satisfying the relations

e_{1}^{2}=e_{2}^{2}=e_{3}^{2}=-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}e_{1}e_{2}=-e_{2}e_{1}=e_{3},\\
e_{2}e_{3}=-e_{3}e_{2}=e_{1},\\
e_{3}e_{1}=-e_{1}e_{3}=e_{2}.\end{array}

For every quaternion
s\in\mathbb{H}
, we set

\displaystyle\text{Re}(s)

\displaystyle:=s_{0},

\displaystyle(\textit{real part})

\displaystyle\text{Im}(s)

\displaystyle:=s_{1}e_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}+s_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle(\textit{imaginary part})

\displaystyle\overline{s}

\displaystyle:=s_{0}-s_{1}e_{1}-s_{2}e_{2}-s_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle(\textit{conjugate})

\displaystyle|s|

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{s_{0}^{2}+s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{3}^{2}}.

\displaystyle(\textit{modulus})

The unit sphere of purely imaginary quaternions is defined as

\mathbb{S}:=\Set{s\in\mathbb{H}}{s_{0}=0\text{ and }|s|=1},

and for every
i\in\mathbb{S}
we consider the complex plane

\mathbb{C}_{i}:=\Set{x+iy}{x,y\in\mathbb{R}},

which is an isomorphic copy of the complex numbers, since every
i\in\mathbb{S}
satisfies
i^{2}=-1
. Moreover, for every
s\in\mathbb{H}
we consider the corresponding
2
-sphere

[s]:=\Set{\text{Re}(s)+i|\text{Im}(s)|}{i\in\mathbb{S}}.

Definition 2.1

(Right quaternionic Banach space)
.

A
quaternionic right vector space
is an additive group
(X,+)
equipped with a scalar multiplication from the right
\cdot:X\times\mathbb{H}\rightarrow X
, which satisfies

(x+y)s=xs+ys,\qquad x(s+q)=xs+sq,\qquad x(sq)=(xs)q,\qquad x1=x,

for every
x,y\in X
,
s,q\in\mathbb{H}
. A
right norm
on
X
is a mapping
\|\cdot\|:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, with the properties

i)

\|xs\|=\|x\|\,|s|
,
x\in X,\,s\in\mathbb{H}
,

ii)

\|x+y\|\leq\|x\|+\|y\|
,
x,y\in X
,

iii)

\|x\|=0\Rightarrow x=0
,
x\in X
.

Finally,
X
is called
right Banach space
, if it is complete with respect to this norm.

On right Banach spaces we can now define right linear operators.

Definition 2.2

(Right linear operator)
.

Let
X
be a right Banach space. An operator
T:X\rightarrow X
is called right linear, if its domain
D(T)\subseteq X
is a right linear subspace of
X
and for every
x,y\in D(T)
,
s\in\mathbb{H}
there holds

T(x+y)=T(x)+T(y)\qquad\text{and}\qquad T(xs)=T(x)s.

For any right linear operator
T
and any real number
\lambda
, we define the operators

(\lambda T)(x):=(T\lambda)(x):=T(x)\lambda\qquad\text{with }D(\lambda T):=D(T\lambda):=D(T).

The operator
T
is called closed, if
D(T)
is complete with respect to the norm

\|x\|_{D(T)}:=\|x\|+\|Tx\|,\qquad x\in D(T).

Definition 2.3

(Interpolation couple)
.

Two right Banach spaces
X
and
Y
are called
interpolation couple
if there exists some topological Hausdorff space
Z
, such that both
X
and
Y
are continuously embedded in
Z
.

For any interpolation couple
X,Y
, the intersection
X\cap Y
as well as the sum
X+Y
are right Banach spaces with norms

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X\cap Y}:=

\displaystyle\max\{\|x\|_{X},\|x\|_{Y}\},

\displaystyle\|x\|_{X+Y}:=

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{x=a+b,a\in X,b\in Y}\big(\|a\|_{X}+\|b\|_{Y}\big).

The proof is the same as in the complex case
[
4
, Lemma 2.3.1]
. In between
X\cap Y
and
X+Y
we will now construct several interpolation spaces. A key role will play the following
K
-functional, which is a family of equivalent norms on
X+Y
.

Definition 2.4

(
K
-functional)
.

For an interpolation couple
X,Y
we define the
K
-functional

(2.1)

K(t,x):=\inf_{x=a+b,a\in X,b\in Y}(\|a\|_{X}+t\|b\|_{Y}),\qquad t>0,\,x\in X+Y.

Clearly, the
K
-functional depends on the spaces
X
and
Y
, but for simplicity we will omit this dependence. In the following we will need for any
p\in[1,\infty]
the space

(2.2)

L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty):=L^{p}\big((0,\infty),dt/t\big),

which is the Lebesgue-space on
(0,\infty)
with the weighted measure
dt/t
. One can easily check by substituting
t\rightarrow t^{\alpha}
, that with the convention
|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{\infty}}:=1
, this space has the property

(2.3)

\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}=|\alpha|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f(t^{\alpha})\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

With the space (
2.2
) and the
K
-functional (
2.1
), we now define interpolation spaces.

Definition 2.5

(Interpolation spaces)
.

Let
X,Y
be an interpolation couple. Then for every
0<\theta<1
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we define the
interpolation space

(2.4)

(X,Y)_{\theta,p}:=\Set{x\in X+Y}{t\mapsto t^{-\theta}K(t,x)\in L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}.

Equipped with the right norm

\|x\|_{\theta,p}:=\|t^{-\theta}K(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)},

this space becomes a right Banach space.

That
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,p}
is a right norm, follows immediately from the fact that
x\mapsto K(t,x)
as well as
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}
are right norms, see
[
4
, Lemma 3.1.1]
. For the completeness of
(X,Y)_{\theta,p}
one can imitate the proof of
[
17
, Proposition 1.1.5]
. These interpolation spaces admit now certain immediate properties. Some of them will be
continuous embeddings
, where we use the notation that
V\hookrightarrow W
, whenever
V\subseteq W
and there exists some constant
C\geq 0
such that
\|v\|_{W}\leq C\|v\|_{V}
, for every
v\in V
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X,Y
be an interpolation couple,
\theta,\eta\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. Then there holds:

i)

(X,Y)_{\theta,p}=(Y,X)_{1-\theta,p}
, with equal norms.

ii)

(X,X)_{\theta,p}=X
, with equivalent norms.

iii)

(X,Y)_{\theta,p}=\{0\}
,               if
X\cap Y=\{0\}
.

iv)

X\cap Y\hookrightarrow(X,Y)_{\theta,p}\hookrightarrow(X,Y)_{\theta,q}\hookrightarrow X+Y
,  if
p\leq q
.

v)

(X,Y)_{\theta,p}\hookrightarrow(X,Y)_{\eta,q}
,             if
Y\hookrightarrow X
and
\theta>\eta
.

vi)

X\cap Y
is dense in
(X,Y)_{\theta,p}
,          if
p<\infty
.

Proof.

The proofs are the formally the same as in the complex case. The statements i) – iv) can be found in
[
17
, Proposition 1.1.3]
, the property v) is proven in
[
17
, Proposition 1.1.4]
and the result vi) can be taken from
[
17
, Proposition 1.2.5]
.
∎

Next, we consider the boundedness of operators restricted to interpolation spaces.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X,Y
and
V,W
be two interpolations couples,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Consider some right linear operator
T:X+Y\rightarrow V+W
, with bounded restrictions
T|_{X}\in\mathcal{B}(X,V)
and
T|_{Y}\in\mathcal{B}(Y,W)
. Then also the restriction
T|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,p}}\in\mathcal{B}\big((X,Y)_{\theta,p},(V,W)_{\theta,p}\big)
is bounded, with operator norm

\|T|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,p}}\|\leq\|T|_{X}\|^{1-\theta}\|T|_{Y}\|^{\theta}.

Proof.

See
[
17
, Theorem 1.1.6]
.
∎

Beside the interpolation spaces (
2.4
), which are embedded in between
X\cap Y
and
X+Y
by Lemma
2.6
iv), we will now discuss classes of intermediate spaces in between
X\cap Y
and
X+Y
.

Definition 2.8

(Intermediate spaces)
.

Let
X,Y
be an interpolation couple and
\theta\in(0,1)
. For right Banach spaces
E
with
X\cap Y\hookrightarrow E\hookrightarrow X+Y
, we now consider two classes of
intermediate spaces
:

We say
E\in J_{\theta}(X,Y)
, if there exists
C_{J}\geq 0
, such that

\|x\|_{E}\leq C_{J}\|x\|_{X}^{1-\theta}\|x\|_{Y}^{\theta},\qquad x\in X\cap Y.

We say
E\in K_{\theta}(X,Y)
, if there exists some
C_{K}\geq 0
, such that

K(t,x)\leq C_{K}t^{\theta}\|x\|_{E},\qquad t>0,\,x\in E.

There exists the following characterization of the set of intermediate spaces
J_{\theta}(X,Y)
and
K_{\theta}(X,Y)
, which connects them to the interpolation spaces (
2.4
).

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X,Y
be an interpolation couple,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
E
a right Banach space with
X\cap Y\hookrightarrow E\hookrightarrow X+Y
. Then there holds

i)

E\in J_{\theta}(X,Y)\Leftrightarrow(X,Y)_{\theta,1}\hookrightarrow E
.

ii)

E\in K_{\theta}(X,Y)\Leftrightarrow E\hookrightarrow(X,Y)_{\theta,\infty}
.

Proof.

See
[
4
, Theorem 3.2.5]
.
∎

In particular, it follows from Lemma
2.6
iv), that for every
\theta\in(0,1)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
there holds

(X,Y)_{\theta,p}\in J_{\theta}(X,Y)\cap K_{\theta}(X,Y).

The final example is taken from
[
17
, Example 1.3.3]
and shows that there indeed exist intermediate spaces which are not interpolation spaces.

Example 2.10
.

Let
C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the space of
k
-times continuously differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounded derivatives. Then

C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\in J_{\frac{1}{2}}\big(C(\mathbb{R}^{n}),C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)\cap K_{\frac{1}{2}}\big(C(\mathbb{R}^{n}),C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big),

but
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is not of the form
\big(C(\mathbb{R}^{n}),C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)_{\frac{1}{2},p}
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Theorem 2.11

(Reiteration theorem)
.

Let
X,Y
be an interpolation ,
\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta\in(0,1)

E_{0}\in J_{\theta_{0}}(X,Y)\cap K_{\theta_{0}}(X,Y)
and

(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta,p}=(X,Y)_{(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta\theta_{1},p},\quad\text{with equivalent norms}.

3.
Interpolating between domains of powers of operators

In this section we consider the particular class of unbounded right linear operators
T
in right Banach spaces
X
, given in Definition
3.2
. First, Theorem
3.5
characterizes the interpolation spaces
(X,D(T^{n}))_{\theta,p}
in terms of estimates of the resolvent operator (
3.3
). Secondly, in Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
we prove that for
n<k<m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the domain
D(T^{k})
is an intermediate space between
D(T^{n})
and
D(T^{m})
in the sense of Definition
2.8
.

While all the results in Section
2
are mainly copies of their analogs in complex interpolation theory, a significant difference arises in the definition of quaternionic spectra and resolvent operators. In the complex case the spectral problem is related to the bounded invertibility of the operator
\lambda-A
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. For bounded operators
A\in\mathcal{B}(X_{\mathbb{C}})
, this inverse is for example given by the series

(3.1)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A^{n}\lambda^{-n-1}=(\lambda-A)^{-1},\qquad|\lambda|>\|A\|.

However, for quaternionic operators
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
and quaternionic numbers
s\in\mathbb{H}
, the explicit form of this series is given by

(3.2)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}s^{-n-1}=(T^{2}-2s_{0}T+|s|^{2})^{-1}(\overline{s}-T),\qquad|s|>\|T\|,

where the discrepancy between (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) comes from the noncommutativity of quaternionic numbers and operators. Motivated by the explicit value of the series (
3.2
), we now define the following notion of spectrum, for more details see
[
10
]
.

Definition 3.1

(
S
-spectrum)
.

Let
T:D(T)\subseteq X\rightarrow X
be a closed right linear operator. For every
s\in\mathbb{H}
we consider the operator

(3.3)

Q_{s}(T):=T^{2}-2s_{0}T+|s|^{2},\qquad\text{with }D(Q_{s}(T)):=D(T^{2}),

and define the
S
-resolvent set
and the
S
-spectrum
as

\rho_{S}(T):=\Set{s\in\mathbb{H}}{Q_{s}^{-1}(T)\in\mathcal{B}(X)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{S}(T):=\mathbb{H}\setminus\rho_{S}(T).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a right Banach space and
T:D(T)\subseteq X\rightarrow X
a right linear, closed operator. We assume that there exists some
\omega\in[0,\pi]
, such that

(3.4)

S_{\omega}:=\Set{te^{i\omega}}{t>0,\,i\in\mathbb{S}}\subseteq\rho_{S}(T),

and there exists some
M\geq 0
such that the inverse of (
3.3
) is bounded by

(3.5)

\|Q_{s}^{-1}(T)\|\leq\frac{M}{|s|^{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|TQ_{s}^{-1}(T)\|\leq\frac{M}{|s|},\qquad s\in S_{\omega}.

The estimates (
3.5
) can now be generalized also to other powers of
T
and
Q_{s}^{-1}(T)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be as in Definition
3.2
. Then with the constant
M\geq 0
from (
3.5
) there holds for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
n\leq 2m
the estimate

(3.6)

\|T^{n}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)\|\leq\frac{(1+3M)^{m}}{|s|^{2m-n}},\qquad s\in S_{\omega}.

Proof.

Let
s\in S_{\omega}
. We will repeatedly apply (
3.5
), but distinguish the cases:

\circ
In the case
0\leq n\leq m
, we get

\|T^{n}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)\|=\|(TQ_{s}^{-1}(T))^{n}Q_{s}^{-(m-n)}(T)\|\leq\Big(\frac{M}{|s|}\Big)^{n}\Big(\frac{M}{|s|^{2}}\Big)^{m-n}=\frac{M^{m}}{|s|^{2m-n}}.

\circ
In the case
m<n\leq 2m
on the other hand, we get

\displaystyle\|T^{n}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\big(T^{2}Q_{s}^{-1}(T)\big)^{n-m}\big(TQ_{s}^{-1}(T)\big)^{2m-n}\big\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\big(1+2s_{0}TQ_{s}^{-1}(T)-|s|^{2}Q_{s}^{-1}(T)\big)^{n-m}\big(TQ_{s}^{-1}(T)\big)^{2m-n}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+2|s_{0}|\frac{M}{|s|}+|s|^{2}\frac{M}{|s|^{2}}\Big)^{n-m}\Big(\frac{M}{|s|}\Big)^{2m-n}\leq\frac{(1+3M)^{m}}{|s|^{2m-n}}.\qed

Next we will prove that the powers
T^{n}
are closed operators, i.e.
D(T^{n})
are Banach spaces, and that these domains are continuously embedded in one another. In particular this shows that any pair
D(T^{n})
,
D(T^{m})
is an interpolation couple, and interpolating between these spaces makes sense.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T
be as in Definition
3.2
. Then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the power
T^{n}
is a closed operator, and for every
n\leq m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there holds

(3.7)

D(T^{m})\hookrightarrow D(T^{n}).

Proof.

Since
\rho_{S}(T)\setminus\{0\}\neq\emptyset
, by the assumption (
3.4
), let us choose some arbitrary
0\neq s\in\rho_{S}(T)
. For this
s
we can write

(3.8)

T^{n}=T^{n}Q_{s}^{n}(T)Q_{s}^{-n}(T)=Q_{s}^{n}(T)(TQ_{s}^{-1}(T))^{n}.

Now we observe that
TQ_{s}^{-1}(T)\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is bounded, as the everywhere defined product of a closed and a bounded operator. Moreover,
Q_{s}^{n}(T)
is closed, as it is the inverse of
Q_{s}^{-n}(T)\in\mathcal{B}(X)
. Consequently, the power
T^{n}
in (
3.8
) is the product of a closed and a bounded operator, and hence is closed.

For the proof of the embedding (
3.7
), we use a similar formula as in (
3.8
) to decompose every
x\in D(T^{m})
into

x=(T^{2}+2s_{0}T+|s|^{2})^{m}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)x=\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}}}^{m}\frac{m!}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}T^{2\alpha}(2s_{0}T)^{\beta}|s|^{2\gamma}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)x.

In this representation, we can use (
3.6
), to estimate

\displaystyle\|T^{n}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m,2\alpha+\beta<m-n}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}|s|^{\beta+2\gamma}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\|T^{n+2\alpha+\beta}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)x\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m,2\alpha+\beta\geq m-n}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}|s|^{\beta+2\gamma}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\|T^{n+2\alpha+\beta-m}Q_{s}^{-m}(T)T^{m}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}|s|^{n}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}(1+3M)^{m}\|x\|+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{(1+3M)^{m}}{|s|^{m-n}}\|T^{m}x\|

\displaystyle=(4+12M)^{m}|s|^{n}\|x\|+\frac{(4+12M)^{m}}{|s|^{m-n}}\|T^{m}x\|.

This estimate then shows the embedding

\|x\|_{D(T^{n})}\leq\max\Big\{1+(4+12M)^{m}|s|^{n},\frac{(4+12M)^{m}}{|s|^{m-n}}\Big\}\|x\|_{D(T^{m})}.\qed

The following theorem characterizes the interpolation spaces between
X
and the domains
D(T^{n})
, in terms of the pseudo
S
-resolvent operator, that is the inverse of the operator
Q_{s}(T)
defined in (
3.3
). This result is the quaternionic analog, but also a generalization, of the complex results in
[
17
, Proposition 3.1.1 & Proposition 3.1.6]
. The major difference between the complex and the quaternionic case is that the function
\psi_{x}
in (
3.9
) has an additional term for odd values
n
. This is mainly due to the second order resolvent
Q_{s}^{-1}(T)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T
be as in Definition
3.2
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
x\in X
let

(3.9)

\psi_{x}(t):=\begin{cases}\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)x\|,&\text{n }\text{ even,}\\
\|T^{n+1}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|+t\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|,&\text{n }\text{odd,}\end{cases}\qquad t>0.

Then, for every
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
we obtain the representation

(X,D(T^{n}))_{\theta,p}=\Set{x\in X}{t\mapsto t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\in L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}

of the interpolation space, and the norms

\|x\|_{\theta,p}\quad\text{and}\quad\|x\|_{\theta,p}^{*}:=\|x\|+\|t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}\quad\text{are equivalent.}

Note that the operator
Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-1}(T)=(T^{2}+2tT\cos\omega+t^{2})^{-1}
does not depend on the imaginary unit
i\in\mathbb{S}
.

Proof.

For the embedding
\text{“}\hookrightarrow\text{“}
let
x\in(X,D(T^{n}))_{\theta,p}
. For any
t>0
and any decomposition
x=a+b
with
a\in X
,
b\in D(T^{n})
, we want to estimate
\psi_{x}(t)
.

\circ
If
n
is even, we use (
3.6
), to estimate

(3.10)

\psi_{x}(t)\leq\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)a\|+\|Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)T^{n}b\|\leq(1+3M)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Big(\|a\|+\frac{\|T^{n}b\|}{t^{n}}\Big).

\circ
If
n
is odd, we similarly get

\displaystyle\psi_{x}(T)

\displaystyle=\|T^{n+1}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|+t\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|

\displaystyle\leq(1+3M)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\Big(\|a\|+\frac{\|T^{n}b\|}{t^{n}}+t\frac{\|a\|}{t}+t\frac{\|T^{n}b\|}{t^{n+1}}\Big)

(3.11)

\displaystyle=2(1+3M)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\Big(\|a\|+\frac{\|T^{n}b\|}{t^{n}}\Big).

Since the estimates (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) hold true for every decomposition
x=a+b
, we can take the respective infimum of the right hand side. Hence, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we end up with an estimate of the
K
-functional (
2.1
), namely

\psi_{x}(t)\leq 2(1+M)^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big),\qquad t>0,

where we used the ceil function
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, which gives the smallest integer larger or equal to
\frac{n}{2}
. If we multiply this inequality by
t^{n\theta}
and take the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}
-norm, we get

\displaystyle\|t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+M)^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}\Big\|t^{n\theta}K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(1+M)^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|t^{-\theta}K(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}=\frac{2(1+M)^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\|x\|_{\theta,p},

where in the second line we used (
2.3
) with the convention
n^{\frac{1}{\infty}}:=1
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.6
iv) and Lemma
3.4
we have

(X,D(T^{n}))_{\theta,p}\hookrightarrow X+D(T^{n})\hookrightarrow X+X=X.

Hence
\|x\|\leq C\|x\|_{\theta,p}
for some
C\geq 0
, and together with (
3.12
) we get

\|x\|_{\theta,p}^{*}=\|x\|+\|t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}\leq\Big(C+\frac{2(1+M)^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\Big)\|x\|_{\theta,p}.

For the embedding
\text{“}\hookleftarrow\text{“}
let
x\in X
with
\psi_{x}\in L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)
. For
0<t\leq 1
, we choose
a=x
and
b=0
in the definition (
2.1
) of the
K
-functional, and get

(3.13)

K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)\leq\|a\|+\frac{1}{t^{n}}\|b\|_{D(T^{n})}=\|x\|,\qquad 0<t\leq 1.

For
t\geq 1
on the other hand, we distinguish two cases.

\circ
If
n
is even, we decompose the vector
x
into

\displaystyle x=(T^{2}+2tT\cos\omega+t^{2})^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-n}(T)x=\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}}}^{n}\frac{n!t^{2\gamma}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}T^{2\alpha}(2tT\cos\omega)^{\beta}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-n}(T)x

(3.14)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq n+1}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!(2\cos\omega)^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!t^{-\beta-2\gamma}}T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-n}(T)x}_{=:a\in X}+\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq n}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!(2\cos\omega)^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!t^{-\beta-2\gamma}}T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-n}(T)x.}_{=:b\in D(T^{n})}

With this decomposition and the inequality (
3.6
), we can estimate the
K
-functional

\displaystyle K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq n+1}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{\|T^{2\alpha+\beta-n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)x\|}{t^{-\beta-2\gamma}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq n}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{\|T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)x\|+\|T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-n}(T)x\|}{t^{n-\beta-\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq n+1}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}(1+3M)^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)x\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq n}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\Big((1+3M)^{\frac{n}{2}}\|T^{n}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-\frac{n}{2}}(T)x\|+(1+3M)^{n}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}(1+3M)^{\frac{n}{2}}\psi_{x}(t)+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}(1+3M)^{n}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}}

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{n}(1+3M)^{\frac{n}{2}}\psi_{x}(t)+4^{n}(1+3M)^{n}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}},

where in the second last line we inserted the definition (
3.9
) of
\psi_{x}(t)
and in the last inequality we solved the trinomial sum
\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}=4^{n}
.

\circ
If
n
is odd we again use the same decomposition
x=a+b
as in (
3.14
), and similarly estimate the
K
-functional by

\displaystyle K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq n+1}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{\|T^{2\alpha+\beta-n-1}Q^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}(T)T^{n+1}Q^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|}{t^{-\beta-2\gamma}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq n}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{\|T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}(T)T^{n}Q^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|+\|T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q^{-n}(T)x\|}{t^{n-\beta-2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq n+1}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}(1+3M)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\|T^{n+1}Q^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|

\displaystyle\quad+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq n}}}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\Big((1+3M)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}t\|T^{n}Q^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}(T)x\|+(1+3M)^{n}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}(1+3M)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\psi_{x}(t)+\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\alpha+\beta+\gamma=n}}}}^{n}\frac{n!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}(1+3M)^{n}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{n}(1+3M)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\psi_{x}(t)+(1+3M)^{n}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}}.

The estimates (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) now show that the
K
-functional admits for some
M_{1},M_{2}\geq 0
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
the upper bound

(3.17)

K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)\leq M_{1}\psi_{x}(t)+M_{2}\frac{\|x\|}{t^{n}},\qquad t\geq 1.

The inequalities (
3.13
) and (
3.17
), together with the identity (
2.3
), now give

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\theta,p}

\displaystyle=\|t^{-\theta}K(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}=n^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|t^{n\theta}K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq n^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|t^{n\theta}K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,1)}+n^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big\|t^{n\theta}K\Big(\frac{1}{t^{n}},x\Big)\Big\|_{L^{p}_{*}(1,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq n^{\frac{1}{p}}\|x\|\|t^{n\theta}\|_{L^{p}_{*}(0,1)}+M_{1}n^{\frac{1}{p}}\|t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(1,\infty)}+M_{2}n^{\frac{1}{p}}\|x\|\|t^{n(\theta-1)}\|_{L^{p}_{*}(1,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|x\|}{p^{\frac{1}{p}}}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{M_{2}}{(1-\theta)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\Big)+M_{1}n^{\frac{1}{p}}\|t^{n\theta}\psi_{x}(t)\|_{L^{p}_{*}(1,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq\max\bigg\{\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{p}}}\Big(\frac{1}{\theta^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{M_{2}}{(1-\theta)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\Big),M_{1}n^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg\}\|x\|_{\theta,p}^{*}.\qed

The upcoming two Theorems

&

show that the domain
D(T^{k})
is an intermediate space between
D(T^{n})
and
D(T^{m})
according to Definition

.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
T
be as in Definition
3.2
. Then for every
n<k<m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there holds

D(T^{k})\in J_{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}(D(T^{n}),D(T^{m}));

i.e. according to Definition
2.8
there exists some
C_{n,k,m}\geq 0
, such that

\|x\|_{D(T^{k})}\leq C_{n,k,m}\|x\|_{D(T^{n})}^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{m})}^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}},\qquad x\in D(T^{m}).

Proof.

Note that it is enough to prove that for some
C_{n,k,m}\geq 0
, there holds

(3.18)

\|T^{k}x\|\leq C_{n,k,m}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|T^{m}x\|^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}},\qquad x\in D(T^{m}).

Indeed, if (
3.18
) holds true, we get

\displaystyle\|x\|_{D(T^{k})}

\displaystyle=\|x\|+\|T^{k}x\|\leq\|x\|+C_{n,k,m}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|T^{m}x\|^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,C_{n,k,m}\}\big(\|x\|+\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|T^{m}x\|^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{1,C_{n,k,m}\}\big(\|x\|+\|T^{n}x\|\big)^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\big(\|x\|+\|T^{m}x\|\big)^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}

\displaystyle=\max\{1,C_{n,k,m}\}\|x\|_{D(T^{n})}^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{m})}^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}},

where in the third line we used the basic inequality

c+a^{1-\theta}b^{\theta}\leq(c+a)^{1-\theta}(c+b)^{\theta},\qquad a,b,c\geq 0,\,\ \ \theta\in(0,1).

Note, that this inequality is trivial for
c=0
, but also for
c>0
it is true since the
c
-derivative of the left side is always smaller than the one of the right side,

\frac{d}{dc}(c+a^{1-\theta}b^{\theta})=1\leq(1-\theta)\Big(\frac{c+b}{c+a}\Big)^{\theta}+\theta\Big(\frac{c+a}{c+b}\Big)^{1-\theta}=\frac{d}{dc}(c+a)^{1-\theta}(c+b)^{\theta},

where the inequality comes from the convexity of the function
[0,1]\ni x\mapsto(\frac{c+b}{c+a})^{x}
.

Hence it is left to prove is the inequality (
3.18
), which will be done for fixed
n<k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and by induction with respect to
m\in\{k+1,\dots\}
. For the induction start
m=k+1
, let
x\in D(T^{k+1})
and consider the function

f_{x}(t):=t^{2(k-n)}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n)}(T)x,\qquad t>0.

Thanks to Lemma
3.3
we compute the limit

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|t^{2}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-1}(T)y-y\|

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|(T-2t\cos\omega)Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-1}(T)Ty\|

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(1+3M)(1+2\cos\omega)}{t}\|Ty\|=0,\qquad y\in D(T),

so there is also the convergence

(3.19)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}f_{x}(t)=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}(t^{2}Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-1}(T))^{k-n}x=x.

Secondly, the derivative of the function is given by

f_{x}^{\prime}(t)=2(k-n)t^{2(k-n)-1}(T^{2}-tT\cos\omega)Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n+1)}(T)x.

For some arbitrary
\lambda>0
we can now integrate this derivative over the interval
[\lambda,\infty)
. Using also the limit (
3.19
), this gives

\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}f_{x}^{\prime}(t)dt=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}f_{x}(t)-f_{x}(\lambda)=x-\lambda^{2(k-n)}Q_{\lambda e^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n)}(T)x.

Bringing the term
\lambda^{2(k-n)}Q_{\lambda e^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n)}(T)x
to the other side and applying the operator
T^{k}
on both sides of this equation, allows us to estimate

\displaystyle\|T^{k}x\|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2(k-n)}\|T^{k}Q_{\lambda e^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n)}(T)x\|+\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}\|T^{k}f_{x}^{\prime}(t)\|dt

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2(k-n)}\|T^{k-n}Q_{\lambda e^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n)}(T)T^{n}x\|

\displaystyle\quad+2(k-n)\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}t^{2(k-n)-1}\|(T-t\cos\omega)Q_{te^{i\omega}}^{-(k-n+1)}T^{k+1}x\|dt

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{k-n}(1+3M)^{k-n}\|T^{n}x\|+4(k-n)(1+3M)^{k-n+1}\|T^{k+1}x\|\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}dt

(3.20)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k-n}(1+3M)^{k-n}\|T^{n}x\|+\frac{4(k-n)(1+3M)^{k-n+1}\|T^{k+1}x\|}{\lambda},

where in the third line we used Lemma
3.3
. Next we will minimize the right hand side with respect to the parameter
\lambda>0
.

\circ
First we consider the case
T^{k+1}x\neq 0
. Since
k+1\geq n
also
T^{n}x\neq 0
, and the minimum of the right hand side is attained at the point
\lambda=\big(\frac{4(1+3M)\|T^{k+1}x\|}{\|T^{n}x\|}\big)^{\frac{1}{k-n+1}}>0
. Plugging this value
\lambda
into (
3.20
), gives the upper bound

(3.21)

\|T^{k}x\|\leq(k-n+1)4^{\frac{k-n}{k-n+1}}(1+3M)^{\frac{(k-n)(k-n+2)}{k-n+1}}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{1}{k-n+1}}\|T^{k+1}x\|^{\frac{k-n}{k-n+1}}.

\circ
If
T^{k+1}x=0
, the estimate (
3.20
) looks like

\|T^{k}x\|\leq\lambda^{k-n}(1+3M)^{k-n}\|T^{n}x\|.

But since
\lambda>0
is arbitrary, we conclude
\|T^{k}x\|=0
and the estimate (
3.21
) is satisfied also in this case. Hence in both cases the estimate (
3.21
) is satisfied, which is exactly the stated inequality (
3.18
) with
m=k+1
.

For the induction step
m\rightarrow m+1
let
x\in D(T^{m+1})
. Using the induction assumption once for
n<k<m
in the first upcoming inequality, and with
n<m<m+1
in the second inequality, gives

\displaystyle\|T^{k}x\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,k,m}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\|T^{m}x\|^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,k,m}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{m-k}{m-n}}\big(C_{n,m,m+1}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{1}{m-n+1}}\|T^{m+1}x\|^{\frac{m-n}{m-n+1}}\big)^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}

\displaystyle=C_{n,k,m}C_{n,m,m+1}^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\|T^{n}x\|^{\frac{m-k+1}{m-n+1}}\|T^{m+1}x\|^{\frac{k-n}{m-n+1}}.\qed

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
T
be as in Definition
3.2
. Then for every
n<k<m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there holds

D(T^{k})\in K_{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}(D(T^{n}),D(T^{m}));

i.e. according to Definition
2.8
there exists some
C_{n,k,m}\geq 0
, such that

(3.22)

K(t,x)\leq C_{n,k,m}t^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{k})},\qquad t>0,\,x\in D(T^{k}),

where the
K
-functional is the one of the couple
D(T^{n}),D(T^{m})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in D(T^{k})
. For every
t\geq 1
, we can choose
a=x
and
b=0
to estimate the
K
-functional by

(3.23)

K(t,x)\leq\|a\|_{D(T^{n})}+t\|b\|_{D(T^{m})}=\|x\|_{D(T^{n})}\leq C_{n,m}t^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{k})},\qquad t\geq 1,

where in the last inequality we used
t\geq 1
as well as the constant
C_{n,m}
from the embedding
D(T^{m})\hookrightarrow D(T^{n})
in Lemma
3.4
. For
0<t\leq 1
on the other hand we choose
\tau=t^{-\frac{1}{m-n}}\geq 1
, and consider a similar splitting as in (
3.14
), namely

\displaystyle x=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq k}}}}}}^{m}\frac{m!(2\cos\omega)^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!\tau^{-\beta-2\gamma}}T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{\tau e^{i\omega}}^{-m}(T)x}_{=:a\in D(T^{k})\subseteq D(T^{n})}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq k-1}}}}}}^{m}\frac{m!(2\cos\omega)^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!\tau^{-\beta-2\gamma}}T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{\tau e^{i\omega}}^{-m}(T)x}_{=:b\in D(T^{2m+1})\subseteq D(T^{m})}.

Using Lemma
3.3
, the norms of these vectors
a
and
b
can now be estimated by

\displaystyle\|a\|_{D(T^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq k}}}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{\|T^{2\alpha+\beta-k}Q_{\tau e^{i\omega}}^{-m}(T)T^{k}x\|+\|T^{n-k+2\alpha+\beta}Q_{\tau e^{i\omega}}^{-m}(T)T^{k}x\|}{\tau^{-\beta-2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}{{2\alpha+\beta\geq k}}}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}(1+3M)^{m}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{k}}+\frac{1}{\tau^{k-n}}\Big)\|T^{k}x\|

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m}(1+3M)^{m}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{k}}+\frac{1}{\tau^{k-n}}\Big)\|T^{k}x\|\leq 2(4+12M)^{m}\frac{\|T^{k}x\|}{\tau^{k-n}},

as well as

\displaystyle\|b\|_{D(T^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq k-1}}}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\frac{\|T^{2\alpha+\beta}Q_{\tau e^{i\omega}}^{-m}(T)x\|+\|T^{m-k+2\alpha+\beta}Q_{\tau e^{i\omega}}^{-m}(T)T^{k}x\|}{\tau^{-\beta-2\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0}}{{{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}}{{2\alpha+\beta\leq k-1}}}}}}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}(1+3M)^{m}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}\big(\|x\|+\tau^{m-k}\|T^{k}x\|\big)

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{m}(1+3M)^{m}\big(\|x\|+\tau^{m-k}\|T^{k}x\|\big),

where from the respective second to third lines we added the missing terms in the sum by removing the conditions
2\alpha+\beta\geq k
and
2\alpha+\beta\leq k-1
, and used the trinomial sum
\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=0,\alpha+\beta+\gamma=m}^{m}\frac{m!2^{\beta}}{\alpha!\beta!\gamma!}=4^{m}
. With (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) we then get

\displaystyle K(t,x)

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{D(T^{n})}+t\|b\|_{D(T^{m})}

\displaystyle\leq(4+12M)^{m}\Big(\frac{2\|T^{k}x\|}{\tau^{k-n}}+t\|x\|+t\tau^{m-k}\|T^{k}x\|\Big)

\displaystyle=(4+12M)^{m}\Big(3t^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\|T^{k}x\|+t\|x\|\Big)

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq 3(4+12M)^{m}t^{\frac{k-n}{m-n}}\|x\|_{D(T^{k})},\qquad 0<t\leq 1.

Hence, in (
3.23
) and (
3.26
) we have shown that in both cases
t\geq 1
and
0<t\leq 1
the estimate (
3.22
) is satisfied.

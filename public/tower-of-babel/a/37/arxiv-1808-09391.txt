Abstract
I propose an alternative method for measuring the C ​ P CP violating phase ϕ 2 \phi_{2} ( α \alpha ) without ambiguity in an extended SU(2) isospin triangle analysis, which can ultimately be achieved by exploiting interference effects between B 0 → ρ 0 ​ ρ 0 B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0} and B 0 → a 1 ± ​ π ∓ B^{0}\to a_{1}^{\pm}\pi^{\mp} in a time-dependent flavour-tagged amplitude analysis. Under certain assumptions on the effective ϕ 2 \phi_{2} in each channel, I demonstrate with an idealised amplitude model that potential deviations in the measured ϕ 2 \phi_{2} due to penguin contamination in B 0 → a 1 ± ​ π ∓ B^{0}\to a_{1}^{\pm}\pi^{\mp} are sufficiently large within current experimental uncertainties that this programme could be executed with Run 3 data at LHCb and easily at Belle II.

Strong-penguin containment in
\phi_{2}

In general, the extraction of
\phi_{2}
is complicated by the presence of interfering amplitudes that distort the experimentally determined value of
\phi_{2}

It is possible to remove the isospin-conserving component of this contamination by invoking SU(2) arguments. The original method considers the three possible charge configurations of

pipi_th1

3
Resolving solutions with time-dependent amplitude analysis

The time-dependent decay rates of
B^{0}
and
\bar{B}^{0}
decays to a self-conjugate final state are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma(t)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle e^{-t/\tau}[(|A|^{2}+|\bar{A}|^{2})+(|A|^{2}-|\bar{A}|^{2})\cos\Delta m_{d}t-2\Im(\bar{A}A^{*})\sin\Delta m_{d}t],

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}(t)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle e^{-t/\tau}[(|A|^{2}+|\bar{A}|^{2})-(|A|^{2}-|\bar{A}|^{2})\cos\Delta m_{d}t+2\Im(\bar{A}A^{*})\sin\Delta m_{d}t],

(2)

respectively, where
A
is the static decay amplitude,
\tau
is the
B^{0}
lifetime and

is the mass difference between the
B_{H}
and
B_{L}
mass eigenstates. This form assumes no
CP
violation in the mixing
|q/p|=1
, and that the total decay rate difference between the two mass eigenstates is negligible.

In the typical isobar approach, the total amplitude

A_{i}
, as a function of 4-body phase space
\Phi_{4}
,

A\equiv\sum_{i}a_{i}A_{i}(\Phi_{4}),

(3)

where

is a strong complex coupling determined directly from the data. Incorporating a complex

can be written as

\bar{A}\equiv\sum_{i}a_{i}\lambda_{i}\bar{A}_{i}(\Phi_{4})=\sum_{i}a_{i}\lambda_{i}A_{i}(\bar{\Phi}_{4}),

(4)

where the phase space has been transformed under
C
and
P

A_{i}
should only contain strong dynamics blind to flavour.

As
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
is an all-pion final state, only intermediate decay channels carrying the weak phase
\phi_{2}
, may populate the surrounding phase space in the limit of non-existent penguin contributions. In such a scenario, it is clear that
\lambda=\exp(2i\phi_{2})
can factorise out of the coherent sum given in eq.
4

3

\Phi_{4}
. Then by the final term of eq.
3

\Im(\bar{A}A^{*})=\Im(\lambda AA^{*})=\Im(\lambda|A|^{2})

must be real-defined, the imaginary part of the aforementioned product evaluates to

, leaving two solutions remaining for
\phi_{2}
.

Perhaps ironically, sizeable penguin contamination in the four-charged-pion final state is the key to extracting a single solution for the effective
\phi_{2}
in
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
with the time-dependent amplitude analysis technique. Consider that current analyses have yet to find evidence for other significant contributions in the
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
region, limiting interference opportunities. While there is little doubt these should emerge with increased statistics, it is difficult to envision that the penguin contributions to such amplitudes already suppressed relative to
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
can be so large as to prevent the weak phase from factorising. However, by expanding the analysis phase space to include
B^{0}\to a_{1}^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}

CP
violation parameters from the penguin-free expectations of
\phi_{2}
and null, respectively. These penguin-induced deviations in turn, allow for a unique determination of the effective
\phi_{2}
in
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
through interference, with the amount of data expected to be collected by
B
physics experiments in the near future.

It should be reminded at this point that isospin-breaking (
I=1
)
\rho

\rho

B^{0}\to\rho^{+}\rho^{-}
and

which cannot be in an

Extension to the isospin triangle analysis

I follow the general approach outlined by the CKMfitter Group given in ref.

\frac{1}{\tau_{B}^{i+j}}{\cal B}^{ij}=\frac{|\bar{A}^{ij}|^{2}+|A^{ij}|^{2}}{2},\hskip 10.0pt{\cal A}_{CP}^{ij}=\frac{|\bar{A}^{ij}|^{2}-|A^{ij}|^{2}}{|\bar{A}^{ij}|^{2}+|A^{ij}|^{2}},\hskip 10.0pt{\cal S}_{CP}^{ij}=\frac{2\Im(\bar{A}^{ij}A^{ij*})}{|\bar{A}^{ij}|^{2}+|A^{ij}|^{2}},

5
Time-dependent amplitude model

Now that the possibility to constrain a single solution for
\phi_{2}
in
B\to\rho\rho
is established under an ideal set of circumstances, it is important to estimate the feasibility of distinguishing between the two solutions of
\phi_{2}^{00}
given the current experimental knowledge of the
4\pi
final state. A primary concern regarding this proposed method is whether the hadronic uncertainties, particularly those arising from the
a_{1}^{\pm}
, can be controlled at a level that will still permit a clear distinction between the two solutions. Fortunately, parameters obtained from asymmetry measurements generally tend to be more immune to the effects of the model. However, in order to demonstrate this assertion, I generate a set of pseudo-experiments varying the hadronic model within its current experimental uncertainties. I then set the yields to those expected at various milestones expected during the timelines of the Belle II and LHCb experiments and record the difference in
-2\log{\cal L}
for each solution of
\phi_{2}^{00}
obtained from a maximum likelihood fit to each pseudo-experiment. In any case, we only need sufficient precision to resolve a solution as most of the statistical power on the central value of
\phi_{2}
will come from
B^{0}\to\rho^{+}\rho^{-}
. Experimental effects such as detection efficiency, timing resolution and background contributions will be neglected in this study.

2S+1
in accordance with the number of degrees of freedom available to a spin-
S
state. The sum over these polarisation indices of the inner product of polarisation tensors form the fundamental basis on which all spin amplitudes are built. Called the spin projection operator
P
, it has the capacity to project an arbitrary tensor onto the subspace spanned by the spin projections of the spin-
S
state.

Another particularly useful object is the relative orbital momentum spin tensor
L
, which for some process
R\to AB
, is the relative momenta of the decay products
q_{R}\equiv p_{A}-p_{B}
projected to align with the spin of
R
,

L_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\mu_{L}}(p_{R},q_{R})=P_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\mu_{L}\nu_{1}\nu_{2}...\nu_{L}}(p_{R})q_{R}^{\nu_{1}}q_{R}^{\nu_{2}}...q_{R}^{\nu_{L}}.

(13)

Finally, to ensure that the spin amplitude behaves correctly under parity transformation, it is sometimes necessary to include the Levi-Cevita totally antisymmetric tensor
\epsilon_{abcs}p_{R}^{d}
. Each stage of a decay is represented by a Lorentz scalar obtained by contracting an orbital tensor between the decay products with a spin wavefunction of equal rank representing the final state.

Four topologies are necessary for this analysis. They include three for
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
as
S
,
P
and
D
waves are permitted between the two
\rho^{0}
resonances,

\displaystyle S\text{-wave}:\hskip 10.0ptS\propto L_{a}(p_{\rho^{0}_{1}},q_{\rho^{0}_{1}})L^{a}(p_{\rho^{0}_{2}},q_{\rho^{0}_{2}}),

\displaystyle P\text{-wave}:\hskip 10.0ptS\propto\epsilon_{abcd}L^{d}(p_{B^{0}},q_{B^{0}})L^{c}(p_{\rho^{0}_{1}},q_{\rho^{0}_{1}})L^{b}(p_{\rho^{0}_{2}},q_{\rho^{0}_{2}})p^{a}_{B^{0}},

\displaystyle D\text{-wave}:\hskip 10.0ptS\propto L_{ab}(p_{B^{0}},q_{B^{0}})L^{b}(p_{\rho^{0}_{1}},q_{\rho^{0}_{1}})L^{a}(p_{\rho^{0}_{2}},q_{\rho^{0}_{2}}).

(14)

For
B^{0}\to a_{1}^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}

S

a_{1}^{\pm}\to\rho^{0}\pi^{\pm}

S\propto L_{a}(p_{B^{0}},q_{B^{0}})P^{ab}(p_{a_{1}^{\pm}})L_{b}(p_{\rho^{0}},q_{\rho^{0}}),

(15)

7
Conclusion

I present an extension to the SU(2) isospin triangle analysis, that has the capacity to resolve the
\phi_{2}
degeneracy in the
B\to\rho\rho
system under certain conditions. For this purpose a time-dependent flavour-tagged amplitude analysis involving
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
decays is proposed, where the phase space must be expanded over that used in current analyses to allow its interference with
B^{0}\to a_{1}^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}
to be sufficiently understood. If meaningful discrimination between the two solutions of the effective
\phi_{2}
in
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
can be achieved and its central value is not located in the middle of the two solutions for the effective
\phi_{2}
coming from
B^{0}\to\rho^{+}\rho^{-}
, then the
\phi_{2}
solution degeneracy in
B\to\rho\rho
can be resolved.

The current experimental uncertainty in
B^{0}\to a_{1}^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}
combined with a theoretical prediction of its own effective
\phi_{2}
, indicates that the benefits of potential deviation from tree-level expectations as would be induced by penguin contributions to these channels, are sufficient to warrant the additional experimental complications involved. Under varying assumptions on the
CP
violation present in
B^{0}\to\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
, the main hadronic uncertainties due to the
a_{1}^{\pm}
also appear to be under control as studies with roughly estimated signal yields at LHCb and Belle II indicate a real possibility to resolve
\phi_{2}
in the
B\to\rho\rho
system at the end of Run 2 and with 10 ab
-1
, respectively.

Abstract
. In this paper, we present an innovative particle system characterized by moderate interactions, designed to accurately approximate kinetic flocking models that incorporate singular interaction forces and local alignment mechanisms. We establish the existence of weak solutions to the corresponding flocking equations and provide an error estimate for the mean-field limit. This is achieved through the regularization of singular forces and a nonlocal approximation strategy for local alignments. We show that, by selecting the regularization and localization parameters logarithmically with respect to the number of particles, the particle system effectively approximates the mean-field equation.

1.
INTRODUCTION

In this work, we explore the following kinetic Cucker-Smale (C-S) flocking model characterized by its inclusion of confinement, a nonlocal interaction, a local alignment force, and a diffusion component

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{v}\cdot[\big(\gamma v+\lambda(\nabla_{x}V+\nabla_{x}W*\rho)\big)f]=\nabla_{v}\cdot[\beta(v-u)f+\sigma\nabla_{v}f],

subject to the initial data

f(x,v,0)=f_{0},\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here
d\geq 3
,
f=f(x,v,t)
is the particles distribution function at
(x,v,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
are local particles density and velocity respectively, which are given by

\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fdv\quad\mbox{and}\quad u=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}vfdv}{\rho}.

The function
V(x)=|x|^{2}/2
is a smooth confinement potential and
W(x)
is an interaction potential, which is chosen to be the Newtonian potential case as following form

\displaystyle W(x)=\frac{1}{(d-2)|B(0,1)|}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}},

where
|B(0,1)|
denotes the volume of unit ball
B(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
|B(0,1)|=\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}/\Gamma(d/2+1)
,
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the Gamma function. In (
1.1
), the first two terms account for the free transport of particles, while the third term consists of linear damping with strength
\gamma>0
and the confinement and interaction forces due to potentials with strength
\lambda>0
.
The right-hand side of (
1.1
) represents the local alignment forces with
\beta>0
and the diffusion term in velocity with
\sigma>0
. It denotes the nonlinear
Fokker–Planck operator in
[
31
]
given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}\cdot[\beta(v-u)f+\sigma\nabla_{v}f]=\sigma\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big(f\nabla_{v}\log\frac{f}{M_{u}}\Big)

with the local Maxwellian

\displaystyle M_{u}:=\frac{\beta^{d/2}}{(2\pi\sigma)^{d/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{\beta|u-v|^{2}}{2\sigma}\Big).

Note that this local alignment force had been introduced in
[
18
]
for swarming models. In fact, it can be understood as the localized version of the nonlinear damping term introduced by
[
23
]
as a suitable normalization of the C-S model
[
9
]
. And it is also a nonlinear damping relaxation towards the local velocity used in classical kinetic theory
[
4
,
31
]
.

The kinetic C-S equation, pivotal in describing the motion of self-propelled particles such as bird flocking, fish schooling, and phenomena across biology, the internet, and sociology, was first introduced by Cucker and Smale in 2007
[
9
,
10
]
. This foundational work has been further examined and applied in various studies, including those by
[
12
,
16
,
22
,
23
,
27
,
30
,
32
]
. In 2008, Ha and Tadmor
[
13
]
expanded upon this by deriving a Vlasov-type mean-field model incorporating the C-S particles term and demonstrating its time-asymptotic flocking behavior for compactly supported initial data. Motsch and Tadmor, in 2011
[
23
]
, proposed a modified C-S model featuring normalized and non-symmetric alignment. Although this variant was not the central focus of their study, it introduced an important perspective on alignment mechanisms. The exploration of the C-S model continued with Karper, Mellet, and Trivisa in 2013
[
18
]
, who investigated the model’s dynamics with strong local alignment, noise, self-propulsion, and friction, proving the existence of weak solutions. Their subsequent works in 2014
[
19
]
and 2015
[
20
]
further validated the existence of weak solutions for the Motsch–Tadmor model and examined the singular limits of the C-S model under conditions of strong noise and alignment, respectively, illustrating convergence to the Euler-flocking system. Choi’s 2016 study
[
8
]
on the global well-posedness and asymptotic behavior of the Vlasov–Fokker–Planck equation with local alignment forces marked a significant advancement, demonstrating global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions. Lastly, in 2018, Figalli and Kang
[
11
]
rigorously analyzed the singular limit of the C-S model with strong local alignment, showing its convergence to a pressureless Euler system with nonlocal flocking dissipation.
More recently, Carrillo and Choi explored the kinetic C-S flocking model, focusing on aspects of confinement, nonlocal interaction, and local alignment forces. In their 2020 study
[
2
]
, they not only discussed the existence of weak solutions to this model but also established quantitative bounds on the error between the kinetic equation and the aggregation equation through the limit of large friction. Building on this work, they further investigated a class of Vlasov-type equations with nonlocal forces in 2021
[
3
]
, paying special attention to the existence of weak solutions for a continuity type equation as in (
1.1
).

The primary goal of this paper is to deduce equation (
1.1
) from a stochastic many-particle system. To this end, we consider a filtered probability space defined by
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and introduce
\{B^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
, a collection of independent
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-Brownian motions. The dynamics of the system, which features moderate interactions among numerous particles, are described in relation to a set of parameters denoted by
\xi:=(\varepsilon,\delta,\nu)
. The model’s formulation is as follows:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)=&V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)dt,\qquad i\in[N]:=\{1\cdots\cdots N\},\\
dV_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)=&\sqrt{2\sigma}dB^{i}(t)-\gamma V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)dt-\beta\Big(V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-u_{\xi}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)\big)\Big)dt\\
&-\lambda\Big(\nabla_{x}V(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\neq j}^{N}\nabla_{x}W_{\varepsilon}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(t)\big)\Big)dt,\end{aligned}\right.

where
X_{\xi,N}^{i},V_{\xi,N}^{i}
denote the position and velocity of the
i
th particles at time
t
, and we assume the initial data
\{(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(0),V_{\xi,N}^{i}(0))\}_{i=1}^{N}
are i.i.d. with the common distribution
f_{0}
. Here
W_{\varepsilon}(x)
is the regularized function of
W(x)
, which is given by

W_{\varepsilon}(x)=C_{d}(\varepsilon+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{d-2}{2}},\qquad C_{d}=\frac{1}{(d-2)|B(0,1)|},

and we can directly compute

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{d}{2}},\qquad\|D^{2}W_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{d+2}{2}}.

The approximated local particle’s velocity is

(1.4)

\displaystyle u_{\xi}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))=\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\xi,N}^{j}(t)\phi_{2}^{\delta}(V_{\xi,N}^{j}(t))\cdot\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(t))}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(t))+\nu}.

For each
\varepsilon>0
,
\phi_{1}
is the standard mollifier, the function
\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}dx=1,\ spt(\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon})\subset B(0,\varepsilon)
. For
\phi_{2}(v)\in C^{2}[0,+\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(v)=\begin{cases}1,&v\leq 1,\\
0,&v\geq 2,\end{cases}\quad 0\leq\phi_{2}\leq 1,\ |\phi_{2}^{\prime}|\leq C\ \mbox{and}\ |\phi_{2}^{\prime\prime}|\leq C,

and taking
\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)=\phi_{2}(\delta|v|),\ v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\parallel v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\parallel_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2/\delta,\qquad\parallel\nabla\big(v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\big)\parallel_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C.

We will demonstrate that the proposed particle system (
1.2
) accurately approximates the kinetic equation (
1.1
) in the limit as
N\to\infty
and
\xi\to 0
.

Firstly, we study the large particle limit. Specifically, with
\xi>0
held fixed, we illustrate how the system (
1.2
) approaches an intermediate stochastic system in the limit of
N\to\infty

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)=&\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)dt,\\
d\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)=&\sqrt{2\sigma}dB^{i}(t)-\gamma\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)dt-\beta\Big(\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)-\overline{u}_{\xi}\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)\big)\Big)dt\\
&-\lambda\Big(\nabla_{x}V(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))+\nabla_{x}W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)\big)\Big)dt,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\rho_{\xi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\xi}(x,dv)
and
f_{\xi}(x,v,t)
is the probability density of
(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t),\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t))
.
The initial data
\{(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(0),\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(0))\}_{i=1}^{N}
have the same distribution as the initial data of (
1.2
). Here
\overline{u}_{\xi}
is given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\cdot\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))dv}{\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))+\nu}.

System (
1.5
) is uncoupled, since
\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}
depends on
N
only through the initial datum.
In Section 3, we present a key contribution of this paper: an error estimate of the mean-field limit through comparing the solutions of the moderately interacting many-particle system,
\{(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t),V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from (
1.2
), with those of the intermediate system,
\{(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t),\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from (
1.5
):

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<t<T}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}+|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{3}\ln(N^{\alpha})}{N}\big(1+T^{3}N^{T^{3}\alpha}\ln(N^{\alpha})\big)\,,

where for fixed
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

\ln(N^{\alpha})\sim 1/(\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2})
, namely we have chosen
\xi
to be logarithmically with respect to the number of particles.

The rigorous derivation of mean-field equations from their underlying many-particle systems has been a subject of investigation since the 1980s, with seminal contributions from Sznitman
[
28
,
29
]
.
To address the singularity presented in the interaction force
W
, the use of a cut-off parameter has become a common approach, as highlighted in works such as
[
1
,
14
,
15
,
21
]
. For the treatment of the local alignment term
u
, a strategy known as moderate interaction is applied within
u_{\xi}
, resulting in interactions that are more localized compared to those found in the mean-field regime. A closely related work by Oelschläger
[
24
]
demonstrated that interacting stochastic particle systems weakly converge to a deterministic nonlinear process as the number of particles increases. He further elucidated the derivation of the porous-medium equation from the limit dynamics of a large interacting particle system
[
25
]
, with subsequent applications found in
[
17
,
26
]
. In more recent developments, Chen, Göttilich and Knapp
[
6
]
applied Oelschläger’s approach to rigorously derive error estimates for solutions to stochastic particle systems and their diffusion-aggregation equation limits. Additionally, Chen, Daus and Jüngel
[
5
]
established the mean-field limit for weakly interacting stochastic many-particle systems across multiple population species in the entire space. Finally, Chen, Holzinger, Jüngel and Zamponi
[
7
]
executed a mean-field-type limit for stochastic moderately interacting many-particle systems with singular Riesz potential, leading to the emergence of nonlocal porous-medium equations.

Furthermore, for a fixed
\xi>0
, applying Itô’s formula to the intermediate stochastic system yields a regularized version of the equation (
1.1
) for
f_{\xi}
-the probability density function of the solutions
\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)
to (
1.5
):

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{\xi}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\xi}-\nabla_{v}\cdot[\big(\gamma v+\lambda(\nabla_{x}V+\nabla_{x}W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi})\big)f_{\xi}]=\nabla_{v}\cdot[\beta(v-u_{\xi})f_{\xi}+\sigma\nabla_{v}f_{\xi}],

\displaystyle f_{\xi}(x,v,0)=f_{0},\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(1.8)

\displaystyle u_{\xi}(x,t)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\cdot\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}dv}{\nu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}dv}.

Lastly, by taking the limit as
\xi\to 0
in the regularized equation (
1.7
), we establish that the limit function
f
, derived from
f_{\xi}
, fulfills the equation (
1.1
). In Section 2, we provide proofs for the existence of solutions to both (
1.1
) and (
1.7
). Our proof methodology draws upon the approaches found in the works of Carrillo et al.
[
2
,
3
]
and Karper et al.
[
18
]
, yet it introduces a novel perspective through the application of our different regularized equation.

This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 details the establishment of global existence for weak solutions to the kinetic equation (
1.1
) and its regularized counterpart, alongside deriving the convergence relations between their solutions. In Section 3, we demonstrate the existence and uniqueness for the moderately interacting particle system (
1.2
) and the intermediate system (
1.5
). Moreover, this section highlights the main contribution of our study: mean-field limit error estimates between the solutions of the moderately interacting many-particle system and the intermediate system, quantified in terms of expectation.

2.
EXISTENCE OF WEAK SOLUTIONS

This section focuses on establishing the global existence of weak solutions for the kinetic equation (
1.1
) and its associated regularized problem, drawing inspiration from the methodologies detailed in
[
3
]
and
[
18
]
. In the first subsection, we address the global existence of weak solutions to the regularized problem (
1.7
). Subsequently, the subsection 2.2 demonstrates the derivation of weak solutions for the kinetic equation (
1.1
) by considering the limit as
\xi\to 0
.

We first show the following
lemma, which is quoted from
[
3
, Lemma 5.3, Lemma 5.4]
and
[
18
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{f^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
and
\{G^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be bounded in
L^{p}_{loc}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<p<\infty
. If
f^{n}
and
G^{n}
satisfy

(2.1)

\displaystyle f_{t}^{n}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}=\partial_{v}G^{n},\qquad f^{n}|_{t=0}=f^{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and assume that for
r\geq 2
,

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\|(|v|^{r}+|x|^{2})f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))}<\infty.

Then for any
\varphi(v)
such that
|\varphi(v)|\leq c(1+|v|)
, the sequence

\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\Big\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}

is relatively compact in
L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
q\in(1,\frac{d+r}{d+1})
.

To clarify the limit of
\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\Big\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
, we further give the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
r\geq 2
, let
\{f^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be a sequence satisfying

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\|(|v|^{r}+|x|^{2})f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))}<\infty

and

f^{n}\rightharpoonup f\qquad in\ L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))\quad\forall p\in(1,\infty]\,.

Assume that for any
\varphi(v)
satisfying
|\varphi(v)|\leq c(1+|v|)
, the sequence
\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\Big\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
converges strongly to some
h\in L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
q\in(1,\frac{d+r}{d+1})
. Then it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\varphi(v)dv\qquad in\ L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad as\ n\to\infty\,.

Proof.

Firstly, according to the assumption, it holds that for
q\in(1,\frac{d+r}{d+1})
,

(2.2)

f^{n}\rightharpoonup f\quad\mbox{ in }L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})\,.

Then we claim that

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(v)f^{n}dv\rightharpoonup\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(v)fdv\quad\mbox{ in }L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

To prove the claim, we consider the text function
\Psi(t,x)\in L^{q^{\prime}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. Then for any
R_{\varepsilon}>0
it holds that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\Psi(t,x)dxdt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\varphi(v)dv\Psi(t,x)dxdt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|v|\leq R_{\varepsilon}}(f^{n}-f)\varphi(v)\Psi(t,x)dvdxdt\Big|+\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}(f^{n}-f)\varphi(v)\Psi(t,x)dvdxdt\Big|.

On the one hand, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
R_{\varepsilon}
sufficiently large such that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}(f^{n}-f)\varphi(v)\Psi(t,x)dvdxdt\Big|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\|\Psi(t,x)\Big\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\Big\|\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}\varphi(v)(f^{n}-f)dv\Big\|_{L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}c(1+|v|)|f^{n}-f|dv\Big|^{q}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}(c(1+|v|))^{\frac{r}{q}}|f^{n}-f|^{\frac{1}{q}}\frac{|f^{n}-f|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}{(c(1+|v|))^{\frac{r}{q}-1}}dv\Big|^{q}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}c^{r}(1+|v|)^{r}|f^{n}-f|dv\Big(\int_{|v|\geq R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|f^{n}-f|}{(c(1+|v|))^{\frac{rq^{\prime}}{q}-q^{\prime}}}dv\Big)^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}dxdt

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\|f^{n}-f\|^{\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})}}{R_{\varepsilon}^{r-q-\frac{dq}{q^{\prime}}}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|v|)^{r}|f^{n}-f|dvdxdt\leq\varepsilon,

where
\frac{rq^{\prime}}{q}-q^{\prime}>d
is equivalent to
1<q<\frac{d+r}{d+1}
.

On the other hand, for fixed
R_{\varepsilon}>0
, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(v)\textbf{I}_{|v|\leq R_{\varepsilon}}\Psi(t,x)|^{q^{\prime}}dvdxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}c^{q^{\prime}}(1+|v|)^{q^{\prime}}\textbf{I}_{|v|\leq R_{\varepsilon}}dv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Psi(t,x)|^{q^{\prime}}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{\varepsilon},c,d)\|\Psi\|^{q^{\prime}}_{L^{q^{\prime}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty.

This means that
\varphi(v)\textbf{I}_{|v|\leq R_{\varepsilon}}\Psi(t,x)
can be seen as a test function in
L^{q^{\prime}}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which according to (
2.2
) leads to

(2.5)

\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|v|\leq R_{\varepsilon}}(f^{n}-f)\varphi(v)\Psi(t,x)dvdxdt\Big|\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

This combining with (
2.4
) concludes that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\rightharpoonup\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\varphi(v)dv\qquad\mbox{as}\ n\to\infty\quad\mbox{in}\ L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

for any
q\in(1,\frac{d+r}{d+1})
.

Notice that by relative compactness assumption there exists some
h\in L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
converges to
h
strongly. Due to the uniqueness of the limit we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{n}\varphi(v)dv\to h=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\varphi(v)dv\qquad\mbox{as}\ n\to\infty\quad\mbox{in}\ L^{q}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

∎

2.1.
Solvability of the regularized problem

As already noted in the introduction, the regularized problem for
f_{\xi}
is

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{\xi}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\xi}-\nabla_{v}\cdot[\big(\gamma v+\lambda(\nabla_{x}V+\nabla_{x}W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi})\big)f_{\xi}]=\nabla_{v}\cdot[\beta(v-u_{\xi})f_{\xi}+\sigma\nabla_{v}f_{\xi}],

\displaystyle f_{\xi}(x,v,0)=f_{0},\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
u_{\xi}
is defined in (
1.8
) and has the following form

\displaystyle u_{\xi}(x,t)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\cdot\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}dv}{\nu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}dv}.

The purpose of this subsection is to prove existence of weak solutions for regularized problem (
2.6
). The main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T>0
. Suppose that
f_{0}
satisfies

f_{0}\in L_{+}^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad and\quad(|v|^{2}+V+W*\rho_{0})f_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then for any
\lambda,\beta,\gamma>0
, there exists a weak solution
f_{\xi}
,
\xi=(\varepsilon,\delta,\nu)
of Eq.(

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\|\rho_{\varphi}^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{h_{2}}|j_{\nu}|\leq\frac{C}{h_{2}}|j_{\nu}|\qquad\mbox{on}\ \mathcal{A}_{\mu}.

Thus, we can use the dominated
convergence theorem to get

\displaystyle K_{1}=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\mu}}\Big(\frac{1}{\nu+\rho_{\nu}}-\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)j_{\nu}\cdot\rho_{\varphi}^{\nu}dxdt\to 0\qquad\mbox{as}\ \nu\to 0.

For
K_{2}
, since
\rho>h_{2}
in
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
, we estimate from (
2.52
),

\displaystyle K_{2}=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\mu}}\frac{1}{\rho}(k_{\nu}-j)\cdot\rho_{\varphi}^{\nu}dxdt\leq\frac{1}{h_{2}}\|j_{\nu}-j\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{A}_{\mu})}\|\rho_{\varphi}^{\nu}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A}_{\mu})}\to 0\qquad\mbox{as}\ \nu\to 0,

where
q\in(1,(p+2)/(p+1))
.
For the estimate of
K_{3}
, since
u\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f_{\nu}\rightharpoonup f
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}))
for some
q\in[1,\infty]
, obviously,

\displaystyle K_{3}=\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\mu}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(f_{\nu}-f)\varphi dvdxdt\to 0\qquad\mbox{as}\ \nu\to 0.

In summary,

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{\mu}}u_{\nu}\rho_{\varphi}^{\nu}-u\rho_{\varphi}dxdt\to 0\qquad\mbox{as}\ \nu\to 0,

and by (
2.55
), we have

M=u\rho_{\varphi}\qquad\mbox{on}\ \ \mathcal{A}_{\mu}.

Since the choices of
h_{1},h_{2}
and
\mu
are arbitrary, we now obtain

M=u\rho_{\varphi}\qquad\mbox{on}\ \{\rho>0\}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\nu}f_{\nu}\Psi dxdvdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\nu}\rho_{\varphi}^{\nu}\psi dxdt

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\rho_{\varphi}\psi dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}uf\Psi dxdvdt\quad\mbox{as}\ \nu\to 0.

Thus, for all test functions
\Psi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\nu\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{\nu}f_{\nu}\Psi dxdvdt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}uf\Psi dxdvdt,

which implies
u_{\nu}f_{\nu}
weakly converges to
uf
. Therefore,
f
is a weak solution to (
1.1
).

∎

3.
Error Estimation for the Mean-Field Limit

In this section, we primarily focus on establishing the error estimate for solutions in the expectation sense for the stochastic system with moderately many particles, denoted as (
1.2
), and the mean-field system, denoted as (
1.5
), as outlined in Theorem
3.2
. Additionally, we demonstrate the existence and uniqueness of solutions for these systems.

3.1.
Unique existence of solutions to systems (
1.2
) and (
1.5
)

Firstly, we establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions for systems (
1.2
) and (
1.5
). Notice that for fixed
\xi>0
, since
\nabla_{x}W_{\varepsilon}
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous in (
1.3
), we can obtain the following result by standard SDE theory.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any fixed
\xi
, the problem (
1.2
) has a unique global solution
(X^{i}_{\xi,N}(t),V^{i}_{\xi,N}(t))
.

Then, with the help of Theorem
2.1
and
2.2
, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

If the regularized problem (
2.6
) has a unique solution
f_{\xi}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
, then the initial value problem (
1.5
) has a unique global solution
(\overline{X}_{\xi}(t),\overline{V}_{\xi}(t),f_{\xi})
.

Proof.

Let
f_{\xi}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\big)
be the unique solution of the regularized problem (
2.6
) with the initial data
f_{\xi}(x,v,0)=f_{0}
. Then the following stochastic particles system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t)=&\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)dt,\\
d\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)=&\sqrt{2\sigma}dB(t)-\gamma\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)dt-\beta\big(\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)-u_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t))\big)dt\\
&-\lambda\Big(\nabla V(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t))+\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}(t))\Big)dt,\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle\rho_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}(t),v)dv,\qquad u_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}(t))=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\cdot\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}(t),v)dv}{\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}(t))+\nu},

has a unique global solution
(\widetilde{X}(t),\widetilde{V}(t))
because the coefficients are globally Lipschitz for any fixed
\xi
. Denote by
\widetilde{f}_{\xi}
as the probability density function of
(\widetilde{X}(t),\widetilde{V}(t))
, then it follows from Itô’s formula that for any smooth test function
\varphi(x,v,t)\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, it holds

\displaystyle\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(T),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(T),T\big)-\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(0),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(0),0\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big[-\partial_{t}\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),t\big)-\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)\nabla_{x}\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),t\big)+\gamma\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)\nabla_{v}\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),t\big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\lambda\big(\nabla V(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t))+\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t))\big)+\beta\big(\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t)-u_{\xi}(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t))\big)\Big)\nabla_{v}\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),t\big)

\displaystyle-\sigma\Delta_{v}\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),t\big)\Big]dt+\sqrt{2\sigma}\int_{0}^{T}\nabla_{v}\varphi\big(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),t\big)dB(t),

by taking the expectation, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widetilde{f}_{\xi}(x,v,T)\varphi(x,v,T)dxdv-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}\varphi(x,v,0)dxdv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widetilde{f}_{\xi}(x,v,t)\Big[-\partial_{t}\varphi(x,v,t)-v\nabla_{x}\varphi(x,v,t)+\gamma v\nabla_{v}\varphi(x,v,t)

\displaystyle+\Big(\lambda(\nabla V+\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(x,t))+\beta\big(v-u_{\xi}(x,t)\big)\Big)\nabla_{v}\varphi(x,v,t)-\sigma\Delta_{v}\varphi(x,v,t)\Big]dxdvdt\,.

Thus
\widetilde{f}_{\xi}
satisfies the weak formulation of (
2.6
) also. Thus we have
\widetilde{f}_{\xi}=f_{\xi}
due to the uniqueness.
In other words, the unique solution to (
1.5
) is given by
(\widetilde{X}_{\xi}(t),\widetilde{V}_{\xi}(t),f_{\xi})
, which then can be denoted as
(\overline{X}_{\xi}(t),\overline{V}_{\xi}(t),f_{\xi})
.
∎

3.2.
Convergence estimates for

In the following subsection, we establish the error estimate between solutions of the stochastic moderately many-particle system (

) and the mean-field stochastic system (

Lemma 3.2

Let
\{(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t),V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}\}_{i=1}^{N}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|

\displaystyle u_{\xi}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))-\overline{u}_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))\big|^{2}\big]dt\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\big]dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\big]dt+\frac{T}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\Big),

Proof.

Let
\forall\ i\in[N]

1.4

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\cdot\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))dv}{\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))+\nu}.

By taking their difference and applying the expectation lead to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|u_{\xi}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))-\overline{u}_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))\big|^{2}\big]dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu^{4}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\xi,N}^{j}(t)\phi_{2}^{\delta}(V_{\xi,N}^{j}(t))\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(t))\Big(\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))+\nu\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))dv\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(t))+\nu\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big]dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\nu^{4}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\Big(\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i})+\nu\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\xi,N}^{j}\phi_{2}^{\delta}(V_{\xi,N}^{j})\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}-X_{\xi,N}^{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i})dv\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big]dt

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\nu^{4}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\phi_{2}^{\delta}(v)\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*f_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i})dv\Big(\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}-X_{\xi,N}^{j})\Big|^{2}\Big]dt

The first term is estimated as follows

where for
j\neq l
the expectation is zero. Hence,

|I_{2}^{1}|\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))-\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(t))\Big|^{2}\Big]dt\leq\frac{T}{N\varepsilon^{2d}}\|f_{\xi}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}.

The second term is estimated as follow

\displaystyle I_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N^{2}}\|\nabla\phi_{1}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}N^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|(X_{\varepsilon,N}^{\delta,i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))\big|^{2}\big]dt\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2d+2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)\big|^{2}\big]dt.

Thus,

(3.3)

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\frac{4}{\delta^{2}\varepsilon^{2d}}\|\rho_{\xi}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big(\frac{T}{N\varepsilon^{2d}}\|f_{\xi}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2d+2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\big]dt\Big).

Finally, bring (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) into the inequality (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|

\displaystyle u_{\xi}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))-\overline{u}_{\xi}(\bar{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t))\big|^{2}\big]dt\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\big]dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\big]dt+\frac{T}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\Big),

where
C
is a constant only depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
.

∎

3.3.
Error estimations of the particles solutions

In this section, by Lemma
3.2
and discussion of Section 2, we are able to state the main theorem as following.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t),V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\{(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t),\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be solutions of equations (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) up to some
T>0
. Then for any
\gamma,\lambda,\beta>0
and fixed
0<\alpha\ll 1
,
1/(\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2})\sim\ln(N^{\alpha})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<t<T}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}+|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{3}\ln N^{\alpha}}{N}\big(1+T^{3}N^{T^{3}\alpha}\ln N^{\alpha}\big)\,,

where
C
is a constant depending on
\gamma
,
\lambda
,
\beta
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Proof.

Let
\forall\ i\in[N]
.
By taking the difference of two problems (
1.2
) and (
1.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{i}(t)=|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle t\int_{0}^{t}|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(s)|^{2}ds,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{i}(t)=|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(t)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(t)|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle t\int_{0}^{t}\Big|(\beta+\gamma)\big(\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-V_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)\big)+\lambda\nabla V\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\xi}\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)\big)-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(s)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta\Big(u_{\xi}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)\big)-\overline{u}_{\xi}\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)\big)\Big)\Big|^{2}ds.

Applying the expectation leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|V_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(s)|^{2}]ds\leq t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(s)]ds,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(t)]\leq

\displaystyle\ 4(\beta+\gamma)^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|\overline{V}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-V_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)|^{2}]ds+4\beta^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|u_{\xi}(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s))-\overline{u}_{\xi}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s))|^{2}]ds

\displaystyle+4\lambda^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\nabla V\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)\big)-\nabla V\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)\big)\big|^{2}\big]ds

\displaystyle+4\frac{\lambda^{2}}{N^{2}}t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s),s)-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(s)\big)\Big)\Big|^{2}\big]ds

(3.4)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\ \ 4(\beta+\gamma)^{2}t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(s)]ds+4\beta^{2}tJ_{1}+4\lambda^{2}tJ_{2}+4\lambda^{2}tJ_{3}.

By Lemma
3.2
,
J_{1}
can be estimated

(3.5)

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\ C\Big(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(s)]ds+\frac{1}{\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(s)]ds+\frac{t}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\Big),

where
C
is a constant only depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
. For
J_{2}
, simply,

(3.6)

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\nabla V)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)\big|^{2}\big]ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(s)]ds,

The
J_{3}
can be handled

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-X_{\xi,N}^{j}(s)\big)-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s)\big)\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\big(X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s)\big)-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\big(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s)\big)\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s),s)\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big]ds

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 3(J_{3}^{1}+J_{3}^{2}+J_{3}^{3}).

Now, we derive the estimates for
J_{3}^{1}
,
J_{3}^{2}
and
J_{3}^{3}
separately. For the
J_{3}^{1}
,

\displaystyle J_{3}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla(\nabla W_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}|X_{\xi,N}^{j}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s)|\Big)^{2}\Big]ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big|X_{\xi,N}^{j}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s)\big|^{2}\big]ds\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(s)]ds,

where
\|\nabla(\nabla W_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon^{-(d+2)/2}
. The
J_{3}^{2}
can be handled

\displaystyle J_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla(\nabla W_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\big|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)\big|\Big)^{2}\Big]ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[N^{2}|X_{\xi,N}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)|^{2}\big]ds\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(s)]ds.

The
J_{3}^{3}
is estimated as follows

\displaystyle J_{3}^{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s),s)\Big)\Big|^{2}\Big]ds

\displaystyle\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s))\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{l=1}^{N}\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{l}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s))\Big)\Big]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s))\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\Big(\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{l}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s))\Big)\Big]ds,

where for
j\neq l
the expectation is zero. Hence,

\displaystyle|J_{3}^{3}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\nabla W_{\varepsilon}(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s)-\overline{X}_{\xi}^{j}(s))-\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho(\overline{X}_{\xi}^{i}(s),s)\Big|^{2}\Big]ds\leq\frac{t\parallel f\parallel_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}}{N\varepsilon^{d}},

while exploiting the fact that

\|\nabla W_{\varepsilon}*\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\nabla W_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\rho\parallel_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d/2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}.

Combining
J_{3}^{1}
,
J_{3}^{2}
and
J_{3}^{3}
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(s)]ds+\frac{t\parallel f\parallel_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}}{N\varepsilon^{d}}.

Thus, bring
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
and
J_{3}
into the inequality (
3.4
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(t\big(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}+1\big)\int_{0}^{t}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{M}^{i}(s)\big]ds

\displaystyle\qquad+t\big(\frac{1}{\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}+1\big)\int_{0}^{t}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{N}^{i}(s)\big]ds+t^{2}\big(\frac{1}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}+\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{d}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{t^{2}}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{N}^{i}(s)\big]ds+\frac{t^{2}}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\Big),

where
C
is a constant depending on
\gamma
,
\lambda
,
\beta
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
. By the
\mathrm{Gr\ddot{o}nwall}
’s inequation and taking the supremum in time on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<t<T}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{2}}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\Big(1+\frac{T^{3}}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\exp\frac{T^{3}}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\Big).

Further, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<t<T}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq T\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(s)]ds\leq\frac{CT^{3}}{N\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d}}\Big(1+\frac{T^{3}}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\exp\frac{T^{3}}{\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2}}\Big).

Finally, for any
0<\alpha\ll 1
, we can choose
\varepsilon
,
\nu
and
\delta
so small that
1/(\delta^{2}\nu^{4}\varepsilon^{4d+2})\sim\ln(N^{\alpha})
, having

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<t<T}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}^{i}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{3}\ln N^{\alpha}}{N}\big(1+T^{3}N^{T^{3}\alpha}\ln(N^{\alpha})\big),

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<t<T}\sup\limits_{i=1\dots N}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{N}^{i}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CT^{2}\ln N^{\alpha}}{N}\big(1+T^{3}N^{T^{3}\alpha}\ln(N^{\alpha})\big),

where
C
is a constant depending on
\gamma
,
\lambda
,
\beta
and
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
.
∎

Gapless superconducting state and mirage gap in altermagnets
Miaomiao Wei, Longjun Xiang, Fuming Xu, Lei Zhang, Gaomin Tang, Jian Wang

Abstract
The interplay between spin-orbit interaction (SOI) and magnetism produces interesting phenomena in superconductors. When a two-dimensional (2D) system with strong SOI is coupled to an s s -wave superconductor, an in-plane magnetic field can drive the system into a gapless superconducting state and induce a mirage gap at finite energies for an Ising superconductor. In this work, we demonstrate that when an s s -wave superconductor is proximitized to an altermagnet, the intrinsic anisotropic spin splitting of the altermagnet can result in a gapless superconducting state and a pair of mirage gaps at finite energy. The gapless superconductivity exhibits spin-polarized segmented Fermi surfaces, with coexisting spin-singlet and spin-triplet pairings that have a d d -wave character. Importantly, the gapless superconducting and mirage gap features are quantified through quantum transport. Our results suggest that altermagnet is an ideal platform for studying gapless superconducting states and mirage gap physics.

Quasi-particle spectrum
—
The Hamiltonian of AM is given by

\displaystyle H_{0}(\textbf{k})={{\xi}_{k}}\sigma_{0}+{t_{J}}(k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2})\sigma_{z}

where
{{\xi}_{k}}=(tk^{2}-\mu),k^{2}=k^{2}_{x}+k^{2}_{y}
,

{t_{J}}

{{k}_{F}}=\sqrt{{\mu}/{t}\;}

|{k_{x}}|=|{k_{y}}|

C_{4}

When the altermagnet is proximitized by an

H_{BdG}=\left(\begin{matrix}{{H}_{0}}(\textbf{k})&\Delta i{{\sigma}_{y}}\\
-\Delta i{{\sigma}_{y}}&-H_{0}^{*}(-\textbf{k})\\
\end{matrix}\right),

(1)

where

[

This indicates that the quasi-particle spectrum exhibits spin-splitting characteristics. In the calculation, we set

52
]

By diagonalizing the BdG Hamiltonian, one can determine the quasi-particle spectrum. Figure
1

To analytically analyze the evolution of the quasi-particle spectrum versus
t_{J}
, our focus is on the Fermi momentum
k_{F}
. After diagonalizing the BdG Hamiltonian, four eigenvalues
E_{\pm,\pm}=\pm T_{J}\pm\Delta
, where
T_{J}=t_{J}k_{F}^{2}\cos 2\theta,\theta=\arctan({{{k}_{F,y}}}/{{{k}_{F,x}}})
and

represents the Fermi momenta along the

direction. The ASS results in a direction-dependent spin splitting, which is maximum at
\theta=n{\pi}/{2},n=0,1,2,3
and zero for
\theta=n{\pi}/{4},n=1,3,5,7
. The main superconducting gap is determined by

. When

is zero, there is no spin-splitting superconducting gap, i.e.,

[
18
]
. The location (mid point of the gap) and width of the mirage gap are determined by

\begin{split}{{\varepsilon}^{\pm}_{0}}&={\left({{E}_{\pm,+}}+{{E}_{\pm,-}}\right)}/{2}\;=\pm t_{J}k_{F}^{2}cos2\theta,\\
\delta&={{E}_{+,+}}-{{E}_{+,-}}={{E}_{-,+}}-{{E}_{-,-}}=2\Delta.\end{split}

(2)

As
t_{J}
increases further, the order of the eigenvalues can change. Specifically,
E_{-,-}<E_{-,+}<E_{+,-}<E_{+,+}
. This implies that normal states appear at
E=0
, and a segmented Fermi surface emerges in the system.

—

Conclusion
— In summary, we have demonstrated that in the absence of SOI, the AM-SC can display
d
-wave-like gapless superconducting states. Moreover, both singlet and triplet pairings occur simultaneously at finite energy, resulting in the emergence of mirage gaps in the system. These key features have been validated and quantified through quantum transport calculations. Our findings highlight the AM as an ideal platform for studying gapless superconducting states and mirage gap physics.

Quantum dynamics of Gaudin magnets
Wen-Bin He, Stefano Chesi, H.-Q. Lin, Xi-Wen Guan

Abstract
Quantum dynamics of many-body systems is a fascinating and significant subject for both theory and experiment. The question of how an isolated many-body system evolves to its steady state after a sudden perturbation or quench still remains challenging. In this paper, using the Bethe ansatz wave function, we study the quantum dynamics of an inhomogeneous Gaudin magnet. We derive explicit analytical expressions for various local dynamic quantities with an arbitrary number of flipped bath spins, such as: the spin distribution function, the spin-spin correlation function, and the Loschmidt echo. We also numerically study the relaxation behavior of these dynamic properties, gaining considerable insight into coherence and entanglement between the central spin and the bath. In particular, we find that the spin-spin correlations relax to their steady value via a nearly logarithmic scaling, whereas the Loschmidt echo shows an exponential relaxation to its steady value. Our results advance the understanding of relaxation dynamics and quantum correlations of long-range interacting models of Gaudin type.

I
Model and Bethe ansatz equations

The Gaudin magnet describes a central spin at position “
0
” coupled to bath spins through long-range interactions. The Hamiltonian reads

H=B\mathbf{s}^{z}_{0}+2\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{j}\textbf{s}_{0}\cdot\textbf{s}_{j},

(1)

where the bath spins are labelled with
1\rightarrow N
. For convenience, we parametrize the magnetic field as
B=-{2}/{g}
. Furthermore, we write
A_{j}=1/(\epsilon_{0}-\epsilon_{j})
for the inhomogeneous interaction strength determined in experiments. Setting
\epsilon_{0}=0
, the
\epsilon_{j}
(with
j=1,\ldots,N
) correspond to the energy levels of a discrete BCS model associated with
H
, which can be constructed from the conserved quantities
H_{j}=B\textbf{s}_{j}^{z}+\sum_{k\neq j}\textbf{s}_{j}\cdot\textbf{s}_{k}/(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})

J.Dukelsky2004
;
H.-Q. Zhou
;
Guan-2002
.
Following the algebraic Bethe ansatz method, and considering a subspace where
M
spins are flipped-down with respect to the reference state
|\Uparrow\rangle
, the eigenstates are given below
J.Dukelsky2004
;
H.-Q. Zhou
:

|\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{M}\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}B_{\nu_{\alpha}}|\Uparrow\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{s_{j}^{-}}{\nu_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{j}}|\Uparrow\rangle,

(2)

where the
M
parameters
\{\nu_{\alpha}\}
should satisfy the following Bethe ansatz equations

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{N}\frac{1}{\nu_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{j}}=\frac{2}{g}+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha,\beta=1}^{M}\frac{2}{\nu_{\alpha}-\nu_{\beta}},

(3)

with
\alpha=1,2,\ldots,M
. The equations (
3
) are also called Richardson-Gaudin equations. There are
C_{N+1}^{M}
sets of solutions to Eq. (
3
), which correspond to the number of choices of flipping
M
spins
AFprb
. These
C_{N+1}^{M}
states
|\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{M}\rangle
span the subspace with
M
spins flipped-down.
In Appendix D we give details of the numerical method for solving the Bethe Ansatz Eq. (
3
). Moreover, their eigenenergy is given by

E=\frac{B}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}-\epsilon_{j}}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}-\nu_{\alpha}}.

(4)

We exploited the above eigenfunctions to study the quantum dynamics of the central spin model. The initial state (see introduction) is a simple product state
|\Phi_{0}\rangle=|a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\rangle
, where
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\}
is a set of mutually unequal site indexes which can range from “0” to “
N
”. After the interaction is switched on, the wave function evolves as

|\psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{k}|\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\phi_{k}|\Phi_{0}\rangle e^{-iE_{k}t},

(5)

where we introduced the orthonormalized wave functions
|\phi_{k}\rangle=N_{\nu_{k}}|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle
, with
N_{\nu_{k}}
the normalization coefficient
SuppM
(see Appendix
B
). The index “
k
” labels all the states corresponding to the roots of Eq. (
3
). Since the initial state has
M
spins which are flipped-down, we only need to consider the Bethe ansatz basis in this particular subspace. A critical quantity in Eq. (
5
) is the overlap between the initial state and the eigenfunctions, given as follows:

\langle\Phi_{0}|\phi_{k}\rangle=N_{\nu_{k}}\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\}}\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}\frac{1}{(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}})},

(6)

where “
\mathcal{P}
” means summing over all permutation of indexes
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\}
, see Appendices A and B. The overlap can be also written as
\langle\Phi_{0}|\phi_{k}\rangle=N_{\nu_{k}}\mathrm{perm}(1/(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}})
, where “
\mathrm{perm}
” is the permanent of matrix
1/(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}})
, similar to the determinant
J.Dukelsky2004
;
H.-Q. Zhou
. It is worth noting that both permanent and determinant representation can give the exact quantum dynamics of the Gaudin magnets. However, the numerical tasks from the two representations is quite different as determinants can be evaluated in polynomial time. The interested readers can find more details about permanent and determinant representations in
caux2008
;
AFJPA
;
Claeys2017
. For either representation, it still remains challenging to get
dynamical quantities for the Gaudin magnets with a large system size, and values up to
N=48
were reached
AFprl
.

From expressions (
5
) and (
6
), the evolution of observables (for example the spin distribution) can be calculated rather straightforwardly. Based on this approach, we will present in the rest of the paper our key analytical and numerical results on the quantum dynamics of Gaudin magnets.

II
Spin distribution

By making use of Eq. (
5
), the time-dependent polarization of site
j
is immediately written as

s^{z}_{j}(t)=\sum_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{0}|\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\phi_{k}|s_{j}^{z}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\phi_{k^{\prime}}|\Phi_{0}\rangle e^{-iw_{kk^{\prime}}t},

(7)

where
w_{kk^{\prime}}=E_{k^{\prime}}-E_{k}
simplifies to
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}1/(\epsilon_{0}-\nu_{\alpha,k})-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}1/(\epsilon_{0}-\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}})
. While the overlaps are given in Eq. (
6
), the remaining difficulty is to obtain the matrix elements of the observable in the eigenfunction basis. As detailed in Appendix B, one can insert the complete set of states
|a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\rangle
, thus writing the matrix elements in terms of overlaps of type
\langle\Phi_{0}|\phi_{k}\rangle
. We finally obtain the exact expression:

\displaystyle s_{j}^{z}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}}\cos(w_{kk^{\prime}}t)\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{jj_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k^{\prime}}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})},

(8)

where, here and throughout the article, “
\mathcal{P}
” and “
\mathcal{Q}
” indicate summing over all the permutations of indexes
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\}
and
\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\}
, respectively.

Although it is not immediately obvious from Eq. (
8
),
s_{j}^{z}(t)
recovers the correct initial value when we take the limit
t\rightarrow 0
. Detailed proof can be found in Appendix
B
. There we also show that, as expected,
m^{z}=\sum_{j}s_{j}^{z}(t)=(N+1)/2-M
is conserved by Eq. (
8
). The evolution of spin distribution function for a more general initial state is just an extension of above result Eq. (
8
).

For models with short-range interactions, it is well-known that the time-evolution of non-equilibrium states is characterized by a light-cone dynamics of local observables

;
lieb72
;
Ibloch
;
caux2014

Comparing the two initial states, we see that with

the bath spins need a much longer time than
|\Phi_{B}\rangle
to evolve into a steady state. In fact, for the initial state
|\Phi_{A}\rangle
the two groups of bath spins remain well distinct, and the oscillation in the spin polarization nearly approach periodically their initial values (

show an initial quick decay, leading to oscillations around

displays a strong memory of the initial domain-wall order. In order to further understand such peculiar dynamics, in later sections we will study the time evolution of the spin-spin correlations and the Loschmidt echo.

If the time-average

of an observable coincides its micro-canonical ensemble average, i.e.,
\bar{\mathcal{O}}=\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle
, the system reaches thermalization. Here the time average of the spin distribution

8

with
\mathrm{sinc}(w_{kk^{\prime}}t)=\sin(w_{kk^{\prime}}t)/w_{kk^{\prime}}t

(a) that the time-average of spin polarization of the central spin converges to clearly different values for the two initial states. The difference in the steady-state spin polarizations does not decrease by increasing

, see Fig.

(b), in which smaller values of

(blue diamond) are obtained by MPS method. The combined results of BA and MPS method verify the persistence of a finite difference

for the steady-state value. This observation confirms a breakdown of thermalization in the inhomogeneous central spin model, due to the restricted time evolution implied by integrability.
Since the existence of conserved quantities, like
H_{j}=B\textbf{s}_{j}^{z}+\sum_{k\neq j}\textbf{s}_{j}\cdot\textbf{s}_{k}/(\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{k})
, the time-evolution cannot be ergodic, neither in the whole Hilbert space nor in a subspace with fixed
M
.

\displaystyle G^{z}_{mn}(t)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k^{\prime}}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\cos(w_{kk^{\prime}}t),

IV
Loschmidt echo and MPS approach

The Loschmidt echo, defined as
L(t)=|\langle\Phi_{0}|\psi(t)\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle L(t)=\sum_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\langle\Phi_{0}|\phi_{k}\rangle|^{2}|\langle\phi_{k^{\prime}}|\Phi_{0}\rangle|^{2}e^{-iw_{kk^{\prime}}t},

(11)

\displaystyle L(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}}\left[\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k}}|}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}})}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k^{\prime}}}|}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}})}\right]^{2}\cos(w_{kk^{\prime}}t).

(12)

\chi
in the simulation of Fig.
6
is shown in the inset. As seen, the bond dimension
\chi
of initial state
|\Phi_{B}\rangle
grows faster in time than
|\Phi_{A}\rangle
. Nevertheless, the range of times we can simulate is sufficient to establish conclusively the initial exponential form of decay of Loschmidt echo.
In classical systems, the exponential decay of Loschmidt echo usually implies chaotic dynamics
Dubertrand
;
Prosen
;
Weinstein
. For the central spin model, the exponential decay of Loschmidt echo may indicate the dynamics is chaotic here as well
Emerson
.

V
Conclusion

In summary, based on Bethe Anstaz techniques, we have analytically studied the time evolution of several dynamic quantities of Gaudin magnets with arbitrary number
M
of flipped-down spins, such as spin distribution function, spin-spin correlation function, and Loschmidt echo. Furthermore, we have numerically studied the scaling behavior of the relaxation dynamics, up to relatively large system sizes. Significantly, the correlation function relaxes to its steady value with a logarithmic dependence, and the Loschmidt echo reveals an exponential loss of the memory of the initial state. Our results highlight several interesting features of the dynamical evolution of a quantum many-body system with long range interactions, thus advancing the understanding of decoherence for single qubits coupled to a spin bath at the many-body level. With respect to universal features of quantum many-body systems, much effort is still necessary to better understand the non-equilibrium quantum dynamics
suter
. In future studies, it would be desirable to extend our methods to investigate scrambling in many-body system
htshen
;
prxotoc
, and the out-of-time-of-correlation function (OTOC)
mblotoc
of central-spin models. The details of such dynamical evolution of spin correlations may be accessible experimentally, e.g., through NMR techniques
niknam
.

Appendix A
Bethe ansatz basis

The eigenstates of Eq. (
1
) can be obtained with Bethe ansatz (BA) approach, as discussed in detail in Ref.
H.-Q. Zhou
. Here we discuss several properties of these states which will find wide use in the following derivations. Since the Hilbert space of the Gaudin model can be decomposed into the direct sum of subspaces with
M
flipped-down spins, we introduce two basis sets for such subspaces. One is the natural basis
|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle=S_{j_{1}}^{-},\cdots,S_{j_{M}}^{-}|\Uparrow\rangle
, and another is the Bethe ansatz basis
|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle=B_{\nu_{M,k}},\cdots,B_{\nu_{1,k}}|\Uparrow\rangle
. The corresponding completeness relations are simply given by:

\displaystyle\sum_{M}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle\langle j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}|=I,

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{M,k}|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle\langle\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}|=I,

(14)

where
|N_{\nu_{k}}|
are the normalization coefficients for the Bethe ansatz basis. The two sets of states
|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle
and
|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle
are both complete in the subspace with
M
flipped-down spins. Including all the
M
, the complete basis of the whole Hilbert space is obtained.

The explicit form of the Bethe ansatz eigenfunctions reads

\displaystyle|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}B_{\nu_{\alpha}}|\Uparrow\rangle=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\sum_{j}\frac{s_{j}^{-}}{\nu_{\alpha}-\epsilon_{j}}|\Uparrow\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\left[\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}})}\right]|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle,

(15)

where, as in the main text,
\mathcal{Q}
are the permutations of indexes
\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\}
. Note also that any of the two “
j
” indexes are necessarily unequal. The overlap of state
|a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\rangle
with a BA eigenstate
|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle
can be simply obtained from the above expression as:

\displaystyle\langle a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})},

(16)

where
\mathcal{P}
are the permutations of indexes
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\}
. The above formula can be also written in terms of the permanent of the matrix
M_{kj}=1/(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{a_{j}})

AFJPA
.

The scalar product between eigenstates can be obtained by insertion of
\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle\langle j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}|
:

\displaystyle\langle

\displaystyle\nu_{M,k},\cdots,\nu_{1,k}|\nu_{1,k^{\prime}},\cdots,\nu_{M,k^{\prime}}\rangle=\frac{\delta_{kk^{\prime}}}{|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\left[\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right],

(17)

where we used the overlaps Eq. (
16
) between the two basis sets. The above expression gives the normalization coefficient
|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}
.

We can also derive another useful formula:

\displaystyle\langle j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}|a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\rangle=\sum_{\mathcal{Q}\in\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\}}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{a_{\alpha},j_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k}\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})}

(18)

which is obtained by inserting the resolution of the identity in terms of the BA eigenstates.

Appendix B
Evaluation of physical observables

We now discuss the explicit evaluation of Eq. (
7

|\phi_{k}\rangle=N_{\nu_{k}}|\nu_{1,k},\cdots,\nu_{M,k}\rangle
are the normalized eigenfunctions. Therefore, for an initial state in the form
|\Phi_{0}\rangle=s_{a_{1}}^{-}\cdots s_{a_{M}}^{-}|\Uparrow\rangle=|a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\rangle
, the overlap matrix elements
\langle\Phi_{0}|\phi_{k}\rangle

|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle
and:

\displaystyle\langle j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}|s_{j}^{z}|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{\alpha=1}\delta_{jj_{\alpha}}.

(19)

By inserting the completeness relation in terms of the
|j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\rangle
states, and computing the overlaps with eigenstates
|\phi_{k}\rangle
as in Eq. (
6
), it is easy to find that:

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k}|s_{j}^{z}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle=N_{\nu_{k}}^{*}N_{\nu_{k^{\prime}}}\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{jj_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum\limits_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right]\left[\sum\limits_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right].

(20)

Finally, combining overlaps and matrix element gives:

\displaystyle s_{j}^{z}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\cos(w_{kk^{\prime}}t)\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{jj_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum\limits_{\mathcal{P}}\sum\limits_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right]

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum\limits_{\mathcal{P}}\sum\limits_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k^{\prime}}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right].

(21)

In the above formulas, as usual,
\mathcal{P}
are permutations of the indexes
\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{M}\}
(from the initial state) and
\mathcal{Q}
are permutations of the indexes
\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}\}
. The term proportional to 1/2 (from the parenthesis in the first line) can be further simplified as follows. First we note that this term contains a factor of type Eq. (
17
), which is
\propto\delta_{kk^{\prime}}
. Therefore, we can simply set

in this particular contribution. After noticing this, we can use Eq. (
18
) to perform the summations over
k
and
k^{\prime}
and finally recover the 1/2 appearing in Eq. (
8
) of the main text.

Using a similar method, we can derive the spin-spin correlation function, given by

While the overlaps are found in Eq. (
6

can be obtained in a way completely analogous to Eq. (

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{k}|s_{m}^{z}s_{n}^{z}|\phi_{k^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=N_{\nu_{k}}^{*}N_{\nu_{k^{\prime}}}\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{mj_{\alpha}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{nj_{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[\sum\limits_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right]\left[\sum\limits_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}\right],

(23)

leading to Eq. (
9
) in the main text.

Finally, we discuss how to recover the initial conditions, and prove the conservation of magnetization. When time
t\rightarrow 0
, the spin distribution becomes

\displaystyle s_{j}^{z}(0)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{k}\sum_{k^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{j_{1}<\cdots<j_{M}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M}\delta_{jj_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\sum_{\mathcal{Q}}\frac{|N_{\nu_{k}}|^{2}}{\prod\limits_{\alpha=1}^{M}(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}})(\nu_{\alpha,k}-\epsilon_{{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}})}

Appendix D
Numerical method for solving BA roots

In our work we solve the BA equations (
3
) using the numerical method presented in
AFprb
. For convenience of the reader, in this Appendix we present the numerical method in detail. For general
M
, the asymptotic solution to Eq. (
3

\nu_{\alpha}(0)\approx\epsilon_{j}+\frac{g}{2}+\mathcal{O}(g^{2}).

We then introduce the quantities

(32)

\Lambda_{j}^{2}=-\sum_{l\neq j}\frac{\Lambda_{j}-\Lambda_{l}}{\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{l}}+\frac{2}{g}\Lambda_{j}.

The advantage of using the
\Lambda_{j}
variables is in avoiding the singular dependence of
\nu_{\alpha}
on system parameters. With
\nu_{\alpha}(0)
as initial value, we use Newtonian iteration to find the solution of the above equations at fixed

.

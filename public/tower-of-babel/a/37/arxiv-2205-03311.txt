One-sorted Program Algebras
Igor Sedlár, Johann J. Wannenburg

Abstract
Kleene algebra with tests, KAT, provides a simple two-sorted algebraic framework for verifying properties of propositional while programs. Kleene algebra with domain, KAD, is a one-sorted alternative to KAT. The equational theory of KAT embeds into KAD, but KAD lacks some natural properties of KAT. For instance, not each Kleene algebra expands to a KAD, and the subalgebra of tests in each KAD is forced to be the maximal Boolean subalgebra of the negative cone. In this paper we propose a generalization of KAD that avoids these features while still embedding the equational theory of KAT. We show that several natural properties of the domain operator of KAD can be added to the generalized framework without affecting the results. We consider a variant of the framework where test complementation is defined using a residual of the Kleene algebra multiplication.

Introduction

Kleene algebra with tests
[
9
]
, KAT, is a simple algebraic framework for verifying properties of propositional while programs. KAT subsumes propositional Hoare logic
[
10
]
and it has been applied in a number of verification tasks. KAT has computationally attractive fragments
[
14
]
and its extensions have been applied beyond while programs, for instance in network programming languages
[
1
]
.

KAT is two-sorted, featuring a Boolean algebra of tests embedded into a Kleene algebra of programs. For various reasons, a one-sorted alternative to KAT may be desirable. One such alternative is Kleene algebra with domain, KAD, in its “internal” formulation
[
4
]
. The idea of KAD is to expand Kleene algebra with a domain operator
d
such that the set of images of elements of the algebra under
d
forms a Boolean algebra. Hence, one obtains a Boolean algebra of tests in a one-sorted setting. Consequently, the equational theory of KAT embeds into the equational theory of KAD.

However, the properties of the domain operator in KAD lead to some curious features of the framework. For instance, the algebra of tests is always the maximal Boolean subalgebra of the negative cone in the underlying Kleene algebra. In a sense, then, every “proposition” is considered a test, contrary to some of the intuitions expressed in
[
9
]
. Moreover, not each Kleene algebra can be expanded with a domain operator, not even every finite one. This is in contrast with the fact that each KA is expands to a KAT.

In this paper we generalize KAD to a framework we call
one-sorted Kleene algebra with tests
, KAt. Our framework has the desired features of KAD: every KAt contains a Boolean subalgebra of tests and the equational theory of KAT embeds into the equational theory of KAt. In addition, every Kleene algebra expands into a KAt, and the subalgebra of tests in KAt is not necessarily the maximal Boolean subalgebra of the negative cone. We also consider various extensions of KAt with axioms known from KAD to show which properties of the domain operator are still consistent with the desired features of KAt. In addition, we consider a variant of the KAt framework where test complementation is defined using a residual of Kleene algebra multiplication. It can be shown that algebras in this framework form a variety.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sections
2
and
3
we recall Kleene algebra with tests and Kleene algebra with domain, respectively. In Section
4
we introduce one-sorted Kleene algebra with domain. We show that every Kleene algebra with domain is a one-sorted Kleene algebra with tests, that the test algebra in every one-sorted KAT is a Boolean algebra, and that every Kleene algebra expands into a one-sorted Kleene algebra with tests. In Section
5
we consider some extensions of one-sorted KAT that retain the good properties. In Section
6
we show that the equational theory of KAT embeds into the equational theory of one-sorted KAT and a number of its extensions. Section
7
briefly considers a residuated variant of one-sorted KAT that forms a variety.

•

•

3
Kleene algebra with domain

KAT is a two-sorted framework, and for various reasons it may be desirable to look for a one-sorted alternative. For instance, “one-sorted domain semirings are easier to formalise in interactive proof assistants and apply in program verification and correctness.”
[
5
, p. 576]
. A one-sorted alternative called Kleene algebra with domain was introduced in
[
4
]
, following earlier work on a related two-sorted framework
[
3
]
. The idea is to expand a Kleene algebra with an operation
d
that transforms elements
x\in K
into
test-like
elements
d(x)
, and to show that the set of images of
x\in K
under
d
,
d(K)
, forms a Boolean algebra. In other words, instead of assuming the existence of a Boolean subalgebra, such a subalgebra arises from images under a new operation
d
.

The desired properties of
d
are inspired by relational KAT, where tests are subsets of the identity relation. One obvious choice of a
d
such that
d(R)
is a subset of the identity relation is the
relational domain
operation

d(R):=\{(s,s)\mid\exists u.(s,u)\in R\}

Informally,
d(R)
represents the set of states in which the program associated with
R
has a terminating computation. The main question shaping the definition of KAD is the following:
which properties of relational domain should be adopted on the abstract algebraic level in order to yield a Boolean subalgebra?

Definition 2
.

A
predomain
operation on a Kleene algebra is any
d:K\to K
such that

\displaystyle x\leq d(x)x

(1)

\displaystyle d(xy)\leq d(xd(y))

(2)

\displaystyle d(x)\leq 1

(3)

\displaystyle d(0)=0

(4)

\displaystyle d(x+y)=d(x)+d(y)

(5)

A
domain
operation on a Kleene algebra is any predomain operation that satisfies

d(xy)=d(xd(y))

(6)

It is easily checked that the relational domain operation is a domain operation on relational Kleene algebras.

Proposition 1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{K}
be a Kleene algebra and let
d
be a domain operation on
\mathscr{K}
. Let
d(K):=\{y\mid\exists x.y=d(x)\}
. Then:

1.

d(K)
contains
1
and
0
, and it is closed under
\cdot
and
+
;

2.

d(\mathscr{K})=(d(K),\cdot,+,1,0)
is a bounded distributive lattice.

In order to obtain a Boolean algebra from the distributive lattice

, one has to make sure that each test
d(x)
is complemented in
d(K)
, that is, for each
d(x)
there is
y\in d(K)
such that
d(x)y=0
and

. An elegant solution to this problem presented in
[
4
]
consists in expanding Kleene algebras with a single unary operation

(

) that allows to define a domain operation

and has properties entailing that

is a complement of
d(x)
.

Definition 3
.

A
Kleene algebra with domain
is a Kleene algebra expanded with an operation
a:K\to K
such that

\displaystyle a(x)x=0

(7)

\displaystyle a(xy)\leq a(x\,a(a(y))

(8)

\displaystyle a(x)+a(a(x))=1

(9)

We define
d(x):=a(a(x))
.

Lemma 1

(
[
4
]
)
.

In each KAD,
d
is a domain operation and

is a complement of
d(x)
.

Theorem 1

(
[
4
]
)
.

In each KAD,
d(\mathscr{K})=(d(K),\cdot,+,1,0)
is a Boolean algebra.

It has been shown that, at least in the semiring signature extended with

6

[
4
]
; see the first counterexample in the appendix. This is in contrast to the fact that every Kleene algebra expands to a KAT. This feature is caused by (

[

]
express interest in variants of

The immediate candidate is predomain, but it can be shown that if

Hence, it is interesting to ask if there is a suitable one-sorted alternative to KAT, in the sense of satisfying the following properties:

•

(P0)
The algebras in the given class

4
One-sorted KAT

In the present section, we define a class of algebras satisfying (P0–3). The algebras in the class will be called simply
one-sorted Kleene algebras with tests
, KAt. Property (P4) is established in Section
6
.

Definition 4
.

A
one-sorted Kleene algebra with tests
is a Kleene algebra expanded by two unary operations
t
and
t^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle t(0)=0

(10)

\displaystyle t(1)=1

(11)

\displaystyle t(t(x)+t(y))=t(x)+t(y)

(12)

\displaystyle t(t(x)t(y))=t(x)\,t(y)

(13)

\displaystyle t(x)t(x)=t(x)

(14)

\displaystyle t(x)\leq 1

(15)

\displaystyle 1\leq t^{\prime}(t(x))+t(x)

(16)

\displaystyle t^{\prime}(t(x))\,t(x)\leq 0

(17)

\displaystyle t^{\prime}(t(x))=t(t^{\prime}(t(x)))

(18)

Example 1
.

Relational KA with
t(R)=\{(w,w)\mid\exists v.\,(w,v)\in R)\}
and
t^{\prime}(R)=\{(w,w)\mid\neg\exists v.\,(w,v)\in R)\}

Every KAD

Every KAD is a KAt.

Proof.

It needs to be checked that the KAt axioms are satisfied by any KAD where

∎

.

For all KAt,

Proof.

The set

contains constants

by (

is closed under

∎

For each KAt
\mathscr{K}
, let

by Lemma

Theorem 2
.

Each

is a Boolean algebra.

Proof.

Each

is a bounded distributive lattice by (

Theorem 3
.

Each Kleene algebra expands to a KAt.

Proof.

The proof is similar as in the case of KAT. For any Kleene algebra
(K,\cdot,+,^{*},1,0)
, define:

t(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }x=0\\

The rest is checked by easy calculation.

Theorem
3
shows that, unlike KAD and like KAT, KAt is a proper generalization of Kleene algebra. The proof of the theorem also shows that

need not be the maximal Boolean subalgebra of the negative cone.

On the other hand, one may say that

lacks any intuitive relation to

is a left preserver of

, that is,

. This lack of connection between

can be seen as the reason why (P0–3) are satisfied. However, the following section shows that this is only partially so.

5
An extension of KAt

In this section we consider a particular extension of KAt with a number of axioms known from KAD. We show that the extension still has properties (P0–3).

Definition 5
.

A
strong KAt
, sKAt, is a KAt satisfying the following axioms:

\displaystyle t(x+y)=t(x)+t(y)

\displaystyle x\leq t(x)x

(

The algebra from Prop. 5.3 in

It is clear that sKAt satisfies (P0) and (P1). It can also be shown, by revisiting the construction in the proof of Theorem

8
Conclusion

In this paper we studied a one-sorted variant of Kleene algebra with tests, KAt. We have shown that KAt generalizes Kleene algebra with domain, KAD, and that it retains the good properties of KAD, namely (P1) the test algebra of any KAt is a Boolean algebra and (P4) the equational theory of KAT embeds into the equational theory of KAt. Moreover, KAt avoids the bad properties of KAD: unlike KAD, every Kleene algebra expands to a KAt (P2), and the test algebra in KAt is not necessarily the maximal Boolean subalgebra of the negative cone (P3). We have shown that adding several axioms familiar from KAD to KAt does in fact not injure (P2) and (P3). We believe that this leads to a better general view of the landscape of one-sorted program algebras.

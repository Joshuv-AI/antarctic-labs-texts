Matrices of simple spectrum in irreducible representations of cyclic extension of simple algebraic groups
A.E. Zalesski

Abstract




Abstract. Let
H
be a linear algebraic group whose connected
component
G\neq 1
is simple,
H/G
is cyclic and
Z(H)\subseteq Z(G)
.
We determine the irreducible projective
representations
\phi
of
H
such that
\phi(G)
is irreducible and
\phi(h)
has simple spectrum for some
h\in H
. The latter means that all irreducible constituents of the group
\phi(\langle h\rangle)
are of multiplicity 1. (Here
\langle h\rangle
is the subgroup of
H
generated by
h
.) This extends an earlier known result for
H=G
.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to extend the result of
[
4
, §6]
,
[
11
]
on the irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups whose image contains a matrix of simple spectrum. We assume that the representation field
F
is algebraically closed;
in this case, the spectrum of a square matrix
M
over
F
is the set of its eigenvalues and the spectrum of
M

is called simple if every eigenvalue has multiplicity 1. In particular, a matrix with simple spectrum is similar to
a diagonal matrix with all diagonal entries are distinct.

Let
G
be a linear algebraic group,
\rho
a rational representation and
g\in G
. If
M=\rho(g)
has simple spectrum then
g
is a semisimple element and all weights of
\rho
are of multiplicity 1. In fact, this is equivalent for
\rho(G)

to contain a matrix with simple spectrum. For every simple algebraic group the irreducible representations whose all weights are of multiplicity 1 have been determined in
[
11
]
, extending an earlier results by Seitz
[
4
, §6]
.

Recently R. Guralnick raised a question whether this result can be extended to groups
H=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
,
where
G
is a simple algebraic group and
\sigma
is an outer automorphism of
G
. We prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let G be a simple algebraic group in defining characteristic
p\geq 0
, let
\sigma
be a non-trivial graph automorphism of G and
H=\langle G,\sigma\rangle
. Let V be an H-module such that
V|_{G}
is rational and irreducible. Let
\omega
be the highest weight of
V|_{G}
.
Suppose that some
h\in H
has simple spectrum on V. Then one of the following holds:

(1)
There exists
g\in G
whose spectrum on V is simple;

(2)

G
is of type
A_{2}
,
p\neq 2,3
and
\omega=p^{j}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})
for some integer
j\geq 0;

(3)

G
is of type
D_{4}
,
p=2
and
\omega=2^{j}\omega_{2}
for some integer
j\geq 0.

Moreover, in each case
(2),(3)
there is an element
h\in(H\setminus G)
with simple spectrum on
V
.

It is well known that
|\sigma|\in\{2,3\}
. If
|\sigma|=p
and
h
is diagonalizable then
h
has simple spectrum on
V

if and only if so has
g=h^{p}
so (1) holds. So we assume that
(|\sigma|,p)=1
. In this case
h\in(H\setminus G)
is diagonalizable if and only if so is
h^{|\sigma|}
.

Theorem
1.1
answers the original question by R. Guralnick, and is sufficient to an application he held in mind.
The assumption on
V|_{G}
to be rational is not essential due to a theorem of Borel and Tits
[
6
, Theorem 42]
.
The assumption “
V|_{G}
is irreducible” is essential.
In general, the problem of describing the irreducible
FH
-modules
V
containing a simple spectrum matrix but reducible on
G
seems to be more complex. We have the following observations:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G,H,\sigma
be as in Theorem
1.1
,
k=|\sigma|
, and let V be an irreducible
FH
-module such that
V|_{G}

is reducible. Let
h\in(H\setminus G)
. Then the spectrum of h on V is simple if and only if
h^{k}
has simple spectrum on every
irreducible constituent of
V|_{G}
. In particular, if the spectrum of h on V is simple then the weights of every irreducible constituent of V are of multiplicity
1
.

The converse is not true in general.

For finite groups of Lie type
G(q)
the irreducible representations (over a field of describing characteristic)
whose image contains a matrix with simple spectrum are determined in
[
8
,
9
]
.
The problem of extending the results of
[
8
,
9
]
to cyclic extensions
H(q)=\langle G(q),h\rangle
, where
h\in H
normalizes
G(q)
, remains open. We obtain some partial results in the framework of Theorem
1.1
, that is, in the case where
H(q)\subset H
. Then Theorem
1.1
reduces
the problem to the cases
(1)-(3)
of it, and the case (1) is sorted out in
[
8
,
9
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G(q)=SL_{3}(q)
or

,
(q,6)=1
and

, where

is the transpose-inverse automorphism of

has simple spectrum on V for some

The sentence “

is irreducible with highest weight

Reduction to representations with non-zero weights of

multiplicity
1

The following lemma is obvious:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a diagonalizable element and

is an integer. If h has simple spectrum then the multiplicity of every eigenvalue of

does not exceed

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a vector space over an algebraically closed field and let
M=M_{1}\oplus...\oplus M_{l}
, where
d:=\dim M_{1}>1
. Let
x\in GL(M)
be a semisimple element; suppose that
xM_{l}=M_{1}
,
xM_{i}=M_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,l-1
, and that
x^{l}

acts scalarly on
M_{1}
. Then each eigenvalue of

on

is of multiplicity

.

Proof.

This is straightforward.∎

In the following statement we do not assume
V|_{G}
to be irreducible.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G,H,\sigma
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Let V be an H-module such that
V|_{G}
is rational and
h\in H
. Suppose that h has simple spectrum on V. Then all non-zero weights of G on V are of multiplicity
1
and the multiplicity of weight
0
is at most
k:=|\sigma|
.

Proof.

If
h\in G
then
h\in T
for some maximal torus of
G
. Then obviously, all
T
-weights are of multiplicity 1, as required. So we assume
h\notin G
. Set
g=h^{k}
so we have
g\in G
.
Then
g\in G
is semisimple, and hence
g\in T
, where
T
is some maximal torus of
G
. Moreover,
T
can be chosen
h
-stable. (Indeed,
C_{G}(g)^{0}
, the connected component of
C_{G}(g)
, is an
h
-stable semisimple algebraic group on which
h
acts as a semisimple automorphism; by
[
5
, Theorem 7.5]
,

C_{G}(g)^{0}
has an
h
-stable maximal torus, which remains maximal in
G
.) We fix this

, so
h\in N_{H}(T)
.)

Set
W_{H}=N_{H}(T)/T

and
W=N_{G}(T)/T
. Then
W
is the Weyl group of
G
and
W_{H}=\langle\sigma,W\rangle
. The conjugation action of
N_{H}(T)
on
T
induces the action of
W_{H}
on
\Omega:={\rm Hom}\,(T,F^{\times})\cong\mathop{\mathbb{Z}}\nolimits^{n}
, the set of
T
-weights of
G
. Denote by
\omega(V)
the set of
T
-weights of
V
. Then
\omega(V)
is stable under
W_{H}
. For
\lambda\in\omega(V)
denote by
V_{\lambda}
the
\lambda
-weight space of
V
. Then
hV_{\lambda}=V_{\sigma(\lambda)}
for every
\lambda\in\Omega
, so
h
permutes
V_{\lambda}
’s. (Here we define
\sigma(\lambda)(t)=\lambda(\sigma(t))
and
\sigma(t)=h^{-1}th
for
t\in T
.)
Clearly,
g
stabilizes every
V_{\lambda}
and acts scalarly on it.

Suppose the contrary, and choose
0\neq\lambda\in\omega(V)
so that
\dim V_{\lambda}>1
. Then
y(\lambda)\in\omega(V)

and
\dim V_{y(\lambda)}=\dim V_{\lambda}
for every
y\in W_{H}
.

(i) Suppose first that
hV_{w(\lambda)}\neq V_{w(\lambda)}
for some
w\in W
. Since
k
is a prime,
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}h^{i}V_{w(\lambda)}
is a direct sum as
any sum of distinct weight spaces is a direct sum of them.
By Lemma
2.2
,
h
has an eigenvalue of multiplicity at least 2 (in fact,
the multiplicity in question equals
\dim V_{\lambda}
).
(Indeed, set
M_{1}=V_{w(\lambda)}
,
M_{i}=h^{i}V_{w(\lambda)}
in Lemma
2.2
.) So we have a contradiction.

(ii) Suppose that
hV_{w(\lambda)}=V_{w(\lambda)}
for all
w\in W
.
Then
V_{w(\lambda)}=hV_{w(\lambda)}=V_{\sigma(w(\lambda))}
and hence

\sigma
fixes
w(\lambda)
or all
w\in W
.

Next let
R
be the vector space over
\mathop{\mathbb{Q}}\nolimits
spanned by
\Omega.
Then
W_{H}\subset GL(R)
, and
W
is well known to be a (finite) irreducible subgroup of
GL(R)
. Let
L
be the
\mathop{\mathbb{Q}}\nolimits
-span of the set
\{w(\lambda):w\in W\}
. Then
L=R
as
WL=L
,
L\neq 0
and

is irreducible. This means that
\sigma
fixes every weight of
G
, and hence
\sigma(t)=t
for every
t\in T
. In addition,
\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha
for every root
\alpha
of
G
(as
\alpha\in R
).
This implies that
\sigma
is a trivial automorphism of
G
, which is a contradiction.

Let
V_{0}=\{v\in V:tv=v
for all
t\in T\}
, so
V_{0}
is the zero weight space on
V
. Then
hV_{0}=V_{0}
and
h^{k}
acts on
V_{0}
trivially. Let
h_{0}
be the restriction of
h
to
V_{0}
. Then
h_{0}
has simple spectrum on
V_{0}
, and the claim follows from Lemma
2.1
.∎

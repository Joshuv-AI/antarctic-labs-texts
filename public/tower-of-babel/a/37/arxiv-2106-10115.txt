Quot Schemes for Kleinian Orbifolds This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Enumerative and Gauge-Theoretic Invariants in honor of Lothar Göttsche on the occasion of his 60th birthday. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Gottsche.html
Alastair CRAW a, Søren GAMMELGAARD b, Ádám GYENGE c and Balázs SZENDRŐI b

Abstract
For a finite subgroup Γ ⊂ SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℂ ) \Gamma\subset{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{C}) , we identify fine moduli spaces of certain cornered quiver algebras, defined in earlier work, with orbifold Quot schemes for the Kleinian orbifold [ ℂ 2 / Γ ] \big[\mathbb{C}^{2}\!/\Gamma\big] . We also describe the reduced schemes underlying these Quot schemes as Nakajima quiver varieties for the framed McKay quiver of Γ \Gamma , taken at specific non-generic stability parameters. These schemes are therefore irreducible, normal and admit symplectic resolutions. Our results generalise our work [ Algebr. Geom. 8 (2021), 680–704] on the Hilbert scheme of points on ℂ 2 / Γ \mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma ; we present arguments that completely bypass the ADE classification.

Introduction

Let
\Gamma\subset{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{C})
be a finite subgroup and let
\Hilb^{n}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)
denote the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on the singular surface
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
, parametrising
ideals of colength
n
in the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[x,y]^{\Gamma}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
. In an earlier paper
[
craw2019punctual
]
, we proved that (the reduced scheme underlying) this scheme is irreducible, normal, and admits a unique projective symplectic resolution

\pi_{n}\colon\ n\Gamma\mathchar 45\relax\Hilb\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)\longrightarrow\Hilb^{n}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)_{\rm red},

where
n\Gamma\mathchar 45\relax\Hilb\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
is a certain component of the
\Gamma
-fixed locus

\Hilb\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)^{\Gamma}
on the Hilbert scheme of points on

\mathbb{C}^{2}
itself. It was well known that
n\Gamma\mathchar 45\relax\Hilb\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
is a quiver variety
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,n\delta)
associated to the framed McKay quiver of
\Gamma
, where
\theta
is generic in a GIT chamber
C_{n\delta}^{+}
and where the dimension vector on the McKay quiver is taken as a multiple of a fixed vector
\delta
.
In
[
craw2019punctual
, Theorem 1.1]
we reconstructed
the morphism
\pi_{n}
by variation of GIT quotient for quiver varieties; specifically, we identified a parameter
\theta_{0}
in the boundary of the chamber
C_{n\delta}^{+}
such that
\pi_{n}
is the morphism from
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,n\delta)
to
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{0}}(1,n\delta)
. This allowed us to interpret by variation of GIT quotient all possible ways in which
\pi_{n}
can be factored as a sequence of primitive contractions.

To prove these results in
[
craw2019punctual
]
, we introduced
a family of algebras
A_{I}
obtained by ‘cornering’, each
indexed by a non-empty subset
I
of the set of irreducible
representations of
\Gamma
. We showed
that the fine moduli spaces of modules
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n\delta_{I})

for these algebras with very specific dimension vectors
are isomorphic to the quiver varieties mentioned above.
Moreover, for
I
consisting of the trivial
representation only, we recovered the space
\Hilb^{n}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)
.

In this paper, we have three main goals.
First, we aim to
understand better the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}
,
constructing them as a kind of Quot scheme
that is both natural and geometric.
Second, with applications in mind, we consider arbitrary dimension vectors, not just multiples of some set of fixed dimension vectors as above. One interpretation of our results is a new fine moduli space structure, as well as a (noncommutative)
geometric interpretation, of a large class of
Nakajima quiver varieties for certain non-generic stability parameters.
Our third and final goal is to provide proofs that completely bypass any case-by-case analysis of ADE diagrams.

To state our main result,
let
r
denote the number of nontrivial irreducible representations of
\Gamma
.
For any non-empty subset
I\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,r\}
, let
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
denote the Nakajima quiver variety associated to the affine ADE graph for some dimension vector
(1,v)
, where
\theta_{I}
is a specific stability condition determined by our choice of
I
; see (
5.1
) for the definition.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subseteq\{0,\ldots,r\}
be a non-empty subset and
let
n_{I}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{I}
be a vector. There is an orbifold Quot scheme
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
such that:

1)

there is an isomorphism
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\cong\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
to a fine moduli space associated to the algebra
A_{I}
obtained by cornering;

2)

when
I
is a singleton corresponding to a one-dimensional representation of
\Gamma
, the orbifold Quot scheme is isomorphic to a
classical Quot scheme for
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
;

3)

the orbifold Quot scheme is non-empty if and only if the quiver variety
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is non-empty for some vector

v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
satisfying
v_{i}=n_{i}
for all
i\in I
, in which case, after changing the values of
v_{k}
for
k\not\in I
if necessary, we have

\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)_{\rm red}\cong\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v),

where on the left we take
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
with the reduced scheme structure.

In particular, when it is non-empty,
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)_{\rm red}
is irreducible, normal, and it has symplectic, hence rational Gorenstein, singularities. Moreover, it admits at least one projective symplectic resolution.

Remark 1.2
.

Fix the dimension vector
v=n\delta
that we refer to in our opening paragraph.

1.

For
I=\{0\}
, the statement of
Theorem
1.1
specialises to
[
craw2019punctual
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Indeed, the classical Quot scheme from Theorem
1.1
(2) specialises to the Hilbert scheme in this case by Example
3.6
, and the isomorphism from Theorem
1.1
(3) constructs
\Hilb^{[n]}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)_{\rm red}
as a quiver variety by Example
6.3
and Theorem
. Moreover, the polarising ample bundle on
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
lies in an extremal ray of the movable cone described in
[
bellamy2020birational
]
, so it admits a unique projective symplectic resolution.
In fact, our arguments here reprove the main result of
[
craw2019punctual
]
without having to resort to any case-by-case analysis of ADE diagrams.

2.

For
I=\{1,\dots,r\}
, the stability condition
\theta_{I}
lies in the relative interior of a wall of the chamber
C^{+}_{n\delta}
. It follows from
[
bellamy2020birational
, Theorem 1.2]
that the polarising ample bundle on
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
lies in the interior of the movable cone, so
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
admits more than one (in fact, precisely two) projective crepant resolutions.

Thus, while the projective symplectic resolution is unique in
[
craw2019punctual
, Theorem 1.1]
, it is not possible to assert uniqueness
in Theorem
1.1
above.

While some of our
arguments follow
[
craw2019punctual
]
closely,
we introduce
Quot schemes in a general noncommutative
context, establishing a basic representability result in Proposition
3.1
that may be of broader interest. Furthermore, while the
morphism

\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)

induced by varying a generic stability condition
\theta
to a special one
\theta_{I}
is always surjective for the
dimension vector
v=n\delta
relevant to the case
\Hilb^{n}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)
discussed above,
the same is not true in general.
Instead, one has to correct
v
to
obtain a surjective morphism of quiver varieties.

The main application of
this paper will be in studying
generating functions attached to these moduli spaces,
following
[
gyenge2017euler
,
gyenge2018euler
]
.
In forthcoming work, various generating functions of Euler numbers of Hilbert
and Quot schemes for ADE singularities and orbifolds will be studied, demonstrating
specialisation phenomena also on the level of these generating series.
A poset of Quot schemes corresponding to the poset of
subsets of
\{0,\dots,r\}
,
generalising that of
[
craw2019punctual
, Section 5]
,
will play an important role in that study.
As in
[
gyenge2017euler
,
gyenge2018euler
]
, it will be essential
that we work with arbitrary dimension vectors,
not just multiples of
\delta
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we collect
the necessary preliminaries on semi-invariants of finite group actions and the McKay correspondence. In Section
3
, we define Quot schemes for Kleinian orbifolds and discuss some of their properties. In Section
4
, we introduce the algebras
A_{I}

and the associated fine moduli spaces, and we identify these moduli spaces with Quot schemes for Kleinian orbifolds. In Section
5
,
we obtain a resolution of singularities for the quiver varieties of interest.
Finally, in Section
6
we establish the key isomorphism from Theorem
1.1
(3).

Conventions.

We work throughout over
\mathbb{C}
. In particular, all schemes are
\mathbb{C}
-schemes and all tensor products are taken over
\mathbb{C}
unless otherwise indicated. We often use the following partial order on dimension vectors: for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for
u=(u_{i}),v=(v_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we define
u\leq v
if
u_{i}\leq v_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. We adopt the sign convention of King
[
king1994moduli
]
for
\theta
-stability, so a module
M
over an algebra is
\theta
-stable (resp.
\theta
-semistable) if
\theta(M)=0
and every nonzero proper submodule
N\subsetneq M
satisfies
\theta(N)>0
(resp.
\theta(N)\geq 0
).

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Three algebras arising from the McKay graph

Let
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{GL}(V)
be a finite subgroup of the general linear group for an
m
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
.
Choosing a basis of
V
, we can write the symmetric algebra of the dual vector space
V^{*}
as a polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
. The group
\Gamma
acts dually on
R
: for
g\in\Gamma
and
f\in R
, we have
(g\cdot f)(v)=f\big(g^{-1}v\big)
for all
v\in V
. Let
R^{\Gamma}
denote the
\Gamma
-invariant subring of
R
.

List the irreducible representations of
\Gamma
as
\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{r}\}
, where
\rho_{0}
is the trivial representation.
The decomposition of
R
as an
R^{\Gamma}
-module can be written
[
goodman2009symmetry
, Proposition 4.1.15]

\displaystyle R\cong\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq r}R_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\rho_{i},

where
R_{i}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma}(\rho_{i},R)
is an

R^{\Gamma}
-module; note that
R_{0}=R^{\Gamma}
.

From now on, let
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
be a finite subgroup acting as above on
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]
. The
McKay graph
of
\Gamma

has vertex set
\{0,1,\dots,r\}
corresponding to the irreducible representations of
\Gamma
, where for each
0\leq i,j\leq r
, we have
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma}(\rho_{j},\rho_{i}\otimes V)
edges joining vertices
i
and
j
. McKay
[
McKay80
]
observed that this graph is an affine Dynkin diagram of ADE type. We frame this graph by introducing an additional vertex, denoted
\infty
, together with an edge joining the vertices
\infty
and
0
; we refer to the resulting graph as the
framed McKay graph
of
\Gamma
.

We now associate a doubled quiver to each graph. For this, consider
the set of pairs
Q_{1}
comprising an edge in the framed McKay graph
and an orientation of the edge. For each
a\in Q_{1}
, we write
t(a)
,

h(a)
for the vertices at the tail and head respectively of
the oriented edge, and we write
a^{\ast}
for the same edge with
the opposite orientation. Define the
framed McKay quiver of
\Gamma
,
denoted
Q
, to be the quiver with vertex set
Q_{0}=\{\infty,0,\dots,r\}

and arrow set
Q_{1}
. The (unframed)
McKay quiver
,
denoted
Q_{\Gamma}
, is the complete subquiver of
Q
on
the vertex set
\{0,1,\dots,r\}
. Using these quivers
we define the following three algebras.

First, let
\mathbb{C}Q
denote the path algebra of the framed McKay quiver
Q
. For
i\in Q_{0}
, let
e_{i}\in\mathbb{C}Q
denote the idempotent corresponding to the trivial path at vertex
i
. Let
\epsilon\colon Q_{1}\to\{\pm 1\}
be any map such that
\epsilon(a)\neq\epsilon(a^{\ast})
for all
a\in Q_{1}
. The
preprojective algebra of
Q
, denoted
\Pi
, is defined as the quotient of
\mathbb{C}Q
by the ideal

\left\langle\sum_{\operatorname{h}(a)=i}\epsilon(a)aa^{\ast}\mid{i\in Q_{0}}\right\rangle.

(2.1)

The preprojective algebra
\Pi
does not depend on the choice of the map
\epsilon
. For each
i\in Q_{0}
, we simply write
e_{i}\in\Pi
for the image of the corresponding vertex idempotent.

Second, in the framed McKay quiver
Q
, let
b^{*}\in Q_{1}
be the unique arrow with head at vertex
\infty
. Define a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra as the quotient of the preprojective algebra
\Pi
by the two-sided ideal generated by the class of
b^{*}
:

A\coloneqq\Pi/(b^{*}).

Equivalently, if we define a quiver
Q^{*}
to have vertex set
Q^{*}_{0}=\{\infty,0,1,\dots,r\}
and arrow set
Q_{1}^{*}=Q_{1}\setminus\{b^{*}\}
, then
A
is the quotient of the path algebra
\mathbb{C}Q^{*}
by the ideal of relations

\left\langle\sum_{\operatorname{h}(a)=i}\epsilon(a)aa^{*}\mid 0\leq i,\operatorname{h}(a^{*})\leq r\right\rangle.

Third, let
\mathbb{C}Q_{\Gamma}
denote the path algebra of the (unframed) McKay quiver
Q_{\Gamma}
. Since
\mathbb{C}Q_{\Gamma}
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}Q
, we use the same symbol
e_{i}\in\mathbb{C}Q_{\Gamma}
for the idempotent corresponding to the trivial path at vertex
i
of
Q_{\Gamma}
. The
preprojective algebra of
Q_{\Gamma}
, denoted
B
, is defined as the quotient of
\mathbb{C}Q_{\Gamma}
by an ideal defined similarly to that from (
2.1
) for the quiver
Q_{\Gamma}
.

Remark 2.1
.

For each vertex
0\leq i\leq r
we denote by
e_{i}
the class in
A
of the idempotent at the vertex
i
. Note that
B
is not a subalgebra of
\Pi
. On the other hand,
B
is isomorphic to the subalgebra
\oplus_{0\leq i,j\leq r}e_{j}Ae_{i}
of
A
due to
[
craw2019punctual
, Lemma 3.2]
, and moreover

A\cong B\oplus Bb\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}.

(2.2)

as complex vector spaces. In particular, the classes
e_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq r
are also contained in
B
.

2.2
Morita equivalence

The skew group algebra
S\coloneqq R\ast\Gamma=\mathbb{C}[x,y]\ast\Gamma
of the finite subgroup
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
contains the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
as a subalgebra. For
0\leq i\leq r
, choose an idempotent
f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma
such that
\mathbb{C}\Gamma f_{i}\cong\rho_{i}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There is an isomorphism
Sf_{0}\cong R
of
S\text{-}R^{\Gamma}
-bimodules.

Proof.

As in
[
crawley1998noncommutative
, Lemma 1.1]
,
S
has a basis given by all elements of the form
x^{j}y^{k}g
, where
j,k\geq 0
and
g\in\Gamma
. As
f_{0}
is invariant under multiplication by any
g\in\Gamma
, the subspace
Sf_{0}
has a basis given by elements of the form
x^{j}y^{k}f_{0}
for
j,k\geq 0
. This gives the required isomorphism
of vector spaces which is readily seen to be an isomorphism of
S\text{-}R^{\Gamma}
-bimodules.
∎

To recall the key result, define
f=f_{0}+\dots+f_{r}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The skew group algebra
S
and the preprojective algebra
B
are Morita equivalent via an isomorphism
fSf\cong B
; explicitly, the equivalence on left modules is

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ S\module

\displaystyle\longrightarrow B\module,

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto fM.

Moreover, there are
\mathbb{C}
-algebra isomorphisms

S\cong\operatorname{End}_{R_{0}}\big(R)
and
B\cong\operatorname{End}_{R_{0}}\big({\bigoplus}_{0\leq i\leq r}R_{i}\big)
.

Proof.

The Morita equivalence is a bi-product of the proof of
[
reiten1989two
, Proposition 2.13]
, and may have been first stated explicitly in
[
crawley1998noncommutative
, Theorem 0.1]
. The algebra isomorphism
fSf\cong B
that in particular sends
f_{i}
to
e_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq r
is described in
[
crawley1998noncommutative
, Theorem 3.4]
. The description of
S
as
\operatorname{End}_{R_{0}}(R)
follows from
[
Auslander62
]
, spelled out in

[
LeuschkeWiegand12
, Theorem 5.12]
, while the description of
B
follows by applying the isomorphism
fSf\cong B
; see for example Buchweitz
[
Buchweitz
]
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
0\leq i\leq r
, there is an isomorphism
f_{i}Sf_{i}\cong e_{i}Be_{i}
of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, as well as an isomorphism
R_{i}\cong e_{i}Be_{0}
of
e_{i}Be_{i}\text{-}e_{0}Be_{0}
-bimodules.

Proof.

Apply
f_{i}
to both sides of
fSf
while simultaneously applying
e_{i}
to both sides of
B
; the algebra isomorphism
fSf\cong B
then restricts to the algebra isomorphism
f_{i}Sf_{i}\cong e_{i}Be_{i}
. For the second statement, apply
e_{i}
and
e_{0}
to the left and right respectively of the isomorphism
B\cong\operatorname{End}_{R_{0}}\big({\bigoplus}_{0\leq i\leq r}R_{i}\big)
to obtain
R_{i}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R_{0}}(R_{0},R_{i})\cong e_{i}Be_{0}
as required.
∎

We finally look at how the Morita equivalence
\Phi
acts on dimension vectors.

Corollary 2.5
.

Given an
S
-module of the form
R/J
, with dimension vector
\sum_{0\leq i\leq r}v_{i}\rho_{i}
, its image under
\Phi
is a
B
-module of the form
Be_{0}/fJ
with dimension vector
(v_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
shows that
\Phi
sends the polynomial ring
R\cong Sf_{0}
to
fSf_{0}=(fSf)f_{0}=Be_{0}
. It follows that for any

S
-submodule

J\subseteq R
, the quotient
R/J
is an
S
-module whose image under
\Phi
is
Be_{0}/fJ
. Finally,
\Phi
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear isomorphism between the Grothendieck groups of the categories of finite-dimensional left modules over
S
and
B
which we identify with the representation ring
\operatorname{Rep}(\Gamma)
and with
\mathbb{Z}^{r+1}
respectively.
∎

3
Orbifold Quot schemes

3.1
Quot schemes for modules over associative algebras

Let
H
denote an arbitrary finitely generated,
not necessarily commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, and let
M
be a finitely
generated left
H
-module.
For a scheme
X
, we denote by
H_{X}
the sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras

H\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
, where
H
is considered as a constant sheaf on
X
. Similarly, denote by
M_{X}
the sheaf of
H_{X}
-modules

M\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Consider the functor

\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H}(M)\colon\ \mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sets}

sending a scheme
X
to the set of isomorphism classes of left
H_{X}
-modules
Z
equipped with a surjective
H_{X}
-module homomorphism

M_{X}\to Z
such that
Z
,
when considered as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, is locally free of rank
n
.

The following result may be known to experts, though we could not find it in the literature.

Proposition 3.1
.

The functor
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H}(M)
is represented by a scheme
\Quot^{n}_{H}(M)

of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

As
H
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, we can fix a surjection
H^{+}\to H
with
H^{+}
a free noncommutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra on a finite number of generators.
Then
M
is also a left
H^{+}
-module. Define
J\coloneqq\ker\big(H^{+}\to H\big)
. Consider a left
H^{+}
-module
Z
that is a quotient of
M
via a surjective
H^{+}
-module morphism
M\to Z
.

Since any element of the two-sided ideal
J
of
H^{+}
acts trivially on
M
, and so also on quotients of
M
,
Z
is automatically an
H
-module. Conversely, any quotient of
M
as an
H
-module is automatically an
H^{+}
-module. It follows that there is a canonical isomorphism of functors
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H}(M)=\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H^{+}}(M)
.

Thus, we may assume that
H
is a finitely generated and free
\mathbb{C}
-algebra.
Fix a left
H
-module surjection
\psi\colon H^{r}\to M
for some
r
.
Write elements of
\ker\psi
in the form
\sum_{1\leq k\leq r}w_{k}e_{k}
, where the
e_{k}\in H^{r}
are the standard module generators and
w_{k}\in H
.
For any quotient module
q\colon M\to Z
, composition with
\psi
presents
Z
as a quotient
\tilde{q}\colon H^{r}\to Z
,
such that
\ker\psi\subseteq\ker\tilde{q}
.
The equations
\sum_{1\leq k\leq r}w_{k}\tilde{q}(e_{k})=0
give
a closed condition on
[\tilde{q}]\in\Quot^{n}_{H}(H^{r})
: the vanishing of a collection of vectors in the vector space underlying
Z
.
When trivialising the universal sheaf on some open cover of
Q^{n}_{H}(H^{r})
, these closed conditions glue together, and
we see that this construction realises

\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H}(H^{r})

as a closed subfunctor.

To conclude, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{H}(H^{r})
is representable for a free noncommutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
H
. This follows from
[
beentjes2018virtual
, Theorem 2.5]
; while that paper discusses the case when
H
is freely generated by
3
elements, the proof generalises to finitely many generators.
∎

3.2
Quot schemes for Kleinian orbifolds

Let
B
be the preprojective algebra of the unframed McKay quiver and
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]
as
in Section
2.1
.
Recall that
R_{0}=R^{\Gamma}
, and that for each
0\leq i\leq r
, the

R_{0}
-module
R_{i}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{\Gamma}(\rho_{i},R)
satisfies
R_{i}\cong e_{i}Be_{0}
.

For any subset
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
,
consider the idempotent

e_{I}\coloneqq\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}
in
B
,
and define the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

B_{I}\coloneqq e_{I}Be_{I}

(3.1)

comprising linear combinations of
classes of paths in
Q_{\Gamma}
whose tails and heads lie in the set
I
. The process of passing
from
B
to
B_{I}
is called
cornering
; see
[
craw2018multigraded
, Remark 3.1]
.
Then

R_{I}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in I}R_{i}\cong e_{I}Be_{0}

is naturally a finitely generated left
B_{I}
-module.

For a given dimension vector
n_{I}=(n_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
,
consider the contravariant functor

\mathcal{Q}^{n_{I}}_{B_{I}}(R_{I})\colon\ \mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sets}

sending a scheme
X
to the set of isomorphism classes of left
B_{I,X}
-modules
Z
equipped with a surjective
B_{I,X}
-module homomorphism

\phi_{Z}\colon\ R_{I}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\to Z

such that each submodule
e_{i}Z
, for
i\in I
,
when considered as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, is locally free of rank
n_{i}
. Note that we can recover
Z
from these submodules via

Z=\bigoplus_{i\in I}e_{i}Z.

The next result provides the link between this functor and that introduced in Section
3.1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There is a finite decomposition

\mathcal{Q}^{n}_{B_{I}}(R_{I})=\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{I}\\
\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}=n\end{subarray}}\mathcal{Q}^{n_{I}}_{B_{I}}(R_{I})

(3.2)

into open and closed subfunctors.
In particular, each functor

\mathcal{Q}^{n_{I}}_{B_{I}}\big(R_{I}\big)
is represented by a scheme
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

The dimension vector
n_{I}=(n_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
is locally constant in a flat family of
B_{I}
-modules of fixed rank
n
, with each of the entries being lower semicontinuous by the Schur lemma.
So we have a decomposition (
3.2
) into open and closed subfunctors.
As
R_{I}
is finitely generated as a left
B_{I}
-module, the functor on the left hand side of (
3.2
) is represented by a scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
by Proposition
3.1
. The last claim then follows.
∎

When the index set is a singleton
I=\{i\}
, we
often simply write
\Quot_{i}^{n_{i}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
for
\Quot_{\{i\}}^{n_{\{i\}}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
. See Section
3.3
for a
discussion of this special case.

As is common with Hilbert schemes in other contexts, we
consider collections of our Quot schemes for all
possible dimension vectors. Define the
orbifold Quot scheme for
\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]
and for the index set
I
to be

\Quot_{I}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\coloneqq\coprod_{n_{I}}\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)

where
n_{I}=(n_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
as before.
Again, when
I=\{i\}
is a singleton, we simply write

\Quot_{i}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\coloneqq\coprod_{n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}}\Quot_{i}^{n_{i}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big).

Denote by
\Hilb\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
the Hilbert scheme of
\Gamma
-invariant finite colength subschemes of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. As explained in more detail in
[
gyenge2018euler
, Section 1.1]
, this space decomposes as a disjoint union of quasi-projective varieties

\Hilb\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)=\coprod_{v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}}\Hilb^{v}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big),

where

\Hilb^{v}\big([\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma]\big)=\Big\{J\in\Hilb\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\mid H^{0}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}/J\big)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\{0,\dots,r\}}\rho_{i}^{\oplus v_{i}}\Big\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
I=\{0,\dots,r\}
, there is an isomorphism

\Quot_{I}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\cong\Hilb\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)

which respects the decompositions on both sides into pieces indexed by
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
.

Proof.

This follows from the Morita equivalence between the algebras
S

and
B
from Proposition
2.3
, and
its amplification Corollary
2.5
, together with the fact that a finite colength left
S
-submodule of
R=\mathbb{C}[x,y]
is nothing but a finite colength
\Gamma
-invariant ideal of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
.
∎

3.3
Quot schemes for Kleinian singularities

In this section, we discuss the relation of the construction from Section
3.2
to the more classical (commutative) version of the Quot scheme, in the case when
I=\{i\}
is a singleton.
Note that in this case,
R_{i}\cong e_{i}Be_{0}

is both a left
B_{i}
-module and a right
R_{0}\cong e_{0}Be_{0}
-module.

Let
\Quot_{i}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)
be the scheme parameterising finite codimension
R^{\Gamma}
-submodules of
R_{i}

or, equivalently, finite colength subsheaves of the coherent sheaf on the Kleinian singularity
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
corresponding to
R_{i}
.
Again, this space decomposes as

\Quot_{i}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)=\coprod_{n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}}\Quot_{i}^{n_{i}}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)

with
\Quot_{i}^{n_{i}}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)=\big\{M\in\Quot_{i}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)\mid\mathrm{dim}\,R_{i}/M=n_{i}\big\}
.

Suppose that
i\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
satisfies
\dim\rho_{i}=1
. Then there is a diagram automorphism of
Q_{\Gamma}
taking vertex
i
to vertex
0
. Pick any such
automorphism
\iota
and let

\iota(0)\in\{0,\dots,r\}
denote the vertex to which the
vertex
0
is mapped under this diagram automorphism. This transformation of the diagram induces an algebra isomorphism

B_{i}=e_{i}Be_{i}\cong e_{0}Be_{0}=B_{0}.

Also, it induces a bijection between left
B_{i}
-submodules of
e_{i}Be_{0}
and left
B_{0}
-submodules of
e_{0}Be_{\iota(0)}
. Reversing the arrows gives an algebra automorphism
B_{0}\xrightarrow{\sim}B_{0}
as well as a bijective correspondence between left
B_{0}
-submodules of
e_{0}Be_{\iota(0)}
and right
B_{0}
-submodules of
e_{\iota(0)}Be_{0}
. Putting these two correspondences together, we get a bijection between the left
B_{i}
-submodules of
R_{i}=e_{i}Be_{0}
and right
B_{0}
-submodules of
R_{\iota(0)}=e_{\iota(0)}Be_{0}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
I=\{i\}
is a singleton such
that
\dim\rho_{i}=1
. Let
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}

be a dimension vector for the index set
\{i\}
, and denote
by
n_{\iota(0)}
the same dimension vector for the
index set
\{\iota(0)\}
with
\iota(0)

as above. Then
\Quot_{i}^{n_{i}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
is isomorphic
to
\Quot_{\iota(0)}^{n_{\iota(0)}}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)
.

Proof.

The above correspondence between left
B_{i}
-submodules of
e_{i}Be_{0}

and right
B_{0}
-submodules of
e_{\iota(0)}Be_{0}
is functorial
and preserves the colength. By definition, the scheme

\Quot_{\iota(0)}^{n_{\iota(0)}}\!\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)
represents the functor
that sends a scheme
X
to the set of isomorphism classes of
right
B_{0,X}
-module quotients
R_{\iota(0),X}\to Z
which are
locally free of rank
n_{\iota(0)}
over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The assumption of Proposition
3.4
is satisfied for type
A_{r}
and any
0\leq i\leq r
, type
D_{r}
and
i\in\{0,1,r-1,r\}
,
E_{6}
and
i\in\{0,1,6\}
,
E_{7}
and
i\in\{0,7\}
and for
E_{8}
and
i=0
. Here we have used the Bourbaki convention
[
BourbakiLie
]
for enumerating the vertices.

Example 3.6
.

For
i=0
, the assumption of Lemma
3.4
is satisfied in all types, with
\iota(0)=0
also. Writing
n_{0}=n
, we obtain isomorphisms

\Quot_{0}^{n_{0}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\cong\Quot_{0}^{n_{0}}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big)\cong\Hilb^{n}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big).

4
Fine moduli spaces of modules

4.1
Cornering and fine moduli spaces

Let
\operatorname{Rep}(\Gamma)

denote the representation ring of
\Gamma
. Introduce a formal symbol
\rho_{\infty}
so that
\{\rho_{i}\mid i\in Q_{0}\}
provides a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\operatorname{Rep}(\Gamma)
considered as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.

Recall that
A=\Pi/(b^{\ast})
is the quotient of the
preprojective algebra of the framed McKay quiver of
\Gamma
by the two sided ideal
(b^{\ast})
, where
b^{\ast}
is the arrow from
0
to
\infty
.
Let
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
be a non-empty subset; we work under the assumption that
I\neq\varnothing
for the rest of the paper.
Define the idempotent
\overline{e}_{I}\coloneqq e_{\infty}+\sum_{i\in I}e_{i}\in A
.
The subalgebra

A_{I}\coloneqq\overline{e}_{I}A\overline{e}_{I}

(4.1)

of
A
comprises linear combinations of
classes of paths in
Q
whose tails and heads lie in the set
\{\infty\}\cup I
; this is also an instance of
cornering

[
craw2018multigraded
, Remark 3.1]
.

Let

n_{I}\coloneqq{(n_{i})_{i\in I}}=\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
.
Then
(1,n_{I})\coloneqq\rho_{\infty}+n_{I}\in\mathbb{N}\oplus\mathbb{N}^{I}

is a dimension vector for
A_{I}
-modules, and we consider the stability condition
\eta_{I}\colon\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{I}\to\mathbb{Q}

given by

\eta_{I}(\rho_{i})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\sum_{j\in I}n_{j}&\textrm{for }i=\infty,\\
1&\textrm{if }i\in I.\end{cases}

(4.2)

It follows directly from the definition that an
A_{I}
-module
N
of dimension vector
(1,n_{I})
is
\eta_{I}
-stable if and only if
there exists a surjective
A_{I}
-module homomorphism
A_{I}e_{\infty}\to N
.
The quiver moduli space construction of King
[
king1994moduli
, Proposition 5.3]
for finite dimensional algebras can be adapted to any algebra presented as the quotient of a finite, connected quiver by an ideal of relations. We established in
[
craw2019punctual
, Proposition 3.3]
that the algebra
A_{I}
has this property.
The vector
(1,n_{I})
is indivisible and
\eta_{I}
is a generic stability condition for
A_{I}
-modules of dimension vector
(1,n_{I})
, so there is a fine moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})

of
\eta_{I}
-stable
A_{I}
-modules of dimension
vector
(1,n_{I})
. Let
T_{I}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in\{\infty\}\cup I}T_{i}
denote the tautological vector bundle on
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
, where
T_{\infty}
is the trivial bundle and
T_{i}
has rank
n_{i}
for
i\in I
.
After multiplying
\eta_{I}
by a positive integer
m\geq 1
if necessary, we may assume that the polarising ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{I}\coloneqq\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathrm{det}(T_{i})^{m}
on
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
given by the GIT construction is very ample.

4.2
The Quot scheme as a fine moduli space of modules

We now establish the link between the fine moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
and the orbifold Quot scheme
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
.

For any non-empty subset
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
, consider the algebras
A_{I}
and
B_{I}
from (
4.1
) and (
3.1
) respectively.
In
[
craw2019punctual
, Proposition 3.3]
, we used the isomorphism
B_{I}\cong\operatorname{End}_{R_{0}}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R_{i}\big)
to introduce quivers
Q_{I}
and
Q_{I}^{*}
and a commutative diagram of
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphisms

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}Q_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}Q_{I}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{I},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{I}}

where the vertical maps are surjective and the horizontal maps are injective. In particular, we obtain quiver presentations
B_{I}\cong\mathbb{C}Q_{I}/\ker(\alpha_{I})
and
A_{I}\cong\mathbb{C}Q_{I}^{*}/\ker(\beta_{I})
. The vertex set of
Q^{*}_{I}
is
\{\infty\}\cup I
, while the
edge set
comprises three kinds of arrows: loops at each vertex
i\in I
; arrows between pairs of
distinct vertices in
I
; and
arrows
from vertex
\infty
to vertices in
I
.
Note that
Q_{I}
is the complete
subquiver of
Q_{I}^{*}
on the vertex set
I
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Equip the vector space
B_{I}\oplus R_{I}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}
with the following multiplication:

(b_{1},r_{1},c_{1}e_{\infty})\cdot(b_{2},r_{2},c_{2}e_{\infty})\coloneqq(b_{1}b_{2},b_{1}r_{2}+r_{1}c_{2},c_{1}c_{2}e_{\infty}).

Then there is an isomorphism

A_{I}\cong B_{I}\oplus R_{I}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}

of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, where
R_{I}=e_{I}Be_{0}
is also a left
B_{I}
-module.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.2
) that there is an isomorphism of vector spaces

A_{I}\cong(e_{I}+e_{\infty})(B\oplus Bb\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty})(e_{I}+e_{\infty})\cong B_{I}\oplus e_{I}Bb\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}.

For the middle summand, consider the map of left
B_{I}
-modules

R_{I}=e_{I}Be_{0}\longrightarrow e_{I}Bb

(4.3)

defined on representing paths in the framed McKay quiver
Q
by composing with the arrow
b
on the right. This map is by definition surjective. But it is also injective, as every relation in
A
involving the arrow
b
can be factored into the product of
b
and a relation in
B
. This establishes the isomorphism

A_{I}\cong B_{I}\oplus R_{I}\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}

of vector spaces. To enhance this to an isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras,
note that the algebra structure on
A_{I}\cong B_{I}\oplus e_{I}Bb\oplus\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}
is given by

(b_{1},r_{1}b,c_{1}e_{\infty})\cdot(b_{2},r_{2}b,c_{2}e_{\infty})=\big(b_{1}b_{2},b_{1}r_{2}b+r_{1}bc_{2}e_{\infty},c_{1}c_{2}e_{\infty}\big).

Since
c_{2}
is a scalar, it commutes with
b=be_{\infty}
, giving
b_{1}r_{2}b+r_{1}bc_{2}e_{\infty}=(b_{1}r_{2}+r_{1}c_{2})b
in the middle term above. The isomorphism (
4.3
) allows us to drop the
b
from the right-hand side, leaving the second statement as required.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For any non-empty subset
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
and
n_{I}\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
, there is an isomorphism of schemes

\omega_{n_{I}}\colon\ \Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I}).

In particular,
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
is non-empty if and only if
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
is non-empty.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}
the tautological bundle on
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
, a family of quotient
B_{I}
-modules of
R_{I}
of rank
\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}
be the trivial bundle on
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
.

We give
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{T}
the structure of a family of
A_{I}
-modules using the decomposition from Lemma
4.1
.
Indeed, the
B_{I}
-module structure on
\mathcal{T}
extends to a
B_{I}
-module structure on
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{T}
by acting by
0
on the first summand. The summand
\mathbb{C}e_{\infty}
acts by scaling on
\mathcal{O}
and trivially on
\mathcal{T}
. Finally, as the bundle
\mathcal{T}
is a quotient of the trivial family
R_{I}
, any element
r
of the middle summand
R_{I}\subset A_{I}
determines an
\mathcal{O}
-module homomorphism
\phi_{r}\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{T})
via the correspondence
1\mapsto[r]
. As
R_{I}
is a left
B_{I}
-module, the
\mathbb{C}
-linear correspondence
r\mapsto\phi_{r}
is left
B_{I}
-linear. The action of two elements
(b_{1},r_{1},c_{1}e_{\infty}),(b_{2},r_{2},c_{2}e_{\infty})\in A_{I}
on
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{T}
therefore composes as

\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}&0\\
\phi_{r_{1}}&b_{1}\end{pmatrix}\circ\begin{pmatrix}c_{2}&0\\
\phi_{r_{2}}&b_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}c_{2}&0\\
\phi_{r_{1}}c_{2}+b_{1}\phi_{r_{2}}&b_{1}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}c_{2}&0\\
\phi_{r_{1}c_{2}+b_{1}r_{2}}&b_{1}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma
4.1

shows that
the bundle
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{T}
on
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)

is a flat family of
A_{I}
-modules
of dimension vector
(1,n_{I})
. Over any closed point, the resulting

A_{I}
-module is generated at
\infty
, and so it is
\eta_{I}
-stable.
The universal property of the fine moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})

induces the morphism of schemes
\omega_{n_{I}}
.

Conversely, applying Lemma
4.1

in the opposite direction gives that to any
\eta_{I}
-stable
A_{I}
-module of dimension vector
(1,n_{I})
, there corresponds a cyclic
B_{I}
-module of dimension vector
n_{I}
. By applying this to the family given by the tautological bundle
T_{I}
on
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
, we see that the universal property of the fine moduli space
\Quot_{I}^{n_{I}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)

induces the inverse of the morphism
\omega_{n_{I}}
.
∎

5
Quiver varieties for the framed McKay quiver

5.1
Variation of GIT quotient for quiver varieties

Let
v\coloneqq(v_{i})_{0\leq i\leq r}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a dimension vector for the McKay quiver
Q_{\Gamma}
.
Recall that we identify
\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\cong\mathbb{Z}\rho_{\infty}\oplus\operatorname{Rep}(\Gamma)
, so we obtain the
dimension vector

(1,v)\coloneqq\rho_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{r}v_{i}\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}

for the framed McKay quiver
Q
. Write

\Theta_{v}=\big\{\theta\colon\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\to\mathbb{Q}\;:\;\theta\big((1,v)\big)=0\big\}

for the space of stability conditions for the
the framed quiver. Following Nakajima
[
nakajima1994instantons
,
Nakajima98
]
, for every
\theta\in\Theta_{v}
, the quiver variety
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
is the coarse moduli space of S-equivalence classes of
\theta
-semistable
\Pi
-modules of dimension vector
(1,v)
. The GIT construction of
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
is summarised for example in
[
craw2019punctual
, Section 2]
; see also
[
nakajima2009quiver
, Section 2]
. Note in particular that we give
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
the reduced scheme structure.

The set of stability conditions
\Theta_{v}
admits a preorder
\geq
, and this determines a wall-and-chamber structure, where

\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{v}
lie in the relative interior of the same cone if and only if both
\theta\geq\theta^{\prime}
and
\theta^{\prime}\geq\theta
hold in this preorder, in which case
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)\cong\mathfrak{M}_{\theta^{\prime}}(1,v)
. The interiors of the top-dimensional cones in
\Theta_{v}
are chambers, while the codimension-one faces of the closure of each chamber are walls. We say that
\theta\in\Theta_{v}
is generic with respect to
v
, if it lies in some GIT chamber. The vector
(1,v)
is indivisible, so again,
[
king1994moduli
, Proposition 5.3]
implies that for generic
\theta\in\Theta_{v}
the quiver variety
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
is the fine moduli space of isomorphism classes of
\theta
-stable
\Pi
-modules of dimension vector
(1,v)
. In this case, the universal family on
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
is a tautological locally-free sheaf
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\oplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathcal{R}_{i}
together with a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi\colon\Pi\to\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{R})
, where
R_{\infty}
is the trivial bundle on
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
and where
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{R}_{i})=v_{j}
for
i\geq 0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

1.

For all
\theta\in\Theta_{v}
, the scheme
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
is irreducible and normal, with symplectic singularities.

2.

Let
\theta\geq\theta^{\prime}
. The morphism
\pi\colon\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta^{\prime}}(1,v)
obtained by variation of GIT quotient is a projective morphism of varieties over the affine quotient
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(1,v)
.

Proof.

Part (1) is
[
bellamy2016symplectic
, Theorem 1.2, Proposition 3.21]
while part (2) is well-known.
∎

5.2
Wall-and-chamber structure

Define
\delta\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq i\leq r}\dim(\rho_{i})\rho_{i}\in\operatorname{Rep}(\Gamma)
. For the dimension vector
v\coloneqq n\delta
, the GIT wall-and-chamber structure on
\Theta_{n\delta}
is computed explicitly in
[
bellamy2020birational
, Theorem 4.6]
. More generally, for arbitrary
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
,
it is possible to compute the wall-and-chamber structure on
\Theta_{v}
by applying recent work of
Dehority
[
dehority2020affinizations
, Proposition 4.8]
. We do not need the wall-and-chamber structure here, but we do use the following distinguished region of the space of stability conditions:

C^{+}_{v}\coloneqq\{\theta\in\Theta_{v}\mid\theta(\rho_{i})>0\textrm{ for }0\leq i\leq r\}.

This open cone need not be a GIT chamber, though it is always contained in one (and it is a chamber in the special case
v=n\delta
, see
[
bellamy2020birational
, Example 2.1]
or
[
nakajima2009quiver
, Section 2.8]
).
The next statement, which appeared originally in
[
nakajima2002geometric
, Section 2]
, illustrates the importance of
C^{+}_{v}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
and
\theta\in C^{+}_{v}
, there is an isomorphism

\Hilb^{v}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\cong\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v).

Proof.

In the special case
v=n\delta
,
the statement is proved in
[
kuznetsov2007quiver
, Theorem 4.6]
. However, the argument given there applies for
an arbitrary dimension vector
v
; note in particular that the auxiliary
[
kuznetsov2007quiver
, Corollary 3.5]

does not need any assumption on the dimension vector (and we have that
\Hilb^{v}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)
is non-empty if and only if
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
is non-empty).
∎

Every face of the closed cone
\overline{C^{+}_{v}}
is of the form

\sigma_{I}\coloneqq\big\{\theta\in\overline{C^{+}_{v}}\mid\theta(\rho_{i})>0\textrm{ if and only if }i\in I,\textrm{ and }\theta(\rho_{i})=0\textrm{ otherwise}\big\}

for some non-empty subset
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
. When
v=n\delta
, these faces are contained in walls of the wall-and-chamber structure on
\Theta_{n\delta}
, though this need not be the case for arbitrary
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
.
In any case, the parameter

\theta_{I}(\rho_{j})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}&\textrm{for }j=\infty,\\
1&\textrm{if }j\in I,\\
0&\textrm{if }j\in\{0,1,\dots,r\}\setminus I\end{cases}

(5.1)

lies in the relative interior of the face
\sigma_{I}
.

5.3
Resolution of singularities

Let
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a dimension vector and
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
. As mentioned above, the quiver variety
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is singular in general for
\theta_{I}
as defined in (
5.1
) above. Moreover, for
\theta\in C_{v}^{+}
,
the morphism

\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)

obtained by variation of GIT need not be birational.
It is nevertheless possible to find a resolution of
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
using a
quiver variety if one modifies the dimension vector
v
:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
and let
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
be non-empty. Assume further that

\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is non-empty.
There exists a dimension vector
\widetilde{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
satisfying
\widetilde{v}_{i}\leq v_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq r
and
\widetilde{v}_{i}=v_{i}
for
i\in I
,
such that there is a projective resolution of singularities

\pi_{I}\colon\ \mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v),

where the stability condition
\widetilde{\theta}
satisfies
\widetilde{\theta}\in C_{\widetilde{v}}^{+}
.

Proof.

Following Nakajima
[
nakajima1994instantons
, Section 6]
(cf.
[
bellamy2020birational
, Section 3.5]
),
there is a finite stratification

\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)=\coprod_{\gamma}\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}

by smooth locally-closed subvarieties, where the union is over conjugacy classes of subgroups of the reductive group applied during the GIT construction of
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
, and where the stratum
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}
consists of
S
-equivalence classes of semistable modules with polystable representative having stabiliser in the conjugacy class
\gamma
. Since
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is irreducible by Lemma
5.1
, there is a unique dense stratum and we fix a conjugacy class
\gamma

such that the dense open stratum in
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}
. Apply
[
nakajima2009quiver
, Proposition 2.25]
to obtain a dimension vector
\widetilde{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
as in the statement of the lemma
together with an isomorphism

\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}\cong\mathfrak{M}^{s}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})\times{Y},

(5.2)

where
\mathfrak{M}^{s}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})
is the fine moduli space of
\theta_{I}
-stable
\Pi
-modules of dimension vector
(1,\widetilde{v})
, and
{Y}\subset\mathfrak{M}_{0}(0,v-\widetilde{v})
is a locally-closed subvariety.
Note that
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}
is non-empty by assumption, hence so are
\mathfrak{M}^{s}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})
and
{Y}
.

We claim that
{Y}
is a closed point. Indeed,
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(0,v-\widetilde{v})
parametrises
0-polystable representations, i.e., direct sums of simple representations. The framing is 0-dimensional,
and since
I
is non-empty, there is at least one
i
such that
v_{i}=\widetilde{v}_{i}
. It follows that the representations parameterised by
{Y}

are supported on a doubled quiver obtained from an affine ADE diagram with at least one vertex removed. Removing a vertex from an affine ADE diagram leaves a graph in which every connected component is a subgraph of an finite type ADE diagram. Therefore
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(0,v-\widetilde{v})
parametrises direct sums of simple representations of preprojective algebras of doubled quivers associated to finite type ADE diagrams. A simple representation of any such quiver is one-dimensional
[
savage2011quiver
, Lemma 2.2]
, so the only polystable representation of dimension vector
(0,v-\widetilde{v})
is
necessarily of the form

\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq r}S_{i}^{\oplus(v_{i}-\tilde{v}_{i})},

(5.3)

where each
S_{i}
is a vertex simple
\Pi
-module. Therefore
\mathfrak{M}_{0}(0,v-\widetilde{v})
is a closed point, and hence so too is
{Y}
because
{Y}\neq\varnothing
. Thus
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}\cong\mathfrak{M}^{s}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})
.

For

\widetilde{\theta}\in C_{\widetilde{v}}^{+}
, we claim that the desired morphism
\pi_{I}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is induced by sending each
\tilde{\theta}
-stable
\Pi
-module
V
of dimension vector
(1,\tilde{v})
to

\pi_{I}(V):=V\oplus\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq r}S_{i}^{\oplus(v_{i}-\tilde{v}_{i})}.

Indeed, if
\tilde{\mu}
and
\mu
denote the moment maps for the actions of
G_{\tilde{v}}:=\prod_{0\leq i\leq r}\operatorname{GL}(\tilde{v}_{i})
and
G_{v}:=\prod_{0\leq i\leq r}\operatorname{GL}(v_{i})
on the representation spaces of
\Pi
-modules of dimension vectors
(1,\tilde{v})
and
(1,v)
respectively, then the above assignment induces an inclusion
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(0)\hookrightarrow\mu^{-1}(0)
that is equivariant with respect to the actions of
G_{\tilde{v}}
and
G_{v}
on
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(0)
and
\mu^{-1}(0)
respectively. Any submodule of
\pi_{I}(V)
is
W\oplus W^{\prime}
for submodules
W\subseteq V
and
W^{\prime}\subseteq\bigoplus_{0\leq i\leq r}S_{i}^{\oplus(v_{i}-\tilde{v}_{i})}
, and we have
\theta_{I}(W\oplus W^{\prime})=\theta_{I}(W)\geq 0
. Thus, the image of the
\tilde{\theta}
-stable locus of
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(0)
lies in the
\theta_{I}
-semistable locus of
\mu^{-1}(0)
, and this inclusion induces the morphism
\pi_{I}
as claimed.

It remains to establish the properties of
\pi_{I}
. Adapting the proof from
[
bellamy2016symplectic
, Lemma 2.4]
shows that
\pi_{I}
is a projective morphism, while
[
bellamy2016symplectic
, Theorem 1.15]
gives that
\mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})
is non-singular as
\widetilde{\theta}
is generic. Our explicit description of
\pi_{I}
shows that it factors via the morphism

\mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})

obtained by variation of GIT quotient. This morphism is birational by
[
nakajima2009quiver
, Lemma 2.12]
. To see that the induced morphism
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is also birational, notice that its restriction to the (open dense) stable locus
\mathfrak{M}^{s}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})
simply adds the summand (
5.3
) to each representative
\Pi
-module, so it agrees with the isomorphism from (
5.2
) above. Thus, the image of
\mathfrak{M}^{s}_{\theta_{I}}(1,\widetilde{v})
under this induced morphism is
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)_{\gamma}
which is dense in
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
by our choice of
\gamma
. This completes the proof.
∎

Recall that
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is a coarse moduli space of
\theta_{I}
-polystable modules up to
isomorphism
[
nakajima2009quiver
, Proposition 2.9(2)]
, and that every
\theta_{I}
-polystable module has a unique summand that has dimension 1 at the vertex
\infty
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
M
be a
\theta_{I}
-polystable representative for an arbitrary class
[M]\in\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
, and let
M_{\infty}
be the unique summand that has dimension 1 at the vertex
\infty
.
Then

\dim_{i}\,M_{\infty}\leq\widetilde{v}_{i}

for
0\leq i\leq r
where
\widetilde{v}
is as in Proposition
5.3
.

Proof.

The morphism
\pi_{I}
from Proposition
5.3
is surjective, because quiver varieties are irreducible and
\pi_{I}
is both proper and birational. Therefore
[M]=[\pi_{I}(V)]
for some
\tilde{\theta}
-stable
\Pi
-module
V
of dimension vector
(1,\tilde{v})
. Each vertex simple
S_{i}
arising as a summand of
\pi_{I}(V)
has dimension 0 at the vertex
\infty
, so we must have
M_{\infty}\subseteq{V}
. This implies the lemma.
∎

Suppose now that
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is non-empty, and apply Proposition
5.3
to obtain
\tilde{v}\leq v
and a resolution of singularities
\pi_{I}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})\to\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
for
\theta\in C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}
. For any
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{{\tilde{v}}}
, there is a line bundle
L_{C^{+}_{{\tilde{v}}}}(\theta^{\prime})\coloneqq\bigotimes_{0\leq i\leq r}\det(\mathcal{R}_{i})^{\theta^{\prime}_{i}}
on
\mathfrak{M}_{\tilde{\theta}}(1,{\tilde{v}})
. The line bundle
L_{I}\coloneqq L_{C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}}(\theta_{I})
plays an important role in what follows.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\theta\in C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}
, Then

1)

for each
\theta^{\prime}\in\overline{C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}}
the line bundle
L_{C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}}(\theta^{\prime})
on
\mathfrak{M}_{\tilde{\theta}}(1,{\tilde{v}})
is globally generated;

2)

for any
I\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
, after multiplying
\theta_{I}
by a positive integer if necessary, the morphism to the linear series of
L_{I}
decomposes as the composition of the
morphism
\pi_{I}
from Proposition
5.3
and a closed immersion:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{\tilde{\theta}}(1,\tilde{v})}
L_I—. ;
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{|L_{I}|}}

Proof.

Since
\theta\in C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}
, the tautological bundles
\mathcal{R}_{i}
on the quiver variety
\mathfrak{M}_{\tilde{\theta}}(1,{\tilde{v}})
are globally generated for
i\in{Q_{0}}
by
[
craw2018multigraded
, Corollary 2.4]
. Therefore,
L_{C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}}(\theta^{\prime})
is globally generated as
\theta_{i}^{\prime}\geq 0
for all
0\leq i\leq r
. In particular, since
\theta_{I}\in\overline{C^{+}_{\tilde{v}}}
, the rational map
\varphi_{|L_{I}|}
is defined everywhere.

For (2), after taking a positive multiple of
\theta_{I}
if necessary, we may assume that the polarising ample
line bundle
L^{\prime}_{I}
on
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is very ample. Our explicit construction of
\pi_{I}
from the proof of Proposition
5.3
implies that the
G_{v}
-equivariant line bundle on
\mu^{-1}(0)
determined by the character associated to
\theta_{I}
restricts under the inclusion
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(0)\hookrightarrow\mu^{-1}(0)
to the
G_{\tilde{v}}
-equivariant line bundle on
\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(0)
determined by the character associated to
\theta_{I}
. Thus, after descent,
we obtain
\pi_{I}^{*}(L^{\prime}_{I})=L_{I}
as required.

If
\varphi_{|L^{\prime}_{I}|}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)\to|L^{\prime}_{I}|
is the closed immersion, then

(\varphi_{|L^{\prime}_{I}|}\circ\pi_{I})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{|L^{\prime}_{I}|}(1))=\pi_{I}^{*}(L^{\prime}_{I})=L_{I}=\varphi^{*}_{|L_{I}|}\big(\mathcal{O}_{|L_{I}|}(1)\big)

on
\mathfrak{M}_{\tilde{\theta}}(1,{\tilde{v}})
. The morphism to a complete linear series of a specific line bundle is unique up to an automorphism of the linear series
[
hartshorne1977algebraic
, Chapter II, Theorem 7.1]
. Thus, after a change of basis on
H^{0}(L^{\prime}_{I})
if necessary, we have
\varphi_{|L_{I}|}=\varphi_{|L^{\prime}_{I}|}\circ\pi_{I}
as required.
∎

6
Quiver varieties and moduli spaces for cornered algebras

6.1
The key commutative diagram

Once and for all, fix a non-empty
I\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,r\}

and a dimension vector
n_{I}=(n_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{N}^{I}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{0\leq j\leq r}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be any vector satisfying
v_{i}=n_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is non-empty, and define
\widetilde{v}
as in Proposition
5.3
. Then
\widetilde{v}
satisfies
\widetilde{v}_{i}=n_{i}
for
i\in I
, and there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{I}}

of schemes over
\mathbb{C}
for any
\widetilde{\theta}\in C^{+}_{\widetilde{v}}
,
where
\pi_{I}
is surjective and where
\iota_{I}
is a closed immersion. In particular, the fine moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
is non-empty.

Proof.

The fact that
\widetilde{v}_{i}=n_{i}
for all
i\in I
is immediate from Proposition
5.3
. We now construct a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{\widetilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})}
L_I—;
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{|L_{I}|}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{I}}

of schemes over
\mathbb{C}
, where both morphisms drawn diagonally in the bottom half of the diagram are closed immersions.
Commutativity of the triangle on the left is given by Lemma
5.5
, while surjectivity of
\pi_{I}

is established in the proof of Lemma
5.4
.

The isomorphism
\Hilb^{\widetilde{v}}\big(\big[\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma\big]\big)\cong\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,\widetilde{v})
from Proposition
5.2
allows us to apply the proof of
[
craw2019punctual
, Lemma 3.1]
, using
\widetilde{v}
in place of
n\delta
throughout, to conclude that

\mathfrak{M}_{\tilde{\theta}}(1,\widetilde{v})
may be regarded as a fine moduli space of
A
-modules. Applying the proof of
[
craw2019punctual
, Lemma 4.1]
, with
\widetilde{v}_{i}
replacing
n\mathrm{dim}\,\rho_{i}
for all
i\in I
, gives the universal morphism
\tau_{I}
.

For commutativity of the right-hand triangle, recall that the polarising line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{I}
on
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
is very ample. As pullback commutes with tensor operations on the
T_{i}
, the isomorphisms
\tau_{I}^{*}(T_{i})\cong\mathcal{R}_{i}
for
i\in\{\infty\}\cup I
from

ibid.
imply that
L_{I}=\tau_{I}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{I})
. If
\varphi_{|\mathcal{L}_{I}|}\colon\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})\to|\mathcal{L}_{I}|
is the closed immersion for
\mathcal{L}_{I}
, then

(\varphi_{|\mathcal{L}_{I}|}\circ\tau_{I})^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{|\mathcal{L}_{I}|}(1))=\tau_{I}^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{I})=L_{I}=\varphi^{*}_{|L_{I}|}\big(\mathcal{O}_{|L_{I}|}(1)\big)

on
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta}(1,v)
. Now, just as in the proof of Lemma
5.5
, we deduce
\varphi_{|L_{I}|}=\varphi_{|\mathcal{L}_{I}|}\circ\tau_{I}
, so the right-hand triangle commutes and hence so does the diagram.

It remains to construct the closed immersion
\iota_{I}
. Commutativity of the diagram, combined with surjectivity of
\pi_{I}
, shows that
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is isomorphic to the closed subscheme
\mathrm{Im}(\sigma_{I})=\mathrm{Im}(\sigma_{I}\circ\pi_{I})=\mathrm{Im}(\varphi_{I}\circ\tau_{I})
of

. The closed immersion
\varphi_{I}
induces an isomorphism
\lambda_{I}\colon\mathrm{Im}(\varphi_{I})\to\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
and hence we obtain a closed immersion

\iota_{I}\coloneqq\lambda_{I}\circ\sigma_{I}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)\to\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I}).

Since
\mathfrak{M}_{\theta_{I}}(1,v)
is non-empty by assumption, it follows that
\mathcal{M}_{A_{I}}(1,n_{I})
is non-empty.
∎

6.2
Selecting a suitable dimension vector

We don’t yet know whether the morphism
\tau_{I}
from the key commutative diagram from Proposition
6.1
is surjective. We now introduce a collection of dimension vectors that will allow us to establish surjectivity.

For any vector
v\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, it is convenient to write
v_{\infty}\coloneqq 1
, so that the dimension vector
(1,v)
has components
v_{i}
for all
i\in Q_{0}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{0\leq j\leq r}\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
satisfy
v_{i}=n_{i}
for all
i\in I
. Let
V
be a
\theta_{I}
-stable

-module
of dimension vector
(1,v)
. Then
2v_{k}\leq\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=k\}}v_{\operatorname{h}(e)}

.

Fix

and define

V_{\oplus}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in{Q_{1}},\\
\;\operatorname{t}(e)=k\end{subarray}}V_{\operatorname{h}(e)}.

V
determined by arrows with head and tail at vertex

combine to define maps

and

satisfying

.

, Proposition A.2]
implies that the complex

has nonzero homology only at

, so

. This proves the claim.

For our chosen vector

). Before we prove that this set is non-empty, recall from McKay

]
that for each vertex

in the McKay quiver, we have

, so the indexing set in the sum from (

) differs from the set

only for

.

.

, then equation (

) shows that the vector

N\gg 0
and for each
k\in K^{\prime}
, let

denote the distance in the graph from

to

, define

N\dim(\rho_{k})&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since

v^{\prime}_{k}\geq v_{k}
for all

and

, we have

.

It remains to establish the inequality (

) for all
k\in K
. To do this, consider three cases, depending on whether
k\in K^{\prime}
and whether
k=0
.

First, suppose
k\in K^{\prime}
. If there is a vertex
j\in I
that lies adjacent to
k
, then (
6.2
) follows by combining (
6.1
) with the inequality
v^{\prime}_{k}\gg v^{\prime}_{j}
. Otherwise, precisely two vertices adjacent to
k
lie in
\{0\}\cup K^{\prime}
, while every other vertex adjacent to
k
lies in
K
. Then

\displaystyle 2v^{\prime}_{k}=2(N\dim(\rho_{k})-d_{k})&=-(d_{k}-1)-(d_{k}+1)+\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}^{*}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=k\}}N\dim(\rho_{\operatorname{h}(e)})

\displaystyle&=\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}^{*}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=k\}}v^{\prime}_{\operatorname{h}(e)},

so (
6.2
) holds because
k\neq 0
.

Next, suppose
k\in K\setminus K^{\prime}
with
k\neq 0
. Then each vertex
j
adjacent to
k
lies in
\{0,1,\dots,r\}
and satisfies
N\dim(\rho_{j})\geq v_{j}^{\prime}
. Combine these inequalities with (
6.1
) to obtain

2v^{\prime}_{k}=2N\dim(\rho_{k})=\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}^{*}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=k\}}N\dim(\rho_{\operatorname{h}(e)})\geq\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}^{*}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=k\}}v^{\prime}_{\operatorname{h}(e)}.

This establishes the inequality (
6.2
) since
k\neq 0
.

Finally, the only remaining case is when
k=0\in K
. If there is a vertex
j\in I
that lies adjacent to
0
, then (
6.2
) follows by combining (
6.1
) with the inequality
v^{\prime}_{0}\gg v^{\prime}_{j}
and
v^{\prime}_{\infty}=1
. Otherwise, we have
0\in K
and one vertex of
K^{\prime}
lies adjacent to
0
, giving

2v^{\prime}_{0}=2N=v^{\prime}_{\infty}+(-1)+\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}^{*}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=0\}}N\dim(\rho_{\operatorname{h}(e)})=v^{\prime}_{\infty}+\sum_{\{e\in Q_{1}^{*}\mid\operatorname{t}(e)=0\}}v^{\prime}_{\operatorname{h}(e)}.

The quiver
Q_{1}
has a unique arrow with tail at
0
and head at
\infty
, so (
6.2
) holds for
k=0
if
0\in K
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let

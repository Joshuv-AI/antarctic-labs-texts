Local and global regularity for the Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations with the localized boundary data in the half-space
Kyungkeun Kang and Chanhong Min

Abstract
. We study the Stokes system with the localized boundary data in the half-space. We are concerned with the local regularity of its solution near the boundary away from the support of the given boundary data which are product forms of each spatial variable and the temporal variable. We first show that if the boundary data are smooth in time, the corresponding solutions are also smooth in space and time near the boundary, even if the boundary data are only spatially integrable. Secondly, if the normal component of the boundary data is absent, we are able to construct a solution such that its second normal derivatives of the tangential components become singular near the boundary. Perturbation argument enables us to construct solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations with similar singular behaviors near the boundary in the half-space as the case of Stokes system. Lastly, we provide specific types of the localized boundary data to obtain the pointwise bounds of the solutions up to second derivatives. It turns out that such solutions are globally strong, and the second normal derivatives are, however, unbounded near the boundary. These results can be compared to the previous works in which only the normal component is present. In fact, the temporally non-smooth tangential boundary data can also cause spatially singular behaviors near the boundary, although such behaviors are milder than those caused by the normal boundary data of the same type.

1.
Introduction

We consider the non-stationary Stokes system in the half-space:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla p&=0\\
\text{div }u&=0\end{aligned}\end{cases}\quad\text{ in }\,\,\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

with zero initial data and non-zero boundary data:

(1.2)

u\mid_{t=0}=0,\qquad u\mid_{x_{n}=0}=g.

Here the boundary data
g:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
are separable forms for the spatial and temporal variables, which are given by

(1.3)

g(y^{\prime},s)=(g_{1}(y^{\prime},s),\,g_{2}(y^{\prime},s),\,\cdots,\,g_{n}(y^{\prime},s))=\left(a_{1}g_{1}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})g_{1}^{\mathcal{T}}(s),\,a_{2}g_{2}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})g_{2}^{\mathcal{T}}(s),\,\cdots,\,a_{n}g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(s)\right),

where

a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
g_{i}^{\mathcal{S}}\geq 0
and
g_{i}^{\mathcal{T}}\geq 0
for
i=1
,
2
,
\cdots
,
n
.
Our main concern is the local regularity of the solution near the boundary.
Assuming that the boundary conditions are localized, we come up with the following simple situation away from the support of the boundary data:

(1.4)

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla p=0,\quad{\rm div}\,u=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ \ B_{1}^{+}\times(0,\infty)

together with the no-slip boundary condition given only on the flat part of the boundary, namely

(1.5)

u=0\qquad\mbox{ on }\,\,\Lambda^{\prime}\times(0,\infty).

Here we denote
B^{+}_{1}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|x\right|<1,x_{n}>0\right\}
(via translation, the center of the half ball can be assumed to be the origin) and
\Lambda^{\prime}:=B_{1}\cap\left\{x_{n}=0\right\}
.
We emphasize that no condition is imposed on the rounded boundary, denoted by
\Lambda^{+}:=\partial B_{1}\cap\left\{x_{n}\geq 0\right\}
.
One can imagine a similar situation to compare to the heat equation, i.e.
u_{t}-\Delta u=0
for which the classical boundary regularity theory implies that

(1.6)

\left\lVert D^{l}_{x}D^{m}_{t}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{r})}\leq C_{l,m,r,n}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(Q^{+}_{1})},\qquad l,~m\geq 0,

where
Q_{r}^{+}=B^{+}_{r}\times(t-r^{2},t)\subset B_{1}^{+}\times(0,\infty)
and
r<1
.
Due to the non-local effect of the Stokes system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
), such estimate (
1.6
) is, however, not clear and, as a matter of fact, it turned out that the estimate (
1.6
), in general, isn’t true for the Stokes system (see
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
3
]
).

Indeed, in
[
8
]
, the first author constructed in three dimensions the examples of solutions such that their gradients are singular near the boundary, although the solutions themselves are bounded.
More specifically,
there exist solutions of the Stokes system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) such that
they are bounded and their derivatives are square integrable, but their normal derivatives are unbounded, i.e.,

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x,t}(Q_{1}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad\left\lVert\partial_{x_{3}}u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x,t}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}=\infty.

Such results recently became refined in the sense that the
boundary data causing a wider range of singularity are analyzed and the corresponding blow-up rates are calculated near the boundary in general dimensions, and furthermore, a construction of a solution to the Navier-Stokes equations is also made to show similar singular behaviors (see
[
10
]
for the details).
We remark that such constructed solution is an analog near the boundary to the well-known Serrin’s example, namely
u(x,t)=\nabla h(x,t)
, where
h
is harmonic in spatial variables, in the interior. We remark, however, that no non-trivial solution of the form
u(x,t)=\nabla h(x,t)
near the boundary exists because of the homogeneous boundary condition (
1.5
).
It is worth referring to the a priori estimate for (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) proved in
[
11
, Proposition 2]
, which is given as follows: For given
p,\,q\in(1,2]
and any
r
with
p\leq r<\infty
, it holds that

(1.7)

\left\lVert\partial_{t}u\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}+\left\lVert\nabla^{2}u\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}+\left\lVert\nabla\pi\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}\leq C_{p,q,r}\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\lVert\pi\right\rVert_{L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right).

Therefore, it follows from the parabolic embedding that

(1.8)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{\mathcal{C}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{t}\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad 0<\alpha<2\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right).

It was shown very recently in
[
3
]
that the estimates (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) are optimal. In fact, it turned out that the integrability in time is crucial to control
\nabla u
(see
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
). Indeed, it was also proved that if

is bounded. More precisely, for given

and

, the estimate (

(1.9)

\nabla u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{t}{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+}),\qquad 0<\alpha<1-\frac{2}{q}.

As in the case of the Stokes system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
), we also consider
the following Navier-Stokes equations:

(1.10)

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=0,\quad{\rm div}\,u=0\qquad\mbox{in}\ \ B_{1}^{+}\times(0,\infty),

with the no-slip boundary condition (

Perturbation argument of the Stokes system enables a construction of solutions of (

]

As a problem related to (
1.6
) and (
1.7
),
we also consider the Caccioppoli type inequality near the boundary for both the Stokes system and Navier-Stokes equations. Here we mean the Caccioppoli type inequality
for the Stokes system (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) by

(1.11)

\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})}

and similarly for the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.10
) and (
1.5
) by

(1.12)

\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{3}_{L^{3}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right),

where

in (

) and (

) are independent of respective

. The main concern for the above inequalities (
1.11
) and (

) is whether or not the pressure appears in the right hand sides.
One can ask the same question for the interior case and it has been known that the Caccioppoli type inequalities are valid in the interior for both the Stokes system and Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
).
The answer for validation of the Caccioppoli type inequalities is, however, negative near the boundary.
Indeed, it was proved in
[
4
]
that the Caccioppoli inequality (
1.11
) for the Stokes system and Caccioppoli type inequality (
1.12
) for the Navier-Stokes equations, in general, fail near the boundary.

On the other hand, instead of non-zero boundary data, we remark that a non-zero external force, may also cause similar singular behaviors to the gradients of the solutions near the boundary for the Stokes system and the Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
2
]
). The constructed solution is an analog in the energy class to that of a shear flow type developed in
[
12
]
. We are not going to pursue this direction, since we deal with only the non-zero boundary data in this paper.

The main tool for our construction of such singular solutions is to use the explicit representation formula for the solution of the Stokes system in the half-space (see
[
6
]
and
[
13
]
). More precisely, using the Golovkin tensor

(1.13)

K_{ij}(x,t)=-2\,\delta_{ij}\,\partial_{n}\Gamma(x,t)-4\,\partial_{j}\int_{0}^{x_{n}}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{n}\Gamma(z,t)\,\partial_{i}E(x-z)\,dz^{\prime}\,dz_{n}-2\,\delta_{nj}\partial_{i}E(x)\delta(t),

we recall that the solution
w
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is given by

(1.14)

u_{i}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma}K_{ij}(x-\xi^{\prime},t-s)g_{j}(\xi^{\prime},s)\,d\xi^{\prime}\,ds.

The singular solutions that have been formulated so far are based on the boundary data (
1.3
) with non-zero normal component but zero tangential components. One can expect singular behaviors of the solutions with only the tangential components of the localized boundary data, but this is not obvious.
The motivation of our study in this paper is to analyze behaviors of the solutions near the boundary when the boundary data are composed of the tangential components with zero normal component.
In addition, we would like to make distinct comparisons between singular behaviors caused by the tangential components and those of the normal component of the boundary data.
By the way, we remark that all the examples of the boundary data constructed in the previous works were sufficiently regular in the spatial variables and some lack of regularity was assigned to the temporal variable, and thus, in this paper, we consider the opposite case as well.

We now state our main theorems.
Our first result states that if the temporal boundary data are smooth, then the solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with pressure, are locally smooth away from the support of the boundary data. Therefore, we can say that a local smoothing effect is available if the boundary data are smooth in the temporal variable.
For convenience, we first define the set

(1.15)

A:=\left\{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\left|~3<|y^{\prime}|<4\sqrt{n},~-4<y_{i}<-3\right.\right\}.

We are now ready to state the first main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
j=1,\,2,\,\cdots,\,n
and
A
be the set defined in (
1.15

u
is the solution of the Stokes system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) defined by (

2.
Preliminaries

We recall that the
heat kernel

\Gamma
and the
fundamental solution

E
of
-\Delta
are given by

\Gamma(x,t)=\left\{\begin{matrix}(4\pi t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}&\text{ for }t>0,\\
0&\text{ for }t\leq 0,\\
\end{matrix}\right.~~~\text{ and }~~E(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n(n-2)|B_{1}|}\frac{1}{|x|^{n-2}}&\text{ for }n\geq 3,\\
-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x|&\text{ for }n=2.\\
\end{cases}

We also remind the following functions: for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.1)

A(x,t):=\int_{\Sigma}\Gamma(z^{\prime},0,t)E(x-z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}=\int_{\Sigma}\Gamma(x^{\prime}-z^{\prime},0,t)E(z^{\prime},x_{n})dz^{\prime},

(2.2)

B(x,t):=\int_{\Sigma}\Gamma(x-z^{\prime},t)E(z^{\prime},0)dz^{\prime}=\int_{\Sigma}\Gamma(z^{\prime},x_{n},t)E(x^{\prime}-z^{\prime},0)dz^{\prime},

where
\Sigma:=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

and for
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

C_{i}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{x_{n}}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{n}\Gamma(x-z,t)\partial_{i}E(z)dz,\qquad 1\leq i\leq n.

The following relations are known between above functions and their proofs are given in
[
10
]
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{n}C_{i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}C_{n}(x,t)+\partial_{i}\partial_{n}B(x,t),\qquad i\neq n,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{n}C_{n}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\partial_{k}C_{k}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{n}\Gamma(x,t).

We consider the following nonstationary Stokes system in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
:

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}w-\Delta w+\nabla\pi&=f\\
\text{div }w&=0\end{aligned}\end{cases}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,\infty),

with zero initial data and non-zero boundary data:

(2.6)

w\mid_{t=0}=0,\qquad w\mid_{x_{n}=0}=g.

We collect some estimates of the solution
w
which will be used later.

Proposition 1
.

Let
1<p,\,q<\infty
.
Suppose that
w
is the solution of (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) with
g=0
. Then,
w

satisfies the following:

1.

(
[
3
]
, Proposition 2.6)
If
f=\nabla\cdot F
with
F_{in}\mid_{x_{n}=0}=0
, then

\left\|w(t)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|F(s)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})}ds,\qquad 0<t<\infty.

2.

(
[
4
]
, Proposition 2.3)
If
f=\nabla\cdot F
with
F\in L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}))
,
F\mid_{x_{n}=0}\in L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{pp}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))
, then

\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}))}\leq C\left(\left\|F\right\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}))}+\left\|F\mid_{x_{n}=0}\right\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}_{pp}^{-\frac{1}{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1}))}\right).

3.

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 1.2)
If
f=\nabla\cdot F
with
F\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then

\left\|w\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times(0,T))}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|F\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T))}.

The
Golovkin tensor

K_{ij}(x,t):\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Poisson kernel of the nonstationary Stokes system (
2.5
) in the half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
with zero external force. A solution
w
of (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) with
f=0
is given by

(2.7)

w_{i}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma}K_{ij}(x-y^{\prime},t-s)g_{j}(y^{\prime},s)dy^{\prime}ds,

where we extend
g_{j}(x^{\prime},t)=0
for
t<0
. Here the Golovkin tensor
K_{ij}(x,t)
is

(2.8)

K_{ij}(x,t)=-2\delta_{ij}\partial_{n}\Gamma(x,t)-4\partial_{j}C_{i}(x,t)-2\delta_{nj}\partial_{i}E(x)\delta(t),\qquad i,j=1,\,2,\,\cdots,\,n

and the associated pressure tensor
k_{j}
is given by

(2.9)

k_{j}(x,t)=2\partial_{j}\partial_{n}E(x)\delta(t)+2\delta_{nj}E(x)\delta^{\prime}(t)+\frac{2}{t}\partial_{j}A(x,t),\qquad j=1,\,2,\,\cdots,\,n.

We remark that the last term of the above formula is not integrable and thus the formula has to be understood in either one of the following senses:

\displaystyle p(x,t)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\partial_{n}\int_{\Sigma}E(x-y^{\prime})g_{i}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}+2\int_{\Sigma}E(x-y^{\prime})\partial_{t}g_{n}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{2}{t-s}A(x-y^{\prime},t-s)\left[g_{j}(y^{\prime},s)-g_{j}(y^{\prime},t)\right]dy^{\prime}ds,

or

\displaystyle p(x,t)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\partial_{n}\int_{\Sigma}E(x-y^{\prime})g_{i}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}+2\int_{\Sigma}E(x-y^{\prime})\partial_{t}g_{n}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}

(2.10)

\displaystyle-4\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x^{\prime}})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{j}A(x-y^{\prime},t-s)g_{j}(y^{\prime},s)dy^{\prime}ds.

The proof of the above representations for
n=3
, is given in
[
14
]
.

We now recall some useful estimates of the previous functions and their proofs can also be found in
[
14
]
.

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}\Gamma(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(|x|^{2}+t)^{\frac{l+n}{2}+m}},

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}A(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{m+\frac{1}{2}}(|x|^{2}+t)^{\frac{l+n-2}{2}}}\qquad l+n\geq 3,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{x_{n}}^{k}\partial_{t}^{m}B(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\begin{matrix}\displaystyle\frac{1}{(|x|^{2}+t)^{\frac{l+n-2}{2}}(x_{n}^{2}+t)^{\frac{k+1}{2}+m}}&m\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{10t}}}{(|x|^{2}+t)^{\frac{l+n-2}{2}}t^{\frac{k+1}{2}}}&m=0,\\
\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{x_{n}}^{k}\partial_{t}^{m}C_{i}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{m+\frac{1}{2}}(|x|^{2}+t)^{\frac{l+n-1}{2}}(x_{n}^{2}+t)^{\frac{k}{2}}}\qquad 1\leq i\leq n.

We finally list some useful lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
)

Let
\Gamma_{1}(x,t):=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}
be the 1-dimensional heat kernel and
B
be the function defined in (
2.2
).
Then, for
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
|x^{\prime}|\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\partial_{n}B(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{n}\Gamma_{1}(x_{n},t)\left(\partial_{j}E(x^{\prime},0)+J(x^{\prime},t)\right)

and
|J(x^{\prime},t)|\leq c_{n}t^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Lemma 2
.

(
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
)

For positive
L,~a,~d
and
k
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\frac{r^{d-1}}{(r+a)^{k}}dr\lesssim\begin{cases}L^{d}(a+L)^{-k}&\textup{if }k<d,\\
L^{d}(a+L)^{-d}(1+\log_{+}\frac{L}{a})&\textup{if }k=d,\\
L^{d}(a+L)^{-d}a^{-(k-d)}&\textup{if }k>d.\\
\end{cases}

Lemma 3
.

(
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Let
a>0
,
b>0
,
k>0
,
m>0
and
k+m>d
. Let
0\neq x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle I:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{dz}{(|z|+a)^{k}(|z-x|+b)^{m}}.

Then, with
R=\max\left\{|x|,\,a,\,b\right\}\sim|x|+a+b
,

\displaystyle I\lesssim R^{d-k-m}+\delta_{kd}R^{-m}\log\frac{R}{a}+\delta_{md}R^{-k}\log\frac{R}{b}+\mathbbm{1}_{k>d}R^{-m}a^{d-k}+\mathbbm{1}_{m>d}R^{-k}b^{d-m}.

Lemma 4
.

(
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
)

Let
n\geq 3
and
\displaystyle K(x^{\prime},t)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|^{2}}{4t}}}{|z^{\prime}|^{n-2}}dz^{\prime}
for
x^{\prime}\in\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. For any
m\in[2,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle K(x^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{m}{(m+1)|x^{\prime}|}\right)^{n-2}s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(4\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(1-2^{\frac{n-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{8m^{2}s}}\right),

\displaystyle K(x^{\prime},s)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{m}{(m-1)|x^{\prime}|}\right)^{n-2}s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(4\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\frac{C}{|x^{\prime}|^{n-2}}s^{\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{8m^{2}s}},

where
C=C(n)>0
is a constant.

Lemma 5
.

If
0<s<t<1
,
0<a<1
and
c>0
, then

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}}{(1-t+s)^{1-a}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s)^{1-a}}.

Proof.

The proof will be provided in the Appendix
8.1
.
∎

Finally we will frequently use the following integral estimates.

Lemma 6
.

For any
c>0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{s^{k}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}ds\lesssim\begin{cases}\frac{e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2ct}}}{x_{n}^{2k-2}}&\textup{if }k>1,\\
e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{ct}}\log\left(1+\frac{ct}{x_{n}^{2}}\right)&\textup{if }k=1,\\
e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2ct}}&\textup{if }0<k<1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The case
k=1
follows from the inequality (5) in
[
5
]
.
The case
k>1
follows from the integration by parts after a suitable change of variables with the aid of the estimate
u^{k-1}e^{-u}\lesssim e^{-\frac{u}{2}}
. Lastly, the case
0<k<1
can be similarly obtained as the case
k>1
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

For
b,\,c>0
and
\alpha\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\,1\right)
,
\beta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\,1\right]
,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+c)^{\alpha}(u+b)^{\beta}}du\lesssim\begin{cases}\log\left(2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b+c}}\right)&\textup{if }\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}&\textup{if }\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1,\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta<1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha}}\log\left(\frac{c}{b}+1\right)&\textup{if }\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1,\quad\beta=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof will be provided in the Appendix 8.2.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1
for the Stokes equations

Since the Stokes system is linear, we treat the cases of the normal and tangential components of the boundary data separately.

\bullet

(Step 1)
(Case that
g_{j}=0
for
j\neq n
and
g_{n}\neq 0
) We first consider the case that only the normal component of the boundary data is not zero, i.e.
j=n
.

1) We first estimate the tangential components of the velocity
w
.
Let
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Reminding that

\displaystyle K_{in}(x,t)=-4\partial_{n}C_{i}(x,t)-2\partial_{i}E(x)\delta(t)=-4\partial_{i}C_{n}(x,t)-4\partial_{i}\partial_{n}B(x,t)-2\partial_{i}E(x)\delta(t),

we note that
w_{i}
is decomposed as follows.

\displaystyle w_{i}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma}K_{in}(x-y^{\prime},t-s)g_{n}(y^{\prime},s)dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{i}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},t-s)g_{n}(y^{\prime},s)dy^{\prime}ds-4\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{i}\partial_{n}B(x-y^{\prime},t-s)g_{n}(y^{\prime},s)dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{i}E(x-y^{\prime})g_{n}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)+w_{i}^{\mathcal{B}}(x,t)+w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}(x,t).

We control
w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}
,
w_{i}^{\mathcal{B}}
and
w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}
separately.

(Estimate of
w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}
)
We note that

w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}(x,t)=-2g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{i}E(x-y^{\prime})g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}.

Thus we find that

\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}(x,t)=-2\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{i}E(x-y^{\prime})g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime},

and we get that using
|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1
for any
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}(0,1)
and
y^{\prime}\in A
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}(x,t)\right|\lesssim\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t)\right|\int_{A}\left|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{i}E(x-y^{\prime})\right|\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Thus we obtain that

(3.1)

\left\|\partial_{x}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{E}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{+}^{1})}<\infty.

(Estimate of
w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}
)
We note that

w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)=-4\int_{0}^{t}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{i}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds

and since
\partial_{t}^{k}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(0)=0
for any
k\geq 0
, we find that

\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)=-4\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds.

Thus we find that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\left|\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},s)\right|\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds.

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{2}}(|x-y^{\prime}|^{2}+s)^{\frac{l+n}{2}}}\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{t}\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}},

where we used that
(|x-y^{\prime}|^{2}+s)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\geq|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\gtrsim 1
for any
\alpha>0
.

Next, the normal derivatives of
w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}
are calculated as follows using (
2.4
):

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)=

\displaystyle-4\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}C_{k}(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\Gamma(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds.

As shown above in the case of tangential derivatives, similar computations show that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)\right|\lesssim(t+\sqrt{t})\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Hence, we obtain

(3.3)

\left\|\partial_{x_{n}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{+}^{1})}\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Next, the second normal derivatives of
w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}
is estimated as follows using (
2.3
) and (
2.4
):

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle+4\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\partial_{n}\partial_{k}B(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\Gamma(x-y^{\prime},s)g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Here each
I_{k}
is estimated as follows:

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\left|\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}C_{n}(x-y^{\prime},s)\right|\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{2}}(|x-y^{\prime}|^{2}+s)^{\frac{l+n+2}{2}}}\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{t}\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\left|\partial_{x_{k}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\partial_{n}\partial_{k}B(x-y^{\prime},s)\right|\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\frac{e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{10s}}}{s(|x-y^{\prime}|^{2}+s)^{\frac{l+n+1}{2}}}\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{10s}}}{s}ds

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}\log\left(1+\frac{10t}{x_{n}^{2}}\right),

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
6
.
Thus we conclude that

|I_{2}|\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}\log\left(1+\frac{10t}{x_{n}^{2}}\right).

Unfortunately, we cannot directly estimate the
L^{\infty}
norm of
I_{2}
. However using the above estimate for
I_{2}
and the estimate
\int_{0}^{1}\left|\log\left(1+\frac{10t}{x_{n}^{2}}\right)\right|^{p}dx_{n}\leq c(p)
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
and
t\in(0,1)
, we see that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
we have the bound

\left\|I_{2}\right\|_{L^{p}(Q_{+}^{1})}<\infty.

Finally,

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\left|\partial_{n}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{i}\Gamma(x-y^{\prime},s)\right|\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(t-s)\right|\int_{A}\frac{1}{(|x-y^{\prime}|^{2}+s)^{\frac{l+n+2}{2}}}\left|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}(y^{\prime})\right|dy^{\prime}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim t\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Hence we conclude that for
p\in(1,\infty)
,

(3.5)

\left\|\partial_{x_{n}}^{2}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}\right\|_{L^{p}(Q_{+}^{1})}\lesssim\left\|\partial_{t}^{m}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Next, the we estimate the third normal derivatives of
w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}
. Using (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and the fact that the function
B(x,t)
satisfies the heat equation,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{n}}^{3}\partial_{x^{\prime}}^{l}\partial_{t}^{m}w_{i}^{\mathcal{C}}(x,t)

2. For the estimate of

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+p\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{2}\left((x_{n}^{2}+1)^{p\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)+1}-x_{n}^{2p\left(a-\frac{1}{2}\right)+2}\right)dx_{n}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{2}\left|\log\left(2+\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}}\right)\right|^{p}dx_{n},

which is finite for all

(Step 5) Pressure estimate.

We next estimate the pressure. We remind that

\displaystyle p(x,t)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{k}\partial_{n}\int_{\Sigma}E(x-y^{\prime})g_{k}(y^{\prime},t)dy^{\prime}-4(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x^{\prime}})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma}\partial_{k}A(x-y^{\prime},t-s)g_{k}(y^{\prime},s)dy^{\prime}ds

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=0&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n},\\
u(x^{\prime},0)&=\partial_{k}g_{k}^{\mathcal{S}}(x^{\prime})&&\text{on }\Sigma,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

and thus we have the estimate

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim\left\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}g_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbbm{1}_{\frac{1}{4}\leq t\leq 1}(t).

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\lesssim\mathbbm{1}_{\frac{1}{4}\leq t\leq 1}\frac{1}{\left<x\right>^{n}}\left\|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}g_{k}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(5.15)

8.1.
Proof of Lemma 5

Lemma
5
.

If
0<s<t<1
,
0<a<1
and
c>0
, then

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}}{(1-t+s)^{1-a}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s)^{1-a}}.

Proof.

First note that the function
f(t)=(t+1)e^{-\frac{t}{c}}
(
t>0
), has its maximum at
t=c-1
and thus
f(t)\leq ce^{-\frac{c-1}{c}}=:C
.
Then letting
u=\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{s}
gives

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}\leq C\frac{s}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\leq C\left(\frac{s}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right)^{1-a},

since
0<a\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Clearly
e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}\lesssim C
, which implies that

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}\lesssim C\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{s}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right)^{1-a}\right\}.

Now we note that

\displaystyle\min\left\{1,~\frac{s}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right\}\frac{1}{1-t+s}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{1-t+s},~\frac{s}{1-t+s}\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right\}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{1-t+s},~\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right\},

and

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{1}{1-t+s},~\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right\}\leq\frac{2}{x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s}.

The above inequality follows since if
x_{n}^{2}+s>1-t+s
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{n}^{2}+s}=\frac{2}{x_{n}^{2}+x_{n}^{2}+2s}<\frac{2}{x_{n}^{2}+1-t+2s}<\frac{2}{x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s},

while if
x_{n}^{2}+s<1-t+s
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t+s}=\frac{2}{(1-t)+(1-t)+2s}<\frac{2}{x_{n}^{2}+1-t+2s}<\frac{2}{x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s}.

Thus we have that

\displaystyle\min\left\{1,~\frac{s}{x_{n}^{2}+s}\right\}\leq\frac{2(1-t+s)}{x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s},

and hence

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{cs}}\lesssim\frac{2^{1-a}(1-t+s)^{1-a}}{(x_{n}^{2}+1-t+s)^{1-a}}.

We obtain the desired inequality.
∎

8.2.
Proof of Lemma 7

Lemma
7
.

For
b,\,c>0
and
\alpha\in\left[\frac{1}{2},\,1\right)
,
\beta\in\left[\frac{1}{2}\,,1\right]
,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+c)^{\alpha}(u+b)^{\beta}}du\lesssim\begin{cases}\log\left(2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{b+c}}\right)&\textup{if }\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{2},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}&\textup{if }\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1,\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta<1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha}}\log\left(\frac{c}{b}+1\right)&\textup{if }\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1,\quad\beta=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first show when
b,\,c<1
.

1) If
\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{2}
, it is direct that

(8.1)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+b)^{\beta}(u+c)^{\alpha}}du=2\log\frac{\sqrt{b+1}+\sqrt{c+1}}{\sqrt{b}+\sqrt{c}},

which gives the last formula in the lemma.

2) Next, we consider the case that
\alpha,\beta\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.
Via the change of variable, we rewrite the integral as follows:

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+b)^{\beta}(u+c)^{\alpha}}du=\frac{1}{b^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\int_{0}^{1/b}\frac{1}{(u+\frac{c}{b})^{\alpha}(u+1)^{\beta}}du=:I.

If
c>\frac{1}{2}
, then using
\frac{b+c}{4}<c
,

I\leq\frac{1}{c^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+b)^{\beta}}du\leq\frac{(1+b)^{1-\beta}}{c^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}.

We now treat the case
c<\frac{1}{2}
. We split the integral
I
as follows:

I=\frac{1}{b^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\int_{0}^{c/b}\frac{1}{(u+\frac{c}{b})^{\alpha}(u+1)^{\beta}}du+\frac{1}{b^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\int_{c/b}^{1/b}\frac{1}{(u+\frac{c}{b})^{\alpha}(u+1)^{\beta}}du=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Estimating each term separately, we have that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{b^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\left(\frac{b}{c}\right)^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{c/b}\frac{1}{(u+1)^{\beta}}du\lesssim\frac{1}{b^{\beta-1}c^{\alpha}}\left[\left(\frac{c}{b}+1\right)^{1-\beta}-1\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(c+b)^{1-\beta}-b^{1-\beta}}{c^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{c^{1-\alpha}}{(c+b)^{\beta}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}.

On the other hand, we have that since
c<\frac{1}{2}
,

I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{b^{\alpha-1}(c+b)^{\beta}}\int_{c/b}^{1/b}\frac{1}{\left(u+\frac{c}{b}\right)^{\alpha}}du\lesssim\frac{b^{1-\alpha}}{(c+b)^{\beta}}(1+c)^{1-\alpha}\lesssim\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}.

3) It remains to treat the case when
\alpha\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and
\beta=1
. Indeed, again considering
c>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

I\leq\frac{1}{c^{\alpha}}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{b}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{\alpha}}\log\left(1+\frac{c}{b}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha}}\log\left(1+\frac{c}{b}\right),

and if
c<\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain that

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{c/b}\frac{1}{(u+\frac{c}{b})^{\alpha}(u+1)}du.

If
\frac{c}{b}<\frac{1}{2}
, then

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(u+\frac{c}{b})^{\alpha}(u+1)}du\lesssim\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha}},

since the integral above is convergent.

If
\frac{c}{b}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, it is straight forward that

I_{1}\leq\frac{1}{c^{\alpha}}\log\left(1+\frac{c}{b}\right)\lesssim\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha}}\log\left(\frac{c}{b}+1\right).

For
I_{2}
, we have that if
\frac{c}{b}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\int_{\frac{c}{b}}^{\frac{1}{b}}\frac{1}{(u+1)^{\alpha+1}}du\lesssim\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha}},

and if
\frac{c}{b}<\frac{1}{2}
, then

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\int_{\frac{c}{b}}^{\frac{1}{b}}\frac{1}{\left(u+\frac{c}{b}\right)^{\alpha}(u+1)}du\leq\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(u+\frac{c}{b}\right)^{\alpha}(u+1)}du\lesssim\frac{1}{b^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(b+c)^{\alpha}},

since the last integral is convergent.

We now show when
b>1
or
c>1
.

1) If
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\beta<1
, we find that if
c\geq b
(so that
c>1
), then

I\leq\frac{1}{c^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+b)^{\beta}}du\lesssim\frac{(1+b)^{1-\beta}}{c^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{(c+b)^{1-\beta}}{c^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}.

If
b>c
(so that
b>1
), we may switch the role of
b
and
\beta
to
c
and
\alpha
to get the desired result.

2) If
\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{2}
, the result follows from the identity (
8.1
).

3) If
\alpha\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right)
and
\beta=1
, we find that if
c\geq b
, then

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+c)^{\alpha}(u+b)}du\leq\frac{1}{c^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{u+b}du=\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{1}{b}\right)}{c^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{\log\left(1+\frac{c}{b}\right)}{(c+b)^{\alpha}},

and finally if
b>c
, then

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+c)^{\alpha}(u+b)}\leq\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u+c)^{\alpha}}du\lesssim\frac{(1+c)^{1-\alpha}}{b}\lesssim\frac{1}{(c+b)^{\alpha}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

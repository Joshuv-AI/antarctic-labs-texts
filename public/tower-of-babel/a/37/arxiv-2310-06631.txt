Dense circuit graphs and the planar Turán number of a cycle
Ruilin Shi, Zach Walsh, Xingxing Yu

Abstract
The planar Turán number ex 𝒫 ​ ( n , H ) \textrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,H) of a graph H H is the maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex planar graph without H H as a subgraph. Let C k C_{k} denote the cycle of length k k . The planar Turán number ex 𝒫 ​ ( n , C k ) \textrm{ex}_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k}) is known for k ≤ 7 k\leq 7 . Introduction

The
Turán number

\ex(n,H)
of a graph
H
is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex graph without
H
as a subgraph.
Turán’s theorem
[
19
]
, a cornerstone of extremal graph theory, determines
\ex(n,K_{t})
.
This has led to a huge amount of related work for graphs and hypergraphs; see
[
6
]
,
[
1
]
, and
[
11
]
.

A well-studied variant of Turán’s theorem involves restrictions of the host graph to an interesting class of graphs, such as hypercubes (see
[
16
]
) or Erdős-Rényi random graphs (see
[
17
]
).
In 2015, Dowden
[
4
]
considered planar graphs, and defined the
planar Turán number

\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,H)
of a graph
H
to be the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex planar graph without
H
as a subgraph. This initiated a flurry of research on planar Turán numbers; see the survey paper of Lan, Shi, and Song
[
13
]
for details regarding recent related work.

In this paper, we focus on the planar Turán number of
C_{k}
, the cycle of length
k
. Euler’s formula easily gives
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{3})=2n-4
for all
n\geq 3
. Dowden proved that
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{4})\leq\frac{15(n-2)}{7}
for all
n\geq 4
and
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{5})\leq\frac{12n-33}{5}
for all
n\geq 11
, and showed that in both cases equality holds infinitely often
[
4
]
.
In
[
7
]
, Ghosh, Győri, Martin, Paulos, and Xiao took the next step and proved that
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{6})\leq\frac{5n}{2}-7
for all
n\geq 18
with equality holding infinitely often, improving upon a result of Yan, Shi, and Song
[
12
]
.
They also made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})\leq\frac{3(k-1)}{k}n-\frac{6(k+1)}{k}
for all
k\geq 7
and all sufficiently large
n
.

It was recently shown independently by Győri, Li, and Zhou
[
9
]
and the present authors
[
18
]
that Conjecture
1.1
holds for
k=7
.
However, the conjecture was disproved for all
k\geq 11
by Cranston, Lidický, Liu, and Shantanam
[
3
]
, and later also by Lan and Song
[
14
]
and Győri, Varga, and Zhu
[
10
]
, using the fact that planar triangulations with at least
11
vertices need not be Hamiltonian. Specifically, Moon and Moser
[
15
]
showed that for each
k\geq 11
there exists a
C_{k}
-free planar triangulation with
(2k/7)^{\log_{2}3}
vertices; this is tight up to a constant factor by a result of Chen and Yu
[
2
]
.

We next describe the construction that disproves Conjecture
1.1
, following Cranston et al.
[
3
]
. For a graph
G
, we use
V(G)
and
E(G)
to denote its vertex set and edge set, respectively, and we write
v(G)
for
|V(G)|
and
e(G)
for
|E(G)|
.
Let
k
and
n
be integers with
k\geq 5
and
n\geq k+1
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex planar graph with girth
k+1
, each vertex having degree
2
or
3
, and
\frac{k+1}{k-1}(n-2)
edges; such a graph exists for infinitely many integers

[
3
, Lemma 2]
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be obtained from
G
by sudividing each edge and then
substituting
for each vertex of
G
a
C_{k}
-free planar triangulation
B
on as many vertices as possible, which means that each vertex
v
of
G
is replaced by a copy of
B
and that
\deg_{G}(v)
vertices of
B
on a facial triangle are identified with the neighbors of
v
in
G
.
Then
G^{\prime}
is a
C_{k}
-free graph.
Cranston et al. conjecture that
G^{\prime}
has
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(v(G^{\prime}),C_{k})
edges
[
3
]
, but acknowledge that this will be difficult to prove, in part because the number of vertices of

is only known up to a constant factor.
Because of this, they also made the following weaker conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

There is a constant
D
so that for all
k
and sufficiently large

we have
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})\leq 3n-\frac{Dn}{k^{\log_{2}3}}
.

While this might not provide an exact upper bound, it is tight up to the constant

when
k\geq 11
.
This follows from Cranston et al.’s construction, and also a more recent simpler construction of Győri, Varga, and Zhu
[

which shows that

can be at most

.
We prove this conjecture with

.

Theorem 1.3
.

\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,C_{k})\leq 3n-6-\frac{n}{4k^{\log_{2}3}}
for all
k,n\geq 4
.

To prove this, we first show that it suffices to consider graphs that are close to being
3
-connected.
A
circuit graph
is a pair
(G,C)
where
G
is a
2
-connected plane graph and

is a facial cycle of

so that for any

-cut

We show that if there is a counterexample to Theorem

We then show that a dense circuit graph has a large near triangulation as a subgraph, where a

is a plane graph in which every face is bounded by a triangle except possibly the outer face.
For a
2
-connected plane graph
G
with outer cycle

we write

for the number of interior edges needed to make

m(G)=3v(G)-6-e(G)-(|C|-3).

We will apply the following theorem with

.

Theorem 1.4

For all

, if

is a circuit graph with

has a near triangulation subgraph

.

This bound is sharp, as shown by iterating the construction of Figure

Each iteration adds three copies of a

-vertex triangulation arranged in a cyclic order, which adds

Since every near triangulation is a circuit graph we can apply the following theorem of Chen and Yu
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
2
]
)
.

For all

, if
(G,C)
is a circuit graph with at least

vertices, then

has a cycle of length at least

.

Finally, it is straightforward to show (see Lemma

) that every near triangulation with a cycle of length at least

In fact, we will prove a result that is stronger than Theorem

.
For each integer

, we write

for the family of graphs (called

by adding a single edge, and we write

for the graph obtained from

The number

is known only when

, by results of Lan, Shi, and Song
[

]
and Ghosh, Győri, Paulos, Xiao, and Zamora
[

]
.
We prove the following, which again is tight up to the constant

by the construction of Cranston et al

]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

\ex_{\mathcal{P}}(n,\theta_{k})\leq 3n-6-\frac{n}{4k^{\log_{2}3}}
for all
k,n\geq 4
.

This also holds with
\Theta_{k}
in place of
\theta_{k}
because
\theta_{k}\in\Theta_{k}
.
Interestingly, this bound may not hold if we replace
\theta_{k}
with another graph in

After describing all relevant notation, we will prove Theorem
1.4
in Section

In Section

For any positive integer

, we let

.
It is well known that if

is a 2-connected plane graph then each face of

is bounded by a cycle of

.
The

A path or cycle will be represented as a sequence of vertices such that consecutive vertices in the sequence are adjacent.
For instance,

represents a path of length

,

, are the edges of the path, and

represents a cycle of length

and

,

.
For any distinct vertices

in a graph

are on a path

and

.
For any cycle

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Proof.

(ii)

4
Related problems

Theorem
1.3
has interesting consequences related to circumference.
The
circumference
of a graph (with a cycle) is the length of a longest cycle in the graph.
Erdős and Gallai proved that every
n
-vertex graph with circumference less than
k
has at most
\frac{(n-1)(k-1)}{2}
edges, and this is tight for infinitely many integers
n

[
5
]
.
Theorem
1.3
gives an analogue for planar graphs, because graphs with circumference less than
k
are certainly
C_{k}
-free.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n,k\geq 4
. If
G
is an
n
-vertex planar graph with circumference less than
k
, then
e(G)\leq 3n-6-\frac{n}{4k^{\log_{2}3}}
.

Again, this is tight up to the constant
1/4
because the construction of Győri, Varga, and Zhu
[
10
]
gives a lower bound of
3n-6-\frac{12n}{k^{\log_{2}3}}
.

We believe that analogues of the Erdős-Gallai theorem are interesting for other classes of graphs (such as graphs with bounded genus, or without a fixed subgraph
H
), and also more generally for classes of matroids.
The
circumference
of a matroid (with a circuit) is the number of elements in a largest circuit in the matroid.
We mention one question in this direction that is closely related to Theorem
1.3
.
A matroid is
cographic
if it is the dual of the cycle matroid of a graph.
It is well-known that a rank-
n
cographic matroid has at most
3n-3
elements; this bound is tight for planar-graphic matroids.

Problem 4.2
.

Show that there is a constant
D
so that for all
n,k\geq 4
, if
M
is a rank-
n
cographic matroid with circumference less than
k
, then the number of elements of
M
is at most
3n-3-\frac{Dn}{k^{\log_{2}3}}
.

This would follow from Theorem
1.3
with
D=1/4
if every rank-
n
cographic matroid with circumference less than
k
and greater than
3n-3-\frac{Dn}{k^{\log_{2}3}}
elements is in fact planar-graphic.

Finally, we point out that Theorem
1.4
may be true for all
2
-connected plane graphs, not just circuit graphs.

Problem 4.3
.

Does Theorem
1.4
hold for all
2
-connected plane graphs?

The structure of 2-cuts in plane graphs that are not circuit graphs can be quite complicated, and it is unclear how to reduce from general 2-connected plane graphs to circuit graphs.
However, while we do make use of the connectivity properties of circuit graphs in Claim
2.0.3
, there may be other arguments that hold for all
2
-connected plane graphs.

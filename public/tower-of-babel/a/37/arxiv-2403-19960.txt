A note on density of geodesics
J. Beck, W.W.L. Chen, Y. Yang

Abstract
. We extend the famous result of Katok and Zemlyakov on the density of half-infinite geodesics on finite flat rational surfaces to half-infinite geodesics on a finite polycube translation 3 3 -manifold. We also extend this original result to establish a weak uniformity statement.

11K38, 37E35

1.
A density result

A finite
polysquare region

P
is an arbitrary connected, though not necessarily simply-connected,
polygon on the plane which is tiled with closed unit squares, called the
atomic squares
or
faces
of
P
,
and which satisfies the following conditions:

(i) Any two atomic squares in
P
either are disjoint, or intersect at a single point, or have a common edge.

(ii) Any two atomic squares in
P
are joined by a chain of atomic squares where any two neighbours
in the chain have a common edge.

Note that
P
may have
holes
, and we also allow
whole barriers
which are vertical or
horizontal
walls
that consist of one or more boundary edges of atomic squares, as shown
in the picture on the left in Figure 1.

\begin{array}[]{c}\includegraphics[scale]{figure-1.pdf}\\
\mbox{Figure 1: bla bla bla}\end{array}

Given such a finite polysquare region
P
, we can convert it into a finite
polysquare translation surface

\mathcal{P}

by identifying pairs of the boundary edges with inward normals in opposite directions,
as illustrated in the passage from the picture on the left to the picture on the right in Figure 1.
Geodesic flow on such a finite polysquare translation surface
\mathcal{P}
is then
1
-direction geodesic flow.

We say that a geodesic
\mathcal{L}(t)
,
t\geqslant 0
, on
\mathcal{P}
is
half-infinite
if it does not hit a singularity of
\mathcal{P}
and becomes undefined.
Furthermore, such a geodesic is
dense
if it gets arbitrarily close to every point of
\mathcal{P}
.

The following result of Katok and Zemlyakov
[
1
]
in 1975
includes density of half-infinite geodesics on finite polysquare translation surfaces as a special case.

Theorem A
.

With the exception of a countable set of given directions, every half-infinite geodesic on a finite rational surface
\mathcal{P}
is dense.
In the special case when
\mathcal{P}
is a finite polysquare translation surface, any half-infinite geodesic with irrational slope is dense in
\mathcal{P}
.

We remark that a finite rational surface is a flat surface with finitely many faces and where every face is a rational polygon,
where each angle is a rational multiple of
\pi
,
and parallel edges with the same length are identified in pairs, while identified edges from the same face have
opposite orientation.

In this paper, we consider the
3
-dimensional analogue of the special case of this result.

A finite
polycube region

M
is an arbitrary connected, though not necessarily simply-connected,
polyhedron in
3
-space which is tiled with closed unit cubes, called the
atomic cubes
of
M
,
and which satisfies the following conditions:

(i) Any two atomic cubes in
M
either are disjoint, or intersect at a single point, or have a common edge, or have a common face.

(ii) Any two atomic cubes in
M
are joined by a chain of atomic cubes where any two neighbours
in the chain have a common face.

Given such a finite polycube region
M
, we can convert it into a finite
polycube translation
3
-manifold

\mathcal{M}

by identifying pairs of the boundary faces with inward normals in opposite directions.
Geodesic flow in such a finite polycube translation
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
is then
1
-direction geodesic flow.

A half-infinite geodesic in a finite polycube translation
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
is dense if it gets arbitrarily close to every point of
\mathcal{M}
.

To state our first result, we need a simple definition.

A vector
\mathbf{v}^{*}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a Kronecker direction if
v_{1},v_{2},1
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a polycube translation
3
-manifold with
s
atomic cubes.
Then any half-infinite geodesic with a Kronecker direction
\mathbf{v}^{*}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is dense in
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Suppose, on the contrary, that
\mathcal{L}(t)
,
t\geqslant 0
, is not dense.
Then its closure
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{M}
,
and the open set
\mathcal{M}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{L}}
contains an open ball
B
.
We may assume, without loss of generality, that
B
is contained in a single atomic cube of
\mathcal{M}
.
Then the subset

\mathcal{I}(B)=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{M}:\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}_{0}+t\mathbf{v}^{*}\mbox{ for some $\mathbf{v}_{0}\in B$ and $t\geqslant 0$}\}\subset\mathcal{M}

is a
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow-invariant open set.

Consider the multiplicity function
m:[0,1)^{3}\to\mathbb{N}
, given by

m(\mathbf{x})=|\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{I}(B):\mathbf{p}\equiv\mathbf{x}\bmod{1}\}|,\quad\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{3}.

In other words,
m(\mathbf{x})
counts the number of points in
\mathcal{I}(B)
that have common image
\mathbf{x}\in[0,1)^{3}
under modulo
1
projection
from
\mathcal{M}
to the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
Since the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
is ergodic, the function
m
is constant almost everywhere,
and the constant is an integer
m_{0}
satisfying
0\leqslant m_{0}\leqslant s
.

As
\mathcal{I}(B)
is an open set, clearly
m_{0}\geqslant 1
.
On the other hand, the complement of
\mathcal{I}(B)
in
\mathcal{M}
is a closed set that contains
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
.
Clearly the image under modulo
1
projection of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is
[0,1)^{3}
,
as the image of
\mathcal{L}(t)
,
t\geqslant 0
, under modulo
1
projection from
\mathcal{M}
to the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}

is a geodesic in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
with a Kronecker direction, and is therefore dense in
[0,1)^{3}
.
Hence
1\leqslant m_{0}\leqslant s-1
.

Lemma 1.1
.

There exist
m_{0}

B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{m_{0}}
of identical positive radius such that
B^{\prime}_{1}\subset B

\mathcal{M}

\mathcal{M}

Clearly there exists

.
This means that there exist

Since

[0,1)^{3}

Let
B^{\prime}_{m_{0}+1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{s}
denote open balls in the other

atomic cubes of
\mathcal{M}

such that
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{s}
have common image under modulo

projection from
\mathcal{M}
to the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
Observe that all these open balls have the same relative position within their own atomic cubes.

It is convenient to
2
-colour the polycube translation
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
as follows.
We colour the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow-invariant set
\mathcal{I}(B)

white

and colour its complement
silver
.
Then each of the open balls
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{m_{0}}
is almost everywhere white,
while each of the open balls
B^{\prime}_{m_{0}+1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{s}
is almost everywhere silver.

For technical reasons which will become clear later, we consider a collection
of open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
, where for every
i=1,\ldots,s
, the open ball
B_{i}

has the same centre as the open ball
B^{\prime}_{i}
, but has radius equal to half of the radius of
B^{\prime}_{i}
.
Of course, each of the open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m_{0}}
is almost everywhere white,
while each of the open balls
B_{m_{0}+1},\ldots,B_{s}
is almost everywhere silver.

We next spread the open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
with the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow.
Suppose that
B_{\dagger}
is the common image of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}

under modulo
1

\mathcal{M}
to the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
Then the projected
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow spreads
B_{\dagger}
over the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
splitting free
such that every point in
[0,1)^{3}
is covered.
This can be achieved in a sufficiently large finite time, in view of the Kronecker density theorem.

Consider now what happens to the open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
under the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow.
Since the system is in general non-integrable, there is splitting.
We make use of this splitting, and we first explain this using a very simple example.

Consider the polysquare translation surface with
4

Here the vertical edges on the bottom row of atomic squares are barriers.

\mathcal{P}
with the unit torus

Here the

Clearly it is cut into

After this example, we return to the general problem concerning the open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
.
Each of these open balls is monochromatic.
After encountering the splittings, we have
s
new open balls, some of which may not be monochromatic any more,
as the
2
individual parts of a new open ball may come from distinctly coloured open balls.

Recall that the projected
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow spreads
B_{\dagger}
over the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
splitting free
such that every point in
[0,1)^{3}
is covered, and this can be achieved in finite time.
Let us move the the open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
by the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow for this finite time,
and call this the finite
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow process.
Splitting will occur a finite number of times during this finite process.
There are two possibilities:

Case 1
.

At some stage of the finite
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow process, there is a colour split open ball.
Thus a positive proportion of this ball is white, and a positive proportion of this ball is silver.

Case 2
.

After each splitting, all the new open balls remain monochromatic.
Thus there is no colour split balls at any stage of the finite
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow process.

We show that both possibilities lead to a contradiction.

Case 1: colour split
.

We first show that colour splitting is not reversible, in the sense that any further splitting cannot lead to a monochromatic open ball.

Lemma 1.2
.

If there is a colour split open ball at any stage of the finite
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow process,
then there is a colour split open ball at the end of the finite
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow process.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the effect of a splitting edge in the
y
-direction,
as the planes that cut
S
into
2
parts corresponding to splitting edges
in the
x
-,
y
- and
z
-directions are perpendicular to the directions
given by the vector products

\mathbf{v}^{*}\times(1,0,0),\quad\mathbf{v}^{*}\times(0,1,0),\quad\mathbf{v}^{*}\times(0,0,1),

respectively, and so are not parallel.

Suppose that a point of an open ball
S
hits some splitting edge
of the polycube translation
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
in the
y
-direction
at a point
(m_{1},y_{1},n_{1})
, where for the sake of convenience, we take
m_{1},n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then this point belongs to the plane that cuts
S
into
2
parts.
As
S
continues in the direction
\mathbf{v}^{*}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1)
of the flow,
this point takes on values of the form

(m_{1},y_{1},n_{1})+t(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1),\quad t\geqslant 0.

(1.1)

To establish the lemma, it suffices to show that the half-infinite geodesic (
1.1
)
never hits a splitting edge in the
y
-direction again.
Suppose, on the contrary, that it does.
Then there exist
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
m_{2},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

such that

(m_{1},y_{1},n_{1})+t(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1)=(m_{2},y_{2},n_{2}),\quad\mbox{so that}\quad\alpha_{1}=\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}}{n_{2}-n_{1}},

contradicting the assumption that
\mathbf{v}^{*}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1)
is a Kronecker direction.
∎

Let
B^{\prime}_{\dagger}
be the common image of
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{s}
under modulo
1

from
\mathcal{M}
to the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
In view of the Kronecker density theorem, the centre of the open ball
B_{\dagger}
will
move under the projected
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
to a point close to
the centre of the open ball
B^{\prime}_{\dagger}
.
This means that the open ball
B_{\dagger}
will move under the projected
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow in the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}

to inside the bigger open ball
B^{\prime}_{\dagger}
.

The implication of this is that the colour split open ball will eventually move under the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow in
\mathcal{M}

to within one of the monochromatic open balls
B^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,B^{\prime}_{s}
,
and this is clearly absurd.

Case 2: no colour split
.

In this case, the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow always exhibits
m_{0}
white open balls and
s-m_{0}
silver open balls.
The
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow spreads the open balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
and eventually covers the whole
polycube translation
3
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
.
Within any atomic cube of
\mathcal{M}
, consider any two overlapping open balls that are the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow images
of two of
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}
.
Since both are monochromatic, they must have the same colour.
Thus this colour-coincidence spreads within the atomic cube,

\mathcal{M}
is monochromatic.
Thus there are
m_{0}
white atomic cubes and

silver atomic cubes.
However, since
\mathcal{M}
is face-connected, there must be

where one is white and the other is silver.
Then the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow transports a set of positive volume from an atomic cube of one colour

-flow invariant.

2.
When faces have gates and barriers

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1
can be extended to prove the following more general result.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be modified from a finite polycube translation
3
-manifold as follows.
Each square face is in part a barrier, coloured red (shaded), and in part a gate, coloured green (white).
The latter permits travel between the two cubes that share the common square face.
We then modify boundary identification accordingly to ensure that
\mathcal{M}
is boundary free
and remains connected.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be any polycube
3
-manifold with
s
atomic cubes and barriers,
where each square face has a
2
-colouring such that each of the red and green parts
is the union of finitely many polygons and
\mathcal{M}
is connected.
Then for almost every direction
\mathbf{v}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, any half-infinite geodesic with direction
\mathbf{v}^{*}

is dense in
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

We need to modify the proof of Theorem
1
appropriately at a number of points.
These concern those planes that cut the open balls
B
into
2
parts.
Apart from arising from the edges of
\mathcal{M}
, these now also arise from the edges of the polygons
on the faces of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let us first deal with the latter, and let us restrict ourselves first to those that are on the
X
-faces of
\mathcal{M}
.

The collection of polygons on one
X
-face may differ significantly from that on another
X
-face.
To handle this, we first observe that not all edges of the atomic cubes of
\mathcal{M}
are singularities,
but we still consider

on them.
If no splitting actually occurs, then each part of an open ball after splitting reunites with its complement.

Let
\Gamma_{i}
denote the collection of colour split boundaries arising from the green and red polygons
on the right
X
-face of the
i
-th atomic cube of
\mathcal{M}
,
and let
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
denote the image of
\Gamma_{i}
under modulo
1

\mathcal{M}
to

[0,1)^{3}
.
Then
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}
lies on the right
X
-face of the unit torus
[0,1)^{3}
.
Let

\Gamma=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\Gamma^{\prime}_{i},

and now replace
\Gamma_{i}
on the right
X
-face of the
i
-th atomic cube by
\Gamma^{*}_{i}
,
where the image of
\Gamma^{*}_{i}
under modulo
1

\mathcal{M}
to
the unit torus

is
\Gamma
.
Thus all the right
X
-faces of the atomic cubes of
\mathcal{M}
have essentially the same collection
of

arising from the green and red polygons.
We refer to these as the
X
-face splitting edges
.

Similarly there are
Y
-face splitting edges
and
Z
-face splitting edges

We have already commented at the beginning of the proof of Lemma

S
into
2

The plane corresponding

.
We need to be careful in case this plane coincides with one of the following:

(2) the plane corresponding to an edge of an atomic cube in direction

;

(3) the plane corresponding to an edge of an atomic cube in direction

(4) the plane corresponding to a

(5) the plane corresponding to a

-face splitting edge

.

In case (4), the common plane must contain

,

.

.
This case can therefore only happen if the vector

.

0

In case (6), the common plane must contain

.
Since

.

\mathbf{v}^{*}

\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

In case (7), the plane containing the two

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

Z

let
\mathcal{E}(E)

.

Proof.

The edge
E

c_{1}x+c_{2}y=c_{3}
,
where the coefficients
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
are fixed.
Consider a point
(x_{1},y_{1},m_{1})
on
E
,
where
x_{1},y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and for the sake of convenience, we take

.
Then

c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}y_{1}=c_{3}.

The effect of the
\mathbf{v}^{*}
-flow on this point is described by the half-infinite geodesic

(x_{1},y_{1},m_{1})+t(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1),\quad t\geqslant 0.

Suppose that this half-infinite geodesic takes the point
(x_{1},y_{1},m_{1})

back to
E
or to some
Z
-face splitting edge analogous to
E
.
Then there exist
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
m_{2},n_{2},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x_{2},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(x_{1},y_{1},m_{1})+t(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},1)=(x_{2},y_{2},m_{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad c_{1}(x_{2}-n_{2})+c_{2}(y_{2}-q_{2})=c_{3},

from which we deduce that

c_{1}(m_{2}-m_{1})\alpha_{1}+c_{2}(m_{2}-m_{1})\alpha_{2}=c_{1}n_{2}+c_{2}q_{2},

(2.1)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are fixed and
m_{1}
is determined by
E
.
For any fixed choice of the integers
(m_{2},n_{2},q_{2})
, the equation (
2.1
)
shows that the exceptional points
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
must lie on a line.
Since there are only countably many choices for the integers
(m_{2},n_{2},q_{2})
,
it follows that the set
\mathcal{E}(E)
can be described as a countable union of lines,
and so has measure
0
.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

Remark
.

We can compare Lemmas
1.2
and
2.1
.
In the former, we see from the proof that the exceptional set of directions are the non-Kronecker directions,
whereas in the latter, this is not always the case.

Suppose that in the statement of Theorem
2
, we add an extra condition
and require the edges of the finitely many polygons to have
rational
slopes.
Then in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, the coefficients
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

of the line
c_{1}x+c_{2}y=c_{3}
can be taken to be rational.
Then the equation (
2.1
) becomes an equation in the variables
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}

with rational coefficients, so that any solution leads to a non-Kronecker direction.

This observation suggests that face splitting edges with rational slopes behave in a similar fashion
to edges of the atomic cubes, whereas face splitting edges with irrational slopes are somewhat
more delicate.

3.
Weak uniformity with bounded returns

For Theorem A in its full generality, we can go beyond the class of finite flat rational surfaces,
and consider the larger class of finite
translation surfaces
, where the restriction on the angles is dropped.
A finite translation surface
\mathcal{P}
is a flat surface with finitely many faces and where every face is a polygon,
and parallel edges with the same length are identified in pairs, while identified edges from the same face have
opposite orientation.

In this generality, the directions in
\mathcal{P}
such that no geodesic on
\mathcal{P}
can contain more than one vertex of
\mathcal{P}

defines the
good
directions
and plays the perfect analogue to irrational slopes in the special case when
\mathcal{P}
is a polysquare translation surface.
It remains to show that the set of
bad
directions is countable.
A geodesic on the unit torus
[0,1)^{2}
can be extended to a straight line on the plane.
Here every geodesic on
\mathcal{P}
containing at least two vertices of
\mathcal{P}
can be extended to a straight line
on a countable covering of the plane.
Each plane on this countable covering contains a countable number of extended vertices.
Since two points determine a straight line, the set of bad directions is countable.

For a finite translation surface
\mathcal{P}
, we can identify the bad directions in terms of
saddle connections
.
A finite geodesic segment on a finite translation surface
\mathcal{P}
is called a saddle connection if both endpoints
of the geodesic segment are vertices of
\mathcal{P}
and there are no vertices of
\mathcal{P}
in between.
The set of saddle connections of a finite translation surface is countable.
Furthermore, the collection of saddle connections with a given fixed slope is finite.
Then the exceptional directions in Theorem A are precisely those directions of the saddle connections of
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a finite translation surface.
Density of a half-infinite geodesic on
\mathcal{P}
, as given by the full generality of Theorem A, means that
the orbit of the geodesic visits every non-empty open set in
\mathcal{P}
.
Since the orbit is infinite, this trivially means that it returns to this open set infinitely many times.

A less trivial consequence of density of a half-infinite geodesic is a new concept
which we call
weak uniformity with bounded returns
.
This means that for every non-empty open set
G\subset\mathcal{P}
,
there is a constant
\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{F}(G;\mathcal{P};\alpha)>0
, where
\alpha
is the slope of the geodesic,
and a finite threshold
c_{5}=c_{5}(G;\mathcal{P};\alpha)>0
such that
for every geodesic segment
L
with slope
\alpha
and length
|L|\geqslant c_{5}
,
the inequality

\lambda_{1}(G\cap L)\geqslant\mathfrak{F}|L|

holds, where
\lambda_{1}
denotes
1
-dimensional Lebesgue measure,
so that
L
visits
G
in total length at least
\mathfrak{F}|L|
.
Intuitively,
\mathfrak{F}>0
gives a lower bound to what may be called the
visiting frequency
of
G

relative to geodesic segments of slope
\alpha
.

Every open set in a finite rational translation surface
\mathcal{P}
is Lebesgue measurable.
However, some open sets
G\subset\mathcal{P}
are not Jordan measurable,
and the characteristic function
\chi_{G}
has no well defined
2
-dimensional Riemann integral.
The traditional Weyl type uniformity requires that the asymptotic visiting frequency of
G

is precisely equal to the relative volume of
G
, assuming that
G
is Jordan measurable.
This motivates us to describe this new concept as a weak form of uniformity.

We establish the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
\alpha
is not the slope of any saddle connection of a finite translation surface
\mathcal{P}
.
Then every non-empty open set
G\subset\mathcal{P}
has positive visiting frequency relative to geodesic segments of slope
\alpha
.

Before we start the proof, let us draw some simple and useful consequences from Theorem A.

Let
\alpha
be fixed and not equal to the slope of any saddle connection of
\mathcal{P}
.
Consider the direction
(1,\alpha)
.
A point
Q\in\mathcal{P}
is said to be a pathological starting point in this direction if a geodesic in this direction
and starting from
Q
hits a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}
and becomes undefined.
Otherwise
Q\in\mathcal{P}
is said to be a non-pathological starting point in this direction.

Let
Q\in\mathcal{P}
be a non-pathological starting point of a half-infinite geodesic
\mathcal{L}(t)
,
t\geqslant 0
,
in the direction
(1,\alpha)
, so that
\mathcal{L}(0)=Q
.
Then Theorem A implies that for every non-empty open set
G\subset\mathcal{P}
,
there is a finite threshold
T=T(G;Q)
such that

\{\mathcal{L}(t):0\leqslant t\leqslant T(G;Q)\}\cap G\neq\emptyset,

so that a geodesic segment of length
T=T(G;Q)
in the direction
(1,\alpha)
and starting from the point
Q

visits the set
G
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Suppose that
\alpha
is fixed and not equal to the slope of any saddle connection of
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
G_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}
be an open disk of radius
r>0
, and consider the set

\{T(G_{1};Q):Q\in\mathcal{P}\mbox{ not pathological in direction }(1,\alpha)\}.

(3.1)

We first show that this set is bounded.

Suppose, on the contrary, that the set (
3.1
) is not bounded.
Then there exists a sequence of points
Q_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
,
i=1,2,3,\ldots,
such that
T(G_{1};Q_{i})\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
.
Since
\mathcal{P}
is compact, there exists a subsequence
Q_{i_{j}}
,
j=1,2,3,\ldots,
such that

Q_{i_{j}}\to Q_{\infty}
as
j\to\infty
for some point
Q_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}
and

T(G_{1};Q_{i_{j}})\to\infty\quad\mbox{as $j\to\infty$}.

(3.2)

The limit point
Q_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}
may be non-pathological or pathological.

Suppose that
Q_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}
is pathological.
This means that either
Q_{\infty}
is a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}
and so there is no unique geodesic in the direction
(1,\alpha)

that starts from
Q_{\infty}
, or the geodesic in the direction
(1,\alpha)
and starting from
Q_{\infty}
hits a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}

and becomes undefined thereafter.
However, there are only finitely many ways to extend the geodesic in the direction
(1,\alpha)
and starting from
Q_{\infty}
beyond the singular vertex.

Let
G_{2}\subset\mathcal{P}
be an open disk of radius
r/2
and with the same centre as
G_{1}
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
is one such extension of the geodesic from
Q_{\infty}
.
Let
Q^{(\kappa)}\in\mathcal{P}
be a point beyond the singular vertex on
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
.
Since
\alpha
is not the slope of any saddle connection of
\mathcal{P}
, it follows that
Q^{(\kappa)}
is non-pathological.
Let
d=d(Q_{\infty},Q^{(\kappa)})
be the length of the extended geodesic segment of
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
from
Q_{\infty}
to
Q^{(\kappa)}
.
Then an initial geodesic segment of
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
of length
d+T(G_{2};Q^{(\kappa)})
visits
G_{2}
.
Furthermore, apart from very short segments near the singular vertex it intersects, this finite geodesic segment of
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}

from
Q_{\infty}
to
G_{2}
has a positive distance
\delta^{(\kappa)}
from other singular vertices of
\mathcal{P}
.
Let
\delta_{\kappa}=\min\{\delta^{(\kappa)},r/2\}
.

Consider next a half-infinite geodesic
\mathcal{L}_{j}
in the direction
(1,\alpha)
and starting from a point
Q_{i_{j}}
in the

\delta_{\kappa}
-neighbourhood of
Q_{\infty}
.
If
\mathcal{L}_{j}
does not split from
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
up to
Q^{(\kappa)}
, then it does not split from
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
before it reaches
G_{1}
.
More precisely, if an extended geodesic segment of length
d+T(G_{2};Q^{(\kappa)})
starting from
Q_{\infty}
reaches a point
Q_{\infty}^{*}\in G_{2}
,
then a geodesic segment of length
d+T(G_{2};Q^{(\kappa)})
starting from
Q_{i_{j}}
reaches a point in the
\delta_{\kappa}
-neighbourhood of
Q_{\infty}^{*}
.
It is clear that any point in this
\delta_{\kappa}
-neighbourhood of
Q_{\infty}^{*}
is a distance less that
\delta_{\kappa}+r/2\leqslant r
from the centre of
G_{2}
,
which is also the centre of
G_{1}
, and so is contained in
G_{1}
.
It follows that

T(G_{1};Q_{i_{j}})\leqslant d+T(G_{2};Q^{(\kappa)}).

(3.3)

There are only finitely many possible extensions
\mathcal{L}^{(\kappa)}
,
\kappa\in\mathscr{K}
, of the geodesic from
Q_{\infty}
.
Let

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\delta^{*}=\min_{\kappa\in\mathscr{K}}\delta_{\kappa}>0.

Then there are clearly infinitely many values of
j=1,2,3,\ldots
such that
Q_{i_{j}}
is in the
\delta^{*}
-neighbourhood of
Q_{\infty}
.
It follows that there exists
\kappa\in\mathscr{K}
such that the inequality (
3.3
) holds for infinitely many values of
j
.
This clearly contradicts (
3.2
).

For the case when
Q_{\infty}
is not pathological, there is a half-infinite geodesic in the direction
(1,\alpha)
that starts from
Q_{\infty}
.
This essentially means that there is only one value of
\kappa
, and we simply take
Q^{(\kappa)}=Q_{\infty}
and
d=0
.

This completes the deduction that the set (
3.1
) is bounded.
It is also clear that a similar argument shows that both sets

\{T(G_{2};Q):Q\in\mathcal{P}\mbox{ not pathological in direction }(1,\alpha)\}

and

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\{T(G_{2};Q):Q\in\mathcal{P}\mbox{ not pathological in direction }(-1,-\alpha)\}

are bounded.
Let
T^{*}(G_{2})
be a finite upper bound of the two sets.

A point
Q\in\mathcal{P}
cannot be pathological in both directions
(1,\alpha)
and
(-1,-\alpha)
, unless
Q
is a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}
.
It then follows that every geodesic segment of slope
\alpha
and length
T^{*}(G_{2})
that does not contain a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}

visits
G_{2}
, and so visits
G_{1}
in total length at least
r/2
.

Next, let
G\subset\mathcal{P}
be a non-empty open set.
Then there exists an open disk
G_{1}\subset G
with some radius
r>0
.
Let
L
be a geodesic segment on
\mathcal{P}
with length

|L|\geqslant 2T^{*}(G_{2}).

(3.4)

If
L
contains a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}
, then this can only be one of the two endpoints.
It follows that
L
contains at least

\left[\frac{|L|}{T^{*}(G_{2})}\right]-1=k\geqslant 1

distinct geodesic segments
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}
, each of length
T^{*}(G_{2})
and not containing a singular vertex of
\mathcal{P}
.
Each of
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k}
visits
G_{1}
in total length at least
r/2
, so
L
visits
G
in total length at least

\frac{rk}{2}=\frac{r}{2}\left(\left[\frac{|L|}{T^{*}(G_{2})}\right]-1\right)\geqslant\frac{r}{4}\left[\frac{|L|}{T^{*}(G_{2})}\right]\geqslant\mathfrak{F}|L|,

(3.5)

where

xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx

\mathfrak{F}=\mathfrak{F}(G;\mathcal{P};\alpha)=\frac{r}{8T^{*}(G_{2})}.

(3.6)

The theorem follows on combining (
3.4
)–(
3.6
).
∎

3

Then every non-empty open set

Some Remarks on the Regularized Hamiltonian for Three Bosons with Contact Interactions Thanks: The authors acknowledge the support of the GNFM Gruppo Nazionale per la Fisica Matematica - INdAM
Daniele Ferretti, Alessandro Teta

Abstract
. We discuss some properties of a model Hamiltonian for a system of three bosons interacting via zero-range forces in three dimensions. In order to avoid the well known instability phenomenon, we consider a regularized Hamiltonian with a repulsive three-body force. We review the main result recently obtained in [ 1 ] where, starting from a suitable quadratic form Q Q , the self-adjoint and bounded from below Hamiltonian ℋ \mathcal{H} is constructed provided that the strength γ \gamma of the three-body repulsion is larger than a threshold parameter γ c \gamma_{c}\mspace{1.5mu} . We also show that the threshold value γ c \gamma_{c} found in [ 1 ] is optimal, in the sense that the quadratic form Q Q is unbounded from below if γ < γ c \gamma<\gamma_{c}\mspace{1.5mu} . Finally, we give an alternative and much simpler proof of the result in [ 1 ] whenever γ > γ c ′ \gamma>\gamma^{\prime}_{c}\mspace{1.5mu} , with γ c ′ \gamma^{\prime}_{c} strictly larger than γ c \gamma_{c}\mspace{1.5mu} .



Keywords: Zero-range interactions; Three-body Hamiltonians; Schrödinger operators.

1.
Introduction

In this note we discuss some properties of a model Hamiltonian describing the dynamics of three identical bosons interacting via zero-range forces in dimension three.
Since the seminal papers by Minlos and Faddeev (
[
12
,
13
]
), it is known that a natural candidate for such Hamiltonian turns out to be unbounded from below, giving rise to the so-called Thomas effect.
Here natural means that the boundary condition defining the Hamiltonian (known as TMS boundary condition) is the direct generalization to the three-body case of the boundary condition characterizing the Hamiltonian of the two-body problem.
Roughly speaking, the reason of such instability is due to the interaction becoming too singular as all the three particles are close to each other.
We note that this pathology is absent in dimension one, where perturbation theory of quadratic forms can be used, and in dimension two, where the renormalized two-body boundary condition is sufficient to avoid the collapse (see e.g.
[
3
,
5
]
).

It is worth to underline that the construction of a self-adjoint and bounded from below Hamiltonian for three, or more, interacting bosons with zero-range forces in dimension three is a challenging open problem in Mathematical Physics.
Following a suggestion contained in
[
12
]
, it has been recently studied (
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
1
]
) a regularized version of the Hamiltonian for a system of three bosons (see also
[
6
]
for the case of
N
bosons interacting with an impurity).
The main idea is to introduce a three-body repulsion that reduces to zero the strength of the contact interaction between two particles if the third particle approaches the common position of the first two.
On the other hand, when the third particle is far enough, the usual two-body point interaction is restored.
The result is that the regularized Hamiltonian is self-adjoint and bounded from below if the strength
\gamma
of the three-body interaction is larger than a threshold value
\gamma_{c}\mspace{1.5mu}
.

The aim of this paper is to describe the construction of such regularized Hamiltonian following the approach developed in
[
1
]
and also to prove two further results.
More precisely, in section
2
we introduce the notation and we formulate the main result of
[
1
]
, essentially based on the analysis of a suitable quadratic form
Q
.

In section
3
we prove that the threshold value
\gamma_{c}
obtained in
[
1
]
is optimal, in the sense that for
\gamma<\gamma_{c}
the quadratic form
Q
is unbounded from below.

In section
4
we give a different proof of the main result in
[
1
]
based on a new approach in position space.
The proof is surely less general since it is valid only for
\gamma>\gamma^{\prime}_{c}\mspace{1.5mu}
, where
\gamma^{\prime}_{c}>\gamma_{c}\mspace{1.5mu}
.
On the other hand it has the advantage to be much simpler and to show that the choice of the three-body force is not arbitrary but it is dictated by the inherent singularity of the problem.

2.
Regularized Hamiltonian

Let us consider a system composed of three identical spinless bosons of mass
\frac{1}{2}
in three dimensions and let us fix the center of mass reference frame so that
\bm{x}_{1}
,
\bm{x}_{2}
and
\bm{x}_{3}=-\bm{x}_{1}-\bm{x}_{2}
represent the Cartesian coordinates of the three particles.
We also introduce the Jacobi coordinates

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\bm{r}_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\epsilon_{ijk}(\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{j}),\\
\bm{\rho}_{k}=\frac{3}{2}\,\bm{x}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\bm{x}_{\ell},\end{cases}\qquad k\in\{1,2,3\}

where
\epsilon_{ijk}
is the Levi-Civita symbol,
so that one has the following identities

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\bm{r}_{k\pm 1}=-\frac{1}{2}\bm{r}_{k}\mp\bm{\rho}_{k},\\
\bm{\rho}_{k\pm 1}=\pm\frac{3}{4}\bm{r}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\rho}_{k},\end{cases}\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1.5mu}\diagup_{\mspace{-4.5mu}\{3\}}.

Denoting by
\bm{x}=\bm{r}_{1}=\bm{x}_{2}-\bm{x}_{3}
and
\bm{y}=\bm{\rho}_{1}=\bm{x}_{1}-\frac{\bm{x}_{2}\,+\,\bm{x}_{3}}{2}
, the Hilbert space of the system is

(2.3)

L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{6}):=\left\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\,\big|\;\psi(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\psi(-\bm{x},\bm{y})=\psi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}+\bm{y},\tfrac{3}{4}\,\bm{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{y}\right)\right\}\!.

Indeed, notice that the symmetry conditions in (
2.3
) corresponds to the exchange of particles
2,3
and
1,2
that implies also the condition
\psi(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\psi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}-\bm{y},-\tfrac{3}{4}\,\bm{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{y}\right)
, associated with the exchange of particles
3,1
.
If the bosons interact via zero-range forces, then the system is described, at least formally, by the Hamiltonian

(2.4)

\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{H}_{0}+\mu\mspace{1.5mu}\delta(\bm{x})+\mu\mspace{1.5mu}\delta(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}+\bm{y})+\mu\mspace{1.5mu}\delta(\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}-\bm{y})

where
\mu\!\in\!\mathbb{R}
is a coupling constant and
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the free Hamiltonian of the system, i.e.

(2.5)

\mathcal{H}_{0}=-\Delta_{\bm{x}}-\tfrac{3}{4}\,\Delta_{\bm{y}}.

In order to define a rigorous counterpart of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
, one needs to build a perturbation of the free Hamiltonian supported on the coincidence hyperplanes

(2.6a)

\pi_{k}:=\left\{(\bm{r}_{k},\bm{\rho}_{k})\!\in\mathbb{R}^{6}\,\big|\;\bm{r}_{k}\!=\bm{0}\right\},\qquad\pi:=\textstyle{\bigcup_{k=1}^{3}\pi_{k}}

or, equivalently,

(2.6b)

\begin{split}\pi_{1}:=\!\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\!\in\mspace{-1.5mu}\mathbb{R}^{6}\right.&\left.\!\big|\;\bm{x}=\bm{0}\right\}\!,\quad\pi_{2}:=\!\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\!\in\mspace{-1.5mu}\mathbb{R}^{6}\,\big|\;\bm{y}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}\right\}\!,\\
&\pi_{3}:=\!\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\!\in\mspace{-1.5mu}\mathbb{R}^{6}\,\big|\;\bm{y}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}\right\}\!.\end{split}

In other words, we look for a self-adjoint and bounded from below extension in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
of the following symmetric and densely defined operator

(2.7)

\dot{\mathcal{H}}_{0}:=\mathcal{H}_{0}\big\rvert_{\mathscr{D}(\dot{\mathcal{H}}_{0})},\qquad\mathscr{D}(\dot{\mathcal{H}}_{0}):=H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{6}\setminus\pi)\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{6})

that is closed according to the graph norm of
\mathcal{H}_{0},\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H}_{0})=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
.
In particular, we are interested in the family of self-adjoint extensions studied in
[
1
]
(see also
[
11
]
) which, at least formally, are characterized by the boundary condition

(2.8)

\psi(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{\xi(\bm{y})}{\lvert\bm{x}\rvert}+\alpha(\bm{y})\mspace{1.5mu}\xi(\bm{y})+o(1),\qquad\lvert\bm{x}\rvert\rightarrow 0,

where
\alpha
is a position dependent parameter given by

(2.9)

\alpha:\bm{y}\longmapsto-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}}+\frac{\gamma}{\lvert\bm{y}\rvert}\,\theta(\lvert\bm{y}\rvert)

with
\mathfrak{a}
the two-body scattering length,
\gamma
a positive parameter representing the strength of the regularization and
\theta
a real measurable function with compact support such that

(2.10)

1-\frac{s}{b}\leq\theta(s)\leq 1+\frac{s}{b}\,,\qquad s\geq 0

for some
b\!>\mspace{-1.5mu}0
.
Notice that assumption (
2.10
) forces the function
\theta
to be continuous at zero, with
\theta(0)=1
.
Moreover, the simplest choice for
\theta
is the characteristic function of the ball of radius
b
centered in the origin. We also stress that, due to the symmetry constraints of
L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
, the boundary condition (
2.8
) implies

\displaystyle\psi(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{\xi(\bm{x})}{\left\lvert\bm{y}+\frac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}\right\rvert}+\alpha(\bm{x})\mspace{1.5mu}\xi(\bm{x})+o(1),\;\;\;\qquad\bm{y}\rightarrow-\tfrac{1}{2}\mspace{1.5mu}\bm{x},

\displaystyle\psi(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{\xi(-\bm{x})}{\left\lvert\bm{y}-\frac{1}{2}\,\bm{x}\right\rvert}+\alpha(-\bm{x})\mspace{1.5mu}\xi(-\bm{x})+o(1),\qquad\bm{y}\rightarrow\tfrac{1}{2}\mspace{1.5mu}\bm{x}.

Observe that for
\gamma=0
equation (
2.8
) reduces to the standard TMS boundary condition, which leads to the Thomas effect. Then, for
\gamma>0
we are introducing a three-body repulsion meant to regularize the ultraviolet singularity occurring when the positions of all particles coincide.
However, since
\mathrm{supp}\,\theta
is a compact, the usual two-body point interaction is restored when the third particle is far enough.

The procedure adopted in
[
1
]
for the rigorous construction of the Hamiltonian is the following: one first introduces the quadratic form
Q
in
L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
describing, at least formally, the expectation value of the energy of our three-body system.
Then one defines a suitable form domain
\mathscr{D}(Q)
and proves that
Q,\mathscr{D}(Q)
is closed and bounded from below.
Finally, the Hamiltonian is defined as the unique self-adjoint and bounded from below operator associated to the quadratic form.

In order to define the quadratic form

(2.12a)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{diag}}[\xi]

\displaystyle:=12\pi\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mspace{-7.5mu}d\bm{p}\,\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{4}\mspace{1.5mu}p^{2}+\lambda\mspace{1.5mu}}\,\lvert\hat{\xi}(\bm{p})\rvert^{2},

(2.12b)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{off}}[\xi]

\displaystyle:=-\frac{12}{\pi}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mspace{-7.5mu}d\bm{p}\mspace{1.5mu}d\bm{q}\;\frac{\overline{\hat{\xi}(\bm{p})}\,\hat{\xi}(\bm{q})}{p^{2}+q^{2}+\bm{p}\!\cdot\mspace{-1.5mu}\bm{q}+\lambda},

(2.12c)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{reg}}[\xi]

\displaystyle:=\frac{6\gamma}{\pi}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mspace{-7.5mu}d\bm{p}\mspace{1.5mu}d\bm{q}\;\frac{\overline{\hat{\xi}(\bm{p})}\,\hat{\xi}(\bm{q})}{|\bm{p}-\bm{q}|^{2}},

(2.12d)

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}[\xi]

\displaystyle:=12\pi\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mspace{-7.5mu}d\bm{y}\>\beta(\bm{y})\lvert\xi(\bm{y})\rvert^{2},\qquad\beta:\bm{y}\longmapsto-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}}+\gamma\,\frac{\theta(y)-1}{y}.

By assumption (

The proof of the fact that

The conclusion is that there exists

3.
Optimality of
\gamma_{c}

In this section we prove the optimality of the threshold parameter
\gamma_{c}
defined by (
2.13
).
More precisely our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Whenever
\gamma<\gamma_{c}
, the quadratic form
Q
given by (
2.17
) is unbounded from below.

In order to achieve the result, we shall adapt the ideas contained in
[
8
, section 5]
.
Denote for short
G^{\lambda}\!:=\mspace{-1.5mu}G(-\lambda)
for any
\lambda\!>\!0
and let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\,\mathbb{N}}\!\subset\!\mathscr{D}(Q)
be a sequence of trial functions given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle u_{n}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=(G^{\lambda}\mspace{1.5mu}\eta_{n})(\bm{x},\bm{y}),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\eta_{n}(\bm{y})=n^{2}\mspace{1.5mu}f(n\mspace{1.5mu}\bm{y}),\qquad f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We stress that, by an explicit estimate due to (
2.16
), one finds

(3.3)

\inf_{n\in\mspace{1.5mu}\mathbb{N}}\,\lVert G^{\lambda}\eta_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}_{\mathrm{sym}}(\mathrm{R}^{6})}\gneq 0.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\lVert G^{\lambda}\eta_{n}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mspace{-7.5mu}d\bm{k}d\bm{p}\>\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{\left\lvert\hat{f}\!\left(\tfrac{\bm{p}}{n}\right)+\hat{f}\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(\tfrac{\bm{k}}{n}-\tfrac{\bm{p}}{2n}\right)+\hat{f}\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(\tfrac{\bm{k}}{n}+\tfrac{\bm{p}}{2n}\right)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left(k^{2}+\frac{3}{4}p^{2}+\lambda\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mspace{-7.5mu}d\bm{\kappa}d\mspace{-1.5mu}\bm{q}\;\frac{\left\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{q})+\hat{f}\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(\bm{\kappa}-\tfrac{\bm{q}}{2}\right)+\hat{f}\mspace{-1.5mu}\left(\bm{\kappa}+\tfrac{\bm{q}}{2}\right)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left(\kappa^{2}+\frac{3}{4}q^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{n^{2}}\right)^{2}}>\lVert G^{\lambda}f\rVert^{2}.

Our goal is to show that whenever
\gamma
is smaller than the threshold value
\gamma_{c}
given by (
2.13
), one has

(3.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\,+\infty}Q[u_{n}]=-\infty,

According to (
2.17

\lim_{n\rightarrow\,+\infty}\Phi^{\lambda}[\eta_{n}]=-\infty.

Proof.

First of all, we can neglect the bounded component

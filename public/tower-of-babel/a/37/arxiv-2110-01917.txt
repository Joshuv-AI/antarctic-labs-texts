Introduction

In this paper we obtain quantitative weighted
L^{p}
-inequalities for some operators involving convolutions products in the Bessel setting. We obtain the

We now define the operators we are going to deal with. Let
\lambda>0
. We denote by
m_{\lambda}
the measure on
(0,\infty)
defined by
dm_{\lambda}=x^{2\lambda}dx
.
m_{\lambda}
is doubling with respect to the usual metric in
(0,\infty)
defined by the absolute value
|\cdot|
(see for instance
[
68
, (1.5)]
). In other words the triple
((0,\infty),|\cdot|,m_{\lambda})
is a space of homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss (
[
19
]
).

For every
f,g\in L^{1}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
we define the convolution product
f\#_{\lambda}g
of
f
and
g
by

(f\#_{\lambda}g)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(y)\;_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(g)(y)y^{2\lambda}dy,\;\;\;\;x\in(0,\infty),

where, for every
x\in(0,\infty)
,

{}_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(g)(y)=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+1/2)}{\Gamma(\lambda)\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\pi}g(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta})\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta d\theta,\;\;\;y\in(0,\infty).

The main properties of
\#_{\lambda}
and
{}_{\lambda}\tau_{x}
,
x\in(0,\infty)
, can be found in
[
35
]
and
[
36
]
. We define the Hankel transform
h_{\lambda}f
of
f\in L^{1}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
as follows

h_{\lambda}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(xy)^{-\lambda+1/2}J_{\lambda-1/2}(xy)f(y)y^{2\lambda}dy,\;\;\;\;x\in(0,\infty),

where
J_{\nu}
represents the Bessel function of first kind and order
\nu>-1
.
h_{\lambda}
plays in the harmonic analysis in the Bessel setting the same role than the one of the Fourier transform in the Euclidean harmonic analysis. The operations
\#_{\lambda}
and
{}_{\lambda}\tau_{x}
,
x\in(0,\infty)
, are usually named Hankel convolution and Hankel translation because the following equalities hold for every
f,g\in L^{1}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
(
[
35
]
and
[
36
]
)

h_{\lambda}(f\#_{\lambda}g)=h_{\lambda}(f)h_{\lambda}(g),

and

h_{\lambda}(_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(g))(y)=2^{\lambda-1/2}\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})(xy)^{-\lambda+1/2}J_{\lambda-1/2}(xy)h_{\lambda}(g)(y),\quad x,y\in(0,\infty).

We now consider the Bessel operator
\Delta_{\lambda}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\frac{d}{dx}
on
(0,\infty)
.
h_{\lambda}
,
\#_{\lambda}
and
{}_{\lambda}\tau_{x}
,
x\in(0,\infty)
, are connected with
\Delta_{\lambda}
. For every
f,g\in S(0,\infty)
, the Schwartz space on
(0,\infty)
, we have that

h_{\lambda}(\Delta_{\lambda}f)(x)=x^{2}h_{\lambda}(f)(x),\quad x\in(0,\infty),

\Delta_{\lambda}(f\#_{\lambda}g)=\Delta_{\lambda}(f)\#_{\lambda}g,

and, for every
x\in(0,\infty)
,

\Delta_{\lambda}(_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(g))=\;_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(\Delta_{\lambda}g).

The heat and Poisson semigroups associated with
\Delta_{\lambda}
are
\#_{\lambda}
-convolution semigroups. Indeed, if
\{e^{-t\Delta_{\lambda}}\}_{t>0}
represents the semigroup generated by

we have that

e^{-t\Delta_{\lambda}}(f)=W_{\sqrt{2t}}^{\lambda}\#_{\lambda}f,\quad t>0,

where

W^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{2^{1/2-\lambda}}{\Gamma(\lambda+1/2)}e^{-x^{2}/2},\quad x\in(0,\infty).

The Poisson semigroup
\{e^{-t\sqrt{\Delta_{\lambda}}}\}_{t>0}
associated with
\Delta_{\lambda}
is defined by

e^{-t\sqrt{\Delta_{\lambda}}}(f)=P_{t}^{\lambda}\#_{\lambda}f,\quad t>0,

being

P^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{2\lambda\Gamma(\lambda)}{\Gamma(\lambda+1/2)\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{\lambda+1}},\quad x\in(0,\infty).

If
\phi

(0,\infty)

Let

by

Let
\rho>2
. The
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}^{\lambda}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})
associated with

is defined, for every

V_{\rho}^{\lambda}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})(f)(x)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t_{n}<t_{n-1}<\cdots<t_{1}\\

Maximal and
\rho
-variation operators and square functions for families of operators have had a considerable amount of attention in probability, ergodic theory, and harmonic analysis. As it is well-known the maximal operators
\phi_{*}^{\lambda}
are related with the convergence of
\{f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{t}\}_{t>0}
, for instance, as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
. If
\phi_{*}^{\lambda}
is bounded in
L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
for some
1\leq p<\infty
, the almost everywhere convergence of
\{f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{t}\}_{t>0}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
is got, for every
f\in L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
when the almost everywhere convergence is known for every
f\in\mathcal{A}
being
\mathcal{A}
a dense subset of
L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
. Also, the
L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
-boundedness of the
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}^{\lambda}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})
implies almost everywhere convergence of
\{f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{t}\}_{t>0}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, for every
f\in L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
but in this case it is not necessary to know the almost everywhere convergence property for
f
in some dense subset in
L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
. Furthermore the
\rho
-variation operator
V_{\rho}^{\lambda}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})
gives extra information about the convergence of
\{f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{t}\}_{t>0}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
. For instance
L^{p}
-boundedness properties of
V_{\rho}^{\lambda}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})
allow us to obtain estimates of the
\alpha
-jump that
\{f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{t}\}_{t>0}
has as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
L^{p}
-boundedness properties for the square function

lead to
L^{p}
-estimates for spectral multipliers for Bessel operators.

We recall the definition of systems of dyadic cubes. Let
\delta\in(0,1)
and
0<a\leq A<\infty
. A system of dyadic cubes with parameters
(\delta,a,A)
is a family
\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\mathcal{D}_{k}
where
\mathcal{D}_{k}=\{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\subset(0,\infty)\;\mbox{measurable},\;\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{k}\}
being
\mathcal{A}_{k}
countable, for every
k\in\mathbb Z
, and the following properties are satisfied

(i)

Q_{\alpha}^{k}\cap Q_{\beta}^{k}=\emptyset
,
\alpha\neq\beta
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb Z
and
\displaystyle(0,\infty)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{k}}Q_{\alpha}^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb Z
.

(ii)

If
\ell\geq k
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
, then either
Q_{\alpha}^{\ell}\subseteq Q_{\beta}^{k}
or
Q_{\alpha}^{\ell}\cap Q_{\beta}^{k}=\emptyset
.

(iii)

There exists
M\in\mathbb N
such that for every
k\in\mathbb Z
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{k}

\mbox{card}\{\beta\in\mathcal{A}_{k+1}:\;Q_{\beta}^{k+1}\subseteq Q_{\alpha}^{k}\}\leq M,

and
\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}^{k}=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}_{k+1},Q\subseteq Q_{\alpha}^{k}}Q
.

(iv)

For every
k\in\mathbb Z
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
there exists
x_{\alpha}^{k}\in(0,\infty)
such that

B(x_{\alpha}^{k},a\delta^{k})\subseteq Q_{\alpha}^{k}\subseteq B(x_{\alpha}^{k},A\delta^{k}).

Furthermore, if
\ell\geq k
and
Q_{\beta}^{\ell}\subseteq Q_{\alpha}^{k}
, then
B(x_{\beta}^{\ell},A\delta^{\ell})\subseteq B(x_{\alpha}^{k},A\delta^{k})
.

The set
Q_{\alpha}^{k}
is usually named a dyadic cube of generation
k
with center at
x_{\alpha}^{k}\in Q_{\alpha}^{k}
and sidelength
\delta^{k}
.

Main properties of dyadic cubes in quasimetric spaces with the geometric doubling property can be found in
[
37
]
(see also
[
18
]
).

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a system of dyadic cubes and
0<\eta<1
. As in
[
23
]
we say that a collection
S\subset\mathcal{D}
is
\eta
-sparse when there exists
c\geq 1
such that for every
Q\in S
there exists a measurable set
E_{Q}\subset Q
such that
m_{\lambda}(E_{Q})\geq\eta m_{\lambda}(Q)
and

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in S}\mathcal{X}_{E_{Q}}(x)\leq c,\;\;\;\;x\in(0,\infty).

If
S\subset\mathcal{D}
is
\eta
-sparse, the sparse operator
\mathfrak{A}_{S}
is defined by

\mathfrak{A}_{S}f(x)=\sum_{Q\in S}f_{Q}\mathcal{X}_{Q}(x),\quad x\in(0,\infty).

Here, as above,
f_{Q}=\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(Q)}\int_{Q}f(x)x^{2\lambda}dx
,
Q\in S
.

Quantitative
L^{p}
-weighted estimates for sparse operators were established in
[
56
, Proposition 4.1]
.

In order to use the sparse domination strategy we take into account that the operators that we study can be seen as Banach valued linear operators. Indeed, let
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
be a Banach space of measurable functions defined in
(0,\infty)
. We define

\mathcal{P}_{\phi;E}^{\lambda}(f)(x)=\|f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{\centerdot}(x)\|_{E},\quad x\in(0,\infty),

and

\mathcal{C}_{\phi,b;E}^{\lambda,m}(f)(x)=\|[(b(\cdot)-b(x))^{m}f]\#_{\lambda}\phi_{\centerdot}(x)\|_{E},\quad x\in(0,\infty).

we have that

(i)

\phi_{*}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{P}_{\phi;L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}^{\lambda}
and
\phi_{*,b}^{\lambda,m}=\mathcal{C}_{\phi,b;L^{\infty}(0,\infty)}^{\lambda,m}
.

(ii)

g_{\phi}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{P}_{\phi;L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}^{\lambda}
and
g_{\phi,b}^{\lambda,m}=\mathcal{C}_{\phi,b;L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}^{\lambda,m}
.

(iii)

We define
v_{\rho}
as the space of measurable functions
g:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb C
such that

\|g\|_{v_{\rho}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<t_{n}<t_{n-1}<...<t_{1}\\
n\in\mathbb N\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}|g(t_{k})-g(t_{k+1})|^{\rho}\right)^{1/\rho}<\infty.

By identifying the constant functions the space
(v_{\rho},\|\cdot\|_{v_{\rho}})
is Banach. Furthermore,

V_{\rho}^{\lambda}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})=\mathcal{P}_{\phi;v_{\rho}}^{\lambda}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;V_{\rho,b}^{\lambda,m}(\{\phi_{t}\}_{t>0})=\mathcal{C}_{\phi,b;v_{\rho}}^{\lambda,m}.

In the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, that we write in the next sections, the results in
[
23
]
and
[
56
]
concerning to sparse domination on spaces of homogeneous type in a vector valued setting play an important role.

Throughout this paper
C
will always represents a positive constant that can change in each occurrence.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In
[
49
]
a sparse domination principle was given for an operator
T
by using the grand maximal truncated operator
\mathcal{M}_{T}
of
T
defined by

\mathcal{M}_{T}(f)(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\quad X\in\mathbb R^{n},

that can be established as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
49
, Theorem 4.2]
).
Let
1\leq q\leq r<\infty
. Assume that
T
is a sublinear operator that is bounded from
L^{q}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})
and such that
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is bounded from
L^{r}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})
. Then, for every compactly supported
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb R^{n})
, there exists a sparse family
S
such that

|Tf(x)|\leq C\sum_{Q\in S}\langle f\rangle_{r,Q}\mathcal{X}_{Q}(x),\quad\mbox{a.e. }x\in\mathbb R^{n},

where
C=C_{n,q,r}\left(\|T\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb R^{n})\rightarrow L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})}+\|\mathcal{M}_{T}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb R^{n})\rightarrow L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})}\right)
.

Here,
\langle f\rangle_{r,Q}=\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)|^{r}dx\right)^{1/r}
,
Q\in S
.

In
[
51
]
Theorem
2.1
was improved by weakening some of the hypotheses. The operator
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is replaced by the one
\mathcal{M}_{T,\alpha}^{\#}
defined for every
\alpha>0
by

\mathcal{M}_{T,\alpha}^{\#}(f)(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\;\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup\;}}_{x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in Q}|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(\alpha Q)})(x^{\prime})-T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(\alpha Q)})(x^{\prime\prime})|,\quad x\in\mathbb R^{n},

(see
[
51
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Note that the operators
\mathcal{M}_{T}
and
\mathcal{M}_{T,3}^{\#}
are connected as follows. Let
Q
be a cube in
\mathbb R^{n}
and
x\in Q
. We have that

\displaystyle|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(y)-T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(z)|

\displaystyle+|Tf(z)|+|T(f\mathcal{X}_{3Q})(z)|,\;\;\;\;y,z\in Q.

Then,

\displaystyle|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup\;}}_{y,z\in Q}|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(y)-T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(z)|

\displaystyle+\inf_{z\in Q}\left(|Tf(z)|+|T(f\mathcal{X}_{3Q})(z)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup\;}}_{y,z\in Q}|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(y)-T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(z)|

\displaystyle+C\left[\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T(f\mathcal{X}_{3Q})(z)|^{1/2}dz\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|Tf(z)|^{1/2}dz\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup\;}}_{y,z\in Q}|T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(y)-T(f\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb R^{n}\setminus(3Q)})(z)|

\displaystyle+C\left[\left(\mathcal{M}[(T(f\mathcal{X}_{3Q}))^{1/2}]\right)^{2}(x)+\left(\mathcal{M}[(Tf)]^{1/2}\right)^{2}(x)\right],

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. We get

\mathcal{M}_{T}(f)(x)\leq C\left(\mathcal{M}_{T,3}^{\#}(f)(x)+\left(\mathcal{M}[(Tf)]^{1/2})(x)\right)^{2}+\left(\mathcal{M}[(T(f\mathcal{X}_{3Q}))^{1/2}](x)\right)^{2}\right).

Suppose that
T
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})
. By using Kolmogorov inequality (
[
34
, p. 91]
) we deduce that

\mathcal{M}_{T}(f)(x)\leq C\left(\mathcal{M}_{T,3}^{\#}(f)(x)+\left(\mathcal{M}(|Tf|^{1/2})(x)\right)^{2}+\mathcal{M}(|f|)(x)\right).

By using
[
59
, Lemma 3.2]
we conclude that
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})
provided that
\mathcal{M}_{T,3}^{\#}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})
.

On the other hand it is clear that
\mathcal{M}_{T,3}^{\#}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb R^{n})
when
\mathcal{M}_{T}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb R^{n})
into
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In our Bessel contexts we consider the spaces of homogeneous type
((0,\infty),|\cdot|,m_{\lambda})
. A version of Theorem
2.1
for spaces of homogeneous type in vector valued settings was established in
[
56
, Theorem 1.1]
. We will prove that the operators under consideration in Theorem
1.1
are bounded from
L^{1}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
. Then the above comments concerning to
\mathcal{M}_{T}
and
\mathcal{M}_{T,3}^{\#}
also works in the
((0,\infty),|\cdot|,m_{\lambda})
context.

In the sequel for each
x,r\in(0,\infty)
, we write
B(x,r)
to denote the interval
(x-r,x+r)\cap(0,\infty)
, and
cB(x,r)=B(x,cr)
,

.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
\phi_{*}^{\lambda}

Assume That
\phi
satisfies (i) (see the definition of
Z^{\lambda}
). According to
[
68
, (2.4)]
we have that

|_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(\phi_{t})(y)|\leq\frac{C}{m_{\lambda}(B(x,t))+m_{\lambda}(B(x,|x-y|))}\frac{t}{t+|x-y|},\quad t,x,y\in(0,\infty).

Then,

\displaystyle|(f\#_{\lambda}\phi)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{k}t)\setminus B(x,2^{k-1}t)}|_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(\phi_{t})(y)||f(y)|y^{2\lambda}dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B(x,t)}|_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(\phi_{t})(y)||f(y)|y^{2\lambda}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(B(x,2^{k}t))}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\int_{B(x,2^{k}t)}|f(y)|y^{2\lambda}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(f)(x),\;\;\;\;t,x\in(0,\infty).

Here
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}
represents the corresponding Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator defined by

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(f)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(B(x,r))}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|y^{2\lambda}dy,\quad f\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda}).

Hence,

\phi_{*}^{\lambda}(f)(x)\leq C\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(f)(x),\quad x\in(0,\infty).

Since

\|\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{p}((0,\infty),w,m_{\lambda})}\leq C[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|f\|_{L^{p}((0,\infty),w,m_{\lambda})},

for every
f\in L^{p}((0,\infty),w,m_{\lambda})
(see, for instance,
[
10
, p. 890]
) we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
\phi_{*}^{\lambda}
. Note that here it is sufficient to assume that
\phi
satisfies (i).

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
g_{\phi}^{\lambda}

We firstly establish
L^{p}
-boundedness properties for the sublinear operator
g^{\lambda}_{\phi}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Suppose that
\phi
is a differentiable function on
(0,\infty)
satisfying the following three conditions:

(a)

|\phi(x)|\leq C(1+x^{2})^{-\lambda-1}
,
x\in(0,\infty)
;

(b)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|u^{2\lambda+2}\phi^{\prime}(u)\right|^{2}\frac{du}{u}<\infty
;

(c)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|h_{\lambda}(\phi)(u)\right|^{2}\frac{du}{u}<\infty
.

Then, the operator
g_{\phi}^{\lambda}
is bounded from
L^{p}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
into itself, for every
1<p<\infty
, and from
L^{1}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
into
L^{1,\infty}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

We have that

g^{\lambda}_{\phi}(f)(x)=\|f\#_{\lambda}\phi_{\centerdot}(x)\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})},\quad x\in(0,\infty).

In order to see the
L^{p}
-boundedness properties for
g^{\lambda}_{\phi}
we use vector valued singular integral theory (
[
62
]
).

We define

\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)f(y)y^{2\lambda}dy,\quad x,t\in(0,\infty),

where
\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)=\,_{\lambda}\tau_{x}(\phi_{t})(y)
,
x,y,t\in(0,\infty)
.

By using Minkowski inequality we get, for every
x,y\in(0,\infty)

\left\|\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)\right\|_{L^{2}\left((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t}\right)}\leq C\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{t^{2\lambda+1}}\phi\left(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}{t}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\theta.

We have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{t^{2\lambda+1}}\phi\left(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}{t}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{2\lambda+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u^{2\lambda+1}\phi(u)|^{2}\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\leq

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{C}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{2\lambda+1}},\quad x,y\in(0,\infty),\,\theta\in(0,\pi),

because
\phi
satisfies (a).

According to
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
we obtain

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int^{\pi}_{0}\frac{\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1/2}}d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{m_{\lambda}(B(x,|x-y|))},\quad x,y\in(0,\infty),\;x\not=y.

We can write for each
x,y,t\in(0,\infty)
,

\partial_{x}\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(\lambda+\frac{1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\lambda)t^{2\lambda+1}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}{t}\right)\frac{(x-y\cos\theta)\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta}{t\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}d\theta.

Since
|a+bs|\leq(a^{2}+b^{2}+2abs)^{1/2}
,
a,b\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in(-1,1)
, it follows that

\left|\partial_{x}\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{t^{2\lambda+2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}{t}\right)\right|\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta d\theta,\quad x,y,t\in(0,\infty).

By proceeding as above by using (b), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{x}\mathfrak{G}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq C\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{t^{2\lambda+2}}\phi^{\prime}\left(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}{t}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\theta

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq C\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|u^{2\lambda+2}\phi^{\prime}(u)|^{2}\frac{du}{u}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\leq C\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}d\theta,\quad x,y\in(0,\infty).

According to
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
we obtain

(2.2)

\left\|\partial_{x}\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|m_{\lambda}(B(x,|x-y|))},\quad x,y\in(0,\infty),\,x\not=y.

By symmetry we also get

(2.3)

\left\|\partial_{y}\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)\right\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|m_{\lambda}(B(x,|x-y|))},\quad x,y\in(0,\infty),\,x\not=y.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We consider for every smooth function
f
with compact support in
(0,\infty)

(2.4)

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,N}(f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)f(y)y^{2\lambda}dy,\quad x\in(0,\infty)\setminus{\rm supp}f,

where the integral is understood in the
L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
-Bochner sense. Note that, given a smooth function
f
with compact support in
(0,\infty)
and
x\in(0,\infty)\setminus{\rm supp}f
, the function

F_{x}(y)=\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)f(y)y^{2\lambda},\quad y\in(0,\infty),

is continuous from
(0,\infty)
into
L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
. Then, since
L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
is a separable space we obtain that
F_{x}
,
x\not\in{\rm\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}}f
, is a strongly measurable function. Furthermore, according to (
2.1
) the integral in (
2.4
) is
L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
-norm convergent provided that
x\notin\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f
.

Let
f
be a smooth function with compact support in
(0,\infty)
. Then,
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,N}(f)(x)\in L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
, for every
x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f
.

Consider
h\in L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
. We have that

\int_{1/N}^{N}h(t)\left[\mathcal{G}_{\phi,N}^{\lambda}(f)(x)\right](t)\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/N}^{N}h(t)\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)\frac{dt}{t}f(y)y^{2\lambda}dy,\quad x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f.

By using again (
2.1
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{1/N}^{N}|h(t)||\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)|\frac{dt}{t}|f(y)|y^{2\lambda}dy

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt\leq\|h\|_{L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(x,y)\|_{L^{2}((0,\infty),\frac{dt}{t})}|f(y)|y^{2\lambda}dy

\displaystyle\hskip-142.26378pt\leq\|h\|_{L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|f(y)|y^{2\lambda}}{m_{\lambda}(B(x,|x-y|))}dy<\infty,\quad x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f.

It follows that

\int_{1/N}^{N}h(t)[\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,N}(f)(x)](t)\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{1/N}^{N}h(t)\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(f)(x)\frac{dt}{t},\quad x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f.

Hence, for every
x\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}f
,

[\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,N}(f)(x)](t)=\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(f)(x),\quad\mbox{a.e. }t\in\big(\frac{1}{N},N\big).

We now observe that the function

G(x,y,t)=\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,t}(x,y)f(y)y^{2\lambda},\quad x,y,t\in(0,\infty),

is continuous. Then, if
x_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, we have that

\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\Big\|\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(f)(x)-\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(f)(x_{0})\Big\|_{L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})}=0.

So, as above we deduce that
\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(f):(0,\infty)\rightarrow L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N),\frac{dt}{t})
is a strongly measurable function.

By using Plancherel formula for Hankel transformation we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{G}^{\lambda}_{\phi,\centerdot}(f)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{L^{2}((\frac{1}{N},N)\frac{dt}{t})}((0,\infty),m_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\,_{\lambda}\tau_{y}(\Phi_{t})(x)\Big|\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{2\lambda-1}\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big|\Phi\Big(\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta}}{t}\Big)\Big|\frac{dt}{t^{2\lambda+2}}d\theta

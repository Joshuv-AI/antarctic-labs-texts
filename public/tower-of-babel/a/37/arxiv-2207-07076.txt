Existence results for variational-hemivariational inequality systems with nonlinear couplings
Yunru Bai a, Nicuşor Costea b, Shengda Zeng c

Abstract
In this paper we investigate a system of coupled inequalities consisting of a variational-hemivariational inequality and a quasi-hemivariational inequality on Banach spaces. The approach is topological, and a wide variety of existence results is established for both bounded and unbounded constraint sets in real reflexive Banach spaces. Applications to Contact Mechanics are provided in the last section of the paper. More precisely, we consider a contact model with (possibly) multivalued constitutive law whose variational formulation leads to a coupled system of inequalities. The weak solvability of the problem is proved via employing the theoretical results obtained in the previous section. The novelty of our approach comes from the fact that we consider two potential contact zones and the variational formulation allows us to determine simultaneously the displacement field and the Cauchy stress tensor. \subclass 35J88 39B72 58E50 70G75 74M15

Introduction and preliminaries

Let
X,{Y}
be two real reflexive Banach spaces and
K\subseteq X
,
\Lambda\subseteq{Y}
be nonempty, closed and convex subsets. Assume
B\colon X\times{Y}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a nonlinear functional,
\phi\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous function such that
D(\phi)\cap K\neq\emptyset
,
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are Banach spaces and
\gamma_{1}\colon X\to Z_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}\colon Y\to Z_{2}
are linear and compact operators,
J\colon Z_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
L\colon Z_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
are locally Lipschitz functionals,
H\colon Y\to(0,\infty)
is a given function, and
F\colon X\to X^{\ast}
,
G\colon Y\to Y^{\ast}
are prescribed nonlinear operators. In the present, we are interested in the following system of inequalities:

Find
(u,\sigma)\in\left(K\cap D(\phi)\right)\times\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}B(v,\sigma)-B(u,\sigma)+\phi(v)-\phi(u)+J^{0}(\gamma_{1}(u);\gamma_{1}(v-u))\geq\langle F(u),v-u\rangle,\forall v\in K\\
B(u,\mu)-B(u,\sigma)+H(\sigma)L^{0}(\gamma_{2}(\sigma);\gamma_{2}(\mu-\sigma))\geq\langle G(\sigma),\mu-\sigma\rangle,\forall\mu\in\Lambda.\end{array}\right.

We point out the fact that the first line of problem (
1
) represents a
nonlinear variational-hemivariational inequality
, while the second line is
a quasi-hemivariational inequality
. Observe that the unknown pair
(u,\sigma)
appears in both inequalities of (
1
) via the functional

which will be called
coupling functional
, so, the system (
1
) is called

.

To our best knowledge, the study of systems consisting of a variational inequality and a functional equations goes back to
[
13
,

]
, whereas coupled systems governed by a hemivariational inequality were first investigated by Matei
[
19
]
who established existence results (see also
[
1
]

Find
(u,\sigma)\in K\times\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle A(u),v-u\rangle+b(v-u,\sigma)+J^{0}(\gamma(u);\gamma(v)-\gamma(u))\geq\langle f,v-u\rangle,&\forall v\in K,\\
b(u,\mu-\sigma)\leq 0,&\forall\mu\in\Lambda,\end{array}\right.

where
X,Y
are reflexive Banach spaces,
K\subseteq X
and
\Lambda\subseteq Y
are nonempty closed convex constraint sets,

is a Banach space,

,

is a nonlinear operator,

is locally Lipschitz,
\gamma\colon X\to Z
is a linear and continuous operator and

is a bilinear continuous function.

We point out the fact that system (
1

[

,

The first paper that we are aware of dealing with coupled systems consisting of two hemivariational inequalities is

Find
(u,\sigma)\in X\times\Lambda

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a(u,v-u)+b(v-u,\sigma)+\phi(v)-\phi(u)+J^{0}(\gamma_{1}(u);\gamma_{1}(v)-\gamma(u))\geq(f,v-u)_{X},&\forall v\in X,\\
b(u,\mu-\sigma)-\psi(\mu)+\psi(\lambda)-L^{0}(\gamma_{2}(\lambda);\gamma_{2}(\mu-\lambda))\leq 0,&\forall\mu\in\Lambda,\end{array}\right.

where

,

is a bilinear continuous coercive form,

and

are locally Lipschitz,
\gamma_{1}\colon X\to Z

\gamma_{2}:Z_{2}\to\mathbb{R}

Note that, if we choose

Find

To our best knowledge, systems with nonlinear coupling functions were only recently considered in

Find
(u,\sigma)\in K\times\Lambda

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}B(v,\sigma)-B(u,\sigma)+\chi(u,v-u)\geq\langle f,v-u\rangle,\forall v\in K\\

Note that the second inequality of (

Moreover, besides the fact that hemivariational inequalities are a generalization of variational inequalities further difficulties occur due to the presence of the nonlinear operators

Consequently, the novelty of our approach comes on the one hand from the fact that this is the first paper dealing with coupled systems consisting of a variational-hemivariational inequality and a quasi-hemivariational inequality and, on the other hand, that the coupling functional is assumed nonlinear (and even in the bilinear case our system is similar but not equivalent to systems studied in the aforementions papers, as the second inequality is reversed.)

Finally, we point out the fact that choosing opposite sign for the second inequality of (

For a real Banach space
E
we denote its norm by
\|\cdot\|_{E}
and by
E^{\ast}
its dual space. The duality pairing between
E^{\ast}
and
E
will be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E^{\ast}\times E}
. If there is no danger of confusion the subscripts will be ignored, i.e., we will simply write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
from time to time. Particularly, if
E
is a Hilbert space, then
(\cdot,\cdot)_{E}
stands for the inner product of
E
.

Let
E,F
be two real Banach spaces. If for each
u\in E
there exists a corresponding subset
A(u)\subseteq F
, then we say
A\colon E\to 2^{F}
is a
set-valued mapping
(or
multifunction
) from
E
to
F
. For a set-valued mapping
A\colon E\to 2^{F}
we define
domain
of
A
to be the set

D(A):=\{x\in E:\ A(x)\neq\emptyset\}.

The
range
of
A
is the set

R(A):=\bigcup_{x\in E}A(x),

and the
graph
of
A
is the set

Gr(A):=\{(x,y)\in E\times F:\ \ y\in A(x)\mbox{ and }x\in D(A)\}.

The
inverse
of the set-valued mapping
A\colon E\to 2^{F}
is the set-valued mapping
A^{-1}\colon F\to 2^{E}
defined by

A^{-1}(y):=\{x\in E:\ \ y\in A(x)\}.

We point out the fact that every single-valued map
A:E\rightarrow F
may be regarded as the set-valued map
E\ni x\mapsto\{A(x)\}\in 2^{F}
. In this case we identify
A(x)
with its unique element.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\beta\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be a given functional. A set-valued mapping
A\colon E\to 2^{E^{\ast}}
is called
\beta
-relaxed dissipative if

\langle\zeta_{y}-\zeta_{x},y-x\rangle\leq\beta(y-x)\mbox{ for all }(x,\zeta_{x}),(y,\zeta_{y})\in Gr(A).

(1.12)

Remark 1.2
.

If
\beta(x):=-m\|x\|^{2}
for some
m>0
, then
A
is said to be strongly dissipative, while for
\beta\equiv 0
the set-valued mapping
A
is called dissipative. If
A
is single-valued, then (
1.12
) reduces to

\langle A(y)-A(x),y-x\rangle\leq\beta(y-x)\mbox{ for all }x,y\in E.

Moreover, we have

\bullet

if
-A
is
-\beta
-relaxed dissipative, then
A
is called
\beta
-relaxed monotone, i.e.,

\langle\zeta_{y}-\zeta_{x},y-x\rangle\geq\beta(y-x)\mbox{ for all }(x,\zeta_{x}),(y,\zeta_{y})\in Gr(A).

\bullet

if
-A
is strongly dissipative (namely,
A
is
-\beta
-relaxed dissipative and
\beta(x):=m\|x\|^{2}
for some
m>0
), then
A
is called strongly monotone, i.e.,

\langle\zeta_{y}-\zeta_{x},y-x\rangle\geq m\|y-x\|^{2}\mbox{ for all }(x,\zeta_{x}),(y,\zeta_{y})\in Gr(A).

\bullet

if
-A
is dissipative (i.e.,
\beta\equiv 0
), then
A
is called monotone, i.e.,

\langle\zeta_{y}-\zeta_{x},y-x\rangle\geq 0\mbox{ for all }(x,\zeta_{x}),(y,\zeta_{y})\in Gr(A).

\bullet

if
-A
is
-\beta
-relaxed dissipative and
\beta(x):=-m\|x\|^{2}
for some
m>0
, then
A
is called relaxed monotone with constant
m
, i.e.,

\langle\zeta_{y}-\zeta_{x},y-x\rangle\geq-m\|y-x\|^{2}\mbox{ for all }(x,\zeta_{x}),(y,\zeta_{y})\in Gr(A).

Definition 1.3
.

A single-valued operator
A\colon E\to E^{\ast}
is called hemicontinuous, if for any
u,v,w\in E
one has

\lim_{t\to 0_{+}}\langle A(u+t(v-u)),w\rangle=\langle A(u),w\rangle.

Next, we recall two kinds of set-valued mappings that play an important role in Nonsmooth Analysis, namely, the
subdifferential
of a convex functional and the
Clarke subdifferential
of a locally Lipschitz functional.

Let
\phi\colon E\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a prescribed functional. We define its
effective domain
to be the set

D(\phi):=\{x\in E:\ \ \phi(x)<+\infty\}.

Definition 1.4
.

A functional
\phi\colon E\to(-\infty,\infty]
is said to be:

\bullet
proper, if
D(\phi)\neq\emptyset
;

\bullet
lower semicontinuous, if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\phi(x_{n})\geq\phi(x)
, whenever
x_{n}\to x
;

\bullet
convex, if
\phi(tx+(1-t)y)\leq t\phi(x)+(1-t)\phi(y)
, for all
x,y\in E
and all
t\in[0,1]
;

\bullet
Gâteaux differentiable at
x\in D(\phi)
, if there exists an element
\nabla\phi(x)\in E^{\ast}
(called the gradient of
\phi
at
x
) such that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0_{+}}\frac{\phi(x+ty)-\phi(x)}{t}=\left\langle\nabla\phi(x),y\right\rangle,\mbox{ for all }y\in E.

Conversely, we say that
\phi\colon[-\infty,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is
upper semicontinuous
(resp.
concave
) if
-\phi
is lower semicontinuous (resp. convex).

The following result characterizes the Gâteaux differentiability of convex functionals.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\phi\colon E\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a Gâteaux differentiable functional. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\phi
is convex;

(ii)

\phi(y)-\phi(x)\geq\langle\nabla\phi(x),y-x\rangle
, for all
y\in E
;

(iii)

\langle\nabla\phi(y)-\nabla\phi(x),y-x\rangle\geq 0
, for all
x,y\in E
.

A direct consequence of the above result is that convex and Gâteaux differentiable functions are in fact lower semicontinuous. Proposition
1.5
also suggests the following generalization of the gradient of a convex function.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\phi:E\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be a convex function. The subdifferential of
\phi
at a point
x\in D(\phi)
is the set

\partial\phi(x):=\left\{\xi\in E^{\ast}:\ \ \langle\xi,y-x\rangle\leq\phi(y)-\phi(x)\mbox{ for all }y\in E\right\},

(1.13)

and
\partial\phi(x):=\emptyset
if
x\not\in D(\phi)
.

It is well known that if
\phi
is convex and Gâteaux differentiable at a point
x\in{\rm int\;}D(\phi)
, then
\partial\phi(x)
contains exactly one element, namely,
\partial\phi(x)=\{\nabla\phi(x)\}
. Moreover, it is well-known that the set-valued mapping
\partial\phi\colon E\to 2^{E^{*}}
is monotone, i.e.,
-\partial\phi
is dissipative.

The
Fenchel conjugate
of
\phi\colon E\to(-\infty,+\infty]
is the functional
\phi^{\ast}\colon E^{\ast}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
defined by

\phi^{\ast}(\xi):=\sup\limits_{x\in E}\left\{\langle\xi,x\rangle-\phi(x)\right\}\mbox{ for all $\xi\in E^{*}$}.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\phi\colon E\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous function. Then, the following statements are true:

(i)

\phi^{\ast}
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous;

(ii)

\phi(x)+\phi^{\ast}(\xi)\geq\langle\xi,x\rangle
, for all
x\in E,\ \xi\in E^{\ast}
;

(iii)

\xi\in\partial\phi(x)\Leftrightarrow x\in\partial\phi^{\ast}(\xi)\Leftrightarrow\phi(x)+\phi^{\ast}(\xi)=\langle\xi,x\rangle
.

Definition 1.8
.

A bipotential is a function
b\colon E\times E^{\ast}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

for any
x\in E
, if
D(b(x,\cdot))\neq\varnothing
, then
b(x,\cdot)
is convex and lower semicontinuous, and for any
\xi\in E^{\ast}
, if
D(b(\cdot,\xi))\neq\varnothing
, then
b(\cdot,\xi)
is convex and lower semicontinuous;

(ii)

for all
x\in E
and all
\xi\in E^{\ast}
it holds
b(x,\xi)\geq\langle\xi,x\rangle
;

(iii)

\xi\in\partial b(\cdot,\xi)(x)\Leftrightarrow x\in\partial b(x,\cdot)(\xi)\Leftrightarrow b(x,\xi)=\langle\xi,x\rangle
.

We recall that a function
h\colon E\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called
locally Lipschitz
if for every
x\in E
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
x
and a positive constant
c_{x}=c_{x}(U)
such that

|h(z)-h(y)|\leq c_{x}\|z-y\|\mbox{ for all }y,z\in U.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
h:E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally Lipschitz function. The generalized derivative (in the sense of Clarke) of
h
at a point
x
in the direction
y
, denoted by
h^{0}(x;y)
, is defined by

h^{0}(x;y):=\limsup\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle z\rightarrow x}}{{t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{h(z+ty)-h(z)}{t}.

Let
h\colon E_{1}\times\ldots\times E_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be locally Lipschitz with respect to the
k^{th}
variable. So, in the sequel, we denote by
h^{0}_{,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},\ldots,x_{n};y_{k})
the
partial generalized derivative
of
h
with respect to the
k^{th}
variable, that is,

h^{0}_{,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},\ldots,x_{n};y_{k}):=\limsup\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle z_{k}\rightarrow x_{k}}}{{t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{h(x_{1},\ldots,z_{k}+ty_{k},\ldots,x_{n})-h(x_{1},\ldots,z_{k},\ldots,x_{n})}{t}.

The next results recall some important properties of the generalized derivative and subgradient in the sense of Clarke, (for the details proof see Clarke
[

]
).

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
h\colon E\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally Lipschitz function such that
c_{x}>0
is the constant near the point
x\in E
. Then, we have

(i)

the function
y\mapsto h^{0}(x;y)
is finite, positively homogeneous, subadditive and satisfies

|h^{0}(x;y)|\leq c_{x}\|y\|\mbox{ for all $x,y\in E$};

(ii)

the function

is upper semicontinuous;

(iii)

(-h)^{0}(x;y)=h^{0}(x;-y)
.

Definition 1.11
.

The Clarke subdifferential of a locally Lipschitz function
h\colon E\to{\mathbb{R}}
at a point
x
, denoted by
\partial_{C}h(x)
, is the following subset of

\partial_{C}h(x):=\left\{\xi\in E^{\ast}:\ \ \langle\xi,y\rangle\leq h^{0}(x;y)\mbox{ for all }y\in E\right\}.

Let
h\colon E_{1}\times\ldots\times E_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be locally Lipschitz with respect to the

variable. In what follows, we use the similar way to denote the partial Clarke subdifferential of

with respect to the

variable, thus,

\partial_{C}^{k}h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\left\{\xi_{k}\in E_{k}^{\ast}:\ \ \langle\xi_{k},x_{k}\rangle\leq h^{0}_{,k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\ldots,x_{n};y_{k})\mbox{ for all }y_{k}\in E_{k}\right\}.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
h\colon E\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally Lipschitz functional. Then the following properties hold:

(i)

For each
x\in E
,
\partial_{C}h(x)
is a nonempty, convex, weak

\|\zeta\|_{E^{*}}\leq c_{x},\mbox{ for all }\zeta\in\partial_{C}h(x),

where

is the Lipschitz constant near

(ii)

For each

;

(iii)

The set-valued mapping

∗

(iv)

The set-valued mapping

Theorem 1.13

(Mosco
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a nonempty, compact and convex subset of a topological
vector space
E
and
\varphi:E\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper,
convex and lower semicontinuous functional such that
{D}(\varphi)\cap\mathcal{K}\neq\emptyset
. Assume
g,h\colon E\times E\to{\mathbb{R}}
are
two functions that satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

g(x,y)\geq h(x,y)\mbox{ for all }x,y\in E
;

(b)

y\mapsto h(x,y)
is convex for each
y\in E
;

(c)

x\mapsto g(x,y)
is upper semicontinous for
each
x\in E
.

Then, for each
a\in\mathbb{R}
the following alternative holds:

\bullet
either there exists
x_{0}\in{D}(\varphi)\cap\mathcal{K}
such that

\displaystyle g(x_{0},y)+\varphi(y)-\varphi(x_{0})\geq a\mbox{ for all }y\in E,

\bullet
or there exists
y_{0}\in E
such that
h(x_{0},x_{0})<a
.

2
Existence results for bounded and unbounded constraint sets

The primary goal of this section is to establish the existence of solutions for the nonlinear coupled system (
1
). Our approach is topological. More precisely we employ the alternative Theorem
1.13
for general equilibrium problems to obtain the existence theorems. The first set of assumptions under which we are able to establish the existence of solutions is listed below.

{\bf(H_{0})}
The functionals
\phi,J,L,H
and the operators
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

\phi\colon X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous functional such that
D(\phi)\cap K\neq\varnothing
;

(ii)

J\colon Z_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
and
L\colon Z_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
are locally Lipschitz functionals;

(iii)

\gamma_{1}\colon X\to Z_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}\colon Y\to Z_{2}
are linear and compact operators;

(iv)

H\colon Y\to(0,m_{H})
is weakly continuous, i.e.,
H(\sigma_{\alpha})\to H(\sigma)
whenever
\sigma_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup\sigma
in
Y
, with some
m_{H}>0
.

{\bf(H_{B})}

B\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}
is convex and lower semicontinuous in
X\times Y
.

{\bf(H_{F}^{1})}

F\colon X\to X^{\ast}
is a nonlinear operator such that the mapping
u\mapsto\langle F(u),v-u\rangle
is weakly lower semicontinuous for each
v\in X
, i.e.,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle F(u_{n}),v-u_{n}\rangle\geq\langle F(u),v-u\rangle,

whenever
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
X
as
n\to\infty
and
v\in X
.

{\bf(H_{G}^{1})}

G\colon Y\to Y^{\ast}
is a nonlinear operator such that the mapping
\sigma\mapsto\langle G(\sigma),\mu-\sigma\rangle
is weakly lower semicontinuous for each

\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}=u_{i}v_{i},\quad|\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}|=(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})^{1/2},\quad\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}:=(u_{i}),\ \mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}:=(v_{i})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},

and, respectively,

\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}=\tau_{ij}\sigma_{ij},\quad|\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}|=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}})^{1/2},\quad\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}:=(\tau_{ij}),\ \mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}:=(\sigma_{ij})\in{\cal S}^{m}.

Here and hereafter,
m
is a positive integer which stands for the dimension of the spatial variable, indices
i
and
j
run from
1
to
m
and the summation convention of the repeated indices is adopted. For a bounded open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with sufficiently smooth boundary
\Gamma
(normally, we assume that
\Gamma
is Lipschitz continuous) we denote by
\nu
the outward unit vector on
\Gamma
and we introduce the following function spaces which will play a key role in applications to nonsmooth mechanics problems

H:=L^{2}\left(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right),\quad{\cal H}:=\left\{\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}=(\tau_{ij}):\ \ \tau_{ij}=\tau_{ji}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)\right\}=L^{2}\left(\Omega;{\cal S}^{m}\right),

H_{1}:=\left\{\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\in H:\ \ \mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\in{\cal H}\right\}=H^{1}\left(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\right),\quad{\cal H}_{1}:=\left\{\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}\in{\cal H}:\ \ {\rm Div\;}\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}\in H\right\},

where
\varepsilon
and
{\rm Div}
are the deformation operator and the divergence operator, respectively, and are defined in the following way

\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}):=(\varepsilon_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})),\quad\varepsilon_{ij}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}):=\frac{1}{2}(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i}),\qquad{\rm Div\;}\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}=(\tau_{ij,j}),

accordingly. The index following a comma represents the partial derivative with respect to the corresponding component of
x\in\Omega
, i.e.,
u_{i,j}=\partial u_{i}/\partial x_{j}
. Keep in mind that the above mentioned spaces are Hilbert spaces endowed with the following corresponding inner products

(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{H}:=\int_{\Omega}\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\;dx,\quad(\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})_{\cal H}:=\int_{\Omega}\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\;dx,\quad(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{H_{1}}:=(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{H}+(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}),\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\cal H},

(\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})_{{\cal H}_{1}}:=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})_{\cal H}+({\rm Div\;}\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}},{\rm Div\;}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})_{H}.

We recall that the trace operator
\gamma:H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})\rightarrow H^{1/2}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})\subset L^{2}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
is linear and compact. Sometimes, for simplicity, we will omit to write
\gamma
to indicate the Sobolev trace on the boundary, i.e., writing
v
instead of
\gamma\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}
. Also, for a given
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
we denote by
v_{\nu}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}
the normal and the tangential components of
v
on the boundary, i.e.,
v_{\nu}:=\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}:=\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-v_{\nu}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}
, respectively. Similarly, for a tensor field
\sigma
, we define
\sigma_{\nu}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{\tau}
to be the normal and the tangential components of the Cauchy vector field
\sigma
\nu
, that is
\sigma_{\nu}:=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{\tau}:=\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}-\sigma_{\nu}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}
, respectively. Recall that the following Green formula holds

(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\cal H}+({\rm Div\;}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{H}=\int_{\Gamma}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\;d\Gamma,\mbox{ for all }\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in H_{1}.

(3.1)

We consider the following contact model involving a deformable body which occupies a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
(
m\geq 2
) with Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma
, which is partitioned into four disjoint measurable parts
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{3}
.

{\bf(P)}
Find a displacement
\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and a stress tensor
\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}:\Omega\rightarrow{\cal S}^{m}
such that

\displaystyle-{\rm Div\;}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}=\mathbf{f}_{0}

in

\displaystyle\Omega,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\in\partial\chi(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}))

in

\displaystyle\Omega,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}=0

on

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}=\mathbf{f}_{2}

on

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2},

(3.5)

\displaystyle u_{\nu}=0,-\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{\tau}\in\partial_{C}j(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})

on

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}^{a},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{\tau}=0,\sigma_{\nu}\leq 0,u_{\nu}\leq 0,\sigma_{\nu}u_{\nu}=0

on

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}^{b}.

(3.7)

Problem
{\bf(P)}
describes the contact between a deformable body and a foundation. Relation (
3.2
) represents the equilibrium equation, showing that the body is subjected to volume forces of density
\mathbf{f}_{0}
. The behaviour of the material is described by a nonlinear constitutive law expressed as a
(convex) subdifferential inclusion, namely, (
3.3
). On
\Gamma_{1}
the body is clamped, therefore the displacement vanishes here. Equation (
3.5
) shows that surface tractions of density
\mathbf{f}_{2}
act on
\Gamma_{2}
. On
\Gamma_{3}
the body may come in frictional contact with the foundation and this contact is modelled considering there are two potential contact zones:
\Gamma_{3}^{a}
where a nonmonotone friction law is considered; and
\Gamma_{3}^{b}
where the contact is frictionless and satisfies Signorini unilateral contact model. Note that we allow the case of only one contact zone, i.e.,
meas(\Gamma_{3}^{a})=0
or
meas(\Gamma_{3}^{b})=0
.

For examples of nonlinear constitutive laws of the form
(
3.3
)
and nonmonotone friction laws of the form (
3.6
) we refer the reader to
[
8
,
10
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

We assume that the constitutive function
\chi
and the initial data
\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{f}_{2}
satisfy the following conditions:

(\mathcal{H}_{1})

\mathbf{f}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\mathbf{f}_{2}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{2};\mathbb{R}^{m})
;

(\mathcal{H}_{2})

\chi:\mathcal{S}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex and l.s.c. functional with the property that there exist
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
such that

\alpha|\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}|^{2}\leq\chi(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\leq\beta|\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}|^{2},\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{S}^{m};

(\mathcal{H}_{3})

If
meas(\Gamma_{3}^{a})>0
, then
j:\Gamma_{3}^{a}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is functional enjoying the following properties:

(i)

\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}}\mapsto j(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},t)
is measurable on
\Gamma_{3}^{a}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

j(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},0)\in L^{1}(\Gamma_{3}^{a})
;

(iii)

there exists
p\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{3}^{a})
such that

|j(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},t_{1})-j(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},t_{2})|\leq p(x)|t_{1}-t_{2}|,\mbox{ for a.e. $\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}}\in\Gamma_{3}^{a}$ and all $t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$}.

In order to derive the variational formulation of problem
{\bf(P)}
assume
u
and
\sigma
are regular functions that satisfy (
3.2
)-(
3.7
). Multiplying (
3.2
) by
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}
and integrating over
\Omega
we apply Green’s formula to find that

(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{H}=-(\mbox{\rm Div\,}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{H}=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}-\int_{\Gamma}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}})\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})d\Gamma,

for all
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in X:=\{\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}\in H_{1}:\ \mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}=0\mbox{ a.e. on }\Gamma_{1}\}
. It is well known that
X
is a closed subspace of
H_{1}
, therefore it is a Hilbert space endowed with the inner product

(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{X}:=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}),\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}.

Keeping in mind the boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{3}
we define the set of
admissible displacements

\mathcal{K}_{0}:=\{\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}\in X:\ w_{\nu}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{3}^{a}\mbox{ and }w_{\nu}\leq 0\mbox{ on }\Gamma_{3}^{b}\},

which is nonempty, unbounded, closed and convex subset of
X
. Keeping in mind that

(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}})\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})=\sigma_{\nu}(v_{\nu}-u_{\nu})+\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{\tau}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})

for all
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
we have

-\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma=0,

-\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma=-\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma,

-\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{\tau}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma\leq\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma,

and

-\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{b}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\mbox{\boldmath{$\nu$}}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma=-\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{b}}\sigma_{\nu}(v_{\nu}-u_{\nu})\;d\Gamma=-\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{b}}\sigma_{\nu}v_{\nu}\;d\Gamma\leq 0.

Thus,

(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma\geq(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{H}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma,\quad\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}.

(3.8)

Since the stress tensor
\sigma
satisfies (
3.8
) it is natural to define now the set of
admissible stress tensors with respect to a displacement
\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
to be the following subset of
\mathcal{H}

\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}):=\left\{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{H}:\ (\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma\geq(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{H}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\;d\Gamma,\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}\right\}.

We define the separable bipotential
b:\mathcal{S}^{m}\times\mathcal{S}^{m}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
by

b(\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}):=\chi(\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}})+\chi^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}).

We point out the fact that
b
connects the constitutive law (
3.3
), the function
\chi
and its Fenchel conjugate
\chi^{\ast}
as

b(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}),\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})=\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})

and

b(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}),\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\geq\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}),\quad\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in X,\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{H}.

Note that, if
(\mathcal{H}_{3})
holds, then according to
[
16
, Lemma 1]
one has

(1-\beta)|\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}|^{2}\leq\chi^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\leq\frac{1}{4\alpha}|\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}|^{2},\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{S}^{m}.

Therefore, we have

\chi(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}(\cdot))\in L^{1}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }\chi^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}(\cdot))\in L^{1}(\Omega),\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{H}.

Thus, the coupling function
B:X\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
defined via the bipotential
b

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}):=\int_{\Omega}b(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}),\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\;dx,

is well defined and the following estimates hold

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}\mbox{ and }B(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\geq(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\mathcal{H}},\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in X,\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{H}.

(3.9)

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta)
such that

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\geq C(\|\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\|^{2}_{X}+\|\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}),\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in X,\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\mathcal{H}.

(3.10)

Choosing
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}=\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}+\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}
in (
3.8
) we deduce that
\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\in\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})
, therefore
\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\neq\varnothing
. Keeping in mind the definition of
\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})
and (
3.9
) we get

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})+\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma\geq(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{H}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\;d\Gamma.

(3.11)

On the other hand, taking
\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}:=\mathbf{0}_{X}
in (
3.8
)

-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})+\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma\geq-(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{H}-\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\;d\Gamma.

(3.12)

Adding (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) we get

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})\geq-\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}[j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})+j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})]\;d\Gamma,\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}).

(3.13)

Using relations (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.13
) we derive the following
variational formulation in terms of bipotentials
of problem
{\bf(P)}
:

{\bf(P)}
: Find
\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\in\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\displaystyle B(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})+\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma\geq(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{H}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})\;d\Gamma}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0ptB(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

(3.14)

for all
(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})\in\mathcal{K}_{0}\times\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})
.

Note that, if
(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})
is a
weak solution
of problem
{\bf(P)}
, i.e., it solves (
3.14
), then (
3.8
) and (
3.13
) are automatically fulfilled as

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})\geq(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}))_{\mathcal{H}},\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0},

and

0=j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mathbf{0}_{X})=j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})\leq j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})+j_{,2}^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau}).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
(\mathcal{H}_{1}),(\mathcal{H}_{2})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{3})
hold. Then problem
{\bf(P)}
possesses at least one weak solution.

Proof 3.2
.

Let us define the function spaces
Y:=\mathcal{H}
,
Z_{1}:=L^{2}(\Gamma_{3}^{a};\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
Z_{2}:=L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{S}^{m})
. Moreover, assume that
\gamma_{2}:Y\to Z_{2}
is the embedding operator and
\gamma_{1}:X\to Z_{1}
is defined by

\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}):=(\gamma(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau}))|_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}},

where
\gamma:X\to H^{1/2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the trace operator. Also define
J:Z_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\phi:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
,
H:Y\to(0,\infty)
,
L:Z_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,

and

by the following instructions

and

\langle F(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}),\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\rangle:=(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}d\Gamma,

respectively.

By virtue of definition of

, it is easy to check that
{\bf(H_{B})}
,
{\bf(H_{F}^{1})}
and
{\bf(H_{G}^{1})}
are fulfilled. Furthermore, the Aubin-Clarke Theorem (see, e.g., Clarke
[
6

) ensures
J
is Lipschitz continuous of constant
\|p\|_{L^{2}}
and

J^{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$y$}};\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}})\leq\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$y$}};\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}})\;d\Gamma,\quad\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$y$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\in Z_{1}.

It follows immediately that

J^{0}(\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}});\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}))\leq\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{\tau})d\Gamma,\quad\forall\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\in X.

(3.15)

Thus,
{\bf(H_{0}^{\prime})}
holds with
\alpha_{J}:=\|p\|_{L^{2}}
and
\alpha_{L}:=1
. Let
\bar{w}_{0}:=\mathbf{0}_{X}
and
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}}_{0}\in Y

\displaystyle\frac{2B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})-B(\mathbf{0}_{X},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\bar{\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}}_{0})+\langle F(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}),-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\rangle+\langle G(\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}),\bar{\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}}_{0}-\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\rangle}{\|(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})-{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[\chi(\mathbf{0}_{X})+\chi^{\ast}(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath{$\tau$}}}_{0})]\;dx-(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}})_{\mathcal{H}}-\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}d\Gamma}}{\sqrt{\|\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\|_{Y}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C(\|\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\|_{Y}^{2})-c_{1}\|u\|_{X}-c_{2}}{\sqrt{\|\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}\|_{Y}^{2}}}\to\infty\mbox{ as }\|(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}})\|\to\infty,

for some suitable constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Therefore, the coercivity condition
{\bf(C)}
also holds. Consequently, problem (
1
) possesses at least one solution for any constraint sets
K\subseteq X
and
\Lambda\subseteq Y
.

We point out the fact
\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}})\neq\emptyset
for all
\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
. In order to see this fix
\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
,
\xi_{0}\in\partial_{C}J(\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}))

\mathbf{f}\in X
to be the unique element provided by the Riesz representation theorem such that

Let
\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}_{0}:=\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mathbf{f}-\gamma_{1}^{\ast}(\xi_{0}))
, where
\gamma_{1}^{\ast}:Z_{1}\to X

\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{f}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{\Gamma_{2}}\mathbf{f}_{2}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}d\Gamma

\displaystyle=\left(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}_{0})+\gamma_{1}^{\ast}(\xi_{0}),\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}\right)_{X}

\displaystyle=(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}+\left(\xi_{0},\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}})\right)_{Z_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}+J^{0}(\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}});\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))

\displaystyle\leq(\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}_{0},\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}))_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{\Gamma_{3}^{a}}j^{0}_{,2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}}_{\tau};\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}_{\tau})\;d\Gamma

which shows that
\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}_{0}\in\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$w$}})
.

Choosing

and

, Theorem
2.5
ensures the existence of a pair
(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{1})\in\mathcal{K}_{0}\times Y

B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{1})\geq 0,\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in Y.\end{array}\right.

(3.16)

Now, applying again Theorem
2.5
with
K:=\mathcal{K}_{0}
and
\Lambda:=\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{1})
we infer there exist
\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
and
\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{2}\in\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{1})
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}B(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{2})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{2})+J^{0}(\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2});\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2}))\geq\langle F(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2}),\mbox{\boldmath{$v$}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2}\rangle,\ \forall v\in\mathcal{K}_{0},\\
B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}})-B(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath{$\sigma$}}_{2})\geq 0,\ \forall\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}\in\Theta(\mbox{\boldmath{$u$}}_{1}).\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

But, from the definition of the coupling function

we have

from this equivalence class and this we do for each
\widehat{x}\in\widehat{G}_{1}
. This defines a set
\{x_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
of members of
G_{1}
. Thus for
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
,
\widehat{x}_{\lambda}=\widehat{x}_{\mu}
if and only if
\lambda=\mu
. By our hypothesis (2), there exists for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, a bijection
\Phi_{\lambda}:\widehat{x}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda})
. It is also clear that
\widehat{G}_{2}=\{\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda}):\lambda\in\Lambda\}
. Define a map
\psi:V(G_{1})\rightarrow V(G_{2})
as follows: if
x\in V(G_{1})
, then there exists a unique
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
x\in\widehat{x}_{\lambda}
. We set
\psi(x)=\Phi_{\lambda}(x)
. Then
\psi
is a well defined map from
V(G_{1})
onto
V(G_{2})
.

To show that
\psi
is a one-to-one map, choose any two distinct vertices
a
and
b
from
V(G_{1})
. If
a
and
b
are contained in the same equivalence class
\widehat{x}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then as
\Phi_{\lambda}
is one-to-one it follows that
\Phi_{\lambda}(a)\neq\Phi_{\lambda}(b)
and this implies that
\psi(a)\neq\psi(b)
. Assume therefore that
a
and
b
belong to different equivelence classes:
a\in\widehat{x}_{\lambda}
and
b\in\widehat{x}_{\mu}
with
\lambda\neq\mu
in
\Lambda
. As
\Phi:\widehat{G}_{1}\rightarrow\widehat{G}_{2}
is a one-to-one map, this implies that
\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda})\neq\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\mu})
, furthermore,
\psi(a)=\Phi_{\lambda}(a)\in\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda})
. Analogously
\psi(b)\in\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\mu})
. Since the equivalence classes
\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda})
and
\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\mu})
in
G_{2}
are disjoint, it follows that
\psi(a)\neq\psi(b)
. Thus
\psi
is a bijection on
V(G_{1})
onto
V(G_{2})
. To complete the proof it remains to show that
\psi
preserves the adjacency relation. So let
a
and
b
be adjacent vertices in
G_{1}
, i.e.,
a\sim b
. Then there exist
\lambda,\ \mu\in\Lambda
such that
a\in\widehat{x}_{\lambda}
and
b\in\widehat{x}_{\mu}
. Since no two elements in the same equivalence class
\widehat{x}
in
V(G_{1})
can be adjacent in the original graph
G_{1}
, a fact which can be easily checked because no vertices in
G_{1}
can be adjacent to itself, it follows that
\lambda\neq\mu
. This yields that
\widehat{x}_{\lambda}\sim\widehat{x}_{\mu}
in
\widehat{G}_{1}
. Since
\Phi
is graph isomorphism, this implies that
\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda})\sim\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\mu})
in
\widehat{G}_{2}
. But as
\psi(a)=\Phi_{\lambda}(a)\in\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\lambda})
and analogously
\psi(b)\in\Phi(\widehat{x}_{\mu})
it follows that
\psi(a)\sim\psi(b)
in
V(G_{2})
. Conversely, if
\psi(a)\sim\psi(b)
in
V(G_{2})
for
a,\ b\in V(G_{1})
, then it can be proved by reversing back the above chain of arguments that
a\sim b
in
V(G_{1})
. Thus
\psi:V(G_{1})\rightarrow V(G_{2})
is a graph isomorphism from the graph
G_{1}
onto the graph
G_{2}
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
X
be a discrete topological space. Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
designate the zero-divisor graph of
C(X)
and the comaximal graph of
C(X)
respectively. Then the quotient graphs
\widehat{G}_{1}
and
\widehat{G}_{2}
are graph isomorphic.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
V(G_{1})=
the set of all vertices in
G_{1}=V(G_{2})=
the set of all vertices in
G_{2}=\{f\in C(X):\emptyset\neq Z(f)\ \text{and}\ Z(f)\neq X\}
. It is clear that for any
f\in V(G_{1})=V(G_{2})
,
\widehat{f}=\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(f)}=
the characteristic function of
X\setminus Z(f)
. Therefore
\widehat{G}_{1}=\widehat{G}_{2}=\{\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(f)}:f
is a nonzero non-unit in
C(X)\}
. Let
\Phi:\widehat{G}_{1}\rightarrow\widehat{G}_{2}
be the map defined as follows:
\Phi(\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(f)})=\widehat{\chi_{Z(f)}}
. Then clearly
\Phi
is a bijection between the vertices
V(\widehat{G}_{1})
and
V(\widehat{G}_{2})
of the quotient graphs
\widehat{G}_{1}
and
\widehat{G}_{2}
. Furthermore, for nonzero non-units
f,g
in
C(X)
,
\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(f)}\sim\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(g)}
in
\widehat{G}_{1}
if and only if
\widehat{f}\sim\widehat{g}
(recall that
\widehat{f}=\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(f)}
, a relation obtained earlier) if and only if
Z(f)\cup Z(g)=X
if and only if
(X\setminus Z(f))\cap(X\setminus Z(g))=\emptyset
if and only if
Z({\chi}_{Z(f)})\cap Z({\chi}_{Z(g)})=\emptyset
. The last relation is true if and only if
{\chi}_{Z(f)}\sim{\chi}_{Z(g)}
in the comaximal graph
G_{2}
. Surely this is the case when and only when
{\widehat{\chi}_{Z(f)}}\sim{\widehat{\chi}_{Z(g)}}
in
\widehat{G}_{2}
, i.e., if and only if
\Phi(\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(f)})\sim\Phi(\widehat{\chi}_{X\setminus Z(g)})
in
\widehat{G}_{2}
. Thus it is proved that both of
\Phi:\widehat{G}_{1}\rightarrow\widehat{G}_{2}
and
\Phi^{-1}:\widehat{G}_{2}\rightarrow\widehat{G}_{1}
preserve the adjacency relation. Hence
\Phi:\widehat{G}_{1}\rightarrow\widehat{G}_{2}
defined above is a graph isomorphism.
∎

The next theorem shows that the countable hypothesis of the set in Theorem
5.10
can not be dropped.

Theorem 5.12
.

We assume continumm hypothesis
(
CH
)
. Suppose
X
is an uncountable discrete topological space. Then the zero-divisor graph
G_{1}=\Gamma(C(X))
and the comaximal graph
G_{2}=\Gamma_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}(C(X))
of
C(X)
are not isomorphic.

Proof.

If possible let there exist a graph isomorphism
\psi:G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}
. Choose an
f\in C(X)
with
Z(f)=\{p\}
for some point
p
in
X
. Then by Theorem
5.4
,

|\widehat{f}|=|\widehat{\psi(f)}|\ \text{and}\ |[f]|=|[\psi(f)]|,

the notations having their usual meaning as explained while defining the quotient graph
\widehat{G}
of a simple graph
G
. The first relation implies in view of Remark
5.8
that
c^{X\setminus Z(f)}=c^{X\setminus Z(\psi(f))}
. Since

|X\setminus Z(f)|=|X\setminus\{p\}|=|X|\geq c\ \text{(by CH)},

it follows therefore that
|X\setminus Z(\psi(f))|\geq c
(the reason is that
c^{\aleph_{0}}=c
and
c^{c}=2^{c}>c
). To get the desired contradiction we shall show that
|[f]|\neq|[\psi(f)]|
. Indeed we shall show that
|[f]|<|[\psi(f)]|
. For that purpose we observe that
[f]=\{g\in C(X):gf=0\}=\{g\in C(X):g(x)f(x)=0
for each
x\in Z(f)
and
g(x)=0
for each
x\in X\setminus Z(f)\}=\{g\in C(X):g(x)=0
for each
x\in X\setminus\{p\}\}
and therefore
|[f]|=c
. On the other hand, in the comaximal graph of
C(X)
for any
h\in C(X)
,
h\in[\psi(f)]
if and only if
Z(h)\cap Z(\psi(f))=\emptyset
. Therefore
[\psi(f)]=\{h\in C(X):h
takes nonzero values at each point in
Z(\psi(f))\}\geq c^{|X\setminus Z(\psi(f))|}\geq c^{c}
, as
|X\setminus Z(\psi(f))|\geq c
, observed earlier. Thus we get
|[\psi(f)]|\geq c^{c}>c=|[f]|
.
∎

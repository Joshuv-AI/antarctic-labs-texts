Composition-Differentiation Operator on Weighted Bergman Spaces
Vasudevarao Allu, Himadri Halder, Subhadip Pal

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the complex symmetry of weighted composition-differentiation operator D n , ψ , ϕ D_{n,\psi,\phi} on weighted Bergman spaces 𝒜 α 2 \mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha} with respect to the conjugation C μ , η C_{\mu,\eta} for μ , η ∈ { z ∈ ℂ : | z | = 1 } \mu,\eta\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\} . We obtain explicit conditions for which the operator D n , ψ , ϕ D_{n,\psi,\phi} is Hermitian and normal. We also characterize the complex symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator for derivative Hardy spaces.

1.
Introduction

The study of the composition operators has been initiated with two goals in
mind, namely, to have concrete examples of bounded operators on Hilbert spaces or
Banach spaces of functions, and to encounter the “Invariant Subspace Problem” of functional analysis from a different angle. Banach and Stone (see
[
21
]
) long back ago have proved that linear, surjective isometries between
spaces of continuous functions on compact Hausdorff spaces are of type of a weighted composition operator. There have been several interesting generalizations and extensions of the Banach-Stone theorem by several auhors in many different contexts. Among the existing works, the isometries of function spaces, particularly on Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}^{p}
are much more interesting (see
[
5
,
17
]
). Forelli
[
5
]
and Leeuw
[
17
]
have shown that, for
p\geq 1
, the only surjective isometries of Hardy spaces
\mathcal{H}^{p}
are precisely type of weighted composition operator, except the case
p=2
. Banach-Stone theorem not only motivates and inspires to study isometries on function spaces, but also sets the stage for the study of weighted composition operators.

Let
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
be the space of all analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. The Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is the Hilbert space consisting of functions
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{2}\,d\theta<\infty.

For
-1<\alpha<\infty
, the weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
is the set of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)
is the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. When
\alpha=0
, the space is called the classical Bergman space, and it is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{2}
(see
[
14
]
). It is well-known that
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the reproducing kernel function at each
w\in\mathbb{D}
,

K_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{\alpha+2}},\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

For every non-negative integer
n
and
w\in\mathbb{D}
, let
K^{[n]}_{w}(z)
be the reproducing kernel for point-evaluation of the
n
-th derivative, where

K^{[n]}_{w}(z)=\frac{(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)\cdots(\alpha+n+1)z^{n}}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{n+\alpha+2}},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

It can be shown that
\left\langle f(z),K^{[n]}_{w}(z)\right\rangle=f^{(n)}(w)
, for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. For any non-negative integer
n
, let

\gamma_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(n+2+\alpha)}{n!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{n},\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the standard Gamma function. Here the set
\{\gamma_{n}:n\geq 0\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
(see
[
14
]
).

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
and
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
. A weighted composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
is a linear operator
\psi C_{\phi}
given by

(\psi C_{\phi}f)(z)=\psi(z)f(\phi(z)),\,f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}).

If
\psi(z)=1
,
\psi C_{\phi}
reduces to the usual composition operator
C_{\phi}
. It is worth to note that every composition operator is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
(see
[
1
]
). Composition operators on various analytic function spaces in
\mathbb{D}
have been extensively studied by several authors. For more detailed exposition on composition operator, we refer to the books by Cowen and MacCluer
[
1
]
and Hedenmalm
et al.

[
14
]

In the context of functions in
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
, it is also worth to consider the operators on
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
defined in terms of differentiation. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the differential operator of order
n
on
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
is defined by
D^{(n)}(f)=f^{(n)}
. It is easy to see that the differentiation operator of order
n
is not bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. However, for many analytic self-maps
\phi
of
\mathbb{D}
, the operator
C_{\phi}D^{(n)}
is given by

f(z)\rightarrow f^{n}(\phi(z))

is bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Hibschweiler and Portnoy
[
15
]
and Ohno
[
20
]
have initially considered these operators and afterwards these operators have been noticed by many researchers (see
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
13
,
22
]
). Boundedness and compactness of
C_{\phi}D^{(1)}
on Hardy space and
C_{\phi}D^{(n)}
on weighted Bergman spaces respectively have been studied by Ohno
[
20
]
and Stević
[
22
]
. We denote the bounded operator
C_{\phi}D^{(n)}
by
D_{n,\phi}
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
, the weighted composition differentiation operator
D_{m,\psi,\phi}
is defined by

(D_{m,\psi,\phi}f)(z):=\psi(z)f^{m}(\phi(z)),\,\,\,f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}).

For
m=0
, the operator
D_{m,\psi,\phi}
reduces to
\psi C_{\phi}
. When
m=0
and
\psi(z)=1
,
D_{m,\psi,\phi}
reduces to the composition operator
C_{\phi}
. For
m=1
and
\psi(z)=1
, we have
D_{m,\psi,\phi}=C_{\phi}D
. When
m=1
and
\psi(z)=\phi^{\prime}(z)
,
D_{m,\psi,\phi}
becomes
DC_{\phi}
.

A
conjugation
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is an antilinear map
\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
which satisfies
\left\langle\mathcal{C}x,\mathcal{C}y\right\rangle=\left\langle y,x\right\rangle
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{C}^{2}=I
. We say that a bounded linear operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is
complex symmetric
if there exists a conjugation
\mathcal{C}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
T=\mathcal{C}T^{*}\mathcal{C}
.

The concept of complex symmetric operators arises as a natural generalization of complex symmetric matrices. Since the last two decades, the theory complex symmetric operators has been extensively studied by many mathematicians. The development has been initiated by Garcia and Putinar
[
8
,
9
]
, Garcia and Poore
[
7
]
and Garcia and Wogen
[
10
,
11
]
in this direction. In 2014, Jung
et al.

[
16
]
obtained some characterizations for complex symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. In addition, the authors have studied the spectral properties of these operators in
[
16
]
. In 2014, Garcia and Hammond
[
6
]
investigated the complex symmetric weighted composition operator for weighted Hardy spaces. For more intriguing aspects on complex symmetric operators, we refer to the work of Fatehi
[
2
]
, Lim and Khoi
[
18
]
, Wang and Xao
[
24
]
and Wang and Han
[
23
]
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
be the space of analytic functions whose derivatives are in Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, and it is defined by

\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}=|f(0)|^{2}+\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=|f(0)|^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2}|f_{m}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

In 2017, Gu and Luo
[
12
]
introduced an equivalent norm on
\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and defined another derivative Hardy space
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}<\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}):\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m+1)(m+2)}{2}|f_{m}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

For
f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
, the inner product on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
is given by

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\frac{3}{2}\left\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}.

For any
w\in\mathbb{D}
define

K_{w}(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\delta_{m}(\overline{w}z)^{m},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where

\delta_{m}=\frac{2}{(m+1)(m+2)}\quad\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

It is easy to see that

\left\langle f(z),K_{w}(z)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}}=f(w),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Therefore,
K_{w}
is the reproducing kernel function for
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
.
For every
w\in\mathbb{D}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the reproducing kernel for point evaluation of the
n
-th derivative as

K^{[n]}_{w}(z)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}\delta_{m}(\overline{w})^{m-n}z^{m}

such that
\left\langle f,K^{[n]}_{w}\right\rangle=f^{(n)}(w)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, we observe that for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
,

\left\langle f,D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})}=\left\langle D_{n,\psi,\phi}f,K_{w}(z)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})}=\psi(w)f^{(n)}(\phi(w))=\left\langle f,\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})}.

Therefore,
D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}
.

The main aim of this paper is to study complex symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator on weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
and on the derivative Hardy space
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
with respect to the conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. For
\mu,\eta
on the unit circle
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
,
C_{\mu,\eta}
is defined by

C_{\mu,\eta}f(z)=\mu\overline{f(\overline{\eta z})}

for all analytic function
f
. The organization of this paper is as follows. In Theorem
2.1
, we characterize the functions
\psi
and
\phi
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric on
A^{2}_{\alpha}
with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. When
\phi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
, Theorem
2.2
gives explicit forms of
\phi
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded and
C_{\mu,\eta}
-symmetric. In Theorem
2.3
, we obtain a sufficient condition for
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
to be normal whereas, Theorem
3.7
concerns a necessary and sufficient condition for
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
to be Hermitian. In Theorem
2.5
, we characterize the functions
\psi
and
\phi
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric on
A^{2}_{\alpha}
with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. For
n=1
, Theorems
2.1
,
2.4
and
2.5
generalize the results which are recently proved by Liu
et al.

[
19
]
.

2.
Main Results

We study the weighted composition-differentiation operator on weighted Bergman space
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. We first prove the following lemma to prove one of our main results. Throughout the article, we shall denote
p=(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)\cdots(\alpha+n+1)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Then for any
w\in\mathbb{D}
,

D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}.

We now give a complete characterization for the complex symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
with the conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be an analytic self-map on
\mathbb{D}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
with
\psi\neq 0
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Then
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
if, and only if,

\psi(z)=\frac{az^{n}}{(1-\eta bz)^{n+\alpha+2}}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\eta bz}\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where
a,c\in\mathbb{C}
and
b\in\mathbb{D}
.

In the following result, we obtain the condition on
\phi
so that
\phi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
and the operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi
be an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
be not identically zero such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded and
C_{\mu,\eta}
-symmetric on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Then one of the following statements hold:

(a)

\phi(z)=-\xi z
with
|\xi|=1
for some
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
.

(b)

\phi(z)=\frac{\overline{a_{0}}}{\eta a_{0}}\cdot\frac{a_{0}-z}{1-\overline{a_{0}}z}\,\,\,\,\mbox{for some}\,\,a_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}.

We obtain a sufficient condition for the
C_{\mu,\eta}
-symmetric bounded operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
to be normal. A bounded linear operator
T
defined on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be normal if
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
or, for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
,
\left\lVert Tx\right\rVert=\left\lVert T^{*}x\right\rVert
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
with
\phi(0)=0
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
be not identically zero such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded and complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. Then
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is normal.

A bounded linear operator is called Hermitian if
T=T^{*}
. In the following result, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the bounded operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
to be Hermitian on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
and
\psi
be a nonzero analytic function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Then
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is Hermitian if, and only if,

\psi(z)=\frac{az^{n}}{(1-\overline{b}z)^{n+\alpha+2}}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\overline{b}z}

for some
a,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{D}
.

Next, we study the weighted composition-differentiation operator on the derivative Hardy space
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
. We give a complete characterization of complex symmetric weighted composition-differentiation operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map on
\mathbb{D}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
such that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
. If
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
, then there exist
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.1)

\psi(z)=\frac{a}{n!\delta_{n}}F_{1}(z)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\phi(z)=b+c\frac{(n+3)}{(n+1)^{2}}\cdot\frac{F_{2}(z)}{F_{1}(z)}\,\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where

F_{1}(z)=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}z^{k}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,F_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n-1)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n-1}z^{k}.

Conversely, if
\psi
and
\phi
are defined by (
2.1
) for some
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
, then
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
only if
b=0
or
c=0
or both are zero.

3.
Proof of the Main Results

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle f,D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle D_{n,\psi,\phi}f,K_{w}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\psi\cdot f^{(n)}(\phi)K_{w}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\psi(w)f^{(n)}(\phi(w))=\left\langle f,\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}}

holds for any
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
. Consequently,
D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
be complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. Then we have

(3.1)

D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)=C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
.
A simple computation shows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\overline{w}z)^{\alpha+2}}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{n,\psi,\phi}\left(\frac{\mu}{(1-\eta wz)^{\alpha+2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu p\psi(z)(\eta w)^{n}}{(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}.

Therefore, the right hand side of (
3.1
) yields

(3.3)

C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=C_{\mu,\eta}\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}=\frac{\mu p\psi(w)(\eta z)^{n}}{(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}}.

By making use of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) in (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.4)

\frac{\mu p\psi(z)(\eta w)^{n}}{(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{\mu p\psi(w)(\eta z)^{n}}{(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Letting
z=0
in (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.5)

\frac{\mu p\psi(0)(\eta w)^{n}}{(1-\eta w\phi(0))^{n+\alpha+2}}=0

for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
\mu,\eta\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
, it is easy to see that (
3.5
) is equivalent to
\psi(0)=0
.

Let
\psi(z)=z^{m}g(z)
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
g
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
with
g(0)\neq 0
. Now our aim is to show that
m=n
. If
m>n
, from (
3.4
) it follows that

(3.6)

\frac{z^{m-n}g(z)}{(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{w^{m-n}g(w)}{(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}}

for any
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Putting
w=0
in (
3.6
) we obtain
g(0)=0
, which is a contradiction of the fact that
g(0)\neq 0
. If
m<n
, from (
3.4
) we obtain

(3.7)

\frac{w^{n-m}g(z)}{(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{z^{n-m}g(w)}{(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}}.

Setting
w=0
in (
3.7
) gives
g(0)=0
, which contradicts the assumption that
g(0)\neq 0
. Therefore,
m=n
and hence (
3.4
) reduces to

(3.8)

\frac{g(z)}{(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{g(w)}{(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. By letting
w=0
in (
3.8
) we obtain

g(z)=\frac{g(0)}{(1-\eta z\phi(0))^{n+\alpha+2}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\psi(z)

\displaystyle=z^{n}g(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{az^{n}}{(1-\eta bz)^{n+\alpha+2}},\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\,a=g(0)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,b=\phi(0).

By substituting
\psi(z)
in (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.9)

(1-\eta bz)^{n+\alpha+2}(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}=(1-\eta bw)^{n+\alpha+2}(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Differentiating both sides of (
3.9
) with respect to
w
, we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle(n+\alpha+2)

\displaystyle(1-\eta bz)^{n+\alpha+2}(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+1}(-\eta\phi(z))

\displaystyle=(n+\alpha+2)(1-\eta bw)^{n+\alpha+1}(-\eta b)

\displaystyle+(n+\alpha+2)(1-\eta bw)^{n+\alpha+2}(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}(-\eta z\phi^{\prime}(w)).

Letting
w=0
in (
3.10
) we obtain

\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\eta bz}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
c=\phi^{\prime}(0)
.

Conversely, let
a,c\in\mathbb{C}
and
b\in\mathbb{D}
be such that

\psi(z)=\frac{az^{n}}{(1-\eta bz)^{n+\alpha+2}}\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\eta bz}\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu p\psi(z)(\eta w)^{n}}{(1-\eta w\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a\mu p(\eta zw)^{n}}{(1-\eta bz-\eta bw-\eta czw)^{n+\alpha+2}}.

On the other hand,

(3.12)

\displaystyle C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{\mu,\eta}\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu p\psi(w)(\eta z)^{n}}{(1-\eta\phi(w)z)^{n+\alpha+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a\mu p(\eta zw)^{n}}{(1-\eta bw-\eta bz-\eta czw)^{n+\alpha+2}}.

In view of (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), it follows that

D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)=C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Since the span of the reproducing kernel functions is dense in
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
, the operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Since
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
, in view of Theorem
2.1
we have

\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\eta bz},\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\,b=\phi(0)\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,c=\phi^{\prime}(0).

Since
\phi
is an automorphism on
\mathbb{D}
, there exist
a_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\xi|=1
such that

(3.13)

\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\eta bz}=\xi\cdot\frac{a_{0}-z}{1-\overline{a_{0}}z}

for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
. It is easy to see that (
3.13
) is equivalent to

(3.14)

b-b\overline{a_{0}}z-\eta b^{2}z+\eta b^{2}\overline{a_{0}}z^{2}+cz-c\overline{a_{0}}z^{2}=\xi\overline{a_{0}}-\xi z-\eta b\xi a_{0}z+\xi\eta bz^{2}

for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Comparing the constant terms of both sides of (
3.14
) we obtain
b=\xi a_{0}
. Again, comparing the coefficients of
z
and
z^{2}
, we have the following relations

(3.15)

c-b\overline{a_{0}}-\eta b^{2}=-\xi-\eta b\xi a_{0},

and

(3.16)

\eta b^{2}\overline{a_{0}}-c\overline{a_{0}}=\xi\eta b.

Now we consider the following two cases:

Case I:
If
a_{0}=0
, then
b=0
and hence (
3.15
) is equivalent to
c=-\xi
, which shows that

\phi(z)=\xi z\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,\,|\xi|=1.

Case II:
If
a_{0}\neq 0
, using
b=\xi a_{0}
and (
3.16
), we obtain

c=\frac{\xi^{2}\eta a_{0}}{\overline{a_{0}}}\cdot(|a_{0}|^{2}-1).

By making use of the value of
c
in (
3.15
) we obtain

\xi=\frac{\overline{a_{0}}}{\eta a_{0}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Since
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is
C_{\mu,\eta}
-symmetric on
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
and
\phi(0)=0
, in view of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\psi(z)=az^{n}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\phi(z)=cz,\,\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,a,c\in\mathbb{C}.

Then for any non-negative integer
k
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert D_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle D_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{k},\gamma_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\psi\cdot\gamma^{(n)}_{k}(\phi),\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(j+2+\alpha)}{j!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\frac{k!ac^{k-n}}{(k-n)!}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(k+2+\alpha)}{k!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{k},\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(j+2+\alpha)}{j!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{k}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{k},\gamma_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\gamma_{k},D_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(k+2+\alpha)}{k!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{k},\psi\cdot\gamma^{(n)}_{j}(\phi)\right\rangle\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\left\langle\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(k+2+\alpha)}{k!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{k},\frac{j!ac^{j-n}}{(j-n)!}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(j+2+\alpha)}{j!\Gamma(2+\alpha)}}z^{j}\right\rangle\right|^{2}.

As the set
\{\gamma_{n}:n\geq 0\}
forms an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
it follows that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we have

(3.17)

\left\lVert D_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}\gamma_{k}\right\rVert^{2}=|ac^{k-n}|^{2}\left(\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\right).

Consequently, from (
3.17
) it follows that
\left\lVert D_{n,\psi,\phi}(f)\right\rVert=\left\lVert D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}(f)\right\rVert
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
, and
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is a normal operator. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Let
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
be Hermitian. Then
D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}=D_{n,\psi,\phi}
, which is equivalent to

D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=D_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

We observe that

(3.18)

D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=\frac{pz^{n}\overline{\psi(w)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,D_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=\frac{p(\overline{w})^{n}\psi(z)}{(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}.

Therefore, from (
3.18
) we have

(3.19)

\frac{pz^{n}\overline{\psi(w)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{p(\overline{w})^{n}\psi(z)}{(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}.

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Letting
w=0
in (
3.19
) we obtain
\psi(0)=0
. For
\psi\in\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
, if
\psi(z)=z^{m}g(z)
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
g
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
with
g(0)\neq 0
, then we prove that that
m=n
.

Case I:
If
m>n
, then (
3.19
) becomes

(3.20)

\frac{z^{m-n}g(z)}{(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{(\overline{w})^{m-n}\overline{g(w)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Setting
w=0
in (
3.20
) it follows
g(0)=0
, which is a contradiction to the assumption that
g(0)\neq 0
.

Case II:
If
m<n
, then from (
3.19
) we obtain

(3.21)

\frac{z^{n-m}\overline{g(w)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{\overline{w}^{n-m}g(z)}{(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Putting
w=0
in (
3.21
) we obtain
g(0)=0
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
m=n
. Now (
3.19
) is equivalent to

(3.22)

\frac{\overline{g(w)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}}=\frac{g(z)}{(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Letting
w=0
in (
3.22
), it follows that

g(z)=\frac{\overline{g(0)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(0)})^{n+\alpha+2}}.

Therefore,

(3.23)

\psi(z)=\frac{az^{n}}{(1-\overline{b}z)^{n+\alpha+2}},\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\,a=\overline{g(0)}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,b=\phi(0).

Substituting (
3.23
) in (
3.19
) we obtain

(3.24)

a(1-b\overline{w})^{n+\alpha+2}(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}=\overline{a}(1-\overline{b}z)^{n+\alpha+2}(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. If we set
w=0
in (
3.24
) we obtain that
a=\overline{a}
, and hence
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Differentiating both sides of the equation (
3.24
) with respect to
\overline{w}
we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle(n+\alpha+2)

\displaystyle(1-\overline{b}z)^{n+\alpha+2}(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+1}(-\phi(z))

\displaystyle=(n+\alpha+2)(1-b\overline{w})^{n+\alpha+1}(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}(-b)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+(n+\alpha+2)(1-b\overline{w})^{n+\alpha+2}(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+1}(-z\phi^{\prime}(w))

fol all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For
w=0
, it is easy to see that (
3.25
) yields

(3.26)

\phi(z)=b+\frac{cz}{1-\overline{b}z},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where
b=\phi(0)
and
c=\phi^{\prime}(0)
. In view of (
3.26
), we obtain
\phi^{\prime}(0)=c
. Therefore,
c=\overline{\phi^{\prime}(0)}=\overline{c}
which implies that
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Conversely, assume that
\psi
and
\phi
be of the form given by (
3.23
) and (
3.26
). Then we have

(3.27)

\displaystyle D_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{p(\overline{w})^{n}\psi(z)}{(1-\overline{w}\phi(z))^{n+\alpha+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{ap(z\overline{w})^{n}}{(1-\overline{b}z-b\overline{w}+\overline{w}|b|^{2}z-\overline{w}cz)^{n+\alpha+2}}

and

(3.28)

\displaystyle D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{apz^{n}\overline{\psi(w)}}{(1-z\overline{\phi(w)})^{n+\alpha+2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{ap(z\overline{w})^{n}}{(1-b\overline{w}-\overline{b}z+\overline{w}|b|^{2}z-\overline{w}cz)^{n+\alpha+2}}.

From (
3.27
) and (
3.28
), it is clear that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)=D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since the span of reproducing kernel functions is dense in
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\alpha}
, it follows that
D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}=D_{n,\psi,\phi}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
be complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
. Then we have the following:

(3.29)

D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)=C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. Using (
3.29
), we obtain

\displaystyle D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}(\overline{w}z)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=D_{n,\psi,\phi}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu\delta_{k}(\eta wz)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\psi(z)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta w)^{k}\phi(z)^{k-n}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{\mu,\eta}\overline{\psi(w)}K^{[n]}_{\phi(w)}

\displaystyle=C_{\mu,\eta}\overline{\psi(w)}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}z^{k}(\overline{\phi(w)})^{k-n}

\displaystyle=\mu\psi(w)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta z)^{k}\phi(w)^{k-n}.

Therefore, we have

(3.30)

\mu\psi(z)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta w)^{k}\phi(z)^{k-n}=\mu\psi(w)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta z)^{k}\phi(w)^{k-n}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. By substituting
w=0
in (
3.30
), we obtain

\mu\psi(0)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta z)^{k}\phi(0)^{k-n}=0,\,\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}

which implies that
\psi(0)=0
.

Let
\psi(z)=z^{m}\chi(z)
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\chi(z)
be an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
with
\chi(0)\neq 0
. By going the similar lines of argument as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.4
, it can be easily shown that
m=n
. Therefore, (
3.30
) becomes

(3.31)

\chi(z)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}\eta^{k}(w\phi(z))^{k-n}=\chi(w)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}\eta^{k}(z\phi(w))^{k-n}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
. If we set
w=0
in (
3.31
), we obtain

\chi(z)=\frac{a}{n!\delta_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta bz)^{k-n},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where
a=\chi(0)
and
b=\phi(0)
. Therefore, we have

(3.32)

\psi(z)=\frac{a}{n!\delta_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}z^{k}(\eta b)^{k-n},\,\,z\in\mathbb{D}.

Substituting the expression of
\chi(z)
in (
3.31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta bz)^{k-n}\right)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}\eta^{k}(w\phi(z))^{k-n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta bw)^{k-n}\right)\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}\eta^{k}(z\phi(w))^{k-n}\right).

Differentiating both the sides of the above relation with respect to
w
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta bz)^{k-n}\right)\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}(k-n)\delta_{k}\eta^{k}w^{k-n-1}\phi(z)^{k-n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}(k-n)\delta_{k}w^{k-n-1}(\eta b)^{k-n}\right)\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}\eta^{k}(z\phi(w))^{k-n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta bw)^{k-n}\right)\left(\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}(k-n)\delta_{k}\eta^{k}z^{k-n}\phi(w)^{k-n-1}\phi^{\prime}(w)\right).

By letting
w=0
in the above expression, we obtain

(3.33)

\phi(z)=b+\frac{n+3}{(n+1)^{2}}c\cdot\frac{\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n-1)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n-1}z^{k}}{\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}z^{k}}\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

where
c=\phi^{\prime}(0)
.

Conversely, let
\psi
and
\phi
be respectively of the form given by (
3.32
) and (
3.33
) for some
a,b,c\in\mathbb{C}
. Then it is easy to see that for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{F_{2}(z)}{F_{1}(z)}=\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{n+3}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}(\eta b)^{j-1}z^{j}

with
d_{1}=1
and
d_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
for
j=2,3,\cdots.
Therefore,
\phi(z)
can be written as

(3.34)

\phi(z)=b+c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}(\eta b)^{j-1}z^{j}.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the operator
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
will be complex symmetric with conjugation
C_{\mu,\eta}
on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
if (
3.30
) holds for all
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
.

In view of (
3.32
) and (
3.34
), the relation (
3.30
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a}{n!\delta_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}z^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}\delta_{m}(\eta w)^{m}\left(b+c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}(\eta b)^{j-1}z^{j}\right)^{m-n}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{a}{n!\delta_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}w^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}\delta_{m}(\eta z)^{m}\left(b+c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}d_{j}(\eta b)^{j-1}w^{j}\right)^{m-n}\right)

which implies

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}z^{k}\right)\left\{\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}\delta_{m}(\eta w)^{m}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{m-n}b^{l}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}cd_{j}(\eta b)^{j-1}z^{j}\right)^{m-n-l}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}w^{k}\right)\left\{\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{m!}{(m-n)!}\delta_{m}(\eta z)^{m}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{m-n}b^{l}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}cd_{j}(\eta b)^{j-1}w^{j}\right)^{m-n-l}\right)\right\}.

By comparing the coefficients of
z^{n+2}w^{n+1}
from the both sides of the above equation we obtain

(3.35)

\displaystyle(n+1)!\eta^{n+1}\delta_{n+1}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}(n+2)!\eta^{2}b^{3}\delta_{n+1}+\frac{n!\delta^{2}_{n}}{(n+1)\delta_{n+1}}d_{2}\eta bc+n!d_{1}\eta bc\delta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n+2)!\eta^{n+2}\delta_{n+2}\left(\frac{2n!d_{1}bc\delta^{2}_{n}}{\delta_{n+1}}+(n+1)!\eta b^{3}\delta_{n+1}\right).

Here we notice that the relation (
3.35
) holds only if, either
b=0
or
c=0
or both are zero. Now we discuss about the following cases.

Case I:
Let
b=0
and
c\neq 0
. Then,
\psi(z)=az^{n}
and
\phi(z)=cz
. Therefore, in this case,

D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)=a\mu\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!\delta_{k}c^{k-n}(\eta zw)^{k}}=C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z).

Case II:
Let
b\neq 0
and
c=0
. Then we have

\phi(z)=b\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\psi(z)=\frac{a}{n!\delta_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}z^{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}.

An observation shows that

\displaystyle D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{a\mu}{n!\delta_{n}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}z^{k}(\eta b)^{k-n}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{(k-n)!}\delta_{k}(\eta w)^{k}b^{k-n}

\displaystyle=C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z).

Case III:
Let
b=c=0
. Then
\phi(z)=0
and
\psi(z)=az^{n}
. Therefore,

D_{n,\psi,\phi}C_{\mu,\eta}K_{w}(z)=a\mu n!\delta_{n}(\eta zw)^{n}=C_{\mu,\eta}D^{*}_{n,\psi,\phi}K_{w}(z).

Therefore, we conclude that
D_{n,\psi,\phi}
is
C_{\mu,\eta}
-symmetric on
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(\mathbb{D})
for each case. This completes the proof.
∎

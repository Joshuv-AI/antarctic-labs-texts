Compact subspaces of the space of separately continuous functions with the cross-uniform topology Note: The research activities co-financed by the funds granted under the Research Excellence Initiative of the University of Silesia in Katowice
Oleksandr Maslyuchenko, Vadym Myronyk, Roman Ivasiuk

Abstract
We consider two natural topologies on the space S ⁡ ( X × Y , Z ) S(X\times Y,Z) of all separately continuous functions defined on the product of two topological spaces X X and Y Y and ranged into a topological or metric space X X . These topologies are the cross-open topology and the cross-uniform topology. We show that these topologies coincides if X X and Y Y are pseudocompacts and Z Z is a metric space. We prove that a compact space K K embeds into S ⁡ ( X × Y , Z ) S(X\times Y,Z) for infinite compacts X X , Y Y and a metrizable space Z ⊇ ℝ Z\supseteq\mathbb{R} if and only if the weight of K K is less than the sharp cellularity of both spaces X X and Y Y .

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be pseudocompact spaces,
Z
be a metrizable space and

be a metric which generates the topology of

coincides with the cross-open topology, that is
S_{cru}(X\times Y,Z)=S_{cro}(X\times Y,Z)
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that the cross-open topology is weaker than the cross-uniform topology. Let
P=X\times Y
,
c=(a,b)\in P
,
C=\mathrm{cr}\{c\}
,
G
be an open set in
C
,
K=\overline{G}\cap C
and
W
be an open subset of
Z
. Let us prove that
{O=O_{K,W}}
is open in
S_{cru}(P,Z)
. Consider
s\in O
. Since
s
is separately continuous, the restriction
s|_{C}
is continuous. Observe that
C
is pseudocompact and
K
is the closure of an open set
G
in
C
. By
[
5
, 3.10.23]
one can easily prove that
K
is a pseudocompact. Therefore,
s(K)
is pseudocompact subset of a metrizable space
Z
. Then
[
5
, 3.10.21, 4.1.17]
implies the compactness of
s(K)
. But
s(K)\subseteq W
. Thus, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that

B\big(s(p),\varepsilon\big)\subseteq W
for any
p\in K
. Denote
U=B_{C}(s,\varepsilon)
and prove that
U\subseteq G
. Let
t\in U
. Then
d\big(s(p),t(p)\big)<\varepsilon
for any
p\in C
. Hence,
t(p)\in B\big(s(p),\varepsilon\big)\subseteq W
for any
p\in K
. Therefore,
t(K)\subseteq G
. Consequently,
t\in O
. Thus,
G
is open in
S_{cru}(P,Z)
.

Secondly, we prove that the cross-uniform topology is weaker than the cross-open topology. Let
c=(a,b)\in P
,
C=\mathrm{cr}\{c\}
,
s\in S(P,Z)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
U=B_{C}(s,\varepsilon)
. Since
X
and
Y
are pseudocompact spaces, we conclude that
C
is pseudocompact as well. But the separate continuity of
s
implies that the restriction
s|_{C}
is continuous. Therefore,
s(C)
is a pseudocompact subset of
Z
. Then
s(C)
is compact by
[
5
, 3.10.21, 4.1.17]
. Set
B_{p}=B\big(s(p),\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\big)
for any
p\in C
. Then
\big\{B_{p}:p\in C\big\}
is an open covering of the compact set
s(C)
. Hence, there are

and
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{n}\in C
such that
s(C)\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}B_{p_{i}}
. Put

G_{i}=C\cap s^{-1}\big(B_{p_{i}}\big)
and
K_{i}=\overline{G}_{i}\cap C

for any

. Since
s|_{C}
is continuous,
G_{i}
is open in
C
. Therefore,
K_{i}\in\mathcal{C}ro(P)
and

for any

. Let

and

for any

. Then the set

is open in the cross-open topology. Since

for any

, we conclude

. So,

is an open neighborhood of

in the cross-open topology. To prove

we pick

. Then
t(K_{i})\subseteq W_{i}
for any

. On the other hand
s(K_{i})\subseteq W_{i}
as well. Consider
p\in C
. Since
s(p)\in s(C)\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}B_{p_{i}}
, there is
i
with

. Then

. Therefore,

and

. Consequently,

Hence,

. Thus, we have proved the inclusion

and so

is open in the cross-open topology.

The evaluation function and meagerness of
S_{cro}(X\times Y,Z)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

,

and

be topological spaces such that

is regular,

,

,

Obviously, the separate continuity of

(or

) implies that

of

. The regularity of

implies that there is a neighborhood

. Set

and

. Since

is separately continuous, there are open sets

in

and

in

such that

,

. Set

. Then

and

. Put

. So,

. Set

. Consider

Reduction to the case of Eberlein compacts

Denote by
C_{p}(X,Y)
the space of all continuous functions
u:X\to Y
equipped with the topology of pointwise convergent and
C_{p}(X)=C_{p}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Recall that a compact space is called
an Eberlein compact
if it is homeomorphic to a subspace of a Banach space with the weak topology (or, equivalently, to a subspace of
C_{p}(X)
for a compact

).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a compact space,
Y
be a metrizable space
and
K
be a compact subspace of

. Then
K
is an Eberlein compact.

Proof.

Let
d
be a metric which generates the topology of

. Following

, Proof of Step 1 of Theorem 1]

\Lambda=\Big\{\lambda\in[0;1]^{Y}:\forall y,y^{\prime}\in Y\ \Big|\ \big|\lambda(y)-\lambda(y^{\prime})\big|\leq d(y,y^{\prime})\Big\}

\Lambda
is compact and so

is compact as well. Define a mapping
\Phi:C_{p}(X,Y)\to[0;1]^{Z}
by setting

\Phi(u)(z)=\lambda\big(u(x)\big),\ \text{where }u\in C_{p}(X,Y)\text{ and }z=(x,\lambda)\in Z

Firstly, we prove that
\Phi:C_{p}(X,Y)\to C_{p}(Z)
. Fix
u\in C_{p}(X,Y)
and
c=(a,\alpha)\in Z
. Let us prove that
w=\Phi(u)
is continuous at
c
. Consider,
\varepsilon>0
. Set
b=u(a)
and
U=u^{-1}\big(B(b,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\big)
. Since
u
is continuous,
U
is a neighborhood of
a
. Put
O=\Big\{\lambda\in\Lambda:\big|\lambda(b)-\alpha(b)\big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big\}
. Then
O
is a neighborhood of
\alpha
in
\Lambda
. Therefore,
W=U\times O
is a neighborhood of
c
in
Z
. Consider a point

. Then
y=u(x)\in B(b,\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
and
\big|\lambda(b)-\alpha(b)\big|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\big|w(z)-w(c)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\lambda(y)-\alpha(b)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\lambda(y)-\lambda(b)\big|+\big|\lambda(b)-\alpha(b)\big|

\displaystyle<d(y,b)+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}=\varepsilon.

Thus
w
is continuous at
c
.

Secondly, we prove that
\Phi:C_{p}(X,Y)\to C_{p}(Z)
is continuous. Fix
{c=(a,\alpha)\in Z}
. Define a function
\varphi:C_{p}(X,Y)\to[0;1]
by setting
{\varphi(u)=\Phi(u)(c)=\alpha\big(u(a)\big)}
,

. It is enough to prove that
\varphi
is continuous. Let
(u_{m})_{m\in M}
be a net in
C_{p}(X,Y)
and

such that
u_{m}\to v
in
C_{p}(X,Y)
. Then
u_{m}(a)\to v(a)
. Consequently, the continuity of
\alpha
implies that
\varphi(u_{m})=\alpha\big(u_{m}(a)\big)\to\alpha\big(v(a)\big)=\varphi(v)
. Thus,
\varphi
is continuous.

Finally, let us prove that
\Phi
is injective. Consider distinct points
u,u^{\prime}\in C_{p}(X,Y)
. Then there is a point
a\in X
such that
b=u(a)
and
b^{\prime}=u^{\prime}(a)
are distinct points in
Y
. Define
\alpha\in\Lambda
by
\alpha(y)=\min\big\{1,d(b,y)\big\}
,

. Since

\alpha(b^{\prime})>0=\alpha(b)
. Let

\Phi(u)(c)=\alpha\big(u(a)\big)=\alpha(b)<\alpha(b^{\prime})=\alpha\big(u^{\prime}(a)\big)=\Phi(u^{\prime})(c).

Consequently,
\Phi(u)\neq\Phi(u^{\prime})
.

Thus, we have proved that
\Phi:C_{p}(X,Y)\to C_{p}(Z)
is a continuous injection. Therefore,
K
is homeomorphic to the compact subspace
\widetilde{K}=\Phi(K)
of

and, hence,

is an Eberlein compact.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be Hausdorff compact spaces,
\alpha:X\to Z
be a continuous function and
\gamma:X\to Y
be a continuous surjection such that

\forall x,x^{\prime}\in X\ \big|\ \gamma(x)=\gamma(x^{\prime})\Rightarrow\alpha(x)=\alpha(x^{\prime}).

(2)

Then there is a unique continuous function
\beta:Y\to Z
such that
\alpha=\beta\circ\gamma
.

Proof.

Existence.

Let
\xi:Y\to X
be a right inversion of
\gamma
, that is
\gamma\big(\xi(y)\big)=y
for any
y\in Y
. Define
\beta=\alpha\circ\xi
. By (
2
) we conclude that
\alpha=\beta\circ\gamma
. Indeed, if
x\in X
,
y=\gamma(x)
and
x^{\prime}=\xi(y)
then
\gamma(x)=\gamma(x^{\prime})=y
and, hence,
\alpha(x)=\alpha(x^{\prime})=\alpha\big(\xi(y)\big)=\beta(y)=\beta\big(\gamma(x)\big)
.

Uniqueness.
Let
\beta,\beta_{1}:Y\to Z
be functions with
\alpha=\beta\circ\gamma=\beta_{1}\circ\gamma
. Fix
y\in Y
. Since
\gamma
is a surjection, there exists
x\in X
with
y=\gamma(x)
. Therefore,
\beta(y)=\beta\big(\gamma(x)\big)=\alpha(x)=\beta_{1}\big(\gamma(x)\big)=\beta_{1}(y)
. Thus,
\beta=\beta_{1}

Continuity.
Consider a closed subset
C
of
Z
. Then
A=\alpha^{-1}(C)

and, hence,
A
is compact. Therefore,
B=\gamma(A)
is compact and, hence,
B
is closed. Since
\gamma
is surjective and

\beta^{-1}(C)

\beta

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

,

\widetilde{X}

.

Proof.

Let

\widetilde{Y}=Y\times K

5
The cellularity and the sharp cellularity of Eberlein compacts

Let
w(X)
denote the
weight
of a topological space
X
,
d(X)
denote the
density
of
X
,
and
c(X)
denote the
cellularity
of
X
, that is

\displaystyle w(X)

\displaystyle=\min\{\mathfrak{|}\mathcal{B}|:\mathcal{B}\text{ is a base of }X\},

\displaystyle d(X)

\displaystyle=\min\{|A|:\overline{A}=X\}\ \ \ \ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle c(X)

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|\mathcal{U}|:\mathcal{U}\mbox{ is
a disjoint family of open sets in }X\Big\},

where
|A|
means the cardinality of a set
A
. The
sharp cellularity
is, by definition, the cardinal number

c^{\sharp}(X)=\sup\Big\{|\mathcal{U}|^{+}:\mathcal{U}\mbox{ is
a disjoint family of open sets in }X\Big\},

where
\mathfrak{m}^{+}
means the least cardinal number which is grater than
\mathfrak{m}
.
Remark that in
[
6
]
the sharp cellularity is denoted by
\hat{c}(X)
. In
[
6
, 4.1]
it was proved that
c^{\sharp}(X)=c(X)^{+}
if
c(X)
is either singular or successor cardinal number. The next lemma can be obtained from the previous fact. But the direct proof is also not so hard.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
\mathfrak{m}>\aleph_{0}
, and
(\mathcal{G}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of disjoint open systems
\mathcal{G}_{n}\not\ni\emptyset
such that
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\mathcal{G}_{n}|=\mathfrak{m}
. Then there exists a disjoint open system
\mathcal{G}\not\ni\emptyset
such that
|\mathcal{G}|=\mathfrak{m}
.

Proof.

If the cofinality
\mathrm{cf}(\mathfrak{m})>\aleph_{0}
then there is
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
|\mathcal{G}_{n}|=\mathfrak{m}
and we put
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{n}
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{cf}(\mathfrak{m})=\aleph_{0}
. Without lose of generality we may assume that
|\mathcal{G}_{n}|=\mathfrak{m}_{n}
is regular,
\aleph_{0}<\mathfrak{m}_{n}<\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}
and
\bigcup\mathcal{G}_{n}
is dense in
X
for any
n
.

We are going to construct sequences of disjoint open families
\mathcal{U}_{n}\not\ni\emptyset
and sets
W_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{n}
is dense in
X
,
\mathcal{U}_{n}\setminus\{W_{n}\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{n+1}
and
|\mathcal{U}_{n}|=\mathfrak{m}_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{1}=\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Suppose that we have already constructed
\mathcal{U}_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(U)=\{G\in\mathcal{G}_{n+1}:G\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}=\bigcup_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(U)
. Since
|\mathcal{U}_{n}|=\mathfrak{m}_{n}<\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}
,
\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}=|\mathcal{G}_{n+1}|\leq\sum_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{n}}|\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(U)|\leq\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}
. So, by the regularity of
\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}
there exists
W_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
such that
|\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(W_{n})|=\mathfrak{m}_{n+1}
. Set
\mathcal{U}_{n+1}=(\mathcal{U}_{n}\setminus\{W_{n}\})\cup\{G\cap W_{n}:G\in\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(W_{n})\}
. Thus,
\mathcal{U}_{n+1}
and
W_{n}
have the needed properties.

Let
\mathcal{W}=\{W_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\mathcal{U}_{n}\setminus\mathcal{W}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Obviously,
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is a disjoint open family,
\mathcal{V}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{n+1}
and
|\mathcal{V}_{n}|=\mathfrak{m}_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, the family
\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{V}_{n}
is disjoint and
|\mathcal{G}|=\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

In
[
2
, Theorem 4.2]
the authors proved that any Eberlein compact includes into
c_{0}(\Gamma)
with the pointwise topology such that
|\Gamma|=c(X)
. Formally, they proof is incomplete, because they proved that for any cardinal
\mu<\lambda=|\Gamma|
there is an open disjoint family of the cardinality
\geq\mu
only. But if
\lambda
is regular then this is not enough to assert that
c(X)=\lambda
. Of course, we can easily to complete the proof for a regular
\lambda
. The authors also remarked in
[
2
, Remark (a) after Theorem 4.2]
that one can prove that there is a disjoint open family of cardinality
\mu
. In the next proposition we do it directly using their method and our Lemma
5.1
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
X
be an Eberlein compact. Then
c^{\sharp}(X)=c(X)^{+}
and
c(X)=d(X)=w(X)
.

Proof.

By the Amir-Lindersrauss theorem
[
1
, Theorem 1]
we may assume that
X
be a subspace of
c_{0}(T)
with the pointwise topology. Denote

{S(x)=\big\{t\in T:x(t)\neq 0\big\}}
,
S=\bigcup_{x\in X}S(x)
,

S_{n}(x)=\big\{t\in T:|x(t)|>\tfrac{1}{n}\big\}
and
S_{n}=\bigcup_{x\in X}S_{n}(x)

for any
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Evidently,
S_{n}(x)
’s are finite and
S=\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n}
. Set
\mathfrak{m}=|S|
. Observe that
X
is homeomorphic with the subspace
X|_{S}=\big\{x|_{S}:x\in X\big\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{S}
. Therefore
w(X)\leq w(\mathbb{R}^{S})=\mathfrak{m}\cdot\aleph_{0}
.

In the case where
\mathfrak{m}\leq\aleph_{0}
we have that
X
is metrizable and separable. Thus,
c(X)=d(X)=w(X)\leq\aleph_{0}
and
c^{\sharp}(X)=c(X)^{+}
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}>\aleph_{0}
. Then
w(X)\leq\mathfrak{m}
. Set

\mathcal{S}_{n}=\big\{S_{n}(x):x\in X\big\}
and

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\big\{M:\text{$M$ is a maximal element of $\mathcal{S}_{n}$}\big\}
.
Since
X
compact subset of
c_{0}(T)
,
\mathcal{S}_{n}
does not contain an infinite increasing chain. Therefore, each element of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
includes in some element of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Consequently,
S_{n}=\bigcup\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Denote
\mathfrak{m}_{n}=|\mathcal{M}_{n}|
.
Since any element of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is finite,
\mathfrak{m}_{n}=|S_{n}|
if
S_{n}
is infinite. Thus,
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{m}_{n}=\mathfrak{m}
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and define for any
M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}

G_{M}=\big\{x\in X:|x(t)|>\tfrac{1}{n}\text{ for any }t\in M\big\}.

The maximality of elements of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
yields
G_{M}\cap G_{N}=\emptyset
for any distinct sets
M,N\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Therefore, the disjoint open family
\mathcal{G}_{n}=\big\{G_{M}:M\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\}
has the cardinality
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
. By Lemma
5.1
there is a disjoint open system
\mathcal{G}
in
X
such that
|\mathcal{G}|=\mathfrak{m}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{m}\leq c(X)\leq d(X)\leq w(X)\leq\mathfrak{m}
. Thus, these cardinals are equal and
c^{\sharp}(X)=\mathfrak{m}^{+}
.
∎

The weight of compact subspaces of
S(X\times Y,Z)

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
M
be a metrizable space and
K
be a compact subspace of

. Then

. In particular, if

then

.

Proof.

Let
A
be a dense subset of
X
such that
|A|=d(X)
. Let
{\Phi:C_{p}(X,M)\to M^{A}}
be defined by
\Phi(u)=u|_{A}
,

. Obviously,
\Phi
is a continuous injection. Consequently,
K
is homeomorphic to a subspace

of

. Let
\mathrm{pr}_{a}:M^{A}\to M
be the coordinate projection and
L_{a}=\mathrm{pr}_{a}(L)
for any

. Therefore,
L_{a}
’s are metrizable compacts and, hence,
w(L_{a})\leq\aleph_{0}
. By
[
5
, 2.3.13]
we obtain that

\textstyle w(K)=w(L)\leq w\Big(\prod_{a\in A}L_{a}\Big)\leq|A|\cdot\aleph_{0}=d(X)\cdot\aleph_{0}.

If
d(X)\geq\aleph_{0}
then
w(K)\leq d(X)\cdot\aleph_{0}=d(X)
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
,
Y
be infinite compacts,
Z
be a metrizable space and
K
be a compact subspace of
S(X\times Y,Z)
. Then
w(K)<\min\big\{c^{\sharp}(X),c^{\sharp}(Y)\big\}
.

Proof.

Set
\mathfrak{m}=w(K)
. It is enough to prove that
\mathfrak{m}<c^{\sharp}(X)
. By Lemma
4.3
there is an Eberlein compact
\widetilde{X}
such that
K
embeds into

and
\widetilde{X}

. Consequently,

. So, it will be enough to prove that

. Thus, without loss of generality we may assume that
X
is an Eberlein compact.

Let
P=X\times Y
,

be a metric which generates the topology of
Z
and
(d_{c})_{c\in P}
be defined by (
1
). Then the cross-uniform topology on the space

is generated by this family of pseudometrics. Consider the metric space

with the supremum metric

\varrho(u,v)=\sup\limits_{y\in Y}d\big(u(y),v(y)\big),\ \ u,v\in M.

Let
\Phi:S\to C_{p}(X,M)
be given by
\Phi(s)(x)(y)=s(x,y)
,

,

,

. Let

,

\varrho\big(u(a),v(a)\big)=\sup\limits_{y\in Y}d\big(u(a)(y),v(a)(y)\big)=\sup\limits_{y\in Y}d\big(s(a,y),t(a,y)\big)\leq d_{c}(s,t).

\Phi
is a continuous injection. Consequently,

is homeomorphic to the subspace
\widetilde{K}=\Phi(K)
of

. Thus, Propositions

and

now leads to

.
∎

,

Since

and

. Fix some points

,

f_{i}:X\times Y\to[0;1]
such that
f_{i}(c_{i})=1
and
f_{i}(p)=0
for any
p\in P\setminus W_{i}
. Define a mapping
\Phi:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{P}
by setting

\Phi(u)(p)=\sum\limits_{i\in I}u_{i}f_{i}(p),\ \ u=(u_{i})_{i\in I}\in Q,\ \ p\in P.

Since
f_{i}
’s vanish outsides of
W_{i}
and
(W_{i})_{i\in I}
is disjoint,
\Phi
is well defined.

Let
{c=(a,b)\in P}
and
C=\mathrm{cr}\{c\}
. Then there are
i_{0},j_{0}\in I
such that

a\notin U_{i}
for any
i\neq i_{0}
and
b\notin V_{j}
for any
j\neq j_{0}
. Consequently,
C\cap W_{i}=\emptyset
for any
i\notin\{i_{0},j_{0}\}
. Therefore,
\Phi(u)(p)=u_{i_{0}}f_{i_{0}}(p)+u_{j_{0}}f_{j_{0}}(p)
for any
u\in Q
and

p\in C
. Thus,
\Phi:Q\to S
. To prove the continuity of
\Phi
consider a net
v_{m}=(v_{m,i})_{i\in I}
which is convergent to
u=(u_{i})_{i\in I}
in
Q
. Therefore,
\Phi(v_{m})(p)=v_{m,i_{0}}f_{i_{0}}(p)+v_{m,j_{0}}f_{j_{0}}(p)
and
\Phi(u)(p)=u_{i_{0}}f_{i_{0}}(p)+u_{j_{0}}f_{j_{0}}(p)
for any
m\in M
and
p\in C
. Since
f_{i_{0}}(p),f_{j_{0}}(p)\in[0;1]
, we conclude that

\displaystyle d_{c}\big(\Phi(v_{m}),\Phi(u)\big)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{p\in C}\Big|\big(v_{m,i_{0}}-u_{i_{0}}\big)f_{i_{0}}(p)+\big(v_{m,j_{0}}-u_{j_{0}}\big)f_{j_{0}}(p)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{p\in C}\Big(f_{i_{0}}(p)\big|v_{m,i_{0}}-u_{i_{0}}\big|+f_{j_{0}}(p)\big|v_{m,j_{0}}-u_{j_{0}}\big|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\big|v_{m,i_{0}}-u_{i_{0}}\big|+\big|v_{m,j_{0}}-u_{j_{0}}\big|\to 0

Hence,
\Phi
is continuous. But
\Phi

Q

of

.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let

,

be infinite metrizable compacts and

be a

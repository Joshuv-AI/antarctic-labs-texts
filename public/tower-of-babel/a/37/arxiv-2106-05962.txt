Semiclassical states for a magnetic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with exponential critical growth in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Pietro d’Avenia, Chao Ji

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the magnetic nonlinear Schrödinger equation ( ε i ∇ − A ( x ) ) 2 u + V ( x ) u = f ( | u | 2 ) u in ℝ 2 , \Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{i}\nabla-A(x)\Big)^{2}u+V(x)u=f(|u|^{2})u\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}, where ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 is a parameter, V : ℝ 2 → ℝ V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} and A : ℝ 2 → ℝ 2 A:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2} are continuous functions and f : ℝ → ℝ f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} is a C 1 C^{1} function having exponential critical growth. Under a global assumption on the potential V V , we use variational methods and Ljusternick-Schnirelmann theory to prove existence, multiplicity, concentration, and decay of nontrivial solutions for ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 small.

Introduction and main results

(1.1)

In particular, the operator

2.
The variational framework and some preliminaries

In this section we introduce the functional spaces that we use and a
classical
equivalent version of (
1.1
). We also present some results for a
limit problem
which will be useful for our arguments.

From now on, in the whole paper, we will assume that (
V
) holds.

For a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, let us denote by

\nabla_{A}u:=\Big(\frac{\nabla}{i}-A\Big)u

and consider the space

H_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}):=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}):|\nabla_{A}u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})\},

which is an Hilbert space endowed with the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle:=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\nabla_{A}u\overline{\nabla_{A}v}+u\overline{v}\Big)dx,\quad\text{for any }u,v\in H_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}),

where
\operatorname{Re}
and the bar denote the real part of a complex number and the complex conjugation, respectively. Hence, let
\|\cdot\|_{A}
be the norm induced by this inner product.

Observe that a useful tool when we work on
H_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
is the diamagnetic inequality

(2.1)

|\nabla_{A}u(x)|\geq|\nabla|u(x)||

(see e.g.
[
31
, Theorem 7.21]
).

Moreover, a simple change of variables allows us to write equation (
1.1
) as

(2.2)

\Big(\frac{1}{i}\nabla-A_{\varepsilon}(x)\Big)^{2}u+V_{\varepsilon}(x)u=f(|u|^{2})u\quad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
A_{\varepsilon}(x)=A(\varepsilon x)
and
V_{\varepsilon}(x)=V(\varepsilon x)
.

Thus, we introduce the Hilbert space

H_{\varepsilon}:=\Big\{u\in H_{A_{\varepsilon}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V_{\varepsilon}(x)|u|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\}

endowed with the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\varepsilon}:=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\nabla_{A_{\varepsilon}}u\overline{\nabla_{A_{\varepsilon}}v}+V_{\varepsilon}(x)u\overline{v}\Big)dx

and
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
is the norm induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\varepsilon}
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, the functional space
H_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following embeddings. For the sake of completeness here we give some details about their proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

The space
H_{\varepsilon}
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
for
r\geq 2
and compact embedded in
L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
for
r\geq 1
.
Moreover, if
V_{\infty}=+\infty
, then
H_{\varepsilon}
is compactly embedded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
for
r\geq 2
.

Proof.

According to the diamagnetic inequality (
2.1
), if
u\in H_{\varepsilon}
, then
|u|\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
and

(2.3)

\||u|\|\leq C\|u\|_{\varepsilon}.

Thus, the continuous embedding
H_{\varepsilon}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
is a consequence of (
2.3
) and of the continuous embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
for
r\geq 2
.

To get the compact embedding of
H_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{r}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
for
r\geq 1
we can argue in the following way.

Let
K
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with compact closure.

First of all, we prove that
H_{\varepsilon}
is continuously embedded in
H^{1}(K,\mathbb{C})
.

Indeed we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}(K,\mathbb{C})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{K}|u|^{2}dx=\int_{K}\Big|\frac{\nabla}{i}u\Big|^{2}dx+\int_{K}|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{K}|\nabla_{A_{\varepsilon}}u|^{2}dx+\int_{K}|A_{\varepsilon}u|^{2}dx+\int_{K}V_{\varepsilon}(x)|u|^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{K}|\nabla_{A_{\varepsilon}}u|^{2}dx+\int_{K}V_{\varepsilon}(x)|u|^{2}dx\right)\leq C\|u\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}.

Let now
\mathcal{F}
be a bounded subset of
H_{\varepsilon}
. We want to prove that
\mathcal{F}|_{K}
, which is a subset of
H^{1}(K,\mathbb{C})
due to the continuous embedding proved before, has compact closure in
L^{r}(K,\mathbb{C})
,
r\geq 1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
be an extension operator found in
[
12
, Theorem 9.7]
for
H^{1}(K,\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{P}:=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Re}(u))+i\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Im}(u))
.

We know that, for each
v\in H^{1}(K,\mathbb{R})
,

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}v|_{K}=v,\ \|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(K,\mathbb{R})}\text{ and }\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}(K,\mathbb{R})}.

Thus, we get

\mathcal{P}u|_{K}=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Re}(u))|_{K}+i\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Im}(u))|_{K}={\rm Re}(u)+i{\rm Im}(u)=u,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Re}(u))+i\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Im}(u))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})}^{2}\leq C(\|{\rm Re}(u)\|_{L^{2}(K,\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|{\rm Im}(u)\|_{L^{2}(K,\mathbb{R})}^{2})

\displaystyle=C\|u\|_{L^{2}(K,\mathbb{C})}^{2}

and, analogously,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Re}(u))+i\tilde{\mathcal{P}}({\rm Im}(u))\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})}^{2}\leq C(\|{\rm Re}(u)\|_{H^{1}(K,\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|{\rm Im}(u)\|_{H^{1}(K,\mathbb{R})}^{2})

\displaystyle=C\|u\|_{H^{1}(K,\mathbb{C})}^{2}.

Let now
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}|_{K})
.

Since
H_{\varepsilon}
is continuously embedded in
H^{1}(K,\mathbb{C})
, we have that
\mathcal{H}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
and
\mathcal{H}|_{K}=\mathcal{F}|_{K}
.

Thus we can proceed in a classical way, see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 9.16]
, using
[
12
, Theorem 4.26]
.

Indeed, first observe that, since
K
is bounded, we may assume
r\geq 2
.

Then, let
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
and
\tau_{h}u:=u(\cdot+h)
, with
h\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we have

\displaystyle|\tau_{h}u(x)-u(x)|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{[{\rm Re}(u)(x+h)-{\rm Re}(u)(x)]^{2}+[{\rm Im}(u)(x+h)-{\rm Im}(u)(x))]^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|{\rm Re}(u)(x+h)-{\rm Re}(u)(x)|+|{\rm Im}(u)(x+h)-{\rm Im}(u)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|v_{\rm R}^{\prime}(t)|dt+\int_{0}^{1}|v_{\rm I}^{\prime}(t)|dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}|h\cdot\nabla{\rm Re}(u)(x+th)|dt+\int_{0}^{1}|h\cdot\nabla{\rm Im}(u)(x+th)|dt

\displaystyle\leq|h|\left(\int_{0}^{1}(|\nabla{\rm Re}(u)(x+th)|+|\nabla{\rm Im}(u)(x+th)|)dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}|h|\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u(x+th)|dt

where
v_{\rm R}(t):={\rm Re}(u)(x+th)
and
v_{\rm I}(t):={\rm Im}(u)(x+th)
, and so, using the Hölder inequality,

|\tau_{h}u(x)-u(x)|^{2}\leq 2|h|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u(x+th)|^{2}dt.

Hence, using the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
, see
[
25
, Proposition 2.1]
,

\|\tau_{h}u-u\|_{2}\leq C|h|\|\nabla u\|_{2}\leq C|h|

uniformly with respect to
u\in\mathcal{H}
.

For
r>2
, if we take
q>r
, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1-\alpha}{q},

so that, for every
u\in\mathcal{H}
,

\|\tau_{h}u-u\|_{r}\leq\|\tau_{h}u-u\|_{2}^{\alpha}\|\tau_{h}u-u\|_{q}^{1-\alpha}\leq C|h|^{\alpha}

with
C
independent of the elements of
\mathcal{H}
.

Thus,
[
12
, Theorem 4.26]
implies that the closure of
\mathcal{H}|_{K}=\mathcal{F}|_{K}
is compact in
L^{r}(K,\mathbb{C})
.

Finally, if
V_{\infty}=+\infty
, let
\{u_{n}\}\subset H_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence weakly convergent to
u
in
H_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{n}:=u_{n}-u
.

We have that
v_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
.

Indeed, for every
\eta>0
, there exists
\sigma>0
large enough such that

\frac{1}{V(x)}<\eta\text{ in }B_{\sigma}^{c}(0).

Moreover, since
v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H_{\varepsilon}
, then
v_{n}\to 0
in
L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
.
Thus, for
n
large enough,

\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2}<\eta+\frac{\eta}{V_{0}}\int_{B_{\sigma/\varepsilon}^{c}(0)}V_{\varepsilon}(x)|v_{n}|^{2}dx<C\eta.

Hence, using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality applied to
|v_{n}|
, for every
r\geq 2
we have

\|v_{n}\|_{r}\leq C\|\nabla|v_{n}|\|_{2}^{1-2/r}\|v_{n}\|_{2}^{2/r},

and, by the diamagnetic inequality (
2.1
) and the boundedness of
\{v_{n}\}
in
H_{\varepsilon}
, we conclude.
∎

For compact supported functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
, we have the following result which will be very important for some estimates below.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
and
u
has a compact support, then
\omega:=e^{iA(0)\cdot x}u\in H_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\text{supp}(u)\subset B_{R}(0)
. Since
V
is continuous, it is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V_{\varepsilon}(x)|\omega|^{2}dx=\int_{B_{R}(0)}V_{\varepsilon}(x)|\omega|^{2}dx\leq C\|u\|_{2}^{2}<+\infty.

Moreover, since
V
and
A
are continuous, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{A_{\varepsilon}}\omega|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla\omega|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|A_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}|\omega|^{2}dx+2\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}iA_{\varepsilon}(x)\overline{\omega}\nabla\omega dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla\omega|^{2}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|A_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}|\omega|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}dx\right]<+\infty

and we conclude.
∎

1

) and (

For

If (

\zeta>0

\begin{split}0\leq\|u_{n}\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(|u_{n}|^{2})|u_{n}|^{2}dx+o_{n}(1)\\

&\leq\frac{\zeta}{V_{0}}\|u_{n}\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u_{n}|^{q}(e^{\alpha|u_{n}|^{2}}-1)dx+o_{n}(1).\end{split}

Lemma 4.6

For every

, let

Lemma 4.7

2.4

\zeta>0
and

in

\begin{split}\left|\text{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(|u|^{2})u\overline{(u-u_{n})}\psi dx\right|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(|u|^{2})|u||u-u_{n}|\psi dx\\
&\leq\zeta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u||u-u_{n}|\psi dx\\

&=o_{n}(1).\end{split}

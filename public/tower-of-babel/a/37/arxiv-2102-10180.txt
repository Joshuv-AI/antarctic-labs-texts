On the Long range Dependence of time-changed mixed fractional Brownian motion model
Shaykhah Alajmi 1 and Ezzedine Mliki 1,2

Abstract
. A time-changed mixed fractional Brownian motion is an iterated process constructed as the superposition of mixed fractional Brownian motion and other process. In this paper we consider mixed fractional Brownian motion of parameters a , b a,b and H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,\,1) time-changed by two processes, gamma and tempered stable subordinators. We present their main properties paying main attention to the long range dependence. We deduce that the fractional Brownian motion time-changed by gamma and tempered stable subordinators has long range dependence property for all H ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) H\in(0,1) .

1.
Introduction

The fractional Brownian motion (fBm)
B^{H}=\{B_{t}^{H},\,t\geq 0\}
with parameter
H
, is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{Cov}(B_{t}^{H},\,B_{s}^{H})=\frac{1}{2}[t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}],\quad s,t\geq 0,

where
H
is a real number in
(0,1),
called the Hurst index or Hurst exponent. The case
H=\frac{1}{2}
corresponds to the Brownian motion (Bm).

An extension of the fBm was introduced by Cheridito
[
5
]
, called the mixed fractional Brownian motion (mfBm for short) which is a linear combination between a Brownian motion and an independent fractional Brownian motion of Hurst exponent
H
, with stationary increments exhibit a long-range dependent for
H>\frac{1}{2}.
A mfBm of parameters
a,b
and
H
is a process
N^{H}(a,b)=\{N^{H}_{t}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}
, defined on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
by

\displaystyle N_{t}^{H}(a,b)=aB_{t}+bB_{t}^{H},

where
B=\left\{B_{t},t\geq 0\right\}
is a Brownian motion and
B^{H}=\left\{B_{t}^{H},t\geq 0\right\}
is an independent fractional Brownian motion of Hurst exponent
H\in(0,1)
. We refer also to
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
19
,
31
]
for further information on mfBm process.

The time-changed mixed fractional Brownian motion is defined as

\displaystyle Y^{H}_{\beta}(a,b)=\{Y^{H}_{\beta_{t}}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}=\{N^{H}_{\beta_{t}}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}

, where the parent process
N^{H}(a,b)
is a mfBm with parameters
a,b,

H\in(0,\,1)
and the subordinator
\beta=\{\beta_{t},\,t\geq 0\}
is assumed to be independent of both the Brownian motion and the fractional Brownian motion. If
H=\frac{1}{2}
, the process
Y^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\beta}(0,1)
is called subordinated Brownian motion, it was investigated in
[
17
,
24
]
. Also, the process
Y^{H}_{\beta}(0,1)
is called subordinated fractional Brownian motion it was investigated in
[
15
,
16
]
.

Time-changed process is constructed by taking superposition of tow independent stochastic systems. The evolution of time in external process is replaced by a non-decreasing stochastic process, called subordinator. The resulting time-changed process very often retain important properties of the external process, however certain characteristics might change. This idea of subordination was introduced by Bochner
[
4
]
and was explored in many papers (e.g.
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
21
,
22
,
25
,
27
,
30
]
).

The time-changed mixed fractional Brownian motion has been discussed in
[
10
]
to present a stochastic model of the discounted stock price in some arbitrage-free and complete financial markets. This model is the process

\displaystyle X_{t}^{H}(a,b)=X_{0}^{H}(a,b)\exp\{\mu\beta_{t}+\sigma N^{H}_{\beta_{t}}(a,b)\},

where
\mu
is the rate of the return and
\sigma
is the volatility and
\beta_{t}
is the
\alpha
-inverse stable subordinator.

The time-changed processes have found many interesting applications, for example in finance
[
20
,
10
,
13
,
25
,
28
]
, in statistical inference
[
14
]
and in physics
[
9
]
.

Our goal in this parer is to study the main properties of the time-changed mixed fractional Brownian motion model paying attention to the long range dependence property. In the first case the internal process, which plays role of time, is the tempered stable subordinator while in the second case the internal process is the gamma subordinator.

2.
MfBm time-changed by Tempered Stable Subordinator

Definition 2.1
.

Tempered Stable Subordinator with index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and tempering parameter
\lambda>0
(TSS) is the non-decreasing and non-negative Lévy process
S^{\lambda,\alpha}=\{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
with density function:

\displaystyle f_{\lambda,\alpha}(x,t)=exp(-\lambda x+\lambda^{\alpha}t)f_{\alpha}(x,t),\ \ \lambda>0,\ \ \alpha\in(0,1),

where

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xy}e^{-ty^{\alpha}\cos\alpha\pi}\sin(ty^{\alpha}\sin\alpha\pi)dy.

More detail about TSS can be founded in
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
15
]
for the proof)

For
q>0,
the asymptotic behavior of q-th order moments of
S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t})^{q}\sim(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1}t)^{q},\quad as\;t\rightarrow\infty.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
N^{H}(a,b)=\{N^{H}_{t}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}
be a mfBm and let
S^{\lambda,\alpha}=\{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
be a TSS with index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and tempering parameter
\lambda>0
. The time-changed process of
N^{H}(a,b)
by means of
S^{\lambda,\alpha}
is the process
Y_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}}^{H}(a,b)=\{Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},\;t\geq 0\}
defined by:

(2.1)

\displaystyle Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}=N^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}(a,b)=aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}+bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},\quad(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

where the subordinator
S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}
is assumed to be independent of both the Bm and the fBm.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two random variables defined on the same probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P).
We denote the correlation coefficient
Corr(X,Y)
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle Corr(X,Y)=\frac{Cov(X,Y)}{\sqrt{Var(X)Var(Y)}}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
Y_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}}^{H}(a,b)
be the mfBm time-changed by
S^{\lambda,\alpha}
. Then by Taylor’s expansion we get, for fixed
s
and large
t
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}Hs(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H-1},\ \ as\ \ t\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

For fixed
s
and using (
[
15
]
, pp 195), the process
Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}
follows

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}-(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(N^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}(a,b))^{2}+(N^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}(a,b))^{2}-(N^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}(a,b)-N^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}(a,b))^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}+bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}+bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[\left(a(B_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-B_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})+b(B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})\right)^{2}\right].

Since
B^{H}
has stationary increments, then

(2.4)

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}+bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}+bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}-(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}}+bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+(bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+2abB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}+(bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}+2abB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}\right]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}E\left[(aB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}})^{2}+(bB^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}})^{2}+2abB_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}}B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}}\right].

By the independence of
B_{t}
and
B_{t}^{H}
, we get

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\left[E(B_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+E(B_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}-E(B_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left[E(B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}+E(B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}-E(B^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}E(B^{\frac{1}{2}}(1))^{2}\left[E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t})+E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s})-E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s})\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}E(B^{H}(1))^{2}\left[E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t})^{2H}+E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s})^{2H}-E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t-s})^{2H}\right].

Hence for large
t
and using Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\left[(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})t+E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s})-(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1}){(t-s)}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left[(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H}+E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s})^{2H}-(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}{(t-s)}^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})t\left(\frac{s}{t}+E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s})t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H}\left(2H\frac{s}{t}+E(S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s})^{2H}t^{-2H}+O(t^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})s+b^{2}Hs(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H-1}.

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
N^{H}(a,b)=\{N^{H}_{t}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}
be the mfBm of parameters
a,b
and
H
. Let
S^{\lambda,\alpha}=\{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
be the TSS with index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and tempering parameter
\lambda>0

and let
Y_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}}^{H}(a,b)
be the mfBm time-changed process by means of
S^{\lambda,\alpha}.
Then for fixed
s>0
and
t\rightarrow\infty
, we get

\displaystyle E[(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a^{2}t(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}H(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H}-a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})-2b^{2}Hs(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H-1}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}H(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}s^{2H}.

Proof.

Let
s>0
be fixed and
t\rightarrow\infty.
Then by using Eq. (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle E[(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}-Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}+(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}})^{2}-2Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}}+(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}a^{2}t(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}H(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H}-a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2b^{2}Hs(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H-1}+\frac{1}{2}a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}H(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}s^{2H}.

∎

Now we discuss the long range dependence behavior of
Y_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}}^{H}(a,b).

Definition 2.3
.

Note that, a finite variance stationary process
\{X_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
is said to have long range dependence property (Cont and Tankov
[
6
]
), if
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}=\infty
, where

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=Cov(X_{k},X_{k+1}).

In the following definition we give the equivalent definition for a non-stationary process
\{X_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
s>0
be fixed and
t>s
. Then process
\{X_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
is said to have long range dependence property property if

\displaystyle Corr(X_{t},X_{s})\sim c(s)t^{-d},\ \ as\ \ t\rightarrow\infty,

where
c(s)
is a constant depending on
s
and
d\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
N^{H}(a,b)=\{N^{H}_{t}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}
be the mfBm of parameters
a,b
and
H
. Let
S^{\lambda,\alpha}=\{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
be the TSS with index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and tempering parameter
\lambda>0
. Then the time-changed mixed fractional Brownian motion by means of
S^{\lambda,\alpha}
has long range dependence property for every
H>\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

The process
Y_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}}^{H}(a,b)
is not stationary, hence the Definition
2.4
will be used to establish the long range dependence property.

Let
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
. Using Eqs. (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and by Taylor’s expansion we get, as
t\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle Corr(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}},Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}Hs(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H-1}}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{2}a^{2}(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})t+b^{2}H(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H}\right)}\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}a^{2}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})+b^{2}Hs(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H-1}}{\sqrt{b^{2}H(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{2H}t^{2H}\left[\frac{a^{2}}{2b^{2}H}(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{1-2H}t^{1-2H}+1\right]}\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{a^{2}}{2}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{1-H}}{|b|\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}}}t^{-H}+\frac{|b|H^{\frac{1}{2}}s(\alpha\lambda^{\alpha-1})^{H}}{\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{s}})^{2}}}t^{H-1}.

Then the correlation function of
Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}_{t}}
decays like a mixture of power law
t^{-H}+t^{-(1-H)}
and the time-changed process
Y^{H}_{S^{\lambda,\alpha}}(a,b)
exhibits long range dependence property for all
H>\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

3.
MfBm time-changed by the gamma subordinator

Definition 3.1
.

Gamma process
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{t},t\geq\}
is a Stationary independent increments process with gamma distribution. More precisely, the increment
\Gamma_{t+s}-\Gamma_{s}
have density function

\displaystyle f(x,t)=\dfrac{1}{\Gamma(t/\nu)}x^{(t/\nu)-1}e^{-x},\quad x>0,\quad\nu>0.

More detail about gamma subordinator can be founded in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(see
[
16
]
for the proof)

For
q>0,
the asymptotic behavior of q-th order moments of
\Gamma_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle E(\Gamma_{t})^{q}\sim\left(\frac{t}{\nu}\right)^{q},\quad as\;t\rightarrow\infty.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
N^{H}(a,b)=\{N^{H}_{t}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}
be a mfBm and let
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
be a gamma subordinator. The time-changed process of
N^{H}(a,b)
by means of
\Gamma
is the process
Y_{\Gamma}^{H}(a,b)=\{Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}},\;t\geq 0\}
defined by:

(3.1)

\displaystyle Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}=N^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}(a,b)=aB_{\Gamma_{t}}+bB^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}},\quad(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},

where the subordinator
\Gamma_{t}
is assumed to be independent of both the Bm and the fBm.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
Y_{\Gamma}^{H}(a,b)
be the mfBm time-changed by
\Gamma
. Then we have

(1)

For
s<t
, the covariance function for the process
Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}
follows

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}},Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{\Gamma(1+t/\nu)}{\Gamma(t/\nu)}+\frac{\Gamma(1+s/\nu)}{\Gamma(s/\nu)}-\frac{\Gamma(1+(t-s)/\nu)}{\Gamma((t-s)/\nu)}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{\Gamma(2H+t/\nu)}{\Gamma(t/\nu)}+\frac{\Gamma(2H+s/\nu)}{\Gamma(s/\nu)}-\frac{\Gamma(2H+(t-s)/\nu)}{\Gamma((t-s)/\nu)}\right].

(2)

For fixed
s
and large
t
, the process
Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}
follows

(3.2)

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}},Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{s}})\sim a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}.

Proof.

(1)

Let
s<t
. Using similar procedure as the proof of Eq. (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}},Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\left[E(B_{\Gamma_{t}})^{2}+E(B_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}-E(B_{\Gamma_{t-s}})^{2}\right]+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left[E(B^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}})^{2}+E(B^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}-E(B^{H}_{\Gamma_{t-s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{\Gamma(1+t/\nu)}{\Gamma(t/\nu)}+\frac{\Gamma(1+s/\nu)}{\Gamma(s/\nu)}-\frac{\Gamma(1+(t-s)/\nu)}{\Gamma((t-s)/\nu)}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}\left[\frac{\Gamma(2H+t/\nu)}{\Gamma(t/\nu)}+\frac{\Gamma(2H+s/\nu)}{\Gamma(s/\nu)}-\frac{\Gamma(2H+(t-s)/\nu)}{\Gamma((t-s)/\nu)}\right].

(2)

Let
g(x)=\Gamma(x+2H)/\Gamma(x)
and
f(x)=\Gamma(x+1)/\Gamma(x)
. By Taylor expansion and
[
16
]
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{g(x+h)}{g(x)}=1+2H(h/x)+H(2H-1)(h/x)^{2}+O(x^{-3}),

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{f(x+h)}{f(x)}=1+(h/x)+O(x^{-2}).

For fixed
s
and large
t
, using Eqs. (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
),
Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}
follows

\displaystyle Cov(Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{t}},Y^{H}_{{\Gamma}_{s}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}f(t/\nu)\left[1+\frac{f(s/\nu)}{f(t/\nu)}-\frac{f((t-s)/\nu)}{f(t/\nu)}\right]+\frac{b^{2}}{2}g(t/\nu)\left[1+\frac{g(s/\nu)}{g(t/\nu)}-\frac{g((t-s)/\nu)}{g(t/\nu)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}}{2}(t/\nu)\left[1+\frac{f(s/\nu)}{f(t/\nu)}-\left(1-\frac{s}{t}+O(t^{-2})\right)\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b^{2}}{2}(t/\nu)^{2H}\bigg[1+\frac{g(s/\nu)}{g(t/\nu)}-\bigg(1-2H\left(\frac{s}{t}\right)+H(2H-1)\left(\frac{s^{2}}{t^{2}}\right)+O(t^{-3})\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
N^{H}(a,b)=\{N^{H}_{t}(a,b),\;t\geq 0\}
be the mfBm and let
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{t},\;t\geq 0\}
be a gamma subordinator. Let
Y_{\Gamma}^{H}(a,b)=\{Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}},\;t\geq 0\}
be the mfBm time-changed by means of
\Gamma.
Then for fixed
s>0
and

, we have

(1)

\displaystyle E[(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}-Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}]\sim a^{2}\frac{t}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}H}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H}-2a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}-\frac{4b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}+a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}H}{\nu^{2H}}s^{2H}.

(2)

For
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
. The correlation function is given by

(3.5)

\displaystyle Corr(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}},Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})\sim\frac{a^{2}(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s}{\nu^{1-H}|b|\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}t^{-H}+\frac{|b|(2H)^{\frac{1}{2}}s}{\nu^{H}\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}t^{H-1}.

Proof.

Let
s>0
be fixed and large
t
. Then

(1)

Using Eq. (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle E[(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}-Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}})^{2}-2Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}}+(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle a^{2}\frac{t}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}H}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H}-2a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}-\frac{4b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}+a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}H}{\nu^{2H}}s^{2H}.

(2)

Let
\frac{1}{2}<H<1
. Using Eqs. (
2.2
), (
3.2
) and by Taylor’s expansion we get, as
t\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle Corr(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}},Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}}{\sqrt{(a^{2}\frac{t}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}H}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H})}\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|b|(2H)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{t}{\nu})^{H}\sqrt{\left[1+\frac{a^{2}}{2b^{2}H\nu^{1-2H}}t^{1-2H}\right]}}\frac{[a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}]}{\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{t}{\nu})^{-H}}{|b|\left[1+\frac{a^{2}}{2b^{2}H\nu^{1-2H}}t^{1-2H}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{[a^{2}\frac{s}{\nu}+\frac{2b^{2}Hs}{\nu^{2H}}t^{2H-1}]}{\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}(2H)^{-\frac{1}{2}}s}{\nu^{1-H}|b|\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}t^{-H}+\frac{|b|(2H)^{\frac{1}{2}}s}{\nu^{H}\sqrt{E(Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{s}})^{2}}}t^{H-1}.

Hence the correlation function of
Y^{H}_{\Gamma_{t}}
decays like a mixture of power law
t^{-H}+t^{-(1-H)}.

On faithfully balancedness in functor categories
Julia Sauter

Abstract
. This is a generalization of some results of Ma-Sauter from module categories over artin algebras to more general functor categories (and partly to exact categories). In particular, we generalize the definition of a faithfully balanced module to a faithfully balanced subcategory and find the generalizations of dualities and characterizations from Ma-Sauter.

Introduction

For an exact category
\mathcal{E}
in the sense of Quillen and a full subcategory
\mathcal{M}

(and

We give the relatively obvious but technical generalizations of results in

]
related to these categories and functors. If

Let
\mathcal{P}

Cogen

-duality:

.

The symmetry principle

Let

2.
In additive categories

Here we want to extend Yoneda’s embedding to a bigger subcategory:
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category and
\mathcal{M}
an essentially small full additive subcategory. A right
\mathcal{M}
-module is a contravariant additive functor from
\mathcal{M}
into abelian groups. We denote by
\Modu-\mathcal{M}
the category of all right
\mathcal{M}
-modules. This is an abelian category. We have the fully faithful (covariant) Yoneda embedding
\mathcal{M}\to\Modu-\mathcal{M}
defined by
M\mapsto\Hom_{\mathcal{M}}(-,M)
. Clearly, we can extend this functor to a functor
\Phi\colon\mathcal{C}\to\Modu-\mathcal{M},\;\Phi(X):=\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,X)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}=(-,X)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
where the last notation is our shortage for the Hom functor.
The aim of this section is to define a subcategory
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{C}
such that
\Phi\lvert_{\mathcal{G}}
is fully faithful.

We define a full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
as follows

\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M}):=\Bigg\{Z\in\mathcal{C}\mid\begin{aligned} &\exists M_{1}\xrightarrow{f}M_{0}\xrightarrow{g}Z,\;M_{i}\in\mathcal{M},\;g=\coKer(f)\text{ is an epim. }\\
&(M,M_{1})\to(M,M_{0})\to(M,Z)\to 0\text{ ex. seq. of ab. groups }\forall M\in\mathcal{M}\end{aligned}\Bigg\}

We observe that
g=\coKer(f)
and
g
an epimorphism is equivalent to that we have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{C}^{op}
-modules

0\to(Z,-)\to(M_{0},-)\to(M_{1},-)

Furthermore the second line in the definition is equivalent to an exact sequence in
\Modu-\mathcal{M}

(-,M_{1})\to(-,M_{0})\to(-,Z)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}\to 0.

Dually, we define
\cogen_{\add}^{1}(\mathcal{M}):=(\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M}^{op}))^{op}
where
\mathcal{M}^{op}
is considered as a full additive subcategory of
\mathcal{C}^{op}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

The functor
\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M})\to\Modu-\mathcal{M}
defined by
Z\mapsto(-,Z)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
is fully faithful. We even have for every
Z\in\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M}),C\in\mathcal{C}
a natural isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(Z,C)\to\Hom_{\Modu-\mathcal{M}}((-,Z)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}},(-,C)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}})

(2)

The functor
\cogen^{1}_{\add}(\mathcal{M})\to\Modu-\mathcal{M}^{op}
defined by
Z\mapsto(Z,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
is fully faithful.
We even have for every
Z\in\cogen^{1}_{\add}(\mathcal{M}),C\in\mathcal{C}
a natural isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(C,Z)\to\Hom_{\Modu-\mathcal{M}^{op}}((Z,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}},(C,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}})

Proof.

We only prove (1), the second statement follows by passing to opposite categories. We consider the functor
\Phi\colon\mathcal{C}\to\Modu-\mathcal{M}
defined by
\Phi(X):=(-,X)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
. Since
Z\in\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M})
we an exact sequences

0\to(Z,C)\to(M_{0},C)\to(M_{1},C)\quad\text{of ab. groups}

and
\Phi(M_{1})\to\Phi(M_{0})\to\Phi(Z)\to 0
in
\Modu-\mathcal{M}
.
By applying
(-,\Phi(C))
to the second exact sequence we obtain an exact sequence

0\to(\Phi(Z),\Phi(C))\to(\Phi(M_{0}),\Phi(C))\to(\Phi(M_{1}),\Phi(C))\quad\text{of ab. groups.}

Since
\Phi
is a functor, we find a commuting diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(Z,C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M_{0},C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M_{1},C)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\Phi(Z),\Phi(C))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\Phi(M_{0}),\Phi(C))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(\Phi(M_{1}),\Phi(C))}

By the Lemma of Yoneda, we have for every
F\in\Modu-\mathcal{M}
and
M\in\mathcal{M}
that
\Hom_{\Modu-\mathcal{M}}(\Phi(M),F)=F(M)
. This implies that the maps
(M_{i},C)\to(\Phi(M_{i}),\Phi(C))
are isomorphisms of groups. and therefore, the induced map on the kernels is an isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is not essentially small,
\Hom_{\mathcal{M}-\Modu}(F,G)
is not necessarily a set. But if one passes to the full subcategory of finitely presented
\mathcal{M}
-modules
\modu_{1}-\mathcal{M}
, this set-theoretic issue does not arise:
Observe that
Z\mapsto(-,Z)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
defines by definition a covariant functor

\Phi\colon\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M})\to\modu_{1}-\mathcal{M},

the same proof as before shows that this is fully faithful. Similarly, the functor
Z\mapsto(Z,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
defines a fully faithful contravariant functor

\Psi\colon\cogen^{1}_{\add}(\mathcal{M})\to\modu_{1}-\mathcal{M}^{op}.

3.
In exact categories

This section is a generalization of results from
[
3
]
.
For exact categories we have subcategories of
\cogen^{1}_{\add}
such that
\Psi
induces isomorphisms on (some) extension groups (cf. Lemma
3.3
).

Given an exact category
\mathcal{E}
with a full additive subcategory
\mathcal{M}
, we define
\cogen^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{E}
to be the full subcategory of all objects
X
such that there is an exact sequence

0\to X\to M_{0}\to\cdots\to M_{k}\to Z\to 0

with
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M},0\leq i\leq k
such that for every
M\in\mathcal{M}
the sequence

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(M_{k},M)\to\cdots\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(M_{0},M)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,M)\to 0

is an exact sequence of abelian groups.

We define
\gen_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
to be the full additive category of
\mathcal{E}
given by all
X
such that there is an exact sequence

0\to Z\to M_{k}\to\cdots\to M_{0}\to X\to 0

with
M_{i}\in\mathcal{M},0\leq i\leq k
such that for every
M\in\mathcal{M}
we have an exact sequence

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(M,M_{k})\to\cdots\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(M,M_{0})\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(M,X)\to 0

of abelian groups.

If it is clear from the context in which exact category we are working, then we leave out the index
\mathcal{E}
and just write
\cogen^{k}(\mathcal{M})
and
\gen_{k}(\mathcal{M})
.

Remark 3.1
.

Observe that
\cogen^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})\subset\cogen^{1}_{\add}(\mathcal{M})
,
\gen_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})\subset\gen_{1}^{\add}(\mathcal{M})
for
k\geq 1
and therefore the functor
\Psi\colon X\mapsto(X,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
(resp.
\Phi\colon X\mapsto(-,X)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}
) is fully faithful on
\cogen^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
(resp. on
\gen_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
) by Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.2
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
. We denote by
\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}
the category of
\mathcal{M}
-modules which admit a
k
-presentation (indexed from
0
to
k
) by finitely presented projectives. For
F\in\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}
the Ext-groups
\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{M}-\Modu}(F,G)
with
0\leq i<k
are sets.

If
X\in\cogen^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
, then we have
\Psi(X)=(X,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}\;\in\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}^{op}(=:\mathcal{M}-\modu_{k})
.

If
Y\in\gen_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
, then we have
\Phi(Y)=(-,Y)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}\;\in\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}
.

Since we are now working in exact categories, we observe the following isomorphisms on extension groups:

.

Let

.

(a)

If

(a) the proof is a straight forward generalization of

We will later use the following simple observation:

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category,
\mathcal{X}
be a fully exact category and
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{X}
an additive subcategory. We say
\mathcal{X}
is

Now, we study these subcategories in more general exact categories.
For an exact category

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}\colon\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{op}}

Corollary 3.15
.

(of Lem.
3.13

3.14

(1) Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category with enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{M}
a full additive subcategory. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

X\in\gen_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})

(2)

\Phi(X)\in\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}

\Phi(X)\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi(P)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(P,X),\;\;g\otimes f\mapsto g\circ f

\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{M}

(1)

X\in\cogen^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})

(2)

\Psi(X)\in\mathcal{M}-\modu_{k}

\Phi(I)\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi(X)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{F}}(X,I),\;\;g\otimes f\mapsto g\circ f

is an isomorphism,
\Tor^{i}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Phi(I),\Psi(X))=0
,
1\leq i<k

Theorem 3.16
.

(Symmetry principle).
Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category with enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
and enough injectives
\mathcal{I}
and
k\geq 1
. The following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{P}\subset\cogen^{k}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
and
\Phi(I)=\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(-,I)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}\quad\in\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}
for every
I\in\mathcal{I}

(2)

\mathcal{I}\subset\gen_{k}^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathcal{M})
and
\Psi(P)=\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(P,-)\lvert_{\mathcal{M}}\quad\in\mathcal{M}-\modu_{k}
for every
P\in\mathcal{P}

Proof.

We consider
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{I}
as before defined for the category
\mathcal{E}
.
Then, it is straight forward from the previous Lemma to see that (1) and (2) are both equivalent to for all
P\in\mathcal{P},I\in\mathcal{I}
,
\Psi(P)\in\mathcal{M}-\modu_{k},\Phi(I)\in\modu_{k}-\mathcal{M}
and

\Phi(I)\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}\Psi(P)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(P,I),\;\;g\otimes f\mapsto g\circ f

is an isomorphism,
\Tor^{i}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Phi(I),\Psi(P))=0
,
1\leq i<k
.
Therefore (1) and (2) are equivalent.
∎

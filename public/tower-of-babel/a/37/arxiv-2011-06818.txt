A new iterative method for solving a class of two-by-two block complex linear systems
Davod Khojasteh Salkuyeh

Abstract




Abstract.
We present a stationary iteration method, namely
Alternating Symmetric positive definite and Scaled symmetric positive semidefinite Splitting (ASSS)
, for solving the system of linear equations obtained by using finite element discretization of a distributed optimal control problem together with time-periodic parabolic equations. An upper bound for the spectral radius of the iteration method is given which is always

less than 1
. So convergence of the ASSS iteration method is guaranteed. The induced ASSS preconditioner is applied to accelerate the convergence speed of the GMRES method for solving the system. Numerical results are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of both the ASSS iteration method and the ASSS preconditioner.

Keywords
:
Iterative, PDE-constrained, optimization, convergence, finite element, GMRES, preconditioning.

AMS Subject Classification
: 49M25, 49K20, 65F10, 65F50.

Introduction

Consider the distributed control problems of the form: (see
[
14
,
12
,
24
,
25
]
)

\displaystyle\min_{y,u}~~~\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|y(x,t)-y_{d}(x,t)|^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt+\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}dxdt,

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\text{s.t.}~~~~~~~~~~\frac{\partial}{\partial t}y(x,t)-\Delta y(x,t)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle u(x,t)~~{\rm in}~~Q_{T},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hskip 56.9055pt

\displaystyle y(x,t)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle 0~~{\rm on}~~\Sigma_{T},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hskip 2.84544pt\hskip 56.9055pt

\displaystyle y(x,0)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle y(x,T)~~{\rm on}~~\partial\Omega,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hskip 56.9055pt

\displaystyle u(x,0)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle u(x,T)~~{\rm in}~~\Omega,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hskip 1.42271pt\hskip 56.9055pt

where
\Omega
is an open and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\in\{1,2,3\})
and its boundary
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz-continuous. We introduce the space-time cylinder
Q_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
and its lateral surface

\Sigma_{T}=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
. Here,
\nu
is a regularization parameter,
y_{d}(x,t)
is a desired state and

T>0
.
Problems of the form above arise widely in many areas of science and engineering
[
12
,
13
]
.

We may assume that
y_{d}(x,t)
is time-harmonic, i.e.,

y_{d}(x,t)=y_{d}(x)e^{i\omega t}
, with
\omega={2\pi k}/{T}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If we substitute
y(x,t)
,
y_{d}(x,t)
and
u(x,t)
in the problem then we get the following time-independent problem

\displaystyle\min_{y,u}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|y(x)-y_{d}(x)|^{2}dx+\frac{\nu}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle s.t:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i\omega y(x)}-\Delta y(x)=u(x)~~\text{in}~~\Omega,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\hskip 54.06006pt{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}y(x)=0},\hskip 17.07182pt\text{on}~~\partial\Omega.

We assume that
V_{h}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is an
m
-dimensional vector space spanned by the basis
\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{m}\}
. The subspace
V_{h}
is utilized for computing both the functions
y
and
u
.
By applying the approach of discretize-then-optimization (see
[
16
]
), the above problem can be described as the following form
[
13
]

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\min_{y,u}&&\frac{1}{2}(\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{d})^{*}M(\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{d})+\frac{\nu}{2}\bar{u}^{*}M\bar{u},\\
s.t:&&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}iwM}\bar{y}+K\bar{y}=M\bar{u},\end{array}

(1)

where the discretized negative Laplacian is interpreted by the matrix
K=(\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{i}.\nabla\varphi_{j}dx)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
(the stiffness matrix) and
M=(\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}dx)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
denotes the mass matrix. Here, the vectors
\bar{y}
,
\bar{y}_{d}
, and
\bar{u}
denote the coefficients of the basis functions in
V_{h}
. We define the Lagrangian functional for the discretized problem as

{\cal L}(\bar{y},\bar{u},\bar{p})=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{d})^{*}M(\bar{y}-\bar{y}_{d})+\frac{\nu}{2}\bar{u}^{*}M\bar{u}+{\bar{p}}^{*}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}i\omega M}\bar{y}+K\bar{y}-M\bar{u}),

with
\bar{p}
being the Lagrange multiplier of the constraint. By using the Lagrange multiplier technique, we set
\nabla{\cal L}(\bar{y},\bar{u},\bar{p})=0
, which is equivalent to

\begin{pmatrix}M&0&K-i\omega M\\
0&\nu M&-M\\
K+i\omega M&-M&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{y}}\\
{\bar{u}}\\
{\bar{p}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}M{\bar{y}_{d}}\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

It is known that, Eq. (
2
) gives the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a solution for the problem (
1
). It follows from the second equation that
\bar{u}=\frac{1}{\nu}\bar{p}
and substituting
\bar{u}
in the third equation, gives the following complex system of equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}M\bar{y}+(K-i{\omega}M)\bar{p}&=M{\bar{y}_{d}},\\
(K+i{\omega}M)\bar{y}-\frac{1}{\nu}M\bar{p}&=0.\end{array}\right.

The above system can be equivalently rewritten as

A{x}=\begin{pmatrix}M&\sqrt{\nu}(K-i\omega M)\\
\sqrt{\nu}(K+i\omega M)&-M\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\bar{y}}\\
{\bar{q}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\hat{y}_{d}}\\
0\end{pmatrix}=b,

(3)

where
\bar{q}=\bar{p}/\sqrt{\nu}
and
\hat{y}_{d}=M{\bar{y}_{d}}
.

Since the systems of the form (
3
) are of very large size, it is important to employ iterative methods in incorporated with suitable preconditioners for solving these systems.
In
[
13
]
, Krendl et al. presented the real block diagonal (
\mathcal{P}_{BD}
) and the alternative indefinite (
\mathcal{P}_{AI}
) preconditioners

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{BD}=\begin{pmatrix}M+\sqrt{\nu}(K+\omega M)&0\\
0&M+\sqrt{\nu}(K+\omega M)\end{pmatrix},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{AI}=\begin{pmatrix}0&M+\sqrt{\nu}(K-i\omega M)\\
M+\sqrt{\nu}(K+i\omega M)&0\end{pmatrix},

(5)

for the system (
3
). Zheng et al. in
[
25
]
presented an improved version of the block-diagonal preconditioner
\mathcal{P}_{BD}
. Zheng et al. in
[
24
]
designed the block alternating splitting (BAS) iteration method for solving the system (
3
) which can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(\alpha V+H_{1})x^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}&=(\alpha V-S_{1})x^{(k)}+\mathcal{P}_{1}b,\\
(\alpha V+H_{2})x^{(k+1)}&=(\alpha V-S_{2})x^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}+\mathcal{P}_{2}b,\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha>0
,
V
is a symmetric positive definite matrix,

H_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\
0&M\end{pmatrix},\quad H_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\nu}K&0\\
0&\sqrt{\nu}K\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\omega^{2}\nu}\begin{pmatrix}-{i\omega\nu}K&{\sqrt{\nu}}K\\
-{\sqrt{\nu}}K&{i\omega\nu}K\end{pmatrix},\qquad S_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}i\sqrt{\nu}\omega M&-M\\
M&-i\sqrt{\nu}\omega M\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\omega^{2}\nu}\begin{pmatrix}-I&-i\omega\sqrt{\nu}I\\
i\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&-I\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{2}=\frac{1}{1+\omega^{2}\nu}\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
I&0\end{pmatrix}.

They proposed using the matrix
V=H_{1}
as the preconditioner in practical implementation
. They proved that the BAS iteration method is convergent if
\alpha\geq\nu\omega^{2}/2
. Numerical results presented in
[
24
]
demonstrate that the BAS iteration method outperforms the GMRES method
[
19
,
20
]
. On the other hand, the BAS iteration method induces the preconditioner

P_{BAS}(\alpha)=(1+\alpha)P(\alpha)\begin{pmatrix}\alpha M+\sqrt{\nu}K&0\\
0&\alpha M+\sqrt{\nu}K\end{pmatrix},

with

P(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha(2+\omega^{2}\nu)}\begin{pmatrix}I&(1+\omega^{2}\nu-i\omega\sqrt{\nu})I\\
(1+\omega^{2}\nu+i\omega\sqrt{\nu})I&-I\end{pmatrix}.

They also numerically illustrated that the parameter
\alpha=1+\nu\omega^{2}
usually gives good results for the BAS iteration method and

\alpha=(1+\nu\omega^{2})/(1+\sqrt{\nu}\omega)
is a good choice for the BAS preconditioner.
The reported numerical results in
[
24
]
showed that the BAS preconditioner is superior to the preconditioners
\mathcal{P}_{BD}
and
\mathcal{P}_{AI}
.

Recently, Mirchi and Salkuyeh
[
15
]
stated the single block splitting (SBS) iteration method for solving (
3
). They proved that if

\frac{\nu\omega\mu_{\max}}{\sqrt{(1+\omega^{2}\nu)^{2}+\nu\mu_{\max}^{2}}}<1,

then the SBS method is convergent, where
\mu_{\max}
denotes the largest eigenvalue of the matrix
Q=M^{-\frac{1}{2}}KM^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
However, if
\sqrt{\nu}\omega
is large, then the SBS method may converge slowly or even fail to converge
[
15
]
.

One may rewrite the system (
3
) in the real form

\mathcal{K}\begin{pmatrix}r\\
s\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}E&F^{T}\\
F&-E\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}r\\
s\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}t\\
0\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where

E=\begin{pmatrix}M&O\\
O&M\end{pmatrix},\quad F=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\nu}K&-\sqrt{\nu}\omega M\\
\sqrt{\nu}\omega M&\sqrt{\nu}K\end{pmatrix},

(7)

and

r=\begin{pmatrix}\Re(\bar{y})\\
\Im(\bar{y})\end{pmatrix},\quad s=\begin{pmatrix}\Re(\bar{q})\\
\Im(\bar{q})\end{pmatrix},\quad t=\begin{pmatrix}\Re({\hat{y}_{d}})\\
\Im({\hat{y}_{d}})\end{pmatrix}.

It is noted that if
z
is a complex vector, then
\Re(z)
and
\Im(z)
denote the real and imaginary parts of
z
, respectively.
Axelsson and Lukáš in
[
1
]
(see also
[
4
]
) applied the preconditioned square block (PRESB) preconditioner

\mathcal{C}=\begin{pmatrix}E+F+F^{T}&F^{T}\\
F&-E\end{pmatrix},

(8)

for system of linear equations of the form (
6
). They proved that every eigenvalue of the matrix
\mathcal{C}^{-1}\mathcal{K}
lies in the interval
[\frac{1}{2},1]
(see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
]
). Therefore, the GMRES method is indeed appropriate for solving the preconditioned system. It turns out that for applying the preconditioner
\mathcal{C}
, we need solving two systems where the corresponding coefficient matrices are
E+F
and
E+F^{T}
. We can also solve these systems using the GMRES method incorporated with the PRESB preconditioner. To apply the PRESB preconditioner for solving these systems, we require to solve two subsystems with the coefficient matrix
S=(1+w\sqrt{\nu})M+\sqrt{\nu}K
. Since the matrix
S
is symmetric positive definite, these systems can be solved either exactly by the Cholesky factorization or inexactly by the conjugate gradient (CG) method. It is worth nothing that if these systems are solved inexactly then we must employ the flexible GMRES (FGMRES)
[
18
]
instead of the GMRES method.

In this paper, we present a stationary iteration method, namely Alternating Symmetric positive definite and Scaled symmetric positive semidefinite Splitting (ASSS), for solving the system (
3
) and verify its convergence properties and the corresponding induced preconditioner.

We use the following notation in the rest of the paper. For a square matrix
Z
, we denote
\sigma(Z)
,
\rho(Z)
and
\|Z\|_{2}
for the spectrum, spectral radius and 2-norm of the matrix, respectively. The
Matlab
notation
(x;y)
is used to denote the vector
(x^{T},y^{T})^{T}
. The imaginary unit (
\sqrt{-1}
) is shown by the letter
i
.
The matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is called positive real if
x^{T}Ax>0
, for every
0\neq x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
the ASSS iteration method is introduced and its convergence analysis is given. Inexact version of the ASSS iteration method is presented in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to introducing the ASSS preconditioner and its implementation issues. Numerical results are presented in Section
5

6
.

2
The ASSS iteration method

Using the idea of
[
23
]
, the system (
3
) can be written in the 4-by-4 block real system

\mathcal{A}{\bf x}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}M&0&\sqrt{\nu}K&\omega\sqrt{\nu}M\\
0&M&-\omega\sqrt{\nu}M&\sqrt{\nu}K\\
\sqrt{\nu}K&-\omega\sqrt{\nu}M&-M&0\\
\omega\sqrt{\nu}M&\sqrt{\nu}K&0&-M\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\Re(\bar{y})\\
\Im(\bar{y})\\
\Re(\bar{q})\\
\Im(\bar{q})\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\Re(\hat{y}_{d})\\
\Im(\hat{y}_{d})\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right)\equiv\hat{\mathbf{b}}.

(9)

We define the matrices
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
as following

{\mathcal{G}}_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}I&0&0&\omega\sqrt{\nu}I\\
0&I&-\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&0\\
0&-\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&-I&0\\
\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&0&0&-I\\
\end{array}\right),\quad{\mathcal{G}}_{2}=\sqrt{1+\nu\omega^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&I&0\\
0&0&0&I\\
I&0&0&0\\
0&I&0&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad

where
I\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is the identity matrix. Then, the system (
9
) can be equivalently rewritten as

({\mathcal{G}}_{1}{\mathcal{M}}+\frac{\sqrt{\nu}}{\sqrt{1+\nu\omega^{2}}}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}\hat{{\mathcal{K}}}){\bf x}=\hat{{\bf b}}.

(10)

where

{\mathcal{M}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}M&0&0&0\\
0&M&0&0\\
0&0&M&0\\
0&0&0&M\\
\end{array}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad\hat{{\mathcal{K}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}K&0&0&0\\
0&K&0&0\\
0&0&K&0\\
0&0&0&K\\
\end{array}\right).

Lemma 1
.

The matrix
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}
is nonsingular and

{\mathcal{G}}_{1}^{-1}=\frac{1}{1+\nu\omega^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}I&0&0&\omega\sqrt{\nu}I\\
0&I&-\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&0\\
0&-\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&-I&0\\
\omega\sqrt{\nu}I&0&0&-I\\
\end{array}\right).

Moreover, the matrix
{\mathcal{G}}:={\mathcal{G}}_{1}^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}_{2}
is of the form

{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu(1+\nu\omega^{2})}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&\omega\nu I&\sqrt{\nu}I&0\\
-\omega\nu I&0&0&\sqrt{\nu}I\\
-\sqrt{\nu}I&0&0&-\omega\nu I\\
0&-\sqrt{\nu}I&\omega\nu I&0\\
\end{array}\right).

Furthermore, we have
{\mathcal{G}}^{2}=-I
with
I
being the identity matrix of order
4m
,
{\mathcal{G}}^{-1}=-{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}^{T}=-{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward and is omitted here.
∎

Premultiplying both sides of the system (
10
) by
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}^{-1}
, gives the system

\mathcal{B}\bf{x}\equiv({\mathcal{M}}+{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{K}}){\bf x}={\bf b},

(11)

where
\mathcal{K}=\eta\hat{\mathcal{K}}
with
\eta=\sqrt{\nu}/\sqrt{1+\nu\omega^{2}}
and
{\bf b}={\mathcal{G}}_{1}^{-1}\hat{\bf{b}}
.

Given
\alpha>0
, we rewrite Eq. (
11
) as

(\alpha I+{\mathcal{M}}){\bf x}=(\alpha I-{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{K}}){\bf x}+{\bf b}.

(12)

Eq. (
11
) can also be reformulated in the following form

\mathcal{G}(\alpha I+{\mathcal{K}}){\bf x}=(\alpha{\mathcal{G}}-\mathcal{M}){\bf x}+{\bf b}.

Premultiplying both sides of this equation by
\mathcal{G}^{-1}
and having in mind that
\mathcal{G}^{-1}=-\mathcal{G}
, gives

(\alpha I+{\mathcal{K}}){\bf x}=(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M}){\bf x}-\mathcal{G}{\bf b}.

(13)

Now, using Eqs. (
12
) and (
13
) we establish the ASSS iteration method for solving the system (
11
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}(\alpha I+{\mathcal{M}}){\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}&=(\alpha I-{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{K}}){\bf x}^{(k)}+{\bf b},\\
(\alpha I+{\mathcal{K}}){\bf x}^{(k+1)}&=(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M}){\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}-\mathcal{G}{\bf b},\end{array}\right.

(14)

where
\bf{x}^{(0)}
is an initial guess.
It is worth noting that both of the matrices
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}
are
symmetric positive definite and the ASSS method is obtained using the splitting
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{M}}+{\mathcal{G}}{\mathcal{K}}
, which is indeed a splitting of a symmetric positive definite matrix and a scaled (by
\mathcal{G}
) symmetric positive definite matrix.

Eliminating
{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}
from Eq. (
14
) yields
the following stationary iterative method

{\bf x}^{(k+1)}=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}{\bf x}^{(k)}+\bf{f},

(15)

where

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}=(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(\alpha I-\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}),

is the iteration matrix of the ASSS method and

{\bf f}=\alpha(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}(I-\mathcal{G})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}{\bf b}.

The following theorem states the convergence of the ASSS method.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the matrices
K
and
M
are symmetric positive definite, and let
\alpha>0
. Then the spectral radius
\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})
of the ASSS iteration matrix

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}=(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(\alpha I-\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}),

satisfies

\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})\leq\gamma(\alpha)=\max_{\mu\in\sigma(M)}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}}}{\alpha+\mu}\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(K)}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(\eta\lambda)^{2}}}{\alpha+\eta\lambda},

where
\eta=\sqrt{\nu}/\sqrt{1+\nu\omega^{2}}
. Hence, it holds that

\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})\leq\gamma(\alpha)<1,\qquad\forall\alpha>0,

which shows that the ASSS iteration method converges unconditionally.

Proof.

By setting
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}=(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}
, we see that

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(\alpha I-\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K})(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha},

where
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}=(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}=(\alpha I-\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K})(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}
. Note that
the matrices
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha}
are similar, and as a result we deduce that

\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})=\rho(\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha})=\rho(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha})\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\|_{2}\|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\|_{2}.

On the other hand, it follows from
\mathcal{G}^{2}=-I
,
\mathcal{G}^{T}=-\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M}
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\left((\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(\alpha I-\mathcal{M}\mathcal{G})(\alpha I+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}\right)

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho((\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(\alpha^{2}I+\alpha\mathcal{M}\mathcal{G}-\alpha\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{M}\mathcal{G}^{2}\mathcal{M})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\left((\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-2}(\alpha^{2}I+\mathcal{M}^{2})(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\sigma(\mathcal{M})}\frac{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}}{(\alpha+\mu)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\sigma(M)}\frac{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}}{(\alpha+\mu)^{2}}.

In the same way, we deduce that

\|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}=\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(K)}\frac{\alpha^{2}+(\eta\lambda)^{2}}{(\alpha+\eta\lambda)^{2}}.

(17)

Since the matrices
M
and
K
are symmetric positive definite we have
\mu,\lambda>0
, for all
\mu\in\sigma(M)
and
\mu\in\sigma(K)
, and as result we deduce that
\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}}<\alpha+\mu
and

\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(\eta\lambda)^{2}}<\alpha+\eta\lambda
. Therefore, we see that
\|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\|_{2}<1
and
\|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\|_{2}<1
. Hence,
\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})\leq\gamma({\alpha})=\|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\|_{2}\|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\|_{2}<1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
, we have

\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(K)}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(\eta\lambda)^{2}}}{\alpha+\eta\lambda}<1,\quad\forall\alpha>0.

Hence,

\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})\leq\zeta(\alpha)=\max_{\mu\in\sigma(M)}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}}}{\alpha+\mu}.

(18)

Similar to Corollary 2.1 in
[
6
]
the minimum value of the
\zeta({\alpha})
is obtained at

\alpha^{*}=\sqrt{\mu_{\min}\mu_{\max}},

(19)

where
\mu_{\min}
and
\mu_{\max}
are the smallest and largest eigenvalues of the matrix
M
, respectively.
It is worth noting that the parameter
\alpha^{*}
is independent of the parameters
\nu
and
\omega
.

In practice, we can use a few iterations of the power method for computing
\mu_{\min}
and the inverse power method to calculate
\mu_{\max}
.

Remark 2
.

In Theorem
1
if the matrix
K
is symmetric positive semidefinite, then

\max_{\lambda\in\sigma(K)}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(\eta\lambda)^{2}}}{\alpha+\eta\lambda}\leq 1,\quad\forall\alpha>0,

and we have
\rho(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha})\leq\zeta(\alpha)<1
, where
\zeta(\alpha)
was defined in Eq. (
18
).
Hence, the ASSS iteration method is still convergent unconditionally.

There are several methods to estimate the parameters of the iterative methods and their induced preconditioners having similar structure to the ASSS iteration method (see
[
11
,
9
,
22
,
17
,
8
]
). In
[
17
]
, Ren and Cao proposed an efficient estimation formula for the iteration parameter of a class of alternating positive-semidefinite splitting preconditioner. The same idea was used by Cao to estimate the parameter of the block positive-semidefinite splitting preconditioner for generalized saddle point linear systems
[
8
]
. Similar to the strategy used in
[
17
,
8
]
, we can estimate the parameter of the ASSS method. However, it is not as effective as the parameter
\alpha^{*}=\sqrt{\mu_{\min}\mu_{\max}}
. We will shortly see in Section
5
that the value of
\alpha^{*}
can be computed inexpensively.

3
Inexact version of ASSS

Let
{\bf x}^{(k)}
be the computed solution solution at iteration
k
. Setting

{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}={\bf x}^{(k)}+{\delta}^{(k)},

and substituting it in the first step of Eq. (
14
) gives

(\alpha I+{\mathcal{M}}){\delta}^{(k)}={\bf b}-{\cal B}{\bf x}^{(k)}=:{\bf r}^{(k)}.

In the same way if we set
{\bf x}^{(k+1)}={\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}+{\delta}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}
and substitute it in the second step of Eq. (
14
), then we get

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\alpha I+{\mathcal{K}}){\delta}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{K}}{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M}{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}-\mathcal{G}{\bf b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{2}{\mathcal{K}}{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{M}{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}-\mathcal{G}{\bf b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{G}\left({\bf b}-(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}){\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{G}\left({\bf b}-\mathcal{B}{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{G}{\bf r}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}.

Now using the above results we establish the inexact version of ASSS (IASSS) as following.

Algorithm 1. The IASSS algorithm for solving
\mathcal{B}{\bf x}={\bf b}
.

1.

Choose an initial guess
{\bf x}^{(0)}
.

2.

For
k=0,1,2,\ldots
until convergence, Do

3.

Compute
{\bf r}^{(k)}={\bf b}-\mathcal{B}{\bf x}^{(k)}
.

4.

Solve
(\alpha I+{\mathcal{M}}){\delta}^{(k)}={\bf r}^{(k)}
approximately using an iteration method.

5.

{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}:={\bf x}^{(k)}+\delta^{(k)}
.

6.

Compute
{\bf r}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}={\bf b}-\mathcal{B}{\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}
.

7.

Solve
(\alpha I+\mathcal{K}){\delta}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}=-\mathcal{G}{\bf r}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}
approximately using an iteration method.

8.

{\bf x}^{(k+1)}:={\bf x}^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}+\delta^{(k+\frac{1}{2})}
.

9.

EndDo

In the steps 4 and 7 of Algorithm 1 two
linear systems of equations
with the coefficient matrices
\alpha I+{\mathcal{M}}
and
\alpha I+{\mathcal{K}}
should be solved. Since these matrices are SPD, they can be solved inexactly using the CG method. However, since

\alpha I+{\mathcal{M}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha I+M&0&0&0\\
0&\alpha I+M&0&0\\
0&0&\alpha I+M&0\\
0&0&0&\alpha I+M\\
\end{array}\right),

we need to solve four systems with the same coefficient matrices
\alpha I+M
and different right-hand sides. In fact if we set

{\hat{\delta}}^{(k)}=[{\delta}^{(k)}_{1};{\delta}^{(k)}_{2};{\delta}^{(k)}_{3};{\delta}^{(k)}_{4}]

\in\mathbb{R}^{4m}

and
{\hat{\bf r}}^{(k)}=[{\bf r}^{(k)}_{1};{\bf r}^{(k)}_{2};{\bf r}^{(k)}_{3};{\bf r}^{(k)}_{4}]\in\mathbb{R}^{4m}
with

{\delta}^{(k)}_{i},{\bf r}^{(k)}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, for
i=1,2,3,4
, then we only need to solve the linear
system of equations with multiple right-hand sides

(\alpha I+M){\hat{\delta}}^{(k)}={\hat{\bf r}}^{(k)}.

This system can be solved using the global CG algorithm with a suitable preconditioner, which can be written as following. In this algorithm the inner product used is
\langle X,Y\rangle=trace(X^{T}Y)
.

Algorithm 2. Global CG algorithm for
P^{-1}AX=P^{-1}B
.

1.

Compute
R_{0}=B-AX_{0}
,
Z_{0}=P^{-1}R_{0}
and
P_{0}=Z_{0}
.

2.

For
j:=0,1,\ldots,
until convergence, Do

3.

\alpha_{j}:=\langle R_{j},Z_{j}\rangle/\langle AP_{j},P_{j}\rangle
.

4.

X_{j+1}:=X_{j}+\alpha_{j}P_{j}
.

5.

R_{j+1}:=R_{j}-\alpha_{j}AP_{j}
.

6.

Z_{j+1}:=P^{-1}R_{j+1}
.

7.

\beta_{j}:=\langle R_{j+1},Z_{j+1}\rangle/\langle R_{j},Z_{j}\rangle
.

8.

P_{j+1}:=Z_{j+1}+\beta_{j}P_{j}
.

9.

Enddo

In the same way the system of Step 7 of Algorithm 1 can be solved. So in each iteration of the IASSS iteration method two linear systems of equations with multiple right-hand sides should be solved which can be solved using Algorithm 2.
The incomplete Cholesky factorization of the coefficient matrix can be used as the preconditioner in Algorithm 2.

As Benzi and Golub mentioned in
[
7
]
, adding
\alpha>0
to the main diagonal of

M
(or
\eta K
) improves the condition number of the matrix. So this improves, in turn, the convergence of the CG method much.

4
The ASSS preconditioner

It follows from
\mathcal{G}^{T}=-\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}\mathcal{G}
that

\mathcal{B}^{T}+\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{K}\mathcal{G}^{T}=\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{M}-\mathcal{K}\mathcal{G}=2\mathcal{M},

which shows that the matrix
\mathcal{B}
is positive definite (positive real). Hence, from Theorem 6.30 in
[
19
]
the restarted GMRES(
\ell
)
for solving the system (
11
)
converges for any
\ell\geq 1
.
On the other hand, if we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}(I+\mathcal{G})^{-1}(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})\mathcal{G}(\alpha I+\mathcal{K}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}(I+\mathcal{G})^{-1}(\alpha\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{M})(\alpha I-\mathcal{G}\mathcal{K}),

then

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}-\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{P}^{-1}_{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}.

Hence, we get

\mathcal{P}^{-1}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}=I-\mathcal{T}_{\alpha},

which shows that the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{P}^{-1}_{\alpha}\mathcal{B}
are clustered in a circle with radius 1 centered at
(1,0)
.
Hence, the GMRES method would be quite appropriate for solving the system incorporated with the preconditioner
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{-1}\mathcal{B}{\bf x}=\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{-1}{\bf b}.

In applying the preconditioner in each iteration of a Krylov subspace method like GMRES a linear system of equations of the form
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}s=r
should be solved. Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(I+\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}\mathcal{G}(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})^{-1}(I+\mathcal{G}),

we can state the following algorithm for solving the system
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}s=r
.

Algorithm 3. Solution of
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}s=r
.

1.

Compute
v=-\alpha(I+\mathcal{G})r
.

2.

Solve

(\alpha I+\mathcal{M})w=v
for
w
.

3.

Compute
z=\mathcal{G}w
.

4.

Solve

(\alpha I+\mathcal{K})s=z
for
s
.

Both of the systems in Steps 2 and 4 of the above algorithm can be solved exactly using the Cholesky factorization of the matrices
\alpha I+M
and
\alpha I+\eta K
, or inexactly using the CG method. When these systems are solved inexactly we can apply the global CG algorithm described in the previous section. It is noted that, in this case the flexible version of GMRES (FGMRES)
[
18
]
should be used for solving the main system instead of the GMRES algorithm.

6
Conclusion

We have presented a method called alternating symmetric positive definite and scaled symmetric positive semidefinite splitting (ASSS) method, for solving the system arisen from finite element discretization of a distributed optimal control problem with time-periodic parabolic equations. We have proved that the method is unconditionally convergent. We have compared the numerical results of the ASSS method and the corresponding induced preconditioner with those of the BAS iteration method. We also compared the numerical results of the ASSS preconditioner to those of the PRESB preconditioner. Numerical results showed that the proposed
method has some advantages over the two other tested methods.

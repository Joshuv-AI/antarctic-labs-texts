Abstract
. Ethical Consideration: Please be aware that this paper may contain potentially harmful or aggressive content. Such content is solely used for the analysis and testing of ChatGPT and does not imply any intent to promote criminal activities. ChatGPT is currently the most popular large language model (LLM), with over 100 million users, making a significant impact on people’s lives. However, due to the presence of jailbreak vulnerabilities, ChatGPT might have negative effects on people’s lives, potentially even facilitating criminal activities. Testing whether ChatGPT can cause jailbreak is crucial because it can enhance ChatGPT’s security, reliability, and social responsibility. Inspired by previous research revealing the varied performance of LLMs in different language translations, we suspected that wrapping prompts in multiple languages might lead to ChatGPT jailbreak. To investigate this, we designed a study with a fuzzing testing approach to analyzing ChatGPT’s cross-linguistic proficiency. Our study includes three strategies by automatically posing different formats of malicious questions to ChatGPT: (1) each malicious question involving only one language, (2) multilingual malicious questions, (3) specifying that ChatGPT responds in a language different from the questions. In addition, we also combine our strategies by utilizing prompt injection templates to wrap the three aforementioned types of questions. We examined a total of 7,892 Q&A data points, discovering that multilingual wrapping can indeed lead to ChatGPT’s jailbreak, with different wrapping strategies having varying effects on jailbreak probability. Prompt injection can amplify the probability of jailbreak caused by multilingual wrapping. This study provides insights for OpenAI developers to enhance ChatGPT’s support for language diversity and inclusion.



1.
introduction

ChatGPT
Open AI 2023
is currently the most popular LLM on the market, which has attracted over 100 million users since its emergence
11 11
, and its user numbers continue to grow rapidly. More and more people are coming to ChatGPT to solve real-world problems as an alternative to traditional search engines, such as Google. According to a survey of 642 professionals
13 13
, 42% expressed a preference for using AI-powered chatbots, while only 24% insisted on utilizing traditional search engines.

As ChatGPT gains popularity and becomes increasingly intertwined with our daily lives, it also has the potential to adversely impact people’s lives, such as facilitating criminal activities. This is due to the existence of ChatGPT’s jailbreak vulnerabilities. Jailbreaking refers to the utilization of specific prompts to elicit responses from LLMs that they might not typically generate
10 10
, even including harmful information, such as violent or gory content.
Testing whether ChatGPT is susceptible to jailbreaking is crucial for the following reasons: (1) it can improve the mechanism for ChatGPT’s safety check; (2) it can increase ChatGPT’s reliability, ensuring it generates reliable content; and (3) it aligns with ChatGPT’s responsibility to society, as AI should not be exploited by criminals to engage in harmful activities.

Extensive research
Huang et al. 2023
;
Siu 2023
;
Jiao et al. 2023
has explored how LLMs may produce various performances for translations across different languages. This exploration has inspired us to consider leveraging multilingual wrapping of malicious questions to create prompts that could potentially bypass safety checks in ChatGPT. It is well-known that English is the predominant language in ChatGPT’s training data, leading ChatGPT to have robust safety checks for malicious questions in English. However, safety checks for other languages, especially those considered underrepresented, may not be as strict.
To comprehensively assess whether multilingual wrapping can result in ChatGPT jailbreak, we introduce a study with a fuzzing testing approach designed to evaluate ChatGPT’s cross-linguistic proficiency.

The fuzzing testing approach (implemented in Python) could automatically generate prompts through three strategies, which involve wrapping malicious questions in multiple languages: (1) each malicious question exclusively in one language, (2) multilingual malicious questions, and (3) specifying that ChatGPT responds in a language different from the questions. Furthermore, we also combine our strategies by utilizing prompt injection templates to wrap the three aforementioned types of questions.

This approach automatically interacts with ChatGPT, poses questions, and collects data. Our initial malicious question list consists of 30 questions, resulting in the generation of 7,892 distinct prompts.
For detailed information about our testing strategies, please refer to the following RQs:

RQ1:
Is jailbreak triggered differently when ChatGPT responds to different languages?

Through the analysis of responses to a total of 3,630 questions in 121 languages, we observed varying levels of support from ChatGPT for different languages. Prompts generated in different languages indeed have the potential to trigger ChatGPT’s jailbreak.

RQ2:
Would jailbreak be triggered when ChatGPT is asked a question written in multiple languages?

We tested 1,681 prompts that combined English and non-English and found that multilingual questions did lead to jailbreak, but it can also increase the difficulty of ChatGPT in comprehending questions.
The probability of jailbreak is significantly influenced by the content of the questions.

RQ3:
Would jailbreak be triggered when ChatGPT is specifically asked to respond in a
language different from the question’s language?

We tested 1,681 prompts, specifying that the language in ChatGPT responses should differ from the languages involved in the questions. We found that this requirement also led to jailbreak, albeit to a lesser extent compared to RQ2. However, it significantly increased the difficulty for ChatGPT to comprehend the prompts.

RQ4:
Is there an increased probability of ChatGPT’s jailbreak when handling prompt injection with multilingual wrapping?

We randomly selected 300 non-jailbreaking outputs from the previous RQs as inputs for this RQ, analyzing a total of 900 samples. Our findings indicate that prompt injection can increase the probability of ChatGPT jailbreaking when exposed to questions wrapped in multiple languages.

This paper illuminates the cross-linguistic questions that can lead to ChatGPT’s jailbreak vulnerabilities, drawing attention to the issues of Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion (EDI) for ChatGPT’s language support.

•

This is currently the most comprehensive testing for ChatGPT’s cross-linguistic proficiency. It encompasses an extensive list of 121 languages, employs three distinct testing strategies, along with a combined testing solution, and includes a substantial Q&A list with 7,892 data points.

•

To foster the open-source community, we have made the code and data public.

3.1.
Research Questions

Fuzzing testing is a widely renowned software testing technique that detects weaknesses in software by using a large number of test inputs
Liang et al. 2018
.
We introduce a study with fuzzing testing to comprehensively evaluate ChatGPT’s cross-linguistic proficiency, which provides insights into its level of support for language diversity and inclusiveness.

Our formulation of RQs includes three testing strategies and an RQ that combines these three strategies. Each RQ comprises numerous test inputs, aligning with the requirements of fuzzing testing. Figure
2
presents an overview of our study.

RQ1:
Is jailbreak triggered differently when ChatGPT responds to different languages?

Motivated by our motivating example, RQ1 is designed to assess the reliability of ChatGPT’s responses to questions written in different languages separately. Despite prior work
Deng et al. 2023
;
Lai et al. 2023
having explored similar issues, their studies have focused on a limited number of languages. In our experiment, we cover a broad range of languages. This question serves as the foundation for our study, which inspires subsequent RQs.

RQ2:
Would jailbreak be triggered when ChatGPT is asked a question written in multiple languages?

When designing RQ2, we aim to further analyze ChatGPT’s capability in handling multiple languages, building upon the foundation laid by RQ1. Consequently, we craft RQ2’s input questions to be those that were identified in RQ1 as not causing a jailbreak in ChatGPT. Then, we automatically modify these questions to incorporate more than one language, unlike the questions in RQ1, which are only in a single language. Our goal is to investigate whether the introduction of multiple languages in the questions can lead to ChatGPT’s jailbreak.

RQ3:
Would jailbreak be triggered when ChatGPT is specifically asked to respond in a
language different from the question’s language?

The design of RQ3 also aims to further analyze ChatGPT’s proficiency in meeting requirements for responding in different languages. Similar to RQ2, the input for RQ3 consists of questions identified in RQ1 as not causing a jailbreak, but with the additional requirement that responses must be in a language different from the one in the question. Prior work
Deng et al. 2023
;
Lai et al. 2023
on multilingual tasks primarily considered multilingual questions without any requirements for responses. This RQ addresses this gap, examining whether ChatGPT’s reliability in a multilingual context is compromised when required to provide responses in languages different from those in the input questions.

RQ4:
Is there an increased probability of ChatGPT’s jailbreak when handling prompt injection with multilingual wrapping?

Previous work
Deng et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2023
focuses on the impact of prompt injection attacks on jailbreak issues. Motivated by the previous work, in this RQ,
we examine whether prompt injection will have an impact on ChatGPT’s ability to handle cross-linguistic questions by
wrapping up non-jailbreaking questions derived from RQ1/2/3 using prompt injection.
This also allows us to compare the strengths and weaknesses of the different testing strategies.

3.2.
Data Collection

Question Collection

We built our question list by referring to the United States Federal Crime List
2 2
. Our question list consists of 30 malicious questions. Each question’s implementation clearly violates U.S. federal law in real-life scenarios and should not receive concrete suggestions. We asked these questions in English to ChatGPT, ensuring that each question was rejected by ChatGPT. The responses were similar to “
I can’t assist with that.
” These 30 questions cover almost all major types of crimes, ranging from personal injury to financial fraud. The diversity of the questions also secures accuracy in language analysis.

Language Selection

To encompass a broad spectrum of a language collection, we chose not to follow the approach of some existing work, which deliberately focused on a few languages
Deng et al. 2023
. Our goal is to broaden the scope of our research to encompass more languages, ensuring a more inclusive language representation. Many languages are regional, like Haitian Creole as we mentioned in the Introduction. Even though they may not be well-represented on the international stage, they still have thousands and millions of users.
Our language selection process involves a thorough examination of ChatGPT’s and the translator’s documentation. First, we recorded languages included in the Common Crawl
4 4
documentation—a pivotal open-source dataset widely used in the training of ChatGPT. Then, we built a list of languages supported by Google Cloud Translation, which is our chosen translator. Google Cloud Translation is a well-known translator with the broadest language support in the market
3 3
, backed by a massive user base worldwide and offering Python API interface support.
Later, we identified overlapping languages between the two language lists. Initially, we compared language names, but we realized that some languages had different names, which led to inaccurate text matching. We then utilized the ISO 639 standard for comparison. The language codes in Common Crawl’s documentation are ISO 639-2 Codes, while those in the Google Translator documentation are ISO 639-1 Codes. Therefore, we used an ISO 639 code mapping table
7 7
to find overlapping languages, resulting in a list of 121 languages.

3.3.
Jailbreak Identification

The determination of whether ChatGPT is jailbreaking is completed through manual inspection. We chose manual inspection due to the following considerations: (1) There is no uniform answer to malicious questions, making it challenging to capture answer patterns for automated evaluation. (2) Since questioning ChatGPT involves multiple languages, some answers do not make semantic sense, and in certain cases, part of the answer may be semantically meaningful while the other part is incomprehensible to humans. Humans, being precise judges, are best suited to assess these answers.
(3) As the majority of answers are not in English, they need to be translated by a translator. Humans can make efforts to understand the answers as much as possible to minimize the noise introduced by the translator.
(4) The users of ChatGPT are humans, not machines. Using humans to evaluate it aligns more closely with the most realistic scenario.

Our manual inspection involves four participants. Three of us are master’s students majoring in computer science at an English-speaking institute, and the fourth participant is a professor specializing in software engineering at the same institute. All four participants possess robust backgrounds in Computer Science and Software Engineering with a thorough understanding of English.
At the beginning of the experiment, we employed a method of randomly selecting questions and target languages to generate 100 translated versions of malicious questions for inquiry with ChatGPT. We then built our label list based on the answers from ChatGPT. We also referred to some existing work. For example,
Deng et al. 2023
categorized their responses into
unsafe
,
safe
, and
invalid
labels, while
Yu et al. 2023
employed a more fine-grained approach, including
Full Refusal
,
Partial Refusal
,
Partial Compliance
, and
Full Compliance
. However, detailed categorization can result in many examples crossing boundaries between labels, making them applicable to different labels. Moreover, due to the potential for generating answers that are challenging to comprehend in this study, detailed categorization could bring troubles. Therefore, we adopted a relatively high-level label identification strategy. Table 1 presents a detailed introduction to our label list.

To ensure the accuracy of our jailbreaking identification and mitigate biases introduced by manual inspection, we conducted three rounds of labeling for each RQ’s data. In the first round, each record of Q&A was independently labeled by two participants. In the second round, participants reviewed their labels along with another participant, making corrections to mislabeled instances and discussing disagreements. Finally, in the third round, a third participant acted as a mediator to resolve any remaining disagreements and finalize the labels.

4.1.
Questions in Different Languages Separately (RQ1).

Approach.

To assess whether ChatGPT would jailbreak when questioned in different languages, we propose an automated approach to investigate this issue. Initially, we automatically translate all questions from the malicious question list into versions in different languages. Given the nature of fuzzing testing, which involves using a large number of test inputs to comprehensively test a software system, we opted to cover all 121 target languages without any sampling. This approach resulted in translating 30 questions into 121 different versions, creating a total of 3,630 malicious questions in different languages. The summary of the generated Q&A quantity can be found in Table
2
.

Subsequently, we utilized Python code to invoke ChatGPT’s interface
12 12
, automatically presenting these 3,630 questions to ChatGPT. After each Q&A session, we reset the session to prevent interactions between sessions from affecting one another. The Python API automatically returns ChatGPT’s answers, which we record in a CSV file. It is worth noting that ChatGPT defaults to responding in the language implied by the question. For example, if the question is in German, ChatGPT will recognize this and respond in German. To facilitate Q&A labeling, we employ Python to automatically translate the answers using Google Translator and record them in the CSV file. Then, we distribute the labeled data, equitably assigned according to the number of participants, to each individual for labeling. After labeling, we summarize the results.

4.2.
Questions Written in Multiple Languages (RQ2).

Approach.
To investigate whether multilingual questions impact the probability of ChatGPT jailbreaking, we took the questions labeled as
FALSE
in RQ1 and used their translated versions as input for RQ2. We then modified the questions to create multilingual versions and examined if jailbreak instances occurred. There are 1,681 questions in RQ2’s input.
For each question, we divided it into three parts based on verbs: the part before the verb, the verb itself, and the part after the verb. We randomly retained the translated part from RQ1 and restored the rest to English. Additionally, given that the questions include English, to prevent ChatGPT from preferring English in its answers, we explicitly mandated a requirement at the end of each question to answer in the non-English language involved in the question. Below is an example comparing the original question with the wrapped question for RQ2.
Since the input questions in RQ2 are those determined within RQ1 to be incapable of causing a jailbreak, if multilingual questions do not increase the jailbreak probability, we would expect the observed jailbreak probability for this RQ to be close to or equal to 0.

5.
Discussion

How does our study help OpenAI reduce the probability of ChatGPT’s jailbreak?

From the insights gained in the previous four RQs, multilingual questions can lead to ChatGPT jailbreaking. This study explores multilingual issues, including questions asked in non-English, questions containing multiple languages, or requiring ChatGPT to respond in different languages. Previous RQs have also indicated that the actual content of the question is a crucial factor in determining whether ChatGPT will jailbreak. For certain types of questions, those with multilingual wrapping consistently trigger jailbreaks. This insight provides OpenAI with an approach to enhance their safety checks by using the question type and language as criteria, thereby reducing the probability of inducing ChatGPT’s jailbreak. Additionally, prompt injection can serve as an amplifier, increasing the probability of jailbreaks caused by multilingual wrapping. Therefore, when OpenAI is testing multilingual questions, prompt injection can be used to quickly identify jailbreaks triggered by such questions.

The impact of English training data on ChatGPT’s handling of non-English questions.

In our experiments, we observed evidence indicating the effects of English training data on ChatGPT’s handling of non-English questions. In the example from RQ1, we noted that when presented with a question in a language used in West Africa, ChatGPT generated a phone number from California. Moreover, in RQ2, the occurrences of
N/A
and
NO SUPPORT
were fewer compared to RQ3. Furthermore, in RQ4, the significant decrease in
N/A
and
NO SUPPORT
instances with the incorporation of English prompt templates suggests that the introduction of English can enhance the model’s understanding of non-English content. However, this raises concerns about potential cultural biases introduced by the English training data, impacting ChatGPT’s understanding and processing of non-English content. Ensuring cultural respect and language sensitivity in training data is crucial for addressing these concerns.

6.1.
Fuzzing Testing

Fuzz testing is a well-established software testing technique, and a considerable amount of prior studies have been dedicated to this area.

Klees et al. 2018
reviewed 32 papers on fuzzing to evaluate the quality of fuzzing testing.
Zhu et al. 2022
examined existing fuzzing test solutions to help developers better understand fuzz testing. Due to the popularity of AI, fuzzing testing techniques are now being applied to test AI systems. For example,
Guo et al. 2018
and
Wang et al. 2021
utilized fuzzing testing to propose a testing framework for evaluating deep learning systems.
Li et al. 2020
introduced a fuzzing testing framework to assist in testing autonomous driving systems. With the rising popularity of LLMs, fuzzing techniques are also being incorporated into the studies related to LLMs.
Yu et al. 2023
introduced a black-box jailbreak fuzzing framework to inject malicious questions into the jailbreak template to test the safety check capabilities of LLMs. However, to the best of our knowledge, there are currently no studies of fuzzing testing to assess the language diversity of LLMs.

6.2.
Testing LLMs

LLMs are a recent techinique that has gained significant attention since the emergence of ChatGPT
Open AI 2023
. The exploration and evaluation of LLMs are experiencing a constant surge in research activities.
Perez et al. 2022
proposed evaluating LLM’s responses to generated test questions using a classifier trained for malicious content detection.
Liu et al. 2023
conducted an empirical study to investigate the characteristics of LLMs’ jailbreaking cases.
Wang et al. 2023
explored whether ChatGPT could defend its belief in truth through a range of reasoning benchmarks.
Regarding the cross-linguistic capabilities of LLMs,
Blevins & Zettlemoyer 2022
revealed that when the proportion of non-English data is small, these data can still facilitate cross-linguistic transfer for models that are trained on them. Some studies specifically tested LLM’s cross-linguistic abilities, however, as mentioned earlier, they did not integrate fuzzing testing techniques. This limitation results in a restricted number of tested languages and a limited evaluation of LLMs’ cross-linguistic capabilities.
Bang et al. 2023
studied ChatGPT’s abilities in multitasking, multilingualism, and multimodal tasks, focusing on a limited number of languages in tasks like sentiment analysis and translation analysis.

Deng et al. 2023
investigated whether LLMs could bypass safety checks for 30 non-English languages, and their evaluation was straightforward, only using prompt injection techniques to test if LLMs could jailbreak.

Lai et al. 2023
studied 37 languages to analyze LLM cross-linguistic abilities through NLP tasks. This study is more aligned with the field of NLP than assessing the safety-check capabilities of ChatGPT in handling different languages.
In summary, the existing state-of-the-art research indicates a need for a comprehensive study to investigate the multilingual capabilities of GPT models, particularly concerning safety checks.

6.3.
EDI in Software

In recent years, EDI issues in software have been receiving increasing attention. Modern software has a wide range of users, yet software development tends to be biased towards a specific user group, resulting in poor user experiences for those users who are not considered by developers, and in some cases, even leading to offensive user experiences. Currently, research on software EDI primarily focuses on gender, race, and disabilities.

Bosu & Sultana 2019
examined code review repositories of ten popular open-source software (OSS) and discovered that the ongoing problem of insufficient gender diversity persists.
Sultana 2023
proposed a study to identify and address gender biases to promote EDI in open-source projects.
Trinkenreich et al. 2022
conducted a literature review to investigate the participation of women in OSS.

Nadri et al. 2022
studied the racial and ethnic diversity of developers in OSS and discovered that only 16.56% are perceived as non-white developers. IEEE Software has dedicated a special issue
Albusays et al. 2021
featuring 37 papers on various dimensions of diversity-related topics, including gender, disability, and race. All these studies highlight the importance of EDI issues in software.

With the growing popularity of LLMs, language diversity has gained increasing attention. Some studies
Lai et al. 2023
;
Deng et al. 2023
;
Bang et al. 2023
have shown the dominant advantage of English in LLMs. However, these studies have often focused on a limited number of widely used non-English languages, which do not include underrepresented languages.
Therefore, studying language diversity is crucial for LLMs to promote EDI in LLMs.

7.
Threats to validity

Construct Validity.

When we built our questions, we only referred to U.S. federal law, which may introduce some bias. To mitigate this, our question list comprises 30 questions, covering most types of crimes. Additionally, we believe the list can include questions that have different interpretations in various countries and regions. For example, while prostitution is illegal in most parts of the United States, it is legal in Germany. However, considering that ChatGPT cannot confirm the asker’s location and whether they are subject to U.S. law when asking in German, ChatGPT should respond to such questions in a manner similar to certain illegal questions, such as “how to kill someone?” Therefore, it is acceptable not to filter out questions related to violations of U.S. federal law based on the laws of other countries and regions.

We chose ChatGPT for investigation as it currently stands as the most popular LLM. We also attempted to explore other LLMs for fuzzing testing on their cross-linguistic capabilities. Our selection criteria encompassed two key properties: (1) support for multiple languages and (2) provision of a programmatic API for automatic queries. We examined Llama 2
Touvron et al. 2023
and Claude

Furthermore, we developed an unofficial API to access PaLM 2-powered conversational AI called Bard

Given these challenges, we believe the current and most effective solution is to first uncover language diversity issues through ChatGPT. This will shed light on potential improvements in language diversity for other LLMs, providing valuable insights for future developers.

Internal Validity.

Translation inaccuracies, possibly resulting from the translation tool or language ambiguities, might impact the experiments’ results. We chose Google Translator as it is currently one of the widely used translation tools and supports most languages

8.
conclusion & future work

In this paper, we presented a study with a fuzzing testing approach that includes three strategies by automatically posing different formats of malicious questions to ChatGPT: (1) each malicious question involving only one language, (2) multilingual malicious questions, (3) specifying that ChatGPT responds in a language different from the questions. Additionally, we also combine our strategies by utilizing prompt injection templates to wrap the three aforementioned types of questions.
We analyzed a total of 7,892 Q&A data points and found that wrapping malicious questions in multiple languages can lead to jailbreak, and prompt injection can increase the probability of jailbreak.

In the future, we will continue to explore the state-of-the-art LLMs and conduct fuzzing testing to assess their support for language diversity.

Capacity Analysis and Sum Rate Maximization for the SCMA Cellular Network Coexisting with D2D Communications
Yukai Liu, Wen Chen \corinfo wenchen@sjtu.edu.cn

Abstract
Sparse code multiple access (SCMA) is the most concerning scheme among non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) technologies for 5G wireless communication new interface. Another efficient technique in 5G aimed to improve spectral efficiency for local communications is device-to-device (D2D) communications. Therefore, we utilize the SCMA cellular network coexisting with D2D communications for the connection demand of the Internet of things (IOT), and improve the system sum rate performance of the hybrid network. We first derive the information-theoretic expression of the capacity for all users and find the capacity bound of cellular users based on the mutual interference between cellular users and D2D users. Then we consider the power optimization problem for the cellular users and D2D users jointly to maximize the system sum rate. To tackle the non-convex optimization problem, we propose a geometric programming (GP) based iterative power allocation algorithm. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm converges fast and well improves the sum rate performance.

Introduction

The large connection and high data rate constitute the core goals for the 5G wireless networks. Some key technologies of 5G systems include device-to-device (D2D) communications, non-orthogonal multiple access techniques (NOMA) along with massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO), ultra-dense radio networking, all-spectrum access, and so on

When multiple users transmit at the same RB by power-domain NOMA, users are allocated with different power levels, while the code-domain NOMA transmits at the same RB in distinguishing spreading codes

Among the currently proposed code domain NOMA, sparse code multiple access (SCMA) has been commonly considered as a promising method. SCMA is proposed by Nikopour and Baligh

The sparsity of the SCMA codewords enables massive connectivity and the use of suboptimal message passing algorithm (MPA) to detect multiple users. For general SCMA system, the users’ SCMA codeword in each dimension will be modulated to an Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple (OFDM) subcarrier before the air interface. The number of users is larger than the number of subcarriers, which lead to the non-orthogonal feature. On the other side, D2D communication is a promising technology for local communications, which allows nearby devices to communicate without base station (BS) or with limited BS involvement, and improves the link reliability, spectral efficiency and system capacity

In this paper, our main contribution contains the derivation of SCMA capacity bound for general SCMA system and the sum rate maximization problem. We first try to derive the information-theoretic expression of the capacity for all users in the hybrid network model. Since it is hard to derive a close form expression of cellular capacity, we find the capacity bound for cellular users based on general SCMA codeword structure. After the theoretic derivation and analysis, we focus on the sum rate maximization problem by power optimization allocation for different users. Since this is a non-convex optimization problem, we propose an algorithm based on geometric programming (GP), which transforms parts of the objective function and rewrite the problem in standard GP form to get the optimal solution with convex optimization theory. By iteration, the solution of GP problem will converge to the optimal solution of the original problem. The convergence and gain of the proposed algorithm are conducted by simulation part. The performance shows that this algorithm is appropriate and significant.

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{k}(\bm{K}_{\tilde{\bm{n}}})+\lambda_{1}(\bm{H}\bm{K}_{\bm{x}}\bm{H}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{N}_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{1}(\bm{H}_{j}\bm{K}_{\bm{x}_{j}}\bm{H}_{j}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{N}_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}[\lambda_{1}(\bm{K}_{\bm{x}_{j}})\cdot\min\limits_{k}(|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2})].

By Lemma
3.1
and Eq. (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\bm{K}_{\bm{x}_{j}})

(20)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}(\bm{M}_{j1}\bm{A}_{j1}\bm{M}_{j1}^{*}+\bm{M}_{j2}\bm{A}_{j2}\bm{M}_{j2}^{*})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1}(\bm{M}_{j1}\bm{A}_{j1}\bm{M}_{j1}^{*})+\lambda_{1}(\bm{M}_{j2}\bm{A}_{j2}\bm{M}_{j2}^{*})

\displaystyle\triangleq\tau_{1,j}.

Thus, we conclude in general condition that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(\bm{K}_{\tilde{\bm{n}}}

\displaystyle+\bm{H}\bm{K}_{\bm{x}}\bm{H}^{*})

(21)

\displaystyle>\tilde{N}_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\tau_{1,j}\cdot\min\limits_{k}(|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2}).

We have

\displaystyle C_{c}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{\bm{K}_{\bm{x}}}I(\bm{x};\bm{y}|\bm{H}=\textup{H})

(22)

\displaystyle=\log\det(\bm{K}_{\bm{y}})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(\tilde{N}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log\lambda_{k}(\bm{K}_{\bm{y}})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(\tilde{N}_{k}),

where the derivation is based on
\bm{x}\sim\textit{CN}(0,\bm{K}_{\bm{x}})

According to Eq. (

\displaystyle\tau_{K,j}

(23)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{K}(\bm{M}_{j1}\bm{A}_{j1}\bm{M}_{j1}^{*})+\lambda_{K}(\bm{M}_{j2}\bm{A}_{j2}\bm{M}_{j2}^{*})

\displaystyle+\lambda_{K}(\bm{M}_{j2}diag\{P_{j,N+1}^{u},...,P_{j,2N}^{u}\}\bm{M}_{j2}^{*}).

To maximize the rate of cellular network, the upper bound of SCMA capacity should be figured out based on Eq. (

4
Sum Rate Maximization

In this section, we focus on the sum rate maximization issue. We formulate the optimization problem as the power allocation of cellular users and D2D users. The GP based iterative algorithm is introduced to find the numerical solution.

4.1
Problem Formulation

In section
3
, the analysis is based on general SCMA codebook. However, according to the sparsity of SCMA codeword, most non-diagonal entries of
\bm{K}_{\bm{x}_{j}}
are zero. Actually, most SCMA system models utilize optimal codebook design principles to obtain the diagonal covariance matrix
[
28
,
29
,
33
]
. In this section, we consider the general situation as

\displaystyle\bm{K}_{\bm{x}_{j}}

\displaystyle=E[\bm{x}_{j}\bm{x}_{j}^{*}]

(31)

\displaystyle=diag\{E[x_{j1}x_{j1}^{*}],\dots,E[x_{jK}x_{jK}^{*}]\}

\displaystyle\triangleq diag\{P_{j1},P_{j2},...,P_{jK}\}.

Notice that the maximum mutual information in Eq. (
9
) can get closed form expression, we have the simplified derivation as

\lambda_{k}(\bm{K}_{y})=\tilde{N}_{k}+\sum_{j\in\xi_{k}}|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2}P_{jk},k=1,2,...,K,

(32)

where we should notice that
P_{jk}=0
for
j\notin\xi_{k}
with the corresponding factor graph. Then we have

\displaystyle\log\det(\bm{K}_{y})

(33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log\left(N_{0}+|h_{k}^{db}|^{2}P_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}+\sum_{j\in\xi_{k}}|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2}P_{jk}\right).

Finally, the capacity of cellular users is

C_{c}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log\left\{1+\frac{\sum_{j\in\xi_{k}}|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2}P_{jk}}{N_{0}+|h_{k}^{db}|^{2}P_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}\right\}.

(34)

Besides, for decoding the codewords of the CU
j
at the BS, we define the SINR in subcarrier
k\in\zeta_{j}
as

\gamma_{jk}^{c}=\frac{|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2}P_{jk}}{N_{0}+|h_{k}^{db}|^{2}P_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}}.

(35)

Accordingly, the sum rate maximization problem is formulated as

\displaystyle\bm{P1:}

\displaystyle\max_{\bm{P},\bm{P^{{}^{\prime}}}}(C_{c}+C_{d})

(36)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle C1:\gamma_{jk}^{c}\geq\gamma_{0}^{c},\quad j=1,\dots,J,\quad k\in\zeta_{j},

\displaystyle C2:\gamma_{\ell}^{d}\geq\gamma_{0}^{d},\quad\ell=1,\dots,J_{D},

\displaystyle C3:0\leq P_{j}=\sum_{k\in\zeta_{j}}P_{jk}\leq P_{0},\quad j=1,\dots,J,

\displaystyle C4:0\leq P_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}\leq P_{0}^{{}^{\prime}},\quad\ell=1,\dots,J_{D},

where the constraints
C1
and
C2
denote the minimum QoS requirements of cellular users and D2D users, determined by the SINR threshold
\gamma_{0}^{c}
and
\gamma_{0}^{d}
, respectively,
C3
and
C4
mean the transmitted power limitation. Since the objective function is nonlinear and nonconvex, P1 is a nonconvex optimization problem. We decide to rely on the tractable Geometric Programming problem to find the appropriate algorithm.

4.2
Geometric Programming

Geometric Programing (GP) is a special optimization problem with useful properties
[
34
]
. The standard form of GP is nonlinear and nonconvex, while the convex form of GP can make use of the standard convex optimization theory. Define a monomial
g
as

g(\bm{x})=cx_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}...x_{n}^{a_{n}},

(37)

where
c\geq 0
is the multiplicative constant,
a_{\ell}\in\bm{R}
for
\ell=1,2,...,n
is the exponents, and the argument
x_{\ell}>0
for
\ell=1,2,...,n
. Then a posynomial is defined as a summation of monomials shown as

G(\bm{x})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}c_{m}x_{1}^{a_{1}^{m}}x_{2}^{a_{2}^{m}}...x_{n}^{a_{n}^{m}},

(38)

where
a_{\ell}^{m}\in\bm{R}
and
c_{m}\geq 0
for
\ell=1,2,\dots,n
and
m=1,2,\dots,M
. The standard form for a GP is as follows.

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{x}}\quad G_{0}(\bm{x})

(39)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle G_{s}(\bm{x})\leq 1,\quad s=1,\dots,S,

\displaystyle h_{t}(\bm{x})=1,\quad t=1,\dots,T,

where the posynomial
G_{s}(\cdot)
and the monomial
h_{t}(\cdot)
for
s=0,1,\dots,S
and
t=1,\dots,T
are defined as

G_{s}(\bm{x})=\sum_{m=1}^{M_{s}}c_{sm}x_{1}^{a_{1}^{sm}}x_{2}^{a_{2}^{sm}}...x_{n}^{a_{n}^{sm}},

(40)

and

h_{t}(\bm{x})=c_{t}x_{1}^{a_{1}^{t}}x_{2}^{a_{2}^{t}}...x_{n}^{a_{n}^{t}}.

(41)

In Eq. (
40
) and Eq. (
41
), we have
c_{sm}>0
and
c_{t}>0
for
m=1,2,...,M_{s}
,
s=0,1,...,S
, and
t=1,2,...,T
.

For the standard form of GP, the posynomials are not convex functions. In order to simplify the problem, the logarithmic conversion as
y_{\ell}=\log x_{\ell}
,
b_{sm}=\log c_{sm}
, and
b_{t}=\log c_{t}
are utilized. Then the equivalent problem in term of
\bm{y}
is

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{y}}\quad G_{0}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{y})=\log\sum_{m=1}^{M_{0}}\exp(\bm{a}_{0m}^{T}\bm{y}+b_{0m})

(42)

\displaystyle s.t.\quad G_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{y})=\log\sum_{m=1}^{M_{s}}\exp(\bm{a}_{sm}^{T}\bm{y}+b_{sm})\leq 0,

\displaystyle s=1,2,\dots,S,

\displaystyle h_{t}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{y})=\bm{a}_{t}^{T}\bm{y}+b_{t}=0,\quad t=1,2,\dots,T,

where
\bm{a}_{sm}=(a_{1}^{sm},\cdots,a_{n}^{sm})^{T}
and
\bm{a}_{t}=(a_{1}^{t},\cdots,a_{n}^{t})^{T}
. The problem in (
42
) is the convex form of GP.

4.3
The Proposed GP Based Iterative Algorithm

The objective function in
\bm{P1}
is obviously neither a posynomial nor a convex function. We consider the deformation of the objective function to construct the GP problem. For the transmitted power of cellular users, we further improve the constraint
C3
as
P_{jk}\leq\frac{P_{0}}{d_{f}},\forall k\in\zeta_{j}
,
\forall j
. According to Eq. (
30
), (
34
), we have

\displaystyle C_{c}+C_{d}

(43)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(1+\sum_{j\in\xi_{k}}{\gamma_{jk}^{c}})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{J_{D}}\log\left\{1+\gamma_{\ell}^{d}\right\}

\displaystyle=\log\left\{\prod_{k=1}^{K}\frac{g_{k}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})}{f_{k}(\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})}\cdot\prod_{\ell=1}^{J_{D}}\frac{g_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})}{f_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p})}\right\},

where

\displaystyle f_{k}(\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})\triangleq N_{0}+|h_{k}^{db}|^{2}P_{k}^{{}^{\prime}},

(44)

\displaystyle f_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p})\triangleq N_{0}+\sum_{j\in\xi_{\ell}}|h_{j\ell}^{cd}|^{2}P_{j\ell},

\displaystyle g_{k}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})\triangleq N_{0}+|h_{k}^{db}|^{2}P_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}+\sum_{j\in\xi_{k}}|h_{jk}^{cb}|^{2}P_{jk},

\displaystyle g_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})\triangleq N_{0}+\sum_{j\in\xi_{\ell}}|h_{j\ell}^{cd}|^{2}P_{j\ell}+|h_{\ell}^{dd}|^{2}P_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}.

Note that the logarithmic function is a monotonic function, we can change the maximization of the objective function to the minimization of its reciprocal. Then problem
\bm{P1}
can be transformed into

\displaystyle\bm{P2:}

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}}}\quad\prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod_{\ell=1}^{J_{D}}\frac{f_{k}(\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})f_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p})}{g_{k}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})g_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})},

(45)

\displaystyle s.t.

\displaystyle C1:(\gamma_{jk}^{c})^{-1}\gamma_{0}^{c}\leq 1,j=1,\dots,J,k\in\zeta_{j},

\displaystyle C2:(\gamma_{\ell}^{d})^{-1}\gamma_{0}^{d}\leq 1,\ell=1,\dots,J_{D},

\displaystyle C3:P_{jk}(\frac{P_{0}}{d_{f}})^{-1}\leq 1,j=1,\dots,J,k\in\zeta_{j},

\displaystyle C4:P_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(P_{0}^{{}^{\prime}})^{-1}\leq 1,\ell=1,\dots,J_{D},

where all the constraints are rewritten as posynomials with upper bounds.

The transformed objective function is a ratio between two posynomials, which belongs to Complementary GP and is an intractable NP-hard problem
[
34
]
. However, the denominator posynomial can be approximated by a monomial such that the ratio is a posynomial as shown in Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let the
g(\bm{x})=\sum_{\ell}u_{\ell}(\bm{x})
is a posynomial where
u_{\ell}(\bm{x})
is a monomial. The argument
\bm{x}
is with all positive elements. There must have a monomial
\tilde{g}(\bm{x})
as

g(\bm{x})\geq\tilde{g}(\bm{x})\triangleq\prod_{\ell}\left(\frac{u_{\ell}(\bm{x})}{\beta_{\ell}}\right)^{\beta_{\ell}},

(46)

where
\beta_{\ell}>0
and
\sum_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}=1
, and that becomes an equality when
\beta_{\ell}=\frac{u_{\ell}(\bm{x})}{g(\bm{x})}
for
\forall\ell
.

Proof 4.2
.

See Appendix.

By using Lemma
4.1
, we can rely on GP to find the globally optimal solution.
\bar{\bm{p}}\triangleq(\bm{p}^{T},(\bm{p}^{{}^{\prime}})^{T})^{T}
denotes the complete optimization variables in our algorithm. Specifically, we focus on the expansion for the denominator of the objective function in
\bm{P2}
and set an initial power vector
\bar{\bm{p}}_{0}
to calculate a group of coefficients. Then, the denominator is transformed into a monomial
\tilde{g}(\bar{\bm{p}})
with calculated coefficients. This problem is GP in standard form, and we can modify it to a GP in convex form and use the interior point method to solve. With the optimal solution
\bar{\bm{p}^{*}}
, we solve different GP constantly to improve the accuracy for the approximation of original problem. Notice that the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions is satisfied obviously as we take derivatives of
g(\bar{\bm{p}})
and
\tilde{g}(\bar{\bm{p}})
, this is verified as the best local monomial approximation
[
35
]
. The algorithm is convergent to the optimal solution of
\bm{P2}
, and we will show numerical results in simulation section. The proposed algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
1
, and we define

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod_{\ell=1}^{J_{D}}g_{k}(\bar{\bm{p}})g_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{\bm{p}})\triangleq\sum_{s}u_{s}(\bar{\bm{p}})

(47)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{s}c_{s}(P_{1}^{a_{s}^{1}}\cdots P_{J}^{a_{s}^{J}})[(P^{{}^{\prime}}_{1})^{b_{s}^{1}}\cdots(P^{{}^{\prime}}_{J_{D}})^{b_{s}^{J_{D}}}],

where
c_{s}>0
, and
a_{s}^{j},b_{s}^{\ell}\in\bm{R}
.

Algorithm 1

Iterative GP power allocation algorithm.

1:

Initialize
\bar{\bm{p}}=\bar{\bm{p}}_{0}
and set the maximum iterative number
T^{\max}
.

2:

Set
I=1
.

3:

while

I\leq T^{\max}

do

4:

Compute coefficients
\beta_{s}
by
\beta_{s}=u_{s}(\bar{\bm{p}})/[\prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod_{\ell=1}^{J_{D}}g_{k}(\bar{\bm{p}})g_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{\bm{p}})]
.

5:

Substitute the denominator of the objective function in
\bm{P2}
by
\tilde{g}(\bar{\bm{p}})
, where
\tilde{g}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})=\prod_{s}\left[\frac{u_{s}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})}{\beta_{s}}\right]^{\beta_{s}}
.

6:

Solve the new GP optimization problem in convex optimization methods with the objective function
\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{K}\prod_{\ell=1}^{J_{D}}f_{k}(\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})f_{\ell}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bm{p})}{\tilde{g}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})}
and the same constraints as in
\bm{P2}
.

7:

Update
\bar{\bm{p}}={\bar{\bm{p}}^{*}}
, where
{\bar{\bm{p}}^{*}}
is the optimal solution of the new GP problem.

8:

I=I+1
.

9:

end

while

10:

return
The optimal solution
{\bar{\bm{p}}^{*}}
of
\bm{P2}
.

The main complexity in Algorithm
1
for each iteration reflects on the computation of coefficients
\bm{\beta}
and the process of solving GP. Actually, the computation of new function
\tilde{g}(\bm{p},\bm{p^{{}^{\prime}}})
requires
(K+J_{D})(d_{c}+1)+\sum_{s}(J+J_{D}+3)
multiplications and
\sum_{s}(J+J_{D}+1)
exponentiations. Besides, GP is usually solved by interior point methods, which is proved to have polynomial time complexity
[
34
,
36
]
.

6
Conclusion

We investigate the hybrid cellular network with D2D communications, where SCMA schemes is employed for cellular users. To improve the performance of system sum rate region, we first derive the capacity for cellular users as well as D2D users. Then we consider the joint power optimization problem to maximize the system sum rate and propose a GP based iterative algorithm. Simulation results prove the fast convergence and show the much improvement of proposed algorithm compared with random power allocation. With the increasing number of D2D users, the proposed algorithm performs prominent for the hybrid network model.

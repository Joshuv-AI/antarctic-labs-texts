Inversion of limited-aperture Fresnel experimental data using orthogonality sampling method with single and multiple sources
Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
In this study, we consider the application of orthogonality sampling method (OSM) with single and multiple sources for a fast identification of small objects in limited-aperture inverse scattering problem. We first apply the OSM with single source and show that the indicator function with single source can be expressed by the Bessel function of order zero of the first kind, infinite series of Bessel function of nonzero integer order of the first kind, range of signal receiver, and the location of emitter. Based on this result, we explain that the objects can be identified through the OSM with single source but the identification is significantly influenced by the location of source and applied frequency. For a successful improvement, we then consider the OSM with multiple sources. Based on the identified structure of the OSM with single source, we design an indicator function of the OSM with multiple sources and show that it can be expressed by the square of the Bessel function of order zero of the first kind an infinite series of the square of Bessel function of nonzero integer order of the first kind. Based on the theoretical results, we explain that the objects can be identified uniquely through the designed OSM. Several numerical experiments with experimental data provided by the Institute Fresnel demonstrate the pros and cons of the OSM with single source and how the designed OSM with multiple sources behave.

Indicator function with single source

In this section, we consider the design an indicator function with single source. Let us denote
\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{a}_{m})
as the following arrangement of measurement data

\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{a}_{m})=\Big(u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{1},\mathbf{r}),u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{2},\mathbf{r}),\ldots,u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{N},\mathbf{r})\Big).

(Availability and limitation of object detection)

[

, the following indicator function (say, OSMM) can be used: for each
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in\Omega

\mathfrak{F}_{\osmm}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathfrak{F}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m}).

Although one can obtain good result via the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\osmm}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
, we introduce another indicator function to obtain a better result. To this end, we denote
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
as the following arrangement

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Big(\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{1}),\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{2}),\ldots,\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{N})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{\mathrm{b}}}{6|\mathbf{b}|}\begin{pmatrix}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\displaystyle\int_{D}\left(\frac{\varepsilon(\mathbf{r})-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{1})\bigg[J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\mathbf{r}|)+\frac{3}{\pi}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{1})\bigg]\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\\
\displaystyle\int_{D}\left(\frac{\varepsilon(\mathbf{r})-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{2})\bigg[J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\mathbf{r}|)+\frac{3}{\pi}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{2})\bigg]\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\vdots\\
\displaystyle\int_{D}\left(\frac{\varepsilon(\mathbf{r})-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{m})\bigg[J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-\mathbf{r}|)+\frac{3}{\pi}\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{m})\bigg]\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\end{pmatrix}^{T}.

where
\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m})
satisfies
\mathfrak{F}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m})=|\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m})|
.
Then, based on the structure of the
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
, it seems natural to test the orthogonality relation between the
\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m})
and
G(\cdot,\mathbf{a}_{m})
. Thus, by introducing a test vector,

\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\Big(G(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{1}),G(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{2}),\ldots,G(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{M})\Big),\quad\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in\Omega,

the following indicator function (say, MOSM) with multiple sources can be introduced

\mathfrak{F}_{\mosm}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=|\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\cdot\overline{\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}|=\left|\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Phi(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m})\overline{G(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{a}_{m})}\right|.

The map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\mosm}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
will contain peaks of large magnitude at
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in D_{s}
thereby, it will be possible to recognize the existence or outline shape of
D_{s}
,
s=1,2,\ldots,S

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},\mathbf{r},\mathbf{a}_{m})|

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have considered the application of the OSM with single source to identify the existence and outline shape of small dielectric objects from real-world experimental data. Thanks to the asymptotic expansion formula for the scattered field data in the presence of small object, we derived an accurate relationship between the indicator function and infinite series of the Bessel function of the first kind. On the basis of the derived relationship, we explored various properties of the indicator function including the applicability and limitation. We have also verified the theoretical result with experimental data at various frequencies.

In order to improve the imaging performance of the OSM, we have collected scattered field data with multiple sources and introduced a new indicator function. Throughout a careful analysis, we have shown that the imaging performance of the designed indicator function is independent to the location of the emitter and better than the traditional ones with single and multiple sources. Moreover, it can be possible to identify small object uniquely if applied frequency is not extremely low or high.

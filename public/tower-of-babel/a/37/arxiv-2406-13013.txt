Abstract
. We present a lower bound for the classical Kloosterman sum S ⁡ ( a , b , c ) S(a,b;c) where ( a ​ b , c ) = 1 (ab,c)=1 and c c is an odd integer. 1.
Introduction

The classical Kloosterman sum
S(a,b;c)
is given by

S(a,b;c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\text{mod}\hskip 2.84526ptc)\\
\gcd(x,c)=1\end{subarray}}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{ax+b\overline{x}(c)}{c}\right)},

where
a,b
are integers,
c
is a natural number and
\overline{x}(c)
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
x
modulo
c
.
The sums for the special case of
a=0
or
b=0
are called Ramanujan sums. Kloosterman sums appear in various problems in number theory. One of the important places of its occurrence is in the Kuznetsov trace formula
[
Kuz80
, Theorem 1]
and Petersson’s trace formula.
Recently, the non-vanishing aspect of Kloosterman sums has been used in
[
BDS23
, Theorem 2]
and
[
Das24
]
to obtain discrepancy results for the distribution of eigenvalues of Hecke operators. Whenever we have non-vanishing of a sum, a natural question to ask is whether we can obtain a lower bound for the same.
To the best of our knowledge, no such lower bound for Kloosterman sums has been established in the literature. In the next section, we answer this question for
S(a,b;c),
where
(ab,c)=1
.
First, we obtain the following lower bound for classical Kloosterman sums.

Theorem 1
.

Let
c
be an odd natural number such that
c=du
, where
u
is squarefree and
d
is a powerful number with
(d,u)=1
. Let
a,b
be integers such that
(ab,c)=1
and
ab
is a quadratic residue modulo each prime dividing
d
(and hence,
ab
is a square modulo
d
by Hensel’s lemma and Chinese remainder theorem).
Then

\left|S(a,b;c)\right|\geq\frac{2^{\omega(d)}}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\tau(u)\sqrt{u}\right)^{\phi(u)/\tau(u)-1}}\right),

where
\omega(d)
is the number of distinct prime divisors of
d
,
\tau(u)
is the number of positive divisors of
u,
and
\phi(u)
denotes the Euler’s phi function of
u
.
If
ab
is a quadratic non-residue modulo a prime dividing
d
(and hence
ab
is not a square modulo
d
), then
S(a,b;c)=0
.

Let
S_{k}(N)
denote the space of cusp forms of even weight
k\geq 2
and level
N.
The discrepancy between two probability measures involving eigenvalues of cusp forms

Upper bounds (see

The Bessel functions of the first kind

2.
Lower bound for Kloosterman sums

In this section, we primarily prove Theorem
1
by considering squarefree and odd prime power cases separately, and we consider the general case in Section

2.1.
Squarefree case

Proposition 1
.

Let
c
be a squarefree positive integer such that
(a,c)=1.
Then

|S(a,b;c)|\geq\frac{1}{\left(\tau(c)\sqrt{c}\right)^{\phi(c)/\tau(c)-1}},

Proof.

Let
\alpha=S(a,b;c)\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{c})
, where
\zeta_{c}=e^{2\pi i/c}
.
An element of Gal
(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{c})/\mathbb{Q})

sends
\zeta_{c}
to
\zeta_{c}^{n}
where
(n,c)=1.
Hence, the conjugates of
\alpha
are the Kloosterman sums
S(an,bn;c),
where
(n,c)=1.
In fact, we have
S(an,bn;c)=S(an^{2},b;c)=S(a,bn^{2};c).

For
c
prime,
S(a,b;c)
is a linear combination of at most
c-1

c
-th roots of unity, but they are necessarily linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. So if the linear combination becomes
0
, all the coefficients need to be
0
which is not the case.
Hence
S(a,b;c)\neq 0
for
c
prime. Using the multiplicativity of Kloosterman sums (see Lemma
4
),
S(a,b;c)\neq 0
for squarefree
c
.
Let
\mathcal{N}(\alpha)
denote the norm of
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{c})
over
\mathbb{Q}.

Since
\alpha=S(a,b;c)
is a non-zero algebraic integer in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{c})
, its norm
\mathcal{N}(\alpha)
is a non-zero integer and therefore,

1\leq|\mathcal{N}(\alpha)|=|\mathcal{N}(S(a,b;c))|=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,c)=1\\
1\leq n\leq c\end{subarray}}|S(an^{2},b;c)|.

Hence,

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,c)=1\\
1\leq n\leq c\end{subarray}}|S(an^{2},b;c)|\geq 1.

Let
\rho(c)
be the number of solutions modulo
c
of
x^{2}\equiv 1
(mod
c
). We have
\rho(c)=\tau(c)
for squarefree
c
.
Using Weil’s bound together with
(a,c)=1
, we have

\frac{1}{|S(a,b;c)|^{\tau(c)}}\leq\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,c)=1\\
1<n\leq c\\
n^{2}\not\equiv 1(c)\end{subarray}}|S(an^{2},b;c)|\leq\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,c)=1\\
1<n\leq c\\
n^{2}\not\equiv 1(c)\end{subarray}}\left(\tau(c)\sqrt{c}\right)=\left(\tau(c)\sqrt{c}\right)^{\phi(c)-\tau(c)}.

Hence we obtain

|S(a,b;c)|\geq\frac{1}{\left(\tau(c)\sqrt{c}\right)^{\phi(c)/\tau(c)-1}}.

∎

2.2.
Odd prime power case

Let
q
be odd, and
\epsilon_{q}
be
1
or
i
depending on whether
q\equiv

1
or
-1

For odd prime power moduli

Proposition 2
.

[
IK04
, Chapter 12, Exercise 1]

Let
q=p^{\beta}
for some prime
p
and
\beta\geq 2.
Suppose
p
does not divide
2ab
. If
ab
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
, then

(2.1)

S(a,b;q)=2\left(\frac{l}{q}\right)\sqrt{q}\text{ Re}\left(\epsilon_{q}e\left(\frac{2l}{q}\right)\right),

where
l^{2}\equiv ab
(mod

. If

is a quadratic non-residue modulo

From the above Proposition, we deduce the following lower bound for
|S(a,b;q)|,
if

Proposition 3
.

Let
q=p^{\beta}
for some prime
p
and
\beta\geq 2.

Suppose
p
does not divide
2ab
. If
ab
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
, then

|S(a,b;q)|\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{q}}.

If
ab
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
p
, then
S(a,b;q)=0
.

Proof.

Assume that
ab
is a quadratic residue modulo
p.
Using Proposition
2
, we have

S(a,b;q)=2\left(\frac{l}{q}\right)\sqrt{q}\text{ Re}\left(\epsilon_{q}e\left(\frac{2l}{q}\right)\right),

where

(2.2)

l^{2}\equiv ab\ (\mbox{mod }q).

We start by noting that
q
does not divide
l
and hence
\left|\left(\dfrac{l}{q}\right)\right|=1.
Let
l=qt+r
where
0\leq r\leq q-1.

Note that
p\nmid 2ab
implies that
p
is odd and so is
q.
We consider the following two cases depending on whether
q\equiv

1
or
-1
modulo
4.

Case 1:
q\equiv 1(\text{mod }4)

In this case, we have
\epsilon_{q}=1
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\|x\|
denote the distance from
x
to the nearest integer. Using
|\sin\pi x|\geq 2\|x\|
for all real
x
, we have

\displaystyle|S(a,b;q)|=

\displaystyle 2\left|\left(\frac{l}{q}\right)\sqrt{q}\,\text{Re}\,\left(e\left(\frac{2l}{q}\right)\right)\right|=2\sqrt{q}\left|\cos\left(\frac{4\pi r}{q}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{q}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{4\pi r}{q}\right)\right|\geq 4\sqrt{q}\left\|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4r}{q}\right\|.

Case 2:
q\equiv-1(\text{mod }4)

In this case, we have
\epsilon_{q}=i
and hence, we have

\displaystyle|S(a,b;q)|=

\displaystyle 2\left|\left(\frac{l}{q}\right)\sqrt{q}\,\text{Re}\,\left(ie\left(\frac{2l}{q}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{q}\left|\sin\left(\frac{4\pi r}{q}\right)\right|\geq 4\sqrt{q}\left\|\frac{4r}{q}\right\|.

Combining both cases, we obtain

(2.3)

|S(a,b;q)|\geq 4\sqrt{q}\min\left(\left\|\frac{q-8r}{2q}\right\|,\left\|\frac{4r}{q}\right\|\right).

We first show that
\left\|\frac{q-8r}{2q}\right\|\neq 0.
Suppose not, then
\frac{q-8r}{2q}=t
for some
t\in\{0,-1,-2,-3\}
as
0\leq r\leq q-1.

We have
p\,|\,r^{2}-ab
which implies that
p
divides
\frac{(1-2t)^{2}q^{2}-64ab}{64}.
This is an impossibility since
p
does not divide
2ab.

A similar argument yields
\left\|\frac{4r}{q}\right\|\neq 0.

Proceeding now from the equation (
2.3
), we conclude
that

|S(a,b;q)|\geq 4\sqrt{q}\min\left(\left\|\frac{q-8r}{2q}\right\|,\left\|\frac{4r}{q}\right\|\right)\geq 4\sqrt{q}\min\left(\frac{1}{2q},\frac{1}{q}\right)\geq\frac{2}{\sqrt{q}}.

∎

2.3.
General Case

In this section, we apply the multiplicativity property of Kloosterman sums.
For
m,n\in\mathbb{N},

(m,n)=1,
let
\overline{m}(n)
denote the multiplicative inverse of
m
in

(mod

We have the following factorization of Kloosterman sums into Kloosterman sums to prime power moduli.

Proposition 4
.

[

Let

where
q_{1},\,\dots\,,q_{t}
are distinct prime numbers,
q_{i}^{\beta_{i}}=l_{i},
and

c_{i}=\frac{c}{\prod_{j=1}^{i}l_{j}}

Further let

m_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\overline{l_{j}}({c_{j}})

Then

S(a,b;c)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}S(am_{i-1}\overline{c_{i}}({l_{i}}),bm_{i-1}\overline{c_{i}}({l_{i}});l_{i}).

Proof.

The proof uses equation (1.59) of
[
IK04
]
repetitively which is given by

S(a,b;ll^{\prime})=S(a\overline{l^{\prime}}(l),b\overline{l^{\prime}}(l);l)S(a\overline{l}(l^{\prime}),b\overline{l}(l^{\prime});l^{\prime})

where
(l,l^{\prime})=1
.
∎

.

Assume the hypotheses in the above Proposition

is a quadratic residue modulo

with

.

We first factorise

using Proposition

. Then we apply Proposition

to the Kloosterman sum with modulus

Corollary 2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c
be an odd natural number such that
c=du,
where
u
is squarefree and
d
is a powerful number with
(d,u)=1,
and
(ab,c)=1
. Assume that
ab
is a quadratic residue modulo each prime dividing
d
.
Then

\left|S(a,b;c)\right|\geq\left(\frac{u^{2}}{c}\right)^{1/2}\frac{2^{\omega(c)}}{(\tau(u)^{2}u)^{\phi(u)/2\tau(u)}}.

Proof.

The proof follows from the following observation:

\begin{split}&\frac{2^{\omega(d)}}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\tau(u)\sqrt{u}\right)^{\phi(u)/\tau(u)-1}}\right)\\
\geq&\frac{2^{\omega(d)+\omega(u)}}{\sqrt{du}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\tau(u)\right)^{\phi(u)/\tau(u)}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left(\sqrt{u}\right)^{\phi(u)/\tau(u)-2}}\right)\\
\geq&\frac{2^{\omega(c)}}{\sqrt{c}}\frac{1}{\tau(u)^{\phi(u)/\tau(u)}}\frac{1}{u^{\phi(u)/2\tau(u)-1}},\end{split}

where we have used
\tau(u)=2^{\omega(u)}
for squarefree
u.

∎

3.
An application

Let
k
and
N
be positive integers with
k
even. Recall that

S_{k}(N)
denotes the vector space of cusp forms of weight
k
and level
N
. For
(n,N)=1,
the
n
-th (normalised) Hecke operator acting on
S_{k}(N)
is given by

T_{n}(f)(z)=n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\sum_{ad=n,d>0}\frac{1}{d^{k}}\sum_{b\,(\text{mod}\,d)}f\Big(\frac{az+b}{d}\Big).

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
be an orthonormal basis of
S_{k}(N)
consisting only of joint eigenfunctions of the Hecke operators
T_{n}
. Since
S_{k}(N)
is finite dimensional,
|\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)|
is finite. For
f\in S_{k}(N),
the Fourier expansion of
f
at the cusp
\infty
is given by

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}.

We denote
\lambda_{n}(f)
to be the
n
-th normalised Hecke eigenvalue of
f
, that is,
T_{n}(f)=\lambda_{n}(f)f
. The Fourier coefficient
a_{n}(f)
and
\lambda_{n}(f)
are related by the condition
a_{n}(f)=a_{1}(f)\lambda_{n}(f).

Petersson (see

[
Pet32
]
) expressed a weighted sum of
\overline{a_{m}(f)}a_{n}(f)
over
f
with
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
in terms of Bessel function of the first kind and Kloosterman sums. Let us consider the Fourier coefficient
a_{n}(f)
with harmonic weights

\rho_{n}(f)=\Bigg(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}(f).

The sum under consideration is

(3.1)

\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\overline{\rho_{m}(f)}\rho_{n}(f).

Putting
m=1
in the above equation, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,N}(1,n)=

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\overline{\rho_{1}(f)}\rho_{n}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\right)\overline{a_{1}(f)}a_{n}(f)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\right)|a_{1}(f)|^{2}\lambda_{n}(f),

since
a_{n}(f)=a_{1}(f)\lambda_{n}(f)
.
Hence,

(3.2)

\Delta_{k,N}(1,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\right)|a_{1}(f)|^{2}\lambda_{n}(f).

The above equation demonstrates how Petersson’s trace formula is indeed a weighted trace formula for Hecke operators.

We use the following version of Petersson’s trace formula given in
[

, Proposition 2.1]
.

Proposition 5

(
[

, Proposition 2.1]
)

Let
k,m,n,N
be natural numbers with
k
even. We have

\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\sum_{N|c,c>0}\frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\Big),

where
\delta(m,n)
is the Kronecker diagonal symbol,
J_{k-1}(x)
is the Bessel function of the first kind, and
S(m,n;c)
is the classical Kloosterman sum.

We will use the following two lemmas concerning the
J
-Bessel function.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\nu>0
and
0<x\leq 1
, we have

1\leq\frac{J_{\nu}(\nu x)}{x^{\nu}J_{\nu}(\nu)}\leq e^{\nu(1-x)}.

Proof.

We refer to
[
Par84
]
for a proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,C_{0})
be arbitrary and
A(\epsilon):=C_{0}-\epsilon
, where
C_{0}=\frac{2^{1/3}}{3^{2/3}\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}=0.4473\dots.

Then, for all
\nu\geq\left(\frac{3}{\epsilon}\right)^{3/2},
we have

(3.3)

\frac{A(\epsilon)}{\nu^{1/3}}\leq J_{\nu}(\nu)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\nu^{1/3}}+\frac{3}{\nu}.

In particular, when
\epsilon=0.4470,

\frac{A(\epsilon)}{\nu^{1/3}}\leq J_{\nu}(\nu)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\nu^{1/3}}+\frac{3}{\nu}

for all
\nu\geq 18.

Proof.

The proof follows from a direct application of
[
Kra14
, Theorem 1]
with
z=0,
which implies that

(3.4)

J_{\nu}(\nu)=\frac{2^{1/3}}{\nu^{1/3}}Ai(0)+\frac{3\theta}{\nu},

where
\theta
is such that
|\theta|\leq 1,
and
Ai(x)
is the Airy function. We have

Ai(0)=\frac{1}{3^{2/3}\Gamma(\frac{2}{3})}.
Therefore,

-\frac{3}{\nu}\leq J_{\nu}(\nu)-\frac{C_{0}}{\nu^{1/3}}\leq\frac{3}{\nu}.

For all
\nu\geq\left(\frac{3}{\epsilon}\right)^{3/2},
we have
\nu^{2/3}\geq\frac{3}{C_{0}-A(\epsilon)},
that is,
\frac{C_{0}}{\nu^{1/3}}-\frac{3}{\nu}\geq\frac{A(\epsilon)}{\nu^{1/3}}
, and this proves the lower bound for
J_{\nu}(\nu).

∎

after an outline of the same.

Outline of the proof of Theorem

We first break the infinite sum over
c
in Proposition
5
into two parts given by
c=N
and
c>N.
For
c>N,
applying the integral test, we give an upper bound using the exponential bound for the J-Bessel function. This is achieved in equation (

). In the next step, we use Lemma
3.2
and Theorem

to get a lower bound for
\left|\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)-\delta(m,n)\right|.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Applying Proposition
5
, breaking the
c
-sum into the parts with
c=N
and
c>N
, and writing
c=bN
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)

\displaystyle=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n;N)}{N}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\Big)+2\pi i^{k}\sum_{b\geq 2}\frac{S(m,n;bN)}{bN}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big).

Using Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\left|J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|J_{k-1}\left((k-1)\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)bN}\right)\right|\leq e^{\nu(1-x)}x^{\nu}|J_{\nu}(\nu)|,

where
\nu=k-1
and
x=\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)bN}.

For
b\geq 2,
and
k\geq 28,
we have

(3.5)

\frac{8}{9b}\leq\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)bN}\leq\frac{10}{9b},

which implies

\displaystyle e^{\nu(1-x)}x^{\nu}

\displaystyle=e^{(k-1)(1-x+\log x)}\leq e^{(k-1)\big(1-\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{10}{9b}\big)}.

Therefore, using a trivial bound
|S(m,n;bN)|\leq\phi(bN),
we obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{b\geq 2}\frac{S(m,n;bN)}{bN}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\right|\\
\leq&\sum_{b\geq 2}\left|J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\right|\leq|J_{k-1}(k-1)|e^{(k-1)}\sum_{b\geq 2}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\\
=&|J_{k-1}(k-1)|e^{(k-1)}\Bigg(\sum_{b=2}^{4}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg)+|J_{k-1}(k-1)|e^{(k-1)}\Bigg(\sum_{b\geq 5}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg).\end{split}

Note that we have replaced
\log\frac{10}{9b}
by
-\log\frac{9b}{10}
. Since

(3.7)

e^{(k-1)}\Bigg(\sum_{b=2}^{4}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg)\leq 3e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)},

we focus on
\displaystyle\sum_{b\geq 5}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}
only.

Let
F(x)=e^{-(k-1)\left(\frac{8}{9x}+\log\frac{9x}{10}\right)}
. Then
F
is decreasing for
x>2.
Using integral test, we obtain

(3.8)

\begin{split}\sum_{b\geq 5}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\leq&\int_{4}^{\infty}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9x}+\log\frac{9x}{10}\big)}\,dx\\
=&\frac{25}{18}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{9}}e^{-(k-1)y}\left(\frac{5y}{4}\right)^{k-3}\,dy\\
\leq&\frac{25}{18}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{9}}\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k-3}e^{-(k-1)y}\,dy\\
=&\frac{25}{18}\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k-3}\left(\frac{1-e^{\frac{-2(k-1)}{9}}}{(k-1)}\right)\leq\frac{25}{18}\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k-3}\frac{1}{(k-1)}.\end{split}

Combining equations (
3.6
), (
3.7
), and (
3.8
), we have

(3.9)

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{b\geq 2}\frac{S(m,n;bN)}{bN}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\right|\leq|J_{k-1}(k-1)|\left(3e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}+\frac{25}{18}\frac{e^{2}}{(k-1)}\Big(\frac{5e}{18}\Big)^{k-3}\right).\end{split}

On considering equation (
3.9
) and equation (
3.6
), we obtain

\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n;N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)+B,

where

\begin{split}B=2\pi i^{k}\sum_{b\geq 2}\frac{S(m,n;bN)}{bN}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big),\end{split}

with

(3.10)

\begin{split}|B|\leq&2\pi|J_{k-1}(k-1)|\left(3e^{H_{0}(k-1)}+\frac{25}{18}\frac{e^{2}}{(k-1)}\Big(\frac{5e}{18}\Big)^{k-3}\right)\\
\leq&6.01\pi|J_{k-1}(k-1)|e^{H_{0}(k-1)},\end{split}

where we use the fact that

\frac{25}{18}\frac{e^{2}}{(k-1)}\Big(\frac{5e}{18}\Big)^{k-3}\leq 0.005e^{H_{0}(k-1)},

for all
k\geq 22.

Since
\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N(k-1)}\leq 1,
using Lemma
3.1
together with the fact that
J_{k-1}(k-1)>0
(this follows from Lemma
3.2
), we obtain

(3.11)

J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)\geq J_{k-1}\left(k-1\right)\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N(k-1)}\right)^{k-1}\geq D_{0}^{k-1}J_{k-1}\left(k-1\right).

Let
G(N)=\frac{u}{(\tau(u)^{2}u)^{\phi(u)/2\tau(u)}},
for
N=du.
Then, Corollary
2
gives

\left|S(m,n;N)\right|\geq 2^{\omega(N)}\frac{G(N)}{N^{1/2}}.

Combining the previous equation with Equation (
3.11
), we get

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\left\lvert\frac{S(m,n;N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)\right\rvert\geq 2^{\omega(N)}\frac{G(N)}{N^{3/2}}D_{0}^{k-1}J_{k-1}(k-1)=H(N)D_{0}^{k-1}J_{k-1}(k-1).\end{split}

We now find a lower bound for
\left|\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)-\delta(m,n)\right|
.
For all
k\geq k_{0}(N),
we have

k-1\geq 1+\left\lfloor\frac{\log 7-\log H(N)}{\log A_{0}}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{\log 7-\log H(N)}{\log A_{0}},

that is,
\left(D_{0}e^{-H_{0}}\right)^{k-1}\geq\frac{7}{H(N)},
and this condition implies that

(3.13)

\begin{split}7e^{H_{0}(k-1)}J_{k-1}(k-1)\leq H(N)D_{0}^{k-1}J_{k-1}(k-1)\end{split}

holds.

Combining equations (
3.10
), (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we obtain the following inequality for all
k\geq k_{0}(N),

\begin{split}\frac{|B|}{2\pi}\leq 3.005e^{H_{0}(k-1)}J_{k-1}(k-1)\leq 7e^{H_{0}(k-1)}J_{k-1}(k-1)\leq\left\lvert\frac{S(m,n;N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)\right\rvert.\end{split}

Therefore, for all
k\geq k_{0}(N),
we have

\begin{split}\left|\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)-\delta(m,n)\right|=&\left|2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n;N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)+B\right|\\
\geq&2\pi\left(\left|\frac{S(m,n;N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)\right|-\frac{|B|}{2\pi}\right)\\
\geq&7.99\pi J_{k-1}(k-1)e^{H_{0}(k-1)},\end{split}

since
\left(D_{0}e^{-H_{0}}\right)^{k-1}\geq\frac{7}{H(N)}
is true for all
k\geq k_{0}(N).

∎

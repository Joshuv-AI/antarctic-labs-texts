Abstract
. If p : ℬ → G p\colon\mathcal{B}\to G is a Fell bundle over an étale groupoid, then we show that there is an norm reducing injective linear map j : C r ∗ ​ ( G , ℬ ) → Γ 0 ​ ( G , ℬ ) j\colon C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})\to\Gamma_{0}(G;\mathcal{B}) generalizing the well know map j : C r ∗ ​ ( G ) → C 0 ​ ( G ) j\colon C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C_{0}(G) in the case of an étale groupoid.



1.
Introduction

One of the key tools in working with étale groupoids is Renault’s
“j-map” from
[
14
]
*Proposition II.4.2.
Specifically, Renault shows that there is an injective norm reducing
linear map
j\colon C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C_{0}(G)
such that

j(f)(\gamma)=f(\gamma)
when
f\in C_{c}(G)
. Crucially,
j
also
preserves the algebraic operations so that if
a,b\in C^{*}_{r}(G)
, then

(1.1)

j(a^{*})(\gamma)=\overline{j(a)(\gamma^{-1})}\quad\text{and}\quad j(a*b)(\gamma)=\sum_{\eta\in G^{r(\gamma)}}j(a)(\eta)\,j(b)(\eta^{-1}\gamma).

In fact, Renault works with a continuous
2
-cocycle, and has observed
in
[
16
]
that, more generally, a similar result holds for
twists over étale groupoids. A proof is supplied in

[
1
]
*Proposition 2.8.

The purpose of this note is to extend this result to the
C^{*}
-algebra
of a Fell bundle
p\colon\mathcal{B}\to G
over an étale groupoid
G
.
Specifically, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
p\colon\mathcal{B}\to G
is a separable,
saturated Fell bundle over a second countable, locally compact
Hausdorff étale groupoid
G
. Then there is an
injective

norm reducing linear map
j\colon C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})\to\Gamma_{0}(G;\mathcal{B})
such
that
j(f)(\gamma)=f(\gamma)
if
f\in\Gamma_{c}(G;\mathcal{B})
. Furthermore, for all

a,b\in C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})
and
\gamma\in G
, we have

(1.2)

j(a^{*})(\gamma)=j(a)(\gamma^{-1})^{*}

and

(1.3)

j(a*b)(\gamma)=\sum_{\eta\in G^{r(\gamma)}}j(a)(\eta)\,j(b)(\eta^{-1}\gamma)

where the sum converges in the norm topology in
B_{\gamma}
.

The existence of
j
is reasonably straightforward as is the adjoint
property (
1.2
). However, to be truly useful in applications,
we clearly need to establish the convolution result (
1.3
).
Unlike the situation of Renault’s original result, the convolution
result requires considerable technology. Renault is able to show that
the sum in (
1.1
) converges absolutely basically using
Hölder’s inequality. Here we have to use the internal tensor
product of Hilbert modules. As a consequence, we are only able to
show convergence of the sum in (
1.3
) in the strong sense
described in Remark
2.3
. We suspect that the sum does not
converge absolutely in general.

Our result subsumes earlier results for crossed
products by discrete groups twisted by a unitary
2
-cocycle in

[
21
]
.

Since
u\in G^{(0)}
was arbitrary, we have

(3.19)

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{r}\quad\text{for any $f\in\Gamma_{c}(G;\mathcal{B})$.}

It follows that the map sending

to

is a bounded linear map. Hence we can
extend to the completion and obtain a linear map

(3.20)

\displaystyle j\colon C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})\to\Gamma_{0}(G;\mathcal{B})

such that
\|j(f)\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{r}
and such that
j(f)=f

for all

.

To see that

is injective, it suffices to see that if

a\in C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})
and
a\not=0
, then
j(a)\not=0
. But if
a\not=0
,
then by Lemma
3.1
, there is a
u\in G^{(0)}
such that

V_{u}(a)\not=0
. Since elements of the form
h_{\gamma}^{b}
with

\gamma\in G_{u}
and

span a dense subspace of

\mathsf{X}_{u}
, there must be vectors
h_{\gamma}^{b}
and
h_{\beta}^{c}

in
\Gamma_{c}(G_{u};\mathcal{B})\subset\mathsf{X}_{u}
such that

(3.21)

\langle h_{\gamma}^{b}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(a)h_{\beta}^{c}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}\not=0.

Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
\Gamma_{c}(G;\mathcal{B})
converging to
a
in

C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})
. Since

is bounded,
j(f_{n})\to j(a)
in

\Gamma_{0}(G;\mathcal{B})
. In particular,

(3.22)

j(a)(\gamma\beta^{-1})=\lim_{n}j(f_{n})(\gamma\beta^{-1}).

Since multiplication in
\mathcal{B}
is continuous and associative, this
implies

(3.23)

b^{*}j(a)(\gamma\beta^{-1})c=\lim_{n}b^{*}j(f_{n})(\gamma\beta^{-1})c.

On the other hand, by (

),

(3.24)

b^{*}j(f_{n})(\gamma\beta^{-1})c=b^{*}f_{n}(\gamma\beta^{-1})c=\langle h_{\gamma}^{b}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(f_{n})h_{\beta}^{c}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}.

Since
V_{u}
is bounded,

(3.25)

\lim_{n}\langle h_{\gamma}^{b}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(f_{n})h_{\beta}^{c}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}=\langle h_{\gamma}^{b}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(a)h_{\beta}^{c}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}.

We have shown that

converges to
both

and

\langle h_{\gamma}^{b}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(a)h_{\beta}^{c}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}
in a fibre of

. Since those fibres are Hausdorff by Lemma

, we

\langle h_{\gamma}^{b}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(a)h_{\beta}^{c}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}=b^{*}j(a)(\gamma\beta^{-1})c.

Since the left-hand side of (

) is nonzero by assumption,
it follows that

. Thus,

is
injective.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
a\in C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})
, then

j(a^{*})(\eta)=j(a)(\eta^{-1})^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
(f_{n})
be a sequence in
\Gamma_{c}(G;\mathcal{B})
such that

in

. Then for all

,

and

. But by

. Hence

(f_{n}(\eta^{-1})^{*})
converges to both

and

in

. Since the relative topology on

∎

If

, then for any

to

of

. In particular, we have the
following.

Proposition 3.7
.

If

and

\|j_{u}(a)\|_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}\leq\|a\|_{r}.

Suppose that

is an approximate

(3.28)

\bigl[V_{u}(f)h^{e_{n}}_{u}\bigr](\gamma)=f(\gamma)e_{n}.

Thus, on the one hand,

\|V_{u}(f)h_{u}^{e_{n}}\|^{2}_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}\leq\|f\|_{r}^{2}\|h_{u}^{e_{n}}\|_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{r}^{2}.

On the other hand,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\|V_{u}(f)h_{u}^{e_{n}}\|^{2}_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}

\displaystyle=\|\langle V_{u}(f)h_{u}^{e_{n}}\penalty\mathrel{,}\penalty V_{u}(f)h_{u}^{e_{n}}\rangle_{{\mskip-3.0mu}\hbox{\vrule height=8.5pt,width=0.0pt}\scriptscriptstyle{A_{u}}}\|_{A_{u}}

Suppose that
(f_{n})
is a sequence in
\Gamma_{c}(G;\mathcal{B})
such that

in

. Since

Corollary 5.3
.

After realizing

as in

and
\mathsf{W}_{\gamma}
as in Lemma

, the

) extends to elementary tensors in

. That is, if

and

, then

\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{y})=\sum_{\eta\in G_{u}}\mathbf{x}(\eta)^{*}\,\mathbf{y}(\eta).

In particular, the sum in (

) converges in
B_{\gamma}
,
and

(5.11)

\Bigl\|\sum_{\eta\in G_{u}}\mathbf{x}(\eta)^{*}\,\mathbf{y}(\eta)\Bigr\|\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathsf{W}_{\gamma}}.

Proof.

If
F
is a finite subset of
G_{u}
, then let

\mathbf{x}_{F}=\mathbb{1}_{F}\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}_{F}=\mathbb{1}_{F}\mathbf{y}
. Then, just as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, the nets

(\mathbf{x}_{F})
and
(\mathbf{y}_{F})
converge to
\mathbf{x}
in
\mathsf{X}_{u}
and

\mathbf{y}
in
\mathsf{W}_{\gamma}
, respectively. By Lemma
2.4
,
the net

thus converges to
\flat(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{y}
in

\widetilde{\mathsf{X}}_{u}\otimes_{\mathcal{K}(\mathsf{X}_{u})}\mathsf{W}_{\gamma}
. Therefore

\bigl(\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x}_{F})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{F})\bigr)
converges
to
\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x})\otimes\mathbf{y})
in

. Since

\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x}_{F})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{F})=\sum_{\eta\in F}\mathbf{x}(\eta)^{*}\,\mathbf{y}(\eta),

and since

is Hausdorff, this establishes (

).

Since

preserves the inner product,

\Bigl\|\sum_{\eta\in F}\mathbf{x}(\eta)^{*}\,\mathbf{y}(\eta)\Bigr\|=\|\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x}_{F})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{F})\|=\|\flat(\mathbf{x}_{F})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{F}\|.

, we conclude that

) as well.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose that
a,b\in C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})
and that

\gamma\in G^{u}_{v}
. Then

(5.15)

\sum_{\eta\in G^{u}}j(a)(\eta)\,j(b)(\eta^{-1}\gamma)

converges in
B_{\gamma}
. Moreover,

(5.16)

j(a*b)(\gamma)=\sum_{\eta\in G^{u}}j(a)(\eta)\,j(b)(\eta^{-1}\gamma).

Proof.

To see that (
5.15
) converges, it suffices to see that

(5.17)

\sum_{\eta\in G_{u}}j(a)(\eta^{-1})\,j(b)(\eta\gamma)

converges. Using Corollary
3.6
, the sum in
(
5.17
) is the same as

(5.18)

\sum_{\eta\in G_{u}}j(a^{*})(\eta)^{*}\,j(b)(\eta\gamma).

Now let
\mathbf{x}_{a}(\eta)=j(a^{*})(\eta)
for
\eta\in G_{u}
. Then
by Proposition
3.7
,
\mathbf{x}_{a}\in\mathsf{X}_{u}
with

\|\mathbf{x}_{a}\|_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}\leq\|a\|_{r}
. Note that the same proposition
states that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{\eta\in G_{u}}j(b)(\eta\gamma)^{*}\,j(b)(\eta\gamma)\Bigr\|=\Bigl\|\sum_{\eta\in G_{v}}j(b)(\eta)^{*}\,j(b)(\eta)\Bigr\|\leq\|b\|_{r}^{2}.

In particular, if we let
\mathbf{y}_{b}(\eta)=j(b)(\eta\gamma)
for

\eta\in G_{u}
, then
\mathbf{y}_{b}
is an element of
\mathsf{W}_{\gamma}
with norm
bounded by
\|b\|_{r}
. Now Corollary
5.3
, applied
to
\flat(\mathbf{x}_{a})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{b}
, immediately implies that
(
5.15
) converges. Let
(f_{i})
and
(g_{i})
be sequences
in
\Gamma_{c}(G;\mathcal{B})
converging in
C^{*}_{r}(G;\mathcal{B})
to
a
and
b
, respectively.
Then by the above

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{f_{i}}-\mathbf{x}_{a}\|_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}=\|\mathbf{x}_{(f_{i}-a)}\|_{\mathsf{X}_{u}}\leq\|f_{i}-a\|_{r}.

Hence
(\mathbf{x}_{f_{i}})
converges to
\mathbf{x}_{a}
in
\mathsf{X}_{u}
.
Similarly,
(\mathbf{y}_{g_{i}})
converges to
\mathbf{y}_{b}
in
\mathsf{W}_{\gamma}
.
Thus

\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x}_{f_{i}})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{g_{i}})\to\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x}_{a})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{b})
. But

(5.21)

\displaystyle\Phi(\flat(\mathbf{x}_{f_{i}})\otimes\mathbf{y}_{g_{i}})=(f_{i}*g_{i})(\gamma)=j(f_{i}*g_{i})(\gamma).

This suffices since the continuity of
j
and multiplication
implies that
j(f_{i}*g_{i})(\gamma)
converges to
j(a*b)(\gamma)
.
∎

, now follows from

, Corollary

, and
Corollary
5.4

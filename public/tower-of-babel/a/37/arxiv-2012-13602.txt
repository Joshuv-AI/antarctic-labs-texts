Approximation of function by α − \alpha- Baskakov Durrmeyer type operators


Abstract
. In the present note, we give the generalization of α − \alpha- Baskakov Durrmeyer operators depending on a real parameter ρ > 0 . \rho>0. We present the approximation results in Korovkin and weighted Korovkin spaces. We also prove the order of approximation, rate of approximation for these operators. In the end, we verify our results with the help of numerical examples by using Mathematica. Keywords: Baskakov operators, Korovkin-type approximation theorem, Rate of convergence, Modulus of continuity. Mathematics Subject Classification(2020): 41A25, 41A35, 41A36, 26A15.

1.
Introduction

Weierstrass’s approximation theorem is the stepping stone of approximation theory, proved by Weierstrass in 1885. In 1912, S. N. Bernstein presented an another method to prove the theorem, which is quite simpler than the original proof. For this, he defined positive and linear operators (named after him) as Bernstein operators. For
f\in C[0,1],
the operators are defined as:

\mathcal{B}_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{n,k}(x)f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right),

where
p_{n,k}(x)=\displaystyle{n\choose k}x^{k}(1-x)^{n-k}.
These operators enjoy remarkable properties of approximation theory. Due to its useful properties, immense research has been initiated on these operators, which is still going on (one can see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
). We can also find its generalizations based on different parameters (see
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
21
]
). Despite having all these interesting properties, the operators are defined only on [0,1]. Then in 1950, Sz
\acute{a}
sz
[
22
]
extended it to the infinite interval by introducing Sz
\acute{a}
sz operators.

In 1957, Baskakov
[
5
]
acquainted a sequence of positive linear operators for
f\in C_{B}[0,\infty)
i.e. continuous bounded functions on
[0,\infty)
as:

\displaystyle L_{n}(f;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)l_{n,k}(x),\qquad n\geq 1,\qquad x\in[0,\infty)

(1.1)

where
l_{n,k}(x)=\displaystyle{n+k-1\choose k}\displaystyle\frac{x^{k}}{(1+x)^{n+k}}
.

Due to the extension of these operators on unbounded interval, it fetched the attention of many researchers
[

Recently, Aral and Erbay
[
2
]
introduced the generalization of classical Baskakov operators based on a real parameter
\alpha\in[0,1]
, as :

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\alpha}(f;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right).p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x),\qquad n\geq 1,\qquad x\in[0,\infty)

(1.2)

where
p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)
is given by:

\displaystyle p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{x^{k-1}}{(1+x)^{n+k-1}}\left\{\frac{{\alpha}x}{1+x}{n+k-1\choose k}-(1-\alpha)(1+x){n+k-3\choose k-2}+(1-\alpha)x{n+k-1\choose k}\right\}.

(1.3)

For
\alpha=1,
it comes back to the classical Baskakov operators.

The authors studied convergence, error approximation and Voronovskaja type result for these operators. They verified that the parameter
\alpha
does not affect the convergence but it effects the error of approximation for these operators.

Very recently, Nasiruzzaman et al.
[
18
]
defined the Durrmeyer variant of these operators to extend the results on Lebesgue integral functions, called
\alpha-
Baskakov Durrmeyer operators as:

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\alpha}(f;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right).p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{n,k}(t)f(t)\,dt,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\quad s_{n,k}(t)=\frac{t^{k}}{B(k+1,n)(1+t)^{n+k+1}}

and
B(m,n)
is a Beta function.
The authors studied the order of approximation, rate of convergence, Korovkin-type and weighted Korovkin-type approximation theorems for these operators.

In order to reduce the error of approximation of the linear positive operators, a great variety of research is going on its generalization which is based on different parameters (as

By motivated from these benefits of the generalizations of the positive linear operators, which reduces the error of approximation, we modify the operators (
1.4
) by using a parameter
\rho>0
in the next section.

2.
Construction of operators

We define the generalization of
\alpha-
Baskakov Durrmeyer operators based on a real parameter
\rho>0
as follows:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right).p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)f(t)\,dt,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\quad\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{k\rho}}{B(k\rho+1,n\rho)(1+t)^{n\rho+k\rho+1}}.

(2.2)

The following sections are organized as: In section 3, we give the moments, central moments and some preliminary results that we will use in subsequent sections. In section 4, we show main results for the operators (
2.1
) which are divided in two sub-sections. In the first sub-section, we prove basic convergence theorems in Korovkin and weighted Korovkin spaces and in the second sub-section, we study pointwise approximation properties of these operators. In section 5, we verify our theoretical results by numerical examples with the use of Mathematica.

Through out the paper, we use
e_{i}=x^{i},
for
i=0,1,2,\dotsb.

Basic Results

Lemma 1
.

The moments of the operators
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(.;x)
are given as:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{0};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1;

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{1};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho nx-2\rho(1-\alpha)x+1}{n\rho-1};

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{2};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\bigg[x^{2}\big\{{\rho}^{2}n^{2}+(4\alpha-3){\rho}^{2}n\big\}

\displaystyle+x\big\{{\rho}^{2}(n+4\alpha-4)+3\rho n-6\rho(1-\alpha)\big\}+2\bigg].

Lemma 2
.

The central moments of the operators
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(.;x)
are given as:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(t-x;x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\rho-1}\bigg[x\left\{1-2\rho(1-\alpha)\right\}+1\bigg];

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\bigg[x^{2}\big\{n\rho(\rho+1)-8\rho(1-\alpha)+2\big\}

\displaystyle+x\big\{n\rho(\rho+1)-4{\rho}^{2}(1-\alpha)-6\rho(1-\alpha)+4\big\}+2\bigg];

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{4};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rho}^{2}(1+\rho)^{2}x^{2}(1+x)^{2}-96(1-\alpha){\rho}^{3}x^{3}+24{\rho}^{3}x^{3}}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)(n\rho-3)(n\rho-4)}n^{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\right).

Now, we recall the definitions and the results that are useful in the subsequent sections.

Definition 1
.

(First order modulus of continuity)

Let
f(x)
be bounded on
[a,b],
the modulus of continuity of
f(x)
on
[a,b],
is denoted by
\omega(f;\delta),
and is defined for
\delta>0
as:

\displaystyle\omega(f;\delta)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y)\in[a,b]\\
|x-y|\leq\delta\end{subarray}}|f(x)-f(y)|,

with the following relation for
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\omega(f;\lambda\delta)\leq(1+\lambda)\,\omega(f;\delta).

(3.1)

Definition 2
.

(Lipschitz continuity)

A function
f
satisfies the Lipschitz condition of order
\tau
with the constant
L
on
[a,b],
i.e.

\displaystyle|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|\leq L\,|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\tau},\qquad 0<\tau\leq 1,\quad L>0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad x_{1},x_{2}\in[a,b],

iff
\omega(f;\delta)\leq L\,\delta^{\tau}.

Definition 3
.

(
K-
Functional)

Peetre
[
19
]
introduced the second order
K-
functional for
f\in C_{B}[0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle K_{2}(f;\delta)=\displaystyle\inf\{\|f-g\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}+\delta\|g^{\prime\prime}\|_{C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)};g\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)\},

where

C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty):=\{f\in C_{B}[0,\infty);f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime}\in C_{B}[0,\infty)\}.

In order to prove the uniform convergence of the positive linear operators to a continuous function, Korovkin has proved a simple theorem which is defined as:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(L_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive linear operators such that for every
f\in\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}L_{n}(f)=f\qquad\mbox{uniformly on}\qquad[a,b].

Then for a function
h\in C[a,b],
we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}L_{n}(h)=h\qquad\mbox{uniformly on}\qquad[a,b].

To get the uniform convergence on an unbounded interval, we define the following conditions:

Let
f\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)
with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}+\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\qquad\|f\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}|f(x)|.

\displaystyle\mbox{Define}\qquad D[0,\infty)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{f\in C_{B}[0,\infty):x\in[0,\infty)\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{1+x^{2}}<\infty\}.

Suppose
D_{2}[0,\infty):=\{f:|f(x)|\leq A_{f}(1+x^{2})\}

such that
A_{f}>0,
a constant number depends only on
f.

4.
Main results

4.1.
Approximation in Korovkin and weighted Korovkin spaces

Theorem 2
.

For every
f\in C[0,\infty)\cap D[0,\infty),
the operators (
2.1
) are uniformly convergent to
f
on each compact subset of
[0,A],
where
A\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

For the uniform convergence of the operators (
2.1
) to
f\in C[0,\infty)\cap D[0,\infty),
we need to prove:

A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{i};x)\rightarrow e_{i},
for
i=0,1,2.

From Lemma
1
, it is obvious that
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{0};x)\rightarrow e_{0},
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

For
i=1,
we can check from below:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{1};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n\rho x-2\rho(1-\alpha)x+1}{n\rho-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(x+\frac{x-2\rho(1-\alpha)x+1}{n\rho-1}\right)=x.

Now, for
i=2

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{2};x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\bigg[x^{2}\big\{{\rho}^{2}n^{2}+(4\alpha-3){\rho}^{2}n\big\}

\displaystyle+x\big\{{\rho}^{2}(n+4\alpha-4)+3\rho n-6\rho(1-\alpha)\big\}+2\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{2}+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\bigg[x^{2}\big\{3\rho n-2+(4\alpha-3){\rho}^{2}n\big\}

\displaystyle+x\big\{{\rho}^{2}(n+4\alpha-4)+3\rho n-6\rho(1-\alpha)\big\}+2\bigg]=x^{2}.

Hence, by using Theorem
1
, we get the uniform convergence of our operators.
∎

For the approximation in weighted Korovkin spaces, we define the spaces as:

C_{2}[0,\infty)=D_{2}[0,\infty)\cap C[0,\infty)

and

C_{2}^{*}[0,\infty)=\left\{f\in C_{2}[0,\infty):\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+x^{2}}<\infty\right\}
with
\|f\|_{2}=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+x^{2}}.

Theorem 3
.

For
f\in C_{2}^{*}[0,\infty),
the operators
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)
satisfy:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;.)-f\|_{2}=0.

Proof.

By weighted Korovkin thm, it is sufficient to prove:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{i};.)-e_{i}\|_{2}=0,\quad\mbox{ for}\quad i=0,1,2.

For
i=0
, using Lemma
1
, we have
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{0};x)=1.

Therefore, we have
\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{0};.)-e_{0}\|_{2}=0.

For
i=1,

\displaystyle\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{1};x)-e_{1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\left|\frac{n\rho x-2\rho(1-\alpha)x+1}{n\rho-1}-x\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{x}{1+x^{2}}\left|\frac{1-2\rho(1-\alpha)}{n\rho-1}\right|+\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n\rho-1}\right|.

Thus, we get:

\displaystyle\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{1};x)-e_{1}\|_{2}=0\,\mbox{as}\,\,n\rightarrow\infty.

Similarly, we can prove for
i=2
:

\displaystyle\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{2};x)-e_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{2};x)-x^{2}|}{1+x^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{x^{2}}{1+x^{2}}\left|\frac{{\rho}^{2}n^{2}+(4\alpha-3){\rho}^{2}n}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\right|

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{x}{1+x^{2}}\left|\frac{{\rho}^{2}(n+4\alpha-4)+3\rho n-6\rho(1-\alpha)}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\right|

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,\infty)}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\left|\frac{2}{(n\rho-1)(n\rho-2)}\right|,

which gives us
\|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(e_{2};x)-e_{2}\|_{2}=0\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,n\rightarrow\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Pointwise Approximation Properties by
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)

Theorem 4
.

Let
f\in C_{B}[0,\infty)
and
x\in[0,b]
such that
b>0,
then we have:

|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq 2\omega\left(f;\sqrt{A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)}\,\right).

Proof.

From Definition
1
, we have:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,f(t)\,dt\right)-f(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,|f(t)-f(x)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,\omega(f;|t-x|)\,dt.

By using the property of
\omega(f;\delta\lambda)\leq(1+\lambda)\,\omega(f;\delta)
with
\lambda=\dfrac{1}{\delta}|t-x|
, we get:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\,|t-x|\right)\omega(f;\delta)\,dt

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,\omega(f;\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,|t-x|\,\omega(f;\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(f;\delta)+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,|t-x|\,\omega(f;\delta)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,|t-x|\,dt\right)\omega(f;\delta).

Now, by using Holder’s inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,|t-x|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}\,dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\left[\because\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)\,dt=1\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\because\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)=1\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=[A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus (4.1) reduces to:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta}\sqrt{A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)}\right)\omega(f;\delta).

On choosing
\delta=\sqrt{A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)},
we obtain the desired result.
∎

Theorem 5
.

For every
f\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n\rho-1)\left[A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[1-2\rho(1-\alpha)x+1]f^{\prime}(x)+(\rho+1)x(x+1)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x)}{2}

uniformly for
0\leq x\leq b,
where
b>0.

Proof.

The Taylor’s polynomial for
f\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)
, i.e.

\displaystyle f(t)=f(x)+(t-x)f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{(t-x)^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x)+\epsilon(x,a)(t-x)^{2}\,\quad\mbox{where}\quad a\in[0,\infty)

(4.2)

having the conditions:

\epsilon(x,a)\in C_{B}[0,\infty)\,\mbox{and}\,\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow a}\epsilon(x,a)=0.

Now, applying
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(.;.)
and then multiplying with
(n\rho-1)
in both sides of (
4.2
):

\displaystyle(n\rho-1)(A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n\rho-1)\bigg[A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(t-x;x)f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{1}{2}A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)

(4.3)

\displaystyle+A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(\epsilon(x,a)(t-x)^{2};x)\bigg]{}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[x(1-2\rho(1-\alpha))+1\bigg]f^{\prime}(x)+\left[(\rho+1)x(x+1)+\frac{2(\rho+1)x(x+1)}{n\rho-2}\right.{}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{-8\rho(1-\alpha)x^{2}+2x^{2}-4{\rho}^{2}(1-\alpha)x-6\rho(1-\alpha)x+4x+2}{n\rho-2}\right]\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x)}{2}{}

\displaystyle+(n\rho-1)A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(\epsilon(x,a)(t-x)^{2};x).

(4.4)

By Cauchy-Schwartz inequality on the last term of above equation, we get:

\displaystyle(n\rho-1)A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(\epsilon(x,a)(t-x)^{2};x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n\rho-1)\sqrt{A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}({\epsilon}^{2}(x,a);x)}.\sqrt{A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{4};x)}.

Hence, by using Lemma
2
, we have:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(n\rho-1)A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(\epsilon(x,a)(t-x)^{2};x)=0.

Thus, by taking limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
on equation (
4.4
), we obtain the required result.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
f\in Lip_{\mathcal{M}}^{\gamma}
with
\mathcal{M}>0
and
0<\gamma\leq 1.
Then the operators
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(.;.)
satisfy

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq\mathcal{M}\left(A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

Proof.

By using the linearity of the operators
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)
, we obtain:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(|f(t)-f(x)|;x).

From Definition
2
, we have

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(\mathcal{M}|t-x|^{\gamma};x)=\mathcal{M}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)|t-x|^{\gamma}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)dt\right]^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\qquad\,\,[\mbox{By Holder's inequality with}\,\,p=\frac{2}{2-\gamma}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,q=\frac{2}{\gamma}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\right]^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n,k}^{\alpha}(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n,k}^{\rho}(t)(t-x)^{2}dt\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left(A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}.

Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 7
.

Let
f\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)
and
n\rho>2
, then:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq\left(\Gamma_{n}(x)+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{2}\right)\|f\|_{C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)}

where
\quad\Gamma_{n}(x)=A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x);x)
and
\quad\Delta_{n}(x)=A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x).

Proof.

The second order Taylor’s polynomial for
f\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)
and
\theta\in(x,t),
we have:

\displaystyle f(t)=f(x)+(t-x)f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{(t-x)^{2}}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\theta).

By applying the operators
A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(.;x)
on both sides, we get:

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)+A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(t-x;x)f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{1}{2}A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\theta);x)

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(t-x;x)|\|f^{\prime}\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}+\frac{1}{2}|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}((t-x)^{2};x)|\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}.

(4.5)

Now from equation (
3.2
), we obtain the following inequalities,

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}\leq\|f\|_{C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}\leq\|f\|_{C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)}.

Thus, by using these inequalities in equation (
4.2
), we get the required upper bound for the error of approximation.
∎

Theorem 8
.

For every
f\in C_{B}[0,\infty),
we have:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq 2Q\left\{{\omega}_{2}\left(f;\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n}(x)}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{4}}\right)+min\left(1,\frac{\Gamma_{n}(x)}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{4}\right)\|f\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}\right\},

where
Q>0
is any constant,
\Gamma_{n}(x)
and
\Delta_{n}(x)
are defined as in Theorem
7
.

Proof.

For
g\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)
, we have:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(g;x)+A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(g;x)-g(x)+g(x)-f(x)|

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f-g;x)|+|g(x)-f(x)|+|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(g;x)-g(x)|.

Since

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)\rightarrow f(x),\,\,\qquad\qquad A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(g;x)\rightarrow g(x),

then

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f-g;x)|\rightarrow|f(x)-g(x)|\leq\|f-g\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}.

(4.7)

By using equations (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and Theorem
7
, we get:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f-g\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}+\left(\Gamma_{n}(x)+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{2}\right)\|g\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\bigg(\|f-g\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}+\left(\frac{\Gamma_{n}(x)}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{4}\right)\|g\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}\bigg).

Now taking the infimum over all
g\in C_{B}^{2}[0,\infty)
and using the Definition
3
, we have:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq 2K_{2}\left(f;\frac{\Gamma_{n}(x)}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{4}\right).

Also, from
[
10
]
, using the equivalence relation of second order
K-
functional and second order modulus of continuity, i.e.

K_{2}(f;\delta)\leq Q\big({\omega}_{2}(f;\sqrt{\delta})+min(1,\delta)\|f\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}\big),\quad Q>0

Thus:

\displaystyle|A_{n}^{\alpha,\rho}(f;x)-f(x)|\leq 2Q\left\{{\omega}_{2}\left(f;\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{n}(x)}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{4}}\right)+min\left(1,\frac{\Gamma_{n}(x)}{2}+\frac{\Delta_{n}(x)}{4}\right)\|f\|_{C_{B}[0,\infty)}\right\}.

Rigid secondary characteristic classes Thanks: Version date: March 14, 2024
Steven Hurder

Abstract
. We construct families of non-trivial universal rigid secondary classes for foliations, and then discuss their application to prove that foliations are not homotopic. An observation of Lawson about the non-triviality of the normal Pontrjagin classes of foliations is extended, and then used to construct new families of examples of foliations with non-trivial rigid secondary classes. Examples are given of (abstractly constructed) foliations of compact manifolds with homotopic tangent bundles, but which are not homotopic as foliations.

1.
Introduction

A basic problem in foliation theory is whether a given smooth manifold
M
has at least one foliation of codimension
q
. The survey of foliation theory by Lawson
[
26
]
discusses this problem at length. An alternate formulation of this problem is to introduce the space

{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
of foliations of
M
with codimension
q
, with the topology induced from the Grassmannian space of subbundles of
TM
of codimension
q
, and consider whether the set
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
is non-empty, and if so, what are the topological properties of this space.

Lawson discusses various notions of equivalence between foliations of a manifold
M
in
[
26
, Section 5]
, and in particular that of homotopic foliations.
Given foliations
{\mathcal{F}}_{0},{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
we say they lie in the same smooth path component of
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
if there exists a smooth path
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
of foliations between them. That is, the collection
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\mid 0\leq t\leq 1\}
defines a smooth foliation of codimension
q+1
on
M\times[0,1]
. We then say that
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
are smoothly homotopic.

PROBLEM 1.1
.

Determine the set
\pi_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M))
of smooth path components of
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
.

When
M
has dimension
m
and
q=(m-1)
, so the leaves of
{\mathcal{F}}
are
1
-dimensional, then the set
\pi_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M))
equals the set of homotopy classes of
1
-dimensional subbundles of
TM
. On the other hand, when
1\leq q<m-1
, the space
{\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M)
is far more mysterious, and there is no direct approach to understanding its set of smooth path components. Also, in the definition of homotopy it becomes important whether the induced foliation on
M\times[0,1]
is smooth, or just continuous, as discussed by Rosenberg and Thurston
[
32
]
. In any case, if
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
are homotopic foliations, then their tangent and normal bundles are homotopic as subbundles of
TM
, and Lawson proposed:

PROBLEM 1.2
.

[
26
, Problem 3]

Show there foliations of a smooth manifold
M
of codimension
q
which have homotopic tangential distributions, but are not foliated homotopic.

In this work, we use the non-trivial rigid secondary classes to distinguish classes in
\pi_{0}^{\infty}({\mathcal{F}}_{q}(M))
for a variety of compact manifolds
M
, and give examples of solutions to Problem
1.2
.

Recall that the secondary characteristic classes of smooth foliations (see Section
3
) distinguish foliations up to some notion of equivalence, such as diffeomorphism or foliated concordance. Examples of Thurston
[
33
]
show that the Godbillon-Vey class for codimension-one foliations can vary non-trivially along a smooth path of foliations. The examples of Heitsch
[
13
]
show that the generalized Godbillon-Vey classes for codimension
q>1
also vary along smooth paths of foliations. The works of Heitsch in
[
11
,
12
]
and Gel’fand, Feĭgin, and Fuks in
[
7
]
, showed that this variation can be expressed in terms of a foliated cohomology space associated to
{\mathcal{F}}
.

In contrast, there are a subset of the secondary classes, called
rigid classes
, that depend only on the smooth homotopy class of the foliation. These classes are best understood in terms of the classifying spaces of foliations.
Let

.

Define the subspaces of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{q}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Image}\{\nu^{*}\colon H^{*}(B{\rm O}(q);{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{*}(B\Gamma_{q};{\mathbb{R}})\mid 4\leq*\leq q+2\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{q}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Image}\{\nu^{*}\colon H^{*}(B{\rm O}(q);{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{*}(B\Gamma_{q};{\mathbb{R}})\mid q+2<*\leq 2q\}\ .

Our first result is the construction of families of linearly independent secondary classes in

THEOREM 1.4
.

For

is non-trivial, and its dimension

DEFINITION 1.5

Let

(5)

H^{n}_{s}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})=\{\omega\in H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})\mid\exists\ z\in\pi_{n}(Y)~{\rm such\ that}~\langle\omega,h(z)\rangle\neq 0\}\ ,

where

In Section

THEOREM 1.6

For all even

2.
Pontrjagin classes

Let
B\Gamma_{q}^{+}
denote the classifying space for smooth foliations of codimension
q
with orientable normal bundles. There is a classifying map
\nu_{q}\colon B\Gamma_{q}^{+}\to B{\rm SO}(q)
of the normal bundle to the universal
\Gamma_{q}^{+}
-structure on
B\Gamma_{q}^{+}
.
In this section, we analyze the induced map
\nu_{q}^{*}\colon H^{*}(B{\rm SO}(q);{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(B\Gamma_{q}^{+};{\mathbb{Q}})

Recall from the definition of the homotopy fiber
B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
that there is a sequence of fibrations,

(6)

{\rm SO}(q)\cong\Omega B{\rm SO}(q)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\Gamma_{q}^{+}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu_{q}}}{{\longrightarrow}}B{\rm SO}(q)\ .

Some facts are known about each of these maps
\beta,\iota,\nu_{q}
, but in general their study presents many open problems.
The seminal observation was made by Bott around 1970. The cohomology ring
H^{*}(BO(q);{\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{R}}[p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}]
where
2k\leq q
, and
p_{j}
has graded degree
4j
.

THEOREM 2.1
.

[
4
]
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a codimension-
q
,
C^{2}
-foliation, and

The group

First, recall some elementary facts about

The key point is there is a fibration

{\rm SO}(q)\to{\rm SO}(q+1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{S}}^{q}

Let

For

For odd

Given a collection of non-negative integers
\vec{n}=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\}

p(\vec{n})=p_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots p_{k}^{n_{k}}\in H^{q+2|\vec{n}|}(B{\rm SO}(q(\vec{n}));{\mathbb{Q}})

Here is the main result of this section.

THEOREM 2.2

For

3.
Rigid Secondary classes

The survey by Lawson
[
27
]
gives a nice overview of the multiple approaches in the literature to defining the secondary classes of smooth foliations, by Bernshtein and Rozenfeld
[
2
,
3
]
, Bott and Haefliger
[
6
]
, and Kamber and Tondeur
[
23
,
24
]
. The author’s survey
[
19
]
discusses the various approaches to showing that these invariants are non-trivial for various constructions of foliations. In this section, we give the construction of examples of foliated manifolds with non-trivial secondary classes, especially the rigid secondary classes mentioned in Section
1
, using the results on the non-triviality of the Pontrjagin classes in Section
2
.

The normal bundle
Q
to a smooth-foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, when restricted to a leaf
L_{x}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
, has a natural flat connection
\nabla^{L_{x}}
defined by the leafwise parallel transport on
Q
restricted to
L_{x}
. An
adapted connection

\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
on
Q\to M
is a connection whose restrictions to leaves equals this natural flat connection. An adapted connection need not be flat over
M
. The connection data provided by
\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
can be thought of as a “linearization” of the normal structure to
{\mathcal{F}}
along the leaves. Thus,
\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
captures aspects of the data provided by the Haefliger groupoid
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{r}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
; it is a “partial linearization” of the nonlinear data which defines the homotopy type of
B\Gamma_{{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Denote by
I(\mathfrak{gl}(q,{\mathbb{R}}))
the graded ring of adjoint-invariant polynomials on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(q,{\mathbb{R}})
of the real general linear group
GL(q,{\mathbb{R}})
. As a ring,
I(\mathfrak{gl}(q,{\mathbb{R}}))\cong{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{q}]
is a polynomial algebra on
q
generators, where the
i^{th}
-Chern polynomial
c_{i}
has graded degree
2i
.
Associate to each generator
c_{i}
the closed
2i
-form
c_{i}(\Omega({\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}}))\in\Omega_{deR}^{2i}(M)
, so that one obtains the Chern homomorphism

\Delta^{{\mathcal{F}}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{q}]\to\Omega_{deR}^{ev}(M)
.
The Bott Vanishing Theorem holds at the level of the differential forms representing the characteristic classes of the normal bundle to a
C^{2}
-foliation, which is the basis for the construction of the secondary characteristic classes for
C^{2}
-foliations.

THEOREM 3.1

(Strong Bott Vanishing
[
6
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a codimension-
q
,
C^{2}
-foliation, and
\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
an adapted connected on the normal bundle
Q
. Then for any polynomial
c_{J}\in{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{q}]
of graded degree
deg(c_{J})>2q
, the Chern form
c_{J}(\Omega({\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}}))
is identically zero.

Thus, there is an induced map of DGA’s (differential graded algebras):

(23)

\Delta^{{\mathcal{F}}}\colon{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{q}]_{2q}\longrightarrow\Omega_{deR}^{ev}(M)\ ,

where
{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{q}]_{2q}
denotes the polynomial ring truncated in degrees greater than
2q
.

Now assume that the normal bundle to the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on
M
is trivial. The choice of a framing of
Q
, denoted by
s
, induces an isomorphism
Q\cong M\times{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
. Let
\nabla^{s}
denoted the connection for the trivial bundle, then the curvature forms of
\nabla^{s}
vanish identically. It follows that the transgression form
y_{i}=Tc_{i}(\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}},\nabla^{s})\in\Omega_{deR}^{2i-1}(M)
of
c_{i}
satisfies the equation

dy_{i}=c_{i}(\Omega({\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}}))
.
Consider the DGA

(24)

W_{q}=\Lambda(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{q})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}[c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{q}]_{2q}\ ,

where the differential is defined by
d(y_{i}\otimes 1)=1\otimes c_{i}
.

The data
({\mathcal{F}},s,\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}})
determine a map
\Delta^{{\mathcal{F}},s}\colon W_{q}\to\Omega^{*}(M)
of DGA’s.
The induced map in cohomology,
\displaystyle\Delta^{{\mathcal{F}},s}\colon H^{*}(W_{q})\to H^{*}(M)
, depends only on the homotopy class of the framing
s
and the framed concordance class of
{\mathcal{F}}
, and moreover, this construction is functorial.

There is a well-defined universal characteristic map

4.
Spherical classes

Given a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
of codimension
q
on a paracompact manifold
M
, there is a classifying map
\nu_{{\mathcal{F}}}\colon M\to B\Gamma_{q}
whose homotopy class is uniquely defined by
{\mathcal{F}}
. Conversely, a map
f\colon M\to B\Gamma_{q}
determines a
\Gamma_{q}
-structure on
M
, which is a foliated microbundle over
M
(see
[
9
,
27
]
). This motivates the interest in the set
[M,B\Gamma_{q}]
of homotopy classes of maps from
M
to
B\Gamma_{q}
, and similarly for the set
[M,B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}]
which classifies
\Gamma_{q}
-structures on
M
with framed normal bundles.
The following result from
[
15
, proof of Theorem 4.5]
is useful for the study of
[M,B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}]
.

PROPOSITION 4.1
.

Let
M
be a compact oriented
n
-manifold. Then there is an exact sequence of sets

(42)

[\Sigma M^{\prime},B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi_{n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho^{\prime}}}{{\longrightarrow}}[M,B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}]\ ,

where the image of
\beta^{\prime}
is contained in the torsion subgroup of
\pi_{n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
.

Here, the space
M^{\prime}
is the (n-1)-skeleton for a CW decomposition of
M
,
\rho\colon M\to M/M^{\prime}\cong{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
is the map collapsing
M^{\prime}
to a point,
\rho^{\prime}
is the induced map, and
\beta^{\prime}
is the map induced by the connecting map
\beta\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n}\to\Sigma M^{\prime}
for the Barratt-Puppe sequence of the attaching map
\alpha\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\to M^{\prime}
.
See the details of the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 4.5]
for further details.
Observe that a real valued invariant for
\pi_{n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
must vanish on the image of
\beta^{\prime}
, hence any element of
\pi_{n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
which is non-trivial for the invariant determines a non-trivial
\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
-structure on
M
.

The Rational Hurewicz Theorem is the basic result for the study of spherical cohomology:

THEOREM 4.2

(see
[
25
]
)
.

Let
Y
be a simply connected space with
\pi_{k}(Y)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=0
for
0\leq k\leq r
. Then the Hurewicz map induces an isomorphism

(43)

h\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\colon\pi_{k}(Y)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow H_{k}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})

for
1\leq k\leq 2r
, and a surjection for
k=2r+1
.

We next use Theorem
4.2
to show that
H^{n}_{s}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q};{\mathbb{R}})
is non-trivial for many degrees

Let

and

be the space constructed in the proof of Proposition

5.
Applications

In this section, we apply the conclusions of Theorem
1.6
to construct solutions of Problem
1.2
. The results of Theorem

are also applied in
[

]
to show that the homotopy groups

[

to construct group actions with non-trivial secondary invariants that are invariant under deformations of the group actions.

Let
M
be a compact
n
-manifold without boundary. Suppose that the tangent bundle admits a decomposition

classified by a map

\widetilde{\nu}_{Q}\colon M\to B\Gamma_{q}

[

that there is a codimension

[
30

31

We apply the Thurston Realization Theorem for the case when

is a trivial bundle, so the classifying map restricts to a map
\widetilde{\nu}_{Q}\colon M\to B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
.
There are two cases to consider, when

contains one class of degree

contains a trivial rank-4 bundle

where

is the 4-torus, and

is any compact oriented

-manifold. Another interesting example is

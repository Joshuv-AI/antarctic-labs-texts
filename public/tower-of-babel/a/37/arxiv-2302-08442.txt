Relieving nematic geometric frustration in the plane
Andrea Pedrini, Epifanio G Virga

Abstract
Frustration in nematic-ordered media (endowed with a director field) is treated in a purely geometric fashion in a flat, two-dimensional space. We recall the definition of quasi-uniform distortions and envision these as viable ways to relieve director fields prescribed on either a straight line or the unit circle. We prove that using a planar spiral is the only way to fill the whole plane with a quasi-uniform distortion. Apart from that, all relieving quasi-uniform distortions can at most be defined in a half-plane; however, in a generic sense, they are all asymptotically spirals.

Introduction

Nematic liquid crystals are perhaps the most typical example of a soft matter system whose order parameter is a unit vector
\bm{n}
enjoying the head-tail symmetry, which requires all their physical properties to be invariant under the transformation
\bm{n}\mapsto-\bm{n}
. Such an order parameter is often called a
director
to emphasize that only the direction of
\bm{n}
has physical significance, whereas in a
spin
also the sense of orientation is meaningful. On occasion, a director is said to generate a
line field
, as opposed to a vector field.

The microscopic origin of
\bm{n}
is to be retraced in the orientation of the elongated molecules that constitute these fascinating, ordered phases: on average, these molecules tend to be oriented alike, with equally likely distributions of heads and tails, whenever these can be distinguished from one another.

Properly speaking, the target of the director mapping
\bm{n}
is the real projective plane
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, although in a number of practical cases (but not all),
\bm{n}
is
orientable
, meaning that it can be lifted into the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}

[
1
]
.

Our approach here will be more general than our original liquid crystal motivation might suggest. The director field
\bm{n}
will have any possible interpretation compatible with nematic symmetry. Our focus will be on
geometric frustration
and ways of relieving it in two space dimensions. By geometric frustration we refer to any means capable of preventing a director field from filling space
uniformly
. Beyond its intuitive meaning, the latter property is properly defined by identifying scalar measures of distortion, which we call the
distortion characteristics
, and requiring them to be
constant
throughout space.

This notion was introduced in
[
2
]
in three-dimensional Euclidean space and further extended to the non-Euclidean case in
[
3
,
4
]
. In flat two-dimensional space, the request of uniformity confines
\bm{n}
to be a trivial constant field
[
5
]
, and the question then arises as to whether prescribing
\bm{n}
on a line in the plane, so as
not
to be constant there, induces a geometric frustration that can be relieved via a purely geometric mechanism, with no reference to any energy consideration.

The purely geometric mechanism we envision was introduced in
[
6
]
; it appears to be the most natural extension of the notion of uniform distortion, one for which the distortion characteristics instead of being constant are in constant ratios to one another, so that a single function of position in space is left free. We call these distortions
quasi-uniform
.

This paper explores the possibility that such an avenue can indeed be taken to relieve frustration in flat, two-dimensional space. The material presented here is organized as follows.

In section
2
, we recall the definition of distortion characteristics and their role in defining uniformity. Section
3
is concerned with the definition of quasi-uniform distortions and their representation in the plane. In section
4
, we show how to fill half a plane with a quasi-uniform distortion relieving a geometric frustration concentrated on a straight line. It will emerge there that some frustrations can be relieved globally, but others only locally. Incidentally, our analysis will prove that no genuine quasi-uniform distortion can exist in the whole plane. Geometric distortions enforced on a circle will be considered in section
5
. The possibility of relieving them in the whole plane outside the circle will be related to the topological charge of the frustration. In section
6
, we extract from the families of quasi-uniform distortions constructed in this paper some features that they have in common. The notion of frustration adopted in this paper admittedly bears a rather extended meaning; a more restricted one, which also entails a definition of
one-dimensional
uniformity is introduced and discussed in section
7
. Finally, in section
8
, we collect our main conclusions and comment on the general nature of the asymptotic distortion field relieving the two classes of geometric frustration considered in this work.

A number of appendices close this paper; there we display the mathematical details of our development that could have hampered our presentation in the main text.

Distortion characteristics

A natural measure of distortion for a director field
\bm{n}

\nabla\bm{n}

\nabla\bm{n}=-\bm{b}\tpr\bm{n}+\frac{1}{2}T\mathbf{W}(\bm{n})+\frac{1}{2}S\mathbf{P}(\bm{n})+\mathbf{D}.

3
Quasi-uniform distortions

A
quasi-uniform
distorsion
[
6
]
is represented by a nematic director field
\bm{n}
with distortion characteristics in a constant ratio to one another. More formally, either the distortion characteristics
(S,T,b_{1},b_{2},q)
are all zero, but one, or they are all proportional through constants to a nonzero scalar function
f=f(\bm{x})
of position
\bm{x}
in space,

S=S^{*}f,\quad b_{1}=b^{*}_{1}f,\quad b_{2}=b^{*}_{2}f,\quad T=T^{*}f,\quad q=q^{*}f,

(7)

with
S^{*}
,
b^{*}_{1}
,
b^{*}_{2}
,
T^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
and
q^{*}\geqslant 0
all constants. For a quasi-uniform distortion, decomposition (
1
) is therefore factorized by
f

and, by explicitly expanding
\nabla\bm{n}
in the distortion frame
(\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\bm{n})
, we give it the form

\eqalign{\nabla\bm{n}=f\Bigg[&\Bigg(\frac{S^{*}}{2}+q^{*}\Bigg)\bm{n}_{1}\otimes\bm{n}_{1}-\frac{T^{*}}{2}\bm{n}_{1}\otimes\bm{n}_{2}-b^{*}_{1}\bm{n}_{1}\otimes\bm{n}\\
&+\frac{T^{*}}{2}\bm{n}_{2}\otimes\bm{n}_{1}+\Bigg(\frac{S^{*}}{2}-q^{*}\Bigg)\bm{n}_{2}\otimes\bm{n}_{2}-b^{*}_{2}\bm{n}_{2}\otimes\bm{n}\Bigg].}

(8)

To ease our notation, we shall suppress the superscript
∗
wherever the distinction between distortion characteristics and their associated constants need not be specified; in such a case, the distortion characteristics will be denoted
(fS,fT,fb_{1},fb_{2},fq)
; otherwise, they will be denoted
(fS^{*},fT^{*},fb^{*}_{1},fb^{*}_{2},fq^{*})
.

In
[
6
]

(\bm{e}_{x},\bm{e}_{y},\bm{e}_{z})
as

\bm{n}=(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x\bm{e}_{x}+y\bm{e}_{y}+z\bm{e}_{z}),

(9)

2.

the

\bm{n}=(x^{2}+y^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(-y\bm{e}_{x}+x\bm{e}_{y})
,

3.

the

, and

(10)

3.1
Planar quasi-uniform distortions

Consider a planar, non-constant distortion for which

\bm{n}=\cos\varphi\bm{e}_{x}+\sin\varphi\bm{e}_{y},

(11)

where
\varphi=\varphi(x,y)
depends only on the
(x,y)
coordinates in the plane, so that
(\nabla\bm{n})\bm{e}_{z}\equiv\bm{0}
. Moreover, since
\bm{n}\cdot\bm{e}_{z}\equiv 0
, we also have that
(\nabla\bm{n})^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{e}_{z}\equiv\bm{0}
, and so
\curl\bm{n}\parallel\bm{e}_{z}
and

. By applying both sides of equation (
1

\mathbf{D}\bm{e}_{z}=-\frac{1}{2}S\bm{e}_{z},

(12)

which by (
2

\bm{n}_{2}\equiv\bm{e}_{z}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad S>0\quad\mathrm{or}\quad\bm{n}_{1}\equiv\bm{e}_{z}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad S<0

(13)

and, correspondingly,

S=\pm 2q.

(14)

In general, we say that a distortion is a
splay-bend
, whenever
T=0
and (
14
) applies. Thus, any planar distortion is a splay-bend, no matter whether quasi-uniform or not.

We now show that there is no non-trivial function
\varphi=\varphi(x)
of a single variable
x
that makes
\bm{n}
in (
11
) a
modulated
quasi-uniform field on the whole plane.
This result is obtained by direct inspection of the distortion characteristics. In fact, by calling
\varphi_{,x}
the derivative of
\varphi
, the gradient of the director becomes

\nabla\bm{n}=\varphi_{,x}(-\sin\varphi\bm{e}_{x}\tpr\bm{e}_{x}+\cos\varphi\bm{e}_{y}\tpr\bm{e}_{x})=\varphi_{,x}\bm{n}_{\perp}\tpr\bm{e}_{x},

(15)

with
\bm{n}_{\perp}:=\bm{e}_{z}\times\bm{n}=-\sin\varphi\bm{e}_{x}+\cos\varphi\bm{e}_{y}
.
Since the skew-symmetric part of
\nabla\bm{n}
is

\nabla\bm{n}_{\rm skw}=\frac{\varphi_{,x}\cos\varphi}{2}(\bm{e}_{y}\tpr\bm{e}_{x}-\bm{e}_{x}\tpr\bm{e}_{y}),

(16)

\curl\bm{n}=\varphi_{,x}\cos\varphi\bm{e}_{z}
and
\bm{b}=-\varphi_{,x}\cos\varphi\bm{n}_{\perp}
. Furthermore
S=-\varphi_{,x}\sin\varphi
and

\mathbf{D}=\frac{\varphi_{,x}\sin\varphi}{2}(\bm{e}_{z}\tpr\bm{e}_{z}-\bm{n}_{\perp}\tpr\bm{n}_{\perp}),

(17)

in agreement with (
14
).
As a result, the distortion characteristics
(fS^{\ast},fT^{\ast},fb_{1}^{\ast},fb_{2}^{\ast},fq^{\ast})
can be written by making use of one the following expressions:

\displaystyle f=-\frac{\varphi_{,x}\sin\varphi}{2},\ S^{*}=2,\ b^{*}_{1}=2\cot\varphi,\ b^{*}_{2}=T^{*}=0,\ q^{*}=1,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{if}\ \varphi_{,x}\sin\varphi\leqslant 0,

(18)

\displaystyle f=\frac{\varphi_{,x}\sin\varphi}{2},\ S^{*}=-2,\ b^{*}_{1}=T^{*}=0,\ b^{*}_{2}=-2\cot\varphi,\ q^{*}=1,\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{if}\ \varphi_{,x}\sin\varphi\geqslant 0,

(19)

and it is clear that
\bm{n}
is never quasi-uniform, unless
\varphi
is constant (which would make
\bm{n}
constant too).

We note that all planar elementary quasi-uniform distortions, ranging from pure bend to planar splay, have
defects
: in the plane, the origin is indeed a singular point; it extends to an entire line defect in the third dimention
z

1

The lack of modulated quasi-uniform distortions filling the whole plane and the presence of defects in the most elementary ones suggest that in our quest we should restrict the domain of
\bm{n}
and allow for the existence of defects. In the next two sections, we present the explicit construction of two families of planar quasi-uniform distortions: one is defined in a half-plane and prescribed on the boundary, the other includes fields winding outside a circle with prescribed topological charge.
These will identify two separate mechanisms for relieving frustration in the plane, from a line and from a circle, respectively. We shall see that not all frustrations can be relieved in this way; geometric criteria will be given that single out those that can.

In this section, we present a construction for a class of planar quasi-uniform distortions winding outside a circle upon which
\bm{n}
is prescribed. We consider the
(x,y)
-plane deprived of the region enclosed by the (unit) circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with center at the origin of a Cartesian frame
(\bm{e}_{x},\bm{e}_{y})
.

A uniform scaling would map
\mathbb{S}^{1}
onto a circle of any selected radius
r_{0}
with no qualitative consequences on our development.
We imagine
\bm{n}
prescribed on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with a given
topological charge

m
(also known as the

) and we ask whether it can be relieved quasi-uniformly outside
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Again by use of the method of characteristics, depending on the value of

or new families of planar quasi-uniform distortions defined outside
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, some filling the rest of the plane, others living only in a half-plane.

In the present geometric setting, we find it convenient to rewrite the nematic field (
11
) in the local frame
(\bm{e}_{r},\bm{e}_{\vartheta})
of polar coordinates
(r,\vartheta)
as

\bm{n}=\cos\alpha\bm{e}_{r}+\sin\alpha\bm{e}_{\vartheta},\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\alpha=\alpha(r,\vartheta),\quad\mathrm{for}\quad r>1,

(33)

so that the azimuthal angle
\varphi
between
\bm{n}
and
\bm{e}_{x}
is given by
\varphi=\alpha+\vartheta
, where
\vartheta\in[0,2\pi)
. Here
\alpha
represents the
local
orientation angle of
\bm{n}
relative to the radial direction
\bm{e}_{r}
.
For the gradient of
\bm{n}
we then have

\eqalign{\nabla\bm{n}&=-\alpha_{,r}\sin\alpha\bm{e}_{r}\tpr\bm{e}_{r}-\frac{1+\alpha_{,\vartheta}}{r}\sin\alpha\bm{e}_{r}\tpr\bm{e}_{\vartheta}\\
&\qquad+\alpha_{,r}\cos\alpha\bm{e}_{\vartheta}\tpr\bm{e}_{r}+\frac{1+\alpha_{,\vartheta}}{r}\cos\alpha\bm{e}_{\vartheta}\tpr\bm{e}_{\vartheta}}

(34)

and the distortion characteristics become (see
C.2
for details)

S=\frac{1+\alpha_{,\vartheta}}{r}\cos\alpha-\alpha_{,r}\sin\alpha,\ b_{1}=\alpha_{,r}\cos\alpha+\frac{1+\alpha_{,\vartheta}}{r}\sin\alpha,\ b_{2}=T=0,\ q=\frac{S}{2},

(35)

valid for
S\geqslant 0
. The natural choice for the factorizing function is then
f=q
, so that

S^{*}=2,\quad b^{*}_{2}=T^{*}=0,\quad q^{*}=1.

(36)

For a genuine quasi-uniform distortion we also require
f
not to vanish identically. It follows from (
35
) that if
f\neq 0
then

\frac{1+\alpha_{,\vartheta}}{r}\cos\alpha\neq\alpha_{,r}\sin\alpha

(37)

and

\quad b^{*}_{1}=2\frac{(1+\alpha_{,\vartheta})\sin\alpha+r\alpha_{,r}\cos\alpha}{(1+\alpha_{,\vartheta})\cos\alpha-r\alpha_{,r}\sin\alpha}=2\frac{(1+\alpha_{,\vartheta})\tan\alpha+r\alpha_{,r}}{1+\alpha_{,\vartheta}-r\alpha_{,r}\tan\alpha}.

(38)

Without loss of generality, we also assume that
b^{*}_{1}\geqslant 0
.

1

1

Were this not the case, we could take
(-\bm{n}_{1},-\bm{n}_{2},\bm{n})
as distorsion frame instead of
(\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\bm{n})
, thus recovering the case
b^{*}_{1}\geqslant 0
.

Quasi-uniformity is then achieved when
b^{*}_{1}=2b^{*}\geqslant 0
is constant, that is, when

r\alpha_{,r}(b^{*}\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha)-\alpha_{,\vartheta}(b^{*}\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha)=b^{*}\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha.

(39)

By using the method of characteristics, we parametrize
r
,
\vartheta
and
\alpha
in
t
and we follow the propagation of the values
r=r_{0}
,
\vartheta=\vartheta_{0}
, and
\alpha_{0}=\alpha(1,\vartheta_{0})
for

along the solutions of (

\frac{\operatorname{d}\!r}{\operatorname{d}\!t}=r(b^{*}\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha),\quad\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\vartheta}{\operatorname{d}\!t}=-(b^{*}\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha),\quad\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\alpha}{\operatorname{d}\!t}=b^{*}\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha.

(40)

In the following we focus on the main properties of the characteristic curves for (
40
): more details and full computations are collected in

By solving the nonparametric equations

\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\alpha}{\operatorname{d}\!\vartheta}=-1\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\frac{\operatorname{d}\!r}{\operatorname{d}\!\vartheta}=-\frac{r(b^{*}\sin\alpha+\cos\alpha)}{b^{*}\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha}

(41)

obtained from (
40
), we arrive at

\alpha=\varphi_{0}-\vartheta,\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\varphi_{0}(\vartheta_{0}):=\alpha_{0}+\vartheta_{0},\quad\alpha_{0}:=\alpha(1,\vartheta_{0})

(42)

and

r=R_{0}|b^{*}\cos(\varphi_{0}-\vartheta)-\sin(\varphi_{0}-\vartheta)|^{-1},\quad\mathrm{with}\quad R_{0}:=|b^{*}\cos\alpha_{0}-\sin\alpha_{0}|>0.

(43)

We note that both
\varphi=\alpha+\vartheta
and
R:=r|b^{*}\cos\alpha-\sin\alpha|
are constant along the characteristic curves. Therefore, by recalling that
x=r\cos\vartheta
and
y=r\sin\vartheta

|(b^{*}\cos\varphi_{0}-\sin\varphi_{0})x+(b^{*}\sin\varphi_{0}+\cos\varphi_{0})y|=R\\

(44)

is constant along a characteristic curve. Thus, any such curve is a straight line with slope

M_{\rm c}:=-\frac{b^{*}\cos\varphi_{0}-\sin\varphi_{0}}{b^{*}\sin\varphi_{0}+\cos\varphi_{0}}

(45)

through the point
(\cos\vartheta_{0},\sin\vartheta_{0})
of

7
One-dimensional uniformity

In this paper, the concept of frustration has been employed with quite an extensive meaning. Since uniformity for a director field
\bm{n}
in two space dimensions is equivalent to have
\bm{n}\equiv\bm{n}_{0}
, we regarded any director field prescribed on a curve in the plane so as to be incompatible with a constant extension to the whole plane as a source of frustration. One may wonder whether there is a sensible, restricted notion of frustration, which would further illuminate the role of quasi-uniform distortions as means of frustration relief.

Suppose that
\mathscr{C}
is a regular curve in the plane and that a director field
\bm{n}
is prescribed on it as a function of the arch-length parameter

for
\mathscr{C}
. Letting
\bm{e}_{z}
be a unit vector orthogonal to the plane that contains

, we define

as a unit vector field everywhere orthogonal to
\bm{n}
, so that

Elementary computations show that

Let now

8
Conclusions

Finding systematic ways for relieving frustration in an ordered medium endowed with a director field as order descriptor is a topic generally tackled in energetic terms. Here, we took instead a completely geometric approach to this problem, identifying quasi-uniformity as a viable notion of frustration relief. Our study was confined to two space dimensions in flat geometry.

Having first realized that, apart from
planar spirals
, no non-trivial quasi-uniform distortion exists in the whole plane, a conclusion that extends the negative results already known for planar uniform distortions
[
2
,
5
]
, we endeavoured to find planar quasi-uniform distortions in half a plane.

Two general settings were considered: in one, the director field was prescribed along a straight line, in the other it was prescribed on the unit circle. In both cases, we provided a method to construct quasi-uniform distortions obeying the prescribed frustration. We also identified the frustrations that can be relieved in a half-plane and those that cannot, thus turning our method into a selection criterion for relievable frustrations. To show how such a criterion works, we considered the case where a classical Frank’s disclination field is prescribed on the unit circle; we proved that such frustrations relax quasi-uniformly in a half-plane only if the topological charge is either
m=1
or
m=3/2
(the case
m=1/2
being trivially a planar spiral).

Quasi-uniform distortions in a half-plane are plenty, but a universal feature emerged which is common to all the quasi-uniform distortions that we constructed in this paper: away from the generating frustration, they all tend to become a planar spiral (being exactly so only in very selected cases).

A number of issues remained unresolved; we mention just two. First, we have not classified all possible quasi-uniform distortions in half a plane. Second, we have not attempted to characterize quasi-uniform distortions on curved surfaces. Accomplishing these tasks would possibly extend to the realm of quasi-uniformity in two space dimensions results already achieved for uniformity in dimensions three
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
and two
[
5
]
, respectively.

For ordinary nematic liquid crystals, the undistorted ground state coincides with a constant director field

: the molecular orientation is the same at each point occupied by the material, with no distortion.

The elastic free energy penalizes any distortion that would make

(61)

where each term corresponds to a specific elastic distortion and the nonnegative coefficients

K_{22}

K_{33}

,

, at most quadratic in
\nabla\bm{n}
, which enjoys the nematic symmetry (see, for example, Chapt. 3 of
[
16
]
).

By use of (

) and
denoting

,

takes the following equivalent form (see

W_{\mathrm{OF}}=\frac{1}{2}(K_{11}-K_{24})S^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(K_{22}-K_{24})T^{2}+\frac{1}{2}K_{33}B^{2}+K_{24}(2q^{2}),

(62)

which is semipositive definite whenever the elastic constants satisfy
Ericksen’s inequalities

[
17
]

K_{11}\geqslant K_{24}\geqslant 0,\quad K_{22}\geqslant K_{24}\geqslant 0,\quad K_{33}\geqslant 0.

(63)

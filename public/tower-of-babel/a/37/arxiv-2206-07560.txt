Sobolev-Orthogonal Systems with Tridiagonal Skew-Hermitian Differentiation Matrices
Arieh Iserles, Marcus Webb

Abstract
We introduce and develop a theory of orthogonality with respect to Sobolev inner products on the real line for sequences of functions with a tridiagonal, skew-Hermitian differentiation matrix. While a theory of such L 2 \mathrm{L}_{2} -orthogonal systems is well established, Sobolev orthogonality requires new concepts and their analysis. We characterise such systems completely as appropriately weighed Fourier transforms of orthogonal polynomials and present a number of illustrative examples, inclusive of a Sobolev-orthogonal system whose leading N N coefficients can be computed in 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ​ log ⁡ N ) {\cal O}\!\left(N\log N\right) operations.



Keywords
Orthogonal systems, Sobolev orthogonality, spectral methods, Malmquist–Takenaka functions

Mathematics Subject Classification
42C05, 42C10, 42C30, 65M12, 65M70

1
Introduction

1.1
Orthonormal systems on the real line

The theory of
\mathrm{L}_{2}
-orthonormal systems on the real line with a tridiagonal differentiation matrix has been developed in
[
iserles19oss
,
iserles20for
,
iserles21fco
,
Iserles21daf
]
. In its simplest (real) version, let
w\geq 0
be an absolutely continuous, nonzero weight function, symmetric with respect to the origin, and
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
the underlying system of orthonormal polynomials, which must satisfy

b_{n}p_{n+1}(\xi)=\xi p_{n}(\xi)-b_{n-1}p_{n-1}(\xi),\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

for some real numbers
\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
. Setting

\varphi_{n}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{n}(\xi)\sqrt{w(\xi)}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad x\in\mbox{\Bbb R},\quad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(1.1)

we obtain by Parseval’s theorem an orthonormal system of functions in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
. Moreover, under the mild assumption that polynomials are dense in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R};w)
, this system is dense in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
if the support of
w
is all of
ℝ
, otherwise its closure is the
Paley–Wiener space

\mathcal{PW}_{\mathrm{supp}\,w}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
of all
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
functions whose Fourier transform is supported on
\mathrm{supp}\,w
. Moreover,
\Phi=\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
obeys

\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(x)=-b_{n-1}\varphi_{n-1}(x)+b_{n}\varphi_{n+1}(x),\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

(1.2)

In vector form, (
1.2
) is
\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$\unboldmath}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$\unboldmath}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the
differentiation matrix
of the system, which in this case is tridiagonal and skew-symmetric. Skew symmetry and the tridiagonal form provide important advantages on the design of spectral methods with the basis
\Phi

[
iserles19oss
]
.

In this paper we generalise the theory to the case of Sobolev-orthogonal systems, where the Sobolev inner product is of the form

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{v}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}v_{\ell}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi^{(\ell)}(x)\overline{\psi^{(\ell)}(x)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x,

defined by the non-zero, non-negative sequence
\{v_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset[0,\infty)
. The
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
norm, where
s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
corresponds to
v_{\ell}=1
for
\ell=0,1\ldots,s
and
v_{\ell}=0
otherwise.

Besides the resulting theory being of interest in its own right, we can motivate our exploration in the context of spectral methods for PDEs using the example of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}-a\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(xu\right),\qquad x\in\mbox{\Bbb R},\quad t\geq 0,

(1.3)

with coefficient of friction described by the positive constant
a

[
daprato14sei
,
lawler06isp
]
. Solutions to this PDE satisfy

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u_{x}^{2}(x)+u^{2}(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}2u_{xx}^{2}(x)+\left(2+3a\right)u_{x}^{2}(x)+au^{2}(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}x,

(1.4)

which shows that the solution decays monotonically to zero in the
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
norm. In fact, we can drop some terms and show that
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\langle u,u\rangle_{H^{1}}\leq-a\langle u,u\rangle_{H^{1}}
, and hence the norm decreases at least exponentially with rate dependent on
a
.

Now, consider semi-discretising equation (
1.3
) in space by spectral method
u(x,t)\approx u_{N}(x,t):=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}(t)\varphi_{n}(x)
, where
\Phi=\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
are orthonormal with respect to the
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
inner product. If a Galerkin scheme is used with respect to the
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
inner product (i.e. the residual of the PDE at each time
t
is orthogonal to
\mathrm{span}\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
), then the inequality (
1.4
) is also satisfied by
u_{N}
(cf.
[
hesthaven2007spectral
, Ch. 8]
). It therefore follows that any A-stable discretisation in time will be stable.

The plan of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, basing ourselves upon our earlier theory on
\mathrm{L}_{2}
inner products, we present a complete framework for the construction of Sobolev-orthogonal systems on the real line with a tridiagonal differentiation matrix. This leads to two alternatives towards the construction of
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
-orthogonal systems, which are debated in Section 3: the first is the arguably more obvious approach, yet it leads to formulæ which typically are impossible to express explicitly, while the second, less natural, results in a more constructive approach. Section 4 is concerned with systems based upon the familiar Hermite weight and Section 5 with bilateral (i.e., symmetrised with respect to the origin) Laguerre weights. In Section 6 we discuss Bessel-like orthogonal systems originating in various ultraspherical weights: in that case the closure of the orthogonal system is not
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
but a relevant Paley–Wiener space. Section 7 generalises the discourse to non-symmetric measures. In that instance our orthogonal systems are complex-valued but the approach confers some important advantages. In particular, it allows us to generalise the Malmquist–Takenaka system to Sobolev setting while retaining the most welcome feature of this system, namely that the coefficients can be computed rapidly with Fast Fourier Transform. Finally, in Section 8 we present brief conclusions.

1.2
Sobolev norms beyond this paper

As an aside, our original interest in orthonormal systems (
1.1
) has been motivated in
[
iserles19oss
]
by the numerical solution of the linear Schrödinger equation in the semi-classical regime,

{\mathrm{i}}\varepsilon\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+V(x)u,\qquad x\in\mbox{\Bbb R},\quad t\geq 0,

given with an initial condition at
t=0,x\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
. Here
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, while the
interaction potential

V
is real. The solution of this equation conserves the standard
\mathrm{L}_{2}
norm (which motivates the use of
\mathrm{L}_{2}
-orthogonal systems), but it also has another important invariant: its Hamiltonian,

H(u)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[\varepsilon|u_{x}(x)|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}]\,\mathrm{d}x,

is conserved. This might be viewed as a conservation of a non-standard Sobolev norm (if
V
is positive). While the design of Hamiltonian methods for the Schrödinger equation is still an open problem, it motivates the work reported in this paper.

We mention in passing another example in which nonstandard Sobolev norms are non-increasing, the diffusion equation,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[a(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right],\qquad x\in\mbox{\Bbb R},\quad t\geq 0,

where
a(x)>a_{\mathrm{min}}>0
for all
x\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
, given with an initial condition for
t=0,x\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
. It is readily shown that norm induced by the following nonstandard Sobolev inner product is non-increasing as a function of time,

\langle u,u\rangle_{a}:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}[a(x)u_{x}^{2}(x)+u^{2}(x)]\,\mathrm{d}x.

We do not pursue these general Sobolev inner products in this paper, but anticipate reporting on such results in the future.

1.3
Related work

Sobolev orthogonality:
Polynomials orthogonal with respect to Sobolev measures have been considered for a long while but the subject received considerable impetus with the introduction of coherent pairs in
[
iserles91opo
]
and has been surveyed in
[
marcellan91ops
,
marcellan15oso
]
. Natural questions, given the constructs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are, firstly, how to generate Sobolev-orthogonal systems on the real line and, secondly, is a Fourier integral of an orthogonal polynomial system scaled by
any
reasonable function orthogonal with respect to
some
inner product, whether in a classical or Sobolev sense, in line with the
\mathrm{L}_{2}
theory as briefly reviewed in Section 2. These related questions are the focus of this paper. Intriguingly, as things stand, the theory in this paper is heavily based on the theory of classical orthogonal polynomials (as distinct from Sobolev-orthogonal polynomials).

Fourier–Bessel functions

[
diekema12diu
,
mantica06fbf
]
: Given a Borel measure
\,\mathrm{d}\mu
and the underlying orthonormal system
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
, we define

\varphi_{n}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{n}(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)

(1.5)

as the
n
th
Fourier–Bessel function:
the name is motivated by the Legendre measure
\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\chi_{(-1,1)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
, whereby
\varphi_{n}(x)=\sqrt{2\pi/x}\mathrm{J}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}(x)
. Note the similarity between (
1.1
) and (
1.5
) (disregarding the normalising factor and the sign in the exponential, neither of which is of much importance), namely that both are Fourier transforms of
p_{n}
with added scaling function:
\sqrt{w}
in the first instance,
w
in the second.

Further variation on this theme is the identity

\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{T}_{n}(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}=\pi{\mathrm{i}}^{n}\mathrm{J}_{n}(x),\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(1.6)

where
\mathrm{T}_{n}
is the
n
th Chebyshev polynomial
[
diekema12diu
]
. In that case the weight function has disappeared altogether in the Fourier integral. Note that, unlike (
1.1
), Fourier–Bessel functions need not be orthogonal although, interestingly enough, disregarding signs and normalising constants, the two formulæ concide (and orthogonality is recovered) for the Legendre measure.

2
Characterisation of Sobolev-orthogonal systems

Let us first state the desiderata. We are interested in functions
\Phi=\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
such that
both
of the following properties hold.

(A)

There exists sequences
\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset\mbox{\Bbb C}\setminus\{0\}
and
\{c_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset\mbox{\Bbb R}
such that

\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(x)=-\overline{b_{n-1}}\varphi_{n-1}(x)+\mathrm{i}c_{n}\varphi_{n}(x)+b_{n}\varphi_{n+1}(x)

(2.1)

for
n=0,1,\ldots
(with
b_{-1}=0
by convention);

(B)

\Phi
is an orthonormal sequence with respect to the Sobolev inner product

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{v}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}v_{\ell}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi^{(\ell)}(x)\overline{\psi^{(\ell)}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.2)

defined by the non-zero, non-negative sequence
\{v_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset[0,\infty)
such that
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}v_{\ell}>0
.

Theorem 1
(
[
iserles19oss
,
iserles20for
]
)

A sequence
\Phi=\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
satisfies criterion
(A)
if and only if

\varphi_{n}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{n}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}p_{n}(\xi)g(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

(2.3)

where

•

P=\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
is an orthonormal polynomial system on the real line with respect to a probability measure on the real line with all moments finite and with infinitely many points of increase;

•

\Theta=\{\theta_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}\subset[0,2\pi)
;

•

g\in\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
satisfies
\lim_{\xi\to\pm\infty}|\xi^{k}g(\xi)|=0\text{ for }k=0,1,2,\ldots
. We call such functions
mollifiers
.

Remark 2

It is possible to ensure that the paramters
\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
satisfy
b_{n}>0
without any genuine loss of generality. This is achieved by simply setting
\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{n}}={\mathrm{i}}^{n}
. We henceforth assume that
b_{n}>0
.

Remark 3

Under the assumption of Remark
2
, the functions
\Phi
are real if and only if
g(\xi)
has even real part and odd imaginary part, and
P
is orthonormal with respect to an even measure (i.e. it is symmetric about the axis). In this case,
b_{n}>0
and
c_{n}=0
for all
n
.

Theorem
1
and Remarks
2
and
3
were proved by the present authors in
[
iserles19oss
,
iserles20for
]
along with results characterising when such systems are orthogonal with respect to the standard inner product on
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
. The following Theorem generalises these orthogonality results to the Sobolev inner products in equation (
2.2
).

Theorem 4

Let
\varphi
satisfy criterion
(A)
, which implies that (
2.3
) holds. Then
\varphi
also satisfies criterion
(B)
if and only if the mollifier
g
satisfies

w(\xi)=v(\xi)|g(\xi)|^{2},

(2.4)

where
w(\xi)
is the positive weight function with respect to which the polynomials
P
are orthonormal, and
v(\xi)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}v_{\ell}\xi^{2\ell}
. In particular, it is necessary for the non-negative sequence
\{v_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
to decay sufficiently fast that
v(\xi)
is finite on the support of
w
.

Proof
By Parseval’s Theorem,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi_{m}(x)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=(-{\mathrm{i}})^{m-n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{n}(\xi)p_{m}(\xi)|g(\xi)|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Furthermore, since
\widehat{\varphi^{(\ell)}}(\xi)=(-{\mathrm{i}}\xi)^{\ell}\hat{\varphi}(\xi)
(where
\hat{\varphi}
denotes the Fourier transform of
\varphi
) we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi^{(\ell)}_{n}(x)\overline{\varphi^{(\ell)}_{m}(x)}\,\,\mathrm{d}x=(-{\mathrm{i}})^{m-n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{n}(\xi)p_{m}(\xi)\xi^{2\ell}|g(\xi)|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Therefore,

\langle\varphi_{n},\varphi_{m}\rangle_{v}=(-{\mathrm{i}})^{m-n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{n}(\xi)p_{m}(\xi)v(\xi)|g(\xi)|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi

This makes it clear that
\varphi
is orthonormal with respect to the Sovolev inner product if and only if
P
is orthonormal with respect to the measure
v(\xi)|g(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi
.

\Box

Remark 5

There are infinitely many choices of
g
which satisfy (
2.4
), namely

g(\xi)=\sqrt{\frac{w(\xi)}{v(\xi)}}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\vartheta(\xi)},

for any measurable real-valued function
\vartheta
. Our canonical choice is
\vartheta\equiv 0
, although we know of no good reason, except for simplicity, why this might be superior to other choices.

It is important to answer what space the resulting orthonormal system is dense in: ideally this is the inner product space

\mathrm{H}_{v}(\mbox{\Bbb R}):=\left\{\psi\in\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R}):\langle\psi,\psi\rangle_{v}<\infty\right\},

(2.5)

endowed with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
, but this need not be the case.

Theorem 6
(Orthogonal bases of Paley–Wiener spaces)

Let
\Phi=\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
satisfy the requirements of Theorem
4
with weight function
w(\xi)
such that polynomials are dense in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R};w(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi)
. Then
\Phi
forms a basis for the closure (in
\mathrm{H}_{v}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
) of the Paley–Wiener space
\mathcal{PW}_{\Omega}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
, where
\Omega
is the support of
w
.

A proof of Theorem
6
can be obtained by modifying Theorem 9 from
[
iserles19oss
]
. The key corollary is that for a basis
\Phi
satisfying the requirements of Theorem
4
to be complete in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
, it is necessary that the polynomial basis
P
is orthogonal with respect to a measure which is supported on the whole real line.

3
Sobolev cascades

In this section we derive two methods for producing orthonormal systems in the Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
where
s=0,1,2,\ldots
.

3.1
Cascades of first and second kind

For a weight function
w
and
s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
we can define the following two sequences of bases:

\varphi_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,p_{n}(\xi)\,\sqrt{\frac{w(\xi)}{\sum_{k=0}^{s}\xi^{2k}}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

(3.1)

where
P=\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
are orthonormal polynomials with respect to
w(\xi)
, and

\varphi_{n}^{[s]}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,p^{[s]}_{n}(\xi)\,\sqrt{w(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

(3.2)

where
P^{[s]}=\{p_{n}^{[s]}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
are orthonormal polynomials with respect to the weight

w^{[s]}(\xi)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s}\xi^{2k}\right)\!w(\xi)=\frac{1-\xi^{2(s+1)}}{1-\xi^{2}}w(\xi).

By the theory described in Section
2
, both systems
\Phi^{\langle s\rangle}=\{\varphi_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
and
\Phi^{[s]}=\{\varphi_{n}^{[s]}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
have skew-Hermitian tridiagonal differentiation matrices and both are orthonormal systems with respect to the standard
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
Sobolev inner product described in the introduction. Furthermore, all of these systems are bases for (closure of) the Paley–Wiener space
\mathcal{PW}_{\Omega}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
, where
\Omega
is the support of
w
.

We call the sequence
\Phi^{\langle 0\rangle},\Phi^{\langle 1\rangle},\Phi^{\langle 2\rangle}\ldots
a
Sobolev cascade of the first kind
for the weight function
w
, and
\Phi^{[0]},\Phi^{[1]},\Phi^{[2]}\ldots
a
Sobolev cascade of the second kind
for the weight function
w
. Note that
\varphi_{n}^{[0]}=\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}
.

While a cascade of the first kind is perhaps a more natural generalisation of
\mathrm{L}_{2}
-orthogonality, it is also more problematic. Typically the polynomials
p_{n}
might be already known, however the explicit form of the integrals (
3.1
), hence of the
\varphi_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}
s, is often unknown, even for
s=1
. The issue with cascades of the second kind is different: the polynomials
P^{[s]}
are usually unknown for
s\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
even for the most familiar measures like Legendre or Hermite. On the other hand, once we know
p_{n}^{[s]}
and can compute
\varphi_{n}^{[0]}
explicitly, the closed form of
\varphi_{n}^{[s]}
is available for all
n,s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
through the integral (
3.2
). Note that to compute (
1.1
) in a closed form we need to be able to integrate explicitly Fourier transforms of
p\sqrt{w}
for polynomials
p
: exactly the same is required for the computation of (
3.2
).

3.2
Sobolev cascades of the second kind

Orthogonal systems in a cascade of the second kind have a simple relationship.

Theorem 7

Let
s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
. There exists an infinite, lower triangular matrix
C^{[s]}
which has bandwidth
2s
, such that

\boldsymbol{\varphi^{[0]}}=C^{[s]}\boldsymbol{\varphi^{[s]}}.

(3.3)

Proof
Since
p_{n}^{[0]}
and
p_{n}^{[s]}
are polynomials of degree
n
(for every
n
), there exists an upper triangular
connection coefficient matrix

C^{[s]}
such that

p_{n}^{[0]}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\tilde{C}^{[s]}_{n,j}p_{j}^{[s]}.

(3.4)

Since
P^{[s]}
is an orthonormal basis with respect to the weight function
\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s}\xi^{2k}\right)w(\xi)
, we have the formula

\tilde{C}^{[s]}_{n,j}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{n}^{[0]}(\xi)p_{j}^{[s]}(\xi)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s}\xi^{2k}\right)\!w(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

(3.5)

Since
p_{j}^{[s]}(\xi)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s}\xi^{k}\right)
is a polynomial of degree at most
j+2s
, and
P^{[0]}
is orthonormal with respect to
w
, we have that
C^{[s]}_{n,j}=0
if
j\leq n-2s-1
, which proves the desired bandwidth of the matrix. The proof is completed by multiplying equation (
3.4
) by
\sqrt{w(\xi)}
and taking the inverse Fourier transform:

\varphi_{n}^{[0]}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}C^{[s]}_{n,j}\varphi_{j}^{[s]},\text{ where }C^{[s]}_{n,j}={\mathrm{i}}^{n-j}\tilde{C}^{[s]}_{n,j}.

(3.6)

\Box

Note further that if the weight function
w
is symmetric then all the polynomials
p_{n}^{[s]}
maintain the parity of
n
and it follows easily that
C_{n,j}^{[s]}=0
for
n+j
odd.

Theorem
7
has two consequences. Firstly, if one can calculate
\{\varphi_{0}^{[0]},\varphi_{1}^{[0]},\ldots,\varphi_{N}^{[0]}\}
, then it is possible to calculate
\{\varphi_{0}^{[s]},\varphi_{1}^{[s]},\ldots,\varphi_{N}^{[s]}\}
in
\mathcal{O}(N)
operations by applying forward substitution to the banded lower triangular system with matrix
C^{[s]}
.

Secondly, given
N+1
expansion coefficients in the basis
\Phi^{[0]}
, we can compute the equivalent expansion coefficients in the basis
\Phi^{[s]}
in
\mathcal{O}(N)
operations. Specifically, if

\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}^{[0]}\varphi_{n}^{[0]}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}^{[s]}\varphi_{n}^{[s]}(x),

then

{C^{[s]}}^{\top}\!\boldsymbol{a^{[s]}}=\boldsymbol{a^{[0]}},

(3.7)

which can be solved in
\mathcal{O}(N)
operations by back substitution.

In general, it appears that the nonzero entries of
C_{n,j}^{[s]}
obey no recognisable numerical relations: for example, the
6\times 6
principal minor of
C^{[1]}
for the Hermite weight is

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{\frac{5}{2}}&0&0&0&0\\
\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}&0&\sqrt{\frac{19}{6}}&0&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{\frac{3}{5}}&0&\sqrt{\frac{39}{10}}&0&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{\frac{18}{19}}&0&\sqrt{\frac{173}{38}}&0\\
0&0&0&\sqrt{\frac{819}{407}}&0&\sqrt{\frac{407}{78}}\end{array}\right]\!.

It is difficult to discern a pattern: numerical experiments for large values of
n
indicate that both
C_{n,n}^{[1]}
and
C_{n+2,n}^{[1]}
grow like
{\cal O}\!\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
.

All this does not rule out computing the
C^{[s]}_{n,j}
s numerically. Modifying a weight by a quadratic factor and computing the connection coefficients is discussed in
[
gautschi2004orthogonal
]
and
[
golub2009matrices
]
. We won’t go into the details of this in this paper.

4
Hermite-type systems

4.1
The Hermite–Sobolev cascade of the first kind

A natural starting point is the Hermite weight
w(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}}
,
\xi\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
, and
s=1
. The mollifier, by the definitions in Section
3
, is
g(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}}/(1+\xi^{2})^{1/2}
, so

\varphi_{n}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}(\xi)\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}}}{1+\xi^{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(4.1)

where

Lemma 8

For every
n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
we have
\varphi_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}
, where
\lambda_{n}
is an
n
th-degree polynomial.

Proof
It is enough to prove that

\sigma_{n}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi^{n}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}/2+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

is of the asserted form, i.e. an
n
th degree polynomial times
\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}
. This follows readily by induction on
n
from
\sigma_{0}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}
and
\sigma_{n}^{\prime}=\sigma_{n+1}
because, letting
\sigma_{n}(x)=\alpha_{n}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}
, we obtain
\alpha_{n+1}(x)=\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(x)-x\alpha_{n}(x)
.

\Box

Alternatively, substituting into (
2.1
), it is easy to see that

\lambda_{n}^{\prime}(x)=-b_{n-1}\lambda_{n-1}(x)+x\lambda_{n}(x)+b_{n}\lambda_{n+1}(x),\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

The proof that
\lambda_{n}
is an
n
th degree polynomial follows at once by induction on this differential recurrence, since
b_{n}>0
,
n\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
.

The bad news is that the
\lambda_{n}
s are not known and, as is trivial to verify, they do not obey a three-term recurrence relation (hence, by the Favard theorem, cannot be orthogonal with respect to any Borel measure). Having said so, the result is highly interesting. It has been proved in
[
iserles19oss
]
that there exists a unique
\mathrm{L}_{2}
-orthonormal system on the real line which obeys (
1.2
) and where each function is a polynomial multiple of the same
\mathrm{L}_{2}
function, specifically Hermite functions (or
\varphi_{n}^{[0]}=\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}
in present notation). The functions
\{\varphi_{n}^{[1]}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
, though, are
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-orthonormal, they obey (
1.2
) and
\varphi_{n}^{[1]}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}\lambda_{n}(x)
.

Lemma 9

The only
\mathrm{H}_{v}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
-orthonormal systems (see equation (
2.5
)) with a tridiagonal differentiation matrix which are of the form
\varphi_{n}(x)=G(x)\lambda_{n}(x)
,
n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
, for some function
G\in\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
,
G>0
(and
G(0)=1
without loss of generality), where each
\lambda_{n}
is a polynomial of degree
n
, correspond to

G(x)=\exp\left(-\gamma x^{2}+\delta x\right)

(4.2)

for some constants
\gamma>0
and
\delta\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
. The corresponding weight of orthonormality for
P
in Theorem
1
is

w(\xi)\propto v(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}/(2\gamma)}.

(4.3)

Proof
We substitute
\varphi_{n}(x)=G(x)\lambda_{n}(x)
into (
2.1
), bearing in mind that
G>0
, to obtain,

\lambda_{n}^{\prime}(x)=-b_{n-1}\lambda_{n-1}(x)+\left({\mathrm{i}}c_{n}-\frac{G^{\prime}(x)}{G(x)}\right)\lambda_{n}(x)+b_{n}\lambda_{n+1}(x),\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

Since
\deg\lambda_{m}=m
by assumption, we deduce, comparing degrees, that
G^{\prime}/G
is a linear polynomial, and hence that
G(x)
is the exponential of a quadratic polynomial. We can set the constant term in this quadratic to zero since
G(0)=1
without loss of generality, so we obtain equation (
4.2
).

Inverting the representation in Theorem
1
, we have

p_{n}(\xi)g(\xi)=\frac{(-{\mathrm{i}})^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{(-{\mathrm{i}})^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma x^{2}\xi+\delta x-{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The case
n=0
tells us that

g(\xi)\propto\exp(-(\xi-{\mathrm{i}}\delta)^{2}/(4\gamma)).

(4.4)

Theorem 2 tells us that for
\mathrm{H}_{v}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
orthonormality we require

w(\xi)=v(\xi)|g(\xi)|^{2},

(4.5)

which completes the proof of necessity of the forms of
G
and
w
.

Now we prove sufficiency. Let
w(\xi)=C^{2}v(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}/2\gamma}
where
C
ensures
w
has unit integral,
g(\xi)=C\mathrm{e}^{-(\xi-{\mathrm{i}}\delta)^{2}/(4\gamma)}
, and define
\Phi
as in Theorem
1
. By Theorem
4
,
\Phi
is an
\mathrm{H}_{v}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
-orthonormal system, so all that remains to prove is that
\varphi_{n}(x)=G(x)\lambda_{n}(x)
where
\lambda_{n}
is a polynomial of degree
n
. It is sufficient to show that
\rho_{n}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\xi^{n}g(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi
is
G(x)
times a polynomial of degree
n
, which can be readily shown by induction starting from
\rho_{0}(x)\propto G(x)
and leveraging
\rho_{n+1}(x)=-{\mathrm{i}}\rho_{n}^{\prime}(x)
.

\Box

4.3
An
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}(\mbox{\Bbb R})

Let
\sigma\in(0,1)

Theorem 10

The Hermite weight
w(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}}
,
x\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
, generates the
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
system

\varphi_{n}^{[\infty]}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sigma}}\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{\!n/2}\varphi_{n}^{[0]}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}\right)\!\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma x^{2}}{2(1-\sigma^{2})}\right)\!,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(4.7)

where
\varphi_{n}^{[0]}
is the standard
n
th Hermite function.

Proof
Let

\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{H}_{n}(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}(1+\sigma)\xi^{2}+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

whereby, orthonormalising Hermite polynomials, (
2.3
) yields
\varphi_{n}(x)=\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(x)/\sqrt{2^{n}n!\sqrt{\pi}}
. Using the standard generating function for Hermite polynomials,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(x)}{n!}t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{H}_{n}(\xi)}{n!}({\mathrm{i}}t)^{n}\right]\!\exp\!\left(-\frac{1+\sigma}{2}\xi^{2}+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi\right)\!\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\!\left(2{\mathrm{i}}\xi t+t^{2}-\frac{1+\sigma}{2}\xi^{2}+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi\right)\!\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sigma}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)}\right)\!\exp\!\left(-\frac{2xt}{1+\sigma}-\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}t^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sigma}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)}\right)\!\exp\!\left(-\frac{2x}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1\!-\!\sigma}{1\!+\!\sigma}}t\right)-\left(\sqrt{\frac{1\!-\!\sigma}{1\!+\!\sigma}}t\right)^{\!2}\right)

and, using the same generating function in the opposite direction,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(x)}{n!}t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sigma}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)}\right)\!\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\mathrm{H}_{n}\!\left(-\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}\right)\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}}t\right)^{\!n}.

Therefore

\tilde{\varphi}_{n}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{1+\sigma}}\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{\!n/2}\mathrm{H}_{n}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}\right)\!\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)}\right)\!.

Normalising,

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{[\infty]}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\sqrt{(1+\sigma)2^{n}n!\sqrt{\pi}}}\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{\!n/2}\mathrm{H}_{n}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}\right)\!\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+\sigma)}\right)\!,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\sigma}}\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{1+\sigma}\right)^{\!n/2}\varphi_{n}^{[0]}\!\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-\sigma^{2}}}\right)\!\exp\!\left(\frac{\sigma x^{2}}{2(1-\sigma^{2})}\right)\!,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

and (
4.7
) is true.

\Box

6.1
Transformation of Chebyshev polynomials

We rewrite (

\varphi_{n}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-1}^{1}\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{n}(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\xi}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}=(-1)^{n}\mathrm{J}_{n}(x),

where
\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{n}=\sqrt{2/\pi}\mathrm{T}_{n}
(except that
\tilde{\mathrm{T}}_{0}\equiv\mathrm{T}_{0}/\sqrt{\pi}
) are orthonormal Chebyshev polynomials. This does not fit within the framework of Theorem 2, because
v(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})^{-3/2}\chi_{(-1,1)}(\xi)
(derived using (
2.3
)) is not
\mathrm{L}_{2}
. Moreover, it is easy to verify directly that the
\varphi_{n}
s cannot be bounded in
any
Sobolev norm because the Weber–Schafheitlin formula
[
dlmf
, 10.22.57]
implies that for
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda>0

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{x^{\lambda}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{J}_{n}^{2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x}{x^{\lambda}}=\frac{\mathrm{\Gamma}(n+\frac{1}{2})\mathrm{\Gamma}(\lambda)}{2^{\lambda-1}\mathrm{\Gamma}^{2}(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+\frac{1}{2})\mathrm{\Gamma}(\frac{1}{2}\lambda+n+\frac{1}{2})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda\rightarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty.

If instead of a Chebyshev measure we use the Legendre measure,
w(\xi)=\chi_{(-1,1)}(\xi)
, the state of affairs is different:
g(\xi)=\chi_{(-1,1)}
results in

\varphi_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\frac{n+\frac{1}{2}}{x}}\mathrm{J}_{n+\frac{1}{2}}(x),\qquad x\in\mbox{\Bbb R},

(6.1)

and the
\varphi_{n}
s are integrable on
ℝ
.

The most obvious example of an

While

is orthonormal in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mbox{\Bbb R})

[-1,1]
, yet it might be of an independent interest. Perhaps more vexing issue is that above integrals are not available in an explicit form. This is not an insurmountable problem in the computation of the
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}
s which we can compute for individual values of

using a Fast Fourier Transform
[

6.3
Sobolev–Legendre cascades

We revisit the essence of Subsections 3.1.1–2, except that the range of integration is now
[-1,1]
. Firstly, we set

, let

be the (orthonormalised) Legendre polynomials and, for every

set

(6.2)

Secondly, we might define
w_{s}(\xi)=\chi_{(-1,1)}(\xi)\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\xi^{2\ell}
,
s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
, and set

\varphi_{n}^{[s]}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-1}^{1}p^{[s]}_{n}(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(6.3)

where
\{p_{n}^{[s]}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
is the orthonormal polynomial system corresponding to the weight
w_{s}
. It follows at once from Theorem 2 that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{\ell}\varphi_{m}^{\langle s\rangle}(x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x^{\ell}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{\ell}\varphi_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}(x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x^{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{\ell}\varphi_{m}^{[s]}(x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x^{\ell}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{\ell}\varphi_{n}^{[s]}(x)}{\,\mathrm{d}x^{\ell}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\delta_{m,n}

for all
m,n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
and both
\{\varphi_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
and
\{\varphi_{n}^{[s]}\}_{n\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
are orthonormal sets in
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
. Of course, neither is dense in the Sobolev space because their Fourier spectra are restricted to
[-1,1]
– they are dense in an obvious generalisation of Paley–Wiener spaces to the realm of Sobolev spaces. The systems (
6.2

and

\varphi_{1}^{[s]}
and
\varphi_{1}^{[0]}
, respectively. This follows from (
6.3
) because
p_{0}^{[s]}
is a constant, while
p_{1}^{[s]}
is a scalar multiple of
\xi
. Another obvious indication is that, as
s
grows,
\varphi_{n}^{[s]}
converges pointwise to a function
\varphi_{n}^{[\infty]}
yet this might be less interesting than it appears. In particular,

Lemma 11

\varphi_{n}^{[\infty]}\equiv 0
.

Proof
Let

u_{s}=\int_{-1}^{1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\xi^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

Then
p_{0}^{[0]}\equiv 1/\sqrt{u_{2}}
and, by (
6.3
),

\varphi_{0}^{[s]}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi u_{s}}}\frac{\sin x}{x}.

But

u_{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\frac{1}{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ell\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty

and the lemma follows.

\Box

Alternatively,
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}w^{[s]}(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})^{-1}\chi_{(-1,1)}\not\in\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
. An obvious remedy, which we do not pursue in this paper, is to consider the weight
w_{s}(\xi)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\sigma^{\ell}\xi^{2\ell}
for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
.

6.4
The Sobolev-ultraspherical cascade of the second kind

We construct a cascade of the second kind based on the ultraspherical weight
w^{[0]}(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})\chi_{(-1,1)}(\xi)
. Therefore

w^{[s]}(\xi)=w^{[0]}(\xi)\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\xi^{2\ell}=(1-\xi^{2s+2})\chi_{(-1,1)},\qquad s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}

and, as
s\rightarrow\infty
,
w^{[s]}
converges weakly to the Legendre weight.

7
Non-symmetric measures

The most obvious non-symmetric weight function is the Laguerre weight
w(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\xi)
. In that case the
\varphi_{n}
s are the
Malmquist–Takenaka functions,
which have a particularly neat form,

\varphi_{n}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\mathrm{i}}^{n}\frac{(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{n}}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{n+1}},\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(7.1)

[
iserles20for
]
, and they are dense in
\mathcal{PW}_{[0,\infty)}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
. It is possible to extend them to a system dense in all of
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
by melding them with another system, generated by the mirror image of the Laguerre weight,
\mathrm{e}^{\xi}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(\xi)
: together we obtain the same system as (
7.1
), except that now
n
ranges over all of
ℤ
.

It is, of course, perfectly possible for a system with a non-symmetric measure to be dense in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
, provided that the support of
w
is all of
ℝ
: an example is the Hermite-type weight
w(\xi)=(1-\xi)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}}
.

7.1
Shifted Hermite weight

Letting
\rho\in\mbox{\Bbb R}
, we consider the weight
w(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-(\xi-\rho)^{2}}
. The underlying orthonormal polynomials are
p_{n}(\xi)=\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}(\xi-\rho)
, where
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}
is the orthonormalised Hermite polynomial,
\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}=\mathrm{H}_{n}/\sqrt{2^{n}n!\sqrt{\pi}}
. Therefore, seeking
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
orthogonality,

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}(x,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}(\xi-\rho)\mathrm{e}^{-(\xi-\rho)^{2}/2+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\rho x}\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}(x,0),

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}(\xi-\rho)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\xi-\rho)^{2}/2+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}}{\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

It is easy to verify that

\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}(x,\rho)=\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\rho x}\varphi_{0}^{\langle 0\rangle}(x,0),\qquad x,\rho\in\mbox{\Bbb R}.

Thus,
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}=\varphi_{n}^{[0]}
is merely a complex-valued rotation of the standard Hermite function. The situation is more intriguing with regard to
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}
. Shifting the variable of integration,

\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x,\rho)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\rho x}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n}(\xi)\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi^{2}}}{1+(\sigma+\xi)^{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

On the face of it, we recover an expression similar to (
2.3
), except that
v(\xi)=1+(\sigma+\xi)^{2}
is not an even function and does not define a Sobolev inner product.

7.2
The Laguerre weight

7.2.1
Sobolev–Laguerre functions of first kind

Let
w(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\xi)
, a Laguerre weight. Thus, the
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 0\rangle}
s are Malmquist–Takenaka functions (
7.1
), which we need to complement with their ‘reflections’ for
n\in-\mbox{\Bbb N}
to form a system dense in
\mathrm{L}_{2}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
. By similar token, we need to complement
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}
s,
n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
, with functions generated with
w(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{\xi}\chi_{(-\infty,0]}(\xi)
(and indexed by
n\in-\mbox{\Bbb N}
) to attain completeness in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mbox{\Bbb R})
.

It is possible to compute individual
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}
s explicitly in terms of Bessel functions of the second kind (a.k.a. Weber functions)
\mathrm{Y}_{n}

[
dlmf
, 10.2.4]
and Struve functions
{\bf H}_{n}

[
dlmf
, 11.2.1]
. We first let

z=\frac{1}{2}-{\mathrm{i}}x,\qquad g(z)=\mathrm{Y}_{0}(z)-{\bf H}_{0}(z).

The functions
\varphi_{n}
can be represented explicitly as linear combinations of derivatives of the function
g
.

Lemma 12

The explicit form of the functions
\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}
is

\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x)=-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}{\mathrm{i}}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}{n\choose\ell}\frac{1}{\ell!}g^{(\ell)}\!\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-{\mathrm{i}}x\right)\!,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

(7.2)

while
\varphi_{-n-1}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x)=(-1)^{n+1}\overline{\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x)}
,
n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
.

Proof
Letting

\eta_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{n}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/2+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}}{\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+},

we compute the generating function

\displaystyle G(x,T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{n}(x)}{n!}T^{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\xi}{2}+T\xi+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi\right)\!\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}\left[\mathrm{Y}_{0}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-{\mathrm{i}}x-T\right)-{\bf H}_{0}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-{\mathrm{i}}x-T\right)\!\right]\!.

Therefore

\eta_{n}(x)=\frac{\partial^{n}G(x,T)}{\partial T^{n}}\,\rule[-8.0pt]{0.75pt}{22.0pt}_{\,T=0}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}g^{(n)}\!\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-{\mathrm{i}}x\right)\!,\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}.

Laguerre polynomials
\mathrm{L}_{n}
are orthonormal and

\mathrm{L}_{n}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^{\ell}{n\choose\ell}\frac{x^{\ell}}{\ell!},\qquad n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}

[
dlmf
, 18.5.12]
and it follows from Theorem 1 that

\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x)=\frac{{\mathrm{i}}^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{L}_{n}(\xi)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/2+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}}{\sqrt{1+\xi^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi={\mathrm{i}}^{n}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}(-1)^{\ell}{n\choose\ell}\frac{\eta_{\ell}(x)}{\ell!},

thereby proving (
7.2
) upon the substitution of the explicit form of
\eta_{\ell}
.

Extending this to
n\leq-1
is trivial.

\Box

Corollary 1

The functions
\varphi_{n}
for
n\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
have the generating function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x)}{n!}t^{n}=-\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\mathrm{i}}t)^{\ell}}{\ell!^{2}}g^{(\ell)}\!\!\left(\frac{1}{2}-{\mathrm{i}}x\right)\!.

(7.3)

The proof is elementary, using (
7.2
). Moreover, (
7.3
) can be easily extended to

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\!\frac{\varphi_{n}^{\langle 1\rangle}(x)}{|n|!}\zeta^{n}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}\!\left[\mathrm{e}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\zeta^{-1}}\!\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-{\mathrm{i}}\zeta^{-1})^{\ell}}{\ell!^{2}}g^{(\ell)}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\!+\!{\mathrm{i}}x\!\right)-\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\zeta}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\mathrm{i}}\zeta)^{\ell}}{\ell!^{2}}g^{(\ell)}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\!-\!{\mathrm{i}}x\!\right)\!\right]\!,

which makes sense for
|\zeta|=1
.

Since

\displaystyle z^{2}\mathrm{Y}_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z)+z\mathrm{Y}_{n}^{\prime}(z)+(z^{2}-n^{2})\mathrm{Y}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle z^{2}{\bf H}_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z)+z{\bf H}_{n}^{\prime}(z)+(z^{2}-n^{2}){\bf H}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{n+1}}{2^{n-1}\sqrt{\pi}\mathrm{\Gamma}(n+\frac{1}{2})}

[
dlmf
, 11.10.5 & 11.2.7]
, it follows that
g
obeys

zg^{\prime\prime}(z)+g^{\prime}(z)+zg(z)=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}\mathrm{\Gamma}(\frac{1}{2})}=-\frac{2}{\pi}

and we can express
g^{(\ell)}
as a linear combination of
g
and
g^{\prime}
with rational coefficients. We do not pursue further this course of action. Functions
\varphi_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}
for
s\geq 2
(or even the underlying orthogonal polynomials
p_{n}^{\langle s\rangle}
) are no longer available in an explicit form.

7.2.2
Sobolev–Laguerre functions of the second kind

An alternative is to consider the Sobolev–Laguerre cascade of the second kind. While the orthogonal polynomials
p_{n}^{[s]}
for the weight
w_{s}(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}\chi_{[0,\infty)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\xi_{\ell}^{2}
are unknown for
s\in\mbox{\Bbb N}
, the underlying moments are trivial to compute and such polynomials can be generated at will. Also the computation of the
\varphi_{n}^{[s]}
s does not present a problem: for example

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}^{[1]}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\pi}}\frac{1}{1-2{\mathrm{i}}x},

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{[1]}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{87\pi}}{\mathrm{i}}\left[-\frac{4}{1-2{\mathrm{i}}x}+\frac{3(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{2}}\right]\!,

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}^{[1]}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{16211\pi}}{\mathrm{i}}^{2}\left[\frac{34}{1-2{\mathrm{i}}x}-\frac{40(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{2}}+\frac{29(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{2}}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{3}}\right]\!,

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}^{[1]}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{9812127\pi}}{\mathrm{i}}^{3}\left[-\frac{480}{1-2{\mathrm{i}}x}+\frac{762(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{2}}-\frac{804(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{2}}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{3}}+\frac{559(1+2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{3}}{(1-2{\mathrm{i}}x)^{4}}\right]

and so on: all this seems very similar to (
7.1
) and for a good reason: for any
s\in\mbox{\Bbb Z}_{+}
we can expand the relevant orthonormal polynomials in the Laguerre basis,

p_{n}^{[s]}(x)=\sum_{j={0}}^{n}\gamma_{n,j}^{[s]}\mathrm{L}_{j}(x)

(cf. (
3.5
)), whereby it follows from (
3.2
) that

\varphi_{n}^{[s]}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{n,j}^{[s]}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{L}_{j}(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{-\xi/2+{\mathrm{i}}x\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{n,j}^{[s]}\varphi_{j}^{[0]}(x).

Note that the matrix
\{\gamma_{n,j}^{[s]}\}_{n,j\in\mbox{\sBbb Z}_{+}}
is the inverse of the banded connection matrix
{C^{[s]}}^{\top}
from Theorem
7
. A similar construction applies also to
\varphi_{n}^{[s]}
for
n\leq-1
.

The most remarkable feature of the Malmquist–Takenaka system is that the expansion coefficients
\hat{f}^{[0]}_{n}=\langle f,\varphi_{n}^{[0]}\rangle
can be computed for
-N+1\leq n\leq N
in
{\cal O}\!\left(N\log N\right)
operations using the Fast Fourier Transform. By (
3.7
), however, once
\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$f$\unboldmath}}^{[0]}
is known and assuming that the requisite derivatives of
f
are available, it costs just
{\cal O}\!\left(N\right)
operations to compute

f_{n}^{[s]}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f^{(\ell)}(x)\overline{{\varphi_{n}^{[s]}}^{(\ell)}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad-N+1\leq n\leq N.

(The derivatives of
\varphi_{n}^{[s]}
s can be computed similarly to the functions themselves,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$\unboldmath}_{n}^{[s]}}^{(\ell)}={C^{[s]}}^{-\top}{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$\unboldmath}^{[0]}}^{(\ell)}
.) Altogether, the cost scales as
{\cal O}\!\left(sN\log N\right)
.

8
Conclusion

In a sequence of previous papers
[
iserles19oss
,
iserles20for
,
iserles21fco
,
Iserles21daf
]
the current authors have sketched different aspects of an overarching theory of
\mathrm{L}_{2}
-orthonormal systems on the real line with a tridiagonal differentiation matrix. In this paper we extend the framework to orthogonality with respect to Sobolev spaces. Unlike in the case of orthogonal polynomials, where Sobolev orthogonality is of a completely different flavour to orthogonality with respect to a Borel measure
[
iserles91opo
,
marcellan91ops
,
marcellan15oso
]
, in our case we can leverage many elements of the “
\mathrm{L}_{2}
theory” to a Sobolev setting: the connection to standard orthogonal polynomials via a weighed Fourier transform, density in Paley–Wiener spaces and fast computation of certain expansions. Other aspects of the theory are new, in particular the existence of two cascades, the latter of which can be ascended by banded triangular connection coefficients.

The work of this paper is a stepping stone toward the development of spectral methods on the real line that respect a wide range of invariants that can be expressed as conservation of a variable-weight Sobolev norm: a couple of examples have been given in Section 1. We expect to return to this issue in a forthcoming paper.

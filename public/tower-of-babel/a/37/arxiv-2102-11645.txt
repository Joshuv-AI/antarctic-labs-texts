Dyadic Martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces: Embeddings and Duality
Justice Sam Bansah and Benoît F. Sehba

Abstract
. We present in this paper some embeddings of various dyadic martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces H p , q S , H p , q s , H p , q ∗ , 𝒬 p , q H^{S}_{p,q},\,\,H^{s}_{p,q},\,\,H^{*}_{p,q},\,\,\mathcal{Q}_{p,q} and 𝒫 p , q \mathcal{P}_{p,q} of the real line. In the same settings, we characterize the dual of H p , q s H^{s}_{p,q} for large p p and q q . We also introduce a Garsia-type space 𝒢 p , q \mathcal{G}_{p,q} and characterize its dual space.

1.
Introduction

Our setting is a quadruplet
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}\}_{n\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
where
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is the sigma algebra generated by all dyadic intervals of
\mathbb{R}

and the characterization of their dual spaces for large exponents. We also define a natural extension of the Garsia space to the Wiener amalgam setting for which we characterize the dual space.

Martingale inequalities have proven to be very useful in various applications. For instance, the justification of martingale convergence theorems for both forward and backward convergences have been established by applying classical martingale inequalities (see
[
9
]
). In Fourier analysis, we have the involvement of martingale inequalities in the establishment of the boundedness of the maximal Fejér operator (see
[

). Martingale inequalities also play some important roles in the study of properties of Brownian motions (see for example
[
4
]
).

In the past few decades, there have been various studies about the embeddings of classical martingale Hardy spaces. Some of these discussions can be found in
[
6
,
13
,
14
]
. There are many other important inequalities involving martingales that have been proved and applied in the literature (see for example

We recall (see

In this paper, we extend the embeddings of the classical martingale Hardy spaces, (see

2.
Notations and Basic Definitions

In this section, we introduce the necessary definitions and recall the various martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces we shall consider in this paper. We will also state some important classical results, such as Doob’s inequality and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality that we will need later in this work.

Let
\mathbb{R}
be the set of real numbers and consider the following dyadic intervals of
\mathbb{R};

I_{n,k}=\left[\frac{k}{2^{n}},\frac{k+1}{2^{n}}\right)\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}=\{I_{n,k},\,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{n})
be the
\sigma-
algebra generated by
\mathcal{D}_{n}.
Then
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a (dyadic) filtration.
With this, we define the probability space as

\mathbf{Ps}:=(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\mathbb{P})

where
\mathbb{P}
is the probability measure and
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus all the martingales defined in this paper are with respect to this probability space with the underlying filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.
Let
J_{k,n,j}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
be the dyadic interval defined as

J_{k,n,j}=\left[\frac{k+j2^{n}}{2^{n}},\frac{k+1+j2^{n}}{2^{n}}\right).

Then

(1)

\displaystyle A_{j}=[j,j+1)=\bigcup_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}J_{k,n,j}

Therefore,
A_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all
n.

Note that the
A_{j}
’s are dyadic intervals. Also,
A_{i}\cap A_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
and
\bigcup_{j}A_{j}=\mathbb{R}.

Let
L_{p}
denote the classical Lebesgue space and let
\ell_{q}
denote the sequence space. For
f\in L_{p}
, we will be using the notation

\|f\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{p}d\mathbb{P}\right)^{1/p}.

The amalgam space of
L_{p}
and
\ell_{q}
is defined as the space

L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{f:\sum_{j}\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}<\infty\right\}

equipped with the (quasi)-norm

\|f\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for
0<p,q<\infty
and

\|f\|_{L_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for
0<p<\infty
. It is interesting to note that
L_{p,p}(\mathbb{R})=L_{p}(\mathbb{R}).
We refer the interested reader to (
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
]
) for more on amalgam spaces.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the spaces of all martingales defined on
\mathbf{Ps}
relative to the underlying filtration
\mathcal{F}_{n}.
For
f=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{M},
we define the martingale difference as
d_{n}f=f_{n}-f_{n-1}
and we will agree that
f_{0}=0
and
d_{0}f=0.
Let
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denote the usual
L_{p}-
norm for
0<p\leq\infty.
Then
f=(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{M}
is said to be
L_{p}
bounded if

\|f\|_{p}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|_{p}<\infty

and we define the maximal function,
f^{*},
or
M(f)
of
f
as

M(f)=f^{*}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}|.

Let
\mathbb{E}
and
\mathbb{E}_{n}
be the expectation and the conditional expectation operators respectively. Then the following measurable functions are well defined (see for example
[
4
]
);

S(f)=\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{n}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad s(f)=\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and we shall agree that

S_{n}(f)=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}|d_{i}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{n}(f)=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{i-1}|d_{i}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
\rho
be the space of all sequences
\varrho=(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of adapted (that is for all
n\in\mathbb{Z},\,\,\varrho_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-measurable), non-decreasing, non-negative functions and define

\varrho_{\infty}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho_{n}.

We are now in the position to define the martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces. These spaces were originally introduce in
[
1
]
. Let
0<p,q\leq\infty
. Then

i.

H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of all
f\in\mathcal{M}
such that
S(f)\in L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
with (quasi)-norm

\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|S(f)\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

ii.

H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of all
f\in\mathcal{M}
such that
s(f)\in L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
with (quasi)-norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|s(f)\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

iii.

H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of all
f\in\mathcal{M}
such that
f^{*}\in L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
with (quasi)-norm

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|f^{*}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

iv.

\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of all
f\in\mathcal{M}
for which there is a
sequence of functions
\varrho=(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}\in\rho
such that
S_{n}(f)\leq\varrho_{n-1}
and
\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty
with (quasi)-norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}:=\inf_{\varrho\in\rho}\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

v.

\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of all
f\in\mathcal{M}
for which there is a
sequence of functions
\varrho=(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}\in\rho
such that
|f_{n}|\leq\varrho_{n-1}
and
\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty
with (quasi)-norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}:=\inf_{\varrho\in\rho}\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

We also introduce here the spaces
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{BD}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
which we shall refer to as variation integrable space and Jump bounded space respectively.

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{M}:\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|d_{n}f|\in L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\right\}

endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|d_{n}f|\right\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}

for
1\leq p\leq q<\infty
and

\mathcal{BD}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{M}:\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{n}f|\in L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\right\}

endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{n}f|\right\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}

for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty.

For the sake of presentation, we sometimes write
H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),

\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\mathcal{BD}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
as
H^{S}_{p,q},H^{s}_{p,q},H^{*}_{p,q}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{p,q},\mathcal{P}_{p,q},\mathcal{G}_{p,q},\mathcal{BD}_{p,q}
respectively and
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}
as
\|\cdot\|_{p,q}.

We say that the stochastic basis,
\mathcal{F}_{n},
is regular if there exists
R>0
such that
f_{n}\leq Rf_{n-1}.

Presentation of Results

In this section, we present the main results of this paper. We start with the presentation of the inequalities that relate the first five spaces defined above. To be specific, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Martingale Embeddings)
.

Let
0<q\leq\infty
. Then

(i)

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p\leq 2).

(ii)

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(2\leq p<\infty).

(iii)

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p<\infty).

(iv)

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p<\infty).

(v)

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p<\infty).

Moreover, if
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is regular, then

,

and

4.
Martingale Embeddings

In this section we will discuss the various inclusions of the martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces
H^{S}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\,\,H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\,\,H^{*}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\,\,\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
as described in Theorem
3.1
above. Ferenc (
[
14
]
) has discussed the classical cases including the Doob’s maximal inequality for
p>1
and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality. We recall here that the disjoint cover
(A_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is such that
A_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and all
n\geq 1
.

We refer to (
[
14

) for the following classical result.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{M}
, the following hold.

(i)

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p\leq 2)

(ii)

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p}}(\mathbb{R})\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(2\leq p<\infty)

(iii)

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p<\infty)

(iv)

\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p<\infty)

(v)

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\qquad(0<p<\infty)

Moreover, if the
(\mathcal{F})_{n\geq 0}
is regular, the above five spaces are equivalent.

We observe the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
A\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Then if
f\in\mathcal{M}
, then
f\mathbf{1}_{A}=(f_{n}\mathbf{1}_{A})_{n\geq 0}
is also a martingale in

. Moreover, if

,
S
and

T(f\mathbf{1}_{A})=T(f)\mathbf{1}_{A}.

Combining the above lemma with (

), we obtain the following.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
A\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Then for any martingale
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
0<p<\infty
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left(|f_{n}|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A}\right)\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left((f^{*})^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A}\right)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\left(|S_{n}(f)^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A}\right)\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left(S(f)^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A}\right).

With the help of Lemma
4.2
and Lemma

we now present the proof to the martingale inequalities that relate the five sets.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
T
be any of the operators
s
,
S
and
M
, and
H_{p}^{T}(\mathbb{R}),H_{p,q}^{T}(\mathbb{R})
the corresponding martingale spaces, then since

for all

and all

, by Lemma
4.2
, we have that for any martingale

and any

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}T(f)^{p}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}d\mathbb{P}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}T(f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}})^{p}d\mathbb{P}=\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{H_{p}^{T}(\mathbb{R})}^{p},

and consequently,

(3)

\|T(f)\|_{H_{p,q}^{T}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}=\sum_{j}\|T(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}=\sum_{j}\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{H_{p}^{T}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}.

The two first assertions of the theorem then follow from (
3
) and Proposition
4.1
.

To obtain the other assertions, following (
3
) and Proposition
4.1
, we only need to prove that

(4)

\sum_{j}\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}

and

(5)

\sum_{j}\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}.

We only prove (
4
) as (
5
) follows similarly.

Let
(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be an arbitrary nonnegative nondecreasing adapted sequence such that

S_{n}(f)\leq\varrho_{n-1},\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

We have that the sequence
(\gamma_{n}^{j})_{n\geq 0}=(\varrho_{n}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}})_{n\geq 0}
is also nonnegative nondecreasing and adapted, and

S_{n}(f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}})=S_{n}(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\leq\varrho_{n-1}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}=\gamma_{n-1}^{j}

and

\|\gamma_{\infty}^{j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\varrho_{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

It follows that

\sum_{j}\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq\sum_{j}\|\gamma_{\infty}^{j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}=\sum_{j}\|\varrho_{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}=\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}.

As the sequence
(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
was chosen arbitrarily, we conclude that

\sum_{j}\|f\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq\inf_{\varrho\in\rho}\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}.

Let us now assume that the
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is regular. To prove the equivalence between the five spaces, we only need to prove that

(6)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{H_{p,q}^{S}(\mathbb{R})}

and

(7)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|f\|_{H_{p,q}^{*}(\mathbb{R})}.

We only prove the (
6
) since the proof of (
7
) use similar arguments.

Let
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a martingale in
H_{p,q}^{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Then using the definition of the regularity, one obtain that

(8)

S_{n}(f)\leq\left[C_{p}\left(S_{n-1}^{p}(f)+\mathbb{E}_{n-1}(S_{n}^{p}(f))\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

(see
[
14
, p. 39]
).
Define the sequence
\varrho=(\varrho_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by

\varrho_{n}=\left[C_{p}\left(S_{n}^{p}(f)+\mathbb{E}_{n}(S_{n+1}^{p}(f))\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then
\varrho\in\rho
and by (
8
),

S_{n}(f)\leq\varrho_{n-1}.

Also, we have that

\varrho_{\infty}=\sup_{n}\varrho_{n}=\left[C_{p}\left(S^{p}(f)+\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{n}(S_{n+1}^{p}(f))\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then using Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we obtain for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|\varrho_{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 3C_{p}\|S^{p}(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Hence

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|\varrho_{\infty}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|S(f)\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|_{H_{p,q}^{S}(\mathbb{R})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We finish this section with the proofs of martingale inequalities, the generalization of Doob’s inequality and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality. For this, we recall the following classical Doob’s inequality, that can be found in
[
14
]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p>1.
For every non-negative
L_{p}-
bounded submartingale
(f_{n},\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}),
we have that

\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

We start with the proof of the extension of Doob’s inequality.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
A_{j}
be defined as equation (
1
). Let
g_{n,j}=f_{n}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}.
Since
g_{n,j}
is a martingale, Proposition
4.4
implies that

\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n,j}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n,j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Therefore by definition, and for
0<q<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{p}^{q}=\sum_{j}\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n,j}\right\|_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{q}\sum_{j}\left(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n,j}\|_{p}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{q}\sum_{j}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|g_{n,j}\|_{p}^{q}=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{q}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j}\|g_{n,j}\|_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{q}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j}\|f_{n}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}

Thus

\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f_{n}\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{q}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j}\|f_{n}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}=\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{q}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|f\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}^{q}.

The case
q=\infty
follows similarly. The proof is complete.
∎

We also obtain the proof of the extension of Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality below.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The proof follows from Lemma
4.2
and the classical Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality (see
[
14
, Theorem 2.12]
). We note that for the second equivalence, we simply replace the summation with the supremum and the result follows.
∎

5.
Dual Characterizations

In this Section, we focus our attention on the characterization of the the dual of the spaces
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
when
1<p,q<\infty
and
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}).
Let us start by identifying the dual space of
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}).

5.1.
Dual of
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})

As noted in the introduction, the dual of
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
when
0<p\leq q\leq 1
is a Campanato-type space (see
[
1
]
). Hence our focus is on characterizing the dual of
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
when
1<p\leq q<\infty
. Let us start with the justification of the following important result.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
2\leq p\leq q<\infty
. Then the space
H_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is uniformly convex.

Proof.

We recall that a Banach space
\mathcal{H}
is uniformly convex if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1
,
\|y\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 1
and
\|x-y\|_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\epsilon
, then
\|x+y\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 2(1-\delta)
.

We recall that for
1\leq r<\infty
and for
a,b>0
,

(a+b)^{r}\leq 2^{r-1}(a^{r}+b^{r})\quad\textrm{and}\quad a^{r}+b^{r}\leq(a+b)^{r}.

Let
\epsilon>0
, and assume that
f,g\in H_{p,q}^{s}
with
\|f\|_{H_{p,q}^{s}}\leq 1
,
\|g\|_{H_{p,q}^{s}}\leq 1
and
\|f-g\|_{H_{p,q}^{s}}\geq\epsilon
. We start by observing that

s^{2}(f+g)+s^{2}(f-g)=2(s^{2}(f)+s^{2}(g)).

We then obtain

\displaystyle\left(s^{2}(f+g)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\left(s^{2}(f-g)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\left(s^{2}(f+g)+s^{2}(f-g)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq 2^{p-1}\left[s^{p}(f)+s^{p}(g)\right].

Hence for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\|s(f+g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}+\|s(f+g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\left(\|s(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}+\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}\right).

Raising both members of the last inequality to the power
\frac{q}{p}\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|s(f+g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}+\|s(f+g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|s(f+g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}+\|s(f+g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{q}{p}(p-1)}\left(\|s(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}+\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{q}{p}(p-1)}2^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\left(\|s(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}+\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}\right).

Hence taking the sum over
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain

\|s(f+g)\|_{p,q}^{q}+\|s(f-g)\|_{p,q}^{q}\leq 2^{q-1}\left(\|s(f)\|_{p,q}^{q}+\|s(g)\|_{p,q}^{q}\right)

and so

\displaystyle\|s(f+g)\|_{p,q}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q-1}\left(\|s(f)\|_{p,q}^{q}+\|s(g)\|_{p,q}^{q}\right)-\|s(f-g)\|_{p,q}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q}-\epsilon^{q}.

Thus

\|s(f+g)\|_{p,q}\leq 2(1-\delta)

where

\delta=1-\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{q}}{2^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

and the proof is complete.
∎

From the above Lemma and Milman’s Theorem (see
[
16
, p.127]
), we deduce the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
2\leq p\leq q<\infty
. Then the space
H_{p,q}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is reflexive.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

It follows from Corollary
5.3
above that we only need to prove for the case
1<q\leq p\leq 2.
Let
g\in H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\kappa_{g}(f):=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}fd_{n}g\right)\quad\left(f\in H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\right).

Hence by Schwarz’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle|\kappa_{g}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}f||d_{n}g|\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{A_{j}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}f||d_{n}g|\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{A_{j}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}g|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{A_{j}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}g|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{A_{j}}s(f)s(g)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}.

Applying the Hölder’s inequality to the right hand of the last inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|\kappa_{g}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|s(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|s(f)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus
\kappa_{g}\in\left(H^{s}_{p,q}\right)^{\prime}
and

\|\kappa_{g}\|\leq\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}.

Conversely, let
\kappa
be a continuous linear functional on
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}).
Then as
H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
embeds continuously into
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
(since
q<p
), we have by the Hahn-Banach theorem that
\kappa
can be extended to a continuous linear functional
\tilde{\kappa}
on
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})
having the same operator norm as
\kappa
. It follows from
[
14
, Theorem 2.26]
that there exists some
g\in H^{s}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\tilde{\kappa}(f)=\mathbb{E}(fg)\quad\left(\forall f\in H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R})\right).

In particular

(9)

\kappa(f)=\tilde{\kappa}(f)=\mathbb{E}(fg)\quad\left(\forall f\in H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\right).

Let us prove that

(10)

\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\sup_{f\in H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}),\,\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1}|\kappa(f)|<\infty.

Obviously, this holds if
\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}=0
. Hence we assume that
\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}\neq 0
.

We recall that,
A_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\geq 1
. Set

(11)

\displaystyle\mu_{n}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{p^{\prime}-2}_{n}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}.

Since the
A_{j}
’s are pairwise disjoint, we have that

\mu_{n}^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{2p^{\prime}-4}_{n}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}.

From the definition of
s(\cdot),
we have that
\mu_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{n-1}
-measurable. We define
h
as the martingale transform of
g
by
\mu_{n}.
That is

(12)

\displaystyle d_{n}h=\mu_{n}d_{n}g.

We then obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}h|^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}^{2}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}g|^{2}

or equivalently

s^{2}(h)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{2p^{\prime}-4}_{n}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}g|^{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle s^{2}(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s^{2p^{\prime}-4}_{n}(g)\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}g|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s^{2p^{\prime}-4}_{n}(g)(s_{n}^{2}(g)-s_{n-1}^{2}(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[s^{2p^{\prime}-2}_{n}(g)-s^{2p^{\prime}-4}_{n}(g)s_{n-1}^{2}(g)].

It follows that

\displaystyle s^{2}(h)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[s^{2p^{\prime}-2}_{n}(g)-s^{2p^{\prime}-2}_{n-1}(g)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{2q^{\prime}-2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{2p^{\prime}-2}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})}}.

Thus, by disjointness of the
A_{j}
’s,

(13)

\displaystyle s(h)\leq\frac{s^{p^{\prime}-1}(g)}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}.

We also have that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

s(h)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{p^{\prime}-1}(g)}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}=\frac{s^{p^{\prime}-1}(g)}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}.

Therefore

\|s(h)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p}\leq\frac{\|s^{p^{\prime}-1}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}=\frac{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-1}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}=\frac{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}.

Hence

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|s(h)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p}^{q}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q(q^{\prime}-1)}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q(q^{\prime}-1)}}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{k}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}}=\frac{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}}=1.

That is

\|h\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1.

We now test (
10
) with the martingale
h
above. First proceeding as in
[
14
, p.37]
(this is why we need
p
to be smaller than
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\kappa(h)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}hd_{n}g\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}|d_{n}g|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{p^{\prime}-2}_{n}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}_{n-1}|d_{n}g|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{s^{p^{\prime}-2}_{n}(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}(s^{2}_{n}(g)-s^{2}_{n-1}(g))\right)

It follows that

\displaystyle|\kappa(h)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s^{p^{\prime}}_{n}(g)-s^{p^{\prime}}_{n-1}(g)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}s^{p^{\prime}}(g)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}s^{p^{\prime}}(g)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|s(g)\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\frac{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}}{\|s(g)\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}-1}}=\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}\|s(g)\|_{L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

5.4.
Dual of
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}

This part is devoted to the characterization of the dual of the variation integrable space. We begin our characterization of the dual of
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
with the introduction of the following larger space.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
1\leq p,q,r<\infty.
We define the space
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})
by

\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})=\left\{\mbox{measurable process}\,\,\epsilon=(\epsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}:\|\epsilon\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})}<\infty\right\}

where

\|\epsilon\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})}=\left\|\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}|\epsilon_{n}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

We observe that
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1}).
Indeed let
f
be a martingale. Then it is measurable with respect to the underlining filtration hence its increment,
d_{n}f,
is also measurable. Thus we can take
\epsilon_{n}=d_{n}f
and the inclusion then follows by setting
r=1.

In the same way, we obtain that
\mathcal{BD}_{p,q}\subseteq\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{\infty}).

We also observe that since
L_{p,p}(\mathbb{R})=L_{p}(\mathbb{R}),
then
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,p},\ell_{r})
is the space defined in
[
14
, Definition 2.8]
.
The following lemma is part of the proof of Proposition
5.7
that follows, but for the sake of the presentation, we isolate it.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
,
1\leq r<\infty
and let
(p,p^{\prime}),\,\,(q,q^{\prime}),\,\,(r,r^{\prime})
be their respective conjugate pairs. Let
\eta\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}}).
Consider the sequence
h=(h_{k})_{k\geq 0}
defined as follows

h_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{|\eta_{k}|^{r^{\prime}}}{\eta_{k}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-r^{\prime}}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}&,&\eta_{k}\neq 0\\
0&,&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

if
r>1
, and

h_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\sign(\eta_{k})}{2^{k+1}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-1}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}&,&\eta_{k}\neq 0\\
0&,&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for
r=1
.
Then

h
has a unit norm in
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).
Consequently
h\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).

Proof.

By definition,

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{k}|h_{k}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{p}{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\left\|\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|^{q}_{L_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Now for
r>1
, we obtain

|h_{k}|^{r}=|\eta_{k}|^{r^{\prime}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-r^{\prime})r}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)r}}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)^{r}

so that

\sum_{k}|h_{k}|^{r}=\sum_{k}|\eta_{k}|^{r^{\prime}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-r^{\prime})r}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)r}}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)^{r}

and hence

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-r^{\prime})r}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)r}}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}+(p^{\prime}-r^{\prime})r}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)r}}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-1)r}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)r}}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)^{r}

and then

(14)

\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}=\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-1}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}.

One can easily check that (
14
) also holds for
r=1
.

As the
A_{k}
s are disjoint, we obtain

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-1}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{i}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-1}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}.

We now take the
L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
-norm of both sides.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|h\|^{p}_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-1)p}}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)p}}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})p}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-1)p}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})p}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)p}}\frac{1}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)p}}\frac{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{(p^{\prime}-q^{\prime})p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)p}}\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{(q^{\prime}-1)p}.

Therefore

\left\|\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p}}=\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{q^{\prime}-1}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\left\|\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|^{q}_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{(q^{\prime}-1)q}}\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{(q^{\prime}-1)q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{q^{\prime}}

and then

\sum_{j\geq 0}\left\|\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|^{q}_{L_{p}}=\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}}}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}}=1.

Therefore

\|h\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})}=\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\left\|\|h\|_{\ell_{r}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|^{q}_{L_{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=1.

Thus
h=(h_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})
since
h=(h_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is measurable.
∎

The following Proposition characterizes the dual of
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).

Proposition 5.7
.

For
1<p,q<\infty
and
1\leq r<\infty
, the dual space,
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})^{*},
of
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})
is
\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})
where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\quad\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1,\quad\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1.

Proof.

Let
\eta=(\eta_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})
and
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).
Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the usual inner product, that is,

\langle\eta,\epsilon\rangle=\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\epsilon_{k}.

and define the functional,
\Lambda,
by

\Lambda_{\eta}(\epsilon)=\mathbb{E}\langle\eta,\epsilon\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\epsilon_{k}\eta_{k}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{A_{j}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\epsilon_{k}\eta_{k}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

for all
\eta=(\eta_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})
measurable and
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).

Then by Hölder inequality,

(15)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\eta}(\epsilon)|=\left|\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{A_{j}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\epsilon_{k}\eta_{k}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right|\leq\|\epsilon\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}.

and since
\Lambda_{\eta}(\cdot)
is linear and bounded, it is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).
From inequality (
15
), we deduce that
\Lambda_{\eta}\in\left(\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})\right)^{\prime}
and

(16)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{\eta}\|\leq\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}.

For the converse, we can suppose that
q<p
. Let
\Lambda
be a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).
Then as
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})
embeds continuously into
\mathcal{K}(L_{p},\ell_{r})
(since
q<p
), we have by Hahn-Banach Theorem that
\Lambda
can be extended to a continuous linear functional
\tilde{\Lambda}
on
\mathcal{K}(L_{p},\ell_{r})
having the same operator norm as
\Lambda.
It follows from (
[
14
, Lemma 2.9]
) that there exists some
\eta\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})
such that

\tilde{\Lambda}_{\eta}(\epsilon)=\mathbb{E}\langle\eta,\epsilon\rangle

for all
\epsilon\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p},\ell_{r}).
In particular

\Lambda_{\eta}(\epsilon)=\tilde{\Lambda}_{\eta}(\epsilon)=\mathbb{E}\langle\eta,\epsilon\rangle

for all
\epsilon\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}).
Let us now show that

\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}\lesssim\sup_{\epsilon\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r}),\,\|\epsilon\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})}\leq 1}|\Lambda_{\eta}(\epsilon)|.

Set
h=(h_{k})_{k\geq 0}
to be the sequence defined in Lemma
5.6
. Since
h=(h_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{r})
with a unit norm, by linearity of the expectation operator, we have that for
1<r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\eta}(h)|=\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}|\eta_{k}|^{r^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}})}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{q^{\prime}}\right].

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}}=\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}.

Thus

(17)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\|\geq\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}

In the case of
r=1
, we note that there is and integer
k_{0}
such that

\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq|\eta_{k_{0}}|.

Thus using the test function
h
defined in Lemma
5.6
for
r=1
, and following the steps above, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\eta}(h)|=\frac{1}{\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{|\eta_{k}|}{2^{k+1}}\right)\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-1}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k_{0}+2}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}^{q^{\prime}-1}}\mathbb{E}\left(\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}}{\left\|\|\eta\|_{\ell_{r^{\prime}}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{j}}\right\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}-q^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k_{0}+2}}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{r^{\prime}})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We then see that
(\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1}))^{*}=\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{\infty})
and thus it is now evident that the dual of the variation integrable space is the jump bounded space. More rigorously, we prove Theorem
3.5
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
and set

\kappa_{g}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[d_{k}fd_{k}g]\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,f\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}).

We obtain

\displaystyle|\kappa_{g}(f)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[d_{k}fd_{k}g]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[|d_{k}f||d_{k}g|]\leq\mathbb{E}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}[|d_{k}f|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{A_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\int_{A_{j}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\int_{A_{j}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\int_{A_{j}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right]^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\int_{A_{j}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|^{p^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\right]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

That is

\displaystyle|\kappa_{g}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|d_{n}f|\right\|_{p,q}\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{n}g|\right\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}.

Therefore
\kappa_{g}\in\left(\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\right)^{\prime}
and

\|\kappa_{g}\|\leq\|g\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}.

To prove the converse, we first assume that
\tau
is an arbitrary element in the dual of
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})
then we show that there exists
g\in\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\tau=\kappa_{g}
and
\|g\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq C\|\tau\|
for some constant
C.

By setting
\epsilon_{k}=d_{k}f
for
f\in\mathcal{M}
we saw earlier that
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1}).
We also recall that the dual space of
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1})
is
\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{\infty})
and
\tau
is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1}).
By Hahn-Banach Theorem,
\tau
can be extended to a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1})
having the same operator norm as
\tau.
Let
\Lambda
be this extension of
\tau.
Then we have by Proposition
5.7
that there exists
\eta\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{\infty})
such that

\|\Lambda\|=\|\tau\|\approx\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{\infty})}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda_{\eta}(\epsilon)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{E}(\epsilon_{k}\eta_{k})

for
\epsilon\in\mathcal{K}(L_{p,q},\ell_{1}).
Hence

(18)

\displaystyle\tau(f_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(d_{k}f)\eta_{k}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(d_{k}f)(\mathbb{E}_{k}\eta_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1}\eta_{k})]

is well defined (we agree for a moment to work with
f_{n}
as we will show that
f_{n}\to f
in
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
as
n\to\infty
).
We shall now set

g_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{k=1}^{n}[\mathbb{E}_{k}\eta_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1}\eta_{k}]\quad\textrm{if}\quad n\neq 0\\
g_{0}=0\end{array}\right.

and show that
g_{n}
is a bounded martingale. Indeed we observe that as
k\to\infty,\,\,\mathbb{E}_{k}\eta_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1}\eta_{k}\to 0.
Thus
g_{n}
is finite (i.e.
\sup_{n}|g_{n}|<\infty
) and also

\mathbb{E}_{n-1}(g_{n}-g_{n-1})=\mathbb{E}_{n-1}(\mathbb{E}_{n}\eta_{n}-\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\eta_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\eta_{n}-\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\eta_{n}=0.

This means that
g_{n}
is a martingale. Consequently, since

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\mathbb{E}_{k}\eta_{k}-\mathbb{E}_{k-1}\eta_{k}|\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(|\mathbb{E}_{k}|\eta_{k}|+\mathbb{E}_{k-1}|\eta_{k}|)\leq 2\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{n}\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\eta_{k}|\right),

we can invoke inequality (
2
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|d_{k}g|\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}_{n}\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\eta_{k}|\right)\right\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\leq\frac{2p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\mathbb{E}_{n}\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\eta_{k}|\right)\right\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\eta_{k}|\right\|_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}=\frac{2p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{\infty})}.

Hence
g\in\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
and
\|g\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|\tau\|
since
\|\tau\|=\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{K}(L_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},\ell_{\infty})}.

We now show that
f_{n}\to f
in
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
as
n\to\infty.

We observe that since
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}-f_{k-1},
we have that

f_{n}-f=f_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}-f_{k-1}=f_{n}-\sum_{k=0}^{n}f_{k}-f_{k-1}-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}f_{k}-f_{k-1}=-\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}f_{k}-f_{k-1}.

Hence for
n\to\infty,\,\,f_{n}-f\to 0.
Also

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}|d_{k}(f_{n}-f)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}|d_{k}f_{n}-d_{k}f|\leq 2\sum_{k\geq 0}\left||d_{k}f_{n}|+|d_{k}f|\right|\leq 4\sum_{k\geq 0}|d_{k}f|<\infty

since
f\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
and thus by the Dominated Convergence Theorem

\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\|\sum_{k\geq 0}|d_{k}(f_{n}-f)|\right\|_{L_{p,q}(\mathbb{R})}\to 0\quad\mbox{for}\,\,n\to\infty.

That is
f_{n}\to f
in
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}).
Consequently, from equation (
18
), as
n\to\infty,
we have that, by setting
\eta_{k}=d_{k}g,

\tau(f_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(d_{k}f)\eta_{k}]\to\tau(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[(d_{k}f)\eta_{k}]=\kappa_{g}(f).

Hence
\|g\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\|\tau\|=C\|\kappa\|
and the poof is complete.
∎

Further discussions and conclusion

In this work, we were able to to characterize the dual of
H^{s}_{p,q}
for
1<p\leq q<\infty.
We were also able to extend the Doob’s inequality and the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality from the classical Lebesgue space to the amalgam space. We have discussed the various relations between the martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces and showed that upon the condition of regular basis, the spaces are all equivalent. We have also introduced the variation integrable space relative to our setting and characterized its dual space.

We observe that the Davis’ decomposition is also valid in the martingale Hardy-amalgam spaces. Indeed, we have the following decomposition which can be proved as in the case
p=q
(see
[
14
, Lemma 2.13]
)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in H^{S}_{p,q}\,\,(1\leq p<\infty,\,\,0<q<\infty).
Then there exists
h=\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
and
g=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}
such that
f=\{f_{n}=h_{n}+g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\|h\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\leq(2+2C)\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}}

and

\|g\|_{\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}}\leq(7+2C)\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}}.

Combining the above theorem with the second of the inequalities (v) in Theorem
3.1
, we derive the following.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
f=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in H^{S}_{p,q}\,\,(1\leq p<\infty,\,\,0<q<\infty).
Then there exists
h=\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
and
g=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in H^{s}_{p,q}
such that
f=\{f_{n}=h_{n}+g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\|h\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\leq(2+2C)\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}}

and

\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\leq(7+2C)\|f\|_{H^{S}_{p,q}}.

The following Davis’ decomposition of
H^{*}_{p,q}
follows also as in
[
14
, Lemma 2.14]
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
f=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in H^{*}_{p,q}\,\,(1\leq p<\infty,\,\,0<q<\infty).
Then there exists
h=\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
and
g=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{P}_{p,q}
such that
f=\{f_{n}=h_{n}+g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\|h\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\leq(4+4C)\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}

and

\|g\|_{\mathcal{P}_{p,q}}\leq(13+4C)\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}.

Combining the above theorem with inequality (v) in Theorem
3.1
, we derive the following.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
f=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in H^{*}_{p,q}\,\,(1\leq p<\infty,\,\,0<q<\infty).
Then there exists
h=\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
and
g=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in H^{s}_{p,q}
such that
f=\{f_{n}=h_{n}+g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\|h\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\leq(4+4C)\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}

and

\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\leq(13+4C)\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}.

We close this work with the proof of the following duality result that extend the one of
[
14
, Theorem 2.34]
.

Theorem 6.5
.

The dual space of
H^{*}_{p,q}\,\,(1<q\leq p\leq 2)
can be given with the norm

\|\phi\|:=\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}+\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}}

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}.
Then
\phi\in L_{2}
since
L_{2}
is dense in
H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
(from its atomic decomposition). Define the functional
\kappa_{\phi}
by

(19)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\phi}(f)=\mathbb{E}(f\phi),\quad(f\in L_{2}).

We will show that (
19
) is a bounded linear functional on
H^{*}_{p,q}\,\,(1<p\leq q\leq 2).

Linearity follows since the expectation operator is linear. Also as
L_{2}
is dense in
H^{*}_{p,q}
, we have that
\kappa_{\phi}
is well defined.
As
f_{n}\to f
in
L_{2}
norm (as
n\to\infty
), we have that

\kappa_{\phi}(f):=\mathbb{E}(f\phi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(f_{n}\phi).

Now for
f\in H^{*}_{p,q},
we know from Davis’ decomposition (Corollary
6.4
) that
f_{n}=h_{n}+g_{n}
where
h=\{h_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
g=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are martingales such that

(20)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}

and

(21)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}.

Hence we have by linearity of
\mathbb{E}
that

(22)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(f_{n}\phi)|=|\mathbb{E}(h_{n}\phi+g_{n}\phi)|\leq|\mathbb{E}(g_{n}\phi)|+|\mathbb{E}(h_{n}\phi)|

From (
21
) we have that
g\in H^{s}_{p,q}
since
f\in H^{*}_{p,q}.
Hence since
\phi\in H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},
it follows from Theorem
3.4
for
(q\leq p)
that

(23)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(g_{n}\phi)|\leq\|g_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}.

Similarly, since
h\in\mathcal{G}_{p,q}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}},
we have by Theorem 4.13 that

(24)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(h_{n}\phi)|\leq\|h_{n}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}.

Therefore (
22
) becomes

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(f_{n}\phi)|\leq\|g_{n}\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}+\|h_{n}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}

and thus

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(f\phi)|\leq\|g\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}+\|h\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}.

It then follows from (
20
) and (
21
) that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(f\phi)|\leq\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}+\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}.

In other words,

(25)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}(f\phi)|\leq\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}(\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}+\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}).

Thus the functional
\kappa_{\phi}
is continuous linear functional on
H^{*}_{p,q}.

Conversely, assume that
\kappa_{\phi}
is an arbitrary continuous linear on
H^{*}_{p,q}.
Then as
H^{*}_{p,q}
embeds continuously in
H^{*}_{p}\,\,(q<p),
we have by Hahn-Banach theorem that
\kappa_{\phi}
can be extended to a continuous linear functional
\tilde{\kappa_{\phi}}
on
H^{*}_{p}
having the same operator norm as
\kappa_{\phi}.
It follows from
[
14
, Theorem 2.34]
that there exists some
\phi\in L_{2}
such that

\tilde{\kappa_{\phi}}(f)=\mathbb{E}(f\phi),\,\,(f\in L_{2}).

In particular

\kappa_{\phi}(f)=\tilde{\kappa_{\phi}}(f)=\mathbb{E}(f\phi),\,\,(f\in H^{*}_{p,q}).

We observe that since

\displaystyle f^{*}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{k}f\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|d_{k}f|\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|,

we have that

\|f^{*}\|_{p,q}\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|d_{k}f|\right\|_{p,q}\quad(\mbox{in other words},\,\,\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{G}_{p,q}}).

Thus
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}\subseteq H^{*}_{p,q}.
Therefore
\kappa_{\phi}
is also a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}.
It follows from Theorem
3.5
that

(26)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq C\|\kappa_{\phi}\|.

We also recall that from Theorem
3.1
, we have
\|f\|_{H^{*}_{p,q}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{s}_{p,q}}\,\,(1\leq p,q\leq 2)
Therefore
\kappa_{\phi}
is also a continuous linear functional on
H^{s}_{p,q}.
Hence by Theorem
3.4
, for
(q\leq p)
,

(27)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq C\|\kappa_{\phi}\|.

From (
26
) and (
27
), we obtain that

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{BD}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{s}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leq C\|\kappa_{\phi}\|

and the proof is complete.
∎

Finally, we note that even in the dyadic case, the characterization of the dual of
H^{s}_{p,q}
for
(p,q)\notin\{(p,q)\in[1,\infty)^{2}:1<q\leq p\leq 2\quad\textrm{or}\quad 2\leq p\leq q<\infty\}
is still open.

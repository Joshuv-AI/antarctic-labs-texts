On Carlotto-Schoen-type scalar-curvature gluings Preprint UWThPh 2016-19
Piotr T. Chruściel, Erwann Delay

Abstract
We carry out a Carlotto-Schoen-type gluing with interpolating scalar curvature on cone-like sets, or deformations thereof, in the category of smooth Riemannian asymptotically Euclidean metrics.



1
Introduction

In an outstanding paper
[
6
]
Carlotto and Schoen have constructed non-trivial asymptotically Euclidean scalar-flat metrics which are Minkowskian outside of a solid cone. The reading of their paper suggests strongly, though no explicit statements are made, that their construction can be generalised as follows:

1.

Rather than gluing an asymptotically Euclidean metric to a flat one, any two asymptotically Euclidean metrics
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
can be glued together.

2.

In the spirit of
[
12
]
, the gluings at zero-scalar curvature can be replaced by gluings where the scalar curvature of the final metric equals

\chi R(g_{1})+(1-\chi)R(g_{2})

with a cut-off function
\chi
.

3.

The geometry of the gluing region can be somewhat more general than the interface between two rotationally-symmetric cones.

The aim of this paper is to give detailed proofs of the above. While the overall structure of the argument is rather similar, the details are different:
Both the proof in
[
6
]
and ours require a weighted Poincaré inequality, with nonstandard weights adapted to the geometry of the problem at hand. We provide a version of this inequality different from the one in
[
6
]
, which might be of more general interest. We also provide a detailed proof of the gluing with exponential weights near the boundary, only sketched in
[
6
]
.
Finally, we replace some of the tailor-made arguments in
[
6
]
by off-the-shelf results from
[
8
]
.

A special case of our main Theorem
3.1
below provides the following variant of the Riemannian-geometry version of the main theorem of
[
6
]
:

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Consider two smooth
n
-dimensional asymptotically Euclidean Riemannian metrics
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
and two nested cones with non-zero opening angles and vertices displaced along the symmetry axis, see Figure
1.1
. Simultaneously scaling-up the cones if necessary, or simultaneously shifting them to the asymptotic region, there exists a smooth asymptotically Euclidean metric
g
which coincides with
g_{1}
outside of the larger cone and with
g_{2}
inside the smaller one, with the scalar curvature
R(g)
lying between
R(g_{1})
and
R(g_{2})
in the intermediate region.

Figure 1.1:
Left picture: If the second metric is flat and both metrics are scalar-flat, then the new metric is flat inside the smaller cone, scalar-flat everywhere, and coincides with the first one outside the larger cone. Both cones extend to infinity, and their tips are located very far in the asymptotically Euclidean region.
Right picture: Iterating the construction, one can embed any finite number of distinct initial data sets into Minkowskian data, or paste-in Minkowskian data inside several cones into a given data set.

In particular if
R(g_{1})=R(g_{2})=0
, then
R(g)=0
.

In Section
6
we show how Theorem
1.1
follows from our main Theorem
3.1
below.
The key to the proof of this last theorem is a weighted Poincaré inequality involving radially-scaled exponential weights, proved in Proposition
5.6
below.
The rest of the proof is a verification of the hypotheses of the rather general results proved in
[
8
]
.

As already mentioned, Theorem
3.1
below allows more general cones than the ones in
[
6
]
, as described at the beginning of Section
3
. In fact, our arguments apply to a large class of deformed cones as well, e.g. “logarithmically-rotated ones”, cf. Theorem
4.1
and the discussion in Section
4
.

Definitions, notations and conventions

We use the summation convention throughout, indices are lowered with
g_{ij}

and raised with its inverse

.

We will have to frequently control the asymptotic behavior of the objects at hand. Given a tensor field

A metric

For

let

be the space of

functions or tensor fields such that the norm

3
Gluing on sets which are scale-invariant at large distances

Let
S(p,R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote a sphere of radius
R
centred at
p
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain with smooth boundary, thus
\Omega
is open and connected, and
\partial\Omega=\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Omega
is a smooth manifold. In other words,
\overline{\Omega}
is a smooth (connected) submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary and
\Omega
is its interior.
We further assume that
\partial\Omega
has
exactly two
connected components, and that
there exists
R_{0}\geq 1
such that
the boundary
\partial\Omega_{S}
of

\Omega_{S}:=\Omega\cap S(0,R_{0})

(3.1)

also has again exactly two
connected components, with

\Omega\setminus B(0,R_{0})=\{\lambda p\ |\ p\in\Omega_{S}\,,\lambda\geq 1\}\,.

(3.2)

When (
3.2
) holds, we say that
\Omega
is
invariant under scaling at large distances
.

We let
x:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
be any smooth defining function for
\partial\Omega
which has been chosen so that

x(\lambda p)=\lambda x(p)
for
\lambda\geq 1
and for
p,\lambda p\in\Omega\setminus B(0,R_{0})
.

(3.3)

Equivalently, for
p\in\Omega_{S}
and
\lambda
larger than one, we require
x(\lambda p)=\lambda x_{S}(p)
, where
x_{S}
is a defining function for
\partial\Omega_{S}
within
S(0,R_{0})
.

By definition of
\Omega
there exists a constant
c
such that the distance function
d(p)
from a point
p\in\Omega
to
\partial\Omega
is smooth for all
d(p)\leq cr(p)

for some (perhaps small) constant
c
and where, as before,
r=|\vec{x}|
in the explicitly Euclidean coordinates in the asymptotic end.
The function
x
can be chosen to be equal to
d
in that region.

A simple example, considered in
[
6
]
, satisfying the above is the following: let
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\pi
and let
\Omega_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}
any domain with smooth boundary such that

\Omega_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\setminus B(0,R_{0})=(\mathring{C}_{\theta_{2}}\setminus C_{\theta_{1}})\setminus B(0,R_{0})\,,

(3.4)

where
C_{\theta}
is a closed solid cone with aperture
\theta>0
;
compare the left Figure
1.1
. Here, and elsewhere,
\mathring{U}
denotes the interior of a set
U
. In this example
\Omega_{S}:=\Omega_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}\cap S(0,R_{0})
is connected, with a boundary which has two connected components.

We will denote by
r
a smooth positive function which coincides with
|\vec{x}|
for
|\vec{x}|\geq 1
.

For
\beta,s,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
we define

\phi=\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)^{2}r=\frac{x^{2}}{r}\,,\quad\psi={r}^{-n/2-\beta}\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)^{\sigma}e^{-sr/x}=:{r}^{\mu}{x}^{\sigma}e^{-sr/x}

(3.5)

on
\Omega
. The factor
r^{-\beta}
is directly related to the large-distance radial behavior of the solutions, while the exponential factor with
s>0
will force the solutions we construct to vanish at all orders at
\partial\Omega
(compare (
3.13
) below).

We will make a gluing-by-interpolation of scalar curvature. The main interest is that of scalar-flat metrics, which then remain scalar-flat, or for metrics with positive scalar curvature, which then remains positive. Since the current problem is related to the construction of initial data sets for Einstein equations, in some general-relativistic matter models, such as Vlasov or dust, an interpolation of scalar curvature is of direct interest.

In order to carry out the interpolation, recall that
\partial\Omega
has exactly two connected components. We denote by
\chi
a smooth function with the following properties:

1.

0\leq\chi\leq 1
;

2.

\chi
equals one in a neighborhood of one of the components and equals zero in a neighborhood of the other component;

3.

on
\Omega\setminus B(0,R_{0})
the function
\chi
is required to depend only upon the variable
p
in (
3.2
).

Starting with two AE metrics
g
and
\hat{g}
we define

\displaystyle g_{\chi}:=\chi\hat{g}+(1-\chi)g\,,

(3.6)

\displaystyle R_{\chi}:=\chi R(\hat{g})+(1-\chi)R(g)\,,\quad\delta R_{\chi}:=R_{\chi}-R\big(g_{\chi}\big)\,.

(3.7)

We denote by
P_{g}
the linearisation of the scalar-curvature operator at a metric
g
, and will write
P
for
P_{g}
when
g
is obvious in the context. Its formal adjoint
P_{g}^{*}
reads

P_{g}^{*}(f)=\nabla\nabla f-\Delta fg-f\mbox{\rm Ric}(g)\,,

(3.8)

where
\Delta=\nabla^{i}\nabla_{i}
.

Letting
\Omega
,
x
and
r
be as just described, we have:

Theorem
3.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
,
k>n/2
,
\beta\in[-(n-2),0)
,
\tilde{\beta}<-\epsilon<\beta
,
s>0
, and
\sigma\neq-1/2
.

Set

\phi=\frac{x^{2}}{r}\,,\quad\psi={r}^{-n/2-\beta}\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)^{\sigma}e^{-sr/x}\,.

(3.9)

Suppose that
g\in M_{\delta+C^{k+4}_{r,r^{\epsilon}}}\cap C^{\infty}
.
For all real numbers
\sigma
and
s>0
and

all smooth metrics

{\hat{g}{}}
close enough to
g
in
C^{k+4}_{r,r^{-{\tilde{\beta}}}}(\Omega)

there exists on
\Omega
a unique smooth two-covariant symmetric tensor field
h
of the form

h=\phi^{4}\psi^{2}P^{*}_{g_{\chi}}(\delta N)\in\psi^{2}\phi^{2}\mathring{H}^{k+2}_{\phi,\psi}(g)

(3.10)

such that the metric
g_{\chi}+h
solves

R\left[g_{\chi}+h\right]=\chi R(\hat{g})+(1-\chi)R(g)\,.

(3.11)

The tensor field
h
vanishes at
\partial\Omega
and can be
C^{\infty}
-extended by zero across
\partial\Omega
, leading to a smooth asymptotically Euclidean metric
g_{\chi}+h
.

Remark
3.2
.

Some comments about the asymptotic behaviour of the metrics involved are in order. The requirement
g\in M_{\delta+C^{k+4}_{r,r^{\epsilon}}}\cap C^{\infty}
(equivalently,
g-\delta\in{C^{k+4}_{r,r^{\epsilon}}}\cap C^{\infty}
) guarantees that
g
is asymptotically Euclidean, with
g
approaching the Euclidean metric as
O(r^{-\epsilon})
. Note that
\epsilon
can be arbitrary small. Next,
g-\hat{g}
is required to fall off as
r^{\tilde{\beta}}
, so that
g_{\chi}
will approach
\delta
as

O(r^{\max(-\epsilon,{\tilde{\beta}})})
.
The final metric
g_{\chi}+h
will a priori approach
\delta
somewhat slower.
Indeed, the proof below shows that there exists a constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|h\|_{\psi^{2}\phi^{2}\mathring{H}^{k+2}_{\phi,\psi}(g_{\chi})}\leq C\left\|\delta R_{\chi}\right\|_{\psi^{2}\mathring{H}^{k}_{\phi,\psi}(g_{\chi})}\,.

(3.12)

(The right-hand side is finite and small only if the weight
\beta
is larger than
\max({\tilde{\beta}},-\epsilon)
, which explains the hypothesis.)
(Also note that
\delta N\in\mathring{H}^{k+4}_{\phi,\psi}(g_{\chi})
, in particular
\delta N=o(r^{\beta})
.)

Equation (
3.12
) implies (compare (
3.26
) below)

h=o(r^{-n-\beta+2}(x/r)^{\sigma+4-n}e^{-sr/x})=o(r^{-n-\beta+2})\,.

(3.13)

Since
\beta<0
, (
3.13
) shows that the decay of the metric to the flat-one is
most likely
slower than the Schwarzschildian decay rate
O(r^{-(n-2)})
.

Remark
3.3
.

A well defined ADM energy-momentum requires the metric to approach the flat-one as
o(r^{-(n-2)/2})
. Comparing with (
3.13
), this will be satisfied if in Theorem
3.1
we require

\epsilon>(n-2)/2
and

-\frac{n-2}{2}\leq\beta\leq 0\,.

(3.14)

Remark
3.4
.

An analogous result holds if
g
is not necessarily smooth: If
g\in M_{\delta+C^{k+4}_{r,r^{\epsilon}}}
, then the construction can be modified as in
[
9
]
so that the final metric

will be of

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to use some general results proved in
[
8
]
.
Now, the whole setup of
[
8
]
was geared towards the general relativistic constraint equations, which involve both a metric
g
and an extrinsic curvature tensor
K
. However, the analysis there applies
mutatis mutandis
to the Riemannian problem, where only the scalar curvature
R
of the metric
g
is considered, by setting
K\equiv 0
, and e.g. setting to zero the vector field
Y
appearing in
[
8
]
. It then suffices to ignore all the conditions imposed in
[
8
]
on the part of the equations there which involve a vector field
Y
. For example, in the current setting the inequality (3.4) of
[
8
]
should be replaced by

C\|{\phi^{2}}P_{g_{0}}^{*}(N)\|_{L^{2}_{\psi}(g_{0})}\geq\|N\|_{\mathring{H}^{1}_{\phi,\psi}(g_{0})}\,.

(3.15)

with
P_{g}^{*}
defined in (
3.8
).

Given this, we
start by showing that Theorem 3.6 of
[
8
]
with
K\equiv 0\equiv Y
applies, with
g_{0}
taken to be
a Ricci flat metric which is uniformly equivalent to the Euclidean one.
This requires, first, checking the “scaling property” of the functions
\phi
and
\psi
as defined in
[
8
, Appendix B]
.

This is a routine exercise, using scaling in
r
and the arguments in
[
8
, Appendix B]
. Next, note first that with a Ricci flat metric
g_{0}
, Equation (
3.8
) reads

P_{g_{0}}^{*}(f)=\nabla\nabla f-\Delta fg_{0}\,.

(3.16)

Since
\beta\neq 0
, the

inequality (
3.15
)
needed for
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]

follows directly from Proposition
5.6
below used twice, first for
N
and then for
\nabla N
. See also Remark
5.3
.
When
g_{0}
is
not
Ricci flat, the Ricci terms in
P^{*}
are dominated by the remaining ones by choosing

small enough in the relevant estimates: indeed, the term

\phi^{2}\mbox{\rm Ric}(g)N=(x^{2}/r)^{2}O(r^{-\epsilon-2})N=O(r^{-\epsilon})(x/r)^{4}N=o(1)N

can be absorbed in the right-hand side of (

) after using the triangle inequality on the left-hand side, resulting in (

) with

replaced by, say,

.
Further note that the integrals involving
u
in the right-hand side
of (

) will vanish if the support of

lies in the region
|\vec{x}|\geq R
.

Now, the operator
\pi_{{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{\perp}}
appearing in the statement of
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
projects on the space
{\mathcal{K}}_{0}^{\perp}
of
static KIDs
, namely the kernel of

. We claim that under our hypotheses this space is trivial, and so the
operator

{L}_{\phi,\psi}
of
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
is an isomorphism:
Indeed, it is well-known that static KIDs that are
o(1)
along some cone

have to vanish everywhere. (This is essentially

, Proposition 2.1]
, together with unique continuation for solutions of the KID equation.)
So it suffices to show that functions
\delta N\in\mathring{H}^{k+4}_{\phi,\psi}(g)
are
o(1)
in some cone contained in
\Omega
. For this let us choose a cone
C_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega
on which
x/r
is bounded away from zero. Then the restriction
\delta N|_{C_{\varepsilon}}
is in the space

\,\,\,\,\mathring{\!\!\!\!\mycal H}_{k}^{\beta}(C_{\varepsilon}):=\mathring{H}^{k}_{r,{r}^{-n/2-\beta}}(C_{\varepsilon})\,.

(3.17)

We note the inclusions
[
3
]

C^{\beta^{\prime}}_{k}(C_{\varepsilon})\subset\,\,\,\,\mathring{\!\!\!\!\mycal H}_{k}^{\beta}(C_{\varepsilon})\,,\ \beta^{\prime}<\beta\,,\ \mbox{ and }\ \quad\,\,\,\,\mathring{\!\!\!\!\mycal H}_{k}^{\beta}(C_{\varepsilon})\subset C^{\beta}_{\lfloor k-n/2\rfloor}(C_{\varepsilon})\,,\ k>n/2\,.

(3.18)

In fact
[
3
]

f\in\,\,\,\,\mathring{\!\!\!\!\mycal H}_{k}^{\beta}(C_{\varepsilon})\,,\ k>n/2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f=o(r^{\beta})\,.

(3.19)

So, the requirement
that there are no KIDs in the space under consideration will be satisfied for

\beta\leq 0\,.

(3.20)

(A more careful inspection shows (compare (

) below) that

\delta N\in\mathring{H}^{k}_{\phi,\psi}\,,\ k>n/2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\delta N=o\Big(r^{\beta}\big(\frac{x}{r}\big)^{-\sigma-n}e^{sr/x}\Big)\,,

but a possible blow-up of

when

tends to zero is irrelevant as long as

is forced to go to zero when receding to infinity away from

.)

Summarising,

[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
applies and shows that the map

there is an isomorphism.

We now want to use the
inverse function-type theorem
as in

[
8
, Theorem 3.9]

to solve the equation (

); equivalently

R\left[g_{\chi}+h\right]-R(g_{\chi})=\delta R_{\chi}\,.

(3.22)

This requires checking differentiability of the map defined there. This will follow by standard arguments if the metrics of the form

, with

given by (

of the Ricci scalar on

In cones

staying away from the boundary we have
\delta N\in\,\,\,\,\mathring{\!\!\!\!\mycal H}_{k+4}^{\beta}(C_{\varepsilon})
, and since on

we have

and

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{4}\psi^{2}P^{*}\delta N=r^{-n-2\beta+4}o(r^{\beta-2})=o(r^{-\beta-n+2})\,.

(3.24)

We conclude that

will decay to zero in such cones provided that

-(n-2)\leq\beta\,.

(3.25)

A more careful treatment, without invoking interior cones, uses the weighted Sobolev embedding

\displaystyle h\in\psi^{2}\phi^{2}\mathring{H}^{k+2}_{\phi,\psi}(g_{\chi})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle r^{-n-2\beta+2}(x/r)^{2\sigma+4}e^{-2sr/x}C^{k+1-\lfloor n/2\rfloor+\alpha}_{x^{2}/r,r^{-\beta}(x/r)^{\sigma+n}e^{-sr/x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{k+1-\lfloor n/2\rfloor+\alpha}_{x^{2}/r,r^{n+\beta-2}(x/r)^{-\sigma-4+n}e^{sr/x}}

where
\alpha
is any number in
(0,1)
when

is even, and
\alpha=1/2
when

is odd. (The inclusion above is obtained by a calculation as in
[

, Equation (B.4)]
where, using the notation there, in the fifth line the elliptic regularity estimate is replaced by the Sobolev inequality on

for the function

.)
This guarantees differentiability of the constraints map
(compare
[
4
, Corollary 3.2]
), as required for applicability of the inverse function theorem.

Smoothness of solutions is standard: away from
\partial\Omega
this follows from elliptic regularity, while near
\partial\Omega
this is guaranteed by the exponential decay of
h
and its derivatives at
\partial\Omega
. The proof is now complete.

4
Beyond scale-invariant sets

Let
(\mycal S,g)
be an asymptotically Euclidean manifold and let
\Omega\subset\mycal S
,
r
and
x
be as described at the beginning of Section
3
.
Let
g_{0}
be any smooth metric on
\mycal S

5
Weighted Poincaré inequalities

We will need the following
[
8

:

Proposition
5.1
.

Let
u
be a
C^{1}
compactly supported tensor field on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, and
let
w,v
be two
C^{2}
functions defined on a neighborhood of the
support of
u
. Then

\int_{M}e^{2v}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\geq\int_{M}e^{2v}\left[\Delta v+\Delta w+|\nabla v|^{2}-|\nabla w|^{2}\right]|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\,.

(5.1)

Throughout this section we assume that the metric
g
approaches the Euclidean metric as one recedes to infinity, with no decay rate imposed. The first derivatives are required to fall-off as
o(1/r)
. Wherever second derivatives of the metric arise in a calculation, they are required to fall-off as
o(1/r^{2})
, etc.
These conditions are more than satisfied under the usual hypotheses of asymptotic flatness.

We let
r
be any smooth positive function on
\Omega
bounded away from zero which coincides with the coordinate radius on
\Omega\setminus B(R_{0})
.

Our first goal is to prove Proposition
5.4
below. As a first step, we prove:

Lemma
5.2
.

Let
\Omega
be as in Section
3

and let
\sigma,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\sigma\neq-1/2
. There exist constants
c,C>0
such that for all
C^{2}
tensor fields
u
compactly supported in

\Omega^{\prime}:=\Omega\cap\{0<x<cr\}

it holds that

\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma+2}r^{2\mu}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\geq C\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\,.

(5.2)

Remark
5.3
.

Once the inequality has been established for a metric
g
as explained above, it is immediate that (
5.2
) also holds, when
u
is a function, for any metric which is uniformly equivalent to
g
. But then it holds for tensors for all differentiable metrics uniformly equivalent to
g
(no decay conditions on the derivatives needed) by a standard argument; compare
[
10
, Remark 4.7]
.

Proof.

We use (
5.1
) with

v=(\sigma+1)\ln x+\mu\ln r\,.

(5.3)

Then

\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}=\frac{\sigma+1}{x^{2}}\bigg[\sigma|\nabla x|^{2}+{2}{\mu x}\frac{\nabla x\cdot\nabla r}{r}+x\Delta x\bigg]+\frac{\mu}{r^{2}}\bigg[(\mu-1){|\nabla r|^{2}}+r\Delta r\bigg]\,.

(5.4)

Letting

w=-\frac{1}{2}\ln x

one similarly finds

\Delta w-|\nabla w|^{2}=\frac{1}{4x^{2}}\bigg[|\nabla x|^{2}-2x\Delta x\bigg]\,.

(5.5)

Given
\sigma\neq-\frac{1}{2}
,

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\epsilon>0
, and a compact set
K
, for small

the dominant contribution comes from the
|\nabla x|^{2}/x^{2}
terms. This shows that we can choose
x_{0}>0
small enough so that for
0<x<x_{0}
on

we have

\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}+\Delta w-|\nabla w|^{2}\geq\frac{(2\sigma+1)^{2}-\epsilon}{4x^{2}}|\nabla x|^{2}\,.

(5.6)

We consider now
p\in\Omega
with

\lambda:=r(p)\geq 2R_{0}\,,

(5.7)

for an
R_{0}
sufficiently large, to be determined below.
Then the scaling transformation
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni\vec{y}\mapsto 2R_{0}\lambda^{-1}\vec{y}

maps the set

\Gamma_{\lambda}:=\Omega\cap\{\lambda/2\leq|\vec{y}|\leq 2\lambda\}

to
\Gamma_{1}=\Omega\cap\{R_{0}\leq|\vec{y}|\leq 4R_{0}\}
.
The associated pull-back will carry the metric
g
on
\Gamma_{\lambda}

to a metric
\lambda^{-2}g_{\lambda}
on
\Gamma_{1}
, with
g_{\lambda}
approaching the flat metric on
\Gamma_{1}
as
\lambda
tends to infinity. We can use (
5.6
) for all rescaled metrics
g_{\lambda}
, with perhaps a smaller constant
x_{0}
independent of
\lambda
for
R_{0}
large enough. Since both sides are invariant under the transformation
(x,r)\mapsto(ax,ar)

up to terms which are subdominant when

is large enough, scaling back to
\Gamma_{\lambda}

gives there (taking
\epsilon
in (

) small enough and recalling that

is close to one when

is small)

\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}+\Delta w-|\nabla w|^{2}\geq\frac{(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}{2x^{2}}\,,

(5.8)

for all

small enough, as desired.

We let
K=\overline{\Omega\cap B(0,2R_{0})}
.
By compactness and (

) there exists a constant

such that on

we have for

(5.9)

This, together with (

Let

be as above and let

\displaystyle\phantom{C\bigg(}+\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma+2}r^{2\mu}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\bigg)\,,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{3}}{2}\bigg[\int_{\{0<x\leq re^{-\lambda}\}}x^{2\sigma+2}r^{2\mu}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}+\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\{0<x\leq re^{-\lambda}\}}x^{{2\sigma}}r^{2\mu}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle C_{3}\bigg[c_{3}\max(C^{2\sigma}\,,e^{{-4}\lambda\sigma})\bigg(\int_{\{re^{-2\lambda}\leq x\leq re^{-\lambda}\}}r^{2\mu+2\sigma}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}+\int_{\{w\neq 0\}\cap\{r=R\}}|u|^{2}d\sigma_{g}

We note a standard consequence of Proposition

:

Corollary

.

Let
\Omega
be as above and let

be such that

and

\mathring{H}

compactly supported tensor fields in

\mathring{H}

. Then there exists a constant

such that for all

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma+2}r^{2\mu}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\,.

Proof.

As already mentioned, the result is standard, we give the proof for completeness.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma+2}r^{2\mu}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}\,.

(5.19)

We can normalize the sequence
u_{n}
so that

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\geq x_{0},\,r=R\}}|u_{n}|^{2}d\sigma_{g}+\int_{\{x\geq x_{0},\,r\leq R\}}|u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}=1\,.

(5.20)

Equation (
5.10
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg(1+\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}\bigg)\,.

Thus, for
n\geq 2C
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}|u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2C\,.

(5.21)

Equation (
5.19
) gives now

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\geq x_{0},\,r\leq R\}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma+2}r^{2\mu}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}d\mu_{g}\leq\frac{2CC_{1}}{n}\,.

(5.22)

Let
K=\Omega\cap\{x>x_{0},\,r<R\}
.
Compactness of the embeddings

W^{1,2}(K)\subset L^{2}(K)\,,\quad W^{1,2}(K)\subset L^{2}(\{r=R\}\cap K)\,,

implies that
\{u_{n}\}
contains a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
, which is Cauchy both in
L^{2}(K)
and in
L^{2}(\{r=R\}\cap K)
. Equation (
5.10
) applied to
u_{n}-u_{m}
shows that
\{u_{n}\}
is Cauchy in
\mathring{H}
. The limit is a non-trivial tensor field satisfying
\nabla u=0
, which contradicts the fact that zero is the only such tensor field in
X
.

\Box

We have the exponentially-weighted version of the above:

Proposition
5.6
.

Let
\Omega
be as above and let
s,\sigma,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
s\neq 0
and
\beta\equiv{-\sigma-\mu-n/2}\neq 0
.
There exist constants
x_{0},C,R
such that for all
C^{2}
tensor fields
u
compactly supported in
\Omega

it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}e^{-sr/x}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg(\int_{\{x\geq x_{0},\,r=R\}}|u|^{2}d\sigma_{g}+\int_{\{x\geq x_{0},\,r\leq R\}}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\phantom{C\bigg(}+\int_{\Omega}x^{2\sigma+4}r^{2\mu-2}e^{-sr/x}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\bigg)\,.

Proof.

The proof is a direct repetition of that of Proposition
5.4
, with
\psi=x^{\sigma}r^{\mu}e^{-sr/(2x)}
,
\phi=x^{2}/r
, and where instead of Lemma
5.2
the following Lemma is used:

\Box

Lemma
5.7
.

Let
\Omega
be as above and let
s,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
s\neq 0
.
There exist constants
c,C>0
such that for all
C^{2}
tensor fields
u
compactly supported in

\Omega^{\prime}:=\Omega\cap\{0<x\ <cr\}

it holds that

\int_{M}x^{4+2\sigma}r^{2\mu-2}e^{-sr/x}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\geq C\int_{M}x^{2\sigma}r^{2\mu}e^{-sr/x}|u|^{2}d\mu_{g}\,.

(5.24)

Proof.

We use again (
5.1
), with
w=0
and

v=-\frac{sr}{x}+(2+\sigma)\ln x+(\mu-1)\ln r\,.

(5.25)

On any compact set one finds, for
0<x/r
small enough,

\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}=\big(\frac{s^{2}r^{2}}{x^{4}}+O(rx^{-3})+O(x^{-2})+O(r^{-2})\big)|\nabla x|^{2}\,.

(5.26)

A scaling argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
shows that
for
s\neq 0
and for
0<x<cr
with
c
small enough we have

\Delta v+|\nabla v|^{2}\geq\hat{C}\frac{r^{2}}{x^{4}}\,,

(5.27)

for some constant
\hat{C}>0
. This,
together with (
5.1
) leads to (
5.24
).

\Box

6
Applications

In this section we wish to show how to arrange things so that all conditions of Theorem
3.1
are met.

Consider, then, two smooth metrics
g,\hat{g}\in M_{\delta+C^{k+4}_{r,r^{\epsilon}}}\cap C^{\infty}
,
i=1,2
, for some
\epsilon>0
and
k>n/2
. Let
\Omega
,
\Omega_{S}
be as in Section
3
, cf. (
3.1
)-(
3.2
).

Let
\psi_{\vec{y}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the translation by
\vec{y}
,

\phi_{\vec{y}}(\vec{x}):=\vec{x}+\vec{y}\,.

When
|\vec{y}|
tends to infinity the three tensor fields
\phi_{\vec{y}}^{*}g-\delta
,
\phi_{\vec{y}}^{*}\hat{g}-\delta
, and
\phi_{\vec{y}}^{*}(g-\hat{g})
approach zero in
C^{k+4}_{r,r^{-\epsilon}}(\Omega)
. Thus Theorem
3.1
applies for all
|\vec{y}|
large enough, and provides a gluing of
\phi_{\vec{y}}^{*}g
with
\phi_{\vec{y}}^{*}\hat{g}
. Taking
\Omega
to be a translation of the asymptotic cone as in (
3.4
) proves Theorem
1.1
.

An alternative construction proceeds as follows, assuming that
\Omega\cap B(0,1)=\emptyset
: For
\lambda\geq 1
let
\psi_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the scaling map,
\psi_{\lambda}(\vec{x}):=\lambda\vec{x}
. Set

g_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-2}\psi_{\lambda}^{*}g\,,\qquad\hat{g}_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-2}\psi_{\lambda}^{*}\hat{g}\,.

(6.1)

Then both
g_{\lambda}
and
\hat{g}_{\lambda}
tend to
\delta
in
C^{k+4}_{r,r^{-\epsilon}}(\Omega)
as
\lambda
tends to infinity. Hence Theorem
3.1
applies for all
\lambda
large enough. Scaling back the glued metric provides a gluing of
g
and
\hat{g}
along a rescaled set
\Omega
.

Consider, finally, a collection
\Omega_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, of disjoint sets satisfying the requirements set forth at the beginning of Section
3
, possibly after translations. The construction just given can be repeated
N
-times to obtain a gluing of
g
and
\hat{g}
across
\cup\Omega_{i}
.

On a cross-diffusive SIS epidemic model with power-like nonlinear incidence Thanks: † Corresponding author.
Huicong Li, Tian Xiang †

Abstract
. We study global existence, boundedness and convergence of nonnegative classical solutions of a Neumann initial-boundary value problem for the following cross-diffusive SIS (susceptible-infected-susceptible) epidemic model with power-like infection mechanism generalizing the standard mass action mechanism: { S t = d S Δ S + χ ∇ ⋅ ( S ∇ I ) − β S q I p + γ I , x ∈ Ω , t > 0 , I t = d I ​ Δ ​ I + β ​ S q ​ I p − ( γ + μ ) ​ I , x ∈ Ω , t > 0 , \begin{cases}S_{t}=d_{S}\Delta S+\chi\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)-\beta S^{q}I^{p}+\gamma I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\[7.11317pt] I_{t}=d_{I}\Delta I+\beta S^{q}I^{p}-(\gamma+\mu)I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\end{cases} in a bounded smooth domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n ​ ( n ≥ 1 ) \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (n\geq 1) . The incidence of the form β ​ S q ​ I p \beta S^{q}I^{p} with p , q > 0 p,\,q>0 is a natural extension of the classical mass action type β ​ S ​ I \beta SI , and the cross-diffusive term χ ∇ ⋅ ( S ∇ I ) \chi\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I) with χ ≥ 0 \chi\geq 0 describes the effect that the susceptible individuals tend to move away from higher concentration of infected populations. Global existence and boundedness of classical solutions are established in certain parameter ranges, and threshold/non-threshold long-time behaviors of global bounded solutions are also detected. Our findings significantly improve and extend previous related studies.

Introduction

In the theory of epidemiology, incidence is the occurrence of new cases of a disease via contact between infected individuals and susceptible ones within a specified period of time, and it is usually characterized by the infection mechanism in the modeling of infectious diseases. In the seminal work of Kermack and McKendrick
[
19
]
, the authors studied a susceptible-infected-recovered compartmental model with bilinear incidence rate
\beta SI
, where
S
and
I
, respectively, are the numbers of susceptible and infected populations, and the positive constant
\beta
measures the disease transmission rate. However, as argued in
[
12
,
35
]
, this type of bilinear infection mechanism, describing the simple mass action law, has its own shortcomings and may not fit the field data well. Thus, various different types of transmission mechanism were proposed and investigated, such as the general nonlinear transmission rate of the form
\beta S^{q}I^{p}
with
p
,
q>0

[
15
,
20
,
23
,
24
,
30
,
32
,
33
,
40
,
44
]
, the standard incidence type of
\beta\frac{SI}{S+I}

[
1
,
2
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
38
,
39
,
41
,
42
]
, the saturating incidence rate
\beta\frac{SI}{1+mI}
with parameter
m>0

[
6
,
8
,
17
,
31
,
34
,
43
,
51
,
63
]
, etc; see also references therein for many other types of nonlinear infection mechanisms.

In this paper, inspired mainly from
[
13
,
29
,
40
]
, we are interested in the dynamical properties of solutions to the following homogeneous Neumann IBVP (initial-boundary value problem) of (possibly) cross-diffusive SIS epidemic model with generalized mass action infection mechanism:

\begin{cases}S_{t}=d_{S}\Delta S+\chi\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)-\beta S^{q}I^{p}+\gamma I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\[7.11317pt]
I_{t}=d_{I}\Delta I+\beta S^{q}I^{p}-(\gamma+\mu)I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\[7.11317pt]
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial I}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>0,\\[7.11317pt]
S(x,0)=S_{0}(x),\ \ I(x,0)=I_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here and below,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}~(n\geq 1)
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. The unknown functions

S=S(x,t)
and
I=I(x,t)
, respectively, denote the population density of susceptible and infected individuals at location
x
and time
t
;
d_{S}
and
d_{I}
are positive constants measuring the random mobility of susceptible and infected populations respectively; the featured cross-diffusion term
\chi\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)
describes the biased movement (widely known as repulsive chemotaxis effect in chemotactic models) of those “smart” susceptible individuals who tend to stay away from regions of higher concentration of infected individuals with nonnegative constant
\chi
measuring the strength of such effect; positive constants
\beta
and
\gamma
, respectively, stand for the disease transmission rate and recovery rate, whereas the parameter
\mu\geq 0
is the disease-induced death rate. The homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions imply there is no population flux across the boundary
\partial\Omega
. As explained in
[
40
]
, we assume within this paper that the initial datum
(S_{0},I_{0})
satisfies

\begin{cases}S_{0}\geq 0,\ I_{0}\geq,\,\not\equiv 0,\ (S_{0},I_{0})\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\times W^{1,r_{0}}(\Omega)\text{ for some }r_{0}>\max\{2,n\},\\[5.69046pt]
\inf_{\Omega}S_{0}>0,\ \ \text{ if }0<q<1,\\[5.69046pt]
\inf_{\Omega}I_{0}>0,\ \ \text{ if }0<p<1.\end{cases}

(1.2)

The PDE model (
1.1
) with
\chi=0
,
q=p=1
and
\mu=0
was proposed and studied by Deng and Wu
[
13
]
, wherein they established the global attractiveness of the DFE (disease-free equilibrium) and EE (endemic equilibrium) in some special cases, and the existence (and uniqueness) of EE in a heterogeneous environment was also discussed when the basic reproduction number is greater than one. The asymptotic behavior of EE with respect to diffusion rates
d_{S}
and
d_{I}
was investigated later in
[
60
]
; see also further development in
[
7
,
52
,
28
]
. Recently, Peng and Wu
[
40
]
studied the reaction-diffusion model (
1.1
) without cross-diffusion (
\chi=0
) and they obtained the global existence and boundedness of classical solutions for any
p,\,q>0
by using a duality argument, and then, they derived long time behaviors of solutions for
\mu>0
and uniform persistence for
\mu=0
upon establishing the ultimately uniform boundedness. More dynamics on many special cases of the reaction-diffusion model (
1.1
) without cross-diffusion can be found in the introduction of
[
40
]
.

Due to the strong coupling in the (highest) 2nd order term, in study of chemotaxis systems, it is well-known that the chemotactic cross-diffusion
\chi\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)
has a strong tendency towards driving the solutions of the underlying models to blow-up in finite or infinite time; see
[
3
,
54
,
56
]
for example. Compared to the extensive literature on various chemotactic systems, it seems that epidemic models with cross-diffusive terms were much less studied. To the best of our knowledge, we are only aware of very few papers
[
16
,
29
,
59
]
dealing with such mathematical models (see
[
4
,
49
,
57
]
, etc, for May-Nowak type cross-diffusive models for virus infection). Moreover, it should be noted that in these works, the infection mechanism is taken to be the standard incidence type of the form
\beta\frac{SI}{S+I}
(in SIS models) or
\beta\frac{SI}{S+I+R}
(in SIRS models). From the mathematical point of view, such frequency-dependent transmission term grows at most linearly in
I
, and thus one can take full advantage of this property to derive not only the
L^{\infty}
-bound of
I
from its
L^{1}
-bound, but also the
L^{\infty}
-bound of
\nabla I
. As a result, global existence and boundedness of solutions can be established in
[
16
,
29
,
59
]
in any spatial dimensions, regardless of the magnitude of
\chi
. Nevertheless, in our cross-diffusive model (
1.1
) with power-like nonlinear incidence
\beta S^{q}I^{p}
, the above strategy is no longer available, and the duality argument used in
[
40
]
also fails because of the presence of the chemotactic cross-diffusion. Even when
p=q=1
, performing similar yet lengthy computations as done in
[
47
,
55
,
61
]
aimed to cancel out the chemotaxis involving term from (
1.1
), we find that

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left[2\beta S(\ln S-1)+2\chi\left|\nabla I^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right]+8\beta d_{S}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla S^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\\
&\quad+4(\gamma+\mu)\chi\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla I^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}+2d_{I}\chi\int_{\Omega}I\left|D^{2}\ln I\right|^{2}\\
&=-2\beta^{2}\int_{\Omega}SI\ln S+2\beta\gamma\int_{\Omega}I\ln S+d_{I}\chi\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{1}{I}\frac{\partial\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}}{\partial\nu}+\beta\chi\int_{\Omega}\frac{S}{I}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(1.3)

Thus, the chemotactic cross-diffusion induced term has been offset but the last term seems uncontrollable in terms of the dissipative terms on the left-hand-side of (
1.3
). Even facing such challenges (strong -no saturation- chemotactic cross-diffusion in the second order term and strong nonlinear coupling in the zeroth order term), we shall employ subtle energy estimates and/or semi-group arguments to bootstrap the easily obtained a priori
L^{1}
-boundedness to
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of classical solutions to the IBVP (
1.1
) under either

np+\left(n-2\right)^{+}q<n+\min\left\{n,\ 2\right\},\ \ \ |\chi|<\chi_{0}\ \text{with}\ \chi_{0}\ \text{defined by}\ \eqref{chi-max},

or

q<\frac{1}{n+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ p+(n+1)q<1+\min\left\{1,\frac{2}{n}\right\},

(1.4)

or

\begin{cases}10q+4p<15,\ \ \ \ q+p<3,&\text{ if }n=1,\\[7.11317pt]
3q+p<3,\ \ \ \ q+p<2,&\text{ if }n=2.\end{cases}

(1.5)

We here underline that, under either (
1.4
) or (
1.5
), boundedness of classical solutions to the IBVP (
1.1
) is ensured without any smallness assumption on the initial data; see Theorems
3.3
,
3.4
and
3.9
for details
.

Going beyond boundedness, the second part of this project is devoted to long-time behaviors of global bounded solutions to the IBVP (
1.1
).
In the presence of mortality, i.e.,
\mu>0
, it is shown in Theorem
4.1
that the usual threshold dynamics for epidemic models in terms of a so-called basic reproduction number do not exist
. More specifically, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, any bounded global solution
(S,I)
converges uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
according to

\left(S(\cdot,t),I(\cdot,t)\right)\rightarrow\begin{cases}(0,0),&\text{ if }0<p<1,\\[5.69046pt]
(S_{\infty},0),&\text{ if }p\geq 1.\end{cases}

Furthermore,
S_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{\gamma+\mu}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
if
p=1
and
(S,I)\rightarrow(S_{\infty},0)
exponentially if
p>1
. Here,
S_{\infty}
is a positive constant and is given implicitly by

S_{\infty}=|\Omega|^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(S_{0}+I_{0}\right)-\mu\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}I\right).

(1.6)

Comparing with
[
40
, Theorem 2.4]
, we see that the dynamics of the cross-diffusive system stay the same as that of the system without cross-diffusion, indicating the effect of chemotaxis is overbalanced by mortality in respective of long time behavior.
Here, we would like to add that, we use a different approach to derive such refined convergence by identifying
S_{\infty}
in (
1.6
) and showing that the convergence is exponential when
p>1
.

In the absence of mortality and of cross-diffusion, i.e.,

and

The remainder of this project is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present some preliminary results and the local well-posedness of our model. Section 3 is devoted to the global existence and boundedness of solutions where we prove a couple of results based on different approaches. Finally, in the last section, we shall investigate the large-time convergence of bounded and global classical solutions.

Let us start with the elementary Young’s inequality with epsilon: Let

ab\leq\epsilon a^{r}+\frac{b^{s}}{(\epsilon r)^{\frac{s}{r}}},\quad\quad\forall\,a,b\geq 0.

Lemma 2.1

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality
[

36
]
)
.

Let

, and let

\frac{1}{m}-\frac{l}{n}=a\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{k}{n}\right)+(1-a)\frac{1}{r}.

Then, for any

, there exist a positive constant

depending only on

and

such that

\|D^{l}f\|_{L^{m}}\leq C\left(\|D^{k}f\|_{L^{s}}^{a}\|f\|_{L^{r}}^{1-a}+\|f\|_{L^{r}}\right),

with the following exception: if

and

.

Proof.

In view of the variation-of-constants formula, we have

0\leq I(\cdot,t)\leq e^{td_{I}(\Delta-C_{0})}I_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}(\Delta-C_{0})}(\beta S^{q}I^{p})(\cdot,\tau)d\tau,

where
C_{0}>0
is such that
\gamma+\mu\geq C_{0}
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
t>0
.
Next, we apply (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and the
L^{r}
-
L^{s}
-smoothing properties of the Neumann semigroup
\{e^{td_{I}\Delta}\}_{t\geq 0}
in Lemma
2.2
along with the maximum principle to derive, for
t\in(0,T)
, that

\begin{split}0\leq I(\cdot,t)&\leq\left\|e^{td_{I}(\Delta-C_{0})}I_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}(\Delta-C_{0})}\left(\beta S^{q}I^{p}\right)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\\
&\leq\|I_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\right]e^{-C_{0}(t-\tau)}\left\|(S^{q}I^{p})(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{m}}d\tau.\end{split}

(3.6)

We observe from (
2.3
), (
3.2
) and the definition of
N
in (
3.3
) that, for
\tau\in(0,T)
,

\begin{split}&\|\beta(S^{q}I^{p})(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{m}}\leq\beta^{0}M^{q}\|I(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{pm}}^{p}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}\beta^{0}M^{q}\|S_{0}+I_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\min\{1,p\}}N^{(p-1)^{+}},&\text{if }n=1,\\[7.11317pt]
\beta^{0}M^{q}\|S_{0}+I_{0}\|_{L^{1}},&\text{if }p<\frac{2}{n},\ n\geq 2,\\[7.11317pt]
\beta^{0}M^{q}\|S_{0}+I_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{m}}N^{p-\frac{1}{m}},&\text{if }\frac{2}{n}\leq p<1+\frac{2}{n},\ n\geq 2.\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.7)

Thus, we infer from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) that

L\leq\begin{cases}C_{2}\left(1+M^{q}\right),&\text{if }\left\{p\leq 1,\ n=1\right\}\text{ or }\left\{p<\frac{2}{n},\ n\geq 2\right\},\\[7.11317pt]
C_{2}\left(1+M^{q}L^{p-\frac{1}{m}}\right),&\text{if }\left\{1<p<2,\ n=1\right\}\text{ or }\left\{p\geq\frac{2}{n},\ n\geq 2\right\}.\end{cases}

(3.8)

In the first case of (
3.8
), observe that
pm=1
and (
3.3
) holds automatically, whereas in the second scenario, notice that the choice of
m
in (
3.2
) implies
p-\frac{1}{m}<1
, and then we use Young’s inequality with epsilon to deduce from the second case of (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}+C_{2}M^{q}L^{p-\frac{1}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}+\frac{1}{2}L+\frac{(m+1-pm)}{m}\left(\frac{2(pm-1)}{m}\right)^{\frac{pm-1}{m+1-pm}}\left(C_{2}M^{q}\right)^{\frac{m}{m+1-pm}},

which quickly gives rise to our desired estimate (
3.3
).

Next, by the variation-of-constants formula, it holds

\displaystyle I(\cdot,t)=e^{td_{I}\Delta}I_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}\Delta}(\beta S^{q}I^{p})(\cdot,\tau)d\tau-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}\Delta}((\gamma+\mu)I)(\cdot,\tau)d\tau.

We take the gradient and then use the
L^{r}
-
L^{s}
-estimates of the Neumann semigroup
\{e^{td_{I}\Delta}\}_{t\geq 0}
in Lemma
2.2
and (
3.3
) to derive

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla I(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{r_{0}}}&\leq\left\|\nabla e^{td_{I}\Delta}I_{0}\right\|_{L^{r_{0}}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}\Delta}(\beta S^{q}I^{p})(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{r_{0}}}d\tau\\
&\ \ +\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}\Delta}((\gamma+\mu)I)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{r_{0}}}d\tau\\
&\leq\|I_{0}\|_{W^{1,r_{0}}}+C_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)}\right]e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-\tau)}\left\|(S^{q}I^{p})(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{s}}d\tau\\
&\ \ +C_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)}\right]e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-\tau)}\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{s}}d\tau\\
&\leq C_{4}\left[1+M^{q}L^{\left(p-\frac{1}{s}\right)^{+}}+L^{\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right)^{+}}\right]\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+\tau^{-1+\frac{\zeta}{2}}\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}\tau}d\tau,\end{split}

which along with (
3.3
) immediately shows (
3.5
).
∎

With such preparations, we now close the loop in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
T\in(0,T_{m})
, and suppose that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold. Then, for
r>\frac{n}{2}
and
r\geq 1
, one can find constant
K_{3}>\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
independent of
M
and
L
such that

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq K_{3}\Bigg\{1+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ +M|\chi|\left[1+M^{q+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{+}}+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)}\right]\Bigg\}.\end{split}

(3.9)

Proof.

We first rewrite the
S
-equation via the semigroup representation as

S(t)=e^{td_{S}\Delta}S_{0}+\chi\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}\Delta}\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)d_{I}\Delta}\left(-\beta S^{q}I^{p}+\gamma I\right)d\tau.

Then we use (
3.1
), (
3.3
) and the well-known
L^{r}
-
L^{s}
-smoothing properties of the Neumann semigroup
\{e^{td_{I}\Delta}\}_{t\geq 0}
along with the maximum principle to deduce, for
t\in(0,T)
, that

\begin{split}S(\cdot,t)&\leq\left\|e^{td_{S}\Delta}S_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+|\chi|\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-\tau)d_{S}\Delta}\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\\
&\ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-\tau)d_{S}\Delta}\left(\gamma I\right)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\\
&\leq\left\|S_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{1}|\chi|\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2r_{0}}}\right]e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-\tau)}\left\|(S\nabla I)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{r_{0}}}d\tau\\
&\ \ \ +C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2r}}\right]e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-\tau)}\left\|I(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{r}}d\tau\\
&\leq\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{3}|\chi|M\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|\nabla I(\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{r_{0}}}+C_{4}N^{1-\frac{1}{r}},\end{split}

(3.10)

which along with (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) with
s=r_{0}
yields the estimate (
3.9
).
∎

Now, we are ready to establish our first result on the well-posedness of system (
1.1
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (n\geq 1)
be a bounded and smooth domain. Assume that

np+(n-2)^{+}q<n+\min\left\{n,\ 2\right\}.

(3.11)

Then, for any initial data
(S_{0},I_{0})
fulfilling (
1.2
), there exists a positive constant
\chi_{0}>0
(cf. (
3.14
) below) such that whenever
|\chi|<\chi_{0}
, the corresponding classical solution to the IBVP (
1.1
) exists globally in time and is uniformly bounded in the sense there exists a positive constant
C=C(S_{0},I_{0},\chi,\Omega,p,q,\beta,\gamma,\mu)>0
such that

\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,r_{0}}}\leq C,\ \ \ \forall\,t>0.

(3.12)

Proof.

Based on (
3.2
) and (
3.11
), we specify
m
and
r
as follows:

\begin{cases}m=\max\left\{1,\ \frac{1}{p}\right\},\ \ r=1,&\text{if }n=1,\\[5.69046pt]
m=\frac{1}{p},\ \ r=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{n}{2}+\min\left\{\frac{q}{(q-1)^{+}},\ n\right\}\right],&\text{if }n\geq 2,\ p<\frac{2}{n},\\[5.69046pt]
m=\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon},\ \ r=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{n}{2}+\min\left\{\frac{q}{\left[q-\left(1+\frac{2}{n}-p\right)+\epsilon\right]^{+}},\ n\right\}\right],&\text{if }n\geq 2,\ \frac{2}{n}\leq p<1+\frac{2}{n},\end{cases}

where the positive constant
\epsilon
is given by

\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\frac{2}{n},\ 1+\frac{2}{n}-p-\left(1-\frac{2}{n}\right)q\right\}>0,\ \ n\geq 2.

Then by plain verifications we find that both
m
and
r
satisfy the conditions set forth in Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, and moreover that

\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)<1.

(3.13)

For those geometrical constants
K_{i}
depending on the initial data and
\Omega
provided by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, thanks to (
3.13
), we first define a positive and finite constant
\chi_{0}
by

\begin{split}\chi_{0}&=\sup_{z>0}\frac{z-K_{3}\left[1+z^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\right]}{K_{3}z\left[1+z^{q+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{+}}+z^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)}\right]}\\
&\ \ =\frac{M-K_{3}\left[1+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\right]}{K_{3}M\left[1+M^{q+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{+}}+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)}\right]}\in(0,\infty)\end{split}

(3.14)

for some
M>K_{3}
. This, along with the fact
K_{3}\geq\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, necessarily implies that

\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<M,\quad\mbox{and}\quad K_{3}\left(1+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\right)<M.

(3.15)

Now, for
\chi\in[-\chi_{0},\chi_{0}]
, we let
(S,I)
be the corresponding maximally extended solution of the IBVP (
1.1
) on
\Omega\times(0,T_{m})
with
T_{m}\in(0,\infty]
. Thanks to Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we next employ a standard continuity argument to close the loop-argument and then conclude global existence (i.e.,
T_{m}=\infty
) and boundedness (
3.12
). To this end, we define

\mathcal{U}:=\Bigl\{(0,T_{0})\subset(0,T_{m}):\ \|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M,\ \forall\,t\in(0,T_{0})\Bigr\}.

(3.16)

Then the continuity of
S
and (
3.15
) imply that
\mathcal{U}
is nonempty and so
T=\sup\mathcal{U}
is well-defined and
T\in(0,\infty]
. Furthermore, by (
3.9
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), we infer that

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq K_{3}\left[1+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\right]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ +K_{3}M|\chi|\left[1+M^{q+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)^{+}}+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{0}}\right)}\right]\\
&\leq M.\end{split}

(3.17)

Finally, combining (
3.17
), (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) and the extensibility criterion in (
2.2
) of Lemma
2.3
, we must have that
T_{m}=\infty
and and then we achieve our claimed uniform
(L^{\infty},W^{1,r_{0}})
-boundedness of
(S,I)
on
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
.
∎

3.2.
Large
\chi
-boundedness and global existence

In this subsection, we shall present two types of large

-boundedness of solutions to (

) via mainly semi-group method and energy estimate method as illustrated in Subsections 3.2.1 and 3.2.2.

3.2.1.
Large
\chi
-boundedness in any dimensions with

restrictive

We first use arguments in sprit similar to Subsection 3.1 to obtain the following large
\chi
-boundedness in any dimensions while with more restrictive
p
and

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\ (n\geq 1)
be a bounded and smooth domain. Assume that

q<\frac{1}{n+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\ p+(n+1)q<1+\min\left\{1,\frac{2}{n}\right\}.

(3.18)

Then, for any initial data
(S_{0},I_{0})
fulfilling (
1.2
), the corresponding classical solution to the IBVP (
1.1
) exists globally in time and is uniformly bounded in the sense of (
3.12
).

Proof.

Taking
r_{0}=\infty
and
s=\frac{n}{1-\zeta}
in (
3.4
) with
\zeta\in(0,1]
, we get from (
3.5
) that

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|\nabla I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K_{\zeta}\left[1+M^{q+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}}+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}}\right].

(3.19)

With this, for any
r>\frac{n}{2}
and
r\geq 1
, we re-bound (
3.10
) as follows:

\begin{split}S(\cdot,t)&\leq\left\|e^{td_{S}\Delta}S_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+|\chi|\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-\tau)d_{S}\Delta}\nabla\cdot(S\nabla I)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\\
&\ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-\tau)d_{S}\Delta}\left(\gamma I\right)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\\
&\leq\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{1}|\chi|\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{4r}}\right]e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-\tau)}\left\|(S\nabla I)(\cdot,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2r}}d\tau\\
&\ \ \ +C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2r}}\right]e^{-\lambda_{1}(t-\tau)}\|I(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{r}}d\tau\\
&\leq\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{3}|\chi|M^{1-\frac{1}{2r}}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|\nabla I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{4}N^{1-\frac{1}{r}},\end{split}

(3.20)

which along with (
3.19
) and (
3.3
) allows us to infer

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq C_{5}\Bigg[1+|\chi|M^{1-\frac{1}{2r}}+|\chi|M^{q+1-\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}}\\
&\ \ \ +|\chi|M^{1-\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}}+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\Bigg].\end{split}

(3.21)

Now, we observe that (
3.18
) implies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}q+1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{q\frac{n}{2}}{\frac{n}{2}+1-p\frac{n}{2}}\left(p-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\\[8.5359pt]
1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{q\frac{n}{2}}{\frac{n}{2}+1-p\frac{n}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\\[8.5359pt]
\frac{q\frac{n}{2}}{\frac{n}{2}+1-p\frac{n}{2}}\left(1-\frac{2}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\end{array}\right.

which upon continuity allows us to fix
0<\epsilon<\min\left\{\frac{2}{n},\ 1+\frac{2}{n}-p\right\}
(cf. (
3.2
)) such that

\begin{cases}q+1-\frac{1}{n+\epsilon}+\frac{q\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}}{\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}+1-p\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}}\left(p-\frac{1-\epsilon}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\\[7.11317pt]
1-\frac{1}{n+\epsilon}+\frac{q\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}}{\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}+1-p\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}}\left(1-\frac{1-\epsilon}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\\[7.11317pt]
\frac{q\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}}{\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}+1-p\frac{n}{2-n\epsilon}}\left(1-\frac{2}{n+\epsilon}\right)^{+}<1.\end{cases}

(3.22)

Based on (
3.18
) and (
3.21
), we first choose
m
,
r
and
\zeta
as follows:

\begin{cases}m=\frac{1}{p},\ \ r=1,\ \ \zeta=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{1-p,\ \frac{1}{2q}\right\},&\text{if }n=1,\ p<1,\\[5.69046pt]
m=1,\ \ r=1,\ \ \zeta=\frac{2-p-2q}{3q},&\text{if }n=1,\ p\geq 1,\\[5.69046pt]
m=\frac{1}{p},\ r=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n}{2q[(np-1)^{+}+n]}\right\},&\text{if }n\geq 2,\ p<\frac{2}{n},\\[5.69046pt]
m=\frac{n}{2-n\zeta},\ r=\frac{n+\zeta}{2},\ \zeta=\epsilon,&\text{if }n\geq 2,\ p\geq\frac{2}{n}.\end{cases}

(3.23)

In the third case of (
3.23
),
\zeta>0
is chosen so that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}<0,&\text{if }p<\frac{1}{n},\\[7.11317pt]
\left(np-1+n+\zeta\right)\left[\frac{q}{2}+\frac{1}{2(np-1+n)}\right]<1,&\text{if }p\geq\frac{1}{n},\end{array}\right.

which is feasible since

\displaystyle\left(np-1+n\right)\left[\frac{q}{2}+\frac{1}{2(np-1+n)}\right]=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{q}{2}(np-1+n)<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{q}{2}(n+1)<1,

for
p\geq\frac{1}{n}
and
(p,q)
fulfilling (
3.18
).
With such chosen
m
,
r
and
\zeta
, we then compute from (
3.21
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
) that

\begin{cases}q+1-\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\\[5.69046pt]
1-\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}<1,\\[5.69046pt]
\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)<1.\end{cases}

(3.24)

In light of (
3.24
), one can easily use Young’s inequality with epsilon to (
3.21
) to derive the existence of
M=M(S_{0},I_{0},\beta,\gamma,\mu,\Omega,\chi,,p,q)>\|S_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq C_{5}\Bigr[1+|\chi|M^{1-\frac{1}{2r}}+|\chi|M^{q+1-\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(p-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}}\\
&\ \ \quad+|\chi|M^{1-\frac{1}{2r}+\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\zeta}{n}\right)^{+}}+M^{\frac{qm}{m+1-pm}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\Bigr]\\
&\leq M.\end{split}

With this, we see the set defined by (

) is a nonempty and both open and closed set, and thus, using similar continuity arguments as done in Theorem

, we readily obtain that

and that
(S,I)
is uniform-in-time bounded according to (
3.12
).
∎

3.2.2.
Large
\chi
-boundedness in lower dimentions

In this subsection, we shall employ coupled subtle energy estimates to improve the boundedness range in (

) for

.

To move from

-boundness obtained in (
2.3
) to higher order regularity for the local solution
(S,I)
of (

), we begin to study the time evolutions of
\|(S+1)\ln(S+1)\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
; upon integration by parts on (
1.1
), we find the following energy identities.

Lemma 3.5
.

The local-in-time solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(S+1)\ln(S+1)+4d_{S}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}+\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}[\ln(S+1)+1]\\
&\ \ =\chi\int_{\Omega}[S-\ln(S+1)]\Delta I+\gamma\int_{\Omega}I[\ln(S+1)+1]\end{split}

(3.25)

and

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+(\gamma+\mu)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+d_{I}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}=-\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}\Delta I.

(3.26)

To bound the bad terms on the right-hand sides of (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) in terms of the dissipation terms on their left-hand sides, based on the
L^{1}
-boundedness of
S+I
in (
2.3
), we employ the G-N inequality (cf. Lemma
2.1
) to deduce the following estimates.

Lemma 3.6
.

(i)
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{1}
be a finite interval. Then, for
r\in(1,\infty]
and
\epsilon>0
, it holds

\begin{cases}\left\|S+1\right\|_{L^{r}}^{\frac{2r}{r-1}}\leq C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,\\[7.11317pt]
\forall s<\frac{2r}{r-1},\ \ \left\|S+1\right\|_{L^{r}}^{s}\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon},\\[7.11317pt]
\left\|(S+1)\ln(S+1)\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\end{cases}

(3.27)

and

\begin{cases}\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{\frac{5r}{r-1}}\leq C\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,\\[7.11317pt]
\forall s<\frac{5r}{r-1},\ \ \|I\|_{L^{r}}^{s}\leq\epsilon\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}.\end{cases}

(3.28)

(ii)
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be bounded and smooth. Then, for
r\in(1,\infty)
and
\epsilon>0
, it holds

\begin{cases}\|S+1\|_{L^{r}}^{\frac{r}{r-1}}\leq C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,\\[7.11317pt]
\forall s<\frac{r}{r-1},\ \ \|S+1\|_{L^{r}}^{s}\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon},\\[7.11317pt]
\|(S+1)\ln(S+1)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\end{cases}

(3.29)

and, for
r\in(1,\infty]
and
\epsilon>0
, it holds

\begin{cases}\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{\frac{3r}{r-1}}\leq C\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,\\[7.11317pt]
\forall s<\frac{3r}{r-1},\ \ \|I\|_{L^{r}}^{s}\leq\epsilon\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}.\end{cases}

(3.30)

Proof.

(i) By the
L^{1}
-bound of
S
in (

), we apply the 1-D G-N inequality as stated in Lemma
2.1
to infer, for

\displaystyle\|S+1\|_{L^{r}}^{s}

\displaystyle=\left\|(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2r}}^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(r-1)s}{r}}\left\|(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(r+1)s}{r}}+C\left\|(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{(r-1)s}{r}}+C

\displaystyle\begin{cases}=C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,&\text{ if }s=\frac{2r}{r-1},\\[7.11317pt]
\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon},&\text{ if }s<\frac{2r}{r-1}.\end{cases}

This, together with the simple algebraic fact that

\|(S+1)\ln(S+1)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|S+1\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{\frac{3}{2}},

(3.31)

shows the desired estimates stated in (

).

As for the bounds for

guaranteed by (

), we use the 1-D G-N inequality to derive, for

\begin{split}\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{s}&\leq C\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(r-1)s}{5r}}\left\|I\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{(3r+2)s}{5r}}+C\|I\|_{L^{1}}^{s}\\
&\leq C\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(r-1)s}{5r}}+C\\
&\begin{cases}=C\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,&\text{ if }s=\frac{5r}{r-1},\\[7.11317pt]
\leq\epsilon\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon},&\text{ if }s<\frac{5r}{r-1}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.32)

In a similar manner, we also have, for bounded smooth

,

\|I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\epsilon\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon}.

On the other hand, for bounded smooth

\|D^{2}I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.34)

Joining this with (

).

(ii) By the

-bound of
S
in (
2.3

to obatin, for

,

\displaystyle\|S+1\|_{L^{r}}^{s}

\displaystyle=\left\|(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2r}}^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(r-1)s}{r}}\left\|(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2s}{r}}+C\left\|(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2s}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(r-1)s}{r}}+C

\displaystyle\begin{cases}=C\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,&\text{ if }s=\frac{r}{r-1},\\[7.11317pt]
\leq\epsilon\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon},&\text{ if }s<\frac{r}{r-1}.\end{cases}

This together with the simple fact (
3.31
) entails the desired estimates stated in (
3.29
).

To achieve the bounds for

\displaystyle\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(r-1)s}{3r}}+C

\displaystyle\begin{cases}=C\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C,&\text{ if }s=\frac{3r}{r-1},\\[7.11317pt]
\leq\epsilon\left\|D^{2}I\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon},&\text{ if }s<\frac{3r}{r-1}.\end{cases}

This along with (

) and (

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
q
and
p
satisfy

\begin{cases}10q+4p<15,\ \ \ \ q+p<3,&\text{ if }n=1,\\[7.11317pt]
3q+p<3,\ \ \ \ q+p<2,&\text{ if }n=2.\end{cases}

(3.35)

Then the local-in-time solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

\|(S+1)\ln(S+1)\|_{L^{1}}+\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,\quad\quad\forall\,t\in(0,T_{m}).

(3.36)

Moreover, there exists
C>0
such that

\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,\quad\quad\forall\,t\in(0,T_{m}).

(3.37)

Proof.

Due to the
L^{1}
-boundedness of
S+I
, Lemma
3.6
and (
3.33
), we bound the bad terms on the right-hand sides of (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) in terms of the dissipation terms on their left-hand sides in the following manners:

\begin{split}\chi\int_{\Omega}[S-\ln(S+1)]\Delta I&\leq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}[S-\ln(S+1)]^{2}+\frac{\chi^{2}}{4\epsilon}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}(S+1)^{2}+\frac{\chi^{2}}{4\epsilon}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}\\
&\leq C\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}+C\epsilon+\frac{\chi^{2}}{4\epsilon}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2},\end{split}

(3.38)

\begin{split}\gamma\int_{\Omega}I[\ln(S+1)+1]&\leq\gamma\int_{\Omega}\ln^{2}(S+1)+\gamma\int_{\Omega}I^{2}+\gamma\int_{\Omega}I\\
&\leq\gamma\int_{\Omega}(S+I)+\gamma\int_{\Omega}I^{2}\\
&\leq\gamma\int_{\Omega}(S_{0}+I_{0})+\gamma\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+\gamma C_{\epsilon}\end{split}

(3.39)

and

-\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}\Delta I\leq\frac{d_{I}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{d_{I}}\int_{\Omega}S^{2q}I^{2p}.

(3.40)

In the sequel, we apply Hölder inequality and Young’s inequality with epsilon to estimate the second integral on the right-hand of (
3.40
) as follows: In the simple case of

q+p\leq\frac{1}{2},

(3.41)

we have from Hölder inequality that

\int_{\Omega}S^{2q}I^{2p}\leq\|S\|_{L^{1}}^{2q}\|I\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{1-2q}}}^{2p}\leq\|S\|_{L^{1}}^{2q}\|I\|_{L^{1}}^{2p}|\Omega|^{(1-2q-2p)}\leq\|S_{0}+I_{0}\|_{L^{1}}|\Omega|^{(1-2q-2p)}.

(3.42)

In the opposite scenario of (
3.41
), we break our argument into two cases:

Case of
n=1
. Because of (
3.35
), we can take
r\in(1,\infty)
such that

\max\left\{1,\ \ 2p,\ \ \frac{6p}{15-10q-4p}\right\}<r<\min\left\{\frac{2p}{(2p-5)^{+}},\ \ \frac{2p}{(1-2q)^{+}}\right\},

which imply

r>1,\ r>2p,\ 2p<\frac{5r}{r-1},\ \frac{2qr}{r-2p}=:s>1,\ 2q<\frac{10qr}{5r-2p(r-1)}<\frac{2s}{s-1};

with such chosen
r
, we utilize Hölder and Young’s inequality with epsilon, (
3.27
) and (
3.28
) to estimate, for any
\epsilon,\eta>0
, that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}S^{2q}I^{2p}&\leq\left\|S+1\right\|_{L^{\frac{2qr}{r-2p}}}^{2q}\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{2p}\\
&\leq\epsilon\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{\frac{5r}{r-1}}+C_{\epsilon}\left\|S+1\right\|_{L^{\frac{2qr}{r-2p}}}^{\frac{10qr}{5r-2p(r-1)}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon+\eta\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon,\eta}.\end{split}

(3.43)

Case of
n=2
. In this case, under (
3.35
), we will show that (
3.43
) type estimate also holds. Indeed, because of (
3.35
), we can take
r\in(1,\infty)
such that

\max\left\{1,\ \ 2p,\ \ \frac{2p}{3-3q-p}\right\}<r<\min\left\{\frac{2p}{(2p-3)^{+}},\ \ \frac{2p}{(1-2q)^{+}}\right\},

which imply

r>1,\ \ r>2p,\ \ 2p<\frac{3r}{r-1},\ \ \frac{2qr}{r-2p}=:s>1,\ \ 2q<\frac{6qr}{3r-2p(r-1)}<\frac{s}{s-1};

with such chosen
r
, we utilize Hölder and Young’s inequality with epsilon, (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) to infer, for any
\epsilon,\eta>0
, that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}S^{2q}I^{2p}&\leq\|S+1\|_{L^{\frac{2qr}{r-2p}}}^{2q}\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{2p}\\
&\leq\epsilon\|I\|_{L^{r}}^{\frac{3r}{r-1}}+C_{\epsilon}\|S+1\|_{L^{\frac{2qr}{r-2p}}}^{\frac{6qr}{3r-2p(r-1)}}\\
&\leq C\epsilon\|\Delta I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon\|\nabla I\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon+\eta\left\|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\epsilon,\eta}.\end{split}

(3.44)

In summary, we substitute (
3.38
), (
3.39
), (
3.40
), (
3.43
), (
3.42
) and (
3.44
) into (
3.25
) and (

) to conclude, for any

, there exist constants

such that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(S+1)\ln(S+1)+3d_{S}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\leq\gamma\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+M\chi^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}+M_{\epsilon},

(3.45)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+(\gamma+\mu)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+d_{I}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}\leq L\eta\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}+L_{\eta}.

(3.46)

Multiplying (
3.45
) by
d_{I}
and (
3.46
) by
2M\chi^{2}
and then choosing sufficiently small
\epsilon
and
\eta
and then using Lemma

, we finally end up with, for some

,

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left[d_{I}(S+1)\ln(S+1)+2M\chi^{2}|\nabla I|^{2}\right]+d_{I}\int_{\Omega}(S+1)\ln(S+1)\\
&\ \ +(\gamma+\mu)M\chi^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}+d_{S}d_{I}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(S+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}+d_{I}M\chi^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta I|^{2}\leq K.\end{split}

Solving this standard Grownall inequality, we directly obtain the uniform estimate (
3.36
).

In the case of
n=1
, by (

) and (

), we know that the

-norm of

is uniformly bounded, and thus (

) follows from the continuous embedding of

into

.

Next, in the case of

\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{q},\ \ \ \forall\,t\in(0,T_{m}).

(3.47)

To show the

-boundedness of
I
, we use semi-group representation to rewrite

as

I=e^{t(d_{I}\Delta-(\gamma+\mu))}I_{0}+\beta\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)(d_{I}\Delta-(\gamma+\mu))}S^{q}I^{p}ds.

(3.48)

Based on the

-

, the

, (

) and the fact that

\displaystyle\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{t(d_{I}\Delta-(\gamma+\mu))}I_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\beta\int_{0}^{t}\left\|e^{(t-s)(d_{I}\Delta-(\gamma+\mu))}S^{q}I^{p}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|I_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-s)^{-\frac{2q}{q+1}}\right]e^{-(\gamma+\mu)(t-s)}\left\|S^{q}I^{p}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q+1}{2q}}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C+C\int_{0}^{t}\left[1+(t-s)^{-\frac{2q}{q+1}}\right]e^{-(\gamma+\mu)(t-s)}\|S^{q}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|I^{p}\|_{L^{\frac{q+1}{(1-q)q}}}ds

which precisely gives rise to (

).
∎

Based on the basic but crucial starting boundedness provided in Lemma

Lemma 3.8
.

Let (
3.35
) hold. Then the local-in-time solution of (

) satisfies

\|S(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{4}}\leq C,\quad\quad\forall\,t\in(0,T_{m}).

(3.49)

Proof.

We test the
S
-equation in (
1.1
) by
S
and then integrate by parts to derive that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}S^{2}+d_{S}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}+\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{1+q}I^{p}

\displaystyle=-\chi\int_{\Omega}S\nabla S\cdot\nabla I+\gamma\int_{\Omega}SI

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d_{S}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}+\frac{\chi^{2}}{d_{S}}\int_{\Omega}S^{2}|\nabla I|^{2}+\gamma\|I\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega}S,

which, together with (
2.3
) and (
3.37
), gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}S^{2}+\frac{3d_{S}}{4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}+\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{1+q}I^{p}\leq\frac{\chi^{2}}{d_{S}}\int_{\Omega}S^{2}|\nabla I|^{2}+C_{1}.

(3.50)

Next, taking gradient of the
I
-equation and then multiplying it by
\nabla I|\nabla I|^{2}
and integrating by parts, we conclude that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{4}+d_{I}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right|^{2}+2d_{I}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\left|D^{2}I\right|^{2}+2(\gamma+\mu)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla I\right|^{4}\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}-2\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}\Delta I|\nabla I|^{2}-2\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}\nabla I\cdot\nabla|\nabla I|^{2}.\end{split}

(3.51)

In the above, we have used the identity

2\nabla I\cdot\nabla\Delta I=\Delta|\nabla I|^{2}-2\left|D^{2}I\right|^{2},

where
\left|D^{2}I\right|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}I}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right|^{2}

For the boundary integral in (
3.51

[

48

\begin{split}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}&\leq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right|^{2}+C_{\epsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{2}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq\epsilon\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right|^{2}+C_{\epsilon},\quad\quad\forall\epsilon>0.\end{split}

(3.52)

For the remaining terms in (
3.51

\begin{split}&-2\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}\Delta I|\nabla I|^{2}-2\beta\int_{\Omega}S^{q}I^{p}\nabla I\cdot\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{2d_{I}}{n}\int_{\Omega}\left|\Delta I\right|^{2}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}+\frac{n\beta^{2}}{2d_{I}}\|I\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}\int_{\Omega}S^{2q}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\\
&\ +\frac{d_{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right|^{2}+\frac{2\beta^{2}}{d_{I}}\|I\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}\int_{\Omega}S^{2q}\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\\

(3.53)

Now, we claim, for
n=1,2
and for any

(3.56)

Case of

. The Young’s inequality with epsilon entails

(3.57)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla I\right|^{6}=\left\|\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{3}}^{3}&\leq C_{5}\left(\left\|\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{3}\right)\\

Using the 2-D G-N inequality and noting that

) and (

) holds for

), (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla I|^{4}+\frac{d_{I}}{3}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left|\nabla I\right|^{2}\right|^{2}+2(\gamma+\mu)\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla I\right|^{4}\leq\eta\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}+C_{\eta}.

(3.60)

Now, we shall estimate the first item on the right-hand-side of (

). The case of

, instead of (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}S^{3}\leq C_{7}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}+C_{7}.

(3.61)

Then, in light of (

Theorem 4.1

) and applying (
4.6
) and (
4.8
), we obtain the implicit formula for
S_{*}
in (
4.3
).

(iv) We shall show that the convergence rate of
\left(S,I\right)\to(S_{*},0)
is exponential in the case of
p>1
. Since
I
converges uniformly to zero, the above arguments indeed have shown (cf. (
4.13
), (
4.15
) and (
4.16
)), for some
C_{5},\,C_{6}>0
, that

\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C_{5}e^{-C_{6}t},\ \ t\geq T_{1}.

(4.18)

This along with (
4.7
), (
4.4
) and (
4.3
) allows us to deduce that

\left|\overline{S}-S_{*}\right|=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\left|\mu\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}I-\int_{\Omega}I\right|\leq C_{7}e^{-C_{6}t},\ \ \ \ t\geq T_{1}.

(4.19)

Now, testing the
S
-equation in (
1.1
) by
(S-S_{*})
and using the boundedness of
(S,I)
, we find that there exists some
C_{8}>0
fulfilling

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left(S-S_{*}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=-d_{S}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}-\chi\int_{\Omega}S\nabla I\cdot\nabla S+\int_{\Omega}\left(\gamma-\beta S^{q}I^{p-1}\right)\left(S-S_{*}\right)I

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{d_{S}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}+C_{8}\|\nabla I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C_{8}\|I(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad\forall\,t\geq T_{1},

which along with (
4.11
), (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) enables us to derive a differential inequality for
\|S-S_{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
as follows: for some
a,\,\delta,\,C_{9}>0
with
\delta<a
,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left(S-S_{*}\right)^{2}+a\int_{\Omega}\left(S-S_{*}\right)^{2}\leq C_{9}e^{-\delta t},\ \ t\geq T_{1}.

This directly entails

\int_{\Omega}\left(S(\cdot,t)-S_{*}\right)^{2}\leq e^{-a(t-T_{1})}\int_{\Omega}\left(S(\cdot,T_{1})-S_{*}\right)^{2}+\frac{C_{9}}{a-\delta}e^{-\delta t},\ \ t\geq T_{1},

which, in conjunction with (
4.12
) and (
4.18
), yields our desired exponential convergence of
(S,I)
to
(S_{*},0)
.
The proof is thus completed.
∎

4.2.
Without cross-diffusion
\chi=0
and without mortality
\mu=0

In this subsection, we shall always assume that
\chi=0
and
\mu=0
. That is, we focus on the following reaction-diffusion SIS epidemic model

\begin{cases}S_{t}=d_{S}\Delta S-\beta S^{q}I^{p}+\gamma I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\[3.0pt]
I_{t}=d_{I}\Delta I+\beta S^{q}I^{p}-\gamma I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\[3.0pt]
\frac{\partial S}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial I}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>0,\\[3.0pt]
S(x,0)=S_{0}(x),\ \ I(x,0)=I_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.20)

From now on, we no longer assume that transmission rate
\beta
and recovery rate
\gamma
and positive constants.
Instead, we take spatial heterogeneity into consideration. That is, we assume
\beta(x)
and
\gamma(x)
are positive
C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
-smooth functions for some
\alpha>0
.
According to
[
40
, Theorem 2.3]
, the model (
4.20
) admits a unique global and bounded solution
(S,I)
for any
p,\,q>0
with
S(x,t),\,I(x,t)>0
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
t>0
. As a result, (
4.1
) still holds.
In addition, it is easily seen that the total population number is conserved:

\int_{\Omega}(S+I)=\int_{\Omega}(S_{0}+I_{0})=:N>0,\quad\forall\,t>0.

(4.21)

Concerning the long-time behavior of solutions, in the case of
p\in(0,1)
, the authors in
[
40
]
only showed the uniform persistence of solutions; if
p=1
, a basic reproduction number
\mathcal{R}_{0}
can be defined and the uniform persistence property still holds if
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
; if
p>1
, their analysis about the long-time behavior of the ODE version of (
4.20
) suggests that its dynamics depend on the initial data. To the best of our knowledge, we are not aware of any further results on the global attractivity of disease-free equilibrium (DFE) or endemic equilibrium (EE) for the reaction-diffusion system (
4.20
) presented in literature, although there are some studies
[
20
,
23
]
on the S(E)IR type ODE and PDE models with varying total population. Here, for
0<p\leq 1
, we shall investigate the global asymptotic stability of DFE or EE in two special cases: (i)
\gamma(x)=r\beta(x)
for some constant
r>0
and all
x\in\Omega
, and (ii)
d_{S}=d_{I}
.

We first consider the case of
0<p<1
.

Let
q,\,r>0
. For
S\in\left[0,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right]
, we define a function

f(S)=r\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-S\right)^{1-p}-S^{q}.

Then it can be readily checked that
f(0)>0
and
f\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)<0
. Moreover,
f^{\prime}(S)<0
for all
S\in\left(0,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)
. Consequently, there exists a unique
S^{*}\in\left(0,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)
satisfying

r\left(I^{*}\right)^{1-p}:=r\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-S^{*}\right)^{1-p}=(S^{*})^{q}.

(4.22)

Obviously,
(S^{*},I^{*})
is an EE of (
4.20
) if
\frac{\gamma(x)}{\beta(x)}\equiv r
for all
x\in\Omega
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
0<p<1
. Assume there exists some
r>0
such that
\gamma(x)=r\beta(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
. Then, as
t\to\infty
, the unique global and bounded solution
(S,I)
of the reaction-diffusion model (
4.20
) satisfies

\left(S(x,t),I(x,t)\right)\to(S^{*},I^{*}),

uniformly for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, where
(S^{*},I^{*})
is defined by (
4.22
).

Proof.

Suppose
q\neq 1
.
We define the following Lyapunov functional

V_{1}(S,I)=\int_{\Omega}\left\{\left[\left(S-S^{*}\right)-\frac{(S^{*})^{q}}{1-q}\left(S^{1-q}-(S^{*})^{1-q}\right)\right]+\left[(I-I^{*})-\frac{(I^{*})^{1-p}}{p}\left(I^{p}-(I^{*})^{p}\right)\right]\right\}.

Elementary analysis shows that
V_{1}(S,I)\geq 0
for all
S,\,I>0
. Moreover, in light of
(S^{*})^{q}=r(I^{*})^{1-p}
due to (
4.22
), by direct calculations, we compute from (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}V_{1}(S,I)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(1-\frac{(S^{*})^{q}}{S^{q}}\right)S_{t}+\left(1-\frac{(I^{*})^{1-p}}{I^{1-p}}\right)I_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=-qd_{S}(S^{*})^{q}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla S|^{2}}{S^{q+1}}-d_{I}(1-p)(I^{*})^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla I|^{2}}{I^{2-p}}-(I^{*})^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\beta\frac{(S^{q}I^{p}-rI)^{2}}{S^{q}I}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Thus, upon an integration in
t\in(1,\infty)
and a use of the boundedness of
(S,I)
, we find

\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[qrd_{S}\frac{|\nabla S|^{2}}{S^{q+1}}+d_{I}(1-p)\frac{|\nabla I|^{2}}{I^{2-p}}+\beta\frac{(S^{q}I^{p}-rI)^{2}}{S^{q}I}\right]<\infty.

(4.23)

The regularity in (
4.1
) along with Ascoli-Arzelà theorem implies there exists a sequence
t_{k}\rightarrow\infty
and nonnegative
C^{2}
-function
(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})
such that
(S(\cdot,t_{k}),I(\cdot,t_{k}))\rightarrow(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})
in
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, as
k\rightarrow\infty
. One the other hand, recall that
(S,I)
is uniformly persistent by
[
40
, Theorem 2.5]
, then the function

t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\left[qrd_{S}\frac{|\nabla S|^{2}}{S^{q+1}}+d_{I}(1-p)\frac{|\nabla I|^{2}}{I^{2-p}}+\beta\frac{(S^{q}I^{p}-rI)^{2}}{S^{q}I}\right]

is uniformly bounded and uniformly continuous. Thus, we infer from (
4.23
) that

\int_{\Omega}\left[qrd_{S}\frac{|\nabla S_{\infty}|^{2}}{S_{\infty}^{q+1}}+d_{I}(1-p)\frac{|\nabla I_{\infty}|^{2}}{I_{\infty}^{2-p}}+\beta\frac{(S_{\infty}^{q}I_{\infty}^{p}-rI_{\infty})^{2}}{S_{\infty}^{q}I_{\infty}}\right]=0,

which entails both
S_{\infty}
and
I_{\infty}
are constants and
S^{q}_{\infty}I^{p}_{\infty}-rI_{\infty}=0
. In addition,
S_{\infty}
and
I_{\infty}
must be positive constants again by the uniform persistence of
(S,I)
. As a result, it must hold
(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})=(S^{*},I^{*})
by uniqueness. Since the limiting function
(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})
is unique, it follows that
(S(\cdot,t),I(\cdot,t))\to(S^{*},I^{*})
in
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, as
t\to\infty
.

When
q=1
, we define the following Lyapunov functional

V_{2}(S,I)=\int_{\Omega}\left\{\left(S-S^{*}-S^{*}\ln\frac{S}{S^{*}}\right)+\left[(I-I^{*})-\frac{(I^{*})^{1-p}}{p}\left(I^{p}-(I^{*})^{p}\right)\right]\right\}.

Then
V_{2}(S,I)\geq 0
for all
S,\,I>0
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}V_{2}(S,I)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\left(1-\frac{S^{*}}{S}\right)S_{t}+\left(1-\frac{(I^{*})^{1-p}}{I^{1-p}}\right)I_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=-d_{S}S^{*}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla S|^{2}}{S^{2}}-d_{I}(1-p)(I^{*})^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla I|^{2}}{I^{2-p}}-(I^{*})^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\beta\frac{(SI^{p}-rI)^{2}}{S^{q}I}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Then the rest of the argument is the same as that of
q\neq 1
as done before.
∎

Now, suppose that
d_{S}=d_{I}
. For our later purpose, given constant
\tau_{0}>0
, we consider the following initial-boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}=d_{I}\Delta u+\beta(x)\left(\tau_{0}-u\right)^{q}u^{p}-\gamma(x)u,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.24)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<p<1
. Inside the interval
\left[0,\tau_{0}\right]
, the problem (
4.24
) admits a unique positive steady state
U(x)
, which is globally asymptotically stable for (
4.24
) with initial data fulfilling
\inf_{\Omega}u_{0}>0
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\tau_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
0<p<1
, define

\delta_{0}=\sup\left\{\tilde{\delta}\in(0,\tau_{0}):\ \left(\tau_{0}-\delta\right)^{q}\inf_{\Omega}\frac{\beta}{\gamma}-\delta^{1-p}>0,\ \forall\delta\in(0,\tilde{\delta})\right\}.

Then one can easily check for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, that the two positive constants
\delta
and
\tau_{0}
, respectively, are lower and upper solutions for the steady state problem of (
4.24
). Thus, the existence of a positive equilibrium
U(x)\in\left[0,\tau_{0}\right]
is guaranteed by the standard upper-lower solutions method.

Next, we first show there exists a unique steady state in
\left[\delta,\tau_{0}\right]
. To this end, we let
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
be the minimal and maximal solutions in
\left[\delta,\tau_{0}\right]
. It then follows that
\delta\leq u_{1}\leq u_{2}\leq\tau_{0}
on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then we multiply by
u_{2}
the equation satisfied by
u_{1}
to derive

-d_{I}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left[\beta\left(\tau_{0}-u_{1}\right)^{q}u_{1}^{p}u_{2}-\gamma u_{1}u_{2}\right]=0.

(4.25)

By interchanging the role of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, we have

-d_{I}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla u_{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left[\beta\left(\tau_{0}-u_{2}\right)^{q}u_{2}^{p}u_{1}-\gamma u_{1}u_{2}\right]=0.

(4.26)

Thus, we see from (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) that

\int_{\Omega}\beta u_{1}^{p}u_{2}^{p}\left[\left(\tau_{0}-u_{1}\right)^{q}u_{2}^{1-p}-\left(\tau_{0}-u_{2}\right)^{q}u_{1}^{1-p}\right]=0.

This along with the facts
\left(\tau_{0}-u_{1}\right)^{q}\geq\left(\tau_{0}-u_{2}\right)^{q}>0
and
u_{2}^{1-p}\geq u_{1}^{1-p}
shows that
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
on
\overline{\Omega}
, yielding the desired uniqueness on
[\delta,\tau_{0}]
. Now, for any two positive equilibria
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
on
[0,\tau_{0}]
, we put
\delta=\min\{\min_{\overline{\Omega}}u_{1},\ \min_{\overline{\Omega}}u_{2},\ \delta_{0}\}
; then
\delta>0
and
u_{1},u_{2}
are two equilibria on
[\delta,\tau_{0}]
, and so the proved uniqueness shows
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Given initial data fulfilling
\inf_{\Omega}u_{0}>0
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\tau_{0}
, for any
0<\delta\leq\min\{\inf_{\Omega}u_{0},\ \delta_{0}\}
, we see that
\delta\leq u_{0}(x)\leq\tau_{0}
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Denoting by
u(x,t;\delta)
and
u(x,t;\tau_{0})
, respectively, the solutions of (
4.24
) with initial data
u(x,0)\equiv\delta
and
u(x,0)\equiv\tau_{0}
, we then conclude from the maximum principle that

\delta\leq u(x,t;\delta)\leq u(x,t)\leq u\left(x,t;\tau_{0}\right)\leq\tau_{0},

(4.27)

for all
(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty)
. In addition,
u(x,t;\delta)
is non-decreasing and
u(x,t;\tau_{0})
is non-increasing with respect to
t\in(0,\infty)
. As a result, the point-wise limits

\delta\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}u(x,t;\delta)=:\underline{U}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}u\left(x,t;\tau_{0}\right)=:\overline{U}(x)\leq\tau_{0},

(4.28)

exist. Furthermore, notice that both
\underline{U}
and
\overline{U}
are positive equilibria of (
4.24
) in
(0,\tau_{0})
, and thus,
\underline{U}\equiv\overline{U}
. Hence, in light of the standard parabolic regularity as in (
4.1
) or simply Dini’s theorem, we infer from (
4.27
) and (
4.28
) that
u(x,t;u_{0})\rightarrow U(x)\equiv\underline{U}(x)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
uniformly for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

Now, we are ready to show the global asymptotic stability of EE when
d_{S}=d_{I}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
0<p<1
and
d_{S}=d_{I}
. Then, as
t\to\infty
, the unique global and bounded solution
(S,I)
of the reaction-diffusion model (
4.20
) fulfills

(S(x,t),I(x,t))\to(\tilde{S}(x),\tilde{I}(x)),

uniformly for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, where
(\tilde{S}(x),\tilde{I}(x))
is the unique endemic equilibrium of (
4.20
).

Proof.

Let
w(x,t)=S(x,t)+I(x,t)
. Obviously,
w
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}w_{t}=d_{S}\Delta w,&x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
\vskip 4.0pt\cr\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>0,\\
\vskip 4.0pt\cr w(x,0)=S_{0}(x)+I_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\vskip 4.0pt\cr\int_{\Omega}w=N,&t>0.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, as
t\to\infty
,
w(x,t)\to\frac{N}{|\Omega|}
uniformly for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. So, for any small
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
T=T(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-I(x,t)-\varepsilon<S(x,t)<\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-I(x,t)+\varepsilon,

(4.29)

for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
t\geq T
. Consider the auxiliary problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\bar{I}_{t}-d_{I}\Delta\bar{I}=\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon-\bar{I}\right)^{q}\bar{I}^{p}-\gamma\bar{I},&x\in\Omega,\,t>T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\frac{\partial\bar{I}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\bar{I}(x,T)=\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}I(x,T),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(4.30)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\underline{I}_{t}-d_{I}\Delta\underline{I}=\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\varepsilon-\underline{I}\right)^{q}\underline{I}^{p}-\gamma\underline{I},&x\in\Omega,\,t>T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\frac{\partial\underline{I}}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\underline{I}(x,T)=\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}I(x,T),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(4.31)

In view of the maximum principle for parabolic equations, comparing (
4.20
), (
4.30
) and (
4.31
), we find that

\underline{I}(x,t)\leq I(x,t)\leq\bar{I}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times(T,\infty).

(4.32)

It follows readily from (
4.29
) and the fact
S>0
that

0<\bar{I}(x,T)=\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}I(x,T)<\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon.

In order to verify that the initial data in (
4.31
) fulfills the requirement in Lemma
4.3
with
\tau_{0}=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\varepsilon
, let us recall from
[
40
, Theorem 2.5]
that there exists some positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
depending only on
N
such that
\liminf_{t\to\infty}S(x,t)\geq\varepsilon_{0}
uniformly for
\overline{\Omega}
. Thus, by choosing
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
small and enlarging
T
if necessary, this, together with (
4.29
), yields

0<\underline{I}(x,T)=\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}I(x,T)<\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\varepsilon.

Thus, we now apply Lemma
4.3
to problems (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) with
\tau_{0}=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon
and
\tau_{0}=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\varepsilon
, respectively, to conclude the following uniform convergence

\lim_{t\to\infty}\underline{I}(x,t)=U_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\bar{I}(x,t)=U^{\varepsilon}(x),

(4.33)

where
U^{\varepsilon}
and
U_{\varepsilon}
are the unique positive steady state of (
4.30
) and (
4.31
), respectively.

Recall also from
[
40
, Theorem 2.5]
that there exists
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that
\liminf_{t\to\infty}I(x,t)\geq\varepsilon_{1}
uniformly for
\overline{\Omega}
. This allows one to infer there exists some small
\delta>0
such that
\left(\delta,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\delta\right)
is a pair of lower-and-upper solutions to the steady state problem of (
4.30
) for any small
\varepsilon>0
. Hence,
0<\delta\leq U^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\delta
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
by the uniqueness of positive equilibrium recorded in Lemma
4.3
. Then the elliptic regularity estimates yield that
\{U^{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
C^{2+\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha>0
. We then infer from the Ascoli-Arzelà theorem that, along a subsequence of
\varepsilon\to 0
, it holds
U^{\varepsilon}\to U^{0}\geq 0
in
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. By sending
\varepsilon\to 0+
, we see that
U^{0}
is in fact a positive solution of the elliptic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-d_{I}\Delta u=\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-u\right)^{q}u^{p}-\gamma u,&x\in\Omega,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.34)

In the similar fashion, we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}U_{\varepsilon}=U_{0}>0
in
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, where
U_{0}
is also a positive solution of (
4.34
). On the other hand, as in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, one can show that the positive solution of (
4.34
) in
(0,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}]
is unique. Then it must hold
U_{0}\equiv U^{0}
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Finally, by sending
\varepsilon\to 0+
, we conclude from (
4.29
), (
4.32
) and (
4.33
) that

\lim_{t\to\infty}(S(x,t),I(x,t))=\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\tilde{I}(x),\tilde{I}(x)\right)

uniformly for
x\in\Omega
, where
\tilde{I}\equiv U_{0}\equiv U^{0}
is the unique positive solution of (
4.34
). Defining
\tilde{S}(x)=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-\tilde{I}(x)>0
and noting
d_{S}=d_{I}
, one can readily verify that
(\tilde{S},\tilde{I})
is the unique endemic equilibrium of (
4.20
). The proof is thus completed.
∎

Now, we consider the case of
p=1
. System (
4.20
) has a unique disease-free-equilibrium (DFE)
(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0)
. According to
[
50
]
, we define the basic reproduction number
\mathcal{R}_{0}
via

\mathcal{R}_{0}=\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)^{q}\sup_{0\neq\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\beta\varphi^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}\left(d_{I}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\gamma\varphi^{2}\right)}.

(4.35)

We here point out that
\mathcal{R}_{0}<1
when
\lambda_{*}>0
,
\mathcal{R}_{0}=1
when
\lambda_{*}=0
and
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
when
\lambda_{*}<0
. Here and below,
\lambda_{*}
is the principal eigenvalue of the eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-d_{I}\Delta\phi+\left(\gamma-\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)^{q}\right)\phi=\lambda\phi,&x\in\Omega,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.36)

The DFE
(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0)
is locally asymptotically stable if
\mathcal{R}_{0}<1
, while it is unstable if
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1.

Now, suppose there exists some
r>0
such that
\gamma(x)=r\beta(x)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. It is not hard to see from (
4.35
) that
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)^{q}
. If
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
, a constant EE
(\hat{S},\hat{I}):=(r^{\frac{1}{q}},\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-r^{\frac{1}{q}})
exists.

Concerning the large-time dynamics of (
4.20
), we have the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
p=1
and let
r>0
be such that
\gamma(x)=r\beta(x)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then the reaction-diffusion SIS model (
4.20
) admits threshold dynamics. More precisely, the unique classical solution
(S,I)
of (
4.20
) enjoys the following convergence properties:

(i)

If
\mathcal{R}_{0}\leq 1
, then as
t\to\infty
, the following uniform convergence on
\overline{\Omega}
holds:

\left(S(\cdot,t),I(\cdot,t)\right)\to\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0\right).

(4.37)

(ii)

If
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
, then as
t\to\infty
, the following uniform convergence on
\overline{\Omega}
holds:

\left(S(\cdot,t),I(\cdot,t)\right)\to\left(r^{\frac{1}{q}},\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-r^{\frac{1}{q}}\right).

(4.38)

Proof.

(i) Since
\mathcal{R}_{0}\leq 1
implies
r^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\frac{N}{|\Omega|}
, we define the Lyapunov functional

V_{3}(S,I)=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(S-\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)^{2}+\left(r^{\frac{1}{q}}-\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)I\right].

Then we compute from (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}V_{3}(S,I)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(S-\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)(d_{S}\Delta S-\beta S^{q}I+r\beta I)

\displaystyle\ +\left(r^{\frac{1}{q}}-\frac{N}{|\Omega|}\right)\int_{\Omega}(d_{I}\Delta I+\beta S^{q}I-r\beta I)

\displaystyle=-d_{S}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\beta I\left(S^{q}-r\right)\left(S-r^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)\leq 0,

which upon integration and the boundedness of
(S,I)
implies

\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left[d_{S}|\nabla S|^{2}+\beta I\left(S^{q}-r\right)\left(S-r^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)\right]<\infty.

The regularity in (
4.1
) along with Ascoli-Arzelà theorem implies there exists
t_{k}\rightarrow\infty
and nonnegative
C^{2}
-function
(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})
such that
(S(\cdot,t_{k}),I(\cdot,t_{k}))\rightarrow(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
in
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. On the other hand, since the function

t\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\left[d_{S}|\nabla S|^{2}+\beta I\left(S^{q}-r\right)\left(S-r^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)\right]

is uniformly bounded and uniformly continuous, we infer that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S_{\infty}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\beta I_{\infty}\left(S_{\infty}^{q}-r\right)\left(S_{\infty}-r^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)=0,

(4.39)

which entails either
S_{\infty}\equiv r^{1/q}
or
I_{\infty}\equiv 0
. If
S_{\infty}\equiv r^{1/q}
, then we infer from the conservation of total population (
4.21
) that
I_{\infty}=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-r^{\frac{1}{q}}
, which is non-positive since
\mathcal{R}_{0}\leq 1
. Thus, we must have
I_{\infty}\equiv 0
and consequently
S_{\infty}\equiv\frac{N}{|\Omega|}
. Since this type of argument can be applied to every subsequence of
\{t\}_{t\geq 1}
, (
4.37
) follows readily.

(ii) Recall that
(\hat{S},\hat{I}):=(r^{\frac{1}{q}},\frac{N}{|\Omega|}-r^{\frac{1}{q}})
is an EE. Define

V_{4}(S,I)=\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(S-\hat{S}+I-\hat{I}\right)^{2}+\frac{(d_{S}+d_{I})^{2}}{8d_{S}d_{I}}\left(S-\hat{S}\right)^{2}\right].

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}V_{4}(S,I)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left(S-\hat{S}+I-\hat{I}\right)\left(d_{S}\Delta S+d_{I}\Delta I\right)

\displaystyle\quad\ +\frac{(d_{S}+d_{I})^{2}}{4d_{S}d_{I}}\int_{\Omega}\left(S-\hat{S}\right)\left(d_{S}\Delta S-\beta S^{q}I+r\beta I\right)

\displaystyle=-d_{S}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla S|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{d_{S}+d_{I}}{2\sqrt{d_{I}}}\nabla S+\sqrt{d_{I}}\nabla I\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\ -\frac{(d_{S}+d_{I})^{2}}{4d_{S}d_{I}}\int_{\Omega}\beta I\left(S-\hat{S}\right)\left(S^{q}-\hat{S}^{q}\right)\leq 0,

which implies that (
4.39
) holds for some limiting
C^{2}
-nonnegative functions
(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})
of
(S,I)
.
Hence, as argued above, we see that

(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})\in\left\{\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0\right),\ \left(\hat{S},\hat{I}\right)\right\}.

(4.40)

(Note that the limiting functions must be unique since
\lim_{t\to\infty}V_{4}(S,I)
exists and
V_{4}(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0)\neq V_{4}(\hat{S},\hat{I})
.)
Since
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
, the former case can be eliminated by the uniform persistence property of
(S,I)
in
[
40
, Theorem 2.5]
, which is obtained by abstract dynamical system theory. Nevertheless, here we would like to present an elementary argument to rule out this possibility. In fact, suppose
(S_{\infty},I_{\infty})=(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0)
. Observe
S_{\infty}=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}>r^{\frac{1}{q}}
since
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
. Then there exists some positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
and
T>0
such that

S^{q}-r\geq\varepsilon_{0}>0\ \ \text{on}\ \ \overline{\Omega}\times[T,\infty).

Hence, by the
I
-equation in (
4.20
), we find that

\begin{cases}I_{t}\geq d_{I}\Delta I+\varepsilon_{0}\left(\min_{\overline{\Omega}}\beta\right)I,&x\in\Omega,\,t>T,\\[3.0pt]
\frac{\partial I}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>T,\\[3.0pt]
I(x,T)\geq\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}I(x,T),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

which upon comparison with the corresponding ODE shows, for
(x,t)\in\overline{\Omega}\times[T,\infty)
, that

I(x,t)\geq\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}I(x,T)\exp\left\{\varepsilon_{0}\left(\min_{\overline{\Omega}}\beta\right)\left(t-T\right)\right\}.

This clearly is incompatible with the boundedness of
I
. Then the desired uniform convergence in (
4.38
) follows from (
4.40
).
∎

Finally, we turn our attention to the scenario of equal diffusion rates.

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose
p=1
and
d_{S}=d_{I}
. Then the reaction-diffusion SIS model (
4.20
) has the following threshold dynamics:

(i)

If
\mathcal{R}_{0}\leq 1
, then as
t\to\infty
, the following uniform convergence on
\overline{\Omega}
holds:

\left(S(\cdot,t),I(\cdot,t)\right)\to\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|},0\right).

(4.41)

(ii)

If
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
, then as
t\to\infty
, the following uniform convergence on
\overline{\Omega}
holds:

\left(S(\cdot,t),I(\cdot,t)\right)\to\left(\tilde{S},\tilde{I}\right),

where
(\tilde{S},\tilde{I})
is the unique EE of (
4.20
).

Proof.

(i) Firstly, suppose
\mathcal{R}_{0}<1
. Then
\lambda_{*}>0
, where
\lambda_{*}
is the principal eigenvalue of (
4.36
). As in the reasoning in Theorem
4.4
, we are led to the auxliary problems (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) (with
p=1
). To analyze the large-time dynamics of the unique solution to (
4.30
), for any small
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-d_{I}\Delta\phi+\left(\gamma-\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon\right)^{q}\right)\phi=\lambda\phi,&x\in\Omega,\\
\vskip 3.0pt\cr\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

whose principal eigenvalue is denoted by
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
. It follows from
\lambda_{*}>0
that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}>0
for small
\varepsilon>0
. We then claim that (
4.30
) does not admit non-negative non-trivial steady state in the interval
[0,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon]
. In fact, suppose
I_{\varepsilon}\geq 0,\,\not\equiv 0
is such a steady state. Then we derive from the variational formulation of
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
and the equation satisfied by
I_{\varepsilon}
that

\begin{split}\lambda_{\varepsilon}&=\inf_{0\neq\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}\left[d_{I}|\nabla\phi^{2}|+\left(\gamma-\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon\right)^{q}\right)\phi^{2}\right]}{\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}\left[d_{I}|\nabla I_{\varepsilon}^{2}|+\left(\gamma-\beta\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon\right)^{q}\right)I_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right]}{\int_{\Omega}I_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\int_{\Omega}\beta I_{\varepsilon}^{2}\left[\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon-I_{\varepsilon}\right)^{q}-\left(\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon\right)^{q}\right]}{\int_{\Omega}I_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\\
&<0,\end{split}

(4.42)

contradicting
\lambda_{\varepsilon}>0
. As a result,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\bar{I}(\cdot,t)=0
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then we derive from (
4.32
) that
\lim_{t\to\infty}I(\cdot,t)=0
and therefore
\lim_{t\to\infty}S(\cdot,t)=\frac{N}{|\Omega|}
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Now suppose
\mathcal{R}_{0}=1
or equivalently
\lambda_{*}=0
. This coupled with (
4.42
) shows
\lambda_{\varepsilon}<0
for small
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\phi_{\varepsilon}>0
be the corresponding principal eigenfunction, normalized by
\|\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. For the steady state problem of (
4.30
), one can choose small
\tau>0
and use
\tau\phi_{\varepsilon}
as a lower solution and
\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon
as an upper solution to establish the existence of a positive solution
I^{\varepsilon}
in
(0,\frac{N}{|\Omega|}+\varepsilon)
. The uniqueness and global asymptotic stability can be proved similarly as in the argument of Lemma
4.3
. Thus, we have

0\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}I(\cdot,t)\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\bar{I}(\cdot,t)=I^{\varepsilon}(\cdot),

(4.43)

uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
. On the other hand, notice that
\{I^{\varepsilon}\}
is uniformly bounded for all small
\varepsilon>0
. As before, along a subsequence of
\varepsilon\to 0
, it holds
I^{\varepsilon}\to I^{0}\geq 0
in
C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
. The fact
\lambda_{*}=0
enforces that the limiting function
I^{0}
must be zero, since otherwise one can easily derive a contradiction as in (
4.42
). Thus, (
4.41
) follows from (
4.43
) and (
4.29
).

(ii) Since
\mathcal{R}_{0}>1
, we have
\lambda_{*}<0
and
\lambda_{\varepsilon}<0
for all small
\varepsilon>0
. Then both (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) admit a unique positive equilibrium which is globally asymptotically stable. The rest of the argument is quite similar to that of Theorem
4.4
and hence is omitted.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

For model (
4.20
) with
0<p<1
, our findings in Theorems
4.2
and
4.4
strengthen the results in
[
40
, Theorem 2.5]
, where only uniform persistence property of solutions is shown. For (
4.20
) with
p=1
, the global stability results in Theorems
4.5
and
4.6
confirm the conjecture in
[
40
, Remark 2.3 (i)]
and thus extends
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
.

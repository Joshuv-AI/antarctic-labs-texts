Superconformal invariants and spinning correlators in 3 ​ d 3d 𝒩 = 2 {\cal N}=2 SCFTs
Aditya Jain and Amin A. Nizami

Abstract
We construct superconformal invariants in superspace which are used to build 3-point correlators of spinning operators in general 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal field theories in three dimensions. Our systematic analysis includes various relations between these invariants and provides a minimal set of parity-even and parity-odd invariants which is further used to construct general 3-point functions in any 3d 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 SCFT. For conserved (super)currents, we explicitly compute various 3-point functions using Wick contractions in the free field case, and express them in terms of the constructed parity-even invariants. We give evidence through examples for the claim that the 3-point function of conserved currents generally comprises of two parts - a parity-even piece coming from the free theory, and a parity-odd piece.

Introduction

Superconformal invariance is the largest spacetime symmetry possible in a local relativistic quantum field theory. Consequently, it is extremely constraining. Conformal algebras exist in all space-time dimensions but the supersymmetry algebra can be extended to a closed superconformal algebra only for
d\leq 6

[

[

[
4

5

Since the number of these tensor structures, as well as the complexity in the form of each, increases rapidly with increasing spin of the correlator, it is useful to have an efficient and tractable way of keeping track of the tensorial nature of such correlators. For non-supersymmetric theories, one such approach was provided by
[

In this paper, we will study the constraints of

2.2
Supercovariant derivative and chiral co-ordinates

In studying conservation constraints in Section

\displaystyle D^{a}_{\alpha}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{a\alpha}}+\frac{i}{2}\theta^{a\beta}\partial_{\beta\alpha},

As is well-known, the operator

\displaystyle\{D^{a}_{\alpha},Q^{b}_{\beta}\}=0.

(24)

Also,

\displaystyle\{D^{a}_{\alpha},D^{b}_{\beta}\}=-P_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{ab}.

(25)

Chiral basis for
{\cal N}=2

The
R
-symmetry group for

SO(2)
, which is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\theta^{\alpha}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\theta^{1\alpha}+i\theta^{2\alpha})\ ,\qquad\bar{\theta}^{\alpha}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\theta^{1\alpha}-i\theta^{2\alpha}).

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(D^{1}_{\alpha}-iD^{2}_{\alpha})\ ,\qquad\bar{D}_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(D^{1}_{\alpha}+iD^{2}_{\alpha}).

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\alpha}}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\theta}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta\alpha}\ ,\qquad\bar{D}_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\theta}^{\alpha}}+\frac{i}{2}\theta^{\beta}\partial_{\beta\alpha}.

(28)

Also, the (anti-)chiral supercovariant derivatives satisfy the algebra

\displaystyle\{D_{\alpha},\bar{D}_{\beta}\}=-P_{\alpha\beta},\qquad\{D_{\alpha},D_{\beta}\}=\{\bar{D}_{\alpha},\bar{D}_{\beta}\}=0.

(29)

5
Three-point function examples

The purpose of constructing the parity even and odd invariants is to make simpler the enumeration of tensor structures for particular 3-point correlators, a task we turn to in this section.
Before turning to specific examples of 3-point functions of spinning operators, let us make some general observations.

The general 3-point function of spinning operators has the following form in terms of the invariants constructed in the previous sections:

Discussion

In this paper we studied 3-point correlators of spinning operators in
\mathcal{N}=2
3d SCFTs in terms of superconformal invariants. Our main results include:

•

Construction of the parity-even invariants and determination of relations between them.

•

Construction of the parity-odd invariants and determination of relations between them.

•

Expressions for several 3-point functions of spinning operators in terms of a linearly independent basis made up of the minimal set of invariants.

•

Computing the 3-point conserved current correlators in the free field case and expressing them in terms of the invariants.

•

Studying the constraints of conservation - and verifying through several examples that conserved current 3-point correlators contain 2 OPE coefficients comprising of the parity-even and odd parts of the correlator.

We would like to emphasise again that the construction of the invariants in the
\mathcal{N}=2
case is more involved and subtle compared to the
\mathcal{N}=1,\,0
cases due to the presence of R-symmetry. Although the
P,Q,S
invariants for
\mathcal{N}=0
have a relatively straightforward extension in superspace, the fermionic invariants for
\mathcal{N}=2
are completely different from the
\mathcal{N}=1
case, although built out of similar building blocks which are now charged under R-symmetry. To build our minimal set of
\mathcal{N}=2
invariants, we had to deal with two layers of complexity. Firstly, as discussed in Section
3
, not all R-symmetry singlets built by suitably contracting the covariant building blocks will be super-inversion invariant. Secondly, even when they are, they will not be independent and we found (in Sections
3
,
4
and Appendix
B
) relations which give our final minimal set of invariants:
P_{i},Q_{i},\bar{R}_{i},R^{\prime},S_{i},T^{\prime}
.

It would be interesting to take forward this work in various directions:

•

Higher dimensions –
The same methods should work for correlators in SCFTs in higher than 3 dimensions. The supercovariant structures in this case are known from the work of Park and Osborn
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. One new feature would be the existence of mixed symmetry tensors not present in the 3d case, which would engender the use of grassmanian polarisation spinors.

•

More supersymmetry –
It should be straightforward, though cumbersome, to extend this analysis to higher supersymmetry. Off-shell superspace formulations for SCFTs in three dimensions exist for
\mathcal{N}=1,2,3,4

[
24
]
. For
\mathcal{N}>2
, there will be other invariant tensors of
SO(\mathcal{N})
which may also be used to build R-symmetry singlets. For example, for
\mathcal{N}=3
we can have
\delta_{ab}
as well as the anti-symmetric
\epsilon_{abc}
.

•

Generating functions –
Building on the various examples, it would be interesting to work out the form of the generating function for 3-point correlators in the
\mathcal{N}=1,2
free theory. The form of this generating function is known for the non-supersymmetric case
[
6
]
in terms of the parity-even invariants
P\,\text{and}\,\,Q
, but not known for the SCFT case.

•

Bootstrap –
Since 4-point superconformal blocks can be built from the product of 3-point functions of spinning operators, our analysis should aid in implementations of the superconformal bootstrap for 4-point functions in 3d
\mathcal{N}=2
SCFTs. Bootstrap studies of 3d SCFTs include
[
25
,
26
]
for
\mathcal{N}=2
and
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
for
\mathcal{N}=1
(see also
[
30
,
31
]
for higher
\mathcal{N}
).

•

Momentum space –
It would be instructive to carry out our general analysis in momentum space. Firstly, this is useful because most perturbative computations of correlators in specific theories are carried out directly in momentum space. Secondly, the structure of momentum space correlators in (non-supersymmetric) CFTs has been actively investigated in the last decade, starting with the works
[
32
,
33
,
34
]
, with a number of interesting conceptual and technical results and it would be natural to study this also for SCFTs. In particular, conserved current correlators with spin have a simple form in momentum space (and more so in terms of spinor-helicity variables) for 3d CFTs
[
32
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
and it would be interesting to investigate the same for SCFT correlators.

•

\mathcal{N}=2

Chern-Simons-Matter theories –
A prime example of 3d Lagrangian CFTs are the Chern-Simons theories coupled to matter. Such theories are exactly conformal and have a well-controlled weakly broken higher-spin symmetry
[
38
]
. Our analysis should be of use in computations of correlators in
\mathcal{N}=2
3d Chern-Simons-Matter theories
[
39
]
.

B.3
A few more relations

Using the two relations (

) and (

), other relations with higher homogeneity in

can be generated. For example, using Eq. (

fermionic relation

Appendix C
Calculational details for free theory correlators

In this appendix we provide some calculation details for results quoted in Section
6
. In particular, we will explain through two examples how one computes current correlators in the free-field theory using Wick contractions of the chiral scalar superfield.
For
{\cal N}=2
, the basic superfields are (anti-)chiral,

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\alpha}\Phi=D_{\alpha}\bar{\Phi}=0

(93)

and obey the following free field equations of motion,

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\Phi=\bar{D}^{\alpha}\bar{D}_{\alpha}\bar{\Phi}=0\,\,.

(94)

Solving the above, we get the super-multiplet for the (anti-)chiral superfields to have the form

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\phi+\theta\psi-\frac{i}{2}\theta\gamma^{\mu}\bar{\theta}\partial_{\mu}\phi

(95)

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\bar{\phi}+\bar{\theta}\psi^{*}+\frac{i}{2}\theta\gamma^{\mu}\bar{\theta}\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\,.

(96)

Here
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy, respectively, the free Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations, (
\theta\gamma^{\mu}\bar{\theta}\equiv\theta^{\alpha}(\gamma^{\mu})_{\alpha}^{\ \beta}\bar{\theta}_{\beta}
).

Next, one can construct the 2-point function

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{y_{12}}

(97)

where
\Phi_{i}
is the superfield at superspace location
(x_{i},\theta_{i})
, while
y_{12}
is built from
\bar{x}_{12}

[
11
]
,

\displaystyle y_{12}^{2}=\bar{x}_{12}^{2}v_{12},\quad v_{12}=\frac{1}{2}\left((V_{12})^{aa}+i\epsilon_{ab}(V_{12})^{ab}\right)

(98)

where we’ve used the 2-point structure
V_{12}^{ab}=\delta^{ab}+i\theta_{12}^{a\alpha}(X_{12+}^{-1})_{\alpha}^{\ \beta}\theta_{12\beta}^{b}
.

This form of the 2-point correlator can be explicitly checked by expanding the superfields and using the expressions

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\phi}_{1}\phi_{2}\rangle=\langle\phi_{1}\bar{\phi}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{12}}

(99)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1\alpha}^{*}\psi_{2\beta}\rangle=-\langle\psi_{2\alpha}\psi_{1\beta}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=i\frac{(X_{12})_{\alpha\beta}}{x_{12}^{3}}

(100)

Abstract
. We introduce the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on Riemannian manifolds with bounded geometry. We then use these spaces to obtain improved local smoothing estimates for Fourier integral operators satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, and for wave equations on compact manifolds. The estimates are essentially sharp, for all 2 < p < ∞ 2<p<\infty and on each compact manifold. We also apply our local smoothing estimates to nonlinear wave equations with initial data outside of L 2 L^{2} -based Sobolev spaces.

1.
Introduction

This article concerns fixed-time and local smoothing estimates for wave equations and Fourier integral operators on manifolds, and associated function spaces.

1.1.
Setting

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
with Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
, and consider the Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})u(x,t)=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\end{cases}

on
M\times\mathbb{R}
, for suitable initial data
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
on
M
. It is a natural problem to determine, for a given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, conditions on
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
which guarantee that the solution
u(t):=u(\cdot,t)
to (
1.1
) at a fixed time
t\in\mathbb{R}
is an element of
L^{p}(M)
.

It was shown by Seeger, Sogge and Stein in
[
40
]
that
u(t)\in L^{p}(M)
if
1<p<\infty
,
u_{0}\in W^{2s(p),p}(M)
and
u_{1}\in W^{2s(p)-1,p}(M)
, and these exponents are sharp for all but a discrete set of times, on each compact manifold. Here and throughout, we write

s(p):=\frac{n-1}{2}\Big|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\Big|

for
p\in[1,\infty]
. This is a variable-coefficient version of the corresponding results from
[
35
,
33
]
for the Euclidean Laplacian
\Delta_{g}=\Delta
on
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Although
[
40
]
determined the optimal fixed-time
L^{p}
regularity for wave equations with smooth coefficients, it was observed by Sogge in
[
44
]
that, for
2<p<\infty
and
\Delta_{g}=\Delta
on
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, less regularity is required of the initial data to guarantee that
u\in L^{p}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. In fact, the
local smoothing conjecture
asserts that
u\in L^{p}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
if
u_{0}\in W^{\sigma(p)+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{1}\in W^{\sigma(p)-1+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\varepsilon>0
, where
\sigma(p):=0
for
2<p\leq 2n/(n-1)
, and
\sigma(p):=2s(p)-1/p
for
2n/(n-1)<p<\infty
. Again, the exponent
\sigma(p)
cannot be improved. Local smoothing estimates on compact manifolds were subsequently obtained in
[
34
]
. However, the local smoothing conjecture for the Euclidean wave equation is still open in dimensions
n\geq 3
.

The most relevant work for the present article is the recent contribution
[
4
]
by Beltran, Hickman and Sogge. They showed that
u\in L^{p}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(M))
if
u_{0}\in W^{d(p)+\varepsilon,p}(M)
and
u_{1}\in W^{d(p)-1+\varepsilon,p}(M)
for some
\varepsilon>0
, on each compact manifold
M
. Here

(1.2)

d(p):=\begin{cases}s(p)&\text{if }2\leq p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\\
2s(p)-\frac{1}{p}&\text{if }\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq p<\infty.\end{cases}

This is a variable-coefficient version of the corresponding result due to Bourgain and Demeter
[
6
]
for the Euclidean wave equation. For
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
one has
d(p)=\sigma(p)
, which means that the exponent
d(p)
is optimal for such
p
.

1.2.
Local smoothing for wave equations

Even though the exponent
d(p)
is sharp for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, it was observed in
[
37
]
that the local smoothing estimates from
[
6
]
, for the Euclidean wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, can be improved by replacing the spaces of initial data
W^{d(p)+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{d(p)-1+\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators
\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)-1+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively. The main result of the present article is the following version of
[
37
]
on a compact manifold
M
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon,t_{0}>0
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that, for all
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(M)
and
u_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)-1+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(M)
, the solution
u
to (
1.1
) satisfies

\Big(\int_{-t_{0}}^{t_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(M)}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)-1+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(M)}).

Theorem
1.1
is a special case of Theorem
6.1
, which considers more general Sobolev regularity of the solution. Moreover, the index
d(p)-s(p)
is sharp, for all
2<p<\infty
and on each compact manifold
M
, by Theorem
7.5
.

The Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
are introduced in Definition
4.1
, for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, these spaces coincide with the ones from
[
17
]
, which in turn are an extension of a construction due to Smith
[
43
]
for
p=1
. They are invariant under compactly supported Fourier integral operators (FIOs) of order zero associated with a local canonical graph, cf. Theorem
4.16
. Moreover, for
1<p<\infty
the Sobolev embeddings

(1.3)

W^{s+s(p),p}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)\subseteq W^{s-s(p),p}(M)

hold, with the natural modifications involving the local Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
for
p=1
, and
\mathrm{bmo}(M)
for
p=\infty
, cf. Theorem
4.15
. In particular, Theorem
1.1
recovers the main result of
[
4
]
. However, since the exponents in (
1.3
) are sharp, Theorem
1.1
is in fact a strict improvement of the main result of
[
4
]
. And since
d(p)=\sigma(p)
for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, Theorem
1.1
improves upon the bounds in the local smoothing conjecture for such
p
.

For
n=2
, the local smoothing conjecture for the Euclidean wave equation was proved by Guth, Wang and Zhang in
[
15
]
, and a variable-coefficient version of their result, on compact surfaces, was obtained in
[
14
]
. Note that

d(p)-s(p)=0=\sigma(p)\quad\text{for}\quad 2<p\leq 2\frac{n}{n-1},

and

d(p)-s(p)=0<\sigma(p)<d(p)\quad\text{for}\quad 2\frac{n}{n-1}<p<2\frac{n+1}{n-1}.

Hence, for
n=2
and
2<p<6
, due to the sharpness of the embeddings in (
1.3
), Theorem
1.1
neither follows from the bounds in
[
14
]
, nor does Theorem
1.1
imply those bounds. In higher dimensions, certain Laplace–Beltrami operators on compact manifolds satisfy weaker local smoothing estimates than the Euclidean wave equation does, cf.
[
32
]
. We refer to
[
5
, Conjecture 23]
for a local smoothing conjecture for wave equations on general compact manifolds. For
p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
, Theorem
1.1
neither follows from that conjecture, nor does the converse hold.

In
[
28
]
, local smoothing estimates without
\varepsilon
loss were obtained in the
L^{p}
scale for
n\geq 4
and
p>2\frac{n-1}{n-3}>2\frac{n+1}{n-1}
. This is a variable-coefficient version of a similar result for the Euclidean wave equation from
[
19
]
. It is not clear whether one may also let
\varepsilon=0
in Theorem
1.1
for certain
p
. For
p>2\frac{n-1}{n-3}
, by the sharpness of the exponents in (
1.3
), the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
neither follows from the local smoothing result in
[
28
]
, nor does the converse hold.

We also obtain new fixed-time estimates for (
1.1
). Namely, in Section
6
we show that, in the setting of Theorem
1.1
,

u\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(M))\cap L^{p}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R};L^{p}(M)).

Due to (
1.3
), the first inclusion improves upon the fixed-time bounds in
[
40
]
, and in particular (
1.1
) is well-posed on the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators.

1.3.
Nonlinear wave equations

Local smoothing estimates from
[
37
]
were used in
[
38
]
to prove new well-posedness results for nonlinear wave equations with slowly decaying initial data on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, building on similar work for the Schrödinger equation from
[
39
]
. In turn, the present article yields variable-coefficient versions of
[
38
]
, for nonlinear wave equations on compact manifolds with initial data outside of
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces.

Consider the cubic nonlinear wave equation on
M\times\mathbb{R}
:

(1.4)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})u(x,t)=\pm|u(x,t)|^{2}u(x,t),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x).\end{cases}

For the Euclidean wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the following result is contained in
[
38
, Theorem 1.2]
. Instead, here we consider compact Riemannian surfaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
n=2
, and let
\varepsilon,t_{0}>0
. Then (
1.4
) is quantitatively well posed with initial data space

(1.5)

X:=(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M))\times(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon-1,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{-1/2,2}(M))

and solution space

(1.6)

S_{t_{0}}:=L^{4}\big([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M)\big)\cap C\big([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M)\big).

We refer to Section
6.2
for the definition of quantitative well-posedness. Loosely speaking, it implies that there exists a
\delta=\delta(t_{0})>0
such that, if
\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{X}<\delta
, then (
1.4
) has a unique solution
u\in S_{t_{0}}
, and this solution depends analytically on the initial data. Moreover, in Theorem
1.2
, for all
(u_{0},u_{1})\in X
there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that there is a unique solution
u\in S_{t_{0}}
to (
1.4
).

Theorem
1.2
is contained in Theorem
6.3
, with a proof which is analogous to that for the Euclidean wave equation. More precisely, in Duhamel’s formula for the solution to (
1.4
), one can use the invariance of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
under the solution operators to the linear equation to deal with the linear term, whereas Strichartz estimates suffice for the term involving the nonlinearity. The latter then leads to the Sobolev spaces
W^{s,2}(M)
appearing in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). In this manner one obtains local existence, and global existence holds for the defocusing equation
if
\varepsilon>1/2
, cf. Proposition
6.7
.

The terminology “slowly decaying initial data” from
[
38
,
39
]
, which refers to the fact that initial data in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
may decay slower at infinity than an
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
function does if
p>2
, does not seem appropriate on compact manifolds. Moreover, by Hölder’s inequality one has
L^{p}(M)\subseteq L^{2}(M)
, and one can combine this embedding with Strichartz estimates to solve (
1.4
) in
L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))
. However, this procedure works for

(u_{0},u_{1})\in W^{1/2,6}(M)\times W^{-1/2,6}(M)\subsetneq W^{1/2,2}(M)\times W^{-1/2,2}(M),

whereas Theorem
1.2
applies when
(u_{0},u_{1})\in W^{1/6+\varepsilon,6}(M)\times W^{-5/6+\varepsilon,6}(M)
, due to (
1.3
). In particular, the initial data space in (
1.5
) is larger than what one obtains by using Strichartz estimates in this manner. In fact, due to the sharpness of the embeddings in (
1.3
), Theorem
1.2
applies to even rougher initial data, including certain data in Sobolev spaces of negative order.

Moreover, one of the advantages of working with the Hardy spaces for FIOs is that they are invariant under the solution operators to the linear flow, cf. Theorem
6.1
. This in turn leads to the fixed-time invariance of the nonlinear flow which is captured by the space of continuous functions in (
1.6
).

1.4.
Local smoothing for Fourier integral operators

As in
[
34
,
28
,
4
,
14
]
, we derive local smoothing for wave equations from bounds for FIOs whose canonical relation satisfies the so-called
cinematic curvature condition
from
[
44
]
. This condition is a homogeneous counterpart of the Carleson–Sjölin condition for (nonhomogeneous) oscillatory integrals (see
[
8
,
20
]
), and it captures, on the level of FIOs, some of the underlying curvature and dispersion effects which govern the phenomenon of local smoothing.

Let
N
be another compact Riemannian manifold, of dimension
n+1
. For
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
the class of FIOs of order
m-1/4
associated with a canonical relation
{\mathcal{C}}
from
T^{*}M
to
T^{*}N
. We refer to Section
A.3
for more this class of operators and on the cinematic curvature condition. A special case of our main result on local smoothing for Fourier integral operators, Theorem
5.7
, is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
T\in I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
, for
{\mathcal{C}}
a canonical relation satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. Then

T:\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon+m,p}_{FIO}(M)\to L^{p}(N)

is bounded.

The connection between Theorems
1.3
and
1.1
comes from the fact that the solution
u
to (
1.1
) is given by
u=T_{0}u_{0}+T_{1}u_{1}
, where
T_{j}\in I^{-j-1/4}(M,M\times\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{C}})
for a
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition,
j\in\{0,1\}
.

Again, the index
d(p)-s(p)
is sharp, for all
2<p<\infty
and for each
T
which is non-characteristic somewhere, by Theorem
7.5
.

By the sharpness of the embeddings in (
1.3
), Theorem
1.3
is a strict improvement of the local smoothing result for FIOs in
[
4
]
. In particular, if
n
is odd, then Theorem
1.3
improves upon the local smoothing conjecture for FIOs from
[
5
, Conjecture 24]
. For
n
even and
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
, Theorem
1.3
neither follows from this conjecture nor implies it. A special case concerns the case where
n=2
, in
[
14
]
. Similarly, for
n\geq 4
and
p>2(n-1)/(n-3)
, Theorem
1.3
neither follows from the local smoothing result for FIOs without
\varepsilon
loss in
[
28
]
, nor does the converse hold.

1.5.
Wolff-type inequalities

The local smoothing estimates for the Euclidean wave equation in
[
37
]
were proved by connecting the Hardy spaces for FIOs to the
\ell^{p}
decoupling inequality for the light cone from
[
6
]
. In fact, local smoothing estimates were obtained for a more general class of constant-coefficient operators using a more general class of
\ell^{p}
decoupling inequalities, from
[
7
]
. Similarly, the local smoothing estimates in this article are proved using a variable-coefficient analog of the
\ell^{p}
decoupling inequality, the variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities from
[
4
]
(see Theorem
5.1
).

More precisely, instead of bounding the
decoupling norm
that appears in those inequalities by the
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of the initial data for a suitable
s\in\mathbb{R}
, which is the approach that is used in
[
4
]
, we bound the decoupling norm in terms of the
\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of the initial data. By (
1.3
), this yields a stronger estimate. Moreover, as in the case of the Euclidean wave equation in
[
37
]
, the resulting inequalities are, in a quantified sense, equivalent to the Wolff-type inequalities. Hence, at least when restricted to dyadic frequency shells, the Hardy spaces for FIOs form the
largest
space of initial data for which one can obtain local smoothing estimates using Wolff-type inequalities as in
[
4
]
.

To bound the decoupling norm by the
\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm, one proceeds in a similar way as in
[
37
]
, using that the discrete and continuous dyadic-parabolic decompositions are equivalent on dyadic frequency shells (see Proposition
5.3
). On the other hand, the reverse inequality is more involved than that in
[
37
]
, due to the fact that the operators which appear in the variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities are neither invertible nor elliptic, unlike the Euclidean half-wave propagators. Instead, loosely speaking, we bound the
\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of the initial data in terms of the right-hand side of the Wolff-type inequality wherever the FIO is microlocally invertible. We refer to Proposition
C.3
(see also Remark
C.4
) for the precise statement.

Hence Theorem
1.3
, and its version in local coordinates in Corollary
5.4
, could be viewed as replacements of the Wolff-type inequalities. Moreover, whereas the discrete decoupling norm is different on each dyadic frequency shell and involves the FIO, the
\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm is defined using a continuous dyadic-parabolic decomposition that is the same on each dyadic frequency shell and is independent of the operator. Here it is worth mentioning that a microlocal version of the
\ell^{2}
decoupling inequality, of a different nature, was obtained in
[
25
]
. It would be of interest to determine the connection between the
\ell^{p}
version of that inequality and the Hardy spaces for FIOs, or alternatively between that inequality and function spaces adapted to the
\ell^{2}
decoupling inequality as in
[
38
]
.

1.6.
Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators

The results discussed so far, and the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
, rely mostly on estimates in local coordinates, FIO calculus and properties of the Hardy spaces for FIOs on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This material can be found in Sections
2
,
5
,
6
and
7
, and Appendix
C
. In particular, loosely speaking, for a compact manifold
M
, one has
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in each coordinate chart, and geometric considerations do not play a significant role. Hence readers who are satisfied with such a definition and who are mainly interested in Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
may skip ahead to Section
5
, looking up results from other sections where needed.

However, apart from proving new local smoothing estimates for wave equations and Fourier integral operators, one of the aims of this article is to develop a proper theory of the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on manifolds. In particular, both with future applications in mind and to unify the theory on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with that on compact manifolds, we introduce these spaces on Riemannian manifolds with bounded geometry. More precisely, we consider
M
with positive injectivity radius, and with the property that all covariant derivatives of the curvature tensor are bounded. This geometric setting
incorporates all compact manifolds and homogeneous spaces, as well as asymptotically Euclidean, asymptotically conic and asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds.
It has been used for microlocal analysis on non-compact manifolds before (see e.g.
[
2
,
41
,
48
]
). On the other hand, connected components of
M
do not necessarily have the doubling property when endowed with the Riemannian distance and measure (although they do have the local doubling property). This places our geometric setting outside of the scope of various results in harmonic analysis which hold on doubling metric measure spaces.

Our definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is inspired by Triebel, who introduced the classical local Hardy spaces, and more general Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, on manifolds with bounded geometry, in
[
50
,
51
]
(see also Taylor
[
48
]
). We follow Triebel’s template, using that the assumption of bounded geometry implies the existence of a suitable uniformly locally finite cover by geodesic coordinate charts (see Lemma
A.1
). Then, loosely speaking,
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if and only if
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in each such coordinate chart, and if the
\ell^{p}
sum of the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms of
u
over these charts is finite (see Definition
4.1
). To show that one recovers the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from
[
43
,
17
]
when
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we prove in Theorem
3.6
a localization principle for
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

This construction yields a space
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
that is as one would expect on both compact manifolds and on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, to derive the basic properties of these spaces, such as complex interpolaton, duality, Sobolev embeddings, the invariance under FIOs, and an atomic decomposition for
p=1
, it is convenient to work with
\ell^{p}
spaces of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued sequences. This operator-theoretic framework is introduced in Section
3
, and in Section
4
it allows us to directly derive properties of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
from those of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Our approach can be compared with the use of tent spaces for the analysis of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
[
17
]
, proving concrete estimates in local coordinates only where necessary and otherwise relying on existing theory.

We define the Hardy spaces for FIOs to consist of half densities. Doing so leads to a more convenient duality and
L^{2}
theory, and it is typical for the theory of FIOs on manifolds. For readers unfamiliar with half densities, we note that elements of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
coincide with generalized functions on
M
, or with functionals on
C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
, after multiplication or division by the square root of the Riemannian density.

1.7.
Organization

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we define the Hardy spaces for FIOs on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and we collect some of their basic properties. In Section
3
we develop an operator-theoretic framework using
\ell^{p}
spaces of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued sequences, which is then applied in Section
4
to introduce and study the Hardy spaces for FIOs on manifolds. Section
5
contains Theorem
1.3
, as well as our version of the variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities, and in Section
6
we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. In Section
7
we show that our local smoothing estimates are essentially sharp. In Appendix
A
we collect some background material on manifolds with bounded geometry, distributional densities and Fourier integral operators, for readers unfamiliar with this theory. Finally, Appendix
B
contains information on the atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the main result of Appendix
C
is an inequality involving the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm and the decoupling norm.

1.8.
Notation and terminology

Throughout, all manifolds are assumed to be infinitely smooth and without boundary. The cotangent bundle of a manifold
M
is
T^{*}M
, and
o
is the zero section of
T^{*}M
. The cosphere bundle of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
S^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S^{n-1}
.

The natural numbers are
{{\mathbb{N}}}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}:={{\mathbb{N}}}\cup\{0\}
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
{\langle}\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
, and
\hat{\xi}=\xi/|\xi|
if
\xi\neq 0
. We use multi-index notation, where
\partial_{\xi}=(\partial_{\xi_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{\xi_{n}})
and
\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{\xi_{1}}\ldots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}_{\xi_{n}}

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. We also write
\partial\chi
for the Jacobian of a smooth map
\chi
between open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi
is the mixed Hessian of a function
\Phi
of the variables
x
and
\eta
.

For
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
open we write
\mathcal{D}(V)=C^{\infty}_{c}(V)
, and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V)
is the dual space of distributions on
V
. We write
{\langle}f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
for the standard distributional pairing between
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(V)
and
g\in\mathcal{D}(V)
, or simply
{\langle}f,g\rangle
when it is clear that we are working on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Fourier transform of a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
, and the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\varphi(D)
.

The space of bounded linear operators between Banach spaces
X
and
Y
is
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(X):={\mathcal{L}}(X,X)
. We let
|E|
denote either the cardinality of a finite set or the measure of a subset of a measure space, depending on the context.

We write
f(s)\lesssim g(s)
to indicate that
f(s)\leq Cg(s)
for all
s
and a constant
C>0
independent of
s
, and similarly for
f(s)\gtrsim g(s)
and
g(s)\eqsim f(s)
.

2.
Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In this section we first recall the definition of the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We then collect some of their basic properties, and most notably their invariance under suitable Fourier integral operators.

2.1.
Definitions

Throughout, for simplicity of notation, we set
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Also,
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the local real Hardy space, with norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|q(D)f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|(1-q)(D)f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here and throughout,
q\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a fixed cut-off such that
q(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 2
, and
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the classical real Hardy space. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the dual of
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and we set
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):={\langle}D\rangle^{-s}\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

First consider the case
n=1
, where one simply sets
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}):=\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R})
.

For the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
n\geq 2
, let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-negative and radial, such that
\varphi(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|>1
, and such that
\varphi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of zero. For
\omega\in S^{n-1}
a unit vector,
\sigma>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, set
\varphi_{\omega,\sigma}(\xi):=c_{\sigma}\varphi(\tfrac{\hat{\xi}-\omega}{\sqrt{\sigma}})
, where
c_{\sigma}:=(\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi(\frac{e_{1}-\nu}{\sqrt{\sigma}})^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu)^{-1/2}
for
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
the first basis vector of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Also set
\varphi_{\omega,\sigma}(0):=0
. Let
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be non-negative and radial, such that
\Psi(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\notin[1/2,2]
, and such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(\sigma\xi)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}=1
for all
\xi\neq 0
. Now set

\varphi_{\omega}(\xi):=\int_{0}^{4}\Psi(\sigma\xi)\varphi_{\omega,\sigma}(\xi)\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\sigma}

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Some properties of these functions are as follows (see
[
36
, Remark 3.3]
):

(1)

For all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
one has
\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|<1/8
or
|\hat{\xi}-\omega|>2|\xi|^{-1/2}
.

(2)

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
there exists a
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
such that

|(\omega\cdot\partial_{\xi})^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}|\xi|^{\frac{n-1}{4}-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}-\beta}

for all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
.

(3)

There exists a radial
m\in S^{(n-1)/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that if
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\xi|\geq 1/2\}
, then
f=\int_{S^{n-1}}m(D)\varphi_{\nu}(D)f\mathrm{d}\nu
.

We can now define the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of those
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
q(D)f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for almost all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
, and

\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}\|\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)^{1/p}<\infty,

endowed with the norm

(2.1)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}\|\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)^{1/p}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
{\langle}D\rangle^{s}f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|{\langle}D\rangle^{s}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In fact, this is not how the Hardy spaces for FIOs were originally defined in
[
43
]
and
[
17
]
, namely in terms of conical square functions and tent spaces over the cosphere bundle
S^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. That these definitions coincide, up to norm equivalence, was shown in
[
12
,
36
]
. We will often rely on results from
[
17
]
; this typically leads to harmless additional constants, arising from the equivalence of norms. We also note that, in
[
13
]
, function spaces related to the Hardy spaces for FIOs were defined by modifying the framework in Definition
2.1
.

To explain the nomenclature “Hardy spaces for FIOs,” we emphasize the connection of these spaces to the classical local Hardy spaces, cf. (
2.1
), their invariance under FIOs, cf. Proposition
2.3
, and the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has an atomic decomposition, cf. Theorem
B.1
.

2.2.
Basic properties

Here we collect some of the basic properties of these spaces.

We first recall the Sobolev embeddings

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}^{s+s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, which follow from
[
17
, Theorem 7.4]
.

Next, the Hardy spaces for FIOs form a complex interpolation scale. That is, let
p_{0},p_{1},p\in[1,\infty]
,
s_{0},s_{1},s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
and
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
. Then, as follows from
[
17
, Proposition 6.7]
,

(2.3)

[\mathcal{H}^{s_{0},p_{0}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{H}^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})]_{\theta}=\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with equivalent norms.

Moreover, by
[
17
, Proposition 6.8]
, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
one has

(2.4)

(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}=\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with equivalent norms. Here the duality pairing is the standard distributional pairing
{\langle}f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

By
[
17
, Proposition 6.6]
, the Schwartz functions are contained in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
, and they lie dense for
p<\infty
. In fact, the following lemma improves the latter statement in a manner which will be useful in the next section.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense, it suffices to approximate Schwartz functions by compactly supported functions in the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm. However, by (
2.2
),
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{N,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
N\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
with
N>s+s(p)
. Hence we need only approximate
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
functions in the
W^{N,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm. In turn, to this end one may choose the approximants
f_{\varepsilon}(x):=\chi(\varepsilon x)f(x)
for
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
\chi(0)=1
.
∎

Finally, we note that
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has an atomic decomposition, in terms of atoms that are supported on anisotropic balls. Appendix
B
contains more information about this decomposition.

2.3.
Boundedness of Fourier integral operators

In this subsection we collect some results on the boundedness of Fourier integral operators on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The main result of this subsection concerns compactly supported FIOs. For more on the class
I^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}})
of Fourier integral operators of order
m
associated with a canonical relation
{\mathcal{C}}
, we refer to Section
A.3
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
T\in I^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}})
, for
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
a local canonical graph, and suppose that
T
is compactly supported. Then

T:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For
m=s=0
, the result is contained in
[
17
, Theorem 6.10]
. The same techniques can be applied for general
m,s\in\mathbb{R}
, at least when combined with the theory of weighted tent spaces as used in
[
18
]
. Instead, here we use the composition theorem for FIOs to easily reduce to the case where
m=s=0
.

We have to show that

{\langle}D\rangle^{s}T{\langle}D\rangle^{-s-m}:\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded. To this end, we first use the compact support assumption on
T
to write
T=\rho_{1}T\rho_{2}
, for suitable
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By Lemma
A.5
, we have

{\langle}D\rangle^{s}\rho_{1}=S_{1}+R_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{2}{\langle}D\rangle^{-s-m}=S_{2}+R_{2},

for compactly supported pseudodifferential operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of orders
s
and
-s-m
, respectively, and smoothing operators
R_{1},R_{2}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence

{\langle}D\rangle^{s}T{\langle}D\rangle^{-s-m}={\langle}D\rangle^{s}\rho_{1}T\rho_{2}{\langle}D\rangle^{-s-m}=S_{1}TS_{2}+R,

where
R
is smoothing. Moreover, the composition theorem for FIOs implies that
S_{1}TS_{2}
is a compactly supported FIO of order zero. Now
[
17
, Theorem 6.10]
concludes the proof.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain a corresponding statement for FIOs that have a convenient oscillatory integral representation. For the standard Kohn–Nirenberg symbol class
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see (
A.13
).

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be open, conic and such that
(x,\eta)\in V
whenever
(x,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
and
\eta\neq 0
. Let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
be real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree
1
in the fiber variable, and such that
\det\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi(x,\eta)\neq 0
for all
(x,\eta)\in V
. Set

Tf(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(x,\eta)}a(x,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that there exists a compact
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
a(x,\eta)=0
for all
(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x\notin K
. Then
T:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Note that the Schwartz kernel of
T
need not even be properly supported. Nonetheless, we can reduce to the setting of Proposition
2.3
.

Set

(2.5)

c:=\sup\{|\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,\eta)|\mid(x,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a),\eta\neq 0\}<\infty,

and let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\chi(y)=1
if
|y|\leq 2c
. Then, by design, the phase function in the Schwartz kernel of
T(1-\chi)
is non-singular, so one can integrate by parts to conclude that
T(1-\chi)
is in fact a smoothing operator. Here one uses that
a
has compact spatial support to obtain the required decay of the kernel at infinity. Hence it suffices to show that
T\chi:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedly.

Next, an inspection of the kernel of
T\chi
shows that
T\chi\in I^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}})
for

{\mathcal{C}}:=\{(x,\partial_{x}\Phi(x,\eta),\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,\eta),\eta)\mid(x,\eta)\in V\}.

Moreover,
(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,\eta),\eta)\mapsto(x,\partial_{x}\Phi(x,\eta))
is locally a well-defined diffeomorphism. Indeed, since this map is the composition of
(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,\eta),\eta)\mapsto(x,\eta)
and
(x,\eta)\mapsto(x,\partial_{x}\Phi(x,\eta))
, the statement follows from the inverse function theorem, combined with the assumption on
\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi
. We thus see that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a local canonical graph, and Proposition
2.3
concludes the proof.
∎

In particular, we can now conclude that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under coordinate changes. Recall that, for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and for
\chi:U\to U^{\prime}
a diffeomorphism between open subsets
U,U^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the pullback
\chi^{*}_{\gamma}f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U)
of
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U^{\prime})
, viewed as a distributional density of order
\gamma
, is given by

{\langle}\chi^{*}_{\gamma}f,\phi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}={\langle}f,|\det\partial(\chi^{-1})|^{1-\gamma}\phi\circ\chi^{-1}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

for
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(U)
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\chi:U\to U^{\prime}
be a diffeomorphism between open subsets
U,U^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U^{\prime})
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\|\chi^{*}_{\gamma}(\psi f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We will mostly use this corollary for
\gamma=1/2
. In this case the pullback
\chi^{*}_{1/2}:L^{2}(U^{\prime})\to L^{2}(U)
is a unitary operator.

Proof.

Note that, for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in U
, one has

\displaystyle\chi^{*}_{\gamma}(\psi f)(x)

\displaystyle=|\!\det(\partial_{x}\chi(x))|^{\gamma}\psi(\chi(x))f(\chi(x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\chi(x)\cdot\eta}|\!\det(\partial_{x}\chi(x))|^{\gamma}\psi(\chi(x))\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta.

Now apply Corollary
2.4
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

By keeping track of the implicit constants in the proof of Corollary
2.4
, one can check that the following holds. For each
i
in an index set
I
, let
T_{i}
be an operator as in Corollary
2.4
, with symbol
a_{i}\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and phase function
\Phi_{i}\in C^{\infty}(V)
, for some fixed conic
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Suppose that

\sup\{{\langle}\eta\rangle^{-m+|\beta|}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}a_{i}(x,\eta)|\mid x,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n},i\in I\}<\infty

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, and that there exists a fixed compact
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
a_{i}(x,\eta)=0
for all
i\in I
and
(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
x\notin K
. Moreover, suppose that

\inf\{|\!\det\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi_{i}(x,\eta)|\mid(x,\eta)\in V,i\in I\}>0,

and that

\sup\{|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}\Phi_{i}(x,\eta)|\mid(x,\eta)\in V\cap S^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n},i\in I\}<\infty

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq 2
. Then

(2.6)

\sup\{\|T_{i}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\mid i\in I\}<\infty

for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

A specific case of (
2.6
) concerns Corollary
2.5
. That is, let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a fixed open set, and let
(\psi_{i})_{i\in I}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
and
(\chi_{i})_{i\in I}
be collections with
\chi_{i}:U\to U
a diffeomorphism for each
i\in I
. Suppose that

\sup\{|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\psi_{i}(x)|+|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\chi_{i}(x)|+|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\chi_{i}^{-1}(x)|\mid x\in U,i\in I\}<\infty

for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, and let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

\|(\chi_{i})^{*}_{\gamma}(\psi_{i}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
i\in I
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

3.
Sequence spaces

To derive the basic properties of the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on manifolds, we will rely on operators that map to and from suitable sequence spaces. In this section we prove the required results about these sequence spaces and the operators between them. Moreover, in the final subsection we prove a localization principle that allows one to apply this framework to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

3.1.
Definitions

Here we introduce the sequence spaces which we will work with, and the relevant operators. These operators can be viewed as a symmetrized version of the standard correspondence between distributional densities and sequences of distributions (see (
A.8
) and (
A.10
)).

For the remainder of this section, let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
with bounded geometry and with smooth structure
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
\mathrm{inj}(M)>0
be the injectivity radius of
M
,
\delta\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M)/9]
, and let
(\psi_{\kappa}^{2})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
be a uniformly locally finite partition of unity as in Lemma
A.1
, associated with a countable collection
K\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
of geodesic coordinate charts
\kappa:U_{\kappa}\to\tilde{U}_{\kappa}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the
U_{\kappa}
,
\kappa\in K
, cover
M
. Write
\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}:=\psi_{\kappa}\circ\kappa^{-1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\tilde{U}_{\kappa})
.

One of the sequence spaces that we will use is the locally convex space of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued sequences with finitely many nonzero values, denoted
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, endowed with the natural inductive limit topology. These sequences will play the role of “test sequences” in our setup, in analogy with the role that the test functions
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
play on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover,
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
is its dual, and we write
{\langle}F,G\rangle_{K}
for the duality between
F\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
and
G\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Then
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
is dense, if one sets

(3.1)

{\langle}F,G\rangle_{K}:=\sum_{\kappa\in K}{\langle}F_{\kappa},G_{\kappa}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

for
F=(F_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K},G=(G_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Indeed,

c_{00}(K^{\prime};\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq c_{00}(K^{\prime};\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}=c_{00}(K^{\prime};\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is dense for each finite subset
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
, as follows from the density of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and this in turn implies that
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
is dense. Also note that
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is dense for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, by Lemma
2.2
and because the finite sequences
c_{00}(K)
are dense in
\ell^{p}(K)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
be the space of smooth compactly supported half densities on
M
, and let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
be its dual, the space of distributional half densities. Denote by
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
the associated duality pairing. Then
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is the completion of
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
with respect to the norm

(3.2)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})}:={\langle}u,\overline{u}\rangle_{M}^{1/2},

for
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. Write
\kappa_{*}=(\kappa^{-1})^{*}
, where
\kappa^{*}=(\kappa^{*})_{1/2}
is pull-back by
\kappa
, acting on half densities. For
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
G=(g_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, set

(3.3)

{\langle}Qu,G\rangle_{K}:=\sum_{\kappa\in K}{\langle}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u),g_{\kappa}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Moreover, for
F=(f_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, set

(3.4)

PF:=\sum_{\kappa\in K}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f_{\kappa}).

3.2.
Basic properties

The following proposition collects some basic properties of the operators
Q
and
P
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following properties hold:

(1)

Q:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
and
Q:\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
are continuous;

(2)

P:c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is continuous, and
P
extends uniquely to a continuous linear operator
P:c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
;

(3)

{\langle}PF,v\rangle_{M}={\langle}F,Qv\rangle_{K}
for all
F\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
and
v\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, and
{\langle}Qu,G\rangle_{K}={\langle}u,PG\rangle_{M}
for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
G\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
;

(4)

PQu=u
for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
;

(5)

Q:L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to\ell^{2}(K;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is an isometry.

Proof.

(
1
): Firstly,
\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)=\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}\kappa_{*}u
is a well-defined and compactly supported distribution on
\tilde{U}_{\kappa}
, for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
\kappa\in K
. In particular, it extends to a distribution on all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting it equal to zero outside of
\tilde{U}_{\kappa}
. Moreover, the sum in (
3.3
) is finite for each
G\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, and appropriate bounds hold for the resulting functional because
(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, suppose that
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, and note that
\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\in\mathcal{D}(\tilde{U}_{\kappa})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
\kappa\in K
, because
\psi_{\kappa}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U_{\kappa})
. Moreover, since the cover
(U_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
of
M
is locally finite, a given compact set only intersects finitely many of the
U_{\kappa}
. Hence
\psi_{\kappa}u=0
for all but finitely many
\kappa\in K
, and

Qu=(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

with the appropriate bounds for the associated seminorms.

(
2
): Let
F=(f_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Then
\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f_{\kappa})\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
for all
\kappa\in K
, since
\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f_{\kappa}\in\mathcal{D}(\tilde{U}_{\kappa})
. Moreover, the sum in (
3.4
) is finite, so
PF\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, with the appropriate bounds for the associated seminorms. The second statement now follows from the fact that
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is dense in
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
.

(
3
): Let
F=(f_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
v\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. Then

{\langle}PF,v\rangle_{M}=\sum_{\kappa\in K}{\langle}\psi_{\kappa}\kappa^{*}f_{\kappa},v\rangle_{M}=\sum_{\kappa\in K}{\langle}f_{\kappa},\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}={\langle}F,Qv\rangle_{K}.

By (
2
) and because
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
lies dense in
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
, this proves the first statement. Similarly, since
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is dense, it suffices to prove the second statement for
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. But in this case one can simply use the first statement.

(
4
): By (
1
) and (
2
), and because
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is dense, it suffices to consider
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. In this case we have

PQu=\sum_{\kappa\in K}\psi_{\kappa}\kappa^{*}(Qu)_{\kappa}=\sum_{\kappa\in K}\psi_{\kappa}\kappa^{*}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)=u,

since
(\psi_{\kappa}^{2})_{\kappa\in K}
is a partition of unity.

(
5
): Let
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and note that

\overline{(Qu)_{\kappa}(y)}=\overline{\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)(y)}=(Q\overline{u})_{\kappa}(y)

for all
\kappa\in K
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by definition of the pullback of a smooth density (see (
A.6
)) and because
\psi_{\kappa}
is real-valued. Now (
3
) and (
4
) yield

\|Qu\|_{\ell^{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{2}={\langle}Qu,\overline{Qu}\rangle_{K}={\langle}PQu,\overline{u}\rangle_{M}={\langle}u,\overline{u}\rangle_{M}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})}^{2},

by definition of the
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
norm in (
3.2
). This concludes the proof, given that
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is the completion of
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
with respect to this norm.
∎

3.3.
Operators between sequence spaces

In this subsection we will consider operators between
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued sequence spaces.

Throughout, let
(N,g^{\prime})
be another complete
n
-dimensional
Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry, with smooth structure
{\mathcal{B}}
,
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(N)/9]
,
L\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}

and
(\theta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
be data as in Lemma
A.1
, with
\theta_{\lambda}:U_{\lambda}\to\tilde{U}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
\lambda\in L
. That is,
\varepsilon
,
L

and
(\theta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}
have similar properties as
\delta
,
K
and
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
from the previous subsection. Set
\tilde{\theta}_{\lambda}:=\theta_{\lambda}\circ\lambda^{-1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\tilde{U}_{\lambda})
for
\lambda\in L
.
Next, let
Q^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
be defined as
Q
and
P
:

(3.5)

{\langle}Q^{\prime}u,G\rangle_{L}:=\sum_{\lambda\in L}{\langle}\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}u),g_{\lambda}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

for
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
and
G=(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\in c_{00}(L;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, and

(3.6)

P^{\prime}F:=\sum_{\lambda\in L}\lambda^{*}(\tilde{\theta}_{\lambda}f_{\lambda})

for
F=(f_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\in c_{00}(L;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Then
Q^{\prime}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N,\Omega_{1/2})\to c_{00}(L;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
and
P^{\prime}:c_{00}(L;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
have the same properties as
Q
and
P
, from Proposition
3.1
.

Before we get to the main results of this section, we prove an abstract lemma about vector-valued sequence spaces. Recall that
|E|
denotes the cardinality of a finite set
E
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces, let
I
and
J
be countable sets, and let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Let
A=(A_{ij})_{i\in I,j\in J}
be an operator matrix with the following properties:

(1)

A_{ij}\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
for all
i\in I
and
j\in J
, and

\sup\{\|A_{ij}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}\mid i\in I,j\in J\}<\infty;

(2)

\sup_{i\in I}|J(i)|<\infty
, where
J(i):=\{j\in J\mid A_{ij}\neq 0\}
for
i\in I
, and
\sup_{j\in J}|I(j)|<\infty
, where
I(j):=\{i\in I\mid A_{ij}\neq 0\}
for
j\in J
.

Then
A:\ell^{p}(J;X)\to\ell^{p}(I;Y)
is bounded.

Proof.

We consider
p<\infty
, with the proof for
p=\infty
being identical up to an obvious change of notation.

Let
F=(f_{j})_{j\in J}\in\ell^{p}(J;X)
. Then Hölder’s inequality and (
2
) yield

\displaystyle\|(AF)_{i}\|_{Y}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{j\in J(i)}A_{ij}f_{j}\Big\|_{Y}\leq\sum_{j\in J(i)}\|A_{ij}f_{j}\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j\in J(i)}\|A_{ij}f_{j}\|_{Y}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

for an implicit constant independent of
i\in I
. Now combine this with (
1
) and (
2
):

\displaystyle\|AF\|_{\ell^{p}(I;Y)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\|(AF)_{i}\|_{Y}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J(i)}\|A_{ij}f_{j}\|_{Y}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{i\in I(j)}\|A_{ij}f_{j}\|_{Y}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{i\in I(j)}\|f_{j}\|_{X}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(J;X)},

for implicit constants independent of
F
.
∎

We now consider a specific instance of this lemma, involving the types of operators that we will encounter in the next section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s,m\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
T\in I^{m}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
, for
{\mathcal{C}}
a local canonical graph. Suppose that the following conditions hold:

(1)

There exists a
C\geq 0
such that
\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for all
\lambda\in L
,
\kappa\in K
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;

(2)

\sup_{\lambda\in L}|K(\lambda)|<\infty
, where
K(\lambda):=\{\kappa\in K\mid\theta_{\lambda}T\psi_{\kappa}\neq 0\}
for
\lambda\in L
, and
\sup_{\kappa\in K}|L(\kappa)|<\infty
, where
L(\kappa):=\{\lambda\in L\mid\theta_{\lambda}T\psi_{\kappa}\neq 0\}
for
\kappa\in K
.

Then
Q^{\prime}TP:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded.

Condition (
2
) implies that
T
is properly supported, and it in fact prescribes proper support in a certain uniform sense.

Proof.

We will apply Lemma
3.2
with
A_{\lambda\kappa}f:=\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))
for
\lambda\in L
,
\kappa\in K
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. To this end, we only need to address a minor technicality, by checking that
Q^{\prime}TP
is given by matrix multiplication with
A=(A_{\lambda\kappa})_{\lambda\in L,\kappa\in K}
.

Indeed,
Q^{\prime}TPF\in c_{00}(L,\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
for each
F\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, by Proposition
3.1
. However, a priori
Q^{\prime}TPF
is defined through adjoint action, as

(3.7)

{\langle}Q^{\prime}TPF,G\rangle_{L}=\sum_{\lambda\in L}{\langle}PF,T^{t}\lambda^{*}(\tilde{\theta}_{\lambda}g_{\lambda})\rangle_{M}

for
G=(g_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\in c_{00}(L;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, where
P:c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is the unique continuous extension of (
3.4
). On the other hand, for
F\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
one can use (
3.4
) to obtain
Q^{\prime}TPF=AF
, and for a general
F\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
one can approximate by test sequences in (
3.7
) to obtain the same conclusion.
∎

The following corollary of Theorem
3.3
deals with the special case where
N=M
and
T
is the identity operator, although we still allow the sequence
(\theta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
to be distinct from
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
. This corollary will frequently be used in the next section, and allowing for distinct sequences will enable us to prove that the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is independent of the choice of such a sequence.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
Q^{\prime}P:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
3.3
with
T
the identity operator, which is an FIO of order zero associated with the graph of the identity map on
T^{*}M\setminus o
. It thus suffices to verify conditions (
1
) and (
2
).

For (
1
), we only have to consider
\lambda\in L
and
\kappa\in K
with
U_{\lambda}\cap U_{\kappa}\neq\emptyset
.
Then Corollary
2.5
, Remark
2.6
, (
A.3
) and Lemma
A.1
(
4
) yield

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\theta}_{\lambda}\mu_{\kappa\lambda}^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\mu_{\kappa\lambda}^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for implicit constants independent of
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\lambda\in L
and
\kappa\in K
.

For (
2
), we suppose that
\delta\geq\varepsilon
, with the proof for the other case being analogous. First fix
\lambda\in L
, and note that
K(\lambda)\subseteq\{\kappa\in K\mid U_{\lambda}\cap U_{\kappa}\neq\emptyset\}
. Hence, for a given
\kappa\in K(\lambda)
, one has
U_{3\delta}(p_{\kappa})\cap U_{3\delta}(p_{\kappa^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
for all
\kappa^{\prime}\in K(\lambda)
. Since
\delta\leq\mathrm{inj}(M)/9
, the conclusion for
|K(\lambda)|
now follows from Lemma
A.1
(
2
). One can similarly apply Lemma
A.1
(
2
) for fixed
\kappa\in K
and
\lambda\in L(\kappa)
, since then
U_{3\delta}(p_{\lambda})\cap U_{3\delta}(p_{\lambda^{\prime}})\neq\emptyset
for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in L(\kappa)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Theorem
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
were formulated for
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued sequences, but both relied on Lemma

, and one could equally well apply that abstract result with

. Then condition (

) in Theorem

will typically not be satisfied for general Fourier integral operators, and pseudodifferential operators form a more natural class to consider. For us, it is only relevant to note that Corollary

holds with
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
replaced by
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as follows from the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is also invariant under coordinate changes as in Corollary
2.5
and Remark
2.6
(see e.g.
[
52
, Sections 4.2 and 4.3]
).

3.4.
A localization principle

In this subsection, we will consider the special case where
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and prove a localization principle for the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Throughout this subsection, let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and

(3.8)

\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\psi(x-\kappa)^{2}=1\quad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Set
\psi_{\kappa}(x):=\psi(x-\kappa)
for
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and note that
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is a uniformly locally finite family as in Lemma
A.1
. Here the associated family of geodesic charts consists of translations
(\tau_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
of a single ball
B
, centered at zero and containing
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)
.

More precisely, set
\tau_{\kappa}(x):=x-\kappa
for
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\tau_{\kappa}:B+\kappa\to B
is a geodesic normal chart as in Lemma
A.1
for each
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is the associated uniformly locally finite family. In this case, the operators
Q
and
P
from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) are given by

(3.9)

{\langle}Qf,G\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}{\langle}\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}(\psi_{\kappa}f),g_{\kappa}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}{\langle}\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}f,g_{\kappa}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},

for
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
G=(g_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\in c_{00}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, and

(3.10)

PF=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\tau_{\kappa}^{*}(\psi f_{\kappa}),

for
F=(f_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in c_{00}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Now, for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, an
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
(\psi_{\kappa}f)_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, in which case

(3.11)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|(\psi_{\kappa}f)_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}=\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi_{\kappa}f\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)^{1/p},

with the obvious modification in the final identity for
p=\infty
. This statement is essentially contained in
[
52
, Theorem 2.4.7]
, although there only the case
p<\infty
is considered, and
\psi_{\kappa}
is replaced by
\psi_{\kappa}^{2}
. To obtain (
3.11
) one can rely on the arguments below, combined with Remark
3.5
and the atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in Lemma
B.2
.

The following theorem is an analogue of (
3.11
) for
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
C>0
such that an
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
(\psi_{\kappa}f)_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, in which case

\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|(\psi_{\kappa}f)_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let
Q
and
P
be as in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). It suffices to show that

Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\text{ and }P:\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

are bounded, given that the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm is invariant under translations, and that
PQf=f
for all
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We claim that it in fact suffices to show that
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. To prove this claim, first note that
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded, since

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here we used that
\psi
acts boundedly on
\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Corollary
2.5
, and that the
\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm is invariant under translations. Moreover, by Corollary
3.4
,
QP:\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is a bounded projection for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence, if we know that
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded, then interpolation of complemented subspaces (see
[
49
, Theorem 1.17.1.1]
) shows that
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded.

On the other hand, the boundedness of
P:\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is immediate. Indeed, for all
F=(f_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
one has

\|PF\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\tau_{\kappa}^{*}(\psi f_{\kappa})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|f_{\kappa}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover, if
Q:\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded, then for all
F\in\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
one has, by Proposition
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
,

\displaystyle|{\langle}PF,g\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|

\displaystyle=|{\langle}PF,PQg\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|=|{\langle}QPF,Qg\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|QPF\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|Qg\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here we also used that

\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))^{*},

by
[
24
, Proposition 1.3.1]
and because
\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=(\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{-s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is dense, so
P:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded. Now interpolation of complemented subspaces shows that
P:\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded for all
p\in[1,\infty]
an
s\in\mathbb{R}
. This proves the claim.

It remains to show that
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{n};\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded. To this end, we will rely on the atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. More precisely, by Proposition
B.1
, it suffices to prove that

(3.12)

\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}(r(D)f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and that

(3.13)

\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

whenever
f
is an
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atom associated with a ball of radius at most
1
. Here
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
r(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
and
r(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq 2
, although we will only use that
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For (
3.12
), fix
N\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
N>s+s(1)
. Then (
2.2
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}(r(D)f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}(r(D)f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s+s(1),1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}(r(D)f)\|_{W^{N,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi_{\kappa}r(D)f\|_{W^{N,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},|\alpha|\leq N}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{\kappa}r(D)f)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for implicit constants independent of
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, since
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a uniformly locally finite family, we can write
\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\sqcup_{j=1}^{M}A_{j}
for some
M\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, where each
A_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has the property that
\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\kappa})\cap\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\lambda})=\emptyset
whenever
\kappa,\lambda\in A_{j}
. Hence, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
with
|\alpha|\leq N
we can combine Leibniz’ rule with the Sobolev embeddings in (
2.2
):

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{\kappa}r(D)f)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sum_{\kappa\in A_{j}}\int_{\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\kappa})}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{\kappa}r(D)f)(x)|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\sum_{\kappa\in A_{j}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(\psi_{\kappa}r(D)f)(x)\Big|\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{M}\|r(D)f\|_{W^{N,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-s(1),1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here we also used that
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This proves (
3.12
).

On the other hand, let
f
be an
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atom, associated with a ball in
S^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius at most
1
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(f)
is contained in a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius at most
1
, by (
B.1
). Hence the collection
K^{\prime}
of
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
\psi\tau_{\kappa}^{*}f\neq 0
has at most
N^{\prime}\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
elements, where
N^{\prime}
is independent of
f
. We thus obtain (
3.13
):

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa\in K^{\prime}}\|\psi\tau_{-\kappa}^{*}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in K^{\prime}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on manifolds

In this section we define the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on manifolds, and we derive their fundamental properties.

Throughout,
(M,g)
is a complete
Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
with bounded geometry, with smooth structure
{\mathcal{A}}
(see Section
A.1
).

4.1.
Definitions

Throughout, fix
\delta\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M)/9]
,
K\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
and a uniformly locally finite family
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
as in Lemma
A.1
, with
\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}:=\psi_{\kappa}\circ\kappa^{-1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\tilde{U}_{\kappa})
for
\kappa\in K
. We will interchangeably write
U_{\kappa}
and
U_{\delta}(p_{\kappa})
for the domain of
\kappa
. Recall also the definition of the pullback

(\kappa^{-1})^{*}=(\kappa^{-1})^{*}_{1/2}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(U_{\kappa},\Omega_{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\tilde{U}_{\kappa}),

cf. (
A.7
), and that
\kappa_{*}=(\kappa^{-1})^{*}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
consists of those
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
such that
(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))_{\kappa\in K}\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, with the norm

(4.1)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}:=\|(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

We write
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(M):=\mathcal{H}^{0,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Note that, after trivially identifying
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\Omega_{1/2})
, Definition
4.1
extends the case where
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
from Definition
2.1
, as follows from Theorem
3.6
.

To relate the Hardy spaces for FIOs to classical function spaces on manifolds, we also need to define the latter. This is done in the same way as in Definition
4.1
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
consists of those
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
such that
(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))_{\kappa\in K}\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M))
, with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)}:=\|(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}.

We write
\mathcal{H}^{p}(M):=\mathcal{H}^{0,p}(M)
, and
\mathrm{bmo}(M):=\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(M)
.

Remark 4.3
.

When formulating Definition
4.1
using the typical identification of a distributional density
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
with a sequence of distributions
(u_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in{\mathcal{A}}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, via
u_{\kappa}=\kappa_{*}u
, one gets

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}=\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}u_{\kappa}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

for
p<\infty
, with the obvious modification for
p=\infty
. Note that if
M
is compact and
K
is finite, then
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if and only if
\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}u_{\kappa}\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\kappa\in K
. The same statements hold with
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
replaced by
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
.

In Section
4.6
we will show that, for
1<p<\infty
, the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
coincide with classical Sobolev spaces on
M
as introduced in
[
1
,
47
]
in a coordinate-free manner, up to a fractional power of the Riemannian density
\rho_{g}\in C^{\infty}(M,\Omega_{1})
. To this end, we first define Sobolev spaces on
M
using fractional powers of the non-positive Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
. Recall that the latter acts on smooth functions
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
in local coordinates by

(4.2)

\Delta_{g}f:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i}\big(\sqrt{\det g}\,g^{ij}\partial_{j}f\big).

Definition 4.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
W^{s,p}(M)
is the completion of
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
with respect to the norm

(4.3)

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(M)}:=\Big(\int_{M}|(1-\Delta_{g})^{s/2}(\rho_{g}^{-1/2}u)(x)|^{p}\rho_{g}(x)\Big)^{1/p}

for
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. We write
L^{p}(M):=W^{0,p}(M)
.

Note that
\rho_{g}^{-1/2}u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{0})=C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
for each
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, so that
(1-\Delta_{g})^{s/2}(\rho^{-1/2}_{g}u)\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{0})
is a well defined function for
s\leq 0
, due to the spectral theorem. It follows from abstract methods (see e.g.
[
47
,
16
]
) that (
4.3
) is finite for such
u
and
s
. For general
s\in\mathbb{R}
one sets

(1-\Delta_{g})^{s/2}f:=(1-\Delta_{g})^{s/2-k}(1-\Delta_{g})^{k}f

for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
and some
k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
with
k\geq s/2
, which is independent of the choice of
k
. Then
W^{s,p}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
.

4.2.
Basic properties

In this subsection we derive the basic properties of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
. To this end, and also in later subsections, we will use the operators
Q
and
P
from Section
3
. This is natural because a
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
satisfies
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if and only if
Qu\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, in which case

(4.4)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}=\|Qu\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

as follows from the definition of
Q
in (
3.3
).

We first show that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is a Banach space.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)\to\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is an isometry, and
P:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is bounded. In particular,
Q\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)\subseteq\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is a complemented subspace,

(4.5)

P:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))/\ker(P)\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)

is an isomorphism, and
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is a Banach space.

Proof.

The first statement is (
4.4
). The second statement follows by observing that

QP:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is bounded, by Corollary
3.4
in the special case where
Q^{\prime}=Q
. Now Proposition
3.1
(
4
) shows that
P:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is surjective, which implies that (
4.5
) is an isomorphism. For the same reason,
Q\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is the range of the bounded projection
QP
on
\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Since complemented subspaces are closed, or alternatively because
\ker(P)
is closed,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is a Banach space.
∎

Next, we show that the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is independent of the choice of data as in Lemma
A.1
. To this end, let
\varepsilon\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M)/9]
,
L\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
and a uniformly locally finite family
(\theta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)

as in Lemma
A.1
be given, possibly different from the data that was used to define
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
C>0
with the following property. A
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
satisfies
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if and only if
(\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}u))_{\lambda\in L}\in\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, in which case

(4.6)

\frac{1}{C}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}\leq\|(\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}u))_{\lambda\in L}\|_{\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}.

Proof.

We use the operators
Q^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
). More precisely, applying Proposition
3.1
(
4
) with
Q
and
P
replaced by
Q^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
, we have
P^{\prime}Q^{\prime}u=u
for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. Hence Proposition
4.5
and Corollary
3.4
yield

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

\displaystyle=\|Qu\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}=\|QP^{\prime}Q^{\prime}u\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q^{\prime}u\|_{\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))},

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
for which the right-hand side is finite. This proves the first inequality in (
4.6
). The second inequality follows by symmetry.
∎

The following lemma deals with the simplest case of the theory, where
p=2
.

Lemma 4.7
.

One has
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(M)=L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, with equivalence of norms.

Proof.

This follows directly from Propositions
3.1
(
5
) and
4.5
, since
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with equivalence of norms.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The definition of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in Definition
2.1
does not yield equality of the
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norms. However, in
[
17
]
the Hardy spaces for FIOs were defined in such a way that
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
isometrically.
It thus follows from the proof of Lemma
4.7
that, when starting off with the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from
[
17
]
, one has
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(M)=L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
isometrically.

To conclude this subsection, we consider the smooth compactly supported half densities as a subset of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
. Moreover, if
p<\infty
, then
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
(
1
),
Qu\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for all
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. Since
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, this implies that
Qu\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, which in turn means that
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, by Proposition
4.5
.

Now suppose that
p<\infty
, and let
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
. Then
c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
lies dense, as remarked in Section
3.1
, so there exists a sequence
(F_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\subseteq c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
such that
F_{j}\to Qu
in
\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, as
j\to\infty
. Then, by Propositions
3.1
and
4.5
,
(PF_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and

\|PF_{j}-u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}=\|PF_{j}-PQu\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}\lesssim\|F_{j}-Qu\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\to 0

as
j\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

It was shown in
[
17
, Proposition 6.6]
(for
s=0
, which suffices) that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The first part of Proposition
4.9
could be strengthened to also cover this statement, by considering
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
for which each of the Schwartz seminorms of
(Qu)_{\kappa}=\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is rapidly decreasing in
\kappa\in K
.

4.3.
Interpolation and duality

In this subsection we extend interpolation and duality properties of the Hardy spaces for FIOs from
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

First, we prove that the Hardy spaces for FIOs form a complex interpolation scale.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
p_{0},p_{1},p\in[1,\infty]
,
s_{0},s_{1},s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in[0,1]
be such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
,
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
and
(p_{0},p_{1})\neq(\infty,\infty)
. Then

[\mathcal{H}^{s_{0},p_{0}}_{FIO}(M),\mathcal{H}^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{FIO}(M)]_{\theta}=\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)

with equivalent norms.

Proof.

By (
2.3
) and by a basic fact about interpolation of vector-valued function spaces (see
[
24
, Theorem 2.2.6]
), one has

[\ell^{p_{0}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s_{0},p_{0}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),\ell^{p_{1}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))]_{\theta}=\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

for
(p_{0},p_{1})\neq(\infty,\infty)
. Then one can use interpolation of complemented subspaces, cf.
[
49
, Theorem 1.17.1.1]
, combined with Corollary
3.4
, to conclude that

[QP\ell^{p_{0}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s_{0},p_{0}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),QP\ell^{p_{1}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))]_{\theta}=QP\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

with equivalence of norms. Since
Q:\mathcal{H}^{t,q}_{FIO}(M)\to QP\ell^{q}(K;\mathcal{H}^{t,q}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is an isomorphism for all
q\in[1,\infty]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, this proves the required statement.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

The reason for the restriction
(p_{0},p_{1})\neq(\infty,\infty)
in Proposition
4.11
is that we used a general fact about interpolation of Bochner spaces,
[
24
, Theorem 2.2.6]
, the proof of which relies on approximation by simple functions. Since the finite sequences do not lie dense in
\ell^{\infty}
, this approach does not immediately extend to
p_{0}=p_{1}=\infty
. For the sake of brevity and because the latter case plays no real role in this article, we will not attempt to extend Proposition
4.11
. We do note that Proposition
4.13
below and
[
49
, Lemma 1.11.3]
immediately yield

[\mathcal{H}^{s_{0},\infty}_{FIO}(M),\mathcal{H}^{s_{1},\infty}_{FIO}(M)]_{\theta}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(M).

Moreover, if
M
is compact and
K
is finite, then Proposition
4.11
also holds for
p_{0}=p_{1}=\infty
, because in this case
\ell^{\infty}(K,\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))=\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for any
1\leq p<\infty
, so that one can apply
[
24
, Theorem 2.2.6]
after all.

Next, we extend the duality statement in (
2.4
) from
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
. We write
{\langle}f,g\rangle_{s,p}
for the duality pairing between
f\in\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which in turn is equal to the standard duality
{\langle}f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
if
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Also recall the duality pairing
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{K}
for vector-valued sequence spaces over
K
from (
3.1
).

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M))^{*}=\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)

with equivalent norms, where the duality pairing is given by

(4.7)

{\langle}Qu,Qv\rangle_{K}=\sum_{\kappa\in K}{\langle}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u),\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\rangle_{s,p},

for
u\in\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)
and
v\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, and by
{\langle}u,v\rangle_{M}
if
v\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
.

Proof.

For the most part, the statement follows from Proposition
4.5
and the characterization of the dual of a quotient space, but we argue slightly more explicitly.

First let
u\in\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
, (
4.7
) defines a functional on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{\kappa\in K}{\langle}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u),\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\rangle_{s,p}\Big|\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\|Qu\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\|Qv\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)},

for an implicit constant independent of
u
and
v\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with the obvious notational modification for
p^{\prime}=\infty
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.1
, if
v\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
then
{\langle}Qu,Qv\rangle_{K}={\langle}u,v\rangle_{M}
. In particular, if
{\langle}Qu,Qv\rangle_{K}=0
for all
v\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, then
u=0
. Hence
\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)\subseteq(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M))^{*}
.

Conversely, let
l\in(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M))^{*}
. Then
l\circ P\in(\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
, by Proposition
4.5
. Moreover, since
(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}=\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
[
24
, Proposition 1.3.3]
yields

(4.8)

(\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))^{*}=\ell^{p^{\prime}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

with the duality pairing
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{K}
. Hence there exists an
F\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}(K;\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
such that
l(P(G))={\langle}F,G\rangle_{K}
for all
G\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})))
. Set
u:=PF
. Then
u\in\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)
, by Proposition
4.5
. Now Proposition
3.1
implies that

{\langle}u,v\rangle_{M}={\langle}PF,v\rangle_{M}={\langle}F,Qv\rangle_{K}=l(PQv)=l(v)

for all
v\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. Finally, Propositions
3.1
and
4.5
yield

\displaystyle|{\langle}Qu,G\rangle_{K}|

\displaystyle=|{\langle}u,PG\rangle_{M}|=|{\langle}F,QPG\rangle_{K}|=|l(PQPG)|=|l(PG)|

\displaystyle\leq\|l\|\|PG\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}\lesssim\|l\|\|G\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

for all
G\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. This concludes the proof, since it follows from (
4.8
) that

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(M)}=\|Qu\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\eqsim\sup|{\langle}Qu,G\rangle_{K}|,

where the supremum is over all
G\in c_{00}(K;\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with
\|G\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq 1
.
∎

4.4.
Sobolev embeddings and Fourier integral operators

In this subsection we extend the Sobolev embeddings for
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, and we show that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is invariant under suitable FIOs.

We first extend the Sobolev embeddings in (
2.2
) from
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(4.9)

\mathcal{H}^{s+s(p),p}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}(M).

Combined with Corollary
4.21
below, (
4.9
) implies (
1.3
).

Proof.

This follows directly by arguing in local coordinates. That is, by (
2.2
),

\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s+s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for implicit constants independent of
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
\kappa\in K
, whenever the relevant quantities are finite.
∎

We note that the exponents in (
4.9
) are sharp, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, on each Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with bounded geometry. This follows from the fact that the “loss of
2s(p)
derivatives” in
[
40
]
is sharp on every compact manifold.

Next, let
(N,g^{\prime})
be another complete
Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry. In the case where
(N,g^{\prime})=(M,g)
and
m=0
, the following theorem extends the invariance of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under suitable Fourier integral operators to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
T\in I^{m}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
, for
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
a local canonical graph, and suppose that
T
is compactly supported. Then

T:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(N)

is bounded for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
Q^{\prime}:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(N)\to\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
be the isometry from (
3.5
). By Proposition
3.1
(
4
), one has
Q^{\prime}Tu=Q^{\prime}TPQu
for all
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)
. Given that
Q:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)\to\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is an isometry as well, it thus suffices to prove that
Q^{\prime}TP:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(L;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is bounded.

To this end, we show that the conditions of Theorem
3.3
are satisfied. Note that, since
T
is compactly supported, (
2
) automatically holds, and for (
1
) we may fix
\kappa\in K
and
\lambda\in L
and ignore the dependence of the constants on
\kappa
and
\lambda
. Moreover, the operators
f\mapsto\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f)
and
v\mapsto\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}v)
are compactly supported FIOs of order zero, associated with local canonical graphs, as follows e.g. from the proof of Corollary
2.5
, after representing the operators in local coordinates. Hence, by the composition theorem for FIOs,
f\mapsto\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))
is a compactly supported element of
I^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
, associated with a local canonical graph
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
. Proposition
2.3
concludes the proof.
∎

4.5.
An atomic decomposition

In this subsection we provide an atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)
. This decomposition is a direct consequence of that of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in Appendix
B
, by working in local coordinates. For the sake of brevity and because we will typically work in local coordinates anyway, we focus on the decomposition as such, instead of introducing terminology for the resulting atoms on
M
and developing a full atomic theory. We use notation and terminology as in Appendix
B
. In particular,
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
r(\xi)=1
if
|\xi|\leq 1
, and
r(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\geq 2
.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the collection of all
\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f)
, for
\kappa\in K
and
f
an
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atom associated with a ball of radius at most 1, is a uniformly bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)
. Moreover, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a
C_{t}\geq 0
with the following property. For all
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)
and
\kappa\in K
there exist a
v\in\mathcal{H}^{t,1}_{FIO}(M)
, a sequence
(f_{j,\kappa})_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atoms associated with balls of radius at most
1
, and an
(\alpha_{j,\kappa})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{1}
, such that

(4.10)

u=v+\sum_{\kappa\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j,\kappa}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f_{j,\kappa})

and

\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{t,1}_{FIO}(M)}+\sum_{\kappa\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{j,\kappa}|\leq C_{t}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)}.

In fact, one may choose

(4.11)

v:=\sum_{\kappa\in K}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}r(D)\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)).

Proof.

To prove the first statement, let
f
be an
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atom associated with a ball of radius at most
1
, and let
\kappa\in K
. Set
K(\kappa):=\{\lambda\in K\mid U_{\kappa}\cap U_{\lambda}\neq\emptyset\}
and note that
|K(\kappa)|
is uniformly bounded in
\kappa
, by Lemma
A.1
(
2
). Hence one can use Corollary
2.5
, Remark
2.6
, Lemma
A.1
(
4
) and (
A.3
) to write

\displaystyle\|\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)}=\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\|\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\|\tilde{\psi}_{\lambda}\mu_{\kappa\lambda}^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 1,

where for the final inequality we used the first statement in Proposition
B.1
.

Next, fix
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)
and let
\kappa\in K
. Then
\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\in\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so Proposition
B.1
yields a collection
(f_{j,\kappa})_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-atoms, associated with balls of radius at most
1
, and an
(\alpha_{j,\kappa})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{1}
, such that

\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)=r(D)(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j,\kappa}f_{j,\kappa}.

Let
v
be as in (
4.11
). Then we can use that
(\psi_{\kappa}^{2})_{\kappa\in K}
is a partition of unity to obtain (
4.10
):

u=\sum_{\kappa\in K}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))=v+\sum_{\kappa\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j,\kappa}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f_{j,\kappa}).

Moreover, again by Proposition
B.1
,

\sum_{\kappa\in K}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{j,\kappa}|\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)}.

Also, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}^{t,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}r(D)\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{t,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|r(D)\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{t,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(M)}.

Here we used that
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in the penultimate step.
∎

In the same manner, relying instead on the atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
from Lemma
B.2
, one obtains an atomic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}(M)
. For alternative approaches to atomic decompositions of
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}(M)
we refer to
[
42
,
48
]
.

4.6.
Equivalent norms

In this subsection we provide equivalent norms on both
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
and
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
.

We first show that one could alternatively define
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
by replacing
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
in (
4.1
) by the partition of unity
(\psi_{\kappa}^{2})_{\kappa\in K}
. Nonetheless, we have chosen to work with
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
because it is more natural for the
L^{2}
theory and for duality purposes, cf. Remark
4.8
and Proposition
4.13
, and because the operators
Q
and
P
have better mapping properties when defined in the current symmetric manner, cf. Proposition
3.1
and the proofs in the rest of this section.

Proposition 4.18
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a
C>0
with the following property. A
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
satisfies
u\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if and only if
(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}u))_{\kappa\in K}\in\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, in which case

(4.12)

\frac{1}{C}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}\leq\|(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}u))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}.

Proof.

The right-hand side of (
4.12
) follows from the estimate

\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for each
\kappa\in K
, for which we used Corollary
2.5
, Remark
2.6
and Lemma
A.1
(
4
).

On the other hand, for
\kappa\in K
we have, with
K(\kappa):=\{\lambda\in K\mid U_{\kappa}\cap U_{\lambda}\neq\emptyset\}
,

\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)=\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u)=\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}\mu_{\lambda\kappa}^{*}\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u).

By Lemma
A.1
,
|K(\kappa)|
is uniformly bounded in
\kappa
. Hence we can combine a minor modification of Corollary
2.5
and Remark
2.6
with Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\|\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\|\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

with the obvious notational modification for
p=\infty
.
This in turn yields

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in K}\sum_{\lambda\in K(\kappa)}\|\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in K}\sum_{\kappa\in K(\lambda)}\|\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in K}\|\lambda_{*}(\psi_{\lambda}^{2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

for implicit constants independent of
u
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

The same proof yields an analogue of Proposition
4.18
for
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
. In fact, it follows as in the proof that, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

\|((\kappa_{*})_{\gamma}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}u))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\eqsim\|((\kappa_{*})_{\gamma}(\psi_{\kappa}u))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{\gamma})
such that either of these quantities is finite. Here
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{\gamma})
is the space of distributional densities of order
\gamma
(see Section
A.2
).

Remark 4.20
.

For
p<\infty
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
was defined in
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
to consist of all
v\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1})
such that
\|((\kappa_{*})_{1}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}v))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}<\infty
.
Recall that
\rho_{g}\in C^{\infty}(M,\Omega_{1})
is the Riemannian density on
M
. For each
\kappa\in K
one then has

\displaystyle\|(\kappa_{*})_{1}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}\rho_{g}^{1/2}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|(\kappa_{*})_{1/2}(\psi_{\kappa}\rho_{g}^{1/2})\cdot(\kappa_{*})_{1/2}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\kappa_{*})_{1/2}(\psi_{\kappa}u)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, and

\displaystyle\|(\kappa_{*})_{1/2}(\psi_{\kappa}^{2}\rho_{g}^{-1/2}v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\|(\kappa_{*})_{-1/2}(\psi_{\kappa}\rho_{g}^{-1/2})\cdot(\kappa_{*})_{1}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\kappa_{*})_{1}(\psi_{\kappa}v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
v\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1})
. Here we used Lemma
A.1
(
4
), as well as (
A.2
) to estimate away powers of
\rho_{g}
. It now follows from Remark
4.19
that
u\mapsto\rho_{g}^{1/2}u
is an isomorphism between the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
from Definition
4.2
and the one from
[
50
,
51
,
52
]
.

As a corollary of the previous remark, we can now show that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
coincides with
W^{s,p}(M)
, as introduced in Definition
4.4
, for
1<p<\infty
. We also give a more concrete definition of these spaces for
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, in terms of the
k
-th covariant derivative
\nabla^{k}
for
k\in\{0,\ldots,s\}
. Recall that, for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

|\nabla^{k}f|^{2}=g^{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\cdots g^{\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}}\nabla_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\alpha_{k}}f\cdot\nabla_{\beta_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{\beta_{k}}\overline{f}

using the Einstein summation convention, where each
\nabla_{j}
is a covariant derivative with respect to a given local chart.

Corollary 4.21
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)=W^{s,p}(M)
, with equivalent norms. Moreover, for each
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
there exists a
C>0
such that

(4.13)

\frac{1}{C}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)}\leq\max_{k\in\{0,\ldots,s\}}\Big(\int_{M}|\nabla^{k}(\rho_{g}^{-1/2}u)(x)|^{p}\rho_{g}(x)\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)}

for all
u\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
.

Proof.

Simply combine Remark
4.20
with
[
52
, Theorem 7.4.5]
.
∎

In particular,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
, for
1<p<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, is the completion of
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
with respect to the norm in (
4.13
).

5.
Local smoothing

In this section we prove our main local smoothing result for Fourier integral operators satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, Theorem
1.3
. To do so, we first derive a version of the variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities from
[
4
]
, with the Hardy spaces for FIOs as initial data.

Throughout this section, we fix a dimension
n\geq 2
. The phenomenon of local smoothing does not occur for
n=1
, since in that case the relevant operators are already bounded on the classical function spaces
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
at each fixed time.

5.1.
Variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities

In this subsection we will use the variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities from
[
4
]
to derive a statement involving the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators. In Appendix
C
we show that the resulting inequalities are, in a sense, equivalent to those in
[
4
]
, but for our main results we will only need one implication.

Throughout this subsection, we fix
m\in\mathbb{R}
and symbols
a_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and
a_{2}\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the standard Kohn–Nirenberg class of symbols depending only on the fiber variable.
Set
a(z,\eta):=a_{1}(z)a_{2}(\eta)
for
(z,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Next, let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}))
be real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree one in the fiber variable. Suppose that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0})=n
for all
(z_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
with
\eta_{0}\neq 0
, where we write
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Moreover, we assume that for all such
(z_{0},\eta_{0})
, one has

(5.1)

\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}(\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\eta)\cdot G(z_{0},\eta_{0}))|_{\eta=\eta_{0}}=n-1,

where
G(z_{0},\eta_{0}):=G_{0}(z_{0},\eta_{0})/|G_{0}(z_{0},\eta_{0})|
is the generalized Gauss map, with

G_{0}(z_{0},\eta_{0}):=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{\eta_{i}}\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0}).

Now set

(5.2)

Tf(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We note that
T
is a Fourier integral operator of order
m-1/4
, associated with a canonical relation
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition (see Section
A.3
). Later on we will use that the analysis of general FIOs satisfying this condition can be reduced to ones as in (
5.2
), by Lemma
A.2
.

Next, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we fix a maximal collection
\Theta_{k}\subseteq S^{n-1}
of unit vectors such that
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\geq 2^{-k/2}
for all
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Theta_{k}
with
\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}
. Let
(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be an associated partition of unity. More precisely, each
\chi_{\nu}
is positively homogeneous of degree
0
and satisfies
0\leq\chi_{\nu}\leq 1
and

\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\nu})\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\hat{\eta}-\nu|\leq 2^{-k/2+1}\}.

Moreover,
\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\chi_{\nu}(\eta)=1
for all
\eta\neq 0
, and for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
there exists a
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
independent of
k
such that, if
2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}
, then

|(\hat{\eta}\cdot\partial_{\eta})^{\beta}\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\chi_{\nu}(\eta)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}2^{-k(|\alpha|/2+\beta)}

for all
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
. Such a collection is straightforward to construct, in a similar manner as the wave packets in Section
2.1
(see also
[
46
, Section IX.4]
).

Now, for
p\in[1,\infty]
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
, define the
k
-th decoupling norm
of
Tf
as

(5.3)

\|Tf\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}:=\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|T\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

with the obvious modification for
p=\infty
.

The main result of this section relies crucially on the following variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequality from
[
4
]
. Recall the definition of the exponent
d(p)
from (
1.2
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then, for each
L\geq 0
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(5.4)

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq C\big(2^{k(d(p)+\varepsilon)}\|Tf\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}+2^{-kL}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
.

Remark 5.2
.

We note that
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
has a slightly different form, with a straightforward rescaling argument connecting the two versions. On the other hand, Theorem
5.1
follows directly from
[
5
, Theorem 53]
, where the term
2^{-kL}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is omitted. Theorem
5.1
is also contained in
[
4
, p. 424]
, although there additional support assumptions are made on the symbol
a
. Those assumptions can be removed by applying suitable partitions of unity.

We will now show that each of the decoupling norms can be bounded by the
\mathcal{H}^{m-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of the initial data.
The following proposition is a variable-coefficient version of
[
37
, Corollary 4.2]
, which concerns the solution operator to the Euclidean half-wave equation and related Fourier multipliers.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(5.5)

\|Tf\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
.

For
T
the solution operator to the Euclidean wave equation on a compact time interval, (
5.5
) is in fact an equivalence. For general FIOs the corresponding statement is more involved, and it is relegated to Appendix
C
.

Proof.

By replacing
T
by
T{\langle}D\rangle^{-m}
, we may suppose that
m=0
.

Set
T_{t}f(x):=Tf(x,t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then each
T_{t}
is an FIO of order
0
as in Corollary
2.4
, with uniformity in
t
on its support, phase function and symbol, as in Remark
2.6
. Suppose that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and let
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
. Then, by writing
\chi_{\nu}=\widetilde{\chi}_{\nu}\chi_{\nu}
for
\widetilde{\chi}_{\nu}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
with similar support and smoothness properties as
\chi_{\nu}
, a standard argument from the
L^{p}
theory of FIOs, contained in e.g.
[
46
, Section IX.4]
and the proof of
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
, shows that

(5.6)

\|T_{t}\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|T_{t}\widetilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for an implicit constant independent of
t
,
f
,
k
and
\nu
. This in turn yields

\displaystyle\|T\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{I}\|T_{t}\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{I}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for some bounded interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that
T_{t}=0
whenever
t\notin I
. Hence

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|T\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\eqsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where we used
[
37
, Proposition 4.1]
for the final step.
∎

We now arrive at a corollary which is crucial for the proof of our main result.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(5.7)

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We may again fix a specific value of
m
, although this time we choose
m=2s(p)-d(p)-\varepsilon
. This choice will be convenient, as it ensures that

(5.8)

\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

by (
2.2
). Moreover, we may suppose that
a(z,\eta)=0
whenever
|\eta|<1/2
, since the low frequencies only contribute a smoothing operator
R
, and

(5.9)

R:\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Our first goal is to preemptively deal with the
L^{2}
term in (
5.4
), which we will do by writing
f
as the sum of a compactly supported function and a remainder, on the latter of which the phase function
\Phi
of
T
is non-singular. The argument is similar to that in the proof of Corollary
2.4
.

More precisely, as in (
2.5
), set

c:=\sup\{|\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\eta)|\mid(z,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a),\eta\neq 0\}<\infty,

and let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\chi(y)=1
if
|y|\leq 2c
. Then the phase function of
T(1-\chi)
is non-singular, and integration by parts shows that
T(1-\chi)
is a smoothing operator. Hence we only have to show that

(5.10)

\|T\chi f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for an implicit constant independent of
f
.

To this end, fix a Littlewood–Paley decomposition
(\phi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with inverse Fourier transforms uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, such that
\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{k})\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for each
k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Then the compact support of
\chi
, combined with (
5.8
), yields

\|\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\chi f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for implicit constants independent of
k
and
f
. Also note that

\|\chi f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

by Corollary
2.5
. Hence, for each
k
, we can apply Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
, with
\varepsilon
replaced by
\varepsilon/2
:

\displaystyle\|T\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(d(p)+\varepsilon/2)}\|T\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}+2^{-kL}\|\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k(d(p)+\varepsilon/2)}\|\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+2^{-kL}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{-k\varepsilon/2}\|\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)+\varepsilon-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+2^{-kL}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k\varepsilon/2}\|\chi f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+2^{-kL}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k\varepsilon/2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

if we choose
L\geq\varepsilon/2
. Since
T\chi f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}T\phi_{k}(D)\chi f
, we thus obtain (
5.10
):

\displaystyle\|T\chi f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|T\phi_{k}(D)\chi f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k\varepsilon/2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\qed

Remark 5.5
.

We will use that, for a fixed phase function
\Phi
, the constant
C
in (
5.7
) only depends on
T
through the size of the spatial support of the symbol
a
, and on finitely many of its
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
seminorms. This follows either by keeping track of the constants in the proof of (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), or from an application of the closed graph theorem.

5.2.
Local smoothing

In this subsection we will prove our main theorem on local smoothing for Fourier integral operators.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
with bounded geometry, with smooth structure
{\mathcal{A}}
. Fix
K\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
and a uniformly locally finite family
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
as in Lemma
A.1
.
Let
Q
and
P
be the associated maps from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). Next, let
(N,g^{\prime})
be another complete Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry, this time of dimension
n+1
. Fix
L\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}
and a uniformly locally finite family
(\theta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
as in Lemma
A.1
, where
{\mathcal{B}}
is the smooth structure on
N
. Let
Q^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
be as in (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).

To prove our main result, it will be convenient to formulate a version of Theorem
3.3
adapted to the present setting.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
,
r,s,m\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
T\in I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
be compactly supported, for
{\mathcal{C}}
a canonical relation from
T^{*}M
to
T^{*}N
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. Suppose that, for all
\lambda\in L
and
\kappa\in K
, there exists a
C_{\lambda\kappa}\geq 0
such that, for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has
\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and

(5.11)

\|\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\leq C_{\lambda\kappa}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Then
Q^{\prime}TP:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{r,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(L;W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}))
is bounded.

Proof.

Just as was the case for Theorem
3.3
, the proof consists of an application of Lemma
3.2
, with
A_{\lambda\kappa}f:=\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))
. Note that condition (
2
) in Lemma
3.2
is automatically satisfied, since
T
is compactly supported. In particular, one may choose
C_{\lambda\kappa}=0
for all but finitely many
\lambda
and
\kappa
, so condition (
1
) is also satisfied.
∎

The main theorem of this section is as follows.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
T\in I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
, for
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
a canonical relation from
T^{*}M
to
T^{*}N
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, and suppose that
T
is compactly supported. Then

(5.12)

T:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)\to W^{s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon,p}(N)

is bounded for all
p\in(2,\infty)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

We first reduce to working in local coordinates. By Proposition
4.5
, Remark
4.14
and Corollary
4.21
,

Q^{\prime}:W^{s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon,p}(N)\to\ell^{p}(L;W^{s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}))

is an isomorphic embedding. By combining this with Propositions
4.5
and
3.1
, it suffices to show that

Q^{\prime}TP:\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\to\ell^{p}(L;W^{s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}))

is bounded. To this end, by Lemma
5.6
, we may fix
\lambda\in L
and
\kappa\in K
, and prove (
5.11
) with
r=s+m
and with
s
replaced by
s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon
. Moreover, by the composition theorem for FIOs, using also that the cinematic curvature condition is invariant under changes of coordinates,
f\mapsto\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))
is a compactly supported element of
I^{m-1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
, associated with a canonical relation
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. Hence it suffices to prove (
5.12
) with
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

In fact, we claim that it suffices to prove (
5.12
) for
s=d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon
. To prove this claim, set
r:=d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon
and note that, for a general
s\in\mathbb{R}
, (
5.12
) is equivalent to

(5.13)

{\langle}D_{z}\rangle^{s-r}T{\langle}D_{y}\rangle^{-(s-r)}:\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}),

where
D_{z}=-i\partial_{z}
with respect to the
z
variable in
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and
D_{y}=-i\partial_{y}
with respect to the
y
variable in
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, since
T
is compactly supported, we can multiply
T
by smooth cutoffs
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
and apply Lemma
A.5
twice, to obtain

{\langle}D_{z}\rangle^{s-r}T{\langle}D_{y}\rangle^{-(s-r)}={\langle}D_{z}\rangle^{s-r}\rho_{1}T\rho_{2}{\langle}D_{y}\rangle^{-(s-r)}=S_{1}TS_{2}+R

for compactly supported pseudodifferential operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of orders
s-r
and
-(s-r)
, respectively, and a smoothing operator
R
. As in (
5.9
),
R
has the required mapping property. Moreover, the composition theorem for FIOs implies that
S_{1}TS_{2}
is a compactly supported FIO of order
m
, associated with the same canonical relation as
T
. This proves the claim, and we may thus suppose that
s=d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon
from here on.

Next, we reduce to working with operators in standard form. As in Lemma
A.2
, write
T=\sum_{j=1}^{l}S_{j,1}T_{j}S_{j,2}+R
for some
l\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, FIOs
(T_{j})_{j=1}^{l}\subseteq I^{m-1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1};{\mathcal{C}}_{j})
in standard form associated with canonical relations
({\mathcal{C}}_{j})_{j=1}^{l}
, changes of coordinates
(S_{j,1})_{j=1}^{l}
and
(S_{j,2})_{j=1}^{l}
, and a harmless smoothing operator
R
. Each change of coordinates
S_{j,1}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
, and each
S_{j,2}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{m+d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by Corollary
2.5
. Hence it suffices to prove the required statement for
T
in standard form, as in (
5.2
). Moreover, by the second part of Lemma
A.2
, we may suppose that the phase function
\Phi
of
T
satisfies the conditions in Section
5.1
. Finally, for simplicity of notation, we may assume without loss of generality that
m=0
, so that
a\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We are now almost in the setting of Corollary
5.4
, but we still have to reduce to the case where the symbol
a\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
T
is of the form
a(z,\eta)=a_{1}(z)a_{2}(\eta)
for
a_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and
a_{2}\in S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This reduction to symbols of product type is contained in e.g.
[
46
, Section VI.2]
and
[
4
, pp. 424-425]
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
be such that
\chi(z)a(z,\eta)=a(z,\eta)
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Write

Tf(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}e^{iz\cdot\zeta}{\langle}\zeta\rangle^{-(n+2)}T_{\zeta}f(z)\mathrm{d}\zeta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, where

T_{\zeta}f(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a_{\zeta}(z,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

and
a_{\zeta}(z,\eta):=\chi(z){\langle}\zeta\rangle^{n+2}\mathcal{F}a(\cdot,\eta)(\zeta)
, for
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
\mathcal{F}a(\cdot,\eta)
denotes the Fourier transform of
a(z,\eta)
with respect to the
z
variable.

It then suffices to show that
\|T_{\zeta}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for an implicit constant independent of
\zeta
and
f
. To this end it is relevant to note that, as follows from integration by parts,
(a_{\zeta})_{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\subseteq S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a collection of symbols of product type, with uniform bounds on each of the
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
seminorms and with spatial support contained in
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)
. Hence, to conclude the proof, one can simply apply Corollary
5.4
and Remark
5.5
to each
T_{\zeta}
.
∎

6.
Regularity of wave equations

In this section we apply our theorems for Fourier integral operators from Sections
4.4
and
5.2
to obtain new regularity results for linear wave equations, and new well-posedness results for nonlinear wave equations. In particular, we will prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Throughout, let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, with (non-positive) Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
.

6.1.
Linear wave equations

In this subsection we consider the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) on
M\times\mathbb{R}
:

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})u(x,t)=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x).\end{cases}

Recall that
\Delta_{g}
acts on functions in local coordinates as in (
4.2
). Then
\Delta_{g}
also acts on half densities, using the identification
u\mapsto\rho_{g}^{-1/2}u
between half densities and functions on
M
, where
\rho_{g}
is the Riemannian density on
M
. Moreover,
\Delta_{g}
is a non-positive operator on
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the operators
\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})
and
\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})/\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
, initially defined through the spectral calculus for
\Delta_{g}
on
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, extend to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, and the function
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
,

(6.1)

u(t):=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})u_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}u_{1}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, solves (
1.1
) for all
u_{0},u_{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
.

Here we will be interested in the regularity of solutions for initial data in the Hardy spaces for FIOs. To this end, we recall that (see e.g.
[
45
, Sections 4.1 and 8.1]
)
\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})
and
\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})/\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
are compactly supported Fourier integral operators of order
0
and
-1
, respectively, associated with a local canonical graph, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, as space-time solution operators, they are elements of
I^{-1/4}(M,M\times\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{C}})
and
I^{-5/4}(M,M\times\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{C}})
for
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition. Hence Theorems
4.16
and
5.7
, combined with the uniform boundedness principle, immediately yield regularity results for (
1.1
). However, in this case we have a distinguished time variable, and it is natural to consider a version of these theorems which takes this into account.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
t_{0}>0
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that, for all
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
and
u_{1}\in\mathcal{H}^{s-1,p}_{FIO}(M)
, the function
u
in (
6.1
) satisfies

(6.2)

\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}\leq C\big(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1,p}_{FIO}(M)}\big)

for all
t\in[-t_{0},t_{0}]
. Moreover, if
n\geq 2
and
p\in(2,\infty)
, then for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
C_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that

(6.3)

\Big(\int_{-t_{0}}^{t_{0}}\|u(t)\|_{W^{s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon,p}(M)}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1,p}_{FIO}(M)}\big).

Proof.

For (
6.2
), Theorem
4.16
immediately yields norm bounds for each fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
, but we also need the bounds to be locally uniform in
t
. Moreover, Theorem
5.7
yields (
6.3
) for
s-d(p)+s(p)-\varepsilon\geq 0
, but for
s<d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon
we want to take advantage of the specific structure of the underlying canonical relation, which yields integrability in
t
and (
6.3
). To do so, we will slightly modify the proof of Theorem
5.7
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\phi(t)=1
for all
t\in[-t_{0},t_{0}]
. Then it suffices to prove (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) with
u
replaced by
\phi u
. Now,
\phi u=T_{0}+T_{1}
for compactly supported
T_{0}\in I^{-1/4}(M,M\times\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{C}})
and
T_{1}\in I^{-5/4}(M,M\times\mathbb{R},{\mathcal{C}})
, where
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition.

Next, as in the proofs of Theorems
4.16
and
5.7
, one can reduce to working in local coordinates, and (due to the product structure of
M\times\mathbb{R}
) one can choose geodesic normal coordinates independently for
M
and
\mathbb{R}
. This expresses each
T_{k}
as a finite sum of compactly supported elements of
I^{-k-1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1},{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
, where

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}=\{(x,t,\xi,\tau,y,\eta)\mid(x,\xi)=\chi_{t}(y,\eta),\tau=p(x,\xi)\}

for
\chi_{t}
a canonical transformation on
T^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus o
for each
t
(see e.g.
[
45
, pp. 129-130]
). It then suffices to prove mapping properties for these FIOs in local coordinates.

At this point one can reduce to the case where
s=d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon
, using a similar reduction as in the proof of Theorem
5.7
. However, here one has to replace the derivative
D_{z}=-i\partial_{z}
with respect to
z=(x,t)
in (
5.13
) by the derivative
D_{x}=-i\partial_{x}
with respect to
x
. The resulting compactly supported FIOs are still associated with
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
, since
{\langle}D_{x}\rangle^{s-r}
and
{\langle}D_{y}\rangle^{-(s-r)}
are pseudodifferential operators.

Finally, an application of Lemma
A.2
and Remark
A.3
expresses each of the resulting FIOs as a sum of operators in standard form. One can then apply Corollary
2.4
at each time
t
to obtain (
6.2
), with the desired uniformity in
t
coming from Remark
2.6
. Moreover, Theorem
5.7
with
s=d(p)-s(p)+\varepsilon
yields (
6.3
).
∎

Remark 6.2
.

One can reformulate (
1.1
) as an abstract Cauchy problem on
X:=\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)\times\mathcal{H}^{s-1,p}_{FIO}(M)
. That is, one wants to find a
U:\mathbb{R}\to X
such that

(6.4)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}U(t)=\left(\!\begin{array}[]{cc}\!0&I\\
\!\Delta_{g}&0\end{array}\!\right)U(t)\text{ and }U(0)=\left(\!\begin{array}[]{c}\!u_{0}\\
\!u_{1}\end{array}\!\right).

Now, the function
u
in (
6.1
) is strongly continuous on
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, as follows from its strong continuity on
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
combined with Sobolev embeddings. Then Theorem
6.1
, combined with the density of
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
from Proposition
4.9
, implies that (
6.4
) is well posed for
p<\infty
. In particular,
u
is the unique strongly continuous solution to (
1.1
) as in Theorem
6.1
. For
p=\infty
,
u
is weak-star continuous, cf. Proposition
4.13
.

6.2.
Nonlinear wave equations

We consider the cubic nonlinear wave equation from (
1.4
) on
M\times\mathbb{R}
:

(6.5)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{g})u(x,t)=\pm|u(x,t)|^{2}u(x,t),\\
u(x,0)=f_{1}(x),\ \partial_{t}u(x,0)=f_{2}(x).\end{cases}

We will assume throughout that
M
has dimension
n=2
, although our techniques extend to higher dimensions and to other nonlinearities (see e.g. Proposition
6.5
below).

Our notion of well-posedness of (
6.5
) is taken from
[
3
]
. Consider the abstract evolution equation

(6.6)

u=L(f_{1},f_{2})+N(u,u,u),

where
L:X\to S
is a densely defined linear operator, and
N:S\times S\times S\to S
is a densely defined operator which is either linear or antilinear in each of its variables. We say that (
6.6
) is
quantitatively well posed
(with initial data space
X
and solution space
S
) if there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\|L(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{X},

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{S}

\displaystyle\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|u_{j}\|_{S},

for all
(f_{1},f_{2})\in X
and
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in S
. If (
6.6
) is quantitatively well posed, then a fixed-point argument yields
C_{0},\delta_{0}>0
such that, for all

(f_{1},f_{2})\in B_{X}(0,\delta_{0}):=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in X\mid\|(g_{1},g_{2})\|_{X}<\delta_{0}\},

there exists a unique solution
u[f_{1},f_{2}]\in B_{S}(0,C_{0}\delta_{0})
to (
6.6
). Moreover, the map
(f_{1},f_{2})\mapsto u[f_{1},f_{2}]
is Lipschitz continuous from
B_{X}(0,\delta_{0})
to
B_{S}(0,C_{0}\delta_{0})
, and one can expand
u[f_{1},f_{2}]
in terms of its Picard iterates. For more on this see
[
3
, Theorem 3]
.

In our setting,
S=S_{t_{0}}
is a space-time function space on the time interval
[0,t_{0}]
, for
t_{0}>0
. Then (
6.6
) arises by rewriting (
6.5
) using Duhamel’s formula, with

\begin{split}L(f_{1},f_{2})(t)&:=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})f_{1}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f_{2},\\
N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})(t)&:=\pm\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}u_{1}(s)\overline{u_{2}(s)}u_{3}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
. In fact, we will improve (
6.8
) by showing that

(6.9)

\|N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{S}\leq Ct_{0}^{\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|u_{i}\|_{S}

for all
t_{0}\in(0,1]
and
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in S=S_{t_{0}}
, where
C
and
\gamma>0
are independent of
t_{0}\in(0,1]
. Then the fixed-point argument also yields well-posedness for large data: for all
(f_{1},f_{2})\in X
there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that the power series for
u[f_{1},f_{2}]
converges in
S_{t_{0}}
.

The main result of this section deals with two critical values of
p
, namely
p=6=2(n+1)/(n-1)
and
p=4=2n/(n-1)
. It implies in particular Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\varepsilon,t_{0}>0
. Then (
6.5
) is quantitatively well posed with initial data space

X:=(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M))\times(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon-1,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{-1/2,2}(M))

and solution space

S_{t_{0}}:=L^{4}\big([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M)\big)\cap C\big([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M)\big).

Moreover, (
6.5
) is quantitatively well posed with initial data space

(6.10)

Y:=(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,4}_{FIO}(M)+W^{3/8,2}(M))\times(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon-1,4}_{FIO}(M)+W^{-5/8,2}(M))

and solution space

T_{t_{0}}:=L^{24/7}\big([0,t_{0}];L^{4}(M)\big)\cap C\big([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,4}_{FIO}(M)+W^{3/8,2}(M)\big).

Proof.

The proof follows the same template as that of
[
38
, Theorem 1.2]
. We will only consider
p=6
, with the other case being analogous. The spaces
W^{s,2}(M)
arise due to the use of Strichartz estimates, to deal with the nonlinear term.

We have to prove (
6.7
) and (
6.8
). In the process we will also prove (
6.9
) for
t_{0}\in(0,1]
. In fact, we will obtain estimates for the solution spaces
L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))
and
C([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M))
separately.

Firstly, for a given
(f_{1},f_{2})\in X
, write
(f_{1},f_{2})=(f_{11},f_{21})+(f_{12},f_{22})
with

(f_{11},f_{21})\in\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)\times\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon-1,6}_{FIO}(M)\text{ and }(f_{12},f_{22})\in W^{1/2,2}(M)\times W^{-1/2,2}(M).

Then Hölder’s inequality, the second part of Theorem
6.1
, and Strichartz estimates on compact manifolds (cf.
[
26
,
27
]
) yield

\displaystyle\|L(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}\leq t_{0}^{1/12}\|L(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{6}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|L(f_{11},f_{21})\|_{L^{6}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}+\|L(f_{12},f_{22})\|_{L^{6}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(f_{11},f_{21})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)\times\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon-1,6}_{FIO}(M)}+\|(f_{12},f_{22})\|_{W^{1/2,2}(M)\times W^{-1/2,2}(M)}.

Taking the infimum over all such decompositions, one obtains

\|L(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}\lesssim\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{X}.

On the other hand, since
W^{1/2,2}(M)=\mathcal{H}^{1/2,2}_{FIO}(M)
(see e.g. Theorem
4.15
and Corollary
4.21
), the first part of Theorem
6.1
, with
p=6
and
p=2
, yields

\|L(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M))}\lesssim\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{X}.

Moreover, applying Remark
6.2
with
p=6
and
p=2
, we obtain

L(f_{1},f_{2})\in C\big([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M)\big),

which completes the proof of (
6.7
).

Next, we prove (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) for
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in S
. Firstly, Hölder’s inequality and Strichartz estimates yield

(6.11)

\displaystyle\|N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}\leq t_{0}^{1/12}\|N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{6}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{0}^{1/12}\|u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\|_{L^{1}([0,t_{0}];L^{2}(M))}\leq t_{0}^{1/3}\|u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\|_{L^{4/3}([0,t_{0}];L^{2}(M))}

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}^{1/3}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))},

where the implicit constant is independent of
t_{0}
if
t_{0}\in(0,1]
. On the other hand, Minkowski’s inequality, the spectral theorem and Hölder’s inequality yield

\displaystyle\|N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t_{0}];\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon,6}_{FIO}(M)+W^{1/2,2}(M))}\leq\|N(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t_{0}];W^{1/2,2}(M))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}\Big\|\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}u_{1}(s)\overline{u_{2}(s)}u_{3}(s)\Big\|_{W^{1/2,2}(M)}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{1}\overline{u_{2}}u_{3}\|_{L^{1}([0,t_{0}];L^{2}(M))}\leq t_{0}^{1/4}\|u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}\|_{L^{4/3}([0,t_{0}];L^{2}(M))}

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}^{1/4}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\|u_{j}\|_{L^{4}([0,t_{0}];L^{6}(M))}

for an implicit constant independent of
t_{0}
. Using also the strong continuity of
t\mapsto\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}})
, cf. Remark
6.2
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

One can combine the same proof strategy with local smoothing estimates that use classical Sobolev spaces as spaces of initial data. For
p=6
, this would yield a weaker statement than that in Theorem

, given that Theorem

In (

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta})}{\sqrt{-\Delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{5}u_{j}(s)ds\Big\|_{L^{6}([0,T],L^{6}(M))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T];W^{1/2,2}(M))}

Remark 6.6
.

Note that we did not show that (

7.
Sharpness of the results

In this section we show that Theorems
1.1
and

are sharp, and Theorems
1.3
and
5.7
are sharp under mild assumptions. To this end, we first prove that the index
d(p)-s(p)
in Corollary
5.4
cannot be improved, from which we then derive the same conclusion in the setting of Theorem
5.7
.

Throughout, fix a dimension
n\geq 2
.

7.1.
Sharpness for operators in standard form

Throughout this subsection, fix
m\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a real-valued
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}))
, positively homogeneous of degree one in the fiber variable, such that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{z\eta}^{2}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0})=n
for all
(z_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
with
\eta_{0}\neq 0
. Set

Tf(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Using terminology from Section
A.3
, we say that
T
is a Fourier integral operator in standard form.

To prove sharpness, we will proceed in a similar way as in
[
37
, Section 5]
, which dealt with the case where
Tf(x,t)=e^{it\phi(D)}f(x)
for
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
positively homogeneous of degree one. A key step in that proof was to approximate
T
by translations of the form
x\mapsto x+t\partial_{\eta}\phi(\nu)
, which arise through propagation of singularities from the canonical relation

\{(x,t,\eta,\phi(\eta),x+t\partial_{\eta}\phi(\eta),\eta)\}.

In the present variable-coefficient case, the relevant canonical relation is

\{(z,\partial_{z}\Phi(z,\eta),\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\eta),\eta)\},

and we will adjust the flow. However, we do not need to take the momentum variable into account. This leads to the following version of
[
37
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose that
m=0
and that there exists a non-empty conic
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
a(0,\eta)=1
and
\Phi(0,\eta)=0

for all
\eta\in V
with
|\eta|\geq 1/2
. Suppose also that
|(1-r)\nu+r\omega|\geq 1/2
for all
\nu,\omega\in V\cap S^{n-1}
and
r\in[0,1]
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

|Th(z)-(2\pi)^{n}h(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\leq C\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|z|(1+\gamma(h,\nu))

for all
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\subseteq V\cap\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\eta|\geq 1/2\}
compact, all
\nu\in S^{n-1}\cap V
and all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
|z|\leq 1
. Here

\gamma(h,\nu):=\sup\{|\hat{\eta}-\nu||\eta|\mid\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\}.

Proof.

Fix
\nu\in S^{n-1}\cap V
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and write

\displaystyle Th(z)-(2\pi)^{n}h(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)-e^{i\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta}\big)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}(a(z,\eta)-1)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}-e^{i\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta}\big)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta.

We will bound each of the terms on the second line separately. The first term is easy:

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}(a(z,\eta)-1)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\Big|\lesssim\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|z|,

where we used Taylor approximation and the assumptions that
m=0
and that
a(0,\eta)=1
for
\eta\in V
.

To deal with the second term, we first use Taylor approximation for the exponential:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}-e^{i\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta}\big)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|e^{i(\Phi(z,\eta)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta)}-1\big|\,|\widehat{h}(\eta)|\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\delta(h,\nu,z),

where

\delta(h,\nu,z):=\sup\{|(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\eta)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))\cdot\eta|\mid\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\}.

It thus suffices to show that
\delta(h,\nu,z)\lesssim|z|\gamma(h,\nu)
. To do so, for
\eta\in V
, we can use the homogeneity of
\Phi
and another Taylor expansion to write

\displaystyle(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\eta)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\Phi(z,(1-r)\nu+r\hat{\eta})(\hat{\eta}-\nu)\mathrm{d}r\Big)\cdot\eta.

Finally, note that
\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\Phi(0,(1-r)\nu+r\hat{\eta})=0
for all
r\in[0,1]
, since
\Phi(0,\eta^{\prime})=0
for
\eta^{\prime}\in V
with
|\eta^{\prime}|\geq 1/2
, and because
|(1-r)\nu+r\hat{\eta}|\geq 1/2
, by assumption. Hence, for each
r
we can use another Taylor approximation, this time with respect to
z
, to obtain

|\Phi(z,\eta)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta|\lesssim|z|\,|\hat{\eta}-\nu|\,|\eta|,

as required.
∎

To prove sharpness for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
, we will use a version of
[
37
, Lemma 5.2]
which relates the Hardy spaces for FIOs to square functions as in
[
15
,
14
]
. The proof in
[
37
]
, which relies on Khintchine’s inequality, extends directly to this setting. We use notation as in the decoupling norm in (
5.3
).

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
T:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is bounded. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}|T\chi_{\nu}(D)f(z)|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

We are now ready to prove that the index
d(p)-s(p)
in Corollary
5.4
is sharp. Note, however, that our assumptions on
T
are much weaker than those in Section
5.1
. In particular, we do not assume that the canonical relation
{\mathcal{C}}
associated with
T
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition, nor even that the projection condition holds which guarantees sharpness of the fixed-time
L^{p}
estimates (see
[
40
]
).

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
T:\mathcal{H}^{s+m}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is bounded. Suppose that there exist a
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a non-empty open conic set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
c,C>0
such that
|a(z_{0},\eta)|\geq c|\eta|^{m}
for all
\eta\in V
with
|\eta|\geq C
. Then
s\geq d(p)-s(p)
.

Proof.

The approach is similar to that in
[
37
, Theorem 5.3]
, as are the examples that show sharpness. However, we need additional work to reduce to a setting where we can apply Lemma
7.1
.

By translating and precomposing with
{\langle}D\rangle^{-m}
, we may assume that
z_{0}=0
and
m=0
, and by adding a smoothing operator which modifies the low frequencies near
z_{0}
, we may assume that
C=1/2
. We may also suppose, without loss of generality, that
|(1-r)\nu+r\omega|\geq 1/2
for all
\nu,\omega\in V\cap S^{n-1}
and
r\in[0,1]
. Finally, we will assume for the moment that
a(0,\eta)=1
and
\Phi(0,\eta)=0
for all
\eta\in V
with
|\eta|\geq 1/2
. This assumption will be removed at the end of the proof.

Now, we want to show that
s\geq d(p)-s(p)=\max(s(p)-1/p,0)
. Fix a standard Littlewood–Paley decomposition
(\phi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as in the proof of Corollary
5.4
, and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Our construction will only rely on estimates for large
k
. Given that
V
is non-empty and open, we may thus choose
k
large enough such that the collection
V_{k}
of
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
with
\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\nu})\subseteq V
is non-empty. Note that then
V_{k}
has approximately
2^{k(n-1)/2}
elements, because
V
has a fixed nonzero aperture.

We first prove that
s\geq s(p)-1/p
.
In this case we will apply Lemma
7.1
to functions whose Fourier support fills up a dyadic-parabolic piece.

We will construct a collection
(f_{\nu})_{\nu\in V_{k}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the following properties. For each
\nu\in V_{k}
one has
\phi_{k}(D)f_{\nu}=\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{\nu}=f_{\nu}
, and
\Real(f_{\nu}(x))\gtrsim 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x|\leq 2^{-k}
. Moreover,
\|\mathcal{F}f_{\nu}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 1
and
\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{-k\frac{n+1}{2p}}
. Here all the implicit constants are independent of
\nu
and
k
. To construct such a collection, one may fix a
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi(x)\geq 1
whenever
|x|\leq 1
, and
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|>c^{\prime}
, for some small
c^{\prime}>0
. Then set

f_{\nu}(x):=e^{i2^{k}\nu\cdot x}\psi(2^{k}(\nu\cdot x)\nu+2^{k/2}\Pi_{\nu}^{\perp}x),

where
\Pi^{\perp}_{\nu}
is the orthogonal projection onto the complement of the span of
\nu
.

Now set
f:=\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}f_{\nu}
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq V
,
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(7.1)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p})},

by
[
37
, Proposition 4.1]
(see also
[
37
, Equation (5.3)]
).

Now,
\Phi
and all of its derivatives are bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Hence
z\mapsto\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)
is a locally Lipschitz flow for each
\nu\in V_{k}
, with Lipschitz constants independent of
\nu
. Moreover, by assumption,
\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,\nu)=0
. It thus follows that
\Real(f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)))\gtrsim 1
whenever
|z|\lesssim 2^{-k}
for some implicit constant independent of
k
and
\nu
. On the other hand, because
\mathrm{supp}(f_{\nu})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{k}\chi_{\nu})
, one has
|\hat{\eta}-\nu|\,|\eta|\lesssim 2^{k/2}
for all
\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(f_{\nu})
. Hence Lemma
7.1
yields

|Tf_{\nu}(z)-(2\pi)^{n}f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\lesssim|z|2^{k/2}

whenever
|z|\lesssim 2^{-k}
. By combining all this, we find

\Real(Tf_{\nu}(z))\geq(2\pi)^{n}\Real(f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)))-|Tf_{\nu}(z)-(2\pi)^{n}f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\gtrsim 1

if
|z|\leq c_{1}2^{-k}
for some small
c_{1}>0
independent of
k
and
\nu
. Now we can use the assumption on
T
and (
7.1
):

\displaystyle 2^{-k\frac{n+1}{p}}2^{k\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\Big|\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}Tf_{\nu}(z)\Big|^{p}\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{1/p}=\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p})}.

This implies that
s\geq s(p)-1/p
.

Next, we will show that
s\geq 0
, which is the required statement for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
. Here we will apply Lemma
7.1
to functions with frequency support of unit size in a dyadic-parabolic piece, and we will also use Lemma
7.2
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be as before, and set
g_{\nu}(x):=e^{i2^{k}\nu\cdot x}\psi(x)
for
\nu\in V_{k}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|>c^{\prime}
, for some small
c^{\prime}>0
, we may again suppose that
\phi_{k}(D)g_{\nu}=\chi_{\nu}(D)g_{\nu}=g_{\nu}
. Also,
\|g_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 1\eqsim\|\mathcal{F}(g_{\nu})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. Set
g:=\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}g_{\nu}
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{g})\subseteq V
,
g\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(7.2)

\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}\|g_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{4})},

by
[
37
, Proposition 4.1]
(see also
[
37
, Equation (5.5)]
).

Let
\nu\in V_{k}
. Since
\psi(x)\geq 1
for
|x|\leq 1
, we find in a similar way as before that
|g_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\gtrsim 1
whenever
|z|\lesssim 1
, for small implicit constants independent of
k
and
\nu
. Moreover, since
\mathrm{supp}(g_{\nu})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{k}\chi_{\nu})
is of unit size, Lemma
7.1
yields

|Tg_{\nu}(z)-(2\pi)^{n}g_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\lesssim|z|

if
|z|\lesssim 1
. Combined, this implies that

|Tg_{\nu}(z)|\geq(2\pi)^{n}|g_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|-|Tg_{\nu}(z)-(2\pi)^{n}g_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\gtrsim 1

whenever
|z|\leq c_{2}
for some small
c_{2}>0
independent of
k
and
\nu
. Now we can use the assumption on
T
, Lemma
7.2
and (
7.2
):

\displaystyle 2^{k\frac{n-1}{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}|Tg_{\nu}(z)|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}|T\chi_{\nu}(D)g(z)|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\mathrm{d}z\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{4})},

which shows that
s\geq 0
.

We have now proved that
s\geq d(p)-s(p)
, and it only remains to remove the assumption that
a(0,\eta)=1
and
\Phi(0,\eta)=0
for all
\eta\in V
with
|\eta|\geq 1/2
. To do so, and in particular for the second part of this assumption, we will use the invariance of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under FIOs.

First note that, so far, we have not used any information about
V
and
c
, beyond what is listed in the statement of the theorem, as well as the assumption that
|(1-r)\nu+r\omega|\geq 1/2
for all
\nu,\omega\in V\cap S^{n-1}
and
r\in[0,1]
, which can be made without loss of generality by choosing
V
small enough. Hence, by choosing slightly smaller
V
and
c
, we may suppose that the closure of
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is a strict subset of a closed cone
V_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|a(z_{0},\eta)|\geq c
for all
\eta\in V_{0}
with
|\eta|\geq c^{\prime}
, for some
c^{\prime}<1/2
.

Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq V_{0}\cap\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\eta|\geq c^{\prime}\}
, and such that
\rho(\eta)=1
for all
\eta\in V\cap\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\eta|\geq 1/2\}
. Set

\theta(\eta):=e^{-i\Phi(0,\eta)}\frac{\rho(\eta)}{a(0,\eta)}

for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\theta(D)
is an FIO in standard form, with phase function
x\cdot\eta-\Phi(0,\eta)
and symbol
\rho(\eta)/a(0,\eta)
. In fact,
\theta(D)\in I^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
for

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}:=\{(x,\eta,x-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,\eta),\eta)\mid(x,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\},

which is the graph of
(y,\eta)\mapsto(y+\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,\eta),\eta)
on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus o
. It thus follows from
[
17
, Theorem 6.10]
(see also
[
36
, Remark 3.7]
) that
\theta(D):\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded.

By assumption on
T
, this in turn implies that
T\theta(D):\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is bounded. Moreover,
T\theta(D)
is an FIO in standard form, with phase function given by
\Phi(z,\eta)-\Phi(0,\eta)
for
\eta\in V\cap\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|\eta|\geq 1/2\}
, and symbol given by
a(z,\eta)/a(0,\eta)
for such
\eta
. Now the first part of the proof shows that
s\geq d(p)-s(p)
, thereby concluding the proof for general
T
as in the statement of the theorem.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

Note that the sharpness examples are universal, in the sense that
they can be used for any FIO satisfying the conditions of Theorem
7.3
. Also note that, when combined with Proposition
C.3
, Theorem
7.3
shows that the exponent
d(p)
in Theorem
5.1
is sharp whenever
T
is non-characteristic somewhere.

7.2.
Sharpness on manifolds

Throughout, let
(M,g)
and
(N,g^{\prime})
be complete Riemannian manifolds of dimensions
n
and
n+1
, respectively, with bounded geometry.

We will prove that the index
d(p)-s(p)
in Theorem
5.7
is sharp. However, the assumptions under which we show sharpness are, for the most part, much weaker than those in Theorem
5.7
, and in particular we do not assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
T\in I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
, for
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq(T^{*}N\setminus o)\times(T^{*}M\setminus o)
a homogeneous canonical relation such that the projections
\Pi_{N}:{\mathcal{C}}\to N
and
\Pi_{M}:{\mathcal{C}}\to M
are submersions. Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that

T:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)\to W^{r,p}(N)

is bounded, and that
T
is non-characteristic at some point
c\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
r\leq s-d(p)+s(p)
.

Note that, if
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition, then it also satisfies the projection conditions in the theorem, given that the natural projection
\Pi_{M}:T^{*}M\setminus o\to M
is a submersion. Moreover, the space-time solution operator to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies the conditions of the theorem, given that the wave propagators are invertible and therefore elliptic. Hence the index
d(p)-s(p)
in Theorems
1.1
and
6.1
is sharp on every compact manifold.

Proof.

Firstly, we may work in a sufficiently small conic neighborhood of
c
, to be chosen later. More precisely, let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be compactly supported pseudodifferential operators of order zero on
N
and
M
, whose kernels are smooth away from small conic neighborhoods of
(z_{0},\zeta_{0},z_{0},\zeta_{0})
and
(y_{0},\eta_{0},y_{0},\eta_{0})
, and whose principal symbols are equal to one near
(z_{0},\zeta_{0})
and
(y_{0},\eta_{0})
, respectively. Then, by the composition theorem for FIOs and because the canonical relation of a pseudodifferential operator is the diagonal,
S_{1}TS_{2}\in I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})
is non-characteristic at
c
, and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
is a small neighborhood of
c
in
{\mathcal{C}}
. Moreover,
S_{2}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)
, by Theorem
4.16
, and
S_{1}:W^{r,p}(N)\to W^{r,p}(N)
is bounded as well, by Corollary
4.21
and because pseudodifferential operators of order zero are bounded on
W^{r,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
. Hence
S_{1}TS_{2}:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(M)\to W^{r,p}(N)
is bounded, and we may replace
T
by
S_{1}TS_{2}
in the remainder. For simplicity of notation, we will continue working with
T
and simply assume that
T\in I^{m-1/4}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
for
{\mathcal{C}}
a small neighborhood of
c
.

Next, we reduce to working in local coordinates. Let uniformly locally finite families
K\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
,
L\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}
,
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
and
(\theta_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(N)
as in Lemma
A.1
be given, where
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
are the smooth structures on
M
and
N
, respectively. Write
c=(z_{0},\zeta_{0},y_{0},\eta_{0})
, and fix
\kappa\in K
and
\lambda\in L
such that
\psi_{\kappa}(y_{0})\neq 0
and
\theta_{\lambda}(z_{0})\neq 0
.
Set
\tilde{T}f:=\lambda_{*}(\theta_{\lambda}T\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}f))
for
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, by the composition theorem for FIOs,
\tilde{T}\in I^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1};\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}})
for

\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}:=\{(\lambda(z),(\partial\lambda(z))_{*}\zeta,\kappa(y),(\partial\kappa(y))_{*}\eta)\mid(z,\zeta,y,\eta)\in{\mathcal{C}},z\in U_{\lambda},y\in U_{\kappa}\}.

Note that the natural projections
\Pi_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}:\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
\Pi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are submersions, by the assumption on
{\mathcal{C}}
. Moreover, the principal symbol of
\tilde{T}
is just the principal symbol of
T
, expressed in local coordinates near
z_{0}
and
y_{0}
. In particular,
\tilde{T}
is non-characteristic at

\tilde{c}:=(\lambda(z_{0}),(\partial\lambda(z_{0}))_{*}\zeta_{0},\kappa(y_{0}),(\partial\kappa(y_{0}))_{*}\eta_{0})\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}},

given that
\theta_{\lambda}(z_{0})\neq 0\neq\psi_{\kappa}(y_{0})
. Also, by Definition

and Corollary

, and

is bounded from
\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

by Theorem

. Hence
\tilde{T}:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to W^{r,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is bounded. We may thus work with
\tilde{T}
in the remainder. Note also that
\tilde{T}
is compactly supported.

Next, we want to reduce to the case where

. To this end, note that
{\langle}D\rangle^{r}\tilde{T}{\langle}D\rangle^{-r}:\mathcal{H}^{s-r+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
is bounded. Since
\tilde{T}
is compactly supported, we can multiply
\tilde{T}
by smooth cutoffs

and
\rho_{2}

twice, to write

for compactly supported pseudodifferential operators
\tilde{S}_{1}
and
\tilde{S}_{2}
of order

and
-r

is bounded, and the composition theorem for FIOs implies that
\tilde{S}_{1}\tilde{T}\tilde{S}_{2}\in I^{m-1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1};\tilde{C})

and
{\langle}D\rangle^{-r}

\tilde{c}

It only remains to express

to write

as a sum of FIOs of order

, composed with changes of coordinates, plus a smoothing term. Moreover, at the start of the proof we observed that we are allowed to work in a sufficiently small neighborhood of

in
{\mathcal{C}}
(and therefore also of
\tilde{c}
in
\tilde{C}
). Hence Remark
A.4
in fact yields a decomposition

an FIO of order
m-1/4
as in Section
7.1
,
S^{\prime}
a change of coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
R^{\prime}:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})

is non-characteristic at
\tilde{c}
and bounded from
\mathcal{H}^{s-r+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to

, given that

does not affect these properties. And, since
\tilde{S}_{1}\tilde{T}\tilde{S}_{2}
is compactly supported and we are allowed to work in a sufficiently small neighborhood of

does not affect the mapping properties of

To conclude, the operator

is as in Section

Appendix A
Geometry and Fourier integral operators

In this appendix we collect some basics on manifolds with bounded geometry and on the theory of Fourier integral operators on manifolds. We first discuss the main geometric assumptions on the underlying manifolds. We then recall some basics on distributional densities on manifolds, which will allow us to introduce Fourier integral operators on manifolds.

A.1.
Manifolds with bounded geometry

In this subsection we recall some basics from Riemannian geometry. The underlying geometric assumptions correspond to those used in
[
50
,
51
,
48
]
to define the classical local Hardy spaces on manifolds.

Throughout this subsection, we let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{A}}
the smooth structure on
M
. That is,
{\mathcal{A}}
is a maximal smooth atlas of homeomorphisms
\kappa:U_{\kappa}\to\tilde{U}_{\kappa}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the
U_{\kappa}\subseteq M
are open sets that cover
M
, and

(A.1)

\mu_{\lambda\kappa}:=\lambda\circ\kappa^{-1}:\kappa(U_{\kappa}\cap U_{\lambda})\to\lambda(U_{\kappa}\cap U_{\lambda})

is a diffeomorphism for all
\kappa,\lambda\in{\mathcal{A}}
such that
U_{\kappa}\cap U_{\lambda}\neq\emptyset
.

We assume throughout that
(M,g)
has
bounded geometry
, by which we mean that the injectivity radius of
(M,g)
is positive, and that all covariant derivatives
\nabla^{k}R
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, of the Riemannian curvature tensor
R
are bounded. Recall that the injectivity radius
\mathrm{inj}(M)\in[0,\infty]
is the infimumum over
p\in M
of the supremum over all
r>0
such that the exponential map

\exp_{p}:B_{r}(0)\to U_{r}(p):=\exp_{p}(B_{r}(0))\subseteq M

at
p
is a diffeomorphism. Here
B_{r}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid|x-y|<r\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where we identify
T_{p}M
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\kappa=\exp^{-1}_{p}:U_{r}(p)\to B_{r}(0)
is an element of
{\mathcal{A}}
, and the corresponding local coordinates are
geodesic normal coordinates
. We will often alternate between denoting the domain of
\kappa
by
U_{\kappa}
and by
U_{r}(p)
.

One can reformulate the assumption that all covariant derivatives of the curvature tensor are bounded using the matrix expression
(g_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
of the metric tensor in local coordinate charts (see
[
11
]
). That is, we assume that for all
0<r<\mathrm{inj}(M)
there exists a
c>0
and, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, a
C_{\alpha}\geq 0
such that

(A.2)

|\det(g_{ij}(y))_{i,j=1}^{n}|\geq c\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial^{\alpha}_{y}g_{ij}(y)|\leq C_{\alpha}\text{ for }1\leq i,j\leq n,

in every normal geodesic coordinate chart
\exp^{-1}_{p}:U_{r}(p)\to B_{r}(0)
,
p\in M
, and for all
y\in B_{r}(0)
. This implies similar uniform bounds for the inverse matrix
(g^{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
.

There is yet another equivalent formulation of the assumption of bounded geometry; this one, in terms of the changes of coordinates in (
A.1
), will be used frequently by us. Namely, we assume that, for all
0<r<\mathrm{inj}(M)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
, there exists a
C_{\alpha}\geq 0
such that

(A.3)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{y}\mu_{\lambda\kappa}(y)|\leq C_{\alpha}

for all normal geodesic charts
\kappa=\exp_{p}^{-1}:U_{r}(p)\to B_{r}(0)
and
\lambda=\exp_{q}^{-1}:U_{r}(q)\to B_{r}(0)
with
U_{r}(p)\cap U_{r}(q)\neq\emptyset
,
p,q\in M
, and all
y\in\kappa(U_{r}(p)\cap U_{r}(q))
.

To avoid confusion, we note that some authors use the terminology “bounded geometry” for the weaker assumption that
M
has positive injectivity radius and Ricci tensor bounded from below (see e.g.
[
31
,
30
]
).

For us, the main reason to make the assumption of bounded geometry is that it implies the existence of a
uniformly locally finite
cover by geodesic balls, in a sense which is specified by the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

For every
\delta\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M)/3]
, there exist countable collections
K\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}
and
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
with the following properties.

(1)

Each
\kappa=\exp_{p_{\kappa}}^{-1}:U_{\delta}(p_{\kappa})\to B_{\delta}(0)
, for
\kappa\in K
, is a geodesic normal coordinate chart for some
p_{\kappa}\in M
, and
\cup_{\kappa\in K}U_{\delta}(p_{\kappa})=M
.

(2)

There exists an
N\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
such that, for every
\kappa\in K
, the collection of
\lambda\in K
with
U_{\mathrm{inj}(M)/3}(p_{\kappa})\cap U_{\mathrm{inj}(M)/3}(p_{\lambda})\neq\emptyset
contains at most
N
elements.

(3)

For every
\kappa\in K
one has
\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\kappa})\subseteq U_{\delta}(p_{\kappa})
, and

\sum_{\kappa\in K}\psi_{\kappa}(x)^{2}=1\quad(x\in M).

(4)

Writing
\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}:=\psi_{\kappa}\circ\kappa^{-1}
for
\kappa\in K
, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
one has

\sup\{|\partial_{y}^{\alpha}\tilde{\psi}_{\kappa}(y)|\mid\kappa\in K,y\in B_{\delta}(0)\}<\infty.

Note that the family
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
is also uniformly locally finite, in the sense that, for each
x\in M
, one has
\psi_{\kappa}(x)\neq 0
for at most
N
different

. In fact, we typically choose
\delta\leq\mathrm{inj}(M)/9

3.4
.

The statement and the proof of Lemma
A.1
are essentially contained in
[
41
, Lemmas A1.1.2 and A1.1.3]
. See also
[
52
, Section 7.1]
) and
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
.

A.2.
Distributional densities

In this subsection we collect some basics on distributional densities on manifolds, cf.

Let

\Omega_{\gamma}(T_{x}M)

\sigma:\Lambda^{n}T_{x}M\to{\mathbb{C}}

\sigma(\lambda v)=|\lambda|^{\gamma}\sigma(v)

(A.4)

f\mapsto f\rho_{0}^{\gamma}\quad(f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M))

The topology on

\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{\gamma})
inherits the topology of

via (

\rho_{0}
, by definition of the inductive limit topology on

and because the quotient of two strictly positive densities is bounded from above and below on compact subsets of

A distributional density of order
\gamma

is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1-\gamma})

denotes the duality between

is embedded in

{\langle}u,\rho\rangle_{M}=\int_{M}u(x)\rho(x)

and
\rho\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1-\gamma})
. This is well defined because
u\rho\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1})
is an integrable density in the classical sense. Moreover,
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{\gamma})

in local coordinate charts by test functions, and then lifting these back to

As is typical for the coordinate-invariant theory of Fourier integral operators (see e.g.
[
10
,
23
]

identifies functionals on

with distributional densities of a general

1

In particular, after making this identification, our approach coincides with the notion of distributions on manifolds from

Let

(A^{*}\sigma)(v)=\sigma(Av_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge Av_{n})

\chi^{*}_{\gamma}\rho(x):=(\partial_{x}\chi(x))^{*}\big(\rho(\chi(x))\big)

Let

A.3.
Fourier integral operators

This subsection contains some background on the Fourier integral operators under consideration in this article. We first collect the relevant definitions, and then some basic results about these operators.

Definitions

We refer to
[
22
,
23
,
10
]
for the general theory of Fourier integral operators on manifolds and the associated symplectic geometry. In particular, as in
[
23
, Section 25.1]
, Fourier integral operators are defined using the class
I^{m}(X,\Lambda,\Omega_{1/2})\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X,\Omega_{1/2})
of Lagrangian distributions of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
, for
X
a smooth manifold and
\Lambda\subseteq T^{*}X\setminus o
a closed conic Lagrangian submanifold. Recall that
o
is the zero section. On the other hand, our main results rely on estimates in local coordinates, and these distributions have well-known concrete oscillatory integral representations in local coordinates (see e.g. Lemma
A.2
below).

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
n_{1},n_{2}\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, we will work with the standard Kohn–Nirenberg symbol class
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})
, consisting of those
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})
such that

(A.13)

\sup\{{\langle}\eta\rangle^{|\beta|-m}|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}a(z,\eta)|\mid z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\}<\infty

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n_{1}}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n_{2}}
. We write
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}})
for the subspace of symbols which do not depend on the
z
variable. As in
[
17
]
, one could consider symbols from more general symbol classes, but for simplicity we will not do so here.

Let
M
and
N
be smooth manifolds. The Schwartz kernel theorem (see
[
10
, Theorem 1.1.1]
) yields a bijection between continuous linear maps
T:\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
and kernels
K\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N\times M,\Omega_{1/2})
. A
homogeneous canonical relation
from
T^{*}M
to
T^{*}N
is a submanifold
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq(T^{*}N\setminus o)\times(T^{*}M\setminus o)
such that

{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}:=\{(x,y,\xi,-\eta)\mid(x,\xi,y,\eta)\in{\mathcal{C}}\}

is a closed conic Lagrangian submanifold of
T^{*}(N\times M)\setminus o
. Then a
Fourier integral operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
associated with
{\mathcal{C}}
is an operator
T:\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
such that the Schwartz kernel of
T
is an element of
I^{m}(N\times M,{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime},\Omega_{1/2})
. The class of all such Fourier integral operators will be denoted by
I^{m}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
.

An FIO
T\in I^{m}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
is
compactly supported
if its Schwartz kernel
K
is compactly supported in
N\times M
, and
properly supported
if both projections from
\mathrm{supp}(K)\subseteq N\times M
, to
N
and to
M
, are proper maps. We say that
T
is
non-characteristic
at a point

if the principal symbol
a\in S^{m}({\mathcal{C}},L_{{\mathcal{C}}})
of
T
is of the form

for some
a_{0}\in S^{m}({\mathcal{C}},L_{{\mathcal{C}}})
homogeneous of degree
m
and nowhere vanishing, and
|s_{0}|
bounded away from zero in a conic neighborhood of
c
. We refer to
[
10
,
23
]
for more on the principal symbol of an FIO, the Maslov line bundle
L_{{\mathcal{C}}}
and the symbol class
S^{m}({\mathcal{C}},L_{{\mathcal{C}}})
, but we note that these notions reduce to more concrete concepts when working in local coordinates (see Section
7
and (
C.1
)). One says that
T

elliptic
if it is non-characteristic at every point

.

The transpose
T^{t}:\mathcal{D}(N,\Omega_{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
of a
T
, given by
{\langle}T^{t}v,u\rangle_{M}={\langle}Tu,v\rangle_{N}
for

and

, is an element of
I^{m}(N,M;{\mathcal{C}}^{-1})
, where

(A.14)

{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}:=\{(y,\eta,x,\xi)\mid(x,\xi,y,\eta)\in{\mathcal{C}})\subseteq(T^{*}M\setminus o)\times(T^{*}N\setminus o)

is the inverse canonical relation.

We consider two types of FIOs, by making geometric assumptions on their canonical relations. Firstly, we deal with
T\in I^{m}(M,N;{\mathcal{C}})
for
{\mathcal{C}}
a canonical relation which is a
local canonical graph
. This means that the natural projections

\Pi_{T^{*}N}:{\mathcal{C}}\to T^{*}N\setminus o\ \ \text{and}\ \ \Pi_{T^{*}M}:C\to T^{*}M\setminus o

are local diffeomorphisms, or equivalently that
{\mathcal{C}}
is locally the graph of a symplectomorphism
\chi
between open subsets of
T^{*}M\setminus o
and
T^{*}N\setminus o
. This condition implies in particular that
M
and
N
have the same dimension, and
{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}
is then a local canonical graph as well. If
T
is properly supported, then
T:\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
continuously. Then
T^{t}
has the same properties with
M
and
N
reversed, so
T
extends uniquely to a continuous map
T:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
by setting
{\langle}Tu,v\rangle_{N}:={\langle}u,T^{t}v\rangle_{M}
for
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
v\in\mathcal{D}(N,\Omega_{1/2})
.

Secondly, we consider the case where
M
has dimension
n\geq 2
and
N
has dimension
n+1
. Then, cf.
[
34
]
, we say that
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the
cinematic curvature condition
if the natural projections

\Pi_{T^{*}M}:{\mathcal{C}}\to T^{*}M\setminus o\ \ \text{and}\ \ \Pi_{N}:{\mathcal{C}}\to N

are submersions, and if for each
z\in\Pi_{N}{\mathcal{C}}
the conic hypersurface
\Pi_{T^{*}_{z}N}{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq T^{*}_{z}N\setminus\{0\}
has everywhere
n-1
non-vanishing principal curvatures. Here
\Pi_{T_{z}^{*}N}:{\mathcal{C}}\to T^{*}_{z}N\setminus\{0\}
is the natural projection onto the fiber
T^{*}_{z}N
of
z
. This condition is invariant under changes of coordinates on
M
and
N
.

Results

We now collect some basic results about Fourier integral operators.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a homogeneous canonical relation from
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, for
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and

. Also let

be open, conic and such that
(z,\eta)\in V
whenever
(z,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
and
\eta\neq 0
. Let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
be real-valued and positively homogenous of degree
1
in the fiber variable
\eta
, and let

Tf(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for all

and

is a Fourier integral operator
in standard form
, with phase function
\Phi
and symbol

. Moreover, we call an operator

a

if there exists a diffeomorphism

between open subsets

. Finally, an operator

if the Schwartz kernel of

is an element of

.

The following lemma reduces the analysis of relatively general Fourier integral operators to operators in standard form.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
n\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a homogeneous canonical relation from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to

Appendix C
More on variable-coefficient Wolff-type inequalities

In this appendix we will prove a converse statement to Proposition
5.3
. Such a converse statement is contained in
[
37
, Corollary 4.2]
for the Euclidean half-wave equation, with a relatively simple proof that uses the invertibility of the Euclidean half-wave propagators
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By contrast, a general FIO need not be invertible, and in fact the operators in Proposition
5.3
are never invertible at fixed times, due to the assumption that their symbol has compact spatial support. Instead, we may merely hope for microlocal invertibility wherever the operator is non-characteristic. This makes the converse statement to Proposition
5.3
less elegant than that in
[
37
, Corollary 4.2]
. Nonetheless, the main result of this appendix shows that, microlocally near any point where the operator
T
is non-characteristic, one may equivalently replace the decoupling norm in (
5.4
) by the
\mathcal{H}^{m-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of the initial data.

Throughout, we consider an operator
T\in I^{m-1/4}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+1};{\mathcal{C}})
as in Section
5.1
. Additionally, we will assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is as in Remark
A.3
:

{\mathcal{C}}=\{(x,t,\xi,\tau,y,\eta)\mid(x,t,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a),\eta\neq 0,(x,\xi)=\chi_{t}(y,\eta),\tau=q(x,t,\xi)\},

where each
\chi_{t}
is a homogeneous canonical transformation between open subsets of

. This assumption always holds locally in appropriate coordinate systems and, in this appendix, it only serves to simplify the statement of the main result. In particular, it implies the following. Set
T_{t}f(x):=Tf(x,t)
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
T_{t}\in I^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}}_{t})

whenever this set is non-empty. The inverse canonical relation

We note that
T
is non-characteristic at a point

(C.1)

|a(z,\eta)|\geq c{\langle}\eta\rangle^{m}\text{ for all }(z,\eta)\in V_{0}.

Also recall that the

Lemma C.1
.

{\langle}\eta\rangle^{-m}
.
Then, as in
[
10
, Theorem 4.2.5]
, one can cut off this reciprocal away from a small neighborhood of
c
to construct, for each
t\in J
and each conic subset
V\subseteq V^{\prime}
which is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
, a properly supported
\tilde{S}_{t}\in I^{-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n};{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{-1})
such that
\operatorname{WF}^{\prime}(I-\tilde{S}_{t}T_{t})\cap V=\emptyset
.
The relevant bounds for
\tilde{S}_{t}
and the pseudodifferential operator
R_{t}:=I-\tilde{S}_{t}T_{t}
are uniform in
t
. Moreover, since
a
has compact spatial support and because each
\tilde{S}_{t}
is properly supported, we may assume in addition that each
\tilde{S}_{t}
is compactly supported. By assumption,
{\mathcal{C}}_{t}
is a canonical graph, and therefore so is
{\mathcal{C}}_{t}^{-1}
.

Now set
S_{t}:=|J|^{-1}\tilde{S}_{t}
for each
t\in J
. Then (
2
) holds by construction.
Moreover, (
1
) is the dual of a standard estimate from the
L^{p}
theory of FIOs (see (
5.6
)):

\|S_{t}^{*}\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(W^{s-m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 1,

with an implicit constant which is uniform in
k
,
\nu
and
t\in J
, due to Remark
2.6
.
∎

Remark C.2
.

Note that we did not use anywhere in the proof that
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition. Indeed, we only used the specific form of the operator
T
in (
5.2
), and the assumption that the canonical relations
{\mathcal{C}}_{t}
are canonical graphs. In fact, the results in this appendix hold under these more general assumptions. The product structure of the symbol in Section
5.1
also plays no role here, nor do the low frequencies. In fact, by the very statement of Proposition
C.3
, it suffices to only require (
C.1
) when
|\eta|\geq 1/2
.

We can now prove a converse to Proposition
5.3
, a version for general FIOs of
[
37
, Corollary 4.2]
.
Recall that the wavefront set
\operatorname{WF}(f)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
of an
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the complement of the set of
(y_{0},\eta_{0})
such that there exists a pseudodifferential operator
S
, elliptic at
(y_{0},\eta_{0})
, with
Sf\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proposition C.3
.

Suppose that
T
is non-characteristic at a point
(z_{0},\zeta_{0},y_{0},\eta_{0})\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Then there exists a conic neighborhood
V
of
(y_{0},\eta_{0})
and, for all
p\in(2,\infty)
and
L\geq 0
, a
C\geq 0
such that

(C.3)

\|\phi_{k}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\big(\|T\phi_{k}(D)f\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}+2^{-kL}\|\phi_{k}(D)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\operatorname{WF}(f)\subseteq V
.

Proof.

By replacing
T
by
T{\langle}D\rangle^{-m}
, we may suppose that
m=0
.

Let
V
,
J
and
R
be as in Lemma
C.1
, and write
f_{k}:=\phi_{k}(D)f=\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\phi_{k}(D)f
. Then

(C.4)

f_{k}=\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)R\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}+\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)(1-R)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k},

and we will bound the
\mathcal{H}^{-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of each of the terms in (
C.4
) separately.

To deal with the first term on the right-hand side of (
C.4
), let
S
be a pseudodifferential operator of order zero, the symbol of which is supported away from
\operatorname{WF}^{\prime}(R)
and is equal to
1
on
V
. Then
\operatorname{WF}^{\prime}(R)\cap\operatorname{WF}^{\prime}(S)=\emptyset
and, for each
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\|(1-S)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{W^{r,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{W^{-L-(n-1)/2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

uniformly in
k
and
\nu
. Hence (
2.2
) and a standard Sobolev embedding yield, for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)R\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)R\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{W^{r,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|RS\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{W^{r,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|R(1-S)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{W^{r,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{W^{-L-(n-1)/2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 2^{k(n-1)/2}\|f_{k}\|_{W^{-L-(n-1)/2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{-kL}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where we also used that the
\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)
and
\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)
have kernels uniformly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

On the other hand, there exists a constant
N\geq 0
, independent of
k
and
\mu\in\Theta_{k}
, such that
\chi_{\mu}\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}\neq 0
for at most
N
elements
\nu\in\Theta_{k}
. Hence one can use
[
37
, Proposition 4.1]
and Hölder’s inequality to write

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)(1-R)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim\Big(\sum_{\mu\in\Theta_{k}}\Big\|\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\chi_{\mu}(D)\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)(1-R)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\mu\in\Theta_{k}}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\chi_{\mu}(D)\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)(1-R)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\mu\in\Theta_{k}}\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\chi_{\mu}(D)\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)(1-R)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)(1-R)\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\Big\|\int_{J}\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)S_{t}T_{t}\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\mathrm{d}t\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

Now Lemma
C.1
(
1
), together with Hölder’s inequality and the fact that
J
is a bounded interval, allows us to bound the quantity on the final line by

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\Big(\int_{J}\|\tilde{\phi}_{k}(D)\tilde{\chi}_{\nu}(D)S_{t}T_{t}\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\Big(\int_{J}\|T_{t}\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\int_{J}\|T_{t}\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\|Tf_{k}\|_{L^{p,k}_{\mathrm{dec}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})},

as required.
∎

Remark C.4
.

The reason to formulate (
C.3
) using the
\phi_{k}(D)f
, instead of a general
f
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
, is that a function with the latter property is necessarily smooth. Hence, in order for the condition
\operatorname{WF}(f)\subseteq V
to be useful, we dyadically localize a single function
f
in frequency, and it is the uniformity in
k
which is relevant. Note also that, when deriving local smoothing estimates, Theorem
5.1
is applied to functions of the form
\phi_{k}(D)f
.

For the same reason, one could replace the a priori assumption that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
f\in W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand, we work with square integrable functions to match the setting of Theorem
5.1
. Doing so limits us to
p>2
, because of the use of a standard Sobolev embedding. The same proof as above would work for any
p\in[1,\infty]
upon replacing
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark C.5
.

One could attempt to choose the set
V
as large as possible, which would lead to a stronger statement in Proposition
C.3
. Moreover, one could work with a more refined notion of wavefront set, taking into account only microlocalized
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
regularity. For the sake of simplicity, we will not do so here. Note also that, since the symbol
a
has compact spatial support, the operator
T
always has characteristic points.

Abstract
. We consider singularities of frontal surfaces of corank one and finite frontal codimension. We look at the classification under 𝒜 \mathscr{A} -equivalence and introduce the notion of frontalisation for singularities of fold type. We define the cuspidal and the transverse double point curves and prove that the frontal has finite codimension if and only if both curves are reduced. Finally, we also discuss about the frontal versions of the Marar-Mond formulas and the Mond’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we are interested in the local behaviour of complex analytic mappings
f\colon N\to Z
of frontal type, where
N
and
Z
are complex analytic manifolds of dimensions 2 and 3 respectively. In general, a mapping
f\colon N\to Z
is called a frontal if it admits a Legendrian lifting
\tilde{f}:N\to PT^{*}Z
, where
PT^{*}Z
is the projectivised cotangent bundle of
Z
. Roughly speaking, this means that the image
f(N)
has a well defined tangent hyperplane at each point
f(x)
, with
x\in N
.

Singularities of frontals were considered for the first time by Zakalyukin and Kurbatskii in
[
34
]
and they
are a natural generalisation of wave fronts, which occur in the particular case that the Legendrian lifting
\tilde{f}
is an immersion. There has been a great interest for frontals in the last decades, specially in the
C^{\infty}
real category and looking at differential geometric properties. The fact that you have a well defined tangent plane everywhere provides a nice starting point if you want to extend things like first or second fundamental forms, curvatures, etc to submanifolds with singularities (see for instance
[
5
,
18
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
).

In a forthcoming paper
[
26
]
, we will develop the general Thom-Mather theory of frontals of any dimension, but restricted to corank one singularities. Our approach is based on Ishikawa’s work
[
11
]
about stability and infinitesimal deformations of integral mappings
\tilde{f}\colon N\to PT^{*}Z
under Legendrian equivalence,
although we want to understand the singularity downstairs, at the level of the frontal, rather than upstairs, at the level of the Legendrian lifting. In particular, we consider infinitesimal deformations which come from frontal unfoldings of
f
itself. The corank one assumption is a technical but necessary condition in order to be able to apply Ishikawa’s theory.

In our case, by taking local charts in
N
and
Z
we can restate our problem in terms of classification of frontal map germs
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
of corank one under
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence, that is, under holomorphic coordinate changes in the source and the target. It is well known that the only frontal stable singularities of surfaces are cuspidal edges, swallowtails, folded Whitney umbrellas or their transverse self-intersections (see Figure
3
). By the frontal version of the Mather-Gaffney criterion (see Theorem
3.6
), if
f
has finite frontal codimension, then it has isolated instability and hence, the only singularities outside the origin are cuspidal edges or transverse double points. Our main goal is to study the geometry and the invariants of this type of frontal surface singularities.

In Section 3, we look at the classification of simple frontal singularities. We introduce the notion of frontalisation of a germ of fold type and we deduce that the classification of frontals of fold type is closely related to the classification of
\mathscr{A}
-simple singularities obtained by Mond in
[
21
]
. In particular, all simple germs of fold type in Mond’s classification
S_{k},B_{k},C_{k}
and
F_{4}
have a frontal version with the same frontal codimension.

In Section 4, we define the cuspidal curve
C(f)
and the transverse double point curve
D_{+}(f)
. These two curves are considered with a certain analytic structure and they have the property that
f
has finite frontal codimension if and only if both curves are reduced. We recall that
f
has finite
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension if and only if the double point curve
D(f)
is reduced (see
[
15
]
). If
f
has corank one, then we can assume that is given by
f(x,y)=(x,p(x,y),q(x,y))
, for some function germs
p
and
q
. The space
D^{2}(f)
is defined in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
by the divided differences:

\frac{p(x,y^{\prime})-p(x,y)}{y^{\prime}-y}=\frac{q(x,y^{\prime})-q(x,y)}{y^{\prime}-y}=0,

and the projection to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
given by
(x,y,y^{\prime})\mapsto(x,y)
is precisely the double point curve
D(f)
. As set germs,
D(f)=C(f)\cup D_{+}(f)
. However when
f
is a frontal,
D(f)
has a non reduced equation
p_{y}^{2}\tau=0
, where
p_{y}=0
is the equation of
D(f)
and
\tau=0
is the equation of
D_{+}(f)
.

Finally, in Section 5 we consider a frontal stable perturbation
f_{s}
of
f
. This always exists and is well defined when
f
has finite frontal codimension. We get invariants which count the number of 0-stable singularities of
f_{s}
which we call
S,W,K
and
T
and correspond to the number of swallowtails, folded Whitney umbrellas, cuspidal double points and triple points, respectively. We give algebraic formulas to compute these invariants in terms of some algebras related to
f
and prove the frontal version of the Marar-Mond formulas (see
[
17
]
). Moreover, as it happens with germs of finite
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension, the image of
f_{s}
has the homotopy type of a wedge of
2
-spheres and the number of such spheres is called the frontal Milnor number, denoted by
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
. This is analogous of the image Milnor number
\mu_{I}(f)
defined by Mond in
[
22
]
in the context of finite
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension. We discuss some basic properties of this frontal Milnor number, and propose a frontal version of Mond’s conjecture
[
23
]
, which states that the image Milnor number of
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
is greater than or equal to its
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension for all values of
n
such that
(n,n+1)
are in Mather’s nice dimensions, with equality if and only if
f
is quasihomogeneous.

3.
Frontal map-germs

Roughly speaking, a frontal hypersurface is an analytic hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
that has a well-defined tangent space at each point.
If
X
has a singularity at a point
x
, we consider a sequence of regular points
(x_{m})\subseteq X
converging to
x
.

Let
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be the projectivized cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
If
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, we equip
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with the contact structure given by the differential form

\alpha=\omega_{1}\,dz^{1}+\dots+\omega_{n+1}\,dz^{n+1}

A holomorphic
F\colon N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
integral
if
F^{*}\alpha=0
.
We also say a projection
\pi\colon PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is a
Legendrian fibration
for
\alpha
if
\ker d\pi_{(z,[\omega])}\subseteq\ker\alpha_{(z,[\omega])}
for all
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A holomorphic
f\colon N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
frontal
if there exists an integral
F\colon N\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and a Legendrian projection
\pi\colon PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that

f=\pi\circ F

If
F
is an immersion, we say
f
is a
wavefront
.
Similarly, a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is frontal (resp. a front) if there exists a frontal (resp. wavefront)
f\colon N\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that
X=f(N)
.

Let
(z,,p)
be coordinates for
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, and
\pi\colon PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be the canonical projection,
\pi(z,p)=z
.
It is easy to see that
\pi
is a Legendrian fibration for
\alpha
.
If
F\colon N\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is an integral map and
f=\pi\circ F
,

0=F^{*}\alpha=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\nu_{i}d(Z_{i}\circ F)=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}\nu_{i}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx^{j}

for some
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n+1}\in\mathscr{O}_{n}
, not all of them zero (since they come from a projective space).
This is the same as claiming that there exists a nowhere-vanishing
\nu\colon N\to T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that, for all vector fields
\xi
on
N
,

\nu(df\circ\xi)=0

Since

is a fibre bundle, we can find for each pair

an open neighbourhood

of

such that

Therefore, the integral map

is
\mathscr{A}
-equivalent to

.
This map will be an epimorphism by construction and will send

onto

Let

be the germ of immersion given by

.
If

We then define the linear map

(1)

(2)

if and only if there exist

such that

.
It then follows that

\mathscr{F}(\check{f})\cong\frac{\theta(\check{f})}{\mathscr{O}_{2}\{(0,0,y)\}}

Lemma 3.8

Given a fold surface

(1)

\mathscr{O}_{2}^{T}:=\{g(x,y^{2}):g\in\mathscr{O}_{2}\}
;

(2)

;

(3)

if

,
T_{e}\mathscr{K}^{T}(p\circ h):=h^{*}(p_{x},yp_{y},p)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Let

, and

.
By definition of

, we have

\displaystyle T_{e}\mathscr{K}^{T}(q\circ h)

\displaystyle=h^{*}(q_{x},yq_{y},q)=h^{*}(yp_{x},y(p+yp_{y}),yp)=

\displaystyle=y^{2}h^{*}(p_{x},yp_{y},p)=y^{2}T_{e}\mathscr{K}^{T}(p\circ h)

Using
[

\displaystyle T\mathscr{A}_{e}f=

4.
Double point curve of a frontal surface

Marar and Tari
[
16
]
studied the geometric invariants of corank
1

One of their findings was that the source double point space (see §5.2 below) of the discriminant of

is given by an equation in the form

, where

are the equations for the cuspidal edge and transverse double point curves in the discriminant of

In particular, the discriminant of

is a front if and only if
\Sigma(F)
is smooth in

(see
[
2
]
).
Therefore, this formula can be also applied to

In this section, we show that this formula holds for any frontal, not just fronts.

C(f_{t})^{1}
and
D_{+}(f_{t})^{1}
, which we shall simply denote as
C_{t}^{1}
and
D_{t}^{1}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
be a corank
1
frontal map-germ with isolated instability.
If
(f_{t})
is an
\mathscr{F}
-stabilisation of
f
, the projection
\pi_{X}:X(F)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
is flat for
X\in\{C,D\}
.

Proof.

Since
f
is in prenormal form, we can write

F(x,y,t)=(x,p_{t}(x,y),q_{t}(x,y),t)

and we can compute
D(F)
using the same procedure as described in §
4

We then have that
C(F)=V((p_{t})_{y})
and
D_{+}(F)=V(\tau_{t})
.
Both of these spaces are analytic surfaces contained in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}
with codimension
1

Let
X=C,D
and consider the projection
\pi_{X}\colon X(F)\to\mathbb{C}
given by
\pi_{X}(x,y,t)=t
.
We have
\pi^{-1}(\{0\})=X(f)
, which is a subspace of codimension

in

(\mathbb{C},0)
.
It then follows from

§23 that
\pi_{X}
is flat.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
be a corank
1
frontal surface with isolated
\mathscr{F}
-instability.
If
V(p_{y},\mu_{y})=\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\mu(f(C(f)),0)

\displaystyle=2S+\mu(C(f),0);

\displaystyle 2\mu(f(D(f)),0)

\displaystyle=2K+2T+\mu(D(f),0)-W-S+1

To prove this result, we shall make use of the following

Theorem 5.5

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to D\subset\mathbb{C}
be a good representative of a flat family
\pi\colon(X,x)\to(D,0)
of reduced curves. Given

,

\mu(X_{0},x)-\mu(X_{t})=1-\chi(X_{t})

where
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
and
\mu(X_{t})

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

On the one hand, cuspidal edges are
1

, hence
C_{t}^{1}\cong f(C_{t}^{1})

Since
\chi(C_{t})-\chi(C_{t}^{1})=\chi(f(C_{t}))-\chi(f(C_{t}^{1}))

\chi(C_{t})=\chi(f(C_{t}))

On the other hand, transverse double points are 2-to-1, so

\displaystyle\chi(D_{t})-\chi(D_{t}^{1})

5.3.
Frontal Milnor number

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a smooth multi-germ with isolated instability and let
f\colon N\to Z
be a representative of
f
.
Mond
[
22
]
showed that the disentanglement of
f
has the homology type of a wedge of
n
-spheres for a small enough
t\neq 0
.
These groups are known as the
vanishing homology
of
f
.

Lê
[
33
]
proved that the vanishing homology does not depend on the choice of stabilisation, thus being an invariant for
f
.
The number of spheres is known as the
image Milnor number
of
f
,
\mu_{I}(f)
.
Moreover, Lê’s result can also be applied in the case when
f
is frontal and has an isolated frontal instability, allowing us to define the notion of a frontal Milnor number.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a frontal multi-germ with isolated
\mathscr{F}
-instability.
We define the
frontal Milnor number

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
of
f
as the number of
n
-spheres in the image of an
\mathscr{F}
-stabilisation of
f
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Given a non-constant, holomorphic plane curve
\gamma\colon(\mathbb{C},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
,

\mu_{I}(\gamma)=\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(\gamma)+\kappa

where
\kappa
is the number of cusps in a frontal stabilisation of
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\colon N\to Z
be a representative of
\gamma
.
Since
\gamma
is non-constant, the Curve Selection Lemma implies that
S
is an isolated subset of
\Sigma(\gamma)
.
Shrinking
N
if necessary, we can further assume that
N\cap\Sigma(\gamma)=S
, so
\gamma
is immersive outside
S
.
Since immersive maps are both
\mathscr{A}
-stable and
\mathscr{F}
-stable, the Mather-Gaffney criterion then gives us Item 1, and Theorem
3.6
gives us Item 2.

Let
\gamma_{t}\colon N_{t}\to Z
be a frontal stabilisation of
\gamma
.
The curve
\gamma_{t}(N_{t})
contains at most normal crossings and plane cusps.
By the conservation property of the image Milnor number
[
8
]
, we have

\mu_{I}(\gamma)=\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(\gamma)+\sum_{\mathclap{x\in\Sigma(\gamma_{t})}}\mu_{I}(\gamma_{t};\gamma_{t}(x))

Let
x\in\Sigma(\gamma_{t})
.
Since
\gamma_{t}
is
\mathscr{F}
-stable, the germ of
\gamma_{t}
at
x
is a cusp, so
\mu_{I}(\gamma_{t};\gamma_{t}(x))=1
and the statement follows.
∎

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)

We consider the function

\pi(X,Y,Z,t)=t
.
A result by Lê
[

]
states that the fibres of
\pi
have the homotopy type of a bouquet of
2
-spheres; however, one of such fibres is
\Delta_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
.
Therefore,

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\chi(f_{t}(N_{t}))-1=\chi(f_{t}(N_{t}^{2}))+\chi(f_{t}(N_{t}^{1})+\chi(f_{t}(N_{t}^{0})-1

Theorem 5.7
.

Given a corank
1
frontal
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
with isolated
\mathscr{F}
-instability,

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)

\displaystyle=\mu(f(D(f)),0)-S-W+T+1=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mu(D(f),0)+3(1-S-W)\Big)+K+2T

Proof.

We can assume without any loss of generality that
N_{t}
is an open, convex neighbourhood of
0
, in which case
\chi(N_{t}^{2})=1-\chi(N_{t}^{0})-\chi(N_{t}^{1})
.
Since
f_{t}(N_{t}^{2})\cong N_{t}^{2}
by construction,

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\chi(f_{t}(N_{t}^{1}))-\chi(N_{t}^{1})+\chi(f_{t}(N_{t}^{0}))-\chi(N_{t}^{0})

Recall that
N_{t}^{1}=C(f_{t})^{1}\sqcup D(f_{t})^{1}
.
Since
C_{t}^{1}\cong f_{t}(C_{t}^{1})
and
D_{t}^{1}
is a double cover for
f_{t}(D_{t}^{1})
,

\displaystyle\chi(f_{t}(N_{t})^{0})-\chi(N_{t}^{0})

\displaystyle=-K-2T;

\displaystyle\chi(f_{t}(N_{t})^{1})-\chi(N_{t}^{1})

\displaystyle=\chi(f_{t}(D_{t}))-S-W-K-T;

from which follows that
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\chi(f_{t}(D_{t}))-S-W-2K-3T
.
Using Theorem
5.5

\chi(f_{t}(D_{t}))=2K+2T-\mu(f(D),0)+1
, from which follows that

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\mu(f(D),0)-S-W+T+1

∎

Precise Performance Analysis of the Box-Elastic Net under Matrix Uncertainties
Ayed M. Alrashdi, Ismail Ben Atitallah, Tareq Y. Al-Naffouri

Abstract
In this letter, we consider the problem of recovering an unknown sparse signal from noisy linear measurements, using an enhanced version of the popular Elastic-Net (EN) method. We modify the EN by adding a box-constraint, and we call it the Box-Elastic Net (Box-EN). We assume independent identically distributed (iid) real Gaussian measurement matrix with additive Gaussian noise. In many practical situations, the measurement matrix is not perfectly known, and so we only have a noisy estimate of it. In this work, we precisely characterize the mean squared error and the probability of support recovery of the Box-Elastic Net in the high-dimensional asymptotic regime. Numerical simulations validate the theoretical predictions derived in the paper and also show that the boxed variant outperforms the standard EN.

II
Problem Setup

II-A

System Model

We consider the problem of recovering an unknown signal
{\bf x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from a noisy linear measurement vector
{\bf y}={\bf H}{\bf x}_{0}+{\bf z}
.
The unknown signal vector
{\bf x}_{0}
is assumed to be
k
-sparse, i.e., only
k
of its entries are sampled iid from a distribution
p_{X_{0}}
which has zero mean and unit variance (
\mathbb{E}[X_{0}^{2}]=1
), and the remaining entries are zeros.
For the measurement matrix, we consider the following additive uncertainty model:

{\bf A}=\gamma{\bf H}+\epsilon\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$},

where
{\bf H}
, and
\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
have iid entries
\mathcal{N}(0,1/n)
,
and
\epsilon^{2}\in[0,1]
is the variance of the error such that
\gamma^{2}+\epsilon^{2}=1
.
The noise vector
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
has entries iid
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2})
.
The analysis is performed when the system dimensions (
m
,
n
and
k
) grow simultaneously large at fixed ratios:

\frac{m}{n}\longrightarrow\delta\in(0,\infty)
, and
\frac{k}{n}\longrightarrow\kappa\in(0,1).
Under these settings, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) becomes SNR :=
\frac{\kappa}{\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}
.

II-B

Performance Metrics

We consider the following two performance metrics of the Box-Elastic Net:

Mean squared error (MSE)
: A natural and heavily used measure of performance is the reconstruction
mean squared error
, which measures the deviation of
\hat{{\bf x}}
from the true signal
{\bf x}_{0}
. Formally, the MSE is defined as
\text{MSE}:=\frac{1}{n}\|\hat{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}
.

Support Recovery
: In the problem of sparse recovery, a natural measure of performance that is used in many applications is support recovery, which is defined as identifying whether an entry of
{\bf x}_{0}
is on the support (i.e., non-zero), or it is off the support (i.e., zero). The decision is based on the Box-EN solution
\hat{{\bf x}}
: we say the
i^{th}
entry of
\hat{{\bf x}}
is on the support if
|\hat{{\bf x}}_{i}|\geq\xi
, where
\xi>0
is a user-defined hard threshold on the entries of
\hat{{\bf x}}.
Formally, let

\displaystyle\Phi_{\xi,\text{on}}(\hat{{\bf x}})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in S({\bf x}_{0})}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\hat{{\bf x}}_{i}|\geq\xi\}},

(3a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\xi,\text{off}}(\hat{{\bf x}})=\frac{1}{n-k}\sum_{i\notin S({\bf x}_{0})}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\hat{{\bf x}}_{i}|\leq\xi\}},

(3b)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{A}\}}
is the indicator function of a set
\mathcal{A}
, and
S({\bf x}_{0})
is the support of
{\bf x}_{0}
, i.e., the set of the non-zero entries of
{\bf x}_{0}
. In Theorem
2
, we precisely predict the
per-entry
rate of successful on-support and off-support recovery.

III
Approach and Sketch of the Proof

In this section, we provide the asymptotic analysis of the MSE of the proposed Box-EN optimization. Our approach is based on the recently developed Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem (CGMT) which is summarized in the next subsection.

III-A

Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem (CGMT)

We first need to state the key ingredient of the analysis which is the Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem CGMT. Here, we recall the statement of the theorem, and we refer the reader to
[
18
]
for the complete technical requirements.
Consider the following two min-max problems, which we refer to as the Primary Optimization (PO) and Auxiliary Optimization (AO):

\displaystyle\Phi({\bf G}):=\underset{{\bf w}\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ {\bf u}^{T}{\bf G}{\bf w}+\psi({\bf w},{\bf u}),

(4a)

\displaystyle\phi({\bf g},{\bf h}):=\underset{{\bf w}\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \|{\bf w}\|_{2}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}-\|{\bf u}\|_{2}{\bf h}^{T}{\bf w}+\psi({\bf w},{\bf u}),

(4b)

where
{\bf G}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},{\bf g}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},{\bf h}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathcal{S}_{w}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathcal{S}_{u}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
. Denote by
{\bf w}_{\Phi}:={\bf w}_{\Phi}({\bf G})
and
{\bf w}_{\phi}:={\bf w}_{\phi}({\bf g},{\bf h})
any optimal minimizers of (
4a
) and (
4b
) respectively. Let
\mathcal{S}_{w},\mathcal{S}_{u}
be convex, and assume that
\psi({\bf w},{\bf u})
is convex-concave continuous on
\mathcal{S}_{w}\times\mathcal{S}_{u}
and
{\bf G},{\bf g}
and
{\bf h}
all have iid standard normal entries. Let
\mathcal{S}
be any arbitrary open subset of
\mathcal{S}_{w}
.
Then, if
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}[{\bf w}_{\phi}\in\mathcal{S}]=1,
it also holds that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}[{\bf w}_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{S}]=1.

In a nutshell, we study the performance of the (PO) by analyzing its corresponding (AO) which is much easier to study, since it depends on the random vectors
{\bf g}
and
{\bf h}
instead of the large random matrix
{\bf G}
, simplifying the latter to a Scalar Optimization (SO), and finally studying the asymptotic performance of the (SO). To make use of the CGMT, the set
\mathcal{S}
should be properly chosen as the set in which the MSE concentrates. In the following subsection, we start the MSE analysis by specializing the CGMT to the Box-EN problem at hand and identifying its corresponding (PO) and (AO).

III-B

Identifying the (PO) and the (AO)

Under the imperfect measurements assumption, the Box-EN optimization in (
2
) becomes

\hat{{\bf x}}=\text{arg}\ \underset{{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{B}}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \|{\bf y}-{\bf A}{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1}\|{\bf x}\|_{1}+\lambda_{2}\|{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}.

(5)

For convenience, we consider the vector
{\bf w}:=\gamma{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0}
, and also the modified Box set:

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{{\bf w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:l-{\bf x}_{0,i}\leq{\bf w}_{i}\leq u-{\bf x}_{0,i},i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\},

(6)

then the problem in (
5
) can be reformulated as

\hat{{\bf w}}=\text{arg}\ \underset{{\bf w}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \|{\bf H}{\bf w}+\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0})-{\bf z}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

This minimization is not in the (PO) form as it is missing the max part. So to go around this, let us express the loss function in its dual form through the Fenchel conjugate,

\|{\bf H}{\bf w}+\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0})-{\bf z}\|_{2}^{2}

=\max_{{\bf u}}\sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}({\bf H}{\bf w}+\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0})-{\bf z})-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}
.
Hence, the problem above is equivalent to the following

\displaystyle\underset{{\bf w}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}{\bf H}{\bf w}+\frac{\sqrt{n}\epsilon}{\gamma}{\bf u}^{T}\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0})-\sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}{\bf z}

\displaystyle-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}.

(7)

To reach the desired (PO) form, we introduce the variables

{\bf v}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf w}\\[3.00003pt]
\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0})\\[3.00003pt]
\end{bmatrix}

\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,

{\bf G}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf H}&\hbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}\\[3.00003pt]
\end{bmatrix}

\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 2n}
and

{\bf C}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bf O}_{n}&\frac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\mathbf{I}_{n}\\[3.00003pt]
\end{bmatrix}

\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 2n}
, where
{\bf O}_{n}
and
\mathbf{I}_{n}
are
n\times n
matrices that represent the all-zeros matrix and the identity matrix, respectively and use them to rewrite (
III-B
) in the desired (PO) form as

\displaystyle\underset{{\bf v}\in\mathcal{D}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}{\bf G}{\bf v}-\sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}{\bf z}-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\|{\bf C}{\bf v}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf C}{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2},

where
\mathcal{D}=\{{\bf v}^{T}=[{\bf w}^{T}\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0})^{T}]:{\bf w}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\}
. The corresponding (AO) problem is thus given by

\displaystyle\underset{{\bf v}\in\mathcal{D}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \|{\bf v}\|_{2}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}-\|{\bf u}\|_{2}\hbox{\boldmath$\mu$}^{T}{\bf v}-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}{\bf z}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\|{\bf C}{\bf v}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf C}{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2},

(8)

where
\hbox{\boldmath$\mu$}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and
{\bf g}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are independent standard normal vectors.

III-C

Simplifying the (AO)

The next step is to simplify the (AO) to a scalar optimization (SO) problem.
Since the vectors
{\bf g}
and
{\bf z}
are independent,

\|{\bf v}\|_{2}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}-\sqrt{n}{\bf u}^{T}{\bf z}
is equivalent in distribution to
\sqrt{\|{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2}+n\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}
. Therefore, the (AO) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\underset{{\bf v}\in\mathcal{D}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \sqrt{\|{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2}+n\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}-\|{\bf u}\|_{2}\hbox{\boldmath$\mu$}^{T}{\bf v}-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\|{\bf C}{\bf v}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf C}{\bf v}\|_{2}^{2}.

(9)

Let us write (
III-C
) now in terms of the original
{\bf w}
variable

\displaystyle\underset{{\bf w}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \sqrt{\|{\bf w}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+n\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-\|{\bf u}\|_{2}({\bf h}_{1}^{T}{\bf w}+\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma}{\bf h}_{2}^{T}({\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}))

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\gamma}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{1}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\gamma^{2}}\|{\bf w}+{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2},

where
{\bf h}_{1},{\bf h}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are independent standard normal vectors.
Alternatively, since
{\bf w}=\gamma{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0}
, we can express the above optimization over
{\bf x}
instead as

\displaystyle\underset{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{B}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\bf u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\sqrt{\|{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-2\gamma{\bf x}_{0}^{T}{\bf x}+n\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{\bf g}^{T}{\bf u}-\frac{n}{4}\|{\bf u}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-\|{\bf u}\|_{2}(\gamma{\bf h}_{1}+\epsilon{\bf h}_{2})^{T}{\bf x}+\|{\bf u}\|_{2}{\bf h}_{1}^{T}{\bf x}_{0}+\lambda_{1}\|{\bf x}\|_{1}+\lambda_{2}\|{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}.

Fixing the norm of
{\bf u}
to
\beta:=\|{\bf u}\|_{2}
, we can easily optimize over its direction by aligning it with
{\bf g}
further simplifying the (AO) to

\displaystyle\underset{\beta\geq 0}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \underset{{\bf x}\in\mathcal{B}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \sqrt{n}\beta\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}(\|{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-2\gamma{\bf x}_{0}^{T}{\bf x})+\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}\|{\bf g}\|_{2}

\displaystyle-\beta(\gamma{\bf h}_{1}+\epsilon{\bf h}_{2})^{T}{\bf x}+\beta{\bf h}_{1}^{T}{\bf x}_{0}-\frac{n\beta^{2}}{4}+\lambda_{1}\|{\bf x}\|_{1}+\lambda_{2}\|{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}.

(10)

To have a separable optimization problem, we use the following variational form:

\sqrt{\chi}=\underset{\alpha>0}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\chi}{2\alpha},

where

\chi=\frac{1}{n}(\|{\bf x}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-2\gamma{\bf x}_{0}^{T}{\bf x})+\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}.

(11)

which reduces (
III-C
) to

\displaystyle\underset{\tau>0}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{\beta\geq 0}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \frac{\beta\tau\|{\bf g}\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{n\beta\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{2\tau}-\frac{n\beta^{2}}{4}+\frac{\beta\|{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{2\tau}

\displaystyle+\beta{\bf h}_{1}^{T}{\bf x}_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\biggl(\underset{l\leq{\bf x}_{i}\leq u}{\operatorname{\min}}\ (\frac{\beta}{2\tau}+\lambda_{2}){\bf x}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle-\beta\biggl({{\bf h}}_{i}+\frac{\gamma{\bf x}_{0,i}}{\tau}\biggr){\bf x}_{i}+\lambda_{1}|{\bf x}_{i}|\biggr),

(12)

where
\tau:=\frac{\sqrt{n}\alpha}{\|{\bf g}\|_{2}}
, and
\theta=\frac{\beta}{2\tau}+\lambda_{2}
.
We need to prove that the optimal
\beta_{*}
,
denoted by
\beta_{n}
, is positive alomst surely.
To do so, suppose by contradiction that
\beta_{n}
= 0. First, it is possible to show using
problem (10) that
\beta_{n}=2\text{max}\{0,\sqrt{\chi}\frac{||\mathbf{g}||}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{\tilde{h}}^{T}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{0}\}
, where
\mathbf{\tilde{h}}=\gamma\mathbf{h}_{1}+\epsilon\mathbf{h}_{2}
. If
\beta_{n}=0
, then this is equivalent to say that

\sqrt{\chi}\frac{||\mathbf{g}||}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{\tilde{h}}^{T}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{0}\ \leq 0.

(13)

If
\beta_{n}=0
, the problem in (12) simplifies to the following:
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underset{l\leq\mathbf{x}_{i}\leq u}{\text{min}}\lambda_{2}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{2}+\lambda_{1}|\mathbf{x}_{i}|
, which implies that the optimal

is the zero vector. Consequently,
\sqrt{\chi}\frac{||\mathbf{g}||}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{\tilde{h}}^{T}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}||\mathbf{x}_{0}||^{2}+\sigma_{\mathbf{z}}^{2}}\frac{||\mathbf{g}||}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{0}

\mathbf{h}_{1}
is a standard Gaussian
vector and then
\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{h}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{0}
converges almost surely to zero. Hence, the latter quantity is almost surely strictly
positive, which contradicts (13), i.e. the assumption
\beta_{n}=0
.
Thus,
\beta_{n}>0

After some algebraic manipulations, we can write

\displaystyle\underset{\tau>0}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{\beta>0}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \frac{\beta\tau\|{\bf g}\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{n\beta\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{2\tau}-\frac{n\beta^{2}}{4}+\beta{\bf h}_{1}^{T}{\bf x}_{0}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{\beta}{2\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}}{4\theta\tau^{2}}){\bf x}_{0,i}^{2}-\frac{\beta^{2}\gamma}{2\theta\tau}{{\bf h}}_{i}{\bf x}_{0,i}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\theta}{{\bf h}}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\theta\biggr(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underset{l\leq{\bf x}_{i}\leq u}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \frac{1}{2}({\bf x}_{i}-\frac{\beta}{2\theta}(\frac{\gamma}{\tau}{\bf x}_{0,i}+{{\bf h}}_{i}))^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta}|{\bf x}_{i}|\biggl).

The optimization over

can be solved in closed-form using the

\eta(a;\lambda,l,u)=\text{argmin}_{l\leq x\leq u}

\frac{1}{2}(x-a)^{2}+\lambda|x|

\eta(a;\lambda,l,u)=\begin{cases}u&,\text{if}\ a>u+\lambda\\

(14)

Also, let

\frac{1}{2}a^{2}&,\text{if}\ |a|\leq\lambda\\
-\lambda a-\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}&,\text{if}\ l-\lambda<a<-\lambda\\
\frac{1}{2}(l-a)^{2}-\lambda l&,\text{if}\ a\leq l-\lambda.\end{cases}

(15)

Then, the above optimization problem finally simplifies to the following Scalar Optimization (SO) problem:

\displaystyle\underset{\tau>0}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{\beta>0}{\operatorname{\max}}\ \tilde{D}(\tau,\beta,{\bf g},{\bf h}):=\frac{\beta\tau\|{\bf g}\|_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{n\beta\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{2\tau}-\frac{n\beta^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{\beta}{2\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}}{4\theta\tau^{2}}){\bf x}_{0,i}^{2}+(\beta-\frac{\beta^{2}\gamma}{2\theta\tau}){\bf h}_{i}{\bf x}_{0,i}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\theta}{\bf h}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\theta\sum_{i=1}^{n}e\biggr(\frac{\gamma\beta}{2\theta\tau}{\bf x}_{0,i}+\frac{\beta}{2\theta}{\bf h}_{i};\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta},l,u\biggl).

(16)

\beta>0
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e(\frac{\gamma\beta}{2\theta\tau}{\bf x}_{0,i}+\frac{\beta}{2\theta}{\bf h}_{i};\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta},l,u)\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}[e(\frac{\gamma\beta}{2\theta\tau}X_{0}+\frac{\beta}{2\theta}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta},l,u)]
, and
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{{\bf x}}_{i}\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}[\eta(\frac{\gamma\beta}{2\theta\tau}X_{0}+\frac{\beta}{2\theta}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta},l,u)]
, where
\tilde{{\bf x}}
is the solution of (AO) defined in (
III-C
) and
H
is a scalar standard normal random variable.
Therefore, the point-wise convergence in
\tau
and
\beta
of the objective function in (
III-C
) is

D(\tau,\beta):=\frac{\beta\tau\delta}{2}+\frac{\beta\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{2\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\theta}(\theta+1)+(\frac{\beta}{2\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}}{4\theta\tau^{2}})\kappa+2\theta\mathbb{E}_{X_{0},H}[e(\frac{\gamma\beta}{2\theta\tau}X_{0}+\frac{\beta}{2\theta}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta},l,u)]
. Furthermore, it is possible to show that with probability one, the functions

\tau\mapsto{\operatorname{\max}}_{\beta>0}\tilde{D}(\tau,\beta,{\bf g},{\bf h})
and
\tau\mapsto{\operatorname{\max}}_{\beta>0}D(\tau,\beta)
are convex in
\tau
. Hence, it is possible to show using Theorem 2.7 in
[
26
]
that
\tau_{n}({\bf g},{\bf h})\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\tau_{*}
. Likewise, it can be similarly proved that
\beta_{n}({\bf g},{\bf h})\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\beta_{*}
.

III-E

Applying the CGMT

Now, we are in a position to study the convergence limit of the MSE of the Box-EN. First, recall from (
11
) that
\frac{1}{n}\|\tilde{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{\|{\bf g}\|_{2}^{2}}{n}\tau_{n}^{2}({\bf g},{\bf h})+\frac{2}{n}(\gamma-1)\tilde{{\bf x}}^{T}{\bf x}_{0}-\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}
. Moreover, it can be shown that
\frac{1}{n}\tilde{{\bf x}}^{T}{\bf x}_{0}\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{E}[\eta(\frac{\gamma\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}\tau_{*}}X_{0}+\frac{\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}},l,u)X_{0}]:=\Upsilon(\tau_{*},\beta_{*})
. Using
\tau_{n}({\bf g},{\bf h})\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\tau_{*}
, and
\beta_{n}({\bf g},{\bf h})\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\beta_{*}
, we can show that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\|\tilde{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{P}{\longrightarrow}\delta\tau_{*}^{2}-\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}+2(\gamma-1)\Upsilon(\tau_{*},\beta_{*})

\displaystyle:=M(\tau_{*},\beta_{*}).

(17)

The last step is to use the CGMT to prove that the quantities
\hat{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}
and
\tilde{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}
are concentrated in the same set. Formally,
for any fixed
\zeta>0
, we define the set

\mathcal{S}=\bigl\{{\bf r}:\bigl|\frac{1}{n}\|{\bf r}\|_{2}^{2}-M(\tau_{*},\beta_{*})\bigr|<\zeta\bigr\}
.

Equation (
III-E
) proves that for any
\zeta>0
,
\tilde{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
with probability one. Then, we conclude using the CGMT that
\hat{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
with probability one.
The asymptotic prediction of the MSE is summarized in Theorem
1
given in the next section.

IV
Main Results

This section summarizes our main results on the precise analysis of the mean squared error and the probability of support recovery of the Box-EN.
We use standard notation
\textsl{plim}\ X_{n}=X
to denote that a sequence of random variables

X_{n}
converges in probability towards a constant
X
.

Theorem 1

(Box-EN MSE)
.

Fix
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
, and let
\hat{{\bf x}}
be a minimizer of the Box-EN problem in (
5
), where
{\bf A},{\bf z}
and
{\bf x}_{0}
satisfy the working assumptions of Section
II-A
.
Then, in the limit of

m,n\to+\infty,m/n=\delta
, it holds:

\displaystyle\textsl{plim}\ \frac{1}{n}\|\hat{{\bf x}}-{\bf x}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}=\delta\tau_{*}^{2}-\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}

\displaystyle+2(\gamma-1)\mathbb{E}_{\underset{H\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}{X_{0}\sim p_{X_{0}}}}\biggr[\eta\biggr(\frac{\gamma\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}\tau_{*}}X_{0}+\frac{\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}},l,u\biggl)X_{0}\biggl],

(18)

where

\theta_{*}=\frac{\beta_{*}}{2\tau_{*}}+\lambda_{2}
, and
(\tau_{*},\beta_{*})
is the unique solution to the following:

\displaystyle\underset{\tau>0}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{\beta>0}{\operatorname{\max}}\ D(\tau,\beta):=\frac{\beta\tau\delta}{2}+\frac{\beta\sigma_{{\bf z}}^{2}}{2\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\theta}(\theta+1)

\displaystyle+(\frac{\beta}{2\tau}-\frac{\beta^{2}\gamma^{2}}{4\theta\tau^{2}})\kappa+2\theta\mathbb{E}_{X_{0},H}\biggr[e\biggr(\frac{\gamma\beta}{2\theta\tau}X_{0}+\frac{\beta}{2\theta}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta},l,u\biggl)\biggl].

(19)

Remark
1 (Optimal Solutions). Note that
\tau_{*}
and
\beta_{*}
can be efficiently computed by writing the first order stationary point conditions, i.e.,
\nabla_{(\tau,\beta)}D(\tau,\beta)=0.

Remark
2 (Optimal Regularizers). Theorem 1 can be used to find the optimal pair of regularizers
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
that minimizes the MSE.

Remark
3. For a sparse Bernoulli vector
{\bf x}_{0}
, the expectation in (18) is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggr[\eta\biggr(\frac{\gamma\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}\tau_{*}}X_{0}+\frac{\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}},l,u\biggl)X_{0}\biggl]=\kappa uQ(\frac{2\theta_{*}(u-\xi)+\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}})

\displaystyle+\kappa lQ(\frac{2\theta_{*}(\xi-l)+\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}})+\kappa\int_{\frac{\lambda_{1}-2\theta_{*}\xi}{\beta_{*}}}^{\frac{2\theta_{*}(u-\xi)+\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}}}(\xi+\frac{\beta_{*}h-\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}})p(h)dh

\displaystyle+\kappa\int_{\frac{2\theta_{*}(l-\xi)-\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}}}^{\frac{-2\theta_{*}\xi-\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}}}(\xi+\frac{\beta_{*}h+\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}})p(h)dh,

(20)

where
p(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}
is the pdf of a standard Gaussain random variable and
Q(x)
is its associated Q-function.
The expectation in (19) can be found in a similar way.

The following Theorem precisely characterizes the support recovery metrics introduced in (
3
).

Theorem 2

(Probability of support recovery)
.

Under the same settings of Theorem
1
and for any fixed
\xi>0
,
and in the limit of

m,n\to+\infty,m/n=\delta
, it holds that:

\textsl{plim}\ \Phi_{\xi,on}(\hat{{\bf x}})=\mathbb{P}\biggl[\biggl|\eta\biggr(\frac{\gamma\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}\tau_{*}}X_{0}+\frac{\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}},l,u\biggl)\biggr|\geq\xi\biggr],

(21)

and

\textsl{plim}\ \Phi_{\xi,\text{off}}(\hat{{\bf x}})=\mathbb{P}\biggl[\biggl|\eta\biggr(\frac{\beta_{*}}{2\theta_{*}}H;\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\theta_{*}},l,u\biggl)\biggr|\leq\xi\biggr].

(22)

The proof of Theorem
2
is also based on the CGMT and largely follows the proof of Theorem
1
but is omitted for space limitations (see
[
19
]
for a similarly detailed treatment).

Remark
1 (Small/Large Regularizers). From Theorem 2, it can be seen that as
\lambda_{1}
becomes large
\Phi_{\xi,\text{off}}(\hat{{\bf x}})
converges to one while
\Phi_{\xi,\text{on}}(\hat{{\bf x}})
converges to zero. Opposite behavior is observed when
\lambda_{1}
takes values close to zero.
This behavior is expected since large values of
\lambda_{1}
parameter put more emphasis on the
\ell_{1}
-norm term, thus promoting sparser solution. This is clearly depicted in Figure 3.

Remark
2 (Optimal Regularizers).
In order to trade-off between on- and off- support recovery
probabilities a reasonable performance metric will be
\Phi_{\xi}=\omega\Phi_{\xi,\text{on}}+(1-\omega)\Phi_{\xi,\text{off}}
for
\omega\in[0,1]
. Theorem 2 precisely characterizes the behavior of this as a function of
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
; thus, it determines the optimal value of
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
that minimizes
\Phi_{\xi}

[
27
]
.

Remark
3.
The parameters
l
and
u
generally do not need to be tuned because the support of the signal is known (take the example of digital communication systems where the constellation is known by both the transmitter and the receiver). Hence, the choice of
l
and
u
is straightforward, i.e.
l
= min
\mathbf{x}_{0}
and

= max
\mathbf{x}_{0}
.

Remark
4. For a sparse Bernoulli vector
{\bf x}_{0}
, the expressions in (
21
), and (
22
) simplify to

\displaystyle\textsl{plim}\ \Phi_{\xi,\text{on}}(\hat{{\bf x}})=\ \left(\frac{2\theta_{*}\xi+\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}}-\frac{\gamma}{\tau_{*}}\right),

(23)

and

\textsl{plim}\ \Phi_{\xi,\text{off}}(\hat{{\bf x}})=1-Q\left(\frac{2\theta_{*}\xi+\lambda_{1}}{\beta_{*}}\right).

(24)

Remark
5. Theorems 1 and 2 can also be used to characterize the performance of the standard EN by letting
l\to-\infty
, and
u\to\infty
, in which the saturated soft-thresholding operator reduces to the conventional soft-thresholding operator.
To the best of our knowledge, the precise analysis of the standard EN under matrix uncertainties has not been studied before as well. However, we focus in this paper on the Box-EN since it generalizes the standard EN and gives better performance as compare to the standard one.

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have used the recently developed Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem (CGMT) framework to derive precise asymptotic characterizations of the mean squared error (MSE) and probability of support recovery of the Box-Elastic Net (Box-EN) under the presence of uncertainties in the measurement matrix. Numerical simulations show the close agreement to the proposed theoretical predictions. Also, we showed that the Box-EN outperforms standard Elastic-Net. These predictions can be used to optimally tune the involved parameters of the algorithm. Finally, we note that following the same steps as in this paper, our results can be used to characterize the performance of the standard EN.

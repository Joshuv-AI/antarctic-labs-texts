Abstract
A graph whose nodes have degree 1 1 or 3 3 is called a { 1 , 3 } \{1,3\} -graph. Liu and Osserman associated a polytope to each { 1 , 3 } \{1,3\} -graph and studied the Ehrhart quasi-polynomials of these polytopes. They showed that the vertices of these polytopes have coordinates in the set { 0 , 1 4 , 1 2 , 1 } \{0,\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2},1\} , which implies that the period of their Ehrhart quasi-polynomials is either 1 , 2 1,2 , or 4 4 . We show that the period of the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of these polytopes is at most 2 if the graph is a tree or a cubic graph, and it is equal to 4 4 otherwise. In the process of proving this theorem, several interesting combinatorial and geometric properties of these polytopes were uncovered, arising from the structure of their associated graphs. The tools developed here may find other applications in the study of Ehrhart quasi-polynomials and enumeration problems for other polytopes that arise from graphs. Additionally, we have identified some interesting connections with triangulations of 3-manifolds.

1.1
Ehrhart quasi-polynomials and period collapse

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and fix
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
We define the
discrete volume
of a polytope
\mathcal{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with respect to the coset
\mathcal{L}+u\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by

\textup{vol}_{\mathcal{L}+u}(\mathcal{P}):=|\mathcal{P}\cap(\mathcal{L}+u)|.

Here we use the standard notation for
the translation of any set
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
by a fixed vector
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, namely

Y+u:=\{y+u:y\in Y\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Ehrhart
[
5
]
introduced the function

L_{\mathcal{P}}(t):=\textup{vol}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(t\mathcal{P}),

which is the number of lattice points in the
dilated polytope

t\mathcal{P}:=\{tx:x\in\mathcal{P}\}
,
for a nonnegative integer dilation
t
.
Ehrhart showed that if
\mathcal{P}
is an integral polytope, then this function is a polynomial in the integer
parameter
t
.
A
quasi-polynomial

f(t)
is a function defined by
a list
p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{\alpha-1}
of polynomials
such that

\displaystyle f(t)=

\displaystyle p_{0}(t)

if
t\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \alpha)
,

\displaystyle f(t)=

\displaystyle p_{1}(t)

if
t\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \alpha)
,

\displaystyle f(t)=

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

⋮

\displaystyle f(t)=

\displaystyle p_{\alpha-1}(t)

if
t\equiv\alpha-1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \alpha)
.

The minimal such
\alpha
is the
period
of
f
and

p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{\alpha-1}
are the
constituents
of
f
.
More generally, Ehrahrt also showed that if
\mathcal{P}
is a rational polytope,
then the function
L_{\mathcal{P}}(t)
is a quasi-polynomial, for integer values of
t
,
whose period divides
the least common multiple of the denominators in the coordinates
of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}

[
2
,
5
]
.

If a rational polytope
\mathcal{P}
has a quasi-polynomial
L_{\mathcal{P}}(t)

whose period is
p
, we also call
p
the period of
\mathcal{P}
. The
denominator of
\mathcal{P}
is the minimal integer
\alpha
such that the
vertices of the dilated polytope
\alpha\mathcal{P}
are integral. For a
‘generic’ rational polytope, one expects its period to be equal to its
denominator. Regarding the complexity of computing the periods,
Woods
[
18
]
has shown that, for fixed dimension
d
, there
is a polynomial-time algorithm which, given a rational polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an integer
n>0
, decides whether
n
is a
multiple of the period of the quasi-polynomial
L_{\mathcal{P}}(t)
.

When we have a rational polytope whose period is smaller than its
denominator, we refer to this situation as
period collapse
.
Usually,
researchers
[
3
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
19
]

prove that the phenomenon of period collapse exists for some particular
rational polytope
\mathcal{P}
by exhibiting a decomposition of
\mathcal{P}

into rational simplices, and then applying a (different) unimodular
affine linear transformation to each simplex.
If, somewhat magically, the reassembling of all of the images of
these simplices form an integral polytope, then the Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
is in
fact a polynomial. It is a well-known open problem whether it is
always possible to carry out this process, for any rational polytope
that possesses period collapse
[
9
]
.

Here we develop a different technique for proving period collapse,
building on an idea of Liu and Osserman. Namely, rather than
decomposing the object into smaller polytopes, and using special types
of unimodular transformations for each of them, we instead decompose
the integer lattice into a certain sublattice
\cal L
, together with
all of its cosets in the integer lattice. Then, for each fixed coset
of
\cal L
, we count the number of points of
\cal L
that belong to

\mathcal{P}
, and show that this number is in bijection with the number of integer
points that belong to another, naturally-defined, integral polytope.
This novel technique seems interesting in itself and might be useful in a wider context.

In this research, we have made extensive use of both of the following software packages: LattE
[
1
]
, and
polymake

[
7
,
8
]
.

1.2
Liu and Osserman’s polytopes

Let
G
be a
\{1,3\}
-graph. We say that a node of degree 1 is a

leaf node
and a node of degree 3 is an
internal node
. We denote by

V(G)
,

, and

G

,
E
, and

A subgraph

if the degree in
H

of
G

In particular, the empty subgraph is internally Eulerian.

If
X

\mathbbm{1}_{S}:X\rightarrow\{0,1\}

S\subseteq X
.
If
S=\{e\}

\mathbbm{1}_{e}

If

We allow all of our graphs to have loops and parallel edges.

10

-graph

For each internal node

,

v

Denote by
S^{\triangle}(v)

,
w_{b}

\displaystyle w_{a}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w_{b}+w_{c}

\displaystyle w_{b}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w_{a}+w_{c}

\displaystyle w_{c}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w_{a}+w_{b}\,.

From
S^{\triangle}(v)
, one can derive that
w_{a}
,
w_{b}
, and
w_{c}
are nonnegative.
We denote by
S^{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(v)
the linear system of inequalities, at each internal node
v

of
G

w_{a}+w_{b}+w_{c}\ \leq\ t\,.

(1)

Now, consider the union of all the linear systems
S^{\mathcal{P}}_{1}(v)
,

G

Add the constraint
0\leq w_{e}\leq 1/2

in
G

.
The set
\mathcal{P}_{G}
consists of all real solutions

10

.
These last two constraints imply that

is a polytope, and we denote its Ehrhart quasi-polynomial by

L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t):=\textup{vol}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(t\mathcal{P}_{G}),

(2)

where

.

If

has connected components

would be the Cartesian product of
\mathcal{P}_{H}

.

In order to derive properties of

for a

-graph

To define

of

,

,

, and

be the previous linear system

parity constraint

\displaystyle w_{a}+w_{b}+w_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,z_{v}

(3)

\displaystyle z_{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t\,.

We now consider the union of all the linear systems
S^{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}(v)
,
taken over all internal nodes
v
of
G
.
We add the constraint
0\leq w_{e}\leq 1

in

The polytope

consists of the solutions

.
For each nonnegative integer

Hightlighting context of our main results

Liu and Osserman

In particular, Liu and Osserman

(a)

Is it true that if

For which

However, we point out that the periods of
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t)
and
L^{\mathcal{Q}}_{G}(t)
are different.
Indeed, from the above,

\displaystyle q_{0}(1)

\displaystyle=N_{G}\,p_{0}(1)-(N_{G}{-}1)(\frac{3}{2})^{k-1}=2^{k}\,p_{0}(1)-(2^{k}-1)(\frac{3}{2})^{k-1}\mbox{\ and \ }

\displaystyle q_{1}(1)

\displaystyle=N_{G}\,p_{1}(1)=N_{G}=2^{k}.

Therefore, if
L^{\mathcal{Q}}_{G}(t)
has period 1, then
q_{0}=q_{1}
,
hence
q_{0}(1)=q_{1}(1)
, which implies
that
p_{0}(1)=1+\frac{2^{k}-1}{2^{k}}(\frac{3}{2})^{k-1}\neq 1=p_{1}(1)
, because
k\geq 2
,
thus
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t)
has period 2.
Similarly, if
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t)
has period 1, then
L^{\mathcal{Q}}_{G}(t)
has period 2.
This tackles Question
3
(
c
).

Finally, we derive that the period of
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t)
is 4 for any
\{1,3\}
-graph
G

that is not a tree or a cubic graph, applying the same strategy used for cubic graphs.
This shows that cubic graphs are the only ones that satisfy
Question
3
(
b
).
Together with Theorem
4
, this result leads to Theorem
5
.

Although our approach allows us to control the behavior for the difference of polynomials,
we were not able, despite many efforts, to find a method to compute the desired polynomials
explictly. This seems a challenging task even for
\{1,3\}
-trees as stated
in
[
6
, Problem 6.5]
.

3
Cosets and combinatorial tools

The intent of this section is to derive a relation between

L^{\mathcal{Q}}_{G}(t)
and
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t)
for (even or odd) nonnegative integer
t
.
For this task, internally Eulerian subgraphs, a class of graphs that resembles
caterpillars, and a local move performed on graphs called nearest neighbor
interchange (NNI) play central roles.
The general idea is partitioning the integer points of the
polytope
t\mathcal{Q}
into parts of size having
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{G}(t)
as our unit of
standard measurement.

3.1
Eulerianicity

Let
G
be a
\{1,3\}
-graph and
\mathcal{L}_{G}:=(2\mathbb{Z}^{E})\times\mathbb{Z}^{I}

For each integer

In words, we are now counting integer points in cosets of a lattice, not

The term “

We note that

where the union and the summation are over all internally Eulerian subgraphs
H
of

.
Indeed, because of the parity constraint (

for every integer point

in

the set of edges

is odd induces an internally Eulerian subgraph of

.

When the central path is empty, both nodes are roots.

(h,k)
-caterpillar obtained from

2(h+k-1)
.

We denoted by

\displaystyle=N_{G_{h,k}}\,\Big(L^{\mathcal{P}}_{h,k}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor{h/2}\rfloor}(-4)^{-j}\,{h-j\choose j}\frac{h}{h-j}\,L^{\mathcal{P}}_{h{-}j,k}(t)\Big).

(27)

Using (
26
) with
t=0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
, and substituting
N_{G_{h,k}}=2^{h+k-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{q^{h,k}_{0}(t)}{N_{G_{h,k}}}

\displaystyle=p_{0}^{h,k}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor{h/2}\rfloor}(-4)^{-j}\,{h-j\choose j}\frac{h}{h-j}\,p_{0}^{h{-}j,k}(t)-\frac{1}{2^{h-1}}(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}})\,\big(\frac{t}{2}+1\big)^{k-1}.

(28)

Because both sides of Equality (
28
) are polynomials in
t
,
it holds for every
t
. Similarly, from Equality (
27
), we deduce that, for every
t
,

\displaystyle\frac{q^{h,k}_{0}(t)}{N_{G_{h,k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}^{h,k}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor{h/2}\rfloor}(-4)^{-j}\,{h-j\choose j}\frac{h}{h-j}\,p_{2}^{h{-}j,k}(t).

(29)

By subtracting (
28
) from (
29
) and rearranging the terms, we deduce that

\displaystyle(p^{h,k}_{0}-p^{h,k}_{2})(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor{h/2}\rfloor}(-4)^{-j}\,{h-j\choose j}

\displaystyle\frac{h}{h-j}\,(p^{h-j,k}_{0}-p^{h-j,k}_{2})(t)

\displaystyle\ =\ \frac{1}{2^{h-1}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)\,\left(\frac{t}{2}+1\right)^{k-1}.

(30)

We define

d(\alpha)\ :=\ \frac{(-4)^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\,\frac{(p^{\alpha,k}_{0}-p^{\alpha,k}_{2})(t)}{(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}})\,(\frac{t}{2}+1)^{k-1}},

(31)

so that we may divided (
4.2
) by
\frac{h}{(-4)^{h}}(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}})(\frac{t}{2}+1)^{k-1}

and obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}{h-j\choose j}\,d(h-j)\ =\ \frac{(-4)^{h}}{h\,2^{h-1}}\ =\ \frac{(-1)^{h}\,2^{h+1}}{h}.

(32)

The
j=\lfloor{h/2}\rfloor+1,\ldots,h-1
terms are all equal to zero.

From one of the so called simpler Chebyshev inverse relations
[
15
, Table 2.3, item 5]
, we derive that

\displaystyle d(h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\left[{h+j-1\choose j}-{h+j-1\choose j-1}\right]\frac{(-1)^{j}{(-1)^{h-j}2^{h-j+1}}}{h-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\frac{h-j}{h+j}{h+j\choose j}\frac{(-1)^{h}2^{h-j+1}}{h-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{h}\,\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\frac{2^{h-j+1}}{h+j}\,{h+j\choose j}.

Thus, we have that
d(h)>0
for every positive even
h
and
d(h)<0
for every odd
h
.
Finally, substituting in (
31
) with
\alpha=h
, we obtain that

p^{h,k}_{0}(t)-p^{h,k}_{2}(t)\ =\ \frac{h}{4^{h}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}\right)\,\left(\frac{t}{2}+1\right)^{k-1}\,\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\frac{2^{h-j+1}}{h+j}\,{h+j\choose j}>0

and conclude that
p^{h,k}_{0}(t)>p^{h,k}_{2}(t)
for every
t\geq 0
.
Consequently, the period of
L^{\mathcal{P}}_{h,k}
is 4.
∎

5.3
Bijections by shifting

Here we derive a purely set-theoretic tool, Lemma

,
that gives a partial bijection
by shifting
.
We will employ this tool repeatedly in the
remaining proofs of this section, and in those of the next section.

We begin with some notation that is followed by the lemma.
For an

in

and a set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Out}_{e}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{w\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}:w_{e}\mbox{ is even and }w+\mathbbm{1}_{e}\not\in\mathcal{X}\}\mbox{\ and}

\displaystyle\mathrm{In}_{e}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{w\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}:w_{e}\mbox{ is odd and }w-\mathbbm{1}_{e}\not\in\mathcal{X}\}.

We think of the function

as a shifting of the integer points with

-coordinate to the integer points with odd
e
-coordinate.

\mathrm{Out}_{e}(\mathcal{X})
is the set of integer points of
\mathcal{X}
with even
e
-coordinate
that are moved outside
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathrm{In}_{e}(\mathcal{X})

(Figure

(the bijection by shifting)
.

If

is bounded and

, then

We have that

\mathrm{Out}_{e}(\mathcal{X})\subseteq\{w\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}:w_{e}\mbox{ is even}\}\mbox{ and }\mathrm{In}_{e}(\mathcal{X})\subseteq\{w\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}:w_{e}\mbox{ is odd}\}.

\displaystyle\textup{vol}_{{e}\mathrm{\,even}}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{w\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}:w_{e}\mbox{ is even}\}\setminus\mathrm{Out}_{e}(\mathcal{X})|+|\mathrm{Out}_{e}(\mathcal{X})|,\mbox{ and}

(37)

\displaystyle\,\textup{vol}_{{e}\mathrm{\,odd}}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{w\in\mathcal{X}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{E}:w_{e}\mbox{ is odd}\}\setminus\mathrm{In}_{e}(\mathcal{X})|+|\mathrm{In}_{e}(\mathcal{X})|.

(38)

The function

is bounded,
\textup{vol}_{{e}\mathrm{\,even}}(\mathcal{X})
and
\textup{vol}_{{e}\mathrm{\,odd}}(\mathcal{X})

are finite and the result follows from (
37
) and (
38
).

Theorem 37

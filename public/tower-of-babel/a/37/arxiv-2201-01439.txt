Construction of extremal Type II ℤ 2 ​ k \mathbb{Z}_{2k} -codes
Masaaki Harada

Abstract
We give methods for constructing many self-dual ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} -codes and Type II ℤ 2 ​ k \mathbb{Z}_{2k} -codes of length 2 ​ n 2n starting from a given self-dual ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} -code and Type II ℤ 2 ​ k \mathbb{Z}_{2k} -code of length 2 ​ n 2n , respectively. As an application, we construct extremal Type II ℤ 2 ​ k \mathbb{Z}_{2k} -codes of length 24 24 for k = 4 , 5 , … , 20 k=4,5,\ldots,20 and extremal Type II ℤ 2 ​ k \mathbb{Z}_{2k} -codes of length 32 32 for k = 4 , 5 , … , 10 k=4,5,\ldots,10 . We also construct new extremal Type II ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} -codes of lengths 56 56 and 64 64 .

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the definitions and basic facts
on self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-codes and Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes
used throughout this paper.

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
be the ring of integers modulo
m
, where
m
is a positive
integer with
m\geq 2
.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code
C
of length
n

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
.
An element of
C
is called a
codeword
.
A
generator matrix
of
C
is a matrix whose rows generate
C
.
We define the inner product of

x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
by

\langle x,y\rangle=x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}.

The
dual code

C^{\perp}
of
C
is defined as

C^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}\mid\langle x,y\rangle=0\text{ for all }y\in C\}.

A code
C
is
self-dual
if
C=C^{\perp}
.
Two
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-codes
C
and
C^{\prime}
are
equivalent
,
denoted
C\cong C^{\prime}
,
if one can be obtained from the
other by permuting the coordinates and (if necessary)
changing the signs of certain coordinates.

We now consider self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes.
In this paper, we take the set
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
to be either

\{0,1,\ldots,2k-1\}
or

\{0,\pm 1,\ldots,\pm(k-1),k\}
, using whichever form is more convenient.
We define a map
\rho
from
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
to
\mathbb{Z}
as follows

\rho(i)=\begin{cases}0,\ldots,k&\text{ if }i=0,\ldots,k,\text{ respectively,}\\
1-k,\ldots,-1&\text{ if }i=k+1,\ldots,2k-1,\text{ respectively.}\end{cases}

(2)

The
Euclidean weight

\wt_{E}(v)
of a vector
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2k}^{n}

is

\sum_{i=1}^{k}i^{2}n_{i}(v)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k-1}(2k-i)^{2}n_{i}(v),

where

n_{i}(v)=|\{j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\mid\rho(v_{j})\equiv i\pmod{2k}\}|\ (i=0,1,\ldots,2k-1).

A binary doubly even self-dual code is often called Type II.
Using Euclidean weights,
the notion of binary Type II codes has been generalized as follows.
Self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes with the property that all
Euclidean weights are multiples of
4k
are called

Type II

\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes
(see
[
2
]
and
[
22
]
for
k=2
and
[
1
]
for
k\geq 3
).
It was shown in
[
1
]
that there is a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code of
length
n
if and only if
n
is divisible by eight.

Throughout this paper,

I_{n}
denotes the identity matrix of order
n
, and

A^{T}
denotes the transpose of a matrix
A
.
The following gives a criteria for self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes
and Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

Let
C
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code of length
2n
having
generator matrix of form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\end{array}\right)
,
where
A
is an
n\times n
matrix.
If
AA^{T}=-I_{n}
, then
C
is self-dual.

(ii)

Let
C
be a self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code having
generator matrix
G
.
If each row of
G
has Euclidean weight divisible by
4k
, then

C
is Type II.

Proof.

(i) is trivial.
(ii) follows from
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

The
minimum Euclidean weight

d_{E}(C)
of a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code
C

is the smallest Euclidean
weight among all nonzero codewords of
C
.
For Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes of length
n
,
the upper bound (
1
) on the minimum Euclidean weight
d_{E}(C)

holds if
k=1

[
24
]
, if
k=2

[
2
]
and
[
22
]

and if

k\in\{3,4,5,6\}

[
21
]
.
Also, the bound (
1
) holds under the
assumption that
\lfloor n/24\rfloor\leq k-2
for arbitrary
k

[
1
]
.
When the bound (
1
) holds,
we say that a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code meeting (
1
)
with equality is
extremal
.

The
binary part

C^{(2)}
of a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code
C

of length
n
is defined as

\{(\rho(c_{1})\!\!\!\pmod{2},\rho(c_{2})\!\!\!\pmod{2},\ldots,\rho(c_{n})\!\!\!\pmod{2})\mid(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\in C\},

where
\rho
is the map given in (
2
).
If a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code
has generator matrix of form

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\end{array}\right)
, then the binary part
is a binary doubly even self-dual code
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
.
The following is trivial.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
C
and
C^{\prime}
be Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes.
If
C\cong C^{\prime}
, then

C^{(2)}\cong{C^{\prime}}^{(2)}
.

An
n\times n

negacirculant
matrix has the following form

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}r_{0}&r_{1}&r_{2}&\cdots&r_{n-1}\\
-r_{n-1}&r_{0}&r_{1}&\cdots&r_{n-2}\\
-r_{n-2}&-r_{n-1}&r_{0}&\cdots&r_{n-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
-r_{1}&-r_{2}&-r_{3}&\cdots&r_{0}\end{array}\right).

Let
C
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code of length
4n

having the following generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc@{}c}&{\Large I_{2n}}&&\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
-B^{T}&A^{T}\end{array}\end{array}\right),

(3)

where
A
and

are

negacirculant matrices.
Such a code is called a

code
[
18
]
.
By Lemma
2.1
(i),
if
AA^{T}+BB^{T}=-I_{n}
, then

is self-dual.
Let
C
be a four-negacirculant self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code with
generator matrix of form (

).
Let
r_{A}
and
r_{B}
denote the first rows of
A
and
B
, respectively.
By Lemma
2.1
(ii),
if
\wt_{E}(r_{A})+\wt_{E}(r_{B})\equiv-1\pmod{4k}
, then

is Type II.

We end this section by describing unimodular lattices and Construction A.
A (Euclidean) integral lattice
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

in dimension
n

is
unimodular
if
L=L^{*}
, where

L^{*}
is the dual lattice under the standard inner product
(x,y)
.
A unimodular lattice
L
is
even

if the norm
(x,x)
of every vector
x
of
L
is even,
and
odd
otherwise.
There is an even unimodular lattice in dimension
n

if and only if
n
is divisible by eight.
The
minimum norm

\min(L)
of
a unimodular lattice
L
is the smallest
norm among all nonzero vectors of
L
.
The
kissing number
of
L
is the number of vectors of minimum norm in
L
.
We now give a method to construct
even unimodular lattices from Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes, which
is called Construction A.
For a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code
C
of length
n
,
define the following lattice

A_{2k}(C)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}\{\rho(C)+2k\mathbb{Z}^{n}\},

(4)

where

\rho(C)=\{(\rho(c_{1}),\rho(c_{2}),\ldots,\rho(c_{n}))\ |\ (c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\in C\}.

It is known that
A_{2k}(C)
is an even unimodular lattice
with minimum norm
\min\{d_{E}(C)/2k,2k\}
,
where
d_{E}(C)
denotes the minimum Euclidean weight of
C

[
1
]
.
The minimum norms of
A_{2k}(C)
are used to determine the
minimum Euclidean weights of
C
in Sections
4
,

5
and
6
.

Methods for constructing self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-codes

In this section, starting from a given self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code
(resp. Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code)
with generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\end{array}\right)
, by transforming
A
,
we give a new method for constructing many
self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-codes
(resp. Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C
be a self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code of length
2n

having generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\end{array}\right)
.
Let
r_{i}
be the
i
-th row of
A
.
Let
x
and
y
be vectors of
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{n}
.
Define an
n\times n

matrix
A(x,y)
, where the
i
-th row
r^{\prime}_{i}
is given by

r^{\prime}_{i}=r_{i}+\langle r_{i},y\rangle x-\langle r_{i},x\rangle y.

Let
C(A,x,y)
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code having the following generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A(x,y)\end{array}\right).

If

\langle x,x\rangle=\langle y,y\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle=0
,
then
C(A,x,y)
is a self-dual
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code of length
2n
.

Proof.

Since

\langle x,x\rangle=\langle y,y\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle=0
,
we have

\begin{split}\langle r^{\prime}_{i},r^{\prime}_{j}\rangle=&\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle+\langle r_{j},y\rangle\langle r_{i},x\rangle-\langle r_{j},x\rangle\langle r_{i},y\rangle\\
&+\langle r_{i},y\rangle\langle x,r_{j}\rangle+\langle r_{i},y\rangle\langle r_{j},y\rangle\langle x,x\rangle-\langle r_{i},y\rangle\langle r_{j},x\rangle\langle x,y\rangle\\
&-\langle r_{i},x\rangle\langle y,r_{j}\rangle-\langle r_{i},x\rangle\langle r_{j},y\rangle\langle y,x\rangle+\langle r_{i},x\rangle\langle r_{j},x\rangle\langle y,y\rangle\\
=&\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle+\langle r_{j},y\rangle\langle r_{i},x\rangle-\langle r_{j},x\rangle\langle r_{i},y\rangle+\langle r_{i},y\rangle\langle x,r_{j}\rangle\\
&-\langle r_{i},x\rangle\langle y,r_{j}\rangle\\
=&\langle r_{i},r_{j}\rangle.\end{split}

(5)

This implies that
AA^{T}=A(x,y)A(x,y)^{T}
.
By Lemma
2.1
(i),
C(A,x,y)
is self-dual.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We modify the above theorem as follows.
Let
C
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-code of length

having generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\end{array}\right)

We consider a method for constructing Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-codes.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
C
is a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code of length
2n
having
generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A\end{array}\right)
.
Suppose that

x
and
y
are vectors of
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}^{n}
satisfying the condition

\wt_{E}(x)\equiv\wt_{E}(y)\equiv 0\pmod{4k}
and

\langle x,y\rangle=0
.
Then the code
C(A,x,y)
having generator matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&A(x,y)\end{array}\right)

defined in Theorem
3.1

is a Type II
\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
-code of length
2n
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be the map given in (
2
).
For a vector
v=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2k}
,
define the positive integer

v*v=\sum_{i=1}^{2k-1}i^{2}n_{i}(v)
, where

n_{i}(v)=|\{j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\mid\rho(v_{j})\equiv i\pmod{2k}\}|

(i=0,1,\ldots,2k-1)
.
By
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
,

\wt_{E}(v)\equiv v*v\pmod{4k}
.
This implies that
\langle x,x\rangle=\langle y,y\rangle=0
.
Thus, by Theorem
3.1
,
C(A,x,y)
is self-dual.
Let
r_{i}
and
r^{\prime}_{i}
denote the
i
-th row of
A
and
A(x,y)
, respectively.
By Lemma
2.1
(ii),
it is sufficient to show that
\wt_{E}(r^{\prime}_{i})\equiv 4k-1\pmod{4k}

(i=1,2,\ldots,n)
.
From (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\wt_{E}(r^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv r^{\prime}_{i}*r^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv r_{i}*r_{i}+x*x+y*y

\displaystyle\equiv\wt_{E}(r_{i})+\wt_{E}(x)+\wt_{E}(y)

\displaystyle\equiv\wt_{E}(r_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv 4k-1\pmod{4k}.

The result follows.
∎

Abstract
We interprete results of Markman on monodromy operators as a universality statement for descendent integrals over moduli spaces of stable sheaves on K ​ 3 K3 surfaces. This yields effective methods to reduce these descendent integrals to integrals over the punctual Hilbert scheme of the K ​ 3 K3 surface. As an application we establish the higher rank Segre–Verlinde correspondence for K ​ 3 K3 surfaces as conjectured by Göttsche and Kool.

1.3
Segre/Verlinde correspondence

Göttsche and Kool conjectured that the Segre numbers of moduli spaces of stable sheaves on surfaces

of these moduli spaces.

and we refer to

The Verlinde numbers of the Hilbert schemes of points of

]

3.3
Dependence

By Theorem
2.9
we conclude that any integral

\int_{M}P(\alpha_{M,k},\mu(L),\mu(u{\mathsf{p}}))

(3.2)

(such as the Segre number) only depends upon
P
and
the intersection pairings in the Mukai lattice of the classes

v,\quad{\mathsf{p}}/\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v),\quad v(\alpha)^{\vee}\exp\left(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\right),\quad L\exp\left(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\right),\quad u{\mathsf{p}}.

(3.3)

Explicitly, the interesting pairings for the first three classes are

(i)

\displaystyle v\cdot v(\alpha)^{\vee}\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)=-v_{2}(\alpha)\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}(v\cdot v),

(ii)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{p}}/\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)\cdot v(\alpha)^{\vee}\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)=-\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)},

(iii)

\displaystyle\bigg(v(\alpha)^{\vee}\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)\bigg)^{2}=v(\alpha)\cdot v(\alpha).

The interesting intersections involving
L
are

(iv)

\displaystyle v\cdot L\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)=L\cdot c_{1}(v)-L\cdot c_{1}(v)=0,

\displaystyle v(\alpha)^{\vee}\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)\cdot L\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)=v(\alpha)^{\vee}\cdot L=-c_{1}(\alpha)\cdot L,

\displaystyle\bigg(L\exp\bigg(\frac{c_{1}(v)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}\bigg)\bigg)^{2}=L^{2}.

The pairings with
u{\mathsf{p}}
are

u\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)
times the pairings with
{\mathsf{p}}/\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)
.

3.4
Moving to the Hilbert scheme

Since (
3.2
) only depends on the intersection pairings of (
3.3
) we have that

\int_{M}P(\alpha_{M,k},\mu(L),\mu(u{\mathsf{p}}))=\int_{S^{[n]}}P(\beta_{S^{[n]},k},\mu(L),\mu(u^{\prime}{\mathsf{p}}))

for any
K
-theory class
\beta\in K(S)

and
u^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that the list

1-(n-1){\mathsf{p}},\qquad{\mathsf{p}},\qquad v(\beta)^{\vee},\qquad L,\qquad u^{\prime}{\mathsf{p}}

(3.4)

has the same intersection numbers as the list (

).
(The list (
3.4
) is obtained from (

) by specializing to

The interesting parts of the intersections of (

) are

(i)

\displaystyle v\cdot v(\beta)^{\vee}=-v_{2}(\beta)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\beta)(2n-2),

(ii)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{p}}\cdot v(\beta)^{\vee}=-\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\beta),

(iii)

\displaystyle v(\beta)^{\vee}\cdot v(\beta)^{\vee}=v(\beta)\cdot v(\beta),

(iv)

\displaystyle v(\beta)^{\vee}\cdot L=-c_{1}(\beta)\cdot L.

Equating (i)–(iv) for
M
and
S^{[n]}
we hence get the system

\displaystyle-v_{2}(\alpha)\cdot\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}(v\cdot v)=-v_{2}(\beta)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\beta)(2n-2),

\displaystyle-\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)}=-\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\beta),\qquad v(\alpha)\cdot v(\alpha)=v(\beta)\cdot v(\beta),\qquad-c_{1}(\alpha)\cdot L=-c_{1}(\beta)\cdot L.

Since
v(\alpha)^{2}=c_{1}(\alpha)^{2}-2\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\alpha)v_{2}(\alpha)
,
this is equivalent to the system:

\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\beta)=\frac{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)},\quad\ v_{2}(\beta)=\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)v_{2}(\alpha),\quad\ c_{1}(\alpha)^{2}=c_{1}(\beta)^{2},\quad\ c_{1}(\alpha)\cdot L=c_{1}(\beta)\cdot L.

(3.5)

Moreover, we must have

-u^{\prime}=u^{\prime}{\mathsf{p}}\cdot(1-(n-1){\mathsf{p}})=u{\mathsf{p}}\cdot v=-\operatorname{\mathsf{rk}}(v)u.

Theorem
1.2

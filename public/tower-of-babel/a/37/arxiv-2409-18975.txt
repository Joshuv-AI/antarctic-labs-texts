Unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions
Gamaliel Morales

Abstract
. In this study, we introduce a new class of quaternions associated with the well-known modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers. There are many studies about the quaternions with special integer sequences and their generalizations. All of these studies used consecutive elements of the considered sequences. Here, we extend the usual definitions into a wider structure by using arbitrary modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers. Moreover, we present unrestricted complex modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers. In addition, we give some properties of this type of quaternions and complex modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers, including generating function and Binet-like formula.

Primary 11B37, 16H05; Secondary 11R52.

1.
Introduction

In 1843, the Irish mathematician Sir W.R. Hamilton introduced quaternions which are members of a non-commutative division algebra as a kind of extension of complex numbers to higher spatial dimensions. Let
\mathbb{R}^{r,s}
be the vector space with quadratic forms and
Cl_{r,s}(\mathbb{R})
be the Clifford algebra on
{\mathbb{R}}^{r,s}
. This means that a standard basis
\{e_{i}\}
has
r
vectors square to
1
and
s
vectors square to
-1
. Quaternions can be constructed as the even subalgebra of the
Cl_{0,3}(\mathbb{R})
. For more details, the monograph
[
4
]
can be investigated.

A quaternion
q
is formally defined as follows:

q=r_{0}+r_{1}\textbf{i}+r_{2}\textbf{j}+r_{3}\textbf{k},

where
r_{j}
(
j=0,1,2,3
) are any real numbers, and the standard basis
\{1,\textbf{i},\textbf{j},\textbf{k}\}
satisfies

\textbf{i}^{2}=\textbf{j}^{2}=\textbf{k}^{2}=-1,\ \textbf{i}\textbf{j}=-\textbf{j}\textbf{i}=\textbf{k},\ \textbf{j}\textbf{k}=-\textbf{k}\textbf{j}=\textbf{i},\ \textbf{k}\textbf{i}=-\textbf{i}\textbf{k}=\textbf{j}.

The conjugate of
q
is
q^{*}=r_{0}-r_{1}\textbf{i}-r_{2}\textbf{j}-r_{3}\textbf{k}
, and its norm is

Nr(q)=qq^{*}=r_{0}^{2}+r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+r_{3}^{2}.

(1.1)

In
[
3
]
, Horadam used another perspective to the above-mentioned subject by defining the Fibonacci and Lucas quaternions in the forms

QF_{n}=F_{n}+F_{n+1}\textbf{i}+F_{n+2}\textbf{j}+F_{n+3}\textbf{k}

and

QL_{n}=L_{n}+L_{n+1}\textbf{i}+L_{n+2}\textbf{j}+L_{n+3}\textbf{k},

respectively, where
F_{n}
is the famous
n
-th Fibonacci number and
L_{n}
is the
n
-th Lucas number. Szynal-Liana and Wloch
[
8
]
defined Jacobsthal and Jacobsthal-Lucas quaternions as

JQ_{n}=J_{n}+J_{n+1}\textbf{i}+J_{n+2}\textbf{j}+J_{n+3}\textbf{k}

and

JLQ_{n}=j_{n}+j_{n+1}\textbf{i}+j_{n+2}\textbf{j}+j_{n+3}\textbf{k},

respectively, where
J_{n}
and
j_{n}
are
n
-th Jacobsthal and Jacobsthal-Lucas numbers, respectively. Dasdemir and Bilgici
[
2
]
investigated Binet’s formulas and generating functions for Unrestricted Fibonacci and Lucas quaternions.There are many papers devoted to the quaternions whose coefficients are some special integer sequences, which are not mentioned here.

Based on these studies, we define a new class of quaternions and Gaussian modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers associated with the modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers. In addition, we give a Binet-like formula, generating functions, and certain identities such as Cassini’s identity for these quaternions.

Before giving the main results, let us introduce these numbers briefly. Modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers are of the form

K_{n}^{(3)}=2^{n}+\omega_{1}^{n}+\omega_{2}^{n}=2^{n}+M_{n},

(1.2)

where

M_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}2&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 0\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\\
-1&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 1,2\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

and
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}^{2}=\frac{-1+i\sqrt{3}}{2}
(
i^{2}=-1
).

In fact, this sequence appears when we study the third-order Jacobsthal numbers with indices from an arithmetic progression, for example,

J_{a(n+3)+r}^{(3)}=\left(2^{a}+\omega_{1}^{a}+\omega_{2}^{a}\right)J_{a(n+2)+r}^{(3)}-\left(2^{a}(\omega_{1}^{a}+\omega_{2}^{a})+1\right)J_{a(n+1)+r}^{(3)}+2^{a}J_{an+r}^{(3)},

for fixed integers
a
,
r
with
0\leq r<a
. Note that
K_{n}^{(3)}=J_{n}^{(3)}+2J_{n-1}^{(3)}+6J_{n-2}^{(3)}
, where
J_{n}^{(3)}
is the
n
-th third-order Jacobsthal number (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
).

In number theory, recall that a modified third-order Jacobsthal number of order
n
, is a number of
the form
2^{n}+\omega_{1}^{n}+\omega_{2}^{n}
, where
n
is a nonnegative number. This identity is called as the Binet formula for modified third-order Jacobsthal sequence and it comes from the fact that the modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers can also be defined recursively by

K_{n+3}^{(3)}=K_{n}^{(3)}+7\cdot 2^{n},

(1.4)

with initial conditions
K_{0}^{(3)}=3
,
K_{1}^{(3)}=1
and
K_{2}^{(3)}=3
. Since this recurrence, substituting
n
by
n+1
and
n+2
, we obtain the new form

K_{n+4}^{(3)}=K_{n+1}^{(3)}+7\cdot 2^{n+1}

(1.5)

and

K_{n+5}^{(3)}=K_{n+2}^{(3)}+7\cdot 2^{n+2}.

(1.6)

Subtracting (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) to (
1.6
), we have that
K_{n+5}^{(3)}-K_{n+4}^{(3)}-K_{n+3}^{(3)}=7\cdot 2^{n}+K_{n+2}^{(3)}-K_{n+1}^{(3)}-K_{n}^{(3)}
and then

K_{n+5}^{(3)}=K_{n+4}^{(3)}+K_{n+3}^{(3)}+2K_{n+2}^{(3)},

(1.7)

other form for the recurrence relation of modified third-order Jacobsthal sequence. See
[
5
]
for more details and properties of this type of numbers.

2.
Main results

In this section, our definitions, some concepts, and the fundamental results are presented. First of all, we give the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

For an integer
n\geq 0
, the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternion
\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}
is defined by

\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}=K_{n}^{(3)}+K_{n+a}^{(3)}\textbf{i}+K_{n+b}^{(3)}\textbf{j}+K_{n+c}^{(3)}\textbf{k},

where
a
,
b
and
c
are any integers.

According to our definition, we have the following special cases:

•

For
a=b=c=-n
, the usual modified third-order numbers are obtained
\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(-n,-n,-n)}=K_{n}^{(3)}
.

•

For
b=c=-n
, the unrestricted complex modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers are obtained
\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,-n,-n)}=K_{n}^{(3)}+K_{n+a}^{(3)}\textbf{i}
.

•

For
a=1
and
b=c=-n
, the complex modified third-order Jacobsthal numbers are obtained
\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(1,-n,-n)}=K_{n}^{(3)}+K_{n+1}^{(3)}\textbf{i}
.

•

For
a=1
,
b=2
and
c=3
, the modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions in the usual form of quaternions are obtained:

\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(1,2,3)}=K_{n}^{(3)}+K_{n+1}^{(3)}\textbf{i}+K_{n+2}^{(3)}\textbf{j}+K_{n+3}^{(3)}\textbf{k}.

In
[
3
]
, Horadam introduced the complex Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. These were named Gaussian Fibonacci and Lucas numbers by Berzsenyi
[
1
]
and they have been generally referred to this name since that day. So we prefer to use it for the second and third sequences. It should be noted that all the results obtained for the generalized modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions can be reduced to these numbers. To reduce the volume of the current paper, we only take the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions into account.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any non-negative integer
n
, we have

\mathcal{QK}_{n+3}^{(a,b,c)}=\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}+7\cdot 2^{n}\Theta

(2.1)

and

\mathcal{QK}_{n+3}^{(a,b,c)}=\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}+\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}+2\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)},

(2.2)

where
\Theta=1+2^{a}\textbf{i}+2^{b}\textbf{j}+2^{c}\textbf{k}
.

Proof.

Considering the Definition
2.1
and Eq. (
1.4
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=K_{n}^{(3)}+K_{n+a}^{(3)}\textbf{i}+K_{n+b}^{(3)}\textbf{j}+K_{n+c}^{(3)}\textbf{k}

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(K_{n+3}^{(3)}-7\cdot 2^{n}\right)+\left(K_{n+a+3}^{(3)}-7\cdot 2^{n+a}\right)\textbf{i}+\left(K_{n+b+3}^{(3)}-7\cdot 2^{n+b}\right)\textbf{j}

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(K_{n+c+3}^{(3)}-7\cdot 2^{n+c}\right)\textbf{k}

\displaystyle\ \ =\mathcal{QK}_{n+3}^{(a,b,c)}-7\cdot 2^{n}\Theta.

The last equation gives Eq. (
2.1
). Using the Definition
2.1
and Eq. (
1.7
), Eq. (
2.2
) can be proved similarly.
∎

A Binet-like formula for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(Binet-like formula)
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, the
n
-th unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternion is

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\Theta+\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=M_{n}+M_{n+a}\textbf{i}+M_{n+b}\textbf{j}+M_{n+c}\textbf{k}=\omega_{1}^{n}\Phi_{1}+\omega_{2}^{n}\Phi_{2},

where
\Phi_{1}=1+\omega_{1}^{a}\textbf{i}+\omega_{1}^{b}\textbf{j}+\omega_{1}^{c}\textbf{k}
and
\Phi_{2}=1+\omega_{2}^{a}\textbf{i}+\omega_{2}^{b}\textbf{j}+\omega_{2}^{c}\textbf{k}
.

Proof.

Using Eqs. (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and Definition
2.1
, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=K_{n}^{(3)}+K_{n+a}^{(3)}\textbf{i}+K_{n+b}^{(3)}\textbf{j}+K_{n+c}^{(3)}\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{n}+M_{n}\right)+\left(2^{n+a}+M_{n+a}\right)\textbf{i}

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(2^{n+b}+M_{n+b}\right)\textbf{j}+\left(2^{n+c}+M_{n+c}\right)\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\left(1+2^{a}\textbf{i}+2^{b}\textbf{j}+2^{c}\textbf{k}\right)+\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\Theta+\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}=\omega_{1}^{n}\left(1+\omega_{1}^{a}\textbf{i}+\omega_{1}^{b}\textbf{j}+\omega_{1}^{c}\textbf{k}\right)+\omega_{2}^{n}\left(1+\omega_{2}^{a}\textbf{i}+\omega_{2}^{b}\textbf{j}+\omega_{2}^{c}\textbf{k}\right).

The last equation gives the theorem.
∎

The following lemma gives the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions with negative indices.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{-n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\Theta+M_{n}\left(1-X_{a-1}\textbf{i}-X_{b-1}\textbf{j}-X_{c-1}\textbf{k}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +M_{n-1}\left(X_{a}\textbf{i}+X_{b}\textbf{j}+X_{c}\textbf{k}\right),

where
\Theta
is as Theorem
2.1
and
X_{n}=\frac{\omega_{1}^{n}-\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}
.

Proof.

From Eqs. (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{-n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=K_{-n}^{(3)}+K_{-n+a}^{(3)}\textbf{i}+K_{-n+b}^{(3)}\textbf{j}+K_{-n+c}^{(3)}\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{-n}+M_{-n}\right)+\left(2^{-n+a}+M_{-(n-a)}\right)\textbf{i}

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(2^{-n+b}+M_{-(n-b)}\right)\textbf{j}+\left(2^{-n+c}+M_{-(n-c)}\right)\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\left(1+2^{a}\textbf{i}+2^{b}\textbf{j}+2^{c}\textbf{k}\right)+\mathcal{QM}_{-n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\Theta+\mathcal{QM}_{-n}^{(a,b,c)}.

Using the identities
M_{-n}=M_{n}
and
M_{n+m}=X_{m+1}M_{n}-X_{m}M_{n-1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{QM}_{-n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=M_{-n}+M_{-(n-a)}\textbf{i}+M_{-(n-b)}\textbf{j}+M_{-(n-c)}\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=M_{n}+M_{n-a}\textbf{i}+M_{n-b}\textbf{j}+M_{n-c}\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=M_{n}\left(1-X_{a-1}\textbf{i}-X_{b-1}\textbf{j}-X_{c-1}\textbf{k}\right)+M_{n-1}\left(X_{a}\textbf{i}+X_{b}\textbf{j}+X_{c}\textbf{k}\right),

where
X_{n}
is the sequence defined by
X_{n}=\frac{\omega_{1}^{n}-\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}
. The result follows from the last equation.
∎

The following theorem gives the norm of a unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternion.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any integer
n
, we have

Nr\left(\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}2^{2n}Nr\left(\Theta\right)+2^{n+1}M_{n}\left(1+2^{a}X_{a+1}+2^{b}X_{b+1}+2^{c}X_{c+1}\right)\\
-2^{n+1}M_{n-1}\left(1+2^{a}X_{a}+2^{b}X_{b}+2^{c}X_{c}\right)\\
+M_{2n}+M_{2(n+a)}+M_{2(n+b)}+M_{2(n+c)}+8\end{array}\right\},

where
X_{n}=\frac{\omega_{1}^{n}-\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}
.

Proof.

From Eqs. (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle Nr\left(\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(K_{n}^{(3)}\right)^{2}+\left(K_{n+a}^{(3)}\right)^{2}+\left(K_{n+b}^{(3)}\right)^{2}+\left(K_{n+c}^{(3)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{n}+M_{n}\right)^{2}+\left(2^{n+a}+M_{n+a}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(2^{n+b}+M_{n+b}\right)^{2}+\left(2^{n+c}+M_{n+c}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2^{2n}\left(1+2^{2a}+2^{2b}+2^{2c}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +2^{n+1}\left(M_{n}+2^{a}M_{n+a}+2^{b}M_{n+b}+2^{c}M_{n+c}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +M_{n}^{2}+M_{n+a}^{2}+M_{n+b}^{2}+M_{n+c}^{2}.

Using the identities
M_{n}^{2}=M_{2n}+2
and
M_{n+m}=X_{m+1}M_{n}-X_{m}M_{n-1}
, we have

\displaystyle Nr\left(\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{2n}Nr\left(\Theta\right)+2^{n+1}M_{n}\left(1+2^{a}X_{a+1}+2^{b}X_{b+1}+2^{c}X_{c+1}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ -2^{n+1}M_{n-1}\left(1+2^{a}X_{a}+2^{b}X_{b}+2^{c}X_{c}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +M_{2n}+M_{2(n+a)}+M_{2(n+b)}+M_{2(n+c)}+8,

where
X_{n}=\frac{\omega_{1}^{n}-\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}
and this equation gives the theorem.
∎

Now we give some summation formulas for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions.

Theorem 2.6
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mathcal{QK}_{j}^{(a,b,c)}=\frac{1}{3}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}+2\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\\
-3\Theta+(1-\omega_{2})\Phi_{1}+(1-\omega_{1})\Phi_{2}\end{array}\right\}.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.3
and applying Eqs. (
1.2
) for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mathcal{QK}_{j}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=\Theta\sum_{j=0}^{n}2^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mathcal{QM}_{k}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=\Theta\left(2^{n+1}-1\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left(\Phi_{1}\omega_{1}^{j}+\Phi_{2}\omega_{2}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\Theta\left(2^{n+1}-1\right)+\Phi_{1}\left(\frac{\omega_{1}^{n+1}-1}{\omega_{1}-1}\right)+\Phi_{2}\left(\frac{\omega_{2}^{n+1}-1}{\omega_{2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\Theta\left(2^{n+1}-1\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{1}\left(\omega_{1}^{n}-\omega_{1}^{n+1}-\omega_{2}+1\right)\\
+\Phi_{2}\left(\omega_{2}^{n}-\omega_{2}^{n+1}-\omega_{1}+1\right)\end{array}\right\}.

Using
\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}=\omega_{1}^{n}\Phi_{1}+\omega_{2}^{n}\Phi_{2}
and
\omega_{1}\omega_{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mathcal{QK}_{j}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}+2\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\\
+\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{2}^{(a,b,c)}\end{array}\right\},

which is the desired result.
∎

A generating function for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions can be found in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7

(Generating function)
.

A generating function for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions is

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{QK}_{j}^{(a,b,c)}x^{j}=\frac{\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\Theta(1+x+x^{2})\\
\Phi_{1}(1+(\omega_{1}-1)x+2\omega_{2}x^{2})\\
\Phi_{2}(1+(\omega_{2}-1)x+2\omega_{1}x^{2})\end{array}\right\}}{1-x-x^{2}-2x^{3}}.

Proof.

Let us define
g_{\mathcal{K}}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{QK}_{j}^{(a,b,c)}x^{j}
. Multiplying this equation by
1
,
-x
,
-x^{2}
and
-2x^{3}
, respectively, and summing the last equations, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-x-x^{2}-2x^{3})g_{\mathcal{K}}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}+\left(\mathcal{QK}_{1}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}\right)x

\displaystyle\ \ +\left(\mathcal{QK}_{2}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{1}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}\right)x^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}+\left(\mathcal{QK}_{1}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}\right)x+2\mathcal{QK}_{-1}^{(a,b,c)}x^{2}.

Also we have

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{QK}_{-1}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=2\cdot 2^{-1}\Theta+2\omega_{1}^{-1}\Phi_{1}+2\omega_{2}^{-1}\Phi_{2},

\displaystyle=\Theta+2\omega_{2}\Phi_{1}+2\omega_{1}\Phi_{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{0}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=\Theta+\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{1}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=2\Theta+\omega_{1}\Phi_{1}+\omega_{2}\Phi_{2}.

The theorem is proven using the previous equalities.
∎

We give Cassini’s identity for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions. We calculate this identity by using the multiplication rules for quaternions.

Theorem 2.8

(Cassini like-identity for the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions)
.

Let
n
be integer such that
n\geq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\cdot\mathcal{QK}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}-\left(\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\cdot\mathcal{QM}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}-\left(\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}\\
+2^{n}\Theta\left(2\mathcal{QM}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)\\
+2^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}-2\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)\Theta\end{array}\right\},

where
\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}
is as in Theorem
2.3
.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
2.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\cdot\mathcal{QK}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle-\left(\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{n+1}\Theta+\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\right)\cdot\left(2^{n-1}\Theta+\mathcal{QM}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ -\left(2^{n}\Theta+\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\cdot\mathcal{QM}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}-\left(\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +2^{n}\Theta\left(2\mathcal{QM}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +2^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}-2\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)\Theta.

Using
\mathcal{QM}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}=\mathcal{QM}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}
. Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\cdot\mathcal{QK}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle-\left(\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\cdot\mathcal{QM}_{n-1}^{(a,b,c)}-\left(\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +2^{n}\Theta\left(2\mathcal{QM}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ +2^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{QM}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}-2\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\right)\Theta.

The proof is proved.
∎

Using the definition
2.1
and properties of sequence
\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}
, we can deduce the following relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{QM}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1}^{n}\Phi_{1}+\omega_{2}^{n}\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle=M_{n}+M_{n+a}\textbf{i}+M_{n+b}\textbf{j}+M_{n+c}\textbf{k}

\displaystyle=M_{n}\left(1+X_{a+1}\textbf{i}+X_{b+1}\textbf{j}+X_{c+1}\textbf{k}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ -M_{n-1}\left(X_{a}\textbf{i}+X_{b}\textbf{j}+X_{c}\textbf{k}\right),

where

X_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 0\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\\
1&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 1\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\\
-1&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 2\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\end{array}\right.

Matrix generators play an important role in the theory of the third-order Jacobsthal numbers and the third-order Jacobsthal quaternions (see, for example
[
6
,
7
]
). We derive the matrix representation of the unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternions.

Definition 2.9
.

Unrestricted modified third-order Jacobsthal quaternion matrix
\textbf{J}_{n}(\mathcal{QK})
is defined by

\textbf{J}_{n}(\mathcal{QK})=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{QK}_{n+3}^{(a,b,c)}&\mathcal{QK}_{n+4}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{n+3}^{(a,b,c)}&2\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}\\
\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}&\mathcal{QK}_{n+3}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}&2\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}\\
\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}&\mathcal{QK}_{n+2}^{(a,b,c)}-\mathcal{QK}_{n+1}^{(a,b,c)}&2\mathcal{QK}_{n}^{(a,b,c)}\end{array}\right],

for all
n\geq 0
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. Then

\textbf{J}_{n}(\mathcal{QK})=\textbf{J}_{0}(\mathcal{QM})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&2\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right]^{n}.

Proof.

(by induction on
n
) If
n=0
then assuming that the matrix to the power 0 is the
identity matrix, the result is obvious. Now assume that for any
r\geq 0
holds

\textbf{J}_{r}(\mathcal{QK})=\textbf{J}_{0}(\mathcal{QM})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&2\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right]^{r}.

By simple calculation using induction’s hypothesis and relation (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\textbf{J}_{0}(\mathcal{QM})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&2\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right]^{r+1}

\displaystyle=\textbf{J}_{0}(\mathcal{QM})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&2\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right]^{r}\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&2\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\textbf{J}_{r}(\mathcal{QK})\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&2\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\textbf{J}_{r+1}(\mathcal{QK}),

which ends the proof.
∎

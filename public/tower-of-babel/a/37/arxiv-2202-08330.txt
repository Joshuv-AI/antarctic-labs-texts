Large deviations for subcomplex counts and Betti numbers in multi-parameter simplicial complexes Thanks: Samorodnitsky’s research is partially supported by the NSF grant DMS-2015242 at Cornell University. Owada’s research is partially supported by the NSF grant, DMS-1811428 at Purdue University.
Gennady Samorodnitsky, Takashi Owada

Abstract
. We consider the multi-parameter random simplicial complex as a higher dimensional extension of the classical Erdös-Rényi graph. We investigate appearance of “unusual” topological structures in the complex from the point of view of large deviations. We first study upper tail large deviation probabilities for subcomplex counts, deriving the order of magnitude of such probabilities at the logarithmic scale precision. The obtained results are then applied to analyze large deviations for the number of simplices at the critical dimension and below. Finally, these results are also used to deduce large deviation estimates for Betti numbers of the complex in the critical dimension.

1.
Introduction

One can view a simplicial complex as a network with connections
potentially
involving more than 2 vertices at a time. Given a set
V
of vertices,
an undirected
graph allows for the existence of only edges of the type
(v_{1},v_{2})
for

v_{1},v_{2}
in
V
, while potentially higher-dimensional “edges” would
have the form
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
with
k\geq 2
for
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}

in
V
. If
k>2
, this is a hyperedge, which is not allowed in a graph
but is allowed in a hypergraph. A simplicial complex is a
special kind of a hypergraph, in which a subset of hyperdge is itself
a hyperedge. That is, if
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
with
k>2
is a
hyperedge, then so is
the collection of
k-1
vertices obtained by removing from

(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
any one of its
k
vertices. When describing a
simiplical complex, one typically says that
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
forms
a
(k-1)
-dimensional simplex (henceforth we call it a
(k-1)
-simplex), and not a hyperedge.

In a random simplicial complex, the simplices of different dimensions are
added according to a randomized rule. Some of the models of random
simplicial complexes are
extensions of the classical Erdös-Rényi random graph, in which
potential edges between two vertices are formed with probability
p
,
independently of other potential edges. Possible rules of constructing
a random simplicial complex include the
flag complex
(also known as
the clique complex), in which a potential
k
-simplex is
formed whenever a set of
k+1
vertices constitutes a clique in the
Erdös-Rényi graph (see e.g.,
Kahle 2009
).

The Linial-Meshulam-Wallach complex
of a fixed maximal dimension
k

is a random simplicial complex, in which all of the
(k-1)
-simplices are present with probability
1
, while the potential
k
-simplices are included with probability
p
, independently of other
k
-simplices (
Linial and Meshulam 2006
;
Meshulam and Wallach 2009
).
The most general model in this direction is

the Costa-Farber multi-parameter simplicial complex
, for which
potential simplices are added inductively in their dimensions; for
every
k=1,2,\dots
, each potential
k
-simplex is included with
probability
p_{k}
, independently of other simplices, only when all of its
(k-1)
-faces are present (see

Costa and Farber 2016
;
Costa and Farber 2017
).

The randomness of the simplicial complexes induces
randomness on the topological structure of the complex and its
topological invariants, such as

the Betti numbers
and
the Euler characteristic
. The distributions of
topological invariants have been a subject of recent interest for
various models of random simplicial complexes. This includes the existence
of a dominating dimension and central limit theorems for the Euler
characteristic; see e.g.
Kahle and Meckes 2013
;
Thoppe et al. 2016
;
Fowler 2019
;
Kanazawa 2022
. Functional
limit theorems for a dynamic version of the multi-parameter model have
been established in
Owada et al. 2021
.

However, the previous work describes only the “usual” topological
structure of random simplicial complexes, in the sense of the
“average” behavior and likely deviations from the “average”
behavior of the topological invariants.
In contrast, the primary focus of this paper is in the situations when the
topological structure of the complex is less usual, in the sense of
a topological invariant being far away from the average. Such events
are, by definition, rare but may have an oversized impact on the
function of the network, and they are typically referred to as large
deviations events.
Such events are often related to situations when
certain subcomplexes appear significantly more or significantly
less than expected. Understanding the
probabilities of such events is sometimes described as the upper (or
lower) tail large deviations problem for a
subcomplex count.

Within the context of the Erdös-Rényi random graphs,
large deviation problems for subgraph counts have attracted much attention over the last decade in, among many others,
Chatterjee and Varadhan 2011
;
Chatterjee and Dembo 2016
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2017
;
Lubetzky and Zhao 2017
;
Eldan 2018
;
Yan 2020
;
Cook and Dembo 2020
.
In particular,
Chatterjee 2017
gives a comprehensive presentation, covering various large deviation problems for the random graph of different degrees of denseness. Moreover,
Janson et al. 2004
developed a general
framework for the upper tail large deviation problems for subgraph counts in the random graph.
The combinatorial part of this approach is based on a result in
Alon 1981
, and an extension to uniform subhypergraph counts in a random hypergraph setup is provided in
Dudek et al. 2010
.

The present work receives much inspiration from
Janson et al. 2004
and addresses the upper tail large deviation problems for subcomplex counts in the multi-parameter random simplicial complex.
Due to its high-dimensional topological structure,
this problem is more involved than the analogous problems for
random graphs.
Although some of the results available for the Erdös-Rényi random
graph have not yet been fully extended to the multi-parameter random
simplicial complex, the results we obtain are useful for
understanding certain rare events in the latter
complex. For instance, we are able to describe the upper tail large
deviations for the Betti numbers at the dominating dimensions.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we
present a formal definition of the multi-parameter random simplicial
complex.
In Section
3
, we address
general upper tail large deviation problem for subcomplex
counts. Section
4
specializes to the large
deviation problem for the number of simplices at the
dominating dimension and below, under the setup of

Owada et al. 2021
. Finally, Section

5
discusses the upper tail large deviations for Betti numbers
at the dominating dimension.

2.
The multi-parameter random simplical complex and the
subcomplex count problem

In this section we formally construct the multi-parameter random
simplical complex introduced in
Costa and Farber 2016
;
Costa and Farber 2017
. This complex is a model of an abstract
simplicial complex on the alphabet
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
parametrized by
{\bf p}={\bf p}(n)=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1})\in[0,1]^{n-1}
.
A simplex
(or a face, or a word) in this complex is a nonempty collection of letters in
the alphabet, and the dimension of a simplex is equal to the number of
letters in the word minus 1. The
i
th skeleton of a complex is the
subcomplex consisting of all faces of dimension
i
or less. The
multi-parameter random simplicial complex is built recursively,
starting with the
[n]
as the 0th skeleton. For
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, once the
(i-1)
st
skeleton has been constructed, each of the potential
i
-simplices whose
boundary is in that
(i-1)
st skeleton, is added to the complex with
probability
p_{i}
, independently of other potential
i
-simplices. We
denote the obtained random complex by
K\big(n;{\bf p}(n)\big)=K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1})
.

We are interested in the subcomplex counting problem for the
multi-parameter random simplicial complex.
Given two simplicial complexes,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, an
ordered copy of
F_{1}
in
F_{2}
is an injective simplicial map from
the vertex set of
F_{1}
to the vertex set of
F_{2}
. In particular, this map has the
property that the vertices of every simplex in
F_{1}
are mapped
into the vertices of a simplex in
F_{2}
of the same dimension. Similarly, an unordered
copy of
F_{1}
in
F_{2}
is a subcomplex
F_{3}
of

F_{2}
, which is isomorphic to
F_{1}
; that is, there is a bijective mapping between
the vertex set of
F_{1}
and the vertex set of
F_{3}
, such that a set of vertices forms a simplex in
F_{1}
if and only if the
corresponding vertex set under this mapping forms a simplex in
F_{3}
. If

n_{o}(F_{2},F_{1})
and
n(F_{2},F_{1})
are the numbers of
ordered and unordered copies of
F_{1}
in
F_{2}

correspondingly, then it is
clear that
n_{o}(F_{2},F_{1})/n(F_{2},F_{1})=\#({\rm Aut}(F_{1}))
,
the number of automorphisms of
F_{1}
consisting of all permutations of
the vertices of
F_{1}
preserving the complex.

Let
F
be a fixed simplicial complex of dimension
k\geq 1
. For

n>k
and probabilities
{\bf p}(n)
, we denote by
N_{n}(F)
and
N_{o,n}(F)
the (random)
numbers of unordered and ordered copies of
F
in the multi-parameter simplicial complex

K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1})
, correspondingly. Note that
p_{i}
with
i>k
do not affect

N_{n}(F)
and
N_{o,n}(F)
, so we may assume that

p_{i}=0
for
i>k
and simply write the complex as
K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
. If we
denote for
i=0,1,\ldots,k
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle F_{i}=

the set of
i
-simplices in
F
,

\displaystyle s_{i}(F)=

\displaystyle\ \text{the number of $i$-simplices in $F$},

then

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{o,n}(F):=\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(F)]=(n)_{s_{0}(F)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{s_{i}(F)},

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(F):=\mathbb{E}[N_{n}(F)]=\mu_{o,n}(F)/\#({\rm Aut}(F)),

where
(n)_{s_{0}(F)}:=n(n-1)\cdots\bigl(n-s_{0}(F)+1\bigr)
.

As
n\to\infty
, the copies of
F
can potentially be found in many
(nearly) independent parts of the multi-parameter simplicial complex

K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
, so one expects
that for large
n
enough,
N_{n}(F)
and
N_{o,n}(F)
do not deviate
“too much” from their corresponding means. Therefore, the upper tail
large deviation probabilities

(2.3)

\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(F)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(F)\bigr),\ \ \varepsilon>0,

and the lower tail large deviation probabilities

(2.4)

\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(F)\leq(1-\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(F)\bigr),\ \ 0<\varepsilon<1,

are expected to be exponentially small for large
n
enough. Our
subject of interest is to investigate exactly how small these
probabilities are. In this paper we focus only on the upper tail large
deviations in (
2.3
). An analysis of the lower tail
large deviations in (
2.4
) is postponed to a future
publication.

3.
Upper tail large deviations

Our approach to understanding the upper tail large deviations for
subcomplex counts is inspired by

Janson et al. 2004
.
If
G
is a fixed simplicial complex of dimension
k\geq 1
, we denote by

N(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)
the maximum of
n(F,G)
taken over all
simplicial complexes
F
with
s_{i}(F)\leq m_{i}
,

i=0,1,\ldots,k
. Clearly,
N(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)=0
unless

s_{i}(G)\leq m_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,k
.

The number
N(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)
is often referred to as the
extremal parameter
and is related to a certain
linear optimization problem that we now describe. Using the notation
in (
2.1
), we consider the linear program

(3.1)

\displaystyle\max\sum_{v\in G_{0}}x_{v}

subject to

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{v\in\sigma_{i}}x_{v}\leq\log m_{i},\ \sigma_{i}\in G_{i},\ i=0,1,\ldots,k.

Denote by
\gamma=\gamma(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)
the optimal value of
this problem.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
s_{i}(G)\leq m_{i}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,k
. Then, there are finite
positive constants
c(G),C(G)
that depend only on
G
, such that

(3.2)

c(G)e^{\gamma(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)}\leq N(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)\leq C(G)e^{\gamma(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)}.

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound in (
3.2
).
Let
\bigl(x_{v}^{*},\,v\in G_{0}\bigr)
be an optimal solution to the
linear program (
3.1
).
We construct a simplicial complex
F
as
follows. Let
c>0
be a small constant described in the sequel.
We start with a family of disjoint sets
(V_{v})_{v\in G_{0}}
, where
V_{v}
consists of
n_{v}:=\lceil ce^{x_{v}^{*}}\rceil
points for each
v\in G_{0}
.
Define the vertices of
F
to be the points in
the union
\bigcup_{v\in G_{0}}V_{v}
, i.e., we take
F_{0}=\bigcup_{v\in G_{0}}V_{v}
.
Next, for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and distinct vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{j+1}\in G_{0}
, a point set
(w_{1},\dots,w_{j+1})\in\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}V_{v_{i}}
forms a
j
-simplex in
F
if and only if
the vertices

v_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,j+1
, form a
j
-simplex in
G
.

We claim that if we choose a sufficiently small constant
c>0
that depends only on
G
,
then it can be assured that

s_{j}(F)\leq m_{j},\ \ j=0,1,\ldots,k.

For this purpose, we use the constraints in (
3.1
).
Consider first
s_{0}(F)
. Suppose first that
s_{0}(G)<m_{0}
; then,

\displaystyle s_{0}(F)=\sum_{v\in G_{0}}\lceil ce^{x_{v}^{*}}\rceil\leq\sum_{v\in G_{0}}\bigl(ce^{x_{v}^{*}}+1\bigr)\leq s_{0}(G)(cm_{0}+1)\leq m_{0},

if we choose
c
to satisfy

c\leq\frac{1}{s_{0}(G)(1+s_{0}(G))}.

Suppose next that
s_{0}(G)=m_{0}
. In this case,
e^{x_{v}^{*}}\leq s_{0}(G)
for
each
v\in G_{0}
, so choosing
c\leq 1/s_{0}(G)
leads to

s_{0}(F)=\sum_{v\in G_{0}}\lceil ce^{x_{v}^{*}}\rceil=s_{0}(G)=m_{0}.

Similarly, for any
j=1,\ldots,k
, if
s_{j}(G)<m_{j}
, then

\displaystyle s_{j}(F)=

\displaystyle\sum_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1})\in G_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}\lceil ce^{x_{v_{i}}^{*}}\rceil\leq\sum_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1})\in G_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}\bigl(ce^{x_{v_{i}}^{*}}+1\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1})\in G_{j}}\bigg(1+\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}\sum_{A\subset\{1,\ldots,j+1\},\#(A)=i}c^{i}\exp\Big\{\sum_{\ell\in A}x_{v_{\ell}^{*}}\Big\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1})\in G_{j}}\bigg(1+\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}c^{i}{j+1\choose i}m_{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{j}(G)\Big(1+m_{j}\bigl((1+c)^{j+1}-1\bigr)\Big).

Thus, if we choose
c
to satisfy

c\leq\left[\left(\frac{1}{s_{j}(G)(1+s_{j}(G))}+1\right)^{1/(j+1)}-1\right],

it then holds that
s_{j}(F)\leq m_{j}
, as required.
On the other hand, if
s_{j}(G)=m_{j}
, then for
(v_{1},\dots,v_{j+1})\in G_{j}
, we have
e^{x_{v_{i}}^{*}}\leq s_{j}(G)
,
i=1,\dots,j+1
; so choosing
c\leq 1/s_{j}(G)
again leads to

s_{j}(F)=\sum_{(v_{1},\dots,v_{j+1})\in G_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{j+1}\lceil ce^{x_{v_{i}}^{*}}\rceil=s_{j}(G)=m_{j}.

For the simplicial complex
F

constructed above, the number of ordered copies of
G
in
F
is at least

\prod_{v\in G_{0}}n_{v}\geq c^{s_{0}(G)}\exp\Big\{\sum_{v\in G_{0}}x_{v}^{*}\Big\}=c^{s_{0}(G)}e^{\gamma}.

We thus conclude that

N(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)\geq N(F,G)\geq\frac{c^{s_{0}(G)}}{\#({\rm Aut}(G))}e^{\gamma},

establishing the lower bound in (
3.2
).

In order to prove the upper bound in (
3.2
), we start with
the dual problem to the optimization problem
(
3.1
). It is the linear program

(3.3)

\displaystyle\min\left[\sum_{v\in G_{0}}y_{v}\log m_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i}}z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}\log m_{i}\right]

subject to

\displaystyle y_{v}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i}\atop v\in\sigma_{i}}z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}\geq 1\ \ \text{for any $v\in G_{0}$,}

\displaystyle y_{v}\geq 0,\ z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}\geq 0\ \ \text{for all $v\in G_{0}$ and $\sigma_{i}\in G_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,k$.}

The optimal value of the dual problem (
3.3
) equals

\gamma=\gamma(m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{k};G)
; that is, it has the same optimal value as the original linear program in (
3.1
). For later use, let
(y_{v}^{*}),\bigl(z_{\sigma_{i}}^{(i*)}\bigr)
be an optimal solution to the dual problem in (
3.3
), so that

(3.4)

\gamma=\sum_{v\in G_{0}}y_{v}^{*}\log m_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i}}z_{\sigma_{i}}^{(i*)}\log m_{i}.

Now, let us fix a simplicial complex
F
of dimension
k
, satisfying
s_{i}(F)\leq m_{i},\,i=0,1,\ldots,k
.
Then, the upper bound in (
3.2
) is obtained as an
immediate consequence of the bound

(3.5)

n_{o}(F,G)\leq C(G)e^{\gamma}

for some constant
C(G)
that does not depend on
F
.
For the proof of (
3.5
), we consider a partition

(3.6)

F_{0}=\bigcup_{v\in G_{0}}V_{v}

of the vertex set of
F
into subsets indexed by the vertices of

G
.
Denote by
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(F,G)
the collection of all ordered copies of
G
in
F
, so that
\#(\mathcal{H}(F,G))=n_{o}(F,G)
. Further, let
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}(F,G)
be a subset of
\mathcal{H}
, such that each
v\in G_{0}
is mapped into one of the vertices in
V_{v}
.

We create a random
partition (
3.6
) as follows. To each vertex
w\in F_{0}
, assign randomly and
independently a vertex
U(w)\in G_{0}
. Now, let

V_{v}=\bigl\{w\in F_{0}:\,U(w)=v\bigr\},\ v\in G_{0}

(some sets
V_{v}
may be empty). In this setting,
\mathcal{W}
is a
random
subset of
\mathcal{H}
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\#(\mathcal{W})\bigr]=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{P}(\varphi\in\mathcal{W})=\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}}\mathbb{P}\Big(U\big(\varphi(v)\big)=v\ \ \text{for each $v\in G_{0}$}\Big)=s_{0}(G)^{-s_{0}(G)}n_{o}(F,G).

This indicates that there exists a
nonrandom
partition (
3.6
) of the
vertex set of
F
, for which

(3.7)

\#(\mathcal{W})\geq s_{0}(G)^{-s_{0}(G)}n_{0}(F,G).

Fixing a collection
\mathcal{W}
that satisfies (
3.7
), we define
\mathcal{W}_{0}:=\bigl\{H_{0}:\,H\in\mathcal{W}\bigr\}
to be
the collection of
ordered
vertex sets of complexes in
\mathcal{W}
,
so that
\#(\mathcal{W}_{0})=\#(\mathcal{W})
. For a subset
U\subseteq F_{0}
, define the trace of
\mathcal{W}_{0}
on
U
by

{\rm Tr}(\mathcal{W}_{0},U)=\bigl\{H_{0}\cap U:\,H_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\bigr\}.

We choose a large
positive integer
t
and define

l_{0}(v)=\lceil ty_{v}^{*}\rceil,\,v\in G_{0},\ \ l_{i}(\sigma_{i})=\lceil tz_{\sigma_{i}}^{(i*)}\rceil,\,\sigma_{i}\in G_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,k,

where
y_{v}^{*}
and
z_{\sigma_{i}}^{(i*)}
are given in (
3.4
).
Referring to the partition (
3.6
) of
F_{0}
constructed above
that satisfies (
3.7
),
we now construct a family
U_{1},\ldots,U_{s}
of subsets of
F_{0}

as follows. Take each set
V_{v}
in (
3.6
) exactly

l_{0}(v)
times for every
v\in G_{0}
. Next, for each
\sigma_{i}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i+1}\}\in G_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
, take the union

V_{v_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup V_{v_{i+1}}
exactly
l_{i}(\sigma_{i})

times.
Finally, we enumerate these subsets as
U_{1},\dots,U_{s}
, where

s=\sum_{v\in G_{0}}l_{0}(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i}}l_{i}(\sigma_{i}).

Then, for every
v\in G_{0}
, each of the vertices in
V_{v}
appears exactly
l_{0}(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i},\,v\in\sigma_{i}}l_{i}(\sigma_{i})
times in the sets
U_{1},\dots,U_{s}
.
By the constraint of the dual problem
(
3.3
),

\displaystyle l_{0}(v)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i}\atop v\in\sigma_{i}}l_{i}(\sigma_{i})\geq t\bigg(y_{v}^{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in G_{i}\atop v\in\sigma_{i}}z^{(i*)}_{\sigma_{i}}\bigg)\geq t.

This implies that every vertex in
F
appears at least
t
times in the sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{s}
.
By Lemma 1.2 in

Friedgut and Kahn 1998
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\bigl(\#(\mathcal{W})\bigr)^{t}=\bigl(\#(\mathcal{W}_{0})\bigr)^{t}\leq\prod_{m=1}^{s}\#\bigl({\rm Tr}(\mathcal{W}_{0},U_{m})\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in G_{0}}\left[\#\bigl({\rm Tr}(\mathcal{W}_{0},V_{v})\bigr)\right]^{l_{0}(v)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\sigma_{j}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1}\}\in G_{j}}\left[\#\bigl({\rm Tr}(\mathcal{W}_{0},V_{v_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup V_{v_{j+1}})\bigr)\right]^{l_{j}(\sigma_{j})}.

By the definition of
\mathcal{W}
,
each
H_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
has at most one element in
V_{v}
for each
v\in G_{0}
, so

(3.9)

\#\bigl({\rm Tr}(\mathcal{W}_{0},V_{v})\bigr)\leq\#(V_{v})\leq s_{0}(F)\leq m_{0}.

Similarly, for each
\sigma_{j}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1}\}\in G_{j}
, the intersection of
H_{0}\in\mathcal{W}_{0}
and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{j+1}V_{v_{i}}
either forms a
j
-simplex in
F
or becomes an empty set. Therefore,

(3.10)

\#\bigl({\rm Tr}(\mathcal{W}_{0},V_{v_{1}}\cup\ldots\cup V_{v_{j+1}})\bigr)\leq s_{j}(F)\leq m_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,k.

Substituting the bounds in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) back into (
3.8
), we have, as
t\to\infty
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\#(\mathcal{W})\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in G_{0}}m_{0}^{l_{0}(v)/t}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\sigma_{j}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1}\}\in G_{j}}m_{j}^{l_{j}(\sigma_{j})/t}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in G_{0}}m_{0}^{y_{v}^{*}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{\sigma_{j}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j+1}\}\in G_{j}}m_{j}^{z^{(j*)}_{\sigma_{j}}}=e^{\gamma},

where the last equality follows from (
3.4
).
Combining (
3.7
) and (
3.11
), we have

n_{0}(F,G)\leq s_{0}(G)^{s_{0}(G)}e^{\gamma},

which establishes the bound (
3.5
), as desired.
∎

The following lemma is a useful consequence of Proposition

3.1
. It is a higher-dimensional version of Lemma 2.1 in

Janson et al. 2004
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a
k
-dimensional subcomplex of
G
with
s_{k}(H)>0
. Then, there
exists a constant
C_{H}\in(1,\infty)
such that if
0\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq m_{0}^{k+1}/s_{k}(G)
,

\displaystyle N\bigl(m_{0},m_{1}s_{1}(G),\ldots,m_{1}s_{k}(G);H\bigr)\leq C_{H}\Big(\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}\Big)^{1/(k+1)}N\bigl(m_{0},m_{2}s_{1}(G),\ldots,m_{2}s_{k}(G);H\bigr).

Proof.

It is enough to consider the case
m_{1}>0
.
By Proposition
3.1
,

(3.12)

\displaystyle N\bigl(m_{0},m_{1}s_{1}(G),\ldots,m_{1}s_{k}(G);H\bigr)\leq C_{1}(H)e^{\gamma_{1}}

for some
C_{1}(H)\in(1,\infty)
, where

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=\max\sum_{v\in H_{0}}x_{v}

subject to

\displaystyle 0\leq x_{v}\leq\log m_{0},\ v\in H_{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\sigma_{i}}x_{v}\leq\log s_{i}(G)+\log m_{1},\ \sigma_{i}\in H_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,k.

Let
(x_{v}^{*},\,v\in H_{0})
be an optimal solution for this problem. Consider all

v\in H_{0}
that belong to a
k
-simplex in
H
, and choose
among them a vertex

\tilde{v}\in H_{0}
with the smallest value of
x_{v}^{*}
; that is,

x_{\tilde{v}}^{*}=\min_{\sigma_{k}\in H_{k},\,v\in\sigma_{k}}x_{v}^{*}.

By the feasibility
of
(x_{v}^{*})
, we have

(3.13)

x_{\tilde{v}}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\log s_{k}(G)+\frac{1}{k+1}\log m_{1}.

Define for
v\in H_{0}
,

x_{v}^{**}=x_{v}^{*}\ \text{for $v\not=\tilde{v}$},\ \ x_{\tilde{v}}^{**}=x_{\tilde{v}}^{*}+\frac{1}{k+1}\log\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}.

Then for
\sigma_{i}\in H_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\sigma_{i}}x_{v}^{**}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in\sigma_{i}}x_{v}^{*}+\frac{1}{k+1}\log\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\log s_{i}(G)+\log m_{1}+\frac{1}{k+1}\log\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\leq\log s_{i}(G)+\log m_{2},

while, by (
3.13
),

\displaystyle x_{\tilde{v}}^{**}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\log s_{k}(G)+\frac{1}{k+1}\log m_{1}+\frac{1}{k+1}\log\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k+1}\log s_{k}(G)+\frac{1}{k+1}\log m_{2}\leq\log m_{0}.

We thus conclude that
(x_{v}^{**})
is a feasible solution to the linear program

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\max\sum_{v\in H_{0}}x_{v}

subject to

\displaystyle 0\leq x_{v}\leq\log m_{0},\ v\in H_{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\sigma_{i}}x_{v}\leq\log s_{i}(G)+\log m_{2},\ \sigma_{i}\in H_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,k.

Therefore,

\gamma_{2}\geq\sum_{v\in H_{0}}x_{v}^{**}=\gamma_{1}+\frac{1}{k+1}\log\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}.

Appealing once again to Proposition
3.1
, as well as (
3.12
), we have for some constant

C_{2}(H)\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle N\bigl(m_{0},m_{2}s_{1}(G),\ldots,m_{2}s_{k}(G);H\bigr)\geq C_{2}(H)e^{\gamma_{2}}\geq C_{2}(H)\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\right)^{1/(k+1)}e^{\gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{C_{2}(H)}{C_{1}(H)}\left(\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}\right)^{1/(k+1)}\hskip-5.0ptN\bigl(m_{0},m_{1}s_{1}(G),\ldots,m_{1}s_{k}(G);H\bigr),

as required.
∎

For numbers
0\leq p_{i}\leq 1,\,i=1,\ldots,k
, and a simplicial complex
G
of dimension
k
, denote

\Psi_{G,n}:=n^{s_{0}(G)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{s_{i}(G)}\sim\mu_{o,n}(G),\ n\to\infty,

and define

\displaystyle M^{*}_{G,n}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}):=\max\biggl\{

\displaystyle 1\leq m\leq\frac{{n\choose k+1}}{s_{k}(G)}:N\bigl(n,ms_{1}(G),\ldots,ms_{k}(G);H\bigr)\leq\Psi_{H,n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{for every non-empty subcomplex $H$ of
$G$}\biggr\}.

The following theorem is the main result of this section. It is an
extension of Theorem 1.2 of
Janson et al. 2004

to the multi-parameter random simplicial complexes.
Its statement uses
the notation
K_{k,n}
for the complete complex of dimension
k
of

n
vertices (i.e. a complex on
n
vertices containing all possible
simplices of dimensions
k
and smaller).

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
C(\varepsilon,G)>0
so that for all
n\geq 1
,

(3.14)

\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl\{-C(\varepsilon,G)M^{*}_{G,n}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\Bigr\}.

Moreover, if
(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\leq N\bigl(K_{k,n},G\bigr)
, there exists

B(\varepsilon,G)>0
, such that for all
n\geq 2k+1
,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\right)^{B(\varepsilon,G)M^{*}_{G,n}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})}.

Remark 3.4
.

Theorem
3.3
identifies the order of magnitude of the upper tail large
deviation probability at the logarithmic scale precision.
The logarithmic order of magnitude
differs between the upper bound (
3.14
) and the
lower bound (
3.15
) by a factor of
\log\big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\big)
.
We note that under a common setup
p_{i}=n^{-\alpha_{i}}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in[0,\infty]
,
i\geq 1
, as in Section
4
below, the factor
\log\big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\big)
is logarithmic in
n
. In contrast,
the main term
M^{*}_{G,n}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
typically grows
polynomially, determining largely the order of magnitude of the
large deviation probability.

Note also that the condition
(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\leq N\bigl(K_{k,n},G\bigr)
is rarely restrictive. In fact, it is equivalent to

(1+\varepsilon)\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)}\leq 1
; this, however, trivially
holds whenever
p_{j}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for some
j
.

Proof.

We start with proving the upper bound in (
3.14
). Let

G_{1},\ldots,G_{M}
be the ordered copies of
G
in
K_{k,n}
where
M=(n)_{s_{0}(G)}=n(n-1)\cdots\bigl(n-s_{0}(G)+1\bigr)
. Clearly,

N_{o,n}(G)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}I_{j},

where

I_{j}={\mathbbm{1}}\bigl\{G_{j}\text{ is a subcomplex of }K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\bigr\},\ \ j=1,\ldots,M.

Therefore, for each
m=1,2\ldots
, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m}]=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq M}\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_{i_{1}}\cdots I_{i_{m}}\bigr]=\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\leq M}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G_{i_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup G_{i_{m}})},

with
s_{j}(\cdot)
as in (
2.1
). For a fixed
{\bf i}^{(m-1)}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m-1})\in\{1,\dots,M\}^{m-1}
, denote
F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}=G_{i_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup G_{i_{m-1}}
. Then
(
3.16
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m}]=\sum_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}})}\sum_{i_{m}=1}^{M}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i_{m}})}.

For every fixed
{\bf i}^{(m-1)}
,
if
F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i_{m}}
contains at least one simplex of positive
dimension, then this intersection is isomorphic to some subcomplex
H
of
G
of positive dimension.
We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}})}\bigg[M\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)}+\hskip-10.0pt\sum_{i_{m}:F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i_{m}}\neq\emptyset}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i_{m}})}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}})}\bigg[\mu_{o,n}(G)+\sum_{H\subseteq G}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}\#\big\{i:F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i}\cong H\big\}\bigg],

where
\mu_{o,n}(G)
is given in (
2.2
), and the sum
\sum_{H\subseteq G}
is taken over all subcomplexes of
G
with positive dimension, and
\cong
means isomorphism between simplicial complexes.
For every subcomplex
H
of
G
, there are at most

\displaystyle N\bigl(s_{0}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}),s_{1}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}),\ldots,s_{k}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}});H\bigr)\leq N\bigl(n,(m-1)s_{1}(G),\ldots,(m-1)s_{k}(G);H\bigr)

ways to choose an unordered copy of
H
in
F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}
. To
bound the number of
ordered copies of
G
in
K_{k,n}
whose intersection with
F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}
is isomorphic to
H
(i.e.,
\#\{i:F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i}\cong H\}
), notice that each choice of
an unordered copy of
H
in
F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}
determines
s_{0}(H)
vertices in the
copy of
G
; thus, the number of ways to select the remaining vertices
of the copy of
G
is at most
\big(n-s_{0}(H)\big)_{s_{0}(G)-s_{0}(H)}=(n)_{s_{0}(G)}/(n)_{s_{0}(H)}
.
Finally, the vertices of the copy of
G
can be numbered in at most

s_{0}(G)!
ways. From these observations, we conclude that

\displaystyle\#\big\{i:F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\cap G_{i}\cong H\big\}\leq N\bigl(n,(m-1)s_{1}(G),\ldots,(m-1)s_{k}(G);H\bigr)\frac{(n)_{s_{0}(G)}}{(n)_{s_{0}(H)}}\,s_{0}(G)!.

Now (
2.2
) gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m}]\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(F_{{\bf i}^{(m-1)}})}

\displaystyle\times\mu_{o,n}(G)\bigg[1+s_{0}(G)!\sum_{H\subseteq G}\frac{N\bigl(n,(m-1)s_{1}(G),\ldots,(m-1)s_{k}(G);H\bigr)}{\mu_{o,n}(H)}\bigg].

Using (
3.16
) with
m
replaced by
m-1
results in

\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m}]\leq\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m-1}]\mu_{o,n}(G)\bigg[1+s_{0}(G)!\sum_{H\subseteq G}\frac{N\bigl(n,(m-1)s_{1}(G),\ldots,(m-1)s_{k}(G);H\bigr)}{\mu_{o,n}(H)}\bigg].

By the monotonicity of the function
N
in all of its arguments,
an inductive argument gives us the bound

(3.17)

\mathbb{E}[N_{o,n}(G)^{m}]\leq\mu_{o,n}(G)^{m}\Biggl[1+s_{0}(G)!\sum_{H\subseteq G}\frac{N\bigl(n,(m-1)s_{1}(G),\ldots,(m-1)s_{k}(G);H\bigr)}{\mu_{o,n}(H)}\Biggr]^{m-1},

for every
m\geq 1
.

For
\theta\in(0,1)
to be determined in the sequel, we take

m=\lceil\theta M^{*}_{G,n}(p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\rceil=:\lceil\theta M_{G,n}^{*}\rceil
. Note that by
Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle N\bigl(n,(m-1)s_{1}(G),\ldots,(m-1)s_{k}(G);H\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\bigl(n,\theta M_{G,n}^{*}s_{1}(G),\ldots,\theta M_{G,n}^{*}s_{k}(G);H\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{H}\theta^{1/(k+1)}N\bigl(n,M_{G,n}^{*}s_{1}(G),\ldots,M_{G,n}^{*}s_{k}(G);H\bigr)\leq\theta^{1/(k+1)}C_{H}\Psi_{H,n}.

Therefore,
using (
3.17
) with
m=\lceil\theta M_{G,n}^{*}\rceil
and Markov’s inequality,
we obtain for
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{-\theta M_{G,n}^{*}}\bigg[1+\theta^{1/(k+1)}s_{0}(G)!\sum_{H\subseteq G}\frac{C_{H}\Psi_{H,n}}{\mu_{o,n}(H)}\bigg]^{\theta M_{G,n}^{*}}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon)^{-\theta M_{G,n}^{*}}\bigg[1+\theta^{1/(k+1)}s_{0}(G)!\sum_{H\subseteq G}\frac{C_{H}n^{s_{0}(H)}}{n(n-1)\cdots\bigl(n-s_{0}(H)+1\bigr)}\bigg]^{\theta M_{G,n}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\Big(1+\theta^{1/(k+1)}s_{0}(G)^{s_{0}(G)}\sum_{H\subseteq G}C_{H}\Big)\bigg]^{\theta M_{G,n}^{*}}.

Choosing

0<\theta<\min\bigg\{1,\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon}{s_{0}(G)^{s_{0}(G)}\sum_{H\subseteq G}C_{H}}\bigg)^{k+1}\bigg\}

establishes (
3.14
).

Our proof of (
3.15
) is also inspired by an argument in

Janson et al. 2004
. Suppose there exist
a_{i}>0,\,i=1,\ldots,k
,
m\geq 1
, and a subcomplex
H
of
G
, such that

(3.18)

N\bigl(n,a_{1}m,\ldots,a_{k}m;H\bigr)\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\Psi_{H,n}.

This implies that there is a complex
F
on at most
n
nodes with

s_{i}(F)\leq a_{i}m,\,i=1,\ldots,k
, such that

(3.19)

n(F,H)\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\Psi_{H,n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(H).

Our first goal is to show that under the assumption (
3.18
),

(3.20)

\mathbb{P}\big(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\big)\geq\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)+a_{j}m}.

Given a subcomplex
H
of
G
satisfying (
3.18
), we see that each of the ordered copies of
G
in

K_{k,n}
has a
unique corresponding ordered copy of
H
in
K_{k,n}
; if the latter
is also in
F
, we refer to that ordered copy of
G
as being
F
-rooted. Since there
are
n_{o}(F,H)=\#({\rm Aut}(H))n(F,H)
ordered copies of
H
in
F
, the number of

F
-rooted ordered copies of
G
in
K_{k,n}
is

J:=n_{o}(F,H)\bigl(n-s_{0}(H)\bigr)_{s_{0}(G)-s_{0}(H)}.

Denote these ordered copies of
G
in
K_{k,n}
by
G_{1},\ldots,G_{J}
. Since
\#({\rm Aut}(H))\geq 1
, it follows from (
3.19
) that

(3.21)

\displaystyle J\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(H)\bigl(n-s_{0}(H)\bigr)_{s_{0}(G)-s_{0}(H)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}=2(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G).

Let
K_{F}(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
be the multi-parameter simplicial complex
K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
conditioned on
F\subseteq K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
. For

i=1,\ldots,J
, let
Z_{i}
be the indicator function of the event that
G_{i}
is a subcomplex of

K_{F}(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})
. Then,

(3.22)

\mathbb{P}(Z_{i}=1)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G_{i}\setminus F)}\geq\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}.

Since the rightmost term in (
3.22
) is independent of
i
, the lower bound in Lemma 3.3 of

Janson et al. 2004
gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(N_{o,n}(G)\geq\frac{J}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}\Bigg|F\subseteq K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{J}Z_{i}\geq\frac{J}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)}.

Therefore, by (
3.21
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\big)\geq\mathbb{P}\Big(N_{o,n}(G)\geq\frac{J}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)-s_{j}(H)}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)}\mathbb{P}\big(F\subseteq K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(F)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)+a_{j}m},

establishing (
3.20
), as desired.

Now, we are ready to prove the lower bound in (
3.15
).
Suppose first that

(3.23)

2\big(2(1+\varepsilon)C_{H}\big)^{k+1}M_{G,n}^{*}\leq s_{k}(G)^{-1}{n\choose k+1},

where
C_{H}>1
is the constant in Lemma
3.2
, that is increased, without loss of generality, to
be the nearest positive integer. Then,
M_{G,n}^{*}<\lfloor s_{k}(G)^{-1}{n\choose k+1}\rfloor
, so there is
a subcomplex
H
of
G
such that

N\Bigl(n,\bigl(M_{G,n}^{*}+1\bigr)s_{1}(G),\ldots,\bigl(M_{G,n}^{*}+1\bigr)s_{k}(G);H\Bigr)>\Psi_{H,n}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle 2(1+\varepsilon)\Psi_{H,n}<

\displaystyle 2(1+\varepsilon)N\Bigl(n,\bigl(M_{G,n}^{*}+1\bigr)s_{1}(G),\ldots,\bigl(M_{G,n}^{*}+1\bigr)s_{k}(G);H\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(1+\varepsilon)N\Bigl(n,2M_{G,n}^{*}s_{1}(G),\ldots,2M_{G,n}^{*}s_{k}(G);H\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\Bigl(n,2(2(1+\varepsilon)C_{H})^{k+1}M_{G,n}^{*}s_{1}(G),\ldots,2(2(1+\varepsilon)C_{H})^{k+1}M_{G,n}^{*}s_{k}(G);H\Bigr).

Since the condition (
3.18
) is now satisfied with
a_{i}=s_{i}(G)
,
i=1,\dots,k
, and
m=2\big(2(1+\varepsilon)C_{H}\big)^{k+1}M_{G,n}^{*}
, we conclude by
(
3.20
) that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\big)\geq\frac{1}{4}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{s_{j}(G)+2(2(1+\varepsilon)C_{H})^{k+1}M_{G,n}^{*}s_{j}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\bigg)^{B_{1}(\varepsilon,G)M_{G,n}^{*}},

where

B_{1}(\varepsilon,G)=\max_{j=1,\ldots,k}s_{j}(G)\Bigl[1+2\bigl(2(1+\varepsilon)\max_{H\subseteq G}C_{H}\bigr)^{k+1}\Bigr].

Next, we need to consider the case when (
3.23
) does not hold.
In this case, it follows from the assumption

(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\leq N(K_{k,n},G)
that, for
n\geq 2k+1
,

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(N_{o,n}(G)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(G)\big)\geq\mathbb{P}\big(N_{o,n}(G)\geq N\bigl(K_{k,n},G\bigr)\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Bigl(K(n;p_{1},\ldots,p_{k})=K_{k,n}\Bigr)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{n\choose j+1}\geq\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{n\choose k+1}\geq\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}\bigg)^{B_{2}(\varepsilon,G)M_{G,n}^{*}},

where

B_{2}(\varepsilon,G)=2\bigl(2(1+\varepsilon)\max_{H\subseteq G}C_{H}\bigr)^{k+1}s_{k}(G).

Now, (
3.15
) follows from (
3.24
) and
(
3.25
) .
∎

4.
Simplices at the critical dimension and below

Distributional limit theorems for the multi-parameter simplicial
complex
K(n;{\bf p})
were obtained in

Owada et al. 2021
. These
results are obtained under the assumption

(4.1)

p_{i}=n^{-\alpha_{i}},\ i\geq 1,

for
\alpha_{i}\in[0,\infty]
,
i\geq 1
. In this section we retain this
assumption and, instead of distributional results, we investigate
large deviation probabilities for the number of certain simplices in

K(n;{\bf p})
. We will use the general results obtained in
the previous section.

We are particularly interested in counting the simplices at or below the

critical dimension
of the model. In other words, we consider the simplices of dimension
k\geq 1
, satisfying

(4.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}{k\choose i}\alpha_{i}<1,\ \ \text{ and }\ \ q:=\min\{i\geq 1:\alpha_{i}>0\}\leq k;

this introduces a minor unimportant abuse of notation by conflating the dimension
of the simplex with the largest dimension of an entire complex. Counting
the simplices
at the critical dimension is particularly important since their
numbers largely determine the behaviour of the Euler characteristic
of
K(n;{\bf p})
;

Thoppe et al. 2016
;
Owada et al. 2021
.

Let
\sigma_{k}
be a simplex of dimension
k
satisfying (
4.2
).
The following proposition, a part of which requires an extra
assumption on the parameters, describes the size of a
crucial ingredient in the logarithmic order of magnitude of the upper tail
large deviations probability:
\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(\sigma_{k})>(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(\sigma_{k})\bigr)
for
\varepsilon>0
. Notice that the extra assumption (
4.3
) below will be used only for proving a lower bound in (
4.4
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that (
4.2
) holds. In the case of
q<k
, suppose also that for any
k_{0}=q+1,\ldots,k
,

(4.3)

\frac{k-q}{k+1}\binom{k+1}{q+1}\alpha_{q}+\sum_{j=q+1}^{k_{0}}{k+1\choose j+1}\alpha_{j}<k_{0}-q.

Then,
for large enough
n
,

(4.4)

C_{k}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\leq M_{\sigma_{k},n}^{*}\bigl(n^{-\alpha_{1}},\ldots,n^{-\alpha_{k}}\bigr)\leq C_{k}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}

for some
C_{k}\geq 1
. In particular, for
\varepsilon>0
and all large
enough
n
,

(4.5)

\exp\Bigl\{-C_{k}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\log n\Bigr\}\leq\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{o,n}(\sigma_{k})\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mu_{o,n}(\sigma_{k})\bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl\{-C_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\Bigr\}

for some positive constants
C_{k}^{\prime}(\varepsilon),\,C_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Proposition
4.1
indicates that, at least under an extra
condition, it is the skeleton of dimension
q
, the lowest non-trivial dimension of the complex, that plays a crucial role in
determining the rate of decay of the upper large deviation
probabilities at the critical dimension and below it. The reason appears to be the fact that “flipping” of a
q
-simplex from “on” to “off” or vice verse affects the topology of the complex more than does any flipping in other dimensions. The same phenomenon has already been observed in the central limit theorem for the simplex counts at the critical dimension; see Proposition 3.6 in
Owada et al. 2021
.

Remark 4.3
.

Suppose
q=1
in (
4.2
). Then, if
k=1
, the statement of Proposition
4.1

follows from Corollary 1.7 in
Janson et al. 2004
. For
k=2
,
it is easy to see that condition (
4.3
) follows from the
subcriticality condition (
4.2
), but that is no longer the case for

k\geq 3
. However, if
k=3
, one can still directly compute the value of
M_{\sigma_{3},n}^{*}(n^{-\alpha_{1}},n^{-\alpha_{2}},n^{-\alpha_{3}})
and verify the inequalities in (
4.4
), without using condition (
4.3
).
To summarize, the claim of Proposition
4.1
holds at least for
k\in\{1,2,3\}
, under the assumption (
4.2
) only. We do not know if one can deduce the same conclusion for
k\geq 4
.

Proof.

Since (
4.5
) follows from (
4.4
) and Theorem

3.3
, we only need to prove the bounds in (
4.4
).
We start with the upper bound. Recall that

M_{\sigma_{k},n}^{*}\bigl(n^{-\alpha_{1}},\ldots,n^{-\alpha_{k}}\bigr)=\min_{H:\text{ subcomplex of $\sigma_{k}$}}K_{H},

where for a subcomplex
H
of
\sigma_{k}
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle K_{H}=\max\biggl\{m\leq{n\choose k+1}:N\left(n,m{k+1\choose 2},m\binom{k+1}{3},\ldots,m\binom{k+1}{k},m;H\right)\leq\Psi_{H,n}\biggr\}.

Therefore, to prove the upper bound in (
4.4
) we only need
to detect a specific subcomplex
H
of
\sigma_{k}
, such that

(4.7)

K_{H}\leq C_{k}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}.

Let us take
H
to be the
q
-skeleton of
\sigma_{k}
. For this
H
, in
the obvious notation,

(4.8)

K_{H}=\max\biggl\{m\leq{n\choose k+1}:N\left(n,m{k+1\choose 2},\dots,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};H\right)\leq n^{k+1-{k+1\choose q+1}\alpha_{q}}\biggr\}.

By Proposition
3.1
,

(4.9)

a_{k}e^{\gamma}\leq N\left(n,m{k+1\choose 2},\dots,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};H\right)\leq b_{k}e^{\gamma}

for some
a_{k},b_{k}>0
, where

(4.10)

\displaystyle\gamma=\max\sum_{v=1}^{k+1}x_{v}

subject to

\displaystyle 0\leq x_{v}\leq\log n,\ v=1,\ldots,k+1,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\sigma_{i}}x_{v}\leq\log\bigg\{m\binom{k+1}{i+1}\bigg\},\ \ \sigma_{i}\in H_{i},\,i=1,\dots,q.

First, suppose
\binom{k+1}{q+1}m>n^{q+1}
, in which case, we have

\binom{k+1}{j+1}m>n^{j+1}
,
j=1,\dots,q
. Then,
x_{v}=\log n
,
v=1,\dots,k+1
, is easily seen to be an optimal solution to (
4.10
). It then follows from (
4.9
) that

(4.11)

N\left(n,m{k+1\choose 2},\dots,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};H\right)\geq a_{k}n^{k+1}.

However, as
\alpha_{q}>0
, there is no
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
that satisfies (
4.11
) and the inequality in (
4.8
).

Therefore, we only need to consider the case
\binom{k+1}{q+1}m\leq n^{q+1}
. Then, one can see that

x_{v}=\frac{1}{q+1}\log\bigg\{m\binom{k+1}{q+1}\bigg\},\ \ v=1,\dots,k+1,

is an optimal solution to the linear program (
4.10
), so that

\gamma=\frac{k+1}{q+1}\log\bigg\{m\binom{k+1}{q+1}\bigg\}.

Therefore, by (
4.9
),

b_{k}^{-\frac{q+1}{k+1}}\binom{k+1}{q+1}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\leq K_{H}\leq a_{k}^{-\frac{q+1}{k+1}}\binom{k+1}{q+1}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}},

and (
4.7
) follows.

We now prove the lower bound in (
4.4
). For this purpose we need to prove that for every subcomplex
H

of
\sigma_{k}
,

(4.12)

K_{H}\geq C_{k}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}.

Consider first a subcomplex
H
of dimension
1,\dots,q-1
. In this case, it is clear that
\Psi_{H,n}=n^{k+1}
, and thus,
K_{H}=\binom{n}{k+1}
and (
4.12
) trivially holds.
Consider next a subcomplex
H
of dimension
q
. Let
\bar{H}
be a
(q+1)
-uniform hypergraph on
k+1
vertices with its hyperedges identified as a
q
-simplex in
H
. Given another hypergraph
\bar{F}
, define
\bar{n}(\bar{F},\bar{H})
to be the number of unordered copies (as a hypergraph) of
\bar{H}
in
\bar{F}
. Define also

\bar{N}\left(n,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};\bar{H}\right):=\max\bigg\{\bar{n}(\bar{F},\bar{H}):v_{\bar{F}}\leq n,\ e_{\bar{F}}\leq m\binom{k+1}{q+1}\bigg\},

where
v_{\bar{F}}
is the number of vertices in
\bar{F}
and
e_{\bar{F}}
the number of hyperedges in
\bar{F}
. Then, by construction, we have

\bar{N}\left(n,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};\bar{H}\right)\geq N\left(n,m\binom{k+1}{2},\dots,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};H\right).

By virtue of this inequality together with
v_{\bar{H}}=k+1
and
e_{\bar{H}}=s_{q}(H)
,

(4.13)

\displaystyle K_{H}

\displaystyle=\max\bigg\{m\leq\binom{n}{k+1}:N\left(n,m\binom{k+1}{2},\dots,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};H\right)\leq n^{k+1-s_{q}(H)\alpha_{q}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\max\bigg\{m\leq\binom{n}{k+1}:\bar{N}\left(n,m\binom{k+1}{q+1};\bar{H}\right)\leq n^{v_{\bar{H}}}p_{q}^{e_{\bar{H}}}\bigg\}.

Observe that
\bar{H}
is seen to be a subhypergraph of a
\binom{k}{q}
-regular,
(q+1)
-uniform hypergraph. Moreover, by (
4.2
),

p_{q}=n^{-\alpha_{q}}\geq n^{-\binom{k}{q}^{-1}}>n^{-(q+1)\binom{k}{q}^{-1}}.

Hence, Proposition 4.3 in
Dudek et al. 2010
implies that the last quantity in (
4.13
) is at least

Cn^{q+1}p_{q}^{\binom{k}{q}}=Cn^{q+1-\binom{q}{k}\alpha_{q}},

for some constant
C
, as desired for (
4.12
).

Now, it remains to establish
(
4.12
) for subcomplexes
H
of dimension

k_{0}=q+1,\ldots,k
.
By (
4.6
), we need to show that there exists
C_{k}>0
such
that for any subcomplex
H
of
\sigma_{k}
on

k+1
vertices, and all
n
large enough,

\displaystyle N\left(n,\lceil C_{k}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\rceil{k+1\choose 2},\ldots,\lceil C_{k}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\rceil{k+1\choose k_{0}+1};H\right)\leq n^{k+1-\sum_{j=q}^{k_{0}}s_{j}(H)\alpha_{j}}.

It follows from Proposition
3.1
, together with the dual formulation (
3.3
),
that for a given subcomplex
H
and
n

large enough,
it is sufficient to exhibit non-negative numbers
y_{v},\,v=1,\ldots,k+1
and

z_{\sigma_{i}}^{(i)},\,\sigma_{i}\in H_{i},\,i=1,\ldots,k_{0}
, such that

(4.14)

y_{v}+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in H_{i}\atop v\in\sigma_{i}}z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}\geq 1\ \ \text{for any $v=1,\ldots,k+1$},

and

(4.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{k+1}y_{v}\log n+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in H_{i}}z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}\log\left(\lceil C_{k}^{-1}n^{q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}}\rceil{k+1\choose i+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(k+1-\sum_{j=q}^{k_{0}}s_{j}(H)\alpha_{j}\Big)\log n+B,

where
B
is a
k
-dependent constant. It is
clear that if we can choose these numbers in such a way that

(4.16)

\sum_{v=1}^{k+1}y_{v}+\Big(q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}\Big)\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in H_{i}}z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}<k+1-\sum_{j=q}^{k_{0}}s_{j}(H)\alpha_{j},

then (
4.15
) will be satisfied for large
n
, regardless of
the constant
C_{k}
above. Specifically,
we choose the numbers
(y_{v})
and
(z_{\sigma_{i}}^{(i)})
as follows.

\displaystyle y_{v}=1-\frac{s_{k_{0},v}}{s_{k_{0}}(H)},\ v=1,\ldots,k+1,

\displaystyle z^{(k_{0})}_{\sigma_{k_{0}}}=\frac{1}{s_{k_{0}}(H)},\ i=k_{0},\ \ \ z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}=0,\,i\not=k_{0},

where for a vertex
v
,
s_{k_{0},v}
is the number of
k_{0}
-simplices
in
H
to which
v
belongs. It is elementary to check that these variables satisfy the
constraints in (
4.14
) as equalities. Moreover, it is evident that

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{0}}\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in H_{i}}z^{(i)}_{\sigma_{i}}=1,

while

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{k+1}y_{v}=k+1-\frac{1}{s_{k_{0}}(H)}\sum_{v=1}^{k+1}s_{k_{0},v}=k+1-(k_{0}+1)=k-k_{0},

since every
k_{0}
-simplex contributes to exactly
k_{0}+1
vertices. Therefore, (
4.16
) reduces to

(4.17)

k-k_{0}+q+1-\binom{k}{q}\alpha_{q}<k+1-\sum_{j=q}^{k_{0}}s_{j}(H)\alpha_{j}.

Since

s_{j}(H)\leq{k+1\choose j+1},\ \ \ j=q,\ldots,k_{0},

(
4.17
) follows from (
4.3
).
∎

5.
The Betti number at the critical dimension

In this section we will use the results of Section
4

to derive large deviation results for the Betti number at the critical
dimension. We still assume that the
probabilities
(p_{i},\,i\geq 1)
are given by (
4.1
).
Recall that a dimension
k^{*}\geq 1
is called critical if

(5.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{k^{*}}{k^{*}\choose i}\alpha_{i}<1<\sum_{i=1}^{k^{*}+1}{k^{*}+1\choose i}\alpha_{i},\ \ \text{ and }\ \ q\leq k^{*};

see
Fowler 2019
;
Owada et al. 2021
. We consider the Betti
number
\beta_{k^{*},n}
at the critical dimension. By the
Morse inequalities (see e.g. Exercise 1, p. 61 in

Munkres 1984
), for any dimension
j
,

(5.2)

N_{n}(\sigma_{j})-N_{n}(\sigma_{j-1})-N_{n}(\sigma_{j+1})\leq\beta_{j,n}\leq N_{n}(\sigma_{j}),

where
N_{n}(\sigma_{j})
is the number of
j
-simplices in the
multi-parameter simplicial complex.
It follows from Proposition 3.1 in

Owada et al. 2021
that

(5.3)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[N_{n}(\sigma_{j})\bigr]\sim\frac{n^{\tau_{j}}}{(j+1)!},\ \ \ n\to\infty,

with

\tau_{j}=j+1-\sum_{i=1}^{j}{j+1\choose i+1}\alpha_{i},\ \ j=1,2,\ldots,

and the sequence
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots
reaches its unique maximum at
the critical dimension
k^{*}
. It thus follows from (
5.2
) and
(
5.3
) that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\beta_{k^{*},n}\bigr]\sim\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!},\ \ \ n\to\infty.

The following theorem considers the upper tail large deviations
for
\beta_{k^{*},n}
. An extra condition is needed.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
4.1
, assume also
that

(5.4)

\sum_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}\alpha_{i}-1\geq\frac{q(k^{*}+2)!}{(q+1)!(k^{*}+1-q)!(k^{*}+1)}\,\alpha_{q}.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist positive constants
C_{1}(\varepsilon),\,C_{2}(\varepsilon)>0
, such that

(5.5)

\exp\Big\{-C_{1}(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k^{*}}{q}\alpha_{q}}\log n\Big\}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\beta_{k^{*},n}\geq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!}\right)\leq\exp\Big\{-C_{2}(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k^{*}}{q}\alpha_{q}}\Big\}

for all
n
large enough.

Proof.

Using (
5.2
) and Proposition
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\beta_{k^{*},n}\geq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}(\sigma_{k^{*}})\geq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!}\right)\leq\exp\Bigl\{-C(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k^{*}}{q}\alpha_{q}}\Bigr\},

for some constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
.
This proves the upper bound in (
5.5
).

To prove the lower bound, let
D
be a positive constant to be determined in a
moment and take

m=\Bigl\lceil(D(1+\varepsilon))^{1/(k^{*}+1)}n^{1-\frac{1}{q+1}\binom{k^{*}}{q}\alpha_{q}}\Bigr\rceil.

Fix arbitrary
m
vertices out of
n
(say, the vertices numbered

1,\ldots,m
).
Let
A
be the event for which all
{m\choose q+1}

simplices of dimension
q
based on these
m
vertices are present in
K(n;{\bf p})
.
Given a simplicial complex, its simplex is said to be
free
if it is not in
the boundary of any simplex of a larger dimension in that complex.
Define
F_{k^{*},n}
to be the number of free
k^{*}
-simplices in
K(n;{\bf p})
.
Then, conditionally on
A
, a set of
k^{*}+1
vertices in
\{1,\dots,m\}
forms a free
k^{*}
-simplex in
K(n;{\bf p})
with probability

(5.6)

\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{m-(k^{*}+1)}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{n-m}.

Note that the probability (
5.6
) is independent of the choice of
k^{*}+1
vertices in
\{1,\dots,m\}
.
Since there are
{m\choose k^{*}+1}
such potential free
k^{*}
-simplices in
\{1,\dots,m\}
, it follows from
Lemma 3.3 in
Janson et al. 2004
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(F_{k^{*},n}\geq\frac{1}{2}{m\choose k^{*}+1}\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{m-(k^{*}+1)}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{n-m}\,\bigg|\,A\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{m-(k^{*}+1)}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{n-m}.

It follows from (
5.4
) and the criticality condition (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\Big(1-\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{m-(k^{*}+1)}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{n-m}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(1-n^{-\sum_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}+1}\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}\alpha_{i}}\Big)^{m}\Big(1-n^{-\sum_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}\alpha_{i}}\Big)^{n}

is bounded away from
0
(by some constant, say,
\rho
). Thus, choosing
D>6/\rho
, one can obtain the following bound:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{m\choose k^{*}+1}\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{m-(k^{*}+1)}\Big(1-\prod_{i=q}^{k^{*}+1}p_{i}^{\binom{k^{*}+1}{i}}\Big)^{n-m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho m^{k^{*}+1}}{3(k^{*}+1)!}\,\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}\geq\frac{D\rho(1+\varepsilon)}{3(k^{*}+1)!}\,n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}>2(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!}.

Now, combining all of these results gives us

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(F_{k^{*},n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho}{4}\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}\mathbb{P}(A)=\frac{\rho}{4}\prod_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}p_{i}^{{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}}p_{q}^{\binom{m}{q+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{4}\exp\left\{-\left(\sum_{i=q+1}^{k^{*}}{k^{*}+1\choose i+1}\alpha_{i}+{m\choose q+1}\alpha_{q}\right)\log n\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho}{4}\exp\left\{-C(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k^{*}}{q}\alpha_{q}}\log n\right\},

for some constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
.

Note that the indicator function of each free
k^{*}
-simplex is an
independent
k^{*}
-dimensional cocycle. If we denote by
\gamma_{k^{*},n}

the rank of the group of
k^{*}
-dimensional cocycles in
K(n;{\bf p})
, then we have proved that for some constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
,

(5.7)

\mathbb{P}\left(\gamma_{k^{*},n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}}{(k^{*}+1)!}\right)\geq\frac{\rho}{4}\exp\left\{-C(\varepsilon)n^{q+1-\binom{k^{*}}{q}\alpha_{q}}\log n\right\}.

In order to deduce the lower bound in (
5.5
) from
(
5.7
), we only need to show that the rank of the group
of
k^{*}
-dimensional coboundaries in
K(n;{\bf p})
exceeds
(1+\varepsilon)n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}/(k^{*}+1)!
only on
an event
whose probability is of smaller order than the probability in
(
5.7
). The rank of the latter group does not exceed
the rank of the group of
(k^{*}-1)
-dimensional cochains in
K(n;{\bf p})
, which is, of course, equal to the number
N_{n}(\sigma_{k^{*}-1})
of

(k^{*}-1)
-simplices in
K(n;{\bf p})
.

We know from Proposition 3.1 in
Owada et al. 2021
that

\mathbb{E}[N_{n}(\sigma_{k^{*}-1})]\sim\frac{n^{\tau_{k^{*}-1}}}{k^{*}!}=o\bigl(n^{\tau_{k^{*}}}\bigr),\ \ \ n\to\infty,

by the criticality of the dimension
k^{*}
. It
remains to notice that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(N_{n}(\sigma_{k^{*}-1})\geq(1+\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[N_{n}(\sigma_{k^{*}-1})]\bigr)\leq\exp\bigl\{-Cn^{q+1-\binom{k^{*}-1}{q}\alpha_{q}}\bigr\}

by the upper bound in (
4.5
). The right-hand side above exhibits a smaller order
than the probability in (
5.7
), as required.
∎

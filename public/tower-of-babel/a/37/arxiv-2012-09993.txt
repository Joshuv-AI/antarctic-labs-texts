T T -convex valued fields with tempered exponentiation
Yimu Yin

Abstract
. We continue the effort of grokking the structure of power-bounded T T -convex valued fields, whose theory is in general referred to as TCVF \TCVF . In the present paper our focus is on certain expansion of it that is equipped with a tempered exponential function beyond the valuation ring. In order to construct such a tempered exponential function, the signed value group Γ \Gamma is also converted into a model of T T plus exponentiation and is in fact identified with (a section of) the residue field via the composition of a diagonal cross-section and an angular component map. In a sense, the resulting universal theory TKVF \TKVF is a halfway point between power-bounded TCVF \TCVF and exponential TCVF \TCVF . This theory is reasonably well-behaved. In particular, we show that it admits quantifier elimination in a natural language, a notion of dimension, a generalized Euler characteristic, etc.

Theorem 2.5
.

\TCVF^{\flat}
admits quantifier elimination.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{M}\models\TCVF^{\flat}
,
\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{M}
, and
\mathbb{U}\models\TCVF^{\flat}
be highly saturated. Suppose that there is an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}^{\flat}
-embedding
\sigma:\mathbb{S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{U}
. All we need to do is to pass the Shoenfield test, that is, to extend
\sigma
to an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}^{\flat}
-embedding
\mathbb{M}\longrightarrow\mathbb{U}
.

To that end, consider any element
a\in K(\mathbb{S})\smallsetminus{\ac}{\pi}(\Gamma(\mathbb{S}))
. Let
\alpha={\ac}{\pi}^{-1}(a)
and
\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp}
be the
T_{\exp}
-submodel of
\Gamma(\mathbb{M})
generated by
\Gamma(\mathbb{S})
and
\alpha
. Then
{\ac}{\pi}(\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp})
is the
T_{\exp}
-submodel
\langle{\ac}{\pi}(\Gamma(\mathbb{S})),a\rangle_{\exp}
of
K(\mathbb{S})
. We may tentatively extend
\sigma
by a
T_{\exp}
-embedding
\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp}\longrightarrow\Gamma(\mathbb{U})
, sending
\alpha
to
{\ac}{\pi}^{-1}(\sigma(a))
. Let
\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma}
be the
\TCVF
-submodel of
\mathbb{M}
generated by
\mathbb{S}
and
\pi(\alpha)
; so
\VF(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})
is just the
T
-submodel of
\mathbb{M}
generated by
\mathbb{S}
and
\pi(\alpha)
. By the Wilkie inequality
[
1
, § 5]
, we have that
K(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})=K(\mathbb{S})
and
\Gamma(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})
is the subgroup of
\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp}
generated by
\alpha
over
\Gamma(\mathbb{S})
. The latter equality implies that
\pi(\Gamma(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma}))
is the subgroup of
\pi(\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp})
generated by
\pi(\alpha)
over
\pi(\Gamma(\mathbb{S}))
and hence is indeed contained in
\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma}
, also
{\ac}{\pi}(\Gamma(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma}))
is the subgroup of
K(\mathbb{S})
generated by
a
over
{\ac}{\pi}(\Gamma(\mathbb{S}))
. The proof of quantifier elimination in
T_{\textup{convex}}

[
3
, Theorem 3.10]
then gives an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}
-extension
\sigma_{\alpha}:\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma}\longrightarrow\mathbb{U}
of
\sigma
with

\sigma_{\alpha}(\pi(\alpha))=\pi({\ac}{\pi}^{-1}(\sigma(a)))=\pi(\sigma_{\alpha}(\alpha))\quad\text{and hence}\quad\ac(\sigma_{\alpha}(\pi(\alpha)))=\sigma(a)=\sigma_{\alpha}({\ac}{\pi}(\alpha)).

It follows that, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})
,

\sigma_{\alpha}(\pi(\gamma))=\pi(\sigma_{\alpha}(\gamma))\quad\text{and}\quad\ac(\sigma_{\alpha}(\pi(\gamma)))=\sigma_{\alpha}({\ac}{\pi}(\gamma)).

For any
a\in\VF^{\times}(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})
with
\vv(a)=\gamma
, we have

\sigma_{\alpha}(\ac(a))=\sigma_{\alpha}(\ac(\pi(\gamma))\ac(a/\pi(\gamma)))=\ac(\sigma_{\alpha}(\pi(\gamma)))\ac(\sigma_{\alpha}(a/\pi(\gamma)))=\ac(\sigma_{\alpha}(a)).

So
\sigma_{\alpha}
is almost an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}^{\flat}
-extension of
\sigma
, except that
\Gamma(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})
is not a
T_{\exp}
-model (not even a
T
-model for that matter). But we can repeat the above construction for any
\alpha^{\prime}\in\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp}\smallsetminus\Gamma(\langle\mathbb{S},\pi(\alpha)\rangle_{T\Gamma})
, and so on. Eventually we shall obtain an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}
-extension
\sigma^{\prime}:\mathbb{S}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathbb{U}
of
\sigma
with
\Gamma(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})=\langle\Gamma(\mathbb{S}),\alpha\rangle_{\exp}
and
K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})=K(\mathbb{S})
, which then must be an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}^{\flat}
-extension as well. Keep going if there is an
a^{\prime}\in K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})\smallsetminus{\ac}{\pi}(\Gamma(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}))
, we may assume that
K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})={\ac}{\pi}(\Gamma(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}))
and hence
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
is a
\TCVF^{\flat}
-model.

Now we may consider any
a\in K(\mathbb{M})\smallsetminus K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})
. By an almost identical construction (quantifier elimination in
T_{\textup{convex}}
and the Wilkie inequality are applied twice here, once to all
b\in\langle K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}),a\rangle_{\exp}
and then — in this order — as above, to all
\gamma\in{\ac}{\pi}^{-1}(\langle K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime}),a\rangle_{\exp})
), we may assume
K(\mathbb{M})=K(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})
and hence
\Gamma(\mathbb{M})=\Gamma(\mathbb{S}^{\prime})
. From this point on, any
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}
-extension of
\sigma
is an
\mathcal{L}_{T\Gamma\textup{}}^{\flat}
-extension of
\sigma
and hence we are done by quantifier elimination in
T_{\textup{convex}}
.
∎

Theorem 2.13
.

\TKVF
is complete, has a prime model, and admits quantifier elimination.

Proof.

Given any
\TKVF
-model
\mathbb{M}
there is a
\TCVF^{\flat}
-model
\mathbb{M}^{\flat}
such that if we interpret the function
\vv
in
\mathbb{M}
as the function
{\ac}{\pi}\circ\vv
in
\mathbb{M}^{\flat}
then
\mathbb{M}^{\flat}
admits a
0
-interpretation of
\mathbb{M}
via the identity map on the
\VF
-sort. Moreover, if
\mathbb{N}
is a
\TKVF
-submodel of
\mathbb{M}
then
\mathbb{N}^{\flat}
is a
\TCVF^{\flat}
-submodel of
\mathbb{M}^{\flat}
. This shows that the corresponding
\TKVF
-model that the prime
\TCVF^{\flat}
-model interprets is indeed the prime
\TKVF
-model. Since a substructure of
\mathbb{M}

\TKVF

\TCVF^{\flat}

\TKVF

\TKVF

\TKVF
-model, which implies that
\TKVF

∎

Definition 4.15
.

Let
A\subseteq A^{\prime}\subseteq\VF^{n}
and
B\subseteq B^{\prime}\subseteq\VF^{n^{\prime}}
be definable sets. Suppose that, for some
k\leq\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}
,
A\subseteq\VF^{k}\times\KD^{n-k}
and
B\subseteq\VF^{k}\times\KD^{n^{\prime}-k}
. For
i\leq k
let
\tilde{i}=\{1,\ldots,k\}\smallsetminus\{i\}
. We say that a definable bijection
G:A^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
with
G(A)=B
is
relatively unary in the
(i,j)
-coordinate
if
G(x)=x
for
x\in A^{\prime}\smallsetminus A
and
(\pr_{\tilde{j}}\circ G)(x)=\pr_{\tilde{i}}(x)
for
x\in A
. If, in addition,
G\upharpoonright A_{a}
is also a special bijection for every
a\in\pr_{\tilde{i}}(A)
then
G
is
relatively special
in the
(i,j)
-coordinate.

Alternatively, for simplicity, we may take
i=j
in this definition and stipulate that the condition in question holds up to coordinate permutation.

\RV
-polydisc).

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
A\subseteq\VF^{k}\times\KD^{m}
,
B\subseteq\VF^{k}\times\KD^{m^{\prime}}
, and
f:A\longrightarrow B
be a definable bijection. Then there is a definable finite partition

of
A
such that each
f\upharpoonright A_{i}
is a composition of relatively unary bijections.

Proof.

Let
p
be a
K
-partition of (the graph of)
f
. Then we may view each
p^{-1}(a)
as an
a
-
\mathcal{L}_{T\textup{RV}}
-definable bijection between a subset of
\VF^{k}\times\RV^{m}
and a subset of
\VF^{k}\times\RV^{m^{\prime}}
. Since the bijection on
A
given by
a\longmapsto(a,p(a,f(a)))
is relatively unary in all the relevant coordinates, we see that the claim follows from
[
11
, Lemma 5.24]
and compactness.
∎

Observe that if a bijection is piecewise (over a definable finite partition) a composition of relatively unary bijections then it is indeed a composition of relatively unary bijections.

Let
A\subseteq\VF^{n}
be a definable set and
T_{i}:A\longrightarrow A^{\sharp}
a definable bijection that is relatively special in the
(i,i)
-coordinate (up to coordinate permutation). Suppose that the “
\KD
-coordinates” of
A
are passed on to
A^{\sharp}
via
T_{i}
in the obvious way (the simplest example is that
T_{i}
is the regularization of
A
). Moreover, for all
a\in A_{\tilde{i}}
(here
\tilde{i}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\smallsetminus\{i\}
), the image
T_{i}(A_{a})=A_{a}^{\sharp}
of the fiber
A_{a}\subseteq A
over
a
is an
\RV
-pullback over
a
. Let

A_{-i}=\bigcup_{a\in A_{\tilde{i}}}a\times\lg(A_{a}^{\sharp})\subseteq\VF^{n-1}\times\KD^{m_{i}},

which we think of as obtained from
A^{\sharp}
by “
\lg
-contracting” the coordinate in question. Note that, under Convention
3.1
,
A^{\sharp}
can be fully recovered from
A_{-i}
. Let
\hat{T}_{i}:A\longrightarrow A_{-i}
be the function induced by
T_{i}
.

For any suitable
j\leq n-1
, we repeat the above setup on
A_{-i}
with respect to the
j
-coordinate, and thereby obtain a definable set
A_{-j}\subseteq\VF^{n-2}\times\RV^{m_{j}}
and a function
\hat{T}_{j}:A_{-i}\longrightarrow A_{-j}
that is induced by a relatively special bijection
T_{j}:A^{\sharp}\longrightarrow A^{\sharp\sharp}
(the coordinate in
A^{\sharp}
that gets “
\lg
-contracted” in
A_{-i}
is simply carried along by
T_{j}
). Continuing thus, a sequence of such bijections

and a corresponding function
\hat{T}_{\sigma}:A\longrightarrow\KD^{l}
result, where
\sigma
is the permutation of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
in question. The composition
T_{\sigma(n)}\circ\ldots\circ T_{\sigma(1)}
, which is referred to as the
lift
of
\hat{T}_{\sigma}
, is denoted by
T_{\sigma}
. Note that
T^{-1}_{\sigma}
is
\rv
-contractible. Also, since
T_{\sigma}(A)
is an
\RV
-pullback, the set
\hat{T}_{\sigma}(A)
may be regarded as an object
\coprod_{i\leq n}\hat{T}_{\sigma}(A)_{i}\in\Lambda[\leq\!n]
such that
(b,a)\in\hat{T}_{\sigma}(A)_{i}
if and only if there is an
\RV
-polydisc
\mathfrak{p}\times a\subseteq T_{\sigma}(A)
with
\lg(\mathfrak{p})=b
.

Definition 4.18
.

The definable function
\hat{T}_{\sigma}
, or the object
\hat{T}_{\sigma}(A)\in\Lambda[\leq\!n]
, is called a
standard contraction
of
A
.

By Corollary
3.5

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\VF^{n}\times\KD^{m}
be definable sets with
\pr_{\leq n}(A^{\prime})=\pr_{\leq n}(A^{\prime\prime})=A

\dim(A)=l

,
(A^{\prime}_{a},A^{\prime\prime}_{a})\in\isp[k]
. Let
\hat{T}_{\sigma}
,

be standard contractions of

,

(\hat{T}_{\sigma}(A^{\prime}),\hat{R}_{\sigma}(A^{\prime\prime}))\in\isp[k{+}l].

Note that the condition

is construed over the substructure
\mathbb{S}\langle a\rangle
.

Proof.

By induction on
n
this is immediately reduced to the case
n=1
. Let
f
be the definable function on
A
given by
a\longmapsto(\textup{sig}([A^{\prime}_{a}]),\textup{sig}([A^{\prime\prime}_{a}]))
(in fact, by compactness, the range of

is finite). Let
\phi^{\prime}
,
\phi^{\prime\prime}
be quantifier-free formulas that define
A^{\prime}
,
A^{\prime\prime}
. Applying Theorem
3.8
to
f
and the top terms of
\phi^{\prime}
,
\phi^{\prime\prime}
(for the first clause below Lemma
3.7
suffices, but for the second clause the stronger Theorem
3.8
is needed), we obtain a special bijection
F:A\longrightarrow A^{\sharp}
such that
A^{\sharp}
is an
\RV
-pullback and, for all
\RV
-polydisc
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq A^{\sharp}
,

•

(f\circ F^{-1})(\mathfrak{p})
is a singleton,

•

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in F^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})
,
A^{\prime}_{a_{1}}=A^{\prime}_{a_{2}}
and
A^{\prime\prime}_{a_{1}}=A^{\prime\prime}_{a_{2}}
.

Let

, similarly for
B^{\prime\prime}
. Note that
B^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime\prime}
are obtained via special bijections on

\RV

Proposition 4.25
.

For

[\mathbb{L}U]=[\mathbb{L}V]\quad\text{if and only if}\quad([U],[V])\in\isp.

In light of this description, Proposition

Theorem 4.26
.

For each
n\geq 0
there is a canonical isomorphism of Grothendieck semigroups

\int_{+}:\gsk\VF_{n}\longrightarrow\gsk\Lambda[\leq\!n]/\isp

such that

\int_{+}[A]=[U]/\isp\quad\text{if and only if}\quad[A]=[\mathbb{L}U].

Putting these together, we obtain a canonical isomorphism of Grothendieck semirings

\int_{+}:\gsk\VF_{*}\longrightarrow\gsk\Lambda[*]/\isp\cong\mathds{O}\llcorner\mathds{O}

and, upon groupification, a canonical isomorphism of Grothendieck rings

\int:\ggk\VF_{*}\longrightarrow\ggk\Lambda[*]/\isp\cong\mathds{Z}.

Abstract
. A smooth curve on G / H G/H is called a Riemannian equigeodesic if it is a homogeneous geodesic for all G G -invariant Riemannian metrics on G / H G/H . With the G G -invariant Riemannian metric replaced by other classes of G G -invariant metrics, we can similarly define Finsler equigeodesic, Randers equigeodesic, ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) equigeodesic, etc. In this paper, we study Randers and ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) equigeodesics. For a compact homogeneous manifold, we prove Randers and ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) equigeodesics are equivalent, and find a criterion for them. Using this criterion we can classify the equigeodesics on many compact homogeneous manifolds which permit non-Riemannian homogeneous Randers metrics, including four classes of homogeneous spheres. Mathematics Subject Classification(2010): 53C22, 53C30, 53C60. Keywords: equigeodesic, equigeodesic vector, homogeneous ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) metric, homogeneous gedesics, homogeneous manifold, homogeneous Randers metric

Homogeneous geodesic and equigeodesic in Riemannian and Finsler geometry

Equigeodesic was introduced by N. Cohen, L. Grama and C.J.C. Negreiros in 2010

A smooth curve on a homogeneous space

is called an

a homogeneous geodesic for any
G

Riemannian, Finsler, Randers,

1.3.
Main results

In this paper, we mainly discuss Randers and
(\alpha,\beta)
equigeodesics. Firstly, we prove they are equivalent for a compact homogeneous manifold.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G/H
be a homogeneous manifold with a compact Lie group
(G,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}})
and the corresponding orthogonal reductive decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}
can be further
\mathrm{Ad}(H)
-invariantly decomposed as
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}+\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}

\mathfrak{m}_{0}\neq 0

\mathrm{Ad}(H)
-action on

, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

X
is an

(\alpha,\beta)
equigeodesic vector;

(2)

X
is a Randers equigeodesic vector;

(3)

X
is a Riemannian equigeodesic vector and
[X,\mathfrak{m}_{0}]\subset\mathfrak{h}
.

As applications of Theorem
1.1

(\alpha,\beta)
equigeodesic vectors on some compact homogeneous manifolds.
To avoid iteration,
(\alpha,\beta)
equigeodesic or

(\alpha,\beta)
equigeodesic vector will not be mentioned later.
We only concern those homogeneous manifolds which admits non-Riemannian homogeneous Randers metrics, otherwise there is no new story.

Let

Let

Let

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}

We decompose
\mathfrak{m}

\mathrm{Ad}(H)
-equivariantly as

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{0}+\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
, in which
\mathfrak{m}_{0}

is the fixed point set for the
\mathrm{Ad}(H)
-action on
\mathfrak{m}

Any
G

(\alpha,\beta)

F(y)=\alpha(y,y)^{1/2}\varphi(\tfrac{\alpha(y,u)}{\alpha(y,y)^{1/2}}).

Here

\mathrm{Ad}(H)
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{m}

\mathfrak{m}_{0}

with the inequality

\varphi(s)-s\varphi^{\prime}(s)+(b^{2}-s^{2})\varphi^{\prime\prime}(s)>0

satisfied everywhere. In particular, this homogeneous metric

is Randers when
\varphi(s)=1+s
, which is of the form

(2.4)

F(y)=\alpha(y,y)^{1/2}+\alpha(y,u).

A vector

is called a

for
(G/H,F)
if
c(t)=\exp tX\cdot o
is a homogeneous geodesic. When

is

(\alpha,\beta)
or Randers, we have the following criterion for geodesic vectors (see Proposition 3.4 in
[

]

Lemma 2.1
.

Keep all assumptions and notations in this subsection. Then

is a geodesic vector of the homogeneous
(\alpha,\beta)

metric

in (
2.3
) if and only if
X\notin\mathfrak{h}
and

(2.5)

\alpha([X,Z]_{\mathfrak{m}},(\varphi(s)-s\varphi^{\prime}(s))X_{\mathfrak{m}}+\varphi^{\prime}(s)\alpha(X_{\mathfrak{m}},X_{\mathfrak{m}})^{1/2}u)=0,\quad\forall Z\in\mathfrak{m},

where

.
In particular, when
\varphi(s)=1+s
, (

(2.6)

\alpha([X,Z]_{\mathfrak{m}},X_{\mathfrak{m}}+\sqrt{\alpha(X_{\mathfrak{m}},X_{\mathfrak{m}})}u)=0.

See

8

\displaystyle 0=\alpha([X,\mathfrak{m}]_{\mathfrak{m}},u)=\langle[X,\mathfrak{m}],\Lambda(u)\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}}=\langle\mathfrak{m},[X,\Lambda(u)]\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}},

G

\displaystyle\alpha([X,\mathfrak{m}]_{\mathfrak{m}},u)=\langle[X,\mathfrak{m}],\Lambda(u)\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}}=\langle\mathfrak{m},[X,\Lambda(u)]\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}}\subset\langle\mathfrak{m},[X,\mathfrak{m}_{0}]\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}}\subset\langle\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{h}\rangle_{\mathrm{bi}}=0.

Malak Lafi and Artem Zvavitch

Abstract
. We study a version of the Busemann-Petty problem for log \log -concave measures with an additional assumption on the dilates of convex, symmetric bodies. One of our main tools is an analog of the classical large deviation principle applied to log \log -concave measures, depending on the norm of a convex body. We hope this will be of independent interest.

Primary: 52A20, 52A21; Secondary: 46T12, 60F10

1.
Introduction

We denote by
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space equipped with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and the standard orthonormal basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
, and denote by
|\cdot|
the standard Euclidean norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For a measurable set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we refer to its volume (the Lebesgue measure) by
|A|,
and its boundary by
\partial A.
The notation
B_{2}^{n}
stands for the closed unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n},
and
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
for the unit sphere, i.e.
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}=\partial B_{2}^{n}
. A convex body is a convex, compact set with a nonempty interior. Furthermore, a convex body
K
is symmetric if
K=-K
. A measure
\mu
is
\log
-concave on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
if for every pair of non-empty compact sets
A
and
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<\lambda<1
we have

\mu(\lambda A+(1-\lambda)B)\geq\mu(A)^{\lambda}\mu(B)^{1-\lambda},

where the addition is the Minkowski sum which is defined as the set
A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\},
and the constant multiple (dilation) of a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
is defined as
\alpha A=\{\alpha a:a\in A\}
. It follows from the Prékopa-Leindler inequality
[
18
,
19
,
9
]
, that if a measure
\mu
that is defined on the measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is generated by a
\log
-concave density, then
\mu
is also
\log
-concave. Furthermore, Borell provides a characterization for
\log
-concave measures
[
3
]
, precisely a locally finite and regular Borel measure
\mu
is
\log
-concave, if and only if, its density (with respect to the Lebesgue on the
appropriate subspace) is
\log
-concave.

In 1956, Busemann and Petty
[
5
]
posed the following volume comparison problem:
Let
K
and
L
be symmetric convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume of every central hyperplane section of
K
is smaller than the same section for
L
. Does it follow that the
n
-dimensional volume of
K
is smaller than the
n
-dimensional volume of
L
? In the late 90’s, the Busemann-Petty problem was solved as a result of many works
[
8
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
21
]
. The answer is affirmative when
n\leq 4,
and negative whenever
n\geq 5
. It is natural to consider an analog of the Busemann-Petty problem for a more general class of measures. The first result in this direction was a solution of the Gaussian analog of the Busemann-Petty problem
[
22
]
. It turns out that the answer for the Busemann-Petty problem is the same if we replace the volume with the Gaussian measure. Moreover, it was proved in
[
23
]
that the answer is the same if we replace the volume with any measure with continuous, positive, and even density.

V. Milman
[
16
]
asked whether the answer to the Gaussian Busemann-Petty problem would change in a positive direction if we compared not only the Gaussian measure of sections of the bodies but also the Gaussian measure of sections of their dilates, that is, consider two convex symmetric bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

\gamma_{n-1}(rK\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\gamma_{n-1}(rL\cap\xi^{\perp}),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0,

where
\xi^{\perp}
denotes the central hyperplane perpendicular to
\xi.
Does it follow that

\gamma_{n}(K)\leq\gamma_{n}(L)?

Here
\gamma_{n}
denotes the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\gamma_{n-1}(K\cap\xi^{\perp})=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n-1}}\int_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}}dx.

The addition of dilation to the Busemann-Petty problem, clearly, would not change anything in the case of the volume measure. Still in the case of more general
\log
-concave measures the behavior of the measure of a dilation of a convex body is very interesting, we refer to
[
1
,
6
,
12
,
15
]
for just a few examples of such results.

Even though the dilation adds some strength to the condition of the bodies, the answer to the dilation problem for Gaussian measure is positive for
n\leq 4
and negative for
n\geq 7
(see
[
24
]
). That leaves the problem open for
n=5,6.

To show the strength of the condition of the dilates, it was proved in
[
24
]
that the dilation problem has an aﬃrmative answer when
K
is a dilate of a centered Euclidean ball: Consider a star body
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and assume there exists
R>0,
such that

\gamma_{n-1}(rRB_{2}^{n}\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\gamma_{n-1}(rL\cap\xi^{\perp}),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0

then it follows that
RB_{2}^{n}\subseteq L.

In this paper, we review some generalizations of the above fact. In particular, we study measures
\mu
for which we have an affirmative answer for the following problem:

Question 1
.

Consider a convex, symmetric body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that for every
t
large enough and for some
R>0

\mu(tRB_{2}^{n})\leq\mu(tK),

does it follow that
RB_{2}^{n}\subseteq K?

In Section
2
, we will present a solution for Question
1
for the case of a
\log
-concave, rotation invariant probability measure
\mu.

In Section
3
, we consider a more general case, instead of comparing
K
with
B_{2}^{n}
, we will compare
K
with another convex, symmetric body
L
. Let us denote by
\|x\|_{L}
the Minkowski functional of
L
which is defined to be
\|x\|_{L}=\min\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda L\}.

Question 2
.

Let
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex, symmetric bodies, and let
\mu
be a
\log
-concave probability measure, with density
e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}
, where
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an increasing convex function. If for every
t
large enough and some
R>0

\mu(tRL)\leq\mu(tK),

does it follow that
RL\subseteq K
?

One of the core steps in answering Questions
1
and
2
is a generalization of the classical large deviation principle, which is provided in Lemma
2
and equation (
11
) below: Consider two symmetric, convex bodies
K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let
r(K,L)=\max\{R>0:RL\subset K\}
, then

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(r(K,L)t)}=-1,

where
\mu
is a
\log
-concave probability measure, with density
e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}
and by
A^{c}
we denote a complement of a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},
i.e.

A^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus A
.

Finally, in Section
4
, we will discuss the generalization of the dilation problem for Gaussian measures:

Question 3
.

Consider a measure
\mu
with continuous positive density
f
. Let
\mu_{n-1}(K\cap\xi^{\perp})=\int_{K\cap\xi^{\perp}}f(x)dx.
Consider two convex symmetric bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

\mu_{n-1}(rK\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\mu_{n-1}(rL\cap\xi^{\perp}),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0,

does it follow that

\mu(K)\leq\mu(L)?

We show that in general, the answer is still negative in dimension
n\geq 5
, even under the assumption that
f
is non-constant
\log
-concave function. We also prove that if we add the requirement for the measure to be rotation invariant, the answer will be negative in dimension
n\geq 7
, which leaves the case of rotation invariant
\log
-concave measures open in dimension
n=5,6.

2.
The case of rotation invariant measures

In this section we consider a rotation invariant probability
\log
-concave measure
\mu
with non-constant density, i.e.

\mu(A)=\int_{A}e^{-\phi(|x|)}dx,

where
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an increasing convex function. We will denote by
\phi^{\prime}(t)
the left derivative, in the case when the convex function
\phi(t)
is not differentiable at
t
.

Theorem 1
.

Consider a convex, symmetric body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that for every
t
large enough and some
R>0

\mu(tRB_{2}^{n})\leq\mu(tK),

then
RB_{2}^{n}\subseteq K.

In order to prove the above theorem, we will need two lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Consider
R>0,
then

(1)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tRB_{2}^{n})^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}=-1.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

R=1
. Let us first show that the left-hand side of equality (
1
) is less or equal to
-1
.
Writing the integral in polar coordinates, we get

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}r^{n-1}drd\theta}}{\phi(t)}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}r^{n-1}dr}}{\phi(t)},

we remind that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\phi(t)=\infty.

Let
\eta(t)=-(n-1)\ln{t}+\phi(t)
. Using that
\phi
is a convex and non-constant function, we get that there exists
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})>0
. Thus
\phi^{\prime}(t)>0,
for all
t>t_{0}
and there exists constant
a>0
such that
\eta^{\prime}(t)>a,
for all
t>t_{0}
. Thus,
\eta(r)\geq\eta(t)+a(r-t)
, for
r>t>t_{0}
, and

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}r^{n-1}dr}}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\eta(t)-a(r-t)}dr}}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{e^{-\eta(t)}}+\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-a(r-t)}dr}}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln(t^{n-1}e^{-\phi(t)})+\ln{\frac{1}{a}}}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=-1+\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{a}}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=-1.

Next, we will show that the right-hand side of equality (
1
) is greater or equal to
-1
. Since
r>t
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}r^{n-1}dr}}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\left(t^{n-1}\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr\right)}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}}{\phi(t)}.

To finish proving the lemma, we prove the following claim.

Claim 1
.

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}}{\phi(t)}\geq{-1}.

Proof of Claim
1
.
Assume the result is not true, then there exists
\alpha>1
such that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}}{\phi(t)}<{-\alpha}.

Thus, there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for all
t>t_{0}
we have,

(2)

\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr\leq e^{-\alpha\phi(t)}.

Let
F(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr
, note that
F^{\prime}(t)=-e^{-\phi(t)}
and thus (
2
) is equivalent to

F(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq-F^{\prime}(t).

Therefore for
t>t_{0}
we have

1\leq-\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

Integrating both sides of the above inequality over
t\in[t_{0},\infty)
, we get that
\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}F(t)^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
is unbounded, which gives a contradiction and the claim is proved. This finishes the proof of Lemma
1
.
∎

Remark 1
.

We note that in Claim
1
we have proved a stronger statement. Indeed, fix
\alpha>1
and let

E=\left\{t:\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}<-\alpha\phi(t)\right\}

then
|E|<\infty
. This follows from the fact that, again, for all
t\in E
we have that

1<-\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}},

thus

(3)

|E|\leq\int_{E}-\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}dt\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}-\frac{F^{\prime}(t)}{F(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}dt<\infty.

Remark 2
.

It is tempting to replace limit superior by the actual limit in the statement of Lemma
1
. This may be done in many particular cases of measure
\mu
, but it is not true in general. Indeed, if we assume that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}}{\phi(t)}={-1}.

then there exits
T>0
such that for all
t>T
we have

\left|\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}}{\phi(t)}+1\right|\leq 1.

In particular

(4)

\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-(\phi(r)-\phi(t))}dr\geq e^{-\phi(t)}.

Using convexity of
\phi
we get that
\phi(r)-\phi(t)\geq\phi^{\prime}(t)(r-t)
and thus, combining this with (
4
) we get that

(5)

\phi^{\prime}(t)\leq e^{\phi(t)},\mbox{ for all }t>T.

Let us show that there is an increasing,
positive, convex, piecewise quadratic function
\phi
which has sufficiently large derivative at a sequence
of points
t_{k}\to\infty,
such
\phi
would contradict (
5
).

We define function
\phi
to be quadratic on each interval
[k,k+1]
and show that there exist
t_{k}\in(k,k+1)
, for all
k\in\{0,1,\dots\},
which would contradict (
5
). Let
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=1
. Assume we have constructed desired function
\phi
on interval
[0,k]
with
\phi(k)=a_{k},\phi^{\prime}(k)=b_{k}
. Consider an auxiliary quadratic function
\phi_{k}:[k,\infty)\to[a_{k},\infty)
, such that
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)=\alpha_{k}(t-k)+b_{k},
where
\alpha_{k}>0
to be selected later. Thus,
\phi_{k}(t)=\alpha_{k}(t-k)^{2}/2+b_{k}(t-k)+a_{k}
. Our goal is to find
t_{k}\in(k,k+1)
and
\alpha_{k}
such that

\alpha_{k}(t-k)+b_{k}>e^{\alpha_{k}(t-k)^{2}/2+b_{k}(t-k)+a_{k}}.

Let
t_{k}=k+1/\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
. Then the previous inequality becomes

\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}+b_{k}>e^{1/2+b_{k}/\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}+a_{k}}

which is true for all
\alpha_{k}
large enough (in particular allows us to guarantee that
t_{k}\in(k,k+1)
). We now set
\phi(t)=\phi_{k}(t)
, for
t\in[k,k+1]
and repeat the process for the interval
[k+1,k+2]
.

We remind that for two convex, symmetric bodies
K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we define
r(K,L)=\max\{R>0:RL\subset K\}
.
The next lemma may be seen as a generalization of the classical large deviation principle (see, for example, Corollary 4.9.3 in
[
2
]
).

Lemma 2
.

Consider a symmetric body
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, then

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(r(K,B_{2}^{n})t)}=-1.

Proof.

Let
R=r(K,B_{2}^{n})
, then
(tK)^{c}\subset(tRB_{2}^{n})^{c}
. Using Lemma
1
we get

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tRB_{2}^{n})^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}=-1.

To obtain the reverse inequality, we denote by
P
a plank of width
2R
which contains
K
. More precisely, using the maximality of
R
there exists at least two tangent points
y,-y\in R{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap\partial K
, thus we may consider
P=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq R\}
. Next,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tP)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}.

By the rotation invariant of
\mu
, we may assume that
y=Re_{n}
, and so

\mu((tP)^{c})=2\int_{tR}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{-\phi(|ze_{n}+x|)}dxdz.

Using the triangle inequality and the polar coordinates we get

\displaystyle\mu((tP)^{c})

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{tR}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{-\phi(z+|x|)}dxdz

\displaystyle=2\int_{tR}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(z+r)}r^{n-2}drd\theta dz

\displaystyle=2|{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}\int_{tR}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(z+r)}dzdr

\displaystyle=2|{\mathbb{S}^{n-2}}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-2}\int_{tR+r}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(z)}dzdr

\displaystyle=2|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|\int_{tR}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(z)}\int_{0}^{z-tR}r^{n-2}drdz

\displaystyle=2\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|}{n-1}\int_{tR}^{\infty}(z-tR)^{n-1}e^{-\phi(z)}dz.

Now to finish the proof of Lemma 2 we need to prove the following claim.

Claim 2
.

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{tR}^{\infty}(z-tR)^{m}e^{-\phi(z)}dz}}{\phi(Rt)}\geq-1,

for any non-negative integer
m
.

Proof of Claim
2
.

Making the change of variables we get

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{tR}^{\infty}(z-tR)^{m}e^{-\phi(z)}dz}}{\phi(Rt)}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}(r-t)^{m}e^{-\phi(r)}dr}}{\phi(t)}.

We will first prove the following inductive step: fix a non-negative integer
m
, and let

F_{m}(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}(r-t)^{m}e^{-\phi(r)}dr,

then

(6)

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln F_{m}(t)}{\ln F_{m-1}(t)}=1,\mbox{ for all }m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We note that
F_{m}(t)\leq 1
, for
t
large enough, and thus the denominator and numerator are negative. It is a bit easier to work with a fraction when both the denominator and numerator are non-negative. So we will prove that

\liminf\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\ln F_{m}(t)}{-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}=1.

Using integration by parts, we get

F_{m-1}(t)=\frac{1}{m}\int_{t}^{\infty}(r-t)^{m}\phi^{\prime}(r)e^{-\phi(r)}dr\geq\frac{1}{m}\phi^{\prime}(t)\int_{t}^{\infty}(r-t)^{m}e^{-\phi(r)}dr,

where, again, we denote by
\phi^{\prime}(t)
the left derivative of
\phi
. Thus

-\ln F_{m-1}(t)\leq-\ln(\phi^{\prime}(t)/m)-\ln F_{m}(t),

and

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\ln F_{m}(t)}{-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln(\phi^{\prime}(t)/m)-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}{-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}.

Now we may use that
\phi^{\prime}(t)>a>0
for
t
large enough, and
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\ln F_{m-1}(t)=-\infty
to claim that

(7)

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\ln F_{m}(t)}{-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln(a/m)-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}{-\ln F_{m-1}(t)}\geq 1.

To prove the reverse inequality we note that

F_{m}^{\prime}(t)=-m\int_{t}^{\infty}(r-t)^{m-1}e^{-\phi(r)}dr.

Assume that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{-\ln{F_{m}(t)}}{-\ln(-\frac{1}{m}F_{m}^{\prime}(t))}>\alpha>1,

but then, again there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for all
t>t_{0},
we have

-\ln{F_{m}(t)}>-\alpha\ln(-\frac{1}{m}F_{m}^{\prime}(t)),

and thus

m<\frac{-F_{m}^{\prime}(t)}{F_{m}(t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

Take an integral over
t\in[x,\infty)
from both sides to get

F_{m}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}(x)=\infty,

which is a contradiction. This finishes the proof of the inductive step, but we
actually need a bit stronger statement, which is similar to Remark
1
. Indeed, consider any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\alpha>1
. Let

E_{m,\alpha}=\{t:-\ln F_{m}(t)>-\alpha\ln F_{m-1}(t)\},

then, using the same ideas as in (
3
) we get

|E_{m,\alpha}|<\infty
.

To complete our proof, let

X_{i}(t)=\frac{\ln F_{i}(t)}{\ln F_{i-1}(t)}\mbox{ and }Y(t)=\frac{\ln F_{0}(t)}{\phi(t)}.

Using (
6
) we get

\liminf\limits_{t\to{\infty}}X_{i}(t)=1
and using (
1
)
we get
\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}Y(t)=-1.

Now let
X(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}(t)
. Our goal is to prove that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}X(t)Y(t)\geq-1.

Assume that this is not true, then there exists
\alpha>1
such that

\limsup_{t\to\infty}X(t)Y(t)<{-\alpha}<-1.

Therefore, there exists
t_{0}
such that for all
t>t_{0}

(8)

X(t)Y(t)\leq-\alpha.

Using (
7
) we may also assume that
X_{i}(t)>0
for all
t>t_{0}.
Next, consider the set

A:=\left\{t>t_{0}:X(t)>\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\right\},

we claim that
|A|<\infty
. Note that

\left|\left\{t:X(t)>\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\right\}\right|\leq\left|\left\{t:X_{i}(t)>\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\!\!\!\!\!\!,\mbox{ for some }i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\right\}\right|

<\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|\left\{t:X_{i}(t)>\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\right\}\right|<\infty.

We also note that
\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+1}>1
and thus

\left|\left\{t:Y(t)<-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+1}\right\}\right|<\infty.

Finally

\left|\left\{t>t_{0}:X(t)Y(t)<-\alpha\right\}\right|=\left|\left\{t>t_{0}:Y(t)<-\frac{\alpha}{X(t)}\right\}\right|

\leq|A|+\left|\left\{t>t_{0}:Y(t)<-\frac{\alpha}{X(t)}\mbox{ and }X(t)<\frac{\alpha+1}{2}\right\}\right|

\leq|A|+\left|\left\{t:Y(t)<-\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha+1}\right\}\right|<\infty,

which contradicts with (
8
). The claim is proved and this finishes the proof of Lemma
2
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof.

Let
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a convex, symmetric body such that
\mu(tRB_{2}^{n})\leq\mu(tK)
holds for for some fixed
R>0
and every
t
large enough, but
RB_{2}^{n}\not\subset K.
Thus, the maximal Euclidean ball in
K
has radius
rR
, with
r\in(0,1)
. From the assumption it follows that

\mu((tRB_{2}^{n})^{c})\geq\mu((tK)^{c}),

which implies that

\frac{\ln{\mu((tRB_{2}^{n})^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}\geq\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(trR)}\frac{\phi(trR)}{\phi(tR)}.

From the convexity of
\phi
and
r\in(0,1)
we get that

\phi(trR)=\phi(trR+(1-r)0)\leq r\phi(tR)+(1-r)\phi(0).

Using that
\phi(tR)\to\infty
we get that there exists
r^{\prime}\in(0,1)
and
t_{0}>0
such that
\frac{\phi(trR)}{\phi(tR)}\leq r^{\prime},
for all
t>t_{0}
. Thus

(9)

\frac{\ln{\mu((tRB_{2}^{n})^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}\geq r^{\prime}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(trR)},

for all
t>t_{0}
. Taking the limit superior, as
t\to\infty
, from both sides of the inequality (
9
), we obtain
-1\geq-r^{\prime}.
But this contradicts the fact that
r^{\prime}
is less than
1
. Therefore, our assumption that
RB_{2}^{n}\not\subset K
must be false.
∎

Remark 3
.

The rotation invariant assumption on
\mu
in Theorem
1
is necessary. Indeed, one can construct an example of a
\log
-concave probability measure that is not rotation invariant in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which does not satisfy the statement of Theorem
1
. Consider the rectangle
\Omega=\{(x,y):|x|\leq\frac{\pi}{2},|y|\leq\frac{1}{2}\}
, and define the measure
\mu
as
\mu(K)=\frac{|K\cap\Omega|}{|\Omega|}
.
Taking
K=\Omega
, we have

B_{2}^{2}\not\subset\Omega,\mbox{ but }|B_{2}^{2}|=|\Omega|=\pi\mbox{ and }|tB_{2}^{2}|=|t\Omega|,\hskip 2.84544pt\forall t>0.

Note that
\mu(tB_{2}^{2})\leq\mu(t\Omega);\hskip 2.84544pt\forall t>0
, indeed, this is equivalent to
|tB_{2}^{2}\cap\Omega|\leq|t\Omega\cap\Omega|
. If
t\leq 1
, then we have
|tB_{2}^{2}\cap\Omega|\leq|tB_{2}^{2}|=|t\Omega|
, and if
t\geq 1
we get
|tB_{2}^{2}\cap\Omega|\leq|\Omega|.
So, we provided an example where

\mu(tB_{2}^{2})\leq\mu(tK);\hskip 14.22636pt\forall t>0

but
B_{2}^{2}\not\subset K.

3.
The cases where density depends on the norm

In this section, we would like to give a proof Theorem
1
in a more general case, which would answer Question
2
. The main idea and computation are in the same spirit as in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex, symmetric bodies, and let
\mu
be a
\log
-concave probability measure, with density

e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}
, where
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is an increasing, convex function. If for every
t
large enough and some
R>0

\mu(tRL)\leq\mu(tK),

then
RL\subseteq K.

Proof.

We have to check Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
, i.e. to prove yet another generalization of the classical large deviation principle (see (
11
) below).

We claim that for any
R>0

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tRL)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}=-1.

We can assume
R=1
. Moreover, as before, using convexity of
\phi
we may assume that
\phi(t)
is a strictly increasing function for large enough
t
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mu(

\displaystyle(tL)^{c})=\int_{(tL)^{c}}e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}dx=\int_{(tL)^{c}}\int_{\phi(\|x\|_{L})}^{\infty}e^{-u}dudx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\phi(\|x\|_{L})}^{\infty}\!\!\!\chi_{(tL)^{c}}(x)e^{-u}dudx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\{x:\phi(\|x\|_{L})<u\}}\!\!\!\chi_{(tL)^{c}}(x)e^{-u}dxdu

\displaystyle=\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!e^{-u}|\{x\!\!:\|x\|_{L}\in[t,\phi^{-1}(u)]\}|du=|L|\!\int_{\phi(t)}^{\infty}((\phi^{-1}(u))^{n}-t^{n})e^{-u}du

\displaystyle=|L|\int_{t}^{\infty}(v^{n}-t^{n})\phi^{\prime}(v)e^{-\phi(v)}dv=-|L|\int_{t}^{\infty}(v^{n}-t^{n})de^{-\phi(v)}

(10)

\displaystyle=n|L|\int_{t}^{\infty}v^{n-1}e^{-\phi(v)}dv,

note that
\phi(t)
may be a constant function on some interval
[0,t_{0}]
and strictly increasing on
[t_{0},\infty)
, in such a case we define
\phi^{-1}(\phi(0))=t_{0}
.
So, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\mu((tL)^{c})}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln({n|L|\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(v)}v^{n-1}dv})}{\phi(t)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(v)}v^{n-1}dv}}{\phi(t)},

\displaystyle=-1,

where the last equality follows from the proof of Lemma
1
.

To finish the proof, we must check Lemma
2
. In particular, we want to show that

(11)

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(r(K,L)t)}=-1,

for symmetric, convex bodies
K,L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, convex, increasing function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and measure
\mu
with density
e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}
.

Let
R=r(K,L)
, then we have
(tK)^{c}\subset(tRL)^{c}
from the assumption. Thus using Lemma
1
we get

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tRL)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}=-1.

Using that
RL
is the maximal dilate of
L
inside
K
we get that there is a pair of points
v,-v\in\partial RL\cap\partial K
. Let
P
be a plank created by tangent planes to
RL
and
K
at
v
and
-v
. Let
n_{v}
be a normal vector to
\partial RL
at
v
. Then the width of the plank
P
is
2Rh_{L}(n_{v})=2h_{K}(n_{v})
:
P=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:|\langle x,n_{v}\rangle|\leq Rh_{L}(n_{v})\}
, where
h_{L}(x)=\sup\{\langle x,y\rangle:y\in L\}
is the support function of
L
(see
[
20
]
for basic definitions and properties).
Next,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tK)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln{\mu((tP)^{c})}}{\phi(tR)}.

Selecting a proper system of coordinates, we may assume that
n_{v}=e_{n}
, let
a=tRh_{L}(e_{n}).
Then,

\displaystyle\mu((tP)^{c})

\displaystyle=2\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{e_{n}^{\perp}}e^{-\phi(\|ze_{n}+x\|_{L})}dxdz

\displaystyle=2\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{e_{n}^{\perp}}\int^{\infty}_{\phi(\|ze_{n}+x\|_{L})}e^{-u}dudxdz

\displaystyle=2\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\{x\in e_{n}^{\perp}:\phi(\|ze_{n}+x\|_{L})<u\}}e^{-u}dxdudz

\displaystyle=2\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}\left|\left\{x\in e_{n}^{\perp}:\|ze_{n}+x\|_{L}\leq\phi^{-1}(u)\right\}\right|dudz.

Now note that

\left|\left\{x\in e_{n}^{\perp}:\|ze_{n}+x\|_{L}\leq\phi^{-1}(u)\right\}\right|=\left|\left\{x\in e_{n}^{\perp}:ze_{n}+x\in\phi^{-1}(u)L\right\}\right|.

The above volume is zero if
z>\phi^{-1}(u)h_{L}(e_{n})
(or
\phi(z/h_{L}(e_{n}))>u
), for
z\in[0,\phi^{-1}(u)h_{L}(e_{n})]
we note that
\phi^{-1}(u)L
is a convex body and thus contains inside a pyramid
\Delta
with base
L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}
and the height
\phi^{-1}(u)h_{L}(e_{n})
(with apex
\phi^{-1}(u)h_{L}(e_{n})v/R
), then

\left|\left\{x\in e_{n}^{\perp}:ze_{n}+x\in\phi^{-1}(u)L\right\}\right|\geq|\Delta\cap(e_{n}^{\perp}+ze_{n})|

=(\phi^{-1}(u)h_{L}(e_{n})-z)^{n-1}|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|.

Thus

\displaystyle\mu((tP)^{c})

\displaystyle\geq 2|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{\phi(z/h_{L}(e_{n}))}^{\infty}e^{-u}(\phi^{-1}(u)h_{L}(e_{n})-z)^{n-1}dudz

\displaystyle=2|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{z/h_{L}(e_{n})}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(u)}\phi^{\prime}(u)(uh_{L}(e_{n})-z)^{n-1}dudz

\displaystyle=-2|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{z/h_{L}(e_{n})}^{\infty}(uh_{L}(e_{n})-z)^{n-1}de^{-\phi(u)}dz

\displaystyle=2(n-1)|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{a}^{\infty}\int_{z/h_{L}(e_{n})}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(u)}(uh_{L}(e_{n})-z)^{n-2}dudz

\displaystyle=2(n-1)|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{a/h_{L}(e_{n})}^{\infty}\int_{a}^{h_{L}(e_{n})u}e^{-\phi(u)}(uh_{L}(e_{n})-z)^{n-2}dzdu

\displaystyle=2|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{a/h_{L}(e_{n})}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(u)}(uh_{L}(e_{n})-a)^{n-1}du

\displaystyle=2h_{L}^{n-1}(e_{n})|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{tR}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(u)}(u-tR)^{n-1}du.

So, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\mu((tP)^{c})}{\phi(tR)}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\left(2h_{L}^{n-1}(e_{n})|L\cap e_{n}^{\perp}|\int_{tR}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(u)}(u-tR)^{n-1}du\right)}{\phi(tR)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\int_{tR}^{\infty}e^{-\phi(u)}(u-tR)^{n-1}du}{\phi(tR)}.

By Claim 2 the above quantity is greater than or equal to
-1
, thus Lemma
2
is applied here which finishes the proof for our main result.
∎

4.
The Busemann - Petty type problems

In this section, we will discuss Question
3
. We first note that one must make some additional assumptions on the measure
\mu
to avoid a trivial answer. Indeed, if a measure
\mu
has a homogeneous density (i.e.
f(rx)=r^{p}f(x),
for
r>0
and
p>1-n
), then the answer is identical to the one given in
[
23
]
.

Let us first show that in dimension
n\geq 5
one can always find a pair of convex, symmetric bodies
K
and
L
and measure
\mu
, such that the answer to Question
3
is negative. The main idea follows from the construction in
[
24
]
. We begin with the following fact:

Fact.

If
d\mu=e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}dx
is a
\log
-concave measure and
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are convex, symmetric bodies such that
|K|\leq|RL|
for some
R>0,
then

\mu(K)\leq\mu(RL).

Proof.

Using calculations similar to (
3
) we get

\mu(K)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}|K\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|du

and

\mu(RL)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}|RL\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|du.

To get
\mu(K)\leq\mu(RL)
we only need to check that
|K\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|\leq|RL\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|.
Indeed, if
R\leq\phi^{-1}(u),
then we have

|K\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|\leq|K|\leq|RL|=|RL\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|,

and if
R\geq\phi^{-1}(u),
then we get

|K\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|\leq|\phi^{-1}(u)L|=|RL\cap\phi^{-1}(u)L|.

Hence,
\mu(K)\leq\mu(RL),
for any
R>0.

∎

Next, we show that Question
3
has a negative answer for
n\geq 5
.

Theorem 3
.

For
n\geq 5,
there are convex symmetric bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\log
-concave measure
\mu
with density
e^{-\phi(\|x\|_{L})}
, such that

(12)

\mu(rK\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\mu(rL\cap\xi^{\perp}),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0,

but
\mu(K)>\mu(L).

Proof.

Let us assume, towards the contradiction, that Question
3
has an affirmative answer in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for some fixed
n\geq 5.
So, for any pair of convex symmetric bodies
K,L
that satisfy (
12
), we would get
\mu(K)\leq\mu(L).
The condition on sections (
12
) will be also satisfied for the dilated bodies
tK
and
tL
, for all
t>0.
Therefore, we have

(13)

\mu(tK)\leq\mu(tL),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall t>0.

Which by definition of
\mu
means

\int_{tK}{e^{-\phi(||x||_{L})}dx}\leq\int_{tL}{e^{-\phi(||x||_{L})}dx},

or equivalently, applying the change of variables
x=tx
, we have

\int_{K}{e^{-\phi(t||x||_{L})}dx}\leq\int_{L}{e^{-\phi(t||x||_{L})}dx}.

Using the continuity of
\phi
and compactness of
K
and
L
, we can take the limit for the above inequality as
t\to 0^{+}
, to obtain

|K|\leq|L|.

Therefore, we have a relation between the dilation problem for a
\log
-concave probability measure, with the Busemann-Petty problem for volume measure, which is if

\mu(rK\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\mu(rL\cap\xi^{\perp})),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0,

then
|K|\leq|L|
.

A number of very interesting counterexamples to the Busemann-Petty problem were shown by Papadimitrakis
[
17
]
; Gardner
[
7
]
; Gardner, Koldobsky, and Schlumprecht
[
10
]
: there are convex symmetric bodies
K,L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 5
such that

(14)

|K\cap\xi^{\perp}|\leq|L\cap\xi^{\perp}|,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

but

(15)

|K|>|L|.

Note that because the volume measure is homogeneous the condition on sections (
14
) is also true for dilates of
K
and
L
, so we have

(16)

|rK\cap\xi^{\perp}|\leq|rL\cap\xi^{\perp}|,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0.

Now, applying the Fact to (
16
), we get that

\mu(rK\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\mu(rL\cap\xi^{\perp}),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0.

Thus, using (
13
), we have

\mu(tK)\leq\mu(tL),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall t>0,

dividing by
t^{n}
and taking the limit of the above inequality as
t\to 0^{+}
we get

|K|\leq|L|,

and this contradicts (
15
).

∎

It is interesting to note that the measure
\mu
constructed above is very specific. For example, we can not use this construction directly with the assumption that
\mu
is rotation invariant.

Still, we can show that the answer to Question
3
is negative in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
n\geq 7
even when
\mu
is a
\log
-concave measure with rotation invariant density.

Theorem 4
.

For dimension
n\geq 7,
and
d\mu=e^{-\phi(|x|)}dx
there is a convex symmetric body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mu(rK\cap\xi^{\perp})\leq\mu(rB_{2}^{n}\cap\xi^{\perp}),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall r>0,

but
\mu(K)>\mu(B_{2}^{n}).

Proof.

Giannopoulos
[
11
]
and Bourgain
[
4
]
constructed an example in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 7
of convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that satisfies

|K\cap\xi^{\perp}|\leq|B_{2}^{n}\cap\xi^{\perp}|,\hskip 14.22636pt\forall\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

but
|K|>|B_{2}^{n}|
. To prove Theorem
4
one may take the same convex body
K
and
B_{2}^{n}
as provided in
[
11
,
4
]
and repeat the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

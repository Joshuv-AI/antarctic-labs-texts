Subresiduated Nelson Algebras
Noemí Lubomirsky, Paula Menchón, Hernán San Martín

Abstract
In this paper we generalize the well known relation between Heyting algebras and Nelson algebras in the framework of subresiduated lattices. In order to make it possible, we introduce the variety of subresiduated Nelson algebras. The main tool for its study is the construction provided by Vakarelov. Using it, we characterize the lattice of congruences of a subresiduated Nelson algebra through some of its implicative filters. We use this characterization to describe simple and subdirectly irreducible algebras, as well as principal congruences. Moreover, we prove that the variety of subresiduated Nelson algebras has equationally definable principal congruences and also the congruence extension property. Additionally, we present an equational base for the variety generated by the totally ordered subresiduated Nelson algebras. Finally, we show that there exists an equivalence between the algebraic category of subresiduated lattices and the algebraic category of centedred subresiduated Nelson algebras. Keywords: Subresiduated lattices, Nelson algebras, twist construction, Kleene algebras.



1
Introduction

In this paper we study the convergence of ideas arising from different varieties of algebras
related to intuitionistic logics: Heyting algebras, subresiduated lattices and Nelson algebras.

Subresiduated lattices, which are a generalization of Heyting algebras, were introduced during the decade of 1970
by Epstein and Horn
[
7
]
as an algebraic counterpart of some logics with
strong implication previously studied by Lewy and Hacking
[
8
]
. These logics are examples of
subintuitionistic logics, i.e., logics in the language of intuitionistic logic that are defined semantically
by using Kripke models, in the same way as intuitionistic logic is defined but without
requiring from the models some of the properties required in the intuitionistic case. Also in relation with the
study of subintuitionistic logics, Celani and Jansana
[
4
]
got these algebras as the elements of a subvariety
of the variety of weak Heyting algebras (see also
[
3
,
5
]
).
It is known that the variety
\mathsf{S4}
, whose members are the S4-algebras, is the algebraic semantics of the modal logic

\mathbf{S4}
. This means that
\phi
is a theorem of
\mathbf{S4}
if and only if the variety
\mathsf{S4}
satisfies
\phi\approx 1
.
The variety of subresiduated lattices corresponds to the variety of algebras defined for all the equations
\phi\approx 1
satisfied in the variety
\mathsf{S4}
where the only connectives that appear are conjunction

\wedge
, disjunction
\vee
, bottom
\perp
, top
\top
and a new connective of implication
\Rightarrow
, called strict implication, defined by
\varphi\Rightarrow\psi:=\square(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)
, where
\rightarrow
denotes the classical implication.

Nelson’s constructive logic with strong negation, which was introduced in
[
14
]
(see also
[
16
,
18
,
20
]
), is a well-known and by now fairly well-understood non-classical logic that combines the constructive approach of positive intuitionistic logic with a classical (i.e. De Morgan) negation.
The algebraic models of this logic, forming a variety whose members are called Nelson algebras, have been studied since at least the late 1950’s (firstly by Rasiowa; see
[
16
]
and references therein) and are also by now a fairly well-understood class of algebras. One of the main algebraic insights on this variety came, towards the end of the 1970’s, with the
realisation (independently due to Fidel and Vakarelov) that every Nelson algebra can be represented as a special binary product (here called a twist structure) of a Heyting algebra.

The main goal of this manuscript is to extend the twist construction in the framework of subresiduated lattices,
thus obtaining a new variety, whose members will be called subresiduated Nelson algebras.
More precisely, we will show that every subresiduated Nelson algebra can be represented as a twist structure of a subresiduated lattice. Another central objective of this paper is to study the subvariety of its totally ordered members.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the definition of Nelson algebra and also
sketch the main constructions linking Heyting algebras with Nelson algebras. Moreover,
we recall the definition of a subresiduated lattice and some of its properties.
In Section
3
we introduce subresiduated Nelson algebras, proving that the class of subresiduated Nelson algebras
(which is a variety) properly contains the variety of Nelson algebras.
We also show that every subresiduated Nelson algebra can be represented as a twist structure of a subresiduated lattice.
In Section
4
we prove that given an arbitrary subresiduated Nelson algebra, there exists an order isomorphism
between the lattice of its congruences and the lattice of its open implicative filters (which are a kind of implicative filters).
We use it in order to give a characterization of the principal congruences.
In particular, the mentioned characterization proves that the variety of subresiduated Nelson algebras has equationally definable principal congruences (EDPC).
We also give a description of the simple and subdirectly irreducible algebras,
and we prove that the variety of subresiduated Nelson algebras has the congruence extention property (CEP).
In Section
5
we study the class of totally ordered subresiduated Nelson algebras
in order to give an equational base for the class generated by this variety.
Finally, in Section
6
we characterize the subresiduated Nelson algebras that
can be represented as a twist structure of a subresiduated lattice and we also prove that there exists
an equivalence between the algebraic category
of subresiduated lattices and the algebraic category of centered subresiduated Nelson algebras, where
a centered subresiduated Nelson algebra is a subresiduated Nelson algebra endowed with a center, i.e.,
a fixed element with respect to the involution (this element is necessarily unique).

Basic results

In this section we recall the definition of Nelson algebra
[
1
,
6
,
21
]
and some links between Heyting algebras and Nelson algebras
[

]
, as well as the definition of subresiduated lattice and some of its properties
[

A Kleene algebra
[
1
,
6
,
11
]
is a bounded distributive lattice endowed with a unary operation
\sim

which satisfies the following identities:

Ne1)

\sim\sim x=x
,

Ne2)

\sim(x\wedge y)=\sim x\vee\sim y
,

Ne3)

(x\wedge\sim x)\wedge(y\vee\sim y)=x\wedge\sim x
.

Definition 2.1
.

An algebra
\langle T,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,\sim,0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,2,1,0,0)
is called a Nelson algebra if

\langle T,\wedge,\vee,\sim,0,1\rangle
is a Kleene algebra and the following identities are satisfied:

Ne4)

x\rightarrow x=1
,

Ne5)

x\rightarrow(y\rightarrow z)=(x\wedge y)\rightarrow z
,

Ne6)

x\wedge(x\rightarrow y)=x\wedge(\sim x\vee y)
,

Ne7)

x\rightarrow y\leq\sim x\vee y
,

Ne8)

x\rightarrow(y\wedge z)=(x\rightarrow y)\wedge(x\rightarrow z)
.

If
\langle T,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,\sim,0,1\rangle
is an algebra of type
(2,2,2,1,0,0)
where

\langle T,\wedge,\vee,0,1\rangle
is a bounded distributive lattice
and the condition Ne6) is satisfied, then conditions Ne7) and Ne8) are also satisfied
[
12
,
13
]
.
We write
\mathsf{NA}
for the variety of Nelson algebras.

There are two key constructions that relate Heyting algebras and Nelson algebras.
Given a Heyting algebra
A
, we define the set

\mathrm{K}(A)=\{(a,b)\in A\times A:a\wedge b=0\}

(1)

and then endow it with the following operations:

•

(a,b)\wedge(c,d)=(a\wedge c,b\vee d)
,

•

(a,b)\vee(c,d)=(a\vee c,b\wedge d)
,

•

\sim(a,b)=(b,a)
,

•

(a,b)\Rightarrow(c,d)=(a\rightarrow c,a\wedge d)
,

•

\perp=(0,1)
,

•

\top=(1,0)
.

Then
\langle\mathrm{K}(A),\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\perp,\top\rangle\in\mathsf{NA}

[
20
]
. In the same manuscript,
Vakarelov proves that if
T\in\mathsf{NA}
, then the relation
\theta

defined by

x\theta y\;\text{if and only if}\;x\rightarrow y=1\;\text{and}\;y\rightarrow x=1

(2)

is an equivalence relation such that
\langle T/\theta,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,0,1\rangle
is a Heyting algebra
with the operations defined by

•

x/\theta\wedge y/\theta:=x\wedge y/\theta
,

•

x/\theta\vee y/\theta:=x\vee y/\theta
,

•

x/\theta\rightarrow y/\theta:=x\rightarrow y/\theta
,

•

0:=0/\theta
,

•

1:=1/\theta
.

It is a natural question whether these constructions can be extended to subresiduated lattices.

Definition 2.2
.

A subresiduated lattice (sr-lattice for short) is a pair
(A,D)
, where
A

is a bounded distributive lattice and
D
is a bounded sublattice of
A
such
that for each
a,b\in A
there exists the maximum of the set
\{d\in D:a\wedge d\leq b\}
.
This element is denoted by
a\rightarrow b
.

Let
(A,D)
be a subresiduated lattice. This pair can be regarded as an algebra

\langle A,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,2,0,0)
where

D=\{a\in A:1\rightarrow a=a\}=\{1\rightarrow a:a\in A\}
.
Moreover, an algebra
\langle A,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,0,1\rangle
is an sr-lattice if and only if

(A,\wedge,\vee,0,1)
is a bounded distributive lattice and the
following conditions are satisfied for every
a,b,c\in A
:

1)

(a\vee b)\rightarrow c=(a\rightarrow c)\wedge(b\rightarrow c)
,

2)

c\rightarrow(a\wedge b)=(c\rightarrow a)\wedge(c\rightarrow b)
,

3)

(a\rightarrow b)\wedge(b\rightarrow c)\leq a\rightarrow c
,

4)

a\rightarrow a=1
,

5)

a\wedge(a\rightarrow b)\leq b
,

6)

a\rightarrow b\leq c\rightarrow(a\rightarrow b)
.

We write
\mathsf{SRL}
to denote the variety whose members are sr-lattices.
In every sr-lattice the following cuasi-identity is satisfied:
if
a\leq b\rightarrow c
then
a\wedge b\leq c
.

The following example of sr-lattice will be used throughout the paper.

Example 2.1
.

Let
A
be the Boolean algebra of four elements, where
a
and
b
are the atoms. This algebra can be
seen as a bounded distributive lattice.
Define
D=\{0,1\}
. We have that
(A,D)
is an sr-lattice. With an abuse of notation we write

A
for this sr-lattice. Note that since
a\rightarrow 0=0\neq b
then
A
is not a Heyting algebra.

In this work, we attempt to find a more general definition than the one of a Nelson algebra in order to take the first steps toward exploiting its relation to sr-lattices.

5
The variety generated by the class of totally ordered subresiduated Nelson algebras

The aim of this final section is to give an equational base for the variety generated by the totally
ordered subresiduated Nelson algebras.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
. A proper implicative filter
P
of
T
is called prime if for every
x,y\in T

we have that if
x\vee y\in P
then
x\in P
or
y\in P
.
We write
\mathrm{X}^{o}(T)
to denote the set of open prime implicative filters of
T
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
such that

T\vDash((x\rightarrow y)\wedge(\sim y\rightarrow\sim x))\vee((y\rightarrow x)\wedge(\sim x\rightarrow\sim y))=1.

Let
P\in\mathrm{X}^{o}(T)
. Then
T/P
is a chain.

Proof.

Let
P\in\mathrm{X}^{o}(T)
and
x,y\in T
. Taking into account that

((x\rightarrow y)\wedge(\sim y\rightarrow\sim x))\vee((y\rightarrow x)\wedge(\sim x\rightarrow\sim y))=1\in P

we get
(x\rightarrow y)\wedge(\sim y\rightarrow\sim x)\in P
or
(y\rightarrow x)\wedge(\sim x\rightarrow\sim y)\in P
. Thus,
(x,x\wedge y)\in\Theta(P)

or
(y,y\wedge x)\in\Theta(P)
, i.e.,
x/P\leq y/P
or
y/P\leq x/P
.
Hence,
T/P
is a chain.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
such that
T\vDash\square(x\vee y)=\square(x)\vee\square(y)
. Let
F\in\mathrm{IF}^{o}(A)
and
I
an
ideal such that
F\cap I=\emptyset
. Then there exists a filter
P\in\mathrm{X}^{o}(T)
such that
F\subseteq P
and
P\cap I=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=\{G\in\mathrm{IF}^{o}(T):F\subseteq G\;\text{and}\;G\cap I=\emptyset\}
. Since
F\in\Sigma
then
\Sigma\neq\emptyset
.
A direct computation shows that
\Sigma
is under the hypothesis of Zorn’s lemma, so there exists a maximal element
P
in
\Sigma
.
In particular,
F\subseteq P
and
P\cap I=\emptyset
. Moreover, it is immediate that
P
is a proper open implicative filter.
Suppose that
P
is not prime, so there exist
x,y\in T
such that
x\vee y\in P
and
x,y\notin P
.
Let
P_{x}=\langle P\cup\{x\}\rangle
and
P_{y}=\langle P\cup\{y\}\rangle
. The maximality of
P
in
\Sigma
implies that

P_{x}\cap I\neq\emptyset
and
P_{y}\cap I\neq\emptyset
, so there exist
z,w\in I
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
such that

(p_{1}\wedge\square(x))\rightarrow z=1
and
(p_{2}\wedge\square(y))\rightarrow w=1
. Let

.
Thus,
(p\wedge\square(x))\rightarrow(z\vee w)=1
and
(p\wedge\square(y))\rightarrow(z\vee w)=1
.
Thus,

((p\wedge\square(x))\rightarrow(z\vee w))\wedge((p\wedge\square(y))\rightarrow(z\vee w))=1.

But

((p\wedge\square(x))\rightarrow(z\vee w))\wedge((p\wedge\square(y))\rightarrow(z\vee w))=((p\wedge\square(x))\vee(p\wedge\square(y)))\rightarrow(y\vee z)

and

((p\wedge\square(x))\vee(p\wedge\square(y)))\rightarrow(y\vee z)=(p\wedge\square(x\vee y))\rightarrow(y\vee z).

Then

(p\wedge\square(x\vee y))\rightarrow(y\vee z)=1\in P

p\wedge\square(x\vee y)\in P
, so
y\vee z\in P
. Besides, since
y,z\in I
then
y\vee z\in I
,
so
P\cap I\neq\emptyset
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,

.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
be such that
T\vDash\square(x\vee y)=\square(x)\vee\square(y)
. Suppose that
T
is not trivial.
Then

. Indeed, let

x\neq 1
. Then

, where

(x]:=\{y\in T:y\leq x\}
. Then there exists a filter

. Therefore,
\mathrm{X}^{o}(T)\neq\emptyset
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
be such that
T\vDash\square(x\vee y)=\square(x)\vee\square(y)
. Suppose that
T
is not trivial.
Then the intersection of all open prime implicative filters of

is equal to

.

Let
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
and
x,y\in T
. We define

We also define

\mathsf{SNA}^{c}=\mathsf{SNA}+\{\square(x\vee y)=\square(x)\vee\square(y)\}+\{t(x,y)=1\}.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be the class of totally ordered members of
\mathsf{SNA}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

\mathrm{V}(\mathsf{C})=\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
.

Proof.

A straightforward computation shows that
\mathsf{C}\subseteq\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
, so
\mathrm{V}(\mathsf{C})\subseteq\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
.
In order to show the converse inclusion, let

. If

is trivial then

.
Now suppose that

is not trivial. Let

the homomorphism

is injective. Let

Then

for every

Note that
\mathsf{SNA}^{c}

\mathsf{SNA}
. Indeed, let

.

\mathrm{K}(A)\in\mathsf{SNA}

6
Centered subresiduated Nelson algebras

A Kleene algebra (Nelson algebra) is called centered if there exists an element which
is a fixed point with respect to the involution, i.e., an element
\mathsf{c}
such that

\sim\mathsf{c}=\mathsf{c}
. This element is necessarily unique. If
T=\mathrm{K}(A)
where
A
is a bounded distributive
lattice, the center is
\mathsf{c}=(0,0)
.
Let
T
be a centered Kleene algebra. We define the following condition:

\mathsf{(CK)}
For every
x,y\in T
if
x,y\geq\mathsf{c}
and
x\wedge y\leq\mathsf{c}

then there exists
z\in T
such that
z\vee\mathsf{c}=x
and
\sim z\vee\mathsf{c}=y
.

The condition
\mathsf{(CK)}
is not necessarily satisfied in every centered Kleene algebra,
see for instance Figure 1 of
[
2
]
. However, every centered Nelson algebra
satisfies the condition
\mathsf{(CK)}
(see
[
6
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
2
, Proposition 6.1]
).

The following two properties are well known:

•

Let
T
be a Kleene algebra. Then
T
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{K}(A)
for some
bounded distributive lattice
A
if and only if
T
is centered and satisfies the
condition
\mathsf{(CK)}
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
and
[
2
, Proposition 6.1]
).

•

Let
T
be a Nelson algebra. Then
T
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{K}(A)
for some
Heyting algebra
A
if and only if
T
is centered (see
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
).

An algebra
\langle T,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,\sim,0,1,\mathsf{c}\rangle
is
a centered subresiduated Nelson algebra if
\langle T,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,\sim,0,1\rangle

is as a subresiduated Nelson algebra and
\mathsf{c}
is a center.
We write
\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
for the variety whose members are centered subresiduated Nelson algebras.

In this section we prove that given
T\in\mathsf{SNA}
,
T
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{K}(A)

for some subresiduated lattice

if and only if
T
is centered and satisfies the
condition
\mathsf{(CK)}
(we also show that the condition
\mathsf{(CK)}
is not necessarily satisfied in every
centered subresiduated Nelson algebra). Finally we show that there exists a categorical equivalence
between
\mathsf{SRL}
and the full subcategory of
\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
whose objects satisfy the condition
\mathsf{(CK)}

a,b\in A
such that
a\wedge b=0
. Moreover,
y=(a,\neg a)/\theta
,
so
y=\alpha(a)
. Thus,
\alpha
is suryective. Therefore,
\alpha
is an isomorphism.
∎

A direct computation shows that if
f:A\rightarrow B
is a morphism in
\mathsf{SRL}
and
a\in A

then
\mathsf{C}(K(f))(\varphi_{A}(a))=\varphi_{B}(f(a))
, and that if
f:T\rightarrow U
is a morphism in
\mathsf{SNA}
and
x\in T

then
\mathrm{K}(\mathsf{C}(f))(\rho_{T}(x))=\rho_{U}(f(x))
.

Remark 6.1
.

Note that if
T,U
are Kleene algebras,
T
has center and
f
is a morphism in
\mathsf{SNA}
from
T
to
U
, then

U
has center and
f(\mathsf{c})=\mathsf{c}
. Indeed,
f(\mathsf{c})=f(\sim\mathsf{c})=\sim f(\mathsf{c})
.

Therefore, the following result follows from the previous results of this section, and Lemmas
6.2
and
6.3
.

Theorem 6.6
.

There exists a categorical equivalence between
\mathsf{SRL}
and
\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
.

7
Conclusions and two open problems

In this paper we extended, in the framework of sr-lattices, the well known twist construction given for Heyting algebras.
In order to make it possible, we introduced and studied the variety
\mathsf{SNA}

by showing that every subresiduated Nelson algebra can be represented as a twist structure of an sr-lattice.
We also characterized the congruences of subresiduated Nelson algebras and we applied this result in order to obtain
some additional properties for these algebras. In particular, we described simple and subdirectly
irreducible algebras, we proved that
\mathsf{SNA}
has EDPC and CEP, we presented an equational
base for the variety of
\mathsf{SNA}
generated by the class of its totally ordered members and finally we proved
that there exists a categorical equivalence between
\mathsf{SRL}
and
\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
.

We finish this paper by considering two (open) problems concerning the matter of this paper.

Problem 1: Generalize the term equivalence between Nelson algebras and Nelson lattices

We assume the reader is familiar with commutative residuated
lattices
[
9
]
. An involutive residuated lattice is a
bounded, integral and commutative residuated lattice
(T,\wedge,\vee,\ast,\rightarrow,0,1)
such that for every
x\in T
it holds that

\neg\neg x=x
, where
\neg x:=x\rightarrow 0
and
0
is the first
element of
T

[
1
]
. In an involutive residuated lattice it
holds that
x\ast y=\neg(x\rightarrow\neg y)
and

x\rightarrow y=\neg(x\ast\neg y)
.
A Nelson lattice
[
1
]
is an involutive residuated
lattice
(T,\wedge,\vee,*,\rightarrow,0,1)
which satisfies the additional
inequality

(x^{2}\rightarrow y)\wedge((\neg y)^{2}\rightarrow\neg x)\leq x\rightarrow y
,
where
x^{2}:=x\ast x
. See also
[
20
]
.

Remark 7.1
.

Let
(T,\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,{\sim},0,1)
be a Nelson algebra.
We define on
T
the binary operations
*
and
\rightarrow
by

x*y:=\sim(x\Rightarrow{\sim}y)\vee{\sim}(y\Rightarrow{\sim}x)

and
x\rightarrow y:=(x\Rightarrow y)\wedge({\sim}y\Rightarrow{\sim}x)
.
Then
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
says that
(T,\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,*,0,1)

is a Nelson lattice. Moreover,
{\sim}x=\neg x=x\rightarrow 0
.

Let
(T,\wedge,\vee,*,\rightarrow,0,1)
be a Nelson lattice. We define on
T

a binary operation
\Rightarrow
and a unary operation
\sim
by

x\Rightarrow y:=x^{2}\rightarrow y
and

\sim x:=\neg x
,
where
x^{2}=x*x
. Then Theorem 3.6 of
[
1
]
says that the

(T,\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,{\sim},0,1)
is a Nelson
algebra. In
[
1
, Theorem 3.11]
it was also proved that the
category of Nelson algebras and the category of Nelson lattices
are isomorphic. Taking into account the construction of this
isomorphism in that paper we have that the variety of Nelson
algebras and the variety of Nelson lattices are term equivalent
and the term equivalence is given by the operations we have
defined before.

Remark 7.2
.

Let
A
be a Heyting algebra. Then

(\mathrm{K}(A),\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,{\sim},\mathsf{c},0,1)
is a centered Nelson algebra. Thus,
it follows from Remark
7.1
that
\hat{\mathrm{K}}(A):=(\mathrm{K}(A),\wedge,\vee,*,\rightarrow,\mathsf{c},0,1)
is a centered Nelson lattice,
where for
(a,b)
and
(d,e)
in
\mathrm{K}(A)
the operations

\ast
and
\rightarrow
are given by

\displaystyle(a,b)*(c,d)=(a\wedge c,(a\rightarrow d)\wedge(c\rightarrow b)),

\displaystyle(a,b)\rightarrow(c,d)=((a\rightarrow c)\wedge(d\rightarrow b),a\wedge d).

The following question naturally arises:

•

Is it there a variety of algebras,
in the language of Nelson lattices, which is term equivalent to the variety of subresiduated Nelson lattices?

We do not have an answer for this question.

In
[
2
, Corollary 4.18]
it was proved that there exists an equivalence between
\mathsf{SRL}
and an algebraic category
whose objects are in the language of centered Nelson lattices. We write
\mathsf{KSRL}
for this algebraic category.
In particular, if
A\in\mathsf{SRL}
then
\hat{\mathrm{K}}(A)\in\mathsf{KSRL}
,
where the binary operation
\rightarrow
is defined as in Remark
7.2
. Moreover, for every
T\in\mathsf{KSRL}
there exists

A\in\mathsf{SRL}
such that
T
and
\hat{\mathrm{K}}(A)
are isomorphic algebras.

The following result follows from Theorem
6.6
and
[
2
, Corollary 4.18]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

The categories
\mathsf{KSRL}
and
\mathsf{SNA}^{c}
are equivalent.

The following question also naturally arises:

•

Is there a variety of algebras,
in the language of centered Nelson lattices, which is term equivalent to the variety of centered subresiduated Nelson lattices?

We do not have an answer for this question. However, we know that the usual construction (the one used to show the term equivalence between Nelson algebras and Nelson lattices) does not work by considering centered subresiduated Nelson algebras and the objects of the algebraic category

\mathsf{KSRL}
. In order to show it, assume that the construction works,
which is equivalent to say that this works for the centered subresiduated Nelson algebra
(\mathrm{K}(A),\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,{\sim},\mathsf{c},0,1)

and the algebra
\hat{\mathrm{K}}(A)
where
A
is an arbitrary sr-lattice.
Then, for every
A\in\mathsf{SRL}
and
a,b,c,d\in A

such that
a\wedge b=c\wedge d=0
the equality
(a,b)\Rightarrow(c,d)=(a,b)^{2}\rightarrow(c,d)
is satisfied,
i.e., the inequality
a\rightarrow c\leq d\rightarrow(a\rightarrow b)
is satisfied.
Consider the Boolean algebra of four elements, where
a
and
b
are the atoms, and
D=\{0,a,1\}
.
We have that
(A,D)
, or directly
A
, is an sr-lattice. Define
c=a
and
d=b
. We have that

a\rightarrow c=1
and
d\rightarrow(a\rightarrow b)=d\rightarrow 0=a
, so
a\rightarrow c\nleq d\rightarrow(a\rightarrow b)
, which is a contradiction.

Problem 2: Generalize the equivalence between
\mathsf{NA}
and a
category of enriched Heyting algebras

We know that every Nelson algebra can be represented as a special twist structure of a Heyting
algebra. This correspondence was formulated as a categorical equivalence (by Sendlewski
in the early 1990’s, see
[
19
]
and also in
[
21
]
) between Nelson algebras and a category of enriched Heyting algebras,
which made it possible to transfer a number of fundamental results from the more widely
studied theory of intuitionistic algebras to the realm of Nelson algebras.

The objects of the category of enriched Heyting algebras above mentioned are pairs
(A,R)
, where
A
is a Heyting
algebra and
R
is a Boolean congruence of
A
(i.e.,
R
is a congruence such that
A/R
is a Boolean algebra).
Congruences of any Heyting algebra can be represented by filters, and filters corresponding to Boolean congruences
are precisely those containing all dense elements, i.e., elements a such that
\neg a=0

[
15
]
. This
allows to replace the notion of a Boolean congruence by the notion of a filter
containing dense elements, which will be called Boolean filter.
Thus, we may consider pairs
(A,F)
where
A
is a Heyting algebra and
F
is a Boolean filter.
The categorical equivalence for
\mathsf{NA}
can be presented as follows. We define
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
as the category whose objects are pairs
(A,F)
, where
A
is a Heyting algebra and
F
is a Boolean filter, and whose morphisms

f:(A,F)\rightarrow(B,G)
are homomorphisms such that
f(F)\subseteq G
. If
(A,F)\in\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
then

\mathrm{K}(A,F):=\{(a,b)\in A\times A:a\wedge b=0\;\text{and}\;a\vee b\in F\}

is a Nelson algebra with the operations mentioned in Section
3
. If
f:(A,F)\rightarrow(B,G)
is a
morphism in
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
then
\mathrm{K}(f):\mathrm{K}(A,F)\rightarrow\mathrm{K}(B,G)
defined by
\mathrm{K}(f)(a,b):=(f(a),f(b))
is a morphism in
\mathsf{NA}
.
Moreover,
\mathrm{K}
can be extended to a functor from
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
to
\mathsf{NA}
.
Conversely, if
T\in\mathsf{NA}
then
\mathsf{C}(T):=(T/\theta,T^{+}/\theta)\in\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
, where
T^{+}:=\{x\in T:x\geq\sim x\}
.
If
f:T\rightarrow U
is a morphism in
\mathsf{NA}
then
\mathsf{C}(f):\mathsf{C}(T)\rightarrow\mathsf{C}(U)
given by
\mathsf{C}(f)(x/\theta):=f(x)/\theta
is a morphism in
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
.
Moreover,
\mathsf{C}
can be extended to a functor from
\mathsf{NA}
to
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
. Furthermore, if
(A,F)\in\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
then

\alpha:(A,F)\rightarrow\mathsf{C}((\mathrm{K}(A,F))
, given as in Lemma
6.5
, is an isomorphism in
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
, and if
T\in\mathsf{NA}

then
\rho:T\rightarrow\mathrm{K}(\mathsf{C}(T))
, given as in Theorem
3.7
, is an isomorphism in
\mathsf{NA}
. The functors
\mathrm{K}
and
\mathsf{C}
establish
a categorical equivalence between
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
and
\mathsf{NA}

[
20
,
21
]
. Therefore, it is natural to think in
the following problem:

•

Is it possible to generalize the above mentioned categorical equivalence in the framework of
\mathsf{SNA}
?

Let
A\in\mathsf{SRL}
. One might think that a possible solution is to consider filters of an algebra
A\in\mathsf{SRL}
that contain the set of dense elements
\mathsf{De}(A):=\{a\in A:\neg a=0\}
(as in the case of Heyting algebras). It is known that in Heyting algebras
the set of dense elements is a filter. However, in sr-lattices it is not necessarily
satisfied. Indeed, let
A
be the sr-lattice given in Example
2.1
.
Then
\mathsf{De}(A)=\{a,b,1\}
, which is not a filter. In sr-lattices the congruences are given by open filters, i.e., filters
F
such that
1\rightarrow a\in F

whenever
a\in F

[
7
,
4
]
.
In order to try to generalize the equivalence between
\mathsf{Hey}^{*}
and
\mathsf{NA}
, it seems natural to work with pairs

(A,F)
, where
A
is a sr-lattice and
F
is an open filter such that
\mathsf{De}(A)\subseteq F

(these filters will be called subresiduated filters).

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
A\in\mathsf{SRL}
. Then
\mathsf{De}(A)=\{a\vee\neg a:a\in A\}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathsf{De}(A)
. Then
\neg a=0
, so
a=a\vee\neg a
.
Hence
a\in\{b\vee\neg b:b\in A\}
.
Conversely, let
a\in A
. We shall see that
\neg(a\vee\neg a)=0
.
Indeed,
\neg(a\vee\neg a)=\neg a\wedge\neg\neg a=0
. Hence,
a\vee\neg a\in\mathsf{De}(A)
.
∎

We write
\mathsf{SRL}^{*}
for the set whose elements are pairs
(A,F)
, with
A\in\mathsf{SRL}
and

F
a subresiduated filter.

Let
(A,F)\in\mathsf{SRL}^{*}
. We define

\mathrm{K}(A,F):=\{(a,b)\in A\times A:a\wedge b=0\;\text{and}\;a\vee b\in F\}.

Note that
\mathrm{K}(A,A)=\mathrm{K}(A)
.
Let
(a,b),(c,d)\in\mathrm{K}(A,F)
. Then, we have that
(a,b)\wedge(c,d),(a,b)\vee(c,d)
and
\sim(a,b)\in\mathrm{K}(A,F)

(for details see for instance
[
21
]
).

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
(A,F)\in\mathsf{SRL}^{*}
and
(a,b),(c,d)\in\mathrm{K}(A,F)
.
Then
(a,b)\Rightarrow(c,d)\in\mathrm{K}(A,F)
.

Proof.

Let
(a,b),(c,d)\in\mathrm{K}(A,F)
. We will prove that
(a\rightarrow c,a\wedge d)\in\mathrm{K}(A,F)
. First, note that since
(a\rightarrow c,a\wedge d)\in\mathrm{K}(A)
,
(a\rightarrow c)\wedge a\wedge d=0
. We will see that
(a\rightarrow c)\vee(a\wedge d)\in F
. By distributive law,
(a\rightarrow c)\vee(a\wedge d)=((a\rightarrow c)\vee a)\wedge((a\rightarrow c)\vee d)
. It is immediate to see that
\neg a\leq a\rightarrow c
and in consequence
\neg a\vee a\leq(a\rightarrow c)\vee a
. Since
\neg a\vee a\in\mathsf{De}(A)\subseteq F
, we get
(a\rightarrow c)\vee a\in F
. We only need to check that
(a\rightarrow c)\vee d\in F
. To do so, we know that
(1\rightarrow(c\vee d))\wedge((c\vee d)\rightarrow c)\leq 1\rightarrow c
. Since,
(c\vee d)\rightarrow c=(c\rightarrow c)\wedge(d\rightarrow c)=d\rightarrow c
, and
d\rightarrow c=(d\rightarrow c)\wedge(d\rightarrow d)=d\rightarrow(c\wedge d)=d\rightarrow 0
, we get
(1\rightarrow(c\vee d))\wedge\neg d\leq 1\rightarrow c
. Then,

((1\rightarrow(c\vee d))\wedge\neg d)\vee d\leq(1\rightarrow c)\vee d
, i.e.,

((1\rightarrow(c\vee d))\vee d)\wedge(\neg d\vee d)\leq(1\rightarrow c)\vee d.

Since
c\vee d\in F
and
F
is an open filter, it follows that
1\rightarrow(c\vee d)\in F
and thus,
(1\rightarrow(c\vee d))\vee d\in F
. From
\neg d\vee d\in F
, we get
(1\rightarrow c)\vee d\in F
. It is easy to see that
(1\rightarrow c)\vee d\leq(a\rightarrow c)\vee d
, therefore we get
(a\rightarrow c)\vee d\in F
, which was our aim.
∎

Thus, for
(A,F)\in\mathsf{SRL}^{*}
we have that
\langle\mathrm{K}(A,F),\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\sim,(0,1),(1,0)\rangle
is a subresiduated Nelson algebra because this is a subalgebra of the subresiduated Nelson algebra
\langle\mathrm{K}(A),\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\sim,(0,1),(1,0)\rangle
. However, this construction can not be extended to a categorical equivalence, unless following the construction which shows the categorical equivalence for the category of enriched Heyting algebras we have mentioned in this section. Indeed, let us consider
T
as the subresiduated Nelson algebra whose Hasse diagram is given in Figure
1
. Then
T/\theta
is isomorphic to the sr-lattice
A
given in the Example
2.1
. Note that
\mathsf{De}(A)=\{a,b,1\}
. So, the only open filter
F
that contains the set
\mathsf{De}(T/\theta)
is
F=T/\theta
and we know that
T
is not isomorphic to
\mathrm{K}(T/\theta,T/\theta)=\mathrm{K}(T/\theta)
.

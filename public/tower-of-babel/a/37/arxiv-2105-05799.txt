Kahan discretizations of skew-symmetric Lotka-Volterra systems and Poisson maps
C. A. Evripidou, P. Kassotakis, P. Vanhaecke

Abstract
. The Kahan discretization of the Lotka-Volterra system, associated with any skew-symmetric graph Γ \Gamma , leads to a family of rational maps, parametrized by the step size. When these maps are Poisson maps with respect to the quadratic Poisson structure of the Lotka-Volterra system, we say that the graph Γ \Gamma has the Kahan-Poisson property. We show that if Γ \Gamma is connected, it has the Kahan-Poisson property if and only if it is a cloning of a graph with vertices 1 , 2 , … , n 1,2,\dots,n , with an arc i → j i\to j precisely when i < j i<j , and with all arcs having the same value. We also prove a similar result for augmented graphs, which correspond with deformed Lotka-Volterra systems and show that the obtained Lotka-Volterra systems and their Kahan discretizations are superintegrable as well as Liouville integrable.

1.
Introduction

With any complex skew-symmetric
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{i,j})
is associated a (skew-symmetric) Lotka-Volterra
system, which is the Hamiltonian system, described in terms of the standard coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}

by the following system of differential equations:

\dot{x}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\;,\ \ i=1,2,\dots,n\;.

(1.1)

The Hamiltonian structure, which is also determined by
A
, is defined by the basic Poisson brackets

\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}=a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}
, for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
, with Hamiltonian
H:=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}
. The matrix

A
may be viewed as the adjacency matrix of a (skew-symmetric) graph
\Gamma
, having the integers
1,2,\dots,n

as vertices and with an arc from
i
to
j
with value
a_{i,j}
when
a_{i,j}\neq 0
and
i<j
. We often think of
the Lotka-Volterra system as being associated with
\Gamma
and denote it by
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
. Notice that
\Gamma
is
determined, up to isomorphism, by
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
, as was shown in
[
2
]
. When the entries of
A
are all
real, one may also consider (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so in what follows we suppose that the entries of

A
belong to
\mathbb{F}
, where
\mathbb{F}
stands for
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
and we consider (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
.

For a system of quadratic differential equations, such as (
1.1
), a natural discretization has
been constructed by Kahan
[
5
]
, leading to a rational map, called its Kahan map (see also

[
4
,
6
]
). Applied to (
1.1
), the Kahan map with step size
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
is the
rational map, corresponding to the field automorphism
\widetilde{}\;:\mathbb{F}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\to\mathbb{F}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, defined by the following formulas, where the right hand side has been obtained from
(
1.1
) by polarization:

\frac{\tilde{x}_{i}-x_{i}}{\varepsilon}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\left(\tilde{x}_{i}x_{j}+x_{i}\tilde{x}_{j}\right)\;,\ \ i=1,2,\dots,n\;.

From the point of view of discrete integrability, a natural question is whether the Kahan map is a Poisson map with
respect to the above Poisson structure. In formulas, this means that
\left\{\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}=a_{i,j}\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{x}_{j}
, for

1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n.
When this is the case, we say that
\Gamma
has the Kahan-Poisson property. A general
skew-symmetric graph
\Gamma
does not have the Kahan-Poisson property. In order to give a more precise answer, let
us denote by
\Gamma_{n}
the skew-symmetric graph with vertices
1,\dots,n
, and with an arc
i\to j
, with value
1
,
for any
i<j
; for
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
we denote by
\gamma\Gamma_{n}
the graph with the same vertices and arcs as
\Gamma_{n}
, but where the value of every arc is
\gamma
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected skew-symmetric graph. Then
\Gamma
has the Kahan-Poisson property if and only if

\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\gamma\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
and some weight
vector
\varpi
for
\Gamma_{n}
.

For a skew-symmetric graph which is not connected, Theorem
1.1
applies to each one of its connected
components.

The notions of cloning and decloning of skew-symmetric graphs and Lotka-Volterra systems were introduced in

[
2
]
in the study of morphisms and automorphisms of graphs and Lotka-Volterra systems. For a graph,
decloning amounts to identifying two (or more) vertices when they have the same neighborhood (which means that the
corresponding lines of its adjacency matrix
A
are identical); the quotient graph is then said to be
irreducible. With this terminology, the theorem can also be stated by saying that the only connected, irreducible
skew-symmetric graphs
\Gamma
which have the Kahan-Poisson property are the graphs
\gamma\Gamma_{n}
, where

n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
.

The fact that
\Gamma_{n}
(and hence
\gamma\Gamma_{n}
, for all
\gamma
) has the Kahan-Poisson property has already been
shown in
[
11
, Prop. 3.8]
. We show in Proposition
2.3
that the Kahan-Poisson property
is preserved by cloning and decloning. This proves the easier, inverse implication in Theorem
1.1
. The
main result which we prove in this paper is the direct implication, which we first show in dimension 3
(Section
3
), then in dimension 4, using the result in dimension 3 (Section
4.2
), and
finally in dimension
n>4
, using the result in dimension 4 (Section
4.3
).

In Section
5
, we prove the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
to augmented
graphs, which correspond to deformed Lotka-Volterra systems (see Section
5
for the definition
of an augmented graph and the KP property for such graphs):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Delta
be an augmented graph of a connected skew-symmetric graph
\Gamma
. Then
\Delta
has the
Kahan-Poisson property if and only if
\Delta
is isomorphic to an augmented graph of
\gamma\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
for some

n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
and some weight vector
\varpi
for
\Gamma_{n}
.

We show in Section
6
that the (deformed) Lotka-Volterra systems, corresponding to the (augmented)
graphs which appear in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, have a discretization with good
integrability properties, namely the Kahan discretization, which is in these cases a Poisson map with respect to
the original Poisson structure, is both superintegrable and Liouville integrable. It follows that the (deformed)
Lotka-Volterra systems whose Kahan discretization is integrable with respect to their original Poisson structure
are characterized by the Kahan-Poisson property.

2.
The Kahan-Poisson property

In this section we introduce the Kahan-Poisson property for skew-symmetric graphs and establish it for a
particular family of such graphs. We first recall the basic facts which we will use about the Kahan discretization of
systems of quadratic differential equations and about skew-symmetric Lotka-Volterra systems; see

[
2
,
5
]
for details.

2.1.
The Kahan discretization

Consider a system of differential equations on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
:

\dot{x}_{i}=Q_{i}(x)\;,\ \ i=1,2,\dots,n\;.

(2.1)

Here,
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
Q_{i}
is assumed to be a quadratic form, whose corresponding symmetric bilinear form
is denoted by
B_{i}
, so that
Q_{i}(x)=B_{i}(x,x)
. The
Kahan discretization
of (
2.1
) is
by definition given by

\frac{\tilde{x}_{i}-x_{i}}{2\varepsilon}=B_{i}(\tilde{x},x)\;,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n\;,

(2.2)

where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
is a non-zero parameter, the
step size
. When (linearly!) solved for
\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}
, one gets a family of birational maps from
\mathbb{F}^{n}
to itself, parametrized by

\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
. Thinking of
\varepsilon
as being fixed, it is called the
Kahan map
. We will mostly
work with the corresponding endomorphism
K
of the field of rational functions
\mathbb{F}(x)=\mathbb{F}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
,
defined by
K(x_{i}):=\tilde{x}_{i}
, for
i=1,\dots,n
; we call it the
Kahan morphism
.

2.2.
Lotka-Volterra systems

We are interested in the Kahan discretization of skew-symmetric Lotka-Volterra systems. As recalled in the
introduction, a Lotka-Volterra system is associated with any skew-symmetric
n\times n
matrix
A
; we also view

A=(a_{i,j})
as the adjacency matrix of a skew-symmetric graph
\Gamma=(S,A)
, with vertex set

S=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
, and think of the Lotka-Volterra system as being associated with the graph
\Gamma
, denoted

\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
. The Poisson structure of
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
, which we consider here as a Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{F}(x)
, is
the quadratic bracket, given by
\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}=a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}
, for
i,j=1,2,\dots,n
. It makes
(\mathbb{F}(x),\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\})
into
a Poisson field. The Hamiltonian vector field
\mathcal{X}_{H}=\left\{\cdot\,,H\right\}
on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, associated with the Hamiltonian

H:=x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots+x_{n}
, is given by the following quadratic differential equations:

\dot{x}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\;,\ \ i=1,2,\dots,n\;.

Its Kahan map is defined by the following specialisation of (
2.2
):

\frac{\tilde{x}_{i}-x_{i}}{\varepsilon}=\tilde{x}_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{j}+x_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\tilde{x}_{j}\;,\ \ i=1,2,\dots,n\;.

(2.3)

An important example for this paper is the Lotka-Volterra system
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma_{n})
, whose underlying graph
\Gamma_{n}
has
vertices
1,2,\dots,n
and has an arc from
i
to
j
with value
1
when
i<j
(see Figure
1
).

\Gamma_{n}
corresponds to the skew-symmetric

. The pictured graph is

2.3.
The Kahan-Poisson property

The property of the Kahan map in which we are interested in this paper is its preservation of the Poisson structure
of the associated Lotka-Volterra system, i.e., that the Kahan map, which is a birational automorphism of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
,
is also a Poisson map (and hence a birational Poisson automorphism). Before giving the definition, let us clarify
the independence on
\varepsilon
: when the Kahan map is a Poisson map for some value of
\varepsilon
then it is a
Poisson map for all values of
\varepsilon
. Indeed, the defining equations (
2.3
) of the Kahan map
are homogeneous when
\varepsilon
is given weight
-1
, while giving a weight
1
to all
x_{i}
; the claim then
follows from the fact that homotheties of quadratic Poisson structures are Poisson maps (see
[
8
, Proposition
8.16]
). It also shows that if the Kahan map of a Lotka-Volterra system
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
is a Poisson map then
the Kahan map of the Lotka-Volterra system
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\gamma\Gamma)
, where
\gamma\Gamma
is
\Gamma
with all of its
values scaled by
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
, is also a Poisson map. We therefore set
\varepsilon=1
and when we speak of
the Kahan map or the Kahan morphism of a Lotka-Volterra system, it is implictly assumed that

\varepsilon=1
. Notice that when
\gamma=-1
, then
\gamma\Gamma=-\Gamma
is the graph
\Gamma
with the direction
of all its arcs reversed.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a skew-symmetric graph. Then
\Gamma
is said to have the
Kahan-Poisson property
(or

KP property
) if the Kahan map of its associated Lotka-Volterra system
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
is a Poisson map.

In algebraic terms, this means that the Kahan morphism
K
is an automorphism of Poisson fields, i.e.,

\left\{K(x_{i}),K(x_{j})\right\}=K\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}
, for all
i,j=1,\dots,n
, which can also be written as
\left\{\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}=\widetilde{\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}}
.

A first family of skew-symmetric graphs which have the KP property is given by the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2
.

[
11
]

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the graph
\Gamma_{n}
has the KP property.

Proof.

For the proof, we refer to
[
11
, Proposition 3.8]
. Yet, we point out the crucial fact that makes the
computation feasible. In terms of the variables
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
, and
\tilde{u}_{1},\dots,\tilde{u}_{n}
, defined by

u_{i}:=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{i}
, and
\tilde{u}_{i}:=\tilde{x}_{1}+\tilde{x}_{2}+\cdots+\tilde{x}_{i}
, the Kahan map (with
\varepsilon=1
) takes the
simple separated form

\tilde{u}_{i}=u_{i}\frac{1+H}{1-H+2u_{i}}\;,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n\;,

(2.4)

while the Poisson bracket takes the form

\left\{u_{i},u_{j}\right\}=u_{i}(u_{j}-u_{i})\;,\quad\hbox{ for }1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n\;.

(2.5)

It therefore suffices to verify that
\left\{\tilde{u}_{i},\tilde{u}_{j}\right\}=\tilde{u}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{j}-\tilde{u}_{i})
, for

1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
, with
\tilde{u}_{i}
given by (
2.4
), which is easily done by direct
computation, using (
2.5
).
∎

As we mentioned earlier, if we multiply the adjacency matrix
A
of
\Gamma
by any non-zero scalar, the property
for the corresponding graph of having the KP property is not affected. In particular, Proposition
2.2

implies that
\gamma\Gamma_{n}
has the KP property for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
.

2.4.
Cloning and decloning

The cloning of Lotka-Volterra systems, which was introduced in
[
2
]
, is the inverse operation to
decloning, which we already recalled in the introduction. Let
\Gamma=(S,A)
be a skew-symmetric graph with vertex
set
S=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
. Let
\varpi
be a
weight vector
for
\Gamma
, i.e.,
\varpi
is a function

\varpi:S\to\mathbb{N}^{*}
. The
cloning
of
(\Gamma,\varpi)
is the skew-symmetric graph

\Gamma^{\varpi}=(S^{\varpi},A^{\varpi})
, constructed as follows: on the one hand, every vertex
i\in S
gives rise
to
\varpi(i)
vertices in
S^{\varpi}
, which we denote by
(i,1),(i,2),\dots,(i,\varpi(i))
. On the other hand, the
entries
a^{\varpi}_{(i,k),(j,\ell)}
of the (skew-symmetric) adjacency matrix
A^{\varpi}
of
\Gamma^{\varpi}
are defined
by
a^{\varpi}_{(i,k),(j,\ell)}:=a_{i,j}
, for
i,j\in S
and
1\leqslant k\leqslant\varpi(i)
,
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant\varpi(j)
. By
definition, the
cloning
of
(\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma),\varpi)
is
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma^{\varpi})
. We will denote the linear
coordinates corresponding to the vertices
(i,k)
by
y_{i,k}
and write, as above,
\mathbb{F}(y)
for the field of
rational functions
\mathbb{F}(y_{1,1},y_{1,2},\dots,y_{n,\varpi(n)})
. The Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{F}(y)
associated with

A^{\varpi}
is given by
\left\{y_{i,k},y_{j,\ell}\right\}^{\varpi}=a_{i,j}y_{i,k}y_{j,\ell}
, as follows from the definition
of
\Gamma^{\varpi}
. For
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and
1\leqslant k,m\leqslant\varpi(i)
the functions

y_{i,m}/y_{i,k}
are
Casimir functions
of
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}^{\varpi}
: they belong to the center of the Poisson
bracket. As Hamiltonian, we again take the sum of all variables,

H^{\varpi}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\varpi(i)}y_{i,k}
. The Hamiltonian vector field
\mathcal{X}_{H^{\varpi}}
of

\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma^{\varpi})
is then given by the following differential equations:

\dot{y}_{i,k}=y_{i,k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\varpi(j)}a_{i,j}y_{j,\ell}\;,\qquad\ i=1,\dots,n,\ k=1,\dots,\varpi(i)\;.

Its Kahan discretization is implicitly defined, as in (
2.3
), by the following equations:

\frac{\bar{y}_{i,k}-y_{i,k}}{\varepsilon}=\bar{y}_{i,k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\varpi(j)}a_{i,j}y_{j,\ell}+y_{i,k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\varpi(j)}a_{i,j}\bar{y}_{j,\ell}\;,\qquad\ i=1,\dots,n,\ k=1,\dots,\varpi(i)\;.

(2.6)

The corresponding automorphism
K^{\varpi}
of
\mathbb{F}(y)
is defined by

K^{\varpi}(y_{i,k}):=\bar{y}_{i,k}
, for
i=1,\dots,n
and
k=1,\dots,\varpi(i)
. We view
\mathbb{F}(y)
as a field extension of

\mathbb{F}(x)
using the
decloning morphism

\Sigma:\mathbb{F}(x)\to\mathbb{F}(y)
, defined by

\Sigma(x_{i}):=\sum_{k=1}^{\varpi(i)}y_{i,k}\;.

(2.7)

We show in the following proposition that the decloning morphism commutes with the Kahan morphism and that the KP
property is preserved under cloning and decloning:

Proposition 2.3
.

With the above definitions and notations, the following diagram of fields and field morphisms is commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{F}(x),\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{F}(x),\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathbb{F}(y),\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}^{\varpi})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathbb{F}(y),\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}^{\varpi})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{\varpi}}

(2.8)

The vertical arrow
\Sigma
is a Poisson morphism, while
K
is a Poisson morphism if and only if
K^{\varpi}
is a
Poisson morphism.

Proof.

We first show that the diagram is commutative. Let us set
\varepsilon=1
, as before. Using
\Sigma
,
(
2.6
) can be rewritten as

\bar{y}_{i,k}-y_{i,k}=\bar{y}_{i,k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\Sigma(x_{j})+y_{i,k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\overline{\Sigma(x_{j})}\;.

(2.9)

For fixed
i
, summing up (
2.9
) for
k=1,\dots,\varpi(i)
, we get

\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})}-\Sigma(x_{i})=\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\Sigma(x_{j})+\Sigma(x_{i})\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}\overline{\Sigma(x_{j})}\;.

(2.10)

Recall that
\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}
is the unique solution to (
2.3
); if we write this solution
by making explicit its dependency on
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
as
\tilde{x}_{i}=R_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, then it follows from
comparing (
2.3
) and (
2.10
) that

\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})}=R_{i}(\Sigma(x_{1}),\dots,\Sigma(x_{n}))
. Since
\Sigma
is an algebra homomorphism and
R_{i}
is
a rational function of its arguments,

\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})}=R_{i}(\Sigma(x_{1}),\dots,\Sigma(x_{n}))=\Sigma(R_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}))=\Sigma(\tilde{x}_{i})\;,

showing the commutativity of the diagram. For
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
we have

\left\{\Sigma(x_{i}),\Sigma(x_{j})\right\}^{\varpi}=\sum_{k=1}^{\varpi(i)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\varpi(j)}\left\{y_{i,k},y_{j,\ell}\right\}^{\varpi}=a_{i,j}\sum_{k=1}^{\varpi(i)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\varpi(j)}y_{i,k}y_{j,\ell}=a_{i,j}\Sigma(x_{i})\Sigma(x_{j})=\Sigma\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}\;,

as follows from the definitions of the Poisson brackets and of
\Sigma
. As a consequence, the vertical arrows in
the diagram (
2.8
) are morphisms of Poisson fields. In order to show that the two
horizontal arrows in that diagram are at the same time morphisms of Poisson fields, we first show that

\Sigma(x_{i})/y_{i,k}\hbox{ is a Casimir of }\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}^{\varpi}\hbox{ and is an invariant of }K^{\varpi}\;,

(2.11)

for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and
1\leqslant k\leqslant\varpi(i)
. The first statement in (
2.11
)
follows at once from the fact that
y_{i,m}/y_{i,k}
is for any
1\leqslant k,m\leqslant\varpi(i)
a Casimir
function of
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}^{\varpi}
, as we already recalled. The second statement means that

\Sigma(x_{i})/y_{i,k}=\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})}/\bar{y}_{i,k}
. To prove the latter, divide (
2.9
) by

, to see that
\bar{y}_{i,k}/y_{i,k}
is independent of

; then
\bar{y}_{i,k}/y_{i,k}=\bar{y}_{i,m}/y_{i,m}
, for
k
and

as above, so that
y_{i,m}/y_{i,k}
is an invariant, and hence also

\Sigma(x_{i})/y_{i,k}=\sum_{m=1}^{\varpi(i)}y_{i,m}/y_{i,k}
. We can therefore write

\bar{y}_{i,k}=\frac{y_{i,k}}{\Sigma(x_{i})}\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\bar{y}_{j,\ell}=\frac{y_{j,\ell}}{\Sigma(x_{j})}\overline{\Sigma(x_{j})}\;.

(2.12)

In the second formula above,
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
and
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant\varpi(j)
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\{\bar{y}_{i,k},\bar{y}_{j,\ell}\right\}^{\varpi}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:y_to_Y}}}{{=}}\left\{\frac{y_{i,k}}{\Sigma(x_{i})}\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})},\frac{y_{j,\ell}}{\Sigma(x_{j})}\overline{\Sigma(x_{j})}\right\}^{\varpi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Casimirs}}}{{=}}\frac{y_{i,k}y_{j,\ell}}{\Sigma(x_{i}x_{j})}\left\{\overline{\Sigma(x_{i})},\overline{\Sigma(x_{j})}\right\}^{\varpi}

where we have used in the last step that

is a Poisson morphism. It follows that

\displaystyle\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\hbox{$K^{\varpi}$ is a Poisson morphism.}

3.2.
Computing efficiently the Poisson brackets

Since the Kahan map of a Lotka-Volterra system is given by rational functions, which are already quite complicated
in dimension 3, we explain here how the condition that the Kahan morphism is a Poisson morphism leads to necessary
conditions that are computable by hand, and which will actually be sufficient for our purposes. Since we will use
our method also in dimension 4, we will explain it for any skew-symmetric graph
\Gamma
with
n>2
vertices. As
before, the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
is denoted by
A
. We first write down the basic equations and introduce
some notation. We write the equations (
2.3
) in two different ways (recall that we have set

\varepsilon=1
). On the one hand, we write them as a linear system in
\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}
,

M\tilde{x}^{T}=x^{T}\;.

(3.1)

Notice that every entry of
M
is an affine function of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. For a point
P\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
we denote by

M(P)
the evaluation of
M
at
P
and by
\tilde{P}
the image of
P
under the Kahan map, i.e.,

\tilde{P}=(\tilde{x}_{1}(P),\tilde{x}_{2}(P),\tilde{x}_{3}(P))
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that (
2.3
) can also be written
as
\mathcal{L}_{k}=0
, for
k=1,2,\dots,n
, where
\mathcal{L}_{k}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{k}:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{k,j}x_{j}-1\right)\tilde{x}_{k}+x_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{k,j}\tilde{x}_{j}+x_{k}\;.

(3.2)

Indeed, (
3.2
) is the
k
-th entry of
x^{T}-M\tilde{x}^{T}
. We denote the column vector whose
k
-th entry is

\mathcal{L}_{k}
by
\mathcal{L}
and write
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{P})
for
\mathcal{L}
with the functions
\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}
evaluated at
P
. Each
entry of
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{P})
is also an affine function of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
.

We can now explain how to explicitly compute, for given
i,j
and
P
, the condition
\left\{\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)=a_{i,j}\tilde{x}_{i}(P)\tilde{x}_{j}(P)
. We do this in four different steps.

Step 1:
The image point
\tilde{P}
.

If
M(P)
is non-singular, i.e.,
\det M(P)\neq 0
, the Kahan map is defined at
P
and its image
\tilde{P}
is computed
from

M(P)\tilde{P}^{T}=P^{T}\;.

(3.3)

Notice that
\det M(P)
depends (linearly) on the entries
a_{k,\ell}
of
A
, so for a given
P
it may be zero
for some values of these entries
a_{k,\ell}
. The computations that follow are then not valid for these values; as
we will see, it is important to keep track of these values. In the steps which follow, we assume that
\det M(P)\neq 0
.

Step 2:
Computation of the Poisson brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{j},x_{\ell}\right\}(P)
,
\ell=1,\dots,n
.

We compute the Poisson brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{k},x_{\ell}\right\}(P)
for
k,\ell=1,\dots,n
. This can easily be done directly
from (
3.1
) without solving the latter for
\tilde{x}
, as follows. Let us denote by

\left\{M,x_{\ell}\right\}
the matrix obtained by taking the Poisson bracket of every entry of
M
with
x_{\ell}
, and
similarly for the column vector
\left\{\tilde{x}^{T},x_{\ell}\right\}
. Then it follows from (
3.1
), using
the Leibniz rule, that
\left\{M,x_{\ell}\right\}\tilde{x}^{T}+M\left\{\tilde{x}^{T},x_{\ell}\right\}=\left\{x^{T},x_{\ell}\right\},

so that, at
P
,

M(P)\left\{\tilde{x}^{T},x_{\ell}\right\}(P)=\left\{x^{T},x_{\ell}\right\}(P)-\left\{M,x_{\ell}\right\}(P)\tilde{P}^{T}\;.

(3.4)

This gives a linear system for the brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{k},x_{\ell}\right\}(P)
,
k=1,\dots,n
. Notice that the matrix governing
the linear system is again
M(P)
, so that these brackets are uniquely determined from (
3.4
) (recall
that we have assumed that
\det M(P)\neq 0
). Also, the right hand side of (
3.4
) is equal to

\left\{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{P}),x_{\ell}\right\}(P)
, since
\mathcal{L}_{k}
is the
k
-th entry of
x^{T}-M\tilde{x}^{T}
(see
(
3.1
)). It means that, in order the compute the right hand side of (
3.4
), we
can start from the equations (
2.3
) defining the Kahan map, evaluate the functions
\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}

at
P
, and then take the Poisson bracket at
P
of the remaining affine functions in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with

x_{\ell}
. Doing this for
\ell=1,\dots,n
and solving the resulting linear system, we find the brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{k},x_{\ell}\right\}(P)
for
k,\ell=1,\dots,n
. They are rational functions of the entries of the adjacency matrix
A
of

\Gamma
.

Step 3:
Computation of the Poisson brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)
.

The Poisson brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)
for
k=1,\dots,n
are computed in a quite similar way, using the
n

Poisson brackets which were computed in Step 2 (recall that
j
is fixed). In this step we take the Poisson bracket
of (
3.1
) with
\tilde{x}_{j}
at
P
to obtain, as in Step 2,

M(P)\left\{\tilde{x}^{T},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)=\left\{x^{T},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)-\left\{M,\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)\tilde{P}^{T}=\left\{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{P}),\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)\;.

(3.5)

Notice that, again, the defining matrix of the linear system is
M(P)
and that, again, the right hand side can
easily be computed from the equations defining the Kahan map, where the functions
\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}
are evaluated
at
P
; it is here that one needs the Poisson brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{j},x_{\ell}\right\}
, for
\ell=1,2,\dots,n
, which were
computed in Step 2. Solving the resulting linear system, we find the brackets
\left\{\tilde{x}_{k},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)
for

k=1,\dots,n
. Again, they are rational functions of the entries of the adjacency matrix
A
of
\Gamma
.

Step 4:
The Poisson morphism condition(s).

From the previous step we know
\left\{\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)
and we can now write down explicitly the condition that

\left\{\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{j}\right\}(P)=a_{i,j}\tilde{x}_{i}(P)\tilde{x}_{j}(P)\;,

which is a sufficient condition for the Kahan morphism to be a Poisson morphism. Since the left hand side is a
rational function of the entries of the adjacency matrix
A
, we get a rational condition on these entries. If the
condition is not satisfied, we can conclude that the graph
\Gamma
does not have the KP property. This is how we will use
this condition in what follows.

4.1.
Reduction

Lotka-Volterra systems admit a class of natural reductions, which we first recall. Let
\Gamma=(S,A)
be a graph
with vertex set
S=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
, where
n>1
. Let
S^{\prime}\subset S
be a proper, non-empty subset and denote

m:=\#S^{\prime}
. We denote by
\Gamma^{\prime}=(S^{\prime},A^{\prime})
the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
. The natural inclusion map

\Gamma^{\prime}\to\Gamma
is a graph morphism, hence induces a morphism of Lotka-Volterra systems

\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma^{\prime})\to\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
(see
[
2
, Prop. 3.2]
). If we denote the natural coordinates on these
Lotka-Volterra systems respectively by
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, then the induced morphism

\imath:\mathbb{F}(y)\to\mathbb{F}(x)
is an injective Poisson morphism; if we denote by
\tau
the unique strictly increasing
function
\tau:\left\{1,\dots,m\right\}\to\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
which takes values in
S^{\prime}
, then
\imath(y_{i})=x_{\tau(i)}
, for

i=1,\dots,m
. Let us denote by
K:\mathbb{F}(x)\to\mathbb{F}(x)
and by
K^{\prime}:\mathbb{F}(y)\to\mathbb{F}(y)
the Kahan morphisms of

\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
, respectively of
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma^{\prime})
. Then
K^{\prime}
is the restriction of
K
to
\mathbb{F}(y)
, i.e., the
following diagram is commutative:

5.
Deformed Lotka-Volterra systems

In this section we consider deformations of Lotka-Volterra systems, associated with diagonal Poisson brackets which
are deformed by constants. We first introduce and study these deformed Poisson brackets, which are associated with
augmented graphs, and define the KP property for such graphs. We then characterize these graphs showing that they are
closely related to the graphs

5.1.
Deformed diagonal Poisson brackets

We consider in this section deformations of diagonal Poisson brackets with constant terms; they lead to
Hamiltonian systems which are deformations of Lotka-Volterra systems, and which we will study in the next
subsection.

Definition 5.1

A Poisson bracket

)

The conditions on a matrix

Proposition 5.2

We define a biderivation

\displaystyle 0

5.2.
The Kahan-Poisson property for deformations of Lotka-Volterra systems

We show in this subsection that the deformations with constant terms of the Lotka-Volterra systems
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)

for which their Kahan map is a Poisson map with respect to the corresponding deformed Poisson structure, are
precisely those for which their underlying graph
\Gamma
has the KP property. According to Theorem
1.1
, this
means that every connected component of
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\gamma\Gamma_{n}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
and

n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

We first recall from
[
5
]
the recipe of the Kahan discretization of a general class of systems of
differential equations which covers the deformations of Lotka-Volterra systems that we consider. To this end,
consider a system of differential equations on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
of the form

\dot{x}_{i}=Q_{i}(x)+c_{i}\;,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n\,,

(5.2)

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}),\,Q_{i}
is a quadratic form and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}
. If we denote by
B_{i}
the bilinear
form, corresponding to
Q_{i}
, so that
Q_{i}(x)=B_{i}(x,x)
, then the
Kahan discretization of (
5.2
)

is given by

\frac{\tilde{x}_{i}-x_{i}}{2\varepsilon}=B_{i}(\tilde{x}_{,}x)+c_{i}\;,\quad i=1,2,\dots,n\,.

(5.3)

Solving (
5.3
) linearly for
\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}
we get a family of birational maps on

\mathbb{F}^{n}
, parametrized by the step size
\varepsilon
. As in the undeformed case, for fixed
\varepsilon
we call
the corresponding map the
Kahan map
. The corresponding endomorphism of the field of rational functions

\mathbb{F}(x)
, defined for
i=1,\dots,n
by
K_{b}(x_{i}):=\tilde{x}_{i}
, is called the
Kahan morphism
.

We now introduce the deformed Lotka-Volterra systems which we will study. Let
\Delta=(S,A,B)
be an augmented
graph, whose associated Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{F}(x)
is denoted by
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}_{b}
. Recall that it is defined by

\left\{x_{i},x_{j}\right\}_{b}:=a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}+b_{i,j}
for
i,j\in S
. Taking
H:=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}
as Hamiltonian, the
Hamiltonian vector field
\left\{\cdot\,,H\right\}_{b}
is given by the following system of differential equations:

\dot{x}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}+c_{i}\;,\ \ i\in S\,,

Proposition 5.9

Let
\Gamma

\Delta

We first prove that (i) implies (ii). If
\Gamma

1.1
that

\Gamma
is isomorphic to

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}

on
\Gamma_{n}

be any augmented graph of
\Gamma

is also a Poisson morphism. This shows that
\Delta
also has the KP property. The proof that

deformation matrix. Suppose now that

6.
Liouville and superintegrability

We have shown in the previous sections that the only connected skew-symmetric graphs
\Gamma
which have
the KP property are of the form
\Gamma=\gamma\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
\varpi
is a weight
vector on
\Gamma_{n}
; also, that the only augmented graphs
\Delta
which have the KP property are augmented graphs of

\Gamma=\gamma\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
. We now show that the corresponding Lotka-Volterra systems
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma)
and deformed
Lotka-Volterra systems
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Delta)
are both Liouville integrable and superintegrable, and that their Kahan
discretizations are both Liouville integrable and superintegrable as well. By a simple rescaling argument, already
used several times above, we may assume that
\gamma=1
, so we will consider in what follows only
\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
and
its augmentations.

We first fix the notation and the context. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, let
\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
be a cloned graph of
\Gamma_{n}
, with

\varpi(1)=\varpi(n)=1
, and let
b\in\mathbb{F}
. We denote by
\Delta_{n}
the augmented graph of
\Gamma_{n}
, where the
deformation matrix
B
has as only possible non-zero entries
b_{1,n}=-b_{n,1}:=b
. Similarly,
\Delta_{n}^{\varpi}

denotes the augmented graph of
\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
, where the deformation matrix
B
has as only possible non-zero
entries
b_{(1,1),(n,1)}=-b_{(n,1),(1,1)}:=b
. We consider the fields
\mathbb{F}(x)
and
\mathbb{F}(y)
, where

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1,1},y_{2,1},y_{2,2},\dots,y_{n,1})
, as before.
We consider on
\mathbb{F}(x)
the Poisson bracket
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}_{b}
, associated with
\Delta_{n}
, and on
\mathbb{F}(y)
the Poisson
bracket
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}_{b}^{\varpi}
, associated with
\Delta_{n}^{\varpi}
.

Since we will take in this section a more geometrical point of view, we view
\mathbb{F}(x)
and
\mathbb{F}(y)
as the field of
(rational) functions on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
, respectively on
\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|}
; the Poisson structure on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
and
on
\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|}
corresponding to
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}_{b}
and
\left\{\cdot\,,\cdot\right\}_{b}^{\varpi}
will respectively be denoted by
\pi_{b}
and

\pi_{b}^{\varpi}
. The standard Lotka-Volterra Hamiltonians on
\mathbb{F}^{n}
and on
\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|}
, which are always the
sum of all coordinates, are denoted by
H
and
H^{\varpi}
.

In order to show the integrability of the Hamiltonian system
(\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|},\pi_{b}^{\varpi},H^{\varpi})
and of its
Kahan discretization, we first recall from
[
1
]
the integrability of the deformed Lotka-Volterra
system
(\mathbb{F}^{n},\pi_{b},H)
and its Kahan discretization. For
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant\left[\frac{n-1}{2}\right]
, consider
the following rational functions:

F_{\ell}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{2\ell-1}+\frac{\beta}{x_{n}}\right)\displaystyle\frac{x_{2\ell+1}x_{2\ell+3}\ldots x_{n}}{x_{2\ell}x_{2\ell+2}\ldots x_{n-1}}\;,&\mbox{ if}\ n\mbox{ is odd},\\
\\
\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{2\ell}+\frac{\beta}{x_{n}}\right)\displaystyle\frac{x_{2\ell+2}x_{2\ell+4}\ldots x_{n}}{x_{2\ell+1}x_{2\ell+3}\ldots x_{n-1}}\;,&\mbox{ if}\ n\mbox{ is even},\end{array}\right.

(6.1)

and let
G_{\ell}:=\jmath(F_{\ell})
, where
\jmath:\mathbb{F}(x)\to\mathbb{F}(x)
is the involutive field automorphism, defined by

\jmath(x_{i}):=x_{n+1-i}
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. Together with the Hamiltonian
H
, this yields exactly
n-1
different
rational functions: for example, when
n
is odd then all
F_{\ell}
and
G_{\ell}
are different, except for

F_{1}=G_{1}
. The following facts were obtained in
[
1
]
:

(1)

The
n-1
rational functions
F_{\ell}
,
G_{\ell}
and
H
are first integrals of
(\mathbb{F}^{n},\pi_{b},H)
;

(2)

They are
independent
, i.e., their differentials are independent on an open dense subset of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
;

(3)

The rank
\hbox{Rk\,}\pi_{b}
of the Poisson structure
\pi_{b}
is
n
when
n
is even, otherwise it is
n-1
;

(4)

The first integrals
F_{\ell}
are
in involution
, i.e., commute for the Poisson bracket;

(5)

The first integrals
F_{\ell},G_{\ell}
and
H
are invariants of the Kahan discretization of
(\mathbb{F}^{n},\pi_{b},H)
.

Items (1) and (2) say that
(\mathbb{F}^{n},\pi_{b},H)
is
superintegrable
, i.e., has
n-1
independent first
integrals, where
n
is the dimension of the ambient space. The items (1) – (4) imply that the independent first
integrals
F_{\ell}
are in involution and that, with the Hamiltonian, their number is
n-\frac{1}{2}\hbox{Rk\,}\pi_{b}
, which is
exactly the number required for the
Liouville integrability
of
(\mathbb{F}^{n},\pi_{b},H)
; for example, when
n
is
odd,
\hbox{Rk\,}\pi_{b}=n-1
and we have
(n+1)/2
functions
F_{1},\dots,F_{(n-1)/2},H
which are in involution. Combined
with (5) and the fact that the Kahan map is a Poisson map one gets from it that the Kahan discretization of

(\mathbb{F}^{n},\pi_{b},H)
is both superintegrable and Liouville integrable, with as invariants the first integrals of the
continuous system.

We use these five properties, the properties of the decloning map and of the Poisson structure
\pi_{b}^{\varpi}
to
prove the integrability the Hamiltonian system
(\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|},\pi_{b}^{\varpi},H^{\varpi})
and its Kahan
discretization. As we have already seen in the proof of Proposition
5.8
, for any

1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and
1<k\leqslant\varpi(i)
,
y_{i,k}/y_{i,1}
is a Casimir function of
\pi_{b}^{\varpi}
,
yielding
|\varpi|-n
different Casimir functions, which are clearly independent. The rank of
\pi_{b}^{\varpi}
is
therefore at most
n
, i.e., at most
n-1
when
n
is odd, and at most
n
when
n
is even. In fact, we have
equality. To see this, consider the
decloning map

S:\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|}\to\mathbb{F}^{n}
corresponding to decloning
morphism
\Sigma
, i.e.,
\Sigma=S^{*}
, which is a dominant (actually surjective) Poisson map, since
\Sigma
is an
injective Poisson morphism. It follows that the rank of
\pi_{b}^{\varpi}
is also bounded from below by the rank of

\pi_{b}
, which is
n-1
when
n
is odd, and
n
when
n
is even, and so we have equality.

The cited properties of the decloning map
S
also imply that the
n-1
functions
S^{*}(F_{\ell})
,
S^{*}(G_{\ell})
and

H^{\varpi}
are independent first integrals of
(\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|},\pi_{b}^{\varpi},H^{\varpi})
and that they are in
involution. Combined with the Casimir functions we get
n-1+|\varpi|-n=|\varpi|-1
different functions; from the
simple form of the Casimir functions, it is clear that the former first integrals are independent from the Casimir
functions, so that we have
|\varpi|-1=\dim\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|}-1
independent first integrals, which shows that

(\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|},\pi_{b}^{\varpi},H^{\varpi})
is superintegrable.

Since
S
is a Poisson map, the independent functions
S^{*}(F_{\ell})
and
H^{\varpi}=S^{*}(H)
are in involution;
together with the
|\varpi|-n
Casimir functions, we get
(n+1)/2+|\varpi|-n=|\varpi|-\frac{1}{2}\hbox{Rk\,}\pi_{b}^{\varpi}

independent functions, including the Hamiltonian, in involution, which shows that

(\mathbb{F}^{|\varpi|},\pi_{b}^{\varpi},H^{\varpi})
is Liouville integrable.

Moreover, the
|\varpi|-1
independent first integrals are invariants of
K_{b}^{\varpi}
. For the Casimir functions

y_{i,k}/y_{i,1}
, we have already seen this in the proof of Proposition
5.8
. For

1\leqslant\ell\leqslant\left[\frac{n-1}{2}\right]
, the commutativity of (
5.6
) implies that

K_{b}^{\varpi}(\Sigma(F_{\ell}))=\Sigma(K_{b}(F_{\ell}))=\Sigma(F_{\ell})\;,

where we have used in the last step that
F_{\ell}
is an invariant of
K_{b}
,
K_{b}(F_{\ell})=F_{\ell}
. Since
H^{\varpi}
is
linear, it is also an invariant of
K_{b}^{\varpi}
.

Summing up, and combined with the integrability results in the non-deformed case, it leads to the following
proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and let
\varpi
be any weight vector on
\Gamma_{n}
. Suppose that
\Delta_{n}^{\varpi}
is any augmented
graph of
\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi}
.

(1)

The Lotka-Volterra system
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Gamma_{n}^{\varpi})
and its Kahan discretization are superintegrable and
Liouville integrable;

(2)

The deformed Lotka-Volterra system
\mathop{\rm LV}\nolimits(\Delta_{n}^{\varpi})
and its Kahan discretization are superintegrable
and Liouville integrable.

∎

The Lotka-Volterra and deformed Lotka-Volterra systems having Kahan discretizations which are integrable with
respect to the original Poisson structure are therefore characterized by the KP property.

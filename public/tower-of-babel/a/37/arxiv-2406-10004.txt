Cohomological stabilization, perverse filtrations, and refined BPS invariants for del Pezzo surfaces
Weite Pi, Junliang Shen, Fei Si, Feinuo Zhang

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic product formula for the refined BPS invariants associated with a local del Pezzo surface. Our formula governs the cohomological stabilization of the perverse filtration on the intersection cohomology of the moduli space of 1-dimensional semistable sheaves on a del Pezzo surface. Combined with the theory of Fourier transform of Maulik–Shen–Yin, we show that the perverse filtration matches asymptotically with the Chern filtration defined via tautological classes. In the case of the projective plane, our results resolve conjectures of Kononov–Pi–Shen.

Moduli of 1-dimensional sheaves

The geometry of such moduli spaces was studied by Le Potier
[
27
]
and was connected to enumerative geometry by Katz
[
19
]
. In recent years, further rich connections have been found between cohomological structures of
M_{\beta,\chi}
and other directions. For example, the perverse filtration for
M_{\beta,\chi}
categorifies the refined BPS invariants associated with the local Calabi–Yau threefold
\mathrm{Tot}_{S}(K_{S})

[
18
,
22
,
34
,
31
]
; the geometry of perverse filtrations for del Pezzo surfaces are mysteriously analogous to the geometry of the (recently resolved)

conjecture in non-abelian Hodge theory
[
9
,
32
,
17
,
33
]
; beautiful links have also been found between the Betti numbers of

, relative Gromov–Witten invariants, and scattering diagrams
[
5
,
7

6

. In this paper, we provide a complete description of the asymptotic cohomological behavior of

from both perspectives of numerical invariants and perverse filtrations. Our results prove conjectures in
[
23

concerning the refined BPS invariants and the “

” phenomenon in the case of

Since

is singular in general, it is more natural to consider the intersection cohomology

(with rational coefficients). The Hilbert–Chow morphism

h:M_{\beta,\chi}\to|\beta|,\quad{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto\mathrm{supp}({\mathcal{F}})

(1)

P_{0}\mathrm{IH}^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})\subset P_{1}\mathrm{IH}^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})\subset\cdots\subset\mathrm{IH}^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi}).

We refer to Section
1.1
for generalities on the perverse filtration. The following result of Maulik–Shen
[
31
]
shows that the data (
1

.

This result was proven in
[
31

for toric del Pezzo surfaces, where the toric condition was used to prove a relative dimension bound; this bound was later generalized to all del Pezzo surfaces by the work of Yuan

Theorem 0.1

(

For any two integers

The main character of this paper are the refined BPS invariants

given by dimensions of the associated graded of the perverse filtration. They do not depend on the choice of

\dim\mathrm{IH}^{k}(M_{\beta,\chi})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}n^{i,k-i}_{\beta},

and conjecturally also refine the Gromov–Witten/Pandharipande–Thomas invariants of the local surface

in all genus

0.2.
Göttsche’s formula and refined BPS invariants

We say that an assignment

\beta\mapsto N(\beta)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}

of a positive integer to an ample curve class is an
asymptotic bound,
if for any given ample curve class
\beta_{0}
and
m_{0}>0
, there exists
d_{0}>0
such that
N(d\beta_{0})>m_{0}
for all
d>d_{0}
. Roughly, this says that
N(\beta)\to+\infty
when the ample curve class
\beta
is positive enough.

Now we describe the asymptotic formula for the invariants (
2
). Let
\rho
be the Picard number of
S
. We recall from the Göttsche formula
[
15
]
that the Betti number
b_{k}(S^{[n]})
of the Hilbert scheme of points on
S
stabilizes to the
q^{k}
-coefficient of

(4)

T(q):=\prod_{i\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1-q^{2i+2})^{\rho+1}(1-q^{2i+4})}.

We define an
exotic
refinement of
T(q)
as follows:

H(q,t):=\frac{1}{(1-qt)^{\rho-1}}\cdot\prod_{i\geq 0}\frac{1}{(1-(qt)^{i}q^{2})(1-(qt)^{i+2})^{\rho}(1-(qt)^{i}t^{2})};

note that this is very different from the natural Hodge polynomial refinement. The formula
H(q,t)
recovers both the (asymptotic) Göttsche formula
T(q)=H(q,q)
and the product formula of Kononov–Pi–Shen
[
23
]
when
S={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

Our first main result is the following, which states that
H(q,t)
completely describes the asymptotic behavior of the refined BPS invariants
n_{\beta}^{i,j}
.

Theorem 0.2

(Refined cohomological stabilization)
.

Let
\beta
be an ample class on
S
. There exists an asymptotic bound
N(\beta)
such that

n^{i,j}_{\beta}=[H(q,t)]^{i,j},\quad{i+j}\leq N(\beta).

In particular, we obtain from (
3
) the cohomological stabilization

\dim\mathrm{IH}^{k}(M_{\beta,\chi})=[T(q)]^{k},\quad k\leq N(\beta).

Here
[-]^{i,j}
and
[-]^{k}
denote the
q^{i}t^{j}
-coefficient and the
q^{k}
-coefficient respectively.

Cohomological stabilization has been studied by Coskun–Woolf
[
14
]
in positive rank cases. For torsion sheaves, the moduli space admits an extra Hilbert–Chow morphism, refining the (intersection) cohomology via the perverse filtration. Theorem
0.2
can be viewed as a torsion sheaf analog of the Coskun–Woolf cohomological stabilization phenomenon.

The asymptotic bound
N(\beta)
in Theorem
0.2
is explicit and relies on the geometry of the linear system
|\beta|
on the surface
S
; see the formula (
28
) and Remark
3.2
. We will discuss this bound further in the case
S=\mathbb{P}^{2}
in Section
0.3.1
; in particular, our bound for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is optimal, proving conjectures of Kononov–Pi–Shen
[
23
]
. This includes
the
P=C
conjecture

[
23
, Conjecture 0.3]
which we discuss next.

0.3.
The
P=C
phenomenon

In this section, we assume that the moduli space
M_{\beta,\chi}
is nonsingular and it admits a universal family. For example, this is the case when

\mathrm{gcd}(\beta\cdot L,\,\chi)=1.

We may then consider the
ring structure
and
tautological classes
of the rational cohomology
H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})
. Our goal is to determine the perverse filtration for
M_{\beta,\chi}
using the
Chern filtration
obtained from a universal family. This is analogous to the case of the Hitchin system; see the discussions in Section
1.4
.

Following
[
41
]
, we introduce the (normalized) tautological classes

c_{k}(\gamma)\in H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})

given by integrating
\mathrm{ch}_{k+1}(\mathbb{F})
of a normalized universal family
\mathbb{F}
over
\gamma\in H^{*}(S)
.
The tautological classes induce an increasing filtration — the Chern filtration

C_{0}H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})\subset C_{1}H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})\subset\cdots\subset H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi}),

where the
k
-th piece
C_{k}H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})
is spanned by monomials

\prod_{i=1}^{s}c_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i})\in H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi}),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}\leq k.

We refer to Section
1.2
for the precise definition and more details. The following result is a consequence of Theorem
0.2
and the theory of Fourier transform of Maulik–Shen–Yin
[
33
]
:

Theorem 0.3

(Asymptotic
P=C
)
.

Let
N(\beta)
be the asymptotic bound in Theorem
0.2
. Then we have

P_{k}H^{\leq N(\beta)}(M_{\beta,\chi})=C_{k}H^{\leq N(\beta)}(M_{\beta,\chi}).

Compared to other del Pezzo surfaces, the case of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
has been more extensively explored in terms of the cohomology of the moduli of 1-dimensional sheaves and the
P=C
phenomenon. We discuss this special case next.

0.3.1.
Conjectures of Kononov–Pi–Shen for
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

For convenience, we denote by
M_{d,\chi}
the moduli space associated with the curve class
\beta=dH
and

, where

Using scattering diagrams, Bousseau

obtained an algorithm to compute the Betti numbers

, which links

to Gromov–Witten theory
[

. In

,
48

, Yuan studied the cohomology of

using motivic methods; she proved that

\displaystyle b_{k}(M_{d,\chi})=[T(q)]^{k},\quad k\leq 2d-4,

\displaystyle b_{2d-2}(M_{d,\chi})=[T(q)]^{2d-2}-3.

In particular, Yuan’s results imply that

is the optimal bound for the cohomological stabilization

to hold. This stabilization was further refined recently by a conjecture of Kononov–Pi–Shen

concerning the refined BPS invariants

Our bound

in the proof of Theorem

indeed achieves the optimal bound

; thus we complete the proof of the conjecture (

[
23
]
. Our method also gives a new proof of Yuan’s stabilization result
[

for the Betti numbers

via the perverse filtration.

The identity (

1.
Perverse filtrations and Chern filtrations

In view of Theorem
0.1
, from now on we may work only with the moduli space
M_{\beta,\chi}
that satisfies the assumption of Section

1.1.
Perverse filtrations

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism between irreducible nonsingular quasiprojective varieties. We assume that
\dim X=a
,
\dim Y=b
, and the morphism
f
has equal-dimensional fibers of dimension
r=a-b
. The perverse filtration

P_{0}H^{m}(X)\subset P_{1}H^{m}(X)\subset\dots\subset P_{2r}H^{m}(X)=H^{m}(X)

is an increasing filtration on the cohomology of
X
governed by the topology of the morphism
f
. It is defined to be

P_{k}H^{m}(X):=\mathrm{Im}\left\{H^{m-b}(Y,{{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq k}}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[b]))\to H^{m-b}(Y,Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[b])\right\}\subset H^{m}(X)

where
{}^{\mathbf{p}}\tau_{\leq\bullet}
is the perverse truncation functor
[
3
]
. If we apply the decomposition theorem to
f:X\to Y
, we obtain that

(6)

R\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[b]\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2r}\mathcal{P}_{i}[-i]\in D^{b}_{c}(Y)

with
\mathcal{P}_{i}
a semisimple perverse sheaf on
Y
. The perverse filtration can be identified as

P_{k}H^{m}(X)=\mathrm{Im}\Big\{H^{m-b}(Y,\bigoplus_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{P}_{i}[-i])\to H^{m}(X)\Big\}.

We recall the following standard lemma, which follows for example from the description
[
8
]
of the perverse filtration.

Lemma 1.1
.

If
\alpha\in H^{*}(X)
lies in the kernel of the restriction map
H^{m}(X)\to H^{m}(X_{y})
with
X_{y}:=f^{-1}(y)
a closed fiber, then we have
\alpha\in P_{m-1}H^{m}(X)
.

In this paper, we mainly consider the Hilbert–Chow morphism
h:M_{\beta,\chi}\to|\beta|
; the refined BPS invariants are the dimensions of the cohomology of the perverse sheaves that appeared in the decomposition theorem (
6
) associated with
h
; see also Section
3.3.1
.

1.2.
Chern filtrations

The choice of a universal family over

D

D

{\mathbb{F}}

Before discussing the case of general del Pezzo surfaces, we recall the case of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

We denote by

Proof.

For a fixed ample curve class

|d\beta|

and

For

1.4.
Remarks on
P=C

Before we start to prove our main theorems, we conclude this section with some remarks concerning “
P=C
” phenomena that appeared in several geometries and strategies to approach them.

The term “
P=C
” stands for a phenomenon that two structures of very different flavors match on the cohomology of certain moduli of stable sheaves — the perverse filtration associated with a natural proper map carried by the moduli space, and the Chern filtration obtained from a universal family and tautological classes. The perverse filtration is abstract but the Chern filtration is explicit.

To the best of our knowledge, the
P=C
phenomenon was first found for the Hitchin system
[
9
]
, and served as a key step in the proofs of the
P=W
conjecture in non-abelian Hodge theory
[
32
,
17
,
33
]
. More precisely, the
P=W
conjecture was reduced to
P=C
for the Hitchin moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}
:

(16)

P_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=C_{k}H^{*}(M_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}}).

There are two approaches to prove (
16
). The approach of
[
32
,
33
]
is to prove first the weaker statement
P\supset C
. By the results of Shende
[
45
]
and Mellit
[
36
]
on the character variety, we know that the right-hand side of (
16
), which is identified with the weight filtration
W_{2k}H^{*}(M_{B},{\mathbb{Q}})
of the character variety, satisfies the Lefschetz symmetry. Therefore the weaker
P\supset C
is sufficient to deduce (
16
). The approach of
[
17
]
does not rely on Mellit’s Lefschetz symmetry
[
36
]
; instead, the authors constructed a
splitting
of the perverse filtration using the cohomological Hall algebra, and placed the tautological classes in the desired pieces of this decomposition. In other words, the
P=W
or the
P=C
match for the Hitchin system is stronger than a match of two filtrations — it is indeed a match of two multiplicative decompositions.

Similar
P=C
phenomena have been discovered for Lagrangian fibrations. Let
S
be a K3 or an abelian surface, the moduli of 1-dimensional stable sheaves on
S
admits a Hilbert–Chow morphism, which is Lagrangian and is now referred to as the Beauville–Mukai system. Using Markman’s monodromy symmetry
[
29
,
30
]
, it was proven in
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
that
P=C
holds. This result was further applied in
[
10
]
to prove the
P=W
conjecture for genus 2 curves via a degeneration argument. Note that in the compact Lagrangian setting,
P=C
is again a match of two decompositions as in the Hitchin system case.

Compared to the cases above, the
P=C
match for a del Pezzo surface seems to be quite different. The perverse filtration associated with the Hilbert–Chow morphism
h
does not admit a multiplicative splitting.
4
4

4

In the case
S={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, this can be seen easily from the obstruction on the existence of a multiplicative splitting of the perverse filtration used in
[
1
]
, or from the presentations of the cohomology rings in low degrees
[
24
]
.
Therefore, it seems hard to upgrade Conjecture
0.5
to a match of two decompositions. On the other hand, it is in general challenging to prove a match of two
honest
filtrations — the lack of natural splittings of the filtrations provides difficulty in proving the equality of the two filtrations
directly
. For example, on the perverse filtration side, for a class
\gamma\in P_{k}H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})
, it is in general hard to prove a statement like
\gamma\not\in P_{k-1}H^{*}(M_{\beta,\chi})
; on the Chern filtration side, the precise location of a class is significantly influenced by the relations between the tautological classes.

In this paper, our approach to
P=C
for del Pezzo surfaces is
numerical
.
5
5

5

However, it seems hard to generalize the numerical argument of this paper to prove the full

conjecture (Conjecture

More precisely, the following numerical formula (

Now we prove Proposition
2.1
. We note the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a complete and irreducible variety of dimension
a
. Let
W\subset X
be a nonsingular open subset. Assume that

2\cdot\mathrm{codim}_{X}(X\setminus W)>i+1.

Then we have

b_{i}(W)=b_{2a-i}(X).

If we further assume that
X
is nonsingular, then

b_{i}(W)=b_{i}(X).

Proof.

We write
Z:=X\setminus W
. The excision sequence reads

\cdots\to H^{2a-i-1}(Z)\to H_{c}^{2a-i}(W)\to H^{2a-i}(X)\to H^{2a-i}(Z)\to\cdots.

By the codimension assumption, we have

2\cdot\dim Z<2a-i-1

which implies the vanishing

H^{2a-i-1}(Z)=H^{2a-i}(Z)=0.

Consequently, combined with Poincaré duality we obtain

b_{i}(W)=\dim H_{c}^{2a-i}(W)=\dim H^{2a-i}(X)=b_{2a-i}(X).

This proves the first part. The second part follows directly from Poincaré duality.

The purpose of this section is to introduce the asymptotic bound

3.1.
Compactified Jacobians

Recall the (twisted) compactified Jacobian fibration

h^{\circ}:\overline{J}^{e}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}}\to|\beta|^{\circ}

obtained from the base change of
h:M_{\beta,\chi}\to|\beta|
to the open subset

. Since the restriction map

H^{\leq{N_{1}(\beta)}}(M_{\beta,\chi})\to H^{\leq N_{1}(\beta)}(\overline{J}^{e}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}})

is an isomorphism preserving the perverse filtrations
[
33
, Corollary 5.3]
, we have

(26)

n_{\beta}^{i,j}=\dim\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{i}H^{i+j}\left(\overline{J}^{e}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}}\right),\quad i+j\leq N_{1}(\beta).

Our final key ingredient is the following result of Maulik–Yun
[
35
]
and Migliorini–Shende
[
37
]
, which relates the perverse filtration associated with
h^{\circ}
to the cohomology of the relative Hilbert scheme of points
{\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[k]}
by a support theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
35
,
37
]
)
.

For any
m\geq 0
, we have an isomorphism of vector spaces

H^{m}\left({\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[k]}\right)\simeq\bigoplus_{i+j\leq k,\;j\geq 0}\mathrm{Gr}^{P}_{i}H^{m-2j}\left(\overline{J}^{e}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}}\right).

In particular, we obtain from (

(27)

b_{m}\left({\mathcal{C}}^{[k]}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}\right)=\sum_{i+j\leq k,\;j\geq 0}n_{\beta}^{i,m-i-2j},\quad m\leq N_{1}(\beta).

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
0.2

We first introduce the bound for Theorem
0.2
:

(28)

N(\beta):=\min\left\{N_{2}(\beta),~\beta(\beta+K_{S})+2\right\}=\min\left\{N_{1}(\beta),~-\beta\cdot K_{S}-1,~\beta(\beta+K_{S})+2\right\}.

This is clearly an asymptotic bound by Proposition
1.5
; see also Remark
3.2
.

We proceed by induction on
i
to prove that

n_{\beta}^{i,j}=[H(q,t)]^{i,j},\quad i+j\leq N(\beta).

We only consider
n_{\beta}^{i,j}
in this range. For
i=0
, a direct calculation or using
[
11
, Proposition 5.2.4]
gives

n_{\beta}^{0,j}=\begin{cases}1\quad j\textrm{ is even};\\
0\quad j\textrm{ is odd}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, we have
[H(q,t)]^{0,j}=1
for even
j
and
0
otherwise. Thus the case
i=0
holds, which gives the induction base.

Assume now that Theorem
0.2
holds for
i\leq\ell-1
where
\ell>0
is an integer satisfying
\ell-1<N(\beta)
. This means that we have the numerical match

n_{\beta}^{i,j}=[H(q,t)]^{i,j},\quad i\leq\ell-1,\quad i+j\leq N(\beta).

We first show for
k\leq N_{1}(\beta)
that

(29)

b_{k}\left({{\mathcal{C}}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[\ell]}\right)-b_{k}\left({{\mathcal{C}}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[\ell-1]}\right)=\sum_{i+j=\ell}[H(q,t)]^{i,k-i-2j}.

The left-hand side is governed by Proposition
2.1
. If
k
is odd, then both sides are zero and clearly (
29
) holds. So we only focus on the case where
k
is even. In view of the formula of Proposition
2.3
(iii) and the asymptotic bound (
28
), we have

\dim{\mathcal{C}}^{[\ell]}-\dim S^{[\ell]}=\dim|\beta|-\ell\geq\frac{k}{2}.

Therefore Proposition
2.1
implies that

b_{k}({\mathcal{C}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[\ell]})=\sum_{s\leq k}b_{s}(S^{[\ell]}),\quad k\leq N_{1}(\beta).

This also holds if we replace
\ell
with
\ell-1
. Consequently, we have

(30)

b_{k}\left({{\mathcal{C}}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[\ell]}\right)-b_{k}\left({{\mathcal{C}}}_{|\beta|^{\circ}}^{[\ell-1]}\right)=\sum_{s=0}^{k}b_{s}({S}^{[\ell]})-b_{s}({S}^{[\ell-1]}),\quad k\leq N_{1}(\beta).

We will need the Göttsche formula
[
15
]
(in two variables)

G(z,w):=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-z^{2i-2}w^{i})(1-z^{2i}w^{i})^{\rho}(1-z^{2i+2}w^{i})},

which calculates Betti numbers of
{S}^{[m]}
:

G(z,w)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{s\geq 0}b_{s}({S}^{[m]})z^{s}w^{m}.

Note that
G(z,w)
specializes to the asymptotic version (
4
) via
z=q
and
w=1
. Combining this and (
30
), we see that (
29

(31)

\sum_{s=0}^{k}([G(z,w)]^{s,\ell}-[G(z,w)]^{s,\ell-1})=\sum_{i+j=\ell}[H(q,t)]^{i,k-i-2j}.

Now we compare the two sides:

LHS of
(
31
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{k}[G(z,w)\cdot(1-w)]^{s,\ell}=\left[G(z,w)\cdot\frac{1-w}{1-z^{2}}\right]^{k,\ell},

RHS of
(
31
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}[H(q,t)]^{i,k-2\ell+i}=\left[H(q,t)\cdot\frac{1}{1-qt}\right]^{\ell,k-\ell}.

The identity (
31

G(z,w)\cdot\frac{1-w}{1-z^{2}},\quad H(q,t)\cdot\frac{1}{1-qt}

are identified by the change of variables

z=t,\quad w=q/t.

This completes the proof of (
29

Now we finish the induction. Note that the left-hand side of (

\sum_{i+j=\ell}n_{\beta}^{i,k-i-2j}=n_{\beta}^{\ell,k-\ell}+\sum_{i<\ell,\,i+j=\ell}n_{\beta}^{i,k-i-2j}

by combining (

n_{\beta}^{\ell,k-\ell}=[H(q,t)]^{\ell,k-\ell},\quad k\leq N(\beta).

This completes the induction step, and hence the proof of Theorem

3.3.
Final remarks

We conclude the paper with a few further remarks.

3.3.1.
Cohomology of perverse sheaves and stabilization

The invariants
n_{\beta}^{i,j}
only depend on nonsingular curves in the linear system
|\beta|
. More precisely, let
V\subset|\beta|
be the open subset of nonsingular curves in
S
, with
f_{V}:{\mathcal{C}}_{V}\to V
the universal curve. Then we can obtain a natural local system

{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta}:=R^{1}f_{V*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{V}}\in\mathrm{LocSys}(V)

which calculates the cohomology
H^{1}
of the fibers. By the full support theorem
[
31
]
, the refined BPS invariants can be computed as the cohomology

(32)

n_{\beta}^{i,j}:=\dim H^{j-\dim{|\beta|}}\left(|\beta|,\mathrm{IC}(\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{L}}_{\beta})\right).

It would be interesting to find an effective algorithm to calculate the cohomology groups on the right-hand side directly. Our main result implies the stabilization of
n^{i,j}_{\beta}
but our proof is
indirect
. A natural question is to find a direct explanation of this stabilization phenomenon for the cohomology of perverse sheaves.

3.3.2.
Formulas for refined BPS invariants associated with surfaces

Although the (unrefined) BPS invariants have been calculated in many cases via Gromov–Witten/Donaldson–Thomas theory over the last few decades, very few closed formulas are known for the refined BPS invariants.

In a few special cases, there are closed formulas for all the refined BPS invariants. For the case of a local K3 surface, it was proven in
[
43
]
that the refined BPS invariants match perfectly with the Hodge numbers of the Hilbert scheme of points on a K3 surface, as predicted by
[
21
,
20
]
. We refer to
[
46
]
for other refined invariants associated with K3 surfaces.

For the local curve
X=T^{*}C\times{\mathbb{C}}
with
C
a curve of genus
g\geq 2
, the geometry of BPS invariants are closely related to the topology of the Hitchin system. As a consequence of the
P=W
conjecture (now a theorem), the refined BPS invariants of
X
are given by the weight polynomials of the character variety associated with the curve
C
, whose closed formulas were conjectured by
[
16
]
in terms of Macdonald polynomials. We refer to
[
13
]
for more details on this connection.

Recently, Oberdieck conjectured in certain curve classes closed formulas for the local Enriques surface
[
39
]
, refining the Gromov–Witten calculation
[
38
]
.

All these formulas have asymptotic product expressions whose shapes are similar to
H(q,t)
.

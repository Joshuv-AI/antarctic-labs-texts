On an inhomogeneous coagulation model with a differential sedimentation kernel
Iulia Cristian, Barbara Niethammer, Juan J. L. Velázquez

Abstract
We study an inhomogeneous coagulation equation that contains a transport term in the spatial variable modeling the sedimentation of clusters. We prove local existence of mass conserving solutions for a class of coagulation kernels for which in the space homogeneous case instantaneous gelation (i.e., instantaneous loss of mass) occurs. Our result holds true in particular for sum-type kernels of homogeneity greater than one, for which solutions do not exist at all in the spatially homogeneous case. Moreover, our result covers kernels that in addition vanish on the diagonal, which have been used to describe the onset of rain and the behavior of air bubbles in water.



Keywords:
inhomogeneous coagulation equations, mass-conserving solutions, sum-type kernels.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background

In
[
25
]
the authors suggest a space-dependent coagulation equation to model the onset of rain. Here spherical particles of volume
v
move in space vertically, for example due to gravitation, and merge when their trajectories cross. This leads to the following inhomogeneous coagulation equation for the density
f
of particles of size
v
at the point
x
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\frac{2}{3}}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,v)}K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

(1.1)

with a so-called differential sedimentation kernel of the form

\displaystyle K(v,v^{\prime})=|v^{\frac{2}{3}}-v^{\prime\frac{2}{3}}|(v^{\frac{1}{3}}+v^{\prime\frac{1}{3}})^{2}.

(1.2)

This choice of kernel is motivated by the following consideration (see
[
25
]
): the cross-section of interaction between two particles of radii
r
and
r^{\prime}
, and volume
v
and
v^{\prime}
, respectively, that merged upon touching is given by
\pi(r+r^{\prime})^{2}\approx(v^{\frac{1}{3}}+v^{\prime\frac{1}{3}})^{2}
.

Additionally, the velocity is approximately
v^{\frac{2}{3}}
, which represents the Stokes velocity of a rigid sphere with no slip boundary condition, and the collision rate between two particles is taken to be proportional to their relative velocities
|v^{\frac{2}{3}}-v^{\prime\frac{2}{3}}|
.

The model is used to describe the behavior of air bubbles in water which move due to buoyancy and it is also valid for water droplets. We refer to
[
20
,
12
,
32
]
for more details. In
[
25
]
, slip-flow corrections for water droplets are discussed and it is mentioned that this requires to change the power of the volume for the velocity. More precisely, the left-hand side of (
1.1
) becomes
\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and the kernel in (
1.2
) has the form
K(v,v^{\prime})=|v^{\alpha}-v^{\prime\alpha}|(v^{\frac{1}{3}}+v^{\prime\frac{1}{3}})^{2}
.

The model in (
1.1
) with kernel (
1.2
) is referred to as the
free merging regime
in
[
25
]
, since it is assumed in its derivation that the particles merge when their trajectories cross. When studying equations like (
1.1
), it is customary to look for stationary solutions of non-zero flux (cf.
[
34
]
) of the form
f\approx v^{d}
, for some
d\in\mathbb{R}
. One of the possible approaches is to compute them using the so-called Zakharov transform (see
[
38
]
) and using it we find the solution
f(x,v,t)\approx v^{-\frac{13}{6}}
. However, this approach can be made rigorous only if the integral containing the coagulation kernel in (
1.1
) is finite, which is not the case for the kernel (
1.2
). In order to be able to rigorously find a solution for kernels with the same homogeneity, the so-called
forced locality regime
in which only particles of similar sizes can merge is studied in
[
25
]
. More precisely, for the
forced locality regime
a cut-off in the coagulation kernel is introduced that makes the kernel vanish outside the region where
\frac{1}{q}<\frac{v^{\prime}}{v}<q
, for some
q>1
. With this cut-off, the integral containing the coagulation kernel converges and thus the stationary solution
f\approx v^{-\frac{13}{6}}
is a valid solution.

Our main goal in this paper is to show that mass conserving solutions exist, at least for a short time interval, for a class of inhomogeneous coagulation equations that includes example (
1.1
) with (
1.2
). At a first glance this might look surprising since the homogeneity of the kernel in (
1.2
) is greater than one. Indeed, it is well-known that gelation (mass loss) occurs for the standard one-dimensional coagulation equation,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(v,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,v)}K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(v-v^{\prime},t)f(v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}-\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(v,t)f(v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime},

(1.3)

when the coagulation kernel behaves like a power law of homogeneity
\gamma>1
(see, for example
[
19
,
18
,
26
]
). In particular, for sum kernels of the form

\displaystyle K(v,v^{\prime})=v^{\gamma}+v^{\prime\gamma},

(1.4)

with
\gamma>1
, gelation happens instantaneously. Actually, making use of this property, one can prove that solutions which belong to
L^{1}
for the standard coagulation equation do not exist at all for kernels as in (
1.4
) (see
[
2
,
6
,
15
,
3
]
).
In addition, it has been proven in
[
13
]
that the instantaneous gelation phenomenon holds for Radon measure solutions of the standard coagulation equation with sum kernels of homogeneity greater than one which vanish on the diagonal, i.e.
K(v,v^{\prime})=0
, such as the kernel in (
1.2
).

Our main goal is then to prove that, in contrast to the homogeneous case, there exist, at least for short times, mass conserving solutions to the inhomogeneous model

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,v)}K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime},

(1.5)

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Our proof holds for a rather general class of coagulation kernels (see Assumption
1.1
), in particular kernels of the form (
1.2
) and (
1.4
).
Thus, the model (
1.5
) provides a coagulation model in which existence for kernels of the form (
1.4
) with
\gamma>1
holds, at least for short times.

1.2
Main result

Short time existence of mass conserving solutions for the inhomogeneous model

Our goal is to prove short-time existence of mass conserving solutions for the inhomogeneous model

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,v)}K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime},

(1.6)

where

\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1).

(1.7)

Assumption 1.1
.

We assume that
K\colon[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a symmetric and continuous function that satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq K(v,v^{\prime})\leq K_{1}(v^{\gamma}+v^{\prime\gamma}),\textup{ with }\gamma\in[0,1+\alpha)

(1.8)

for some constant
K_{1}>0
and

\displaystyle K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})\leq K(v,v^{\prime}),\textup{ when }v^{\prime}\in\big[0,\tfrac{v}{2}\big].

(1.9)

Condition (
1.9
) is a rather standard assumption in the study of coagulation equations, see for example
[
27
]
, and most of the kernels used in applications satisfy this condition, in particular, kernels of the form
K(v,v^{\prime})=v^{\gamma}+v^{\prime\gamma}
or (
1.2
). The condition
\gamma<1+\alpha
in (
1.8
) is such that the transport term will control the contribution coming from the coagulation term.

Definition 1.2
.

[Mild solutions] Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\in[0,1+\alpha)
and
m>\frac{\gamma+1}{\alpha}
. Let
T>0
and
K
satisfy Assumption
1.1
. We say that a non-negative function
f\in\textup{C}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty))
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{(0,\infty)}(1+v^{\gamma})f(x,v,t)\textup{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{T}}{\operatorname{max}\Big\{1,|x|^{m-\frac{\gamma+1}{\alpha}}\Big\}},\textup{ for }x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.10)

is a mild solution of equation (
1.6
) if

\displaystyle f(x,v,t)-f(x-v^{\alpha}t,v,0)S[f](x,v,0,t)=

(1.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{(0,v)}S[f](x,v,s,t)K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x-(t-s)v^{\alpha},v-v^{\prime},s)f(x-(t-s)v^{\alpha},v^{\prime},s)\textup{d}s,

for all
t\in[0,T]
,
v\in(0,\infty)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\displaystyle S[f](x,v,s,t):=\textup{e}^{-\int_{s}^{t}a[f](x-v^{\alpha}(t-\tau),v,\tau)\textup{d}\tau},

(1.12)

with

\displaystyle a[f](x,v,t):=\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}.

(1.13)

Definition 1.3
.

We call
f\in\textup{C}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty))
a mass-conserving solution of equation (
1.6
) if
f
is as in Definition
1.2
and satisfies in addition

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,\infty)}vf(x,v,t)\textup{d}v\textup{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{(0,\infty)}vf(x,v,0)\textup{d}v\textup{d}x

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Local existence of solutions)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\in[0,1+\alpha)
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
even, and
p=\alpha m
with
m>\operatorname{max}\{\frac{2\gamma+1}{\alpha},\frac{2}{\alpha}+3\}
. Let
K
satisfy Assumption
1.1
and
T>0
be sufficiently small. Let
f_{\textup{in}}\in\textup{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty))
such that

\displaystyle f_{\textup{in}}(x,v)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{1+|x|^{m}+v^{p}},

for some
C_{0}>0
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
v\in(0,\infty)
. Then there exists a mass-conserving solution
f
of (
1.11
) as in Definition
1.3
that satisfies

\displaystyle f(x,v,t)\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{m}+v^{p}},

(1.14)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, for some
C>0
.

Remark 1.5
.

Theorem
1.4
is valid for coagulation kernels
K
as in (
1.2
), as well as coagulation kernels of the form
K(v,v^{\prime})=v^{\gamma}+v^{\prime\gamma}.

Remark 1.6
.

It is worthwhile to mention that mass conservation will follow due to the fact that our solution will have sufficiently fast decay for large values of
|x|
and
v
, see (
1.14
). For more details, see the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

When
\gamma<1
, our result could be expected according to the general theory of existence for one-dimensional coagulation equations. This states that solutions exist for kernels that behave like power laws of homogeneity
\gamma<1
, see, for example,
[
33
]
for existence of solutions and
[
22
]
for existence of self-similar profiles.

Some multi-dimensional coagulation models have been studied in the mathematical literature, see
[
21
,
14
]
. Moreover, several classes of coagulation models for the distribution of particles with space dependence have also been considered. In particular, models in which in addition to coagulation there is space diffusion of the aggregating particles can be found in
[
1
,
8
,
7
,
27
]
. Models that contain coagulation of particles as well as transport terms (that might include sedimentation terms) were studied in
[
10
,
16
,
17
,
11
,
5
,
24
]
. In all the models mentioned above the homogeneity of the coagulation kernel is either
\gamma<1
, case in which the solutions are globally defined and preserve the total mass, or product-type kernels are discussed, for which solutions preserve the mass up to a certain point in time.

To our knowledge, the only exception that considers the case
\gamma>1
for space-dependent models is
[
23
]
. Indeed, existence of solutions for the discrete version of the model in (
1.1
) has been established for coagulation kernels of the form
K(i,j)=\sigma_{ij}|\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}|,

i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. Here,
\nu_{i}
is a non-negative function of volume which represents the sedimentation velocity of the particles and
\sigma_{ij}
can be estimated by a function of the form
C(i^{\gamma}+j^{\gamma})
with
\gamma<1
. Assuming that
\nu_{i}
scales like a power law
i^{\beta}
, we would obtain a coagulation kernel
K(i,j)
that behaves like a homogeneous function with homogeneity
\beta+\gamma
that might be larger than one. As a matter of fact, the results in
[
23
]
hold for any choice of
\nu_{i}\geq 0
. In particular, the solutions constructed in
[
23
]
have total mass of particles that changes continuously in time. Nevertheless it has not been proven in
[
23
]
that the mass of solutions is conserved. By contrast, in the present paper we construct a theory of existence for mild solutions which conserve mass in finite time. The class of kernels considered in
[
23
]
has a non-empty intersection with the class of kernels considered in this paper and this intersection includes the coagulation kernel (
1.2
), which is relevant in the physical literature as explained above. However, none of two classes of kernels is included in the other. In addition, the arguments in
[
23
]
seem to rely significantly on the discrete character of the coagulation process and it is not clear how difficult would be to extend these results to the continuous case. On the other hand, the sedimentation
\nu_{i}
can be arbitrary in
[
23
]
. This opens the question of whether we can extend our result to powers of
\alpha
which are larger than one for the model in (
1.6
). However, a different approach of obtaining information from the characteristics created by the transport term in (
1.6
) will be needed in this case.

Moreover, kernels of the form
K(v,v^{\prime})=v^{\gamma}+v^{\prime\gamma},
with
\gamma\in(1,1+\alpha)
, also satisfy the conditions (
1.8
), (
1.9
). Solutions of the standard one-dimensional coagulation equation do not exist for these types of kernels, see
[
2
,
6
,
15
,
3
]
. This is to our knowledge the first result of existence of mass-conserving solutions involving sum kernels of homogeneity
\gamma>1
, regardless of whether one considers one-dimensional or multi-dimensional coagulation models.

It is worth mentioning that our results also include the cases
\gamma=0
and
\gamma=1
, which are normally studied separately in the literature due to their rich features, such as being able to predict the long-time behavior of solutions, see
[
29
,
30
,
37
,
9
,
4
]
, or prove uniqueness of self-similar profiles, see
[
29
,
35
,
36
]
, where the constant kernel and perturbations of the constant kernel are discussed.

The strategy for proving existence of solutions is to consider an iterative scheme based on a linear version of (
1.6
). For this equation, we are able to find a suitable supersolution, which in turn will provide sufficiently good moment estimates. This will give us compactness of the iterated sequence and enable us to pass to the limit in the equation. The idea of finding appropriate supersolutions was also used in
[
31
]
, in this case for finding self-similar solutions with fat tails. The idea of considering a linear version of the model in order to better study properties of its solutions is common in the study of coagulation equations, see for example
[
35
]
where this idea is used to study uniqueness of solutions.

We present the proof of our main Theorem
1.4
in the following Section
2
with some technical computations moved to the appendix.

2
Proof of the main theorem

2.1
Formal approximation and discussion on the assumptions

Our approach to prove existence of a solution to (
1.6
) is based on constructing a suitable supersolution by approximating the coagulation term for large particles by a transport term.
To motivate this we present in this subsection this formal approximation of (
1.6
).
Similar computations can be found in
[
25
, Section 4 and Appendix 1]
.

Suppose now that
f
is a solution of (
1.6
).
Since we are interested in the behavior for large values of
v
, we can assume, due to the fast decay of
f(x,v,t)
, that the term
\int_{[\frac{v}{2},\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)

f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}
gives a small contribution. This is consistent with the known results in coagulation equations. We can then approximate (
1.6
) via

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}-\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}[K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)-K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)]f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{[\frac{v}{2},\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}[K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)-K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)]f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}.

(2.1)

Since our strategy relies on finding a suitable supersolution, it suffices to find a lower bound for (
2.1
). This is where the assumption (
1.9
) is needed. We thus use that
K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})\leq K(v,v^{\prime})
when
v^{\prime}\in[0,\frac{v}{2}]
and obtain that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)

\displaystyle-\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}[K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)-K(v,v^{\prime})f(x,v,t)]f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)-\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}K(v,v^{\prime})[f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)-f(x,v,t)]f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}.

(2.2)

Assuming now that the coagulation kernel
K
behaves like
v^{\prime\gamma}+v^{\gamma}
and since
v^{\prime}\in[0,\frac{v}{2}]
, we further deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)

\displaystyle\approx\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}v^{\gamma}[f(x,v-v^{\prime},t)-f(x,v,t)]f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx-v^{\gamma}\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}\int_{v-v^{\prime}}^{v}\partial_{w}f(x,w,t)\textup{d}wf(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}.

(2.3)

Assume that
\partial_{w}f(x,w,t)
behaves similarly for
w\in[\frac{v}{2},v]
and thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)\approx-v^{\gamma}\partial_{v}f(x,v,t)\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}v^{\prime}f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}.

(2.4)

We denote by

M_{1}(x,t):=\int_{(0,\infty)}v^{\prime}f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}
the first moment in
v
of
f
.
We consider only large values of
v
so that we can safely assume that
\int_{(0,\frac{v}{2})}v^{\prime}f(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}
contains most of the mass. In this manner, we can further approximate (
2.4
) by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)\approx-v^{\gamma}\partial_{v}f(x,v,t)M_{1}(x,t).

(2.5)

Notice that in order for our approximation to hold, the assumption (
1.9
) was needed in (
2.2
). Otherwise, an analogous approximation of the model could be obtained by replacing
v^{\gamma}\partial_{v}f(x,v,t)
in (

) by
\partial_{v}\big(v^{\gamma}f(x,v,t)\big)
. The approximation containing the term

is the one actually used in

]
. However, due to (

Suppose now that

decays sufficiently fast for large values of

, that is

\displaystyle M_{1}(x,t)\leq\frac{L}{1+|x|^{\overline{m}}},

for some sufficiently large

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f(x,v,t)+\frac{Lv^{\gamma}\partial_{v}f(x,v,t)}{1+|x|^{\overline{m}}}=0.

(2.7)

This motivates the analysis of the equation (

2.2
Upper and lower bounds for the solution of the approximated model

To prove short time existence of a solution to (
1.6
) we will set up an iterative scheme and derive, using (
2.7
), a uniform supersolution for the solutions of this scheme. More precisely, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define inductively a sequence of functions
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{n+1}(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f_{n+1}(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(0,v)}K(v-v^{\prime},v^{\prime})f_{n+1}(x,v-v^{\prime},t)f_{n}(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{(0,\infty)}K(v,v^{\prime})f_{n+1}(x,v,t)f_{n}(x,v^{\prime},t)\textup{d}v^{\prime},

(2.8)

with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

\displaystyle f_{n+1}(x,v,0)=f_{n}(x,v,0)=f_{\textup{in}}(x,v)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{1+|x|^{m}+v^{p}},\qquad\textup{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.9)

We take
f_{0}
to be a function such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{0}(x,v,t)+v^{\alpha}\partial_{x}f_{0}(x,v,t)=0

(2.10)

with

\displaystyle f_{0}(x,v,0)=f_{\textup{in}}(x,v)\leq\frac{C_{0}}{1+|x|^{m}+v^{p}}.

(2.11)

Notice that, if we have equality in (
2.11
), the solution of (
2.10
) is
f_{0}(x,v,t)=\frac{C_{0}}{1+|x-v^{\alpha}t|^{m}+v^{p}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In principle we have to prove that the sequence in (
2.8
) is well-defined. A rigorous proof would work as follows. First we approximate
K
with a kernel
K_{N}
which is such that

\displaystyle K_{N}(v,v^{\prime})=K(v,v^{\prime})\chi_{N}(v+v^{\prime}),

(2.12)

for
N>1
and where
\chi_{N}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a continuous functions such that
\chi_{N}(x)=1
, when
x\leq\frac{N}{2}
, and
\chi_{N}(x)=0,
when
x\geq N
. Then we can establish with a standard fixed-point argument the existence and uniqueness of
f_{n,N}
. The key result is then that we obtain a uniform fast decaying bound for the sequence of solutions
which is in particular independent of
N
. Then one can pass to the limit
N\to\infty
. Since this procedure is standard once one has the bounds on the solution, we omit the details here and work directly with
K
.

Notations and Assumptions:

Let
R>0

\xi_{R}\in C([0,\infty))\,,\quad\xi_{R}\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]\;\mbox{ such that }\xi_{R}(v)=1\mbox{ if }v\geq 2R\mbox{ and }\xi_{R}(v)=0\mbox{ if }v\leq R.

\displaystyle+\frac{vv^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}}{\bigg(1+(\gamma-1)v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|X|^{m-d}}\bigg)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}=:\frac{T_{1}}{T_{2}^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}},

where

\displaystyle T_{1}:=1+v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}

and

\displaystyle T_{2}:=1+(\gamma-1)v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|X|^{m-d}}.

Differentiating in
v
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{v}V(x,v,t)=\frac{\partial_{v}T_{1}}{T_{2}^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{T_{1}\partial_{v}T_{2}}{T_{2}^{\frac{2\gamma-1}{\gamma-1}}}.

It holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{v}T_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}-2v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\big|(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\big|^{2}\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{3}}

\displaystyle\qquad+v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial^{2}_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}+v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)(m-d-1)|X|^{m-d-2}|\partial_{v}X|^{2}\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}.

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{v}T_{2}=(\gamma-1)^{2}v^{\gamma-2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|X|^{m-d}}-(\gamma-1)v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}.

We first prove the following estimates:

\displaystyle 0\leq v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|X(x,v,\xi)|^{m-d}}\leq\frac{1}{2}

(A.22)

and

\displaystyle v^{\gamma}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X(x,v,\xi)|^{m-d-2}X(x,v,\xi)\partial_{v}X(x,v,\xi)\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X(x,v,\xi)|^{m-d})^{2}}\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(A.23)

We first prove that (
A.22
) holds. For sufficiently small
t
, we are in the case when
x\geq(1+2\delta)v^{\alpha}t
since
v
is as in (
A.2
). We use (
2.19
) and the fact that
x\geq(1+2\delta)v^{\alpha}t\geq(1+2\delta)v^{\alpha}s
for all
s\in[0,t]
. Thus,
|X(s)|\geq|x-(1+\delta)v^{\alpha}s|
when
x\geq(1+2\delta)v^{\alpha}t
, for all
s\in[0,t]
. It follows that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|X(x,v,\xi)|^{m-d}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|x-(1+\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{v^{\alpha}t}\frac{\textup{d}z}{1+|x-(1+\delta)z|^{m-d}}\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1},

(A.24)

which, since
v\geq R
and
\gamma<\alpha+1
, implies (
A.22
) if
R
is sufficiently large.

We now prove (
A.23
). As before, we consider the case when
x\geq(1+2\delta)v^{\alpha}t
since we can choose
t
sufficiently small. It follows that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|x-(1-\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d-1}v^{\alpha}\xi\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d})^{2}}.

If
v
is as in (
A.2
), then
x-(1+\delta)z\geq x-2z\geq\frac{x}{2}
for sufficiently small
t
, for
z\in[0,v^{\alpha}t]
. By making the change of variables
z=v^{\alpha}\xi
and using the fact that
x-(1-\delta)z\leq x
we further obtain that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|x-(1-\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d-1}v^{\alpha}\xi\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{v^{\alpha}t}\frac{|x-(1-\delta)z|^{m-d-1}z\textup{d}z}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)z|^{m-d})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{1+2\delta}}\frac{|x|^{m-d}\textup{d}z}{(1+|x|^{m-d})^{2}}\leq\frac{Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}|x|^{m-d+1}}{(1+|x|^{m-d})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1},

and thus (
A.23
) follows if we take
R
sufficiently large since
v\geq R
and
\gamma<\alpha+1
.

From (
A.22
) and (
A.23
), it holds that
T_{1}\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and

T_{2}\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. From this we deduce that

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}_{v}V(x,v,t)|\leq C\big(|\partial_{v}T_{1}|+|\partial_{v}T_{2}|\big)\,.

We analyze each term of
\partial_{v}T_{1}
separately. Following the computations for (
A.23
), we deduce that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma-1}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}\bigg|\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}v^{-1}.

(A.25)

Similarly, by making the change of variables
z=v^{\alpha}\xi
, it follows that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\big||X|^{m-d-2}X\partial_{v}X\big|^{2}\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\big(|x-(1-\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d-1}v^{\alpha-1}\xi\big)^{2}\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)v^{\alpha}\xi|^{m-d})^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{v^{\alpha}t}\frac{\big(|x-(1-\delta)z|^{m-d-1}z\big)^{2}\textup{d}z}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)z|^{m-d})^{3}}\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}v^{-1}.

(A.26)

Moreover, (
A.11
) implies that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-2}X\partial^{2}_{v}X\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\alpha-2}v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(m-d)|X|^{m-d-1}\xi\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{-\alpha-2}v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{v^{\alpha}t}\frac{|x-(1-\delta)z|^{m-d-1}z\textup{d}z}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)z|^{m-d})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}v^{-1}.

(A.27)

We now analyze the last term in
\partial_{v}T_{1}
. We have that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|X|^{m-d-2}|\partial_{v}X|^{2}\textup{d}\xi}{(1+|X|^{m-d})^{2}}\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-2}\int_{0}^{v^{\alpha}t}\frac{|x-(1-\delta)z|^{m-d-2}z^{2}\textup{d}z}{(1+|x-(1+\delta)z|^{m-d})^{2}}\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}v^{-1}.

(A.28)

We continue by finding upper bounds for each term in
\partial_{v}T_{2}
. From (
A.24
), it holds that

\displaystyle v^{\gamma-2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\textup{d}\xi}{1+|X|^{m-d}}\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-1}v^{-1},

(A.29)

which offers the right estimate in order to prove (

, which is

Combining (

\displaystyle|v^{p-1}\partial_{v}^{2}V(x,v,t)|\leq Cv^{\gamma-\alpha-2}v^{p-1}\leq\epsilon v^{p-2},

Proof of (

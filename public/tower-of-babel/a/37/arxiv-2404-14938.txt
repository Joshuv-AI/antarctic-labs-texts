Derived functors and Hilbert polynomials over hypersurface rings
Tony J. Puthenpurakal

Abstract
. Let ( A , 𝔪 ) (A,\mathfrak{m}) be a hypersurface local ring of dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 and let I I be an 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} -primary ideal. We show that there is a non-negative integer r I r_{I} (depending only on I I ) such that if M M is any non-free maximal Cohen-Macaulay (= MCM) A A -module the function n → ℓ ⁡ ( Tor 1 A ⁡ ( M , A / I n + 1 ) ) n\rightarrow\ell(\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{1}(M,A/I^{n+1})) (which is of polynomial type) has degree r I r_{I} . Analogous results hold for Hilbert polynomials associated to Ext-functors. Surprisingly a key ingredient is the classification of thick subcategories of the stable category of MCM A A -modules (obtained by Takahashi, see [ 10 , 6.6] ).

2.1

Let

be a skeletally small triangulated category with shift operator

(2)

(3)

(4)

\xi(\Sigma X)=\xi(X)
for all

.

(5)

If

We prove

(with hypotheses as above)

(1)

are functions on

(2)

(1) Let

where

are free

is a function on

The proof for assertions on

(2) We may assume that

Using the second exact sequence we find that

The proof for assertions on

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a hypersurface local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then there is an integer
r_{I}\geq-1
(depending only on
I
) such that if
M\in\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
is non-zero then
\deg t_{I}^{M}(z)=r_{I}
.

Proof.

We first note that for any MCM
M
we have
\deg t_{I}^{M}(z)\leq d-1
, see
[
11
, Corollary 4]
. Recall the degree of the zero polynomial to be
-1
.
Set

r=\max\{\deg t_{I}^{M}(z)\mid M\in\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)\}.

If
r=-1
then we have nothing to prove. So assume
r\geq 0
. For
M\in\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
define

\xi_{I}(M)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{r!}{n^{r}}t_{I}(M,n).

We note that
\xi_{I}(M)\geq 0
and is zero precisely when
\deg t_{I}(M,z)<r
.

Claim:
\xi_{I}(-)
is a weak triangle function on
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
, see
2.1
.

Assume the claim for the time being. Then
\ker\xi
is a thick subcategory of
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
.
Also if
\deg t_{i}^{L}(z)=r
then
L\notin\ker\xi
. So
\ker\xi\neq\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
. As
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
has no proper thick subcategories, see
[
10
, 6.6]
, it follows that
\ker\xi=0
.
Therefore
\deg t_{I}^{M}(z)=r
for all
M\neq 0
in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
.

It remains to show
\xi_{I}
is a weak triangle function on
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
. The first three conditions are trivial to satisfy.
By
2.5
(2) it follows that
\xi_{I}(\Omega^{-1}_{A}(M))=\xi_{I}(M)
.
Let
L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}(L)
is a triangle in
\operatorname{\underline{CM}}_{0}(A)
then note that we have a short exact sequence of
A
-modules

0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\oplus F\rightarrow\Omega^{-1}(L)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{where $F$ is free.}

Therefore we have an inequality

t_{I}(N,n)\leq t_{I}(M,n)+t_{I}(\Omega^{-1}(L),n).

The result follows.
∎

Assume

and assume

A

x\in I
be such that it is
M\oplus A
-superficial and
xt
is
L_{1}(M)
,
E^{i}(M)
-filter regular for

and
(I^{n+1}M\colon x)=I^{n}M
for

We have an exact sequence for

0\rightarrow\ker\alpha_{n}\rightarrow A/I^{n}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{n}}A/I^{n+1}\rightarrow B/J^{n+1}\rightarrow 0,

\alpha_{n}(a+I^{n})=xa+I^{n+1}

for

Thus for

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{1}(M,A/I^{n})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\alpha^{1}_{n}}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{1}(M,A/I^{n+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{B}_{1}(N,B/J^{n+1})\rightarrow

\displaystyle M/I^{n}M

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\alpha^{0}_{n}}M/I^{n+1}M\rightarrow N/J^{n+1}N\rightarrow 0.

. Furthermore the map

-component of the multiplication map by

on

. As

-filter regular it follows that

for

5.6
.

We note that if
A
is a hypersurface and
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal then if we go mod an
I
-superficial element
x
then the resulting ring
B=A/(x)
is no longer a hypersurface. It is however a complete intersection. Thus we prove the following result:

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete intersection of dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
M
be a non-free MCM
A
-module with bounded betti-numbers. Then
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)=\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)
.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on dimension
d
of
A
. We may assume
M
has no free summands. As
M
has bounded betti numbers it follows that
M
is
2
-periodic, i.e.,
\Omega^{2}_{A}(M)\cong M
.

We first consider the case when
d=1
. We note that in this case
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)=0
or
=-1
. Similarly we have
\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)=0
or
=-1
. We show that
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
if and only if
\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
.
Suppose
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
. So
t_{I}^{M}(z)=0
and therefore

t_{I}(M,n)=0
for
n\gg 0
(say from
n\geq m
). As
t_{I}(M,n)=t_{I}(\Omega^{1}_{A}(M),n)
for all
n
and
M
is
2
-periodic we have
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(M,A/I^{n+1})=0
for all
i\geq 1
and for
n\geq m
. It follows from
[
1
, Theorem III]
that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,A/I^{n+1})=0
for all
i\gg 0
and for all
n\geq m
. As
s_{I}(M,n)=s_{I}(\Omega^{1}_{A}(M),n)
for all
n
and
M
is
2
-periodic we have

s_{I}(M,n)=0
for all
n\geq m
. It follows that
s_{I}^{M}(z)=0
. Thus
\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
. The proof of when
\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
then
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
is similar and left to the reader.

We now assume that
d\geq 2
and the result is proved when
\dim A=d-1
.
A proof similar to above shows that
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
if and only if
\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)=-1
. So assume
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)\geq 0
(and so
\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)\geq 0
). We may assume that the residue field of
A
is infinite. Let
x\in I
be
M\oplus A
-superficial and also assume that
xt\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
is
L_{1}(M)\oplus E_{1}(M)\oplus E_{2}(M)
-filter regular.
We note that
B=A/(x)
is a complete intersection and
N=M/xM
is a MCM
B
-module with bounded betti-numbers. Set
J=I/(x)
. So it follows from our induction hypotheses that

\deg t^{N}_{J}(z)=\deg s^{M}_{J}(z)
. As
\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)\geq 0
, it follows from
5.4
that
\deg t^{N}_{J}(z)=\deg t^{M}_{I}(z)-1
. Similarly it follows from
5.5
that
\deg s^{N}_{J}(z)=\deg s^{M}_{I}(z)-1
. The result follows.
∎

We now give

Proof of Theorem
1.5

Let
M
be non-free MCM

-module. Then

and

Note

∎

In this section we give

Let
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
. Recall

As

Notice

is a

. In particular if

.

Quasi-Spherical Metrics and the Static Minkowski Inequality
Brian Harvie, Ye-Kai Wang

Abstract
We prove that equality in the Minkowski inequality for asymptotically flat static manifolds from [ McC18 ] is achieved only by slices of Schwarzschild space. To show this, we establish uniqueness of quasi-spherical static metrics: rotationally symmetric regions of Schwarzschild are the only static vacuum metrics which are quasi-spherical with vanishing shear vector in the sense of [ Bar93 ] . In addition, we observe that the static Minkowski inequality extends to all dimensions for a connected boundary and to asymptotically flat static manifolds with decay order τ > 0 \tau>0 . Altogether, this yields a robust rigidity criterion for Schwarzschild space. Using this criterion, we recover Israel’s static black hole uniqueness theorem under this mild decay assumption. Likewise, the uniqueness theorems for photon surfaces and static metric extensions from [ HW24 ] extend to all dimensions under these weaker asymptotics. Finally, as a notable by-product of our analysis, we establish regularity of weak inverse mean curvature flow in asymptotically flat manifolds– that is, a weak IMCF is eventually smooth in an arbitrary asymptotically flat background.

1.1
Applications of the Main Theorems

As a consequence of Theorem
1.3
, an asymptotically flat static
(M^{n},g,V)
with connected, outer-minimizing boundary
\partial M=\Sigma^{n-1}
is isometric a piece of Schwarzschild space if and only if

\frac{1}{(n-1)w_{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma}VHd\sigma+2m=\left(\frac{|\Sigma|}{w_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}

This rigidity criterion for Schwarzschild is robust in that it holds in all dimensions, is independent of the positive mass theorem, and is valid regardless of the specific inner boundary condition. Therefore, we apply it to several geometric boundary value problems in general relativity.

The first application is to the famed static black hole uniqueness problem. The question is whether an asymptotically flat
(M^{n},g,V)
with
V|_{\partial M}\equiv 0
is isometric to Schwarzschild space (
1.2
), which would physically mean that Schwarzschild spacetime is the only (standard) static vacuum spacetime containing a black hole. Israel
[
Isr67
]
originally answered this question affirmatively for a connected boundary in
3
dimensions, and Bunting-Massood ul Alam
[
BM87
]
later showed this to be true for
\partial M
not a priori connected. A recent higher-dimensional result due to Agostiniani-Mazzieri
[
AM17
]
involves the Einstein-Hilbert energy of
\partial M=\Sigma
. The normalized Einstein-Hilbert energy of an
(n-1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma^{n-1},\sigma)
is the functional

E(\Sigma,\sigma)=\left(|\Sigma|\right)^{\frac{3-n}{n-1}}\int_{\Sigma}R_{\sigma}d\sigma.

(1.8)

Due to the landmark works of Schoen
[
Sch84
]
and Aubin
[
Aub98
]
, we know that
E(\Sigma,\sigma)\leq E(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},g_{S^{n-1}})
whenever
\sigma
is a constant scalar curvature (CSC) metric that minimizes (
1.8
) within its conformal class. Although CSC metrics are not always Yamabe minimizers, this suggests a dichotomy between CSC metrics with “small” and “large” Einstein-Hilbert energy. Indeed, Agostiniani and Mazzieri in
[
AM17
]
derive a uniqueness theorem for asymptotically flat static black holes of order
\tau>\frac{n-2}{2}
assuming boundary Einstein-Hilbert energy smaller than that of the unit sphere. Here, we recover their result for any positive
\tau

\Sigma
is outer-minimizing.

(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times(r_{m},\infty),g_{m},V_{m})
from (
1.2
).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF). Given a Riemannian manifold

and a smooth, closed manifold

solve IMCF if

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F(\theta,t)=\frac{1}{H}\nu(\theta,t),\hskip 28.45274pt(\theta,t)\in\Sigma^{n-1}\times[0,T),

(2.12)

where

and

are the outer unit normal and mean curvature of the surface

\displaystyle Vu^{-1}\cdot ru_{r}

\displaystyle=(1-2mr^{2-n}+O(r^{1-n}))\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left(-(n-2)mr^{2-n}-(n-1)\dot{u}r^{1-n}-3(n-2)m^{2}r^{4-2n}+O_{\infty}(r^{\nu})\right)

\displaystyle V(u^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=(1-mr^{2-n}+O_{1}(r^{1-n}))\left(2mr^{2-n}+2\dot{u}r^{1-n}+m^{2}r^{4-2n}+O_{\infty}(r^{\nu})\right)

Since

decays faster than

, we obtain

\displaystyle u^{-2}\left(-rV_{r}+rVu^{-1}u_{r}\ +(n-2)V(u^{2}-1)\right)=-\frac{2}{n}\dot{u}r^{1-n}+O(r^{-\nu}).

On the other hand, since
\dot{u}
is an eigenfunction in
\mathcal{H}_{\ell=1}
, we have
\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\dot{u}=-\dot{u}\sigma_{ab}
and hence

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}V+Vu^{-1}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}u=\frac{n-2}{n}\dot{u}\sigma_{ab}+\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}F-\dot{u}\sigma_{ab}+O_{\infty}(r^{-\nu}).

Therefore,
\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}F=O(r^{-\nu})
and the proof of

is completed.
∎

Next we separate the cases of dimension

Lemma 6.3

In

Lemma 6.4
.

In dimensions

\displaystyle V_{\ell=2}=\hat{V}r^{-n-\frac{2}{n-1}}+{\mathop{\kern 0.0ptV}\limits^{\vbox to-1.4pt{\kern-2.0pt\hbox{...}\vss}}}r^{2-2n}+o_{2}(r^{2-2n})

7
Proof of the Main Theorems

7.1
Rotational symmetry outside a compact set

Let
\breve{u}
and
\breve{V}
be the non-rotationally-symmetric part of
u
and
V
. Namely,
\partial_{a}\breve{u}=\partial_{a}u
,
\partial_{a}\breve{V}=\partial_{a}V
are fixed by requiring

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\breve{u}(r,\cdot)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\breve{V}(r,\cdot)=0\mbox{ for any }r.

(7.43)

We thus write

\displaystyle u=u_{0}(r)+\breve{u},\quad V=V_{0}(r)+\breve{V}

The main result of this subsection is

Lemma 7.1
.

There exists a constant
r_{1}>r_{0}
that depends only on
n,m,
and
r_{0}
such that
\check{u}=\check{V}=0
on
[r_{1},\infty)\times S^{n-1}
.

Proof.

We begin by deriving the equations satisfied by
\check{u}
and
\check{V}
. Firstly, multiplying the
ra
-component of the static equation (
4.39
) by
u^{-2}
and rewrite it as

\displaystyle r\partial_{a}\left(u^{-2}V_{r}\right)+ru^{-3}V_{r}\partial_{a}\check{u}-\left(u^{-2}-1\right)\partial_{a}\check{V}-\partial_{a}\check{V}=(n-2)\left(Vu^{-3}-1\right)\partial_{a}\check{u}+(n-2)\partial_{a}\check{u}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle ru^{-2}V_{r}-\check{V}-(n-2)\check{u}=a(r)+F

(7.44)

where
F
and
a(r)
are uniquely determined by

\displaystyle\partial_{a}F=-ru^{-3}V_{r}\partial_{a}\check{u}+(u^{-2}-1)\partial_{a}\check{V}+(n-2)(Vu^{-3}-1)\partial_{a}\check{u},\quad\int_{S^{n-1}}F(\cdot,r)=0\mbox{ for any }r.

(7.45)

Secondly, rewriting the
ab
-component of the static equations (
4.40
) as

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\check{V}+\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\check{u}=(1-Vu^{-1})\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\check{u}+\left[-ru^{-2}V_{r}+Vru^{-3}u_{r}+(n-2)V(1-u^{-2})\right]\sigma_{ab}

and taking the divergence, we get

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}(\Delta+n-2)(\check{V}+\check{u})=\nabla^{b}\left[(1-Vu^{-1})\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\check{u}\right]+\partial_{a}\left[-ru^{-2}V_{r}+Vru^{-3}u_{r}+(n-2)V(1-u^{-2})\right]

and hence

\displaystyle(\Delta+n-2)(\check{V}+\check{u})=b(r)-ru^{-2}V_{r}+Vru^{-3}u_{r}+(n-2)V(1-u^{-2})+G

(7.46)

where
G
and
b(r)
are uniquely determined by

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}G=\nabla^{b}\left[(1-Vu^{-1})\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\check{u}\right],\quad\int_{S^{2}}G(\cdot,r)=0\mbox{ for any }r.

(7.47)

Thirdly, adding the equation
\Delta_{g}V=0
and the
rr
-component of the static equation yields

\displaystyle\Delta\check{V}+(n-1)ru^{-2}V_{r}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}(u^{-2}-1)V=0.

(7.48)

Finally, rewrite the zero scalar curvature equation

\displaystyle\Delta\check{u}+(u^{2}-1)\Delta\check{u}-(n-1)ru_{r}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}(u-u^{3})=0.

(7.49)

Subtracting (
7.48
) and (
7.49
) from (
7.46
) to eliminate
\Delta\check{V}
,
\Delta\check{u}
and adding
(n-2)\cdot
(
7.44
) to further eliminate
\check{V},ru^{-2}V_{r}
, we obtain

\displaystyle-(n-2)(n-3)\check{u}-\frac{(n-2)(n-3)}{2}(u^{-2}-1)V+(n-1)ru_{r}-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}(u-u^{3})

\displaystyle=(n-2)F+(n-2)a(r)+b(r)+Vru^{-3}u_{r}+G+(u^{2}-1)\Delta\check{u}

Writing

\displaystyle Vru^{-3}u_{r}

\displaystyle=(Vu^{-3}-1)ru_{r}+ru_{r},

\displaystyle ru_{r}

\displaystyle=r\check{u}_{r}+r(u-\check{u})_{r},

\displaystyle(u^{-2}-1)V

\displaystyle=(u^{-2}-1+2\check{u}-2\check{u})V=-2\check{u}+2(1-V)\check{u}+(u^{-2}-1+2\check{u})V,

we get

\displaystyle(n-2)r\check{u}_{r}+(n-1)(n-2)\check{u}

\displaystyle=(n-2)a(r)+b(r)+(n-2)F+G

\displaystyle\quad+(Vu^{-3}-1)ru_{r}-(n-2)r(u-\check{u})_{r}

\displaystyle\quad+(u^{2}-1)\Delta\check{u}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}(u-u^{3}+2\check{u}).

We now abuse the notion of modes: For a function
f(r,\theta)
,
f_{\ell=0}(r)
is the function with
\int_{S^{n-1}}[f(r,\cdot)-f_{\ell=0}(r)]=0
for any
r
and
f_{\ell\geq 1}(r,\theta)=f(r,\theta)-f_{\ell=0}(r)
. In this terminology,
\check{u},\check{u}_{r},F,G
all have no 0-mode and we obtain

\displaystyle r\check{u}_{r}+(n-1)\check{u}

\displaystyle=F+\frac{G}{n-2}+I

(7.50)

where

\displaystyle I=\left[\frac{1}{n-2}(Vu^{-3}-1)ru_{r}+\frac{1}{n-2}(u^{2}-1)\Delta\check{u}+\frac{n-1}{2}(u-u^{3}+2\check{u})\right]_{\ell\geq 1}.

In the following, we write
C^{k,\alpha}=C^{k,\alpha}(S^{n-1})
for the Hölder space on
S^{n-1}
. Therefore,
\|\dot{u}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
is a function of
r
. In the previous section we have shown that
u
and
V
are rotationally symmetric up to the order
r^{1-n}
. Moreover, the next order appears at
r^{-3}
for
n=3
and
r^{-n-\frac{2}{n-1}}
for
n\geq 4
. Hence there exist constants
r_{1}\gg 1
and

such that

\displaystyle\|\check{u}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}}\leq Mr^{-n},

(7.51)

\displaystyle\|\check{u}_{r}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq Mr^{-n-1},

(7.52)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\check{V}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq Mr^{-n},

(7.53)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\check{V}_{r}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq Mr^{-n-1}

(7.54)

for
r\geq r_{1}
.

In the following estimates,
c
will denote a constant that depends only on
m,n
and changes from line to line.

Lemma 7.2
.

The right-hand side of (
7.50
) is an error term that decays faster than
\check{u}
by
r^{2-n}
.

\displaystyle\left\|F+\frac{G}{n-2}+I\right\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2}.

Proof.

By (
7.51
) and (
7.53
), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2}.

Since
F
takes both positive and negative values, its sup norm is bounded by that of its gradient times the diameter of
S^{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2}.

Similarly, using the bounds up to 3 derivatives (
7.51
), we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla G\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2},\quad\|G\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2}.

The last two terms of
I
are

\displaystyle\begin{split}(u^{2}-1)\Delta\check{u}&=\left(\frac{2m}{r^{n-2}}+O(r^{1-n})\right)\Delta\check{u}\\
(u-u^{3}+2\check{u})_{\ell\geq 1}&=\left[-3(u_{0}^{2}-1)\check{u}-3u_{0}\check{u}^{2}-\check{u}^{3}\right]_{\ell\geq 1}.\end{split}

(7.55)

For the first term of
I
, the binomial theorem implies

\displaystyle u^{-3}=u_{0}^{-3}-3u_{0}^{-4}\check{u}+\mathfrak{Q},\quad\|\mathfrak{Q}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq c\|\check{u}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}^{2}

(7.56)

Hence,

\displaystyle(Vu^{-3}-1)ru_{r}=(V_{0}u_{0}^{-3}-1)ru_{r}+\left(u_{0}^{-3}\check{V}-3V_{0}u_{0}^{-4}\check{u}+\mathfrak{Q}\right)ru_{r}.

As
(V_{0}u_{0}^{-3}-1)=-\frac{4m}{r^{n-2}}+O(r^{1-n})
and
ru_{r}=\frac{(2-n)m}{r^{n-2}}+O(r^{1-n})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[(V_{0}u_{0}^{-3}-1)ru_{r}\right]_{\ell\geq 1}&=\left(-\frac{4m}{r^{n-2}}+O(r^{1-n})\right)r\check{u}_{r}\\
\|\left(u_{0}^{-3}\check{V}-3V_{0}u_{0}^{-4}\check{u}+\mathfrak{Q}\right)ru_{r}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}&\leq cr^{2-n}(\|\check{V}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|\check{u}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}).\end{split}

(7.57)

Note that
\|\check{V}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-n}
by (
7.53
) because
\check{V}
takes both positive and negative values. Therefore, by (
7.51
) and (
7.52
), we get

\displaystyle\|I\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq cr^{-2n+2}M.

∎

Now we integrate (
7.50
)

(r^{n-1}\check{u})_{r}=r^{n-2}(F+\frac{G}{n-2}+I)

from
r
to
\infty
. Note that
there is no constant of integration from
\infty
by (
7.51
) and we obtain the improved bounds

\displaystyle\|\check{u}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2},\quad\|\check{u}_{r}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+1}

(7.58)

where the latter follows from
r\check{u}_{r}=-(n-1)\check{u}+F+\frac{G}{n-2}+I
.

For higher derivative estimates, we differentiate (
7.50
) by
\partial_{a}
and use the bounds on 3 derivatives to get

\displaystyle\|\check{u}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2},\quad\|\check{u}_{r}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+1}.

Next, differentiate the zero scalar curvature equation by a Killing vector
X
to get

\displaystyle u^{2}\Delta(X\check{u})=-2uX(\check{u})\Delta\check{u}+2r(X\check{u})_{r}-X\check{u}+3u^{2}X\check{u}.

The
C^{0,\alpha}
norm of the right-hand side is bounded by
cr^{-2n+2}M
and hence Schauder estimates imply
\|Xu\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq cr^{-2n+2}M
.

For
\nabla V
and
\nabla V_{r}
, we view the
ra
-component of the static equation

\displaystyle r(\partial_{a}\check{V})_{r}-\partial_{a}\check{V}=\left(ru^{-1}V_{r}+(n-2)Vu^{-1}\right)\partial_{a}\check{u}

as an ODE of
\nabla V
. After integration, the improved decay of
\check{u}
implies

\|\nabla\check{V}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+2},\quad\|\nabla\check{V}_{r}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq cMr^{-2n+1}.

In conclusion, the initial bounds are improved to

\displaystyle\|r^{n}\check{u}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|r^{n+1}\check{u}_{r}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}},\|r^{n}\nabla\check{V}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}},\|r^{n+1}\nabla\check{V}_{r}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq\frac{c}{(r_{1})^{n-2}}M

on
r\geq r_{1}
. Finally we observe that the error terms

are of the form

\displaystyle F,G,I=O(r^{2-n})\cdot\left(\check{u},\nabla\check{u},\nabla^{2}\check{u},\nabla^{3}\check{u},\check{u}_{r}\right)+O(r^{2-n})\cdot\left(\check{V}+\nabla\check{V}\right)

(7.59)

from Lemma

. Therefore we can iterate the estimates with improved bounds on

and
\check{V}
to get

\displaystyle\|r^{n}\check{u}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|r^{n+1}\check{u}_{r}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}},\|r^{n}\nabla\check{V}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}},\|r^{n+1}\nabla\check{V}_{r}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\leq\left(\frac{c}{(r_{1})^{n-2}}\right)^{k}M

for any

inductively. This implies

and
\nabla\check{V}=0
on

if

. Since
\check{V}
has no zero mode, we also have
\check{V}=0
on
r\geq r_{1}
. This completes the proof of Lemma
7.1
.
∎

7.2
Proof of rotational symmetry and Theorem
1.2

We first observe that Theorem
1.2
follows from the rotational symmetry of
u
on
[r_{0},\infty)\times S^{n-1}
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
g=u^{2}(\theta,r)dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{S^{n-1}}
be a solution to the static equations (
1.1
) on
[r_{1},\infty)\times S^{n-1}
. Suppose
u
is rotationally symmetric,
u\rightarrow 1
, and
V=1+o_{1}(1)
. Then

\displaystyle u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2m_{0}r^{2-n}}},\quad V=\sqrt{1-2m_{0}r^{2-n}}

for some constant
m_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

As
u
is rotationally symmetric, the zero scalar curvature equation becomes an ODE and integration yields

u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2m_{0}r^{2-n}}}

for some constant
m_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. The
rr
-component of the static equations becomes

\displaystyle V_{rr}-u^{-1}u_{r}V_{r}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2r^{2}}(1-u^{2})V

which is viewed as an ODE in each direction. Let
V_{0}=\sqrt{1-2m_{0}r^{2-n}}
be a solution to the above ODE. Then the Wronskian

\displaystyle W=\begin{vmatrix}V&V_{0}\\
V_{r}&(V_{0})_{r}\end{vmatrix}

satisfies
W_{r}-u^{-1}u_{r}W=0
or
(u^{-1}W)_{r}=0
. The assumption
V=1+o_{1}(1)
implies
W\rightarrow 0
and hence
W
vanishes identically. It follows that
V=V_{0}
.
∎

Now it remains to push the rotational symmetry from
[r_{1},\infty)\times S^{n-1}
to
[r_{0},\infty)\times S^{n-1}
. This is a problem of unique continuation and we supply three proofs.

Proof 1.

Consider the set

\displaystyle S=\{r_{2}\in[r_{0},\infty):\check{u}=\check{V}=0\mbox{ on }[r_{2},\infty)\times S^{n-1}\}

We have shown that
S
is not empty. By continuity,
S
is a closed set. We will show that
S
is open to conclude the rotational symmetry. Fix
r_{2}\in S
and a small constant

8.2
Black Hole Uniqueness

To begin, we recall two well-known facts for static horizons.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V)
be asymptotically flat of order
\tau>0
with mass
m>0
and connected boundary
\partial M=\Sigma
. Suppose that
\Sigma
is a horizon, i.e.
V|_{\Sigma}\equiv 0
. Then on a connected component
\Sigma
of
\partial M
, we have

i.

\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}=\kappa
on
\Sigma
for a constant
\kappa>0
.

ii.

\Sigma^{n-1}\subset(M^{n},g)
is totally geodesic.

Proof.

By the first static equation, we have that
\text{Hess}\left(V\right)(x)=0
for
x\in\Sigma
. Denoting the unit normal on
\Sigma
by
\nu
, we have in local coordinates
(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})
of
\Sigma^{n-1}
that

0=\text{Hess}\left(V\right)_{a\nu}=\partial_{a}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}\right)-h_{a}^{b}\partial_{b}V=\partial_{a}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}\right).

Thus
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}
is constant on
\Sigma
. The positivity of this constant follows for
m>0
. In directions tangent to
\Sigma

0=\text{Hess}_{\Sigma}\left(V\right)_{ab}+\kappa h_{ab}=\kappa h_{ab}.

∎

The idea for the proof of Theorem
1.4
is to consider the level set flow of the static potential beginning from the horizon and apply the equipotential Minkowski inequality (
8.64
) to the near-horizon level sets. The cumbersome technical aspect of this argument is establishing that the near-horizon level sets are outer-minimizing in
(M^{n},\hat{g})
. Luckily, we can verify this by considering an additional conformal metric

\check{g}=\left(\frac{V+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g.

(8.69)

Bunting and Masood ul-Alam
[
BM87
]
used this metric in their

[
Rau21
]
and Cederbaum-Galloway
[
CG17
]
, probably among others. Unlike in those works, we do
not
use this metric for a gluing argument– rather, we will argue that the level set
\{V=s\}\subset(M^{n},\check{g})
is outer-minimizing for sufficiently small

using barriers that arise from IMCF in
(M^{n},\check{g})
. In turn, this guarantees the outer-minimizing property in
(M^{n},\hat{g})
as well (note that for Corollary
8.2
, we do not need outer-minimizing W.R.T. the original metric

(M^{n},g,V)

m>0

If

\check{H}
of
\Sigma

(M^{n},\check{g})

in (

It is also immediately clear by the choice of conformal factor that

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,V)
be static and
u:M^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a proper smooth function with
u|_{\partial M}\equiv 0
. Suppose also that the set
C(u)
of critical points of
u
has
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
measure
0
. Define the domain
\Omega_{t}=\{u\leq t\}\subset M^{n}
, and let
\Phi:(0,t)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be Lipschitz and compactly supported. Then for the function

\phi(x)=(\Phi\circ u)(x),\hskip 56.9055ptx\in\Omega_{t},

we have the integral identity

-\int_{\Omega_{t}}VH\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}d\Omega=\int_{\Omega_{t}}\phi[2H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}+V(H^{2}-|h|^{2})]d\Omega,

(A.79)

where
\nu
,
H
, and
h
are the unit normal, mean curvature, and second fundamental form of the regular level sets of
u
.

Proof.

We will initially assume
\Phi\in C^{1}_{0}(0,t)
. By Sard’s theorem,
\nabla u
is non-vanishing over the level set
\Sigma_{s}=\{u=s\}
for a.e.
s\in[0,t)
, and hence those level sets are regular. For a regular level set
\Sigma_{s}
, we consider the variation field

\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{2}}=|\nabla u|^{-1}\nu.

The variation formula for mean curvature reads

-|\nabla u|^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}=\Delta_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{-1}+(|h|^{2}+\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu))|\nabla u|^{-1}.

Multiplying by
V
and integrating yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{s}}-V|\nabla u|^{-1}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{s}}V\Delta_{\Sigma_{s}}|\nabla u|^{-1}+(|h|^{2}+\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu))V|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma

(A.80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{s}}(\Delta_{\Sigma_{s}}V+V\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|h|^{2})|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{s}}-H|\nabla u|^{-1}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}+V|h|^{2}|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma.

In the last line of we used the static equation
\Delta_{\Sigma_{s}}V+V\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)=-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}H
. Since (
A.80
) holds on
\Sigma_{s}
for a.e.
s\in(0,t)
, we find by the co-area formula that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}V\phi\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}V\phi\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma ds=\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s)\int_{\Sigma_{s}}V\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma ds

(A.81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(s)\int_{\Sigma_{s}}H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}|\nabla u|^{-1}-V|h|^{2}|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}\phi(H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}-V|h|^{2})d\Omega.

We compute

\displaystyle\text{div}(V\phi H\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}+VH\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}+V\phi\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}+V\phi H\text{div}\nu

(A.82)

Because
\Phi
is compactly supported,
\phi
vanishes over
\partial\Omega_{t}=\{u=t\}\cup\partial M
. Therefore, the integral of the left-hand side of (

) vanishes, and we are left with

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{t}}VH\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}\phi H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}+V\phi\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}+V\phi H\text{div}\nu d\Omega

(A.83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}\phi(2H\frac{\partial V}{\partial\nu}-V|h|^{2})d\Omega+\int_{\Omega_{t}}V\phi H\text{div}\nu d\Omega.

In the last line, we applied (
A.81

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}V\phi H\text{div}\nu d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}V\phi H\text{div}(\nu)|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}V\phi H\text{div}_{\Sigma_{s}}(\nu)|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Sigma_{s}}V\phi H^{2}|\nabla u|^{-1}d\sigma ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{t}}V\phi H^{2}d\Omega.

Substituting this into (
A.83
) yields (
A.79

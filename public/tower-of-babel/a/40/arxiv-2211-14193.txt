Null recurrence and transience for a binomial catastrophe model in random environment Keywords: Markov chain, binomial catastrophes, null recurrence, transience.


Abstract
We consider a discrete time population model for which each individual alive at time n n survives independently of everybody else at time n + 1 n+1 with probability β n \beta_{n} . The sequence ( β n ) (\beta_{n}) is i.i.d. and constitutes our random environment. Moreover, at every time n n we add Z n Z_{n} individuals to the population. The sequence ( Z n ) (Z_{n}) is also i.i.d. We find sufficient conditions for null recurrence and transience (positive recurrence has been addressed by Neuts in [ 7 ] ). We apply our results to a particular ( Z n ) (Z_{n}) distribution and deterministic β \beta . This particular case shows a rather unusual phase transition in β \beta in the sense that the Markov chain goes from transience to null recurrence without ever reaching positive recurrence.

The model and the results

Consider a sequence of independent identically distributed (i.i.d. in short) random vectors,
(\beta_{1},Z_{1}),(\beta_{2},Z_{2}),\dots
The
Z
distribution is discrete with support on the set of natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
.
The
\beta
distribution is continuous or discrete with support on
(0,1)
.
Assume that,

\mu=E(-\ln\beta)\in(0,+\infty).

(1)

To ensure that this Markov chain is irreducible we also assume that

P(\beta\in(0,1))=1\mbox{ and }P(Z\geq 2)>0.

For
n\geq 1
, let
{\cal F}_{n}
be the
\sigma
-algebra by
(\beta_{1},Z_{1})\dots(\beta_{n},Z_{n}).

Let
(X_{n})
be a Markov chain defined as follows. Set
X_{0}=1
and
B_{0}=0
. For
n\geq 1
,

X_{n}=B_{n-1}+Z_{n},

where the conditional distribution of
B_{n}
given
{\cal F}_{n+1}
is distributed according to a binomial distribution with parameters
X_{n}
and
\beta_{n+1}
.

We may think of
X_{n}
as the size of a population at time
n
. At any time
n
we sample a
\beta_{n+1}
(the random environment) from a fixed distribution. Each individual alive at time
n
survives to time
n+1
independently of everybody else with probability
\beta_{n+1}
(the binomial catastrophe). The population
X_{n+1}
at time
n+1
is made up of the survivors from time
n
and of an influx of
Z_{n}
new individuals.

The next two results state sufficient conditions for recurrence and transience under additional hypotheses.

Hypothesis 1.
If
\beta_{1}
and
Z_{1}
are independent then the sequences
(\beta_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and
(Z_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are independent.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that Hypothesis 1 holds.
If

\limsup_{t\to\infty}tP(\ln Z>t)<\mu

(2)

then the Markov chain
(X_{n})
is recurrent.

Hypothesis 2.
Assume that
E(\beta^{-\theta})<+\infty
for some
\theta>0
.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that Hypothesis 2 holds.
If

\liminf_{t\to\infty}tP(\ln Z>t)>\mu

(3)

then the Markov chain
(X_{n})
is transient.

We now give a necessary and sufficient condition for positive recurrence.

Theorem 3
.

The Markov chain
(X_{n})
is positive recurrent if and only if

E(\ln Z)<+\infty.

Even when the chain is positive recurrent the paths of the chain can have huge fluctuations. See the simulation in Figure 1.

An example

For this example we will assume that
\beta
has a point mass distribution. This constant is also denoted by
\beta
. Let the distribution of
Z
be given by

P(Z=k)=\frac{C}{k(\ln k)^{a+1}},

for all
k\geq 2
. The parameter
a
is strictly positive and
C>0
only depends on
a
. The Markov chain
(X_{n})
exhibits three different behaviors.

•

If
a>1
the chain
(X_{n})
is positive recurrent for all
\beta
in
(0,1)
.

•

If
a<1
the chain
(X_{n})
is transient for all
\beta
in
(0,1)
.

•

If
a=1
then there exists a critical value
\beta_{c}\in(0,1)
such that
if
\beta<\beta_{c}
then
(X_{n})
is transient while if
\beta>\beta_{c}
the chain is null recurrent.

Note that the case
a=1
gives a (rare?) example of a phase transition from transience to null recurrence without ever reaching positive recurrence. This example requires
E(\ln Z)=+\infty
which seems like an extreme hypothesis for a population model. However, the main point of this example is conceptual. Based on classical examples one may wrongly believe that null recurrence is a fleeting phenomenon. For instance, the simple random walk exhibits null recurrence for a single point in the parameter space. For our model, however, null recurrence can occur for a whole interval in the parameter space.

We now justify our claims. An easy comparison with an integral shows that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{tP(\ln Z>t)}{C/(at^{a-1})}=1.

Using this limit with Theorems 1 and 2 yields the behavior of the chain for
a<1
and
a>1
.

Turning to
a=1
we see that
E(\ln Z)=\infty
. In this case the chain is positive recurrent for no
\beta
in
(0,1)
. The limit above becomes

\lim_{t\to\infty}tP(\ln Z>t)=C.

Let
\beta_{c}=\exp(-C)
. Since
\beta
is deterministic
\mu=-\ln\beta
.
By Theorem 1,
if
\beta<\beta_{c}
the chain is recurrent and therefore null recurrent. On the other hand if
\beta>\beta_{c}
by Theorem 2 the chain is transient.

Neuts in

Recently, the chain

Lemma 4

For

Proof.

We prove the Lemma by induction on
n
. Recall that
X_{1}=B_{0}+Z_{1}

B_{0}=0

which has the same distribution as

n=k
. By construction,

\displaystyle X_{k+1}=

\displaystyle B_{k}+Z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{X_{k}}\xi_{i,k+1}+Z_{k+1},

independent of
(\xi_{i,k+1})_{i\geq 1}

Let
S_{0,k}=0

S_{j,k}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}B_{i,k}

1\leq j\leq k
. Note that

X_{k+1}\overset{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{S_{k,k}}\xi_{i,k+1}+Z_{k+1}.

Define,

\tilde{B}_{\ell,k}=\sum_{i=S_{\ell-1,k}+1}^{S_{\ell,k}}\xi_{i,k}.

Hence,

\sum_{i=1}^{S_{k,k}}\xi_{i,k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\tilde{B}_{\ell,k},

where
\tilde{B}_{1,k},\dots,\tilde{B}_{k,k}
are independent. Moreover,

\tilde{B}_{\ell,k}\overset{d}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{B_{\ell,k}}\xi_{i,k+1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle X_{k+1}\overset{d}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{S_{k,k}}\xi_{i,k}+Z_{k+1}

\displaystyle\overset{d}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\tilde{B}_{\ell,k}+Z_{k+1}.

To conclude the proof of the Lemma we show that
\tilde{B}_{\ell,k}\overset{d}{=}B_{\ell,k+1}.
This comes from the following elementary fact. Assume that
X
is a binomial random variable with parameters
N
and
p
and that
X
is independent of the i.i.d. sequence
(\xi_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of Bernoulli random variables with parameter
f
. Then,

\sum_{i=1}^{X}\xi_{i}

N
and

\sum_{n\geq 1}P(X_{n}=1|X_{0}=1)=+\infty.

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}Z_{i}\ln\left(1-\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}\beta_{j}\right)\right)

Proof of Theorem 1

Proof.

For
j\geq 1
, let
W_{j}=-\ln\beta_{j}
and

\overline{W}_{i}=\frac{1}{i}\sum_{i=1}^{j}W_{i}.

Recall that
E(W_{1})=\mu<+\infty
. Hence, for every
\epsilon
in
(0,\mu)
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
i\geq 1
,

P(\overline{W}_{i}<\mu-\epsilon)\leq e^{-\alpha i}

(5)

Define the event

A_{N}=\{\overline{W}_{i}\geq\mu-\epsilon\,\forall i>N\}.

Choose a natural number
N
such that
P(A_{N})>0
. Let

S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-\prod_{j=1}^{i}\beta_{j}\right).

From (
4
) we have for
n>N
,

\displaystyle I_{n}\geq

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(S_{N}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right);A_{N}\right].

By Hypothesis 1,
\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)
is independent of
A_{N}
and of
\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-\prod_{j=1}^{i}\beta_{j}\right)
. Hence,

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(S_{N}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right);A_{N}\right]=

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(S_{N}\right);A_{N}\right]E\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right)\right].

By the Harris-FKG inequality (see for instance (2.4) in
[
4
]

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(S_{N}\right);A_{N}\right]\geq E\left[\exp\left(S_{N}\right)\right]P(A_{N})

For fixed
N
the r.h.s. is just a constant
K(N)>0
. Hence,

I_{n}\geq K(N)E\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right)\right].

We now turn to,

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}\ln\left(1-e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right)\right]\geq

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(-(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}Z_{i+1}e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right]\geq

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(-(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i+1}e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}\right)\right]\equiv L_{n}

where we use that
\ln(1-x)\geq-(1+\epsilon)x
for
x
small enough. We take
N
large enough so that
this inequality holds for
x=e^{-i(\mu-\epsilon)}
for all
i>N
.

Hence, to show recurrence it is enough to show that the series with general term
L_{n}
is infinite.
In fact this will be a consequence of

\sum_{n\geq 1}\tilde{L}_{n}=+\infty,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{n}=E(\exp(-\sum_{i=1}^{n}c^{i}Z_{i}))=\prod_{i=1}^{n}E(\exp(-c^{i}Z_{i})),

where
0<c<1
.

To estimate
\tilde{L}_{n}
we start with the following Lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
Z
be a random variable with support on the positive integers. Then, for
\lambda>0
,

E(e^{-\lambda Z})=1-(e^{\lambda}-1)\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda k}P(Z\geq k).

Proof.

\displaystyle E(e^{-\lambda Z})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}e^{-\lambda k}P(Z=k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}e^{-\lambda k}\left(P(Z\geq k)-P(Z\geq k+1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}e^{-\lambda k}P(Z\geq k)-\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda(k-1)}P(Z\geq k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(e^{\lambda}-1)\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda k}P(Z\geq k).

∎

Using Lemma
5
with
\lambda=c^{i}
,

\displaystyle E(e^{-c^{i}Z_{i}})=

\displaystyle 1-(e^{c^{i}}-1)\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-c^{i}k}P(Z\geq k)

We now use that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}kP(\ln Z>k)<\mu.

For
\epsilon>0
small enough there is an integer
k_{0}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

P(\ln Z\geq\ln k)\leq\frac{\mu-\epsilon}{\ln k}.

Hence,

E(e^{-c^{i}Z})\geq 1-(e^{c^{i}}-1)\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}e^{-c^{i}k}P(Z\geq k)-(\mu-\epsilon)(e^{c^{i}}-1)\sum_{k\geq k_{0}+1}\frac{1}{\ln k}e^{-c^{i}k}

(6)

We first bound the finite sum,

\displaystyle(e^{c^{i}}-1)\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}e^{-c^{i}k}P(Z\geq k)

\displaystyle\leq(e^{c^{i}}-1)\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}e^{-c^{i}k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e^{c^{i}}-1)e^{-c^{i}}\frac{1-e^{-c^{i}k_{0}}}{1-e^{-c^{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-e^{-c^{i}k_{0}}

Using this bound in equation (
6
),

E(e^{-c^{i}Z})\geq 1-(1-e^{-c^{i}k_{0}})-(\mu-\epsilon)(e^{c^{i}}-1)\sum_{k\geq k_{0}+1}\frac{1}{\ln k}e^{-c^{i}k}

(7)

Observe that for any
\delta>0
there exists a
\gamma>0
such that if
|x|<\gamma
then

e^{x}-1\leq(1+\delta)x.

Hence, there exists
i_{0}
such that if
i\geq i_{0}
then

E(e^{-c^{i}Z})\geq 1-(1-e^{-c^{i}k_{0}})-(1+\delta)c^{i}\left((\mu-\epsilon)\sum_{k\geq k_{0}+1}\frac{1}{\ln k}e^{-c^{i}k}\right)

(8)

The following elementary lemma estimates the tail of the series in equation (
8
).

Lemma 6
.

Let
0<c<1
,
d=1/c
and
i\geq 1
then

c^{i}\sum_{k\geq d^{i}}\frac{1}{\ln k}e^{-c^{i}k}\leq\frac{k_{1}}{i\ln d},

where
k_{1}=\frac{e^{-1}}{1-e^{-1}}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle c^{i}\sum_{k\geq d^{i}}\frac{1}{\ln k}e^{-c^{i}k}=

\displaystyle c^{i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=\ell d^{i}}^{(\ell+1)d^{i}-1}\frac{1}{\ln k}e^{-c^{i}k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c^{i}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}d^{i}e^{-c^{i}\ell d^{i}}\frac{1}{\ln(\ell d^{i})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}e^{-\ell}\frac{1}{i\ln d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\frac{e^{-1}}{(1-e^{-1})\ln d}.

∎

Let
c=e^{-(\mu-\epsilon)}
and therefore,

\ln d=\mu-\epsilon.

Using Lemma
6
in equation (
8
),

\displaystyle E(e^{-c^{i}Z})\geq 1-(1-e^{-c^{i}k_{0}})-(1+\delta)\frac{k_{1}}{i}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{n}=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}E(e^{-c^{i}Z_{i}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{i_{0}}E(e^{-c^{i}Z_{i}})\prod_{i=i_{0}+1}^{n}\left(1-(1-e^{-c^{i}k_{0}})-(1+\delta)\frac{k_{1}}{i}\right)

For

large enough and

converges and that

Thus, for

.

V_{n}=\sum_{i=K\ln n}^{n}v_{i}.

Then,
U_{n}=\exp(-V_{n})

\displaystyle v_{i}=

\displaystyle-\ln\left[1-(e^{\lambda_{i}}-1)\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda_{i}k}P(Z\geq k)\right]

\displaystyle(e^{\lambda_{i}}-1)\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-\lambda_{i}k}P(Z\geq k)

\displaystyle v_{i}\geq

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}\lambda_{i}\int_{\delta B^{i}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{i}x}}{\ln x}dx

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}\lambda_{i}B^{i}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-x}}{\ln x+i\ln B}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{i\ln B}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{i\ln B}{\ln x+i\ln B}e^{-x}dx

By the Monotone Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{i\ln B}{\ln x+i\ln B}e^{-x}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{\infty}e^{-x}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\delta}

Let
N_{1}
such that for
i\geq N_{1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{i\ln B}{\ln x+i\ln B}e^{-(1-\epsilon)x}dx\geq

\displaystyle e^{-\delta}-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\delta-\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-2\epsilon,

where we let
\delta=\epsilon.

Hence, for
i
large enough,

\displaystyle v_{i}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{i\ln B}(1-2\epsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu+\epsilon}(1-2\epsilon)\frac{1}{i}.

Let

k_{4}=\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu+\epsilon}(1-2\epsilon).

Since
\mu^{\prime}>\mu
, by taking
\epsilon>0
small enough we get
k_{4}>1
.
Finally, for
n
large enough

\displaystyle V_{n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=K\ln n}^{n}v_{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=K\ln n}^{n}\frac{k_{4}}{i}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k_{4}\left(\ln n-\ln(K\ln n)\right)-\epsilon

Hence,

\displaystyle U_{n}\leq

\displaystyle\exp(-V_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-k_{4}\left(\ln n-\ln(K\ln n)\right)+\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\epsilon}(\frac{n}{K\ln n})^{-k_{4}}.

This shows that the series with general term
U_{n}
converges and completes the proof of Theorem 2.

4.3
Proof of Theorem 3

Define for
1\leq i\leq n
,

\beta_{i,n}=\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}\beta_{j}.

By convention
\beta_{n,n}
is set to be 1.
Let
0<s<1
and for
1\leq i\leq n
,

s_{i}=1-(1-s)\beta_{i,n}.

Note that
s_{n}=s
. Recall that

X_{n}=B_{n-1}+Z_{n}.

Given
{\cal F}_{n}
,
B_{n-1}
is a binomial random variable with parameters
X_{n-1}
and
\beta_{n}
. Thus,

\displaystyle E(s_{n}^{X_{n}}|{\cal F}_{n})=

\displaystyle s_{n}^{Z_{n}}E\left[\left(1-\beta_{n}\right)^{X_{n-1}}|{\cal F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{n}^{Z_{n}}E\left[s_{n-1}^{X_{n-1}}|{\cal F}_{n}\right]

Iterating the preceding equality we get,

\displaystyle E(s_{n}^{X_{n}}|{\cal F}_{n})=

\displaystyle s_{n}^{Z_{n}}s_{n-1}^{Z_{n-1}}E\left[s_{n-2}^{X_{n-2}}|{\cal F}_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{n}^{Z_{n}}s_{n-1}^{Z_{n-1}}\dots s_{1}^{Z_{1}}

Hence,

\displaystyle E(s^{X_{n}})=

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\ln\left(1-\beta_{i,n}(1-s)\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\ln\left(1-\beta_{1,i}(1-s)\right)\right)\right]

We now prove the direct implication in Theorem 3. That is, if
E(\ln Z)<+\infty
then the process
(X_{n})
is positive recurrent.

Note that
\beta_{1,i}
converges to 0 a.s. as
i
goes to infinity. Hence, to show convergence of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\ln\left(1-\beta_{1,i}(1-s)\right)
it is enough to show convergence of
S_{n}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\beta_{1,i}
. Let
{\cal F}_{Z}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the sequence
(Z_{n})
. Then,

\displaystyle E\left(S_{n}|{\cal F}_{Z}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}E(\beta_{1,i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}E(\beta)^{i-1}.

Since
(S_{n})
is an increasing sequence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}E\left(S_{n}|{\cal F}_{Z}\right)=

\displaystyle E\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}|{\cal F}_{Z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}Z_{i}E(\beta)^{i-1}

Thus, in order to prove that
(S_{n})
converges a.s. we will show that the series
\sum_{i\geq 1}Z_{i}b^{i-1}
converges for any
0<b<1
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
(Z_{i})
be an i.i.d. sequence with support in
\mathbb{N}
. The series

\sum_{i\geq 1}Z_{i}b^{i}

converges almost surely for all
b
in
(0,1)
if and only if
E(\ln Z)<+\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that
E(\ln Z)<+\infty
. Then, for any constant
c>0

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P(\ln Z_{i}>ci)<\infty.

Hence, by Borel-Cantelli Lemma

P(\ln Z_{i}>ci\mbox{ i.o.})=0.

Since

\sum_{i\geq 1}Z_{i}b^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\exp(\ln Z_{i}+i\ln b),

we see that
\sum_{i\geq 1}Z_{i}b^{i}<+\infty
for all
0<b<1
.

Conversely, assume that
E(\ln Z)=+\infty
. Then, for any
b
in
(0,1)
,

\sum_{i\geq 1}P(\ln Z_{i}\geq-i\ln b)=+\infty.

Hence, by the second Borel-Cantelli Lemma,

P(\ln Z_{i}\geq-i\ln b\mbox{ i.o.})=1.

Thus,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b^{i}Z_{i}=+\infty.

This concludes the proof of the Lemma and of the direct implication in Theorem 3.

We now turn to the converse in Theorem 3. Assume that
E(\ln Z)=+\infty
.
We will show that for
0<s<1
,
E(s^{X_{n}})
converges to
0
. This is enough to show that
(X_{n})
is not positive recurrent.
Recall that

\displaystyle E(s^{X_{n}})=E\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\ln\left(1-\beta_{1,i}(1-s)\right)\right)\right]

Note that

-\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\ln\left(1-\beta_{1,i}(1-s)\right)=+\infty
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\beta_{1,i}=+\infty
.
For
i\geq 1
, let

W_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}-\ln\beta_{j}.

For
N\geq 1
, let

B_{N}=\{W_{i}\leq i(\mu+1)\mbox{ for all }i\geq N\},

where
\mu=E(-\ln\beta)
. For
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\beta_{1,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\exp(-W_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=N}^{n}Z_{i}\exp(-W_{i})

\displaystyle\geq{\bf 1}_{B_{N}}\sum_{i=N}^{n}Z_{i}\exp(-i(\mu+1))

Note that
{\bf 1}_{B_{N}}
converges to 1 as
N
goes to infinity. By Lemma
7
we know that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}b^{i}Z_{i}=+\infty,

for any
0<b<1
. Hence,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\beta_{1,i}=+\infty
a.s. and
E(s^{X_{n}})
converges to
0
as
n
goes to infinity for any
0<s<1
.
This completes the proof of Theorem 3.
∎

5
Neuts’ model

In this section we show that Theorems 1, 2, and 3 are true for Neuts’ model. Actually, the Markov chain
(Y_{n})
that we construct below generalizes Neut’s model in that the sequence
(\beta_{i})
is i.i.d.

Let
(G_{n})
be an i.i.d. sequence of random variables with support on
\mathbb{N}
and such that

P(G>k)=p^{k-1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
k\geq 1
, let

T_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}G_{i}.

Let
(\beta_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with support in
(0,1)
. For
i\geq 1
let
(\xi_{i,j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent Bernoulli random variables with parameter
\beta_{i}
. All these sequences (for different
i
’s) are independent of each other. Let
(Z_{n})
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with support in
\mathbb{N}
. We define the process
(Y_{n})
as follows. By convention, in what follows any sum from
k
to
\ell
where
k>\ell
is set to 0.

•

Y_{0}=Z_{0}
.

•

If
n=T_{k}
for some
k\geq 1
and if
Y_{n-1}\geq 1
,

Y_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{Y_{n-1}}\xi_{n,j}.

•

If
n\not=T_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
then

Y_{n}=Y_{n-1}+Z_{n}.

We now construct a Markov chain
(X_{n}^{\prime})
on the same probability space. For
n\geq 1
, let
T^{\prime}_{n}=T_{n}-1
and define

X_{n}^{\prime}=Y_{T_{n}^{\prime}}.

Let

Z_{n}^{\prime}=\sum_{k=T_{n-1}+1}^{T_{n}-1}Z_{k}.

Then,

X_{n}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=1}^{X_{n-1}}\xi_{n,j}+Z_{n}^{\prime}.

Note that
(Z_{n}^{\prime})
is an i.i.d. sequence for which
Z^{\prime}
has the same distribution as

\sum_{i=1}^{G^{\prime}}Z_{i},

where
P(G^{\prime}\geq k)=p^{k}
for
k\geq 0
. Observe now that the conditions on the distribution of
Z
in Theorems 1, 2 and 3 are equivalent to the conditions on the distribution of
Z^{\prime}
. Hence, Theorems 1, 2 and 3 apply to the chain
(X_{n}^{\prime})
. Since
X_{n}^{\prime}=Y_{T_{n}^{\prime}}
, these theorems apply to
Neut’s chain
(Y_{n})
as well.

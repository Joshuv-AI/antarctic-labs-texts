Uniform Convergence of Deep Neural Networks with Lipschitz Continuous Activation Functions and Variable Widths
Yuesheng Xu, Haizhang Zhang

Abstract
We consider deep neural networks with a Lipschitz continuous activation function and with weight matrices of variable widths. We establish a uniform convergence analysis framework in which sufficient conditions on weight matrices and bias vectors together with the Lipschitz constant are provided to ensure uniform convergence of the deep neural networks to a meaningful function as the number of their layers tends to infinity. In the framework, special results on uniform convergence of deep neural networks with a fixed width, bounded widths and unbounded widths are presented. In particular, as convolutional neural networks are special deep neural networks with weight matrices of increasing widths, we put forward conditions on the mask sequence which lead to uniform convergence of resulting convolutional neural networks. The Lipschitz continuity assumption on the activation functions allows us to include in our theory most of commonly used activation functions in applications.



Keywords:
uniform convergence, deep neural network, convolutional neural network, Lipschitz continuous activation function, variable widths

Introduction

The last decade has witnessed the immense success of deep learning
[
9
,
20
]
. As we all know, a major part of such successes are due to the powerful expressiveness of the deep neural networks in representing a function. In other words, the deep neural network is the engine of deep learning. In order to explain why deep learning works so well, there is need to understand deep neural networks from rigorous mathematical viewpoints.
As deep neural networks have advanced in machine learning, they have gained much attention in the applied mathematics community
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
22
,
23
,

,
28
,

,

,

,

]
and have gone beyond machine learning.
Deep neural networks, considered as a function class to represent or approximate a function, have exhibited superiority in many aspects to classical approximation functions such as polynomials, trigonometric polynomials, splines, finite elements, wavelets, and kernel functions in approximation and numerical analysis. Unlike the classical function approximation in which a function is approximated by a linear combination of basis functions, deep neural networks approximate a given function by compositions of functions with a networks. Specifically, a neural network is a vector-valued function defined through consecutive function compositions of a given activation function with parameters consisting of weight matrices and bias vectors. A deep neural network of a given function may be determined by finding the parameters that minimize the difference between it and the given function. Mathematically, one would expect that as the number of layers of the deep neural network increases, the difference diminishes and eventually goes to zero as the number tends to infinity. In a special case when the activation function is the rectified linear unit (ReLU), this question was investigated in a number of studies
[
5
,
29
,
39
]
.

A closely related mathematical question, even more basic, is when a deep neural network converges to a meaningful function as its layer number tends to infinity. This question was studied recently for the ReLU activation function with a fixed width in
[
35
]
, for the ReLU activation function with a convolution network in
[
36
]
and for contractive activation functions with a fixed width in
[
16
]
. Although the contractivity hypothesis covers interesting activation functions such as sigmoid, there are many activation functions frequently used in applications that are not contractive, for example, ReLU, parametric rectified linear unit (PReLU), exponential linear unit (ELU) and scaled exponential linear unit (SELU), to name a few. Therefore, there is a need to understand uniform convergence of deep neural networks defined by a non-contractive activation function. This paper will study uniform convergence of deep neural networks of Lipschitz continuous activation functions with weight matrices of variable widths (including bounded and unbounded widths).

Main difficulty in analyzing convergence of deep neural networks is a result of the nonlinearity of the activation function. This was overcome in
[
35
]
for the ReLU activation function by re-expressing the functional application of the activation function in terms of matrix-vector multiplication with activation matrices. Pointwise convergence of neural networks with the ReLU activation function was then analyzed by using the matrix-vector multiplication. When a general Lipschitz continuous activation function is chosen, one can take the advantage of its Lipschitz continuity to overcome the difficulty caused by its nonlinearity. We propose a condition that intertwines the Lipschitz constant of the activation function with the weight matrices to ensure the uniform convergence of the resulting neural networks. The main contribution of this work lies in laying out a general framework for uniform convergence analysis of deep neural networks with general activation functions and pooling operators, both of which are Lipschitz continuous.

Understanding conditions that ensure convergence of deep neural networks is not only theoretically interesting,
but also practically advantageous in guiding their training in applications.
As we know, a deep learning model usually possesses a large number of hidden layers and a massive amount of parameters. For example, Residual Networks (ResNets) can reach over 1,000 layers
[
13
]
. A ResNet with only 50 layers has over 23 million parameters, and the overwhelming ChatGPT (GPT-3) model has approximately 175 billion parameters. In applications, the parameters of a DNN are determined via a training process by minimizing a loss function on given training data. It is desirable to figure out whether or not a DNN system with so many parameters can eventually converge to a meaningful function in a rigorous mathematical sense. Mathematical conditions on the parameter that ensure convergence of the DNNs as the number of their layers increases will be beneficial to the interpretability of the DNNs. Such conditions can also be helpful in training a DNN. For instance, if such conditions are available, people can generate initial parameters of a DNN that satisfy or nearly satisfy the conditions. With such an initial deployment of parameters, the DNN will be inclined to converge more quickly. Finally, uniform convergence rates of DNNs will be applicable to mathematical analysis on the generalization ability of DNN models
[
15
]
.

We organize this paper in seven sections. In section 2, we describe the setting of deep neural networks. Section 3 is devoted to developing a general framework for uniform convergence analysis of deep neural
networks with pooling. A key ingredient that ensures uniform convergence of the deep neural networks is a condition that intertwines the Lipschitz constants of the activation function and the pooling operator with the norm of the weight matrices. In sections 4 and 5, we present uniform convergence results for deep neural networks with weight matrices of fixed widths and bounded widths, respectively. While in section 6, we consider deep neural networks with weight matrices of unbounded widths. Finally, in section 7 we present uniform convergence theorems for convolutional neural networks.

Deep Neural Networks

In this section, we recall the definition of deep neural networks.

We now describe deep neural networks with width
m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
at the
n
-th level, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, from input space
\mathbb{R}^{s}
to the output space
\mathbb{R}^{t}
, where
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a given univariate activation function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we define the vector-valued function

\sigma({\bf{x}}):=[\sigma(x_{1}),\sigma(x_{2}),\dots,\sigma(x_{s})]^{\top},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ {\bf{x}}:=[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{s}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

(2.1)

As in
[
35
]
, for
n
vector-valued functions
f_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{n}:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, such that the range of
f_{i}
is contained in the domain of
f_{i+1}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n-1}
, the consecutive composition of
f_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
, is denoted by

\bigodot_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}:=f_{n}\circ f_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{2}\circ f_{1},

(2.2)

whose domain is that of
f_{1}
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by
{\bf{W}}_{n}
we denote the weight matrix, and by
{\bf{b}}_{n}
the bias vector, of the
n
-th hidden layer. Clearly,
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m_{0}:=s
, and
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The deep neural network is a function defined by

{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}}):=\left(\bigodot_{i=1}^{n}\sigma({\bf{W}}_{i}\cdot+{\bf{b}}_{i})\right)({\bf{x}}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

(2.3)

Given the weight matrix
{\bf{W}}_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times m_{n}}
and bias vector
{\bf{b}}_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}
, of the output layer, the output of the DNN is then given by

{\bf{y}}={\bf{W}}_{o}{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})+{\bf{b}}_{o},\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

We are concerned with convergence of the functions
{\cal{N}}_{n}
determined by the deep neural network as
n
increases to infinity. Because the output layer is a linear function of
{\cal{N}}_{n}
and thus, it does not affect the convergence. Hence, we will consider convergence of the function sequence
{\cal{N}}_{n}
, as
n\to\infty
.
For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\cal{N}}_{n}
is a vector-valued function.

It is convenient to express the neural network
{\cal{N}}_{n}
in recursion in
n
.
From (
2.3
) and the definition (
2.1
), we have the recursion

{\cal{N}}_{1}({\bf{x}}):=\sigma({\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}}+{\bf{b}}_{1})

(2.4)

and

{\cal{N}}_{n+1}({\bf{x}})=\sigma({\bf{W}}_{n+1}{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})+{\bf{b}}_{n+1}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.5)

The goal of this paper is to understand what conditions are required for the weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}
and the bias vectors
{\bf{b}}_{n}
to ensure convergence of the deep neural network for a general activation function.
For this purpose, we suppose that
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L
. That is,

|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)|\leq L|x-y|,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.6)

When
L<1
,
\sigma
is contractive and when
L=1
,
\sigma
is non-expansive. Convergence of deep neural networks with a non-expansive activation function and uniform convergence of deep neural networks with a contractive activation function were established in a recent paper
[
16
]
. We are particularly interested in understanding conditions that ensure uniform convergence of deep neural networks with a Lipschitz continuous activation function with
L>1
.
Many commonly used activation functions are Lipschitz continuous. Among them, some have their Lipschitz constants
L>1
. For example, the parametric rectified linear unit (PReLU)
[
12
]

\sigma(x):=\begin{cases}\alpha x,\ \ &x<0,\\
x,\ \ &x\geq 0\end{cases}

is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L:=\max\{\alpha,1\}
. When
\alpha>1
, PReLU is expanding.
The exponential linear unit (ELU)
[
2
]
is defined by

\sigma(x):=\begin{cases}\alpha(e^{x}-1),\ \ &x\leq 0,\\
x,\ \ &x>0.\end{cases}

It can be verified that ELU is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L:=\max\{\alpha,1\}
. When
\alpha>1
, ELU is expanding. Moreover,
the scaled exponential linear unit (SELU) was proposed in
[
18
]
to construct self-normalized neural networks and it has the form

\sigma(x):=\lambda\begin{cases}\alpha(e^{x}-1),\ \ &x<0,\\
x,\ \ &x\geq 0,\end{cases}

with parameter
\lambda=1.0507
and
\alpha=1.67326
. Clearly, SELU is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L:=\lambda\alpha
, which is greater than 1. Hence, SELU is again expanding.

Convergence of deep neural networks is measured by a vector norm.
We say that a vector norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{l}
satisfies the extension invariant condition if

\|\tilde{{\bf{a}}}\|=\|{\bf{a}}\|,\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ \tilde{{\bf{a}}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{a}}\\
{\bf 0}\end{array}\right],\ \mbox{with}\ {\bf{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\ {\bf 0}\in\mathbb{R}^{l-\nu}\ \mbox{for any}\ \nu\in\mathbb{N}_{l}

(2.7)

and satisfies the monotonicity condition if

\|{\bf{a}}\|\leq\|{\bf{b}}\|\mbox{ whenever }|a_{i}|\leq|b_{i}|,\ i\in\mathbb{N}_{l},\ \mbox{for}\ {\bf{a}}:=[a_{1},\dots,a_{l}]^{T},{\bf{b}}:=[b_{1},\dots,b_{l}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}.

(2.8)

For each
{\bf{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, we recall the
\ell_{p}
-norms as

\|{\bf{a}}\|_{p}:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{l}|a_{j}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 1\leq p<+\infty

and

\|{\bf{a}}\|_{\infty}:=\max\{|a_{j}|:j\in\mathbb{N}_{l}\},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ p=+\infty.

It can be confirmed that the
\ell_{p}
-norms for all
1\leq p\leq+\infty
satisfy both of these conditions. We also need a matrix norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{k\times l}
, which we require to be induced by a vector norm, that is,

\|{\bf{A}}\|=\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l},x\neq 0}\frac{\|{\bf{A}}{\bf{x}}\|}{\|{\bf{x}}\|},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ {\bf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times l}.

Clearly, this matrix norm has the property that

\|{\bf{A}}{\bf{B}}\|\leq\|{\bf{A}}\|\|{\bf{B}}\|,\ \mbox{ for all}\ {\bf{A}},{\bf{B}}\ \mbox{with}\ {\bf{A}}{\bf{B}}\ \mbox{well-defined}.

(2.9)

It follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.8
) that

\|\sigma({\bf{x}})-\sigma({\bf{y}})\|\leq L\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}\|,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ {\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

(2.10)

When
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L
, the neural network
{\cal{N}}_{n}
is also Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
L^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|{\bf{W}}_{j}\|
. In fact, from the recursion (
2.5
) and the Lipschitz continuity (
2.1
), we observe that

\|{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})-{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{y}})\|\leq\left(L^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|{\bf{W}}_{j}\|\right)\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}\|,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ {\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

When the weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}
have variable widths, the resulting neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
have variable dimensions. Considering convergence of such a sequence of neural networks requires special care. We first assume that the weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}
have a fixed width
l\in\mathbb{N}
.
In this case, we say that the deep neural network
{\cal{N}}_{n}
defined by (
2.3
) via
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and an activation function
\sigma
converges uniformly in a bounded set

to a limit function

if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists

such that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})-{\cal{N}}({\bf{x}})\|<\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{whenever}\ \ n\geq N.

By

, we denote the space of continuous vector-valued functions
{\cal{N}}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
defined on
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
with

\sup\{\|{\cal{N}}({\bf{x}})\|:{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}\}<+\infty,

\|\cdot\|
is a vector norm on
\mathbb{R}^{l}
. For any vector norm,

We now consider the case when the matrix widths

, and

by (

is a vector-valued function in

Considering convergence of such a sequence requires us to extend the weight matrices

and bias vectors

to
\tilde{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}

, respectively.

When the widths

,

, of neural networks are unbounded, there exists a subsequence

, with

We now review the notion of sequence spaces.
For

, by

we denote the space of sequences

, where

\|{\bf{x}}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}:=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ 1\leq p<+\infty

and

\|{\bf{x}}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}:=\sup\{|x_{j}|:j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Throughout this paper, we reserve
\|\cdot\|_{p}
as the vector norm in

for a

.
We also need the notion of spaces of semi-infinite matrices. For

, we let

denote the spaces of semi-infinite matrices

, viewed as operators, with
\|{\bf{W}}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}<+\infty
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}
are operator norms induced from the
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
norms.
We extend matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
to
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}_{s})
for

and
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})

, and extend vectors

to
\tilde{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
. For
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, we also define

to be the space of continuous sequence-valued functions

defined on
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
with

\sup\{\|{\cal{N}}({\bf{x}})\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}:{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}\}<+\infty.

When the matrix widths
m_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, of neural networks are variable, either bounded or unbounded,
we define
\tilde{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
by (
2.3
) with
{\bf{W}}_{n}
and

being replaced by
\tilde{\bf{W}}_{n}
and
\tilde{\bf{b}}_{n}
, respectively.
The neural networks

are extensions of

and satisfy the same recursion as (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) with
\tilde{\bf{W}}_{n}
and
\tilde{\bf{b}}_{n}
. We then consider uniform convergence of the sequence

in either

3
Uniform Convergence Analysis Framework

This section is devoted to establishing an analysis framework for uniform convergence of deep neural networks with pooling in a general setting. We will apply this framework to various scenarios in later sections to produce convergence results for deep neural networks of various types.

We now describe the setting. Let
l,\mu\in\mathbb{N}
.
By
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote a sequence of weight matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{(l+\mu)\times l}
(resp.
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
) and by
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a sequence of bias vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
(resp.
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
). Let
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
(resp.
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})\to\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
) denote a pooling operator (linear or nonlinear). We assume that the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant
P
. When
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
is linear, its Lipschitz constant is identical to its norm. Specific pooling operators will be discussed later.
Suppose that
{\cal{N}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy the recursion

\hat{\cal{N}}_{1}({\bf{x}}):=\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}(\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}})+\hat{\bf{b}}_{1})

(3.1)

and

\hat{\cal{N}}_{n+1}({\bf{x}})=\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}(\hat{\bf{W}}_{n+1}\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}}))+\hat{\bf{b}}_{n+1}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.2)

associated with the weight matrices
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
, the bias vectors
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
, and an activation function
\sigma
. If
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
is the identity operator, then
\mu=0
and there is no pooling.
When
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
n>1
, we have that
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, and when
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}:\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})\to\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
n>1
, we have that
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
. The goal of this section is to establish results on uniform convergence of
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Throughout this section, we assume that the norms for appropriate vectors/sequences/matrices involved are well-defined and the neural networks
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy the recursion (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) without further mentioning. We adopt the convention for the notation of the product of numbers

\prod_{j=\mu}^{\nu}a_{j}:=1,\ \ \mbox{if}\ \ \mu>\nu.

The next lemma prepares us for convergence analysis of neural networks. To this end, for
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
e_{m,k}:=\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{m+k}-\hat{\bf{b}}_{k}\|
and
E_{m,k}:=\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{m+k}-\hat{\bf{W}}_{k}\|
.

Lemma 3.1

If the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
are Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively, then
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n+m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq

\displaystyle L\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}e_{m,n-i}+LP\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n-1-i}({\bf{x}})\|E_{m,n-i}

\displaystyle+LP\Lambda_{n+m}^{n-2}\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{m+1}\hat{\cal{N}}_{m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}}\|,\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s},

(3.3)

where

\Lambda_{n}^{i}:=\prod_{j=0}^{i}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n-j}\|,\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ i<n.

(3.4)

Proof:

We prove inequality (
3.1
) by induction on
n
. We first consider the case
n=1
. Since both the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator are Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively, by recursion (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), and the Lipschitz conditions, we obtain for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
that

\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{m+1}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{1}({\bf{x}})\|\leq Le_{m,1}+LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{m+1}\hat{\cal{N}}_{m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}}\|.

That is, inequality (
3.1
) holds for
n=1
. We now assume that inequality (
3.1
) holds for
n=k
.
We next proceed for the case
n=k+1
. Again, by the Lipschitz continuity of the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively, and by recursion (
3.2

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1+m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1}({\bf{x}})\|\leq

\displaystyle Le_{m,k+1}+LP\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k}(x)\|E_{m,k+1}+LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{k+1+m}\|\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{k}({\bf{x}})\|.

(3.5)

Substituting the induction hypothesis into the third term on the right-hand-side of inequality (
3.5
) and noting for all
i,k,m\in\mathbb{N}
that

\Lambda_{k+m}^{i-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{k+1+m-j}\|,

we find for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1+m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1}({\bf{x}})\|\leq

\displaystyle Le_{m,k+1}+LP\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k}(x)\|E_{m,k+1}+L\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Lambda_{k+1+m}^{i}e_{m,k-i}

\displaystyle+LP\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}\Lambda_{k+1+m}^{i}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k-1-i}(x)\|E_{m,k-i}

\displaystyle+LP\Lambda_{k+1+m}^{k-1}\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{m+1}\hat{\cal{N}}_{m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}}\|.

(3.6)

By replacing
i+1
with
i
, the third and fourth terms of the right-hand-side of inequality (
3
) become

L\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Lambda_{k+1+m}^{i-1}e_{m,k+1-i}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ LP\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\Lambda_{k+1+m}^{i-1}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1-1-i}(x)\|E_{m,k+1-i},

respectively. Substituting them into the third and fourth terms of the right-hand-side of
(
3
) leads to
inequality (
3.1
) with
n=k+1
. Thus, the induction principle ensures that inequality (
3.1
) holds for all positive integers
n
and
m
.

\Box

Lemma
3.1
has pointed the direction for establishing the analysis framework for uniform convergence of deep neural networks.
Along this line, we now derive two additional technical lemmas that help estimate the products and sums appearing in (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.2

If
\alpha_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is a sequence of non-negative numbers satisfying the condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=\alpha<1,

(3.7)

then there exists a positive constant
c
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}\leq c,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}

(3.8)

and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)\leq c,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.9)

Proof:

We introduce the notation

A_{n}:=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j},

and

B_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right).

Since condition (
3.7
) is satisfied, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\leq\gamma<1
such that

\alpha_{n}\leq\gamma,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\geq N.

(3.10)

The sequence
A_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is decreasing if
n
is sufficiently large. Therefore, it must be bounded and thus, (
3.8
) holds true.

It remains to show that there exists a positive constant
c
such that
B_{n}\leq c
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be the integer chosen so that inequality (
3.10
) holds.
For all
n\geq N
, we write

B_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)+\sum_{j=N}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right).

(3.11)

For the first sum of the right-hand-side of equation (
3.11
), by (
3.10
) we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{N-1}\alpha_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{i=N}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{n-N+1}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{N-1}\alpha_{i}\right).

The inequality above together with
0<\gamma<1
leads to

\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)<B_{N-1}.

(3.12)

For the second sum of the right-hand-side of equation (
3.11
), once again, according to (
3.10
), we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=N}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=N}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n-N}\gamma^{j}<\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

Substituting this inequality and the estimate (
3.12
) into the right-hand-side of equation (
3.11
) leads to

B_{n}<B_{N-1}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\geq N.

Defining

c:=\max\left\{B_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{N-2},B_{N-1}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right\},

we conclude that
B_{n}\leq c
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\Box

Here comes the second technical lemma.

Lemma 3.3

Let
\alpha_{n}
,
\beta_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be two sequences of non-negative numbers. If

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}<1,\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=0,

(3.13)

then for any positive
\epsilon>0
, there exits
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{n-j}\right)\beta_{n-i}<\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n>N.

(3.14)

Proof:

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the sequence

A_{n}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{n-j}\right)\beta_{n-i}.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. By the second limit of (
3.13
), there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{n}\leq 1
for all
n\geq N_{1}
and

\beta_{n}<\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\geq N_{1}.

(3.15)

We split
A_{n}
into two terms according to
N_{1}
. That is,

A_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-N_{1}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{n-j}\right)\beta_{n-i}+\sum_{i=n-N_{1}+1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{n-j}\right)\beta_{n-i}.

(3.16)

We denote by
A^{1}_{n}
and
A_{n}^{2}
the first and second sums of (
3.16
), respectively. By using the inequality (
3.15
), we obtain for all
n\geq N_{1}
that

A_{n}^{1}<\epsilon\sum_{i=0}^{n-N_{1}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{n-j}\right).

By changing the index
k:=n-j
, we have that

\left(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\alpha_{n-j}\right)=\left(\prod_{k=n-i+1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right).

Hence, we get that

A_{n}^{1}<\epsilon\sum_{i=0}^{n-N_{1}}\left(\prod_{k=n-i+1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right).

(3.17)

Letting
j:=n-i
, using the non-negativity of the numbers
\alpha_{k}
and employing (
3.9
) of Lemma
3.2
, there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-N_{1}}\left(\prod_{k=n-i+1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{n}\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)\leq c_{1}.

This together with (
3.17
) yields that
A_{n}^{1}<c_{1}\epsilon
for all
n\geq N_{1}
.

We next estimate
A_{n}^{2}
. Clearly, there exists a positive constant
c_{2}
such that
\beta_{n}\leq c_{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using this fact and the change of indices
k:=n-j
, we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sum_{i=n-N_{1}+1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{k=n-i+1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right).

We then perform an additional change of indices
j:=n-i+1
and find that

\displaystyle A_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N_{1}}\left(\prod_{k=j}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right).

(3.18)

The first limit of (
3.13
) implies that there exist
0<\gamma<1
and
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
N_{2}\geq N_{1}
such that
\alpha_{n}\leq\gamma
for all
n>N_{2}
. It follows from (
3.18
) that

\displaystyle A_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N_{1}}\left(\prod_{k=j}^{N_{2}-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\left(\prod_{k=N_{2}}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\gamma^{n-N_{2}+1}\sum_{j=2}^{N_{1}}\left(\prod_{k=j}^{N_{2}-1}\alpha_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{2}\gamma^{n-N_{2}+1}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}-1}\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{N_{2}-1}\alpha_{k}\right).

Again, by the non-negativity of the numbers
\alpha_{k}
and estimate (
3.9
) of Lemma
3.2
, we observe for all
n>N_{2}
that

\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}-1}\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{N_{2}-1}\alpha_{k}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{n}\alpha_{k}\right)\leq c_{1}.

This implies for all
n>N_{2}
that

A_{n}^{2}\leq c_{1}c_{2}\gamma^{n-N_{2}+1}.

Since
0<\gamma<1
, there exists
N_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n>N_{3}
,
\gamma^{n}<\epsilon
. Therefore, we conclude that
A_{n}^{2}<c_{1}c_{2}\epsilon
, for all
n>N_{2}+N_{3}
. Adding this estimate to the estimate for
A_{n}^{1}
, we have that

A_{n}<(c_{1}+c_{1}c_{2})\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n>N_{2}+N_{3},

proving (
3.14
).

\Box

With the help of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
, we establish the following result on uniform convergence of the sequence
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
generated by the recursion (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) in a general setting. We say that
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges if there exists
{\bf{b}}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
(resp.
{\bf{b}}^{*}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
) such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}-{\bf{b}}^{*}\|=0
when
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
(resp.
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
),
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\|\cdot\|
denotes a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
(resp. in
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
). Likewise, we say that
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges if there exists
{\bf{W}}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
(resp.
{\bf{W}}^{*}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
) such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}-{\bf{W}}^{*}\|=0
when
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
(resp.
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
),
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\|\cdot\|
denotes a norm in
\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
(resp. in
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
). Here, we do not distinguish the notation of a vector norm from a matrix norm since they can be clearly differentiated from the context. It is known that the sequence
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges if and only if it is Cauchy in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, and the sequence
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges if and only if it is Cauchy in
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
.

Theorem 3.4

Suppose that the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
are Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively,
and that
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded. If the sequences
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converge,
the sequence
\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\|
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies the condition

\omega:=\lim_{n\to\infty}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\|<1,

(3.19)

and there exists a positive constant
\rho
such that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\rho,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.20)

then the sequence
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}
converges uniformly to
{\cal{N}}\in C_{l}(\mathbb{D})
(resp.
{\cal{N}}\in C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
) if
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
(resp.
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
).

Proof:

It suffices to show that the sequence
{\cal{N}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is Cauchy in space
C_{l}(\mathbb{D})
or
C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
. Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary. We wish to prove that there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
c>0
such that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n+m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|<c\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\geq N,m\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.21)

Motivated by Lemma
3.1
, we introduce

J_{n,1}:=L\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}e_{m,n-i},\ \ J_{n,2}({\bf{x}}):=LP\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n-1-i}({\bf{x}})\|E_{m,n-i},\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D},

and

J_{n,3}({\bf{x}}):=LP\Lambda_{n+m}^{n-2}\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{m+1}\hat{\cal{N}}_{m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}}\|,\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}.

By Lemma
3.1
, for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}

we have that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n+m}({\bf{x}})-\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq J_{n,1}+\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,2}({\bf{x}})+\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,3}({\bf{x}}).

(3.22)

We next show that the three terms on the right-hand-side of inequality (
3.22
) tend to zero as
n\to\infty
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

We first consider
J_{n,1}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

\alpha_{n}:=LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n+m}\|\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \beta_{n}:=e_{m,n},\ \ m\in\mathbb{N}.

By hypothesis (
3.19
), we have that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}=\omega<1
.
Since the sequence
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges, we note that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=0
. Applying Lemma
3.3
and noting the definition (
3.4
) of
\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}
, we conclude that there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

J_{n,1}<L\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n>N_{1},\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.23)

For the second term on the right-hand-side of (
3.22
), by the uniform boundedness assumption (
3.20
) on
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
, we see that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,2}({\bf{x}})\leq\rho LP\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}E_{m,n-i},\ \ m\in\mathbb{N}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\alpha_{n}:=LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n+m}\|\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \beta_{n}:=E_{m,n},\ \ m\in\mathbb{N}.

By hypothesis (
3.19
), we observe that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}<1
and by the convergence of sequence
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we find that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=0
.
Again, by employing Lemma
3.3
, there exists
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,2}({\bf{x}})<\rho LP\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n>N_{2}.

(3.24)

It remains to estimate the third term on the right-hand-side of (
3.22
). To this end, we assume that
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded by
D>0
. Again, due to the uniform boundedness of
\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and convergence of
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies that
\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\|\leq w
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for some
w>0
, we reach that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,3}({\bf{x}})\leq LPw(\rho+D)\Lambda_{n+m}^{n-2}.

(3.25)

By the definition (
3.4
) of
\Lambda_{n+m}^{n-2}
with the change of indices
k:=n+m-j
, we note that

\Lambda_{n+m}^{n-2}=\prod_{k=m+2}^{n+m}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{k}\|.

(3.26)

By virtue of hypothesis (
3.19
), there exist
N_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega_{0}\in[\omega,1)
such that
LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{k}\|<\omega_{0}
, for all
k>N_{3}
.
Therefore, by (
3.25
) and (
3.26
),
for all
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 2
,

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,3}({\bf{x}})\leq LP\omega(\rho+D)\omega_{0}^{n+m-N_{3}}\omega^{N_{3}-m-1}..

(3.27)

In view of
0<\omega_{0}<1
, there exists
N_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\omega_{0}^{n-1}<\epsilon
, for all
n>N_{4}
. Upon substituting this result into the right-hand-side of (
3.27
) yields

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}J_{n,3}({\bf{x}})\leq LPw(\rho+D)\epsilon,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ m\geq 1,\ n>N_{4}.

(3.28)

Now, we choose
N:=\max\{N_{i}:i=1,2,3,4\}
. Upon substituting
estimates (
3.23
), (
3.24
) and (
3.28
) into the right-hand-side of (
3.22
) gives rise to (
3.21
) with
c:=L+\rho LP+LPw(\rho+D)
.

\Box

It was indicated in
[
16
]
that when the activation function is contractive, the neural networks
converge exponentially as the layer number tends to infinity if both the sequence of weight matrices and that of bias vectors converge exponentially. Our next task is to establish an exponential convergence theorem for the deep neural network with general Lipschitz continuous activation functions, not necessarily contractive, and general Lipschitz continuous poolings.

Lemma 3.5

Suppose that the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
are Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively,
and that
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded by
D>0
.
If
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}={\bf{b}}^{*}
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}={\bf{W}}^{*}
, and satisfies (
3.19
), and there exists a positive constant
\rho
such that (
3.20
) holds, then there exists a function
{\cal{N}}\in C_{l}(\mathbb{D})
(resp.
{\cal{N}}\in C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
) if
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
(resp.
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
) such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})-{\cal{N}}({\bf{x}})\|\leq L\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\omega_{0}^{i}e_{n-i}+\rho LP\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\omega_{0}^{i}E_{n-i}+LPw(\rho+D)\omega_{0}^{n-1}\right],

(3.29)

where
e_{n}:=\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}-{\bf{b}}^{*}\|
,
E_{n}:=\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}-{\bf{W}}^{*}\|
,
w
is an upper bound of the sequence
\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\|
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\omega\leq\omega_{0}<1
.

Proof:

Theorem
3.4
ensures that the deep neural networks
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
converges to a function
{\cal{N}}
. By Lemma
3.1
, for all positive integers
n
,
m
, inequality (
3.1
) holds. Due to hypothesis (
3.19
), there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\omega\leq\omega_{0}<1
such that

LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{m}\|\leq\omega_{0}
, for all
m\geq N
.
It follows that

\Lambda_{n+m}^{i-1}\leq\omega_{0}^{i}
,
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
, for all
m\geq N,n\in\mathbb{N}

and
\Lambda_{n+m}^{n-2}\leq\omega_{0}^{n-1}
, for all
m\geq N,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Substituting these bounds into the right-hand-side of the inequality (
3.1
),
letting
m\to\infty
in the both sides of the resulting inequality, and using the limits of
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
and
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
, and the uniform boundedness of
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
, we obtain for
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}
the error bound (
3.29
).

\Box

Lemma
3.5
allows us to establish the following exponential convergence result.

Theorem 3.6

Suppose that the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
are Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively, and
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded.
If
\hat{\bf{b}}_{n}
and
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}
converge, respectively, to
{\bf{b}}^{*}
and
{\bf{W}}^{*}
exponentially with (
3.19
), and there exists a positive constant
\rho
such that
\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\rho
, for all
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then the neural networks
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge to a function

{\cal{N}}\in C_{l}(\mathbb{D})
(resp.
{\cal{N}}\in C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
) if
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
(resp.
\hat{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
)
exponentially and uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof:

First of all, Theorem
3.4
ensures that the neural networks
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge to a function
{\cal{N}}
uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
. It remains to prove that the convergence rate of
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}
to
{\cal{N}}
is exponential.
For this purpose, by the hypothesis of this theorem, we conclude that there exist
0<r<1
and
c>0
such that

e_{n}\leq cr^{n}

and

E_{n}\leq cr^{n}.

Substituting these estimates into the right-hand-side of (
3.29
) in Lemma
3.5
, we find that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})-{\cal{N}}({\bf{x}})\|\leq(cL+c\rho L^{2}P)nr_{0}^{n}+\frac{L^{2}Pw(\rho+D)}{\omega_{0}}r_{0}^{n},

where
r_{0}:=\max\{r,\omega_{0}\}
and
D>0
is a bound of
\mathbb{D}
.
Thus, there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
r_{1}\in(r_{0},1)
such that for all
n>N
,

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})-{\cal{N}}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\left[cL+c\rho L^{2}P+\frac{L^{2}Pw(\rho+D)}{\omega_{0}}\right]r_{1}^{n}.

That is, the neural networks
\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge to the function
{\cal{N}}
exponentially and uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
.

\Box

In Theorems
3.4
and
3.6
, we assume that the sequence
\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|
is uniformly bounded in a bounded set
\mathbb{D}
. This can be derived from the next lemma and an additional hypothesis on the vector/sequence norm. In the next lemma, we assume that either
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{(m_{n}+\mu)\times m_{n-1}}
with
m_{0}:=s
, and
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, or
{\bf{W}}_{1}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}_{s})
,
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
,
n>1
, and
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 3.7

If the activation function
\sigma
and the pooling operator
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
are Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constants
L
and
P
, respectively, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{j}\|\right)\|{\bf{x}}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{i}\|\right)(L\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{j}\|+\|(\sigma\circ{\cal{P}}_{\mu})({\bf 0}_{j})\|),

(3.30)

where
{\bf 0}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{j}+\mu}
are zero vectors for the finite dimensional case and
{\bf 0}_{j}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
are zero vectors for the infinite dimensional case.

Proof:

We establish inequality (
3.30
) by induction on
n
. When
n=1
, by (
2.4
), for all
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
we have that

\displaystyle\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{1}({\bf{x}})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}(\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}})+\hat{\bf{b}}_{1})-\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{1}))\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{1}))\|

\displaystyle\leq LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{1}\|\|{\bf{x}}\|+L\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{1}\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{1}))\|.

Thus, inequality (
3.30
) holds for
n=1
. We assume that inequality (
3.30
) holds for
n=k
and proceed for the case
n=k+1
. Using the recursion formula (
3.2
) and the induction hypothesis, for all
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we obtain that

\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}(\hat{\bf{W}}_{k+1}\hat{\cal{N}}_{k}({\bf{x}}))+\hat{\bf{b}}_{k+1})-\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{k+1}))\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{k+1}))\|.

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
\sigma
and
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
, we find that

\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1}({\bf{x}})\|\leq LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{k+1}\|\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k}({\bf{x}})\|+L\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{k+1}\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{k+1}))\|.

Invoking the induction hypothesis in the right-hand-side of the last inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{k+1}({\bf{x}})\|\leq

\displaystyle LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{k+1}\|\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{j}\|\right)\|{\bf{x}}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{k}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{i}\|\right)(L\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{j}\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{j}))\|)\right]

\displaystyle+L\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{k+1}\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{k+1}))\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{j}\|\right)\|{\bf{x}}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{k+1}LP\|\hat{\bf{W}}_{i}\|\right)(L\|\hat{\bf{b}}_{j}\|+\|\sigma({\cal{P}}_{\mu}({\bf 0}_{j}))\|).

Thus, (
3.30
) holds for
n=k+1
and the induction principle ensures that inequality (
3.30
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\Box

Lemma
3.7
with an additional hypothesis on the norm guarantees that the sequence
\|\hat{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is uniformly bounded in a bounded set
\mathbb{D}
. Such a hypothesis will be made clear in later sections in specific contexts.

4
Uniform Convergence of Deep Neural Networks with a Fixed Width

In this section, we establish uniform convergence of deep neural networks with a fixed width.

Throughout this section, we let
l\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed and suppose that the sequence of weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times s}
,
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
,
n\geq 2
, and the sequence of bias vectors
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We then define neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
by (
2.3
)
without pooling
and they satisfy the recursions (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). We assume that the vector norm used in this section satisfies
(
2.7
) and (
2.8
).

The next lemma that follows directly from Lemma
3.7
provides a bound of the deep neural network by the norms of the weight matrices and bias vectors. Here, the widths of the weight matrices and bias vectors are assumed to be variable, which is somewhat more general than what we need in this section. Since the neural networks are constructed without pooling, in this case we have that
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
is the identity operator and
P=1
.

Lemma 4.1

If the activation function
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
,
the weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m_{0}:=s
, and the bias vectors
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}L\|{\bf{W}}_{j}\|\right)\|{\bf{x}}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}L\|{\bf{W}}_{i}\|\right)(L\|{\bf{b}}_{j}\|+\|\sigma({\bf 0}_{m_{j}})\|),

(4.1)

where
{\bf 0}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
is a zero vector.

Inequality (
4.1
) in Lemma
4.1
may be translated to the boundedness of the deep neural network sequence in a bounded domain.

Lemma 4.2

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
,
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded by
D>0
, the weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m_{0}:=s
, and the bias vectors
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\|{\bf{b}}_{n}\|\leq c
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy the condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}L\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|<1,

(4.2)

then there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
such that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq c_{1}D+Lcc_{1}+c_{1}\max\{\|\sigma({\bf 0}_{m_{j}})\|:j\in\mathbb{N}_{n}\},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

(4.3)

where
{\bf 0}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{j}
is a zero vector.
If the widths
m_{n}
are bounded, then there exists a constant
\rho>0
such that
\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\rho
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof:

By condition (
4.2
), according to Lemma
3.2
with
\alpha_{n}:=L\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|
, there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\prod_{j=1}^{n}L\|{\bf{W}}_{j}\|\leq c_{1}

and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}L\|{\bf{W}}_{i}\|\right)\leq c_{1}.

The hypothesis of this lemma ensures that inequality (
4.1
) in Lemma
4.1
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using inequality (
4.1
) together with the bounds given above, we obtain the estimate (
4.3
).

When the widths
m_{n}
are bounded, there is a positive constant
c_{2}
such that

\max\{\|\sigma({\bf 0}_{m_{j}})\|:j\in\mathbb{N}_{n}\}\leq c_{2}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, (
4.3
) ensures that

\sup_{{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}}\|{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|\leq\rho:=c_{1}D+Lcc_{1}+c_{1}c_{2},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},

proving the lemma.

\Box

We are now ready to derive the uniform convergence of deep neural networks with a fixed width from Theorem

3.4
.

Theorem 4.3

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
and
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded.
If the sequences
{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converge with (
4.2
), then the neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof:

Note that the neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
satisfy the recursions (
2.4
) and (
2.5
). Since the sequence
{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges, it is bounded. By hypothesis, the sequence
\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies
(
4.2
). Moreover, the neural networks have a fixed width. By Lemma
4.2
, we know that
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
is uniformly bounded by a constant
\rho
for all

and
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.4
, the neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converge uniformly to a function

in
\mathbb{D}
.

\Box

When we restrict the activation functions to contractions, Theorem
4.3
is specialized to the uniform convergence theorem established in
[
16
]
, since in the special case of having a contractive activation function the assumption (

) becomes

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|<\gamma,

(4.4)

which was assumed in
[
16
]
. When

which ensures the uniform convergence of deep neural networks with a non-expansive activation function, according to Theorem

guarantees uniform convergence of neural networks with not only contractive but also expansive (Lipschitz continuous) activation functions.

The next result on the rate of uniform convergence of neural networks follows directly from Theorem
3.6
.

Theorem 4.4

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant

and

is bounded. If

.

To close this section, we present a convergence result for deep neural networks with pooling. In building a neural network, pooling is used to enhance features and reduce the dimension of features.
Uniform convergence of deep neural networks with the average pooling and a fixed width in the vector norms
\|\cdot\|_{p}
, for
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, and that of deep neural networks with the max pooling in the vector norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
were established in
[
16
]
.
Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
may be modified for uniform convergence of deep neural networks with pooling. The average pooling and the max pooling are most popular in application. For an integer
\mu\geq 2
, the average pooling
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}^{A}
is the linear operator from
\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{l}
defined by

({\cal{P}}_{\mu}^{A}{\bf{x}})_{i}:=\frac{1}{\mu+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\mu}x_{i+j},\ \ i\in\mathbb{N}_{l},\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}

(4.6)

and the max pooling

is the nonlinear map from
\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}
to

({\cal{P}}_{\mu}^{M}{\bf{x}})_{i}:=\max\{x_{i+j}:j=0,1,\dots,\mu\},\ \ i\in\mathbb{N}_{l},\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}.

(4.7)

It is known that if
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, then for all
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}

\|{\cal{P}}_{\mu}^{A}{\bf{x}}\|_{p}\leq\|{\bf{x}}\|_{p},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu},\ p\in[1,+\infty]

\|{\cal{P}}_{\mu}^{M}{\bf{x}}-{\cal{P}}_{\mu}^{M}{\bf{y}}\|_{p}\leq(\mu+1)^{1/p}\|{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}\|_{p},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ {\bf{x}},{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}.

(4.9)

It can be seen from (

is a non-expansive linear operator, and from (

is Lipschitz continuous with the Lipschitz constant

\|\cdot\|_{p}

are expanding and when

is non-expansive.
In general, by

we denote the pooling map from
\mathbb{R}^{l+\mu}
to
\mathbb{R}^{l}
. Suppose that
{\bf{W}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{(l+\mu)\times s}
,
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{(l+\mu)\times l}
for

,
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
for

. Deep neural networks with pooling have the form

{{\cal{N}}}^{\cal{P}}_{n}({\bf{x}}):=\left(\bigodot_{i=1}^{n}\sigma({\cal{P}}({\bf{W}}_{i}\cdot)+{\bf{b}}_{i})\right)({\bf{x}}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

(4.10)

Clearly, the sequence
{{\cal{N}}}^{\cal{P}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,

, satisfies the recursions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). The next theorem follows directly from Theorems
3.4
and

Theorem 4.5

Suppose that

5
Uniform Convergence of Deep Neural Networks with Bounded Widths

In this section, we extend the uniform convergence theorems established in the last section for deep neural networks with weight matrices of a
fixed
width to those with weight matrices of
bounded
widths.

We adopt the setting described in the beginning of section 2 and assume in this section that the matrix widths
m_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, of the deep neural networks are bounded. Specifically, we assume
l:=\max\{m_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}<+\infty
. Thus,
1\leq m_{n}\leq l
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{n_{0}}=l
for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the deep neural network
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
by (
2.3
). Clearly,
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
is a vector-valued function in
\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
. The size of
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
varies according to
n
. The goal of this section is to establish uniform convergence theorems of the sequence
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The approach used in the last section for convergence analysis is limited to deep neural networks with weight matrices of a
fixed
width. To address this issue,
we augment the matrix
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
to
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
and the vector
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
to
\tilde{{\bf{b}}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
by the zero-padding. That is,
we let

\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{1}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf{W}}_{1}\\
{\bf 0}_{(l-m_{1})\times s}\end{array}\right],

(5.1)

\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}{\bf{W}}_{n}&{\bf 0}_{m_{n}\times(l-m_{n-1})}\\
{\bf 0}_{(l-m_{n})\times m_{n-1}}&{\bf 0}_{(l-m_{n})\times(l-m_{n-1})}\end{array}\right],\ \ n>1,

(5.2)

and

\tilde{{\bf{b}}}_{n}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf{b}}_{n}\\
{\bf 0}_{l-m_{n}}\end{array}\right].

(5.3)

We then define the deep neural network
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
by

\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}}):=\left(\bigodot_{i=1}^{n}\sigma(\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{i}\cdot+\tilde{{\bf{b}}}_{i})\right)({\bf{x}}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

(5.4)

Clearly,
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
is a vector-valued function in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, that is, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
have the same size
l
. Moreover,
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
has the recursion

\tilde{\cal{N}}_{1}({\bf{x}}):=\sigma(\tilde{\bf{W}}_{1}{\bf{x}}+\tilde{\bf{b}}_{1})

(5.5)

and

\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n+1}({\bf{x}})=\sigma(\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n+1}\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})+\tilde{{\bf{b}}}_{n+1}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.6)

We need to define the uniform convergence of
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 5.1

We say that a sequence of vector-valued functions
{\cal{N}}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges uniformly to a function
{\cal{N}}\in C_{l}(\mathbb{D})
if the sequence of the augmented vector-valued functions
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges uniformly to
{\cal{N}}
in the space
C_{l}(\mathbb{D})
in a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{l}
.

Likewise, we need to define the convergence of
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 5.2

We say that a sequence of matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
converges to
{\bf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
as
n\to\infty
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}-{\bf{W}}\|=0,

where
\|\cdot\|
is the induced matrix norm of a vector norm on
\mathbb{R}^{l}
.

Convergence of a sequence of vectors
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, can be understood as convergence of a sequence of
m_{n}\times 1
matrices.

We need to understand the relation between the norm of a matrix and that of its augmented matrix with zero blocks.
For
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
{\bf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mu\times\nu}
and for
l\geq\max\{\mu,\nu\}
we let

\tilde{{\bf{W}}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}{\bf{W}}&{\bf 0}_{\mu\times(l-\nu)}\\
{\bf 0}_{(l-\mu)\times\nu}&{\bf 0}_{(l-\mu)\times(l-\nu)}\end{array}\right].

(5.7)

In the next lemma, we show that the matrix augmentation process described above preserves certain matrix norms.

Lemma 5.3

For
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that
l\geq\max\{\mu,\nu\}
.
If
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times l}
is an augmented matrix of
{\bf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mu\times\nu}
defined by (
5.7
) and
\|\cdot\|
is the matrix norm induced from a vector norm on
\mathbb{R}^{l}
satisfying the extension invariant (
2.7
) and the monotonicity condition
(
2.8
),
then

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\|=\|{\bf{W}}\|.

(5.8)

Proof:

By the definition (
5.7
) of
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}
, we have that

\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\tilde{{\bf{x}}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{W}}{\bf{x}}\\
{\bf 0}_{l-\nu}\end{array}\right],\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ \tilde{{\bf{x}}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{x}}\\
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\ {\bf{x}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{l-\nu}.

Since the vector norm
\|\cdot\|
satisfies (
2.7
),
we obtain that

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\tilde{{\bf{x}}}\|=\|{\bf{W}}{\bf{x}}\|,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ \tilde{{\bf{x}}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{x}}\\
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\ {\bf{x}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{l-\nu}.

(5.9)

The definition of the matrix norm induced from the vector norm together with equation (
5.9
) ensures that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\frac{\|{\bf{W}}{\bf{x}}\|}{\|\tilde{{\bf{x}}}\|}:\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ \tilde{{\bf{x}}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{x}}\\
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu},\ {\bf{x}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{l-\nu}\right\}.

In the right-hand-side of equation (
5
), we restrict
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}={\bf 0}
and since the vector norm satisfies the extension invariant property (
2.7
), we note that
\|\tilde{{\bf{x}}}\|=\|{\bf{x}}\|
with this restriction. Therefore, we find that

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\|\geq\sup\left\{\frac{\|{\bf{W}}{\bf{x}}\|}{\|{\bf{x}}\|}:{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\right\}=\|{\bf{W}}\|.

(5.12)

On the other hand, in light of the monotonicity condition (
2.8
) that the vector norm satisfies, we observe for all

\tilde{{\bf{x}}}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{x}}\\
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{l}

with
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}
and
{\bf{x}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{l-\nu}

that

\|\tilde{{\bf{x}}}\|\geq\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\bf{x}}\\
{\bf 0}\end{array}\right]\right\|=\|{\bf{x}}\|,\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ {\bf 0}\in\mathbb{R}^{l-\nu}.

This inequality together with (
5
) ensures that

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}\|\leq\sup\left\{\frac{\|{\bf{W}}{\bf{x}}\|}{\|{\bf{x}}\|}:{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\nu}\right\}=\|{\bf{W}}\|.

(5.13)

Combining inequalities (
5.12
) and (
5.13

equation (
5.8
).

,

Proof:

According to Definition
5.1
, it suffices to prove that

,

4.3

(5.14)

Because

This equation with the hypothesis that the sequence

7
Uniform Convergence of Convolutional Neural Networks

In this section, we establish uniform convergence results of convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Pointwise convergence of CNNs with the ReLU activation function was investigated in
[
36
]
by considering CNNs as deep neural networks with weight matrices of increasing widths.
We study uniform convergence of CNNs with Lipschitz continuous activation functions by considering two types of matrix extensions.
We first consider the matrix extension by the zero-padding which is described in the last section, and apply Theorem
6.7
to CNNs with Lipschitz continuous activation functions to obtain a uniform convergence result of CNNs. We then consider the extension of the weight matrices to semi-infinite matrices by the constant-padding along the diagonals, which is natural for CNNs, and present a uniform convergence theorem for CNNs, with a weaker hypothesis on the filter mask.

We now recall the construction of CNNs.
Given a vector
{\bf{x}}:=[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{l}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
and a filter mask
{\bf{w}}:=[w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{\tau}]^{\top}
, the convolution
{\bf{x}}*{\bf{w}}
of
{\bf{x}}
with
{\bf{w}}
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{l+\tau}
defined by

({\bf{x}}*{\bf{w}})_{i}:=\sum_{j=\max(0,i-l)}^{\min(i-1,\tau)}{\bf{w}}_{j}{\bf{x}}_{i-j},\ \ i\in\mathbb{N}_{l+\tau}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by
\tau_{n}
we denote a sequence of positive integers.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, given a filter mask
{\bf{w}}^{(n)}:=[w^{(n)}_{0},w^{(n)}_{1},\dots,w^{(n)}_{\tau_{n}}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{\tau_{n}+1}
and a bias vector
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
, we construct a CNN by

{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}}):=\sigma({\cal{N}}_{n-1}({\bf{x}})*{\bf{w}}^{(n)}+{\bf{b}}_{n}),

(7.1)

with
{\cal{N}}_{0}({\bf{x}}):={\bf{x}}
. In (
7.1
),
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz continuous activation function with the Lipschitz constant
L
.

One can express the convolution
{\bf{x}}*{\bf{w}}
via multiplication of
{\bf{x}}
with an
(l+\tau)\times l
Toeplitz matrix

T_{ij}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} w_{i-j},&\quad 1\leq j\leq i\leq j+\tau\leq l+\tau,\\
0,&\quad\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

in the form

{\bf{x}}*{\bf{w}}=T{\bf{x}}.

Clearly, matrix
T
has a specific form

T=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}w_{0}&0&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
w_{1}&w_{0}&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
w_{\tau}&w_{\tau-1}&\cdots&w_{0}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&w_{\tau}&\cdots&w_{1}&w_{0}&0\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&0&w_{\tau}&w_{\tau-1}&\cdots&w_{0}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&0&w_{\tau}\cdots&\cdots&w_{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots 0&\cdots&w_{\tau}&w_{\tau-1}\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots 0&w_{\tau}\end{array}\right].

That is, we map the filter mask
{\bf{w}}
to the Toeplitz matrix
T
whose diagonal and the first to the
\tau
th sub-diagonals are the components of
{\bf{w}}
.
The CNNs
{\cal{N}}_{n}
may be written in a form of the DNN with increasing widths:

m_{0}:=s,\ \ m_{n}:=m_{n-1}+\tau_{n},\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(7.2)

Introducing weight matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}\times m_{n-1}}
defined by

({\bf{W}}_{n})_{ij}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} w^{(n)}_{i-j},&\quad 1\leq j\leq i\leq j+\tau_{n}\leq m_{n-1}+\tau_{n},\\
0,&\quad\mbox{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

(7.3)

we have for
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})=\sigma({\bf{W}}_{n}{\cal{N}}_{n-1}({\bf{x}})+{\bf{b}}_{n}),\ \ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

(7.4)

Clearly, from (
7.2
), the CNNs
{\cal{N}}_{n}
are deep neural networks with unbounded widths.

We first consider uniform convergence of CNNs
{\cal{N}}_{n}
in the sense of Definition
6.2
.
To this end, we extend
{\bf{W}}_{1}
,
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and
{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, to
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{1}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}_{s})
,
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})
,
n\geq 2
, and
\tilde{{\bf{b}}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, respectively,
by (
6.1
), (
6.2
) and (
6.3
). We then define
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
by
(
6.6
) and note that
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
is an extension of
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
, that results from the zero-padding. We confine ourselves to filter masks of a fixed length. Uniform convergence of CNNs in this sense follows directly from Theorem
6.7
.

We need the following preliminary result.

Lemma 7.1

Suppose that
\tau
is a positive integer and
\tau_{n}=\tau
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
w_{i}^{(n)}
satisfies the condition

w_{i}^{(n)}=o\left(1\right),\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ i=0,1,\dots,\tau,

(7.5)

then there hold

(i)
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is a Cauchy sequence;

(ii)
\lim_{n\to+\infty}L\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{p}<1
for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
, where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
\sigma
.

Proof:

Suppose that
n
and
m
are arbitrary positive integers with
n>1
. A direct computation leads to

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n+m}-\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}-w_{k}^{(n)}|+\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}|

(7.6)

and

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n+m}-\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}-w_{k}^{(n)}|+\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}|.

(7.7)

By the interpolation theorem of Mitjagin
[
21
]
, it follows from inequalities (
7.6
) and (
7.7
) for all
p\in[1,\infty]
that

\|\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n+m}-\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}-w_{k}^{(n)}|+\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}|.

(7.8)

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrarily small. According to hypothesis (
7.5
), there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n,m>N
, the right-hand-side of (
7.8
) is bounded by
\epsilon
, which ensures that the sequence
\tilde{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is Cauchy in spaces
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
, for
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

It remains to prove (ii). According to the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem, we observe for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
that

\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{p}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n)}|.

Invoking hypothesis (
7.5
) in the right-hand-side of the above estimate, we conclude for all
p\in[1,+\infty]
that for
0<\epsilon_{0}<1
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
L\leq N
such that for all
n>N

L\|{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{p}\leq L\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n)}|<\epsilon_{0}<1,

which implies (ii).

\Box

Lemma
7.1
enables us to derive the next theorem.

Theorem 7.2

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
,
\tau_{n}=\tau
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\tau
being a positive integer, and
\mathbb{D}
is a bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
. If the sequences
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges and
w_{i}^{(n)}
satisfies the condition
(
7.5
), then the CNNs
{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge uniformly on
\mathbb{D}
to a function in
C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
, for
p\in[1,+\infty]
if
\sigma(0)=0
and
p=+\infty
if
\sigma(0)\neq 0
.

Proof:

The hypothesis of this theorem together with Lemma
7.1
ensures that the assumption of
Theorem
6.7
is satisfied. Hence, the result of this theorem follows directly from Theorem
6.7
.

\Box

The exponential convergence of the CNNs that we present next follows directly from the second part of Theorem
6.7
.

Theorem 7.3

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
,
\tau_{n}=\tau
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\tau
being a positive integer, and
\mathbb{D}
is a bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
. If the sequences
{\bf{b}}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges exponentially and
w_{i}^{(n)}
satisfies the condition

w_{i}^{(n)}={\cal O}\left(r^{n}\right),\ \ \mbox{for some }\ \ 0<r<1\mbox{ and for all}\ \ i=0,1,\dots,\tau,

(7.9)

then the neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge uniformly and exponentially on
\mathbb{D}
to a function in
C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
, for
p\in[1,+\infty]
if
\sigma(0)=0
and
p=+\infty
if
\sigma(0)\neq 0
.

Proof:

Condition (
7.9
) implies that hypothesis (
7.5
) is satisfied, which in turn ensures that Lemma
7.1
holds true. Moreover, it implies that the sequence
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges exponentially. The result of this theorem follows directly from the second part of Theorem
6.7
.

\Box

Hypothesis (
7.5
) on the filter mask may be relaxed if a different extension of matrices
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, is adopted.
We next present a relaxation on the hypothesis (
7.5
) of the filter mask.
To this end, instead of using the zero-padding adopted previously for matrix extension, we adopt the constant padding along each of the diagonal and sub-diagonals.
Specifically, we extend
{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, to
\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\in\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})

by

(\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n})_{ij}:=\begin{cases}w^{(n)}_{i-j},&0\leq i-j\leq\tau_{n}\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(7.10)

Note that the matrix
\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}
is a semi-infinite Toeplitz matrix whose diagonal and the first to the
\tau_{n}
-th sub-diagonals are the components of
{\bf{w}}^{(n)}:=[w^{(n)}_{0},w^{(n)}_{1},\dots,w^{(n)}_{\tau_{n}}]^{\top}
.

We define
\bar{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
by
(
6.4
) with
\tilde{{\bf{W}}}_{n}
being replaced by
\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}
and
\bar{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
is an extension of
{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
, different from
\tilde{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
. Then,
\bar{{\cal{N}}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy the recursions (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

Definition 7.4

We say that a sequence of vector-valued functions
{\cal{N}}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges uniformly in
\mathbb{D}
to a function
{\cal{N}}:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
if the functions
\bar{{\cal{N}}}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges uniformly to
{\cal{N}}
in the space
C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
.

We next study the uniform convergence of
{\cal{N}}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We first transfer a condition on the masks to the weight matrices.

Lemma 7.5

If a sequence of masks
[w^{(n)}_{0},w^{(n)}_{1},\dots,w^{(n)}_{k}]\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies the conditions that for each
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
, the sequence
w_{k}^{(n)}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges in
\mathbb{R}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}L\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w^{(n)}_{k}|<1,

(7.11)

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}
exists and

\lim_{n\to\infty}L\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}<1.

(7.12)

Proof:

First, we prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}
exists. Since for each
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
, the sequence
w_{k}^{(n)}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges in
\mathbb{R}
, it is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}
. We next show that the sequence
\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is Cauchy in
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})
.
To this end, we suppose that
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
are arbitrary. A direct computation leads to

\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n+m}-\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{N})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}-w_{k}^{(n)}|

(7.13)

and

\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n+m}-\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}-w_{k}^{(n)}|.

(7.14)

Again, by the interpolation theorem of Mitjagin
[
21
]
, it follows from equations (
7.13
) and (
7.14
) for all
p\in[1,\infty]
that

\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n+m}-\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n+m)}-w_{k}^{(n)}|.

(7.15)

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrarily small. Since for each
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
, the sequence
w^{(n)}_{k}
converges, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n,m>N
, the right-hand-side of (
7.15
) is bounded by
\epsilon
, which ensures that
\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is Cauchy. Moreover, since for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}

\left|\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}-\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n+m}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}\right|\leq\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}-\bar{\bf{W}}_{n+m}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})},

the sequence
\|\bar{\bf{W}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is Cauchy in
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, this sequence has a limit in
\mathbb{R}
.

Likewise, by direct computation, we find that

\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n)}|

(7.16)

and

\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n)}|.

(7.17)

Again, by the interpolation theorem of Mitjagin, it follows from equations (
7.16
) and (
7.17
) for all
p\in[1,\infty]
that

\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\tau}|w_{k}^{(n)}|.

(7.18)

Convergence of the sequence
\|\bar{{\bf{W}}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N}^{2})}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, together with inequality (
7.18
) and
hypothesis (
7.11
) leads to (
7.12
).

\Box

We next establish the uniform boundedness of
\bar{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 7.6

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
and
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
is bounded. If there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\|\tilde{\bf{b}}_{n}\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq c
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the sequence of masks
[w^{(n)}_{0},w^{(n)}_{1},\dots,w^{(n)}_{k}]\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies condition that for each
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
, the sequence
w_{k}^{(n)}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges in
\mathbb{R}
and (
7.11
), then there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

\|\bar{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq c_{1}c_{2}+Lcc_{2}+c_{2}\max\{\|\sigma({\bf 0}_{\infty})\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}:j\in\mathbb{N}_{n}\},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},\ {\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}.

(7.19)

Moreover, there exists a constant
\rho>0
such that

\|\bar{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\rho
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}
, for
p\in[1,+\infty]
if
\sigma(0)=0
and for
p=+\infty
if
\sigma(0)\neq 0
.

Proof:

By Lemma
7.5
, we have that inequality (
7.12
) holds true. This together with Lemma
3.7
with
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
being the identity operator ensures that estimate (
7.19
) is satisfied. It follows from (
7.19
) that
\|\bar{\cal{N}}_{n}({\bf{x}})\|_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}
is bounded for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{D}
, for
p\in[1,+\infty]
if
\sigma(0)=0
and for
p=+\infty
if
\sigma(0)\neq 0
.

\Box

With the above preparation, we are ready to present our second uniform convergence theorem for CNNs.

Theorem 7.7

Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant
L
and
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
is an arbitrary bounded set. If the sequence
{\bf{b}}_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, converges, and for each
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
,
\{w^{(n)}_{k}\}
is convergent and satisfies hypothesis (
7.11
)
then the neural networks
{\cal{N}}_{n}
converge uniformly to a function in
C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
if
\sigma(0)=0
and for
p=+\infty
if
\sigma(0)\neq 0
.

Moreover, if the sequences
{\bf{b}}_{n}
, and
w^{(n)}_{k}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
, converge exponentially, then the uniform convergence of
{\cal{N}}_{n}
to a function in
C_{\ell_{p}(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{D})
is exponential, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
if
\sigma(0)=0
and for
p=+\infty
if
\sigma(0)\neq 0
.

Proof:

Since for all
k=0,1,\dots,\tau
, the sequence
w^{(n)}_{k}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfies hypothesis (
7.11
), we see that Lemmas

7.5
and
7.6
hold true.
This theorem follows from Theorems
3.4
and
3.6
with
{\cal{P}}_{\mu}
being the identity operator.

\Box

Theorem
7.7
may be further extended to CNNs with pooling and we leave this to the interested reader.

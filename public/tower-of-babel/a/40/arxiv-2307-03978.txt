Separable MV-algebras and lattice-groups Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary: 06D35. Secondary: 06F20, 18B50, 12F10
Vincenzo Marra, Matías Menni

Abstract
. General theory determines the notion of separable MV-algebra (equivalently, of separable unital lattice-ordered Abelian group). We establish the following structure theorem: An MV-algebra is separable if, and only if, it is a finite product of algebras of rational numbers—i.e., of subalgebras of the MV-algebra [ 0 , 1 ] ∩ ℚ [0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q} . Beyond its intrinsic algebraic interest, this research is motivated by the long-term programme of developing the algebraic geometry of the opposite of the category of MV-algebras, in analogy with the classical case of commutative K K -algebras over a field K K .



1.
Introduction

For any field
K
, a (commutative)
K
-algebra is separable if, and only if, it is a finite product of finite separable field extensions of
K
. See, for example,
[
13
, Corollary 4.5.8]
. The aim of the present paper is to establish the analogue of this fact for MV-algebras and lattice-groups. We show as our main result that an MV-algebra is separable exactly when it is a finite product of algebras of rational numbers—the subalgebras of
[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}
(Theorem
10.1
). By a well-known theorem of Mundici
[
28
]
, the category of MV-algebras is equivalent to the category of lattice-ordered Abelian groups with a unit. We frame our treatment in the language of MV-algebras, and postpone to the final Appendix
A
a synopsis of its translation to lattice-groups.

While the main result of this paper holds independent algebraic interest, it finds its deeper motivation in a broader mathematical landscape on which we offer some comments in this introduction.

As explained in
[
25
]
, some of Grothendieck’s algebro-geometric constructions may be abstracted to the context of extensive categories
[

,
6
]

A category
\mathcal{C}
with finite coproducts is
extensive
if the canonical functor

{\mathcal{C}/X\times\mathcal{C}/Y\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/(X+Y)}

is an equivalence for every pair of objects
X
,

\mathcal{C}

Extensivity attempts to make explicit a most basic property of (finite) coproducts in categories ‘of spaces’. For instance, the category
\sf Top
of topological spaces and continuous functions between them is extensive; the category of groups is not.

Extensive experience indeed confirms that conceiving an extensive category as a category ‘of spaces’ is a useful conceptual guide. Essential to the development of Algebraic Geometry is the fact that
\mathsf{Ring}^{\mathrm{op}}
, the opposite of the category of (commutative unital) rings, is extensive.
(It easily follows that, for any ring
R
, the opposite of the category
{R/\mathsf{Ring}}
of
R
-algebras is extensive.)
Extensivity naturally determines a notion of
complemented
subobject.
So, in an extensive category with finite products, it is also natural to consider the objects with complemented diagonal. These are traditionally called
decidable objects
, and it is useful to think of them as the ‘discrete spaces’ inside the category ‘of spaces’ where they live. For instance, a topological space is decidable if, and only if, it is discrete. For any ring
R
, and any
R

A
, let
{\Spec A}
be the corresponding object in the extensive category
{(R/\mathsf{Ring})^{\mathrm{op}}}
. Then

is separable as an
R

R

Let us say that a category is
coextensive
if its opposite is extensive. In light of the above comments, an object in a coextensive category
\mathcal{A}
is called

if the corresponding object in
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
is decidable.

The category
{\sf MV}
of MV-algebras is coextensive.
This provides the notion of separable MV-algebra that is the topic of the present paper. Explicitly, the MV-algebra

such that the span

The geometry of

The characterisation of decidable objects in

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Sections

Definition 2.3

3.
Finite-coproduct preserving functors

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{S}
be extensive categories, and let
{L\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}}
preserve finite coproducts. Such a functor preserves complemented monomorphisms so, for any

in
\mathcal{C}
,

induces a function
{\mathrm{B}X\rightarrow\mathrm{B}(LX)}
which is actually a map in
{{\sf BA}}

[

We will say that

is

if and only if

has the corresponding property for every

\mathcal{C}

Lemma 3.1
.

The functor
{L\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}}
is injective on complemented subobjects if and only if it reflects
0
. In this case,

also reflects connected objects.

Proof.

Assume first that
L
is injective on complemented subobjects and let
X
in
\mathcal{C}
be such that
{LX=0}
.
Then
{\mathrm{B}(LX)}
is the terminal Boolean algebra and, as
{\mathrm{B}X\rightarrow\mathrm{B}(LX)}
is injective by hypothesis,
{\mathrm{B}X}
is also trivial.
For the converse notice that if
L
reflects
0
then the map
{\mathrm{B}X\rightarrow\mathrm{B}(LX)}
in
{\sf BA}
has trivial kernel for every
X
in
\mathcal{C}
.

To prove the second part of the statement assume that
X
in
\mathcal{C}
is such that
{LX}
is connected in
\mathcal{S}
.
If
X
were initial then so would
{LX}
because
L
preserves finite coproducts and, in particular, the initial object. So
X
is not initial.
Now assume that
{U\rightarrow X\leftarrow V}
is a coproduct diagram.
Then so is
{LU\rightarrow LX\leftarrow LV}

{LX}
is connected, either
{LU}
or
{LV}
is initial by Lemma

As
L
reflects
0
, either

∎

We will be particularly interested in extensive categories wherein every object is a finite coproduct of connected objects.

If

Let

An object

For any object

(1)

For a lextensive category

we have considered several conditions.

(1)

(2)

(3)

6.

The aim of this section is to characterize subterminal objects in
{{\sf MV}^{\mathrm{op}}}

Perhaps unexpectedly, the following fact will play an important rôle.

Lemma 6.1

Monomorphisms in
{\sf MV}

Proof.

It is well known

[

[

[

Spectra of MV-algebras

In this section we recall the material about spectra of MV-algebras that is needed in the sequel.

Recall that an ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of an MV-algebra
A
is
prime
if it is proper, and the quotient
A/\mathfrak{p}
is totally ordered. The (
prime
)
spectrum
of an MV-algebra
A
is

\Spec{A}\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{p}\subseteq A\mid\mathfrak{p}\text{ is a prime ideal of }A\}

topologised into the

of
A
, as follows. For a subset

, define

\displaystyle\V{(S)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec{A}\mid S\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\},

\displaystyle\Su{(S)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Spec{A}\setminus\V{(S)}=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec{A}\mid S\not\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}.

The set
\V{(S)}
is called the
vanishing locus
, or
zero set
, of
S
, while
\Su{(S)}
is called its
support
. If
a\in A
, write
\V{(a)}
as a shorthand for
\V{(\{a\})}
, and similarly for
\Su{(a)}
. Then the collection

\displaystyle\{\V{(I)}\mid I\text{ is an ideal of }A\}

is the set of closed sets for a topology on
\Spec{A}
that makes the latter a spectral space in the sense of Hochster
[
19
]
. The collection

\displaystyle\{\Su{(a)}\mid a\in A\}

is a basis of compact open sets for this topology; see
[
3
, Chapitre 10]
and
[
30
, Chapter 4]
. The topology is variously known as the
Stone
,
Zariski
, or

topology of
A
.

The assignment
{A\mapsto\Spec{A}}
extends to a functor
{{\sf MV}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\sf Top}

\Spec
as the category of spectral spaces and spectral maps, for our purposes in this paper it is expedient to regard
\Spec
as taking values in
\sf Top
.

The

of an MV-algebra

is

\Max{A}\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{m}\subseteq A\mid\mathfrak{m}\text{ is a maximal ideal of }A\}.

We have

, or equivalently, any simple MV-algebra is totally ordered (see e.g.
[

The

of

equipped with the subspace topology it inherits from

. Then
\Max{A}
is a compact Hausdorff space
[

The standard example of MV-algebra, the interval

, the subset

\displaystyle\C(X)\coloneqq\{f\colon X\to[0,1]\mid f\,\text{ is continuous}\}

is a subalgebra of the MV-algebra

. We shall describe a natural MV-homomorphism

, for each MV-algebra

. Its existence descends from Hölder’s Theorem (Lemma

We thereby arrive at the announced homomorphism

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\C(\Max{A})

The first assertion is proved in

9.
The Pierce functor preserves coproducts

The category
{\sf BA}
of Boolean algebras may be identified with the domain of the full subcategory

determined by the MV-algebras whose operation

We write

because of the close analogy with the theory developed in

Lemma 9.1
.

The functor

is right adjoint to the functor
\I
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the fact that
\PP{A}
is the largest Boolean subalgebra of

∎

The proof of Proposition

—in particular, Lemma

—makes it clear that

is essentially the ‘complemented subobjects’ functor

determined by the extensive category
{{\sf MV}^{\mathrm{op}}}

We now embark on the proof of the central fact that

preserves coproducts. Our aim is to reduce the problem to a situation where we can apply the topological results in Section

Lemma 9.2
.

For any MV-algebra
A
and any element
a\in A
,
a
is Boolean if, and only if, for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
, we have
a/\mathfrak{p}\in\{0,1\}\subseteq A/\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

Let
C
be any totally ordered MV-algebra. For
x\in C
, either
x\leqslant\neg x
or
\neg x\leqslant x
. If the former holds then
x\wedge\neg x=x
, so that if
x
is Boolean then
x=0
. If the latter holds then
x\vee\neg x=x
, and thus
x=1
if
x
is Boolean. In summary, if
x\in C
is Boolean then
x\in\{0,1\}
. The converse implication is clear. Summing up, the Boolean elements of
C
are precisely
0
and
1
.

Boolean elements, being definable by equational conditions, are preserved by homomorphisms. Hence if
a
is Boolean then
a/\mathfrak{p}\in A/\mathfrak{p}
is Boolean, and therefore, since
A/\mathfrak{p}
is totally ordered,
a/\mathfrak{p}\in\{0,1\}
by the argument in the preceding paragraph. This proves the left-to-right implication in the statement of the lemma.
For the converse implication, we recall that in any MV-algebra
A
we have
\bigcap\Spec{A}=\{0\}

[
8
, 1.3.3]
. Hence, the function
\iota\colon A\longrightarrow\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec{A}}A/\mathfrak{p}
defined by
a\in A\longmapsto(a/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec}\in\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec{A}}A/\mathfrak{p}
is an injective homomorphism. Assume that for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec{A}
we have
a/\mathfrak{p}\in\{0,1\}
. Since operations in
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec{A}}A/\mathfrak{p}
are computed pointwise, we infer
\iota(a)\vee\neg\iota(a)=(a/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec}\vee\neg(a/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Spec}=1
, and therefore, since
\iota
is an isomorphism onto its range,
a\vee\neg a=1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 9.3

Let

Proof.

By

, there is exactly one ideal

of

.
∎

We record a corollary that will have further use in the paper. It is the exact analogue for MV-algebras of a standard result for the category

[

. In order to state it, let us write

for the Boolean algebra of clopen sets of any topological space

allows us to define, for any MV-algebra

Corollary 9.4
.

For any MV-algebra

that sends

By Lemma

for each

is a homomorphism. Let us consider

[

The

Let

Lemma 10.2

For any MV-algebra

(ii)

\eqref{i:rat2}\Rightarrow\eqref{i:rat3}

\eqref{i:rat3}\Rightarrow\eqref{i:rat2}

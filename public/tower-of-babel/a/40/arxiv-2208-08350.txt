1.
Introduction

In the paper we use the standard arrow notation and write
G\to(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
if any coloring of the edges of a graph

G
with
r
colors leads to a copy of
H_{i}
in the
i
th
color for some
i=1,2,\dots,r
. Let us recall that the Ramsey number
r(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
is the minimum number of vertices
in graph
G
such that
G\to(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
, while the size Ramsey number
\hat{r}(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
is the minimum number of edges in a graph
G
with such a property. In general Ramsey numbers are hard to find, yet it have been computed, or estimated, for some simple families of graphs. In particular,
r(H_{1},H_{2})
is known for
H_{1},H_{2}
which are either paths or cycles (see Gerencsér, Gyárfás
[
10
]
, Rosta
[
18
]
, Faudree, Schelp
[
7
]
, or Radziszowski
[
17
]
for a survey of results on Ramsey Theory). The size Ramsey number
for paths and cycles are not so well understood. In the fundamental paper on this subject
Beck
[
3
]
proved that
\hat{r}(P_{n},P_{n})=O(n)
.
The exact value of the hidden constant is not known and finding it seems to be hard. The best estimates we have got so far are those by Bal, DeBiasio
[
2
]
, and Dudek, Prałat
[
6
]

(3.75-o(1))n\leq\hat{r}(P_{n},P_{n})\leq 74n\,.

The size Ramsey number for cycles was studied by Haxell, Kohayakawa, and Łuczak
[
11
]
who proved that

\hat{r}(C_{n},C_{n})\leq An

for some huge constant
A
. The value of the constant
A
has been substantially improved by Javadi, Khoeini, Omidi, and Pokrovskiy
[
12
]
, and then by Javadi and Miralaei
[
13
]
;
both of the above papers also considered the case of more than one color.
However, finding the exact value of the limit
\hat{r}(C_{n},C_{k})/n
(or even prove that it exists) seems to be out of reach right now.

In this paper we study yet another version of Ramsey number. By the restricted size Ramsey number
r^{*}(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
we denote the minimum number of edges in a graph
G
on
r(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
vertices such that
G\to(H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{r})
. Although this notion has been around for quite some time (see Faudree and Schelp
[
7
]
) there are rather few results on the restricted Ramsey number in the literature.
The goal of this note is to find the exact value of

r^{*}(C_{n},C_{k})
where
k
is odd and
n
is large. Note that for such choice of
k
and
n
,
r(C_{n},C_{k})=2n-1
and one of the colorings used to show the lower bound is when we divide
K_{2n-2}
into two equal parts and color all edges between them in the second color.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
n_{0}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
and any odd
k
, with

3\leq k\leq n
, we have

r^{*}(C_{n},C_{k})=\lceil(n+1)(2n-1)/2\rceil\,.

The structure of the paper is the following. In the next section we derive Theorem
1.1
from two Lemmata

and
2.2
on colorings of a pseudorandom graph

. A precise definition of

, and the way one can construct it, is given in Section

, while the proofs of Lemmata

and
2.2
can be found in Sections

and
5
respectively.

We conclude this part of the paper with some remarks and comments.
Note that calculating

, in a way, supplements results related to Shelp’s problem who asked about the minimum
a
so that every graph
G
on
N=r(C_{n},C_{n})
vertices and the minimum degree

has the property

(see Benevides
et al.

[
4
]
for the solution of Shelp’s problem, and Łuczak and Rahimi
[
19
,
20
]
for the case of three colors).

Finding

can be also viewed as a special case of the following question: given a graph

on
n
vertices, and a function

, what is the minimum number of edges in a graph

on

vertices such that

? Although in this formulation this problem looks slightly artificial, a moment of reflection reveals that it could be (and usually is) related to the following question on random graphs: what is the largest

so that with probability

each coloring of the edges of the random graph

when

, whereas for cycles and all range of

[

.

Finally, one can ask about the value of

when

is an even number. This question seems to be particularly interesting when
k
is much smaller than

. For instance, in the simplest case when

, we have

and so one can expect that
r^{*}(C_{n},C_{4})=n^{1/2+o(1)}
, but at this moment the lower and upper bounds for

we can prove are both far from the conjectured

.

using two Lemmata

a large induced bipartite subgraph in one of the colors. Our result, proved in Section

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists

such that for every

the following holds. If

is odd and the edges of an
n
-fit graph
\tilde{G}_{n}
are colored with two colors so that there are no copies of

in the first color and no copies of

in the second
color, then there exists two disjoint subsets of vertices of
\tilde{G}_{n}
,

and
V^{\prime\prime}
,
|V^{\prime}|,|V^{\prime\prime}|\geq 0.99n
, such that
all edges between
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
are of the same color.

Then we supplement the above with the following result proved in Section
5
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists
n_{2}
such that for every
n\geq n_{2}
the following holds.
Let us suppose that the edges of an
n
-fit graph
\tilde{G}_{n}
are colored with two colors so that there exists two disjoint subsets of vertices
\tilde{G}_{n}
,
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
,
|V^{\prime}|,|V^{\prime\prime}|\geq 0.99n
, such that all edges between
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
are of the same color.
Then the subgraph induced by one of the colors contains a copy of cycle

Now we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Clearly, for

we must have

since otherwise Lemmata

2.2

Hence

Moreover, each graph

such
that

and

be a set of

Now color all edges between

∎

Degree of h h -polynomials of edge ideals
Jennifer Biermann, Selvi Kara, Augustine O’Keefe, Joseph Skelton, Gabriel Sosa Castillo

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the degree of h h -polynomials of edge ideals of finite simple graphs. In particular, we provide combinatorial formulas for the degree of the h h -polynomial for various fundamental classes of graphs such as paths, cycles, and bipartite graphs. To the best of our knowledge, this marks the first investigation into the combinatorial interpretation of this algebraic invariant. Additionally, we characterize all connected graphs in which the sum of the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and the degree of the h h -polynomial of an edge ideal reaches its maximum value, which is the number of vertices in the graph.



1.
Introduction

Edge ideals of graphs provide a fertile ground for uncovering combinatorial expressions for algebraic invariants of monomial ideals, thereby making abstract algebraic concepts more accessible. There has thus been extensive research into expressing or bounding fundamental algebraic invariants—such as the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and projective dimension —in terms of graph invariants (see, for example,
[
2
;
4
;
12
;
16
]
). In this paper, we continue this trend by investigating the degree of the
h
-polynomial of edge ideals, a fundamental yet less explored algebraic invariant.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple graph with vertex set
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The edge ideal of
G
, denoted by
I(G)
, is defined as the monomial ideal

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G))\subseteq R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

The
h
-polynomial of
R/I(G)
, denoted by
h_{R/I(G)}(t)
, is related to the Hilbert series. It is well-known that Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
can be expressed as a rational function (see
[
14
, Theorem 13.2]
) and the numerator of this (reduced) rational function is called the
h
-polynomial of
R/I(G)
.

There has been recent interest in studying the degree of
h_{R/I(G)}(t)
, denoted by
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)
, in relation to fundamental invariants of
R/I(G)
such as the dimension, projective dimension, depth, and regularity. The recent research has focused on determining the possible values of these invariants for particular classes of graphs
[
9
;
11
]
and the existence of a graph
G
for given values of these invariants
[
7
]
. Additionally, researchers have studied the relationships between the degree of the
h
-polynomial and these invariants
[
8
]
. Moreover, similar studies have been undertaken for monomial ideals in
[
6
]
and toric ideals of graphs in
[
3
]
.

In this paper, we take a different approach by solely focusing on computing the degree of the
h
-polynomial for edge ideals. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first in-depth study of this invariant. Our primary goal here is to provide formulas for
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)
based on the combinatorial data of
G
for various graph classes. In particular, we are interested in expressing
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)
in terms of the independence number of a graph.

Remark 1.1
.

The relationship between the degree of the Hilbert polynomial and combinatorial invariants of the graph arises from the following expression for the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
:

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha(G)}\frac{f_{i-1}t^{i}}{(1-t)^{i}}

where
H_{R/I(G)}(t)
denotes the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
,
f_{i-1}
denotes the number of independent sets of cardinality
i
in
G
(by convention
f_{-1}=1
), and
\alpha(G)
represents the size of the largest independent set in
G
(see
[
5
, Theorem 6.2.1]
). It follows from this expression that

\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)\leq\alpha(G).

In light of this upper bound on the degree, one of our objectives is to identify classes of graphs for which
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
.

In this paper, we provide explicit formulas for the degree of the
h
-polynomial for paths, cycles and complete bipartite graphs in terms of the independence number. In addition, we provide a classification of connected bipartite graphs where
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
. We now list the main results of this paper.

Theorem
4.5
.

Let
C_{n}
denote the cycle on
n
-vertices and
P_{n}
denote the path on
n
-vertices.

(1)

The degree of the
h
-polynomial of
R/I(C_{n})
for
n\geq 3
is

\deg h_{R/I(C_{n})}(t)=\alpha(C_{n})

where
\displaystyle\alpha(C_{n})=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
.

(2)

The degree of the
h
-polynomial of
R/I(P_{n})
for

is

\deg h_{R/I(P_{n})}(t)=\begin{cases}\alpha(P_{n})&:n\equiv 0,2\pmod{3},\\
\alpha(P_{n})-1&:n\equiv 1\pmod{3}\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle\alpha(P_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
.

Our result on bipartite graphs is presented based on notation introduced in Section
5
. To make the statement of our result more accessible, we briefly introduce some necessary notation here, and refer the reader to Section
5
for details.

Theorem
5.9
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph with bipartition
(U,V)
such that
|U|\geq|V|
. Let
V_{W}
denote the collection of whiskered vertices (i.e. vertices adjacent to a leaf) in

and
\overline{V_{W}}
denote the complement of

in

Similarly, let

(1)

A nice consequence of

.

The other focus of this paper is to study the relationship between the degree of the
h
-polynomial and
\reg R/I(G)

. Our interest stems from the following inequality
[

\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)\leq|V(G)|.

In particular, we are interested in determining when this bound is sharp. One of the main results of this paper, stated below, characterizes all connected finite simple graphs for which the equality is achieved.

Theorem

.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then

\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=|V(G)|

if and only if

The structure of the paper is as follows: We collect the necessary background on graph theory and algebra in

.

Remark 1.2
.

The term degree is an overloaded one within mathematics. Throughout this paper, we may refer to the degree of the

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect fundamental definitions and results that are relevant to this paper. A reader who is familiar with this background material can skip this section. We start with the algebraic concepts and then introduce the related notions from graph theory.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Hilbert function
of the graded ring
R/I(G)
,
HF_{R/I(G)}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},
is defined by

HF_{R/I(G)}(i)=\dim_{k}(R/I(G))_{i}

where
\dim_{k}(R/I(G))_{i}
is the
k
-vector space dimension of the
i^{\text{th}}
graded piece of
R/I(G)
.

The
Hilbert series
of
R/I(G)
, denoted by
H_{R/I(G)}(t)
, is defined to be

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}HF_{R/I(G)}(i)t^{i}.

Via the following well-known theorem we see that we can compute the Hilbert function of
R/I(G)
by counting
standard monomials
, i.e. those monomials not in
\init_{<}I(G)=I(G)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

15
, Proposition 1.1

Let
I
be an ideal in the polynomial ring
R=k[\mathbf{x}]
. The standard monomials of
I
form a
k
-vector space basis for
R/I.

Definition 2.3
.

A graded minimal free resolution of
R/I(G)
is an exact sequence of the form

0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}R(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}R(-j)^{\beta_{p-1,j}}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}R(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}}\longrightarrow R/I(G)\longrightarrow 0.

The exponents
\beta_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}(R/I(G))
are invariants of
R/I(G)
called the graded Betti numbers of
R/I(G)
. The Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity, denoted by
\reg(R/I(G))
, is defined in terms of the non-zero graded Betti numbers of
R/I(G)
:

\reg(R/I(G))=\max\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}(R/I(G))\neq 0\}.

One can bound the regularity of
R/I(G)
in terms of combinatorial invariants of
G
. In what follows, we recall such graph invariants.

Definition 2.4
.

A
matching
of
G
is a collection of pairwise disjoint edges of
G
. The
matching number
of
G
is the maximum size of a matching of
G
and it is denoted by
\mu(G)
.

A matching
\mathcal{M}
is called an
induced matching
if the set of edges of the induced graph on the vertices of
\mathcal{M}
is equal to
\mathcal{M}
. The
induced matching number
of
G
is the maximum size of an induced matching of
G
and it is denoted by
\nu(G)
.

It is immediate that
\nu(G)\leq\mu(G)
. Graphs for which
\nu(G)=\mu(G)
are called
Cameron-Walker graphs
.

Remark 2.5
.

From
12
, Lemma 2.2
and
4
, Theorem 6.7
one has

\nu(G)\leq\reg(R/I(G))\leq\mu(G)

for any finite simple graph
G
.

A characterization of graphs where
\reg(R/I(G))=\mu(G)
is given in
[
16
]
and we recall its statement below.

Theorem 2.6
.

16
, Theorem 11

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
\reg R/I(G)=\mu(G)
if and only if
G
is a Cameron-Walker graph or
G
is a 5-cycle.

The main graph invariant that is relevant to our discussion of the degree of the
h
-polynomial is the independence number. We therefore recall the notion of independent sets and independence number of a graph here.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. A collection of vertices
\mathcal{C}\subseteq V(G)
is called an
independent set
of
G
if
\{x,y\}\notin E(G)
for any
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
. The
independence number
of
G
is the maximum size of an independent set and it is denoted by
\alpha(G)
. When
G
is understood we will simply denote
\alpha(G)
by
\alpha
.

The following result which provides an upper bound for the sum of the matching number and independence number of a graph follows from one of the Gallai identities (see for example
[
13
, Lemma 1.0.1]
).

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then
\alpha(G)+\mu(G)\leq n
.

The final graph theory concepts we recall are the neighbors of a vertex and the notion of leaves.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph and
u,v\in V(G)
. If
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, we say
u
and
v
are
neighbors
in
G
. The set of all neighbors of a vertex
u
, denoted
N(u),
is called its
neighborhood
, ie.
N(u)=\{v\in V(G)\,:\,\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
.

A vertex with only one neighbor in
G
is called a
leaf vertex
, or merely
leaf
. A neighbor of a leaf is said to be
whiskered
.

Lastly, we present the following combinatorial identity which will be useful in
Section

3
for determining when
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
. Its proof is provided for completeness.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
k
and
d
be non-negative integers such that
d\geq 1
. Then

(1)

\binom{d-1}{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{d+k-i}{k-i}\binom{k+1}{i}.

Proof.

We count the positive integer solutions to the equation

(2)

\hskip 14.22636ptx_{1}+x_{2}+\dots+x_{k+1}=d

in two ways.
On one hand, it is straightforward to see that there are
\binom{d-1}{k}
many positive integer solutions to Equation
2
. On the other hand, one can also count the non-negative integer solutions and remove those which contain zeros using an inclusion/exclusion argument. First note that there are
\binom{d+k}{k}\binom{k+1}{0}
many non-negative integer solutions to
Equation

2
. For
0<i\leq k
, a naive way to count the number of non-negative solutions to
Equation

2
with at least
i
of the variables being
0
would be to first choose
i
of the variables to be zero and then the number of non-negative integer solutions to the equation in the remaining
k+1-i
variables is
\binom{d+k-i}{k-i}
. This results in the following expression

\binom{d+k-i}{k-i}\binom{k+1}{i}

which over-counts those solutions that have more than
i
variables valued zero. Applying the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion to deal with the overlap leads to the alternating sum on the right side of
Equation

1
.∎

3.
Maximum Degree via Independent Sets

In this section, we analyze when the degree of the
h
-polynomial of
R/I(G)
achieves its maximum value
\alpha(G)
, the independence number of
G
. We adopt the following notation for the remainder of this paper.

Notation 3.1
.

We use the notation
g_{j}(G)=g_{j}
to denote the number of independent sets of size
j
in a graph
G
for
1\leq j\leq\alpha(G)
. Equivalently,

g_{j}=g_{j}(G)=|\{\mathcal{C}\subseteq V(G):\mathcal{C}\text{ is an independent set of }G\text{ and }|\mathcal{C}|=j\}|

Let
g(G)=g
denote the alternating sum of
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{\alpha(G)}
where

g=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha(G)}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
if and only if
g\neq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha=\alpha(G)
. First, observe that the support of a standard monomial of
R/I(G)
coincides with an independent set of
G
. In particular, for an independent set
\mathcal{C}
of size
j
, there are
\displaystyle\binom{d-1}{j-1}
many standard monomials of degree
d
with support
\mathcal{C}
. Thus, the Hilbert series of
R/I(G)
can be expressed as

(3)

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=1+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\text{HF}_{R/I(G)}(d)t^{d}=1+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}g_{j}\binom{d-1}{j-1}\right)t^{d}.

Claim:
The Hilbert series can be re-written as follows

(4)

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=\frac{\left(1-\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}\right)(1-t)^{\alpha}+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}(1-t)^{\alpha-s-1}}{(1-t)^{\alpha}}

where

(5)

D_{s}=\sum_{j=s+1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1-s}g_{j}\binom{j}{j-1-s},~~0\leq s\leq\alpha-1.

Observe that the constant term of the numerator in
Equation

4
as a polynomial in
(1-t)
is nonzero, namely
D_{\alpha-1}=g_{\alpha}\neq 0
. Therefore, if the claim is true,
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha
if and only if
1-\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}\neq 0.
We can then rewrite
\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}
as follows by first reversing the order of summation and then applying a consequence of the Binomial Theorem, namely that
\sum_{s=0}^{j}(-1)^{s}\binom{j}{s}=0
.

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}\left[\sum_{j=s+1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1-s}g_{j}\binom{j}{j-1-s}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}g_{j}\left[\sum_{s=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{s}\binom{j}{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}=g.

We then have that
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
whenever
g\neq 1
, thus proving the lemma.

Proof of Claim:
To see that (
4
) follows from from (
3
) we use Lemma
2.10
to break the binomial factor
\binom{d-1}{j-1}
into a summation to obtain

H_{R/I(G)}(t)=1+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{i}g_{j}\binom{d+j-1-i}{j-1-i}\binom{j}{i}\right)t^{d}\right).

We then substitute
s=j-1-i
to get

\displaystyle H_{R/I(G)}(t)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{j-1-s}g_{j}\binom{d+s}{s}\binom{j}{j-1-s}\right)t^{d}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{j-1}\left((-1)^{j-1-s}g_{j}\binom{j}{j-1-s}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\binom{d+s}{s}t^{d}\right)\right).

Reversing the order of the summations in
j
and
s
yields

\displaystyle H_{R/I(G)}(t)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}\left(\sum_{j=s+1}^{\alpha}\left((-1)^{j-1-s}g_{j}\binom{j}{j-1-s}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\binom{d+s}{s}t^{d}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}\left(\left(\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\binom{d+s}{s}t^{d}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{j=s+1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1-s}g_{j}\binom{j}{j-1-s}\right)\right).

We are then able to substitute
D_{s}
as described above to obtain

\displaystyle H_{R/I(G)}(t)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}\left[\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\binom{d+s}{s}t^{d}\right]

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}\frac{D_{s}}{(1-t)^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1-\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}\right)(1-t)^{\alpha}+\sum_{s=0}^{\alpha-1}D_{s}(1-t)^{\alpha-s-1}}{(1-t)^{\alpha}}

where the second to last equality is due to the following

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\binom{d+s}{s}t^{d}=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{s+1}}

which is the Hilbert series of the polynomial ring
k[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}]
.
∎

4.
Degrees of Paths and Cycles

In this section, we provide formulas for the degree of the
h
-polynomial for edge ideals of paths and cycles.

Let
P_{n}
and
C_{n}
denote the path and cycle on
n
vertices, respectively. Label the vertices of
P_{n}
and
C_{n}
as
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
such that
\{v_{j},v_{j+1}\}
is an edge of
P_{n}
and
C_{n}
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
, and
\{v_{1},v_{n}\}
is an edge of
C_{n}
. Recall from Notation
3.1
that
g_{i}
denotes the number of independent sets in size
i
for a given graph
G
. We begin this section by presenting closed formulas for
g_{i}
where
G
is
P_{n}
or
C_{n}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p_{n,i}
denote the number of independent sets of size
i
for the path
P_{n}
. Then

\displaystyle p_{n,i}=\begin{cases}\binom{n-i+1}{i}&\text{if }n\geq 2i-1,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on
n
with
0\leq i\leq n
. The statement is clear when
n=1
and
n=2
, or when
i=0
.

An independent set of size
i>0
for
P_{n}
with
n\geq 3
either contains the leaf
v_{n}
or does not. Hence the number of independent sets containing
v_{n}
is
p_{n-2,i-1}
while the number of independent sets that do not is
p_{n-1,i}
. Thus we have the recurrence relation
p_{n,i}=p_{n-1,i}+p_{n-2,i-1}
, and there are now three cases to consider based on the relation of
n
with
2i-1
.

If
n\geq 2i
, then

p_{n,i}=\binom{n-i}{i}+\binom{n-i}{i-1}=\binom{n-i+1}{i}.

If
n=2i-1
, then

p_{n,i}=0+\binom{i-1}{i-1}=\binom{i}{i}=\binom{n-i+1}{i}.

Finally, if
n<2i-1
, we have
\displaystyle p_{n,i}=0+0=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
c_{n,i}
denote the number of independent sets of size
i
for the cycle
C_{n}
for
n\geq 3
. Then

\displaystyle c_{n,i}=\begin{cases}\frac{n}{i}\binom{n-i-1}{i-1}&\text{if }n\geq 2i,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
c_{n,i}=0
for
n<2i
so we assume
n\geq 2i
. It is immediate that
c_{n,1}=n
for
n\geq 3
. Suppose
i\geq 2
. For a vertex
v_{j}
on the cycle, consider an independent set
\mathcal{C}
of
C_{n}
such that
v_{j}\in\mathcal{C}
and
|\mathcal{C}|=i
. The set
\mathcal{C}\setminus\{v_{j}\}
is an independent set of a path on
(n-3)
-vertices (because
v_{j-1}
and
v_{j+1}
(modulo
n
) are not in
\mathcal{C}
).

So, the number of independent sets of size
i
of
C_{n}
containing the vertex
v_{j}
is equal to
p_{n-3,i-1}
. Since there are
n
vertices in
C_{n}
we multiply by
n
to get
np_{n-3,i-1}
. This counts each independent set of size
i
in
C_{n}
a total of
i
times, so
ic_{n,i}=np_{n-3,i-1}
. Thus, we have

c_{n,i}=\frac{n}{i}(p_{n-3,i-1})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{n}{i}\binom{n-i-1}{i-1}&\text{if }n\geq 2i,\\
0&\text{otherwise }\end{cases}

by
Lemma

4.1
.
∎

It is known that
\alpha(P_{n})=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
and
\alpha(C_{n})=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor
. Additionally, these values can be derived as corollaries of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
.

As discussed in
Section

3
, one can deduce the degrees of the
h
-polynomials of
R/I(P_{n})
and
R/I(C_{n})
from the alternating sums of

and

. In the next lemma, we provide the values of these sums using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
. For ease of notation, let

p_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}(-1)^{j-1}p_{n,j}\text{ and }c_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}(-1)^{j-1}c_{n,j}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle p_{n}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }n\equiv 2,3\pmod{6},\\
1&\text{if }n\equiv 1,4\pmod{6},\\
0&\text{if }n\equiv 0,5\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

For
n\geq 3
,

\displaystyle c_{n}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }n\equiv 0\pmod{6},\\
0&\text{if }n\equiv 1,5\pmod{6},\\
2&\text{if }n\equiv 2,4\pmod{6},\\
3&\text{if }n\equiv 3\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

Proof.

In order to find the values of
p_{n}
and
c_{n}
, we first show that
p_{k}=p_{k+6}
for
k\geq 1
and
c_{\ell}=c_{\ell+6}
for
\ell\geq 3
. Then, the statement follows by computing the values of
p_{n}
and
c_{n}
for small
n
.

As the first step, consider the successive differences of
p_{2m+2},p_{2m+1}
and
p_{2m}
for
m\geq 1
. In particular, using
Lemma

4.1
and the following binomial identity

\displaystyle\binom{n+1}{i+1}-\binom{n}{i+1}=\binom{n}{i},

we get

\displaystyle p_{2m+1}-p_{2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\frac{2m+1}{2}\rceil}(-1)^{j-1}p_{2m+1,j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\frac{2m}{2}\rceil}(-1)^{j-1}p_{2m,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}(-1)^{j-1}\binom{2m+1-j+1}{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}\binom{2m-j+1}{j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}\binom{2m+1-j}{j-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{2m-j}{j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}+(-1)^{0}\binom{2m}{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{2m-j}{j}-(-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=1-p_{2m-1}.

Similarly, one can verify that
p_{2m+2}-p_{2m+1}=1-p_{2m}
.

These identities imply that regardless of parity
p_{k+2}-p_{k+1}=1-p_{k}\text{ for }k\geq 1.
Then, by adding
p_{k+3}-p_{k+2}=1-p_{k+1}
and
p_{k+2}-p_{k+1}=1-p_{k}
, we get

(6)

p_{k}+p_{k+3}=2\text{ for }k\geq 1

which in turn implies

p_{k}=p_{k+6}\text{ for }k\geq 1.

Thus to find all values of
p_{n}
it suffices to compute
p_{1},p_{2}
and
p_{3}
. Using
Lemma

4.1
, we get
p_{1}=1
and
p_{2}=p_{3}=2
. Hence, we obtain the value of
p_{n}
for each
n
as given in the statement of the lemma.

For
c_{n}
, we use
Lemma

4.2
and the same arguments used for
p_{n}
.
As for
p_{n}
, one can verify that
c_{\ell+2}-c_{\ell+1}=1-c_{\ell}
for
\ell\geq 3
. In addition,
c_{\ell}+c_{\ell+3}=2
and
c_{\ell}=c_{\ell+6}
for
\ell\geq 3
. Thus, one can conclude the values of
c_{n}
for each
n
by only evaluating
c_{n}
for
n=3,4
and
5
. A quick computation shows that
c_{3}=3,c_{4}=2,c_{5}=0
. This completes the proof for
c_{n}
.
∎

We are now ready to present the formulas for the degrees of the

-polynomials of paths and cycles. However, before we proceed, we introduce the following auxiliary lemma, which will be useful in providing the explicit formula for
\deg h_{R/I(P_{n})}(t)

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
D_{0}
be defined for

as in

5
from the proof of

3.2
. As the value of

depends on

, refer to the sum as

, i.e.

T_{n}=D_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil}(-1)^{j-1}jp_{n,j}

where

is as defined in
Lemma

4.1
.
Then we have

(7)

T_{n}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k}(k+1)&\text{if }n=3k+1\text{ or }n=3k+3,\\
(-1)^{k}2(k+1)&\text{if }n=3k+2.\end{cases}

Proof.

We begin by rewriting the summation defining
T_{n}
using
Lemma

4.1

T_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil}(-1)^{j-1}j\binom{n-j+1}{j}.

Using a similar strategy as in the proof of
Lemma

4.3
, we consider successive differences of
T_{2m+2},T_{2m+1}
and
T_{2m}
for
m\geq 1
to obtain the recurrence relation

T_{n+2}-T_{n+1}+T_{n}=1-p_{n},\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Adding the corresponding expressions of the above recursion with first terms equal to

,
T_{n+5}
,

and

one obtains

T_{n}+2T_{n+3}+T_{n+6}=4-p_{n+4}-p_{n+3}-p_{n+1}-p_{n}.

Then applying
Equation

6
gives

T_{n}+2T_{n+3}+T_{n+6}=0.

We now proceed by induction on

to prove

. We only provide details for the

Since

. Suppose

It then follows from Equation (

∎

5.
Degrees of Bipartite Graphs

In this section, we study the degrees of the
h
-polynomials of edge ideals of bipartite graphs. In particular, we determine which conditions guarantee the degree of the
h
-polynomial of a bipartite graph is equal to its independence number.

For the remainder of this section, let
G
denote a connected bipartite graph with a bipartition
(U,V)
such that

.

As noted in

3.2
, the degree of the

-polynomial of

in

and

according to the following notation, which will be used throughout this section.

Notation 5.1
.

We use the notation

in

\overline{V_{W}}

We use the notation

so that

. The complement of
U_{L}
in

is denoted by
\overline{U_{L}}
. Unlike

, there are multiple possible choices for the set

. However, the following combinatorial results do not depend on this choice.

.

Consider the following bipartite graph in

Building on

5.1
, our goal of counting the independent sets in

\overline{U_{L}}
are included in an independent set. For instance, the number of independent sets of size

is

\sum_{i=0}^{j}2^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}.

In what follows, we provide a similar formula for any subset of
\overline{U_{L}}
and use it to express

in terms of these subsets. We start with the following notation, which is used for the remainder of this section.

Notation 5.3
.

Let

denote a subset of
\overline{U_{L}}
for the remainder of this section.

(1)

We use the notation

The notation

Remark 5.5
.

Since independent sets in

g_{j}=\displaystyle\sum_{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}}g_{j}(S).

(a)

With the above expression of

(9)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1}\left(\sum_{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}}g_{j}(S)\right)=\sum_{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(S)\right)=\sum_{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}}g(S)

where
\displaystyle g(S):=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(S)
.

(b)

When
S\neq\emptyset
, we can express
g(S)
as follows:

g(S)=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(S)=\sum_{j=|S|}^{\alpha}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(S)=(-1)^{|{S}|}\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha-|S|}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j+|S|}(S)

such that

g_{j+|S|}(S)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}2^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\binom{|W^{\prime}_{S}|+|U_{L}|-|W_{S}|}{j-i}.

The last expression for
g_{j+|S|}(S)
represents how many ways one can construct an independent set of size

whose intersection with
\overline{U_{L}}
is
S
by choosing the remaining
j
vertices. In particular, we can first choose
i
vertices from either
|W_{S}|
or
N(W_{S})\cap U_{L}
and then choose
j-i
vertices from the remaining vertices in
G
that are not adjacent to
S
and not in
\overline{U_{L}}
.

(c)

As previously discussed, when
S=\emptyset
, we have

\displaystyle g(\emptyset)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(\emptyset)\text{ such that }g_{j}(\emptyset)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}2^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}.

Equation (
9
) suggests that value of
g
can be analyzed by studying
g(S)
for
S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}
. Thus we investigate
g(S)
by considering which vertices of
V
are whiskered and which are not. In particular, we examine the cases
\overline{V_{W}}=\emptyset
and
\overline{V_{W}}\neq\emptyset
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
\overline{V_{W}}=\emptyset
, i.e.
V_{W}=V
. Then
g(S)=0
when
S\neq\emptyset
; otherwise,

g(\emptyset)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $|V|$ is even},\\
2&\text{if $|V|$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Under the assumption of this lemma, we have
m=|V_{W}|=|U_{L}|
. In addition,
W^{\prime}_{S}=\emptyset
for any
S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}
. Also note that in this case
\alpha(G)=n
.

Suppose
S\neq\emptyset
. Observe that
W_{S}\subsetneq V_{W}
since
N(S)\neq\emptyset
as
G
is a connected bipartite graph and
\overline{V_{W}}
is assumed to be empty. Next, by
Remark

5.5
part (b) and reversing the order of the double sum, we obtain the following set of equalities:

\displaystyle g(S)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|{S}|}\sum_{j=0}^{n-|S|}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j+|S|}(S)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|{S}|+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-|S|}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-1)^{j}2^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\binom{m-|W_{S}|}{j-i}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|{S}|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-|S|}\left(\sum_{j=i}^{n-|S|}(-1)^{j}2^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\binom{m-|W_{S}|}{j-i}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|{S}|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-|S|}(-2)^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\left(\sum_{j=i}^{n-|S|}(-1)^{j-i}\binom{m-|W_{S}|}{j-i}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|{S}|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{|W_{S}|}(-2)^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m-|W_{S}|}(-1)^{k}\binom{m-|W_{S}|}{k}\right)

where the last equality follows from substituting
k=j-i
in the inner sum and the following facts: (1)
n-|S|\geq n-\left|\overline{U_{L}}\right|=|U_{L}|=|V_{W}|=|V|=m
, (2)
n-|S|-i\geq n-|S|-|W_{S}|\geq m-|W_{S}|
, for all
0\leq i\leq|W_{S}|
, and (3)
|V_{W}|=|V|>|W_{S}|
.

By the Binomial Theorem and the fact that
W_{S}\subsetneq V_{W}
, we conclude that
g(S)=0
when
S\neq\emptyset
as follows:

g(S)=(-1)^{|{S}|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{|W_{S}|}(-2)^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\underbrace{\left((1-1)^{m-|W_{S}|}\right)}_{0}=0.

Suppose
S=\emptyset
. Recall from
Remark

5.5
part (b) that

g(\emptyset)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(\emptyset)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j}2^{i}\binom{m}{i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}2^{j}\binom{m}{j}.

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\displaystyle\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}=0
unless

as
|\overline{V_{W}}|=0.
Then, by the Binomial Theorem, we obtain the desired equality as follows:

g(\emptyset)=1-\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-2)^{j}\binom{m}{j}=1-(1-2)^{m}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $m=|V|$ is even},\\
2&\text{ if $m=|V|$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose
\overline{V_{W}}\neq\emptyset
. Then,
g(\emptyset)=1
and for
S\neq\emptyset

g(S)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|S|+|V_{W}|+1}&\text{if }N(S)=\overline{V_{W}},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall from
Remark

5.5
part (b) that

g(\emptyset)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}(\emptyset)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{j-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{j}2^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}\right).

By separating out the
i=0
term and then re-indexing the first summation, we can express
g(\emptyset)
as follows:

(10)

g(\emptyset)=1-\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{j}(-1)^{j}2^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}\right).

Note that the bounds of the second term of (
10
) can be restricted since
0<|\overline{V_{W}}|<m
. Thus, we can apply the Binomial Theorem to obtain

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{|V_{W}|}(-1)^{j}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j}=(1-1)^{|\overline{V_{W}}|}=0.

In addition, by reversing the order of the sum in the third term of
Equation

10
and substituting
k=j-i
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{j=i}^{m}(-1)^{j}2^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-2)^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\left(\sum_{j=i}^{m}(-1)^{j-i}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{j-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-2)^{i}\binom{|U_{L}|}{i}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m-i}(-1)^{k}\binom{|\overline{V_{W}}|}{k}\right).

Since
|U_{L}|=|V_{W}|<m
and
|\overline{V_{W}}|=m-|U_{L}|
, we can restrict the bounds of the above sum and, by applying the Binomial Theorem, conclude that the last term of
Equation

10
is zero. Therefore,
g(\emptyset)=1
.

For
S\neq\emptyset
, let
p=|W_{S}^{\prime}|+|U_{L}|-|W_{S}|
. Then, using
Remark

5.5
part (b) and following the same steps from the proof of

5.6
, we can express

g(S)=(-1)^{|S|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{\alpha-|S|}(-2)^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha-|S|-i}(-1)^{k}\binom{p}{k}\right).

Note that disjoint union
W_{S}\sqcup S
is an independent set in
G
, as is
W_{S}^{\prime}\sqcup U_{L}\sqcup S
. Therefore
|W_{S}|\leq\alpha-|S|
and
|W_{S}^{\prime}|+|U_{L}|\leq\alpha-|S|
. The latter implies that
p\leq\alpha-|S|-i
whenever

. Thus, by restricting the bounds of the sums, we get

g(S)=(-1)^{|S|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{|W_{S}|}(-2)^{i}\binom{|W_{S}|}{i}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^{k}\binom{p}{k}\right).

It is immediate that the inner sum is equal to

when

. Otherwise, it is equal to zero by the Binomial Theorem. Thus,
g(S)=0
when

.

Finally, observe that
p=0
if and only if
|W_{S}^{\prime}|=0
and

since

and
|U_{L}|=|V_{W}|
. This means

if and only if

In this case, we have

g(S)=(-1)^{|S|+1}\sum_{i=0}^{|V_{W}|}(-2)^{i}\binom{|V_{W}|}{i}=(-1)^{|S|+|V_{W}|+1}.

Therefore, we obtain the desired equality.
∎

Before the main result of this section, we introduce notation to simplify the statement of our result.

Notation 5.8
.

Let
X
denote the collection of non-empty subsets of
\overline{U_{L}}
such that the neighborhood of each element in this collection is
\overline{V_{W}}
, i.e.,

X=\{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}:S\neq\emptyset\text{ and }N(S)=\overline{V_{W}}\}.

In addition, let
q
denote the difference between the even cardinality elements and odd cardinality elements in
X
, i.e.

q=|\{S\in X:|S|\text{ is even}\}|-|\{T\in X:|T|\text{ is odd}\}|.

Now, we present the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph on vertex set
U\cup V
.

(1)

If every vertex in
V
is whiskered so that
\overline{V_{W}}=\emptyset
, then
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
.

(2)

If there are unwhiskered vertices in
V
,
\overline{V_{W}}\neq\emptyset
, then
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
if and only if
q\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose
\overline{V_{W}}=\emptyset
. Then by Equation (
9
) and Lemma
5.6
we see that

g=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}g_{j}=\sum_{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}}g(S)=\begin{cases}0&:|V|\text{ is even},\\
2&:|V|\text{ is odd}\end{cases}

so that
g\neq 1
.
Thus,
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
by
Theorem

3.2
which proves part (a).

Now suppose
\overline{V_{W}}\neq\emptyset
. Recall from
Lemma

5.7
that
g(S)=(-1)^{|S|+|V_{W}|+1}
whenever
S\in X
and it is zero otherwise. So, using Equation (
9
) and
Lemma

5.7
, we have

g=\sum_{S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}}g(S)=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}\\
S\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}g(S)=1-(-1)^{|V_{W}|}\sum_{S\in X}(-1)^{|S|}=1-(-1)^{|V_{W}|}q.

Then, by
Theorem

3.2
,
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
if and only if
q\neq 0
, proving part (b).
∎

Observe that
q=-1
when
G
is a complete bipartite graph
K_{n,m}
with
n\geq m>1
because
N(S)=\overline{V_{W}}=V
for any
S\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}=U
and
X\cup\{\emptyset\}=\mathcal{P}(U)
. Additionally, for a complete bipartite graph, the number of even and odd cardinality subsets of
U
(including the empty set) are equal. In the following corollary, we present a more general version of this observation.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph.

(1)

If every vertex in
V
is whiskered, then
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)=n
.

(2)

If there are vertices in
V
that are not whiskered and
X\cup\{\emptyset\}=\mathcal{P}(T)
for some non-empty subset
T\subseteq\overline{U_{L}}
, then
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=\alpha(G)
. In particular, if
G
is a complete bipartite graph, then
\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=n
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then

\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=|V(G)|

if and only if
G
is a Cameron-Walker graph with no pendant triangles.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices. We investigate
\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)
by considering whether
G
is a Cameron-Walker graph or not and repeatedly applying
Theorem

6.3
.

Assume
G
is a Cameron-Walker graph with notation as in
Notation

6.2
. If
G
has no pendant triangles then

\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)=r+|L_{U}|+d=|V(G)|.

If
G
has pendant triangles, i.e.

, then we have

\displaystyle\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)

\displaystyle=(r+|T|)+(|L_{U}|+|T|+(d-k))

\displaystyle<r+d+|L_{U}|+2|T|

\displaystyle=|V(G)|.

Now assume
G
is not a Cameron-Walker graph. If

and

, by

\reg R/I(G)+\deg h_{R/I(G)}(t)<5=|V(G)|.

If

from

1.1

Abstract
. In this paper, we give the Assouad dimension formula and the upper bound of the lower dimension for homogeneous Moran sets under the condition sup k ≥ 1 { n k } < + ∞ \sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty . We also give the Assouad spectrum and the lower spectrum formulas for Cantor-like sets.

1.
Introduction

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space, for any set
F\subset(X,d)
, define the Assouad dimension of
F
(denoted by
\dim_{A}F
) as

(1.1)

\begin{split}\dim_{A}F=\inf\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ &\text{there exist}\ b,\ c>0\ \text{such that for any}\ \ 0<r<R<b,\ x\in F,\\
&N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap F\big)\leq c\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}\bigg\},\end{split}

where
N_{r}(E)
denotes the smallest number of the balls with radius
r
which can cover the set
E\subset X
.
The definition of the Assouad dimension was first introduced by Assouad
[
1
]

[
2
]
and used for the study of the embedding
theories in the metric space. Unlike the Hausdorff, box and packing dimensions, the Assouad dimension mainly describes the local strcture of the sets(the ”density” of the most ”dense” part of the sets), it plays effective roles on the study of fractal geometry, more details for the properties and the applications of the Assouad dimension can be found in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

For any subset
F
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have
\dim_{H}F\leq\dim_{P}F\leq\dim_{A}F
, and we also have
\overline{\operatorname{dim}}_{B}F\leq\dim_{A}F
for any bounded set
F
, see in
[
3
]
. Furthermore, by
[
6
]
, we have for any Ahlfors
-
David
s-
regular set
F
, the Assouad dimension of
F
is
s
, which is coincided with the Hausdorff dimension and the packing dimension of
F
. Calculate the Assouad dimensions of the sets which are not Ahlfors
-
David regular is always a difficult work. Fraser
[
4
]
and Mackay
[
7

discussed the Assouad dimensions for some quasi
-
self
-

-

As a dual to the Assouad dimension, Larman
[
13
]
introduced the lower dimension. Let
(X,d)
be a metric space, for any set
F\subset(X,d)
, define the lower dimension of
F
(denoted by
\dim_{L}F
) as

(1.2)

\begin{split}\dim_{L}F=\sup\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ &\text{there exist}\ b,\ c>0\ \text{such that for any}\ \ 0<r<R<b,\ x\in F,\\
&N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap F\big)\geq c\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Similar to the Assouad dimension, the lower dimension mainly describes the local strcture of the sets(the ”sparsity” of the most ”sparse” part of the sets), it also plays effective roles on the study of fractal geometry. But unlike the Assouad dimension, the lower dimension does not have the monotone property and the finitely stable property, more details for the properties and the applications of the lower dimension can be found in
[
3
]
.

By
[
13
]
, we have
\dim_{L}F\leq\underline{\dim}_{B}F\leq\overline{\dim}_{B}F\leq\dim_{A}F
for any totally bounded set
F
. In general, since the lower dimension does not have the monotone property and the finitely stable property, it is alway difficult to do the study of the lower dimensions for fractal sets. Chen, Wu, Wei
[
14
]
discussed the lower dimensions for some Moran sets. Chen
[

]
, Yang, Li and Hu
[
15
]
obtained some results of the lower dimension formulas for some Moran cut-out sets and some homogeneous Moran sets. In this paper, we study the lower dimensions for the homogeneous Moran sets which satisfy
\sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty
, and obtain an upper bound of the lower dimension.

By the definitions above, the Assouad dimension and the lower dimension are infimum and supremum depending on the two independent scales
r
and
R
, but there is no information that how the scales approach the values. To see how the infimum and supremum depend on the scales, Fraser and Han
[
16
]
restricted the relationship
between

and
R

\frac{\log R}{\log r}=\theta(0<\theta<1)
and gived the definitions of the Assouad spectrum and the lower spectrum. Let
(X,d)
be a metric space, for any set
F\subset(X,d)
and any
0<\theta<1
, define the Assouad spectrum of
F
(denoted by
\dim_{A}^{\theta}F
) as

(1.3)

\begin{split}\dim_{A}^{\theta}F=\inf\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ &\text{there exist}\ b,\ c>0\ \text{such that for any}\ \ 0<R<b,\ x\in F,\\
&N_{R^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}\big(B(x,R)\cap F\big)\leq c\big(\frac{R}{R^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}\big)^{s}\bigg\},\end{split}

and define the lower spectrum of
F
(denoted by
\dim_{L}^{\theta}F
) as

(1.4)

\begin{split}\dim_{L}^{\theta}F=\sup\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ &\text{there exist}\ b,\ c>0\ \text{such that for any}\ \ 0<R<b,\ x\in F,\\
&N\big(B(x,R)\cap F,R^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\big)\geq c\big(\frac{R}{R^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}\big)^{s}\bigg\}.\end{split}

If
F
is a totally bounded set, it is easy to see that
\dim_{A}^{\theta}F
and
\dim_{L}^{\theta}F
are continuous functions for any
0<\theta<1
, and we have

\overline{\dim}_{B}F\leq\dim_{A}^{\theta}F\leq\min\bigg\{\frac{\overline{\dim}_{B}F}{1-\theta},\dim_{A}F\bigg\}

and

\dim_{L}F\leq\dim_{L}^{\theta}F\leq\underline{\dim}_{B}F.

More details for the properties and the applications of the Assouad spectrum and the lower spectrum can be found in
[

2.
Preliminaries

We recall the definition of the homogeneous Moran sets.
Let the sequences
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}

with
n_{k}\geq 2
,
n_{k}c_{k}<1
for any
k\geq 1
. Set
\Omega_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
,
\Omega_{k}=\{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{k}:1\leq\sigma_{j}\leq n_{j},1\leq j\leq k\}
for any
k\geq 1
, and define
\Omega=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\Omega_{k}
. For any
k\geq 1
,
m\geq 1
, if
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{k}\in\Omega_{k}
,
\tau=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\cdots\tau_{m}(1\leq\tau_{j}\leq n_{k+j},1\leq j\leq m)
, then write
\sigma\ast\tau=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{k}\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{m}\in\Omega_{k+m}
.

Definition 1
.

(Homogeneous Moran sets
[
18
]
)

Let
I\neq\emptyset
be a closed interval in
\mathbb{R}
, we say the collection of the closed intervals
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{\sigma}\subset I:\sigma\in\Omega\}
fulfills the homogeneous Moran structure if it satisfies:

(i)

I_{\emptyset}=I
;

(ii)

for any
k\geq 1
and
\sigma\in\Omega_{k-1}
,
I_{\sigma\ast 1\text{,}}

I_{\sigma\ast 2\text{,}}\cdots
,
I_{\sigma\ast n_{k}}
are closed intervals with
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{k}}I_{\sigma\ast i}\subset I_{\sigma}
and
\text{int}(I_{\sigma\ast i_{1}})\cap\text{int}(I_{\sigma\ast i_{2}})=\emptyset
for any
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n_{k}
;
where
\text{int}(A)
denotes the interiors of the set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
;

(iii)

for any
k\geq 1
,
\sigma\in\Omega_{k-1}
, and
1\leq i\leq n_{k}
,

\frac{|I_{\sigma\ast i}|}{|I_{\sigma}|}=c_{k},

where
|A|
denotes the diameter of the set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
.

If the collection
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Omega\}
fulfills the homogeneous Moran structure, then we call

(2.1)

E=E(\mathcal{F}):=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Omega_{k}}I_{\sigma}

a homogeneous Moran set defined by
\mathcal{F}
. For any
k\geq 1
, if
\sigma\in\Omega_{k}
, then
I_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{F}
is called a
k-
level basic interval of
E
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\})
the class of all homogeneous Moran sets which are associated with
I,\{n_{k}\}
and
\{c_{k}\}
.

Remark 1
.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
I=[0,1]
.

Let

N_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i},\ \ \ \ \delta_{0}=1,\ \ \ \ \delta_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}

for any
k\geq 1
, then
N_{k}
is the total number of the
k-
level basic intervals of
E
and
\delta_{k}
is the length of any
k-
level basic interval of
E
.

Remark 2
.

Any uniform Cantor set(see in
[
10
]
) is a homogeneous Moran set, any homogeneous perfect set(see in
[
11
]
) is a homogeneous Moran set, any parameterized homogeneous Moran set(see in
[

]
) is a homogeneous Moran set .

More details for the homogeneous Moran sets can be found in

Definition 2
.

(Cantor-like sets
[
8
]
)

Let the sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}<+\infty
. Let
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the sequences
with
n_{k}\geq 2
,
0<c_{k}<1
and
c_{\ast}=\inf_{k}\{c_{k}\}>0
for any
k\geq 1
. Set
\Omega_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
,
\Omega_{k}=\{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{k}:1\leq\sigma_{j}\leq n_{j},1\leq j\leq k\}
for any
k\geq 1
, and define
\Omega=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\Omega_{k}
. For any
k\geq 1
,
m\geq 1
, if
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\cdots\sigma_{k}\in\Omega_{k}
,
\tau=\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\cdots\tau_{m}(1\leq\tau_{j}\leq n_{k+j},1\leq j\leq m)
, then write
\sigma\ast\tau=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{k}\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{m}\in\Omega_{k+m}
.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 1)
be a closed set with
\text{int}(J)\neq\emptyset
. For any
k\geq 1
and
\sigma\in\Omega_{k-1}
, if

J_{\sigma*1},J_{\sigma*2},\cdots,J_{\sigma*n_{k}}\subset J_{\sigma}

are geometrically similar to
J_{\sigma}
with

c_{k}(1-a_{k})\leq\frac{|J_{\sigma*j}|}{|J_{\sigma}|}\leq c_{k}(1+a_{k})

for any
1\leq j\leq n_{k}
, and
\text{int}(J_{\sigma\ast i_{1}})\cap\text{int}(J_{\sigma\ast i_{2}})=\emptyset
for any
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n_{k}
, then

(2.2)

E=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Omega_{k}}J_{\sigma}

is called a Cantor-like set. Denote by
\mathcal{C}(J,\{c_{k}\},\{n_{k}\},\{a_{k}\})
the class of all Cantor-like sets associated with
J,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\}
and
\{a_{k}\}
.

For any
k\geq 1
, we also write

N_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}n_{i},\ \ \ \ \delta_{0}=1,\ \ \ \ \delta_{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}.

Remark 3
.

If
E\in\mathcal{M}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\})
is a homogeneous Moran set with
c_{\ast}=\inf_{k}\{c_{k}\}>0
, then
E
is a Cantor-like set with

and
a_{k}=0
for all
k\geq 1
. Cantor-like set may not be Moran set(see in
[
18
]

3.
Main results

We state our main results as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{M}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\})
, if
\ \sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty
, then

\dim_{A}E=\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}.

Remark 4
.

Any homogeneous Cantor set
E\in\mathcal{C}(\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\})
, any homogeneous perfect set
F\in\mathcal{J}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\},\{\eta_{k,j}\})
and any parameterized homogeneous Moran set
G\in\mathcal{M}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\},\{\varepsilon_{k}(t)\})
are homogeneous Moran sets, where
E,F,G\in\mathcal{M}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\})
. If
c_{\ast}=\inf_{k}\{c_{k}\}>0
, then
\ \sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty
. Thus Theorem
1
generalizes Theorem 2.1 of
[
10
]
and Theorem 1 of
[
12
]
under the condition
\ \sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty
, it also generalizes Theorem 1(2) of
[
11
]
and Corollary 1 of
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{M}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\})
, if
\ \sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty
, then

\dim_{L}E\leq\liminf\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\inf\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{C}(J,\{c_{k}\},\{n_{k}\},\{a_{k}\})
, then

\dim_{A}^{\theta}E=\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{l(\theta,k)}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log\delta_{k}},

where
l(\theta,k)=\max\{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:\delta_{l}\geq(\delta_{k})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\}
for any
k\geq 1
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{C}(J,\{c_{k}\},\{n_{k}\},\{a_{k}\})
, then

\dim_{L}^{\theta}E=\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{l(\theta,k)}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log\delta_{k}},

where
l(\theta,k)=\max\{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:\delta_{l}\geq(\delta_{k})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\}
for any
k\geq 1
.

Remark 5
.

Any homogeneous perfect set
E\in\mathcal{J}(I,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\},\{\eta_{k,j}\})
with
c_{\ast}=\inf_{k}\{c_{k}\}>0
is a Cantor-like set, where
E\in\mathcal{C}(I,\{c_{k}\},\{n_{k}\},\{a_{k}\})
with
a_{k}=0
for all
k\geq 1
. Thus Theorem
3
generalizes Theorem 2 of
[
11
]
and Theorem
4
generalizes Theorem 2 of
[
15
]
.

To prove Theorem
1
and Theorem

, we need the equivalent definitions of the Assouad dimension and the lower dimension as follows, see in

Lemma 1
.

(equivalent definitions
[
9
]
)

Let
F
be a compact set in a metric space
(X,d)
, then

(4.1)

\begin{split}\dim_{A}F=\inf\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ &\text{there exist}\ b,\ c>0\ \text{such that for any}\ \ 0<r<b,~~~~\text{any}\ \ r<R<|F|,\\
&\text{and any}\ x\in F,~~~~~N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap F\big)\leq c\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}\bigg\},\end{split}

(4.2)

\begin{split}\dim_{L}F=\sup\bigg\{s\geq 0:\ &\text{there exist}\ b,\ c>0\ \text{such that for any}\ \ 0<r<b,\text{any}\ \ r<R<|F|,\\
&\text{and any}\ x\in F,~~~~~N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap F\big)\geq c\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}\bigg\}.\end{split}

We start to study the Assouad dimensions and the lower dimensions of homogeneous Moran sets under the condition
\sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1

To prove Theorem
1
. First, we proof

\dim_{A}E\geq\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}.

If
s>\dim_{A}E
, by (
4.1
), there exist constants
b,C>0
, such that for any
0<r<b
, any
r<R<|E|
, and any
x\in E
,

N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap E\big)\leq C\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}.

Then there exist
L_{1}>0
and
C_{1}>0
, such that for any
k\geq 1
,
l\geq L_{1}
,
R=\delta_{k}
and
r=\delta_{k+l}
,

(4.3)

N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap E\big)\leq C_{1}\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}=C_{1}(c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l})^{-s}

for any
x\in E
.

Notice that for any
x\in E
,
B(x,R)
contains at least one
(k+1)-
level basic interval of
E
, and
B(x,r)
meets at most four
(k+l)-
level basic intervals of
E
. Thus

(4.4)

\begin{split}N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap E\big)&\geq\frac{n_{k+2}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l}}{4}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4M}(n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l})\end{split}

for any
x\in E
.

Combine (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we have

(4.5)

\frac{1}{4M}(n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l})\leq C_{1}(c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l})^{-s}

for any
k\geq 1
and

, which implies that there is a constant

, such that

for any
k\geq 1
and
l\geq L_{1}

Let

\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}\leq s.

By the arbitrariness of

, we have

\dim_{A}E\geq\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}.

Now we proof

\dim_{A}E\leq\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}.

For any

, there is a positive integer
L_{2}
, such that for any
l\geq L_{2}
and
k\geq 1
, we have

s>\frac{\log{n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l}}}{-\log{c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l}}},

which implies that

(4.6)

n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l}\leq(c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l})^{-s}.

For any
0<R<\delta_{1}
, there exists a positive integer
k\geq 2
, such that

(4.7)

\delta_{k}<R\leq\delta_{k-1}.

For any
0<r<\delta_{k+L_{2}}
, there exists a positive integer

, such that

(4.8)

\delta_{k+l+1}<r\leq\delta_{k+l}.

Then for any

(k-1)-

, and

) and (

for any

. By the arbitrariness of
s>\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}
, we obtain

\dim_{A}E\leq\limsup\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}

and complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
2

If
s<\dim_{L}E
, by (
4.2
), there exist constants
b,C>0
, such that for any
0<r<b
, any
r<R<|E|
, and any
x\in E
,

N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap E\big)\geq C\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}.

Then there exist
L_{3}>0
and
C_{3}>0
, such that for any
k\geq 1
,
l\geq L_{3}
,
R=\delta_{k}
and
r=\delta_{k+l}
,

(4.11)

N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap E\big)\geq C_{3}\big(\frac{R}{r}\big)^{s}=C_{3}(c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l})^{-s}

for any
x\in E
.

Notice that for any
x\in E
,
B(x,R)
meets at most four
k-
level basic intervals of
E
, and
B(x,r)
contains at least one
(k+l+1)-
level basic interval of
E
. Thus

(4.12)

\begin{split}N_{r}\big(B(x,R)\cap E\big)&\leq 4n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l+1}\\
&\leq 4M(n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l})\end{split}

for any
x\in E
.

Combine (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we have

(4.13)

4M(n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l})\geq C_{3}(c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l})^{-s}

for any
k\geq 1
and
l\geq L_{3}
, which implies that there is a constant
C_{4}
, such that

\frac{\log{n_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot n_{k+l}}}{-\log{c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l}}}\geq s+\frac{C_{4}}{\log{c_{k+1}\cdot\cdot\cdot c_{k+l}}}

for any
k\geq 1
and
l\geq L_{3}
.

Let
l\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

\liminf\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\inf\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}\geq s.

By the arbitrariness of
s<\dim_{L}E
, we have

\dim_{L}E\leq\liminf\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\inf\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{k+l}}{-\log c_{k+1}\cdots c_{k+l}}

and complete the proof of Theorem
2
.

5.
Assouad spectrum and lower spectrum of Cantor-like sets

Let
X
be a metric space and
s\geq 0
,
A
be a compact set in
X
, if for any
x\in A
and
0<r\leq|A|
, there exist
c\geq 1
and a Borel measure
\mu
supported on
A
satisfying

c^{-1}r^{s}\leq\mu\big(B(x,r)\big)\leq cr^{s},

then we call
A
an Ahlfors
s-
regular set
[
22
]
.

The Ahlfors regular sets contain many typical fractal sets, such as the self-similar and the self conformal sets which satisfy the open set condition, they play effective roles in the study of the fractal geometry. For any Ahlfors
s-
regular set
F
, the Assouad dimension of
F
is
s
, which is coincided with the Hausdorff dimension and the packing dimension of
F
, see in
[
6
]
.

Reference
[
22
]
showed that for any Ahlfor
s-
regular set
A
, there are constants
\lambda\geq 1
,
a\in(0,1)
, and
1<\alpha\leq\beta<+\infty
which satisfy that for any
x,\ x_{1},\ x_{2}\in A
and
r\in(0,|A|)
,

(5.1)

\lambda^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu\big(B(x_{1},r)\big)}{\mu\big(B(x_{2},r)\big)}\leq\lambda,

(5.2)

\alpha\leq\frac{\mu\big(B(x,r)\big)}{\mu\big(B(x,ar)\big)}\leq\beta,

which implies that
\mu
is a doubling measure on the set
A
.
For any set
E
satisfying (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), Lü, Lou, Wen and Xi
[
23
]
showed that
\dim_{H}E=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}h(r),\ \ \dim_{P}E=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}h(r)
, where
h(r):\ (0,\epsilon)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which is called the scale function of
E
, is the function such that

(5.3)

0<\inf\limits_{r<\epsilon}h(r)\leq\sup\limits_{r<\epsilon}h(r)<+\infty

for a positive constant
\epsilon
. Futhermore, for any
x\in E
and
0<r<\epsilon
, there exists a constant
c\geq 0
with

(5.4)

\big|h(r)\log r-\log\mu\big(B(x,r)\big)\big|\leq c.

For an Ahlfors
s-
regular set, we can take
h(r)=s
.

We are ready to prove of Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
. The next propositions give the Assouad spectrum and the lower spectrum by the scale function for the sets satisfying (
5.1
) and (
5.2
).

Proposition 1
.

[
11
]

Let
F
be a set in a metric space
X
, and
F
satisfies (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) with a scale function
h(r)
, then

(5.5)

\dim_{A}^{\theta}F=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-h(r^{\frac{1}{\theta}})\log r^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|.

Proposition 2
.

[
15
]

Let
F
be a set in a metric space
X
, and
F
satisfies (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) with a scale function
h(r)
, then

(5.6)

\dim_{L}^{\theta}F=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-h(r^{\frac{1}{\theta}})\log r^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|.

The next proposition shows a scale function of a Cantor-like set.

Proposition 3
.

[
8
]

Let
E\in\mathcal{C}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\},\{a_{k}\})
, then
F
satisfies (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) with
a scale function

h(r)=\frac{\log N_{k}}{-\log\delta_{k}}

for any
k\geq 1
and
\delta_{k}|J|<r\leq\delta_{k-1}|J|
.

Now we finish the proof of Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{C}(J,\{n_{k}\},\{c_{k}\},\{a_{k}\})
. For any
r\in(0,\min\{1,|J|\})
, there is
k\geq 1
satisfying

\delta_{k}|J|<r\leq\delta_{k-1}|J|.

Since
l(\theta,k)=\max\{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:\delta_{l}\geq(\delta_{k})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\}
, we have
l(\theta,k)\geq k

and

\delta_{l(\theta,k)+1}<(\delta_{k})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\delta_{l(\theta,k)}.

Let
l^{\ast}(\theta,r)
be the non-negative integer satisfying

\delta_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}<(\frac{r}{|J|})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\delta_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)}.

Notice that

\delta_{l(\theta,k)+1}<(\delta_{k})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}<(\frac{r}{|J|})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

and

(\frac{r}{|J|})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq(\delta_{k-1})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\delta_{l(\theta,(k-1))},

then we have

l(\theta,(k-1))\leq l^{\ast}(\theta,r)\leq l(\theta,k).

Let
l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)
be the non-negative integer satisfying

\delta_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}|J|<r^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\delta_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)}|J|.

Since
0<c_{\ast}<c^{\ast}=\sup_{k}\{c_{k}\}<1
and
\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}a_{k}<+\infty
, we have
\sup_{k\geq 1}\{n_{k}\}<+\infty
,
\sup_{k\geq 1}\{l(\theta,k)-l(\theta,(k-1))\}<+\infty
and
\sup_{0<r<\min\{1,|J|\}}\{|l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)-l^{\ast}(\theta,r)|\}<+\infty
, which together with Proposition
1
, Proposition
2
and Proposition
3
yields

\begin{split}\dim_{A}^{\theta}E&=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-h(r^{\frac{1}{\theta}})\log r^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|\\
&=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-\frac{\log N_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{-\log\delta_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}\log\delta_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|\\
&=\limsup\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-\frac{\log N_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{-\log\delta_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}\log\delta_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|\\
&=\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg|\frac{\frac{\log N_{k}}{-\log\delta_{k}}\log\delta_{k}-\frac{\log N_{l(\theta,k)}}{-\log\delta_{l(\theta,k)}}\log\delta_{l(\theta,k)}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log\delta_{k}}\bigg|\\
&=\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{l(\theta,k)}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log\delta_{k}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\dim_{L}^{\theta}E&=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-h(r^{\frac{1}{\theta}})\log r^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|\\
&=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-\frac{\log N_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{-\log\delta_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}\log\delta_{l^{\ast\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|\\
&=\liminf\limits_{r\rightarrow 0}\bigg|\frac{h(r)\log r-\frac{\log N_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{-\log\delta_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}\log\delta_{l^{\ast}(\theta,r)+1}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log r}\bigg|\\
&=\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg|\frac{\frac{\log N_{k}}{-\log\delta_{k}}\log\delta_{k}-\frac{\log N_{l(\theta,k)}}{-\log\delta_{l(\theta,k)}}\log\delta_{l(\theta,k)}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log\delta_{k}}\bigg|\\
&=\liminf\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\log n_{k+1}\cdots n_{l(\theta,k)}}{(1-\frac{1}{\theta})\log\delta_{k}}.\end{split}

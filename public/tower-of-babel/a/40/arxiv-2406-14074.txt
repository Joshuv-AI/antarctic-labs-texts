Strong existence and uniqueness of a calibrated local stochastic volatility model
Scander Mustapha

Abstract
. We study a two-dimensional McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation, whose volatility coefficient depends on the conditional distribution of the second component with respect to the first component. We prove the strong existence and uniqueness of the solution, establishing the well-posedness of a two-factor local stochastic volatility (LSV) model calibrated to the market prices of European call options. In the spirit of [ 10 ] , we assume that the factor driving the volatility of the log-price takes finitely many values. Additionally, the propagation of chaos of the particle system is established, giving theoretical justification for the algorithm [ 5 ] .



1.
Introduction

For the family of local stochastic volatility (LSV) models

(1.1)

\begin{cases}dS_{t}=\sigma_{\textrm{lsv}}(t,S_{t})a_{t}S_{t}dB_{t},\ t\geq 0,\\
S_{t=0}=S_{0},\\
\end{cases}

to be calibrated to the market prices of European call options
(C(t,K))_{t>0,K>0}
it is enough to take the leverage function
\sigma_{\textrm{lsv}}
as

(1.2)

\sigma_{\textrm{lsv}}(t,K)=\frac{\sigma_{\textrm{loc}}(t,K)}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[a_{t}^{2}|S_{t}=K\right]}},\ t>0,K>0,

where
(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the price process,
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a real standard Brownian motion,
S_{0}
is a positive random variable independent of
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
\sigma_{\textrm{loc}}(t,K):=\sqrt{\frac{2\partial_{t}C(t,K)}{\partial_{K}^{2}C(t,K)}},t>0,K>0

[
4
]
and

is any stochastic process. This is justified by Gyöngy’s theorem
[
7
]
that asserts that a stochastic process

1.1

1.2
) has fixed-time marginal distributions given by the marginal distributions of the local volatility model

and starting at
S_{0}
. In order to make the model tractable, one commonly assumes that
(a_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is given by
(\sqrt{f(Y_{t})})_{t\geq 0}
where
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
is a bounded smooth function and
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is another real Itô-Lévy process eventually correlated to
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Formally, the joint density
p(t,s,y)
of
(S_{t},Y_{t}),(t,s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
, solves a quasi-linear and non-local Fokker-Planck partial differential equation (PDE), whose coefficients depend upon the non-linear term

(1.3)

\frac{\int p(\cdot,\cdot,z)dz}{\int f(z)p(\cdot,\cdot,z)dz}.

While the applications are important in calibration to market implied volatility surfaces (
[
8
]

[
6

[
1
]
,
[
15
]
) the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the stochastic differential equation (SDE) and the partial differential equation (PDE) problems is still an open problem. Only partial results have been obtained in particular cases.

Abergel and Tachet showed in
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
, the existence of a classical solution to the PDE problem in a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and with additional Dirichlet boundary condition - for short time, and for
\sup|f^{\prime\prime}|
small enough. In
[
12

, the authors established the existence and uniqueness of a stationary solution to a similar SDE (a drift needs to be added to the dynamic of

to allow the possibility of a stationary measure). Jourdain and Zhou proved in
[
10

, the existence of a weak solution to the SDE. Under the assumption that
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a jump process taking finitely many values and after writing the PDE problem as a system of parabolic equations in non-divergence form, the authors are able to write a variational formulation of the problem, for which existence of a solution can be proved - by the Galerkin’s method - provided that the range of
f
is small enough. They are then able to prove the existence of a weak solution to the SDE. Uniqueness and propagation of chaos are out of reach in the approach of
[
10
]
because higher regularity of the weak solution is needed. Global regularity of weak solutions to parabolic systems is still an open problem.

\int f(z)p(\cdot,\cdot,z)dz

[

]

]

Under the condition that the norm

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a constant

.

Next, we observe that under Condition
1.1
, there exist
\Gamma\in S^{++}
and
\kappa>0
such that for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(3.8)

\langle X,\Gamma A^{\varepsilon}(\hat{p})X\rangle\geq\kappa\|X\|_{2}^{2}.

Like in the proof of Proposition
2.4
, set
\hat{p}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\Gamma}\hat{p}
,
\widetilde{p}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\Gamma}\widetilde{p}
,
A^{\prime}(\hat{p})=\sqrt{\Gamma}A^{\varepsilon}(\hat{p})\sqrt{\Gamma}^{-1}
,
a_{1}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\Gamma}a_{1}\sqrt{\Gamma}^{-1}
,
a_{2}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\Gamma}a_{2}\sqrt{\Gamma}^{-1}
and
\phi^{\prime}=\sqrt{\Gamma}\phi
. With (
2.53
), there exists
\kappa^{\prime}>0
such that for all
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

(3.9)

\langle X,A^{\prime}X\rangle\geq\kappa^{\prime}\|X\|^{2}_{2}.

Moreover

(3.10)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\int\|\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int\langle\partial_{x}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime}),A^{\prime}(\hat{p})\partial_{x}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime})\rangle dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime},a_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{x}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime})\rangle dt+\int_{0}^{t}\int\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime},a_{2}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime})\rangle dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle dt.\end{split}

Using the boundedness of
a_{1}^{\prime}
,
a_{2}^{\prime}
and standard techniques, it is easy to see that for some appropriate constant
C>0
and for all
t\in[0,T]

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\int\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime},a_{1}^{\prime}\partial_{x}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime})\rangle+\int\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime},a_{2}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime})\rangle\\
&\leq\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2}\int\|\partial_{x}(\hat{p}^{\prime}-\tilde{p}^{\prime})\|^{2}_{2}+C\int\|\hat{p}^{\prime}-\tilde{p}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{2}.\end{split}

In the view of the uniform coercivity of
A^{\prime}
, and
\phi^{\prime}
, and the estimate

(3.12)

\int\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rangle\leq C\delta_{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\int(\hat{p}_{n}+\widetilde{p}_{n})=C\delta_{M},

we conclude that there exists some constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]

(3.13)

\frac{1}{2}\int\|\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\delta_{M}+C\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\int\|\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}dt.

The result is proved by applying Gronwall’s inequality and using the obvious inequality
\|\hat{p}-\widetilde{p}\|_{2}\leq\|\hat{p}^{\prime}-\widetilde{p}^{\prime}\|_{2}
, for some constant
C>0
depending only on
\Gamma
and independent of
\delta_{M}
.
∎

Let
1\leq i\leq M
.
To simplify the notation, we will drop the subscripts and superscripts in
i
and
M
, and we denote
\delta=\delta_{M}
,
W=W_{\delta_{M}}
,
X=X^{i}
,
\widetilde{X}=\widetilde{X}^{i}
,
\hat{X}=\hat{X}^{i}
, and for
j\neq i
,
X^{j}=X^{j,M}
and
\widetilde{X}^{j}=\widetilde{X}^{j,M}
.
Denote
W_{\delta_{M}}*X_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}W_{\delta_{M}}(x-X^{j}_{t})
and
W_{\delta_{M}}^{f}*X_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}f(Y^{j})W_{\delta_{M}}(x-X^{j}_{t})
. Under this notation, the volatility coefficients of
X
,
\widetilde{X}
and
\hat{X}
are respectively given by

(3.14)

\Sigma_{t}:=f(Y)\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon+W*X_{t}(X_{t})}{\varepsilon+W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})}}\sigma(t,X_{t}),

(3.15)

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}:=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon+f(Y)\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}}\sigma(t,\widetilde{X}_{t}),

and

(3.16)

\hat{\Sigma}_{t}:=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon+f(Y)\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})}{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)\hat{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}}\sigma(t,\hat{X}_{t}).

Notice immediately that

(3.17)

\sigma_{0}^{2}\frac{\inf f}{\sup f}\leq\Sigma^{2},\widetilde{\Sigma}^{2},\hat{\Sigma}^{2}\leq\frac{\sup f}{\inf f}\sigma_{1}^{2}.

In the rest of the section, we denote, for notational simplicity, by
C
any constant depending on
N
,
\varepsilon
,
f
,
\sigma_{0}
,
\sigma_{1}
, and
|W_{1}|_{\infty}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
requires the Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
below.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
1\leq i\leq M
. Then for all
T>0
.

(3.18)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(X^{i,M}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{i,M}_{t})^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{M}\frac{\exp{(CT\delta_{M}^{-4})}}{\delta_{M}^{2}}.

Proof.

According to the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality, for any
s\in[0,T]

(3.19)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq s}(X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t})^{2}\right]&\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\langle X-\widetilde{X}\rangle_{s}\right]\\
&=C\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{E}\left[(\Sigma_{t}-\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t})^{2}\right]dt.\end{split}

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
(U^{j})_{1\leq j\leq M}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, we abbreviate
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}W(x-U^{j})
by
W*U(x)
and
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}f(Y^{j})W(x-U^{j})
by
W^{f}*U(x)
.

The boundedness
\Sigma_{t}
,
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}
,
\sigma
and
\partial_{x}\sigma
gives for all
t\in[0,T]

(3.20)

\begin{split}|\Sigma_{t}-\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}|&\leq C|\Sigma_{t}^{2}-\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}^{2}|\\
&\leq C\left|\frac{\varepsilon+W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})}{\varepsilon+W*X_{t}(X_{t})}-\frac{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}\right|\\
&+C|\sigma(t,X_{t})-\sigma(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})|\\
&\leq C\left|\frac{\varepsilon+W^{f}*X(X_{t})}{\varepsilon+W*X_{t}(X_{t})}-\frac{\varepsilon+W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})}{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}\right|\\
&+C\left|\frac{\varepsilon+W^{f}*X(X_{t})}{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}-\frac{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}{\varepsilon+\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})}\right|\\
&+C|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t}|\\
&\leq C\left|W*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|\\
&+C\left|W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|+C|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t}|.\end{split}

Let
t\in[0,T]
. After estimating the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\left|W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]
, we will apply the estimate with
f=1
to treat the term
\mathbb{E}\left[\left|W*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]
.

Upper-bound

(3.21)

\begin{split}\left|W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*p_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|&\leq(\textrm{I})+(\textrm{II})+(\textrm{III}),\end{split}

where each term
(\textrm{I}),(\textrm{II})
and
(\textrm{III})
is respectively defined by

(3.22)

(\textrm{I}):=\left|W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})-W^{f}*X_{t}(\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|,

(3.23)

(\textrm{II}):=\left|W^{f}*X_{t}(\widetilde{X}_{t})-W^{f}*\widetilde{X}_{t}(\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|,

and

(3.24)

(\textrm{III}):=\left|W^{f}*\widetilde{X}_{t}(\widetilde{X}_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|.

By using
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|W^{\prime}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}
, we find

(3.25)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(\textrm{I})^{2}\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y^{j})(W(X_{t}-X^{j}_{t})-W(\widetilde{X}_{t}-X^{j}_{t}))\right]\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t}|^{2}\right].\end{split}

Likewise

(3.26)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(\textrm{II})^{2}\right]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}f(Y^{j})(W(\widetilde{X}_{t}-X^{j}_{t})-W(\widetilde{X}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}))\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{4}M}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}|X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}|^{2}\right)\right]\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t}|^{2}\right],\end{split}

where we used that by symmetry
\mathbb{E}\left[|X^{j}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t}|^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t}|^{2}\right]
for all
j=1,\dots,M
.

By independence of the
(\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})_{1\leq j\leq M}
and using that

(3.27)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})\right],\ 1\leq j\neq i\leq N,

we get

(3.28)

\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbb{E}\left[(\textrm{III})^{2}\right]=\displaystyle\int\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j\neq i}f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})\right]\right)^{2}\right]\\
\times\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in dx\right).\end{array}

Therefore

(3.29)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(\textrm{III})^{2}\right]&\leq C\int\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})\right]\right)\right)^{2}\right]\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in dx\right)\\
&+\frac{C}{M^{2}}\int\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y)W(x-\widetilde{X}_{t})\right]^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in dx\right).\end{split}

Making use of the bound
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|W(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}
, we see that the second integral in the right-hand side of (
3.29
) is dominated by

(3.30)

\begin{split}\int\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y)W(x-\widetilde{X}_{t})\right]^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in dx\right)&\leq C\int W(x-y)^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in dx\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\widetilde{X}_{t}\in dy\right)\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}.\end{split}

In estimating the first integral of (
3.29
), we utilize the independence of the
(\tilde{X}^{j}_{t})_{1\leq j\leq N}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, which gives

(3.31)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\left[f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})\right]\right)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=\textrm{Var}\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j\neq i}f(Y^{j})W(x-\widetilde{X}^{j}_{t})\right)\\
&=\frac{M-1}{M^{2}}\textrm{Var}\left(f(Y)W(x-\widetilde{X}_{t})\right)\\
&\leq\frac{C}{M\delta^{2}}.\end{split}

Putting together (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.29
)-(
3.31
), we infer

(3.32)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|W^{f}*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{M\delta^{2}}+\frac{C}{\delta^{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-\widetilde{X}_{t}|^{2}\right].

Taking
f=1
leads to the same estimate for
\mathbb{E}\left[\left|W*X_{t}(X_{t})-\sum_{n=1}^{N}W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]
.

If we insert this in (
3.20
) and use (
3.19
), we obtain

(3.33)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}(X_{s}-\widetilde{X}_{s})^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{M}\frac{C}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{C}{\delta^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq r}(X_{s}-\widetilde{X}_{s})^{2}\right]dr.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality proves (
3.18
) and concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
1\leq i\leq M
. Then for some constant
C>0
independent of
\delta

(3.34)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\widetilde{X}^{i,M}_{t}-\hat{X}^{i}_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\delta_{M}.

Proof.

In the same manner of (
3.20
), for some constant
C>0

(3.35)

|\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}-\hat{\Sigma}_{t}|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|+C|\widetilde{X}_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}|.

For all
t\in[0,T]

(3.36)

\begin{split}\left|W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|&\leq\left|W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})-\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|\\
&+\left|W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|\\
&+\left|W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|.\end{split}

Next, using the boundedness of the gradient of
\hat{p}_{n}
, we estimate the first two terms of the left-hand side of (
3.36
) as follows

(3.37)

\begin{split}\left|W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})-\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|&\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\int W_{1}(y)|\hat{p}_{n}(t,x-\delta y)-\hat{p}_{n}(t,x)|dy\\
&\leq\delta\|\hat{p}_{n}\|_{C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}}\int|y|W_{1}(y)dy,\end{split}

and

(3.38)

\left|W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|\leq\|\hat{p}_{n}\|_{C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}}|\widetilde{X}_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}|.

Therefore, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\delta
such that

(3.39)

\left|W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})-\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|+\left|W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\hat{X}_{t})\right|\leq C(\delta+|\widetilde{X}_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}|).

For the last term of (
3.36
), write

(3.40)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|W*\widetilde{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})-W*\hat{p}_{n}(t,\widetilde{X}_{t})\right|^{2}\right]&=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\int|W*(\widetilde{p}_{n}-\hat{p}_{n})|^{2}p_{m}\\
&\leq\|p\|_{(C^{1+\alpha/4,2+\alpha/2})^{N}}\int|W*(\widetilde{p}_{n}-\hat{p}_{n})|^{2}\\
&\leq\|p\|_{(C^{1+\alpha/4,2+\alpha/2})^{N}}\int|\widetilde{p}_{n}-\hat{p}_{n}|^{2}\\
&\leq\|p\|_{(C^{1+\alpha/4,2+\alpha/2})^{N}}\delta,\end{split}

where we used Proposition
3.1
.

Combining the previous estimate and (
3.36
) we deduce the existence of
C>0
independent of
\delta
such that

(3.41)

\mathbb{E}\left[|\widetilde{\sigma}_{t}-\hat{\sigma}_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\left(\delta^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[|\widetilde{X}_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}|^{2}\right]\right).

Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality and Gronwall’s inequality yield

(3.42)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\widetilde{X}_{t}-\hat{X}_{t}|^{2}\right]\leq C\delta.

∎

We are now ready for the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

According to Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
above, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
1\leq i\leq N

(3.43)

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(X^{i,M}_{t}-\hat{X}^{i,M}_{t}\right)^{2}\right]\leq\frac{C}{M}\frac{\exp{(CT\delta_{M}^{-4})}}{\delta_{M}^{2}}+C\delta_{M}.

It is enough to choose
(\delta_{M})_{M\geq 1}
such that
\lim_{M\to\infty}\delta_{M}=0
and

(3.44)

\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\frac{\exp{(CT\delta_{M}^{-4})}}{\delta_{M}^{2}}=0.

∎

Abstract
We explain the correspondences between twisted monopoles with Dirac type singularity and polystable twisted mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity on a 3-dimensional torus. We also explain that they are equivalent to polystable parabolic twisted difference modules on elliptic curves.

Introduction

We studied the Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondences for singular monopoles with periodicity
in one direction
[
4
]

or two directions
[
5
]
.
In this paper, we study singular monopoles with periodicity in three directions.
In the analytic aspect, this case is much simpler than the other cases
because a
3
-dimensional torus is compact.
But, there still exist interesting correspondences
with algebro-geometric objects.
Moreover, everything is generalized
to the twisted case. (See Section
2
for the twisted objects.)
Though we also study a generalization to the twisted case,
this introduction is devoted to explain the results in the untwisted case.

1.1
Triply periodic monopoles with Dirac type singularity

Let
Y
be an oriented
3
-dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space
with an Euclidean metric
g_{Y}
.
Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of
Y
.
We set
\mathcal{M}:=Y/\Gamma
,
which is equipped with the induced metric

g_{\mathcal{M}}
.
Let
Z
be a finite subset of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
E
be a
C^{\infty}
-vector bundle on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z

with a Hermitian metric
h
,
a unitary connection
\nabla

and an anti-self-adjoint endomorphism
\phi
.
A tuple
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
is called a monopole
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z

if the Bogomolny equation

F(\nabla)=\ast\nabla\phi

is satisfied, where
F(\nabla)
denotes the curvature of
\nabla
,
and
\ast
denotes the Hodge star operator
with respect to
g_{\mathcal{M}}
.
A point of
P\in Z
is called a Dirac type singularity of
the monopole
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)

if
|\phi_{Q}|_{h}=O\big(d(Q,Z)^{-1}\big)
for any
Q\in\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
,
where
\phi_{Q}
denotes the element of
the fiber
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits(E)_{|Q}
over
Q
induced by
\phi
,
and
d(Q,Z)
denotes the distance between
Q
and
Z
.
Note that the notion of Dirac type singularity was
originally introduced by
Kronheimer
[
3
]
.
The above condition is equivalent to the original definition,
according to
[
6
]
.

1.2
Mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity

Let us explain a correspondence between monopoles with Dirac type singularity and polystable mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity on a
3
-dimensional torus. (See Section
2
below for more details on the notions of mini-complex structures
and mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity on
3
-dimensional manifolds.) It was formulated by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
2
]
.

1.2.1
Mini-complex structure

We take a linear coordinate system
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
on
Y

compatible with the orientation such that
g_{Y}=\sum dx_{i}\,dx_{i}
,
and we set
t:=x_{1}
and
w=x_{2}+\sqrt{-1}x_{3}
.
The coordinate system induces a mini-complex structure
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.
A
C^{\infty}
-function
f
on an open subset of
\mathcal{M}

is called mini-holomorphic
if
\partial_{t}f=\partial_{\overline{w}}f=0
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}\setminus Z}
denote the sheaf of
mini-holomorphic functions on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.

1.2.2
Mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}\setminus Z}
-module.
Let
P
be a point of
Z
.
We take a lift
(t_{0},w_{0})\in Y
of
P
.
Let
\epsilon
and
\delta
denote small positive numbers.
Set

B_{w_{0}}(\delta):=\bigl\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,|w-w_{0}|<\delta\bigr\}
and

B_{w_{0}}^{\ast}(\delta):=\bigl\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,0<|w-w_{0}|<\delta\bigr\}
.
For any
t_{0}-\epsilon<t<t_{0}+\epsilon
,
the restriction

\mathcal{V}_{|\{t\}\times B^{\ast}_{w_{0}}(\delta)}

is naturally a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{B^{\ast}_{w_{0}}(\delta)}
-module.
If
t\neq t_{0}
,
they extend to locally free
\mathcal{O}_{B_{w_{0}}(\delta)}
-modules

\mathcal{V}_{|\{t\}\times B_{w_{0}}(\delta)}
.
Because mini-holomorphic functions are constant
in the
t
-direction,
we obtain an isomorphism of

\mathcal{O}_{B^{\ast}_{w_{0}}(\delta)}
-modules

\mathcal{V}_{|\{t_{0}-\epsilon_{1}\}\times B^{\ast}_{w_{0}}(\delta)}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{|\{t_{0}+\epsilon_{1}\}\times B^{\ast}_{w_{0}}(\delta)}

for
0<\epsilon_{1}<\epsilon
.
If it is meromorphic at
w_{0}
,
then
P
is called a Dirac type singularity of
\mathcal{V}
.
If every point of
Z
is Dirac type singularity,
then
\mathcal{V}
is called a mini-holomorphic bundle
with Dirac type singularity on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.

1.2.3
Stability condition

Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
2
]

introduced a sophisticated way
to define a stability condition for
mini-holomorphic bundles
with Dirac type singularity on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.

Let
H^{j}(\mathcal{M}\setminus Z)

denote the
j
-th cohomology group of
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z

with
{\mathbb{R}}
-coefficient.
Let
H_{j}(\mathcal{M},Z)
denote the relative
j
-th homology group
of
(\mathcal{M},Z)
with
{\mathbb{R}}
-coefficient.
Note that there exists the natural isomorphism

\Phi_{Z}\colon\ H^{2}(\mathcal{M}\setminus Z)\simeq H_{1}(\mathcal{M},Z).

Let
\mathfrak{T}
denote the space of left invariant vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
,
and let
\mathfrak{T}^{\lor}
denote the left invariant
1
-forms on
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\sigma
denote the image of
1
via the canonical morphism

{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{T}\otimes\mathfrak{T}^{\lor}
.
It is described as

\sigma=\sum_{i=1,2,3}\partial_{x_{i}}\otimes dx_{i}
.

For any mini-holomorphic bundle with Dirac type singularity

\mathcal{V}
on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
,
we obtain

c_{1}(\mathcal{V})\in H^{2}(\mathcal{M}\setminus Z)
,
and hence

\Phi_{Z}(c_{1}(\mathcal{V}))\in H_{1}(\mathcal{M},Z)
.
Then, we obtain the following invariant vector field

\int_{\Phi_{Z}(c_{1}(\mathcal{V}))}\sigma=\sum_{i=1,2,3}\left(\int_{\Phi_{Z}(c_{1}(\mathcal{V}))}dx_{i}\right)\partial_{x_{i}}\in\mathfrak{T}.

Kontsevich and Soibelman discovered that

\int_{\Phi_{Z}(c_{1}(\mathcal{V}))}\sigma

is a scalar multiplication of
\partial_{t}=\partial_{x_{1}}
,
and they define the degree

\deg^{\mathop{\rm KS}\nolimits}(\mathcal{V})
for
\mathcal{V}

as follows

\int_{\Phi_{Z}(c_{1}(\mathcal{V}))}\sigma=\deg^{\mathop{\rm KS}\nolimits}(\mathcal{V})\partial_{t}.

They introduced the following stability condition.

Definition 1.1
.

A mini-holomorphic bundle with Dirac type singularity

\mathcal{V}
on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
is called stable (resp. semistable)
if

\displaystyle\deg^{\mathop{\rm KS}\nolimits}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})<\deg^{\mathop{\rm KS}\nolimits}(\mathcal{V})/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle\bigl(\mbox{\rm resp. }\deg^{\mathop{\rm KS}\nolimits}(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})\leq\deg^{\mathop{\rm KS}\nolimits}(\mathcal{V})/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})\bigr)

for any locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}\setminus Z}
-submodule

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{V}
such that

0<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{V}^{\prime})<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{V})
.
It is called polystable
if it is semistable and a direct sum of stable submodules.

1.2.4
Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence

Let
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
be a monopole with Dirac type singularity
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.
We set
\partial_{E,\overline{w}}:=\nabla_{\overline{w}}

and
\partial_{E,\partial_{t}}:=\nabla_{t}-\sqrt{-1}\phi
.
Let
\mathcal{V}
be the sheaf of sections
s
of
E

such that
\partial_{E,\overline{w}}s=\partial_{E,t}s=0
.
It is a standard fact that
\mathcal{V}
is
a mini-holomorphic bundle with Dirac type singularity
on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.
The following theorem was formulated by
Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(the untwisted case in Theorem
3.16
, Proposition
4.2
)
.

The procedure induces
an equivalence between
monopoles with Dirac type singularity
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z

and polystable mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity
of degree
0
on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.

We shall relate the degree of Kontsevich and Soibelman
with the analytic degree defined in terms of Hermitian metrics
(Proposition
4.2
).
Then,
Theorem
1.2
follows
from the fundamental theorem due to Simpson
[
7
]

as we shall explain in
the proof of Theorem
3.16
,
which is an analogue of a result due to
Charbonneau and Hurtubise
[
1
]

for singular monopoles on
3
-dimensional manifolds
obtained as the product of
S^{1}
and a compact Riemann surface.
See also the work of Yoshino
[
8
]
on the Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence for monopoles with Dirac type singularity on mini-complex 3-dimensional manifolds.

1.3
Parabolic difference modules on elliptic curves

Let us give a complement
on correspondences between mini-holomorphic bundles
with Dirac type singularity
on a
3
-dimensional torus
and parabolic difference modules on elliptic curves.

Remark 1.3
.

After completing the first version of this paper,
the author was informed that

[
2
]
also already contains
the correspondence with difference modules
on elliptic curves.

1.3.1
Parabolic difference modules on elliptic curves
and a stability condition

Let
\Gamma_{0}
be a lattice of
{\mathbb{C}}
.
We set
T:={\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{0}
.
Let
\mathfrak{a}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
\Phi\colon T\longrightarrow T
be the morphism
induced by
\Phi(z)=z+\mathfrak{a}
.
Let
D\subset T
be a finite subset.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)
denote the sheaf of
meromorphic functions on
T
which may have
poles along
D
.
For any
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-module
\mathcal{F}
,
we set
\mathcal{F}(\ast D):=\mathcal{F}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)
.
A parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference module on
T

consists of the following data

V_{\ast}=\bigl(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D}\bigr)
:

•

A locally free
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-module
V
.

•

An isomorphism of
\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)
-modules

V(\ast D)\simeq(\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V)(\ast D)
.

•

A sequence

0\leq\tau_{P,1}<\tau_{P,2}<\cdots<\tau_{P,m(P)}<1

for each
P\in D
.

•

Lattices
\mathcal{L}_{P,i}

(i=1,\ldots,m(P)-1)

of the stalk
V(\ast D)_{P}
at each
P\in D
.
We formally set

\mathcal{L}_{P,0}:=V_{P}

and
\mathcal{L}_{P,m(P)}:=(\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V)_{P}

at each
P\in D
.

When we fix
({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P})_{P\in D}
,
it is called a parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference module
on
(T,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P})_{P\in D})
.

The degree of a parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference module

(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})

is defined as follows

\displaystyle\deg\bigl(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D}\bigr):=\deg(V)+\sum_{P\in D}\sum_{i=1}^{m(P)}(1-\tau_{P,i})\deg(\mathcal{L}_{P,i},\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}).

(1.1)

Here, we set

\deg(\mathcal{L}_{P,i},\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}):=\mathop{\rm length}\nolimits\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{P,i}/\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{P,i}\bigr)-\mathop{\rm length}\nolimits\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}/\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{P,i}\bigr)
.
The degree can be rewritten as

\deg\bigl(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D}\bigr):=\deg(V)-\sum_{P\in D}\sum_{i=1}^{m(P)}\tau_{P,i}\deg(\mathcal{L}_{P,i},\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}),

because
\sum_{P\in D}\sum_{i=1}^{m(P)}\deg(\mathcal{L}_{P,i},\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1})=0
.
The slope is defined in the standard way

\mu(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D}):=\deg(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits V.

For any
\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)
-submodule
0\neq V^{\prime}\subset V

such that

V^{\prime}(\ast D)\simeq(\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V^{\prime})(\ast D)
,
we obtain lattices

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{P,i}
of
V^{\prime}(\ast D)_{P}

by setting

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{P,i}:=\mathcal{L}_{P,i}\cap V^{\prime}(\ast D)_{P}

in
V(\ast D)_{P}
,
and we obtain a parabolic

\mathfrak{a}
-difference module

(V^{\prime},({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}_{P})_{P\in D})
.
Such
(V^{\prime},({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}_{P})_{P\in D})

is called a parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference submodule
of
(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})
.

Definition 1.4
.

(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})

is called stable (resp. semistable)
if

\displaystyle\mu(V^{\prime},({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}_{P})_{P\in D})<\mu(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})

\displaystyle\bigl(\mbox{\rm resp. }\mu(V^{\prime},({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}_{P})_{P\in D})\leq\mu(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})\bigr)

for any parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference submodules
such that
0<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits V^{\prime}<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits V
.
It is called polystable if it is semistable
and a direct sum of stable objects.

1.3.2
Equivalence

We return to the situation in Section
1.2
.
We take a generator

e_{i}=(a_{i},\alpha_{i})

(i=1,2,3)

of
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{w}=Y
,
which is compatible with the orientation of
Y
.
We also assume that
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
generate
a lattice in
{\mathbb{C}}

and compatible with the orientation of
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
\Gamma_{0}
denote the lattice,
and we set
T:={\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{0}
.
We set

\gamma:=-\frac{a_{1}\overline{\alpha}_{2}-a_{2}\overline{\alpha}_{1}}{\alpha_{1}\overline{\alpha}_{2}-\alpha_{2}\overline{\alpha}_{1}},\qquad\mathfrak{t}:=a_{3}+2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\gamma\alpha_{3}),\qquad\mathfrak{a}:=\alpha_{3}.

It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{t}>0
.
We define the isomorphism

F\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{w}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{u}

by

s=t+2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\gamma w),\qquad u=w.

Note that the induced action of
\Gamma

on
{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{u}
is expressed as follows:

e_{i}(s,u)=(s,u+\alpha_{i})\quad(i=1,2),\qquad e_{3}(s,u)=(s+\mathfrak{t},u+\mathfrak{a}).

We set
[0,\mathfrak{t}[{}:=\{0\leq s<\mathfrak{t}\}
.
Let
Z_{Y}
be the pull back of
Z

by
Y\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
.
Let
D
denote the image of the composite
of the following maps:

F(Z_{Y})\cap\bigl([0,\mathfrak{t}[\times{\mathbb{C}}_{u}\bigr)\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{u}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}_{u}\longrightarrow T.

For any
P\in D
,
we take
u_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
which is mapped to
P
.
We obtain a sequence

0\leq s_{P,1}<s_{P,2}<\cdots<s_{P,m(P)}<\mathfrak{t}

by the condition:

\{(s_{P,i},u_{0})\,|\,i=1,\ldots,m(P)\}=F(Z_{Y})\cap\bigl([0,\mathfrak{t}[\times\{u_{0}\}\bigr).

It is independent of the choice of
u_{0}
.
We set

\tau_{P,i}:=s_{P,i}/\mathfrak{t}
.

Proposition 1.5

(the untwisted case in Propositions
3.13
and
3.14
)
.

There exists an equivalence between
parabolic difference modules on

(T,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P})_{P\in D})

and mini-holomorphic bundles
with Dirac type singularity on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.
The equivalence preserves the degree
up to the multiplication of a positive constant.
As a result, the equivalence preserves
the
(
poly
)
stability condition.

See Section
3.2.2

for the explicit correspondence.
As a consequence
of Theorem
1.2

and Proposition
1.5
,
we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

We have the equivalence of the following objects:

•

Monopoles with Dirac type singularity
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.

•

Polystable mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity
of degree
0
on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.

•

Polystable parabolic difference modules
of degree
0
on
(T,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P})_{P\in D})
.

Here,
Z
and
({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P})_{P\in D}

are related as above.

This study is partially motivated by
the holomorphic Floer theory
[
2
]

of Kontsevich and Soibelman.
Among other things,
they revisit the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence for
D
-modules
from the viewpoint of symplectic topology,
and they extend it to the context of difference modules
of various types.
Moreover, they propose an analogue of the non-abelian Hodge theory
in the context of difference modules,
where the role of harmonic bundles should be played by monopoles
as in Theorem
1.6
.

Though the untwisted case is explained
in this introduction,
we shall study the twisted case,
i.e.,
equivalences of twisted mini-holomorphic bundles,
twisted difference modules,
and twisted monopoles.
We should note that
Kontsevich and Soibelman suggested
that there should exist a twisted version of
of Theorem
1.6
.

2
Preliminary

We introduce the notions of
twisted mini-holomorphic bundles
and twisted monopoles
as generalizations of
the notions of mini-holomorphic bundles
[
4
]

and monopoles.
We are interested only in the case
where the base manifolds are
3
-dimensional torus.
We also introduce twisted difference modules
on elliptic curves.

2.1
Mini-complex structure on 3-dimensional manifolds

Let
(t,w)
denote the standard coordinate system
on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
M
be an oriented
3
-dimensional
C^{\infty}
-manifold.
A mini-complex coordinate system on
M

is a family of open subsets

U_{\lambda}

(\lambda\in\Lambda)

equipped with an oriented embedding

\varphi_{\lambda}\colon U_{\lambda}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}

satisfying the following conditions.

•

M=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_{\lambda}
.

•

Let
F_{\lambda,\mu}\colon\varphi_{\mu}(U_{\lambda}\cap U_{\mu})\longrightarrow\varphi_{\lambda}(U_{\lambda}\cap U_{\mu})

denote the induced diffeomorphism
of open subsets in
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Note that

F_{\lambda,\mu}

is expressed as

((F_{\lambda,\mu})_{t}(t,w),(F_{\lambda,\mu})_{w}(t,w))

in terms of the coordinate systems.
Then,
it holds that

\partial_{t}(F_{\lambda,\mu})_{w}=0

and
\partial_{\overline{w}}(F_{\lambda,\mu})_{w}=0
.

Two mini-complex coordinate systems

\{(U_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

and

\{(V_{\mu},\psi_{\mu})\}_{\mu\in\Gamma}

are called equivalent
if their union is also a mini-complex coordinate system.
A mini-complex structure on
M

is an equivalence class of mini-complex coordinate systems.
We shall not distinguish a mini-complex structure
and a mini-complex coordinate system
contained in the mini-complex structure.

Suppose that
M
is equipped with a mini-complex structure.
On a mini-complex coordinate neighbourhood

(U;t,w)
,
let
T_{S}U
denote the subbundle of
the tangent bundle
TU

generated by
\partial_{t}
.
By patching
T_{S}U
for any mini-complex
coordinate neighbourhoods
(U;t,w)

we obtain the subbundle
T_{S}M\subset TM
.

Let
T^{\ast}_{S}M
denote the dual bundle of
T_{S}M
.
Let
T_{Q}^{\ast}M

denote the kernel of the natural surjection

T^{\ast}M\longrightarrow T^{\ast}_{S}M
.
It is naturally equipped with a complex structure
J
.
Let
\Omega_{Q}^{1,0}M\subset T_{Q}^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}

(resp.
\Omega_{Q}^{0,1}M
)
denote the eigen subbundle
with respect to
J
corresponding to
\sqrt{-1}

(resp.
-\sqrt{-1}
).
We set
\Omega^{0,1}M:=(T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}})/\Omega_{Q}^{1,0}M

and
\Omega^{0,i}M:=\bigwedge^{i}\Omega^{0,1}M

for
i=0,1,2
.
Similarly,
we set
\Omega^{1,0}M:=(T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}})/\Omega_{Q}^{0,1}M

and
\Omega^{i,0}M:=\bigwedge^{i}\Omega^{1,0}M

for
i=0,1,2
.

Let
\overline{\partial}_{M}
denote the differential operator

C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,1}M\big)

induced by the exterior derivative
and the projection

T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\longrightarrow\Omega^{0,1}M
.
The induced operator

C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,1}M\big)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,2}M\big)

is also denoted by
\overline{\partial}_{M}
.
Similarly, we obtain the operator

\partial_{M}\colon\ C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{i,0}M\big)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{i+1,0}M\big).

2.1.1
Riemannian case

Suppose that
M
is also equipped with a Riemannian metric
g_{M}
.
Let
T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M
denote the orthogonal complement of
T_{Q}^{\ast}M
.
We shall naturally identify
T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M

and
T^{\ast}_{S}M
.

Because
T^{\ast}M
and
T_{Q}^{\ast}M

are oriented,

T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M
is also oriented.
Let
\eta
be the unique section of
T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M

in the positive direction
such that the norm of
\eta
is
1
.
By
\eta
,

T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M
is identified with
{\mathbb{R}}\times M
.
If there exists a mini-complex coordinate system

(U;t,w)
such that
g_{M|U}=dt\,dt+dw\,d\overline{w}
,
then
\eta_{|M}=dt
.

We obtain a decomposition

\displaystyle T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}=\Omega_{Q}^{1,0}M\oplus\Omega_{Q}^{0,1}M\oplus T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}.

(2.1)

We also obtain the isomorphisms

\Omega_{Q}^{1,0}M\oplus T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\Omega^{1,0}M,\qquad\Omega_{Q}^{0,1}M\oplus T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\Omega^{0,1}M.

If the complex structure
J
on
T^{\ast}_{Q}M

is an isometry with respect to
g_{M}
,
the decomposition (
2.1
)
is orthogonal.

2.2
Twisted mini-holomorphic bundles

Let
M
be a mini-complex
3
-dimensional manifold.
Let
E
be a
C^{\infty}
-vector bundle on
M
.
We shall always assume that the rank of
E
is finite.
Let
\varrho\in C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,2}M\big)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic structure of
E

is a differential operator

\overline{\partial}_{E}\colon\allowbreak C^{\infty}(M,E)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,1}M\otimes E\big)

such that the following conditions are satisfied.

•

\overline{\partial}_{E}(fs)=f\overline{\partial}_{E}(s)+(\overline{\partial}_{M}f)\otimes s

holds for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

and
s\in C^{\infty}(M,E)
.

•

The induced operator

C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,1}M\otimes E\big)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(M,\Omega^{0,2}M\otimes E\big)

is also denoted by
\overline{\partial}_{E}
.
Then,
\overline{\partial}_{E}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}=\varrho\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
holds.

Such
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is called
a
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic vector bundle.
If
\varrho=0
, we shall omit the adjective “
0
-twisted”.

Remark 2.2
.

A
C^{\infty}
-function
f
on
an open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset M

is called mini-holomorphic
if
\overline{\partial}_{M}f=0
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{M}
denote the sheaf of mini-holomorphic functions.
In the case
\varrho=0
,
mini-holomorphic bundles
are naturally identified with
locally free
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-modules of finite rank.
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a mini-holomorphic bundle on
M
.
A local section
s
of
E
is called mini-holomorphic
if
\overline{\partial}_{E}(s)=0
.
Let
\widetilde{E}
denote the sheaf of mini-holomorphic sections
of
E
.
Then, it is easy to observe that

\widetilde{E}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-module
of finite rank.
This correspondence induces an equivalence
between mini-holomorphic bundles
and locally free
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-modules of finite rank.

2.2.1
Scattering map

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a
\varrho
-twisted
mini-holomorphic vector bundle on
M
.
Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\longrightarrow M
be a
C^{\infty}
-path
such that

T\gamma(T[0,1])\subset T_{S}M
.
Then,

\gamma^{-1}(E)
is equipped with a connection
induced by the
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic structure
\overline{\partial}_{E}
,
and hence we obtain the induced isomorphism

E_{\gamma(0)}\simeq E_{\gamma(1)}
.
It is called the scattering map in
[
1
]
.

Let
(U;t,w)
be a mini-complex coordinate neighbourhood of
M
.
Let
\partial_{E,t}
(resp.
\partial_{E,\overline{w}}
)
denote the differential operators of
E_{|U}

induced by
\overline{\partial}_{E}
and
\partial_{t}

(resp.
\partial_{\overline{w}}
).
We have the expression

\varrho=\varrho_{0}\,dt\,d\overline{w}
.
Then, the condition
\overline{\partial}_{E}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}=\varrho\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
on
U

is equivalent to

\bigl[\partial_{E,t},\partial_{E,\overline{w}}\bigr]=\varrho_{0}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
.
Assume that there exists

\nu=\nu_{t}\,dt+\nu_{\overline{w}}d\overline{w}\in C^{\infty}(U,\Omega^{0,1})

such that
\overline{\partial}\nu=\varrho
on
U
.
Note that such
\nu
always exists locally.
On
U
,
we set
\overline{\partial}_{E}^{\nu}=\overline{\partial}_{E}-\nu\,\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
.
Then,
(E_{|U},\overline{\partial}_{E}^{\nu})
is clearly a mini-holomorphic bundle.

Suppose that
U
is isomorphic to

\{t_{0}<t<t_{1}\}\times B_{\delta}
,
where
B_{\delta}=\{w\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,|w|<\delta\}
.
Take
t_{0}<b_{1}<b_{2}<t_{1}
.
We obtain the scattering map

F\colon E_{|\{t=b_{1}\}\times B_{\delta}}\simeq E_{|\{t=b_{2}\}\times B_{\delta}}
.
Let
\partial_{E,\overline{w},b_{i}}
denote the operators
on
E_{|\{t=b_{i}\}\times B_{\delta}}

by
\partial_{E,\overline{w}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

F^{\ast}(\partial_{E,\overline{w},b_{2}})=\partial_{E,\overline{w},b_{1}}+\bigl(\int_{b_{1}}^{b_{2}}\varrho_{0}\,dt\bigr)\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
.

Proof.

Take
\nu=\nu_{\overline{w}}\,d\overline{w}
such that
\overline{\partial}\nu=\varrho
,
i.e.,

\partial_{t}\nu_{\overline{w}}=\varrho_{0}
.
Then,

F^{\ast}(\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w},b_{2}})=\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w},b_{1}}

because of
[\partial^{\nu}_{E,t},\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w}}]=0
.
Then, the claim of the lemma follows.
∎

2.2.2
Twisted mini-holomorphic bundles with Dirac type singularity

Let
Z\subset M
be a discrete subset.
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a
\varrho
-twisted
mini-holomorphic bundle on
M\setminus Z
.
Let
P
be a point of
Z
.
Let
(U;t,w)
be a mini-complex coordinate
neighbourhood around
P\in Z
.
We may assume
(t(P),w(P))=(0,0)
.
By shrinking
U
,
we assume that

U\simeq\{-2\epsilon<t<2\epsilon\}\times B_{\delta}

by the mini-complex coordinate system
for some
\epsilon>0
and
\delta>0
.
Set
B^{\ast}_{\delta}:=B_{\delta}\setminus\{0\}
.
We obtain the scattering map

F\colon E_{|\{-\epsilon\}\times B^{\ast}_{\delta}}\simeq E_{|\{\epsilon\}\times B^{\ast}_{\delta}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

P
is a Dirac type singularity of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})

if
F
and
F^{-1}
are
O(|w|^{-N})
for some
N>0

with respect to
C^{\infty}
-frames of

E_{|\{\pm\epsilon\}\times B^{\ast}_{\delta}}
.
If each point of
Z
is Dirac type singularity of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
,
we say that
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
is
a
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle
with Dirac type singularity on
(M;Z)
.

Take
\nu=\nu_{t}\,dt+\nu_{\overline{w}}d\overline{w}\in C^{\infty}\big(U,\Omega^{0,1}\big)

such that

\overline{\partial}\nu=\varrho
.
We set

\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}:=\overline{\partial}_{E_{|U}}-\nu\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits

so that

\big(E_{|U},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}\big)

is mini-holomorphic.
The scattering map

F^{\nu}\colon E_{|\{-\epsilon\}\times B_{\delta}^{\ast}}\simeq E_{|\{\epsilon\}\times B_{\delta}^{\ast}}

for
\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}

is holomorphic with respect to

\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w}}
.
Note that

F^{\nu}=\exp\big(\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\nu_{t}\big)F
.
The condition in Definition
2.4

is satisfied if and only if

F^{\nu}
extends to a meromorphic isomorphism

\big(E_{|\{-\epsilon\}\times B_{\delta}},\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w},-\epsilon}\big)(\ast 0)\simeq(E_{|\{\epsilon\}\times B_{\delta}},\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w},\epsilon})(\ast 0)
,
i.e.,

P
is Dirac type singularity of

\big(E_{|U},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}\big)

in the sense of
[
4
, Section 2.2]
.

We regard
U
as an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}

by the coordinate system
(t,w)
.
Let
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}

be given by

\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=\big(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2},2z_{1}z_{2}\big)
.
Let
\widetilde{U}
be the pull back of
U

by
\varphi
.
The mini-holomorphic bundle

\big(E,\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}\big)_{|U\setminus\{P\}}

induces an
S^{1}
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle

\big(\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{P},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{P}}\big)
on

\widetilde{U}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
,
which uniquely extends to an
S^{1}
-equivariant
holomorphic vector bundle

\big(\widetilde{E}^{\nu}_{P},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{\widetilde{E}_{P}}\big)

on
\widetilde{U}
.
(See
[
6
, Section 2.2]
for
a more detailed explanation.)

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
\nu_{i}=\nu_{i,t}\,dt+\nu_{i,\overline{w}}d\overline{w}\in C^{\infty}\big(U,\Omega^{0,1}\big)

(i=1,2)

satisfy

\overline{\partial}\nu_{i}=\varrho
.
Then,
the natural identification

\widetilde{E}^{\nu_{1}}_{P|\widetilde{U}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}=\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{P}=\widetilde{E}^{\nu_{2}}_{P|\widetilde{U}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}

uniquely extends to
a
C^{\infty}
-isomorphism

\widetilde{E}^{\nu_{1}}_{P}\simeq\widetilde{E}^{\nu_{2}}_{P}
.

Proof.

Set
\nu_{0}=\nu_{0,t}\,dt+\nu_{0,\overline{w}}\,d\overline{w}:=\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}
.
We have

\overline{\partial}_{E}^{\nu_{2}}=\overline{\partial}_{E}^{\nu_{1}}-\nu_{0}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
.
By the construction
(see
[
6
, Section 2.2]
),
we have

\overline{\partial}^{\nu_{2}}_{\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{P}}=\overline{\partial}^{\nu_{1}}_{\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{P}}-\bigl(\varphi^{\ast}(\nu_{0,t})\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\ast}(t)+\varphi^{\ast}(\nu_{0,\overline{w}})\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\ast}(\overline{w})\bigr)\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
.
Then, the claim of the lemma is clear.
∎

We set

\widetilde{E}_{P}:=\widetilde{E}^{\nu}_{P}

for
\nu\in C^{\infty}\big(U,\Omega^{0,1}\big)

such that
\overline{\partial}\nu=\varrho
,
which is called the Kronheimer resolution of

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
at
P
.

Definition 2.6
.

A Hermitian metric
h
of
E

is called adapted at
P

if the induced metric
\widetilde{h}_{P}

of
\widetilde{E}^{\prime}_{P}
extends to a
C^{\infty}
-metric
of the Kronheimer resolution
\widetilde{E}_{P}
.
If
h
is adapted at any point of
Z
,
then
h
is called an adapted metric of
\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
.

2.2.3
Chern connections and Higgs fields

Suppose that we are given a splitting

TM/T_{S}M\longrightarrow TM
.
It induces the following decompositions:

\displaystyle T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\Omega^{1,0}_{Q}M\oplus\Omega^{0,1}_{Q}M\oplus T^{\ast}_{S}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Omega^{0,1}M\simeq\Omega^{0,1}_{Q}M\oplus T^{\ast}_{S}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Omega^{1,0}M\simeq\Omega^{0,1}_{Q}M\oplus T^{\ast}_{S}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}.

(2.4)

Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle
on
M
. By (
2.3
),
we obtain a decomposition

\overline{\partial}_{E}=\overline{\partial}^{S}_{E}\oplus\overline{\partial}^{Q}_{E}
,
where

\overline{\partial}^{S}_{E}(s)\in C^{\infty}\big(X,(T_{S}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}})^{\lor}\big)

and

\overline{\partial}^{Q}_{E}(s)\in C^{\infty}\big(X,\Omega^{0,1}_{Q}M\big)
.

Let
h
be a Hermitian metric of
E
.
We obtain the differential operator

\partial_{E,h}\colon C^{\infty}(X,E)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(X,\Omega^{1,0}M\otimes E\big)

satisfying the condition

\overline{\partial}_{M}h(u,v)=h(\overline{\partial}_{E}u,v)+h(u,\partial_{E,h}v)

for any
u,v\in C^{\infty}(X,E)
.
We also obtain the decomposition

\partial_{E,h}=\partial_{E,h}^{Q}+\partial_{E,h}^{S}

induced by (
2.4
).
For a mini-complex coordinate neighbourhood

(U;t,w)
,
we obtain the operators

\partial_{E,h,w}

(resp.
\partial_{E,h,t}
) on
E

induced by

\partial_{E,h}
and
\partial_{w}

(resp.
\partial_{t}
).

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
4
]
,
\partial_{E,h,t}
is denoted
as
\partial_{E,h,t}^{\prime}
.

By using (
2.2
), we set

\nabla_{h}:=\overline{\partial}_{E}^{Q}+\partial_{E,h}^{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\overline{\partial}^{S}_{E}+\partial^{S}_{E,h}\bigr),\qquad\phi_{h}:=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\bigl(\overline{\partial}_{E}^{S}-\partial_{E,h}^{S}\bigr).

They are called the Chern connection and the Higgs field
of
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},h)
.
Note that they depend on the choice of a splitting

TM/T_{S}M\longrightarrow TM
.

If
M
is also equipped with a Riemannian metric
g_{M}
,
we shall use the splitting
TM/T_{S}M\longrightarrow TM
induced by
g_{M}
.
Moreover, by the section
\eta
in Section
2.1.1
,

T^{\ast}_{S,g_{M}}M
is identified with
the product bundle
{\mathbb{R}}\times M
.
Hence, we regard
\phi_{h}

as an anti-Hermitian endomorphism of
E
.
In particular,
if
g_{M}=dt\,dt+dw\,d\overline{w}

on a mini-complex coordinate neighbourhood
(U;t,w)
,
the following holds for any
s\in C^{\infty}(U,E)
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{h}(s)=(\partial_{E,\overline{w}}s)\,d\overline{w}+(\partial_{E,h,w}s)\,dw+\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\partial_{E,t}s+\partial_{E,h,t}s\bigr)\,dt,

\displaystyle\phi_{h}(s)=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\bigl(\partial_{E,t}s-\partial_{E,h,t}s\bigr).

2.3
Twisted monopoles in the locally Euclidean case

2.3.1
Twisted monopoles

Let
(M,g_{M})
be an oriented Riemannian
3
-dimensional manifold.
Let
B
be a real
2
-form on
M
.
Let
E
be a vector bundle on
M

equipped with a Hermitian metric
h
,
a unitary connection
\nabla
,
and an anti-Hermitian endomorphism
\phi
.

Definition 2.8
.

Such a tuple
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
is called
a
B
-twisted monopole
if the following
B
-twisted Bogomolny equation is
satisfied:

F(\nabla)=\ast\nabla\phi+\sqrt{-1}B\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}.

Here
F(\nabla)
denotes the curvature of
\nabla
, and
\ast
denotes the Hodge star operator.

Let
A
and
f
be a real
1
-form and an
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued
C^{\infty}
-function on
M
, respectively.
We set

\widetilde{\nabla}:=\nabla+\sqrt{-1}A\,\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
,

\widetilde{\phi}:=\phi+\sqrt{-1}f\,\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits

and

\widetilde{B}:=B+dA-\ast(df)
.
Then, the following is easy to see.

Lemma 2.9
.

(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
is a
B
-twisted monopole
if and only if

\big(E,h,\widetilde{\nabla},\widetilde{\phi}\big)

is a
\widetilde{B}
-twisted monopole.

Remark 2.10
.

If
M
is compact,
any real
2
-form
B
on
M
is expressed as

B=dA-\ast df+B_{0}
,
where
A
is a real
1
-form,

f
is a
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued
C^{\infty}
-function,
and
B_{0}
is a harmonic
1
-form.
Indeed,
let
G
denote the Green operator
for the Laplace-Beltrami operator
on the space of
2
-forms on
M
.
Then,
B-(d^{\ast}d+dd^{\ast})G(B)
is a harmonic
2
-form,
and
G(B)
is
C^{\infty}
.
We can also deduce that
for any point
P\in M
,
there exists a neighbourhood
M_{P}
of
P

such that
B_{|M_{P}}=dA_{P}-\ast df_{P}

for a real
1
-form
A_{P}
and an
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued

C^{\infty}
-function
f_{P}
on
M_{P}
.

2.3.2
Twisted monopoles and twisted mini-holomorphic bundles

in the locally Euclidean case

Suppose that
M
is also equipped with a mini-complex structure. Moreover, we assume that
M
is a locally Euclidean,
i.e.,
for each
P\in M
,
there exists a mini-complex coordinate neighbourhood

(U;t,w)
of
P

such that the Riemannian metric of
M
on
U

is
dt\,dt+dw\,d\overline{w}
.
Note that
d\eta=0

for the global trivialization
\eta

of
T_{S,g_{M}}^{\ast}M

in Section
2.1.1
.
By (
2.1
),
for any complex vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
on
M
,
we obtain the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\otimes\bigwedge^{2}\bigl(T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\bigr)=\bigl(\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1,0}_{Q}M\wedge\eta\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{0,1}_{Q}M\wedge\eta\bigr)\oplus\bigl(\mathcal{V}\otimes\Omega^{1,1}_{Q}M\bigr),

(2.5)

where

\Omega^{1,1}_{Q}M:=\Omega_{Q}^{1,0}M\wedge\Omega_{Q}^{0,1}M
.
For any section
s
of

\mathcal{V}\otimes\bigwedge^{2}\bigl(T^{\ast}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\bigr)
,
we obtain the decomposition

s=s^{(1,0),\eta}+s^{(0,1),\eta}+s^{(1,1)}

according to (
2.5
).
In particular,
we obtain the decomposition

B=B^{(1,0),\eta}+B^{(0,1),\eta}+B^{(1,1)}
.
Because
B
is real,

B^{(1,1)}
is also real,
and
B^{(1,0),\eta}=\overline{B^{(0,1),\eta}}
holds.

We can check the following lemma
by a direct computation.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
be a
B
-twisted monopole on
M
.
We have the decomposition

\nabla=\nabla_{Q}^{1,0}+\nabla_{Q}^{0,1}+\nabla_{S}

induced by
(
2.1
)
.
We set

\overline{\partial}_{E}:=\nabla_{Q}^{0,1}+\nabla_{S}-\sqrt{-1}\phi\eta
.
Then,
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})

is a
\sqrt{-1}B^{(0,1),\eta}
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle.

Conversely,
let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle
on
M
.
Let
h
be a Hermitian metric of
E
.
We obtain the Chern connection
\nabla_{h}

and the Higgs field
\phi_{h}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

We have

\bigl(F(\nabla_{h})-\ast\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}\bigr)^{(0,1),\eta}=\varrho\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}

and

\bigl(F(\nabla_{h})-\ast\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}\bigr)^{(1,0),\eta}=-\overline{\varrho}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
.

Proof.

We have

\overline{\partial}_{E}=\nabla_{h,Q}^{0,1}+\nabla_{h,S}-\sqrt{-1}\phi_{h}\eta

and

\partial_{E,h}=\nabla_{h,Q}^{1,0}+\nabla_{h,S}+\sqrt{-1}\phi_{h}\eta
.
Because

\overline{\partial}_{E}\circ\overline{\partial}_{E}=\varrho\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
,
we obtain

\partial_{E,h}\circ\partial_{E,h}=-\overline{\varrho}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
.
Then, we obtain the claim of the lemma by computations.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

There exists a real
2
-form
B

such that

(E,h,\nabla_{h},\phi_{h})
is a
B
-twisted monopole
if and only if
the trace-free part of

\bigl(F(\nabla_{h})-\ast\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}\bigr)^{(1,1)}
is
0
,
i.e., there exists a real
2
-form
\varpi

such that

\bigl(F(\nabla_{h})-\ast\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}\bigr)^{(1,1)}=\sqrt{-1}\varpi\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits
.
In that case,

B=-\sqrt{-1}(\varrho-\overline{\varrho})+\varpi
.

Remark 2.14
.

If the condition in Corollary
2.13

is satisfied,

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},h\big)
is also called
a
B
-twisted monopole.

2.3.3
Dirac type singularity

Let
Z
be a discrete subset of
M
.
Let
B
be a real
2
-form on
M
.
Let
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
be a
B
-twisted monopole
on
M\setminus Z
.
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be the underlying

\sqrt{-1}B^{(0,1),\eta}
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle.

Definition 2.15
.

A point
P\in Z
is called Dirac type singularity
of
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)

if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

P
is Dirac type singularity of

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
.

•

h
is an adapted metric of

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)

in the sense of Definition
2.6
.

We say that
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
is a
B
-twisted monopole
with Dirac type singularity on

if any point

is Dirac type singularity of
(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
.

Lemma 2.16
.

P
is Dirac type singularity of

(E,h,\nabla,\phi)

if and only if
there exists a neighbourhood
M_{P}
of
P

in

such that

|\phi_{Q}|_{h}=O\bigl(d(P,Q)^{-1}\bigr)

for
Q\in M_{P}\setminus\{P\}
.

Proof.

If
M_{P}
is sufficiently small,
there exists a real
1
-form
A_{P}

and an
{\mathbb{R}}
-valued

-function

such that

.
Then,

(\widetilde{E},\widetilde{h}):=(E,h)_{|M_{P}\setminus\{P\}}

with

and
\widetilde{\phi}:=\phi-\sqrt{-1}f_{P}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}

is a monopole on

.
If
P
is Dirac type singularity of

(E,h,\nabla,\phi)
,
then

is Dirac type singularity of

(\widetilde{E},\widetilde{h},\widetilde{\nabla},\widetilde{\phi})
.
According to
[

]
,
it is equivalent to

|\widetilde{\phi}_{Q}|_{h}=O\bigl(d(P,Q)^{-1}\bigr)

around any point

,
which is equivalent to

|\phi_{Q}|_{h}=O\bigl(d(P,Q)^{-1}\bigr)

around any point
P\in Z
.
∎

2.4
Twisted difference modules

Let
\Gamma_{0}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a lattice.
We put
T:={\mathbb{C}}/\Gamma_{0}
.
Take any
\mathfrak{a}\in T
,
and define the automorphism
\Phi
of
T

by
\Phi(z)=z+\mathfrak{a}
.
Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a holomorphic line bundle
of degree
0
on
T
.

A parabolic
\mathfrak{L}
-twisted difference module

V_{\ast}=(V,({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}_{P})_{P\in D})

on
T

consists of the following data:

•

A locally free
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-module
V

equipped with an isomorphism

V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)\simeq(\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V)\otimes\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)
,
where
D
is a finite subset of
T
.

•

A sequence

0\leq\tau_{P,1}<\tau_{P,2}<\cdots<\tau_{P,m(P)}<1

for each
P\in D
.

•

Lattices
\mathcal{L}_{P,i}

(i=1,\ldots,m(P)-1)

of the stalk
V(\ast D)_{P}
at each
P\in D
.
We formally set

\mathcal{L}_{P,0}:=V_{P}

and
\mathcal{L}_{P,m(P)}:=\bigl((\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V)\otimes\mathfrak{L}\bigr)_{P}

at each
P\in D
.

The degree of
V_{\ast}
is defined
by the formula (
1.1
), i.e.,

\deg(V_{\ast}):=\deg(V)+\sum_{P\in D}\sum_{i=1}^{m(P)}(1-\tau_{P,i})\deg(\mathcal{L}_{P,i},\mathcal{L}_{P,i-1}).

We set

\mu(V_{\ast}):=\deg(V_{\ast})/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(V)
.

For any
\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ast D)
-submodule

0\neq V^{\prime}\subset V

such that

V^{\prime}(\ast D)\simeq(\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V^{\prime})(\ast D)
,
we obtain lattices

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{P,i}
of
V^{\prime}(\ast D)_{P}

by setting

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{P,i}:=\mathcal{L}_{P,i}\cap V^{\prime}(\ast D)_{P}

in
V(\ast D)_{P}
,
and we obtain a parabolic

\mathfrak{L}
-twisted

\mathfrak{a}
-difference module

V^{\prime}_{\ast}=(V^{\prime},({\boldsymbol{\tau}}_{P},{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}}^{\prime}_{P})_{P\in D})
.
Such
V^{\prime}_{\ast}

is called a parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference submodule
of
V_{\ast}
.

Definition 2.17
.

V_{\ast}

is called stable (resp. semistable)
if

\mu(V^{\prime}_{\ast})<\mu(V_{\ast})\qquad\bigl(\mbox{\rm resp. }\mu(V^{\prime}_{\ast})\leq\mu(V_{\ast})\bigr)

for any
parabolic
\mathfrak{a}
-difference submodules
V^{\prime}

such that
0<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits V^{\prime}<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits V
.
It is called polystable if it is semistable
and a direct sum of stable objects.

2.4.1
Example

It is easy to construct examples of parabolic difference modules.

Lemma 2.18
.

For any holomorphic line bundle
\mathfrak{L}
of degree
0
,
and for any
d\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
there exists a parabolic
\mathfrak{L}
-difference module
V_{\ast}

of rank one
such that
\deg(V_{\ast})=d
.

Proof.

There exist
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in T

and
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

such that

\mathfrak{L}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}P_{i}\big)=\mathcal{O}_{T}
.
Note that
\sum\ell_{i}=0
.
We take
P_{0}\in T\setminus\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
.
We set
D:=\{P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
.
We set
V:=\mathcal{O}_{T}
.
By our choice of
D
,
there exists an isomorphism

F\colon V(\ast D)\simeq(\Phi^{\ast})^{-1}(V)\otimes\mathfrak{L}(\ast D)
.
We set
m(P_{i})=1

and
\tau_{P_{i},1}=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
We set
m(P_{0})=2
,
and we choose
0\leq\tau_{P_{0},1}<\tau_{P_{0},2}<1
.
We set
\mathcal{L}_{P_{0},1}=\mathcal{O}_{T}(\ell P_{0})_{P_{0}}

for an integer
\ell
.
Then, we obtain a parabolic
\mathfrak{L}
-twisted
difference module

V_{\ast}^{(\ell,\tau_{P_{0},1},\tau_{P_{0},2})}

for which

\deg\big(V_{\ast}^{(\ell,\tau_{P_{0},1},\tau_{P_{0},2})}\big)=(\tau_{P_{0},2}-\tau_{P_{0},1})\ell
.
Then, the claim is clear.
∎

3
Equivalences

We shall study equivalences of
twisted mini-holomorphic bundles,
twisted difference modules,
and twisted monopoles.
First, in Section
3.1
,
we introduce analytically stability condition
for twisted mini-holomorphic bundles
in terms of adapted metrics.
We also prepare some formulas for the curvature
and the Higgs field
of a twisted mini-holomorphic bundle with a Hermitian metric
which are standard in the context of
mini-holomorphic bundles as in
[
4
]
.
In Section
3.2
,
we shall explain the equivalence between
twisted mini-holomorphic bundles
and twisted difference modules,
which preserves the stability conditions.
In Section
3.3
,
we shall explain the equivalence between
polystable twisted mini-holomorphic bundles
and twisted monopoles.

3.1
Analytic stability condition for twisted mini-holomorphic bundles

3.1.1
3-dimensional torus with mini-complex structure

We take an oriented base

(a_{i},\alpha_{i})

(i=1,2,3)

of the
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space

{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
Y:={\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}

with the Riemannian metric
dt\,dt+dw\,d\overline{w}
.
It is equipped with the mini-complex structure
induced by the mini-complex coordinate system
(t,w)
.
We consider the action of

\Gamma:={\mathbb{Z}}{\tt e}_{1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}{\tt e}_{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}{\tt e}_{3}

on
Y
given by

{\tt e}_{i}(t,w)=(t,w)+(a_{i},\alpha_{i}),\qquad i=1,2,3.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the quotient space
of
Y
by the action of
\Gamma
.
It is equipped with a naturally induced mini-complex structure.

3.1.2
Contraction of the curvature

Let
Z
be a finite subset of
\mathcal{M}
.
Take
\varrho\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M},\Omega^{0,2}\mathcal{M})
.
Let
(E,\overline{\partial}_{E})
be a
\varrho
-twisted
mini-holomorphic bundle
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.
Let
h
be a Hermitian metric of
E
.
As in
[
4
]
,
we set

\displaystyle G(h):=\bigl[\nabla_{h,w},\nabla_{h,\overline{w}}\bigr]-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\nabla_{h,t}\phi_{h}.

If we emphasize the dependence on
\overline{\partial}_{E}
,
we use the notation
G(h,\overline{\partial}_{E})
.
Note that

\displaystyle G(h)\,dw\,d\overline{w}=\bigl(F(\nabla_{h})-\ast\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}\bigr)^{(1,1)}

(3.1)

for the notation in Section
2.3.2
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z

with
\nu=\nu_{t}\,dt+\nu_{\overline{w}}\,d\overline{w}\in C^{\infty}\big(U,\Omega^{0,1}\big)
.
On
U
,
we set

\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}:=\overline{\partial}_{E}-\nu\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
.
Then,

\big(E_{|U},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}\big)

is a

(\varrho_{|U}-\overline{\partial}\nu)
-twisted
mini-holomorphic bundle on
U
.
We obtain the Chern connection

\nabla^{\nu}_{h}

and the Higgs field
\phi^{\nu}_{h}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following holds:

\displaystyle\phi_{h}^{\nu}=\phi_{h}-\sqrt{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\nu_{t})\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E},\qquad\nabla_{h}^{\nu}=\nabla_{h}-\sqrt{-1}\bigl(2\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\nu_{\overline{w}}d\overline{w})+\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\nu_{t}\,dt)\bigr)\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E},

\displaystyle\nabla_{h}^{\nu}\big(\phi_{h}^{\nu}\big)=\nabla_{h}(\phi_{h})-\sqrt{-1}d\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\nu_{t})\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E},

\displaystyle F\big(\nabla^{\nu}_{h}\big)=F(\nabla_{h})-\sqrt{-1}d\bigl(2\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\nu_{\overline{w}}dw)+\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(\nu_{t})dt\bigr)\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E},

\displaystyle G\big(h,\overline{\partial}_{E}^{\nu}\big)=G(h,\overline{\partial}_{E})-\bigl(2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\partial_{w}\nu_{\overline{w}})+2^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\partial_{t}\nu_{t})\bigr)\,\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}.

We can check the formulas by direct computations.

Let
E^{\prime}
be any
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic subbundle of
E
,
i.e.,

\overline{\partial}_{E}C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}\setminus Z,E^{\prime})\subset C^{\infty}\big(\mathcal{M}\setminus Z,\Omega^{0,1}\mathcal{M}\otimes E^{\prime}\big)
.
We have the natural
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic
structure
\overline{\partial}_{E^{\prime}}
on
E^{\prime}
.
Let
h_{E^{\prime}}
be the induced metric of
E^{\prime}
.
Let
p_{E^{\prime}}
be the orthogonal projection of
E

onto
E^{\prime}
with respect to
h
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following Chern–Weil formula holds:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits G(h_{E^{\prime}})=\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\bigl(G(h_{E})\cdot p_{E^{\prime}}\bigr)-\bigl|\partial_{E,\overline{w}}p_{E^{\prime}}\bigr|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\bigl|\partial_{E,t}p_{E^{\prime}}\bigr|^{2}.

(3.2)

Proof.

If
\varrho=0
,
it is proved in
[
4
, Section 2.8.2]
.
Let us study the general case.
It is enough to prove the equality
locally around any point of
Q\in\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.
On a neighbourhood
U
of
Q
,
there exists

\nu\in C^{\infty}\big(U,\Omega^{0,1}\big)

such that

\overline{\partial}_{E}^{\nu}

is a mini-holomorphic structure of
E_{|U}
.
Note that

\partial^{\nu}_{E,\overline{w}}p_{E^{\prime}}=\partial_{E,\overline{w}}p_{E^{\prime}}
,

\partial^{\nu}_{E,t}p_{E^{\prime}}=\partial_{E,t}p_{E^{\prime}}
.
Moreover,

\big(E^{\prime},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E^{\prime}}\big)
is a mini-holomorphic subbundle
of
\big(E,\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E}\big)
,
and

G\big(h_{E^{\prime}},\overline{\partial}^{\nu}_{E^{\prime}}\big)=G\big(h_{E^{\prime}},\overline{\partial}_{E^{\prime}}\big)-\bigl(2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\partial_{w}\nu_{\overline{w}})+2^{-1}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(\partial_{t}\nu_{t})\bigr)\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E^{\prime}}
.
Then, we obtain the desired formula.
∎

3.1.3
Analytic stability condition for mini-holomorphic bundles

with a Hermitian metric

Let
\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
be a
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z

with a Hermitian metric

.

Definition 3.3
.

If
\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits G(h)
is expressed as a sum of an

-function and a non-positive function, then we set

Suppose that
|G(h)|_{h}
is

.
By (

),

is defined in

-twisted mini-holomorphic subbundle

of

.

Suppose that
|G(h)|_{h}
is

.

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},h\big)

\varrho
-twisted
mini-holomorphic subbundle

with

.

In the case

The general case follows from

.

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)

\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle

3.1.5
Analytic stability condition for
\boldsymbol{\varrho}
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundles

with Dirac type singularity

Let
\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
be a
\varrho
-twisted
mini-holomorphic bundle
with Dirac type singularity on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
.
We set

\deg^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big):=\deg\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},h\big),\qquad\mu^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big):=\deg^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)/\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)

for an adapted Hermitian metric
h
of
E
,
which is independent of the choice of
h
.
The numbers are called
the analytic degree and the analytic slope of

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},h\big)
, respectively.

Definition 3.9
.

We say that
\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
is analytically stable
if
\mu^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E^{\prime},\overline{\partial}_{E^{\prime}}\big)<\mu^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)

holds for any
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic subbundle

E^{\prime}\subset E
with

0<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E^{\prime})<\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)
.
It is called polystable if

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)=\bigoplus\big(E_{i},\overline{\partial}_{E_{i}}\big)
,
where
each
\big(E_{i},\overline{\partial}_{E_{i}}\big)
is stable
such that

\mu^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E_{i},\overline{\partial}_{E_{i}}\big)=\mu^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
.

We obtain the following lemma from Lemma
3.6
.

Lemma 3.10
.

A
\varrho
-twisted
mini-holomorphic bundle with Dirac type singularity

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
on
(\mathcal{M};Z)

is analytically stable if and only if

\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E},h\big)
is analytically stable
for an adapted Hermitian metric
h
of
E
.

3.1.6
Complement on the choice of
\boldsymbol{\varrho}

Let
H^{i}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
denote the
i
-th cohomology group
of the complex

\bigl(C^{\infty}\big(\mathcal{M},\Omega^{0,i}\mathcal{M}\big),\overline{\partial}_{\mathcal{M}}\bigr)

For any
\nu\in C^{\infty}\big(\mathcal{M},\Omega^{0,1}\mathcal{M}\big)

\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundles
are equivalent to

(\varrho-\overline{\partial}\nu)
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundles.
Hence, the essential ambiguity of the choice of
\varrho

lives in

.

Lemma 3.11
.

We have the following isomorphisms:

H^{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}},\qquad H^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}\,dt\oplus{\mathbb{C}}\,d\overline{w},\qquad H^{2}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}\,dt\wedge d\overline{w}.

Hence, for the study of twisted mini-holomorphic bundles,
it is essential to study the case

\varrho=\alpha\,dt\,d\overline{w}
for some
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

We have the isomorphism

{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\times({\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{w})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}_{z}\times{\mathbb{C}}_{w}

given by

(s,t,w)\longmapsto\big(s+\sqrt{-1}t,w\big)
.
We consider the action of
{\mathbb{Z}}\times\Gamma

on
{\mathbb{R}}\times({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}})

induced by the natural action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}

and the
\Gamma
-action on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
X
denote the quotient space.
We have the projection
\varphi\colon X\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}

induced by
(s,t,w)\longmapsto(t,w)
.
We have the natural
S^{1}={\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
-action on
X
,
and the quotient space is identified with
\mathcal{M}
.
Let

\varphi^{\ast}\colon C^{\infty}\big(\mathcal{M},\Omega^{0,i}\mathcal{M}\big)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}\big(X,\Omega^{0,i}(X)\big)

be the map induced by

\varphi^{\ast}(d\overline{w})=d\overline{w}
,

\varphi^{\ast}(dt)=\partial_{\overline{z}}(t)\,d\overline{z}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}d\overline{z}

and
the natural pull back

\varphi^{\ast}\colon C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M},{\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Then, it is easy to check that
it is a morphism of complexes,
and that it induces an isomorphism between

and
the

-invariant part of

Then, the claim of the lemma follows.

Remark 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}U_{\lambda}

be an open covering such that
the following holds:

•

There exist

\nu_{\lambda}\in C^{\infty}\big(U_{\lambda},\Omega^{0,1}_{U_{\lambda}}\big)

\varrho_{|U_{\lambda}}=\overline{\partial}\nu_{\lambda}
.

•

There exist

\alpha_{\lambda,\mu}\in C^{\infty}(U_{\lambda}\cap U_{\mu})

such that

.
We assume that

\alpha_{\lambda,\lambda}=0

and

\alpha_{\lambda,\mu}=-\alpha_{\mu,\lambda}
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}

be the
\mathcal{O}_{U_{\lambda}}
-module
obtained as the sheaf of

.

by the multiplication of

\exp(-\alpha_{\lambda,\mu})
.
We obtain the holomorphic functions

\theta_{\lambda,\mu,\kappa}

on

such that

in

.

\displaystyle\bigl[\partial_{E,h,u},\partial_{E,\overline{u}}\bigr]=\bigl[\nabla_{h,w},\nabla_{h,\overline{w}}\bigr]-\overline{\gamma}\bigl[\nabla_{h,w},\nabla_{h,t}\bigr]+\overline{\gamma}\sqrt{-1}\nabla_{h,w}\phi_{h}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\bigl[\partial_{E,h,u},\partial_{E,\lx@text@overline{u}} \bigr]=}{}+\gamma[\nabla_{h,\overline{w}},\nabla_{h,t}]+\gamma\sqrt{-1}\nabla_{h,\overline{w}}\phi-2\sqrt{-1}|\gamma|^{2}\nabla_{h,t}\phi_{h}.

\displaystyle\bigl[\partial_{E,h,u},\partial_{E,\overline{u}}\bigr]=\bigl[\nabla_{h,w},\nabla_{h,\overline{w}}\bigr]+2\sqrt{-1}\overline{\gamma}\nabla_{w}\phi+2\sqrt{-1}\gamma\nabla_{\overline{w}}\phi

\partial_{\widetilde{E},\overline{w}}p^{-1}(u)=p^{-1}(\partial_{E,\overline{w}}u),\qquad\partial_{\widetilde{E},\overline{z}}p^{-1}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot p^{-1}\big(\phi\cdot u+\sqrt{-1}\partial_{E,t}u\big)

For any adapted Hermitian metric

of

,

.

Let
F\big(\widetilde{h}_{0}\big)

denote the curvature of the Chern connection of

\big(\widetilde{E},\overline{\partial}_{\widetilde{E}},\widetilde{h}_{0}\big)
.
Let
\Lambda
denote the contraction
from
(1,1)
-forms to
(0,0)
-forms
with respect to the Kähler form of
(X,g_{X})
.
Then,

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda F\big(\widetilde{h}_{0}\big)=p^{-1}\bigl(G(h_{0})\bigr)

holds.

For any saturated coherent

\mathcal{O}_{X\setminus p^{-1}(Z)}
-submodule

Because of the Chern–Weil formula,

as explained in
[

]
.
Then,

-invariant

The following is clear.

Lemma 3.23

4
A more sophisticated formulation of the stability condition

We explain that the analytic stability condition (Definition
3.9
)
is equivalent to the stability condition
introduced by Kontsevich and Soibelman
in the case
\varrho=0
(see Section
1.2
).
This section is devoted to explain their idea of degree.

4.1
Preliminary

4.1.1
Closed 1-forms and 1-homology

Let
A
be a
3
-dimensional manifold.
Let
Z^{i}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A)
denote the space of
closed
i
-form
\tau
on
A
.
Let
B
be finite subset of
A
.
Let
H_{j}(A,B)
denote the relative
j
-th homology group
with
{\mathbb{R}}
-coefficient.

Let
\gamma
be any element of
H_{1}(A,B)
.
We take a representative of
\gamma

by a smooth
1
-chain
\widetilde{\gamma}
.
For any
\omega\in Z^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A)
,
the number
\int_{\widetilde{\gamma}}\omega

is independent of the choice of
a representative
\widetilde{\gamma}
.
They are denoted by
\int_{\gamma}\omega
.

Let
C^{\infty}(A,B)
denote the space of

C^{\infty}
-functions
f
on
A

such that
f(P)=0
for any
P\in B
.
Let
Z^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A)
denote the space of
closed
1
-forms on
A
.
Let
B^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A,B)
denote the image of

d\colon C^{\infty}(A,B)\longrightarrow Z^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A)
.
Because
\int_{\gamma}df=0

for any
f\in C^{\infty}(A,B)
,
we obtain the well defined map

\int_{\gamma}\colon\ Z^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A)\big/B^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A,B)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}.

4.1.2
Duality

Suppose that
A
is compact and oriented.
Let
H^{j}(A\setminus B)

denote the
j
-th de Rham cohomology group
of
A\setminus B
.
Let
H^{j}_{c}(A\setminus B)

denote the
j
-th de Rham cohomology group
with compact support.
We have the non-degenerate pairing between

H^{2}(A\setminus B)

and
H^{1}_{c}(A\setminus B)

induced by the cup product and the integration.
We also have the non-degenerate pairing
between

H^{1}_{c}(A\setminus B)

and

H_{1}(A,B)

induced by the integration.
Hence, we obtain the isomorphism

\Phi_{A,B}\colon\ H^{2}(A\setminus B)\simeq H_{1}(A,B).

By definition,
for any
a\in H^{2}(A\setminus B)

and
b\in H^{1}_{c}(A\setminus B)
,
the following holds:

\int_{\Phi_{A,B}(a)}b=\int_{A}a\wedge b.

Take any Riemannian metric
g_{A}
of
A
.
For any
j
-form
\tau
on
A\setminus B
,
let
|\tau|_{g_{A}}
denote the function on
A\setminus B

obtained as the norm of
\tau
with respect to
g_{A}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\tau\in Z^{2}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A\setminus B)

such that
|\tau|_{g_{A}}
is an
L^{1}
-function on
A
.
Then, the following holds
for any
\rho\in Z^{1}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A)
:

\int_{\Phi_{A,B}([\tau])}\rho=\int_{A}\rho\wedge\tau.

Here,
[\tau]\in H^{2}_{\mathop{\rm DR}\nolimits}(A\setminus B)

denotes the cohomology class of
\tau
.

Proof.

For any point
P\in Z
,
we take a small coordinate neighbourhood

(A_{P},x_{P,1},x_{P,2},x_{P,3})
of
P
such that
(i)
P
corresponds to
(0,0,0)
,
(ii)
A_{P}\simeq\big\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\,|\,\sum x_{i}^{2}<1\big\}

by the coordinate system.
Set
\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P}\|:=\bigl(x_{P,1}^{2}+x_{P,2}^{2}+x_{P,3}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}
.
Then, there exists a
C^{\infty}
-function
f_{P}

on
A_{P}
such that
(i)
df_{P}=\rho
on
\{\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P}\|<1/2\}
,
(ii)
f_{P}(P)=0
,
(iii)
f_{P}(Q)=0
for
Q\in\{\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P}\|>2/3\}
.
We naturally regard
f_{P}
as a
C^{\infty}
-function on
A
.
Then, the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\Phi_{A,B}([\tau])}\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{\Phi_{A,B}([\tau])}\bigg(\rho-\sum_{P\in B}df_{P}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\bigg(\rho-\sum_{P\in B}df_{P}\bigg)\wedge\tau=\int_{A}\rho\wedge\tau-\sum_{P}\int_{A}d(f_{P}\tau).

For each
P
, we set
S_{P}^{2}(r):=\bigl\{\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P}\|=r\bigr\}

with the orientation as the boundary of

\bigl\{\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P}\|\leq r\bigr\}
.
Then, we obtain the following

\displaystyle\int_{A}d(f_{P}\tau)=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{S_{P}^{2}(\epsilon)}f_{P}\tau.

(4.1)

Note that the limit exists
because
d(f_{P}\tau)=df_{P}\wedge\tau
is integrable.
Because
|\tau|_{g_{A}}
is
L^{1}
,
we have

\int dr\int_{S_{P}^{2}(r)}|\tau|_{g_{A}}<\infty
,
and hence there exists a sequence

r_{i}\to 0
such that

r_{i}\int_{S_{P}^{2}(r_{i})}|\tau|_{g_{A}}\to 0
.
Because
|f_{P}|=O(\|{\boldsymbol{x}}_{P}\|)
,
we obtain
that (
4.1
) is
0
.
∎

4.2
Relation between degrees of mini-holomorphic bundles

Let
\mathcal{M}
be as in Section
3
.
We may naturally regard
\mathcal{M}

as a
3
-dimensional abelian Lie group.
Let
\mathfrak{T}
denote the space of the invariant
vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\mathfrak{T}^{\lor}
denote the space of
the invariant
1
-forms on
\mathcal{M}
.
We have the natural non-degenerate paring

\mathfrak{T}\otimes\mathfrak{T}^{\lor}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
.
We have the dual morphism

{\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{T}^{\lor}\otimes\mathfrak{T}
.
Let
\sigma
denote the image of
1
.
If we take a base
e_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
of
\mathfrak{T}

and the dual frame
e_{i}^{\lor}

(i=1,2,3)
,
then
\sigma=\sum e_{i}^{\lor}\otimes e_{i}
.
For the mini-complex coordinate

(t,w)
,
we have

\sigma=dt\otimes\partial_{t}+dw\otimes\partial_{w}+d\overline{w}\otimes\partial_{\overline{w}}
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on
\mathcal{M}\setminus Z
.
Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
2
]

introduced the following element:

\int_{\Phi_{\mathcal{M},Z}(c_{1}(E))}\sigma\in\mathfrak{T}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varrho=\varrho_{0}\,dt\,d\overline{w}
be a
2
-form on
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
be a
\varrho
-twisted mini-holomorphic bundle with Dirac type singularity on
(\mathcal{M};Z)
. Then,

\int_{\Phi_{\mathcal{M},Z}(c_{1}(E))}\sigma=\frac{1}{\pi}\deg^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}(E)\cdot\partial_{t}-\frac{\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)}{\pi}\left(\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\varrho_{0}\bigg)\partial_{w}+\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\varrho_{0}}\bigg)\partial_{\overline{w}}\right).

In particular,
if
\varrho=0
,
then the following holds:

\int_{\Phi_{\mathcal{M},Z}(c_{1}(E))}\sigma=\frac{1}{\pi}\deg^{\mathop{\rm an}\nolimits}(E)\cdot\partial_{t}.

Proof.

Let
h
be an adapted metric of
\big(E,\overline{\partial}_{E}\big)
.
By Lemma
4.1
,
it is enough to prove the following equality:

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits F(h)\cdot\sigma=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits G(h)\mathop{\rm dvol}\nolimits_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2} \int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits F(h)\cdot\sigma=}{}-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)\left(\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\varrho_{0}\bigg)\partial_{w}+\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\varrho_{0}}\bigg)\partial_{\overline{w}}\right).

(4.2)

For
\kappa=t,w,\overline{w}
,
we obtain the following by the Stokes formula
and the estimate
|\phi_{h,Q}|_{h}=O\bigl(d(P,Q)^{-1}\bigr)
:

\displaystyle\int\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\bigl(\nabla_{h,\kappa}\phi_{h}\bigr)\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}dt\,dw\,d\overline{w}=0.

(4.3)

Note that

F(h)_{t\overline{w}}+\sqrt{-1}\nabla_{\overline{w}}\phi=\varrho_{0}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}

and

F(h)_{tw}-\sqrt{-1}\nabla_{w}\phi=-\overline{\varrho_{0}}\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
,
according to Lemma
2.12
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits F(h)\,dw\otimes\partial_{w}

\displaystyle=\int\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\bigl(F(h)_{t\overline{w}}+\sqrt{-1}\nabla_{\overline{w}}\phi\bigr)\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}dt\,d\overline{w}\,dw\otimes\partial_{w}

\displaystyle=-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\varrho_{0}\bigg)\partial_{w}.

Similarly, we obtain

\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits F(h)\,d\overline{w}\otimes\partial_{\overline{w}}=-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(E)\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\overline{\varrho_{0}}\bigg)\partial_{\overline{w}}.

We also obtain the following from (
4.3
):

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits F(h)_{w\overline{w}}\,dw\,d\overline{w}\,dt\otimes\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=\int\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits\left(F(h)_{w\overline{w}}-\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\nabla_{h,t}\phi_{h}\right)\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\,dw\,d\overline{w}\,dt\,\otimes\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits G(h)\bigg)\partial_{t}.

Thus, we obtain (
4.2
), and the proof of Proposition
4.2
is completed.
∎

formulated the stability condition for mini-holomorphic bundles in terms of the coefficient of

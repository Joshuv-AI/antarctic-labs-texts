Rigidity of projective symmetric manifolds of Picard number 1 associated to composition algebras
Yifei Chen, Baohua Fu, Qifeng Li

Abstract




Abstract.

To each complex composition algebra
{\mathbb A}
is associated a projective
symmetric manifold
X({\mathbb A})
of Picard number
1
, which is just a
smooth hyperplane section of one of the following varieties:

\mathop{\rm Lag}\nolimits(3,6),\quad\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits(3,6),\quad{\mathbb S}_{6},\quad E_{7}/P_{7}.

In this paper, we prove that these varieties are rigid; namely, for any smooth family of projective manifolds over a connected base, if one fiber is isomorphic to
X({\mathbb A})
, then every fiber is isomorphic to
X({\mathbb A})
.

Keywords.
Deformation rigidity, symmetric varieties, composition algebras

1. Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over the complex number field. A smooth projective variety
X
is said to be
rigid
if for any smooth projective family over a connected base with one fiber isomorphic to
X
, all fibers are isomorphic to
X
. It is a difficult and subtle problem to prove the rigidity. Even for rational homogeneous varieties
G/P
of Picard number
1
, the rigidity does not always hold. To wit, let
B_{3}/P_{2}
be the variety of lines on a
5
-dimensional smooth hyperquadric
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
. An explicit family specializing
B_{3}/P_{2}
to a smooth projective
G_{2}
-variety is constructed by Pasquier and Perrin in
[
PP10
]
. In
[
HL23
]
, it is shown that this is the only smooth non-isomorphic specialization of
B_{3}/P_{2}
. It turns out that
B_{3}/P_{2}
is the only exception among all
G/P
of Picard number
1
, as shown by the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
cf.

[
Hwa97
,
HM98
,
HM02
,
HM05
]
)
.

A rational homogeneous variety of Picard number
1
is rigid except in the case of
B_{3}/P_{2}
.

The key ingredient for the proof is the VMRT theory developed by Hwang and Mok. In the simplest case of a projective manifold
X
covered by lines (which is the case for our paper), the VMRT
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subset{\mathbb P}T_{x}X
at a general point
x
is just the Hilbert scheme of lines through
x
. This projective subvariety
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}\subset{\mathbb P}T_{x}X
encodes a lot of global geometry of
X
, and in some cases, we can even recognize
X
from its VMRT at general points.

As
G/P
is locally rigid, we only need to prove that
G/P
is rigid under specialization; namely, for a smooth projective family
{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
such that
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\simeq G/P
for all
t\neq 0
, we have
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}\simeq G/P
. The proof essentially consists of two steps: the first is to show the VMRT of
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is isomorphic to that of
G/P
, and the second is to use the recognization of
G/P
from its VMRT.

In many cases, the invariance of the VMRT can be proved, while the recognization problem is in general much more difficult. In
[
Par16
]
, it is observed that for odd Lagrangian Grassmannians (which are not homogeneous), one can directly show that
H^{1}({\mathcal{X}}_{0},T_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}})=0
by using VMRT theory. Hence
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is locally rigid and isomorphic to nearby fibers, which proves the rigidity for odd Lagrangian Grassmannians.

The goal of this paper is to prove the rigidity for projective symmetric varieties associated to composition algebras. Recall that there are exactly four complex composition algebras:
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb C}\oplus{\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb H}_{\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb O}_{\mathbb C}
. To such an
{\mathbb A}
, we can associate algebraic groups
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
and
\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
with an involution
\theta
such that
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})^{\theta}=\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
. The quotient
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})/\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
is a symmetric homogeneous space, which admits a unique smooth equivariant completion of Picard number
1
, denoted by
X({\mathbb A})
. It turns out that
X({\mathbb A})
is a smooth hyperplane section of one of the following varieties (
cf.

[
Ruz10
]
):

\mathop{\rm Lag}\nolimits(3,6),\quad\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits(3,6),\quad{\mathbb S}_{6},\quad E_{7}/P_{7},

where
\mathop{\rm Lag}\nolimits(3,6)
is the Lagrangian Grassmannian associated to
{\mathbb C}^{6}
and
{\mathbb S}_{6}
is the
15
-dimensional spinor variety. The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any complex composition algebra
{\mathbb A}
, the variety
X({\mathbb A})
is rigid.

We first remark that for
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb C}
,
X({\mathbb A})
is a Mukai variety, so its smooth deformation is again a Mukai variety, hence again a hyperplane section of
\mathop{\rm Lag}\nolimits(3,6)
by the classification of Mukai varieties. This shows that
X({\mathbb A})
is rigid in this case. We will assume
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
in the following.

The rigidity problem of
X({\mathbb A})
was studied by Kim and Park in
[
KP19
]
. When
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb H}_{\mathbb C}
or
{\mathbb O}_{\mathbb C}
, they prove the invariance of the VMRT and, moreover, observe that
\dim H^{1}({\mathcal{X}}_{0},T_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}})\leq 1
. If
H^{1}({\mathcal{X}}_{0},T_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}})=0
, then
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is locally rigid, and thus it is isomorphic to nearby fibers
X({\mathbb A})
. When
\dim H^{1}({\mathcal{X}}_{0},T_{{\mathcal{X}}_{0}})=1
,
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of the vector group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
with
n=\dim X({\mathbb A})
. With the help of
[
Wiś91
]
, this result can be easily extended to the case
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb C}\oplus{\mathbb C}
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we will exclude the case of equivariant compactifications. Let
{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
be a specialization of
X({\mathbb A})
;
i.e.

{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\simeq X({\mathbb A})
for all
t\neq 0
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
. The Lie algebra of the automorphism group of the central fiber is given by
\mathfrak{aut}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}\rtimes(\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\oplus\mathbb{C})
. We will consider a family of tori
{\mathbf{H}}_{t}\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{t})
induced from a maximal torus of
\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
and then take a connected component
{\mathcal{Y}}
of the torus-fixed locus
{\mathcal{X}}^{\mathbf{H}}
. As the rank of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
is
2
more than that of
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, there is an extra
2
-dimensional torus acting on
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
for
t\neq 0
. It turns out that
{\mathcal{Y}}\to\Delta
is a family of smooth projective surfaces with general fiber
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
isomorphic to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
along three coordinate points. The central fiber
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
. By delicate computations, we will show that
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
is isomorphic to the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
along three colinear points. On the other hand, the involution
\theta
on
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
induces an involution
\Theta
on the family
{\mathcal{Y}}/\Delta
, which preserves the boundaries of
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
for all
t
. It turns out that the involution
\Theta_{0}\colon{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}\to{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
sends extremal rays of the Mori cone
\overline{\NE}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
to non-extremal rays, which gives a contradiction.

2. Projective symmetric manifolds of Picard number 1 associated to composition algebras

2.1. Composition algebras and associated Lie groups

Let
{\mathbb A}_{\mathbb R}
be one of the four real normed division algebras,
{\mathbb R}
,
{\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb H}
,
{\mathbb O}
, which admits an involution
x\mapsto\bar{x}
, called conjugation. It is well known that the fixed points under this conjugation are exactly the base field
{\mathbb R}
. Note that
{\mathbb H}
,
{\mathbb O}
are non-commutative and
\overline{ab}=\bar{b}\bar{a}
for all
a,b\in{\mathbb A}_{\mathbb R}
.

Let
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb A}_{\mathbb R}\otimes_{\mathbb R}{\mathbb C}
be the complexification of
{\mathbb A}_{\mathbb R}
, which is one of the following:
{\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb C}\oplus{\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb H}_{\mathbb C}
,
{\mathbb O}_{\mathbb C}
. The algebra structure on
{\mathbb A}
is given by
(a\otimes c,a^{\prime}\otimes c^{\prime})\mapsto aa^{\prime}\otimes cc^{\prime}
for multiplication and
\overline{a\otimes c}=\bar{a}\otimes\bar{c}
for conjugation. Note that the conjugation fixes exactly elements in
{\mathbb C}
. It turns out that
{\mathbb A}
is a composition algebra and any finite-dimensional composition algebra over
{\mathbb C}
is isomorphic to one of these

{\mathbb A}
(see for example
[
VGO90
, Chapter 5, Section 1]
).

We consider the following vector space of
{\mathbb A}
-Hermitian matrices of order
3
with coefficients in
{\mathbb A}
:

{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&\bar{x}_{3}&\bar{x}_{2}\\
x_{3}&r_{2}&\bar{x}_{1}\\
x_{2}&x_{1}&r_{3}\end{pmatrix},r_{i}\in{\mathbb C},x_{i}\in{\mathbb A}\right\}.

It turns out that
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
has the structure of a Jordan algebra with multiplication given by
A\circ B=\frac{1}{2}(AB+BA)
, where
AB
is the usual matrix multiplication. The comatrix of
A\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
is defined as

\com(A)=A^{2}-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A)A+\tfrac{1}{2}((\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A))^{2}-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{2}))\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits.

Then there exists a degree
3
polynomial
\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)
(called the determinant of
A
) such that
\com(A)\circ A=\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
. From this equality, we can easily deduce that

\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)=\tfrac{1}{3}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{3})-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A)\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{2})+\tfrac{1}{6}(\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A))^{3}.

For
A=\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&\bar{x}_{3}&\bar{x}_{2}\\
x_{3}&r_{2}&\bar{x}_{1}\\
x_{2}&x_{1}&r_{3}\end{pmatrix}
, we have the following explicit formulae:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A)=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i}r_{i},\quad\quad\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{2})=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\left(r_{i}^{2}+2x_{i}\bar{x}_{i}\right),

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{3})=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i}\left(r_{i}^{3}+3\sumop\displaylimits_{j\neq i}r_{i}x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}\right)+\left(x_{1}x_{3}\bar{x}_{2}+\bar{x}_{2}x_{1}x_{3}+x_{3}\bar{x}_{2}x_{1}+x_{2}\bar{x}_{3}\bar{x}_{1}+\bar{x}_{3}\bar{x}_{1}x_{2}+\bar{x}_{1}x_{2}\bar{x}_{3}\right).

It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)=

\displaystyle r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}-r_{1}x_{1}\bar{x}_{1}-r_{2}x_{2}\bar{x}_{2}-r_{3}x_{3}\bar{x}_{3}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{3}(x_{1}x_{3}\bar{x}_{2}+\bar{x}_{2}x_{1}x_{3}+x_{3}\bar{x}_{2}x_{1}+x_{2}\bar{x}_{3}\bar{x}_{1}+\bar{x}_{3}\bar{x}_{1}x_{2}+\bar{x}_{1}x_{2}\bar{x}_{3}).

Let us have a closer look at the
{\mathbb C}
-valued polynomial
\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits
on
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
. For
A,B,C\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, define

\displaystyle A\times B=\tfrac{1}{2}(2A\circ B-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A)B-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(B)A+(\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A)\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(B)-\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A\circ B))\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits),

\displaystyle(A,B,C)=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A\circ(B\times C)).

Then it follows that

\com(A)=A\times A,\quad\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)=\tfrac{1}{3}(A,A,A)\ {\text{ and }}\ \com(A)\times\com(A)=\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)A.

We now define the following two subgroups of
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{\mathbb C}({\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A}))
:

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

\displaystyle=\{g\in\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{\mathbb C}({\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A}))|\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(g(A))=\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A),\forall A\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})\}

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

\displaystyle=\{g\in\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})|\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(g(A)^{2})=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{2}),\forall A\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})\}.

The following table gives the corresponding groups:

{\mathbb A}

\quad{\mathbb C}

\quad{\mathbb C}\oplus{\mathbb C}

\quad{\mathbb H}_{\mathbb C}

\quad{\mathbb O}_{\mathbb C}

\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

\quad\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}

\quad\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}\times\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}

\quad\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{6}

\quad E_{6}

\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

\quad\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}

\quad\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}

\quad\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits_{6}

\quad F_{4}

Consider the two matrices

M_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad M_{23}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
M_{12}^{2}=M_{23}^{2}=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
. Define

\sigma_{12}\colon{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A}),\quad\quad A\longmapsto M_{12}AM_{12}.

Similarly, we can define
\sigma_{23}
by using
M_{23}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The two elements
\sigma_{12},\sigma_{23}
are in
\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
, and the subgroup
\langle\sigma_{12},\sigma_{23}\rangle
generated by them is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.

Proof.

For
A=\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&\bar{x}_{3}&\bar{x}_{2}\\
x_{3}&r_{2}&\bar{x}_{1}\\
x_{2}&x_{1}&r_{3}\end{pmatrix}
, we have

\sigma_{12}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}r_{2}&x_{3}&\bar{x}_{1}\\
\bar{x}_{3}&r_{1}&\bar{x}_{2}\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&r_{3}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad\sigma_{23}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&\bar{x}_{2}&\bar{x}_{3}\\
x_{2}&r_{3}&x_{1}\\
x_{3}&\bar{x}_{1}&r_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Now it is straightforward to check that
\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(\sigma_{12}(A))=\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(A)
and
\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(\sigma_{12}(A)^{2})=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A^{2})
by using previous explicit formulae; hence
\sigma_{12}\in\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
. Similarly, we can show
\sigma_{23}\in\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
. By regarding
\sigma_{12}
and
\sigma_{23}
as the permutations
(12)
and
(23)
in
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, respectively, we have
\langle\sigma_{12},\sigma_{23}\rangle=\mathfrak{S}_{3}\subset\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
\sigma\cdot\diag(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=\diag(r_{\sigma(1)},r_{\sigma(2)},r_{\sigma(3)})\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}
and any diagonal matrix
\diag(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
.

2.2. The involution on Lie algebras

There exists an involution
\theta\colon\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})\to\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
coming from the symmetry of the Dynkin diagram of
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
, which satisfies
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})^{\theta}=\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
. The quotient
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})/\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
is a symmetric homogeneous space. The involution
\theta
induces an involution (still denoted by
\theta
) on
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
whose fixed locus is
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
. We have the following description of these Lie algebras:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})

\displaystyle=\{\phi\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A}))|(\phi(B),B,B)=0,\forall B\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})\}.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})

\displaystyle=\{\psi\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A}))|\psi(B\circ C)=\psi(B)\circ C+B\circ\psi(C),\forall B,C\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})\}.

The following result is well known, but we include a proof here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
be the vector subspace of
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
consisting of traceless elements.

(i)

The map
\mu\colon{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}\to\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits({\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A}))
given by
A\mapsto[B\mapsto 2A\circ B]
embeds
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
into
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
.

(ii)

The involution
\theta
acts on
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
by
-1
, and we have the decomposition into
\theta
-eigenvector spaces

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})=\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\oplus{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
.

Proof.

For
A,B,C\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, it is straightforward to show that

\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits((A\circ B)\circ C)=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A\circ(B\circ C)).

Taking
A\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
and
B\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, one gets

\displaystyle(A\circ B,B,B)=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits((A\circ B)\circ\com(B))=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A\circ(B\circ\com(B)))=\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(B)\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits(A)=0.

It follows that
\mu(A)\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
. As
\mu(A)(\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits)=2A
, the map
\mu
is injective. This shows (
i
).

For (
ii
), we first check
\mu({\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0})\cap\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})=0
. Assume
\mu(A)\in\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
; then
A\circ(B\circ C)=(A\circ B)\circ C+B\circ(A\circ C)
for all
B,C
. We may take
B=C=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
, which shows that
A=0
. By a dimension check, we get
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})=\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\oplus{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
. As the first part is the
\theta
-eigenspace of 1, the second part is the
\theta
-eigenspace of
-1
.
∎

The subspace
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
is not necessarily a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
. On the other hand, we do have the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)

The subset
T_{0}:=\{\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})\mid\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}=1\}
of
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
is a group under the Jordan algebra structure of
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, which is isomorphic as a group to the
2
-dimensional torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
.

(ii)

The map

\displaystyle\nu\colon

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\longmapsto[B\longmapsto ABA]

is an injective homomorphism of groups, and the associated homomorphism of Lie algebras is
\mu|_{{\mathfrak h}_{0}}\colon{\mathfrak h}_{0}\to\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, where
{\mathfrak h}_{0}=\{\diag(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\mid t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}=0\}\subset{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
.
In particular, via
\nu
and
\mu
we can regard
T_{0}
as a 2-dimensional torus of
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak h}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
.

(iii)

Take any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}\subset\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
. Then the inner automorphism and the adjoint representation of
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
give rise to
\mathop{\rm Inn}\nolimits_{\sigma}(T_{0})=T_{0}
and
\Ad_{\sigma}({\mathfrak h}_{0})={\mathfrak h}_{0}
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Inn}\nolimits_{\sigma}\colon

\displaystyle T_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{0}

\displaystyle\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\diag(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\lambda_{\sigma(2)},\lambda_{\sigma(3)}),

\displaystyle\Ad_{\sigma}\colon

\displaystyle{\mathfrak h}_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathfrak h}_{0}

\displaystyle\diag(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\diag(t_{\sigma(1)},t_{\sigma(2)},t_{\sigma(3)}).

Proof.

(i)

One has
A_{1}\circ A_{2}=A_{1}A_{2}=A_{2}A_{1}
for
A_{1},A_{2}\in T_{0}
. Hence
T_{0}
is an abelian group. It follows that the group structure is the same as that of the
2
-dimensional torus.

(ii)

Assume
A\in\ker(\nu)
; then
B=ABA
for any
B\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}(A)
, which implies that

A^{2}=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
and
BA=(ABA)A=AB
. This implies
A={\pm\rm Id}
. Since
A\in T_{0}
, we get
A=\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
. The assertion on Lie algebras follows immediately.

(iii)

For
A=\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})\in T_{0}
(viewed as an element in
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

via

\nu
) and
B\in{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, we have

\mathop{\rm Inn}\nolimits_{\sigma_{12}}(A)(B)=(\sigma_{12}A\sigma_{12})(B)=\sigma_{12}A(M_{12}BM_{12})=M_{12}AM_{12}BM_{12}AM_{12}=\sigma_{12}(A)\cdot B.

It follows that
\mathop{\rm Inn}\nolimits_{\sigma_{12}}(A)=\sigma_{12}(A)=\diag(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3})\in T_{0}
. Similarly, we have
\mathop{\rm Inn}\nolimits_{\sigma_{23}}(A)=\sigma_{23}(A)=\diag(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{2})\in T_{0}
. Consequently,
\mathop{\rm Inn}\nolimits_{\sigma}(A)=\diag(\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\lambda_{\sigma(2)},\lambda_{\sigma(3)})\in T_{0}
for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak l}=\{v\in\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})|[v,{\mathfrak h}_{0}]=0\}
be the centralizer of
{\mathfrak h}_{0}
. Then there exists a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak h}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
such that
{\mathfrak h}_{0}\subset{\mathfrak h}\subset{\mathfrak l}
,
\theta({\mathfrak h})={\mathfrak h}
,
{\mathfrak h}_{0}={\mathfrak h}\cap{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}

is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})

Proof.

By Lemma

{\mathfrak h}_{0}
is the Lie algebra of a torus in

is

-stable,

is
\theta
-stable. There is a direct sum decomposition into
\theta
-eigenspaces

{\mathfrak h}_{1}
be any Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak l}\cap\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})

(hence of

\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})

and

, which implies that
D:=ABC+CBA-BAC-CAB=0

and called the
variety of minimal rational tangents
(VMRT) at the point
x
. By
[
HM04
, Theorem 1]
and
[
Keb02
, Theorem 3.4]
, composing
\iota_{x}
with the normalization map
{\mathcal{K}}_{x}^{\rm n}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{x}
yields the normalization of
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
. Also,
{\mathcal{K}}_{x}^{\rm n}
is a union of components of the variety
\mathop{\rm RatCurves}\nolimits^{\rm n}(x,X)
defined in
[
Kol96
, II.(2.11.2)]
and hence is smooth for
x\in X
general by
[
Kol96
, Corollary II.3.11.5]
. In this case,
{\mathcal{U}}_{x}\simeq{\mathcal{K}}_{x}^{\rm n}
is smooth, and the rational map
\iota_{x}
induces a birational morphism
{\mathcal{U}}_{x}\simeq{\mathcal{K}}_{x}^{\rm n}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
, which is still denoted by
\iota_{x}
by abuse of notation. Since
{\mathcal{U}}_{x}
is both the normalization of
{\mathcal{K}}_{x}
and that of
{\mathcal{C}}_{x}
, we call
{\mathcal{U}}_{x}
the normalized Chow space or the normalized VMRT.

By Proposition
2.8
, the variety
X({\mathbb A})
is covered by lines, and its VMRT at a general point is just the variety of lines through that point, denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A})\subset{\mathbb P}(V_{\mathbb A})
with
V_{\mathbb A}
being the tangent space of
X({\mathbb A})
at a general point, which is respectively
\nu_{2}({\mathbb Q}^{1})
,
{\mathbb P}T^{*}_{{\mathbb P}^{2}}
,
\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits_{\omega}(2,6)
,
F_{4}/P_{1}
with the natural embedding.

For a family of smooth projective varieties
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
with
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\simeq X({\mathbb A})
for all
t\neq 0
, we take a general section
\tau\colon\Delta\to{\mathcal{X}}
such that
\tau(t)
is a general point in
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
for all
t\in\Delta
. By considering the VMRT of
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
at
\tau(t)
, we get a family of embedded projective subvarieties with general fibers isomorphic to
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A})\subset{\mathbb P}(V_{\mathbb A})
.

We first prove the invariance of the VMRT, which means that the central fiber has the same VMRT as the general fiber. Note that the cases when
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb H}_{\mathbb C}
or
{\mathbb O}_{\mathbb C}
are proved in
[
KP19
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we include the proof for all three cases here.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
. Consider a family of smooth projective varieties
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
with
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\simeq X({\mathbb A})
for all
t\neq 0
. Then the VMRT of
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
at a general point is projectively equivalent to the VMRT of
X({\mathbb A})
at a general point.

Proof.

Firstly we show that the normalized Chow space
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{0}}
of
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
at a general point
x_{0}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A})
. Take a general section
t\in\Delta\mapsto x_{t}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
of
\pi
passing through the general point
x_{0}
in
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
. Shrinking
\Delta
if necessary, we can assume that
x_{t}
is general in
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
for each
t\neq 0
. The normalized Chow spaces
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{t}}
along this section give a family of smooth projective varieties such that
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{t}}\simeq{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A})
for
t\neq 0
. If
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}\oplus{\mathbb C}
, then
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{0}}\simeq{\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A})
by Theorem
1.1
. Now assume
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb C}\oplus{\mathbb C}
. Consider the normalization map
\iota_{x_{t}}\colon{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{t}}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{x_{t}}\subset{\mathbb P}T_{x_{t}}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
. Note that for any
t\neq 0
, we have
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{t}}\simeq{\mathbb P}T^{*}_{{\mathbb P}^{2}}
, thus
K_{{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{t}}}+\iota_{x_{t}}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}(2)=0
. It follows that this equality also holds for
t=0
, which implies that
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{0}}
is a Fano threefold with index
2
and we can apply

[
Wiś91
, Theorem]
to deduce that
{\mathcal{U}}_{x_{0}}\simeq{\mathbb P}(T^{*}_{{\mathbb P}^{2}})
(note that in
[
Wiś91
]
, the projectivisation is taken in the sense of Grothendieck).

Recall that for a smooth projective subvariety
Z\subset{\mathbb P}V
, the variety of tangential lines of
Z
is the subvariety
{\mathcal{T}}_{Z}\subset\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits(2,V)\subset{\mathbb P}(\wedge^{2}V)
consisting of the tangential lines of
Z
. By
[
FL20
, Lemma 2.12]
, the tangential variety of
{\mathbb P}(T^{*}_{{\mathbb P}^{2}})
is non-degenerate. By
[
Hwa01
, Proposition 2.6]
, the tangential varieties of
\mathop{\rm Gr}\nolimits_{\omega}(2,6)
and
F_{4}/P_{1}
are both non-degenerate. Now we can use the same argument as that of
[
FL20
, Proposition 3.9]
to conclude the proof.
∎

Recall that a vector group of dimension
g
is the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{g}
. An equivariant compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{g}
is a smooth projective
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{g}
-variety

which admits an open
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{g}
-orbit

isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{g}
. The boundary
\partial Y=Y\setminus O
is a union of irreducible reduced divisors
\cup_{j}E_{j}
. It follows that
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y)=\oplus_{j}\mathbb{Z}[E_{j}]
. Moreover, we have
-K_{Y}=\sumop\displaylimits_{j}a_{j}E_{j}
with
a_{j}\geq 2
by
[
HT99
, Theorem 2.7]
. In particular, the support of
-K_{Y}
is the whole boundary of
Y
.

By Proposition
2.8
, we have
\mathfrak{aut}(X({\mathbb A}))=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
. Note that
\mathfrak{aut}({\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A}))=\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
, which implies that

(3.1)

\dim\mathfrak{aut}(X({\mathbb A}))=\dim\mathfrak{aut}({\mathcal{C}}({\mathbb A}))+\dim X({\mathbb A}).

For a specialization
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
of
X({\mathbb A})
, equality (
3.1
) implies the following precise information on the central fiber by the proofs of
[
FL20
, Lemma 4.6 and Corollary 4.8]
. This result extends
[
KP19
, Theorem 1.1]
to
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
.
Consider a family of smooth projective varieties
\pi\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
with
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\simeq X({\mathbb A})
for all
t\neq 0
. Then

(i)

either
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}\simeq X({\mathbb A})
,

(ii)

or
\mathfrak{aut}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}\rtimes(\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\oplus\mathbb{C})
and
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
with
n=\dim X({\mathbb A})
.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to exclude case (
ii
) in Proposition
3.2
. In the following, we will assume case (
ii
) to deduce a contradiction.

3.2. Reduction to a family of surfaces

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\pi_{*}T_{{\mathcal{X}}/\Delta}
, which is a vector bundle over
\Delta
such that
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}\simeq\mathfrak{aut}({\mathcal{X}}_{t})\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb A})
for
t\neq 0
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
be the subbundle such that
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}\simeq\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
for
t\neq 0
, which is the Lie algebra of the stabilizer of
\tau(t)\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
. It follows that
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}\simeq\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
. For each
t\in\Delta
, the fiber
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
is a completely reducible
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
-module, which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\oplus{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
. By a dimension check,
{\mathcal{V}}_{0}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\subset\mathfrak{aut}({\mathcal{X}}_{0})
. Our construction and argument here for
{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{W}}
is an analogue of the proof of
[
FL20
, Lemma 4.11]
.

We fix a family of Cartan subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}\subset{\mathcal{W}}
;
i.e.

{\mathcal{H}}_{t}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}
for all
t
. Consider
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
defined by

\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t}:=\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}_{t}|[v,{\mathcal{H}}_{t}]=0\}.

It follows that
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
for all
t\neq 0
, by Lemma
2.6
. Note that
\rk(\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}})=\rk({\mathcal{H}})+2
(as
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
).

For
t\in\Delta
, let
{\textbf{H}}_{t}=\exp({\mathcal{H}}_{t})\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{t})_{\tau(t)}
, which is a family of tori. Set
\tilde{{\textbf{H}}}_{t}=\exp(\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t})\subset\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{t})
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
. Let
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
be the connected component of the fixed locus
{\mathcal{X}}^{\textbf{H}}
along the section
\tau(\Delta)\subset{\mathcal{X}}
.

(i)

The map
{\mathcal{Y}}\to\Delta
is a smooth family of projective surfaces.

(ii)

For each
t\in\Delta
,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is the closure of
\tilde{{\textbf{H}}}_{t}\cdot\tau(t)
in
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
.

(iii)

When
t\neq 0
,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is isomorphic to
Y({\mathbb A})
, the blowup of
{\mathbb P}^{2}
along three coordinate points.

(iv)

The inclusion
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is a smooth equivariant compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
.

Proof.

By Białynicki-Birula’s theorem on torus actions
[
Bia73
]
, the map
{\mathcal{Y}}\to\Delta
is a smooth family of projective varieties. For each
t\in\Delta
, the representation of
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}\simeq\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
on
T_{\tau(t)}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
coincides with that on
{\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
, and the subspace
T_{\tau(t)}{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is contained in the
{\textbf{H}}_{t}
-eigenspace of weight zero, which is of dimension
2
. It follows that
\dim{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}\leq 2
. Since
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t}\cdot\tau(t)\subset{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
, we have
\dim{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}\geq\dim\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t}\cdot\tau(t)=\dim(\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t}/{\mathcal{H}}_{t})=2
. Then
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is the closure of
\tilde{{\textbf{H}}}_{t}\cdot\tau(t)
in
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
, and it is a projective surface. This proves (
i
) and (
ii
).

By Proposition
2.7
, when
t\neq 0
, the projective surface
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is isomorphic to the closure of
T_{0}\cdot o
in
X({\mathbb A})
. Then (
iii
) follows from Lemma
2.9
. By the structure of
{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
,
\tilde{{\textbf{H}}}_{0}
is the semi-direct product of the torus
{\textbf{H}}_{0}
and a vector group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
. Then (
iv
) follows from (
ii
).
∎

It follows that the
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
are quasi-homogeneous for all
t\in\Delta
. Denote by
\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
the boundary,
i.e.
the complement of the open orbit. Let
\partial{\mathcal{Y}}
be the closure of
\cup_{t\neq 0}\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
, and let
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{t}
be the fiber of
\partial{\mathcal{Y}}
over
t\in\Delta
.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{t}=\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
as sets for each
t\in\Delta
.

Proof.

When
t\neq 0
, it is immediate from the construction that
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{t}=\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
. The subvariety
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{t}\subset{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is stable under the vector fields in
\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{t}
for
t\neq 0
. By continuity, this is also the case for
t=0
. Consequently,
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{0}
has no intersection with the open orbit
\tilde{{\textbf{H}}}_{t}\cdot\tau(0)
on
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
, implying
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{0}\subset\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
as sets.

Since
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{t}=\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
is the anticanonical divisor on
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
when
t\neq 0
,
-K_{{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}}
is given by the divisor
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{0}
(as a scheme-theoretic divisor, so each irreducible component has a multiplicity). As
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of a vector group, the support of its
{\mathbb G}_{a}^{2}
-stable anticanonical divisor is the whole boundary by

HT99
, Theorem 2.7]
. It follows that
(\partial{\mathcal{Y}})_{0}=\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
as sets.
∎

In the following, we will construct an involution that acts well on
\mathcal{Y}/\Delta
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There is a direct sum decomposition of vector bundles
{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{W}}\oplus\mathcal{M}
over
\Delta
which is a direct sum decomposition of irreducible
\mathfrak{so}_{3}(\mathbb{A})
-modules for all
t\in\Delta
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}\cong\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
for all
t\in\Delta
, while
\mathcal{M}_{t}={\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
for
t\neq 0
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the radical of the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
, where
n=\dim X(\mathbb{A})
.

Proof.

For each
t\neq 0
,
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}\cong\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
is contained in the isotropic subalgebra at
\tau(t)\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
, and
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}/{\mathcal{W}}_{t}
is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
isomorphic to the representation
T_{\tau(t)}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
of the isotropic subalgebra. For each
t\in\Delta
, the evaluation of vector fields at
\tau(t)\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
gives rise to an injective homomorphism of
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}
-modules
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}/{\mathcal{W}}_{t}\to T_{\tau(t)}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
, whence
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}/{\mathcal{W}}_{t}
is isomorphic to the irreducible
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
-module

by Proposition
3.1
and by the fact that
\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{t}/{\mathcal{W}}_{t}=\dim{\mathcal{X}}_{t}

, the module
{\mathcal{V}}_{t}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})\oplus T_{o}X({\mathbb A})
. Since the two direct summands

and

are irreducible modules that are not isomorphic to each other, this decomposition is unique. Then we obtain a direct sum decomposition of the holomorphic family

of
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
-modules

{\mathcal{M}}_{t}={\mathcal{J}}_{3}({\mathbb A})_{0}
. When

,

is already a decomposition into irreducible
\mathfrak{so}_{3}({\mathbb A})
-modules. By the uniqueness of the decomposition,
{\mathcal{M}}_{0}={\mathbb C}^{n}
is the radical of
{\mathcal{V}}_{0}
.
∎

For each
t\in\Delta
, define
\xi_{t}(\phi)=\phi
for

and
\xi_{t}(\phi)=-\phi
for

. This gives an automorphism

of the vector bundle

of order

. When

,
\xi_{t}
is nothing but the involution

.

(i)

The element
\xi\in\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits({\mathcal{V}}/\Delta)
induces an involution
\Theta
of
\mathcal{X}/\Delta
.

(ii)

We have
\tau(\Delta)\subset\mathcal{X}^{\Theta}
and

for

.

(iii)

For each

(

3.3. The central fiber as a blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}

Recall that general fibers of the smooth family

{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}

{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of a vector group, its boundary is of pure codimension

and spans
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits({\mathcal{Y}}_{0})
freely. In particular,
\partial\mathcal{Y}_{0}

F_{0}
,
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
and
F_{3}

\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits({\mathcal{Y}}_{0})=\oplus_{i=0}^{3}{\mathbb Z}[F_{i}]

Denote by
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}
(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}_{j}
) the prime divisor on
{\mathcal{Y}}/\Delta
such that
{\mathcal{D}}_{i,t}=D_{i}
(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}_{j,t}=E_{j}

{\mathcal{D}}_{i,0}

and
{\mathcal{E}}_{j,0}

There is an
\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})
-action on the family
{\mathcal{X}}/\Delta
that is isotropic along the section
\tau(\Delta)

Lemma 3.7

For each

{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}\subset{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
as well as the boundary
\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
are stable under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{3}\subset\mathop{\rm SO}\nolimits_{3}({\mathbb A})

\sigma\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{i}={\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma(i)}

\sigma\cdot{\mathcal{E}}_{j}={\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma(j)}

\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}
and

Proof.

By Proposition
2.10
,
{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
and
\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}
are
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-stable for

{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
and
\partial{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
. Since
\sigma\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{i,t}={\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma(i),t}
and
\sigma\cdot{\mathcal{E}}_{j,t}={\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma(j),t}

\sigma\cdot{\mathcal{D}}_{i}={\mathcal{D}}_{\sigma(i)}

Proposition 3.8

The four irreducible components of

F_{1}
,

For any

Let
{\mathcal{S}}:=\{F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}

\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(\mathcal{Y}_{0})^{\sigma_{12}}

\sigma_{12}

{\mathcal{S}}
consists of three orbits under the

\{F_{3}\}

\{F_{0}\}

Applying the action of
\sigma_{23}

\mathcal{Y}/\Delta

such that
{\mathcal{T}}

Remark 3.14
.

By choosing a family of three points in general position on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
degenerating to three colinear points, we can construct a smooth projective family
\mathcal{Z}/\Delta
such that
\mathcal{Z}_{t}\simeq Y({\mathbb A})
for each
t\neq 0
while
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is a blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
along three colinear points. But in our situation, we have an extra involution
\Theta
which prevents this situation.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The case of
{\mathbb A}={\mathbb C}
follows from the classification of Mukai varieties. Now assume
{\mathbb A}\neq{\mathbb C}
.

Take
{\mathcal{X}}\to\Delta
to be a specialization of
X({\mathbb A})
. Assume that
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is not isomorphic to
X({\mathbb A})
. By Proposition
3.2
,
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}
is an equivariant compactification of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
. By Proposition
3.3
, we have a smooth family of surfaces
{\mathcal{Y}}\to\Delta
with the central fiber
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}
being a
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{2}
-surface. By Proposition
3.13
, we may assume
{\mathcal{D}}_{i,0}=F_{0}+F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, and
{\mathcal{E}}_{j,0}=F_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq 3
(the proof for the other case is similar). By Lemma
2.11
and Proposition
3.6
, the involution
\Theta
satisfies
\Theta({\mathcal{D}}_{i})={\mathcal{E}}_{i}
and
\Theta({\mathcal{E}}_{i})={\mathcal{D}}_{i}
. It follows that
\Theta_{0}(F_{0}+F_{i})=F_{i}
and
\Theta_{0}(F_{i})=F_{0}+F_{i}
. Consider the Mori cone
\overline{\NE}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
, which is the numerical effective cone of curves of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. Since each
F_{i}
has negative self intersection, each
F_{i}
spans an extremal ray of

\overline{\NE}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
. Then
F_{0}+F_{i}
is an interior point of a
2
-dimensional extremal face of
\overline{\NE}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
. Since
\Theta_{0}
induces an isomorphism of the Mori cone
\overline{\NE}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
, it cannot send the extremal ray of
F_{i}
to the non-extremal ray of
F_{0}+F_{i}
. This contradiction shows that
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}\simeq X({\mathbb A})
.
∎

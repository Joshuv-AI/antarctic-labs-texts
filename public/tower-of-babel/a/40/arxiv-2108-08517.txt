Abstract
We extend Polyak’s theorem on the convexity of joint numerical range from three to any number of quadratic forms on condition that they can be generated by three quadratic forms with a positive definite linear combination. Our new result covers the fundamental Dines’s theorem. As applications, we further extend Yuan’s lemma and S-lemma, respectively. Our extended Yuan’s lemma is used to build a more generalized assumption than that of Haeser (J. Optim. Theory Appl. 174(3): 641-649, 2017), under which the standard second-order necessary optimality condition holds at local minimizer. The extended S-lemma reveals strong duality of homogeneous quadratic optimization problem with two bilateral quadratic constraints.

Introduction

The convexity result on the joint numerical range of two quadratic forms in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
dates back to Dines
Dines41
in 1941. As a next milepost,
Brickman
Brickman61
proves in 1961 that the joint numerical range of two quadratic forms over the unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ (n\geq 3)
is convex. In 1998, based on Brickman’s result, Polyak
Polyak98
proves the convexity and closeness of the joint numerical range of three quadratic forms in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ (n\geq 3)
, provided that there exists a positive definite linear combination of the three quadratic forms. Generally, the convexity of the joint numerical range of
m
(
m>3
) quadratic forms remains unknown.
As is observed in
Polyak98
, the existence of a linear positive definite combination of quadratic forms no longer guarantees the convexity for arbitrary
m>3
. To our knowledge, the only known convexity result is that the Hessian matices of the
m
quadratic forms can be simultaneously diagonalized in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

fradkov1973
.

Convexity of the joint numerical range of quadratic forms has many applications, especially in establishing classical alternative results on two quadratic forms including Yuan’s lemma
Yuan90
and S-lemma
polik2007
;
Yakubovich71
.
Both lemmas can be regarded as nonconvex extensions of the celebrated Farkas lemma and then proved by Dines’s theorem
Dines41
.
Yuan’s lemma provides an equivalent characterization on the nonnegativity of the maximum of two quadratic forms. It has applications in characterizing optimality conditions
Baccari04
and minimax optimization
haeser17
. As an extension, Chen and Yuan
chen1999
analyze the nonnegativity of the maximum of three quadratic forms and establish a necessary condition.
Haeser
haeser17
extends Yuan’s lemma to more than two quadratic forms, provided that the rank of the involved Hessian matrices is at most
2
.
The motivation inherits from the extended S-lemma under the same assumption, see
(
polik2007
, Proposition 3.5)
. S-lemma has essential applications in control theory and robust optimization
polik2007
.
As an application of Polyak’s convexity theorem
Polyak98
, Polyak himself generalizes in the same paper a novel S-lemma on three quadratic forms with a positive definite linear combination, see
(
Polyak98
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Our motivation in this paper dates back to Lao Tzu, an ancient Chinese philosopher, who said

the three begets all things of the world.

We first extend Polyak’s convexity theorem to arbitrary
m\ (\geq 1)\
quadratic forms, provided that they can be generated by three quadratic forms with a positive definite linear combination. It covers not only Polyak’s convexity theorem but also the fundamental Dines’s theorem.

One application is to further extend Yuan’s lemma to
m\ (\geq 1)\
quadratic forms. It generalizes the classical Yuan’s lemma and the extended Yuan’s lemma due to Haeser
haeser17
as special cases.
Yuan’s lemma plays a great role in characterizing optimality conditions in nonlinear programming.
As Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification (MFCQ)
Mangasarian67
cannot guarantee that the standard second-order necessary optimality condition holds at the local minimizer
Anitescu00
, more assumptions have to be introduced.
Based on the classical Yuan’s lemma, Baccari and Trad
Baccari04
give such an assumption that the set of Lagrange multiplier is a bounded line segment.
Haeser
haeser17
uses an extension of Yuan’s lemma to generalize
Baccari and Trad’s assumption to the case that the Hessian matrices of the Lagrangian, evaluated at the vertices of the Lagrange multiplier set, form a matrix set with rank at most two. Based on our
further extended Yuan’s lemma, we can relax the rank assumption on the Hessian matrices set from two to three, provided the matrices have a positive definite linear combination. Our result generalizes not only Baccari and Trad’s assumption
Baccari04
but also Haeser’s assumption
haeser17
.

The second application of the extended Polyak’s convexity theorem is to further extend the homogeneous S-lemma and its extension
(
polik2007
, Proposition 3.5)
, and Polyak’s S-lemma
(
Polyak98
, Theorem 4.1)
.
As an application, we establish the strong duality of the homogeneous quadratic optimization with two homogeneous quadratic bilateral constraints, which includes the generalized trust region subproblem with interval bounds and the two-side regularized total least squares problem as special cases.

The remainder of this paper is arranged as follows. Section 2 extends Polyak’s convexity theorem to
m\ (\geq 1)
quadratic forms on condition that their Hessian matrices can be generated by three matrices with a positive definite linear combination. In Section 3, Yuan’s lemma and its extension are further extended. We then establish a more generalized assumption so that the standard second-order necessary optimality condition holds at local minimizer. In Section 4, we further extend the homogeneous S-lemma and its extension. As an application, we establish strong duality of the homogeneous quadratic programming with two quadratic bilateral constraints, which includes the generalized trust region subproblem with interval bounds and the two-side regularized total least squares problem as special cases. We make a conclusion in Section 5.

2
Extended Polyak’s theorem

The following Polyak’s theorem
(
Polyak98
, Theorem 2.1)
reveals the convexity of the joint numerical range of three quadratic forms with a positive definite linear combination.

Theorem 2.1
(Polyak’s theorem)

Let
n\geq 3
and
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
. Suppose that there exists
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that
s_{1}A_{1}+s_{2}A_{2}+s_{3}A_{3}\succ 0
. Then, the joint numerical range
\{(x^{T}A_{1}x,x^{T}A_{2}x,x^{T}A_{3}x):x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}
is an acute closed convex cone.

In this section, we study the joint numerical range of
m\ (m\geq 1)
matrices.
We notice that Polyak’s theorem fails to extend to the case
m\geq 4
even when the
m
matrices have a positive definite linear combination, see the counterexample
(
Polyak98
, Example 3.7)
.

We begin with the definition of matrices generation.

Definition 1

The set of matrices
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{i}:i=1,\cdots,m\}
is generated by
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{j}:j=1,\cdots,k\}
, if there exist
r_{ij}\in{\mathbb{R}}~(i=1,\cdots,m,\ j=1,\cdots,k)
such that
B_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}r_{ij}A_{j}
for
i=1,\cdots,m
.

Definition
1
coincides with “linear expression” in linear algebra, if we regard
\mathcal{S}^{n}
as a linear space of dimension
n(n+1)/2
. We define the rank of
\mathcal{B}
as

{\rm rank}(\mathcal{B})=\min\{k:~\mathcal{B}{\rm\ can\ be\ generated\ by\ }\{B_{i_{1}},\cdots,B_{i_{k}}\}\},

which is equal to the dimension of the subspace generated by
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, not only the rank of
\mathcal{B}
but also the corresponding index set
\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\}
can be efficiently obtained by Gaussian elimination.
\mathcal{A}
is called a basis of
\mathcal{B}
, if
\mathcal{B}
can be generated by
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathcal{A}
is a set of
{\rm rank}(\mathcal{B})
matrices.

Our main result is to show that the joint numerical range of
m
quadratic forms is convex on condition that the
m
Hessian matrices can be generated by three matrices with a positive definite linear combination.

Theorem 2.2

Suppose that
n\geq 3,~m\geq 1
,
\{B_{i}:i=1,\cdots,m\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{n}
can be generated by
\{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\}\subset\mathcal{S}^{n}
, and there exists a vector
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that
s_{1}A_{1}+s_{2}A_{2}+s_{3}A_{3}\succ 0
. Then,

\{(x^{T}B_{1}x,x^{T}B_{2}x,\cdots,x^{T}B_{m}x):~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}

(1)

is a convex cone.

Proof

Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}=\{(x^{T}A_{1}x,x^{T}A_{2}x,x^{T}A_{3}x):~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\},

\displaystyle\Omega=\{(x^{T}B_{1}x,x^{T}B_{2}x,\cdots,x^{T}B_{m}x):~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}.

Since
\{B_{i}:i=1,\cdots,m\}
can be generated by
\{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\}
, there exist
r_{ij}~(i=1,\cdots,m,\ j=1,2,3)
such that

B_{i}=r_{i1}A_{1}+r_{i2}A_{2}+r_{i3}A_{3},~i=1,\cdots,m.

Let
Q=(r_{ij})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times 3}
. Then, we have

\Omega=\Omega_{0}Q^{T}.

(2)

According to Theorem
2.1
,
\Omega_{0}
is a convex cone. Under linear transformation (
2
),
\Omega
remains a convex cone.∎

Remark 1

The basis of
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{i}:~i=1,\cdots,m\}
is not unique. However, if the positive definite linear combination assumption is satisfied for one basis, it holds for any other basis.

Suppose the rank of
\mathcal{B}
is
k
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{j}:~j=1,\cdots,k\}
and
\bar{\mathcal{A}}=\{\bar{A}_{j}:~j=1,\cdots,k\}
be two bases of
\mathcal{B}
. Suppose that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{j}A_{j}\succ 0
holds for some
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, we show in the following that there exists
s^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}s^{\prime}_{j}\bar{A}_{j}\succ 0
holds.

Since
{\rm span}(\mathcal{A})={\rm span}(\bar{\mathcal{A}})={\rm span}(\mathcal{B})
,

\mathcal{A}
can be generated by
\bar{\mathcal{A}}
. That is, there exist
a_{i,j}\ (i,j=1,\cdots,k)
such that

A_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{i,j}\bar{A}_{j}\ {\rm for}\ i=1,\cdots,k.

Then, it holds that

0\prec\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}A_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{i,j}\bar{A}_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}a_{i,j}\right)\bar{A}_{j}.

Letting
s^{\prime}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}a_{i,j}
yields that
\sum_{j=1}^{k}s^{\prime}_{j}\bar{A}_{j}\succ 0
.

Remark 4

Different from Theorem
2.1
, the set (
1
) may be not closed under the condition of Theorem

The following counterexample is motivated by
Juan05

B_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\

0&0&0\end{bmatrix},~B_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}2&-1&0\\

0&0&0\end{bmatrix},

M=\{(x^{T}B_{1}x,x^{T}B_{2}x):~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\}

We claim that

(1,1)\notin M

we have

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}x(k)^{T}B_{i}x(k)=1,~i=1,2.

3
Further extended Yuan’s lemma and its application

Based on Theorem
2.2
, we further extend Yuan’s lemma. As an application, we establish a more generalized assumption to guarantee the standard second-order necessary condition for local minimizer.

We start from the classical Yuan’s lemma
(
Yuan90
, Lemma 2.3)
.

Theorem 3.1
(Yuan’s lemma)

For any
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
, the following two assertions are equivalent:

(a)

\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:~\max\{x^{T}A_{1}x,x^{T}A_{2}x\}\geq 0
.

(b)

There exists
t\in\Delta_{2}
such that

t_{1}A_{1}+t_{2}A_{2}\succeq 0
.

Yuan’s lemma has been extended from
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to the first-order cones by Baccari and Trad
Baccari04
, and then to the regular cones introduced by Jeyakumar et al.
jeyakumar09
. For completeness, we present their definitions.

Definition 2

The cone
K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a first-order cone, if there exists a subspace
S
and a vector
d\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
K=S+{\mathbb{R}}_{+}d
holds.
K
is a regular cone, if
K\cup-K
is a subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

According to the above definitions, any first-order cone (including
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
) is a regular cone.

Recently, Haeser extended Yuan’s lemma to
m\ (\geq 2)
quadratic forms that can be generated by two quadratic forms, see
(
haeser17
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Theorem 3.2
(Extended Yuan’s lemma
haeser17
)

Let
A_{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{n}~(i=1,\cdots,m)
be such that

A_{i}=\alpha_{i}A_{1}+\beta_{i}A_{2}
for some
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},~i=3,\cdots,m
, and
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a first-order cone. Then, the following are equivalent to each other:

(c)

\forall x\in K:~\max_{i=1,\cdots,m}\{x^{T}A_{i}x\}\geq 0.

(d)

\exists\ t\in\Delta_{m}
such that

\forall x\in K:\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}x^{T}A_{i}x\geq 0
.

As a main result in this section, we further extend Theorem
3.2
to
m\ (\geq 1)
quadratic forms, provided that they can be generated by three quadratic forms with a positive definite linear combination.

Theorem 3.3

Let
K
be a regular cone with
{\dim}(K)\geq 3
, and
\{B_{i}:i=1,\cdots,m\}

(m\geq 1)
can be generated by
\{A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}\}
satisfying

\forall 0\neq x\in K:\ s_{1}x^{T}A_{1}x+s_{2}x^{T}A_{2}x+s_{3}x^{T}A_{3}x>0

(3)

for some
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, the following are equivalent to each other:

(e)

\forall x\in K:\ \max\{x^{T}B_{1}x,\cdots,x^{T}B_{m}x\}\geq 0
.

(f)

\exists\ t\in\Delta_{m}
such that
\forall x\in K:\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}x^{T}B_{i}x\geq 0
.

Proof

Proof of
(e)\rightarrow(f)
.
We first assume that
K={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Let

\Omega=\{(x^{T}B_{1}x,x^{T}B_{2}x,\cdots,x^{T}B_{m}x):~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\}.

It holds from
(e)
that
{\rm int}{\mathbb{R}}_{-}^{m}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
. According to Theorem
2.2
,
\Omega
is a convex cone. Then, according to the well-known convex set separation theorem, there exists
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\displaystyle t^{T}y\leq 0,\ \forall y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{-}^{m},

(4)

\displaystyle t^{T}y\geq 0,\ \forall y\in\Omega.

(5)

It follows from the definition of the set
{\mathbb{R}}_{-}^{m}
and (
4
) that
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{m}
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}>0
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
t\in\Delta_{m}
in (
4
) and (
5
), since otherwise, we can divide both sides by
\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}
.
Then (
5
) implies that

\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}x^{T}B_{i}x\geq 0,

or equivalently,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}B_{i}\succeq 0
.

Now let
K
be a regular cone. Then (
3
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\forall 0\neq x\in K\cup(-K):\ s_{1}x^{T}A_{1}x+s_{2}x^{T}A_{2}x+s_{3}x^{T}A_{3}x>0,

(6)

and
(e)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\forall x\in K\cup(-K):\ \max\{x^{T}B_{1}x,x^{T}B_{2}x,\cdots,x^{T}B_{m}x\}\geq 0.

(7)

Let
k=\dim(K)\ (\geq 3)
. There exists
Q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times k}
such that

K\cup(-K)=\{Qy:~y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\}.

(8)

By substituting (
8
)
into (
6
) and (
7
), respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\forall 0\neq y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}:\ s_{1}y^{T}Q^{T}A_{1}Qy+s_{2}y^{T}Q^{T}A_{2}Qy+s_{3}y^{T}Q^{T}A_{3}Qy>0,

(9)

\displaystyle\forall y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}:\ \max\{y^{T}Q^{T}B_{1}Qy,y^{T}Q^{T}B_{2}Qy,\cdots,y^{T}Q^{T}B_{m}Qy\}\geq 0.

(10)

According to the above first part of this proof, there exists
t\in\Delta_{m}
such that

\forall y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k}:\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}y^{T}Q^{T}B_{i}Qy\geq 0,

which is equivalent to

\forall x\in K:\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}x^{T}B_{i}x\geq 0.

Proof of
(f)\rightarrow(e)
. Suppose, on the contrary,
there exists
\tilde{x}\in K
such that
\tilde{x}^{T}B_{i}\tilde{x}<0
holds for
i=1,\cdots,m
. Then there is no
t
satisfying

t\geq 0,\ t\neq 0,\ \tilde{x}^{T}(t_{1}B_{1}+\cdots+t_{m}B_{m})\tilde{x}\geq 0,

which is a contradiction to
(f)
.
∎

Remark 5

Based on an analysis similar to that in Remark
2
, we can show that Theorem
3.3
covers not only the original Yuan’s lemma (Theorem
3.1
), but also the extended Yuan’s lemma (Theorem
3.2
).

We apply our further extended Yuan’s lemma to the second-order necessary optimality condition in nonlinear programming.

Consider the following general nonlinear programming in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
:

\begin{array}[]{cl}\min&f(x)\\
{\rm s.t.}&g_{i}(x)\leq 0,~i=1,\cdots,p,\\
&h_{j}(x)=0,~j=1,\cdots,q,\\
\end{array}

(NLP)

where all involved functions are twice continuously differentiable.

Firstly, we review some definitions and classical results on the second-order necessary optimality condition, which can be found in
Bonnans00
.

(1)

Denote by
I(x)
the set of inequality constraints active at
x
, i.e.,

I(x):=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,p\}:~g_{i}(x)=0\}.

(2)

The Lagrangian and the generalized Lagrangian associated with (
NLP
) are defined as

\displaystyle L(x,\lambda,\mu):=f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mu_{j}h_{j}(x),

\displaystyle L^{g}(x,\lambda_{0},\lambda,\mu):=\lambda_{0}f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mu_{j}h_{j}(x),

respectively.

(3)

The set
\Lambda(\bar{x})
of Lagrange multipliers at a feasible point
\bar{x}
of (
NLP
) is defined as the set of nonzero vectors
(\lambda,\mu)
satisfying the following first-order necessary optimality conditions:

\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda,\mu)=0;\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \lambda_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,~i=1,\cdots,p.

(4)

The set
\Lambda^{g}(\bar{x})
of generalized Lagrangian multiplier at the feasible point
\bar{x}
of (
NLP
) is defined as the set of nonzero vectors
(\lambda_{0},\lambda,\mu)
satisfying the following necessary optimality conditions:

\nabla_{x}L^{g}(\bar{x},\lambda_{0},\lambda,\mu)=0;\ \lambda_{0}\geq 0;\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \lambda_{i}g_{i}(\bar{x})=0,~i=1,\cdots,p.

For a generalized Lagrange multiplier
(\lambda_{0},\lambda,\mu)
with
\lambda_{0}=0
, we call
(\lambda,\mu)
a singular Lagrange multiplier.

(5)

The critical cone associated with a feasible point
\bar{x}
of
(
NLP
)
is

\begin{array}[]{cl}C(\bar{x}):=\{v:&\nabla f(\bar{x})^{T}v\leq 0,\ \nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})^{T}v\leq 0,~i\in I(\bar{x});\\
&\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})^{T}v=0,~j=1,\cdots,q\}.\end{array}

By the definition of the Lagrange multiplier,

is a closed polyhedral convex set, since it can be expressed as the intersection of finite collection of closed half-spaces.
And by the definition of the generalized Lagrange multiplier,

is a convex cone.
If a generalized Lagrange multiplier

satisfies

is a Lagrange multiplier.

The first lemma could help to characterize the relation between the singular Lagrange multiplier and the Lagrange multiplier.

Lemma 1
(Proposition 3.14 in

If the set of Lagrange multiplier

According to Lemma

Corollary 1

The set of Lagrange multiplier
\Lambda(\bar{x})
is bounded if and only if the set of singular Lagrange multiplier is empty.

The following

If

Based on the inconsistency of the generalized Lagrange multiplier in terms of

Definition 3

Let
\bar{x}
be a local minimizer of (

\forall v\in C(\bar{x}):\ v^{T}\nabla^{2}L(\bar{x},\lambda,\mu)v\geq 0.

The usual approach to avoid the inconsistency of the generalized Lagrange multiplier and hence strengthen Lemma

, is to add qualification constraints, for example, the following well-known linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ).

Definition 4

LICQ holds at
\bar{x}
, a feasible point of (

), if the vectors
\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})~(i\in I),\ \nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})~(j=1,\cdots,q)

\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})=0

(13)

in terms of
(\lambda,\mu)
has no nonzero solution.

LICQ ensures not only that the singular Lagrangian multiplier does not exist, but also that the set of Lagrangian multiplier is a single-point set. Therefore, it follows from Lemma

that the standard second-order necessary optimality condition holds true under LICQ.

However, LICQ could fail to hold at local minimizer of (

Definition 5

MFCQ holds at

(i)

The vectors

(ii)

\exists u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\nabla g_{i}(\bar{x})^{T}u<0,~i\in I(\bar{x});\ \nabla h_{j}(\bar{x})^{T}u=0,~j=1,\cdots,q.

A further extended S-lemma and its application

The celebrated S-Lemma is due to
Yakubovich71
, see
polik2007
for a survey. In this section, based on Theorem

, we further extend S-lemma. It covers the homogeneous S-lemma and its several extensions. Then we present its applications in revealing hidden convexity of quadratic optimization.

Theorem 4.1
(Homogeneous S-lemma)

For
A_{0},A_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{n},\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, suppose that there exists
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\bar{x}^{T}A_{1}\bar{x}<\beta_{1}
. The following two assertions are equivalent:

(a)

x^{T}A_{1}x\leq\beta_{1}~\Longrightarrow~x^{T}A_{0}x\geq\beta_{0}
.

(b)

\exists\ t\geq 0
such that
A_{0}+tA_{1}\succeq 0,\ \beta_{0}+t\beta_{1}\leq 0.

As presented in
(
polik2007
, Proposition 3.5)
, Theorem
4.1
is extended to
m\ (\geq 1)
quadratic forms generated by at most two quadratic forms. We remark that the original version
(
polik2007
, Proposition 3.5)
missed assuming Slater condition, which is necessary.

Theorem 4.2
(Extended S-lemma)

For
m\geq 1
and

, suppose that
{\rm rank}({B_{0},B_{1},\cdots,B_{m}})\leq 2.
Under Slater condition, i.e., there exists
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
\bar{x}^{T}B_{i}\bar{x}<0,~i=1,\cdots,m
, the following two assertions are equivalent:

(c)

x^{T}B_{i}x\leq 0,\ i=1,2,\cdots,m~\Longrightarrow~x^{T}B_{0}x\geq 0
.

(d)

\exists\ t\geq 0
such that

B_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}B_{i}\succeq 0.

We extend Theorems
4.1
and

to

quadratic forms that can be generated by three quadratic forms with a positive definite linear combination.

Theorem 4.3

Suppose that

\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\ t_{0}(x^{T}B_{0}x-\beta_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}(x^{T}B_{i}x-\beta_{i})\geq 0,

(16)

5
Conclusion

We extend Polyak’s theorem on the convexity of the joint numerical range from three to arbitrary number of quadratic forms, provided that their Hessian matrices can be generated by three matrices with a positive definite linear combination. It also covers the classical Dines’s theorem. As applications, we further extend Yuan’s lemma and S-lemma to the system with more than three quadratic forms. With the help of our extended Yuan’s lemma, we establish a more general assumption under which the standard second-order necessary condition holds at local minimizer. It is unknown whether our assumption can be further relaxed. The further extended S-lemma helps to reveal strong duality of homogeneous quadratic optimization problem with two bilateral constraints, which includes the generalized trust region subproblem with interval bounds and the two-sided regularized total least squares problem as special cases. Future works include more applications of our new results and further extensions to nonhomogeneous alternative theorems.

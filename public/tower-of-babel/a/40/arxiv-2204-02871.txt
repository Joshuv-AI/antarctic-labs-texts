2.
Associated primes of
\operatorname{Tor}

In this note
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a commutative noetherian local ring. The notation
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(-)
(resp.
\operatorname{id}_{R}(-)
) stands for the projective (resp. injective) dimension.

Question 2.1
.

How can compute depth and dimension of
\operatorname{Tor}
-modules?

We start by recalling the following basic result of Auslander:

Fact 2.2
.

(See
[
4
, Lemma 1.1]
)
Let
M
and
N
be finitely generated modules such that
p:=\operatorname{pd}_{R}M<\infty
and
0pt_{R}(N)=0
. Then
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{p}(M,N)\neq 0

and it is of depth zero.

Remark 2.3
.

i) The finitely generated assumption of
M
is really important. Here, we follow an idea
of Lazard
[
20
]
: Let
R:=k[x,y,z]/x\mathfrak{m}
and look at
0\to R_{(x)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}R_{(x,y)}\to M:=\operatorname{Coker}f\to 0
and we set
N:=k
which is of depth zero. From this flat resolution,
\operatorname{Tor}_{+}^{R}(M,N)=0
. Let
t:=\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)
. Since
M
is of finite flat dimension, it follows that
t<\infty
. It remains to note that
\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(M,N)=0
.

ii) There is a way to drop the finite assumption of
N
. In this regard we need to fix the notion
of depth. We say a general module
\mathcal{N}
is of E-depth zero if
R/\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathcal{N}
.
This holds if any
x
is zero-divisor over
\mathcal{N}
. Having this
in mind, the finitely generated assumption of
N
is not needed. In order to see this, it is enough to apply Auslander’s argument. We leave the details to the reader.

Definition 2.4
.

For every
R
-module
L
, the non-flat locus of
L
is defined by

\text{NF}(L):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\ \text{the}\ R_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{-module}\ L_{\mathfrak{p}}\ {is\ not\ flat}\}.

In the case
L
is finitely generated,
we call
\text{NF}(L)
the non-free locus, since over local rings
a finitely generated flat module is free.

We denote the set of all associated prime ideals by
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(-)
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
M
and
N
be two
R
-modules.
If
M
has finite projective dimension
t\geq 1
, then
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N\cap\text{NF}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
\textbf{F}:=0\longrightarrow R^{n_{t}}\overset{d_{t}}{\longrightarrow}R^{n_{t-1}}\overset{d_{t-1}}{\longrightarrow}\dots\overset{d_{1}}{\longrightarrow}R^{n_{0}}\longrightarrow 0
be a free resolution of
M
where
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.
Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(M,N)&=\text{H}_{t}(\textbf{F}\otimes_{R}N)\\
&=\ker(d_{t}\otimes 1)\\
&\cong\ker(N^{n_{t}}\longrightarrow N^{n_{t-1}})\\
&\subseteq N^{n_{t}},\end{array}

and so

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(N^{n_{t}})=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N.

Since
t\geq 1
,
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\text{NF}(M)
. Thus

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N\cap\text{NF}(M),

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Adopt the previous assumption and suppose in addition that
M
ad
N
are finitely generated. If

M
has projective dimension one, then
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(M,N))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N\cap\text{NF}(M)
.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
2.5
, we only need to show that

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N\cap\text{NF}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(M,N)).

For a finitely
generated
R
-module
(-)
, it is known that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(-)
if and only if
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((-)_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N\cap\text{NF}(M)
. As
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N
, we get
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
. Also, from
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{NF}(M)
and
\operatorname{pd}_{R}M=1
,
we deduce that
\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=1
. Fact
2.2
yields that

0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}},N_{\mathfrak{p}}))=0
, and so
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}^{R}(M,N))
.
∎

.

By
\operatorname{Syz}_{i}(M)

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and

and

-modules. Assume that

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N\cap\text{NF}(\operatorname{Syz}_{t-1}(M))

Proof.

Set

and

.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring,
M
and
N
be finitely generated. If
M
has finite
projective dimension
t\geq 1
and
N
is
\mathfrak{p}
-primary for some

, then
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. In particular, if
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N)\neq 0
, then
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}

The first part is in Proposition

. To see the particular case note that

\emptyset\neq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}\},

and so
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a local domain and
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module with

\operatorname{pd}_{R}M=1
. Then
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(M,M))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Let
(-)_{0}
be the localization with respect to
0\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. Since
M_{0}
is an
R_{0}
-vector space, it follow that
0\notin\text{NF}(M)
. Let
0\neq\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M
. Then
0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
, and so by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, we deduce that

1\leq 0pt(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+0pt_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq\operatorname{pd}_{R}M\leq 1,

i.e.,
\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=1
. In particular,
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is not a free
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module.
Hence
\text{NF}(M)\cap\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M\setminus\{0\}
, and Corollary
2.6
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring,
M
and
N
be finitely generated. Assume that
M

is a locally free on the punctured spectrum, and it has finite projective dimension
t\geq 1
. Then
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N)\neq 0
if and only if
0pt_{R}N=0
.

Proof.

First, assume that
0pt_{R}N=0
. Fact
2.2
implies that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N)\neq 0
.
Conversely, suppose
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N)\neq 0
. In view of Proposition
2.5
we observe that

\emptyset\neq\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{t}(M,N))\subseteq\text{NF}(M)\cap\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N=\{\mathfrak{m}\}\cap\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N.

Thus
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}N
, and so
0pt_{R}N=0
.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local ring and
\mathfrak{p}
a Cohen-Macaulay prime ideal of
R
of
finite projective dimension. The following assertions are true:

(i)

R
is a Cohen-Macaulay integral domain.

(ii)

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.

(iii)

\dim(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=\dim R/\mathfrak{p}
.

(iv)

\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

(i) Recall that
R
is integral domain by intersection theorem. For more details, see
[
29
]
. In particular,
R
is equidimensional
and catenary. This allows us to apply
[
24
, Page 250]
and deduce that

\dim R-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}=:t.

The Auslander-Buchsbaum
formula implies that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})&=0pt_{R}R-0pt_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\\
&=0pt_{R}R-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ast)}}{{\leq}}\dim R-\dim_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\\
&=\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}\\
&=t.\end{array}

Since
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})<\infty
, it follows that
\operatorname{pd}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})<\infty
, and so the local ring
R_{\mathfrak{p}}

is regular. Now,

\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong\operatorname{Tor}_{\dim_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}}^{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}},R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}})\neq 0.

Consequently,
\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
. So,
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})=t
. In particular,
(\ast)

is an equality, i.e.,
\dim R=0pt_{R}R
.

(ii) Since
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})={\mathfrak{p}}
, and without loss of generality, we may and do assume that
t>0
. According to Proposition
2.5
,

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))\subseteq\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. As
\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
, it follows that

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))\neq\emptyset
, and so
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
.

(iii) Thanks to part ii) we know
\operatorname{Supp}(\operatorname{Tor}_{t}^{R}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=\operatorname{Var}(\mathfrak{p})
. From this, the desired claim is clear.

(iv) Clearly,
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))
. If the inclusion
were be strict, we should had

\dim({R}/{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))})<\dim R/\mathfrak{p},

because
\mathfrak{p}
is prime.
This is in contradiction with the third item. So,
\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=\mathfrak{p}
,
as claimed.
∎

Fact 2.13
.

(See
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
)
Let
R
be any local ring and
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty
. Let
q
be the largest number such that
\operatorname{Tor}_{q}^{R}(M,N)\neq 0
. If
0pt_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}_{q}^{R}(M,N))\leq 1

Corollary 2.14
.

Adopt the assumption of Corollary
2.12
and suppose in addition that
\dim R/\mathfrak{p}\leq 2
. Then

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

We only deal with the case
\dim R/\mathfrak{p}=2
. Thanks to Corollary
2.12
,

\dim(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=2
, and recall that

0pt_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))\leq\dim(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=2
.
Suppose on the way of contradiction that

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})
is not Cohen-Macaulay.
Since
\operatorname{zd}(-)=\bigcup_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}(-)}P
, we observe that any
x\in\mathfrak{m}\setminus\mathfrak{p}
is a regular sequence over
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})
.
It follows that
0pt_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))=1
. This enables us to apply Fact
2.13
. We combine this along with Corollary
2.12
to see

\begin{array}[]{ll}2&=\dim R/\mathfrak{p}\\
&=0pt_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\\
&=0pt_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p}))+\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})-{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}\\
&=1+0ptR-0pt_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})-\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}\\
&=1+\dim R-\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})-\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}\\
&=1,\end{array}

which is a contradiction. So,
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}}(R/\mathfrak{p},R/\mathfrak{p})
is Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

(ii)

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R/I,R/I))=\operatorname{Ass}_{R}R/I

(iii)

3.
Freeness criteria via vanishing of
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}

We start with:

Definition 3.1
.

A nonzero
R
-module
L
is called (strong) Lichtenbaum if for every (not-necessarily) finitely generated
R
-module
F
, the vanishing of

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{1}(L,F)
implies that

Notation 4.6

By

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a

-dimensional local integral domain and let

Let

, and recall that the above complex can be used for a part of a free resolution of the

-module

Tongseok Lim

Abstract
. Lloyd S. Shapley [ 19 , 20 ] introduced a set of axioms in 1953, now called the Shapley axioms , and showed that the axioms characterize a natural allocation among the players who are in grand coalition of a cooperative game . Recently, Stern and Tettenhorst 2019 showed that a cooperative game can be decomposed into a sum of component games , one for each player, whose value at the grand coalition coincides with the Shapley value . The component games are defined by the solutions to the naturally defined system of least squares linear equations via the framework of the Hodge decomposition on the hypercube graph. In this paper we propose a new set of axioms which characterizes the component games. Furthermore, we realize them through an intriguing stochastic path integral driven by a canonical Markov chain. The integrals are natural representation for the expected total contribution made by the players for each coalition, and hence can be viewed as their fair share. This allows us to interpret the component game values for each coalition also as a valid measure of fair allocation among the players in the coalition. Our axioms may be viewed as a completion of Shapley axioms in view of this characterization of the Hodge-theoretic component games, and moreover, the stochastic path integral representation of the component games may be viewed as an extension of the Shapley formula .



Keywords: Shapley axioms, Shapley value, Shapley formula, cooperative game, component game, Hodge decomposition, least squares, path integral representation

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
denote the set of positive integers. For
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
[N]:=\{1,2,...,N\}
denote the set of players. The set of
cooperative games
is defined by

{\cal G}_{N}=\{v:2^{[N]}\to{\mathbb{R}}\ |\ v(\emptyset)=0\}.

Thus a cooperative game here is simply a (value) function on the subsets of
[N]

S\subseteq[N]
represents a coalition of players in
S

represents the value assigned to the coalition
S

Lloyd Shapley considered the question of how to split
the grand coalition value
v([N])
among the players for a given game
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
. It is uniquely defined according to the following theorem, which is quoted from
Stern and Tettenhorst 2019
.

Theorem 1.1

(
Shapley 1953b
)
.

There exists a unique allocation

v\in{\cal G}_{N}\mapsto\bigl(\phi_{i}(v)\bigr)_{i\in[N]}
satisfying the following conditions:

\cdot
efficiency:

\sum_{i\in[N]}\phi_{i}(v)=v([N])
.

\cdot
symmetry:

v\bigl(S\cup\{i\}\bigr)=v\bigl(S\cup\{j\}\bigr)
for all
S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i,j\}
yields

\phi_{i}(v)=\phi_{j}(v)
.

\cdot
null-player:

v\bigl(S\cup\{i\}\bigr)-v(S)=0
for all

S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
yields
\phi_{i}(v)=0
.

\cdot
linearity:

\phi_{i}(\alpha v+\alpha^{\prime}v^{\prime})=\alpha\phi_{i}(v)+\alpha^{\prime}\phi_{i}(v^{\prime})
for all

\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v,v^{\prime}\in{\cal G}_{N}
.

Moreover, this allocation is given by the following explicit formula:

(1.1)

\phi_{i}(v)=\sum_{S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}}\frac{\lvert S\rvert!\bigl(N-1-\lvert S\rvert\bigr)!}{N!}\Bigl(v\bigl(S\cup\{i\}\bigr)-v(S)\Bigr).

The four conditions listed above are often called the

Shapley axioms
. Quoted from
[
23
]
, they say that [efficiency] the value obtained by the grand coalition is fully distributed among the players, [symmetry] equivalent players receive equal amounts, [null-player] a player who contributes no marginal value to any coalition receives nothing, and [linearity] the allocation is linear in the game values.

(
1.1
) can be rewritten also quoted from
[
23
]
: Suppose the players form the grand coalition by
joining, one-at-a-time, in the order defined by a permutation
\sigma

of
[N]
. That is, player
i
joins immediately after the coalition

S_{\sigma,i}=\bigl\{j\in[N]:\sigma(j)<\sigma(i)\bigr\}
has formed, contributing marginal value

v\bigl(S_{\sigma,i}\cup\{i\}\bigr)-v(S_{\sigma,i})
. Then
\phi_{i}(v)
is the average marginal value contributed by
player
i
over all
N!
permutations
\sigma
, i.e.,

(1.2)

\phi_{i}(v)=\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{\sigma}\Bigl(v\bigl(S_{\sigma,i}\cup\{i\}\bigr)-v(S_{\sigma,i})\Bigr).

We see an important principle here, which we may call the
Shapley’s principle
:

Shapley’s principle.
The value allocated to player
i
is based entirely on the marginal values

v\bigl(S\cup\{i\}\bigr)-v(S)
the player
i

In particular,

2.
An extension of Shapley axioms

Let
{\cal G}=\bigcup_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\cal G}_{N}
. For
i,j\in[N]
and
S\subseteq[N]
, define
S^{ij}\subseteq[N]
by

S^{ij}=\begin{cases}S&\text{if }\ S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i,j\}\,\text{ or }\,\{i,j\}\subseteq S,\\
S\cup\{i\}\setminus\{j\}&\text{if }\ i\notin S\,\text{ and }\,j\in S,\\
S\cup\{j\}\setminus\{i\}&\text{if }\ j\notin S\,\text{ and }\,i\in S.\end{cases}

Given
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
, define
v^{ij}\in{\cal G}_{N}
by
v^{ij}(S)=v(S^{ij})
. Intuitively, the contributions of the players
i,j
in the game
v
are interchanged in the game
v^{ij}
.

Of course, a cooperative game can be considered on any finite set of players
M
through a bijection
M\hookrightarrow[|M|]
. In this sense, for
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
and
i\in[N]
, we define
v_{-i}
to be the restricted game of
v
on the set of players
[N]\setminus\{i\}
, i.e.
v_{-i}(S)=v(S)
for all
S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
. We are ready to describe our extension of Shapley axioms.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a unique allocation

v\in{\cal G}\mapsto\bigl(\Phi_{i}[v]\bigr)_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfying
\Phi_{i}[v]\in{\cal G}_{N}
with
\Phi_{i}[v]\equiv 0
if
i\geq N+1
for
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
, and moreover the following:

A1
(efficiency):

v=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Phi_{i}[v]
.

A2
(symmetry):

\Phi_{i}[v^{ij}](S^{ij})=\Phi_{j}[v](S)
for all
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
,
i,j\in[N]
and
S\subseteq[N]
.

A3
(null-player):
If
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
and
\mathrm{d}_{i}v=0
for some
i\in[N]
, then
\Phi_{i}[v]\equiv 0
, and

\Phi_{j}[v](S\cup\{i\})=\Phi_{j}[v](S)=\Phi_{j}[v_{-i}](S)\ \text{ for all }\ j\in[N]\setminus\{i\},\,S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}.

A4
(linearity):
For any
v,v^{\prime}\in{\cal G}_{N}
and
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\Phi_{i}[\alpha v+\alpha^{\prime}v^{\prime}]=\alpha\Phi_{i}[v]+\alpha^{\prime}\Phi_{i}[v^{\prime}]
.

A5
(reflection):
For any
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
,
i\in[N]
and
S,T\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
, it holds

\Phi_{i}[v](S\cup\{i\})-\Phi_{i}[v](T\cup\{i\})=-\bigl(\Phi_{i}[v](S)-\Phi_{i}[v](T)\bigr).

Moreover, the solutions
(v_{i})_{i\in[N]}
to (
1.3
) satisfy
A1–A5
with
\Phi_{i}[v]=v_{i}
.

Discussion.
(i) A1, A4 seem natural analogues of corresponding Shapley axioms.

(ii) The condition in A2 is just as if the players
i,j
exchange their labels. We may interpret as, if the contributions of
i,j
are interchanged, then so are their payoffs.

(iii) A3 says that if
\mathrm{d}_{i}v=0
then everything is just as if
i
is not present. In other words, if player
i
contributes nothing, then the reward of the rest is independent of the participation of the null player
i
, and hence by efficiency, the player
i
receives nothing. So
\Phi_{i}[v]\equiv 0
is in fact a consequence rather than a part of the axioms.

(iv) Perhaps A5 – which is newly introduced – is the least intuitive of all the axioms. To convince its innocuousness, let
S,T\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
, and consider an arbitrary connected path
\theta
of cooperation process from
S
to
T
on the hypercube graph:

\theta:X_{0}\to X_{1}\to\dots\to X_{n}

where
X_{0}=S
,
X_{n}=T
, and each
(X_{k},X_{k+1})
is either a forward- or reverse-oriented edge of the hypercube graph. Then there exists its reflection with respect to
i
:

\theta^{\prime}:X^{\prime}_{0}\to X^{\prime}_{1}\to\dots\to X^{\prime}_{n}

where
X^{\prime}_{k}:=X_{k}\cup\{i\}
if
i\notin X_{k}
, and
X^{\prime}_{k}:=X_{k}\setminus\{i\}
if
i\in X_{k}
. Now observe that the contribution of the player
i
(i.e. the integral of
\mathrm{d}_{i}
) along the path
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
have the opposite sign, since whenever the player

joins / leaves coalition along
\theta

i
leaves / joins coalition along
\theta^{\prime}
respectively. In view of the Shapley’s principle and the arbitrariness of

1.2
) takes into account the coalition processes of joining direction only, our consideration above allows for the coalitions to proceed in either direction.

on
[N]\setminus\{i\}
. Let
\tilde{v}_{j}
be the corresponding component game on
[N]\setminus\{i\}
, solving the defining equation
\mathrm{\tilde{d}}^{\ast}\mathrm{\tilde{d}}\tilde{v}_{j}=\mathrm{\tilde{d}}^{\ast}\mathrm{\tilde{d}}_{j}\tilde{v}
. Finally, in view of A3, define
v_{j}\in{\cal G}_{N}
by
v_{j}=\tilde{v}_{j}
on
2^{[N]\setminus\{i\}}
and
\mathrm{d}_{i}v_{j}=0
. Now observe that A3 will follow if we verify this
v_{j}
indeed solves the equation
\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{j}=\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{j}v
.

To show this, let
S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
. In fact the following string of equalities holds:

\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{j}(S\cup\{i\})=\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{j}(S)=\mathrm{\tilde{d}}^{\ast}\mathrm{\tilde{d}}\tilde{v}_{j}(S)=\mathrm{\tilde{d}}^{\ast}\mathrm{\tilde{d}}_{j}\tilde{v}(S)=\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(S)=\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(S\cup\{i\})

which simply follows from the definition of the differential operators. For instance

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{j}(S)=\sum_{T\sim S}\mathrm{d}v_{j}(T,S)=\sum_{T\sim S,\,T\neq S\cup\{i\}}\mathrm{d}v_{j}(T,S)=\mathrm{\tilde{d}}^{\ast}\mathrm{\tilde{d}}\tilde{v}_{j}(S)

where the second equality is due to
\mathrm{d}_{i}v_{j}=0
. On the other hand, since
j\neq i
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(S)=\sum_{T\sim S}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(T,S)=\sum_{T\sim S}\mathrm{d}_{j}\tilde{v}(T,S)=\mathrm{\tilde{d}}^{\ast}\mathrm{\tilde{d}}_{j}\tilde{v}(S).

The first and last equalities in the string should now be obvious, verifying A3.

Finally we verify A5. For this, we need to verify the following claim

v_{i}(S)+v_{i}(S\cup\{i\})\,\text{ is constant over all }\,S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}.

Let
S\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
, and recall
\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{i}v(S)=v(S)-v(S\cup\{i\})=-\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}_{i}v(S\cup\{i\})
. Hence
\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{i}(S)+\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{i}(S\cup\{i\})=0
. Define
w_{i}\in\ell^{2}(2^{[N]})
by
w_{i}(S)=v_{i}(S\cup\{i\})
and
w_{i}(S\cup\{i\})=v_{i}(S)
. Then it is clear
\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}v_{i}(S\cup\{i\})=\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}w_{i}(S)
, thus
\mathrm{d}^{\ast}\mathrm{d}(v_{i}+w_{i})\equiv 0
, hence
v_{i}+w_{i}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{d})
, meaning
v_{i}+w_{i}
is constant. This proves the claim.
∎

3.
A path integral representation of the component games

Recall the Shapley value
\phi_{i}(v)=v_{i}([N])
admits the representation formula (
1.2
). For
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
, could
v_{i}(S)
for
S\subsetneq[N]
also have an analogous representation?

To give an answer, consider a Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}
on the state space
2^{[N]}
,
X_{0}=\emptyset
, equipped with the transition probability
p_{S,T}
from
S
to
T
as follows:

p_{S,T}=1/N\,\text{ if }(S,T)\text{ is a forward- or reverse-oriented edge, }\ p_{S,T}=0\text{ otherwise.}

Let
(\Omega,{\cal F},{\cal P})
be the underlying probability space for formality. For each
S\subseteq[N]
, let
\tau_{S}=\tau_{S}(\omega)
denote the first time the Markov chain
\big(X_{n}(\omega)\big)_{n}
visits
S
. We define the total contribution of the player
i
along the sample path
\omega\in\Omega
toward
S
by

{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)={\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)(\omega):=\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{S}(\omega)}\mathrm{d}_{i}v\big(X_{n-1}(\omega),X_{n}(\omega)\big).

Finally, we define value functions for each
i\in[N]
via the following path integral

V_{i}(S)=V_{i}[v](S):=\int_{\Omega}{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)(\omega)d{\cal P}(\omega)={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)].

Observe that
V_{i}(S)
represents the expected total contribution made by player
i
toward the cooperation
S
, and hence can be interpreted as a fair allocation of the value
v(S)
to the player
i
given the coalition process would terminate at
S
.

Now the following theorem shows
V_{i}
is a valid representation of the component game
v_{i}
. This gives a further justification for the interpretation of the component game value
v_{i}(S)
to be a fair and natural reward allocation for the player
i
at
S
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
and
i\in[N]
it holds
V_{i}=v_{i}
, where
v_{i}\in{\cal G}_{N}
is the unique solution to the equation (
1.3
) characterized by A1–A5 in Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in{\cal G}_{N}
. We may show that
(V_{i})_{i\in[N]}
satisfies A1–A5 with
\Phi_{i}(v):=V_{i}
thanks to Theorem
2.1
. A4 may be trivially verified and we omit. For A1, notice

\sum_{i\in[N]}{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)=\sum_{i\in[N]}\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{S}}\mathrm{d}_{i}v\big(X_{n-1},X_{n}\big)=\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{S}}\mathrm{d}v\big(X_{n-1},X_{n}\big)=v(S)

since
\mathrm{d}=\sum_{i}\mathrm{d}_{i}
.
Hence
\sum_{i}V_{i}(S)={\mathbb{E}}[\sum_{i}{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)]=v(S)
. This verifies A1.

For
i\neq j
, note that each sample path
\omega
has its counterpart
\omega^{ij}
satisfying

X_{n}(\omega)=S\ \text{ if and only if }\ X_{n}(\omega^{ij})=S^{ij}\ \text{ for every $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$ and }S\subseteq[N]

and moreover,
{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)(\omega)={\mathrm{D}}_{j}v(S^{ij})(\omega^{ij})
. Taking expectation then verifies A2.

For A3, assume
\mathrm{d}_{i}v=0
. Then
{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v=0
readily gives
V_{i}\equiv 0
. Next we may proceed by an induction on
N
, so let
(\tilde{X}_{n})_{n}
denote the Markov chain on the state space
2^{[N]\setminus\{i\}}
with the analogous transition rates and
(\tilde{V}_{i})_{i}
be the associated value functions. Observe that
(\tilde{X}_{n})_{n}
can be embedded in
(X_{n})_{n}
via the identification

\tilde{X}_{n}:=S\in 2^{[N]\setminus\{i\}}\ \text{ if and only if }\ X_{n}=S\,\text{ or }\,X_{n}=S\cup\{i\}

yielding
\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{j}(S)=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{S}\wedge\tau_{S\cup\{i\}}}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(X_{n-1},X_{n})d{\cal P}
for all
j\in[N]\setminus\{i\}
. Now consider an arbitrary connected finite path
\theta:X_{0}\to X_{1}\to\dots\to X_{n}
on the hypercube graph
where
X_{0}=S
and
X_{n}=S\cup\{i\}
, or
X_{0}=S\cup\{i\}
and
X_{n}=S
. Its reversed path is then given by

\theta^{\prime}:X_{0}^{\prime}\to\dots\to X_{n}^{\prime}
where
X_{k}^{\prime}=X_{n-k}
. Finally flip the reversed path with respect to
i
and get the path
\theta^{*}:X_{0}^{*}\to\dots\to X_{n}^{*}
, where
X^{*}_{k}=S\in 2^{[N]\setminus\{i\}}
if
X^{\prime}_{k}=S\cup\{i\}
and
X^{*}_{k}=S\cup\{i\}
if
X^{\prime}_{k}=S
. Observe that the correspondence
\theta\leftrightarrow\theta^{*}
is bijective, both starts at
S
and ends at
S\cup\{i\}
(or
S\cup\{i\}
and
S
), and

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(X_{k-1},X_{k})=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}_{j}v(X^{*}_{k-1},X^{*}_{k})

since
\mathrm{d}_{i}v=0
. Notice, in view of the path integral formula for
V_{j}
, this implies

\tilde{V}_{j}(S)=V_{j}(S)=V_{j}(S\cup\{i\}).

Induction hypothesis gives
\tilde{V}_{j}(S)=\tilde{v}_{j}(S)
where
\tilde{v}
is the restriction of
v
on
2^{[N]\setminus\{i\}}
, and moreover
\tilde{v}_{j}(S)=v_{j}(S)=v_{j}(S\cup\{i\})
by Theorem
2.1
. This verifies A3.

Finally let us verify A5. For any states
S,T\subseteq[N]
, consider the Markov chain
(X^{S}_{n})_{n}
whose initial state is
X^{S}_{0}=S
(instead of
\emptyset
). Define analogously
\mathrm{D}^{S}_{i}v(T)
and

V^{S}_{i}(T)={\mathbb{E}}[\mathrm{D}^{S}_{i}v(T)]
via
X^{S}
in place of
X
. By the correspondence between paths from
S
to
T
and their reverse from
T
to
S
, it readily follows
V^{S}_{i}(T)=-V^{T}_{i}(S)
. Now we prove the following transition formula

(3.1)

V_{i}(T)-V_{i}(S)=V^{S}_{i}(T).

To see this, we compute

\displaystyle{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(T)-{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{T}}\mathrm{d}_{i}v\big(X_{n-1},X_{n}\big)-\sum_{n=1}^{\tau_{S}}\mathrm{d}_{i}v\big(X_{n-1},X_{n}\big)

\displaystyle={\bf 1}_{\tau_{S}<\tau_{T}}\sum_{n=\tau_{S}+1}^{\tau_{T}}\mathrm{d}_{i}v\big(X_{n-1},X_{n}\big)-{\bf 1}_{\tau_{T}<\tau_{S}}\sum_{n=\tau_{T}+1}^{\tau_{S}}\mathrm{d}_{i}v\big(X_{n-1},X_{n}\big).

By taking expectation, we obtain via the Markov property

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(T)]-{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathrm{D}}_{i}v(S)]={\cal P}(\{\tau_{S}<\tau_{T}\})V^{S}_{i}(T)-{\cal P}(\{\tau_{T}<\tau_{S}\})V^{T}_{i}(S)=V^{S}_{i}(T)

which proves (
3.1
). Now for any
S,T\subseteq[N]\setminus\{i\}
, we observe

\displaystyle V_{i}(T\cup\{i\})-V_{i}(S\cup\{i\})=V^{S\cup\{i\}}_{i}(T\cup\{i\})=-V^{S}_{i}(T)=-\big(V_{i}(T)-V_{i}(S)\big)

where the middle equality stems from the correspondence of reflected paths with respect to
i
given in the discussion (iv) in the previous section. This verifies A5.
∎

Global well-posedness for the non-viscous MHD equations with magnetic diffusion in critical Besov spaces
Weikui Ye and, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we mainly investigate the Cauchy problem of the non-viscous MHD equations with magnetic diffusion. We first establish the local well-posedness (existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence) with initial data ( u 0 , b 0 ) (u_{0},b_{0}) in critical Besov spaces B p , 1 d p + 1 × B p , 1 d p {B}^{\frac{d}{p}+1}_{p,1}\times{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1} with 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , and give a lifespan T T of the solution which depends on the norm of the Littlewood-Paley decomposition of the initial data. Then, we prove the global existence in critical Besov spaces. In particular, the results of global existence also hold in Sobolev space C ⁡ ( [ 0 , ∞ ) , H s ​ ( 𝕊 2 ) ) × ( C ⁡ ( [ 0 , ∞ ) , H s − 1 ​ ( 𝕊 2 ) ) ∩ L 2 ​ ( [ 0 , ∞ ) , H s ​ ( 𝕊 2 ) ) ) C([0,\infty);{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))\times\Big(C([0,\infty);{H}^{s-1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))\cap L^{2}\big([0,\infty);{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\big)\Big) with s > 2 s>2 , when the initial data satisfies ∫ 𝕊 2 b 0 ​ 𝑑 x = 0 \int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}b_{0}dx=0 and ‖ u 0 ‖ B ∞ , 1 1 ​ ( 𝕊 2 ) + ‖ b 0 ‖ B ∞ , 1 0 ​ ( 𝕊 2 ) ≤ ϵ \|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|b_{0}\|_{{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\epsilon . It’s worth noting that our results imply some large and low regularity initial data for the global existence, which improves considerably the recent results in [ 21 ] .

Introduction

The incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations can be written as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}-\mu\Delta u+\nabla P=b\nabla b-u\nabla u,\\
b_{t}-\nu\Delta b+u\nabla b=b\nabla u,\\
{\rm{div}}u={\rm{div}}b=0,\\
(u,b)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},b_{0}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where the vector fields
u=(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{d}),\ b=(b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{d})
are the velocity and magnetic respectively, the scalar function
P
denotes the pressure. The MHD equation is a coupled system of the Navier-Stokes equation and Maxwell’s equation. This model describes the interactions between the magnetic field and the fluid of moving electrically charged particles such as plasmas, liquid metals, and electrolytes. For more physical background, one can refer to
[
4
,
10
]
.

The MHD equations are of great interest in mathematics and physics. Let’s review some well-posed results about the MHD equations. When
\nu=0,~\mu\neq 0
(non-magnetic diffusion but full viscosity) in system (
1.1
), we refer to
[
1
,
15
,
17
,
19
,
18
]
about the global existence results with initial data sufficiently close to the equilibrium. The
L^{2}
decay rate was studied by Agapito and Schonbek
[
2
]
.
Fefferman et al. obtained a local existence result in
\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,3)
with the initial data
(u_{0},B_{0})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})(s>d/2)
in
[
11

and

(u_{0},B_{0})\in H^{s-1-\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})(s>d/2,0<\epsilon<1)
in
[

[
7

(u_{0},B_{0})\in B^{\frac{d}{2}-1}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times B^{\frac{d}{2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})(d=2,3)
and also proved the uniqueness with
d=3

[

obtained the uniqueness with

However, when
\mu=0,~\nu\neq 0
(non-viscosity but full magnetic diffusion), (
1.1

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}+\nabla P=b\nabla b-u\nabla u,\\
b_{t}-\Delta b+u\nabla b=b\nabla u,\\
{\rm{div}}u={\rm{div}}b=0,\\
(u,b)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},b_{0}).\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

The study of (

Recently, Wei and Zhang

In
[
21

{B}^{\frac{d}{p}+1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times\big({B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\cap{B}^{\frac{d}{p}+2}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\big),~\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}~or~\mathbb{S}

\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+\|b_{0}\|_{{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}

[
21

Our main theorem can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(u_{0},b_{0})\in\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}+1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\times\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

E^{p}_{T}:=C([0,T];\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}+1}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\times\Big(C([0,T];\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\cap L^{1}([0,T];\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}+2}_{p,1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\Big)
.

6

In the whole space, the global existence for (

Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some properties about the Littlewood-Paley decomposition and Besov spaces.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
3
]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
. There exist radial functions
\chi
and
\varphi

[0,1]

\mathcal{D}(B(0,\frac{4}{3}))
and

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\},\ \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,

|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2\Rightarrow\mathrm{Supp}\ \varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\cap\mathrm{Supp}\ \varphi(2^{-j^{\prime}}\cdot)=\emptyset,

j\geq 1\Rightarrow\mathrm{Supp}\ \chi(\cdot)\cap\mathrm{Supp}\ \varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)=\emptyset.

The set
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}=B(0,\frac{2}{3})+\mathcal{C}
is an annulus, and we have

|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 5\Rightarrow 2^{j}\mathcal{C}\cap 2^{j^{\prime}}\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}=\emptyset.

Further, we have

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\chi^{2}(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\leq 1,

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\},\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\leq 1.

5
Global existence

The proof of Theorem
1.4
:

Proof.

We use the bootstrap argument to prove this Theorem. Without loss of generality, we only consider the critical case:
s=1+\frac{2}{p}
. Let
T^{*}
be the maximal existence time of the solution. Assume that for any
t\leq T<T^{*}
,

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{0}_{\infty,1})\cap L^{1}_{T}(B^{2}_{\infty,1})}\leq 4.

(5.1)

Let
\epsilon
be a sufficient small positive constant such that

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|b_{0}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\epsilon\leq\frac{c}{16(1+C^{20})},

(5.2)

where
0<6c<1
and
20<C
are some fixed constants and will be determined later. The proof can be divided into 4 parts:

(1) First, we give the estimation of
\|b(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.

It’s trivial to verify that

\frac{1}{2}(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|b\|^{2}_{L^{2}})+\int_{0}^{T}\|b\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}{{\rm d}}\tau=\frac{1}{2}(\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|b_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}).

Let
w={\rm curl}u
with
d=2
, we have

\displaystyle w_{t}+u\nabla w=b\nabla{\rm curl}b.

(5.3)

By (
5.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{B^{1}_{\infty,1}}+C\|b\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{0}_{\infty,1})}\|b\|_{L^{1}_{T}(B^{2}_{\infty,1})}\leq C(\epsilon+16)\leq C^{2}.

(5.4)

Since
\int_{\mathbb{S}}b_{0}{{\rm d}}x=0
, one can easily deduce that
\int_{\mathbb{S}}b{{\rm d}}x=0
, which means that
\Delta_{-1}b=0
and
\|b\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S})}\leq C\|b\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{S})}
. Taking
L^{2}
inner product with
b
to the second equation of (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{{{\rm d}}}{{{\rm d}}t}\|b\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|b\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|b\|_{L^{2}}\|b\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}})\|b\|_{L^{2}}\|b\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|b\|_{L^{2}}\|b\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|b\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}

(5.5)

Hence, there exists a positive constant
0<6c<1
such that

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|b_{0}\|_{L^{2}}e^{-6ct}\leq C\epsilon e^{-6ct}.

(5.6)

(2) Then, we give the estimation of
\|b(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Using the fact that
\|\nabla^{k}e^{t\Delta}f\|_{L^{q}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}}~(1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty)
for
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}fdx=0
in
[
21
]
, we have

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}e^{-t}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{3}}e^{-(t-s)}\|b\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\|u\nabla b\|_{L^{\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon e^{-6ct}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{3}}e^{-(t-s)}\|b\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{6}}ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\|b\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|b\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon e^{-6ct}+C^{3}\epsilon\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{3}}e^{-2c(t-s)}e^{-2cs}ds+C^{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c(t-s)}e^{-2cs}\|b\|_{B^{2}_{\infty,1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon e^{-6ct}+C^{3}\epsilon t^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{-2ct}+C^{3}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}t^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{-2ct}\|b\|_{L^{1}_{t}(B^{2}_{\infty,1})}^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{-ct}.

(5.7)

That is

\displaystyle\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C^{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}e^{-ct}.

(5.8)

(3) Next, we give the estimation of
\|w(t)\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}
.

Applying Lemma
2.6
to (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{0}_{\infty,1})}\leq

\displaystyle C(\|w_{0}\|_{B^{0}_{\infty,1}}+\|b\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{\infty})}\|b\|_{L^{1}_{T}(B^{2}_{\infty,1})})(1+\int_{0}^{T}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{0}_{\infty,1})}ds)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\epsilon+4C^{4}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})(1+\int_{0}^{T}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{0}_{\infty,1})}ds)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{small}e^{c_{small}t}

where the last inequality holds by the Gronwall’s inequality, and

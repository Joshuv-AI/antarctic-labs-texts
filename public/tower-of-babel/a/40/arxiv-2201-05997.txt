Generalization of the Extended Minimal


Abstract
In a recent pioneering work, Andrews and Newman defined an extended function p A , a ​ ( n ) p_{A,a}(n) of their minimal excludant or “mex” of a partition function. By considering the special cases p k , k ​ ( n ) p_{k,k}(n) and p 2 ​ k , k ​ ( n ) p_{2k,k}(n) , they unearthed connections to the rank and crank of partitions and some restricted partitions. In this paper, we build on their work and obtain more general results associating the extended mex function with the number of partitions of an integer with arbitrary bound on the rank and crank. We also derive a new result expressing the smallest parts function of Andrews as a finite sum of the extended mex function in consideration with a curious coefficient. We also obtain a few restricted partition identities with some reminiscent of shifted partition identities. Finally, we define and explore a new minimal excludant for overpartitions.

We recall the partition statistics rank, crank, and

Proof of the main results

We simultaneously state and prove some background theorems and lemmas in the build up to the proof of our main results stated in the previous section. In the following two theorems, we find the generating functions for
p_{A,a}(n)
and
\overline{p}_{A,a}(n)
for arbitrary values of
A
and
a
. These two are at the heart of proving all our subsequent results.

Theorem 14
.

F_{A,a}(q)=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{An(n-1)}{2}+an}
.

Proof.

The generating function for
p_{A,a}(n)
is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{q^{a+(a+A)+\cdots+(a+(2n-1)A)}}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq a+2nA\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(1-q^{m})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q^{a+(a+A)+\cdots+a+(2n-1)A}(1-q^{a+2nA})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q^{2n^{2}A-nA+2na}(1-q^{a+2nA})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(q^{\frac{(2n)^{2}A-(2n)A+2(2n)a}{2}}-q^{\frac{(2n+1)^{2}A-(2n+1)A+2(2n+1)a}{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n^{2}A+(2a-A)n}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{An(n-1)}{2}+an}.

∎

Theorem 15
.

\overline{F}_{A,a}(q)=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{An(n+1)}{2}+a(n+1)}
.

Proof.

The generating function for
\overline{p}_{A,a}(n)
is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{q^{a+(a+A)+(a+2A)+\cdots+(a+2nA)}}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=1\\
m\neq a+(2n+1)A\end{subarray}}^{\infty}(1-q^{m})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q^{a+(a+A)+(a+2A)+\cdots+(a+2nA)}(1-q^{a+(2n+1)A})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}q^{(2n+1)(nA+a)}(1-q^{a+A+2nA})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{(n+1)(nA+2a)}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{An(n+1)}{2}+a(n+1)}.

∎

2.1
Proof of Theorem
4

Proof.

Using Theorem
14
, we have

\displaystyle F_{3,1}(q)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{3,1}(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n-1)}{2}},

\displaystyle F_{3,2}(q)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p_{3,2}(n)q^{n}=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}}.

Adding the two, we have

\displaystyle F_{3,1}(q)+F_{3,2}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(p_{3,1}(n)+p_{3,2}(n))q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}(q^{\frac{n(3n-1)}{2}}+q^{\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\Bigl(2+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}(q^{\frac{n(3n-1)}{2}}+q^{\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=1+\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n},

where the last step follows from Euler’s pentagonal number theorem
[
2
, p. 11]
.

Therefore,
\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(p_{3,1}(n)+p_{3,2}(n))q^{n}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}p(n)q^{n}
. Comparing the coefficients of
q^{n}
on both sides, we obtain the theorem.
∎

Proof.

Theorem

gives

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}p(n)q^{n}\Bigr)\Bigl(1+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(q^{An(2n-1)+a.2n}-q^{A(n-1)(2n-1)+a(2n-1)})\Bigr)

Comparing the coefficients of

Proof.

We have the following generating function for the second rank moment due to Andrews

3
]

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{2}N_{2}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}}(1+q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}}\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)q^{rn}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n+1)}{2}+rn}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)\left(\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{3n(n+1)}{2}+(r+2)(n+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-2}(2r+1)\overline{p}_{3,r+2}(n)\Bigr)q^{n},

where the last step follows from Corollary
17
.

Similarly, the generating function for the second crank moment due to Garvan
[
13
]
is

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{2}M_{2}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\dfrac{(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}(1+q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)q^{rn}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+rn}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r\geq 0}(2r+1)\left(\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+(r+1)(n+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-1}(2r+1)\overline{p}_{1,r+1}(n)\Bigr)q^{n},

where the last step follows from Corollary
17
. Comparing the coefficients of
q^{n}
on both sides of the two equations, we obtain the theorem.
∎

Proof.

Andrews
[

]
gave the following identity relating the smallest parts function to the second rank moment

\text{spt}(n)=np(n)-\frac{1}{2}N_{2}(n).

Dyson
[

]
showed that
\frac{1}{2}M_{2}(n)=np(n)
. Then, using Theorem
8
, we have

\displaystyle\text{spt}(n)=np(n)-\frac{1}{2}N_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}M_{2}(n)-\frac{1}{2}N_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{n-1}(2r+1)(p_{3,r+2}(n)-p_{1,r+1}(n)).

The rest of our results concern the connections of the extended mex function

with partitions of the integer

\psi(-q)=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(2n-1)}=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}}.

Then

\displaystyle(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}(q;q^{4})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(2n-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{n(2n-1)}-\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{2(n+1)^{2}+(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}(q^{\frac{4n(n-1)}{2}+n}-q^{\frac{4n(n+1)}{2}+3(n+1)})

and so,

\frac{1}{(q)_{\infty}}(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}(q;q^{4})_{\infty}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}(p_{4,1}(n)-\overline{p}_{4,3}(n))q^{n}.

Thus, we see that
p_{4,1}(n)-\overline{p}_{4,3}(n)
equals the number of partitions of
n
into parts congruent to
2
modulo
4
. This find indicates the presence of a general class of identities involving congruences of similar type. We discover the two such families of identities in Theorem

Theorem 21

(Jacobi’s triple product identity,

For

\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}z^{n}q^{n^{2}}=\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}(1-q^{2n+2})(1+zq^{2n+1})(1+z^{-1}q^{2n+1}).

(Andrews,
[
2

The following is an equivalent form of Jacobi’s triple product identity, due to Andrews

\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{(2k+1)n(n+1)/2-in}=\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}(1-q^{(2k+1)(n+1)})(1-q^{(2k+1)n+i})(1-q^{(2k+1)(n+1)-i}).

Lemma 23

The following is another equivalent form of Jacobi’s triple product identity:

\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{kn(n+1)-in}=\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}(1-q^{2k(n+1)})(1-q^{2kn+i})(1-q^{2k(n+1)-i}).

The proof follows from substituting

4
Minimal excludant of overpartitions

Analogous to Andrews’ and Newman’s minimal excludant or “mex” of a partition function, we define what we call the “mex” of overpartitions. A surprising but welcome discovery is its connection to the Ramanujan function
R(q)
. This is a work in progress and will appear in a forthcoming paper. We present our initial findings here.

Recall that an overpartition is a partition in which the first occurrence
of a number may be overlined
[
9
]
. For an overpartition
\pi
, we define
\overline{\text{mex}}(\pi)
to be the smallest positive integer that is not a part of
\pi
. For example, if we consider the following two overpartitions

\displaystyle\pi_{1}

\displaystyle=\overline{14}+12+9+7+\overline{4}+3+\overline{2}+1,

\displaystyle\pi_{2}

\displaystyle=\overline{5}+\overline{4}+4+2+1,

then,
\overline{\text{mex}}(\pi_{1})=5
and
\overline{\text{mex}}(\pi_{2})=3
. Let
\overline{m}(n)
denote the number of overpartitions of
n
having the property that no positive integer less than
\overline{\text{mex}}(\pi)
is overlined. As an example, below are the eight overpartitions of
3

\displaystyle 3,\overline{3},2+1,\overline{2}+1,2+\overline{1},\overline{2}+\overline{1},1+1+1,\overline{1}+1+1.

Then, for
\pi\in\{3,\overline{3},2+1,1+1+1\},
the
quantity
\overline{\text{mex}}(\pi)
has the stated property, and hence
\overline{m}(3)=4
.
Let
\overline{M}(q)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{m}(n)q^{n}
. Then

Theorem 30
.

We have
\overline{M}(q)=\overline{P}(q)\,(2-R(q))
, where
\overline{P}(q)=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}
is the generating function for the number of overpartitions of
n
and
R(q)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(-q;q)_{n}}
is Ramanujan’s function from his Lost Notebook.

Remark 31
.

The function
R(q)
has been studied by Andrews
[
1
]
. It is also the generating function for number of partitions into distinct parts with even rank minus those with odd rank.

To prove the above theorem, we need a result of Gasper and Rahman
[
14
, Appendix II, Formula II.5]
, which we state as a lemma below.

Lemma 32
.

{}_{1}\phi_{1}(a;c;q,\frac{c}{a})=\dfrac{(\frac{c}{a};q)_{\infty}}{(c;q)_{\infty}}
.

The proof of the lemma follows easily from a suitable substitution and limit in Heine’s transformation. We replace
a\mapsto q
and
c\mapsto-zq
in the above lemma to get

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}}{(-zq;q)_{n}}=1+z.

(3)

Proof of Theorem

Proof.

By standard combinatorial arguments, we deduce that

\displaystyle\overline{M}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{m}(n)q^{n}

On the computable cross norm in tensor networks and holography
Alexey Milekhin, Pratik Rath, and Wayne Weng

Abstract
The computable cross norm or realignment (CCNR) was recently discussed in Ref. Yin:2022toc as a measure of multipartite entanglement in a condensed matter context. In this short note, we point out that it is closely related to the ( 2 , n ) (2,n) -Rényi reflected entropy, which has been studied in the context of AdS/CFT. We discuss the calculation of the CCNR in random tensor networks as well as holographic CFTs. The holographic dual involves a backreacted entanglement wedge cross section in a geometry sourced by Rényi-2 cosmic branes. We perform explicit calculations for two intervals in a hyperbolic random tensor network as well the vacuum state of a 2D holographic CFT, and analyze the occurence of a connected-to-disconnected phase transition. The example illustrates the validity of the proposal for analytic continuation in holography for arbitrary values of Rényi parameter n n . We comment on a symmetry-resolved generalization of this quantity.

Introduction

The computable cross-norm or realignment (CCNR) is a diagnostic of entanglement for bipartite mixed states derived from the separability criterion of the same name. Given a density matrix
\rho_{AB}
on subsystems
A
and
B
, the realignment matrix
R
is defined as
2002quant.ph..5017C
;
2003JPhA…36.5825R

\bra{a, a'}R\ket{b,b'}:=\bra{a,b}\rho\ket{a',b'},

(1)

where
\{\ket{a}\}
and
\{\ket{b}\}
are orthonormal bases for
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
, respectively. In general, this is a rectangular matrix of dimensions
d_{A}^{2}\times d_{B}^{2}
, with
d_{A},d_{B}
being the corresponding Hilbert space dimensions.The CCNR is then defined as the Schatten 1-norm or trace norm of
R

||R||_{1}=\Tr\left(\sqrt{RR^{\dagger}}\right).

(2)

The usefulness of the CCNR for detecting entanglement arises from the fact that if
\rho
is separable, then
||R||_{1}\leq 1
; therefore, if
||R||_{1}>1
,
\rho_{AB}
must be entangled. The converse is not true.
A particular advantage of this CCNR criterion is that, unlike the commonly-used positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion, it is able to detect some bound entangled states
Rudolph_2005
. (However, it is not strictly stronger than the PPT criterion
peres1996separability
;
horodecki2001separability
since, for example, it is not sufficient for
2\times 2
systems in contrast to the PPT criterion
Rudolph_2005
.) Note that the CCNR is not an entanglement monotone,
1
1

1

Consider two bipartite states
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
that satisfy
||R(\rho_{1})||_{1}<1
and
||R(\rho_{2})||_{1}>1
. Then
||R(\rho\equiv\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})||_{1}=||R(\rho_{1})||_{1}||R(\rho_{2})||_{1}
. Locally tracing out
\rho_{1}
, then increases the value of
||R(\rho)||_{1}
, while tracing out
\rho_{2}
decreases
||R(\rho)||_{1}
.
and thus can not strictly be called an entanglement measure
Rudolph_2005
.

1.1
Replica trick

The trace norm of a matrix is difficult to compute in general, due to the appearance of a matrix square-root. One can instead define a Rényi generalization of the CCNR as
Liu_2022
;
Yin:2022toc

Z_{n}(A:B)=\Tr[\!\left(RR^{\dagger}\right)^{n}\!\Big]

(3)

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, which we call the Rényi CCNR. The CCNR is simply the
n\to 1/2
limit of (
3
),

||R||_{1}=\lim_{n\to 1/2}Z_{n}(A:B)\equiv Z_{1/2}(A:B).

(4)

From now on, we will simply refer to the CCNR as
Z_{1/2}
.

With this definition, the CCNR can be computed via a replica trick analogous to that of the von Neumann entropy and logarithmic negativity. It is easy to show that the Rényi CCNR can be expressed as the expectation value
of permutation operators on
2n
copies of the original system:

Z_{n}(A:B)=\Tr[\rho_{AB}^{\otimes 2n}\left(g_{A}\otimes g_{B}\right)\Big].

(5)

Here
\rho_{AB}^{\otimes 2n}
is the tensor product of
2n
copies of the original density matrix, while
g_{A}=(1\,2)(3\,4)\cdots(2n\!-\!1\,2n)
and
g_{B}=(2\,3)(4\,5)\cdots(2n\,1)
are elements of the symmetric group
\mathcal{S}_{2n}
. The group elements act on the
2n
copies by permuting the states on the corresponding subsystem.

1.2
Relation to reflected entropy

A similar construction to the replica trick in (
5
) previously appeared in the holographic context under the guise of reflected entropy
Dutta:2019gen
.

The reflected entropy is a von Neumann entropy computed in a specific purification of
\rho_{AB}
called the canonical purification. The canonical purification of
\rho_{AB}
is defined as the state
\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Tr\rho_{AB}}}\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}
, where we compute the matrix square-root and then interpret it as a state in the vector space of operators acting on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. This state is a purification of the original density matrix and lives on the doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A^{*}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{*}}
, where the
A^{*},B^{*}
are CPT copies of the
A,B
.

A Rényi generalization of the canonical purification is
\ket{\psi_m}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Tr\rho_{AB}^{m}}}\ket{\rho_{AB}^{m/2}}
, which is useful since it can be constructed using a replica trick for
m\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Given the state
\ket{\psi_m}
, we can trace out
\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B^{*}}
to obtain the density matrix
\rho_{AA^{*}}^{(m)}
, whose Rényi entropy can then be computed:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\rho_{AA^{*}}^{(m)}=\Tr_{BB^{*}}\left(\ket{\psi_m}\bra{\psi_m}\right)=\frac{1}{\Tr{\rho_{AB}^m}}\Tr_{BB^{*}}\left(\ket{\rho_{AB}^{m/2}}\bra{\rho_{AB}^{m/2}}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle S_{R}^{(m,n)}(A:B)=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\Tr\left[\left(\rho_{AA^{*}}^{(m)}\right)^{n}\right].

The above quantity is referred to as the
(m,n)
-Rényi reflected entropy.

Now, the key observation is that the replica trick for the Rényi CCNR defined in (
5
) is identical to that of the
(2,n)
-Rényi reflected entropy.

On the other hand, the unnormalized density matrix is related to the one in (

The goal of this paper is to initiate the study of the CCNR in a holographic context. In particular, we will compute

in simple examples of holographic states and, based on the result, propose a holographic dual for general semiclassical states. Let us now briefly summarize the rest of the paper.

In Section

Hyperbolic networks

As an illustrative example, we compute

2.2
General formula

Finally, we propose a general formula for the Rényi CCNR in random tensor network states in terms of bulk minimal surfaces. First, let us define some notation. In the connected phase, let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{AB}
be the surface bounding the squeezed/expanded entanglement wedge and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{A:B}
its cross-section. Then, the Rényi CCNR is given by the following expression

\log Z_{n}\simeq-\log\chi\min_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{AB}\sim A\cup B}\big\{2(n-1)|\widetilde{\gamma}_{A:B}|+n|\widetilde{\gamma}_{AB}|\big\},

(15)

where the minimization is over all wedges homologous to
A\cup B
. This is the same as the triway cut with tensions
\{2(n-1),n,n\}

Akers:2024pgq
. In the disconnected phase, the cross-section goes away and the formula simplifies to

\log Z_{n}\simeq{-n\log\chi\left(|\gamma_{A}|+|\gamma_{B}|\right)},

(16)

which agrees with the second Rényi entropy.

3
CCNR in holographic duality

Having seen how to compute Rényi CCNR in random tensor networks, let us now consider the same quantity for holographic theories. We will first propose a replica construction of the bulk geometry which computes the Rényi CCNR for semiclassical states in general holographic theories to leading order in the gravitational constant
G_{N}
. The discussion will closely follow an analogous construction for holographic negativity
2021JHEP…06..024D
. Then, we specialize to the vacuum of two-dimensional holographic CFTs where we can obtain an explicit expression at leading order.

3.1
General replica construction

To begin, we can express the replica trick in (
5

Z_{n}\simeq\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{2n}}{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{2n}},

(17)

where
\mathcal{Z}_{2n}
is the boundary partition function on a
2n

M_{2n}^{A,B}

2n
copies of the original boundary spacetime
M_{1}
on
A
and
B

and
g_{B}

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
subgroup under the permutation
(1\,3\,5\dots 2n-1)(2\,4\,6\dots 2n)
.

2

\mathcal{Z}_{2n}\simeq e^{-I\left[B_{2n}\right]}=e^{-nI\left[\hat{B}_{2n}\right]}.

The quotient space has conical defects at the fixed point locus
\gamma^{(n)}_{A:B}
with opening angle

M_{2}^{AB}

\hat{B}[M_{2}^{AB},\gamma_{A:B}^{(n)}]

\mathcal{Z}_{2n}\simeq e^{-nI\left[M_{2}^{AB},\gamma_{A:B}^{(n)}\right]}.

\log Z_{n}=-n\left(I\left[M_{2}^{AB},\gamma_{A:B}^{(n)}\right]-2I\left[M_{1}\right]\right).

4
Discussion

In this paper, we observed a direct link between the CCNR and its Rényi generalization, and the
(2,n)
-Rényi reflected entropy. We evaluated the Rényi CCNR for random tensor networks and holographic CFTs. In both cases, the Rényi CCNR has a connected-to-disconnected phase transition which, for holographic CFTs, is related to a Hawking-Page phase transition between thermal AdS and a BTZ black hole. We demonstrated explicit examples where one can observe the presence of a squeezed or expanded entanglement wedge cross-section, depending on the Rényi index.

We now comment on some potential future directions.

Symmetry-resolved CCNR:

One can instead consider a charged BTZ black hole in the bulk. What does it correspond to at the boundary? The answer is that it corresponds to the symmetry-resolved
Belin:2013uta
;
Goldstein:2017bua
;
Xavier:2018kqb
;
Casini:2019kex
;
Milekhin:2021lmq
;
Zhao:2020qmn
version of CCNR in the connected phase. Symmetry-resolved quantities arise whenever a density matrix can be decomposed into block diagonal form (for example, labeled by representations of a global symmetry). Then one can assign different weights to these blocks. Previously, symmetry-resolution for the reflected entropy was addressed in
Bueno:2020vnx
. The symmetry-resolution is based on the following general statement: if the state
\ket{\psi}
is invariant under a certain global symmetry,
e^{i\mu Q}\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi}
and that symmetry has a split property,
10
10

10

In simple terms it means that the charge
Q
is the integral of a local quantity.
then the reduced density matrix
\rho_{AB}
on
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
is also invariant:

e^{i\mu Q_{AB}}\rho_{AB}=\rho_{AB}e^{i\mu Q_{AB}},\ Q_{AB}=Q_{A}+Q_{B}.

(34)

In terms of the canonical purification, it means that the state
\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}
on

\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}^{*}
is invariant under the following action:

e^{i\mu Q_{AB}-i\mu Q_{A^{*}B^{*}}}\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}=\ket{\sqrt{\rho_{AB}}}.

(35)

Tracing out
BB^{*}
implies that
\rho_{AA^{*}}
and
RR^{\dagger}
are invariant under
e^{i\mu Q_{A}-i\mu Q_{A^{*}}}
. Now we can introduce the symmetry-resolved Rényi CCNR (cf. (
3
)):

Z_{n}(\mu)=\Tr\left[(RR^{\dagger})^{n}e^{i\mu Q_{A}-i\mu Q_{A^{*}}}\right].

(36)

It is not difficult to see that once we glue all the replicas together, there will be an extra Wilson loop wrapping one of the torus cycles. Holographically, this corresponds to turning on a chemical potential for the black hole. Again, we would expect two geometries, either a black hole or AdS, to potentially dominate, leading to a phase transition. Similarly, we can introduce a chemical potential for rotations, which is the angular momentum. If the angular momentum is imaginary, other black holes (related to BTZ by
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
transformations) may become dominant
Dijkgraaf:2000fq
.

Interestingly, the above construction introduces an electric Wilson loop around only one of the torus cycles. In the path integral it is straightforward to introduce a Wilson loop around the other cycle. However, its operational meaning is not clear, as the corresponding charge operator acts on a region outside
AA^{*}
. We leave this question for future work.

Let us conclude with a list of other open questions:

•

We computed the leading contribution to the CCNR at large
c
, which has a universal form. It would be interesting to analyze subleading quantum corrections in some examples by computing the one-loop determinants.

•

As we saw, the CFT result is well-defined for
n<1/2
, whereas the naïve random tensor network computation reveals a square-root branch cut below
n=1/2
. It would be interesting to understand the nature of this branch cut in the random tensor network calculation. Moreover, it would be interesting to understand the validity of our proposed analytic continuation in other holographic settings, especially for
n<1
.

•

It would be illustrative to see how the full CFT answer emerges in random tensor network once we sum over all possible fixed-area states. This may help understand better tensor network models of holography (for instance, see
dong2023rtn
;
dong2024entanglementnegativityreplicasymmetry
;
Cheng:2022ori
).

•

The Rényi CCNR at certain values of
n
is related to other known information quantities. Apart from the CCNR at
n=1/2
, it is also equal to the purity when
n=1
and related to the multipartite measure studied in
Penington:2022dhr
when
n=2
. It would be interesting to understand whether any such relations exist for other values of
n
.

•

It is well-known that thermal AdS and BTZ are not the only solutions of three-dimensional gravity; there is a whole family parametrized by the group
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})

Dijkgraaf:2000fq
;
Kraus:2006wn
. In our case of purely imaginary
\tau
all other saddles are subdominant. Nevertheless, it would be interesting to see if they have some relevance here, especially near the phase transition.

•

The CCNR is not a useful diagnostic of entanglement for subregions in quantum field theory due to the presence of UV divergences. It would be interesting to study the CCNR in setups where it is manifestly finite, for example in multiboundary wormholes.

•

Finally, through (
8
) our CFT expression for Rényi CCNR gives a prediction for
S_{R}^{(2,n)}
in the vacuum state of two-dimensional CFTs. It would be interesting to compare this answer with an explicit calculation of
S_{R}^{(2,n)}
in CFT using the monodromy method. In particular, in two-dimensional large-
c
CFTs we can give an explicit expression for the entire range of cross-ratio.

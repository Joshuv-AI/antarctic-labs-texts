The continuity of curvature flow in Fréchet distance
Shiyi Ma

Abstract
We study the evolution of a Jordan curve on the plane by curvature flow, also known as curve shortening flow, and by level-set flow, which is a weak formulation of curvature flow. We show that the evolution of the curve depends continuously on the initial curve in Fréchet distance. This is an extension of Michael Dobbins’ work on the evolution of Jordan curves bisecting the 2 2 -sphere by curvature flow.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{J}
denote the set of Jordan curves (which are also called simple closed curves) with zero Lebesgue measure, on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Michael Gage and Richard Hamilton proved that all smooth convex curves eventually contract to a point without forming any other singularities
[
4
]
, and Matthew Grayson proved that every non-convex curve will eventually become convex
[
3
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{J}_{T}
be a subset of
\mathfrak{J}
for which the curvature flow problem has a solution up to time
T
, for
0<T<\infty
.
Define a function
\cf:\mathfrak{J}_{T}\times[0,T)_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to\mathfrak{J}
by evolution by curvature flow. The main result of this article is that this function is continuous in Fréchet distance.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma_{k}\in\mathfrak{J}_{T}
, and
t_{k}\in[0,T)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
for
k\in\{1,...,\infty\}
. If
\gamma_{k}\to\gamma_{\infty}
in Fréchet distance and
t_{k}\to t_{\infty}
, then
\cf(\gamma_{k},t_{k})\to\cf(\gamma_{\infty},t_{\infty})
in Fréchet distance.

The challenge of proving Theorem
1.1
is to prove Lemma
3.2
where given a curve
\gamma
, we need to show the existence of a curve that always intersects
\gamma
at exactly two points until
\gamma
shrinks to a point. To prove Lemma
3.2
, we construct a family of Jordan curves through homotopy, and prove that curves in this family instantly evolve into three classes, among which one class contains curves that intersect
\gamma
at exactly two points throughout the lifetime of
\gamma
.

1.1
Definitions and notations

We denote real intervals by
(a,b]_{{\mathbb{R}}}=\{x\in R:a<x\leq b\}
for bounds
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
any combination of round or square brackets for (half) open or closed intervals. We also similarly denote segments in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by
[a,b]_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}=\{ta+(1-t)b:t\in[0,1]_{{\mathbb{R}}}\}
for
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
We denote the unit circle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by
\mathbf{S}^{1}
. Given a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, we denote the interior of
A
by
\tr A
.
For
X\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\delta>0
, let
X\oplus\delta
be the set of points at most distance
\delta
from
X
. Every Jordan curve on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is treated as a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Note that for every Jordan curve
\gamma
,
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\gamma
consists of two components. We denote
the bounded component, which is the interior of the region enclosed by
\gamma
by
\mathcal{I}(\gamma)
, and the other unbounded component by
\mathcal{O}(\gamma)
.
Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
be two Jordan curves on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. The Hausdorff distance is defined by

\dist_{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\inf\{\delta\ |\ \gamma_{1}\subseteq\gamma_{2}\oplus\delta,\gamma_{2}\subseteq\gamma_{1}\oplus\delta\}.

The Fréchet distance on
\mathfrak{J}
is defined by

\dist_{F}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\inf\limits_{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}}\sup\limits_{x}||\varphi_{1}(x)-\varphi_{2}(x)||,

where
\varphi_{i}:{\mathbf{S}}^{1}\to\gamma_{i}
are homeomorphisms.
Let
\mathfrak{J}_{c^{\infty}}
be the set of smooth Jordan curves. Given a smooth curve
\gamma\in\mathfrak{J}_{C^{\infty}}
and a point
p\in\gamma
, let
\mathrm{kn}(\gamma,p)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
denote the vector of curvature of
\gamma
at
p
. A solution to the curvature flow problem for a given initial curve
\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{J}
and stopping time
T\in(0,\infty)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, is a map
\Gamma:{\mathbf{S}}^{1}\times(0,T)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
\partial_{t}\Gamma(x,t)=\mathrm{kn}(\Gamma({\mathbf{S}}^{1},t),\Gamma(x,t))
and
\Gamma({\mathbf{S}}^{1},t)\to\gamma_{0}
in Fréchet distance as
t\to 0
. Let

\cf(\gamma_{0},t)=\Gamma({\mathbf{S}}^{1},t),

where
\Gamma
is the solution to the curvature flow problem starting from
\gamma_{0}
, provided that a unique solution exists.

1.2
Level-set flow

Given an oriented curve
\gamma\in\mathfrak{J}
, let
\alpha_{k}\in\mathfrak{J}
be a sequence of curves approaching
\gamma
from one side of
\gamma
, and
\beta_{k}\in\mathfrak{J}
be a sequence of curves approaching
\gamma
from the other side of
\gamma
. Now, let
\{A_{k}\}
be the sequence of nested annuli between
\alpha_{k}

and
\beta_{k}
and let
A_{k}(t)
be the annulus between
\alpha_{k}(t)
and
\beta_{k}(t)
, the time
t
evolutions of
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
by curvature flow. The avoidance principle says that if two curves are initially disjoint then they remain disjoint throughout their evolution, for as long as a solution exists. Since
\gamma\subset A_{k}
for all
k\in\{1,...,\infty\}
, the avoidance principle implies that for any
t>0
,
\cf(\gamma,t)\subset\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}(t)
. By Lauer’s Proposition 8.3
[
5
]
,
\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}(t)
is the level-set flow of
\gamma
. Lauer also showed that if the Lebesgue measure of the initial curve is zero, then the level-set flow immediately becomes a smooth Jordan curve evolving by curvature flow
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
. Therefore, the level-set flow is the unique solution to the curvature flow problem in this case, which implies
\cf(\gamma,t)=\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}(t)
.

Previous results

In this section, we list out the lemmas about Jordan curves bisecting the
2
-sphere, proved by Michael Dobbins
[
2
]
. These lemmas also apply to Jordan curves in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
\gamma\in\mathfrak{J}
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
\delta=\delta(\gamma,\epsilon)>0
such
that for every Jordan curve
\eta\subset(\gamma\oplus\delta)
, there is a continuous map
\varphi:\eta\to\gamma
such
that for all
x\in\eta,\ \ ||\varphi(x)-x||<\epsilon
.

Note that
\varphi
is not necessarily bijective, so this does not provide an upper bound on Fréchet
distance.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\gamma_{k}\in\mathfrak{J}_{T}
and
t_{k}\in[0,T)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
for
k\in\{1,...,\infty\}
. If
\gamma_{k}\to\gamma_{\infty}

in Fréchet distance and
t_{k}\to t_{\infty}
, then
\cf(\gamma_{k},t_{k})\to\cf(\gamma_{\infty},t_{\infty})
in Hausdorff distance.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathfrak{J}_{S}
be a pair of curves that intersect at finitely many points, and let
p_{T}\in(\cf(\gamma_{1},T)\cap\cf(\gamma_{2},T))
be a point of intersection at time
T\in(0,S)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Then, there is a continuous trajectory
p:[0,T]_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for all
t\in[0,T]_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, we have
p(t)\in(\cf(\gamma_{1},T)\cap\cf(\gamma_{2},T))
and
p(T)=p_{T}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\gamma,\eta\in\mathfrak{J}
be a pair of curves that intersect at only 2 points, and let
x
be one of the points of intersection. By the time the two points of intersection merge, there is a unique continuous trajectory
p(\gamma,\eta,x):[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that for all
t
we have
p(\gamma,\eta,x;t)\in(\cf(\gamma,t)\cap\cf(\eta,t))
and
p(\gamma,\eta,x;0)=x
. Furthermore,
p(\gamma,\eta,x,t)
is continuous as a function of
\gamma
and
\eta
in Fréchet distance and
x
and
t
.

The proofs of these lemmas are analogous to Dobbins’s proofs. One difference is that in Lemma
2.2
and
2.4
, we need to define a closed circular region
\mathbf{D}
that contains
\gamma_{\infty}
, because we need to use the compactness of
\mathbf{D}
to show that some subsequence is convergent. The arguments we need for the proofs are as follows:

Suppose the diameter of
\gamma_{\infty}
is
d
. Let
\mathbf{D}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a closed circular region with the diameter
d+1
such that
\gamma_{\infty}\subset\mathbf{D}
. Since
\gamma_{\infty}
and
\partial\mathbf{D}
are disjoint and
\partial\mathbf{D}
encloses
\gamma_{\infty}
, by the avoidance principle, the shrinking circle
\cf(\partial\mathbf{D},t)
encloses
\cf(\gamma_{\infty},t)
for all
t\in[0,T]_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Therefore,
\cf(\gamma_{\infty},t)\subset\mathbf{D}
for all
t\in[0,T]_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Dobbins proved these two lemmas for Jordan curves on the
2
-sphere by using the compactness of the
2
-sphere. Another difference is that for Lemma
2.3
, we need to change geodesic curvature in Dobbins’s proof to curvature in the argument that the transversal intersection point of two curves evolving by curvature flow continues to be a point of intersection provided that the intersection remains transversal.

Proof of continuity

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathfrak{J}
be a pair of distinct curves that have a common arc

\mathcal{A}=\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}

4
Conclusions

We showed that the evolution of a Jordan curve on the plane by curvature flow depends continuously on the initial curve in Fréchet distance.
The theoretical results we obtain in this work
helps to connect this geometric evolution to the broader framework of functional analysis, providing a deeper understanding of the underlying mathematical structures. The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on Joseph Lauer’s theorem that a nonsmooth curve in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
instantly becomes smooth and has length bounded by some constant
[
5
]
. Therefore, it remains an open question whether our results can be extended to Jordan curves on other surfaces. Another promising research direction can be the study of the continuity of surfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, where the challenge in establishing continuity arises from the potential formation of singularities, such as the neckpinch singularity.

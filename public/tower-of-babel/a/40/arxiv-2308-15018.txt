Weak Leibniz algebras and Transposed Poisson algebras
A.S. Dzhumadil’daev

Abstract
. An algebra with identities [ a , b ] ​ c = 2 ​ a ​ ( b ​ c ) − 2 ​ b ​ ( a ​ c ) , a ⁡ [ b , c ] = 2 ​ ( a ​ b ) ​ c − 2 ​ ( a ​ c ) ​ b [a,b]c=2a(bc)-2b(ac),a[b,c]=2(ab)c-2(ac)b is called weak Leibniz. Introduction

1.1.
Leibniz algberas and weak Leibniz algebras

An algebra
A
is called
weak Leibniz
, if it satisfies the following identities

[a,b]c=2\,a(bc)-2\,b(ac),

a[b,c]=2\,(ab)c-2\,(ac)b,

for any
a,b,c\in A.
Here

[a,b]=ab-ba

is Lie commutator.

Example.
An algebra is called (two-sided) Leibniz
[
4
]
, if

(ab)c=a(bc)-b(ac),

a(bc)=(ab)c-(ac)b,

are identities. Any Leibniz algebra is weak Leibniz.

Proof repeats arguments of J.L. Loday.
By left Leibniz identity

(ab)c=a(bc)-b(ac),

(ba)c=b(ac)-a(bc).

Addition of these relations gives us Loday’s observation: anti-commutator of any elements of left-Leibniz algebra is left central,

\{a,b\}c=0.

If we use substraction of these relations instead of addition we obtain left weak Leibniz identity

[a,b]c=2(a(bc)-b(ac)).

Similarly, by right Leibniz identity

a(bc)=(ab)c-(ac)b,

a(cb)=(ac)b-(ab)c,

and addition of these relations show that anti-commutator of any elements of right-Leibniz algebra is right-central,

a\{b,c\}=0.

If we use substraction instead of addition we obtain right weak Leibniz identity

a[b,c]=2((ab)c-(ac)b)).

In particular any Lie algebra is weak Leibniz. Important property of Leibniz agebras.
If
L
is simple Leibniz, then
L
is Lie.
Proof is easy.
Since anti-commutators generate an ideal,
\{a,b\}=0
for any
a,b\in L.
This means that
L
is Lie.

Example.
Let
U
be associative commutative algebra with derivation
\partial:U\rightarrow U
and for any
u,v\in U
endow
U
by new multiplication

\times=\times_{u,v}
constructed by

a\times b=u(a\partial(b)-b(\partial(a))+vab.

Then an algebra with vector space
U
and multiplication
\times
is weak Leibniz. In general this algebra is not Leibniz.

Example.
Let
S
be finite set of indices and
\varepsilon_{s}\in{\bf R}
for any
s\in S.
An infinite dimensional algebra
A(S)
with base elements
e_{i},i\in{\bf Z}
and multiplication

e_{i}\times e_{j}=(j-i)e_{i+j}+\sum_{s\in S}\varepsilon_{s}e_{i+j+s}

is weak Leibniz. It is simple. Proof of this fact is easy also.

Denote by
A(S)^{-}
an algebra with base
\{e_{i},i\in{\bf Z}\}
and multiplication

[a,b]=1/2(a\times b-b\times a).
Then
[e_{i},e_{j}]=(j-i)e_{i+j},
and the algebra
A(S)^{-}
is isomorphic to Witt algebra (= Lie algebra of formal vector fields on the circle). If
A(S)
has an ideal
J
then
J
should be an ideal of the algebra
A(S)^{-}.
Since Witt algebra is simple,
J
should be trivial. So, the algebra
A(S)
is simple.

These arguments show that classes of Leibniz algebras and weak Leibniz algebras are different.
We do not know whether exists simple weak Leibniz algebras except algebras of a form
A(S).

1.2.
Dialgebas, polarizations and depolarizations

A dialgebra is a linear space with two binary operations.
A dialgebra
(A,\circ,\bullet)
is called
polarization
of algebra
(A,\times)
if

a\circ b=\frac{1}{2}(a\times b-b\times a),

a\bullet b=\frac{1}{2}(a\times b+b\times a),

for any
a,b\in A.
Conversely, an algebra
(A,\times)
is called
depolarization
of
dialgebra
(A,\circ,\bullet)
if

a\times b=a\circ b+a\bullet b

for any
a,b\in A.

A subspace
J\subseteq A
is called
ideal
of dialgebra
(A,\circ,\bullet),
if

J
is ideal of algebra
(A,\circ),

a\circ b\in J,\quad b\circ a\in J,

for any
a\in J,b\in A
and
J
is ideal of algebra
(A,\bullet),

a\bullet b\in J,\quad b\bullet a\in J,

for any
a\in J,b\in A.
It is clear that ideal of dialgebra
(A,\circ,\bullet)
generates an ideal of its depolarization
(A,\times).
Conversely, an ideal of algebra
(A,\times)
generates an ideal of its polarization
(A,\circ,\bullet).
Therefore, the following statement holds.

Proposition 1.1
.

A dialgebra
(A,\circ,\bullet)
is simple if and only if its polarization
(A,\times)
is simple.

1.3.
Transposed Poisson algebras

A dialgebra
(A,\circ,\bullet)
is called
transposed Poisson

[
1
]
if

•

an algebra
(A,\circ)
is Lie

•

an algebra
(A,\bullet)
is associative commutative, and

•

associative part acts on Lie part as
1/2
-derivation,

2\,a\bullet(b\circ c)-(a\bullet b)\circ c-b\circ(a\bullet c)=0,

for any
a,b,c\in A.

1.4.
Main results

Theorem 1.2
.

If
(A,\times)
is weak Leibniz over a field of characteristic
p\neq 2,3,
then its polarization
(A,\circ,\bullet)
is transposed Poisson.

Conversely, if
(A,\circ,\bullet)
is transposed Poisson algebra over a field of characteristic
p\neq 2,
then
its depolarization
(A,\times)
is weak Leibniz.

Corollary 1.3
.

(p\neq 2,3)
An algebra is weak Leibniz iff its polarization is transposed Poisson.

Theorem 1.4
.

(p\neq 2,3)
Weak Leibniz operad is self-dual, i.e., its Koszul dual is equivalent to weak Leibniz operad.
Weak Leibniz operad is not Koszul.

Remark.
Any weak Leibniz algebra is associative-admissible and two-sided Alia, but not Poisson-admissible. In particular, any weak Leibniz algebra is Lie-admissible and 1-Alia. On Alia algebras see

3.1.
Novikov-Poisson algebras

An algebra

Abstract
. We consider a population growth model given by a two-type continuous-state branching process with immigration and competition, introduced by Ma in [ 17 ] . We study the relative frequency of one of the types in the population when the total mass is forced to be constant at a dense set of times. The resulting process is described as the solution to an SDE, which we call the culled frequency process , generalizing the Λ \Lambda -asymmetric frequency process introduced by Caballero et al. in [ 2 ] . We obtain conditions for the culled frequency process to have a moment dual and show that it is given by a branching-coalescing continuous-time Markov chain that describes the genealogy of the two-type CBI with competition. Finally, we obtain a large population limit of the culled frequency process, resulting in a deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE). Two particular cases of the limiting ODE are studied to determine if general two-type branching mechanisms and general Malthusians can lead to the coexistence of the two types in the population.



1.
Introduction

The main result by Birkner et al. in
[
1
]
shows that the frequency process describing the proportion of the mass descended from an ancestral individual associated with a measure-valued branching process can be transformed into a
\Lambda
-Fleming–Viot process using a suitable time change involving a functional of the total mass if and only if the measure-valued process is
\alpha
-stable.
In particular, this implies that if
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
are independent and identically distributed
\alpha
-stable continuous-state branching processes (CSBPs), then the ratio process
R=X^{(1)}/(X^{(1)}+X^{(2)})
can be time-changed to become Markovian.
This allows describing the time-changed genealogy of an
\alpha
-stable CSBP through the block-counting process of a
\mathrm{Beta}(2-\alpha,\alpha)
-coalescent that is in moment duality to the aforementioned Markovian version of the frequency process.
Foucart and Hénard, in
[
4
]
, extended this result by adding an immigration term to the CSBP, making it a continuous-state branching process with immigration (CBI).
This modification is reflected in the genealogy, which is now described by the block-counting process of a
\mathrm{Beta}(2-\alpha,\alpha-1)
-coalescent, where the difference with the result of Birkner et al. is due to a mutation mechanism in the genealogy that arises because of immigration in the CBI.
Nonetheless, their work remains in the independent and identically distributed
\alpha
-stable case as they use the time change methodology of Birkner et al.

In
[
2
]
, Caballero et al. studied the case where
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
are modeled by CBIs with different reproduction mechanisms that are not necessarily
\alpha
-stable, enabling the study of a population with two species reproducing by different mechanisms.
In their work, the processes are still assumed to be independent.
The time change perspective does not apply here, see Lemma 3.5 of
[
1
]
.
Instead, inspired by the works
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
of Gillespie, Caballero et al. used a novel method called culling (see section 4.2
[
2
]
) that allows them to control the total population size, see Section
3.3
.
By using this technique, they were able to extend the result proved by Birkner et al. in
[
1
]
to non-
\alpha
-stable cases with different reproduction mechanisms.
The resulting frequency process was called the
\Lambda
-asymmetric frequency process
.

In certain cases, the
\Lambda
-asymmetric frequency process has a moment dual that is the block-counting process of a branching-coalescing process, which is reminiscent of the ancestral selection graph of Krone and Neuhauser
[
14
]
.
The resulting genealogy includes mutation and pairwise branching, reflecting, respectively, immigration in the associated two-type CBI and efficiency in resource consumption (as in
[
11
]
).

Gónzalez-Casanova et al. in
[
8
]
dropped the independent and identically distributed assumptions made by Birkner et al. on
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
by considering a two-type CBI
X=(X^{(1)},X^{(2)})
.
Gónzalez-Casanova et al. showed that the frequency process
R
can still be time-changed to be Markovian by keeping only the
\alpha
-stability assumption on the associated two-type CBI.
In some cases, the time-changed frequency process coincides with the
\beta
-Fleming–Viot process defined by Griffiths in
[
12
]
, which is the weighted moment dual to the block-counting process of a two-type branching-coalescing process.
This connection is enlightening as it implies that the time-changed genealogy of the two-type
\alpha
-stable CBI
X
is described by a branching-coalescing continuous-time Markov chain.

This paper aims to generalize these results to a population with two types of individuals (
1
and
2
), where: i) individuals of one type might produce individuals of the other type (cross-branching); ii) the reproduction mechanisms of each one of the two types might be different; iii) there might be limited resources for which both types compete, leading to different carrying capacities according to the efficiency of the types in resource usage.
The two types of individuals will be modeled by
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
, the components of a two-type population growth model
X
that extends a CBI using general Malthusians (inspired by Lambert’s branching process with logistic growth
[
15
]
), introduced by Ma in
[
17
]
, which we call a two-type CBI with competition.
Hence, in this paper, we will not assume that
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
are independent, identically distributed nor
\alpha
-stable.
Our approach consists of applying the culling technique developed in
[
2
]
to obtain the frequency process of individuals of type
1
in the population, under the assumption that the total population size remains constant in a dense set of times. We call the resulting process the
culled frequency process
.
This will allow us to study the dynamics of the genetic profile of populations with two types of individuals where the two types are closely related by the reproduction mechanisms and the competition for limited resources.

The model introduced in this paper can be valuable for studying populations with two types of individuals that interact and compete for limited resources (see
[
19
]
for an example in ecology).
By considering competition and resource constraints, our model provides insights into how genetic diversity is maintained or altered, and how certain traits prevail or coexist under selective pressures. This framework is a powerful tool for exploring genetic variation, reproduction, mutation, and competition in evolutionary biology, ecology, and conservation biology.

The outline of the paper is the following.
In section
2
, we establish the basic notation used throughout the paper and also introduce the population growth model
X
studied in this work.
In section
3
we define the culled frequency process of individuals of type 1, characterizing its dynamics through a stochastic differential equation (SDE), see (
3.12
), that is seen to be the limit of the culling procedure, given in section
3.3
. The main result in section
3
is Proposition
3.2
, which ensures the existence of a unique strong solution to the SDE (
3.12
).
The proof relies on a simple but enlightening extension of Theorem 3.2 in
[
5
]
.
With the culled frequency process defined, in section
4
, we establish a result stating that whenever it has a moment dual, it is a branching-coalescing continuous-time Markov chain, describing the genealogy of the two-type CBI with competition.
Finally, in section
5
, the large population limit of the frequency process is considered, resulting in a deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE), see (
5.2
).
Two particular cases of the limiting ODE are studied to determine if general two-type branching mechanisms and general Malthusians can lead to the coexistence of the two types in the population.

3.
The culled frequency process

Our motivation is to study the dynamics of the genetic profile of a population consisting of two types of individuals who might reproduce using different mechanisms that in general are not independent, subject to competition and immigration.
To this end, we start this section by introducing two processes that characterize the relative frequency of individuals of type
1
and the total size of the population.

3.1.
Total population and frequency process

We start by defining the total population process
Z
by

Z_{t}=X_{t}^{(1)}+X_{t}^{(2)},\quad t\geq 0,\ Z_{0}=z,

where we assume that
z>0
, recalling that
z=x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}
.
Additionally, we introduce the frequency process of type
1
individuals given by

R_{t}=\frac{X_{t}^{(1)}}{X_{t}^{(1)}+X_{t}^{(2)}}1_{\{t<\zeta\}}+\dagger 1_{\{t\geq\zeta\}},\quad t\geq 0,\ R_{0}=r,

where
\dagger
is a cemetery state,
\zeta:=\inf\{t\geq 0:Z_{t}\in\{0,\infty\}\}
and
r=x^{(1)}/(x^{(1)}+x^{(2)})

We introduce the cemetery state in order to define the process
R
when the process
Z
explodes or reaches zero in finite time.
We note that the frequency process
R
is not Markovian.
To address this issue, throughout this section, we will use the culling method developed in
[

]
to construct a Markov process that encodes the dynamics of the frequency process

It is readily seen that

Then the process

This is formalized by a local martingale problem that will be stated in the

3.3.
Culling

We now turn to show that we can recover the dynamics of
R
with a Markovian process
by applying the culling method, which we recall is a sampling technique,
inspired by the works of Gillespie

[
6
,
7
]

that takes advantage of the Markovianity of
(R,Z)
and restarts the process with
total population
z>0
at every sample time.

Before going into the technical aspect of the process, let us describe the underlying idea behind it.
For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let the process

\{(R_{t\wedge\tau},Z_{t\wedge\tau}):t\geq 0\}
evolve starting from the
point
(r,z)
, where
\tau
is defined in Section
3.1
.
At time
t=1/n
we sample the first coordinate
R_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}
,
which serves as the value to which a continuous-time Markov chain
\overline{R}^{(z,n)}
jumps to.
Then, by using that
(R,Z)
is a homogeneous Markov process, we restart the
process
\{(R_{t\wedge\tau},Z_{t\wedge,\tau})\}
from the point

(R_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau},z)
by culling (or growing) the population accordingly.
The procedure just described is then followed inductively.
Hence, we construct a collection of continuous-time pure jump processes
\{\overline{R}^{(z,n)}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that for a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the trajectory
t\mapsto\overline{R}_{t}^{(z,n)}
follows the first coordinate of the bivariate process
(R,Z)
, which is observed discretely, and with the property that as
n
tends to infinity, the fluctuations of
Z
around
z
become zero in a dense set of times over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Formally, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define a pure-jump Markov process

\overline{R}^{(z,n)}
with jump times
\{T_{m}^{(n)}:m\geq 1\}
given by
independent exponentially distributed random variables with common rate
n
.
We denote the law of the process started at
r\in[0,1]
by
P_{r}
.
The transition kernel
\rho^{(z,n)}:[0,1]\times\mathcal{B}([0,1])\to[0,1]

of the process will be given by

\rho^{(z,n)}(r,A)\equiv P_{r}\bigl(\overline{R}_{T_{1}^{(n)}}^{(z,n)}\in A\bigr):=\mathbb{P}_{(r,z)}\bigl(R_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}\in A,Z_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\bigr),

where
\mathbb{P}_{(r,z)}
stands for the law of
(R,Z)
started in
(r,z)
.
With these specifications the infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{A}^{(z,n)}
of

\overline{R}^{(z,n)}
is bounded,
see Proposition 17.2 in
[
13
]
,
and for
f\in C([0,1])
we get

(3.34)

\mathcal{A}^{(z,n)}f(r)=n\int_{[0,1]}\bigl(f(y)-f(r)\bigr)\rho^{(z,n)}(r,dy).

Using this process we can obtain an analogous result to Theorem 4.1 of

[
2
]
, which states that the sequence of processes

R^{(z,n)}
converges weakly to the unique solution of (
3.12

Theorem 3.1
.

For any fixed
z>0
and
T>0
,
\overline{R}^{(z,n)}\Rightarrow R^{(z,r)}

as
n\to\infty
whenever
\overline{R}_{0}^{(z,n)}\Rightarrow R_{0}^{(z,r)}=r

as
n\to\infty
in

D([0,T],[0,1])
with the
J_{1}
Skorokhod topology.

The proof of the previous result follows by a minor modification of the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
2
]
.
We include the proof for completeness.

Proof.

We start by noting that
[0,1]
is compact and, by Proposition
3.3
,
R^{(z,r)}
is Feller.
Therefore, due to Theorem 17.28 in
[
13
]
, it is enough to prove that,
for any

,

\mathcal{A}^{(z,n)}f(r)\to\mathcal{L}^{(z)}f(r)\quad\text{uniformly for $r\in[0,1]$ as $n\to\infty$,}

where
\mathcal{L}^{(z)}
is the generator defined in (
3.30
).
By definition of
\rho^{(z,n)}
and (
3.34
) we obtain

(3.35)

\mathcal{A}^{(z,n)}f(r)=n\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{(r,z)}\bigl[f(R_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau})\bigr]-f(r)\Bigr).

Due to Proposition
3.1
we know that, for any
f\in C^{2}([0,1])

f(R_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau})-f(r)=\int_{0}^{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}\mathcal{G}f(R_{s},Z_{s})ds+M_{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}^{f},

where

is a local martingale and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}f(r,z)={}

\displaystyle\bigl[\widetilde{D}(r)+S(r)+m(r)\bigr]f^{\prime}(r)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma(r)^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle+rz\int_{U_{2}}\biggl(f\biggl(r+(1-r)\frac{w_{1}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}-r\frac{w_{2}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\biggr)-f(r)-(1-r)\frac{w_{1}}{z}f^{\prime}(r)\biggr)\mu_{1}(dw)

\displaystyle+(1-r)z\int_{U_{2}}\biggl(f\biggl(r+(1-r)\frac{w_{1}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}-r\frac{w_{2}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\biggr)-f(r)+r\frac{w_{2}}{z}f^{\prime}(r)\biggr)\mu_{2}(dw)

\displaystyle+\int_{U_{2}}\biggl(f\biggl(r+(1-r)\frac{w_{1}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}-r\frac{w_{2}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\biggr)-f(r)\biggr)\nu(dw).

Therefore, as
f\in C^{2}([0,1])
and the fact that, by the definition of

(R_{s},Z_{s})\in[0,1]\times[\varepsilon,L]
for all
s\in[0,n^{-1}\wedge\tau)
, by similar arguments to those given in the proof of Proposition

\lvert\mathcal{G}f(R_{s},Z_{s})\rvert\leq K_{f}\quad\text{for all $s\in[0,n^{-1}\wedge\tau)$}\quad\text{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.}

This entails that

Hence, taking expectation with respect to

in (

\Bigl\lvert n\int_{0}^{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}\mathcal{G}f(R_{s},Z_{s})ds\Bigr\rvert\leq K_{f}\quad\text{with whole probability.}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{A}^{(z,n)}f(r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{(r,z)}\Bigl[n\int_{0}^{n^{-1}\wedge\tau}\mathcal{G}f(R_{s},Z_{s})ds\Bigr]=\mathcal{G}f(r,z)=\mathcal{L}^{(z)}f(r).

4.
Moment duality

The objective of the section is to present a process that will serve as a moment dual to the culled frequency process defined in section
3
.
This will be a block-counting process where blocks are allowed to branch and coalesce, where the branching in the dual process is due to selection and coalescence that arises as a result of the asymmetry in the reproductive mechanisms that we consider in the two-type CBI with competition.

4.1.
Block-counting process

To define the transition rates of the block counting process we will consider,
let us establish further notation for convenience.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\{1,2,3,\ldots,n\}
and
i\in\{1,2\}
set

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,k}^{i}:={}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}u_{1}^{k}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n-k}\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\mu_{i}(du),

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,k}^{I}:={}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}u_{1}^{k}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n-k}\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\nu(du).

In addition, let us define

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{1}:={}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\biggl(1-(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n}-n\frac{u_{1}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}\biggr)\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\mu_{1}(du),

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{2}:={}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\biggl(1-(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n}-n\frac{u_{2}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}\biggr)\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\mu_{2}(du),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{n,\mu}:={}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\bigl(1-(1-u_{2})^{n}\bigr)\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\mu_{1}(du),

\displaystyle\vartheta_{n,\nu}:={}

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\bigl(1-(1-u_{2})^{n}\bigr)\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\nu(du).

We aim to deduce moment duality, which is why we will consider the generator

\mathcal{L}^{(z)}
in Proposition
3.3
applied to monomials
of the form
f(r)=r^{n}
.
Therefore we will need to assume that the functions
b_{ij}
are polynomials, this is

(AP)

b_{ij}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m_{ij}}a_{ij}^{(k)}x^{k},\quad i,j\in\{1,2\}.

Suppose now that
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\in[0,1]
, and
f(r)=r^{n}
.
Then we get

f^{\prime}(r)=nr^{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\prime\prime}(r)=2\binom{n}{2}r^{n-2},

where we consider
\binom{1}{2}=0
.
Thus, by using (
3.15
) we deduce

\frac{1}{2}\sigma(r)^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)=\binom{n}{2}\frac{2}{z}\bigl[c_{1}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})+(c_{1}-c_{2})(r^{n+1}-r^{n})\bigr].

Using
S
as in (
3.15
) we get

S(r)f^{\prime}(r)=n\frac{2}{z}(c_{1}-c_{2})(r^{n+1}-r^{n}),

so

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma(r)^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(r)+S(r)f^{\prime}(r)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{2}\frac{2}{z}c_{1}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})+\binom{n+1}{2}\frac{2}{z}(c_{1}-c_{2})(r^{n+1}-r^{n}).

On the other hand, considering
m
as in (
3.15
),

(4.3)

m(r)f^{\prime}(r)=n\frac{\eta_{1}}{z}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})-n\frac{\eta_{2}}{z}r^{n}.

Let us now consider the term
\widetilde{D}(r)f^{\prime}(r)
, with
\widetilde{D}
defined in (
3.15
).
We start by noting that

\displaystyle\frac{1-r}{z}b_{11}(zr)nr^{n-1}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=0}^{m_{11}}a_{11}^{(k)}z^{k-1}(r^{n+k-1}-r^{n+k})

\displaystyle=n\frac{a_{11}^{(0)}}{z}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})+n\sum_{k=2}^{m_{11}}a_{11}^{(k)}z^{k-1}(r^{n+k-1}-r^{n})-n\sum_{k=1}^{m_{11}}a_{11}^{(k)}z^{k-1}(r^{n+k}-r^{n})

\displaystyle=n\frac{a_{11}^{(0)}}{z}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})+n\sum_{k=1}^{m_{11}-1}\bigl(a_{11}^{(k+1)}z^{k}-a_{11}^{(k)}z^{k-1}\bigr)(r^{n+k}-r^{n})-na_{11}^{(m_{11})}z^{m_{11}-1}(r^{n+m_{11}}-r^{n}).

Now we note that

\displaystyle-\frac{r}{z}b_{22}\bigl(z(1-r)\bigr)nr^{n-1}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{l=0}^{m_{22}}(-a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1})\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{l}{k}(-1)^{k}r^{n+k}=n\sum_{k=0}^{m_{22}}(-1)^{k}\bigl[\sum_{l=k}^{m_{22}}\binom{l}{k}(-a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1})\bigr]r^{n+k}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=0}^{m_{22}}(-1)^{k}\bigl[\sum_{l=k}^{m_{22}}\binom{l}{k}(-a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1})\bigr](r^{n+k}-r^{n})+n\sum_{k=0}^{m_{22}}(-1)^{k}\bigl[\sum_{l=k}^{m_{22}}\binom{l}{k}(-a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1})\bigr]r^{n}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=1}^{m_{22}}(-1)^{k+1}\bigl[\sum_{l=k}^{m_{22}}\binom{l}{k}a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1}\bigr](r^{n+k}-r^{n})-nr^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m_{22}}a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{l}{k}(-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=n\sum_{k=1}^{m_{22}}(-1)^{k+1}\bigl[\sum_{l=k}^{m_{22}}\binom{l}{k}a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1}\bigr](r^{n+k}-r^{n})-n\frac{a_{22}^{(0)}}{z}r^{n}.

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{r}{z}b_{21}(rz)nr^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m_{21}}n(-a_{21}^{(k)})z^{k-1}(r^{n+k}-r^{n})-n\frac{b_{21}(z)}{z}r^{n},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1-r}{z}b_{12}\bigl(z(1-r)\bigr)nr^{n-1}={}

\displaystyle n\frac{b_{12}(z)}{z}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})

\displaystyle+n\sum_{k=1}^{m_{12}-1}(-1)^{k+1}\bigl[a_{12}^{(k)}z^{k-1}+\sum_{l=k+1}^{m_{12}}\binom{l+1}{k+1}a_{12}^{(l)}z^{l-1}\bigr](r^{n+k}-r^{n})

\displaystyle+n(-1)^{m_{12}+1}a_{12}^{(m_{12})}z^{m_{12}-1}(r^{n+m_{12}}-r^{n}).

Thus, by defining for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{k}:={}

\displaystyle a_{11}^{(k+1)}z^{k}1_{\{k<m_{11}\}}-a_{11}^{(k)}z^{k-1}1_{\{k\leq m_{11}\}}-a_{21}^{(k)}z^{k-1}1_{\{k\leq m_{21}\}}+\biggl[(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{l=k}^{m_{22}}\binom{l}{k}a_{22}^{(l)}z^{l-1}\biggr]1_{\{k\leq m_{22}\}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{k+1}\biggl[\sum_{l=k+1}^{m_{12}}\binom{l+1}{k+1}a_{12}^{(l)}z^{l-1}\biggr]1_{\{k<m_{12}\}}+(-1)^{k+1}a_{12}^{(k)}z^{k-1}1_{\{k\leq m_{12}\}},

and employing the definition of
\widetilde{D}
in (
3.15
) we deduce

(4.4)

\widetilde{D}(r)f^{\prime}(r)=n\frac{a_{11}^{(0)}+b_{12}(z)}{z}(r^{n-1}-r^{n})+\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{m}}n\theta_{k}(r^{n+k}-r^{n})-n\frac{a_{22}^{(0)}+b_{21}(z)}{z}r^{n},

where
\tilde{m}=m_{11}\vee m_{12}\vee m_{21}\vee m_{22}
.

We now turn to the terms corresponding to jumps in the culled frequency process.
By using the equalities

\displaystyle zr\biggl[\bigl(r+u_{1}(1-r)-u_{2}r\bigr)^{n}-r^{n}-\frac{(1-r)u_{1}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}nr^{n-1}\biggr]

\displaystyle=z\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u_{1}^{k}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n-k}(r^{n-k+1}-r^{n})-z\bigl(1-(1-u_{2})^{n}\bigr)r^{n}

\displaystyle\quad-z\biggl[1-(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n}-\frac{nu_{1}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}\biggr](r^{n+1}-r^{n}),

\displaystyle z(1-r)\biggl[\bigl(r+u_{1}(1-r)-u_{2}r\bigr)^{n}-r^{n}+\frac{ru_{2}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}nr^{n-1}\biggr]

\displaystyle=z\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}\binom{n}{k-1}u_{1}^{k-1}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n-k+1}(r^{n-k+1}-r^{n})

\displaystyle\quad-z\sum_{k=2}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u_{1}^{k}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n-k}(r^{n-k+1}-r^{n})

\displaystyle\quad+z\biggl[1-(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n}-\frac{nu_{2}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}\biggr](r^{n+1}-r^{n}),

and

\displaystyle\bigl(r+u_{1}(1-r)-u_{2}r\bigr)^{n}-r^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}u_{1}^{k}(1-u_{1}-u_{2})^{n-k}(r^{n-k}-r^{n})-\bigl(1-(1-u_{2})^{n}\bigr)r^{n},

we deduce

(4.5)

\displaystyle zr\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\biggl[f\bigl(r+u_{1}(1-r)-u_{2}r\bigr)-f(r)-\frac{(1-r)u_{1}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}f^{\prime}(r)\biggr]\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\mu_{1}(du)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad+z(1-r)\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\biggl[f\bigl(r+u_{1}(1-r)-u_{2}r\bigr)-f(r)+\frac{ru_{2}}{1-u_{1}-u_{2}}f^{\prime}(r)\biggr]\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\mu_{2}(du)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{[0,1]^{2}}\Bigl[f\bigl(r+u_{1}(1-r)-u_{2}r\bigr)-f(r)\Bigr]\mathbf{T}_{2}^{(z)}\nu(du)

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{n}\binom{n}{k}z\lambda_{n,k}^{1}(r^{n-k+1}-r^{n})-z\vartheta_{n,\mu}r^{n}-z\gamma_{n}^{1}(r^{n+1}-r^{n})

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad+z\lambda_{n,n}^{2}(1-r^{n})+\sum_{k=2}^{n}\biggl[\binom{n}{k-1}z\lambda_{n,k-1}^{2}-\binom{n}{k}z\lambda_{n,k}^{2}\biggr](r^{n-k+1}-r^{n})+z\gamma_{n}^{2}(r^{n+1}-r^{n})

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\lambda_{n,k}^{I}(r^{n-k}-r^{n})-\vartheta_{n,\nu}r^{n}.

Finally, let us define the collection
\{q_{nm}:n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\dagger\}\}
by setting

(4.11)

q_{nm}=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &1_{\{m=n-1\}}\biggl(\binom{n}{2}\frac{2}{z}c_{1}+n\frac{a_{11}^{0}+b_{12}(z)+\eta_{1}}{z}\biggr)\\
&\quad+1_{\{m>0\}}\binom{n}{n-m+1}z(\lambda_{n,n-m+1}^{1}-\lambda_{n,n-m+1}^{2})\\
&\quad+\binom{n}{n-m}z\lambda_{n,n-m}^{2}+\binom{n}{n-m}\lambda_{n,n-m}^{I}\end{aligned}&if $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $0\leq m<n$,\\
1_{\{m=n+1\}}\biggl(\binom{n+1}{2}\frac{2}{z}(c_{1}-c_{2})-z(\gamma_{n}^{1}-\gamma_{n}^{2})\biggr)+n\theta_{m-n}&if $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $n<m\leq n+\tilde{m}$,\\
z\vartheta_{n,\mu}+\vartheta_{n,\nu}+n\frac{a_{22}^{(0)}+b_{21}(z)+\eta_{2}}{z}&if $n\in\mathbb{N}$ and $j=\dagger$,\\

Note that if
q_{nm}\geq 0
for all
n\neq m
, then this collection defines the
transition rates of a continuous-time Markov Chain with state space
\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\dagger\}
.
Now we consider the function
H(r,n):[0,1]\times(\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\dagger\})\to[0,1]
defined by
H(r,n)=r^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
H(r,\dagger)=0
.
Under the assumption of
q_{nm}\geq 0
for all
n\neq m
,
using the equalities (
4.1
), (
4.3
),
(
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we deduce that for
any
r\in[0,1]
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(z)}H(r,n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}q_{nm}(r^{m}-r^{n})+\sum_{m=n+1}^{n+\tilde{m}}q_{nm}(r^{m}-r^{n})-q_{n\dagger}r^{n}=\mathcal{Q}^{(z)}H(r,n),

where
\mathcal{Q}^{(z)}
is the generator defined by the collection

\{q_{nm}:n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\dagger\}\}
.
This reasoning proves the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that (
AP
) holds.
If
q_{nm}\geq 0
for all
n\neq m
, then for any
r\in[0,1]
and

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\dagger\}
and any

where

\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\dagger\}

whose transition rates are defined by (
4.11
).

Remark 4.1
.

As pointed out in section 6 of
[
2
]
,
the assumption

for all

restricts the cases we may study
by duality to those where there exists a selective advantage of one type (in this case type 1) over the other.
Moreover, it imposes restrictions, apart from

4.2.
Biological interpretation of duality

Whenever the dual process of the culled frequency process exists, the rates given in
(
4.11
) allow us to give a characterization of the evolutionary
forces that arise due to the different reproduction mechanisms of the two species that constitute the population.
Most of the mechanisms we will mention also appear in Section 9 of
[
2
]
,
but there are new terms that appear in the moment dual because, unlike the population model in
[
2
]
, we are not assuming independence between the processes
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
.
These effects are the following:

Selection:

Considering the branching rate in (
4.11
), we find two types of selection:

(i):

Classical selection:
This type can be found in the following two terms:
The term
\theta_{1}
, which is the difference between the drift terms;
and
2(c_{1}-c_{2})/z
, observed previously by Gillespie
[
6
,
7
]
, corresponding to the difference between diffusion terms.

(ii):

Frequency dependent selection:
This kind of selection arises in two forms. The first one
is present in the transition rates (
4.11
) through the terms
\theta_{m}
for
m\geq 2
. This type of selection was observed in
[
9
]
, and we want to emphasize that this form of selection can lead to coexistence in the culled frequency process.
The second one is due to the difference in the jump measures of the two-type CBI with competition in the terms
-z(\gamma_{n}^{1}-\gamma_{n}^{2})
. This kind of selection arises from the asymmetry in large reproduction events between the types in the population and was recently studied in the context of the ancestral selection graph of a
\Lambda
-asymmetric Moran model in
[
10
]
.

Frequency dependent variance:

As noted in
[
2
]
, from the form of the culled frequency process (given as the solution to (
3.12
)), one observes that the asymmetry between the diffusion terms
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
modifies the variance. In
[
11
]
, this term was obtained in the context of populations
that require different amounts of resources to reproduce and is referred to as
efficiency
.

Coalescence:

Both the terms
2c_{1}/z
and
z(\lambda_{n,n-m+1}^{1}-\lambda_{n,n-m+1}^{2})
are associated with coalescence; the former being the rate of pairwise mergers, while the latter corresponds to mergers of multiple ancestral lines.

Mutation:

Mutation is obtained through the inclusion of immigration and cross-branching into the model, and is found in the terms
(a_{11}^{0}+b_{12}(z)+\eta_{1})/z
,
\lambda_{n,n-m}^{2}
,
\lambda_{n,n-m}^{I}
,
(a_{22}^{0}+b_{21}(z)+\eta_{2})/z
,
z\vartheta_{n,\mu}
and
\vartheta_{n,\nu}
. Apart from the terms included in
[
2
]
we obtain new terms thanks to the cross-branching; namely,
\lambda_{n,n-m}^{2}
,
b_{12}(z)/z
,
b_{21}(z)/z
and
z\vartheta_{n,\mu}
.

5.
Large population limit

In previous sections, we developed the culled frequency process,
R^{(z,r)}
, specifically designed to describe the dynamics of the first coordinate of
(R,Z)
, while keeping the total population size constant at
z>0
.
In this section, we explore the large population limit of the culled frequency process, proving that it exists under certain hypotheses, and present two examples that exhibit coexistence.
The latter term means that, in these examples, neither population becomes extinct; equivalently, the frequency process never reaches
0
or
1
.

5.1.
The large population limit

We start by noting that if we see the culled frequency process as the solution of
the SDE (
3.12
), we can actually consider that the functions

b_{ij}
depend on
z
(which is considered fixed for (
3.12
)).
We stress this fact by writing
b_{ij}^{(z)}
for the rest of the section.
This dependence will allow us to introduce suitable rescalings such that the limit

(5.1)

\lim_{z\to\infty}\frac{b_{ij}^{(z)}(zr)}{z}

is well defined and finite for all
r\in[0,1]
.
To see that this is necessary, notice that if
b_{11}^{(z)}(x)=x(1-x)
for all
z>0
,
then the limit in (
5.1
) exists but is equal to
-\infty
, while the limit
does not exist if
b_{11}^{(z)}=x\sin(x)
for all
z>0
.

Let us assume then that the limit in (
5.1
) exists uniformly for

r\in[0,1]
and denote said limit by
\beta_{ij}
.
Let us further assume that
\beta_{ij}(r)=\lim_{z\to\infty}b_{ij}^{(z)}(rz)/z
is
Lipschitz in
[0,1]
.
Having stated the assumptions on
\beta_{ij}
, consider the ordinary differential
equation

(5.2)

\displaystyle dR_{t}^{(\infty,r)}={}

\displaystyle\beta_{11}(R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})(1-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})dt-\beta_{22}(1-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})R_{t}^{(\infty,r)}dt

(5.3)

\displaystyle+\beta_{12}(1-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})(1-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})dt-\beta_{21}(R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})R_{t}^{(\infty,r)}dt

(5.4)

\displaystyle+(1-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})^{2}\int_{U_{2}}w_{1}\mu_{2}(dw)-(R_{t}^{(\infty,r)})^{2}\int_{U_{2}}w_{2}\mu_{1}(dw),

with initial condition
R_{0}^{(\infty,r)}=r
.
By comparing (
3.12
) with (
5.2
) one might guess that
the solution of (
5.2
) is the large population limit of the
culled frequency process.
Indeed, this is what the following result states.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
T>0
we get

\lim_{z\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{t\leq T}\lvert R_{t}^{(z,r)}-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)}\rvert^{2}\biggr]=0;

this is,
R^{(z,r)}\to R^{(\infty,r)}
uniformly in compacts in
L^{2}
as

z\to\infty
.

Proof.

Considering the SDE (
3.12
) and the ODE (
5.2
) we get

R_{t}^{(z,r)}-R_{t}^{(\infty,r)}=A_{t}^{(z,1)}+A_{t}^{(z,2)}+A_{t}^{(z,3)},

where

\displaystyle A_{t}^{(z,1)}={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U_{2}}\bigl[\widetilde{g}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)+\widetilde{h}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)\bigr]\widetilde{N}_{3}(ds,dw)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[g_{1}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w,v)+h_{1}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w,v)\bigr]\widetilde{N}_{1}(ds,dw,dv)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[g_{2}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w,v)+h_{2}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w,v)\bigr]\widetilde{N}_{2}(ds,dw,dv),

\displaystyle A_{t}^{(z,2)}={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\bigl[S(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})+S_{c}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})+m(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})\bigr]ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{U_{2}}\bigl[\widetilde{g}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)+\widetilde{h}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)\bigr]\nu(dw)ds,

and

\displaystyle A_{t}^{(z,3)}={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl[\widetilde{D}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})-\begin{aligned} \bigl(&\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})-\beta_{22}(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)}\\
&+\beta_{12}(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})-\beta_{21}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)}\bigr)\Bigr]ds\end{aligned}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl[m_{c}^{1}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})+(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})^{2}\int_{U_{2}}w_{2}\mu_{1}(dw)\Bigr]ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl[m_{c}^{2}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})-(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})^{2}\int_{U_{2}}w_{1}\mu_{2}(dw)\Bigr]ds.

We first consider the term
A_{t}^{(z,1)}
.
Note that by the definition of
\sigma
given in (

) together with Doob’s

inequality and Itô’s isometry we obtain, for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\sigma(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})dB_{s}\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})^{2}ds\biggr]\leq\frac{8}{z}(c_{1}+c_{2})T.

Applying again Doob’s

inequality we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\int_{U_{2}}\bigl[\widetilde{g}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)+\widetilde{h}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)\bigr]\widetilde{N}_{3}(ds,dw)\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\biggl[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U_{2}}\bigl[\widetilde{g}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)+\widetilde{h}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w)\bigr]^{2}\nu(dw)ds\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq 4T\int_{U_{2}}\frac{(w_{1}+w_{2})^{2}}{(z+w_{1}+w_{2})}\nu(dw).

Analogous computations yield

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\int_{U_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl[g_{i}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w,v)+h_{i}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)},w,v)\bigr]\widetilde{N}_{i}(ds,dw,dv)\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq 4T\int_{U_{2}}\frac{z(w_{1}+w_{2})^{2}}{(z+w_{1}+w_{2})^{2}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})(dw).

By the Dominated Convergence Theorem we note that

\int_{U_{2}}\frac{(w_{1}+w_{2})^{2}}{(z+w_{1}+w_{2})}\nu(dw)+\int_{U_{2}}\frac{z(w_{1}+w_{2})^{2}}{(z+w_{1}+w_{2})^{2}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})(dw)\to 0\quad\text{as $z\to\infty$.}

Hence, we deduce the existence of a collection of constants
\{K_{1}(T,z):z>0\}
that fulfill
K_{1}(T,z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty
and

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{u\leq t}(A_{u}^{(z,1)})^{2}\Bigr]\leq K_{1}(T,z)\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$ and $z>0$.}

Meanwhile, to control the term
A_{t}^{(z,2)}
we simply use the triangle inequality to deduce, for any
t\in[0,T]
, that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{u\leq t}(A_{u}^{(z,2)})^{2}\Bigr]\leq{}

\displaystyle 4\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\biggl(\frac{\lvert c_{2}-c_{1}\rvert}{2z}+\frac{\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}}{z}\biggr)ds\biggr)^{2}+4\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{1}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{1}(dw)ds\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle+4\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{2}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{2}(dw)ds\biggr)^{2}+4\biggl(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\nu(dw)ds\biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 4T^{2}\begin{aligned} \biggl(&\frac{1}{4z^{2}}\bigl(\lvert c_{2}-c_{1}\rvert+2\eta_{1}+2\eta_{2}\bigr)^{2}+\biggl(\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{1}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{1}(dw)\biggr)^{2}\\
&+\biggl(\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{2}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{2}(dw)\biggr)^{2}+\biggl(\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\nu(dw)\biggr)^{2}\biggr).\end{aligned}

Thus, by noting that

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{i}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{i}(dw)+\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\nu(dw)\to 0\quad\text{as }z\to\infty

by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we obtain a collection of constants
\{K_{2}(T,z):z>0\}
that satisfy both
K_{2}(T,z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty
and

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{u\leq t}(A_{u}^{(z,2)})^{2}\Bigr]\leq K_{2}(T,z)\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$ and $z>0$.}

Let us now tackle the term
A_{t}^{(z,3)}
.
Recall, from (
3.15
), that for
r\in[0,1]
,

\widetilde{D}(r)=\frac{b_{11}^{(z)}(zr)}{z}(1-r)-\frac{b_{22}^{(z)}(z(1-r))}{z}r+\frac{b_{12}{(z)}(z(1-r))}{z}(1-r)-\frac{b_{21}^{(z)}(zr)}{z}r.

Now note that, for
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\biggl(\frac{b_{11}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})}{z}(1-R_{s-}^{(z,r)})-\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})\biggr)ds\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 3\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\biggl[\frac{b_{11}^{(z)}(zR_{s-}^{(z,r)})}{z}-\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})\biggr](1-R_{s-}^{z,r})ds\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle+3\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{u}\bigl[\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})-\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})\bigr](1-R_{s-}^{(z,r)})ds\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle+3\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{u}\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)}-R_{s-}^{(z,r)})ds\Bigr)^{2}\biggr],

By assumption
\beta_{11}
is Lipschitz, say with constant
L_{11}>0
, so

\lVert\beta_{11}\rVert_{\infty}:=\sup\{\lvert\beta_{11}(r)\rvert:r\in[0,1]\}<\infty
.
Moreover,
b_{11}^{(z)}(zr)/z\to\beta_{11}(r)
uniformly on

, so there exists a collection of constants
\{C_{z}^{(11)}:z>0\}
, with

By these remarks, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\biggl(\frac{b_{11}^{(z)}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})}{z}(1-R_{s-}^{(z,r)})-\beta_{11}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})(1-R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})\biggr)ds\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

By proceeding in the same way for the rest of the terms in

, with

as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{u}(\widetilde{D}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})-\widetilde{D}_{\infty}(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)}))ds\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq T^{2}C_{z}^{(D)}+TC_{D}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{u\leq s}\lvert R_{u}^{(z,r)}-R_{u}^{(\infty,r)}\rvert^{2}ds\Bigr],

where

\widetilde{D}_{\infty}(r)\coloneq\beta_{11}(r)(1-r)-\beta_{22}(1-r)r+\beta_{12}(1-r)(1-r)-\beta_{21}(r)r.

On the other hand, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\Bigl(m_{c}^{1}(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})+(R_{s-}^{(\infty,r)})^{2}\int_{U_{2}}w_{2}\mu_{1}(dw)\Bigr)ds\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\biggl[\sup_{u\leq t}\biggl(\int_{0}^{u}\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{2}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{1}(dw)(R_{s-}^{(z,r)})^{2}ds\biggr)^{2}\biggr]

\displaystyle 2\Bigl(\int_{U_{2}}\frac{w_{2}(w_{1}+w_{2})}{z+w_{1}+w_{2}}\mu_{1}(dw)\Bigr)^{2}T^{2}+8\Bigl(\int_{U_{2}}w_{2}\mu_{1}(dw)\Bigr)^{2}T\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{u\leq s}\lvert R_{u}^{(z,r)}-R_{u}^{(\infty,r)}\rvert^{2}ds\Bigr].

By doing similar computations to the term involving

such that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{u\leq t}(A_{u}^{(z,3)})^{2}\Bigr]\leq K_{3}(T,z)+K_{4}(T)\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{u\leq s}\lvert R_{u}^{(z,r)}-R_{u}^{(\infty,r)}\rvert^{2}ds\Bigr]\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$ and $z>0$.}

As a consequence, for all

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{u\leq t}\lvert R_{u}^{(z,r)}-R_{u}^{(\infty,r)}\rvert^{2}\Bigr]\leq 3\bigl(K_{1}(T,z)+K_{2}(T,z)+K_{3}(T,z)\bigr)+3K_{4}(T)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sup_{u\leq s}\lvert R_{u}^{(z,r)}-R_{u}^{(\infty,r)}\rvert^{2}\Bigr]ds.

Now, Gronwall’s lemma entails

The proof concludes by noting that

\bigl(K_{1}(T,z)+K_{2}(T,z)+K_{3}(T,z)\bigr)\exp\{3K_{4}(T)T\}\to 0

Nonlinear Stability and Instability of Plasma Boundary Layers
Masahiro Suzuki, Masahiro Takayama, and Katherine Zhiyuan Zhang

Abstract
We investigate the formation of a plasma boundary layer (sheath) by considering the Vlasov–Poisson system on a half-line with the completely absorbing boundary condition. In [ 54 ] , the solvability of the stationary problem is studied. In this paper, we study the nonlinear stability and instability of these stationary solutions of the Vlasov–Poisson system.



Keywords:

Vlasov–Poisson system,
Sheath,
Bohm criterion.

Introduction

The goal of this paper is to investigate the stability and instability of a plasma boundary layer near the surface of materials immersed in a plasma, called a
sheath
. The sheath appears when a material is surrounded by a plasma and the plasma contacts with its surface. Since electrons have much higher thermal velocities than ions, more electrons tend to hit the material compared with ions. Hence the material gets negatively charged, and this material with a negative potential attracts and accelerates ions toward the surface, and simultaneously repels electrons away from it. Eventually, a non-neutral potential region appears near the surface, where a nontrivial equilibrium of the densities is achieved. This non-neutral region is referred as the sheath. For more discussion on the physics in sheath development, we refer the reader to
[
7
,
9
,
30
,
31
,
47
,
48
]
.

It is observed by Langmuir
[
30
]
that in order to form a sheath, positive ions must enter the sheath region with a sufficiently large flow velocity. Bohm
[
7
]
proposed the original
Bohm criterion
using the Euler–Poisson system. The Bohm criterion states that the flow velocity of positive ions at the plasma edge must exceed the ion acoustic speed. There have been a large amount of mathematical results investigating the sheath formation by using the Euler–Poisson system ever since. The studies
[
1
,
2
,
41
,
51
,
52
,
53
]
established theories of existence and stability of stationary solutions assuming the Bohm criterion, and verified this criterion in a rigorous mathematical sense. There are also varied studies from other perspectives on the sheath. In
[
14
,
15
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
, the sheath formation was discussed by considering the quasi-neutral limit
as letting the Debye length in the Euler–Poisson system tend to zero. Moreover, in
[
13
]
, the sheath formation was considered by adopting a certain hydrodynamic model which describes the dynamics of an interface between the plasma and sheath, see also
[
49
]
.

Beside those studies using hydrodynamic models, it is also of important to analyze the sheath formation from a kinetic point of view. Boyd–Thompson
[
8
]
proposed a kinetic Bohm criterion (see (
1.3
) below). Then Riemann
[
47
]
pointed out (although without a rigorous proof) that the kinetic Bohm criterion (
1.3
) is a necessary condition for the boundary value problems of the stationary Vlasov–Poisson system to be solvable. Recently, the first two authors
[
54
]
analyzed rigorously the solvability of the stationary problem, and clarified in all possible cases whether or not there is a stationary solution. It was concluded that the Bohm criterion is necessary but not sufficient for the solvability. In this paper, we study the nonlinear stability and instability of the stationary solutions of the Vlasov–Poisson system.

The Vlasov–Poisson system is written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+\xi_{1}\partial_{x}F+\partial_{x}\Phi\partial_{\xi_{1}}F=0,\quad t>0,\ x>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{xx}\Phi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Fd\xi-n_{e}(\Phi),\quad t>0,\ x>0,

(1.1b)

where
t>0
,
x>0
, and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})=(\xi_{1},\xi^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are the time, spatial, and velocity variables, respectively.
The unknown functions
F=F(t,x,\xi)
and
-\Phi=-\Phi(t,x)
stand
for the velocity distribution of positive ions
and the electrostatic potential, respectively.
The given function
n_{e}(\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the number density of electrons.
We assume that

\displaystyle n_{e}(0)=1,\quad n_{e}^{\prime}(0)=-1,\quad n_{e}^{\prime}(\Phi)<0.

(1.1c)

One of typical functions is the Boltzmann relation
n_{e}(\Phi)=e^{-{\Phi}}
.
We study the initial–boundary value problem of (
1.1a
)–(
1.1c
) with the following initial and boundary data:

\displaystyle F(0,x,\xi)=F_{0}(x,\xi),\quad x>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.1d)

\displaystyle F(t,0,\xi)=0,\quad t>0,\ \xi_{1}>0,

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,0)=\Phi_{b}>0,\quad t>0,

(1.1f)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}F(t,x,\xi)=F_{\infty}(\xi),\quad t>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.1g)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\Phi(t,x)=0,\quad t>0.

(1.1h)

The constant

denotes the voltage on the boundary.
Furthermore,
F_{0}=F_{0}(x,\xi)
and
F_{\infty}=F_{\infty}(\xi)
are nonnegative functions.
The boundary condition (
1.1e
) on
\xi_{1}>0
does not allow positive ions to flow into the domain from the boundary.
On the other hand, we do not put any boundary condition on
\xi_{1}<0
, and therefore positive ions can flow out through the boundary.
In other words, we consider the

As mentioned above, the studies
[

,

investigated the solvability of the stationary problem associated to the problem (

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\partial_{x}F^{s}+\partial_{x}\Phi^{s}\partial_{\xi_{1}}F^{s}=0,\quad x>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{xx}\Phi^{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F^{s}d\xi-n_{e}(\Phi^{s}),\quad x>0,

(1.2b)

\displaystyle F^{s}(0,\xi)=0,\quad\xi_{1}>0,

(1.2c)

\displaystyle\Phi^{s}(0)=\Phi_{b}>0,

(1.2d)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}F^{s}(x,\xi)=F_{\infty}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.2e)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\Phi^{s}(x)=0.

(1.2f)

It was concluded by

that the following Bohm criterion is a necessary condition

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\xi_{1}^{-2}F_{\infty}(\xi)d\xi\leq 1.

(1.3)

In this paper, we consider the nonlinear stability and instability of these stationary solutions.

In this paper, we consider the Vlasov–Poisson system on a half-line with the completely absorbing boundary condition under the assumption that the electron density obeys
n_{e}=n_{e}(\Phi)
, and establish the nonlinear stability and instability of stationary solutions in a weighted
L^{2}
energy framework. We make use of the dissipative nature of the problem by exploiting the completely absorbing boundary condition (in particular, the outflow part on
\xi_{1}<0
) as well as observing the transport of the support of the velocity distribution
F
. It is concluded that the
\xi

F_{0}
is a major factor leading to stability/instability. Roughly speaking, the
\xi
-support being constrained to
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,|\,\xi_{1}<0\}
helps in stability;
A nonempty intersection of the
\xi
-support and
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,|\,\xi_{1}>0\}
helps in instability.
To our knowledge, this is the first asymptotic stability result on spatially inhomogeneous stationary solutions of the Vlasov–Poisson system.

Proof.

For any

,

, and

,

\displaystyle\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})=\{(t,x,\xi)\in[0,t_{0}]\times\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\times\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}\,|\,(x,\xi)\in D_{k,\ell}(t)\},

\displaystyle D_{k,\ell}(t):=D(s(t))\cap\{(x,\xi)\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\times\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{3}\,|\,x\geq-k+\ell t,\,\xi_{1}\leq\ell-c_{0}t\}.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}:=\{(t,x,\xi)\in\partial\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})\,|\,t=t_{0}\}=\{t_{0}\}\times D_{k,\ell}(t_{0}),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}:=\{(t,x,\xi)\in\partial\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})\,|\,t=0\}=\{0\}\times D_{k,\ell}(0),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}:=\{(t,x,\xi)\in\partial\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})\,|\,\xi_{1}=-s(t)\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{4}:=\{(t,x,\xi)\in\partial\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})\,|\,\xi_{1}=\ell-c_{0}t\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{5}:=\{(t,x,\xi)\in\partial\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})\,|\,x=-k+\ell t\},

Multiply (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{k,\ell}(t_{0})}\partial_{t}|f|^{2}+\partial_{x}(\Xi_{1}|f|^{2})+\partial_{\xi_{1}}\{(\partial_{x}\tilde{\Phi}^{s}+\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi})|f|^{2}\}dtdxd\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{7}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\vec{\cal F}\cdot\vec{n}\,dS,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{\prime}(t)^{2}+1}}\Bigl\{-s^{\prime}(t)+\frac{x\Xi_{1}}{s(t)}+\frac{\xi_{1}}{s(t)}(\partial_{x}\tilde{\Phi}^{s}+\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi})\Bigr\}|f|^{2}

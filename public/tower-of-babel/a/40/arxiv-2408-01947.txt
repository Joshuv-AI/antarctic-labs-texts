Restriction of Schrödinger eigenfunctions to submanifolds
Xiaoqi Huang, Xing Wang and Cheng Zhang

Abstract
. For Schrödinger operators H V = − Δ g + V H_{V}=-\Delta_{g}+V with critically singular potentials V V on compact manifolds, we prove sharp estimates for the restriction of eigenfunctions to submanifolds. Our method refines the perturbative argument by Blair-Sire-Sogge [ 12 ] and enables us to deal with submanifolds of all codimensions. As applications, we obtain improved estimates on negatively curved manifolds and flat tori. In particular, we extend the uniform L 2 L^{2} restriction estimates on flat tori by Bourgain-Rudnick [ 20 ] to singular potentials.



1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
(M,g)
be a compact, smooth,
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
\Delta_{g}
denote the associated Laplace-Beltrami operator. The concentration properties of the eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{g}
depend on the geometry of
(M,g)
. On manifolds with integrable geodesic flows, such as the sphere, eigenfunctions like zonal harmonics or Gaussian beams may be highly concentrated near certain submanifolds
[
57
]
. In contrast, on manifolds with chaotic geodesic flows, such as hyperbolic surfaces, high-energy eigenfunctions are usually expected to become equidistributed
[
54
,
34
,
35
,
72
]
. Nevertheless, quantum scarring may still occur near unstable closed orbits in some chaotic systems
[
41
]
. When studying quantum systems associated with Schrödinger operators
H_{V}=-\Delta_{g}+V
, the presence of a potential
V
complicates the behavior of these systems. A fundamental principle is that eigenfunctions are confined by potential barriers, decaying exponentially in classically forbidden regions where the potential energy exceeds the particle’s energy level. There are also other types of localization phenomena caused by potentials. For example, Anderson localization
[
3
]
, which occurs for certain disordered potentials, is due to destructive interference from scattering. The locations of these localized states have recently been shown to be predictable by landscape functions
[
1
,
4
]
. Notably, potentials with singularities on submanifolds may alter the chaotic nature of the Hamiltonian flow in the potential-free case on hyperbolic manifolds. In this paper, we aim to quantitatively investigate the concentration behaviors of Schrödinger eigenfunctions with singular potentials.

To measure the concentration of an eigenfunction, an important approach is to study the growth of its
L^{p}
norms restricted to submanifolds. This type of estimates was studied by Reznikov
[
52
]
for Maass forms on hyperbolic surfaces and by Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[
23
]
, Hu
[
44
]
for Laplacian eigenfunctions on general compact manifolds. To be specific, let
\Sigma\subset M
be a smoothly embedded submanifold of dimension
k
. For
2\leq p\leq\infty
, let

(1.1)

\delta(k,p)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{n-1}{2}-\dfrac{k}{p},\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{if}~1\leq k\leq n-2,\ \ 2\leq p\leq\infty,\\
\dfrac{n-1}{4}-\dfrac{n-2}{2p},\quad\quad\text{if}~k=n-1,\ 2\leq p\leq\dfrac{2n}{n-1},\\
\dfrac{n-1}{2}-\dfrac{n-1}{p},\quad\quad\text{if}~k=n-1,\ \dfrac{2n}{n-1}<p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

For the Laplacian eigenfunction
e_{\lambda}
with
-\Delta_{g}e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda}
, Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[
23
]
and Hu
[
44
]
proved the following sharp restriction bounds

(1.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{{\delta(k,p)}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},

albeit with a potential
(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}
loss when
(k,p)=(n-2,2)
. The log loss can be removed under certain geometrical assumptions but remains generally open, see Chen–Sogge
[
29
]
, Wang–Zhang
[
66
]
for detailed discussions. Related works on restrictions of eigenfunctions also include those by Greenleaf-Seeger
[
39
]
, Tataru
[
65
]
, Bourgain
[
18
]
, Tacy
[
64
]
, Bourgain–Rudnick
[
20
]
, Chen
[
28
]
, Xi–Zhang
[
71
]
, Hezari
[
42
]
, Blair
[
7
]
, Zhang
[
74
]
, Huang–Zhang
[
47
]
, and Park
[
51
]
. These types of restriction estimates are related to the stabilization of weakly damped wave equations, as discussed in Burq–Zuily
[
24
]
.

Next, we recall some important classes of potentials in spectral theory. To ensure that
H_{V}
is essentially self-adjoint and bounded from below and its eigenfunctions are bounded, the “minimal condition” for
V
is that it belongs to the Kato class,
\mathcal{K}
. The definition will be given in the next section. The spaces
L^{n/2}
and
\mathcal{K}
have the same scaling properties, and both obey the scaling law of the Laplacian, which accounts for their criticality. On the compact manifold
M
, we have
L^{q}(M)\subset\mathcal{K}(M)\subset L^{1}(M)
for
q>n/2
. The spectrum of
H_{V}
for
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
is discrete, and the eigenfunctions are continuous, which allows for their restriction to submanifolds. For a detailed introduction to Kato potentials and their physical motivations, see Simon
[
56
]
and Blair-Sire-Sogge
[
12
]
.

1.1.
Main results

We shall assume throughout that
M
is a compact manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
, and
\Sigma
be a submanifold of dimension
k
, and the potentials
V
are real-valued. Let
\lambda\geq 10
and let
e_{\lambda}
be an eigenfunction of
H_{V}
with

H_{V}e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda}.

Let
2\leq p\leq\infty
and let
\Lambda=\Lambda(k,p)
denote the classical bounds in (
1.2
), i.e.

\Lambda(k,p)=\begin{cases}\lambda^{\delta(k,p)},~~~~\quad\quad\quad\text{ if}~(k,p)\neq(n-2,2),\\
\lambda^{\delta(k,p)}(\log\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~~~\text{if}~(k,p)=(n-2,2).\end{cases}

First, we prove essentially sharp estimates for the restriction of Schrödinger eigenfunctions to submanifolds of all codimensions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V\in L^{n/2}(M)\cap\mathcal{K}(M)
. Then for all
n,k,p
we have

(1.3)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\Lambda\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},

albeit with a potential logarithmic loss if
(n,k,p)\in\{(n,k,p):n\geq 5,k>n/2,p<2.3\}
. The loss can be removed if
V\in L^{q}(M)
with
q>n/2
.

When
V\equiv 0
, the bounds are essentially sharp on the standard sphere. For the restriction to curved hypersurfaces, see the forthcoming Theorem
5.1
for the improvements. We also obtain improvements on negatively curved manifolds and flat tori.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a negatively curved manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
. When

, we assume it has constant negative sectional curvatures. Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a geodesic segment. Let
V\in L^{n/2}(M)\cap\mathcal{K}(M)

(1.4)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\delta(1,p)}(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

This type of improvements for Laplacian eigenfunctions were investigated in a series of works by Sogge-Zelditch

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. Bourgain-Rudnick
[

established uniform

bounds on the restriction of Laplacian eigenfunctions on the flat tori

and

to curved hypersurfaces. Recently, Huang-Zhang
[

characterized the

.

Let
V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}

(1.6)

(1.7)

1.2.
Proof methods

To obtain eigenfunction bounds for
H_{V}
, a powerful tool is the second resolvent formula used by Blair-Sire-Sogge
[
12
]
and Blair-Huang-Sire-Sogge
[
9
]
. Recently, Blair-Park
[
10
,
11
]
used this type of perturbative argument to obtain estimates for eigenfunctions restricted to submanifolds of codimension 1 and 2, while higher codimension analogues were open. This argument focuses on estimating the difference between the resolvent operators for perturbed and unperturbed cases, where the differences only contribute to the error terms in the
main theorems. However, a limitation of this method is that it cannot handle submanifolds of all codimensions. Specifically, it is easy to see that
((\lambda+i)^{2}+\Delta_{g})^{-1}
is not
L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
bounded when
k\leq n-4
.

A natural idea is to replace the resolvent operator by the spectral projection operator in the perturbative argument, since both of them can reproduce eigenfunctions. However, the new difficulty in the perturbative argument is to handle the difference between the spectral projection operators for perturbed and unperturbed cases. Indeed, we use the Duhamel principle for the wave equation to represent the spectral projection operators of
H_{V}
. By the spectral theorem, we split the lower and higher frequencies respect to
\lambda
in the Fourier expansion. We need to carefully handle several different cases regarding the interactions of different frequency regimes. This is the technical part of our proof.

We give some remarks on our new method. First, we use a bootstrap argument involving induction on the dimensions of the submanifolds to utilize the Kato class condition. This is the crucial idea that enables us to handle submanifolds of all codimensions (see Subsection
3.1
). Moreover, an advantage of the method is that it is particularly useful to handle spectral projection operators with small windows. For instance, we can handle the spectral projection on the window
[\lambda,\lambda+\lambda^{-1}]
to prove Theorem
1.3
on flat tori. The method is expected to be useful in more general settings.

In Section 3, we presents the main argument for the proof and we deal with higher codimensions in Subsection 3.1. In Section 4, we remove the logarithmic loss in Case 2 and finish the proof of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
,
1.4
,
1.5
. In Section 5, we obtain some improvements on curved hypersurfaces. In Section 6, we prove Theorem
1.3
on the flat tori. In Section 7, we discuss potential improvements on oscillatory integrals.

2.
Preliminary

We shall assume throughout that the potentials
V
are real-valued and
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, which is the Kato class. It is all
V\in L^{1}(M)
satisfying

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{x\in M}\int_{d_{g}(y,x)<\delta}|V(y)|W_{n}(d_{g}(x,y))dy=0,

where

W_{n}(r)=\begin{cases}r^{2-n},\quad\quad\quad\quad n\geq 3\\
\log(2+r^{-1}),\ \ n=2\end{cases}

and
d_{g}
,
dy
denote geodesic distance, the volume element on
(M,g)
.
Note that by Hölder inequality, we have
L^{q}\subset\mathcal{K}(M)\subset L^{1}(M)
for all
q>\frac{n}{2}
. The Kato class
\mathcal{K}(M)
and
L^{n/2}(M)
share the same critical scaling
behavior, while neither one is contained in the other one for
n\geq 3
. For instance, singularities of the type
|x|^{-\alpha}
for
\alpha<2
are allowed for both classes.

The Kato class is the “minimal condition” to ensure that
H_{V}
is essentially self-adjoint and bounded from below, and eigenfunctions of
H_{V}
are bounded. Since
M
is compact, the spectrum of
H_{V}
is discrete. Also, the associated
eigenfunctions are continuous. The following Gaussian heat kernel bounds holds for all
x,y\in M

(2.1)

|e^{-tH_{V}}(x,y)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-cd_{g}(x,y)^{2}/t},\ 0<t\leq 1.

The constants
C
and
c
are positive, and they can be independent of
V
. See e.g. Sturm
[
63
]
, Huang-Wang-Zhang
[
50
]
for a detailed proof.

After possibly adding a constant to
V
we may assume throughout that
H_{V}
is bounded below by one. We shall write the spectrum
of
\sqrt{H_{V}}
as
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty},

where the eigenvalues are arranged in increasing order and we account for multiplicity. For each
\tau_{k}
there is an
eigenfunction
e_{\tau_{k}}\in\text{Dom }(H_{V})
(the domain of
H_{V}
) so that

(2.2)

H_{V}e_{\tau_{k}}=\tau^{2}_{k}e_{\tau_{k}},\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \int_{M}|e_{\tau_{k}}(x)|^{2}\,dx=1.

Moreover, we shall let
H^{0}=-\Delta_{g}

be the unperturbed operator. The corresponding eigenvalues and associated
L^{2}
-normalized
eigenfunctions are denoted by
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\{e^{0}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, respectively so
that

(2.3)

H^{0}e^{0}_{j}=\lambda^{2}_{j}e^{0}_{j},\quad\text{and }\,\,\int_{M}|e^{0}_{j}(x)|^{2}\,dx=1.

Both
\{e_{\tau_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{e^{0}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
are orthonormal bases for
L^{2}(M)
. Let
P^{0}=\sqrt{H^{0}}
and
P_{V}=\sqrt{H_{V}}
. Let
\lambda\geq 10
and let
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{I}(\tau)
be the indicator function of the interval
I
. We can define the spectral projection operator
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{I}(P^{0})
and
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{I}(P_{V})
by the spectral theorem.

Suppose
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)\cap L^{n/2}(M)
. Blair-Sire-Sogge
[
12
]
and Blair-Huang-Sire-Sogge
[

]
established the following sharp

bounds

(2.4)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}(P_{V})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)},

with Sogge’s exponent

(2.5)

\sigma(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ q_{c}\leq p\leq\infty\\
\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}),\ \ \ \ 2\leq p<q_{c},\end{cases}

where

.
This extends Sogge’s seminal work
[

]
. Moreover, for

when

and

, the requirement on potential can be relaxed to

. Furthermore, (

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\delta]}(P_{V})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On negatively curved manifolds, for
\varepsilon=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
, we have for all

(2.7)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\varepsilon]}(P_{V})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.

When

[

]
(

[

]
(

[

(

[

and

(2.8)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda,\lambda+\delta]}(P_{V})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\sigma(p)}\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, we recall some estimates for the restriction of Laplacian eigenfunctions to submanifolds. We have

(2.9)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda,\lambda+1]}(P^{0})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\delta(k,p)}

3.
Main argument

For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, let
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon]}
,

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda,\ell}(s)={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{|s-\lambda|\in(2^{\ell},2^{\ell+1}]}(s)
,
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\leq 2\lambda}={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{(-\infty,2\lambda]}
. To prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
, we fix
\varepsilon=1
. To prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.5
, we fix
\varepsilon=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
. We shall use the argument in this section to prove these theorems.

In the following, we denote
p_{c}=\frac{2n}{n-1}
when
k=n-1
and
p_{c}=2
when
k\leq n-2
. These are the endpoints in the restriction bounds. We mainly focus on the endpoint estimates at
p=p_{c}
, as one can easily obtain other estimates by the same argument or by interpolation. Note that
p=2
is also an endpoint when
k=n-1
, and we shall handle it independently in our proof. Let
\|f\|_{X}
be the norms
\|f\|_{L^{p_{c}}(\Sigma)}
,
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}
or the semi-norm
|\int_{\Sigma}fd\sigma|
. Suppose

(3.1)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P^{0})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to X}\lesssim A.

Our goal is to prove

(3.2)

\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to X}\lesssim A,

except for some log loss in certain cases.
We use the short notation
A
in many cases where we do not need its explicit form, and the parameters are fixed.

Fix a nonnegative function

\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, such that supp
{\chi}\subset(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
. Let
\lambda\geq 10
. Let
\chi_{\lambda}(s)=\chi(\lambda-s)
. By (
3.1
), we have

\|\chi_{\lambda}(P^{0})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to X}\lesssim A.

For
s\geq 0
, we have
\chi(\lambda+s)=0
, and then

\chi(\lambda-s)=\chi(\lambda-s)+\chi(\lambda+s)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int\hat{\chi}(t)e^{it\lambda}\cos(ts)dt.

By Duhamel’s principle and the spectral theorem, we can calculate the difference between the wave kernel and its perturbation as in
[
45
]
,
[
48
]
,
[
49
]

\displaystyle\cos tP_{V}(x,y)

\displaystyle-\cos tP^{0}(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\sum_{\tau_{k}}\int_{M}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin(t-s)\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}\cos s\tau_{k}\ e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dzds

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\sum_{\tau_{k}}\int_{M}\frac{\cos t\lambda_{j}-\cos t\tau_{k}}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dz.

So we have

\chi_{\lambda}(P_{V})(x,y)-\chi_{\lambda}(P^{0})(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\sum_{\tau_{k}}\int_{M}\frac{\chi_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})-\chi_{\lambda}(\tau_{k})}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dz

Let
m(\lambda_{j},\tau_{k})=\frac{\chi_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})-\chi_{\lambda}(\tau_{k})}{\lambda_{j}^{2}-\tau_{k}^{2}}
.
It suffices to estimate the
L^{2}(M)\to X
bound of the operator associated with the kernel

(3.3)

K(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\sum_{\tau_{k}}\int_{M}m(\lambda_{j},\tau_{k})e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dz.

By the support property of
m(\lambda_{j},\tau_{k})
, we need to consider five cases.

(1)

|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
,
|\lambda_{j}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
.

(2)

|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
,
|\lambda_{j}-\lambda|\in(2^{\ell},2^{\ell+1}]
,
\varepsilon\leq 2^{\ell}\leq\lambda
.

(3)

|\lambda_{j}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
,
|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\in(2^{\ell},2^{\ell+1}]
,
\varepsilon\leq 2^{\ell}\leq\lambda
.

(4)

|\lambda_{j}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
,
\tau_{k}>2\lambda
.

(5)

|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
,
\lambda_{j}>2\lambda
.

Cases 1, 3, 4 are relatively straightforward and their contributions are
O(A)
as desired. Case 2 will give a log loss for critical potentials, but we shall remove it in the next section by the resolvent method. Case 5 is more involved and we shall use a bootstrap argument involving an induction on the dimensions of the submanifolds.

In the following, we fix
q=\frac{n}{2}
, and fix
\frac{1}{p_{0}}\in[\frac{n+3}{2n+2}-\frac{2}{n},\frac{n-1}{2n+2}]
and
\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q}

p_{0}=q_{0}=\infty

. So we always have
q_{0}\geq\frac{2n+2}{n-1}
and then
\sigma(p_{0})+\sigma(q_{0})=1
. In general, for
n\geq 3
we will see that
\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{n+3}{2n+2}-\frac{2}{n}
and
q_{0}=\frac{2n+2}{n-1}
is the best choice.

Case 1.

|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon
,
|\lambda_{j}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon

|m(\lambda_{j},s)|+\varepsilon|\partial_{s}m(\lambda_{j},s)|\lesssim(\lambda\varepsilon)^{-1}.

\displaystyle\sum_{|\lambda_{j}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon}\sum_{|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon}\int_{M}m(\lambda_{j},\tau_{k})e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dz

To handle the complete sum (

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\log(2+(\lambda d_{g}(x,y))^{-1})(1+\lambda d_{g}(x,y))^{-N},\quad n=2\\

\displaystyle\sum_{|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon}\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\int_{M}m_{1}(\lambda_{j},\tau_{k})e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq\varepsilon}\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\int_{M}\int_{\lambda-\varepsilon}^{\lambda+\varepsilon}\partial_{s}m_{1}(\lambda_{j},s){\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\tau_{k}]}(s)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dzds

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\sum_{|\tau_{k}-\lambda|\leq 1}\sum_{\lambda_{j}}\int_{M}m_{1}(\lambda_{j},\lambda-\varepsilon)e_{j}^{0}(x)e_{j}^{0}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(z)e_{\tau_{k}}(y)V(z)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m_{1}(P^{0},\lambda-\varepsilon)(V\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\Sigma)}

3.1.

In this subsection, we only handle

with

and

norms. We shall use a bootstrap argument involving an induction on the dimensions of the submanifolds.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
with
1\leq 2^{\ell}\ll\lambda
. Let
\{x_{j}\}
be a maximal
\lambda^{-1}
-separated set on
\Sigma
, and
\{y_{i}\}
be a maximal
\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}
-separated set on
M
. In local coordinate, let
\alpha\in C_{0}^{\infty}
with
\alpha_{j}=\alpha(\lambda(x-x_{j}))
being a partition of unity on
\Sigma

\beta\in C_{0}^{\infty}
with
\beta_{i}^{\ell}=\beta(\lambda 2^{-\ell}(y-y_{i}))
being a partition of unity on
M
. For each fixed
j

i
such that the support of
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{i}^{\ell}

are within distance
\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}

is bounded by some constant independent of
\ell
, for simplicity we may assume that there is just one such
i_{j}
with
|y_{i_{j}}-x_{j}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}
.

We split the kernel
m_{1}(P^{0},\lambda-1)(x,y)
into the sum of

K_{0}(x,y)=m_{1}(P^{0},\lambda-1)(x,y){\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{d_{g}(x,y)<\lambda^{-1}}

and

K_{\ell}(x,y)=m_{1}(P^{0},\lambda-1)(x,y){\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{d_{g}(x,y)\approx\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}}

with
1\leq 2^{\ell}\ll\lambda
. The operators associated with these kernels are denoted by
K_{0}
and
K_{\ell}
respectively.

Since
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, for any
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, we have for large
\lambda

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{d_{g}(y,x)<\lambda^{-1}}K_{0}(x,y)|V(y)||g(y)|dy<\varepsilon_{1}\|\beta^{0}_{i_{j}}g\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

Since
V\in L^{n/2}(M)
, we can split
V
into the sum of a bounded part and an unbounded part with small
L^{n/2}
-norm. So we may only consider the unbounded part and assume
\|V\|_{L^{n/2}(M)}<\varepsilon_{1}
, while the bounded part can be handled similarly with a better bound. Then

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{d(y,x)\approx\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}}K_{\ell}(x,y)|V(y)||g(y)|dy<\varepsilon_{1}2^{-N\ell}\|\beta_{i_{j}}^{\ell}g\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}.

Let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be nonnegative and satisfy
\rho(0)=1
and
\hat{\rho}(t)=0
if
|t|>1
. Let

\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}\tau)=\sum_{\pm}\rho(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}(\lambda\pm\tau))=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda 2^{-\ell}\hat{\rho}(\lambda 2^{-\ell}t)e^{it\lambda}\cos t\tau dt.

So

\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}\tau)\approx 1
when
|\tau-\lambda|\leq 1
.
By the finite propagation property
[
33

, the wave kernel
\cos tP_{V}(x,y)

. So we have

\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})(x,y)=0\ \ \text{when}\ \ d_{g}(x,y)>\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}.

Since

\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we have for
\tau>0

|\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}\tau)|\lesssim(1+\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}|\tau-\lambda|)^{-N},\ \forall N.

2.4

\sup_{x,y\in M}|\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})(x,y)|\lesssim\lambda^{n}2^{-\ell}.

Thus, by Young’s inequality, we have

\|\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})f\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{n/2}(2^{\ell})^{-1+\frac{n}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

Then for each fixed
j
and
\ell

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{j}K_{\ell}(V\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-k}\|\alpha_{j}K_{\ell}(V\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-k}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}^{2}2^{-2N\ell}\|\beta_{i_{j}}^{\ell}\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(M)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-k}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}^{2}2^{-2N\ell}\|\beta_{i_{j}}^{\ell}\cdot\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})^{-1}{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-k}\cdot\varepsilon_{1}^{2}2^{-2N\ell}\cdot\lambda^{n}\|\tilde{\beta}_{i_{j}}^{\ell}\cdot\eta_{\ell}(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})^{-1}{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)}.

Here the function

is supported in the
4\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}
-neighborhood of

. Thus, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|K_{\ell}(V\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{j}\|\alpha_{j}K_{\ell}(V\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}\lambda^{\frac{n-k}{2}}2^{-N\ell}(\sum_{j}\|\tilde{\beta}_{i_{j}}^{\ell}\cdot\eta(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})^{-1}{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M)})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}\lambda^{\frac{n-k}{2}}2^{-N\ell}\|\eta(\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}P_{V})^{-1}{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P_{V})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1}2^{\ell}}(\Sigma))}

\Sigma

\bigcup_{|y|\leq C_{1}r}\Sigma_{y}

By the proof of (

, the constant

\sup_{|y|<\delta}\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P^{0})\|_{L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(\Sigma_{y})}\leq C_{2}A.

Thus

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P^{0})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Sigma))}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\int_{|y|\leq C_{1}r}\|{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}(P^{0})f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{y})}dy)^{\frac{1}{2}}

In the following, we shall work in a fixed local coordinate chart

\Sigma
. Let

Without loss of generality, we always assume that in this local coordinate,

and

.

Base Step.
We start with the base case
k=1
. Let
\Sigma
be a curve on
M

\|S_{j}(P^{0})\psi(P^{0}/\lambda)\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)\to X}\lesssim A_{1}\lambda^{\sigma(p_{0})-1}.

So we get

\|\sum_{\lambda<2^{j}\leq\lambda/\varepsilon}S_{j}(P^{0})\psi(P^{0}/\lambda)\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)\to X}\lesssim A_{1}\lambda^{\sigma(p_{0})-1}\log(2+\varepsilon^{-1})

This trivially implies an

operator bound by Young’s inequality. In some cases, it can be used to improve (

Let

in

where the smooth function

is supported in
\{d_{g}(x,y)/\theta\in(1/4,4)\}
and

4.1.
Period integrals

In this subsection, we prove (
4.2
) for
\|f\|_{X}=|\int_{\Sigma}fd\sigma|
and
A_{1}=\lambda^{\frac{n-k-1}{2}}
, albeit with a potential
\log\log\lambda
loss in the case
\varepsilon=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
. We first handle the remainder term in (
4.8
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\|r_{j}f\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\theta^{1-n}\cdot\theta^{k}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\theta}(\Sigma))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\theta^{1-n}\cdot\theta^{k}\theta^{\frac{n-k}{p_{0}}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\theta^{1-\frac{n-k}{p_{0}^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|r_{j}f\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda^{-1}+\lambda^{-2+\frac{n-k}{p_{0}^{\prime}}})\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)}.

It is better than the desired bound, since

-2+\frac{n-k}{p_{0}^{\prime}}<-2+\frac{n}{p_{0}^{\prime}}-\frac{k}{2}=\frac{n-k-1}{2}+\sigma(p_{0})-1.

For the following type oscillatory integrals

\int_{\Sigma}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a(x,y)dx,

where
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}
is supported in
\{(x,y):d_{g}(x,y)\approx 1\}
and
|\partial_{x,y}^{\gamma}a(x,y)|\leq C_{\gamma}.
If the support of
a(x,y)
is sufficiently small, then

is a critical point for the phase function
if and only if the geodesic connecting
x,y
is perpendicular to
\Sigma
, and in this case the hessian is nondegenerate. Thus, by stationary phase we know it is of size
O(\lambda^{-k/2})
.

Let
\{x_{\ell}\}
be a maximal
\theta
-separated set in
\Sigma
. Suppose that
\rho_{\theta}
is a smooth cutoff function on
\Sigma
with support of diameter
\sim\theta
, and
\eta_{\theta}
is a smooth cutoff function on

with support of diameter
\sim\theta
, and

is a partition of unity on

. Then by rescaling and stationary phase we have

\displaystyle\|T_{j}f\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\theta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\sum_{\ell}\Big|\iint e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a_{\theta}(x,y)\rho_{\theta}(x-x_{\ell})\eta_{\theta}(y-x_{\ell})f(y)dydx\Big|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\theta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\theta^{n+k}\sum_{\ell}\Big|\iint e^{i2^{j}d_{g}(\theta x+x_{\ell},\theta y+x_{\ell})/\theta}a_{\theta}(\theta x+x_{\ell},\theta y+x_{\ell})\rho_{\theta}(\theta x)\eta_{\theta}(\theta y)f(\theta y+x_{\ell})dydx\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\theta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\theta^{n+k}(2^{j})^{-k/2}\sum_{\ell}\int|\eta_{\theta}(\theta y)f(\theta y+x_{\ell})|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\theta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot\theta^{k}\cdot(2^{j})^{-k/2}\cdot\theta^{\frac{n-k}{p_{0}}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{n-k-3}{2}}\theta^{-\frac{n-k-1}{2}+\frac{n-k}{p_{0}}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)}.

Here we apply stationary phase to the phase function
d_{\theta}(x,y)=d_{g}(\theta x+x_{\ell},\theta y+x_{\ell})/\theta
and use the fact that
d_{\theta}(x,y)
becomes arbitrarily close to Euclidean distance in
C^{\infty}
-topology as
\theta\to 0
. Since

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|T_{j}f\|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda^{\frac{n-k-3}{2}}\log\lambda+\lambda^{-2+\frac{n-k}{p_{0}^{\prime}}}\log\lambda)\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)}.

It is better than the desired bound, since

\frac{n-k-3}{2}<\frac{n-k-1}{2}+\sigma(p_{0})-1.

So we complete the proof of (
4.2
) for period integrals, albeit with a potential
\log\log\lambda
loss from (
4.6
) if
\varepsilon=(\log\lambda)^{-1}
.

without log loss. To remove the log loss for

5.
Restriction to curved hypersurfaces

In the potential-free case, for
k=n-1
and
p\geq\frac{2n}{n-1}
, zonal functions saturate the restriction estimate in Theorem
1.1
for any hypersurface. However, for
p<\frac{2n}{n-1}
, Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov
[
23
]
and Hu
[
44
]
proved that a power improvement is possible for curved hypersurfaces. We prove this type of improved estimates for singular potentials.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a hypersurface with positive
(or negative) definite second fundamental form. Suppose
2\leq p<\frac{2n}{n-1}
. If
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)\cap L^{n/2}(M)
, then we have

(5.1)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{3}-\frac{2n-3}{3p}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},

albeit with a potential logarithmic loss when
(n,p)\in\{(n,p):n\geq 4,p<2.3\}
. The loss can be removed if
V\in L^{q}(M)
with
q>n/2
.

As in Theorem
1.2
, we can obtain improved bounds on negatively curved surfaces. These results were established by Park
[
51
]
for Laplacian eigenfunctions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
M
be a negatively curved surface. Let
\Sigma\subset M
be a curve with non-vanishing geodesic curvature. Suppose
2\leq p<4
. If
V\in\mathcal{K}(M)
, then we have

(5.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{3p}}(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

The proof of Theorem
5.2
is essentially the same as the proof of Theorem
1.2
, so we only prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

The main argument in Section
3
can still work if we replace the exponent
\delta(k,p)
by
\tilde{\delta}(k,p)=\frac{n-1}{3}-\frac{2n-3}{3p}
. We just need to refine Case 2 to remove the log loss. We follow the strategy in Section
4
. We first consider
p\geq p_{c}
. By interpolation between the
L^{2}-L^{2}
bound and the
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
bound we have

(5.3)

\|T^{\lambda}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}(n-\frac{4}{3})}.

By interpolation between (
5.3
) and (
4.20
) we get

(5.4)

\|T^{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{0}^{\prime}}(M)\to L^{p}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{n-2}{6p}-\frac{5n-6}{6p_{0}}}.

Thus, inserting the power
\alpha
from (
5.4
) into (

Restriction of toral eigenfunctions

Let
\varepsilon=\lambda^{-1}
and
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{\lambda}={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[\lambda-\varepsilon,\lambda+\varepsilon]}

essentially contain at most one eigenvalue of

By Bourgain-Rudnick

We shall extend these results for

with

So we complete the proof of Theorem

Appendix: Further discussions on oscillatory integrals

In the Section 4, we have removed the log loss for
k\leq n/2
when
n\geq 5
and for all
k
when
n\leq 4
. It remains to handle
k>\frac{n}{2}
when
n\geq 5
. By Remark
4.1
, we can reduce the problem to the estimate of the oscillatory integral operator in (
4.9
). In this section, we shall further investigate this operator bound. We show that in the Euclidean model case, one can improve the range of
k
to
k\leq[2n/3]-2
for
n\geq 11
.

For the Euclidean distance, we have
d_{g}(x,y)=\sqrt{|u-y|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}}
with
x=(u,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let

(7.1)

T_{\lambda}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{i\lambda\sqrt{|u-y|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}}}a(x,y)f(y)dy,

where
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}
is supported in
\{(x,y):d_{g}(x,y)\approx 1\}
and
|\partial_{x,y}^{\gamma}a(x,y)|\leq C_{\gamma}.
This is the adjoint operator of (
4.9
). In our problem, we are interested in the norm

\|T_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{c}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
\frac{1}{p_{0}}\in[\frac{n+3}{2n+2}-\frac{2}{n},\frac{n-1}{2n+2}]
,
n\geq 5
and
k>n/2
. By Remark
4.1
, we want to beat the bound
\lambda^{-\frac{k}{p_{c}}+\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}}
.

7.1.
Lower bound

Knapp type.

Suppose that
y\sim 0
and
x\sim e_{n}
on the support of
a(x,y)
, and
a(x,y)
is real-valued and has a fixed sign on the support. Here
e_{n}=(0,...0,1)
. We fix
f(y)=1
when
|y|<\frac{1}{100}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
f(y)=0
otherwise. Let

E=\{x=(u,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|u|<\frac{1}{100}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},\ |x-e_{n}|<\frac{1}{100}\}.

Then

|\sqrt{|u-y|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}}-|x^{\prime}||\leq\frac{1}{10}\lambda^{-1}

if
x\in E
and
|y|<\frac{1}{100}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Thus,

|T_{\lambda}f(x)|=|e^{-i\lambda|x^{\prime}|}T_{\lambda}f(x)|\approx\lambda^{-k/2},\ \ \forall x\in E.

We get

\|T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{k}{2q}},

and

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\approx\lambda^{-\frac{k}{2p}}.

So we have

\|T_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{k}{2}(\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q})}.

This lower bound is sharp when
p^{\prime}=q
and
k\leq n-2
.

Gaussian beam type.

Suppose that
x\sim 0
and
y\sim e_{1}
on the support of
a(x,y)
, and
a(x,y)
is real-valued and has a fixed sign on the support. Here
e_{1}=(1,0,...,0)
. Let
y=(y_{1},y^{\prime})
. We fix
f(y)=e^{-i\lambda y_{1}}
when
|y_{1}-1|<\frac{1}{100}
and
|y^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{100}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and
f(y)=0
otherwise. Let

E=\{x=(u_{1},u^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|u_{1}|<\frac{1}{100},\ |u^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}<\frac{1}{100}\lambda^{-1}\}

Then

|\sqrt{|y_{1}-u_{1}|^{2}+|u^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}+|x^{\prime}|^{2}}-(y_{1}-u_{1})|\leq\frac{1}{10}\lambda^{-1}

if
x\in E
and
|y^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{100}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thus,

|T_{\lambda}(x)|=|e^{i\lambda u_{1}}T_{\lambda}f(x)|\approx\lambda^{-\frac{k-1}{2}},\ \forall x\in E.

We get

\|T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{k-1}{2}-\frac{n-1}{2q}},

and

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\approx\lambda^{-\frac{k-1}{2p}}.

So we have

\|T_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{k-1}{2p^{\prime}}-\frac{n-1}{2q}}.

This lower bound is sharp when
p^{\prime}=q
and
k\geq n-2
.

7.2.
An upper bound

We shall use the Stein-Tomas argument to estimate an upper bound. We only consider
k\leq n-2
in the following, since
p_{c}=2
and we can apply
TT^{*}
argument.

For any fixed
x^{\prime}=t\omega
with
t=|x^{\prime}|
we analyze the operator

T_{\lambda}^{t}f(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}e^{i\lambda\sqrt{|u-y|^{2}+t^{2}}}a(u,t\omega,y)f(y)dy.

We shall establish operator bounds for
T_{\lambda}^{t}
independent of
\omega
. We first claim that if
t,t^{\prime}\approx r
and
t\neq t^{\prime}
then

(7.2)

\|T_{\lambda}^{t}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\lesssim\lambda^{-k/2}r^{-(k+1)/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})},

(7.3)

\|T_{\lambda}^{t}(T_{\lambda}^{t^{\prime}})^{*}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\lesssim((\lambda r^{-1}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-k/2}r^{-1}+(1+\lambda r^{11}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-N})\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})},\ \forall N.

Then by the proof of the Stein-Tomas restriction theorem (see
[
59
, Corollary 0.3.7]
), we have for
q=2(k+2)/k

(7.4)

(\int_{r}^{2r}\|T_{\lambda}^{t}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}^{q}dt)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{k+1}{q}}r^{-\frac{k+12}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}.

Moreover, by fixing one variable, one can verify the Carleson-Sjölin condition and apply Stein’s oscillatory integral theorem (see
[
59
, Theorem 2.2.1]
). We claim that

(7.5)

\|T_{\lambda}^{t}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\lesssim\lambda^{-(k-1)/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}

(7.6)

\|T_{\lambda}^{t}f\|_{L^{q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\lesssim\lambda^{-k/q_{0}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}

for
q_{0}=\frac{2(k+1)}{k-1}
, and then by interpolation we get

(7.7)

\|T_{\lambda}^{t}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{k+1}{q}}\lambda^{\frac{k+1}{kq}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}.

So

(7.8)

(\int_{r}^{2r}\|T_{\lambda}^{t}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}^{q}dt)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{k+1}{q}}\lambda^{\frac{k+1}{kq}}r^{1/q}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}.

Using the polar coordinate in
x^{\prime}
and the dyadic decomposition in
t
, by (
7.4
) and (
7.8
) we get

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-(k+1)/q}\Big(\sum_{j\geq 0}\min\{2^{j(k+12)},\ \lambda^{\frac{k+1}{k}}2^{-j}\}\cdot 2^{-j(n-k-1)}\Big)^{1/q}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-\frac{(1+k)(-13+11k+k^{2}+n)}{2(2+k)(13+k)}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}.

By the proof of (
1.2
) we have for
k\leq n-3

\|T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{-k/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}.

We fix
\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{n+3}{2n+2}-\frac{2}{n}
. Note that
p_{0}\in[2,q]
when
k\leq n-3
. So by interpolation we get

(7.9)

\|T_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{\alpha(p_{0},k,n)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})},

where

\displaystyle\alpha(p_{0},k,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{26+11n-n^{2}+k(56+13n-14n^{2})+k^{2}(6+2n-n^{2})}{2(13+k)n(1+n)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{3k}{2n}-\frac{1}{2}+O(\frac{1}{n})

for
n/2<k\leq n-3
.
However,

-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}=-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+O(\frac{1}{n}).

A direct calculation shows that
\alpha(p_{0},k,n)<-\frac{k}{2}+\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p_{0}}
holds for
k\leq[2n/3]-2
when
n\geq 11
. This improves the range

obtained in Section 4.

However, we have

\alpha(p_{0},k,n)+\frac{k}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{0}}+\frac{1}{2})=\frac{(1+k)(13+2k-n)(2+n)}{2(13+k)n(1+n)}>0

for

. So the upper bound here is strictly greater than the Knapp type lower bound. It would be interesting to find the sharp upper bound.

To prove (

7.2.1.
Discussions of oscillatory integrals

Both of the lower bounds are still valid on general manifolds. The Knapp type lower bound is greater than the Gaussian beam type lower bound if and only if
k<n-1-\frac{q}{p^{\prime}}
. Both of the lower bounds are sharp when
p^{\prime}=q
. If we fix
\frac{1}{p_{0}}=\frac{n+3}{2n+2}-\frac{2}{n}
, then the lower bounds are strictly less than the bounds that we want to beat (see (
4.17
), (
4.29
)) when
(p,q)=(p_{c}^{\prime},p_{0})
. One might expect that these two examples together saturate the sharp upper bounds of the oscillatory integral operator.

The proof of the upper bound relies on the explicit formula of the Euclidean distance and the flatness of the submanifold. The main difficulty is that the rank of mixed Hessian
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}d_{g}(x,y)
becomes degenerate (rank=
k-1
) when
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\{0\}^{n-k}
. In general, this degeneracy happens when the geodesic connecting
x\in M
and
y\in\Sigma
is tangent to the submanifold
\Sigma
at
y
. We call the point
x
a degenerating point of
\Sigma
if such degeneracy occurs for some
y\in\Sigma
. Let the degenerating set of
\Sigma
be the collection of all such degenerating points. For example, in the above model case, the degenerating set of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is just
\mathbb{R}^{k}
itself. However, in general, the dimension of the degenerating set can be as large as
\min\{2k,n\}
, making it difficult to precisely estimate the operator norm.

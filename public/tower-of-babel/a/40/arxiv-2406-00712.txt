A Worldsheet Derivation of the Classical Off-shell Boundary Action for the Dilaton in Half-Space
Amr Ahmadain, Rifath Khan

Abstract
We use the method of images to present a worldsheet derivation of the sphere partition function for the dilaton in half-space to leading order in α ′ \alpha^{\prime} . We use Tseytlin’s sphere prescription to obtain the total (bulk and boundary) off-shell classical bosonic string action for the dilaton in half-space and show that it satisfies the requirement for a well-defined variational principle with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

The method of images

The quantity we want calculate is

\displaystyle Z_{\text{HS}}

\displaystyle=\int_{X^{D}\geq 0}[DX]e^{-I_{\text{NLSM}}[X,\Phi,\omega,\gamma]}.

This is the path integral of embeddings of the spherical worldsheet in half-space i.e. on the right side of the wall (

However, this is not all of the embeddings one can consider. For example, one can also consider an embedding which intersects the wall in only one point or in a disc. We expect all such embeddings to be a measure zero set in the path integral and thus can be thrown away. Therefore, all embeddings that give a nonzero contribution can be classified by the number of bounces off the wall.

It is important to note that whenever the worldsheet bounces off the wall, there is a contribution to the path integral. This is analogous to the particle case, where the contribution of the bounce is a plus sign for Neumann boundary conditions and a minus sign for Dirichlet boundary conditions
Bastianelli et al. (2007)
;
Bastianelli et al. (2008)
;
Bastianelli et al. (2010)
. We expect that the sign in the string bounce case should impose some boundary condition, but it is unclear which boundary condition it is meant to impose (since it was only an analogy). We will choose the plus sign as the contribution from the worldsheet bounce, and find that it gives results consistent with Dirichlet boundary conditions. A deeper understanding of why this sign imposes Dirichlet boundary conditions is needed, and we leave this as an open problem.

Consider the embedding that bounces off the wall once. The worldsheet intersects the wall in a circle. The action being an integral on the worldsheet can be split into two pieces, each for a piece emanating from the circle. Now, if we reflect the spacetime and a single piece, the action remains the same. So we get an embedding in the reflected spacetime that crosses the wall once. But, there are exactly two embeddings in the reflected target space that correspond to a single embedding in half space. The same argument holds for any number of bounces (including zero bounces). Therefore, we can express the path integral of embeddings in half-space as one half the path integral in the reflected space. We will explore in a bit more detail the relationship between half-space and reflected space in light of reflected Brownian motion and stochastic dynamics in section

The discussion in this section is speculative in nature and, as such, is not rigorous. 5.2

A generalization of the current work to

and curved target spaces with boundaries is an interesting direction. The generalization to codimension-2 boundaries, especially conical geometries are all interesting future directions. A worldsheet understanding of how to impose Dirichlet boundary conditions on propagating fields in target spacetime is required to derive the GHY boundary term in the on-shell classical action. This, in turn, is important for deriving the classical black hole entropy

.

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l0}Y_{l0}(0)\right)\ \delta\left(\xi-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l0}Y_{l0}(0)\right).

(128)

The
m\neq 0
terms of the above integral factorises out and cancels the corresponding part in
Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\int\left(\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}dx_{l0}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l(l+1)e^{\epsilon l(l+1)}x^{2}_{l0}}

(129)

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l0}Y_{l0}(0)\right)\ \delta\left(\xi-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l0}Y_{l0}(0)\right).

(130)

Also, we have
Y_{l0}(0)=\sqrt{\dfrac{2l+1}{4\pi}}=:\omega_{l}
. So we have

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\int\left(\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}dx_{l}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l(l+1)e^{\epsilon l(l+1)}x^{2}_{l}}

(131)

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\omega_{l}x_{l}\right)\ \delta\left(\xi-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\omega_{l}x_{l}\right),

(132)

where we have dropped the subscript
``0"
from
x_{l0}
and
Y_{l0}
. We can absorb
\omega_{l}
into
x_{l}
by a change of integration variable to get

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\int\left(\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\omega_{l}}dx_{l}\right)e^{-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{l(l+1)}{\omega_{l}^{2}}e^{\epsilon l(l+1)}x^{2}_{l}}

(133)

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l}\right)\ \delta\left(\xi-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x_{l}\right).

(134)

Now we shall pick a mode to integrate over, say
x_{1}
. Integrating over
x_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)\int\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\omega_{l}}dx_{l}\right)

(135)

\displaystyle\exp{{-\frac{1}{4 \pi} \sum_{l=2}^\infty\frac{l(l+1)}{\omega^2_l} e^{\epsilon l(l+1)} x^2_{l}} -\frac{2}{4 \pi\omega^2_1} e^{2\epsilon} \left(\xi-\sum_{l=2}^\infty x_{l}\right)^2}

(136)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\frac{2}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi^{2}}\int\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\omega_{l}}dx_{l}\right)

(137)

\displaystyle\exp{-\frac{1}{4 \pi} \sum_{l=2}^\infty\frac{l(l+1)}{\omega^2_l} e^{\epsilon l(l+1)} x^2_{l} -\frac{2}{4 \pi\omega^2_1} e^{2\epsilon} \left(\sum_{l=2}^\infty x_{l}\right)^2 -\frac{4}{4 \pi\omega^2_1} e^{2\epsilon} \xi\sum_{l=2}^\infty x_{l}}

(138)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\frac{2}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi^{2}}\int\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\omega_{l}}dx_{l}\right)e^{\Gamma},

(139)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{l(l+1)}{\omega^{2}_{l}}e^{\epsilon l(l+1)}x^{2}_{l}-\frac{2}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\left(\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}x_{l}\right)^{2}-\frac{4}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}x_{l}

(140)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{n(n+1)}{\omega^{2}_{n}}e^{\epsilon n(n+1)}x_{n}\delta_{nm}x_{m}-\frac{2}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}x_{n}x_{m}-\frac{4}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}x_{l}

(141)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}_{1}}{\omega^{2}_{n}}\frac{n(n+1)}{2}e^{\epsilon n(n+1)-2\epsilon}\delta_{nm}+1\right)x_{n}x_{m}-\frac{4}{4\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}x_{l}

(142)

\displaystyle=-M_{nm}\tilde{x}_{n}\tilde{x}_{m}-\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{\epsilon}\xi\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\tilde{x}_{l},

(143)

and

\displaystyle M_{nm}

\displaystyle:=\left(\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\delta_{nm}+1\right)

(144)

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\omega^{2}_{1}}{\omega^{2}_{n}}\frac{n(n+1)}{2}e^{\epsilon n(n+1)-2\epsilon}

(145)

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\omega_{1}}e^{\epsilon}x_{n}

(146)

So we get

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{l}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi^{2}}\int\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\sqrt{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-\epsilon}d\tilde{x}_{l}\right)e^{\Gamma},

(147)

Now let us diagonalise the real symmetric matrix M by an orthogonal transformation
\tilde{x}_{n}=\Lambda_{nm}y_{m}
to get a diagonal matrix
D=\Lambda^{T}M\Lambda

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{l}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi^{2}}\frac{1}{\text{Det}(\Lambda)}\int\left(\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\sqrt{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-\epsilon}dy_{n}\right)

(148)

\displaystyle\exp{- \sum_{n=2}^\infty D_{n} y^2_n -\xi\sum_{n=2}^\infty k_n y_n},

(149)

where
k_{n}:=\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{\epsilon}\Lambda_{mn}
and a factor of
\text{Det}(\Lambda)
comes from the Jacobian. Now one can complete the squares and shift the mean to get (one can check that all
D_{n}>0
)

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{l}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi^{2}}\frac{1}{\text{Det}(\Lambda)}\exp{ \sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{k^2_n}{4D_{n}}\xi^2}

(150)

\displaystyle\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\int\sqrt{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-\epsilon}dy_{n}\exp{- D_{n} \left(y_n +\xi\frac{k_n}{2D_{n}}\right)^2}

(151)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{l=2}^{\infty}\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{l}}\right)\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}e^{-2\Phi_{0}}}{\omega_{1}Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}}\frac{1}{\text{Det}(\Lambda)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Det}(M)}}\left(\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\sqrt{2\alpha^{\prime}}\pi e^{-\epsilon}\right)

(152)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}e^{2\epsilon}\xi^{2}}\exp{ \sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{k^2_n}{4D_{n}}\xi^2}.

(153)

All of the annoying factors in (
152
) gets absorbed into
Z^{(D)}_{\text{nz}(m\neq 0)}
and we finaly get

\displaystyle\left\langle f\left(\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ f\left(\xi\right)e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\xi\ e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}},

(154)

where

\displaystyle\Omega^{-1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{e^{2\epsilon}}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}\left(1-\mathbb{M}\right)\,,

(155)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}

\displaystyle:=\omega^{2}_{1}\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-2\epsilon}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}_{n}}{D_{n}}\,.

(156)

Now let us calculate
\mathbb{M}
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}_{1}\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-2\epsilon}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}_{n}}{D_{n}}=\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-2\epsilon}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\tilde{m}=2}^{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{\epsilon}\Lambda_{mn}\frac{1}{D_{n}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{\epsilon}\Lambda_{\tilde{m}n}

(157)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}[\Lambda D^{-1}\Lambda^{T}]_{nm}=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}[M^{-1}]_{nm},

(158)

which follows from
\Lambda
being an orthogonal matrix. So
\mathbb{M}
is the sum of all the entries of the inverse of the matrix

. One can show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\tilde{\lambda}_{n}^{-1}}}\,\,.

(159)

One can also show that

\displaystyle\Omega=\left(\frac{e^{2\epsilon}}{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}\left(1-\mathbb{M}\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi\omega^{2}_{1}}{e^{2\epsilon}}\left(1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\tilde{\lambda}_{n}^{-1}\right)=\frac{3}{2}e^{-2\epsilon}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2n+1}{n(n+1)}e^{-\epsilon n(n+1)}

(160)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n+1}{n(n+1)}e^{-\epsilon n(n+1)}\,.

(161)

By Euler–Maclaurin summation formula,
\Omega
can be written as

\displaystyle\Omega=\log\epsilon^{-1}+\dots,

(162)

where
\dots
represent polynomial divergences in
\epsilon
and also the finite terms.

If we specialize to

, then

\displaystyle\left\langle\absolutevalue{\eta^D(z_0)}^{N}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+N}{2}\right)\Omega^{N/2}.

(164)

We thus get

\displaystyle\left\langle\absolutevalue{\eta^D(z_0)}^{N}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+N}{2}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n+1}{n(n+1)}e^{-\epsilon n(n+1)}\right)^{N/2}\,.

(165)

Let us do a consistency check of (
165
) for

, in which case, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta^{D}(z_{0})\eta^{D}(z_{0})\right\rangle

(166)

which agrees with
Andreev et al. (1990)

Now we focus on the original term we set out to compute:

(167)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\int_{-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}}^{\infty}d\xi\ \left(Y^{D}+\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\xi\right)e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}}d\xi\ \left(Y^{D}+\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\xi\right)e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\int_{-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}}^{\infty}d\xi\ e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}}d\xi\ e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}

(169)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\int_{-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}}^{\infty}d\xi\ \xi e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}}d\xi\ \xi e^{-\xi^{2}/\Omega}

(170)

\displaystyle=\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}{2}\left(1+\text{erf}\left(\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}\right)\right)-\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}{2}\left(\text{erfc}\left(\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}\right)\right)

(171)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\frac{\Omega}{2}e^{-\frac{(Y^{D})^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\pi\Omega}}\frac{\Omega}{2}e^{-\frac{(Y^{D})^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}

(172)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Omega}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{(Y^{D})^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}+\frac{Y^{D}}{2}\left(1+\text{erf}\left(\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}\right)-\text{erfc}\left(\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}\right)\right)

(173)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\Omega}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\frac{(Y^{D})^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}+Y^{D}\text{erf}\left(\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}\right).

(174)

So finally, we obtain our desired expression

(175)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}\left(\left\langle\absolutevalue{\eta^D(z_0)}\right\rangle e^{-\frac{(Y^{D})^{2}}{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}+\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}\text{erf}\left(\frac{Y^{D}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}\Omega}}\right)\right).

where we used (

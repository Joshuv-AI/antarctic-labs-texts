Higher Auslander–Solberg correspondence for exact categories
Jacob Fjeld Grevstad

Abstract
We introduce the concept of an n n -minimal Auslander–Gorenstein category and n n -precluster tilting subcategory. With this, we create an analog of the higher Auslander–Solberg correspondence (Iyama–Solberg 2018) for exact categories. Our approach is based on the recent generalization of the (higher) Auslander correspondence to exact categories (Henrard–Kvamme–Roosmalen 2020, Ebrahimi–Nasr-Isfahani 2021).



Introduction

In the early ’70s Auslander showed a correspondence between representation finite algebras, and algebras
\Gamma
satisfying

\gldim\Gamma\leq 2\leq\domdim\Gamma

up to Morita equivalence
[
Aus71
]
. Historically, this was the starting point of Auslander–Reiten theory.

In more recent years the Auslander correspondence has been generalized in several ways. In 2007, Iyama established a correspondence between
n
-cluster tilting modules and algebras satisfying

\gldim\Gamma\leq n+1\leq\domdim\Gamma,

known as the higher Auslander correspondence
[
Iya07
]
. In the same way the Auslander-correspondence is significant for Auslander–Reiten theory,
n
-cluster tilting modules form an important part of higher Auslander–Reiten theory. One way this manifests is that
n
-cluster tilting modules satisfy
\tau_{n}(M)\in\add M
and
\tau^{-}_{n}(M)\in\add M
, where
\tau_{n}
and
\tau^{-}_{n}
are the higher Auslander–Retien translates.

Inspired by this property, Iyama and Solberg defined
n
-precluster tilting modules
[
IS18
]
to be
n
-rigid generator-cogenerators which satisfied
\tau_{n}(M)\in\add M
and
\tau^{-}_{n}(M)\in\add M
. They also established a higher Auslander–Solberg correspondence between
n
-precluster tilting modules and Gorenstein algebras satisfying

\id_{\Gamma}\Gamma\leq n+1\leq\domdim\Gamma,

replacing finite global dimension with being Gorenstein. The case of
n=1
was proved by Auslander and Solberg in 1993
[
AS93a
]
, hence the name.

In 2020 Henrard, Kvamme and Roosmalen introduced an analog of the Auslander correspondence for exact categories. Instead of taking the endomorphism ring of an additive generator for a representation finite algebra, they take an arbitrary exact category an consider the category of admissibly presented modules. This creates a correspondence between exact categories and what they call Auslander exact categories.

The following year Ebrahimi and Nasr-Isfahani showed that the construction of Henrard–Kvamme–Roosmalen also gives a correspondence between
n
-cluster tilting subcategories and
n
-Auslander exact categories.

In this paper, in much the same way, we introduce the concept of an
n
-precluster tilting subcategory of an exact category and the concept of an
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein exact category. We also show that the construction by Henrard–Kvamme–Roosmalen gives a bijection between these.

Structure and main theorems

The goal of this paper is to generalize the correspondence in
[
HKvR22
]
to create an analog of the higher Auslander–Solberg correspondence for exact categories. In
Section

1
we go over some basic results and definition relating to exact categories, and in
Section

2
we recall some properties of the category of admissible modules from
[
HKvR22
]
.

The main theory of this paper is in
Section

3
where we discuss a few different related correspondences.

The Morita–Tachikawa correspondence
[
GK15
,
Rin07
]
says that there is a bijection between generator-cogenerators and algebras with dominant dimension at least 2. Since we will be working in exact categories which don’t necessarily have enough injectives, the concept of dominant dimension is not directly useful. Therefore we define Morita–Tachikawa categories (
Definition

3.1
) as an exact analog to algebras with dominant dimension at least 2. We do this in an analogues way to how Henrard–Kvamme–Roosmalen define Auslander exact categories
[
HKvR22
, Def 1.3]
. This gives us our first main theorem.

Theorem A

(
Theorem

3.3
)
.

There is a bijection between equivalence classes of contravariantly finite, generating-cogenerating subcategories and equivalence classes of Morita–Tachikawa categories.

The proof of this theorem is implicit in
[
ENI21
]
, but they do not state it explicitly.

Another generalization of the Auslander correspondence is the higher Auslander–Solberg correspondence
[
IS18
]
. This is a correspondence between
n
-precluster tilting modules and Gorenstein algebras
\Gamma
satisfying

\id_{\Gamma}\Gamma\leq n+1\leq\domdim\Gamma.

Such algebras are called
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein algebras.

Precluster tilting can be defined as a weakening of
n
-cluster tilting, where instead of requiring
{}^{\perp_{n-1}}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\perp_{n-1}}
we only require
{}^{\perp_{n-1}}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\perp_{n-1}}
. This definition also works in the exact case and is discussed in
Section

4
. However, this definition does not work when
n=1
.

In
[
IS18
]
Iyama and Solberg define precluster tilting using the higher Auslander–Reiten translate, by requiring that
\tau_{n}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and
\tau_{n}^{-}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. This is not available in the exact setting, so we replace it by the condition of
\mathcal{M}
being relative
(n-1)
-cotilting (
Definition

3.5
). This definition is motivated by the proof of
[
IS18
, Thm 4.4]

A finite dimensional algebra is Gorenstein if and only if the subcategory of projectives is cotilting. This is therefore the restriction we put on
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein categories. The analog of
\id_{\Gamma}\Gamma
is simply the injective dimension of the projectives, while for the dominant dimension we use a similar construction as for Morita–Tachikawa categories (
Definition

3.4
) .

For finite dimensional algebras the criteria for
\Gamma
to be Gorenstein is redundant, since it can be proven that any algebra satisfying

\id_{\Gamma}\Gamma\leq n+1\leq\domdim\Gamma

is Gorenstein. This eludes us to a weaker definition of
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein categories, namely if we only require the projectives to be partial cotilting. We call this partial
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein categories, and we similarly define partial
n
-precluster tilting subcategories to only be partial relative cotilting.

Our second main theorem is then two analogs of the Auslander–Solberg correspondence.

Theorem B

(
Theorem

3.8
,
Theorem

3.11
)
.

There is a correspondence between equivalence classes of (partial)
n
-precluster tilting subcategories and equivalence classes of (partial)
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein categories.

In
Section

5
we verify that the correspondence actually is an analog of the Auslander–Solberg correspondence by comparing it to the classical case. Specifically, we show that a module
M
over an Artin algebra, is
n
-precluster tilting if and only if
\add M
is an
n
-precluster tilting subcategory. We show that
\operatorname{mod}\Gamma
is
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein if and only if
\Gamma
is
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein. And we show that the correspondence in
Section

3
restricts to the classical one for Artin algebras, as described in
[
IS18
]
.

In
Section

6
we extend some propositions from
[
HKvR22
]
about injective and projective objects to Morita–Tachikawa categories.

Lastly, in
Section

7
, we give a few simple examples of
n
-precluster tilting subcategories.

3
Auslander–Solberg correspondence

In this section we apply the theory of localization at two-sided admissibly percolating subcategories as described in
[
HvR20
]
to construct a correspondence between Morita–Tachikawa categories and contravariantly finite generating–cogenerating subcategories. This restricts to a correspondence between (partial)
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein categories and (partial)
n
-precluster tilting subcategories. We use the term
weak equivalence
to refer to a map which becomes an isomorphism under localization, and we write
X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
for a weak equivalence from
X
to
Y
.

3.1
The bijection

In
[
HKvR22
]
a correspondence between
Auslander exact categories
and exact categories is constructed. Here we generalize this correspondence slightly to what we have chosen to call
Morita–Tachikawa categories
.

Definition 3.1

(Morita–Tachikawa category)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category and let
\mathcal{P}
the projective objects of
\mathcal{E}
. We write
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
for the
\Hom
-orthogonal complement

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}:=\{X\in\mathcal{E}|\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,P)=0,\forall P\in\mathcal{P}\}.

We say that
\mathcal{E}
is a
Morita–Tachikawa category
iff

1.

\mathcal{E}
has enough projectives.

2.

({}^{\perp}\mathcal{P},\cogen\mathcal{P})
forms a torsion pair.

3.

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
satisfies axiom
A2
of
[
HvR20
, Def 6.1]
, i.e. any morphism
X\to E
with
E\in{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
is admissible with image in
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
.

4.

\Ext^{1}({}^{\perp}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P})=0
.

Note, condition 2 and 3 together gives us that
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
is two-sided admissibly percolating
[
HKvR22
, Prop 2.22(1)]
.

Throughout this section we consider the localization
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. The following lemma ensures that this is a well defined category even when we don’t assume
\mathcal{E}
to be essentially small.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Morita–Tachikawa category, with projective objects
\mathcal{P}
. Consider the localization functor
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. Then for every
X\in\mathcal{E}
there is a weak equivalence
X\xrightarrow{\sim}F
where
F
is a second syzygy. Further, whenever
F
is a second syzygy and
X
is arbitrary, we have

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,F)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}}(X,F).

In particular the localization functor induces an equivalence of (non-exact) categories between
\Omega^{2}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. Further, the inverse
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\to\Omega^{2}\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
is left exact, and gives a right adjoint to the localization functor.

Proof.

Since
({}^{\perp}\mathcal{P},\cogen\mathcal{P})
is a torsion pair we have a conflation
tX\rightarrowtail X\twoheadrightarrow fX
with
tX\in{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
and
fX\in\cogen\mathcal{P}
. This shows that
X
is weakly equivalent to a first syzygy, namely
fX
. The argument in
[
HKvR22
, Prop 4.4(3)]
shows that every first syzygy is weakly equivalent to a second syzygy, and thus
X
is weakly equivalent to a second syzygy.

Next we show that
\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,F)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}}(X,F)
whenever

is a second syzygy. The argument is similar to
[
HKvR22
, Prop 4.5]
. The localization functor gives a map
\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,F)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}}(X,F)
. We wish to show that this is a bijection. Let
f\colon X\to F
be in the kernel. Then by
[
HKvR22
, Prop 2.22(1)]
and
[
HvR20
, Prop 4.10]
there is a weak equivalence
g\colon F\xrightarrow{\sim}Y
such that the composition
gf
is 0. The kernel of
g
is an element of
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
mapping to
F
, hence it must be 0. Thus
g
is a monomorphism and it follows that
f=0
, which shows injectivity.

We now show surjectivity. Any map in
\Hom_{\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}}(X,F)
is of the form
X\to Y\xleftarrow{\sim}F

[
HvR20
, Prop 4.6, Construction 2.8(3)]
. We want to show that the weak equivalence is a split monomorphism. Just like before it is clear that it is mono, so we have a conflation
F\rightarrowtail Y\twoheadrightarrow tY
, with
tY\in{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. Since
F
is a second syzygy, we also have a conflation
F\rightarrowtail P\twoheadrightarrow F^{\prime}
with
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
F^{\prime}\in\cogen\mathcal{P}
. Then we get an exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0=\Hom(tY,F^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ext^{1}(tY,F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ext^{1}(tY,P)=0.}

Thus
\Ext^{1}(tY,F)=0
and the map is split. Then we get a preimage of

by composing
X\to Y
with the splitting. Thus the map is surjective and so

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,F)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}}(X,F).

Lastly, since when we restrict the localization to
\Omega^{2}\mathcal{E}
we get a dense fully faithful functor, this means that it is an equivalence between
\Omega^{2}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. The formula

\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,F)\cong\Hom_{\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}}(X,F)

says exactly that the inverse is right adjoint to the localization. Right adjoints preserve limits, so they are in particular left exact.
∎

With this lemma we now have the necessary tools to construct our desired bijection.

Theorem 3.3

(The exact Morita–Tachikawa correspondence)
.

There is a bijection between

1.

equivalence classes of Morita–Tachikawa categories,

2.

equivalence classes of pairs consisting of an exact category and a contravariantly finite generating-cogenerating subcategory,

where equivalence means equivalence of exact categories, and in the latter case we require the equivalence to restrict to an equivalence between the subcategories.

\mathcal{E}
with projectives

to the image of
\mathcal{P}
in the localization
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
, and mapping an exact category

with subcategory

to

.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Morita–Tachikawa category, and let

be the image of the projectives under the localization

. Then because

has enough projectives and projectives are closed under retractions we see that
\mathcal{M}
is generating and closed under retractions. By

every object in

is the image of a second syzygy. Since second syzygies embeds into projectives
\mathcal{M}
is cogenerating. Lastly, since

3.2
gives us an equivalence, we have that an
\mathcal{M}
-approximation corresponds to a
\mathcal{P}
-approximation of a second syzygy. Since
\mathcal{P}
is contravariantly finite, so is
\mathcal{M}
.

For the other direction we see that
\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})
has enough projectives, simply by how it is defined.

That

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-,M_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-,M_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where

In the previous section we defined

Lemma 4.1

\mathcal{M}
is
1
-precluster tilting if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is presicely the
F_{\mathcal{M}}
-injectives and
(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda,F_{\mathcal{M}})
has enough injectives. The
F_{\mathcal{M}}
-injectives are given by
\add(\tau\mathcal{M}\cup\add D\Lambda)

[
AS93b
, Prop 1.9]
, so if
\mathcal{M}
is
1
-precluster tilting, then
\tau\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and
\tau^{-}\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. For the converse, if
\tau\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and
\tau^{-}\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, then
\mathcal{M}
is equal to
\add(\tau\mathcal{M}\cup\add D\Lambda)
, which is the class of
F_{\mathcal{M}}
-injectives. By
[
AS93b
, Prop 1.8, Prop 1.12(b)]
, since
\mathcal{M}
is covariantly finite
(\operatorname{mod}\Lambda,F_{\mathcal{M}})
has enough injectives.
∎

Because of the correspondence we have set up, this gives us the analogues statement for
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein algebras for free. We state it here as a corollary.

Corollary 5.5.1
.

If
\Gamma
is an Artin algebra, then
\Gamma
is
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein if and only if
\operatorname{mod}\Gamma
is an
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein category.

Proof.

We saw in
Proposition

5.3
that
\Gamma
has dominant dimension 2 if and only if
\operatorname{mod}\Gamma
is Morita–Tachikawa, so we may assume this. Then
Proposition

5.4
and
Theorem

3.11
gives us that
\operatorname{mod}\Gamma
is
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein if and only if
\add DI
is
n
-precluster tilting in
\operatorname{mod}\End(I)
, where
I
is an additive generator for the subcatgeory of projective-injective
\Gamma
-modules. At the same time,
[
IS18
, Thm 4.5]
tells us that
DI
is
n
-precluster tilting if and only if
\Gamma
is
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein. Thus the two conditions are equivalent.
∎

6
Injectives and projectives

In this section we investigate the projective and the injective objects of
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
for a Morita–Tachikawa category
\mathcal{E}
. The results and proofs are almost identical to those in
[

, Section 4.3–4.4]

We begin by considering injective objects.

Proposition 6.1
.

[
HKvR22
, Lemma 4.11]

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category and let

be a generating-cogenerating contravariantly finite subcategory. Then
(-,I)
is injective in
\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})
if and only if

is injective in
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Assume

is injective, and let

be in
\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})
. Then
F
fits into an exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-,K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-,M^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Applying
(-,(-,I))
and using
Lemma

2.3
we get
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F,(-,I))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M^{\prime},I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K,I)}

Since

is injective.

Conversely, assume

is not injective. Then there exists an inflation

that is not split. Since

is cogenerating this means there exists an inflation

with

that is not split. This gives rise to an inflation

2.3

is not injective.
∎

Next, we investigate the projective objects.

Proposition 6.2
.

[
HKvR22
, Lemma 4.25]

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Morita–Tachikawa category with projective objects

. Then

if and only if
P
is in

.

Proof.

Assume that
P\in\mathcal{P}
is not projective in
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. Then there is some nonsplit deflation
X\twoheadrightarrow P
in
\mathcal{E}/{}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}
. By
Lemma

3.2
this corresponds to an exact sequence

in
\mathcal{E}
with

. Since
P
is projective in
\mathcal{E}
and the map is not split,

must be nonzero. Therefore
P
is not in
{}^{\perp}({}^{\perp}\mathcal{P})
.

Conversely, if
P
is not in

then there is some non-zero map

. Let

. Then

Now that we have an understanding of what the projective and the injective objects are we bring our attention to when we have enough projectives/injectives.

Proposition 6.3
.

[
HKvR22
, Lemma 4.13]

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a generating-cogenerating contravariantly finite subcategory of an exact category

. Then
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}

\domdim\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})\geq 1
.

Proof.

Assume
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
has enough injectives. Then for every object

, which induces an inflation

. By

\domdim\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})\geq 1

Conversely, since

embeds into an object of
\mathcal{M}
. Since
\domdim\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})\geq 1
each object of
\mathcal{M}
embeds into an injective, and therefore so does any object.
∎

6.1
Flavors of
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein categories

In
[
IS18
, Section 6]
Iyama and Solberg classify
n
-precluster tilting modules into 4 distinct classes, and describe the corresponding classes of
n
-minimal Auslander–Gorenstein algebras. Specifically they say that for an Artin algebra
\Lambda
, an
n
-precluster tilting module
M
satisfies
\add M=\add\{\mathcal{P}_{n}\wedge\mathcal{I}_{n},N\}
where
\mathcal{P}_{n}\wedge\mathcal{I}_{n}
is the unique minimal
n
-precluster tilting module, and
N
some module satisfying
\tau_{n}(N)\cong N
. Then one of four distinct cases can occur:

(A)

\Lambda
is selfinjective and
N=0
.

(B)

\Lambda
is selfinjective and
N\neq 0
.

(C)

\Lambda
is not selfinjective and
N=0
.

(D)

\Lambda
is not selfinjective and
N\neq 0
.

Flavor A has an obvious generalization to the exact case. Namely, that for a Frobenius catgeory, the subcatgeory of projectives is the unique minimal
n
-precluster tilting subcategory for any
n
. In this case
\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})
is just equivalent to the original category.

For flavor B, there are a few possibilites for the ‘correct’ generalization. The most obvious is perhaps Frobenius categories that are not of flavor A, but we propose a slightly more general defintion. Namely, that
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{M})
is of flavor
B
if it is not of flavor A and the injective and projective modules of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
coincide. From
Proposition

6.1
and
Proposition

6.2
we see that this is equivalent to
\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})
having
{}^{\perp}({}^{\perp}\mathcal{P})\cap\mathcal{P}
as projective-injective objects without being Frobenius.

For flavor C and D it is not clear to the author what the correct generalization should be. We do not suspect that a unique minimal
n
-precluster tilting subcategory should exist in general, and it is not clear what it would say about
\operatorname{mod}_{\adm}(\mathcal{M})
if it did. We frame this as an open problem.

Question
.

If
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a category that admits an
n
-precluster tilting subcategory. Does
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
admit a minimal
n
-prelcuster tilting subcatgeory, and under what conditions is this subcategory unique?

In

Iya11
]
, Iyama gives several exmaples of cluster tilting modules in
T^{\perp_{\infty}}
for a tilting module
T
, which they call relative cluster tilting. In a private conversation Sondre Kvamme suggested an idea of how to use this to construct ‘relative’ precluster tilting. The idea is that if we start with a relative cluster tilting module and take the tensor with a selfinjective algebra, we should get a precluster tilting module in the
\Ext
-complement of a tilting module. We give a proof of this below.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional algebra over a field
k
, and let
T
be a tilting module over
\Lambda
. Let
M
be
n
-precluster tilting in
T^{\perp_{\infty}}
for some
n>1
, and let
\Gamma
be a selfinjective algebra over
k
. Then
M\otimes\Gamma
is
n
-precluster tilting in
(T\otimes\Gamma)^{\perp_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be any
\Lambda
-module and let
Y
be any
\Lambda\otimes\Gamma
-module. Then we have

X\otimes\Gamma=X\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\Lambda}(\Lambda\otimes\Gamma).

If we use this together with the Hom-Tensor adjunction, it gives us

\Hom_{\Lambda\otimes\Gamma}(X\otimes\Gamma,Y)=\Hom_{\Lambda}(X,Y).

Using duality, and the fact that
\Gamma\cong D\Gamma
we also get

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Lambda\otimes\Gamma}(Y,X\otimes\Gamma)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\Lambda\otimes\Gamma}(DX\otimes D\Gamma,DY)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\Lambda}(DX,DY)

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\Lambda}(Y,X).

With these two facts one can show that
T\otimes\Gamma
is a tilting module, and that
M\otimes\Gamma
is
n
-rigid.

Now, since whenever
P_{M}^{\bullet}
is a projective resolution of
M
, we have that
P_{M}^{\bullet}\otimes\Gamma
is a projective resolution of
M\otimes\Gamma
, we get

\Ext_{\Lambda\otimes\Gamma}^{i}(M\otimes\Gamma,Y)=\Ext_{\Lambda}^{i}(M,Y)\quad\text{and}\quad\Ext_{\Lambda\otimes\Gamma}^{i}(Y,M\otimes\Gamma)=\Ext_{\Lambda}^{i}(Y,M).

Thus
{}^{\perp_{n-1}}M=M^{\perp_{n-1}}
also in
(T\otimes\Gamma)^{\perp_{\infty}}
, and we conclude that
M\otimes\Gamma
is
n
-precluster tilting.
∎

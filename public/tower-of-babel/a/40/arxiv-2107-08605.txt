The Singular Evolutoids Set and the Extended Evolutoids Front
Michał Zwierzyński

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the notion of the singular evolutoid set which is the set of all singular points of all evolutoids of a fixed smooth planar curve with at most cusp singularities. By the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem for Coherent Tangent Bundles over Surfaces with Boundary (Theorem 2.20 in [ 4 ] ) applied to the extended front of evolutoids of a hedgehog we obtain an integral equality for smooth periodic curves.

For a planar regular curve
\mathcal{C}

\alpha

at some

[

]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
smooth planar curve
, that is the image of the

f_{\mathcal{C}}(s_{0})
is singular. The singular point

f(s_{0})
(in the source and in the target) to the curve
t\mapsto(t^{2},t^{3})
at
t=0
. It’s well known that a singular point
f(s_{0})
is a cusp if and only if the vectors
f^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})
,
f^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})
are linearly independent (e.g. see Theorem B.9.1 in
[
21
]
). A smooth curve is
closed
if it is a
C^{\infty}
map from
S^{1}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. A regular closed curve is
locally convex
if its curvature does not vanish. A locally convex curve with rotation number equal to

is called an
m

(see Figure
1

is a
1
-rosette. A regular point

\mathcal{C}
if its curvature changes sign at

and is called an

\mathcal{C}
if its curvature vanishes at
s_{0}
but does not change sign at
s_{0}

Now, let’s recall the notion of a hedgehog and a support function. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an oval of class
C^{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The curve
\mathcal{C}
can be viewed as the envelope of the family of its support lines given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle x\cos\theta+y\sin\theta=p(\theta),

where the
support function

p
is the signed distance of the support line to
\mathcal{C}
with exterior normal vector
(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
from the fixed origin. Furthermore, for any
p
at least class
C^{2}
the envelope (even if degenerate) of family (
2.2
) exists and can be considered. Such curves are a special case of
hedgehogs
– curves that can be parameterized using their Gauss maps. Note that rosettes are non-singular hedgehogs. On the general theory of hedgehogs please refer to
[
12
,
13
]
and the literature therein.

The parameterization of a hedgehog in so-called polar tangential coordinates
(\theta,p(\theta))
is

(2.3)

\displaystyle\theta\mapsto\big(p(\theta)\cos\theta-p^{\prime}(\theta)\sin\theta,p(\theta)\sin\theta+p^{\prime}(\theta)\cos\theta\big).

The usefulness of the support function, among other things, is that the basic geometric quantities of an oval can be calculated only using its support functions:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{p(\theta)+p^{\prime\prime}(\theta)},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=p(\theta)+p^{\prime\prime}(\theta),

(2.6)

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}p(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

(2.7)

\displaystyle A_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big(p^{2}(\theta)-p^{\prime 2}(\theta)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

where
L_{\mathcal{C}}
and
A_{\mathcal{C}}
denote the length and the area of
\mathcal{C}
, respectively. The formulas (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) are known as the Cauchy and the Blaschke formulas, respectively. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a parameterized smooth curve. Its
oriented area
(or
algebraic area
) is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{C}}:=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}-y\,\mathrm{d}x+x\,\mathrm{d}y=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
\omega_{\mathcal{C}}(P)
is the winding index of
\mathcal{C}
around the point
P
. The oriented area of a hedgehog
\mathcal{C}
with the rotation number
k
(
k

(2.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\mathcal{C}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2k\pi}\big(p^{2}(\theta)-p^{\prime 2}(\theta)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Oriented areas of many curves, such that the evolute, the Wigner caustic, the constant width measure set, the secant caustic, isoptic curves, and the evolutoids made it possible to prove many isoperimetric inequalities and equalities associated with hedgehogs (e.g. see
[

By direct observations one can get that the function

(2.9)

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}(\theta):=p(\theta-\alpha)\cos\alpha+p^{\prime}(\theta-\alpha)\sin\alpha

is a support function of the

3.
The Singular Evolutoids Set

The evolute of
\mathcal{C}
can be defined as the set of all singular points of offsets (or parallel curves) of
\mathcal{C}
. Similarly, an
\alpha
-evolutoid of
\mathcal{C}
can be viewed as a set of all singular points of a suitable family of ‘skew parallel curves’ (
[
6
]
). In some similar way, we define the singular evolutoids set.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smooth curve with at most cusp singularities. Then the
singular evolutoids set of
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})
, is the set of all singular points of a family of
\alpha
-evolutoids of
\mathcal{C}
for
\alpha\in[0,\pi]
.

In Figure
3(d)
we illustrate an oval and a
2
-rosette together with their singular evolutoids sets and families of evolutoids. See also Figure
2(d)
for the singular evolutoids set of a singular curve.

By Proposition
2.2
(ii) an
\alpha
-evolutoid of
\mathcal{C}
is singular at
f_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
if and only if
\rho^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)=-\cot\alpha
. Therefore, by (
2.1
) we get the following parameterization of the singular evolutoids set of
\mathcal{C}
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}(s):

\displaystyle=\gamma_{-\mathrm{arccot}(-\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(s))}(s)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{C}}(s)+\dfrac{-\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\rho^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\mathbbm{t}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)+\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\mathbbm{n}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)}{1+\rho^{\prime 2}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)}.

Similarly, like for evolutoid sets, whenever we write about singular points of the singular evolutoids set we will understand a singular point of the parameterization (
3.1
). The following theorem describes geometric properties of the singular evolutoids set.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smooth curve with at most cusp singularities and with the arc length parameterization (except for singular points)
s\mapsto f_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
which is not locally a straight line. Let
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}
be defined as in (
3.1
). Then

(i)

the singular evolutoids set of
\mathcal{C}
can be given by a smooth parameterization.

(ii)

if
f_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
is not a cusp, then the singular evolutoids set of
\mathcal{C}
is singular at
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}(s)
if and only if
\rho^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)=0
and the singular point is a cusp if and only if
\rho^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\neq 0
.

(iii)

if
f_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
is a cusp of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})
has a cusp at
f_{\mathcal{C}}(s)
.

(iv)

the singular evolutoids set of
\mathcal{C}
has an inflexion point or an undulation point at
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}(s)
if and only if
1+\rho^{\prime 2}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)+2\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(s)\rho^{\prime\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}(s)=0,
and the inflexion point is non-degenerate if and only if
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(s)+\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime 3}(s)-\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(s)\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\neq 0
.

(v)

the subset of smooth closed curves
f
with at most cusp singularities for which the singular evolutoids set has at most cusp singularities, no undulation points, and at most non-degenerate inflexion points is dense in
C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
with Whitney
C^{\infty}
topology.

Proof.

(i)

It follows directly from the parameterization (
3.1
). The parameterization is still correct even if
\mathcal{C}
has an inflexion or undulation points. It can be seen using a Taylor expansion of
f_{\mathcal{C}}
.

(ii)

If
f_{\mathcal{C}}(p)
is not a cusp, it’s easy to verify that
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime}(s)=0
if and only if
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime}(s)=0
. Furthermore, the singular point
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}(s)
is a cusp if and only if the vectors
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime\prime}(s)
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)
are linearly independent, which is equivalent to
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\neq 0
.

(iii)

If
f_{\mathcal{C}}(p)
is a cusp, then without loss of generality we can assume that
p=0
and
f_{\mathcal{C}}
is
t\mapsto(t^{2},t^{3})
. Then the singular evolutoids set has the following parameterization:

(3.2)

\displaystyle t\mapsto\left(t^{2}\cdot\dfrac{-8-54t^{2}+243t^{4}}{8+162t^{2}+648t^{4}},t^{3}\cdot\dfrac{4-27t^{2}+81t^{4}}{4+81t^{2}+324t^{2}}\right),

which is also a cusp at

(see Figure

(iv)

It is enough to check that what the equations

\det\left(\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime}(s),\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime\prime}(s)\right)=0\text{ and }\det\left(\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime}(s),\gamma_{\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\right)=0

are equivalent to the stated conditions.

(v)

Let
f_{\mathcal{C}}:S^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be smooth with at most cusp singularities. The property

(3.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime}(s)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\rho_{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\neq 0

is equivalent to
\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})
having at most cusp singularities.
Property (

) can be viewed as the transversality of the map

\displaystyle\big\{j^{3}g(s)\in J^{3}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\,\big|\,\rho_{g}^{\prime\prime}(s)=0\}.

\mathrm{SES}(\mathcal{C})
is also a generic property.

Extended Evolutoids Front

The
extended euclidean space
is the space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{e}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with coordinate
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
(called the

) on the first factor and a projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{3}_{e}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
on the second factor denoted by
\pi(\alpha,\mathbbm{x})=\mathbbm{x}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a smoothly parameterized curve with at most cusp singularities. Then the
extended evolutoids front
of

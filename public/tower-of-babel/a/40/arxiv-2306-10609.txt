Realizations of the Extended Snyder Model
Tea MARTINIĆ BILAĆ a and Stjepan MELJANAC b

Abstract
We present the exact realization of the extended Snyder model. Using similarity transformations, we construct realizations of the original Snyder and the extended Snyder models. Finally, we present the exact new realization of the κ \kappa -deformed extended Snyder model.

The Snyder model is defined as a Lie algebra generated by noncommutative coordinates
\hat{x}_{\mu}
and Lorentz generators
M_{\mu\nu}
,
(M_{\mu\nu}=-M_{\nu\mu})
, satisfying the commutation relations

\displaystyle\big[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}\big]={\rm i}\beta^{2}M_{\mu\nu},\qquad\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3,\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

\displaystyle\big[M_{\mu\nu},\hat{x}_{\lambda}\big]=-{\rm i}\big(\hat{x}_{\mu}\eta_{\nu\lambda}-\hat{x}_{\nu}\eta_{\mu\lambda}\big),

(1.2)

\displaystyle[M_{\mu\nu},M_{\rho\sigma}]={\rm i}\big(\eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma}-\eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho}-\eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma}+\eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho}\big),

(1.3)

where
\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
is the Minkowski metric.

Our goal is to construct realizations of the Snyder algebra (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) in terms of the Heisenberg algebra generated by
coordinates
x_{\mu}
and momenta
p_{\mu}
satisfying the commutation relations

\displaystyle[x_{\mu},{x_{\nu}}]=[p_{\mu},p_{\nu}]=0,\qquad[x_{\mu},p_{\nu}]={\rm i}\eta_{\mu\nu}.

In Section
2
, we start with the original Snyder realization with
M_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu}
and use similarity transformations to construct a family of realizations of Snyder model. In Section
3
, we apply this method to construct realizations of the extended Snyder model in which the Lorentz generators are realized by
M_{\mu\nu}=\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}+x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu}
, where
\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}
are additional tensorial generators. In Section
4
, we present the exact new realization of the
\kappa
-deformed extended Snyder model. Finally, in Section
5
, we give the discussion and conclusion.

2
Realizations of the Snyder model

The original Snyder realization in terms of
x_{\mu}
and
p_{\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu},

(2.1)

\displaystyle M_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu},

(2.2)

where
x\cdot p=x_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}
.
1
1

1

We denote
x_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\eta_{\alpha\beta}x_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}
, and generally summation over pair of repeated indices is assumed.

Further realizations of the Snyder model can be obtained by similarity transformations by the operator
S={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}
, where

\displaystyle G=F_{0}(u)+(x\cdot p)F(u),\qquad u=\beta^{2}p^{2},\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle F_{0}(0)=0,\qquad F(0)=0,\qquad p^{2}=p_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}.

(2.3)

Note that for
\beta^{2}=0
we have
G=0
and
S=\text{id}

and
G
is Lorentz invariant and linear in the coordinates
x_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Using similarity transformation defined by
S={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}
, where
G
is given by (
2.3
), we obtain the corresponding realizations of Snyder model

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=S\big(x_{\mu}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}\big)S^{-1}=x_{\mu}\varphi_{1}(u)+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}\varphi_{2}(u)+\beta^{2}p_{\mu}\varphi_{3}(u),

where

\varphi_{2}(u)=\frac{1+2\dot{\varphi}_{1}(u)\varphi_{1}(u)}{\varphi_{1}(u)-2u\dot{\varphi}_{1}(u)},\qquad\dot{\varphi}_{1}=\frac{{\rm d}\varphi_{1}(u)}{{\rm d}u}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u=\beta^{2}p^{2}.

(2.4)

In order to prove the above theorem, first we prove the following propositions. Note that if
F_{0}(u)=0
, then
\varphi_{3}(u)=0
, hence, for simplicity in what follows we assume that
F_{0}(u)=0
and
G=(x\cdot p)F(u)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
x_{\mu}^{\prime}=Sx_{\mu}S^{-1}
, where
S={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}
and
G=(x\cdot p)F(u)
. Then

x_{\mu}^{\prime}=x_{\mu}g_{1}(u)+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}g_{2}(u),

(2.5)

where

g_{1}(u)=\big({\rm e}^{F(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u})}\big)(1).

(2.6)

Proof.

By defining the iterated commutator

({\rm ad}_{G})^{n}(x_{\mu})=\underset{n\hskip 16.38895pt\text{times}}{\underbrace{[G,\ldots,[G,[G}},x_{\mu}]]\ldots],\qquad({\rm ad}_{G})^{0}(x_{\mu})=x_{\mu},

and using the Hadamard formula, we have

x_{\mu}^{\prime}=Sx_{\mu}S^{-1}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}x_{\mu}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}G}=x_{\mu}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})^{n}(x_{\mu})}{n!}.

(2.7)

We prove relation (
2.5
) by induction on
n
. Using the Leibniz rule for adjoint representation and

[F,x_{\mu}]=-{\rm i}\frac{\partial F}{\partial p_{\mu}}=-{\rm i}2\beta^{2}p_{\mu}\dot{F},\qquad F\equiv F(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dot{F}=\frac{{\rm d}F}{{\rm d}u},

(2.8)

it is easy to see that
for
n=1
we have

({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})(x_{\mu})={\rm i}[(x\cdot p)F,x_{\mu}]=x_{\mu}g_{11}(u)+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}g_{21}(u),

where
g_{11}(u)=F
and
g_{21}(u)=2\dot{F}
. In following, we denote
g_{ij}\equiv g_{ij}(u)
.
Assume that the relation

({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})^{n}(x_{\mu})=x_{\mu}g_{1n}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}g_{2n}

(2.9)

holds for some
n>1
. Then by the induction assumption, we have

\displaystyle({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})^{n+1}(x_{\mu})={\rm i}\big[(x\cdot p)F,x_{\mu}g_{1n}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}g_{2n}\big]=x_{\mu}g_{1(n+1)}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}g_{2(n+1)},

where, using the Leibniz rule and (
2.8
), we obtain

g_{1(n+1)}=Fg_{1n}-2u\dot{g}_{1n}F

(2.10)

and

g_{2(n+1)}=2\dot{F}g_{1n}+2u\dot{F}g_{2n}-g_{2n}F-2u\dot{g}_{2n}F.

Let us denote

g_{1}(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{g_{1n}}{n!},\qquad\text{where}\qquad g_{10}=1,

and

g_{2}(u)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{g_{2n}}{n!}.

Then, substituting (
2.9
) into (
2.7
), it follows that (
2.5
) holds.
Now, we expand

\big({\rm e}^{F(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u})}\big)(1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(F\big(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\big)\big)^{n}(1)}{n!}

and prove by induction on
n
that

g_{1n}=\left(F\left(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)^{n}(1).

(2.11)

Note that for
n=0
, we have
g_{10}={\rm id}(1)=1
and
for
n=1
it is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\left(F\left(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)(1)=F=g_{11}.

Suppose that relation (
2.11
) is true for some
n>1
. By the induction assumption and from (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\left(F\left(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)^{n+1}(1)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(F\left(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)\circ\left(F\left(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)^{n}\right)(1)

\displaystyle=\left(F\left(1-2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)(g_{1n})=Fg_{1n}-2u\dot{g}_{1n}F=g_{1(n+1)},

which proves our claim (
2.11

2.6
) holds.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p_{\mu}^{\prime}=Sp_{\mu}S^{-1}
, where
S={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}
and
G=(x\cdot p)F(u)
. Then

p_{\mu}^{\prime}=p_{\mu}g_{3}(u),

(2.12)

where

g_{3}(u)=\big({\rm e}^{-F(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u})}\big)(1).

(2.13)

Proof.

Analogous to the proof of the previous proposition, first by using the Hadamard formula, we find

p_{\mu}^{\prime}=Sp_{\mu}S^{-1}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}p_{\mu}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}G}=p_{\mu}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})^{n}(p_{\mu})}{n!}.

Then, by induction on
n
, we prove that

({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})^{n}(p_{\mu})=p_{\mu}g_{3n}.

(2.14)

After short computation, for
n=1
we have

{\rm i}[(x\cdot p)F,p_{\mu}]={\rm i}[(x\cdot p),p_{\mu}]F=p_{\mu}g_{31},

where
g_{31}=-F
.
Assume that relation (
2.14
) holds for some
n>1
.
Then by the induction assumption, we find

({\rm ad}_{{\rm i}G})^{n+1}(p_{\mu})={\rm i}[(x\cdot p)F,p_{\mu}g_{3n}]=p_{\mu}g_{3(n+1)},

where

g_{3(n+1)}=-Fg_{3n}-2u\dot{g}_{3n}F,

(2.15)

which proves claim (
2.14
). Finally, if we denote

g_{3}(u)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{g_{3n}}{n!},\qquad\text{where}\qquad g_{30}=1,

then (
2.12
) holds.
Also, we prove by induction on
n
that

g_{3n}=\left(-F\left(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)^{n}(1).

(2.16)

Note that for
n=0
, we have
g_{30}={\rm id}(1)=1
, and
for
n=1
, we get

\left(-F\left(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)(1)=-F=g_{31}.

Suppose that relation (
2.16
) holds for some
n>1
. Then by the induction assumption and from (

\displaystyle\left(-F\left(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)^{n+1}(1)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(-F\left(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)\circ\left(-F\left(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)^{n}\right)(1)

\displaystyle=\left(-F\left(1+2u\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}u}\right)\right)\left(g_{3n}\right)

\displaystyle=-Fg_{3n}-2u\dot{g}_{3n}F=g_{3(n+1)}.

Therefore, (
2.16

3
Realizations of the extended Snyder model

Different realizations of the Snyder algebra can be obtained introducing additional tensorial generators
\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}=-\hat{x}_{\nu\mu}
. This alternative approach was suggested in
[
12
]
and it was studied perturbatively from a different point of view in
[
26
,
31
,
32
]
based on the results in
[
23
]
. The additional generators
\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}
are assumed to satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle\big[\hat{x}_{\mu\nu},\hat{x}_{\rho\sigma}\big]={\rm i}\big(\eta_{\mu\rho}\hat{x}_{\nu\sigma}-\eta_{\mu\sigma}\hat{x}_{\nu\rho}-\eta_{\nu\rho}\hat{x}_{\mu\sigma}+\eta_{\nu\sigma}\hat{x}_{\mu\rho}\big),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\big[\hat{x}_{\mu\nu},x_{\lambda}\big]=0,\qquad\big[\hat{x}_{\mu\nu},p_{\lambda}\big]=0.

(3.2)

In this case, we consider realizations of the Lorentz generators of the form

\displaystyle M_{\mu\nu}=\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}+x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu},\qquad M_{\mu\nu}\rhd 1=\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}\rhd 1=x_{\mu\nu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{\mu}\rhd 1=0,

where
x_{\mu\nu}
are commuting variables.

Theorem 3.1
.

Extension of the Snyder realization (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) with additional generators
\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}-\beta^{2}\hat{x}_{\mu\alpha}p_{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+u}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle M_{\mu\nu}=\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}+x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu}.

(3.4)

Proof.

In order to prove that we can construct the realization of the Snyder model by (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we show that (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) satisfy Snyder algebra (
1.1
)–(
1.3
). A short computation using (
2.8
) yields

\displaystyle\left[x_{\mu},\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+u}}\right]=\frac{-{\rm i}\beta^{2}p_{\mu}}{\sqrt{1+u}\big(1+\sqrt{1+u}\big)^{2}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+u}},(x\cdot p)\right]=\frac{{\rm i}u}{\sqrt{1+u}\big(1+\sqrt{1+u}\big)^{2}},

(3.6)

and

\left[M_{\mu\nu},\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+u}}\right]=0.

(3.7)

Now, from (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) and (
3.5
)–(
3.6
) using bilinearity of the Lie bracket, we obtain

\displaystyle\big[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}\big]

\displaystyle=\left[x_{\mu}+\beta^{2}(x\!\cdot\!p)p_{\mu}-\beta^{2}\hat{x}_{\mu\alpha}p_{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\!\sqrt{1+u}},x_{\nu}+\beta^{2}(x\!\cdot\!p)p_{\nu}-\beta^{2}\hat{x}_{\nu\rho}p_{\rho}\frac{1}{1+\!\sqrt{1+u}}\right]

\displaystyle={\rm i}\beta^{2}(x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu})+{\rm i}2\beta^{2}\frac{\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}}{1+\sqrt{1+u}}+{\rm i}\beta^{2}u\frac{\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}}{(1+\sqrt{1+u})^{2}}

\displaystyle={\rm i}\beta^{2}\big(x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu}+\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm i}\beta^{2}M_{\mu\nu}.

Similarly, by using (
3.7
), we check that
(
3.3
) and (
3.4
) satisfy (
1.2
)–(
1.3
), therefore
(
3.3
) and (
3.4
) is a realization of the extended Snyder model.
∎

In order to obtain a family of realizations of the extended Snyder model, we use similarity transformations from Section
2
, by
S={\rm e}^{{\rm i}G}
where
G=(x\cdot p)F(u)
. First, note that

\displaystyle S\left(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+u}}\right)S^{-1}

\displaystyle=S\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{m}u^{m-1}\right)S^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{m}\big(\beta^{2}p^{\prime 2}\big)^{m-1}=\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}p^{\prime 2}}}

(3.8)

and

S\big(\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}\big)S^{-1}=\hat{x}_{\mu\nu}.

(3.9)

Now (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) implies that

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=S\left(x_{\mu}+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}-\beta^{2}\hat{x}_{\mu\alpha}p_{\alpha}\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+u}}\right)S^{-1}

\displaystyle=x_{\mu}^{\prime}+\beta^{2}(x^{\prime}\cdot p^{\prime})p_{\mu}^{\prime}-\beta^{2}\hat{x}_{\mu\alpha}p_{\alpha}^{\prime}\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{1+\beta^{2}p^{\prime 2}}}.

Finally, by using results given in Section
2
, (
2.19
) and (
2.22
), we obtain a family of realizations of the extended Snyder model

\hat{x}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}\varphi_{1}(u)+\beta^{2}(x\cdot p)p_{\mu}\varphi_{2}(u)-\beta^{2}\hat{x}_{\mu\alpha}p_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\varphi_{1}(u)+\sqrt{\varphi_{1}^{2}(u)+u}},

(3.10)

where
\varphi_{1}(u)
and
\varphi_{2}(u)
satisfy (
2.4
).

Note that realizations (
3.3
), (
3.4
), (
3.10
) and (
2.4
) are the exact results written in closed form.

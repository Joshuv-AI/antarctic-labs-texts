M-dissipative boundary conditions and boundary tuples for Maxwell operators


Abstract
For Maxwell operators ( 𝐄 , 𝐇 ) → ( i ϵ − 1 ∇ × 𝐇 , − i μ − 1 ∇ × 𝐄 ) (\mathbf{E},\mathbf{H})\to(\mathrm{i}\bbespilon^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{H},-\mathrm{i}\bbmu^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{E}) in Lipschitz domains, we describe all m-dissipative boundary conditions and apply this result to generalized impedance and Leontovich boundary conditions including the cases of singular, degenerate, and randomized impedance coefficients. To this end we construct Riesz bases in the trace spaces associated with the 𝐜𝐮𝐫𝐥 \curlm -operator and introduce a modified version of boundary triple adapted to the specifics of Maxwell equations, namely, to the mixed-order duality of the related trace spaces. This provides a translation of the problem to operator-theoretic settings of abstract Maxwell operators. In particular, we show that Calkin reduction operators are naturally connected with Leontovich boundary conditions and provide an abstract version of impedance boundary condition applicable to other types of wave equations. Taking Friedrichs and Krein-von Neumann extensions of related boundary operators, it is possible to associate m-dissipative Maxwell operators to arbitrary non-negative measurable impedance coefficients.

Introduction

The evolution of the electromagnetic field in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is governed by Maxwell’s equations

\quad\bbespilon e_{t}=\nabla\times h,\qquad\bbmu h_{t}=-\nabla\times e,

where the electric field
e=e(t,\mathbf{x})
and the magnetic field
h=h(t,\mathbf{x})
are

\mathbb{C}^{3}
-valued functions. The
3\times 3
-matrix-functions
\bbmu=\bbmu(\mathbf{x})
and
\bbespilon=\bbespilon(\mathbf{x})
are the magnetic permeability and electric permittivity, respectively, which we suppose to be time-independent.
Assuming that
e(t,\mathbf{x})=e^{-i\kappa t}\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x})
,
h(t,\mathbf{x})=e^{-i\kappa t}\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{x})
, the vector fields
\mathbf{E}
and
\mathbf{H}
satisfy the time-harmonic Maxwell equations

\kappa\bbespilon\mathbf{E}=\mathrm{i}\nabla\times\mathbf{H},\quad\kappa\bbmu\mathbf{H}=-\mathrm{i}\nabla\times\mathbf{E}.

These equations can be understood as an eigenvalue problem for the Maxwell operator
\{\mathbf{E},\mathbf{H}\}\to\{\mathrm{i}\bbespilon^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{H},-\mathrm{i}\bbmu^{-1}\nabla\times\mathbf{E}\}
.

The primary goal of this work is to determine all boundary conditions imposed on the boundary
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
which make this operator
m
-dissipative. The second goals is to minimize the assumptions on the regularity and topology of
\partial\Omega
and on the regularity of specific coefficients in boundary conditions and in Maxwell’s equations. We apply our results to various classes of impedance, Leontovich, and mixed boundary conditions.

In particular, the only our assumption on
\Omega
is that it is a Lipschitz domain, i.e.,
\Omega
is an nonempty open connected bounded set with a Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega

[
39
]
(in particular,

\partial\Omega
may consist of finitely many connected components, which are not necessarily simply-connected). The constitutive tensor-fields
\bbmu(\mathbf{x})
and
\bbespilon(\mathbf{x})
are represented by arbitrary uniformly positive definite and essentially bounded
3\times 3
-matrix-functions.

To achieve the above aims, we modify various tools of the operator extension theory and introduce abstract versions of Maxwell operators. With the use of the theory of differential operators on Lipschitz manifolds and corresponding Hodge decompositions, the abstract results written in terms of generalized boundary tuples are translated then to the results on concrete boundary conditions.

M-dissipative boundary conditions naturally appear in the theory of linear evolution equations because they ensure well-posedness and uniform stability. More precisely, an operator
T
in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
is m-dissipative exactly when
(-\mathrm{i})T
is a generator of strongly continuous semigroup of contractions
[
40
]
. This provides an elegant semigroup approach to
dynamic Maxwell equations and to modeling of leaky optical cavities. Note that we use the Physics conventions
[
25
]
that put the numerical range of a dissipative operator into the closed lower complex half-plane
\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
, and that in the PDE theory a related result is well-known in a more general context of Banach spaces under the name of the Lumer-Phillips theorem.

While a general description m-dissipative boundary conditions for Maxwell systems has not been available even in the case of smooth
\partial\Omega
, some classes of selfadjoint and m-dissipative boundary conditions for the time-harmonic Maxwell equations are well known, for example, the condition
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{E}=0
which, roughly speaking, models the boundary as a perfect conductor. Here
\mathbf{n}
is the exterior normal vector field of
\Omega
along
\partial\Omega
. This boundary condition leads to a self-adjoint operator
[
9
,
39
,
5
]
.
(Rigorously,
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{E}=0
have to be supplemented by
\mathbf{n}\cdot(\bbmu\mathbf{H})=0
to obtain the perfect conductor condition).

Another physical relevant example is the
Leontovich boundary condition

[
35
,
29
,
47
]

\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{E}=z(\mathbf{x})\pi_{\top}(\mathbf{H})
where the
impedance coefficient

z
is a complex valued function on
\partial\Omega
with non-negative real part and
\pi_{\top}(\mathbf{H})=\mathbf{n}\times(\mathbf{H}\times\mathbf{n})
is the tangential component of the magnetic field
\mathbf{H}
on
\partial\Omega
. Kapitonov
[
29
]
showed how to assign naturally an m-dissipative Maxwell operator to this condition as long as
\partial\Omega
is smooth and
z\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. On the other hand Lagnese and Leugering
[
34
, Lemma 7.2.2.1]
using the density result of Belgacem et al.
[
8
]
proved that as long as
z\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R})
is strictly positive on
\partial\Omega
, the condition
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{E}=z(\mathbf{x})\pi_{\top}(\mathbf{H})
produces an m-dissipative operator (formally,
\partial\Omega
in
[
34
]
is piecewise smooth, but actually the proof can be easily adapted to Lipschitz domains).
The case of constant impedance
z\equiv\alpha>0
is sometimes referred to as the transparent or absorbing boundary condition since it is an approximation of the Silver-Müller radiation condition.

Of particular interest are
mixed boundary conditions
that in the context of this paper always understood as the mix of the conservative condition
\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{E}=0
on a part
[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{p}}
of the boundary with an absorbing or Leontovich boundary condition on the rest
[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{a}}=\partial\Omega\setminus[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{p}}

(in other papers, the same name is often used also for other types of mixed conditions).

If Kapitonov’s regularity assumptions
[
29
]
are not fulfilled, the question of m-dissipativity of mixed boundary conditions and well-posedness of associated Maxwell systems become substantially more difficult
[
39
,
5
]
. Certain results are obtained under the assumptions that the impedance coefficient on
[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{a}}
is uniformly strictly positive and bounded and, additionally,
\Omega
and
[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{a}}
are ‘good enough’ (in particular, the boundary of
[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{a}}
is piecewise smooth) and have no certain undesirable interplays with possible “pathologies” of
\partial\Omega
(see the discussion in the recent monograph
[
5
, Section 5.1.2]
).
As a particular applications of our general results we show how to approach the m-dissipativity for arbitrary measurable subsets
[\partial\Omega]_{\mathrm{a}}
of the Lipschitz manifold
\partial\Omega
and arbitrary measurable nonnegative
z(\cdot)
, see Section
9
.

Our interest is motivated by contemporary studies in radiophysics, photonics, and optical engineering that involve singular structures (like fractal antennas
[
36
]
) or modeling of leaky optical cavities
[
39
,
37
]
, which is needed, in particular, for the rigorous formulation for Maxwell systems of nonselfadjoint eigenvalue optimization problems
[
15
,
30
,
3
,
37
,
23
]
.
In photonics context, leaky cavities are
surrounded by a medium with an uncertain structure, e.g., due to uncertain coupling or random fabrication errors
[
37
]
. The latter rises a natural question of an accurate randomization of dissipative boundary conditions (see Section
9
).

Additional conditions of type
\mathbf{n}\cdot(\bbmu\mathbf{H})=0
are closely connected with the ‘divergence-free’ conditions
\Div(\bbespilon(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}))=0
,
\Div(\bbmu(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{x}))=0
, and corresponding Weyl decompositions, which in the m-dissipative case are treated briefly by Remark
2.2
(cf. the selfadjoint case in
[
9
]
). However, the condition
\mathbf{n}\cdot(\bbmu\mathbf{H})=0
on
\partial\Omega
(or on a part of
\partial\Omega
) remains outside the scope of the paper.

With the ‘divergence-free’ conditions the Maxwell system
becomes elliptic, which makes it possible, in the case where
\partial\Omega
and the coefficients are smooth enough, to apply the theory of boundary value problems for elliptic equations
[
22
]
. However, photonics and optical engineering applications, as well as numerical methods, often involve polyhedral domains and discontinuous coefficients, which makes the use of the elliptic theory difficult.

2.1
Main results on m-dissipative Maxwell operators

A closeable operator

Definition 2.2

The formulation of this result uses the following objects and notions.

2.2
Boundary tuples and operator-theoretic tools of the paper

The PDE nature of the problem of finding of all m-dissipative boundary conditions for Maxwell operators can be separated from its operator theoretic features in several steps.

In the 1st step (see Sections
3.2
and
4
), we put the duality of the trace spaces
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\curl_{\partial\Omega})
and
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\Div_{\partial\Omega})
w.r.t. the pivot space
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
into the abstract framework of
[
42
, Appendix to IX.4, Example 3]
, where a generalization of the notion of rigged Hilbert space is considered from the interpolation point of view.

We call this generalized type of duality
mixed-order norm duality
(in short,
m-order duality
) to distinguish it from the standard duality of rigged Hilbert spaces.
A standard rigged Hilbert space with the continuous imbeddings
\mathfrak{H}_{+}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{H}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{-}
involves the Hilbert spaces
\mathfrak{H}_{\pm}
, which are called the spaces with positive- and negative-order norms, see e.g.
[
21
]
.
It is clear from the Hodge decompositions (
4.2
)-(
4.4
) obtained in
[
13
,
10
,
11
]
that
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\curl_{\partial\Omega})
,
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
, and
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\Div_{\partial\Omega})
are not ordered by such imbeddings, since, roughly speaking, one part of each of the trace spaces has a norm of positive order, while the other part a norm of negative order. That is why
we denote a pair of abstract Hilbert spaces connected by a mixed-order norm duality by
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
and
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
and call them mixed-order spaces (in short,
m-order spaces
, see Section
3.2
). By

\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mp,\pm}:=\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}\cap\mathfrak{H}
, their intersections with the pivot space
\mathfrak{H}
are denoted.

We define the
\mathfrak{H}
-pairing as the
duality pairing
\langle\cdot|\star\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}
of the spaces
\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}
that is constructed, roughly speaking, as an extension of the inner product
(\cdot|\star)_{\mathfrak{H}}
of
\mathfrak{H}
(see Proposition
3.2
).
We systematically use
\mathfrak{H}
-pairing adjoint operators
T^{\#}
, see (
3.9
) and Theorems
2.1
–
2.2
.

In Section
3.2
, m-order spaces and their duality is considered from the extrapolation point of view. This process produces various auxiliary operators, which are used through the rest of the paper as technical tools.
The most important of these auxiliary operators are the homeomorphisms
\mathcal{U}_{\pi}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\pi}^{\#}
of Theorem
2.2
and their abstract versions
U_{\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}\to\mathfrak{H}}
and
U_{\mathfrak{H}\to\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}}
, see Proposition
3.2
and Corollary
4.4
.

In Section
4
, we construct Riesz bases in the trace spaces
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\curl_{\partial\Omega})
and
\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\Div_{\partial\Omega})
and introduce in these spaces associated Hilbertian norms
\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}
, which are equivalent to the norms
|\cdot|_{\pi}
and
|\cdot|_{\gamma}
of Section
2.1
, but make the homeomorphisms
\mathcal{U}_{\pi}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\pi}^{\#}
unitary operators and make the Hodge decompositions orthogonal.

In the 2nd step, the integration by parts for the sesquilinear form

(\mathcal{M}^{*}\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\bbespilon,\bbmu}}

associated with the Maxwell operator
\mathcal{M}^{*}

is placed into abstract settings. An abstract approach to the description of boundary value conditions for elliptic partial differential operators (PDOs) traces its origin to works of Calkin, M. Krein, Birman, Vishik, and Grubb (see the monographs
[
21
,
22
,
41
,
18
,
6
]
). It is one of the points of the present paper that Calkin’s reduction operators (see
[
12
,
27
]
and Section
3.1
) are especially well suited for writing of an abstract version for Leontovich-type operators, see Section
7.2
and Corollary
5.4
. (Note that we unite a Calkin reduction operator
G
, its target space
\mathfrak{H}
, and an associated rotation
W
into a triple
(G,\mathfrak{H},W)
and call it a
Calkin triple
.)

For a general description of boundary conditions for PDOs and, in particular, for Maxwell operators, the use of Calkin’s reduction operators based on natural trace maps is difficult because they usually are not surjective
onto their target spaces
[
12
,
27
]
. A general description of m-dissipative/selfadjoint boundary conditions require other operator-theoretic notions involving surjective boundary maps.
A powerful and well-developed abstract tool of such type is the notion of boundary triple (or boundary value space), which was introduced by
Talyush, Kochubei, and Bruk
for an operator
\mathcal{A}^{*}
adjoint to a densely defined symmetric operator
\mathcal{A}
with equal deficiency indices
[
32
,
21
]
(see also the review
[
17
]
and the monographs
[
41
,
18
,
6
]
).
Namely,
(\mathfrak{H},\widehat{\Gamma}_{0},\widehat{\Gamma}_{1})
is called a
boundary triple for

\mathcal{A}^{*}
if an auxiliary Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
and
the maps
\widehat{\Gamma}_{j}:\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathfrak{H}
,
j=0,1
, are such that

\text{$\widehat{\Gamma}:f\mapsto\{\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}f,\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}f\}$ is a surjective linear operator from $\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}$ onto $\mathfrak{H}\oplus\mathfrak{H}$}\\
\text{and $(\mathcal{A}^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f|\mathcal{A}^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{X}}=(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}f|\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}g)_{\mathfrak{H}}-(\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}f|\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}g)_{\mathfrak{H}}$ for all $f,g\in\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}$. }

(2.11)

As soon as a boundary triple is constructed for a differential operator, this leads to a description of all selfadjoint/m-dissipative extensions via the result of Kochubei
[
32
]
.
The substantial difficulty of this abstract approach is that boundary triples are often not well adjusted to natural trace maps of the PDO. We refer to
[
22
,
1
]
and references therein for the review on general linear boundary value problems for even-order PDOs in domains with smooth boundaries
\partial\Omega
and the use of the techniques of differential and pseudo-differential boundary operators. Substantial efforts aimed on the connection of the PDE and operator-theoretic approaches led to a number of modifications of the notion of boundary triple,
see
[
17
, Definition 6.1 and Proposition 6.3]
,
[
4
]
,
[
16
, Sections 7.4 and 7.6]
,
[
41
, Sections 1.2.9 and 3.4]
,
[
7
, Section 2]
, and references therein. Up to our understanding, these generalizations of boundary triples are not well suited for the particular case of Maxwell operators.

We introduce one more modification of the notion of boundary triple, which uses mixed-order duality and is natural from the point of view of the integration by parts for
(\mathcal{M}^{*}\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{v})_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\bbespilon,\bbmu}}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a closed densely defined symmetric operator in a certain Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
.
Let
\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}
be m-order Hilbert spaces dual to each other w.r.t. a pivot Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
in the sense of Section
3.2
.
We shall say that
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
is a
mixed-order boundary tuple
(in short,
m-boundary tuple
) for the operator
\mathcal{A}^{*}

if the following conditions hold:

the map

\Gamma:f\mapsto\{\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}f\}
is a surjective linear operator from
\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}
onto
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
;

(\mathcal{A}^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f|\mathcal{A}^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{X}}=\langle\Gamma_{1}f|\Gamma_{0}g\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}-\langle\Gamma_{0}f|\Gamma_{1}g\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}
for all
f,g\in\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Remark 2.3
.

A more complete notation for m-boundary tuples is
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H}_{+,-},G,W)

because
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
also participates in the definition.
However, we skip
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
since its m-order duality to
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
w.r.t.
\mathfrak{H}
defines it essentially uniquely (uniquely up to the identification of sequences
(u_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{+,-}
fundamental w.r.t.
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}}
, see Section
3.2
).

Remark 2.4
.

A notion of m-boundary tuple is generalization a of the notion of boundary triple. Indeed,
with every boundary triple
(\mathfrak{H},\widehat{\Gamma}_{0},\widehat{\Gamma}_{1})
, one can associate a ´trivial´ m-boundary tuple
(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H},\widehat{\Gamma}_{0},\widehat{\Gamma}_{1})
employing the trivial duality

(\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm},\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}})=(\mathfrak{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{H}})
. On the other hand,
every m-boundary tuple can be regularized to produce a boundary triple, see Proposition
6.2
. These regularizations are equivalent to a choice of a particular pair of biorthogonal Riesz bases in
\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}
. Roughly speaking, m-boundary tuple can be seen as a ‘coordinate-free’ replacement of regularized boundary triples.

We show (see Theorem
8.1
) that

(\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\curl_{\partial\Omega}),\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega),\pi_{\top}^{\mathfrak{h}},\ \mathrm{i}\gamma_{\top}^{\mathfrak{e}})
with
\pi_{\top}^{\mathfrak{h}}\{\mathbf{E},\mathbf{H}\}:=\pi_{\top}\mathbf{H}
and
\gamma_{\top}^{\mathfrak{e}}\{\mathbf{E},\mathbf{H}\}:=\gamma_{\top}\mathbf{E}
is an m-boundary tuple for the Maxwell operator
\mathcal{M}^{*}
.
This m-boundary tuple occurs to be especially convenient for the study of the generalized impedance boundary conditions of
[
5
, Section 1.6.1]
. In particular, Corollary
2.3
follows from Theorem
8.1
.

It occurs that Leontovich-type boundary conditions (
2.4
) are written in terms of two mutually dual Calkin’s reduction operators for the operator
\curlm
, namely, in terms of
\pi_{\top,2}
and
\gamma_{\top,2}
. For the study of associated Leontovich-Maxwell operators, we introduce in Section
6
an abstract ‘
S
-weighted’ Maxwell operators

M\psi:=S^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{i}A\\
-\mathrm{i}A&0\end{pmatrix}
,
where
A
is an abstract densely defined symmetric operator, and introduce in Section
5
the notion of a reduction tuple for
A^{*}
.
A reduction tuple is, roughly speaking, a surjective completion of Calkin’s triple. We construct in Proposition
6.1

an m-boundary tuple for an abstract Maxwell operator
M^{*}
using two mutually dual
reduction tuples for
A^{*}
. In Section
7.2
, this lead to to an abstract version of Leontovich-type boundary conditions and to complete characterizations of the corresponding m-dissipative and essentially m-dissipative cases in Theorems
2.4
and
7.5
.

With minor modifications our description of m-dissipative boundary conditions for abstract Maxwell operators (Theorem
6.7
) is applicable to other types of wave equations. In this connection, let us note that abstract Maxwell operator
M
and its adjoint
M^{*}
are closely related to abstract Dirac-type operators considered in
[
19
]
.

3
Trace spaces for
\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)
and Calkin’s triples

In this section we collect and adapt to our needs the facts concerning trace spaces
for the space
\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)
and the notions connected with Calkin’s reduction operator.
The Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
is a 2-D closed surface with bi-Lipschitz ‘differentiable-type’ structure
[
5
]
.
We use for
s>0
the standard
Hilbertian Sobolev spaces
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)=W^{s,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{3})
and
\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)=W^{s,2}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{3})
, where
\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is the closure in
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\Omega)
of the subspace
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{3})
of smooth compactly supported in
\Omega
vector-fields. The space of generalized vector-fields
\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\Omega)
is dual to
\mathbb{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
w.r.t. the pivot space
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)=L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{3})
.
The analogous spaces of scalar
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions are denoted for
s>0
by
H^{s}(\Omega)=W^{s,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
and
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)=W^{s,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
.

3.1
Integration by parts for
\curlm
and Calkin’s reduction operator

The continuous imbedding
\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)
is dense (for this and the other basic facts listed below we refer to
[
13
,
10
,
39
,
38
,
5
]
).
The (vector) trace
\gamma(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{u}\!\upharpoonright_{\partial\Omega}
, the tangential trace
\gamma_{\top}(\mathbf{u})=-\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\!\upharpoonright_{\partial\Omega}
, and the tangential component trace
\pi_{\top}(\mathbf{u})=-\mathbf{n}\times(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}\!\upharpoonright_{\partial\Omega})
first defined for
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C}^{3})
-fields on the closure
\overline{\Omega}
of
\Omega
have unique extensions as continuous operators

\gamma:\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega),\quad\gamma_{\top}:\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)\to\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega),\quad\pi_{\top}:\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)\to\mathbb{H}^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega),

where we use the complex Hilbertian Sobolev spaces
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\partial\Omega)=W^{s,2}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{C}^{3})
,
s\in(0,1]
, of vector-fields, and their dual spaces of generalized vector-fields
\mathbb{H}^{-s}(\partial\Omega)
. The duality is taken w.r.t.
the pivot space
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
.
Analogously,
H^{s}(\partial\Omega)=W^{s,2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
,
s\in(0,1]
, are Sobolev spaces of scalar functions on
\partial\Omega
, and
H^{-s}(\partial\Omega)=W^{-s,2}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{C})
are their dual spaces w.r.t.
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
.
The scalar trace

\gamma_{0}(f)=f\!\upharpoonright_{\partial\Omega}
is a continuous operator from

H^{1}(\Omega)
to
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
.

For the boundary spaces
H^{s}(\partial\Omega)
and
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\partial\Omega)
with
s>0
and so for their duals, there exist many mutually equivalent Hilbertian norms generated by various Lipschitz local coordinates.
Through the paper, we assume that a certain choice of this family of norms is fixed in a consistent way.

Consider the bounded selfadjoint operator
\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\mapsto\mathrm{i}\,\mathbf{n}(\cdot)\times\mathbf{u}(\cdot)
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. The square of this operator is the orthogonal projection
P_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}}
onto

\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
.
The closed subspace
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)\subset\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is invariant for the operator
\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\mapsto\mathbf{n}(\cdot)\times\mathbf{u}(\cdot)
. We denote the restriction of this operator to
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
by
\mathbf{n}_{\times}
.
So the operator
\mathbf{n}_{\times}:\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)\to\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
defined by
\mathbf{n}_{\times}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{u}
is a unitary in the Hilbert space
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
and
(\mathbf{n}_{\times})^{*}=-\mathbf{n}_{\times}
.

Following
[
10
,
38
]
(with minor adjustments to the notation of
[
39
,
5
]
), let us consider alternative descriptions of the
trace spaces

\pi_{\top}\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega):=\{\pi_{\top}(\mathbf{u})\ :\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)\}
and
\gamma_{\top}\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega):=\{\gamma_{\top}(\mathbf{u})\ :\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{H}(\curl,\Omega)\}
.

The space
H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega):=\gamma_{0}H^{2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}:=\inf\{\|g\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\ :\ f=\gamma_{0}(g),\ g\in H^{2}(\Omega)\},

and the space
H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)
is the dual of
H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)
w.r.t.
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
.
Equipping the sets

\mathbb{V}_{\gamma}:=\gamma_{\top}\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{V}_{\pi}:=\pi_{\top}\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)

with the norms

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathbb{V}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{V}_{\pi})}:=\inf\{\|\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)}\ :\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega),\gamma_{\top}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{v}\text{ (resp., $\pi_{\top}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{v}$) }\},

one obtains Hilbert spaces. Since
\mathbb{H}^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is dense in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and
\mathbf{n}_{\times}\mathbb{V}_{\pi}=\mathbb{V}_{\gamma}
, the spaces
\mathbb{V}_{\gamma}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\pi}
are densely and continuously embedded in
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
.
Let
\mathbb{V}^{\prime}_{\pi(\gamma)}
be the dual Hilbert space to
\mathbb{V}_{\pi(\gamma)}
w.r.t.
\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}(\partial\Omega)
. Note that the spaces
\mathbb{V}_{\pi}
and
\mathbb{V}_{\gamma}
are generally different
[
10
]
(but in the particular case of smooth
\partial\Omega
they both can be naturally identified to the space of tangential vector fields of order 1/2).

Remark 3.1
.

The (sesquilinear) duality pairings
{}_{\mathfrak{H}_{\pm}}\langle\cdot|\star\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}_{\mp}}
of the rigged Hilbert space

\langle\cdot|\star\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}

with

\langle\cdot|\star\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
,
the pairing between

and

\langle\cdot|\star\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{t}}}

.

The

\grad_{\partial\Omega}
and the operator

of

The restriction
\grad_{\partial\Omega}\upharpoonright_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}

5

Let
A
be a symmetric closed densely defined operator in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
, and let

\mathfrak{X}^{2}=\mathfrak{X}\oplus\mathfrak{X}
. We consider the graph
\Gr A^{*}=\{\{f,A^{*}f\}\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}:f\in\dom A^{*}\}
of the adjoint operator
A^{*}
and the domain
\dom A^{*}
of
A^{*}
as Hilbert spaces equipped with the graph norm and use systematically the identification of the normed spaces
(\Gr A,\|\cdot\|_{\Gr A})
and
(\dom A,\|\cdot\|_{\dom A})
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}
be m-order spaces dual to each other w.r.t.
\mathfrak{H}
and
let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{\mp,\pm}:=\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}\cap\mathfrak{H}

(see Section
3.2
).
We say that
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},G^{\prime},W)
is a
reduction tuple for

A^{*}
if
the following conditions hold:

G^{\prime}:\Gr A^{*}\to\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
is a surjective operator,

W
is a linear homeomorphism from
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
onto
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
,

(A^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f,A^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{X}}=\langle G^{\prime}\{f,A^{*}f\}|WG^{\prime}\{g,A^{*}g\}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}
for all
f,g\in\dom A^{*}
.

Remark 5.1
.

Similar to Remark
3.2
, we will use the shortened notations
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},G,W)
with
G
instead of
G^{\prime}
,
where the operator
Gf:=G^{\prime}\{f,A^{*}f\}
acts from from
\dom A^{*}
to
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
. So

\displaystyle\text{(R3) takes the shorter form }(A^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f,A^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{X}}=\langle Gf|WGg\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}},\quad f,g\in\dom A^{*}.

(5.1)

Definition 5.2
.

A reduction tuple
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},G,W)
will be called unitary if it satisfies the following conditions:

W:\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}\to\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
is a unitary operator,

W
maps
\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{-,+}
onto
\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{+,-}
and
\|Wh\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=\|h\|_{\mathfrak{H}}
for all
h\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{-,+}
.

Remark 5.2
.

For every operator adjoint to a certain symmetric operator, there exists a unitary reduction tuple. A trivial example can be obtained from the example of the surjective Calkin reduction operator
G^{\prime}
in
[
12
, Theorem 2.8]
, which obviously produces a unitary reduction tuple
(\mathfrak{H},\mathfrak{H},G^{\prime},W)
.

Definition 5.3
.

We say that a reduction tuple
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},G,W)
and a Calkin triple
(\mathfrak{H},\widetilde{G},\widetilde{W})
for
A^{*}
are associated with each other if

Gf=\widetilde{G}f
for all
f\in\dom\widetilde{G}
,
Wh=\widetilde{W}h
for all
h\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{-,+}
, and
W^{-1}h=\widetilde{W}^{-1}h
for all
h\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{+,-}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},G,W)
be a reduction tuple for
A^{*}
. Then:

The operator
G^{\prime}:\Gr A^{*}\to\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
is closed and bounded.

The tuple
(\mathfrak{H}_{+,-},\mathfrak{H},cWG,\,-W^{-1})
is a reduction tuple for
A^{*}
for every
c\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
.
The reduction tuple
(\mathfrak{H}_{+,-},\mathfrak{H},\mathrm{i}WG,\,-W^{-1})
will be called dual to

.

\Gr A=\ker G^{\prime}
,
\dom A=\ker G
, and
W^{\#}=-W
.

Proof.

(i) can be obtained from (
5.1
) by standard arguments based on the surjectivity of
G^{\prime}
and
W
(cf. the case of boundary triple in
[
18
, Lemma 7.2]
).
(ii)-(iii) The skew-symmetry of the form
[f|g]_{A^{*}}=(A^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f,A^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{X}}
and (

) imply

\langle cWGf|(-W^{-1})cWGg\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=-\langle WGf|Gg\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=\langle Gf|WGg\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}

for

.
This gives

and (

) for the tuple

and

\dom A^{*}\ominus\dom A

\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}

contains

. This implies (C1) for the triple

.

Step 2.
The implication
\Leftarrow
of (C2) takes the form
(A^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f|A^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{X}}=(\widetilde{G}f|\widetilde{W}\widetilde{G}g)_{\mathfrak{H}}
,

, and follows immediately from (R3).

Step 3.
Let us prove the implication
\Rightarrow
of (C2), i.e., we assume
(A^{*}f|g)_{\mathfrak{X}}-(f,g^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{X}}=(\widetilde{G}f|h)_{\mathfrak{H}}
for all

, and have to prove that

,
g^{\prime}=A^{*}g
, and
h=\widetilde{W}\widetilde{G}g
.
For arbitrary

\widetilde{G}f=0

, which implies
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in\Gr A^{*}

for all

\langle h^{\prime}|WGg\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=(h^{\prime}|h)_{\mathfrak{H}}
for all

(see (

and

g\in\dom\widetilde{G}
and

\widetilde{W}\widetilde{G}g=WGg=h

The fact that the tuples

and

∎

6
M-boundary tuples and abstract Maxwell operators

The aim of this section is the description of all m-dissipative extensions of an ´abstract version´ of the Maxwell operator
\mathcal{M}
using m-boundary tuples of Definition
2.3
.
We introduce ´abstract Maxwell operators´ in the following way.
Let
A
be a closed densely defined symmetric operator in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}
. Let
S
be a bounded uniformly positive selfadjoint operator in
\mathfrak{X}^{2}=\mathfrak{X}\oplus\mathfrak{X}
(so
S^{-1}
is also bounded). One can define on
\mathfrak{X}^{2}\times\mathfrak{X}^{2}
another inner product
(\cdot|\star)_{\mathfrak{X}^{2,S}}:=(S\cdot|\star)_{\mathfrak{X}^{2}}
,
which generates an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{X}^{2,S}}
. This defines the ‘weighted’ Hilbert space
\mathfrak{X}^{2,S}:=(\mathfrak{X}^{2},\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{X}^{2,S}})
.
As an
abstract symmetric Maxwell operator
, we consider in
\mathfrak{X}^{2,S}
the ‘
S
-weighted’ operator

\displaystyle M\psi:=S^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{i}A\\
-\mathrm{i}A&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\dom M=(\dom A)^{2},

(6.1)

which is obviously symmetric, closed, and has the adjoint

M^{*}=S^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mathrm{i}A^{*}\\
-\mathrm{i}A^{*}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right).

If
A=\curln
, the operator
M
with a suitable
S
becomes the Maxwell operator

\mathcal{M}
of (
2.1
).

6.1
M-boundary tuples: properties and connections with reduction tuples

Reduction tuples (see Definition

) and m-boundary tuples (see Definition

) are connected by the following statement.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let two closed densely defined symmetric operators,

in

and

Let

.
Let

\Gamma_{0}\psi:=G\psi_{2}

M^{*}
.

Proof.

The verification of conditions (M1)-(M2) of Definition
2.3
is straightforward.
∎

Let
V
be a linear homeomorphism from
\mathfrak{H}
to
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
.
Then it is obvious from the definitions of Section

that

is a linear homeomorphism from

to

\displaystyle(V^{\#}h_{+,-}|V^{-1}h_{-,+})_{\mathfrak{H}}=\langle h_{+,-}|h_{-,+}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\text{ for all }h_{\mp,\pm}\in\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}.

(6.2)

.

Let

∎

In abstract settings, the unitary operator

6.2
Extensions of symmetric operators in terms of m-boundary tuples

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a densely defined symmetric operator in
\mathfrak{X}
with equal deficiency indices.
Let
(\mathfrak{H}_{-,+},\mathfrak{H},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
be a certain m-boundary tuple for
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.
So
\Gamma:f\mapsto\{\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}f\}
is a surjective
bounded operator from

(\dom\mathcal{A}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}})
onto
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
(see Proposition
6.3
).

A
linear relation
from a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{1}
to a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}_{2}
is by definition a linear subspace in
\mathfrak{H}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{2}
, e.g., a graph
\Gr B
of an operator
B:\dom B\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{1}\to\mathfrak{H}_{2}
is a linear relation. Identifying operators with their graphs, one can consider operators as particular cases of linear relations, i.e., a linear relation
\Theta
can be considered as an operator if and only if its
multivalued part

\Theta(0):=\left\{h_{2}\in\mathfrak{H}_{2}\ :\ \{0,h_{2}\}\in\Theta\right\}
is equal to
\{0,0\}
. Then the notions of closed relation and closed operator are consistent (see
[
32
,
17
,
27
,
18
,
6
]
for the basics on linear relations). If
\Theta
is a linear relation from
\mathfrak{H}
to
\mathfrak{H}
,
\Theta
is said to be a linear relation in
\mathfrak{H}
.

Recall that an operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is called an
admissible extension of

\mathcal{A}
if
\Gr\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Gr\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\Gr\mathcal{A}^{*}
. It follows from Definition
2.3
that
\Gr\mathcal{A}=\ker\Gamma
. Consequently, there exists 1–to–1 correspondence between: (i) the family of admissible extensions
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
of
\mathcal{A}
,
(ii) the family of subspaces
\Gr(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}})/\Gr(\mathcal{A})
of the factor space
\Gr(\mathcal{A}^{*})/\Gr(\mathcal{A})
,
(iii) and the family of
linear relations
\Theta=\left\{\{\Gamma_{0}u,\Gamma_{1}u\}\ :\ u\in\Gr\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}\right\}
from
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
to
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
.
Namely, for the correspondence (i)
\leftrightarrow
(iii), the restriction
\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}
of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
associated with a linear relation
\Theta
is defined by

\displaystyle\text{$\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}:=\mathcal{A}^{*}\upharpoonright_{\dom\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}$, \ where $\dom\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}:=\Gamma^{-1}\Theta=\left\{u\in\dom\mathcal{A}^{*}\ :\ \{\Gamma_{0}u,\Gamma_{1}u\}\in\Theta\right\}$}.

(6.3)

Then
\Gr(\mathcal{A}_{\Theta})/\Gr(\mathcal{A})
is the corresponding subspace of the factor space
\Gr(\mathcal{A}^{*})/\Gr(\mathcal{A})
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\Theta
be a linear relation from
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
to
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
.

A numerical cone of
\Theta
is defined by

\Ncone(\Theta):=\{\langle h_{1}|h_{0}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\ :\ \{h_{0},h_{1}\}\in\Theta\}.

A linear relation
\Theta
is said to be symmetric, nonnegative, dissipative, or accretive if, resp.,
\Ncone(\Theta)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
\Ncone(\Theta)\subseteq[0,+\infty)
,
\Ncone(\Theta)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
, or
\Ncone(\Theta)\subseteq\mathrm{i}\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
, where
\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\ :\ \pm\im z\geq 0\}
. Besides, a linear relation
\Theta
in each of these classes is called maximal if it cannot be extended to another linear relation of the same class.

The
\mathfrak{H}
-pairing-adjoint linear relation
\Theta^{\#}
consist of all
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}\oplus\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
such that

\langle f^{\prime}|g\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=\langle f|g^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}

for all
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in\Theta
.

\Theta
is called
\mathfrak{H}
-pairing-selfadjoint if
\Theta=\Theta^{\#}
.

A linear operator
T:\dom T\subseteq\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}\to\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
is called symmetric, nonnegative, dissipative, or accretive if the linear relation
\Gr T
is so.

These are mixed-order duality analogues of standard definitions, which are well-known in the case
\mathfrak{H}_{\mp,\pm}=\mathfrak{H}
. In particular, (iii) is a generalization of the standard definition of the adjoint linear relation
\Theta^{*}
in
\mathfrak{H}
. The names for the above classes of linear relations and operators are often interchanged, cf.
[
31
,
34
,
18
]
. We use the terminology of mathematical physics
[
25
]
which places numerical ranges of dissipative operator to
\overline{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.

Remark 6.1
.

Obviously,

\Gr\mathcal{A}_{\Theta_{1}}\subseteq\Gr\mathcal{A}_{\Theta_{2}}
if and only if
\Theta_{1}\subseteq\Theta_{2}
.
Note that
\Theta^{\#}
is a closed linear relation from
\mathfrak{H}_{-,+}
to
\mathfrak{H}_{+,-}
and that
(\Theta^{\#})^{\#}
is the closure
\overline{\Theta}
of
\Theta
.
The definitions of
\mathfrak{H}
-pairing-adjoint linear relations and operators are obviously consistent in the sense that
\Gr(T^{\#})=(\Gr T)^{\#}
, see Section
3.2
. For the operator (
6.3
),
(\mathcal{A}_{\Theta})^{*}=\mathcal{A}_{\Theta^{\#}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}}=\mathcal{A}_{\overline{\Theta}}
. It is clear that

\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}
is closed if and only if
\Theta
is so.

.

A maximal dissipative linear relation is closed. A graph
\Gr T
of a maximal dissipative operator

(i)
\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}=(\mathcal{A}_{\Theta})^{*}

Indeed, the dissipativity of

implies the following statement:

for

\text{the linear relation $\Xi=\Bigl\{\{(T-I_{\mathfrak{H}})f,\mathrm{i}(T+I_{\mathfrak{H}})f\}\ :\ f\in\dom T\Bigr\}$ is dissipative }\\

-pairing-selfadjoint if and only if

.

The equalities

Let us compare various approached to the study of the mix of the conservative condition

The condition

Below we give several examples, where

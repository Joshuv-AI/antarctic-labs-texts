On internal categories and crossed objects in the category of monoids
Ilia Pirashvili

Abstract
. It is a well-known fact that the category 𝖢𝖺𝗍 ⁡ ( 𝐂 ) \mathsf{Cat}(\bf{C}) of internal categories in a category 𝐂 \bf{C} has a description in terms of crossed modules, when 𝐂 = 𝐆𝐫 \bf{C}=\bf{Gr} is the category of groups. The proof of this result heavily uses the fact that any split epimorphism decomposes as a semi-direct product. An equivalent statement does not hold in the category 𝐌𝐨𝐧 \bf{Mon} of monoids. In a previous work on quadratic algebras, [ 3 ] I constructed an internal category in the category of monoids, see Section 6 . Based on this construction, this paper will introduce the notion of a crossed semi-bimodule and show that it gives rise to an object in 𝖢𝖺𝗍 ⁡ ( 𝐌𝐨𝐧 ) \mathsf{Cat}(\bf{Mon}) . I will also relate this new notion to the crossed semi-modules introduced earlier by A. Patchkoria [ 2 ] .



1.
Introduction

The notion of a crossed module was introduced by Whitehead in his study of
homotopy theory
[
4
]
. A crossed module is a group homomorphism

\partial:K\to A
, together with an action of
A
on the group
K
, satisfying
some conditions. Starting with such data, one can construct the category

\mathsf{Cat}(\partial:K\to A)
. Objects are elements of
A
, while morphisms from
b

to
a
are essentially elements
x
of
K
, such that
b=\partial(x)a
and the
composition is induced by the multiplication in
K
.

One can readily checks that
\mathsf{Cat}(\partial:K\to A)
is an internal category in
the category of groups, meaning that all structural maps are group
homomorphisms. It is well-known that in this way, one obtain an isomorphism of
the category of internal categories in the category of groups, and the category
of crossed modules
[
1
]
.

This paper will introduce the notion of a crossed semi-bimodule, which is pair
of monoids
A,K
, and triple of maps
A\times K\xrightarrow{\circ}A
,
A\times K\xrightarrow{\lambda}K
,
K\times A\xrightarrow{\rho}K
, satisfying certain conditions. We
will show that one can construct a category
\mathsf{Cat}(A,K,\circ)
from such a data,
which is internal in the category of monoids.

We will show that crossed semi-bimodules for which
\lambda
is a trivial
homomorphism, is equivalent to the category of crossed semi-modules introduced
by A. Patchkoria
[
2
]
. We will also show that in the cases when both
monoids
A
and
K
are groups, our notion is essentially equivalent to a
crossed module.

In the last section, we will describe a particular crossed module, which arose
during my work on quadratic étale algebras
[
3
]
. It is an example
which does not comes from crossed semi-modules.

2.2.
Crossed modules and internal categories in
\bf{Gr}

Let
\bf{Gr}
be the category of groups and group homomorphisms. It was known since
the 60’s that the internal categories in
\bf{Gr}
are equivalent to crossed
modules. Crossed modules were introduced by Whitehead
[
4
]
in the study of
homotopy theory.

Definition 2.1
.

A
crossed module
is a group homomorphism
\partial:K\to A
, together
with a right action of the group
A
on the group
K
, denoted by
x^{a}
, where

x\in K
,
a\in A
. It must satisfy the following equalities

(1)

\partial(x^{a})=a^{-1}\partial(x)a
,

(2)

y^{\partial x}=x^{-1}yx

for all
x,y\in K
and
a\in A
.

Denote by
\bf{Xmod}
the category of crossed modules. From this, we can construct
an internal category in
\bf{Gr}
in the following way:

Let
(K,A,\partial)
be a crossed module. Define
C_{0}=A
and
C_{1}
to be

A\times K
as a set. The group structure is defined by

(a,x)(b,y)=(ab,x^{b}\,y).

This construction is compare with
[
1
]
.

The group
C_{2}
is
A\times K\times K
as a set with the following operation

(a,x,y)(b,u,v)=(ab,x^{b}u,y^{b\partial u}v).

Next, we put

s^{0}_{0}(a)=(a,1),\ d_{i}^{1}(a,x)=\begin{cases}a,&{\rm if}\quad i=0\\
a\partial(x),&{\rm if}\quad i=1\end{cases}

s^{1}_{i}(a,x)=\begin{cases}(a,x,1)&{\rm if}\quad i=0\\
(a,1,x)&{\rm if}\quad i=0\end{cases}

d^{2}_{i}(a,x,y)=\begin{cases}(a,x)&{\rm if}\quad i=0\\
(a,xy)&{\rm if}\quad i=1\\
(a\partial(x),y)&{\rm if}\quad i=0\end{cases}

s^{1}_{i}(a,x)=\begin{cases}(a,x,1)&{\rm if}\quad i=0\\
(a,1,x)&{\rm if}\quad i=1.\end{cases}

One checks that all maps are group homomorphisms and in this way, obtains an
internal category in
\sf Gr
. This is denoted by
\mathsf{Cat}(K\xrightarrow{\partial}A)
. It
is well-known that any internal category in
\sf Gr
is of this form, and the
functor

{\bf{Xmod}}\to\mathsf{Cat}(\bf{Gr}),\hskip 11.49994pt(K,A,\partial)\mapsto\mathsf{Cat}(K\xrightarrow{\partial}A)

2.3.
Crossed semi-modules

A. Patchkoria
[
2
]
introduced the notion of a crossed semi-module and
proved that the category of crossed semi-modules is equivalent to the category
of Schreier categorical objects in the category of monoids.

Definition 2.2
.

A crossed semi-module
[
2
]
is a monoid homomorphism
\partial:K\to A
,
together with a right action of the monoid
A
on the monoid
K
, denoted by

x^{a}
,
x\in K
,
a\in A
. It must satisfy the following equalities

i)

a\partial(x^{a})=\partial(x)a
,

ii)

yx^{\partial y}=xy

for all
a\in A
,
x,y\in K
.

By defining morphisms in the analogous way to groups, we can form a category,
which we will denote by
\bf{Xsmod}
.

Starting from a crossed semi-module, one can construct an internal category in

\bf{Mon}
, the category of monoids, in the same exact way as the crossed modules.

3.
Crossed semi-bimodules and internal categories in the category of
monoids

3.1.
Definition and first consequences

The following definitions are probably more appropriate for monoids than crossed
semi-modules.

Definition 3.1
.

A crossed semi-bimodule consists of monoids
A
and
K
, together with maps

A\times K\xrightarrow{\circ}A;\quad(a,x)\mapsto a\circ x,

A\times K\xrightarrow{\lambda}K;\quad(a,x)\mapsto\ ^{a}x,

K\times A\xrightarrow{\rho}K;\quad(x,a)\mapsto x^{a}.

The first map makes
A
into a right
K
-set, the second map makes
K
into a
left
A
-monoid and the third map makes
K
into a right
A
-monoid.
Moreoever, the following conditions need to hold for every
x,y\in K
and

a,b\in A
:

(1)

(^{a}x)^{b}=\ ^{a}(x^{b})
,

(2)

(ab)\circ(^{a}x)=a(b\circ x)
,

(3)

(ab)\circ(x^{b})=(a\circ x)b
,

(4)

(^{a}y)x^{b\circ y}=x^{b}(^{a\circ x}y).

For simplicity, instead of saying that
(K,A,\circ,\lambda,\rho)
is a crossed
semi-bimodule, we will say that
(K,A,\circ)
is a crossed semi-bimodule

Definition 3.2
.

A morphism from a crossed semi-bimodule
(K,A,\circ)
to a crossed
semi-bimodule
(K^{\prime},A^{\prime},\circ)
is given by the homomorphisms
\kappa:K\to K^{\prime}

and
\alpha:A\to A^{\prime}
, for which the following conditions hold

(1)

\alpha(a\circ x)=\alpha(a)\circ\kappa(x)
,

(2)

\kappa(^{a}x)=\ ^{\alpha(a)}\kappa(x)
,

(3)

\kappa(x^{a})=\kappa(x)^{\alpha(a)}
.

Denote the category of crossed semi-bimodules by
\bf{Xbsmod}
. We will need also a
weaker version.

Definition 3.3
.

A weak morphism from a crossed semi-bimodule
(K,A,\circ)
to a crossed
semi-bimodule
(K^{\prime},A^{\prime},\circ)
is given by a monoid homomorphism
\kappa:A\to A^{\prime}
and by the map
\gamma:A\times K\to K^{\prime}
, such that the following
conditions hold

(1)

\gamma(a,xy)=\gamma(a,x)\gamma(a,y)
,

(2)

\kappa(a)\circ\gamma(a,x)=\kappa(a\circ x)
,

(3)

{}^{\kappa(a)}\gamma(b,y)\gamma(a,x)^{\kappa(a)\circ\gamma(b,y)}=\gamma(ab,\,^{a}yx^{b\circ y})
.

Any morphism
(\kappa,\alpha)
gives rise to a weak morphism
(\kappa,\gamma)
,
where
\gamma(a,x)=\alpha(x)
. Weak morphisms can be composed as follows

(\kappa^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})\circ(\kappa,\gamma)=(\kappa^{\prime}\circ\kappa,\gamma^{\prime\prime}),

where
\gamma^{\prime\prime}(a,x)=\gamma^{\prime}(\kappa(a),\gamma(a,x))
. Thus, we obtain the
category
\bf{Xbsmod}_{w}
. Isomorphisms in
\bf{Xbsmod}_{w}
are called weak isomorphisms.

Recall that a
semi-bimodule
over
A
is a commutative monoid
K
,
together with left and right monoid actions of
A
on
K
, satisfying Equality
i) of Definition
3.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The category of semi-bimodule over a monoid

is equivalent to the full
subcategory of crossed semi-bimodules over
A
, for which

a\circ x=a

holds for all
a\in A
and all

The proof is just a straightforward verification.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(K,A,\circ)
be a crossed semi-bimodule. Define

\partial:K\to A

by
\partial(y)=1\circ y
. For any
x,y\in K

Proof.

Substitute
a=1=b
in Identity iv) of the definition of a crossed
semi-bimodule
3.1
to obtain
yx^{1\circ y}=\,x(^{1\circ x}y)

∎

4.
Relationship between crossed semi-modules and crossed semi-bimodules

The aim of this section is to show that crossed semi-modules are exactly the
crossed semi-bimodules for which
{}^{a}x=x
, for every
a\in A
and every
x\in K
.

Proposition 4.1
.

i)

Let
\partial:K\to A
be a crossed semi-module. For elements
a\in M
and
x\in G
, define

{}^{a}x=x,\quad a\circ x=a\partial(x).

This yields obtains a crossed semi-bimodule structure, denoted by

\Phi(K,A,\partial)
.

ii)

Conversely, assume we have a crossed semi-bimodule for which

{}^{a}x=a
, for all
a\in A
and
x\in K
. Define
\partial:K\to A
by

\partial(x)=1\circ x.

We obtain a crossed semi-module.

iii)

The constriction in i) defines a full and faithful functor

\Phi:\bf{Xsmod}\to\bf{Xbsmod}
from the category of crossed semi-modules to the
category of crossed semi-bimodules. Moreover, it induces an isomorphism
of
\bf{Xsmod}
to the full subcategory of crossed semi-bimodules for which

{}^{a}x=x
, for all
a\in A
and
x\in K
.

Proof.

i) Equality (1) of Definition
3.1
is obvious. We have

(ab)\circ(^{a}x)=(ab)\circ(x)=ab\partial(x)=a(b\circ x),

which proves Equality (2) of Definition
3.1
. We also have

(ab)\circ(x^{b})=ab\partial(x^{b})=a\partial(x)b=(a\circ x)b,

which gives us Equality (3). Finally, we have

(^{a}y)x^{b\circ y}=yx^{b\partial(y)}=x^{b}y=x^{b}(^{a\circ x}y),

and part i) follows.

ii) Assume
{}^{a}x=x
. By putting
b=1
in Identity (2) of the definition of a
crossed semi-bimodule Definition
3.1
, we obtain

a\circ x=a\partial(x).

By the definition of
\partial
and setting
a=\partial(x)
, we get

\partial(xy)=1\circ(xy)=(1\circ x)\circ y=\partial(x)\circ y=\partial(x)\partial(y).

Hence,
\partial
is a monoid homomorphism. It remains to show that the
defining relations of crossed semi-modules are satisfied. To this end, we put

m=1
in Identity (3) of Definition
3.1
to obtain

b\partial(x^{b})=b\circ x^{b}=(1\circ x)b=\partial(x)b.

Quite similarly, taking
b=1
in Identity (4) of Definition
3.1
gives us

yx^{\partial(y)}=xy,

and part ii) proved.

iii) By our construction, in any crossed semi-bimodule coming from
\bf{Xsmod}
,
we have
\partial(x)=1\circ x
. This implies that
\Phi
is a full and
faithful functor. The rest follows from part ii).
∎

6.
A construction related to rank two commutative algebras

The notion of crossed semi-bimodules arose during my work on rank two
commutative algebras
[
3
]
. In more detail, let
R
be a commutative ring
and
p,q\in R
, such that

pq+2=0.

We define a crossed semi-bimodule, denoted by
\mathsf{Qu}(R):=(\mathsf{K}(R),\mathsf{A}(R),\circ)
.
Here,
\mathsf{K}(R)
is the multiplicative monoid of
2\times 2
matrices of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\
0&s\end{pmatrix},

where
r,s\in R
. The monoid
\mathsf{A}(R)
, as a set, is simply
R^{2}
. Elements of

\mathsf{A}(R)
are denoted by
[a,b]
. The monoid operation is defined by

[a,b][c,d]=[ac,a^{2}d+bc^{2}+p^{2}bd].

One easily sees that in this way, we obtain a commutative monoid with
[1,0]
as
the unit.

Lemma 6.1
.

For elements

k=\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}\in\mathsf{K}(R)\quad{\rm and}\quad m=[a,b]\in\mathsf{A}(R),

we set

m\circ k=[as-pr,s^{2}b-qrsa-r^{2}].

This defines an action of the monoid
\mathsf{K}(R)
on the set
\mathsf{A}(R)
.

Proof.

Taking
r=0
and
s=1
, we see that
m\circ 1=m
. Next, take
k_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{1}\\
0&s_{1}\end{pmatrix}
.
We have

\displaystyle(m\circ k)\circ k_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[as-pr,s^{2}b-qrsa-r^{2}]\circ\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{1}\\
0&s_{1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(as-pr)s_{1}-pr_{1},s_{1}^{2}(s^{2}b-qrsa-r^{2})-qr_{1}s_{1}(as-pr)-r_{1}^{2}].

On the other, hand we have

\displaystyle m\circ(kk_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a,b]\circ\begin{pmatrix}1&r_{1}+rs_{1}\\
0&ss_{1}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[ass_{1}-p(r_{1}+rs_{1}),s^{2}s_{1}^{2}b-q(r_{1}+rs_{1})ss_{1}a-(r_{1}+rs_{1})^{2}].

Comparing these expression and using the fact that
pq=-2
, we see that

(m\circ k)\circ k_{1}=m\circ(kk_{1}),

implying the lemma.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

For elements

k=\begin{pmatrix}1&r\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}\in\mathsf{K}(R)\quad{\rm and}\quad m=[a,b]\in\mathsf{A}(R)

we set

k^{m}=\begin{pmatrix}1&ra\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}=\ ^{m}k.

In this way, we obtain a crossed semi-bimodule
(\mathsf{K}(R),\mathsf{A}(R),\circ)
.

Proof.

For
n=[c,d]
, we have

\displaystyle(mn)\circ(^{m}k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[ac,a^{2}d+bc^{2}+p^{2}bd]\circ\begin{pmatrix}1&ra\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[acs-pra,s^{2}(a^{2}d+bc^{2}+p^{2}bd)-qarsac-r^{2}a^{2}]

On the other hand

\displaystyle m(n\circ k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[a,b][cs-pr,s^{2}d-qrsc-r^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[acs-par,a^{2}(s^{2}d-qrsc-r^{2})+b(cs-pr)^{2}+p^{2}b(s^{2}d-qrsc-r^{2})].

Since
pq=-2
, we obtain

(mn)\circ(^{m}s)=m(n\circ s).

For

h=\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\
0&v\end{pmatrix}\in K(R),

we have

\displaystyle(^{m}h)k^{n\circ h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&ua\\
0&v\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&crv-pur\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&crv-pur+asu\\
0&sv\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&rc\\
0&s\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&asu-pru\\
0&v\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{n}(^{m\circ k}h)

∎

The internal category in

intimately related to the Galois algebras over the Hopf

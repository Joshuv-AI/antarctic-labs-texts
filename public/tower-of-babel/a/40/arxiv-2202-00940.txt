Ballistic transport in periodic and random media
Anne Boutet de Monvel, Mostafa Sabri

Abstract
. We prove ballistic transport of all orders, that is, ∥ x m ​ e − i ​ t ​ H ​ ψ ∥ ≍ t m \lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert\asymp t^{m} , for the following models: the adjacency matrix on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , the Laplace operator on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , periodic Schrödinger operators on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , and discrete periodic Schrödinger operators on periodic graphs. In all cases we give the exact expression of the limit of ∥ x m ​ e − i ​ t ​ H ​ ψ ∥ / t m \lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert/t^{m} as t → + ∞ t\to+\infty . We then move to universal covers of finite graphs (these are infinite trees) and prove ballistic transport in mean when the potential is lifted naturally, giving a periodic model, and when the tree is endowed with random i.i.d. potential, giving an Anderson model. The limiting distributions are then discussed, enriching the transport theory. Some general upper bounds are detailed in the appendix.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Ballistic motion is a dynamical property found in certain delocalized Schrödinger operators.

Broadly speaking, in considering a Schrödinger operator, say
H=-\Delta+V
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can understand
localization
in some spectral interval
I
in three senses: (a) Spectral localization means that
\sigma(H)\cap I
is pure point. (b) Exponential localization means that moreover the eigenfunctions decay exponentially. (c) Dynamical localization means that states initially localized in a bounded domain will not leave this domain much as time goes on.

The opposite regime of
delocalization
is similarly understood in three senses: (a’) spectrally, one expects absolutely continuous (AC) spectrum, (b’) spatially, the (generalized) eigenfunctions may ideally be equidistributed in some sense, (c’) dynamically, one expects the wave packets to spread on the space as time goes on.

The RAGE theorem establishes some links between
spectral
and
dynamical
(de)localization. Recall that if
\psi_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is some initial state of the system, then
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi_{0}
describes the state of the system at time
t
. Now define the following subspaces of
\mathscr{H}\coloneqq L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
:

\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{p}}\coloneqq\overline{\text{span}\{\text{eigenvectors of }H\}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{c}}\coloneqq\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{p}}^{\bot}.

The RAGE theorem (see
[
29
]
*Section 5.2) asserts that

(1.1)

\displaystyle f\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{p}}

\displaystyle\iff\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ compact s.t.}\ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\,\lVert\chi_{K^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert\leq\varepsilon,

(1.2)

\displaystyle f\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{c}}

\displaystyle\iff\forall K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ compact}\colon\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T}^{T}\lVert\chi_{K}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Hence: (i) A state is in the pure point space
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{p}}
iff at all times, most of its mass lies within a fixed compact set. (ii) A state is in the continuous space
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{c}}
iff its time evolution escapes (in average) from any compact set, after sufficient time has passed.

Exploring further the dynamical aspects of delocalization, a common object of study is the
mean square displacement

r_{f}^{2}(t)\coloneqq\lVert x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert^{2}
. In presence of AC spectrum, i.e., if the absolutely continuous subspace
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{ac}}
is nontrivial, one can deduce from the RAGE theorem
1
1

1

It is known
[
29
]
that for
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{ac}}
, (
1.2
) strengthens to the fact that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\lVert\chi_{K}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert=0
. This implies that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\lVert\chi_{K^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert=\lVert f\rVert
. Taking
K=\Lambda_{r}\coloneqq\{\lvert x\rvert\leq r\}
, we thus get
\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert\geq\liminf_{t\to+\infty}r^{m}\lVert\chi_{\Lambda_{r}^{c}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert=r^{m}\lVert f\rVert
. As
r
is arbitrary, this shows
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert=\infty
.
that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert=\infty
for any
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{ac}}
,
f\neq 0
.

In this article we are interested in the rate of this divergence. It is known that
r_{f}^{2}(t)\lesssim t^{2}
, see Theorem
A.4
for a more general result. The operator exhibits
ballistic transport
if we also have
r_{f}^{2}(t)\gtrsim t^{2}
, at least in a time-averaged sense.

Here we discuss several models that exhibit the exact ballistic rate, that is,
\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}f\rVert\asymp t^{m}
for
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{ac}}
,
f\neq 0
and all
m\geq 1
.

1.2.
Contents

Our aim is to provide a comprehensive outlook of various techniques to prove ballistic motion for different models.

In Section
2
we establish ballistic transport for the adjacency matrix on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(Section
2.1
), followed by the Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(Section
2.2
). This is of course folklore, however we are not aware of references computing the exact limit of
\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}/t^{2m}
as
t\to+\infty
.

Then, in Section
3.1
, we move on to Schrödinger operators with periodic potential on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, extending the result of
[
5
]
to all
m
. After that, in Section
3.2
, we proceed to the less studied discrete periodic Schrödinger operators on periodic graphs. This includes the hexagonal lattice, face-centered and body-centered cubic lattices, and a lot more. For these models we establish ballistic motion, assuming
\psi
has a nontrivial AC component, i.e., its spectral projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{ac}}
is nontrivial.

In Section
4
we move to infinite trees that enjoy some form of periodicity. More specifically, we consider a finite graph
G
endowed with some potential
W
, then consider the universal cover
\mathcal{T}=\widetilde{G}
endowed with the naturally lifted potential in Section
4.2
. We proceed afterwards to replace such lifted potentials by random i.i.d. potentials in Section
4.3
, which gives an Anderson model. For both models (periodic and random) we establish ballistic transport
in mean
, that is, we consider
2\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2\eta t}\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{\beta/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t
instead, with
\eta\downarrow 0
. The periodic result seems new, the random result appeared before in the special case of the
d
-regular tree in
[
1
,
17
]
.

In Section
5
we refine our results for the periodic models of Section
3
by studying the limiting distribution of
X_{t}/t
, when
X_{t}
has distribution
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}
and
\psi
is normalized. Note that if
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
is the distribution of
X_{t}/t
, then ballistic motion is merely the statement that the second moment
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(x^{2})
is
>0
. Here we actually compute the nontrivial limit
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}
of the measures
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
as
t\to+\infty
. This gives a richer understanding of the transport theory; in particular, this gives an expression for the limit
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(f)=\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}}(f)
for any continuous
f
.

Appendix
A
contains complete proofs of the ballistic upper bounds, which is well-known
[
24
]
for
m=1,2
. We also prove some differentiability results for the moments in the Heisenberg picture, i.e., for the maps
t\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
, which are needed in the text.

To conclude this introduction we refer to the beautiful paper
[
22
]
for finer interplay between the spectral measures and quantum dynamics. We also mention the recent papers
[

14
]
which prove ballistic transport for quasiperiodic Schrödinger operators, under certain conditions.

2.
The free Laplacian

2.1.
Discrete case

We consider the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and the space
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
of square summable sequences
\psi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the scalar product
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\overline{\phi(n)}\psi(n)
. On the other hand we consider the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}=[0,2\pi)^{d}
and the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of square integrable functions
f\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the scalar product
\langle f,g\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\overline{f(\theta)}g(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta
. The Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is defined by

(\mathscr{F}f)(n)\equiv\hat{f}(n)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\theta)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta=\langle e_{n},f\rangle,

where
e_{n}(\theta)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\cdot\theta}}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{d}}
. In particular,
\mathscr{F}e_{n}=\delta_{n}
where
\{\delta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is the standard Hilbert basis of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. The inverse Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}^{-1}\colon\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is given by

[\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\psi)](\theta)=\hat{\psi}(\theta)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{d}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(n)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\cdot\theta}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(n)e_{n}(\theta).

In this section we consider the adjacency matrix
\mathcal{A}
acting in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, that is,

(\mathcal{A}\psi)(n)=\sum_{w\sim n}\psi(w)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(\psi(n-\mathfrak{e}_{j})+\psi(n+\mathfrak{e}_{j})\bigr)

where
\{\mathfrak{e}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
w\sim v
means that the vertices
v,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are nearest neighbors. By Fourier transform
\psi(n-\mathfrak{e}_{j})+\psi(n+\mathfrak{e}_{j})
becomes
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}})\hat{\psi}(\theta)
. So
\hat{\mathcal{A}}\coloneqq\mathscr{F}^{-1}\mathcal{A}\mathscr{F}
is the operator of multiplication by
\phi(\mathbf{\theta})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}2\cos\theta_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
is absolutely continuous:
\sigma(\mathcal{A})=\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathcal{A})=[-2d,2d]
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\psi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
n^{m}\psi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
denote the map defined by
n^{m}\psi(n)\coloneqq(n_{1}^{m}\psi(n),\dots,n_{d}^{m}\psi(n))
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
\psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with
\lVert n^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(2.1)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert n^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}=d\binom{2m}{m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}+\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}(-1)^{q+m}\langle\psi,S_{2m-2q}\psi\rangle,

where
(S_{k}\psi)(n)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(\psi(n-k\mathfrak{e}_{j})+\psi(n+k\mathfrak{e}_{j})\bigr)
is a
k
-step adjacency matrix.

Moreover, this limit is
>0
if
\psi\neq 0
and we have the upper bound

(2.2)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert n^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}\leq 4^{m}d\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}.

This somehow suggests that
\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}n^{2m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}}{t^{2m}}
converges weakly to the bounded operator
d\binom{2m}{m}\mathrm{Id}+\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}(-1)^{q+m}S_{2m-2q}
.

Proof.

Denote
\psi_{t}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi
. We first prove that
\lVert n^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
implies
\lVert n^{m}\psi_{t}\rVert<\infty
for any
t\geq 0
. By Fourier transform
\widehat{n^{m}\psi}=D^{m}\hat{\psi}
where
D^{m}\coloneqq(-\mathrm{i})^{m}(\partial_{\theta_{1}}^{m},\dots,\partial_{\theta_{d}}^{m})
. So
\lVert n^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
means
\hat{\psi}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Moreover, since
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is the operator of multiplication by
\phi(\mathbf{\theta})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}2\cos\theta_{j}
it leaves invariant
H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and similarly for
\widehat{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{\mathcal{A}}}
. So,
\hat{\psi}_{t}=\widehat{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}}\hat{\psi}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\widehat{n^{m}\psi_{t}}=D^{m}\hat{\psi}_{t}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
which means that
n^{m}\psi_{t}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

The limit in (
2.1
) is strictly positive if
\psi\neq 0
as a consequence of (
2.6
) below. We then show how formula (
2.1
) implies the upper bound (
2.2
) which is a slight improvement over the general bound (
A.8
) of Appendix
A
. By using
\lVert S_{k}\rVert\leq 2d
to estimate the right-hand side of (
2.1
) we get

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert n^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}\leq\biggl(d\binom{2m}{m}+2d\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}\biggr)\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}=\biggl(d\sum_{q=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{q}\biggr)\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}=(1+1)^{2m}d\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}.

Now we give the proof of formula (
2.1
). Let
\psi_{t}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi
be as above. Since
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is the operator of multiplication by
\phi(\theta)\coloneqq 2\sum_{j=1}^{d}\cos\theta_{j}
, we have

\widehat{n^{m}\psi_{t}}=D^{m}\hat{\psi}_{t}=D^{m}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\hat{\mathcal{A}}}\hat{\psi})=D^{m}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\phi}\hat{\psi}).

In particular,

(2.3)

\lVert n^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}=\lVert D^{m}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\phi}\hat{\psi})\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

By Leibniz,

(2.4)

\partial_{\theta_{j}}^{m}\bigl(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\phi(\theta)}\hat{\psi}(\theta)\bigr)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom{m}{r}\partial_{\theta_{j}}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\phi(\theta)}\partial_{\theta_{j}}^{m-r}\hat{\psi}(\theta).

Clearly the leading term in
t
is for
r=m
and we have

(2.5)

\partial_{\theta_{j}}^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\phi(\theta)}=(-2\mathrm{i}t\sin\theta_{j})^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\phi(\theta)}+\mathrm{O}(t^{m-1})

for some
\mathrm{O}(t^{m-1})
which can be made explicit. Since
\hat{\psi}\in H^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we deduce from (
2.3
)-(
2.5
) that

(2.6)

\lVert n^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert^{2}=4^{m}t^{2m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lVert(\sin\theta_{j})^{m}\hat{\psi}\rVert^{2}+\mathrm{O}(t^{2m-1}).

Now we observe that

(\sin\theta)^{2m}=\frac{(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta})^{2m}}{(2\mathrm{i})^{2m}}=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{4^{m}}\sum_{q=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{q}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}q\theta}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta(2m-q)}(-1)^{2m-q}.

Using
(-1)^{2m-q}=(-1)^{q}
and
\binom{2m}{2m-q}=\binom{2m}{q}
, we thus have

4^{m}(\sin\theta)^{2m}=\binom{2m}{m}+\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}(-1)^{q+m}(2\cos(2m-2q)\theta)\,.

Recall that
S_{k}\colon\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is defined by

(S_{k}\psi)(n)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(\psi(n-k\mathfrak{e}_{j})+\psi(n+k\mathfrak{e}_{j})\bigr).

By Fourier transform
\psi(n-k\mathfrak{e}_{j})+\psi(n+k\mathfrak{e}_{j})
becomes
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k\theta_{j}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}k\theta_{j}})\hat{\psi}
. So
\hat{S}_{k}\coloneqq\mathscr{F}^{-1}S_{k}\mathscr{F}
is the operator of multiplication by the function
\phi_{k}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, with
\phi_{k}(\theta)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}2\cos k\theta_{j}
. It follows that

\displaystyle 4^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lVert(\sin\theta_{j})^{m}\hat{\psi}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle\hat{\psi},4^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\sin\theta_{j})^{2m}\hat{\psi}\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=d\binom{2m}{m}\lVert\hat{\psi}\rVert^{2}+\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}(-1)^{q+m}\langle\hat{\psi},\phi_{2m-2q}\hat{\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=d\binom{2m}{m}\lVert\hat{\psi}\rVert^{2}+\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}(-1)^{q+m}\langle\hat{\psi},\widehat{S_{2m-2q}}\hat{\psi}\rangle

\displaystyle=d\binom{2m}{m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}+\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{2m}{q}(-1)^{q+m}\langle\psi,S_{2m-2q}\psi\rangle.

Recalling (
2.6
), dividing by
t^{2m}
and taking
t\to+\infty
, we obtain the statement.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For
m=1
we get
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert n\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2}}=2d\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}-\langle\psi,S_{2}\psi\rangle
. In particular a bound like
\lVert n\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{A}}\psi\rVert\geq Ct\lVert\psi\rVert
cannot be valid for a positive constant
C
independent of
\psi
. In fact, let
d=1
. Consider
\psi=(\psi_{j})
given by
\psi_{1}=\dots=\psi_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
and
\psi_{j}=0
otherwise. Then
\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}=1
and
\sum_{j}\psi_{j}\overline{\psi_{j+2}}=\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{3}}+\dots+\psi_{n-2}\overline{\psi_{n}}+\psi_{n-1}\overline{\psi_{n+1}}+\psi_{n}\overline{\psi_{n+2}}=\frac{1}{n}+\dots+\frac{1}{n}+0+0=\frac{n-2}{n}
. So in this case the limit is
2(1-\frac{n-2}{n})=\frac{4}{n}
, which can get arbitrarily small while conserving
\lVert\psi\rVert=1
.

2.2.
Continuous case

Consider now the case of the Laplacian
\Delta
on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is equipped with the scalar product
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\phi(x)}\psi(x)\mathrm{d}x
and the Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

\mathscr{F}(\psi)(y)\equiv\hat{\psi}(y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot x}\mathrm{d}x.

For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote
x^{k}\coloneqq(x_{1}^{k},\dots,x_{d}^{k})
and
x^{k}\psi(x)\coloneqq(x_{1}^{k}\psi(x),\dots,x_{d}^{k}\psi(x))
. Let also
D^{k}\coloneqq(-\mathrm{i})^{k}(\partial_{x_{1}}^{k},\dots,\partial_{x_{d}}^{k})
and
\Delta\coloneqq\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\dots+\partial_{x_{d}}^{2}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
\sum_{k=0}^{m}\lVert x^{m-k}D^{k}\psi\rVert^{2}<\infty
. Then

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}=4^{m}\lVert D^{m}\psi\rVert^{2}.

Let
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})
,
m\coloneqq\max\{m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\}
, and
x^{\mathbf{m}}\coloneqq(x_{1}^{m_{1}},\dots,x_{d}^{m_{d}})
. The proof shows more generally that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert x^{\mathbf{m}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}=4^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
m_{j}=m\end{subarray}}\lVert\partial_{x_{j}}^{m}\psi\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

Using the Fourier transform one sees that

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi(x)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot x/4t}}{(4\pi\mathrm{i}t)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot y/4t}\psi(y)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}x\cdot y/2t}\mathrm{d}y,

see, e.g.,
[
29
]
*Section 7.4, (7.43). Denoting
\phi_{t}(y)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot y/4t}\psi(y)
, this can be written as

(2.7)

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi(x)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot x/4t}}{(2\mathrm{i}t)^{d/2}}\,\hat{\phi}_{t}\Bigl(\frac{x}{2t}\Bigr).

Thus,

\displaystyle\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi\rVert

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2t)^{d/2}}\Bigl\lVert x^{m}\hat{\phi}_{t}\Bigl(\frac{\cdot}{2t}\Bigr)\Bigr\rVert=(2t)^{m}\frac{1}{(2t)^{d/2}}\Bigl\lVert\Bigl(\frac{x}{2t}\Bigr)^{m}\hat{\phi}_{t}\Bigl(\frac{\cdot}{2t}\Bigr)\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle=(2t)^{m}\lVert x^{m}\hat{\phi}_{t}\rVert=(2t)^{m}\lVert\widehat{D^{m}\phi_{t}}\rVert=(2t)^{m}\lVert D^{m}\phi_{t}\rVert.

By Leibniz formula,

(2.8)

\partial_{y_{j}}^{m}\phi_{t}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\partial_{y_{j}}^{m-k}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot y/4t}\partial_{y_{j}}^{k}\psi(y)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot y/4t}\partial_{y_{j}}^{m}\psi(y)+\mathrm{O}_{\psi}(t^{-1}).

The error term
\mathrm{O}_{\psi}(t^{-1})
depends on
\psi
and its derivatives. It can be made explicit as follows. The Faà di Bruno formula implies that

\partial_{y_{j}}^{p}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot y/4t}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot y/4t}\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}\frac{p!}{(p-2r)!r!}\frac{\mathrm{i}^{p-r}}{2^{p}t^{p-r}}y_{j}^{p-2r}.

Thus,

P_{1}(2\pi\theta)=\mathrm{Id}
. So Theorem
3.3
asserts that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}=4^{m}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\bigl[(\sin\theta_{1}+\sin(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}))^{2m}+(\sin\theta_{2}+\sin(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}))^{2m}\bigr]\lvert\hat{\psi}(\theta)\rvert^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta.

Example 3.6

(the hexagonal lattice)
.

The hexagonal (honeycomb/graphene) lattice is
3
-regular, see
[
19
]
*Fig. 7. Here
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=a(1,0)
,
\mathfrak{a}_{2}=\frac{a}{2}(1,\sqrt{3})
, where
a
is the distance between a vertex and its second nearest neighbor. We have
V_{f}=\{0,v\}
, where

,
V=\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}+V_{f}
, and

(3.24)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}})&=\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v)+\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v-\mathfrak{a}_{1})+\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v-\mathfrak{a}_{2}),\\
\mathcal{A}\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v)&=\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}})+\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+\mathfrak{a}_{1})+\psi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+\mathfrak{a}_{2}).\end{split}

By (
3.19
), we have
H(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})f(0)=\sum_{u\sim 0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot u}f(\{u\}_{\mathfrak{a}})
. Using (

H(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})f(0)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot v}f(v)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(v-\mathfrak{a}_{1})}f(v)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(v-\mathfrak{a}_{2})}f(v)

H(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})f(v)=\sum_{u\sim v}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(u-v)}f(\{u\}_{\mathfrak{a}})=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot v}f(0)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(\mathfrak{a}_{1}-v)}f(0)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(\mathfrak{a}_{2}-v)}f(0).

H(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot v}\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\\
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot v}\overline{\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}&0\end{pmatrix},\text{ where }\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\coloneqq 1+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{1}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{2}}.

Since

\frac{\nabla_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}}E_{\pm}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}{2\pi}=\frac{\mp(\sin 2\pi\theta_{1}+\sin 2\pi(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))}{|\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})|}\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\frac{\mp(\sin 2\pi\theta_{2}+\sin 2\pi(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}))}{|\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})|}\mathfrak{a}_{2}.

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{a^{2m}[(\sin\theta_{1}+\frac{\sin\theta_{2}+\sin(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})}{2})^{2m}+(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(\sin\theta_{2}+\sin(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})))^{2m}]}{|1+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{1}}+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{2}}|^{2m}}

4.
Universal covers

4.1.
Background

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tree, that is, a connected graph with no cycles. Consider a Schrödinger operator

H=\mathcal{A}+W

on
\mathcal{T}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the adjacency matrix of the tree and
W
a potential on
\mathcal{T}
. We start by recalling some properties of the Green function of
H
.

Given
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{+}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits z>0\}
and
(v,w)
a directed edge in
\mathcal{T}
, let

G^{\gamma}(v,w)\coloneqq\langle\delta_{v},(H-\gamma)^{-1}\delta_{w}\rangle

denote the Green function of
H
and

\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)\coloneqq\frac{G^{\gamma}(v,w)}{G^{\gamma}(v,v)}.

The following identities are then classical (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
*Section 2):

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G^{\gamma}(v,v)}=W(v)+\sum_{u\sim v}\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(u)-\gamma,

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\zeta_{w}^{\gamma}(v)}=W(v)+\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{v}\setminus\{w\}}\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(u)-\gamma,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{w}^{\gamma}(v)}-\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)=\frac{-1}{G^{\gamma}(v,v)},

\displaystyle\zeta_{w}^{\gamma}(v)=\frac{G^{\gamma}(v,v)}{G^{\gamma}(w,w)}\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w),

(4.3)

\displaystyle G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{k})=G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{0})\zeta_{v_{0}}^{\gamma}(v_{1})\cdots\zeta_{v_{k-1}}^{\gamma}(v_{k}),

\displaystyle G^{\gamma}(v,w)=G^{\gamma}(w,v)

for any non-backtracking path
(v_{0};v_{k})
in a tree
\mathcal{T}
.

The quantity
-\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)
can also be expressed as the Green function
G_{\mathcal{T}^{(w|v)}}^{\gamma}(w,w)
of a subtree of
\mathcal{T}
. By the Herglotz property, we thus have
-\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)=|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)|
for any
(v,w)
. In particular, (
4.1
) implies the important relation

(4.4)

\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}_{v}\setminus\{w\}}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(u)|=\frac{|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{w}^{\gamma}(u)|}{|\zeta_{w}^{\gamma}(v)|^{2}}-\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\gamma.

4.2.
Universal covers

Suppose
(G,W)
is any finite graph endowed with a potential
W
and let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{W})=(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{W})
be its universal cover, where the potential
W
is lifted naturally by
\widetilde{W}(v)=W(\pi v)
, if
\pi\colon\mathcal{T}\to G
is the covering projection. For example, if
G
is
d
-regular, then
\mathcal{T}
is the infinite
d
-regular tree. We assume that the minimal degree of
G
is
\geq 2
.

The spectral theory of such trees has a rich history, see
[
4
]
and references therein. It is known in particular that the spectrum consists of bands of absolutely continuous spectrum, possibly with infinitely degenerate eigenvalues between the bands. Moreover, in the interior of the bands, all Green functions limits
G^{\lambda}(v,w)\coloneqq\lim_{\eta\downarrow 0}G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(v,w)
and
\zeta^{\lambda}_{v}(w)\coloneqq\lim_{\eta\downarrow 0}\zeta_{v}^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(w)
exist,
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(v,v)>0
for any
v
, while
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\lambda}(w)
is strictly negative, that is
\lvert\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\lambda}(w)\rvert=-\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\lambda}(w)>0
within the bands.

We henceforth denote

\gamma\coloneqq\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta\quad\text{with}\quad\eta>0.

As in
[
23
]
, we define the spectral quantities

z_{\gamma}\coloneqq\inf_{(v,w)\in B(\mathcal{T})}\lvert\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)\rvert\quad\text{and}\quad z_{\lambda}\coloneqq\inf_{(v,w)\in B(\mathcal{T})}\lvert\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\lambda}(w)\rvert,

where the infimum is over the set
B(\mathcal{T})
of directed edges of
\mathcal{T}
. Actually
\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)=\zeta_{v^{\prime}}^{\gamma}(w^{\prime})
if
\pi(v,w)=\pi(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
, so
z_{\lambda}
is just a minimum over the (lifts of) directed edges of
G
. In particular,
z_{\lambda}>0
.

Back to the topic of ballistic transport, fix an origin
o\in\mathcal{T}
, let
\lvert x\rvert\coloneqq d(x,o)
and
(x\psi)(x)\coloneqq\lvert x\rvert\psi(x)
. In this section we shall consider averaged moments instead:

\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\psi,\eta}\coloneqq 2\eta\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2\eta t}\lVert x^{\beta/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Let denote
G^{z}\coloneqq(H-z)^{-1}
. A well-known application of the Plancherel identity yields that

(4.5)

\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\psi,\eta}=\frac{\eta}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lVert x^{\beta/2}G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\psi\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Our aim is to find lower bounds for
\liminf\limits_{\eta\downarrow 0}\eta^{\beta}\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\psi,\eta}
. Using Theorem
A.1
and the Tauberian theorem
[
28
]
*Theorem 10.3, such a lower bound implies

\liminf_{a\to+\infty}\frac{1}{a^{\beta+1}}\int_{0}^{a}\lVert x^{\beta/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t>0\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert x^{\beta/2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{\beta}}>0.

Theorem 4.1
.

We have

\liminf_{\eta\downarrow 0}\eta^{\beta}\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\delta_{o},\eta}\geq\frac{1}{2^{\beta+1}\pi}\int_{\sigma(H)}z_{\lambda}^{\beta}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(o,o)\mathrm{d}\lambda>0.

Note
.

For
\beta=0
it follows from (
4.5
) that

\eta^{0}\langle x^{0}\rangle_{\psi,\eta}=\frac{\eta}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lVert G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\psi\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\langle\psi,G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\psi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t.

So the presence of
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(o,o)
above is quite natural, while the moment effect is captured by
z_{\lambda}^{\beta}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\eta^{\beta+1}\sum_{v}|v|^{\beta}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\eta^{\beta+1}\sum_{|v|>b\eta^{-1}}|v|^{\beta}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\eta^{\beta+1}b^{\beta}\eta^{-\beta}\sum_{|v|>b\eta^{-1}}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta b^{\beta}\Big[\lVert G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\delta_{o}\rVert^{2}-\sum_{|v|\leq b\eta^{-1}}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\Big]

(4.6)

\displaystyle=b^{\beta}\Big[\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)-\eta\sum_{|v|\leq b\eta^{-1}}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\Big]\,,

where we used the spectral theorem
G^{z}(o,o)=\int_{\sigma(\mathcal{A})}\frac{1}{x-z}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{o,o}(x)
in the last equality.

Note that

\eta\sum_{|v|\leq b\eta^{-1}}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\leq\eta\lVert G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\delta_{o}\rVert^{2}=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o).

This trivial estimate makes the lower bound (
4.2
) useless however, which is natural since it doesn’t use any delocalization of
H
. The argument below can be summarized as showing that
b
can be chosen so that

\eta\sum_{|v|\leq b\eta^{-1}}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o).

Denoting
v_{0}\coloneqq o
, we have

\sum_{|v|\leq M}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(v_{0},v)|^{2}=|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(v_{0},v_{0})|^{2}+\sum_{r=1}^{M}\sum_{v_{1}\sim v_{0}}\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2}

where the last sum is over all nonbacktracking paths
(v_{2};v_{M})
of length
r-2
“outgoing” from the directed edge
(v_{0},v_{1})
, i.e.,
v_{2}\sim v_{1}
and
v_{2}\neq v_{0}
. Now, for
\gamma=\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}|G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|G^{\gamma}(o,o)|^{2}\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}|\zeta_{v_{0}}^{\gamma}(v_{1})\cdots\zeta_{v_{r-1}}^{\gamma}(v_{r})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|G^{\gamma}(o,o)|^{2}\max_{(v,w)}\frac{|\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)|^{2}}{|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)|}\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}|\zeta_{v_{0}}^{\gamma}(v_{1})\cdots\zeta_{v_{r-2}}^{\gamma}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v_{r-1}}^{\gamma}(v_{r})|

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq|G^{\gamma}(o,o)|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(v_{1})|\max_{(v,w)}\frac{1}{|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\frac{1}{\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)}|},

where we applied (
4.4
)
r-1
times in the last step.

By (
4.1
),
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)=(\sum_{u\sim o}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(u)|+\eta)|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)|^{2}
. Thus,

\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2}\leq\frac{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)}{\sum_{u\sim o}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(u)|+\eta}\frac{|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(v_{1})|}{z_{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}}

where we estimated
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\frac{1}{\zeta_{v}^{\gamma}(w)}
using (
4.1
) and the definition of
z_{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}
. Thus,

(4.8)

\sum_{|v|\leq M}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\leq|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)|^{2}+M\frac{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)}{z_{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}}\,.

Take
M=b\eta^{-1}
. It follows from (
4.2
) that

\eta^{\beta+1}\sum_{v}|v|^{\beta}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\geq b^{\beta}\Big[\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)-b\frac{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)}{z_{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}}-\eta|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)|^{2}\Big]\,.

Choose
b=\frac{z_{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}}{2}
. Then

(4.9)

\eta^{\beta+1}\sum_{v}|v|^{\beta}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\geq\frac{z_{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}^{\beta}}{2^{\beta+1}}\Big[\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)-2\eta|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)|^{2}\Big]\,.

As
\eta\downarrow 0
, we get the lower bound
\frac{z_{\lambda}^{\beta}}{2^{\beta+1}}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(o,o)>0
.

This estimate is true for any
\lambda
in the union of intervals
\cup_{j}\mathring{I}_{j}
of AC spectrum, which is a set of positive measure. Using Fatou’s lemma, we thus get

\liminf_{\eta\downarrow 0}\eta^{\beta+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{v}|v|^{\beta}|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\geq\int_{\cup_{j}\mathring{I}_{j}}\frac{z_{\lambda}^{\beta}}{2^{\beta+1}}\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(o,o)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda>0\,.

The claim follows by (
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We may extend the result to functions
\psi
of compact support, but the bound we get is not very good. Namely, if
\psi
is supported in
\Lambda\subset\mathcal{T}
, we obtain

\liminf_{\eta\downarrow 0}\eta^{\beta}\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\psi,\eta}\geq\frac{1}{2^{2\beta+1}|\Lambda|^{\beta}}\int_{\sigma(H)}\frac{z_{\lambda}^{\beta}\,(\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\langle\psi,G^{\lambda}\psi\rangle)^{\beta+1}}{\langle\psi,\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)\psi\rangle^{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

where
[\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)\psi](w)=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda}(w,w)\psi(w)
. In particular, the bound becomes useless as the size of the support becomes infinite, which does not seem natural in view of our previous ballistic estimates. We thus omit the proof and think it a worthwhile question to obtain estimates for general
\psi
, without
\eta
-time averaging, and to find the exact limit.

4.3.
The Anderson model on universal covers

We now study weak random perturbations of universal covering trees. The simplest example is the Anderson model on the
(q+1)
-regular tree, for which ballistic transport was previously established in the time-averaged sense by A. Klein
[
17
]
, and by M. Aizenman and S. Warzel
[
1
]
. Here we study the general case.

Assumptions
.

Compared to the previous subsection, we need to assume the tree
\mathcal{T}=\widetilde{G}
has a bit nicer geometry. If
b\in B(\mathcal{T})
we let
o(b)
and
t(b)
denote the origin and terminus of
b
, respectively.

•

We now assume the minimal degree is
\geq 3
,

•

Since
G
is finite, there are finitely many isomorphism classes of
(\mathcal{T},b)
as
b
runs over the directed edges of
\mathcal{T}
and
(\mathcal{T},b)
is considered as a tree with “root”
b
. If
\mathcal{N}_{b}\coloneqq\{b_{+}:o(b_{+})=t(b)\}
is the set of edges outgoing from
b
, then we assume that for each
b
, there is at least one
b_{+}\in\mathcal{N}_{b}
such that
(\mathcal{T},b)
is isomorphic to
(\mathcal{T},b_{+})
.

The second condition is perhaps better visualized using the language of cone types, see
[
16
]
*condition (M1*) in which it was introduced. Explicit examples of such trees can be found in
[
4
]
. Concerning the random potential, we assume

•

Each vertex
v\in\mathcal{T}
is endowed with a random variable
\mathcal{W}(v)
, the
(\mathcal{W}(v))_{v\in\mathcal{T}}
are i.i.d. with common distribution
\nu
of compact support. We also impose some regularity as in
[
1
]
*assumption A2 for comfort, but perhaps this can be avoided.

It is known
[
16
]
that under these conditions the random Schrödinger operator
H=\mathcal{A}+\epsilon\mathcal{W}
inherits the purely absolutely continuous spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
almost surely if
\epsilon
is small enough – one of the few results of Anderson delocalization. The result of
[
16
]
can also be used to derive some inverse moments bounds on the imaginary parts of the Green functions, see
[
2
]
*Theorem 5.2 for details (in a different model). More precisely, it can be shown that within the stable intervals
I
of pure AC spectrum, we also have

(4.10)

\sup_{\lambda\in I}\sup_{\eta\in(0,1)}\sup_{b\in B(\mathcal{T})}\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o(b)}^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(t_{b})|^{-s})<\infty\,,

for
0\leq s\leq 5
, where
\sup_{b}
runs over the set
B(\mathcal{T})
of directed edges of
\mathcal{T}
and it is in fact a maximum as it can be equivalently taken over the directed edges of
G
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the previous assumptions,

\liminf_{\eta\downarrow 0}\eta^{\beta}\mathbb{E}\bigl(\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)\delta_{o},\eta})>0.

The proof also works for states
f(H)\delta_{o}
instead of
\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)\delta_{o}
, if
f
is piecewise continuous and supported in
I
. This result appeared before in
[
1
,
17
]
in the special case
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{T}_{q}
, the
(q+1)
-regular tree.

Proof.

The main argument is the same as in Theorem
4.1
, but there are two technical difficulties. First, we are dealing with the state
\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)\delta_{o}
instead of
\delta_{o}
. Second, we cannot estimate the expectation of a maximum as in (
4.2
).

Let us begin the proof. Let
f(H)=\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)
. By (
4.5
), we have

\eta^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{f(H)\delta_{o},\eta})=\frac{\eta^{\beta+1}}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{T}}|v|^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(\lvert(f(H)G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\delta_{o})(v)\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Essentially the same proof as
[
1
]
*Lemma 2.1 shows that
f(H)
can be replaced by
f(\lambda)
. More precisely, we get

(4.11)

\eta^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)\delta_{o},\eta})=\frac{\eta^{\beta+1}}{\pi}\int_{I}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{T}}|v|^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(\lvert G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)\rvert^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\mathrm{o}(\eta).

This settles the first difficulty mentioned above.

Note
.

We mention in passing that for the proof of
[
1
]
*Lemma 2.1, one doesn’t really need a Wegner bound. Inequality
[
1
]
*(2.7) holds deterministically since
\frac{\eta}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(\lambda)|^{2}\lVert G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}\varphi\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}^{2}}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\sigma(H)}\frac{\eta}{(x-\lambda)^{2}+\eta^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varphi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\frac{\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}^{2}}{\pi}\int_{\sigma(H)}(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{-\pi}{2})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varphi}(x)=\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\lVert\varphi\rVert^{2}
. Similarly, one can avoid the Wegner bound in
[
1
]
*(2.10) by writing
Q(\eta)=\mathbb{E}\int_{\sigma(H)}F_{\eta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varphi}(x)
, where
F_{\eta}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x)-f(\lambda)|^{2}\frac{\eta}{(\lambda-x)^{2}+\eta^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
, then use dominated convergence. In fact
F_{\eta}(x)\to 0
by a well-known calculation, cf.
[
13
]
*p. 6, for continuous
f
. The extension to piecewise continuous
f
is straighforward as long as
\mathbb{E}(\mu_{\varphi}(\{a\}))=0
for any
a
, which is weaker than a Wegner bound.

Using Fatou’s lemma and (
4.2
), we see as before that the proof is now reduced to showing that
\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}\mathbb{E}(\lvert G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{r})\rvert^{2})
stays bounded independently of
r
and
\eta\in(0,1)
. Let
\gamma\coloneqq\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta
. We have

\mathbb{E}(\lvert G^{\gamma}(v_{0};v_{r})\rvert^{2})=\mathbb{E}(|\zeta_{v_{1}}^{\gamma}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|G^{\gamma}(v_{r},v_{r})|^{2})

but

|G^{\gamma}(v_{r},v_{r})|\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{u\sim v_{r}}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(u)|+\eta}\leq\frac{1}{|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|},

and
\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})
is independent of
\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})
. Indeed,
\zeta_{v_{r}}^{\gamma}(v_{r+1})=-G_{\mathcal{T}^{(v_{r+1}|v_{r})}}(v_{r+1},v_{r+1})
is the Green function of the connected component
\mathcal{T}^{(v_{r+1}|v_{r})}
containing
v_{r+1}
obtained by removing the directed edge
(v_{r},v_{r+1})
from
\mathcal{T}
, while
\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})=-G_{\mathcal{T}^{(v_{r-1}|v_{r})}}(v_{0},v_{r-1})
lives on a disjoint subtree (see
[
3
]
*Eq. (2.7)). Thus,

\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{0};v_{r})|^{2})\leq\mathbb{E}(|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2})\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|^{-1}).

Again by independence, we may insert
\frac{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|)}{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|)}
and get

(4.12)

\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{0};v_{r})|^{2})\leq\mathbb{E}(|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|)\frac{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|^{-1})}{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|)}\,.

The second fraction is
\frac{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{0}}(v_{1})|^{-1})}{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{0}}(v_{1})|)}\leq\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{0}}(v_{1})|^{-1})^{2}\leq c
by (
4.10
). For the first term, now that we gained the
|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|
, we would like to put back the
|G^{\gamma}(v_{r},v_{r})|^{2}
we removed. More precisely, we would like to find some
C
independent of
r
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\mathbb{E}(|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})||G^{\gamma}(v_{r},v_{r})|^{2})\,.

Define
\mathbb{E}_{2}(f)\coloneqq\frac{\mathbb{E}(|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|f)}{\mathbb{E}(|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r+1})|)}
. We should thus establish a lower bound
\mathbb{E}_{2}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{r},v_{r})|^{2})\geq C_{-}
for some
C_{-}
independent of
r
and
\gamma
. This is nontrivial, but can be done exactly like
[
1
]
*pp. 8-10, using (
4.10
).

Since
|\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{1}}(v_{0})\cdots\zeta^{\gamma}_{v_{r}}(v_{r-1})G^{\gamma}(v_{r},v_{r})|^{2}=|G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2}
, we have finally shown that

\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2})\leq cC\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v_{r}}^{\gamma}(v_{r+1})|)\,.

We may now apply (
4.4
) multiple times to get

\displaystyle\sum_{(v_{2};v_{r})}\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{r})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v_{r}}^{\gamma}(v_{r+1})|)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(v_{0},v_{0})|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{v_{0}}^{\gamma}(v_{1})|)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\biggl(\frac{\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\gamma}(o,o)|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(v_{1})|}{\sum_{u\sim o}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|+\eta}\biggr)\leq\mathbb{E}(\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\gamma}(o,o)).

Taking
b=\frac{cC}{2\mathrm{D}}
, where
\mathrm{D}
is the maximal degree, we deduce as in (
4.9
) that

\eta^{\beta+1}\sum_{v}|v|^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,v)|^{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{cC}{2\mathrm{D}}\Bigr)^{\beta}\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)-2\eta|G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)|^{2}].

By (
4.11
), we thus have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\eta\downarrow 0}\eta^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(\langle x^{\beta}\rangle_{\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)\delta_{o},\eta})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{cC}{2\mathrm{D}}\Bigr)^{\beta}\liminf_{\eta\downarrow 0}\int_{I}\mathbb{E}[\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\lambda+\mathrm{i}\eta}(o,o)]\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigl(\frac{cC}{2\mathrm{D}}\Bigr)^{\beta}\mathbb{E}[\mu_{\delta_{o}}(I)]=\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigl(\frac{cC}{2\mathrm{D}}\Bigr)^{\beta}\mathbb{E}(\lVert\mathbf{1}_{I}(H)\delta_{o}\rVert^{2})>0.

The last quantity is strictly positive by (
4.10
) and classical identities. Note indeed that

\mathbb{E}(\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\gamma}(o,o))\geq\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(o,o)|^{2}|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|)\text{ for any }u\sim o.

By Cauchy–Schwarz this is
\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|^{1/2})}{\mathbb{E}(|G^{\gamma}(o,o)|^{-2})}
. But, by (
4.1
)-(
4.2
),

|G^{\gamma}(o,o)|^{-2}\leq 2|\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|^{2}+\frac{2}{|\zeta_{u}^{\gamma}(o)|^{2}}\leq 2(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{u}^{\gamma}(u^{\prime})|^{-2}+|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{u}^{\gamma}(o)|^{-2}).

Also,
\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|^{1/2})\geq\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|^{-1/2})}
. Thus, using (
4.10
),

\mathbb{E}(\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits G^{\gamma}(o,o))\geq\frac{1}{4}\min_{u,u^{\prime}\sim o}\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{o}^{\gamma}(u)|^{-1/2})^{-1}\mathbb{E}(|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\zeta_{u}^{\gamma}(u^{\prime})|^{-2})^{-1}>0.

The proof is completed.
∎

5.
Epilogue: limiting distributions

As the paper was being finalized, we came upon some articles on the topic of
quantum walks
and found interesting connections. The results we derive in this section are inspired by
[
11
,
26
,
27
]
. Quantum walks are discrete in nature, traditionally consisting of simple shift operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with additional degrees of freedom. Schrödinger evolutions
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
are a lot more complex than shifts, however the quantum walks have been recently generalized in
[
26
,
27
]
and the theories now seem to share a good common ground.

Suppose in either continuous or discrete model that
\lVert\psi\rVert=1
. Then
\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert=1
as the operator is unitary, so the entries
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}
define a probability density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(resp.
G
), and
\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}
is just the
2m
-moment of this measure. Our results thus imply that for certain models, all the (normalized, even) moments of this probability measure converge. This naturally evokes the method of moments, see
[
7
]
*Theorem 30.2.

We shall here give a fuller probabilistic/quantum mechanical picture of the transport theory by discussing the limiting behavior of this measure. If
H
is a Schrödinger operator on a general graph and
X_{t}
is a random vector with distribution
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(v)\rvert^{2}
, we first show that
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
is always asymptotically confined in a compact set. Afterwards we compute exactly the limiting distribution of
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
for the periodic models of Section
3
.

5.1.
General facts

Our first task is to properly modify the probability measure to incorporate the division by
t^{m}
. This is easy. On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if

(5.1)

\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{\psi}(x)\coloneqq\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and if
Q^{(t)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by
Q^{(t)}(x)\coloneqq x/t
then

\frac{1}{t^{m}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{t}^{\psi}}(x^{m})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigl(\frac{x}{t}\Bigr)^{m}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{\psi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigl\lvert Q^{(t)}(x)\bigr\rvert^{m}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{\psi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{m}\,\mathrm{d}Q_{\star}^{(t)}\mu_{t}^{\psi}(y),

where
Q_{\star}^{(t)}\mu_{t}^{\psi}
is the image measure, see e.g.
[
29
]
*Appendix A.7. Thus,
\frac{1}{t^{m}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{t}^{\psi}}(x^{m})=\mathbb{E}_{Q_{\star}^{(t)}\mu_{t}^{\psi}}(x^{m})
and we have a statement about the convergence of moments of the measure

(5.2)

\nu_{t}^{\psi}\coloneqq Q_{\star}^{(t)}\mu_{t}^{\psi}\,.

This measure has a very natural meaning. Suppose
X_{t}
is a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with distribution
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
. Then for
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)=\mu_{t}^{\psi}(tB)=\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in tB)=\mathbb{P}(\frac{X_{t}}{t}\in B)
. In other words,
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
is the distribution of
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
. Note that
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
is also a probability measure since
\nu_{t}^{\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu_{t}^{\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=1
.

Similarly, on a graph
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if
\psi
is normalized, we define
\mu_{t}^{\psi}(v)\coloneqq\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(v)\rvert^{2}
, then
Q^{(t)}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
Q^{(t)}(v)\coloneqq v/t
, and
\nu_{t}^{\psi}\coloneqq Q_{\star}^{(t)}\mu_{t}^{\psi}
. Note that
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
while
\mu_{t}^{\psi}
is a measure on
G
.

The map
\psi\mapsto\nu_{t}^{\psi}
enjoys a nice form of uniform continuity:

Lemma 5.1
.

For any measurable
B
we have
\lvert\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)-\nu_{t}^{\varphi}(B)\rvert\leq(\lVert\psi\rVert+\lVert\varphi\rVert)\lVert\psi-\varphi\rVert
.

Proof.

We write the proof on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the same works on graphs. We have

\displaystyle\lvert\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)-\nu_{t}^{\varphi}(B)\rvert

\displaystyle=|\mu_{t}^{\psi}(tB)-\mu_{t}^{\varphi}(tB)|=\Bigl\lvert\int_{tB}\left(\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}-\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\varphi(x)\rvert^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)\rvert+\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\varphi(x)\rvert\right)\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\varphi(x)\rvert\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\varphi\rVert_{2})\lVert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}(\psi-\varphi)\rVert_{2}=(\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{2})\lVert\psi-\varphi\rVert_{2}.\qed

To understand this measure further, we state a result which is always valid, regardless of the transport being ballistic or not.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
H=\mathcal{A}+V
be a Schrödinger operator on a countable graph
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with maximal degree
\leq\mathrm{D}
. Assume
\lvert x-y\rvert_{2}\leq\mathrm{L}
for any
x\sim y
in
G
, where
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{2}
is the Euclidean norm. Assume the potential
V
is bounded. Suppose
\psi\in\ell^{2}(G)
satisfies
\lVert\psi\rVert=1
. Let
\Lambda_{\mathrm{LD}}\coloneqq[-\mathrm{LD},\mathrm{LD}]^{d}
. Then
\lim\limits_{t\to+\infty}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{LD}})=1
.

Physically, this means that if
X_{t}
is a random vector with distribution
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(v)\rvert^{2}
,
v\in G
, then
X_{t}/t
is asymptotically confined in the compact set
\Lambda_{\mathrm{LD}}
, which is independent of
V
, regardless of the transport behavior. If
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
then
\mathrm{L}=1
and
\mathrm{D}=2d
.

A quantum walk analog of this result previously appeared in
[
27
]
*Theorem 3.10 for “smooth”
\psi
, with a different proof.

Proof.

This follows from the moment upper bounds, Theorem
A.1
. Fix some
o\in G
and denote
\lvert x\rvert\coloneqq d(x,o)
where
d(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
is the graph distance. We first assume that
\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
for all
m
. We then know from Remark
A.3
that

(5.3)

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{k}}\sum_{x\in G}\lvert x\rvert^{k}|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)|^{2}\leq\mathrm{D}^{k}

for any
k
. To pass from the graph distance to the Euclidean distance
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{2}
, we note that given
x\in G
, if
\lvert x\rvert=n
, then there is a path
(v_{0},\dots,v_{n})
such that
v_{0}=o
and
v_{n}=x
. So
\lvert x-v_{0}\rvert_{2}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|v_{j+1}-v_{j}|_{2}\leq n\mathrm{L}
by our assumptions. Thus,
\lvert x-v_{0}\rvert_{2}\leq\mathrm{L}\lvert x\rvert
. Since
\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2m}\leq(\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2})^{m}=\lvert x\rvert_{2}^{2m}\leq(\lvert x-v_{0}\rvert_{2}+|v_{0}|_{2})^{2m}
, we get
\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2}\leq(\mathrm{L}\lvert x\rvert+|o|_{2})^{2m}
. It now follows from (
5.3
) that

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2})=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2m}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{t}^{\psi}}(\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2})=\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2m}}\sum_{x\in G}\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2}|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)|^{2}\leq(\mathrm{LD})^{2m}.

Now let
K=\Lambda_{\mathrm{LD}}
,
K_{\delta}=[-\mathrm{LD}-\delta,\mathrm{LD}+\delta]^{d}
and
B\subset K_{\delta}^{c}
. Then
x\in B
implies
|x_{i}|>\mathrm{LD}+\delta
for some
i
, which implies
\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{2m}>(\mathrm{LD}+\delta)^{2m}
. Thus,
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2})\geq\int_{B}\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(x)\geq(\mathrm{LD}+\delta)^{2m}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)
. It follows that
\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)\leq(\frac{\mathrm{LD}}{\mathrm{LD}+\delta})^{2m}
for all
m
. Taking
m\to\infty
yields
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)=0
. As
\delta
is arbitrary and
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
is a probability measure, this shows that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(K)=1
. This completes the proof when
\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
for all
m
.

For general
\psi
, we argue by approximation. In fact, if
\psi_{N}\to\psi
,
\psi_{N}
has compact support, then
|\nu_{t}^{\psi}(K)-1|\leq|\nu_{t}^{\psi_{N}}(K)-1|+|\nu_{t}^{\psi}(K)-\nu_{t}^{\psi_{N}}(K)|
. Using Lemma
5.1
then taking
t\to+\infty
, we get
\limsup_{t\to+\infty}|\nu_{t}^{\psi}(K)-1|\leq(\lVert\psi_{N}\rVert+\lVert\psi\rVert)\lVert\psi-\psi_{N}\rVert
. Since
\lVert\psi_{N}\rVert\to\lVert\psi\rVert
, the claim follows by taking
N\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

One may be tempted to deduce the same result in case of the continuous Schrödinger operator with smooth potential, evoking Theorem
A.4
. This does not work however because the constant
C_{m}
in Theorem
A.4
depends on
m
, in contrast to the discrete case. This is not just an artefact, the result is wrong in general in the continuous case. In fact, for the simplest case
H=-\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have by (
2.7
),

\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)=\mu_{t}^{\psi}(tB)=\int_{tB}|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{(2t)^{d}}\int_{tB}\Big|\widehat{\phi}_{t}\Big(\frac{x}{2t}\Big)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B/2}|\widehat{\phi}_{t}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

for any measurable
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\phi_{t}(y)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y^{2}/4t}\psi(y)
. As
t\to+\infty
we have
\phi_{t}(y)\to\psi(y)
pointwise, so
\phi_{t}\to\psi
in
L^{2}
by dominated convergence, and
\widehat{\phi}_{t}\to\hat{\psi}
in
L^{2}
by Parseval. In particular,
\lVert\mathbf{1}_{B}(\widehat{\phi}_{t}-\hat{\psi})\rVert_{2}\to 0
. We thus get for any measurable
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(5.4)

\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)\to\int_{B/2}|\hat{\psi}(y)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y.

This has no reason to vanish for
B
outside some compact region. For example, if
\psi(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/2}
, then
\hat{\psi}(y)=\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/2}
and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)>0
for any
B
of positive measure.

On a different note, (
5.4
) also implies the following result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Consider
H=-\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for any
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}=1
,
\nu_{t}^{\psi}
converges weakly to the AC measure
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}
given by
\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}(x)=2^{-d}|\hat{\psi}(\frac{x}{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the portemanteau theorem.
∎

Lemma
5.4
says that if
X_{t}
is a random vector with distribution
|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\Delta}\psi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
then
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
converges in distribution to a random vector
Y
with distribution
2^{-d}|\hat{\psi}(\frac{x}{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
.

5.2.
Periodic models

We start with the discrete case. Given a periodic graph
\Gamma
as in Section
3.2
, and a
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}
-periodic Schrödinger operator

H=\mathcal{A}+Q,

let
\psi\in\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfy
\lVert\psi\rVert=1
. Let
\Omega\coloneqq\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}\times\{1,\dots,\nu\}
and define
\mu^{\psi}
on
\Omega
by

(5.5)

\int_{\Omega}f(\theta,n)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\psi}(\theta,n)=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}f(\theta,n)\lVert P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta.

This is a probability measure since

\mu^{\psi}(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}\lVert P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta=\lVert U\psi\rVert^{2}=\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}=1.

Next, consider the map
h\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by
h(\theta,n)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})
and let
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}
be the image measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

(5.6)

\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}\coloneqq h_{\star}\mu^{\psi}.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a periodic discrete graph endowed with a periodic Schrödinger operator
H
. Let
X_{t}
be a random vector with distribution
\lvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(v)\rvert^{2}
,
v\in\Gamma
. Then
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
converges to a random vector
Y
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with distribution
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}
given by (
5.5
)-(
5.6
).

This agrees with Theorem
3.3
, as
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}}(\lvert x^{m}\rvert_{2}^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}}(x_{i}^{2m})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\psi}}(h_{i}^{2m}(\theta,n))
is the RHS of (
3.20
).

We prove this result using our analysis in Section
3
and the idea from
[
11
]
to use the Cramér-Wold device. Perhaps the theory in
[
27
]
could also be adapted to prove a theorem of this kind.

Proof.

We may assume
\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
for all
m
, see Remark
5.10
.

We first note that
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}
is supported on a compact set since
\Omega
is compact and
h
is continuous. In one dimension this implies that
Y
is characterized by its moments. In higher dimensions and lattices things are a bit more complicated.

Denote
x=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}(x)\mathfrak{a}_{i}
and let
p_{m}^{(c)}(x)\coloneqq\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\alpha_{i}(x)\bigr)^{m}
where
c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{d})
. Suppose we showed that
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(p_{m}^{(c)}(x))\to\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}}(p_{m}^{(c)}(x))
for any
m=1,2,\dots
and
c
. Denoting
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
and
q_{m}^{(\ell)}(x)\coloneqq\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\ell_{j}x_{j}\bigr)^{m}
, since
x_{j}=x\cdot\mathfrak{e}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}(x)\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{e}_{j}
, we get

q_{m}^{(\ell)}(x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\ell_{j}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{e}_{j}\alpha_{i}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\alpha_{i}(x)=p_{m}^{(c)}(x)

for
c_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\ell_{j}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\cdot\mathfrak{e}_{j}
. Consequently, we have
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(q_{m}^{(\ell)}(x))\to\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}}(q_{m}^{(\ell)}(x))
for any
m
and
\ell
, so
\mathbb{E}(q_{m}^{(\ell)}(\frac{X_{t}}{t}))\to\mathbb{E}(q_{m}^{(\ell)}(Y))
for
Y
as in the statement. This implies that
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\ell_{j}\frac{X_{t,j}}{j}
converges in distribution to
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\ell_{j}Y_{j}
for any
\ell
by
[
7
]
*Theorem 30.2, where
X_{t}=(X_{t,1},\dots,X_{t,d})
and
Y=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d})
. By the Cramér-Wold device
[
7
]
*Theorem 29.4, this implies that
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
converges in distribution to
Y
.

So consider
p_{m}^{(c)}(x)
. Since
h(\theta,n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathfrak{a}_{i}\partial_{\theta_{i}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}}(p_{m}^{(c)}(x))=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\psi}}(p_{m}^{(c)}(h))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\partial_{\theta_{j}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\Big)^{m}\lVert P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle U\psi,\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}^{\oplus}\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\partial_{\theta_{j}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\Bigr)^{m}P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr)U\psi\Bigr\rangle.

On the other hand,
\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{t}^{\psi}}(p_{m}^{(c)}(x))=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{t}^{\psi}}(p_{m}^{(c)}(x/t))=\sum_{x\in\Gamma}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\frac{\alpha_{j}(x)}{t})^{m}|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)|^{2}=\frac{1}{t^{m}}\langle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi,(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x))^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{t^{m}}\langle U\psi,U\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x))^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rangle
. It thus suffices to prove that

(5.7)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{U\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x))^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi}{t^{m}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}^{\oplus}\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\partial_{\theta_{j}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\Big)^{m}P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})\mathrm{d}\theta\Big)U\psi.

By the multinomial theorem we have

\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x)\biggr)^{m}=\sum_{l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}=m}\frac{m!}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{r}!}c_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots c_{r}^{l_{r}}\alpha_{1}(x)^{l_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{r}(x)^{l_{r}}.

We showed before (
3.22
) that
\partial_{\theta_{i}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})}=-2\pi\mathrm{i}(k_{i}+s_{i})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})}
if
k_{\mathfrak{a}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{i}
and
v_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{i}
, i.e., if
\alpha_{i}(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})=k_{i}+s_{i}
. We thus have
\alpha_{i}(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})^{l_{i}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})}=(\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi})^{l_{i}}\partial_{\theta_{i}}^{l_{i}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})}
. This yields

\displaystyle(U\alpha_{1}(x)^{l_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{r}(x)^{l_{r}}\phi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}(v_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{\mathfrak{a}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\mathfrak{a}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})}\alpha_{1}(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})^{l_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{r}(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})^{l_{r}}\phi(k_{\mathfrak{a}}+v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}^{m}}{(2\pi)^{m}}\partial_{\theta_{1}}^{l_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\theta_{r}}^{l_{r}}(U\phi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}(v_{n})

and so,

\displaystyle\biggl(U\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x)\biggr)^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\biggr)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}\biggl(U\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}\alpha_{j}(x)\biggr)^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\biggr)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\sum_{l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}=m}\frac{m!}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{r}!}c_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots c_{r}^{l_{r}}\frac{\mathrm{i}^{m}}{(2\pi)^{m}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}\partial_{\theta_{1}}^{l_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\theta_{r}}^{l_{r}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}\,.

As in (
3.2.2
),

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta_{1}}^{l_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\theta_{r}}^{l_{r}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tE_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}(-\mathrm{i}t\partial_{\theta_{1}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}))^{l_{1}}\cdots(-\mathrm{i}t\partial_{\theta_{r}}E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}))^{l_{r}}P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}+\mathrm{O}_{n,\theta}(t^{l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}-1})\,,

where the error term contains as usual derivatives of
E_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})
,
P_{n}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})
, and
(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}
. Since
l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}=m
, using the multinomial theorem again we conclude that (
5.7
) holds, the details are the same as (
3.12
)-(
3.13
).
∎

H=\mathcal{A}

f\colon[-2,2]^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,

Example 5.8

(the hexagonal lattice)
.

The hexagonal distribution can be calculated similarly using the information in Example

. Here we also need
P_{\pm}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})
. This is simple: if
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\cdot v}\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})=|\xi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}
, then the normalized eigenvectors of
H(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})

w_{\pm}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,\pm\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\phi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})})^{T}
, so
P_{\pm}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})=\langle w_{\pm},\,\cdot\,\rangle w_{\pm}
. In particular,
\lVert P_{\pm}(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}\rVert^{2}=\frac{|(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}(0)\pm\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi(\theta_{\mathfrak{b}})}(U\psi)_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}}(v)|^{2}}{2}
.

We may now tackle the continuous case.

Theorem 5.9
.

Consider a Schrödinger operator
H
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with smooth periodic potential
V
having bounded derivatives and let
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
X_{t}
be a random vector with distribution
|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
. Then
\frac{X_{t}}{t}
converges to a random vector
Y
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with distribution
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}
having the analogous expression (
5.5
)-(
5.6
) (so
\sum_{n=1}^{\nu}
becomes
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
).

Proof.

Here
\Omega\coloneqq\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
is no longer compact so we cannot use the method of moments directly. However we can argue by approximation. Namely, given
\psi\in L^{2}
, we know that
\psi_{N}\coloneqq U^{-1}\bigl(\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}^{\oplus}\sum_{n=1}^{N}P_{n}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigr)U\psi
satisfies
\lVert\psi_{N}-\psi\rVert_{2}\to 0
(in fact, in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, Step 3 we showed the stronger fact
\lVert\psi_{N}-\psi\rVert_{H^{2m}}\to 0
if
\psi\in H^{2m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
).

For fixed
N
, we may take
\Omega_{N}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}\times\{1,\dots,N\}
. Then the proof of Theorem
5.5
shows that
\nu_{t}^{\psi_{N}}
converges weakly to
\nu_{\infty}^{\psi_{N}}
as
t\to+\infty
. Given
B\subset\Omega
, it remains to control
|\nu_{t}^{\psi_{N}}(B)-\nu_{t}^{\psi}(B)|
and
|\nu_{\infty}^{\psi_{N}}(B)-\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}(B)|
. The former vanishes uniformly in
t,B
by Lemma
5.1
. Similarly,

\displaystyle|\nu_{\infty}^{\psi}(B)-\nu_{\infty}^{\psi_{N}}(B)|

\displaystyle=\Bigl\lvert\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{h^{-1}B}(\theta,n)(\lVert P_{n}(\theta)(U\psi_{N})_{\theta}\rVert^{2}-\lVert P_{n}(\theta)(U\psi)_{\theta}\rVert^{2})\mathrm{d}\theta\Bigr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\lVert P_{n}(\theta)(U\psi_{N})_{\theta}\rVert+\lVert P_{n}(\theta)(U\psi)_{\theta}\rVert)\lVert P_{n}(\theta)((U\psi)_{\theta}-(U\psi_{N})_{\theta})\rVert\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert\psi_{N}\rVert+\lVert\psi\rVert)\lVert\psi-\psi_{N}\rVert

where we used Cauchy–Schwarz for
\int_{\Omega}
in the last step and the fact that
\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}=\lVert U\phi\rVert^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\ast}^{d}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lVert P_{n}(\theta)(U\phi)_{\theta}\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The same approximation trick works for any dense subspace. In fact we did not use the explicit form of
\psi_{N}
in the previous proof.

Appendix A
Upper bounds and derivatives

Here we first prove that the
m
-moments grow at most like
t^{m}
. This already appeared in various forms: for continuous Schrödinger operators see
[
24
]
for
m=1,2
; for discrete Schrödinger operators, upper bounds can be deduced from
[
1
]
*Appendix B for general moments but
\psi=\delta_{x}
. Using the upper bounds, we then give rigorous proofs of the moment derivative formulas (
A.11
) and (
A.22
).

We start with some general remarks on lower bounds.

A.1.
Lower bounds

On
L^{2}(X)
, if
x_{0}\in X
is fixed and
\lvert x\rvert\coloneqq d(x,x_{0})
, then we have

\displaystyle\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{j}\phi\rVert^{2}=\langle\phi,\lvert x\rvert^{2j}\phi\rangle=\int_{X}\lvert x\rvert^{2j}|\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int_{X}\lvert x\rvert^{2m}\lvert\phi\rvert^{2}\Bigr)^{j/m}\Bigl(\int_{X}|\phi|^{2}\Bigr)^{(m-j)/m}

(A.1)

\displaystyle=\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{m}\phi\rVert^{2j/m}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2(m-j)/m},

where we used Hölder’s inequality with
f=\lvert x\rvert^{2j}|\phi|^{2j/m}
and
g=|\phi|^{(2m-2j)/m}
. It follows that
\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{j}\phi\rVert^{m}\leq\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{m}\phi\rVert^{j}\lVert\phi\rVert^{m-j}
. In particular,

(A.2)

\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2m}}\geq\Big(\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert\lvert x\rvert\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}}{t^{2}}\Big)^{m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2-2m}.

On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we usually consider
\lVert x^{j}\phi\rVert
instead of
\lVert\lvert x\rvert^{j}\phi\rVert
, where
x^{j}\phi\coloneqq(x_{1}^{j}\phi,\dots,x_{d}^{j}\phi)
. We have
\lVert x_{k}^{j}\phi\rVert^{2}\leq\lVert x_{k}^{m}\phi\rVert^{2j/m}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2(m-j)/m}
by the same argument. Using the Plancherel identity gives the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality
\lVert D_{k}^{j}\phi\rVert\leq\lVert D_{k}^{m}\phi\rVert^{j/m}\lVert\phi\rVert^{(m-j)/m}
.

If
H
is a Schrödinger operator,
x^{j}_{k}(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x^{j}_{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
and
D^{j}_{k}(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}(-\mathrm{i}\partial_{x_{k}})^{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
, then this implies

(A.3)

\begin{split}\lVert x_{k}^{j}(t)\psi\rVert&\leq\lVert x_{k}^{m}(t)\psi\rVert^{j/m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{(m-j)/m},\\
\lVert D_{k}^{j}(t)\psi\rVert&\leq\lVert D_{k}^{m}(t)\psi\rVert^{j/m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{(m-j)/m}.\end{split}

Lastly in this connection, recall the uncertainty principle
\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\leq 2\lVert x_{k}\phi\rVert\lVert\partial_{x_{k}}\phi\rVert
. The above yields the generalization
\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\leq 2\lVert x_{k}^{m}\phi\rVert^{1/m}\lVert\phi\rVert^{(m-1)/m}\lVert\partial_{x_{k}}^{m}\phi\rVert^{1/m}\lVert\phi\rVert^{(m-1)/m}
, i.e.

(A.4)

\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\leq 2^{m}\lVert x_{k}^{m}\phi\rVert\cdot\lVert D_{k}^{m}\phi\rVert.

Applying this to
\phi=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
, we thus get

(A.5)

\lVert x_{k}^{j}(t)\psi\rVert\cdot\lVert D_{k}^{j}(t)\psi\rVert\leq 2^{m-j}\lVert x_{k}^{m}(t)\psi\rVert\cdot\lVert D_{k}^{m}(t)\psi\rVert.

More generally, for
j,n\leq m
,

(A.6)

\lVert x_{k}^{j}(t)\psi\rVert\cdot\lVert D_{k}^{n}(t)\psi\rVert\leq 2^{\frac{2m-j-n}{2}}\lVert x_{k}^{m}(t)\psi\rVert^{\frac{2m+j-n}{2m}}\lVert D_{k}^{m}(t)\psi\rVert^{\frac{2m-j+n}{2m}}.

The preceding estimates provide useful lower bounds for
x^{m}(t)\psi
and
D^{m}(t)\psi
in terms of lower moments.

We now consider upper bounds.

A.2.
Discrete case

In the following, given a countable graph
G
, we fix some vertex
o\in G
regarded as an origin and denote
\lvert x\rvert\coloneqq d(x,o)
and
x^{m}\psi(x)\coloneqq\lvert x\rvert^{m}\psi(x)
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
H=\mathcal{A}+V
be a Schrödinger operator on a countable graph
G
with maximal degree
\leq\mathrm{D}
. We assume the potential
V
is bounded. Then for any
t\geq 0
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
, then

(A.7)

\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert\leq\sum_{r=0}^{m}p_{m-r}(t)\lVert x^{r}\psi\rVert,

where
p_{k}(t)
is a polynomial in
t
of degree
k
with
p_{0}(t)=1
, and the leading term of the top polynomial
p_{m}(t)
is
\mathrm{D}^{m}t^{m}
. In particular,

(A.8)

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert}{t^{m}}\leq\mathrm{D}^{m}\lVert\psi\rVert.

Note that each
p_{k}(t)
also depends on
m
, that is, for each fixed
m
there is a set of polynomials
p_{0,m}(t),\dots,p_{m,m}(t)
with
p_{k,m}
of degree
k
such that (
A.7
) is satisfied with
p_{k}\equiv p_{k,m}
. See Remark
A.3
for a further comment.

Proof.

By induction on
m
. The statement is trivial for
m=0
since
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
is unitary.

Let
m=1
. For an operator
O
, recall we denote
O(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}O\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
. In particular,
x(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
when
O
is the operator of multiplication by
x
. We formally have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x(t)\psi

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}H\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}xH\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}[H,x]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi

(A.9)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}[\mathcal{A},x]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\,.

This calculation is formal because the first derivative
\mathrm{i}H\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
requires
x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\in\ell^{2}(G)
, while the second
-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}xH\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
requires
\lim_{\delta\to 0}x\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}}{\delta}\psi=-\mathrm{i}xH\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
. See, e.g.,
[
12
]
*Lemma 10.17. None of these facts is a priori clear (in fact the first point is partly what the theorem tries to prove, we only know that
x\psi\in\ell^{2}(G)
, a priori). Note that this formal calculation is justified however if instead of
x
we multiply by a bounded function.

So, similar to
[
24
]
, given
\epsilon>0
, we consider
f_{\epsilon}(\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{\lambda}{1+\epsilon\lambda}
for
\lambda\geq 0
. Then multiplication by
f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)
is a bounded operator and we have
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}[f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)](t)=\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}[\mathcal{A},f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
. But

[\mathcal{A},f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\phi(x)=\sum_{y\sim x}[f_{\epsilon}(|y|)-f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\phi(y)=:\sum_{y\sim x}\alpha_{x,y}\phi(y).

Here
|\alpha_{x,y}|=|f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lambda)|
for some
\lambda\in[\lvert x\rvert-1,\lvert x\rvert+1]
. Hence,
|\alpha_{x,y}|\leq\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon\lambda)^{2}}\leq 1
. We thus get

\lVert[\mathcal{A},f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\phi\rVert^{2}\leq\mathrm{D}\sum_{x}\sum_{y\sim x}|\phi(y)|^{2}\leq\mathrm{D}^{2}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}.

Applying this to
\phi=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
, we get
\lVert[\mathcal{A},f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert\leq\mathrm{D}\lVert\psi\rVert
. So using
[
8
]
*Theorem 5.6.1,

\displaystyle\lVert(f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert))(t)\psi\rVert

\displaystyle=\Bigl\lVert(f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert))(0)\psi+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert))(s)\psi\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\psi\rVert+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert[\mathcal{A},f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi\rVert\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\lVert x\psi\rVert+t\mathrm{D}\lVert\psi\rVert\,.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, taking
\epsilon\downarrow 0
and using Fatou’s lemma, we get

\displaystyle\lVert x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}=\sum_{x}\lvert x\rvert^{2}|(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi)(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{x}\frac{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}{(1+\epsilon\lvert x\rvert)^{2}}|(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi)(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}

(A.10)

\displaystyle\leq(\lVert x\psi\rVert+t\mathrm{D}\lVert\psi\rVert)^{2},

where we used
\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert=\lVert(f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert))(t)\psi\rVert
since
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}
is unitary.
This settles
m=1
.

Now assume the statement holds for all
k<m
. Let
f_{\epsilon}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{m}}{1+\epsilon\lambda^{m}}
. Here
|f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lambda)|\leq m|\lambda|^{m-1}
. Arguing as before, we get

\displaystyle\lVert[\mathcal{A},f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)]\phi\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{D}m^{2}\sum_{x}(\lvert x\rvert+1)^{2(m-1)}\sum_{y\sim x}|\phi(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{D}^{2}m^{2}\lVert(\lvert x\rvert+2)^{m-1}\phi\rVert^{2}\,.

Hence,

\lVert(f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert))(t)\psi\rVert\leq\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\psi\rVert+\mathrm{D}m\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{q}2^{m-1-q}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert x^{q}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi\rVert\,\mathrm{d}s.

Since
f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\leq\lvert x\rvert^{m}
, using the induction hypothesis we get

\lVert(f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert))(t)\psi\rVert\leq\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert+\mathrm{D}m\sum_{q=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{q}2^{m-1-q}\sum_{r=0}^{q}\tilde{p}_{q-r+1}(t)\lVert x^{r}\psi\rVert\,.

As the RHS is independent of
\epsilon
, taking
\epsilon\downarrow 0
and using Fatou’s lemma again yields
\lVert x^{m}(t)\psi\rVert\leq\sum_{s=0}^{m}p_{m-s}(t)\lVert x^{s}\psi\rVert
. The above also shows the coefficient of
\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert
is
p_{0}(t)=1
. The top polynomial
p_{m}(t)
is found by taking
q=m-1
and
r=0
and equals
\mathrm{D}m\tilde{p}_{m}(t)
, where
\tilde{p}_{m}(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}p_{m-1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
. As the leading term of
p_{m-1}(s)
is
\mathrm{D}^{m-1}s^{m-1}
by hypothesis, the leading term of

is

∎

A posteriori, the formal differentiation (
A.2
) is actually valid. Recall
x(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}
.

Corollary A.2
.

Under the same assumptions,
\lim\limits_{s\to t}x^{m}(s)\psi=x^{m}(t)\psi
and

(A.11)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x^{m}(t)\psi=\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}[\mathcal{A},x^{m}]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\,.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\lVert x^{m}(s)\psi-x^{m}(t)\psi\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert+\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert

(A.12)

\displaystyle=\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert+\lVert\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert\,.

We know from Theorem
A.1
that
\phi=x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\in D(H)=\ell^{2}(G)
for any
t\geq 0
, so
\lim_{s\to t}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}sH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
. This settles the second term in the RHS.

For the first term, we use Fatou’s lemma as in (
A.2
). Let
f_{\epsilon}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda^{m}}{1+\epsilon\lambda^{m}}
. We have
f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi=f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi+\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha H}\psi\,\mathrm{d}\alpha
. Now

\Bigl\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha H}\psi\Bigr\rVert=\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)H\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha H}\psi\rVert\leq\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha H}H\psi\rVert\leq\sum_{r=0}^{m}p_{m-r}(\alpha)\lVert x^{r}H\psi\rVert

for some polynomials
p_{k}
, by Theorem
A.1
. These are uniformly bounded by some
M(t)
for all
\alpha\in[t-1,t+1]
and we get
\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert\leq\left|t-s\right|M(t)\sum_{r=0}^{m}\lVert x^{r}H\psi\rVert
, with
M(t)
independent of
\epsilon
.

By Fatou’s lemma,
\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}\leq\liminf\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert^{2}
. Thus,
\lVert x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi-x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\rVert\leq\left|t-s\right|M(t)\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}\lVert x^{r}H\psi\rVert\to 0
as
s\to t
. Recalling (
A.2
), this completes the proof of the first claim.

For the derivative we first make some simplifications. Given
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\lVert\frac{x^{m}(t+\delta)\psi-x^{m}(t)\psi}{\delta}-\mathrm{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}tH}[H,x^{m}]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\quad=\Bigl\lVert\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta H}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}\psi-x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi}{\delta}-\mathrm{i}[H,x^{m}]\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Bigl\lVert\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta H}-I}{\delta}x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}\psi-\mathrm{i}Hx^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}\psi\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert Hx^{m}(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H})\psi\rVert+\Bigl\lVert x^{m}\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}}{\delta}\psi+\mathrm{i}H\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\Bigr)\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Bigl\lVert\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta H}-I}{\delta}-\mathrm{i}H\Bigr)x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\Bigr\rVert+\Bigl\lVert\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta H}-I}{\delta}-\mathrm{i}H\Bigr)x^{m}(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi)\Bigr\rVert

(A.13)

\displaystyle\qquad+\lVert Hx^{m}(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H})\psi\rVert+\Bigl\lVert x^{m}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}}{\delta}\psi+\mathrm{i}H\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\Big)\Bigr\rVert\,.

For the first term, we know from Theorem
A.1
that
x^{m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\in D(H)=\ell^{2}(G)
, so this term vanishes as
\delta\to 0
. For the second term, we use the spectral theorem:
\lVert\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta H}-I}{\delta}\phi\rVert^{2}=\int|\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\delta\lambda}-1}{\delta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\phi}(\lambda)\leq\int\lambda^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\phi}(\lambda)=\lVert H\phi\rVert^{2}
. With this bound, we see the second and third terms vanish as

by the argument of (
A.2

is bounded; see also Corollary
A.5
for unbounded operators). So it remains to control the last term. For this we first use Fatou’s lemma to replace

by

as follows.

We know that

It follows from

]

\Bigl\lVert f_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert)\Big(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(t+\delta)H}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}}{\delta}\psi+\mathrm{i}H\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi\Big)\Bigr\rVert\leq\frac{\lvert\delta\rvert}{2}\sup_{s\in[t-1,t+1]}\lVert x^{m}H^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}sH}\psi\rVert,

where we used that

. Using Theorem

, with

∎

Remark A.3
.

Theorem
A.1
implies that

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2m}}\sum_{x\in G}\lvert x\rvert^{2m}|\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi(x)|^{2}\leq\mathrm{D}^{2m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}.

This also implies a control for odd powers. Namely, if
\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert<\infty
, letting
\psi_{t}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}tH}\psi
, we have by Cauchy–Schwarz that
\sum\lvert x\rvert^{m}|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2}\leq(\sum\lvert x\rvert^{2m}|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2})^{1/2}(\sum|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2})^{1/2}
. So
\frac{1}{t^{m}}\sum\lvert x\rvert^{m}|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2}\leq(\frac{1}{t^{2m}}\sum\lvert x\rvert^{2m}|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2})^{1/2}\lVert\psi\rVert
and thus

\limsup_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{m}}\sum_{x\in G}\lvert x\rvert^{m}|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2}\leq\mathrm{D}^{m}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}.

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\Bigl\lvert\Bigl\langle\psi_{t},D_{j}^{p}\frac{x_{j}^{2r}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}D_{j}^{2k-p}\psi_{t}\Bigr\rangle\Bigr\rvert^{1/2}

\partial_{x_{j}}^{p}\frac{x_{j}^{2r}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\binom{p}{\ell}(2r)\cdots(2r-\ell+1)x_{j}^{2r-\ell}\partial_{x_{j}}^{p-\ell}\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}.

If
f_{\epsilon}(u)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon u)^{2}}
and
g(x)\coloneqq x^{2m}
then by the Faà di Bruno formula,

\partial_{x_{j}}^{n}\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}=\partial_{x_{j}}^{n}f_{\epsilon}(g(x))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}im_{i}=n\end{subarray}}c_{n,m_{i}}f_{\epsilon}^{(m_{1}+\dots+m_{n})}(g(x))\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{x_{j}}^{i}g(x))^{m_{i}}\,.

But
f_{\epsilon}^{(q)}(u)=(-\epsilon)^{q}(q+1)!(1+\epsilon u)^{-2-q}
and
\partial_{x_{j}}^{i}g(x)=(2m)\cdots(2m-i+1)x_{j}^{(2m-i)}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}^{n}\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}im_{i}=n\end{subarray}}\tilde{c}_{n,m_{i}}\frac{\epsilon^{m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2+m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}}}(2m)x_{j}^{(2m-1)m_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times(2m)(2m-1)x_{j}^{(2m-2)m_{2}}\cdots(2m)\cdots(2m-n+1)x_{j}^{(2m-n)m_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}im_{i}=n\end{subarray}}C_{n,m_{i}}\frac{\epsilon^{m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2+m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}}}x_{j}^{2m(m_{1}+\dots+m_{n})-n}\,,

where we used
\sum im_{i}=n
in the last step. But

\epsilon^{m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}}x_{j}^{2m(m_{1}+\dots+m_{n})}\leq(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{m_{1}+\dots+m_{n}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{j}}^{p}\frac{x_{j}^{2r}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}\binom{p}{\ell}(2r)\cdots(2r-\ell+1)x_{j}^{2r-\ell}\sum_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{p-\ell})}C_{p-\ell,m_{i}}\frac{x_{j}^{\ell-p}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p}c_{p,\ell,r}\frac{x_{j}^{2r-p}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\Bigl\lVert\frac{x_{j}^{r}}{1+\epsilon x^{2m}}(t)D_{j}^{k}(t)\psi\Bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=0}^{k}c_{p,k,r}\Bigl\lvert\Bigl\langle\psi,\frac{x_{j}^{2r-p}}{(1+\epsilon x^{2m})^{2}}(t)D_{j}^{2k-p}(t)\psi\Bigr\rangle\Bigr\rvert^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=0}^{k}c_{p,k,r}\Bigl\lVert\frac{x_{j}^{m}}{1+\epsilon x^{2m}}(t)\psi\Bigl\lVert^{1/2}\Bigl\lVert\frac{x_{j}^{2r-p-m}}{1+\epsilon x^{2m}}(t)D_{j}^{2k-p}(t)\psi\Bigr\rVert^{1/2}.

This proves the analog of (
A.16
)-(
A.17
). From here, the proof goes as before and we get

\Bigl\lVert\frac{x_{j}^{m}}{1+\epsilon x^{2m}}\psi\Bigr\rVert^{1/m}\leq\lVert x^{m}\psi\rVert^{1/m}+Ct\sum_{k=0}^{m}\lVert H^{k}\psi\rVert^{1/m},

independently of
\epsilon
. The result follows by taking
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, using Fatou’s lemma.
∎

Admissibility over Semi-Global Fields in the Bad Characteristic Case
Yael Davidov

Abstract
A finite group G G is said to be admissible over a field F F if there exists a division algebra D D central over F F with a maximal subfield L L such that L / F L/F is Galois with group G G . In this paper we give a complete characterization of admissible groups over function fields of curves over equicharacteristic complete discretely valued fields with algebraically closed residue fields, such as the field 𝔽 P ¯ ​ ( ( t ) ) ​ ( x ) \overline{\mathbb{F}_{P}}((t))(x) .



1
Introduction

Admissibility was first studied over global fields by Schacher
[
Sch68
]
. He found that the
q
-Sylow subgroups of an admissible group over a characteristic
p
global field had to be metacyclic (i.e. the extension of a cyclic group by another cyclic group) for all primes
q\neq p
. Sufficient conditions for admissibility over global fields is still an open question, with results for solvable groups given by Sonn
[
Son83
]
. Admissibility over other types of fields have been studied by Harbater, Hartmann and Krashen
[
HHK11
]
, Suresh and Reddy
[
RS13
]
and Neftin and Paran
[
NP10
]
.

In 2011 Harbater, Hartmann, and Krashen used field patching techniques to study admissibility over semi-global fields
[
HHK11
]
. A semi-global field
F
is the function field of a curve over a complete discretely valued field
K
. Harbater, Hartmann, and Krashen showed that if
G
is admissible over a semi-global field
F
, and the residue field of
K
,
k
, is algebraically closed, then the
q
-Sylow subgroups of
G
must be abelian of rank at most 2 for every
q\neq p:=\text{char}(k)
. As in the global field case, it was not clear what restrictions, if any, should be placed on the
p
-Sylow subgroups.

The main contribution of this work is to show that, in the equicharacteristic
p
case, the structure of the
p
-Sylow subgroup of a group
G
does not impact the admissibility of
G
over
F
. With this result in hand we can completely characterize admissible groups over a new class of semi-global fields.

Theorem

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

Let
K
be a complete discretely valued field with algebraically closed residue field
k
, and let F be a finitely generated field extension of K with transcendence degree 1. Assume further that
\text{char}(K)=\text{char}(k)
. A finite group
G
is admissible over
F
if and only if the
q
-Sylow subgroups of
G
are abelian of rank at most 2 for every prime
q\neq\text{char}(k)
.

This result builds on the work of Guico
[
Gui20
]
and uses field patching techniques as used in
[
HHK11
]
. One difficulty in addressing this case is that while Guico’s work establishes admissibility for
p
-groups, it leaves open the question of how to patch these compatibly to obtain more general groups. An important part of our strategy is changing the patching model by blowing up and constructing compatible objects on intermediate patches using generic Galois theory.

In section 2 we provide a quick review of the theory of field patching which will be the primary technique used in the proof of the main result and establish some notation. Section 3 establishes the local admissibility of the
p
-Sylow subgroup, recalling results of Guico. In Section 4 we prove some technical results which establish control over the behavior of the
p
-Sylow piece on overlapping patches. In Section 5 we construct compatible structures over a sequence of patches using generic Galois theory. The proof of the main result is given in section 6.

2
Field Patching

The primary underlying framework in the proof of the main result is the field patching technique introduced by Harbater and Hartmann in 2007. We will be using a streamlined setup described in
[
HHK11
]
and
[
HHK15
]
. Broadly speaking, field patching allows one to prove the existence of certain algebraic objects over a given field by constructing corresponding objects over a special system of field extensions in a compatible way, when such a system of overfields exists.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a finite partially ordered set with a field
F_{i}
for each

. Suppose for each

there is an inclusion
\iota_{ij}:F_{i}\rightarrow F_{j}
. We call this a system of fields and denote it by
\mathscr{F}=\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Suppose also that
\mathcal{A}(F_{i})
is a category which we think of as consisting of “algebraic objects of type

over

” such that extension of scalars
\mathcal{E}_{ij}:\mathcal{A}(F_{i})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(F_{j})
given by

is a functor for all

.

Remark 2.2
.

An example of the type of algebraic objects that might be considered are finite dimensional vector spaces. In this case

would denote the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over

. More generally, we can consider vector spaces with a fixed tensor as described by Serre in Section X.2 of
[

]
. For a discussion of the types of algebraic objects that one can consider when using field patching techniques see Definition 3.2.1 in
[

3
Local Admissibility of
p
-Sylow Subgroup

A key step in proving admissibility over a semi-global field using Theorem
2.5
is establishing the admissibility of Sylow subgroups over points in the patching framework. For convenience, we record some definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a field
F
and a finite field extension

,

is called
F
-adequate if there exists a division algebra central over
F
containing

as a maximal subfield.

Definition 3.2
.

A finite group
G
is admissible over
F
if there exists a Galois field extension
E/F
with group
G
such that
E
is
F
-adequate.

Harbater, Hartmann and Krashen proved the existence of certain adequate extensions over points in the patching framework in the case that

is a function field over a complete discretely valued field with residue field
k
where char
(k)
does not divide the order of the group.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 4.3 and Proposition 4.4 in
[
HHK11
]
)
.

Let
K
be a complete discretely valued field with algebraically closed residue field
k
, and let F be a finitely generated field extension of K with transcendence degree 1. Let
(\widehat{X},\mathscr{I},\mathscr{F})
be a semi-global patching framework for

with a point

such that

is regular at

. Suppose

is an abelian group of rank at most two with
\text{char}(k)\nmid|G|
, then there exists an
F_{P}
-adequate field extension

with Galois group

that splits over the unique branch of

at

, i.e.
E\otimes_{F_{P}}F_{\wp}\cong F_{\wp}^{|G|}
where
\wp
is the unique branch of

at

.

In this section we prove a statement similar to Lemma

in the case that

is a
p
-group and

is the characteristic of

. Unfortunately, we were not able to find a direct proof that the adequate field extension splits over the branch field, like the final statement proved in the lemma. In Section

we will gain weaker control over the behavior of the adequate field extension over the branch field which will be enough to establish the compatibility of algebraic objects required for patching later on.

Working with

be a regular local ring with fraction field
F
, and let
E/F
be a finite separable extension. Let S be the integral closure of
R
in

, and let
\wp
be a prime ideal of

. We say that

is unramified at

if the induced map

is an unramified local homomorphism of local rings for every prime

of S lying over
\wp
. Otherwise, we say that

is ramified at
\wp
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
R
,
S
,
F
, and
E
be as in Definition
4.2
. Additionally, assume that
R
has Krull dimension 2. Then
E
is only ramified at finitely many height 1 prime ideals of
R
.

Proof.

By Theorem B.11 in
[
LW12
]
,
E
is ramified at a prime ideal,
\wp
, of

if and only if there exists a prime ideal,
q
, of
S
lying over
\wp
such that the homological different
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
is contained in
q
. We want to show there are only finitely many such prime ideals of
R
of height 1.

Let
q
be a prime ideal of
S
lying over a height 1 prime ideal of R,
\wp
, and suppose
q
contains
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
q
is not an associated prime of
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
, then it must strictly contain a prime ideal minimal over
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
,
q^{\prime}
, so we have
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)\subseteq q^{\prime}\subsetneq q
. This implies that
\wp^{\prime}:=q^{\prime}\cap R\subsetneq q\cap R=\wp
. Since
\wp^{\prime}
is a prime ideal of
R
strictly contained in a height 1 prime ideal, and

is a domain, this implies that
\wp^{\prime}
is the zero ideal. Since E is an algebraic extension of F, this implies that
q^{\prime}
is the zero ideal of

. However, since

is regular and

is normal, Theorem B.12 in
[
LW12
]
implies that
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
is an ideal of pure height 1. So

must be a height 1 prime in

, which is a contradiction. Therefore,

must be an associated prime of
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
.

Since

is Noetherian,
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
has only finitely many associated primes. Let
Q=\{q_{1},q_{2},\dots q_{n}\}
be the subset of the set of associated primes of
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
consisting of all associated primes of
\mathfrak{H}(S/R)
such that
\wp_{i}=q_{i}\cap R
is a height 1 prime of

. Then
\{\wp_{1},\wp_{2},\dots,\wp_{n}\}
is the set of all height 1 prime ideals of

at which

is ramified.
∎

Division Algebra Splits Almost Everywhere

Similarly, the next lemma proves that if
R
is an equicharacteristic complete regular local ring of dimension 2 with fraction field
F
and algebraically closed residue field, and

is a division algebra central over

, then

splits over all but finitely many height 1 primes of

. We say that

splits over a height 1 prime ideal

of

if

is trivial in the Brauer group of

,

, where

is the fraction field of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
R
be an integrally closed Noetherian domain,
F
be the fraction field of
R
, and

be a central simple algebra over
F
. We will call a subring
\Lambda
of

an

-order in

if
\Lambda
is a finitely generated torsion-free

-module, and the

-span of a set of

-generators of

is

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be an equicharacteristic complete regular local ring of dimension 2 with algebraically closed residue field. Let
F
be the fraction field of
R
, and
D
be a division algebra central over
F
. Then
D
splits over all but finitely many height 1 prime ideals of
R
.

Proof.

First we note that
D
contains an
R
-order,
\Lambda
, central over
R
(see p.109 of
[
Rei03
]
for an explicit construction). We will begin by considering the separability of
\Lambda
over height 1 prime ideals of
R
and later show the connection to the splitting behavior of
D
. Given a prime ideal
\wp
of
R
,
\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\wp}
is separable over
R_{\wp}
if and only if
\wp
does not contain
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
. This is due to Proposition 4.4 of
[
AG60
]
which implies that
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\wp}/R_{\wp})=\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)\otimes_{R}R_{\wp}
.

We first show that only finitely many height 1 prime ideals of
R
can contain
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
. Each height 1 prime ideal must either be an associated prime of
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
, or strictly contain a minimal prime of
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
. However, the zero ideal of
R
is the only prime ideal strictly contained in a height 1 prime ideal. Therefore, as long as
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
is non-zero, the height 1 prime ideals containing
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
are all associated primes of
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
and there are only finitely many associated primes of
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
since R is Noetherian.

We see that
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
is not zero because
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda\otimes_{R}F/F)=\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)\otimes_{R}F
(Proposition 4.4 in
[
AG60
]
). Since
\Lambda\otimes_{R}F=D
, and
D
is a separable
F
-algebra,

\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)\otimes_{R}F=\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda\otimes_{R}F/F)=\mathfrak{H}(D/F)=F.

Therefore,
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)\otimes_{R}F\neq 0
.

Now suppose
\wp
is a height 1 prime ideal that does not contain
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
. Then
\Lambda_{\wp}
is a central separable
R_{\wp}
-algebra, which implies that
\widehat{\Lambda_{\wp}}:=\Lambda_{\wp}\otimes_{R_{\wp}}\widehat{R_{\wp}}=\Lambda\otimes_{R}\widehat{R_{\wp}}
is a central separable
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
-algebra (see Lemma 2.5.1 of
[
DI06
]
). In this case the class of
\widehat{\Lambda_{\wp}}
, denoted
[\widehat{\Lambda_{\wp}}]
, is an element of the Brauer group of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
. Since
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
is a complete local ring, the canonical map from the Brauer group of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
to the Brauer group of its residue field is injective (Proposition IV.1.6 in
[
Mil16
]
). Therefore we examine the residue field of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
and its Brauer group.

By the Cohen structure theorem,
R\cong k[[x,y]]
where
k
is the residue field of
R
and we can assume that the isomorphism maps
x
to a generator of
\wp
, which is principal. So
R_{\wp}\cong k[[x,y]]_{(x)}
and
\widehat{R_{\wp}}\cong k((y))[[x]]
. This implies that the residue field of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
is isomorphic to
k((y))
, and since
k
is algebraically closed by assumption,
k((y))
is a
C_{1}
-field. Therefore, the Brauer group of the residue field of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
is trivial (see Proposition 6.2.3 and Theorem 6.2.11 in
[
GS17
]
). As a result, Br
(\widehat{R_{\wp}})
is trivial as well.

Since
\widehat{\Lambda_{\wp}}\otimes_{\widehat{R_{\wp}}}F_{\wp}\cong D\otimes_{F}F_{\wp}
, where
F_{\wp}
is the fraction field of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
, the class of
D\otimes_{F}F_{\wp}
in Br
(F_{\wp})
is the image of the class of
\widehat{\Lambda_{\wp}}
in Br
(\widehat{R_{\wp}})
under the map induced by the inclusion of
\widehat{R_{\wp}}
in
F_{\wp}
. Therefore
D
splits over every height one prime ideal
\wp
where the class of
\widehat{\Lambda_{\wp}}
is the trivial element of Br
(\widehat{R_{\wp}})
, i.e. all the height 1 primes that do not contain the homological different,
\mathfrak{H}(\Lambda/R)
. So
D
splits over all but finitely many height 1 prime ideals of
R
.
∎

Now we show that a ring such as the one in the previous lemma has infinitely many distinct height 1 prime ideals.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
R
be an equicharacteristic complete regular local ring of dimension 2. Then
R
has infinitely many height 1 prime ideals.

Proof.

Again, by the Cohen structure theorem,
R\cong k[[x,y]]
where
k
is the residue field of
R
and the isomorphism can be chosen to send
x
and
y
to any given regular system of parameters in
R
. Therefore, we can just show that
k[[x,y]]
has infinitely many height 1 prime ideals.

We note that
m=(x,y)
is the maximal ideal of
k[[x,y]]
and it is clear that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the ideal
(x+y^{n},y)
is also equal to
m
. Since this is a minimal way to generate the maximal ideal, we can see that for each
n
,
x+y^{n}
is a regular parameter and therefore
(x+y^{n})
is a height 1 prime of
k[[x,y]]
. Since
(x+y^{i})\neq(x+y^{j})
when
i\neq j
, these height 1 primes are distinct and there are infinitely many of them.
∎

Changing Coordinates

The following proposition is the main result of this section which we will use to control the behavior of division algebras and maximal subfields such as those shown to exist in Section
3
over a chosen height 1 prime ideal. To obtain good behavior over a specific height 1 prime ideal we leverage the Cohen structure theorem and make a change of coordinates to transport good behavior from an arbitrary height 1 prime ideal.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
R
be an equicharacteristic complete regular local ring of dimension 2 with algebraically closed residue field,
F
be the fraction field of
R
, and
D
be a division algebra central over
F
with a maximal subfield
E
separable over
F
. Then, given a height 1 prime ideal
\wp
of
R
, there exists a finite dimensional division algebra

over
F
with maximal subfield
E^{\prime}
isomorphic to
E
such that
E^{\prime}
is unramified at
\wp
and
D^{\prime}
splits over
\wp
.

Proof.

First we note that if
E
is unramified at
\wp
and
D
splits over
\wp
then we’re done. So we suppose now that this is not the case. By Lemma
4.3
,
E
is unramified over all but finitely many height 1 prime ideals of
R
, and by Lemma
4.5
,
D
is split over all but finitely many height 1 primes ideals of
R
. Since
R
has infinitely many height 1 prime ideals, there exists a height 1 prime ideal of
R
,
\tilde{\wp}\neq\wp
, such that
E
is unramified at
\tilde{\wp}
and
D
splits over
\tilde{\wp}
simultaneously.

As in previous proofs we know there exists a ring isomorphism
\psi:k[[x,y]]\rightarrow R
which maps
x
to a generator of
\tilde{\wp}
. Similarly, there is an isomorphism
\psi^{\prime}:k[[x,y]]\rightarrow R
which maps
x
to a generator of
\wp
and so by composing
\psi^{\prime}\circ\psi^{-1}
we obtain an automorphism of
R
which maps a generator of
\tilde{\wp}
to a generator of
\wp
.

This automorphism can be extended to an automorphism
\phi
of
F
. We consider the algebra
D^{\prime}=D\otimes_{\phi}F
. Here we regard
D^{\prime}
as an
F
-algebra via
\phi
, so the action of
F
on
D^{\prime}
is defined by
c\cdot(x\otimes d)=x\otimes cd=x\phi^{-1}(c)\otimes d
for
c,d\in F
and

. We note that
D^{\prime}
is isomorphic to

as a ring via the map

therefore it is still a division ring with center
F
. For this same reason

also has a maximal subfield isomorphic to

,

.

However
D
and
D^{\prime}
are not necessarily isomorphic as
F
-algebras, and the ring isomorphism described above does not respect the
F
-algebra structure. We show that
D^{\prime}
is a division algebra central over
F
which splits over
\wp
and that

is unramified at
\wp
.

We let
\varphi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
be the ring isomorphism defined by
e\mapsto e\otimes 1
but consider
R
and
F
as subrings of
E^{\prime}
via the injection
c\mapsto 1\otimes c
for all

. We note that as subsets of

these are equal to
\varphi(R)
and
\varphi(F)
respectively. Therefore, the integral closure of

in
E^{\prime}
is the integral closure of
\varphi(R)
in
E^{\prime}
which is just

where
S
is the integral closure of
R
in
E
. Now we consider
\wp
in
E^{\prime}
where we think of it inside
\varphi(R)
as the set

\{1\otimes p\mid p\in\wp\}=\{\phi^{-1}(p)\otimes 1\mid p\in\wp\}=\{p^{\prime}\otimes 1\mid p^{\prime}\in\tilde{\wp}\}=\varphi(\tilde{\wp}),

since
\phi^{-1}(\wp)=\tilde{\wp}
. So for any prime ideal
q^{\prime}
of
S^{\prime}
lying over
\wp
we have
q^{\prime}=\varphi(q)
for some prime

of

lying over
\tilde{\wp}
. Then the map

is just the composition of

, and so is an unramified local homomorphism of local rings. Therefore

is unramified at
\wp
.

Now we consider the class

in Br
(F_{\wp})
. We note first that the original automorphism of

can be extended to an isomorphism
R_{\tilde{\wp}}\rightarrow R_{\wp}
and this induces an isomorphism of the completions. Since the completions are domains, this can be extended to an isomorphism of the fraction fields and so we have an isomorphism
\overline{\phi}:F_{\tilde{\wp}}\rightarrow F_{\wp}
, which when restricted to

considered as a subfield of each of these fields is just the automorphism

. Therefore, we have an isomorphism of Brauer groups Br
(F_{\tilde{\wp}})\rightarrow\text{Br}(F_{\wp})
given by
[A]\mapsto[A\otimes_{\overline{\phi}}F_{\wp}]
. Now we note that
D^{\prime}\otimes_{F}F_{\wp}=D\otimes_{\phi}F\otimes_{F}F_{\wp}\cong D\otimes_{F}F_{\tilde{\wp}}\otimes_{\overline{\phi}}F_{\wp}
as
F_{\wp}
- algebras via the map
d\otimes c\otimes e\mapsto d\otimes\phi^{-1}(c)\otimes e
. Since
[D\otimes_{F}F_{\tilde{\wp}}\otimes_{\overline{\phi}}F_{\wp}]
is the image of
[D\otimes_{F}F_{\tilde{\wp}}]
under the isomorphism of the Brauer groups described above, and

is trivial in Br
(F_{\tilde{\wp}})
, it’s image must be trivial in Br
(F_{\wp})
. Therefore,
D^{\prime}
splits over
\wp
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
A_{U}
is a split central simple algebra over
F_{U}
containing a
G
-Galois
F_{U}
-subalgebra
E_{U}
where
|G|=p^{m}
. Then there exists a central simple algebra
A_{P^{\prime}}
over
F_{P^{\prime}}
with a
G
-Galois
F_{P^{\prime}}
-subalgebra
E_{P^{\prime}}
such that:

i.

A_{P^{\prime}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}}}F_{\wp_{U}}\cong A_{U}\otimes_{F_{U}}F_{\wp_{U}}
and the isomorphism restricts to an isomorphism of
G
-Galois
F_{\wp_{U}}
-algebras,
E_{P^{\prime}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}}}F_{\wp_{U}}\cong E_{U}\otimes_{F_{U}}F_{\wp_{U}}

A_{P^{\prime}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}}}F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}\cong M_{p^{m}}(F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}})
and the isomorphism restricts to an isomorphism of
G
-Galois
F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}
-algebras,
E_{P^{\prime}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}}}F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}\cong F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}^{p^{m}}
.

As in the previous proof, we construct

first. Note that since

is a
G
-Galois
F_{\wp_{U}}
-algebra and

G
-Galois extension

-Galois
F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}
-algebra, there exists a ring homomorphism

-Galois
F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}
-algebras.

Now we note that

is the completion of

with respect to a height one discrete valuation (with valuation ring

) and

is the completion of

with respect to a different height one discrete valuation (with valuation ring

). Since these are distinct height one valuations on
F_{P^{\prime}}
they are independent and so

is simultaneously dense in

and
F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}
(see Theorem VI.7.2.1 of
[
Bou72
]
).

Lemma
3.5
implies that there exists a
\delta_{1}>0
such that if
b_{i}\in F_{\wp_{U}}
satisfy
\lVert b_{i}-f_{E_{\wp_{U}}}(x_{i})\rVert_{\wp_{U}}<\delta_{1}
, then the ring homomorphism
f^{\prime}:R(G)\rightarrow F_{\wp_{U}}
defined by setting

has the property that
S(G)\otimes_{f_{E_{\wp_{U}}}}F_{\wp_{U}}\cong S(G)\otimes_{f^{\prime}}F_{\wp_{U}}
as
G
-Galois
F_{\wp_{U}}
-algebras. Similarly, there exists a
\delta_{2}>0
such that if
c_{i}\in F_{\wp_{U^{\prime}}}

-Galois extensions of

.

6
Main Theorem

We will now prove the main theorem using a patching argument which follows the strategy employed by Harbater, Hartmann and Krashen in section 4 of
[
HHK11
]
.

In the proof we will use induced algebras so we record the definition of an induced algebra in this context below for convenience.

Definition 6.1
.

If
F
is a field,
G
is a finite group with subgroup
H
, and
E
is an
H
-Galois
F
-algebra, then we define the induced
G
-Galois
F
-algebra,
\text{Ind}_{H}^{G}E
, as follows. Let
(G:H)=m
and fix
C=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{m}\}
, a set of left coset representatives. As an
F
-algebra,
\text{Ind}_{H}^{G}E
is the direct product of
m
copies of
E
indexed by the elements of
C
. To define the
G
-action we note that given
\sigma\in G
, and
\sigma_{i}\in C
, we have
\sigma\sigma_{i}\in\sigma_{j(i)}H
for some unique coset
\sigma_{j(i)}H
depending on
i
. So there is some
h_{i}\in H
such that
\sigma\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j(i)}h_{i}
and we define

\sigma\cdot(c_{\sigma_{1}},c_{\sigma_{2}},\dots,c_{\sigma_{m}}):=(x_{\sigma_{1}},x_{\sigma_{2}},\dots,x_{\sigma_{m}}),

where
x_{\sigma_{j(i)}}=h_{i}\cdot c_{\sigma_{i}}
. This action permutes the entries by coset and acts within each copy of
E
by the original
H
-action.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
K
be a complete discretely valued field with algebraically closed residue field
k
, and let F be a finitely generated field extension of K with transcendence degree 1. Assume further that
\text{char}(K)=\text{char}(k)
. A finite group
G
is admissible over
F
if and only if the
q
-Sylow subgroups of
G
are abelian of rank at most 2 for every prime
q\neq\text{char}(k)
.

Proof.

In the case that
\text{char}(k)\nmid|G|
, this result is precisely Theorem 4.5 in
[
HHK11
]
, and in the case
\text{char}(k)=p\mid|G|
the forward direction is implied by Proposition 3.5 in
[
HHK11
]
. Therefore we only need to prove that the conditions placed on the
q
-Sylow subgroups of
G
are sufficient in the
\text{char}(k)=p\mid|G|
case.

First we set some notation. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
be all of the distinct primes dividing
n:=|G|
and order them so that
p_{1}=p
. So we can write
n=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}p_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots p_{l}^{\alpha_{l}}
, where
\alpha_{i}>0
. For each prime
p_{i}
, we fix a
p_{i}
-Sylow subgroup
H_{i}
of
G
.

We begin with an outline of the proof that follows. First we obtain a good model of
F
and check that it satisfies the conditions imposed in Section
5
. Then we build an adequate extension for each Sylow subgroup over a point in the model, for the
p
-Sylow subgroup this will be done over a special point in the model. We will then invoke results from Section
5
to build compatible structures on a portion of the model. In the next step we explain how to fill in the rest of the model with trivial patches for the maximal separable subalgebras and show that the induced structures over all of the fields in our framework are compatible and therefore form a patching problem. We patch these together to obtain a
G
-Galois
F
-algebra which we call
L
. This process is repeated with the central simple algebras that contain the maximal separable algebras. Finally, the patched central simple algebra over
F
, which we call
A
, is shown to be a division algebra which then implies that
L
is a maximal subfield of
A
, completing the proof.

Establishing a Good Model

Let
\widehat{X}
be a regular model of
F
and let
X
denote the closed fiber of
\widehat{X}
. For each
1\leq i\leq l
we pick a distinct closed point on
X
,
P_{i}
, at which
X
is regular. Now let
b:\widehat{X}_{B}\rightarrow\widehat{X}
be the blow-up of
\widehat{X}
at
P_{1}
. We note that
\widehat{X}_{B}
is also a regular model of
F
.

We let
X_{B}
denote the closed fiber of
\widehat{X}_{B}
and note that
X_{B}
is the union of
b^{-1}(X\backslash\{P_{1}\})
and
b^{-1}(P_{1})
where
b^{-1}(X\backslash\{P_{1}\})\cong X\backslash\{P_{1}\}
and
b^{-1}(P_{1})\cong\mathbb{P}_{k(P_{1})}^{1}
. We also know that the strict transform of
X
in
\widehat{X}_{B}
,
W:=\overline{b^{-1}(X\backslash\{P_{1}\})}
, is isomorphic to
X
. This is because we can apply Proposition IV-21 in
[
EH06
]
to the closed subscheme
X
of
\widehat{X}
. The proposition implies that
b|_{W}:W\rightarrow X
is the blow-up of
X
at
P_{1}
. Since
P_{1}
is already an effective Cartier divisor of
X
, the universal property of the blow-up implies that
b|_{W}
is an isomorphism.

So, for each
1\leq i\leq l
, we let
P^{\prime}_{i}
denote the unique point in
W
that is mapped to
P_{i}
by
b
. Each
P^{\prime}_{i}
is a distinct closed point on
X_{B}
, and
X_{B}
is regular at
P^{\prime}_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq l
. Note that
P^{\prime}_{1}
is a point on
b^{-1}(P_{1})
and we can pick another closed point,
P
on
b^{-1}(P_{1})
different from
P^{\prime}_{1}
.

We let
\mathscr{P}
be the set consisting of all points on
X_{B}
at which irreducible components of
X_{B}
meet, as well as the points
P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l}
and
P
. Then we have the set
\mathscr{W}
of all connected components of
X_{B}\backslash\mathscr{P}
, and the set
\mathscr{B}
of all branches of
X_{B}
at the points in
\mathscr{P}
as described in Notation
2.4
. At each
\xi\in I:=\mathscr{P}\cup\mathscr{W}\cup\mathscr{B}
we also have the associated field
F_{\xi}
, and ring
\widehat{R_{\xi}}
, also defined in Notation
2.4
.

Building Sylow Adequate Extensions

For
2\leq i\leq l
,
P^{\prime}_{i}
is a closed point at which
X_{B}
is regular. Therefore, there is a unique branch
\wp_{i}
of
X_{B}
at
P^{\prime}_{i}
. We also know that
F
(and therefore
F_{P^{\prime}_{i}}
) contains a primitive
p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
-th root of unity. This is because
p_{i}\neq\text{char}(k)
and
k
is algebraically closed. By assumption,
H_{i}
, a
p_{i}
-Sylow subgroup of
G
, is an abelian group of rank at most 2 and so there exists a field extension
E_{P^{\prime}_{i}}/F_{P^{\prime}_{i}}
and a division algebra
D_{P^{\prime}_{i}}
central over
F_{P^{\prime}_{i}}
containing
E_{P^{\prime}_{i}}
as a maximal subfield such that
E_{P^{\prime}_{i}}/F_{P^{\prime}_{i}}
is
H_{i}
-Galois and
E_{P^{\prime}_{i}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}_{i}}}F_{\wp_{i}}
is split. The constructions of these extensions and division algebras is described in detail in the proof of proposition 4.4 in
[
HHK11
]
.

Now we note that
X_{B}
is also regular at
P
and so there is a unique branch of
X_{B}
at
P
which we call
\wp
. We know that
\widehat{R_{P}}
is an equicharacteristic complete regular local ring of dimension 2 with residue field
k(P)
. Since
H_{1}
is a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
it is a finite p-group, so by Proposition
4.8
there exists an
H_{1}
-Galois field extension
E_{P}/F_{P}
and a division algebra
D_{P}
that contains
E_{P}
as a maximal subfield such that
E_{P}
is unramified at
\wp
and
D_{P}
splits over
\wp
.

Compatibility on the Exceptional Fiber

Since
b^{-1}(P_{1})\cong\mathbb{P}_{k(P_{1})}^{1}
, and
\mathscr{P}\cap b^{-1}(P_{1})=\{P,P^{\prime}_{1}\}
, we know that
b^{-1}(P_{1})\backslash\{P,P^{\prime}_{1}\}
(which is isomorphic to the affine line over
k(P_{1})
with one point removed) is a connected component of
X_{B}\backslash\mathscr{P}
. In other words,
U:=b^{-1}(P_{1})\backslash\{P,P^{\prime}_{1}\}
is an element of
\mathscr{W}
. Since
P^{\prime}_{1}
is in the closure of
U
, there is a branch of
X_{B}
at
P^{\prime}_{1}
lying along
U
, which we denote
\wp_{U}
.

We observe that
P^{\prime}_{1}
is contained in the closure of exactly one other connected component of
X_{B}\backslash\mathscr{P}
. This is because every element of
\mathscr{W}
other than
U
is contained in
W
and is therefore isomorphic to a connected component of
X\backslash\{b(Q):Q\in\mathscr{P}\cap W\}
. So if
U^{\prime}
is an element of
\mathscr{W}
with
P^{\prime}_{1}\in\overline{U^{\prime}}
and
U^{\prime}\neq U
, then
b(U^{\prime})
is a connected component of
X\backslash\{b(Q):Q\in\mathscr{P}\cap W\}
with
P_{1}\in\overline{b(U)}
, with the closure taken in
X
. Since
X
is regular at
P_{1}
, there is only one such component. We let
U^{\prime}
denote the unique element of
\mathscr{W}
described above, and we let
\wp_{1}
denote the branch of
X_{B}
at
P^{\prime}_{1}
along
U^{\prime}
. This discussion illustrates that the semi-global patching framework
(\widehat{X}_{B},\mathscr{I},\mathscr{F})
satisfies the conditions outlined in the beginning of Section
5
. The notation we use is only slightly different from that used in Section
5
, with
P^{\prime}_{1}
corresponding to
P^{\prime}
and
\wp_{1}
corresponding to
\wp_{U^{\prime}}
in that section.

By the lemmas in Section
5
, we have
H_{1}
-Galois algebras
E_{U}
and
E_{P^{\prime}_{1}}
over
F_{U}
and
F_{P^{\prime}_{1}}
respectively. These are embedded inside algebras
C_{U}:=\Delta(E_{U},H_{1},1)
and
C_{P^{\prime}_{1}}:=\Delta(E_{P^{\prime}_{1}},H_{1},1)
which are central simple algebras over the respective fields. Furthermore, these central simple algebras and the designated subalgebras satisfy the compatibility conditions over
F_{\wp}
that were outlined in those lemmas. We let
E_{\wp}:=E_{P}\otimes_{F_{P}}F_{\wp}
and
E_{\wp_{U}}:=E_{P^{\prime}_{1}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}_{1}}}F_{\wp_{U}}
, and we note that
E_{P^{\prime}_{1}}
splits over
\wp_{1}
by construction, so
E_{P^{\prime}_{1}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}_{1}}}F_{\wp_{1}}\cong F_{\wp_{1}}^{|H_{1}|}
.

Filling in Trivial Patches

For all of the remaining fields in the patching framework (i.e. for all
F_{\xi}
with
\xi\in I\backslash\{P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l},P,U,\wp,\wp_{U}\}
) we set
E_{\xi}:=F_{\xi}
. For consistency, we will use
H_{\xi}
to denote the subgroup of
G
associated to the
F_{\xi}
-algebra
E_{\xi}
for all
\xi\in I
. So
H_{P^{\prime}_{i}}=H_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq l
, additionally
H_{P}=H_{U}=H_{\wp}=H_{\wp_{U}}=H_{1}
and for all other
\xi\in I
,
H_{\xi}=1
. Now we define for all
\xi\in I
a
G
-Galois
F_{\xi}
-algebra by setting
L_{\xi}:=\text{Ind}_{H_{\xi}}^{G}E_{\xi}
as in Definition
6.1
.

Recall that given

\xi
.

In the case that

(Q,V)\in\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{W}
is such that

\tilde{\phi}_{Q,\xi}^{E}:\text{Ind}_{H_{Q}}^{G}(E_{Q}\otimes_{F_{Q}}F_{\xi})\rightarrow\text{Ind}_{H_{Q}}^{G}(\text{Ind}_{1}^{H_{Q}}F_{\xi})

we get an isomorphism
\phi_{Q,\xi}:L_{Q}\otimes_{F_{Q}}F_{\xi}\rightarrow L_{\xi}
. Similarly,
\phi_{V,\xi}^{E}:E_{V}\otimes_{F_{V}}F_{\xi}\xrightarrow{\sim}F_{\xi}
so we have an induced
G

\tilde{\phi}_{V,\xi}^{E}:\text{Ind}_{1}^{G}(E_{V}\otimes_{F_{V}}F_{\xi})\rightarrow\text{Ind}_{1}^{G}F_{\xi}
which gives a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
\phi_{V,\xi}:L_{V}\otimes_{F_{V}}F_{\xi}\rightarrow L_{\xi}
.

In the case that
\xi=\wp
, which is associated to
(P,U)

F_{\wp}

\phi_{U,\wp}:L_{U}\otimes_{F_{U}}F_{\wp}\rightarrow L_{\wp}
is
G
-equivariant.

The same argument holds for
\xi=\wp_{U}
, associated to
(P^{\prime}_{1},U)

H_{1}
-equivariant isomorphism
E_{U}\otimes_{F_{U}}F_{\wp_{U}}\cong E_{P^{\prime}_{1}}\otimes_{F_{P^{\prime}_{1}}}F_{\wp_{U}}=E_{\wp_{U}}
is established in Lemma

. Therefore by Lemma 4.2 in
[

, there exists a
G
-Galois
F
-algebra,

, with the property that for all
\xi\in I

-algebras) in a manner compatible with the maps

and

for each

associated to
(Q,V)

Repeated Process with CSA’s

Now for each
\xi\in I
we construct a central simple algebra over
F_{\xi}
. For
\xi\in\{P^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime}_{3},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l},P\}

D_{\xi}

A_{\xi}
consisting of all diagonal matrices with entries in
E_{\xi}
is a maximal commutative subalgebra of
A_{\xi}
. Furthermore, this subalgebra is isomorphic to
E_{\xi}^{(G:H_{\xi})}

G
-Galois
F_{\xi}

G
-action on

G
-action on

. Therefore we have a
G
-equivariant injective homomorphism
i_{\xi}:L_{\xi}\rightarrow A_{\xi}
such that the image of
i_{\xi}
is the subalgebra of
A_{\xi}
of diagonal matrices with entries in
E_{\xi}
.

Similarly, for
\xi\in\{P^{\prime}_{1},U\}
, we set
A_{\xi}:=\text{Mat}_{(G:H_{\xi})}(C_{\xi})
and again we have an embedding of
L_{\xi}
in

as the set of diagonal matrices with entries in
E_{\xi}
, a maximal commutative subalgebra. For
\xi=\wp

where again

can be embedded in
A_{\wp}
as a maximal commutative subalgebra by mapping to diagonal matrices with entries in
E_{\wp}=E_{P}\otimes_{F_{P}}F_{\wp}
. Similarly, we set

L_{\wp_{U}}
is embedded in
A_{\wp_{U}}
as diagonal matrices with entries in
E_{\wp_{U}}

Patched Algebra is Division

We observe that
A
is in fact a division algebra over
F
. This is because the degree of
A
is equal to the index of
A
. We see this by noting that for
\xi\in\{P^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime}_{3},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l},P\}
, we have
\text{ind}(A_{\xi})=n/(G:H_{\xi})
. This is because over these points
A_{\xi}
is constructed as a matrix algebra over a division algebra
D_{\xi}
and the degree of
D_{\xi}
is equal to the degree of its maximal subfield
E_{\xi}
(as an extension of
F_{\xi}
) which is
n/(G:H_{\xi})
. In the set
\{H_{\xi}:\xi\in\{P^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime}_{3},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l},P\}\}
we have a Sylow subgroup of
G
for every prime divisor of
n=|G|
so
n=\text{lcm}\{n/(G:H_{\xi}):\xi\in\{P^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime}_{3},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l},P\}\}
. We also know that
\text{ind}(A_{\xi})\mid\text{ind}(A)
(Proposition 13.4 (iv) in
[
Pie82
]
) and that
\text{ind}(A)\mid\text{deg}(A)
. Taken all together this shows that

\text{deg}(A)=n=\text{lcm}\{\text{ind}(A_{\xi}):\xi\in\{P^{\prime}_{2},P^{\prime}_{3},\dots,P^{\prime}_{l},P\}\}\mid\text{ind}(A)\mid\text{deg}(A).

Therefore it must be that
\text{deg}(A)=\text{ind}(A)
so
A
is a division algebra over
F
. This in turn implies that
L
, as a commutative subalgebra of a finite dimensional division algebra, is actually a domain and therefore a field. Since
L
is a maximal commutative subalgebra it must be a maximal subfield of
A
, and as it is
G
-Galois over
F
, we conclude that
G
is
F
-admissible.

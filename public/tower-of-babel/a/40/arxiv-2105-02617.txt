Towards the Doran-Harder-Thompson conjecture via the Gross-Siebert program
Lawrence J. Barrott, Charles F. Doran

Abstract
The Doran-Harder-Thompson “gluing/splitting” conjecture unifies mirror symmetry conjectures for Calabi-Yau and Fano varieties, relating fibration structures on Calabi-Yau varieties to the existence of certain types of degenerations on their mirrors. This was studied for the case of Calabi-Yau complete intersections in toric varieties in [ 12 ] for the Hori-Vafa mirror construction. In this paper we prove one direction of the conjecture using a modified version of the Gross-Siebert program. This involves a careful study of the implications within tropical geometry and applying modern deformation theory for singular Calabi-Yau varieties.



Mirror symmetry started with the study of Calabi-Yau manifolds, predicting a duality associating to any Calabi-Yau
X
a mirror partner
\check{X}
. As it grew it came to incorporate other statements, most notably it predicts a similar duality associating to any Fano variety and anti-canonical divisor
(Z,D)
a Landau-Ginzburg model, a variety
\check{W}
and a super-potential
w:\check{W}\to\mathbb{C}
. The theory of Landau-Ginzburg models was formalised in
[
1
]
where these were required to be a fibration of
\check{X}
by lower dimensional Calabi-Yau varieties.

The two worlds, Calabi-Yau and Fano, meet in the example of Tyurin degenerations. A Tyurin degeneration is a semi-stable degeneration
\mathfrak{X}_{t}\to\Delta
such that the central fibre is the union of two smooth components
\mathfrak{X}_{0}=\mathfrak{Z}_{1}\cup\mathfrak{Z}_{2}
meeting along a smooth divisor
\mathfrak{D}
. From the work of Kawamata-Nakayama
\mathfrak{D}
is an effective anti-canonical divisor on the components
\mathfrak{Z}_{i}
, and the normal bundles are dual,
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{Z}_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{Z}_{0}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{D}}
, and this is a necessary and sufficient condition for such smoothings to exist. This was expanded on by Kato in
[
13
]
where he proved that this condition is equivalent to the existence of a log structure of semi-stable type (in modern language locally free) on the central fibre, log smooth over the standard log point. This generalises the Kawamata-Nakayama condition since there are surgeries permitted by log geometry which produce central fibres necessarily smoothable but not of the type they consider, where the central fibre is a chain of components. The most basic example would be to blow up
D
inside the total space, to obtain a smoothable central fibre with three smooth components,
\mathcal{Z}_{0}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{1}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{2}
meeting along two copies of
\mathcal{D}
, say
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
. Note that it is not true that we have a balancing condition
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}/\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}/\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}
, instead we only have
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}/\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}_{1}/\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}/\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{D}_{2}/\mathcal{Z}_{2}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
.

We will study higher order degenerations extending the definition of Tyurin degenerations. This produces a notion of higher rank Landau-Ginzburg model, appearing in situations where we have not a smooth divisor pair, but an snc divisor pair.

.

be a family such that
\mathfrak{X}_{gen}
and

are both smooth and the central fibre is an snc divisor whose smallest strata is codimension

There is relatively little literature on higher rank LG models. A sensible restriction is to look at those families such that

Theorem 0.4
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}

is given by an explicit log smoothing of a combinatorially determined central fibre. It is a smoothing of a singular space over a monoid ring of effective curve classes in

We define a compatibility of toric degenerations and Tyurin degenerations, and in general between toric and type
k
degenerations with smooth total space. From this we find a fibration of a component of the mirror before we smooth. It is not clear at the moment how to simultaneously smooth both
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}_{0}}
and a Calabi-Yau subvariety inside it purely using the techniques of the program. The algebra map on the central fibre is clear, but there are quantum corrections that occur during the smoothing procedure which we cannot control.

Thankfully there has been a large amount of progress in the past year on how to smooth log Calabi-Yau varieties using scattering diagrams. For us the most notable are recent advances first by Chan-Leung-Ma in
[
5
]
and then by Felten-Filip-Ruddat in
[
31
]
. These perturb the Maurer-Cartan equation in an iterative way to construct a formal, and then analytic, smoothing. The problem of smoothing complexes was studied in
[
7
]
where the authors give a smoothing criterion for locally free complexes, which is not satisfied by our examples. Following classical deformation theory we prove the smoothability of a Calabi-Yau variety and a Calabi-Yau subvariety with trivial normal bundle by directly relating the BV-algebras used in the correction process. This also shows that if the normal bundle is not trivial then the subspace is at least stable. This result is perhaps of independent interest for constructing subvarieties tropically:

Theorem 0.5

(Theorem
2.12
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a log toroidal family over
\Spec k^{\dagger}
, and
\mathfrak{D}\subset\mathfrak{X}
a strictly embedded complete intersection Calabi-Yau subvariety transverse to the singularities of the log structure on

over
\Spec k[\![t]\!]

This statement could be proved by other means, showing that the normal bundle in this case describes locally isotrivial smoothings. Our eventual goal would be to construct a scattering process that extends this result to the intrinsic construction of
[
25

. This requires that we study an appropriate differential graded Lie algebra (dgLa) via this more complicated machinery.

Finally we apply relative deformation theory to the interior of the LG models and the fibration. This process only applies on the level of formal geometry, as relative deformation theory over an affine base is not very well behaved in the algebraic or analytic categories. We prove that
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}_{0}}
restricted to the interior
\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}
is a versal deformation space for families of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}
-bundles under a surjectivity assumption that can be checked on the mirror. If
\widecheck{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}
has one dimensional deformation space this allows us to produce an open formal embedding of

into
\mathfrak{X}
.

In the case of
k
-Tyurin degenerations the analogous statement requires a surjectivity
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}})\to H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{D}}})
. For the simple toric degenerations we consider this can be studied on the mirror.

Comments on restrictions

There are four comments we must make about this work. Firstly we do not know how to construct a quasi-Fano variety directly from a Landau-Ginzburg model. There is the work of Prince in
[
28
]
which has partial solutions but this does not apply in the generality we are looking for. This makes the converse of the theorem impossible to resolve using these techniques in complete generality.

On the other hand the statement that the mirror to a fibred Calabi-Yau admits a Tyurin degeneration is tractable. In this case there is a canonical affine direction on the base of the mirror, corresponding to pairing with a fibre class. Once one smooths perpendicularly to this one should obtain a log scheme which is log smooth over the standard log point, which by
[
13
]
is enough to produce a Tyurin degeneration. This requires a much stronger analysis of the local structure of partial smoothings of the log structure than appears in say
[
17
]
.

The third comment is that we are restricted here to working in the case where
\mathfrak{X}
is not dimension two. This is to avoid a polarisation issue that is intrinsic to the geometry of
K3
surfaces. So long as the tropicalisation
\Sigma(\mathfrak{X})
has vanishing
H^{2}
every choice of open gluing data for the fan picture gives rise to a choice of gluing data for the cone picture and our construction is written directly in terms of the cone picture. A more detailed study of the
K3
surface case and its subtleties will be performed in
[
15
]
.

Since there are already subtleties of mirror symmetry for
K3
surfaces that have not been addressed in the wider literature we pass on addressing this.

The final comment is that it is possible to prove that the mirror is fibred directly from the recent paper
[
25
]
. Unfortunately it is not immediately clear what it is fibred by. Essentially the toric degeneration assumption here forces enough local rigidity to allow us to study the scattering diagram through other deformation theoretic means. Historically this has precedent, the work of
[
25
]
came only after the work of
[
17
]
. In future work we intend to use the gluing calculations of
[
9
]
and
[
35
]
and the period calculations of
[
29
]
to prove the period gluing formulae of
[
12
]
from the perspective of tropical curves, even though we do not know a precise method to prove that fibres are mirror to
\mathfrak{D}
.

. But by the above observations the scattering diagram is consistent to order zero. Therefore applying the construction of
[
17
]
to this now bounded affine manifold we obtain a pair
(\overline{\mathfrak{W}_{0}},\partial\mathfrak{W}_{0})
and the open subscheme
\overline{\mathfrak{W}_{0}}\setminus\partial\mathfrak{W}_{0}
admits a map to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
given by
u
. This fits into the philosophy of
[
23
]
where one expects to see a compactification at infinity with a canonical choice of differential form on the interior.

Such a compactification exists for higher rank Tyurin degenerations at least so long as each of the components themselves have nef anti-canonical bundle. We believe that the restriction to this case is a necessary first step to understanding the “Mirror
P=W
conjecture” of
[
2
]
through the Gross-Siebert construction. The compactifications have the property that there are no broken lines passing in from infinity. In particular one obtains a compactification of the base
\mathcal{B}
,
\overline{\mathcal{B}}
by an
snc
divisor and a relative compactification of
\mathfrak{W}
which is relatively log divisorial over
\overline{\mathcal{B}}
and possesses a strata of dimension
d
over a
d
-dimensional strata of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}
.

3.2
Relative deformation theory

To prove the existence of the desired embedding we construct a relative deformation theory for the interior of the Landau-Ginzburg models. This is simple on the central fibre where we know precisely where the singular fibres are, the same techniques apply to relatively smooth families of Calabi-Yau varieties with minor modifications.

To begin with we have
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Spec\widehat{k[P]}
a formally versal smoothing of a toric Calabi-Yau space fibred over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a fixed
\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
a dense affine open with fibres generically divisorial smoothings of
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
. We have an LG model
w_{0}:\mathfrak{W}_{0}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\Spec\widehat{k[P]}
and again a dense open
\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}
whose fibres are generically divisorial smoothings of
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
. Over
0\in\Spec\widehat{k[P]}
the superpotential
\mathfrak{W}_{0}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
admits compatible open embeddings into
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We write
.^{\circ}
for the restriction of an object to the corresponding open
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

Our goal is to produce an open embedding of
\mathfrak{W}_{0}
into
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
. We begin by introducing relative toric CY spaces and our choice of formal deformation functor.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{B}}
be the category of log schemes strict over
\Spec k[\mathbb{N}]
whose underlying scheme is an infinitesimal extension of
\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{B}
a fixed affine scheme. This category inherits a notion of small extension from the notion for the underlying schemes, noting that all morphisms are strict.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{B}^{\dagger}:=\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k^{\dagger}
be a morphism of log schemes such that:

(i)

The log scheme
\mathfrak{Y}
is a toric Calabi-Yau space.

(ii)

\rho
is flat as a morphism of schemes and
\rho
is log smooth on the log smooth locus of
\mathfrak{Y}

(iii)

There are diagrams as appearing in
[
24
]
(2.1) but with
X^{\dagger}
replaced by
X^{\dagger}\times\mathfrak{B}
.

A relative divisorial deformation of
\rho
over
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{B}}
is a map
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{A}
and an isomorphism of the central fibre
\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}}\times_{\mathcal{A}}\Spec k^{\dagger}\cong\mathfrak{Y}
such that:

(i)

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
is flat as a morphism of schemes and log smooth on the restriction to the log smooth locus of
\mathfrak{Y}
.

(ii)

There are diagrams as appearing in
[
24
]
(2.2) but with
X^{\dagger}_{A}:=Y^{\dagger}\times_{\Spec k[\mathbb{N}]}\Spec A^{\dagger}
replaced by
X^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}}:=Y^{\dagger}\times_{\Spec k[\mathbb{N}]}\mathcal{A}
.

The deformation functor
F
we consider will be have
\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}
the common dense open sending an element
\mathcal{A}
to the set of relative divisorial deformations over it, where two relative divisorial deformations are isomorphic if there is an isomorphism fixing the central fibre. The action on morphisms is given by pullback.

We first of all claim that this deformation problem has a deformation and obstruction sheaf on
\mathfrak{B}
. To be precise we mean that the analogue of Theorem 2.11 of
[
24
]
holds, but with
\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/k^{\dagger}}
replaced everywhere with
\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}^{\dagger}}
. The proof is simply compatibility of these sheaves with base change and the previous result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{A}
be elements of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{B}}
, with
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
a small extension of
\mathcal{A}
by an ideal
I
, finite over
\mathfrak{B}
. Let
a\in F(\mathcal{A})
be a divisorial deformation of
\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{B}^{\dagger}
. Then:

(i)

Let
\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}
be a lift of
\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}}
to
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
. Then the set of log automorphisms of
\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}
fixing
\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}}
is:

R^{0}\rho_{*}(\mathfrak{Y},\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}I)

(ii)

The set of equivalence classes of lifts
\mathfrak{Y}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
if non-empty is a torsor over:

R^{1}\rho_{*}(\mathfrak{Y},\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}I)

(iii)

An obstruction sheaf for the existence of liftings is:

R^{2}\rho_{*}(\mathfrak{Y},\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}I)

Let us check that the relative log versions of the Schlessinger axioms hold.

Theorem 3.3
.

The following three axioms hold:

(i)

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}
be a small extension and
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
be a surjective morphism, then the canonical map:

F(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\times_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B})\to F(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\times_{F(\mathcal{A})}F(\mathcal{B})

is surjective.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{A}=\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{B}+\mathcal{M}
for a finite module over
\mathfrak{B}
. Then the above map is bijective.

(iii)

For a finite
\mathfrak{B}
module
M
the set
F(\mathfrak{B}\oplus M)
is a finite
\mathfrak{B}
-module.

Proof.

The third item follows from the above theorem together with relative properness of
\rho
. The second follows from the first once one notices that isomorphisms are preserved under deformation, see Lemma 9.1 of
[
13
]
. Therefore it remains to prove the first. Suppose we are given the data of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}
a small extension and
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
a surjective morphism. For objects
X^{\prime}\in F(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and
X^{\prime\prime}\in F(\mathcal{B})
, both restricting to
X\in F(\mathcal{A})
. Then we can fix closed immersions
X\to X^{\prime}
and
X\to X^{\prime\prime}
. We then define
\underline{X^{*}}
to be the scheme whose structure sheaf is
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X^{\prime}}}\times_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}}}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X^{\prime\prime}}}
. We give this the log structure given by
\mathcal{M}_{X^{\prime}}\times_{\mathcal{M}_{X}}\mathcal{M}_{M^{\prime\prime}}
, by the universal property of products it admits a map to
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X^{\prime}}}\times_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}}}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X^{\prime\prime}}}
. That this is a log scheme with the desired restriction properties is proved in section 9 of
[
13
]
. Condition i) of the definition of relative divisorial deformations is trivial, flat deformations of log smooth morphisms remain log smooth. We briefly prove condition ii) of the definition,
X^{*}
admits étale locally maps to
X^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\times_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}}
whose central fibres are étale. But flat deformations of an étale map remain étale and so we have the result.

∎

We cannot apply the machinery built by Artin directly, even if it were translated into logarithmic geometry for the simple reason that we do not know openness of versality for log deformations. Raffaele Caputo has some results in this direction using analytic techniques in the absolute case, see
[
4
]
. Instead we argue directly, that we can construct a versal family over an open affine subset of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
by inducing one from the deformations of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}/\Spec k})\to H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{D}}_{0}/\Spec k})
is surjective under a splitting of the morphism
\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}/\Spec k}\to\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}/\Spec k}\mid_{\mathfrak{D}_{0}}\to\Theta_{\mathfrak{D}_{0}/\Spec k}
for some initial choice of fibre. Then the space
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}\to\Spec k[\![P]\!]
induces a versal family over some choice of
\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

We prove this in several steps, first we relate the absolute and relative deformation theories (depending of course on a choice of splitting).

Lemma 3.5
.

There is a non-canonical morphism
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k})\otimes_{k}k[x^{\pm}]\to R^{1}\pi_{*}(\Theta_{\mathfrak{W}_{i}/\mathcal{T}_{i}})^{\circ}
.

Proof.

First take the relative tangent triangle for the triple of morphisms
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\Spec k^{\dagger}
, and push it forward under
\rho
. This produces an exact sequence on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

R\rho_{*}\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to R\rho_{*}\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k^{\dagger}}\to R\rho_{*}\rho^{*}\Theta_{\mathbb{P}^{1}/\Spec k^{\dagger}}

applying the projection formula to the last term we see that the cokernel of
R^{1}\rho_{*}\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}\to R^{1}\rho_{*}\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k^{\dagger}}
is torsion supported on the singular fibres. In particular over
\mathbb{G}_{m}
it is split. Pulling back to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and applying cohomology and base change we obtain a map:

(R^{1}\rho_{*}\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\mathbb{P}^{1}})^{\circ}\cong R^{1}\rho_{*}^{\circ}\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}^{\circ}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}

The Grothendieck spectral sequence gives a map
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k^{\dagger}})\to\Gamma(R^{1}\rho_{*}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\mathbb{P}^{1}}))
, and since
\mathbb{G}_{m}
is affine composing with a choice of splitting we get the desired map.

∎

By our assumptions on the surjectivity of the map
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}/\Spec k})\to H^{1}(\Theta_{\mathfrak{D}_{0}/\Spec k})
there is an open dense
\mathfrak{B}\subset\mathbb{G}_{m}
where the above map is surjective on fibres. This is our choice of
\mathfrak{B}
.

We lift this formal statement about differentials to show that we can glue the formal smoothings:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}\to\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]
be a divisorial family deforming a trivial
\mathfrak{D}_{0}
bundle. Then there is a non-canonical map
\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]\to\mathfrak{B}\times\mathbb{A}(H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k^{\dagger}}))
.

Proof.

For
t=0
there is a canonical such map. We now induct on the order, suppose that we have a choice of map
\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]/\langle t^{k}\rangle\to\mathfrak{B}\times\mathbb{A}(H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k}))
for some
k
. Then from the above analysis the lift of
\mathfrak{F}
to
\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]/\langle t^{k}\rangle
defines an element of
R^{1}\rho_{*}(\mathfrak{Y},\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathfrak{B})
, up to an element of
R^{0}\rho_{*}(\mathfrak{Y},\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathfrak{B})
. But the map
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k})\otimes_{k}k[x^{\pm}]\to R^{1}\rho_{*}(\mathfrak{Y},\Theta_{\mathfrak{Y}/\mathfrak{B}}\otimes_{\mathfrak{B}}\mathfrak{B})
is surjective and so we may lift it to an element of
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k})\otimes_{k}k[x^{\pm}]
.

But this gives a lift of
\mathfrak{F}
inside
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
, hence an embedding
\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]/\langle t^{k+1}\rangle\to\mathfrak{B}\times\mathbb{A}(H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k}))
.

∎

We need only one more tool to prove the claimed theorem.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
h^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{D}}/\Spec k})=1
. Then there is a formal automorphism of
\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]
such that the induced map
\mathfrak{B}\times\Spec k[\![t]\!]\to\mathfrak{B}\times\mathbb{A}(H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k}))
sends elements of
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k})
to pure powers of
t
.

Proof.

This follows from the formal version of the inverse function theorem applied to generators of
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}/\Spec k^{\dagger}})
.

∎

We can now prove the existence of the embedding:

Theorem 3.8
.

There is a formal open embedding of
\mathfrak{W}^{0}
into
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
compatible with the fibration structure.

Proof.

Suppose we have a divisorial smoothing of
\mathfrak{W}^{0}
over
\Spec k[\![t]\!]
. The same trick as
3.6
produces a relative deformation of the fibres. Then we can find an embedding of the restriction of this smoothing to
\mathfrak{B}
horizontally into the smoothings of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
restricted to
\mathfrak{B}
. We take the corresponding degeneration of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
over
\Spec k[\![t]\!]
. Then there is a common dense open
\mathfrak{U}
of
\mathfrak{W}^{i}
and
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
which we can identify.

Remove from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
the set
\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\mathfrak{B}\cup\{0\}
to obtain an open subset of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
and glue along the open dense set found above. This produces a family which is locally everywhere a divisorial deformation of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}
over
\Spec k[\![t]\!]
. But the family
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
is a versal deformation space for divisorial deformations of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}
, and hence this lifts to an open embedding of
\mathfrak{W}^{i}
into a family of deformations of
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}
, which is the desired gluing statement.

∎

Example 3.9
.

The mirror to a quintic threefold, a sextic fourfold, etc, can be constructed as a specialisation of a family of Calabi-Yau’s obtained by gluing the two Landau-Ginzburg models obtained as mirrors to the components of the Tyurin degeneration constructed by blowing up both sides evenly.

The same technique works for higher rank LG models under some comparable restrictions so long as one works with formal complex analytic spaces. The restriction is introduced part way through the construction, after we have introduced some terminology.

Construction 3.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}\to\mathcal{S}
be a type
k+1
toric-Tyurin degeneration. There are two associated tropical structures. The first is the tropicalisation of the type
k+1
locus which defines an affine manifold with singularities
\Xi_{gen}=\Sigma(\mathfrak{X}_{gen})
but without a fan structure around the vertices. The second is the tropicalisation of the central fibre, defining for us an affine manifold with singularities
\Xi_{0}=\Sigma(\mathfrak{X}_{0})
with a fan structure around the vertices. There is a canonical map
\rho_{\Xi}:\Xi_{gen}\to\Xi_{0}
given by specialisation. We take the vanilla gluing data on
\Xi_{0}
and
\Xi_{gen}

To this data we have a reduced reducible scheme given by taking the cone picture on
\Xi_{gen}
. We write
\mathcal{B}_{gen}=\Proj k[\Xi_{gen}]
for this scheme, noting that without any more integral affine structure we cannot deform this even to first order. The map
\rho_{\Xi}
lifts to a map
\rho_{\mathfrak{X}}:\Proj k[\Xi_{0}]\to\Proj k[\Xi_{gen}]
.

We are now ready for our assumption. Choose a zero dimensional strata
b\in\Xi_{gen}
corresponding to a component of the general fibre over the Tyurin degenerate locus
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
. Suppose that the induced degeneration of
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
is toric, simple and the boundary divisors of
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
are nef. This furnishes us with a collection of top dimensional strata of
\Xi_{gen}
,
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
corresponding to the zero-dimensional strata of
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
and intersecting at
b
. We write
\mathfrak{D}_{i}
for the Calabi-Yau variety corresponding to
\sigma_{i}
, and we assume that all the induced degenerations are simple. Locally there is a morphism of affine manifolds with singularities
\rho_{\Sigma}:\Xi_{gen}\to\Sigma(\mathfrak{Z}^{0},\partial\mathfrak{Z}^{0})
.

We can construct a higher rank
LG
model as the mirror to
(\mathfrak{Z}^{0},\partial\mathfrak{Z}^{0})
, by taking the induced tropical map to be evaluation against the irreducible strata of
\partial\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
and we write this
\mathfrak{W}\to\mathfrak{B}
. The base
\mathfrak{B}
is obtained by gluing a collection of toric varieties along toric strata, and each
\sigma_{i}
corresponds to a dense torus in a toric component. By assumption there is a unique zero dimensional strata which we are free to conflate with
b
. Over the interior of each
\sigma_{i}
the map
\rho_{\mathfrak{X}}
is a locally trivial
\mathfrak{D}_{i}
bundle. In particular it is a relatively divisorial family. We assume that the map
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}/\Spec k})\to\oplus H^{1}(\Theta_{\mathfrak{D}_{i}/\Spec k})
is surjective and
\dim\Spec k[P]\geq\sum h^{1}(\Theta_{\mathfrak{D}_{i}/\Spec k})
.

Restrict the higher rank LG model mirror to
\mathfrak{W}_{0}\to\mathfrak{B}
to an analytic open set
U_{P}
containing
b
. Take an open set of
\mathcal{B}
overlapping
U_{P}
only inside the
\sigma_{i}
, and so that the complement
\delta_{P}
is codimension two. The fibre of
\rho_{\mathfrak{X}}
over this set is then codimension also at least two inside the total space
\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}
. The same argument above would show that we can glue these two complex analytic varieties except over a closed set of codimension two. This uniquely defines a global deformation of
\mathfrak{X}
compatible with the above gluing. Hence we can extend it to a globally defined gluing since differentials extend uniquely across codimension two sets.

Our restriction on
H^{1}(\Theta_{\widecheck{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}/\Spec k^{\dagger}})\to H^{1}(\Theta_{\mathfrak{D}_{0}/\Spec k^{\dagger}})
has an interpretation on the mirror side which is more easily checkable. Assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is an ample complete intersection inside a Fano toric variety
T
of dimension at least 3. Up to torsion we have an isomorphism
H^{2}(\mathfrak{X})\cong H^{1}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{X}})
. Then by the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem there is an isomorphism
H^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(T)\cong H^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{X})
. Via composition with restriction there is therefore a canonical morphism
H^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{X})\to\oplus H^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{D}_{i})
.

Lemma 3.11
.

In this situation the above condition holds so long as each degeneration of
\mathfrak{D}_{i}
is simple and
H^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{X})\to\oplus H^{2}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{D}_{i})
is surjective.

Proof.

We apply Theorem 3.22 of
[
16
]
.

∎

Constructing examples of such degenerations seems to be a hard problem, and the initial assumptions on surjectivity are certainly not sharp, it rarely includes the case of even toric hypersurfaces! One family of examples can be constructed via subdividing the hypercube
[-1,1]^{k}
along the coordinate hyperplanes and studying an anti-canonical section. This produces a
k
-Tyurin degeneration whose minimal strata are therefore points. There is a further toric degeneration given by scaling the toric parameters.

A better solution than classifying examples would be to extend the work of
[
5
]
and
[
31
]
to the case of log toroidal families over bases where the log structure on the base is allowed to change. How much of the theory can be extended from the fibrewise theory is at present unclear. Nonetheless there are some forthcoming results of Doran, Kostiuk and You in which they prove gluing formulae for periods under such higher rank degenerations. Since their results do not require such strong assumptions one would hope that better technology would allow us to lift the restrictions here.

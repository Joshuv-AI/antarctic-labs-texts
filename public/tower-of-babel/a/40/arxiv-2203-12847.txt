Abstract
In this paper, we consider the boundary stabilization and observation of the multi-dimensional unstable heat equation. Since we consider the heat equation in a general domain, the usual partial differential equation backstepping method is hard to apply to the considered problems. The unstable dynamics of the heat equation are treated by combining the finite-dimensional spectral truncation method and the dynamics compensation method. By introducing additional finite-dimensional actuator/sensor dynamics, the unbounded stabilization/observation turns to be a bounded one. As a result, the controller/observer design becomes more easier. Both the full state feedback stabilizer and the state observer are designed. The exponential stability of the closed-loop and the well-posedness of the observer are obtained.

Introduction

When there is at least one point spectrum in the right-half complex plane, the system is referred to as an unstable system.
Owing to the pole assignment theorem,
stabilization of the finite-dimensional unstable system is almost trivial.
However, the problem may become pretty difficult for the infinite-dimensional unstable system.
When there are only finite point spectrums in the right-half complex plane,
the system can be stabilized by the finite spectral truncation technique
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
.
Since heat equation with the boundary or interior source term is usually an unstable system which has finite unstable point spectrums,
the finite spectral truncation technique can be used to
stabilize the unstable heat system. See, for instance,
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, to name just a few.

The partial differential equation (PDE) backstepping method is another way to stabilize the infinite-dimensional unstable system. It has been used to stabilize the unstable heat
equations in

[
15
]
,
[
19
]
and
[
21
]
. The PDE backstepping method can also cope with other infinite-dimensional systems such as
the first order hyperbolic equation system
[
11
]
,
the
unstable wave equation system
[
10
]
and even for ODE-PDE cascade system
[
22
]
.
Although the PDE backstepping is powerful,
it
is still hard to apply to the general multi-dimensional infinite-dimensional system.
Very recently,

[
8
]
has considered the stabilization and observation of the unstable heat equation in a general multi-dimensional region.
By using the dynamic compensation method
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
and the finite dimensional spectral truncation method
[
4
]
, both the full state feedback
and the state observer are proposed for the unstable multi-dimensional heat equation under the assumption that the unstable point spectrums are algebraic simple.
In this paper, we shall extend the results in
[
8
]

to the more general case.

We consider the following system

The main goal of this paper is to design an output feedback to stabilize system (

of the linear systems, the output feedback will be available once we address the following two problems: (i)
stabilize system (
1

We will consider these two problems separately.

We

\begin{cases}Af=\Delta f,\ \ \forall f\in D(A)\displaystyle=\left\{f\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{\Gamma_{0}}(\Omega)\ |\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}(x)=0,x\in{\Gamma_{1}}\right\},\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Gamma_{0}}(\Omega)=\{f\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ |\ f(x)=0,x\in{\Gamma_{0}}\}.\end{cases}

.

\begin{cases}w_{t}(\cdot,t)=(\tilde{A}+\mu)w(\cdot,t)+Bu(\cdot,t),\qquad t>0,\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr y(\cdot,t)=B^{*}w(\cdot,t),\qquad t\geq 0,\end{cases}

2
Preliminaries for full state feedback design

In this section, we will give some preliminaries that are very important to the
state feedback design for system (
1
).
For any positive integer
i
, we can define, in terms of the function
\phi_{i}(x)
of (
5
), the operator
P_{\phi_{i}}
:
\mathbb{R}\backslash\sigma(A)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
by

(13)

P_{\phi_{i}}\theta=\zeta_{\phi_{i}},\qquad\forall\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\sigma(A),\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,N,

where
\zeta_{\phi_{i}}
is the solution of following elliptic equation:

(14)

\begin{cases}\Delta\zeta_{\phi_{i}}(x)=\theta\zeta_{\phi_{i}}(x),\qquad x\in\Omega,\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle\zeta_{\phi_{i}}(x)=0,\ \ x\in\Gamma_{0};\qquad\frac{\partial\zeta_{\phi_{i}}(x)}{\partial\nu}=\phi_{i}(x),\ \ x\in\Gamma_{1}.\end{cases}

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(15)

\theta\neq\lambda_{j},\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Then the function
P_{\phi_{i}}
defined by (

) satisfies

(16)

\langle P_{\phi_{i}}\theta,\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{1}{\theta-\lambda_{j}}\langle\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{1})},\qquad i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Proof.

A straightforward computation shows that

(17)

\theta\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{\phi_{i}}\phi_{j}(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}\Delta\zeta_{\phi_{i}}\phi_{j}(x)dx=\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(x)dx+\lambda_{j}\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{\phi_{i}}\phi_{j}(x)dx,

which, together with (
13
), yields (
16
) easily.
∎

Inspired by
[
8
]
, the controller design is closely related to the

\begin{cases}\displaystyle z_{t}(x,t)=\Delta z(x,t)+\mu z(x,t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(P_{\phi_{i}}\theta)(x)u_{i}(t),\ \ (x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle z(x,t)=0,\ \ (x,t)\in\Gamma_{0}\times(0,+\infty),\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial z(x,t)}{\partial\nu}=0,\ \ (x,t)\in\Gamma_{1}\times(0,+\infty),\end{cases}

is defined by (

),
\mu>0
,

is a positive integer satisfies Assumption
1.1
and

Since the sequence

under the

,

In view of (

\displaystyle\dot{z}_{k}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}z_{t}(x,t)\phi_{k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta z(x,t)+\mu z(x,t)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(P_{\phi_{i}}\theta)(x)u_{i}(t)\right]\phi_{k}(x)dx

,

. Consequently, it is sufficient to consider

Lemma 2
.

In addition to Assumption
1.1
, assume that
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies (

such that

is Hurwitz, where
\Lambda_{N}
and
F_{N}
are defined by (
23
). Moreover, the operator
A+\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(P_{\phi_{i}}\theta)K_{i}
generates an exponentially stable
C_{0}
-semigroup on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, where
P_{\phi_{i}}\theta
is given by (
13
) and

is given by

(24)

K_{i}:g\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}g(x)\sum_{k=1}^{N}l_{ik}\phi_{k}(x)dx,\ \ \forall\ g\in L^{2}(\Omega),\ \ \forall\ i=1,2\cdots,N.

Proof.

Since the sequence
\{\phi_{j}(\cdot)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
under the
Assumption
1.1
are linearly
independent on

,
it follows from Lemmas

7
and
8
in Appendix and (

) that the pair

Hence, there exists a matrix

such that

is Hurwitz. Since

, it follows from

By a straightforward computation,

, Theorem 4.3, p.118]
, the proof will be

\sigma_{p}(A+\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{\phi_{i}}\theta K_{i})\subset\{s\ |\ {\rm Re}\,s<0\}.

Actually, for all
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(A+\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{\phi_{i}}\theta K_{i})

(A+\mu+\sum_{i=1}^{N}P_{\phi_{i}}\theta K_{i})g=\lambda g

. Since

\{\phi_{j}(\cdot)\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forms an orthonormal basis for

, we can suppose that

(26)

0\neq g=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}\phi_{k},\ \ g_{k}=\langle g,\phi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad k=1,2,\cdots.

As a result, the characteristic equation becomes

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda g_{k}\phi_{k}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}(A+\mu)\phi_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(P_{\phi_{i}}\theta)g_{k}K_{i}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}(\lambda_{k}+\mu)\phi_{k}+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}(P_{\phi_{i}}\theta)l_{ik}g_{k}.

When
(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{N})\neq 0
,
we take the inner product with
\phi_{j}
,
j=1,2,\cdots,N
on equation
(
27
) to obtain

(28)

\lambda g_{j}=g_{j}(\lambda_{j}+\mu)+\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}f_{ji}l_{ik}g_{k},

which, together with (

), leads to

(29)

(\lambda-\Lambda_{N}-F_{N}L_{N})(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{N})^{\top}=0.

Since
(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{N})^{\top}\neq 0
, (
29
) implies that

(30)

{\rm Det}(\lambda-\Lambda_{N}-F_{N}L_{N})=0.

Hence,
\lambda\in\sigma(\Lambda_{N}+F_{N}L_{N})\subset\{s\ |\ {\rm Re}\,s<0\}
due to that
\Lambda_{N}+F_{N}L_{N}
is Hurwitz.

When
(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{N})=0
, there exists a
j_{0}>N
such that
\int_{\Omega}g(x)\phi_{j_{0}}(x)dx\neq 0
due to
g\neq 0
, and at the same time, (

) is reduced to

(31)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda g_{k}\phi_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}(\lambda_{k}+\mu)\phi_{k}.

Take the inner product
with
\phi_{j_{0}}
on equation (
31
) to get

(32)

\displaystyle(\lambda_{j_{0}}+\mu)g_{j_{0}}=\lambda g_{j_{0}},

which, together with (
7
), implies that

. So
{\rm Re}\,\lambda<0
. Therefore, we can get (

∎

State feedback

Let the operators

Suppose that

\alpha+\mu\in\rho(-A).

Then the solution of Sylvester equation (

(43)

Sg=-\varphi_{g}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\qquad\forall\ g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{1}),

where
\varphi_{g}

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Delta\psi_{k}(x)=\lambda\psi_{k}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x)=0,\quad x\in\Gamma_{0},\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi_{k}(x)}{\partial\nu}=0,\quad x\in\Gamma_{1},\end{cases},\ \ k=1,2,\cdots,m_{\lambda}.

Noting that the matrix in (
83
) happens to be a Gram matrix of the sequence
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\cdots,\psi_{m_{\lambda}}
, the proof will be accomplished if we can prove that

\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\cdots,\psi_{m_{\lambda}}

are linearly independent on
L^{2}(\Gamma_{1})
(
[
9
, p.441, Theorem 7.2.10]
).

Actually, suppose there exist
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots,\ell_{m_{\lambda}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(85)

\ell_{1}\psi_{1}(x)+\ell_{2}\psi_{2}(x)+\cdots+\ell_{{m_{\lambda}}}\psi_{m_{\lambda}}(x)=0,\quad x\in\Gamma_{1}.

If we let

(86)

\beta_{\ell}(x)=\ell_{1}\psi_{1}(x)+\ell_{2}\psi_{2}(x)+\cdots+\ell_{m_{\lambda}}\psi_{m_{\lambda}}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

then it follows from (
84
) that

(87)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Delta\beta_{\ell}(x)=\lambda\beta_{\ell}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle\beta_{\ell}(x)=0,\quad x\in\Gamma,\cr\vskip 3.69885pt\cr\displaystyle\frac{\partial\beta_{\ell}(x)}{\partial\nu}=0,\quad x\in\Gamma_{1},\end{cases}

which admits a unique solution
\beta_{\ell}(x)\equiv 0
,
x\in\Omega
(
[
7
, Theorem 2.4]
).
Since
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\cdots,\psi_{m_{\lambda}}

are linearly independent on
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
we can conclude that
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=\cdots=\ell_{m_{\lambda}}=0
.
As a result,
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\cdots,\psi_{m_{\lambda}}
are linearly independent on
L^{2}(\Gamma_{1})
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
N
be a positive integer,

F_{N}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
and

(88)

\Lambda_{N}={\rm diag}(J_{1},\cdots,J_{p}),\ \ J_{j}={\rm diag}(\lambda_{j},\cdots,\lambda_{j})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{j}\times n_{j}},\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,p,

where
n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{p}
are
p
positive integers such that
n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{p}=N
.
Suppose that

(89)

\lambda_{k}\neq\lambda_{j}\ \mbox{if and only if}\ \ k\neq j,\ \ \ k,j=1,2,\cdots,p

and the matrix
F_{N}
can be written as

(90)

F_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}P_{1}&*&\cdots&*\\
*&P_{2}&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
*&*&\cdots&P_{p}\\
\end{pmatrix},\ \ P_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{j}\times n_{j}},\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,p.

Then, the pair
(\Lambda_{N},F_{N})
is controllable provided the matrix determinant of
P_{j}
satisfies

(91)

\left|P_{j}\right|\neq 0,\ \ \ j=1,2,\cdots,p.

Proof.

Otherwise, we can conclude that
(\Lambda_{N}^{\top},F^{\top}_{N})
is not observable.
By the Hautus test
[
20
, p.15, Remark 1.5.2]
,
there exist
0\neq V\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,p\}
such that

(92)

\Lambda_{N}V=\lambda_{k}V\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ F_{N}^{\top}V=0.

Without loss of generality,
we suppose that

(93)

V=\begin{pmatrix}V_{1}\\
V_{2}\\
\vdots\\
V_{p}\end{pmatrix},\ \ V_{j}=(v^{j}_{1},\cdots,v_{n_{j}}^{j})^{\top},\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,n_{j}.

The first equation of (
92
) becomes

(94)

\Lambda_{N}V-\lambda_{k}V=\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{k})V_{1}\\
\vdots\\
(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k})V_{2}\\
\vdots\\
(\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{k})V_{p}\end{pmatrix}=0.\ \

By the assumption (
89
),
V_{i}=0
when
i\neq k
. As a result,
F_{N}^{\top}V=0
implies that
P_{k}^{\top}V_{k}=0
. Owing to the assumption (
91
), we can conclude that
V_{k}=0
and hence
V=0
. This is contradict to the fact
V\neq 0
. Therefore,
the pair
(\Lambda_{N},F_{N})
is controllable.
∎

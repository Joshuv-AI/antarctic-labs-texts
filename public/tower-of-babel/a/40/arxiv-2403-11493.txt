Forward-backward-forward dynamics for bilevel equilibrium problem
Kanchan Mittal, Pankaj Gautam, V. Vetrivel

Abstract
We introduce a forward-backward-forward (FBF) algorithm for solving bilevel equilibrium problem associated with bifunctions on a real Hilbert space. This modifies the forward-backward algorithm by relaxing cocoercivity with monotone and Lipschitzness. Further, we present the FBF dynamical system and investigate the generated trajectory’s existence, uniqueness and weak convergence. We illustrate the proposed method for equilibrium problem under saddle point constraint.



keywords
Bilevel equilibrium problem, Monotone operator, Dynamical system, Fitzpatrick function.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space with the norm and scalar product denoted by
\|\cdot\|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, respectively. Consider a non-empty closed and convex subset
K
of
\mathcal{H}
and a real-valued bifunction
f:K\times K\to\mathbb{R}
. The equilibrium problem corresponding to
f
on
K
is to find:

x\in K\text{ such that }f(x,y)\geq 0,~\forall y\in K.

(1)

The problem (
1
) is denoted by
\operatorname{EP}(f,K)
. The classical variational inequality problem (VIP)
Stampacchia 1964
, is an example of an equilibrium problem by taking
f(x,y)=\langle F(x),y-x\rangle,
where
F:K\to\mathcal{H}
is a vector-valued function. This variational formulation is a well-suited mathematical model for many problems in life sciences and plays a crucial role in applied mathematics such as optimization, hemivariational inequalities, network equilibrium problem, saddle point problem, obstacle problems, Nash equilibrium problem, and many other areas, (for instance see
E. Blum 1994
;
kinderlehrer2000introduction 2000
;
Facchinei and Kanzow 2010
and references therein).

Chadli et al.
Chaldi et al. 2000
introduced the concept of bilevel
equilibrium problem, shortly BEP, which was named the viscosity principle for equilibrium problem. This principle aims to have a good selection of the upper equilibrium among the solutions of the lower-level equilibrium problem. Besides this combinatory aspect, bilevel equilibrium problem seen in different fields like mechanics, engineering sciences, and economy (see
Dempe 2003
and references therein). The BEP is as follows: for any two real-valued bifunctions
f,g:K\times K\to\mathbb{R}

\text{find }x\in S_{f}\text{ such that }g(x,y)\geq 0,~\forall y\in S_{f},

(2)

where
S_{f}:=\{y\in K:f(y,z)\geq 0,~\forall z\in K\}
, the set of constraints. The solution set of BEP (
2
) is denoted by
S
, and throughout the paper, we will assume that this set is non-empty. Moudafi
Moudafi 2010
introduced the regularized iterative method to solve the bilevel monotone equilibrium problem. Chbani et al.
Chbani and Riahi 2013
proposed an alternating proximal method and removed the difficulty mentioned in
Moudafi 2010
related to the limiting iteration behavior. Recently, Riahi et al.
Riahi et al. 2018
and Balhag et al.
Balhag et al. 2023
have proposed the forward-backward iterative methods to solve the BEP (
2
) by assuming the bifunction
f
to be pseudomonotone and monotone, respectively. Balhag et al.
Balhag et al. 2023
proposed the inertial forward-backward algorithm to solve particular BEP in which the lower-level EP is given by

f(x,y)=\langle Bx,y-x\rangle,

where
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a cocoercive operator. This algorithm is described as follows: for each
n\geq 1
, and current iterates
x_{n-1},x_{n}\in K
and set

y_{n}=x_{n}+\alpha(x_{n}-x_{n-1}).

and define
x_{n+1}\in K
such that

\beta_{n}\langle Bx_{n},y-x_{n+1}\rangle+g(x_{n+1},y)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left\langle x_{n+1}-y_{n},y-x_{n+1}\right\rangle\geq 0,\forall y\in K,

where
\lambda_{n}
and
\beta_{n}
are two sequences of positive real numbers.

While the BEP holds significant real-life applications, the stringent cocoercivity condition imposed on
B
proves restrictive. Consequently, investigating scenarios with more relaxed conditions on the operator
B
becomes intriguing. The method proposed by Tseng
Tseng 2000
innovatively employed the forward-backward splitting technique to find zeros of the sum of two maximal monotone operators. In alignment with Tseng’s framework, we aim to present an iterative method for tackling the BEP, considered in
Balhag et al. 2023
, by presuming the monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity of the operator
B
.

The continuous approach to iterative methods in equilibrium problems can give a better understanding of their behavior, and we can use the techniques from continuous cases to get results for discrete algorithms. The dynamic approach for optimization problems received much attention due to the work done in
Baillon 1976
;
Brézis 1973
;
Bruck Jr 1975
;
Csetnek 2020
. They have been observed an important tool for numerical methods by discretizing continuous dynamical systems.
Attouch and Czarnecki
Attouch and
Czarnecki 2010
studied the asymptotic behavior of nonautonomous dynamical systems involving multiscale features. They considered the particular system that models the emergence of various collective behaviors in game theory and the asymptotic control of coupled systems. Chbani et al.
Chbani et al. 2019
introduced first-order continuous evolution dynamical equilibrium systems and showed the system’s existence and uniqueness of solution, and studied the asymptotic behavior of the dynamical trajectories to the Ky Fan minimax inequalities with nonautonomous equilibrium bifunctions under monotonicity conditions. This conviction has led us to explore the dynamic approach as a means to address the BEP, an avenue that has not yet been explored.

The subsequent sections of this paper are structured as follows: In Section
2
, we gather necessary definitions and some tools essential for substantiating our main results. Section
3
deals with weak convergence of proposed forward-backward-forward (FBF) algorithm.
In Section
4
, we consider FBF dynamical system and study the existence and uniqueness of the orbit by Cauchy-Picard-Lipschitz theorem.
In this section, we have also analyzed the weak convergence of the generated orbit of FBF dynamical system to a solution of BEP. Section
5
develops a method to solve equilibrium problem with saddle point constraint.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we shall collect some definitions and technical lemmas that will be used later in the paper. Throughout, the set
K
is a non-empty closed and convex subset of a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.

Definition 1
.

A bifunction
f:K\times K\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be

(\rm{i})

monotone if

f(x,y)+f(y,x)\leq 0,\forall x,y\in K;

(\rm{ii})

upper-hemicontinuous if

\lim_{t\to 0}f(tx_{1}+(1-t)x_{2},y)\leq f(x_{2},y),\forall x_{1},x_{2},y\in K;

(\rm{iii})

lower semicontinuous at
y
with respect to the second argument on
K,
if

f(x,w)\leq\liminf_{y\to w}f(x,y),\forall x,y,w\in K;

(\rm{iv})

an equilibrium function, if for all
x\in K
,
f(x,x)=0
and
f(x,\cdot)
is convex and lower semicontinuous.

Definition 2
.

An operator
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is called

1.

monotone if

\langle Bx-By,x-y\rangle\geq 0,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H};

2.

maximally monotone if
B
is monotone and it has no monotone extension;

3.

L
-Lipschitz
(L>0)
if
\|Bx-By\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H}.

Let
A:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
be any mapping. Then the effective domain of
A
is denoted as
\operatorname{dom}A
and defined as

\operatorname{dom}A:=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:Ax\neq\emptyset\}
, also
A^{-1}(0):=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:0\in Ax\}.
A multi-valued mapping
A
is said to be a monotone operator on
\mathcal{H}
if
\langle x-y,u-v\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\operatorname{dom}A,u\in Ax
and
v\in Ay.
Also, a monotone operator is maximal if
its graph is not strictly contained in the graph of any other monotone operator on
\mathcal{H}
. For any bifunction
f
, the resolvent of
f
(see
E. Blum 1994
) is defined by

J_{\lambda}^{f}(x):=\{z\in K:f(z,y)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\langle z-x,y-z\rangle\geq 0,\forall y\in K\},

(3)

for each
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathcal{H}.
Moudafi
Moudafi 1999
proposed and analyzed a proximal method for solving an equilibrium problem in which an iterative sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is generated with the help of resolvent operator such that
x_{n+1}=J_{r_{n}}^{f}(x_{n}),
that is,

f(x_{n+1},y)+\frac{1}{r_{n}}\langle x_{n+1}-x_{n},y-x_{n+1}\rangle\geq 0,\forall y\in K,

where
r_{n}>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Corresponding to the equilibrium problem, we talk about the dual equilibrium problem:

\text{find }x^{*}\in K\text{ such that }f(y,x^{*})\leq 0,~\forall y\in K.

(4)

We denote the problem by
\operatorname{DEP}(f,K)
.

The next lemma relates the solution of EP (
1
) and DEP (
4
).

Lemma 1
.

E. Blum 1994

(\rm{i})

If

is monotone, then every solution of
\operatorname{EP}(f,K)
is a solution of
\operatorname{DEP}(f,K)
.

(\rm{ii})

Conversely, if f is upper hemicontinuous and an equilibrium bifunction, then each solution of
\operatorname{DEP}(f,K)
is a solution of
\operatorname{EP}(f,K)
.

for all

if and only if

\partial\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}\{v\in H:\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\geq\langle v,y-x\rangle,\forall y\in\mathcal{H}\},&\text{if }\phi(x)<\infty\\

The resolvent operator

3
Convergence of Forward-backward-forward method from discrete perspective

In this section, we analyze the convergence of Tseng’s forward-backward-forward algorithm for two monotone operators in the setup of bilevel equilibrium problem (
2
) satisfying the following geometric assumption:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\lambda_{n}}{\beta_{n}}\Big[\mathcal{F}_{B}\bigg(u,\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\bigg)-\sigma_{S_{f}}\bigg(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\bigg)\Big]<\infty,~\forall u\in S_{f},p\in N_{S_{f}}(u).

(5)

The condition (
5
) is very much known in the literature, and we can see it as the discrete counterpart of the condition taken in continuous-time dynamical equilibrium problem
Chbani et al. 2019
. The same kind of condition is taken while finding the solution of variational inequalities expressed as a monotone inclusion problem
Boţ and Csetnek 2014
and for constrained convex optimization problem
Boţ et al. 2018
via inertial proximal-gradient penalization scheme.

Let
f
and
g
be two real-valued bifunctions defined on
K
and
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be an operator such that

f(x,y)=\langle Bx,y-x\rangle.
We shall assume that the bifunctions
f
and
g
are satisfying the following conditions:

\bullet

\operatorname{dom}A^{g}=K,
that is,
\partial g_{y}(y)
is non-empty for all
y\in K;

\bullet

\partial g_{x}+N_{S_{f}}=\partial(g_{x}+\delta_{S_{f}})
which is realised when
K\cap S_{f}\neq\emptyset
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}(K-S_{f})
is a closed linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}.

We will show the weak convergence of the sequences
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}

generated by the following forward-backward-forward algorithm.

Algorithm 1

Forward-backward-forward (FBF) algorithm for BEP from discrete perspective

\mathbf{Initialization}
: Take starting points
x_{1}\in K
and choose arbitrary positive sequences
\{{\lambda}_{n}\},\{{\beta}_{n}\}
.

\mathbf{Step~1}
: Given
x_{n}\in K
,
set

y_{n}=J_{\lambda_{n}}^{g}(x_{n}-\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}Bx_{n})

and define

x_{n+1}=y_{n}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}(Bx_{n}-By_{n}),

(6)

i.e.,

g(y_{n},y)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\langle y_{n}-x_{n}+\beta_{n}\lambda_{n}Bx_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle\geq 0,~\forall y\in K.

\mathbf{Step~2}
: If
x_{n+1}=x_{n},
stop and return
x_{n}
. Otherwise, set
n=n+1
and go back to Step 1.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
be the sequences generated by the Algorithm
1
and the bifunction
g
satisfy
(\rm{i}),(\rm{ii})
and
(\rm{iv})
of Definition
1
. Let
B
be monotone and
L
-Lipschitz with
L>0
. Consider
u\in S
, set
a_{n}={\|x_{n}-u\|}^{2}
and take
p\in N_{S_{f}}.
Then for each
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle a_{n+1}-a_{n}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}f(u,y_{n})

\displaystyle\leq-(1-{\lambda_{n}}^{2}{\beta_{n}}^{2}L^{2}){\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2}+

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\lambda_{n}}{\beta_{n}}\Big[\mathcal{F}_{B}\bigg(u,\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\bigg)-\sigma_{S_{f}}\bigg(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\bigg)\Big].

(7)

Proof.

From the Algorithm
1
, we have

g(y_{n},y)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\langle y_{n}-x_{n}+\beta_{n}\lambda_{n}Bx_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle\geq 0,~\forall y\in K.

We can also write as

g(y_{n},y)+\beta_{n}\langle By_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle+\beta_{n}\langle Bx_{n}-By_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\langle y_{n}-x_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle\geq 0,~\forall y\in K.

Using (
6
), we have

g(y_{n},y)+\beta_{n}f(y_{n},y)+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\langle x_{n+1}-x_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle\geq 0,~\forall y\in K.

(8)

Note that
2\langle y-x,z-y\rangle={\|x-z\|}^{2}-{\|y-z\|}^{2}-{\|y-x\|}^{2}
for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}.

Then

\displaystyle 2\langle x_{n+1}-x_{n},y-y_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle x_{n+1}-x_{n},y-x_{n+1}+2\langle x_{n+1}-x_{n},x_{n+1}-y_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=a_{n}-a_{n+1}-{\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2}+{\|\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}(Bx_{n}-By_{n})\|}^{2}.

Then (
8
) reduces to

0\leq g(y_{n},y)+\beta_{n}f(y_{n},y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{n}}\big[a_{n}-a_{n+1}-{\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2}+{\|\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}(Bx_{n}-By_{n})\|}^{2}\big],

for all
y\in K.
By Lipschtzness of
B

a_{n+1}-a_{n}\leq 2\lambda_{n}g(y_{n},y)+2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}f(y_{n},y)+-(1-\lambda_{n}^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}L^{2}){\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2},

for all
y\in K.

On putting
y=u\in S_{f},
we have

a_{n+1}-a_{n}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}f(u,y_{n})\leq-(1-\lambda_{n}^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}L^{2}){\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}g(y_{n},u)+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}f(y_{n},u).

(9)

The last inequality is followed by the monotonicity of
B.
Since
u\in S_{f}
, according to first order optimality condition,

0\in\partial(g_{u}+\delta_{S_{f}})(u)=A^{g}(u)+N_{S_{f}}(u).

Let
p\in N_{S_{f}}
such that
-p\in A^{g}(u).
Thus
\forall n\geq 1,

g(u,y_{n})+\langle-p,u-y_{n}\rangle\geq 0.

By monotonicity of
g
, we have

g(y_{n},u)\leq\langle-p,u-y_{n}\rangle.

Inequality (
9
)
becomes

a_{n+1}-a_{n}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}f(u,y_{n})\leq-(1-\lambda_{n}^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}L^{2}){\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[f(y_{n},u)+\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{-2p}{\beta_{n}},u\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{-2p}{\beta_{n}},y_{n}\right\rangle\bigg].

For
p\in N_{S_{f}}
,
u\in S_{f}
,
\delta_{S_{f}}(u)+\sigma_{S_{f}}(p)=\langle p,u\rangle.
This implies
\sigma_{S_{f}}(p)=\langle p,u\rangle.

Continuing the last inequality, we have

a_{n+1}-a_{n}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}f(u,y_{n})\leq-(1-\lambda_{n}^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}L^{2}){\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|}^{2}+\displaystyle{\lambda_{n}}{\beta_{n}}\Big[\mathcal{F}_{B}\bigg(u,\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\bigg)-\sigma_{S_{f}}\bigg(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\bigg)\Big].

This proves the result.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y(t)=J_{\lambda(t)}^{g}\big(x(t)-\lambda(t)\beta(t)Bx(t)\big),\\

Definition 3

Attouch and

(\rm{i})

(\rm{iii})

J_{i}\cap J_{k}=\emptyset\text{ and }\sum_{k}|b_{k}-a_{k}|<\delta\implies\sum_{k}\|x(b_{k})-x(a_{k})\|<\epsilon.

If

-Lipschitz,

Definition 4

4.1
Existence and Uniqueness of trajectory

Throughout this subsection, we will assume that the bifunction
g
satisfy the conditions
(\rm{i}),(\rm{ii})
and
(\rm{iv})
of Definition
1
, and
B
is monotone and
L

Define the function

Then, the dynamical system (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)=h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),x(t)),\\

Before proving the main result of this subsection, we will provide some lemmas that will be useful.

Lemma 7
.

For each
\lambda,\beta\in(0,\infty)
such that
\lambda\beta\in(0,1/L)
and
x,y\in\mathcal{H},
we have

\|h(\lambda,\beta,x)-h(\lambda,\beta,y)\|\leq\sqrt{6}\|x-y\|,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H},

that is,
h
is globally Lipschitz continuous with respect to the third variable.

Proof.

Denote
J=J_{\lambda}^{g}
and
C=I-\lambda\beta B,
then
h(\lambda,\beta,x)=C\circ(J\circ C-I)x,

x\in\mathcal{H}.

Consider

\displaystyle{\|h(\lambda,\beta,x)-h(\lambda,\beta,y)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\|C\circ J\circ Cx-C\circ J\circ Cy+Cy-Cx\|}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\|C\circ J\circ Cx-C\circ J\circ Cy\|}^{2}+{\|Cy-Cx\|}^{2}-2\langle C\circ J\circ Cx-C\circ J\circ Cy,Cy-Cx\rangle.

(14)

Since
C\circ J\circ Cx=J\circ Cx-\lambda\beta B\circ J\circ Cx,
we have

\displaystyle{\|C\circ J\circ Cx-C\circ J\circ Cy\|}^{2}

\displaystyle={\|J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy\|}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\beta^{2}{\|B\circ J\circ Cx-B\circ J\circ Cy\|}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\lambda\beta\langle J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy,B\circ J\circ Cx-B\circ J\circ Cy\rangle.

As
B
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous and
J
is firmly non-expansive, we obtain

\displaystyle{\|C\circ J\circ Cx-C\circ J\circ Cy\|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\lambda^{2}\beta^{2}L^{2})\langle Cx-Cy,J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy\rangle

\displaystyle-2\lambda\beta\langle J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy,B\circ J\circ Cx-B\circ J\circ Cy\rangle.

(15)

Also, we can write

\displaystyle-2\langle C\circ J\circ Cx-C\circ J\circ Cy,Cy-Cx\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\langle J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy,Cy-Cx\rangle+2\lambda\beta\langle B\circ J\circ Cy-B\circ J\circ Cx,Cy-Cx\rangle.

(16)

Putting (
15
) and (
16
) in (
4.1
), we get

\displaystyle{\|h(\lambda,\beta,x)-h(\lambda,\beta,y)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\lambda^{2}\beta^{2}L^{2}-2)\langle Cx-Cy,J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy\rangle+{\|Cy-Cx\|}^{2}

\displaystyle-2\lambda\beta\langle J\circ Cx-J\circ Cy,B\circ J\circ Cx-B\circ J\circ Cy\rangle+2\lambda\beta\langle B\circ J\circ Cy-B\circ J\circ Cx,Cy-Cx\rangle.

By monotonicity of

, we have

\displaystyle{\|h(\lambda,\beta,x)-h(\lambda,\beta,y)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda\beta L){\|Cy-Cx\|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda\beta L)(1+\lambda^{2}\beta^{2}L^{2}){\|x-y\|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 6{\|x-y\|}^{2}.

This proves the result.

∎

Lemma 8

Let

be fixed. Then the functions
\beta\to h(\lambda,\beta,x)
and
\lambda\to h(\lambda,\beta,x)

Theorem 4
.

Let
\lambda:[0,\infty)\to(0,\alpha)
,
\alpha>0
and
\beta:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be measurable functions such that
\displaystyle 0<\limsup_{t}\lambda(t)\beta(t)<1/L

Proof.

We apply the Cauchy-Lipschitz-Picard theorem
(

to show the existence and uniqueness of the solution of (

(\rm{i})

\forall x\in\mathcal{H},h(\cdot,\cdot,x)\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}([0,\infty);\mathcal{H});

(\rm{ii})

\forall t\in[0,\infty),h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),\cdot):\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is continuous, moreover,

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H},

\|h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),x)-h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),y)\|\leq\omega(t,\|x\|+\|y\|)\|x-y\|,

for some

for

(\rm{iii})

From Lemma
7
,
(\rm{ii})
follows for every

Let

which is obviously a non-empty set. By Lemma

, we can extend the map

Then, for all

and for all

we can write

\displaystyle\|h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),\bar{x})\|+\|h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),x)-h(\lambda(t),\beta(t),\bar{x})\|

Therefore, condition

∎

We shall now derive the weak convergence of the trajectory generated by dynamical system (

(17)

Lemma 9
.

Let
x
and
y
be given by the dynamical system (
12
) and
\lambda:[0,\infty)\to(0,\alpha)
,
\alpha>0
,
\beta:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be locally absolutely

g

(\rm{i}),(\rm{ii})
and
(\rm{iv})
of Definition

is monotone and

\displaystyle\|\dot{y}(t)\|\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(1+\frac{|\dot{\lambda}(t)|}{\lambda(t)}+\lambda(t)\beta(t)L+\beta(t)\lambda(t)L\sqrt{1+{\lambda(t)}^{2}{\beta(t)}^{2}{L}^{2}}\bigg)\|y(t)-x(t)\|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+L|\dot{\beta}(t)|\lambda(t)\|Bx(t)\|,

(18)

for almost all

Proof.

As argued in Lemma 6 of
Banert and Bot 2015
, we conclude that

is absolutely continuous on
[0,b]
for fixed

Now, by Lemma

J_{\lambda(t)}^{g}(x(t))=(I+\lambda(t)A^{g})x(t),~\forall t\in[0,\infty).

Therefore,

\displaystyle y(t)=

\displaystyle J_{\lambda(t)}^{g}(x(t)-\beta(t)\lambda(t)Bx(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(I+\lambda(t)A^{g})}^{-1}(x(t)-\beta(t)\lambda(t)Bx(t)).

Thus, for all

Taking limit

for almost every

,

\displaystyle\|\dot{y}(t)\|\leq

\displaystyle\left|1+\frac{\dot{\lambda}(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right|\|x(t)-y(t)\|+\lambda(t)\beta(t)L\|x(t)-y(t)\|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\lambda(t)\beta(t)\left\|\frac{d}{dt}Bx(t)\right\|+|\dot{\beta}(t)|\lambda(t)\|Bx(t)\|.

Note that

\|\dot{x}(t)\|\leq\sqrt{1+{\lambda(t)}^{2}{\beta(t)}^{2}{L}^{2}}\cdot\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

Thus, by Remark

\left\|\frac{d}{dt}Bx(t)\right\|\leq L\|\dot{x}(t)\|
and this completes the proof.

(\rm{i})

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)-u\|

(\rm{ii})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{\|x(t)-y(t)\|}^{2}dt<+\infty.

(21)

Let

then

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{u}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\dot{x}(t),x(t)-u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(t)\beta(t)f(y(t),u)-\lambda(t)\langle p,u-y(t)\rangle-\langle\dot{x}(t),y(t)-x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda(t)\beta(t)\left[f(y(t),u)-\left\langle\frac{p}{\beta(t)},u-y(t)\right\rangle\right]-\langle\dot{x}(t),y(t)-x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\langle\dot{x}(t),y(t)-x(t)\rangle+\lambda(t)\beta(t)\left[\mathcal{F}_{f}\left(u,\frac{p}{\beta(t)}\right)-\left\langle\frac{p}{\beta(t)},u\right\rangle\right].

(23)

Now

\displaystyle-\langle\dot{x}(t),y(t)-x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle y(t)-x(t),y(t)-x(t)\rangle+\lambda(t)\beta(t)\langle Bx(t)-By(t),x(t)-y(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(1-\lambda(t)\beta(t)L){\|y(t)-x(t)\|}^{2}.

Therefore, (
23
) becomes

\dot{h}_{u}(t)\leq-(1-\lambda(t)\beta(t)L){\|y(t)-x(t)\|}^{2}+\lambda(t)\beta(t)\left[\mathcal{F}_{f}\left(u,\frac{p}{\beta(t)}\right)-\left\langle\frac{p}{\beta(t)},u\right\rangle\right].

(24)

From the assumption (
17
), we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{h}_{u}(\tau)d\tau\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(\tau)\beta(\tau)\left[\mathcal{F}_{f}\left(u,\frac{p}{\beta(\tau)}\right)-\left\langle\frac{p}{\beta(\tau)},u\right\rangle\right]d\tau<\infty.

Thus,
\dot{h}_{u}\in L^{1}([0,\infty)).
This gives that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}h_{u}(t)
exists in
\mathbb{R}.
Hence,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)-u\|
exists. Also, (
24
) yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\lambda(\tau)\beta(\tau)L){\|y(\tau)-x(\tau)\|}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{h}_{u}(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda(\tau)\beta(\tau)\left[\mathcal{F}_{f}\left(u,\frac{p}{\beta(\tau)}\right)-\left\langle\frac{p}{\beta(\tau)},u\right\rangle\right]d\tau<\infty.

Since
\displaystyle\limsup_{t}\lambda(t)\beta(t)L<1,
we directly conclude that

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\|y(\tau)-x(\tau)\|}^{2}d\tau<\infty.

This proves the result.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
g
satisfy the conditions
(\rm{i}),(\rm{ii})
and
(\rm{iv})
of Definition
1
and
B
is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz
(L>0)
. Let
x
and
y
be given by the dynamical system (
12
), and let
\lambda:[0,\infty)\to(0,\alpha)
,
\alpha>0
,
\beta:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be locally absolutely continuous functions such that

(a)

assumption (
17
) holds;

(b)

\displaystyle 0<\limsup_{t}\lambda(t)\beta(t)<1/L
;

(c)

\displaystyle\liminf_{t}\lambda(t)>0
and
\dot{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty))
;

(d)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta(t)=\infty
and
\dot{\beta}\in L^{2}([0,\infty))
.

Then the trajectories
x(t)
and
y(t)
converge weakly in
S
.

Proof.

We prove the result with the help of Opial Lemma
5
. For any element
u\in S,
by Proposition
5
,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x(t)-u\|
exists. We are left to show that every sequentially weak cluster point of the map
x
belongs in
S.
Let
\tilde{x}
be any weak cluster point of
x.
There exists a sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
such that

x(t_{n})\rightharpoonup\tilde{x}
as
n\to\infty.
Before moving further, first we need to show that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x(t_{n})-y(t_{n})\|=0,
which implies
y(t_{n})\rightharpoonup\tilde{x}
as
n\to\infty
. Consider

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}{\|x(t)-y(t)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle x(t)-y(t),\dot{x}(t)-\dot{y}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\dot{x}(t)\|\|\dot{y}(t)\|\right)\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

By Lemma
9
, for almost every
t\in[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}{\|x(t)-y(t)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1+\frac{|\dot{\lambda}(t)|}{\lambda(t)}+\lambda(t)\beta(t)L+(1+\beta(t)\lambda(t)L)\sqrt{1+{\lambda(t)}^{2}{\beta(t)}^{2}{L}^{2}}\right]{\|y(t)-x(t)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle+L|\dot{\beta}(t)|\lambda(t)\|Bx(t)\|\cdot\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

Since
\beta(t)\lambda(t)L<1
and for almost all
t\in[0,\infty),x(t)
is bounded by constant
k,
(say). Therefore, the last inequality reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}{\|x(t)-y(t)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle<\left(1+2\sqrt{2}+\frac{|\dot{\lambda}(t)|}{\lambda(t)}\right){\|y(t)-x(t)\|}^{2}+Lk|\dot{\beta}(t)|\lambda(t)\|x(t)\|\cdot\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

Also,
\displaystyle\liminf_{t}\lambda(t)>0,
there exist
\delta>0
such that
\delta\leq\lambda(t)
for all
t.
Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}{\|x(t)-y(t)\|}^{2}

\displaystyle<\left(1+2\sqrt{2}+\frac{{\|\dot{\lambda}\|}_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty))}}{\delta}\right){\|y(t)-x(t)\|}^{2}+Lk|\dot{\beta}(t)|\lambda(t)\|x(t)\|\cdot\|x(t)-y(t)\|.

By Proposition
5

(\rm{ii}),
the function in
t
on the right-hand side of the above inequality belongs to
L^{1}([0,\infty)).
On applying Lemma
6
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x(t_{n})-y(t_{n})\|=0.

Consequently,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\dot{x}(t)\|=0.
By (
21
), for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
it follows that

g(y(t_{n}),y)+\beta(t_{n})f(y(t_{n}),y)+\frac{1}{\lambda(t_{n})}\langle\dot{x}(t_{n}),y-y(t_{n})\rangle\geq 0.

By the monotonicity of
f
and
g,
we obtain

f(y,y(t_{n}))\leq\frac{-g(y,y(t_{n}))}{\beta(t_{n})}+\frac{1}{\lambda(t_{n})\beta(t_{n})}\langle\dot{x}(t_{n}),y-y(t_{n})\rangle.

Since for all
y\in K,\partial g_{y}(y)\neq 0,
there exits
x^{*}(y)=\gamma(y)\in\mathcal{H}
such that

-g(y,y(t_{n}))\leq\gamma(y)\|y-y(t_{n})\|,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

This implies

f(y,y(t_{n}))\leq\frac{\gamma(y)\|y-y(t_{n})\|}{\beta(t_{n})}+\frac{\|\dot{x}(t_{n})\|\|y-y(t_{n})\|}{\lambda(t_{n})\beta(t_{n})},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that both the sequences
\|\dot{x}(t_{n})\|
and
\|{y}(t_{n})\|
are bounded, with
\displaystyle\liminf_{t}\lambda(t)>0,\lim_{t\to\infty}\beta(t)
=\infty
, therefore as limit
n\to\infty,

f(y,\tilde{x})\leq 0,\forall y\in K.

By Lemma
1
,

f(\tilde{x},y)\geq 0,\forall y\in K.

Thus,
x\in S_{f}.
Again by (
21
), for
y=u\in S_{f},
and monotone property of
f
and
g
, we get

0\leq\langle\dot{x}(t_{n}),u-y(t_{n})\rangle-\lambda(t_{n})g(u,y(t_{n}))-\lambda(t_{n})\beta(t_{n})f(u,y(t_{n})),\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
u\in S_{f},\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\lambda(t_{n})g(u,y(t_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\langle\dot{x}(t_{n}),u-y(t_{n})\rangle\leq\|\dot{x}(t_{n})\|\|u-y(t_{n})\|.

Thus,

\liminf_{n}\lambda(t_{n})g(u,y(t_{n}))\leq 0,\forall u\in S_{f}.

The last inequality follows as
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\dot{x}(t_{n})\|=0
and
\{\|y(t_{n})\|\}
is bounded. Also,
\displaystyle\liminf_{n}\lambda(t_{n})
>0
and
g
is lower semicontinuous, which implies

g(u,\tilde{x})\leq 0,\forall u\in S_{f}.

Once again, by applying Lemma
1
, we get

g(\tilde{x},u)\geq 0,\forall u\in S_{f}.

Finally,
\tilde{x}\in S
and consequently,
x(t)
converges weakly to some element of
S
as
t\to\infty
. This completes the proof.
∎

5
Application to Bilinear Saddle Point Problem

Consider the following convex-concave minimax problem:

\min_{u\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}\max_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}\Phi(u,v):=h_{1}(u)+\Gamma(u,v)-h_{2}(v),

(25)

where
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are real Hilbert spaces,
\Gamma:U\times V\to\mathbb{R}
is a coupling function with
U\subset\mathcal{H}_{1},V\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}
are non-empty closed convex sets, and
h_{1}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
,
h_{2}:\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

are regularizers. The coupling function
\Gamma
is assumed to be convex-concave, that is, for each
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2},

\Gamma(\cdot,v)
and
-\Gamma(u,\cdot)
are convex and differentiable with
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient (
\nabla\Gamma
) for
L>0
, that is,

\|\nabla\Gamma(u_{1},v_{1})-\nabla\Gamma(u_{2},v_{2})\|\leq L\|(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})\|,

for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{H}_{2}.

Also, the functions
f
and
g
are supposed to be proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous. The solution of problem (
25
) is given by a saddle point
(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}
satisfying the following condition:

\Gamma(\bar{u},v)\leq\Gamma(\bar{u},\bar{v})\leq\Gamma(u,\bar{v}),

(26)

for all
u\in\mathcal{H}_{1},v\in\mathcal{H}_{2}.

Now we consider the bilinear saddle point problem: find
(\bar{u},\bar{v})\in U\times V
such that

\max_{v\in V}\min_{u\in U}\Gamma(u,v)=\min_{u\in U}\max_{v\in V}\Gamma(u,v)=\Gamma(\bar{u},\bar{v}),

(27)

where

\Gamma(u,v)=u^{T}Mv+a^{T}u+b^{T}v,

with
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},a\in\mathbb{R}^{m},b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are non-empty, closed and convex sets. Note that (
27
) is equivalent to (
26
), see
Ekeland and
Témam 1999
. Set
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2},K=U\times V,
we define
f:K\times K\to\mathbb{R}
as

f(x,y)=\langle Bx,y-x\rangle;

where
B(u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\
-M^{T}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}a\\
-b\end{pmatrix}

for
(u,v)\in K.
Clearly,
B
is monotone and Lipschitz with Lipschitz constant
L={\|M\|}_{2}
. Hence, we have

f((u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2}))=\Gamma(u_{2},v_{1})-\Gamma(u_{1},v_{2}),

for all
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in K.
Also, the solution sets of problem (
26
) and equilibrium problem (
1
) coincide. We denote the solution set of (
26
) by
\Omega
.

Consider two monotone operators
A_{1}:\operatorname{dom}A_{1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
A_{2}:\operatorname{dom}A_{2}\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that
K\subset\operatorname{dom}A_{1}\times\operatorname{dom}A_{2}
and
A_{1}\times A_{2}+N_{\Omega}
is a maximally monotone operator (for details, see
Rockafellar 1970a
;
Rockafellar 1970b
), where
A_{1}\times A_{2}
is defined usually as

(A_{1}\times A_{2})(x,y):=(A_{1}x,A_{2}y),

(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}.

Denote
S_{MI}=\{(x,y)\in\operatorname{dom}A_{1}\times\operatorname{dom}A_{2}:0\in Ax\times By+N_{\Omega}(x,y)\}
and suppose that
S_{MI}
is non-empty. Furthermore, when
A_{1}\times A_{2}+N_{\Omega}
is a maximally monotone, we have

(x,y)\in S_{MI}\iff\\
(x,y)\in\Omega\text{ with }\left\langle(A_{1}\times A_{2})(x,y),(u,v)-(x,y)\right\rangle,\forall(u,v)\in\Omega.

(28)

Note that if we consider the bifunction
g:K\times K\to\mathbb{R}

as

g((u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})):=\langle A_{1}u_{1},u_{2}-u_{1}\rangle+\langle A_{2}v_{1},v_{2}-v_{1}\rangle

for each
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in K
,
then the solution set of BEP (
2
) with the above-mentioned bifunctions
f
and
g
coincides with
S_{MI}.
Therefore, the saddle point problem can be solved by Algorithm
1
. Before going further, let us simplify the assumption (
5
) for the above bifunction
f
. By the definition of Fitzpatrick transform, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{f}((u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2}))

\displaystyle=\sup_{(x,y)\in K}\left\{\langle u_{2},x\rangle+\langle u_{2},y\rangle+f((x,y),(u_{1},v_{1}))\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in U}\{\langle u_{2},x\rangle-\Gamma(x,v_{1})\}+\sup_{y\in V}\{\langle u_{2},y\rangle-(-\Gamma(u_{1},y))\}

\displaystyle=(\Gamma(\cdot,v_{1}))^{*}(u_{2})+(-\Gamma(u_{1},\cdot))^{*}(v_{2}),

for each pair
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in K.

Thus, for all
(u,v)\in S_{f}
and
(p,q)\in N_{S_{f}}(u,v)
, the assumption (
5
) reduces to

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[(\Gamma(\cdot,v))^{*}\left(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\right)+(-\Gamma(u,\cdot))^{*}\left(\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)-\sigma_{S_{f}}\left(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}},\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)\bigg]<\infty.

(29)

Now we define Algorithm
1
for equilibrium problem under saddle point constraints. For
n\in\mathbb{N},
given iterates
x_{n}=(x_{n}^{1},x_{n}^{2})\in K,
set
y_{n}=(y_{n}^{1},y_{n}^{2}),
where

y_{n}^{i}=J_{\lambda_{n}}^{g}(x_{n}^{i}-\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}Bx_{n}^{i}),i=1,2

and define
x_{n+1}=(x_{n+1}^{1},x_{n+1}^{1})\in K,
where

x_{n+1}^{i}=y_{n}^{i}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}(Bx_{n}^{i}-By_{n}^{i}),\text{ for }i=1,2.

Thus, Algorithm
1
reduces to

\displaystyle\langle A_{1}y_{n}^{1},s-y_{n}^{1}\rangle+\langle A_{2}y_{n}^{2},t-y_{n}^{2}\rangle+\beta_{n}[\Gamma(s,y_{n}^{2})-\Gamma(y_{n}^{1},t)]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\langle(x_{n+1}^{1},x_{n+1}^{2})-(x_{n}^{1},x_{n}^{2}),(u,v)-(y_{n}^{1},y_{n}^{2})\rangle\geq 0,\forall(s,t)\in U\times V.

(30)

In this scenario, Theorem
3
is recapitulated as follows:

Corollary 2
.

Let
\{(x_{n}^{1},x_{n}^{2})\}
and
\{(y_{n}^{1},y_{n}^{2})\}
be the sequences generated by (
5
) such that

(a)

assumption (
29
) holds;

(b)

\displaystyle 0<\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}<1/L;

(c)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}>0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=\infty.

Then the sequences
\{(x_{n}^{1},x_{n}^{2})\}
and
\{(y_{n}^{1},y_{n}^{2})\}
weakly converge in
S_{MI}
.

The following example illustrates the Corollary
2
.

Example 1
.

Take
U=V=[0,1]\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\Gamma(u,v)=uv+u+v,~\forall u,v\in\mathbb{R}.
Clearly,
\Gamma
is a convex-concave function on
K=[0,1]\times[0,1].
The saddle point set of
\Gamma
over
K
is singleton, that is,
S_{f}=\{(0,1)\}.
Also,
N_{S_{f}}(0,1)=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},
here
(0,1)
is the ordered pair in the set
\{0\}\times\{1\}.
Thus,

\sigma_{S_{f}}(x^{*})=\sup_{y\in S_{f}}\langle x^{*},y\rangle=\langle x^{*},(0,1)\rangle,~\forall x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

Let
(p,q)\in N_{S_{f}}(0,1)
, then

\sigma_{S_{f}}\left(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}},\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)=\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}.

Also,

\displaystyle(-\Gamma(0,\cdot))^{*}\left(\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\left\{\ \left\langle\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}},y\right\rangle+L(0,y)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in[0,1]}\left(1+\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)y

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1+\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}},&\text{ if }q>\frac{-\beta_{n}}{2}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle(\Gamma(\cdot,1))^{*}\left(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left\{\ \left\langle\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}},x\right\rangle+S(x,1)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left(\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}-1\right)2x-1

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}-3,&\text{ if }p>\beta_{n}\\
-1,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now, we need to confirm condition (
29
) and we do that in four cases.

Case I:
If
q>\frac{-\beta_{n}}{2}
and
p>\beta_{n},
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[(\Gamma(\cdot,v))^{*}\left(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}\right)+(-\Gamma(u,\cdot))^{*}\left(\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)-\sigma_{S_{f}}\left(\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}},\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\right)\bigg]

(31)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[1+\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}+\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}-3-\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}-2\bigg].

Since
\displaystyle\frac{p}{\beta_{n}}-1>0
, then the above series and
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}
converges and diverges together.

Case II:
If
q>\frac{-\beta_{n}}{2}
and
p\leq\beta_{n},
then (
31
) becomes

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[1+\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}-1-\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\bigg]=0.

Case III:
If
q\leq\frac{-\beta_{n}}{2}
and
p>\beta_{n},
then (
31
) reduces to

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[0+\frac{2p}{\beta_{n}}-3-\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\bigg]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[\frac{2(p-q)}{\beta_{n}}-3\bigg].

By the conditions taken on
p
and
q,
we have
p-q>\frac{3\beta_{n}}{2}
. Thus the above series and
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}
converges together.

Case IV:
If
q\leq\frac{-\beta_{n}}{2}
and
p\leq\beta_{n},
then (
31
) becomes

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\bigg[0-1-\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\bigg]

and
1+\frac{2q}{\beta_{n}}\leq 0,
thus above series and
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}
converges simultaneously.
Therefore, (
29
) is ensured whenever
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}<\infty.

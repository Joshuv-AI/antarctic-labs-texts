A doubly nonlinear evolution problem involving the fractional p p -Laplacian
Timothy A. Collier, Daniel Hauer

Abstract
In this article we focus on a doubly nonlinear nonlocal parabolic initial boundary value problem driven by the fractional p p -Laplacian equipped with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on a domain in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} and composed with a continuous, strictly increasing function. We establish well-posedness in L 1 L^{1} in the sense of mild solutions, a comparison principle, and for restricted initial data we obtain that mild solutions of the inhomogeneous evolution problem are strong. We obtain L q − L ∞ L^{q}-L^{\infty} regularity estimates for mild solutions, implying decay estimates and extending the property of strong solutions for more initial data. Moreover, we prove local and global Hölder continuity results as well as a comparison principle that yields extinction in finite time of mild solutions to the homogeneous evolution equation.

2.1
A brief primer on Gagliardo-Sobolev-Slobodeckiĭ spaces

In this section, we provide a short summary of
Gagliardo-Sobolev-Slobodeckiĭ spaces, which are necessary to study the
intial boundary value problem (
1.1
) with functional analytical
tools. For a deeper understanding of this theory, we refer the
interested reader to
[
36
,
27
]
or
[
37
]
.

For
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and an open subset
\Omega
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
we write
W^{s,p}
for the
Gagliardo-Sobolev-Slobodeckiĭ space
of fractional order
s
, also known as the
fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p}(\Omega)
given by

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{p}\Big|[u]_{s,p}<\infty\right\}

where

[u]_{s,p}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{p}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{d+sp}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

(2.1)

denotes the
s
-Gagliardo semi-norm
. The space
W^{s,p}
defines a Banach space if it is equipped with the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{s,p}}:=\left(\lVert u\rVert^{p}_{L^{p}}+[u]^{p}_{s,p}\right)^{1/p}.

Further, let
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, denoted by
W^{s,p}_{0}
, be the closure in
W^{s,p}
of the set
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
of test functions. By
[
36
, Theorem 10.1.1]
, the space
W^{s,p}_{0}

admits, for
1<p<\infty
, the characterization

W^{s,p}_{0}=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\,\bigg|\begin{array}[c]{l}\exists\text{ }\overline{u}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}\text{
s.t. }\overline{u}=u\text{ a.e.~on ${\mathbb{R}}^{d}$}\\
\text{ and }\overline{u}=0\text{ quasi-everywhere on }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega\end{array}\right\},

where
\overline{u}
denotes a (quasi-continuous) representative of
u
. Therefore, the space
W^{s,p}_{0}
incorporates
homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
in a weak sense.

2.2
Basics of nonlinear semigroup theory

We begin by reviewing some basic definitions and important results in nonlinear semigroup theory from the standard literature
[
20
,
22
]
and the
monograph
[
14
]
.

2.2.1
The general framework

Let
(\Sigma,\mu)
be a measure space with a positive
\sigma
-finite
measure
\mu
, and
M(\Sigma,\mu)
be the set of
\mu
-a.e. equivalence classes
of measurable functions
u:\Sigma\to{\mathbb{R}}
. For
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we
denote by
L^{q}_{\mu}
the classical
Lebesgue space

equipped with the standard
L^{q}
-norm

\lVert u\rVert_{q}:=\begin{cases}\bigg(\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\lvert u\rvert^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\bigg)^{1/q}&\quad\text{if $q<\infty$,}\\[7.0pt]
\inf\Big\{k\in[0,\infty]\,\Big|\,\mu(\{\lvert u\rvert>k\})=0\Big\}&\quad\text{if $q=\infty$.}\end{cases}

If
\Omega
is an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
, and
\mu
is the classical
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure restricted
to the trace-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap\Omega

then we write
L^{q}
instead of
L^{q}_{\mu}
.

Let
X\subseteq M(\Sigma,\mu)
be a Banach space with norm

\lVert\cdot\rVert_{X}
. The main object in this section is the
abstract Cauchy problem (in
X
)

\begin{cases}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}(t)+Au(t)\ni g(t)&\quad\text{for a.e.~$t\in(0,T)$,}\\
u(0)=u_{0},&\end{cases}

(2.2)

for given initial value
u_{0}\in\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
and
forcing term
g\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
. In (
2.2
),
A
denotes a (possibly) multi-valued operator

A:D(A)\to 2^{X}
on
X
with
effective domain

D(A):=\{u\in X\,|\,Au\neq\emptyset\}
,
the closure of
D(A)
in
X
denoted by
\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
, and

range

\Rg(A):=\bigcup_{u\in D(A)}Au
. In this setting, it is
standard to view an operator
A
as a subset of
X\times X
, or

relation
on
X
, and to identify
A
with its graph

A:=\Big\{(u,v)\in X\times X\,\Big|\;v\in Au\Big\}.

Definition 2.1
.

An operator
A
on
X
is called

accretive
(in
X
) if

\lVert u-\hat{u}\rVert_{X}\leq\lVert u-\hat{u}+\lambda(v-\hat{v})\rVert_{X}

(2.3)

for every
(u,v)
,
(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in A
and every

\lambda>0
. Further, an operator
A
on
X
is called
quasi accretive
if there is
an
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
A+\omega I
is accretive in

X
.

Clearly, if
A+\omega I
is accretive in
X
for some
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}

then
A+\tilde{\omega}I
is accretive for every

\tilde{\omega}\geq\omega
. Thus, there is no loss of generality in
assuming that if
A
is quasi accretive then there is an
\omega\geq 0

such that
A+\omega I
is accretive in
X
.

Equivalently,
A
is accretive in
X
if and only if, for every

\lambda>0
, the
resolvent operator
of
A
, defined by

J_{\lambda}:=(I+\lambda A)^{-1}
, is a single-valued mapping
from
\Rg(I+\lambda A)
to
D(A)
which is
contractive
(also
called
non-expansive
) with respect to the norm of

X
. That is,

\lVert J_{\lambda}u-J_{\lambda}\hat{u}\rVert_{X}\leq\lVert u-\hat{u}\rVert_{X}

for all
u
,
\hat{u}\in\Rg(I+\lambda A)
and
\lambda>0
.

The next definition is taken from
[
14
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
1\leq q<\infty
, we define the
q
-bracket
on

L^{q}_{\mu}
to be the mapping

[\cdot,\cdot]_{q}:L^{q}_{\mu}\times L^{q}_{\mu}\to{\mathbb{R}}

defined by

[u,v]_{q}:=\displaystyle{\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\frac{\frac{1}{q}\lVert u+\lambda v\rVert_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{q}\lVert u\rVert_{q}^{q}}{\lambda}}

for
u
,
v\in L^{q}_{\mu}
. We further define the bracket

[u,v]_{+}:=\displaystyle{\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\frac{\lVert[u+\lambda v]^{+}\rVert_{1}-\lVert[u]^{+}\rVert_{1}}{\lambda}}

for
u
,
v\in L^{1}_{\mu}
.

Then an operator
A
on

L^{q}_{\mu}
is accretive in
L^{q}_{\mu}
if and only if

[u-\hat{u},v-\hat{v}]_{q}\geq 0\qquad\text{for all $(u,v)$,
$(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in A$.}

The
q
-bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{q}

is upper semicontinuous (respectively, continuous if
1<q<\infty
) and if
1<q<\infty
,

[u,v]_{q}=\int_{\Sigma}\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u\,v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

(2.4)

for every

u
,
v\in L^{q}_{\mu}
. While for

,
[\cdot,\cdot]_{1}
reduces to the classical

brackets

[\cdot,\cdot]
on

given by

[u,v]_{1}=\int_{\{u\neq 0\}}\text{sign}_{0}(u)\,v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\{u=0\}}\lvert v\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

(2.5)

for
u
,
v\in L^{1}_{\mu}
, where the
restricted
signum

\sign_{0}
is defined by

\text{sign}_{0}(s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $s>0$,}\\
0&\text{if $s=0$,}\\
-1&\text{if $s<0$,}\end{cases}

for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
(cf.
[
21
, Section 2.2 &
Example (2.8)]
or
[
22
, pp 102]
).

Next, we introduce the following class of operators.

Definition 2.3
.

An operator
A
on
X
is called
m
-accretive in

if
A
is accretive in
X
and satisfies the so-called

\Rg(I+\lambda A)=X\qquad\text{for some (or equivalently all) $\lambda>0$,}

(2.6)

and an operator

on

is called

-accretive in

if there is an
\omega\geq 0
such that
A+\omega I
is

-accretive in

By the classical theory of nonlinear evolution problems
(cf.
[

]
, or
alternatively,
[

), the condition

‘

-accretive in

’
ensures that for given

and
g\in L^{1}(0,T;X)
, the
Cauchy problem (

) admits a unique

, which is
continuously dependent on

and

.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose

for some

in

and a finite sequence

with the following properties:

recursively solve the finite difference equation

u_{i}+(t_{i}-t_{i-1})Au_{i}\ni(t_{i}-t_{i-1})g_{i}+u_{i-1}\qquad\text{for every $i=1,\dots,N$}

and

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert u(t)-u_{\varepsilon,\sigma}(t)\rVert_{X}\leq\varepsilon.

In the homogeneous case
g\equiv 0
, if
A
is quasi
m
-accretive
in
X
, then the Crandall-Liggett theorem
(cf.
[
38
, Theorem I]
) states that for every element

u_{0}
of
\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
, there is a unique mild
solution
u
of (
2.2
) in
X
for every
T>0
and this solution
u
can be given by the
exponential formula

u(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(I+\tfrac{t}{n}A\right)^{-n}u_{0}

(2.8)

uniformly in
t
on compact intervals. For every

u_{0}\in\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
, setting

T_{t}u_{0}:=u(t),\qquad\text{for every $t\geq 0$,}

(2.9)

defines a (nonlinear)
strongly continuous semigroup

\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of
(
\omega
-)quasi contractions

T_{t}:\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}\to\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
with

\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
. More precisely, the family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}

satisfies the following three properties:

1.

semigroup property

T_{s+t}=T_{t}\circ T_{s}\qquad\text{for every $s$, $t\geq 0$,}

2.

strong continuity

\lim_{t\to 0+}\lVert T_{t}u-u\rVert_{X}=0\qquad\text{for every $u\in\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}$,}

3.

exponential growth property in
X
or

(
\omega
-)quasi contractivity in
X

\lVert T_{t}u-T_{t}v\rVert_{X}\leq e^{\omega\,t}\lVert u-v\rVert_{X}\qquad\text{for all $u$,
$v\in\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}$, $t\geq 0$.}

For the family
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on

\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
, the operator

A_{\circ}:=\Bigg\{(u,v)\in X\times X\Bigg|\;\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{T_{h}u-u}{h}=v\text{ in $X$}\Bigg\}

is a well-defined mapping
A_{\circ}:D(A_{\circ})\to X
with domain

D(A_{\circ}):=\Big\{u\in X\,\Big|\,\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{T_{h}u-u}{h}\text{ exists
in }X\Big\}

called the
infinitesimal generator

of
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. If
\{T_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is
\omega
-quasi contractive in

X
, then
-A_{\circ}
is
\omega
-quasi accretive in
X
.

Since mild solutions of Cauchy problem (
2.2
) are only the locally uniform (in time) limit of step functions (
2.7
) with values in

, it is important to know whether they are actually

of (

) in

.

Definition 2.5
.

Given

and

for some

, a
function

is called a

in

X

2.2
) if
u(0)=u_{0}
,

belongs to

W^{1,1}_{loc}((0,T);X)
and for a.e.
0<t<T
, one has that

u(t)\in D(A)
and
g(t)-\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}(t)\in Au(t)
.

Since we typically take

to be the closure of
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
in

, we also want to consider solutions with further regularity on
\varphi(u)
. Hence we consider

with the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

For given

satisfying (

)-(

) and

, a function

in

,
\varphi(u)\in L^{p}_{loc}((0,\infty);W^{s,p})
and for every test function

, one has that

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u\,\xi\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Bigg|_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u\,\xi_{t}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(f(x,u)-g)\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}}\frac{(\varphi(u)(t,x)-\varphi(u)(t,y))^{p-1}(\xi(t,x)-\xi(t,y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\mathrm{d}(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=0

.

Furthermore, if

is also differentiable with

.

If, for example,

for

and

, then

is a
uniformly convex Banach space and so the classical regularity

be a quasi

-accretive operator on

) is a

2.2
) and

every $t>0$,}

(2.10)

where

denotes the

of

given by the operator

Under additional
geometric conditions on the Banach space

, one has that

. Ignoring these details on

, we
nevertheless say that a strongly continuous semigroup

of quasi contractions on

is

is

-accretive in

is the family

).

If

is a Hilbert space

with inner product

then an important class of

-accretive operators in

given by the

of a proper, convex, lower semicontinuous

. In

; that is, an operator

is monotone if

$(\hat{u},\hat{v})\in A$}

(cf.
[

]
, and see

[

,
40
]
). For this class of operators

, the Cauchy problem (

) has the smoothing effect that every mild solution

of (

) is strong. This result is due to Brezis

]
(see
also
[
40
]
).

It is well known that the

-Laplacian

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
defined in (

) equipped with homogeneous

can be realized as such a

in

. To be more precise, let

,

, be an open domain. Define the energy functional
\mathcal{E}:L^{2}\to[0,\infty]
for

and

by

\mathcal{E}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}&\text{ if }u\in W^{s,p}_{0}\cap L^{2},\\
\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}\setminus W^{s,p}_{0},\end{cases}

(2.12)

for every

, where we have defined the

-Gagliardo semi-norm by (

) on

as in the definition of

. It is immediate that this energy functional is convex, lower semicontinuous and proper with effecive domain

]

(see

]

Proposition 2.7

(Characterization of

)
.

For

and

, let

be given by (

\partial\mathcal{E}(u)=\left\{h\in L^{2}\Biggr|\scalebox{0.9}{$\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}h(x)v(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{p-1}(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt\text{ for all }v\in W^{s,p}_{0}\cap L^{2}\end{aligned}$}\right\}.

We note that the case

can be characterized similarly with a formulation presented in
[

]
, although we do not consider

in this article.

2.2.2
Completely accretive and
T
-accretive operators

The notion of
completely accretive operators
was introduced in
[
42
]
by Crandall and Bénilan and further developed in
[
14
]
. Following the same notation as in these two references,
\mathcal{J}_{0}
denotes the set of all convex, lower semicontinuous functions
j:{\mathbb{R}}\to[0,+\infty]
satisfying
j(0)=0
.

Definition 2.8
.

A mapping
S:D(S)\to M(\Sigma,\mu)
with domain
D(S)\subseteq M(\Sigma,\mu)
is called a
complete contraction
if

\int_{\Sigma}j(Su-S\hat{u})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int_{\Sigma}j(u-\hat{u})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

for all
j\in\mathcal{J}_{0}
and every
u
,
\hat{u}\in D(S)
. An operator
A
on
M(\Sigma,\mu)
is called
completely
accretive
if for every
\lambda>0
, the resolvent operator

J_{\lambda}
of
A
is a
complete contraction
.

It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
14
]
) that the
fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
equipped with
Dirichlet boundary conditions is
m
-completely accretive in

L^{2}
. A more general class of operators is that of
T
-accretive
operators. In particular, choosing
j(\cdot)=\lvert[\,\cdot\,]^{+}\rvert^{q}\in\mathcal{J}_{0}
if

1\leq q<\infty
and
j(\cdot)=[[\,\cdot\,]^{+}-k]^{+}\in\mathcal{J}_{0}

for
k\geq 0
large enough if
q=\infty
shows that a complete
contraction
S
satisfies the following
T
-contractivity
property in
L^{q}_{\mu}
for every

1\leq q\leq\infty
.

Definition 2.9
.

A mapping
S:D(S)\to L^{q}_{\mu}
with domain

D(S)\subseteq L^{q}_{\mu}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, is called
a
T
-contraction
if

\lVert[Su-S\hat{u}]^{+}\rVert_{q}\leq\lVert[u-\hat{u}]^{+}\rVert_{q}

for every
u
,
\hat{u}\in D(S)
. We say that an operator
A

on
L^{q}_{\mu}
is
T
-accretive
if, for every
\lambda>0
, the resolvent

J_{\lambda}
of
A
defines a
T
-contraction with domain

D(J_{\lambda})=Rg(I+\lambda A)
.

By the previous remark, a completely accretive operator is
T
-accretive in
L^{q}_{\mu}
for all
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Furthermore, a
T
-accretive operator in
L^{q}_{\mu}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, is accretive in
L^{q}_{\mu}
and the resolvent is order-preserving in
L^{q}_{\mu}
. That is, denoting the usual order
relation on
L^{q}_{\mu}
by
\leq
, if
S
is
T
-contractive in
L^{q}_{\mu}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, then
u\leq\hat{u}
implies that
Su\leq S\hat{u}
for
u
,
\hat{u}\in L^{q}
(see

[
21
, Lemma 19.11]
for further properties).

We can naturally extend these definitions to quasi
m
-completely accretive operators (and similarly for quasi
m
-
T
-accretive operators).

Definition 2.10
.

An operator
A
on
M_{\mu}
is called
quasi completely accretive
if there is an
\omega>0

such that for every
\lambda>0
, the resolvent operator

J_{\lambda}
of
A+\omega I
is a complete contraction. Moreover,
for
1\leq q<\infty
, an operator
A
on
L^{q}_{\mu}
is said to
be
quasi
m
-completely accretive
on
L^{q}_{\mu}
if
there is an
\omega>0
such that
A+\omega I
is completely
accretive and the range condition (
2.6
) holds
with
X=L^{q}_{\mu}
.

2.2.3
T
-accretive operators in
L^{1}
with complete resolvent

In this last part of Section
2.2
, we introduce the class of
operators
A
which are merely
T
-accretive in
L^{1}_{\mu}
but
have a so-called
complete resolvent
. This class of operators was
introduced in
[
43
]
and further elaborated
in
[
14
]
. It’s worth mentioning that for a given completely accretive operator

in
L^{1}
, the composed operator

becomes

-accretive in
L^{1}
with complete resolvent so long as
\varphi
is a strictly increasing, continuous function on
{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
14
]
). Typical examples of this class of operators include the doubly-nonlinear operators
-\Delta_{p}\varphi
and
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}\varphi
. Hence we need to introduce the notion
complete
mappings.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
D(S)
be a subset of
M_{\mu}
. A mapping

S:D(S)\to M(\Sigma,\mu)
is called
complete
if

\int_{\Sigma}j(Su)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\int_{\Sigma}j(u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

(2.13)

for every
j\in\mathcal{J}_{0}
and
u\in D(S)
.

We now introduce the class of accretive operators in
L^{1}_{\mu}
with complete resolvent (similarly for
T
-accretive operators with complete resolvent).

Definition 2.12
.

An operator
A
on
L^{1}_{\mu}
is called
(
m
-)accretive in
L^{1}_{\mu}
with complete resolvent
if
A
is (
m
-)accretive in
L^{1}_{\mu}
and for every
\lambda>0
, the resolvent operator
J_{\lambda}:Rg(I+\lambda A)\to D(A)
of
A
is a complete mapping. For
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we call an operator
A
on
L^{1}_{\mu}

\omega
-quasi (
m
-)accretive in
L^{1}_{\mu}
with complete resolvent
(or simply
quasi (
m
-)accretive in
L^{1}_{\mu}
with complete resolvent
) if

is (
m
-)accretive in
L^{1}_{\mu}
with complete resolvent.

The condition (
2.13
) on the resolvent provides a growth estimate on

, allowing us to estimate
u(t)
in
L^{p}_{\mu}
by the norms of
u_{0}
and

in
L^{p}_{\mu}
. In particular, we prove such an estimate in Lemma
4.3
, extending
[
43
, Proposition 2.4]
.

2.2.4
The sub-differential operator in
X

We rely on the
m
-accretivity of
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
in
L^{1}
to obtain mild and strong solutions to (
1.1
). We also find that it is sufficient to work with the part in
L^{1\cap\infty}
to establish existence of such solutions. Hence we consider operators restricted to
L^{1\cap\infty}
and introduce a definition of subdifferential operators from
[
42
]
which we will apply in the case
X=L^{1}
. We will also use this to apply the results of
[
26
]
and in particular to obtain strong solutions. We include functionals on a (possibly distinct) subspace
Y
for completeness.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be linear subspaces of
M(\Sigma,\mu)
. Then for an energy functional
\mathcal{E}:Y\to(-\infty,\infty]
with effective domain
D(\mathcal{E}):=\{u\in Y\,|\,\mathcal{E}(u)<\infty\}
, we define the
part of
\mathcal{E}
in
X
by

\mathcal{E}_{|_{X}}(u)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{E}(u)&\text{for }u\in D(\mathcal{E})\cap X,\\
\infty&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Further, we define the operator
\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}
in
X
by

\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}=\Bigg\{(u,v)\in X\times X\,\Bigg|\begin{array}[c]{l}u\in D(\mathcal{E})\;\text{ and }\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}v(w-u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\leq\mathcal{E}(w)-\mathcal{E}(u)\\
\text{ for all }w\in X\text{ with }v(w-u)\in L^{1}_{\mu}\end{array}\Bigg\}

and the
part of
\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}
in
Z
by

(\partial_{X}\mathcal{E})_{|_{Z}}=\Big\{(u,h)\in Z\times Z\,\Big|(u,h)\in\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}\Big\}.

In the case
X=L^{2}_{\mu}
and
Y
a linear subspace of
M(\Sigma,\mu)
, this coincides with the previous definition of the subdifferential
\partial\mathcal{E}
in
L^{2}_{\mu}
given by (
2.11
). One sees that for a functional
\mathcal{E}:Y\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
and
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
given by
\mathcal{E}_{|_{L^{2}_{\mu}}}
, that
\partial_{L^{2}_{\mu}}\mathcal{E}=\partial\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
.

In the case
X=L^{1}_{\mu}
,
Y=L^{2}_{\mu}
, we have the inclusion
\left(\partial\mathcal{E}\right)_{|_{L^{1\cap\infty}_{\mu}}}\subseteq\left(\partial_{L^{1}_{\mu}}\mathcal{E}\right)_{|_{L^{1\cap\infty}_{\mu}}}
. However, since we are primarily interested in
m
-accretivity, they will be largely interchangeable due to the
m
-accretivity properties of
\left(\partial\mathcal{E}\right)_{|_{L^{1\cap\infty}_{\mu}}}
proved in Theorem
3.1
. For the class of operators
\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}
, Bénilan and
Crandall
[
42
]
found sufficient conditions implying that the closure
\overline{\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}}^{\mbox{}_{X}}
of

\partial_{X}\mathcal{E}
in
X
is
m
-completely accretive. We note that if
\mathcal{E}
is lower semicontinuous then
\overline{\partial_{L^{2}_{\mu}}\mathcal{E}}^{\mbox{}_{L^{2}_{\mu}}}=\partial_{L^{2}_{\mu}}\mathcal{E}
.

Theorem 2.14

(
[
42
, Lemma 7.1 & Theorem 7.4]
)
.

Let
X
be either
L^{r}_{\mu}
,

, or

. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If a functional

.

A

S_{n}=\{x\in\Sigma:\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{(1-h\omega)^{N-n}}\left|v_{n}+hg_{n}\right|>\lambda\}
so that we may separate terms.

\begin{split}&\left\lVert G_{\lambda}\left(\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{(1-h\omega)^{N-n}}v_{n}\right)\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert G_{\lambda}\left(\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{(1-h\omega)^{N-n+1}}\left(v_{n-1}+hg_{n-1}\right)\right)\right\rVert_{q}\\
&\hskip 17.07182pt\leq\left\lVert G_{\lambda}\left(\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{(1-h\omega)^{N-n+1}}v_{n-1}\right)\right\rVert_{q}+\frac{e^{-\omega t}h}{(1-h\omega)^{N-n+1}}\left\lVert g_{n-1}\mathds{1}_{S_{n-1}}\right\rVert_{q}.\end{split}

Repeating this, we have

\begin{split}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t}v_{N})\rVert_{q}&\leq\left\lVert G_{\lambda}\left(\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{\left(1-\tfrac{\omega(t-s)}{N}\right)^{N}}v_{0}\right)\right\rVert_{q}\\
&\quad+\frac{t-s}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{e^{-\omega t}}{\left(1-\tfrac{\omega(t-s)}{N}\right)^{N-n}}\left\lVert g_{n}\mathds{1}_{S_{n}}\right\rVert_{q}\end{split}

which converges to (
4.18
) as
N\rightarrow\infty
by the definition of mild solution and the projection (
4.20
).
∎

The following proposition introduces the pointwise estimate (
4.21
) for operators with complete resolvent which we will use as the condition for applying Theorem
4.1
to the doubly nonlinear problem (
1.1
) (see Section
5
). In particular, this provides (
4.4
).

Proposition 4.4
.

For
1\leq q_{0}<\infty
and
\omega\geq 0
, let
A
be an

\omega
-quasi
m
-accretive operator on
L^{q_{0}}_{\mu}
with complete resolvent. Suppose there are
q_{0}\leq q<r\leq\infty
,
1\leq\sigma<r
and
C>0
such that
A
satisfies the one-parameter Sobolev type inequality

\lVert G_{\lambda}(u)\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\leq C\;[G_{\lambda}(u),v+\omega(G_{\lambda}(u)+\lambda\mathds{1})]_{q}

(4.21)

for every
(u,v)\in A
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Let
g\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{q_{0}}_{\mu})\cap L^{1}(0,T;L^{q+\varepsilon}_{\mu})
for some
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in C([0,T];L^{q_{0}}_{\mu})\cap L^{1}(0,T;L^{1\cap\infty}_{\mu})
be the mild solution to (
4.1
) where
u_{0}\in\overline{D(A)}^{\mbox{}_{L^{q_{0}}_{\mu}}}\cap L^{1\cap\infty}_{\mu}
. Then for every
\lambda\geq 0
,
u
satisfies the
“level set energy inequality”

\begin{split}&\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}\,u(t_{2}))\rVert_{q}^{q}+\frac{q}{C}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{\omega(\sigma-q)s}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}\,u(s))\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{1}}\,u(t_{1}))\rVert_{q}^{q}+\lambda\,\omega\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}\,u(s))\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\quad+q\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{-\omega s}\left|[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s)),g]_{q}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(4.22)

for all
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
.

Proof.

Let
\set{s_{n}}_{n\in\set{0,1,...,N}}
be the discretization of the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
given by
s_{n}:=t_{1}+\frac{n(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}
. Then for all
n\in\set{0,...,N-1}
set

g_{N}(s):=\frac{N}{t_{2}-t_{1}}\int_{s_{n}}^{s_{n+1}}g(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\quad\text{for }s\in[s_{n},s_{n+1})

which will converge to
g
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{q_{0}}_{\mu})\cap L^{1}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Let
\set{v_{n}}_{n\in\set{0,1,...,N}}
be the associated family of solutions to the time discretized Cauchy problem satisfying

v_{n+1}=J_{\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{N}}^{A}\left(v_{n}+\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{N}g_{N}(s_{n})\right)

(4.23)

for all
n\in\set{0,...,N-1}
with
v_{0}=u(t_{1})
. Note that by the complete resolvent property of
A
with
u_{0}\in L^{q}_{\mu}
and
g\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})
,
v_{n}\in L^{q}_{\mu}
for all
n\in\set{0,...,N}
. We first obtain a discrete version of the integral estimate (
4.22
) by discretizing with a telescoping sum and applying a product rule. For
q\geq 1
we use the following property of
q
-brackets,

[u,v]_{q}\leq\frac{1}{q}\lVert u+v\rVert_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{q}\lVert u\rVert_{q}^{q}

(4.24)

for every
u,v\in L^{q}_{\mu}
. Here we apply (
4.24
) to the following telescoping sum, taking
u=G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})
and
v=G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1})-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})
.

\displaystyle\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}v_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{1}}u(t_{1}))\rVert_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q}^{q}-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1})\rVert_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}q[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1})]_{q}.

Noting that
G_{\lambda}
is a Lipschitz continuous function, we can differentiate almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}
. Here we define

G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }|s|>\lambda,\\
0&\text{ if }|s|\leq\lambda,\end{cases}

and

c_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\theta e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}+(1-\theta)e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1})d\theta

so that we can rewrite this difference as an integral of the derivative with

G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1})=c_{n}\left(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}-e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}\right).

Then returning to the estimate in the discrete setting,

\displaystyle\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}v_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{1}}u(t_{1}))\rVert_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\hskip 31.2982pt\leq q\sum_{n=1}^{N}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),c_{n}\left(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}-e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}\right)]_{q}

\displaystyle\hskip 31.2982pt\leq q\sum_{n=1}^{N}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),c_{n}e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}(v_{n}-v_{n-1})]_{q}

\displaystyle\hskip 31.2982pt\quad+q\sum_{n=1}^{N}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),c_{n}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}-e^{-\omega s_{n-1}})v_{n}]_{q}.

Defining

R_{n}=[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),(c_{n}-1)(v_{n}-v_{n-1})]_{q},

we can rewrite the previous estimate as

\begin{split}&\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}v_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{1}}u(t_{1}))\rVert_{q}^{q}\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt\leq\frac{q(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),-Av_{n}+g_{N}(s_{n-1})]_{q}\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt\quad+q\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}R_{n}+q\sum_{n=1}^{N}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}c_{n}]_{q}\left(1-e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\right).\end{split}

We now consider the values of
v_{n}
and
v_{n-1}
for almost every
x\in\Sigma
to show that
R_{n}
is non-positive. Note that
c_{n}\leq 1
and in particular,

\displaystyle c_{n}(x)\in\begin{cases}\set{0}&\text{ if }|\theta e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}+(1-\theta)e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}|\leq\lambda\text{ for a.e.~}\theta\in(0,1),\\
\set{1}&\text{ if }|\theta e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}+(1-\theta)e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}|>\lambda\text{ for a.e.~}\theta\in(0,1),\\
(0,1)&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
|e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)|\leq\lambda
implies that
G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x))=0
and so does not contribute to
R_{n}
, we consider only
x\in\Sigma
such that
|e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)|>\lambda
. Then there will be some subinterval of
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

|\theta e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)+(1-\theta)e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}(x)|>\lambda

implying that
c_{n}(x)>0
. If
c_{n}=1
then
c_{n}(x)-1=0
and so this will not contribute to
R_{n}
. Hence we only consider
x
such that
c_{n}(x)\in(0,1)
. Since
|e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)|>\lambda
, this implies that either

|e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}(x)|\leq\lambda

or

\sign(v_{n-1}(x))=-\sign(v_{n}(x)).

For the first case,
G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}(x))=0
so

\displaystyle(G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)))^{q-1}(v_{n}(x)-v_{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle\quad=\left((G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)))^{q-1}-(G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}v_{n-1}(x)))^{q-1}\right)(v_{n}(x)-v_{n-1}(x))

\displaystyle\quad\geq 0.

Note that for
q=1
,
(G_{\lambda}(v_{n}(x)))^{q-1}=\sign(G_{\lambda}(v_{n}(x)))
. For the second case,
\sign(v_{n}(x)-v_{n-1}(x))=\sign(v_{n}(x))
so

(G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}(x)))^{q-1}(v_{n}(x)-v_{n-1}(x))\geq 0.

Putting this together we have that
R_{n}\leq 0
. Returning to the discrete estimate,

\begin{split}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}v_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}&-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{1}}u(t_{1}))\rVert_{q}^{q}\\
&\leq\frac{q(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),-Av_{n}+g_{N}(s_{n})]_{q}\\
&\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q}^{q}\left(1-e^{\frac{q\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\right).\end{split}

Note that by (
4.21
),

\displaystyle e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}

\displaystyle[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),Av_{n}]_{q}=e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}e^{-(q-1)\omega s_{n}}[G_{\lambda e^{\omega s_{n}}}(v_{n}),Av_{n}]_{q}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}e^{-q\omega s_{n}}[G_{\lambda e^{\omega s_{n}}}(v_{n}),Av_{n}]_{q}

\displaystyle\geq e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}e^{-q\omega s_{n}}\frac{1}{C}\lVert G_{\lambda e^{\omega s_{n}}}(v_{n})\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad-e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}e^{-q\omega s_{n}}\omega\left(\lVert G_{\lambda e^{\omega s_{n}}}(v_{n})\rVert_{q}^{q}+\lambda e^{\omega s_{n}}\lVert G_{\lambda e^{\omega s_{n}}}(v_{n})\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\frac{e^{\omega s_{n}(\sigma-q)}}{C}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad-e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\omega\left(\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q}^{q}+\lambda\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}\right).

Hence we have

\begin{split}e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}&[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),Av_{n}]_{q}\\
&\geq\frac{e^{\omega s_{n}(\sigma-q)}}{C}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\\
&\quad-e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\omega\left(\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q}^{q}+\lambda\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}\right).\end{split}

We now aim to take
N\rightarrow\infty
, first converting the discrete sums to integrals. Let
U_{N}
be a stepwise solution to (
4.23
) such that

U_{N}(s)=v_{0}\mathds{1}_{\set{0}}(s)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}v_{n}\mathds{1}_{(s_{n-1},s_{n}]}(s)

for every
s\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
. We have

\displaystyle\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}v_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{1}}u(t_{1}))\rVert_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\frac{q(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\frac{e^{\omega s_{n}(\sigma-q)}}{C}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\omega[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})-\lambda\sign(G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}))]_{q}

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{-\omega s_{n-1}}[G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n}),g_{N}(s_{n})]_{q}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s_{n}}v_{n})\rVert_{q}^{q}\left(1-e^{\frac{q\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq-\frac{q}{C}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{\omega(\sigma-q)\left(s-\sign(\sigma-q)\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{N}\right)}\left\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega(s+\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{N})}U_{N})\right\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\frac{e^{\frac{q\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}-1}{\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{N}}-q\omega e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\right|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+q\omega\lambda e^{\frac{\omega(t_{2}-t_{1})}{N}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N})\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+q\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N})|^{q-1}|g_{N}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

We now prove convergence of each term in the estimate in order to obtain the continuous version (
4.22
). Noting that
U_{N}(s)\rightarrow u(s)
in
C([0,T];L^{q_{0}}_{\mu})
and since
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{q}^{q}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}
are lower semicontinuous on
L^{q_{0}}_{\mu}
, we have that

\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}v_{N})\rVert_{q}^{q}\geq\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega t_{2}}u(t_{2}))\rVert_{q}^{q}

and applying Fatou’s lemma,

\begin{split}\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}&e^{\omega(\sigma-q)s}\left\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega(s+\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{N})}U_{N}(s))\right\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\geq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}e^{\omega(\sigma-q)s}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Next, note that by the complete resolvent property of
A
and Lemma
4.3
we can estimate
U_{N}
and
u
in
L^{q}_{\mu}
uniformly on
[0,T]
. Hence let
M
bound both
\lVert U_{N}\rVert_{q}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{q}
.

\begin{split}\left|\frac{1-e^{-\frac{q\omega t}{N}}}{\frac{t}{N}}-q\omega\right|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))\rVert_{q}^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&\leq\left|\frac{1-e^{-\frac{q\omega t}{N}}}{\frac{t}{N}}-q\omega\right|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}M^{q}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\rightarrow 0\end{split}

as
N\rightarrow 0
. For the next term we prove uniform convergence of
G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N})
to
G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u)
in
C([0,T];L^{q-1}_{\mu})
when
\lambda>0
. For this fix
s\in[0,T]
and let

f_{N}=G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))

so that
f_{N}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{q_{0}}
. We note that

\begin{split}\lVert f_{N}\rVert_{q}&\leq 2M\end{split}

and by Chebyshev’s inequality

\begin{split}\mu(\{x\in\Sigma:|f_{N}|>0\})&\leq\mu(\{x\in\Sigma:e^{-\omega s}|U_{N}|\geq\lambda\text{ or }e^{-\omega s}|u|\geq\lambda\})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{q}}\left(\lVert e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s)\rVert_{q}+\lVert e^{-\omega s}u(s)\rVert_{q}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{2M}{\lambda^{q}}.\end{split}

Here we consider cases for
q
. For
q-1\geq q_{0}
, apply Hölder’s inequality with
\theta
chosen to satisfy

\frac{\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{1-\theta}{q}=\frac{1}{q-1}

to obtain

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lVert f_{N}\rVert_{q-1}

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left(\lVert f_{N}\rVert_{q_{0}}^{\theta}\lVert f_{N}\rVert_{q}^{1-\theta}\right)=0.

For
q-1<q_{0}
, we apply Jensen’s inequality noting that
|\cdot|^{\frac{q_{0}}{q-1}}
is convex. Let

\Sigma_{N}=\{x\in\Sigma:|f_{N}|>0\}

then we can again estimate
f_{N}
with

\lVert f_{N}\rVert_{q-1}\leq\mu(\Sigma_{N})^{\frac{q_{0}}{q-1}-1}\lVert f_{N}\rVert_{q_{0}}.

Note that for
q-1<1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N})\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}-\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u)\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left|\left|G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N})\right|^{q-1}-\left|G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u)\right|^{q-1}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left|f_{N}\right|^{q-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu

so that

\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}=\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))\rVert_{q-1}^{q-1}.

For the last term, note that
g_{N}\rightarrow g
in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. So by a corollary of Riesz-Fischer, there exists a subsequence
N_{k}
and a function
h\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})
such that
|g_{N_{k}}(x)|\leq h(x)
for all
k
and a.e.
x\in\Sigma
. Similarly, interpolating between
q_{0}
and
q+\varepsilon
,

\begin{split}\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))\rVert_{q}&\leq\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))\rVert_{q_{0}}^{\theta}\times\\
&\qquad\lVert G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))-G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))\rVert_{q+\varepsilon}^{1-\theta}\end{split}

for some
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then with
U_{N}
and
u
bounded in
L^{q+\varepsilon}_{\mu}
,
G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N}(s))\rightarrow G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}u(s))
in
L^{q_{0}}_{\mu}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
uniformly for all
t\in[0,T]
. Hence taking another subsequence we have a dominant
H_{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})
. So we can estimate the integrand pointwise

\left|G_{\lambda}(e^{-\omega s}U_{N_{k}}(s))^{q-1}g_{N}\right|\leq H_{\lambda}^{q-1}h\quad\text{a.e.~on }\Sigma\times[0,T).

Moreover this dominant is in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}_{\mu})
with

\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Sigma}H_{\lambda}^{q-1}h\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\lVert H_{\lambda}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})}\lVert h\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{q}_{\mu})}.

Hence we apply dominated convergence to obtain the continuous estimate

(
4.22
).
∎

We now show that this pointwise estimate (
4.21
) for Proposition
4.4
implies a similar estimate when adding a Lipschitz perturbation.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
1\leq q<\infty
, let
A
be an operator on
L^{q}_{\mu}

and suppose there are
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
\sigma>0
,

\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\geq 0
and
C>0
such that (
4.21
) is satisfied for all
(u,v)\in A
. Let
F:L^{q}_{\mu}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
be Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
\omega^{\prime}\geq 0
and satisfying
F(0)=0
. Then, the operator
A+F
in
L^{q}_{\mu}
satisfies

\lVert G_{\lambda}(u)\rVert_{r}^{\sigma}\leq C\;[G_{\lambda}(u),v+(\omega+\omega^{\prime})(G_{\lambda}(u)+\lambda\sign(u))]_{q}.

(4.25)

Proof.

Let
\hat{v}=v+F(u)
. Then, since
[\cdot,\cdot]_{q}
is
linear in the second term,

\begin{split}[G_{\lambda}(u),\hat{v}+&(\omega+\omega^{\prime})(G_{\lambda}(u)+\lambda\sign(u))]_{q}\\
&=[G_{\lambda}(u),v+\omega(G_{\lambda}(u)+\lambda\sign(u))]_{q}\\
&\quad+[G_{\lambda}(u),F(u)+\omega^{\prime}(G_{\lambda}(u)+\lambda\sign(u))]_{q}.\end{split}

(4.26)

By the Lipschitz condition,

-\omega^{\prime}|u|\leq F(u)\leq\omega^{\prime}|u|.

Hence

\begin{split}[G_{\lambda}(u),F(u)]_{q}&\geq-\omega^{\prime}[G_{\lambda}(u),u]_{q}\\
&=-\omega^{\prime}[G_{\lambda}(u),G_{\lambda}(u)+\lambda\sign(u)]_{q}.\end{split}

So applying this and the initial assumption to (
4.26
), we have (
4.25
).
∎

We now extend the
L^{q}_{\mu}-L^{\infty}_{\mu}
regularity of Theorem
4.1
to obtain
L^{\ell}_{\mu}-L^{\infty}_{\mu}
regularity as in Theorem
1.2
.
For this we consider (
4.6
) applied to
\tilde{u}_{0}=u(\tfrac{t}{2})
and
\tilde{g}(s)=g(s+\tfrac{t}{2})
.
The following theorem is an extension of
[
14
, Chapter 6]
and in particular with

gives the desired regularity result.

Theorem 4.6
.

For
1\leq\ell<q<r\leq\infty
and
T>0
, let
g\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\ell}_{\mu}\cap L^{q}_{\mu})
and
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\ell}_{\mu}\cap L^{r}_{\mu})
satisfying the exponential growth property (

) for some

. Suppose there exist increasing functions

,
c_{2}(t)
with
c_{1}(t)>0
and
c_{2}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
and exponents
\alpha\geq 0
,
0<\gamma^{*}\leq\gamma<\infty
such that

\begin{split}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{r}&\leq\max\left\{c_{1}(\tfrac{t}{2})\left(\tfrac{2}{t}+\omega\right)^{\alpha}\left(\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{q}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}\right)^{\gamma},\right.\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt\left.c_{2}(\tfrac{t}{2})\left(\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{q}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}\right)^{\gamma^{*}}\right\}\end{split}

(4.27)

for every
t\in(0,T]
. Define

\theta:=1-\gamma\left(\frac{\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{q}}{\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}}\right)

and suppose that
\theta>0
. Then one has the
L^{\ell}_{\mu}-L^{r}_{\mu}
estimate

\begin{split}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{r}&\leq 2^{\gamma}\left(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}+\sup_{s\in(0,t]}N(s)^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\theta}}\max\left\{c_{1}(t)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left(\tfrac{1}{t}+\omega\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\theta}},c_{2}(t)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right\}\times\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(e^{\omega t}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\ell}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(t-\tau)}\lVert g(\tau)\rVert_{\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{\theta_{\ell}\gamma}{\theta}}\end{split}

(4.28)

for all
t\in(0,T]
where

\begin{split}N(t)&:=\sup\limits_{s\in(0,t]}\frac{M(\tfrac{s}{2})\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{s}{2},s;L^{q}_{\mu})}+c_{2}(\tfrac{s}{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}{M(s)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left(e^{\omega s}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\ell}+\int_{0}^{s}e^{\omega(s-\tau)}\lVert g(\tau)\rVert_{\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{\theta_{\ell}}{\theta}}},\\
M(t)&:=\max\left\{c_{1}(t)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\tfrac{1}{t}+\omega\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}},c_{2}(t)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\right\},\end{split}

and

\theta_{\ell}:=\frac{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}}{\frac{1}{\ell}-\frac{1}{r}}.

Proof.

We first note that since
\gamma^{*}\leq\gamma
, we can estimate

\begin{split}\left(\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{q}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}\right)^{\gamma^{*}}&\leq\max\left\{1,\left(\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{q}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}\right)^{\gamma}\right\}\end{split}

for
t\in[0,T]
.
Hence, taking (
4.27
) to the power
\frac{1}{\gamma}
, we have

\begin{split}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{r}^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}&\leq\max\left\{c_{1}(\tfrac{t}{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\tfrac{2}{t}+\omega\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}\left(\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{q}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}\right),\right.\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt\left.c_{2}(\tfrac{t}{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left(\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{q}+\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}\right),c_{2}(\tfrac{t}{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\right\}.\end{split}

We then apply standard interpolation on the
L^{q}_{\mu}
norm with exponent
\theta_{\ell}
and the growth estimate (
4.18
) on
\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{\ell}
to obtain

\begin{split}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{q}&\leq\left(e^{\omega t}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\ell}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(t-\tau)}\lVert g(\tau)\rVert_{\ell}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\theta_{\ell}}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{r}^{1-\theta_{\ell}}\end{split}

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
By choice of
\theta
,
\theta_{\ell}
we have the relation

\gamma(1-\theta_{\ell})=1-\theta.

Then for all
t\in(0,T]
,

\begin{split}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{r}^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}&\leq M(\tfrac{t}{2})\left(e^{\frac{\omega t}{2}}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\ell}+\int_{0}^{\tfrac{t}{2}}e^{\omega(\tfrac{t}{2}-\tau)}\lVert g(\tau)\rVert_{\ell}\right)^{\theta_{\ell}}\lVert u(\tfrac{t}{2})\rVert_{r}^{\frac{1-\theta}{\gamma}}\\
&\quad+M(\tfrac{t}{2})\lVert g\rVert_{L^{1}(\frac{t}{2},t;L^{q}_{\mu})}+c_{2}(\tfrac{t}{2})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.\end{split}

(4.29)

We aim to produce comparable terms on either side of this equation. Since
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are increasing,
M(\tfrac{t}{2})\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}}M(t)
. Furthermore,

Hence we define

K_{u}(t):=\frac{M(t)^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\lVert u(t)\rVert_{r}^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}}{\left(e^{\omega t}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\ell}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(t-\tau)}\lVert g(\tau)\rVert_{\ell}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{\theta_{\ell}}{\theta}}}\quad\text{for }t\in[0,T]

(4.30)

in order to estimate and rearrange (
4.29
) into a relation involving
K_{u}
and
N
. Note that if the denominator of (
4.30
) is zero, we can add some arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
to the denominator of both
K_{u}
and
F
, taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in the final estimate. Now we fix
t\in(0,T]
so that after rearranging we may take a supremum over
(0,t]
to obtain

\sup_{s\in(0,t]}K_{u}(s)\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\sup_{s\in(0,\frac{t}{2}]}K_{u}(s)^{1-\theta}+N(t).

We now aim to split the forcing term
N(t)
so as to incorporate this into each
K_{u}
term. For this we define
D(t)\geq(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}
for

such that

(4.31)

Noting that
\theta\in(0,1)
, this is possible as the function

for

and
\alpha\in(0,1)
is continuous, satisfies

and is strictly increasing for

(in particular for

). Further, we can estimate (

\displaystyle N(t)

\displaystyle=\left(D(t)^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\left(D(t)^{\theta}-2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(D(t)^{\theta}-2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

so that

D(t)\leq\left(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}+N(t)^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

(4.32)

Then,

\sup_{s\in(0,t]}K_{u}(s)-D(t)\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\left(\left(\sup_{s\in\left(0,\tfrac{t}{2}\right]}K_{u}(s)\right)^{1-\theta}-D(t)^{1-\theta}\right).

Noting that

is bounded for all

, either

\sup_{s\in(0,t]}K_{u}(s)\leq D(t)

or we can extend to a supremum over

and combine terms, obtaining

\begin{split}\sup_{s\in(0,t]}K_{u}(s)-D(t)&\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\left(\sup_{s\in(0,t]}K_{u}(s)-D(t)\right)^{1-\theta}\\
\left(\sup_{s\in(0,t]}K_{u}(s)-D(t)\right)^{\theta}&\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}.\end{split}

In either case, we have the uniform bound,

K_{u}(s)\leq\left(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+D(t).

for all
s\in(0,t]
. Applying (

\displaystyle K_{u}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+\left(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}+N(t)^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(2^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma\theta}}+N(t)^{\theta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}.

Rewriting this as an estimate on

The following lemma allows us to estimate the

Recall that we use the notation

for powers and that

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
m\geq 1
and
\lambda\geq 0
. Given
a
,
b\in\mathbb{R}
define
a_{\lambda}=G_{\lambda}(a)
,
b_{\lambda}=G_{\lambda}(b)
. Then,

\displaystyle(a^{m}-b^{m})^{p-1}(a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m})\geq|a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m}|^{p}.

Proof.

If
a_{\lambda}-b_{\lambda}=0
, both sides are
0
. For
a_{\lambda}-b_{\lambda}\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\sign(a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m})

\displaystyle=\sign(a^{m}-b^{m})

\displaystyle|a^{m}-b^{m}|^{p-2}(a^{m}-b^{m})(a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m})

\displaystyle=|a^{m}-b^{m}|^{p-1}|a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m}|

so we only need to prove that
|a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m}|\leq|a^{m}-b^{m}|
. We take cases, assuming without loss of generality that
|a|\geq|b|
. First suppose that
|a|\leq\lambda
or
|b|\leq\lambda
so that
a_{\lambda}=b_{\lambda}=0
and the inequality is clear. Next, if
|b|\leq\lambda
and
|a|>\lambda
, then

|a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m}|=(|a|-\lambda)^{m}\leq(|a|-|b|)^{m}\leq|a|^{m}-|b|^{m}\leq|a^{m}-b^{m}|.

Hence we consider cases for
a
,
b
corresponding to
|a|>\lambda
and
|b|>\lambda
. Suppose that
a>\lambda
and
b>\lambda
. Then noting that for
m\geq 1
,
|x|^{m-1}
is non-decreasing on the set
[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle a^{m}-b^{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{b}^{a}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}x^{m}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{b}^{a}m|x-\lambda|^{m-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=(a-\lambda)^{m}-(b-\lambda)^{m}.

Similarly for
a<-\lambda
,
b<-\lambda
, noting that
|x|^{m-1}
is non-increasing on
(-\infty,0]
,

a^{m}-b^{m}\geq(a+\lambda)^{m}-(b+\lambda)^{m}.

Finally, suppose that
a>\lambda
and
b<-\lambda
(similarly for
a<-\lambda
and
b>\lambda
). Then

\displaystyle|a_{\lambda}^{m}-b_{\lambda}^{m}|

\displaystyle=(a-\lambda)^{m}-(b+\lambda)^{m}

\displaystyle\leq a^{m}-b^{m}

\displaystyle=|a^{m}-b^{m}|.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in

,

for all

and

. In particular, this is (
4.21

,

Proof.

Let

one sees that

\begin{split}&[G_{\lambda}u,(-\Delta_{p})^{s}(u^{m})]_{m+1}\\
&\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\lvert((u(t,x))^{m}-(u(t,y))^{m}\rvert^{p-2}((u(t,x))^{m}-(u(t,y))^{m})}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\times\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt((G_{\lambda}u(t,x))^{m}-(G_{\lambda}u(t,y))^{m})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
&\qquad\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|(G_{\lambda}u(t,x))^{m}-(G_{\lambda}u(t,y))^{m}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

&\in L^{\infty},\end{split}

To prove the density result, fix

Estimating

\begin{split}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y&\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{\sup_{s\in\Omega}|u^{\prime}(s)|^{p-1}|x-y|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{d+sp}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\

In the porous medium case,

8.1
A parabolic comparison principle

We first introduce the inhomogeneous fractional Sobolev space for
\Omega
an open domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

In this setting we consider the following inhomogeneous evolution equation,

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} u_{t}+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}\varphi(u)+f(\cdot,u)&=g\quad&&\text{ on }\Omega\times[0,T],\\

(8.2)

Discrete iterated integrals and cyclic sum formulas
Hanamichi Kawamura

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a discrete version of iterated integrals by the naive (equally divided) Riemann sum. In particular, basic three formulas for usual iterated integrals are discretized. Moreover, we proved cyclic sum formulas for discrete iterated integrals. They imply the cyclic sum formula for multiple polylogarithms.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11M32.

1.
Introduction

We often encounter
iterated integrals
like

I_{\mathrm{dch}}(1_{0};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};(-1)_{1})=\int_{0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}<1}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}-a_{1}}\cdots\frac{dt_{k}}{t_{k}-a_{k}}\qquad(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(0,1)),

in recent researches of number theory, knot theory, mathematical physics, etc.
One of most important examples of iterated integrals is the
multiple zeta value
, which has the expression as a nested sum other than the iterated integral:

(1.1)

(-1)^{r}I_{\mathrm{dch}}(1_{0};e_{1}e_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots e_{1}e_{0}^{k_{r}-1};(-1)_{1})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\coloneqq\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

General iterated integrals, denoted as
I_{\gamma}(x;w;y)
, satisfy many important formulas.
The typical examples are the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be paths.
Denote by
x_{\circ}
(resp.
y_{\circ}
) the initial (resp. final) point of
\circ\in\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\}
.
Assume that
y_{\gamma}
is equal to
x_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.
Then, for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in D
and
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
, we have

(1)

(Path composition formula)
\displaystyle I_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(x_{\gamma};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};y_{\gamma^{\prime}})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}};y_{\gamma})I_{\gamma^{\prime}}(x_{\gamma^{\prime}};e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};y_{\gamma^{\prime}})
.

(2)

(Reversal formula)
I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};y_{\gamma})=(-1)^{k}I_{\gamma^{-1}}(y_{\gamma};e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}};x_{\gamma})
.

(3)

(Shuffle product formula)
I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}w^{\prime};y_{\gamma})=I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};w;y_{\gamma})I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};w^{\prime};y_{\gamma})
.

Recently, Maesaka, Seki and Watanabe
[
15
]
discovered that:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
N,r,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
be positive integers.
Put
k\coloneqq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
and define
S\coloneqq\{0,k_{1},k_{1}+k_{2},\ldots,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}\}
.
Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0=n_{0}\leq n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{k}\leq n_{k+1}=N\\
i\in S\implies n_{i}\neq n_{i+1}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i\in S\setminus\{k\}}\frac{1}{N-n_{i+1}}\right)\left(\prod_{i\in\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\setminus S}\frac{1}{n_{i+1}}\right)=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}<N}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

Comparing this fact and (
1.1
), we can consider the left-hand side above as a
discretization
of
I_{\mathrm{dch}}(1_{0};e_{1}e_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots e_{1}e_{0}^{k_{r}-1};(-1)_{1})

In this paper, we consider a discretization
\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))
for more general iterated integrals and associated notions.
The basic three formulas for iterated integrals, which are known as the
path composition formula
,
reversal formula
and
shuffle product formula
respectively, are discretized:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be composable discrete paths.
Then, for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in D
and a
\gamma
-shuffleable pair
(w,w^{\prime})\in(\mathfrak{H}_{D})^{2}
, we have

(1)

(Path composition formula, Corollary
3.11
)

\Delta_{N,\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma^{\prime}))=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}};t(\gamma))\Delta_{N,\gamma^{\prime}}(s(\gamma^{\prime});e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma^{\prime})).

(2)

(Reversal formula, Theorem
3.12
)

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma))=(-1)^{k}\Delta_{N,\gamma^{-1}}(t(\gamma);e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}};s(\gamma)).

(3)

(Asymptotic shuffle product formula, Theorem
3.17
)

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w^{\prime};t(\gamma))=\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}w^{\prime};t(\gamma))+O(N^{-1}\log^{\bullet}N),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

In the second main theorem, we evaluate a cyclic sum on discrete iterated integrals along a straight path.

Theorem 1.4

(
=
Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}
(
k\geq 2
) be an element of
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
and
\gamma=(x,y)
be a positive straight piece.
Then we have

(1.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\
a_{i}\neq x\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{i}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i-1}}e_{x};(y,-))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\
a_{i}\neq y\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{y}e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i-1}}e_{x})\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\
a_{i}\in\{x,y\}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{i}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}};(y,-))-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i},a_{i},\ldots,a_{k}};(y,-)).

As its consequence, we show that there is a generalization of the cyclic sum formula for multiple zeta values (
[
10
]
) for multiple polylogarithms (see Corollary
5.4
).

This paper is organized as follows: we give a brief review about usual iterated integrals in Section
2
.
After introducing discrete iterated integrals and proving basic properties in Section
3
, we see how discrete iterated integrals converge to usual ones in Section
4
.
Finally, in Section
5
, we prove the cyclic sum formula for discrete iterated integrals along a straight path.

2.
Review on usual iterated integrals

In what follows, we fix a finite subset
D
of
\mathbb{C}
and put
M\coloneqq\mathbb{C}\setminus D
unless noted otherwise.

2.1.
Tangential base points and paths

For
p\in D
and a non-zero tangential vector
v\in T_{p}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}
, we call a tuple
v_{p}\coloneqq(p,v)
a
tangential base point
, where the isomorphism is induced by the canonical coordinate of
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathrm{pr}(v_{p})\coloneqq p
for a tangential base point
v_{p}
and
\mathrm{pr}(p)\coloneqq p
for a usual base point (
=
an element of
\mathbb{C}\setminus D
)
p
.

Definition 2.1

(Path)
.

Let
x
and
y
be (tangential or not) base points.
We call
\gamma
a path if
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
is a piecewise smooth path satisfying
\gamma(0)=\mathrm{pr}(x)
,
\gamma(1)=\mathrm{pr}(y)
,
\gamma((0,1))\in M
,

\lim_{t\to+0}\frac{\gamma(t)-\mathrm{pr}(x)}{t}=v\qquad\text{if }x=v_{\mathrm{pr}(x)}\text{ is tangential}

and

\lim_{t\to 1-0}\frac{\gamma(t)-\mathrm{pr}(y)}{t-1}=-w\qquad\text{if }y=w_{\mathrm{pr}(y)}\text{ is tangential}.

The point
x
(resp.
y
) is called the
initial point
(resp. the
final point
) of
\gamma
.

The set
\pi_{1}(M;y,x)
of homotopy classes for such a path
\gamma
(with the initial point
x
and the final point
y
) is defined and they admit the composition

\pi_{1}(M;y,x)\times\pi_{1}(M;z,y)\to\pi_{1}(M;z,x);~(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\mapsto\gamma\gamma^{\prime}

and inversion
\pi_{1}(M;y,x)\to\pi_{1}(M;x,y);~\gamma\mapsto\gamma^{-1}
(cf.
[
1
, §3.8.3]
).
For a path
\gamma
and real numbers
a,b
satisfying
0\leq a<b\leq 1
, we put
\gamma_{a,b}(t)\coloneqq\gamma((1-t)a+tb)
.
Note that, for a path
\gamma
, choosing suffciently small positive numbers
z
and
z^{\prime}
, we get the path
\gamma_{z,1-z^{\prime}}
whose initial and final point are not tangential.
When
(0,1)\cap D=\varnothing
, the path defined by
t\mapsto t
is usually denoted by
\mathrm{dch}
.

2.2.
Algebraic setup

Define
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
be the non-commutative free
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by formal symbols
e_{z}
, where
z
runs over
D
.
For a path
\gamma
with the initial point
x
and the final point
y
, the subalgebra
\mathfrak{H}_{D}^{\gamma}
of
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
is defined by

\mathfrak{H}_{D}^{\gamma}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in D\setminus\{\mathrm{pr}(x),\mathrm{pr}(y)\}}\mathbb{Q}e_{a}\oplus\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in D\setminus\{\mathrm{pr}(x)\}\\
b\in D\setminus\{\mathrm{pr}(y)\}\end{subarray}}e_{a}\mathfrak{H}_{D}e_{b}.

Definition 2.2

(Shuffle product)
.

Define a
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear product
\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}
on
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
by
1\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}w=w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}1=w
for all
w\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
and the recursive rule

e_{a}w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}e_{b}w^{\prime}=e_{a}(w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}e_{b}w^{\prime})+e_{b}(e_{a}w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}w^{\prime})\qquad(a,b\in D,~w,w^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}).

This product induces an associative and commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
and
\mathfrak{H}_{D}^{\gamma}
for any path
\gamma
.

Proposition 2.3
.

With the following structures,
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
is a Hopf
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

(1)

The product
\mathfrak{H}_{D}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{D}\to\mathfrak{H}_{D}
is the shuffle product.

(2)

The unit
\mathbb{Q}\to\mathfrak{H}_{D}
is determined by
1\mapsto 1
.

(3)

The coproduct
\mathfrak{H}_{D}\to\mathfrak{H}_{D}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{D}
is determined by
\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}})\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{k}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}}\otimes e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}
(
a_{j}\in D
).

(4)

The counit
\mathfrak{H}_{D}\to\mathbb{Q}
is determined by
e_{a}\mapsto 0
(
a\in D
).

(5)

The antipode
\mathfrak{H}_{D}\to\mathfrak{H}_{D}
is determined by
S^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}(e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}})\coloneqq(-1)^{k}e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}}
.

2.3.
Iterated integrals and regularization

Definition 2.4

(Convergent iterated integrals)
.

Let
\gamma
be a path with the initial point
x
and the final point
y
.
For
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
, we put

I_{\gamma}(x;w;y)\coloneqq\int_{0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}<1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\omega_{a_{i}}(\gamma(t_{i})),

where
\omega_{a}(t)
is the
1
-form
dt/(t-a)
.
Extending it
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly, we define the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
I_{\gamma}(x;-;y)\colon\mathfrak{H}_{D}^{\gamma}\to\mathbb{C}
.
We call this map (or its image) the (convergent)
iterated integral
.

Definition 2.5

(Regularized iterated integrals)
.

Take a sufficiently small number
0<z<1
.
Then, for any
w\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
, there uniquely exists
P(x)\in\mathbb{C}[x]
such that

I_{\gamma_{z,1-z}}(\gamma(z);w;\gamma(1-z))=P(\log z)+O(z\log^{J}z)\qquad(z\to+0)

for some
J>0
(cf.
[
1
, Lemma 3.351]
).
From these data, we define the
regularized iterated integral
by
I_{\gamma}(x;w;y)\coloneqq P(0)
.

Remark 2.6
.

On the above setting, if
w
is an element of
\mathfrak{H}_{D}^{\gamma}
the regularized iterated integral
I_{\gamma}(x;w;y)
coincides with the iterated integral in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

The following three formulas are basic properties of iterated integrals.

Theorem 2.7

(
=
Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be paths.
Denote by
x_{\circ}
(resp.
y_{\circ}
) the initial (resp. final) point of
\circ\in\{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\}
.
Assume that
y_{\gamma}
is equal to
x_{\gamma^{\prime}}
.
Then, for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in D
and
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
, we have

(1)

(Path composition formula)
\displaystyle I_{\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(x_{\gamma};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};y_{\gamma^{\prime}})=\sum_{i=0}^{k}I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}};y_{\gamma})I_{\gamma^{\prime}}(x_{\gamma^{\prime}};e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};y_{\gamma^{\prime}})
.

(2)

(Reversal formula)
I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};y_{\gamma})=(-1)^{k}I_{\gamma^{-1}}(y_{\gamma};e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}};x_{\gamma})
.

(3)

(Shuffle product formula)
I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}w^{\prime};y_{\gamma})=I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};w;y_{\gamma})I_{\gamma}(x_{\gamma};w^{\prime};y_{\gamma})
.

3.
Discrete iterated integrals

In what follows, we fix a large positive even integer
N
and put
\mathbb{Z}_{/N}\coloneqq\{n/N\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
.
The symbol
D
stands for a finite subset of
\mathbb{C}
.

3.1.
Discrete paths

In this subsection, we discretize the theory of continuous paths connecting (tangential or not) base points.

Definition 3.1

(Discrete paths)
.

(1)

The set of
endpoints
is
B\coloneqq(\mathbb{Z}_{/N}\setminus D)\sqcup((\mathbb{Z}_{/N}\cap D)\times\{1,\ldots,N\})
.
If
a\in D
, we also write
(a,+)\coloneqq(a,N/2)
and
(a,-)\coloneqq(a,0)\coloneqq(a,N)
.

(2)

Define a map
B\to\mathbb{Z}_{/N}
;
x\mapsto\hat{x}
as
\hat{x}\coloneqq x
if
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{/N}\setminus D
and
\hat{x}\coloneqq a
if
x=(a,i)\in(\mathbb{Z}_{/N}\cap D)\times\{1,\ldots,N\}
.

(3)

We define the following three types of
pieces
:

(a)

A
positive straight piece
is an element
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{Z}_{/N})^{2}
such that
x<y
and the open interval
(x,y)
does not intersect with
D
.
Its
initial point
is
(x,+)
if
x\in D
and is
x
if
x\notin D
.
Its
final point
is
(y,-)
if
y\in D
and is
y
if
y\notin D
.

(b)

A
negative straight piece
is an element
(x,y)\in(\mathbb{Z}_{/N})^{2}
such that
y<x
and the open interval
(-x,-y)
does not intersect with
D
.
Its initial point is
(x,-)
if
x\in D
and is
x
if
x\notin D
.
Its final point is
(y,+)
if
y\in D
and is
y
if
y\notin D
.

(c)

A
positive singular piece
is an element
(x,a,b,+)
of
(D\cap\mathbb{Z}_{/N})\times\{0,\ldots,N\}^{2}\times\{+,-\}
such that
a<b
.
Its initial point is
(x,a)
and final point is
(x,b)
.

(d)

A
negative singular piece
is an element
(x,a,b,-)
of
(D\cap\mathbb{Z}_{/N})\times\{0,\ldots,N\}^{2}\times\{+,-\}
such that
a<b
.
Its initial point is
(x,N-b)
and final point is
(x,N-a)
.

(e)

A
trivial piece
is an element
x
of copy
B^{\prime}
of
B
.
Its initial and final points are
x
.
If
p\in D
, we use the notation
(p,a,a,+)\coloneqq(p,a,a,-)\coloneqq(p,a)\in B^{\prime}
for every
a\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and regard it as one of singular pieces.

(4)

Define maps
s
(resp.
t
) from the set of pieces to
B
to be what assigns a piece to its initial (resp. final) point.

(5)

We say that a finite tuple
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
(
r\geq 1
) is a
discrete path
(on
\mathbb{Z}_{/N}\setminus D
) if each
\gamma_{i}
is one of pieces above such that
t(\gamma_{i})=s(\gamma_{i+1})
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
.
Define
s(\gamma)\coloneqq s(\gamma_{1})
and
t(\gamma)\coloneqq t(\gamma_{r})
.

Note that the above definition of discrete paths depends on
D
.
However, since we fix
D
, we omit the information of
D
and simply say as “a discrete path
\gamma
”.

Definition 3.2

(Operations on discrete paths)
.

(1)

For a discrete path
\gamma_{i}=(\gamma_{i,1},\ldots,\gamma_{i,r_{i}})
(
i\in\{1,2\}
) satisfying
t(\gamma_{1})=s(\gamma_{2})
, the
composition

\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}
is the concatenation
(\gamma_{1,1},\ldots,\gamma_{1,r_{1}},\gamma_{2,1},\ldots,\gamma_{2,r_{2}})
of them as tuples of pieces.

(2)

For a discrete path
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
, the
inverse
is a discrete path
\gamma^{-1}\coloneqq(\gamma_{r}^{-1},\ldots,\gamma_{1}^{-1})
, where the inverse of pieces are defined as:

(a)

If
\gamma_{i}=(x,y)
is a (positive or negative) straight piece, put
\gamma_{i}^{-1}\coloneqq(y,x)
.

(b)

If
\gamma_{i}=(x,a,b,\sigma)
is a (positive or negative) singular piece, put
\gamma_{i}^{-1}\coloneqq(x,N-b,N-a,-\sigma)
.

(c)

If
\gamma_{i}
is a trivial piece, its inverse is itself.

Remark 3.3
.

From the definition, it is clear that a discrete path
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
is composition of its pieces
\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{r}
by identifying the piece
\gamma_{i}
with the discrete path
(\gamma_{i})
.
Hereafter we sometimes use this identification.

Definition 3.4

(Provision of discrete paths)
.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
be a discrete path.
We define the
provision
of
\gamma
as the set
S_{\gamma}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{r}(S_{\gamma_{i}}\times\{i\})
, where
S_{\gamma_{i}}
is determined as follows:

(1)

If
\gamma_{i}=(a,b)
is a positive straight piece,
S_{\gamma_{i}}\coloneqq\{aN+1,\ldots,bN-1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
\gamma_{i}=(a,b)
is a negative straight piece,
S_{\gamma_{i}}\coloneqq\{-aN+1,\ldots,-bN,-1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

If
\gamma_{i}=(x,a,b,\sigma)
is a (positive or negative) singular piece,
S_{\gamma_{i}}
is a set
\{\theta_{a+1}^{\sigma},\ldots,\theta_{b}^{\sigma}\}
, which consists of formal symbols
\theta_{j}^{\pm}
(the sign represents whether the piece is positive or negative, which does not mean the inverse).

(4)

If
\gamma_{i}
is trivial, set
S_{\gamma_{i}}\coloneqq\varnothing
.

For an element
n\in S_{\gamma}
, denote by
\tilde{n}
the first component of
n
and define
n+a\coloneqq(\tilde{n}+a,i)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\{1,\ldots,r\}
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in S_{\gamma_{i}}\subseteq S_{\gamma}
if
\gamma_{i}
is straight.
The projection on the second component
S_{\gamma}\to\{1,\ldots,r\}
is denoted by
J
.

Moreover, we introduce the order
<_{\gamma}
for the provision of a path
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
in the following way.

(1)

(m,i)<_{\gamma}(n,i+1)
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
,
(m,i)\in S_{\gamma_{i}}
and
(n,i+1)\in S_{\gamma_{i+1}}
.

(2)

(m,i)<_{\gamma}(n,i)
if
\gamma_{i}
is a positive or negative piece and
m<n
.

(3)

(\theta_{j}^{\pm},i)<_{\gamma}(\theta_{j+1}^{\pm},i)
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}
if
\gamma_{i}
is a (positive or negative) singular piece.

The condition that
A<_{\gamma}B
or
A=B
holds is denoted by
A\leq_{\gamma}B
as usual.

Definition 3.5

(Elimination of discrete paths)
.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
be a discrete path and
m,n\in S_{\gamma}
satisfying
m\leq_{\gamma}n
.
Write
m=(\tilde{m},i)
and
n=(\tilde{n},j)
.
When
i\neq j
, we define a new discrete path
\gamma_{m,n}
by
(\gamma^{\prime}_{i},\gamma_{i+1},\ldots,\gamma_{j-1},\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j})
where

\gamma^{\prime}_{i}\coloneqq\begin{cases}((\tilde{m}-1)/N,\widehat{t(\gamma_{i})})&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a positive straight path},\\
(-(\tilde{m}-1)/N,\widehat{t(\gamma_{i})})&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a negative straight path},\\
(x,h,b,\pm)&\text{if }\gamma_{i}=(x,a,b,\pm)\text{ is a (positive or negative) singular path and }\tilde{m}=\theta_{h}^{\pm},\end{cases}

and

\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\widehat{s(\gamma_{j})},(\tilde{n}+1)/N)&\text{if }\gamma_{j}\text{ is a positive straight path},\\
(\widehat{s(\gamma_{j})},-(\tilde{n}+1)/N)&\text{if }\gamma_{j}\text{ is a negative straight path},\\
(x,a,h,\pm)&\text{if }\gamma_{j}=(x,a,b,\pm)\text{ is a (positive or negative) singular path and }\tilde{n}=\theta_{h}^{\pm}.\end{cases}

If
i=j
, we define

\gamma_{m,n}\coloneqq\begin{cases}((\tilde{m}-1)/N,(\tilde{n}+1)/N)&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a positive straight path},\\
(-(\tilde{m}-1)/N,-(\tilde{n}+1)/N)&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a negative straight path},\\
(x,j,j^{\prime},\pm)&\text{if }\gamma_{i}=(x,a,b,\pm)\text{ is a (positive or negative) singular path, }\tilde{m}=\theta_{j}^{\pm}\text{ and }\tilde{n}=\theta_{j^{\prime}}^{\pm}.\end{cases}

If
m=n
, the path
\gamma_{m,n}
is defined to be a trivial piece
\tilde{m}
if
\gamma_{i}
(
=\gamma_{j}
) is straight and to be a trivial piece
(x,j)
if
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{j}=(x,a,b,\pm)
is singular, where
j
is the subscript of
\tilde{m}=\theta_{j}^{\pm}
.

Note that, for a discrete path
\gamma
and
a,b,m,n\in S_{\gamma}
satisfying
a\leq_{\gamma}m\leq_{\gamma}n\leq_{\gamma}b
, there is an inclusion
S_{\gamma_{m,n}}\subseteq S_{\gamma_{a,b}}\subseteq S_{\gamma}
and the iterated elimination
(\gamma_{a,b})_{m,n}
is equal to
\gamma_{m,n}
.
Moreover we see that
S_{\gamma_{a,b}}=\{n\in S_{\gamma}\mid a<_{\gamma}n<_{\gamma}b\}
holds.

3.2.
Definition of discrete iterated integrals

Definition 3.6

(Discrete differential forms)
.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
be a discrete path,
n=(\tilde{n},i)\in S_{\gamma}
and
a\in D
.
Then we define
\omega_{a}(n)\in\mathbb{C}[\theta]
by

\omega_{a}^{N}(n)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{n}-aN}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a positive straight piece},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{n}+aN}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a negative straight piece},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{N}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a positive singular piece whose first entry is }a,\\
-\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{N}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ is a negative singular piece whose first entry is }a,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Furthermore, for elements
m=(\tilde{m},i)
and
n=(\tilde{n},j)
of
S_{\gamma}
, we define

\omega^{N}(m,n)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{n}-\tilde{m}}&\text{if }\gamma_{i}\text{ and }\gamma_{j}\text{ are both positive or negative straight pieces and }\tilde{n}\neq\tilde{m},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Definition 3.7

(Discrete iterated integrals)
.

Let
\gamma
be a discrete path and
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
.
Then we define the
discrete iterated integral
by

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in S_{\gamma}\\
n_{1}\leq_{\gamma}\cdots\leq_{\gamma}n_{k}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\omega_{a_{i}}^{N}(n_{i}).

We put
\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);1;t(\gamma))\coloneqq 1
.
Moreover, by the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear extension, we define
\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);-;t(\gamma))\colon\mathfrak{H}_{D}^{\gamma}\to\mathbb{C}[\theta]
.

Example 3.8
.

Let
D=\{0,1\}
,
w=e_{1}e_{0}^{2}
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{1})
be a discrete path which consists of only the positive straight piece
\gamma_{1}=(0,1)
.
Then
S_{\gamma}=\{(1,1),(2,1)\ldots,(N-1,1)\}
and
(i,1)\leq_{\gamma}(j,1)
if and only if
i\leq j
.
Thus we have

\Delta_{N,\gamma}((0,+);e_{1}e_{0}^{2};(1,-))=\sum_{0<n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq n_{3}<N}\frac{1}{(n_{1}-N)n_{2}n_{3}}.

Taking the limit
N\to\infty
and using
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
, we obtain
\lim_{N\to\infty}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((0,+);e_{1}e_{0}^{2};(1,-))=I_{\mathrm{dch}}(1_{0};e_{1}e_{0}^{2};(-1)_{1})
.
More general examples explaining such a limit which tends to the usual iterated integral appears in §
4
.

3.3.
Basic formulas for discrete iterated integrals

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\gamma=(x,a,b,\pm)
is a singular piece and identify it with a discrete path
(\gamma)
.
Then we have

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma))=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(b-a)_{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\pm\theta}{N}\right)^{k}&\text{if }a_{1}=\cdots=a_{k}=x,\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in D
.
Here
(X)_{k}\coloneqq X(X+1)\cdots(X+k-1)
stands for the Pochhammer symbol.

Proof.

We proceed the proof by induction on
k
.
The case where
k=0
is obvious.
If the case for some
k
holds, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k+1}};t(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{a<n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{k+1}\leq b}\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}\omega_{a_{i}}^{N}(\theta_{n_{i}}^{\pm})

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\leq n\leq b}\Delta_{N,(x,a,n,\pm)}((x,a);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};(x,n))\omega_{a_{k+1}}(\theta_{n}^{\pm}).

If
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{k+1}\}\neq\{x\}
we obtain the desired result.
Otherwise from this expression we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k+1}};t(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{a<n\leq b}\frac{(n-a)_{k}}{k!}\left(\frac{\pm\theta}{N}\right)^{k}\cdot\frac{\pm\theta}{N}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\pm\theta}{N}\right)^{k+1}\sum_{a<n\leq b}\frac{(n-a)_{k}}{k!}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\pm\theta}{N}\right)^{k+1}\sum_{a<n\leq b}\left(\frac{(n-a)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}-\frac{(n-a-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{(b-a)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\left(\frac{\pm\theta}{N}\right)^{k+1}.

∎

The following two lemmas are obvious from the definition.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
be a discrete path and
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
.
Then we have

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma))=\sum_{0=i_{0}\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r-1}\leq i_{r}=k}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,n_{i_{j}}\in S_{\gamma_{j}}\\
n_{i_{j-1}+1}\leq_{\gamma_{j}}\cdots\leq_{\gamma_{j}}n_{i_{j}}\end{subarray}}\prod_{h=i_{j-1}+1}^{i_{j}}\omega_{a_{h}}^{N}(n_{h}).

Corollary 3.11

(Path composition formula)
.

Let
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be composable discrete paths (i.e.,
t(\gamma)=s(\gamma^{\prime})
).
Then, for
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}\in\mathfrak{H}_{D}
, we have

\Delta_{N,\gamma\gamma^{\prime}}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma^{\prime}))=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}};t(\gamma))\Delta_{N,\gamma^{\prime}}(s(\gamma^{\prime});e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma^{\prime})).

Theorem 3.12

(Reversal formula)
.

Let
\gamma
be a discrete path.
Then, for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in D
, we have

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma))=(-1)^{k}\Delta_{N,\gamma^{-1}}(t(\gamma);e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}};s(\gamma)).

Proof.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r})
.
Applying Corollary
3.11
repeatedly, we have

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};t(\gamma))=\sum_{0=i_{0}\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r-1}\leq i_{r}=k}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Delta_{N,\gamma_{j}}(s(\gamma_{j});e_{a_{i_{j-1}+1}}\cdots e_{a_{i_{j}}};t(\gamma_{j}))

and

\Delta_{N,\gamma^{-1}}(t(\gamma);e_{a_{k}}\cdots e_{a_{1}};s(\gamma))=\sum_{0=i^{\prime}_{0}\leq i^{\prime}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i^{\prime}_{r-1}\leq i^{\prime}_{r}=k}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Delta_{N,\gamma_{r+1-j}^{-1}}(s(\gamma_{r+1-j});e_{a_{i^{\prime}_{r+1-j}}}\cdots e_{a_{i^{\prime}_{r-j}+1}};s(\gamma_{r+1-j})).

Since we can give a one-to-one correspondence
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r-1})\mapsto(i^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,i^{\prime}_{r})\coloneqq(k-i_{r-1},\ldots,k-i_{1})
between the ranges of each sum above, it is sufficient to prove

\Delta_{N,\gamma_{j}}(s(\gamma_{j});e_{a_{i_{j-1}+1}}\cdots e_{a_{i_{j}}};t(\gamma_{j}))=(-1)^{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}\Delta_{N,\gamma_{j}^{-1}}(t(\gamma_{j});e_{a_{i_{j}}}\cdots e_{a_{i_{j-1}+1}};s(\gamma_{j}))

for
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r-1})
satisfying
0=i_{0}\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r-1}\leq i_{r}=k
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.
If
\gamma_{j}
is a (positive or negative) straight path, we obtain this identity by substitution
n\mapsto-n
.
When
\gamma_{j}=(x,a,b,\pm)
is a singular path, from Proposition
3.9
we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{N,\gamma_{j}}(s(\gamma_{j});e_{a_{i_{j-1}+1}}\cdots e_{a_{i_{j}}};t(\gamma_{j}))

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(b-a)_{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}}{(i_{j}-i_{j-1})!}\left(\frac{\pm\theta}{N}\right)^{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}&\text{if }a_{i_{j-1}+1}=\cdots=a_{i_{j}}=x,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{((N-a)-(N-b))_{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}}{(i_{j}-i_{j-1})!}\left(\frac{\mp\theta}{N}\right)^{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}&\text{if }a_{i_{j-1}+1}=\cdots=a_{i_{j}}=x,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i_{j}-i_{j-1}}\Delta_{N,\gamma_{j}^{-1}}(t(\gamma_{j});e_{a_{i_{j}}}\cdots e_{a_{i_{j-1}+1}};s(\gamma_{j})).

∎

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\gamma=(x,y)
be a positive straight piece (regarded as a discrete path) and
a\in D
.
Then we have

\sum_{xN\leq n\leq yN}\frac{1}{|n-aN|}=O(\log N),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Proof.

For the case where
a\notin\{x,y\}
, put

\rho\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Re}(a)-y&\text{if }\mathrm{Re}(a)>y,\\
x-\mathrm{Re}(a)&\text{if }x>\mathrm{Re}(a),\\
\mathrm{Im}(a)&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then this quantity does not depend on
N
and
|n-aN|\geq\rho N>0
always holds for an integer
xN<n<yN
.
Thus we have

\sum_{xN<n<yN}\frac{1}{|n-aN|}\leq\frac{(y-x)N-1}{\rho N}.

If
a=x
, we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{xN<n<yN}\frac{1}{|n-xN|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<n<(y-x)N}\frac{1}{n}

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\log N+O(1),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

The estimation for the
a=y
case is also obtained by a similar computation.
∎

For treat more general convergences, we define an auxiliary algebra
\mathcal{P}_{D}\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}\langle e_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}\mid a_{i}\in D,~m\geq 1\rangle
and auxiliary sums

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in S_{\gamma}\\
n_{1}\leq_{\gamma}\cdots\leq_{\gamma}n_{k}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\omega_{a_{i,j}}(n_{i}),

for a straight piece
\gamma
and
w=e_{a_{1,1},\ldots,a_{1,m_{1}}}\cdots e_{a_{k,1},\ldots,a_{k,m_{k}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{D}
.
Under the same setting, we also define its variant as

|\Delta|_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in S_{\gamma}\\
n_{1}\leq_{\gamma}\cdots\leq_{\gamma}n_{k}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}|\omega_{a_{i,j}}(n_{i})|.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\gamma
be a straight piece and
w=e_{a_{1,1},\ldots,a_{1,m_{1}}}\cdots e_{a_{k,1}}\cdots e_{a_{k,m_{k}}}\in\mathcal{P}_{D}

Put

and

Then we have the following:

(1)

and

and

(4)

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))=O(N^{-1}\log^{\bullet}N)
as

if there exists

,

.

(5)

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))=O(N^{-1}\log^{\bullet}N)
as

if there exists
1\leq i^{\prime}<i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq m_{i}
and
1\leq j^{\prime}<j^{\prime\prime}\leq m_{i^{\prime}}

m_{i^{\prime}}\geq 2
,

and
a_{i,j}\neq y
.

Proof.

Arguments similar to the below proofs for the positive straight case also works for the negative straight case.
Thus we deal with only the positive straight case.
For
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq m_{i}
define

\mathbf{a}_{i}\coloneqq(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,m_{i}})

and

\mathbf{a}_{i}\langle j\rangle\coloneqq(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,j-1},a_{i,j+1},\ldots,a_{i,m_{i}})

(1)

For the case where
\gamma
is positive straight, it is sufficient that the latter case is proven by the triangle inequality.
From Lemma
3.13
we have

|\Delta|_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xN<n_{i,j}<yN\\
1\leq i\leq k\\
1\leq j\leq m_{i}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|n_{i,j}-a_{i,j}N|}=O(\log^{mk}N),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

(2)

By the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{n_{i}-a_{i,j}N}\frac{1}{n_{i}-a_{i,j^{\prime}}N}=\frac{1}{N(a_{i,j}-a_{i,j^{\prime}})}\left(\frac{1}{n_{i}-a_{i,j}N}-\frac{1}{n_{i}-a_{i,j^{\prime}}N}\right),

we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{xN<n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{k}<yN}\prod_{h=1}^{k}\prod_{h^{\prime}=1}^{m_{i}}\frac{1}{n_{h}-a_{h,h^{\prime}}N}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\frac{1}{N(a_{i,j}-a_{i,j^{\prime}})}\left(\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}\cdots e_{\mathbf{a}_{i-1}}e_{\mathbf{a}_{i}\langle j\rangle}e_{\mathbf{a}_{i+1}}\cdots e_{\mathbf{a}_{k}};t(\gamma))\right.\\
\left.-\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}\cdots e_{\mathbf{a}_{i-1}}e_{\mathbf{a}_{i}\langle j^{\prime}\rangle}e_{\mathbf{a}_{i+1}}\cdots e_{\mathbf{a}_{k}};t(\gamma))\right).\end{multlined}

Then the desired result follows from this equality and (1).
If
\gamma
is singular,

(3)

Put

\rho\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Re}(a_{i,j})-y&\text{if }\mathrm{Re}(a_{i,j})>y,\\
x-\mathrm{Re}(a_{i,j})&\text{if }x>\mathrm{Re}(a_{i,j}),\\
\mathrm{Im}(a_{i,j})&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

\rho
is independent of

and the inequality

.

(3.16)

\displaystyle|\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))|

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\rho N}|\Delta|_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);e_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}\cdots e_{\mathbf{a}_{i-1}}e_{\mathbf{a}_{i}\langle j\rangle}e_{\mathbf{a}_{i+1}}\cdots e_{\mathbf{a}_{k}};t(\gamma))

(3.18)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}O(N^{-1}\log^{\bullet}N)\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

(4)

We have

(3.19)

\displaystyle|\Delta|_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\sum_{xN<n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{k}<yN}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq h\leq k\\

Theorem 3.17

(Asymptotic shuffle product formula)
.

Let
\gamma
be a discrete path and
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}
and
w^{\prime}=e_{b_{1}}\cdots e_{b_{l}}
elements of
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
.
Assume that
(w,w^{\prime})
is
\gamma
-shuffleable.
Then we have

(3.25)

\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w;t(\gamma))\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w^{\prime};t(\gamma))=\Delta_{N,\gamma}(s(\gamma);w\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}w^{\prime};t(\gamma))+O(N^{-1}\log^{\bullet}N),\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in a way similar to
[
16
, Proposition 2.4]
.
First we consider the case where
\gamma
is a piece.
By definition we have

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{n}\leq c<yN}\left(\frac{1}{c-\tilde{m}}-\frac{1}{c-xN}\right)

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\tilde{n}\leq c<yN}\sum_{xN<d\leq\tilde{m}}\left(\frac{1}{c-d}-\frac{1}{c-(d-1)}\right)

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{xN<d\leq\tilde{m}}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{n}-d}-\frac{1}{yN-d}\right)

(5.7)

\displaystyle=B(m,n).

The
m=n
case is also proved by a similar argument.

(2)

In the definition

\Delta_{N,\gamma}^{O^{\prime}}((x,+);we_{z};(y,-))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},n_{k+1}\in S_{\gamma}\\
n_{0}\leq_{\gamma}n_{k+1}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma_{n_{0},n_{k+1}}}(s(\gamma_{n_{0},n_{k+1}});w;t(\gamma_{n_{0},n_{k+1}}))\omega_{z}^{N}(n_{k+1})\left(\omega_{y}^{N}(n_{0})-\omega^{N}(n_{k+1},n_{0})\right),

we can apply the decomposition

(5.8)

\displaystyle\omega_{z}^{N}(n_{k+1})\left(\omega_{y}^{N}(n_{0})-\omega^{N}(n_{k+1},n_{0})\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{k+1}}-zN}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{0}}-yN}-\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{0}}-\tilde{n_{k+1}}}\right)

(5.10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{0}}-zN}\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{k+1}}-\tilde{n_{0}}}-\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{k+1}}-xN}\right)+\frac{1-\delta_{x,z}}{\tilde{n_{0}}-zN}\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{k+1}}-xN}+\frac{1-\delta_{y,z}}{\tilde{n_{0}}-yN}\frac{1}{\tilde{n_{k+1}}-zN}-\frac{1-\delta_{x,z}}{\tilde{n_{0}}-zN}\frac{1-\delta_{y,z}}{\tilde{n_{k+1}}-zN}

\displaystyle=\begin{multlined}\omega_{z}^{N}(n_{0})(\omega^{N}(n_{0},n_{k+1})-\omega^{N}_{x}(n_{k+1}))+(1-\delta_{x,z})\omega^{N}_{z}(n_{0})\omega^{N}_{x}(n_{k+1})\\
+(1-\delta_{y,z})\omega^{N}_{y}(n_{0})\omega^{N}_{z}(n_{k+1})-(1-\delta_{x,z})(1-\delta_{y,z})\omega^{N}_{z}(n_{0})\omega^{N}_{z}(n_{k+1}),\end{multlined}

when
n_{0}\neq n_{k+1}
.
If
n_{0}=n_{k+1}
, such a composition becomes the form

(5.13)

\displaystyle\omega_{z}^{N}(n_{k+1})\left(\omega_{y}^{N}(n_{0})-\omega^{N}(n_{k+1},n_{0})\right)

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\omega_{z}^{N}(n_{0})(\omega^{N}(n_{0},n_{k+1})-\omega^{N}_{x}(n_{k+1}))+(1-\delta_{x,z})\omega^{N}_{z}(n_{0})\omega^{N}_{x}(n_{k+1})

\displaystyle+(1-\delta_{y,z})\omega^{N}_{y}(n_{0})\omega^{N}_{z}(n_{k+1}).

Therefore we obtain the result by summing up them.

∎

Theorem 5.3

(Cyclic sum formula)
.

Let
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}
(
k\geq 2
) be an element of
\mathfrak{H}_{D}
.
Then we have

(5.16)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\
a_{i}\neq x\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{i}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i-1}}e_{x};(y,-))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\
a_{i}\neq y\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{y}e_{a_{i+1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i-1}}e_{x})\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq k\\
a_{i}\in\{x,y\}\end{subarray}}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{i}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{i}};(y,-))-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i},a_{i},\ldots,a_{k}};(y,-)).

Proof.

Using two assertinons in Proposition
5.2
and putting
w=e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k-1}}
and
z=a_{k}
, we have

(5.17)

\Delta_{N,\gamma}^{O}((x,+);e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};(y,-))=\Delta_{N,\gamma}^{O}((x,+);e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k-1}};(y,-))+(1-\delta_{x,a_{k}})\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k-1}}e_{x};(y,-))\\
+(1-\delta_{y,a_{k}})\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{y}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};(y,-))-(1-\delta_{x,a_{k}})(1-\delta_{y,a_{k}})\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{k}}e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}};(y,-))\\
+\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i},a_{i},\ldots,a_{k}};(y,-)),

Applying this equation
k
times derives that the
\Delta_{N,\gamma}^{O}
term vanishes and thus obtain the desired formula.
∎

Corollary 5.4

(Cyclic sum formula for multiple polylogarithms)
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
(
r\geq 1
) be positive integers and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
complex numbers
|z_{i}|\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Suppose that
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\}\neq 1
if
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{r}=1
.
Then we have

(5.18)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\\
k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{r},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}+1\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{i}-2}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}1,z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\\
j+1,k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{r},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i-1},k_{i}-j\end{pmatrix}\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\\
z_{i}\neq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}1,z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\\
k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{r},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i},1\end{pmatrix}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\\
z_{i}\neq 1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Li}^{\mathbin{\mathcyr{sh}}}\begin{pmatrix}z_{i},\ldots,z_{r},z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\\
k_{i+1},\ldots,k_{r},k_{1},\ldots,k_{i},1\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Put
e_{a_{1}}\cdots e_{a_{k}}=e_{1/z_{1}}e_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\cdots e_{1/z_{r}}e_{0}^{k_{r}-1}
in Theorem
5.3
.
The assumption guarantees that

and then we see that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\Delta_{N,\gamma}((x,+);e_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i},a_{i},\ldots,a_{k}};(y,-))=0

Thus we have the theorem by using (

∎

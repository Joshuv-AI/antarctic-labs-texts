Global Dynamical Behaviours and Periodicity of a Certain Quadratic-Rational Difference Equation with Delay
Erkan Taşdemir, Melih Göcen, Yüksel Soykan

Abstract




Abstract

Our aim in this paper is to deal with the dynamics of following higher order
difference equation

x_{n+1}=A+B\frac{x_{n-m}}{x_{n}^{2}}

where
A,B>0
, and initial values are positive, and
m=\{1,2,...\}
.

Furthermore, we discuss the periodicity, boundedness, semi-cycles, global
asymptotic stability of solutions of these equations. We also handle the
rate of convergence of solutions of these difference equations.

Keywords:
Difference equations, periodicity, boundedness,
semicycle, global asymptotic stability, rate of convergence.

AMS Subject Classification:
39A10, 39A23, 39A30.

Introduction

Last few decades, rational difference equations and their systems have
attracted the interest of many researchers for varied reasons. One of the
reason of this rapid growth of interest is, these equations provided a
natural description of many discrete mathematical models. Such discrete
mathematical models are often scrutinized in various fields of science and
technology for instance, biology, ecology, physiology, physics, engineering,
economics, probability theory, genetics, psychology, resource management and
population dynamics. We believe that the interest of studying rational
difference equations will increase in future years as more fascinating and
intresting results are obtaining. Although difference equations are very
simple in form, it is extremely difficult to understand thoroughly the
behaviors of their solutions. Furthermore, higher-order rational difference
equations and systems of rational equations have also been widely studied
but still have many aspects to be investigated. There are many papers

Let
I
be some interval of real numbers and let
f:I^{k+1}\rightarrow I
be
a continuously differentiable function. A difference equation of order
(k+1)
is an equation of the form

x_{n+1}=f(x_{n},x_{n-1},\cdots,x_{n-k}),\text{ \ \ \ }n=0,1,\cdots.

(6)

A solution of Eq.(
6
) is a sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n=-k}^{\infty}
that
satisfies Eq.(
6
) for all
n\geq-k
.

Suppose that the function
f
is continuously differentiable in some open
neighborhood of an equilibrium point
\overline{x}.
Let

q_{i}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial u_{i}}(\overline{x},\overline{x},\cdots,\overline{x}),\text{ \ for }i=0,1,\cdots,k

denote the partial derivative of
f(u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{k})
with respect
to
u_{i}
evaluated at the equilibrium point
\overline{x}
of Eq.(
6
)

Definition 1

The equation

z_{n+1}=q_{0}z_{n}+q_{1}z_{n-1}+\cdots+q_{k}z_{n-k},\text{ }n=0,1,\cdots,

(7)

is called the linearized equation of Eq.(
6
) about the equilibrium
point
\overline{x}
.

(Clark’s Theorem)

Consider Eq.(

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left|q_{i}\right|<1.

Consider the scalar
k

x_{n+k}+p_{1}(n)x_{n+k-1}+\cdots+p_{k}(n)x_{n}=0,

p_{i}:\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

q_{i}=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}p_{i}(n),i=1,\cdots,k,

(9)

exist in
\mathbb{C}

x_{n+k}+q_{1}x_{n+k-1}+\cdots+q_{k}x_{n}=0.

(10)

Consider (
8
) subject to condition (
9

Let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{k}

) and suppose that
\left|\lambda_{i}\right|\neq\left|\lambda_{j}\right|

either

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_{n}}=\lambda_{j}.

2
Boundedness of Solutions of Eq.(
12
)

Now, we handle the bounded solutions of Eq.(
12
). We also find out
that Eq.(
12
) has bounded solutions.

Theorem 6

Let
0<p<1
. Then, there exists the bounded solutions
of Eq.(
12
) such as

1<y_{n}\leq\frac{1}{1-p}+\sqrt[m+1]{p^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}c_{j}\left(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m+1}\left(j-1\right)}\right)^{n},

where
c_{j},

j=1,2,\cdots,m+1
, are arbitrary constants, and
n\in\left\{0,1,2,\cdots\right\}
, and
\sqrt[m+1]{p}
is one of the
(m+1)
th
roots of
p
.

Proof.
Let
p>0
, and
\left\{y_{n}\right\}_{n=-m}^{\infty}
be a positive
solution of Eq.(
12
). Then, we obtain from Eq.(
12
)

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{-m}}{y_{0}^{2}}>1,

\displaystyle y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{1-m}}{y_{1}^{2}}>1.

Hence, by induction, we get
y_{n}>1
for
n\geq 1
.

Now, we take care of the other side. We have from Eq.(
12
)

y_{n+1}=1+p\frac{y_{n-m}}{y_{n}^{2}}\leq 1+py_{n-m}.

According to Theorem
5
, we consider a sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, and
y_{n}\leq u_{n},

n=0,1,\cdots,
and

u_{n+1}=1+pu_{n-m},n\geq 1,

(13)

such that

u_{s+i}=y_{s+i},s\in\left\{-m,-m+1,\cdots\right\},i=\left\{0,1,2,\cdots\right\},n\geq s.

(14)

The characteristic polynomial to Eq.(
13
) is

P_{m+1}\left(\lambda\right)=\lambda^{m+1}-p.

Thus, we have the roots of characteristic polynomial as follows:

\lambda_{j}=\sqrt[m+1]{p}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m+1}\left(j-1\right)},

where
j=1,2,\cdots,m+1.
The homogeneous solution of Eq.(
13
) is

u_{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}c_{j}\sqrt[m+1]{p^{n}}\left(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m+1}\left(j-1\right)}\right)^{n},

where
c_{j}
are arbitrary constants for
j=1,2,\cdots,m+1
. Now, we
handle the equilibrium solution of Eq.(
13
). From Eq.(
13
), we get that

\bar{u}=\frac{1}{1-p}.

Therefore, the solution of Eq.(
13
) is

u_{n}=\frac{1}{1-p}+\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}c_{j}\sqrt[m+1]{p^{n}}\left(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m+1}\left(j-1\right)}\right)^{n},

(15)

where
c_{j}
are arbitrary constants for
j=1,2,\cdots,m+1
. Furthermore,
we have from (
14
) and (
15
)

y_{n+1}-u_{n+1}\leq p\left(y_{n}-u_{n}\right),

where
n>s,
and
p\in\left(0,1\right)
. Therefore, we get
y_{n}\leq u_{n},

n>s
. So, the proof completed.

3
Oscillation Behaviors of Eq.(
12
)

In this section, we discuss the semi-cycles of Eq.(
12
). We also
reveal the oscillation behaviours of solutions of Eq.(
12
) in
detail.

Theorem 7

Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n=-m}^{\infty}
be a positive solution of Eq.(
12
). Then, the following statements are true:

(i) The every semi-cycle at most
m
terms.

(ii) Every solution of Eq.(
12
) oscillates about the positive
equilibrium
\bar{y}
.

Proof.
We firstly handle the positive semi-cycle of solution of Eq.(
12
).
The negative semi-cycle is similar and can be omitted. Assume that Eq.(
12
) has a positive semi-cycle with
m
terms. Suppose that
y_{N}
is
the first term in this positive semi-cycle. Therefore, we get

y_{N},y_{N+1},\cdots,y_{N+m-1}>\bar{y}.

Hence, we obtain from Eq.(
12
)

y_{N+m}=1+p\frac{y_{N-1}}{y_{N+m-1}^{2}}<1+p\frac{y_{N-1}}{\bar{y}^{2}}<\bar{y}.

So, we have that a semi-cycle consists at most
m
terms. We also get that
every solution of Eq.(
12
) oscillates about
\bar{y}
. The proof
completed as desired.

Theorem 8

Let
m
be an odd number and let

y_{-m},y_{-m+2},\cdots,y_{-1}\leq\bar{y}\text{ and }y_{-m+1},y_{-m+3},\cdots,y_{0}>\bar{y}.

(16)

Then, every semi-cycle of Eq.(
12
) has lenght one. Additionally,
the solution
\{y_{n}\}_{n=-m}^{\infty}
of Eq.(
12
) is
oscillatory about unique positive equilibrium point
\bar{y}
.

Proof.
Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n=-m}^{\infty}
be a positive solution of Eq.(
12
). Assume that (
16
) holds. Hence, we obtain from Eq.(
12
),

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{-m}}{y_{0}^{2}}<\bar{y},

\displaystyle y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{-m+1}}{y_{1}^{2}}>\bar{y},

\displaystyle y_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{-m+2}}{y_{2}^{2}}<\bar{y}.

Therefore, we have by induction

y_{2n+1}=1+p\frac{y_{2n-m}}{y_{2n}^{2}}<\bar{y},

and

y_{2n}=1+p\frac{y_{2n-(m+1)}}{y_{2n-1}^{2}}>\bar{y}.

4
Periodicity of Eq.(
12
)

Now, we study the existence of periodic solutions of Eq.(
12
).

Theorem 9

Assume that
m
is an even number. Then, Eq.(
12
) has no two
periodic solution.

Proof.
Let
m
is an even number. We also suppose that Eq.(
12
) has two
periodic solution such that

\cdots,\alpha,\beta,\alpha,\beta,\cdots

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive numbers and
\alpha\neq\beta
.
Hence, we get the followings

\displaystyle y_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{2n-m}}{y_{2n}^{2}}\Rightarrow\alpha=1+\frac{p}{\beta},

(17)

\displaystyle y_{2n+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+p\frac{y_{2n+1-m}}{y_{2n+1}^{2}}\Rightarrow\beta=1+\frac{p}{\alpha}.

where
n\geq 1
. Therefore, we obtain that

\left(\alpha-\beta\right)\left(1-\frac{p}{\alpha\beta}\right)=0.

Now, from our supposition, we have
\alpha\neq\beta
. Thus, we get

1-\frac{p}{\alpha\beta}=0\Rightarrow p=\alpha\beta.

From (
17
), we have

\alpha\beta=\beta+p\Rightarrow\beta=0\text{.}

So, we have a contradiction. The proof completed as desired.

5
Stability of Solutions of Eq.(
12
)

In this section, we deal with the asymptotic stability of the solutions of
Eq.(
5
). Firstly, we find the linearized equation associated with
Eq.(
12
) about its positive equilibrium point.

Let
I
be some interval of real numbers and let

f:I^{m+1}\rightarrow I,

be a continuously differentiable function such that
f
is defined by

f\left(y_{n},y_{n-1},\cdots,y_{n-m}\right)=1+p\frac{y_{n-m}}{y_{n}^{2}}\text{.}

Thus, we obtain that

q_{0}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{n}}=-\frac{2p}{\bar{y}^{2}},

q_{1}=q_{2}=\cdots=q_{m-1}=0,

q_{m}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial y_{n-m}}=\frac{p}{\bar{y}^{2}}.

Then the linearized equation of Eq.(
12
) about its unique positive
equilibrium point
\bar{y}
is:

z_{n+1}+\frac{2p}{\bar{y}^{2}}z_{n}-\frac{p}{\bar{y}^{2}}z_{n-m}=0.

(18)

Hence, the characteristic equation of Eq.(
12
) is as follows,

\lambda^{m+1}+\frac{2p}{\bar{y}^{2}}\lambda^{m}-\frac{p}{\bar{y}^{2}}=0.

(19)

Theorem 10

The positive equilibirium
\overline{y}
of Eq.(
12
) is locally asymptotically stable when
p\in\left(0,\frac{3}{4}\right).

Proof.
From Theorem
2
, all roots of the characteristic equation of Eq.(
18
) lie in an open disc
\left|\lambda\right|<1
, if

\left|q_{0}\right|+\left|q_{1}\right|+\left|q_{2}\right|+\cdots\left|q_{m}\right|<1\text{.}

It follows from (
19
) that

\left|q_{0}\right|+\left|q_{1}\right|+\left|q_{2}\right|+\cdots\left|q_{m}\right|=\frac{3p}{\bar{y}^{2}}.

Note that

\frac{p}{\bar{y}^{2}}=\frac{2p+1-\sqrt{4p+1}}{2p}.

With many numerical calculations, we get that

\displaystyle\left|q_{0}\right|+\left|q_{1}\right|+\left|q_{2}\right|+\cdots\left|q_{m}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3p}{\bar{y}^{2}}<1,

\displaystyle\frac{3\left(2p+1-\sqrt{4p+1}\right)}{2p}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle\frac{4p+3-3\sqrt{4p+1}}{2p}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0.

Hence, we obtain from
p>0
,

\left(\sqrt{4p+1}-1\right)\left(\sqrt{4p+1}-2\right)<0.

Therefore, we get that
0<p<\frac{3}{4}
. And the proof is complete.

Theorem 11

The equilibrium point
\bar{y}
of Eq.(
12
) is globally
asymptotically stable if
0<p<\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.
From Theorem
6
, we know that there exist
I
and
S
such
that

1<I=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\inf y_{n}\leq S=\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\sup y_{n}.

Hence, we get from Eq.(
12
)

I\geq 1+p\frac{I}{S^{2}}\text{ and }S\leq 1+p\frac{S}{I^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain that

S+p\frac{I}{S}\leq IS\leq I+p\frac{S}{I}.

Thus, we have

\left(S-I\right)\left(1-p\left(\frac{1}{S}+\frac{1}{I}\right)\right)\leq 0.

From
S\geq I>1
and
0<p<\frac{1}{2}
, we also get

1-p\left(\frac{1}{S}+\frac{1}{I}\right)>0.

So, we have
S\leq I
which the result follows. Hence, the equilibrium point

\bar{y}
of Eq.(
12
) is globally asymptotically stable if
0<p<\frac{1}{2}.

Conjecture 12

Many numerical simulations show that If
\frac{1}{2}\leq p<\frac{3}{4}
,
then the equilibrium point
\bar{y}
of Eq.(
12
) is globally
asymptotically stable.

6
Rate of Convergence of Eq.(
12
)

Here, we investigate the rate of convergence of solutions of Eq.(
12
).

Theorem 13

Let
\lambda_{j}
be roots of characteristic equation (
19
) where
j\in\left\{1,\cdots,k\right\}
. Then, every
solution of Eq.(
12
) ensures the following relations:

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\left|\frac{y_{n+1}-\bar{y}}{y_{n}-\bar{y}}\right|=\left|\lambda_{j}\right|,

and

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\sup\left(\left|y_{n}-\bar{y}\right|\right)^{1/n}=\left|\lambda_{j}\right|.

Proof.
According to Eq.(
12
), we have that

\displaystyle y_{n+1}-\bar{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+p\frac{y_{n-m}}{y_{n}^{2}}\right)-\left(1+p\frac{\bar{y}}{\bar{y}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{p\left(y_{n}+\bar{y}\right)}{\bar{y}y_{n}^{2}}\left(y_{n}-\bar{y}\right)+\frac{p}{y_{n}^{2}}\left(y_{n-m}-\bar{y}\right).

Now, we consider
e_{n}=y_{n}-\bar{y}
. Then, we obtain

e_{n+1}+p_{n}e_{n}+q_{n}e_{n-m}=0,

such that

p_{n}=-\frac{p\left(y_{n}+\bar{y}\right)}{\bar{y}y_{n}^{2}},

and

q_{n}=\frac{p}{y_{n}^{2}}.

Therefore, we get from globally asymptotic stability

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}p_{n}=-\frac{2p}{\bar{y}^{2}},

and

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}q_{n}=\frac{p}{\bar{y}^{2}}.

So, the proof is completed.

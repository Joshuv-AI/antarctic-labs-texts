Abstract
. We study the categorical-algebraic condition that internal actions are weakly representable (WRA) in the context of varieties of (non-associative) algebras over a field. Our first aim is to give a complete characterization of action accessible, operadic quadratic varieties of non-associative algebras which satisfy an identity of degree two and to study the representability of actions for them. Here we prove that the varieties of two-step nilpotent (anti-)commutative algebras and that of commutative associative algebras are weakly action representable, and we explain that the condition (WRA) is closely connected to the existence of a so-called amalgam . Our second aim is to work towards the construction, still within the context of algebras over a field, of a weakly representing object ℰ ⁡ ( X ) \mathscr{E}(X) for the actions on (or split extensions of) an object X X . We actually obtain a partial algebra ℰ ⁡ ( X ) \mathscr{E}(X) , which we call external weak actor of X X , together with a monomorphism of functors SplExt ⁡ ( − , X ) ↣ Hom ⁡ ( U ⁡ ( − ) , ℰ ⁡ ( X ) ) {\SplExt(-,X)\rightarrowtail\Hom(U(-),\mathscr{E}(X))} , which we study in detail in the case of quadratic varieties. Furthermore, the relations between the construction of the universal strict general actor USGA ⁡ ( X ) \USGA(X) and that of ℰ ⁡ ( X ) \mathscr{E}(X) are described in detail. We end with some open questions.

Introduction

In the article
[

]
, F. Borceux, G. Janelidze and G. M. Kelly introduce the concept of an

, with the aim of extending the correspondence between actions and split extensions from the context of groups and Lie algebras to arbitrary semi-abelian categories
[

[X]

\Hom(-,[X])

[

[
Tim

The first of these was the concept of an

[

[

Alternatively, the properties of the representing object

Such an object

Preliminaries

The present work takes place in

which were introduced in
[

in order to capture categorical-algebraic properties of non-abelian algebraic structures. A category is

\mathbf{Grp}
of groups, the category

of non-associative algebras over a field

, we assume that

is a variety of non-associative algebra over a field

. We fix the field

, so that we may drop it from our notation.

A feature of semi-abelian categories is that one can define a notion of

Definition 1.1

We can weaken this condition assuming instead that for any

of associative algebras or the category

of commutative associative algebras. This notion called

Since by definition the existence of a terminal object in

\textit{action representability}\Rightarrow\textit{action accessibility.}

Recently, in

A semi-abelian category

It is clear from the definitions that every action representable category is weakly action representable. Also in
[

]

In the same paper, the representability of actions in the categories

Another important observation made by G. Janelidze is that every weakly action representable category is action accessible. We thus have that

\textit{action representability}\Rightarrow\textit{weak action representability}\Rightarrow\textit{action accessibility.}

J. R. A. Gray proved in
[

that the varieties of

are action accessible but not weakly action representable. This partially answers a question asked by G. Janelidze in

Our aim here is to study what happens for a different class of categories, namely varieties of not necessarily associative algebras over a field.

Varieties of non-associative algebras

We now recall the algebraic setting we are working in:
varieties of non-associative algebras
over a field
\mathbb{F}
. We think of those as collections of algebras satisfying a chosen set of polynomial equations. The interested reader can find a more detailed presentation of the subject in
[
VdL-NAA
]
.

By a
(non-associative) algebra

A
we mean a vector space
A
equipped with a bilinear operation
A\times A\rightarrow A\colon(x,y)\mapsto xy
which we call the
multiplication
. The existence of a unit element is not assumed, nor are any other conditions on the multiplication besides its bilinearity. Let
\mathbf{Alg}
denote the category of non-associative algebras, where morphisms are linear maps which preserve the multiplication.

We consider the
free algebra

\mathbf{Set}\rightarrow\mathbf{Alg}
which sends a set
S
to the
free algebra
generated by elements of
S
. This functor has the forgetful functor as a right adjoint. Moreover, it factorises through the
free magma functor

\FM\colon\mathbf{Set}\rightarrow\mathbf{Mag}
, which sends a set
S
to the magma
\FM(S)
of non-associative words in
S

magma algebra functor

\mathbb{F}[-]\colon\mathbf{Mag}\rightarrow\mathbf{Alg}
.

Let
S
be a set. An element
\varphi
of
\mathbb{F}[\FM(S)]
is called a
non-associative polynomial
on
S
. We say that such a polynomial is a
monomial
when it is a scalar multiple of an element in
\FM(S)
. For example, if
S=\{x,y,z,t\}
, then
(xy)t+(zy)x
,
xx+yz
and
(xt)(yz)
are polynomials in
S
and only the last one is a monomial. For a monomial
\varphi
on a set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, we define its
type
as the
n

(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
where
k_{i}
is the number of times
x_{i}
appears in
\varphi
, and its
degree
as the natural number
k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}
. A polynomial is said to be
multilinear
if all monomials composing it have the same type of the form
(1,\dots,1)
. Among the examples above, only the last one is multilinear.

Definition 1.3
.

An
identity
of an algebra
A
is a non-associative polynomial
\varphi=\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
such that
\varphi(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0
for all
a_{1}
, …,
a_{n}\in A
. We say that the algebra
A

satisfies
the identity
\varphi
.

Let
I
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}[\FM(S)]
with
S
being a set of variables. The
variety of algebras
determined by
I
is the class of all algebras which satisfy all the identities in
I
. We say that a variety
satisfies the identities in
I
if all algebras in this variety satisfy the given identities. In particular, if the variety is determined by a set of multilinear polynomials, then we say that the variety is
operadic
. If there exists a set of identities of degree
2
or

that generate all the identities of
\mathscr{V}
, we say that the variety is
quadratic
. Recall—see for instance
[

]
where this is explained in detail—that an operadic, quadratic variety of algebras over a field can be viewed as a variety determined by a quadratic operad.

Any variety of non-associative algebras can, of course, be seen as a category where the morphisms are the same as in
\mathbf{Alg}
. In particular, any such variety is a semi-abelian category.

Remark 1.4
.

Whenever the characteristic of the field
\mathbb{F}
is zero, any variety of non-associative algebras over
\mathbb{F}
is operadic. This is due to the well-known multilinearisation process, see

Partial Algebras

We end this chapter with a notion we shall use throughout the text.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
X
be an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space. A
bilinear partial operation
on
X
is a map

\cdot\colon\Omega\rightarrow X,

where
\Omega
is a vector subspace of
X\times X
, which is bilinear on
\Omega
, i.e.

(\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\alpha_{2}x_{2})\cdot y=\alpha_{1}x_{1}\cdot y+\alpha_{2}x_{2}\cdot y

for any
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{F}
and
x_{1},x_{2},y\in X
such that
(x_{1},y),(x_{2},y)\in\Omega
and

x\cdot(\beta_{1}y_{1}+\beta_{2}y_{2})=\beta_{1}x\cdot y_{1}+\beta_{2}x\cdot y_{2}

for any
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{F}
and
x,y_{1},y_{2}\in X
such that
(x,y_{1}),(x,y_{2})\in\Omega
.

Definition 1.13
.

A
partial algebra
over
\mathbb{F}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
X
endowed with a bilinear partial operation

\cdot\colon\Omega\rightarrow X.

We denote it by
(X,\cdot,\Omega)
. When
\Omega=X\times X
we say that the algebra is
total
.

Let
(X,\cdot,\Omega)
and
(X^{\prime},\ast,\Omega^{\prime})
be partial algebras over
\mathbb{F}
. A homomorphism of partial algebras is an
\mathbb{F}
-linear map
f\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
such that
f(x\cdot y)=f(x)\ast f(y)
whenever
(x,y)\in\Omega
, which tacitly implies that
(f(x),f(y))\in\Omega^{\prime}
(i.e. both
x\cdot y
and
f(x)\ast f(y)
are defined). We denote by
\mathbf{PAlg}
the category whose objects are partial algebras and whose morphisms are partial algebra homomorphisms.

Definition 1.14
.

We say that a partial algebra
(X,\cdot,\Omega)

satisfies an identity
when that identity holds wherever the bilinear partial operation is well defined.

For instance, a partial algebra
(X,\cdot,\Omega)
is associative if

x\cdot(y\cdot z)=(x\cdot y)\cdot z

for every
x
,
y
,
z\in X
such that
(x,y)
,
(y,z)
,
(x,yz)
,
(xy,z)\in\Omega
.

Remark 1.15
.

We observe that any variety of non-associative algebras
\mathscr{V}
has an obvious forgetful functor
U\colon\mathscr{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{PAlg}
.

2.
Commutative and anti-commutative algebras

In this section we aim to study the (weak) representability of actions of some varieties of non-associative algebras which satisfy the commutative law or the anti-commutative law. As explained in Section
1
, we may assume our variety satisfies the
\lambda/\mu
-rules, or equivalently is action accessible.

When
\mathscr{V}
is either a variety of commutative or anti-commutative algebras, i.e.
xy=\varepsilon yx
is an identity of
\mathscr{V}
, with
\varepsilon=\pm 1
, the
\lambda/\mu
-rules reduce to

x(yz)=\alpha(xy)z+\beta(xz)y,

for some
\alpha
,
\beta\in\mathbb{F}
. The following proposition is a representation theory exercise:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{V}
be a non-abelian, action accessible, operadic variety of non-associative algebras.

(1)

If
\mathscr{V}
is a variety of commutative algebras, then
\mathscr{V}
is a either a subvariety of
\mathbf{CAssoc}
or a subvariety of
\mathbf{JJord}
.

(2)

If
\mathscr{V}
is a variety of anti-commutative algebras, then
\mathscr{V}
is either a subvariety of
\mathbf{Lie}
or a subvariety of
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
. ∎

Remark 2.2
.

We observe that
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})
is a subvariety of both
\mathbf{CAssoc}
and
\mathbf{JJord}
: in fact, from
x(yz)=(xy)z=0
we may deduce that associativity holds and the Jacobi identity is satisfied:

x(yz)+y(zx)+z(xy)=0+0+0=0.

If
\bchar(\mathbb{F})\neq 3
, then
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})
is precisely the intersection of the varieties
\mathbf{CAssoc}
and
\mathbf{JJord}
. Indeed, let
\mathscr{V}
be a subvariety of both
\mathbf{CAssoc}
and
\mathbf{JJord}
. Since commutativity, associativity and the Jacobi identity hold in
\mathscr{V}
, we have

(xy)z=x(yz)=-y(zx)-z(xy)=-x(yz)-(xy)z=-2(xy)z

and thus
3(xy)z=3x(yz)=0
.

An example of an algebra which lies in the intersection of
\mathbf{CAssoc}
and
\mathbf{JJord}
but which is not two-step nilpotent is the two-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-algebra with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
and bilinear multiplication determined by

e_{1}^{2}=e_{1}e_{2}=e_{2}e_{1}=e_{2}^{2}=e_{2}.

Likewise,
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})
is a subvariety of both
\mathbf{Lie}
and
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
: from
x(yz)=(xy)z=0
we may deduce anti-associativity and the Jacobi identity. If
\bchar(\mathbb{F})\neq 3
, then
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})
coincides with the intersection of the varieties
\mathbf{Lie}
and
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
. Indeed, let
\mathscr{V}
be a subvariety of both
\mathbf{Lie}
and
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
. Since anti-commutativity, anti-associativity and the Jacobi identity hold in
\mathscr{V}
, we have

(xy)z=-x(yz)=-(xy)z-y(xz)=-(xy)z+(yx)z=-2(xy)z

and thus
3(xy)z=-3x(yz)=0
.

When
\bchar(\mathbb{F})=3
, it is possible to construct an algebra that lies in the intersection of
\mathbf{Lie}
and
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
but which is not two-step nilpotent. Let
X
be the algebra of dimension

over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
with basis

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4},e_{5},e_{6},e_{7}\}

and bilinear multiplication determined by

e_{1}e_{2}=-e_{2}e_{1}=e_{4},\;e_{1}e_{3}=-e_{3}e_{1}=-e_{6},\;e_{2}e_{3}=-e_{3}e_{2}=e_{5}

and

e_{1}e_{5}=-e_{5}e_{1}=e_{2}e_{6}=-e_{6}e_{2}=e_{3},e_{4}=-e_{4}e_{3}=e_{7}.

Then
X
is a Lie algebra such that

x(xy)=0

for any
x
,
y\in X
and, using the multi-linearisation process, one can check this identity is equivalent to anti-associativity if the characteristic of the field is different from
2
. This

is not two-step nilpotent, since

e_{1}(e_{2}e_{3})=e_{1}e_{5}=e_{7}.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{V}
be an action accessible, operadic, quadratic variety of non-associative algebras and suppose that

(1)

We already know that

The representability of actions of the variety of commutative associative algebras over a field was studied in

over the thin category of commutative subalgebras of

By the above, the

Recall that for a diagram in a category, an

is a monic cocone, i.e. a cocone which is a monomorphic natural transformation. This means that each component of that cocone is a monomorphism, which implies that all the morphisms of the given diagram were monomorphisms to begin with. Note that in a category with colimits, an amalgam for a diagram exists if and only if its colimit cocone is such an amalgam. A category is said to have the

[

for an overview of examples and references to the rich literature on the subject.

This is as follows related to the problem at hand. The associative algebra

of the diagram consisting of the commutative subalgebras

Theorem 2.9
.

If

The proof depends on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10

Let

Proof.

Note that if

Jacobi–Jordan algebras

We now want to study the representability of actions of the variety
\mathbf{JJord}
of Jacobi–Jordan algebras. As already mentioned in Section
1
, every split extension of
B
by
X
in
\mathbf{Lie}
is represented by a homomorphism
{B\rightarrow\Der(X)}
. For Jacobi–Jordan algebras, the role the derivations have in
\mathbf{Lie}
is played by the so-called
anti-derivations
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
X
be a Jacobi–Jordan algebra. An
anti-derivation
is a linear map
d\colon X\rightarrow X
such that

d(xy)=-d(x)y-d(y)x,\quad\forall x,y\in X.

The (left) multiplications
L_{x}
for
x\in X
are particular anti-derivations, called
inner anti-derivations
. We denote by
\ADer(X)
the space of anti-derivations of
X
and by
\Inn(X)
the subspace of the inner anti-derivations. Anti-derivations play a significant role in the study of cohomology of Jacobi–Jordan algebras: see
[
JJAlg2
]
for further details.

We now want to make explicit what are the derived actions in the category
\mathbf{JJord}
and how they are related with the anti-derivations. The following is an easy application of
Lemma

1.7
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
X
and
B
be two Jacobi–Jordan algebras. Given a pair of bilinear maps

l\colon B\times X\rightarrow X,\qquad r\colon X\times B\rightarrow X

defined by

b\ast x=l(b,x),\qquad x\ast b=r(x,b),

we construct
(B\oplus X,\cdot)
as in (
1.3
). Then
(B\oplus X,\cdot)
is a Jacobi–Jordan algebra if and only if

(1)

b\ast x=x\ast b
;

(2)

b\ast(xx^{\prime})=-(b\ast x)x^{\prime}-(b\ast x)\ast x^{\prime}
;

(3)

(bb^{\prime})\ast x=-b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)-b^{\prime}\ast(b\ast x)
;

for all
b
,
b^{\prime}\in B
and
x
,
x^{\prime}\in X
. ∎

In an equivalent way, a derived action of
B
on
X
in the variety
\mathbf{JJord}
is given by a linear map

B\rightarrow\ADer(X)\colon b\mapsto b\ast-

which satisfies

(2.1)

(bb^{\prime})\ast x=-b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)-b^{\prime}\ast(b\ast x),\quad\forall b,b^{\prime}\in B,\;\forall x\in X.

Remark 2.16
.

The vector space
\ADer(X)
endowed with the
anti-commutator

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\ADer(X)\times\ADer(X)\rightarrow\End(X),\quad\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle=-f\circ f^{\prime}-f^{\prime}\circ f

is not in general an algebra, since the anti-commutator of two anti-derivations is not in general an anti-derivation: in
[
JJAlg3
, Remark 2.2]
, the authors proved that
\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle\in\ADer(X)
if and only if

\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle(xy)=-f(x)f^{\prime}(y)-f^{\prime}(x)f(y),\quad\forall x,y\in X.

Moreover, it can happen that the anti-commutator
\langle-,-\rangle
is a well defined bilinear operation on the space
\ADer(X)
but it does not define a Jacobi–Jordan algebra structure: for instance, if

is the abelian one-dimensional algebra, then
\ADer(X)=\End(X)\cong\mathbb{F}
(every linear endomorphism of
X
is of the form
\varphi_{\alpha}\colon x\mapsto\alpha x
, for some

) and the Jacobi identity is not satisfied. Nevertheless, there are some subspaces of
\ADer(X)
that are Jacobi–Jordan algebras. For instance, the subspace
\Inn(X)
of all inner anti-derivations of
X
. Indeed, the linear map

X\rightarrow\ADer(X)\colon x\mapsto L_{x},

restricts to a Jacobi–Jordan algebra homomorphism
X\rightarrow\Inn(X)
. This is true in general for the image of any linear map
B\rightarrow\ADer(X)
satisfying equation (
2.1
).

Thus we need to use an algebraic structure which includes the space of anti-derivations endowed with the anti-commutator and which allows us to describe categorically the representability of actions of the variety
\mathbf{JJord}
. One possible solution is given by

.

Indeed, the vector space
\ADer(X)
endowed with the anti-commutator

is a commutative partial algebra. In this case

We now analyse the case where

[X]_{2}
as an abelian algebra (i.e.
\langle f,g\rangle=0_{\End(X)}
, for every
f,g\in[X]_{2}
), from the third equation we deduce that the linear map

B\rightarrow[X]_{2}\colon b\mapsto b\ast-

is an algebra homomorphism.

On the other hand, given a morphism of algebras

\varphi\colon B\rightarrow[X]_{2},\quad\varphi(b)=b\ast-

satisfying

b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)=0,\quad\forall b,b^{\prime}\in B,\;\forall x\in X,

we can consider the split extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(B\oplus X,\ast_{\varphi})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

where the two-step nilpotent commutative algebra structure of
B\oplus X
is given by

(b,x)\ast_{\varphi}(b^{\prime},x^{\prime})=(bb^{\prime},xx^{\prime}+b\ast x^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\ast x),\quad\forall(b,x),(b^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in B\oplus X.

We can now claim the following result.

Theorem 2.19
.

(1)

Let
B
and
X
be two-step nilpotent commutative algebras. The isomorphism classes of split extensions of
B
by
X
are in bijection with the algebra homomorphisms

B\rightarrow[X]_{2}\colon b\mapsto b\ast-

satisfying

(2.2)

b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)=0,\quad\forall b,b^{\prime}\in B,\;\forall x\in X.

(2)

The variety
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})
is weakly action representable. A weak representation of the functor
\SplExt(-,X)=\SplExt_{\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})}(-,X)
is given by

\tau\colon\SplExt(-,X)\rightarrowtail\Hom_{\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})}(-,[X]_{2}),

where
\tau_{B}
is the injection which sends any split extension of
B
by
X
to the corresponding homomorphism
B\rightarrow[X]_{2}
, defined by
b\mapsto b\ast-
as above.

(3)

A homomorphism
B\rightarrow[X]_{2}
is an acting morphism if and only if it satisfies
Equation

2.2
.

Proof.

(1) It follows from the analysis above.

(2) We observe that
\tau
is a natural transformation. Indeed, for every morphism
f\colon B^{\prime}\rightarrow B
in
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})
, we can check that the diagram in
\mathbf{Set}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\SplExt(B,X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(B,[X]_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\SplExt(B^{\prime},X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(B^{\prime},[X]_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\SplExt(f,X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom(f,[X]_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{B^{\prime}}}

is commutative. Moreover
\tau_{B}
is an injection since every isomorphism class of split extensions of
B
by
X
is uniquely determined by the corresponding derived action. Thus
\tau
is a monomorphism of functors and
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})
is a weakly action representable category.

(3) Finally,
\varphi\colon B\rightarrow[X]_{2}
is an acting morphism if and only if it defines a split extension of
B
by
X
in
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{Com})
, i.e. it satisfies equation (
2.2
).
∎

Let us observe that not every morphism
B\rightarrow[X]_{2}
defines a split extension of
B
by
X
. For instance, if
B=\mathbb{F}\{b,b^{\prime}\}
and
X=\mathbb{F}\{x\}\cong\mathbb{F}
are abelian algebras, then
[X]_{2}=\End(X)
and the homomorphism
\varphi\colon B\rightarrow[X]_{2}
, defined by

\varphi(b)=\varphi(b^{\prime})=1_{X}

is not an acting morphism. Indeed,

\varphi(b)(\varphi(b^{\prime})(x))=1_{X}(1_{X}(x))=x\neq 0.

Anti-commutative anti-associative algebras

For the variety
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
of anti-commutative anti-associative algebras, a similar description of split extensions and derived actions can be made as for the variety
\mathbf{JJord}
. The role of the anti-derivations is played here by the endomorphisms in the associative partial algebra

[X]\coloneqq\{f\in\End(X)\;|\;f(xy)=-f(x)y,\;\forall x\in X\},

whose bilinear partial operation is given by

\langle f,g\rangle=-f\circ g.

It is easy to see that
\langle-,-\rangle
does not define, in general, a total algebra structure on
[X]
, nor need it be anti-commutative or anti-associative. An example is given by the abelian two-dimensional algebra
X=\mathbb{F}^{2}
, where
[X]=\End(X)
.

We may check that a derived action of
B
by
X
in the variety
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
is the same thing as a partial algebra homomorphism

B\rightarrow[X]\colon b\mapsto b\ast-

which satisfies

(bb^{\prime})\ast-=-b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast-),\quad\forall b,b^{\prime}\in B.

Moreover, we obtain the following result whose proof is similar to the one of
Theorem

2.17
.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
X
be a an object of
\mathbf{ACAAssoc}
and let
U\colon\mathbf{ACAAssoc}\rightarrow\mathbf{PAlg}
denote the forgetful functor.

(1)

There exists a natural isomorphism

\SplExt(-,X)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{PAlg}}(U(-),[X]),

where
\SplExt(-,X)=\SplExt_{\mathbf{ACAAssoc}}(-,X)
;

(2)

if
[X]
is an anti-commutative anti-associative algebra, then the functor
\SplExt(-,X)
is representable and
[X]
is the actor of
X
; ∎

Two-step nilpotent anti-commutative algebras

We conclude this section by studying the representability of actions of the variety
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})
. An important example of a two-step nilpotent anti-commutative algebra is the
(2n+1)
-dimensional
Heisenberg algebra

\mathfrak{h}_{2n+1}
, that is the algebra with basis

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n},h\}

and non-trivial products
e_{i}f_{j}=-f_{j}e_{i}=\delta_{ij}h
, for all
i
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, where
\delta_{ij}
is the
Kronecker delta
.

An analysis, similar to the case of two-step nilpotent commutative algebras can be made, so we simply state the following theorem:

Theorem 2.21
.

(1)

Let
B
and
X
be two-step nilpotent anti-commutative algebras. The isomorphism classes of split extensions of
B
by
X
are in bijection with the algebra homomorphisms

B\rightarrow[X]_{2}\colon b\mapsto b\ast-

where
[X]_{2}
is defined as in the commutative case, which satisfy the condition

(2.3)

b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)=0,\quad\forall b,b^{\prime}\in B,\;\forall x\in X.

(2)

The variety
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})
is weakly action representable. A weak representation of
\SplExt(-,X)=\SplExt_{\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})}
is given by

\tau\colon\SplExt(-,X)\rightarrowtail\Hom_{\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})}(-,[X]_{2}),

where
\tau_{B}
is the injection which associates with any split extension of
B
by
X
, the corresponding homomorphism
B\rightarrow[X]_{2}\colon b\mapsto b\ast-
as in (1).

(3)

A homomorphism
B\rightarrow[X]_{2}
is an acting morphism if and only if it satisfies
Equation

2.3
. ∎

Again, if
B=\mathbb{F}\{b,b^{\prime}\}
is the abelian two-dimensional algebra and
X=\mathbb{F}
is the abelian one-dimensional algebra, the linear map
\varphi\colon B\rightarrow[X]_{2}=\End(X)
, defined by
\varphi(b)=\varphi(b^{\prime})=1_{X}
is an example of a morphism in
\mathbf{Nil}_{2}(\mathbf{ACom})
which is not an acting morphism.

3.
Representability of actions of non-associative algebras

We want to extend the results obtained in the previous section by studying the (weak) representability of actions of a general variety of non-associative algebras over a field
\mathbb{F}
. Again, we assume that
\mathscr{V}
is an action accessible, operadic variety of non-associative algebras over
\mathbb{F}
. Thus
\mathscr{V}
satisfies a set of multilinear identities

\Phi_{k,i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

where
k
is the degree of the polynomial
\Phi_{k,i}
. We fix
\lambda_{1}
, …,
\lambda_{8}
,
\mu_{1}
, …,
\mu_{8}\in\mathbb{F}
which determine a choice of
\lambda/\mu
rules, i.e.

\displaystyle x(yz)=\lambda_{1}(xy)z

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}(yx)z+\lambda_{3}z(xy)+\lambda_{4}z(yx)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{5}(xz)y+\lambda_{6}(zx)y+\lambda_{7}y(xz)+\lambda_{8}y(zx)

and

\displaystyle(yz)x=\mu_{1}(xy)z

\displaystyle+\mu_{2}(yx)z+\mu_{3}z(xy)+\mu_{4}z(yx)

\displaystyle+\mu_{5}(xz)y+\mu_{6}(zx)y+\mu_{7}y(xz)+\mu_{8}y(zx)

which are identities in
\mathscr{V}
. Note that these are not unique, but fixed for our purposes.

For any object
X
of
\mathscr{V}
, we want to define a vector space
\mathscr{E}(X)
such that

\Inn(X)\leq\mathscr{E}(X)\leq\End(X)^{2},

where
\Inn(X)=\{(L_{x},R_{x})\mid x\in X\}
is the vector space of left and right multiplications of
X
, and we want to endow it with a bilinear partial operation

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\Omega\subseteq X\times X\rightarrow X,

such that we can associate in a natural way a homomorphism of partial algebras
B\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)
, with every split extension of
B
by
X
in
\mathscr{V}
. To do this, we describe derived actions in
\mathscr{V}
in a similar fashion as in the previous section.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
and
B
be two algebras in
\mathscr{V}
. Given a pair of bilinear maps

l\colon B\times X\rightarrow X,\qquad r\colon X\times B\rightarrow X,

we construct
(B\oplus X,\cdot)
as in (
1.3
). Then
(B\oplus X,\cdot)
is an object of
\mathscr{V}
if and only if

\Phi_{k,i}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})=0,\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n,

where at least one of the
\alpha_{1}
, …,
\alpha_{k}
is an element of of the form
(0,x)
, with
x\in X
, and the others are of the form
(b,0)
, with
b\in B
. The resulting algebra is the
semi-direct product
of
B
and
X
, denoted by
B\ltimes X
. ∎

Using the same notation of
Remark

1.8
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3.2
.

When every identity of
\mathscr{V}
can be deduced from the
\lambda/\mu
rules,
(B\oplus X,\cdot)
is an object of
\mathscr{V}
if and only if

(1)

b\ast(xx^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}(b\ast x)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{8}x(x^{\prime}\ast b)
;

(2)

(xx^{\prime})\ast b=\mu_{1}(b\ast x)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{8}x(x^{\prime}\ast b)
;

(3)

x(x^{\prime}\ast b)=\lambda_{1}(xx^{\prime})\ast b+\cdots+\lambda_{8}x^{\prime}(b\ast x)
;

(4)

(x^{\prime}\ast b)x=\mu_{1}(xx^{\prime})\ast b+\cdots+\mu_{8}x^{\prime}(b\ast x)
;

(5)

x(b\ast x^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}(x\ast b)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{8}b\ast(x^{\prime}x)
;

(6)

(b\ast x^{\prime})x=\mu_{1}(x\ast b)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{8}b\ast(x^{\prime}x)
;

(7)

x\ast(bb^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}(x\ast b)\ast b^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{8}b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)
;

(8)

(bb^{\prime})\ast x=\mu_{1}(x\ast b)\ast b^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{8}b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)
;

(9)

b\ast(b^{\prime}\ast x)=\lambda_{1}(bb^{\prime})\ast x+\cdots+\lambda_{8}b^{\prime}\ast(x\ast b)
;

(10)

(b^{\prime}\ast x)\ast b=\mu_{1}(bb^{\prime})\ast x+\cdots+\mu_{8}b^{\prime}\ast(x\ast b)
;

(11)

b\ast(x\ast b^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}(b\ast x)\ast b^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{8}x\ast(b^{\prime}b)
;

(12)

(x\ast b)\ast b^{\prime}=\mu_{1}(b\ast x)\ast b^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{8}x\ast(b^{\prime}b)
,

for all
b
,
b^{\prime}\in B
and
x
,
x^{\prime}\in X
. ∎

Definition 3.3
.

For every object
X
of
\mathscr{V}
, we define
\mathscr{E}(X)
as the subspace of all pairs
(f\ast-,-\ast f)\in\End(X)^{2}
satisfying

\Phi_{k,i}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})=0,\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n,

for each choice of
\alpha_{j}=f
and
\alpha_{t}\in X
, where
t\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and
fx\coloneq f\ast x
,
xf\coloneq x\ast f
. We endow it with the bilinear map
\langle-,-\rangle\colon\mathscr{E}(X)\times\mathscr{E}(X)\rightarrow\End(X)^{2}

\langle(f\ast-,-\ast f),(g\ast-,-\ast g)\rangle=(h\ast-,-\ast h),

where

\displaystyle x\ast h=\lambda_{1}(x\ast f)\ast g

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}(f\ast x)\ast g+\lambda_{3}g\ast(x\ast f)+\lambda_{4}g\ast(f\ast x)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{5}(x\ast g)\ast f+\lambda_{6}(g\ast x)\ast f+\lambda_{7}f\ast(x\ast g)+\lambda_{8}f\ast(g\ast x)

and

\displaystyle h\ast x=\mu_{1}(x\ast f)\ast g

\displaystyle+\mu_{2}(f\ast x)\ast g+\mu_{3}g\ast(x\ast f)+\mu_{4}g\ast(f\ast x)

\displaystyle+\mu_{5}(x\ast g)\ast f+\mu_{6}(g\ast x)\ast f+\mu_{7}f\ast(x\ast g)+\mu_{8}f\ast(g\ast x).

When every identity of
\mathscr{V}
is a consequence of the
\lambda/\mu
rules,
\mathscr{E}(X)
becomes the subspace of all pairs
(f\ast-,-\ast f)\in\End(X)^{2}
satisfying

(1)

f\ast(xx^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}(f\ast x)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{8}x(x^{\prime}\ast f)
;

(2)

(xx^{\prime})\ast f=\mu_{1}(f\ast x)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{8}x(x^{\prime}\ast f)
;

(3)

x(x^{\prime}\ast f)=\lambda_{1}(xx^{\prime})\ast f+\cdots+\lambda_{8}x^{\prime}(f\ast x)
;

(4)

(x^{\prime}\ast f)x=\mu_{1}(xx^{\prime})\ast f+\cdots+\mu_{8}x^{\prime}(f\ast x)
;

(5)

x(f\ast x^{\prime})=\lambda_{1}(x\ast f)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{8}f\ast(x^{\prime}x)
;

(6)

(f\ast x^{\prime})x=\mu_{1}(x\ast f)x^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{8}f\ast(x^{\prime}x)
,

for every
x
,
x^{\prime}\in X
.

Note that the choice of
\lambda/\mu
rules does not affect to the definition of the underlying vector space of
\mathscr{E}(X)
, but it does play an important role in the bilinear map
\langle-,-\rangle
.
In general, the vector space
\mathscr{E}(X)
endowed with the bilinear map
\langle-,-\rangle
is not an object of
\mathscr{V}
. It may happen that
\langle-,-\rangle
does not even define a bilinear operation on
\mathscr{E}(X)
, i.e. there exist
(f\ast-,-\ast f)
,
(g\ast-,-\ast g)\in\mathscr{E}(X)
such that

\langle(f\ast-,-\ast f),(g\ast-,-\ast g)\rangle\not\in\mathscr{E}(X)

or that
(\mathscr{E}(X),\langle-,-\rangle)
is a non-associative algebra which does not satisfy some identity of
\mathscr{V}
.

Example 3.4
.

We may check that, if
\mathscr{V}=\mathbf{Assoc}
, then
\mathscr{E}(X)\cong\Bim(X)
as vector spaces. Moreover, with the standard choice of
\lambda/\mu
rules
\lambda_{1}=\mu_{8}=1
and the rest equal to zero, it is also an isomorphism of associative algebras.

Example 3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{V}=\mathbf{Leib}
, it is easy to see that
\mathscr{E}(X)\cong\Bider(X)
as vector spaces. Choosing the
\lambda/\mu
rules as

\displaystyle x(yz)

\displaystyle=(xy)z-(xz)y,

\displaystyle(yz)x

\displaystyle=(yx)z-y(xz),

we get the standard multiplication defined in
\Bider(X)
as in
[
loday1993version
]
, that defines a weak actor in
\mathbf{Leib}
. On the other hand, choosing the
\lambda/\mu
rules as

\displaystyle x(yz)

\displaystyle=(xy)z-(xz)y,

\displaystyle(yz)x

\displaystyle=(yx)z+y(zx),

we get the non-associative algebra structure defined in
[
Casas
, Definition 5.2]
, which, in general, is not a Leibniz algebra.

Example 3.6
.

If
\mathscr{V}=\mathbf{Nil}_{k}(\mathbf{Assoc})
, with
k\geq 3
, then

\mathscr{E}(X)=\{(f\ast-,-\ast f)\in\Bim(X)\mid f\ast(x_{1}\cdots x_{k})=(x_{1}\cdots x_{k})\ast f=0\}.

With the same choice of
\lambda/\mu
rules as in
Example

3.4
, the bilinear operation
\langle-,-\rangle
becomes

\langle(f\ast-,-\ast f),(g\ast-,-\ast g)\rangle=(f\ast(g\ast-),(-\ast f)\ast g)

which makes
\mathscr{E}(X)
an associative algebra, but not a
k
-step nilpotent algebra. For instance, let
X
be the abelian one-dimensional algebra, then

\mathscr{E}(X)=\End(X)\times\End(X)^{\op}\cong\mathbb{F}^{2}

which is not nilpotent. Indeed, every linear endomorphism of
X
is of the form
\varphi_{\alpha}\colon x\mapsto\alpha x
, for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}
and

\langle(\varphi_{\alpha},\varphi_{\beta}),(\varphi_{\alpha^{\prime}},\varphi_{\beta^{\prime}})\rangle=(\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\varphi_{\alpha^{\prime}},\varphi_{\beta^{\prime}}\circ\varphi_{\beta})=(\varphi_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}},\varphi_{\beta\beta^{\prime}}).

Example 3.7
.

If
\mathscr{V}=\mathbf{Alt}
is the variety of alternative algebras over a field
\mathbb{F}
with
\bchar(\mathbb{F})\neq 2
, then
\mathscr{E}(X)
consists of the pairs
(f\ast-,-\ast f)\in\End(X)^{2}
satisfying

f\ast(xy)=(x\ast f)y+(f\ast x)y-x(f\ast y),

(xy)\ast f=x(f\ast y)+x(y\ast f)-(x\ast f)y,

x(y\ast f)=(yx)\ast f+(xy)\ast f-y(x\ast f)

and

(f\ast x)y=f\ast(yx)+f\ast(xy)-(f\ast y)x

for any
x,y\in X
, and the bilinear map

\langle(f\ast-,-\ast f),(g\ast-,-\ast g)\rangle=(h\ast-,-\ast h)

is given by

h\ast x=-(f\ast x)\ast g+f\ast(g\ast x)+f\ast(x\ast g)

and

x\ast h=(x\ast f)\ast g+(f\ast x)\ast g-f\ast(x\ast g).

One can check that
\langle-,-\rangle
does not define an algebra structure. Nevertheless, it is possible to find examples where
\mathscr{E}(X)
is an alternative algebra.

For instance, if
X
is a
unitary
, or
unital
, alternative algebra (i.e. there exists an element
e\in X
such that
xe=ex=x
, for any
x\in X
), such as the algebra of octonions
\mathbb{O}
, then the elements of
\mathscr{E}(X)
satisfy the following set of equations

\displaystyle f\ast x=

\displaystyle(x\ast f)e+(f\ast x)e-x(f\ast e),

\displaystyle x\ast f=

\displaystyle e(f\ast x)+e(x\ast f)-(e\ast f)x,

\displaystyle x\ast f=

\displaystyle x\ast f+x\ast f-x(e\ast f),

\displaystyle f\ast x=

\displaystyle f\ast x+f\ast x-(f\ast e)x,

for any
x\in X
. Thus, if
\alpha\coloneqq f\ast e
and
\beta\coloneqq e\ast f
, one has

f\ast x=\alpha x=\beta x,\qquad x\ast f=x\alpha=x\beta

and, for
x=e
, one obtains
\alpha=\beta
. In other words, an element of
\mathscr{E}(X)
is uniquely determined by an element
\alpha=f\ast e=e\ast f
of
X
, i.e.

\mathscr{E}(X)\cong\{(\alpha,\alpha)\mid\alpha\in X\}\cong X

is an object of
\mathbf{Alt}
.

Remark 3.8
.

The same result can be obtained for unitary algebras in the variety
\mathbf{Assoc}
. In fact, let
X
be a unitary associative algebra and let
(f\ast-,-\ast f)\in\Bim(X)
. Thus

f\ast x=f\ast(ex)=\alpha x,

x\ast f=(xe)\ast f=x\beta

and

x\alpha=(x\ast f)e=x\beta,

where
\alpha\coloneqq f\ast e
and
\beta\coloneqq e\ast f
. For
x=e
, we obtain
\alpha=\beta
and

\Bim(X)\cong\{(\alpha,\alpha)\mid\alpha\in X\}\cong X.

Since unitary algebras are perfect and have trivial center, from
[
Casas
]
we have a natural isomorphism

\SplExt(-,X)\cong\Hom_{\mathbf{Assoc}}(-,X)

for any unitary associative algebra
X
, i.e.
X
is its own actor.

The construction of
\mathscr{E}(X)
gives rise to an alternative characterisation of the split extensions in
\mathscr{V}
. In fact, a split extension of
B
by
X
in
\mathscr{V}
is the same as a linear map

B\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)\colon b\mapsto(b\ast-,-\ast b),

such that
((bb^{\prime})\ast-,-\ast(bb^{\prime}))=\langle(b\ast-,-\ast b),(b^{\prime}\ast-,-\ast b^{\prime})\rangle
and

\Phi_{k,i}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n,

where
\alpha_{1}
, …,
\alpha_{k}
are as in
Proposition

3.1
.

We remark also that the bilinear map

\langle-,-\rangle\colon\mathscr{E}(X)\times\mathscr{E}(X)\rightarrow\End(X)^{2}

defines a partial operation
\langle-,-\rangle\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)
, where
\Omega
is the preimage

\langle-,-\rangle^{-1}(\mathscr{E}(X))

of the inclusion
\mathscr{E}(X)\hookrightarrow\End(X)^{2}
.

Now we are ready to announce and prove our main result about the weak representability of actions of non-associative algebras.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathscr{V}
be an action accessible, operadic variety of non-associative algebras over a field
\mathbb{F}
and let
U\colon\mathscr{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{PAlg}
denote the forgetful functor.

(1)

Let
X
be an object of
\mathscr{V}
. There exists a monomorphism of functors

\tau\colon\SplExt(-,X)\rightarrowtail\Hom_{\mathbf{PAlg}}(U(-),\mathscr{E}(X)),

where
\SplExt(-,X)=\SplExt_{\mathscr{V}}(-,X)
and, for every object
B
of
\mathscr{V}
,
\tau_{B}
is the injection which sends an element of
\SplExt(B,X)
to the corresponding partial algebra homomorphism

B\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)\colon b\mapsto(b\ast-,-\ast b).

(2)

Let
B
,
X
be objects of
\mathscr{V}
. The homomorphism of partial algebras

B\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X)\colon b\mapsto(b\ast-,-\ast b)

belongs to
\Imm(\tau_{B})
if and only if
\Phi_{k,i}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})=0
, as in
Proposition

3.1
.

(3)

If
(\mathscr{E}(X),\langle-,-\rangle)
is an object of
\mathscr{V}
, then
(\mathscr{E}(X),\tau)
becomes a weak representation of
\SplExt(-,X)
.

(4)

If the diagram in
\mathscr{V}
given by those subalgebras of
\mathscr{E}(X)
which occur as a codomain of a morphism
(B\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(X))\in\Imm(\tau_{B})
admits an amalgam in
\mathscr{V}
, then the colimit of that diagram determines an (initial) weak representation of
\SplExt(-,X)
.

(5)

If

The quadratic case

In this section we introduce a systematic approach to finding the explicit structure of
\mathscr{E}(X)
in the setting of operadic, quadratic varieties of algebras. Here we shall denote an element

\displaystyle f(xy)

\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{subsection}{Converse of the implication ``\textit{weakly action representable category} $\Rightarrow$ \textit{action accessible category}''}
\par We studied the representability of actions of a general operadic variety of non-associative algebras over a field but we were not able to find an example of an action accessible variety which is not weakly action representable. Does the converse of the implication
$$\textit{weakly action representable category}\Rightarrow\textit{action accessible category}$$
hold in this context?
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{subsection}{Subvarieties}
We do not know how the condition (WRA) behaves under taking subvarieties (especially in the non-quadratic case, when the degree of the identities may be higher than $3$). For instance, we know that the variety $\mathbf{Assoc}$ is weakly action representable, but we do not know whether the subvariety $\mathbf{Nil}_{k}(\mathbf{Assoc})$ for $k\geq 3$ satisfies the same condition. We recall that in this case, $\mathscr{E}(X)$ is an associative algebra, but it is not $k$-step nilpotent in general (see \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{Example Nilk}).
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{subsection}{Initial weak representation}
As already mentioned in \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{reminitial}, a variety of non-associative algebras $\mathscr{V}$ is weakly action representable if and only if it is \emph{initially weakly action representable}, which means that for every object $X$, the functor $\SplExt(-,X)$ admits an initial weak representation. We do not know whether or not the weak representations that occur in this article when the external weak actor $\mathscr{E}(X)$ is an object of $\mathscr{V}$ (such as for Leibniz algebras or associative algebras) are initial, or how we would check this in practice.
\par\par\@@unnumbered@section{subsection}{Representability of actions of \emph{unitary} algebras}
\par In the recent article \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{IdeallyExact}{}{}]}, G.\penalty\ Janelidze introduced the notion of \emph{ideally exact} category, with the aim of generalising semi-abelian categories in a way which includes relevant examples of \emph{non-pointed} categories, such as the categories $\textbf{Ring}$ and $\textbf{CRing}$ of (commutative) rings with unit.
\par A category $\mathscr{C}$ is \emph{ideally exact} when it is Barr-exact and Bourn-protomodular with finite coproducts, such that the unique morphism $0\to 1$ in $\mathscr{C}$ is a regular epimorphism. Thus, semi-abelian categories are precisely the pointed ideally exact categories.
\par G.\ Janelidze also extended the notions of action representability and weak action representability to ideally exact categories, showing $\textbf{Ring}$ and $\textbf{CRing}$ are action representable, with the actor of a (commutative) unitary ring $X$ being isomorphic to\penalty\ $X$ itself. We do not recall the construction here, since it is essentially the same as the one for alternative algebras with unit given in \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{exalt}.
\par We recall that a variety of non-associative algebras $\mathscr{V}$ is said to be \emph{unitary closed} if for any object $X$ in it, the algebra $\tilde{X}$ spanned by $X$ and the element $1$, equipped with the multiplication $x\cdot 1=1\cdot x=x$ for any $x\in X$, is still an object of\penalty\ $\mathscr{V}$. For instance, $\mathbf{Assoc}$ and $\mathbf{Alt}$ are unitary closed, and the category $\mathbf{Leib}$, or any variety of anti-commutative algebras over a field of characteristic different from $2$, such as $\mathbf{Lie}$, are examples of varieties which are not. Thus, the condition of being unitary closed depends on the set of identities which determine the variety $\mathscr{V}$.
\par When a variety of algebras $\mathscr{V}$ is unitary closed, one can consider the subcategory\penalty\ $\mathscr{V}_{1}$ of unitary algebras of $\mathscr{V}$ with the arrows being the algebra morphisms of\penalty\ $\mathscr{V}$ that preserve the unit. Of course, $\mathscr{V}_{1}$ is an ideally-exact category and it is not pointed.
\par Examples \ref{exalt} and \ref{unitalassociative} suggest that for a unitary closed variety $\mathscr{V}$, one may use the construction of the external weak actor $\mathscr{E}(X)$ to study the representability of actions of the subcategory $\mathscr{V}_{1}$. For instance, it follows easily that $\mathbf{Alt}_{1}$ and $\mathbf{Assoc}_{1}$ are action representable, with the actor of an object $X$ in both cases being the object $X$ itself.

Damping of cosmological tensor modes in Horndeski theories after GW170817
and Simone Vazzoler

Abstract
This paper investigates the propagation of cosmological gravitational waves interacting with free-streaming neutrinos within the context of Horndeski theories of gravity constrained by the detection of GW170817. We apply the theory of cosmological perturbations to explicitly derive the Einstein-Boltzmann equation for the damped propagation of first-order transverse traceless gravitational waves. In contrast to general relativity, we argue that modified gravity can give rise to non-vanishing free-streaming damping effects during the cosmological matter dominated era. We also provide an analytic formula for the main multipole order with which modified gravity and free-streaming neutrinos damp the variety of tensor correlation functions of the cosmic microwave background.

2
Restricted Horndeski theories

The restricted Horndeski (rH) theories correspond to the most general Horndeski ST framework consistent with the tensor propagation speed
c_{GW}=1

[
31
,
32
,
33
]
. In this article, we focus on the action for rH theories that reads

S_{\mbox{\tiny rH}}\equiv\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{16\pi G}\sum_{i=2}^{4}\mathcal{L}_{\mbox{\tiny rH}}^{i}+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right]\,,

(2.1)

where
g
is the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
,
G
is the gravitational constant,
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is the matter Lagrangian density, and

\mathcal{L}_{\mbox{\tiny rH}}^{2}\equiv G_{2}(X,\varphi)\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mbox{\tiny rH}}^{3}\equiv-\,G_{3}(X,\varphi)\Box\varphi\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\mbox{\tiny rH}}^{4}\equiv G_{4}(\varphi)R\,.

(2.2)

We identify
R
as the Ricci scalar, and the symbols
\nabla_{\mu}
and
\Box\equiv\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}
stand for the covariant derivative and the d’Alembert operator, respectively.

As shown in the above definition (
2.2
), the three free functions
G_{i}
depend on the scalar field
\varphi
and the canonical kinetic term
X\equiv-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\nabla^{\mu}\varphi
.

Clearly, by imposing the condition

G_{2}(X,\varphi)=0\qquad G_{3}(X,\varphi)=0\qquad G_{4}(\varphi)=1\,,

(2.3)

the well-known Einstein-Hilbert action for GR is recovered.

Oftentimes, the attempt to predict physical observables from general frameworks such as action (
2.1
) requires setting case-specific profiles for the free functions and taking dedicated couplings of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
with the other fields of the theory.
For simplification purposes, in this case we just assume
\mathcal{L}_{m}
to be coupled with the metric tensor only.

3
FLRW background and tensor perturbations

In the context of the theory of cosmological perturbations, let us consider tensor perturbations around a spatially flat Friedmann Lemaître Robertson Walker (FLRW) background spacetime.
Generally, these perturbations can be described up to the first order by the perturbed non-vanishing metric components

g_{00}=g^{(0)}_{00}\qquad g_{ij}=g^{(0)}_{ij}+g^{(1)}_{ij}\,,

(3.1)

with

g_{00}^{(0)}=-1\qquad g_{ij}^{(0)}=a^{2}\delta_{ij}\qquad g_{ij}^{(1)}=a^{2}h_{ij}\,.

(3.2)

As usual,
a\equiv a(t)
is the scale factor,
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta function, and
h_{ij}\equiv h_{ij}(\vec{x},t)
is treated as a small perturbation
|h_{ij}|\ll 1
.
We assume that the scalar field
\varphi
contributes only to background dynamics and therefore its profile is purely isotropic:

\displaystyle\varphi=\psi(t)\,.

(3.3)

Hereafter, we refer to background and first-order perturbed quantities using, respectively, the superscripts
{}^{(0)}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
and
{}^{(1)}\sim\mathcal{O}(h)
.

For the purposes of this analysis, we also consider the perturbations of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
around a cosmological perfect fluid background. Up to the first order, the only non-vanishing components are

T_{00}=T^{(0)}_{00}\qquad T_{ij}=T^{(0)}_{ij}+T^{(1)}_{ij}\,,

(3.4)

with

T_{00}^{(0)}=\rho\qquad T_{ij}^{(0)}=a^{2}P\delta_{ij}\qquad T_{ij}^{(1)}=a^{2}\big[Ph_{ij}+\pi_{ij}\big]\,,

(3.5)

where
\rho=\rho(t)
is the background energy density and
P=P(t)
is the pressure.
It is worth noticing that in eq. (
3.5
), we identify with
\pi_{ij}\equiv\pi_{ij}(\vec{x},t)
all the first-order anisotropic contributions to the energy-momentum tensor. For a perfect fluid
\pi_{ij}=0
.

Using eqs. (
3.1
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
), we can perturb the field equations (
2.4
), (
2.12
) and (

\mathcal{J}^{(0)}+\mathcal{J}^{(1)}=0\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}^{(0)}+\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}^{(1)}=0\qquad\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{(0)}+\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{(1)}=0\,.

(3.6)

In this article we work in the so-called transverse traceless (TT) gauge, considering only tensor perturbations such that

h^{i}\!\,_{i}=0\qquad\partial^{i}h_{ij}=0\qquad\pi^{i}\!\,_{i}=0\qquad\partial^{i}\pi_{ij}=0\,,

(3.7)

with
h^{i}\!\,_{i}=\delta^{ij}h_{ij}
and
\pi^{i}\!\,_{i}=\delta^{ij}\pi_{ij}

By combining each perturbative order of eqs. (
3.2

3.5

\mathcal{J}^{(0)}=0\qquad\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{00}=0\qquad\mathcal{H}^{(0)}_{ij}=0\qquad\mathcal{T}^{(0)}_{0}=0\,,

(3.8)

and the non-trivial first-order TT equation

\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{ij}=0\,.

(3.9)

\chi^{\prime\prime}(u)+\left[2\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a(u)}+\kappa(u)\right]\chi^{\prime}(u)+\chi(u)=-64\pi\mathcal{G}(u)\frac{a^{2}(u)}{k^{2}}\rho_{\nu}(u)\int_{0}^{u}\mathcal{K}\big[U-u\big]\,\chi^{\prime}(U)\,dU\,,

(4.2)

with
\chi(0)=\chi^{\prime}(0)=0
. For simplification purposes, from now on we use primes to indicate derivatives with respect to arguments we are focusing on.
As eq. (
4.2
) is balanced by
\rho_{\nu}
, it is useful to introduce the neutrino distribution density
f_{\nu}(u)\equiv\rho_{\nu}(u)/\rho(u)
, which inserted into the modified Fridmann eq. (
3.11
) yields

\rho_{\nu}(u)=\frac{3\,k^{2}}{8\pi\,a^{2}(u)\,\mathcal{G}(u)}f_{\nu}(u)\Upsilon_{k}(u)\,,

(4.3)

with

\Upsilon_{k}(u)\equiv\lambda_{1}(u)\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{k}+\lambda_{2}(u)\frac{a^{2}(u)}{k^{2}}+\frac{a^{\prime\,2}(u)}{a^{2}(u)}\,.

(4.4)

From eqs. (
4.3
) and (
4.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime\prime}(u)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[2\frac{a^{\prime}(u)}{a(u)}+\kappa(u)\right]\chi^{\prime}(u)+\chi(u)=-24f_{\nu}(u)\Upsilon_{k}(u)\int_{0}^{u}\mathcal{K}\big[U-u\big]\,\chi^{\prime}(U)\,dU\,,

(4.5)

where the dependence on the running gravitational constant
\mathcal{G}
disappears.
The GR limit of our eq. (
4.5
) has been used by the authors of refs.
[
46
,
47
]
to study the damping of GWs whose wavelengths are short enough to re-enter the cosmological horizon during the radiation dominated era.

To move on and deal with GWs that enter the cosmological horizon during the matter dominated era, let us introduce the new scalar variable

y(u)\equiv\frac{a(u)}{a_{eq}}\,,

(4.6)

where we have identified
a_{eq}\equiv a(t_{eq})
with
t_{eq}
as the matter-radiation equivalence time such that
\rho_{m}(t_{eq})=\rho_{\gamma}(t_{eq})+\rho_{\nu}(t_{eq})
.
The explicit mathematical relation between
y
and the
u
variable can be obtained by solving the modified Fridmann eq. (
3.11
), which appropriately worked, can be expressed as follows

y^{\prime\prime}(u)^{2}+\mathcal{A}_{k}\,\lambda_{1}(u)\,y^{2}(u)\,y^{\prime}(u)+\mathcal{A}_{k}^{2}\,\lambda_{2}(u)\,y^{4}(u)=\frac{y(u)+1}{\mathcal{Q}^{2}_{k}}\,.

(4.7)

The time-independent coefficients
\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{2}_{k}
are

\mathcal{A}_{k}\equiv\frac{a_{0}}{k(1+z_{eq})}\qquad\mathcal{Q}^{2}_{k}\equiv\frac{k^{2}\,\Omega_{0}}{a_{0}^{2}\,H_{0}^{2}\,\Pi_{0}\,\Omega_{0m}\left(1+z_{eq}\right)}\,,

(4.8)

with
z_{eq}\equiv z(t_{eq})
the matter-radiation equivalence redshift such that
1+z_{eq}=a_{0}/a_{eq}
.

After some algebraic manipulations, eq. (
4.7
) can be simplified to read

u^{\prime}_{\pm}(y)=\frac{\mathcal{Q}_{k}}{\sqrt{y+1}}\left[-\mathcal{Z}\frac{\lambda_{1}(y)\,y^{2}}{\sqrt{y+1}}\pm\sqrt{1+\mathcal{Z}^{2}\frac{\big[\lambda_{1}^{2}(y)-4\lambda_{2}(y)\big]\,y^{4}}{y+1}}\,\right]^{-1}\,,

(4.9)

with

\mathcal{Z}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{k}\mathcal{Q}_{k}

(4.10)

which, as shown in the above definition (
4.8
), remains constant over time and is mostly scale-invariant as k-independent.

Being the solution (
4.9
) formally described by two branches, we can focus on the positive sign
du_{+}/dy
that is consistent with the sign of the GR solution, as obtained in ref.
[
46
]
.
Finally, eq. (
4.5
) becomes

(y+1)\chi^{\prime\prime}(y)+\frac{\delta_{1}(4+5y)}{2y}\chi^{\prime}(y)+\delta_{2}\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{2}\,\chi(y)=-24\frac{\delta_{3}f_{0,\nu}}{y^{2}}\int_{0}^{y}\mathcal{K}\big[U(Y)-u(y)\big]\chi^{\prime}(Y)\,dY\!,

(4.11)

with
\chi(y)=\chi[u(y)]
the amplitude. The initial conditions are
\chi(0)=0
and
\chi^{\prime}(0)=0
. In eq. (
4.11
), we point out the presence of the three rH parameters
\delta_{j}=\delta_{j}(y)
, whose explicit expression is

\displaystyle\delta_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Big\{\mathcal{Z}^{2}y^{4}(3y+4)\big[4\lambda_{2}(y)-\lambda_{1}^{2}(y)\big]+2y(y+1)\big\{-\mathcal{Q}_{k}\kappa(y)\,\sigma(y)+\mathcal{Z}^{2}y^{4}\big[2\lambda_{2}(y)

(4.12)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}(y)\lambda_{1}^{\prime}(y)\big]+\mathcal{Z}y\,\sigma(y)\big[y\lambda_{1}^{\prime}(y)+4\lambda_{1}(y)\big]\big\}-(5y+4)\,\sigma^{2}(y)\Big\}

\displaystyle\Big\{\sigma(y)\,(5y+4)\big[\mathcal{Z}y^{2}\lambda_{1}(y)-\sigma(y)\big]\Big\}^{-1}\!,

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(y+1)\Big\{\mathcal{Z}y^{2}\lambda_{1}(y)-\sigma(y)\Big\}^{-2}\!,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\delta_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\Big\{4\mathcal{Z}^{2}\,y^{4}\lambda_{2}(y)-2\mathcal{Z}\,y^{2}\big[\mathcal{Z}y^{2}\lambda_{1}(y)-\sigma(y)\big]\lambda_{1}(y)+\big[\mathcal{Z}y^{2}\lambda_{1}(y)-\sigma(y)\big]^{2}\Big\}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\Big\{\mathcal{Z}y^{2}\lambda_{1}(y)-\sigma(y)\Big\}^{-2}\!,

with

\sigma(y)\equiv\sqrt{y+1-\mathcal{Z}^{2}\,y^{4}\big[4\lambda_{2}(y)-\lambda_{1}^{2}(y)\big]}\,.

(4.15)

The GR limit (
2.3
) of eqs. (
4.12
), (
4.13
), and (
4.14
) leads to
\delta_{j}=1
, and hence, we can exactly recover the Einstein-Boltzmann equation obtained by ref.
[
46
]
.
When compared to such limit, eq. (
4.11
) exhibits important features that are worth examining.

First, the rH framework presents in eq. (
4.11
) the same integro-differential structure, with respect to the amplitude
\chi
, of the analogous result obtained for GR.
Therefore, one now needs facing the presence of the
\delta_{j}
parameters, which make the attempt to computationally solve eq. (
4.11
), without loss of generality, problematic.
Indeed, up to now, such parameters are a priori completely free as their structure is related to the rH free functions
G_{i}
through
\kappa
and
\lambda_{i}
.

Second, though for GR the damping effects are unimportant during the matter dominated era as
\delta_{3}=1
and
f_{\nu}\sim f_{0,\nu}/y\rightarrow 0
, our general result shows that
\delta_{3}
can suppress such decay, giving rise to non-vanishing effects in such era.
One can consider this behavior as an important phenomenological signature that distinguishes between the GR action and modified gravity theories.

Finally, we observe that, in contrast to GR, eq. (
4.11
) relates
\chi
and
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
by explicitly involving cosmological parameters as the rH framework introduces the additional cosmological coefficient
\mathcal{Z}
.

In the next section, we briefly analyze the connection between the damping effects described by eq. (
4.11
) and the various correlation functions that parameterize GWs signatures on CMB in the context of the rH framework. In this regard, according to what was discussed in refs.
[
46
,
47

, we deem relevant to asses which CMB multipole orders
\ell_{k}
are mainly dominated by such damping effects.

4.1
Multipole order effects

In this section we provide an analytic formula for
\ell_{k}
, the CMB multipole order mainly involved through the tensor damping effects of eq. (
4.11
) during the matter-radiation last-scattering epoch
t_{ls}
.

Starting from eq. (
3.19
) and as directly resulting from its evaluation at the equivalence redshift
z_{eq}
, it follows that

H^{2}(z)+\lambda_{1}(z)H(z)+\lambda_{2}(z)=\frac{H^{2}_{eq}\Pi_{eq}}{E^{2}_{eq}}E^{2}(z)\,.

(4.16)

We point out that
E^{2}_{eq}
can be rewritten in a more direct fashion than the trivial evaluation of eq. (
3.20
) at
t_{eq}
. The easier way to proceed is by applying the mathematical relation
\omega_{eq,m}=\omega_{eq,\gamma}+\omega_{eq,\nu}
, to get

E^{2}_{eq}=2\big(1+z_{eq}\big)^{3}\frac{\Omega_{0m}}{\Omega_{0}}\,.

(4.17)

To now derive an explicit expression for
H
, one needs solving eq. (
4.16
) for
H
. The result is

H_{\pm}(z)=\sqrt{\Pi_{eq}}\,\frac{H_{eq}}{E_{eq}}\,\Sigma_{\pm}(z)\,,

(4.18)

with

\Sigma_{\pm}(z)\equiv-\frac{E_{eq}}{2\sqrt{\Pi_{eq}}H_{eq}}\,\lambda_{1}(z)\pm\sqrt{E^{2}(z)+\frac{E_{eq}^{2}}{4H_{eq}^{2}}\Big[\lambda_{1}^{2}(z)-4\lambda_{2}(z)\Big]}\,.

(4.19)

Without loss of generality, we consider the positive branch
H_{+}
as consistent with the sign of the GR solution obtained in ref.
[
46
]
.

Subsequently, we evaluate expression (
4.16
) at
t_{0}
, and as
E^{2}_{0}=1
, and obtain

\Pi_{0}=\frac{1}{E_{eq}^{2}}\left(\frac{H_{eq}}{H_{0}}\right)^{2}\Pi_{eq}\,.

(4.20)

Hence, by computing eq. (
4.20
) inside the definition of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
proposed in eq. (
4.8
), we obtain

\frac{k}{k_{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{\Pi_{eq}}{2}}\,\mathcal{Q}_{k}\,,

(4.21)

where we identify
k_{eq}\equiv a_{eq}H_{eq}
.

The main multipole order
\ell_{k}
that receives a dominant contribution at the last-scattering epoch
t_{ls}
can be defined as

\ell_{k}\equiv\frac{d_{A,ls}}{a_{ls}}\,k\,,

(4.22)

with
a_{ls}\equiv a(t_{ls})
and
d_{A,ls}=d_{A}(z_{ls})
. In our notation,
d_{A}(z)
corresponds to the so-called angular distance that, as function of the redshift, is commonly defined as
[
15
]

d_{A}(z)\equiv\frac{1}{1+z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{d\bar{z}}{H(\bar{z})}\,.

(4.23)

For the matter-radiation equivalence epoch,
\ell_{eq}\equiv\ell_{k_{eq}}
, eqs. (
4.21
) and (
4.22
) give

\ell_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{\Pi_{eq}}{2}}\,\mathcal{Q}_{k}\,\ell_{eq}\,.

(4.24)

Therefore, from eqs. (
4.18
) and (
4.23
), one may write a general expression for
\ell_{eq}
as

\ell_{eq}=\frac{E_{eq}}{\sqrt{\Pi_{eq}}\big(1+z_{eq}\big)}\int_{0}^{z_{ls}}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\Sigma_{+}(\bar{z})}\,,

(4.25)

which, computed inside eq. (
4.23
), finally leads to

\ell_{k}=\vartheta\,\mathcal{Q}_{k}\,.

(4.26)

In this result, we introduce the constant factor

\vartheta\equiv\sqrt{2}\,\frac{H_{eq}\,E_{eq}}{1+z_{eq}}\int_{0}^{z_{ls}}d\bar{z}\left\{-\frac{E_{eq}}{\sqrt{\Pi_{eq}}}\,\lambda_{1}(\bar{z})+\sqrt{4\,H_{eq}^{2}\,E^{2}(\bar{z})+E_{eq}^{2}\big[\lambda_{1}^{2}(\bar{z})-4\lambda_{2}(\bar{z})\big]}\right\}^{-1}\!,

(4.27)

with
E_{eq}
defined in eq. (
4.17
) and
H_{eq}
as well as
\Pi_{eq}
deductible from eqs. (
3.19
) and (
3.21
), respectively, evaluated at
t_{eq}
.

Against this framework, it is clear that
\vartheta
can be seen as a conversion factor from
\ell_{k}
to
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
, which depends on cosmological parameters. The benefit of using such result lies on a direct mathematical relation between the solutions of eq. (
4.11
) and the various values of
\ell_{k}
on which they depend through
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
. Interestingly, as in the case of the GR result of ref
[
46
]
, the rH framework preserves a linear dependence of
\ell_{k}
on only
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
. In (
4.27
) the
\mathcal{Z}
parameter does not provide any contribution.

5
Discussion and Conclusions

In this paper we studied the propagation of cosmological GWs interacting with free-streaming neutrinos within the framework of rH theories of gravity. Firstly, we deduced the covariant scalar-tensor field equations of such theories; subsequently to ref.
[
46
]
, we analyzed the propagation of first-order tensor perturbations embedded in a spatially flat FLRW cosmology. We also assumed the presence of first-order TT terms in the anisotropic stress energy-momentum tensor sourced by free-streaming neutrinos.

Our analysis shows that the rH framework propagates damped GWs through the same integro-differential equation of GR but weighted by three new parameters
\delta_{1,2,3}
. We demostrate that the occurrence of
\delta_{3}
can give rise to non-vanishing free-streaming damping effects during the cosmological matter dominated era.
This behavior could be an important phenomenological signature that is able to highlight the action of modified gravity theories beyond GR.
Within the context of rH theories, we identify an analytic formula for the multipole orders
\ell_{k}
with which free-streaming neutrinos damp the variety of tensor correlation functions of the CMB.

As future generations of gravitational detectors such as LISA
[
42
]
and DECIGO
[
43
]
could get closer to the experimental sensibility to detect cosmological GWs, we deem it of fundamental importance that the combined impact of the two types of GWs modification studied in this work, i.e. the modification of GR theory and the streaming of free cosmological neutrinos, be further analyzed and examined to allow for an effective understanding of their data.

1.
Introduction and main result

Chemotaxis is a phenomenon in which bacteria or, in general, uni- or multi-cellular organisms, direct their movement according to the presence of certain chemicals in the environment, that usually is impenetrable. In
[
7
]
we can find one of the first tests to write a model for this phenomenon, which, in the general case, is described by two coupled PDEs with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\nabla\cdot\left(A(u,v)\nabla u-B(u,v)\nabla v\right)+h(u)&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
\tau v_{t}=\Delta v+E(u,v),&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
u_{\nu}=v_{\nu}=0,&\text{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;\tau v(x,0)=\tau v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded and smooth domain and
T_{\textup{max}}\in(0,\infty])
is the maximum existence time of the solution. The first equation gives us information on how the distribution of the biological material changes over time, conditioned by the natural diffusion with law
A(u,v)
, by the signal sensitivity
B(u,v)
, and a source
h(u)
. The second equation, that can be elliptic or parabolic for
\tau=0
or
\tau=1
respectively, describes the evolution of the chemical signal which affects the biological material: in particular, this chemical can be produced by the cells or, like the case we want to study, it can be consumed, with the law
E(u,v)
. Our specific case deals with linear diffusion
A(u,v)=1
, a sensitivity
B(u,v)=\chi u
and the logistic source
h(u)=\lambda u-\mu u^{2}-c\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{\gamma}
. For the second equation, since we are studying a consumption model,
E(u,v)=-uv
, different from the production model that is written
E(u,v)=-v+f(u)
, where
f(u)
is a positive function. The two main concerns with this type of problem is to find conditions that ensure the global boundedness of the solution or the so called
blow-up
, an uncontrolled aggregation of the biological material. For the linear production case, the reader can see for instance
[
13
]
, researchers found a condition which involves the mass
m=\int_{\Omega}u
and the chemosensitivity coefficient
\chi
. Moreover, the blow-up situation is very much studied in the signal-production version and the literature is full of these results. On the contrary, for the consumption model, there is nothing on that.

1.1.
The consumption model

In our case, except when
n\in\{1,2\}
where the solutions are always bounded, results concerning global boundedness involve both the chemosensitivity
\chi
and the maximum of the initial condition
\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. The first of this results is contained in
[
12
]
, in which the author found
\chi\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\frac{1}{6(n+1)}
for
n\geq 3
. This has been, later, improved in
[
1
]
by
\chi\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2(n+1)}}
. By adding a dampening logistic term, one can find conditions even if it is not verified what we saw previously. One example is given by
[
8
]
, where

u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot u\nabla v+\lambda u-\mu u^{2},\quad\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},

and the solution is globally bounded if
\mu
is larger than a quantity which increases with
\chi\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Another similar result, where
\mu
has to be large enough, can be found in
[
10
]
, where the authors consider both nonlinear diffusion and sensitivity.

1.2.
The logistic in the form of
\boldsymbol{h(u,\nabla u)}

According to
[
11
]
, the evolution of a certain species in an habitat is influenced, not only by births and natural deaths, also by the accidental deaths, which can be described with the term
-\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{\gamma}
. In the literature, we can mention here
[
2
,
3
,
6
]
, there are some results in which gradient nonlinearities are studied in order to ensure or prevent blow-up formations for a semilinear heat equation with Dirichlet conditions. Moreover, in
[
5
]
the authors studied a production chemotaxis problem with a gradient dependent logistic and they achieved, for the boundedness of the solution, a condition on the parameter
\gamma
.

1.3.
Main result

The idea is to consider a consumption model with a dampening logistic that depends also on
\nabla u
:

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot u\nabla v+\lambda u-\mu u^{2}-c\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{\gamma},&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
v_{t}=\Delta v-uv,&\text{in $\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
u_{\nu}=v_{\nu}=0,&\text{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain of class
C^{2+\delta}
, with
\delta\in(0,1)
and the initial conditions are such that

(2)

u_{0},v_{0}\colon\bar{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+},\text{ with }u_{0},v_{0}\in C_{\nu}^{2+\delta}(\bar{\Omega})=\{\psi\in C^{2+\delta}(\bar{\Omega}):\psi_{\nu}=0\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

For
p>1
and
\eta>0
, we define the functions

(3)

K_{1}(p,n)\coloneqq\frac{p}{p+1}\left(\frac{8(4p^{2}+n)}{p(p+1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}},

and

(4)

K_{2}(p,n,\eta)\coloneqq\frac{2p^{\frac{p+1}{2}}(p+n+\eta-1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}{p+1}\left(\frac{8(4p^{2}+n)(p-1)}{p(p+1)}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

Moreover, let
M\coloneqq\max{\left\{\int_{\Omega}u_{0},\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\lvert\Omega\rvert\right\}}
. We enunciate now the main result of this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
be a bounded domain of class
C^{2+\delta}
, with
\delta\in(0,1)
,
u_{0},v_{0}
as in (
2
),
\lambda,\mu,c,\chi>0
. There exists
\mathcal{C}>0
such that whenever

(
\mathcal{A}_{1}
)

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{n+1}<\gamma\leq 2
;

or

(
\mathcal{A}_{2}
)

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{2n}{2+1}
and
\displaystyle c\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{2n}{n^{2}+3n-2}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\mathcal{C}M^{-\frac{2}{n+1}}+\mu\frac{n}{2}>K_{1}\left(\frac{n}{2},n\right)\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{n}}\chi^{\frac{2}{n}(n+2)}+K_{2}\left(\frac{n}{2},n,0\right)\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}
,

problem (
1
) admits a unique and uniformly bounded classical solution.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us dedicate to the case
\gamma=\frac{2n}{n+1}
and rewrite now the inequality in condition
(

A
2

)
as

(5)

c\mathcal{F}M^{-\frac{2}{n+1}}+\frac{n}{2}\mu>\mathcal{K},

where

\mathcal{F}=\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{2n}{n^{2}+3n-2}\right)^{\frac{2n}{n+1}}\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{K}=K_{1}\left(\frac{n}{2},n\right)\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{n}}\chi^{\frac{2}{n}(n+2)}+K_{2}\left(\frac{n}{2},n,0\right)\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}.

We can observe what follows:

(
\mathcal{B}_{1}
)

Fix
c=0
. We can write (
5
) as
\mu=\bar{\mu}>\frac{2}{n}\mathcal{K}
, and this is another way to see the result contained in
[
8
]
, where the authors use
p=n
, so the condition for the boundedness contained in that work is a special case of this one.

(
\mathcal{B}_{2}
)

In the case
\mu<\frac{2}{n}\mathcal{K}
and
c>0
, we have two different possibilities:

\blacktriangleright

if
M=\int_{\Omega}u_{0}
, we obtain

c\mathcal{F}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n+1}}+\frac{n}{2}\mu>\mathcal{K},\quad\text{that leads to}\quad\int_{\Omega}u_{0}<\left(\frac{c\mathcal{F}}{\mathcal{K}-\frac{n}{2}\mu}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}},

so we have a condition on the initial mass;

\blacktriangleright

if
M=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\lvert\Omega\rvert
we have

(6)

\mathcal{E}\mu^{\frac{2}{n+1}}+\frac{n}{2}\mu>\mathcal{K}\quad\text{where}\quad\mathcal{E}=\frac{c\mathcal{F}}{(\lambda\lvert\Omega\rvert)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}},

and so there exists
\mu<\bar{\mu}
such that (
6
) is satisfied.

(
\mathcal{B}_{3}
)

In the situation where
\mu=\frac{2}{n}\mathcal{K}
and
c>0
, relation (
5
) is simply true.

3.
Local existence and extension criterion

The proofs of the next three results are an adaption to the consumption case of the works
[
5
,
9
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}
satisfy (
2
),
\lambda,\mu,c,\chi>0
,
\gamma\geq 1
. Then there exists
T>0
and a unique solution
(u,v)
of system (
1
) in
\Omega\times(0,T)
such that

(u,v)\in\left(C^{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2}}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T])\right)^{2}.

Proof.

The main idea is articulated as follows (for the details see
[
9
]
). First, for any
R>0
and for some
0<T\leq 1
, we define the closed convex set

S_{T}=\{0\leq u\in C^{1,\frac{\delta}{2}}(\bar{\Omega}\times[0,T]):\|u(\cdot,t)-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq R,\,\;\text{for\ all}\ t\in[0,T]\},

and we choose one element
\tilde{u}
from it. From the second equation, we find
v
with a certain regularity that allows us to find the unique solution
u
to the first equation. In particular,
u\in S_{T}
and this implies that the compact operator
\Phi(\tilde{u})=u
is such that
\Phi(S_{T})\subset S_{T}
. So, by the Schauder’s fixed point theorem, let
u
be the fixed point of
\Phi
. Now, by taking
T
as the initial time and
u(\cdot,T)
as the initial condition, we can find a solution
(\hat{u},\hat{v})
defined in
\Omega\times[T,\hat{T}]
for some
\hat{T}>0
, that is the prolongation of
(u,v)
. We can repeat this procedure in order to obtain a maximum interval time of existence
[0,T_{\textup{max}})
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}
be as in (
2
),
\lambda,\mu,c,\chi>0
and
\gamma\geq 1
. There exists
T_{\textup{max}}\in(0,\infty]
such that
u,v>0
in
\Omega\times[0,T_{\textup{max}})
, and

(9)

\text{if }T_{\textup{max}}<\infty,\text{ then }\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{\textup{max}}}{\left(\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{C^{2+\delta}(\bar{\Omega})}+\left\lVert v(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{C^{2+\delta}(\bar{\Omega})}\right)}=\infty.

As the reader may noticed, the fact that
T_{\textup{max}}
is finite, does not ensure the explosion in the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
-norm. In order to have this, we need
\gamma\leq 2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}
be as in (
2
),
\lambda,\mu,c,\chi>0
and
\gamma\in[1,2]
. Then, if
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T_{\textup{max}}),L^{\infty}(\Omega))
, then
T_{\textup{max}}=\infty
.

Proof.

For the details we refer to
[
9
]
. The main idea is to proceed by contradiction: if
T_{\textup{max}}
is finite, the boundedness of
u
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
implies, thanks to
\gamma\in[1,2]
, the uniform-in-time boundedness of
\left\lVert u(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{C^{2+\delta}(\bar{\Omega})}+\left\lVert v(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{C^{2+\delta}(\bar{\Omega})}
so, from (
9
), we have an inconsistency.
∎

From now on,
(u,v)
will be the unique solution of problem (
1
) defined on
(0,T_{\textup{max}})
and every
c_{i}
is a positive constant.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}
be as in (
2
),
\lambda,\mu,c,\chi>0
and
\gamma\geq 1
. Then

(10)

\int_{\Omega}u\leq M\coloneqq\max{\left\{\int_{\Omega}u_{0},\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\lvert\Omega\rvert\right\}}\quad\text{on $(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},

(11)

\lVert v(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert v_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all $t\in(0,T_{\textup{max}})$}.

Proof.

By integrating the first equation and using the Neumann boundary conditions, we have

(12)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}u-\mu\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\quad\text{for all $t\in(0,T_{\textup{max}})$}.

Moreover, an application of Hölder’s inequality leads to

\int_{\Omega}u\leq\lvert\Omega\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{and, consequently, to}\quad-\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}\left(\int_{\Omega}u\right)^{2}\quad\text{for every $t\in(0,T_{\textup{max}})$}.

By inserting the last one into (
12
) and calling
m=\int_{\Omega}u
, we obtain this initial problem

\begin{cases}m^{\prime}\leq\lambda m-\frac{\mu}{\lvert\Omega\rvert}m^{2}&\text{on $(0,T_{\textup{max}})$},\\
m(0)=\int_{\Omega}u_{0},\end{cases}

which ensures that
m\leq\max{\left\{\int_{\Omega}u_{0},\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\lvert\Omega\rvert\right\}}
for all
t\in(0,T_{\textup{max}})
. Moreover, (
11
) is a consequence of the parabolic maximum principle because

\max_{\Omega\times(0,T_{\textup{max}})}v(x,t)\leq\max_{\bar{\Omega}}v(x,0).

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose there exists
\tau\in(0,\min{\{1,T_{\textup{max}}\}})
and
p\in[1,n]
such that
\lVert u(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq c\textsubscript{1}
for all
t\in(\tau,T_{\textup{max}})
. Then for every
\delta\in(0,T_{\textup{max}}-\tau)
and for all
q<\frac{np}{n-p}
there exists
c\textsubscript{2}
such that

\lVert\nabla v(\cdot,t)\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq c\textsubscript{2}\quad\text{for every $t\in(\tau+\delta,T_{\textup{max}})$}.

Proof.

As in
[
4
, Section 2]
, we define

A_{q}w\coloneqq-\Delta w\quad\text{where}\quad w\in D(A_{q})=\left\{\psi\in W^{2,q}(\Omega)\text{ : }\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\}.

It is known that:

(13)

D\left((A_{q}+1)^{\beta}\right)\xhookrightarrow{}W^{1,q}(\Omega)\quad\text{if }\beta>\frac{1}{2},

and, for every
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
and
w\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, there exists a positive constant
\hat{c}
such that

(14)

\left\lVert(A_{q}+1)^{\beta}e^{-tA_{q}}w\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\hat{c}t^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}e^{(1-\mu)t}\lVert w\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Let
q<\frac{np}{n-p}
and
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
such that
\beta+\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)<1
. Now we write the second equation as
v_{t}=-(A_{q}+1)v+v(1-u)
and we use the representation formula to affirm that

v(\cdot,t)=e^{-(t-\tau)}v(\cdot,\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{-(t-s)(A_{q}+1)}v(\cdot,s)(1-u(\cdot,s))\,ds\quad\text{for all $t\in[\tau,T_{\textup{max}})$}.

By applying
(A_{q}+1)^{\beta}
to both sides and using (
14
) and (
11
) we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert(A_{q}+1)^{\beta}v(\cdot,t)\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq&c\textsubscript{3}(t-\tau)^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\lVert v(\cdot,\tau)\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+c\textsubscript{4}\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\left\lVert v(\cdot,s)(1-u(\cdot,s))\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,ds\\
\leq&c\textsubscript{3}\delta^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)}\lVert v(\cdot,0)\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+c\textsubscript{4}\lVert v(\cdot,0)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}e^{-\mu(t-s)}\left(\lvert\Omega\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left\lVert u(\cdot,s)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right)\,ds\\
\leq&c\textsubscript{5}+c\textsubscript{6}\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{-\beta-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}e^{-\mu z}\,dz\leq c\textsubscript{7}\quad\text{on $(0,T_{\textup{max}})$}.\end{split}

Finally, (
13
) gives the claim.
∎

The next Lemma transforms the boundedness of
u
on some
L^{p}
-norm in the boundedness on the
L^{\infty}
-norm.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
T_{\textup{max}}\in(0,\infty]
,
\frac{n}{2}<p<n
,
\lambda,\mu,c,\chi>0
and
\gamma\in[1,2]
. If
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T_{\textup{max}}),L^{p}(\Omega))
, then
u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty),L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.

Proof.

We write
u(\cdot,t)
using the variation-of-constants formula for all
t\in(0,T_{\textup{max}})
and
t_{0}=\max{\{0,t-1\}}

(15)

u(\cdot,t)=e^{(t-t_{0})\Delta}u(\cdot,t_{0})-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\cdot\left(u(\cdot,s)\nabla v(\cdot,s)\right)\,ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(\lambda u(\cdot,s)-\mu u^{2}(\cdot,s)-c\lvert\nabla u(\cdot,s)\rvert^{\gamma}\right)\,ds.

Focusing on the logistic term, we can say that

\lambda u-\mu u^{2}-c\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{\gamma}\leq\lambda u-\mu u^{2}\leq\sup_{\xi>0}{(\lambda\xi-\mu\xi^{2})}\coloneqq c\textsubscript{8},

and, in conjunction with the positivity of the heat semigroup, we have

(16)

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\left(\lambda u(\cdot,s)-\mu u^{2}(\cdot,s)-c\lvert\nabla u(\cdot,s)\rvert^{\gamma}\right)\,ds\leq c\textsubscript{8}(t-t_{0})\leq c\textsubscript{8}.

In addition, thanks to
[
13
, Lemma 1.3]
we know that there exist positive constants
C_{f}
and
\hat{C}_{f}
such that, for all
t>0
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)

(17)

\left\lVert e^{t\Delta}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{f}\left(1+t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\right)\lVert f\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

and for all
t>0
,
r>1
and
f\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
with
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega

(18)

\left\lVert e^{t\Delta}\nabla\cdot\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\hat{C}_{f}\left(1+t^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2r}}\right)e^{-\lambda_{1}t}\left\lVert\nabla f\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\Omega)}

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first nonzero eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
under Neumann boundary conditions. Consequently, if
t\leq 1
, which is equivalent to
t_{0}=0
, we have for the parabolic maximum principle

(19)

\left\lVert e^{(t-t_{0})\Delta}u(\cdot,t_{0})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Instead, if
t>1
, that implies
t-t_{0}=1
, we can use (
17
) with

From Cherednik algebras to torus knot homology via cuspidal D D -modules
Xinchun Ma

Abstract
. We prove a rational generalization of Haiman’s master formula, conjectured by Gorsky and Neguţ: the bigraded Frobenius character of the associated graded of the finite-dimensional representation L m / n \mathrm{L}_{m/n} of the rational Cherednik algebra at slope m / n m/n can be expressed using the generator of the same slope in the elliptic Hall algebra acting on its polynomial representation. The filtration on L m / n \mathrm{L}_{m/n} is induced by the Hodge filtration on the cuspidal character D D -module. As a corollary, we relate the triply-graded Khovanov-Rozansky homology of the torus knot to the sum of hook multiplicity spaces of L m / n \mathrm{L}_{m/n} , proving a conjecture of Gorsky, Oblomkov, Rasmussen and Shende. We further provide a realization of the torus knot homology in terms of explicit coherent sheaves on Hilbert schemes of points in the plane.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The master formula and Hilbert schemes

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on the polynomial ring
R:=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n};y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
by permuting the two sets of variables simultaneously. Let
(R^{S_{n}}_{+})
be the ideal generated by invariant polynomials whose constant terms are
0
. The diagonal coinvariant ring is the quotient
\mathrm{DH}_{n}=R/(R^{S_{n}}_{+}).

With respect to the
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
scaling action on
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
, one may consider the bigraded Frobenius character of
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
. Let
e_{n}
denote the
n
-th elementary symmetric function and
\nabla
the nabla operator
[
BG98
]
on the ring of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
with Macdonald polynomials as eigenvectors. The master formula
[
Hai02
]
states that the bigraded Frobenius character of
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
equals

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{S_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{DH}_{n})=\nabla e_{n}.

Upon specialization, one deduces that
\dim(\mathrm{DH}_{n})=(n+1)^{n-1}
and
\dim(\mathrm{DH}_{n}^{\mathrm{sgn}})=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
, which were open conjectures prior to the landing of the more general formula (
1
).

Haiman’s proof engineers the geometry of the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the plane, which we denote by
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}
. In particular, he shows the existence of the Procesi bundle, a rank
n!
vector bundle over the Hilbert scheme
[
Hai01
]
and that the space of global sections of the Procesi bundle over the punctual Hilbert scheme coincides with
\mathrm{DH}_{n}
.

1.2.
Simple modules of rational Cherednik algebras

The number
(n+1)^{n-1}
, resp.
\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}
, is a special case of a family of integers
m^{n-1}
, resp.
\frac{1}{m+n}\binom{m+n}{n}
, for
m
positive and coprime to
n
, which enumerates various combinatorial objects such as rational parking functions, resp. rational Dyck paths.

For
n>0
, we let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
denote the
(n-1)
-dimensional standard representation of
S_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{n})
the ring of differential operators on
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
. Introduced in
[
EG02
]
, the rational Cherednik algebra
\mathrm{H}_{c}(S_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{n})
is a deformation of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\rtimes S_{n}
at a complex parameter
c
. It turns out that only when
c=m/n
for coprime
m,n
does
\mathrm{H}_{c}(S_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{n})
have finite-dimensional representations. In that case, there is a unique irreducible one
[
BEG03
]
, which we denote by
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}
. As shown in
loc. cit.
, the dimension of
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}
,
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}^{S_{n}}
, is
m^{n-1}
, resp.
\frac{1}{m+n}\binom{m+n}{n}
.

1.3.
The elliptic Hall algebra and a rational master formula

The Hall algebra of the category of coherent sheaves on an elliptic curve has a geometric action on the direct sum of
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant K-theory of
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})

[
FT11
,
SV13
]
over all
n\geq 0
.
For
(m,n)
as above, there is a distinguished element
Q_{m,n}
in the elliptic Hall algebra.
On the other hand, localized at equivariant parameters, Bridgeland-King-Reid equivalence
[
BKR01
]
implies an isomorphism

(2)

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}K^{\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n})_{\mathrm{loc}}\cong\Lambda,

where
\Lambda
denotes the ring of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
.
Therefore, the action of
Q_{m,n}
on
1\in\Lambda
results in a symmetric function.

The main result of the paper is the following character formula, which proves
[
GN15
, Conjecture 5.5]
of Gorsky and Neguţ. Recall that there is a grading on
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}
induced by the Euler element
\mathrm{h}_{m/n}
in
\mathrm{H}_{m/n}
, leading to a decomposition
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}=\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{L}_{m/n}(\ell)
. Moreover, we will define a Hodge filtration
F
on
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}
.

Theorem A
.

(= Theorem
4.6
)
For positive integer
m
coprime to
n
, the bigraded Frobenius character of
\mathrm{gr}^{F}\mathrm{L}_{m/n}
with respect to the
\mathrm{h}_{m/n}
and the filtration gradings (parametrized by
q
and
t
respectively) satisfies

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{S_{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathrm{gr}^{F}(\mathrm{L}_{m/n})\otimes\mathrm{sgn})=(Q_{m,n}\cdot 1)(z;q,q^{-1}t).

When
m=n+1
,
\mathrm{gr}^{F}(\mathrm{L}_{(n+1)/n})\otimes\mathrm{sgn}\cong\mathrm{DH}_{n}
(cf.
[
Gor03
]
) and
Q_{n+1,n}\cdot 1=\nabla e_{n}
. Hence (
3
) generalizes (
1
).
Our proof focuses on computing the left hand side of (
3
). The result is then matched with the right hand side studied using shuffle algebra techniques in
[
Neg14
]
,
[
Neg22
]
,
[
BHM+23
]
and many others.

1.4.
Cuspidal
D
-modules and Hodge filtrations

For every integer pair
(m,n)
as above, there is a
D
-module
\mathbf{N}_{m/n}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
supported on the nilpotent cone, known as the cuspidal character
D
-module in the work of Lusztig
[
Lus86
]
. As shown by Calaque, Enriquez and Etingof
[
CEE09
]
, the simple module
\mathrm{L}_{n/m}
of type
A_{m-1}
can be realized as the multiplicity space of
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes m}
in
\mathbf{N}_{m/n}
as a
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
-module. The
D
-module
\mathbf{N}_{m/n}
naturally has the structure of a Hodge module, according to Saito’s theory
[
Sai90
]
. In this way, we define a Hodge filtration on
\mathrm{L}_{n/m}
.

We may take minimal extension of the integrable connection to the Springer resolution, which is across a simple normal crossing divisor. In particular, the Hodge filtration can be explicitly described in terms of Deligne’s canonical lattice. Using Laumon’s formula on associated graded of a direct image of a filtered
D
-module, we write the Hodge associated graded of
\mathbf{N}_{m/n}
as the pushforward of a DG module, supported on what we call a cuspidal DG commuting variety. This description allows us to compute the left hand side of (
3
) with
m
and
n
flipped.

Such a computation of the Hodge associated graded of
\mathbf{N}_{m/n}
closely resembles that of the Harish-Chandra
D
-module in
[
Gin12
]
, through which the Procesi bundle is realized as the pushforward of a DG algebra. In particular, we also obtain a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{m/n}
on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}
, whose image under the isomorphism (
2
) is
q^{n-1}Q_{m+n,n}\cdot 1
.

1.5.
Vanishing of higher cohomology

In contrast to Haiman’s approach, vanishing of higher cohomology of
\mathcal{F}_{m/n}
is not necessary for proving Theorem
A
. However, we do establish the following result using the “quantization commutes with reduction” theorem of Halpern-Leistner
[
HL
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}
be the dual of the Procesi bundle and for
0\leq j\leq n-1
, let
\mathcal{P}^{\vee}_{\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}(\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{P}^{\vee})
.

Theorem B
.

(=Theorem
6.4
)
For
0\leq j\leq n-1
,
H^{i}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n},\mathcal{F}_{m/n}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\vee}_{\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{h}})=0
for all
i>0
, and there is a bigraded isomoprhism

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n},\mathcal{F}_{m/n}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\vee}_{\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}(\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{h}_{n},\mathrm{gr}^{F}\mathrm{L}_{m/n}).

Beyond
\wedge^{j}\mathfrak{h}
, we conjecture the following:

Conjecture 1.1
.

For
i>0
,
H^{i}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n},\mathcal{F}_{m/n}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\vee})=0
, and there is a bigraded
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{2}]\rtimes S_{n}
-module isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n},\mathcal{F}_{m/n}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\vee})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{gr}^{F}\mathrm{L}_{m/n}

When
m\gg 0
, the vanishing of
H^{>0}(\mathrm{Hilb}^{n},\mathcal{F}_{m/n}\otimes\mathcal{P}^{\vee})
is automatic. Under this assumption, we prove Conjecture
1.1
as Theorem
7.5
by showing the socle of
\mathrm{gr}^{F}{\mathrm{L}_{m/n}}
as a
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{2}]
-module is
\mathrm{gr}^{F}\mathrm{e}{\mathrm{L}_{m/n}}
.

1.6.
Applications of Theorem
A

1.6.1.
Khovanov-Rozansky homology of torus knots

To a braid
\gamma
one associates its Rouquier complex of Soergel bimodules. Applying Hochschild homology termwise and then taking cohomology gives a triply graded vector space, with gradings arising from the Hochschild cohomological degree, internal degree and homological degree. After reduction and grading shifts depending on the braid, this defines the reduced Khovanov-Rozansky homology
\mathrm{HHH}(\overline{\gamma})
of the braid closure. We use the grading conventions of
[
GORS14
, Section 1.5]
, with the reduced unknot concentrated in degree
(0,0,0)
.

For torus knot
T_{m,n}
, Gorsky, Oblomkov, Rasmussen and Shende proposed the following conjecture and proved a bigraded version without the filtration.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(
[
GORS14
, Conjecture 1.2]
) For positive coprime integers
m,n
, set
g=(m-1)(n-1)/2
. There exists a filtration on
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}
compatible with the filtration on
\mathrm{H}_{m/n}({S_{n}},\mathfrak{h}_{n})
and the
\mathrm{h}_{m/n}
-grading such that, for
0\leq i\leq n-1
and
j,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists an isomorphism of vector spaces

\displaystyle\mathrm{HHH}^{2g+2i,\,2j,\,\ell-j-g}(T_{m,n})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}\left(\wedge^{i}\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{gr}^{F}_{\ell}\mathrm{L}_{m/n}(j)\right).

Elias-Hogancamp and Mellit
[
EH19
,
Mel22
]
relate
\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}(T_{m,n})
with the multiplicity of
\wedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{h}
on the right hand side of (
3
). Thus, as a corollary of Theorem
A
, we have:

Theorem C
.

(= Theorem
4.8
) Conjecture
1.2
holds with respect to the re-indexed Hodge filtration.

Moreover, Theorem
B
provides a coherent sheaf realization of
\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{H}(T_{m,n})
. It would be interesting to compare our sheaf with the complex of coherent sheaves on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}
constructed in
[
OR18
]
using matrix factorization.

1.6.2.
Filtrations and compactified Jacobians

In
[
GORS14
]
, the filtration in Conjecture
1.2
admits several constructions. The coincidence of the inductive and algebraic filtrations has been shown in
[
Ma26
]
. Here we show the Hodge filtration coincides with the
\mathfrak{a}
-filtration (cousin of the algebraic filtration).
As a corollary, using the shuffle theorem, we prove the main conjecture in
[
OY17
]
of Oblomkov and Yun as Theorem
7.9
, relating associated graded of cohomology of the compactified Jacobian of the plane curve
y^{m}=x^{n}
with respect to the perverse filtration and associated graded of
\mathrm{L}_{m/n}^{S_{n}}
with respect to the
\mathfrak{a}
-filtration.

We work over
\mathbb{C}

\mathrm{GL}_{n}

\overline{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

\mathrm{SL}_{n}

2.1.
Rational Cherednik algebras

For any
c\in\mathbb{C}
, the rational Cherednik algebra
\mathrm{H}_{c}:=\mathrm{H}_{c}({S_{n}},\mathfrak{h})
is the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by
\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
S_{n}
subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle[x,x^{\prime}]=[y,y^{\prime}]=0,\quad wxw^{-1}=w(x),\quad wyw^{-1}=w(y)

\displaystyle[y,x]=x(y)-\sum_{s\in S}c\langle\alpha_{s},y\rangle\langle\alpha_{s}^{\vee},x\rangle s

where
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}
,
w\in{S_{n}}
and
\langle-,-\rangle
is the natural pairing between
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Here
S\subset S_{n}
is the set of reflections,
\alpha_{s}
and
\alpha_{s}^{\vee}
are the root and coroot associated with
s
, normalized by
\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{s}^{\vee}\rangle=2
.

For an irreducible
{S_{n}}
-representation
\mu
, we regard it as a
S(\mathfrak{h})\rtimes{S_{n}}
-representation by letting the augmentation ideal
S(\mathfrak{h})_{+}
act trivially and define the Verma module
M_{c}(\mu)=\mathrm{H}_{c}\otimes_{S(\mathfrak{h})\rtimes{S_{n}}}\mu.

The Verma module
M_{c}(\mu)
has a unique nonzero irreducible quotient
\mathrm{L}_{c}(\mu)
.

2.2.
Simple modules

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
BEG03
, Theorem 1.2]
) When
c=\frac{m}{n}>0
for

, the unique irreducible finite-dimensional representation of

\mathrm{L}_{c}:=\mathrm{L}_{c}(\mathrm{triv})
. Moreover, for

the algebra
\mathrm{H}_{c}
has finite-dimensional representations only when

for a nonzero integer

coprime to

.

Let
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{H}_{c})
denote category
\mathcal{O}
for rational Cherednik algebras, which is a full subcategory of
\mathrm{H}_{c}\mathrm{-mod}
whose objects are finitely generated over
\mathrm{H}_{c}
and such that the augmentation ideal
S(\mathfrak{h})_{+}
acts locally nilpotently
[
GGOR03
]
. As
\mu
runs over irreducible
{S_{n}}
-representations, the modules
\mathrm{L}_{c}(\mu)
give a complete list of irreducible objects in
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{H}_{c})
.

Let
\mathrm{e}:=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{w\in{S_{n}}}w
be the symmetrizing idempotent in
\mathrm{H}_{c}
. The spherical Cherednik algebra is
\mathrm{A}_{c}:=\mathrm{e}\mathrm{H}_{c}\mathrm{e}
. By
[
BE09
, Corollary 4.2]
, for all

\displaystyle c\notin\{\frac{a}{b}\in(-1,0)|a,b\in\mathbb{Z},2\leq b\leq n\}

there is a Morita equivalence
\mathrm{H}_{c}\mathrm{-mod}\to\mathrm{A}_{c}\mathrm{-mod}
,
M\mapsto\mathrm{e}M.

Let
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{A}_{c})
denote the category
\mathcal{O}
of the spherical Cherednik algebra.

For any smooth algebraic variety
X
, we denote the sheaf of differential operators on
X
by
\mathcal{D}_{X}
and write
\mathcal{D}(X):=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{D}_{X})
. Let
R^{+}

\mathrm{H}_{a}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{r})\rtimes{S_{n}},\qquad y\mapsto D_{y,a}:=\partial_{y}-a\sum_{s\in S}\frac{\langle\alpha_{s},y\rangle}{\alpha_{s}}(1-s).

After localization,
(\mathrm{H}_{a})_{\delta}\cong\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}_{r})\rtimes{S_{n}}
. For each

, let
\phi_{a}
be the automorphism of this localized algebra characterized by
\phi_{a}(x)=x,\phi_{a}(D_{y,a})=D_{y,-a},\phi_{a}(w)=\operatorname{sign}(w)w.
By
[
GGS09
, Lemma 5.6 and Proposition 5.8]
,

(4)

\displaystyle\phi_{c+1}(\mathrm{A}_{c})=\delta^{-1}\mathrm{A}_{-c-1}\delta.

Thus
u\mapsto\delta\phi_{c+1}(u)\delta^{-1}
identifies
\mathrm{A}_{c}

. We denote the induced equivalence by

\Omega_{c}:\mathrm{A}_{c}\mathrm{-mod}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{A}_{-c-1}\mathrm{-mod},\qquad\Omega_{c}^{-1}=\Omega_{-c-1}.

2.3.
Mirabolic
D
-modules

Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{n}
on which
\overline{G}
acts by left multiplication and put
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{g}\times V
. Let
\tau:\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{G})
denote the embedding induced by differentiating the diagonal action of
\overline{G}
on
\mathfrak{G}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{G})
, an element

acts by

(5)

\displaystyle(g\cdot f)(x)=f(g^{-1}\cdot x).

In particular, writing

\tau(\mathbf{1})=-E_{V}

For a constant

(6)

\displaystyle\tau_{d}:\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{G}),\quad x\to\tau(x)-d\cdot\mathrm{tr}(x).

Thus

In Section

\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module

\widetilde{\mathrm{gr}}(M)

2.4.
Quantum Hamiltonian reduction

Fix a nonzero element
\mathrm{vol}\in\bigwedge^{n}V^{*}
. Following
[
BFG06
, (5.3.2)]
, consider the regular function on
\mathfrak{G}
defined by:

(7)

\displaystyle s(x,v)=\langle\mathrm{vol},v\wedge xv\wedge\dots\wedge x^{n-1}v\rangle.

Take
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{cyc}}:=\{(x,v)\in\mathfrak{G}|s(x,v)\neq 0\}
, the locus where
v
is a cyclic vector for
x
, i.e.
\mathbb{C}[x]v=V
. By
[
BFG06
, Lemma 5.3.3]
, the composition of the projection
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{cyc}}\to\mathfrak{g}
and the Chevalley map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\!/G\cong\mathfrak{h}/{S_{n}}
is a principal
\overline{G}
-bundle and the diagonal
\overline{G}
-action on
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{cyc}}
is free along the fibers. Therefore we have an algebra isomorphism

\displaystyle\iota:\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{G}_{\mathrm{cyc}}]^{\overline{G}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{S_{n}}.

Our convention (
5
) gives
g\cdot s=\det(g)^{-1}s
, hence
\tau(x)s=-\mathrm{tr}(x)s
. Consequently the multivalued section
s^{-d}
is annihilated by
\tau_{d}(\overline{\mathfrak{g}})
.

By
[
BG15
, Proposition 2.1.2 and Remark 2.8.3]
, any mirabolic
D
-module is locally finite as a
\tau(\overline{\mathfrak{g}})
-module.
In particular, its global sections decompose as
\Gamma(M)=\bigoplus_{a+\mathbb{Z}\in\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma(M)_{a+\mathbb{Z}},
where

\Gamma(M)_{a+\mathbb{Z}}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\{v\in\Gamma(M)\mid(\tau(\mathbf{1})-a-k)^{N}v=0\text{ for }N\gg 0\}.

Given
d\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\mathscr{C}_{d}(\mathfrak{G})
be the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}(\mathfrak{G})
consisting of modules
M
on which
\tau(\mathbf{1})
acts semisimply and such that

\displaystyle\Gamma(M)=\Gamma(M)_{nd+\mathbb{Z}}.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
GG06
, Theorem 7.4.3]
,
[
GGS09
, Theorem 8.1]
) The radial part map

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{G})^{\overline{G}}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{h}/{S_{n}}),\quad u\mapsto\bigg[\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}/{S_{n}}]\ni f\mapsto\iota\left(s^{d}u\left(s^{-d}\iota^{-1}(f)\right)\right)\bigg]

induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{d}:(\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{G})/\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{G})\tau_{d}(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}))^{\overline{G}}\cong\mathrm{A}_{d-1}.

Thus there is a quantum Hamiltonian reduction functor

Let

6.
Vanishing of higher cohomology via window categories

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem

6.1.
KN stratification

We follow the definitions from
[
HL15
]
.
Let
G
be reductive and fix a
G
-equivariant embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{A}^{m}
to define
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1).
Fix a positive-definite norm on the space of real cocharacter invariant under the Weyl group action. For a one-parameter subgroup
\lambda
and a connected component
Z\subset X^{\lambda}
, the associated attracting locus (blade) is

Y_{\lambda,Z}:=\left\{x\in X\,\middle|\,\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\cdot x\in Z\right\}.

Taking limit defines a contracting morphism
\pi:Y_{\lambda,Z}\rightarrow Z.

Let
P_{\lambda}\subset G
be the parabolic subgroup consisting of those
p\in G
for which
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)p\lambda(t)^{-1}
exists, and let
L_{\lambda}=Z_{G}(\lambda)
be its Levi subgroup. If
X
is smooth along
Z
, then
\pi
is a
P_{\lambda}
-equivariant locally trivial affine bundle.
For a KN stratum
S=G\cdot Y_{\lambda,Z}
, one moreover has
S\simeq G\times^{P_{\lambda}}Y_{\lambda,Z}
, and the restriction to
Z
of the conormal bundle
\mathcal{I}_{S}/\mathcal{I}_{S}^{2}
has strictly positive
\lambda
-weights.

The Kempf–Ness stratification is constructed inductively. At the
i
-th step one chooses, up to
G
-conjugacy, a pair
(\lambda_{i},Z_{i}^{\ast})
maximizing
\mu(\lambda,Z):=-\frac{\mathrm{wt}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}|_{Z})}{\lVert\lambda\rVert}
among pairs for which
Z
is not contained in the union of the previously constructed strata and for which
\mu(\lambda,Z)>0
. One then removes from
Z_{i}^{\ast}
the previously constructed strata, obtaining an open subset
Z_{i}\subset Z_{i}^{\ast}
, and sets
Y_{i}:=\pi_{i}^{-1}(Z_{i})
to be the attracting locus and
S_{i}:=G\cdot Y_{i}.

Thus
X
is obtained from its semistable locus by successively adjoining the unstable strata
S_{i}
. We write
L_{i}=Z_{G}(\lambda_{i})
and
P_{i}:=P_{\lambda_{i}}
.

7.6.
Socle of
\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c}

Let
\mathfrak{m}\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{2}]
be the maximal ideal at the origin. Among modules supported at the origin, the only simple
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{2}]
-module is
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{2}]/\mathfrak{m}
, so

\displaystyle\mathrm{Socle}(\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c})

\displaystyle=\{\phi\in\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c}:\mathfrak{m}\phi=0\}.

Proposition 7.6
.

\mathrm{Socle}\left(\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c}\right)=\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathrm{e}\mathrm{L}_{c}).

Proof.

Suppose
\xi_{i}
is the image of
y_{i}
in
\mathrm{gr}(\mathrm{H}_{c})
.
We first compute
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})
.
Below all powers and operators are taken in
\mathrm{L}_{c}
.
Use the Frobenius pairing from Lemma
7.1
,
\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Fr}}=(u,{\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{c}}(v))_{c}
.
The adjointness relations for the Dunkl form give
\langle yu,v\rangle_{\mathrm{Fr}}=-\langle u,yv\rangle_{\mathrm{Fr}}
.
By Lemma
7.1
, this pairing induces an
S_{n}
-emodule isomorphism
\mathrm{gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c}\cong(\mathfrak{a}^{k}/\mathfrak{a}^{k+1})^{*}.

The Dunkl product rule gives
\mathfrak{h}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{k}
.
Thus, under this identification,

\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})\cap\mathrm{gr}_{k}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c}\cong Q_{k}^{*},\qquad\text{ where }Q_{k}:=\mathfrak{a}^{k}/(\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}+\mathfrak{h}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}),

where
\mathfrak{h}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}=\sum_{i}y_{i}\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
.
We claim that
Q_{k}=Q_{k}^{S_{n}}.

Let
p_{i}
be the
i
-th power sum symmetric polynomial for
i=2,\dots,n
. Every element of
\mathfrak{a}^{k}
is a sum of terms
hp_{a_{1}}\cdots p_{a_{k}}
with
h\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]
.
We prove by downward induction on the exponent
a
of
p_{2}
that for
h
nonconstant homogeneous and
r_{s}>2

hp_{2}^{a}p_{r_{1}}\cdots p_{r_{k-a}}=0
in
Q_{k}
. It suffices to take
h=xh^{\prime}
with
x
linear. Choose
y\in\mathfrak{h}
such that
x=\partial_{y}p_{2}
, and put
g=p_{r_{1}}\cdots p_{r_{k-a}}
.
Since
p_{2}
and
g
are invariant, product rule
[
Gor13
, Corollary 5.3]
gives

y(h^{\prime}p_{2}^{a+1}g)=(yh^{\prime})p_{2}^{a+1}g+(a+1)hp_{2}^{a}g+h^{\prime}p_{2}^{a+1}\partial_{y}g.

The left hand side lies in
\mathfrak{h}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
, and the first term on the right lies in
\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
.
The last term is

\sum_{s}(h^{\prime}\partial_{y}p_{r_{s}})p_{2}^{a+1}\prod_{t\neq s}p_{r_{t}}.

For
a=k
this sum is empty. Otherwise each summand has
k
invariant factors,
a+1
of them equal to
p_{2}
, and a coefficient of positive degree. Hence this term vanishes in
Q_{k}
by induction. Since
a+1\neq 0
, this proves the vanishing of

as desired. Consequently,
Q_{k}
is spanned by products of
k
invariant generators with constant coefficients, proving the claim. As a result,
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})\subset\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{L}_{c}
.

For the other direction, we need to show
\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{h}\cdot\mathfrak{a}^{k+1})\subset\mathrm{e}\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
.
For
g=p_{a_{1}}\cdots p_{a_{k+1}}
, by product rule,

\mathrm{e}\bigl(y(hg)\bigr)=\mathrm{e}(yh)g+\sum_{s}\mathrm{e}(h\partial_{y}p_{a_{s}})\prod_{t\neq s}p_{a_{t}}.

Each coefficient
\mathrm{e}(h\partial_{y}p_{a_{s}})
is an invariant polynomial with zero constant term, so every term belongs to
\mathfrak{a}^{k+1}
. Therefore
Q_{k}=\mathrm{e}(\mathfrak{a}^{k}/\mathfrak{a}^{k+1})
. Dualizing gives
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})=\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathrm{e}\mathrm{L}_{c}).

Let
u_{i}
be the image of
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
in
\mathrm{gr}(\mathrm{H}_{c})
.
Since the
u_{i}
commute with the
\xi_{i}
, we have
u_{i}\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})\subset\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})
.
For
v\in\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})
, triviality of the
S_{n}
-action on
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})
makes the map
\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})
,
u_{i}\mapsto u_{i}v
,
S_{n}
-equivariant.
It is zero because
\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{n}}(\mathfrak{h}^{*},\mathbb{C})=0

Thus
\bigcap_{i}\mathrm{Ker}(\xi_{i})=\mathrm{Socle}(\mathrm{gr}^{\mathfrak{a}}\mathrm{L}_{c})
.
∎

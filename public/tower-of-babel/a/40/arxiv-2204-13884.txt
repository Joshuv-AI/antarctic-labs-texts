GIT for actions of graded unipotent groups on algebraic 𝕜 \Bbbk -schemes
Yikun Qiao

Abstract
Let 𝕜 \Bbbk be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. We extend the so-called U ^ \hat{U} -theorem for projective varieties over 𝕜 \Bbbk proved in [ Bér+18 ] and [ Bér+16 ] to projective schemes over 𝕜 \Bbbk . The group U ^ := U ⋊ 𝔾 m \hat{U}:=U\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m} is a semi-direct product of a unipotent group U U and a multiplicative group 𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} acting on U U by conjugation, such that the weights of 𝔾 m ↷ Lie ⁡ ( U ) \mathbb{G}_{m}\curvearrowright\mathrm{Lie}(U) are all positive. The condition that “semistability coincides with stability” (“ss=s”) required in [ Bér+18 ] and [ Bér+16 ] is reformulated algebraically for our setting. We also describe a blow-up procedure to produce a situation where “ss=s” holds from one in which it does not. This procedure is different from the sequence of blow-ups in [ Bér+16 ] , and produces “ss=s” in one blow-up.

Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
U

denote the semi-direct product

, which is sometimes denoted by

alternatively. Geometric invariant theory for linearisations of

[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[
Bér+16
]
,
[
HJ21
]
, with conditions on dimensions of
U
-stabilisers or conditions on an extension of the

-action to a suitable reductive group.

Geometric invariant theory for
\hat{U}
-linearisations has been applied in the construction of moduli of unstable objects. Recently, moduli of pure sheaves of a fixed Harder-Narasimhan type of length 2 on projective varieties are constructed in
[
Jac21
]
. More applications can be found in
[
Bér+18a
]
,
[
Ham20
]
,
[
HJ21
]
.

Let
X
be a projective variety and let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let
\hat{U}
act on
X
linearly with respect to
L
, denoted by
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
. Let
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq X
be the non-vanishing locus of
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
and let
Z_{\lambda-\min}\hookrightarrow X
be the vanishing locus of
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda<\max}
, where
H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
is the weight space of
\lambda\curvearrowright H^{0}(X,L^{d})
whose weight is the maximal, and
H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda<\max}
is the sum of weight spaces whose weights are not maximal. In
[
Bér+18a
]
, under the condition “ss=s” (Condition
2.10
), it is proved that
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\setminus UZ_{\lambda-\min}
is open and it has a projective geometric quotient by
\hat{U}
. In
[
Bér+16
]
, the condition “ss=s” is replaced by a weaker version that
\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(z)=\{e\}
for all closed points
z\in Z_{\lambda-\min}
. These two versions of “ss=s” are actually equivalent for
\hat{U}
-linearisations on projective varieties (See Corollary
2.13
).

Let
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{n}
be the positive weights of
\lambda\curvearrowright\mathfrak{u}
. Let
U_{i}\subseteq U
be the subgroup such that
\mathrm{Lie}(U_{i})=\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda\geq w_{i}}
, which is the sum of weight spaces whose
\lambda
-weights are greater than or equal to
w_{i}
. As suggested in
[
Bér+16
]
Remark 2.8 and Remark 7.12, the condition “ss=s” can be further weakened to the condition that the minimal dimension of
U_{i}
-stabilisers on
Z_{\lambda-\min}
and the minimal dimension of
U_{i}
-stabilisers on
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
coincide for all
1\leq i\leq n
, when
X
is a variety. We split this relaxation into two steps. The first relaxation of “ss=s” is the condition that the dimension of
U_{i}
-stabilisers are constant on
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, which is assumed in Section
2
. The second relaxation is that the minimal dimensions of
U_{i}
-stabilisers on
Z_{\lambda-\min}
and
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
coincide, which requires a blow-up to satisfy the first relaxation of “ss=s” above, which is assumed in Section
3
.

In Section
2
, we follow ideas in
[
Bér+16
]
, Section 7, and generalise the theorem to projective schemes over
\Bbbk
, and complete the proof of the results stated in
[
Bér+16
]
Remark 2.8 and Remark 7.12, (See Theorem
2.32
, Theorem
2.33
). The condition analogous to “ss=s” is Condition
2.26
(the ‘constant dimension of relative stabilisers’ or CDRS condition), which says cokernels of certain
infinitesimal actions
are locally free. More precisely, for an action
G\curvearrowright Y
over
\Bbbk
, the
infinitesimal action
is a morphism of sheaves
\phi:\Omega_{Y/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
, and for a closed point
y\in Y
, we have
\mathrm{coker}(\phi)\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\kappa(y)\cong\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)^{*}
. Therefore the condition that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is constant can be replaced by the condition that
\mathrm{coker}(\phi)
is locally free of constant rank. If
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is reduced, then Condition
2.26
is equivalent to the condition that
U_{i}
-stabilisers have constant dimensions on
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
(See Lemma
2.28
).

In Section
3
, we give a blow-up construction when Condition
2.26
fails but Condition
3.2
is satisfied. Condition
2.26
requires certain sheaves to be locally free. Fitting ideals can measure the failure of a sheaf to be locally free in the sense that if
\mathrm{Fit}_{r-1}(\mathcal{F})=0
, then
\mathcal{F}
is locally free of rank
r
on the non-vanishing locus of
\mathrm{Fit}_{r}(\mathcal{F})
. They play an important role in determining the centre of the blow-up. In
[
Bér+16
]
, a sequence of equivariant blow-ups are described to achieve the desired “ss=s”. Here we provide a different construction, which involves only one blow-up and our argument is more algebraic. Our main theorem (Theorem
3.10
) says that the result of this blow-up satisfies Condition
2.26
and thus Theorem
2.32
and Theorem
2.33
apply.

Geometric Invariant Theory for
\hat{U}
-actions

Let
\hat{U}:=U\rtimes_{\lambda}\mathbb{G}_{m}
be a semi-direct product of a unipotent group
U
with a grading 1PS
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Aut}(U)
. Let
X
be a projective scheme over
\Bbbk
and let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let
\hat{U}
act linearly on
X
with respect to
L

be the non-vanishing locus of
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
, where
H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}\subseteq H^{0}(X,L^{d})
is the weight space of
\lambda
with its maximal weight. Without assuming that

when sections in

Let

be the weights of
\lambda\curvearrowright\mathfrak{u}:=\mathrm{Lie}(U)
. The grading 1PS
\lambda
on
U
induces a filtration of subgroups
\{U_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
, where
U_{i}\subseteq U
is the subgroup with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda\geq w_{i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}
, where
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda\geq w_{i}}
is the sum of weight spaces whose
\lambda
-weights are greater than or equal to
w_{i}
. The strategy is to construct quotients by
U_{i}/U_{i-1}
successively. For this process, we assume Condition
2.26
, which generalises the relaxed condition “ss=s” in
[
Bér+16
]
, Remark 2.8 to non-reduced situation. When
X
is reduced, Condition
2.26
is equivalent to a condition on dimensions of
U_{i}
-stabilisers (Lemma
2.28
). With this condition, we prove that the universal geometric quotient by
U
of
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
exists and the quotient is quasi-projective (Theorem
2.32
).

Fitting ideals and stabilisers

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X
be a projective scheme over
\Bbbk
and let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

Condition
2.10
(“ss=s”);

(2)

\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma}=0
.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be an integer such that
H^{0}(X,L^{m})
generates
\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{md})
. Then
X^{0}_{\min}
is the union of the open affine subschemes
X_{f}
for
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}
. The following is exact

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{X^{0}_{\min}/\Bbbk}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\min}/\Bbbk}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

(43)

whose local version is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/\Bbbk}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma}(X_{f})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

(44)

So the condition
\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma}=0
is equivalent to the surjectivity of
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
for all
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}
.

For
(1)\implies(2)
, assume Condition
2.10
(“ss=s”). Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be such that
H^{0}(X,L^{m})
generates
\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{md})
and
H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}\subseteq\xi.H^{0}(X,L^{m})^{\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}}
for all
\xi,\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
satisfying
(1)-(3)
in Condition
2.10
. We want to show the following map is surjective

\begin{split}\phi:\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/\Bbbk}&\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\\
a\mathrm{d}b&\mapsto\sum_{i,j}u^{(i)}_{j}\otimes a\big(\xi^{(i)}_{j}.b\big)\end{split}

(45)

where
\big(\xi^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
is a basis of
\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}}
, the weight space of
\mathfrak{u}
whose weight is
w_{i}
, and
\big(u^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,u^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
is the set of basis of
(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}})^{*}
dual to
\big(\xi^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
.

Take
\xi:=\xi^{(i)}_{j}
and

\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{span}\big\{\xi^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}}:1\leq j^{\prime}\leq r_{i},\;j^{\prime}\neq j\big\}+\bigoplus_{i^{\prime}<i}\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i^{\prime}}}.

(46)

Then
\xi,\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
satisfy
(1)-(3)
in Condition
2.10
, so
H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}\subseteq\xi.H^{0}(X,L^{m})^{\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}}
. In particular for
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}
, there exists
g\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})
, which is fixed by
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
, and
\xi^{(i)}_{j}.g=f
. Without loss of generality assume
g\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max-w_{i}}
, since we can decompose
g
according to weights and replace
g
by its eigen-component in
H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max-w_{i}}
. Then
\frac{g}{f}\in\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{-w_{i}}
and

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{g}{f}\big)\mapsto&\sum_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}u^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\xi^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}.g}{f}\\
=&u^{(i)}_{j}\otimes\frac{\xi^{(i)}_{j}.g}{f}+\sum_{i^{\prime}>i}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r_{i^{\prime}}}u^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\xi^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}.g}{f}\\
=&u^{(i)}_{j}\otimes 1+\sum_{i^{\prime}>i}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r_{i^{\prime}}}u^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{\xi^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}.g}{f}.\end{split}

(47)

The first equality is because that
\xi^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}.g=0
when
i^{\prime}<i
or
\begin{cases}i^{\prime}=i\\
j^{\prime}\neq j\end{cases}
, and the second is because
\xi^{(i)}_{j}.g=f
. The above for
1\leq j\leq r_{i}
together show that

(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\phi)+\sum_{i^{\prime}>i}(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i^{\prime}}})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f}).

(48)

In particular
(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{n}})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\phi)
, and if
(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i^{\prime}}})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\phi)
for all
i^{\prime}>i
, then
(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\phi)
. By induction, this proves
(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\phi)
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, i.e.
\phi
is surjective. This proves
(1)\implies(2)
.

For
(2)\implies(1)
, assume
\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma}=0
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be such that
H^{0}(X,L^{m})
generates
\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{md})
. For any non-nilpotent
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}
, the following map is surjective

\begin{split}\phi:\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/\Bbbk}&\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\\
a\mathrm{d}b&\mapsto\sum_{i,j}u^{(i)}_{j}\otimes a\big(\xi^{(i)}_{j}.b\big)\end{split}

(49)

where
\big(\xi^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
is a basis of
\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}}
, and
\big(u^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,u^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
is the dual basis of
(\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i}})^{*}
.

For
u^{(i)}_{j}
, there exists
\omega^{(i)}_{j}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/\Bbbk}
such that
\phi(\omega^{(i)}_{j})=u^{(i)}_{j}\otimes 1
. Write

\omega^{(i)}_{j}=\sum_{\mu}a^{(i)}_{j,\mu}\mathrm{d}b^{(i)}_{j,\mu},\quad a^{(i)}_{j,\mu},b^{(i)}_{j,\mu}\in\mathcal{O}(X_{f}).

(50)

Recall
\phi(\omega^{(i)}_{j})=u^{(i)}_{j}\otimes 1
, that is

\delta^{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}=\sum_{\mu}a^{(i)}_{j,\mu}\big(\xi^{(i^{\prime})}_{j^{\prime}}.b^{(i)}_{j,\mu}\big),\quad 1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq n,\;1\leq j\leq r_{i},\;1\leq j^{\prime}\leq r_{i^{\prime}}.

(51)

Let
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\hat{U}
be the grading 1PS. Then
\lambda
acts on
\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
. Decompose
a^{(i)}_{j,\mu},b^{(i)}_{j,\mu}
according to
\lambda
-weights

\begin{split}a^{(i)}_{j,\mu}=&a^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=0}+a^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda<0}\\
b^{(i)}_{j,\mu}=&b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=-w_{i}}+b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda<-w_{i}}+b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda>-w_{i}}.\end{split}

(52)

Then

\begin{split}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}=&\sum_{\mu}a^{(i)}_{j,\mu}\big(\xi^{(i)}_{j^{\prime},\mu}.b^{(i)}_{j,\mu}\big)\\
=&\sum_{\mu}\big(a^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=0}+a^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda<0}\big)\;\xi^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}}.\big(b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=-w_{i}}+b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda<-w_{i}}+b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda>-w_{i}}\big)\\
=&\sum_{\mu}a^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=0}\big(\xi^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}}.b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=-w_{i}}\big)+\mathrm{err}\end{split}

(53)

where
\mathrm{err}\in\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{<0}
. Then
\delta_{jj^{\prime}}=\xi^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}}.h^{(i)}_{j}
for

h^{(i)}_{j}:=\sum_{\mu}a^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=0}b^{(i)}_{j,\mu,\lambda=-w_{i}}\in\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{-w_{i}}.

(54)

Since
h^{(i)}_{j}\in\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{-w_{i}}
, it is fixed by
\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}
for

\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}:=\mathrm{span}\big\{\xi^{(i)}_{j^{\prime}}:1\leq j^{\prime}\leq r_{i},\;j^{\prime}\neq j\big\}+\sum_{i^{\prime}<i}\mathfrak{u}_{w_{i^{\prime}}}.

(55)

For
h^{(i)}_{j}
, we can write
h^{(i)}_{j}=\frac{g^{(i)}_{j}}{f^{N}}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
g^{(i)}_{j}\in H^{0}\big(X,L^{mN}\big)_{\max-w_{i}}
. We have:

•

\xi^{(i)}_{j}.g^{(i)}_{j}-f^{N}
is annihilated by a power of
f
;

•

for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}
, the section
\xi.g^{(i)}_{j}\in H^{0}\big(X,L^{mN}\big)_{\max}
is annihilated by a power of
f
.

We can enlarge
N
and assume
\xi^{(i)}_{j}.g^{(i)}_{j}=f^{N}
and
g^{(i)}_{j}\in H^{0}\big(X,L^{mN}\big)^{\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}}
. We can enlarge
N
further such that it is uniform for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq r_{i}
. In other words we have

f^{N}\in\xi^{(i)}_{j}.H^{0}\big(X,L^{mN}\big)^{\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,\;1\leq j\leq r_{i}.

(56)

Choose a basis
f_{1},\cdots,f_{p}
of
H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\max}
, then there exists
N_{t}
for each
1\leq t\leq p
such that
f_{t}^{N_{t}}\in\xi^{(i)}_{j}.H^{0}(X,L^{mN_{t}})^{\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}}
. Take
N=\max\{N_{1},\cdots,N_{p}\}
and then
f_{t}^{N}\in\xi^{(i)}_{j}.H^{0}(X,L^{mN})^{\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}}
for any
1\leq t\leq p
and any
i,j
. This implies that
H^{0}(X,L^{mpN})_{\max}\subseteq\xi^{(i)}_{j}.H^{0}(X,L^{mpN})^{\mathfrak{u}^{(i)}_{j}}
for all
i,j
, which is Condition
2.10
(“ss=s”).
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
X
be a projective scheme over
\Bbbk
and let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Let
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

Condition
2.10
(“ss=s”);

(2)

\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma}=0
;

(3)

\mathrm{Fit}_{0}(\gamma^{*}\Omega_{\sigma})=\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\min}}
;

(4)

\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(z)=\{e\}
for all
\Bbbk
-points
z\in Z_{\min}
.

Proof.

It is by definition that
(2)\iff(3)
. Proposition
2.12
proves
(1)\iff(2)
.

The application of Lemma
2.7
to
k=0
and
(\mathfrak{v}:\mathfrak{w})=(\mathfrak{u}:0)
shows
(3)
is equivalent to that
\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(x)=\{e\}
for all
\Bbbk
-points
x\in X^{0}_{\min}
. In particular
(3)\implies(4)
.

By the upper semi-continuity of
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(x)
, we have that
(4)
implies that
\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(x)=\{e\}
for all
\Bbbk
-points
x\in X^{0}_{\min}
, which is equivalent to
(3)
. This proves
(4)\implies(3)
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

When “ss=s” fails, the locus in

2.3

Let
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Aut}(U)
be a grading 1PS on
U
with weights
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{n}
on
\mathfrak{u}
. Let

(102)

be the corresponding filtration of Lie ideals of
\mathfrak{u}
by
\lambda
-weights. Let
U_{i}\subseteq\hat{U}
be the normal subgroup whose Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{u}_{i}
.

Let
X
be a projective scheme over
\Bbbk
, and let
L
be an ample line bundle over
X
. Let
\hat{U}
act linearly on
X
with respect to
L
. Then
\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})
is finitely generated (
[
Sta22

Lemma 0B5T
]
), and
\hat{U}
acts on each

.

For

and

, let

be the weight space for
\lambda
of weight
w
. When
w\in\mathbb{Z}
is not a weight, we can define the subspace
H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=w}
as zero. Let
H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
denote the weight space for
\lambda
of the maximal weight on
H^{0}(X,L^{d})
. Let

H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda<\max}
denote the sum of weight spaces for
\lambda
of weights that are not the maximal weight on
H^{0}(X,L^{d})
. Then
H^{0}(X,L^{d})=H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}\oplus H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda<\max}
, and
H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}\neq 0
.

Let
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq X
be the open subscheme where
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
does not vanish. For the action of
\lambda
on
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
, let
Z_{\lambda-\min}\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
be the
fixed point subscheme
in the sense of Fogarty (
[
Fog73
]
). It is easy to see that
Z_{\lambda-\min}\hookrightarrow X
is the closed subscheme associated to the ideal
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda<\max}
. Let

be such that

generates

. Then
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq X
has an affine open covering

(103)

Remark 2.25
.

If all sections
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
are nilpotent, then

by definition. We can assume there exists a section

for some

and
f
is not nilpotent for

, or we regard statements as vacuous truth when

.

Since
\lambda
is a grading 1PS on

, sections of maximal
\lambda
-weights are

-invariant, i.e. for all

. Then

-invariant for

The ring of functions on

is

. There is an induced action of
\lambda
on
\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
. Since

has maximal
\lambda
-weight in

, the
\lambda
-weights on

(104)

The closed subscheme

\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\to\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{\lambda<0}
. Then

. The closed immersion

\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{\lambda=0}\to\mathcal{O}(X_{f})

\big(X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\setminus UZ_{\lambda-\min}\big)\big/\hat{U}\cong\overline{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}/U}^{\lambda,L_{\theta},\mathrm{(s)s}}/\lambda=\overline{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}/U}/\!/\lambda.

(143)

∎

3
Blow-up

Let
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
and
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{n}
and
\{\mathfrak{u}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
be as above. In this section, we do not assume Condition
2.26
, and describe a blow-up procedure to achieve Condition
2.26
so that Theorem
2.33
applies to the induced action on the blow-up of
X
. A weaker condition is needed for this process (Condition
3.2
), and the main result is Theorem
3.10
.

3.1
Examples with few grading weights

The general construction involves a tedious calculation (Lemma
3.9
). It may help to motivate the construction there by first considering some more tractable examples. In this subsection, we consider the simple situations when there are one or two grading weights for
\lambda
on
U
. Consider the situation:

•

\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Aut}(U)
is a grading on
U
with grading weights
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{n}
for
n=1
or
2
;

•

Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is a
reduced
affine scheme of finite type over
\Bbbk
;

•

\hat{U}
acts on
Y
such that the action of
\lambda
on
A
has all weights non-positive (cf. the first requirement in Condition
2.16
).

The dimensions of unipotent stabilisers are not assumed constant, and the blow-up is to achieve this. Consider the exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{i-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathfrak{u}_{i}/\mathfrak{u}_{i-1})^{*}\otimes A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}(\phi_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{i}}

(144)

where
K_{i-1}=\ker\big(\Omega_{A/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}_{i-1}^{*}\otimes A\big)
. For simplicity, write
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{i})
for
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\mathrm{coker}(\phi_{i}))
. A suitable
\hat{U}
-equivariant blow-up
\tilde{Y}\to Y
with an ample linearisation will satisfy the condition that on
\tilde{Y}^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
, the dimensions of
\mathfrak{u}_{i}
-stabilisers are constant for each
1\leq i\leq n
. By Corollary
2.9
, the condition that
\mathfrak{u}_{i}
-stabilisers have constant dimensions is equivalent to that
\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\phi}_{i})
are locally free of constant ranks. We will verify that
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}-1}(\tilde{\phi}_{i})=0
and
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\tilde{\phi}_{i})=\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{Y}^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
for some
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
. This condition is equivalent to
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i}}(y)=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}
for
\Bbbk
-points
y\in\tilde{Y}^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
and
1\leq i\leq n
by Proposition
2.3
and Corollary
2.9
.

Let
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

k_{i}:=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{i})\neq 0\},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(145)

Recall that
Y=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is a reduced scheme of finite type over
\Bbbk
. If we assume
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})\neq 0
, then the non-vanishing locus of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
is non-empty. This non-vanishing locus equals the intersection of the non-vanishing loci of
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
for
i=1,\cdots,n
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, the non-vanishing locus of
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
is the largest open subscheme such that the restriction of
\mathrm{coker}(\phi_{i})
is locally free of rank
k_{i}
by
[
Sta22
,
Lemma 05P8
]
. Therefore the non-vanishing locus of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
is the largest open subscheme such that
\mathrm{coker}(\phi_{i})
is locally free of rank
k_{i}
for each
i=1,\cdots,n
. By Corollary
2.9
, the closed points of the non-vanishing locus of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
are exactly
y\in Y
such that

\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i}}(y)=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

(146)

3.1.1
One grading weight

Assume there is only one grading weight, i.e.
n=1
. Let
w>0
be the grading weight. Let
r=\dim U
and choose a basis
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{r})
of
\mathfrak{u}
with its dual basis
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{r})
on
\mathfrak{u}^{*}
. Let
\phi:\Omega_{A/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes A
be the morphism of coherent sheaves and denote by
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)\subseteq A
the
k
th Fitting ideal of
\mathrm{coker}(\phi)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)\neq 0
and
\mathrm{Fit}_{k-1}(\phi)=0
.

We need an assumption on
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)
, that is
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)\not\subseteq A_{\lambda<0}
, which is equivalent to that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(z)=k
for some
\Bbbk
-points
z\in\mathrm{Spec}(A/A_{\lambda<0})
(See Condition
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
below). Let
I\subseteq A
be the ideal

I:=\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)+A_{\lambda<0}=\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)_{\lambda=0}\oplus A_{\lambda<0}.

(147)

Let
J\subseteq A
be the kernel of the composition
A\to\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes A\to\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes(A/I)
. The closed subscheme associated to
J\subseteq A
is the scheme theoretic image of
U\times\mathbb{V}(I)\to Y
, where
\mathbb{V}(I)\hookrightarrow Y
is the closed subscheme associated to
I\subseteq A
.

Remark 3.1
.

The
\Bbbk
-points in
\mathbb{V}(I)
are exactly
z\in\mathbb{V}(A_{<0})
such that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{U}(z)>k
. By
[
Sta22
,
Lemma 01R8
]
, the closed subset
\mathbb{V}(J)
is the closure of the
U
-sweep of
\mathbb{V}(I)
.

We choose the centre
C\hookrightarrow Y
of the blow-up as the closed subscheme associated to
J\subseteq A
. The extreme situation when
J=0
does not happen, since the assumption
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)\not\subseteq A_{\lambda<0}
implies
0\neq\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)_{\lambda=0}\subseteq J
.

The ideal
J\subseteq A
is
\hat{U}
-invariant and
J_{\lambda=\max}=J_{\lambda=0}=\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)_{\lambda=0}
. Let
\tilde{Y}\to Y
be the blow-up. Then
\tilde{Y}
is constructed by

\begin{split}\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A):=&\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}J^{d}=A\oplus J\oplus J^{2}\oplus\cdots\\
\tilde{Y}:=&\mathrm{Proj}\Big(\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A)\Big)\end{split}

(148)

where
\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A)
is called the
blowup algebra associated to
J\subseteq A
. The degree on
\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A)
is the natural one from the direct sum. Let
E\hookrightarrow\tilde{Y}
be the exceptional divisor with its associated line bundle
\mathcal{O}(E)
. The line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
from the
\mathrm{Proj}
-construction equals
\mathcal{O}(-E)
and it is relatively very ample. Since
Y
ia affine, we have that
\mathcal{O}(1)
is ample. The blow-up is
\hat{U}
-equivariant, and then
\hat{U}
acts on
\tilde{Y}
linearly with respect to
\mathcal{O}(1)
. With this ample linearisation, we can define
(\tilde{Y})^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
, which is the union of the following open affine subschemes

\begin{split}(\tilde{Y})^{0}_{\lambda-\min}=&\bigcup_{a\in J_{\lambda=\max}}\tilde{Y}_{a}\\
\tilde{Y}_{a}:=&\mathrm{Spec}\Big(A\Big[\frac{J}{a}\Big]\Big)\\
A\Big[\frac{J}{a}\Big]:=&\Big(\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A)\Big)_{(a)}\end{split}

(149)

where
A\big[\frac{J}{a}\big]
is the homogeneous localisation of
\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A)
at
a\in J=\mathrm{Bl}_{J}(A)_{1}
, called the
affine blowup algebra
.

Let
a\in J_{\lambda=\max}=\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)_{\lambda=0}
be a non-zero element of the following form

a=\det\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}.f_{1}&\cdots&\xi_{1}.f_{r-k}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\xi_{r-k}.f_{1}&\cdots&\xi_{r-k}.f_{r-k}\end{pmatrix}

(150)

for
f_{1},\cdots,f_{r-k}\in A_{\lambda=-w}
. It is easy to see that
(\tilde{Y})^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is covered by
\tilde{Y}_{a}
for such
a
. We prove for such
a\in J_{\lambda=\max}

\mathrm{Fit}_{k-1}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=0,\quad\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})

(151)

where
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
is the morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})/\Bbbk}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\phi}_{a}}

(152)

and
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})
denotes the
k
th Fitting ideal of
\mathrm{coker}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})
.

Lemma
.

\mathrm{Fit}_{k-1}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=0
.

Proof.

There is a surjective morphism

A\Big[\frac{J}{a}\Big]\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{J}{a}\Big)\to\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})/\Bbbk},\quad\frac{g}{a^{m}}\otimes\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{h}{a}\Big)\mapsto\frac{g}{a^{m}}\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{h}{a}\Big)

(153)

where
m\in\mathbb{N},g\in J^{m},h\in J
.

Let
h_{1},\cdots,h_{r-k+1}\in J
. Then
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
on
\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{h_{1}}{a}\big),\cdots,\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{h_{r-k+1}}{a}\big)
is represented by the following matrix

\begin{split}\tilde{\phi}_{a}\Big(\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{h_{1}}{a}\Big),\cdots,\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{h_{r-k+1}}{a}\Big)\Big)=\big(u_{1}\otimes 1,\cdots,u_{r}\otimes 1\big)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\xi_{1}.h_{1}}{a}&\cdots&\frac{\xi_{1}.h_{r-k+1}}{a}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\xi_{r}.h_{1}}{a}&\cdots&\frac{\xi_{r}.h_{r-k+1}}{a}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

(154)

To prove
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=0
, it suffices to show all
(r-k+1)\times(r-k+1)
-minors of the above matrix are zero. Without loss of generality we consider the top
(r-k+1)\times(r-k+1)
-minor, which is of the form
\frac{\det(M)}{a^{r-k+1}}
, where
\det(M)
is the determinant of the following square matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}.h_{1}&\cdots&\xi_{1}.h_{r-k+1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\xi_{r-k+1}.h_{1}&\cdots&\xi_{r-k+1}.h_{r-k+1}\end{pmatrix}.

(155)

Since
\mathrm{Fit}_{k-1}(\phi)=0
, we have
\det(M)=0
. This proves
\mathrm{Fit}_{k-1}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=0
.
∎

Lemma
.

\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})
.

Proof.

This is to prove
1\in\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})
. For
1\leq i\leq r-k
, let

b_{i}:=\det\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}.f_{1}&\cdots&\xi_{1}.f_{r-k}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
f_{1}&\cdots&f_{r-k}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\xi_{r-k}.f_{1}&\cdots&\xi_{r-k}.f_{r-k}\end{pmatrix}\in A

(156)

where
(f_{1},\cdots,f_{r-k})
occupies the

th row. We see that
b_{i}\in A_{\lambda=-w}
.

It is easy to see
\xi_{i}.b_{j}=\delta_{ij}a
for
1\leq i,j\leq r-k
. Moreover, we have

\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{u}).b_{j}\subseteq\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi)\subseteq I
. By Corollary
3.15
, the map
A\to\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes(A/I)
sends

to zero, i.e.

. Then
\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{b_{j}}{a}\big)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})/\Bbbk}
.

The map
\tilde{\phi}_{a}:\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})
on
\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{b_{1}}{a}\big),\cdots,\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{b_{r-k}}{a}\big)
is represented by the following matrix

\tilde{\phi}_{a}\Big(\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{b_{1}}{a}\Big),\cdots,\mathrm{d}\Big(\frac{b_{r-k}}{a}\Big)\Big)=\big(u_{1}\otimes 1,\cdots,u_{r}\otimes 1\big)\begin{pmatrix}I_{r-k}\\
M^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

(157)

where
I_{r-k}
is the identity matrix of size
(r-k)\times(r-k)
, and
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})^{k\times(r-k)}
. Then the top
(r-k)\times(r-k)
-minor is

.
∎

In the case when there is one grading weight, when proving
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\tilde{\phi}_{a})=\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})
, we construct
b_{1},\cdots,b_{r-k}\in A
with the following properties:

(1)

b_{1},\cdots,b_{r-k}\in J
, so
\mathrm{d}\big(\frac{b_{j}}{a}\big)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})/\Bbbk}
, the domain of definition of
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
;

(2)

\xi_{i}.b_{j}=\delta_{ij}a
for
1\leq i,j\leq r-k
, which realises

as a sub-matrix of size
(r-k)\times(r-k)
of the matrix representing
\tilde{\phi}_{a}
.

In the general case with multiple grading weights, property
(1)
above,

, becomes much complicated, while property
(2)
and
\mathrm{Fit}_{k-1}(\tilde{\phi}_{i})=0
can be verified essentially the same way. Part of the difficulty of
(1)
is from the difficulty of describing scheme theoretic images. The blow-up centre is such a scheme theoretic image, and the condition

is that
b_{j}
pulls back to a zero function on the centre. Another non-trivial part for
(1)
is to guess what is the form of
b_{j}
in general.

3.1.2
Two grading weights

We focus on the construction of
b_{j}
and the condition

for cases with 2 grading weights. Assume the condition that
\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})\not\subseteq A_{\lambda<0}
.

Let
r_{i}:=\dim\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda=w_{i}}
. Choose a basis
\big(\xi^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
of
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda=w_{i}}

\big(u^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,u^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
be its dual basis on
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda=w_{i}}^{*}
. Let

and let

be the kernel of

.

It is easy to see

is generated by

\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}.f^{(i)}_{1}&\cdots&\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}.f^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}\end{pmatrix}\\

(158)

Recall

\begin{split}\tilde{\phi}_{1,a}:&\Omega_{\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}_{1}^{*}\otimes\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})\\

(159)

where

. Let
b^{(2)}_{\nu}
for

\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
f^{(2)}_{1}&\cdots&f^{(2)}_{r_{2}-k_{2}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\xi^{(2)}_{r_{2}-k_{2}}.f^{(2)}_{1}&\cdots&\xi^{(2)}_{r_{2}-k_{2}}.f^{(2)}_{r_{2}-k_{2}}\end{pmatrix}

where

;

(2)

). Then

M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})^{k_{1}\times(r_{1}-k_{1})}
. Then
1\in\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{1}}(\tilde{\phi}_{1,a})
, i.e.
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{1}}(\tilde{\phi}_{1,a})=\mathcal{O}(\tilde{Y}_{a})
.

The final step is to verify
b^{(1)}_{\nu}\in J
. By Corollary
3.15
, it suffices to check:

•

\xi^{(1)}_{\mu}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}\in\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
for
1\leq\mu\leq r_{1}
, which is seen above;

•

if
w_{1}/w_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, then
\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}\in\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
for
m=w_{1}/w_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and all
1\leq\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{m}\leq r_{1}
.

We have
m\geq 2
since
w_{1}>w_{2}
. When
m=2
, we have

\begin{split}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}=&\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)a^{(2)}\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\Big(\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)+\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\Big)\end{split}

(167)

where we have used that
\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)=0
and
\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}=0
.

Apply equation (
161
) to
\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)a^{(2)}

\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)a^{(2)}=\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big).

(168)

Then

\begin{split}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\big(\big[\xi^{(2)}_{\rho},\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}\big].\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big).\end{split}

(169)

We can exchange
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
to get a similar expression for
\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}
. Then

\begin{split}2\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}=&\big[\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}},\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}\big].b^{(1)}_{\nu}+\big(\xi^{(1)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(1)}_{\mu_{2}}+\xi^{(1)}_{\mu_{2}}\xi^{(1)}_{\mu_{1}}\big).b^{(1)}_{\nu}\\
=&\big[\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}},\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}\big].b^{(1)}_{\nu}\\
&+\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\Big(\big(\big[\xi^{(2)}_{\rho},\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}\big].\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)+\big(\big[\xi^{(2)}_{\rho},\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\big].\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\Big).\end{split}

(170)

Since
w_{1}=2w_{2}
, we have
\big[\xi^{(2)}_{\rho},\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\big]\in\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda=w_{1}}
. We see that
\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{2}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}\in\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
. This proves
b^{(1)}_{\nu}\in J
when
w_{1}=2w_{2}
.

When
w_{1}/w_{2}=m\geq 3
, we have that
\mathfrak{u}
is Abelian, and apply equation (
161
) to get

\begin{split}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}.b^{(1)}_{\nu}=&\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)a^{(2)}\\
&-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\widehat{\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\\
=&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\widehat{\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)a^{(2)}\\
&-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\widehat{\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\\
=&\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\widehat{\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\\
&-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\rho=1}^{r_{2}-k_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\widehat{\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}}\cdots\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{m}}\xi^{(2)}_{\rho}.\beta^{(1)}_{\nu}\big)\big(\xi^{(2)}_{\mu_{j}}.b^{(2)}_{\rho}\big)\\
=&0\in\prod_{i=1}^{2}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i}).\end{split}

(171)

This proves
b^{(1)}_{\nu}\in J
.

In the case with 2 grading weights, the construction is no longer simple. In Lemma
3.9
, the general construction of
b^{(i)}_{\nu}
has to be formulated inductively, and equation (
161
) in the general situation is Lemma
3.8
. The assumption
\prod_{i=0}^{2}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})\not\subseteq A_{\lambda<0}
generalises to Condition
3.2
.

3.2
The Weak Unipotent Upstairs condition

In
[
HJ21
]
, the so-called
Weak Unipotent Upstairs Assumption
(
[
HJ21
]
Assumption 4.42) was assumed for the sequence of blow-ups described in that paper. In this subsection, we describe a similar assumption for our blow-up procedure and call it the
Weak Unipotent Upstairs Condition
as well.

Recall
\hat{U}=U\rtimes_{\lambda}\mathbb{G}_{m}
for a grading 1PS
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Aut}(U)
with grading weights
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{n}
. For a linear action
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
, the open subscheme
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq X
is defined as the non-vanishing locus of
\bigoplus_{d>0}H^{0}(X,L^{d})_{\lambda=\max}
. Recall the notation

\begin{split}&\mathcal{K}_{i}:=\ker\big(\Omega_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}_{i}^{*}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}\big)\\
&\phi_{i}:\mathcal{K}_{i-1}\to(\mathfrak{u}_{i}/\mathfrak{u}_{i-1})^{*}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}\\
&\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{i}):=\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\mathrm{coker}(\phi_{i})).\end{split}

(172)

We call the following condition
Weak Unipotent Upstairs
. The name comes from
[
HJ21
]
Assumption 4.42.

Condition 3.2
.

Let
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
,
\{\mathfrak{u}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
and
\phi_{i}
be as above. Let
k_{i}:=\min\big\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{i})\neq 0\big\}
. This condition for the action
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
assumes that there exists a closed point
z\in Z_{\lambda-\min}
such that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i}}(z)=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Remark 3.3
.

Condition
2.26
implies Condition
3.2
by Corollary
2.9
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\hat{U}\curvearrowright(X,L)
with the grading 1PS
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{Aut}(U)
with weights
w_{1}>\cdots>w_{n}
. Let
\{\mathfrak{u}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
be the filtration of
\mathfrak{u}
such that
\mathfrak{u}_{i}:=\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda\geq w_{i}}
. Let
k_{i}:=\min\big\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{i})\neq 0\big\}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Then for each
1\leq i\leq n
, the following subsets of closed points of
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
are the same:

\begin{split}S^{(1)}_{i}:=&\big\{x\in X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}:x\textrm{ is a closed point in the non-vanishing locus of }\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i^{\prime}}}(\phi_{i^{\prime}})\big\}\\
S^{(2)}_{i}:=&\Big\{x\in X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}:\begin{matrix}x\textrm{ is a closed point and for }i^{\prime}=1,\cdots,i\\
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i^{\prime}}}(x)\leq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i^{\prime}}\end{matrix}\Big\}\\
S^{(3)}_{i}:=&\Big\{x\in X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}:\begin{matrix}x\textrm{ is a closed point and for }i^{\prime}=1,\cdots,i\\
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i^{\prime}}}(x)=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i^{\prime}}\end{matrix}\Big\}.\end{split}

(173)

Moreover the subsets are all non-empty if
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is integral, i.e. reduced and irreducible.

Proof.

If
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is reduced, then the non-vanishing locus of
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})
is an non-empty open subset for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Finite intersections of non-empty open subsets are non-empty if
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is irreducible. This proves
S^{(1)}_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq n
if
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is integral.

We will then prove
S^{(1)}_{i}=S^{(2)}_{i}=S^{(3)}_{i}
by induction on
i=1,\cdots,n
.

Since
X
is of finite type over
\Bbbk
, open subsets are determined uniquely by their closed points (
[
Sta22
,
Lemma 02J6
]
,
[
Sta22
,
Lemma 005Z
]
). We will also denote the non-vanishing locus of
\prod_{i^{\prime}=1}^{i}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i^{\prime}}}(\phi_{i^{\prime}})
by
S^{(1)}_{i}
, which is open in
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
.

Initially consider when
i=1
. Let
x:\mathrm{Spec}(\Bbbk)\to X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
be a closed point in
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
and let
x^{\#}:\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}\to x_{*}(\Bbbk)
be the associated morphism of sheaves of rings. Then
x\not\in S^{(1)}_{1}
if and only if
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{1}}(\phi_{1})\subseteq\ker x^{\#}
, which is equivalent to the condition that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}}(x)>k_{1}
by Lemma
2.7
, i.e.
x\notin S^{(2)}_{1}
. This proves
S^{(1)}_{1}=S^{(2)}_{1}
. Obviously
S^{(3)}_{1}\subseteq S^{(2)}_{1}
. Assume the closed point
x
is in
S^{(2)}_{1}\setminus S^{(3)}_{1}
. Let
l_{1}:=\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}}(x)<k_{1}
. Then
\mathrm{Fit}_{l_{1}}(\phi_{1})=0
since
k_{1}
is the smallest integer such that
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{1})\neq 0
. We have
\mathrm{Fit}_{l_{1}}(\phi_{1})\subseteq\ker x^{\#}
and then
l_{1}=\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}}(x)>l_{1}
by Lemma
2.7
, which is a contradiction. This proves
S^{(2)}_{1}=S^{(3)}_{1}
, so
S^{(1)}_{1}=S^{(2)}_{1}=S^{(3)}_{1}
.

Assume
S^{(1)}_{i}=S^{(2)}_{i}=S^{(3)}_{i}
for some
1\leq i<n
. If
S^{(1)}_{i}=S^{(2)}_{i}=S^{(3)}_{i}=\emptyset
, then
S^{(1)}_{i+1}=S^{(2)}_{i+1}=S^{(3)}_{i+1}=\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, we assume
S^{(1)}_{i}\neq\emptyset
. We have that
\mathrm{coker}(\phi_{i^{\prime}})|_{S^{(1)}_{i}}
is locally free of rank
k_{i^{\prime}}
for each
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq i
by Proposition
2.3
. By Corollary
2.9
, we have that
Q(\mathfrak{u}_{i^{\prime}})|_{S^{(1)}_{i}}
is locally free of rank
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i^{\prime}}
for each
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq i
, where
Q(\mathfrak{u}_{i^{\prime}})
is the cokernel of
\Omega_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}/\Bbbk}\to\mathfrak{u}_{i}^{*}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}
. In particular
Q(\mathfrak{u}_{i})|_{S^{(1)}_{i}}
is flat. Let
x:\mathrm{Spec}(\Bbbk)\to X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
be a closed point. If
x\in S^{(1)}_{i}
, then the condition that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{(\mathfrak{u}_{i+1}:\mathfrak{u}_{i})}(x)\leq k_{i+1}
is equivalent to the condition that
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i+1}}(\phi_{i+1})\not\subseteq\ker x^{\#}
by Lemma
2.7
. Then we have

\begin{split}x\in S^{(2)}_{i+1}\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad&\begin{cases}x\in S^{(1)}_{i}=S^{(2)}_{i}=S^{(3)}_{i}\\
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i+1}}(x)\leq k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}+k_{i+1}\end{cases}\\
\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad&\begin{cases}x\in S^{(1)}_{i}=S^{(2)}_{i}=S^{(3)}_{i}\\
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{(\mathfrak{u}_{i+1}:\mathfrak{u}_{i})}(x)\leq k_{i+1}\end{cases}\\
\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad&\begin{cases}x\in S^{(1)}_{i}=S^{(2)}_{i}=S^{(3)}_{i}\\
\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i+1}}(\phi_{i+1})\not\subseteq\ker x^{\#}\end{cases}\\
\quad\textrm{if and only if}\quad&x\in S^{(1)}_{i+1}.\end{split}

(174)

This proves
S^{(1)}_{i+1}=S^{(2)}_{i+1}
.

Obviously we have
S^{(3)}_{i+1}\subseteq S^{(2)}_{i+1}
. Let
x:\mathrm{Spec}(\Bbbk)\to X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
be a closed point in
S^{(2)}_{i+1}\setminus S^{(3)}_{i+1}
. Then
x\in S^{(3)}_{i}
and
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{(\mathfrak{u}_{i+1}:\mathfrak{u}_{i})}(x)<k_{i+1}
. As above, we have that
Q(\mathfrak{u}_{i})|_{S^{(1)}_{i}}
is flat and
x\in S^{(1)}_{i}
. Let
l_{i+1}:=\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{(\mathfrak{u}_{i+1}:\mathfrak{u}_{i})}(x)<k_{i+1}
. We have that
\mathrm{Fit}_{l_{i+1}}(\phi_{i+1})=0
since
k_{i+1}
is the smallest integer such that
\mathrm{Fit}_{k}(\phi_{i+1})\neq 0
. In particular
\mathrm{Fit}_{l_{i+1}}(\phi_{i+1})\subseteq\ker x^{\#}
. Then
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{(\mathfrak{u}_{i+1}:\mathfrak{u}_{i})}(x)>l_{i+1}
by Lemma
2.7
, which is a contradiction. This proves
S^{(2)}_{i+1}=S^{(3)}_{i+1}
, so
S^{(1)}_{i+1}=S^{(2)}_{i+1}=S^{(3)}_{i+1}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We can define integers
d_{1},\cdots,d_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
by

\begin{split}d_{1}:=&\min\big\{\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{1}}(x):x\in X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\textrm{ is a closed point}\big\}\\
d_{i}:=&\min\Big\{\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i}}(x):\begin{matrix}x\in X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\textrm{ is a closed point and }\\
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i^{\prime}}}(x)=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{i^{\prime}}\textrm{ for }1\leq i^{\prime}<i\end{matrix}\Big\}.\end{split}

(175)

If the sets of closed points described in Lemma
3.4
are non-empty, then
k_{i}=d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. In particular, we can recover
k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}
from the underlying topological spaces when
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is integral.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
k_{i},d_{i},S^{(3)}_{i}
be as above. Condition
3.2
implies the existence of a closed point
z\in Z_{\lambda-\min}
such that
\dim\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathfrak{u}_{i}}(z)=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{i}
for all

, which is the condition in
[

]

.

3.3
Centre of the blow-up

Assume Condition
3.2
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}
. By Lemma
3.4
, Condition
3.2
is equivalent to the condition that
Z_{\lambda-\min}\not\subseteq\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}})
. The blow-up centre
\overline{C}\hookrightarrow X
is a closed subscheme supported on the closure of the
U
-sweep of
Z_{\lambda-\min}\cap\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}})
. In this subsection
\overline{C}\hookrightarrow X
will be defined and its intersection
C
with the open subscheme
X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq X
will be studied locally.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
be such that
H^{0}(X,L^{m})
generates
\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{md})
. Let
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\lambda=\max}
be non-nilpotent. Then
X_{f}\subseteq X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is a non-empty affine open subscheme. On
X_{f}
, let
I_{f}:=\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}(X_{f})+\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{\lambda<0}
. Then

cuts out

. If

, then
\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{\lambda<0}\subsetneq I_{f}
, i.e.
(I_{f})_{\lambda=\max}=(I_{f})_{\lambda=0}
. Else
Z_{\lambda-\min,f}\subseteq\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}})
, and then
I_{f}=\mathcal{O}(X_{f})_{\lambda<0}
. Let
J_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
be the kernel of the composition

\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\to\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\to\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes\big(\mathcal{O}(X_{f})/I_{f}\big)

(176)

where
\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\to\mathcal{O}(U)\otimes\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
is the co-action map of
U\curvearrowright X_{f}
. Let
C_{f}\hookrightarrow X_{f}
be the closed subscheme associated to the ideal
J_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
. The ideals
J_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X_{f})
for
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\lambda=\max}
glue to a sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}}
. The closed subschemes
C_{f}\hookrightarrow X_{f}
glue to a closed subscheme
C\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
, which is the closed subscheme associated to
\mathcal{J}
. Let
\overline{C}\hookrightarrow X
be the scheme theoretic image of
C\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq X
, which is the blow-up centre.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
Z\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
be the scheme theoretic intersection of
Z_{\lambda-\min}\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
and
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}})\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
, where the subscheme structure on
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}})
is associated to
\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}}
. Consider the morphism

, sending a
\Bbbk
-point
(u,z)
to
u.z\in X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
. Then
C\hookrightarrow X^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is the scheme theoretic image of this morphism. By
[
Sta22
,
Lemma 01R8
]
, the closed subset of
C
is the closure of the image, i.e. the
U
-sweep of
Z
. Also by
[
Sta22
,
Lemma 01R8
]
, scheme theoretic images can be constructed locally. Then the scheme theoretic image of

is
C_{f}\hookrightarrow X_{f}
.

Let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be the blow-up of
X
along
\overline{C}\hookrightarrow X
. Let
E\hookrightarrow X
be the exceptional divisor. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, the line bundle
\pi^{*}L^{x}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-yE)
is ample on
\tilde{X}
. Naturally
\hat{U}
acts on
\tilde{X}
linearly with respect to
\pi^{*}L^{x}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-yE)
. Then
(\tilde{X})^{0}_{\lambda-\min}
is defined. Blow-ups are constructed locally. Define the
blowup algebra associated to
J_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X_{f})

\mathrm{Bl}_{J_{f}}\big(\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\big):=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(J_{f})^{k}

(177)

where
(J_{f})^{k}
has degree
k
. For a degree one element

, define the

as the homogeneous localisation at

\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\Big[\frac{J_{f}}{a}\Big]:=\Big(\mathrm{Bl}_{J_{f}}\big(\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\big)\Big)_{(a)}.

(178)

Let
\tilde{X}_{f}\to X_{f}
be the blow-up of
X_{f}
along
C_{f}\hookrightarrow X_{f}
. It has an open affine covering

\tilde{X}_{f}=\bigcup_{a\in J_{f}}\tilde{X}_{f,a},\quad\tilde{X}_{f,a}:=\mathrm{Spec}\Big(\mathcal{O}(X_{f})\Big[\frac{J_{f}}{a}\Big]\Big).

(179)

The open subscheme
(\tilde{X})^{0}_{\min}\subseteq\tilde{X}
is the union of the following affine open subschemes

(\tilde{X})^{0}_{\lambda-\min}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\lambda=\max}\\
a\in(J_{f})_{\lambda=0}\end{subarray}}\tilde{X}_{f,a}.

(180)

The open subscheme
(\tilde{X})^{0}_{\lambda-\min}\subseteq\tilde{X}
is covered by those
\tilde{X}_{f,a}
such that
f\in H^{0}(X,L^{m})_{\lambda=\max}
with
Z_{\lambda-\min,f}\not\subseteq\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}})
and
a\in(J_{f})_{\lambda=0}
of the form
a=a^{(1)}\cdots a^{(n)}
for
a^{(i)}\in\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})(X_{f})_{\lambda=0}
. We focus on one such
\tilde{X}_{f,a}
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
choose a suitable basis
\big(\xi^{(i)}_{1},\cdots,\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}}\big)
of
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda=w_{i}}
and assume without loss of generality that

a^{(i)}=\det\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{(i)}_{1}.f^{(i)}_{1}&\cdots&\xi^{(i)}_{1}.f^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}.f^{(i)}_{1}&\cdots&\xi^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}.f^{(i)}_{r_{i}-k_{i}}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Fit}_{k_{i}}(\phi_{i})(X_{f})_{\lambda=0}

\mathrm{weight}(\xi^{q})=w_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu_{i^{\prime}}=1}^{r_{i^{\prime}}-k_{i^{\prime}}}\binom{p}{q}w_{\max(q)}E^{(i)}_{\mu_{i}}\big(\xi^{(i^{\prime})}_{\mu_{i^{\prime}}}\xi^{p-q}\big)E^{(i^{\prime})}_{\mu_{i^{\prime}}}\big([\xi^{q}]\!]\big)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i^{\prime\prime}>i\\
i^{\prime\prime}\neq i^{\prime}\end{subarray}}a^{(i^{\prime\prime})}\\
&-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i^{\prime}>i\\
0\leq q\leq p\\
\mathrm{weight}(\xi^{q})=w_{i^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\mu_{i^{\prime}}=1}^{r_{i^{\prime}}-k_{i^{\prime}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i^{\prime\prime}>i^{\prime}\\

\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i<i^{\prime\prime\prime}<i^{\prime\prime}\\

(203)

By Lemma

, we have the following equations

(204)

Then for

\begin{split}T_{2,2}=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i^{\prime}>i\\

(205)

There exists

Lemma 3.16
.

If
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,\gamma}\neq 0
, then
|\alpha|\leq|\beta|+|\gamma|
.

Proof.

We prove
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,\gamma}=0
if
|\alpha|>|\beta|+|\gamma|
by induction on
|\alpha|
. When
|\alpha|=1
, we have
|\beta|+|\gamma|=0
, i.e.
\beta=\gamma=0
. Then
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,\gamma}=c^{\alpha}_{0,0}=\delta^{\alpha}_{0}=0
. Assume
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,\gamma}=0
if
k\geq|\alpha|>|\beta|+|\gamma|
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. If
k+1=|\alpha|>|\beta|+|\gamma|
, write
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+e_{i}
for some
|\alpha^{\prime}|=k
and
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

\begin{split}c^{\alpha}_{\beta,\gamma}=&c^{\alpha^{\prime}+e_{i}}_{\beta,\gamma}\\
=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta\\
\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime\prime}=\gamma\end{subarray}}c^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}}.\end{split}

(235)

If there exists a nonzero summand

c^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}\neq 0,\quad c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime}}\neq 0

(236)

then by the induction hypothesis for
|\alpha^{\prime}|=k
and
|e_{i}|=1

|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq|\beta^{\prime}|+|\gamma^{\prime}|,\quad 1\leq|\beta^{\prime\prime}|+|\gamma^{\prime\prime}|

(237)

which implies
|\alpha|=|\alpha^{\prime}|+|e_{j}|\leq|\beta|+|\gamma|
, a contradiction. Then
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,\gamma}=0
as a sum of zeros.
∎

Lemma 3.17
.

If
|\alpha|=|\beta|+1
and
1\leq j\leq n
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}=1+\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}:ke_{j}\leq\beta\}
;

(2)

c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}\neq 0
;

(3)

\alpha=\beta+e_{j}
.

Proof.

We prove the equivalence by induction on
|\alpha|
. When
|\alpha|=1
, we have
\beta=0
and then
c^{\alpha}_{0,e_{j}}=\delta^{\alpha}_{e_{j}}
, which proves the equivalence.

Assume for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
the equivalence is proved for
|\alpha|\leq k
. Let
|\alpha|=k+1
. Obviously
(1)\implies(2)
.

For
(2)\implies(3)
, assume
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}\neq 0
. Write
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+e_{i}
for some
|\alpha^{\prime}|=k
and
1\leq i\leq n
. Then

\begin{split}0\neq c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}=&\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime},e_{j}}c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},0}+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime},0}c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},e_{j}}\\
=&\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime},e_{j}}\delta^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}\delta^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime}}c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},e_{j}}.\end{split}

(238)

If there exist
\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta
such that
c^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime},e_{j}}\delta^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}\neq 0
, and then

\beta^{\prime\prime}=e_{i},\quad|\beta^{\prime}|=|\beta|-|\beta^{\prime\prime}|=k-1.

(239)

So
k=|\alpha^{\prime}|=|\beta^{\prime}|+1
, which implies
\alpha^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}+e_{j}
by the induction hypothesis. So
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+e_{i}=\beta^{\prime}+e_{j}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta+e_{j}
, which is
(3)
.

Else there exist
\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta
such that
\delta^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{\beta^{\prime}}c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},e_{j}}\neq 0
, and then
\alpha^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}
. In particular
|\beta^{\prime\prime}|=|\beta|-|\beta^{\prime}|=0
, i.e.
\beta^{\prime\prime}=0
. Then
c^{e_{i}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},e_{j}}=\delta^{e_{i}}_{e_{j}}\neq 0
, i.e.
e_{i}=e_{j}
. We have
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}+e_{i}=\beta^{\prime}+e_{j}=\beta+e_{j}
, which is
(3)
.

For
(3)\implies(1)
, assume
\alpha=\beta+e_{j}
. Then

\begin{split}c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}=&c^{\beta+e_{j}}_{\beta,e_{j}}\\
=&\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\beta}_{\beta^{\prime},e_{j}}c^{e_{j}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},0}+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\beta}_{\beta^{\prime},0}c^{e_{j}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},e_{j}}\\
=&\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\beta}_{\beta^{\prime},e_{j}}\delta^{e_{j}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}\delta^{\beta}_{\beta^{\prime}}c^{e_{j}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime},e_{j}}\\
=&\sum_{\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta}c^{\beta}_{\beta^{\prime},e_{j}}\delta^{e_{j}}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}}+1.\end{split}

(240)

If
e_{j}\leq\beta
, then

c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}=c^{\beta}_{\beta-e_{j},e_{j}}+1

(241)

and
c^{\beta}_{\beta-e_{j},e_{j}}=1+\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}:ke_{j}\leq\beta-e_{j}\}=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}:ke_{j}\leq\beta\}
by the induction hypothesis, which proves
(1)
. Else
e_{j}\not\leq\beta
, then
c^{\alpha}_{\beta,e_{j}}=1
, which is
(1)
.
∎

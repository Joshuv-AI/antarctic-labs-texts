On nonparametric estimation of the interaction function in particle system models
Denis Belomestny 1, Mark Podolskij 2, Shi-Yuan Zhou 3

Abstract
. This paper delves into a nonparametric estimation approach for the interaction function within diffusion-type particle system models. We introduce two estimation methods based upon an empirical risk minimization. Our study encompasses an analysis of the stochastic and approximation errors associated with both procedures, along with an examination of certain minimax lower bounds. In particular, we show that there is a natural metric under which the corresponding minimax estimation error of the interaction function converges to zero with parametric rate. This result is rather suprising given complexity of the underlying estimation problem and rather large classes of interaction functions for which the above parametric rate holds. Keywords: drift estimation, McKean-Vlasov diffusions, non-linear SDEs, nonparametric statistics, particle systems. AMS 2010 subject classification: 62G20,62M05, 60G07, 60H10.



1.
Introduction

This article is dedicated to the study of
N
-dimensional interacting particle systems described by the equation

(1)

dX_{t}^{i,N}=(\varphi\star\mu_{t}^{N})(X_{t}^{i,N})\,dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

where
t\in[0,T],

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the interaction potential and
(W^{i})_{i=1}^{N}
are independent one-dimensional Brownian motions.
Here,
\mu_{t}^{N}
stands for the empirical measure of the particle system at time
t
, given by

(2)

\mu_{t}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{i,N}},

and
\varphi\star\mu(x):=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x-y)\mu(dy)
.
We make the assumption that we observe

N
paths
(X_{t}^{i,N},t\in[0,T],i=1,\ldots,N)
and aim to estimate the unknown interaction function
\varphi
as
N\to\infty
with
T>0
being fixed. It is noteworthy that, given complete path observations, the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
can be considered known.

Interacting particle systems of the type
Eq.

1
play an important role in probability theory and applications. Initially introduced by McKean in pioneering work
[
34
]
, these systems served as models for plasma dynamics. In more recent years, diffusion-type interacting particle systems have found wide-ranging applications in finance
[
9
,
20
,
21
,
26
,
27
]
, social science
[
12
]
, neuroscience
[
4
]
, and population dynamics
[
35
]
, among others. From a probabilistic perspective, the particle system delineated in
Eq.

1
is inherently linked to its corresponding mean-field limiting equation—a one-dimensional McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation (SDE),

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dX_{t}=(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma dB_{t},\\
\textnormal{Law}(X_{t})=\mu_{t},\end{array}\right.

where
B
is a
1
-dimensional Brownian motion. Under suitable assumptions on the function
\varphi
, a well-known phenomenon known as the
propagation of chaos
emerges. This phenomenon indicates that, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, the empirical probability measure
\mu_{t}^{N}
weakly converges to
\mu_{t}
for any
t>0
, and the McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equation (SDE)
Eq.

3
characterizes the asymptotic trajectory of an individual particle. Classical treatments of the propagation of chaos and McKean-Vlasov SDEs can be found in the monographs
[
31
,
40
]
. Furthermore, the measure

\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure for all
t>0
, assuming certain regularity conditions on
\varphi
.
Moreover, the density of
\mu_{t}
, denoted again by
\mu_{t}

(with a slight abuse of notation), satisfies the granular media equation:

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=\partial_{x}\{(\sigma^{2}/2)\partial_{x}\mu_{t}+(\varphi\star\mu_{t})\mu_{t}\}.

The realm of statistical inference for interacting particle systems remains a less explored domain. While a considerable number of recent articles delve into parametric estimation for particle systems and McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations under diverse model and sampling assumptions (see, for instance,
[
1
,
7
,
13
,
16
,
18
,
24
,
25
,
30
,
33
,
38
,
44
]
), non- and semiparametric estimation of the interaction potential
\varphi
presents a notably more intricate challenge. This complexity arises from the convolution with measures
\mu_{t}^{N}
or
\mu_{t}
and has only been the subject of investigation in three recent papers. Specifically, the articles
[
2
,
5
]
explore the semiparametric estimation of
\varphi
given observations
X_{T}^{i,N},i=1,\ldots,N,
with
N,T\to\infty
. The methodology in
[
2
,
5
]
heavily relies on the existence of the invariant density, necessitating the asymptotic regime
T\to\infty,
and employs the deconvolution method.
Another relevant work,
[
17
]
, focuses on the same observation scheme as the current paper, presenting a nonparametric approach for the drift function
\varphi\star\mu_{t}
but demonstrating consistency only for an estimator of the interaction function
\varphi
.

This paper aims to investigate nonparametric estimation of the function
\varphi
using the sampling scheme
X_{t}^{i,N},t\in[0,T],i=1,\ldots,N,
with
N\to\infty
and
T
held fixed. We propose two interconnected methods, both based on empirical risk minimization over sieves
(S_{m})_{m\geq 1}
, employing an unconventional underlying loss function
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
that depends on the unknown measure
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.
Our first approach addresses the scenario where the functional spaces
(S_{m})_{m\geq 1}
are compact
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. In this case, the analysis of the proposed estimator’s rate heavily relies on empirical processes theory for (degenerate) U-statistics.
The second approach investigates a more traditional setting, where

(S_{m})_{m\geq 1}
are finite-dimensional vector spaces. Here, we require a further truncation of the empirical risk minimizer, akin to the approach introduced in
[
15
]
for classical stochastic differential equations, where the coefficients are independent of the laws
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
. We derive convergence rates with respect to the loss function
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
, involving an examination of stochastic and approximation errors. We will demonstrate that the resulting convergence rate is often
\sqrt{\log(N)/N}

in the first scenario, which essentially matches the minimax lower bound. However, for the second method, the convergence rate decreases due to the truncation procedure.
Additionally,
we will establish that the minimax lower bound for the traditional
L^{2}
-loss is logarithmic, consistent with the findings of
[
5
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
concentrates on a risk minimization approach over compact functional classes. This section elucidates the core concepts, establishes convergence rates in terms of metric entropy, and analyses the approximation error associated with the estimation method. In Section
3
, we introduce an alternative approach that involves risk minimization over vector spaces coupled with an additional dimension truncation. Uniform convergence rates for the resulting estimator are presented. Section
4
delves into an exploration of minimax lower bounds. Specifically, we demonstrate that the estimator developed in Section
2
attains essentially optimal rates with respect to the
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
-norm. Additionally, we establish a logarithmic lower bound when considering the traditional
L^{2}
-loss. For the detailed proofs of the main results, we refer to Section
5
.

For two sequences

C>0

.
The Orlicz norm of a real valued random variable
\eta
with respect to a nondecreasing, convex

\psi
on
\mathbb{R}

\psi(0)=0
is defined by

\|\eta\|_{\psi}:=\inf\left\{t>0:\mathbb{E}\psi\bigl(|\eta|/t\bigr)\leq 2\right\}.

When

with

the corresponding Orlicz norm is (up to a constant) the

-norm

\|\eta\|_{p}=(\mathbb{E}[|\eta|^{p}])^{1/p}.

We say that

is

if
\|\eta\|_{\psi_{e,2}}<\infty
with
\psi_{e,2}:=\exp(x^{2})-1.
In particular,
this implies that for some constants
C,c>0
,

\mathbb{P}(|\eta|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{ct^{2}}{\|\eta\|_{\psi_{e,2}}^{2}}\right)\text{\ \ and \ \ }\mathbb{E}[|\eta|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq C\sqrt{p}\|\eta\|_{\psi_{e,2}},\quad p\geq 1.

Consider a real valued random process
(X_{t})_{t\in\mathcal{T}}
on a metric

(\mathcal{T},\mathsf{d})
. We say that the process has

if there exists
C>0

\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|_{\psi_{e,2}}\leq C\mathsf{d}(t,s),\quad\forall t,s\in\mathcal{T}.

Let
(\mathsf{Y},\rho)

\mathsf{X\subseteq Y}
. For

\varepsilon>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}(\mathsf{X},\rho,\varepsilon)
the

\mathsf{X}
with respect to the metric

,

-balls in

that covers
\mathsf{X}
. Then
\log\mathcal{N}(\mathsf{X},\rho,\varepsilon)
is called the

of
\mathsf{X}
and

\mathsf{DI}(\mathsf{X},\rho,\psi):=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\operatorname{diam}(\mathsf{X})}\psi^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(\mathsf{X},\rho,u)\bigr)\,du

\operatorname{diam}(\mathsf{X}):=\max_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathsf{X}}\rho(x,x^{\prime}),
is called the Dudley

For example, if
|\mathsf{X}|<\infty
and

we get
\mathsf{DI}(\mathsf{X},\rho,\psi_{e,2})=\sqrt{\log|\mathsf{X}|}.

2.
Risk minimization over compact functional classes

We start by introducing assumptions on the underlying interacting particle system that insure the propagation of chaos phenomenon. We assume that the initial law of the model is given by
\textnormal{Law}(X_{0}^{1,N},\ldots,X_{0}^{N,N})=\mu_{0}^{\otimes N}
, where
\mu_{0}^{\otimes N}
denotes the
N
-fold product measure of some probability measure
\mu_{0}
with

\intop\nolimits x^{k}\mu_{0}(dx)<\infty
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Furthermore, we assume the following condition.

Assumption 1
.

The interaction function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is globally Lipschitz continuous and bounded, that is,

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq L_{\varphi}|x-y|,\quad|\varphi(x)|\leq K_{\varphi},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}

for some finite
L_{\varphi},K_{\varphi}>0.

This condition guarantees the validity of propagation of chaos in the sense that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\mu_{t}^{N}
converges weakly to
\mu_{t}
(see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
).

2.1.
Construction of the estimator and main results

We initiate our exposition by outlining the fundamental principles of our estimation methodology. To begin, we consider a sequence of spaces
(S_{m})_{m\geq 1}
(sieves). The crucial idea of our approach lies in the following minimization strategy:

(5)

\min_{f\in S_{N}}\frac{1}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left(f\star\mu_{t}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N})-\varphi\star\mu_{t}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N})\right)^{2}dt.

However, the above risk function cannot be directly computed from the data since the interaction function
\varphi
is unknown. We derive an empirical (noisy) version of the minimization problem by omitting the irrelevant term
\varphi\star\mu_{t}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N})^{2}
in the integrand and minimizing the resulting quantity:

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}(f):=\frac{1}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\left(\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}f\star\mu_{t}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N})^{2}\,dt-2\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}f\star\mu_{t}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N})\,dX_{t}^{i,N}\right)

over
S_{N}
. For further analysis, we introduce the following bilinear forms:

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}(f\star\mu_{t}^{N})(X_{t}^{i,N})(g\star\mu_{t}^{N})(X_{t}^{i,N})\,dt,

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{\star}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(f\star\mu_{t})(x)(g\star\mu_{t})(x)\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt.

We set
\|f\|_{N}^{2}:=\langle f,f\rangle_{N}
and
\|f\|_{\star}^{2}:=\langle f,f\rangle_{\star}
. With these notations at hand, we deduce the identity

(6)

\|f-\varphi\|_{N}^{2}=\gamma_{N}(f)+\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)+\|\varphi\|_{N}^{2}

where
\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)
is the martingale term defined via

\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f):=\frac{2\sigma}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}(f\star\mu_{t}^{N})(X_{t}^{i,N})\,dW_{t}^{i}.

Finally, our estimator
\varphi_{N}
is defined as follows:

(7)

\varphi_{N}:=\text{argmin}_{f\in S_{N}}\gamma_{N}(f),\qquad\varphi_{\star}:=\text{argmin}_{f\in S_{N}}\|f-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}.

The identity (
6
) means that by minimizing
\gamma_{N}(f)
we minimize (up to a martingale term
\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)
)
\|f-\varphi\|_{N}
which is close to
\|f-\varphi\|_{\star}
for
N
large enough.

Before we proceed with the asymptotic analysis, we give some remarks about a rather unconventional loss function
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
and its relation to the classical
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-norm.

Remark 2.1
.

The upcoming analysis will reveal that the risk
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
is the intrinsic norm for evaluating error bounds. Unlike conventional literature on empirical risk minimization, it is crucial to note that this norm is elusive since the laws
\mu_{t}
remain unobserved. This presents one of the principal mathematical challenges in our statistical analysis. ∎

Remark 2.2
.

The norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
can be bounded from above by the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-norm. Indeed,
if we assume that the densities
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfy
\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\infty}\,dt<{\infty}
,
we conclude by the Young’s convolution inequality that

\|f\|_{\star}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\infty}\|f\star\mu_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,dt\leq\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\|\mu_{t}\|_{\infty}\,dt.

Nonetheless, these two norms are far from being equivalent. To illustrate this point, we assume, for simplicity, that
\mu_{t}=\mu
represents the density of the standard normal distribution for all
t
. Now, consider the function
f(x)=1_{[2M,3M]}(x)
for some
M>0
. In this scenario, it is evident that
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=M
. On the other hand, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\star}^{2}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{[-M,M]}(f\star\mu)^{2}(x)\mu(x)\,dx+\intop\nolimits_{[-M,M]^{c}}(f\star\mu)^{2}(x)\mu(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}M^{2}).

In particular,
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\to\infty
while
\|f\|_{\star}\to 0
as
M\to\infty
at an exponential rate.
∎

To formulate our main statements, we assume that the spaces
(S_{m})_{m\geq 1}

are compact with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}

with
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\varphi}
for
f\in S_{m}
.
In certain cases, we employ the notation
S_{m}(K_{\varphi})
to explicitly express the dependency of spaces on the parameter
K_{\varphi}
. We assume that the following property holds:

f,g\in S_{m}(K_{\varphi})\implies f\pm g\in S_{m}(2K_{\varphi}).

Subsequently, we establish the bounds for the error term
\|\varphi_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}.

Theorem 2.3
.

It holds under Assumption
1
,

\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\varphi_{N}-\varphi\|^{q}_{\star}\bigr]\Bigr\}^{1/q}\leq\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}+C\left(\frac{K_{\varphi}\,\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{e,2})}{\sqrt{N}}\right.\\
\left.+\frac{p^{2}K_{\varphi}\,\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{p/2})}{N}+\frac{p^{3}K_{\varphi}\,\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{p/3})}{N^{3/2}}\right)

for any
p>2,

2\leq q\leq p,
where
C>0
is an absolute constant and
\varphi^{\star}
stands for the best approximation of
\varphi
as defined in (
7
).

Let us outline the key concepts behind the proof of Theorem
2.3
. We commence with the inequality:

(8)

\|\varphi_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\leq\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}+2\left(\sup_{f\in S_{N}}\left|\|f-\varphi\|_{N}^{2}-\|f-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\right|+\sup_{f\in S_{N}}|\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)|\right).

This inequality decomposes the estimation error into an approximation error
\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}
and stochastic errors related to the last two terms. To handle the stochastic errors, we initially consider i.i.d. observations from the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
3
). Remarkably, the term
\|f-\varphi\|_{N}^{2}-\|f-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}
turns out to be a linear combination of (degenerate) U-statistics, while
\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)
represents a martingale, as noted earlier. Concentration inequalities are applied to both terms, leading to the moment bound in Theorem
2.3
via the metric entropy. In the final step of the proof, we employ a change of measure device initially proposed in
[
17
]
to transfer the statement from i.i.d. observations of the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
3
) to observations of the original particle system (
1
).
In case we have a bound on the covering number of the space
S_{N}
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{N}\bigl(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\varepsilon\bigr)\lesssim\varepsilon^{-D_{N}}
for all
N
and an increasing sequence of positive numbers
D_{N}
satisfying
D^{2}_{N}N^{-1/2}\to 0
as
N\to\infty.
Then it holds

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\varphi_{N}-\varphi\|^{q}_{\star}\bigr]\Bigr\}^{1/q}\leq\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}+CK_{\varphi}\,\sqrt{\frac{D_{N}}{N}}.

Our focus now shifts to examining the approximation error
\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}
, which will be addressed in the subsequent analysis.

2.2.
Approximation error

In this segment, we delve into an examination of the approximation error outlined in Theorem
2.3
. The strong contractivity inherent in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
introduces a surprising characteristic wherein the error
\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}
often exhibits exponential decay in the dimensionality of the space
S_{N}
.
In the following, we adopt the subsequent assumption regarding the initial distribution:

Assumption 2
.

Assume that the initial distribution is normal, that is,

\mu_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\zeta^{2}}}\exp(-x^{2}/(2\zeta^{2}))

for some
\zeta>0.

We also consider a condition on the interaction function
\varphi
:

Assumption 3
.

There is a monotone increasing sequence
(\alpha_{n})
with

\alpha_{n}\to\infty,\quad\alpha_{n+1}/\alpha_{n}\to 1,\quad n\to\infty

such that the following representation holds

\displaystyle(\mu_{t}\star\varphi)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\exp(i\pi kx/\alpha_{n})\widehat{\mu}_{t}(-k/\alpha_{n}),\quad x\in[-\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n}]

with coefficients
c_{k}=c_{k}(\alpha_{n})
satisfying
\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|\lesssim K_{\varphi}\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}.

Remark 2.5
.

Let us examine Assumption
3
. This assumption holds for any function
\varphi
, which admits a Fourier series representation of the form:

(9)

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\exp(i\pi kx/\alpha),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

with absolutely summable coefficients
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
satisfying
\sumop\displaylimits_{k}|a_{k}|\leq\sqrt{\alpha}K_{\varphi}
and some
\alpha>0.
This type of periodic potentials appear in McKean-Vlasov equations arising in physics, see e.g. the Kuramoto-Shinomoto-Sakaguchi model in
[
23
]
. Indeed, for such functions we readily deduce the identity

(\mu_{t}\star\varphi)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\exp(i\pi kx/\alpha)\widehat{\mu}_{t}(-k/\alpha).

Taking, for instance,
\alpha_{n}=\alpha n
and comparing the coefficients,
we obtain the statement of Assumption
3
with
c_{kn}=a_{k}\sqrt{n}
and
c_{l}=0
for
l\notin\{kn:~k\geq 0\}
.
∎

Next,
for any
A>1,

D\in\mathbb{N},
we consider the functional space

S(A,D):=\left\{x\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{D}c_{k}\exp(i\pi kx/A):\quad\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{D}|c_{k}|\leq\sqrt{A}K_{\varphi}\right\}.

The main result of this subsection is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

Set with arbitrary small
\delta>0,

A_{N}:=\sqrt{2(\zeta^{2}+T)\log(N)},\qquad D_{N}:=\sqrt{(2+\delta)\frac{\zeta^{2}+T}{\zeta^{2}}}\log(N)

and define
n_{N}:=\min\{n:\alpha_{n}\geq A_{N}\},

N\in\mathbb{N}.

Then under Assumptions
2
and
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\inf_{g\in S_{N}=S(\alpha_{n_{N}},D_{N})}\|g-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\lesssim\frac{\exp(K_{\varphi}^{2}T)}{N}.

As a consequence, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\varphi_{N}-\varphi\|^{2}_{\star}\bigr]\Bigr\}^{1/2}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\exp(K_{\varphi}^{2}T)}{N}}+K_{\varphi}\,\sqrt{\frac{\log(N)}{N}}.

Here
\lesssim
stands for inequality up to an absolute constant.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.6
, the resulting convergence rate is established as
\sqrt{\log(N)/N}
. We will demonstrate in Section
4
that this rate is essentially optimal. However, it is important to note, as highlighted in Remark
2.2
, that this does not necessarily imply a polynomial convergence rate with respect to the classical
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
-norm. In fact, we will establish in Section
4
that the minimax lower bound for the
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
-norm is logarithmic, consistent with the findings of
[
5
]
.

Remark 2.7
.

Theorem
2.6
suggests that the approximation error decays exponentially when dealing with functions of the form described in
Eq.

9
. However, this phenomenon is not exclusive to the Fourier basis. Remarkably, a similar result can be observed for the polynomial basis. The forthcoming explanation sheds light on the fast rates of approximation in the
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
-norm.
Suppose that
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then we have by the Parseval identity

(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(x+iy)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\exp(iux-uy)\widehat{\varphi}(u)\widehat{\mu}_{t}(-u)\,du.

Under Assumption
2
,
|\widehat{\mu}_{t}(u)|\leq\exp(-u^{2}\zeta^{2}/2)
(see
Eq.

35
)
and hence

\displaystyle|(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(x+iy)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits\exp(-uy)|\widehat{\mu}_{t}(u)|\,du

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\zeta^{2}}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\exp\left(y^{2}/(2\zeta^{2})\right).

Thus

\max_{|z|=r}|(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(z)|\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\zeta^{2}}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\exp\left(r^{2}/(2\zeta^{2})\right),\quad r>0.

As a result, the function
\varphi\star\mu_{t}
is entire of order
2
and finite type. Furthermore, we deduce the power series expansion

(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(z)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}z^{n}

with
c_{n}\leq\left(e/\zeta^{2}n\right)^{n/2}.
This implies the inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[-A,A]}\left|(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(x)-\sumop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{D}c_{n}x^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{D,A}=\sumop\displaylimits_{n=D+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{eA^{2}}{\zeta^{2}n}\right)^{n/2}

Note that

\varepsilon_{D,A}\leq 2e^{-(1+D)/2}

provided
(1+D)^{-1}A^{2}\leq\zeta^{2}/e^{2}.
Let
T_{K}[f]=1_{\{|f(x)|\leq K\}}f(x)+K\mathrm{sign}(f(x))

be a truncation operator at level
K
. Then it holds that

\inf_{\psi\in\mathrm{span}\{z^{n},n\leq D\}}\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits\left((\mu_{t}\star\varphi)(x)-T_{K_{\varphi}}[\psi\star\mu_{t}](x)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt\leq\varepsilon_{D,A}^{2}+R_{T}(A)

with

R_{T}(A)=\frac{4K_{\varphi}^{2}}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{|x|>A}\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt.

Here we used the fact that for any
\psi\in\mathrm{span}\{z^{n}:~n\leq D\}
it holds
\psi\star\mu_{t}\in\mathrm{span}\{z^{n}:~n\leq D\}.

Similarly to the case of Fourier basis (see proof of Theorem
2.6
), we get

R_{T}(A)\leq 8K_{\varphi}^{2}\exp\left(K_{\varphi}^{2}T/2\right)\exp\left(-\frac{A^{2}}{2(\zeta^{2}+T)}\right).

If we chose
A_{N}=\sqrt{2(\zeta^{2}+T)\log(N)}
and
D_{N}=\frac{2e^{2}(\zeta^{2}+T)}{\zeta^{2}}\log(N)
,
then

\inf_{\varphi\in\mathrm{span}\{z^{n}:~n\leq D_{N}\}}\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits\left((\mu_{t}\star\varphi)(x)-T_{K_{\varphi}}[\psi\star\mu_{t}](x)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt\lesssim\frac{\exp\left(K_{\varphi}^{2}T/2\right)}{N}.

In other words, we obtain an exponential decay of the approximation error for the polynomial basis as well. ∎

3.
Risk minimization over vector spaces

While the empirical minimization approach presented in the preceding section serves as a valuable estimation method, it is not without its limitations. Firstly, the construction of the estimator
\varphi_{N}
in (
7
) inherently assumes knowledge of the constant
K_{\varphi}
. Secondly, a significant drawback lies in the necessity of the compactness property of functional spaces
S_{m}
with respect to the sup-norm, a condition that is notably stringent.

3.1.
Setting and construction of the estimator

This section focuses on a more traditional setting of risk minimization within vector spaces. We assume that the finite-dimensional spaces

S_{N}
are equipped with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,
and
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{D_{N}})
represents an orthonormal basis of
S_{N}
satisfying
\|e_{j}\|_{\infty}<\infty
for all
j=1,\ldots,D_{N}
.

Due to the vector space structure, the estimator
\varphi_{N}
introduced at (
7
) can be computed explicitly as

(10)

\varphi_{N}=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{D_{N}}\left(\theta_{N}\right)_{j}e_{j},

where
\theta_{N}={}_{N}^{-1}Z_{N}
and the random vectors
{}_{N},\ Z_{N}
, taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{D_{N}\times D_{N}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{D_{N}}
respectively, are given by

\displaystyle({}_{N})_{jk}

\displaystyle=\langle e_{j},e_{k}\rangle_{N},

(11)

\displaystyle(Z_{N})_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}e_{j}\star\mu_{t}^{N}(X_{t}^{i,N})\,dX_{t}^{i,N}.

There is a theoretical counterpart to the empirical matrix
N
. Indeed, we have that

\mathbb{E}{{}_{N}}\approx\Psi
where the matrix
\Psi
is given by the formula

(12)

(\Psi)_{jk}=\langle e_{j},e_{k}\rangle_{\star}.

Obviously, both matrices are positive semidefinite by construction.
In the next step, we will regularise the estimator
\varphi_{N}
. For a given sequence
D_{N}
, the operator norm
\|{}_{N}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}
may become too large compared to the sample size
N
and we would like to avoid such situations. For this purpose, we restrict the growth of
\|{}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}.

Assumption 4
.

Let
\eta\geq 5
be a given number. We assume that
N
is invertible almost surely and the sequence
D_{N}
is such that the following growth condition is satisfied:

L_{N}^{2}\|{}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\leq\frac{c_{\eta,T}NT}{4\log(NT)},

where
L_{N}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{D_{N}}\|e_{j}\|^{2}_{\infty}
and
c_{\eta,T}:=(72\eta T)^{-1}
.

We now consider the regularised version of the initial estimator
\varphi_{N}
introduced at
Eq.

7
:

(13)

\widehat{\varphi}_{N}=\varphi_{N}1_{\left\{L_{N}^{2}\|{}_{N}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\leq\frac{c_{\eta,T}NT}{\log(NT)}\right\}}

An analogous methodology was suggested in
[
15
]
within the framework of classical stochastic differential equations. Nevertheless, the probabilistic analysis of
\widehat{\varphi}_{N}
becomes notably more intricate due to the sophisticated structure of the model. Moreover, unlike the analysis in
[
15
]
, the theoretical quantity
\|{}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}
in Assumption
4
proves challenging to control due to the absence of information about
\mu_{t}
. We will discuss this condition in details in Section
3.3
.

3.2.
Main results

This section is devoted to the asymptotic analysis of the estimator
\widehat{\varphi}_{N}
.
We start by introducing two random sets, which will be crucial for our proofs. We define

(14)

\Lambda_{N}:=\left\{L_{N}^{2}\|{}_{N}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\leq\frac{c_{\eta,T}NT}{\log(NT)}\right\},\qquad{}_{N}:=\left\{\left|\frac{\|f\|_{N}^{2}}{\|f\|_{\star}^{2}}-1\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}~\forall f\in S_{N}\right\}.

We recall that
\Lambda_{N}
reflects the cut-off introduced in
Eq.

13
. On the other hand, on

N
the norms
\|\cdot\|_{N}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
are equivalent, i.e.

\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\star}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{N}^{2}1_{{}_{N}}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|f\|_{\star}^{2}.

Our first theoretical statement shows that, under
4
,
\mathbb P(\Lambda_{N})
and
\mathbb P({}_{N})
approach
1
as
N\to\infty
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
4
are satisfied.

(i)

There exists
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq n
:

\mathbb P\left(\left\|{}^{-1/2}{}_{N}{}^{-1/2}-I_{D_{N}}\right\|_{\text{\rm op}}>\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq D_{N}^{k/4}\exp\left(-\frac{kc_{\eta,T}\eta NT}{16L_{N}^{2}\|{}^{-1}\|_{\text{\rm op}}^{2}}\right),

where
I_{D_{N}}
denotes the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{D_{N}\times D_{N}}
.

(ii)

It holds that

\mathbb P(\Lambda_{N}^{c})\leq\mathbb P({}_{N}^{c})\leq(NT)^{-\eta+1}.

The concentration bound in
3.1
(i) is key in understanding the asymptotic behaviour of the estimator
\widehat{\varphi}_{N}
.
The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
4
are satisfied. Then we obtain uniform bounds

(15)

\sup_{\varphi}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\widehat{\varphi}_{N}-\varphi\|_{N}^{2}\right]\leq\sup_{\varphi}\mathbb{E}\left[\inf_{f\in S_{N}}\|f-\varphi\|_{N}^{2}\right]+\frac{CD_{N}}{NT}

as well as

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\widehat{\varphi}_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+o(1)\right)\sup_{\varphi}\inf_{f\in S_{N}}\|f-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle+CN^{-1/2}(1+L_{N})\sup_{\varphi}\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star\star}^{2}+\frac{CD_{N}}{NT},

where the supremum is taken over the class of functions
\Phi
defined as

\Phi:=\left\{\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}:~\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq K_{1},~K_{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\text{\rm Lip}}\leq K_{3}\right\}

for fixed constants
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}>0
. Here the norm
\|f\|_{\star\star}
is defined as

\|f\|_{\star\star}^{2}:=\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}f^{2}\star\mu_{t}(x)\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt.

We remark that
\|f\|_{\star}\leq\|f\|_{\star\star}
. However, it does not hold a priori that
\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star\star}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. We will discuss this term in the next subsection.

3.3.
Checking Assumption
4
and bounding the norm

\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star\star}

The primary challenge in implementing the theoretical findings from the preceding section lies in selecting an appropriate dimension
D_{N}
, which ensures the fulfillment of Assumption 4. This requirement is essentially equivalent to determining an upper bound for the quantity
\|{}^{-1}\|_{\operatorname{op}}
. In this context, we introduce an approach to establish an upper bound on the operator norm
\|{}^{-1}\|_{\operatorname{op}}
, guided by a Gaussian-type condition on the densities
\mu_{t}
.
We introduce the following condition on
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
:

Assumption 5
.

We suppose that the densities
\mu_{t}
are symmetric and there exist numbers
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and functions
g_{2}\in L^{2}([0,T])
,
g_{1}g^{2}_{2}\in L^{1}([0,T])
such that

\mu_{t}(x)\geq g_{1}(t)\exp(-x^{2}/c_{1})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{\mu}_{t}(x)\geq g_{2}(t)\exp(-x^{2}/c_{2}).

Remark 3.3
.

The lower bound for the densities
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
can be deduced via

[
37
, Theorem 1]
. Indeed, under Assumption
2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\intop\nolimits{R}\mu_{0}(y)\intop\nolimits_{\frac{\left|x-y\right|}{\sqrt{t}}}^{\infty}z\exp\left(-\frac{(z+K_{\varphi}\sqrt{t})^{2}}{2}\right)\,dz\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\exp\left(-K_{\varphi}^{2}t\right)}{2\pi\sqrt{t\zeta^{2}}}\intop\nolimits{R}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2\zeta^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{t}\right)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left(-K_{\varphi}^{2}t\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi(\zeta^{2}+t/2)}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2\zeta^{2}+t}\right).

Obtaining lower bounds for the Fourier transforms
(\widehat{\mu}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a more delicate problem. Some partial results in this direction have been demonstrated in
[
2
, Theorem 5.4]
in the ergodic setting.
∎

We consider the vector space
S_{N}
generated by functions

(e_{k})_{1\leq k\leq D_{N}}
with

e_{k}(x)=(2A_{N})^{-1/2}\exp(i\pi kx/A_{N}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

which is an orthonormal system in
L^{2}([-A_{N},A_{N}])
. For this choice of basis we obtain the identity

(17)

\displaystyle{}_{kl}=\langle e_{k},e_{l}\rangle_{\star}=\frac{1}{2A_{N}T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\widehat{\mu}_{t}(-k/A_{N})\widehat{\mu}_{t}(l/A_{N})\widehat{\mu}_{t}((l-k)/A_{N})dt.

We deduce the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that Assumption
5
holds. Assume that
D_{N}=a_{D}\log(N)
and
A_{N}=a_{A}\sqrt{\log(N)}
for some
a_{D},a_{A}>0
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|{}^{-1}\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq v_{T}N^{\frac{2a_{D}^{2}}{c_{2}a_{A}^{2}}+\frac{a_{A}^{2}}{c_{1}}}(1+o(1)),

where
v_{T}:=2\sqrt{2\pi}T\left(\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}g_{1}(t)g_{2}(t)^{2}dt\right)^{-1}
.

In the next step we will study the norm
\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star\star}
. For this purpose, we assume that
\varphi
has the representation

\varphi(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}e_{k}(x)

with coefficients
c_{k}=c_{k}(N)
satisfying the condition
\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|<\infty
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that Assumption
2
is satisfied. Then it holds that

\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star\star}^{2}\lesssim\left(1+A_{N}^{-1}D_{N}^{4}\|{}^{-1}\|_{\text{\rm op}}^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{3D_{N}^{2}\zeta^{2}}{2A_{N}^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=D_{N}+1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|\right)^{2}.

Now, we can combine the statements of Theorem
3.2
, Proposition

3.4
and Proposition
3.5
, to derive the convergence rate for
\|\widehat{\varphi}_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}
.

For clarity, we focus on the ergodic scenario where

\mu_{t}=\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
satisfies Assumption
2
. In this context,
the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
from Assumption
5
are explicitly given by

c_{1}=2\zeta^{2},\qquad c_{2}=\frac{2}{\zeta^{2}}.

As
\|e_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}=A_{N}^{-1}
and
D_{N}=a_{D}\log(N)
,
A_{N}=a_{A}\sqrt{\log(N)}
, Proposition
3.4
implies the following bound:

L_{N}^{2}\|{}^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{a_{D}^{2}}{a_{A}^{2}}\log(N)N^{\frac{2a_{D}^{2}\zeta^{2}}{a_{A}^{2}}+\frac{a_{A}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}}.

Hence, for Assumption
4
to hold true, the constants
a_{A}
and
a_{D}
need to fulfill

\frac{2a_{D}^{2}\zeta^{2}}{a_{A}^{2}}+\frac{a_{A}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}<1.

On the other hand, exploring the proof of Theorem

2.6
, the approximation error is obtained as

\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\lesssim N^{-\frac{a_{D}^{2}\zeta^{2}}{a_{A}^{2}}}+N^{-\frac{a_{A}^{2}}{2\zeta^{2}}}.

Consequently, if we choose
a_{D}^{2}\zeta^{2}/a_{A}^{2}=a_{A}^{2}/(2\zeta^{2})=(1-\epsilon)/4
for a small
\epsilon>0
, we finally conclude that

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\widehat{\varphi}_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\right]\lesssim N^{-1/4+\epsilon}

provided the condition
(\sumop\displaylimits_{k=D_{N}+1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|)^{2}\leq CN^{-z}
with

z>5/8
holds.
The substantial decrease in the convergence rate, as observed compared to Theorem
2.6
, directly stems from the stringent constraint imposed by Assumption
4
. Still this is the first result in literature regarding the convergence rates of the linear-type estimates within the context of McKean-Vlasov Stochastic Differential Equations.

4.
Lower bounds

This section is dedicated to deriving minimax lower bounds. We start by establishing a lower bound for the previously examined estimation problem concerning the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
. To achieve this, we focus on a simplified scenario involving i.i.d. observations drawn from a McKean-Vlasov SDE:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dX_{t}=(\varphi\star\mu_{t})(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma dB_{t},\\
\textnormal{Law}(X_{t})=\mu_{t},\end{array}\right.

and denote by
\mathbb P_{\varphi}
the associated probability measure. We assume that
X_{0}^{\varphi}=x\in\mathbb{R}
for all
\varphi
.
The ensuing theorem establishes a minimax lower bound with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(19)

\inf_{\widehat{\varphi}_{N}}\sup_{\varphi}\mathbb P^{\bigotimesop\displaylimits N}_{\varphi}\left(\|\widehat{\varphi}_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}>cN^{-1/2}\right)>0,

where
\mathbb P^{\bigotimesop\displaylimits N}_{\varphi}
is the
N
-fold product measure, the supremum is taken over all functions

\varphi
satisfying Assumption
3
and
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq(32T)^{-1/2}
, and the infimum is taken over all estimators of
\varphi
retrieved from
N
observations of
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

Proof.

Consider two functions
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in S
and denote by
\mu_{t}^{0}
and
\mu_{t}^{1}
the corresponding marginal densities. The Kullback-Leibler divergence
\text{KL}(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}})
between the probability measures

\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}}
and
\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}}
can be explicitly computed as

\displaystyle\text{KL}(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\varphi_{0}\star\mu_{t}^{0}(x)-\varphi_{1}\star\mu_{t}^{1}(x)\right)^{2}\mu_{t}^{0}(x)\,dx\,dt.

As a consequence we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\text{KL}(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}})

\displaystyle\leq T\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{1}\|_{\star}^{2}+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}^{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left[\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mu_{t}^{0}(x)-\mu_{t}^{1}(x)\right|\,dx\right]^{2}\,dt,

where the
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
-norm is computed with respect to
\mu_{t}^{0}
.
Furthermore, using Theorem 1.1 of
[
8
]
, we derive

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left[\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mu_{t}^{0}(x)-\mu_{t}^{1}(x)\right|\,dx\right]^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq 4\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left[\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\intop\nolimits(\varphi_{0}\star\mu_{s}^{0}(x)-\varphi_{1}\star\mu_{s}^{1}(x))^{2}\mu_{s}^{0}(x)\,dx\,ds\right]\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 8T\text{KL}(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}}).

Since
\|\varphi_{1}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq(16T)^{-1}
we finally deduce that

\text{KL}(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}})\leq 2T\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{1}\|_{\star}^{2}.

Applying the two hypotheses method described in
[
42
, Theorem 2.2]
, we obtain the statement of the theorem.
∎

We will now consider the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-norm instead of
\|\cdot\|_{\star}
and compare the results to

[
5
]
. We recall that logarithmic rates have been obtained in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-norm in
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
for a similar estimation problem given the observation scheme
(X_{T}^{i,N})_{1\leq i\leq N}
with
N,T\to\infty
. It turns out that these rates cannot be improved to polynomial ones in their framework, and the same holds in our case (note however that polynomial rates may appear under a different set of conditions as it has been demonstrated in
[
2
]
). To see this, we again consider simplified setting of i.i.d. observations drawn from McKean-Vlasov SDE

Eq.

3
. Furthermore, we consider a similar model as presented in
[
5
]
: Let
W:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be an even and strictly convex interaction potential of the form

W(x)=p(x)+\beta(x),\qquad p(x)=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{J}a_{j}x^{2j},

where
(a_{j})_{j=1,\dots,J}
are known constants and
\beta\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The potential
W
determines the interaction function
\varphi
via
\varphi=W^{\prime}
and we are interested in the estimation of the non-parametric part
\beta
in the case of stationarity. The invariant law
\mu
solves the elliptic nonlinear Fokker-Planck equation

(20)

\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textnormal{d}^{2}}{\textnormal{d}x^{2}}+\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}x}\left(\varphi\star\mu\right)\right]\mu=0,

meaning that
\mu
is given by the implicit equation

(21)

\mu(x)=c_{\mu}\exp\left(-W\star\mu(x)\right),

where
c_{\mu}
is a normalising constant such that
\mu
is a probability density.

To introduce the appropriate class of functions, we consider
C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-wavelets. More specifically, we let the mother wavelet be a symmetric function

\psi\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\text{supp}(\psi)=[-1/2,1/2]
and, for
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

\psi_{n,k}(x)=2^{n/2}\psi(2^{n}x-k).

The set
(\psi_{n,k})_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
forms a basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Now, for
\alpha,\ K>0
, we consider the functional space

\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{K}=\left\{\beta^{\prime}:~\beta\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}),\ \sumop\displaylimits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sumop\displaylimits_{|k|>m}\langle\beta^{\prime},\psi_{n,k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq Km^{-\alpha}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

For the estimation of the nonparametric part
\beta^{\prime}
of
\varphi
based on i.i.d. observations of
Eq.

3
we obtain the following lower bound.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that the coefficients
(a_{j})_{1\leq j\leq J}
and the function

\beta
satisfy the conditions of
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(22)

\inf_{\widehat{\beta}^{\prime}_{N}}\sup_{\beta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{K}}\mathbb P^{\bigotimesop\displaylimits N}_{\varphi}\left(\|\widehat{\beta}^{\prime}_{N}-\beta^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}>c\left(\log N\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{4J}}\right)>0,

where
\mathbb P^{\bigotimesop\displaylimits N}_{\varphi}
is the
N
-fold product measure and the infimum is taken over all estimators of
\beta^{\prime}
retrieved from
N
observations of
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.

Proof.

We will use the two hypotheses method described in
[
42
, Theorem 2.2]
. We will find two hypotheses of interaction forces
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{K}
such that, for some constant
c>0
,

\left\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=c\left(\log N\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{4J}}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{KL}\left(\mathbb P^{\bigotimesop\displaylimits N}_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P^{\bigotimesop\displaylimits N}_{\varphi_{1}}\right)\lesssim 1.

The Radon-Nikodym density of
\textnormal{d}\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}}/\textnormal{d}\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}}
can be computed using Girsanov’s theorem: denoting the stationary marginals as
\mu_{i}
,
i=0,1
, we have for
X\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\textnormal{d}\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}}}{\textnormal{d}\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}}}(X)\right)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}\star\mu_{1}(X_{t})-\varphi_{0}\star\mu_{0}(X_{t})\textnormal{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}\star\mu_{1}(X_{t})^{2}-\varphi_{0}\star\mu_{0}(X_{t})^{2}\textnormal{d}t,

such that

\displaystyle\text{KL}\left(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits{R}\log\left(\frac{\textnormal{d}\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}}}{\textnormal{d}\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}}}(x)\right)\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2}\intop\nolimits{R}\left(\varphi_{1}\star\mu_{1}(x)-\varphi_{0}\star\mu_{0}(x)\right)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x.

In a similar manner as in
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
, we can show that

\text{KL}\left(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}}\right)\leq\gamma^{2}\text{KL}\left(\mathbb P_{\varphi_{1}},\mathbb P_{\varphi_{0}}\right)+\frac{T}{2}\intop\nolimits{R}(\beta^{\prime}_{0}-\beta^{\prime}_{1})\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x,

with
\gamma\in(0,1)
. So, it remains to show the estimate

(23)

\intop\nolimits{R}(\beta^{\prime}_{0}-\beta^{\prime}_{1})\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x\lesssim\frac{1}{N}.

Now we will choose the two hypotheses. Let
\rho>0
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
be rescaling parameters to be determined later. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
l_{n}:=\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor
and define

f=\rho\sumop\displaylimits_{\left|n\right|,\left|k\right|=M}^{2M}\psi_{l_{n},k}.

Note that our choice of basis elements implies that
\operatorname{supp}f\subset[-2M^{2},2M^{2}]
and
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\rho^{2}M^{2}
. We will consider the hypotheses
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{K}
and
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{0}+f
. In order for
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{F}^{\alpha}_{K}
to hold, we need
d(f,S_{m})\leq Km^{-\alpha}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There are two cases to consider for this, namely

\rho^{2}\sumop\displaylimits_{n_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sumop\displaylimits_{\left|k_{1}\right|>m}\sumop\displaylimits_{\left|n_{2}\right|,\left|k_{2}\right|=M}^{2M}\left<\psi_{n_{1},k_{1}},\psi_{{l_{n_{2}}},k_{2}}\right>_{2}^{2}\begin{cases}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}=\rho^{2}M^{2},&m\leq l_{2M},\\
=0,&m>l_{2M}.\end{cases}

Hence, we require the condition
\rho^{2}M^{2}\lesssim\lfloor\log_{2}(2M)\rfloor^{-\alpha}
. We enforce a stricter condition

\rho^{2}=O(M^{-\alpha-2}).

This means we choose

\left\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\rho^{2}M^{2}=M^{-\alpha}.

We will now verify that
Eq.

23
holds. The main ingredient is the observation that, for
i=0,1
,

(24)

\mu_{i}(x)\lesssim\exp\left(-a_{J}x^{2J}\right),

which in particular implies
\|\mu_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty
. By symmetry of
\mu_{i}
and
f
, it suffices to show

(25)

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}f\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x\lesssim\frac{1}{N}.

Let us split the integral at some
k>0
which we determine later. The tail behaviour is governed by
Eq.

24
: Using Cauchy-Schwarz, we have
\|f\star\mu_{0}\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lesssim\rho^{2}M^{2}
, such that

\intop\nolimits_{k}^{\infty}f\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x\lesssim\rho^{2}M^{2}\frac{\exp\left(-k^{2J}\right)}{k^{2J-1}}.

For the remaining integral, we bound
\mu_{1}
such that

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{k}f\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\lesssim k\sup_{x\in[0,k]}f\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}.

We recall that
\operatorname{supp}f=[-2M^{2},2M^{2}]
, which implies

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[0,k]}\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{x-2M^{2}}f(x-y)\mu_{0}(y)\textnormal{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{k-2M^{2}}\mu_{0}(y)^{2}\textnormal{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{2}M^{2}\frac{\exp\left(-(2M^{2}-k)^{2J}\right)}{(2M^{2}-k)^{2J-1}}.

From this, we see that by choosing
k=M^{2}
, the entire integral is indeed bounded by

\intop\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}f\star\mu_{0}(x)^{2}\mu_{1}(x)\textnormal{d}x\lesssim\exp\left(-M^{4J}\right),

such that choosing
M=c\left(\log N\right)^{\frac{1}{4J}}
with some constant
c>0
gives
Eq.

25
, which in turn gives us the rate

\left\|\varphi_{0}-\varphi_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=CM^{-\alpha}=C\left(\log N\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{4J}},

which implies the claim of the theorem.
∎

5.1.
Proof of Theorem

In the first part of the proof we consider the setting of i.i.d. observations drawn from the mean field limit

3

\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}

We recall the inequality

(26)

\|\varphi_{N}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\leq\|\varphi_{\star}-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}+2\left(\sup_{f\in S_{N}}\left|\|f-\varphi\|_{N}^{2}-\|f-\varphi\|_{\star}^{2}\right|+\sup_{f\in S_{N}}|\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)|\right),

where
\varphi_{\star}
has been introduced in
Eq.

7
.
Next, we set
\gamma^{\prime}_{N}(f)=:2M_{N}(f)/\sqrt{NT}
where for all
f\in S

M_{N}(f)
is a martingale with quadratic variation

\frac{\sigma^{2}}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}[(f\star\mu_{t}^{N})(X_{t}^{i,N})]^{2}dt\leq\sigma^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{\infty}.

Hence, we deduce by the Bernstein inequality for continuous martingales,

\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}(|M_{N}(f)-M_{N}(g)|\geq a)\leq 2\exp\left(-a^{2}/(\sigma^{2}\|f-g\|^{2}_{\infty})\right)

for any
a>0
and
f,g\in S_{N}
. Since for all
q\geq 1
, the
L^{q}(\Omega)

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\psi_{e,2}}

[
32
, Theorem 8.4]
implies

(27)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sup_{f\in S_{N}}\left|M_{N}(f)-M_{N}(f_{0})\right|\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\mathsf{DI}(S_{N},\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{e,2})

for any
f_{0}\in S_{N}
. Consider now the difference

\|g\|_{N}^{2}-\|g\|_{\star}^{2}
for a function
g\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})
. We obtain the decomposition

(28)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{N}^{2}-\|g\|_{\star}^{2}=U_{1,N}(g)+U_{2,N}(g)+U_{3,N}(g)+O(N^{-1})

where
\Delta_{t}(x,y):=g(x-y)-(g\star\mu_{t})(x),
and

\displaystyle U_{1,N}(g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{N}\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{j,N})\right)(g\star\mu_{t})(x)\mu_{t}(x)dxdt

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}(g\star\mu_{t})(X_{t}^{i,N})^{2}dt-\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(g\star\mu_{t})(x)^{2}\mu_{t}(x)dxdt\right),

\displaystyle U_{2,N}(g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{N(N-1)(N-2)}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{j,k\neq i,j\neq k}\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{j,N})\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{k,N})\mu_{t}(x)dxdt

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j\neq i}\Delta_{t}(X_{t}^{i,N},X_{t}^{j,N})(g\star\mu_{t})(X_{t}^{i,N})dt

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{j\neq i}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{j,N})(g\star\mu_{t})(x)\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle U_{3,N}(g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{N(N-1)(N-2)}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{j,k\neq i,j\neq k}\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\Delta_{t}(X_{t}^{i,N},X_{t}^{j,N})\Delta_{t}(X_{t}^{i,N},X_{t}^{k,N})\,dt

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N(N-1)(N-2)}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{j,k\neq i,j\neq k}\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{j,N})\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{k,N})\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\,dt.

The key observation is that each
U_{r,N}
is a degenerate
U
-statistics of order
r
for
r=1,2,3
, while

U_{1,N}
is the leading term.
First, turn to
U_{1,N}
and note that

\displaystyle U_{1,N}(g)=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{N}\left(Z_{j}(g)-\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}Z_{j}(g)\right)

with

\displaystyle Z_{j}(g)=\frac{1}{T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left(2\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta_{t}(x,X_{t}^{j,N})(g\star\mu_{t})(x)\mu_{t}(x)\,dx\right)+(g\star\mu_{t})(X_{t}^{i,N})^{2}\,dt.

It holds that

|Z_{j}(g)|\leq 8K_{\varphi}^{2},\quad|Z_{j}(g)-Z_{j}(g^{\prime})|\leq 4K_{\varphi}\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

with probability
1
for
g,g^{\prime}\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}).
Hence,

\|Z_{j}(g)-Z_{j}(g^{\prime})\|_{\psi_{e,2}}\lesssim K_{\varphi}\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

This implies that the process
\widetilde{U}_{1,N}=\sqrt{N}U_{1,N}
has subgaussian increments and

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g)-\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g^{\prime})\|_{\psi_{e,2}}\lesssim K_{\varphi}\|g-g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}.

Fix
some
g_{0}\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})
. By the triangle inequality,

\sup_{g\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g)|\leq\sup_{g,g^{\prime}\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g)-\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g^{\prime})|+|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g_{0})|.

By the Dudley integral inequality, see
[
43
, Theorem
8.1.6]
, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we conclude that

\sup_{g,g^{\prime}\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g)-\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g^{\prime})|\lesssim K_{\varphi}\bigl[\mathsf{DI}(S_{N},\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{e,2})+\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}\bigr]

holds with probability at least
1-\delta
. Applying Hoeffding’s inequality,

[
43
, Theorem 2.6.2.]
, we have for any

\delta\in(0,1)
,

|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g_{0})|\lesssim K_{\varphi}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)},

with probability at least
1-\delta
. In other words, for any

,

\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}\left(\sup_{g\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}|\widetilde{U}_{1,N}(g)|>u\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{(u-K_{\varphi}\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{e,2}))^{2}}{2K_{\varphi}^{2}}\right).

Putting things together we conclude that

(29)

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\Bigl[\sup_{g\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}|U_{1,N}(g)|^{q}\Bigr]\Bigr\}^{1/q}\lesssim\frac{qK_{\varphi}\,\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{e,2})}{\sqrt{N}}.

Furthermore, due to Theorem
A.1
,

(30)

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\Bigl[\sup_{g\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}\left|NU_{2,N}(g)\right|^{q}\Bigr]\Bigr\}^{1/q}\lesssim p^{2}K_{\varphi}\,\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{p/2})

(31)

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\Bigl[\sup_{g\in S_{N}(2K_{\varphi})}\left|N^{3/2}U_{3,N}(g)\right|^{q}\Bigr]\Bigr\}^{1/q}\lesssim p^{3}K_{\varphi}\,\mathsf{DI}(S_{N}(2K_{\varphi}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{p/3})

for any
p>2
and

This implies the statement of Theorem
2.3

in the setting of i.i.d. observations drawn from the McKean-Vlasov SDE

Eq.

3
.

Now, we will transfer the result from i.i.d. observations to observations of the original particle system at
Eq.

1
via a change of measure device established in
[
17
]
.
Recalling the definition of probability measures
\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}
and
\mathbb P^{N}

\frac{\textnormal{d}\mathbb P^{N}}{\textnormal{d}\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}=\mathcal{E}_{T}(\overline{M}^{N})

where
\mathcal{E}_{t}(\overline{M}^{N})=\exp\bigl(\overline{M}_{t}^{N}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\overline{M}^{N}\rangle_{t}\bigr)

\overline{M}_{t}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\sigma^{-1}\left[\varphi\star(\mu_{t}^{N}-\mu_{t})(\overline{X}_{t}^{i})\right]\,dW_{t}^{i}

\sup_{N\geq 1}\sup_{t\in[0,T-\delta]}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[\exp\left(\tau\left(\langle\overline{M}^{N}\rangle_{t+\delta}-\langle\overline{M}^{N}\rangle_{t}\right)\right)\right]\leq C_{\delta,\tau}

\tau>0,

C_{\delta,\tau}>0

\xi

\mathcal{F}_{T}

(33)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb P^{N}}[|\xi|^{r}]=\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}[|\xi|^{r}\mathcal{E}_{T}(\overline{M}^{N})]\leq\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}[|\xi|^{2r}]\right\}^{1/2}\left\{\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}[\mathcal{E}_{T}^{2}(\overline{M}^{N})]\right\}^{1/2}.

Next, fix a grid

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}[\mathcal{E}_{T}^{2}(\overline{M}^{N})]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\prodop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{K}\exp\Bigl(2(\overline{M}_{t_{k}}^{N}-\overline{M}_{t_{k-1}}^{N})+\langle\overline{M}^{N}\rangle_{t_{k-1}}-\langle\overline{M}^{N}\rangle_{t_{k}}\Bigr)

repeatedly applying the Hölder inequality and

As a consequence the bounds

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\frac{1}{NT}\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\bar{e}_{j}\star\delta_{x(t)}(x_{i}(t))\bar{e}_{k}\star\delta_{x(t)}(x_{i}(t))\textnormal{d}t\right)_{1\leq j,k\leq D_{N}}

where
\delta_{x(t)}=\frac{1}{N}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{j}(t)}
. We have that

\displaystyle{}^{-1/2}\overline{\Psi}_{N}{}^{-1/2}-{}^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[\overline{\Psi}_{N}\right]{}^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=H\left(\overline{X}^{1,N},\ldots,\overline{X}^{N,N}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[H\left(\overline{X}^{1,N},\ldots,\overline{X}^{N,N}\right)\right].

For
x=\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\right)\in\left(C([0,T],\mathbb{R})\right)^{N}
we denote by
\tilde{x}
the same vector where the
l
-th entry is replaced by
{x_{l}}^{\prime}
. We need to study
\Delta H_{jk}:=\left(H(x)-H(\tilde{x})\right)_{jk}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\Delta H_{jk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{TN^{3}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i,r_{1},r_{2}=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\bar{e}_{j}(x_{i}(t)-x_{r_{1}}(t))\bar{e}_{k}(x_{i}(t)-x_{r_{2}}(t))

\displaystyle-\bar{e}_{j}(\tilde{x}_{i}(t)-\tilde{x}_{r_{1}}(t))\bar{e}_{k}(\tilde{x}_{i}(t)-\tilde{x}_{r_{2}}(t))dt.

If either none or all of the indices are equal to
l
, the difference vanishes. Therefore, there are at most
3N(N-1)
non-zero terms. Consequently, for any
1\leq l\leq N
, we can bound the difference as

|\Delta H_{jk}|\leq\frac{6}{N}\left\|\bar{e}_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\bar{e}_{k}\right\|_{\infty}.

Moving on to the spectral norm of
\Delta H
, we have for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{N}}
:

\displaystyle y^{\top}\Delta H^{2}y

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{j,k=1}^{D_{N}}y_{j}y_{k}\sumop\displaylimits_{l=1}^{D_{N}}\Delta H_{jl}\Delta H_{kl}\leq\frac{36}{N^{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{j,k=1}^{D_{N}}|y_{j}y_{k}|\sumop\displaylimits_{l=1}^{D_{N}}\left\|\bar{e}_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\bar{e}_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\bar{e}_{l}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{36}{N^{2}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{D_{N}}\left\|\bar{e}_{j}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{2}\left\|y\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{36}{N^{2}}L_{N}^{2}\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}\left\|y\right\|^{2}.

This means

A_{l}^{2}:=\frac{36}{N^{2}}L_{N}^{2}\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}I_{D_{N}}

satisfies the condition
A_{l}^{2}\succcurlyeq\Delta H^{2}
for all
1\leq l\leq N
. We also define

\nu^{2}:=\frac{36}{N}L_{N}^{2}\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}
. Now we apply the concentration inequality of
5.2
and deduce the statement
Eq.

41
, which completes this part of the proof.

(ii) To finish the proof of
3.1
(i) in the independent case we observe that

\displaystyle\left\|{}^{-1/2}\overline{\Psi}_{N}{}^{-1/2}-I_{D_{N}}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{}^{-1/2}\overline{\Psi}_{N}{}^{-1/2}-{}^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[\overline{\Psi}_{N}\right]{}^{-1/2}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle+\left\|{}^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[\overline{\Psi}_{N}\right]{}^{-1/2}-I_{D_{N}}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}.

Thus we only need to show the convergence

(42)

\left\|{}^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[\overline{\Psi}_{N}\right]{}^{-1/2}-I_{D_{N}}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.

Since the particles are exchangeable and independent, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[({\overline{\Psi}_{N}})_{jk}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{TN^{3}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i,r_{1},r_{2}=1}^{N}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[e_{j}(\overline{X}_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{X}_{t}^{r_{1},N})e_{k}(\overline{X}_{t}^{i,N}-\overline{X}_{t}^{r_{2},N})\right]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{TN^{2}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left((N-1)^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[e_{j}\star\mu_{t}(\overline{X}^{1,N}_{t})e_{k}\star\mu_{t}(\overline{X}^{1,N}_{t})\right]+e_{j}(0)e_{k}(0)\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.(N-1)\left(\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[e_{j}\star\mu_{t}(\overline{X}^{1,N}_{t})\right]e_{k}(0)+\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[e_{k}\star\mu_{t}(\overline{X}^{1,N}_{t})\right]e_{j}(0)\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{N^{2}}{}_{jk}+\frac{e_{j}(0)e_{k}(0)}{N^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{N-1}{TN^{2}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[e_{j}\star\mu_{t}(\overline{X}^{1,N}_{t})\right]e_{k}(0)+\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[e_{k}\star\mu_{t}(\overline{X}^{1,N}_{t})\right]e_{j}(0)\right)dt.

Hence, we deduce that

(43)

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\overline{\mathbb P}^{N}}\left[({\overline{\Psi}_{N}})_{jk}\right]-{}_{jk}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\left((I_{D_{N}})_{jk}+\left\|e_{j}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|e_{k}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

Now, the spectral norm can be bounded through
4
:

(44)

\displaystyle\left\|I_{D_{N}}-{}^{-1/2}\mathbb{E}{\overline{\Psi}_{N}}{}^{-1/2}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\left(1+\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\left(\sumop\displaylimits_{j,k=1}^{D_{N}}\left\|e_{j}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|e_{k}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L_{N}\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}}{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}\log(N)}.

This gives the claim
Eq.

42
and thus the validity of

3.1
(i) in the independent case.

(iii) On
\Lambda_{N}^{c}
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle c_{\eta,T}\frac{NT}{\log(NT)}

\displaystyle\leq L_{N}^{2}\left(\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}+\left\|{}^{-1}-\overline{\Psi}_{N}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2L_{N}^{2}\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}+2L_{N}^{2}\left\|{}^{-1}-\overline{\Psi}_{N}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}.

So, if
4
holds, we deduce that

\left\|{}^{-1}-\overline{\Psi}_{N}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}\geq\frac{c_{\eta,T}}{4L_{N}^{2}}\frac{NT}{\log(NT)}\geq\left\|{}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2},

and
[
14
, Proposition 4 (ii)]
yields the claim.
∎

\operatorname{II}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\widehat{\varphi}_{N}-\varphi\right\|_{\star}^{2}1_{\Lambda_{N}\cap{}_{N}^{c}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\varphi_{N}\right\|_{\star}^{2}1_{\Lambda_{N}\cap{}_{N}^{c}}\right]+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\star}^{2}\mathbb P\left({}_{N}^{c}\right).

Recalling the definition of
\varphi_{N}
given in
Eq.

10
and that
(Z_{N})_{j}=(E_{N})_{j}+\langle e_{j},\varphi\rangle_{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi_{N}\right\|^{2}_{\star}

\displaystyle=\left({}_{N}^{-1}Z_{N}\right)^{\top}\Psi{}_{N}^{-1}Z_{N}\leq\left\|{}_{N}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\left\|Z_{N}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{}_{N}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\left(\left\|\left<e,\varphi\right>_{N}\right\|^{2}+\left\|E_{N}\right\|^{2}\right).

Since
\varphi
is bounded,

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{D_{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left<e_{j},\varphi\right>_{N}^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(NT)^{2}}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{D_{N}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[e_{j}\star\mu^{N}_{t}(X^{1,N}_{t})^{4}\varphi\star\mu^{N}_{t}(X^{1,N}_{t})^{4}\right]\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{D_{N}}\frac{\left\|e_{j}\right\|^{4}_{\infty}}{(NT)^{2}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi\star\mu^{N}_{t}(X^{1,N}_{t})^{4}\right]\textnormal{d}t\leq\frac{L_{N}^{2}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\infty}^{4}}{(NT)^{2}},

such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\left\|\left<e,\varphi\right>_{N}\right\|^{2}+\left\|E_{N}\right\|^{2}\right)1_{\Lambda_{N}\cap{}_{N}^{c}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\left<e,\varphi\right>_{N}\right\|^{4}\right]^{1/2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|E_{N}\right\|^{4}\right]^{1/2}\right)\mathbb P\left({}_{N}^{c}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\infty}^{2}L_{N}}{NT}+\frac{\sqrt{D}_{N}L_{N}}{NT}\right)(NT)^{-\frac{\eta-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\sqrt{D_{N}}}+1\right)L_{N}(NT)^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}.

Finally, with
4
and
5.3
, and the fact that
\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\leq L_{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\varphi_{N}\right\|_{\star}^{2}1_{\Lambda_{N}\cap{}_{N}^{c}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{}_{N}^{-1}\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}^{2}\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\operatorname{op}}\left(\left\|\left<e,\varphi\right>_{N}\right\|^{2}+\left\|E_{N}\right\|^{2}\right)1_{\Lambda_{N}\cap{}_{N}^{c}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c_{\eta,T}\left(\frac{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\infty}^{2}}{\sqrt{D}_{N}}+1\right)(NT)^{-(\eta-1)/2}.

Last but not least, we have that

\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{II}_{3}]\lesssim\mathbb P(\Lambda_{N}^{c})

which completes the proof due to Proposition
3.1
.

5.5.
Proof of
3.4

Let us denote by
\lambda_{\min}(Q)
the smallest eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{N}\times D_{N}}
. Since

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(Q_{1}Q_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\min}(Q_{1})\lambda_{\min}(Q_{2})\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}\left(T^{-1}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}Q_{t}dt\right)

\displaystyle\geq T^{-1}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\lambda_{\min}(Q_{t})dt,

for all positive semi-definite matrices
Q_{1},Q_{2},(Q_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we obtain via
Eq.

17
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\min}(\Psi)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2A_{N}T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}\min_{k=1,\ldots,D_{N}}(\widehat{\mu}_{t}(k/A_{N}))^{2}\lambda_{\min}(R_{t})dt

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\exp\left(-2D_{N}^{2}/(c_{2}A_{N}^{2})\right)}{2A_{N}T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}g_{2}^{2}(t)\lambda_{\min}(R_{t})dt

where
R_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{N}\times D_{N}}
is a Toeplitz matrix given as
R_{t}^{kl}=\widehat{\mu}_{t}((l-k)/A_{N})
. To deduce a lower bound for

\lambda_{\min}(R_{t})
, we introduce the discrete-time Fourier transform

F(\omega):=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mu}_{t}(n/A_{N})\exp(-i\omega n),\qquad\omega\in[-\pi,\pi].

A well-known result states that

\lambda_{\min}(R_{t})\geq\min_{\omega\in[-\pi,\pi]}F(\omega),

and hence we need to compute
F(\omega)
. For this purpose, we will apply
the Poisson summation formula. Define the function
f(x):=\widehat{\mu}_{t}(x/A_{N})\exp(-i\omega x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{F}f(z):=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\exp(-i2\pi zx)dx
. The Poisson summation formula states that

F(\omega)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)=\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}f(n).

By the Fourier inversion formula we obtain the identity

\mathcal{F}f(n)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\mu}_{t}(x/A_{N})\exp\left(-ix(\omega+2\pi n)\right)dx=\frac{A_{N}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mu_{t}\left(A_{N}(\omega+2\pi n)/\pi\right).

Due to Assumption
5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle F(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{A_{N}g_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sumop\displaylimits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{A_{N}^{2}(\omega+2\pi n)^{2}}{c_{1}\pi^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{A_{N}g_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{A_{N}^{2}}{c_{1}}\right)(1+o(1)).

Putting things together, we conclude that

\lambda_{\min}(\Psi)\geq\frac{\exp\left(-2D_{N}^{2}/(c_{2}A_{N}^{2})\right)\exp\left(-A_{N}^{2}/c_{1}\right)}{2\sqrt{2\pi}T}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{T}g_{1}(t)g_{2}(t)^{2}dt.

This implies the statement of Proposition
3.4
.

Consider the decomposition

In the next step we will find a bound for the term

Due to Assumption
2
, the leading order of the above term is achieved for

and

|\langle\bar{e}_{j},\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\star}|\lesssim D_{N}\|{}^{-1}\|^{1/2}_{\text{op}}\exp\left(-\frac{3D_{N}^{2}\zeta^{2}}{4A_{N}^{2}}\right)\sumop\displaylimits_{j=D_{N}+1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|

Appendix A
Maximal inequalities for
U
-statistics

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of real-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{K}
and
(U_{N}(g):

g\in\mathcal{G})
be
U
-process of the form

\displaystyle U_{N}(g)=\frac{1}{(N)_{K}}\sumop\displaylimits_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{K}=1}^{N}g(X_{i_{1}},\ldots,X_{i_{K}})

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
is a sequence of iid random variables with common distribution
\mathbb P
and
(N)_{K}=N(N-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(N-K+1).
If for each
g\in\mathcal{G},

\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathbb P}[g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},X,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{K})]=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,K,

for arbitrary
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{K}\in\mathbb{R},
then the process
(U_{N}(g),

g\in\mathcal{G})
is called degenerate.

Theorem A.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a class of uniformly bounded functions with
\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\|g\|_{\infty}\leq G
and the
U
-process
(U_{N}(g),

g\in\mathcal{G})
is degenerate. Then it holds

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\mathbb{E}\sup_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\left|N^{K/2}U_{N}(g)\right|^{q}\Bigr\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim Gp^{K}\,\mathsf{DI}(\mathcal{G},\|\cdot\|_{\infty},\psi_{p/K})

for any
p\geq q.

Proof.

We can apply the maximal inequality from
[
39
, Section 5]
and note that for uniformly bounded classes
\mathcal{G}

d_{U_{N}}:=\bigl[U_{N}\bigl(|f-g|^{2}\bigr)\bigr]^{1/2}\leq\|f-g\|_{\infty}

as well as
U_{N}\bigl(|g|^{2}\bigr)\leq G^{2}
with probability
1
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{G}.

∎

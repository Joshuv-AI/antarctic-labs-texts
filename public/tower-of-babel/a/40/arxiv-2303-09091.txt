The families Clifford index and differential KO-theory
Daniel Berwick-Evans

Abstract
. Extending ideas of Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro and Quillen, we construct a model for differential KO {\rm KO} -theory whose cocycles are families of Clifford modules with superconnection. The model is built to accommodate an analytic pushforward for bundles of spin manifolds, affording a differential refinement of Atiyah and Singer’s families index.



1.
Introduction

This paper gives a model for differential
{\rm KO}
-theory in degree
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
that generalizes a generators and relations description of
\widehat{\rm K}{}^{0}
, differential complex K-theory in degree 0.

For a smooth manifold
M
, generators for
\widehat{\rm K}{}^{0}(M)
are triples
(V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi)
where
V\to M
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector bundle with hermitian metric,
{\mathbb{A}}
is a hermitian superconnection, and
\phi
is an odd differential form on
M
.
These data are subject to an equivalence relation involving a Chern–Simons form; see §
7.1
for details.
At first pass, generators for
\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{n}(M)
replace
{\mathbb{C}}
by the real Clifford algebra
{\rm Cl}_{n}
: consider
(V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi)
where
V
is a real, metrized vector bundle with self-adjoint
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-action,
{\mathbb{A}}
is a
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-linear metric-preserving connection on
V
, and
\phi
is a differential form in degrees
n-1
mod 4. The appropriate equivalence relation on these data generalizes the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro equivalence relation on Clifford modules
[
ABS64
]
, see also Donovan–Karoubi
[
DK70
]
. Roughly, triples
(V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi)
and
(V^{\prime},{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
are equivalent when there is an inclusion
g\colon V\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}
of metrized vector bundles together with the data of a
{\rm Cl}_{n}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{-1}
-action on the orthogonal complement
V^{\perp}\subset V^{\prime}
, plus a condition on a Chern–Simons form. The main subtlety is that this equivalence relation needs to be implemented locally on
M
, meaning an element of
\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{n}(M)
is given by an open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
with
(V_{\alpha},{\mathbb{A}}_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
over each
U_{\alpha}
and equivalences
g_{\alpha\beta}
for each
U_{\alpha}\bigcap U_{\beta}
satisfying a cocycle condition that refines the one in the Čech–de Rham complex; §
6
and §
7
for details.

Definition 1.1

(Sketch of Definition
7.6
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. Consider the free abelian group on equivalence classes of tuples
\{V_{\alpha}\to U_{\alpha},{\mathbb{A}}_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha},g_{\alpha\beta}\}
, modulo concordances with exact Chern–Simons form.
Define
\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{n}(M)
as the quotient gotten from identifying the direct sum of bundles with addition in
\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{n}(M)
; see (
118
).

Theorem 1.2
.

The graded ring
\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{\bullet}(M)
is naturally isomorphic to the differential KO-theory of
M
. When
M={\rm pt}
, this description refines the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro isomorphism that sends a Clifford module to a class in
{\rm KO}^{\bullet}({\rm pt})
.

We emphasize that the real vector bundles
V_{\alpha}\to U_{\alpha}
in Definition
1.1
are finite rank. By contrast, Atiyah and Singer’s construction of (non-differential) KO-theory comes from families of
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-linear Fredholm operators acting on infinite-rank bundles
[
AS69
]
. As we explain below, an index bundle construction permits a comparison between Atiyah and Singer’s description of
{\rm KO}^{n}(M)
and the one from Theorem
1.2
; see §
4.2
-§
4.5
and Proposition
5.12
. As outlined in §
2
, Definition
1.1
emerges naturally when considering a differential refinement of the index bundle for a family of Clifford linear Dirac operators.
The kernel of such a family is usually not a vector bundle (it need not have locally constant rank), which leads to the subtle gluing data for the index bundle and its differential refinement.
Definition
1.1
is purpose-built to accommodate this information, providing a natural home for the analytic index in differential
{\rm KO}
-theory.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a proper family of
n
-dimensional Riemannian spin manifolds and
V\to X
be a metrized real vector bundle with compatible connection
\nabla
. The families Clifford index determines a degree
-n
differential cocycle
\widehat{\pi}_{!}(V,\nabla)
on
B
whose associated class
[\widehat{\pi}_{!}(V,\nabla)]\in\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{-n}(B)
refines the analytic pushforward in
{\rm KO}
-theory.

1.1.
Comparison with other work

In contrast to differential K-theory for complex bundles, the literature on differential KO-theory is quite sparse. The most thorough treatment to date is by Grady and Sati
[
GS21
]
. They work with Hopkins and Singer’s model for differential cohomology
[
HS05
]
phrased in terms of sheaves of spectra
[
BNM16
]
. When applied to KO, this general construction elucidates structural aspects like differential twists and communicates with powerful homotopical tools, e.g., the differential Atiyah–Hirzebruch spectral sequence.

The Hopkins–Singer differential refinement of complex K-theory is frequently augmented by geometrically-motivated cocycle models. These more tailored presentations of
\widehat{\rm K}{}
are crucial in certain constructions such as the differential analytic index in
[
GL15
]
and differential index theorem in
[
FL10
]
. In turn, these constructions allow one to refine and enhance classical results, e.g.,
[
Lot94
,
Fre01
,
Bun09
,
Bun11
]
.
The analog of these geometric models and their ensuing applications remain under-developed in differential KO-theory. Freed sketches one candidate construction
[
Fre00
, page 34]
, and further suggestions are given by Bunke and Schick
[
BS12
, §4.9]
; however, these ideas were never fleshed out. More recently, Gomi and Yamashita
[
GY22
]
developed a model as an enhancement of Karoubi’s KO-theory
[
Kar78
]
, with the goal of studying differential refinements of invariants of condensed matter systems
[
FH21
]
.

The cocycle model for differential KO constructed in this paper is motivated by the geometry of index bundles with intended applications in real differential index theory. At the heart of this model is the definition of a
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-bundle (see Definition
5.2
), which arises as a finite-dimensional families index of
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-linear Dirac operators, see §
2.2
. This geometry resonates with Furuta’s vectorial bundles
[
Fur02
,
Gom10
]
and Henrique’s quasi-bundles
[
Hen09
]
, though below we emphasize the connection with the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro construction
[
ABS64
]
. In future work we plan to construct a differential topological index via a realization of the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro Thom class in the framework of Theorem
1.2
.

Replacing the real Clifford algebras
{\rm Cl}_{n}
with complex Clifford algebras
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}
leads to analogs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
for differential complex K-theory. This makes contact with previous results. For example, when
n=0
and
M
is compact, Theorem
1.2
generalizes the generators and relations presentation of
\widehat{{\rm K}}^{0}(M)
in terms of finite-rank vector bundles with connection and an odd degree differential form; see Remark
7.7
. One also finds overlap with models of a more analytic flavor: applying the index bundle construction (see §
2
) to the Hilbert bundle model for differential K-theory from
[
GL15
]
leads to a version of Definition
1.1
with
{\mathbb{C}}
-coefficients, where the odd degree groups in
[
GL15
, §6]
can be understood in terms of
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{1}
-linear superconnections.
Finally, Theorem
1.3
(both in the statement and proof) generalizes Freed and Lott’s construction of the differential analytic index for families of spin
c
-manifolds
[
FL10
]
.

Definition
1.1
is also designed to receive a cocycle map from a class of
1|1
-dimensional Euclidean field theories in the style of Stolz and Teichner
[
ST04
,
ST11
]
. A fundamental assertion of the Stolz–Teichner program is that quantization of such field theories should correspond to the analytic pushforward in KO-theory. It is conjectured that a
2|1
-dimensional generalization supplies an analytic pushforward in the cohomology theory of topological modular forms. Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
give a context in which one can start to make some of these ideas precise, see
[
BE23a
,
BE23b
,
BE23c
]
.
Owing to its independent interest in geometry, the present paper is written to be independent from this discussion of field theories.

The main idea from the families index

Definition
1.1
emerges naturally when generalizing Freed and Lott’s families differential index
[
FL10
, §3]
to families of Clifford linear Dirac operators. In this section, we review versions of the families index and describe the relationship with our definitions and results.

2.1.
The families index in
{\rm K}
-theory

Let
X
be a compact even-dimensional spin
c
manifold. The index of the Dirac operator
\not{D}
on
X
is

(1)

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}(\not{D})=[{\rm ker}(\not{D})_{\rm ev}]-[{\rm ker}(\not{D})_{\rm odd}]\in{\rm K}({\rm pt})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}},

where we consider
{\rm ker}(\not{D})
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space with even and odd subspaces
{\rm ker}(\not{D})_{\rm ev}
and
{\rm ker}(\not{D})_{\rm odd}
, respectively.
A family of Riemannian spin
c
manifolds
\pi\colon X\to B
with compact even-dimensional fibers determines a family of operators
\not{D}
on
B
. The fiber at
b\in B
is a Dirac operator
\not{D}_{b}
on the spin
c
manifold
\pi^{-1}(\{b\})=X_{b}
. One would like to construct a class in
{\rm K}(B)
from this data.
Taking the fiberwise index yields a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded vector space
{\rm ker}(\not{D}_{b})
for each
b\in B
. However, these typically fail to assemble into a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded vector bundle, e.g., the dimension of the kernel need not be locally constant. Hence, this naive attempt at a families index does not determine a class in
{\rm K}(B)
generalizing (
1
).

There are two well-known ways to remedy this. The first is analytic: the space of Fredholm operators represents the functor
{\rm K}(-)
, e.g., see
[
LM94
, §III.8]
. A family of spin
c
manifolds
\pi\colon X\to B
determines a
B
-family of Dirac operators, which in turn provides a map from
B
into the space of Fredholm operators. The homotopy class of this map defines the
families analytic index

{\rm Ind}(\not{D})\in{\rm K}(B)
. The second approach is geometric: consider the open cover
\{U_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}}
of
B
defined as

(2)

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}:=\{b\in B\mid\lambda\notin{\rm Spec}(\not{D}_{b}^{2})\},

where
{\rm Spec}(\not{D}_{b}^{2})
denotes the spectrum of the self-adjoint, nonnegative Dirac Laplacian
\not{D}_{b}^{2}
. For
b\in U_{\lambda}
, let
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}_{b}
denote the sum of eigenspaces of
\not{D}_{b}^{2}
with eigenvalues less than
\lambda
. Ellipticity of
\not{D}_{b}^{2}
implies that the
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}_{b}
fit together into a finite rank, smooth,
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded vector bundle
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda}
. Hence we obtain a
{\rm K}
-theory class
[\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}]=[\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}_{\rm ev}]-[\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}_{\rm odd}]\in{\rm K}(U_{\lambda})
. On overlaps
U_{\lambda}\bigcap U_{\mu}
with
\lambda<\mu
, there is an evident inclusion of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded vector bundles,

(3)

\displaystyle g_{\lambda\mu}\colon\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{<\mu}.

The restriction of
\not{D}
to the orthogonal complement of the inclusion (
3
) determines an invertible odd endomorphism
e_{\lambda\mu}
that witnesses an equality of
{\rm K}
-theory classes
[\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}]=[\mathcal{H}^{<\mu}]\in{\rm K}(U_{\lambda}\bigcap U_{\mu})
, see (
142
).
In fact, the inclusions (
3
) and odd isomorphisms
e_{\lambda\mu}
satisfy a cocycle condition on
U_{\lambda}\bigcap U_{\mu}\bigcap U_{\mu}
. This constructs a well-defined class

(4)

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}(\not{D}):=[\{\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda},g_{\lambda\mu},e_{\lambda\mu}\}]\in{\rm K}(B)

equal to the families analytic index of
\pi\colon X\to B
; see
[
Fre87
, §7.2]
,
[
BGV92
, §9.5]
.

The geometric description (
4
) of the index also leads to a natural refinement in the differential
{\rm K}
-theory of
B
that uses the
Bismut superconnection
, denoted
{\mathbb{B}}
and recalled in (
143
) below. The restriction of the degree 1 part of
{\mathbb{B}}
to
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}
determines ordinary connections
\nabla^{<\lambda}
on each finite-rank bundle
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}|_{U_{\lambda}}
with Chern forms

(5)

\displaystyle{\rm Ch}(\nabla^{<\lambda})\in\Omega^{\rm even}(U_{\lambda};{\mathbb{R}}[u^{\pm 1}]),\qquad|u|=-2.

Properties of the Bismut superconnection further determine a unique extension of the data (
5
) to a cocycle in the Čech–de Rham complex of
M
relative to the open cover (
2
) (see Proposition
6.15
),

(6)

\displaystyle{\rm Ch}({\mathbb{B}},\{U_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}})\in\Omega^{\bullet}(U_{*};{\mathbb{R}}[u^{\pm 1}]),\qquad(d+\delta){\rm Ch}({\mathbb{B}},\{U_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}})=0.

By construction, this Čech–de Rham cocycle represents the Chern character of the index bundle. By the Riemann–Roch theorem, another representative is the integral along the fibers

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{X/B}{\rm Ch}(\nabla^{V})\wedge{\rm Todd}(\nabla^{X/B})\in\Omega^{\bullet}_{\rm cl}(B;{\mathbb{R}}[\alpha,\alpha^{-1}])

of the Chern form of
V
modified by the
{\rm Todd}(\nabla^{X/B})
, the Todd form of the spin
c
-family.

The families differential index comes from specifying a coboundary in the Čech–de Rham complex that mediates between the cocycles (
6
) and (
7
).
Adapting methods of Bismut
[
Bis86
]
, Bismut–Cheeger
[
BC89
]
and Freed–Lott
[
FL10
, §3]
, there are forms
\eta_{\lambda}\in\Omega^{\rm odd}(U_{\lambda},{\mathbb{R}}[u,u^{-1}])
satisfying

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{X/B}{\rm Ch}(\nabla^{V})\wedge{\rm Todd}(X/B)-{\rm Ch}({\mathbb{B}},\{U_{\lambda}\})=(d+\delta)\{\eta_{\lambda}\}\in\Omega^{\bullet}(U_{*};{\mathbb{R}}[u,u^{-1}]),

where
d
is the de Rham differential and
\delta
is the Čech differential, i.e., the
\eta_{\lambda}
determine the desired coboundary in the Čech–de Rham complex. This determines a differential
{\rm K}
-cocycle

(9)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\rm Ind}}(\not{D})=[\{(\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda},\nabla^{<\lambda},\eta_{\lambda}),(g_{\lambda\mu},e_{\lambda\mu})\}]\in\widehat{{\rm K}}(B)

representing the differential index.

2.2.
The families index in
{\rm KO}
-theory

Now consider the Clifford linear Dirac operator
\not{D}
on an
n
-dimensional Riemannian spin manifold. The kernel of
\not{D}
is a finite-rank Clifford module
[
LM94
, II.7]
. The equivalence class of this module under the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro map gives a class in
{\rm KO}^{-n}({\rm pt})

[
ABS64
]

(10)

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}(\not{D}):=[{\rm ker}(\not{D})]\in{\rm KO}^{-n}({\rm pt}).

This is the
Clifford index
of
\not{D}
. We refer to §

for further details on the Clifford linear Dirac operator and §

for a review of the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro construction.

When generalizing (
10

n

\pi\colon X\to B
, the kernels of the fiberwise Clifford linear Dirac operators fail to determine a smooth bundle of Clifford-modules for the same reasons as in the spin

{\rm Fred}_{n}
of Clifford linear Fredholm operators represents the functor
{\rm KO}^{-n}(-)

[
AS69
]
,
[
LM94
, III.10]
. A family of Clifford linear Dirac operators associated to a bundle of spin manifolds
\pi\colon X\to B

B\to{\rm Fred}_{n}
whose homotopy class defines the
families analytic index

(11)

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}(\not{D})\in[B,{\rm Fred}_{n}]\simeq{\rm KO}^{-n}(B).

The main goal of this paper is to develop the complementary geometric approach to the families Clifford index, in parallel to the index bundle for the spin
c
-Dirac operator. In the remainder of this section we sketch the main ideas.

Remark 2.1
.

There are several sign conventions to fix when working with Clifford linear Dirac operators. Our conventions (explained in Remarks
3.7
and
4.1
) lead to the kernel of the Clifford linear Dirac operator being a (left)
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-module.

For a family
\not{D}
of
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-linear Dirac operators, define an open cover (
2
) as before: consider the subspace where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
is not an eigenvalue of
\not{D}^{2}
. Taking the direct sum of eigenspaces with eigenvalues
<\lambda
yields finite-rank bundles of Clifford modules
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda}
over each component. Smoothness of this bundle follows from ellipticity of the Dirac Laplacian
\not{D}^{2}
. On an overlap
U_{\lambda}\bigcap U_{\mu}
, the restriction of
\not{D}
to the orthogonal complement to the inclusion of bundles (
3
) determines an invertible odd endomorphism that commutes with the
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-action, see (
142
). This odd endomorphism generates a
{\rm Cl}_{-1}
-action, leading to a
{\rm Cl}_{-n}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{-1}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{-n-1}
-action on the orthogonal complement extending the prior
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-action. The existence of such an extension implies that the bundles of Clifford modules over
U_{\lambda}\bigcap U_{\mu}
represent the same class in
{\rm KO}
-theory. In fact, the analogous information (
4
) in the Clifford linear case determines a class in
{\rm KO}^{-n}(B)
.

To summarize, the index bundle for a family of
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-linear Dirac operators is data:

(i)

an open cover with finite-rank
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-module bundles
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda}
;

(ii)

for each intersection
U_{\lambda}\bigcap U_{\mu}
, inclusions (
3
) of
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-modules together with the data of a
{\rm Cl}_{-n-1}
-action on the orthogonal complement extending the
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-action.

These data satisfy a cocycle condition. This essentially recovers Definition
5.2
of a
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-bundle. In turn,
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-bundles can be used to construct
{\rm KO}^{-n}(M)
(Proposition
5.12
), yielding a version of Theorem
1.2
in (non-differential)
{\rm KO}
-theory.

A differential refinement comes from generalizing the differential index (
9
) in complex K-theory. To start, the Bismut superconnection
{\mathbb{B}}
defined in (
143
) for a family of Clifford linear Dirac operators is automatically
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-linear. It restricts to an ordinary
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-linear connection
\nabla^{<\lambda}
on each
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}
with Chern forms analogous to (
5
) comprising part of the data of a Čech–de Rham cocycle (
6
) representing the Pontryagin character of the index bundle. Replacing the Todd form in (
7
) by the
\hat{A}
-form, the remaining data of the differential index comes from
\eta_{\lambda}
-forms determining a Čech–de Rham coboundary as in (
8
). These are constructed from Clifford linear generalizations of the standard constructions. With these definitions in hand, the differential
{\rm KO}
-index is defined essentially the same as in (
9
),

(12)

\displaystyle\widehat{\rm Ind}(\not{D})=[\{(\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda},\nabla^{<\lambda},\eta_{\lambda}),(g_{\lambda\mu},e_{\lambda\mu})\}]\in\widehat{\rm KO}{}^{-n}(B)

where
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}\to U_{\lambda}
is a bundle of
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-modules,
g_{\lambda\mu}
is an inclusion of Clifford module bundles,
e_{\lambda\mu}
extends a
{\rm Cl}_{-n}
-action on the orthogonal complement of the inclusion to a
{\rm Cl}_{-n-1}
-action,
\nabla^{<\lambda}
is a Clifford linear connection on
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda}
, and the forms
\{\eta_{\lambda}\}
determine a Čech–de Rham coboundary.

Definition

of a differential cocycle is designed so that the differential index (
12
) is an immediate example. Constructing a map from data like (

) to differential
{\rm KO}
-theory basically follows the same route as in complex K-theory. The main work in Theorem
1.2
is to show that this map is surjective; this comes down to a computation (Proposition

). Theorem

then follows from the differential index construction sketched above.

3.
Clifford modules

In this section we review the theory of Clifford modules. Our focus is on real Clifford algebras and real Clifford modules, with a few pieces of the theory over
{\mathbb{C}}
. For definitions and constructions that apply to either real or complex coefficients, we use
{\mathbb{F}}
to denote either
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
.

3.1.
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded
*
-algebras

The category of
real
, respectively
complex
,
super vector spaces
is the category whose objects are
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded vector spaces over
{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively
{\mathbb{C}}
, and morphisms are linear maps that preserve the grading. We promote this to a symmetric monoidal category via the graded tensor product, where the adjective “super” refers to the sign in the braiding isomorphism

V\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}W\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}V\qquad v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{|v||w|}w\otimes v\quad v\in V,\ w\in W

where
v
and
w
are homogeneous elements of degree
|v|,|w|\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. For a super vector space
V
, let
V_{\rm ev}\oplus V_{\rm odd}
denote the direct sum decomposition of
V
into its even and odd subspaces. The
grading involution
is the linear map
(-1)^{\sf F}\colon V\to V
that acts by
+1
on
V_{\rm ev}
and
-1
on
V_{\rm odd}
.
1
1

1

The notation
(-1)^{\sf F}
is from physics where
{\sf F}
stands for “fermion”, e.g., see
[
Wit82
, §1]
.
Let
\Pi V
denote the parity reversal of
V
, i.e.,
(\Pi V)_{\pm}=V_{\mp}
. Throughout,
{\mathbb{F}}^{n|m}
will denote the super vector space with
({\mathbb{F}}^{n|m})_{\rm ev}={\mathbb{F}}^{n}
and
({\mathbb{F}}^{n|m})_{\rm odd}={\mathbb{F}}^{m}
.

A
superalgebra
over
{\mathbb{F}}
is an algebra object in super vector spaces over
{\mathbb{F}}
. The
super commutator
of elements in a superalgebra is defined as

(13)

\displaystyle[a,b]=ab-(-1)^{|a||b|}ba\qquad a,b\in A,

for homogenous elements
a
and
b
. The super commutator satisfies a graded version of the Jacobi identity.

Example 3.1
.

For a super vector space
V
, let
{\rm End}(V)
denote the set of all linear maps
V\to V
(not necessarily grading-preserving), where grading-preserving maps are regarded as even and grading-reversing maps are odd. Composition of linear maps endows
{\rm End}(V)
with the structure of a superalgebra.

Remark 3.2
.

Generalizing the previous example, for super vector spaces
V
and
W
one can consider the super vector space
\underline{{\sf Hom}}(V,W)
of all linear maps
V\to W
, graded according to whether a linear map is grading preserving versus grading reversing. This endows the category of super vector spaces with a closed monoidal structure, i.e.,
-\otimes V
is left adjoint to
\underline{{\sf Hom}}(V,-)
.

A
super trace
on a superalgebra is a map of super vector spaces

(14)

\displaystyle{\sf sTr}_{A}\colon A\to{\mathbb{F}}\qquad{\sf sTr}_{A}([a,b])=0

that vanishes on super commutators, and hence is determined by a linear map
A/[A,A]\to{\mathbb{F}}
. The
opposite
of a superalgebra
A
is a superalgebra
A^{\sf{op}}
with the same underlying super vector space and multiplication

a\cdot_{{\sf{op}}}b:=(-1)^{|a||b|}b\cdot a

where
b\cdot a
is the multiplication in
A
. For superalgebras
A
and
B
, let
A\otimes B
denote the superalgebra with multiplication

(a\otimes b)\cdot(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})=(-1)^{|b||a^{\prime}|}aa^{\prime}\otimes bb^{\prime}.

Example 3.3
.

The
super trace
of a linear endomorphism
T\colon V\to V
is defined as

(15)

\displaystyle{\sf sTr}(T):={\sf Tr}((-1)^{\sf F}\circ T)

where
{\sf Tr}
is the ordinary trace. The super trace determines a trace on the superalgebra
{\rm End}(V)
from Example
3.1
.

A
*
-structure
on a superalgebra is a
*
-structure on its underlying (ungraded) algebra, i.e., an anti-involution of the underlying algebra (that is conjugate-linear when
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
).

Example 3.4
.

The usual transpose determines a
*
-structure on
{\rm End}(V)
.

Remark 3.5
.

A closely related notion is that of a
*
-superalgebra
, which is a superalgebra together with an involutive
graded
anti-involution, meaning a homomorphism to the opposite algebra in the graded sense. There is a dictionary relating superalgebras with
*
-structures to
*
-superalgebras
[
DEF+99
, pages 89-92]
. The signs associated with
*
-superalgebras (while perhaps more categorically pleasing) compare awkwardly with the standard conventions in index theory. This is why we have chosen to work with ordinary
*
-structures.

3.2.
Modules over superalgebras

Given a superalgebra
A
, let
{}_{A}{\sf Mod}
and
{\sf Mod}_{A}
denote the categories of left and right
A
-modules, respectively, whose objects are super vector spaces with a (graded)
A
-action. These categories have symmetric monoidal structures from the direct sum of
A
-modules. Similarly, let
{}_{A}{\sf Mod}_{B}
denote the category of
A-B
-bimodules. There are canonical equivalences of categories

(16)

\displaystyle{}_{A\otimes B}{\sf Mod}\simeq{}_{A}{\sf Mod}_{B^{\sf{op}}}\simeq{\sf Mod}_{A^{\sf{op}}\otimes B^{\sf{op}}}

where a left
A\otimes B
-bimodule
W
is sent to the
A-B^{\sf{op}}
-bimodule with the same underlying super vector space and action

a\cdot w\cdot b:=(-1)^{|w||b|}(a\otimes b)\cdot w

with similar formulas defining a right
A^{\sf{op}}\otimes B^{\sf{op}}
-module. We often use notation like
{}_{A}W
for an object of
{}_{A}{\sf Mod}
, and
{}_{A}W_{B}
for an object of
{}_{A}{\sf Mod}_{B}
.

A super trace on a superalgebra
A
induces a super trace on the category of (finite-dimensional)
A
-modules. For right
A
-modules, this super trace comes from the composition

(17)

\displaystyle{\rm End}_{A}(W)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\leftarrow}}W\otimes_{A}W^{\vee}\to A/[A,A]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\sf sTr}_{A}}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{F}}

where
W^{\vee}:={\rm Hom}_{A}(W,A)
is the dual (left)
A
-module, and the map to
A/[A,A]
is the Hattori–Stallings trace
[
Hat65
,
Sta65
]
induced by the evaluation pairing,

{\rm eval}\colon W\otimes_{\mathbb{F}}W^{\vee}\to A.

One obtains traces for bimodules (and left modules) from traces on right modules using (
16
).

Super algebras
A
and
B
are
Morita equivalent
if there exist bimodules
{}_{A}W_{B}
and
{}_{B}V_{A}
such that
{}_{A}W\otimes_{B}V_{A}\simeq{}_{A}A_{A}
and
{}_{B}V\otimes_{A}W_{B}\simeq{}_{B}B_{B}
, where
A
and
B
are regarded as bimodules over themselves. For Morita equivalent algebras, there are equivalences of categories

({}_{B}V\otimes_{A}-)\colon{}_{A}{\sf Mod}\leftrightarrows{}_{B}{\sf Mod}\colon({}_{A}W\otimes_{B}-).

Remark 3.6
.

Super algebras, bimodules, and bimodule maps are the objects, 1-morphisms and 2-morphisms of the (super) Morita bicategory, e.g., see
[
Fre12
, §1.27]
. Objects in this bicategory are 1-isomorphic when they are Morita equivalent. We do not explicitly use this bicategorical structure below, but we do employ some of the terminology. In particular, a Morita equivalence will be referred to as an
invertible
bimodule in reference to its role as an invertible 1-morphism in the Morita bicategory.

3.3.
Clifford algebras

For a real (ungraded) vector space
V
with quadratic form
q
, the
Clifford algebra
is the quotient of the tensor algebra of
V

(18)

\displaystyle{\rm Cl}(V,q):=T(V)/\langle v\otimes v+q(v)\rangle.

This is a superalgebra with grading involution determined by
v\mapsto-v
for
v\in V\subset{\rm Cl}(V,q)
.

Remark 3.7
.

Sign conventions in (
18
) vary; ours are consistent with
[
ABS64
,
LM94
,
ST04
]
but differ from
[
Bou88
, IX.9]
.

Let
{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
denote the Clifford algebra associated to the real vector space
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
for quadratic form given by the direct sum of the standard positive definite inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and the standard negative definite inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Hence, we have the generators and relations presentation,

(19)

\displaystyle{\rm Cl}_{n,m}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{n},e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\mid[f_{i},f_{j}]=-2\delta_{ij},\ [e_{i},e_{j}]=2\delta_{ij},\ [f_{i},e_{j}]=0\rangle

where the
f_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\subset{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
and
e_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\subset{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
are odd, and the relations all involve the super commutator (
13
). Since quadratic forms over
{\mathbb{R}}
are classified by their signature, there exists an isomorphism
{\rm Cl}(V,q)\simeq{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
for any
(V,q)
where
q
has signature
(n,m)
.

We equip
{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
with a
*
-structure determined by the values on generators,

(20)

\displaystyle e_{i}^{*}=e_{i},\qquad f_{i}^{*}=-f_{i}.

We also adopt the notation

\displaystyle{\rm Cl}_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Cl}_{n,0}&\phantom{BB}n\geq 0\\
{\rm Cl}_{0,n}&\phantom{BB}n<0\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n,m}=2^{-(n+m)/2}f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{n}e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{m},\qquad\Gamma_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Gamma_{n,0}&n\geq 0\\
\Gamma_{0,n}&n<0.\end{array}\right.

The element
\Gamma_{n,m}
depends on a choice of orientation of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, and changing the orientation introduces a sign. There are canonical isomorphisms

(27)

\displaystyle{\rm Cl}_{n}^{\sf{op}}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{-n},\quad{\rm Cl}_{n}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{1}^{\otimes n},\quad{\rm Cl}_{n}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{m}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{n+m},\quad{\rm Cl}_{n}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{-m}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{n,m},

for
n,m
both nonnegative or both nonpositive. The isomorphism
{\rm Cl}_{n}^{\sf{op}}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{-n}
is determined by the identity map on underlying super vector spaces, so below we will write
{\rm Cl}_{n}^{\sf{op}}={\rm Cl}_{-n}
. The other isomorphisms critically use that
\otimes
is the graded tensor product of superalgebras.

Define the complex Clifford algebras by
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n,m}:={\rm Cl}_{n,m}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}
. These have
*
-structures by taking the
{\mathbb{C}}
-antilinear extension of the assignments (
20
). There are isomorphisms
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n,m}\simeq\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n+m}\simeq\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{-n-m}
implemented by multiplying the appropriate generators by
i=\sqrt{-1}
. These isomorphisms do not preserve the elements
\Gamma_{n,m}\in{\rm Cl}_{n,m}\subset\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n,m}
in (
3.3
).

3.4.
Clifford modules

A (real)
Clifford module
is a graded left module over
{\rm Cl}(V,q)
. Right Clifford modules and Clifford bimodules are defined analogously. The first isomorphism in (
27
) determines equivalences of categories

(28)

\displaystyle{}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{-m}}{\sf Mod}\simeq{}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}}{\sf Mod}_{{\rm Cl}_{m}}\simeq{\sf Mod}_{{\rm Cl}_{-n}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{m}}

which will allow us to translate any Clifford (bi)module into a left Clifford module.

An
inner product
on a real super vector space
W
is an inner product on the ungraded vector space underlying
W
such that the even and odd subspaces of
W
are orthogonal. We take a similar definition for a hermitian inner product on a complex super vector space.

Remark 3.8
.

Sign conventions in differential geometry often use a mix of the graded and ungraded tensor product for
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Hilbert spaces, Clifford algebras, and differential forms valued in sections of a super vector bundle, e.g., conventions in
[
BGV92
]
. Making these conventions consistent with each other uses an equivalence between inner products on super vector spaces defined via the graded and ungraded tensor product. This equivalence involves signs and factors of
\sqrt{-1}
(see
[
DEF+99
, pages 90-91]
).
Such factors appear when the two conventions collide. For example, self-adjoint superconnections inherit signs from this translation, see Definition
6.2
.

A left Clifford module
\rho\colon{\rm Cl}(V,q)\to{\rm End}(W)
is
self-adjoint

[
BGV92
, Definition 3.3]
if
W
is equipped with an inner product and

(29)

\displaystyle\rho(v^{*})=\rho(v)^{\dagger}

for all
v\in V\subset{\rm Cl}(V,q)
, using the
*
-structure (
20
) and where
(-)^{\dagger}
is the adjoint on
{\rm End}(W)
. Self-adjoint right modules and self-adjoint bimodules are defined using the equivalences (
28
). Given a self-adjoint left
{\rm Cl}(V,q)
-module
W
, a
Clifford linear endomorphism
is a linear map
T\colon W\to W
(not necessarily grading preserving) that graded commutes with the Clifford action, i.e., for all
v\in V\subset{\rm Cl}(V,q)
,

T(\rho(v)\cdot w)=(-1)^{|T|}\rho(v)\cdot T(w),\quad\iff\quad[T,\rho(v)]=0

where
[-,-]
denotes the super commutator (
13
) on
{\rm End}(W)
. A Clifford linear odd endomorphism is
self-adjoint
when it is self adjoint with respect to the inner product on
W
. Note that the grading involution on
W
is always self-adjoint.

Complex Clifford modules are defined analogously, where self-adjoint modules are defined relative to a hermitian inner product.

Example 3.9
.

Define the left
{\rm Cl}_{1,1}

3.5.
Super traces for Clifford modules

There is a canonical isomorphism
[
BGV92
, Proposition 3.21]

{\rm Cl}_{n}/[{\rm Cl}_{n},{\rm Cl}_{n}]\simeq{\rm Cl}_{n}/{\rm Cl}_{n-1}\simeq\Lambda^{n}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}={\rm Det}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

with the determinant line bundle of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Hence, a (nontrivial) super trace (
14
) on
{\rm Cl}_{n}
is equivalent to the data of a trivialization of the determinant line on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, i.e., a choice of volume form. Any such volume form is equivalent to an element
c\cdot\Gamma_{n}
for
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Gamma_{n}
from (
3.3
). This gives a formula for the associated super trace,

(47)

\displaystyle{\sf sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}}(a):=c\cdot{\sf sTr}(\Gamma_{n}\circ a)\qquad c\in{\mathbb{R}}

where the super trace on the right is taken for the action of
\Gamma_{n}\circ a
on the vector space
\Lambda^{\bullet}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\simeq{\rm Cl}_{n}
coming from the left action of the Clifford algebra on itself.

After choosing a scalar
c
, equations (
17
) and (
47
) determine a super trace on free
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-modules. We extend this to a super trace on arbitrary Clifford modules by writing a finite-rank module as a summand of a free module. The resulting super trace

{\rm End}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}}(W)\to{\rm Det}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}{\mathbb{R}}

inherits all the properties of the Hattori–Stallings trace (
17
), e.g., it vanishes on super commutators of Clifford linear endomorphisms. When comparing with the Pontryagin character in KO-theory, it turns out to be convenient to modify the above to include a formal parameter; compare
[
FL10
, §2.5]
.

Definition 3.14
.

Given a left
{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
-module
W
, the
Clifford super trace
of a
{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
-linear map

\displaystyle{\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n,m}}(T)={\rm sTr}(u^{(m-n)/2}\Gamma_{n,m}\circ T)\in{\mathbb{R}}[u^{1/2},u^{-1/2}],

where
u^{1/2}
is an invertible formal variable of degree

with inverse
u^{-1/2}
.

From properties of the ordinary super trace and the definition of

in (

), we observe that the Clifford super trace satisfies

(49)

\displaystyle{\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n,m}}(T_{1}\oplus T_{2})={\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n,m}}(T_{1})+{\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n,m}}(T_{2})

for

an endomorphism of a
{\rm Cl}_{n,m}
-module

(50)

\displaystyle{\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n,m}\otimes{\rm Cl}_{n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}(T_{1}\otimes T_{2})={\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n,m}}(T_{1})\cdot{\rm sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n^{\prime},m^{\prime}}}(T_{2})

for

an endomorphism of a

There is a completely analogous trace theory for modules over complex Clifford algebras, enjoying the same formal properties as the real Clifford super trace. The choice of normalization in (

4.1.
The Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro construction

Let

{\rm KO}^{0}_{c}(M)
can be specified by a pair
(V,Q)
for a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded real vector bundle

an odd endomorphism that is invertible outside of a compact subset of

(54)

\displaystyle[V]=[V_{\rm ev}]-[V_{\rm odd}]\in{\rm KO}^{0}(M)

determines a class, and on the subspace of
M
where
Q
is invertible we obtain an isomorphism
V_{\rm ev}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}V_{\rm odd}
, so that the formal difference (
54

Given a right
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-module

\displaystyle{\sf Mod}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}}\to{\rm KO}^{0}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\simeq{\rm KO}^{-n}({\rm pt}),\qquad W\mapsto(\underline{W},{\rm cl}_{x})

is multiplication by a scalar.
The isomorphism in (

Let

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{M}}_{n}/i^{*}{\mathfrak{M}}_{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}{\rm KO}^{-n}({\rm pt})\qquad n\geq 0.

Furthermore, the tensor product of Clifford modules is compatible with the graded ring structure on

Remark 4.1
.

We compare sign conventions in the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro isomorphism (

4.2.
Unbounded operators that represent
{\rm KO}

Next we review a space of unbounded operators that is known to represent

Definition 4.3
.

Let

\mathcal{H}
with compact resolvent.

Remark 4.4
.

The notation

[

of a super semigroup representation. Although we do not use super semigroup representations, we keep the same notation for ease of comparison with
[
HST10

HST10

Equip the set of unbounded operators with the subspace topology inherited from

Next we consider variations on the space

of unbounded operators that involve gradings and Clifford actions. Define a
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-graded Hilbert space

-module structure such that every isomorphism class of irreducible

-module appears with infinite multiplicity; for example, starting with a

[

-action. Let

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{-n}\subset{\rm Inf}(\mathcal{H}_{n}).

In

Consider a graded,

4.3.
Topological categories and their classifying spaces

A
topological category
is a category in which the objects and morphisms are topological spaces for which the structure maps (source, target, unit, composition) are continuous. For a topological category
\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{0}
denote the space of objects and
\mathcal{C}_{1}
the space of morphisms.

Given an open cover of a topological space, the following two examples construct topological categories.

Example 4.8
.

Let
Z
be a topological space, and
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
an open cover of
Z
. For
\sigma\subset I
a nonempty finite subset, let
U_{\sigma}=\bigcap_{i\in\sigma}U_{i}
denote the intersection. Define the
Čech category
of the cover, denoted
\check{{\rm C}}(U_{i})
, as the topological category whose objects are
\check{{\rm C}}(U_{i})_{0}=\coprod_{\sigma}U_{\sigma}
and whose morphisms are the space
\check{{\rm C}}(U_{i})_{1}=\coprod_{\sigma\subseteq\tau}U_{\tau}
. The source and target maps are determined by the projection and inclusion
U_{\tau}\subseteq U_{\sigma}
, the unit is induced by the identity map
U_{\sigma}\to U_{\sigma}
, and composition comes from nested inclusions.

Example 4.9
.

Let
Z
be a topological space, and
\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
an open cover of
Z
with the data of an ordering on the indexing set
I
. Then define a topological category
\check{{\rm C}}^{\rm ord}(U_{i})
with objects and morphisms

\check{{\rm C}}^{\rm ord}(U_{i})_{0}=\coprod_{i\in I}U_{i},\qquad\check{{\rm C}}^{\rm ord}(U_{i})_{1}=\coprod_{i\leq j}U_{ij},\qquad U_{ij}=U_{i}\bigcap U_{j}.

The source and target maps are the inclusions
U_{i}\bigcap U_{j}\subset U_{i},U_{j}
, the unit map is the identity on
U_{i}=U_{i}\bigcap U_{i}
, and composition comes from inclusions of triple intersections into double intersections.

Definition 4.10
.

The
nerve
of a topological category
\mathcal{C}
is the simplicial space whose
k
-simplices are
N_{k}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{k}
for
k=0,1
, and for
k>1
are length
k
chains of composable morphisms

\displaystyle N_{k}\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\to}}x_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\to}}x_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{3}}}{{\to}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{k}}}{{\to}}x_{k}\mid x_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{0},f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\cdots\times_{\mathcal{C}_{0}}\mathcal{C}_{1},

topologized as the fibered product.
The face maps in
N_{\bullet}\mathcal{C}
are determined by composing morphisms, and degeneracies are determined by the unit map in
\mathcal{C}
.

The
geometric realization
of a simplicial space
Z_{\bullet}
is

(61)

\displaystyle|Z_{\bullet}|:=(\coprod_{n}Z_{n}\times\Delta^{n})/{\sim}\qquad(f^{*}x,t)\sim(x,f_{*}t)\quad\forall f\colon[l]\to[k]

where
\Delta^{n}
is the standard
n
-simplex, and
f\colon[l]\to[k]
is an order-preserving map. Geometric realization is natural: a map of simplicial spaces
F\colon Z_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}
determines a map between realizations,
|F|\colon|Z_{\bullet}|\to|Y_{\bullet}|
.

Definition 4.11
.

Given a topological category
\mathcal{C}
, define the
classifying space of
\mathcal{C}
as the geometric realization of its nerve,
{\rm{B}}\mathcal{C}:=|N_{\bullet}\mathcal{C}|.

Example 4.12
.

The nerve of the topological category
\check{C}^{\rm ord}(U_{i})
from Example
4.9
has as
k
-simplices the intersections

(62)

\displaystyle N_{k}\check{C}(U_{i})=\coprod_{i_{0}\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}}U_{i_{0}i_{1}\cdots i_{k}},\qquad U_{i_{0}i_{1}\cdots i_{k}}=U_{i_{0}}\bigcap U_{i_{1}}\bigcap\cdots\bigcap U_{i_{k}}.

whereas the nerve of the topological category
\check{C}(U_{i})
from Example
4.8
has as
k
-simplices

N_{k}\check{C}(U_{i})=\coprod_{\sigma_{0}\subseteq\sigma_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\sigma_{k}}U_{\sigma_{k}}.

Results of Segal
[
Seg68
, Proposition 4.12]
, and Dugger–Isaksen
[
DI04
, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.6]
show that the canonical maps

(63)

\displaystyle{\rm{B}}\check{C}^{\rm ord}(U_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}{\rm{B}}\check{C}(U_{i}),\qquad{\rm{B}}\check{C}^{\rm ord}(U_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}Z,\qquad{\rm{B}}\check{C}(U_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}Z,

are homotopy equivalences.

There is a homotopy equivalence
{\rm{B}}\mathcal{C}^{\sf{op}}\simeq{\rm{B}}\mathcal{C}
between the realization of a category and the realization of its opposite. Below we will often identify
{\rm{B}}\check{C}(U_{i})
with
{\rm{B}}\check{C}(U_{i})^{\sf{op}}
.

Let

-submodules

For

Let

denote the space of bounded

-linear operators on

(70)

\displaystyle{\rm Obj}({\mathcal{M}}_{n}^{\delta})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n}),\qquad{\rm Mor}({\mathcal{M}}_{n}^{\delta})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{n})^{\times 3}.

The first map comes from identifying a submodule

Define the

.

Since a tensor product of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{n}\times{\mathcal{M}}_{m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\otimes}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{M}}_{n+m},

4.5.
The families index and the Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro category

We will compare the classifying space of the
n
th Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro category with the space
{\rm Inf}_{-n}
through an index map, similar to the families index described in §
2

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}

(72)

\displaystyle{\rm Inf}_{n,\lambda}:=\{D\in{\rm Inf}_{-n}\mid\lambda\notin{\rm Spec}(D^{2})\}\subset{\rm Inf}_{-n}.

Let
\check{{\rm C}}({\rm Inf}_{n,\lambda})
denote the Čech category of this cover of
{\rm Inf}_{-n}

D
and a
(k+1)

satisfying the indicated property. Morphisms are given by the same data together with a specified subset
\{\lambda_{i_{0}},\dots,\lambda_{i_{l}}\}\subseteq\{\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{k}\}
with
\lambda_{i_{0}}\leq\lambda_{i_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{i_{l}}

Define a continuous functor

(73)

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}\colon\check{{\rm C}}({\rm Inf}_{n,\lambda})^{\sf{op}}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{n}

as follows.
On objects,
{\rm Ind}
assigns to
(D,\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k})

The subspace
\mathcal{H}^{<\lambda_{k}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{n}

The sum is finite because

Since

6.1.
Review of Chern forms of superconnections

For a real super vector bundle

Definition 6.1

(
[

)
.

A
superconnection

on

-linear map

and

On an

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{\mathbb{A}}_{(j)},

where

is an ordinary connection on

{\mathbb{A}}_{(2j+1)}\in\Omega^{2j+1}(M;{\rm End}(V)_{\rm ev})

Definition 6.2

(
[

]

Let

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}^{*}=\sum_{j}(-1)^{j(j+1)/2}{\mathbb{A}}_{(j)}^{\dagger}

A superconnection is

Remark 6.3

Self-adjointness is equivalent to

For

\displaystyle{\mathbb{A}}(u):=u^{-1/2}{\mathbb{A}}_{(0)}+{\mathbb{A}}_{(1)}+u^{1/2}{\mathbb{A}}_{(2)}+\dots+u^{j/2}{\mathbb{A}}_{(j)}+\dots\quad

Then the

.

Example 6.7

For superconnections

\displaystyle\tilde{{\mathbb{A}}}=dt\partial_{t}+(1-t){\mathbb{A}}_{0}+t{\mathbb{A}}_{1}\qquad i_{0}^{*}\tilde{{\mathbb{A}}}={\mathbb{A}}_{0},\ \ i_{1}^{*}\tilde{{\mathbb{A}}}={\mathbb{A}}_{1}

Using (

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}{\rm CS}(\tilde{{\mathbb{A}}}\oplus\tilde{{\mathbb{B}}})&=&{\rm CS}(\tilde{{\mathbb{A}}})+{\rm CS}(\tilde{{\mathbb{B}}})\\

6.2.
Clifford linear superconnections

In

Definition 6.8

Definition 6.9

-linear self-adjoint superconnection

\displaystyle{\rm Ch}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}):={\sf sTr}(u^{-n/2}\Gamma_{n}\circ\exp(-u{\mathbb{A}}(u)^{2}))\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M;{\mathbb{R}}[u^{1/2},u^{-1/2}]),

Lemma 6.10
.

For a self-adjoint
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-linear superconnection
{\mathbb{A}}

lies in the subalgebra

. Furthermore,

is closed and of total degree

Proof.

The fact that

follows from the super trace of an odd endomorphism being zero. In more detail, (
82
) specifies whether a component of a superconnection takes values in even or odd endomorphisms; the odd endomorphisms correspond to the half-integer coefficients in (

). The endomorphism

is even or odd depending on the parity of

, and carries a factor of

from the definition of the Clifford super trace. Hence, the half-integer powers of

in the expansion of

are coefficients of odd endomorphisms and therefore sent to zero under the super trace. Self-adjoint superconnections give a further refinement to even powers of

using that the super trace of a skew-adjoint operator is zero, see Remark

. This verifies that
{\rm Ch}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})\in\Omega^{\bullet}(M;{\mathbb{R}}[\alpha,\alpha])

Since

has total degree zero and

has total degree

{\rm Ch}_{n}({\mathbb{A}})
has total degree

. Finally, to see that

is closed, one applies the same argument as in the case ordinary superconnections, e.g.,
[
BGV92
, Proposition 1.41]
. The key fact is

(98)

\displaystyle{\sf sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}}([{\mathbb{A}},\omega])=d{\sf sTr}_{{\rm Cl}_{n}}(\omega).

This follows from a local computation, writing
{\mathbb{A}}=d+\gamma
and using that the Clifford super trace vanishes on super commutators

[{\mathbb{A}},\exp(-{\mathbb{A}}^{2})]=0

∎

6.3.
The Pontryagin form of a
{\rm Cl}_{n}
-bundle

The Pontryagin form of a

-bundle is locally determined by (

\displaystyle{\rm Ch}_{n}(\{U_{i}\},{\mathbb{A}}_{i})\in\Omega^{\bullet}(U_{*};{\mathbb{R}}[u^{\pm 1}]).

Proof.

Starting with the degree

Chern forms for each

,

(105)

\displaystyle{\rm Ch}_{n}({\mathbb{A}}_{i})\in\Omega^{\bullet}(U_{i};{\mathbb{R}}[u^{\pm 1}])

where

computes the image of the Bott class under

. The vertical arrow on the far right is the usual Pontryagin character in degree 0. Since all the horizontal arrows are isomorphisms, compatibility of the Pontryagin character in cohomology with suspension implies that the other vertical arrows are also given by the Pontryagin character.
∎

7.1.
Review of differential complex K-theory

We review the geometric construction of differential complex K-theory from
[
Klo08
, Chapter 2]
. This is an extension of
[
FL10
, Definition 2.14]
to super vector bundles with superconnection; see Remark
7.2
. For a smooth manifold
M
consider triples
(V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi)
where
V\to M
is a hermitian vector bundle,
{\mathbb{A}}
is a self-adjoint superconnection on
V
, and
\phi\in\Omega^{\rm odd}(M)
. Define an equivalence relation

\displaystyle(V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi)\sim(V^{\prime},{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})\iff\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm there\ exists\ an\ isomorphism\ }f\colon V\xrightarrow{\sim}V^{\prime}\\
{\rm with}\ \ \phi^{\prime}={\rm CS}({\mathbb{A}},f^{*}{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime})+\phi\in\Omega^{\rm odd}(M)/{\rm image}(d).\end{array}

i.e., an isomorphism of hermitian vector bundles with a condition on the resulting Chern–Simons form.

Definition 7.1
.

Define
\widehat{\rm K}{}^{0}(M)
as the quotient of the free abelian group generated by equivalence classes (
7.1
), modulo the subgroup generated by

(111)

\displaystyle(V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi)+(V^{\prime},{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})-(V\oplus V^{\prime},{\mathbb{A}}\oplus{\mathbb{A}}^{\prime},\phi+\phi^{\prime})\quad{\rm and}\quad(W\oplus\Pi W,\nabla\oplus\Pi\nabla,0)

where
\Pi W
is the parity reversal of a purely even vector bundle
W
,
\nabla
is an ordinary connection on
W
, and
\Pi\nabla
is the corresponding connection on
\Pi W
.
A triple
(V,\nabla,\eta)
is a
differential cocycle
and
[V,{\mathbb{A}},\phi]\in\widehat{\rm K}{}^{0}(M)
denotes its class in differential K-theory.

Remark 7.2
.

In any given equivalence class (
7.1
) there is a representative involving an ordinary connection: every superconnection is concordant to an ordinary connection.

We note that (
7.1
) implies there is a well-defined map

\widehat{\rm K}{}^{0}(M)\to\Omega^{\rm even}(M),\qquad[V,\nabla,\phi]\mapsto{\rm Ch}(V,\nabla,\phi):={\sf sTr}(e^{-\nabla^{2}})+d\phi,

valued in closed, even differential forms. For the generalization below, we emphasize that
\phi
is a coboundary mediating between the differential forms
{\sf sTr}(e^{-\nabla^{2}})
and
{\rm Ch}(V,\nabla,\phi)
.

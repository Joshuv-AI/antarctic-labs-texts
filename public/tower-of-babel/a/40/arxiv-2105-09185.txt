Stability of regularized Hastings-Levitov aggregation in the subcritical regime


Abstract
We prove bulk scaling limits and fluctuation scaling limits for a two-parameter class ALE ( α , η ) ({\alpha},\eta) of continuum planar aggregation models. The class includes regularized versions of the Hastings–Levitov family HL ( α ) ({\alpha}) and continuum versions of the family of dielectric-breakdown models, where the local attachment intensity for new particles is specified as a negative power − η -\eta of the density of arc length with respect to harmonic measure. The limit dynamics follow solutions of a certain Loewner–Kufarev equation, where the driving measure is made to depend on the solution and on the parameter ζ = α + η {\zeta}={\alpha}+\eta . Our results are subject to a subcriticality condition ζ ⩽ 1 {\zeta}\leqslant 1 : this includes HL ( α ) ({\alpha}) for α ⩽ 1 {\alpha}\leqslant 1 and also the case α = 2 , η = − 1 {\alpha}=2,\eta=-1 corresponding to a continuum Eden model. Hastings and Levitov predicted a change in behaviour for HL ( α ) ({\alpha}) at α = 1 {\alpha}=1 , consistent with our results. In the regularized regime considered, the fluctuations around the scaling limit are shown to be Gaussian, with independent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes driving each Fourier mode, which are seen to be stable if and only if ζ ⩽ 1 {\zeta}\leqslant 1 .

Introduction

1.1
Hastings–Levitov aggregation

In many physical contexts there appear clusters whose shape is complex, formed apparently by some mechanism of random growth.
It has long been a challenge to account for the observed variety of complex cluster shapes, starting from
plausible physical principles governing the aggregation of individual microscopic particles.
For clusters which are essentially two-dimensional,
there is an approach introduced by Carleson and Makarov
[
2
]
and Hastings and Levitov
[
4
]
,
in which clusters are encoded as a composition of conformal maps, one for each particle.
In this approach, a growing cluster is modelled by an increasing sequence of compact sets
K_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
which are assumed to be simply connected.
We will take the initial set
K_{0}
to be the closed unit disk
\{|z|\leqslant 1\}
.
The increments
K_{n}\setminus K_{n-1}
are then thought of as a sequence of particles added to the cluster.
The idea is to study the clusters
K_{n}
via the conformal isomorphisms

\Phi_{n}:D_{0}\to D_{n}

where
D_{n}
is the complementary domain
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K_{n}
and
\Phi_{n}
is normalized by
\Phi_{n}(\infty)=\infty
and
\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(\infty)>0
.
Then
\Phi_{0}(z)=z
for all
z
and
K_{n}
has logarithmic capacity
\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(\infty)>1
for all
n\geqslant 1
.
This formulation is convenient because the harmonic measure from
\infty
on the boundary
\partial D_{n}
,
which provides a natural way to choose the location of the next particle,
is then simply the image under
\Phi_{n}
of the uniform distribution on
\partial D_{0}=\{|z|=1\}
.
Having chosen a random angle
{\Theta}_{n+1}
to locate the next particle,
and a model particle
P_{n+1}
attached to
K_{0}
at
e^{i{\Theta}_{n+1}}
, for example a small disk tangent to
K_{0}
,
the cluster map is updated to

\Phi_{n+1}=\Phi_{n}\circ F_{n+1}

where
F_{n+1}
is the conformal isomorphism
D_{0}\to D_{0}\setminus P_{n+1}
, normalized similarly to
\Phi_{n}
.
Then
\Phi_{n+1}
encodes the cluster

K_{n+1}=K_{n}\cup\Phi_{n}(P_{n+1}).

Thus, once we specify distributions for the angles
{\Theta}_{n}
and model particles
P_{n}
,
we have specified a mechanism to grow a random cluster.

We will write

{\operatorname{cap}}(K_{n})=\log\Phi^{\prime}(\infty),\quad c_{n}=\log F_{n}^{\prime}(\infty)

and we will refer to
{\operatorname{cap}}(K_{n})
as the capacity
4
4

4

This is an abuse of terminology since it is then
e^{{\operatorname{cap}}(K_{n})}
which is
the logarithmic capacity.

of
K_{n}
and
c_{n}
as the capacity of
P_{n}
.
Then

{\operatorname{cap}}(K_{n})=c_{1}+\dots+c_{n}.

We will be looking for scaling limits where the particle capacities
c_{n}
and the associated particles
P_{n}
become small,
but where
n
is chosen sufficiently large that the cluster capacities
{\operatorname{cap}}(K_{n})
grow macroscopically.

A simple case is to choose
{\Theta}_{n+1}
uniformly distributed on the unit circle and to take
P_{n+1}=e^{i{\Theta}_{n+1}}P
,
where
P
is a small disk tangent to the unit disk at
1
, of radius
r(c)
, chosen so that
P
has capacity
c
.
Then in fact
r(c)/\sqrt{c}
has a positive limit as
c\to 0
.
The location of the new particle
\Phi_{n}(P_{n+1})
is then distributed according to harmonic measure on
\partial K_{n}
.
However, if we assume that
\partial K_{n}
is approximately linear on the scale of
P
, then we would have

\Phi_{n}(P_{n+1})\approx\Phi_{n}(e^{i{\Theta}_{n+1}})+\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{i{\Theta}_{n+1}})P

(1)

so we would add an approximate disk of diameter proportional to
\sqrt{c}|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{i{\Theta}_{n+1}})|
.

In order to compensate for this distortion, Hastings and Levitov proposed the HL
({\alpha})
family of models where,
once
{\Theta}_{n+1}
is chosen, we choose
P_{n+1}
to be a particle of capacity

c_{n+1}=|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{i{\Theta}_{n+1}})|^{-{\alpha}}c.

Then, in the case
{\alpha}=2
, the particles added to the cluster would be approximately of constant size.
The approximation (
1
) is in fact misleading, at least on a microscopic level,
because
\partial K_{n}
develops inhomogeneities on the scale of the particles.
Nevertheless, HL
(2)
has been considered as a variant of diffusion-limited aggregation (DLA)
[
17
]
,
with some justification, see
[
4
]
, derived from numerical experiments.

In general, the HL
({\alpha})
model offers a convenient mechanism for such experiments,
and moves away from the lattice formulation of
[
17
]
which has been shown to lead to unphysical effects on large scales.
See for example
[
MR3819427
]
.
Moreover, it might be hoped that an evolving family of conformal maps would present a more tractable framework for
the analysis of scaling limits than other growth models,
while potentially sharing the same bulk scaling limit and fluctuation universality class.
That is the direction explored in this paper.

Besides the mechanism of diffusive aggregation, based on harmonic measure, there is another one-parameter family of models,
conceived originally in the lattice case, called dielectric breakdown models
[
9
]
,
which interpolates between DLA and the Eden model
[
3
]
.
In the Eden model, each boundary site is chosen with equal probability.
In the continuum setting, for an Eden-type model we would choose an attachment point on the boundary according to normalized arc length,
which has density proportional to
|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{i{\theta}})|
with respect to harmonic measure.
We can widen our family of models to include a continuum analogue of dielectric breakdown models by choosing

\mathbb{P}({\Theta}_{n+1}\in d{\theta}|\Phi_{n})\propto|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{i{\theta}})|^{-\eta}d{\theta}.

The case
\eta=-1
then provides a continuum variant of the Eden model.

In a law-of-large-numbers regime,
it might be guessed that bulk characteristics of the cluster for the model incorporating both the
{\alpha}
and
\eta
modifications
would depend only on their sum
{\zeta}={\alpha}+\eta
since,
once this is fixed, up to a global time-scaling,
the growth rate of capacity due to particles attached near
e^{i{\theta}}
does not depend further on
{\alpha}
or
\eta
.
We will show, in the regime which we can address, that this is indeed true.

In this paper we investigate the two-parameter family of models just described,
but modified by the introduction of a regularization parameter
{\sigma}>0
, which controls the minimum length scale over which
feedback occurs through
c_{n+1}
and
{\Theta}_{n+1}
.
We will require throughout that
{\sigma}\gg\sqrt{c}
(and sometimes
{\sigma}\gg c^{1/4}
, or more)
and we will restrict attention to the subcritical regime
{\zeta}\leqslant 1
.
This includes the Eden case (
{\alpha}=2
,
\eta=-1
) but excludes continuum DLA (
{\alpha}=2
,
\eta=0
).
In the regularized models, we will show fluctuation behaviour which is universal over all choices of particle family.
Our first main result shows that, in this regime, in the limit
c\to 0
, disks are stable, that is,
an initial disk cluster remains close to a disk as particles are added and its capacity becomes large.
Our second main result is to prove convergence of the normalized fluctuations of the cluster around its deterministic limit,
to an explicit Gaussian process.
The constraint
{\zeta}\leqslant 1
appears sharp for this behaviour:
we see an explicit dependence of the fluctuations on
{\alpha}
and
\eta
and, in particular,
an exponential instability of rate
({\zeta}-1)k
in the
k
th Fourier mode if we formally take
{\zeta}>1
.

1.2
Statement of results

In this section, we define the continuous-time ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
model, which is our object of study,
and we specify our standing assumptions for individual particles.
We then state our main results.

Our model is constructed as a composition of univalent functions on the exterior unit disk
D_{0}=\{|z|>1\}
.
Each of these functions corresponds to a choice of attachment angle
{\theta}\in[0,2\pi)
and a basic particle
P
.
Recall that
K_{0}=\{|z|\leqslant 1\}
.
By a basic particle
P
we mean a non-empty subset of
D_{0}
such that
K_{0}\cup P
is compact and simply connected.
Set
D=D_{0}\setminus P
.
By the Riemann mapping theorem, there is a
c\in(0,\infty)
and a conformal isomorphism
F:D_{0}\to D
with Laurent expansion of the form

F(z)=e^{c}\left(z+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{-k}\right).

(2)

Then
F
is uniquely determined by
P
, and
P
has capacity
c
.
Our model depends on three parameters
{\alpha},\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\sigma}\in(0,\infty)
,
along with the choice of a family of basic particles
(P^{(c)}:c\in(0,\infty))
with
P^{(c)}
of capacity
c
.
The associated maps
F_{c}:D_{0}\to D^{(c)}
then have the form (
2
) with
a_{k}=a_{k}(c)
for all
k
.
We assume throughout that
F_{c}
extends continuously to
\{|z|\geqslant 1\}
.
We require that our particle family is nested

P^{(c_{1})}\subseteq P^{(c_{2})}\quad\text{for $c_{1}<c_{2}$}

(3)

and satisfies, for some
{\Lambda}\in[1,\infty)
,

{\delta}(c)\leqslant{\Lambda}r_{0}(c)\quad\text{for all $c$}

(4)

where

r_{0}(c)=\sup\{|z|-1:z\in P^{(c)}\},\quad{\delta}(c)=\sup\{|z-1|:z\in P^{(c)}\}.

In our results, only small values of
c
are of interest.
For such
c
, the last condition (
4
) forces our particles
P^{(c)}
to concentrate near the point
1

while never becoming too flat against the unit circle.

The following are all examples of particle families satisfying both conditions (
3
) and (
4
):

P^{(c)}_{\operatorname{slit}}=(1,1+{\delta}(c)],\quad P^{(c)}_{\operatorname{bump}}=\{z\in D_{0}:|z-1|\leqslant{\delta}(c)\}

and

P^{(c)}_{\operatorname{disk}}=\{z\in D_{0}:|z-1-r(c)|\leqslant r(c)\},\quad r(c)={\delta}(c)/2

where in each case
{\delta}
is a suitable increasing homeomorphism of
(0,\infty)
.

It will be convenient to place our aggregation models from the outset in continuous time.
By a (continuous-time) aggregate Loewner evolution of parameters
{\alpha},\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, or ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
, we mean
a finite-rate, continuous-time Markov chain
(\Phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

taking values in the set of univalent functions
D_{0}\to D_{0}
, starting from
\Phi_{0}(z)=z
,
which, when in state
\phi
, jumps to
\phi\circ F_{c({\theta},\phi),{\theta}}
at rate
{\lambda}({\theta},\phi)d{\theta}/(2\pi)
, where

F_{c,{\theta}}(z)=e^{i{\theta}}F_{c}(e^{-i{\theta}}z),\quad c({\theta},\phi)=c|\phi^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\alpha}},\quad{\lambda}({\theta},\phi)=c^{-1}|\phi^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-\eta}.

(5)

Since
{\sigma}>0
, the rate
{\lambda}({\theta},\phi)
is continuous in
{\theta}
, so the total jump rate is finite.
The model may be thought of equivalently in term of the random process of compact sets
(K_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
given by

K_{0}=\{|z|\leqslant 1\},\quad K_{t}=K_{0}\cup\{z\in D_{0}:z\not\in\Phi_{t}(D_{0})\}.

The effect of the jump just described is then to add to the current cluster the set
\phi(e^{i{\theta}}P^{(c({\theta},\phi))})

thereby increasing its capacity by
c({\theta},\phi)
.

The discrete-time process

{\sigma}\to 0
considered in Theorem
1.3
, we have

c^{-1/2}(\hat{\Psi}^{\operatorname{disc}}_{t/c})_{t\leqslant N}\to(\hat{\Gamma}^{\operatorname{disc}}_{t})_{t\leqslant N}

weakly in
D([0,N],{\cal H})
.

1.3
Commentary and review of related work

Hastings and Levitov
[
4
]
introduced the family of planar aggregation models HL(
{\alpha}
),
which are the cases
\eta={\sigma}=0
of our ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
model.
They discovered by numerical experiments that, for small particles, the models underwent a transition at
{\alpha}=1
:
for
{\alpha}\leqslant 1
the cluster grows like a disk, while for
{\alpha}>1
it exhibits fractal properties.
The HL
(0)
model was subsequently investigated rigorously in a series of works

[
13
]
,

[
11
]
(bulk scaling limit),

[
5
]
,

[
15
]
(fluctuation scaling limit).
The
{\sigma}
-regularized variant of HL
({\alpha})
was proposed in
[
6
]
, where it was shown for slit maps that,
if
{\sigma}\gg(\log(1/c))^{-1/2}
, there is disk-like behaviour for all
{\alpha}\geqslant 0
:
it appeared that the observed fractal properties of HL
({\alpha})
for
{\alpha}>1
were suppressed by strong regularization.
In contrast, for the weaker regularization used in the present paper, the phase transition at
{\alpha}=1
(or
{\zeta}=1
) becomes visible.
The method of
[
6
]
used a comparison with an HL
(0)
-type model which breaks down for smaller values of
{\sigma}
.
The regularized ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
model appeared in
[
16
]
, where it was shown that, for slit maps and for
\eta>1
, a

{\sigma}
-regularized ALE
(0,\eta)
grows as a line for sufficiently small
{\sigma}\leqslant c^{\gamma}
, for some
{\gamma}<\infty
depending on
\eta
.

A new approach was begun in
[
10
]
,
treating regularized ALE
(0,\eta)
as a Markov chain in univalent functions by martingale arguments:
a bulk scaling limit and fluctuation scaling limit were shown,
subject to the constraint
\eta\leqslant 1
and to restrictions on
{\sigma}
as a fractional power of
c
.
These limits turn out not to depend on the details of individual particle shapes.
In this paper, we extend the analysis of
[
10
]
to ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
, subject now to the constraint
{\zeta}={\alpha}+\eta\leqslant 1
.
Thus we now include regularized HL
({\alpha})
for
{\alpha}\leqslant 1
.
Hastings and Levitov had argued that there should be a trade-off between
{\alpha}
and
\eta
,
with only
{\zeta}
affecting the bulk scaling limit, and on this basis proposed HL
(1)
, that is ALE
(1,0)
,
as a continuum variant of the Eden model.
A more direct continuum analogue of the Eden model is ALE
(2,-1)
.
Our results, in the regularized case, both justify the trade-off argument and show a disk scaling limit whenever
{\zeta}\leqslant 1
.
On the other hand, we show that ALE
(1,0)
and ALE
(2,-1)
have different fluctuation behaviour.
As in
[
10
]
, the behaviour of fluctuations as a function of
{\zeta}
is consistent with the conjectured transition in
behaviour at
{\zeta}=1
.

Hastings and Levitov
[
4
]
identify a Loewner–Kufarev-type equation,
which they propose as governing the small-particle limit of HL
({\alpha})
,
citing a discussion of Shraiman and Bensimon
[
14
]
for the Hele–Shaw flow, where
{\alpha}
is taken to be
2
.
This is the LK
({\alpha})
equation, which is the subject of the next section.
As noted by Sola in a contribution to
[
7
]
, there is a lack of mathematical theory for the LK
({\alpha})
equation,
except in the case
{\alpha}=2
when some special techniques become available.
In this paper, since our focus is on clusters initiated as a disk, we are able to use an explicit solution of the equation,
along with its linearization around that solution, so we do not rely on a general theory.
However, the particle interpretation established here offers some evidence that for
{\alpha}\leqslant 1
,
the LK
({\alpha})
equation may have a suitable existence, uniqueness and stability theory,
and that it may be possible to derive the equation as a limit of particle models.
McEnteggart
[
8
]
has shown short-time existence and uniqueness for holomorphic initial data,
by adapting a classical argument for the case
{\alpha}=2
.

Our results depend on constraints on the regularization parameter
{\sigma}
,
though substantially weaker ones than those used in
[
6
]
.
These constraints limit the interactions of individual particles and place us in the simplest case of Gaussian fluctuations.
At a technical level, for Theorem
1.1
,
these constraints come from the need to have
\bar{\delta}(e^{\sigma})\leqslant c^{\varepsilon}
in Proposition
5.3
,
while for Theorem
1.3
they are needed to show that the Poisson integral process
(\Pi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

is a good approximation to the fluctuations in Proposition
5.7
.
In the case
{\zeta}=1
, the regularizing operator
Q
obtained by linearization of the LK
({\zeta})
equation collapses from
a fixed multiple of the Cauchy operator to
{\sigma}
times the second derivative.
In general, for scaling regimes where
{\sigma}\to 0
faster than our fluctuation results allow,
it remains possible that ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
has different universal fluctuation behaviour,
such as KPZ, as has been conjectured for the lattice Eden model.

1.4
Structure of the paper

In the next section, we discuss the Loewner–Kufarev equation for the limit dynamics.
Then, in Section
3
, we derive an interpolation formula between ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
and solutions of the limit equation.
The terms in this formula are estimated in Section
4
.
Equipped with these estimates,
we show the bulk scaling limit in Section
5
and the fluctuation scaling limit in Section
6
.
We collect in an Appendix
7
some further estimates needed in the course of the paper,
including estimates on the conformal maps which encode single particles and particle families.

2
Loewner–Kufarev equation

Let
{\cal S}
denote the set of univalent holomorphic functions
\phi
on
\{|z|>1\}
with
\phi(\infty)=\infty
and
\phi^{\prime}(\infty)\in(0,\infty)
.
Then each
\phi\in{\cal S}
has the form

\phi(z)=e^{c}\left(z+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{-k}\right)

for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and some sequence
(a_{k}:k\geqslant 0)
in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Fix parameters
{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\sigma}\geqslant 0
.
Given
\phi_{0}\in{\cal S}
, consider the following Cauchy problem for
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
in
{\cal S}

\dot{\phi}_{t}=a(\phi_{t})

(7)

where

a(\phi)(z)=z\phi^{\prime}(z)\fint_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}\left|\phi^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})\right|^{-{\zeta}}d{\theta}.

The case
{\sigma}=0
of this equation is the equation proposed by Hastings and Levitov as scaling limit for HL
({\zeta})
,
which we will call the LK
({\zeta})
equation.
When
{\zeta}=0
, the value of
{\sigma}
is immaterial and there is a unique solution given by

\phi_{t}(z)=\phi_{0}(e^{t}z).

Where
{\zeta}=2
and
{\sigma}=0
, (
7
) is the Loewner–Kufarev equation associated to the Hele–Shaw flow.
For
{\sigma}>0
, we will refer to (
7
) as the
{\sigma}
-regularized LK
({\zeta})
equation.
We will be interested in the subcritical case
{\zeta}\in(-\infty,1]
.

The general form of the Loewner–Kufarev equation is given by

\dot{\phi}_{t}(z)=z\phi^{\prime}_{t}(z)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}\,\mu_{t}(d{\theta})

with
(\mu_{t}:t\geqslant 0)
a given family of measures on
[0,2\pi)
.
Thus the
{\sigma}
-regularized LK
({\zeta})
equation is obtained by requiring that the driving measures are given by

\mu_{t}(d{\theta})=\left|\phi^{\prime}_{t}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})\right|^{-{\zeta}}d{\theta}/(2\pi).

Note that the density of these driving measures is the product of the density of the local attachment rate and the local
particle capacity (
5
) for ALE
({\alpha},\eta)
.
By the Loewner–Kufarev theory, for any solution
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
of (
7
), the sets

K_{t}={\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{\phi_{t}(z):|z|>1\}

form an increasing family of simply-connected compacts, with capacities given by

{\tau}_{t}={\operatorname{cap}}(K_{t})=\log\phi_{t}^{\prime}(\infty)=\log\phi_{0}^{\prime}(\infty)+\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{s}([0,2\pi))ds.

2.1
Linearization

We compute the linearization of (
7
) around a solution
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
.
For
\psi
holomorphic in
\{|z|>1\}
, we have

(\nabla a(\phi)\psi)(z)=\left.\frac{d}{d{\varepsilon}}\right|_{{\varepsilon}=0}a(\phi+{\varepsilon}\psi)(z)=z\psi^{\prime}(z)h(z)-{\zeta}z\phi^{\prime}(z)g(z)

where

h(z)=\fint_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}|\phi^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\zeta}}d{\theta}

and, setting
\rho=\psi^{\prime}/\phi^{\prime}
,

g(z)=\fint_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}|\phi^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\zeta}}\operatorname{Re}\rho(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})d{\theta}.

(8)

Note that first-order variations in
{\cal S}
have the form

\psi(z)={\delta}z+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_{k}z^{-k},\quad{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\psi_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}.

The process of first-order variations
(\psi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
around a solution
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}

can be expected to satisfy the linearized equation

\dot{\psi}_{t}=\nabla a(\phi_{t})\psi_{t}.

2.2
Linear stability of disk solutions in the subcritical case

For all
{\sigma}\geqslant 0
, a trial solution
\phi_{t}(z)=e^{{\tau}_{t}}z
for (
7
) leads to the equation
\dot{\tau}_{t}=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}}
.
Let us consider first the case where
{\zeta}\geqslant 0
.
We can solve to obtain

{\tau}_{t}=\begin{cases}{\tau}_{0}+t,&\text{if ${\zeta}=0$},\\
{\zeta}^{-1}\log(e^{{\zeta}{\tau}_{0}}+{\zeta}t),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We thus find solutions
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
to (
7
) in which the sets
K_{t}
form a growing family of disks.
For such disk solutions, we have
\phi_{t}^{\prime}(z)=e^{{\tau}_{t}}
for all
z
, so we can evaluate the integral (
8
) to obtain

(\nabla a(\phi_{t})\psi)(z)=-Q\psi(z)\dot{\tau}_{t}

where

Q\psi(z)=-z\psi^{\prime}(z)+{\zeta}z\psi^{\prime}(e^{\sigma}z)=-D\psi(z)+{\zeta}e^{-{\sigma}}D\psi(e^{\sigma}z).

(9)

Here and below, we write
D\psi(z)
for
z\psi^{\prime}(z)
.
Note that
Q
acts as a multiplier on the Laurent coefficients.
For
\psi(z)=\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}\psi_{k}z^{-k}
, we have

Q\psi(z)=\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}q(k)\psi_{k}z^{-k},\quad q(k)=k(1-{\zeta}e^{-{\sigma}(k+1)}).

We split
Q
as a sum of multiplier operators
Q_{0}+Q_{1}
with multipliers given by

q_{0}(k)=(1-{\zeta})k,\quad q_{1}(k)={\zeta}k(1-e^{-{\sigma}(k+1)}).

(10)

Define for
{\delta}\geqslant 0

P({\delta})=e^{-{\delta}Q},\quad P_{0}({\delta})=e^{-{\delta}Q_{0}},\quad P_{1}({\delta})=e^{-{\delta}Q_{1}}.

(11)

We alert the reader to the similarity of this notation with that used for the particle family
(P^{(c)}:c\in(0,\infty))
.
Then

P({\delta})\psi(z)=P_{0}({\delta})P_{1}({\delta})\psi(z)=P_{1}({\delta})\psi(e^{(1-{\zeta}){\delta}}z)

and, at least formally, the linearized equation
\dot{\psi}_{t}=\nabla a(\phi_{t})\psi_{t}
has solution given by

\psi_{t}(z)=P({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})\psi_{0}(z)=P_{1}({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})\psi_{0}(e^{(1-{\zeta})({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})}z).

(12)

In the case
{\zeta}>1
, at least when
{\sigma}=0
so
Q=Q_{0}
, for example in the Hele–Shaw case,
we see that
\psi_{t}
can be holomorphic in
\{|z|>1\}

only if
\psi_{0}
extends to a holomorphic function in the larger domain
\{|z|>e^{-({\zeta}-1)({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})}\}
.
On the other hand,
we will show, for all
{\sigma}\geqslant 0
, that
P_{1}({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})
preserves the set of holomorphic first-order variations,
so, when
{\zeta}\in[0,1]
, the variation
\psi_{t}
as given by (
12
) remains holomorphic for all
t
.

We turn to the case where
{\zeta}<0
.
The differential equation
\dot{\tau}_{t}=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}}
has now only a local solution, given by

{\tau}_{t}=z^{-1}\log(e^{{\zeta}{\tau}_{0}}+{\zeta}t),\quad t<t_{\zeta}=e^{{\zeta}{\tau}_{0}}/|{\zeta}|

with
{\tau}_{t}\to\infty
as
t\to t_{\zeta}
.
It is convenient in this case to split
Q
differently, setting

\tilde{q}_{0}(k)=k,\quad\tilde{q}_{1}(k)=|{\zeta}|ke^{-{\sigma}(k+1)}.

(13)

Then, making similar definitions in all other respects, we have

\psi_{t}(z)=P({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})\psi_{0}(z)=\tilde{P}_{1}({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})\psi_{0}(e^{{\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0}}z)

and
\psi_{t}
remains holomorphic for all
t<t_{\zeta}
, as in the case
{\zeta}\in[0,1]
.

Define for
r>1

\|\psi\|_{p,r}=\left(\fint_{0}^{2\pi}|\psi(re^{i{\theta}})|^{p}d{\theta}\right)^{1/p}.

The following inequality will be used in Section
4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
p\in(1,\infty)
, there is a constant
C(p)<\infty
such that,
for all
{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and all
{\sigma}\geqslant 0
, for all holomorphic functions
\psi
on
\{|z|>1\}
bounded at
\infty
,
all
t\geqslant 0
and all
r>1
, we have

\displaystyle\|P_{1}(t)\psi\|_{p,r}\leqslant C(p)\|\psi\|_{p,r},

\displaystyle\quad\text{if ${\zeta}\geqslant 0$},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{P}_{1}(t)\psi\|_{p,r}\leqslant C(p)\|\psi\|_{p,r},

\displaystyle\quad\text{if ${\zeta}<0$}.

Proof.

Consider first the case
{\zeta}\geqslant 0
.
The operator
P_{1}(t)
acts as multiplication by
p_{1}(k,t)=e^{-tq_{1}(k)}
on the
k
th Laurent coefficient.
We have
0\leqslant q_{1}(k)\leqslant q_{1}(k+1)
so
0\leqslant p_{1}(k+1,t)\leqslant p_{1}(k,t)\leqslant 1
for all
k
.
Hence the conditions of the Marcinkiewicz multiplier theorem (as recalled in Section
7.2
)
hold for
P_{1}(t)
with
A=1
.
The desired estimate follows.

In the case
{\zeta}<0
, we modified the split so that
\tilde{q}_{1}(k)\geqslant 0
, so
0\leqslant\tilde{p}_{1}(k,t)\leqslant 1
for all
k
.
Now
\tilde{q}_{1}(k)
is no longer increasing but is unimodal in
k
, so
\tilde{p}_{1}(k,t)
is also unimodal in
k
, and so

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\tilde{p}_{1}(k+1,t)-\tilde{p}_{1}(k,t)|\leqslant 2.

Hence the Marcinkiewicz theorem applies with
A=2
and we can conclude as before.
∎

2.3
Transformation to (Schlicht function, capacity) coordinates

Write
{\cal S}_{1}
for the set of ‘Schlicht functions at
\infty
’ on
\{|z|>1\}
, given by

{\cal S}_{1}=\{\phi\in{\cal S}:\phi^{\prime}(\infty)=1\}.

It will be convenient to use coordinates
(\hat{\phi},{\tau})
on
{\cal S}
, given by

\hat{\phi}(z)=e^{-{\tau}}\phi(z),\quad{\tau}=\log\phi^{\prime}(\infty).

Then
\hat{\phi}\in{\cal S}_{1}
and
{\tau}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
It is straightforward to show that, for a solution
(\phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
to (
7
),
the transformed variables
(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
satisfy

(\dot{\hat{\phi}}_{t},\dot{\tau}_{t})=b(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau}_{t})=(\hat{b},b^{\operatorname{cap}})(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau}_{t})

(14)

where

\displaystyle\hat{b}(\hat{\phi},{\tau})(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}}z\hat{\phi}^{\prime}(z)\fint_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}|\hat{\phi}^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\zeta}}d{\theta}-e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}}\hat{\phi}(z)\fint_{0}^{2\pi}|\hat{\phi}^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\zeta}}d{\theta},

\displaystyle b^{\operatorname{cap}}(\hat{\phi},{\tau})

\displaystyle=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}}\fint_{0}^{2\pi}|\hat{\phi}^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\zeta}}d{\theta}.

On linearizing (
14
) around a solution
(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
,
we obtain equations for first-order variations
(\hat{\psi}_{t},\psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
in the new coordinates,
where now
\hat{\psi}_{t}
is bounded at
\infty
for all
t
, reflecting the normalization of
\hat{\phi}_{t}
.
These are then related to the first-order variations
(\psi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
in the old coordinates by

\psi_{t}(z)=e^{{\tau}_{t}}(\hat{\psi}_{t}(z)+\psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}\hat{\phi}_{t}(z)).

For a disk solution
(\phi_{t})_{t<t_{\zeta}}
, we have
\hat{\phi}_{t}(z)=z
and
b(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau})=(0,e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}})
.
The equations for first-order variations are then given by

\dot{\hat{\psi}}_{t}(z)=-(Q+1)\hat{\psi}_{t}(z)\dot{\tau}_{t},\quad\dot{\psi}^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}=-{\zeta}\psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}\dot{\tau}_{t}

with solutions

\hat{\psi}_{t}(z)=e^{-({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})}P({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})\hat{\psi}_{0}(z),\quad\psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}=e^{-{\zeta}({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{0})}\psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{0}.

3
Interpolation formula for Markov chain fluid limits

We use an interpolation formula between continuous-time Markov chains and differential equations,
which we first review briefly in a general setting.
This formula is then applied to an ALE(
{\alpha},\eta
) aggregation process
(\Phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
with capacity parameter
c
,
regularization parameter
{\sigma}
and particle family
(P^{(c)}:c\in(0,\infty))
,
taking as limit equation the
{\sigma}
-regularized LK
({\zeta})
equation with
{\zeta}={\alpha}+\eta
.
We use (Schlicht function, capacity) coordinates for both the process and the limit equation.

3.1
General form of the interpolation formula

Let
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be a continuous-time Markov chain with state-space
E
and transition rate kernel
q
, starting from
x_{0}
say.
Suppose for this general discussion that
E={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
b
be a vector field on
E
with continuous bounded derivative
\nabla b
.
Write
(\xi_{t}(x):t\geqslant 0,x\in E)
for the flow of
b
.
The compensated jump measure of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is the signed measure
\tilde{\mu}^{X}
on
E\times(0,\infty)
given by

\tilde{\mu}^{X}(dy,dt)=\mu^{X}(dy,dt)-q(X_{t-},dy)dt,\quad\mu^{X}=\sum_{t:X_{t}\not=X_{t-}}{\delta}_{(X_{t},t)}.

Set
x_{t}=\xi_{t}(x_{0})
and define, for
s\in[0,t]
,

Z_{s}=x_{t}+\nabla\xi_{t-s}(x_{s})(X_{s}-x_{s}).

Then
Z_{0}=x_{t}
and
Z_{t}=X_{t}
and, on computing the martingale decomposition of
(Z_{s})_{s\leqslant t}
,
we obtain the interpolation formula

X_{t}-x_{t}=M_{t}+A_{t}

(15)

where

M_{t}=\int_{E\times(0,t]}\nabla\xi_{t-s}(x_{s})(y-X_{s-})\tilde{\mu}^{X}(dy,ds)

and

A_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\nabla\xi_{t-s}(x_{s})({\beta}(X_{s})-b(x_{s})-\nabla b(x_{s})(X_{s}-x_{s}))ds

where
{\beta}
is the drift of
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, given by

{\beta}(x)=\int_{E}(y-x)q(x,dy).

We will use this formula in a case where the state-space
E
is infinite-dimensional.
Rather than justify its validity generally in such a context, in the next section,
we will prove directly the special case of the formula which we require.
Note that the integrands in
M_{t}
and
A_{t}
depend on
t
.
Nevertheless, we will call
M_{t}
the martingale term and
A_{t}
the drift term.

3.2
Proof of the formula for ALE(
{\alpha},\eta
)

Let
(\Phi_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be an ALE(
{\alpha},\eta
) aggregation process with capacity parameter
c
,
regularization parameter
{\sigma}
and particle family
(P^{(c)}:c\in(0,\infty))
.
See Section
1.2
and (
5
) for the specification of this process.
We use (Schlicht function, capacity) coordinates, as in Section
2.3
,
to obtain a continuous-time Markov chain
(X_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}=(\hat{\Phi}_{t},{\cal T}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
in
{\cal S}_{1}\times[0,\infty)
.
When in state
x=(\hat{\phi},{\tau})
, for all
{\theta}\in[0,2\pi)
,
this process makes a jump of size
(\Delta({\theta},z,c({\theta}),\hat{\phi}),c({\theta}))
at rate
{\lambda}({\theta})d{\theta}/(2\pi)
, where

\Delta({\theta},z,c,\hat{\phi})=e^{-c}\hat{\phi}(F_{c}({\theta},z))-\hat{\phi}(z)

and

c({\theta})=c({\theta},\hat{\phi},{\tau})=ce^{-{\alpha}{\tau}}|\hat{\phi}^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-{\alpha}},\quad{\lambda}({\theta})={\lambda}({\theta},\hat{\phi},{\tau})=c^{-1}e^{-\eta{\tau}}|\hat{\phi}^{\prime}(e^{{\sigma}+i{\theta}})|^{-\eta}.

We can and do assume that the process is constructed from a Poisson random measure

\mu
on
[0,2\pi)\times[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
of intensity
(2\pi)^{-1}d{\theta}dvdt

by the following stochastic differential equation:

\hat{\Phi}_{t}(z)=\int_{E(t)}H_{s}({\theta},z)1_{\{v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})\}}\mu(d{\theta},dv,ds),\quad{\cal T}_{t}=\int_{E(t)}C_{s}({\theta})1_{\{v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})\}}\mu(d{\theta},dv,ds)

where

E(t)=[0,2\pi)\times[0,\infty)\times(0,t]

and

H_{s}({\theta},z)=\Delta({\theta},z,C_{s}({\theta}),\hat{\Phi}_{s-}),\quad C_{s}({\theta})=c({\theta},\hat{\Phi}_{s-},{\cal T}_{s-}),\quad{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})={\lambda}({\theta},\hat{\Phi}_{s-},{\cal T}_{s-}).

We use the vector field
b=(\hat{b},b^{\operatorname{cap}})
of the
{\sigma}
-regularized LK
({\zeta})
equation (
14
),
written in (Schlicht function, capacity) coordinates.
We consider the disk solution
(x_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}=(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau}_{t})_{t<t_{\zeta}}
with initial capacity
{\tau}_{0}=0
,
which is given by

\hat{\phi}_{t}(z)=z,\quad{\tau}_{t}=\begin{cases}t,&\text{if ${\zeta}=0$},\\
{\zeta}^{-1}\log(1+{\zeta}t),&\text{if ${\zeta}\not=0$},\end{cases}\quad t_{\zeta}=\begin{cases}\infty,&\text{if ${\zeta}\geqslant 0$},\\
|{\zeta}|^{-1},&\text{if ${\zeta}<0$}.\end{cases}

(16)

We will compute the form of the interpolation formula in this case and then prove directly that it holds.
Note that

b(x_{t})=(\hat{b},b^{\operatorname{cap}})(\hat{\phi}_{t},{\tau}_{t})=(0,e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}})

and, for
y=(\hat{\psi},\psi^{\operatorname{cap}})
,

\nabla b(x_{t})y=-e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}}((Q+1)\hat{\psi},{\zeta}\psi^{\operatorname{cap}})

and the first-order variation at time
t
due to a variation
y
at time
s\leqslant t
is given by

\nabla\xi_{t-s}(x_{s})y=(e^{-({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{s})}P({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{s})\hat{\psi},e^{-{\zeta}({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{s})}\psi^{\operatorname{cap}}).

Write

for the compensated Poisson random measure

Fix
t\geqslant 0
and set
{\delta}_{s}={\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{s}
.
We alert the reader to the concealed dependence of
{\delta}_{s}
on

.
The martingale term
M_{t}=(\hat{M}_{t},M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t})
in the interpolation formula may then be written

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{E(t)}e^{-{\delta}_{s}}P({\delta}_{s})H_{s}({\theta},z)1_{\{v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})\}}\tilde{\mu}(d{\theta},dv,ds),

\displaystyle M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{E(t)}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{s}}C_{s}({\theta})1_{\{v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})\}}\tilde{\mu}(d{\theta},dv,ds).

The drift
{\beta}=(\hat{\beta},{\beta}^{\operatorname{cap}})
for

is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}(\hat{\phi},{\tau})(z)

\displaystyle=\fint_{0}^{2\pi}\Delta({\theta},z,c({\theta},\hat{\phi},{\tau}),\hat{\phi}){\lambda}({\theta},\hat{\phi},{\tau})d{\theta},

\displaystyle{\beta}^{\operatorname{cap}}(\hat{\phi},{\tau})

\displaystyle=\fint_{0}^{2\pi}c({\theta},\hat{\phi},{\tau}){\lambda}({\theta},\hat{\phi},{\tau})d{\theta}.

Write

The following interpolation identities may then be obtained formally by
splitting equation (

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
t<t_{\zeta}
and all
|z|>1

\hat{\Psi}_{t}(z)=\hat{M}_{t}(z)+\hat{A}_{t}(z),\quad\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}=M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}+A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}

(17)

where

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-{\delta}_{s}}P({\delta}_{s})\left(\hat{\beta}(\hat{\Phi}_{s},{\cal T}_{s})+e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}(Q+1)\hat{\Psi}_{s}\right)(z)ds,

\displaystyle A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{s}}\left({\beta}^{\operatorname{cap}}(\hat{\Phi}_{s},{\cal T}_{s})-e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}+{\zeta}e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{s}\right)ds.

Proof.

Fix
t<t_{\zeta}
.
For
x\in[0,t]
, recall that
{\delta}_{x}={\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{x}
and define for
|z|>1

\hat{\Psi}_{x,t}(z)=e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})(\hat{\Phi}_{x}-\hat{\phi}_{x})(z),\quad\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}=e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}({\cal T}_{x}-{\tau}_{x}).

Set

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{x,t}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{E(x)}e^{-{\delta}_{s}}P({\delta}_{s})H_{s}({\theta},z)1_{\{v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})\}}\tilde{\mu}(d{\theta},dv,ds),

\displaystyle M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{E(x)}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{s}}C_{s}({\theta})1_{\{v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{s}({\theta})\}}\tilde{\mu}(d{\theta},dv,ds)

and

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{x,t}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}e^{-{\delta}_{s}}P({\delta}_{s})\left(\hat{\beta}(\hat{\Phi}_{s},{\cal T}_{s})+e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}(Q+1)\hat{\Psi}_{s}\right)(z)ds,

\displaystyle A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{s}}\left({\beta}^{\operatorname{cap}}(\hat{\Phi}_{s},{\cal T}_{s})-e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}+{\zeta}e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{s}\right)ds.

We will show that, for all
x\in[0,t]
and all
|z|>1
,

\hat{\Psi}_{x,t}(z)=\hat{M}_{x,t}(z)+\hat{A}_{x,t}(z),\quad\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}=M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}+A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}.

The case
x=t
gives the claimed identities.
In the case
x=0
, all terms are
0
.
The left-hand and right-hand sides are piecewise continuously differentiable in
x
,
except for finitely many jumps, at the jump times of
(\Phi_{x})_{0\leqslant x\leqslant t}
,
which occur when
\mu
has an atom at
({\theta},v,x)
with
v\leqslant{\Lambda}_{x}({\theta})
.
It will suffice to check that the jumps and derivatives agree.
Now
\hat{A}_{x,t}(z)
and
A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}
are continuous in
x
and, at the jump times of
\Phi_{x}
,
the jumps in
\hat{\Psi}_{x,t}(z)
and
\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}
are given by

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{\Psi}_{x,t}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})\Delta\hat{\Phi}_{x}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})(e^{-C_{x}({\theta})}\hat{\Phi}_{x-}\circ F_{C_{x}({\theta})}({\theta},.)-\hat{\Phi}_{x-})(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})H_{x}({\theta},z)=\Delta\hat{M}_{x,t}(z)

and

\Delta\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}=e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}\Delta{\cal T}_{x}=e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}C_{x}({\theta})=\Delta M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}.

So it remains to check the derivatives.
We will use a spectral calculation for the semigroup of multiplier operators
P({\tau})=e^{-{\tau}Q}
,
whose justification is straightforward.
Recall that
\dot{\tau}_{t}=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}}
.
We have

\frac{d}{dx}{\delta}_{x}=-e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}},\quad\frac{d}{dx}e^{-{\delta}_{x}}=e^{-{\delta}_{x}}e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}},\quad\frac{d}{dx}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}={\zeta}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}

and

\frac{d}{dx}P({\delta}_{x})=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}QP({\delta}_{x}).

So, between the jump times, we have

\frac{d}{dx}\hat{\Psi}_{x,t}(z)=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})(Q+1)\hat{\Psi}_{x}(z),\quad\frac{d}{dx}\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}(z)=-e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}(1-{\zeta}\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x})

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\hat{M}_{x,t}(z)

\displaystyle=-\fint_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})H_{x}({\theta},z){\Lambda}_{x}({\theta})d{\theta}=-e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})\hat{\beta}(\hat{\Phi}_{x},{\cal T}_{x})(z),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}

\displaystyle=-\fint_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}C_{x}({\theta}){\Lambda}_{x}({\theta})d{\theta}=-e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}{\beta}^{\operatorname{cap}}(\hat{\Phi}_{x},{\cal T}_{x})

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\hat{A}_{x,t}(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-{\delta}_{x}}P({\delta}_{x})\left(\hat{\beta}(\hat{\Phi}_{x},{\cal T}_{x})+e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}(Q+1)\hat{\Psi}_{x}\right)(z),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}

\displaystyle=e^{-{\zeta}{\delta}_{x}}\left({\beta}^{\operatorname{cap}}(\hat{\Phi}_{x},{\cal T}_{x})-e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}+{\zeta}e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{x}}\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x}\right).

Hence, between the jump times,

\frac{d}{dx}\hat{\Psi}_{x,t}(z)=\frac{d}{dx}(\hat{M}_{x,t}(z)+\hat{A}_{x,t}(z)),\quad\frac{d}{dx}\Psi^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}=\frac{d}{dx}(M^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t}+A^{\operatorname{cap}}_{x,t})

as required.
∎

c_{1},c_{2}\in(0,c]
,

|(a_{0}(c_{1})-2c_{1})-(a_{0}(c_{2})-2c_{2})|\leqslant Cc^{1/2}|c_{1}-c_{2}|

and, for all
|z-1|\geqslant C\sqrt{c}
,

\left|\left(\log\left(\frac{F_{c_{1}}(z)}{z}\right)-c_{1}\right)-\left(\log\left(\frac{F_{c_{2}}(z)}{z}\right)-c_{2}\right)\right|\leqslant\frac{C|c_{1}-c_{2}|}{|z-1|}

and

\left|\left(\log\left(\frac{F_{c_{1}}(z)}{z}\right)-c_{1}-\frac{a_{0}(c_{1})}{z-1}\right)-\left(\log\left(\frac{F_{c_{2}}(z)}{z}\right)-c_{2}-\frac{a_{0}(c_{2})}{z-1}\right)\right|\leqslant\frac{C\sqrt{c}|c_{1}-c_{2}|}{|z-1|^{2}}

and

\left|\left(e^{-c_{1}}F_{c_{1}}(z)-z-\frac{a_{0}(c_{1})z}{z-1}\right)-\left(e^{-c_{2}}F_{c_{2}}(z)-z-\frac{a_{0}(c_{2})z}{z-1}\right)\right|\leqslant\frac{C\sqrt{c}|c_{1}-c_{2}||z|}{|z-1|^{2}}.

For our final particle estimates, we use the following integral representation for the family of particle maps

F_{c}(z)=z+\int_{0}^{c}DF_{t}(z)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}\mu_{t}(d{\theta})dt

for some measurable family of probability measures
(\mu_{t}:t\in(0,\infty))
,
with
\mu_{t}
supported on
\{{\theta}:|e^{i{\theta}}-1|\leqslant{\delta}(t)\}
for all
t
.
This follows from our requirements that the particles
P^{(c)}
have capacity
c
,
are contained in
\{|z-1|\leqslant{\delta}(c)\}
and are nested, by the Loewner–Kufarev theory.
Our condition (
4
) and the inequality (
88
)
then give a constant
C({\Lambda})<\infty
such that

\operatorname{supp}\mu_{t}\subseteq\{{\theta}:|e^{i{\theta}}-1|\leqslant C\sqrt{t}\}.

(108)

Define holomorphic functions
L_{t}
and
Q_{t}
on
\{|z|>1\}
by

L_{t}(z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}l_{t}({\theta},z)\mu_{t}(d{\theta}),\quad Q_{t}(z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}q_{t}({\theta},z)\mu_{t}(d{\theta})

(109)

where

l_{t}({\theta},z)=\left(D\log\left(\frac{F_{t}(z)}{z}\right)+1\right)\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}},\quad q_{t}({\theta},z)=DF_{t}(z)\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}-e^{t}z-\frac{2e^{t}e^{i{\theta}}z}{z-1}.

Note that
l_{t}({\theta},z)\to 1
and
q_{t}({\theta},z)\to 0
as
z\to\infty
, uniformly in
{\theta}
.
It is then straightforward to show the integral representations

\log\left(\frac{F_{c}(z)}{z}\right)=\int_{0}^{c}L_{t}(z)dt,\quad e^{c}\left(e^{-c}F_{c}(z)-z-\frac{a_{0}(c)z}{z-1}\right)=\int_{0}^{c}Q_{t}(z)dt.

Proposition 7.5
.

There is a constant
C({\Lambda})<\infty
with the following property.
For all
t\leqslant 1/C
and all
|z|>1
,

|L_{t}(z)|\leqslant\frac{C|z|}{|z-1|}+\frac{C\sqrt{t}|z|}{|z-1|^{2}},\quad|Q_{t}(z)|\leqslant\frac{C\sqrt{t}|z|}{|z-1|^{2}}.

Proof.

We give the details for the second estimate, leaving the first which is similar but simpler to the reader.
We split
q_{t}({\theta},z)=g_{t}({\theta},z)+h_{t}({\theta},z)
, where

g_{t}({\theta},z)=(DF_{t}(z)-e^{t}z)\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}},\quad h_{t}({\theta},z)=e^{t}z\left(\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}-1-\frac{2e^{i{\theta}}}{z-1}\right).

Now

\frac{z+e^{i{\theta}}}{z-e^{i{\theta}}}-1-\frac{2e^{i{\theta}}}{z-1}=\frac{2e^{i{\theta}}(e^{i{\theta}}-1)}{(z-e^{i{\theta}})(z-1)}

so, on the support of
\mu_{t}
, we have, for
|z-1|\geqslant 2C\sqrt{t}
,

|h_{t}({\theta},z)|\leqslant\frac{2Ce^{t}\sqrt{t}|z|}{|z-1|^{2}}

where
C
is the constant in (
108
).
On the other hand, we showed above that, for all
|z|>1
,

|F_{t}(z)-e^{t}z|\leqslant C\sqrt{t}

and
F_{t}
extends by reflection to a holomorphic function on
\{|z-1|>C\sqrt{t}\}
satisfying the same inequality.
Hence, by Cauchy’s integral formula, for
|z-1|\geqslant 2C\sqrt{t}
,

|DF_{t}(z)-e^{t}z|\leqslant\frac{C\sqrt{t}|z|}{|z-1|}

and so, for
{\theta}
in the support of
\mu_{t}
,

|g_{t}({\theta},z)|\leqslant\frac{C\sqrt{t}|z|}{|z-1|^{2}}.

We have shown that, for all
|z-1|\geqslant C\sqrt{t}
,

|Q_{t}(z)|\leqslant\frac{C\sqrt{t}|z|}{|z-1|^{2}}.

∎

7.2
Operator inequalities

Recall that, for a measurable function
f
on
\{|z|>1\}
, for
p\in[1,\infty)
and
r>1
, we set

\|f\|_{p,r}=\left(\fint_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i{\theta}})|^{p}d{\theta}\right)^{1/p},\quad\|f\|_{\infty,r}=\sup_{{\theta}\in[0,2\pi)}|f(re^{i{\theta}})|.

Suppose that
f
is holomorphic and is bounded at
\infty
.
It is standard that, for
\rho\in(1,r)
,

\|f\|_{p,r}\leqslant\|f\|_{p,\rho},\quad\|f\|_{\infty,r}\leqslant\left(\frac{\rho}{r-\rho}\right)^{1/p}\|f\|_{p,\rho}.

(110)

Moreover, there is an absolute constant
C<\infty
such that

\|Df\|_{p,r}\leqslant\frac{C\rho}{r-\rho}\|f\|_{p,\rho}

(111)

where
Df(z)=zf^{\prime}(z)
.
The function
f
has a Laurent expansion

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}z^{-k}.

Let
M
be an operator which acts as multiplication by
m_{k}
on the the
k
th Laurent coefficient.
Thus

Mf(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{k}f_{k}z^{-k}.

Assume that there exists a finite constant
A>0
such that, for all
k\geqslant 0
,

|m_{k}|\leqslant A

and, for all integers
K\geqslant 0
,

\sum_{k=2^{K}}^{2^{K+1}-1}|m_{k+1}-m_{k}|\leqslant A.

Then, by the Marcinkiewicz multiplier theorem
[
18
, Vol. II, Theorem 4.14]
, for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,
there is a constant
C=C(p)<\infty
such that, for all
r>1
,

\|Mf\|_{p,r}\leqslant CA\|f\|_{p,r}.

(112)

We will use also the following estimate.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
f
and
g
be holomorphic in
\{|z|>1\}
and bounded at
\infty
.
Set

f_{\theta}(z)=f(e^{-i{\theta}}z).

Let
M
be a multiplier operator and set

h(z)=\left(\fint_{0}^{2\pi}|M(f_{\theta}.g)|^{2}(z)d{\theta}\right)^{1/2}.

Then, for all
r,\rho>1
, we have

\|h\|_{p,r}\leqslant\|M\|_{p,\rho\to r}\|g\|_{p,\rho}\|f\|_{2,\rho}

where

\|M\|_{p,\rho\to r}=\sup\{\|Mf\|_{p,r}:\|f\|_{p,\rho}\leqslant 1\}.

Proof.

We can write

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}z^{-k},\quad g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g_{k}z^{-k},\quad Mf(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{k}f_{k}z^{-k}.

Then

M(f_{\theta}.g)(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}m_{j+k}f_{k}g_{j}e^{i{\theta}k}z^{-(k+j)}

so

h(z)^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|f_{k}|^{2}|M(\tau_{k}g)(z)|^{2}

where
\tau_{k}g(z)=z^{-k}g(z)
.
Hence

\displaystyle\|h\|_{p,r}\|h^{2}\|_{p/2,r}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|f_{k}|^{2}\|M(\tau_{k}g)\|^{2}_{p,r}\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|f_{k}|^{2}\|M\|^{2}_{p,\rho\rightarrow r}\|\tau_{k}g\|^{2}_{p,\rho}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|f_{k}|^{2}\rho^{-2k}\|M\|^{2}_{p,\rho\rightarrow r}\|g\|^{2}_{p,\rho}=\|M\|^{2}_{p,\rho\rightarrow r}\|f\|^{2}_{2,\rho}\|g\|^{2}_{p,\rho}.

∎

7.3
Push-out estimates

Recall that we write
({\tau}_{t})_{t<t_{\zeta}}
for the capacity of the disk solution to the LK
({\zeta})
equation of initial capacity
{\tau}_{0}=0
,
and that
\dot{\tau}_{t}=e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}}
.
We have found several times an integral such as

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)^{p}ds

where, for
r>1
and
p\geqslant 1
, we set

r_{s}=\begin{cases}e^{(1-{\zeta})({\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{s})}r,&\text{ if ${\zeta}\geqslant 0$},\\
e^{{\tau}_{t}-{\tau}_{s}}r,&\text{ if ${\zeta}<0$}.\end{cases}

We record here an estimate on such integrals which improves on the obvious maximum bound
t(r/(r-1))^{p}

by using the fact that
r_{s}
pushes out as
s
decreases from
t
, away from the singularity at
r=1
.

Lemma 7.7
.

For all
{\zeta}\in[0,1)
, all
r>1
and all
p\geqslant 2
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)^{p}ds\leqslant\frac{2(1+t)}{1-{\zeta}}\left(\frac{r}{r-1}\right)^{p-1}

and

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)ds\leqslant\frac{2(1+t)}{1-{\zeta}}\left(1+\log\left(\frac{r}{r-1}\right)\right).

Moreover, the inequalities remain valid for
{\zeta}<0
and
t<t_{\zeta}
if the denominator
1-{\zeta}
is removed from the right-hand sides.

Proof.

The left-hand sides are increasing in
{\zeta}
, so the claim for
{\zeta}<0
follows from the case
{\zeta}=0
.
For
p>2
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)^{p}ds\leqslant\left(\frac{r}{r-1}\right)^{p-2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)^{2}ds

so, for the first inequality, it will suffice to consider the case
p=2
.
For
r\geqslant 2
it suffices to use the obvious maximum bound.
Suppose then that
r=r_{t}\in(1,2)
.
Choose
t_{0}\in[0,t]
so that
r_{t_{0}}=2
if possible, setting
t_{0}=0
otherwise.
Then
r_{t_{0}}\leqslant 2
and

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)^{2}ds\leqslant 2t\left(\frac{r}{r-1}\right).

Note that

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{1}{r_{s}-1}\right)=(1-{\zeta})e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}\frac{r_{s}}{(r_{s}-1)^{2}}

so

(1-{\zeta})\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)^{2}ds\leqslant 2e^{{\zeta}{\tau}_{t}}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(1-{\zeta})e^{-{\zeta}{\tau}_{s}}\frac{r_{s}}{(r_{s}-1)^{2}}ds=2(1+{\zeta}t)\left(\frac{1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{r_{t_{0}}-1}\right).

The first claimed inequality follows.
A similar argument leads to the inequalities

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)ds\leqslant 2t,\quad(1-{\zeta})\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\left(\frac{r_{s}}{r_{s}-1}\right)ds\leqslant(1+{\zeta}t)\log\left(\frac{r_{t_{0}}-1}{r-1}\right)

from which the second claimed inequality follows.
∎

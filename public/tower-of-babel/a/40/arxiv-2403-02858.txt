Metrically differentiable set-valued functions and their local linear approximants
Nira Dyn, Elza Farkhi, Alona Mokhov

Abstract
A new notion of metric differentiability of set-valued functions at a point is introduced in terms of right and left limits of special set-valued metric divided differences of first order. A local metric linear approximant of a metrically differentiable set-valued function at a point is defined and studied. This local approximant may be regarded as a special realization of the set-valued Euler approximants of M. S. Nikolskii and the directives of Z. Artstein. Error estimates for the local metric linear approximant are obtained. In particular, second order approximation is derived for a class of “strongly” metrically differentiable set-valued maps.

Key words:
compact sets in Euclidean spaces, set-valued functions, metric divided differences of first order, metric differentiability, local metric linear approximants

Introduction

A differential calculus of set-valued functions (SVFs, set-valued maps, multifunctions) is playing a significant role in different areas of applied mathematics.
Various notions of set-valued derivatives, motivated by different needs and applications, have been introduced and studied in the last five decades.
The earlier papers focus on differentiabilty of multifunctions with convex images (see e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
7
]
).
Different concepts of set-valued differentiability (such as Hukuhara-derivatives, contingent derivatives, graphical derivatives, co-derivatives, generalized differentiability)
are introduced, discussed, and studied, for instance, in
[
3
,
13
,
16
,
18
,
19
]
.
In
[
12
,
15
,
21
]
the notions of affine, semi-affine, eclipsing, quasi-affine mappings are suggested for the role of set-valued ”linear” approximants.
Derivatives of set-valued functions also arise in probability and statistics, where another approach still might be required (see e.g.
[
14
]
).

In a series of works (e.g.
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
) we have developed a metric approach to the approximation of SVFs, mapping a compact interval to general
(not necessarily convex) compact sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
This approach, inspired by the ideas from
[
1
]
, includes metric tools such as metric chains, metric linear combinations, metric integral.
The metric tools are applied to adapt classical sample-based approximation operators to multifunctions.
In
[
6
,
8
,
9
]
we obtain approximation order at most
1
for SVFs which are continuous or of bounded variation.
In
[
11
]
we define the notion of metric divided differences of any order
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and obtain high order approximation by metric piecewise-polynomial interpolation,
without introducing a notion of derivative for set-valued functions.

In this paper, we introduce the concept of metric differentiability for a univariate multifunction
F
with the aim of achieving a local approximation of
F
with an error of order higher than 1.
We apply this concept in constructing and analyzing a local metric linear approximant. Our approach yields error estimates beyond first order (including second order) for these approximants,
extending classical error estimates for real-valued functions to set-valued functions.

The notion of local metric linear approximant, introduced here may be regarded as a special unique “realization”
of the Euler approximants of M.S. Nikolskii
[
17
]
, as well as of the “directives” of Artstein
[
2
]
.
They both have proposed a definition of a set-valued derivative by a local “linear approximant” of the given multifunction
in a neghborhood of a given point with error of order
{o}(h)
measured in the Hausdorff metric.
They both have noticed that such a definition does not provide uniqueness of the derivative.

We define here the notion of metric divided differences of first order anchored at a point
y\in F(x_{0})

and introduce one-sided (left and right) metric derivatives anchored at
y
as the one-sided limits (left and right) of the corresponding metric divided differences.
We construct the local metric linear approximant of a metrically differentiable multifunction at a point
x_{0}
and obtain rate of approximation
o(h)
. To get a higher order,
O(h^{m})
,
m>1
,
we introduce a higher order smoothness property for SVFs related to the rate of convergence of the metric divided differences to the one-sided metric derivatives.

The paper is organized as follows:
In the next section we present preliminary material.
In Section 3 we present metric divided differences of first order, define the one-sided metric derivatives and investigate their properties.
In Section 4 we introduce and study the local metric linear approximant.

2
Preliminaries

We use the folowing notation. The Euclidean norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is denoted by
|\cdot|
.
In this paper we consider sets in
{\cal K}_{n}
, where
{\cal K}_{n}
denotes the collection of nonempty compact subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The distance from a point
{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
to a set
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is denoted by
{\mbox{dist}(x,A)=\min_{a\in A}|x-a|}
.

The set of all projections of
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
on a set
B\in{\cal K}_{n}
is

\Pi_{B}{(a)}=\{b\in B:|a-b|=\operatorname{dist}(a,B)\}.

We denote the collection of all metric pairs of two sets
A,B\in{\cal K}_{n}
by

\Pi({A},{B})=\{(a,b)\in A\times B:\;a\in\Pi_{A}{(b)}\;\,\mbox{or}\;\,b\in\Pi_{B}{(a)}\}.

Remark 2.1
.

It follows from the compactness of
A,B\in{\cal K}_{n}
that
\Pi({A},{B})
is a non-empty compact set.
(see Lemma 2.4 in
[
11
]
)

Using the metric pairs, the Hausdorff distance can written as

{\mbox{h}aus}(A,B)=\max\{|a-b|:\;(a,b)\in\Pi({A},{B})\}.

(1)

The space
{\cal K}_{n}
is a complete metric space with respect to this metric
[
20
]
.
In this paper we regard convergence of sets and continuity of set-valued maps
with respect to the Hausdorff metric.

We use in the paper the following property of the Hausdorff metric, which is easy to check:

{\mbox{h}aus}(\bigcup\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda},\bigcup\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}B_{\lambda})\leq\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\mbox{h}aus}(A_{\lambda},B_{\lambda}),\quad\Lambda\;\hbox{is an index set}.

(2)

For a given set
A
we use the notation

\lambda A=\left\{\lambda a\ :\ a\in A\right\},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},

and

\|A\|=\max\left\{|a|\ :\ a\in A\right\}.

A linear Minkowski combination of two sets
A,B
is

\lambda A+\mu B=\{\lambda a+\mu b:a\in A,b\in B\},\quad\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The following relations between operations on sets and the Hausdorff metric are easy to verify.

{\mbox{h}aus}(\{x\}+A,\{x\}+B)={\mbox{h}aus}(A,B),\qquad{\mbox{h}aus}(\lambda A,\lambda B)=|\lambda|{\mbox{h}aus}(A,B),

(3)

where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\{x\}
is the set consisting of the single element
x
.

We recall here the notion of a metric chain and of a metric linear combination
[
10
]
.

Given a finite sequence of sets
A_{0},\ldots,A_{m}\in{\cal K}_{n}
,
m\geq 1
,
the collection of all metric chains of
{A_{0},\ldots,A_{m}}
is

{\mathrm{CH}}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m}):=\left\{(a_{0},\ldots,a_{m}):\;(a_{i},a_{i+1})\in\Pi({A_{i}},{A_{i+1}}),\ i=0,1,\ldots,m-1\right\}.

(4)

For
m=0
we define
{\mathrm{CH}}(A_{0})=A_{0}
. Note that
{\mathrm{CH}}(A_{0},A_{1})=\Pi({A_{0}},{A_{1}})
.

The metric linear combination of the sets
A_{0},\ldots,A_{m}\in{\cal K}_{n}
, is

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{i}A_{i}:=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\,:(a_{0},\ldots,a_{m})\in{\mathrm{CH}}(A_{0},\ldots,A_{m})\right\}.

(5)

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

In the special case
m=1
and
\lambda_{0}=1
,
\lambda_{1}=-1
we obtain the metric difference of two
sets
A,B
defined in
[
8
]
,

A\ominus B:=\{\ a-b:(a,b)\in\Pi({A},{B})\ \}.

(2)

In the special case
m=1
and
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\in[0,1]
,
\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}=1
,
the metric linear combination is the binary operation between compact sets, introduced in
[
1
]
.

(3)

The set
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{i}A_{i}
is compact (see
[
11
]
)).

In this work we consider univariate set-valued functions with values in
{\cal K}_{n}
.
The graph of a multifunction
{F:[a,b]\rightarrow{\cal K}_{n}}
is the set

Graph(F)=\{(x,y):x\in[a,b],\,y\in F(x)\}
.

3
First metric divided differences and one-sided metric derivative

We start with defining divided differences.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
F:(a,b)\to{\cal K}_{n}
. Fix
x_{0},x\in(a,b)
,
x_{0}\neq x
and
y_{0}\in F(x_{0})
. Define
the
first metric divided difference of
F
at
x_{0},x
, anchored at
y_{0}
, as the set
of vectors

[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y_{0}}:=\ \left\{\frac{y-y_{0}}{x-x_{0}}:\,(y_{0},y)\in\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x)})\right\}.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

By the compactness of
F(x),F(x_{0})
, and by Remark
2.1
,
[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y_{0}}
is non-empty and compact.

(ii)

If
(x_{0},y_{0})
is in the interior of
Graph(F)
, then
y_{0}\in F(x)
for
x
in a small enough neighborhood of
x_{0}
, thus
(y_{0},y_{0})\in\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x)})
is the only metric pair containing
y_{0}
, and therefore
[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y_{0}}=\{0\}
.

(iii)

By Definition
3.1
,
F(x)=\bigcup_{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}\{\{y_{0}\}+(x-x_{0})[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y_{0}}\}
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
F:(0,2)\to{\cal K}_{1}
be defined by
F(x)=\{x^{\alpha},x^{\beta}\}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\alpha\neq\beta
, and let
x_{0}\in(0,2)
,
x=x_{0}+h
with
|h|\neq 0
sufficiently small. Then for
x_{0},x_{0}+h\leq 1
or
x_{0},x_{0}+h\geq 1

\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x_{0}+h)})=\left\{\big(x_{0}^{\alpha},(x_{0}+h)^{\alpha}\big),\big(x_{0}^{\beta},(x_{0}+h)^{\beta}\big)\right\}.

For
x_{0}=1
we get
[1,1+h]^{M}F|_{1}=\left\{\frac{(1+h)^{\alpha}-1}{h},\frac{(1+h)^{\beta}-1}{h}\right\}
, while for
x_{0}\neq 1
we have

[x_{0},x_{0}+h]^{M}F|_{x_{0}^{\alpha}}=\left\{\frac{(x_{0}+h)^{\alpha}-x_{0}^{\alpha}}{h}\right\}\quad,\quad[x_{0},x_{0}+h]^{M}F|_{x_{0}^{\beta}}=\left\{\frac{(x_{0}+h)^{\beta}-x_{0}^{\beta}}{h}\right\}.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
F:(a,b)\to{\cal K}_{n}
. Fix
x_{0},x\in(a,b)
,
x_{0}\neq x
. Define
the
first metric divided difference of
F
at
x_{0},x

as

[x_{0},x]^{M}F:=\bigcup\limits_{y\in F(x_{0})}[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y},

Remark 3.5
.

(i)

The set
[x_{0},x]^{M}F
is the metric divided difference of order
1
in
[
11
, Remark 5.2]
. It is compact as is shown in Lemma 5.5 there.

(ii)

Using the metric difference of two sets (see Remark
2.2
), the first metric divided difference can be expressed, similarly to
the divided difference of real-valued functions, as

[x_{0},x_{1}]^{M}F=\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{0}}\left(F(x_{1})\ominus F(x_{0})\right).

It is clear that one can interchange the points
x_{0},x_{1}
in this divided difference.

(iii)

In view of (
1
),
{\mbox{h}aus}(A,B)=\|A\ominus B\|
and by the second property in (
3
),
a multifunction
F
is Lipschitz with a constant
L
in a domain if and only if

\|[x_{0},x_{1}]^{M}F\|=\frac{{\mbox{h}aus}(F(x_{1}),F(x_{0}))}{|x_{1}-x_{0}|}\leq L

for any
x_{0},x_{1}
in this domain.

(iv)

For a single-valued function, Definition
3.4
coincides with the classical definition of divided difference of first order.

Now we introduce the right and the left metric derivatives.
We use the notations
\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}
and
\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{-}}
for the right and left limits, respectively.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
F:(a,b)\to{\cal K}_{n},\ x_{0}\in(a,b)
.

(i)

We say that
F
is
metrically differentiable from the right at
x_{0}
if for any
y\in F(x_{0})
there is a non-empty set
D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y}
of vectors satisfying

D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y}=\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y}

uniformly in
y\in F(x_{0})
, namely, for any
\epsilon>0

there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x
with
0<x-x_{0}<\delta

\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}\left(D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y},[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y}\right)<\epsilon.

We call the set
D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y}

the right metric derivative of
F
at
x_{0}

anchored at

y\in F(x_{0})
.

The set

{\mathfrak{D}}^{M}_{+}F=\left\{D^{M}_{+}F(x)|_{y}:\;\,(x,y)\in Graph(F)\right\}

determines a set-valued vector field of the right metric derivative of
F
on the graph of
F
.

We call the set

D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})=\bigcup_{y\in F(x_{0})}D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y}=\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\frac{1}{x-x_{0}}\left(F(x)\ominus F(x_{0})\right)

the right metric derivative of
F
at
x_{0}
.

(ii)

Similarly, we say that
F
is
metrically differentiable from the left at
x_{0}
and
define the sets
D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})|_{y}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}^{M}_{-}F
,
D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})
.

(iii)

If
F
is both metrically differentiable from the right and from the left at
x_{0}
, then we say that
F
is
metrically differentiable
at
x_{0}
.

Below are some observations concerning Definition
3.6
.

Remark 3.7
.

(i)

Note that in
D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})
,
D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})
the information on the attachments to points in
Graph(F)
is lost in contrary to
{\mathfrak{D}}^{M}_{+}F
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{M}_{-}F
.

(ii)

Although for single-valued functions the existence of derivative is defined when the right derivative equals the left one, for set-valued functions the equality
D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})=D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})

is not satisfied in many cases, and we do not require it for metric differentiability.

(iii)

Note that the sets
D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y},D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})|_{y},D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0}),D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})
are compact as Hausdorff
limits of compact sets (see Remark
3.2
(i), Remark
2.2
(ii)) and are unique as limits in a complete metric space.

(iv)

An equivalent definition of the right metric differentiability of
F
at
x_{0}
is the existence of
a continuous function
\omega_{x_{0}}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\omega_{x_{0}}(0)=0

and such that for
x
in a right neighborhood of
x_{0}

\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}([x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y},D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y})\leq\omega_{x_{0}}(|x-x_{0}|).

Similarly, for the left metric differentiability of
F
at
x_{0},
the following condition should hold

\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}([x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y},D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})|_{y})\leq\omega_{x_{0}}(|x-x_{0}|).

where
x
is within a left neighborhood of
x_{0}
.

.

Consider

The following properties of the metric one-sided derivatives are derived from their definition.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
F:(a,b)\to{\cal K}_{n}
be metrically differentiable from the right (left) at
x_{0}\in(a,b)
.

(i)

F
is right (left) continuous at
x_{0}
.

(ii)

If
F={\rm const}
, then
D^{M}_{+}F(x)|_{y}=\{0\}

(\,D^{M}_{-}F(x)|_{y}=\{0\}\,)
for all
y\in F(x)
,
x\in(a,b)
.

(iii)

D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}}\neq\{0\}

(\,D^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}}\neq\{0\}\,)
only for
(x_{0},y_{0})
on the boundary of
Graph(F)
.

Proof.

We prove the claims for the right derivative. The proofs for the left derivative are analogous.

(i)

By Definition
3.6

(i)
,

\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\sup_{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}\left(D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}},\left\{\frac{y-y_{0}}{x-x_{0}}\ :\ (y_{0},y)\in\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x)})\right\}\right)=0.

Multiplying the two sets above by
x-x_{0}
and using the second relation in (
3
),
we get

\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\sup_{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|}{\mbox{h}aus}\left((x-x_{0})D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}},\{{y-y_{0}}:\ (y_{0},y)\in\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x)})\}\right)=0,

and it follows that

\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\sup_{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}\left((x-x_{0})D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}},\{{y-y_{0}}:\ (y_{0},y)\in\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x)})\}\right)=0.

Since
D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})
is compact (see Remark
3.7
(iii)), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\sup_{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}\|(x-x_{0})D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}}\|=0,

which leads to

\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}\sup\{|y-y_{0}|\ :\ (y_{0},y)\in\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x)})\}=0.

Thus by (
1
)

\lim_{x\to x_{0}^{+}}{\mbox{h}aus}(F(x),F(x_{0}))=0,

which proves the continuity from the right of
F
at
x_{0}
.

(ii)

This property follows from the definition of the metric divided difference and the observation
that if
F
is constant, then for any two points
x_{0},x_{1}
,

\Pi({F(x_{0})},{F(x_{1})})=\{(y,y):y\in F(x_{0})\}

and therefore
[x_{0},x_{1}]^{M}F|_{y}=\{0\}
for any
y\in F(x_{0})
.

(iii)

Let
(x_{0},y_{0})
be an interior point of
Graph(F)
. By Remark
3.2
(ii),
[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y_{0}}=\{0\}
for
x
in a small enough neighborhood of
x_{0}
, implying that
D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}}=\{0\}
.

∎

4
The local metric linear approximant and
\alpha
-differentiability

In this section we define and study the rate of approximation of the local
metric linear approximant of the multifunction
F
at a point
x_{0}\in(a,b)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in(a,b)
and
F
be metrically differentiable at
x_{0}
.

(i)

The
right local metric linear approximant
of
F
at
x_{0}
anchored
at
y_{0}\in F(x_{0})
is defined by

L^{M}_{+}F|_{y_{0}}(x)=\{y_{0}\}+(x-x_{0})D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y_{0}},\qquad x\geq x_{0},\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The
right local metric linear approximant of
F
at
x_{0}
is the set-valued function

L^{M}_{+}F(x)=\bigcup\limits_{y\in F(x_{0})}L^{M}_{+}F|_{y}(x).

(ii)

Similarly, we define the
left local metric linear approximant anchored
at
y_{0}\in F(x_{0})
,

L^{M}_{-}F|_{y_{0}}(x)
, and the
left local metric linear approximant

L^{M}_{-}F(x)
.

(iii)

The
(two-sided) local metric linear approximant
of
F
at
x_{0}
anchored at
{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}
is

L^{M}F|_{y_{0}}(x)=\begin{cases}L^{M}_{+}F|_{y_{0}}(x),&x\geq x_{0},\\
L^{M}_{-}F|_{y_{0}}(x),&x<x_{0}.\end{cases}

The
(two-sided) local metric linear approximant
of
F
at
x_{0}
is the set-valued function

L^{M}F(x)=\begin{cases}L^{M}_{+}F(x),&x\geq x_{0},\\
L^{M}_{-}F(x),&x<x_{0}.\end{cases}

Obviously
L^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})=L^{M}_{-}F(x_{0})=L^{M}F(x_{0})=F(x_{0})
.

The following observations follow from this definition.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
F:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\cal K}_{n}
be metrically differentiable at
x_{0}
. Then for
x\in(a,b)

(i)

{\mbox{h}aus}\left(L^{M}F|_{y_{0}}(x),\{y_{0}\}+(x-x_{0})[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y_{0}}\right)=o(|x-x_{0}|)
uniformly in
{y_{0}\in F(x_{0})}
.

(ii)

{\mbox{h}aus}(F(x),L^{M}F(x))=o(|x-x_{0}|)
.

Proof.

(i) follows directly from Remark
3.7
(iv) and (
3
).

(ii) follows from (i), (
2
) and Remark
3.2
(iii).
∎

Note that (ii) in the above proposition implies that
L^{M}_{+}F(x)
is a right directive
in the sense of Artstein
[
2
, Definition 5.5]
or local approximation of first order in the sense of Nikolskiĭ
[
17
, Definition 1]
.

To obtain error estimates of order
1+\alpha
(
\alpha>0
) for the local linear approximant,
we introduce the notion of
\alpha
-differentiability of a set-valued function at a point.

Definition 4.3
.

(i)

Let a multifunction
F
be metrically differentiable from the right at
x_{0}
.
We call
F

right metrically
\alpha
-differentiable
at
x_{0}
with
\alpha>0
,
if there is a constant
L>0
and a right neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
such that for all
x\in U

\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}([x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y},D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y})\leq L|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}.

F
is called
strongly right metrically differentiable
at
x_{0}
if
\alpha=1
, namely

\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}([x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y},D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y})\leq L|x-x_{0}|.

(ii)

Similarly we define these notions from the left.

(iii)

If
F
is right and left metrically
\alpha
-differentiable at
x_{0}
,
\alpha>0
,
then we say that
F
is
metrically
\alpha
-differentiable
at
x_{0}
with
\alpha>0
.
We say that
F
is
strongly metrically differentiable
at
x_{0}
if it is strongly metrically differentiable from the right and from the left at
x_{0}
.

Remark 4.4
.

It is easy to verify that any real-valued function with Hölder continuous derivative with exponent
\alpha\in(0,1]

in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
is metrically
\alpha
-differentiable at
{x_{0}}
.

The next theorem shows that the local linear approximant of a metrically
\alpha
-differentiable multifunction
has order of approximation
1+\alpha
.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
F
is metrically
\alpha
-differentiable at
x_{0}
, then for
x
from a neighborhood of
x_{0}

{\mbox{h}aus}\left(F(x),L^{M}F(x)\right)\leq L|x-x_{0}|^{1+\alpha}.

Proof.

We consider the case
x>x_{0}
. For
x<x_{0}
the proof is similar.

By Definition
4.1
, by Remark
3.2
(iii) and by (
2
) we get for any
y\in F(x_{0})

\displaystyle{\mbox{h}aus}(F(x),L^{M}F(x))

\displaystyle={\mbox{h}aus}(F(x),L^{M}_{+}F(x))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}\left(\{y\}+(x-x_{0})[x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y}\ ,L^{M}_{+}F|_{y}(x)\right).

Then, using properties (
3
) of the Hausdorff distance and Definitions
4.1
,
4.3
,
we get

\\
haus(F(x),L^{M}_{+}F(x))\leq|x-x_{0}|\sup_{y\in F(x_{0})}{\mbox{h}aus}([x_{0},x]^{M}F|_{y},D^{M}_{+}F(x_{0})|_{y})\leq L|x-x_{0}|^{1+\alpha},

which proves the claim for
x>x_{0}
.
∎

A direct consequence of Theorem
4.5
is

Corollary 4.6
.

If
F
is strongly metrically differentiable at
x_{0}
, then

{\mbox{h}aus}(F(x),L^{M}F(x))=O(|x-x_{0}|^{2}).

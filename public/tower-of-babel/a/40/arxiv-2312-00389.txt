Abstract
. We prove sample path moderate deviation principles (MDP) for the current and the tagged particle in the symmetric simple exclusion process, which extends the results in [ 39 ] , where the MDP was only proved at any fixed time.



1.
Introduction

The exclusion process is informally defined as a family of indistinguishable particles performing random walks on some graph subjected to the exclusion rule,
i.e.
, there is at most one particle at each site. It has been a long-standing problem to investigate the behaviors of a typical particle in the exclusion process, which is called the tagged particle. Due to the interactions with the other particles, the replacement of the tagged particle itself is not a Markov process. Despite that, much progress has been made since Spitzer introduced the exclusion process in
[
36
]
. We refer the readers to
[
24
,
21
]
and references therein for an excellent survey on the tagged particle process.

The model is usually called the symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP) if the underlying graph is the one-dimensional infinite lattice
\mathbb{Z}
and a particle jumps to its left and right neighbors at equal rates
1/2
. Since the total number of particles is conserved by the dynamics, the Bernoulli product measure with constant density
\rho\in[0,1]
, denoted by
\nu_{\rho}
, is invariant for the SSEP, see
[
23
]
for example. Assume the initial distribution of the SSEP is the Bernoulli product measure
\nu_{\rho}
conditioned on having a particle at the origin, and let
X(t)
be the position of this tagged particle at time
t
. Since particles cannot jump over each other in the SSEP, the tagged particle turns out to be sub-diffusive. Precisely speaking, Arratia
[
1
]
and De Masi and Ferrari
[
5
]
showed that

\frac{X_{t}}{t^{1/4}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{X}^{2}),\quad t\rightarrow+\infty,

where
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{X}^{2})
is the normal distribution with mean zero and variance
\sigma_{X}^{2}:=\sqrt{2/\pi}(1-\rho)/\rho
. The above central limit theorem was then extended to the invariance principle by Peligrad and Sethuraman
[
27
]
,

\Big\{\frac{X_{tN^{2}}}{N^{1/2}}:0\leq t\leq T\Big\}\Rightarrow\big\{B_{t}^{1/4}:0\leq t\leq T\big\},\quad N\rightarrow+\infty,

where
B_{\cdot}^{1/4}
is the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
1/4
and
T>0
is fixed. Jara and Landim
[
15
]
also proved central limit theorems for the tagged particle when the initial distribution of the SSEP is non-equilibrium. Recently, Conroy and Sethuraman
[
4
]
showed that the scaled position of the tagged particle converges to a Gumble limit law when the process starts from the step configuration.

Large and moderate deviation behaviors of the tagged particle in the SSEP have also been studied. The large deviation behaviors were investigated in
[
33
,
12
]
. When the initial distribution of the SSEP is the Bernoulli product measure
\nu_{\rho}
conditioned on having a particle at the origin, the same authors
[
39
]
proved that for any
t>0
, the sequence
\{X(tN^{2})/a_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies moderate deviation principles with decay rate
a_{N}^{2}/N
and with rate function
\sqrt{2\pi}\rho\alpha^{2}/[4(1-\rho)\sqrt{t}]
, where
\sqrt{N}\ll a_{N}\ll N
.

We comment that the above deviation results only considered the behaviors of the tagged particle at any fixed time
t
. The main aim of this paper is to investigate sample path moderate deviations for the tagged particle. Roughly speaking, we proved that when the SSEP starts from the same initial distribution as in
[
39
]
, the sequence of processes
\{X(tN^{2})/a_{N}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies moderate deviation principles with decay rate
a_{N}^{2}/N
, where
\sqrt{N\log N}\ll a_{N}\ll N
and
T>0
is fixed. Moreover, the moderate deviation rate function is proportional to the large deviation rate function of the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
1/4
. See Theorem
2.2
for details.

Since the relative ordering of the particles is conserved in the SSEP, closely related to the position of the tagged particle is the current of the process. This current-tagged particle relationship has been used in the above literature
[
5
,
15
,
33
,
39
]
. To prove Theorem
2.2
, we also proved sample path moderate deviation principles for the current, which is the content of Theorem
2.1
and has its own interest.

The main idea of the proof is as follows. As in
[
39
]
, by using moderate deviation principles from hydrodynamic limits of the SSEP
[
10
]
, we first prove finite-dimensional moderate deviation principles for the current and the tagged particle, where the rate function is given by a variational formula. In
[
39
]
, this variational problem was solved by constructing a minimizer of the problem directly and cannot be extended to the finite-dimensional case straightforwardly. Instead, we use the Fourier approach and then obtain an explicit formula for the finite-dimensional deviation rate function. Then, we need to prove the exponential tightness of the sample path of the current and the tagged particle, which requires more delicate analysis compared with
[
39
]
since all the estimates should be uniform in time. Finally, using standard results from large deviations theory, we obtain another variational formula for the rate function of the sample path, which could be solved easily from known results on fractional Brownian motion.

Very recently, the first named author in
[
38
]
proved non-equilibrium moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits of the SSEP. It remains a challenge to extend the results in this paper to the non-equilibrium case by using the above results. Another possible direction is to consider the sample path moderate deviation principles for the tagged particle in the SSEP in random environments or in the asymmetric exclusion process. We leave those as future works.

2.
Model and results

2.1.
Model

The state space of the one-dimensional symmetric simple exclusion process (SSEP) is
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. For a configuration
\eta\in\Omega
,
\eta(x)=1
if and only if there is one particle at site
x
. The infinitesimal generator of the process acts on local functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

(2.1)

\mathscr{L}f(\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\{f(\eta^{x,x+1})-f(\eta)\big\}.

Above, we call
f
a local function if it depends on
\eta
only through a finite number of sites, and
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
by exchanging the values of
\eta(x)
and
\eta(y)
, that is,

\eta^{x,y}(z)=\begin{cases}\eta(z)\quad&\text{if\;}z\neq x,y;\\
\eta(y)\quad&\text{if\;}z=x;\\
\eta(x)\quad&\text{if\;}z=y.\end{cases}

For each
\rho\in[0,1]
, let
\nu_{\rho}
be the Bernoulli product measure on
\Omega
with marginals given by

\nu_{\rho}\{\eta:\eta(x)=1\}=\rho,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is well known that
\nu_{\rho}
is reversible and ergodic for the SSEP,
cf.

[
23
]
for example.

Denote by
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the Markov process with generator
\mathscr{L}
. For a probability measure
\mu
on
\Omega
, denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
the measure on the path space
\mathcal{D}([0,\infty),\Omega)
of the process
\eta_{t}
with initial measure
\mu
, and by
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}
the corresponding expectation.

2.2.
MDP for the current

We first recall basic facts of the fractional Brownian motion. Let
\{B_{t}^{1/4}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
1/4
, which is a mean-zero continuous-time Gaussian process with covariance given by

{\rm Cov}\left(B_{t}^{1/4},B_{s}^{1/4}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(t^{1/2}+s^{1/2}-|t-s|^{1/2}\right)=:a(t,s)

for any
s,t\geq 0
. It is shown in
[
6
]
that the fractional Brownian motion has the following representation,

(2.2)

B_{t}^{1/4}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t,s)dB_{s},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

where
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the standard one-dimensional Brownian motion starting from the origin, and the kernel function is explicitly given by

\mathcal{K}(t,s)=\frac{(t-s)^{-1/4}}{\sqrt{V}\Gamma(3/4)}F(1/4,-1/4,3/4,1-\frac{t}{s})

for any
0\leq s<t
, where

V=\frac{8\Gamma(3/2)\cos(\pi/4)}{\pi}\text{\quad and
\quad}F(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{k}(\beta)_{k}}{(\gamma)_{k}k!}z^{k}

with
(a)_{k}
defined as
\Gamma(a+k)/\Gamma(a)
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that for any
s,t\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}\mathcal{K}(t,\tau)\mathcal{K}(s,\tau)d\tau=a(t,s).

Fix a time horizon
T>0
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of càdlàg functions
f:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that there exists a function
h_{f}\in L^{2}[0,T]
satisfying

f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{K}(t,s)\,h_{f}(s)ds,\quad\forall 0\leq t\leq T.

For any
f\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, define

\mathcal{I}_{path}(f)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}(h_{f}(s))^{2}ds\quad&\text{if~}f\in\mathcal{H},\\
+\infty\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By (
2.2
) and
[
7
, Theorem 3.4.12]
,
\mathcal{I}_{path}(f)
is the large deviation rate function of the sequence of the processes
\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}B_{t}^{1/4}:~0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
.

For each
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
J_{x,x+1}(t)
be the net number of particles across the bond
(x,x+1)
up to time
t
,
i.e.
, the number of particles jumping from
x
to
x+1
minus the number of particles jumping from
x+1
to
x
during the time interval
[0,t]
.

The first result concerns moderate deviations for the path of the current when the process starts from the initial measure
\nu_{\rho}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
. Throughout the article, we assume
\{a_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
to be a sequence of positive numbers such that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{N}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N\log N}{a_{N}^{2}}=0.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
. For
f\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, define

\mathcal{I}_{cur}(f)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{J}^{2}}\mathcal{I}_{path}(f)

where
\sigma_{J}^{2}:=\sigma_{J}^{2}(\rho):=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\rho(1-\rho)
. Then, the sequence of the processes
\left\{\frac{1}{a_{N}}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2}):0\leq t\leq T\right\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies moderate deviation principles with rate
a_{N}^{2}/N
and with rate function
\mathcal{I}_{cur}
. Precisely speaking, for any closed set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\left\{\frac{1}{a_{N}}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2}):0\leq t\leq T\right\}\in\mathcal{C}\Big)\leq-\inf_{f\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{I}_{cur}(f),

and for any open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

\liminf_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\left\{\frac{1}{a_{N}}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2}):0\leq t\leq T\right\}\in\mathcal{O}\Big)\geq-\inf_{f\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{I}_{cur}(f).

2.3.
MDP for the tagged particle

Note that the particles in the SSEP are indistinguishable. In this subsection, we distinguish one particular particle, put it initially at the origin, and call it the
tagged
particle. Denote by
X(t)
the position of the tagged particle at time
t
. By convention,
X(0)=0
. Since particles cannot jump over each other, the relative ordering of the particles is conserved.

Observe that the tagged particle process
\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
itself is not Markovian. However, both the coupled process
\{(\eta_{t},X(t))\}_{t\geq 0}
and the environment process
\{\xi_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are Markovian, where
\xi_{t}(x)=\eta_{t}(X(t)+x)
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\geq 0
.

For
\rho\in(0,1)
, let
\nu_{\rho}^{*}
be the measure obtained from the Bernoulli product measure
\nu_{\rho}
conditioned on having a particle at the origin,
i
.e.,

\nu_{\rho}^{*}(\cdot)=\nu_{\rho}(\cdot\;|\eta(0)=1).

It is also well known that
\nu_{\rho}^{*}
is invariant and ergodic for the environment process
\{\xi_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

The second result of the article concerns moderate deviations for the sample path of the tagged particle when the process starts from the initial measure
\nu_{\rho}^{*}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Fix
\rho\in(0,1)
. For
f\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
, define

\mathcal{I}_{tag}(f)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{X}^{2}}\mathcal{I}_{path}(f)

where
\sigma_{X}^{2}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1-\rho}{\rho}
. Then, the sequence of the tagged particle processes
\left\{\frac{1}{a_{N}}X(tN^{2}):0\leq t\leq T\right\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies moderate deviation principles with rate
a_{N}^{2}/N
and with rate function
\mathcal{I}_{tag}
. Precisely speaking, for any closed set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\left\{\frac{1}{a_{N}}X(tN^{2}):0\leq t\leq T\right\}\in\mathcal{C}\Big)\leq-\inf_{f\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{I}_{tag}(f),

and for any open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

\liminf_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\left\{\frac{1}{a_{N}}X(tN^{2}):0\leq t\leq T\right\}\in\mathcal{O}\Big)\geq-\inf_{f\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{I}_{tag}(f).

3.
Preliminary estimates

3.1.
Moderate deviations from hydrodynamic limits

We first recall moderate deviation principles for the empirical measure of the SSEP when the process starts from its invariant measure
\nu_{\rho}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
, see
[
10
]
for details. For each
N\geq 1
, the centered empirical measure
\mu^{N}_{t}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
of the SSEP, which acts on Schwartz functions
G\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, is defined as

\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},G\big\rangle=\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)-\rho\big)G(x/N).

In this subsection, we assume the sequence of positive numbers
\{a_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{N}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}=0.

Let
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}))
be the set of càdlàg functions from
[0,T]
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
endowed with the topology under which
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\theta^{n}=\theta
in
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}))
if and only if

\{\theta^{n}_{t}(G)\}_{0\leq t\leq T}\rightarrow\{\theta_{t}(G)\}_{0\leq t\leq T}

in
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
for any
G\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
For
G\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and
\mu\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}))
, define the linear functional
l(\cdot,\cdot)
as

l(\mu,G)=\left\langle\mu_{T},G_{T}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mu_{0},G_{0}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mu_{s},\left(\partial_{s}+(1/2)\partial_{u}^{2}\right)G_{s}\rangle ds.

Above,
G_{s}(u)=G(s,u)
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the bilinear form on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})\times\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\langle\mu,g\rangle=\mu(g)
is the output of
\mu
acting on
g
for
\mu\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
. The rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):=\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}(\mu)+\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sup_{G\in\mathcal{C}^{1,\infty}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})}\left\{l(\mu,G)-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{u}G)^{2}(s,u)\,du\,ds\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{0}\left(\mu_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)}\left\{\left\langle\mu_{0},\phi\right\rangle-\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}(u)du\right\},

where
\chi(\rho)=\rho(1-\rho)
is the compressibility of the SSEP.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\rho\in(0,1)
. The sequence of processes
\{\mu^{N}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies the MDP with decay rate
a_{N}^{2}/N
and with rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)
. More precisely, for any closed set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}))
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\big(\{\mu^{N}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}\in\mathcal{C}\big)\leq-\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu),

and for any open set
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}))
,

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\big(\{\mu^{N}_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}\in\mathcal{O}\big)\geq-\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu).

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\eta_{\cdot}
and
\eta_{\cdot}^{*}
be two SSEPs with initial distributions
\nu_{\rho}
and
\nu_{\rho}^{*}
respectively. By the basic coupling (see
[
23
]
for example), one could couple
\eta_{\cdot}
and
\eta_{\cdot}^{*}
together such that
\eta_{t}(x)=\eta^{*}_{t}(x)
for all but one
x
. As a consequence, the associated empirical measures of the two processes satisfy

|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu^{N,*}_{t},G\big\rangle|\leq\frac{\|G\|_{\infty}}{a_{N}},\quad\forall t\geq 0,\;\forall G\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

Thus, the above theorem also holds under
\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}
.

3.2.
Properties

In this subsection, we establish several properties of the measure
\mu
when it satisfies
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<\infty
. Before that, we first introduce some notation. For
H,G\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
, define

[H,G]:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{u}H(t,u)\partial_{u}G(t,u)\,du\,dt.

In order to make
[\cdot,\cdot]
an inner product, we say
H\sim G
if
[H-G,H-G]=0
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{1}
be the Hilbert space obtained as the completion of
\mathcal{C}^{1,2}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}))/\sim
with respect to the inner product
[\cdot,\cdot]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. If
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<\infty
, then there exist functions
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
H\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
such that, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,

\big\langle\mu_{0},\phi\big\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(u)\phi(u)du,

and that
\mu
is the unique weak solution of the following PDE

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mu(t,u)=(1/2)\partial_{u}^{2}\mu(t,u)-\chi(\rho)\partial_{u}^{2}H(t,u),\quad t>0,\;u\in\mathbb{R},\\
\mu(0,u)=\psi(u),\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

In particular,
\mu=\mu(t,u)
is a function. Moreover,

\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0})=\frac{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}{2\chi(\rho)},\quad\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}(\mu)=\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}[H,H].

The proof of the above lemma follows directly from Riesz’s representation theorem. We refer the readers to Appendix
A.1
for its proof.

In the above lemma, we say
\mu
is a weak solution to (
3.1
) if for any
G\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
and for any
0<t\leq T
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu(t,u)G(t,u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(u)G(0,u)du+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu(s,u)(\partial_{s}+(1/2)\partial_{u}^{2})G(s,u)\,du\,ds\\
+\chi(\rho)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{u}G(s,u)\partial_{u}H(s,u)\,du\,ds.

Actually,
\mu
has the following explicit expression: for any
t\geq 0
and for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.2)

\mu(t,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t}(u-v)\psi(v)dv-\chi(\rho)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}^{\prime}(u-v)\partial_{v}H(s,v)\,dv\,ds,

where
p_{t}(u)
is the heat kernel

p_{t}(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-u^{2}/(2t)}

and
p^{\prime}_{t}(u):=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}p_{t}(u)
. Using the explicit formula of
\mu
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<\infty
, then the limit

\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t,u)-\mu(0,u)]du:=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{M}[\mu(t,u)-\mu(0,u)]du

is well-defined. Moreover,

\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t,u)-\mu(0,u)]du=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[-\frac{1}{2}{p}_{s}^{\prime}(v)\psi(v)+\chi(\rho)p_{t-s}(v)\partial_{v}H(s,v)\Big]\,dv\,ds.

Proof.

By (
3.2
),

\int_{0}^{M}[\mu(t,u)-\mu(0,u)]du={\rm I}-{\rm II},

where

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[p_{t}(u-v)-p_{0}(u-v)\big]\psi(v)\,dv\,du,

\displaystyle{\rm II}

\displaystyle=\chi(\rho)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}^{\prime}(u-v)\partial_{v}H(s,v)\,dv\,du\,ds.

For the first term, we write it as

{\rm I}=\int_{0}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{p}_{s}(u-v)\psi(v)\,ds\,dv\,du=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}{p}_{s}^{\prime\prime}(u-v)\psi(v)\,ds\,dv\,du\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}\big[{p}_{s}^{\prime}(M-v)-{p}_{s}^{\prime}(v)\big]\psi(v)\,ds\,dv.

Above,
\dot{p}_{s}(u):=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}p_{s}(u)
and we used the identity
\dot{p}_{s}(u)=(1/2)p_{s}^{\prime\prime}(u)
. For the second term,

{\rm II}=\chi(\rho)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[p_{t-s}(M-v)-p_{t-s}(v)\big]\partial_{v}H(s,v)\,dv\,ds.

Thus, we conclude the proof once we show that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}{p}_{s}^{\prime}(M-v)\,ds\Big)\psi(v)\,dv=0,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(M-v)\partial_{v}H(s,v)\,dv\,ds=0.

One could check directly that, for any
t>0
,
p_{s}(u)\in L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})
and

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{t}{p}_{s}^{\prime}(\cdot)\,ds\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

We refer the readers to Appendix
A.2
for the proof of (
3.5
). Finally, (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) follow from the following claim: if
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(M-u)g(u)du=0.

The similar result holds when
f,g\in L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, by splitting

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(M-u)g(u)du=\int_{|u|>M/2}f(M-u)g(u)du+\int_{|u|\leq M/2}f(M-u)g(u)du,

and by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we bound

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(M-u)g(u)du\Big|\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big(\int_{|u|>M/2}g(u)^{2}du\Big)^{1/2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big(\int_{u>M/2}f(u)^{2}du\Big)^{1/2},

which converges to zero as
M\rightarrow\infty
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By repeating the above arguments, one could also show that if
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<\infty
, then for any
0<t\leq T
,

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{M}\int_{0}^{M}u[\mu(t,u)-\mu(0,u)]\,du=0.

Remark 3.3
.

Using (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.2
, it is not difficult to show that the moderate deviation rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\cdot)
is good. Here we give an outline of the check of this property. The property that
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\cdot)
is lower semi-continuous follows directly from the fact that
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\cdot)
is the supremum of a class of continuous functions from
\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}))
to
\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,

\left\{\mu:~\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)\leq c\right\}:=\mathbb{L}_{c}

is closed for any
c>0
. Hence, we only need to show that
\mathbb{L}_{c}
is compact. Using (
3.2
), Lemma
3.2
, the formula of integration by parts, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\partial_{t}p(t,u)=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{u}p(t,u)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\big\langle\mu_{t},f\big\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\chi(\rho)\sqrt{T}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\sqrt{[H,H]}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{0}(\mu_{0})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\sqrt{T}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\sqrt{2\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}(\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)}\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\sqrt{T}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

and

\displaystyle\left|\big\langle\mu_{t},f\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu_{s},f\big\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t-s}{2}\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\chi(\rho)\sqrt{t-s}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\sqrt{[H,H]}+\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}(t-s)\sqrt{T}\|f^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\sqrt{[H,H]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)\chi(\rho)}\left(\frac{t-s}{2}\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\sqrt{t-s}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{t-s}{2}\sqrt{T}\|f^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

for any
\mu
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<+\infty
, any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and any
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
. Therefore, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
\{\mu_{t}(f):~0\leq t\leq T\}_{\mu\in\mathbb{L}_{c}}
are uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. As a result, the compactness of
\mathbb{L}_{c}
follows from Ascoli-Arzela theorem.

3.3.
Alternative formulas for
\mathcal{Q}_{T}

Introduce

\mathcal{A}=\big\{\mu:\text{there exist}\;H\in\mathcal{C}^{1,2}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}))\;\text{and}\;\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R})\;\text{such that $\mu$ satisfies\,\eqref{mu PDE}}\big\}.

One could check directly that if
\mu\in\mathcal{A}
, then for any
t>0
,
\mu(t,\cdot)
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}
and vanishes at infinity.
For
\mu\in\mathcal{A}
, define
J
the macroscopic instantaneous current as

(3.6)

J=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{u}\mu+\chi(\rho)\partial_{u}H.

Obviously,

\partial_{t}\mu+\partial_{u}J=0.

Define
K(t,u)
the macroscopic current across the macroscopic point
u
during time
[0,t]
as

K(t,u):=\int_{0}^{t}J(s,u)ds.

Then, for
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
t>0
,

(3.7)

\partial_{u}K(t,u)=\mu(0,u)-\mu(t,u).

By direct calculations, for
\mu\in\mathcal{A}
,

(3.8)

\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)=\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{0},K):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{t}K\right)^{2}(t,u)dudt+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{u}K\right)^{2}(T,u)du\\
+\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{u}\mu_{0}(u)-\partial_{u}^{2}K(t,u)\right)^{2}dudt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[\mu_{0}^{2}(u)-\mu_{0}(u)\partial_{u}K(T,u)\big]du.

The above formula is exactly
[
39
, Eqn. (3.8)]
, whose proof is presented in Appendix
A.3
.

4.
Variational formula for the rate function

In this section, we present a variational formula for the rate function
\mathcal{I}_{path}
. For any
n\geq 1
, any column vector
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}
, let

(4.1)

\mathcal{I}_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}(\bm{\alpha})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}\bm{\alpha}

where
A_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}
is the
n\times n
matrix with
(k,\ell)
-component equal to
a(t_{k},t_{\ell})
for
1\leq k,\ell\leq n
. Recall
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is the covariance function of the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
1/4
.

The following variational formula relates
\mathcal{I}_{path}
the rate function of the sample path to the corresponding finite-dimensional rate functions, which is a direct consequence of
[
8
, Theorem 4.28]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{D}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
,

\mathcal{I}_{path}(f)=\sup\Big\{\mathcal{I}_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}\left(f_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}\right):~n\geq 1,0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}\leq T,t_{j}\in\Delta_{f}^{c}\text{~for all~}1\leq j\leq n\Big\},

where
f_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}=(f({t_{1}}),f({t_{2}}),\ldots,f({t_{n}}))^{T}
and
\Delta_{f}^{c}
is the set of continuities of
f
.

Proof.

It is well known that for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}
,
\mathcal{I}_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}(\cdot)
is the large deviation rate function of the sequence of Gaussian vectors
\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(B^{1/4}_{t_{1}},B^{1/4}_{t_{2}},\ldots,B^{1/4}_{t_{n}}\right)\right\}_{N\geq 1}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{path}(\cdot)
is the rate function of the sequence
\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}B_{t}^{1/4}:~0\leq t\leq T\right\}_{N\geq 1}
, see
[
7
]
for example. Then, by
[
8
, Theorem 4.28]
, the result follows immediately.
∎

The following lemma gives a variational formula for the finite-dimensional rate function appearing in the last proposition, which is the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any positive integer
n
, any column vector
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and any
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}\leq T
, we have

(4.2)

\inf\Big\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t_{j},u)-\mu(0,u)]du=\alpha_{j},\;1\leq j\leq n\Big\}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\chi(\rho)}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha},

where
A=A_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}
for short.

Note that

\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\chi(\rho)}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{J}^{2}}\mathcal{I}_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}(\bm{\alpha}).

In the rest of this section, we prove Proposition
4.2
.

4.1.
Restricted to
\mu\in\mathcal{A}

We first show that we could restrict the infimum in Proposition
4.2
to
\mu\in\mathcal{A}
, which is the content of the next result.

Proposition 4.3

(The case
n>1
)
.

For any positive integer
n
, any column vector
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and any
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{n}\leq T
, we have

(4.3)

\inf\Big\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{A},\;\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t_{j},u)-\mu(0,u)]du=\alpha_{j},\;1\leq j\leq n\Big\}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\chi(\rho)}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha},

where
A=A_{{}_{\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}}}
.

We first prove Proposition
4.2
from the last result, and then prove the last proposition in the next subsection.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

By Proposition
4.3
,

\inf\Big\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t_{j},u)-\mu(0,u)]du=\alpha_{j},\;1\leq j\leq n\Big\}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\chi(\rho)}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha},

and it remains to prove the opposite direction of the last inequality.
Take
\mu
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)<\infty
and

\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t_{j},u)-\mu(0,u)]du=\alpha_{j},\;\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

Then, by Lemma
3.2
, there exist
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
H\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
such that
\mu
is the unique weak solution to the PDE (
3.1
). For
\varepsilon>0
, let
\psi^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
H^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1,2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
such that

\psi^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\psi\quad\text{in}\;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\text{and}\quad H^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow H\quad\text{in}\;\mathcal{H}^{1},\quad\;\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Denote by
\mu^{\varepsilon}
the solution of the PDE (
3.1
) associated with
\psi^{\varepsilon}
and
H^{\varepsilon}
. Finally, let

\alpha_{j}^{\varepsilon}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu^{\varepsilon}(t_{j},u)-\mu^{\varepsilon}(0,u)]du,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu^{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu),\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\alpha_{j}^{\varepsilon}=\alpha_{j},\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

Thus,

\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu^{\varepsilon})\\
\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\inf\Big\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{A},\;\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t_{j},u)-\mu(0,u)]du=\alpha^{\varepsilon}_{j},\;1\leq j\leq n\Big\}\\
=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\chi(\rho)}\bm{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\chi(\rho)}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha},

where
\bm{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}=(\alpha_{1}^{\varepsilon},\alpha_{2}^{\varepsilon},\ldots,\alpha_{n}^{\varepsilon})^{T}
. We conclude the proof by taking the infimum over
\mu
on the left-hand side of the last inequality.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
4.3

The proof of Proposition
4.3
is based on Fourier approach and is divided into several lemmas. Observe that if
\mu\in\mathcal{A}
, then by (
3.7
),

K(t,0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}[\mu(t,u)-\mu(0,u)]du,\quad t>0,

which allows us to reduce the constraints inside the infimum in (
4.3
) to
K(t_{j},0)=\alpha_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
. Obviously, we also have
K(0,u)=0
for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
by the definition of
K(t,u)
.

First, we consider the case
n=1
and
K(T,0)=\alpha
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The following result was proved in
[
39
]
by constructing the minimizer of the variational problem directly. Here, we present a different approach, which allows us to generalize it to the case
n>1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,

(4.4)

\inf\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{A},\;K(0,\cdot)=0,\;K(T,0)=\alpha\}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\alpha^{2}}{4\chi(\rho)\sqrt{T}}.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps:

Step 1.
We first minimize
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{0},K)
subject to
K(0,\cdot)=0
and
K(T,\cdot)
being a known function. We claim that the infimum is attained at the point
(K^{T,\alpha},\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0})
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}^{2}K^{T,\alpha}(t,u)+\frac{1}{4}\partial_{u}^{4}K^{T,\alpha}(t,u)-\frac{1}{4}\partial_{u}^{3}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(u)=0,

(4.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{T}{4}\partial_{u}^{2}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{u}^{3}K^{T,\alpha}(t,u)dt+\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{u}K^{T,\alpha}(T,u)=0.

Indeed, the above equations are obtained by solving, for any
\mu_{0}
and
K
such that
K(0,\cdot)=K(T,\cdot)=0
,

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{0}^{T,\alpha}+\varepsilon\mu_{0},K^{T,\alpha})|_{\varepsilon=0}=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{0}^{T,\alpha},K^{T,\alpha}+\varepsilon K)|_{\varepsilon=0}=0.

For any function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, define the Fourier transform of
f
as

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iu\xi}f(u)du,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Then,
(\mathcal{F}f^{\prime})(\xi)=-i\xi\mathcal{F}f(\xi),\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. This permits us to rewrite (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) as

(4.7)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(t,\xi)+\frac{\xi^{4}}{4}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(t,\xi)-\frac{i\xi^{3}}{4}\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)=0,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Big\{1+\frac{T\xi^{2}}{4}\Big\}\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)=-i\Big\{\frac{\xi}{2}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)+\frac{\xi^{3}}{4}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(t,\xi)dt\Big\}.

Solving (
4.7
), we have

(4.9)

\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(t,\xi)=C_{1}(\xi)e^{t\xi^{2}/2}+C_{2}(\xi)e^{-t\xi^{2}/2}+\frac{i\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)}{\xi}

for two functions
C_{1}(\xi)
and
C_{2}(\xi)
, which are determined by the boundary conditions at time
0
and
T
,

(4.10)

\begin{cases}C_{1}(\xi)+C_{2}(\xi)+\frac{i\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)}{\xi}=0,\\
C_{1}(\xi)e^{T\xi^{2}/2}+C_{2}(\xi)e^{-T\xi^{2}/2}+\frac{i\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)}{\xi}=\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi).\end{cases}

Inserting (
4.9
) into (
4.8
), we have

(4.11)

\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)=-\frac{i\xi}{2}\Big\{\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)+C_{1}(\xi)\big[e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1\big]+C_{2}(\xi)\big[1-e^{-T\xi^{2}/2}\big]\Big\}.

Finally, using (
4.11
), and solving (
4.10
), we have

\begin{cases}C_{1}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2(e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1)}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi),\\
C_{2}(\xi)=-\frac{e^{T\xi^{2}/2}}{2(e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1)}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi).\end{cases}

In conclusion, we have shown that, for
0\leq t\leq T
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i\xi}{2}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi),

(4.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{e^{t\xi^{2}/2}-e^{(T-t)\xi^{2}/2}}{e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1}+1\Big]\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi).

Step 2.
By Parseval’s identity,

(4.14)

\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{0},K)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\big|\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}K(t,\xi)\big|^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\big|-i\xi\mathcal{F}\mu_{0}(\xi)+\xi^{2}\mathcal{F}K(t,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big\}d\xi\,dt\\
+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\big|\mathcal{F}\mu_{0}(\xi)\big|^{2}+\big|\mathcal{F}\mu_{0}(\xi)+i\xi\mathcal{F}K(T,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big\}d\xi.

Inserting
(K^{T,\alpha},\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0})
into the last expression, and by (
4.12
) and (
4.13
),

(4.15)

\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0},K^{T,\alpha})\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\xi^{4}}{32[e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1]^{2}}|\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)|^{2}\Big[\Big(e^{t\xi^{2}/2}+e^{(T-t)\xi^{2}/2}\Big)^{2}+\Big(e^{t\xi^{2}/2}-e^{(T-t)\xi^{2}/2}\Big)^{2}\Big]\,d\xi\,dt\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\xi^{2}}{8}|\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\xi^{2}e^{T\xi^{2}/2}}{4[e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1]}|\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

Step 3.
Finally, we need to optimize
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu^{T,\alpha}_{0},K^{T,\alpha})
over
\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)
. The boundary condition
K^{T,\alpha}(T,0)=\alpha
is equivalent to

(4.16)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)d\xi=\alpha.

Subject to (
4.16
), the infimum of (
4.15
) is attained at the point

\mathcal{F}K^{T,\alpha}(T,\xi)=\frac{\alpha(1-e^{-T\xi^{2}/2})}{\sqrt{T}\xi^{2}},

and equals

(\sqrt{2\pi}\alpha)^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{4(e^{T\xi^{2}/2}-1)}{\xi^{2}e^{T\xi^{2}/2}}\,d\xi\Big)^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\alpha^{2}}{4\sqrt{T}}

by (
A.1
). This concludes the proof.
∎

The next lemma extends the last result to the case
n=1
and
K(t,0)=\alpha
for
0<t\leq T
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Fix
0<t\leq T
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,

(4.17)

\inf\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{A},\;K(0,\cdot)=0,\;K(t,0)=\alpha\}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\alpha^{2}}{4\chi(\rho)\sqrt{t}}.

Proof.

For any
t>0
, let
\mu^{t,\alpha}_{T}
be the minimizer of (
4.17
), which does exist by the following construction.
Denote by
H^{t,\alpha}_{T}
the function
H
in (
3.1
), respectively
K^{t,\alpha}_{T}
the function
K
in (
3.7
), associated with the function
\mu^{t,\alpha}_{T}
.
We call
\mu^{t,\alpha}_{T}
, or correspondingly
(K_{T}^{t,\alpha},\mu^{t,\alpha}_{T,0})
,
the minimizer of the rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)
with current
\alpha
at time
t
.
In particular, when
T=t
,

\mu^{T,\alpha}=\mu^{T,\alpha}_{T},\quad H^{T,\alpha}=H^{T,\alpha}_{T},\quad K^{T,\alpha}=K^{T,\alpha}_{T},

the precise expressions of which are given through the proof of Lemma
4.4
.

We claim the infimum in (
4.17
) is attained at the point
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}
such that

(i)

for
0\leq s\leq t
, the function
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}
is the unique solution to

(4.18)

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(s,u)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(s,u)-\chi(\rho)\partial_{u}^{2}H^{t,\alpha}(s,u),\quad u\in\mathbb{R},\\
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(0,u)=\mu^{t,\alpha}_{0}(u),\quad u\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

where
H^{t,\alpha}
is the function
H
associated with the minimizer of the rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\mu)
with current
\alpha
at time
t
;

(ii)

for
t\leq s\leq T
,
the function
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}
is the unique solution to

(4.19)

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(s,u)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{u}^{2}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(s,u),\quad u\in\mathbb{R},\\
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(t,u)=\mu^{t,\alpha}(t,u),\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Roughly speaking, the minimizer
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}
evolves as the minimizer of the rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\mu)
with current
\alpha
at time
t
during time interval
[0,t]
, and then evolves according to the hydrodynamic equation of the SSEP after time
t
. This claim is obvious since

\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}(\mu)=\frac{\chi(\rho)}{2}[H,H],

and thus

\inf\{\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{A},\;K(0,\cdot)=0,\;K(t,0)=\alpha\}\geq\inf\{\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\mu):\mu\in\mathcal{A},\;K(0,\cdot)=0,\;K(t,0)=\alpha\}

Moreover, by Lemma
4.4
, the left-hand side of (
4.17
) equals

\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha})=\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\mu^{t,\alpha})+\int_{t}^{T}0ds=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\alpha^{2}}{4\chi(\rho)\sqrt{t}},

which concludes the proof.
∎

One could check directly that the functions
\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}
and
(K_{T}^{t,\alpha},\mu^{t,\alpha}_{T,0})
constructed in the last proof satisfy the following properties:

(P1)

for
0\leq s\leq t
,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t,\alpha}(\xi)=-\frac{i\xi}{2}\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi),

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}_{T}(s,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{e^{s\xi^{2}/2}-e^{(t-s)\xi^{2}/2}}{e^{t\xi^{2}/2}-1}+1\Big]\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi),

where

\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi)=\frac{\alpha(1-e^{-t\xi^{2}/2})}{\sqrt{t}\xi^{2}};

(P2)

for
t\leq s\leq T
,

(4.22)

\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}_{T}(s,\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\big[1+e^{-(s-t)\xi^{2}/2}\big]\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi).

Moreover, by direct calculations (see Section
A.4
), for
0\leq s\leq T
,

(4.23)

K^{t,\alpha}_{T}(s,0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}_{T}(s,\xi)d\xi=\frac{\alpha a(t,s)}{\sqrt{t}},

where
a(t,s)=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{|t-s|})
is the covariance function of the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
\frac{1}{4}
.

(P1) is exactly (
4.12
) and (
4.13
). (P2) follows from (
4.19
) and (
3.7
). Indeed, by (
4.19
),

\mathcal{F}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(s,\xi)=\mathcal{F}\mu^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi)e^{-(s-t)\xi^{2}/2},\quad t\leq s\leq T,

and by (
3.7
),

(4.24)

-i\xi\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}_{T}(s,\xi)=\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t,\alpha}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(s,\xi),\quad s\geq 0.

Taking
s=t
in the last expression, and then using (P1), we obtain

\mathcal{F}\mu_{T}^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi)=\frac{i\xi}{2}\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi).

Therefore, by (
4.24
), for
s\geq t
,

-i\xi\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}_{T}(s,\xi)=-\frac{i\xi}{2}\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi)-\mathcal{F}\mu^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi)e^{-(s-t)\xi^{2}/2}=-\frac{i\xi}{2}\big[1+e^{-(s-t)\xi^{2}/2}\big]\mathcal{F}K^{t,\alpha}(t,\xi),

as claimed.

Finally, we are ready to prove Proposition
4.3
.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

Let

\mu_{T}^{n}:=\mu_{T}^{n,\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}},\quad(K_{T}^{n},\mu_{T,0}^{n}):=(K_{T}^{n,\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}},\mu_{T,0}^{n,\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}})

be the minimizer of (
4.2
). We claim that

(4.25)

K_{T}^{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}K_{T}^{t_{j},1},\quad\mu_{T,0}^{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{j},1}

for some coefficients
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Recall that for
1\leq j\leq n
,
\big(K_{T}^{t_{j},1},\mu_{T,0}^{t_{j},1}\big)
is the minimizer of the rate function
\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu)
with current one at time
t_{j}
, and is constructed in (
4.18
) and (
4.19
). We prove claim (
4.25
) in Section
A.5
. The coefficients
\{\beta_{j}\}
satisfy the following constraints

(4.26)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}K_{T}^{t_{j},1}(t_{k},0)=\alpha_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

By (
4.23
), the last line is equivalent to

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\frac{a(t_{j},t_{k})}{\sqrt{t_{j}}}=\alpha_{k},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

Let
D={\rm diag}(d_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
be the diagonal matrix with
d_{j}=1/\sqrt{t_{j}}
. Let
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})^{T}
be the column vector. Then, we have shown that

AD\bm{\beta}=\bm{\alpha}.

Note that
AD
is invertible, which ensures the existence and uniqueness of
\bm{\beta}
.

Inserting (
4.25
) into (
4.14
), we have

(4.27)

\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{T,0}^{n},K^{n}_{T})=\bm{\beta}^{T}Q\bm{\beta},

where
Q=(q_{jk})_{1\leq j,k\leq n}
is the matrix with
q_{jk}
being the real part of

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}K_{T}^{t_{j},1}(t,\xi)\overline{\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}K_{T}^{t_{k},1}(t,\xi)}\\
+\frac{1}{8}\big[-i\xi\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{j},1}(\xi)+\xi^{2}\mathcal{F}K_{T}^{t_{j},1}(t,\xi)\big]\overline{\big[-i\xi\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{k},1}(\xi)+\xi^{2}\mathcal{F}K_{T}^{t_{k},1}(t,\xi)\big]}\Big\}d\xi\,dt\\
+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big\{\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{j},1}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{k},1}(\xi)}\\
+\big[\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{j},1}(\xi)+i\xi\mathcal{F}K_{T}^{t_{j},1}(T,\xi)\big]\overline{\big[\mathcal{F}\mu_{T,0}^{t_{k},1}(\xi)+i\xi\mathcal{F}K_{T}^{t_{k},1}(T,\xi)\big]}\Big\}d\xi.

After a long computation (see Section
A.6
), we have

(4.28)

q_{jk}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4\sqrt{t_{j}t_{k}}}a_{jk}.

Equivalently,

Q=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}DAD.

Therefore,

\chi(\rho)\mathcal{Q}_{T}(\mu_{T,0}^{n},K^{n}_{T})=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{4}\bm{\alpha}^{T}A^{-1}\bm{\alpha},

which concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Exponential tightness

In this section, we prove the sequences of the two processes

\{J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})/a_{N}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}\quad\text{and}\quad\{X(tN^{2})/a_{N}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}

are exponentially tight.

5.1.
A uniform super-exponential estimate

We first state a super-exponential estimate for the space average of the current. The following result was proved by the same authors in
[
39
]
without the supremum over time inside the probability.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\delta>0
,

(5.1)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{1}{nNa_{N}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})\Big|>\delta\Big)=-\infty

under both
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}
and
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}
.

Proof.

By Remark
3.1
, we only need to discuss the case where
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}
. By Markov’s inequality, for any
K>0
, the expression in (
5.1
) is bounded by

(5.2)

-\delta K+\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})\Big|\Big\}\Big].

Since
K
could be taken arbitrarily large, we only need to prove that, for any fixed
K>0
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})\Big|\Big\}\Big]=0.

By using the inequality

\exp\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|x_{t}|\}\leq\exp\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}x_{t}\}+\exp\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(-x_{t})\}

for any trajectory
\{x_{t}\}
, and
\log(a+b)\leq\log 2+\max\{\log a,\log b\}
for any
a,b>0
, without loss of generality, it suffices to prove that, for any fixed
K>0
,

(5.3)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})\Big\}\Big]=0.

Since for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\{J_{x,x+1}(t)\}
is a compound Poisson process with intensity

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}(\eta_{s}(x)-\eta_{s}(x+1))ds,

and there are no common jumps between those compound Poisson processes,

M^{N,1}_{t}=\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})-\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}

is a mean-one exponential martingale, where

\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}\Big(e^{a_{N}K/(nN^{2})}-1\Big)\int_{0}^{tN^{2}}(\eta_{s}(x)-\eta_{s}(x+1))ds.

Then, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the expectation in (
5.3
) is bounded by

\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\{M_{t}^{N,1}\}\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}\Big]\\
\leq\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(M_{t}^{N,1})^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}2\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}\Big]^{1/2}.

As a consequence, the expression in (
5.3
) is bounded by

\frac{N}{2a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(M_{t}^{N,1})^{2}\Big]+\frac{N}{2a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}2\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}\Big].

For the martingale term in the last line, we have

(M_{t}^{N,1})^{2}=\exp\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big[\frac{4a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})-2\Gamma^{N,2}_{n,K}(t)\Big]+\Gamma^{N,2}_{n,K}(t)-2\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}\\
=:(M_{t}^{N,2})^{1/2}\exp\Big\{\Gamma^{N,2}_{n,K}(t)-2\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\},

where

2\Gamma^{N,2}_{n,K}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}\Big(e^{4a_{N}K/(nN^{2})}-1\Big)\int_{0}^{tN^{2}}(\eta_{s}(x)-\eta_{s}(x+1))ds,

and

M_{t}^{N,2}=\exp\Big\{\frac{4a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})-2\Gamma^{N,2}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}

is also a martingale. By Doob’s inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(5.4)

\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(M_{t}^{N,1})^{2}\Big]\leq\frac{N\log 4}{a_{N}^{2}}+\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[(M_{T}^{N,1})^{2}\Big]\\
\leq\frac{N\log 4}{a_{N}^{2}}+\frac{N}{2a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[M_{T}^{N,2}\Big]+\frac{N}{2a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{2\Gamma^{N,2}_{n,K}(T)-4\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(T)\Big\}\Big].

Note that the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.4
) is zero. Since
a_{N}\gg\sqrt{N}
, the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.4
) converges to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, to prove (
5.3
), we only need to prove

(5.5)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{2a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}2\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\Big\}\Big]=0,

since the third term on the right-hand side of (
5.4
) could be treated in the same way.

Since
|e^{x}-1-x|\leq(x^{2}/2)e^{|x|}
,

\Gamma^{N,1}_{n,K}(t)\leq\frac{a_{N}K}{2nN^{2}}\int_{0}^{tN^{2}}(\eta_{s}(0)-\eta_{s}(nN))ds+\frac{C_{N,n}ta_{N}^{2}K^{2}}{n^{2}N^{2}},

where
C_{N,n}
is uniformly bounded in
N
and
n
. Thus, we are left to show

(5.6)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\int_{0}^{tN^{2}}(\eta_{s}(0)-\eta_{s}(nN))ds\Big\}\Big]=0.

Since

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\int_{0}^{tN^{2}}(\eta_{s}(0)-\eta_{s}(nN))ds\leq\sup_{0\leq k\leq TN}\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\int_{0}^{kN}(\eta_{s}(0)-\eta_{s}(nN))ds+\frac{a_{N}K}{nN},

where the supremum on the right-hand side is over integers
k
, the left-hand side in (
5.6
) is bounded by

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\sup_{0\leq k\leq TN}\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\int_{0}^{kN}(\eta_{s}(0)-\eta_{s}(nN))ds\Big\}\Big].

Using the inequality

\log\mathbb{E}[\exp\{\sup_{0\leq k\leq TN}X_{k}\}]\leq\log\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{k=0}^{TN}e^{X_{k}}\Big]\leq\log(TN+1)+\max_{0\leq k\leq TN}\log\mathbb{E}[e^{X_{k}}],

and since
a_{N}\gg\sqrt{N\log N}
, we only need to prove

(5.7)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\max_{0\leq k\leq TN}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{N}K}{nN^{2}}\int_{0}^{kN}(\eta_{s}(0)-\eta_{s}(nN))ds\Big\}\Big]=0,

which has been shown in the proof of
[
39
, Lemma 4.1]
. We also refer the readers to Section
A.7
for details. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Exponential tightness of the current

To show the sequence of processes
\{J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})/a_{N}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
is exponentially tight, we only need to prove the following estimates for the current.

Lemma 5.2
.

We have the following estimates for the current under both
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}
and
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}
:

(1)

for any fixed
T>0
,

(5.8)

\limsup_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\big|J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})\big|>M\Big)=-\infty;

(2)

for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.9)

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\big|J_{-1,0}((t+\tau)N^{2})-J_{-1,0}(\tau N^{2})\big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty,

where
\mathcal{T}_{T}
is the set of all stopping times bounded by
T
.

Proof.

As in the last lemma, we only need to deal with the case where
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}
.
For any
n>0
, introduce the function
G_{n}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

G_{n}(u)=(1-u/n)^{+}\mathbf{1}\{u\geq 0\},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, one could check directly that, for any
n>0
,

(5.10)

\frac{1}{a_{N}}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})=\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},G_{n}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mu^{N}_{0},G_{n}\big\rangle+\frac{1}{nNa_{N}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2}).

Thus, we only need to show that the estimates in the lemma hold respectively for the terms on the right-hand side of the last line,
i.e.
,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{1}{nNa_{N}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})\Big|>M\Big)=-\infty,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\delta}\Big|\frac{1}{nNa_{N}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}[J_{x,x+1}((t+\tau)N^{2})-J_{x,x+1}(\tau N^{2})]\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty,

and

(5.13)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},G_{n}\big\rangle\big|>M\Big)

\displaystyle=-\infty,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq\delta}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t+\tau}-\mu^{N}_{\tau},G_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon\Big)

\displaystyle=-\infty.

Recall that (
5.11
) has been proved in Lemma
5.1
. Since the probability in (
5.12
) is bounded by

2\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\frac{1}{nNa_{N}}\sum_{x=0}^{nN-1}J_{x,x+1}(tN^{2})\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big),

the estimate in (
5.12
) also follows directly from Lemma
5.1
.

The estimates in (
5.13
) and (
5.14
) have been proved in
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
for Schwartz test functions. Thus, we only need to find
\tilde{G}_{n}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.15)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},G_{n}-\widetilde{G}_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty.

We underline that this is not obvious since we only have the priori bound

\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},G_{n}-\widetilde{G}_{n}\big\rangle\big|\leq C(n)\frac{N}{a_{N}}

and note that
a_{N}\ll N
. To this end, we choose
\tilde{G}_{n}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that

|\delta_{n}(u)|:=|G_{n}(u)-\tilde{G}_{n}(u)|\leq Cn^{-1},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{R},

and that the support of
\delta_{n}(\cdot)
is contained in
[-2n,2n]
. For
0\leq i\leq N^{3}
, let
t_{i}=iT/N^{3}
. Then, the probability in (
5.15
) is bounded by

(5.16)

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{3}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t_{i}},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)+\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|-\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{3}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t_{i}},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big).

For the first term in the last line, by stationary of the process and since
a_{N}\gg\sqrt{N\log N}
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{3}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t_{i}},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\\
\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{0},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\\
\leq-\frac{A\varepsilon}{2}+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{N}A}{N}\big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\bar{\eta}_{0}(x)\delta_{n}(x/N)\big|\Big\}\Big].

As before, we could remove the absolute value inside the exponential in the last line. Since
\nu_{\rho}
is a product measure, and by Taylor’s expansion up to second order, we bound the last line by

-\frac{A\varepsilon}{2}+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big[\exp\Big\{\frac{a_{N}A}{N}\bar{\eta}_{0}(x)\delta_{n}(x/N)\Big\}\Big]\\
\leq-\frac{A\varepsilon}{2}+\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{CA^{2}}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{n}^{2}(x/N)=-\frac{A\varepsilon}{2}.

Since
A
could be arbitrarily large,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{3}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t_{i}},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)=-\infty.

It remains to bound the second term in (
5.16
). Before that, we first recall the stirring representation for the symmetric exclusion process. Initially, we put a particle at each site of
\mathbb{Z}
. Those particles are distinguishable. For each bond
(x,x+1)
, we have a Poisson process with parameter
\tfrac{1}{2}
, and assume those Poisson processes are independent. When the Poisson clock associated with the bond
(x,x+1)
rings, we exchange the particles at sites
x
and
x+1
. Let
\xi_{t}^{x}
be the position at time
t
of the particle initially at site
x
. For
\eta\in\Omega
, let

\eta_{t}(x)=1\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\text{$\xi_{t}^{y}=x$ for some $y\in\mathbb{Z}$ such that $\eta(y)=1$}.

Then,
\{\eta_{t}\}
is a version of the SSEP with initial configuration
\eta
. Note that for each
x
,
\{\xi_{t}^{x}\}
is a continuous-time simple random walk with jump rate one. Using this representation, for any
s,t\geq 0
, we have

\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu^{N}_{s},\delta_{n}\big\rangle=\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{sN^{2}}}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y).

Thus, we bound the second term in (
5.16
) by

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|-\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{3}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t_{i}},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\\
\leq\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{3}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu^{N}_{t_{i}},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\leq N^{3}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\big|\big\langle\mu^{N}_{t}-\mu^{N}_{0},\delta_{n}\big\rangle\big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\\
=N^{3}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y)\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big).

To conclude the proof, it is sufficient to show that, for any
n>0
,

(5.17)

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y)\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)=-\infty.

For the above sum over
y\in\mathbb{Z}
, we consider the two cases
|y|>3Nn
and
|y|\leq 3Nn
respectively.

For the case
|y|>3Nn
,
\delta_{n}(y/N)=0
since the support of
\delta_{n}
is contained in
[-2n,2n]
. Then, we bound

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{|y|>3Nn}\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)\eta_{0}(y)\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}|\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}|\leq 2Nn\quad\text{for some $|y|>3Nn$}\Big)\\
\leq\sum_{|y|>3Nn}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}|\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}-y|\geq|y|-2Nn\Big).

Since for each
y
,
|\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}-y|
is stochastically bounded by a Poisson process with parameter
tN^{2}
, and recall
t_{1}=T/N^{3}
, the last line is bound by

\sum_{|y|>3Nn}e^{-c(|y|-2Nn)}\leq e^{-cNn}

for some constant
c>0
. Therefore,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{|y|>3Nn}\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)\eta_{0}(y)\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)=-\infty.

It remains to bound

(5.18)

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{|y|\leq 3Nn}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y)\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big)\\
\leq\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}(A_{N}^{c})+\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{|y|\leq 3Nn}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y)\Big|>\varepsilon/2\Big\}\cap A_{N}\Big),

where

A_{N}=\Big\{\sup_{|y|\leq 3Nn}\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}|\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}-y|\leq a_{N}^{3/2}N^{-1/2}\Big\}.

Standard large deviation estimates yield

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}(A_{N}^{c})\leq 7Nne^{-ca_{N}^{3/2}N^{-1/2}}

for some constant
c>0
, and thus

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}(A_{N}^{c})=-\infty.

We claim that the event inside the second probability on the right-hand side of (
5.18
) is empty for
N
large enough, which is sufficient to conclude the proof. Indeed, first note that
\delta_{n}
is only discontinuous at the origin. Moreover, on the event
A_{N}
, for each
0\leq t\leq t_{1}
, the cardinality of

B_{N,t}:=\{|y|\leq 3Nn:y\geq 0,\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}<0\;\text{or}\;y<0,\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}\geq 0\}

is bounded by
2a_{N}^{3/2}N^{-1/2}
. Thus, on the event
A_{N}
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{y\in B_{N,t}}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y)\Big|\leq\frac{4}{n}\sqrt{\frac{a_{N}}{N}},

which goes to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
. By the piecewise smoothness of
\delta_{n}
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}\Big|\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{|y|\leq 3Nn,y\notin B_{N,t}}\Big[\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{\xi^{y}_{tN^{2}}}{N}\Big)-\delta_{n}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)\Big]\eta_{0}(y)\Big|\leq C(n)\sqrt{\frac{a_{N}}{N}},

which also vanishes as
N\rightarrow\infty
, thus concluding the proof.
∎

5.3.
Exponential tightness of the tagged particle

As in the last subsection, if follows from the following estimates that the sequence of processes
\{X(tN^{2})/a_{N}:0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
is exponentially tight.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have the following estimates for the replacement of the tagged particle:

(1)

for any fixed
T>0
,

(5.19)

\limsup_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\big|X(tN^{2})\big|>M\Big)=-\infty;

(2)

for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.20)

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\big|X((t+\tau)N^{2})-X(\tau N^{2})\big|>\varepsilon\Big)=-\infty,

where
\mathcal{T}_{T}
is the set of all stopping times bounded by
T
.

Proof.

We first prove (
5.19
). Since the process is symmetric with respect to the origin, we could remove the absolute value inside the probability in (
5.19
). If
X(tN^{2})>a_{N}M
for some
0\leq t\leq T
, then
J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})\geq\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)
for some
0\leq t\leq T
. Thus, the probability in (
5.19
) is bounded by

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})\geq\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})\geq\rho a_{N}M/2\Big)\\
+\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)\leq\rho a_{N}M/2\Big).

To finish the proof, it remains to show that

\limsup_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}J_{-1,0}(tN^{2})\geq\rho a_{N}M/2\Big)=-\infty,

which follows directly from (
5.8
), and that, for any fixed
M>0
,

(5.21)

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)\leq\rho a_{N}M/2\Big)=-\infty.

Now, we prove (
5.21
). For
0\leq i\leq N^{2}
, define
t_{i}=iT/N^{2}
. Then, for
t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}
,

\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)=\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{t_{i}N^{2}}(x)+J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})-J_{[a_{N}M],[a_{N}M]+1}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2}),

which implies that

\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x)\geq\inf_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{t_{i}N^{2}}(x)\\
-\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|-\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{[a_{N}M],[a_{N}M]+1}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|.

This permits us to bound the probability in (
5.21
) by

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\inf_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{t_{i}N^{2}}(x)\leq 3\rho a_{N}M/4\Big)+\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|\geq\rho a_{N}M/8\Big)\\
+\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{[a_{N}M],[a_{N}M]+1}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|\geq\rho a_{N}M/8\Big).

By Remark
3.1
, we could replace the above
\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}
with
\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}
. Since
\nu_{\rho}
is invariant for the SSEP, using the large deviation principle of the sum of i.i.d. random variables, there exists
C>0
independent of
N
and
1\leq i\leq N^{2}
such that

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{t_{i}N^{2}}(x)\leq 3\rho a_{N}M/4\Big)=-C

for all
1\leq i\leq N^{2}
. Then, since
a_{N}\gg\sqrt{N\log N}
, we have

(5.22)

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\inf_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}M]}\eta_{t_{i}N^{2}}(x)\leq 3\rho a_{N}M/4\Big)=-\infty.

Since a particle crosses an edge at rate at most
1
,
\{\big|J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|\}_{t\geq t_{i}}
is stochastically dominated from above by a Poisson process with rate
1
. Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|\geq\rho a_{N}M/8)\\
\leq N^{2}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}(\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|\geq\rho a_{N}M/8)\leq CN^{2}\exp\{-Ca_{N}M\}

for some constant
C>0
, which implies

(5.23)

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a^{2}_{N}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq i\leq N^{2}}\sup_{t_{i}\leq t\leq t_{i+1}}\big|J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})\big|\geq\rho a_{N}M/8\Big)=-\infty.

By translation invariance, (
5.23
) still holds when
J_{-1,0}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2})
is replaced by

J_{[a_{N}M],[a_{N}M]+1}(t_{i}N^{2},tN^{2}),

thus concluding the proof of (
5.21
).

Now we prove (
5.20
). We first remove the absolute value inside the probability in (
5.20
) as before. For any
M>0
, we define

B_{M,N,\delta}=\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq{T+\delta}}\frac{1}{a_{N}}|X(tN^{2})|\leq M\Big\}.

By (
5.19
),

\limsup_{M\rightarrow+\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}(B_{M,N,\delta}^{c})=-\infty.

Hence, to prove (
5.20
), we only need to show that

(5.24)

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\left(X((t+\tau)N^{2})-X(\tau N^{2})\right)>\varepsilon,B_{N,M,\delta}\Big)=-\infty

for any
M>0
. We further define

C_{N,M,\varepsilon,\delta}=\Big\{\inf_{-a_{N}M\leq k\leq a_{N}M}\inf_{0\leq t\leq T+\delta}\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}\varepsilon]}\eta_{tN^{2}}(x+k)\geq\frac{\rho a_{N}\varepsilon}{2}\Big\},

where the infimum is over integers
k
. Then, by (
5.21
),

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}(C_{N,M,\varepsilon,\delta}^{c})=-\infty.

Therefore, to prove (
5.24
), we only need to show that, for any
M>0
,

(5.25)

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\left(X((t+\tau)N^{2})-X(\tau N^{2})\right)>\varepsilon,\\
B_{N,M,\delta},C_{N,M,\varepsilon,\delta}\Big)=-\infty.

Conditioned on the event
B_{N,M,\delta}\bigcap C_{N,M,\varepsilon,\delta}
, we have
X(\tau N^{2})=k
for some
-a_{N}M\leq k\leq a_{N}M
. Moreover, if
\frac{1}{a_{N}}\left(X((t+\tau)N^{2})-X(\tau N^{2})\right)>\varepsilon
for some
0<t\leq\delta
, then

\displaystyle J_{k-1,k}((t+\tau)N^{2})-J_{k-1,k}(\tau N^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=k}^{X((t+\tau)N^{2})}\eta_{(t+\tau)N^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x=k}^{k+[a_{N}\varepsilon]}\eta_{(t+\tau)N^{2}}(x)=\sum_{x=0}^{[a_{N}\varepsilon]}\eta_{(t+\tau)N^{2}}(x+k)\geq\frac{\rho a_{N}\varepsilon}{2}

for some
-a_{N}M\leq k\leq a_{N}M
.
Thus, to prove (
5.25
), we only need to show that

(5.26)

\limsup_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{T}}\frac{N}{a_{N}^{2}}\log\sum_{k:|k|\leq a_{N}M}\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{\rho}^{*}}\Big(\sup_{0<t\leq\delta}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\left(J_{k-1,k}((t+\tau)N^{2})-J_{k-1,k}(\tau N^{2})\right)\geq\frac{\rho\varepsilon}{2}\Big)\\
=-\infty,

which follows immediately from (
5.9
) since
a_{N}\gg\sqrt{N\log N}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

6.1.

Proof of (

).

, using (

) and (

Proof.

For any
m\geq n
, we write

(6.13)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\mu^{m}(t,u)-\mu^{m}(0,u)]\tilde{G}_{m}(u)\,du\\

We first prove that the first term on the right-hand side in the last identity converges to zero as

3.2

We could also find

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\tilde{\mu}^{m}(t,u)-\tilde{\mu}^{m}(0,u)][G_{m}(u)-G_{n}(u)]\,du=0.

A.1.
Proof of Lemma
3.2

We only prove the statement for
\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}
, and the remaining is much simpler. If
\mathcal{Q}_{T,dyn}(\mu)<\infty
, then there exists some constant

Replacing

Optimizing over

l(\mu,G)^{2}\leq 2\chi(\rho)A[G,G],\quad\forall G\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}).

Since

A.2.
Proof of (
3.5
)

In this subsection, we prove that, for any

,

\int_{0}^{t}{p}_{s}^{\prime}(\cdot)\,ds\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

where

is the heat kernel. By direct calculations,

Making the change of variables

Thus,

\Big|\int_{0}^{t}{p}_{s}^{\prime}(u)\,ds\Big|\leq C\min\Big\{\frac{\sqrt{t}}{|u|}e^{-u^{2}/(2t)},1\Big\}

for some finite constant

, which concludes the proof.

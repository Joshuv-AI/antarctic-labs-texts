A convergent numerical scheme for a model of liquid crystal dynamics subjected to an electric field Thanks: The author’s work was supported in part by NSF awards DMS-1912854 and DMR-2020915.
Franziska Weber

Abstract
. We present a convergent and constraint-preserving numerical discretization of a mathematical model for the dynamics of a liquid crystal subjected to an electric field. This model can be derived from the Oseen-Frank director field theory, assuming that the dynamics of the electric field are governed by the electrostatics equations with a suitable constitutive relation for the electric displacement field that describes the coupling with the liquid crystal director field. The resulting system of partial differential equations consists of an elliptic equation that is coupled to the wave map equations through a quadratic source term. We show that the discretization preserves the unit length constraint of the director field, is energy-stable and convergent. In numerical experiments, we show that the method is stable even when singularities develop. Moreover, predictions about the alignment of the director field with the electric field are confirmed.



1.
Introduction

Liquid crystals are materials that consist of elongated molecules and exhibit states that lie between the liquid and the solid phase, the so called liquid crystal phase.
Some examples include shaving cream, cell membranes, as well as the nematic liquid crystals used in displays (LCD displays).
The microscopic structure of liquid crystals affects at a macroscopic scale the mechanical response to stress and strain. For instance, the molecules of certain liquid crystals react to electric fields on a microscopic scale, which on a macroscopic scale changes the polarization of the light passing through the material. Monitors take advantage of this property to allow a certain amount of red, green, or blue light through each pixel.
Their sensitivity to electric and magnetic fields which allows to orient the molecules in a certain way, makes liquid crystals attractive for many engineering applications.

In this article, we develop a convergent numerical scheme for a simple model for the dynamics of a liquid crystal director field subjected to electromagnetic forces. The model is derived from the Oseen-Frank theory which suggests that the liquid crystal director field,
\mathbf{d}:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
,
n=1,2,3
, which is a unit length vector pointing in the direction of the main orientation of the molecules, behaves in such a way that in an equilibrium, the Oseen-Frank energy
[
35
,
18
]
,

(1.1)

E_{OF}(\mathbf{d})=\int_{\Omega}w_{OF}(\mathbf{d},\nabla\mathbf{d}),

with the energy density

(1.2)

w_{OF}(\mathbf{d},\nabla\mathbf{d})=\frac{1}{2}k_{1}(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{d})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}k_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{d})^{2}+k_{3}(\mathbf{d}\times\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{d})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(k_{2}+k_{4})\operatorname{div}[(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{d}-(\operatorname{div}\mathbf{d})\mathbf{d}],

is minimized. A common approach is to set
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=k>0
and
k_{4}=0
, the so called
one-constant approximation

[
40
]
. In this case, it can be shown that the energy density becomes

(1.3)

w_{OF}=\frac{1}{2}k|\nabla\mathbf{d}|^{2}.

We will also take this approach here, despite it not being realistic for all physical scenarios. The scheme that is proposed here, can be extended in a stable manner to the general case with different constants, but it is unclear whether convergence can be shown in that case also.

Models that incorporate the effects of external fields on the liquid crystal are obtained by adding terms to the Oseen-Frank energy density (
1.2
). For an electric field
E
, this term has been found to be of the form
[
40
]
,

(1.4)

w_{e}=-\frac{1}{2}E\cdot D,\quad D=\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\perp}E+\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{a}(\mathbf{d}\cdot E)\mathbf{d},

where
D
is the electric displacement field,

\epsilon_{0}
is the permittivity of free space,
\epsilon_{\perp},\epsilon_{a}
are dielectric constants depending on material properties and
\epsilon_{a}
is called dielectric anisotropy of the liquid crystal. Values of
\epsilon_{a}
can be negative or positive, depending on the type of liquid crystal. Typical values are between
-0.7
and
11

[
14
,
38
,
39
]
. When
\epsilon_{a}>0
, the director field tends to align with the electric field and when
\epsilon_{a}<0
, it prefers to be perpendicular
[
40
,
12
,
15
]

In an equilibrium situation, the director field minimizes the free energy (
1.1
), now modified with term (
1.4
), while the electric field
E
and the electric displacement field

(1.5)

\operatorname{div}D=\rho_{\text{free}},\quad\operatorname{curl}E=0,

where
\rho_{\text{free}}
is the free electric charge density. In liquid crystal applications, there are generally no free charges, so one can set this source term to be zero. In a smooth, simply-connected domain, the second equation implies that

can be written as the gradient of a potential, i.e.,
E=\nabla\varphi
. Combining (
1.5
) with the expression for

1.4

(1.6)

-\operatorname{div}(\nabla\varphi+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathbf{d})=f,

where
\epsilon_{2}:=\epsilon_{a}\epsilon_{\perp}^{-1}
and
f=-\rho_{\text{free}}/(\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{\perp})
,
that can be augmented with Dirichlet boundary conditions

(1.7)

\varphi(t,x)=g(t,x),\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\,t\geq 0,

for the potential.
To account for non-equilibrium situations such as the dynamic transition between two equilibrium states, e.g., the Freedericksz transition
[
19
,
20
,
37
,
2
]
, one adds inertial and damping terms to the equations for the director field (which can be obtained as the Euler-Lagrange equations from a suitable action functional)

,
33

(1.8)

\begin{split}\sigma\mathbf{d}_{tt}+\alpha\mathbf{d}_{t}=k\Delta\mathbf{d}+\gamma\mathbf{d}+\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{d}\cdot E)E,\end{split}

where
\gamma
is a Lagrange multiplier enforcing the constraint
|\mathbf{d}|=1
and
\epsilon_{1}:=\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{a}
. In addition, one could add the effects of the fluid flow and would end up with a simplified version of the Ericksen-Leslie system
[
16
,
29
]
.
Combining (
1.6
), (
1.7
) and (
1.8
), and augmenting it with initial and boundary conditions for

(1.9a)

\displaystyle\sigma\mathbf{d}_{tt}+\alpha\mathbf{d}_{t}=k\Delta\mathbf{d}+\gamma\mathbf{d}+\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\nabla\varphi,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\,t>0,

(1.9b)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\nabla\varphi+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathbf{d})=f,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\,t\geq 0

(1.9c)

\displaystyle\varphi=g,\quad\nabla\mathbf{d}\cdot\nu=0,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega,\,t\geq 0,

(1.9d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(0,x)=\mathbf{d}_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}\mathbf{d}(0,x)=\mathbf{d}_{1}(x),\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,

(1.9e)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{d}|=1,\quad

\displaystyle\textrm{a.e. }\,x\in\Omega,\,t\geq 0,

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal, and
\mathbf{d}_{0}
,
\mathbf{d}_{1}
a given initial data. Due to the nonlinearities and the constraint

, it may not be realistic to expect classical solutions to system (

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
a bounded domain with uniformly Lipschitz boundary. Assume

and

which can be extended to a function
\widetilde{g}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))

1.9
) for initial data
\mathbf{d}_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
|\mathbf{d}_{0}(x)|=1
a.e. and
\mathbf{d}_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, if
\mathbf{d}\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))
,
\mathbf{d}_{t}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\Omega))
and
\varphi\in L^{\infty}((0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)))
satisfy for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\Omega)
and all
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma(\mathbf{d}_{t}\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\partial_{t}\phi-\alpha(\mathbf{d}_{t}\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\phi\right)dxdt

\displaystyle+\sigma\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{d}_{1}\times\mathbf{d}_{0})\cdot\phi(0,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\big((k\nabla\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{d}):\nabla\phi

\displaystyle-\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)(\nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\phi\big)dxdt,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\psi+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\cdot(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\psi)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f\psi dx,\quad\text{a.e. }t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle|\mathbf{d}|=1,\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T)\times\Omega;

\displaystyle\quad\varphi=g,\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\,t\in[0,T],

and

(1.11)

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\|\mathbf{d}(t,\cdot)-\mathbf{d}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

To design a stable and convergent numerical scheme for (
1.9
), which preserves the unit length constraint for
\mathbf{d}
, we choose to reformulate the equations using the
angular momentum

\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{d}_{t}\times\mathbf{d}
. This approach was taken in
[
25
]
for the wave map equation and resulted in a constraint-preserving convergent scheme. Formally, assuming smooth solutions, (
1.9
) then becomes

(1.12a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{t}=\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{w},\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\,t>0,

(1.12b)

\displaystyle\sigma\mathbf{w}_{t}+\alpha\mathbf{w}=k\Delta\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{d}+\epsilon_{1}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{d},\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\,t>0,

(1.12c)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\nabla\varphi+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathbf{d})=f,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\,t>0

(1.12d)

\displaystyle\varphi=g,\quad\nabla\mathbf{d}\cdot\nu=0,\quad

\displaystyle x\in\partial\Omega,\,t\geq 0,

(1.12e)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(0,x)=\mathbf{d}_{0}(x),\quad\mathbf{w}(0,x)=\mathbf{d}_{1}(x)\times\mathbf{d}_{0}(x),\quad

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,

We notice that the Lagrange multiplier term which enforces the constraint disappears. Nevertheless, this reformulation preserves the constraint, at least at a formal level. One can see this, by taking the inner product of (
1.12a
) with
\mathbf{d}
:

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}|\mathbf{d}|^{2}=\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{t}=(\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{w})\cdot\mathbf{d}=0,

where we used the orthogonality property of the cross product. Hence, the modulus of
\mathbf{d}
stays constant in time at every spatial point, and if initially
|\mathbf{d}_{0}(x)|=1
, this will be true for any
t>0
as well, at least at a formal level. Using an implicit discretization in time, this constraint can be preserved at the discrete level. The implicit discretization will also ensure energy-stability which in turn allows to obtain the necessary a priori estimates for passing the discretization parameters to zero and showing that the limit is a weak solution of (
1.9
). To discretize in space, we use a combination of finite differences for the variables
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{w}
and piecewise linear finite elements for
\varphi
. This ensures preservation of the constraint
|\mathbf{d}|=1
everywhere on the grid. It is probably also possible to use a finite element discretization for
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{w}
, as it was done in
[
6
]
for the wave map equation, but then one can only hope to preserve the constraint at every node of the mesh or on average in each grid cell. In addition, the method we propose here, is rather simple and straightforward to implement.
Because the algebraic system of equations resulting from the discretization is nonlinear, we show that a unique solution exists using a fixed point iteration.

Most rigorous numerical analysis results for related systems do not conserve the constraint
|\mathbf{d}|=1
explicitly and use instead a relaxed version, obtained by adding a term

w_{R}=\frac{1}{4\delta}(|\mathbf{d}|^{2}-1)^{2},

where
\delta>0
is a small parameter, to the energy density (
1.2
), e.g.,
[
31
,
32
,
42
]
. This results in the solution having higher regularity and hence better compactness properties. As we will see, for system (
1.9
), this is not necessary to achieve stability or convergence of the numerical scheme. Besides that, works in which the unit length constraint on the director field is preserved, concern simpler systems, such as the wave or heat map flow or the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation
[
7
,
1
,
5
,
8
,
6
,
10
,
9
,
23
]
, or 1D settings
[
2
,
3
]
. The numerical methods of
[
2
,
3
]
have not been shown to be convergent, to the best of our knowledge. Therefore, our contribution is the construction of a constraint-preserving and convergent discretization of (

Remark 1.2
.

Magnetic forces could be added in a similar way as electric fields, using the magnetostatics equations. We do not consider these here because the resulting system is of similar type and no mathematical difficulty is added, except that the system becomes larger, as it involves second elliptic equation that needs to be solved, and hence its numerical solution takes more computational effort.

2.
The numerical scheme

In this section, we describe the numerical scheme to approximate system (
1.12
) and prove that it preserves the unit length constraint for the director field, and is energy-stable. We start by introducing the time discretization.

2.1.
Time discretization

We let
T>0
the final time and
{\Delta t}>0
some small number, conditions on it will be specified later on. We define
t^{m}:=m{\Delta t}
, the time steps, and
N_{T}=T/{\Delta t}\in\mathbb{N}
(
{\Delta t}
is w.l.o.g. such that
T/{\Delta t}
is an integer). Then we define the time differences and averages,

D_{t}a^{m}:=\frac{a^{m+1}-a^{m}}{{\Delta t}},\quad a^{m+1/2}=\frac{a^{m}+a^{m+1}}{2},

for vectors
a=(a^{0},\dots,a^{N_{T}})
.
We consider the following implicit discretization of (
1.12

(2.1a)

\displaystyle D_{t}\mathbf{d}^{m}=\mathbf{d}^{m+1/2}\times\mathbf{w}^{m+1/2},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\sigma D_{t}\mathbf{w}^{m}=k\Delta\mathbf{d}^{m+1/2}\times\mathbf{d}^{m+1/2}-\alpha\mathbf{w}^{m+1/2}

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\sigma D_t \mathbf{w}^{m}=k}+\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\bigl[(\nabla\varphi^{m+1}\cdot\mathbf{d}^{m+1/2})\nabla\varphi^{m}+(\nabla\varphi^{m}\cdot\mathbf{d}^{m+1/2})\nabla\varphi^{m+1}\bigr]\times\mathbf{d}^{m+1/2},

(2.1d)

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\nabla\varphi^{m}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}^{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi^{m})\mathbf{d}^{m})=f^{m},

m=0,1,\dots
, and
x\in\Omega
, with boundary conditions

(2.2)

\nabla\mathbf{d}^{m}\cdot\nu=0,\quad\varphi^{m}=g^{m},\quad x\in\partial\Omega,

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal and
g^{m}(x)=g(t^{m},x)
,
m=0,1,2,\dots
. We assume that

has an extension
\widetilde{g}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))
to the whole space.
One can derive an energy balance for this method:

\frac{1}{2}D_{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(k|\nabla\mathbf{d}^{m}|^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}|\mathbf{w}^{m}|^{2}+2|\nabla\varphi^{m}|^{2}+2\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}^{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi^{m})^{2}-2\varphi^{m}f^{m}\right)dx\\
-\left(1+\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2\epsilon_{2}}\right)D_{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla\varphi^{m}+\epsilon_{2}(\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{d}^{m})\mathbf{d}^{m}\right)\nabla\widetilde{g}^{m}dx+D_{t}\int_{\Omega}f^{m}\widetilde{g}^{m}dx\\
=-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2\epsilon_{2}}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}\nabla\widetilde{g}^{m}\left(2\nabla\varphi^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\epsilon_{2}(\nabla\varphi^{m}\cdot\mathbf{d}^{m})\mathbf{d}^{m}+\epsilon_{2}(\nabla\varphi^{m+1}\cdot\mathbf{d}^{m+1})\mathbf{d}^{m+1}\right)dx\\
+\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{2}}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}-\widetilde{g}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})D_{t}f^{m}\,dx-\alpha\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{w}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|^{2}dx.

Using Poincaré’s and Grönwall’s inequality, one can derive bounds on
\mathbf{d}^{m},\mathbf{w}^{m}
and
\varphi^{m}
from this. Since we will do this in detail for the fully discrete method, we do not outline the details here.

2.2.
Spatial discretization

To discretize space, we use first order finite differences for the variables
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{w}
(and approximate them using piecewise constants), and a finite element formulation for the variable
\varphi
with continuous piecewise linear basis functions. This way, the constraint
|\mathbf{d}|=1
is conserved at any point in space and time, and it is easy to formulate a convergent fixed point iteration, as we will see later.
We assume for this section that
\Omega=[0,1]^{n}

For the variables
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle\leq C(\Omega)\left\|f(t^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\widetilde{g}^{M}_{h}\right\|_{H^{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|f(t^{M})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\widetilde{g}^{M}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

Comparing (

3.
Convergence

Using the discrete energy estimate (
2.11
), we now proceed to showing convergence of the scheme. We assume that the initial data satisfies

(3.1)

\mathbf{d}_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega),\quad|\mathbf{d}_{0}(x)|=1,\quad\text{a.e.}\,x\in\Omega,\quad\partial_{t}\mathbf{d}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{d}_{1}\in L^{2}(\Omega),

and define
\mathbf{w}_{0}:=\mathbf{d}_{1}\times\mathbf{d}_{0}
and set
\mathbf{w}_{h}(0,\cdot)=\mathbf{w}_{0}
. This implies for the piecewise constant functions (
2.3
)–(
2.6
)

(3.2)

\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C,\quad\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}(0,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C,\quad|\mathbf{d}_{h}(0,x)|=1,\quad x\in\Omega.

Moreover, we assume that

(3.3)

{\Delta t}\leq Ch,\quad\text{for some constant}\,C>0.

Then we can show

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
k>0
,
\sigma>0
and
\alpha\geq 0
, and
\epsilon_{2}>-1
. Assume
f\in W^{1,2}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
and
g\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\partial\Omega)
which can be extended to a function
\widetilde{g}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))
. Moreover, assume that the initial data
(\mathbf{d}_{0},\mathbf{w}_{0})
satisfies (
3.1
) and that the time step
{\Delta t}
and the grid size
h
are related by (
3.3
). Then, as
h,{\Delta t}\to 0
, the approximations
(\mathbf{d}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h},\varphi_{h})
as defined in (
2.3
)–(
2.7
) converge up to a subsequence to a weak solution of (
1.9
) as in Definition
1.1
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, the Poincaré inequality for
u_{0,h}
, and that
\left\|\widetilde{g}_{h}(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\left\|\widetilde{g}(t)\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq C
uniformly in
h
,
we obtain the following uniform in
h>0
bounds for all
t\in[0,T]
,

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,\quad\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,

(3.4b)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{d}_{h}(t,x)|=1,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

(3.4c)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,

(3.4d)

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi_{h}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,\quad\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C.

Using the numerical scheme, we also get a priori estimates on the forward time differences of
\mathbf{d}_{h}
:
Since
D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{d}^{m}_{\underline{i}}=\mathbf{d}_{\underline{i}}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\times\mathbf{w}_{\underline{i}}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
, and
|\mathbf{d}_{\underline{i}}^{m}|=1
by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain,

(3.5)

\left\|D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{d}_{h}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C.

The Banach-Alaoglu theorem then implies the convergence of subsequences, for simplicity of notation denoted by
\{(\mathbf{d}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h},\varphi_{h})\}_{h>0}
,

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{d},\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T);L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{d},\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T);L^{2}(\Omega)),

(3.6b)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{d}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\partial_{t}\mathbf{d},\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T);L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\nabla\mathbf{d},\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T);L^{2}(\Omega)),

(3.6d)

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\varphi,\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{1}(\Omega)),\quad u_{0,h}\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}u_{0},\quad\text{in }\,L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))

as

. The second item follows since

where

Letting

), and (

\psi=u_{0,h}=\varphi_{h}-\widetilde{g}_{h}
as a test function in (

), and integrate over

:

(3.14)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla\varphi_{h}|^{2}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})^{2})dx=&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}fu_{0,h}\,dxdt\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h})\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}_{h}\,dxdt.\end{split}

Subtracting (
3.13
) from (
3.14
), we obtain

(3.15)

\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\varphi_{h}|^{2}-|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})^{2}-\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)^{2}\right)dxdt}_{A}\\
=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f\cdot u_{0,h}dxdt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}f\cdot u_{0}dxdt}_{F}\\
+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h})\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}_{h}\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathbf{d})\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}\,dxdt}_{G}.

We denote
E_{h}:=\nabla\varphi_{h}
and
E:=\nabla\varphi
. Then we can rewrite the term
A
as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(|E_{h}-E|^{2}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot(E_{h}-E))^{2}\right)dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(2E\cdot(E_{h}-E)+\epsilon_{2}\left(2(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot(E_{h}-E))(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot E)+(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot E)^{2}-(\mathbf{d}\cdot E)^{2}\right)\right)dxdt

We note that for
\epsilon_{2}>-1
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(|E_{h}-E|^{2}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot(E_{h}-E))^{2}\right)dxdt\geq\min\{1,1-\epsilon_{2}\}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|E_{h}-E|^{2}dxdt.

Hence (
3.15
) implies

(3.16)

\min\{1,1-\epsilon_{2}\}\left\|E-E_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}^{2}\\
\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left(|E_{h}-E|^{2}+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot(E_{h}-E))^{2}\right)dxdt\\
=\underbrace{2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}E\cdot(E-E_{h})dxdt}_{A_{1}}-\underbrace{2\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot E_{h})(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot E)dxdt}_{A_{2}}\\
+\underbrace{\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left((\mathbf{d}\cdot E)^{2}+(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot E)^{2}\right)dxdt}_{A_{3}}+F+G

We now show that each of the terms
A_{1}
,
F
and
G
go to zero as
h\to 0
. In addition, we will see that
A_{3}-A_{2}\to 0
. The term
A_{1}
goes to zero because
\{E_{h}\}_{h>0}
converges weakly up to a subsequence.
For the term
A_{2}
, we use Lemma
A.1
componentwise for
v_{m}=E_{h_{m}}^{(i)}E^{(j)}
and
u_{m}=\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(k)}\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(\ell)}
,
i,j,k,\ell=1,\dots,n
, since
\mathbf{d}_{h}\in L^{\infty}([0,T)\times\Omega)
and converges almost everywhere, and
E_{h}^{(i)}E^{(j)}
converges weakly in
L^{1}
to
E^{(i)}E^{(j)}
as

. Thus,

A_{2}\longrightarrow 2\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{d}\cdot E)^{2}dxdt,\quad\text{ as }h,\Delta t\to 0.

For the term
A_{3}
, we can use the same lemma with
v_{m}=E^{(i)}E^{(j)}
and
u_{m}=\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(k)}\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(\ell)}
,
i,j,k,\ell=1,\dots,n
to obtain

A_{3}\longrightarrow 2\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{d}\cdot E)^{2}dxdt,\quad\text{ as }h,\Delta t\to 0.

Hence
A_{3}-A_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h,\Delta t\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0
. The term
F
goes to zero due to the weak convergence of
u_{0,h}
, (
3.6d
).

We turn to the remaining term
G
.
We can write it as

G=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}_{h}-\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g})\,dxdt}_{G_{1}}\\
+\underbrace{\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}-(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi)\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g})\,dxdt}_{G_{2}}\\
+\underbrace{\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}-(\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g})\,dxdt}_{G_{3}}\\
+\underbrace{\epsilon_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}_{h}-(\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{g}\,dxdt}_{G_{4}}

G_{1}
goes to zero by the strong convergence of
\nabla\widetilde{g}_{h}
in
L^{2}_{t,x}
and the weak convergence of
\nabla\varphi_{h}
in
L^{2}_{t,x}
, and
G_{2}
goes to zero by the weak convergence of
\nabla\varphi_{h}
in
L^{2}_{t,x}
as well. For
G_{3}
, we use Lemma
A.1
componentwise with
v_{m}=\partial_{i}\varphi_{h_{m}}\partial_{j}\widetilde{g}
and
u_{m}=\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(k)}\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{d}^{(k)}\mathbf{d}^{(\ell)}
since

converges almost everywhere and
\partial_{i}\varphi_{h_{m}}\partial_{j}\widetilde{g}
converges weakly to
\partial_{i}\varphi\partial_{j}\widetilde{g}
in
L^{1}
as
{\Delta t},h\to 0
. Finally,
G_{4}\to 0
due to the strong convergence of
\nabla\widetilde{g}_{h}
in

, c.f. (

). Hence also

as
{\Delta t},h\to 0
.
Therefore, the right hand side of (
3.16
) goes to zero as
h,\Delta t\to 0
(up to subsequence).
Hence

(3.17)

E_{h}=\nabla\varphi_{h}\longrightarrow E=\nabla\varphi,\quad\text{ in }L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega),\,\text{ as }h,\Delta t\to 0.

We observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(S_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})(\nabla\varphi_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt}_{I}

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt}_{J}.

We consider the term
I
(
J
can be treated similarly). We rewrite it as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})(\nabla\varphi_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot{\mathbf{d}})(\nabla\varphi\times{\mathbf{d}})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt}_{I_{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\left[(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})(\nabla\varphi\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})-(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot{\mathbf{d}})(\nabla\varphi\times{\mathbf{d}})\right]\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt}_{I_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})((\nabla\varphi_{h}-\nabla\varphi)\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt}_{I_{3}}.

To analyze term
I_{1}
, we denote
v:=[(\nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\phi_{2}]\mathbf{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
. We assume that
{\Delta t}
is small enough such that the support of
\phi_{2}(t,\cdot)
(and hence
v(t,\cdot)
) is contained in
(0,T-{\Delta t})
. Then

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\Delta t}}^{T+{\Delta t}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot v(t-{\Delta t},x)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\Delta t}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot(v(t-{\Delta t},x)-v(t,x))dxdt+\int_{{\Delta t}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{{\Delta t}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot(v(t-{\Delta t},x)-v(t,x))dxdt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{{\Delta t}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt,

where we used for the second and third equality that
v(t,\cdot)
is supported in
(0,T-{\Delta t})
.
Hence

\displaystyle\left|I_{1}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi(t,x)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{{\Delta t}}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot(v(t-{\Delta t},x)-v(t,x))dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}-\nabla\varphi)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{{\Delta t}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi_{h}(t,x)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\|v(\cdot-{\Delta t})-v\right\|_{L^{2}([{\Delta t},T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}-\nabla\varphi)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+{\Delta t}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v(\cdot-{\Delta t})-v\right\|_{L^{2}([{\Delta t},T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi_{h}-\nabla\varphi)\cdot v(t,x)dxdt\right|+C{\Delta t},

where we used the energy inequality, Lemma
2.2
, for the last inequality. Now the first term on the right hand side goes to zero by the continuity of
L^{2}
-shifts and the second term goes to zero due to the weak convergence of
\nabla\varphi_{h}
, (
3.6d
). Hence

I_{1}\longrightarrow\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{d})(\nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt,\quad\text{ as }{\Delta t},h\to 0,

up to a subsequence. Next, we consider
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle I_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{i}\varphi_{h}(t+{\Delta t},x)\cdot\left\{\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{(i)}(\nabla\varphi\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})-\mathbf{d}^{(i)}(\nabla\varphi\times{\mathbf{d}})\right\}\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt.

We apply Lemma
A.1
componentwise with
v_{m}=\partial_{i}\varphi_{h_{m}}\partial_{j}\varphi\phi_{2}^{(r)}
and
u_{m}=\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(k)}\mathbf{d}_{h_{m}}^{(\ell)}-\mathbf{d}^{(k)}\mathbf{d}^{(\ell)}
,
i,j,k,\ell,r=1,\dots,n
and obtain that
I_{2}\to 0
as
{\Delta t},h\to 0
because
u_{m}\to 0
a.e.. Lastly,
I_{3}
goes to zero due to the
L^{2}
-convergence of
\nabla\varphi_{h}
:

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\phi_{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}(\cdot+{\Delta t})\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}-\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}-\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h,{\Delta t}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

where we also used the energy inequality, Lemma
2.2
. Thus, we conclude that

I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h,{\Delta t}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{d})(\nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt.

In a similar way, one can show that
J
converges to the same quantity and hence

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(S_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h,{\Delta t}\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\epsilon_{1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathbf{d})(\nabla\varphi\times\mathbf{d})\cdot\phi_{2}dxdt.

It remains to show that the limits of
\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}
and
\mathbf{w}_{h}
agree. We note that

\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}(t,x)=\mathbf{w}_{h}(t,x)+\frac{1}{2}{\Delta t}D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{w}_{h}(t,x))

and use the finite difference scheme (
2.10b
) to show that
{\Delta t}D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{w}_{h}
vanishes. We write (
2.10b
) in terms of the piecewise constant functions
\mathbf{d}_{h}
,
\mathbf{w}_{h}
, etc.,

(3.18)

\sigma D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{w}_{h}=-\alpha\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}+k\operatorname{div}_{h}(\nabla_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})+S_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h},

where
S_{h}
is defined in (
3.9
).
Note that
S_{h}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];)L^{1}(\Omega))
for all
h>0
by the a priori estimates (
3.4
).
We multiply (
3.18
) by a test function
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, integrate over the domain, and change variables,

(3.19)

\sigma\int_{\Omega}D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{w}_{h}\cdot\phi dx=-\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha\widehat{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\phi+k(\nabla_{h}\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h}):\nabla_{h}\phi\right)dx+\int_{\Omega}(S_{h}\times\widehat{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\cdot\phi dx.

Multiplying everything with
{\Delta t}
, we see that the right hand side goes to zero as
{\Delta t}\to 0
due to the uniform bounds coming from the energy estimate (
2.11
), and hence
{\Delta t}\int_{\Omega}D_{t}^{+}\mathbf{w}_{h}\cdot\phi dx\to 0
for all test functions
\phi\in L^{1}([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega))
. Hence
\mathbf{w}=\widehat{\mathbf{w}}
in
L^{\infty}([0,T];H^{-2}(\Omega))
for this subsequence. Using density of
H^{2}
-functions in
L^{2}
, and that
\mathbf{w},\widehat{\mathbf{w}}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
, we have
\mathbf{w}=\widehat{\mathbf{w}}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)
and in particular
\mathbf{w}=\widehat{\mathbf{w}}
for almost every
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
. This allows us to pass to the limit in all the terms in (
3.11
) and conclude that the limit is a weak solution of (
1.12
). Since
\mathbf{d}_{t}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))
, the first equation (
1.12a
) holds for a.e.
(t,x)
. Since
|\mathbf{d}|=1
, this means (taking the cross product with
\mathbf{d}
):

(3.20)

\mathbf{d}_{t}\times\mathbf{d}=(\mathbf{d}\times\mathbf{w})\times\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{w}-(\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{w})\mathbf{d}.

But from the numerical method (
2.10a
)–(
2.10b
), we have

D_{t}(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}\cdot\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m})=\mathbf{d}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{h}\cdot D_{t}\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m}+\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\cdot D_{t}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}=0,

and since
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{0}\cdot\mathbf{w}_{h}^{0}=0
, this implies that

\mathbf{d}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{w}_{h}=0,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

Since
\mathbf{d}_{h}
converges strongly, and
\mathbf{w}_{h}
converges weakly in

, we obtain that in the limit

\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0,\quad\text{ a.e. }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

Thus, (
3.20
) becomes

\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{d}_{t}\times\mathbf{d},

and we conclude that the limit
(\mathbf{d},\varphi)
satisfies (
1.10
). The continuity of
\mathbf{d}
at zero, (
1.11

\mathbf{d}_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)

is a weak solution in the sense of Definition

4.
Solving the nonlinear system (
2.10
)

The algebraic system (
2.10
) is nonlinear and implicit, therefore it is not a priori clear that it has a solution. In this section, we show that a solution can be obtained using a fixed point iteration. In 3D, this iteration converges only under a strict assumption for the CFL-condition, specifically, given the estimates on the approximations we have from the energy estimate, Lemma
2.2
, we require
{\Delta t}\leq Ch^{\theta}
, where
\theta=\max\{1,\nicefrac{{n}}{{2}}\}
, for some constant
C>0
. It is possible that there are faster ways of obtaining solutions to (
2.10
) and under milder assumptions on the time step
{\Delta t}
. This is one of our current research efforts.
The fixed point iteration yields a constructive existence result, but in practice, the algebraic system (
2.10
) can also be solved using Newton’s method or similar.
We will assume for this section that
\sigma>0
, if this assumption is not made, then the CFL-condition needed for convergence of the fixed point algorithm might be stricter, c.f.
[
43
]
.

To set up a suitable fixed point iteration, we first collect a few observations:
We first note, that the update for
\mathbf{d}_{\underline{i}}^{m}
, (
2.10a
), can be written as

(4.1)

\mathbf{d}^{m+1}_{\underline{i}}=V(\mathbf{w}_{\underline{i}}^{m+\frac{1}{2}})\mathbf{d}_{\underline{i}}^{m},

where
V
is the matrix given by

V(w)=\frac{1}{1+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{4}|w|^{2}}\left(\left(1-\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{4}|w|^{2}\right)\mathbf{I}+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2}w\otimes w+\Delta tQ(w)\right),

with
Q(w)
defined as the skew-symmetric matrix

Q(w)=\begin{pmatrix}0&w^{(3)}&-w^{(2)}\\
-w^{(3)}&0&w^{(1)}\\
w^{(2)}&-w^{(1)}&0\end{pmatrix}.

In fact,
Q
is such that
Q(w)v=v\times w
. In
[
25
, Lemma 4.8]
we have shown that

(4.2)

\left\|V(\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{1})\mathbf{d}_{h}-V(\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{2})\mathbf{d}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\Delta t\left\|\mathbf{w}^{1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where
\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{i}=(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{i}+\mathbf{w}_{h})/2
,
i=1,2
, for piecewise constant functions
\mathbf{w}_{h}
,
\mathbf{d}_{h}
,
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{i}
,
i=1,2
on the grid on
\Omega
.
Similarly,
\varphi^{m+1}_{h}
solves the equation (
2.10c
), and is therefore a function of
\mathbf{d}^{m+1}_{\underline{i}}
and hence
\mathbf{w}_{\underline{i}}^{m+1}
. So
\varphi^{m+1}_{h}:=A(V(\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}):=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1})
. The bilinear forms
a
and
a_{h}
satisfy

\begin{split}(1+\min\{0,\epsilon_{2}\})\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}&\leq a(v,v),\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\\
(1+\min\{0,\epsilon_{2}\})\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}&\leq a_{h}(v,v),\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left|a(v,w)\right|&\leq C(\epsilon_{2})\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\left\|w\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}},\quad\forall v,w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\\
\left|a_{h}(v,w)\right|&\leq C(\epsilon_{2})\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\left\|w\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}},\quad\forall v,w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{split}

for any
\mathbf{d}
with
\|\mathbf{d}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
(
\|\mathbf{d}_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
) uniformly in
h
, which implies ellipticity and continuity in
H^{1}_{0}
of
a
,
a_{h}
respectively and hence solvability of the elliptic equation for a given
\mathbf{d}
,
\mathbf{d}_{h}
with
\|\mathbf{d}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
(
\|\mathbf{d}_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
) respectively as long as
\epsilon_{2}>-1
.
Furthermore, we can estimate the difference
\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}
between two solutions
\varphi_{h}^{1}
and
\varphi_{h}^{2}
of (
3.8c
) for different given
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}
and
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}
(but same boundary conditions and right hand side
f
): If we assume the same boundary conditions, then
\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2}\in V_{h}
, hence we can use it as a test function in (
3.8c
) for the equation for
\varphi_{h}^{1}
and
\varphi_{h}^{2}
. Subtracting the two resulting equations, we have (we omit the time dependence to simplify the notation)

a_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{1},\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})-a_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{2},\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})=0.

This is the same as

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})|^{2}dx+\epsilon_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})\cdot\frac{\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}+\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}dx\\
+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left((\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})\right)\left((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1})+(\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\right)dx\\
+\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}\left((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}+\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2})\cdot(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\right)\left((\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}+\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2})\right)dx=0.

We rearrange terms:

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})|^{2}dx+\epsilon_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})\cdot\frac{\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}+\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}dx\\
=-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left((\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi_{h}^{2})\right)\left((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1})+(\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\right)dx\\
-\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{4}\int_{\Omega}\left((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}+\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2})\cdot(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\right)\left((\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}+\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2})\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2})\right)dx.

The left hand side of this expression, we can lower bound by

\text{ LHS }\geq(1+\min(0,\epsilon_{2}))\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

The right hand side, we can upper bound by

\text{ RHS }\leq C\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Combining the two, we obtain

\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\epsilon_{2}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Combining the ‘inverse’ estimate for finite element approximations, e.g.
[
13
, Thm. 3.2.6]
,

(4.3)

\left\|f_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq h^{-\frac{n}{q}}\left\|f_{h}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

for any
1\leq q<\infty
with the energy estimate (
2.11
), we obtain

\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\epsilon_{2}}h^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C_{\epsilon_{2}}h^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

When
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}
and
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}
are given by
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}=V(\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{1}_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h}
and
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}=V(\overline{\mathbf{w}}^{2}_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h}
, we also obtain, using (
4.2
),

(4.4)

\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{1}-\nabla\varphi^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\epsilon_{2}}h^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Delta t\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using the Poincaré inequality for the difference of
\varphi_{h}^{1}
and
\varphi_{h}^{2}
which has zero trace, we also get

(4.5)

\left\|\varphi_{h}^{1}-\varphi^{2}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\epsilon_{2}}h^{-\frac{n}{2}}\Delta t\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We can therefore write the updates
\varphi_{h}^{m+1}
and
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m+1}
as functions of
(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m},\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m},\varphi_{h}^{m})
and
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m+1}=V(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}
and
\varphi^{m+1}_{h}:=A(V(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}):=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1})
and consider the mapping
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{\text{old}}\mapsto\mathbf{w}^{\text{new}}_{h}=F(\mathbf{w}^{\text{old}}_{h})
defined by, for given
(\mathbf{d}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h},\varphi_{h})
, (and
f
and boundary data
g
)

(4.6)

\begin{split}\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}&=\frac{\mathbf{w}_{h}+\mathbf{w}_{h}^{\text{old}}}{2},\\
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{\text{new}}&=V(\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{h})\mathbf{d}_{h},\\
\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}&=\frac{\mathbf{d}_{h}+\mathbf{d}_{h}^{\text{new}}}{2},\\
u_{0,h}^{\text{new}}\in V_{h},\quad\text{s.t.}\quad&a_{h}^{\text{new}}(u_{0,h}^{\text{new}},v)=F^{m+1}(v)-a_{h}^{\text{new}}(\widetilde{g}_{h}^{m+1},v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h},\quad\varphi_{h}^{\text{new}}:=u_{0,h}^{\text{new}}+\widetilde{g}_{h}^{m+1},\\
\sigma\frac{\mathbf{w}^{\text{new}}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}}{\Delta t}&=k\Delta_{h}\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}-\alpha\frac{\mathbf{w}_{h}+\mathbf{w}_{h}^{\text{new}}}{2}+S_{h}\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}\\
\end{split}

where

a_{h}^{\text{new}}(v,w):=\int_{\Omega}\left[\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{\text{new}}\cdot\nabla v)(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{\text{new}}\cdot\nabla w)\right]dx

(i.e. the bilinear form using the update
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{\text{new}}
as coefficients), and

S_{h}(x):=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2|\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}}\Big((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{\text{new}}(y)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\nabla\varphi_{h}(y)+(\nabla\varphi_{h}(y)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h})\nabla\varphi_{h}^{\text{new}}(y)\Big)dy,\quad x\in\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}.

We observe that if
(\mathbf{d}_{h},\mathbf{w}_{h},\varphi_{h})=(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m},\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m},\varphi_{h}^{m})
, then
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}
is a fixed point of (
4.6
). Therefore, we will show that
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{\text{old}}\mapsto\mathbf{w}^{\text{new}}_{h}=F(\mathbf{w}^{\text{old}}_{h})
is a contraction under a suitable time step constraint and hence the fixed point exists.

Lemma 4.1
.

The mapping
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{\text{old}}\mapsto\mathbf{w}^{\text{new}}_{h}=F(\mathbf{w}^{\text{old}}_{h})
defined by (
4.6
) is a contraction

\left\|F(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1})-F(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2})\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq q\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}},

for
0<q<1
under the time step constraint

(4.7)

\Delta t\leq\kappa h^{\max\{1,\nicefrac{{n}}{{2}}\}},

for a constant
\kappa>0
sufficiently small.

Proof.

We denote
\mathbf{w}^{1,\text{new}}_{h}=F(\mathbf{w}^{1}_{h})
and
\mathbf{w}^{2,\text{new}}_{h}=F(\mathbf{w}^{2}_{h})
two solutions with starting values
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{i}
,
i=1,2
and
\overline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}^{i}=0.5(\mathbf{w}_{h}+\mathbf{w}_{h}^{i})
,
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{i}:=\mathbf{d}_{h}^{i,\text{new}}
and
\varphi_{h}^{i}:=\varphi_{h}^{i,\text{new}}
.
We rewrite the equation for
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{i,\text{new}}
as

\left(\sigma+\frac{\alpha\Delta t}{2}\right)\mathbf{w}^{i,\text{new}}_{h}=\Delta tk\Delta_{h}\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{i}\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{i}+\left(\sigma-\frac{\alpha\Delta t}{2}\right)\mathbf{w}_{h}+{\Delta t}S_{h}^{i}\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{i}

We subtract the equation for
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1,\text{new}}
from the equation for
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2,\text{new}}
to obtain

\begin{split}&\left(\sigma+\frac{\alpha\Delta t}{2}\right)(\mathbf{w}^{2,\text{new}}_{h}-\mathbf{w}^{1,\text{new}}_{h})\\
&=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\Delta tk\Delta_{h}\left(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right)\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{2}}_{A_{1}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\Delta tk\Delta_{h}\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}\times\left(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right)}_{A_{2}}+\underbrace{\frac{\Delta t}{2}S_{h}^{2}\times(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1})}_{B}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{\underline{i}}\frac{\epsilon_{1}{\Delta t}}{4|\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}}\!\!\!\Big((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}(y)\cdot({\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{2}-{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}))\nabla\varphi_{h}(y)+(\nabla\varphi_{h}(y)\cdot({\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{2}-{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}))\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}(y)\Big)dy\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}}(x)\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}}_{D}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{\underline{i}}\frac{\epsilon_{1}{\Delta t}}{2|\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}}\!\!\!\Big((\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{2}-\varphi_{h}^{1})(y)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1})\nabla\varphi_{h}(y)+(\nabla\varphi_{h}(y)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1})\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{2}-\varphi_{h}^{1})(y)\Big)dy\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}}(x)\times\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}}_{E}.\end{split}

Thus,

\left\|\mathbf{w}^{2,\text{new}}_{h}-\mathbf{w}^{1,\text{new}}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma+\frac{\alpha\Delta t}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left\|A_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|B\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|D\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|E\right\|_{L^{2}}\right),

and we have to bound each of these terms. Again, we will frequently use the estimate

\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1,

which follows because
\mathbf{d}_{h}^{i}
satisfy the unit length constraint (see
[
25
]
for a proof of this fact via analyzing the matrix
V
in (
4.1
)). We will denote by
C
a generic constant that does not depend on
h
or
\Delta t
. For
A_{1}
, we have

(4.8)

\left\|A_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{k\Delta t}{2}\left\|\Delta_{h}(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:destimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C\Delta t^{2}}{h^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Similarly, we find for
A_{2}
,

(4.9)

\left\|A_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{k\Delta t}{2}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta_{h}\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:destimate}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C\Delta t^{2}}{h^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

For
B
, we have

\begin{split}\left\|B\right\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C\Delta t\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\varphi^{2}_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{n}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\varphi^{2}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

using the inverse estimate (
4.3
). Using then the energy estimate (
2.11
) and (
4.2
), we obtain

(4.10)

\left\|B\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C\Delta t^{2}}{h^{n}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

We turn to the term
D
:

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|D\right\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C\Delta t\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{n}}\left\|\mathbf{d}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{d}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\Delta t^{2}}{h^{n}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

using again the inverse bound (
4.3
), the energy estimate and (
4.2
).
We next estimate term
E
:

\displaystyle\left\|E\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\left\|\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{2}-\varphi_{h}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:inverse}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{2}-\varphi_{h}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left\|\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{2}-\varphi_{h}^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we used the energy estimate for the last inequality. Now using (
4.4
), we obtain

(4.12)

\left\|E\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C\Delta t^{2}}{h^{n}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
), and (
4.12
), we obtain

\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2,\text{new}}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1,\text{new}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\sigma+\frac{\alpha\Delta t}{2}}\left(\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{h^{2}}+\frac{\Delta t^{2}}{h^{n}}\right)\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{2}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

So if we take
\Delta t\leq\kappa h^{\max\{1,\nicefrac{{n}}{{2}}\}}
for
\kappa
sufficiently small
we obtain that
F
is a contraction.
∎

The previous lemma will imply that the following algorithm converges. We denote
\theta=\max\{1,\nicefrac{{n}}{{2}}\}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Given
h>0
,
\Delta t=\kappa h^{\theta}
, and functions

(\mathbf{d}^{m}_{h},\mathbf{w}^{m}_{h},\varphi_{h}^{m})
satisfying (
2.10
), we approximate
the next time-step
(d^{m+1}_{h},w^{m+1}_{h},\varphi_{h}^{m+1})
to a given
tolerance
\varepsilon>0
by the following procedure: Set

(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,0},\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,0},\varphi_{h}^{m,0})=(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m},\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m},\varphi_{h}^{m}),

and iteratively solve
(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+1},\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s+1},\varphi_{h}^{m,s+1})
satisfying

(4.13)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathbf{d}^{m,s+1}_{h}-\mathbf{d}^{m}_{h}}{\Delta t}&=\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\times\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{w}^{m}_{h}+\mathbf{w}^{m,s}_{h}\right),\\
u_{0,h}^{m,s+1}\in V_{h},\quad\text{s.t.}\quad&a_{h}^{m,s+1}(u_{0,h}^{m,s+1},v)=F^{m+1}(v)-a_{h}^{m,s+1}(\widetilde{g}_{h}^{m+1},v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h},\\
\varphi_{h}^{m,s+1}&:=u_{0,h}^{m,s+1}+\widetilde{g}_{h}^{m+1},\\
\sigma\frac{\mathbf{w}^{m,s+1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}^{m}_{h}}{\Delta t}&=k\Delta_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\times\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}-\alpha\frac{\mathbf{w}^{m,s+1}_{h}+\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m}}{2}+S_{h}\times\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\end{split}

where
\mathbf{d}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{h}:=(\mathbf{d}^{m}_{h}+\mathbf{d}^{m,s+1}_{h})/2
,

a_{h}^{m,s+1}(v,w):=\int_{\Omega}\left[\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+\epsilon_{2}(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+1}\cdot\nabla v)(\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+1}\cdot\nabla w)\right]dx,

and

S_{h}(x):=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2|\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}|}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}}\Big((\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s+1}\cdot{\mathbf{d}}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{h})\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m}+(\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m}\cdot\mathbf{d}^{m,s+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{h})\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s+1}\Big)dy,\quad x\in\mathcal{C}_{\underline{i}}.

until the following stopping criterion is met:

(4.14)

\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+1}-\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s+1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon.

We note that (
4.13
) corresponds to the nonlinear mapping
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\mapsto F(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s})=\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s+1}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For every
\varepsilon_{0}>0
there is a number of iterations
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that the stopping criterion (
4.14
) is satisfied with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
and moreover

\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m+1}-\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m+1}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon_{0}.

Proof.

We let
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary. Using that
\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}
is a fixed point of
F
, we have

\begin{split}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}&=\left\|F(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1})-F(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s-1})\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem. }\ref{lem:contraction}}}{{\leq}}q\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq q^{s}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

applying Lemma
4.1

s-1
more times. Using the energy estimate, we then obtain

\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2q^{s}\sqrt{\frac{E_{0}}{\sigma}}.

Using an inverse estimate and (
4.2
), we also obtain

(4.15)

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s}-\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\frac{C}{h}\left\|V(\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}-V\left((\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m}+\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s-1})/2\right)\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h}q^{s-1}\sqrt{\frac{E_{0}}{\sigma}}\\
&\leq Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from the CFL-condition (
4.7
).
Similarly, using (
4.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla\varphi^{m+1}_{h}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C\frac{\Delta t}{h^{\frac{n}{2}}}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s-1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{C\Delta t}{h^{\frac{n}{2}}}q^{s-1}\sqrt{\frac{E_{0}}{\sigma}}\\
&\leq Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}},\end{split}

where the last inequality again follows from the CFL-condition. Thus,

\left\|\mathbf{w}^{m+1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla\varphi^{m+1}_{h}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}^{m+1}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}}.

Next, by triangle inequality

\left\|\mathbf{w}^{m,s+1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{w}^{m+1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s+1}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathbf{w}^{m+1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 4q^{s}\sqrt{\frac{E_{0}}{\sigma}}.

By the same argument, we obtain,

\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}^{m,s+1}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|\nabla\varphi^{m,s+1}_{h}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}}.

Hence

\left\|\mathbf{w}^{m,s+1}_{h}-\mathbf{w}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla\varphi^{m,s+1}_{h}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}^{m,s+1}_{h}-\nabla_{h}\mathbf{d}_{h}^{m,s}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}},

and we can pick
s
large enough such that
Cq^{s}\sqrt{E_{0}}<\varepsilon_{0}
.
∎

This proves that the system (
2.10
) has a unique solution that can be found for example by fixed point iteration or some other iterative procedure.

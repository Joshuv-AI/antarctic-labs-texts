Abstract
. We state and prove two gluing formulae for the heat kernel of the Laplacian on a Riemannian manifold of the form M 1 ∪ γ M 2 M_{1}\cup_{\gamma}M_{2} . We present several examples.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to investigate the behavior of the heat kernel of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a Riemannian manifold
M

\gamma\subset M
into two Riemannian manifolds with boundary
M_{1}
and

Namely, we obtain a formula (formula (

on p.

and relies on an intermediate gluing formula (

Our original motivation comes from perturbative quantum field theory, see Appendix

Given the ubiquitousness of the heat kernel, this formula is expected to have other applications. We envision that it could be useful, e.g., in the connection of the heat kernel to random walks, or lead to another proof of the Burghelea-Friedlander-Kappeler gluing formula for determinants

Plan of the paper

1.3.
Conventions

Unless stated otherwise, all Riemannian manifolds are assumed to be smooth, possibly with (smooth) boundary.

For Green’s functions and heat kernels, Dirichlet boundary condition is assumed by default.

For
M
a Riemannian manifold,
\int_{M}dx\cdots
denotes the integral with respect to Riemannian volume form on
M
.

Operators are denoted with superscripts according to which space they act on, e.g.
\Delta^{M}
denotes the Laplace operator on
M
, etc. When there is no danger of confusion, the superscript will sometimes be dropped. If the operator depends on extra parameters, those will be denoted with a subscript, and again this might be dropped when there is no danger of confusion.

2.1.
Laplace transform

The Laplace transform of a locally integrable function
f(t)
on
[0,\infty)

is the function
F(s)
of the complex parameter
s
defined by
1
1

1

Slightly more generally, one defines the Laplace transform of a measure
\mu
on
[0,\infty)
to be

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}e^{-st}d\mu.

Below we will sometimes use the point measure at 0 and denote it
\delta(t)dt
, its Laplace transform is the constant function
F(s)\equiv 1
.

F(s)=\mathbf{L}[f](s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-st}dt.

The function

is defined on the set
R_{f}=\{s\in\mathbb{C},\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)e^{-st}|dt<\infty\}
, which can be seen to be of the form
R_{f}=\operatorname{Re}(s)>a_{f}
or
R_{f}=\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq a_{f}
, for some

. The set

is called the region of (absolute) convergence and

is known as the abscissa of absolute convergence.

Conversely, if

is a holomorphic function of a complex parameter
s

, the inverse Laplace transform of

is the function

defined by

f(t)=\mathbf{L}^{-1}[F](t)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\gamma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\gamma+\mathrm{i}\infty}F(s)e^{st}ds

with
\gamma>a
. It is a classical fact that if
F(s)=\mathbf{L}[f](s)
and
\gamma>a_{f}
, then
\mathbf{L}^{-1}[F](t)=f(t)
.
An important property of the inverse Laplace transform is that it maps product to convolution,

(3)

\mathbf{L}^{-1}\left[\mathbf{L}[f](s)\cdot\mathbf{L}[g](s)\right](t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)g(t-\tau)d\tau.

2.3.
A duality between the heat kernel and the Green’s function for the Helmholtz operator

Denote by
K_{0}(x,y|t)
the heat kernel of
\Delta
, i.e. the integral kernel of
e^{-t\Delta}
. The proof of our gluing formula rests on the observation that the Laplace transform interchanges the heat kernel of the Laplacian, considered as function of
t\in[0,\infty)
, and the Green’s function
G_{m^{2}}
of the Helmholtz operator, considered as a holomorphic function in
m^{2}
. Namely, the heat kernel for the Helmholtz operator
\Delta+m^{2}
is

K_{m^{2}}(x,y|t)=e^{-m^{2}t}K_{0}(x,y|t).

For
x\neq y
fixed, the Green’s function of
\Delta+m^{2}
is given by

(6)

G_{m^{2}}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{m^{2}}(x,y|t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-m^{2}t}K_{0}(x,y|t)dt=\mathbf{L}[K_{0}(x,y)](m^{2}).

The convergence of this integral is uniform on compact subsets of
(M\times M)\setminus\Delta
, as the heat kernel
K_{m^{2}}
vanishes exponentially for
t\to 0
and
t\to\infty
on such subsets.
Therefore, the heat kernel is the inverse Laplace transform of the Green’s function

K_{0}(x,y|t)=\mathbf{L}^{-1}[G_{m^{2}}(x,y)](t).

We will henceforth suppress the subscript
0
in
K_{0}
and denote it simply
K

The assertions in this subsection, in contrast to those on the spectrum of

is not compact. The simplest example is

3.
A conjecture
on the bulk dependence of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator

For a single Riemannian manifold
M
with boundary
\gamma
, the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator can be split as

\mathrm{DN}^{\gamma,M}_{m^{2}}=\sqrt{\Delta^{\gamma}+m^{2}}+(\mathrm{DN}^{\gamma,M}_{m^{2}})^{\prime},

where
(\mathrm{DN}^{\gamma,M}_{m^{2}})^{\prime}
is a pseudo-differential operator of order at most 0, see
[
17
]
.
In particular, if
\gamma
is compact, then
(\mathrm{DN}^{\gamma,M}_{m^{2}})^{\prime}
is bounded by the Calderon-Vaillancourt theorem
[
6
]
. In dimension 2, one actually has that
(\mathrm{DN}^{\gamma,M}_{m^{2}})^{\prime}
is a pseudo-differential operator of order at most -2
[
15
, Proposition A.3]
.

Remark 3.1
.

The term
\sqrt{\Delta^{\gamma}+m^{2}}
is the DN operator for the semi-infinite cylinder
\gamma\times[0,+\infty)
. To see this, consider an eigenfunction

\eta
of
\Delta^{\gamma}+m^{2}
with eigenvalue
\lambda^{2},\lambda>0
. Then
e^{-\lambda t}\eta
is harmonic on
\gamma\times[0,\infty)
and

\mathrm{DN}_{m^{2}}^{\gamma,\gamma\times[0,\infty)}\eta=-\partial_{t}(e^{-t\lambda})\eta\big|_{t=0}=\lambda\eta=\sqrt{\Delta^{\gamma}+m^{2}}\;\eta.

It is easy to see all harmonic functions on the cylinder are of this kind and hence
\mathrm{DN}^{\gamma,\gamma\times[0,\infty)}_{m^{2}}=\sqrt{\Delta^{\gamma}+m^{2}}
.

This means that to leading order, the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator is only sensitive to the boundary geometry (it is given by the square root of the Helmholtz operator) but subleading orders depend on the bulk geometry.
7
7

7

In fact changing the metric away from the boundary changes the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator by a smoothing operator.
The precise nature of this dependence has been the subject of much recent research. To prove our gluing formula however we require a strong control over this dependence that we were unable to locate in the literature, namely the following statement:

Assumption A

We assume that:

For any

I.e. for very large

Appendix A
Motivation: Cutting and gluing in renormalized quantum field theory

Our main motivation comes from heat kernel renormalization of perturbative quantum field theory. Namely, consider the massive scalar field theory on a Riemannian manifold
M
: the space of fields is
F_{M}=C^{\infty}(M)
and the action functional is

S_{M}(\phi)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}d\phi\wedge*d\phi+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\phi*\phi+*p(\phi)

with
*
the Hodge star operator on
M
and
p(\phi)=\sum_{k=3}^{N}\frac{1}{k!}p_{k}\phi^{k}
a polynomial,
m>0
is a parameter (interpreted as mass). We are interested in the cutting and gluing behaviour of its perturbative partition function, given by

Z^{pert}_{M}(\hbar)\text{``=''}\lim_{\hbar\to 0}\int_{F_{M}}e^{-\frac{1}{\hbar}S_{M}(\phi)}D\phi:=\frac{1}{\det_{\zeta}^{1/2}(\Delta+m^{2})}\sum_{\Gamma}\frac{\hbar^{-\chi(\Gamma)}}{|\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)|}F(\Gamma).

Here in the right hand side

•

\Delta
denotes the positive Laplace operator on
M
and
\det_{\zeta}
the zeta-regularized determinant,

•

\Gamma
runs over all graphs which are at least trivalent and at most
N
-valent,

•

For a graph
\Gamma
:

–

\chi(\Gamma)=|V_{\Gamma}|-|E_{\Gamma}|\leq 0
is its Euler characteristic,

–

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
its automorphism group,

–

F(\Gamma)
denotes its Feynman weight, given by

(28)

F(\Gamma)=\int_{(x_{1},\ldots x_{|V_{\Gamma}|})\in M^{|V_{\Gamma}|}}\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}(-p_{val(v)})\prod_{e=(v_{i},v_{j})\in E(\Gamma)}G(x_{i},x_{j})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{j}.

Here
G\in C^{\infty}(M\times M\setminus\mathrm{diag})
denotes the Green’s function of the Helmholtz operator
\Delta+m^{2}
. Close to the diagonal, the Green’s function behaves like

G(x,y)\simeq_{d(x,y)\to 0}\begin{cases}O(1),&\dim M=1,\\
C_{2}\log d(x,y),&\dim M=2,\\
C_{\dim M}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{\dim M-2}},&\dim M>2,\\
\end{cases}

which means that some of the integrals given by (
28

\dim M>1
. In dimension 2, the only divergent integrals are the ones from graphs containing an edge starting and ending at the same vertex (a short loop) as it naively leads to the evaluation of
G
on the diagonal (where it is singluar). In
[
15
]
, we showed how to regularize these in such a way that the result is compatible with cutting and gluing of
M
. However, for
\dim M\geq 3
there are nontrivial divergent graphs and a variety of ways exist to make sense of them. For instance, if
K(x,y|t)
denotes the heat kernel of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta
, then a naive solution to the problem of divergencies is to introduce a regulator
\epsilon
and define

G^{\epsilon}(x,y)=\int_{\epsilon}^{\infty}e^{-m^{2}t}K(x,y|t)dt.

Then
G^{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(M\times M)
, and thus at least on compact manifolds the regularized integrals
F^{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
, where we replace
G
with
G^{\epsilon}
, are convergent. The next problem is to analyze the behaviour of the
F^{\epsilon}(\Gamma)
as
\epsilon\to 0
, trying to consistently remove the divergencies using a finite amount of choices – this leads to some restrictions on
\dim M
and the interaction polynomial
p
as to when this is possible.
12
12

12

A relatively recent write-up aimed a mathematical audience is the textbook by Costello
[
8
]
.

In
[
15
]
we proved a gluing formula for the perturbative partition function of the form

in the case where

A critical first step, covered in this paper, is to understand the behaviour of the heat kernel under gluing.

Appendix F
A gluing formula for the heat kernel on a graph

In this appendix, we prove a gluing formula for the heat kernel on a graph, and show that in this case the path integral formulae of the previous appendix obtain a precise meaning as path sum formulae. We will provide two proofs of the gluing formula: one by computing inverses of block matrices and one by counting paths.

F.1.
Generalities

Let

be a graph, which for simplicity we assume to be simple (i.e. no short loops or multiple edges) and finite. We write
Y\subset X
for a full subgraph

. In the following we will consider operators on the space

of 0-cochains on

with row and columns labeled by

, the entries of such a matrix

.

For example,

is the diagonal matrix with

be the adjacency matrix, with entries

if

and
A^{X}(u,v)=0

\Delta^{X}:=D^{X}-A^{X}\colon C^{0}(X)\to C^{0}(X)

The heat kernel on

F.3.2.
Discrete gluing formula II

We can also prove a version of our “second gluing formula,” Theorem

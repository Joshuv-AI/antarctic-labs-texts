Extragradient-Type Methods with 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right) Last-Iterate Convergence Rates for Co-Hypomonotone Inclusions
Quoc Tran-Dinh

Abstract
We develop two “Nesterov’s accelerated” variants of the well-known extragradient method to approximate a solution of a co-hypomonotone inclusion constituted by the sum of two operators, where one is Lipschitz continuous and the other is possibly multivalued. The first scheme can be viewed as an accelerated variant of Tseng’s forward-backward-forward splitting (FBFS) method, while the second one is a Nesterov’s accelerated variant of the “past” FBFS scheme, which requires only one evaluation of the Lipschitz operator and one resolvent of the multivalued mapping. Under appropriate conditions on the parameters, we theoretically prove that both algorithms achieve 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / k ) \mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right) last-iterate convergence rates on the residual norm, where k k is the iteration counter. Our results can be viewed as alternatives of a recent class of Halpern-type methods for root-finding problems. For comparison, we also provide a new convergence analysis of the two recent extra-anchored gradient-type methods for solving co-hypomonotone inclusions. Keywords: Accelerated extragradient method; Nesterov’s acceleration; co-hypomonotone inclusion; Halpern’s fixed-point iteration; last-iterate convergence rate Mathematics Subject Classification (2010): 90C25 · 90-08

Contribution.

In this paper, we propose new “Nesterov’s accelerated variants” of the forward-backward-forward splitting (FBFS) method
[
77
]
and its past FBFS variant.
The form of our methods is as simple as the one in
[
73
]
for equation root-finding problems.
Our first algorithm can be viewed as an
accelerated FBFS method
, but abbreviated by AEG as it is rooted from the extragradient method in
[
38
]
.
This algorithm achieves a
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
last-iterate convergence rate on the residual norm of (
chMI
).
However, it requires two evaluations of
F
and one evaluation of
J_{\eta T}
(the resolvent of
\eta T
for some
\eta>0
) as often seen in Tseng’s FBFS method
[
77
]
.
Our second variant is an
accelerated past FBFS scheme
(abbreviated by APEG), which adopts the idea from Popov’s method
[
65
]
or optimistic gradient-type algorithms
[
22
]
but using a different past operator value.
This algorithm has almost the same per-iteration complexity as of the forward-reflected-backward splitting method
[
52
]
or the reflected forward-backward splitting scheme
[
16
,
50
]
, but achieves a
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
last-iterate convergence rate on the residual norm of (
chMI
) as in our first algorithm – AEG.
However, as a compensation, APEG has a smaller stepsize and stricter condition, leading to a worse convergence upper bound than AEG.
Our rates are the same order as the ones in recent works
[
13
,
14
]
, but we take a different approach and develop different algorithms with new convergence analysis.
We believe that this paper is the first showing a possibility to develop Nesterov’s accelerated-type methods for nonmonotone (in particular, nonconvex optimization) problems by using different convergence criteria.
In the second part of this paper, we conduct a new convergence analysis for the two extra-anchored gradient-type methods for solving (
chMI
) proposed in
[
13
,
14
]
.
Our analysis can be viewed as a natural extension of the techniques for (
chME
) in
[
40
,
75
,
79
]
and is different from
[
13
,
14
]
.
Moreover, our second scheme is still different from the one in
[
14
]
.

2
Background and Preliminary Results

We first review some background on monotone operators and related concepts.
Then, we recall Tseng’s classical FBFS method and its past FBFS variant as the baselines of our development.

2.1
Monotone-type operators and related concepts

In this paper, we work with a finite dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{p}
equipped with the standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
.
For a single-valued or multivalued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
,
\mathrm{dom}(G)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:Gx\not=\emptyset\right\}
denotes its domain,
\mathrm{ran}(G):=\left\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:u\in Gx,~x\in\mathrm{dom}(G)\right\}
denotes its range,
\mathrm{gra}(G)=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in Gx\right\}
denotes its graph, where
2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
is the set of all subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
The inverse of
G
is defined by
G^{-1}y:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:y\in Gx\}
.

(a)
Monotonicity.

For a multivalued mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{p}\rightrightarrows 2^{\mathbb{R}^{p}}
, we say that
G
is
\mu
-monotone for some
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
if
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}(G)
.
If
\mu=0
, then
G
is called monotone, i.e.
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq 0
for all
(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}(G)
.
If
\mu>0
, then
G
is called strongly monotone with a strong monotonicity parameter
\mu
.
If
\mu<0
, then we say that
G
is
|\mu|
-hypomonotone or
|\mu|
-weakly monotone.
If
G
is single-valued, then the above condition reduces to
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq\mu\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, which defines the corresponding concepts for the single-valued mapping
G
.
We say that
G
is
\mu
-maximally monotone if
\mathrm{gra}(G)
is not properly contained in the graph of any other
\mu
-monotone operator.

(b)
Lipschitz continuity.

A single-valued operator
G
is called
L
-Lipschitz continuous if
\|Gx-Gy\|\leq L\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, where
L\geq 0
is a Lipschitz constant.
If
L=1
, then we say that
G
is nonexpansive, while if
L\in[0,1)
, then we say that
G
is
L
-contractive, and
L
is its contraction factor.
If
G
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, then by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have
\langle Gx-Gy,x-y\rangle\geq-\|Gx-Gy\|\|x-y\|\geq-L\|x-y\|^{2}
, showing that
G
is also
L
-hypomonotone.

(c)
Co-hypomonotonicity and co-coerciveness.

A mapping
G
is said to be
\rho
-co-hypomonotone for
\rho>0
if
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq-\rho\|u-v\|^{2}
for all
(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}(G)
.
The co-hypomonotonicity is also referred to as
-\rho
-co-monotonicity with a parameter
\rho
, see, e.g.,
[
6
,
19
]
.
Clearly, a co-hypomonotone operator is not necessarily monotone.
If
\rho=0
, then
G
is just monotone, while if
\rho<0
, then
G
is
\beta
-co-coercive with the parameter
\beta:=-\rho
.
If
\beta=1
, then we say that
G
is firmly nonexpansive.
If
G
is
\beta
-co-coercive, then it is also monotone and
\frac{1}{\beta}
-Lipschitz continuous (by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality), but the reversed statement is not true in general.

The co-hypomonotonicity concept was extended to a so-called
semimonotonicity
in
[
26
,
60
]
.
It is stronger than the weak Minty solution condition, e.g., in
[
25
]
, which only requires to hold at a given solution
x^{\star}
, i.e.
\langle u,x-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|u\|^{2}
for all
(x,u)\in\mathrm{gra}(G)
.
It is obvious that
G
is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone if and only if
G^{-1}
is
\rho
-hypomonotone, i.e.
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq-\rho\|u-v\|^{2}
for all
(u,x),(v,y)\in\mathrm{gra}(G^{-1})
.

The co-hymonotonicity is also related to a so-called “interaction dominance condition” studied in
[
32
]
.
Proposition 4.6 in
[
26
]
states that if a minimax objective function satisfies an interaction dominance condition, then its gradient is co-hypomonotone.
This structure presents in various minimax models
[
32
]
.

Another related concept is the
strong metric subregularity
(see
[
18
,
67
]
) widely studied in variational analysis, though it is often defined locally.
More specifically, a mapping
G
is said to be
strongly metrically subregular
at
x
for
u
when
u\in Gx
and there exists
\kappa>0
along with a neighborhood
\mathcal{X}
of
x
and
\mathcal{U}
of
u
such that

d(y,x)\leq\kappa\hat{d}(u,Gy\cap\mathcal{U}),\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{X},

where
d
and
\hat{d}
are given metrics.
Note that the [strong] metric subregularity is classical and relates to many other common concepts in variational analysis such as sharp minimizer, Aubin’s property, calmness, and error bound condition.

More concretely, if
G
is strongly metric subregular at any
x
and additionally
\mu
-hypomonotone (i.e.
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq-\mu\|x-y\|^{2}
for all
(x,u),(y,v)\in\mathrm{gra}(G)
.
If the metrics
d
and
\hat{d}
are the standard Euclidean distances, i.e.
d(y,x)=\|y-x\|
and
\hat{d}(u,v)=\|u-v\|
, then we get
\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq-\mu\|x-y\|^{2}\geq-\mu\kappa^{2}\|u-v\|^{2}
, showing that
G
is also
\mu\kappa^{2}
-co-hypomonotone.

Resolvent operator.

Given a possibly multivalued mapping
T
, the operator

J_{T}u:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:u\in x+Tx\right\}

is called the resolvent of
T
, often denoted by
J_{T}u=(\mathbb{I}+T)^{-1}u
, where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity mapping.
Clearly, evaluating
J_{T}
requires solving an inclusion
0\in y-x+Ty
in
y
for given
x
.
If
T
is monotone, then
J_{T}
is singled-valued, and if
T
is maximally monotone, then
J_{T}
is singled-valued and
\mathrm{dom}(J_{T})=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
If
T
is monotone, then
J_{T}
is also firmly nonexpansive
[
5
, Proposition 23.10]
, and hence nonexpansive.
Moreover
0\in Tx
if and only if
x
is a fixed-point of
J_{T}
.
If
T
is
\mu
-hypomonotone, then for
\mathbb{I}+\eta T
is also
(1-\eta\mu)
-strongly monotone, provided that
\eta\mu<1
.
In this case,
J_{\eta T}
remains single-valued and
(1-\eta\mu)
-co-coercive.

2.2
Preliminary results

Let us recall the following preliminary results, which will be used in the sequel.

Solution characterization.

Let us characterize solutions of (
chMI
).
Assume that for some
\eta>0
, the resolvent
J_{\eta T}=(\mathbb{I}+\eta T)^{-1}
associated with (
chMI
) exists and single-valued.
Let us define the following residual operator associated with (
chMI
):

\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x:=\tfrac{1}{\eta}\left(x-J_{\eta T}(x-\eta Fx)\right).

(1)

Then,
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F+T)
if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{\star}=0
.
Our goal is to approximate
x^{\star}
by
x^{k}
generated from a given algorithm after
k
iterations such that
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\varepsilon
for a given tolerance
\varepsilon>0
.
We also characterize the convergence rate of
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|
.

Another way of characterizing approximate solutions of (
chMI
) is to use a residual
r^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}
of (
chMI
) at
x^{k}
for some
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
.
Since
T
is multivalued, there may exist different residuals
r^{k}
at
x^{k}
.
Clearly, since
r^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
, if
\|r^{k}\|=0
, then
x^{k}
is an exact solution of (
chMI
).
Therefore, if
\|r^{k}\|\leq\varepsilon
for some tolerance
\varepsilon>0
, then
x^{k}
can be viewed as an
\varepsilon
-approximate solution of (
chMI
).
We call
r^{k}
a residual of (
chMI
) at
x^{k}
.
In this paper, we will upper bound the norm
\|r^{k}\|
of the residual
r^{k}
at
x^{k}
to characterize an approximate solution
x^{k}
.
If
x^{k}
is the last iterate computed by the algorithm, then we refer to the convergence rate on
\|r^{k}\|
as the last-iterate convergence rate.
If
x^{k}=x^{k_{b}}
with
k_{b}:=\mathrm{arg}\!\displaystyle\min\{\|r^{i}\|:0\leq i\leq k\}
, then we call the convergence rate on
\|r^{k_{b}}\|
the best-iterate convergence rate.

A brief review of extragradient-type methods.

The classical extragradient method
[
25
,
27
,
38
]
is often applied to solve monotone variational inequalities and their special cases.
To solve the inclusion (
chMI
), its modification was proposed by Tseng in
[
77
]
, which is known as a forward-backward-forward splitting (FBFS) method.
This scheme is described as follows:
Given
y^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k}&:=&J_{\eta T}(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}),\\
y^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-(\hat{\eta}Fx^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(FBFS)

where
\eta>0
and
\hat{\eta}>0
are given parameter.
Very often, we choose
\hat{\eta}=\eta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L}\right)
, where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
F
.
However, we can also choose
\hat{\eta}=\frac{\eta}{\beta}
for some
0<\beta\leq 1
as in
[
25
,
46
]
, which is called the extragradient plus (EG+) scheme.

Note that if we replace
Fy^{k}
by
Fx^{k-1}
in the extragradient method from
[
38
]
, then we obtain Popov’s past-extragradient method in
[
65
]
.
Similarly, if we replace
Fy^{k}
in (
FBFS
) by
Fx^{k-1}
, then we obtain the following past FBFS scheme:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k}&:=&J_{\eta T}(y^{k}-\eta Fx^{k-1}),\\
y^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-(\hat{\eta}Fx^{k}-\eta Fx^{k-1}),\end{array}\right.

(PFBFS)

where
x^{-1}:=y^{0}
is given,
\hat{\eta}:=\frac{\eta}{\beta}
for some
\beta\in(0,1]
, and
\eta\in\left(1,\frac{1}{3L}\right]
is a given parameter.
This method requires only one evaluation
Fx^{k}
of
F
and one resolvent
J_{\hat{\eta}T}
.
If we eliminate
y^{k}
and choose
\hat{\eta}=\eta
, then we obtain
\hat{x}^{k+1}=J_{\eta T}(\hat{x}^{k}-\eta(2F\hat{x}^{k}-F\hat{x}^{k-1}))
with
\hat{x}^{k}:=x^{k-1}
, which is exactly the forward-reflected-backward splitting scheme in
[
52
]
.
While the asymptotic convergence and convergence rates of both (
FBFS
) and (
PFBFS
) under the monotonicity of
F
are classical, the best-iterate convergence rates of these methods under the co-hypomonotonicity of
F
can be found, e.g., in
[
46
]
.
Note that (
PFBFS
) is different from the reflected forward-backward splitting method
x^{k+1}:=J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta F(2x^{k}-x^{k-1}))
in
[
16
]
(rooted from the projected reflected gradient method in
[
50
]
) or the golden ratio method in
[
51
]
for solving monotone variational inequality, a special case of (
chMI
).

3
Nesterov’s Accelerated FBFS Method for Solving (
chMI
)

Our goal in this section is to develop an accelerated variant of (
FBFS
) to solve (
chMI
) using Nesterov’s acceleration techniques
[
58
]
.
Motivated from recent works
[
13
,
14
,
24
,
40
,
75
,
79
]
on Halpern’s fixed-point iteration with “optimal” convergence rates on the norm of residual of (
chMI
), in this section we exploit the idea from
[
73
]
to develop an alternative “Nesterov’s accelerated” variant of the FBFS method (
FBFS
) to solve (
chMI
).

3.1
Algorithmic derivation

Our starting point is Nesterov’s acceleration interpretation of (
FBFS
) to solve (
chME
) from
[
73
]
.
Here, we extend this variant to solve the inclusion (
chMI
), which is presented as follows:
Starting from an initial point
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, set
y^{0}:=x^{0}
and
z^{0}:=x^{0}
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}z^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\gamma(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}),\\
y^{k+1}&:=&z^{k+1}+\theta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})+\nu_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1}),\\
x^{k+1}&:=&y^{k+1}-\eta(Fy^{k+1}+\xi^{k+1})+\hat{\eta}_{k+1}(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(2)

where
\gamma
,
\eta
,
\hat{\eta}_{k+1}
,
\theta_{k}
and
\nu_{k}
are given parameters, which will be determined later, and
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
is an arbitrary element of
Tx^{k}
.
Clearly, if
T=0
, then (
2
) exactly reduces to the one in
[
73
]
.

Since
\xi^{k+1}\in Tx^{k+1}
appears on the right-hand side of the last line of (
2
), by conceptually using the resolvent
J_{\eta T}=(\mathbb{I}+\eta T)^{-1}
of
\eta T
, we can transform this scheme into an equivalent form that is more convenient for implementation.
First, we switch the first and last lines of (
2
) and shift the index from
k+1
to
k
as

x^{k}:=y^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}+\xi^{k})+\hat{\eta}_{k}(Fx^{k-1}+\xi^{k-1}).

Next, for simplicity of presentation, we introduce
w^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}
.
Then, from the last expression of
x^{k}
, we have
y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}=x^{k}+\eta\xi^{k}\in(\mathbb{I}+\eta T)x^{k}
.
Hence, we obtain
x^{k}=J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}\big)
provided that
J_{\eta T}
is single-valued.
In addition, we also have
x^{k}=y^{k}-\eta(Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}+w^{k})+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}
, leading to
w^{k}=\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1})+Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}
.
Finally, putting these derivations together, we obtain the following equivalent interpretation of (
2
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x^{k}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}:=}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}\big),}\\
w^{k}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1})+Fx^{k}-Fy^{k},\\
z^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\gamma w^{k},\\
y^{k+1}&:=&z^{k+1}+\theta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})+\nu_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1}),\end{array}\right.

(AEG)

where we choose
w^{-1}:=\frac{\eta}{\hat{\eta}_{0}}w^{0}=\frac{\eta}{\hat{\eta}_{0}}(Fx^{0}+\xi^{0})
for
\xi^{0}\in Tx^{0}
, and set
z^{0}=y^{0}:=x^{0}
for a given initial point
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
We call this scheme an
accelerated FBFS method
to distinguish it from the extra-anchored gradient method in
[
79
]
(see also Section
5
).

If
J_{\eta T}
is computable, then (
AEG
) is implementable. Nevertheless, we have not yet obviously seen the connection between (
AEG
) and (
FBFS
).
Now, let us eliminate
z^{k}
to obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x^{k}}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}:=}&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}{\ +\ \hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}}\big),}\\
w^{k}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1})+Fx^{k}-Fy^{k},\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}y^{k+1}}&:=&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}x^{k}+\theta_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1})-\beta_{k}w^{k}+\sigma_{k}w^{k-1}-\eta\nu_{k}(Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}),}\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
\beta_{k}:=\gamma(1+\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})-\eta\nu_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}:=\gamma\theta_{k}-\nu_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}
.
We can view (
3
) as an accelerated variant of (
FBFS
), where a momentum term
{\theta_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1})}
and other simple correction terms are added to (
FBFS
) as observed in
[
48
]
.

Overall, at each iteration
k
, (
AEG
) requires one evaluation of the resolvent
J_{\eta T}
of
\eta T
and two evaluations
Fx^{k}
and
Fy^{k}
of
F
.
This per-iteration complexity is essentially the same as (
FBFS
).
When
T=0
, as indicated in
[
73
]
, (
2
) can be derived from the Halpern-type schemes developed in
[
40
,
79
]
.
The convergence rates of these Halpern-type schemes were proven in
[
40
,
79
]
for both the monotone and co-hypomonotone cases when
T=0
.
When
T\neq 0
,
[
13
,
14
]
recently proposed corresponding Halpern-type schemes and analyzed their convergence rates for both the monotone and co-hypomonotone cases.

Remark 3.1
.

We can replace the standard resolvent
J_{\eta T}:=(\mathbb{I}+\eta T)^{-1}
by its generalization
J_{\eta T}:=(D+\eta T)^{-1}\circ D
, where
D
is a symmetric and positive semidefinite operator.
In addition, our methods and their convergence results on
\|Fx^{k}+v^{k}\|
remain valid if
J_{\eta T}
is a multivalued mapping and satisfies
\mathrm{ran}(J_{\eta T})\subseteq\mathrm{dom}(F)=\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathrm{dom}(J_{\eta T})=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
These conditions guarantee the well-definedness of (
AEG
).

3.2
Convergence analysis

To establish the convergence of (
AEG
), we use the following potential or Lyapunov function from
[
73
]
:

\mathcal{P}_{k}:=a_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k-1},z^{k}-y^{k}\rangle+\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}\|^{2},

(4)

where
a_{k}>0
,
b_{k}>0
, and
t_{k}>0
are given parameters.
Note that
[
73
]
did not provide convergence analysis for (
AEG
) in the co-hypomonotone case even when
T=0
.
It only established the convergence rate of a Nesterov’s accelerated variant derived from the extra-anchored gradient method in
[
79
]
.
Here, we reuse this potential function to analyze the convergence of (
AEG
) for solving (
chMI
) in the co-hypomonotone setting, which obviously covers the monotone case.

Let us first lower bound the potential function
\mathcal{P}_{k}
as in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k},z^{k},w^{k})\}
be generated by (
AEG
) and
\mathcal{P}_{k}
be defined by (
4
).
Then,
w^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
.
If
J_{\eta T}
is single-valued and nonexpansive for any
\eta>0
, then for
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
defined by (
1
), we have

\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\|w^{k}\|.

(5)

If
F+T
is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone, then

\mathcal{P}_{k}\geq\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-\tfrac{b_{k}}{2t_{k}}w^{k-1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\left(a_{k}-\tfrac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\tfrac{(\gamma+\rho)b_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}.

(6)

Consequently, if
a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{(\gamma+\rho)b_{k}}{t_{k}}\geq 0
and
J_{\eta T}
is single-valued and nonexpansive, then we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{k}\geq\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{(\gamma+\rho)b_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k-1}\|^{2}.

(7)

Proof.

From the first line of (
AEG
), we have
y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}-\eta Fy^{k}\in\eta Tx^{k}
.
Rearranging this expression, inserting
Fx^{k}
to both sides, and using
w^{k}
from the second line of (
AEG
), we have
w^{k}=\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1})-(Fy^{k}-Fx^{k})\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
.
This proves that
w^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
.

From
w^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
, we get
x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k}+\eta w^{k}\in x^{k}+\eta Tx^{k}
, leading to
x^{k}=J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k}+\eta w^{k})
due to the single-valued assumption of
J_{\eta T}
.
Using this relation, the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
from (
1
), and the nonexpansiveness of
J_{\eta T}
from our assumption, we have

\eta\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|=\|x^{k}-J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k})\|=\|J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k}+\eta w^{k})-J_{\eta T}(x^{k}-\eta Fx^{k})\|\leq\eta\|w^{k}\|.

This clearly implies that
\|G_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\|w^{k}\|
as in (
5
).

Next, utilizing
z^{k}=x^{k-1}-\gamma w^{k-1}
from the third line of (
AEG
), and the
\rho
-co-hypomonotonicity of
F+T
with
\langle w^{k-1},x^{k-1}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}&=&\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}-\frac{b_{k}}{2t_{k}}w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle w^{k-1},x^{k-1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&\geq&\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-\frac{b_{k}}{2t_{k}}w^{k-1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{(\gamma+\rho)b_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

This is exactly (
6
).
Combining (
6
) and
\|G_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\|w^{k}\|
, we obtain (
7
).

\square
∎

Our first main result is the following theorem showing the convergence rate of the
accelerated FBFS
scheme (
AEG
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
F+T
in (
chMI
) is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone,
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous such that
2L\rho<1
, and
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F+T)
is an arbitrary solution of (
chMI
).
Let
\gamma>0
be such that
L(2\rho+\gamma)\leq 1

(
e.g.,
\gamma:=\frac{1}{L}-2\rho>0
)
and
\{(x^{k},y^{k},z^{k})\}
be generated by (
AEG
) using the following update rules:

t_{k}:=k+2,\quad\eta:=\gamma+2\rho,\quad\hat{\eta}_{k}=\frac{(t_{k}-1)\eta}{t_{k}},\quad\theta_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\nu_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}.

(8)

Then, the following bound holds:

\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+8\gamma(3\gamma+2\rho)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(k+2)^{2}},\quad\text{where}\quad\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}.

(9)

If
J_{\eta T}
is nonexpansive, then for
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
defined by (
1
), we also have

\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+8\gamma(3\gamma+2\rho)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(k+2)^{2}}.

(10)

Theorem
3.1
provides an alternative view of the “accelerated” FBFS method based on Nesterov’s accelerated interpretation
[
73
]
.
Our condition
2L\rho<1
is the same as in
[
13
,
40
]
for the Halpern-type variants of (
FBFS
).
Note that the estimate (
9
) only requires the
\rho
-co-hypomonotonicity of
F+T
and the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
, while (
10
) additionally requires the nonexpansiveness of
J_{\eta T}
.
If
\rho=0
, i.e.
F+T
is monotone, then our condition on the stepsize
\gamma
becomes
L\gamma<1
, which is used in the clasical extragradient and FBFS methods, e.g., in
[
38
]
.
If
\rho<0
, i.e.
F+T
is
-\rho
-co-coercive, then our bounds remain valid as long as
-1<L(\gamma+2\rho)\leq 1
(or
\max\left\{-\frac{1}{L}-2\rho,0\right\}<\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L}-2\rho)
.
However, we may expect a linear convergence rate instead of a sublinear rate as in Theorem
3.1
.
This requires to adapt our analysis by using the techniques, e.g., in
[
61
]
, which is beyond the scope of this paper.

Note that we can choose different
\eta
such that
L\eta\leq 1
, but not necessary to be
\eta=\gamma+2\rho
as in (
8
).
The update rule (
8
) in Theorem
3.1
only provides one choice of
\eta
and it makes our analysis simpler.
However, other choices of
\eta
and
\gamma
can also work as long as they satisfy the conditions in our analysis below.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
.

First, by inserting
(t_{k}-1)z^{k+1}-(t_{k}-1)z^{k+1}
, we can easily expand
\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}\|^{2}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}\|^{2}&=&\|z^{k+1}-x^{\star}+(t_{k}-1)(z^{k+1}-z^{k})+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1})\|^{2}\\
&=&\|z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+(t_{k}-1)^{2}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|^{2}+t_{k}^{2}\|y^{k}-z^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-1)\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2t_{k}\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-1)t_{k}\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Next, from the fourth line of (
AEG
), we have
y^{k+1}-z^{k+1}=\theta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})+\nu_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1})
.
Using this line, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\|z^{k+1}+t_{k+1}(y^{k+1}-z^{k+1})-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&=&\|z^{k+1}-x^{\star}+t_{k+1}[\theta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})+\nu_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1})]\|^{2}\\
&=&\|z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|^{2}+t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}\|y^{k}-z^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2t_{k+1}\theta_{k}\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle+2t_{k+1}\nu_{k}\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2t_{k+1}^{2}\nu_{k}\theta_{k}\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Combining the last two expressions, we can easily get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[2]}&:=&\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}\|^{2}-\|z^{k+1}+t_{k+1}(y^{k+1}-z^{k+1})-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&=&\left[(t_{k}-1)^{2}-t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}\right]\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|^{2}+(t_{k}^{2}-\nu_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|y^{k}-z^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-1-t_{k+1}\theta_{k})\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k})\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\left[t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-\theta_{k}\nu_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}\right]\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Using the above expression
\mathcal{T}_{[2]}
, the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{k}
from (
4
), and the fourth line of (
AEG
) as
z^{k+1}-y^{k+1}=-\theta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})-\nu_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1})
, we can further derive

\hskip-4.30554pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[(t_{k}-1)^{2}-t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}^{2}\right]\|z^{k+1}-z^{k}\|^{2}+(t_{k}^{2}-\nu_{k}^{2}t_{k+1}^{2})\|y^{k}-z^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle+b_{k+1}\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},y^{k}-z^{k+1}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\left(b_{k+1}\theta_{k}-b_{k}\right)\big[\langle w^{k},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle+\langle w^{k-1},y^{k}-z^{k+1}\rangle\big]\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-1-t_{k+1}\theta_{k})\langle z^{k+1}-z^{k},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2(t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k})\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&&+{~}2\left[t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-t_{k+1}^{2}\theta_{k}\nu_{k}\right]\langle y^{k}-z^{k+1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-17.22217pt

(11)

Let us choose positive parameters
t_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
,
\theta_{k}
, and
b_{k}
such that

\begin{array}[]{lclclcl}t_{k}-t_{k+1}\nu_{k}&=&0,&&t_{k}(t_{k}-1)-\nu_{k}\theta_{k}t_{k+1}^{2}&=&0,\\
t_{k}-1-t_{k+1}\theta_{k}&=&0,&\text{and}&b_{k+1}\theta_{k}-b_{k}&=&0.\end{array}

(12)

These conditions lead to the update of
\theta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
, and
b_{k}
as
\theta_{k}=\frac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}}
,
\nu_{k}=\frac{t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}
, and
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{\theta_{k}}=\frac{b_{k}t_{k+1}}{t_{k}-1}
, respectively.
Clearly, these updates of
\nu_{k}
and
\theta_{k}
are exactly shown in (
8
).

Now, under the choice of parameters as in (
12
), (
11
) reduces to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|w^{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle\\
&&+{~}b_{k+1}\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},y^{k}-z^{k+1}\rangle.\end{array}

(13)

As shown in Lemma
3.1
, we have
w^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
.
By the
\rho
-co-hypomonotonicity of
F+T
and
z^{k+1}=x^{k}-\gamma w^{k}
from the third line of (
AEG
), we have
\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle=\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle-\gamma\|w^{k}-w^{k-1}\|^{2}\geq-(\rho+\gamma)\|w^{k}-w^{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Therefore, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle&\geq&-(\gamma+\rho)\left[\|w^{k}\|^{2}+\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-2\langle w^{k},w^{k-1}\rangle\right].\end{array}

(14)

For our convenience, let us denote
\hat{w}^{k}:=w^{k}+Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}
.
Then, it is obvious that
Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}=\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}
.
From the second line of (
AEG
) and
Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}=\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}
, we have
x^{k}-y^{k}=\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}-\eta\hat{w}^{k}
.
Substituting this into the third line of (
AEG
), we get
y^{k}-z^{k+1}=\gamma w^{k}+\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}
.
Using this expression, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},y^{k}-z^{k+1}\rangle&=&\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},\gamma w^{k}+\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}\rangle\\
&=&\nu_{k}\gamma\|w^{k}\|^{2}+\theta_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\nu_{k}\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}\eta\theta_{k}\langle w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle-(\nu_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}+\gamma\theta_{k})\langle w^{k-1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(15)

Substituting (
14
) and (
15
) into (
13
), and utilizing
b_{k}=b_{k+1}\theta_{k}
, we can show that

\hskip-4.30554pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[a_{k}+b_{k}\left(\hat{\eta}_{k}-\gamma-\rho\right)\right]\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left[b_{k}\left(\frac{\gamma\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\gamma-\rho\right)-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\eta b_{k}\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\right)\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle+\eta b_{k}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}b_{k}\left(\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\gamma-2\rho\right)\langle w^{k-1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-25.83325pt

(16)

Now, using the Lipschitz continuity of
F
and the relations
Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}=\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}
and
x^{k}-y^{k}=\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}-\eta\hat{w}^{k}
as above, we have
\|\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}\|^{2}=\|Fy^{k}-Fx^{k}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}=L^{2}\|\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}\|^{2}
.
This inequality leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&\geq&\|w^{k}\|^{2}+(1-L^{2}\eta^{2})\|\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-2\left(1-L^{2}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}\right)\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}2L^{2}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle-L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{2L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}}
and adding the result to (
16
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[a_{k}+\frac{b_{k}}{2}\left(\hat{\eta}_{k}-2\gamma-2\rho\right)\right]\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}(1-L^{2}\eta^{2})}{2L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\left(\frac{\gamma\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}+\frac{1}{2L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}}-\gamma-\rho\right)-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\left(\frac{\eta\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-\frac{1}{L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}}\right)\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle-b_{k}(\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-2\rho-\gamma)\langle w^{k-1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(17)

Let us choose
\eta:=\gamma+2\rho
and
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{\eta\theta_{k}}{\nu_{k}}=\frac{\eta(t_{k}-1)}{t_{k}}
as in (
8
).
Then, using
\nu_{k}=\frac{t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}
,
\theta_{k}=\frac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}}
,
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}t_{k+1}}{t_{k}-1}
, and
\eta:=\gamma+2\rho
, we can simplify (
17
) as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}(\gamma t_{k}+\eta)}{2t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left[\frac{b_{k}\left(\gamma t_{k}+\gamma+\eta\right)}{2(t_{k}-1)}-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{b_{k}(1-L^{2}\eta^{2})t_{k}}{2L^{2}\eta(t_{k}-1)}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(18)

Next, we impose the following conditions:

1-L^{2}\eta^{2}\geq 0,\quad a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}(\gamma t_{k}+\eta)}{2t_{k}}\geq 0,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{b_{k}\left(\gamma t_{k}+\gamma+\eta\right)}{2(t_{k}-1)}-a_{k+1}\geq 0.

(19)

Then, under the conditions of (
19
), (
18
) reduces to
\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k+1}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
.

We need to show that with the update rules as in (
8
), three conditions of (
19
) hold.
Indeed, the first condition of (
19
) is equivalent to
L\eta=L(\gamma+2\rho)\leq 1
.
If
2L\rho<1
, then we can always choose
0<\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L}-2\rho
such that
1-L^{2}\eta^{2}\geq 0
.
This is our first condition in Theorem
9
.

Now, let us choose
a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}(\gamma t_{k}+\eta)}{2t_{k}}
.
Then, the second condition of (
19
) automatically holds.
The third condition of (
19
) becomes
a_{k+1}\leq\frac{b_{k+1}(\gamma t_{k+1}+\eta)}{2t_{k+1}}
due to
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}t_{k+1}}{t_{k}-1}
and
t_{k}=k+2
.
By the choice of
a_{k}
, this condition holds with equality.
For
t_{k}:=k+2
, we can easily obtain
b_{k}:=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)}{2}
for some
b_{0}>0
, and consequently,
a_{k}=\frac{b_{k}(\gamma t_{k}+\eta)}{2t_{k}}=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)(\gamma k+3\gamma+2\rho)}{4}
.

Finally, since
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}\leq\mathcal{P}_{k}
, by induction we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{k}&\leq&\mathcal{P}_{0}=a_{0}\|w^{-1}\|^{2}+b_{0}\langle w^{-1},z^{0}-y^{0}\rangle+\|z^{0}+t_{0}(y^{0}-z^{0})-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&=&4a_{0}\|w^{0}\|^{2}+\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}=b_{0}(3\gamma+2\rho)\|w^{0}\|^{2}+\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2},\end{array}

due to
z^{0}=y^{0}=x^{0}
and
w^{-1}=\frac{\eta}{\hat{\eta}_{0}}w^{0}=2w^{0}
.
Combining this expression and (
6
), and then using the expressions of
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{P}_{0}&=&\frac{b_{0}(3\gamma+2\rho)}{2}\|w^{0}\|^{2}+\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\mathcal{P}_{k}\geq\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{(\gamma+\rho)b_{k}}{t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}=\frac{b_{0}(4\gamma-b_{0})(k+1)^{2}}{16}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

If we choose
b_{0}:=2\gamma
, then the last estimate leads to
\|w^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(k+2)^{2}}+\frac{8(3\gamma+2\rho)}{\gamma(k+2)^{2}}\|w^{0}\|^{2}
, which is exactly (
9
) since
w^{0}:=Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}
.
If
J_{\eta T}
is nonexpansive, then by (
5
), we obtain (
10
).

\square
∎

4
Nesterov’s Accelerated Past-FBFS Method for Solving (
chMI
)

Alternative to Section
3
, in this section, we develop a new Nesterov’s accelerated variant of the FBFS method (
FBFS
) for solving (
chMI
) by adopting the ideas from
[
65
,
77
]
.
However, our scheme is different from (
PFBFS
) since it replaces
Fx^{k}
by
Fy^{k-1}
instead of replacing
Fy^{k}
by
Fx^{k-1}
.

4.1
Algorithmic derivation

Since (
AEG
) requires two evaluations
Fx^{k}
and
Fy^{k}
of
F
at each iteration as in (
FBFS
), we reduce its per-iteration complexity by replacing
Fx^{k}
by
Fy^{k-1}
computed from the previous iteration.
Rearranging the resulting scheme and using the resolvent of
\eta T
, we obtain the following new scheme:
Given
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, set
z^{0}=y^{0}:=x^{0}
and
\hat{w}^{-1}:=\frac{\eta}{\hat{\eta}_{0}}w^{0}\in\frac{\eta}{\hat{\eta}_{0}}(Fx^{0}+Tx^{0})
, at each iteration
k
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k}&:=&J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}\big),\\
\hat{w}^{k}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}),\\
z^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}-\gamma\hat{w}^{k},\\
y^{k+1}&:=&z^{k+1}+\theta_{k}(z^{k+1}-z^{k})+\nu_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k+1}),\end{array}\right.

(APEG)

where
\theta_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
,
\eta
, and
\gamma
are given parameters, which will be determined later.

Note that the idea of replacing either
Fx^{k}
or
Fy^{k}
by a past value was proposed by Popov in
[
65
]
for the classical extragradient method, and then extended to FBFS variants as seen from (
PFBFS
).
However, unlike (
PFBFS
), we replace
Fx^{k}
by
Fy^{k-1}
, leading to (
APEG
).
Clearly, at each iteration
k
, (
APEG
) only requires one evaluation of
J_{\eta T}
and one evaluation
Fy^{k}
of
F
compared to (
AEG
).

If we eliminate
z^{k}
from (
APEG
), then we obtain the following scheme:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k}&:=&J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}\big),\\
\hat{w}^{k}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}),\\
y^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}+\theta_{k}(x^{k}-x^{k-1})-\beta_{k}\hat{w}^{k}+\sigma_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1},\end{array}\right.

(20)

where
\beta_{k}:=\gamma(1+\theta_{k}-\nu_{k})-\eta\nu_{k}
and
\sigma_{k}:=\gamma\theta_{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\nu_{k}
.
This representation uses both
\hat{w}^{k}
and
\hat{w}^{k-1}
to update
y^{k+1}
.
However, since (
20
) uses
Fy^{k-1}
instead of
Fx^{k-1}
, it is different from (
PFBFS
) as well as the reflected-forward-backward splitting method in
[
16
,
50
]
.

4.2
Convergence analysis

To form a potential function for (
APEG
), starting from the first and second lines of (
APEG
), we have
\hat{w}^{k}-Fy^{k}=\frac{1}{\eta}(y^{k}-x^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1})-Fy^{k}\in Tx^{k}
.
Hence, if we define
w^{k}:=\hat{w}^{k}+Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}
, then
w^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
.
Therefore, we propose to use the following potential or Lyapunov function to analyze the convergence of (
APEG
), which is similar to
\mathcal{P}_{k}
in (
4
):

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}&:=&a_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k-1},z^{k}-y^{k}\rangle+\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}c_{k}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(21)

where
a_{k}>0
,
b_{k}>0
,
c_{k}>0
, and
t_{k}>0
are given parameters, determined later.

Similar to the proof of (
6
), using
z^{k}=x^{k-1}-\gamma\hat{w}^{k-1}
from the third line of (
APEG
), the
\rho
-co-hypomonotonicity of
F+T
as
\langle w^{k-1},x^{k-1}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}
, and Young’s inequality as
-\langle w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k-1}\rangle=-\langle w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k-1}-w^{k-1}\rangle-\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}\geq-\frac{3}{2}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}
, we can lower bound
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}&=&\|z^{k}+t_{k}(y^{k}-z^{k})-x^{\star}-\frac{b_{k}}{2t_{k}}w^{k-1}\|^{2}+c_{k}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle w^{k-1},x^{k-1}-x^{\star}\rangle-\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{t_{k}}\langle w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k-1}\rangle\\
&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{(3\gamma+2\rho)b_{k}}{2t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(c_{k}-\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{2t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(22)

Now, we are ready to establish the convergence of
APEG
in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
F+T
in (
chMI
) is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone,
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous such that
8\sqrt{3}L\rho<1
, and
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F+T)
is an arbitrary solution of (
chMI
).
Let
\gamma>0
be such that
16L^{2}\big[3(3\gamma+2\rho)^{2}+\gamma(2\gamma+\rho)\big]\leq 1
, which always exists, and
\{(x^{k},y^{k},z^{k})\}
be generated by (
APEG
) using

t_{k}:=k+2,\ \ \eta:=2(3\gamma+2\rho),\ \ \hat{\eta}_{k}=\frac{(t_{k}-1)\eta}{t_{k}},\ \ \theta_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}-1}{t_{k+1}},\ \text{and}\ \ \nu_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}}{t_{k+1}}.

(23)

Then, for
k\geq 0
, the following bound holds:

\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+8\gamma(5\gamma+2\rho)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(k+2)(k+4)},\quad\text{where}\quad\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}.

(24)

Consequently, if
J_{\eta T}
is nonexpansive, then for
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
defined by (
1
), we have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+8\gamma(5\gamma+2\rho)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(k+2)(k+4)}.}

(25)

Remark 4.1

(
Choice of
\gamma
)
.

If
8\sqrt{3}L\rho<1
, then
\bar{\gamma}:=\frac{\sqrt{29-92L^{2}\rho^{2}}-74L\rho}{116L}>0
.
In this case, if we choose
\gamma\in(0,\bar{\gamma}]
, then the condition
16L^{2}[3(3\gamma+2\rho)^{2}+\gamma(2\gamma+\rho)]\leq 1
in Theorem
4.1
holds.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Similar to the proof of (
13
) in Theorem
3.1
, using the last line of (
APEG
), the definition (
21
) of
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
, and the update rules (
23
), we can show that

\hskip-8.61108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|w^{k}\|^{2}+c_{k}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}-c_{k+1}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle+b_{k+1}\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},y^{k}-z^{k+1}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(26)

By the
\rho
-co-hypomonotonicity of
F+T
, we have
\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle\geq-\rho\|w^{k}-w^{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Using this expression and
z^{k+1}=x^{k}-\gamma\hat{w}^{k}=x^{k}-\gamma w^{k}+\gamma(w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k})
from the third line of (
APEG
) and both the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities, we can derive that

\hskip-0.86108pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},z^{k+1}-z^{k}\rangle&=&\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},x^{k}-x^{k-1}\rangle-\gamma\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\rangle\\
&\geq&\gamma\langle w^{k}-w^{k-1},(w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k})-(w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1})\rangle\\
&&-{~}(\gamma+\rho)\|w^{k}-w^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&-\left(2\gamma+\rho\right)\|w^{k}-w^{k-1}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\gamma}{2}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-17.22217pt

(27)

Alternatively, from the second lines of (
APEG
), we have
x^{k}=y^{k}-\eta\hat{w}^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}
.
Combining this expression and the third line of (
APEG
), we get
y^{k}-z^{k+1}=(\gamma+\eta)\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}=\gamma w^{k}+\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}+\gamma(\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k})-\hat{\eta}_{k}(\hat{w}^{k-1}-w^{k-1})
.
Utilizing this expression, and both the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities again, for any
\beta>0
, we can show that

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[3]}&:=&\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},y^{k}-z^{k+1}\rangle\\
&=&\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},\gamma w^{k}+\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\langle\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1},\gamma(\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k})-\hat{\eta}_{k}(\hat{w}^{k-1}-w^{k-1})\rangle\\
&\geq&\gamma\nu_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\theta_{k}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-(\hat{\eta}_{k}\nu_{k}+\gamma\theta_{k})\langle w^{k},w^{k-1}\rangle\\
&&-{~}\eta\theta_{k}\langle\hat{w}^{k},w^{k-1}\rangle+\eta\nu_{k}\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle-\frac{\beta}{2\nu_{k}}\|\nu_{k}w^{k}-\theta_{k}w^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\gamma^{2}\nu_{k}}{\beta}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\nu_{k}}{\beta}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(28)

Further expanding (
27
) and (
28
), and then substituting their results into (
26
), and using
b_{k+1}\theta_{k}=b_{k}
from (
12
), we can derive

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[c_{k}-b_{k}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\nu_{k}}{\beta\theta_{k}}\right)\right]\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\left[c_{k+1}+b_{k}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}\nu_{k}}{\beta\theta_{k}}\right)\right]\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[a_{k}-b_{k}\left(2\gamma+\rho+\frac{\beta\theta_{k}}{2\nu_{k}}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\right)\right]\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\left(\frac{(2\gamma-\beta)\nu_{k}}{2\theta_{k}}-2\gamma-\rho\right)-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\left(3\gamma+2\rho+\beta-\frac{\nu_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\right)\langle w^{k},w^{k-1}\rangle+\eta b_{k}\langle\hat{w}^{k},w^{k}-w^{k-1}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\eta b_{k}\left(\frac{\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}-1\right)\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip 0.0pt

(29)

Next, by the Lipschitz continuity of
F
,
w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}=Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}
, and
y^{k}-x^{k}=\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}
, we can show that
\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}=\|Fx^{k}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x^{k}-y^{k}\|^{2}=L^{2}\|\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Therefore, for any
\omega>0
, using Young’s inequality, this expression leads to
0\geq\omega\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}+\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-2(1+\omega)L^{2}\|\eta\hat{w}^{k}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k-1}\|^{2}-2(1+\omega)L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Denoting
M:=2(1+\omega)L^{2}
and expanding the last inequality, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&\geq&\omega\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}+\|w^{k}\|^{2}+(1-M\eta^{2})\|\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-2(1-M\eta\hat{\eta}_{k})\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}2M\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}\langle\hat{w}^{k},w^{k}-w^{k-1}\rangle-M\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}-M\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{b_{k}}{2M\hat{\eta}_{k}}
and adding the result to (
29
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[c_{k}-b_{k}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\nu_{k}}{\beta\theta_{k}}+\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}}{2}\right)\right]\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\left(\frac{\omega}{2M\hat{\eta}_{k}}-\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{\gamma^{2}\nu_{k}}{\beta\theta_{k}}\right)-c_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[a_{k}-b_{k}\left(2\gamma+\rho+\frac{\beta\theta_{k}}{2\nu_{k}}-\frac{\hat{\eta}_{k}}{2}\right)\right]\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{(1-M\eta^{2})b_{k}}{2M\hat{\eta}_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\left(\frac{1}{2M\hat{\eta}_{k}}+\frac{(2\gamma-\beta)\nu_{k}}{2\theta_{k}}-2\gamma-\rho\right)-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}b_{k}\left(3\gamma+2\rho+\beta-\frac{\nu_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\right)\langle w^{k},w^{k-1}\rangle-b_{k}\left(\frac{1}{M\hat{\eta}_{k}}-\frac{\eta\nu_{k}}{\theta_{k}}\right)\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Let us choose
\beta:=3\gamma+2\rho
, and
\eta:=2(3\gamma+2\rho)=2\beta
and
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=\frac{\eta\theta_{k}}{\nu_{k}}
.
Then, we have
3\gamma+2\rho+\beta-\frac{\nu_{k}\hat{\eta}_{k}}{\theta_{k}}=0
.
In this case, we can simplify the last expression as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[c_{k}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}\big(\gamma+\frac{5\eta\theta_{k}}{\nu_{k}}\big)\right]\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[\frac{b_{k}}{2\theta_{k}}\big(\frac{(\omega-4M\gamma^{2})\nu_{k}}{M\eta}-\gamma\theta_{k}\big)-c_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[a_{k}-b_{k}\big(2\gamma+\rho-\frac{\eta\theta_{k}}{4\nu_{k}}\big)\right]\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{(1-M\eta^{2})b_{k}\nu_{k}}{2M\eta\theta_{k}}\left(\|\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-2\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}\rangle\right)\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\big(\frac{\nu_{k}}{2M\eta\theta_{k}}+\frac{(4\gamma-\eta)\nu_{k}}{4\theta_{k}}-2\gamma-\rho\big)-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\left[c_{k}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}\big(\gamma+\frac{5\eta\theta_{k}}{\nu_{k}}\big)\right]\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[\frac{b_{k}}{2\theta_{k}}\big(\frac{(1+\omega-M\eta^{2}-4M\gamma^{2})\nu_{k}}{M\eta}-\gamma\theta_{k}\big)-c_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[a_{k}-b_{k}\big(2\gamma+\rho-\frac{\eta\theta_{k}}{4\nu_{k}}\big)\right]\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[b_{k}\big(\frac{(\eta+4\gamma)\nu_{k}}{4\theta_{k}}-2\gamma-\rho\big)-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(30)

Let us impose the following conditions on the parameters:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}1+\omega-M\eta^{2}&\geq&0,\\
a_{k}-b_{k}\big(2\gamma+\rho-\frac{\eta\theta_{k}}{4\nu_{k}}\big)&\geq&0,\\
b_{k}\left[\frac{(\eta+4\gamma)\nu_{k}}{4\theta_{k}}-2\gamma-\rho\right]-a_{k+1}&\geq&0,\\
c_{k}-\frac{b_{k}}{2}\big(\gamma+\frac{5\eta\theta_{k}}{\nu_{k}}\big)&\geq&0,\\
\frac{b_{k}}{2\theta_{k}}\left[\frac{(1+\omega-M\eta^{2}-4M\gamma^{2})\nu_{k}}{M\eta}-\gamma\theta_{k}\right]-c_{k+1}&\geq&0.\end{array}\right.

(31)

Then, (
30
) reduces to
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k+1}\leq\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

We still update
b_{k}=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)(k+2)}{2}
as in (
AEG
), which leads to
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{\theta_{k}}=\frac{b_{k}t_{k+1}}{t_{k}-1}
by (
23
).
Our next step is to show that the parameters updated by (
23
) guarantee the five conditions of (
31
).
First, let us choose
a_{k}:=b_{k}\left(2\gamma+\rho-\frac{\eta\theta_{k}}{4\nu_{k}}\right)=\frac{b_{k}(2\gamma t_{k}+\eta)}{4t_{k}}=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)(\gamma k+5\gamma+2\rho)}{4}
.
Then, the second condition of (
31
) is automatically satisfied.
Since
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}t_{k+1}}{t_{k}-1}
and
t_{k+1}=t_{k}+1
by (
23
), the choice of
a_{k+1}=\frac{b_{0}(k+2)(\gamma k+6\gamma+2\rho)}{4}
also guarantees the third condition of (
31
).

If we choose
c_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}}{2}\big(\gamma+\frac{5\eta\theta_{k}}{\nu_{k}}\big)=\frac{b_{0}(k+1)[(31\gamma+20\rho)(k+1)+\gamma]}{4}
, then the fourth condition of (
31
) is obviously satisfied with an equality.
The last condition of (
31
) holds if
M(6\eta^{2}+2\gamma\eta+4\gamma^{2})\leq 1+\omega
.
Using
\eta=2(3\gamma+2\rho)
and
M=2(1+\omega)L^{2}
for any
\omega>0
, the last condition is equivalent to

8L^{2}[6(3\gamma+2\rho)^{2}+\gamma(3\gamma+2\rho)+\gamma^{2}]\leq 1.

This condition is exactly equivalent to
16L^{2}[3(3\gamma+2\rho)^{2}+\gamma(2\gamma+\rho)]\leq 1
in Theorem
4.1
.
Moreover, it also guarantee that
2L^{2}\eta^{2}\leq 1
, which shows that the first condition of (
31
) holds.

Using the parameters in (
23
),
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
, and
c_{k}
above, and (
22
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\frac{b_{k}^{2}}{4t_{k}^{2}}-\frac{(3\gamma+2\rho)b_{k}}{2t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\left(c_{k}-\frac{\gamma b_{k}}{2t_{k}}\right)\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}\\
&=&\frac{b_{0}(k+1)[(4\gamma-b_{0})(k+1)+4\gamma]}{16}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{(31\gamma+20\rho)b_{0}(k+1)^{2}}{4}\|w^{k-1}-\hat{w}^{k-1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Let us choose
b_{0}:=2\gamma
.
Then, we have
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}(k+1)(k+3)}{4}\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}
.
Since
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\leq\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}=a_{0}\|w^{-1}\|^{2}+b_{0}\langle w^{-1},z^{0}-y^{0}\rangle+\|z^{0}+t_{0}(y^{0}-z^{0})-x^{\star}\|^{2}+c_{0}\|w^{-1}-\hat{w}^{-1}\|^{2}
, and
y^{0}=z^{0}=x^{0}
and
w^{-1}=\hat{w}^{-1}
, we get
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\leq\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{0}=a_{0}\|w^{-1}\|^{2}+\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}=2\gamma(5\gamma+2\rho)\|w^{0}\|^{2}+\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}
since
w^{-1}=2w^{0}
.
Combining these derivations, we finally get
\|w^{k-1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+8\gamma(5\gamma+2\rho)\|w^{0}\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(k+1)(k+3)}
, which implies (
24
).
If
J_{\eta T}
is nonexpansive, then using
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\|w^{k}\|
from Lemma
3.1
, we obtain (
25
).

\square
∎

5
The Extra-Anchored Gradient-Type Methods for Solving (
chMI
)

In this section, we provide an alternative convergence analysis for Halpern’s fixed-point-type variants of (
FBFS
) and its past FBFS scheme, which is called EAG (extra-anchored gradient) method
[
40
,
79
]
and PEAG (past extra-anchored gradient) method in
[
75
]
, respectively, where their names are stemmed from
[
79
]
.
These schemes have been extended to (
chMI
) in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, respectively.
Our algorithmic derivation as well as our analysis can be viewed as direct extensions of the results in
[
40
,
75
,
79
]
.
Moreover, together with the new analysis, our
PEAG
is significantly different from that of
[
14
]
since we replace
Fx^{k}
by
Fy^{k-1}
instead of replacing
Fy^{k}
by
Fx^{k-1}
as often seen in past extragradient-type methods.

5.1
The extra-anchored gradient method for solving (
chMI
)

We first derive a variant of the extra-anchored gradient method (EAG) from
[
40
,
79
]
to solve (
chMI
).
We call this method (
EAG
) as in
[
79
]
, which can be described as follows:
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, at each iteration
k\geq 0
, we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-(\hat{\eta}_{k}-2\rho(1-\tau_{k}))(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}),\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-\eta(Fy^{k}+\xi^{k+1})+2\rho(1-\tau_{k})(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(EAG)

where
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
,
\tau_{k}\in(0,1)
,
\eta>0
, and
\hat{\eta}_{k}>0
are determined later.

This variant was studied in
[
13
]
.
However, if
T=0
, then (
EAG
) reduces to the EAG variant in
[
40
]
.
We introduce the following quantities to simplify our analysis:

w^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{w}^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}+\xi^{k}.

(32)

Then, we can rewrite (
EAG
) as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-(\hat{\eta}_{k}-2\rho(1-\tau_{k}))w^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}+2\rho(1-\tau_{k})w^{k}.\end{array}\right.

(33)

The interpretation (
33
) looks very similar to the fast extragradient scheme in
[
40
]
for root-finding problem (
chME
), where
w^{k}
and
\hat{w}^{k+1}
play the roles of
Fx^{k}
and
Fy^{k}
, respectively in
[
40
]
.

Since
x^{k+1}
appears on both sides of the second line of (
33
), we will use the resolvent
J_{\eta T}
to rewrite it as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-(\hat{\eta}_{k}-2\rho(1-\tau_{k}))(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}),\\
x^{k+1}&:=&J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k})\big),\\
\xi^{k+1}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}\big(y^{k}-x^{k+1}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}(Fx^{k}+\xi^{k})\big),\end{array}\right.

(34)

where
\tau_{k}\in(0,1)
and
\xi^{0}\in Tx^{0}
is arbitrary.

To establish convergence of (
EAG
), we use the following Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{V}_{k}:=a_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle,

(35)

where
a_{k}>0
and
b_{k}>0
are given parameters.
This function is similar to the one in
[
40
,
79
]
and is the same as the one in
[
13
]
.
The following theorem states the convergence of (
EAG
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
F+T
in (
chMI
) is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone,
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous such that
2L\rho<1
, and
\mathrm{zer}(F+T)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
EAG
) using

\tau_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2},\quad\eta\in\left(2\rho,\frac{1}{L}\right],\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\eta}_{k}:=(1-\tau_{k})\eta.

(36)

Then, for all
k\geq 0
and any
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F+T)
, the following result holds:

\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+2\eta(\eta-2\rho)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}}{(\eta-2\rho)^{2}(k+1)^{2}},\quad\text{where}\quad\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}.

(37)

Consequently, if
J_{\eta T}
is nonexpansive, then for
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
defined by (
1
), we have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+2\eta(\eta-2\rho)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}}{(\eta-2\rho)^{2}(k+1)^{2}}.}

(38)

Proof.

Since (
EAG
) is equivalent to (
33
), from the second line of (
EAG
), we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k+1}-x^{k}&=&-\tau_{k}(x^{k}-x^{0})-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}+2\rho(1-\tau_{k})w^{k}\\
x^{k+1}-x^{k}&=&-\tfrac{\tau_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}(x^{k+1}-x^{0})-\tfrac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\hat{w}^{k+1}+2\rho w^{k}.\end{array}\right.

(39)

Next, since
F+T
is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone and
w^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
, we have
\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle+\rho\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}\geq 0
.
This relation leads to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&\leq&\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle-\langle w^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle+\rho\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:EAG_proof1}}{=}&\tau_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle-\frac{\tau_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{0}\rangle+\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-2\rho(1-\tau_{k})\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle+2\rho\langle w^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle+\rho\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying this expression by
\frac{b_{k}}{\tau_{k}}
, rearranging the result, and using
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}
, we obtain the following expression

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle-b_{k+1}\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{\eta b_{k+1}}{\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle+\eta b_{k+1}\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle-\frac{\rho b_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\rho b_{k}(1-2\tau_{k})}{\tau_{k}}\|w^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Adding
a_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}
to
\mathcal{T}_{[1]}
and using
\mathcal{V}_{k}
from (
35
), we can show that

\hskip-12.91663pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&=&a_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle-b_{k+1}\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\big(a_{k}+\frac{\rho b_{k}(1-2\tau_{k})}{\tau_{k}}\big)\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\big(a_{k+1}+\frac{\rho b_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\big)\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta b_{k+1}}{\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle+\eta b_{k+1}\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-21.52771pt

(40)

Next, from (
33
), we have
x^{k+1}-y^{k}=-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}+\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k}
.
By the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
, we have
\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}=\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}=L^{2}\|\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k}\|^{2}
.
Expanding this inequality and rearranging the result, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&\geq&\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+(1-L^{2}\eta^{2})\|\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}-2\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle\\
&&-{~}2\big(1-L^{2}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k})\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle-L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{\eta b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}
and adding the result to (
40
), we get

\hskip-17.22217pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\frac{L^{2}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}b_{k+1}-2\rho b_{k}(1-2\tau_{k})}{2\tau_{k}}\right)\|w^{k}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{\eta b_{k+1}-2\rho b_{k}}{2\tau_{k}}-a_{k+1}\right)\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta(1-L^{2}\eta^{2})b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta(1-\tau_{k}-L^{2}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k})b_{k+1}}{\tau_{k}}\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-30.1388pt

(41)

Let us choose
\tau_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
and
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=(1-\tau_{k})\eta
as in (
36
).
We also choose
a_{k+1}:=\frac{b_{k+1}[\eta-2\rho(1-\tau_{k})]}{2\tau_{k}}=\frac{[(\eta-2\rho)(k+2)+2\rho]b_{k+1}}{2}
provided that
\eta>2\rho
.
By the update of
b_{k}
as
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}
, we can easily show that
b_{k}=b_{0}(k+1)
for
k\geq 0
.
Therefore, we get
a_{k}=\frac{b_{0}[(\eta-2\rho)(k+1)+2\rho](k+1)}{2}
.

Using these parameters and assuming that
L\eta\leq 1
, we can simplify (
41
) as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}-\mathcal{V}_{k+1}&\geq&\frac{\eta(1-L^{2}\eta^{2})b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k+1}-(1-\tau_{k})w^{k}\|^{2}\geq 0.\end{array}

(42)

Finally, since
0\in Fx^{\star}+Tx^{\star}
, we have
\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|w^{k}\|^{2}
.
Using this inequality, the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{k}
, and Young’s inequality, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{V}_{k}&=&a_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{\star}-x^{0}\rangle+b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\\
&\geq&(a_{k}-\rho b_{k})\|w^{k}\|^{2}-b_{k}\|w^{k}\|\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|\\
&\geq&\left(a_{k}-\rho b_{k}-\frac{(\eta-2\rho)b_{k}^{2}}{4b_{0}}\right)\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{0}}{\eta-2\rho}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&=&\frac{b_{0}(\eta-2\rho)(k+1)^{2}}{4}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{0}}{\eta-2\rho}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Moreover, we also have
\mathcal{V}_{0}=a_{0}\|w^{0}\|^{2}+b_{0}\langle w^{0},x^{0}-x^{0}\rangle=\frac{\eta b_{0}}{2}\|w^{0}\|^{2}
.
Combining all these derivations and using (
42
) to get
\mathcal{V}_{k}\leq\mathcal{V}_{0}=\frac{\eta b_{0}}{2}\|w^{0}\|^{2}
, we obtain (
37
).
The conditions
\eta>2\rho
and
L\eta\leq 1
imply that
2\rho<\eta\leq\frac{1}{L}
, which is satisfied if
2L\rho<1
.
The bound (
38
) is a consequence of (
37
) and
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|
in Lemma
3.1
.

\square
∎

Clearly, the condition on
L
and
\rho
, and the convergence rate bounds (
9
) and (
37
) of (
AEG
) are similar to the ones in (
EAG
), respectively.
As shown in
[
40
]
for the case
T=0
, these convergence rates are tight.

5.2
The past extra-anchored gradient method for solving (
chMI
)

We now develop a variant of the past extra-anchored gradient method (PEAG) from
[
75
]
to solve (
chMI
).
We still call this method (
PEAG
) and it is presented as follows:
Starting from
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we set
y^{-1}:=x^{0}
, and for
k\geq 0
we update

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-(\hat{\eta}_{k}-\beta_{k})(Fy^{k-1}+\xi^{k}),\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-\eta(Fy^{k}+\xi^{k+1})+\beta_{k}(Fy^{k-1}+\xi^{k}),\end{array}\right.

(PEAG)

where
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
is an arbitrary element of
Tx^{k}
,
\tau_{k}\in(0,1)
,
\eta>0
,
\hat{\eta}_{k}>0
, and
\beta_{k}>0
are given parameters, which will be determined later.

Note that if
T=0
, then (
PEAG
) reduces to the past extra-anchored gradient scheme in
[
75
]
.
This scheme can be considered as a modification of (
EAG
) by replacing
Fx^{k}
by
Fy^{k-1}
using Popov’s idea in
[
65
]
.
However, it is different from (
PFBFS
) as we do not replace
Fy^{k}
by
Fx^{k-1}
.
Compared to
[
14
]
, since their method is rooted from the reflected gradient method in
[
50
]
, it is also different from (
PEAG
) though both methods have the same per-iteration complexity and convergence rate.
Moreover, our condition
2\sqrt{34}L\rho<1
in Theorem
5.2
below is better than
12\sqrt{3}L\rho<1
in
[
14
]
.
Our proof below is also different from
[
14
]
, where we follow the proof technique in
[
75
]
.

Let us rewrite (
PEAG
) into a different form.
We introduce
w^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
and
\hat{w}^{k}:=Fy^{k-1}+\xi^{k}
as in (
EAG
).
Then, we can rewrite (
PEAG
) as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-(\hat{\eta}_{k}-\beta_{k})\hat{w}^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}+\beta_{k}\hat{w}^{k}.\end{array}\right.

(43)

This scheme has the same form as
[
75
]
but with different parameters, where
\hat{w}^{k}
plays the same role as
Fy^{k}
in
[
75
]
.

Note that the second line of (
PEAG
) is an implicit update since
x^{k+1}
is also in
\xi^{k+1}\in Tx^{k+1}
on the right-hand side of this line.
To resolve this implicit issue, we rewrite it as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k}&:=&x^{k}+\tau_{k}(x^{0}-x^{k})-(\hat{\eta}_{k}-\beta_{k})\hat{w}^{k},\\
x^{k+1}&:=&J_{\eta T}\big(y^{k}-\eta Fy^{k}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k}\big),\\
\hat{w}^{k+1}&:=&\frac{1}{\eta}\big(y^{k}-x^{k+1}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k}\big),\end{array}\right.

(44)

where
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is given,
y^{-1}:=x^{0}
, and
\hat{w}^{0}:=w^{0}\in Fx^{0}+Tx^{0}
is arbitrary.

To establish convergence of (
PEAG
), we use the following Lyapunov function:

\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}:=a_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}+b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle+c_{k}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2},

(45)

where
w^{k}:=\hat{w}^{k}+Fx^{k}-Fy^{k-1}=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}
,
a_{k}>0
,
b_{k}>0
, and
c_{k}>0
are given parameters.
Then, the following theorem states the convergence rate of (
PEAG
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
F+T
in (
chMI
) is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone,
F
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous such that
2\sqrt{34}L\rho<1
, and
\mathrm{zer}(F+T)\neq\emptyset
.
Let
\eta:=\frac{1}{L}\sqrt{\frac{2}{17}}
be a given stepsize, and
\{(x^{k},y^{k})\}
be generated by (
PEAG
) using the following parameters:

\tau_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2},\quad\beta_{k}:=\frac{4\rho(1-\tau_{k})}{1+\tau_{k}},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\eta}_{k}:=(1-\tau_{k})\eta.

(46)

Then, for all
k\geq 0
and any
x^{\star}\in\mathrm{zer}(F+T)
, we have

\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}\left[\frac{4}{(\eta-4\rho)^{2}}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{2(3\eta-2\rho)}{3(\eta-4\rho)}\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}\right],

(47)

where
\xi^{k}\in Tx^{k}
and
\xi^{0}\in Tx^{0}
.

Consequently, if
J_{\eta T}
is nonexpansive, then for
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
defined by (
1
), we have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}\Big[\frac{4}{(\eta-4\rho)^{2}}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{2(3\eta-2\rho)}{3(\eta-4\rho)}\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}\Big].}

(48)

Proof.

Since (
PEAG
) is equivalent to (
43
), from (
43
), we can easily show that

\hskip-8.61108pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k+1}-x^{k}&=&-\tau_{k}(x^{k}-x^{0})-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}+\beta_{k}w^{k}+\beta_{k}(\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k})\\
x^{k+1}-x^{k}&=&-\tfrac{\tau_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}(x^{k+1}-x^{0})-\tfrac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\hat{w}^{k+1}+\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}w^{k}+\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}(\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}).\end{array}\right.\hskip-8.61108pt

(49)

Next, since
F+T
is
\rho
-co-hypomonotone and
w^{k}=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
, we have
\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},x^{k+1}-x^{k}\rangle+\rho\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}\geq 0
.
Using (
49
) into this inequality, we can expand it as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\tau_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle-\frac{\tau_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\rho\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle-\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1}-(1-\tau_{k})w^{k},\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Using Young’s inequality and choosing
\beta_{k}:=\frac{4\rho(1-\tau_{k})}{1+\tau_{k}}
, we can further derive

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\tau_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle-\frac{\tau_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\rho\|w^{k+1}-w^{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle-\frac{\beta_{k}}{4(1-\tau_{k})}\|w^{k+1}-(1-\tau_{k})w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\frac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\left[\frac{(1+\tau_{k})\beta_{k}}{2(1-\tau_{k})}-2\rho\right]\langle w^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}\left[\frac{\beta_{k}}{4(1-\tau_{k})}+\rho\right]\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+\left[\beta_{k}-\frac{\beta_{k}(1-\tau_{k})}{4}-\rho\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&\frac{\eta}{1-\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\frac{\rho(2+\tau_{k})}{1+\tau_{k}}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\rho(2-3\tau_{k}-\tau_{k}^{2})}{1+\tau_{k}}\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{4\rho}{1+\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying
\mathcal{T}_{[1]}
by
\frac{b_{k}}{\tau_{k}}
, rearranging the result, and using
b_{k+1}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-\tau_{k}}
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{[1]}&:=&b_{k}\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{0}\rangle-b_{k+1}\langle w^{k+1},x^{k+1}-x^{0}\rangle\\
&\geq&\frac{b_{k+1}\eta}{\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle+b_{k+1}\eta\langle w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle-\frac{\rho b_{k}(2+\tau_{k})}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\rho b_{k}(2-3\tau_{k}-\tau_{k}^{2})}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{4\rho b_{k}}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}\|\hat{w}^{k}-w^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Adding
a_{k}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-a_{k+1}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+c_{k}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-c_{k+1}\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}
to both sides of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{[1]}
, then using
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
defined by (
45
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[a_{k}+\frac{\rho b_{k}(2-3\tau_{k}-\tau_{k}^{2})}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\left[a_{k+1}+\frac{\rho b_{k}(2+\tau_{k})}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}\right]\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+\left[c_{k}-\frac{4\rho b_{k}}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}\right]\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}-c_{k+1}\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta b_{k+1}}{\tau_{k}}\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle+\eta b_{k+1}\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(50)

Now, from (
43
), we have
x^{k+1}-y^{k}=-\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}+\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k}
.
Therefore, using the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
and Young’s inequality, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}&=&\|Fx^{k+1}-Fy^{k}\|^{2}\leq L^{2}\|x^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}=L^{2}\|\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&2L^{2}\|\eta\hat{w}^{k+1}-\hat{\eta}_{k}w^{k}\|^{2}+2L^{2}\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
(1+\omega)
for some
\omega>0
, defining
M:=2(1+\omega)L^{2}
, and expanding and rearranging the result, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}0&\geq&\omega\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}+(1-M\eta^{2})\|\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}-2\langle w^{k+1}-w^{k},\hat{w}^{k+1}\rangle\\
&&-{~}2(1-M\eta\hat{\eta}_{k})\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle-M\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-M\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Multiplying this inequality by
\frac{\eta b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}
and adding the result to (
50
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\geq&\left[c_{k}-\frac{4\rho b_{k}}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}-\frac{Mb_{k+1}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}}{2\tau_{k}}\right]\|w^{k}-\hat{w}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left(\frac{\omega\eta b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}-c_{k+1}\right)\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[a_{k}+\frac{\rho b_{k}(2-3\tau_{k}-\tau_{k}^{2})}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}-\frac{Mb_{k+1}\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}^{2}}{2\tau_{k}}\right]\|w^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\left[\frac{\eta b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}-\frac{\rho b_{k}(2+\tau_{k})}{\tau_{k}(1+\tau_{k})}-a_{k+1}\right]\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta(1-M\eta^{2})b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\frac{\eta\left(1-\tau_{k}-M\eta\hat{\eta}_{k}\right)b_{k+1}}{\tau_{k}}\langle\hat{w}^{k+1},w^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

(51)

Let us choose
\tau_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
,
\hat{\eta}_{k}:=(1-\tau_{k})\eta
,
a_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}}{2}\left(\eta(k+1)-4\rho k+\frac{2\rho(k-1)}{k+3}\right)
, and
c_{k}:=\frac{b_{k}}{2}\left(M\eta^{3}(k+1)+\frac{8\rho(k+2)^{2}}{k+3}\right)
.
Then, utilizing these choices and
b_{k+1}(1-\tau_{k})=b_{k}
, (
51
) reduces to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}-\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}&\geq&\frac{\eta(1-M\eta^{2})b_{k+1}}{2\tau_{k}}\|\hat{w}^{k+1}-(1-\tau_{k})w^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2\rho b_{k+1}(k+2)}{(k+3)(k+4)}\|w^{k+1}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{b_{k+1}(k+2)}{2}\left(\omega\eta-M\eta^{3}-\frac{8\rho(k+3)^{2}}{(k+2)(k+4)}\right)\|w^{k+1}-\hat{w}^{k+1}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(52)

Clearly, we need to choose
\omega
such that
\omega\eta\geq M\eta^{3}+\frac{8\rho(k+3)^{2}}{(k+2)(k+4)}
, which holds if
\omega\eta\geq M\eta^{3}+9\rho
.
Moreover, to guarantee
a_{k}>0
, we need to choose
\eta>4\rho
.
Therefore, if the following three conditions hold:

2(1+\omega)L^{2}\eta^{2}\leq 1,\quad\omega\eta\geq 2(1+\omega)L^{2}\eta^{3}+9\rho,\quad\text{and}\quad\eta>4\rho,

(53)

then, from (
52
), we have
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k+1}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

For simplicity of our analysis, let us choose
\omega:=\frac{13}{4}
and
\eta:=\frac{1}{L\sqrt{2(1+\omega)}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{17}L}
.
Then, the last two conditions of (
53
) become
L\rho\leq\frac{\omega-1}{9\sqrt{2(1+\omega)}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{34}}
and
L\rho<\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2(1+\omega)}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{34}}
, respectively.
Therefore, if
2\sqrt{34}L\rho<1
, then all the conditions of (
53
) hold.

Since
\tau_{k}:=\frac{1}{k+2}
, we can easily show that
b_{k}=b_{0}(k+1)
for some
b_{0}>0
.
Moreover, since
0\in Fx^{\star}+Tx^{\star}
, we have
\langle w^{k},x^{k}-x^{\star}\rangle\geq-\rho\|w^{k}\|^{2}
.
Using this inequality, the definition (
45
) of
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
, Young’s inequality,
b_{k}=b_{0}(k+1)
, and the choice of
a_{k}
, we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}&\geq&\left(a_{k}-b_{k}\rho-\frac{(\eta-4\rho)b_{k}^{2}}{4b_{0}}\right)\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{0}}{\eta-4\rho}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\\
&\geq&\frac{b_{0}(\eta-4\rho)(k+1)^{2}}{4}\|w^{k}\|^{2}-\frac{b_{0}}{\eta-4\rho}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Since
y^{-1}=x^{0}
, we have
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}=a_{0}\|w^{0}\|^{2}+b_{0}\langle w^{0},x^{0}-x^{0}\rangle+c_{0}\|w^{0}-\hat{w}^{0}\|^{2}=a_{0}\|w^{0}\|^{2}=b_{0}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}-\frac{\rho}{3}\right)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}
.
Therefore, we get
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}\leq\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{0}=b_{0}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}-\frac{\rho}{3}\right)\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}
from (
52
).
Combining this and the lower bound of
\hat{\mathcal{V}}_{k}
above, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|w^{k}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}}\left[\frac{4}{(\eta-4\rho)^{2}}\|x^{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\frac{2(3\eta-2\rho)}{3(\eta-4\rho)}\|Fx^{0}+\xi^{0}\|^{2}\right],\end{array}

which is exactly (
47
) by using
w^{k}:=Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\in Fx^{k}+Tx^{k}
.
The last bound (
48
) is a direct consequence of (
47
) and
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}x^{k}\|\leq\|Fx^{k}+\xi^{k}\|
in Lemma
3.1
.

\square
∎

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have developed two “Nesterov’s accelerated” variants of the FBFS method and its past-FBFS scheme.
Our approach is different from Halpern’s fixed-point iteration in, e.g.,
[
13
,
14
,
40
,
75
,
79
]
.
These two types of methods are though different, they are related to each other as discussed in
[
73
]
.
We have established
\mathcal{O}\left(1/k\right)
last-iterate convergence rates of both new schemes using explicit parameter update rules and different Lyapunov functions.
We have also provided an alternative convergence rate analysis of the extra-anchored gradient-type methods in
[
13
,
14
]
for solving (
chMI
).
Our analysis exploits the proof techniques in
[
40
,
75
,
79
]
and our second scheme is also different from the one in
[
14
]
.

Beyond the results of this paper, several research questions remain open to us.
We discuss some of them here.
First, though both proposed algorithms theoretically achieve better convergence rates than the classical non-accelerated (
FBFS
) and (
PFBFS
) schemes, their practical performance remains unclear.
It is important to develop practical variants of these methods so that they can perform better or at least not worse than (
FBFS
) and (
PFBFS
) on concrete applications.
One idea is to use adaptive techniques to update the algorithmic parameters without knowing
L
and
\rho
.
Second, it is also interesting to establish
o\left(1/k\right)
(small-o) convergence rates of the proposed methods by using different parameter update rules, at least for the monotone case.
In addition, the convergence of iterate sequences and their convergence rates remains open.
Third, improving the range of
L\rho
is also interesting, at least theoretically.
The classical methods (
FBFS
) and (
PFBFS
) have tight bounds on the range of
L
and
\rho
, while our conditions remain suboptimal and can be improved, especially for (
APEG
).
Fourth, one can also extend our methods to solve
0\in Fx+Hx+Tx
and provide rigorous convergence rate analysis as we have done in this paper.
Finally, developing stochastic and randomized block coordinate variants for these methods to solve (
chMI
) is also interesting and expected to make significant impact in machine learning applications and distributed systems.

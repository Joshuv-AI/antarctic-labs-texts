Constrained quantization for probability distributions
1 Megha Pandey, 2 Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury

Abstract
. In this work, we extend the classical framework of quantization for Borel probability measures defined on normed spaces ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} by introducing and analyzing the notions of the n n th constrained quantization error, constrained quantization dimension, and constrained quantization coefficient. These concepts generalize the well-established n n th quantization error, quantization dimension, and quantization coefficient, traditionally considered in the unconstrained setting, and thereby broaden the scope of quantization theory. A key distinction between the unconstrained and constrained frameworks lies in the structural properties of optimal quantizers. In the unconstrained setting, if the support of P P contains at least n n elements, then the elements of an optimal set of n n -points coincide with the conditional expectations over their respective Voronoi regions; this characterization does not, in general, persist under constraints. Moreover, it is known that if the support of P P contains at least n n elements, then any optimal set of n n -points in the unconstrained case consists of exactly n n distinct elements. This property, however, may fail to hold in the constrained context. Further differences emerge in asymptotic behaviors. For absolutely continuous probability measures, the unconstrained quantization dimension is known to exist and equals the Euclidean dimension of the underlying space. In contrast, we show that this equivalence does not necessarily extend to the constrained setting. Additionally, while the unconstrained quantization coefficient exists and assumes a unique, finite, and positive value for absolutely continuous measures, we establish that the constrained quantization coefficient can exhibit significant variability and may attain any nonnegative value, depending critically on the specific nature of the constraint applied to the quantization process.

For

\|\cdot\|

\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Let
P

\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
r\in(0,\infty)

S

\mathbb{R}^{k}

P

V_{r}(P;\alpha)=\int\mathop{\min}\limits_{a\in\alpha}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x).

V_{n,r}:=V_{n,r}(P)=\inf\Big\{V_{r}(P;\alpha):\alpha\subseteq S,~1\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n\Big\},

\text{card}(A)

. If in the definition of
n

S
, known as
constraint
, is chosen as the set
\mathbb{R}^{k}
itself,
then the
n

is referred to as the
n

, which traditionally in the literature is referred to as the

A set

Proposition 1.1
.

Let the assumption (
2
) be true. Then,
V_{n,r}(P)
exists and is a decreasing sequence of finite nonnegative numbers.

Proof.

Under the assumption, we claim that
\int d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)<\infty
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Since
d(x,a)\leq d(x,0)+d(0,a)\leq 2\max\{d(x,0),d(0,a)\}
, we have

d(x,a)^{r}\leq 2^{r}\max\{d(x,0)^{r},d(0,a)^{r}\}\leq 2^{r}\Big(d(x,0)^{r}+d(0,a)^{r}\Big)

implying

\displaystyle\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{r}\Big(\int(d(x,0)^{r}+d(0,a)^{r})\,dP(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{r}\Big(\int d(x,0)^{r}\,dP(x)+d(0,a)^{r}\Big)<\infty.

Hence, for
0<r<\infty
,
\int d(x,0)^{r}dP(x)<\infty
yields the fact that
\int d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)<\infty
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, which is the claim.
By the claim, for any finite subset
\alpha\subseteq S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have

0\leq\int\min_{a\in\alpha}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)\leq\min_{a\in\alpha}\int d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)<\infty\text{ implying }0\leq V_{r}(P;\alpha)<\infty,

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
which yields

0\leq\inf\Big\{V_{r}(P;\alpha):\alpha\subseteq S,~1\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n\Big\}<\infty,

i.e.,
V_{n,r}(P)
exists as a finite nonnegative number. The statement that
V_{n,r}(P)
is decreasing directly follows from the definition.
Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

The following two propositions reflect two important properties of constrained quantization.

Proposition 1.2
.

In constrained quantization for any Borel probability measure
P
, an optimal set of one-point always exists, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{1,r}(P;S)
is nonempty.

Proof.

Let
0<r<\infty
. Define a function

\psi_{r}:S\to\mathbb{R}^{+}:\psi_{r}(a)=\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x).

The function
\psi_{r}
is obviously continuous. Then, for every
c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with

c>\mathop{\inf}\limits_{b\in S}\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x),

the level sets

\{\psi_{r}\leq c\}:=\{a\in S:\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x)\leq c\},

are closed subsets of
S
.
Proceeding in the similar way, as shown in the previous proposition, for any
0<r<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle d(0,a)^{r}\leq 2^{r}\Big(d(x,a)^{r}+d(x,0)^{r}\Big).

Thus, for
a\in\{\psi_{r}\leq c\}
, we have

\displaystyle d(0,a)^{r}=\int d(0,a)^{r}\,dP(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{r}\Big(\int(d(x,a)^{r}+d(x,0)^{r})\,dP(x)\Big)\leq 2^{r}\Big(c+E\|X\|^{r}\Big),

i.e.,

d(0,a)\leq 2\Big(c+E\|X\|^{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\text{ yielding }\{\psi_{r}\leq c\}\subseteq B\Big(0,2\Big(c+E\|X\|^{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\Big),

where
E\|X\|^{r}=\int d(x,0)^{r}\,dP(x)
.
Hence, the level sets are bounded. As the level sets are both bounded and closed, they are compact.
Let us now consider a decreasing sequence
\{c_{n}\}
of elements in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

(3)

c_{n}>\mathop{\inf}\limits_{b\in S}\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x)\text{ and }c_{n}\to\mathop{\inf}\limits_{b\in S}\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x).

Then,

\{a\in S:\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x)\leq c_{n+1}\}\subseteq\{a\in S:\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x)\leq c_{n}\},\text{i.e.,}

\{\psi_{r}\leq c_{n+1}\}\subseteq\{\psi_{r}\leq c_{n}\}.

Also,
\{\psi_{r}\leq c_{n}\}\neq\emptyset
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If this is not true, then there will exist an element
c_{N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
for all
b\in S
, we have

c_{N}<\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x)\text{ yielding }c_{N}\leq\inf_{b\in S}\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x),

which contradicts (
3
).
Thus, we see that the level sets
\{\psi_{r}\leq c_{n}\}

form a nested sequence of nonempty compact sets, and hence,

\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\{\psi_{r}\leq c_{n}\}\neq\emptyset.

Let
a\in\mathop{\bigcap}\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\{\psi_{r}\leq c_{n}\}
. Then,

\mathop{\inf}\limits_{b\in S}\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x)\leq\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x)<c_{n}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

which by squeeze theorem implies that

\mathop{\inf}\limits_{b\in S}\int d(x,b)^{r}\,dP(x)=\int d(x,a)^{r}\,dP(x),

i.e.,
\{a\}
forms an optimal set of one-point, i.e.,
\{a\}\in\mathcal{C}_{1,r}(P;S)
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}_{1,r}(P;S)
is nonempty.
Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Definition 1.3
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
U
be the largest open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
P(U)=0
. Then,
\mathbb{R}^{k}\setminus U
is called the
support of
P
, and is denoted by
\text{supp}(P)
.
For a locally finite set
\alpha\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
a\in\alpha
, by
M(a|\alpha)
we denote the set of all elements in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
which are nearest to
a
among all the elements in
\alpha
, i.e.,

M(a|\alpha)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:d(x,a)=\mathop{\min}\limits_{b\in\alpha}d(x,b)\}.

M(a|\alpha)
is called the
Voronoi region
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
generated by
a\in\alpha
.
The set
\{M(a|\alpha):a\in\alpha\}
is called the
Voronoi diagram
or
Voronoi tessellation
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with respect to the set
\alpha
. Further, for
\alpha=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\}\subseteq S
, let us define the sets
A_{a_{i}|\alpha}
for
a_{i}\in\alpha
as follows:

(4)

\displaystyle A_{a_{i}|\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}M(a_{1}|\alpha)&\text{ if }i=1,\\
M(a_{i}|\alpha)\setminus\bigcup_{k<i}M(a_{k}|\alpha)&\text{ if }i\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

The set
\{A_{a_{i}|\alpha}:a_{i}\in\alpha\}
is called the
Voronoi partition
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with respect to the set
\alpha
(and
S
).

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for
P
with respect to the constraint
S
. Then,
\alpha_{n}
contains exactly
n
elements if and only if there exists a set
\alpha\subseteq S
containing at least
n
elements such that

P(A_{a|\alpha})>0\text{ for each }a\in\alpha.

Proof.

Let us first assume that there exists a set
\alpha\subseteq S
containing at least
n
elements such that

P(A_{a|\alpha})>0\text{ for each }a\in\alpha.

If
n=1
, then the proposition is a consequence of Proposition
1.2
. Let us now prove the proposition for
2\leq n
. For the sake of contradiction, assume that
\gamma:=\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m}\}\subseteq S
is an optimal set of
n
-points for
P
, such that
\text{card}(\gamma)=m
for some positive integer
m<n
, i.e.,

V_{n,r}(P)=\int\min_{a\in\gamma}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x).

If
m\geq 2
, then we have

V_{n,r}(P)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x).

Since
\text{card}(\alpha)>\text{card}(\gamma)
, let
c\in\alpha
be such that
c\not\in\{b_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
. Then,
c\in A_{b_{\ell}|\gamma}
for some
1\leq\ell\leq m
. Consider the set
\gamma\cup\{c\}
, we can write

\gamma\cup\{c\}=\{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m},b_{m+1}\},\text{ where }b_{m+1}=c.

Notice that
A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}
intersects some of the Voronoi partitions of
A_{b_{j}|\gamma}
for
1\leq j\leq m
. If
A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}
does not intersect
A_{b_{j}|\gamma}
for some
1\leq j\leq m
, then

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)=\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x).

On the other hand, if
A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}
intersects
A_{b_{j}|\gamma}
for some
1\leq j\leq m
, then

(6)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)+\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}^{c}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,c)^{r}dP(x)+\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}^{c}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x).

For
1\leq t<m
, let
A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}
intersects
A_{b_{\ell_{i}}|\gamma}
, where
b_{{\ell_{i}}}\in\{b_{j}:1\leq j\leq m\}
for
i\in\{1,2,\cdots t\}
. Then,

A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}A_{b_{{\ell_{i}}}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}.

Hence, by the expressions in (
5
) and (
6
), we have

\displaystyle V_{n,r}(P)=

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\gamma}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in(\{1,2,\cdots,m\}\setminus\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots,\ell_{t}\})}\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\Big(\int_{A_{b_{{\ell_{i}}}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,c)^{r}dP(x)+\int_{A_{b_{{\ell_{i}}}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}^{c}}d(x,b_{{\ell_{i}}})^{r}dP(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in(\{1,2,\cdots,m\}\setminus\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots,\ell_{t}\})}\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)+\int_{A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,c)^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\int_{A_{b_{{\ell_{i}}}|\gamma}\cap A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}^{c}}d(x,b_{{\ell_{i}}})^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in(\{1,2,\cdots,m\}\setminus\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\cdots,\ell_{t}\})}\int_{A_{b_{j}|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,b_{j})^{r}dP(x)+\int_{A_{c|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,c)^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\int_{A_{b_{{\ell_{i}}}|\gamma\cup\{c\}}}d(x,b_{{\ell_{i}}})^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\min_{a\in\gamma\cup\{c\}}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)\geq V_{n,r}(P),

where the last inequality is true since
\gamma\cup\{c\}
contains no more than
n
elements. Thus, we see that a contradiction arises. Hence, an optimal set of
n
-points contains exactly
n
elements.

Next, assume that
\alpha_{n}
is an optimal set of
n
-points for
P
with respect to the constraint
S
such that
\alpha_{n}
contains exactly
n
elements. We need to show that

P(A_{a|\alpha_{n}})>0\text{ for each }a\in\alpha_{n}.

For the sake of contradiction, let
\gamma
be the nonempty maximal subset of
\alpha_{n}
such that
P(A_{b|\alpha_{n}})=0\text{ for each }b\in\gamma

and
P(A_{b|\alpha_{n}})>0\text{ for each }b\in\alpha_{n}\setminus\gamma
. Since optimal set of one-point always exists, hence
\gamma\neq\alpha_{n}
. Let
\text{card}(\gamma)=m
, and then
\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\setminus\gamma)=n-m
. Thus, we see that

\displaystyle V_{n,r}(P)

\displaystyle=\int\min_{a\in\alpha_{n}}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)=\sum_{a\in\alpha_{n}}\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}d(x,a)^{r}dP(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\alpha_{n}\setminus\gamma}\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}\setminus\gamma}}d(x,b)^{r}dP(x),

which implies that
\alpha_{n}\setminus\gamma
is an optimal set of
n
-points such that
\text{card }(\alpha_{n}\setminus\gamma)=n-m<n
, which contradicts our assumption. Thus, we see that if
\alpha_{n}
is an optimal set of
n
-points containing exactly
n
elements, then

P(A_{a|\alpha_{n}})>0\text{ for each }a\in\alpha_{n}.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete. ∎

Proposition 1.7
.

Let

If an optimal set of

. We need to prove that an optimal set of

-points for

The following proposition is a standard result in quantization theory (see
[
7
]
). However, for the sake of completeness, we provide a proof here.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, if
S=\mathbb{R}^{k}
, i.e., when there is no constraint, then the elements in an optimal set of
n
-points are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi regions provided that the
\text{supp}(P)
contains at least
n
elements.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for
P
and
\{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}:a\in\alpha_{n}\}
be the Voronoi partition of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with respect to the set
\alpha_{n}
. Let
V_{n,r}(P)
be the corresponding
n
th quantization error. Then,

\displaystyle V_{n,r}(P)=\int\min_{a\in\alpha_{n}}d(x,a)^{r}\ dP(x)=\sum_{a\in\alpha_{n}}\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}d(x,a)^{r}\ dP(x).

Notice that
V_{n,r}(P)
will be minimum if the function

\mathsf{F}_{r}(a):=\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}d(x,a)^{r}\ dP(x)

is minimum for each
a\in\alpha_{n}
.
Notice that the value of
a
, where the function
F_{r}(a)
is minimum, does not depend on
r
because for any
u,v,w,z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle d(u,v)\leq d(w,z)\text{ if and only if }d(u,v)^{r}\leq d(w,z)^{r}.

Hence, for simplicity, we calculate the value of
a
, where
F_{r}(a)
is minimum, for
r=2
. For this, first we compute gradient
\nabla\mathsf{F}_{2}(a)
. We have

\displaystyle\nabla\mathsf{F}_{2}(a)=\frac{d}{da}\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}d(x,a)^{2}\,dP(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}\|x-a\|^{2}\,dP(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}\nabla_{a}\|x-a\|^{2}\,dP(x).

Since,

\nabla_{a}\|x-a\|^{2}=\nabla_{a}\left[(x-a)^{T}(x-a)\right]=-2(x-a),

where
x^{T}
gives the transpose of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have

\nabla\mathsf{F}_{2}(a)=\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}-2(x-a)\,dP(x)=-2\left(\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}x\,dP(x)-a\cdot P(A_{a|\alpha_{n}})\right).

Set this to zero to find the minimizer:

-2\left(\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}x\,dP(x)-a\cdot P(A_{a|\alpha_{n}})\right)=0.

Solve for
a
:

a=\frac{1}{P(A_{a|\alpha_{n}})}\int_{A_{a|\alpha_{n}}}x\,dP(x)=E(X\mid X\in A_{a|\alpha_{n}}).

Hence, the elements in an optimal set of
n
-points are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi regions. Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Remark 1.9
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\alpha
be an optimal set of
n
-points for
P
with respect to the constraint
S
and
a\in\alpha
. If the probability measure
P
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then it is easy to observe that
P(\partial M(a|\alpha))=0

\{(-1,0),(1,0)\}
forms an optimal set of two-points with constrained quantization error
V_{2}=\frac{1}{3}
.

Proof.

From the geometry, we see that the boundary of any two elements on the circle passes through the center of the circle. Thus, in an optimal set of two-points, one Voronoi region will contain the left half, and the other Voronoi region will contain the right half of the support of
P
. Hence, by the routine calculation, we can show that
\{(-1,0),(1,0)\}
forms an optimal set of two-points with constrained quantization error

V_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{-1}^{0}\rho((x,0),(-1,0))dx+\int_{0}^{1}\rho((x,0),(1,0))dx\Big)=\frac{1}{3}.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

6.
Quantization dimensions and quantization coefficients

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
equipped with a metric, and let
r\in(0,\infty)
. A quantization without any constraint is called an unconstrained quantization. In unconstrained quantization (see
[
7
]
), the numbers

(15)

\underline{D}_{r}(P):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log V_{n,r}(P)}\text{ and }\overline{D}_{r}(P):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log V_{n,r}(P)},

are called the
lower
and the
upper quantization dimensions
of the probability measure
P
of order
r
, respectively. If
\underline{D}_{r}(P)=\overline{D}_{r}(P)
, the common value is called the
quantization dimension
of
P
of order
r
and is denoted by
D_{r}(P)
. In unconstrained quantization (see
[
7
]
) for any
\kappa>0
, the two numbers
\liminf_{n}n^{\frac{r}{\kappa}}V_{n,r}(P)
and
\limsup_{n}n^{\frac{r}{\kappa}}V_{n,r}(P)
are, respectively, called the
\kappa
-dimensional lower
and the
upper quantization coefficients
for
P
. The quantization coefficients provide us with more accurate information about the asymptotics of the quantization error than the quantization dimension. In unconstrained case, it is known that for an absolutely continuous probability measure, the quantization dimension always exists and equals the Euclidean dimension of the support of
P
, and the quantization coefficient exists as a finite positive number (see
[
1
]
). If the
\kappa
-dimensional lower and the upper quantization coefficients for
P
are finite and positive, then
\kappa
equals the quantization dimension of
P
.

Remark 6.1
.

Unconstrained quantization error
V_{n,r}(P)
goes to zero as
n
tends to infinity (see
[
7
]
). This is not true in the case of constrained quantization. Constrained quantization error
V_{n,r}(P)
can approach to any nonnegative number as
n
tends to infinity, and it depends on the constraint
S
that occurs in the definition of constrained quantization error as given in (
1
). In this regard, below we give some examples.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
P
is uniform on its support
\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0\leq x\leq 2\text{ and }y=0\}
.
Let
V_{n}(P):=V_{n,2}(P)
be its constrained quantization error. If the elements in the optimal sets lie on the line
y=1
between the two elements
(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
(\frac{3}{2},1)
, then by Theorem
3.7
, for
n\geq 3
,

(16)

V_{n}(P)=\frac{25n^{2}-50n+26}{24(n-1)^{2}}\text{ implying }\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n}(P)=\frac{25}{24}.

If the elements in the optimal sets lie on the line
y=1
between the two elements
(0,1)
and
(\frac{28}{15},1)
, then by Theorem
3.9
, for
n\geq 8
,

(17)

V_{n}(P)=\frac{7(5788(n-1)n+3015)}{10125(1-2n)^{2}}\text{ implying }\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n}(P)=\frac{10129}{10125}.

On the other hand, if the elements in the optimal sets lie on the line
y=\sqrt{3}x
between the two elements
(0,0)
and
(2,2\sqrt{3})
, then by Corollary
3.3
, for
n\geq 2
,

(18)

V_{n}(P)=\frac{36n^{2}+49n-144}{12n^{3}}\text{ implying }\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n}(P)=0.

Moreover, notice that if
P
is a uniform distribution on a unit circle, and if the elements in an optimal set of
n
-points lie on a concentric circle with radius
a
, then by Theorem
4.3
, for
n\geq 3
,

(19)

V_{n}(P)=a^{2}+1-\frac{2an}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi}{n}\text{ implying }\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n}(P)=(a-1)^{2},

which is a nonnegative constant depending on the values of
a
.

Remark 6.2
.

If the support of
P
contains infinitely many elements, then the
n
th unconstrained quantization error is strictly decreasing. This fact is not true in constrained quantization, i.e., the
n
th constrained quantization error for a Borel probability measure can eventually remain constant as can be seen from Subsection
5.1
.

Remark 6.3
.

By Remark
6.1
and Remark
6.2
, we can conclude that there are some properties that are true in unconstrained quantization, but are not true in constrained quantization. These motivate us to adopt more general definitions of quantization dimension and quantization coefficient, as given below, which are meaningful both in constrained and unconstrained scenarios.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
equipped with a metric
d
, and let
r\in(0,\infty)
. Let
V_{n,r}(P)
be the
n
th constrained quantization error of order
r
for a given
S
that occurs in (
1
). Let
V_{n,r}(P)
be a strictly decreasing sequence.
Then, it converges to its exact lower bound, which is a nonnegative constant. Set

V_{\infty,r}(P):=\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n,r}(P).

Then,
(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))
is a strictly decreasing sequence of positive numbers such that

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=0.

Write

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\underline{D}_{r}(P):=\mathop{\liminf}\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))},\text{ and }\\
\overline{D}_{r}(P):=\mathop{\limsup}\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{r\log n}{-\log(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))}.\end{array}\right.

\underline{D}_{r}(P)
and
\overline{D}_{r}(P)
are called the
lower
and the
upper constrained quantization dimensions
of the probability measure
P
of order
r
with respect to the constraint
S
, respectively. If
\underline{D}_{r}(P)=\overline{D}_{r}(P)
, the common value is called the
constrained quantization dimension
of
P
of order
r
with respect to the constraint
S
and is denoted by
D_{r}(P)
. The constrained quantization dimension measures the speed how fast the specified measure of the constrained quantization error converges as
n
tends to infinity. A higher constrained quantization dimension suggests a faster convergence of the
n
th constrained quantization error.
For any
\kappa>0
, the two numbers

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{\kappa}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))\text{ and }\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{\kappa}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))

are, respectively, called the
\kappa
-dimensional lower
and the
upper
constrained quantization coefficients for
P
of order
r
with respect to the constraint
S
, respectively. If the
\kappa
-dimensional lower
and the
upper constrained quantization coefficients
for
P
exist and are equal, then we call it the
\kappa
-dimensional constrained quantization coefficient
for
P
of order
r
with respect to the constraint
S
.

The following proposition is a generalized version of
[
7
, Propoisition 11.3]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure, and
\underline{D}_{r}(P)\text{ and }\overline{D}_{r}(P)
be the lower and the upper constrained quantization dimensions, respectively.

(1)

If
0\leq s<\underline{D}_{r}(P)<t
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{s}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=+\infty\text{ and }\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{t}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=0.

(2)

If
0\leq s<\overline{D}_{r}(P)<t
, then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{s}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=+\infty\text{ and }\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{t}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=0.

Proof.

Let us first prove
(1)
. Let
0\leq s<\underline{D}_{r}(P)
. Choose
s^{\prime}\in(s,\underline{D}_{r}(P))
. Then, there exists an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
with

(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))<1\text{ and }\frac{r\log n}{-\log(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))}>s^{\prime}

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. This implies that

n^{r}\left(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{s^{\prime}}>1,

and so,

n^{r}\left(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{s}>\left(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{s-s^{\prime}}

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{r}\left(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{s}=+\infty,\text{ i.e., }\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{s}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=+\infty.

For
\underline{D}_{r}(P)<t
, there is a
t^{\prime}\in(\underline{D}_{r}(P),t)
and a subsequence
\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)
with

\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)<1\text{ and }\frac{r\log n_{k}}{-\log(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))}\leq t^{\prime}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that

n_{k}^{r}\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{t^{\prime}}\leq 1

and so,

n_{k}^{r}\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{t}\leq\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{t-t^{\prime}}.

Recall that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{t-t^{\prime}}=0
, and hence

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{r}\left(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{t}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}n_{k}^{r}\left(V_{n_{k},r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P)\right)^{t}=0,

yielding

\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{\frac{r}{t}}(V_{n,r}(P)-V_{\infty,r}(P))=0.

Thus, the proof of
(1)
is concluded. Proceeding in the similar way, we can prove
(2)
. Thus, the proof of the proposition is obtained.
∎

6.7.
Observations and Conclusions

(1)

In unconstrained quantization the elements in an optimal set, for a Borel probability measure
P
, are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi regions. This fact is not true in constrained quantization, for example, for the probability measure
P
, defined in Corollary
3.3
, the optimal set of two-points is obtained as
\{(\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{3}),(\frac{3}{8},\frac{3}{8}\sqrt{3})\}
, and the set of conditional expectations of the Voronoi regions is
\{(\frac{1}{2},0),(\frac{3}{2},0)\}
, i.e., the two sets are different.

(2)

In unconstrained quantization if the support of
P
contains at least
n
elements, then an optimal set of
n
-points contains exactly
n
elements. This fact is not true in constrained quantization. For example, from Subsection
5.1
, we see that if a Borel probability measure
P
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
has support the diameter of a circle and the constraint
S
is the circle, then the optimal sets of
n
-points containing exactly
n
elements exist only for
n=1
and
n=2
, and they do not exist for any
n\geq 3
, though the support has infinitely many elements.

(3)

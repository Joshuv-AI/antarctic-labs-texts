Wei Zhang

Abstract
. We survey recent developments on generalizing the Gross–Zagier formula to high dimensional Shimura varieties, with an emphasis on the Arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture and the relative trace formula approach.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 11G40, 14G35; Secondary 11F67, 11F70, 14C25

1.
Introduction

The seminal paper of Gross and Zagier
[
23
]
proves a formula relating the Néron–Tate heights of Heegner points on modular curves to the first central derivatives of certain L-functions.
The Gross–Zagier formula and the theorem of Kolyvagin on Euler systems together imply that the (rank part of the) Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture holds if the analytic rank of a modular elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
is (at most) one. In this article we survey some of the recent developments on generalizing the Gross–Zagier formula to high dimensional Shimura varieties, as well as their applications to the generalizations of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture formulated by Beilinson, Bloch, and Kato.

There have been a lot of works on a parallel topic, that is, the relation between automorphic periods and special values of L-functions. Let
F
be a global field and
G
a reductive group over
F
. Let
\pi
be a (tempered) cuspidal automorphic representation of
G
. Let
H\subset G
be a subgroup. A fundamental question in the theory of automorphic forms is to study
the (automorphic)
H
-period integral on
\pi
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{[H]}\varphi(h)\,dh,\quad\varphi\in\pi.

Here
[H]
denotes the automorphic quotient
H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A})
, where
\mathbb{A}
is the ring of adeles of
F
. For many pairs
(H,G)
, the period integrals are related
to special values of L-functions attached to
\pi
. Two notable examples are the pairs
(\mathrm{GL}_{n-1},\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n})
and
(\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n},\mathrm{GL}_{2n})
. The first is related to the Rankin-Selberg convolution L-function
[
27
]
, the second to the standard L-function for
\mathrm{GL}_{2n}

[
19
]
(see also
[
8
]
for a weighted version). These two examples involve
split
groups only; in particular, when
F
is a number field, the group
G
does not satisfy the following condition (unless
G
is commutative):

(1.2)

\displaystyle\text{$G(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})$ is compact modulo its center}.

The compactness of
G(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})
can often allow us to derive algebraicity of period integrals and, together with other inputs, to relate L-values to interesting arithmetic invariants of an appropriate Galois representation associated to
\pi
. As an example in the non-split case, Waldspurger considered

(1.3)

\displaystyle H=\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathbb{G}_{m},\quad G=B^{\times},

where
\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}
denotes the Weil restriction of scalars,
F^{\prime}
is a quadratic field extension of
F
, with an embedding into a quaternion algebra
B
over
F
. In the 1980s, Waldspurger
[
71
]
proved a formula of the following form (after suitably modifying the period integral to take into account the center of
G
)

\left|\int_{[H]}\varphi(h)\,dh\right|^{2}=L(\pi_{F^{\prime}},1/2)\times\text{``Local factors"},

where
\pi_{F^{\prime}}
denotes the base change of
\pi
to
F^{\prime}
.
The group
G(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})
is compact modulo its center if and only if
F
is totally real and
B
is non-split at all archimedean places. In this compact case the formula of Waldspurger played a crucial role in the study of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for (modular) elliptic curves over a totally real number field in the analytic rank zero case (for example, in Bertolini–Darmon’s variant of Kolyvagin systems
[
1
]
).

There has been a large number of (conjectural or proved) generalizations of Waldspurger’s formula to more general pairs
(H,G)
. Most relevant to this survey are the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture
[
20
]
for unitary groups and its refinement by Ichino–Ikeda. We refer to
[
91
, §2]
for a survey on previous results including
[
28
,
89
,
90
,
73
,
2
]
for
\pi
satisfying certain local conditions. Recently the complete results are obtained in
[
6
]
(in the “stable” case) and
[
5
]
(in the “endoscopic” case). We also refer to the articles by Beuzart-Plessis
[
4
]
and Chaudouard
[
9
]
on this topic.

This survey concerns an analogous question to (
1.1
) where one aims to upgrade the embedding of topological spaces
[H]\subset[G]
to one with richer structure. When
F
is a (suitable) number field, we expect such an upgrade when there are Shimura varieties associated to the groups
H
and
G
. The pioneering example is the Gross–Zagier formula
[
23
]
, proved about the same time as Waldspurger’s formula. In the Gross–Zagier formula, one has, for
F=\mathbb{Q}
and
F^{\prime}
an imaginary quadratic field,

H=\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m},\quad G=\mathrm{GL}_{2,\mathbb{Q}}.

Then the Gross–Zagier formula relates the Néron–Tate height pairing of the
\pi
-isotypical component of a special cycle (a divisor) attached to
H
to the central derivative of the L-function for the base change
\pi_{F^{\prime}}
. It applies to all
\pi
whose local components satisfies suitable conditions. In the early 2000s, Shouwu Zhang extended the Gross–Zagier formula from modular curves to a large class of Shimura curves (over general totally real fields)
[
82
,
83
]
; see also a different generalization to Shimura curves over
\mathbb{Q}
due to Kudla–Rapoport–Yang
[
34
]
. The most general form of the Gross–Zagier formula for Shimura curves was then obtained in the work of Xinyi Yuan, Shouwu Zhang and the author
[
75
]
after another ten years. We will recall a special case in §
2
; we refer to
[
88
, §3]
for a survey. As mentioned already, the Gross–Zagier formula has a direct application to the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Together with Kolyvagin’s method of Euler systems, it settles the rank part of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
(more generally, modular elliptic curves over totally real fields) when the analytic rank is at most one.

After
[
75
]
, a natural question is to generalize the Gross–Zagier formula for Shimura curves to higher dimensional Shimura varieties. The goal of these notes is to survey the recent developments on this topic, with an emphasis on the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad (AGGP) conjecture
[
20
]
for
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n+1)
and the relative trace formula approach initiated in
[
86
]
. The first proof of Waldspurger’s formula mostly relies on the theory of theta series, as does the overall strategy of the proof of the Gross–Zagier formula for modular or Shimura curves in
[
23
,
75
]
. Later Jacquet found a new proof of Waldspurger’s formula using his relative trace formula approach, and in
[
28
]
Jacquet and Rallis proposed a relative trace formula approach to prove the global Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for unitary groups (for the automorphic period integral and central L-values). Inspired by their relative trace formula, the author formulated an approach to attack the AGGP conjecture and reduced the conjecture to several local conjectures, notably the arithmetic fundamental lemma conjecture
[
85
,
86
,
45
]
and the arithmetic transfer conjectures
[
65
,
66
,
95
,
46
]
. Recent years have witnessed substantial progress in these local conjectures, see §
4
for a selection of related theorems.

Here is an outline the paper. In §
2
, we recall a special case of the Gross–Zagier formula for Shimura curves over
\mathbb{Q}
.
In §
3
, we first present the AGGP conjecture for unitary Shimura varieties and various variants (including a
p
-adic variant in §
3.8
). We have essentially restricted ourselves to unitary Shimura varieties with certain parahoric level structure at inert places. One of the main difficulty is the lack of regular integral models at deeper levels. We also recall the relative trace formula approach. In §
4
we will state the local conjectures and we update the recent progress on some of the key local questions since the author’s previous survey
[
91
]
: the proof of the arithmetic fundamental lemma conjecture for all
p
-adic fields (with
p
odd) and of arithmetic transfer conjecture in certain parahoric cases.
In §
5
we will briefly mention several other generalizations of the Gross–Zagier formula, including the AGGP conjecture in the
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
-case formulated by Yifeng Liu, and the arithmetic Rallis inner product formula. The latter generalizes the Gross–Zagier formula along the line of the aforementioned work of Kudla–Rapoport–Yang
[
34
]
, and relies on the recent progress on the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula which was envisioned by Kudla
[
30
]
and subsequently formulated as conjectures by Kudla and Rapoport in a series papers. We also present some examples of arithmetic applications to the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture in §
5.2
. In another direction, there is also the higher derivative version of Gross–Zagier formula over a functional field, see §
5.3
.

2.
Gross–Zagier formula for Shimura curves

For simplicity,
we only recall a mild generalization of the Gross–Zagier formula from modular curves
X_{0}(N)
in
[
23
]
to certain Shimura curves over
\mathbb{Q}
; the ramification conditions here can all be dropped, see
[
75
]
.

Let
N>1
be an integer. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[\sqrt{-D}]
be an imaginary quadratic extension with fundamental discriminant
-D<0
. For simplicity we assume
D>4
and
(D,N)=1
. This determines a unique factorization

(2.1)

\displaystyle N=N^{+}N^{-}

where the prime factors of
N^{+}
(
N^{-}
, resp.) are all split (inert, resp.) in
K
. Let us impose the
generalized Heegner hypothesis
:

N^{-}
is square-free with an
even
number of prime factors.

We consider the indefinite quaternion algebra
B
over
\mathbb{Q}
that is ramified precisely at all factors of
N^{-}
. Let
G=B^{\times}
viewed as an algebraic group over
\mathbb{Q}
. We have the usual Shimura datum attached to
G
. Consider the (compactified) Shimura curve
X_{N^{+},N^{-}}=X_{U}
where the compact open
U\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times})=B^{\times}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is defined by

U_{N^{+},N^{-}}=\prod_{\ell<\infty}U_{\ell},\quad U_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{0}(N),&\ell|N^{+},\\
\mathcal{O}_{B_{\ell}}^{\times},&\ell\nmid N^{+}.\end{cases}

Equivalently, we may consider an Eichler order
\mathcal{O}_{B,N^{+}}
in
\mathcal{O}_{B}
with level
N^{+}
and define

(2.2)

\displaystyle U_{N^{+},N^{-}}=(\mathcal{O}_{B,N^{+}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}.

We have an isomorphism for the complex analytic space

(2.3)

\displaystyle X_{N^{+},N^{-}}(\mathbb{C})=B(\mathbb{Q})\backslash(\mathcal{H}\times B(\mathbb{A}_{f}))/(\mathcal{O}_{B,N^{+}}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\cup\{\text{cusps}\}.

In particular, if
N^{-}=1
, the curve
X_{N^{+},N^{-}}
is the (compactified) classical modular curve
X_{0}(N^{+})
, whose complex points are
\Gamma_{0}(N^{+})\backslash\mathcal{H}
together with cusps.

The case
N^{-}=1
corresponds to the classical
Heegner hypothesis
in
[
23
]
:
every prime factor
\ell|N
is split in
K
.
Then there
exists an ideal
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, the ring of integers of
K
, such that

\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathcal{N}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}.

Then the elliptic curves
\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{O}_{K}
and
\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{N}^{-1}
are naturally related by a cyclic isogeny of degree
N
, therefore define a
\mathbb{C}
-point, denoted by
x(1)
, on
X_{0}(N)
. By the theory of complex multiplication, the point
x(1)
is defined over the Hilbert class field
H
of
K
. (This depends on a (non-unique) choice of an ideal
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
above; but they all yield the same Gross–Zagier formula below.) By class field theory, the Galois group
\Gal(H/K)
is isomorphic to the class group
\Pic(\mathcal{O}_{K})
. Similarly, under the generalized Heegner hypothesis, we can define a point
x(1)\in X_{N^{+},N^{-}}(H)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with conductor
N
. Consider a modular parameterization

\phi:X_{N^{+},N^{-}}\longrightarrow E.

If
N^{-}=1
we require this morphism to send
(\infty)
to
0
of
E
; in general we require that it pushes forward the class of the Hodge bundle (in the Picard group) to a multiple of the class of
0
of
E
.
Define

(2.4)

\displaystyle y(1)=\phi(x(1))\in E(H),\quad y_{K}=\tr_{H/K}\,y(1)\in E(K).

Let
f
be the newform of weight two and
\Gamma_{0}(N)
-level associated to the elliptic curve
E
.
Let
L(f/K,s)=L(E/K,s)
be the L-function (without the archimedean factor) attached to the elliptic curve
E
base changed to
K
; here we take the classical normalization for
L(f/K,s)
, i.e, the center of functional equation is at
s=1
. The generalized Heegner hypothesis ensures that the global root number

\epsilon(E/K)=-1.

Then the central value
L(f/K,1)=0
and the Gross–Zagier formula for
X_{N^{+},N^{-}}
is as follows:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}(f/K,1)}{(f,f)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|D|}}\frac{\langle{y_{K},y_{K}}\rangle_{\rm NT}}{\deg(\phi)},

where
\langle{y_{K},y_{K}}\rangle_{\rm NT}
is the Néron–Tate height pairing over
K
and
(f,f)
the Petersson inner product

(2.6)

\displaystyle(f,f):=8\pi^{2}\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathcal{H}}f(z)\overline{f(z)}dxdy=\int_{X_{0}(N)(\mathbb{C})}\omega_{f}\wedge\overline{i\omega}_{f},

where
\omega_{f}:=2\pi if(z)dz
.

This is proved by Gross–Zagier in
[
23
]
when
N^{-}=1
, in general by S. Zhang in
[
82
,
83
]
and Yuan–Zhang–Zhang in
[
75
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

To compare with
the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjectural formula for
L^{\prime}(E/K,1)
, we consider a modular parameterization, still denoted by
f
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle f:X_{0}(N)\to E,

which we assume maps the cusp
(\infty)
to zero.
The pull-back of the Néron differential
\omega
on
E
is
f^{\ast}\omega=c\cdot\omega_{f}
for a constant
c
. It is known that the constant
c
is an integer. If
f
is an optimal parameterization, it is called the Manin constant and conjecturally equal to
1
. By a theorem of Mazur, we have
p|c\Longrightarrow p|2N
(there are various improvements of Mazur’s theorem but for the sake of brevity we do not recall them).
Then an equivalent form of the formula (
2.5
) is

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}(E/K,1)}{|D|^{-1/2}\int_{E(\mathbb{C})}\omega\wedge\overline{\omega}}=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\deg(f)}{\deg(\phi)}\langle{y_{K},y_{K}}\rangle_{\rm NT}.

For comparison, the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture asserts that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{L^{\prime}(E/K,1)}{|D|^{-1/2}\int_{E(\mathbb{C})}\omega\wedge\overline{\omega}}=\frac{\prod_{v|N}m_{v}}{[E(K):\mathbb{Z}y_{K}]^{2}}\cdot\#\Sha_{E/K}\cdot\langle{y_{K},y_{K}}\rangle_{\rm NT},

where
m_{v}
denotes the local Tamagawa number at the bad places of
E/K
,
[E(K):\mathbb{Z}y_{K}]
denotes the index of the subgroup generated by
y_{K}\in E(K)
, and
\#\Sha_{E/K}
denotes the order of the Tate–Shafarevich group. Therefore, by (
2.8
) the conjectural formula (
2.9
) is equivalent to

\#\Sha_{E/K}=\frac{[E(K):\mathbb{Z}y_{K}]^{2}\cdot(\deg(f)/\deg(\phi))}{c^{2}\cdot\prod_{v|N}m_{v}}.

3.1.
Special pairs of Shimura data and special cycles

This subsection is largely from
[
91
]
. We recall the notion of
a special pair of Shimura data
, which is an enhancement of a spherical pair
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
to a homomorphism of Shimura data
(\mathrm{H},X_{\mathrm{H}})\to(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}})
.

Let
\mathbb{S}
be the torus
\mathrm{R}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
over
\mathbb{R}
(i.e., we view
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as an
\mathbb{R}
-group). Recall that a Shimura datum
\bigl(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}}\bigr)
consists of a reductive group
\mathrm{G}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and a
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{R})
-conjugacy class
X_{\mathrm{G}}=\{h_{\mathrm{G}}\}
of
\mathbb{R}
-group homomorphisms
h_{\mathrm{G}}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
(sometimes called Shimura homomorphisms) satisfying Deligne’s list of axioms
[
11
, 1.5]
. In particular,
X_{\mathrm{G}}
is a Hermitian symmetric domain.

Definition 3.1
.

A
special pair
of Shimura data is a homomorphism
[
11
, 1.14]
between two Shimura data

\textstyle{\delta\colon\bigl(\mathrm{H},X_{\mathrm{H}}\bigr)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bigl(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}}\bigr)}

such that

(i)

the homomorphism
\delta:\mathrm{H}\to\mathrm{G}
is injective such that the pair
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
is spherical, and

(ii)

the dimensions of
X_{\mathrm{H}}
and
X_{\mathrm{G}}
(as complex manifolds) satisfy

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X_{\mathrm{H}}=\bigg\lfloor\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X_{\mathrm{G}}}{2}\bigg\rfloor.

Remark 3.2
.

The condition on the pair
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
being spherical may be an artifact. There are certainly examples of non-spherical pairs that result to Shimura data satisfying the two conditions above. However, all the “interesting” (as of today) examples are spherical and there are already many of them. Moreover, interesting automorphic period integrals seem mostly (if not all) attached to spherical pairs.

Remark 3.3
.

It seems an interesting question to enumerate special pairs of Shimura data. In fact, we may consider the analog of special pairs of Shimura data in the context of
local Shimura data
. It seems more realistic to enumerate the pairs in the local situation. Moreover, it is only a matter of interest in this paper that we impose the dimension equality in (ii). There are certainly other interesting cases where the equality does not hold.

For a Shimura datum
(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}})
we have a projective system of
Shimura varieties

\{\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(\mathrm{G})\}
, indexed by compact open subgroups
K\subset\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, of smooth quasi–projective varieties (for neat
K
) defined over a number field
E
—the reflex field of
(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}})
, viewed as a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
.

For a special pair of Shimura data
(\mathrm{H},X_{\mathrm{H}})\to(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}})
, compact open subgroups
K_{\mathrm{H}}\subset\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and
K_{\mathrm{G}}\subset\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
such that
K_{\mathrm{H}}\subset K_{\mathrm{G}}
, we have a finite morphism (over the reflex field of
(\mathrm{H},X_{\mathrm{H}})
)

\delta_{K_{\mathrm{H}},K_{\mathrm{G}}}:\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathrm{H}}}(\mathrm{H})\to\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathrm{G}}}(\mathrm{G}).

The cycle
z_{K_{\mathrm{H}},K_{\mathrm{G}}}:=\delta_{K_{\mathrm{H}},K_{\mathrm{G}},\ast}[\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathrm{H}}}(\mathrm{H})]
on
\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathrm{G}}}(\mathrm{G})
will be called the
special cycle
(for the level
(K_{\mathrm{H}},K_{\mathrm{G}})
). Very often we choose
K_{\mathrm{H}}=K_{\mathrm{G}}\cap\mathrm{H}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
in which case we simply denote the special cycle by
z_{K_{\mathrm{G}}}
.

There are two cases depending on the parity of the dimension of
\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathrm{G}}}(\mathrm{G})
:

•

When
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X_{\mathrm{G}}
is even, the special cycles are in the middle dimension. They can very often account for most Tate cycles on
\mathrm{Sh}_{K_{\mathrm{G}}}(\mathrm{G})
and are therefore related to poles of L-functions at the near central point
s=1
. They are related to automorphic period integrals and distinguished automorphic representations as in many works of Jacquet. One example is due to Harder, Langlands, and Rapoport
[
24
]
on Tate cycles on Hilbert modular surfaces, which has led to many generalizations.

•

When
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X_{\mathrm{G}}
is odd, the special cycles are just below the middle dimension, and we will say that they are in the
arithmetic middle dimension
(in the sense that, once extending both Shimura varieties to suitable integral models, we obtain cycles in the middle dimension). Their height pairings are expected to be related to the central derivative of L-functions and therefore can be used to attack Beilinson and Bloch’s generalizations of Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture to general varieties, at least when the relevant L-function has vanishing order one.

Below we focus on the case where the special cycles are in the arithmetic middle dimension.

3.2.
The main examples for this survey

We will focus on the Gan–Gross–Prasad pair for unitary groups; for more examples see §
5
.

3.2.1.
Gross–Zagier pair

In the case of the Gross–Zagier formula
[
23
]
, one considers an embedding of an
imaginary
quadratic field
F^{\prime}
into
\mathrm{Mat}_{2,\mathbb{Q}}
(the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra of
2\times 2
-matrices), and the induced embedding

\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2,\mathbb{Q}}.

Note that
\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as
\mathbb{R}
-groups (upon a choice of embedding
F^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
). This defines
h_{\mathrm{H}}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and its composition with the embedding
\mathrm{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
defines
h_{\mathrm{G}}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathbb{R}}
. We obtain a special pair
(\mathrm{H},X_{\mathrm{H}})\to(\mathrm{G},X_{\mathrm{G}})
,
where

\dim X_{\mathrm{G}}=1,\quad\quad\dim X_{\mathrm{H}}=0.

In the general case, we replace
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
by a CM extension
F^{\prime}/F
of a totally real number field
F
, and replace
\mathrm{Mat}_{2,\mathbb{Q}}
by a quaternion algebra
B
over
F
that is ramified at all but one archimedean places of
F
. In this generality the Gross–Zagier formula is treated in
[
75
]
.

3.2.2.
Gan–Gross–Prasad pair

Let
F
be a number field, and let
F^{\prime}=F
in the orthogonal case and
F^{\prime}
a quadratic extension of
F
in the Hermitian case. Let
W_{n}
be a non-degenerate orthogonal space or Hermitian space with
F^{\prime}
-dimension
n
. Let
W_{n-1}\subset W_{n}
be a non-degenerate subspace of codimension one. Let
\mathrm{G}_{i}
be
{\mathrm{SO}}(W_{i})
or
\mathrm{U}(W_{i})
for
i=n-1,n
, and
\delta:\mathrm{G}_{n-1}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{G}_{n}
the induced embedding. Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{G}_{n-1}\times\mathrm{G}_{n},\quad\,\quad\mathrm{H}=\mathrm{G}_{n-1},

with the “diagonal” embedding
\Delta:\mathrm{H}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{G}
(i.e., the graph of
\delta
). The pair
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
is spherical and we call it the
Gan–Gross–Prasad pair
.

Now we impose the following conditions.

(i)

F
is a totally real number field, and in the Hermitian case
F^{\prime}/F
is a CM (=totally imaginary quadratic) extension.

(ii)

For an archimedean place
\varphi\in\Hom(F,\mathbb{R})
, denote by
\sgn_{\varphi}(W)
the signature of
W\otimes_{F,\varphi}\mathbb{R}
as an orthogonal or Hermitian space over
F^{\prime}\otimes_{F,\varphi}\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a distinguished real place
\varphi_{0}\in\Hom(F,\mathbb{R})
such that

\sgn_{\varphi}(W_{n})=\begin{cases}(2,n-2),&\varphi=\varphi_{0}\\
(0,n),&\varphi\in\Hom(F,\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\varphi_{0}\}\end{cases}

in the orthogonal case, and

\sgn_{\varphi}(W_{n})=\begin{cases}(1,n-1),&\varphi=\varphi_{0}\\
(0,n),&\varphi\in\Hom(F,\mathbb{R})\setminus\{\varphi_{0}\}\end{cases}

in the Hermitian case. In addition, the quotient
W_{n}/W_{n-1}
is negative definite at every

(so the signature of

is given by similar formulas).

Then Gan, Gross, and Prasad

define Shimura data that enhance

to a homomorphism of Shimura data

, where the dimensions are

\begin{cases}\dim X_{\mathrm{G}}=2n-5,\quad\dim X_{\mathrm{H}}=n-3,\quad\text{in the orthogonal case,}\\
\dim X_{\mathrm{G}}=2n-3,\quad\dim X_{\mathrm{H}}=n-2,\quad\text{in the Hermitian case}.\end{cases}

The reflex field is

(
=F
in the orthogonal case) via the distinguished embedding

3.3.
The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture

3.3.1.
Height pairings

Let
X
be a smooth proper variety over a number field
E
, and let
{\mathrm{Ch}}^{i}(X)
be the group of codimension-
i
algebraic cycles on
X
modulo rational equivalence. We have a cycle class map

{\mathrm{cl}}_{i}\colon{\mathrm{Ch}}^{i}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to H^{2i}(X),

where
H^{2i}(X)
is the Betti cohomology

H^{*}\bigl(X(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{C}\bigr).

The kernel is the group of cohomologically trivial cycles, denoted by
{\mathrm{Ch}}^{i}(X)_{0}
.

Conditional on certain standard conjectures on algebraic cycles, there is a height pairing defined by Beilinson and Bloch,

(3.2)

(\text{~,~})_{\mathrm{BB}}\colon{\mathrm{Ch}}^{i}(X)_{\mathbb{Q},0}\times{\mathrm{Ch}}^{d+1-i}(X)_{\mathbb{Q},0}\to\mathbb{R},\quad d=\dim X.

This is unconditionally defined when
i=1
(the Néron–Tate height), or when
X
is an abelian variety
[
36
]
. In general, there are several approaches to the definitions (conjecturally they all yield the same height pairing). In one of the approaches, if there exists a regular integral model and the cycles have an appropriate extension, so-called “flat extension”, or “admissible extension” (à la Shouwu Zhang) to the integral models, then the height pairing can be defined in terms of the arithmetic intersection theory of Arakelov and Gillet–Soulé
[
21
, §4.2.10]
, see the paper of Künnemann
[
35
]
for a precise statement. In particular, when there exists a smooth proper model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
over
O_{E}
(this is also true for Deligne–Mumford (DM) stacks
X
and
\mathcal{X}
), cf.
[
67
, §6.1]
, one can define the height pairing unconditionally. In general it remains a tantalizing question how to make an unconditional height pairing.

3.3.2.
The arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture

We consider the special cycle in the Gan–Gross–Prasad setting §
3.2.2
, which we also call the
arithmetic diagonal cycle

[
67
]
. We will state a version of the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture assuming some standard conjectures on algebraic cycles (cf.
[
67
, §6]
), in particular, that we have the height pairing (
3.2
).

For each
K\subset\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, one can construct “Hecke–Kunneth” projectors that project the total cohomology of the Shimura variety
\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(\mathrm{G})
(or its toroidal compactification) to the odd-degree part (cf.
[
67
, §6.2]
in the Hermitian case; the same proof works in the orthogonal case). Then we apply this projector to define a cohomologically trivial cycle

[

We form the colimit

{\mathrm{Ch}}^{n-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Sh}(\mathrm{G})\bigr)_{0}:=\varinjlim_{K\subset\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A}_{f})}{\mathrm{Ch}}^{n-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Sh}_{K}(\mathrm{G})\bigr)_{0}.

The (conditionally defined) height pairing (

We are ready to state the arithmetic Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture

, parallel to the global GGP conjecture for the central L-value.

Conjecture 3.4
.

Let

In the orthogonal case with

-adic L-function
\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{\it p}(s,\pi)
times the local canonical invariant forms given by Ichino–Ikeda (
3.7
),

2^{-\beta_{\pi}}e_{p}(\pi)^{-1}\mathscr{L}^{\prime}_{\it p}(1/2,\pi)\prod_{v<\infty}\alpha_{v}(\varphi_{v},\varphi^{\vee}_{v}),

possibly up to certain local modification
e_{p}(\pi)^{-1}
at places above
p
. In particular, this formula would imply that the class
\int_{z_{K,0}}\varphi^{\vee}\in H^{1}_{f}(F,\rho_{\pi}^{\vee})
in the Bloch–Kato Selmer group does not vanish for some
\varphi\in\pi_{f}
if and only if
\ord\mathscr{L}_{\it p}(s,\pi)=1
and the local canonical invariant forms do not vanish.

In
[
13
]
we prove this conjectural formula when all the
p
-adic places are split in
F^{\prime}/F
, at least for
\pi
with mild ramifications and with good ordinary reduction at all
p
-adic places. The overall strategy is similar to the relative trace formula approach: we study the
p
-adic analog of (
3.3
)

(3.27)

\displaystyle\Int_{{\it p}}(f)=\Big(R(f)\ast z_{K,0},\,\,z_{K,0}\Big)_{{\it p}},\quad f\in\mathscr{H}\left(\mathrm{G},K\right)

and a
p
-adic interpolation of the Jacquet–Rallis RTF in §
3.6
. Assume that the cycles have disjoint support. Then the
p
-adic height pairing also localizes into a sum of local heights over all the (non-archimedean) places. For almost all places away from
p
, the local pairing is essentially the same as the intersection pairing in Theorem
3.14
(except replacing
\log q_{v}
in (
3.21
) by a
p
-adic
\log_{p}q_{v}
). For the remaining places away from (hyperspecial)
p
and mostly the places above
p
, substantial work is needed and is beyond the scope of this notes. In some way, the difficulty for the local
p
-adic height at
p
-adic places resembles that for the local
\mathbb{R}
-valued height (
3.2
) at archimedean places. Under the ordinary (at
p
-adic places) hypothesis, we are able to show a suitable limit of the local heights at
p
-adic places vanishes, resembling the treatment by Perrin-Riou of the
p
-adic Gross–Zagier formula.

4.
AFL and AT conjecture for local Shimura varieties

In this section we present more detail on the two local questions, namely the arithmetic analogs of the fundamental lemma and of (smooth) transfer, which are called the arithmetic fundamental lemma (AFL) and arithmetic transfer (AT).

4.1.
Unitary Rapoport–Zink spaces

We first recall the unitary Rapoport–Zink (RZ) spaces, a local analog of CM cycles, local KR divisors, and then we state the AFL conjecture, cf.
[
66
, §4]
and
[
93
, §3]
.

Let
F^{\prime}/F
be an unramified quadratic extension of
p
-adic local fields with
p

odd
and let
n\geq 1
. Denote by
q
the residue cardinality of
O_{F}
.
Let
\breve{F}
denote the completion of a maximal unramified extension of
F
. For
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
-schemes
S
(i.e. a
O_{\breve{F}}
-scheme on which
p
is locally nilpotent), we consider triples
(X,\iota,\lambda)
, where

(1)

X
is a
p
-divisible group of absolute height
2nd
and dimension
n
over
S
, where
d:=[F:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
,

(2)

\iota
is an action of
O_{F^{\prime}}
such that the induced action of
O_{F}
on
\Lie X
is via the structure morphism
O_{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}
, and

(3)

\lambda:X\to X^{\vee}
is a principal (
O_{F}
-relative) polarization.

Hence
(X,\iota|_{O_{F}})
is a strict
O_{F}
-module of relative height
2n
and dimension
n
. We require that the Rosati involution
\Ros_{\lambda}
induces on
O_{F}
the non-trivial Galois automorphism in
\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)
, denoted by
O_{F^{\prime}}\ni a\mapsto\overline{a}
, and that the
Kottwitz condition
of signature
(n-1,1)
is satisfied, i.e.

(4.1)

\charac\bigl(\iota(a)\mid\Lie X;\,T\bigr)=(T-a)^{n-1}(T-\overline{a})\in\mathcal{O}_{S}[T]\quad\text{for all}\quad a\in O_{F^{\prime}}.

An isomorphism
(X,\iota,\lambda)(X^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
between two such triples is an
O_{F^{\prime}}
-linear isomorphism
\varphi\colon XX^{\prime}
such that
\varphi^{*}(\lambda^{\prime})=\lambda
.

Over the residue field
\mathbb{F}
of
O_{\breve{F}}
, there is a triple
(\mathbb{X}_{n},\iota_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}})
such that
\mathbb{X}_{n}
is supersingular, unique up to
O_{F^{\prime}}
-linear quasi-isogeny compatible with the polarization. We fix such a triple which we call the
framing object
. Then there is the Rapoport–Zink formal moduli scheme
\mathcal{N}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{n,F^{\prime}/F}
associated to the unitary group for the quasi-split

n
-dimensional Hermitian
F^{\prime}
-vector space.
By definition
\mathcal{N}_{n}
represents the functor over
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
that associates to each
S
the set of isomorphism classes of quadruples
(X,\iota,\lambda,\rho)
over
S
, where the last entry is an
O_{F}
-linear quasi-isogeny of height zero defined over the special fiber
\overline{S}:=S\times_{\Spf O_{\breve{F}}}\Spec\mathbb{F}
,

\rho\colon X\times_{S}\overline{S}\to\mathbb{X}_{n}\times_{\Spec\mathbb{F}}\overline{S},

such that
\rho^{*}((\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}})_{\overline{S}})=\lambda_{\overline{S}}
. The map
\rho
is called the
framing
.
The formal scheme
\mathcal{N}_{n}
is formally locally of finite type and formally smooth over
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
of relative dimension
n-1
.

The group of quasi-automorphisms of the framing object is

\Aut^{\circ}(\mathbb{X}_{n},\iota_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}})=\{g\in\End^{\circ}_{F}(\mathbb{X}_{n})\mid\ g^{\vee}\circ\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}\circ g=\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}\}.

The condition
g^{\vee}\circ\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}\circ g=\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}
may also be formulated as
gg^{*}=\mathrm{id}
, where
g\mapsto g^{\ast}=\Ros_{\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}}(g)
denotes the Rosati involution induced by the polarization
\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}
. Then
\Aut^{\circ}(\mathbb{X}_{n},\iota_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}})
acts on
\mathcal{N}_{n}
by changing the framing:

g\cdot(X,\iota,\lambda,\rho)=(X,\iota,\lambda,g\circ\rho).

Another description is as follows. Taking
n=1
, there is a unique triple
(\mathbb{E},\iota_{\mathbb{E}},\lambda_{\mathbb{E}})
over
\mathbb{F}
with signature
(1,0)
. Set

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{n}:=\Hom^{\circ}_{O_{F^{\prime}}}(\mathbb{E},\mathbb{X}_{n}),

called the space of
special homomorphisms
. It has the structure of an
n
-dimensional Hermitian
F^{\prime}
-vector space with respect to the Hermitian form

\langle{x,y}\rangle=\lambda_{\mathbb{E}}^{-1}\circ y^{\vee}\circ\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}}\circ x\in\End^{\circ}_{F^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})\simeq F^{\prime}.

It is the unique (up to isomorphism)
n
-dimensional Hermitian space that does
not
contain a self-dual
O_{F}
-lattice.
Then there is a natural isomorphism

(4.3)

\Aut^{\circ}(\mathbb{X}_{n},\iota_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}})\cong\mathrm{U}\bigl(\mathbb{V}_{n}\bigr)(F),

where
\Aut^{\circ}(\mathbb{X}_{n},\iota_{\mathbb{X}_{n}},\lambda_{\mathbb{X}_{n}})
acts by composition on
\mathbb{V}_{n}
.

We also define a broader class of RZ spaces
\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[t]}
for an integer
0\leq t\leq n
. We only need to replace the principal polarization in the triple
(X,\iota,\lambda)
by the following variant:

(3)

\lambda:X\to X^{\vee}
is a polarization such that
\ker(\lambda)\subset X[\varpi]
has height
q^{2t}
.

In particular, we have
\mathcal{N}_{n}=\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[0]}
.
The resulting RZ space
\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[t]}
is formally locally of finite type and of strictly semi-stable reduction over
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
with relative dimension
n-1

[
10
,
22
]
. The case of
t=1
is of special interest, termed as the “almost unramified level” in
[
66
]
, see also §
4.4
below. In general
\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[t]}
is attached to a parahoric subgroup fixing a vertex lattice of type
t
in a Hermitian space
V
whose discriminant has valuation
\equiv t\mod 2
. Then the space
\mathbb{V}_{n}
(depending on
t
but we suppress
t
from the notation) of special homomorphisms defined by (
4.2
) has the structure of a Hermitian space of discriminant with valuation
\equiv 1+t\mod 2
. Moreover, there is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[t]}\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[n-t]}

[
95
, §5.1]
. In particular we have
\mathcal{N}_{1}^{[0]}\simeq\mathcal{N}_{1}^{[1]}
. When
n=2
,
\mathcal{N}_{2}^{[0]}\simeq\mathcal{N}_{2}^{[2]}
is isomorphic to the Lubin–Tate space of height two, and
\mathcal{N}_{2}^{[1]}
is isomorphic to Deligne’s semistable integral model of the Drinfeld half plane
\widehat{\Omega}_{2}

[
33
]
.

4.2.
The AFL conjecture/theorem

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}=\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\times_{\Spf O_{\breve{F}}}\mathcal{N}_{n}
. Then
\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}
admits an action by
G(F)
where we denote now
G=\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n-1})\times\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})
.

There is a natural closed embedding
\delta\colon\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{N}_{n}
(a local analog of the closed embedding (
3.9
)), see (
4.15
) below for more detail. Let

\textstyle{\Delta\colon\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}}

be the graph morphism of
\delta
. We denote by
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}
the image of
\Delta
. For
g\in\mathrm{G}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
, we consider the intersection number on
\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}
of
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}
with its translate
g\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}
, defined through the derived tensor product of the structure sheaves (see also the beginning of §
3.7
),

(4.4)

\Int(g):=\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}},g\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}\right)_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}}:=\chi\left({\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}},\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}}}\mathcal{O}_{g\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}}\right).

Here, for a finite complex
\mathcal{F}
of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}}
-modules, we define its Euler–Poincaré characteristic as

\chi(\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n},\mathcal{F})=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i+j}\mathrm{len}_{O_{\breve{F}}}H^{j}(\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n},H_{i}(\mathcal{F}))

if the lengths are all finite. The regularity of
g
implies that the (formal) schematic intersection
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}\cap g\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}
is a
proper scheme
over
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
, and hence the finiteness of the Euler–Poincaré characteristic.

Recall from (
3.15
) the derivative of the orbital integral.

Theorem 4.1

(Arithmetic Fundamental Lemma (AFL))
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}^{\prime}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
match an element
g\in\mathrm{G}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then

\omega(\gamma)\operatorname{\partial Orb}\left(\gamma,{\bf 1}_{\mathrm{G}^{\prime}(O_{F})}\right)=-2\,\Int(g)\cdot\log q.

Here
\omega(\gamma)\in\{\pm 1\}
is an explicit transfer factor (see
[
66
]
).

For
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the theorem is proved
[
93
]
in large residue characteristic. The general case is established in
[
61
]
in large (meaning
p\geq n
) residue characteristic and in
[
95
]
in any
odd
residue characteristic. A survey on the conjecture can be found in
[
87
]
, and a survey on the proof of AFL can be found in
[
92
]
; see also Remark
4.6
.

This statement was conjectured in
[
86
]
, where the low rank case of
n=2,3
was proved (a simplified proof was given by Mihatsch in
[
60
]
).
Rapoport, Terstiege, and the author in
[
68
]
proved a special case, the so-called
minuscule
case, for
p
large comparing to
n
(a simplified proof was given by Li and Zhu in
[
47
]
).

Remark 4.2
.

Note that in the formulation of AFL we have restricted ourselves to odd residue characteristic
p
. This is caused by the same assumption in the theory of RZ spaces
[
69
]
. We expect the statement in the AFL conjecture to also hold when
p=2
.

We refer to
[
66
, §4]
for some other equivalent formulations of the AFL conjectures. Moreover, in
[
94
]
we also formulated an AFL conjecture for the general Bessel cycles, generalizing the arithmetic diagonal cycle
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}
.
However, there are no global analogs of these local cycles due to the lack of relevant Shimura varieties. The corresponding FL conjecture was formulated by Yifeng Liu in
[
51
]
, which is still unproven.

4.3.
The AFL conjecture of Liu in the Fourier–Jacobi
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
case

A closely related statement is the AFL conjecture in the Fourier–Jacobi
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
case, formulated by Yifeng Liu
[
54
]
. This statement is called the semi-Lie version of AFL conjecture in
[
93
,
61
,
95
]
and is shown to be equivalent to the AFL (the so-called “group version”) in Theorem
4.1
. In fact the interplay between these two statements is crucial to the inductive proof of both simutanously!

In
[
31
]
, Kudla and Rapoport have defined for every non-zero
u\in\mathbb{V}_{n}
a special divisor
\mathcal{Z}(u)
on
\mathcal{N}_{n}
, which for simplicity will be called a KR divisor. For its definition, note that
\mathcal{N}_{1}\cong\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
, so
(\mathbb{E},\iota_{\mathbb{E}},\lambda_{\mathbb{E}})
deforms to a unique triple
(\mathcal{E},\iota_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}})
over
O_{\breve{F}}
, called its
canonical lift
. In other words, this (with the
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}
for the deformation) is the universal object over
\mathcal{N}_{1}
with Galois conjugated
O_{F^{\prime}}
-action. Then the KR-divisor
\mathcal{Z}(u)
is defined as the locus where the quasi-homomorphism
u\colon\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{X}_{n}
lifts to a homomorphism from
\mathcal{E}
to the universal object over
\mathcal{N}_{n}
. By
[
31
, Prop. 3.5]
,
\mathcal{Z}(u)
is a relative Cartier divisor (or empty). It follows from the definition that if
g\in\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})(F)
, then

(4.5)

\displaystyle g\mathcal{Z}(u)=\mathcal{Z}(gu).

The KR divisor plays a fundamental role in the formulation of the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula, where one is interested in the intersection of
n
KR divisors, see (
5.1
) below. It also appears naturally in Liu’s formulation of the AFL conjecture in the Fourier–Jacobi case, as we explain now.
For simplicity we will write
\mathcal{N}_{n}\times\mathcal{N}_{n}
for the fiber product
\mathcal{N}_{n}\times_{\Spf O_{\breve{F}}}\mathcal{N}_{n}
.
For
g\in\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})(F)
, let
\Gamma_{g}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{n}\times\mathcal{N}_{n}
be the graph of the automorphism of
\mathcal{N}_{n}
induced by
g
.
The fixed point locus of
g
is defined as the intersection

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n}^{g}:=\Gamma_{g}\,\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n}},

viewed as a closed formal subscheme of
\mathcal{N}_{n}
.
We also form the
derived fixed point locus
, denoted by
\,{}^{\mathbb{L}}\!\mathcal{N}^{g}_{n}
, i.e. the derived tensor product

(4.7)

\,{}^{\mathbb{L}}\!\mathcal{N}^{g}_{n}:=\Gamma_{g}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\cap}}\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n}}:=\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n}\times\mathcal{N}_{n}}}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n}}}

viewed as an element in the K-group
K_{0}^{\mathcal{N}_{n}^{g}}(\mathcal{N}_{n})
with support, cf.
[
93
, Appendix B]
.

For a pair
(g,u)\in(\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})\times\mathbb{V}_{n})(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
(here “
\mathrm{reg}
” indicates “regular semisimple” in the relative sense, i.e.,
\{g^{i}u\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
form a basis of the vector space
\mathbb{V}_{n}
), we now set

(4.8)

\Int(g,u):=\chi\left({\mathcal{N}_{n}},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}(u)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n}}}\,\,^{\mathbb{L}}\!\mathcal{N}_{n}^{g}\right).

When
(g,u)
is regular semi-simple, the intersection
\mathcal{Z}(u)\cap\mathcal{N}^{g}_{n}
is a proper
scheme
over
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
and hence the right-hand side of (
4.8
) is finite.
The number
\Int(g,u)
depends only on the
\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})(F)
-orbit of
(g,u)\in(\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})\times\mathbb{V}_{n})(F)
. There is an equivalent definition that does not involve the derived fixed point locus
\,{}^{\mathbb{L}}\!\mathcal{N}^{g}_{n}
(cf.
[
93
, Rem. 3.1]
),

(4.9)

\Int(g,u)=\chi\left({\mathcal{N}_{n}\times\mathcal{N}_{n}},\,\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma_{g}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n}\times\mathcal{N}_{n}}}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{\mathcal{Z}(u)}}\right).

To define the analytic side, we consider the symmetric space

(4.10)

S_{n}:=\{\,\gamma\in\mathrm{R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n}\mid\gamma\overline{\gamma}=1_{n}\,\},

and the
F
-vector space

(4.11)

V^{\prime}_{n}=F^{n}\times(F^{n})^{\ast},

where
(F^{n})^{\ast}=\Hom_{F}(F^{n},F)
denotes the
F
-linear dual space. For convenience we will identify
F^{n}
(resp.
(F^{n})^{\ast}
) with the space of column vectors (resp. row vectors). With the tautological pairing,
we will view
V_{n}^{\prime}
as an
(F\times F)/F
-hermitian space.
Let
\mathrm{GL}_{n,F}

act (diagonally) on the product
S_{n}\times V^{\prime}_{n}
,

h\cdot(\gamma,(u_{1},u_{2}))=(h^{-1}\gamma h,(h^{-1}u_{1},u_{2}h)).

For
(\gamma,u^{\prime})\in(S_{n}\times V_{n}^{\prime})(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
,
\Phi^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}((S_{n}\times V_{n}^{\prime})(F))
, and
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the orbital integral

(4.12)

\Orb((\gamma,u^{\prime}),\Phi^{\prime},s):=\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}\Phi^{\prime}(h\cdot(\gamma,u^{\prime}))\lvert\det h\rvert^{s}\eta(h)\,dh,

with special values

(4.13)

\displaystyle\Orb((\gamma,u^{\prime}),\Phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\Orb((\gamma,u^{\prime}),\Phi^{\prime},0),\ \ \text{and}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\partial Orb}((\gamma,u^{\prime}),\Phi^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Orb((\gamma,u^{\prime}),\Phi^{\prime},s).

Theorem 4.3

(AFL, semi-Lie algebra version)
.

Suppose that
(\gamma,u^{\prime})\in(S_{n}\times V_{n}^{\prime})({F})_{\mathrm{reg}}
matches the element
(g,u)\in(\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n})\times\mathbb{V}_{n})(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then

\omega(\gamma,u^{\prime})\operatorname{\partial Orb}\bigl((\gamma,u^{\prime}),\mathbf{1}_{(S_{n}\times V_{n}^{\prime})(O_{F})}\bigr)=-\Int(g,u)\cdot\log q.

Here,
\omega(\gamma,u^{\prime})\in\{\pm 1\}
is the transfer factor
[
93
, (2.13)]
.

Though our formulation appears to differ from that of Liu in
[
54
]
, they are essentially equivalent.
Note that the factor 2 in Theorem
4.1
disappears here due to the change of the orbital integrals in the LHS.

The case of a fixed
u
with
(u,u)\in O_{F}^{\times}
is equivalent to the AFL conjecture in Theorem
4.1
, cf. also
[
93
, Conj. 3.2]
. In that case there is a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{Z}(u)\simeq\mathcal{N}_{n-1}.

By
[
93
, Prop. 4.12]
,
the intersection number (
4.9
) is equal to the one considered in
[
86
]
,

\chi\left({\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}},\,\mathcal{O}_{(1,g)\cdot\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}}}\mathcal{O}_{\Delta_{\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}}\right),

and
the orbital integral reduces to the one in
loc. cit.
as well, and hence Theorem
4.3
implies Theorem
4.1
. It is less trivial to show in
[
93
,
61
,
95
]
that Theorem
4.1
with
n+1
replacing
n
implies Theorem
4.3
.

4.4.
Arithmetic Transfer Conjectures

We finally discuss analogs of the smooth transfer in the arithmetic context over a
p
-adic field. Let
F^{\prime}/F
be as above. Fix an integer
0\leq t\leq n-1
and
e\in\{0,1\}
. Then there is a natural embedding
\delta\colon\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[t+e]}
. In terms of the moduli functors,
\delta
sends a quadruple

(4.15)

\displaystyle(X,\iota_{X},\lambda_{X},\rho_{X})\in\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}(S)\mapsto(X\times\mathcal{E},\iota_{X}\times\iota_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{X}\times\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},\rho_{X}\times\varpi^{e}\rho_{\mathcal{E}})

where
(\mathcal{E},\iota_{\mathcal{E}},\lambda_{\mathcal{E}},\rho_{\mathcal{E}})
is the canonical lift.
Let
\Delta\colon\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}
be the graph morphism of
\delta
.

Consider the product
\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}=\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}\times_{\Spf O_{\breve{F}}}\mathcal{N}_{n}^{[t+e]}
. It is regular precisely when (at least) one of the two
factors is formally smooth over
\Spf O_{\breve{F}}
; these cases were considered in
[
66
]
. In general, Zhiyu Zhang resolves the singularities of
\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}
by blowing up a certain Weil divisor of
\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}
in
[
95
]
. The resulting space is regular and denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n-1,n}
. The action of
\mathrm{G}(F)=(\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n-1})\times\mathrm{U}(\mathbb{V}_{n}))(F)
lifts to one on
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n-1,n}
. Moreover, taking the strict transform does not change
\Delta_{\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}}
and hence the morphism
\Delta
lifts to
\widetilde{\Delta}:\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}\to\widetilde{}\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}
. Similar to (
4.4
) we may then define for
g\in\mathrm{G}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
,

(4.16)

\Int(g):=\chi\left({\widetilde{}\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{}\mathcal{N}_{n-1,n}}}\mathcal{O}_{g\cdot\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathcal{N}^{[t]}_{n-1}}}\right).

Let
\Lambda^{\flat}
be a Hermitian lattice of type
t
(i.e.,
\Lambda^{\flat}\subset(\Lambda^{\flat})^{\vee}
is of colength
t
, where
(\Lambda^{\flat})^{\vee}
is the dual lattice of
\Lambda^{\flat}
). It corresponds to a vertex on the Bruhat–Tits building of the unitary group
\mathrm{U}(V^{\flat})(F)
, where
V^{\flat}=\Lambda^{\flat}\otimes_{O_{F}}F
, and the stabilizer of this vertex is a maximal parahoric subgroup. Let
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\flat}\oplus O_{F^{\prime}}u
be an orthogonal sum for a vector
u
whose norm
(u,u)
has valuation
e\in\{0,1\}
. Then
\Lambda
is a Hermitian lattice of type
t+e
. Choose an orthogonal basis
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\}
of
\Lambda^{\flat}
such that
{\rm val}(u_{i},u_{i})\in\{0,1\}
. Using this basis to identify
\Lambda^{\flat}\simeq O_{F^{\prime}}^{n-1}\subset F^{\prime n-1}
. The lattice chain

\{\Lambda^{\flat},(\Lambda^{\flat})^{\vee}\}
defines a parahoric integral model of
{\rm R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}
and let
{\rm R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(O_{F})
be the parahoric subgroup. Similarly extend the basis to a basis of
\Lambda
by appending
u
and we have a parahoric subgroup
{\rm R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(O_{F})
of
{\rm R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)
. Note that we have a “good-position” condition
{\rm R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(O_{F})\subset{\rm R}_{F^{\prime}/F}\mathrm{GL}_{n}(O_{F})
. Let
\mathrm{G}^{\prime}(O_{F})
denotes the product of the two parahoric subgroups.

In
[
95
]
Zhiyu Zhang proved:

Theorem 4.4

(Arithmetic Transfer Conjecture (ATC) at maximal parahoric levels)
.

Assume that
F
is unramified over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
when
0<t<n-1
.
Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{G}^{\prime}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
match an element
g\in\mathrm{G}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then

\omega(\gamma)\operatorname{\partial Orb}\left(\gamma,c\cdot{\bf 1}_{\mathrm{G}^{\prime}(O_{F})}\right)=-2\,\Int(g)\cdot\log q.

Here
\omega(\gamma)\in\{\pm 1\}
is an explicit transfer factor (see
[
95
]
), and
c
is an explicit constant (depending on
n,t,e
).

(ii)

When

ramified
quadratic extension of
p

. We proved the conjecture for
n=2,3

(iii)

5.
Some other related works

5.1.
Some other generalizations of Gross–Zagier formula

Besides the orthogonal and unitary AGGP conjecture in §
3.3.2
,
there are several other conjectural generalizations of the Gross–Zagier formula, corresponding to different constructions of algebraic cycles (and possibly different families of L-functions). Shouwu Zhang summarized some of them in his notes
[
84
]
and since then there have been a couple of new additions.

The first one is the arithmetic Rallis inner product for both orthogonal and unitary groups, which involves the standard L-function of an automorphic representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}(2n+1)
and
\mathrm{U}(n)
. By the doubling method, this is largely tied with
Kudla’s program on the arithmetic analog of the Siegel–Weil formula. We refer to Chao Li’s articles
[
39
,
40
]
for more detail on the history and the recent progress. A milestone is the precise formulation of the conjecture due to Kudla and Rapoport in
[
31
]
, which relates the intersection numbers of
n
KR divisors defined in §
4.3
on
\mathcal{N}_{n}
,

(5.1)

\Int(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}):=\chi\left({\mathcal{N}_{n}},\,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}(x_{1})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{L}}}{{\otimes}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Z}(x_{n})}\right),\quad x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{V}_{n}

to the central derivative of a local density function. The role of their conjecture in the arithmetic Siegel–Weil formula is similar to that of the AFL in the AGGP Setting. Chao Li and the author have proved the Kudla–Rapoport conjecture in both the unitary and the orthogonal case
[
43
,
44
]
. Together with other non-trivial inputs (particularly a proof of the extension of the Kudla–Rapoport conjecture to the ramified quadratic case), Chao Li
and Yifeng Liu have established the first generalization of the Gross–Zagier formula to arbitrary high dimensional Shimura varieties
[
41
,
42
]
. Similar to the
p
-adic AGGP conjecture in §
3.8
, there is also a
p
-adic arithmetic inner product formula, proved by Disegni and Yifeng Liu under certain constraints on the local ramifications
[
12
]
.

Another generalization is formulated by Yifeng Liu
[
54
]
, which is the arithmetic analog of the
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
Fourier–Jacobi case of GGP conjecture. As already mentioned in §
4.3
, the local questions are closely related to the
\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n+1)
-case. Note that there is no known conjectural generalization of the Gross–Zagier formula for the Rankin–Selberg L-function for other pairs
\mathrm{U}(m)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
(a very curious coincidence: the Rankin–Selberg theory takes the simplest form for
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
when
|n-m|\in\{0,1\}
). Moreover, we do not know whether Liu’s construction has an analog for orthogonal groups. In fact, all the known “arithmetic conjectures” (including the one in the next paragraph) involve “an incoherent group”
\mathrm{G}
satisfying the following condition (cf. (
1.2
))

(5.2)

\displaystyle\text{$\mathrm{G}(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R})$ is compact modulo its center},

a condition labeled as

(\ast)
in Gan–Gross–Prasad
[
20
, §27]
. Here, following Kudla’s terminology, by “an incoherent group” we refer to the isometry group associated to an incoherent collection of quadratic or Hermitian spaces.

More recently, there is another construction of special cycles based on a symmetric pair
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{G})
, a (not necessarily inner) form of the pair
(\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n},\mathrm{GL}_{2n})
, for example
(\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n),\mathrm{U}(2n))
. See the introduction of
[
48
]
for the relevant global Shimura varieties and subvarieties. There are new algebraic cycles on Shimura varieties associated to unitary groups which may locally be the unit group of any central simple algebra, a feature not seen in the AGGP conjecture or Kudla’s program, see also the work in progress by Li–Mihatsch
[
49
,
50
]
. For the
(\mathrm{U}(n)\times\mathrm{U}(n),\mathrm{U}(2n))
-automorphic period integral (“unitary Friedberg–Jacquet period”), a relative trace formula approach is proposed and the stable FL on Lie algebra is established in
[
38
]
; the relative endoscopic FL on Lie algebra is also established in
[
37
]
.

On the more speculative side, from
[
94
]
we see that there may also be “exceptional” examples, for which there is a local cycle (i.e., on a local Shimura variety) but there is no global Shimura variety. Besides the general Bessel case
\mathrm{U}(m)\times\mathrm{U}(m+2r+1)
and
{\mathrm{SO}}(m)\times{\mathrm{SO}}(m+2r+1)
, there is also the (local) cycle for the Ginzburg–Rallis
(H,G)
, which is related to the exterior cube L-function of automorphic representation on
\mathrm{GL}_{6}
. We will have to wait for the invention of new “global Shimura varieties” to formulate the corresponding Gross–Zagier problem!

5.2.
Some arithmetic applications: the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture

In the introduction, we have mentioned that Waldspurger’s formula was a crucial ingredient in the proof of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for (modular) elliptic curves over a totally real number field in the analytic rank zero case, by Bertolini–Darmon’s variant of the Kolyvagin system argument for Heegner points on a Shimura curve. In a higher dimensional (than curve) case, Yifeng Liu first proved the rank zero analog for the “twisted triple product” automorphic motives in the product of a Shimura curve and a Hilbert–Blumenthal modular surface
[
52
]
, which may be placed in the framework of the triple product L-function and the Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycle (or equivalently the
{\mathrm{SO}}(3)\times{\mathrm{SO}}(4)
-case of the Gross–Prasad construction); see also
[
53
,
55
,
72
]
for works on other triple product L-functions.

In
[
56
]
we extend this paradigm to the Rankin–Selberg motives in the context of the Gan–Gross–Prasad construction for unitary groups
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{U}(n-1)\times\mathrm{U}(n)
. Let
\pi
be a cohomological stably cuspidal tempered automorphic representation of
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A})
appearing in the cohomology (
3.25
). Under certain technical conditions (mostly on the image of the residue Galois representation, see
loc. cit.
), we show that

L(1/2,\pi)\neq 0\Longrightarrow H^{1}_{f}(F^{\prime},\rho_{\pi})=0.

This confirms the corresponding Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture in the analytic rank zero case.

We also obtain a partial result towards the rank one case: under certain technical conditions on
\pi
, we show that
if the class
\int_{z_{K_{0}}}\varphi\in H^{1}_{f}(F^{\prime},\rho_{\pi})
in the Bloch–Kato Selmer group (
3.26
) is non-zero, then
\dim H^{1}_{f}(F^{\prime},\rho_{\pi})=1
. This is a direct generalization of Kolyvagin’s theorem for Heegner points. Using a different Euler system argument (more close to Kolyvagin’s Heegner point Euler system),
Jetchev, Nekovář, and Skinner also obtain a similar result (under different conditions) as well as one divisibility in a generalized Iwasawa Main conjecture.
Combining the above results in the rank one case with the
p
-adic variant of AGGP in §
3.8
, we would obtain a
p
-adic version of the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture formulated by Perrin-Riou (under technical conditions on
\pi
)

\ord L_{\it p}(1/2,\pi)=1\Longrightarrow\dim H^{1}_{f}(F^{\prime},\rho_{\pi})=1.

We refer to
[
13
]
for more detail.

We would like to mention a series of recent remarkable works by Loeffler and Zerbes
[
57
,
58
,
47
]
, partly joint with Pilloni and Skinner, in which they utilize the Gross–Prasad construction for orthogonal groups
{\mathrm{SO}}(4)\times{\mathrm{SO}}(5)
and the exceptional isogenies
{\mathrm{SO}}(4)\sim{\mathrm{SL}}(2)\times{\mathrm{SL}}(2)
and
{\mathrm{SO}}(5)\sim{\mathrm{Sp}}(4)
. As one of the applications, Loeffler and Zerbes
[
59
]
proved that, under certain technical hypotheses, for a modular abelian surface
A
over
\mathbb{Q}
,

\ord_{s=1}L(s,H^{1}(A))=0\Longrightarrow\rank A(\mathbb{Q})=0,

as predicted by (a generalization of the) Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture! Rather than using Chow cycles as in the above work on Rankin–Selberg motives, their work makes use of “higher Chow cycles” (elements in higher Chow groups), similar to Kato’s Euler system argument using Siegel’s units on modular curves (this seems to be one of the reasons why the result is currently only known when the base field is
\mathbb{Q}
). One of the difficulties is that the Tate module of an abelian surfaces has non-regular Hodge–Tate weights, hence does not appear directly in the (étale) cohomology of any Shimura varieties. To overcome this difficulty, a limit argument is necessary to extrapolate the Iwasawa theoretical Euler system from the regular case to the non-regular case.

For another example of employing “higher Chow cycles”, Zhou
[
96
]
proved a level-raising result using higher cycle classes on the special fiber of quaternionic Shimura varieties attached to an inner form of
\mathrm{R}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}
for a totally real field
F
. It would be interesting to know whether the local classes constructed in such a way can be lifted to global ones.

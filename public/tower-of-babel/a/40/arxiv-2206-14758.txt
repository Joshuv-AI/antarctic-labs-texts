Composition operators on the polydisc
Łukasz Kosiński

Abstract




1
Abstract

In this paper we study the boundedness of composition operators on the weighted Bergman spaces and the Hardy space over the polydisc
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
. Studying the volume of sublevel sets we show for which
n
the necessary conditions obtained by Bayart are sufficient. For arbitrary polydisc we prove the rank sufficiency theorem which, in particular, provides us with a simple criterion describing boundedness of composition operators on the spaces over the bidisc. Such a consistent characterization is obtained for the classical Bergman space over the tridisc.

2
Introduction and statement of the result

Let
X
be a Banach space of holomorphic functions on a domain
D\subset{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
and
\phi
be a holomorphic self-mapping of
D
. The composition operator, denoted by
C_{\phi}
, is

f\mapsto f\circ\phi,\quad f\in X.

The basic question that matters is for which
\phi
the symbol
C_{\phi}
defines a bounded operator on
X
.

Within the paper we are interested in weighted Bergman and Hardy function spaces over the polydisc. Let us recall their definitions. Let
dA
denote the normalized measure over the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. For
\beta>-1
we put
dA_{\beta}(z)=(\beta+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\beta}dA(z)
. On the polydisc
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
one defines

dV_{\beta}(z)=dA_{\beta}(z_{1})\ldots dA_{\beta}(z_{n}),\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}.

By
d\sigma
we denote the normalized surface measure on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
.

Then the Bergman space
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
, where
\beta>-1
, consists of
f\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
such that

||f||^{2}_{A^{2}_{\beta}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})}=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{n}}|f(z)|^{2}dV_{\beta}(z)<\infty.

Thus
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n}):=A_{0}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
is the classical Bergman space.

The Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
is composed of functions
g\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
such that

||g||^{2}_{H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})}=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{n}}|g(r\xi)|^{2}d\sigma(\xi)<\infty.

The problem of continuity of composition operators on Bergman and Hardy spaces over the polydisc has been studied in
[
KSZ
]
and then revisited and partially corrected in
[
Bay
]
. The second paper constitutes the main motivation for our research. Roughly speaking, the essential result of Bayart is that the problem of continuity of composition operators on the mentioned function spaces involves
delicate
properties of the derivative of the symbol. He showed that if
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
extends holomorphically to a neighbourhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{n}
, then the symbol
\phi
needs to satisfy certain conditions to make
C_{\phi}
continuous. Since they rely only on the first derivative, we shall call them the
first order
or sometimes
linear conditions
. The second name is justified by the fact that Bayart showed that the first order conditions are sufficient for linear maps. Precisely, carrying out complicated and clever computations he found an algorithm that classified all linear maps
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
such that
C_{\phi}
was bounded. The second main result of
[
Bay
]
is a general condition sufficient for continuity of
C_{\phi}
over the classical Bergman space
A({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
if the symbol is in
\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{n},{\mathbb{D}}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{n})
. This allowed Bayart to formulate a necessary and sufficient condition that guaranteed continuity of the composition operator on the classical Bergman space
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{2})
, when the symbol was a holomorphic self-map of the bidisc extending holomorphically to a neighbourhood of its closure.

Let us outline some main results of our paper.
We start with achieving upper and lower bounds for volumes of sublevel sets
\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:\ |f(z)-\eta|<\delta\}
, where
f\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{n},{\mathbb{D}})
is sufficiently smooth on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{n}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{T}}
. Saying briefly we show that these volumes can be estimated by
\delta^{n+1}
from above and
\delta^{(3n+1)/2}
from below. We also show, indicating particular examples, that these bounds are sharp. Equipped with this tool we show that the first order conditions are in general not sufficient. In particular, we prove the following:

•

first order conditions are sufficient for
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
if and only if
n\leq 3
;

•

first order conditions are sufficient for
A^{2}_{\beta}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
,
-1<\beta<0,
(or for
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
) if and only if
n\leq 2
.

To obtain sufficiency in the above statements we shall show

•

the rank-sufficiency theorem for
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
and for
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
.

The above-mentioned rank theorem implies that the answer in two dimensional settings has a simple formulation:

•

\phi\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{2},{\mathbb{D}}^{2})\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{2})
defines a bounded composition operator on
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{2})
with
\beta>-1
or on
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
if and only if the derivative
d_{\zeta}\phi
is invertible for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
such that
\phi(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

It turns out that a similar simple formulation can be achieved for the classical Bergman space over the tridisc.

Most of the results obtained in the sequel work also for more general spaces
A^{p}_{\beta}
and
H^{p}
, due to Lemma 5 in
[
KSZ
]
.

Amid tools that will be involved in the paper let us mention the characterization of boundedness in terms of volumes of Carleson boxes (see e.g. Lemma 5 in
[
KSZ
]
) and its generalization that allowed Bayart to pass from
A_{\beta}
to
H^{2}
as
\beta\to-1
. Let us recall these ideas. For
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in(0,1)^{n}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
the Carleson box is the set
S(\xi,\delta)=\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:\ |z_{j}-\xi_{j}|<\delta_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,n\}
. A holomorphic mapping
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
defines a bounded operator
C_{\phi}
on
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
if and only if

V_{\beta}(\phi^{-1}(S(\xi,\delta)))\leq C_{\beta}V_{\beta}(S(\xi,\delta)),\quad\text{for all $\xi\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}$ and $\delta\in(0,1)^{n}$},

(1)

where
C_{\beta}
is a positive constant. It was additionally shown in
[
Bay
]
, Proposition 9.3, that if
C_{\beta}
can be chosen independently of
-1<\beta<0
, then (
1
) entails boundedness of
C_{\phi}
on the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
.

In the paper we shall need the classical Julia-Carathéodory theorem and its multidimensional formulation in the form stated in
[
Bay
]
(Lemma 2.5 and Corollary 2.7) as well as other classical tools like the Schwarz lemma etc. We shall also use the following very well known observation that is a consequence of the Montel theorem: if
\varphi\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{2})\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{2})
, then
\varphi(z,\cdot)\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}})
for any
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
. To avoid introducing too many constants,
f(x)\preceq g(x)
will always mean that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for some positive
C
that is independent of
x
. The meaning of the symbol
\simeq
is analogous.
\nabla
denotes the complex gradient. Moreover, if
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n
, then we shall sometimes write
z_{I}
for
(z_{i_{1}},\ldots,z_{i_{k}})
.

We refer the reader to
[
Sha
]
for more on composition operators and function spaces. Let us just mention that the problem we are dealing with has been solved for the Hardy space over the Euclidean unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
(
[
Wog
]
) and later extended on the weighted Bergman spaces over
\mathbb{B}_{n}
(
[
KS
]
).

3
Preliminary and main results

3.1
Upper and lower bounds for sublevel sets

Lemma 1

(Upper bound)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}
be holomorphic and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth on the closed polydisc. Suppose that
f(\zeta)=\eta
for some
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{T}}
. Suppose also that
z_{j}\mapsto f(\zeta_{1},\ldots,z_{j},\ldots,\zeta_{n})
is not constant for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then

\operatorname{vol}(\{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}\cap U:\ |f(z)-\eta|\leq\delta\})\preceq\delta^{n+1}

(2)

for a sufficiently small neighbourhood
U
of
\zeta
.

Note that the assumption of Lemma
1
somehow means that
f
does not depend on
n-1
-variables (otherwise the estimate would not be true).
We need the following result:

Lemma 2
.

Let
K
be a compact set and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{D}\times K,\mathbb{D})
be such that
\varphi(\cdot,k)\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}},{\mathbb{D}})
for any
k\in K
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(K)>0
such that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and
k\in K
the following holds

|x|\leq 1-\delta\ \Longrightarrow\ |\varphi(x,k)|\leq 1-C(K)\delta.

Proof.

The assertion is rather clear as an immediate consequence of the Schwarz lemma: put
\alpha_{k}=\varphi(0,k)
. Then for
|x|\leq 1-\delta
elementary computations imply that
1-|\varphi(x)|^{2}\geq\delta|1-\bar{\alpha}_{k}\varphi(x)|^{2}(1-|\alpha_{k}|^{2})^{-1}\geq\delta(1-|\alpha_{k}|)/2
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1
.

We can assume that
\zeta=(1,\ldots,1)
and
\eta=1
. Then, by the Julia-Carathéodory theorem, the assumption implies that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}(\zeta)>0
,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Clearly,
|f(z)-1|\leq\delta
implies that
1-\delta\leq|f(z)|.
It follows from the assumption that for
w\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{n}
close to
\zeta
and fixed
j
the function
z_{j}\mapsto f(w_{1},\ldots,z_{j},\ldots w_{n})
is in
\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}},{\mathbb{D}})
(as it is non-constant). Thus Lemma
2
applied to them guarantees that there is
D>0
such that
|z_{j}|>1-D\delta
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, as
\delta\to 0
. Therefore, volumes of the projections of the set that appears in (
2
) onto
z_{j}
-planes can be estimated from above by
1-(1-D\delta)^{2}\simeq\delta
. We use this estimation for
j=1,\ldots,n-1
.

To get the assertion it is thus enough to get an estimate on
z_{n}
. Fix
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}
and denote
\varphi(\lambda)=f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1},\lambda)
. Let
U_{n}
be the projection of
U
onto the
z_{n}
-plane. Take any
w_{n}\in{\mathbb{D}}\cap U_{n}
such that
|\varphi(w_{n})-1|<\delta
(if there is no such
w_{n}
, the estimate we want to achieve is trivial). Clearly, it is enough to estimate the volume of a bigger set
\{\lambda\in U_{n}\cap{\mathbb{D}}:\ |\varphi(\lambda)-\varphi(w_{n})|<2\delta\}
which, in turn, can be estimated from above by the volume of the disc
\{\lambda:\ |\lambda-w_{n}|\leq\delta\}
, as the derivative
\varphi^{\prime}(w_{n})
does not vanish (providing that
U
is sufficiently small). This gives us an upper bound
\delta^{2}
and the proof is finished.
∎

As we shall observe later (see Proposition
5
) the upper bound that we have just achieved is sharp.

Lemma 3

(Lower bound)
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{D}}
be holomorphic,
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{n}
, and such that
f(\zeta)=\eta
for some
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{T}}
. Then

\operatorname{vol}(\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:\ |f(z)-\eta|\leq\delta\})\succeq\delta^{(3n+1)/2}.

Proof.

We lose no generality assuming that
\zeta=(1,\ldots,1)
and
\eta=1.

The proof is a consequence of the following:

Claim 4
.

Under the assumption of Lemma
3
there is a neighbourhood
U
of
\zeta=(1,\ldots,1)
and a constant
C>0
such that

|f(z)-1|\leq C\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}(\zeta)(z_{1}-1)+\ldots+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{n}}(\zeta)(z_{n}-1)\right|\text{ for $z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\cap U$}.

Proof of the Claim.

As we shall see the above inequality is a direct consequence of a multidimensional Julia-Carathéodory type lemma in the shape that was formulated by Bayart (Corollary 2.7,
[
Bay
]
). We shall explain how to use it. One needs to show that there is a constant
C>0
independent of
t>0
such that if
\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{1}}(\zeta)(z_{1}-1)+\ldots+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{n}}(\zeta)(z_{n}-1)\right|\leq t
, then
|f(z)-1|\leq Ct
.

It follows from the Julia-Carathéodory theorem that
\alpha_{j}:=\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}(\zeta)\geq 0
. Permuting the variables one can assume that
\alpha_{j}
is strictly positive for
j=1,\ldots,k
, where
k\leq n
, and
\alpha_{j}=0
if
j>k
. The crucial part due to Bayart says that

f(z)-1=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}(z_{j}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}O(|z_{j}-1|^{2}).

Clearly,
|\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-1)+\ldots+\alpha_{k}(z_{k}-1)|\leq t
implies that
\Re(1-z_{j})<Dt
, where
D
is independent of
t
. The last fact, together with
|z_{j}|<1
, implies that
|z_{j}-1|\leq D^{\prime}\sqrt{t}
, where again, a constant
D^{\prime}
does not depend on
t
. This observation finishes the proof.
∎

To finish the proof of Lemma
3
it is enough to estimate the volume of the set
\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:\ |\alpha_{1}(z_{1}-1)+\ldots+\alpha_{k}(z_{k}-1)|\leq\delta\}
. That was done by Bayart
[
Bay
]
(see also Proposition
5
in the current paper) — this volume behaves like
\delta^{(3k+1)/2}\succeq\delta^{(3n+1)/2}
.
∎

The estimates obtained above cannot be in general improved:

Proposition 5
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{D}
be given by the formulas
f(z)=z_{1}\cdots z_{n}
,
g(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\alpha_{1}z^{n}_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}z^{n}_{n}
, where
\sum_{j}|\alpha_{j}|=1
, and
\alpha_{j}\neq 0
for
j=1,\ldots,n
. Then for any
\eta\in{\mathbb{T}}
:

V_{\beta}(\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:\ |f(z)-\eta|\leq\delta\})\simeq\delta^{n(\beta+1)+1},

while

V_{\beta}(\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{n}:\ |g(z)-\eta|\leq\delta\})\simeq\delta^{n(\beta+1)+(n+1)/2}.

Proof.

The second estimate is essentially due to Bayart (see
[
Bay
]
, Example 4.1), just a standard change of variables is additionally needed. Let us sketch the first one. We can assume that
\eta=1
. Clearly,
|1-z_{1}\cdots z_{n}|\leq\delta
implies that
|z_{1}\cdots z_{n}|\geq 1-\delta
which is somehow equivalent to
|z_{j}|\succeq 1-\delta
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. In this way we estimate projections on the
z_{2}
-
,\ldots,z_{n}
-planes and the estimation for
z_{1}
follows from the fact that
\{z_{1}:\ |1-z_{1}\cdots z_{n}|\leq\delta\}
is a disc.
∎

The bounds derived above show the following simple result that we will make use of in the sequel:

Corollary 6
.

Let
\Phi(\varphi):=(\varphi,\ldots,\varphi,0):{\mathbb{D}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
, where
\varphi
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
. Let
f,g
be as in Proposition
5
. Then for
\varphi=f
the composition operator
C_{\Phi(f)}
is bounded on
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
precisely when
n\leq\beta+3
.

On the other hand, for
\varphi=g
the operator
C_{\Phi(g)}
is bounded on
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
precisely when
n/2\leq\beta+5/2
.

Proof.

Estimating
V_{\beta}(\Phi(f)^{-1}(S(\zeta,\delta))
we can assume that
\delta_{n}=1
, that is
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n-1},1)
. What we need to do is to check when the inequality
V_{\beta}(\{z:\ |f(z)-\zeta|<\delta_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,n-1\})\preceq\delta_{1}^{2+\beta}\cdots\delta_{n-1}^{2+\beta}
is fulfilled for any
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n-1}>0
, which is equivalent to checking if
V_{\beta}(\{z:\ |f(z)-\zeta|<\delta_{1}\})\preceq\delta_{1}^{(2+\beta)(n-1)}
for
\delta_{1}>0
. This, due to Proposition
5
, boils down to a trivial inequality
\delta_{1}^{n(\beta+1)+1}\preceq\delta_{1}^{(2+\beta)(n-1)}
.

The proof for
g
is exactly the same.
∎

3.2
Rank-sufficiency theorem and linear-necessity conditions

Theorem 7
.

Suppose that
\Phi:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
is holomorphic and
\mathcal{C}^{1}
smooth on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}^{n}
and such that for any
q
and any
|I|=q
:

({\dagger})\quad\forall\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}\text{ such that }\Phi_{I}(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{q}:\quad\operatorname{rank}d_{\zeta}\Phi_{I}=q.

Then for any
\beta>-1
the composition operator
C_{\Phi}
maps
A_{\beta}^{2}
as well as
H^{2}
continuously into itself.

We shall postpone the proof of Theorem
7
to the end of the paper. Note that it provides us with a simple criterion for the weighted Bergman and Hardy spaces over the bidisc.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{D}}^{2},{\mathbb{D}}^{2})\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{2})
,
\beta>-1
. Then
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
A_{\beta}({\mathbb{D}}^{2})
(resp. on
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{2})
) if and only if
d_{\zeta}\phi
is invertible for all
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
such that
\phi(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
.

Proof.

Necessity follows from
[
KSZ
]
, while sufficiency is a direct consequence of Theorem
7
.
∎

Corollary 9
.

First order conditions are sufficient for
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
and for
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
with
\beta<0
if and only if
n=2
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
8
it is enough to show that the first order conditions fail for
n\geq 3
. Let
f
be as in Proposition
5
. If
n>2
, then
C_{\Phi(f)}
is not bounded on
A_{\beta}^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
or
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
. Nevertheless, one can check that it does satisfy the first order conditions. The last fact can also be deduced in the following way: for any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
such that
f(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}
there is
g
as in Proposition
5
such that
g(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}
and the derivatives
d\Phi(f)
and
d\Phi(g)
coincide at
\zeta
.
∎

Therefore the bidisc and the tridisc are very particular for the problem of boundedness of composition operators on the Bergman space. According to Theorem
8
the sufficient and necessary condition is quite simple to formulate in the case of the bidisc. As we shall see below, it turns out it is also the case for the tridisc.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\phi:{\mathbb{D}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{D}}^{3}
be holomorphic on
{\mathbb{D}}^{3}
and
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{3}
. Then
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{3})
if and only if
\phi
satisfies the following properties:

1.

for any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
such that
\phi(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
the derivative
d_{\zeta}\phi
is invertible; and

2.

for
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq 3
and any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
such that
(\phi_{i_{1}}(\zeta),\phi_{i_{2}}(\zeta))\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
either

a)
\nabla\phi_{i_{1}}(\zeta)
,
\nabla\phi_{i_{2}}(\zeta)
are linearly independent, or

b)
\frac{\partial\phi_{i_{k}}}{\partial z_{j}}(\zeta)\neq 0
for
j=1,2,3
and
k=1,2
.

The theorem above says that in the settings of
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{3})
it is possible to characterize boundedness of the operator
C_{\phi}
in terms of the derivative of
\phi
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. We already knew that was the case for
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{2})
. The following corollary shows that there is no hope to achieve such characterization for
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
when
n>3
.

Corollary 11
.

First order conditions are sufficient for
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
if and only if
n\leq 3
.

Proof.

”If” follows from Theorem
10
. To prove ”only if” we proceed as in the proof of Theorem
9
. Keeping the notation from there we see that for
n=4
, the operator
C_{\Phi(f)}
is not bounded on
A^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{n})
, while it satisfies the first order conditions.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10
.

Let us prove the necessity. Seeking a contradiction assume that
1.
and
2.
are not satisfied. Take
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. If
\phi(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
, we use the necessity from
[
KSZ
]
. So suppose that, up to a permutation of components,
\phi(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{D}}
, and condition
2.
is not satisfied, i.e.
\nabla\phi_{1}(\zeta),\nabla\phi_{2}(\zeta)
are linearly dependent and
\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial z_{j}}(\xi)
vanishes for some
j=1,2,3
. Then the assertion follows from the Claim
4
. To see this we can make some reductions: we can assume that
\zeta=(1,1,1)
and
\phi_{1}(\zeta)=\phi_{2}(\zeta)=1
. Let
\delta=(\delta_{1},\delta_{1},1)
. Denote
\alpha_{j}=\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial z_{j}}(\zeta)\geq 0
. Then it follows from Claim
4
, and the fact that the derivatives of
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
are linearly dependent, that there is
C>0
such that
\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{3}:\ |\sum\alpha_{j}(z_{j}-1)|\leq\delta_{1}\}
is contained in
\{z\in{\mathbb{D}}^{3}:\ |\phi_{i}(z)-1|<C\delta_{1},\ i=1,2\}
. As at least one of
\alpha_{j}
vanishes, we infer that
\operatorname{vol}(\Phi^{-1}(S(\zeta,\delta)))\succeq\delta_{1}^{7/2}
. Since
\operatorname{vol}(S(\zeta,\delta))\simeq\delta_{1}^{4}
, the necessity follows.

Before we start the proof of the sufficiency let us observe that the assumptions of the theorem, that are two conditions
1.
and
2.
, are formulated only for
\zeta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{3}
. However, Lemma
13
assures us that they remain true if
\zeta\in\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{3}
. Note also that, by the Julia-Carathéodory theorem,
\phi
automatically satisfies one additional property:

3. if, up to a permutation of the components,
\phi(\zeta)\in{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
, the rank of the derivative
d_{\zeta}\phi_{1}
is
1
.

It is a trivial observation that sets composed of points
\zeta\in\partial{{\mathbb{D}}}^{n}
that satisfy any of conditions
1, 2a), 2b), 3
are open and contain
\phi^{-1}(\partial{\mathbb{D}}^{n})
. We shall cover them with a finite number of domains that posses particular properties.

Let
\{U^{1}_{\iota}\}
be finitely many polydiscs that cover the set
\phi^{-1}({\mathbb{T}}^{3})
such that the derivative
d_{\zeta}\phi
is invertible on
\overline{U}_{\iota}^{1}\cap\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{3}
. Let
\epsilon>0
be such that for any
z\in\partial{\mathbb{D}}^{3}\setminus\bigcup_{i}U_{i}^{1}
there exists
i=1,2,3,
such that
|\phi_{i}(z)|<1-\epsilon
.

We may take a finite number of polydiscs
\{U_{\iota}^{2}\}

Lemma 13

Suppose that

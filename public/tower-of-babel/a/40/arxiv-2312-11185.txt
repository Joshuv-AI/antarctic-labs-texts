A classification of Fourier summation formulas and crystalline measures
Felipe Gonçalves

Abstract
. We completely classify Fourier summation formulas, and in particular, all crystalline measures with quadratic decay. Our classification employs techniques from almost periodic functions, Hermite-Biehler functions, de Branges spaces and Poisson representation. We show how our classification generalizes recent results of Kurasov & Sarnak and Olevskii & Ulanovskii. As an application, we give a new classification result for nonnegative measures with uniformly discrete support that are bounded away from zero on their support. In the Appendix we attempt to give a systematic account of the several types of Fourier summation formulas, including a new construction using eta-products, generalizing an old example of Guinand.

A
Fourier summation formula
should be one where the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\varphi}(t)\mathrm{d}\mu(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a(\lambda_{n})\varphi(\lambda_{n})

holds, at the bear minimum, for all functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, where

is some sequence of real nodes,
\{a(\lambda_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}

We choose the above form of because: (1) We want a summation formula, so there must be a sum somewhere in the definition and such summation must be singular with respect to Lebesgue measure; (2) Allowing the right hand side above to have a vanishing singular part is boring, as otherwise any
L^{1}
-function would give rise to a Fourier summation formula.

Almost Periodic Functions in
\mathbb{R}

We say that a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
in almost periodic (in the sense of Bohr
[
15
]
) if for every
\varepsilon>0
the following set is relatively dense

\tau_{\varepsilon}(f):=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)-f(x+t)|\leq\varepsilon\}.

This set is called the set of
\varepsilon
-translations for
f
.
We denote by
{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
the set of continuous almost period functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
. It is not hard to show that any
f\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
is bounded and uniformly continuous, and that
\tau_{\varepsilon}(f)
is also closed with non-empty interior. The following is a very useful criteria for almost periodicity (see
[
3
, p. 7]
). In what follows
C(\mathbb{R})
denotes the usual Banach algebra of bounded continuous functions with the topology induced by the sup-norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|
.

In the remaining part of this section we compile necessary facts about almost periodic functions and we provide some proofs for completeness.

Theorem 7

(Bochner’s criterion)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be continuous. Then
f\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if the set of functions
\{f(\cdot+h)\}_{h\in\mathbb{R}}
is pre-compact in
C(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 7
.

Bochner’s criterion works also for functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In particular, if
f_{j}\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
for
j=1,...,N
, then
\cap_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{\varepsilon}(f_{j})
is nonempty and relatively dense.

It is now straightforward to show that
{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
closed under multiplication, addition and uniform convergence, hence
{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
is a closed subalgebra of
C(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, it also follows that for every continuous
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
we have
g\circ f\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
whenever
f\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
. Since exponentials
e^{2\pi ix\lambda}
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, are periodic, we conclude by Bochner’s criterion that any
trigonometric polynomial

p(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{2\pi i\lambda_{n}x}\quad\quad(\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{R})

belongs to
{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 8
.

Given any

and
\varepsilon>0

p
such that

. In particular,
{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
is the closure in

of the algebra of trigonometric polynomials.

h_{\delta}(x)=h(x/\delta)/\delta
,
\psi(x)=(2N+1)^{-1}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}h_{\delta}(x-t_{n})
and
\varphi_{M}(x)=\psi*\psi*\psi*\psi
, where
N\geq 1
is selected to be the largest such that
\{t_{n}\}_{|n|\leq N}+(-\delta,\delta)\subset\tau_{\varepsilon/4}\cap(-M/4,M/4)
. Note we must have
N\geq\frac{\delta M}{10}
for sufficiently large
M
. Since
\tau_{\varepsilon/4}+\tau_{\varepsilon/4}+\tau_{\varepsilon/4}+\tau_{\varepsilon/4}\subset\tau_{\varepsilon}
we conclude that
\varphi_{M}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\tau_{\varepsilon}\cap(-M,M))
. Also note that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{M}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi)^{4}=1
and, since
\varphi_{M}^{\prime}=\psi^{\prime}*\psi*\psi*\psi
, that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi^{\prime}_{M}|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi^{\prime}|=O(1/\delta).

Finally note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{M}(n/(4M))|^{1/2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\psi}(n/(4M))|^{2}

\displaystyle=4M\int_{-2M}^{2M}|\psi(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{4M}{\delta(2N+1)}\int_{-1}^{1}|h(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{4M}{\delta(2N+1)}\ll\delta^{-2},

for
M
large enough. This finishes the proof.
∎

We define

\mathbb{E}f:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}f(\lambda):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}\mathrm{d}x,

whenever these limits exist (so
\mathbb{E}f=\mathbb{E}f(0)
).

Lemma 9
.

For any
f\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
we have:

(1)

The average
\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
exists;

(2)

The set

{\rm spec}(f):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)\neq 0\}

it at most countable;

(3)

If
{\rm spec}(f)=\emptyset
then
f=0
;

(4)

If
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|<\infty
, then the following series converges absolutely and uniformly

f(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x};

(5)

We have
\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}=\sum_{\lambda}|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|^{2}
and for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
S\subset{\rm spec}(f)
finite such that
\mathbb{E}_{x}|f(x)-\sum_{\lambda\in S}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}|^{2}<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For item (1), we can assume
\lambda=0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be given and take a trigonometric polynomial
p
such that
\|f-p\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
. Let
A=\mathbb{E}p
, which exists by direct computation. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(x)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2T^{\prime}}\int_{-T^{\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=O(2\varepsilon)+\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}p(x)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2T^{\prime}}\int_{-T^{\prime}}^{T^{\prime}}p(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=O(2\varepsilon)+A-A+o_{T,T^{\prime}}(1).

For item (2), note first that
\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
exists since
f(x)e^{-2\pi i\lambda x}
is also almost periodic (by Bochner’s criterion). Let
p_{n}
be a trigonometric polynomial such that

\|f-p_{n}\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{n}.

Assume that
|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|>0
and let
1/n<|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|
. We obtain that

|\mathbb{E}p_{n}(\lambda)|\geq|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|-\frac{1}{n}>0
, hence
\mathbb{E}p_{n}(\lambda)\neq 0
.
We conclude that
{\rm spec}(f)\subset\bigcup_{n\geq 1}{\rm spec}(p_{n})
.
For item (3), let
\varepsilon>0
be given and take a trigonometric polynomial
p
such that
\|\bar{f}-p\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
. Now, observing that

\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(x)p(x)\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}f(x)(\bar{f}(x)-p(x))\mathrm{d}x=o_{T}(1)+O(\varepsilon\|f\|_{\infty}),

we conclude that
\lim_{T}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=0
. This is impossible if
f
is nonzero, because
|f|^{2}
is also almost periodic and uniformly continuous, and so if
|f(x)|^{2}\geq c
for
x
in some interval
I
then
|f(x)|^{2}\geq c/2
for
x\in\tau_{c/2}(|f|^{2})\ +I
. Since
\tau_{c/2}(|f|^{2})
contains some increasing sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying
t_{n+1}-t_{n}=O(1)
, it implies that
\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}({\bf 1}_{\tau_{c/2}(|f|^{2})+I})>0
, which is absurd. We conclude that
f=0
.
For item (4), note that

g(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x},

is well-defined and converges absolutely and uniformly on
\mathbb{R}
. Also note that dominated convergence implies that
\mathbb{E}g(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and so by item (3)
f=g
.
For item (5), let
\Lambda^{\prime}:=\cup_{m\geq 1}{\rm spec}(p_{m})
for some trigonometric polynomials
p_{m}
such that
\|f-p_{m}\|_{\infty}<1/m
and enumerate
\Lambda^{\prime}=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
. It is enough to show that
\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda_{n})|^{2}
. Let
f_{N}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda_{n})e^{2\pi i\lambda_{n}x}
. A straightforward computation shows that
\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|f_{N}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|f-f_{N}|^{2}
and so
\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda_{n})|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}
for all
N
. Another routine computation shows

\mathbb{E}|f-p|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|f_{N}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}|f_{N}-p|^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|f_{N}|^{2},

for any trigonometric polynomial of the form
p(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}e^{2\pi i\lambda_{n}x}
, for some
b_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Since
p_{m}
is of such form for some
N_{m}
, we conclude that
\mathbb{E}|f-f_{N_{m}}|^{2}<1/m
and that

\mathbb{E}|f_{N_{m}}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|f|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|f_{N_{m}}|^{2}+2/m.

∎

[

, p. 46]

Proposition 10

(Bochner’s approximation)
.

For any
f\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
there is an sequence of functions
c_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
,
c_{n}\leq c_{n+1}
, each
c_{n}
with finite support, satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }\ \mathbb{E}f(\lambda)\neq 0\\
0&\mbox{if }\ \mathbb{E}f(\lambda)=0,\end{cases}

and such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f(x)-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}c_{n}(\lambda)\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\lambda x}|=0.

5.
Almost Periodic Functions in
\mathbb{C}^{+}

Recall that we have defined in the introduction the space
\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
of holomorphic almost periodic functions
f:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a relatively dense set of
\varepsilon
-translations
\tau_{\varepsilon}(f)\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\sup_{\varepsilon<{\rm Im}\,z<1/\varepsilon}|f(z)-f(z+t)|<\varepsilon

for every
t\in\tau_{\varepsilon}(f)
. We say a function
f:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is
bounded on strips
if

\sup_{\varepsilon<{\rm Im}\,z<1/\varepsilon}|f(z)|<\infty

for every
\varepsilon>0
. We now give an alternative characterization of almost periodicity.

Lemma 11
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic. Then following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is bounded on strips and for every
h>0
we have
f(\cdot+ih)\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
;

(2)

f
is bounded on strips and there is
h>0
such that
f(\cdot+ih)\in{\rm AP}(\mathbb{R})
;

(3)

f\in\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
;

In this case the quantity

\mathbb{E}f(\lambda):=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T+iy}^{T+iy}f(z)e^{-2\pi i\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z

exists for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, is independent of
y>0
, it is nonzero in at most countably many
\lambda
’s and

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|^{2}e^{-4\pi y\lambda}=\mathbb{E}[|f(\cdot+iy)|^{2}]

for every
y>0
.

Proof.

The implications
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
and
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
are obvious. We now show
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. Let
\tau_{\varepsilon}
be the set of
\varepsilon
-translations for
f(\cdot+ih)
. We claim that
\tau_{\varepsilon}
also works in any horizontal strip containing the line
{\rm Im}\,z=h
. Indeed, let
t\in\tau_{\varepsilon}
and
0<y_{1}=y_{2}/2<y_{2}<h
. For all
y
with
y_{2}<y<h
, we can then apply Hadamard’s there lines lemma (for
{\rm Im}\,z\in\{y_{1},y,h\}
) to conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\log|f(x+iy)-f(x+iy+t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{y-y_{1}}{h-y_{1}}\sup_{x}\log|f(x+ih)-f(x+ih+t)|+\frac{h-y}{h-y_{1}}\sup_{x}\log|f(x+iy_{1})-f(x+iy_{1}+t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{h-y_{1}}\log\varepsilon+\frac{h-y_{2}}{h-y_{1}}B_{1}

for some
B_{1}>0
. Hence, there is
\alpha=\alpha(y_{2},h)>0
such that

\sup_{y_{2}<{\rm Im}\,z<h}|f(z)-f(z+t)|\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\varepsilon^{\alpha}.

We can apply the same procedure for
h<{\rm Im}\,z<y_{3}
for any
y_{3}>h
to prove the claim. This shows that
f\in\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
. Now notice that by Lemma
9
the limit
\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
exists for every
y>0
. To show is independent of
y
, we can use Cauchy’s formula to deduce that if
0<y_{1}<y_{2}
then

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T+iy_{1}}^{T+iy_{1}}f(z)e^{-2\pi i\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z-\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T+iy_{2}}^{T+iy_{2}}f(z)e^{-2\pi i\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z\bigg]

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T+iy_{1}}^{-T+iy_{2}}f(z)e^{-2\pi i\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z-\frac{1}{2T}\int_{T+iy_{1}}^{T+iy_{2}}f(z)e^{-2\pi i\lambda z}\mathrm{d}z\bigg]=O((y_{2}-y_{1})e^{2\pi|\lambda|y_{2}}/T).

Taking
T\to\infty
, we conclude that
\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
is independent of
y
. Since
e^{-2\pi\lambda y}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}[f(\cdot+iy)](\lambda)
we deduce that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
has countable support and
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}|e^{-2\pi\lambda y}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|f(\cdot+iy)|^{2}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

For a function

The following hold:

Lemma 13
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
and assume
{\rm spec}(f)\subset\{0\}\cup[b,\infty)
for some

F:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}

. Moreover, if in addition

Let
p=\mathbb{E}f(0)
. Since
F
has no zeros in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
we can write
F(z)=e^{-ig(z)-ipz}
for some

holomorphic. Since
g^{\prime}(z)+p=iF^{\prime}(z)/F(z)=f(z)
we conclude that

,
{\rm spec}(g)\subset[b,\infty)
and
\mathbb{E}(g^{\prime})(\lambda)={\boldsymbol{1}}_{\lambda\geq b}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)
. We can now apply
[
10
, Thm. 9, p. 152]
to conclude that
g\in\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
and that
\mathbb{E}g(\lambda)={\boldsymbol{1}}_{\lambda\geq b}\mathbb{E}f(\lambda)/(2\pi i\lambda)
for
\lambda\neq 0
. If
\Phi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is entire and
h\in\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
then it is easy to conclude that
\Phi\circ h\in\mathrm{AP}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
. Indeed, since
h
is bounded in strips and since

is bounded in bounded sets, we conclude that for any horizontal strip contained in
\mathbb{C}^{+}

C>0

for all

and
w
in that strip. This shows that

. This implies that the power series expansion

\Phi(h(z))=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\Phi^{(n)}(0)}{n!}h(z)^{n}.

converges absolutely and uniformly on any horizontal strip. Now assume that

for some

we conclude that
{\rm spec}(\Phi\circ h)

\Phi(z)=e^{-iz}

, we deduce that

and that

. Moreover,
{\rm spec}(F)
is locally finite if
{\rm spec}(f)
also is.

6.
Representations of Analytic Functions

We say a holomorphic function
g:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
if of
bounded type
if there are two bounded holomorphic functions
P,Q:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
g=P/Q
. We write
g\in{\rm BT}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
. If
g
is of bounded type then the
mean type

\vartheta(g):=\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{\log|g(iy)|}{y}

exists and is finite. For more information about these definitions we recommend
[
12
,
19
]
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic and write
g=\exp(f)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

There is a real-valued locally finite measure
\mu
with
\deg(\mu)\leq 2
such that

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)+\overline{f(w)}}{z-\overline{w}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}{(t-z)(t-\overline{w})}

(12)

holds for every
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
;

(2)

g\in{\rm BT}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
and
\vartheta(g)=0
.

Proof.

First we show that
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. Indeed, note first that

{\rm Re}\,f(x+iy)=\frac{y}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}{(t-x)^{2}+y^{2}}.

Writing
\mu=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
where each
\mu_{j}
is nonnegative and defining

f_{j}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1+tz}{t-z}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}(t)}{1+t^{2}}

we conclude that
{\rm Re}\,f_{j}\geq 0
in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
and that
{\rm Re}\,f={\rm Re}\,f_{1}-{\rm Re}\,f_{2}
, hence
f=f_{1}-f_{2}+ih
for some constant
h\in\mathbb{R}
. However, since each
g_{j}=\exp(-f_{j})
is bounded in absolute value by
1
in
\mathbb{C}^{+}
and

g=e^{ih}g_{2}/g_{1}
, we deduce that
g\in{\rm BT}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
. Note also that

\vartheta(g)=\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{{\rm Re}\,f(iy)}{y}=\limsup_{y\to\infty}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu(t)}{t^{2}+y^{2}}=0.

Now we show
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
. Since
g
has no zeros, Nevalinnas’s factorization for functions of bounded type
[
12
, Theprem 9]
implies that there exists a unique real-valued locally finite measure
\mu
, with
\deg(\mu)\leq 2
, and some

We say

\nu_{1}\neq 0

\nu_{2}

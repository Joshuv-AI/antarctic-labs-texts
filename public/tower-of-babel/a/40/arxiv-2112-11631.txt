Gröbner-Shirshov bases and linear bases for free differential type algebras over algebras
Zihao Qi, Yufei Qin and Guodong Zhou

Abstract
. We study a question which can be roughly stated as follows: Given a (unital or nonunital) algebra A A together with a Gröbner-Shirshov basis G G , consider the free operated algebra B B over A A , such that the operator satisfies some polynomial identities Φ \Phi which are Gröbner-Shirshov in the sense of Guo et al., when does the union Φ ∪ G \Phi\cup G will be an operated Gröbner-Shirshov basis for B B ? We answer this question in the affirmative under a mild condition in our previous work with Wang. When this condition is satisfied, Φ ∪ G \Phi\cup G is an operated Gröbner-Shirshov basis for B B and as a consequence, we also get a linear basis of B B . However, the condition could not be applied directly to differential type algebras introduced by Guo, Sit and Zhang, including usual differential algebras. This paper solves completely this problem for differential type algebras. Some new monomial orders are introduced which, together with some known ones, permit the application of the previous result to most of differential type algebras, thus providing new operated GS bases and linear bases for these differential type algebras. Versions are presented both for unital and nonunital algebras. However, a class of examples are also presented, for which the natural expectation in the question is wrong and these examples are dealt with by direct inspection.

Introduction

In our previous work with Wang
[
25
]
, we studied operated Gröbner-Shirshov (aka GS) bases for free operated algebras over associative algebras whose operator satisfies some polynomial identities.
This paper continues the work of
[
25
]
and concentrates into differential type algebras.

0.1.
From usual GS bases to operated GS bases

Gröbner-Shirshov basis theory was invented by Shirshov
[
30
]
and Buchberger
[
6
]
in the sixties of last century. It becomes one of the main tools of computational algebra since then; see for instance
[
13
,
2
,
4
]
. In order to deal with algebras endowed with operators, Guo et al. introduced a GS basis theory in operated contexts in a series of papers
[
14
,
38
,
16
,
11
]
(see also
[
3
]
)
with the goal to attack Rota’s program
[
28
]
to classify “interesting” operators on algebras. For a global view about the state of art, we refer the reader to the survey paper
[
12
]
and for recent development, see
[
33
,
35
,
7
,
34
,
15
,
25
,
36
,
37
]
.

Guo et al. considered operators satisfying some polynomial identities, hence called operated polynomial identities (aka. OPIs). Via GS basis theory and the somewhat equivalent theory: rewriting systems, they could define when OPIs are GS. They are mainly interested into two classes of OPIs: differential type OPIs and Rota-Baxter type OPIs, which are carefully studied in
[
16
,
38
,
11
]
.

In this paper we are interested into differential type OPIs and differential type algebras.

0.2.
Free operated algebras over algebras

Recently, there is a need to develop free operated algebras satisfying some OPIs over a fixed algebras and construct GS bases and linear bases for these free algebras as long as a GS basis is known for the given algebra. Ebrahimi-Fard and Guo
[
10
]
used rooted trees and forests to give explicit constructions of free noncommutative
Rota-Baxter algebras on modules and sets; Lei and Guo
[
18
]
constructed the linear basis of free Nijenhuis algebras over associative algebras;
Guo and Li
[
15
]
gave a linear basis of the free differential algebra over associative algebras by introducing the notion of differential GS bases.

As in
[
25
]
, we want to consider a question which can be roughly stated as follows:

Question 0.1
.

Given a (unital or nonunital) algebra
A
with a GS basis
G
and a set
\Phi
of OPIs,
assume that these OPIs
\Phi
are GS in the sense of
[
3
,
16
,
38
,
11
]
. Let
B

be the free operated algebra satisfying
\Phi
over
A
. Under what conditions,
\Phi\cup G
will be a GS basis for
B
?

We answer this question in the affirmative under a mild condition in
[
25
, Theorem 5.9]
. When this condition is satisfied,
\Phi\cup G
is a GS basis for
B
and as a consequence, we also get a linear basis of
B
. This result has been applied to all Rota-Baxter type OPIs, a class of differential type OPIs, averaging OPIs and Reynolds OPI in
[
25
]
.

However, this result can NOT be applied directly to all differential type OPIs. The main reason is that under the chosen monomial order for differential type OPIs, the condition of
[
25
, Theorem 5.9]
is not satisfied.

The goal of this paper is to introduce some new monomial orders which permit to deal with nearly all free differential type algebras over algebras.

Moreover, we provide a class of counter-examples for which the expectation in Question
0.1
is wrong; see Theorems
4.12
and
4.13
.

0.3.
Differential type algebras over algebras

A commutative algebra equipped with an operator satisfying the Leibniz rule, is called a differential algebra. The notion is originated by Ritt
[
27
,
26
]
in the algebraic study of differential equations in the 1930s. This mathematical subject has been developed into a broad realm such as differential Galois theory
[
20
,
32
]
, differential algebraic geometry and differential algebraic groups
[
17
]
. In recent years, researchers began to investigate noncommutative differential algebras in order to broaden the scope of the theory to include path algebras, for instance, and to have a more meaningful differential Lie algebra theory
[
23
,
24
]
and also from an operadic point of view
[
19
,
9
]
.
There are also some recent work dealing with other algebraic structures endowed with derivations
[
29
,
8
,
31
]
.

As mentioned above,
[
25
, Theorem 5.9]
can NOT be applied directly to all differential type OPIs in order to obtain GS bases and linear bases for free differential type algebras over algebras.
In this paper, we introduce some new monomial orders and solve completely this question.

Our method is as follows: We change a differential type OPI to an equivalent one in the sense of Definition
2.5
; by choosing convenient monomial orders, we can apply
[
25
, Theorem 5.9]
to obtain a GS basis; as a consequence, we get a linear basis.
We also deal with the problem in the nonunital case; see Theorem
2.15
.
Quite interestingly, there are some exceptional cases for which our method could not apply and we treat these cases directly.

We would like to mention that Guo and Li introduced the notion of differential GS bases to deal with the above problem in
[
15
]
and
their method is completely different from ours; see Remark
5.12
.

0.4.
Outline of the paper

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 1 some basic definitions and constructions of free objects in operated contexts are recalled and some new monomial orders are introduced. Section 2 contains an account of the theory of operated GS bases, which serve as a main tool in this paper; the main result of
[
25
]
is recalled and a nonunital version is presented; a remainder about the basic theory of rewriting systems. In Section 3, we consider differential type OPIs and they are replaced by some equivalent ones. Section 4 is devoted to operated GS bases and linear bases for free nonunital differential type algebras over an algebra, while the last section considers unital ones.

Notation:
Throughout this paper,
{\mathbf{k}}
denotes a base field. All the vector spaces and algebras are over
{\mathbf{k}}
.

1.
New monomial orders on free operated semigroups and monoids

In this section, we recall the construction of free operated semigroups and monoids due to Guo
[
14
]
.
We will define a new monomial order
\leq_{{\mathrm{dl}}}
on free operated semigroups by combining several well orders and extend it to a monomial order
\leq_{{\mathrm{udl}}}
on free operated monoids via Remark
1.2
. The main results of this paper will highly depend on these two new monomial orders.

1.1.
Free operated semigroups and monoids

Definition 1.1

(
[
14
,
38
,
16
,
11
]
)
.

An operated semigroup is a semigroup
S
with a map
P:S\rightarrow S
(which is not necessarily a homomorphism of semigroups).
Operated monoids can be defined similarly.
For operated
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra (resp. unital operated
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebra), we ask additionally that the operator should be a
{\mathbf{k}}
-linear map.

We use
\mathfrak{OpAlg}
(resp.
\mathfrak{uOpAlg}
) to denote the category of operated
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras (resp. unital operated
{\mathbf{k}}
-algebras) with obvious morphisms.

Throughout this section,
X
denotes a set. Denote by
{\mathbf{k}}X
(resp.
{\mathcal{S}}(X)
,
{\mathcal{M}}(X)
) the free
{\mathbf{k}}
-space (resp. free semigroup, free monoid) generated by

We recall the construction of the free operated semigroup over a given set
X
due to Guo
[
14
]
.

For a set
X
, denote by
\lfloor X\rfloor
the set of the formal elements
\lfloor x\rfloor,x\in X
. The inclusion
X\hookrightarrow X\sqcup\left\lfloor X\right\rfloor
, which identifies
X
with the first component, induces an injective semigroup homomorphism

i_{0,1}:{\mathfrak{S}}_{0}(X):={\mathcal{S}}(X)\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{1}(X):={\mathcal{S}}(X\sqcup\left\lfloor X\right\rfloor).

For
n\geq 2
, assume that we have constructed
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-2}(X)
and
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}(X)={\mathcal{S}}(X\sqcup\left\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-2}(X)\right\rfloor)
endowed with an injective homomorphism of semigroups

i_{n-2,n-1}:{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-2}(X)\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}(X).

We define the semigroup

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X):={\mathcal{S}}(X\sqcup\left\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}(X)\right\rfloor)

and
the natural injection

{\rm Id}_{X}\sqcup\left\lfloor i_{n-2,n-1}\right\rfloor:X\sqcup\left\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-2}(X)\right\rfloor\hookrightarrow X\sqcup\left\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}(X)\right\rfloor

induces an injective semigroup homomorphism

i_{n-1,n}:{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}(X)={\mathcal{S}}(X\sqcup\left\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-2}(X)\right\rfloor)\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)={\mathcal{S}}(X\sqcup\left\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n-1}(X)\right\rfloor).

Define
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)=\varinjlim{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)
and the map sending
u\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)
to
\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}(X)
induces an operator
P_{{\mathfrak{S}}(X)}
on
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
.

The construction of the free operated monoid
{\mathfrak{M}}(X)
over a set
X
is similar, by just replacing
{\mathcal{S}}
by
{\mathcal{M}}
everywhere in the construction.

Remark 1.2
.

We will use another construction of
{\mathfrak{M}}(X)
. In fact, add a symbol
\lfloor 1\rfloor
to
X
and form
{\mathfrak{S}}(X\bigsqcup\lfloor 1\rfloor)
, then
{\mathfrak{M}}(X)
can be obtained from
{\mathfrak{S}}(X\bigsqcup\lfloor 1\rfloor)
by just adding the empty word
1
.

It is easy to see that
{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
(resp.
{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{M}}(X)
) is the free nonunital (resp, unital) operated algebra generated by
X
.
More constructions of free objects in operated contexts can be found in
[
25
]
.

1.2.
Monomial orders

We need some preliminaries about orders.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set.

(a)

A preorder
\leq
is a binary relation on
X
that is reflexive and transitive, that is, for all
x,y,z\in X
, we have

(i)

x\leq x
; and

(ii)

if
x\leq y,y\leq z
, then
x\leq z
. We denote
x=_{X}y
if
x\leq y
and
x\geq y
. If
x\leq y
but
x\neq y
, we write
x<y
or
y>x
.

(b)

A pre-linear order
\leq
on
X
is a preorder
\leq
such that either
x\leq y
or
x\geq y
for all
x,y\in X
.

(c)

A linear order or a total order
\leq
on
X
is a pre-linear order
\leq
such that
\leq
is symmetric, that is,
x\leq y
and
y\leq x
imply
x=_{X}y
.

(d)

A preorder
\leq
on
X
satisfies the descending chain condition, if
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq x_{3}\geq\cdots
, then there exists
N\geq 1
such that
x_{N}=_{X}x_{N+1}=_{X}\cdots
.
A linear order satisfying the descending chain condition is called a well order.

Before giving the definition of monomial orders, we need to introduce the following notions.

Definition 1.4

(
[
3
,
16
,
38
,
11
]
)
.

Let
Z
be a set and
\star
a symbol not in
Z
.

(a)

Define
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\star}(Z)
to be the subset of
{\mathfrak{M}}(Z\cup\star)
consisting of elements with
\star
occurring only once.

(b)

For
q\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{\star}(Z)
and
u\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)
, we define
q|_{u}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)
obtained by
replacing the symbol
\star
in
q
by
u
. In this case, we say
u
is a subword of
q|_{u}
.

(c)

For
q\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{\star}(Z)
and
s=\sum_{i}c_{i}u_{i}\in{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)
with
c_{i}\in{\mathbf{k}}
and
u_{i}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)
, we define

q|_{s}:=\sum_{i}c_{i}q|_{u_{i}}.

(d)

Define
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
to be the subset of
{\mathfrak{S}}(Z\cup\star)
consisting of elements with
\star
occurring only once. It is easy to see
{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
is a subset of
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\star}(Z)
, so we also have the above definitions for

by restriction.

Definition 1.5

(
[
3
,
16
,
38
,
11
]
)
.

Let
Z
be a set. We deonte
u<v
if
u\leq v
but
u\neq v
for an order
\leq
.

(a)

A monomial order on
{\mathcal{S}}(Z)
is a well-order
\leq
on
{\mathcal{S}}(Z)

u<v\Rightarrow uz<vz\text{ and }wu<wv\text{ for any }u,v,w,z\in{\mathcal{S}}(Z);

a monomial order on

is a well-order

(b)

A monomial order on

\leq

{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)

u<v\Rightarrow q|_{u}<q|_{v}\quad\text{for all }u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)\text{ and }q\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z);

a monomial order on
{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)
is a well-order
\leq
on
{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)
such that

u<v\Rightarrow q|_{u}<q|_{v}\quad\text{for all }u,v\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)\text{ and }q\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{\star}(Z).

Definition 1.6
.

(a)

Given some preorders
\leq_{\alpha_{1}},\dots,\leq_{\alpha_{k}}
on a set
X
with
k\geq 2
, introduce another preorder

\leq_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}}
by imposing recursively

u\leq_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}}v\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u<_{\alpha_{1}}v,\text{ or }\\
u=_{\alpha_{1}}v\text{ and }u\leq_{\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{k}}v.\end{array}\right.

(b)

Let
k\geq 2
and let
\leq_{\alpha_{i}}
be a pre-linear order on
X_{i},~1\leq i\leq k
. Define the lexicographical product order
\leq_{\text{clex}}
on the cartesian product
X_{1}\times X_{2}\times\cdots\times X_{k}
by recursively defining

(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})\leq_{\text{clex}}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{k})\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}<_{\alpha_{1}}y_{1},\text{or }\\
x_{1}=_{X_{1}}y_{1}\text{ and }\left(x_{2},\cdots,x_{k}\right)\leq_{\rm{clex}}\left(y_{2},\cdots,y_{k}\right),\end{array}\right.

where
\left(x_{2},\cdots,x_{k}\right)\leq_{\rm{clex}}\left(y_{2},\cdots,y_{k}\right)
is defined by induction, with the convention that
\leq_{\rm{clex}}
is the trivial relation when
k=1
.

By the proof of
[
38
, Lemma 5.4(a)]
, we have the following result:

Lemma 1.7
.

(a)

Let
k\geq 2.
Let
\leq_{\alpha_{1}},\dots,\leq_{\alpha_{k-1}}
be pre-linear orders on
X
, and
\leq_{\alpha_{k}}
a linear order on
X
. Then
\leq_{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}}
is a linear order on
X
.

(b)

Let
\leq_{\alpha_{i}}
be a well order on
X_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
. Then the lexicographical product
order
\leq_{\rm{clex}}
is a well order on the cartesian product
X_{1}\times X_{2}\times\cdots\times X_{k}
.

Let us first recall the well known degree lexicographical order.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
X
be a set endowed with a well order
\leq_{X}
. For
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{r}\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
with
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\in X,
define
\operatorname{deg}_{X}(u)=r
.

Introduce the degree lexicographical order
\leq_{\rm{dlex}}
on
{\mathcal{S}}(X)
by imposing, for any
u\neq v\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
,
u<_{\rm{dlex}}v
if

(a)

either
\operatorname{deg}_{X}(u)<\operatorname{deg}_{X}(v)
, or

(b)

\operatorname{deg}_{X}(u)=\operatorname{deg}_{X}(v)
, and
u=mu_{i}n
,
v=mv_{i}n^{\prime}
for some
m,n,n^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}(X)
and
u_{i},v_{i}\in X
with
u_{i}<_{X}v_{i}
.

It is obvious that the degree lexicographic order
\leq_{\mathrm{dlex}}
on
{\mathcal{S}}(X)
is a well order and
\leq_{\mathrm{dlex}}
can be extended to
{\mathcal{M}}(X)
by setting
1
to be the least element.

For two well ordered sets
X
and
Y
, one obtains an extended well order on the disjoint union
X\sqcup Y
by defining
a<b
for all
a\in X
and
b\in Y
.

We now define a linear order on
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
, by the following recursion:

(a)

Let
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{0}}(X)={\mathcal{S}}(X)
. We define

u\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{0}}v\Leftrightarrow u\leq_{\mathrm{dlex}}v.

(b)

Assume that we have constructed a well order
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{n}}
on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)
for
n\geq 0
extending all
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{i}}
for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
. The well order
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{n}}
induces a well order on
\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)\rfloor
and by imposing
a>b
for all
a\in\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)\rfloor
and
b\in X
, we obtain an extended well order on
X\sqcup\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)\rfloor
.
Denote by
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{n+1}}
the induced degree lexicographic order on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}(X)={\mathcal{S}}(X\sqcup\lfloor{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(X)\rfloor)
.

Obviously
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{n+1}}
extends
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{n}}
. By a limit process, we get a preorder on
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
which will be denoted by

\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}}
.

It is easy to see that this is a linear order, but it is NOT a well order. An example is given by

u\lfloor v\rfloor>\lfloor u\lfloor v\rfloor\rfloor>\lfloor\lfloor u\lfloor v\rfloor\rfloor\rfloor>\cdots

for
u,v\in X
.

Recall that an arbitrary element of
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
has a unique expression

u=u_{0}\lfloor u_{1}^{*}\rfloor u_{1}\lfloor u_{2}^{*}\rfloor\cdots\lfloor u_{r}^{*}\rfloor u_{r}
, where
u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{r}\in{\mathcal{M}}(X)
and
u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*},\dots,u_{r}^{*}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(X).
Notice that
u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
could be the empty word. Define the
P
-breath
|u|_{P}
of
u=u_{0}\lfloor u_{1}^{*}\rfloor u_{1}\lfloor u_{2}^{*}\rfloor\cdots\lfloor u_{r}^{*}\rfloor u_{r}
to be
r
.

Definition 1.9

(
[
38
, Definition 5.3]
)
.

For two elements
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
,

(a)

define

u\leq_{\operatorname{dgp}}v\Leftrightarrow\operatorname{deg}_{P}(u)\leq\operatorname{deg}_{P}(v),

where the
P
-degree
\operatorname{deg}_{p}(u)
of

is the number of occurrence of

in

;

(b)

define

u\leq_{\operatorname{dgx}}v\Leftrightarrow\operatorname{deg}_{X}(u)\leq\operatorname{deg}_{X}(v),

where the
X
-degree
\operatorname{deg}_{X}(u)
is the number of elements of
X
occurring in

(including the repetitive ones);

(c)

define

u\leq_{\operatorname{brp}}v\Leftrightarrow|u|_{P}\leq|v|_{P}.

Definition 1.10
.

For any
u\in{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
, let
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in X
be all the elements occurring in

from left to right. If a half bracket
\lfloor
(resp.

) is between

and

, where

\lfloor
(resp.

) appearing on the left of

(resp. on the right of

(resp.

For
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
, define the G-degree order
\leq_{\mathrm{dgg}}
by

u\leq_{\mathrm{dgg}}v\Leftrightarrow\deg_{G}(u)\leq\deg_{G}(v).

It is easy to obtain the following lemma whose proof is thus omitted.

Lemma 1.11
.

The orders
\leq_{\mathrm{dgp}}
,
\leq_{\mathrm{dgx}}
,
\leq_{\operatorname{brp}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{dgg}}
are pre-linear orders satisfying the descending chain condition.

By Lemma
1.7
, we can define a linear order
\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}
on
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
,

u\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}v\Leftrightarrow u\leq_{\mathrm{dgp,dgx,dgg},\operatorname{Dlex}}v\Leftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}u<_{\mathrm{dgp}}v,\text{or }\\
u=_{\mathrm{dgp}}v\text{ and }u<_{{\rm dgx}}v,\text{or }\\
u=_{\mathrm{dgp}}v,u=_{\mathrm{dgx}}v\text{ and }u<_{{\rm dgg}}v,\text{or }\\
u=_{\mathrm{dgp}}v,u=_{\mathrm{dgx}}v,u=_{\mathrm{dgg}}v\text{ and }u\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}}v.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 1.12
.

The order
\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}
is a well order on
{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
.

Proof.

Since
\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}
is a linear order, we only need to verify that
\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}
satisfies the descending chain condition. Let
v_{1}\geq_{\mathrm{dl}}v_{2}\geq_{\mathrm{dl}}v_{3}\geq_{\mathrm{dl}}\cdots\in{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
. Since the pre-linear order
\leq_{\mathrm{dgp}}
,
\leq_{\mathrm{dgx}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{dgg}}
satisfy the descending chain condition, there exist
N\geq 1
and
k\geq 0
such that

\deg_{P}(v_{N})=\deg_{P}(v_{N+1})=\deg_{P}(v_{N+2})=\cdots=k,

\deg_{X}(v_{N})=\deg_{X}(v_{N+1})=\deg_{X}(v_{N+2})=\cdots

and

\deg_{G}(v_{N})=\deg_{G}(v_{N+1})=\deg_{G}(v_{N+2})=\cdots.

Thus all
v_{i}
with
i\geq N
belong to
{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}(X)
. The restriction of the order
\leq_{\mathrm{Dlex}}
to
{\mathfrak{S}}_{k}(X)
equals
to the well order
\leq_{\operatorname{Dlex}_{k}}

We record an important technical result.

Proposition 1.20
.

For any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{M}}(X)\backslash\{1\}
, we have

(a)

\lfloor u\rfloor v<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor uv\rfloor<_{\mathrm{udl}}u\lfloor v\rfloor<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor u\rfloor\lfloor v\rfloor;

(b)

u\lfloor 1\rfloor v<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor u\rfloor v;

(c)

\lfloor 1\rfloor uv<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor u\rfloor v;

(d)

u<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor 1\rfloor u;

(e)

u<_{\mathrm{udl}}u\lfloor 1\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
u,v\in{\mathfrak{M}}(X)\backslash\{1\}={\mathfrak{S}}(X^{\prime})
.

(a)

\lfloor u\rfloor v<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor uv\rfloor
holds because

\deg_{G}(\lfloor uv\rfloor)-\deg_{G}(\lfloor u\rfloor v)=\deg_{X^{\prime}}(v)>0,

u\lfloor v\rfloor<_{\mathrm{udl}}\lfloor u\rfloor\lfloor v\rfloor
follows from
u\lfloor v\rfloor<_{\mathrm{dgp}}\lfloor u\rfloor\lfloor v\rfloor
and

\lfloor uv\rfloor<_{\mathrm{udl}}u\lfloor v\rfloor
is obtained from
\deg_{G}(u\lfloor v\rfloor)-\deg_{G}(\lfloor uv\rfloor)=\deg_{X^{\prime}}(u)>0
.

(b)

The inequality is valid as
u\lfloor 1\rfloor v=u\dagger v<_{\mathrm{dgp}}u\lfloor v\rfloor
.

(c)

The statement can be deduced from
\lfloor 1\rfloor uv=\dagger uv<_{\mathrm{dgp}}u\lfloor v\rfloor
.

(d)

The assertion follows from
\deg_{X^{\prime}}(u)<\deg_{X^{\prime}}(\dagger u)=\deg_{X^{\prime}}(\lfloor 1\rfloor u)
.

(e)

We infer the result from
\deg_{X^{\prime}}(u)<\deg_{X^{\prime}}(u\dagger)=\deg_{X^{\prime}}(u\lfloor 1\rfloor)

2.3.
Remainder on rewriting systems

We need some basic notions about rewriting systems in order to introduce OPIs of differential type. The basic references about rewriting systems are, for instance,
[
1
]
and the recent lecture notes
[
21
]
.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
V
be a
\bf{k}
-space with a
\bf{k}
-basis
Z
.

(a)

For
f=\sum_{w\in Z}c_{w}w\in V
with
c_{w}\in\mathbf{k},
the support
\operatorname{Supp}(f)
of
f
is the set
\{w\in Z\mid c_{w}\neq 0\}.
By convention, we take
\operatorname{Supp}(0)=\emptyset
.

(b)

Let
f,g\in V
. We use
f\dot{+}g
to indicate the property that
\operatorname{Supp}(f)\cap\operatorname{Supp}(g)=\emptyset.
If this is the case, we say
f+g
is a direct sum of
f
and
g,
and use
f\dot{+}g
also for the sum
f+g
.

(c)

For
f\in V
and
w\in\operatorname{Supp}(f)
with the coefficient
c_{w}
, write
R_{w}(f):=c_{w}w-f\in V
. So
f=c_{w}w-R_{w}(f)
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
V
be a
\bf{k}
-space with a
\bf{k}
-basis
Z
.

(a)

A term-rewriting system
\Pi
on
V
with respect to
Z
is a binary relation
\Pi\subseteq Z\times V
. An element
(t,v)\in\Pi
is called a (term-)rewriting rule of
\Pi,
denoted by
t\rightarrow v
.

(b)

The term-rewriting system
\Pi
is called simple with respect to
Z
if
t\dot{+}v
for all
t\rightarrow v\in\Pi

(c)

If
f=c_{t}t-R_{t}(f)\in V,
using the rewriting rule
t\rightarrow v,
we get a new element
g:=c_{t}v-R_{t}(f)\in V,
called a one-step rewriting of
f
and denoted by
f\rightarrow_{\Pi}g
.

(d)

The reflexive-transitive closure of
\rightarrow_{\Pi}
(as a binary relation on
V
) is denoted by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightarrow}}_{\Pi}
and, if
f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightarrow}}_{\Pi}g,
we say
f
rewrites to
g
with respect to
\Pi
.

(e)

Two elements
f,g\in V
are joinable if there exists
h\in V
such that
f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightarrow}}_{\Pi}h
and
g\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightarrow}}_{\Pi}h
; we denote this by
f\downarrow_{\Pi}g
.

(f)

A term-rewriting system
\Pi
on
V
is called terminating if there is no infinite chain of one-step rewriting

f_{0}\rightarrow_{\Pi}f_{1}\rightarrow_{\Pi}f_{2}\cdots.

(g)

A term-rewriting system
\Pi
is called compatible with a linear order
\geq
on
Z
, if
t>\bar{v}
for each
t\rightarrow v\in\Pi
.

The following theorem supplies a method to show that a single OPI
\phi
is GS by rewriting system. For convenience, we will use the notation

\mathfrak{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)^{k}
(similar for
\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)^{k}
), and
\phi(\mathfrak{u})
for
\phi(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})
in the following. We say that an element

is in
\phi
-normal form if no monomial of

contains any subword of the form
\overline{\phi(\mathfrak{u})}
with
\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)^{k}
.

Theorem 2.18

(
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
\phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathbf{k}\mathfrak{M}(X)
be a multilinear OPI such that
\phi=\overline{\phi}-R(\phi)
, where
R(\phi)
is in
\phi
-normal form. Suppose that, for any set

there is a monomial order
\leq
on
\mathfrak{M}(Z),
such that the following two conditions hold:

(a)

if

are such that

and

for some

and

then

where the

(b)

Lemma 2.19
.

Assume that
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.
Let
\Phi\subset{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
be a system of OPIs,
Z
a set and
\leq
a monomial order on
{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
.
Consider the rewriting system defined as follows

\Pi_{\Phi}(Z):=\left\{~q|_{\overline{\phi(\mathfrak{u})}}\rightarrow q|_{\overline{\phi(\mathfrak{u})}-\phi(\mathfrak{u})}~|~\phi\in\Phi,~\mathfrak{u}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)^{k},~q\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)~\right\}.

If
f,g\in{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
are joinable, then for any
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
such that
w>\overline{f}
and
w>\overline{g}
, we have
f-g
is trivial modulo
(S_{\Phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.

Proof.

By the assumption, there exists

such that

and
g\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightarrow}}_{\Pi_{\Phi}(Z)}h
. By the definition of rewriting, there exist

and

such that

and

and
q^{\prime}_{j}|_{\bar{l_{j}^{\prime}}}\leq\overline{g},~1\leq j\leq t
. Thus,

is trivial modulo
(S_{\Phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi\in{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
be a nonunital differential OPI of type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{b})
or
({\mathcal{N}}3^{\prime})
in Theorem
3.7
. Let
Z
be a set. Then
S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
is an operated
GS basis in
{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
with respect to
\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}
.

We deduce this result from Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
.

For an OPI
\phi
of type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{b})
or
({\mathcal{N}}3^{\prime})
appearing in Theorem
3.7
, we may write

\phi(x,y)=x\lfloor y\rfloor-R(x,y).

By Theorem
1.19
and Proposition
1.20
,
for each set
Z
and for all
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
,
\phi(u,v)
vanishes or its leading monomial is
u\lfloor v\rfloor
with respect to the monomial order
\leq_{\mathrm{dl}}
on
{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi=x\lfloor y\rfloor-R(x,y)\in{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(X)
be an OPI.
Let
Z
be a set such that
{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
is endowed with a monomial order. Assume that for all
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
,
\phi(u,v)
vanishes or its leading monomial is
u\lfloor v\rfloor
. Denote the rewriting system

\Pi_{\phi}(Z):=\{q|_{u\lfloor v\rfloor}\rightarrow q|_{R(u,v)}~|~u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z),~q\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)\}.

If

R(uv,w)\downarrow_{\Pi_{\phi}(Z)}uR(v,w)
, for any
u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
, then
S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
is an operated GS basis in
{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
with respect to the monomial order.

Proof.

We prove the statement by checking all inclusion compositions and intersection compositions are trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.

We firstly consider inclusion compositions. Let
f=\phi(u,v),g\in S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
for
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
, such that
w=\bar{f}=q|_{\bar{g}}
for some
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
and
q\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
. We have the following three cases:

(a)

If
q
has a right factor
\lfloor v^{\prime}\rfloor
with
v^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
. Since
\bar{f}=u\lfloor v\rfloor
, we have
v=v^{\prime}
and thus
there exists
q^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
such that
q=q^{\prime}\lfloor v\rfloor
and
u=q^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}}
. So we have

\displaystyle\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}

\displaystyle=f-q|_{g}

\displaystyle=\phi(u,v)-\phi(q^{\prime}|_{g},v)+\phi(q^{\prime}|_{g},v)-q|_{g}

\displaystyle=\phi(q^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}},v)-\phi(q^{\prime}|_{g},v)+q^{\prime}|_{g}\lfloor v\rfloor-R(q^{\prime}|_{g},v)-q|_{g}

\displaystyle=\phi(q^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}-g},v)-R(q^{\prime}|_{g},v).

By the monomial order, we have
\overline{\phi(q^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}-g},v)}<\overline{\phi(q^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}},v)}=w
and
\overline{R(q^{\prime}|_{g},v)}<\overline{\phi(q^{\prime}|_{g},v)}=w
. Then since
\phi(q^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}-g},v)
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
and
g\in S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
, the inclusion composition
\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}

is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.

(b)

If
q
has a right factor
\lfloor v^{\prime}\rfloor
with
v^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
. Since
\bar{f}=u\lfloor v\rfloor
, we have
v=v^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}}
and
q=u\lfloor v^{\prime}\rfloor
, thus

\displaystyle\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}

\displaystyle=f-q|_{g}

\displaystyle=\phi(u,v^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}})-u\lfloor v^{\prime}|_{g}\rfloor

\displaystyle=\phi(u,v^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}})-\left(u\lfloor v^{\prime}|_{g}\rfloor-R(u,v^{\prime}|_{g})\right)-R(u,v^{\prime}|_{g})

\displaystyle=\phi(u,v^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}})-\phi(u,v^{\prime}|_{g})-R(u,v^{\prime}|_{g})

\displaystyle=\phi(u,v^{\prime}|_{\bar{g}-g})-R(u,v^{\prime}|_{g}),

which is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.

(c)

If
q
has no right factor of the form
\lfloor v^{\prime}\rfloor
, then
\star
is a right factor of
q
and
\overline{g}
has the right factor
\lfloor v\rfloor
, i.e.,
q=t\star
and
\overline{g}=s\lfloor v\rfloor
for some
t,s\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
. So we have
t\overline{g}=\overline{f}
and
g=\phi(s,v)
. By assumption, we have
R(ts,v)\downarrow_{\Pi_{\phi}(Z)}tR(s,v)
and by Lemma
2.19
the inclusion composition
\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.

Next we consider the intersection compositions. Given
f=\phi(u_{1},u_{2})
and
g=\phi(v_{1},v_{2})
with
u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
, assume there exist
a,b,w\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
such that
w=\overline{f}a=b\overline{g}
, i.e.,
w=u_{1}\lfloor u_{2}\rfloor a=bv_{1}\lfloor v_{2}\rfloor
and max
\left\{|\bar{f}|,|\bar{g}|\right\}<\left|w\right|<|\bar{f}|+|\bar{g}|
.
We will write the intersection composition
\left(f,g\right)^{a,b}_{w}
into two inclusion compositions.
Consider the polynomial
h=\phi(bv_{1},v_{2})
. We have
w=\bar{h}=q_{1}|_{\bar{f}}=q_{2}|_{\bar{g}}
, where
q_{1}=\star a
and
q_{2}=b\star
. As we have shown above, the inclusion compositions
\left(h,f\right)^{\star a}_{w}
and
\left(h,g\right)^{b\star}_{w}
are trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
.
And since

\left(f,g\right)^{a,b}_{w}=fa-bg=-(h-fa)+(h-bg)=-\left(h,f\right)^{\star a}_{w}+\left(h,g\right)^{b\star}_{w},

the intersection composition
\left(f,g\right)^{a,b}_{w}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
. ∎

We need a technical lemma which enables us to apply Proposition
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\phi(x,y)=x\lfloor y\rfloor-R(x,y)
be a nonunital differential OPI of type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{b})
or
({\mathcal{N}}3^{\prime})
given in Theorem
3.7
. Let
Z
be a set. Consider the rewriting system

\Pi_{\phi}(Z):=\{q|_{u\lfloor v\rfloor}\rightarrow q|_{R(u,v)}~|~u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z),~q\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)\}.

Then
R(uv,w)\downarrow_{\Pi_{\phi}(Z)}uR(v,w)
, for any
u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
.

Proof.

For
\phi
of type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{b})
, the OPI
\phi
can be written as
x\lfloor y\rfloor-R(x,y)
, where
R(x,y)=\lfloor xy\rfloor-\lfloor x\rfloor y+cxy
.
We have

\displaystyle uR(v,w)

\displaystyle=~u\lfloor vw\rfloor-u\lfloor v\rfloor w+cuvw

\displaystyle\rightarrow\lfloor uvw\rfloor-\lfloor u\rfloor vw+cuvw-(\lfloor uv\rfloor-\lfloor u\rfloor v+cuv)w+cuvw

\displaystyle=~\lfloor uvw\rfloor-\lfloor uv\rfloor w+cuvw

\displaystyle=~R(uv,w).

For the type
({\mathcal{N}}3^{\prime})
in Theorem
3.7
,
\phi(x,y)=x\lfloor y\rfloor-\lfloor xy\rfloor
with
R(x,y)=\lfloor xy\rfloor
. We have

uR(v,w)=u\lfloor vw\rfloor\rightarrow\lfloor uvw\rfloor=R(uv,w).

So in either case,
R(uv,w)\downarrow_{\Pi_{\phi}(Z)}uR(v,w)
, for any
u,v,w\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
.
∎

4.2
and Lemma
4.3

Remark 4.9

\lfloor u\rfloor\lfloor v\rfloor
and
\bar{g}
with
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z),g\in G
. The phenomenon also occurs for the unital case; see Theorem
5.10
(a) and
[
25
, Theorem 6.7]
.

As a special case of Rota-Baxter type, the same construction of linear bases for the free nonunital Nijenhuis algebras over algebras appeared in
[
18
]
.

Notice that one cannot apply Theorem
2.15
to
\lfloor xy\rfloor-\lambda xy
which is of type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{c})=({\mathcal{N}}2^{\prime}_{b})
in Theorem

. Nevertheless,
we could deal with this case directly.

We need to introduce the notions of linearly self-reduced sets and bases.

Definition 4.10

(
[
5
, Definition 1.2.1.2]
)
.

Let
V
be a vector space with a chosen well ordered basis. Each nonzero element of
V
, expressed as a linear combination of basis elements, has a leading basis element which is maximal among all basis elements having nonzero coefficients in this linear combination.

Let
S
be a subset of
V
.
A basis element is said to be linearly reduced with respect to
S
if it is not a leading basis element of any element of
S
. More generally, an element
f\in V
is said to be linearly reduced with respect to
S
, if all basis monomials having nonzero coefficients in
f
are linearly reduced with respect to
S
.

A subset
S\subset V
is said to be linearly self-reduced if each element
s\in S
has its leading basis element with coefficient
1
and is linearly reduced with respect to
S\backslash\{s\}
.

Proposition 4.11

(
[
5
, Proposition 1.2.1.6]
)
.

Let
V
be a vector space with a chosen well ordered basis. Every finite dimensional subspace
S\subset V
has a unique linearly self-reduced basis.

Now we can consider OPIs of the type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{c})=({\mathcal{N}}2^{\prime}_{b})
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
Z
be a set and
A={\mathbf{k}}{\mathcal{S}}(Z)/\penalty I_{A}
an algebra with a finite GS basis
G
with respect to
\leq_{\rm{dlex}}
.
Let
\phi_{4}=\lfloor xy\rfloor-\lambda xy
be an OPI of type
({\mathcal{N}}1^{\prime}_{c})=({\mathcal{N}}2^{\prime}_{b})
in Theorem
3.7
. Denote by
\pi:{\mathbf{k}}{\mathcal{S}}(Z)\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}Z\hookrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}{\mathcal{S}}(Z)
the canonical projection. Then
\pi(G)
is just the set of linear parts of elements of
G
. Let
{\mathbf{k}}\pi(G)
be the subspace of
{\mathbf{k}}Z
spanned by
\pi(G)
.

Let
G^{(1)}
be the unique linearly self-reduced basis of
{\mathbf{k}}\pi(G)
and define

{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=\{\lfloor g\rfloor-\lambda g~\big|~g\in G^{(1)}\}.

Then
S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
is an operated GS basis of
\langle S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup I_{A}\rangle_{\mathfrak{OpAlg}}
in
{\mathbf{k}}{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
with respect to
\leq_{\rm{db}}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
is a subset of
\langle S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup I_{A}\rangle_{\mathfrak{OpAlg}}
.
Since
G^{(1)}\subset{\mathbf{k}}\pi(G)
, any element in
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}=\{\lfloor g\rfloor-\lambda g~\big|~g\in G^{(1)}\}
can be written as a linear combination of the elements in the set
\left\{\lfloor\pi(g)\rfloor-\lambda\pi(g)~|~g\in G\right\}
.
And for any
g\in G
,

\lfloor\pi(g)\rfloor-\lambda\pi(g)=\left(\lfloor\pi(g)-g\rfloor-\lambda(\pi(g)-g)\right)+\lfloor g\rfloor-\lambda g\in\langle S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup I_{A}\rangle_{\mathfrak{OpAlg}},

since
\lfloor\pi(g)-g\rfloor-\lambda(\pi(g)-g)\in S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
and
\lfloor g\rfloor,g\in\langle I_{A}\rangle_{\mathfrak{OpAlg}}
. It follows that
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\subset\langle S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup I_{A}\rangle_{\mathfrak{OpAlg}}.

Now we show
S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
is an operated GS basis. Notice that for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z)
, the leading monomial of
\phi_{4}(u,v)=\lfloor uv\rfloor-\lambda uv
is
\lfloor uv\rfloor
with respect to
\leq_{\rm{db}}
.
For any elements
a_{1}\in S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
,
a_{2}\in G
and
a_{3}\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
, the leading monomials are of the forms
\overline{a}_{1}=\lfloor uv\rfloor
,
\overline{a}_{2}=w
and
\overline{a}_{3}=\lfloor z\rfloor
, where
u,v\in{\mathfrak{S}}(Z),~w\in G\subset{\mathcal{S}}(Z)
and
z\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\subset Z
. Hence, these three leading monomials have no overlap with each other.
And since each of the sets
S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))
,
G
and
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
is an operated GS basis, we only need to check the triviality of any inclusion composition
\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}
modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
, for the cases
(f\in S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),~g\in G)
,
(f\in S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),~g\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})
and
(f\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},~g\in G)
.

(a) For the case
w=\overline{f}=q|_{\overline{g}}
with
f\in S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),~g\in G
. There exists
q^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
such that
q=\lfloor q^{\prime}\rfloor
and
f=\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}
.

If
g\in{\mathbf{k}}Z
, there exists a factor of
q^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{S}}(Z)
. Then we have

\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}=f-q|_{g}=\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}-\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g}\rfloor=(\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g})-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g}.

It is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
since
\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
and
g\in G
.

If
g\in G\backslash{\mathbf{k}}Z
, it can be written as
g=g_{1}+\pi(g)\in{\mathbf{k}}({\mathcal{S}}(Z)\backslash Z)\oplus{\mathbf{k}}Z
and we have
\overline{g}=\overline{g}_{1}
. The inclusion composition
\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}
can be written as

\displaystyle\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}

\displaystyle=f-q|_{g}

\displaystyle=\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}-\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}+\pi(g)}\rfloor

\displaystyle=\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-(\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}+\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g-\overline{g}})+\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g-\overline{g}}-(\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}}\rfloor+\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}\rfloor)

\displaystyle=(\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}}\rfloor)-(\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}+\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g-\overline{g}})+\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g-g_{1}}+\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}-\overline{g}}-\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}\rfloor

\displaystyle=-(\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}-\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}-\overline{g}})-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g}-(\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}).

Since
\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}-\bar{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g_{1}-\bar{g}}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
and
g\in G
, the first two terms are trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
. Now we only need to consider the third term.
Since
G^{(1)}
is the linearly self-reduced basis of
{\mathbf{k}}\pi(G)
, we have
\pi(g)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}g_{i}
where
g_{i}\in G^{(1)}
. Notice that we have
\bar{g}_{i}\leq\overline{\pi(g)}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Otherwise, there exist
g_{i},g_{j}\in G^{(1)}
such that
\overline{g}_{i}=\overline{g}_{j}
, which contradicts to that
G^{(1)}
is linearly self-reduced. If
q^{\prime}
has a factor in
{\mathcal{S}}(Z)
, we have
\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
; otherwise,
q^{\prime}=\star
and thus
\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\pi(g)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(\lfloor g_{i}\rfloor-\lambda g_{i})
is trivial modulo
({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
. So we have shown that the inclusion composition
\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
.

(b) For the case
w=\overline{f}=q|_{\overline{g}}
with
f\in S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),~g\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
. There exists
q^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{S}}^{\star}(Z)
such that
q=\lfloor q^{\prime}\rfloor
and
f=\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}
. Since
\overline{g}\in\lfloor Z\rfloor
, there exists a factor of
q^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{S}}(Z)
. Thus we have

\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}=f-q|_{g}=\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}}-\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{g}\rfloor=(\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g})-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{g},

which is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
since
\lfloor q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g}\rfloor-\lambda q^{\prime}|_{\overline{g}-g}
is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z)),w)
and
g\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
.

(c) For the case
w=\overline{f}=q|_{\overline{g}}
with
f\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},~g\in G
. Write
f=\lfloor f_{1}\rfloor-\lambda f_{1}
, where
f_{1}\in G^{(1)}
. Since
\overline{f}=q|_{\overline{g}}\in\lfloor Z\rfloor
, we have
q=\lfloor\star\rfloor
,
\bar{f}_{1}=\bar{g}
and
g=\pi(g)\in\pi(G)
. Since
G^{(1)}
is the linearly self-reduced basis of
{\mathbf{k}}\pi(G)
, we have
f_{1}-g=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}g_{i}
, where
g_{i}\in G^{(1)}
and
\bar{g}_{i}<\bar{f}_{1}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. So the inclusion composition

\left(f,g\right)^{q}_{w}=\lfloor f_{1}\rfloor-\lambda f_{1}-\lfloor g\rfloor=\left(\lfloor f_{1}-g\rfloor-\lambda(f_{1}-g)\right)-\lambda g

is trivial modulo
(S_{\phi_{4}}({\mathfrak{S}}(Z))\cup G\cup{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
, since
\lfloor f_{1}-g\rfloor-\lambda(f_{1}-g)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}(\lfloor g_{i}\rfloor-\lambda g_{i})
is trivial modulo
({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime},w)
and
g\in G
.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

The OPIs of type
({\mathcal{U}}1^{\prime}_{a})
in Theorem
3.3
are unital GS with respect to the monomial order
\leq_{\rm{db}}
.

Proof.

We will prove the lemma by checking such OPIs satisfy the conditions in Theorem
2.18
.
According to the monomial order
\leq_{\rm{db}}
, the leading monomial of such an OPI is
\lfloor x\rfloor\lfloor y\rfloor
. Consider the rewriting system defined by

\Pi_{\phi}(Z):=\left\{~\lfloor u\rfloor\lfloor v\rfloor\rightarrow\lambda\lfloor u\rfloor v+\lambda u\lfloor v\rfloor-\mu\lfloor uv\rfloor-\nu uv~|~u,v\in{\mathfrak{M}}(Z)~\right\}.

For the first condition in Theorem
2.18
, assume there are
u,v,v^{\prime},w,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathfrak{M}(Z)
, such that

\lfloor u\rfloor\lfloor v\rfloor=\alpha\beta
and
\lfloor v^{\prime}\rfloor\lfloor w\rfloor=\beta\gamma
. Then we only need to consider the case
\alpha=\lfloor u\rfloor
,
\beta=\lfloor v\rfloor=\lfloor v^{\prime}\rfloor
and
\gamma=\lfloor w\rfloor
.

On one hand, we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}&R(\phi(u,v))\gamma\\
\vskip 4.30554pt=&\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor+\lambda u\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor-\mu\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor-\nu uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\\
\vskip 4.30554pt\rightarrow&\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor+\lambda u(\lambda\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor w+\lambda v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor-\mu\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor-\nu vw)\\
&-b(\lambda\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor w+\lambda uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor-\mu\left\lfloor uvw\right\rfloor-\nu uvw)-\nu uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\\
\vskip 4.30554pt=&\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor+\lambda^{2}u\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor w+\lambda^{2}uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor-\lambda\mu u\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor-\lambda\nu uvw\\
&-\lambda\mu\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor w-\lambda\mu uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor+\mu^{2}\left\lfloor uvw\right\rfloor+\mu\nu uvw-\nu uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\\
\vskip 4.30554pt=&\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor+\lambda^{2}u\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor w-\lambda\mu u\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor\\
&-\lambda\mu\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor w+\mu^{2}\left\lfloor uvw\right\rfloor-(\lambda-\mu)\nu uvw,\end{array}

where the last equation holds since the coefficient of
uv\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor
is
\lambda^{2}-\lambda\mu-\nu
, which vanishes by definition.

On the other hand,

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\alpha R(\phi(v,x))\\
\vskip 4.30554pt=&\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor w+\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor-\mu\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor-\nu\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor vw\\
\vskip 4.30554pt\rightarrow&\lambda(\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v+\lambda u\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor-\mu\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor-\nu uv)w+\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\\
&-\mu(\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor vw+\lambda u\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor-\mu\left\lfloor uvw\right\rfloor-\nu uvw)-\nu\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor vw\\
\vskip 4.30554pt=&\lambda^{2}\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor vw+\lambda^{2}u\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor w-\lambda\mu\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor w-\lambda\nu uvw+\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor\\
&-\lambda\mu\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor vw-\lambda\mu u\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor+\mu^{2}\left\lfloor uvw\right\rfloor+\mu\nu uvw-\nu\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor vw\\
\vskip 4.30554pt=&\lambda\left\lfloor u\right\rfloor v\left\lfloor w\right\rfloor+\lambda^{2}u\left\lfloor v\right\rfloor w-\lambda\mu u\left\lfloor vw\right\rfloor\\
&-\lambda\mu\left\lfloor uv\right\rfloor w+\mu^{2}\left\lfloor uvw\right\rfloor-(\lambda-\mu)\nu uvw\quad(\text{by~}\lambda^{2}=\lambda\mu+\nu).\end{array}

We have shown that
R(\phi(u,v))\gamma\downarrow_{\Pi_{\phi}(Z)}\alpha R(\phi(v,x))
. For the second condition in Theorem
2.18
, assume that there are
q\neq\star\in\mathfrak{M}^{\star}(Z)
and
u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{M}(Z)
, such that
\lfloor u_{1}\rfloor\lfloor u_{2}\rfloor=q|_{\lfloor v_{1}\rfloor\lfloor v_{2}\rfloor}
, then
\lfloor v_{1}\rfloor\lfloor v_{2}\rfloor
is a subword of
u_{1}
or
u_{2}
.

We are done.
∎

Derivation of generalized Einstein’s equations of gravitation in inertial systems based on a sink flow model of particles
Xiao-Song Wang

Abstract
J. C. Maxwell, B. Riemann and H. Poincar e ´ \acute{e} have proposed the idea that all microscopic particles are sink flows in a fluidic aether. Following this research program, a previous theory of gravitation based on a mechanical model of vacuum and a sink flow model of particles is generalized by methods of special relativistic continuum mechanics. In inertial coordinate systems, we construct a tensorial potential which satisfies the wave equations. Inspired by the equations of motion of a test particle, a definition of a metric tensor of a Riemannian spacetime is introduced. Applying Fock’s theorem, generalized Einstein’s equations in inertial systems are derived based on some assumptions. These equations reduce to Einstein’s equations in case of weak field in harmonic coordinate systems.

The Einstein’s equations of gravitational fields in the theory of general relativity can be written as

The Einstein’s equations (

;
Misner et al. 1973

There is a long history of researches of derivations or interpretations of Einstein’s theory of general relativity.

Misner et al. 1973

Introduction of a previous theory of gravitation based on a sink flow model of particles by methods of classical fluid mechanics

What impresses our senses as matter is really a great concentration of energy into a comparatively small space. We could regard matter as the regions in space where the field is extremely strong.

The existence of the

Definition 1

If a coordinates system
S

S_{\Omega(1)}

The field equations Eqs. (

S_{\Omega(1)}

S^{\prime}
. In an inertial coordinate system
S

(t,x,y,z)
. In an inertial system
S^{\prime}
, this event is characterized by four other coordinates
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
. We assume that the origins of the Cartesian coordinates in the two inertial systems
S
and
S^{\prime}

t=t^{\prime}=0
. Then, the connections between these space-time coordinates are given by a homogeneous linear transformation keeping the quantity

invariant, i.e., (
Moller 1955
, p. 92)

s^{2}=c^{2}t^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2}-z^{2}=c^{2}t^{\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2}-y^{\prime 2}-z^{\prime 2}=s^{\prime 2}.

(94)

We introduce the following two coordinate systems

\displaystyle x^{0}=ct,\quad x^{1}=x,\quad x^{2}=y,\quad x^{3}=z,

\displaystyle x^{\prime 0}=ct^{\prime},\quad x^{\prime 1}=x^{\prime},\quad x^{\prime 2}=y^{\prime},\quad x^{\prime 3}=z^{\prime}.

(95)

The homogeneous linear transformation keeping the quantity
s^{2}
invariant, which is usually called the Lorentz transformation, can be written as (
Ohanian and Ruffini 2013
, p. 57;

, p. 92)

x^{\prime\mu}=\alpha^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu},

(96)

where
\alpha^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
are coefficients depend only on the angles between the spatial axes in the two inertial systems
S
and
S^{\prime}
and on the relative velocity of
S
and
S^{\prime}
.

Applying the standard methods in theory of special relativity
Moller 1955
, we have the following results.

T^{\mu\nu}

VIII
The equations of motion of a point particle in a gravitational field and introduction of an effective Riemannian spacetime

In this section, we study the equations of motion of a free point particle in a gravitational field. The Lagrangian of a free point particle can be written as (
Feynman 1995
, p. 57;
Thirring 1961
)

L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}m\frac{dx^{\mu}}{d\tau}\frac{dx_{\mu}}{d\tau}=\frac{1}{2}mu^{\mu}u_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}m\eta_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu},

(99)

where
m
is the rest mass of the point particle,
d\tau\equiv\frac{1}{c}\sqrt{dx^{\mu}dx_{\mu}}
is the infinitesimal proper time interval,
u^{\mu}\equiv dx^{\mu}/d\tau
.

Suppose that
T_{\Omega(1)}^{\mu\nu}\approx 0
. Ignoring those higher terms
O[(f_{0}\psi_{\mu\nu})^{2}]
in Eq. (
51
), the interaction term of the Lagrangian of a system of the
\Omega(0)
substratum, the
\Omega(1)
substratum and the point particle can be written in the following form (
Feynman 1995
, p. 57;
Thirring 1961
)

L_{\mathrm{int}}=f_{0}\psi_{\mu\nu}mu^{\mu}u^{\nu}.

(100)

Using Eq. (
100
) and Eq. (
99
), the total Lagrangian
L_{\mathrm{tot}}
of a system of the
\Omega(0)
substratum, the
\Omega(1)
substratum and the point particle can be written as (
Feynman 1995
, p. 57)

L_{\mathrm{tot}}=L_{0}+L_{\mathrm{int}}=\frac{1}{2}mu^{\mu}u_{\mu}+f_{0}\psi_{\mu\nu}mu^{\mu}u^{\nu}.

(101)

The Euler-Lagrange equations for the total Lagrangian
L_{\mathrm{tot}}
can be written as (
Ohanian and Ruffini 2013
,p. 111)

\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[(\eta_{\mu\nu}+2f_{0}\psi_{\mu\nu})\frac{dx^{\nu}}{d\tau}\right]-f_{0}\frac{\partial\psi_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{dx^{\alpha}}{d\tau}\frac{dx^{\beta}}{d\tau}=0.

(102)

We notice that the equations of motion (
102
) of a point particle in gravitational field are similar to the equations of a geodesic line (
104
) in a Riemannian spacetime. Thus, it is natural for us to introduce the following definition of a metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
of a Riemannian spacetime (
Feynman 1995
, p. 57)

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2f_{0}\psi_{\mu\nu}.

(103)

Then, the equations of motion Eqs. (
102
) can be written as (
Feynman 1995
, p. 58)

\frac{d}{d\tau_{g}}\left(g_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{d\tau_{g}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial g_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{dx^{\alpha}}{d\tau_{g}}\frac{dx^{\beta}}{d\tau_{g}},

(104)

where
\tau_{g}
is the proper time interval in the Riemannian spacetime with a metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
.

Eqs. (
104
) represent a geodesic line in a Riemannian spacetime with a metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
, which can also be written as (
Liu 2004
, p. 51)

\frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{d\tau_{g}^{2}}+\Gamma_{\nu\sigma}^{\mu}\frac{dx^{\nu}}{d\tau_{g}}\frac{dx^{\sigma}}{d\tau_{g}}=0,

(105)

where

\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\nu}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bigtriangleup}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{\partial g_{\mu\alpha}}{\partial x^{\beta}}+\frac{\partial g_{\mu\beta}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial g_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right)

(106)

are the Christoffel symbols.

Thus, we find that the equations of motion (
102
) of a point particle in gravitational field are approximately a geodesic line described in Eqs. (

X
Conclusion

We extend our previous theory of gravitation based on a sink flow model of particles by methods of special relativistic fluid mechanics. In inertial coordinate systems, we construct a tensorial potential of the
\Omega(0)
substratum. Based on some assumptions, we show that this tensorial potential satisfies the wave equations. Inspired by the equations of motion of a test particle, a definition of a metric tensor of a Riemannian spacetime is introduced. Generalized Einstein’s equations in inertial coordinate systems are derived based on some assumptions. These equations reduce to the Einstein’s equations in case of weak field in harmonic coordinate systems. In our theory, gravity is transmitted by the
\Omega(0)
substratum. The theory of general relativity can not provide a physical definition of the metric tensor of the Riemannian spacetime. In our theory, the background spacetime is the Minkowshi spacetime. However, the flat background spacetime is no longer physically observable. According to the equations of motion of a point particle in gravitational field, to first order, the physically observable spacetime is a Riemannian spacetime. The metric tensor of this Riemannian spacetime is defined based on the tensorial potential of gravitational fields.

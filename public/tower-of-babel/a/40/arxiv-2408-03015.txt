Abstract
The CNO cycle is one of the most important nuclear energy sources in stars. At temperatures of hydrostatic H-burning (20 MK < < T < < 80 MK) the 17 O(p, γ \gamma ) 18 F reaction rate is dominated by the poorly constrained 64.5 keV resonance. Here we report on the first direct measurements of its resonance strength and of the direct capture contribution at 142 keV, performed with a new high sensitivity setup at LUNA. The present resonance strength of ω ​ γ ( p , γ ) \omega\gamma_{(p,\gamma)} bare = (30 ± \pm 6 stat ± \pm 2 syst ) peV is about a factor of 2 higher than the values in literature, leading to a Γ \Gamma p bare = (34 ± \pm 7 stat ± \pm 3 syst ) neV, in agreement with LUNA result from the (p, α \alpha ) channel. Such agreement strengthen our understanding of the oxygen isotopic ratios measured in red giant stars and in O-rich presolar grains.

The LUNA deep-underground location guarantees a muon- (and neutron-) background level six (and three) orders of magnitude lower than above ground

A high-intensity proton beam (average current on target

33

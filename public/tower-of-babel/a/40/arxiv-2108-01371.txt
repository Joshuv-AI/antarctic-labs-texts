Coordinate-free exponentials of general multivector (MV) in 𝐶𝑙 p , q \mathit{Cl}_{p,q} algebras for p + q = 3 p+q=3 † \dagger Corresponding author: A. Acus
A. Acus, A. Dargys

Abstract
. Closed form expressions in a coordinate-free form in real Clifford geometric algebras (GAs) 𝐶𝑙 0 , 3 \mathit{Cl}_{0,3} , 𝐶𝑙 3 , 0 \mathit{Cl}_{3,0} , 𝐶𝑙 1 , 2 \mathit{Cl}_{1,2} and 𝐶𝑙 2 , 1 \mathit{Cl}_{2,1} are found for exponential function when the exponent is the most general multivector (MV). The main difficulty in solving the problem is connected with an entanglement or mixing of vector and bivector components. After disentanglement, the obtained formulas simplify to the well-known Moivre-type trigonometric/hyperbolic function for vector or bivector exponentials. The presented formulas may find wide application in solving GA differential equations, in signal processing, automatic control and robotics.

2.1.
General properties of GA exponential

The exponential of MV is another MV that belongs to the same
geometric algebra. Therefore, we shall assume that the defining
equation for exponential is
\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}=\mathsf{B}
, where

\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}\in\mathit{Cl}_{p,q}
and
p+q=3
. The following properties
hold for MV exponential:

\begin{split}&\exp(\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B})=\exp(\mathsf{A})\exp(\mathsf{B})\quad\text{if and only if\ }\mathsf{A}\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{B}\mathsf{A},\\
&\widetilde{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}},\quad\wideparen{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}},\quad\widetilde{\wideparen{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{\wideparen{\mathsf{A}}}},\\
&\mathsf{V}\,\exp(\mathsf{A})\mathsf{V}^{-1}=\exp(\mathsf{V}\mathsf{A}\mathsf{V}^{-1}).\end{split}

(2.5)

From first formula an exponential identity

\exp\mathsf{A}=(\exp\mathsf{A/m})^{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
follows. In the last expression
the transformation
\mathsf{V}
, for example the rotor, has been lifted
to exponent.

The GA exponential
\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{A}}
can be expanded in a series that
has exactly the same structure as a scalar
exponential
[
2
]
, from which GA trigonometric and
hyperbolic GA functions as well as various other relations that
are analogues of respective scalars functions
follow
[
10
]
. For example,

\begin{split}&\cos^{2}\mathsf{A}+\sin^{2}\mathsf{A}=1,\quad\cosh^{2}\mathsf{A}-\sinh^{2}\mathsf{A}=1,\\
&\sin(2\mathsf{A})=2\sin\mathsf{A}\cos\mathsf{A}=2\cos\mathsf{A}\sin\mathsf{A},\\
&\cos(2\mathsf{A})=\cos^{2}\mathsf{A}-\sin^{2}\mathsf{A}.\end{split}

(2.6)

Also, it should be noted that GA functions of the same argument
commute. Thus, the sine and cosine functions as well as hyperbolic
GA sine and cosine functions satisfy:

\sin\mathsf{A}\cos\mathsf{A}=\cos\mathsf{A}\sin\mathsf{A}
and

\sinh\mathsf{A}\cosh\mathsf{A}=\cosh\mathsf{A}\sinh\mathsf{A}
.

6.
Examples of application: Solution of GA differential equations

The exponential function plays a fundamental role in solution of
linear differential equations. For example, the solution of a
homogeneous equation for MV
\mathsf{X}
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{X}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathsf{X}(t),\quad\mathsf{X}(0)=\mathsf{X}_{0},

(6.1)

is GA exponential
\mathsf{X}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{t\mathsf{A}}\mathsf{X}_{0}
, where
t
is the
parameter (in physics usually it is the time). Treating
t\mathsf{A}

as a new MV, after expansion of exponential we will obtain the
evolution of MV in time. More generally, with suitable assumptions
upon smoothness of
\mathsf{X}(t)
, the solution of the inhomogeneous
system

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{X}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathsf{X}(t)+f(t),\quad\mathsf{X}(0)=\mathsf{X}_{0},

(6.2)

may be expressed by

\mathsf{X}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{t\mathsf{A}}\mathsf{X}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{(t-s)\mathsf{A}}f(s)\mathrm{d}s.

(6.3)

Some of differential MV equations, for example,

\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathsf{X}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\mathsf{A}\mathsf{X}(t)+\mathsf{B}\mathsf{X}(t),\quad\mathsf{X}(0)=\mathsf{X}_{0},

(6.4)

have solutions that consist of products of GA exponentials:

\mathsf{X}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{t\mathsf{A}}\,{X}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{t\mathsf{B}}
. The answer can be
easily checked by direct substitution of
\mathsf{X}(t)
into
Eq. (
6.4
) and application of Leibniz’s
differentiation theorem
[
15
]
.

Trigonometric GA functions, as well as GA roots, arise in the
solution of second order differential equations. For example, the
GA equation
[
16
]

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathsf{X}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}+\mathsf{A}\mathsf{X}=0,\quad\mathsf{X}(0)=\mathsf{X}_{0}\text{\ and \ }(\mathrm{d}\mathsf{X}/\mathrm{d}t)_{t=0}=\mathsf{X}^{\prime}_{0}

(6.5)

has the solution

\mathsf{X}(t)=\cos(\sqrt{\mathsf{A}}\,t)\mathsf{X}_{0}+\big(\sqrt{\mathsf{A}}\big)^{-1}\sin(\sqrt{\mathsf{A}}\,t)\mathsf{X}_{0}^{\prime},

(6.6)

where
\sqrt{\mathsf{A}}
is the square root of
\mathsf{A}
. The
trigonometric functions of MV argument can be expressed by
exponentials
[
10
]
. Closed form expression for square
root of MV in
p+q=3
can be found
in
[
17
]
.

A concrete example of application of GA exponential in physics can be found
in
[
10
]
.

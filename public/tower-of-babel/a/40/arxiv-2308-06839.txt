Generalized divisor functions in arithmetic progressions: I Journal: J. Number Theory 227 (2021), pp. 30-93.
David T. Nguyen

Abstract
We prove some distribution results for the k k -fold divisor function in arithmetic progressions to moduli that exceed the square-root of length X X of the sum, with appropriate constrains and averaging on the moduli, saving a power of X X from the trivial bound. On assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, we obtain uniform power saving error terms that are independent of k k . We follow and specialize Y.T. Zhang’s method on bounded gaps between primes to our setting. Our arguments are essentially self-contained, with the exception on the use of Deligne’s work on the Riemann Hypothesis for varieties over finite fields. In particular, we avoid the reliance on Siegel’s theorem, leading to some effective estimates.

Introduction and statement of results

Let
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
be integers. Let
\tau_{k}(n)
denote the
k
-fold divisor function

\tau_{k}(n)=\sum_{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}=n}1,

(1.1)

where the sum runs over ordered
k
-tuples
(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k})
of positive integers for which
n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}=n
. Thus
\tau_{k}(n)
is the coefficient of
n^{-s}
in the Dirichlet series

\zeta(s)^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau_{k}(n)n^{-s}.

(1.2)

It is well known that the function
\tau_{k}
is closely related to prime numbers. This paper is concerned with the distribution of
\tau_{k}(n)
in arithmetic progressions to moduli
d
that exceed the square-root of length of the sum, in particular, provides a sharpening of the result in
[
32
]
. We next give a brief background of the problem and present our main result.

1.1
Survey and main result

Towards the end of the 18th century, Gauss conjectured the celebrated Prime Number Theorem concerning the sum

\sum_{p\leq X}1

(1.3)

as
X
approaches infinity, where
p
denotes a prime. It is more convenience to count primes with weight
\log p
instead of weight 1, c.f. Chebyshev; this leads to consideration of the sum

\sum_{p\leq X}\log p.

(1.4)

To access the Riemann zeta function more conveniently we also count powers of primes, leading to the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{\alpha}\leq X\\
\alpha\geq 1\end{subarray}}\log p,

(1.5)

which is equal to the unconstrained sum over
n

\sum_{n\leq X}\Lambda(n)

(1.6)

where
\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function–the coefficient of
n^{-s}
in the series
-\zeta^{\prime}(s)/\zeta(s)
. In 1837, Dirichlet considered the deep question of primes in arithmetic progression, leading him to consider sums of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(\text{mod }d)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)

(1.7)

for
(d,a)=1
. More generally, the function
\Lambda(n)
is replaced by an arithmetic function
f(n)
, satisfying certain growth conditions, and we arrive at the study of the congruence sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(\text{mod }d)\end{subarray}}f(n).

(1.8)

This sum (
1.8
) is our main object of study.

For most
f
appearing in applications, it is expected that
f
is distributed equally among the reduced residue classes
a(\text{mod }d)
with
(a,d)=1
, e.g., that the sum (
1.8
) is well approximated by the average

\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}f(n)

(1.9)

since there are
\varphi(d)
reduced residue classes modulo
d
, where
\varphi(n)
is the Euler’s totient function. The quantity (
1.9
) is often thought of as the ‘main term’. Different main terms are also considered. Thus, the study of (
1.8
) is reduced to studying the ‘error term’

\Delta(f;X,d,a):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(\text{mod }d)\end{subarray}}f(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}f(n),\quad\text{for }(a,d)=1.

(1.10)

measuring the discrepancy between the the sum (
1.8
) and the expected value (
1.9
). If
f
satisfies

f(n)\leq C\tau^{B}(n)\log^{B}X

(1.11)

for some constants
B,C>0
, which is often the case for most
f
in applications, then a trivial bound for the discrepancy
\Delta(f;X,d,a)
is

\Delta(f;X,d,a)\leq C^{\prime}X\log^{B^{\prime}}X,

(1.12)

for some constants
B^{\prime},C^{\prime}>0
. The objective is then to obtain a non-trivial upper bound such as

\Delta(f;X,d,a)\ll\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\frac{X}{\log^{A}X},\quad A>0,

(1.13)

or

\Delta(f;X,d,a)\ll\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}X^{1-\delta},\quad 0<\delta<1,

(1.14)

with
d
in a certain range depending on
X
.

For
f(n)=\Lambda(n)
, the von Mangoldt function, the clasical Siegel-Walfisz theorem implies that (
1.13
) holds uniformly in the range
d<\log^{B}X
, where
B>0
with
A
depending on
B
. For
f(n)=\tau_{k}(n)
, the estimate (
1.14
) is valid uniformly in the range
d\leq X^{\theta_{k}-\epsilon}

Theorem 1

(Main theorem)
.

Let

\varpi=\frac{1}{1168}

(1.20)

and

\theta_{k}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{12(k+2)},\varpi^{2}\right\}.

(1.21)

For
a\neq 0
, let

\mathcal{D}=\{d\geq 1:(d,a)=1,\ |\mu(d)|=1,\ (d,\prod_{p\leq X^{\varpi^{2}}}p)<X^{\varpi},\ \text{and}\ (d,\prod_{p\leq X^{\varpi}}p)>X^{71/584}\},

(1.22)

where
\mu
is the Möbius function. Then for each
k\geq 4
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathcal{D}\\
d<X^{293/584}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(\text{mod }d)\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)-\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)\right|\ll X^{1-\theta_{k}}.

(1.23)

The implied constant is effective, and depends at most on
a
and
k
.

.

Results on further averaging

In a function field variant, the work of Keating, Rodgers, Roditty-Gershon, and Rudnick in

Conjecture 1

For

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\\

\gamma_{k}(c)=\frac{1}{k!G(k+1)^{2}}\int_{[0,1]^{k}}\delta_{c}(w_{1}+\cdots w_{k})\Delta(w)^{2}d^{k}w,

where

2.2
Sketch of the proof of the main theorem

Here, and in the rest of the paper, we fix an integer
k\geq 4
, unless specified otherwise.

To prove (
1.23
) we follow standard practice and split the summation over moduli
d
in into two sums: one over
d<X^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}
which are called small moduli and the other over
X^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\leq d<X^{\frac{1}{2}+2\varpi}
which are called large moduli. For small moduli, we estimate (
1.23
) directly using the large sieve inequality together with a direct substitute for the Siegel-Walfisz condition. For the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda(n)
, the Möbius function
\mu(n)
is involved and, hence, the Siegel-Walfisz theorem is needed to handle very small moduli. For us, fortunately,
\tau_{k}
is simpler than
\Lambda
in that
\mu
is absent–this feature of
\tau_{k}
allows us to get a sharper bound in place of the Siegel-Walfisz theorem; see Lemma
8
below. The constant here is effective.

For large moduli, we adapt the methods of Zhang in
[
34
]
to bound the error term which goes as follows. After applying suitable combinatorial arguments, we split
\tau_{k}
into appropriate convolutions as Type I, II, and III, as modeled in
[
34
]
. We treat the Type I and II in our Case (b), Type III in our Case (c), and Case (a) corresponds to a trivial case which we treat directly. The main ingredients in Case (b) are the dispersion method and Weil bound on Kloosterman sums. The Case (c) depends crucially on the factorization
d=qr
of the moduli to Weil shift a certain incomplete Kloosterman sum to the modulus
r
. The shift modulo this
r
then induces a Ramanujan sum, which is known to have better than square-root cancellation. This allows for a saving of a power of
r
, and since
d
is a multiple of
r
, and
d
is less than
X
, this saves a small power of
X
from the trivial bound.

In reducing nonprincipal characters, which may have not too small moduli, to primitive characters for the application of large sieve inequality, very small moduli of the primitive characters may occur. We treat contributions from those small moduli via the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive character (mod
d
). For
d<X^{1/3(k+1)}
we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)\ll X^{1-\frac{1}{3(k+2)}}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Decompose the interval
[1,X]
in to dyadic intervals of the form
[N,2N)
. Denote by

\psi(\chi)=\sum_{n\sim N}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n).

(3.4)

Let
0<\eta<1
be a parameter to be specified latter (see (
3.26
) below). Let
f(x)
be a function of
C^{\infty}(-\infty,\infty)
class such that
0\leq f(y)\leq 1
,

f(y)=1\quad\text{ if }\quad N\leq y\leq 2N,

(3.5)

f(y)=0\quad\text{ if }\quad y\notin[N-N^{\eta},2N+N^{\eta}],

(3.6)

and obeying the derivative bound

f^{(j)}(y)\ll N^{-j\eta},\ j\geq 1,

(3.7)

where the implied constant depends on
\eta
and
j
at most.
Let

\psi^{*}(\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)f(n).

(3.8)

By (
3.1
), we have

\psi^{*}(\chi)-\psi(\chi)=\sum_{N-N^{\eta}\leq n\leq N}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)+\sum_{2N\leq n\leq 2N+N^{\eta}}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)\ll N^{\eta+\epsilon}

(3.9)

for any
\epsilon>0
. Let

F(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)x^{s-1}dx

(3.10)

be the Mellin transform of
f(x)
. The function
F(s)
is absolutely convergent for
\sigma>0
with inverse Mellin transform

f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}F(s)x^{-s}ds,

(3.11)

where
\int_{(c)}
denotes the integration
\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}
over the vertical line
c+it
where
t
runs from
-\infty
to
\infty
. Substituting (
3.11
) into (
3.8
) and changing the order of summation and integration, we get

\displaystyle\psi^{*}(\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}F(s)n^{-s}ds\right)

(3.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}F(s)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)n^{-s}\right)ds

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}ds,

(3.14)

where
L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi(n)n^{-s}
is the Dirichlet series for
\chi
. Since the function
L(s,\chi)
, and thus,
F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}
has no poles in
\sigma\geq 0
, we may move the line of integration in (
3.14
) from
\sigma=2
to
\sigma=1/2
and obtain

\psi^{*}(\chi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\frac{1}{2})}F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}ds.

(3.15)

We next estimate this integral by bounding the integrand and splitting the line of integration into two parts, over
|t|<T
and
|t|\geq T
, then choosing
T
suitably (see (
3.24
) below). For
\sigma=1/2
, we have the convexity bound; see, e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem 5.23]
,

|L(s,\chi)|^{k}\ll d^{k/4}|s|^{k}.

(3.16)

We next obtain upper bound for
F(s)
. On the line
\sigma=1/2
, we have, by definitions of
F(s)
and
f(x)
,

F(s)=\int_{N-N^{\eta}}^{2N+N^{\eta}}f(x)x^{s-1}dx\leq\int_{N-N^{\eta}}^{2N+N^{\eta}}x^{-1/2}dx\ll N^{1/2}.

(3.17)

This bound is sufficient for bounding small
|t|
in (
3.15
), but too large for
|t|
large. To bound contribution from large
|t|
we fix an

\ell>k+1

(3.18)

and apply integration by parts
\ell
times to
F(s)
:

\displaystyle F(s)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{s(s+1)\cdots(s+\ell+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(\ell)}(x)x^{s+\ell-1}dx.

(3.19)

Hence, by the derivative bound (
3.7
),
F(s)
is bounded by

\displaystyle|F(s)|\ll\frac{1}{|s|^{\ell}}N^{-\ell\eta+1/2+\ell}\ll\frac{1}{|s|^{\ell}}N^{(1-\eta)\ell+1/2}.

(3.20)

This bound allows us to save an arbitrary negative power of
|s|
; we will use this bound for large
|t|
.

We now split the integral in (
3.15
) into two and estimate each part individually. Let
s=1/2+it
. For
T>2
, we can write
\psi^{*}(\chi)
in (
3.15
) as

\psi^{*}(\chi)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|<T}F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}ds+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|\geq T}F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}ds.

(3.21)

By (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), the first term on the right side is

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|<T}F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}ds\ll N^{1/2}d^{k/4}\int_{-T}^{T}|1/2+it|^{k}dt\ll N^{1/2}d^{k/4}T^{k+1};

(3.22)

while by (
3.16
), (
3.20
), and (
3.18
), the second term on the right side is

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|t|\geq T}F(s)L(s,\chi)^{k}ds\ll N^{(1-\eta)\ell+1/2}d^{k/4}\int_{T}^{\infty}t^{k-\ell}dt\ll N^{(1-\eta)\ell+1/2}d^{k/4}T^{k-\ell+1}.

(3.23)

Hence we choose

T=N^{1-\eta},

(3.24)

so that the contributions in (
3.22
) and (
3.23
are of the same order. With this choice of
T
, we get

\psi^{*}(\chi)\ll N^{(1-\eta)(k+1)+1/2}d^{k/4}.

(3.25)

It remains to specify
\eta
. Let

\eta=1-\frac{1}{3(k+1)},

(3.26)

so that
(1-\eta)(k+1)=1/3
. Thus

\psi^{*}(\chi)\ll N^{5/6}d^{k/4}

(3.27)

and

\psi(\chi)\ll N^{5/6}d^{k/4}+N^{\eta+\epsilon}.

(3.28)

Summing over
N=X2^{-k}
, we find that the left side of (
3.3
) is bounded by

\ll X^{5/6+\epsilon}d^{k/4}+X^{1-\frac{1}{3(k+1)}+\epsilon}.

(3.29)

Thus, if
d<X^{1/3(k+1)}
, then the above estimate is

\ll X^{1-\frac{1}{3(k+1)}+\epsilon}\ll X^{1-\frac{1}{3(k+2)}}

(3.30)

for small enough
\epsilon
. This gives the estimate (
3.3
).
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\gamma
be an arithmetic function. For
(a,d)=1
we have

\Delta(\gamma;X,d,a)=\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\chi(\text{mod }d)}\overline{\chi}(a)\left(\sum_{n\leq X}\gamma(n)\chi(n)\right).

(3.31)

Proof.

By the orthogonality condition

\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\chi(\text{mod }d)}\overline{\chi}(a)\chi(n)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }n\equiv a(d),\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(3.32)

we may write

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(d)\end{subarray}}\gamma(n)=\sum_{n\leq X}\gamma(n)\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\chi(\text{mod }d)}\overline{\chi}(a)\chi(n)\right)=\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{\chi(\text{mod }d)}\overline{\chi}(a)\left(\sum_{n\leq X}\gamma(n)\chi(n)\right).

(3.33)

If
\chi
(mod
d
) is principal, then

\begin{cases}\overline{\chi}(a)=1,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\gamma(n)\chi(n)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
(n,d)=1\end{subarray}}\gamma(n).\end{cases}

(3.34)

Hence the contribution from the principal character gives the main term in (
3.33
) and the discrepancy
\Delta(\gamma;X,d,a)
is given by a sum over nonprincipal characters. This gives (
3.31
).
∎

Proof.

2

3

By Lemma

\leq\sum_{d\leq D}\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\chi(\text{mod }d)}\left|\sum_{n\leq X}\tau_{k}(n)\chi(n)\right|.

By the bound

\displaystyle\ll\log\mathcal{L}\sum_{d\leq D}\frac{1}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=qr\\

(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}\tau_{k}(n)\chi^{*}(n)\right|

Combining the above estimates, (

\ll X^{1-1/12(k+1)+\epsilon}\ll X^{1-1/12(k+2)}.

(4.19)

4.4

4.1

(4.185)

Denote

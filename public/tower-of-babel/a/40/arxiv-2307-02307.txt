The Refined Solution to the Capelli Eigenvalue Problem for 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) ⊕ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) \mathfrak{gl}(m|n)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(m|n) and 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{gl}(m|2n)
Mengyuan Cao, Monica Nevins, Hadi Salmasian

Abstract
. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be either the Lie superalgebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( V ) ⊕ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( V ) \mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(V) where V := ℂ m | n V:=\mathbb{C}^{m|n} or the Lie superalgebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( V ) \mathfrak{gl}(V) where V := ℂ m | 2 ​ n V:=\mathbb{C}^{m|2n} . Furthermore, let W W be the 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -module defined by W := V ⊗ V ∗ W:=V\otimes V^{*} in the former case and W := 𝒮 2 ​ ( V ) W:=\mathscr{S}^{2}(V) in the latter case. Associated to ( 𝔤 , W ) (\mathfrak{g},W) there exists a distinguished basis of Capelli operators { D λ } λ ∈ Ω \left\{D^{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Omega} , naturally indexed by a set of hook partitions Ω \Omega , for the subalgebra of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -invariants in the superalgebra 𝒫 ​ 𝒟 ​ ( W ) \mathscr{PD}(W) of superdifferential operators on W W . Let 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} be a Borel subalgebra of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . We compute eigenvalues of the D λ D^{\lambda} on the irreducible 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} -submodules of 𝒫 ⁡ ( W ) \mathscr{P}(W) and obtain them explicitly as the evaluation of the interpolation super Jack polynomials of Sergeev–Veselov at suitable affine functions of the 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} -highest weight. While the former case is straightforward, the latter is significantly more complex. This generalizes a result by Sahi, Salmasian and Serganova for these cases, where such formulas were given for a fixed choice of Borel subalgebra.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper the base field will be
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie superalgebra and let
W
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then the superalgebra
\mathscr{P}(W)
of superpolynomials on
W
has a canonical
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure, and
\mathscr{P}(W)\cong\mathscr{S}(W^{*})
as
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
Suppose that
\mathscr{P}(W)
has a multiplicity-free decomposition into irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, that is

(1)

\mathscr{P}(W)\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Omega}W_{\lambda},

where the
W_{\lambda}
’s are pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
and
\Omega
is an index set. In addition, assume that
the
W_{\lambda}
are modules of type M, that is,

\mathrm{End}_{\mathfrak{g}}(W_{\lambda})\cong\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathscr{D}(W)
denote the algebra of constant-coefficient superdifferential operators on
W
. Then there is a canonical isomorphism

\mathscr{D}(W)\cong\mathscr{S}(W)
. Hence, as explained in Appendix
A
, we have a
\mathfrak{g}
-module decomposition

\mathscr{D}(W)\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Omega}W_{\lambda}^{*}.

Let
\mathscr{PD}(W)
denote the algebra of superdifferential operators on
W
with superpolynomial coefficients. We have an isomorphism
of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

\mathscr{PD}(W)\cong\mathscr{P}(W)\otimes\mathscr{D}(W).

Taking
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant elements of both sides, it follows that we have vector-space isomorphisms

(2)

\mathscr{PD}(W)^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda,\mu\in\Omega}(W_{\lambda}\otimes W_{\mu}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Omega}\mathbb{C}id_{W_{\lambda}}.

Definition 1.1
.

The Capelli operator
D^{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Omega
is the
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant differential operator in
\mathscr{PD}(W)^{\mathfrak{g}}
that corresponds to
id_{W_{\lambda}}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(W_{\lambda},W_{\lambda})
via the isomorphism (
2
).

Note that
id_{W_{\lambda}}
corresponds to the tensor
\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\lambda}}w_{i}^{*}\otimes w_{i}
, where

d_{\lambda}:=\dim W_{\lambda}
and the sets of vectors

\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d_{\lambda}}

and
\{w_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{d_{\lambda}}

are bases of
W_{\lambda}^{*}
and
W_{\lambda}
that are dual to each other.
The above choices give a well-defined normalization of the operator
D^{\lambda}
. Indeed as we explain in Appendix
A
, if
W_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathscr{P}^{d}(W)
then

D^{\lambda}
acts on
W_{\lambda}
by the scalar
d!
.

Clearly the set
\{D^{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Omega\}
forms a basis for
\mathscr{PD}(W)^{\mathfrak{g}}.

By Schur’s Lemma, the operators
D^{\mu}
for
\mu\in\Omega
act on the irreducible components
W_{\lambda}
of
\mathscr{P}(W)
by scalars
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
. Thus we have the following natural problem.

The Capelli Eigenvalue Problem
.

Find the eigenvalue
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
for each
\lambda,\mu\in\Omega
.

The Capelli Eigenvalue Problem (henceforth CEP) goes back to the work of Kostant and Sahi
[
KS91
,
KS93
]
. It has been studied extensively and spawned the theory of interpolation Jack polynomials
[
Sah94
,
OO97
]
.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is the complexification of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
where
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
and
W
is the complexification of the space of hermitian
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}
, then it follows from the work of Knop and Sahi
[
KS96
]
that
the
eigenvalue
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
is given by a polynomial function of the form
P_{\mu}(\lambda+\rho)
, where
\rho
is the usual shift by half the sum of positive roots, and
P_{\mu}
is a symmetric polynomial whose top-degree homogeneous component yields a distinguished class of special functions that occur in spherical analysis on Riemannian symmetric spaces, known as Jack polynomials (at parameter values
\frac{1}{2},1,2
respectively). Interpolation Jack polynomials also occur as eigenfunctions of the Calogero-Moser-Sutherland operator, which describes the hamiltonian of the quantum
n
-body problem in one dimension
[
SV05
]
.

In this paper we are interested in a refinement of the CEP when
(\mathfrak{g},W)
is either the pair
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(V),V\otimes V^{*})
with
V:=\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
or the pair
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),\mathscr{S}^{2}(V))
with
V:=\mathbb{C}^{m|2n}
. The CEP for these pairs (and several others that are obtained by the TKK construction) was studied
in
[
SSS20
]
.
As explained in
loc. cit.
, these two cases are related to the diagonal symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(V),\mathfrak{gl}(V))
and the symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),\mathfrak{osp}(V))
, respectively.
From this viewpoint, the present paper serves as a starting point to extend the results of
[
SSS20
]
to arbitrary Borel subalgebras. The complex picture of families of Borel subalgebras that arises for the pair
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),\mathfrak{osp}(V))

suggests that a complete and uniform general solution to the refined CEF requires new ideas.

In
[
SSS20
]
the authors first parametrize the representations of
\mathfrak{g}
by hook partitions. Then they fix a Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
(coming from the standard Borel subalgebra or its opposite; see
[
SSS20
, Table 4]
), and write down a formula for the
\mathfrak{b}
-highest
weights of irreducible components of
\mathscr{P}(V)
in terms of the hook partition. Finally they show that a formula for the eigenvalue
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
can be computed as an interpolation super Jack polynomial evaluated at an affine function of
the
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight.

The fixed choice of the Borel subalgebra essentially means that the formula for
the eigenvalues of a Capelli operator is really dependent on the parametrization
of modules by partitions.
Our goal in this paper is to remove this constraint. That is we compute formulas for
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
which are polynomial in the
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weights of the
W_{\lambda}
, for
arbitrary
Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}
.
In other words, our goal in this paper is to study the following variation of the CEP for the aforementioned pairs
(\mathfrak{g},W)
.

The Refined Capelli Eigenvalue Problem
.

For any Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
, find the eigenvalue
c_{\mu}(\lambda)
as a polynomial function in the
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight of
W_{\lambda}
.

Since the Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
are not conjugate by the inner automorphism group, the solution to the CEP does
not
carry over to the refined form. Indeed, the work of Section
5
shows that the choice of affine function varies significantly with the type of Borel subalgebra.

Here is an outline of the paper. In Section
2
we recall the definitions and properties of the interpolation super Jack polynomials of Sergeev and Veselov
[
SV05
]
. In Section
3
we show that for the diagonal symmetric pair

(\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(m|n),\mathbb{C}^{m|n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{m|n})^{*})
the solution of the refined CEP is obtained in a relatively straightforward way from the results of
[
SSS20
]
; this result is stated in Theorem
3.2
.

We begin the study of the pair
(\mathfrak{gl}(m|2n),\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{m|2n}))
in Section
4
, where we establish formulae for
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weights, for any decreasing Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
, and derive necessary conditions for a solution to the refined CEP.
In Section
5
, we solve the refined CEP in a series of results of increasing complexity: for
very even
Borel subalgebras in Section
5.1
; for
generic
highest weights in Section
5.2
; for
relatively even
Borel subalgebras in Section
5.3
and finally in all cases in Section
5.4
. In Appendix
A
, we carefully prove the well-definedness of the CEP and establish the normalization of the Capelli operators.

Notation and Conventions
.

Since all Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
are conjugate by inner automorphisms, for the refined CEP it suffices to consider all Borel subalgebras that contain a fixed Cartan subalgebra.
Throughout this paper
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
denotes the standard (
i.e.
, diagonal) Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
; we denote the standard basis of
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}
, or equivalently of
\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
, indiscriminately by
\{e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq m+n\}
. We also denote the dual basis for
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}^{*}
by
\{\epsilon_{i},\delta_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\}
. Henceforth
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the nondegenerate supersymmetric form on
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}^{*}
that is diagonal with respect to this basis and satisfies
(\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{i})=-(\delta_{j},\delta_{j})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n
.
Let
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
denote the standard upper triangular Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}_{m|n}
its opposite. The set of Borel subalgebras has several orbits under the Weyl group. A representative of each orbit (called an
increasing
Borel subalgebra) may be obtained from
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
by performing a sequence of simple odd reflections; such a Borel subalgebra again contains
\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}
. Alternatively, applying simple odd reflections to
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}_{m|n}
produces another set of representatives, whose elements are the
decreasing
Borel subalgebras.

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
is an integer partition, then we denote by
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\ldots)
the transpose partition
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=|\{j\mid\lambda_{j}\geq i\}|
.
Let
\mathscr{H}(m|n)
denote the set of
m|n
hook partitions,
i.e.
, integer partitions
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
satisfying
\lambda_{m+1}\leq n
. Doubling the elements of such a partition yields an element of
\mathscr{H}_{2}(m|2n)
, which is the subset of
m|2n
hook partitions whose parts are all even.
Each
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
corresponds to a unique irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, denoted
V_{\lambda}
, whose highest weight with respect to the standard Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
is given by
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{j}\max\{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-m,0\}\delta_{j}
. Note that its negative is then the highest weight of
V_{\lambda}^{*}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

2.
Interpolation super Jack Polynomials

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and let
R_{m|n}:=\mathbb{C}\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1}\ldots,y_{n}\right]
be the ring of polynomials in
m+n
indeterminates with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
\theta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{\leq 0}
. We say a polynomial
f\in R_{m|n}
is
separately symmetric
if
f
is symmetric in
\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
and in
\{y_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
separately.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
the subalgebra of separately symmetric polynomials
f
such that

(3)

f\left(\ldots,x_{i}+1/2,\ldots,\ldots,y_{j}-1/2,\ldots\right)=f\left(\ldots,x_{i}-1/2,\ldots,\ldots,y_{j}+1/2,\ldots\right)

on every hyperplane
x_{i}+\theta y_{j}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n.
We call the symmetry property defined in Equation (
3
) the
monoidal symmetry
of
f
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{m+n}
. We say
u
is equivalent to
v
if
f(u)=f(v)
for all
f\in\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
, in which case we write
u\sim_{E}v
. We say that
u
and
v
are
monoidally equivalent
if they are related by a sequence of separate symmetries and monoidal symmetries, and write
u\sim v
in this case.

Evidently if
u\sim v
then
u\sim_{E}v
. As we discuss in more detail (and use) in Section
5.4
, these two notions of equivalence differ when there exist elements in
\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
whose orbits under
\sim
are infinite.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+n}
and suppose
1\leq i\leq m
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Then we have

u\sim\begin{cases}u-e_{i}+e_{m+j}&\text{whenever $x_{i}+\theta y_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta)$, and}\\
u+e_{i}-e_{m+j}&\text{whenever $x_{i}+\theta y_{j}=-\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta)$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
v=(x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{m}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,y_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{m+n}
, we have
v+\frac{1}{2}e_{i}-\frac{1}{2}e_{m+j}\sim v-\frac{1}{2}e_{i}+\frac{1}{2}e_{m+j}
whenever
x_{i}^{\prime}+\theta y_{j}^{\prime}=0
. If
u=v+\frac{1}{2}e_{i}-\frac{1}{2}e_{m+j}
then
x_{i}^{\prime}+\theta y_{j}^{\prime}=0
if and only if
x_{i}+\theta y_{j}=(x_{i}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2})+\theta(y_{j}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta)
, yielding the first equivalence. Alternately, if
u=v-\frac{1}{2}e_{i}+\frac{1}{2}e_{m+j}
, then
x_{i}^{\prime}+\theta y_{j}^{\prime}=0
if and only if
x_{i}+\theta y_{j}=(x_{i}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2})+\theta(y_{j}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2})=-\frac{1}{2}(1-\theta)
, and the second equivalence holds.
∎

We need one extra definition before we can define the interpolation super Jack polynomials. Given an
(m,n)
-hook partition
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m,n)
, the
twisted Frobenius coordinates

\left(p(\lambda),q(\lambda)\right)\coloneqq\left(p_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,p_{m}(\lambda),q_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,q_{n}(\lambda)\right)
of
\lambda
are defined by

(4)

\begin{split}p_{i}(\lambda)\coloneqq\lambda_{i}-\theta\left(i-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(n-\theta m\right)\mbox{, and}\\
q_{j}(\lambda)\coloneqq\langle\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-m\rangle-\theta^{-1}\left(j-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta^{-1}n+m\right),\end{split}

where
1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n
and
\langle x\rangle\coloneqq\max\{0,x\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 2.4
.

(
[
SV05
, Section 6]
)

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m,n)
and let
\theta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{\leq 0}
. The interpolation super Jack polynomial

P_{\lambda,\theta}
is the polynomial in

R_{m|n}
that is uniquely determined by the following properties:

(i)

{P}_{\lambda,\theta}\in\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
;

(ii)

deg
({P}_{\lambda,\theta})\leq|\lambda|
where the degree of
{P}_{\lambda,\theta}
means the total degree in both
x
and
y
;

(iii)

{P}_{\lambda,\theta}(p(\lambda),q(\lambda))=d!
where
d:=|\lambda|
;

(iv)

{P}_{\lambda,\theta}(p(\mu),q(\mu))=0
for all
(m,n)
-hook partitions
\mu
such that
|\mu|\leq|\lambda|
and
\mu\neq\lambda.

Remark 2.5
.

More precisely, the polynomials
SP^{*}_{\lambda}(x,y,\theta)
introduced in
[
SV05
]
differ from the
P_{\lambda,\theta}
by a normalization: for each
\lambda
, the value of
{SP}^{*}_{\lambda}(p(\lambda),q(\lambda),\theta)
is instead given by a hook-type product.

Remark 2.6
.

The set
\{{P}_{\lambda,\theta}\}_{\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m,n)}
forms a basis for
\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
.
Indeed one can prove that there exists a polynomial
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
in
m+n
variables with coefficients in the field of rational functions in a parameter
\bar{\theta}
such that the specialization of
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
at
\bar{\theta}:=\theta
yields
P_{\lambda,\theta}
. In particular, the coefficients of
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
do not have any poles in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Q}^{\leq 0}
. For these facts and further properties of the polynomials
\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}
see
[
SV05
]
.

3.
The refined CEP for
\left(\mathfrak{gl}(V)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(V),V\otimes V^{*}\right)

In this section we set
\theta:=1
,
V:=\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
,
\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)\oplus\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
,
W:=V\otimes V^{*}
, and
\mathfrak{h}\coloneqq\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m|n}
. Then
\mathfrak{b}_{0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}_{m|n}\oplus\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
.
The polynomial algebra
\mathscr{P}(W)
is a completely reducible and multiplicity-free
\mathfrak{g}
-module, with decomposition

\mathscr{P}(W)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)}V_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes V_{\lambda}.

Let us denote
the highest weight of
V_{\lambda}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
by
\underline{\lambda}_{0}
; then the
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}^{\mathrm{op}}
-highest weight of
V_{\lambda}^{*}
is
-\underline{\lambda}_{0}
.

We begin by restating the solution to the Capelli Eigenvalue Problem from
[
SSS20
]
in terms of the Weyl vector of
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
.
Write
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
for the image of
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{m|n}
under the standard isomorphism that sends
\epsilon_{i}
to
e_{i}
and
\delta_{j}
to
e_{m+j}
.
Let
\tau_{0}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
denote the affine map

\tau_{0}(x,y)=\tilde{y}+\tilde{\rho_{0}}

where we identify
(x,y)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with a pair of vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
and
\rho_{0}
denotes the Weyl vector corresponding to
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
. Observing that

\rho_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{m-n+1-2i}{2}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{m+n+1-2j}{2}\delta_{j},

we can rephrase the result of
[
SSS20
, Table 3]
pertaining to
\mathfrak{g}
as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
SSS20
, Theorem 1.13]

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
W
be as above.
For every
\lambda,\mu\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
, the eigenvalue of the Capelli operator
D^{\mu}
on the irreducible summand
V_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes V_{\lambda}
with
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
-highest weight
(-\underline{\lambda}_{0},\underline{\lambda}_{0})
is equal to

P_{\mu,1}\circ\tau_{0}\left(-\underline{\lambda}_{0},\underline{\lambda}_{0}\right),

where
P_{\mu,1}\in\Lambda_{m,n,1}
is the interpolation super Jack polynomial associated to
\mu
for parameter value
\theta=1
.

Now suppose
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{b}_{2}
is an arbitrary Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
. Then
\mathfrak{b}_{i}
is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
for
i=1,2
and we denote its Weyl vector
\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}}
. Write
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}
for the highest weight of
V_{\lambda}^{*}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
and
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}
for the highest weight of
V_{\lambda}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{2}
.
Then we may define affine maps
\tau_{\mathfrak{b},i}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{m|n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{m|n}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
via

\tau_{\mathfrak{b},1}(x,y)=-\tilde{x}-\tilde{\rho}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\mathfrak{b},2}(x,y)=\tilde{y}+\tilde{\rho}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}.

In this section we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{b}_{2}
be an arbitrary Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
and let
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
.
Then

P_{\mu,1}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{b},1}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}})=P_{\mu,1}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{b},2}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}})=P_{\mu,1}\circ\tau_{0}(-\underline{\lambda}_{0},\underline{\lambda}_{0}).

It follows that for any
\mathfrak{b}
, the eigenvalue of the Capelli operator
D^{\mu}
on each irreducible component
V_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes V_{\lambda}
of
\mathscr{P}(W)
is given by evaluating a polynomial on the highest weight of this module with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

Since
P_{\mu,1}\in\Lambda_{m,n,1}
, it suffices to show that for
i=1,2
and any
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
,

(5)

\tau_{\mathfrak{b},i}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}})\sim\tau_{0}(-\underline{\lambda}_{0},\underline{\lambda}_{0}).

We verify this in stages.
First assume that
\mathfrak{b}_{2}
is an
increasing
Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
, that is, it is obtained from the standard Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{m|n}
by a finite sequence of simple odd reflections.
Suppose
\mathfrak{b}_{2}=r_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})
where
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
is an increasing Borel subalgebra and
\alpha=\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j}
is a simple isotropic root with respect to
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
. By induction, we may suppose
that (
5
) holds for
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
, namely, that

\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}\sim\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{0}}+\widetilde{\rho_{0}}.

We need to show that

\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}}\sim\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}
.
Recall from
[
CW13
, Lemma 1.40]
that the highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}
is obtained from
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}
by the formula

(6)

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}=\begin{cases}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}-\alpha&\text{if $(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\alpha)\neq 0$};\\
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}&\text{if $(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\alpha)=0$},\end{cases}

while by
[
CW13
, Prop 1.33]
we have
(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\alpha)=0
and
\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}=\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}+\alpha
. Therefore if
(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\alpha)\neq 0
, we have

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}=(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}-\alpha)+(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}+\alpha)=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},

and we are done. Otherwise, we have
(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\alpha)=0
, so that

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}
and we instead have

\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}}=\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\alpha}=\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+e_{i}-e_{m+j}.

Since
(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}+\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\alpha)=0
, it follows that the vector
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}):=\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}
satisfies
x_{i}+y_{j}=0
.
Thus by Lemma
2.3
with
\theta=1
we have

\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}\sim\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}}+e_{i}-e_{m+j}=\widetilde{\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}}+\widetilde{\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}}},

as required. Thus by induction, (
5
) holds for
i=2
and all increasing Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}_{2}
.

Now suppose
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
is a decreasing Borel subalgebra, meaning that
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}
is increasing. Then by the symmetry of the definition of
\tau_{\mathfrak{b},i}
we deduce directly that

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathfrak{b},1}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{2}})

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}},1}\;(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}})

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}},2}\;(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}},\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}}})

\displaystyle\sim\tau_{0}(-\underline{\lambda}_{0},\underline{\lambda}_{0}),

which implies (
5
) holds with
i=1
whenever
\mathfrak{b}_{1}
is decreasing.

Finally, suppose that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{b}_{2}^{\prime}
is an arbitrary Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then for
i=1,2
there exist permutations
\sigma_{i}\in S_{m}
(permuting
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{m}
) and
\sigma_{i}^{\prime}\in S_{n}
(permuting
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}
) such that
(\sigma_{i}\times\sigma_{i}^{\prime})(\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{b}_{i}
is a decreasing (respectively, increasing) Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(m|n)
when
i=1
(respectively,
i=2
).
Then we have for
i=1,2
that
(\sigma_{i}\times\sigma_{i}^{\prime})(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{\prime}})=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}}
and similarly
(\sigma_{i}\times\sigma_{i}^{\prime})(\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{\prime}})=\rho_{\mathfrak{b}_{i}}
. By the separate symmetry of the elements of

\Lambda_{m,n,1}

these permutations leave polynomials in
\Lambda_{m,n,1}
invariant, so (
5
) follows immediately.
∎

4.
Borel subalgebras and highest weights for
\mathfrak{gl}(m|2n)

From now on, we set
\theta:=1/2
,
V:=\mathbb{C}^{m|2n}
,
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{gl}(m|2n)
and
W:=\mathscr{S}^{2}(V)
.
Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the standard Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}_{0}:=\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
be the
opposite
standard (that is, lower triangular) Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

4.1.
The solution to the CEP with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{0}

The
polynomial algebra
\mathscr{P}(W)
is a completely reducible and multiplicity-free
\mathfrak{g}
-module. A key result of
[
SSS20
]
is that we can parametrize this decomposition by
m|n
hook tableaux. Given
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
, let
2\lambda\in\mathscr{H}_{2}(m|2n)
be the
(m|2n)
hook tableau obtained by doubling each row, and let
W_{\lambda}:=(V_{2\lambda})^{*}
. Then we have the decomposition

(7)

\mathscr{P}(W)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)}W_{\lambda}.

In this case, given
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
with transpose
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\lambda_{2}^{\prime},\ldots)
, the highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{0}
of
W_{\lambda}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=\mathfrak{b}_{m|2n}^{\mathrm{op}}
is given by

(8)

\underline{\lambda}_{0}=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}2\lambda_{i}\epsilon_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}\left(\delta_{2j-1}+\delta_{2j}\right)

where
\mu_{j}:=\langle\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-m\rangle=\max\{0,\lambda^{\prime}_{j}-m\}
. Note that this corresponds precisely to the
m|2n
hook partitition of
2\lambda
, which is
(2\lambda_{1},\dots,2\lambda_{m}|\mu_{1},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n},\mu_{n})\in\mathscr{H}_{2}(m|2n)
.

Let
\Omega_{m|2n}=\{\underline{\lambda}_{0}\mid\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)\}
be the set of all
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
-highest weights of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules appearing in the decomposition (
7
). The Zariski closure of
\Omega_{m|2n}
in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is a subspace we denote
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
Let
\Gamma\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
be the linear map defined by

\Gamma(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}|b_{1},\ldots,b_{2n})=\left(\frac{a_{1}}{2},\cdots,\frac{a_{m}}{2}\mid\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}}{2},\cdots,\frac{b_{2n-1}+b_{2n}}{2}\right).

Observe that if
\rho_{0}
denotes the Weyl vector corresponding to
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
, then we have

(9)

\Gamma(\rho_{0})=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{m-2n+1-2i}{4}e_{i}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{m+2n+2-4k}{2}e_{m+k}.

Define, for any
x\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, the map
\tau_{0}^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\colon\mathfrak{a}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
by
\tau_{0}^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}(x)=-\Gamma(x+\rho_{0})
. Then it is easy to see that
we can rephrase the result of
[
SSS20
, Table 3]
pertaining to
\mathfrak{g}
as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
SSS20
, Theorem 1.13.]

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
W
be as above.
Then for each
\lambda,\mu\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
, the eigenvalue of the Capelli operator
D^{\mu}
on the irreducible summand
W_{\lambda}
with highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{0}\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}_{0}
is equal to

{P}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{0}^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}({\underline{\lambda}_{0}}).

4.2.
Explicit form of
\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})

The affine map
\tau^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}_{0}
is only characterized by the theorem on elements of
\mathfrak{a^{*}}
. To explicitly describe all extensions of
\tau^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}_{0}
to affine maps
\tau_{0}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
, let
M_{0}
denote the matrix of the linear transformation
-\Gamma\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
with respect to our standard bases. Then we have

(10)

M_{0}=\left[\begin{matrix}-\frac{1}{2}I_{m}&0_{m\times 2n}\\
0_{n\times m}&D\end{matrix}\right]

where
D=(d_{ij})
is a
n\times 2n
matrix whose entries satisfy

d_{ij}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }j=2i-1\mbox{ or }2i\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all matrices of the form

M=M_{0}+\left[\begin{matrix}0_{(m+n)\times m}&A\end{matrix}\right]

where

A
is any
(m+n)\times(2n)
matrix whose columns
A_{j}
satisfy the relations

A_{2k}=-A_{2k-1}

for all
1\leq k\leq n
. We also set

(11)

X_{0}=-\Gamma(\rho_{0})=M_{0}\rho_{0}=\tau_{0}^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}(0).

Lemma 4.3
.

Every extension of
\tau_{0}^{\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\colon\mathfrak{a}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
to an affine map on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is given on
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by
\tau_{0}(x)=Mx+X_{0}
for some
M\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that
\mathfrak{a}^{*}=\text{span}\{\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{m},\delta_{1}+\delta_{2},\cdots,\delta_{2n-1}+\delta_{2n}\}
; thus the set of all linear maps to
\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
vanishing on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}

is given by

\mathcal{K}=\left\{\left[\begin{matrix}0_{(n+m)\times m}&A\end{matrix}\right]\mid A_{2k-1}=-A_{2k}\;\text{ for all $1\leq k\leq n,$}\right\}

where
A_{j}
denotes the
j
th column of
A
.
∎

Note that since
\rho_{0}\notin\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, the equation
M\rho_{0}=X_{0}
with
M\in\mathcal{C}
holds
only
for
M=M_{0}
. In the following sections, we will see that the single matrix
M_{0}
is not suitable for use with other Borel subalgebras, but that other subsets of
\mathcal{C}
will be.

4.3.
Borel subalgebras and decreasing
\delta\epsilon
sequences

One may parametrize the set
\mathcal{B}
of Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
by sequences
(\xi_{i})_{i=1}^{m+2n}
where
\{\xi_{i}|1\leq i\leq m+2n\}=\{\epsilon_{i},\delta_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq 2n\}
— called
\delta\epsilon
sequences — by associating to
\mathfrak{b}
the sequence for which
\{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}\mid 1\leq i<m+2n\}
is its set of simple roots. In particular, the simple odd (isotropic) roots of
\mathfrak{b}
correspond to pairs
\epsilon_{i}\delta_{j}
or
\delta_{j}\epsilon_{i}
in the sequence, and if
\alpha
is such a root, then the
\delta\epsilon
sequence of
r_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{b})
is obtained from that of
\mathfrak{b}
by swapping
\epsilon_{i}
and
\delta_{j}
.

For example,
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
corresponds to the sequence
\delta_{2n}\delta_{2n-1}\cdots\delta_{1}\epsilon_{m}\epsilon_{m-1}\cdots\epsilon_{1}
and it has a single odd simple root
\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{m}
.

If the indices of the
\epsilon_{i}
and of the
\delta_{j}
are (separately) decreasing, then we say this is a decreasing
\delta\epsilon
-sequence. These correspond exactly to the decreasing Borel subalgebras defined earlier, since each one may be obtained from
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
be a sequence of simple odd reflections. Let
\mathcal{B}^{d}
denote the set of decreasing Borel subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\mathfrak{h}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
, and consider the
\delta\epsilon
sequence associated to
\mathfrak{b}
.

(1)

For each
1\leq i\leq m
, let
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
be the number of
\delta
s to the right of
\epsilon_{i}
.

(2)

For each
1\leq k\leq 2n
, let
j_{k,\mathfrak{b}}
be the number of
\epsilon
s to the left of
\delta_{k}
.

When
\mathfrak{b}
is clear from the context, we may write
\ell_{i}
and
j_{k}
for
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
and
j_{k,\mathfrak{b}}
respectively. It is easy to see that, for example,
j_{k}=\#\{i\mid\ell_{i}\geq k\}
. Thus a decreasing Borel subalgebra (or equivalently, the corresponding decreasing
\delta\epsilon
sequence) is completely determined by specifying the values
\ell_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\ell_{m}
(or equivalently, the values
j_{1}\geq\cdots\geq j_{2n}
).

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. With
\ell_{i}
as defined above, let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}
be the set of roots

(12)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{m},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{m},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{m}}-\epsilon_{m};

\displaystyle\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{m-1},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{m-1},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{m-1}}-\epsilon_{m-1};

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{1},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{1}}-\epsilon_{1}.

The importance of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is as follows. Suppose that we obtain
\mathfrak{b}
from
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
by a sequence of odd simple reflections, that is, such that
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
,
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}_{t}
for some
t>0
and for each
1\leq k\leq t
,

\mathfrak{b}_{k}=r_{\alpha_{k}}(\mathfrak{b}_{k-1})
where
\alpha_{k}
is an odd simple (isotropic) root of
\mathfrak{b}_{k-1}
.
Then if
t
is minimal we necessarily have the equality of sets
\{\alpha_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq t\}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, since the effect of each simple odd reflection
\pm(\epsilon_{i}-\delta_{j})
is to swap the adjacent terms
\epsilon_{i}
and
\delta_{j}
in the corresponding
\delta\epsilon
sequence. Moreover, the ordered list of roots in (
12
), (ordered from the top row to the bottom row) is one sequence satisfying these conditions.
Set

(13)

r_{\mathfrak{b}}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}}\alpha.

The following lemma is immediate.

Lemma 4.6
.

For each
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
we have

r_{\mathfrak{b}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}\epsilon_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{2n}j_{k,\mathfrak{b}}\delta_{k}.

4.4.
A formula for highest weights with respect to
\mathfrak{b}

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
.
For any
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
(respectively, for
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
) write
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
(respectively,
\underline{\lambda}_{0}
) for the corresponding highest weight of
W_{\lambda}
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}
is obtained from
\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
by the sequence of simple odd reflections in the roots (
12
). For any adjacent pair of Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}=r_{\delta_{j}-\epsilon_{i}}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime})
of this sequence, we have from (
6
) that

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime\prime}}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}-\delta_{j}+\epsilon_{i}

unless
(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}},\delta_{j}-\epsilon_{i})=0
. Since all coefficients of
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}
are nonpositive, this latter holds if and only if its
i
th and
(m+j)
th coefficients are both zero.

It follows that
the highest weight of
W_{\lambda}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
takes the form

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b}),\quad\text{where}\quad r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b}):=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}}\alpha

for some subset
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}
.

Definition 4.7
.

If
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, then
r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b})=r_{\mathfrak{b}}
and we say that
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
is
generic
for
\mathfrak{b}
. Otherwise,

\lambda
is called nongeneric for
\mathfrak{b}
.

Note that since
(\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}})_{i}
is increasing whereas
(2\lambda_{i})_{i}
is decreasing, these sequences cross at most once. The indices
i
for which
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}\leq 2\lambda_{i}
are key to describing
r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b})
in the nongeneric case.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
and
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
. If
\ell_{m,\mathfrak{b}}>2\lambda_{m}
, then let
I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}}
denote the least index
I\geq 1
for which
\ell_{I}>2\lambda_{I}
.
Further, set

\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b}):=\min\{\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}},2\lambda_{i}\}

for all
1\leq i\leq m
; then
\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})=\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
if and only if
i<I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
and
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
. Then
r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}}\alpha
where
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is the following set of roots:

(14)

\displaystyle\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{m},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{m},\cdots,\delta_{\ell({\lambda},m,\mathfrak{b})}-\epsilon_{m};

\displaystyle\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{m-1},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{m-1},\cdots,\delta_{\ell({\lambda},m-1,\mathfrak{b})}-\epsilon_{m-1};

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{1},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\delta_{\ell({\lambda},1,\mathfrak{b})}-\epsilon_{1},

where we interpret any row corresponding to a value of
\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})=0
as an empty row. In particular
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b})
is the
\mathfrak{b}
-highest weight of
W_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

We have already addressed the case that

\lambda
is generic for
\mathfrak{b}
, so suppose

\lambda
is nongeneric for
\mathfrak{b}
and let
I:=I_{{\lambda},\mathfrak{b}}
.
It suffices to show that
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b})
. Note that in
\underline{\lambda}_{0}=(2\lambda_{1},\cdots,2\lambda_{m}|\mu_{1},\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{n},\mu_{n})
, we have
\mu_{k}=0
for all
k>\lambda_{m}
.

We proceed inductively from
m
down to
1
, following the rows of the list of roots (
12
), showing that if
\mathfrak{b}_{j+1}
denotes the Borel subalgebra obtained after completing all reflections up to the end of those in the row corresponding to
\epsilon_{j+1}
, that

(15)

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j+1}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-\sum_{i=j+1}^{m}(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})}-\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})\epsilon_{i}).

The base case corresponds to
j=m
,
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}^{\mathrm{op}}
, where there is nothing to show.

Suppose we have shown our inductive hypothesis up to
\epsilon_{j+1}
, as in (
15
). Let us first determine the coefficients of
\epsilon_{j}
and of
\delta_{k}
with
k>2\lambda_{j}
.

The coefficient of
\epsilon_{j}
in
-\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j+1}}
is the same as that of
-\underline{\lambda}_{0}
; this value is therefore
2\lambda_{j}
. Let
k>2\lambda_{j}\geq 2\lambda_{m}
. Then the coefficient of
\delta_{k}
in
\underline{\lambda}_{0}
was zero. Moreover, since we have

2\lambda_{j}\geq 2\lambda_{i}\geq\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})
for each
j+1\leq i\leq m
, none of the additional terms in (
15
) contribute to the coefficient of
\delta_{k}
. Thus the coefficient of
\delta_{k}
in
-\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j+1}}
is
0
for
k>2\lambda_{j}
.

As we iterate through each of the roots

\delta_{1}-\epsilon_{j},\delta_{2}-\epsilon_{j},\cdots,\delta_{\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})}-\epsilon_{j}

we fall into the first case of (
6
) because the coefficient of
\epsilon_{j}
is nonzero at each iteration. Therefore if
\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}
denotes the resulting Borel subalgebra, we have

(16)

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j+1}}-(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})}-\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})\epsilon_{j}).

If
\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})=\ell_{j,\mathfrak{b}}
(that is, if
j\leq I
) then we are done.
Otherwise,
\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})=2\lambda_{j}
and we infer that the coefficient of
\epsilon_{j}
in
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}
is
0
, as is the coefficient of
\delta_{k}
for any
k>\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})=2\lambda_{j}
.
Thus for any remaining roots

\delta_{\ell(\lambda,j,\mathfrak{b})+1}-\epsilon_{j},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{j,\mathfrak{b}}}-\epsilon_{j}

we fall into the second case of (
6
), and so
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}}
. Therefore (
15
) and (
16
) together imply

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{j}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-\sum_{i=j}^{m}(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})}-\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})\epsilon_{i}),

and we may conclude by induction that

(17)

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})}-\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})\epsilon_{i})=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b}),

as required.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Note in particular that (
17
) implies that the coefficient of
\epsilon_{i}
in
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is given by

-(2\lambda_{i}-\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b}))=\begin{cases}-(2\lambda_{i}-\ell_{i})<0&\text{if $\ell_{i}<2\lambda_{i}$ ;}\\
0&\text{if $\ell_{i}\geq 2\lambda_{i}$.}\end{cases}

The coefficient of
\epsilon_{i}
is always
\leq 0
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
and
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
. The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\lambda
is
\mathfrak{b}
-generic.

(ii)

2\lambda_{m}\geq\ell_{m,\mathfrak{b}}
.

(iii)

2\lambda_{i}\geq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.

Proof.

Equivalence of (ii) and (iii) is an immediate consequence of the fact that the
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
are increasing whereas the
\lambda_{i}
are decreasing. The equivalence of (i) and (ii)
follows from Proposition
4.9
and the fact that
if
2\lambda_{i}\geq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
for all
i
, then
r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})=r_{\mathfrak{b}}
so
\lambda
is generic for
\mathfrak{b}
.
∎

For ease of reference, we list the roots contributing to
r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r({\lambda},\mathfrak{b})
, that is, the subset
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}
of the set of roots in (
12
)
which we will
not
subtract from
\underline{\lambda}_{0}
when computing the highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Using the index
I=I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}}
for simplicity, it follows from (
14
) that they are

(18)

\displaystyle\delta_{2\lambda_{m}+1}-\epsilon_{m},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{m,\mathfrak{b}}}-\epsilon_{m},

\displaystyle\delta_{2\lambda_{m-1}+1}-\epsilon_{m-1},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{m-1,\mathfrak{b}}}-\epsilon_{m-1},

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\delta_{2\lambda_{I}+1}-\epsilon_{I},\cdots,\delta_{\ell_{{I},\mathfrak{b}}}-\epsilon_{I},

4.5.
Some necessary conditions

Our goal is to define, for each
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
, an affine map
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
such that for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
we have the equivalence

(19)

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})\sim\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}).

As in Section
3
, this would imply that

P_{\lambda,1/2}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
is the interpolation polynomial we seek.
Such a map
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
will be given on
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=M_{\mathfrak{b}}x+X_{\mathfrak{b}},

for some
(m+n)\times(m+2n)
matrix
M_{\mathfrak{b}}
and vector
X_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
. In this section derive some necessary conditions that
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
must satisfy in order for the relation (
19
) to hold.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. If (
19
) holds, then we must have

X_{\mathfrak{b}}\sim X_{0}.

Proof.

Since
\underline{\lambda}_{0}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=0
when
\lambda
corresponds to the trivial module, the requirement that

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})\sim\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})
for all
\lambda
implies in particular that

X_{\mathfrak{b}}\sim X_{0}
.
∎

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. If (
19
) holds, then for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
that are
\mathfrak{b}
-generic we must have the equality

(20)

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}.

In consequence, we must have

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}=X_{\mathfrak{b}}-X_{0}.

Proof.

By (
11
), the right hand side of (
19
) is equal to
M_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+\rho_{0})
. On the other hand, the definition of genericity implies
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r_{\mathfrak{b}}
so the left hand side is

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+(-M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}).

We first show that the equivalence of the two sides can only be achieved by equality.

Namely, recall that

\lambda
is generic for a fixed
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
only if
2\lambda_{i}\geq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
for all
i
. Therefore we may freely choose generic
\lambda
sufficiently large such that the vector

M_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+\rho_{0})
does not lie on any hyperplane of the form
x_{i}+\frac{1}{2}y_{j}=\pm 1/4
, where a symmetry of the form
\sim
holds (with
\theta=1/2
) and therefore no symmetry of the form (
3
) can apply.
Similarly, the fact that
X_{0}\neq 0
, and that equivalence must hold for the
\mathfrak{b}
-generic weights
s\underline{\lambda}_{0}
for every
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, excludes the possibility that the equivalence is due to separate symmetry of the variables. Therefore, for all sufficiently large
\mathfrak{b}
-generic
\lambda
, the equivalence (
19
) must occur as an equality, that is, we have

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+(-M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}})=M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}.

Since

\text{span}\{\underline{\lambda}_{0}\in\Omega_{m|2n}\mid\lambda\text{ is generic for $\mathfrak{b}$ and }\underline{\lambda}_{0}\gg 0\}=\mathfrak{a}^{*},

we deduce that this equality is in fact an equality of affine functions on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. Thus we have
X_{0}=-M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}
and, by Lemma
4.3
, we conclude
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

5.
The refined CEP for
\left(\mathfrak{gl}(V),\mathscr{S}^{2}\left(V\right)\right)

We develop our solution to the refined CEP in stages of increasing complexity of the Borel subalgebra.

5.1.
The very even case

We begin with the set of Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}
that have the property that
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
: the ones for which for all
k
, the terms
\delta_{2k}\delta_{2k-1}
are adjacent in the
\delta\epsilon
sequence.

Definition 5.1
.

A decreasing Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
and its associated
\delta\epsilon
sequence are called
very even
if
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
is even for all
1\leq i\leq m
; equivalently, if
j_{2k-1}=j_{2k}
for all
1\leq k\leq n
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}
is very even. For any
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
, the value
\ell(\lambda,i,\mathfrak{b})=\min\{\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}},2\lambda_{i}\}
will also be even.
Since

\mathfrak{a}^{*}=\text{span}\{\delta_{2k-1}+\delta_{2k},2\epsilon_{i}\mid 1\leq k\leq n,1\leq i\leq m\}
, it is immediate that the roots comprising
r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})
can be partitioned into pairs whose sums lie in this span, and so by Proposition
4.9
we have
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.
Henceforth we write
\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}
for the Weyl vector of
\mathfrak{b}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}
is very even and
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}\colon\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
is an affine map satisfying (
19
), in the form
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=Mx+X_{\mathfrak{b}}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Then
X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{0}\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}

where
M_{0}
is as in (
10
), and in fact,
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
is independent of the choice of
M\in\mathcal{C}
.

Note that since
\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}\notin\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, it is not true that
M\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}=X_{\mathfrak{b}}
for all
M\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}
is very even and suppose
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=Mx+X
is an affine function satisfying (
19
). By Proposition
4.13
, we must have
M\in\mathcal{C}
and
X=Mr_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{0}
. If
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
, then
M-M^{\prime}
is zero on
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. Thus
M\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=M^{\prime}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
for every
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
and
M^{\prime}r_{\mathfrak{b}}=Mr_{\mathfrak{b}}
, so if one choice of
M\in\mathcal{C}
gives a function satisfying (
19
), so do all. Further, since by
[
CW13
, Prop 1.33]
we have
\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}=\rho_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}}
, we readily compute
M_{0}\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{0}(\rho_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}})=X_{0}+M_{0}r_{\mathfrak{b}}=X_{\mathfrak{b}}
.
∎

The rest of this subsection is devoted to proving the converse, that is, that
for all very even
\mathfrak{b}
,
the affine functions

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=Mx+M_{0}\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}
for
M\in\mathcal{C}

satisfy (
19
). We first prove our key technical result, which gives a collection of terms that are equivalent to
\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})
. By Lemma
5.2
, it suffices to prove the result for
M=M_{0}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}
is very even and that
\underline{\lambda}_{0}\in\Omega_{m|2n}
. Then we have

M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}\sim M_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}))+X_{0}.

For ease of reference in the proof, for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq n
, let

(21)

E_{i}\coloneqq\frac{m+1-2n-2i}{4}\mbox{ and }F_{k}\coloneqq\frac{m+2+2n-4k}{2}.

Then we have
X_{0}=M_{0}\rho_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}e_{i}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}F_{k}e_{m+k}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}
is very even and let
I
be the least index for which
\ell_{I,\mathfrak{b}}>2\lambda_{I}
; then we must have
\ell_{I,\mathfrak{b}}\geq 2\lambda_{I}+2
. Considering moreover the list of roots occuring in (
18
), whose sum is
r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})
, we note that

\lambda_{m}+1\leq\lambda_{m-1}+1\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{I}+1\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{I,\mathfrak{b}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{I+1,\mathfrak{b}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{m,\mathfrak{b}}.

That is, the intervals

(\lambda_{i}+1,\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}/2)
are nested, as
i
decreases.

To prove the proposition, we proceed by induction on
i
from
m
down to
I
on the rows of (
18
). Let

r_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda,j}=\left(\sum_{k=2\lambda_{j}+1}^{\ell_{j,\mathfrak{b}}}\delta_{k}\right)-(\ell_{j,\mathfrak{b}}-2\lambda_{j})\epsilon_{j}

denote the sum of the roots on the row of (
18
) corresponding to
\epsilon_{j}
. Set

\Lambda_{i+1}=M_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}r_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda,j})+X_{0},

which is the partial sum obtained after completing the row indexed by
i+1
.

We will show that we can transform
\Lambda_{m+1}:=M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}
, by a sequence of monoidal symmetries as in Lemma
2.3
,
into the final result

\Lambda_{I}=M_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}))+X_{0}.

Our inductive hypothesis is that

\Lambda_{i+1}\sim\Lambda_{i},

that
the coefficient of
e_{i}
in
\Lambda_{i+1}
is
\lambda_{i}+E_{i}
and that
the coefficients of
e_{m+j}
in
\Lambda_{i+1}
with
\lambda_{i}+1\leq j\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
are given by
F_{j}-(m-i)
.

The base case is that
i=m
, so equivalence is a tautology and the statement about the coefficients is true.
Suppose it holds for
i+1
.
Let us prove the statement for
i
. Note that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i+1}+M_{0}r_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda,i}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i+1}+M_{0}\left(\left(\sum_{t=2\lambda_{i}+1}^{\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}}\delta_{t}\right)-(\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}-2\lambda_{i})\epsilon_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i+1}+M_{0}\left(\sum_{k=\lambda_{i}+1}^{\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}/2}(\delta_{2k-1}+\delta_{2k}-2\epsilon_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{i+1}+\sum_{k=\lambda_{i}+1}^{\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}/2}(e_{i}-e_{m+k}).

Suppose we have shown for some
K
,
\lambda_{i}+1\leq K<\ell_{i}/2
, that
\Lambda_{i+1}
is monoidally equivalent to

\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}=\Lambda_{i+1}+\sum_{k=\lambda_{i}+1}^{K-1}(e_{i}-e_{m+k}).

We now show that
\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}\sim\Lambda_{i+1,K}
. Then by induction as
K
runs from
\lambda_{i}+1
to
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}/2
, we will conclude that
\Lambda_{i}\sim\Lambda_{i+1}.

Using the outer induction hypothesis, as well as the formula for
\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}
, we compute that the coefficient of
e_{i}
in
\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}
is
\lambda_{i}+E_{i}+(K-\lambda_{i}-1)
and the coefficient of
e_{m+K}
in
\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}
is
F_{K}-(m-i)
.
Since in this case we have
\theta=1/2
, Lemma
2.3
says
y\sim y+e_{i}-e_{m+k}
if
y_{i}+\frac{1}{2}y_{m+k}=-\frac{1}{4}
.
Using (
21
), we compute

\lambda_{i}+E_{i}+\left(K-\lambda_{i}-1\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(F_{K}-(m-i))\right)=-\frac{1}{4}

Therefore monoidal symmetry may be applied and we conclude that

\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}\sim\Lambda_{i+1,K-1}+e_{i}-e_{m+K}=\Lambda_{i+1,K},

as required. Therefore we have deduced by induction that

\Lambda_{i+1}\sim\Lambda_{i+1,\ell_{i}/2}=\Lambda_{i}.

Finally, if
i-1\geq I
, we compute the coefficients of
\Lambda_{i}
, to complete the outer induction.

The coefficient of
e_{i-1}
in
\Lambda_{i}
is the same as the coefficient of
e_{i-1}
in
M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}
, which is
\lambda_{i-1}+E_{i-1}
, because we have added no roots involving
\epsilon_{i-1}
.

Since
i-1\geq I
and
\ell_{i-1,\mathfrak{b}}
is even, we have
\lambda_{i}+1\leq\lambda_{i-1}+1\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i-1,\mathfrak{b}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
. Therefore the coefficient of
e_{m+j}
, for all
\lambda_{i-1}+1\leq j\leq\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i-1,\mathfrak{b}}
was
F_{j}-(m-i)
in
\Lambda_{i+1}
and is thus

F_{j}-(m-i)-1
in
\Lambda_{i}
, since we subtracted each
e_{m+j}
exactly once in the course of computing
\Lambda_{i}
. The induction is complete.
∎

We may now prove our main theorem for very even Borel subalgebras.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be a very even Borel subalgebra in
\mathcal{B}^{d}
. Set
X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{0}\rho_{\mathfrak{b}}
and let
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
be the affine function defined on
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=M_{\mathfrak{b}}x+X_{\mathfrak{b}}.

Then for every
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
we have
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})\sim\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})
so that

P_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}\right)=P_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{0}\left(\underline{\lambda}_{0}\right).

Consequently, the eigenvalue of the Capelli operator
D^{\mu}
on the irreducible component
W_{\lambda}
is given by the evaluation of a polynomial in the highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})\sim\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})
for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
. We have that

\displaystyle M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle=M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}))+X_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle=M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}))+(-M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}).

Since
\underline{\lambda}_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we may replace
M_{\mathfrak{b}}
by
M_{0}
in the first term, and as noted in Lemma
5.2
the latter term is equal to
X_{0}
. Therefore we have

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0}+r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}))+X_{0},

and thus by applying Proposition
5.3
we conclude that
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}\sim M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}
, which completes the proof.
∎

5.2.
A solution for generic weights

From now on we suppose that
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
is not very even. We first characterize the non-adjacent pairs
\{\delta_{2k},\delta_{2k-1}\}
in the
\delta\epsilon
sequence corresponding to
\mathfrak{b}
.

Definition 5.5
.

For each
1\leq k\leq n
, let

\displaystyle T_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle=\{1\leq k\leq n\mid j_{2k,\mathfrak{b}}<j_{2k-1,\mathfrak{b}}\}

\displaystyle=\{1\leq k\leq n\mid\exists 1\leq i\leq m:\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}=2k-1\}.

One can infer the equality of the given sets from the combinatorial description of
\delta\epsilon
sequences in Definition
4.4
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Define
\mathfrak{b}_{e}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
by the values

\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=2\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}\right\rfloor

for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Then
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
is a very even Borel subalgebra and the sequence of odd reflections
r_{\alpha}
that take
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
to
\mathfrak{b}
corresponds to the set of roots
\{\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{i}\mid k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}},\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}=2k-1\}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{b}
is a decreasing Borel subalgebra if and only if the sequence
(\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}})_{i=1}^{m}
is increasing (and bounded by
2n
), it follows that the increasing sequence
(\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}})_{i=1}^{m}
uniquely defines a decreasing Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
, and that it is very even.
In the
\delta\epsilon
sequence of
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
, the terms
\delta_{2k-1}\delta_{2k}
are adjacent. For each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
i
such that
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}=2k-1
, we have
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=2k-2
, but for all other
i
,
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
must be even and therefore
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}=\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
. Thus to obtain the
\delta\epsilon
sequence of
\mathfrak{b}
one must, for each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
, shift
\delta_{2k-1}
to the right using the reflections corresponding to
\{\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{i}\mid\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}=2k-1\}
.
∎

Consequently, if we define for each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}

(22)

r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}:=(j_{2k-1}-j_{2k})\delta_{2k-1}-\sum_{i=m-j_{2k-1}+1}^{m-j_{2k}}\epsilon_{i},

then we have

(23)

r_{\mathfrak{b}}=r_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+\sum_{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. Set

\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{M\in\mathcal{C}\mid\forall k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}},\;Mr_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=0\}.

Lemma 5.8
.

The set
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is nonempty.

Proof.

For any
M=M_{0}+A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
where
A^{\prime}=[0_{(m+n)\times m}|A]
for some
A
with columns satisfying
A_{2k}=-A_{2k-1}
for all
k
, one has

Mr_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=M_{0}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}+A(j_{2k-1}-j_{2k})\delta_{2k-1};

thus to satisfy the condition
Mr_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=0
, one must set

A_{2k-1}:=\frac{-1}{j_{2k-1}-j_{2k}}M_{0}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}

and
A_{2k}:=-A_{2k-1}.

These conditions are distinct as
k
runs over
T_{\mathfrak{b}}
. We conclude that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
is nonempty.
∎

The set
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
contains a unique element if and only if
T_{\mathfrak{b}}=\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Set
X_{\mathfrak{b}}=X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
and suppose
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
is
\mathfrak{b}
-generic. Then for all
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
we have

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda
is
\mathfrak{b}
-generic and suppose
M\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Then since

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+\sum_{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k},

we have, by the conditions defining
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
and the choice of
X_{\mathfrak{b}}
, that

M\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}.

Moreover, if
\lambda
is
\mathfrak{b}
-generic, then it is also
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
-generic since
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Therefore by Theorem
5.4
and Proposition
4.13
, we infer that

M\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0},

as required.
∎

5.3.
A solution for the relatively even case

Suppose now that
\lambda
is not
\mathfrak{b}
-generic, but that for each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
, the set
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}
of roots defining
r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})
include either all, or none, of the roots defining
r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}
. We will call such
\lambda

compatible
with
\mathfrak{b}
, but to define compatibility precisely, we must first make some observations about
\delta\epsilon
sequences.

Suppose that in the
\delta\epsilon
sequence for
\mathfrak{b}
, there exists at least one
\epsilon
between
\delta_{2k}
and
\delta_{2k-1}
.
Since
j_{2k-1}
is the number of
\epsilon
to the left of
\delta_{2k-1}
, and these are in decreasing order, we deduce that these terms are precisely
\epsilon_{m},\cdots,\epsilon_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}
. By the same reasoning,
\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}}
is the first
\epsilon
to the right of
\delta_{2k}
. Therefore, the
\delta\epsilon
sequence contains

(24)

\cdots\delta_{2k}\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}}\cdots\epsilon_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}\delta_{2k-1}\cdots.

Now suppose that
\lambda
is not
\mathfrak{b}
-generic. Since the sequence of
\lambda_{i}
is decreasing, we have that

(25)

\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq\lambda_{m-j_{2k}-1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}.

On the other hand, the value of
\ell_{i}
, for each
m-j_{2k}\leq i\leq m-j_{2k-1}+1
, is exactly
2k-1
. Therefore, depending on the relation among
2k-1,2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}
and
2\lambda_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}
, we should subtract all, none or only some of the roots from the set

\{\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}},\ldots,\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}\}

to obtain
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
from
\underline{\lambda}_{0}
.

These observations motivate the following definition.

Definition 5.10
.

We say that
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)

is
compatible
with
\mathfrak{b}
if it is either
\mathfrak{b}
-generic or, for each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
, we have either
2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}>2k-1
or
2\lambda_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}<2k-1
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
and set
X_{\mathfrak{b}}=X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
. Then for any
M\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, the affine function

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=M_{\mathfrak{b}}x+X_{\mathfrak{b}}

satisfies
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})\sim\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})
for every
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
that is compatible with
\mathfrak{b}
.

For ease of reference in the proof, for each
\lambda
that is compatible with
\mathfrak{b}
, define

(26)

T_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda}\coloneqq\{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}\mid 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k,\mathfrak{b}}}>2k-1\}.

Proof of Proposition
5.11
.

Suppose
\lambda
is compatible with
\mathfrak{b}
. If it is in fact
\mathfrak{b}
-generic then Proposition
5.9
implies that
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})=\tau_{0}(\underline{\lambda}_{0})
, so we are done.

Otherwise, recall that the set of roots
\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{b}}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{b}}
determining
r_{\mathfrak{b}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})
is given by (
18
),
where
I=I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}}
denotes the least index for which
\ell_{I,\mathfrak{b}}>2\lambda_{I}
.
Recall also that for each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=(j_{2k-1}-j_{2k})\delta_{2k-1}-\sum_{i=m-j_{2k-1}+1}^{m-j_{2k}}\epsilon_{i}
, and that for each index
i
in this sum,
\ell_{i}=2k-1
.
The condition of compatibility implies that either

\ell_{i}=2k-1<2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}-1}\leq\cdots\leq 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}

in which case,
I>i
and none of these roots appear in the expression (
18
), or

2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}-1}\leq\cdots\leq 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}<2k-1=\ell_{i}

in which case
I\leq i
and every root vector that occurs in
r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}
appears in (
18
).

Therefore, the set
T_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda}
is exactly the set of
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
such that none of the roots determining
r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}
occur among the roots in (
18
); this set may be empty.

Therefore by construction we have

r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})=r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e})+\sum_{k\in T_{b,\lambda}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k},

and so we deduce

(27)

\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b})=\underline{\lambda}_{0}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e})-\sum_{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}-\sum_{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}.

Let
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
; since
T_{\mathfrak{b},\lambda}\subset T_{\mathfrak{b}}
, this implies

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})-\sum_{k\in T_{b,\lambda}}M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}).

Thus since
X_{\mathfrak{b}}=X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
, we have that
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}})=\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})
. Finally, as
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
is very even and
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}
, we can apply Theorem
5.4
to deduce that

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\sim M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0},

and the proposition follows.
∎

We conclude this section by defining the class of Borel subalgebras
\mathfrak{b}
for which all
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
are compatible with
\mathfrak{b}
.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. Then
\mathfrak{b}
, or its corresponding
\delta\epsilon
sequence, is called
relatively even
if for every
1\leq k\leq n
we have
j_{2k-1}-j_{2k}\leq 1
, that is,
if there is at most one
\epsilon
between

\delta_{{2k}}
and
\delta_{{2k-1}}
.

Corollary 5.13
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
is relatively even. Then for any
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, the affine function
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}(x)=M_{\mathfrak{b}}x+X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}

satisfies

P_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}\left(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}\right)=P_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{0}\left(\underline{\lambda}_{0}\right)

for every
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
.
Consequently, the eigenvalue of the Capelli operator
D^{\mu}
on the irreducible component
W_{\lambda}
is given by the evaluation of a polynomial in the highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
. If
\lambda
is not compatible with
\mathfrak{b}
, then
there exists an index
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
for which

2\lambda_{m-{j_{2k}}}<2k-1<2\lambda_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}.

This is impossible if
j_{2k}=j_{2k-1}
(as the
\lambda_{i}
are decreasing) and if
j_{2k-1}-j_{2k}=1
(as then
m-j_{2k}=m-j_{2k-1}+1
). Therefore, if
\mathfrak{b}
is relatively even it is compatible with every
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
.
That the affine function
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
gives a solution to the refined CEP thus follows from Proposition
5.11
.
∎

5.4.
A different approach for
\mathfrak{b}
that are not relatively even

Unfortunately, following the approach of the preceding section to establish a formula for
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
when
\mathfrak{b}
is not relatively even leads to a piecewise affine function, as in
[
Cao22
]
. In this section, we follow a different approach.

Recall that the functions in
\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
are constant on the orbits
\mathcal{O}
of elements
\mathbb{C}^{m+n}
under monoidal symmetry. Since they are polynomial functions, they are constant on the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{O}
. As
\mathcal{O}
is a discrete set, if it is infinite, then its Zariski closure must contain (infinitely) many orbits, and in particular, the polynomials in
\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
will not separate orbits. That is, we may have
P(x)=P(y)
for
x,y
in distinct orbits, for all
P\in\Lambda_{m,n,\theta}
.

The question of when infinite orbits exist was answered in
[
SV17
]
, and in particular this occurs for
\theta=1/2
. The following is a consequence of their results.

Lemma 5.14
.

Suppose
x\in\mathbb{C}^{m|n}
and that
1\leq i<i_{0}\leq m
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Let
\mathrm{orb}(x)
denote the orbit of
x
under monoidal symmetry. If
x_{i}-x_{i_{0}}=2x_{i}+x_{m+j}=-\frac{1}{2}
then

(28)

\overline{\mathrm{orb}(x)}\supset\{u\in\mathbb{C}^{m|n}\mid u_{i}-u_{i_{0}}=2u_{i}+u_{m+j}=\pm\frac{1}{2},u_{s}=x_{s}\forall s\notin\{i,i_{0},m+j\}\},

where
\overline{\mathrm{orb}(x)}
denotes the Zariski closure of the orbit of
x
.

Proof.

In
[
SV17
, §5 (22)]
, the authors provide a key example for with
\theta=1/2
. Namely, for any
a\in\mathbb{C}
, it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(a)=\{

\displaystyle(\ell+a-\frac{1}{2},\ell+a,-2(\ell+a)+\frac{1}{2}),(\ell+a+\frac{1}{2},\ell+a,-2(\ell+a)-\frac{1}{2}),

\displaystyle(\ell+a,\ell+a-\frac{1}{2},-2(\ell+a)+\frac{1}{2}),(\ell+a,\ell+a+\frac{1}{2},-2(\ell+a)-\frac{1}{2})\mid\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is an orbit in
\mathbb{C}^{2|1}
under monoidal symmetry. The Zariski closure
\overline{\mathcal{O}(a)}
is thus equal to
\cup_{a\in\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}(a)
, which is the union of the two affine lines:

u_{1}-u_{2}=2u_{1}+u_{3}=\pm\frac{1}{2},\quad u_{2}-u_{1}=2u_{2}+u_{3}=\pm\frac{1}{2}.

Applying this to the triple
(i,i_{0}|j)
of indices in place of
(1,2|3)
gives the result.
∎

We now proceed to the definition of our affine function, which we will denote
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}
to distinguish it from the function
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}
defined on relatively even Borel subalgebras in the previous section.

Definition 5.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. Define

\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}=\{M\in\mathcal{C}\mid\forall k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}},M\delta_{2k-1}=\frac{1}{2}e_{m-j_{2k,\mathfrak{b}}}-e_{m+k}\}.

Given any
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{full}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
, we set
X_{\mathfrak{b}}=M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}+X_{0}
and

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}(x)=M_{\mathfrak{b}}x+X_{\mathfrak{b}}
for
x\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

Note that the defining condition of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}

basically describes the columns
A_{2k-1}
of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}
for
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
, hence
\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}
is nonempty.

Remark 5.16
.

Indeed the value of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}}
is independent of the choice of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{full}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
. This can be seen as follows.
By (
23
) we have
r_{\mathfrak{b}}=r_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+\sum_{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}
. Since
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}
, the value of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
is independent of the choice of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Also, by the definition of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}
the value of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}
is independent of the choice of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{full}}_{\mathfrak{b}}
.

Theorem 5.17
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathscr{H}(m|n)
we have

P_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}\left(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}\right)=P_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}\circ\tau_{0}\left(\underline{\lambda}_{0}\right).

Consequently, the eigenvalue of the Capelli operator
D^{\mu}
on the irreducible component
W_{\lambda}
is given by the evaluation of a polynomial in the highest weight
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
.

To prove this, we require several lemmas.
As in Lemma
5.6
, let
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
be the closest very even Borel subalgebra to
\mathfrak{b}
; we will relate
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}
to the function
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
that was defined in Theorem
5.4
. For ease of notation, set
\ell_{i}=\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}}
and
j_{t}=j_{t,\mathfrak{b}}
in what follows.

For each
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
, the
\delta\epsilon
sequence for
\mathfrak{b}
contains

\cdots\delta_{2k}\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}}\cdots\epsilon_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}\delta_{2k-1}\cdots

whereas that for
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
contains

(29)

\cdots\delta_{2k}\delta_{2k-1}\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}}\cdots\epsilon_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}\cdots.

Thus
\ell_{i}=2k-1
and
\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=2k-2
for
m-j_{2k-1}+1\leq i\leq m-j_{2k}
; similarly,
j_{2k-1,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=j_{2k,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
whereas by hypothesis
j_{2k-1}-j_{2k}\geq 1
.

We have

r_{\mathfrak{b}}=r_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+\sum_{k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}

as in (
23
), where

r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}=(\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}})+(\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}-1})+\cdots+(\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{m-j_{2k-1}+1}).

To obtain
\mathfrak{b}
from
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
, we must apply the reflections in the roots
\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{i}
in decreasing lexicographic order, that is, for
k
decreasing in
T_{\mathfrak{b}}
and then
i
decreasing from
m-j_{2k}
until
m-j_{2k-1}+1
.

Definition 5.18
.

Write
(k,i)\succeq(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
if we have
k,k^{\prime}\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
m-j_{2k-1}+1\leq i\leq m-j_{2k}
,
m-j_{2k^{\prime}-1}+1\leq i^{\prime}\leq m-j_{2k^{\prime}}
and
k\geq k^{\prime}
,
i\geq i^{\prime}
. Write
(k,i)\succ(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
if
(k,i)\succeq(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
and
(k,i)\neq(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
.

Note that we retain the index
k
in the notation only for convenience. For two such pairs, the statement
(k,i)\succ(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
is equivalent to stating
i>i^{\prime}
, as in each pair the first term is fully determined by the second.
In particular, we may write
(k,i)\succ(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})
for any
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq m
, not only those corresponding to
k^{\prime}\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
m-j_{2k^{\prime}+1}\leq i^{\prime}\leq m-j_{2k^{\prime}}
, without ambiguity.
We require two technical lemmas.

Lemma 5.19
.

Fix
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}
and let
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
. Suppose the coefficient of
\epsilon_{m-j_{2k}}
in
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
is zero. Then we have

(30)

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})+M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{k^{\prime}>k}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k^{\prime}}\sim\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})+M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{k^{\prime}>k}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k^{\prime}}+(e_{m-j_{2k}}-e_{m+k})

where
\sim
denotes monoidal symmetry.

Proof.

Recall that
\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(x)=M_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}x+X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=M_{0}x+M_{0}r_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+X_{0}
.
As in the proof of Proposition
5.3
, by a direct calculation using (
18
)
we have

M_{0}(r_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}-r(\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}))=\sum_{i=I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}}^{m}\sum_{j=\lambda_{i}+1}^{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}}(e_{i}-e_{m+j}).

Hence

(31)

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})=M_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}+X_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}+X_{0}+\sum_{i=I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}}^{m}\sum_{j=\lambda_{i}+1}^{\frac{1}{2}\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}}(e_{i}-e_{m+j}).

The hypothesis implies
2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq\ell_{m-j_{2k},\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=2k-2
.
Thus
for
i>m-j_{2k}
we have

2\lambda_{i}\leq 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq\ell_{m-j_{2k},\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}-2.

This implies that
I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq m-j_{2k}+1
.

Recall the formula for the sums
r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k}
defined in (
22
).
First note that the coefficients of
e_{m-j_{2k}}
and
e_{m+k}
in

M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{k^{\prime}>k}r_{odd,\mathfrak{b},k^{\prime}}
are zero. Namely, by the definition of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}
we have that
M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\epsilon_{i})=-\frac{1}{2}e_{i}
and
M_{\mathfrak{b}}(\delta_{2k^{\prime}-1})=\frac{1}{2}e_{m-j_{2k^{\prime}}}-e_{m+k^{\prime}}
. The only pairs
(k^{\prime},i)
occuring in this sum have
k^{\prime}>k
and
i>m-j_{2k}
.

Next we compute the coefficients of

e_{m-j_{2k}}
and
e_{m+k}
in

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})=\sum_{i}x_{i}e_{i}
.

Let us begin with
x_{m-j_{2k}}
. The coefficient of
e_{m-j_{2k}}
in
M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}
is
\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}
and in
X_{0}
is
E_{m-j_{2k}}
.
If it happens that
I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq m-j_{2k}
, then the index
i=m-j_{2k}
occurs in the sum (
31
) whence the coefficient of
e_{m-j_{2k}}
in this sum is
\frac{1}{2}\ell_{m-j_{2k},\mathfrak{b}_{e}}-\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}
. Since

\frac{1}{2}\ell_{m-j_{2k},\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=k-1
, we deduce that
x_{m-j_{2k}}=E_{m-j_{2k}}+k-1
. If indeed
I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=m-j_{2k}+1
, then we must have

2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}=2k-2
and the index
i=m-j_{2k}
does not occur in (
31
), hence again we have

x_{m-j_{2k}}=\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}+E_{m-j_{2k}}=E_{m-j_{2k}}+k-1.

Next we compute
x_{m+k}
. Since
2\lambda_{m}\leq 2\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq\ell_{m-j_{2k},\mathfrak{b}_{e}}=2k-2
, we deduce from the structure of the hook partition that
\mu_{2k-1}=\mu_{2k}=0
, so the coefficient of
e_{m+k}
in
M_{0}\underline{\lambda}_{0}
is zero. The coefficient of
e_{m+k}
in
X_{0}
is
F_{k}
. The coefficient of
e_{m+k}
in the sum (
31
) is the number of indices
i
such that

\lambda_{i}<k\leq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}/2
. The inequality

k\leq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
implies
\epsilon_{i}
is to the left of
\delta_{2k}
in
\mathfrak{b}_{e}
in (
29
) and in particular
i>m-j_{2k}
; but then
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq k-1
holds. Thus the coefficient is
-(m-(m-j_{2k}))=-j_{2k}
, whence
x_{m+k}=F_{k}-j_{2k}
.

Now note that
E_{m-i}+\frac{1}{2}F_{j}=\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}i-j
, so that

x_{m-j_{2k}}+\frac{1}{2}x_{m+k}=\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}j_{2k}-k+k-1-j_{2k}=-\frac{1}{4},

which implies that monoidal symmetry may be applied, yielding the result.
∎

Lemma 5.20
.

Fix
M_{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathfrak{b}}^{\mathrm{full}}
. Let
k\in T_{\mathfrak{b}}
and suppose
m-j_{2k-1}+1\leq i<m-j_{2k}
. Suppose the coefficient of
\epsilon_{i}
in
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
is zero. Then we have

(32)

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})

\displaystyle+M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})\succ(k,i)}(\delta_{2k^{\prime}-1}-\epsilon_{i^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\sim_{E}\quad\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})+M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{(k^{\prime},i^{\prime})\succ(k,i)}(\delta_{2k^{\prime}-1}-\epsilon_{i^{\prime}})+\left(\frac{1}{2}e_{m-j_{2k}}+\frac{1}{2}e_{i}-e_{m+k}\right)

although they are not related by monoidal symmetry.

Proof.

We set
i_{0}=m-j_{2k}
and show that with
x=\sum_{t}x_{t}e_{t}
equal to the left hand side above, we may apply Lemma
5.14
to the triple
(i,i_{0},k)
. Since
\frac{1}{2}e_{m-j_{2k}}+\frac{1}{2}e_{i}-e_{m+k}
has coefficients
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},-1)
, it then follows that the left side of the relation lies in
\overline{\mathrm{orb}(x)}
as well, as required.

As noted in the previous proof, the coefficients of
\{e_{i},e_{i_{0}},e_{m+k}\}
in
x
coincide with those of

(33)

\tau_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}(\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}})+M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{i<i^{\prime}\leq m-j_{2k}}(\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{i^{\prime}})

as the terms with
k^{\prime}>k
cannot contribute.

First we compute the coefficients of

\{e_{i},e_{i_{0}},e_{m+k}\}
in
the right hand side of (
31
).
Since the coefficient of
\epsilon_{i}
in
\underline{\lambda}_{\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
is zero, we have
\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}/2=k-1
.
It follows that for
i^{\prime}>m-j_{2k}
we have

\lambda_{i^{\prime}}\leq\lambda_{m-j_{2k}}\leq k-1<\ell_{i^{\prime},\mathfrak{b}_{e}}/2

and
in particular,
I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq m-j_{2k}+1
.

Now consider any
s\leq m
. The coefficient of
e_{s}
in
M_{0}\lambda_{0}+X_{0}
is
\lambda_{s}+E_{s}
. If

s\geq I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
, then the coefficient of
e_{s}
in the sum on the right hand side of (
31
) is
\frac{1}{2}\ell_{s,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}-\lambda_{s}=k-1-\lambda_{s}
. Hence the coefficient of
e-s
on the right hand side of (
31
) is

\lambda_{s}+E_{s}+(k-1-\lambda_{s})=E_{s}+k-1
.
If
i\leq s<I_{\lambda,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}
, then
e_{s}
does not occur in
the sum on the right hand side of (
31
) but we have

k-1=\ell_{i,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq\ell_{s,\mathfrak{b}_{e}}\leq\lambda_{s}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq k-1,

from which it follows that
\lambda_{s}=k-1
. This means that in this case also the coefficient of
e_{s}
on the right hand side of (
31
)
is equal to
\lambda_{s}+E_{s}=E_{s}+k-1
. As a consequence, we obtain that the coefficient of
e_{s}
for
s=i,i_{0}

in (
31
) is
E_{s}+k-1
.

On the other hand, from
\lambda_{m}\leq k-1
it follows that
\mu_{2k-1}=\mu_{2k}=0
so the coefficient of
e_{m+k}
in
M_{0}\lambda_{0}+X_{0}
is
F_{k}
. As in the proof of Lemma
5.19
, the coefficient of
e_{m+k}
in the sum on the right hand side of (
31
) is
-j_{2k}
.

We now compute the contributions of the second term in (
33
). First note that

\sum_{i<i^{\prime}\leq m-j_{2k}}(\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{i^{\prime}})=(m-j_{2k}-i)\delta_{2k-1}-\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{m-j_{2k}}\epsilon_{i^{\prime}}.

Therefore from the definition of
M_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle M_{\mathfrak{b}}\sum_{i<i^{\prime}\leq m-j_{2k}}(\delta_{2k-1}-\epsilon_{i^{\prime}})

Appendix A
A normalization for Capelli operators

Let
W
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Then there exists a natural pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:W^{\otimes d}\times(W^{*})^{\otimes d}\to\mathbb{C}\ ,\ \langle v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d},v_{1}^{*}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d}^{*}\rangle:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\langle v_{d-i+1},v_{i}^{*}\rangle,

where
\langle v,v^{*}\rangle:=v^{*}(v)
.
This gives an isomorphism

(38)

W^{\otimes d}\xrightarrow{\cong}\left((W^{*})^{\otimes d}\right)^{*}.

Now recall the canonical isomorphisms

(39)

\mathscr{S}^{d}(W)\cong(W^{\otimes d})^{S_{d}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{P}^{d}(W)\cong\mathscr{S}^{d}(W^{*})\cong\left((W^{*})^{\otimes d}\right)^{S_{d}}.

From the maps (
38
) and (
39
) we obtain a linear map

(40)

\boldsymbol{\varphi}:\mathscr{S}^{d}(W)\to\mathscr{P}^{d}(W)^{*}

as the composition of the maps

(41)

\mathscr{S}^{d}(W)\xrightarrow{\boldsymbol{i}}W^{\otimes d}\xrightarrow{\cong}\left((W^{*})^{\otimes d}\right)^{*}\xrightarrow{\boldsymbol{p}}\mathscr{P}^{d}(W)^{*},

where
\boldsymbol{i}
is induced by the inclusion
(W^{\otimes d})^{S_{d}}\subseteq W^{\otimes d}
and
\boldsymbol{p}
is induced by the projection that is dual to the inclusion
((W^{*})^{\otimes d})^{S_{d}}\to(W^{*})^{\otimes d}
.
It is straightforward to verify that the map

\boldsymbol{\varphi}

is indeed an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{gl}(W)
-modules. In particular, given
v\in W_{\bar{0}}
and
v^{*}\in(W^{*})_{\bar{0}}
, for the
d
-tensors
v^{\otimes d}\in\mathscr{S}^{d}(W)
and
(v^{*})^{\otimes d}\in\mathscr{P}^{d}(W)
we have

\langle v^{\otimes d},(v^{*})^{\otimes d}\rangle=\langle v,v^{*}\rangle^{d}.

We can also embed
\mathscr{S}(W)
in
\mathrm{End}(\mathscr{P}(W))
as follows. First for any homogeneous
v\in W\cong\mathscr{S}^{1}(W)
we define a superderivation
\partial_{v}
on
\mathscr{P}(W)
by setting

\partial_{v}(v^{*}):=\langle v,v^{*}\rangle\quad\text{ for }v^{*}\in W^{*}\cong\mathscr{P}^{1}(W),

and then extending
\partial_{v}
to all of
\mathscr{P}(W)
by the Leibniz rule

\partial_{v}(ab):=\partial_{v}(a)b+(-1)^{|v|\cdot|a|}a\partial_{v}(b).

The resulting derivations
\partial_{v}
supercommute, and therefore we can extend the embedding
\partial:W\to\mathrm{End}(\mathscr{P}(W))
to all of
\mathscr{S}(W)
by the universal property of
\mathscr{S}(W)
,
i.e.
, by setting

\partial_{v_{1}\cdots v_{d}}:=\partial_{v_{1}}\cdots\partial_{v_{d}}\text{ for }v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\in W.

This map identifies
\mathscr{S}(W)
with the algebra
\mathscr{D}(W)
of constant-coefficient superdifferential operators on
\mathscr{P}(W)
.

Clearly
\partial_{u}(\mathscr{P}^{d}(W))\subseteq\mathscr{P}^{d-d^{\prime}}(W)
for
u\in\mathscr{S}^{d^{\prime}}(W)
. In particular, when
d^{\prime}=d
we have
\mathscr{P}^{0}(W)\cong\mathbb{C}
, hence we obtain a linear map

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}:\mathscr{S}^{d}(W)\mapsto\mathscr{P}^{d}(W)^{*}\quad,\quad u\mapsto\big[p\mapsto\partial_{u}p\big].

Lemma A.1
.

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}=d!\boldsymbol{\varphi}
.

Proof.

The proof of Lemma
A.1
is an exercise in super linear algebra, and we omit the details.
Here we only outline one proof strategy. First one proves that both
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}
are
\mathfrak{gl}(W)
-module isomorphisms and
\mathscr{S}^{d}(W)
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}(W)
-module. Thus, it suffices to compare the values of
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
and
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}
on one nonzero vector. For instance, if
W_{\bar{0}}\neq 0
then for nonzero
v\in W_{\bar{0}}
and
v^{*}\in W^{*}_{\bar{0}}
we have

\partial_{v}((v^{*})^{\otimes d})=d\langle v,v^{*}\rangle(v^{*})^{\otimes(d-1)},

hence by induction we obtain

\partial_{v^{\otimes d}}((v^{*})^{\otimes d})=d!\langle v,v^{*}\rangle^{d}
,
which completes the proof.
∎

-module decomposition

The Cauchy problem and continuation of periodic solutions for a generalized Camassa-Holm equation
Nilay Duruk Mutlubas, Igor Leite Freire

Abstract
Abstract: We consider a three-parameter family of non-linear equations with ( p + 1 ) − (p+1)- order non-linearities. Such family includes as a particular member the well-known b − b- equation, which encloses the famous Camassa-Holm equation. For certain choices of the parameters we establish a global existence result and show a scenario that prevent the wave breaking of solutions. Also, we explore unique continuation properties for some values of the parameters. 2020 AMS Mathematics Classification numbers : 35A01, 35B60. Keywords: Camassa-Holm type equations, Global existence of solution, Continuation of solutions.

Introduction

Our work is concerned with qualitative properties of periodic solutions of the equation

u_{t}-u_{txx}+(b+c)u^{p}u_{x}=bu^{p-1}u_{x}u_{xx}+cu^{p}u_{xxx}.

(1.1)

Henceforth we assume that the parameters
b
,
c
and
p
are non-negative, positive real, and positive integer numbers, respectively.

In the case
p=c=1
we have the
b-
equation
[
9
]

u_{t}-u_{txx}+(b+1)uu_{x}=bu_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx},

(1.2)

whose relevance in hydrodynamics is discussed in
[
10
,
11
]
, see also
[
17
,
18
]
for a wide investigation about properties of these equations.

It is worth mentioning that the Degasperis-Procesi (DP)
[
8
]
and the Camassa-Holm (CH)
[
2
]
equations can be recovered from the
b-
equation when
b=2
and
b=3
, respectively.

Whenever
b=(p+1)
and
c=1
we have the equation

u_{t}-u_{txx}+(p+2)u^{p}u_{x}=(p+1)u^{p-1}u_{x}u_{xx}+u^{p}u_{xxx},

which was obtained in
[
6
]
using symmetries and conservation laws arguments, as well as in

[
1
]
where it can be inferred from a four-parameter family of equation as a member having the
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
norm of the solutions (vanishing as
x\rightarrow\pm\infty
) conserved, peakons, and multi-peakon solutions. It can also be obtained from the family of equations studied in
[
15
]
. The two most famous members of this family is the already mentioned CH equation, for
p=1
, and the Novikov equation
[
21
]
when
p=2
. For further details, see the review
[
14
]
where such equation is discussed in detail.

Our main motivation for this work is some open problems pointed out in
[
16
]
. We extend results on global existence of solutions established in this reference for the periodic problem associated to (
1.1
), for certain parameters. Moreover, we study the prevention of blow up of periodic solutions of (
1.1
), and establish unique continuation results.

Our paper is structured as follows: in Section
2
, we present the notation and conventions used in the manuscript, as well as our main motivation, a revision of literature and our main contributions. Next, in Section
3
, we prove some technical results very useful for establishing our achievements.

Global existence of solutions are proved in Section
4
, whereas the prevention of their blow-up is described in Section
5
. Such results, however, are for
b=0
in (
1.2
). We observe that the value
b=0
in (
1.2
) brings some difficulty to the equations, as can be inferred from
[
1
]
, where very few conserved quantities can be found whenever
b=0
in (
1.1
), at least of low order, see also
[
17
,
24
]
. Moreover, the results proved in
[
4
,
16
,
24
]
dealing with (global) existence of solutions usually assume that
b>0

2.1
and
2.2
), where we use a remarkable functional (whose square root is equivalent to the
H^{3}(\mathbb{S})-
norm of the solutions of (
1.2
) with
b=0
). From this functional, we establish conditions for the existence of global solutions for the equation.

Finally, unique continuation properties of (
1.1
) are presented in Section
6
. Such results are dependent on the values of the parameters, but we do not need to fix a specific order on the non-linearities of the equation.

Last but not least, our results are discussed in Section
7

8

Notation, conventions and main results

In this section, we fix the notations and conventions used throughout the manuscript, as well as our main contributions.

2.1
Notation and conventions

We identify the interval
[0,1)
with the circle
\mathbb{S}
. The Sobolev space of order
s
over the circle
\mathbb{S}
and its corresponding norm are denoted by
H^{s}(\mathbb{S})
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{S})}
, respectively. The functions
u
considered in this work are usually dependent on the variables
t
and
x
, that are regarded as time and space variables, respectively. Derivatives of
u
with respect to its first argument will usually be denoted either by
u_{t}
or
\partial_{t}u
, while derivatives with respect to second argument will be denoted either by
u_{x}
or
\partial_{x}u
, respectively. For each
t\in[0,T)
, the quantity
m(t,x):=(1-\partial^{2}_{x})u(t,x)
is sometimes referred as
momentum
. In particular, at
t=0
we have
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
and
m_{0}=u_{0}-u_{0}^{\prime\prime}
. Also, we assume that
u
is at least
C^{1}
with respect to its first argument.

By
\Lambda^{2}:H^{s}(\mathbb{S})\rightarrow H^{s-2}(\mathbb{S})
we denote the
Helmholtz operator

1-\partial_{x}^{2}
, whose inverse
\Lambda^{-2}
, acting on a function
f
, is given by
\Lambda^{-2}f=g\ast f
, where
\ast
denotes the convolution between functions, and

g(x)=\frac{\cosh(x-\lfloor x\rfloor-1/2)}{2\sinh(1/2)}.

(2.1)

Note that
g(\cdot)
is differentiable
a.e.
and, in particular, as soon as its derivative is defined, we have

\partial_{x}g(x)=\frac{\sinh(x-\lfloor x\rfloor-1/2)}{2\sinh(1/2)}.

(2.2)

Moreover, we have
\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(f)=(\partial_{x}g)\ast f
.

3
Preliminaries

In this section we present some technical and necessary results needed for the proof of our main theorems. It will be very useful to rewrite (
1.1
) in terms of its momentum
m=u-u_{xx}
, that is,

m_{t}+cu^{p}m_{x}+bu^{p-1}u_{x}m=0.

(3.1)

Note that in view of the relation
m(t,x)=\Lambda^{2}u(t,x)
, the solution of (
1.1
) subject to
u(0,x)=u_{0}
is equivalent to (
3.1
) subject to
m(0,x)=m_{0}(x)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{S})
,
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
, and
u\in C^{0}([0,T),H^{3}(\mathbb{S}))\cap C^{1}([0,T),H^{2}(\mathbb{S}))
, be the corresponding solution to (
1.1
) subject to
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
. If
m_{0}(x)\geq 0
or
m_{0}(x)\leq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then the corresponding momentum
m(t,x)
satisfies
m(t,x)\geq 0
or
m(t,x)\leq 0
, respectively.

Proof.

Our demonstration follows the same steps as
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
and we follow it closely, see also
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
. Let us fix
x
and define
\gamma(\cdot,x)
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\gamma_{t}(t,x)&=&cu(t,\gamma)^{p},\quad t\in(0,T),\\
\\
\gamma(0,x)&=&x.\end{array}\right.

Fixing
t
, letting
x
varies, and differentiating the PVI above with respect to
x
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\gamma_{tx}(t,x)&=&pc(u^{p-1}u_{x}\gamma_{x})(t,\gamma),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
\\
\gamma_{x}(0,x)&=&1.\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

From (
3.2
) we obtain

\gamma_{x}(t,x)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}pc(u^{p-1}u_{x})(\tau,\gamma(\tau,x))d\tau}>0.

(3.3)

Proceeding similarly as
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
we conclude that
\gamma(t,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an increasing diffeomorphism for every
t\in[0,T)
. Moreover, since
\gamma_{t}=cu^{p}
and
\gamma_{tx}=pc(u^{p-1}u_{x}\gamma_{x})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}\Big(m(t,\gamma)\gamma_{x}(t,y)^{\frac{b}{pc}}\Big)}&=&\displaystyle{\Big[\Big(m_{t}+m_{x}\gamma_{t}\Big)\gamma_{x}^{\frac{b}{pc}}+\frac{b}{pc}m\gamma_{x}^{\frac{b}{pc}-1}\Big](t,\gamma)}\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle{\Big[\Big(m_{t}+\gamma_{t}m_{x}+bu^{p-1}u_{x}m\Big)\gamma_{x}^{\frac{b}{pc}}\Big](t,\gamma)}\\
\\
&=&\displaystyle{\Big[\Big(m_{t}+cu^{p}m_{x}+bu^{p-1}u_{x}m\Big)\gamma_{x}^{\frac{b}{pc}}\Big](t,\gamma)=0}.\end{array}

The differential equation above implies that
m(t,\gamma(t,x))\gamma_{x}^{\frac{b}{pc}}=m_{0}(x)
. Therefore, if
m_{0}(x)\geq 0
or
m_{0}(x)\leq 0
, then
m(t,y(t,x))\geq 0
or
m(t,y(t,x))\leq 0
, respectively, since (
3.3
) holds.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
0\not\equiv u_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{S})
be a non-trivial function and
u\in C^{0}([0,T),H^{3}(\mathbb{S}))\cap C^{1}([0,T),H^{2}(\mathbb{S}))
, be the corresponding solution of (
1.1
) subject to
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
. If
m_{0}(x)\geq 0
or
m_{0}(x)\leq 0
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then
u(t,x)\geq 0
or
u(t,x)\leq 0
, respectively. Moreover, if
m_{0}(x)
does not change sign, then
\text{sign}\,{(u)}=\text{sign}\,{(u_{0})}=\text{sign}\,{(m_{0})}=\text{sign}\,{(m)}
.

Proof.

Under the conditions above, Theorem
3.1
implies that
x\mapsto m(t,x)
is non-negative or non-positive, for all
t\in[0,T)
. Since

u(t,x)=g\ast m(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}}g(x-y)m(t,y)dy,

and
g
, given by (
2.1
), is strictly positive, then the same will apply to
u
, respectively.
∎

Equation (
1.1
) has several conserved quantities depending on the values of the parameters
b
,
c
and
p
, see
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
. Among them, two are very important for our purposes, namely,

{\cal H}_{1}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}}u(t,x)dx,

(3.4)

as long as
p=1
or
b=pc
, and

{\cal H}_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}}(u^{2}+u_{x}^{2})dx,

(3.5)

in case

.

If

is a solution of (

), then from

we can obtain the relation

\partial_{t}(u-u_{xx})=-\partial_{x}\Big(\frac{c}{p+1}u^{p+1}+\frac{pc-b}{2}u_{x}^{2}-cu^{p}u_{xx}\Big),

provided that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{S}}mdx=\int_{\mathbb{S}}m_{t}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{S}}\partial_{x}\Big(\frac{c}{p+1}u^{p+1}+\frac{pc-b}{2}u_{x}^{2}-cu^{p}u_{xx}\Big)dx=0,

which implies the conservation of the momentum, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{S}}m(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{S}}m_{0}(x)dx.

(3.6)

The main relevance of theorem

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{S})
,
p=1
or
b=pc
, and assume that
u
is either non-negative or non-positive. Then the

Assume that

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let

or

. If

4
Global solution and the proof of theorems
2.1
and
2.2

Through this section we assume that
p=1
and
b=0
in (
1.1
). This fact is assumed henceforth without further mention.

We begin observing that

\int_{\mathbb{S}}u_{xx}dx=0.

This fact is enough to guarantee the existence, for each
t\in(0,T)
, the existence of a point
\xi_{t}-1\in(0,1)
such that
u_{x}(t,\xi_{t}-1)=0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
u_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{S})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{S})
, is such that
m_{0}\geq 0
(or
m_{0}\leq 0
), then there exists a constant
K>0
such that the solution of (
2.3
) satisfies
||u_{x}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})}\leq K
.

Proof.

Assume
m_{0}
does not change sign and
m_{0}\geq 0
. Then, by (
3.6
)

\displaystyle C_{1}=||m_{0}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}}m_{0}(r)dr=\int_{\mathbb{S}}m(t,r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\xi_{t}-1}^{\xi_{t}}m(t,r)dr\geq\int_{{\xi_{t}}-1}^{x}m(t,r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\xi_{t}-1}^{x}(u-u_{xx})(t,r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\xi_{t}-1}^{x}u(t,r)dr-u_{x}(t,x)

holds for every
x\in[\xi_{t}-1,\xi_{t}]
. Hence,

\displaystyle-u_{x}(t,x)\leq C_{1}-\int_{\xi_{t}-1}^{x}u(t,r)dr\leq C_{1}+\int_{\xi_{t}-1}^{x}u(t,r)dr\leq C_{2}

for some positive constant
C_{2}
. Here, we use (
3.4
) and Theorem
3.1
, which guarantee that
u
does not change sign, under the assumption that
m_{0}
does not change sign. Taking into account the final result, we observe that
u_{x}
is bounded from below.

Moreover, the existence of a point
\nu_{t}\in(0,1)
such that
u_{x}(t,\nu_{t})=0
, implies for every
x\in[\nu_{t}-1,\nu_{t}]
that

\displaystyle u_{x}(t,x)+\int_{x}^{\nu_{t}}u(t,r)dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\nu_{t}}(u-u_{xx})(t,r)dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\nu_{t}-1}^{\nu_{t}}(u-u_{xx})(t,r)dr=C_{1}

and

\displaystyle u_{x}(t,x)\leq C_{1}-\int_{x}^{\nu_{t}}u(t,r)dr=C_{1}+\int_{x}^{\nu_{t}}u(t,r)dr\leq C_{2}.

Hence,
u_{x}
is bounded also from above. Therefore, we can conclude
that
||u_{x}||_{\infty}
norm is bounded provided that
m
does not change sign , i.e.
||u_{x}||_{\infty}\leq K
. The case

Blow-up

The following lemma, provided that Lemma
4.1
holds, will imply in Theorem
2.2
that the solution

Lemma 5.1
.

If
T<\infty
, then
\sup_{t\in[0,T)}||u(t,\cdot)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})}<\infty
, where
u
is the solution of (
2.3
) in
C^{0}([0,T),H^{3}(\mathbb{S}))\cap C^{1}([0,T),H^{2}(\mathbb{S}))
.

Proof.

let
y\in\mathbb{S}
and
\tau\in[0,T)
be arbitrary. Consider the
characteristic ordinary differential equation with initial condition
for a curve
\gamma:[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
corresponding to the
solution
u
, that is,

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(r)=cu(r,\gamma(r)),

\displaystyle\gamma(\tau)=y.

Here
u\in C^{0}([0,T),H^{3}(\mathbb{S}))\subseteq C^{0}([0,T),C^{1}(\mathbb{S}))
by Sobolev
embedding theorem. Therefore,
(t,x)\mapsto u(t,x)
is continuous on

[0,T)\times\mathbb{S}
and globally Lipschitz with respect to
x
. By
compactness of
\mathbb{S}
, we get a unique global solution
\gamma\in C^{1}([0,T),\mathbb{S})
to the characteristic initial value problem given
above. That follows,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}(u(r,\gamma(r)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{t}(r,\gamma(r))+u_{x}(r,\gamma(r))\dot{\gamma}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{t}(r,\gamma(r))+cu_{x}(r,\gamma(r))u(r,\gamma(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3c}{2}\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2}),

and

\displaystyle u(\tau,y)=u(\tau,\gamma(\tau))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(0,\gamma(0))-\frac{3c}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2}))(r,\gamma(r))dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}(\gamma(0))-\frac{3c}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2}))(r,\gamma(r))dr.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|u(\tau,y)|\leq||u_{0}||_{\infty}+\frac{3c}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}|(\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2}))(r,\gamma(r))|dr.

(5.1)

The following estimation can be done for the integrand by periodicity and Lemma
4.1
:

\displaystyle|(\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2}))(r,\gamma(r))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2})||_{\infty}\leq||\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}(u_{x}^{2})||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{S})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||u_{x}^{2}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S})}\leq||u_{x}^{2}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})}\leq K^{2}.

Using this in (
5.1
) and observing that
y
and
\tau
are
arbitrary, the upper bound for
|u(\tau,y)|
is independent of these
variables, we can conclude that if
T<\infty
, then

\sup_{t\in[0,T)}||u(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})}<\infty
.
∎

Given a solution

, of (

Finally, consider the families

(6.3)

In view of our assumptions,
{\cal F}_{1}
is a family of continuous and bounded functions, whereas members of

are smooth and bounded.

Throughout this section, very often we will make use of the following hypothesis (they are conditions in theorems

and

H1

p=1
and

;

b=pc
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S})
,
u\in C^{0}([0,T),H^{s}(\mathbb{S}))\cap C^{1}([0,T),H^{s-1}(\mathbb{S}))
be the corresponding solution of (
1.1
) subject to

, and

given by (

).

1.

If
s>3/2
and

holds, then
f_{t}(x)\geq 0
.

2.

If
s>3/2
,

holds and
p
is odd, then

.

3.

If

,

holds,

is either non-negative or non-positive, then

or
f_{t}(x)\leq 0
, respectively.

If

,

In case

Discussion

In
[
16
]
it was proved a global existence result for (
1.1
) under certain conditions on the parameters (
b=p+1
, in our notation) and the authors pointed out that the same problem for other values is an open question. Our Theorem
2.1
covers a case not treated in the mentioned reference, since we consider the situation
b=0
and
p=1
. In this sense, it extends to (
2.3
) the results established in
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
. Also, the way we proved it is completely different from the approach used in
[
16
]
. Therefore, not only we extended the result by Himonas and Thompson for a case not covered before, but the way we extended it is also different when compared with that used in the aforementioned work. In fact, our proof uses the identities (
4.1
)–(
•
‣
4
) to estimate the functional (
4.4
), which is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{S})}
. Our approach is very similar to an analogous result for the non-periodic case firstly established in
[
7
]
.

In line with our contribution mentioned above, we delineated conditions for preventing the manifestation of blow-up of the periodic solutions of (
1.1
) as a wave breaking in our Theorem
2.2
, which is in line with similar results for equations of the Camassa-Holm type, see
[
23
, Theorem 3.4]
.

We note that our theorems
2.1
and
2.2
fill some gaps in the qualitative analysis of solutions of the
0-
equation and, in particular, not only it partially answers some questions pointed out in
[
16
]
, but also extends several results mentioned in our Section
2
to the
0-
equation. As pointed out by Zhou (see
[
24
, page 372]
), the description of necessary and sufficient conditions for the blow-up of the solutions of the
b-
equation is a difficult problem to be considered (and (
2.3
) in particular). Although Zhou’s comments are concerned with the non-periodic case, it is somewhat expected that the periodic case inherits the same issues.

Finally, we also explored the problem of unique continuation of solutions of (
1.1
) in theorems
2.3
and
2.4
. Two key ingredient for dealing with it is the construction of the families (
6.1
) and (

We give two demonstration for unique continuation of the solutions of (

, under restriction on the parameters of the equation and

The second proof uses the continuity of the data-to-solution map to show that the only solution of (

[

We observe that a crucial point in our approach is the existence of a norm of the solutions conserved along time. Under the conditions on the parameters, and the order

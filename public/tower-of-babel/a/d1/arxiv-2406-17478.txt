Vanishing viscosity limit for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with non-linear density dependent viscosities
Luca Bisconti Matteo Caggio Filippo Dell’Oro 1. Università degli Studi di Firenze - Dipartimento di Matematica e Informatica “U. Dini”Viale Morgagni 67/a, I-50134 Firenze, Italy2. Institute of Mathematics of the Academy of Sciences of the Czech RepublicŽitná 25, 11567 Praha 1, Czech Republic3. Politecnico di Milano - Dipartimento di MatematicaVia Bonardi 9, 20133 Milano, Italy* corresponding author: caggio@math.cas.cz

Abstract
In a three-dimensional bounded domain Ω \Omega we consider the compressible Navier-Stokes equations for a barotropic fluid with general non-linear density dependent viscosities and no-slip boundary conditions. A nonlinear drag term is added to the momentum equation. We establish two conditional Kato-type criteria for the convergence of the weak solutions to such a system towards the strong solution of the compressible Euler system when the viscosity coefficient and the drag term parameter tend to zero.



Keywords:
compressible Navier-Stokes equations, density dependent viscosities,
vanishing viscosity limit, boundary layer.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with
sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, and let
T>0
be
arbitrarily fixed. We consider the compressible Navier-Stokes
equations describing the motion of a barotropic fluid with density
dependent viscosities that may vanish on vacuum

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\textrm{div}(\varrho\mathbf{u})=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho\mathbf{u})+\textrm{div}(\varrho\mathbf{u}\otimes\mathbf{u})+\nabla p(\varrho)-\varepsilon[\hskip 0.85358pt\textrm{div}(2\mu(\varrho)\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}))+\nabla(\lambda(\varrho)\text{div}\mathbf{u})]+r_{1}\varrho\hskip 0.85358pt|\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{u}=0,

(1.2)

where
\varepsilon
and
r_{1}
are positive parameters. The unknown
variables

\varrho:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{u}:(0,T)\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3},

with
\varrho=\varrho\left(t,x\right)
,
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}\left(t,x\right)
,
represent the density and the velocity vector field,
respectively. The term
p=p(\varrho)
represent the pressure of the
fluid and is given by a standard power law

p(\varrho)=\varrho^{\gamma},\quad\,\,\gamma>1.

Moreover

\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla^{\top}\mathbf{u})
is the symmetric part of
\nabla\mathbf{u}
. The
density dependent viscosity coefficients
\mu(\varrho)
and

\lambda(\varrho)
satisfy the following algebraic relation

\lambda(\varrho)=2(\varrho\mu^{\prime}(\varrho)-\mu(\varrho))

(1.3)

and further structural assumptions specified later. The last term in the
momentum equation (
1.2
) corresponds to a turbulent drag force
(see, e.g.
[
5
]
).

The system is supplemented with the no-slip boundary condition

\varrho\mathbf{u}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(1.4)

Moreover, as in
[
5
]
, we prescribe the additional boundary
condition

\mu(\varrho)\nabla s(\varrho)\times\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(1.5)

where
\mathbf{n}
is the unit vector normal to
\partial\Omega
, and

s^{\prime}(\varrho)=\mu^{\prime}(\varrho)/\varrho
.
The latter expresses the fact
that the density should be constant on the connected components of

\partial\Omega
(see
[
5
]
). Finally, the initial state of the system is prescribed

\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},\qquad\varrho\mathbf{u}(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0}\mathbf{u}_{0}.

Formally, letting
\varepsilon,r_{1}\rightarrow 0
, one obtains the
compressible Euler system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho^{E}+\textrm{div}(\varrho^{E}\mathbf{u}^{E})=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho^{E}\mathbf{u}^{E})+\textrm{div}\,(\varrho^{E}\mathbf{u}^{E}\otimes\mathbf{u}^{E})+\nabla p(\varrho^{E})=0,

(1.7)

with the boundary condition

\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot\mathbf{n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(1.8)

and the initial conditions

\varrho^{E}(0)=\varrho_{0}^{E},\qquad\varrho^{E}\mathbf{u}^{E}(0)=\varrho_{0}^{E}\mathbf{u}_{0}^{E}.

As is well-known, a rigorous justification of such a singular limit on
domains with non-empty boundary is a challenging problem, due to the
appearance of boundary layers. A remarkable approach was introduced
in the seminal paper of Kato
[
22
]
, where the author studied the
vanishing viscosity limit (also called inviscid limit) of the
incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with no-slip boundary
conditions. He proved that, if the energy dissipation rate of the
viscous flow in a boundary layer of width proportional to the
viscosity vanishes, then the solutions of the incompressible
Navier–Stokes equations converge to the solutions of the
incompressible Euler equations. The analysis requires the construction
of an artificial “fake” boundary layer, needed to compensate the
discrepancy between the boundary conditions of the original system and
the boundary conditions of the target one. The result of
[
22
]

has been improved and generalized by several authors. Without any
claim of completeness, we mention
[
23
,
29
]
among many others. We
also mention the work of Constantin et al.
[
14
]
where the
inviscid limit has been performed under the so-called Oleinik
condition of no back-flow in the trace of the Euler flow and a lower
bound for the Navier-Stokes vorticity in a Kato-like boundary layer.

For the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with no-slip boundary
conditions and constant viscosity coefficients, that is, when the
viscous term

-\varepsilon[\hskip 0.85358pt\textrm{div}(2\mu(\varrho)\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}))+\nabla(\lambda(\varrho)\text{div}\mathbf{u})]

is replaced by

-\varepsilon\,\textrm{div}\,\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u})
, where

\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u})=\mu\Big(\nabla\mathbf{u}+\nabla^{\top}\mathbf{u}-\frac{2}{3}\big(\text{div}\mathbf{u}\,\mathbb{I}\big)\Big)+\eta(\text{div}\mathbf{u})\mathbb{I},

for some constants
\mu>0
and
\eta\geq 0
, some results
have been obtained (without the presence of the drag term in the momentum equation)
by Sueur
[
27
]
. He established the following sufficient
condition for the vanishing viscosity limit to hold:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{c\varepsilon}}\left(\varepsilon\,\frac{\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{d_{\Omega}^{2}}+\varepsilon\,\frac{\varrho^{2}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n})^{2}}{d_{\Omega}^{2}}+\varepsilon\,|\mathbb{S}(\nabla\mathbf{u})|^{2}\right)dxdt\to 0\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,\varepsilon\to 0.

(1.9)

Here and in the sequel, we denote by
d_{\Omega}=d_{\Omega}(x)=d(x,\partial\Omega)
the distance of

x\in\Omega
to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, and by

\Gamma_{c\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:d_{\Omega}(x)<c\varepsilon\},

for a constant
c>0
(in the case of (
1.9
), the
constant
c>0
is arbitrarily fixed). Condition (
1.9
)
can be regarded as a generalization of the result of Kato in the
context of compressible fluids. Indeed, in the light of the Hardy
inequality, when the density is constant (
1.9
) is implied by

\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{c\varepsilon}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}dxdt\to 0\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,\varepsilon\to 0,

which is exactly the condition employed by Kato in
[
22
]
.
Subsequently, Bardos and Nguyen
[
2
]
proved
the convergence to the so-called dissipative solutions of the compressible Euler equations
under the alternative condition

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{c\varepsilon}}\left(\varrho^{\gamma}+\varepsilon\,\frac{\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{d_{\Omega}^{2}}+\varepsilon\,|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)dxdt\to 0\,\,\mbox{ as }\,\,\varepsilon\to 0.

(1.10)

The achievements of
[
27
]
have been somehow improved by Wang and
Zhu
[
30
]
, where the authors assumed sufficient conditions
only on the tangential or the normal component of velocity, at the
cost of increasing the width of the boundary layer. See also the
recent article
[
31
]
which can be regarded as the
analogous of
[
14
]
for compressible fluids.

We now come to the literature concerning the vanishing viscosity
limit for the compressible Navier-Stokes system with density
dependent viscosity coefficients. In the three-dimensional torus,
the convergence has been analyzed by Bresch et. al.
[
7
]

employing the so-called “augmented version” of the compressible
Navier-Stokes system. Further results have been obtained by Geng et
al.
[
20
,
21
]
in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and for viscosity
coefficients of the form
\varrho^{\beta}
with
\beta>1
. We also
mention the articles
[
12
,
15
]
which study the case when the
viscosity coefficients obey a lower order power law of the density,
namely,
\varrho^{\beta}
with
0<\beta\leq 1
. The case of bounded
domains with non-empty boundary has been recently treated in

[
4
]
for the particular choice

\mu(\varrho)=\varrho\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda(\varrho)=0,

namely, for linear density dependent viscosity coefficients. In this article,
the passage to the limit has been performed under the assumption:

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{c\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\frac{\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}}{d_{\Omega}^{2}}\right)dxdt\to 0\,\,\textrm{ as }\,\,\varepsilon\to 0.

(1.11)

Note that, compared to (
1.9
) and (
1.10
),
relation (
1.11
) requires a decay rate for the

L^{\gamma}
-norm,
\gamma>1
, of the density and a stronger
condition on the kinetic energy term. But notably, no assumptions on
the viscous terms are imposed. The strategy adopted in
[
4
]

relies on the so-called relative energy estimates and, in particular,
on the introduction of a relative energy functional “measuring” the
distance between a weak solution of the compressible Navier-Stokes
system and a strong solution of the compressible Euler system. A
relative energy inequality satisfied by the weak solutions of the
Navier-Stokes equations has been derived, which however involves some
test functions that must satisfy the no-slip boundary conditions.
For this reason, as done in the previous
contributions mentioned above, a “correction” based on a Kato-type
“fake” boundary layer was needed. Still, different from the standard
construction of Feireisl et al.
[
18
,
19
]
, the
relative energy inequality constructed in
[
4
]
is derived from
the aforementioned “augmented version” of the compressible
Navier-Stokes system, in the same spirit of Bresch et
al.
[
6
,
7
]
through the use of the Bresch-Desjardins
entropy inequality (see also
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
13
]
for
recent applications).

Description of our results

The aim of the present paper is twofold, and can be summarized in the following points.

•

We extend the results of
[
4
]
to more general
and possibly non-linear density dependent viscosity coefficients

\mu(\varrho)
and
\lambda(\varrho)
. Actually, we also weaken the
second assumption in (
1.11
) by replacing the factor

\varepsilon^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}
with
\varepsilon
. See the
forthcoming Theorem
3.1
.

•

We establish a new conditional criterion analogous to
(
1.9
) for the validity of the vanishing viscosity limit
in the context of compressible fluids with density dependent
viscosities. See Theorem
3.2
below.

In the same spirit of
[
4
]
, we employ the relative energy
inequality approach and the construction of the “fake” boundary
layer. However, unlike
[
4
]
, we completely avoid the use of the
“augmented version” of the compressible Navier-Stokes system as well
as the use of the Bresch-Desjardins entropy inequality, following
instead the construction of Feireisl at al.
[
18
,
19
]
.

Plan of the paper

In the next Section 2, we introduce the assumptions satisfied by the viscosity coefficients

3.1
-
3.2
, while in Section 4 we derive the relative

the proofs of Theorems
3.1
-

.

,

, the space

\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and by
L^{p}(\Omega)
,

, the standard

W^{k,p}(\Omega)
,
k\geq 1
, consist of
functions in
L^{p}(\Omega)
with weak derivatives of order
k
that still

L^{p}(\Omega)
. The Bochner evolution spaces, for time-dependent

X

2.1
Assumptions on viscosity coefficients
\mu
and
\lambda

In the same spirit of
[
5
,
24
]
, and recalling that
\gamma>1
, in addition to the key relation (
1.3
)
we assume that there exists a positive constant

\nu\in\left[\frac{1}{3\gamma-2},1\right)\!,

(2.1)

such that the following conditions on the viscosity coefficients are satisfied:

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(\varrho)\geq\nu,\quad\mu(0)\geq 0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\lambda^{\prime}(\varrho)|\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\mu^{\prime}(\varrho),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\nu\mu(\varrho)\leq 2\mu(\varrho)+3\lambda(\varrho)\leq\frac{1}{\nu}\mu(\varrho).

(2.4)

When
\gamma\geq 3
, we also require that

\liminf_{\varrho\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\varrho)}{\varrho^{\gamma/3+\vartheta}}>0

(2.5)

for some small
\vartheta>0
.
As remarked in
[
5
,
24
]
, hypothesis (
1.3
) and (
2.4
) yield the bounds

\begin{cases}\mu(\varrho)\leq c_{1}\varrho^{\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{\nu}{3}\right)},\quad&\text{if }\varrho\leq 1,\\
\vskip 1.99168pt\cr\mu(\varrho)\leq c_{1}\varrho^{\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3\nu}\right)},\quad&\text{if }\varrho\geq 1,\end{cases}

(2.6)

for some
c_{1}>0
.
Moreover, it follows from (
2.4
) that

|\lambda(\varrho)|\leq c_{2}\mu(\varrho),

(2.7)

for all
\varrho>0
and some
c_{2}>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

The functions
\mu(\varrho)=\varrho
and
\lambda(\varrho)=0
satisfy
all the assumptions stated above, including (
1.3
), provided that

\gamma\geq 4/3
.

2.2
Weak solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes system

We introduce the definition of the weak solution to system
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
). As is well-known, the degenerate
viscosities prevent
\mathbf{u}
to be defined on the vacuum region
(see e.g.
[
24
]
). As a consequence, not
\mathbf{u}
nor

\nabla\mathbf{u}
are defined almost everywhere. On the other hand,
the quantities
\sqrt{\varrho}
and
\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}
turn
out to be well-defined (see e.g.
[
24
]
). Consequently, the
density
\varrho\geq 0
(thought as
(\sqrt{\varrho})^{2}
) and the
momentum
\varrho\mathbf{u}
(thought as

\sqrt{\varrho}\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}
) are also
well-defined. Accordingly, the correct way to interpret the
viscous term is to think it as

\textrm{div}(2\mu(\varrho)\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}))+\nabla(\lambda(\varrho)\text{div}\mathbf{u})=2\,\textrm{div}\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big),

where the matrix-valued function
\mathbb{S}_{\mu}
is the symmetric
part of the matrix-valued function
\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
defined through
the identity (see e.g.
[
10
]
)

\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{T}_{\mu}=\nabla\Big(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\,\frac{\mu(\varrho)}{\sqrt{\varrho}}\Big)-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\otimes\sqrt{\varrho}\nabla s(\varrho),\quad\,\,\,\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\Omega)$},

(2.8)

with
s^{\prime}(\varrho)=\mu^{\prime}(\varrho)/\varrho.

Remark 2.2
.

In the sequel, especially in providing sufficient conditions for the vanishing
viscosity limit (in the spirit of (
1.9
)-to-(
1.11
)),
as a matter of notation, we write
\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}
in place of
|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|^{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T>0
be arbitrarily fixed. We
say that
(\varrho,\mathbf{u})
with
\varrho\geq 0
is a weak
solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with boundary conditions
(
1.4
)-(
1.5
) on the time interval
[0,T]
if the
following hold.

(1)
Regularity properties

\displaystyle\varrho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\quad\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u},\,\,\sqrt{\varrho}\,\nabla s(\varrho),\,\,\mu(\varrho)/\sqrt{\varrho}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

(2)
The continuity equation (
1.1
) is
satisfied in the following sense

-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\partial_{t}\phi\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dxdt=\int_{\Omega}\varrho(0,\cdot)\phi(0,\cdot)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\varrho(\tau,\cdot)\phi(\tau,\cdot)\,dx

for all
\tau\in[0,T]
and every

\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R})
.

(3)
The momentum equation (
1.2
) is satisfied in
the following sense

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\otimes\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho)\textrm{div}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\boldsymbol{\varphi}\Big]dxdt+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi})(0,\cdot)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi})(\tau,\cdot)\,dx

for all
\tau\in[0,T]
and every

\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
where the matrix-valued function
\mathbb{S}_{\mu}
is the symmetric
part of the matrix-valued function
\mathbb{T}_{\mu}
defined in
(
2.8
).

(4)
The following energy inequality is satisfied

\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Big](\tau,\cdot)dx+\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{\varrho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Big]dx

(2.9)

for all
\tau\in[0,T]
, where
\nu
is the same parameter appearing in
(
2.1
)-(
2.4
).

Remark 2.3
.

A discussion concerning the derivation of (
2.9
) is presented in
the Appendix. In this regard, note that
\mu(\varrho)
is a positive
quantity, while

4
Relative energy inequality

In this section, we derive a relative energy inequality satisfied by
the weak solution
(\varrho,\mathbf{u})
. Here and in the sequel, we
denote by

H(\varrho)=\frac{\varrho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}.

For
\tau\in[0,T]
we introduce the relative energy functional (see
e.g.
[
19
]
)

\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,r,\mathbf{U})(\tau)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}-\sqrt{\varrho}\,\mathbf{U}|^{2}(\tau)dx+\int_{\Omega}H(\varrho|r)(\tau)dx,

(4.1)

where
(\varrho,\mathbf{u})
is a weak solution to
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
) while
(r,\mathbf{U})
is a pair of smooth
test functions such that
\mathbf{U}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and

r>0
. Moreover

H(\varrho|r)=H(\varrho)-H(r)-H^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r).

(4.2)

Note that
H(\varrho|r)
is non-negative and strictly convex. In
addition, it is equal to zero when
\varrho=r
and it grows at
infinity as
\varrho^{\gamma}
. Consequently, for any compact set

K\subset(0,\infty)
there exist two constants
c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that
for every
\varrho\geq 0
and every
r\in K

c_{3}\big(\left|\varrho-r\right|^{2}1_{\left\{\left|\varrho-r\right|<1\right\}}+\left|\varrho-r\right|^{\gamma}1_{\left\{\left|\varrho-r\right|\geq 1\right\}}\big)\leq H(\varrho|r)

(4.3)

and

H(\varrho|r)\leq c_{4}\big(\left|\varrho-r\right|^{2}1_{\left\{\left|\varrho-r\right|<1\right\}}+\left|\varrho-r\right|^{\gamma}1_{\left\{\left|\varrho-r\right|\geq 1\right\}}\big)

(4.4)

(see e.g.
[
18
]
and
[
27
, Section 2.1]
). Moreover,
being
\Omega
a bounded domain, for any compact set

K\subset(0,\infty)
there exists a constant
c_{5}>0
such that for
every
\varrho:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
and
r:\Omega\to K

\|\varrho-r\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}\leq c_{5}\left(\int_{\Omega}H(\varrho|r)dx\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}+c_{5}\int_{\Omega}H(\varrho|r)dx,

(4.5)

and

\int_{\Omega}H(\varrho|r)dx\leq c_{5}\|\varrho-r\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}+c_{5}\|\varrho-r\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{2}

(4.6)

(see again
[
27
, Section 2.1]
).
Thanks to the energy inequality (
2.9
), we infer that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,r,\mathbf{U})(s)\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{U}|^{2}-\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}\right)(s,\cdot)dx\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}-\int_{\Omega}\left(H(r)+H^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r)\right)(s,\cdot)dx\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt.

Next, we test the continuity equation by
\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{U}|^{2}
and the momentum equation by
\mathbf{U}
,
to get

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\varrho\partial_{t}|\mathbf{U}|^{2}\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla|\mathbf{U}|^{2}\,dxdt=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{U}|^{2}(s,\cdot)dx\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

and

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\otimes\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{U}\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho)\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{U}\,dxdt

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\mathbf{U}\Big]dxdt+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}dxdt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U})(s,\cdot)dx\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}.

Then, testing the continuity equation by
H^{\prime}(r)
and noticing that

H^{\prime}(r)r-H(r)=p(r)
, after some straightforward computation, we have

\int_{\Omega}\left(H(r)+H^{\prime}(r)(\varrho-r)\right)(s,\cdot)dx\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(r))(\varrho-r)+\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(r))\right]dxdt.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,r,\mathbf{U})(s)\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\varrho\partial_{t}|\mathbf{U}|^{2}\,dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla|\mathbf{U}|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\,dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\otimes\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}):\nabla\mathbf{U}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\mathbf{U}\Big]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(r))(\varrho-r)+\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(r))+p(\varrho)\mbox{div}\mathbf{U}\right]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}dxdt-\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt.

We now consider
(\varrho^{E},\mathbf{u}^{E})
the strong solution of the Euler system
(
1.6
)–(
1.7
).
Rearranging the relative entropy inequality above with
r=\varrho^{E}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,\varrho^{E},\mathbf{U})(s)\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})\right]\,dxdt\\
&\quad+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\mathbf{U}\Big]dxdt\\
&\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))(\varrho-\varrho^{E})+\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))+p(\varrho)\mbox{div}\mathbf{U}\right]dxdt\\
&\quad+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}dxdt-\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt,\end{aligned}

(4.7)

having set
(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)(\,\cdot\,)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})_{i}\,\partial_{i}(\,\cdot\,)
.
We now multiply (
1.6
) by
H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})
. Performing integration by parts, we find

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\partial_{t}\varrho^{E}-\varrho^{E}\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))\cdot\mathbf{u}^{E}\right]dxdt=0.

From
p(\varrho^{E})=H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\varrho^{E}-H(\varrho^{E})
,
we also infer that
\varrho^{E}\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))=\nabla p(\varrho^{E})
. Accordingly,
performing again integration by parts, we obtain

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\partial_{t}\varrho^{E}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}dxdt=0.

Back to (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,\varrho^{E},\mathbf{U})(s)\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})\right]\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\mathbf{U}\Big]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))(\varrho-\varrho^{E})+\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))+p(\varrho)\mbox{div}\mathbf{U}\right]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\partial_{t}\varrho^{E}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}dxdt-\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt.

Now, from (
1.7
) and the continuity equation (
1.6
), we easily deduce that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})+\frac{1}{\varrho^{E}}\nabla p(\varrho^{E})\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})=0.

Hence, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,\varrho^{E},\mathbf{U})(s)\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})\right]\,dxdt

(4.8)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})]dxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varrho^{E}}\nabla p(\varrho^{E})\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})dxdt

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\mathbf{U}\Big]dxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))(\varrho-\varrho^{E})+\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))+p(\varrho)\mbox{div}\mathbf{U}\right]dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\partial_{t}\varrho^{E}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p(\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}dxdt

\displaystyle+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}dxdt-\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt.

Since using again (
1.6
)

\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))=-p^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}-\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))\cdot\mathbf{u}^{E},

we see that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\varrho^{E}}\nabla p(\varrho^{E})\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})dxdt-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))(\varrho-\varrho^{E})dxdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})\partial_{t}\varrho^{E}dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}p^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})(\varrho-\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{u}^{E}-\varrho\mathbf{U})dxdt.

Inserting the identity above into (
4.8
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{u}\,|\,\varrho^{E},\mathbf{U})(s)\big|_{s=0}^{s=\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[\partial_{t}\mathbf{U}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})\right]\,dxdt

(4.9)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}[\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{U}-\varrho\mathbf{u})+(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{E}\cdot(\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{U}-\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u})]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\Big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}+\frac{\lambda(\varrho)}{2\mu(\varrho)}\textrm{Tr}\big(\sqrt{\mu(\varrho)}\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\big)\mathbb{I}\Big):\nabla\mathbf{U}\Big]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left[p(\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}-p(\varrho)\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{U}+p^{\prime}(\varrho^{E})(\varrho-\varrho^{E})\textrm{div}\,\mathbf{u}^{E}\right]dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(H^{\prime}(\varrho^{E}))\cdot(\varrho\mathbf{u}^{E}-\varrho\mathbf{U})dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}|\sqrt{\varrho}\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{U}dxdt-\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\left|\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\right|^{2}dxdt.

Estimate of

Appendix

We discuss briefly the derivation of the energy inequality (
2.9
).
To this end, let us consider smooth solutions
(\varrho,\mathbf{u})

with
\varrho>0
. Multiplying the momentum equation (
1.2
)
by
\mathbf{u}
and after a standard computation, one can show that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}(\tau)+H(\varrho)(\tau)\Big]dx+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\big[2\mu(\varrho)|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\lambda(\varrho)|\text{div}_{x}\mathbf{u}|^{2}|\big]dxdt

(8.1)

\displaystyle+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dxdt=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}+H(\varrho_{0})\Big]dx.

We claim that

2\mu(\varrho)|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\lambda(\varrho)|\text{div}_{x}\mathbf{u}|^{2}\geq\nu\mu(\varrho)|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}

(8.2)

where
\nu>0
is the parameter appearing in assumption
(
2.2
)-(
2.4
). This estimate is trivial when
\lambda(\varrho)\geq 0
.
On the other hand, when
\lambda(\varrho)<0
, we use the
elementary inequality
|\text{div}_{x}\mathbf{u}|^{2}\leq 3|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}
.
Accordingly

2\mu(\varrho)|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\lambda(\varrho)|\text{div}_{x}\mathbf{u}|^{2}\geq[2\mu(\varrho)+3\lambda(\varrho)]|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}

and the conclusion follow from (
2.4
).
Next, using (
8.2
) into (
8.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{2}(\tau)+H(\varrho)(\tau)\Big]dx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\nu\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\mu(\varrho)|\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u})|^{2}dxdt+r_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\varrho|\mathbf{u}|^{3}dxdt

(8.3)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}+H(\varrho_{0})\Big]dx.

Consider now a sequence of smooth solutions
(\varrho_{n},\mathbf{u}_{n})

with
\varrho_{n}>0
satisfying (
8.3
) and such that

\int_{\Omega}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\varrho_{0n}|\mathbf{u}_{0n}|^{2}+H(\varrho_{0n})\Big]\leq E_{0}

with
E_{0}
independent on
n
.
From (
8.3
), it follows that for all
\tau\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\varrho_{n}}\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(E_{0}),

\displaystyle\|\varrho_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))}\leq C(E_{0}),

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\mu(\varrho_{n})}\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{n})\|_{L^{2}((0,\tau)\times\Omega)}\leq C(\varepsilon,\nu,E_{0}),

\displaystyle\|\sqrt[3]{\varrho_{n}}\mathbf{u}_{n}\|_{L^{3}((0,\tau)\times\Omega)}\leq C(r_{1},E_{0}).

These bounds, combined with some further bounds coming from the
Bresch-Desjardins entropy inequality, have been used to prove the
stability of weak solutions and the existence result obtained in

[
5
]
(see also
[
24
]
for the whole space and the periodic
case). In particular, the drag term provides the last bound in

{L^{3}((0,\tau)\times\Omega)}
of the term

\sqrt[3]{\varrho_{n}}\mathbf{u}_{n}
which replaces the bound on

\sqrt{\varrho_{n}}\mathbf{u}_{n}
obtained in
[
24
]
by the use of
the multiplier
(1+\ln{(1+|\mathbf{u}|^{2})})\mathbf{u}
in the momentum
equation. As is well-known, this control on the term

\sqrt[3]{\varrho_{n}}\mathbf{u}_{n}
is sufficient to obtain enough
compactness on
\sqrt{\varrho}_{n}\mathbf{u}_{n}
in order to pass to the
limit. Consequently, with the same arguments of
[
5
,
24
]
, we
are able to pass to the limit as
n\to\infty
, getting the energy
inequality (
2.9
).

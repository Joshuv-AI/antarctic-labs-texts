Convergence rate for homogenization of a nonlocal model with oscillating coefficients Journal: Journal of Applied Mathematics Letters
Li Lin & Jinqiao Duan

Abstract
This letter deals with homogenization of a nonlocal model with Lévy-type operator of rapidly oscillating coefficients. This nonlocal model describes mean residence time and other escape phenomena for stochastic dynamical systems with non-Gaussian Lévy noise. We derive an effective model with a specific convergence rate. This enables efficient analysis and simulation of escape phenomena under non-Gaussian fluctuations.

Introduction

We consider the homogenization of the following nonlocal partial differential equation

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}(x)=f(x),\qquad x\in D,\\
u_{\epsilon}|_{D^{c}}=g(x),\end{cases}

(1)

arising in the study of escape phenomena of stochastic dynamical systems under Lévy fluctuations
[
1
]
. Here
D
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When
f=-1
and
g=0
, the solution of this equation is the mean residence time for such a stochastic system in domain
D
. Specifically, the nonlocal operator depends on a small positive scale parameter
\varepsilon
and is defined by

\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}u=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}u),

where the coefficient
\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)=\Theta(\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{z}{\epsilon})
is of period
1
in
(x,z)
and for a certain positive constant
\lambda
,

\frac{1}{\lambda}<\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)<\lambda.

The nonlocal linear operator
\mathcal{D}
and its adjoint operator
\mathcal{D}^{*}
are defined as follows
[
16
]
.
Given the antisymmetric kernel function

\gamma(x,z)=(z-x)\frac{1}{|z-x|^{\frac{2+d+\alpha}{2}}}
(note that
\gamma(z,x)=-\gamma(x,z)
), the nonlocal divergence
\mathcal{D}
is defined by

\mathcal{D}(\beta)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\beta(x,z)+\beta(z,x))\cdot\gamma(x,z)dz,

x\in D.

The adjoint operator
\mathcal{D^{*}}
of
\mathcal{D}
is then given by

\mathcal{D^{*}}(\phi)(x,z)=-(\phi(z)-\phi(x))\gamma(x,z)
for
x,z\in D.

By the way, if
\Theta\equiv 1
, then we see a relation with the nonlocal Laplace operator

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{D^{*}}=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}.
The nonlocal Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is defined by

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{x\}}\frac{u(z)-u(x)}{|z-x|^{d+\alpha}}dz,

where the integral is in the sense of Cauchy principal value, and it is the generator of a symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy motion
[
2
]

2.1
Function spaces

In this subsection, we define the following two important spaces. One is the space
\mathcal{V}^{D}
, which is the counterpart of the classical Sobolev space
H^{1}(D)
. The other is the space
\mathcal{X}^{D}
, as an analogue of the usual trace space
H^{1/2}(\partial D).

We set
\nu(x,y)=\gamma^{2}(x,z)
. we introduce a quadratic form
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
for
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{E}_{D}(u,u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D^{c}\times D^{c}}(u(x)-u(z))^{2}\nu(x,z)dxdz.

Now we define the corresponding Sobolev space
[
7
]
:

\mathcal{V}^{D}=\{u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\text{such that}\;\mathcal{E}_{D}(u,u)<\infty\}.

We also denote

\mathcal{V}^{D}_{0}=\{u\in\mathcal{V}^{D};u=0\;\;\;a.e.\;on\;D^{c}\}.

More precisely,
\mathcal{V}^{D}_{0}=H_{0}^{\alpha/2}(D)
from

[
8
, Theorem 3.33]
. The space
H_{0}^{\alpha/2}(D)
is the standard fractional Sobolev space.
For
s<0
, we denote
H^{s}(D)
as the dual space of
H_{0}^{-s}(D)
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{V}^{D}
is a Hilbert subspace of
L^{2}(D)
, with the induced norm

||u||^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}=||u||^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}+\mathcal{E}_{D}(u,u).

We will use the notation
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to denote the
d
-dimensional unit torus. The space
H^{\alpha/2}_{\#}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of
1
-periodic functions
u\in H^{\alpha/2}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(y)dy=0
will be interest in this study. Provided with the norm,

\|u\|_{H^{\alpha/2}_{\#}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|u(y)-u(\eta)|^{2}}{|y-\eta|^{d+\alpha}}dyd\eta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We let

\mathcal{E}_{D}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D^{c}\times D^{c}}(u(x)-u(z))(v(x)-v(z))\nu(x,z)dxdz,

and

a^{\epsilon}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D^{c}\times D^{c}}\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)(u(x)-u(z))(v(x)-v(z))\nu(x,z)dxdz,

if the integrals are absolutely convergent, in particular for
u,v\in\mathcal{V}^{D}.

By a solution of (
1
) we mean a weak solution, which is defined as every function
u_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{V}^{D}
equal to
g
a.e. on
D^{c}
such that
for every
\phi\in\mathcal{V}^{D}_{0},

a^{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\phi)=\int f\phi dx,
this integral is infinite, e.g., if
D
is bounded and
f\in L^{2}(D).

Remark 1
.

Without loss of generality, we take the coefficient
\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)
to be a symmetric function. In fact, we can define the symmetric and anti-symmetric parts of
\Theta^{\epsilon}
:

\Theta^{\epsilon}_{s}(x,z)=\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)+\Theta^{\epsilon}(z,x))\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta^{\epsilon}_{a}(x,z)=\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)-\Theta^{\epsilon}(z,x)).

By
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
,

\left(\Theta^{\epsilon}_{a}(x,z)\mathcal{D}^{*}u,\mathcal{D}^{*}\phi\right)=0.
Then
\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)\mathcal{D}^{*}u)=\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}_{s}(x,z)\mathcal{D}^{*}u).

Let
G^{\epsilon}_{D}(x,y)
and
P^{\epsilon}_{D}(x,y)
be the Green function and Poisson kernel of
D
for
\mathcal{A^{\epsilon}}
respectively. At the same time
we let
G_{D}(x,y)
and
P_{D}(x,y)
be the Green function and Poisson kernel for
\mathcal{A^{\epsilon}}
with
\Theta^{\epsilon}=1
on
D
, respectively. In this case

\mathcal{A^{\epsilon}}u=-2(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u.

For
g:D^{c}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we let
P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g](x)=g(x)
for
x\in D^{c}
and

P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g](x)=\int_{D^{c}}g(y)P^{\epsilon}_{D}(x,y)dy
for
x\in D.

Furthermore,
u=P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]
is the unique solution of the following homogeneous Dirchlet problem:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}=0,\qquad x\in D,\\
u^{\epsilon}|_{D^{c}}=g.\end{cases}

(2)

Remark 2

(
[
4
]
)
.

For
f\in H^{-\alpha/2}(D)\text{ and }g\in\mathcal{X}^{D}
, we have the existence and uniqueness of equations (
1
) and (
2
) in
\mathcal{V}^{D}
.

Next, for
\omega,\xi\in D^{c},
we let

\gamma_{D}(\omega,\xi)=\int_{D}\int_{D}\nu(\omega,x)G_{D}(x,y)\nu(y,\xi)dxdy=\int_{D}\nu(\xi,x)P_{D}(x,\omega)dx.

For
g:D^{c}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we introduce a quadratic form

\mathcal{H}_{D}(g,g)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D^{c}\times D^{c}}(g(\omega)-g(\xi))^{2}\gamma_{D}(\xi,\omega)d\omega d\xi.

Then we define as in
[
7
]
a new space

\mathcal{X}^{D}=\{g:D^{c}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\text{such that}\;\mathcal{H}_{D}(g,g)<\infty\}.

If

and
x\in D,
we obtain
\int_{D^{c}}g(z)^{2}P_{D}(x,z)dz<\infty.
We fix an arbitrary (reference) point
x_{0}\in D.
For
g\in\mathcal{X}^{D},
we let

|g|^{2}_{D^{c}}=\int_{D^{c}}g(z)^{2}P_{D}(x_{0},z)dz

(we omit
x_{0}
from the notation).
Then
\mathcal{X}^{D}
is a Hilbert space with the induced norm

||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}=|g|^{2}_{D^{c}}+\mathcal{H}_{D}(g,g).

2.2
Effective equation and convergence rate

Our main result is that the heterogeneous model (
1
) is approximated by a homogenized effective model (
3
) below, with convergence rate
\frac{1}{2}
. This is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For scale parameter
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, the solution
u_{\epsilon}
of heterogeneous model (
1
) is approximated by the solution
u_{0}
of the following homogenized equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-a_{1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u_{0}-a_{2}\mathcal{F}u_{0}(x)=f(x),\qquad x\in D,\\
u_{0}|_{D^{c}}=g(x).\end{cases}

(3)

with coefficients

\displaystyle a_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)dyd\eta,\qquad a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}\chi(y)dyd\eta,

\displaystyle\zeta(u_{0})(x)=\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{D}(D^{*}u_{0})(x,z)dz,\qquad\mathcal{F}u_{0}(x)=\mathcal{D}|_{D}\zeta(u_{0})(x)=\int_{D}\left[\zeta(u_{0})(x)+\zeta(u_{0})(z)\right]\gamma(x,z)dz,

where the function
\chi(y)
is the unique solution of the following variational problem

\begin{cases}$${\hat{a}}(\chi,v)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)D^{*}_{y}vdyd\eta,\\
\chi\in H_{\#}^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).\\
\end{cases}

(4)

Moreover, if
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
and
\chi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then
u_{\epsilon}
has the following asymptotic expansion

u_{\epsilon}=u_{0}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{D}(D^{*}u_{0})(x,z)dz\cdot\chi(\frac{x}{\epsilon})+R(\varepsilon),

and there exists
a constant
C
(independent of
\varepsilon
) such that the remainder is estimated as

\left\|R(\varepsilon)\right\|_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}=\left\|u_{\epsilon}-(u_{0}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{D}(D^{*}u_{0})(x,z)dz\cdot\chi(\frac{x}{\epsilon}))\right\|_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}.

This says that
u_{\epsilon}\rightarrow u_{0},
in Sobolev space
\mathcal{V}^{D},
with convergence rate
\frac{1}{2}.

In order to prove this theorem, we recall some lemmas. The next result is due to
[
7
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded, open and Lipschitz,
|\partial D|=0.

1.

If
g\in\mathcal{X}^{D},
then
P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]\in\mathcal{V}^{D}
and
\mathcal{E}_{D}(P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g],P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g])=\mathcal{H}_{D}(g,g).

2.

If
u\in\mathcal{V}^{D},
then
g=u|_{D^{c}}\in\mathcal{X}^{D}
and
\mathcal{E}_{D}(u,u)\geq\mathcal{H}_{D}(g,g).

Let
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a Lévy process with
(0,\nu,0)
as the Lévy triplet on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}).
We introduce the time of the first exit of
X
from
D,

\tau_{D}=\tau_{D}(X)=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\notin D\}.

Lemma 2
.

The assumptions are the same as in Lemma
1
. Then for every
g\in\mathcal{X}^{D},
there exists a positive constant
C(D)
such that
||P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq C(D)||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}.

Proof.

We write
U\subset\subset D
if U is an open set, its closure
\overline{U}

is bounded, and
\overline{U}\subset D.
Let
\tilde{u}^{\epsilon}
be the unique solution of the homogeneous Dirchlet problem (
2
) and
\mathbb{E}^{x}
be the expectation for

X_{t}
start at
x\in D
. We have

\mathbb{E}^{x}\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(X_{\tau_{D}})=\int_{D^{c}}\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(y)P_{D}^{\epsilon}(x,y)dy.

Then we obtain

||P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]||^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}=\int_{D}(\int_{D^{c}}g(y)P_{D}^{\epsilon}(x,y)dy)^{2}dx\leq\int_{D}\int_{D^{c}}g(y)^{2}P_{D}^{\epsilon}(x,y)dydx,

due to the fact that
\int_{D^{c}}P_{D}^{\epsilon}(x,y)dy=1,
for
x\in D.

That is to say
||P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]||^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\mathbb{E}^{x}\tilde{u}^{2}_{\epsilon}(X_{\tau_{D}}).

Note that
\mathbb{E}^{x}\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(X_{\tau_{D}})
is a closed martingale
[
7
, Remark 4.4]
and for
x\in U,

x\rightarrow\int_{U^{c}}g(y)^{2}P_{U}^{\epsilon}(x,y)dy
satisfies the Harnack inequality
[
9
]
. Thus

\begin{split}||P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]||^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}&\leq\lim_{x\in U\subset\subset D}\int_{U}\int_{U^{c}}g(y)^{2}P_{U}^{\epsilon}(x,y)dydx\leq\lim_{x\in U\subset\subset D}\int_{U}C\int_{U^{c}}g(y)^{2}P_{U}^{\epsilon}(x_{0},y)dydx=C(D)|g|_{D^{c}}^{2}.\end{split}

By Lemma
1
, we obtain

\begin{split}||P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]||^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}&=||P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g]||^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}+\mathcal{E}_{D}(P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g],P^{\epsilon}_{D}[g])\leq C(D)|g|_{D^{c}}^{2}+\mathcal{H}_{D}(g,g)\leq C(D)||g||^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}.\end{split}

Hence Lemma 2 follows.
∎

Next, we obtain a uniform estimate concerning the solution
u_{\epsilon}
for the original heterogeneous equation (
1
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
f
in
H^{-\alpha/2}(D)
,
g
in
\mathcal{X}^{D}
and
u_{\epsilon}
be the unique solution of the original heterogenous equation (
1
).
Then there exist two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

||u_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq C_{1}||f||_{H^{-\alpha/2}(D)}+C_{2}||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}.

Proof.

From Lemma
2
, we can obtain the following conclusion. For every
g\in\mathcal{X}^{D},
there exists
G\in\mathcal{V}^{D}
and a linear operator
\rho
such that
\rho(G)=G|_{D^{c}}=g
and

||G||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq C(D)||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}.

For every
v\in\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D},
we have

\begin{split}|(\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}G,v)|&=\frac{1}{2}|(\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G),v)|\leq\lambda C||\mathcal{D}^{*}G||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}||\mathcal{D}^{*}v||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 4\lambda C||G||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}||v||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}.\end{split}

Then we obtain
\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G)\in(\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D})^{*}.

That is to say, for
x\in D,
we have
f-\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G)\in H^{-\alpha/2}.

Recall that

a^{\epsilon}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D^{c}\times D^{c}}\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)(u(x)-u(z))(v(x)-v(z))\nu(x,z)dxdz,

for every
v\in\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}.
We can find a unique
v_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}
such that
a^{\epsilon}(v_{\epsilon},v)=<f-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G),v>_{(\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D})^{*},\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}.

Due to the Poincaré inequality
[
4
]
, there exists a constant
C\geq 1,
for every
u\in\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D},

||u||_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D^{c}\times D^{c}}(u(x)-u(z))^{2}\nu(x,z)dxdz.

We thus obtain
||u||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}\leq(2C+1)||\mathcal{D}^{*}u||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In other words, the space
\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}
can be equipped by the norm
||\mathcal{D}^{*}u||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Then
a^{\epsilon}(v,v)\geq C||v||^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}
and
|a^{\epsilon}(u,v)|\leq C||u||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}||v||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}.

From the Lax-Milgram theorem,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda}||v_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}^{2}\leq a^{\epsilon}(v_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})=<f-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G),v_{\epsilon}>_{(\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D})^{*},\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}\leq||f-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G)||_{H^{-\alpha/2}(D)}||v_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}.\end{split}

We can see that

||v_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}\leq\lambda||f-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G)||_{H^{-\alpha/2}(D)}.

Set
u_{\epsilon}=v_{\epsilon}+G.
By the linearity of
\rho
, we have
\rho(u_{\epsilon})=\rho(G)=g.
Furthermore,

a^{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},v)=a^{\epsilon}(v_{\epsilon},v)+a^{\epsilon}(G,v)=(f,v),

which means that
u_{\epsilon}
is the unique solution of the original heterogenous equation (
1
).
Then

\begin{split}||u_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}&\leq||u_{\epsilon}-G||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}+||G||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq C||v_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}+\sqrt{C(D)}||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}\\
&\leq\lambda C||f-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G)||_{H^{-\alpha/2}(D)}+\sqrt{C(D)}||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}.\end{split}

On the other hand,

\begin{split}<\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G),v>_{(\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D})^{*},\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D^{c}\times D^{c}}\Theta^{\epsilon}(x,z)\mathcal{D}^{*}G\mathcal{D}^{*}vdxdz\leq\lambda C||\mathcal{D}^{*}G||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}||\mathcal{D}^{*}v||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
&\leq C||G||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}||\mathcal{D}^{*}v||_{L^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}||v||_{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{D}}.\end{split}

That implies
||\mathcal{D}(\Theta^{\epsilon}\mathcal{D}^{*}G)||\leq C||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}
.
Hence

||u_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq C_{1}||f||_{H^{-\alpha/2}(D)}+C_{2}||g||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}.

This completes the proof. ∎

2.3
Proof of Theorem 1

We are now ready to prove our main result in Theorem
1
.

Step 1:
First, we will derive the homogenized equation for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.
For a function
\upsilon(x,y)
, we define

(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}\upsilon)(x,z,y)=-(\upsilon(z,y)-\upsilon(x,y))\gamma(x,z)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{D}_{x}\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}\upsilon)(x,y)&=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}-(\upsilon(z,y)-\upsilon(x,y))\gamma^{2}(x,z)dz\\
&=-2(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}_{x}\upsilon(x,y).\end{split}

Denote
\eta=\frac{z}{\epsilon}
a variable on the period:
\eta\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.
We look for a formal asymptotic expansion:

u_{\epsilon}=u_{0}(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon})+\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}u_{1}(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon})+o(\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}),

with
u_{i}(x,y)
, for

, such that

\begin{cases}u_{i}(x,y)\quad\text{is defined for}\quad x\in{D}\quad\text{and}\quad y\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}},\\
u_{i}(\cdot,y)\quad\text{is $1$-periodic}.\\
\end{cases}

For every function
h,
we denote
h^{\epsilon}(x)=h(\frac{x}{\epsilon})
. Thus

\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{\epsilon}=\mathcal{A}_{1}u_{0}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}(\mathcal{A}_{0}u_{0})^{\epsilon}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\mathcal{A}_{1}u_{1}+(\mathcal{A}_{0}u_{1})^{\epsilon}+o(\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}),

\mathcal{A}_{0}v(x,y):=(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{y}v)(x,y,\eta),\mathcal{A}_{1}v(x,y):=(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}v)(x,z,y).

Then we have
\mathcal{A}_{0}u_{0}=0
. Furthermore,
u_{0}(x,y)=u_{0}(x).

Let
C_{per}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
be the subspace of
C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
1
-periodic functions. For every
v\in\mathcal{M}(D,C_{per}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

(

is the space of functions in

We conclude

\begin{split}(f,v_{\epsilon})&=\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{A}_{1}u_{0}+(\mathcal{A}_{0}u_{1})^{\epsilon}),D^{*}v_{\epsilon})=\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{A}_{1}u_{0}+(\mathcal{A}_{0}u_{1})^{\epsilon}),D^{*}v_{\epsilon})_{L^{2}(D\times D)}\\
&+\int_{D\times D^{c}}\Theta^{\epsilon}(u_{0}(x)-g(z))v_{\epsilon}(x)\gamma^{2}(x,z)dzdx:=I_{1}^{\epsilon}+I_{2}^{\epsilon}.\end{split}

By
[
15
, Lemma 2.34]
, for every
v\in\mathcal{M}(D,C_{per}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

I_{1}^{\epsilon},I_{2}^{\epsilon}
converges, as
\varepsilon
goes to
0.

Now take
v_{\epsilon}(x)=v_{0}(x)+\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}v_{1}(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon}),
where
v_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(D)
and
v_{1}\in L^{2}(D,C_{per}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

As
\varepsilon
goes to
0,
we have

I_{1}^{\epsilon}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{D\times D}\Theta(y,\eta)(\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0}+\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}u_{1})(\mathcal{D}^{*}v_{0}+\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}v_{1})dxdzdyd\eta.

(5)

On one hand, let
v_{0}=0,
we have
I_{2}^{\epsilon}\rightarrow 0
and

\begin{split}\int_{D\times D}(\Theta(y,\eta)\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}u_{1},\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}v_{1})_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}})}dxdz&=-\int_{D\times D}(\Theta(y,\eta)\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0},\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}v_{1})_{L^{2}{(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}})}dxdz.\end{split}

(6)

For all
w,v\in H_{\#}^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we introduce two quadratic forms:

{\hat{a}}(w,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)D^{*}_{y}wD^{*}_{y}vdyd\eta.

From equation (
6

|D|\int_{D}{\hat{a}}(u_{1},v)dx=-\int_{D}(\int_{D}\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0}(x,z)dz){\hat{a}}(\chi,v)dx,

where
\chi(y)
is the unique solution of the following variational problem

\begin{cases}{\hat{a}}(\chi,v)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)D^{*}_{y}vdyd\eta,\\
\chi\in H_{\#}^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}),\\
\end{cases}

(7)

for all
v\in H_{\#}^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}).

So

u_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{D}(D^{*}u_{0})(x,z)dz\cdot\chi(y)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H_{\#}^{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).

(8)

Moreover, let
v_{1}=0,
we conclude that

I_{2}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)dyd\eta\int_{D\times D^{c}}(u_{0}(x)-g(z))v_{0}(x)\nu(x,z)dxdz,

as
\varepsilon
goes to
0.

Substituting the representation of
u_{1}
in (
8
) into the equation (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-a_{1}(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u_{0}-a_{2}\mathcal{F}u_{0}(x)=f(x),\qquad x\in D,\\
u_{0}|_{D^{c}}=g(x).\end{cases}

(9)

Here

\displaystyle a_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)dydn,\qquad a_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Theta(y,\eta)\mathcal{D}_{y}^{*}\chi dydn,

\displaystyle\zeta(u_{0})(x)=\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{D}(D^{*}u_{0})(x,z)dz,\quad\mathcal{F}u_{0}(x)=\mathcal{D}|_{D}\zeta(u_{0})(x)=\int_{D}\left[\zeta(u_{0})(x)+\zeta(u_{0})(z)\right]\gamma(x,z)dz.

Step 2:
In this step, we use the letter

C
for a constant independent of
\varepsilon
. We will prove the convergence rate to be
\frac{1}{2}
, in the Sobolev space
\mathcal{V}^{D}.

Setting

Z_{\epsilon}(x)=u_{\epsilon}(x)-(u_{0}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}u_{1})(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon}),

we have

\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}Z_{\epsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\mathcal{D}(\Theta\mathcal{D}_{x}^{*}u_{1})^{\epsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}F_{\epsilon}(x)\quad\text{in}\;D,\\
Z_{\epsilon}|_{D^{c}}=-\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}u_{1}(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon}):=\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}K_{\epsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}K(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon})\quad\text{on}\;D^{c}.\end{cases}

(10)

We can easily check that
F_{\epsilon}\in H^{-\alpha/2}(D).

Let us now look at the function
K_{\epsilon}.
We prove the following estimate:

||K_{\epsilon}||_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha/2}.

For a large enough constant
N,
we set
M^{c}=\{x,|x|>N\}.
Introduce the function
m_{\epsilon}
defined as follows:

\begin{cases}m_{\epsilon}=1\quad\text{if}\quad dist(x,\partial D)\leq\varepsilon,x\in D\;\text{or}\;x\in D^{c}/M^{c}\\
m_{\epsilon}=0\quad\text{if}\quad dist(x,\partial D)\geq 2\varepsilon,x\in D\;\text{or}\;x\in M^{c}\\
||\nabla m_{\epsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}C,\end{cases}

Moreover, we set
m^{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\partial M),
and the derivative of the function
m^{\epsilon}
at
\{x\in D:dist(x,\partial D)=2\varepsilon\}
is
0.

Set
\psi_{\epsilon}=m_{\epsilon}K_{\epsilon}.
The support of
\psi_{\epsilon}
in the domain
D
is a neighbourhood of thickness
2\varepsilon
which we denote by
U^{\epsilon}.

First of all, we prove the estimates
\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha/2}.

Clearly, from the definition of
m_{\epsilon}
and the regularity properties of
u_{0},
we have

\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(U^{\epsilon})}\leq C.

Moreover,
we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{*}\psi_{\epsilon}&=\mathcal{D}^{*}(m_{\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})=((m_{\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})(x)-(m_{\epsilon}K_{\epsilon})(z))\gamma(x,z)\\
&=m_{\epsilon}(x)\left((\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}K)(x,z,\frac{x}{\epsilon})+\epsilon^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{y}K)^{\epsilon}|_{x=z}(z,\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{z}{\epsilon})\right)+(\mathcal{D}^{*}m_{\epsilon})(x,z)\cdot K_{\epsilon}(z).\end{split}

That is to say
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{U^{\epsilon}}(\psi_{\epsilon},\psi_{\epsilon})&\leq C\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}\psi_{\epsilon})^{2}dzdx\leq\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}K)^{2}(x,z,\frac{x}{\epsilon})dzdx\\
&+\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)\left((\mathcal{D}^{*}_{y}K|_{x=z})^{\epsilon}(z,\frac{x}{\epsilon},\frac{z}{\epsilon})\right)^{2}dzdx\\
&+\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x,z)\cdot(K_{\epsilon})^{2}(z)dxdz:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}.\end{split}

We set
K(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon})=h(x)\chi(\frac{x}{\epsilon}),
here
h(x)=\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{D}(D^{*}u_{0})(x,z)dz.

Clearly, from the regularity of properties of
u_{0}
,
\chi
, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}h)^{2}(x,z)\chi^{2}(\frac{x}{\epsilon})dzdx\leq C\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{x}h)^{2}(x,z)dzdx\leq C,

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)\left((\mathcal{D}^{*}_{y}\chi)^{\epsilon}\right)^{2}h^{2}(z)dzdx=\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\{\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap|x-z|\geq 1\}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)\frac{(\chi^{\epsilon}(x)-\chi^{\epsilon}(z))^{2}}{|x-z|^{d+\alpha}}h^{2}(z)dzdx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\{\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap|x-z|\leq 1\}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)\left((\mathcal{D}^{*}_{y}\chi)^{\epsilon}\right)^{2}h^{2}(z)dzdx\leq C\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\{\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap|x-z|\geq 1\}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)h^{2}(z)dzdx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\{\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap|x-z|\leq 1\}}(m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x)\left((\mathcal{D}^{*}_{y}\chi)^{\epsilon}\right)^{2}h^{2}(z)dzdx\leq C\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times D}(\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0})^{2}dzdx,

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}m_{\epsilon})^{2}(x,z)\cdot(K_{\epsilon})^{2}(z)dzdx=\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\{\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap|x-z|\leq 1\}}\frac{[(m^{\epsilon})^{\prime}(\xi)]^{2}(x-z)^{2}}{|x-z|^{d+\alpha}}h^{2}(z)\chi^{2}(\frac{z}{\epsilon})dzdx

\displaystyle+C\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\{\mathbb{R}^{d}\cap|x-z|\geq 1\}}\frac{1}{|x-z|^{d+\alpha}}h^{2}(z)\chi^{2}(\frac{z}{\epsilon})dzdx\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times D}(\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0})^{2}dzdx.

That is to say

\mathcal{E}_{U^{\epsilon}}(\psi_{\epsilon},\psi_{\epsilon})\leq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1-\alpha}\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times D}(\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0})^{2}dzdx.

We can use a result from
[
10
]
(Chapter
1
, Lemma
1.5
), which states that there exists positive constants
C
, independent of
\varepsilon,
such that

\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(U^{\epsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(D)}.
We can conclude

\int_{U^{\epsilon}}\left(\int_{D}(\mathcal{D}^{*}u_{0})^{2}dx\right)dz\leq C\varepsilon\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}(D)}.

Then
\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}=\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U^{\epsilon})}+\mathcal{E}_{U^{\epsilon}}(\psi_{\epsilon},\psi_{\epsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha}.

That is to say

\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha/2}.

(11)

Secondly, we will show
\left\|K_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}=\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}+C.

Observe now that
\psi_{\epsilon}=K_{\epsilon}
on
D^{c}/M^{c}
and
\psi_{\epsilon}=0
on
M^{c}.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|K_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}

\displaystyle=C\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}\times M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx+\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}\times D^{c}/M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx

\displaystyle+\int_{M^{c}\times M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx,

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}

\displaystyle=C\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}\times M^{c}}(\psi_{\epsilon}(x)-\psi_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx+\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}\times D^{c}/M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx.

Recall that
[
7
, Theorem 2.6]

\gamma_{D}(x,z)\approx\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu(\delta_{D}(z))\nu(\delta_{D}(x))\quad\text{if}\quad diam(D)\leq\delta_{D}(x),\delta_{D}(z),\\
&\nu(\delta_{D}(z))/V(\delta_{D}(x))\quad\text{if}\quad\delta_{D}(x)\leq diam(D)\leq\delta_{D}(z),\\
&\frac{\nu(r(x,z))V^{2}(r(x,z))}{V(\delta_{D}(x))V(\delta_{D}(z))}\quad\text{if}\quad\delta_{D}(x),\delta_{D}(z)\leq diam(D),\end{aligned}\right.

here
\delta_{D}(x)=dist(x,\partial D),r(x,y)=\delta_{D}(x)+|x-y|+\delta_{D}(x)
and
V(r)=Cr^{\alpha/2}.

Then, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{M^{c}\times M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx\leq C\int_{M^{c}\times M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\left(\delta_{D}(x)\right)^{-1-\alpha}\left(\delta_{D}(z)\right)^{-1-\alpha}dzdx\leq C,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}}\int_{\times M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\gamma_{D}(x,z)dzdx\\
&=C\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}\cap\{x:\delta_{D}(x)\leq diam(D)\}}\int_{M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\left(\delta_{D}(x)\right)^{-\alpha/2}\left(\delta_{D}(z)\right)^{-1-\alpha}dzdx\\
&+C\int_{D^{c}/M^{c}\cap\{x:\delta_{D}(x)\geq diam(D)\}}\int_{M^{c}}(K_{\epsilon}(x)-K_{\epsilon}(z))^{2}\left(\delta_{D}(x)\right)^{-1-\alpha}\left(\delta_{D}(z)\right)^{-1-\alpha}dzdx\\
&\leq C.\end{split}

Then, we get the conclusion

\left\|K_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}=\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}+C.

(12)

Next, we will show
\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq max\{C,1\}\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}+C.

In fact,

\begin{split}\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}&=\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(D)}+C\int_{D\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}\psi_{\epsilon})^{2}dxdz=\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U^{\epsilon})}+C\int_{U^{\epsilon}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{D}^{*}\psi_{\epsilon})^{2}dxdz\\
&+C\int_{D/U^{\epsilon}}\int_{\left(D/U^{\epsilon}\right)^{c}}(m^{\epsilon}(x))^{2}K^{2}(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon})\nu(x,z)dzdx\\
&\leq max\{C,1\}\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}+C\int_{D/U^{\epsilon}}\int_{\left(D/U^{\epsilon}\right)^{c}}(m^{\epsilon}(x))^{2}K^{2}(x,\frac{x}{\epsilon})\nu(x,z)dzdx.\end{split}

From the fact that the derivative of the function
m^{\epsilon}
at
\{x\in D:dist(x,\partial D)=2\varepsilon\}
is
0,

we have

\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}\leq max\{C,1\}\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}+C.

(13)

Finally, we will get the convergence rate.
Combining (
11
), (
12
), (
13
) and
[
7
, Corollary 5.1]
we conclude

\begin{split}\left\|K_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}&=\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}+C\leq C\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}+C\leq C\left\|\psi_{\epsilon}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}^{U^{\epsilon}}}+C\leq C\varepsilon^{-\alpha}.\end{split}

We thus estimate from Lemma
3

\begin{split}\left\|Z_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{V}^{D}}&\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\left\|F_{\epsilon}\right\|_{H^{-\alpha/2}(D)}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\left\|K_{\epsilon}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}^{D}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}c_{11}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

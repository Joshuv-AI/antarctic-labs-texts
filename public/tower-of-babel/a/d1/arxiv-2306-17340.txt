Recent results from Belle and Belle II
Francesco Tenchini 1,2 for the Belle and Belle II collaborations

Abstract
B-factories provide a unique environment to test the standard model in search for new physics. The Belle and Belle II experiments succeeded each other at KEK, in Tsukuba, Japan. During its lifetime, Belle collected 1 ab − 1 \mbox{\,ab}^{-1} of data; at the time of this contribution, Belle II recorded 424 fb − 1 \mbox{\,fb}^{-1} more aiming for an unprecedented sample of 50 ab − 1 \mbox{\,ab}^{-1} . This unique data set will allow them to search for new physics with unmatched precision. We discuss select recent results from both experiments.

1
Introduction

B-factories
Bevan:2014iga
are asymmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collider experiments designed to operate at or near the
\mathchar 28935\relax{(4S)}
resonance energy of 10.58
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
to produce large amounts of B mesons, D mesons and
\tau
lepton. This allows to measure the fundamental parameters of the standard model with high precision, as well as to search for deviations from its predictions which could provide a clue of new physics.

The Belle experiment was a general-purpose magnetic spectrometer located at the interaction region of one of such B-factories: the KEKB accelerator at KEK, in Tsukuba, Japan. Belle was composed of a silicon vertex detector, a 50-layer central drift chamber, an array of aerogel Cherenkov counters, a time-of-flight scintillation counter and an electromagnetic calorimeter. These detectors were located within a 1.5 T magnetic field provided by a superconducting solenoid magnet. Resistive plate chambers located outside the magnet detected
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}
mesons and muons. Belle is described in detail in
Belle:2000cnh
. During its operation from 1999 to 2010 it recorded over 1
\mbox{\,ab}^{-1}
of data, of which 711
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
at the
\mathchar 28935\relax(4S)
resonance corresponding to approximately 772 million
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs.

Its successor Belle II is located at the same facility and paired to the SuperKEKB collider which, following the success of KEKB, was designed to reach an unprecedented instantaneous luminosity of
6\times 10^{35}
cm
-2
s
-1
through the use of a novel nano-beam collision scheme
Abe:2010gxa
. The new and harsher environment in which Belle II operates demands substantial upgrades of all its subdetectors. In particular, a new tracking system was devised, composed of a two-layer silicon-pixel detector, a four-layer double-sided silicon-strip detector and a larger drift chamber, in order to provide more accurate decay vertex reconstruction closer to the interaction point.

Over its lifetime Belle II aims to record 50
\mbox{\,ab}^{-1}
of data, enabling an extensive physics program
Belle-II:2018jsg
. At the time of this contribution Belle II recorded approximately 424
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of collision data before entering a technical shutdown period; 190
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of which were available for analysis. Although smaller than Belle’s dataset, this sample can already provide significant physics input in measurements where detector performance is the dominant source of uncertainty.

In the following sections I will discuss selected recent results from both experiments.

2
Lifetimes of charmed particles

Accurate prediction of the lifetime of charmed particles is challenging, as it relies on effective models such as heavy quark expansion (HQE) to calculate strong interaction contributions at low energy. Precise measurements of these lifetimes thus provide an excellent test of these models but conversely require excellent vertex reconstruction capabilities.

The first layer of the Belle II tracking system is located only 1.4 cm from the interaction point and boasts a decay-time resolution two times better than Belle, enabling high precision absolute lifetime measurements.

Belle II reported measurements of the
D^{0}
and
D^{+}
lifetimes with an integrated luminosity of 72
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}

Belle-II:2021cxx
, and more recently of the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
lifetime with an integrated luminosity of 207.2
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}

Belle-II:2022ggx
. These measurements were performed respectively in the
D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{0}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+})\pi^{+}
,
D^{*+}\rightarrow D^{+}(\rightarrow K^{-}\pi^{+}\pi^{+})\pi^{0}
, and
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}\rightarrow pK^{-}\pi^{+}
decay channels and follow a common strategy. The
D^{*+}
and
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
candidates are reconstructed from charged tracks identified as protons, pions and kaons, and from neutral pions reconstructed from two photons. A global decay-chain vertex fit
Belle-IIanalysissoftwareGroup:2019dlq
constrains each decay channel to their respective topology and the
D^{*+}
and
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
to originate from the interaction point.
Lifetimes are extracted with a maximum likelihood fit to the unbinned distributions of the lifetime and its uncertainty (
t
,
\sigma_{t}
) (Fig.
1
). The background contribution in the
D^{+}
and
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
decay-time fits is modeled from sidebands, while in the
D^{0}
fit the background is not modeled and assigned a systematic uncertainty. An additional uncertainty is assigned to the
\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
measurement due to contamination from
\mathchar 28932\relax^{0}_{c}\rightarrow\pi^{-}\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
and
\mathchar 28932\relax^{+}_{c}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\mathchar 28931\relax_{c}^{+}
decays. The resulting lifetimes are

Hadronic B decays

3.1
Combined measurement of
\phi_{3}/\gamma

The CKM angle
\phi_{3}/\gamma
can be measured in a theoretically clean way from the interference of
b\rightarrow c\bar{u}s
(
A_{fav}
) and
b\rightarrow u\bar{c}s
(
A_{sup}
) amplitudes, the latter of which is suppressed with respect to the former. Assuming the absence of new physics at tree level, this measurement of
\phi_{3}
can provide a standard model benchmark for other, indirect measurements which proceed at loop level.
The two phases are related by

\frac{A_{sup}(B^{-}\rightarrow\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0}K^{-})}{A_{fav}(B^{-}\rightarrow D^{0}K^{-})}=r_{B}e^{i(\delta_{B}-\phi_{3})}

(1)

where
\delta_{B}
is the
D^{0}{\kern-1.60004pt\kern 1.99997pt\overline{\kern-1.99997ptD}{}^{0}}
strong phase. The most precise determination of
\phi_{3}/\gamma
stems from a combination of LHCb measurements
Kenzie:2018oob
and has an uncertainty of
\sim 5^{\circ}
.
At B-factories, the most sensitive approach is based on the BPGGSZ method
phi3_method
, an optimally-binned (see Fig.
2
) Dalitz plot analysis of
B^{\pm}\rightarrow DK^{\pm}
and
B^{\pm}\rightarrow D\pi^{\pm}
decays with
D
decaying into
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}K^{+}K^{-}
final states, where
\delta_{B}
is constrained from external inputs.
Recently Belle and Belle II published a combined analysis
Belle:2021efh
using 711
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
and 128
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of data, respectively, which significantly improves on the previous result obtained with Belle data alone.
The dominant background from
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\overline{q}
(
q=u,d,s,c
) events is characterised by different topology and suppressed by means of a multivariate classifier trained on shape variables. The yields in each Dalitz bin are then extracted with a simultaneously for
B^{\pm}\rightarrow DK^{\pm}
and
B^{\pm}\rightarrow D\pi^{\pm}
with a 2D fit in the multivariate output and in the beam-energy difference
\Delta E=\sum_{j}E^{*}_{j}-E^{*}_{beam}
, where the sum is over every signal B decay product. The result is

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(78.4\pm 11.4\pm 0.5\pm 1.0)^{\circ}

\displaystyle r_{B}^{DK}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.129\pm 0.024\pm 0.001\pm 0.002

\displaystyle\delta_{B}^{DK}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(124.8\pm 12.9\pm 0.5\pm 1.7)^{\circ}

where the first uncertainty is statistical, the second is systematic, and the third originates from external input on the strong phase. The uncertainty on
\phi_{3}
improved from the

4^{\circ}

3.2
Charmless decays at Belle II

Charmless hadronic two-body decays
B\rightarrow K\pi
provide an opportunity to test the standard model by studying direct CP violation which is sensitive to new physics contributions. In the standard model the isospin-sum rule
Gronau:2005kz

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{CP}(K^{+}\pi^{-})+\mathcal{A}_{CP}(K^{0}\pi^{+})\frac{\mathcal{B}(K^{0}\pi^{+})}{\mathcal{B}(K^{+}\pi^{-})}\frac{\tau_{B^{0}}}{\tau_{B^{+}}}

(2)

\displaystyle-2\mathcal{A}_{CP}(K^{+}\pi^{0})\frac{\mathcal{B}(K^{+}\pi^{0})}{\mathcal{B}(K^{+}\pi^{-})}\frac{\tau_{B^{0}}}{\tau_{B^{+}}}-2\mathcal{A}_{CP}(K^{0}\pi^{0})\frac{\mathcal{B}(K^{0}\pi^{0})}{\mathcal{B}(K^{+}\pi^{-})}=0,

where
{A}_{CP}
are direct CP asymmetries, holds true within 1%. The precision of this null test is dominated by experimental uncertainties on the
B\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}
decay mode, which can be accurately studied only at B-factories.
Belle II reports a measurement of this process using 190
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of data
Belle-II:2022jij
.
B meson candidates are built using
K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}
and
\pi^{0}
candidates; the flavour of the other B is determined from the remaining particles in the event using a dedicated multivariate algorithm
Belle-II:2021zvj
. The branching fractions and
\mathcal{A}_{CP}
are then extracted by an extended maximum-likelihood fit of the distributions of
\Delta E
,
\Delta t
, a modified version of the beam-constrained mass
M_{bc}=\sqrt{{E^{*}_{beam}}^{2}-\vec{p}^{2}_{B}}
, and a continuum-suppressing classifier.
The results are

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 11.0\pm 1.2(\mathrm{stat})\pm 1.0(\mathrm{syst})]\times 10^{-6}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{CP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-0.41^{+0.30}_{-0.32}(\mathrm{stat})\pm 0.09(\mathrm{syst})

in agreement with the current world average.

The radiative version of this decay,
B\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle S}\pi^{0}\gamma
, is also interesting as
b\rightarrow s\gamma
transitions only occur at loop level in the standard model and with flavour-specific polarization. The photon is right-handed in
B^{0}
decays and left-handed in
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
decays. Therefore, we don’t expect to observe any time-dependent asymmetry. However, new physics occurring at tree level with different chirality rules could alter this picture. Belle II measured the branching fraction of this decay in preparation for a time-dependent analysis. The result,
\mathcal{B}=(7.3\pm 1.8(\mathrm{stat})\pm 1.0(\mathrm{syst}))\times 10^{-6}
, is once again in agreement with the world average value.

4
Semileptonic B decays

4.1
Status of
|V_{ub}|
and
|V_{cb}|

The CKM matrix elements
|V_{ub}|
and
|V_{cb}|
are measured through the study of tree-level semileptonic B decays, where the standard model contribution is assumed to dominate. Such decays can be analyzed either exclusively or inclusively; in the former case a specific decay mode is reconstructed, e.g.
B\rightarrow\pi\ell\nu
, while in the latter every final state is reconstructed irrespectively of the hadronic composition:
B\rightarrow X_{u}\ell\nu
. The two methods, which should be in agreeement, are instead subject to a long-standing
3\sigma
discrepancy
HFLAV:2019otj
. B-factories are uniquely suited to probe this puzzle by tackling the measurement in both approaches.

4.2
Partial branching fractions of
B\rightarrow X_{u}\ell\nu_{\ell}
at Belle

Inclusive measurements of
B\rightarrow X_{u}\ell\nu_{\ell}
are extremely challenging due to the large background from favored
B\rightarrow X_{c}\ell\nu_{\ell}
decays, unless the measurement is performed in regions where the latter are kinematically forbidden. However, doing so introduces large theory uncertainties on the decay rate from to nonperturbative shape functions.
In absence of as-of-yet-unrealized modeling improvements or model-independent approaches, it is best to extend the measurement into the
B\rightarrow X_{c}\ell\nu_{\ell}
dominated region. Background suppression is therefore of critical importance.

To perform this measurement, Belle reconstructs the second B meson in a variety of hadronically decaying modes using a multivariate algorithm
Feindt:2011mr
.
The modeling of
B\rightarrow X_{c}\ell\nu_{\ell}
is then verified in charm-enriched sidebands; a background-suppressing classifier is trained using several discriminating observables, such as the missing squared mass or the number of observed kaons in the decay. The partial branching fraction is extracted from a template fit to the distributions of remaining events, obtaining
Belle:2021eni

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{B}=[1.59\pm 0.07(\mathrm{stat})\pm 0.16(\mathrm{syst})]\times 10^{-3}.

This branching fraction probes about 86% of the phase space available to the decay. It is then converted to
|V_{ub}|=(4.10\pm 0.09\pm 0.22\pm 0.15)\times 10^{-3}
, where the last uncertainty denotes the theory error, using the average of several theory predictions. This result is consistent with previous inclusive determinations
BaBar:2016rxh
and deviates from exclusive results by
1.3\sigma
.

4.3
B\rightarrow\pi e\nu
at Belle II

Tagged measurements at Belle II are limited by the available sample size; nevertheless, they are important to set the framework for future analyses. Belle II produced a first measurement of the exclusive branching fractions of
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{-}e^{+}\nu_{e}
and
B^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{0}e^{+}\nu_{e}

on 190
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of data using a hadronic tagging approach similar to the previous section, but using a novel Full Event Interpretation algorithm
Keck:2018lcd
recently developed for Belle II. The results are
Belle-II:2022pir

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{-}e^{+}\nu_{e})=(1.43\pm 0.27(\mathrm{stat})\pm 0.07(\mathrm{syst}))\times 10^{-4}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{0}e^{+}\nu_{e})=(8.33\pm 1.67(\mathrm{stat})\pm 0.55(\mathrm{syst}))\times 10^{-5}

\displaystyle|V_{ub}|=(3.88\pm 0.45)\times 10^{-3}

the latter of which is extracted with a combined fit using input from lattice QCD. The result agrees with pre-existing exclusive determinations.

4.4
|V_{cb}|
with
q^{2}
moments of
B\rightarrow X_{c}\ell\nu

The inclusive determination of
|V_{cb}|
proceeds through the measurement of the differential decay rate of
B\rightarrow X_{c}\ell\nu
, which using HQE can be written as a power expansion of the inverse b-quark mass,
1/m_{b}
. The coefficients of this expansion can be directly measured on experimental data, typically from moments of the lepton energy and invariant hadronic mass, greatly reducing uncertainty from theory input. However, at higher orders of
1/m_{b}
the number of these coefficients increases rapidly making the inclusion of higher power corrections increasingly difficult.

Following a recent idea
Fael:2018vsp
, both Belle
Belle:2021idw
and Belle II
Belle-II:2022fug
measured the moments of the dilepton moment (
q^{2}
) spectrum for the first time. These quantities depend on a reduced set of parameters, making a fully data driven extraction of
|V_{cb}|
possible up to
1/m_{b}^{4}
. The results from the two experiments were subsequently used in a global fit
Bernlochner:2022ucr
to extract
|V_{cb}|=(41.69\pm 0.59\pm 0.23)\times 10^{-3}
, where the first uncertainty comes from the fit and the second from higher order contributions to the power expansions. This is in good agreement with the measurement performed through the traditional approach
Bordone:2021oof
and can be further improved in the future, placing extremely strong constraints on this element of the CKM matrix.

5
Tests of lepton flavour

5.1
\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
at Belle

Electroweak coupling of gauge bosons in the standard model is assumed identical for every lepton generation; this is known as lepton flavour universality (LFU). The recent emergence of anomalies such as in
R_{K}=\frac{\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow K\mu^{+}\mu^{-})}{\mathcal{B}(B\rightarrow Ke^{+}e^{-})}

LHCb:2021trn
suggest this might not be the case, which would be a clear sign of new physics.

Semileptonic decays of charmed baryons play an important role in the study of weak and strong interactions but are relatively challenging due to either low production rates or complicated backgrounds.
The decay
\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\ell^{+}\nu_{\ell}
in particular had never been tested for LFU. Belle performed the measurement
Belle:2021dgc
using a combined sample of 89.5
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, 711
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
and 121.1
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, taken at 10.52, 10.58 and 10.86
\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
center-of-mass energy, respectively. The semileptonic yields are measured relatively to their hadronic counterpart,
\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\pi^{+}
by means of a binned maximum likelihood fit. They are then corrected for their relative effieciencies, resulting in the branching-fraction ratios

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}e^{+}\nu_{e})/\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\pi^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.98\pm 0.13\pm 0.08

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu})/\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\pi^{+})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.94\pm 0.18\pm 0.10

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}e^{+}\nu_{e})/\mathcal{B}(\mathchar 28938\relax_{c}^{0}\rightarrow\mathchar 28938\relax^{-}\mu^{+}\nu_{\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.02\pm 0.10\pm 0.02.

where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second systematic.
The first result greatly improves on the previous maesurement
CLEO:2002imi
; the second has never been measured before; and the third is also new and in good agreement with standard-model LFU predictions.

5.2
\tau^{\pm}\rightarrow\ell^{\pm}\gamma
at Belle

A related underlying assumption of the standard model is that the flavour of charged leptons is itself conserved, with immeasurably small violations becoming possible at loop level through neutrino oscillation. The same is not true in many new physics models, which envision scenarios in which lepton flavour is violated. Models predicting flavour violation between the first and the second lepton family are tightly constrained by experimental evidence, e.g.
MEG:2016leq
; suggesting that, if indeed flavour is violated, this should occur in the third generation.

B-factories have been historically at the forefront of
\tau
flavour violation searches, with Belle and BaBar providing most of the constraints such decays. Most recently, Belle updated their measurement of the
\tau\rightarrow\ell\gamma
decay
Belle:2021ysv
which was previously performed on a 535
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
data sample. An improved selection provides better suppression of the dominant background stemming from the coincidence of
\tau\rightarrow\ell\nu\overline{\nu}
decays with spurious photons. Belle places an upper limit to this branching fraction, at the 90% confidence level, of
\mathcal{B}(\tau^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}\gamma)<5.6\times 10^{-8}
and
\mathcal{B}(\tau^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{-}\gamma)<4.2\times 10^{-8}
. The limit on the muon channel is the most stringent to date, superseding the previous value from BaBar
BaBar:2009hkt
.

5.3
B^{0}\rightarrow\tau^{\pm}\ell^{\mp}
at Belle

Flavour could also be violated in tauonic B decays through loops involving NP particles such as leptoquarks
Smirnov:2018ske

This process is kinematically similar to
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\pi^{+}
, which constitutes the main experimental background.
Rather than explicitly reconstructing the
\tau
decays, which can prove difficult due to the presence of neutrinos, Belle instead reconstructs
Belle:btotauell
the other B (tag) and the light charged particle (a lepton in the signal case, a pion for the background). Energy conservation is then used to calculate the missing mass of the system, resulting in characteristic peaks at the mass of the D, D* and tau particles, with the addition of an exponential-like combinatorial background.

Signal yields are extracted with an unbinned extended maximum likelihood fit, and no signal evidence is found, resulting in an upper limit on the branching fraction, at 90% confidence level, of

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow\tau^{\pm}e^{\mp})<1.5\times 10^{-5}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(B^{0}\rightarrow\tau^{\pm}\mu^{\mp})<1.6\times 10^{-5}

The bound on the electron channel is the most stringent yet, while the one on the muon channel is similar to the pre-existing limit from LHCb
LHCb:2019ujz
.

6
Conclusions

B factories provide a unique environment for precision measurements. The Belle experiment, although long past its operation date, is still producing abundant physics output thanks to its large data sample. Meanwhile Belle II has entered active operation and collected so far 424
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
of data, or approximately half that of Belle; which has already been used to provide high impact measurements. Ultimately, Belle II aims to collect 50 times the Belle data set, allowing it to search for new physics with unprecedented precision.

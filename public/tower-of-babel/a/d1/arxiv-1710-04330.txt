Entropy on modules over the group ring of a sofic group
Bingbing Liang

Abstract
. We partially generalize Peters’ formula [ 14 ] on modules over the group ring 𝔽 ​ Γ {\mathbb{F}}\Gamma for a given finite field 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} and a sofic group Γ \Gamma . It is also discussed that how the values of entropy are related to the zero divisor conjecture.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 37B99, 16D10, 13C12.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group. An
algebraic action
of
\Gamma
is the induced (continuous) action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
from some (left)
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
. Here
\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}}
is the Pontryagin dual of the discrete abelian group
{\mathcal{M}}
. One of main concerns is about the interplay between the dynamical information of
\Gamma\curvearrowright\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
and the algebraic information of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Weiss considered the case that
{\mathcal{M}}
is a torsion abelian group
[
18
]
. For general
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
, Peters showed that the topological entropy of
\Gamma\curvearrowright\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
coincides with the algebraic entropy of
{\mathcal{M}}
when
\Gamma
is amenable
[
14
]
. This general result does not only establish the beautiful connection but also provides the great flexibility to study the entropy of algebraic actions
[
5
,
13
]
.

Towards more general group actions, Bowen and Kerr-Li developed an entropy theory when
\Gamma
can be approximated by finite groups
[
2
,
10
]
. The groups admitting such approximations are the so-called
sofic groups
, which in particular includes amenable groups and residually groups
[
8
,
19
]
.

On the other hand, given a unital ring
R
and a sofic group
\Gamma
, in
[
12
, Definition 3.1]
, started with a length function
{\rm L}
on left
R
-modules (
[
12
, Definition 2.1]
), Li and the author introduced a mean length function on
R\Gamma
-modules
{\mathcal{M}}
which are
locally
L
-finite
in the sense that each finitely generated
R
-submodule of
{\mathcal{M}}
has finite
{\rm L}
-length. In particular, for
R={\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\rm L}(\cdot)=\log|\cdot|
, the corresponding mean length function can be treated as an algebraic version of entropy, which we may call
sofic algebraic entropy
. Then a natural question to ask is whether one can generalize Peters’ formula to the setting of sofic group actions.

In this paper, we give a partial generalization of Peters’ formula to the case of sofic group actions. The main inputs of the proof are an approximation formula of topological entropy given in
[
7
, Lemma 4.8]
and some techniques appeared in
[
12
]
. Given a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
any
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module can be treated as a
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module. Fix a sofic approximation
\Sigma
for
\Gamma
and a free ultrafilter
\omega
over
{\mathbb{N}}
. For any left
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-modules
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, we have the algebraic entropy of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
relative to
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, denoted by
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
(Definition
3.1
). For any
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
, the sofic algebraic entropy
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}})
of
{\mathcal{M}}
is then defined as
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}|{\mathcal{M}})
. We remark that the main motivation to introduce this relative version of invariants and technical consideration of ultrafilters is that one can establish a modified addition formula under this approach
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Correspondingly, we have the relative version of topological entropy in dynamical systems. Let
\Gamma
act on compact metrizable spaces
X
and
Y
continuously. For any factor map from
X
to
Y
, we have the topological entropy
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(Y|X)
of
\Gamma\curvearrowright Y
relative to
\Gamma\curvearrowright X

[
12
, Definition 9.3]
. When
X=Y
and the factor map is the identity map, the relative topological entropy coincide with the original definition of sofic topological entropy, which we denote by
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(X)
. Similar approaches also independently appears in the works of other experts. Hayes gave a formula for the relative version of sofic measure entropy in terms of a given compact model in
[
9
]
. A similar notion for Rokhlin entropy, called outer Rokhlin entropy, was developed by Seward in
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a sofic group and
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field. Then for any finitely generated
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
, we have

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}).

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be countable
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-modules. Then
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{1}|\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{2})\leq h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2}).

This paper is organized as follows. We recall some background knowledge in Section 2. The algebraic entropy is introduced in Section 3 and some basic properties are discussed there. In particular, we show that the values of algebraic entropies is related to the zero divisor conjecture. We prove the main result in Section 4.

Throughout this paper,
\Gamma
will be a countable discrete group. For any set
S
, we denote by
{\mathcal{F}}(S)
the set of all nonempty finite subsets of
S
. All modules are assumed to be left modules unless specified. For any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we write
[d]
for the set
\{1,\cdots,d\}
and
{\rm Sym}(d)
for the permutation group of
[d]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Group rings and algebraic actions

Let
R
be a unital ring. The
group ring of
\Gamma
with coefficients in
R
, denoted by
R\Gamma
, consists of all finitely supported functions:
f:\Gamma\to R
. Conventionally, we shall write
f
as
\sum_{s\in\Gamma}f_{s}s
, where
f_{s}\in R
for all
s\in\Gamma
and
f_{s}=0
for all except finitely many
s\in\Gamma
.
The algebraic operations on
R\Gamma
are defined by

\sum_{s\in\Gamma}f_{s}s+\sum_{s\in\Gamma}g_{s}s=\sum_{s\in\Gamma}(f_{s}+g_{s})s,\mbox{ and }\big(\sum_{s\in\Gamma}f_{s}s\big)\big(\sum_{t\in\Gamma}g_{t}t\big)=\sum_{s,t\in\Gamma}f_{s}g_{t}(st).

We similarly have the product if one of
f
and
g
sits in
R^{\Gamma}
.

For any countable
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
, treated as a discrete abelian group, its Pontryagin dual
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
consisting of all continuous group homomorphisms
{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, coincides with
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}({\mathcal{M}},{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
. By Pontryagin duality,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is a compact metrizable space under compact-open topology. Furthermore, the
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module structure of
{\mathcal{M}}
naturally induces an adjoint action
\Gamma\curvearrowright\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
by continuous automorphisms. To be precise,

\langle s\chi,u\rangle:=\langle\chi,s^{-1}x\rangle

for all
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}},u\in{\mathcal{M}}
, and
s\in\Gamma
.
Conversely, by Pontryagin duality, each action of
\Gamma
on a compact metrizable abelian group arise this way and thus we call such a dynamical system an
algebraic action

[
15
]
.

2.2.
Amenable and sofic groups

The group
\Gamma
is called
amenable
if for any
K\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)
and any
\delta>0
there is an
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)
with
|KF\setminus F|<\delta|F|
.

A sequence of maps
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{i}:\Gamma\to{\rm{\rm Sym}}(d_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is called a
sofic approximation
for
\Gamma
if it satisfies:

(1)

\lim_{i\to\infty}|\{v\in[d_{i}]:\sigma_{i,s}\sigma_{i,t}(v)=\sigma_{i,st}(v)\}|/d_{i}=1
for all
s,t\in\Gamma
,

(2)

\lim_{i\to\infty}|\{v\in[d_{i}]:\sigma_{i,s}(v)\neq\sigma_{i,t}(v)\}|/d_{i}=1
for all distinct
s,t\in\Gamma
,

(3)

\lim_{i\to\infty}d_{i}=+\infty
.

The group
\Gamma
is called a
sofic group
if it admits a sofic approximation.

Any amenable group is sofic since one can use a sequence of asymptotically-invariant subsets of the amenable group, i.e.
Følner sequence
, to construct a sofic approximation. Residually finite groups are also sofic since a sequence of exhausting finite-index subgroups naturally induces a sofic approximation in which each approximating map is actually a group homomorphism. We refer the reader to
[
3
,
4
]
for more information on sofic groups.

Throughout the rest of this paper,
\Gamma
will be a countable sofic group,
\Sigma
will be a sofic approximation for
\Gamma
, and
\omega
will be a free ultrafilter over
{\mathbb{N}}
.

2.3.
Sofic topological entropy

For any continuous pseudometric
\rho_{X}
on a compact space
X
and any
\varepsilon>0
, a subset
Z
of
X
is called
(
\rho_{X},\varepsilon
)-separated
if
\rho_{X}(z_{1},z_{2})>\varepsilon
for all distinct
z_{1},z_{2}\in Z
. Set
N_{\varepsilon}(X,\rho_{X}):=\max_{Z}|Z|
for
Z
ranging over all
(\rho_{X},\varepsilon)
-separated subsets of
X
.

Let
\Gamma
act continuously on a compact metrizable space
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\rho_{X}
be a continuous pseudometric on
X
. For any
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define continuous pseudometrics
\rho_{X,2}
and
\rho_{X,\infty}
on
X^{d}
by

\rho_{X,2}(\varphi,\psi)=\left(\frac{1}{d}\sum_{v\in[d]}\rho_{X}(\varphi_{v},\psi_{v})^{2}\right)^{1/2},{\rm and\ }\rho_{X,\infty}(\varphi,\psi)=\max_{v\in[d]}\rho_{X}(\varphi_{v},\psi_{v}).

Let
\sigma
be a map from
\Gamma
to
{\rm Sym}(d)
,
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)
, and
\delta>0
. The set of approximately equivariant maps
{\rm Map}(\rho,F,\delta,\sigma)
is defined to be the set of all maps
\varphi:[d]\to X
such that
\rho_{X,2}(s\varphi,\varphi\circ\sigma(s))\leq\delta
.

A continuous pseudometric
\rho_{X}
on
X
is called
dynamically generating
if

\rho_{X}
can distinguish all distinct elements of
X
under the action of
\Gamma
, i.e. for all distinct
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, there exists some
s\in\Gamma
such that
\rho_{X}(sx,sx^{\prime})>0
.

Now let
\Gamma
act on another compact metrizable space
Y
and
\pi:X\to Y
be a factor map. Denote by
{\rm Map}(\pi,\rho,F,\delta,\sigma)
the set of all
\pi\circ\varphi
for
\varphi
ranging in
{\rm Map}(\rho,F,\delta,\sigma)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\rho_{X}
and
\rho_{Y}
be two dynamically generating continuous pseudometrics of
X
and
Y
respectively. Let
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)
and
\delta,\varepsilon>0
. Define

h^{\varepsilon}_{\Sigma,\omega,\infty}(\rho_{Y},F,\delta|\rho_{X})=\lim_{i\to\omega}\frac{1}{d_{i}}\log N_{\varepsilon}({\rm Map}(\pi,\rho_{X},F,\delta,\sigma_{i}),\rho_{Y,\infty}).

If the set of
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
{\rm Map}(\rho_{X},F,\delta,\sigma_{i})=\emptyset
is in
\omega
, we set
h^{\varepsilon}_{\Sigma,\omega,\infty}(\rho_{Y},F,\delta|\rho_{X})=-\infty
. We define the
sofic topological entropy of
\Gamma\curvearrowright Y
relative to
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
as

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(Y|X):=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\inf_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)}\inf_{\delta>0}h^{\varepsilon}_{\Sigma,\omega,\infty}(\rho_{Y},F,\delta|\rho_{X}).

The
sofic topological entropy of
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is defined as

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(X):=h_{\Sigma,\omega}(X|X)

for
\pi:X\to X
being the identity map.

Remark 2.3
.

It was shown that the definition of the relative sofic topological entropy does not depend on the choices of the dynamically generating continuous pseudometrics
\rho_{X}
and
\rho_{Y}

[
12
, Lemma 8.2, Lemma 9.5]
. One has
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(Y|X)\leq\min(h_{\Sigma,\omega}(X),h_{\Sigma,\omega}(Y))
.

3.
Algebraic entropy

When we specialize the length function as
\log|\cdot|
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
-mdoules, the corresponding sofic mean length function in
[
12
, Definition 3.1]
brings us the notion for the algebraic analogue of sofic topological entropy. For reader’s convenience, we recall the complete definition.

For any (abelian) group
G
, denote by
{\mathscr{F}}(G)
the collection of finitely generated subgroups of
G
. Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module and
{\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}}\in{\mathscr{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
. For
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)
and a map
\sigma:\Gamma\to{\rm Sym}(d)
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote by
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{B}},F,\sigma)
the abelian subgroup of
{\mathcal{M}}^{d}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{M}}
generated by the elements
\delta_{v}\otimes b-\delta_{\sigma(s)v}\otimes sb
for all
v\in[d],b\in{\mathscr{B}}
, and
s\in F
, where
\delta_{v}
denotes the element of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
taking value 1 at the coordinate
v
and
0
everywhere else. Put
{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}},F,\sigma)
as the image of
{\mathscr{A}}^{d}
in
{\mathcal{M}}^{d}/{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{B}},F,\sigma)
under the quotient map
{\mathcal{M}}^{d}\to{\mathcal{M}}^{d}/{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{B}},F,\sigma)
. We remark that any
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
is locally
\log|\cdot|
-finite if and only if
{\mathcal{M}}
is torsion as an abelian group.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-modules such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is torsion. For any
{\mathscr{A}}\in{\mathscr{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}),{\mathscr{B}}\in{\mathscr{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})
and
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)
, set

h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathscr{A}}|{\mathscr{B}},F):=\lim_{i\to\omega}\frac{\log|{\mathscr{M}}({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}},F,\sigma_{i})|}{d_{i}}.

We define the
algebraic entropy of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
relative to
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
as

h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2}):=\sup_{{\mathscr{A}}\in{\mathscr{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})}\inf_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)}\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}\in{\mathscr{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})}h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathscr{A}}|{\mathscr{B}},F).

The
sofic algebraic entropy
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is then defined as

h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}):=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{1}).

Apply
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
to the length function
\log|\cdot|
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules, we have the modified addition formula for sofic algebraic entropy.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-modules such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
is torsion as an abelian group. Then

h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})+h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2}/{\mathcal{M}}_{1}).

By the similar argument as in
[
12
, Section 3]
, we have the following proposition, which collects basic properties of the sofic algebraic entropy.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-modules such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
is torsion as an abelian groups. Then the following are true.

(1)

If
{\mathscr{N}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathscr{N}}_{2}
are two torsion abelian groups, then
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathscr{N}}_{1}|{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}\otimes_{\mathbb{N}}{\mathscr{N}}_{2})=\log|{\mathscr{N}}_{1}|
;

(2)

If
\{{\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}}
is an increasing net of
{\mathbb{Z}}\Gamma
-submodules of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
, then
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})\nearrow h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\bigcup_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
. Morerover, if
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})<\infty
, then
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2}/{\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime})\searrow h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2}/\bigcup_{j\in{\mathcal{J}}}{\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime})
;

(3)

If
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-modules, and
\{{\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is an increasing sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-submodules of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
containing
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
with union
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, then
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{j}^{\prime})\searrow h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
.

Using the similar argument in the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 3.5 (2)]
, analogues to
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R
be a finite abelian group. Then for any
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq(R\Gamma)^{k}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
{\mathcal{M}}=0
if and only if
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}|(R\Gamma)^{k})=0
. In fact, for any
f\in({\mathbb{Z}\Gamma})^{k}
,

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma f|(R\Gamma)^{k})\geq\frac{\log|\{rf:r\in R\}|}{(2|{\rm supp}(f)|+1)^{2}}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
R
be a finite abelian group. Assume for any nonzero
f
of
R\Gamma
, we have
h_{\Sigma}(R\Gamma/R\Gamma f)=0
. Then
R\Gamma
contains no nontrivial zero divisors.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
we have
0=h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma/R\Gamma f)=h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma)-h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma f|R\Gamma)
. Since
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma f|R\Gamma)\leq h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma f)\leq h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma)
, we have
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma f)=h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma)
.

On the other hand, let
R(f):R\Gamma\to R\Gamma
be the
R\Gamma
-module homomorphism sending
x
to
xf
. Then we have
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma f)=h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma)-h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\rm ker\ }R(f)|R\Gamma)
. It forces that
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\rm ker\ }R(f)|R\Gamma)=0
. By Lemma
3.4
,
{\rm ker\ }R(f)=0
. That means
R\Gamma
has no nontrivial zero divisor.
∎

We remark the similar approaches also appear in
[
6
, Lemma 4.4]
,
[
17
, Theorem 7.14]
, and
[
12
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Definition 3.6
.

When
\Gamma
is amenable, for any
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}
is torsion as an abelian group, the
algebraic entropy
of
{\mathcal{M}}
is defined as

h({\mathcal{M}})=\sup_{{\mathscr{A}}\in{\mathscr{F}}({\mathcal{M}})}\inf_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}(\Gamma)}\frac{\log|\sum_{s\in F}s^{-1}{\mathscr{A}}|}{|F|}.

From
[
12
, Theorem 5.1]
, we know the sofic algebraic entropy is a generalization of algebraic entropy.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be an amenable group. For any
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-modules
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
is torsion as abelian group, then
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})=h({\mathcal{M}}_{1})
.

Remark 3.8
.

Let
R
be a finite abelian group and
f\in RH\subseteq R\Gamma
be nonzero for some amenable subgroup
H
of
\Gamma
. If
RH
has no nontrivial zero divisor, we have
RH
is isomorphic to
RHf
as
RH
-modules. By
[
12
, Corollary 6.2]
and Theorem
3.7
, we have

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(R\Gamma/R\Gamma f)=h_{\Sigma|_{H},\omega}(RH/RHf)=h(RH/RHf)=h(RH)-h(RHf)=0.

By Bartholdi’s characterization of nonamenability
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
, for any field
{\mathbb{F}}
there exists an injective
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module homomorphism
\varphi:({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{n}\to({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{n-1}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In particular, when
{\mathbb{F}}
is finite, one has
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\Ima\varphi|({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{n-1})<h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\Ima\varphi)
. Combining Theorem
3.7
, we have

Corollary 3.9
.

\Gamma
is amenable if and only if
h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})
holds for any
{\mathbb{Z}\Gamma}
-modules
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
is torsion as an abelian group.

4.
Peters’ formula

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
, which follows from Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field. For a finitely generated free
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module
({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{1\times n}
, if we identify
\widehat{({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{1\times n}}
with
({\mathbb{F}}^{\Gamma})^{1\times n}=({\mathbb{F}}^{1\times n})^{\Gamma}
naturally via the pairing

({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{1\times n}\times({\mathbb{F}}^{\Gamma})^{1\times n}\to{\mathbb{F}},\ (u,x)\mapsto(xu^{*})_{e_{\Gamma}},

the induced natural action of
\Gamma
on
\widehat{({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{1\times n}}
is then given by left shift. Here
u^{*}=\sum_{s\in\Gamma}u_{s}s^{-1}
. For a finitely presented
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
, write it as
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{n}/({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{m}f
for some
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in M_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)
, we can further identify
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
as
{\rm ker\ }R(f^{\ast})
for the linear map
R(f^{\ast}):({\mathbb{F}}^{\Gamma})^{1\times n}\to({\mathbb{F}}^{\Gamma})^{1\times m}
sending
x
to
xf^{\ast}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field. Then for any finitely generated
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-module
{\mathcal{M}}
, we have
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Proof.

We first show the case that
{\mathcal{M}}
is finitely presented. Write
{\mathcal{M}}
as
{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{n}/({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)^{m}f
for some
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in M_{m,n}({\mathbb{F}}\Gamma)
. Given a map
\sigma:\Gamma\to{\rm Sym}(d)
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
f
induces linear maps
\sigma(f):({\mathbb{F}}^{d})^{n\times 1}\to({\mathbb{F}}^{d})^{m\times 1}
and
\bar{\sigma}(f):({\mathbb{F}}^{d})^{m\times 1}\to({\mathbb{F}}^{d})^{n\times 1}
which are uniquely determined by the linear maps
\sigma_{s}:{\mathbb{F}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{d}
and
\bar{\sigma}_{s}:{\mathbb{F}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{F}}^{d}
respectively for every
s\in\Gamma
. Here
(\sigma_{s}(x))(v)=x((\sigma_{s})^{-1}v)
and
(\bar{\sigma}_{s}(x))(v)=x(\sigma_{s}(v))
(see
[
12
, Section 6]
for more details). Apply

\displaystyle h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}})

For a given map
\sigma:\Gamma\to{\rm Sym}(d)

{\mathcal{W}}=\{v\in[d]:\sigma(s^{-1})(v)=\sigma(s)^{-1}(v),{\rm for\ all\ }s\in{\rm supp}(f)\}

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a finite field. Then for any countable
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-modules
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, we have
h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})\leq h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
.

Proof.

Case 1
.
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
are finitely generated.

Combining Lemma
4.2
, Proposition
4.1
, and Theorem
3.2
, we have

h_{\Sigma}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})\leq h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})-h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}/{\mathcal{M}}_{1}})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})-h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{2}/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2}).

Case 2
.
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is finitely generated.

Write
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
as the union of an increasing sequence of finitely generated
{\mathbb{F}}\Gamma
-modules
\{{\mathcal{N}}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
containing
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
. It is clear that by definition, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one has

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})\leq h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}_{j}}).

Thus by Proposition
3.3
and Case 1, we have

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}_{j}})\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{N}}_{j})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2}).

For general case, write
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
as the union of an increasing sequence of finitely generated submodules
\{{\mathcal{N}}_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
. Following the similar argument as in the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 9.10]
, we have

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})=\lim_{j\to\infty}h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{N}}_{j}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}}).

Thus by Proposition
3.3
and Case 2, we have

h_{\Sigma,\omega}(\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}|\widehat{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}})\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{N}}_{j}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2})=h_{\Sigma,\omega}({\mathcal{M}}_{1}|{\mathcal{M}}_{2}).

∎

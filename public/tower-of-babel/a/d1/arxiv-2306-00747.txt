Low-complexity approximations for sets defined by generalizations of affine conditions
W. T. Gowers, Thomas Karam

Abstract
Let p p be a prime, let S S be a non-empty subset of 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} and let 0 < ϵ ≤ 1 0<\epsilon\leq 1 . We show that there exists a constant C = C ⁡ ( p , ϵ ) C=C(p,\epsilon) such that for every positive integer k k , whenever ϕ 1 , … , ϕ k : 𝔽 p n → 𝔽 p \phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p} are linear forms and E 1 , … , E k E_{1},\dots,E_{k} are subsets of 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} , there exist linear forms ψ 1 , … , ψ C : 𝔽 p n → 𝔽 p \psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{C}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p} and subsets F 1 , … , F C F_{1},\dots,F_{C} of 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} such that the set U = { x ∈ S n : ψ 1 ( x ) ∈ F 1 , … , ψ C ( x ) ∈ F C } U=\{x\in S^{n}:\psi_{1}(x)\in F_{1},\dots,\psi_{C}(x)\in F_{C}\} is contained inside the set V = { x ∈ S n : ϕ 1 ( x ) ∈ E 1 , … , ϕ k ( x ) ∈ E k } V=\{x\in S^{n}:\phi_{1}(x)\in E_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}(x)\in E_{k}\} , and the difference V ∖ U V\setminus U has density at most ϵ \epsilon inside S n S^{n} . We then generalize this result to one where ϕ 1 , … , ϕ k \phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k} are replaced by homomorphisms G n → H G^{n}\to H for some pair of finite Abelian groups G G and H H , and to another where they are replaced by polynomial maps 𝔽 p n → 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p} of small degree.



1
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime. If
\phi_{i}(x)=b_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k
is a system of equations over some finite-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then the solution set is either empty or an affine subspace with codimension equal to
\dim\langle\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}\rangle
, and therefore with density equal to

p^{-\dim\langle\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}\rangle}.

It follows that if the density of the set of solutions is at least
\delta
, then there is a set of at most
\log_{p}\delta^{-1}
equations with the same solution set.

In this paper we shall generalize this simple observation in a number of ways. Our initial motivation for doing this was that in the context of results such as the density Hales-Jewett theorem
[
1
,
5
]
certain generalizations of affine subspaces occur naturally. Consider, for instance, the problem of understanding triples of dense subsets
A,B
and
C
of
\{0,1,2\}^{n}
such that the set
A\times B\times C
contains no combinatorial line. (One might wish to do this with a view to obtaining a density increment argument.) One large source of examples is constructed as follows. Pick a prime
p
and three subsets
U,V
and
W
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
U\times V\times W
contains no arithmetic progression (even degenerate), let
\phi:\{0,1,2\}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the function where
\phi(x)
is the mod-
p
sum of the coordinates of
x
, and let
A=\phi^{-1}(U),B=\phi^{-1}(V)
and
C=\phi^{-1}(W)
. Since
\phi
takes combinatorial lines to arithmetic progressions in
\mathbb{Z}
, and therefore in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, it follows that
A\times B\times C
has the desired property.

The above construction can be generalized further, and led us to consider sets of the following form. Let
G
be a finite Abelian group, let
S
be a subset of
G
, let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}:G^{n}\to G
be homomorphisms, and let
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
be subsets of
G
. Then we take the subset

\{x\in G^{n}:\phi_{i}(x)\in E_{i},i=1,2,\dots,k\},

which is subspace-like, in that it is the intersection of sets defined by linear-like conditions. One of our main results will be that, as in the simple case where
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
are singletons, if the above subset is dense, then we may replace it by a subset defined by a number of conditions that is bounded above in terms of the density. However, the new subset is not identical to the old one: in this set-up, the best one can hope for is a good approximation, and that is what we shall obtain. The precise statement will be given later.

When
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we also prove a generalization in a different direction, considering subsets of
S^{n}
that are defined by low-degree polynomial conditions on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
. Again we shall show that if such a subset is dense, then it can be approximated by a subset of bounded complexity, meaning that it is defined using a bounded number of low-degree polynomial conditions. Again, we defer the precise statement till later.

The following examples indicate some of the complexities that arise when we restrict linear conditions to subsets of the form
S^{n}
. For brevity, if
\phi:G^{n}\to G
and
E\subset G
, we write
(\phi,E)
for the condition
\phi(x)\in E
.

Example 1
.

For
i=2,3,\dots,k
, let
\phi_{i}:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the linear form defined by
\phi_{i}(x)=x_{1}+x_{i}
and let
E_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the set
\{0,1\}
. Then the set of points of
\{0,1\}^{n}
satisfying the conditions
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
consists of all
x\in\{0,1\}^{n}
such that either
x_{1}=0
, or
x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=\dots=x_{k}=0
. The conditions are linearly independent, and the density of this set is
1/2+1/2^{k}
. Given
\epsilon>0
, either
k\leq\log_{2}(\epsilon^{-1})
or we can approximate the set to within density
\epsilon
by the set of points satisfying the single condition
x_{1}=0
.

Example 2
.

For
i=2,3,\dots,k
, let
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
be the group homomorphism defined by
\phi_{i}(x)=(x_{1},x_{i})
and let
E_{i}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
be the set
(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cup\{(1,1)\}
. Then the set of points of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
satisfying the conditions
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
consists of all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
such that either
x_{1}=0
, or
x_{1}=x_{2}=\dots=x_{k}=1
. The density of this set is
1/p+1/p^{k}
, so it can be approximated in a similar way.

Example 3
.

For
i=2,3,\dots,k
, let
P:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the degree-
2
polynomial defined by
P(x)=x_{1}x_{i}
and let
E_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the set
\{0\}
. Then the set of points of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
satisfying the conditions
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
consists of all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
such that either
x_{1}=0
, or
x_{1}\neq 0
and
x_{2}=\dots=x_{k}=0
. The density of this set is
1/p+(p-1)/p^{k}
, so again it can be approximated in a similar way.

Note that in each of the three examples, the low-complexity set that approximates the solution set – that is, the set of points that satisfy the conditions – is a subset of the solution set. Our main results will show that this is a general phenomenon. To formulate them it will be useful to have a few definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a finite set and let
U,V
be subsets of
X
. Then
U

internally
\epsilon
-approximates

V
if
U\subset V
and
|V\setminus U|
has density at most
\epsilon
in
X
. Given a set
Z
, a function
f:X\to Z
, and a subset
E\subset Z
, we call the pair
(f,E)
a
condition
on
X
, and we say that
x

satisfies the condition

(f,E)
if
f(x)\in E
. If
\mathcal{F}=\{(f_{1},E_{1}),\dots,(f_{k},E_{k})\}
is a set of conditions on
X
, the
satisfying set
of the conditions of
\mathcal{F}
is the set of
x\in X
that satisfy all the conditions in
\mathcal{F}
. If
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are sets of conditions on
X
, we say that
\mathcal{G}

internally
\epsilon
-approximates

\mathcal{F}
if the satisfying set of
\mathcal{G}
internally
\epsilon
-approximates the satisfying set of
\mathcal{F}
. If
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then a
mod-
p
condition
on
S^{n}
is a condition
(\phi,E)
on
S^{n}
, where
\phi
is the restriction to
S^{n}
of a linear form defined on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
and
E
is a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, every prime
p
and every non-empty
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
there exists
K
such that for every
n
, every set of mod-
p
conditions on
S^{n}
can be internally
\epsilon
-approximated by a set of at most
K
mod-
p
conditions.

In order to give some idea of why this might be an interesting statement, we make a few remarks. Note first that if we set
p=2
, then the result is easy. We can identify
\{0,1\}^{n}
with
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, and the satisfying set will take the form
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\{x:\phi_{i}(x)\in E_{i}\}
, where each
\phi_{i}
is a linear form on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and each
E_{i}
is a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. If
E_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then we can discard the condition
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
without affecting the satisfying set, and if any
E_{i}
is empty then the satisfying set is empty, so we can obtain it using just the condition
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
. So without loss of generality all the sets
E_{i}
are singletons and the satisfying set is an affine subspace. If the codimension of the subspace is greater than
\log_{2}(\epsilon^{-1})
then we can internally
\epsilon
-approximate it by the empty set, which as above just needs one condition. Otherwise, we can pick
k
linearly independent conditions that specify the subspace and we have a set of at most
\log_{2}(\epsilon^{-1})
conditions with the same satisfying set.

It is only slightly harder to prove the following closely related statement about linear conditions on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
with
p>2
, by which we mean conditions
(\phi,E)
, where
\phi
is a linear form on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
and
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
. However, the bound is slightly weaker, owing to a crude argument at the end of the proof. We have not tried to improve the bound, and indeed it is not obviously possible, because in this case satisfying sets do not have to be affine subspaces.

Proposition 1.3
.

For every
\epsilon>0
and every
n
, every set of linear conditions on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
can be internally
\epsilon
-approximated by a set of at most
(p\log\epsilon^{-1})^{p}
linear conditions.

Proof.

Let the conditions be
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,r
. As in the
p=2
case we may assume that each
E_{i}
is a non-empty proper subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We may also assume that the
\phi_{i}
are distinct, since if
\phi_{i}=\phi_{j}
then we may replace the two conditions
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
and
(\phi_{j},E_{j})
by the single condition
(\phi_{i},E_{i}\cap E_{j})
.

Now choose a maximal set
I
such that the forms
\phi_{i}
with
i\in I
are linearly independent. Then if we choose
x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
uniformly at random, the events
\phi_{i}(x)\in E_{i}
with
i\in I
are independent, so the probability that
x
belongs to the satisfying set is at most
\prod_{i\in I}|E_{i}|/p
, which is at most
(1-1/p)^{|I|}
. If
|I|>p\log(\epsilon^{-1})
, then this is at most
\epsilon
, so we can internally
\epsilon
-approximate the satisfying set by the empty set, which requires just one condition. Otherwise, the forms
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}
are contained in a linear subspace of dimension at most
p\log(\epsilon^{-1})
, and there is at most one condition for each
i
, so
r\leq(p\log(\epsilon^{-1}))^{p}
, which proves the result.
∎

The key to the proofs above was that if a set satisfies
k
non-trivial linearly independent conditions, then its density must be at most
\alpha^{k}
for some
\alpha<1
that is independent of
n
. What makes Theorem
1.2
harder is that this is no longer true for mod-
p
conditions on
\{0,1\}^{n}
, as Example
1
shows. Example
1
is a simple representative of a large class of examples, but it illustrates the basic challenge that we have to deal with.

Once we have proved Theorem
1.2
, we shall generalize it to a similar statement with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
replaced by an arbitrary finite Abelian group. If
G
and
H
are finite Abelian groups and
n
is a positive integer, then an
H
-
condition
on
G^{n}
is a pair
(\phi,E)
, where
\phi:G^{n}\to H
is a homomorphism and
E\subset H
. The definition of an internal
\epsilon
-approximation carries over in the obvious way: if
S\subset G
, then we shall say that a set
\mathcal{F}
of
H
-conditions on
G^{n}

internally
\epsilon
-approximates
a set
\mathcal{G}
of
H
-conditions on
G^{n}
if every point that satisfies
\mathcal{F}
satisfies
\mathcal{G}
and the difference of the satisfying sets has density at most
\epsilon
inside
S^{n}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
and
H
be finite Abelian groups, let
S
be a non-empty subset of
G
, and let
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists
A=A(\epsilon,G,S,H)
such that every set of
H
-conditions can be
\epsilon
-approximated on
S^{n}
by a set of at most
A

k
-dimensional
H
-conditions.

This theorem turns out to need additional ideas even when
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
H=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
.

The other generalization we shall prove is to polynomial conditions. If
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then a
k
-dimensional condition of degree at most
d
on
S^{n}
is a pair
(\phi,E)
, where
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
is a polynomial map of degree at most
d
(that is, each coordinate of
\phi(x)
is obtained by applying a polynomial with degree at most
d
to
x
) and
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
. We prove the following theorem, which has a weaker conclusion than in the linear case, but still shows that a set defined by polynomial conditions can be approximated by a set of bounded complexity defined by polynomial conditions.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, every prime
p
, and every pair of positive integers
k
and
d
with
d<p
, there exists
M
such that for every non-empty
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, every set of
k
-dimensional conditions with degree at most
d
on
S^{n}
can be internally
\epsilon
-approximated by a set consisting of a single
m
-dimensional condition with degree at most
d
on
S^{n}
with
m\leq M
.

2
Preliminary lemmas

The next section will be devoted to a proof of Theorem
1.2
. To prepare for this, we collect together some more technical statements that will be useful in the proof. They will mainly concern the joint distribution of the values
\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{k}(x)
, where each
\phi_{i}
is a linear form from
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
x
is chosen uniformly from
S^{n}
for some subset
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of size at least 2. We begin with a result that will help us with the case
k=1
. We write
\omega_{p}
for
\exp(2\pi i/p)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that contains at least two elements. Then

|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S}\omega_{p}^{tx}|\leq 1-p^{-2}

for each
t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, it is sufficient to prove the result in the case
|S|=2
, since

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S}\omega_{p}^{tx}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{T\subset S}\atop{|T|=2}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in T}\omega_{p}^{tx}.

But if
y\neq 0
, then

|1+\omega_{p}^{y}|^{2}=2+2\cos(2\pi y/p)\leq 2+2\cos(2\pi/p).

Using the bound
\cos\theta\leq 1-\theta^{2}/2+\theta^{4}/24
, which is at most
1-\theta^{2}/4
when
\theta\leq 2\pi/3
, we find that this is at most
4-4\pi^{2}/4p^{2}=4-\pi^{2}/p^{2}=4(1-\pi^{2}/4p^{2})
. It follows that
|1+\omega_{p}^{y}|/2\leq 1-\pi^{2}/8p^{2}\leq 1-1/p^{2}
, which implies the result.
∎

From this lemma we deduce an upper bound on the Fourier coefficients of a linear phase function restricted to
S^{n}
. Given a linear form
\phi
defined by a formula
x\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
, we define
Z(\phi)
to be
\{i:\lambda_{i}\neq 0\}
. We shall call this set the
support
of
\phi
, even though technically it is the support of the coefficients of
\phi
with respect to the standard basis. This should cause no confusion, since the complement of the kernel of
\phi
will play no role in the paper.

Our bound shows that the Fourier coefficients are exponentially small in the size of the support.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that contains at least two elements. Let
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a linear form. Then

|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S^{n}}\omega_{p}^{\phi(x)}|\leq(1-p^{-2})^{|Z(\phi)|}.

Proof.

If
\phi(x)=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
, then the left-hand side splits as a product

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S^{n}}\omega_{p}^{\phi(x)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{i}\in S}\omega_{p}^{\lambda_{i}x_{i}}.

The inner expectation of the right-hand side is equal to
1
for every
i\notin Z(\phi)
, and by Lemma
2.1
it has modulus at most
1-p^{-2}
for every
z\in Z(\phi)
. The result follows. ∎

Remark 2.3
.

Note that the same conclusion holds for
t\phi
for every non-zero
t
, since in that case
Z(t\phi)=Z(\phi)
.

A standard calculation now gives us the equidistribution result we shall need. Given a linear map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
and an element
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
, we write
a.\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
for the map that takes
x
to
a.\phi(x)
, where
a.b
is notation for
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}b_{i}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
k
be a positive integer, and let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that contains at least two elements. Let
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
be a linear map and let
x
be chosen uniformly at random from
S^{n}
. Suppose that
|Z(a.\phi)|\geq r
for every non-zero
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
. Then for each
z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
,

|\mathbb{P}[\phi(x)=z]-p^{-k}|\leq(1-p^{-2})^{r}.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{z}
be the function that takes the value
1
at
z
and 0 everywhere else. Then

\mathbb{P}[\phi(x)=z]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S^{n}}\delta_{z}(\phi(x)),

which by the Fourier inversion formula is equal to

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S^{n}}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}}\hat{\delta_{z}}(a)\omega_{p}^{a.\phi(x)}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\hat{\delta_{z}}(a)\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S^{n}}\omega_{p}^{a.\phi(x)}.

But
\hat{\delta_{z}}(a)=p^{-k}\omega_{p}^{-a.z}
, so when
t=0
it is equal to
p^{-k}
, and otherwise it has size at most
p^{-k}
. The result now follows from Proposition
2.2
, the remark after it, and the triangle inequality.
∎

If a linear form
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
takes a value once, then it must take that value with probability at least
p^{-1}
. We now show that something like this, but weaker, holds for the restriction of
\phi
to a set of the form
S^{n}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
S
be a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that contains at least two elements. For every linear form
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
and every
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
the probability
\mathbb{P}_{x\in S^{n}}(\phi(x)=y)
is either
0
or at least
|S|^{-t}
, where
t=\lceil(p-1)/(|S|-1)\rceil
.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a linear form given by the formula
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}x_{i}
. Without loss of generality there exists
m
such that
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}
are non-zero and
\lambda_{m+1}=\dots=\lambda_{n}=0
.

Since there are
|S|^{m}
distinct choices for
(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
if
x\in S^{n}
, each value of
\phi(x)
that is taken at all is taken with probability at least
|S|^{-m}
.

We now prove a second upper bound that is less trivial. Write
\lambda.S
for the dilate
\{\lambda u:u\in S\}
of
S
by
\lambda
. By the Cauchy-Davenport theorem, for every
r
we have the inequality

|\lambda_{1}.S+\lambda_{2}.S+\dots+\lambda_{r}.S|\geq\min\{r(|S|-1)+1,p\}.

In particular, if
r(|S|-1)\geq p-1
, then
\lambda_{1}.S+\dots+\lambda_{r}.S=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

For such
r
, it follows that if
x
is chosen uniformly at random from
S^{n}
, then for every
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the probability that
\lambda_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{r}x_{r}=u
is at least
|S|^{-r}
. By conditioning on the value taken by
\lambda_{r+1}x_{r+1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}x_{n}
and applying the law of total probability, we deduce that each element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is equal to
\phi(x)
with probability at least
|S|^{-r}
.

Combining this with the first bound gives the result claimed.
∎

If
S=\{0,1,\dots,h\}
and
h|(p-1)
, then the bound in Proposition
2.5
is optimal, since the linear form
\phi
defined by
\phi(x)=x_{1}+\dots+x_{(p-1)/h}
takes the value
p-1
with probability
|S|^{-(p-1)/h}
. Note that when
|S|=2
, the lower bound we obtain for each non-zero probability is
2^{-(p-1)}
.

3
Approximating sets of mod-
p
conditions

In this section we shall prove Theorem
1.2
. From now on, we shall write “
\epsilon
-approximated” to mean “internally
\epsilon
-approximated”, since all the approximations we consider will be internal ones. (Of course, it is then important to keep in mind that the relation “can be
\epsilon
-approximated by” is not symmetric.)

Our basic strategy will be to start with a set of mod-
p
conditions and approximate it by sets with more and more structure until eventually we arrive at a set of bounded size. In order to explain this strategy in more detail, we need some definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime. If
\phi_{0}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a linear form, then a
sunflower with centre

\phi_{0}
is a set of forms
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
such that the forms
\phi_{i}
are disjointly supported. A
symmetric family
of forms is a set
\{\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
that are equal up to permutations of the coordinates – that is, for each
y
the number of coefficients of
\phi_{i}
equal to
y
is the same for each
i
. A
symmetric sunflower
is a sunflower
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
such that the set
\{\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
is a symmetric family. A set of forms
\{\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
is
r
-
separated
if
|Z(\sum_{i\in I}u_{i}\phi_{i})|\geq r
for every non-zero
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{I}
– that is, if the support size of every non-trivial linear combination of the
\phi_{i}
is at least
r
. The
support distance
on the set of all linear forms
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the metric given by the formula
d(\phi,\psi)=|Z(\phi-\psi)|
. The
ball of radius
r
about
\phi_{0}
is the set of all
\phi
such that
d(\phi_{0},\phi)\leq r
. We say that a sunflower
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I\}

has radius at most

r
if it is contained in the ball of radius
r
about
\phi_{0}
.

Note that, contrary to what our terminology might at first suggest, the condition that the forms are
r
-separated is stronger than simply saying that
d(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})\geq r
for every
i\neq j
: we need all non-trivial linear combinations to have large support and not just differences between two distinct forms.

We begin with the following simple observation, which we shall use repeatedly.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
X
be a finite set, let
q\geq 1
be a positive integer and let
\epsilon>0
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{q},\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{q}
be sets of conditions on
X
. If
\mathcal{F}_{i}
internally
\epsilon
-approximates
\mathcal{G}_{i}
for each
i\in[q]
then
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{F}_{q}
internally
q\epsilon
-approximates
\mathcal{G}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{G}_{q}
.

Theorem
1.2
concerns arbitrary sets of mod-
p
conditions. We shall begin by proving it under a strong additional hypothesis concerning those conditions, and then we shall progressively weaken this hypothesis until we end up proving the result in full generality. At each stage, if we have a proof for families
\mathcal{F}
of conditions that satisfy an additional property
P
and we would like a proof for families
\mathcal{E}
of conditions that satisfy a weaker property
Q
, it is sufficient to prove that every family
\mathcal{E}
that satisfies
Q
can be approximated by a family
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{F}_{q}
such that
q
is bounded and each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
satisfies
P
. Then using the remark above and the result for families that satisfy
P
, we end up with a bounded number of conditions that approximate
\mathcal{E}
.

Our first additional hypothesis will be that the set of forms is a symmetric sunflower and that all the subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are the same. Having proved the result under that hypothesis, we shall then weaken the hypothesis by no longer insisting that the set is a sunflower, but keeping the symmetry and the assumption that the subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are the same and adding the condition that the set of forms has bounded radius. Finally, we shall prove the result in full generality. These stages will be the contents of Proposition
3.3
, Proposition
3.4
, and Theorem
1.2
respectively.

Given a linear form
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
with coefficients
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
, let
D_{\phi}:\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{N}
be defined by
D_{\phi}(y)=|\{i\in[n]:\lambda_{i}=y\}|
. We shall call
D_{\phi}
the
coefficient distribution
of
\phi
. Note that a symmetric family of forms is one where all the forms have the same coefficient distribution.

We begin, then, by proving the result for symmetric sunflowers and identical subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This is a generalization of the example we gave earlier where the forms were
x\mapsto x_{1}+x_{i}
, and as with that example it will turn out that as long as there are enough forms, we can
\epsilon
-approximate the set of conditions by a set of the form
(\phi_{0},H)
, where
\phi_{0}
is the centre of the sunflower.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
S
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that contains at least two elements, let
\epsilon>0
, let
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
be a symmetric sunflower, and let
E\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then there exists
H\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that the condition
(\phi_{0},H)

p(1-2^{-(p-1)})^{|I|}
-approximates the set of conditions
(\phi_{0}+\phi_{i},E)
.

Proof.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the set of all possible values taken by
\phi_{i}(x)
when
x\in S^{n}
, and note that by the symmetry condition
V
is the same for all
i\in I
. We let
H
be the set
\{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}:V+t\subset E\}
and we let
T
be the complement of
H
. If
x\in S^{n}
and
\phi_{0}(x)\in H
, then by the definition of
H
,
\phi_{0}(x)+\phi_{i}(x)\in E
. This establishes that the satisfying set for the single condition
(\phi_{0},H)
is contained in the satisfying set for the conditions
(\phi_{0}+\phi_{i},E)
, so it remains to obtain an upper bound for the probability that
\phi_{0}(x)\in T
and
\phi_{0}(x)+\phi_{i}(x)\in E
for every
i\in I
.

Let
t\in T
. Then

\mathbb{P}[\phi_{0}(x)=t\ \wedge\ \forall i\in I\ \phi_{0}(x)+\phi_{i}(x)\in E]\leq\mathbb{P}[\forall i\in I\ \phi_{i}(x)\in E-t]=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{P}[\phi_{i}(x)\in E-t],

where the last equality follows from the fact that the
\phi_{i}
are disjointly supported and hence the events
\phi_{i}(x)\in E-t
are independent (when
x
is chosen uniformly at random from
S^{n}
).

Since
V+t
is not contained in
E
, the probability that
\phi_{i}(x)\notin E-t
is non-zero, and therefore by Lemma
2.5
it is at least
2^{-(p-1)}
. It follows that the product above is at most
(1-2^{-(p-1)})^{|I|}
. There are at most
p
possibilities for

, so the result follows.
∎

The proposition above implies that if the set of forms is a symmetric sunflower, then either it is already a family of bounded size, or it can be approximated by a family of size 1.

Proposition 3.4
.

For every prime
p
, every non-negative integer
r
and every

there is a constant

such that for every pair of subsets
S
and
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|S|\geq 2
and every symmetric family
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
contained in the ball about
\phi_{0}
of radius

, the conditions
(\phi_{0}+\phi_{i},E)
on

can be
\epsilon
-approximated by a set of at most
A

p

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on

. The proof will be similar to the proof by Erdős and Rado of the sunflower lemma (that is, their argument that gives a factorial-type bound for the sunflower conjecture). When

, the ball of radius

about
\phi_{0}
consists of

only, so the result holds with

. Now let

and let us assume that the result holds up to

. Let
M
be a maximal subset of
I
such that the forms
\phi_{i}
with

have disjoint supports, and let

be the union of those supports.

If
|M|\geq 2^{p-1}\log(p\epsilon^{-1})
then by Proposition
3.3
there exists a subset
H\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that the condition
(\phi_{0},H)

\epsilon
-approximates the conditions
(\phi_{0}+\phi_{i},E)
with

. From the proof of Proposition
3.3
and the symmetry condition,
(\phi_{0},H)
implies every condition
(\phi_{0}+\phi_{i},E)
with

, so it follows that the condition
(\phi_{0},H)

\epsilon
-approximates the conditions
(\phi_{0}+\phi_{i},E)
with
i\in I
.

For each

, let the coefficients of
\phi_{i}
be
(\lambda_{i1},\dots,\lambda_{in})
. If instead
|M|\leq 2^{p-1}\log(p\epsilon^{-1})
then for each

the support of
\phi_{i}
intersects

and some non-zero

such that

. That is, if we set

to be the set of

Now let us fix

and

with

about the modification of

.

By the inductive hypothesis, we can therefore
\epsilon/(2^{p-1}pr\log(p\epsilon^{-1}))
-approximate each family
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I_{j,y}\}
by a family of at most
A(p,r-1,\epsilon/(2^{p-1}pr\log(p\epsilon^{-1}))

conditions. By Remark

and the fact that

, it follows that we can
\epsilon
-approximate the family
\{\phi_{0}+\phi_{i}:i\in I\}
by a family of at most

A(p,r,\epsilon)=2^{p-1}pr\log(p\epsilon^{-1})A(p,r-1,\epsilon/(2^{p-1}pr\log(p\epsilon^{-1}))

∎

A back-of-envelope calculation shows that

is at most

. (We have not attempted to optimize this bound.)

We have now shown that symmetric families of bounded radius can be approximated. It remains to prove that an arbitrary family can be approximated by a union of boundedly many symmetric families of bounded radius. That will be the content of the rest of the argument in this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let the set of conditions to be approximated be
\{(\phi_{i},E_{i}):i\in I\}
. We may assume that each
E_{i}
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let

.

Case 1.
Suppose that there exists a subset

of

of size at least

such that the linear forms
\phi_{i}
with

are
r

. By Proposition

and our choice of

\mathbb{P}[\forall i\in I^{\prime},\phi_{i}=y_{i}]\leq p^{-|I^{\prime}|}+(1-p^{-2})^{r}\leq 2p^{-|I^{\prime}|}

for each

.

It follows that

\mathbb{P}[\forall i\in I^{\prime}\ \phi_{i}\in E_{i}]\leq(\prod_{i\in I^{\prime}}|E_{i}|)(2p^{-|I^{\prime}|})\leq 2(1-p^{-1})^{|I^{\prime}|}\leq\epsilon

so the set of conditions
\{(\phi_{i},E_{i}):i\in I\}
is
\epsilon
-approximated by the condition
(x_{1},S^{c})
(or the condition
(\phi,\emptyset)
for any form
\phi
).

Case 2.
Now suppose that there does not exist such a subset

. Let
M
be a maximal

-separated subset of

has size at most
p\log(2/\epsilon)
, and for every

such that

has support size at most

.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we now divide the set of conditions into families. This time, given a subset

, an element

, and a function

such that
\sum_{y}D(y)=n
and

, we let

be the set of

such that

, and
\phi_{i}-\sum_{m\in M}a_{m}\phi_{m}
has coefficient distribution

. There are at most

, at most

such that

, so the number of families

are the same.

Therefore, by Proposition

we can
(2^{p}p^{|M|}r^{p})^{-1}\epsilon
-approximate each set of conditions

by a set of at most

is at most

so the result now follows by Remark

, and the number of conditions needed for the approximation is at most

p^{4p\log(2/\epsilon)}A(p,2p^{3}\log p\log(2/\epsilon),p^{-4p\log(2/\epsilon)}\epsilon).

After a back-of-envelope calculation we can bound this above by

. Thus, for fixed

∎

4
More general finite Abelian groups

In this and the next section we shall prove Theorem
1.4
. This needs extra ideas, but it turns out that those ideas are already needed for the simpler case where
S=G
, so in this section we shall prove that special case, and then in the next section we shall combine the proof method of this section with the ideas about sunflowers from the previous section in order to obtain a proof for general
S
.

The following definition will be central to the proof.

Definition 4.1
.

Given homomorphisms
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}:G^{b}\to H
and characters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k}\in\hat{H}
, the map
x\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(\phi_{i}(x))
defines a character on
G
. Let us call
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}

independent
if the only way for
\prod_{i}\chi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}
to be the trivial character is if all the
\chi_{i}
are the trivial character.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}
be independent homomorphisms from
G^{n}
to
H
. Then for every
y\in H
,

\mathbb{P}_{x\in G^{n}}[\forall i\ \phi_{i}(x)=y_{i}]=|H|^{-k}.

Proof.

The probability in question is equal to

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k}\in\hat{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(\phi_{i}(x)-y_{i})

since if
\phi_{i}(x)=y_{i}
for each
i
, then the inner expectation is equal to 1, and if for some
i
we have
\phi_{i}(x)\neq y_{i}
, then
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi_{i}}\chi_{i}(\phi_{i}(x)-y_{i})=0
, so the whole expectation is 0.

We can rewrite this expression as

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k}\in\hat{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(-y_{i})\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(\phi_{i}(x)).

If
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k}
are all equal to the trivial character, then the expression being averaged over is equal to 1. Otherwise, by hypothesis,
\prod_{i}\chi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}
is not the trivial character on
G^{n}
, which implies that
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\prod_{i}\chi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}=0
. Therefore, the probability is
|\hat{H}|^{-k}=|H|^{-k}
.
∎

The next definition and lemma are standard, but we recall them here for convenience.

Definition 4.3
.

Given an Abelian group
G
, a subgroup
H\subset G
, and a character
\chi\in\hat{G}
, say that
\chi

annihilates

H
if
\chi(x)=1
for every
x\in H
. The
annihilator
of
H
, denoted
H^{\perp}
, is the subgroup of
\hat{G}
that consists of all
\chi
that annihilate
H
. Similarly, if
\hat{H}
is a subgroup of
\hat{G}
, we say that
x\in G

annihilates

\hat{H}
if
\chi(x)=1
for every
\chi\in\hat{H}
, and the
annihilator
of
\hat{H}
is the subgroup of all
x\in G
that annihilate
\hat{H}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finite Abelian group and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Then
H^{\perp}\cong\widehat{G/H}
.

Proof.

Define a map
\iota:H^{\perp}\to\widehat{G/H}
by
\iota(\chi)(xH)=\chi(x)
. Note that this is well defined, since if
xy^{-1}\in H
, then
\chi(y)=\chi((xy^{-1})y)=\chi(x)
. It is also a homomorphism, since

\iota(\chi_{1}\chi_{2})(xH)=\chi_{1}\chi_{2}(x)=\chi_{1}(x)\chi_{2}(x)=\iota(\chi_{1})(xH)\iota(\chi_{2})(xH)

for every
x\in G
.

Now let
\psi:G/H\to\mathbb{C}
be a character. Define
\theta(\psi):G\to\mathbb{C}
by
\theta(\psi)(x)=\psi(xH)
. Then
\theta(\psi)
is a character on
G
, since

\theta(\psi)(xy)=\psi(xyH)=\psi(xHyH)=\psi(xH)\psi(yH)=\theta(\psi)(x)\theta(\psi)(y).

It also annihilates
H
, since if
x\in H
, then
\theta(\psi)(x)=\psi(H)=1
. Finally, for any character
\chi
that annihilates
H
, we have that
\theta\iota\chi(x)=\iota\chi(xH)=\chi(x)
for every
x
, so
\theta
is a left inverse for
\iota
. In the other direction, if
\psi:G/H\to\mathbb{C}
is a character, then

\iota\theta\psi(xH)=\theta\psi(x)=\psi(xH)
, so
\theta
is also a right inverse for
\iota
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k}
be homomorphisms from
G^{n}
to
H
. Let

be the subgroup that consists of all

is the trivial character on

. Let

be the annihilator of

\mathbb{P}[\forall i\ \phi_{i}(x)=y_{i}]=\begin{cases}|Y|^{-1}&y\in Y\\
0&y\notin Y\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Again, the probability in question is equal to

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k}\in\hat{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(-y_{i})\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(\phi_{i}(x)).

If
(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k})\notin K
, then
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}
is not the trivial character, so the expectation over

is 0. Therefore, the probability is equal to

(|K|/|\hat{H}|^{k})\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k})\in K}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(-y_{i}).

If
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\in Y
, then
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(y_{i})=1
for every

, and therefore we end up with
(|K|/|\hat{H}|^{k})
. But by Lemma

(and Pontryagin duality),

, so this equals

.

If
(y_{1},\dots,y_{k})\notin Y
, then the evaluation map
(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{k}\chi_{i}(y_{i})
is a non-trivial character on

, and therefore its average is 0.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

Let

But each

∎

Let

We note first that the number of subsets of

We prove the result by induction on the size of a subgroup

k^{\prime}\leq k
. Then for every
\phi_{i}
there is a combination
(\chi\circ\phi_{i})\prod_{j=1}^{k^{\prime}}(\chi_{j}\circ\psi_{j})
that equals the trivial character, where all of
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k^{\prime}}
and
\chi
are characters on
H_{1}
and
\chi
is not the trivial character. By the usual partitioning argument we can assume that the
(k^{\prime}+1)
-tuple
(\chi,\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{k^{\prime}})
is the same for every
\phi_{i}
. So that gives us a character
\gamma:G^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
and a character
\chi\in\hat{H}_{1}
such that
\chi\circ\phi_{i}=\gamma
for every
i
.

Let us write
\xi_{i}=\phi_{i}-\phi_{1}
for each
i
. Then
\phi_{i}=\phi_{1}+\xi_{i}
, and
\chi\circ\xi_{i}=0
for every

. It follows that each
\xi_{i}
takes values in the subgroup
H_{2}=\{y\in H_{1}:\chi(y)=0\}
, which is a proper subgroup of
H_{1}
, as
\chi
is a non-trivial character on
H_{1}
. Therefore, by the inductive hypothesis, the conditions
(\phi_{i},E)
can be
\epsilon
-approximated (by at most

conditions), and we are done.
∎

5
General finite Abelian groups with restricted alphabets

We now show how to combine the methods of proof of the last two sections to give a proof of Theorem
1.4
, which generalizes Theorem
4.7
to the corresponding statement for restricted alphabets.

Before we start on this, we briefly mention a set-up that appears to be more general than that of Theorem
1.4
but is in fact equivalent to it. Suppose that
H
is a finite Abelian group,
S
is a non-empty finite set (so not necessarily a subset of a finite Abelian group), and
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}
are functions from
S
to
H
. We can define a function
\phi:S^{n}\to H
by
\phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}(x_{i})
, and if
E\subset H
, then we can consider the condition
(\phi,E)
on
S^{n}
, which is the condition
\phi(x)\in E
. This more general looking situation is covered by Theorem
1.4
, because for every
k
we can find a finite Abelian group
G
and a subset
S\subset G
of size
k
such that every function from
S
to
H
can be extended to a homomorphism from
G
to
H
. Then if we extend functions
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}
to homomorphisms
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{n}
, we find that the map
\phi
defined above is the restriction to
S^{n}
of the homomorphism
\psi:G^{n}\to H
defined by
\psi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}(x)
. Thus, in a certain sense Theorem
4.7
is not really about subsets of
G
but more about particular kinds of functions from a product
S^{n}
to a finite Abelian group
H
. However, the embedding of
S
into a group
G
is convenient.

Now let us turn to the proof. We begin with some definitions that will generalize those that we used in the case
G=H=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Given a homomorphism
\phi:G^{n}\to H
, we can express it in the form
\phi(x)=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}(x_{i})
, where each
\phi_{i}
is a homomorphism from
G
to
H
. If
S\subset G
is non-empty, then we say that the
support of
\phi
with respect to
S
(or just the support of
\phi
if
S
is clear from the context) is the set of all
i
such that
\phi_{i}
is not constant on
S
. We call the maps
\phi_{i}|_{S}
the
coefficients
of
\phi
(with respect to
S
), and the
coefficient distribution
of
\phi
is the function that takes each function
\psi:S\to H
to the number of
i
such that
\phi_{i}|_{S}=\psi
. The definitions of “sunflower”, “ball”, and “symmetric sunflower” carry over word for word from our earlier definitions (see Definition
3.1
) once the words and phrases “support” and “coefficient distribution” are interpreted in these generalized senses. We say that homomorphisms
\phi_{i}:G^{n}\to H
defined by
\phi_{i}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{ij}x_{j}
for every
i\in I
are
r
-separated if whenever
\chi_{i}\in\hat{H}
are characters for each
i\in I
that are not all trivial, the homomorphism

\prod_{i\in I}\chi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}:x\to\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\phi_{ij}(x_{j}))

has support size at least
r
(with the support being the number of
j
such that
\prod_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\phi_{ij}(x_{j}))
is not constant on
S
).

We begin by proving an analogue of Proposition
2.5
, but with a less satisfactory bound.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G
and
H
be finite Abelian groups, let
S
be a non-empty subset of
G
, let
n
be a positive integer, and let
\phi:G^{n}\to H
be a homomorphism. Let
x
be an element of
S^{n}
, chosen uniformly at random. Then for every
y\in H
, the probability that
\phi(x)=y
is either 0 or at least
c
, where
c=c(H)
is a positive constant that depends only on
H
(and on
S
).

Proof.

Let
Z
be the support of
\phi
. Then
\phi(x)
depends only on
x|_{Z}
, so each value
y
that is taken with non-zero probability is taken with probability at least
|S|^{-|Z|}
.

We also have that

\mathbb{P}[\phi(x)=y]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi\in\hat{H}}\chi(\phi(x)-y)=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi\in\hat{H}}\chi(-y)\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\chi(\phi(x))=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\chi\in\hat{H}}\chi(-y)\prod_{i}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{i}}\chi(\phi_{i}(x_{i})).

Note that
\chi\circ\phi
is a homomorphism and its support is the number of
i
such that
\chi\circ\phi_{i}
is not constant on
S
. For each such
i
,
|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x_{i}}\chi(\phi_{i}(x_{i}))|
is at most
|1+\exp(2\pi i/k)|/2
, where
k
is the order of
\chi
in
\hat{H}
. It is therefore at most
1-e(H)^{-2}
, where
e(H)
is the exponent of
H
.

It follows that if for each non-trivial character
\chi
the support size of
\chi\circ\phi
is at least
r
, then for every
y
the difference between
\mathbb{P}[\phi(x)=y]
and
|H|^{-1}
is at most
\exp(-re(H)^{-2})
. So if
r
is at least
e(H)^{2}\log(2|H|)
, then the probability that
\phi(x)=y
is at least
|H|^{-1}/2
for all
y
.

Suppose that there is some non-trivial character
\chi
such that
\chi\circ\phi
has support size less than
r
. Without loss of generality the support of
\chi
is
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
for some
m\leq r
. Let us write
x=x^{1}+x^{2}
where
x^{1}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},0,\dots,0)
and
x^{2}=(0,\dots,0,x_{m+1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then
\chi(\phi(x))
depends only on
x^{1}
, or to put it another way,
\phi(x^{2})
belongs to a certain coset of
H_{1}=\ker\chi
. Thus, for each coset
z+H_{1}
, either there is no
x
with
\phi(x)\in z+H_{1}
or the probability that
\phi(x)\in z+H_{1}
is at least
|S|^{-r}
. Suppose now that there exists
x
with
\phi(x)\in z+H_{1}
and pick
x^{1}
such that
\phi(x^{1}+x^{2})\in z+H_{1}
for every
x^{2}
. Then for each
y\in z+H_{1}
such that there exists
x^{2}
with
\phi(x^{1}+x^{2})=y
, the probability that
\phi(x^{1}+x^{2})=y
is, by induction on
|H|
, at least
c(H_{1})
. It follows that we may take
c(H)
to be
|S|^{-e(H)^{2}\log(2|H|)}c(H_{1})
. Since
|H_{1}|\leq|H|/2
and
e(H_{1})\leq e(H)
, an easy induction shows that we may therefore take
c(H)
to be at least
|S|^{-e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}
.
∎

The next lemma and its proof combine Proposition
3.3
with Proposition
4.6
. We stress that, consistent with the definition that we have given, maps
\theta_{i}
consisting merely of the same map repeated several times form a sunflower.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
and
H
be finite Abelian groups, let
S
be a non-empty subset of
G
, and let
H_{1}
be a subgroup of
H
. Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{m}\}
be a set of
m
homomorphisms from
G^{n}
to
H
and suppose that we can write
\phi_{i}=\theta_{i}+\psi_{i}
in such a way that the maps
\theta_{i}
form a symmetric sunflower contained in a ball of radius
r
, that the maps
\psi_{i}
take values in
H_{1}
and are
(rm+r^{\prime})
-separated. Then for every proper subset
E\subset H
the conditions
(\phi_{i},E)
, regarded as conditions on
S^{n}
, can be
\epsilon
-approximated by a single condition, where

\epsilon=|H|\Big(\exp\big(-m|S|^{-e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}|H_{1}|\big)+|H_{1}|^{m}\exp\big(-r^{\prime}e(H_{1})^{-2}\big)\Big).

Proof.

Let
\theta_{i}=\gamma+\zeta_{i}
, where the
\zeta_{i}
have disjoint supports of size at most
r
and the same coefficient distribution. For each
x\in H
, let
E_{x}=\{y\in H_{1}:x+y\in E\}
. We now let
F\subset H
be the set of
t\in H
such that
E_{t+\zeta_{i}(x)}=H_{1}
for every
x\in S^{n}
. Note that since the
\zeta_{i}
have the same coefficient distribution, their images are the same, so

does not depend on

.

We shall now show that the single condition
(\gamma,F)

\epsilon
-approximates the set of conditions
(\phi_{i},E)
. First, observe that if
\gamma(x)\in F
, then for every

,

E_{\theta_{i}(x)}=E_{\gamma(x)+\zeta_{i}(x)}=H_{1},

and therefore, since
\psi_{i}
takes values in
H_{1}
,
\phi_{i}(x)=\theta_{i}(x)+\psi_{i}(x)\in E
. Thus the condition
(\gamma,F)
implies all the other conditions.

It remains to prove that the set of
x\in S^{n}
such that
\gamma(x)\notin F
and
\phi_{i}(x)\in E
for every
i
has density at most
\epsilon
. For this purpose let us fix
y\notin F
and obtain an upper bound for the probability that
\gamma(x)=y
and
\phi_{i}(x)\in E
for every
i
. Note that if
\gamma(x)=y
, then
\phi_{i}(x)\in E
if and only if
\psi_{i}(x)\in E_{y+\zeta_{i}(x)}
for every
i
. Since
y\notin F
, there exists
x\in S^{n}
such that
E_{y+\zeta_{i}(x)}\neq H_{1}
. That is, there exist
x\in S^{n}
and
w\in H
such that
\zeta_{i}(x)=w
and
E_{y+w}\neq H_{1}
. By Proposition
5.1
the probability that
\zeta_{i}(x)=w
, when
x
is chosen uniformly from
S^{n}
, is at least
|S|^{-e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}
, from which it follows that the probability that
E_{y+\zeta_{i}(x)}\neq H_{1}
is at least
|S|^{-e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}
. Let
m(x)
be the number of
i
such that
E_{y+\zeta_{i}(x)}\neq H_{1}
.

Now let
Z
be the union of the supports of the maps
\zeta_{i}
. Then
Z
has cardinality at most
rm
. Let us write
W
for
Z^{c}
and
\pi_{Z}
and
\pi_{W}
for the coordinate projections onto
Z
and
W
. Then since the maps
\psi_{i}
are
(rm+r^{\prime})
-separated, it follows that the maps
\psi_{i}\circ\pi_{W}
are
r^{\prime}
-separated. Applying the beginning of the proof of Proposition
5.1
to the map
\psi=(\psi_{1}\circ\pi_{W},\dots,\psi_{m}\circ\pi_{W})
, we find that for each
(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\in H_{1}^{m}
the probability that
\psi_{i}(\pi_{W}x)=z_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,m
differs from
|H_{1}|^{-m}
by at most
\exp(-r^{\prime}e(H_{1})^{-2})
, since
e(H^{m})=e(H)
. Therefore, by conditioning on the restriction of

to
Z
and applying the law of total probability we find that the probability that
\psi_{i}(x)\in E_{y+\zeta_{i}(x)}
for every

is at most
(1-|H_{1}|^{-1})^{m(x)}+|H_{1}|^{m}\exp(-r^{\prime}e(H_{1})^{-2})
.

We now drop the conditioning. Let
q=|S|^{-e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}
. Then from the above calculations it follows that the probability that
\psi_{i}(x)\in E_{y+\zeta_{i}(x)}
for every
i
is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(1-q)^{m-j}

\displaystyle q^{j}(1-|H_{1}|^{-1})^{j}+|H_{1}|^{m}\exp(-r^{\prime}e(H_{1})^{-2})

\displaystyle=(1-q|H_{1}|)^{m}+|H_{1}|^{m}\exp(-r^{\prime}e(H_{1})^{-2})

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-mq|H_{1}|)+|H_{1}|^{m}\exp(-r^{\prime}e(H_{1})^{-2}),

where the equality follows from the binomial theorem.

Since there are at most
|H|
possibilities for

, we obtain the bound stated.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Note that if we wish to obtain an
\epsilon
-approximation, then it is sufficient if
m\geq\log(2|H|/\epsilon)|S|^{e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}|H_{1}|
and
r^{\prime}\geq\log(2|H||H_{1}|^{m}/\epsilon)e(H_{1})^{2}
. Note also that the proof that the condition

implies all the other conditions used only the fact that the

have the same coefficient distribution, so if

, then to obtain an
\epsilon
-approximation it is sufficient if the
\theta_{i}
belong to a symmetric ball of radius
r
and we can find
t
of them that form a sunflower. Taking the contrapositive, if the
\theta_{i}
belong to a symmetric ball and
(\gamma,F)
does
not

\epsilon
-approximate the set of conditions
(\phi_{i},E)
, then it is not possible to find a set
I
of size

such that the conditions
\theta_{i}
with

form a sunflower and the conditions
\psi_{i}
with

are
\log(2|H||H_{1}|^{t}/\epsilon)e(H_{1})^{2}
-separated.

We are now ready to prove the main result of the section.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We prove by induction on the size of
|H_{1}|
and on the radius
r_{H_{1}}
that for every
\epsilon>0
the theorem holds (with bounds depending only on
\epsilon
,
G
,
S
,
H
,
H_{1}
,
r_{H_{1}}
) for every set of conditions
(\theta_{i}+\psi_{i},E)
such that each
\theta_{i}
is a map from
G
to
H
, each
\psi_{i}
is a map from
G
to
H_{1}
, and the
\theta_{i}
are contained in a symmetric ball of radius at most
r_{H_{1}}
.

We again write
\theta_{i}=\gamma+\zeta_{i}
, where the
\zeta_{i}
have disjoint supports of size at most
r_{H_{1}}
and the same coefficient distribution.

We first describe the inductive step in the case where
r_{H_{1}}>0
and
H_{1}
is not the trivial subgroup. The cases where
H_{1}=\{e\}
or

can be dealt with by a degenerate version of the same argument, as we will explain at the end of the proof.

Suppose that the result holds for all
\epsilon>0
and for all pairs
(H,r)
such that
H
is a strict subgroup of
H_{1}
, or such that
H=H_{1}
and
r<r_{H_{1}}
. Let
\epsilon>0
. Then by Lemma
5.2
(and Remark
5.3
) we are done if we can find
t_{H_{1}}=\lceil\log(2|H|/\epsilon)|S|^{e(H)^{2}(\log(2|H|))^{2}}|H_{1}|\rceil
of the maps such that the maps
\theta_{i}
form a sunflower with centre
\gamma
and the maps
\psi_{i}
are
(r_{H_{1}}t_{H_{1}}+r_{H_{1}}^{\prime})
-separated for
r_{H_{1}}^{\prime}=\lceil\log(2|H||H_{1}|^{m}/\epsilon)e(H_{1})^{2}\rceil
. If we cannot find such a set of maps which is that large, then let
M
be a maximal set of such maps.

Then for each
i
, the support of the map
\zeta_{i}
contains a support element inside
Z
, or there exists a non-trivial character
\chi\in\hat{H}_{1}
such that
\chi\circ\psi_{i}
has support distance at most
r_{H_{1}}t_{H_{1}}+r_{H_{1}}^{\prime}
from a product of the form
\prod_{m\in M}\chi_{m}\circ\psi_{m}
, meaning that the set of
j
such that

x_{j}\to\chi_{i}(\psi_{ij}(x_{j}))(\prod_{m\in M}\chi_{i}(\psi_{mj}(x_{j})))^{-1}

is constant on

has size at most

. By Remark
3.2
we may assume that the first situation occurs for all

, or that the second situation occurs for all

.

Assume that we are in the first situation. Let

be the minimal size of a generating set of

. The number of homomorphisms from

to

is at most
|H|^{d}
, so the number of pairs
(\xi,z)
where
\xi
is a non-zero homomorphism from

to

and

is an element of

is at most
|H|^{d}|M|
. We now partition the set of indices

into sets

in such a way that for every pair
(\xi,z)
, the coefficient of the map

at

is equal to

, in our generalized sense of the word “coefficient”.

We claim now that each set
\{\theta_{i}:i\in I_{(\xi,z)}\}
is contained in a ball of radius at most
r_{H_{1}}-1
. To see this, pick
\xi
and
z
as above, and let
\gamma^{\prime}:G^{n}\to H
be the homomorphism that takes

to
\gamma+\xi(x_{q})
. Then each map

with

is a homomorphism of support size at most

, so the homomorphisms

live in a ball of radius at most

. By Remark

it suffices to approximate each set

, which in turn we can do by the inductive hypothesis.

Assume that we are instead in the second situation. There are at most
|H_{1}|^{t_{H_{1}}+1}
choices for

and the
\chi_{m}
with

, and therefore by Remark

we may assume that these choices are the same for each

. We write
\Phi
for the corresponding set of maps

. We now have that there exist a non-trivial character

.

form a symmetric sunflower of radius at most

In the case where

and

, which is the base case of the induction, the result is immediate. If only the first or only the second of these equalities hold, then the inductive step is as described above, but with only the second situation or only the first situation, respectively, rather than both of them.

It remains to remark that we assumed that there was a set

for all the conditions

in the collection. As usual, this is permissible by Remark

.

6
Polynomial conditions with restricted alphabets

The approximation results we have proved so far have concerned linear conditions. The aim of this section is to prove an approximation result for polynomial conditions. Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We shall call a map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V
a
polynomial map of degree at most

if the map

defined by

is a polynomial of degree at most

for each

. If

, then we can regard a polynomial map

as a

-tuple
(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{k})
of polynomials on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, as we did in the introduction, in which case the degree is simply the maximum degree of the
\phi_{i}
, but for most of the argument it will be convenient to think of polynomials in this more basis-free way. (However, we do not replace
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
by an arbitrary

-dimensional vector space, since we are considering sets of the form

, which are tied to the standard basis of

.)

Thus, we now define a
k
-dimensional condition with degree at most

to be a pair
(\phi,E)
, where
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V
for some
k
-dimensional vector space
V
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
\phi
is a polynomial map of degree at most
d
, and
E\subset V
. We shall prove Theorem
1.5
, which stated that for every
\epsilon>0
, every prime
p
, every subset
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, every positive integer
k
and every positive integer
d
there exists
K
such that every set of
k
-dimensional conditions with degree at most
d
on
S^{n}
can be
\epsilon
-approximated by a polynomial condition of degree at most
d
and dimension at most
K
.

As commented in the introduction, this result is a little different from our earlier approximation results, since we approximate by a single condition, but to pay for that we allow the dimension of that condition to be larger than the dimensions of the conditions we started with. It is not clear whether the natural direct generalization of our linear results is true – there does not seem to be an obvious analogue of the sunflower structure that played an important role in the proofs.

Given a
k
-dimensional space
V
and a polynomial map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V
of degree at most
d
, for each
0\leq i\leq d
let
W_{i}^{*}\subset V^{*}
be the space of all
a^{*}\in V^{*}
such that
a^{*}\phi
has degree at most
i
. We can take a basis of
W_{0}^{*}
, extend it to a basis of
W_{1}^{*}
, and so on. Let
V_{i}^{*}
be the subspace of
V^{*}
spanned by the basis vectors that belong to
W_{i}^{*}\setminus W_{i-1}^{*}
. (Since the basis is not uniquely determined, the subspaces
V_{i}^{*}
are not uniquely determined either, but this will not matter to us.) Let
k_{i}=\dim V_{i}^{*}
and note that
V^{*}=V_{0}^{*}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{d}^{*}
, so
k_{0}+k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}=k
. We shall call the sequence
(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
the
degree profile
of the polynomial
\phi
. Let
V=V_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{d}
be the corresponding decomposition of
V
– that is, we take the dual basis and the subspaces spanned by the corresponding subsets. Let
P_{i}
be the projection from
V
to
V_{i}
(that is, the coordinate projection with respect to this basis) and note that if
a^{*}
is a non-zero element of
V_{i}^{*}
, then
a^{*}P_{i}\phi
has degree exactly
i
.

We shall prove Theorem
1.5
by induction on the degree profile in the colex ordering. That is, we shall prove the result for polynomial conditions where the polynomials have a given degree profile
(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
assuming the result for polynomial conditions with degree profile
(k_{0}^{\prime},k_{1}^{\prime},\dots,k_{d}^{\prime})
such that there exists
i
for which
k_{i}^{\prime}<k_{i}
and
k_{j}^{\prime}=k_{j}
for every
j>i
.

The proof will appear somewhat simpler than several of the proofs in earlier sections. This is partly because allowing the dimension to increase gives us considerable extra flexibility, but the simplicity is also partly illusory, as the proof relies on the main result of
[
2
]
, which in turn relies on one of the main results of
[
4
]
.

The result from
[
2
]
concerns the equidistribution of high rank polynomials on sets of the form
S^{n}
, where
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
is non-empty. When
S=\mathbb{F}_{p}
it is a result of Green and Tao
[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
. Before stating it, let us define a notion of rank for polynomials on restricted alphabets. The definition we give here is less granular than the one we gave in the paper
[
2
]
, but it suffices for our purposes and will be slightly more convenient to use.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
d
be a nonnegative integer, and let
P:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a polynomial with degree at most
d
. We define the
degree-
d
rank

\rk_{d}P
of
P
as follows.

1.

If
d=0
then
\rk_{d}P=0
.

2.

If
d=1
then
\rk_{d}P
is the number of indices
i\in[n]
such that
a_{i}\neq 0
when we represent
P
in the form
P(x)=c+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}
, where
c,a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
– that is, the size of the support of the linear form that differs from
P
by an additive constant.

3.

If
d\geq 2
then
\rk_{d}P
is the smallest nonnegative integer
r
such that there exists a function
F:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
and polynomials
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
each with degree strictly smaller than
\deg P
such that
P=F(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})
.

If
S
is a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then the
degree-
d
rank of
P
with respect to
S
, denoted by
\rk_{d,S}P
, is
\min_{P_{0}}\rk_{d}(P-P_{0})
, where the minimum is taken over all polynomials
P_{0}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
P_{0}(S^{n})=\{0\}
. Equivalently, it is the minimum degree-
d
rank of any polynomial
Q
that agrees with
P
on
S^{n}
.

The next result is very similar to Theorem 1.4 of
[
2
]
and is an immediate consequence of it. It shows that the values of a high-rank polynomial are approximately equidistributed on sets of the form
S^{n}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

For every prime
p
, every non-empty subset
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, every positive integer
d<p
, and every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\tau
such that if
P:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
is any polynomial of degree
d
with
\rk_{d,S}(P)\geq\tau
, then
|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x\in S^{n}}\omega_{p}^{P(x)}|\leq\epsilon
.

We shall also simplify the argument in a superficial way by being less explicit about how various bounds depend on each other. Given a property
\kappa
of polynomial conditions such that every condition that satisfies
\kappa
has degree at most
d
, let us say that conditions that satisfy
\kappa
are
boundedly approximable on
S^{n}
, or simply that they are
approximable
, if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
K=K(\epsilon,\kappa,S)
such that every set of polynomial conditions that satisfy
\kappa
can be approximated by a polynomial condition of degree at most
d
and dimension at most
K
. We shall frequently make implicit use of the following lemma, which is very similar to Remark
3.2
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\kappa
be a property of polynomial conditions, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a family of properties of polynomial conditions. Suppose that there exists a constant
M=M(\kappa,S)
such that every set of conditions
\mathcal{C}
that satisfy
\kappa
is a union of at most
M
sets of conditions each satisfying a property of
\mathcal{P}
. If for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
, conditions that satisfy
\lambda
are approximable on
S^{n}
, then conditions that satisfy
\kappa
are approximable on
S^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a set of conditions that satisfy
\kappa
and write
\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{C}_{m}
, where
m\leq M
and the conditions in each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
satisfy
\lambda_{i}
. For each set
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, let
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
be a condition that
(\epsilon/m)
-approximates
\mathcal{C}_{i}
(or more precisely, the set
\{(\phi_{i},E_{i})\}
approximates
\mathcal{C}_{i}
), where
E_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k_{i}}
for some
k_{i}\leq K
. Then let
k=k_{1}+\dots+k_{m}
, let
\phi:S^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
be defined by
\phi(x)=(\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{m}(x))
, and let
E=E_{1}\times\dots\times E_{m}
. Then
x
satisfies
(\phi,E)
if and only if it satisfies
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
for each
i
. Also, if
x
satisfies
\mathcal{C}
but not
(\phi,E)
, then there is some
i
such that it fails to satisfy
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
but satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. For each
i
, the density of such
x
is at most
\epsilon/m
, so we are done by a union bound. ∎

If we have a set of conditions that we wish to approximate, this lemma allows us to partition it into a bounded number of pieces and approximate each piece. We have done this several times already in the paper, but from now on we shall not bother to give a careful justification each time we do so.

We shall also use the following more elaborate version of the principle, which relies heavily on the fact that we allow ourselves to increase the dimensions of conditions as we approximate. If
\kappa
is a property of polynomials, we shall say that a set of conditions satisfies
\kappa
if the corresponding polynomials satisfy
\kappa
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
S
be a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let
k
and
d
be positive integers, let
V
be a
k
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, let
\rho:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V
be a polynomial map of degree at most
d
, and let
\kappa
and
\lambda
be properties of polynomials that imply that they have degree at most
d
. Suppose that
k
is bounded above by a function of
p,S
and
d
. Suppose also that for every
y\in V
and every polynomial
\phi
that satisfies
\kappa
there is a polynomial
\psi
that satisfies
\lambda
such that
\phi
and
\psi
agree on the set
\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}:\rho(x)=y\}
. Finally, suppose that polynomial conditions that satisfy
\lambda
are approximable on
S^{n}
. Then polynomial conditions that satisfy
\kappa
are approximable on
S^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a set of conditions that satisfy
\kappa
. For each
y\in V
and each condition
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
, where
E
is a subset of a vector space
W
, let

E_{y}=\{(v,w)\in V\times W:v=y\implies w\in E\},

so that
((\rho,\phi),E_{y})
is the condition
\rho(x)=y\implies\phi(x)\in E
.

For each
y\in V
, let
\mathcal{C}_{y}
be the set of conditions
\{((\rho,\phi),E_{y}):(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}\}
. If
x
satisfies
(\phi,E)
then it clearly satisfies
((\rho,\phi),E_{y})
for every
y\in V
, and the converse is true as well, since if we let
y=\rho(x)
, then the fact that
x
satisfies
((\rho,\phi),E_{y})
guarantees that
\phi(x)\in E
, as noted above. Therefore, the satisfying sets of
\mathcal{C}
and
\bigcup_{y\in V}\mathcal{C}_{y}
are the same.

By Lemma
6.3
it is therefore sufficient to prove that each set
\mathcal{C}_{y}
of conditions is approximable. Let us fix
y\in V
, and for each
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
let
\tilde{\phi}
be a polynomial that satisfies
\lambda
and that agrees with
\phi
on
\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}:\rho(x)=y\}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{y}
be the set of conditions
(\tilde{\phi},E):(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}\}
and let
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{y}
be the set of conditions
\{((\rho,\tilde{\phi}),E_{y}):(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}_{y}\}
. Note that the satisfying set of
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{y}
is the same as that of
\mathcal{C}_{y}
.

Since the polynomials
\tilde{\phi}
satisfy
\lambda
, and polynomial conditions that satisfy
\lambda
are approximable,
\mathcal{D}_{y}
is approximable by a condition
(\theta,F)
. It follows that
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{y}
is approximable by the condition
((\rho,\theta),F_{y})
. ∎

We are now ready to begin the proof in earnest. The basic strategy is the same as it has been with our earlier proofs: either the conditions we wish to approximate contain a large set of “sufficiently independent” conditions, in which case the satisfying set has small density, or there is a small set of conditions that in some suitable sense “approximately generates” all the other conditions, in which case we can use Lemma
6.4
to reduce to a simpler case that is covered by an inductive hypothesis.

Let us begin with a standard condition for determining whether a function taking values in a
k
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is approximately equidistributed.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
X
be a finite set, let
p
be a prime, let
k
be a positive integer, let
V
be a
k
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and let
\phi:X\to V
. Then for every
y\in V
,

\mathbb{P}[\phi(x)=y]-p^{-k}|\leq p^{-k}\sum_{{a^{*}\in V^{*}}\atop{a^{*}\neq 0}}|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\omega_{p}^{a^{*}(\phi(x))}|.

Proof.

Note first that
\mathbb{P}[\phi(x)=y]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\mathbbm{1}_{y}(\phi(x))
, where we write
\mathbbm{1}_{y}
for the characteristic function of the singleton set
\{y\}
. By the Fourier inversion formula,

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\mathbbm{1}_{y}(\phi(x))=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\sum_{a^{*}\in V^{*}}\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{y}}(a^{*})\omega_{p}^{a^{*}(\phi(x))}=p^{-k}\sum_{a^{*}\in V^{*}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\omega_{p}^{a^{*}(\phi(x)-y)}.

The result follows.
∎

The above lemma immediately implies a version for several functions, but it is convenient to state it separately.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
X
be a finite set, let
p
be a prime, let
r
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
be positive integers, let
k=\sum_{i}k_{i}
, for each
i\leq r
let
V_{i}
be a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of dimension
k_{i}
, and let
\phi_{i}:X\to V_{i}
. Then for every
(y_{1},\dots,y_{r})\in V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{r}
,

|\mathbb{P}[\forall i\ \phi_{i}(x)=y_{i}]-p^{-r}|\leq p^{-r}\sum_{{a^{*}\in V_{1}^{*}\times\dots\times V_{r}^{*}}\atop{a^{*}\neq 0}}|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\omega_{p}^{\sum_{i}a_{i}^{*}(\phi_{i}(x))}|.

Proof.

We can apply the lemma to the map
\phi:X\to V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{r}
defined by
x\mapsto(\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x))
.
∎

A simple further consequence of this corollary and Theorem
6.2
is the following result.

Corollary 6.7
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, every pair of positive integers
d
and
k
, every prime
p
and every non-empty subset
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
there exist
R,\tau
such that for every
r\geq R
, every sequence
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
of vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\dim V_{i}\leq k
for each
i
, every sequence of polynomial maps
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}
of degree at most
d
with
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V_{i}
, and every sequence
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}
with
E_{i}
a proper subset of
V_{i}
, if the satisfying set of the conditions
(\phi_{i},E_{i})
has density greater than
\epsilon
, then there exist
a_{1}^{*},\dots,a_{r}^{*}
with
a_{i}^{*}\in V_{i}^{*}
, not all zero, such that
\sum_{i}a_{i}^{*}\phi_{i}
is a polynomial of degree
d^{\prime}\leq d
with
\rk_{d^{\prime},S}(\sum_{i}a_{i}^{*}\phi_{i})\leq\tau
.

Proof.

If
(1-p^{-k})^{R}\leq\epsilon/2
, then the density of the set
E_{1}\times\dots\times E_{r}
in
V_{1}\times\dots\times V_{r}
is at most
\epsilon/2
, so by Corollary
6.6
with
X=S^{n}
we find that

\mathbb{P}_{x\in S^{n}}[(\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x))\in E_{1}\times\dots\times E_{r}]\leq\epsilon/2+\sum_{{a^{*}\in V_{1}^{*}\times\dots\times V_{r}^{*}}\atop{a^{*}\neq 0}}|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\omega_{p}^{\sum_{i}a_{i}^{*}(\phi_{i}(x))}|.

Therefore, if the satisfying set of the conditions has density at least
\epsilon
, then there exists a non-zero
a^{*}\in V_{1}^{*}\times\dots\times V_{r}^{*}
such that

|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\omega_{p}^{\sum_{i}a_{i}^{*}(\phi_{i}(x))}|\geq\epsilon/2p^{rk}.

Let
\psi=\sum_{i}a_{i}^{*}\phi_{i}
and let
d^{\prime}
be the degree of
\psi
. By Theorem
6.2
there is a constant
\tau
depending only on
p,S,\epsilon,r
and
k
such that
\rk_{d^{\prime},S}(\psi)\leq\tau
, which proves the result.
∎

We now prove an easy result that will turn out to be the base case of an inductive argument. It also has a very similar structure to that of the main proof. Let us define the
support

Z(\phi)
of a linear map
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V
to be the set of coordinates on which
\phi
depends. That is, if
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
is the standard basis, then
Z(\phi)=\{i:\phi(e_{i})\neq 0\}
. If
\phi
is an affine map, then we define
Z(\phi)
to be the support of its linearization, and we also call it the support.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
s
and
k
be positive integers and let
S
be a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Then conditions of the form
(\phi,E)
where
\phi
is an affine map from
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
to some vector space
V
of dimension at most
k
,
E\subset V
, and
\phi
has support size at most
s
, are approximable on
S^{n}
.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction on
s
, observing that it is trivial when
s=0
. We now assume that the result holds for
s-1
and prove it for
s
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a set of
k
-dimensional affine conditions of support size at most
s
. We may assume that each
(\phi,E)
is such that there exists
x\in S^{n}
with
\phi(x)\notin E
, since we can throw away all conditions that do not satisfy this property.

Now let
\{(\phi_{1},E_{1}),\dots,(\phi_{r},E_{r})\}
be a maximal subset of
\mathcal{C}
such that the maps
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}
are disjointly supported. Then the events
\phi_{i}(x)\in E_{i}
are independent, and each holds with probability at most
1-2^{-s}
. Therefore, if
(1-2^{-s})^{r}<\epsilon
, we can approximate
\mathcal{C}
by a single condition
(\phi,\emptyset)
.

If
(1-2^{-s})^{r}\geq\epsilon
, then for every
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
the support of
\phi
intersects the union
Z
of the supports of
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}
. It follows that
\phi
agrees with a map of support size at most
s-1
on each set of the form
\{x:\forall j\in Z\ x_{j}=y_{i}\}
. By the inductive hypothesis and Lemma
6.4
we are done. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

There are at most
(d+1)^{k}
degree profiles that the polynomials can have, so by Lemma
6.3
we may assume that they all have the same degree profile. We may also throw away all conditions
(\phi,E)
where
E=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{k}
. As promised in the introduction to this section, we now prove the result by induction on the degree profile, with respect to the colex order.

Let
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a set of
k
-dimensional polynomial conditions
(\phi,E)
such that each polynomial
\phi
has degree profile
(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
. If the satisfying set of
\mathcal{C}
in
S^{n}
has density less than
\epsilon
, then we can approximate it with a single condition of the form
(\phi,\emptyset)
. Otherwise, Corollary
6.7
gives us
R
and
\tau
such that for any
r
polynomials
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}
involved in the conditions in
\mathcal{C}
, if
r>R
then there exist
a_{1}^{*},\dots,a_{r}^{*}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{*}\phi_{i}
has rank at most
\tau
. (By this we mean it has degree
d^{\prime}
for some
d^{\prime}\leq d
and
(d^{\prime},S)
-rank at most
\tau
.)

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}
be a maximal set of polynomials coming from the conditions in
\mathcal{C}
for which that is
not
the case, and suppose that
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V_{i}
. Then for every
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
with
\phi:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to V
there exist
a_{1}^{*},\dots,a_{r}^{*}
and
b^{*}
with
a_{i}^{*}\in V_{i}^{*}
and
b^{*}\in V^{*}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{*}\phi_{i}+b^{*}\phi
has rank at most
\tau
.

After a further application of Lemma
6.3
, we may assume that
(a_{1}^{*},\dots,a_{r}^{*},b^{*})
is the same for each
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
\psi
be the polynomial
\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{*}\phi_{i}
, so
\psi+b^{*}\phi
has rank at most
\tau
for each
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
.

Let us now fix
(\phi,E)\in\mathcal{C}
. On each set
\{x\in S^{n}:\psi(x)=y\}
, the polynomials
b^{*}\phi
and
\psi+b^{*}\phi-y
agree, so
b^{*}\phi
agrees with a polynomial of rank at most
\tau
. Let
V^{*}=V_{0}^{*}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{d}^{*}
be a decomposition as described at the beginning of the section, so
\dim V_{i}^{*}=k_{i}
and
w^{*}\phi
has degree exactly
i
for every
w^{*}\in V_{i}^{*}
. Let
b^{*}=b_{0}^{*}+b_{1}^{*}+\dots+b_{d}^{*}
with
b_{i}^{*}\in V_{i}^{*}
.

Then if
b^{*}\phi
has degree
d^{\prime}
, it follows that
b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\neq 0
and
b_{i}^{*}=0
for every
i>d^{\prime}
. Now pick
w\in V_{d^{\prime}}
such that
b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}(w)=1
. Then we can write

\phi(x)=b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\phi(x)w+(\phi(x)-b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\phi(x)w)

and the image of the polynomial map
\phi(x)-b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\phi(x)w
lies in the kernel of
b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}
.

Since
b^{*}\phi
agrees with a polynomial of degree
d^{\prime}
and rank at most
\tau
, and since
(b_{0}^{*}+\dots+b_{d^{\prime}-1}^{*})\phi
has degree less than
d^{\prime}
,
b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\phi
has rank at most
\tau+1
. We can therefore write it in the form
F(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t})
with
t\leq\tau+1
and with
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t}
all of degree less than
d^{\prime}
. This allows us to replace the condition
(\phi,E)
by a condition with a degree profile that comes earlier than
(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{d})
and that agrees with
\phi
on
\{x\in S^{n}:\psi(x)=y\}
. Let
\tilde{\phi}
be the polynomial
\phi-(b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\phi)w
, regarded as taking values in
\ker(b_{d^{\prime}}^{*})
, and note that
\tilde{\phi}
has degree profile
(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{d^{\prime}-1},k_{d^{\prime}}-1,k_{d^{\prime}+1},\dots,k_{d})
. Let
\iota:\ker(b_{d^{\prime}}^{*})\to V
be the inclusion map, and let
\theta:\ker(b_{d^{\prime}}^{*})\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{t}\to V
be the map
(u,a)\mapsto\iota u+aw
. Now let us take the polynomial
(\tilde{\phi},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t})
and let
H\subset\ker(b_{d^{\prime}}^{*})\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{t}
be the set
\{(v,u):\theta(v,F(u))\in E\}
.

Then
(\tilde{\phi},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t})(x)\in H
if and only if
\iota\tilde{\phi}(x)+F(\gamma_{1}(x),\dots,\gamma_{t}(x))\in E
, which is true if and only if
\phi(x)-b_{d^{\prime}}^{*}\phi(x)w+F(\gamma_{1}(x),\dots,\gamma_{t}(x))w\in E
, which is true if and only if
\phi(x)\in E
. Thus, the condition
(\phi,E)
is equivalent to the condition
((\tilde{\phi},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t}),H)
. But
\tilde{\phi}
has degree profile
(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{d^{\prime}-1},k_{d^{\prime}}-1,k_{d^{\prime}+1},\dots,k_{d})
, as already noted, and since each
\gamma_{i}
has degree less than
d^{\prime}
, the degree profile of
(\tilde{\phi},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t})
precedes that of
\phi
in colex order. Furthermore, since
t
is bounded independently of
n
, all the degrees in the degree profile of
(\tilde{\phi},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t})
are bounded independently of
n
. Hence we are done by the inductive hypothesis and Lemma
6.4
, once we have established the base case.

Recall that for polynomials of degree 1, we define the rank to be the size of the support of the linearization. So the inductive argument just given allows us to reduce to the case where all the polynomials are linear and have bounded support size. This case is dealt with by Lemma
6.8
.
∎

7
Conclusion and open questions

We finish by mentioning two questions to which we do not know the answers. One is the obvious one of improving the bounds in our approximation results. Even in the most basic approximation result, Proposition
1.3
, the question of the optimal bounds is not yet settled.

Question 7.1
.

Can we replace the bound
(p\log\epsilon^{-1})^{p}
by
O_{p}(\log\epsilon^{-1})
in Proposition
1.3
?

If this is indeed possible, then we may ask whether we can also take bounds
O(\log\epsilon^{-1})
in Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.4
, and Theorem
1.5
. In the case of Theorem
1.5
it would probably help to first have linear bounds
H_{p,d,S}(\epsilon)=O_{p,d,S}(\log\epsilon^{-1})
in Theorem
6.2
on the equidistribution of polynomials.

In a second direction, our first approximation result for linear forms
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
, Proposition
1.2
, involves internal approximation. We can also ask for an external approximation, where it also does not seem obviously unreasonable to require the conditions to belong to the original family of conditions. (In the case of internal approximations, it is not possible to add this additional requirement, as it would imply that the set of
x\in S^{n}
satisfying the approximating set of conditions is exactly the same as that satisfying the original set of conditions, which is not always possible, as can be seen with an example we gave earlier, where we take
S
to be
\{0,1\}
and the family of linear forms to be
\{(x_{1}+x_{i},\{0,1\}):i\in[2,n]\}
.)

.

Let
p

S

. There there exists a constant

\mathcal{F}
of mod-
p
conditions on
S^{n}
there is a subset
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{F}
such that the satisfying set of
\mathcal{G}

\epsilon
-externally approximates that of
\mathcal{F}
(meaning that the density of points that satisfy
\mathcal{G}
but not
\mathcal{F}
is at most
\epsilon
).

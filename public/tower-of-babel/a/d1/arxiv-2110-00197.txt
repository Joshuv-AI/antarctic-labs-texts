The 2-Selmer Group of S n S_{n} -number fields of even degree
Benjamin Breen

Abstract
. This paper is an extension of the work of Dummit and Voight on modeling the 2-Selmer group of number fields. We extend their model to S n S_{n} -number fields of even degree and develop heuristics on the difference in the 2-ranks between the class group and narrow class group.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Their are several extensions of the Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet heuristics to the distributions of narrow class groups. However, these extensions are mainly for odd degree number fields, in particular abelian
[
2
]
and
S_{n}
-number fields
[
7
]
. Predictions for narrow class groups of number fields of even degree are restricted to the case of real quadratic fields
[

In this paper, we provide heuristics on the relationship between the 2-torsion subgroup of the class group and the narrow class group for
S_{n}
-number fields of even degree. These heuristics stem from the 2-Selmer group of a number field
F

\Sel_{2}(F)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\alpha\in F^{\times}:(\alpha)=\mathfrak{a}^{2}\text{ for a fractional ideal }\mathfrak{a}\mbox{ of }F\}/(F^{\times})^{2}

and a study of the image of

where

and

are local signature spaces constructed from the Archimedean and 2-adic algebras of

S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sgn\Sel_{2}(F)
as
F
varies over
S_{n}
-number fields of even degree, from which we derive precise predictions on the difference between the 2-torsion subgroups of the class group and narrow class group.

Definition 2.2.1

2.3.
Bilinear forms

Let

•

\mathbf{I}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}
with the

\begin{pmatrix}1\end{pmatrix};

•

\mathbf{H}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}

Every finite dimensional vector space

\mathbb{F}_{2}
with a symmetric, nondegenerate, bilinear form

is isometric to an orthogonal direct sum of copies of

and

Let

contains a

\phi_{\mathrm{can}}\colon V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}

\phi_{\mathrm{can}}(w)=b(w,w)

. Since the bilinear form

is nondegenerate, we have a canonical isomorphism

given by
w\mapsto b(w,\_)
. The
canonical vector

is the unique vector that maps to
\phi_{\mathrm{can}}
under the isomorphism
V\simeq V^{*}
. Equivalently, it is the unique vector satisfying
b(w_{\mathrm{can}},w)=b(w,w)
for every vector
w\in V
. Up to isometry, the bilinear space

is determined by its dimension and the canonical vector.

2.4.
Relation to class groups

For an abelian group
A
, let
\rk_{2}A=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}A[2]
. There are four ray class groups and their corresponding class fields which are central to our investigation:

•

\Cl(F),H
— the class group and Hilbert class field;

•

\Cl^{+}(F),H^{+}
— the narrow class group and narrow Hilbert class field;

•

\Cl_{4}(F),H_{4}
— the ray class group and class field of modulus
(4)
;

•

\Cl^{+}_{4}(F),H^{+}_{4}
— the narrow ray class group and class field of modulus
(4)\infty
.

where
\infty
denotes the set of all real places. The 2-Selmer group of a number field is related to the narrow ray class group of modulus (4) by

\rk_{2}\Cl_{4}(F)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Sel_{2}(F).

Further, we have the following refinement of this relation in terms of the kernels of the various signature maps as follows:

\displaystyle\rk_{2}\Cl(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\ker(\sgn),

\displaystyle\rk_{2}\Cl^{+}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\ker(\sgn_{2}),

\displaystyle\rk_{2}\Cl_{4}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\ker(\sgn_{\infty}).

F_{p}
up to isomorphism over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with degree
n=2m
and such that each component field
F
of the local algebra satisfies
F[x]/(x^{2}-d)\simeq F\times F
or
F_{un}
. Observe that for each algebra
F_{p}\in\Sigma(2m,d)
then
(F_{p},p)\in S(2m)_{even}
.

Proposition 5.5.7
.

Let
\sigma\in S(2m)_{even}
and let
d\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}_{p}
be a fundamental discriminant. The mass of the local algebras
F_{p}\in\Sigma(2m,d)
with
(F_{p},p)=\sigma
is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{p}\in\Sigma(2m,d)\\
(F_{p},p)=\sigma\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(F_{p})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{p})}=\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{d}))^{m}}\cdot\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\Phi(\sigma))}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut(\Phi(\sigma))}.

Proof.

Same argument as
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
. If
F_{p}
is a local algebra with
(F_{p},p)=\sigma=(f_{1}^{2e_{1}},\dots,f_{r}^{2e_{r}})
then the
i^{th}
-component field is a local field
F_{i}
with inertia degree
f_{i}
and ramification index
2e_{i}
. Counting such local algebras up to isomorphism is equivalent to counting ordered pairs of local fields
F_{1}\times F_{2}\times\dots\times F_{r}
weighted by
\frac{1}{N}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{i})}
where
N
is the number of permutation of
\sigma
which preserve the
f_{i}^{2e_{i}}
.

For each component, the number of possible local fields
F_{i}
weighted inversely proportional to
\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{i})}\cdot\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(F_{i})}
is given by Lemma
5.5.4
as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[F_{i}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]=2e_{i}f_{i}\\
\Inertia_{F_{i}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=f_{i}\\
F_{i}[x]/(x^{2}-d)\text{ is unramified}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(F_{i})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{i})}=\frac{1}{f_{i}}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{f_{i}(e_{i}-1)}}\cdot\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{d}))^{e_{i}f_{i}}}

Hence the total mass of these local algebras is

\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{d}))^{m}}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{f_{i}}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{f_{i}(e_{i}-1)}}\right)

∎

Theorem 5.5.8
.

The mass of the local algebras in
\Sigma(2m,d)
is

\sum_{F_{p}\in\Sigma(2m,d)}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(F_{p})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{p})}=\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{d}))^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}q(k,m-k)p^{-k}.

Proof.

We start by rearranging the summation by clumping the algebras according to their splitting symbols and then applying Proposition
5.5.7
to get

\displaystyle\sum_{F_{p}\in\Sigma(2m,d)}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(F_{p})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{p})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S(m)}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}F_{p}\in\Sigma(2m,d)\\
(F_{p},p)=\Phi^{-1}(\sigma)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(F_{p})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(F_{p})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{d}))^{m}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S(m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut(\sigma)}\right).

By
[
8
, Proposition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3]
then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S(m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{p}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}q(k,m-k)p^{-k}.

∎

.

As

7.5.
Computations:
S_{4}
-number fields

A
quartic ring
is a commutative ring with
1
that is free
of rank 4 as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module. Each
S_{4}
-number field contains a unique maximal quartic ring: the ring of integers.
In
[
12
]
, Bhargava established a correspondence between quartic rings
R
and pairs
A,B
of integral ternary quadratic forms. The forms
A,B
can be represented by two
3\times 3
symmetric matrices

(A,B):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{pmatrix}2a_{11}&a_{12}&a_{13}\\
a_{12}&2a_{22}&a_{23}\\
a_{13}&a_{23}&2a_{33}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}2b_{11}&b_{12}&b_{13}\\
b_{12}&2b_{22}&b_{23}\\
b_{13}&b_{23}&2b_{33}\end{pmatrix}\right)\hskip 28.45274pta_{ij},b_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z},

up to equivalence by an action of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\times\text{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We developed an algorithm in Magma (available online at
[
4
]
) to sample
S_{4}
-number fields with large discriminant. For a bound
X>0
, we select a pair of matrices
(A,B)
whose coefficients are chosen uniformly at random from the interval
[-X,X]
. We perform a series of tests
[
11
, pages 69-71]
on the coefficients of
(A,B)
to confirm that the corresponding ring
R
is a maximal order in an
S_{4}
-number field. We discard any rings that fail these tests.

Let
F
be the field of fractions for one of the remaining rings. The number of real places of
F
corresponds to the points of intersection of the forms
A,B
over
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. If
F
is totally real, there is fundamental domain for the action of
\text{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\times\text{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
on
(A,B)
. We can perform an additional test
[
11
, pages 78-79]
to ensure that our matrices
(A,B)
are reduced with respect to this action. If
F
is not totally real, then we perform a slower check to make sure that we haven’t duplicated any fields. We repeat this process until we have collected a sufficiently large sample of fields.

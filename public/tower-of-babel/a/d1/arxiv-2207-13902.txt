On large Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariants of imaginary quadratic function fields
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let ℓ \ell be a prime number and q q be a power of ℓ \ell . Given an odd prime number p p and an imaginary quadratic extension F F of the rational function field 𝔽 q ​ ( T ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(T) , let λ p ​ ( F ) \lambda_{p}(F) denote the Iwasawa λ \lambda -invariant of the constant ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of F F . 1.
Introduction

Let
\ell
be a prime number and
q
be a power of
\ell
. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. An
imaginary quadratic field
extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is a degree
2
extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
in which the prime
\infty
is ramified. The question studied in this note is motivated by recent results of Ellenberg, Venkatesh and Westerland
[
EVW16
]
on the distribution of class groups of imaginary quadratic field extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. The story begins with predictions made for the distribution of class groups for imaginary quadratic number fields made by Cohen and Lenstra
[
CL84
]
, based in random matrix theory. The work of Cohen-Lenstra led to significant developments in the field of
arithmetic statistics
. In the number field context, the predictions are far from proven unconditionally. We consider function field analogues of such heuristics in positive characteristic. The aforementioned results of Ellenberg-Venkatesh-Westerland show that the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics in the function field context are true in the
large
q
-limit
. We refer the reader to
loc. cit.
or Theorem
4.1
for a precise statement of the result.

The purpose of this note is to shed light on Iwasawa theoretic invariants introduced and systematically studied by Leitzel
[
Lei70
]
and Rosen
[
Ros02
, Chapter 11]
. Let
F
be an imaginary quadratic field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
and
p
be an odd prime. The prime
p
will be fixed, however,
q
will be required to be large for our results to hold. For convenience of notation, set
\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
and choose an algebraic closure
\bar{\kappa}
. Denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the ring of
p
-adic integers and
\kappa_{\infty}^{(p)}
be the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\kappa
which is contained in
\bar{\kappa}
. The constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}
of
F
is the composite
F_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=F\cdot\kappa_{\infty}^{(p)}
. It is constant in the sense that it arises from a subextension of
\bar{\kappa}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
F_{n}
be the extension of
F
which is contained in
F_{\infty}
with Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{n}/F)
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. The main focus is the study of growth patterns of
p
-class groups in the tower of extensions

F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{n}\subset F_{n+1}\subset\dots.

Let
e_{n}
be the largest power of
p
that divides the class number of
F_{n}
. Leitzel and Rosen show that there are well-defined invariants
\lambda_{p}(F)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu_{p}(F)\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all large enough values of
n
,

e_{n}=\lambda_{p}(F)n+\nu_{p}(F).

In particular, the analogue of the
Iwasawa
\mu
-invariant
vanishes. We fix an odd prime
p
and study the distribution of
\lambda_{p}(F)
, where
F
is allowed to vary over all imaginary quadratic function field extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. The main result shows that given any fixed odd prime number
p
and integer
r>0
, there is a constant
c
depending only on
p
and
r
such that for all
q\geq c
, there is a positive proportion of imaginary quadratic field extensions
F
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
for which
\lambda_{p}(F)\geq r
. This result is entirely unconditional, we refer to Theorem
4.4
for a more detailed statement of the result.

We mention here that in the number field context some related questions for
\lambda
-invariants of imaginary quadratic fields were studied by Horie
[
Hor87
]
, Jochnowitz
[
Joc94
]
, Sands
[
San93
]
, Ellenberg-Jain-Venkatesh
[
EJV11
]
, Delbourgo-Knospe
[
DK22
]
, and the author of this note
[
Ray22
]
. We draw our attention in particular to the above mentioned work of Sands, who shows unconditionally that given an odd prime number
p
, there are infinitely many imaginary quadratic number fields
F
for which the
p
-primary
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda_{p}(F)
is
\geq 2
. In the function field context a much stronger result is obtained in this note.

Organization:
Including the introduction, this note has four sections. In section

Preliminaries

In this section, we set up some basic notation and introduce preliminary notions in function field arithmetic and Iwasawa theory of constant

-extensions. For an introduction to function field arithmetic, we refer to
[
Ros02

. For an introduction to the cyclotomic Iwasawa theory of number fields, we refer to
[

2.1.
Function fields and their class groups

Throughout, we fix a prime number
p
and a prime number
\ell
(that are not necessarily distinct). We assume without further mention that
p
is odd, however,
\ell=2
is allowed. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the ring of
p
-adic integers, i.e., the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. The Iwasawa theory of function fields is concerned with growth patterns in the
p
-primary parts of class groups in certain
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers
over a function field, which we now introduce.

Let
F
be a global function field of characteristic
\ell
and field of constants
\kappa=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This means that
\kappa
is algebraically closed in
F
and the field
F
is a finite extension of
\kappa(T)
. We note here in passing that the terminology stems from algebraic geometry, since
F
is the function field of a smooth, geometrically integral, projective algebraic curve defined over
\kappa
. There is a beautiful analogy between number fields and function fields, cf.
[
Ros02
]
for further details.

According to
[
Ros02
, Chapter 5]
, a
prime
of
F
is defined to be the maximal ideal of a discrete valuation ring
R
which is contained in
F
, and has fraction field
F
. For instance, for
a\in\kappa
, the corresponding prime of
\kappa(T)
is the maximal ideal of
\kappa[T]_{(T-a)}
. This corresponds to the point
a\in\mathbf{P}^{1}(\kappa)
. The prime
\infty
corresponds to the maximal ideal of
\kappa[1/T]_{(1/T)}
. The primes of
\kappa(T)
which correspond in this way to points of
\mathbf{P}^{1}(\kappa)
are called
rational primes
, since they correspond to
\kappa
-valued rational points. Let
P
be a prime of
F
, and
R
be the associated discrete valuation ring. The
degree of
P
is the dimension of
R/P
over
\kappa
. The prime
P
is said to be
rational
if has degree
1
. In this case, the prime corresponds to a
\kappa
-valued rational point on the associated projective curve
\mathfrak{X}
. More generally, a prime corresponds to a Galois orbit in
\mathfrak{X}(\bar{\kappa})
, and its degree is the cardinality of its orbit with respect to the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\kappa}/\kappa)
.

A
divisor
of
F
is a formal integral linear combination of primes of
F
. Two divisors are equivalent if they differ by a
principal divisor
. The
class group

\operatorname{Cl}(F)
is the group of all equivalence classes of divisors of degree
0
. We refer to
loc. cit.
for more precise definitions. The group
\operatorname{Cl}(F)
is finite (cf.
[
Ros02
]
) and the class number
h(F)
is the number of elements in
\operatorname{Cl}(F)
. Let
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F)
be the
p
-Sylow subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}(F)
and set
h_{p}(F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F)
.

An imaginary quadratic function field
F
is a function field with field of constants
\kappa
such that

•

there is a prescribed inclusion of
\kappa(T)
into
F
,

•

the degree of
F
over
\kappa(T)
is equal to
2
,

•

\infty
is ramified in
F
.

Let
F
be an imaginary quadratic field and denote by
\infty_{F}
the prime of
F
that lies above
\infty
. The ring of integers of
F
is the integral closure of the polynomial ring
\kappa[T]
in
F
and is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{O}_{F}
consists of the functions in
F
with no poles away from
\{\infty\}
, and thus
\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the ring of
S
-integers for
S=\{\infty\}
. Let
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
denote the class group associated to
\mathcal{O}_{F}
(cf.
[
HK90
, p.62]
for further details). Note that there is a surjective map
\operatorname{Cl}(F)\rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
with kernel generated by the divisor class of
\infty_{F}
(see
loc. cit.
). Thus for any odd prime
p
, denote by
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
the
p
-Sylow subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
; we find that
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
is naturally isomorphic to
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F)
. Let
L/F
be a finite extension in which
\infty_{F}
is totally inert. Identify the prime in
L
above
\infty_{F}
with
\infty_{F}
itself. We find that for any odd prime
p
,
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
is naturally isomorphic to
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(L)
. The Hilbert class field
H(L)
is defined to be the maximal abelian unramified extension of
L
in which
\infty_{F}
is totally split. There is a natural isomorphism
\operatorname{Gal}(H(L)/L)\simeq\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
, cf.
[
Ros87
,
HK90
]
. Let
p
be an odd prime number and
H_{p}(L)
denote the maximal abelian pro-
p
unramified extension of
L
in which
\infty_{F}
is completely split. Then by the above discussion, there is a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Gal}(H_{p}(L)/L)\simeq\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(L).

2.2.
Class group towers over constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions

Let
F
be an imaginary quadratic field and
\infty_{F}
be the prime above
\infty
. The field of constants is
\kappa
, and
\bar{\kappa}
is the algebraic closure of
\kappa
in
\bar{F}
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, let
\kappa_{n}\subset\bar{\kappa}
be the constant extension of
\kappa
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa_{n}/\kappa)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that
p
is an odd prime which is not necessarily distinct from
\ell
. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the ring of
p
-adic integers and set
\kappa_{n}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\kappa_{p^{n}}
,
F_{n}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=F\cdot\kappa_{n}^{(p)}
. When there is no cause for confusion, we shall simply set
F_{n}
to denote
F_{n}^{(p)}
. The tower of function field extensions

F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{n}\subset

is called the
constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
over
F
. The union
F_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n}F_{n}
is the
constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
of
F
. We note that the Galois group
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\infty}/F)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Furthermore,
\infty
is totally inert in
F_{\infty}
. Let
L_{n}
denote the
p
-Hilbert class field
H_{p}(F_{n})
and set
L_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\cup_{n\geq 1}L_{n}
. Denote by
X_{n}
the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{n}/F_{n})
and set
\mathfrak{X}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\infty}/F_{\infty})
. The group
X_{n}
is naturally identified with the
p
-primary class group
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})
. Given
m\geq n
, there is a natural map
X_{m}\rightarrow X_{n}
, which is given by the following composite

X_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L_{n}\cdot K_{m}/K_{m})\rightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L_{n}/L_{n}\cap K_{m})\rightarrow X_{m}.

Since
\infty
is completely split in the extension
L_{n}/K_{n}
and it is completely inert in
K_{m}/K_{n}
, it follows that
L_{n}\cap K_{m}=K_{n}
. Therefore, the map
X_{m}\rightarrow X_{n}
is surjective.

Denote by
\Gamma
the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\infty}/F)
and fix a topological generator
\gamma
in
\Gamma
. The Iwasawa algebra is defined as follows

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}].

Setting
T
to denote
(\gamma-1)
, we identify
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. Let
G
be the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\infty}/F)
and note that
G/\mathfrak{X}
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
. There is a natural action of
\Gamma
on
\mathfrak{X}
; for
g\in\Gamma
, pick a lift
\tilde{g}
of
g
to
G
, and for
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, set
g\cdot x
to denote
\tilde{g}x\tilde{g}^{-1}
. Note that
g\cdot x
is contained in
\mathfrak{X}
since
\mathfrak{X}
is a normal subgroup of
G
, and also is independent of the choice of lift
\tilde{g}
since
\mathfrak{X}
is abelian. Via this action,
\mathfrak{X}
is a module over the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
.

3.
Iwasawa theory of constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions

In this section, we study the algebraic structure of the
\Lambda
-module
\mathfrak{X}
which was introduced in the previous section. Such results are analogous to the number field case, and in the function field case, some of the results may be known to experts. However, we find it of convenience to document them here. Let
D\subset G
be the decomposition group of
\infty_{F}
. Since
\infty_{F}
is totally inert in
F_{\infty}
and
\infty_{F}
is totally split in the extension
L_{\infty}/F_{\infty}
, the natural map
D\rightarrow\Gamma=G/X
is an isomorphism. Hence, we find that
G=DX
.

Let
\sigma
be the topological generator of
D
which maps to
\gamma\in\Gamma
w.r.t. the isomorphism
D\simeq\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the closure of the commutator subgroup of
G
. Then,
G^{\prime}=T\mathfrak{X}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
Was97
, Lemma 13.14]
; we provide details here. Note that an identification of
D
with
\Gamma
has been made. Let
a=\alpha x
and
b=\beta y
be elements of
G
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\Gamma
and
x,y\in\mathfrak{X}
. Given
\eta\in\Gamma
and
v\in\mathfrak{X}
, set
v^{\eta}
to denote
\eta v\eta^{-1}
. A straightforward calculation shows that the commutator of
a
and
b
is given by

aba^{-1}b^{-1}=(x^{\alpha})^{1-\beta}(y^{\beta})^{\alpha-1}.

Setting
\beta=1
and
\alpha=\gamma
, we find that
T\cdot y=y^{\gamma-1}
is contained in
G^{\prime}
. Hence, we find that
T\mathfrak{X}
is contained in
G^{\prime}
.

For the other inclusion, it suffices to observe that
(x^{\alpha})^{1-\beta}
and
(y^{\beta})^{\alpha-1}
are contained in
T\mathfrak{X}
. This clear, since both
1-\beta
and
\alpha-1
are in the augmentation ideal of
\Lambda
, which is generated by
T
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

With respect to notation above, we have the following isomorphism

X_{n}\simeq\frac{\mathfrak{X}}{\left((1+T)^{p^{n}}-1\right)\mathfrak{X}}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
Was97
, Lemma 13.15]
, and the role of the inertia group in the number field context is interchanged with the group
D
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

With respect to notation above,
\mathfrak{X}
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module.

Proof.

By Nakayama’s lemma, it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{M}\mathfrak{X}
is finite, where
\mathfrak{M}=(p,T)
is the maximal ideal of
\Lambda
. According to Proposition
3.2
,
\mathfrak{X}/T\mathfrak{X}
is isomorphic to
X_{0}
, hence is finite. The result follows.
∎

A map of
\Lambda
-modules is a
pseudo-isomorphism
if its kernel and cokernel are finite. Given a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module
M
, there is a pseudo-isomorphism

M\longrightarrow\Lambda^{r}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket}{p^{\mu_{i}}}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket}{f_{i}(T)^{\lambda_{i}}}\right).

Here
f_{i}(T)
are irreducible and distinguished polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. The
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants are defined by

\text{setting }\mu(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\text{ if }s>0,\\
0\text{ if }s=0,\end{cases}\text{ and, }\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\operatorname{deg}(f_{i})\text{ if }t>0,\\
0\text{ if }t=0.\end{cases}

We denote by
\mu_{p}(F)
and
\lambda_{p}(F)
the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of
\mathfrak{X}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mu=\mu_{p}(F)
and
\lambda=\lambda_{p}(F)
, then there is an integer
\nu
such that

e_{n}=p^{n}\mu+n\lambda+\nu

for all values
n\gg 0
.

Proof.

The result follows directly from
[
Was97
, Proposition 13.19, Lemma 13.21]
and Proposition
3.2
.
∎

The following result of Leitzel and Rosen shows that the
\mu
-invariant is always equal to
0
for constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions.

Theorem 3.5

(Leitzel, Rosen)
.

With respect to notation above, the module
\mathfrak{X}
is torsion over
\Lambda
, with
\mu_{p}(F)=0
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{m})
be the natural map for
m\geq n
. This map is injective.

Proof.

Recall that
F_{n}
was the composite
F\cdot\kappa_{n}^{(p)}=F\cdot\kappa_{n}^{(p)}(T)
. Since
\infty
is unramified in
\bar{\kappa}(T)
and totally ramified in
F
, it follows that
F
and
\kappa_{m}^{(p)}(T)
are linearly disjoint over
\kappa_{n}^{(p)}(T)
. Therefore, there are isomorphisms

\operatorname{Gal}(F_{m}/F_{n})\simeq\operatorname{Gal}\left(\kappa_{m}^{(p)}(T)/\kappa_{n}^{(p)}(T)\right)\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa_{m}^{(p)}/\kappa_{n}^{(p)}).

Therefore (by Hilbert’s theorem 90), we have that
H^{1}\left(F_{m}/F_{n},(\kappa_{m}^{(p)})^{\times}\right)=0
. It suffices to show that the kernel of
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{m})
is contained in
H^{1}\left(F_{m}/F_{n},(\kappa_{m}^{(p)})^{\times}\right)
.

Let us define a map

\Phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{m})\right)\longrightarrow H^{1}\left(F_{m}/F_{n},(\kappa_{m}^{(p)})^{\times}\right).

Given a divisor class
[D]
in
\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})\rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{m})\right)
represented by a divisor
D
, and
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F_{m}/F_{n})
, we find that
\sigma[D]-[D]=0
. We have that
D=\operatorname{div}(\alpha)
for a non-zero element
\alpha\in F_{m}
. Therefore,
\alpha^{\sigma}/\alpha
has trivial divisor, and hence lies in
\mathcal{O}_{F_{m}}^{\times}=(\kappa_{m}^{(p)})^{\times}
. We set
\Phi(D)(\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\alpha^{\sigma}/\alpha
and show that
\Phi
is injective. Suppose that
\Phi(D)=0
. Then, there is
\epsilon\in(\kappa_{m}^{(p)})^{\times}
such that for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F_{m}/F_{n})
,

\alpha^{\sigma}/\alpha=\epsilon^{\sigma}/\epsilon.

Replacing
\alpha
by
\alpha/\epsilon
, we find that
\alpha^{\sigma}=\alpha
for all
\sigma
. Thus,
\alpha
must belong to
F_{n}
, and the ideal class of
D
in
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F_{n})
is trivial.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

The module
\mathfrak{X}

Proof.

The result follows from Lemma

by an argument identical to that of

4.
Proof of the main results

Let
p
and
\ell
be prime numbers and assume that
p
is odd. Let
q
be a power of
\ell
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
the field of rational functions in one variable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this section, we recall the main result of
[
EVW16
]
and derive consequences for the distribution of the
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda_{p}(F)
for the family of imaginary quadratic field extensions
F
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of imaginary quadratic function field extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
ordered by disciminant. For
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, set
\mathcal{F}_{q}(x)
to be the subset of
\mathcal{F}
consisting of imaginary quadratic fields with discriminant
\leq x
. Let
A
be a finite abelian
p
-group, and denote by
\mathcal{F}_{q}(x;A)
the subset of
\mathcal{F}(x)
consisting of imaginary quadratic fields
F
with
\operatorname{Cl}_{p}(F)
isomorphic to
A
. Set
\delta^{\pm}_{A}(q)
to denote the upper and lower densities defined as follows

\delta^{+}_{A}(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}_{q}(x;A)}{\#\mathcal{F}_{q}(x)}\text{ and }\delta^{-}_{A}(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}_{q}(x;A)}{\#\mathcal{F}_{q}(x)}.

Theorem 4.1

(Ellenberg-Venkatesh-Westerland)
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
A
be a finite abelian
p
-group. Then, as
q\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
goes to
\infty
, the densities
\delta^{+}(q)
and
\delta^{-}(q)
both converge to
\frac{\prod_{i\geq 1}\left(1-q^{-i}\right)}{\#\operatorname{Aut}(A)}
.

Given any finite abelian group
A
, the
p
-rank is the dimension of
A/pA
over
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\delta_{r}^{-}(q)
be the lower density of imaginary quadratic extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
for which the
p
-rank of the class group is
\geq r
. We have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
r>0
be an integer. Then, there is a constant
c(p,r)>0
depending only on
p
and
r
such that for all values of
q>c(p,r)
satisfying
q\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
, we have that

\delta_{r}^{-}(q)>0
.

Proof.

Let
A
be any choice of abelian
p
-group with
p
-rank
\geq r
. Then, by Theorem
4.1
, we find that
\delta_{A}^{-}(q)>0
for sufficiently large values of
q\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
. Clearly,
\delta_{r}^{-}(q)\geq\delta_{A}^{-}(q)
since
A
has
p
-rank
\geq r
, and the result follows.
∎

Let
r
be a positive integer and
\delta^{-}(q,\lambda\geq r)
be the lower density of imaginary quadratic fields with
\lambda_{p}(F)\geq r
. More precisely, let
\mathcal{F}_{q}(x;\lambda\geq r)
be the subset of
\mathcal{F}_{q}(x)
consisting of all
F
such that
\lambda_{p}(F)\geq r
. Then,
\delta^{-}(q,\lambda\geq r)
is given by the following lower limit

\delta^{-}(q,\lambda\geq r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{F}_{q}(x;\lambda\geq r)}{\#\mathcal{F}_{q}(x)}.

Denote by
r_{p}(F)
the
p
-rank of
\operatorname{Cl}(F)
.
Before proving the main Theorem, we establish a relationship between
\lambda_{p}(F)
and
r_{p}(F)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
F
be an imaginary quadratic field. Then, we have that

\lambda_{p}(F)\geq r_{p}(F)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=(p,T)
be the maximal ideal of
\Lambda
and set
g_{p}(F)
to denote the dimension of
\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{M}
over
\Lambda/\mathfrak{M}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Since by Theorem
3.7
,
\mathfrak{X}
does not contain any non-trivial finite
\Lambda
-submodules, we have therefore by
[
Mat07
, Lemma 2.2]
that
\mu_{p}(F)+\lambda_{p}(F)\geq g_{p}(F)
. Since the
\mu
-invariant vanishes, we find that
\lambda_{p}(F)\geq g_{p}(F)
. Since
\mathfrak{X}
surjects onto
X_{0}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{M}
surjects onto
X_{0}/p
; the action of
T
on
X_{0}
being trivial. Therefore,
g_{p}(F)\geq r_{p}(F)
, and thus the result follows.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
r>0
be an integer. Then, there is a constant
c(p,r)>0
depending only on
p
and
r
such that for all values of
q>c(p,r)
satisfying
q\not\equiv 1\mod{p}
, we have that
\delta^{-}(q,\lambda\geq r)>0
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
4.3
that
\delta^{-}(q,\lambda\geq r)\geq\delta_{r}^{-}(q)
, and the result thus follows from Corollary
4.2
.
∎

On the ℒ {\mathcal{L}} -invariant of the adjoint of a weight one modular form
Marti Roset, Victor Rotger and Vinayak Vatsal

Abstract
. The purpose of this article is proving the equality of two natural ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariants attached to the adjoint representation of a weigth one cusp form, each defined by purely analytic, respectively algebraic means. The proof departs from Greenberg’s [ Gr1 ] definition of the algebraic ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariant as a universal norm of a canonical ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} associated to the representation. We relate it to a certain 2 × 2 2\times 2 regulator of p p -adic logarithms of global units by means of class field theory, which we then show to be equal to the analytic ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariant computed in [ RR ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
g=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{1}(N,\eta)
be a normalized newform of weight one, level
N
and nebentype character
\eta
, with Fourier coefficients in a finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
p
be a rational prime not dividing
N
.
The purpose of this article is proving the equality of two natural
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants that may be associated to the adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(g)
of
g
, each defined by purely analytic, respectively algebraic means.

That these two
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants should be equal was already conjectured by Greenberg
[
Gr1
]
and is indeed natural to expect in view of the main conjecture for
\mathrm{ad}^{0}(g)
, which still remains unsolved in this scenario.

To put our question in context, recall that a problem of the same flavour already takes place for modular forms of higher weight and level divisible by
p
, where several, a priori inequivalent
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants had been introduced by Mazur-Tate-Teitelbaum, Breuil, Coleman, Fontaine and Mazur, Darmon and Orton: cf.
[
Cz
]
for an account of this fascinating subject; nowadays all these
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants in weight
k\geq 2
are known to be equal thanks to the work of many authors. Ever more germane to our setting, the analogous question for the adjoint of cusp forms of weight
k\geq 2
was resolved by the work of Citro and Dasgupta (
[
Ci
]
,
[
Da
]
) by methods that are very different from ours. The main substantial difference is that the
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants arising in the above scenarios take place at a central critical point and are of local nature as they only depend on the restriction to
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
of the underlying Galois representation, while the
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariant of this note is global and the associated
L
-function has no critical points. Our setting is rather closer in spirit to Benois’ fundamental work on
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants at non-central critical points: cf.
[
Be
]
and Horte’s recent Ph.D thesis
[
Ho
]
, although we do not take this approach in our article.

In order to state explicitly our main theorem, fix embeddings
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, let
\tau\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
be complex conjugation and let
I_{p}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the corresponding inertia and decomposition subgroups, respectively. Denote by

\varrho_{g}:\mathrm{Gal\,}(H_{g}/\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{g})\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(L)

the odd Artin representation associated to
g
, where
H_{g}
is a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
V_{g}
is a
2
-dimensional vector space over
L
.

Set
g^{*}:=g\otimes\eta^{-1}=\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{\tau}_{n}q^{n}\in S_{1}(N,\bar{\eta})
and note that
V_{g^{*}}
is the contragredient dual of
V_{g}
. Conjugation by
\varrho_{g}
gives rise to a linear action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
on
\mathrm{End}(V_{g})\simeq V_{g}\otimes V_{g}^{*}
and we denote by
\ad^{0}(g)
the
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariant subspace of
\mathrm{End}(V_{g})
corresponding to null-trace endomorphisms. Let

\varrho_{\mathrm{ad}^{0}(g)}:\mathrm{Gal\,}(H/\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\mathrm{ad}^{0}(g))\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{3}(L)

be the associated
adjoint
representation. Here
H
is a Galois extension sitting in
H_{g}
.

We begin by explaining the analytic side of the story. Label and order the roots of the
p
th Hecke polynomial of
g
as
X^{2}-a_{p}X+\eta(p)=(X-\alpha)(X-\beta)
. Let

g_{\alpha}(q)=g(q)-\beta g(q^{p})

denote the
p
-stabilization of
g
on which the Hecke operator
U_{p}
acts with eigenvalue
\alpha
. Note that the roots of the
p
th Hecke polynomial of
g^{*}
are
1/\alpha
,
1/\beta
so we can also consider the
p
-stabilization
g^{*}_{1/\beta}
.

Let
L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),s)
be Hida-Schmidt’s
p
-adic
L
-function associated to the adjoint of
g_{\alpha}
. It has an exceptional zero at
s=1
. One way of seeing this is as follows: By considering
p
-adic families through
g_{\alpha}
and
g_{1/\beta}^{*}
, Hida constructed a
p
-adic
L
-function attached to the representation space
\mathrm{End}(V_{g})
, which will be denoted by
L_{p}(g,g^{*},s)
, that is analytic at
s=1

[
Hi
]
. Moreover, the work of Dasgupta on the factorization of
p
-adic
L
-functions implies that

L_{p}(g,g^{*},s)=\zeta_{p}(s)L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),s),

where
\zeta_{p}(s)
is the
p
-adic zeta function
[
Da
]
. Since
\zeta_{p}(s)
has a simple pole at
s=1
it follows that
L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),1)=0
.
It is thus natural to define the analytic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant of
\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha})
as

(1)

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha})):=L_{p}^{\prime}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),1),

where
L_{p}^{\prime}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),s)
is the the first derivative of
L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),s)
with respect to
s
.

Enlarge
L
if necessary, so that it contains
all Fourier coefficients of
g_{\alpha}
.
We assume from now on that
L
may be embedded in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. This hypothesis is not substantial and one could easily dispense with it by working throughout in
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, at the cost of making the notations slightly more heavy and adding no actual new ideas in the proof.

Consider the following hypothesis on
g
:

(A1)

g
is
p
-distinguished, i.e.
\alpha\neq\beta\,(\mathrm{mod}\,p)
,

(A2)

\varrho_{g}
is not induced from a character of a real quadratic field in which
p
splits and

(A3)

the reduction of
\varrho_{g}
mod
p
is irreducible.

Assuming Hypothesis (A1), (A2) and (A3), Rivero and the second author computed
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
as an element of

\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/L^{\times}
in terms
p
-adic logarithms of global units and
p
-units of
H
. This expression can be found in Theorem A’ of
[
RR
]
and will be recalled in §
2
.

Now we look at the algebraic side of the theory.
Assuming that
g
is
p
-regular (i.e.
\alpha\neq\beta
) and Hypothesis (A2) stated above, it is possible to describe the existence of an exceptional zero in terms of the representation space
V=\ad^{0}(g)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. As we will see, this is mainly because
V
contains a line where
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
acts trivially. Moreover, following Greenberg
[
Gr1
]
one can attach an algebraic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant, attached to
V
and
\alpha
, arising from a certain non-zero global cohomology class of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},V)
. The definition is in terms of universal norms of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
determined by this class. We will call this invariant Greenberg’s
\mathcal{L}
-invariant and we will denote it by
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
.

As we state below explicitly, the main theorem of this note is that, under (A1-2-3) and a minor additional assumption, the above two
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariants are equal in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\,(\mbox{mod }L^{\times})
. The hint that motivated us to look into this question was an old calculation of Greenberg in
[
Gr2
]
, that was explained by the third author to the other two during a visit to Barcelona. In the very particular case where
g\in S_{1}(23,\eta)
is the theta series of an unramified Hilbert class character of
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})
and
p=23
, Greenberg defined and computed an invariant associated to
g
which remained largely mysterious and adhoc. This note was born with the aim of placing this calculation into a more general and conceptual framework.

The formula Greenberg obtained was the following. Let
H
be the Hilbert class field of
K
, which is the splitting field of
X^{3}-X+1
. Note that
\mathrm{Gal\,}(H/\mathbb{Q})\simeq S_{3}
and let
r
be a generator of
\mathrm{Gal\,}(H/K)
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
be the prime of
H
above
p
singled out by the embedding
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and let
H_{1}
be the subfield of
H
fixed by the corresponding decomposition subgroup at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
. We have that
H_{1}
is a cubic extension with one real embedding and a pair of complex embeddings. Let
\varepsilon\in{\mathcal{O}}_{H_{1}}^{\times}
be a fundamental unit of
H_{1}
and let
\pi
be a generator of the prime ideal of
H_{1}
below
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
. Then, in
[
Gr2
, page 230]
, Greenberg computed an universal norm arising from his study of the Selmer group of a Galois representation naturally associated to
g
, with the following explicit output (the notation on the left-hand side is ours):

(2)

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))=3\left(\log_{p}(\pi)-\frac{\log_{p}(r(\pi)/r^{2}(\pi))}{\log_{p}(r(\varepsilon)/r^{2}(\varepsilon))}\log_{p}(\varepsilon)\right).

Moreover, this calculation relates
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g))
with the characteristic ideal of the Pontryagin dual of a Selmer group attached to a concrete filtration of
V
(which depends on
\alpha
). This is also explained in Remark 4.3 of the recent paper
[
GV
]
of Greenberg and the third author and, for a different setting, in Proposition 4 of
[
Gr1
]
.

Even though the case mentioned above is not considered in our setting, the shape of the expression for
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
of
[
RR
, Theorem A’ ]
is strikingly similar (
2
). This fact, together with the theoretical evidence that
L_{p}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),s)
should be equal to the characteristic ideal mentioned above, suggested that the equality of this two
\mathcal{L}
-invariants should hold in greater generality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
g
satisfies Hypothesis (A1-2-3) mentioned above. In addition, if
g
is an exotic form assume that
\alpha\neq-\beta
. Then,

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))\equiv\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))\quad(\mathrm{mod}\,L^{\times}).

The strategy of the proof consists in extending Greenberg’s calculation presented above: we compute
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
modulo
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
in terms of global units and
p
-units of
H
. Then, we compare the result with the formula given in Theorem A’ of
[
RR
]
in order to prove the theorem.

2.
The analytic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant

Consider the notation given in §
1
. We introduce the necessary tools to state Theorem A’ of
[
RR
]
. This theorem allows us to express
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
in terms of global units and
p
-units of
H

Note that, as
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-modules, we have

(3)

\ad^{0}(g)=L\oplus L^{\alpha/\beta}\oplus L^{\beta/\alpha},

where each line is characterized by the property that the arithmetic Frobenius
\mathrm{Fr}_{p}\in G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
acts on it with eigenvalue
1
,
\alpha/\beta
and
\beta/\alpha
, respectively. Recall that this decomposition is canonical even when
\alpha/\beta=\beta/\alpha
(see §2 of
[
BD
]
for a detailed explanation of the decomposition stated in (

)).

Definition 2.1
.

Following the decomposition given in (
3
), let
e_{1},e_{\alpha/\beta},e_{\beta/\alpha}
be generators of the lines
L,\ L^{\alpha/\beta},\ L^{\beta/\alpha}
respectively. Clearly
\{e_{1},e_{\alpha/\beta},e_{\beta/\alpha}\}
is a basis of
\ad^{0}(g)
.

Now we define the global units and
p
-units that will appear in the expression of
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
. For that we neeed to study the space

\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ad^{0}(g),{\mathcal{O}}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes L).

By Minkowski’s proof of Dirichlet’s unit theorem we have that

\dim_{L}\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ad^{0}(g),{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{\times}\otimes L)=1,\quad\dim_{L}\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ad^{0}(g),{\mathcal{O}}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes L)=2.

Fix a generator
u
of
\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ad^{0}(g),{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{\times}\otimes L)
and
v\in\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ad^{0}(g),{\mathcal{O}}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes L)
such that
\{u,v\}
is a basis of
\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\ad^{0}(g),{\mathcal{O}}_{H}[1/p]^{\times}/p^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes L)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Following the notation above, define

u_{1}=u(e_{1}),u_{\alpha/\beta}=u(e_{\alpha/\beta}),u_{\beta/\alpha}=u(e_{\beta/\alpha}),v_{1}=v(e_{1}),v_{\alpha/\beta}=v(e_{\alpha/\beta}),v_{\beta/\alpha}=v(e_{\beta/\alpha}).

By construction we have
u_{1},v_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
and,

\mathrm{Fr}_{p}(u_{\alpha/\beta})=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}u_{\alpha/\beta},\quad\mathrm{Fr}_{p}(v_{\alpha/\beta})=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}v_{\alpha/\beta},\quad\mathrm{Fr}_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}u_{\beta/\alpha},\quad\mathrm{Fr}_{p}(v_{\beta/\alpha})=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}v_{\beta/\alpha}.

Let

\log_{p}:H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{\times}\otimes L\longrightarrow H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\otimes L

denote the usual
p
-adic logarithm. Here,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
is the prime of
H
singled out by the embeding
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}
is the completion of
H
at this prime.

Recall the definition of the analytic
{\mathcal{L}}
-invariant.

Definition 2.3
.

Define the analytic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant attached to
g
and
\alpha
by

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha})):=L_{p}^{\prime}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),1),

where
L_{p}^{\prime}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}),1)
is the derivative of Hida-Schmidt’s
p
-adic
L
-function associated to the adjoint of
g_{\alpha}
at
s=1
.

The following result was proved as Theorem A’ of
[
RR
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
g
satsifies the hypothesis (A1-2-3) stated in the introduction. Following the notation above, we have

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))\equiv\frac{\log_{p}(u_{1})\log_{p}(v_{\beta/\alpha})-\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})\log_{p}(v_{1})}{\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\,L^{\times}).

Proof.

See Theorem A’ of
[
RR
]
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The elements
e_{1},e_{\alpha/\beta},e_{\beta/\alpha}
are well defined up to a constant. Similarly, if
\{u,v\}
is a pair as above any other possible pair is of the form
\{x_{u}u,x_{v}u+y_{v}v\}
with
x_{u},y_{v}\in L^{\times}
and
x_{v}\in L
. From here it is a computation to check that the right hand side of the expression for
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{an}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
is well defined modulo

.

3.
Greenberg’s
\mathcal{L}
-invariant

Consider the same notation as in §
1
. Following
[
Gr1
]
, in this section we define Greenberg’s
\mathcal{L}
-invariant
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
attached to
g\in S_{1}(N,\eta)
,
\alpha
and
p
, a rational prime such that
p\nmid N
. We then relate
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
to a global
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
H
which will be the crucial ingredient in later sections for computing it.

Recall that
g
has associated the Artin representation
\varrho_{g}
and its adjoint is

\varrho_{\ad^{0}(g)}:\mathrm{Gal\,}(H/\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\ad^{0}(g))\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{3}(L),

where
H
is a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
and
L
is a sufficiently large finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that we assumed could be embedded in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Note that since
p\nmid N
we have that
p
is unramified in
H
. We begin stating the hypothesis we make about
g
and introducing some notation. Suppose that:

(B1)

g
is
p
-regular, i.e.
\alpha/\beta\neq 1
, and

(B2)

\varrho_{g}
is not induced from a character of a real quadratic field in which
p
splits.

These hypothesis are less restrictive than Hypothesis (A1-2-3) made in the introduction in order to apply Theorem A’ of
[
RR
]
.

Let
V
be a
p
-adic realization of
\ad^{0}(g)
, i.e.
V=\ad^{0}(g)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
be the prime of
H
above
p
singled out by the embedding
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Let
\Sigma=\{p,\infty\}\cup\mathrm{Ram}(V)
, where
\mathrm{Ram}(V)
is the set of rational primes that ramify in
\ad^{0}(g)
and let
\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}
be the maximal extension of
\mathbb{Q}
unramified outside
\Sigma
(note that

). Let
\Delta=\mathrm{Gal\,}(H/\mathbb{Q})
.

By (
3
), we have that as
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-modules

(4)

V=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\alpha/\beta}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\beta/\alpha},

where these lines are determined as in (
3
). Recall that this decomposition is canonical even when
\alpha/\beta=\beta/\alpha
. In order to define
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
fix the following
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
-equivariant line of
V
.

Definition 3.1
.

Following the notation above, let
F^{+}V=\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\beta/\alpha}
. Note that
V/F^{+}V=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\alpha/\beta}
and we have a natural injection
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Q}_{p})\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V/F^{+}V)
.

Remark 3.2
.

The choice
F^{+}V=\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\alpha/\beta}
would correspond to
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\beta}))
.

Before starting the procedure of defining
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
we explain its outline. From the study of the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},V)
and of the natural map

(5)

\lambda:H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},V)\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},V/F^{+}V)

we will identify a
1
-dimensional subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\Hom(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(here, and from now on, we are considering continuous homomorphisms). Then, using class field theory, we will express this
1
-dimensional subspace as
\Hom(\mathrm{Gal\,}(H_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, where
H_{\infty}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Finally,
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))
is defined in terms of
H_{\infty}
(in fact, it is defined in terms of some universal norm of

).

Remark 3.3
.

As we will see, the fact that

contains a line where

-dimensional subspace of

-invariant. In other words, it is the main reason why we have an exceptional zero.

3.1.
Identification of the
1
-dimensional subspace of
\Hom(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\mathbb{Q}_{p})

We begin studying the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-vector space
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},V)
: we give a description of it using class field theory and we compute its dimension. For that, we introduce the following notation. Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
be the primes of
H
above
p
. For every

, let
H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
be the completion of
H
at
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
, let
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}=\mathrm{Gal\,}(H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})\subset\Delta
be the corresponding decomposition subgroup and denote by
U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{1}\subset H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{\times}

\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
acts on

U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}=U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
-equivariant isomorphism
\log_{p}:U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}\to H_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}

-adic logarithm map. By identifying
\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}

\Ind_{\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}^{\Delta}(U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1})

\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}

\Ind_{\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}}^{\Delta}(\Reg_{\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}})=\Reg_{\Delta}

{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}
be the global units of
H
that are congruent to
1
modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for every

. The diagonal embedding

{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}\to\prod_{i=1}U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{1}

is
\Delta
-equivariant. This map can be extended to
{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{1}
and we denote the image of this map by
\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}}
. Furthermore, tensoring these objects by
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) we obtain the
\Delta

{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}

{\mathcal{O}}_{H,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}={\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{H,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}}
be the image of
{\mathcal{O}}_{H,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{1}
by the last map. Note the following result from class field theory.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\tilde{H}
be the compositum of all
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
H
. The global Artin map gives a
\Delta
-equivariant map

\prod_{i=1}^{n}U_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{1}/\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{H}^{1}}\to\mathrm{Gal\,}(\tilde{H}/H)

which is injective and has finite cokernel.

The following result is already explained in
[
BD
]
and
[
GV
]
, but we present it here since it will be useful in the next sections in order to compute Greenberg’s
\mathcal{L}
-invariant.

Proposition 3.5
.

The inflation-restriction exact sequence gives the isomorphism

This concludes the proof of the first part of the proposition. To compute the dimension of

in

\Delta
realizable over

(i)

The multiplicity of

(ii)

If

is

(iii)

(iv)

Since

Now we do the case analysis:

•

If

If

If

Proposition 3.6

Proposition 3.7

]

Recall that Remark

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be as in Corollary
5.4
. Then, for every

we have

(11)

(\psi+\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{j})\log_{p}(x)-\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\psi+\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i})\log_{p}(\sigma_{i}\pi)=bn\log_{p}(\sigma_{j}^{-1}\theta\varepsilon).

Now we will use (
11
) for

and

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
b
be as in Corollary
5.4
. Then,

b=-\frac{\log_{p}(v_{\beta/\alpha})}{\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})}.

Proof.

Using Corollary
5.11
, we have

\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=2}^{n}(\psi-\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{j}^{-1})\log_{p}(\sigma_{j}^{-1}\varepsilon)

and a similar expression holds for

as well.
Now, for

we take (

) and multiply both sides by
(\psi-\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{j}^{-1})/n
. If we add these equalities for

the right hand side equals to

bn\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha}),

where we used that
\theta(u_{\beta/\alpha})=u_{\beta/\alpha}
.
Now we compute the left hand side. Note that

\sum_{j=2}^{n}(\psi^{-1}-\psi)(\sigma_{j})(\psi+\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{j})\log_{p}(x)=\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left(\psi^{-2}(\sigma_{j})-\psi^{2}(\sigma_{j})\right)\log_{p}(x)=0.

Hence, the left hand side is

-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\psi^{-1}-\psi)(\sigma_{j})(\psi+\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i})\log_{p}(\sigma_{i}\pi)

which equals to

-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\left(\psi(\sigma_{i})-\psi^{-1}(\sigma_{i})+\psi(\sigma_{j})^{-2}\psi(\sigma_{i})^{-1}-\psi(\sigma_{j})^{2}\psi(\sigma_{i})\right)\log_{p}(\sigma_{i}\pi)=\\
=-(n-1)\log_{p}(v_{\beta/\alpha})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\left(\left(\psi(\sigma_{i})^{-1}-\psi(\sigma_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n}\psi(\sigma_{j})^{2}\right)\log_{p}(\sigma_{i}\pi)\right).

Note that
\sum_{i=2}^{n}\psi(\sigma_{j})^{2}=-1
. Indeed, since
\Ind_{G_{K}}^{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(\psi)
is irreducible, there exists

such that
\psi^{2}(\sigma_{j})\neq 1
which implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi^{2}(\sigma_{j})=0
. Hence, the expression above equals to
-n\log_{p}(v_{\beta/\alpha})
and the result follows.
∎

And now we can use the previous proposition and (
11
) for

to find an expression for

and hence for

up to
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}

.

Let
\{u,v\}
be as in §

Since

is such that

\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{Gr}}(\ad^{0}(g_{\alpha}))\equiv 2\log_{p}(\pi x)=2\log_{p}(x)+2\log_{p}(\pi)\quad(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb{Q}^{\times}).

On the other hand, taking (
11
) with
j=1
and the expression for

found in Proposition
5.13

2\log_{p}(x)-\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\psi+\psi^{-1})(\sigma_{i})\log_{p}(\sigma_{i}\pi)=-n\frac{\log_{p}(v_{\beta/\alpha})}{\log_{p}(u_{\beta/\alpha})}\log_{p}(\theta\varepsilon).

To conclude with the proof of the first statement note that by Corollary
5.11

Proposition 7.12

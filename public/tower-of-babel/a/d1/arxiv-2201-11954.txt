Sharp Threshold for the Fréchet Mean (or Median) of Inhomogeneous Erdős-Rényi Random Graphs
François G. Meyer

Abstract
We address the following foundational question: what is the population, and sample, Frechet mean (or median) graph of an ensemble of inhomogeneous Erdős-Rényi random graphs? We prove that if we use the Hamming distance to compute distances between graphs, then the Frechet mean (or median) graph of the inhomogeneous random graphs with expected adjacency matrix 𝐏 \mathbf{P} is obtained by thresholding 𝐏 \mathbf{P} : an edge exists between the vertices i i and j j in the mean graph if and only if p i ​ j > 1 / 2 p_{ij}>1/2 . We show that the result also holds for the sample mean (or median) when 𝐏 \mathbf{P} is replaced with the sample mean adjacency matrix. Consequently, the Fréchet mean (or median) graph of inhomogeneous Erdős-Rényi random graphs exhibits a sharp threshold: it is either the empty graph if max ⁡ p i ​ j < 1 / 2 \max p_{ij}<1/2 , or the complete graph if min ⁡ p i ​ j > 1 / 2 \min p_{ij}>1/2 . This novel theoretical result has some significant practical consequences; for instance, the Fréchet mean of an ensemble of sparse inhomogeneous random graphs is always the empty graph.



Keywords:
Fréchet mean; Fréchet median; statistical network analysis.

4
Discussion and Conclusion

In this work we derived the expression for the population Fréchet mean for inhomogeneous Erdős-Rényi random graphs. We proved that the
sample Fréchet mean was consistent, and could be estimated using a simple thresholding rule. Our results have several practical
implications.

First, our work implies that the sample Fréchet mean computed from a training set of graphs, which display specific topological
features of interest, will not inherit from the training set the desired topological structure. Indeed, in the context of
inhomogeneous Erdős-Rényi random graphs, the (population or sample) Fréchet mean graph no longer captures the
edge density encoded by the edge probability.

Our answer to the question of the authors in
[
25
]
: “what is the “mean” network (rather than how do we estimate
the success-probabilities of an inhomogeneous random graph), and do we want the “mean” itself to be a network?” is therefore
disappointing in the context of the probability space
\mathcal{G}\mspace{-1mu}\big(\mspace{-2mu}n,\bm{P}\mspace{-1mu}\big)
. While the Fréchet mean is indeed an element of
\mathcal{G}\mspace{-1mu}\big(\mspace{-2mu}n,\bm{P}\mspace{-1mu}\big)
, it only
provides a simplistic sketch of that probability space. Consider for instance sparse graphs where
\min p_{ij}<1/2

(e.g., graphs with
{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}\left(n^{2}\right)
but
\omega(n)
edges), then the sample Fréchet mean is the empty graph, and is pointless.

On a more positive note, our analysis provides a theoretical justification for several algorithms designed to recover a graph
from noisy measurements of its adjacency matrix. For instance, the authors in
[
21
]
devise a method to recover a
fixed network from unlabeled noisy samples. Instead of estimating the Fréchet mean, they compute the sample mean of the noisy
adjacency matrices, and threshold the sample mean to recover an unweighted graph.

Our results offer a theoretical justification of the approach of
[
21
]
, if one assumes that the noisy graphs are
aligned and are realizations of inhomogeneous Erdős-Rényi random graphs, with an unknown edge probability matrix
\bm{P}
, then the
algorithm described in
[
21
]
recovers the sample Fréchet mean graph.

\mathbb{E}\left[a_{ij}\right]=\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=p_{ij},

(23)

and because the edges
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})
and
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})
are independent,

\mathbb{E}\left[a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]=\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=p_{ij}p_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}.

(24)

Therefore (
21
) becomes

-4\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=-4\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}p_{ij}\right]\left[\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}p_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

(25)

Using (
23
) one more time, (
22
) is equal to,

\displaystyle 2\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)\right]

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}p_{ij}\right]-2\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}p_{ij}\right]

(27)

The first term in the righthand side of (
20
) can be evaluated to give

\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{ij}\right]^{2}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{4mu}\sum_{1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}\mspace{4mu}\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}\;a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right),

(28)

where

\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}\left[a_{ij}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]=p^{2}_{ij}&\text{if $(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})$,}\\
\mathbb{E}\left[a_{ij}^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[a_{ij}\right]=p_{ij}&\text{if $(i,j)=(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})$.}\end{cases}

(29)

We conclude that

\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{ij}\right]^{2}\mspace{-12mu}\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{4mu}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}}{{(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}}}\mspace{-16mu}p^{2}_{ij}+\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-8mu}p_{ij}=\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-8mu}p_{ij}\right]^{2}\mspace{-8mu}+\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-8mu}p_{ij}(1-p_{ij}).

(30)

We can substitute (
25
), (
27
), and (
30
) into (
21
), (
22
), and
(
20
) respectively, and we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{A}\in\mathcal{S}}d^{2}_{H}(\bm{A},\bm{B})\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\right)=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}\right]^{2}+2\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}\right]\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-12mu}(1-2p_{ij})\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-12mu}(1-2p_{ij})\right]^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}(1-p_{ij})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-12mu}(1-2p_{ij})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-12mu}p_{ij}\right]^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}(1-p_{ij}),

(31)

which matches the expression (
19
).
∎

5.2
The Fréchet mean and median of
\mathcal{G}\big(n,\mathbf{P}\big)
: proof of Theorem
1

We prove theorem
1
for the mean. The proof for the median is very similar; it is in fact simpler and is therefore
omitted. By lemma
3
, we seek the matrix
\bm{B}
, with edge set
\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})
, that minimizes the Fréchet function defined by
(
19
). Let us denote

x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\left(1-2p_{ij}\right).

(32)

The variable
x
is clearly a function of
\bm{B}
, and
\bm{P}
. To avoid unnecessary complicated notations, we keep these
dependencies implicit in our exposition. Since
0\leq p_{ij}\leq 1
,
x
is confined to the following interval,

-\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}\leq-\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}p_{ij}\leq x\leq\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-16mu}1\mspace{8mu}\leq n(n-1)/2.

(33)

In fact,
x=-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}
, only if
\forall i,j\in[n],\;p_{ij}=1
, and the graph associated
to
\bm{B}
in (
32
) is the complete graph. This case is of no interest to us, and thus we can assume that

\bm{P}
is always chosen such that

-\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}<x.

(34)

We define,

f(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left[x+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}\right]^{2},

and we have

f(x)=F_{2}(\bm{B})-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}(1-p_{ij}).

Minimizing
F_{2}
is therefore equivalent to minimizing
f
. Clearly,
f(x)
is convex, has a global minimum at

x_{\min{}}=-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}
, and is increasing for
x\geq-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}
. We seek
x^{*}
that
minimizes
f(x)
over the interval wherein
x
is enclosed,

\bigg(-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij},\;n(n-1)/2\bigg].

We note that because of (
34
),
x_{\min{}}<x^{\ast}
. Also,
x^{*}
cannot be positive; otherwise, we would get

f(x^{*})>f(0)
. The optimal value
x^{*}
is obtained by minimizing the distance from
x^{*}
to
-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}
,

x^{\ast}-(-\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij})=\mspace{-24mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-16mu}(1-2p_{ij})+\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-8mu}p_{ij}\geq\mspace{-24mu}\sum_{(i,j);1-2p_{ij}<0}\mspace{-24mu}\left(1-2p_{ij}\right)+\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}.

(35)

The lower bound (
35
) is independent of
\bm{B}
, and can be obtained by choosing,

\bm{\mu}\mspace{-2mu}\big[\mspace{-2mu}\bm{A}\mspace{-2mu}\big]_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad p_{ij}>1/2,\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(36)

as advertised in the theorem. ∎

5.3
The sample Fréchet functions for the Hamming distance

We now consider
N
independent random graphs,
\left\{G^{(k)}\right\}_{1\leq k\leq N}
, sampled from
\mathcal{G}\mspace{-1mu}\big(\mspace{-2mu}n,\bm{P}\mspace{-1mu}\big)
, with adjacency
matrices
\bm{A}^{(k)}
. The sample Fréchet functions associated with the sample Fréchet mean and median graph are given by the next lemma,
which corresponds to the sample version of lemma
3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\bm{B}\in\mathcal{S}
, let
\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})
be the set of edges of the graph associated to
\bm{B}
. Then

\widehat{F}_{1}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\left(1-2\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\right)+\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-8mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]

(37)

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{2}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)=

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-8mu}\left(1-2\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\right)+\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\right]^{2}\mspace{-12mu}+\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\left(1-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\mspace{16mu}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{8mu}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}}{{(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}}}\left(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle+4\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\left(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]\right)

(38)

where the sample mean and sample correlation are defined by

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}a^{(k)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}

(39)

Proof.

We prove (
38
), the proof of (
37
) is much simpler and is omitted. The proof of (
38
) is
similar to the proof of lemma
3
. For each graph
G^{(k)}
, we apply equation (
17
), we sum over all the
graphs in the sample, and divide by
N
to get

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{2}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)=

\displaystyle\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}^{2}+2\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{(k)}_{ij}\right]^{2}-4\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}a^{(k)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

(41)

Using the expressions for the sample mean and correlation, in (
39
), we get

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{2}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)=

\displaystyle\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}^{2}+2\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}\Big[\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\Big]+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{(k)}_{ij}\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 4\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-16mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

(43)

We note that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a^{(k)}_{ij}\Big]^{2}=\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\;\sum_{1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}a^{(k)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}=\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\;\sum_{1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}\mspace{-16mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

(44)

Also, we have

\displaystyle\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}\bigg[\mspace{-4mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[\mspace{-2mu}\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})\right\rvert\mspace{-2mu}-2\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\bigg]^{2}-\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\;\sum_{1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}\mspace{-16mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle+4\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-16mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

(45)

We can then substitute (

) and (

) into (

), and we get

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{2}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)

\displaystyle+4\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}}(\bm{B})}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-16mu}\Big[\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]\Big]

(46)

Finally, we can extract from the second term in the first line of (
46
) the term that
corresponds to

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}}{{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\neq(i,j)}}}\mspace{-8mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]+\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-16mu}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{a_{ij}}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,ij}\right],

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\sum_{1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n}\left[\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]\right]

Substituting (

) into (

∎

5.4
Concentration of the sample Fréchet functions for large sample size

In the following two lemmata, we show that for large
N
, the sample Fréchet functions
\widehat{F}_{1}
and
\widehat{F}_{2}

concentrate around their population counterparts,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
.

Lemma 5
.

For all
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
N_{\delta}
, such that for all
N\geq N_{\delta}
,

\widehat{F}_{1}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-8mu}\left(1-2p_{ij}\right)+\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}+{\mathcal{O}}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right),

(50)

with probability
1-\delta
over the realization of the sample
\left\{G^{(k)}\right\}_{1\leq k\leq N}
.

Proof.

The sample mean
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]
, defined in (
39
), is the sum of Bernoulli random variables, and it concentrates
around its mean
p_{ij}
. We use Hoeffding inequality to bound the variation of
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]
around
p_{ij}
. For each

1\leq i<j\leq n
, we have,

\mspace{2mu}\mathbb{P}\mspace{-2mu}\left(\mspace{-2mu}\mspace{-4mu}\bm{A}^{(k)}\sim\mathcal{G}\mspace{-1mu}\big(\mspace{-2mu}n,\bm{P}\mspace{-1mu}\big);\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]-p_{ij}\right\rvert\geq\varepsilon\mspace{-2mu}\right)\leq\exp{\displaystyle\left(-2N\varepsilon^{2}\right)}.

(51)

To control
\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}
for all
1\leq i<j<n
, we use a union bound, and we get,

\forall 1\leq i<j<n,\quad\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]-p_{ij}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{N}},

(52)

with probability
1-\delta
, and where
\alpha=\sqrt{\log(n/\sqrt{2\delta})}
.
From (
52
), and for
N
large enough we obtain the result announced in the lemma,

\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-12mu}\left(1-2\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]\right)+\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]=\mspace{-24mu}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-8mu}\left(1-2p_{ij}\right)+\mspace{-16mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mspace{-8mu}p_{ij}+{\mathcal{O}}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right),

(53)

with probability
1-\delta
.
∎

Similarly, we show in the following lemma that for large sample size
N
, the sample Fréchet function
\widehat{F}_{2}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)
concentrates
around its population counterpart,
F_{2}(\bm{B})
.

Lemma 6
.

For all
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
N_{\delta}
, such that for all
N\geq N_{\delta}
,

\widehat{F}_{2}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\bm{B}\right)=\left[\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}(\bm{B})}\mspace{-8mu}\left(1-2p_{ij}\right)+\mspace{-8mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}\right]^{2}\mspace{-12mu}+\mspace{-12mu}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}p_{ij}\left(1-p_{ij}\right)+{\mathcal{O}}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right),

(54)

with probability
1-\delta
over the realization of the sample
\left\{G^{(k)}\right\}_{1\leq k\leq N}
.

Proof.

We have seen in the proof of lemma
5
that the sample mean concentrates around its population mean. From (
52
) we have,

\forall 1\leq i<j<n,\quad\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]-p_{ij}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{N}}.

(55)

with probability
1-\delta/8
, and where
\alpha=\sqrt{\log{(2n/\sqrt{\delta}})}
. We now study the concentration of the
sample correlation,

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a^{(k)}_{ij}a^{(k)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},

(56)

when the pair of edges are distinct. Because
(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
, the terms
a^{(k)}_{ij}
and
a^{(k)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
are always
independent, and the product
a^{(k)}_{ij}a^{(k)}_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
is a Bernoulli random variable with parameter
p_{ij}p_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
. We conclude that the
sample correlation is the sum of Bernoullli random variables, and thus it concentrates around its mean,
p_{ij}p_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
.

\forall\;1\leq i<j\leq n,\mspace{4mu}\forall\;1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n,\;\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]-p_{ij}p_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}}

(57)

with probability
1-\delta/8
, where
\beta=\sqrt{\log{(n^{2}/\sqrt{\delta/2})}}
. In summary, we have

\displaystyle\forall\;1\leq i<j\leq n,\quad\forall\;1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n,

\displaystyle\quad\text{with}\quad(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]=p_{ij}+{\mathcal{O}}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)\mspace{-4mu},\;

\displaystyle\text{and}\;\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]=p_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}+{\mathcal{O}}\mspace{-4mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right),

(58)

with probability
1-\delta/4
. We are now in position to substitute
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[a_{ij}\right]
and
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}_{N}\left[\rho_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right]

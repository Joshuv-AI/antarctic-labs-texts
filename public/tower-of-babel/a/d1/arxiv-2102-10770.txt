Testing isomorphism of complex and real Lie algebras
Tuan A. Nguyen and Vu A. Le and Thieu N. Vo

Abstract
. In this paper, we give algorithms for determining the existence of isomorphism between two finite-dimensional Lie algebras and compute such an isomorphism in the affirrmative case. We also provide algorithms for determining algebraic relations of parameters in order to decide whether two parameterized Lie algebras are isomorphic. All of the considered Lie algebras are considred over a field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , where 𝔽 = ℂ \mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C} or 𝔽 = ℝ \mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R} . Several illustrative examples are given to show the applicability and the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms.

17B99, 14Q99, 68W30

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we will consider a computer-based approach for solving the following two problems:

Problem 1
.

Given two
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebras
L
and
L^{\prime}
of same dimension. Deciding whether L and
L0 are isomorphic or not, and determine an isomorphism in the affirmative case.

Problem 2
.

Given two
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebras
L(\mathbf{c})
and
L^{\prime}(\mathbf{d})
depending on
r
-tuple
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{r})\in\mathbb{F}^{r}

and
s
-tuple
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\dotsc,d_{s})\in\mathbb{F}^{s}
, respectively.
Find conditions of parameters
\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{d}
such that
L(\mathbf{c})
and
L^{\prime}(\mathbf{d})
are isomorphic.

By definition, an
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra
, say
L
, is an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space endowed with a skew-symmetric bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]\colon L\times L\to L
which obeys the Jacobi identity:

[[X,Y],Z]+[[Y,Z],X]+[[Z,X],Y]=0,\quad\text{for all $X,Y,Z\in L$}.

If
L
is
n
-dimensional with a basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
then we have

[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}a_{ij}^{k}X_{k};\quad 1\leq i<j\leq n.

We call
a_{ij}^{k}\in\mathbb{F}
the
structure constants
of
L
.

An
\mathbb{F}
-linear isomorphism
\phi
between two
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebras
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}\right)
and
\left(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L^{\prime}}\right)
is called an
isomorphism
if it preserves Lie brackets, i.e.,

(1.1)

\phi\left(\left[X,Y\right]_{L}\right)=\left[\phi(X),\phi(Y)\right]_{L^{\prime}},\quad\text{for all $X,Y\in L$}.

The problem of classifying Lie algebras up to isomorphism is a fundamental problem of Lie Theory.
Invariants (such as ideals in characteristic series, the nilradical, the center)
contains partial informations of a Lie algebra. Invariants of isomorphic Lie algebras are the same.
However, it is still an open problem to determine a complete list of invariants such that they are strong enough to characterize a Lie algebra.
Therefore, it is impossible to decide the isomorphism between Lie algebras just by means of their invariants.

To the best of our knowledge, Gerdt and Lassner
[
8
]
were the first authors considering Problem
1

under a view from computer algebra. In their algorithm, condition (
1.1
) is transformed into a system of polynomial equations, therefore, the problem of testing Lie algebra isomorphism is reduced to the problem of testing the existence of a solution of a polynomial system.
Gröbner basis technique is then used to solve the latter problem.
However, since the complexity of computing Gröbner bases is very costly, the algorithm is impractical when the dimension pass 6.
Furthermore, it is not clear whether this algorithm is applicable for solving Problem
2
.

We provide new algorithms for solving Problems
1
and
2
in cases
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{R}
.
Inherited from the idea by Gerdt and Lassner
[
8
]
, we also rewrite the considered problems in terms of polynomial equations.
However, we will use the so-called
triangular decomposition
instead of using Gröbner bases to deal with the polynomial systems.
There are two main advantages of using triangular decomposition. On the one hand, the algorithm for checking the existence
of a solution of a polynomial system by using triangular decomposition runs much faster than that using Gröbner bases.
On the other hand, triangular decomposition can be used to deal with polynomial systems with parameters and over the real fields.
Details about the construction of triangular decomposition for polynomial systems with implementation in Maple were presented
in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

We recall necessary definitions and algorithms for triangular decomposition for polynomial systems over fields of characteristics zero
in Sections
2
, and for semi-algebraic system over the real field in Section
3
.
Algorithms for the projection operator are recalled in Section
4
.
In Section
5
, we present algorithms for solving Problem
1
by using triangular decomposition.
In Section
6
, we construct algorithms for solving Problem
2
by using triangular decomposition and projection.
Several illustrative examples are given in Section
7
to show the applicability and effectiveness of the proposed algorithms.

2.
Triangular decomposition of polynomial systems

Here, we recall the basic ideas of Triangular decomposition of polynomial systems. More detailes on the theory and applications of triangular decomposition appear in
[
1
,
5
]
.

In this section,
\Bbbk
is a field with algebraic closure
\mathbb{K}
. The notation
R\coloneqq\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
indicates the polynomial ring
\Bbbk[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]
with ordered variables
\mathbf{x}=x_{1}<\dotsb<x_{n}
.

Let
p\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]\setminus\Bbbk
. The greates variable of
p
is called the
main variable
of
p
and denoted by
\mathrm{mvar}(p)
. If
\mathrm{mvar}(p)=x_{i}
then we can consider
p
as a univariate polynomial by
x_{i}
, i.e.
p=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}][x_{i}]
, and the greatest coefficient of
p
is called the
initial
of
p
and denote it by
\mathrm{init}(p)
.

For
F\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
, we denote by
\left\langle F\right\rangle
the ideal in
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
spanned by
F
, and
V(F)
the zero set (solution set or algebraic variety) of
F
in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

Let
I\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
be an ideal.

•

A polynomial
p\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
is called a
zerodivisor modulo
I
if there exists
q\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
such that
pq\in I
and
p
or
q
belongs to
I
. If
p
is neither 0 nor zerodivisor modulo
I
then we call
p\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
is
regular modulo
I

•

For
h\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
, the
saturated ideal
of
I
with respect to (hereafter, w.r.t.)
h
is an ideal in
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
as follows:

I:h^{\infty}\coloneqq\left\{q\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]\colon\exists m\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }h^{m}q\in I\right\}.

A subset
T\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]\setminus\Bbbk
consists of polynomials with pairwise distinct main variables is called a
triangular set
. For triangular set
T\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
:

•

We denote by
\mathrm{sat}(T)\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
the
saturated ideal
of
T
defined as follows: if
T=\emptyset
then
\mathrm{sat}(T)
is the trivial ideal
\{0\}
, otherwise it is the ideal
\left\langle T\right\rangle:h_{T}^{\infty}
.

•

Let
p,q\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
. If either
p
or
q
is not constant and has main variable
v
, then we define
\mathrm{res}(p,q,v)
as the resultant of
p
and
q
w.r.t.
v
. We define
\mathrm{res}(p,T)
inductively as follows: if
T=\emptyset
, then
\mathrm{res}(p,T)=p
; otherwise let
v
be greatest variable appearing in
T
, then
\mathrm{res}(p,T)=\mathrm{res}\left(\mathrm{res}(p,T_{v},v),T_{<v}\right)
.

•

Let
h_{T}
be the product of the initials of the polynomials in
T
. The
quasi-component
of
T
is
W(T)\coloneqq V(T)\setminus V(h_{T})
.

Definition 2.1

(Regular chain)
.

A triangular set
T\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
is called a
regular chain
if:

(1)

T=\emptyset
; or

(2)

T\setminus\{T_{\max}\}
, where
T_{\max}
is the polynomial in
T
with maximum rank, is a regular chain and
\mathrm{init}\left(T_{\max}\right)
is regular w.r.t.
\mathrm{sat}\left(T\setminus\{T_{\max}\}\right)
.

Definition 2.2

(Triangular decomposition)
.

Let
F\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
be finite. A finite subset
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{e}\}
of regular chains of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
is called a
triangular decomposition
of
V(F)
if
V(F)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{e}W(T_{i})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Chen and Maza
[
5
]
presented the algorithm
\mathsf{Triangularize}(F)
to compute a triangular decomposition of
V(F)
. It has been implemented in MAPLE.

Example 2.4
.

Consider the following system:

\begin{cases}x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}&=4,\\
x^{2}+2y^{2}&=5,\\
xz&=1.\end{cases}

Set
F\coloneqq\left\{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-4,\\
x^{2}+2y^{2}-5,\\
xz-1\right\}\subset R\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]
. Then,
\mathsf{Triangularize}(F,R)
returns three regular chains as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}T_{1}=\left\{xz-1,2y^{2}-3,x^{2}-2\right\},\\
T_{2}=\left\{z+1,y^{2}-2,x+1\right\},\\
T_{3}=\left\{z-1,y^{2}-2,x-1\right\}.\end{array}

The first regular chain has
h_{T_{1}}=x
, so
W(T_{1})=V(T_{1})\setminus V(x)
. For two remaining ones, we have
W(T_{2})=V(T_{2})
and
W(T_{3})=V(T_{3})
. Since
V(F)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}W(T_{i})
(note that
V(F)\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}
), we need to solve three systems as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l l l l l}\begin{cases}xz-1&=0\\
2y^{2}-3&=0\\
x^{2}-2&=0\\
x&\neq 0\end{cases},&&\begin{cases}z&=-1\\
y^{2}&=2\\
x&=1\end{cases},&&\begin{cases}z&=1\\
y^{2}&=2\\
x&=1.\end{cases}\end{array}

Example 2.5
.

Consider the following system:

\begin{cases}x^{2}+y+z&=1,\\
x+y^{2}+z&=1,\\
x+y+z^{2}&=1.\end{cases}

Due to Cox et al.
[
7
, Chapter 3, §1]
, the Gröbner basis of

I=\left\langle x^{2}+y+z-1,x+y^{2}+z-1,x+y+z^{2}-1\right\rangle

w.r.t. lex order reduces to solve the following system:

\begin{cases}x+y+z^{2}-1&=0\\
y^{2}-y-z^{2}+z&=0\\
2yz^{2}+z^{4}-z^{2}&=0\\
z^{6}-4z^{4}+4z^{3}-z^{2}&=0.\end{cases}

Set
F\coloneqq\left\{x^{2}+y+z-1,x+y^{2}+z-1,x+y+z^{2}-1\right\}\subset R\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]
.
\mathsf{Triangularize}(F,R)
reduces to solve four systems as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l l l l l ll}\begin{cases}z-x&=0\\
y-x&=0\\
x^{2}+2x-1&=0\end{cases},&&\begin{cases}z&=0\\
y&=0\\
x-1&=0\end{cases},&&\begin{cases}z&=0\\
y-1&=0\\
x&=0\end{cases},&&\begin{cases}z-1&=0\\
y&=0\\
x&=0.\end{cases}\end{array}

Remark 2.6
.

A disadvantage of Gröbner bases is that they do not necessarily have a triangular set shape. Consequently, solving Gröbner bases to construct isomorphims is much harder, and in general, it seems to be impossible.

3.
Triangular decomposition of semi-algebraic systems

In this section, we recall a little bit about triangular decomposition of semi-algebraic systems. For more details, we refer the readers to
[
1
,
3
,
6
]
and references therein.

In this section,
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic 0 and
\mathbb{K}
is its algebraic closure. For
p\in\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]\setminus\Bbbk
, we denote by
\mathrm{der}(p)
the derivative of
p
w.r.t.
\mathrm{mvar}(p)
.

Let
T\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
be a triangular set. Denote by
\mathrm{mvar}(T)
the set of main variables of the polynomials in
T
. A variable
v\in\mathbf{x}
is called
algebraic
w.r.t.
T
if
v\in\mathrm{mvar}(T)
, otherwise it is said
free
w.r.t.
T
. We shall denote by
\mathbf{u}=u_{1},\dotsc,u_{d}
and
\mathbf{y}=y_{1},\dotsc,y_{m}
respectively the free and the main variables of
T
. We let
d=0
whenever
T
has no free variables.

Let
T\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
be a regular chain and
H\subset\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
. The pair
[T,H]
is a
regular system
if each polynomial in
H
is regular modulo
\mathrm{sat}(T)
. If
H=\{h\}
then we write
[T,h]
for short. Regular chain
T
or regular system
[T,H]
is
squarefree
if
\mathrm{der}(t)
is regular w.r.t.
\mathrm{sat}(T)
for all
t\in T
.

Let
[T,H]
be a squarefree regular system of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{y}]
. Let
\mathsf{bp}
be the primitive and square free part of the product of all
\mathrm{res}(\mathrm{der}(t),T)
and all
\mathrm{res}(h,T)
for
h\in H
and
t\in T
. We call
\mathsf{bp}
the
border polynomial
of
[T,H]
.

Let us consider four finite subset of
\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l l l l}F=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\},&N=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\},&P=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\},&H=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{l}\}.\end{array}

We denote by
N_{\geq}
and
P_{>}
the sets of inequalities
\{n_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,n_{t}\geq 0\}
and
\{p_{1}>0,\ldots,p_{r}>0\}
, respectively; and
H_{\neq}
the set of inequations
\{h_{1}\neq 0,\ldots,h_{l}\neq 0\}
.

Definition 3.1

(Semi-algebraic systems)
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{S}\coloneqq[F,N_{\geq},P_{>},H_{\neq}]
the
semi-algebraic system
(SAS), that is the conjunction of the following conditions:
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{s}=0
,
N_{\geq}
,
P_{>}
and
H_{\neq}
.

Definition 3.2

(Pre-regular semi-algebraic system)
.

Let
[T,P]
be a squarefree regular system of
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{y}]
with border polynomial
\mathsf{bp}
. Let
B\subset\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u}]
be a polynomial set such that
\mathsf{bp}
divides the product of polynomials in
B
. We call the triple
[B_{\neq},T,P_{>}]
a
pre-regular semi-algebraic system
(PRSAS) of
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{x}]
. Zero set of
[B_{\neq},T,P_{>}]
, denoted by
Z_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{\neq},T,P_{>})
, is the set
(u,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
b(u)\neq 0
for all
b\in B
,
t(u,y)=0
for all
t\in T
, and
p(u,y)>0
for all
p\in P
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
3
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
be a SAS of
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{x}]
. Then there exists finitely many PRSASs
[B_{i\neq},T_{i},P_{i>}]
,
i=1,\ldots,e
, such that
Z_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{S})={\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{i=1}^{e}}Z_{\mathbb{R}}(B_{i\neq},T_{i},P_{i>})
.

Definition 3.4

(Regular semi-algebraic system)
.

Let
T\subset\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{x}]
be a squarefree regular chain. Let
P\subset\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{x}]
be finite and such that each polynomial in
P
is regular w.r.t.
\mathrm{sat}(T)
, i.e.
[T,P]
is a regular system. Define
P_{>}\coloneqq\{p>0\,|\,p\in P\}
. Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a quantifier-free formula over
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{x}]
involving only the
\mathbf{u}
variables. Let
S=Z_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{Q})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the semi-algebraic subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by
\mathcal{Q}
. When
d=0
, the 0-ary Cartesian product
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is treated as a singleton set. We say that
\mathcal{R}\coloneqq[\mathcal{Q},T,P_{>}]
is a
regular semi-algebraic system
(RSAS) if:

(1)

S
is a non-empty open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(2)

The regular system
[T,P]
specializes well at every
u\in S
,

(3)

At each
u\in S
, specialized system
[T(u),P(u)_{>}]
admits real solutions.

The zero set of
\mathcal{R}
, denoted by
Z_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{R})
, is the set of points
(u,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-d}
such that
\mathcal{Q}(u)
holds,
t(u,y)=0
for all
t\in T
and
p(u,y)>0
for all
p\in P
.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
3
, Lemma 3]
)
.

Let
[B_{\neq},T,P_{>}]
be a PRSAS of
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{u},\mathbf{y}]
. One can decide whether its zero set is empty or not. If it is not empty, then one can compute a RSAS
[\mathcal{Q},T,P_{>}]
whose zero set is the same as that of
[B_{\neq},T,P_{>}]
.

Proposition 3.6

(
[
3
, Theorem 2]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
be a SAS of
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbf{x}]
. Then one can compute a (full) triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{S}
, that is finitely many RSASs such that the union of their zero sets is the zero set of
\mathfrak{S}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Chen et al.
[
3
, Section 7]
presented an algorithm to compute a triangular decomposition of a semi-algebraic system
\mathfrak{S}=[F,N_{\geq},P_{>},H_{\neq}]
which was denoted by
RealTriangularize
(\mathfrak{S})
. It has been implemented to MAPLE.

Example 3.8
.

Consider the following system:

\begin{cases}x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+2=0\\
3x^{2}+4y^{2}+4z^{2}+5=0.\end{cases}

Put
F\coloneqq\{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+2,3x^{2}+4y^{2}+4z^{2}+5\}
,
N_{\geq}=P_{>}=H_{\neq}\coloneqq\emptyset
. Set
\mathfrak{S}\coloneqq\left[F,\emptyset,\emptyset,\emptyset\right]
be a SAS in
R\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]
. Since
\mathsf{RealTriangularize}(\mathfrak{S},R)
returns
\emptyset
, the given system has no real root. Note that
\mathsf{Triangularize}(F,R)
returns one regular chain
\left\{z^{2}+y^{2}-1,x^{2}+3\right\}
which implies that the given system has complex roots.

4.
Projections

For many purposes, we need to find solutions of a polynomial system
F
as well as of a SAS
\mathfrak{S}
which consist of
r
parameters and
n-r
unknowns. In particular, we want to find out the values of parameters in which the given system admits solutions since it concerns directly with Problem
2
. In this situation, the projections are very useful (see
[
2
,
4
]
).

4.1.
Polynomial systems

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic zero with algebraic closure
\mathbb{K}
.

Definition 4.1

(Constructible set)
.

Let
F=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\}
and
H=\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{l}\}
be two finite set of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
. The conjunction of
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{s}=0
and
h_{1}\neq 0,\ldots,h_{l}\neq 0
is called a
constructible system
of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
, and denoted by
[F,H]
. Its zero set in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
, i.e.,
V(F,H)\coloneqq V(F)\setminus V(H)
, is called a
basic constructible set
of
\Bbbk[x]
. A
constructible set
of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
is a finite union of basic constructible sets of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{x}]
.

Now, we set
R\coloneqq\Bbbk[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}]=\Bbbk[x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n-r}>u_{1}>\cdots>u_{r}]
be the polynomial ring. For
F\subset R
, we consider
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{u}
respectively as unknowns and parameters, that is, its
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-r})
-solutions are multivariate functions of
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r})
. In other words,
R\coloneqq\Bbbk[\mathbf{u}][\mathbf{x}]=\Bbbk[u_{1}>\cdots>u_{r}][x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n-r}]
. Denote by
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}\colon\mathbb{K}^{n}=\mathbb{K}^{n-r}\times\mathbb{K}^{r}\to\mathbb{K}^{r}
the projection which maps
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})
in the entire space to
\mathbf{u}
in the last
r
-dimensional parameter subspace. There are two situations as follows.

•

For
F\subset R
, we want to find the image
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(V(F))
of its zero set
V(F)\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
under
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}
.

•

For
F,H\subset R
, we want to find the image
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(V(F,H))
of the constructible set
V(F,H)\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
under
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}
.

Note that these images are constructible sets of
\Bbbk[\mathbf{u}]
. In MAPLE, the commands to find
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(V(F))

and
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{K}}(V(F,H))
are
\mathsf{Projection}(F,r,R)
and
\mathsf{Projection}(F,H,r,R)
, respectively.

Example 4.2
.

Consider
F\coloneqq\{x^{2}+y^{2}-1\}
and
H\coloneqq\{x+y-1\}
in
R\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[y,x]\equiv\mathbb{R}[x][y]
, i.e.,
y
and
x
are respectively the unknown and the parameter. We want to find the values of
x
such the system
[F,H]
admits complex roots. To this end, we need to find
\pi^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}(V(F,H))
.
\mathsf{Projection}(F,H,1,R)
returns a constructible set of
\mathbb{R}[x]
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l l}\begin{cases}x&\neq 0\\
x-1&\neq 0\end{cases},&x=0.\end{array}

Thus, the system
[F,H]
admits complex roots iff
x\neq 1
.

4.2.
Semi-algebraic systems

We set
R\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u}]=\mathbb{R}[x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n-r}>u_{1}>\cdots>u_{r}]
be the polynomial ring, where
\mathbf{x}
are unknowns and
\mathbf{u}
are parameters. Denote by
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n-r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
the projection which maps
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u})
in the entire space to
\mathbf{u}
in the last
r
-dimensional parameter subspace. For a SAS
\mathfrak{S}=[F,N,P,H]
of
R
, we want to find the image
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{R}}(Z_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{S}))
of its zero set
Z_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{S})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
under
\pi^{r}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

In this case, the image

Uniform continuity bound for sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy
Ashutosh Marwah and Frédéric Dupuis

Abstract
We prove a simple uniform continuity bound for the sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy for α ∈ [ 1 / 2 , 1 ) ∪ ( 1 , ∞ ] \alpha\in[1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty] , which is independent of the dimension of the conditioning system.



1
Introduction

Conditional entropies quantify the amount of uncertainty of a system when one has access to side information. Rényi conditional entropies have been instrumental in understanding both classical and quantum information theoretic processes in the one-shot domain
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
. There are multiple generalizations and definitions of Rényi conditional entropies for quantum states. One of the most important of these is the sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy
[
7
,
8
]
(which we denote as
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}
following the notation in Tomamichel’s book, Quantum Information Processing with Finite Resources
[
9
]
), which is defined for a quantum state
\rho_{AB}
,
\alpha\in[1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty]
and
\alpha^{\prime}=(\alpha-1)/\alpha
as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta_{B}}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}.

where the supremum is taken over all quantum states
\eta_{B}
. For
\alpha=1/2
, this definition results in
H_{\max}
, which characterizes the rate of compression for a probabilistic source
[
10
]
and for
\alpha=\infty
, it results in
H_{\min}
, which has been used to characterize the amount of randomness which one can extract from a system independent of the conditioning system
[
2
]
. Recently, the sandwiched conditional entropies with
\alpha\in(1,2]
have also been used to derive bounds for randomness extraction
[
6
]
.

Many applications require that we are able to bound the conditional entropy of one state in terms of another state, which is close to the original state. For von Neumann conditional entropy, the Alicki-Fannes-Winter (AFW) uniform continuity bound
[
11
,
12
]
enables us to do this. It states that for two quantum states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle|H(A|B)_{\rho}-H(A|B)_{\sigma}|\leq 2\epsilon\log(d_{A})+(1+\epsilon)h\left(\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\right)

(1)

where the function
h(x)=-x\log(x)-(1-x)\log(1-x)
is the binary entropy and
d_{A}
is the dimension of the vector space
A
. A key feature of this bound is that it is independent of the size
d_{B}
of the conditioning system. This is crucial in many applications, particularly in cryptography, where the conditioning system often represents an adversary’s system whose size cannot be bounded. The AFW bound has been used widely in quantum information theory (see, for example, the converse bounds for channel capacities
[
13
]
).

In this paper, we will prove a uniform continuity bound, like the AFW bound, for sandwiched Rényi conditional entropies. Specifically, in Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
, we prove that for two quantum states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, and for
\alpha<1
we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}|\leq\log(1+\epsilon)+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(1+\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}-\frac{\epsilon}{(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}}\right)

and for
\alpha>1
, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}|

\displaystyle\leq\log(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\log\left(1+(\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{\beta}d_{A}^{2(1-\beta)}-\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{1-\beta}}\right)

where
\beta
is such that
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=2
.

To prove these, we make use of a simple lemma about the subadditivity of the function
X\mapsto\tr(X^{\alpha})
for
\alpha<1
and use the duality relation for sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy, which states that for a pure tripartite state
\rho_{ABC}
and an
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)\cup(1,\infty]
,
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}=-\tilde{H}_{\beta}^{\uparrow}(A|C)_{\rho}
for
\beta
such that
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=2
.

Uniform continuity bound for quantum Rényi entropies

In the following, the dimension of a vector space
\mathcal{V}
will be succinctly referred to as
d_{\mathcal{V}}
. Also, for Hermitian matrices
P
and
Q
, we will use the notation
P\geq Q
to denote that the matrix
P-Q
is positive semidefinite.

We will use the sandwiched Rényi relative entropy
[
7
,
8
]
for our proof. For
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)\cup(1,\infty]
and positive operators
P
and
Q
such that
\tr(P)\neq 0
, the sandwiched Rényi relative entropy of
P
relative to
Q
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P||Q):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\frac{\tr\left(Q^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}PQ^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}}{\tr(P)}&\text{ if }(\alpha<1\text{ and }P\nperp Q)\text{ or }\ker(Q)\subseteq\ker(P)\\
\infty&\text{else.}\end{cases}

For
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)\cup(1,\infty]
, the sandwiched Rényi relative entropy satisfies the data processing inequality, that is, for any completely positive and trace-preserving map
\Phi
and positive operators
P
and
Q
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(P||Q)\geq\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\Phi(P)||\Phi(Q)).

We begin by stating a lemma about the subadditivity of the trace of matrices raised to the power of
\alpha\in[0,1]
. This was proven by McCarthy
[
19
]
. We provide a proof here for completeness.

Lemma 2.1

(McCarthy’s Inequality
[
19
]
)
.

For positive semidefinite matrices
P
and
Q
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\tr((P+Q)^{\alpha})\leq\tr(P^{\alpha})+\tr(Q^{\alpha})

Proof.

First, note that the statement is trivial for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
. For the other values of
\alpha
, we will use the following integral representation of
t^{\alpha}
where
t\in[0,\infty)
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
(see for example Example V.1.10 in Bhatia’s Matrix Analysis
[
20
]
)

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{\lambda+t}d\mu(\lambda)

where
\mu
is a positive measure on
(0,\infty)
. For a positive semidefinite matrix
X
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we can use this representation to write

\displaystyle X^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{X}{(\lambda+X)^{-1}}d\mu(\lambda).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\tr((P+Q)^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\left({(P+Q)}{(\lambda+P+Q)^{-1}}\right)d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\left({P}{(\lambda+P+Q)^{-1}}\right)d\mu(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\left({Q}{(\lambda+P+Q)^{-1}}\right)d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\left({P}{(\lambda+P)^{-1}}\right)d\mu(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\tr\left({Q}{(\lambda+Q)^{-1}}\right)d\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\tr(P^{\alpha}+Q^{\alpha})

where for the inequality we use the fact that the function
X\mapsto X^{-1}
is operator anti-monotone (see for example Proposition V.1.6 in Bhatia’s Matrix Analysis
[
20
]
).
∎

We will now use the lemma above to prove the following uniform continuity bound for sandwiched Rényi entropies with
\alpha<1
.

Theorem 1
.

For
\alpha<1
,
\epsilon\in[0,1]
and bipartite normalized quantum states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, the difference in sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy for the two states can be bounded as

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}|\leq\log(1+\epsilon)+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(1+\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}-\frac{\epsilon}{(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}}\right).

(3)

If, in addition, system
A
is classical for both
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
or system
B
is classical for both states, then we can strengthen the bound to

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}|\leq\log(1+\epsilon)+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(1+\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}-\frac{\epsilon}{\left(d_{A}(1+\epsilon)\right)^{1-\alpha}}\right).

(4)

Proof.

For
\alpha<1
, we can write the sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy as

\displaystyle 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}=\sup_{\eta_{B}}\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}.

(5)

This form will turn out to be useful later.

We can assume that
\epsilon>0
as otherwise the bound is trivial. Since the upper bound in the theorem is increasing in the trace distance, it is sufficient to prove the bound for
\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}=2\epsilon
. Let
\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}=P^{\prime}_{AB}-Q^{\prime}_{AB}
be the decomposition of
\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}
into its positive and negative parts, so that
P^{\prime}_{AB}\geq 0
and
Q^{\prime}_{AB}\geq 0
. Note that
\tr(P^{\prime}_{AB})=\tr(Q^{\prime}_{AB})=\epsilon
. Thus, we can further define the density operators
P_{AB}:=P^{\prime}_{AB}/\epsilon
and
Q_{AB}:=Q^{\prime}_{AB}/\epsilon
.

Now, we have that
\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}=\rho_{AB}+\epsilon Q_{AB}
. For any positive semidefinite operator
\eta_{B}
, we have the following chain of implications

\displaystyle\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}=\rho_{AB}+\epsilon Q_{AB}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\ \eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\left(\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}\right)\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}=\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\ \eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}+\epsilon\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}Q_{AB}\ \eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\left(\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}\right)\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}=\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\ \eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}+\epsilon\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}Q_{AB}\ \eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\left(\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}\right)\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}\leq\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}+\epsilon^{\alpha}\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}Q_{AB}\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}

where we use Lemma
2.1
in the third step. Taking the supremum on the right-hand side, we get

\displaystyle\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\left(\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}\right)\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\omega_{B}}\left\{\tr\left(\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}+\epsilon^{\alpha}\tr\left(\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}Q_{AB}\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\omega_{B}}\left\{\tr\left(\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\rho_{AB}\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}\right\}+\epsilon^{\alpha}\sup_{\omega_{B}}\left\{\tr\left(\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}Q_{AB}\omega_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}\right\}.

Using Eq. (
5
), we derive that for every state
\eta_{B}

\displaystyle\tr\left(\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\left(\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB}\right)\eta_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}+\epsilon^{\alpha}2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{Q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}+\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}

(6)

where we have used
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{Q}\leq\log(d_{A})
to derive the last line. Now, we will try to lower bound the term on the left-hand side. For an arbitrary
\delta>0
, there exist states
\mu_{B}
and
\nu_{B}
such that

\displaystyle 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\tr\left(\mu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\sigma_{AB}\mu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}+\delta

(7)

\displaystyle 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{P}}

\displaystyle\leq\tr\left(\nu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}P_{AB}\nu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}+\delta.

(8)

We use these states to define the state
\theta_{BC}
as

\displaystyle\theta_{BC}:=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\mu_{B}\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}_{C}+\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\nu_{B}\otimes\ket{1}\bra{1}_{C}.

Also, define

\displaystyle\bar{\sigma}_{ABC}:=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\sigma_{AB}\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}_{C}+\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}P_{AB}\otimes\ket{1}\bra{1}_{C}.

With these definitions, we have the following chain of inequalities

\displaystyle\tr\left(\theta_{B}^{\frac{-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}(\sigma_{AB}+\epsilon P_{AB})\theta_{B}^{\frac{-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\tr\left(\theta_{B}^{\frac{-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\bar{\sigma}_{AB}\theta_{B}^{\frac{-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha}2^{-(1-\alpha)\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\bar{\sigma}_{AB}||\Id_{A}\otimes\theta_{B})}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha}2^{-(1-\alpha)\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\bar{\sigma}_{ABC}||\Id_{A}\otimes\theta_{BC})}

\displaystyle=(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\tr\left(\mu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\sigma_{AB}\mu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}+

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\epsilon(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\tr\left(\nu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}P_{AB}\nu_{B}^{-\alpha^{\prime}/2}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\left[2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}}+\epsilon 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{P}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt-(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\delta

\displaystyle\geq(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha-1}\left[2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}}+\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt-(1+\epsilon)^{\alpha}\delta

where we use the data processing inequality for
\tilde{D}_{\alpha}
for the first inequality, the definitions of the states
\mu_{B}
and
\nu_{B}
(Eq. (
7
), (
8
)) in the second last step and the fact that
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{P}\geq-\log(d_{A})
in the last step. Combining this inequality with the upper bound in Eq. (
6
) yields

\displaystyle 2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}+(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}-\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}+(1+\epsilon)\delta.

Since
\delta
is arbitrary we can let
\delta\rightarrow 0
. Further, taking the logarithm on both sides and dividing by
1-\alpha>0
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left((1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}+(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}-\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}\right)

We can now bound the difference of the conditional entropies as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left((1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}2^{(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}+(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}-\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left((1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}+\left((1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}-\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}\right)2^{-(1-\alpha)\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left((1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}+\left((1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}-\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}\right)d_{A}^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\log(1+\epsilon)+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\left(1+\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{2(1-\alpha)}-\frac{\epsilon}{(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}}\right)

where we used the fact that
(1+\epsilon)^{1-\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}d_{A}^{1-\alpha}\geq\epsilon d_{A}^{\alpha-1}
since
d_{A}\geq 1
, which implies that the right-hand side in the second line is a decreasing function in
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}
, which we again lower bound by
-\log(d_{A})
(when
A
or
B
is classical, we can use
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}\geq 0
and derive the stronger bound in Eq. (
4
)). Since we did not use any specific property of
\rho_{AB}
or
\sigma_{AB}
, one can derive the same upper bound by switching
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
.
∎

It is worth noting that in the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 1
, the bound in Eq. (
3
) yields the bound

\displaystyle|H(A|B)_{\rho}-H(A|B)_{\sigma}|\leq 2\epsilon\log(d_{A})+(1+\epsilon)\log(1+\epsilon)-\epsilon\log(\epsilon)

which is equal to the tight continuity bound for conditional von Neumann entropy (Eq.
1
) proven by Winter
[
12
]
. This indicates that our bound is close to being tight in the regime where
\alpha
is close to
1
and
\alpha<1
.

We will now prove a uniform continuity bound for sandwiched Rényi conditional entropies with
\alpha>1
as a corollary to the above theorem by making use of the duality relation for these entropies. Recall that according to the duality relation
[
7
,
21
]
for
\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}
, we have that for a pure state
\rho_{ABC}

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}=-\tilde{H}_{\beta}^{\uparrow}(A|C)_{\rho}

for
\alpha,\beta\in[1/2,1)\cup(1,\infty]
satisfying
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=2
. Note that for
\alpha\in(1,\infty]
, the
\beta
satisfying the duality condition lies in
[1/2,1)
.

Corollary 1
.

For
\alpha>1
,
\epsilon\in[0,1]
and bipartite normalized quantum states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, the difference in sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy for the two states can be bounded as

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}

\displaystyle-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}|

\displaystyle\leq\log(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\log\left(1+(\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{\beta}d_{A}^{2(1-\beta)}-\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{1-\beta}}\right)

(9)

for
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha-1}
defined such that
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=2
.

Proof.

If
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, then using the Fuchs-van de Graaf inequality along with Uhlmann’s theorem, we can find purifications
\rho_{ABC}
and
\sigma_{ABC}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{ABC}-\sigma_{ABC}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\epsilon}.

For
\alpha>1
, choose
\beta<1
such that
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=2
, then using the duality relation and Theorem
1
we have that

\displaystyle|\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\sigma}-\tilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}|

\displaystyle=|\tilde{H}_{\beta}^{\uparrow}(A|C)_{\sigma}-\tilde{H}_{\beta}^{\uparrow}(A|C)_{\rho}|

\displaystyle\leq\log(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})+\frac{1}{1-\beta}\log\left(1+(\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{\beta}d_{A}^{2(1-\beta)}-\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{1-\beta}}\right)

∎

We expect that the bound in Eq. (
9
) is not tight in the constants and the exponents. For
H_{\min}=\tilde{H}_{\infty}^{\uparrow}
, the corollary above gives us the bound

\displaystyle|{H}_{\min}(A|B)_{\sigma}-{H}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}|\leq\log(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})+2\log\left(1+(2\epsilon)^{1/4}d_{A}-\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}{(1+\sqrt{2\epsilon})^{1/2}}\right)

It is easy to prove a tighter uniform continuity bound with an upper bound of
\log(1+\epsilon d_{A}^{2})
for
H_{\min}
. We prove it in the Appendix as Theorem
2
. Nevertheless, the bound above is independent of the conditioning system, and it should be sufficient for most applications.

3
Conclusion

We proved a simple uniform continuity bound for the sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy. Further work should look at improving these bounds, especially for
\alpha>1
. Deriving continuity bounds for other Rényi conditional entropies also remains an open problem.

Appendix A
Uniform continuity bound for
H_{\min}

In the following theorem, we prove a simple and tighter uniform continuity bound for
H_{\min}=\tilde{H}_{\infty}^{\uparrow}
. Recall that
H_{\min}
can be equivalently defined as

\displaystyle H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}:=\max\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\text{there exists $\eta_{B}$ such that }\rho_{AB}\leq 2^{-\lambda}\Id_{A}\otimes\eta_{B}\right\}.

Theorem 2
.

For
\epsilon\in[0,1]
and bipartite normalized quantum states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon
, the difference in the min-entropy for the two states can be bounded as

\displaystyle|H_{\min}(A|B)_{\sigma}-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}|\leq\log(1+\epsilon d_{A}^{2}).

(10)

Proof.

Once again, since the upper bound in the theorem is increasing in
\epsilon
, we can simply prove the bound for states
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}=\epsilon
. We also assume that
\epsilon>0
. Then, following the argument in Theorem
1
, we can write
\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}=\epsilon P_{AB}-\epsilon Q_{AB}
for density operators
P_{AB}
and
Q_{AB}
. We will now use the fact that
\sigma_{AB}\leq\rho_{AB}+\epsilon Q_{AB}
. Using the definition of
H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}
, we know that there exists a state
\eta_{B}
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}\leq 2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}\Id_{A}\otimes\eta_{B}.

Also, since
H_{\min}(A|B)_{Q}\geq-\log(d_{A})
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{AB}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{Q}}\Id_{A}\otimes\eta^{\prime}_{B}

\displaystyle\leq d_{A}\Id_{A}\otimes\eta^{\prime}_{B}

for some state
\eta^{\prime}_{B}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{AB}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{AB}+\epsilon Q_{AB}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}\Id_{A}\otimes\eta_{B}+\epsilon d_{A}\Id_{A}\otimes\eta^{\prime}_{B}

\displaystyle\leq(2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}+\epsilon d_{A})\Id_{A}\otimes\left(\frac{2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}\eta_{B}+\epsilon d_{A}\eta^{\prime}_{B}}{2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}+\epsilon d_{A}}\right)

which implies that

\displaystyle 2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\sigma}}\leq 2^{-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}+\epsilon d_{A}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 2^{H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\sigma}}\leq 1+\epsilon d_{A}2^{H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}-H_{\min}(A|B)_{\sigma}\leq\log\left(1+\epsilon d_{A}^{2}\right).

A similar bound can be proven in the opposite direction if we follow the above procedure with
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{AB}
interchanged.
∎

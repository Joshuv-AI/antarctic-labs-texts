M d M_{d} -multipliers of a locally compact group
Bat-Od Battseren

Abstract
. We show that the space M d ​ ( G ) M_{d}(G) of M d M_{d} -multipliers of a locally compact group G G is isometrically isomorphic to the Banach space of bounded functionals on the d d -fold Haagerup tensor product of L 1 ​ ( G ) L^{1}(G) vanishing on the kernel of the convolution map. Consequently, we see that M d ​ ( G ) M_{d}(G) is isometrically isomorphic to the dual space of X d ​ ( G ) X_{d}(G) , the completion of L 1 ​ ( G ) L^{1}(G) in the dual of M d ​ ( G ) M_{d}(G) . We also show that M d M_{d} -type-approximation-properties are inherited to lattices.



INTRODUCTION

In
[
Pis05
]
, a commutative Banach algebra
M_{d}(\Gamma)
was introduced in pursuit of studying Dixmier’s Similarity Problem, where
\Gamma
is a discrete group and
d
is an integer greater than one. When
d=2
, this is the algebra of Herz-Schur multipliers of
\Gamma
. In general,
M_{d}(\Gamma)
stands between the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra
B(\Gamma)
and the algebra of bounded functions
\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. By the usual
(\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma),\ell^{1}(\Gamma))
-duality, we can embed
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
in the dual of
M_{d}(\Gamma)
. Denote by
X_{d}(\Gamma)
the completion of
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
in
M_{d}(\Gamma)^{*}
. Pisier showed in
[
Pis05
]
that
M_{d}(\Gamma)\cong X_{d}(\Gamma)^{*}
isometrically using Paulsen-Smith’s theorem
[
PS87
]
. In essence, it means the space
M_{d}(\Gamma)
can be alternatively described as the dual of a quotient of the
d
-fold Haagerup tensor product of
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
.

This last construction can be performed for a locally compact group
G
by using
L^{1}(G)
instead of
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
. However, as indicated in
[
Pis05
, Remark after Theorem 2.10]
, it is not clear how the functionals in this space would correspond to (continuous) functions on
G
. In the discrete case, this correspondence was clear because
\Gamma
is naturally contained in
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
. We elaborate on this problem for locally compact second countable groups.

In
[
Bat23a
]
, the author defined the space
M_{d}(G)
of
M_{d}
-multipliers
of a locally compact group
G
as the subspace of all continuous functions in
M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})
, where
G_{\mathrm{d}}
denotes the discrete realization of
G
. More precisely:

Definition 0.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. A bounded continuous function
\varphi
on
G
is called an
M_{d}
-multiplier if there exist Hilbert spaces
\h_{0},\dots,\h_{d}
and bounded maps
\xi_{i}:G\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
for
i=1,\dots,d
such that
\h_{0}=\h_{d}=\C
and

(0.1)

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})=\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})(1)\quad\text{for all}\quad x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in G.

We denote by
M_{d}(G)
the space of all
M_{d}
-multipliers and endow it with the norm

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}=\inf\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\xi_{i}\|_{\infty}

where the infimum is taken over all
\h_{i}
’s and
\xi_{i}
’s satisfying (
0.1
).

This definition is compatible with the definition given by Pisier for discrete groups. Because the identity map
M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(G_{\mathrm{d}})
is contracting,
M_{d}(G)=M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})\cap C(G)
becomes a commutative Banach algebra. Our main result is the following, which demonstrates that the two previously mentioned constructions of
M_{d}(G)
are the same for locally compact second countable groups.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group and let
d
be an integer greater than one.

(1)

If
\varphi
is an
M_{d}
-multiplier of
G
, i.e.
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)=M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})\cap C(G)
, then there is a bounded functional
\hat{\varphi}
on
L^{1}(G)\otimes_{h}\cdots\otimes_{h}L^{1}(G)
such that
\|\hat{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}
and

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})=(\varphi,f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
.

(2)

Conversely, if
\phi
is a bounded functional on
L^{1}(G)\otimes_{h}\cdots\otimes_{h}L^{1}(G)
vanishing on the kernel of the convolution map

\conv_{d}:L^{1}(G)\otimes\cdots\otimes L^{1}(G)\rightarrow L^{1}(G),
then there is an
M_{d}
-multiplier
\check{\phi}\in M_{d}(G)
such that
\|\check{\phi}\|_{M_{d}}=\|\phi\|
and

\displaystyle(\check{\phi},f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}=\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
.

Moreover, we have
\varphi=\check{\hat{\varphi}}
and
\phi=\hat{\check{\phi}}
.

If we restrict the
(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))
-duality on
M_{d}(G)
, we can embed
L^{1}(G)
in
M_{d}(G)^{*}
. Denote by
X_{d}(G)
the completion of
L^{1}(G)
in
M_{d}(G)^{*}
. After Theorem
0.2
and Paulsen-Smith’s theorem, it is easily seen that
X_{d}(G)
is a Banach predual of
M_{d}(G)
.

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group and let
d
be an integer greater than one. Then we have
M_{d}(G)\cong X_{d}(G)^{*}
isometrically.

This duality between
M_{d}(G)
and
X_{d}(G)
was used in
[
Ver23
,
Bat23b
]
to define
M_{d}
type approximation properties. We recall the definitions.

Definition 0.4

(
M_{d}
type approximation properties)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and let
d
be an integer greater than one.

We say that
G
has
M_{d}
-approximation-property (
M_{d}
-AP in short) if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
C_{c}(G)\cap M_{d}(G)
such that
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology.

We say that
G
is
M_{d}
-weakly-amenable (
M_{d}
-WA in short) if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
C_{c}(G)\cap M_{d}(G)
such that
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}\leq C<\infty
and
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology. The number
\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)=\inf C
is called the
M_{d}
-Cowling-Haagerup constant. We conventionally write
\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)=\infty
if
G
is not
M_{d}
-WA.

We say that
G
has
M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup-property (
M_{d}
-WH in short) if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
C_{0}(G)\cap M_{d}(G)
such that

\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}\leq C<\infty
and
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology. The number
\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)=\inf C
is called the
M_{d}
-weak-Haagerup constant. We conventionally write
\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)=\infty
if
G
does not have
M_{d}
-WH.

When
d=2
, these are exactly the usual approximation property, weak amenability, and weak Haagerup property
[
DCH85
,
CH89
,
HK94
,
Knu16
,
Haa16
]
. Allow us to mention a couple of motivations to study
M_{d}
type approximation properties in general extent. The first one is related to the Similarity Problem: A group with
M_{d}
-AP for all
d\in\N
has an affirmative answer to the Similarity Problem
[
Ver23
, Theorem 1.2]
. The second one is their stability:
M_{d}
type approximation properties are stable under von Neumann equivalence
[
Bat23b
]
, hence also under Measure equivalence and W*-equivalence. See
[
IPR24
,
Ish24
,
Bat23a
,
Bat23b
]
for more about von Neumann equivalence.

The following result allows one to relax the convergence conditions in the definition of
M_{d}
-WA and
M_{d}
-WH.

Proposition 0.5
.

Let
G
be a locally compact second countable group and let
d
be an integer greater than one. Then
G
is
M_{d}
-WA if and only if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
C_{c}(G)\cap M_{d}(G)
such that
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}\leq C<\infty
and
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
uniformly on compact subsets. We have
\inf C=\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)
. Similarly,
G
has
M_{d}
-WH if and only if there is a net
(\varphi_{i})
of functions in
C_{0}(G)\cap M_{d}(G)
such that
\sup_{i}\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}\leq C^{\prime}<\infty
and
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
uniformly on compact subsets. We have
\inf C^{\prime}=\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)
.

A key step in proving Theorem
0.2
is Lemma
1.3
in which we observe that we can choose the maps
\xi_{i}:G\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
in Definition
0.1
to be continuous where
\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
is endowed with the weak operator topology. Proposition
0.5
is a simple consequence of this observation. Since the uniform convergence on compact subsets pass to closed subgroups via the restriction map, the following is immediate.

Corollary 0.6
.

If
G
is a locally compact second countable group and
H
is a closed subgroup of
G
, then we have
\Lambda_{CH}(H,d)\leq\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)
and
\Lambda_{WH}(H,d)\leq\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)
for all integers
d\geq 2
.

As shown in
[
Bat23a
, Theorem 3]
, the converse inequalities hold true when
H
is a lattice of
G
, hence the following.

Theorem 0.7
.

If
G
is a locally compact second countable group and
\Gamma
is a lattice of
G
, then we have
\Lambda_{CH}(\Gamma,d)=\Lambda_{CH}(G,d)
and
\Lambda_{WH}(\Gamma,d)=\Lambda_{WH}(G,d)
for all integers
d\geq 2
.

The argument used to prove Corollary
0.6
does not work for
M_{d}
-AP due to the lack of uniform norm boundedness. However, if we assume
H
is a lattice in
G
, there is an easier approach to obtain a similar results.

Theorem 0.8
.

If
G
is a locally compact second countable group and
\Gamma
is a lattice of
G
, then
G
has
M_{d}
-AP if and only if
\Gamma
has
M_{d}
-AP.

We acknowledge that this work is strongly inspired by
[
Haa16
]
and
[
Pis05
]
where the case
d=2
and the discrete case are studied.

1.
Preliminary

Throughout the paper,
G
is a locally compact second countable group,
G_{\mathrm{d}}
is the discrete realization of
G
,
d\in\N
is an integer greater than one, and
\h_{i}
,
\h
, and
\mathcal{K}
are Hilbert spaces. We conventionally write
\h_{0}=\h_{d}=\C
and
v_{0}=v_{d}=1
.

1.1.
Fourier and Fourier-Stieltjes algebras

Recall that a
unitary representation
of
G
is a continuous group homomorphism
\pi:G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\h)
, where
\h
is a Hilbert space and
\mathcal{U}(\h)
is the group of all unitary operators on
\h
endowed with the weak operator topology (WOT). Given vectors
v,w\in\h
, we call the function

(1.1)

\displaystyle\varphi=\pi_{v,w}:g\in G\mapsto\langle\pi(x)v,w\rangle

the
coefficient
of
\pi
associated to
v,w\in\h
. These are typical elements of
M_{d}(G)
. To see that,
put
\h_{1}=\dots=\h_{d-1}=\h
,
\xi_{1}(x)=\langle\pi(x)\cdot,w\rangle
,
\xi_{d}(x):1\mapsto\pi(x)v
, and
\xi_{2}(x)=\dots=\xi_{d-1}(x)=\pi(x)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})(1)=\langle\pi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})v,w\rangle=\pi_{v,w}(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})

with
\|\pi_{v,w}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|v\|\|w\|
.

The
Fourier-Stieltjes algebra

B(G)
consist of all coefficients of unitary representations of
G
. Endowed with the pointwise product and the norm
\|\varphi\|_{B}=\inf\|v\|\|w\|
, where the infimum is taken over all
(\h,\pi,v,w)
satisfying (
1.1
), the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra is a commutative Banach algebra.

The
Fourier algebra

A(G)
is the smallest closed subalgebra of
B(G)
containing
B(G)\cap C_{c}(G)
. Some basic facts about the Fourier algebra include that it consists of the coefficients of the left regular representation
\lambda:G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(G))
and that if
\varphi\in A(G)
then we can find
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
such that

\varphi=\lambda_{f,g}
and
\|\varphi\|_{B}=\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}
. To distinguish that
\varphi
is in the Fourier algebra, we write
\|\varphi\|_{A}=\|\varphi\|_{B}
. We refer the readers to
[
Eym64
,
KL18
,
Run20
]
for more about these algebras.

The Fourier algebra contains abundant continuous functions so that when a function
\varphi
multiplies the Fourier algebra, i.e.
\varphi A(G)\subseteq A(G)
, it is necessarily continuous. We will make use of this fact. The following lemma is comparable to the well known fact
[
Gaa73
, Theorem V.7.3]
that every weakly measurable unitary representation on a separable Hilbert space is continuous.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
A,B:G\rightarrow\h
be two bounded weakly measurable maps (i.e.
\h
is endowed with
\sigma(\h,\h^{*})
-Borel space structure) into a separable Hilbert space
\h
. Assume that
\varphi:G\rightarrow\C
is a function such that

\varphi(yx^{-1})=\langle A(x),B(y)\rangle
for all
x,y\in G
. Then
\varphi
multiplies the Fourier algebra
A(G)
. In particular,
\varphi
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
(e_{n})_{n\in\N}
be an orthonormal basis for
\h
. By assumption, there exist uniformly bounded measurable functions
a_{n},b_{n}\in L^{\infty}(G)
such that

A(x)=\sum_{n}a_{n}(x)e_{n}
,
B(y)=\sum_{n}b_{n}(y)e_{n}
, and

\displaystyle\varphi(yx^{-1})=\langle A(x),B(y)\rangle=\sum_{n}a_{n}(x)\overline{b_{n}(y)}.

Take any
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
. By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\|a_{n}f\|_{2}\|b_{n}g\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{n}\|a_{n}f\|_{2}^{2})^{1/2}(\sum_{n}\|b_{n}g\|_{2}^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(\int_{G}|f(x)|^{2}\sum_{n}|a_{n}(x)|^{2}dx)^{1/2}(\int_{G}|g(x)|^{2}\sum_{n}|b_{n}(x)|^{2}dx)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|_{\infty}\|B\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}<\infty,

thus
\sum_{n}\lambda_{a_{n}f,b_{n}g}\in A(G)
. On the other hand, if we fix
x\in G
,
since the bounding function

\displaystyle|f(x^{-1}y)\overline{g(y)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}(x^{-1}y)\overline{b_{n}(y)}|\leq|f(x^{-1}y)\overline{g(y)}|\|A\|_{\infty}\|B\|_{\infty}

is integrable over
y\in G
,
by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\displaystyle(\varphi\lambda_{f,g})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\varphi(x)f(x^{-1}y)\overline{g(y)}dy=\int_{G}\langle A(x^{-1}y),B(y)\rangle f(x^{-1}y)\overline{g(y)}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\sum_{n}a_{n}(x^{-1}y)\overline{b_{n}(y)}f(x^{-1}y)\overline{g(y)}dy=(\sum_{n}\lambda_{a_{n}f,b_{n}g})(x).

In other words,
\varphi
is a Fourier multiplier, hence continuous.
∎

A similar statement can be achieved for any
M_{d}
-multipliers. More precisely, if
\varphi\in M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})
and the
\xi_{i}
’s in (
0.1
) are weakly measurable, then we can deduce that
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
. This will be used to see that
\check{\phi}
in Theorem
0.2
is continuous.

1.2.
The space
M_{d}(G)
of
M_{d}
-multipliers

The key step in proving Theorem
0.2
.(i) is to show that we can choose
\xi_{i}
’s in (
0.1
) to be WOT-continuous, i.e. the maps
x\in G\mapsto\langle\xi_{i}(x)v_{i},v_{i-1}\rangle
are continuous for any
v_{i}\in\h_{i}
and
v_{i-1}\in\h_{i-1}
. The case
d=2
is done in
[
Haa16
, Theorem 3.2]
. We adapt it to the general case. Beforehand, let us denote

\displaystyle A_{i}=\{\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}\mid x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}\in G\},\quad i=0,\dots,d-1,

and

\displaystyle B_{i}=\{[\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{i}(x_{i})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i}\mid x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1}\in G\},\quad i=1,\dots,d.

Lemma 1.2
.

If
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
is non-zero, we can choose
\h_{i}
’s and
\xi_{i}
’s in (
0.1
) such that
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are total in
\h_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,d-1
.

Proof.

Let
P_{i}\in\B(\h_{i})
be the projection onto the closed linear span of
A_{i}
. Note that
P_{0}
is just the identity map because
\varphi
is nonzero and
\h_{0}\cong\C
. It is easily checked that the Hilbert spaces
P_{i}\h_{i}
and the bounded maps
P_{i-1}\xi_{i}
also satisfy (
0.1
). Thus replacing
(\h_{i},\xi_{i})
’s by
(P_{i}\h_{i},P_{i-1}\xi_{i})
’s, we can and will assume that each
A_{i}
is total in
\h_{i}
.

Let
Q_{i}\in\B(\h_{i})
be the projection onto the closed linear span of
B_{i}
. Again,
Q_{d}
is just the identity map on
\h_{d}
because firstly

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})=\langle v_{d},[\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})]^{*}v_{0}\rangle

is nonzero for some
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in G
and secondly
\h_{d}\cong\C
. Put
\h_{i}^{\prime}=Q_{i}\h_{i}
and
\xi_{i}^{\prime}(x_{i})=Q_{i-1}\xi_{i}(x_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,d
with
Q_{0}=\id_{\h_{0}}
. Then
(\h_{i}^{\prime},\xi_{i}^{\prime})
’s still satisfy (
0.1
). To see that, take any vectors
v_{i}^{\prime}=[\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{i}(x_{i})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i}^{\prime}
and
v_{i+1}\in\h_{i+1}^{\prime}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle Q_{i}\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})Q_{i+1}v_{i+1}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{i+1}^{\prime},Q_{i+1}[\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{i}(x_{i+1})]^{*}v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{i+1}^{\prime},[\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{i}(x_{i+1})]^{*}v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{i}\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})v_{i+1}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle.

Since such
v_{i}^{\prime}
’s are total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
, the above equality is true for any
v_{i}^{\prime}\in\h_{i}^{\prime}
. In other words, we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle Q_{i}\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})Q_{i+1}=Q_{i}\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1}).

From this, it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{\prime}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}^{\prime}(x_{d})v_{d}

\displaystyle=Q_{0}\xi_{1}(x_{1})Q_{1}\xi_{2}(x_{2})Q_{2}\cdots Q_{d-1}\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}=\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d}).

Moreover, the set

\displaystyle B_{i}^{\prime}=\{[\xi_{1}^{\prime}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{i}^{\prime}(x_{i})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i}\mid x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1}\in G\}

is total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
. Now, the main concern is whether

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\prime}=\{\xi_{i+1}^{\prime}(x_{i+1})\cdots\xi_{d}^{\prime}(x_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}\mid x_{i+1},\dots,x_{d}\in G\}

is total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
. Let us prove it by reverse recursion. Firstly,

\displaystyle A_{d-1}^{\prime}=\{Q_{d-1}\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}\mid x_{d}\in G\}

is total in
\h_{d-1}^{\prime}=Q_{d-1}\h_{i-1}
because
A_{d-1}
is total in
\h_{d-1}
by assumption. Assume that
A_{i+1}^{\prime}
is total in
\h_{i+1}^{\prime}=Q_{i+1}\h_{i+1}
. Then by (
1.2
) and the totality condition of
A_{i}
’s, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\spn}A_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\overline{\spn}\bigcup_{x_{i+1}\in G}\xi_{i+1}^{\prime}(x_{i+1})A_{i+1}^{\prime}v_{d}=\overline{\spn}\bigcup_{x_{i+1}\in G}Q_{i}\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})Q_{i+1}\h_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\overline{\spn}\bigcup_{x_{i+1}\in G}Q_{i}\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})\h_{i+1}=\overline{\spn}Q_{i}A_{i}=Q_{i}\h_{i}=\h_{i}^{\prime},

showing that
A_{i}^{\prime}
is total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
be non-zero and let
\h_{i}
’s and
\xi_{i}
’s be as in Lemma
1.2
. Then
\xi_{i}
’s are WOT-continuous and
\h_{i}
’s are separable.

Proof.

Choose
\h_{i}
’s and
\xi_{i}
’s as in Lemma
1.2
. Suppose that
x_{i}^{(j)}\rightarrow x_{i}
in
G
. Take any vectors

v_{i}=\xi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i},\quad v_{i-1}=[\xi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\xi_{i-1}(x_{i-1)}]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i-1}.

Then by continuity of
\varphi
we have

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{i}(x_{i}^{(j)})v_{i},v_{i-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{i}^{(j)}\cdots x_{d})\rightarrow\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})=\langle\xi_{i}(x_{i})v_{i},v_{i-1}\rangle.

By uniform boundedness of
\xi_{i}
and totality assumptions, the above convergence is true for any
v_{i}\in\h_{i}
and
v_{i-1}\in\h_{i-1}
. This shows that each
\xi_{i}
is WOT-continuous. Now,
\h_{i}
is separable since
G
is second countable,
\xi_{i}
is WOT-continuous, and
A_{i}
is dense in
\h_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 1.4
.

If
f\in L^{1}(G)
and
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
, then we have
f*\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
with
\|f*\varphi\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|f\|_{1}\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}
.

Proof.

The continuity of
f*\varphi
is a well known fact of harmonic analysis. Let
\h_{i}
’s and
\xi_{i}
’s be as in Lemma
1.3
. Define
\xi_{1}^{\prime}:G\rightarrow\B(\h_{1},\h_{0})
as

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{\prime}(x)v_{1}=\int_{G}f(y)(\xi_{1}(y^{-1}x)v_{1})dy

for all
x\in G
and
v\in\h_{1}
. Note that
\|\xi_{1}^{\prime}\|\leq\|f\|_{1}\|\xi_{1}\|
and

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{\prime}(x_{1})\xi_{2}(x_{2})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(y)(\xi_{1}(y^{-1}x_{2})\xi_{2}(x_{2})\cdots\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d})dy

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(y)\varphi(y^{-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{d})dy

\displaystyle=(f*\varphi)(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})

for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in G
. Therefore,
f*\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
with
\|f*\varphi\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|f\|_{1}\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
0.5
.

Let
(\varphi_{i})
be a net in
M_{d}(G)
such that
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-topology. It follows that
\varphi_{i}\rightarrow 1
in
\sigma(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))
-topology. By uniform boundedness principle, we have
\sup_{i}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
Take any compactly supported positive function
f\in C_{c}(G)
with
\|f\|_{1}=1
and put
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}=f*\varphi_{i}
.
Now the proof of
[
Haa16
, Lemma 2.2]
works too see that
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\rightarrow 1
uniformly on compact subsets. We note that if
\varphi_{i}
is compactly supported, then so is
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}
. Similarly, if
\varphi_{i}
vanish at infinity, then so does
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}
. By Lemma
1.4
, we have
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\in M_{d}(G)
with
\|\varphi_{i}^{\prime}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\|\varphi_{i}\|_{M_{d}}
. This shows that if we replace the
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-convergence by uniform convergence on compact subsets in the definition of
M_{d}
-WA and
M_{d}
-WH, we will get weaker properties. Conversely, it is always true that uniform convergence on compact subsets implies
\sigma(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))
-convergence. Moreover, for a uniformly bounded net in

, its

-convergence implies
\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))
-convergence as

∎

1.3.
The dual space
M_{d}^{\prime}(G)
of
X_{d}^{\prime}(G)=L^{1}(G)\otimes_{h}\cdots\otimes_{h}L^{1}(G)

Let us recall the following theorem by Paulsen-Smith (see
[
PS87
, Theorem 3.2]
,
[
ER22
, Theorem 9.4.4]
, and
[
Pis03
, Corollary 5.4]
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Given operator spaces
V_{1}
, …,
V_{d}
, a linear mapping

\displaystyle\phi:V_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{d}\rightarrow\B(\h_{d},\h_{0})

extends to a completely bounded map on the Haagerup tensor product if and only if there exist Hilbert spaces
\h_{1}
, …,
\h_{d-1}
and completely bounded linear maps

\displaystyle\eta_{i}:V_{i}\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1}),\quad i=1,\dots,d

such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\phi(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{d})=\eta_{1}(v_{1})\cdots\eta_{d}(v_{d})

for all
v_{1}\in V_{1}
, …,
v_{d}\in V_{d}
. Moreover, we can assume that
\|\phi\|_{cb}=\|\eta_{1}\|_{cb}\cdots\|\eta_{d}\|_{cb}
.

Conventionally, we only consider
\h_{0}=\h_{d}=\C
. Denote

\displaystyle X_{d}^{\prime}(G)=\underbrace{L^{1}(G)\otimes_{h}\cdots\otimes_{h}L^{1}(G)}_{d}

where
L^{1}(G)
is endowed with its maximal operator space structure. Denote by
M_{d}^{\prime}(G)
the (operator) dual of
X_{d}^{\prime}(G)
. If we put
V_{i}=L^{1}(G)
and
\h_{0}=\h_{d}=\C
in Theorem
1.5
, we simply get
\|\phi\|_{cb}=\|\phi\|
because
\C\cong\B(\C,\C)
is abelian
[
ER22
, Proposition 2.2.6]
, and
\|\eta_{i}\|_{cb}=\|\eta_{i}\|
because
L^{1}(G)
has its maximal operator space structure
[
ER22
, Section 3.3]
. With this simplification, we get the following version of Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 1.6
.

A linear map
\phi:L^{1}(G)\otimes\cdots\otimes L^{1}(G)\rightarrow\C
extends to a bounded linear functional
\phi\in M_{d}^{\prime}(G)=X_{d}^{\prime}(G)^{*}
on
X_{d}^{\prime}(G)
if and only if
there exist Hilbert spaces
\h_{i}
’s and bounded linear maps
\eta_{i}:L^{1}(G)\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})=\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
.
Moreover, we can assume that
\|\phi\|=\|\eta_{1}\|\cdots\|\eta_{d}\|
.

Let
\phi\in M_{d}^{\prime}(G)
and let
\h_{i}
’s and
\eta_{i}
’s be as in (
1.4
). Denote

\displaystyle C_{i}=\{\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}\mid f_{i+1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)\},\quad i=0,\dots,d-1,

and

\displaystyle D_{i}=\{[\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i}(f_{i})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i}\mid f_{1},\dots,f_{i-1}\in L^{1}(G)\},\quad i=1,\dots,d.

We have an analogue of Lemma
1.2
. The proof is done in exactly the same way by replacing
(A_{i},B_{i},\xi_{i},x_{i})
’s by
(C_{i},D_{i},\eta_{i},f_{i})
’s. We present the proof for convenience.

Lemma 1.7
.

If
\phi\in M_{d}^{\prime}(G)
is non-zero, we can choose
\h_{i}
’s and
\eta_{i}
’s such that
C_{i}
and
D_{i}
are total in
\h_{i}
. In this case,
\h_{i}
’s are separable.

Proof.

Let
P_{i}\in\B(\h_{i})
be the projection onto
\overline{\spn}C_{i}
.
P_{0}
is the identity because
\phi
is nonzero and
\h_{0}\cong\C
. The Hilbert spaces
P_{i}\h_{i}
and the bounded maps
P_{i-1}\eta_{i}
also satisfy (
1.4
), thus we can and will assume that each
C_{i}
is total in
\h_{i}
. Let
Q_{i}\in\B(\h_{i})
be the projection onto
\overline{\spn}D_{i}
. Again,
Q_{d}
is just the identity on
\h_{d}
because

\displaystyle\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})=\langle v_{d},[\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})]^{*}v_{0}\rangle

is nonzero for some
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
and
\h_{d}\cong\C
. Put
\h_{i}^{\prime}=Q_{i}\h_{i}
and
\eta_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i})=Q_{i-1}\eta_{i}(f_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,d
with
Q_{0}=\id_{\h_{0}}
. Then
(\h_{i}^{\prime},\eta_{i}^{\prime})
’s still satisfy (
1.4
). Indeed, take any vectors
v_{i}^{\prime}=[\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i}(f_{i})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i}^{\prime}
and
v_{i+1}\in\h_{i+1}^{\prime}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle Q_{i}\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})Q_{i+1}v_{i+1}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{i+1}^{\prime},Q_{i+1}[\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i}(f_{i+1})]^{*}v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{i+1}^{\prime},[\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i}(f_{i+1})]^{*}v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle Q_{i}\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})v_{i+1}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle.

Since such
v_{i}^{\prime}
’s are total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
, the above equality is true for any
v_{i}^{\prime}\in\h_{i}^{\prime}
. In other words, we have

(1.5)

\displaystyle Q_{i}\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})Q_{i+1}=Q_{i}\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1}).

From this, it is easily seen that

\displaystyle\eta_{1}^{\prime}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{d}^{\prime}(f_{d})

\displaystyle=Q_{0}\eta_{1}(f_{1})Q_{1}\eta_{2}(f_{2})Q_{2}\cdots Q_{d-1}\eta_{d}(f_{d})

\displaystyle=\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})=\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}).

Moreover, the set

\displaystyle D_{i}^{\prime}=\{[\eta_{1}^{\prime}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i}^{\prime}(f_{i})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i}\mid f_{1},\dots,f_{i-1}\in L^{1}(G)\}

is total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
. Let us prove that

\displaystyle C_{i}^{\prime}=\{\eta_{i+1}^{\prime}(f_{i+1})\cdots\eta_{d}^{\prime}(f_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}\mid f_{i+1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)\}

is total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
by reverse recursion. Firstly,

\displaystyle C_{d-1}^{\prime}=\{Q_{d-1}\eta_{d}(f_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}\mid f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)\}

is total in
\h_{d-1}^{\prime}=Q_{d-1}\h_{d-1}
because
C_{d-1}
is total in
\h_{d-1}
. Assume that
C_{i+1}^{\prime}
is total in
\h_{i+1}^{\prime}=Q_{i+1}\h_{i+1}
. Then by (
1.5
) and the totality condition of
C_{i}
’s, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\spn}C_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\overline{\spn}\bigcup_{f_{i+1}\in L^{1}(G)}\eta_{i+1}^{\prime}(f_{i+1})C_{i+1}^{\prime}=\overline{\spn}\bigcup_{f_{i+1}\in L^{1}(G)}Q_{i}\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})Q_{i+1}\h_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\overline{\spn}\bigcup_{f_{i+1}\in L^{1}(G)}Q_{i}\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})\h_{i+1}=\overline{\spn}Q_{i}C_{i}=Q_{i}\h_{i}=\h_{i}^{\prime},

showing that
C_{i}^{\prime}
is total in
\h_{i}^{\prime}
. This proves the first assertion of the lemma. Recall that
G
is second countable, equivalently
L^{1}(G)
is separable. Now, by density and continuity, it follows that the Hilbert spaces
\h_{i}^{\prime}
are separable.
∎

Consider the
d

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \conv_{d}:\underbrace{L^{1}(G)\otimes\cdots\otimes L^{1}(G)}_{d}&\rightarrow L^{1}(G)\\
f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}&\mapsto f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d}.\end{aligned}

Denote by

the closure of the kernel of

in

. Denote by

(equivalently on the kernel of

\phi\in M_{d}^{\prime}(G)

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})=\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})v_{d}.

Theorem

2.
Main section

In this section, we will prove the main results.

2.1.
Mapping from
M_{d}(G)
into
Y^{\perp}\subseteq M_{d}^{\prime}(G)

For
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
, we write

(2.1)

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})=(\varphi,f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
. Since
\conv_{d}
is linear,
\hat{\varphi}
extends to a linear functional on
L^{1}(G)\otimes\cdots\otimes L^{1}(G)
and vanish on the kernel of
\conv_{d}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The map
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)\mapsto\hat{\varphi}\in Y^{\perp}\subseteq M_{d}^{\prime}(G)
is a well defined contraction.

Proof.

If
\xi:G\rightarrow\B(\h,\mathcal{K})
is a bounded weakly measurable map, we can extend it to a bounded linear map
\hat{\xi}:L^{1}(G)\rightarrow\B(\h,\mathcal{K})
as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\xi}(f)v,w\rangle=\int_{G}f(x)\langle\xi(x)v,w\rangle dx,\quad v\in\h,\quad w\in\mathcal{K}.

Clearly, we have
\|\hat{\xi}\|\leq\sup_{x\in G}\|\xi(x)\|
. For
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
, we choose separable
\h_{i}
’s and WOT-continuous
\xi_{i}
’s as in Lemma
1.3
.
Observe that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle&\hat{\xi}_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\hat{\xi}_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle=\int_{G}f_{1}(x_{1})\langle\xi_{1}(x_{1})\hat{\xi}_{2}(f_{2})\cdots\hat{\xi}_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle dx_{1}\\
&=\int_{G}\int_{G}f_{1}(x_{1})f_{2}(x_{2})\langle\xi_{2}(x_{2})\hat{\xi}_{3}(x_{3})\cdots\hat{\xi}_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},\xi_{1}(x_{1})^{*}v_{0}\rangle dx_{2}dx_{1}\\
&\dots\\
&=\int_{G}\cdots\int_{G}f_{1}(x_{1})\cdots f_{d}(x_{d})\langle\xi_{d}(x_{d})v_{d},\xi_{d-1}(x_{d-1})^{*}\cdots\xi_{1}(x_{1})^{*}v_{0}\rangle dx_{d}\cdots dx_{1}\\
&=\int_{G}\cdots\int_{G}f_{1}(x_{1})\cdots f_{d}(x_{d})\varphi(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})dx_{d}\cdots dx_{1}\\
&=(\varphi,f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}=\hat{\varphi}(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}).\end{aligned}

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
. By Corollary
1.6
,
\hat{\varphi}
extend to a bounded linear functional on
X_{d}^{\prime}(G)
with

\displaystyle\|\hat{\varphi}\|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\hat{\xi}_{i}\|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|{\xi}_{i}\|_{\infty}=\|\varphi\|_{M_{d}}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

2.2.
Mapping from
Y^{\perp}\subseteq M_{d}^{\prime}(G)
into
M_{d}(G)

We want to define a contraction

\displaystyle\phi\in Y^{\perp}\mapsto\check{\phi}\in M_{d}(G)

that is inverse to the map in Lemma
2.1
. Let
(k_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a bounded left-right approximate identity of
L^{1}(G)
consisting of compactly supported, positive, self-adjoint functions, i.e.
k_{n}\in C_{c}(G)_{+}
,
k_{n}^{*}=k_{n}
,
\|k_{n}\|_{1}\leq 1
,
\|k_{n}*f-f\|_{1}\rightarrow 0
, and
\|f*k_{n}-f\|_{1}\rightarrow 0
for all
f\in L^{1}(G)
. For
\phi\in Y^{\perp}
, define
\check{\phi}
as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\check{\phi}:x\in G\mapsto\lim_{n}\phi(\lambda(x)k_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes k_{n})v_{d}.

We will see soon that the above is a well defined continuous function on
G
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The map
\phi\in Y^{\perp}\subseteq M_{d}^{\prime}(G)\mapsto\check{\phi}\in M_{d}(G)
is a well defined contraction.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in Y^{\perp}
. Choose
\h_{i}
’s and
\eta_{i}
’s as in Lemma
1.7
. Denote
h_{n}=k_{n}*\cdots*k_{n}
. Then
(h_{n})_{n\in\N}
is also a bounded approximate identity with the same properties. Take any
x\in G
. For
v_{i}=\eta_{i+1}(f_{i}*f_{i+1})\eta_{i+2}(f_{i+2})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})v_{d}\in\h_{i}
and
v_{i-1}=[\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i-1}(f_{i-1})]^{*}v_{0}\in\h_{i-1}
with
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
, define the operator
\check{\eta}_{i}(x)
as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\check{\eta}_{i}(x)v_{i},v_{i-1}\rangle=\langle\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{i-1}\otimes\lambda(x)f_{i}\otimes f_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle\\
&=\langle\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i-1}(f_{i-1})\eta_{i}(\lambda(x)f_{i})\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle\\
&=\lim_{n}\langle\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i-1}(f_{i-1})\eta_{i}(\lambda(x)h_{n}*f_{i})\eta_{i+1}(f_{i+1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle\\
&=\lim_{n}\langle\tilde{\phi}(f_{1}*\cdots*f_{i-1}*\lambda(x)h_{n}*f_{i}*\cdots*f_{d}),v_{0}\rangle\\
&=\lim_{n}\langle\eta_{1}(f_{1})\cdots\eta_{i-1}(f_{i-1})\eta_{i}(\lambda(x)h_{n})\eta_{i+1}(f_{i}*f_{i+1})\cdots\eta_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle\\
&=\lim_{n}\langle\eta_{i}(\lambda(x)h_{n}){v_{i}},{v_{i-1}}\rangle.\end{aligned}

This extends to a well defined linear map on the linear span of
v_{i}
’s because
\phi\in Y^{\perp}
and
\h_{0}=\h_{d}=\C
. Since
L^{1}(G)*L^{1}(G)
is dense in
L^{1}(G)
and
\eta_{i+1}
is continuous, such
v_{i}
’s are total in
\h_{i}
. By density and boundedness,
\check{\eta}_{i}(x)
extends to a bounded linear map in
\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
with norm not exceeding
\|\eta_{i}\|
. In other words, we have
\check{\eta}_{i}:G\rightarrow\B(\h_{i},\h_{i-1})
well defined and uniformly bounded by
\|\eta_{i}\|
. Observe that

\displaystyle\check{\eta}_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\check{\eta}_{d}(x_{d})v_{d}=\lim_{n_{d}}\cdots\lim_{n_{1}}\eta_{1}(\lambda(x_{1})h_{n_{1}})\cdots\eta_{d}(\lambda(x_{d})h_{n_{d}})v_{d}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{d}}\cdots\lim_{n_{1}}\tilde{\phi}(\lambda(x_{1})h_{n_{1}}*\cdots*\lambda(x_{d-1})h_{n_{d-1}}*\lambda(x_{d})k_{n_{d}}*\cdots*k_{n_{d}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{d}}\cdots\lim_{n_{1}}\eta_{1}(\lambda(x_{1})h_{n_{1}}*\cdots*\lambda(x_{d-1})h_{n_{d-1}}*\lambda(x_{d})k_{n_{d}})\eta_{2}(k_{n_{d}})\cdots\eta_{d}(k_{n_{d}})v_{d}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{d}}\eta_{1}(\lambda(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})k_{n_{d}})\eta_{2}(k_{n_{d}})\cdots\eta_{d}(k_{n_{d}})v_{d}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\phi(\lambda(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})k_{n}\otimes k_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes k_{n})v_{d}.

This shows that the function
\check{\phi}
from (
2.3
) is well defined and in
M_{d}(G_{\mathrm{d}})
with

\displaystyle\|\check{\phi}\|_{M_{d}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\check{\eta}_{i}\|\leq\prod_{i=1}^{d}\|\eta_{i}\|=\|\phi\|.

Since the map
x\in G\mapsto\lambda(x)g\in L^{1}(G)
is continuous for every
g\in C_{c}(G)
(see for example
[
Ped89
, Lemma 6.6.11]
),
\langle\check{\eta}_{i}(\cdot)v_{i},v_{i-1}\rangle
is a pointwise limit of continuous functions, hence measurable.
Now, the continuity of
\check{\phi}
follows from Lemma
1.1
by putting

\displaystyle\h=\h_{d-1},\quad A(x)=\check{\eta}_{d}(x^{-1})v_{d},\quad B(y)=[\check{\eta}_{1}(y))\check{\eta}_{2}(e)\cdots\check{\eta}_{d-1}(e)]^{*}v_{0}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
0.2
.

It only remains to prove that the maps in Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
are inverse to each other. If
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)
, by definition (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\check{\hat{\varphi}}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}\hat{\varphi}(\lambda(x)k_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes k_{n})v_{d}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}(\varphi,\lambda(x)k_{n}*\cdots*k_{n})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}

\displaystyle=\varphi(x).

for almost every
x\in G
, but since both
\check{\hat{\varphi}}
and
\varphi
are continuous, they coincide. Conversely, if
\phi\in Y^{\perp}
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\check{\phi}}

\displaystyle(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})=(\check{\phi},f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{n}}\check{\phi}(x_{1}\cdots x_{d})f_{1}(x_{1})\cdots f_{d}(x_{d})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{d}

\displaystyle=\int_{G^{n}}\langle\check{\eta}_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\check{\eta}_{d}(x_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle f_{1}(x_{1})\cdots f_{d}(x_{d})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{d}

\displaystyle=\langle{\eta}_{1}(f_{1})\cdots{\eta}_{d}(f_{d})v_{d},v_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\phi(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d})

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in C_{c}(G)
. By totality and continuity, we get
\hat{\check{\phi}}=\phi
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
0.3
.

Hanh-Banach’s theorem yields that
Y^{\perp}\cong(X_{d}^{\prime}(G)/Y)^{*}
isometrically. Thus, by Theorem
0.2
we have an isometric isomorphism

\displaystyle i:\varphi\in M_{d}(G)\mapsto[\hat{\varphi}]\in(X_{d}^{\prime}(G)/Y)^{*}

such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle[\hat{\varphi}]([f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}])=(\varphi,f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d})_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))}.

for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
. If we restrict its dual map onto
X_{d}^{\prime}(G)/Y
, we get an isometry

\displaystyle i^{*}:X_{d}^{\prime}(G)/Y\rightarrow M_{d}(G)^{*}

onto a closed subspace of
M_{d}(G)^{*}
(which is a Banach predual of
M_{d}(G)
) such that
i^{*}([f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{d}])=f_{1}*\cdots*f_{d}
for all
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in L^{1}(G)
. Since the inclusions
L^{1}(G)\otimes\cdots\otimes L^{1}(G)\subseteq X_{d}^{\prime}(G)
and
L^{1}(G)^{*d}\subseteq L^{1}(G)\subseteq X_{d}(G)
are dense, we deduce that
i^{*}(X_{d}^{\prime}(G)/Y)=X_{d}(G)

The ”if” part is handled in

Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq G
is a measurable, finite measured fundamental domain for
\Gamma
so that we have
G=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma\Omega
. We can normalize the Haar measure
dw
of
G
so that
\Omega
has measure 1. Define a unital mapping

\displaystyle i:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(G)\mapsto i(\varphi)\in\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma),\quad i(\varphi)(\gamma)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\gamma w)dw.

We claim that this map is normal. To see that, it is enough to show that the dual map
i^{*}
sends
\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
into
L^{1}(G)
. Take any
f\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
.
Observe that

\displaystyle(\varphi,i^{*}(f))_{(L^{\infty}(G),L^{\infty}(G)^{*})}

\displaystyle=(i(\varphi),f)_{(\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma),\ell^{1}(\Gamma))}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\gamma w)dw

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)1_{\gamma\Omega}(g)\right)\varphi(g)dg

and

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)1_{\gamma\Omega}(w)\right|dw\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|f(\gamma)||\gamma\Omega|=\|f\|_{1}<\infty.

This shows that
i^{*}(f)
is in
L^{1}(G)
and the claim is true.

Take any
\varphi\in M_{d}(G)

\xi_{i}
’s be as in Lemma
1.3

\displaystyle i(\varphi)(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d})=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d}w)dw=\xi_{1}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\xi_{d-1}(\gamma_{d-1})\int_{\Omega}\xi_{d}(\gamma_{d}w)dw.

This shows that the mapping

\displaystyle i:M_{d}(G)\rightarrow M_{d}(\Gamma)

\sigma(M_{d}(G),X_{d}(G))

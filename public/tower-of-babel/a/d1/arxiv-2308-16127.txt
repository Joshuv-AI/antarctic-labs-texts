Abstract
. The parabolic integro-differential Cauchy problem with spatially dependent coefficients is considered in generalized Bessel potential spaces where smoothness is defined by Lévy measures with O-regularly varying profile. The coefficients are assumed to be bounded and Hölder continuous in the spatial variable. Our results can cover interesting classes of Lévy measures that go beyond those comparable to d ​ y / | y | d + α . dy/\left|y\right|^{d+\alpha}.

Introduction

Let
\sigma\in\left(0,2\right)
and
\mathfrak{A}^{\sigma}
be the
class of all non-negative measures
\nu
on
\mathbf{R}_{0}^{d}=\mathbf{R}^{d}\backslash\left\{0\right\}

such that
\int\left(\left|y\right|^{2}\wedge 1\right)\nu\left(dy\right)<\infty

and

\sigma=\inf\left\{\alpha<2:\int_{\left|y\right|\leq 1}\left|y\right|^{\alpha}\nu\left(dy\right)<\infty\right\}.

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{A}^{\sigma}
, we consider the non-local parabolic
Cauchy problem with spatially dependent coefficients,

(1.1)

\displaystyle du\left(t,x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[L^{m,\nu}u\left(t,x\right)-\lambda u\left(t,x\right)+f\left(t,x\right)\right]dt

\displaystyle u\left(0,x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\left(t,x\right)\in E=\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbf{R}^{d}

with
\lambda\geq 0
and integro-differential operator
L^{m,\nu}\varphi\left(x\right)=L_{t,x}^{m,\nu}\varphi\left(x\right)=L_{t,z}^{m,\nu}|_{z=x}\varphi\left(x\right)

where
L_{t,z}^{m,\nu}
is given by

L=L_{t,z}^{m,\nu}=\int\left[\varphi\left(x+y\right)-\varphi\left(x\right)-\chi_{\sigma}\left(y\right)y\cdot\nabla\varphi\left(x\right)\right]m\left(t,z,y\right)\nu\left(dy\right),

for
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)
and
\chi_{\sigma}\left(y\right)=0

if
\sigma\in\left[0,1\right),\chi_{\sigma}\left(y\right)=1_{\left\{\left|y\right|\leq 1\right\}}\left(y\right)

if
\sigma=1
and
\chi_{\sigma}\left(y\right)=1
if
\sigma\in\left(1,2\right).

For
\nu\in\mathfrak{A}^{\sigma}
, we define its radial distribution
function

\delta\left(r\right)=\delta_{\nu}\left(r\right)=\nu\left(x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:\left|x\right|>r\right),r>0,

and

w\left(r\right)=w_{\nu}\left(r\right)=\delta_{\nu}\left(r\right)^{-1},r>0.

Let
w
be continuous and positive. Our main assumption is that
w_{\nu}\left(r\right)

is an O-RV function at both infinity and at zero. That is

r_{1}\left(x\right)=\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{w_{\nu}\left(\epsilon x\right)}{w_{\nu}\left(\epsilon\right)}<\infty,\hskip 10.00002ptr_{2}\left(x\right)=\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow\infty}\frac{w_{\nu}\left(\epsilon x\right)}{w_{\nu}\left(\epsilon\right)}<\infty,x>0.

The regular variation functions were introduced in
[
19
]

and used in tauberian theorems which were also extended to O-RV functions
(e.g.
[

20
]

We use standard notation

and

On

the space of locally integrable functions with the finite norm

Let

For

Note that

For

we define, following

27
]

as the closure of
\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)
in the

\displaystyle\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu;s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|J^{s}f\right|_{L_{p}}=\left|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1-\psi^{\nu_{sym}}\right){}^{s}\hat{f}\right]\right|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left(I-L^{\nu_{sym}}\right)^{s}f\right|_{L_{p}},f\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right).

Clearly,
H_{p}^{\nu;0}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)=L_{p}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)

and
H_{p}^{\nu;s}
is a Banach space.

Remark 3
.

We comment on some basic facts regarding

H_{p}^{\nu;s}.

(i) By
[
27
, Theorem 2.3.1]
, if
p\in\left(1,\infty\right),s<t

then
H_{p}^{\nu;t}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)\subseteq H_{p}^{\nu;s}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)

is continuously embedded.

(ii) By
[
27
, Theorem 2.2.7]
, for
s\geq 0,p\in\left[1,\infty\right),

the norm
\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu;s}}
is equivalent
to

\displaystyle\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu;s}}

\displaystyle=\left|f\right|_{L_{p}}+\left|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(-\psi^{\nu_{sym}}\right){}^{s}\mathcal{F}f\right]\right|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle=\left|f\right|_{L_{p}}+\left|L^{\nu;s}f\right|_{L_{p}}.

We define
H_{p}^{\nu;s}\left(E\right)
the space of locally integrable
functions with the finite norm,

\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu;s}\left(E\right)}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu;s}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

For the special case where
s=1
, we denote for simplicity of notation

H_{p}^{\nu}=H_{p}^{\nu;1}
and respectively
H_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)=H_{p}^{\nu;1}\left(E\right)
.
In this case,
\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu}}=\left|f\right|_{L_{p}}+\left|L^{\nu_{sym};1}f\right|_{L_{p}}.

As we will show later in Lemma
7

(see also Remark
6
) that under Assumptions
A

and
B
,
\left|L^{\nu_{sym};1}f\right|_{L_{p}}\asymp\left|L^{\nu}f\right|_{L_{p}}

which implies that the norm
\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu}}

is in fact equivalent to

\left|f\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu}}=\left|f\right|_{L_{p}}+\left|L^{\nu}f\right|_{L_{p}}.

The notation
H_{p}^{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)
and
H_{p}^{\alpha}\left(E\right)

are used when
\nu\left(dy\right)=dy/\left|y\right|^{d+\alpha},\alpha\in\left(0,2\right).

They are consistent with classical Bessel potential spaces and in
this case
L^{\nu}=c\left(\alpha,d\right)\left(-\triangle\right)^{\alpha/2}

where
\left(-\triangle\right)^{\alpha/2}
is a fractional Laplacian.

We will also work with another space of smooth functions
\tilde{C}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)

and
\tilde{C}^{\infty}\left(E\right)
the space of locally integrable
functions such that for all
1\leq p<\infty
and multiindex
\gamma\in\mathbf{N}_{0}^{d}
,

(2.4)

\sup_{x}\left|D^{\gamma}f\left(x\right)\right|+\left|D^{\gamma}f\right|_{L_{p}}<\infty

and

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{x}\left|D^{\gamma}f\left(t,x\right)\right|dt+\left|D^{\gamma}f\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}<\infty

respectively. For a fixed
1\leq p<\infty
, we use
\tilde{C}_{p}^{\infty}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right)

(resp.
\tilde{C}_{p}^{\infty}\left(E\right)
) the space of locally
integrable functions with finite norms (
2.4
) and (
2.5
)
respectively.

We now define the space in which we study (
1.1
). Let

\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)
be the space of all functions

u\in H_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)
such that

u\left(t,x\right)=\int_{0}^{t}F\left(s,x\right)ds,0\leq t\leq T

for some
F\in L_{p}\left(E\right).
It is a Banach space
equipped with the norm

\left|u\right|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)}=\left|u\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)}+\left|F\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}.

Definition 1
.

Let
f\in L_{p}\left(E\right)
. We say that
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)

is a solution of (
1.1
) if
L^{m,\nu}u\in L_{p}\left(E\right)

and

u\left(t\right)=\int_{0}^{t}\left[L^{m,\nu}u\left(s\right)-\lambda u\left(s\right)+f\left(s\right)\right]ds,\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq t\leq T

holds in
L_{p}\left(\mathbf{R}^{d}\right).

We are now ready to state our main results.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{A}^{\sigma},w=w_{\nu}

be a continuous O-RV function and
A, B, C
hold. Let
\beta\in\left(0,1\right),p>d/\beta
.
Then for any
f\in L_{p}\left(E\right)
there exists a unique solution

u\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)
to (
1.1
).
Moreover, there exist
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}\left(d,p,\nu,\beta,\kappa,k,K\right)>0

and
N=N\left(d,p,\nu,\beta,\kappa,k,K\right)>0
such that for any

\lambda\geq\lambda_{0},

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}u\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}+\left|L^{\nu}u\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}

\displaystyle\leq N\left|f\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}

\displaystyle\left|u\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}

\displaystyle\leq N\rho_{\lambda}\left|f\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}

where
\rho_{\lambda}=T\wedge\lambda^{-1}.
In addition, there exists

N=N\left(d,p,\nu,\beta,\kappa,k,K,T\right)>0
such that for any

\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left|u\right|_{H_{p}^{\nu}\left(E\right)}

\displaystyle\leq N\left|f\right|_{L_{p}\left(E\right)}.

2.4.
Examples

In this section, we provide some examples to illustrate that the selected
assumptions can indeed cover a variety of interesting Lévy measures
that go beyond those comparable to
dy/\left|y\right|^{d+\alpha}.

Example 1
.

We start with a concrete example
of an anisotropic measure with the same order
\alpha\in\left(0,2\right)

in every direction. Let
c_{i}>0,i=1,...,d
and
\epsilon_{0}
a
Dirac measure focused on the center in
\mathbf{R}^{d-1},

\nu\left(dy\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\left|y_{i}\right|^{-1-\alpha}dy_{i}\epsilon_{0}\left(dy_{1},...,dy_{i-1},dy_{i+1}dy_{d}\right).

By direct computation,

c_{i}\int_{\left|y\right|\geq r}\left|y_{i}\right|^{-1-\alpha}dy_{i}\epsilon_{0}\left(dy_{1},...,dy_{i-1},dy_{i+1}dy_{d}\right)=2c_{i}\int_{x\geq r}x^{-1-\alpha}dx=2c_{i}r^{-\alpha}.

Thus,
\text{$\delta$}_{\nu}\left(r\right)=\nu\left(x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:\left|x\right|>r\right)=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\right)r^{-\alpha}

and

w_{\nu}\left(r\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\right)^{-1}r^{\alpha}.

We may also check assumption
B
straightforwardly,
for
\left|\hat{\xi}\right|=1
and
r>0,

\displaystyle c_{i}w\left(r\right)\int_{\left|y\right|\leq 1}\left|y\cdot\hat{\xi}\right|^{2}\left|ry_{i}\right|^{-1-\alpha}d\left(ry_{i}\right)\epsilon_{0}\left(dy_{1},...,dy_{i-1},dy_{i+1}dy_{d}\right)

\displaystyle=2c_{i}w\left(r\right)r^{-\alpha}\int_{x\leq 1}\left|\hat{\xi}_{i}\right|^{2}x^{1-\alpha}dx=\frac{c_{i}}{\left(2-\alpha\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\right)}\left|\hat{\xi}_{i}\right|^{2}.

Therefore, for
\left|\hat{\xi}\right|=1
and
r>0
,

\int_{\left|y\right|\leq 1}\left|y\cdot\hat{\xi}\right|^{2}\tilde{\nu}_{r}\left(dy\right)\geq\frac{\min_{i=1,...,d}\left\{c_{i}\right\}}{\left(2-\alpha\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}\right)}>0.

The following two examples of measures are taken from
[
3
]

with slight modifications. In fact, the above example is a special
case of the next example as well.

Example 2
.

According to
[
25
]
(pp. 70-74), any Lévy measure
\nu\in\mathfrak{A}^{\sigma}

can be disintegrated as

\nu\left(\Gamma\right)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S_{d-1}}\chi_{\Gamma}\left(rz\right)\Pi\left(r,dz\right)d\delta_{\nu}\left(r\right),\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbf{R}_{0}^{d}\right),

where
\delta=\delta_{\nu}
, and
\Pi\left(r,d\omega\right),r>0

is a measurable family of measures on the unit sphere
S_{d-1}
with

\Pi\left(r,S_{d-1}\right)=1,r>0.
If

(i)
w_{\nu}=\delta^{-1}
is an continuous, O-RV and satisfies
Assumption
\mathbf{A}

(ii)
\left|\left\{s\in\left[0,1\right]:r_{i}\left(s\right)<1\right\}\right|>0,i=1,2,

and

(iii)
\inf_{\left|\hat{\xi}\right|=1}\int_{S_{d-1}}\left|\hat{\xi}\cdot z\right|^{2}\Pi\left(r,dz\right)\geq c_{0}>0,r>0,

then all assumptions of Theorem
1
holds.

Example 3
.

Consider Lévy measure in radial and angular coordinate in
the form

\nu\left(B\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\left|\omega\right|=1}1_{B}\left(rz\right)a\left(r,z\right)j\left(r\right)r^{d-1}S\left(dz\right)dr,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbf{R}_{0}^{d}\right),

where
S\left(dz\right)
is a finite measure on the unit sphere.

Assume

(i) There is
C>1,c>0,0<\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}<1
such
that

C^{-1}\phi\left(r^{-2}\right)\leq j\left(r\right)r^{d}\leq C\phi\left(r^{-2}\right)

and for all
0<r\leq R
,

c^{-1}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\delta_{1}}\leq\frac{\phi\left(R\right)}{\phi\left(r\right)}\leq c\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^{\delta_{2}}.

(ii) There is a function
\rho_{0}\left(\omega\right)

defined on the unit sphere such that
\rho_{0}\left(z\right)\leq a\left(r,z\right)\leq 1,r>0,z\in S_{d-1}
,
and for all
\left|\hat{\xi}\right|=1
,

\int_{S^{d-1}}\left|\hat{\xi}\cdot z\right|^{2}\rho_{0}\left(z\right)S\left(dz\right)\geq c>0.

Under these assumptions it can be shown that
\mathbf{B}
holds,
and
w_{\nu}
is an O-RV function with
2\delta_{1}\leq p_{i}^{\nu}\leq q_{i}^{\nu}\leq 2\delta_{2},i=1,2.

To provide some examples of
\phi
satisfying (i), we can take
\phi\left(r\right)=r^{\alpha}\left(\ln\left(1+r\right)\right)^{\beta},\alpha\in\left(0,1\right),\beta\in\left(0,1-\alpha\right)

or
\phi\left(r\right)=\left[\ln\left(\cosh\sqrt{r}\right)\right]^{\alpha},

\alpha\in\left(0,1\right).

Let
\nu\in\mathfrak{A}^{\sigma},w=w_{\nu}

0<k\leq m\left(t,y\right)\leq K,t\in\left[0,T\right],y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}

By Itô formula (e.g.
[
2

(3.3)

\displaystyle p^{m,\nu}\left(s,t,x\right)

\displaystyle ck\left|\xi\right|^{\alpha_{2}}\int_{\left|y\right|\leq\left|\xi\right|}\left[\text{$\cos$}\left(2\pi\hat{\xi}\cdot y\right)-1\right]\tilde{\nu}_{\left|\xi\right|R}\left(dy\right)

is an O-RV function at infinity
with the lower index of

p=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log r_{2}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)}{\log\epsilon}=-\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log r_{2}\left(\epsilon\right)}{\log\epsilon}=-q_{2}

and by a similar calculation the upper index is

q=-p_{2}.

Similarly, we consider the function
\gamma\left(x^{-1}\right)^{-1},x>0,

\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\gamma^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon x}\right)}{\gamma^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}=\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\gamma\left(\epsilon\right)}{\gamma\left(\epsilon x^{-1}\right)}=\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\gamma\left(\epsilon x^{-1}x\right)}{\gamma\left(\epsilon x^{-1}\right)}=r_{2}\left(x\right).

Therefore, the function
\gamma\left(x^{-1}\right)^{-1},x>0

is an O-RV function at infinity with

p=p_{2},q=q_{2}.

By applying
[
1
, Theorem 3]
with
\gamma{}^{-1}
,
there exists
N>1
so that for
r\geq N
,

(8.1)

\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds\asymp\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1}.

Now we extend the estimate to
r\in\left[1,N\right)
. On one hand,
by (
8.1
) and Lemma
14
,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds+\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1}\ln\left(N\right)+C\gamma\left(N\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
14
,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds\geq C\gamma\left(N\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq C\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1}.

Therefore,

(8.2)

\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds\asymp\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1},r\geq 1.

For
r<1
, we first note that by applying
[
1
, Theorem 3]
with

\gamma\left(x^{-1}\right)^{-1},x>0
, there exists
N>1
so that
for
x\geq N
.

(8.3)

\int_{1}^{x}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s^{-1}\right)s}ds\asymp\gamma\left(x^{-1}\right)^{-1}.

For
0<r\leq\frac{1}{N}
, we change the variable of integration then
apply (
8.3
), (
8.2
) and Lemma
14
,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{1}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{1/r}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s^{-1}\right)s}ds+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1}.

Moreover,

\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds\geq\int_{1}^{1/r}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s^{-1}\right)s}ds\geq C\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1}.

Therefore,

(8.4)

\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma\left(s\right)s}ds\asymp\gamma\left(r\right)^{-1},r\in\left(0,\frac{1}{N}\right].

Finally, the estimate for
r\in\left[\frac{1}{N},1\right)

follows from (
8.2
), (
8.4
) and
Lemma
14
.
∎

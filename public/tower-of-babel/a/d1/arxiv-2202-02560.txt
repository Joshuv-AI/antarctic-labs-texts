Abstract
. In this paper, we generalize the Bohr-Rogosinski sum for the Ma-Minda classes of starlike and convex functions. Also the phenomenon is studied for the classes of starlike functions with respect to symmetric points and conjugate points along with their convex cases. Further, the connection between the derived results and the known ones are established with the suitable examples.

. Primary 30C45, 30C50, Secondary 30C80.

Keywords and Phrases

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the class of analytic functions of the form
f(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z:|z|<1\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}
, the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
of univalent functions. Using subordination
[
18
]
, Ma and Minda
[
17
]
(also see
[
13
]
) introduced the unified class of univalent starlike and convex functions defined as follows:

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi):=\biggl\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\prec\psi(z)\biggl\}

and

\mathcal{C}(\psi):=\biggl\{f\in\mathcal{A}:1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\psi(z)\biggl\},

where
\psi
is univalent, analytic and sarlike with respect to
1
with
\Re{\psi(z)}>0
,
\psi^{\prime}(0)>0
,
\psi(0)=1
and
\psi(\mathbb{D})
is symmetric about real axis. Note that
\psi\in\mathcal{P}
, the class of Carathéodory functions. Also when
\psi(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
,
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\psi)
reduce to the standard classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}
of univalent starlike and convex functions respectively.

In GFT, radius problems have a long and glorious history that continues to this day, see the recent articles
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
. In 1914, Harald Bohr
[
8
]
proved a significant radius problem for the absolute power series:

Theorem 1.1

(Bohr’s Theorem,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
and
|g(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}||z|^{k}\leq 1,\quad\text{for}\quad|z|\leq\frac{1}{3}.

In analogy with Bohr’s Theorem, there is also the notion of Rogosinski radius, however a little is known about Rogosinski radius (also see
[
16
,
25
,
26
]
) as compared to Bohr radius, which is defined as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Rogosinski Theorem)
.

If
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
with
|g(z)|<1
, then for every
N\geq 1
, we have

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}b_{k}z^{k}\right|\leq 1,\quad\text{for}\quad|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The radius
1/2
is called the Rogosinski radius.

Recently, Kumar and Sahoo
[
29
]
obtained the generalized classical Bohr’s Theorem for functions satisfying
\Re{(f)(z)}\leq 1
. Also see, Kayumov et al.
[
28
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
29
, Theorem 2.2]

Let
\{\nu_{k}(r)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of non-negative continuous functions in
[0,1)
such that the series

\nu_{0}(r)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nu_{k}(r)

converges locally uniformly with respect to
r\in[0,1)
. Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
with
\Re{f(z)}\leq 1
and
p\in(0,1]
. If

\nu_{0}(r)>\frac{2(1+r^{m})}{p(1-r^{m})}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nu_{k}(r).

Then the following sharp inequality holds:

|f(z^{m})|\nu_{0}(r)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|\nu_{k}(r)\leq\nu_{0}(r)\quad\text{for all}\quad|z|=r\leq R_{1},

where
R_{1}
is the minimal postive root of the equation:

\nu_{0}(r)=\frac{2(1+r^{m})}{p(1-r^{m})}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nu_{k}(r).

In case when

\nu_{0}(r)<\frac{2(1+r^{m})}{p(1-r^{m})}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nu_{k}(r)

in some interval
(R_{1},R_{1}+\epsilon)
, then the number
R_{1}
can not be improved.

If we choose
\nu_{k}(r)=r^{k}
in Theorem
1.3
, we get Theorem
1.1
. At this juncture, it is natural to pose the following problem:

Problem 1.4
.

Can we establish the analogue of Theorem
1.3
for the Ma-Minda classes
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\psi)
?

Definition 1.5
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
and
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
. Let
d(f(0),\partial\Omega)
denotes the Euclidean distance between
f(0)
and the boundary of
\Omega=f(\mathbb{D})
. For a fixed
f
, consider a class of analytic functions

S(f):=\{g:g\prec f\}

or equivalently,

S(\Omega):=\{g:g(z)\in\Omega\}.

Then we say
S(f)
satisfies the
Generalized Bohr-Rogosinski phenomenon
, if there exists a constant
r_{0}\in(0,1]
such that

\mathcal{P}(r,g,f)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|b_{k}|\phi_{k}(r)\leq d(f(0),\partial\Omega),

holds for all
|z|=r\leq r_{0}
, where

(1)

\mathcal{P}(r,g,f)
represent some function of
r
or certain proper combination of moduli of
g
,
f
and their derivatives.

(2)

\{\phi_{k}(r)\}
be a sequence of non-negative continuous functions in
[0,1)
such that the series of the form

p_{0}\phi_{0}(r)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{k}\phi_{k}(r)

converges locally uniformly with respect to
r\in[0,1)
, where
p_{k}
depends on the function
f
and provide bounds for
b_{k}
.

For
\mathcal{P}(r,g,f)=|g(z)|
,
\phi_{k}(r)=r^{k}

(k\geq N)
and
0
otherwise in the Definition
1.5
gives the quantity considered by Kayumov et al.
[
11
]
, which is known as the Bohr-Rogosinski sum, given by

|g(z)|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|b_{k}||z|^{k},\quad|z|=r.

The link between the Bohr-Rogosinski and Bohr phenomenon can be noticed, if we replace
|g(z)|
by
g(0)
with
N=1
. We also refer the readers to see
[
1
,
4
]
. Now we see that the family
S(f)
has Bohr-Rogosinski phenomenon provided there exists
r^{f}_{N}\in(0,1]
such that the inequality:

|g(z)|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|b_{k}||z|^{k}\leq|f(0)|+d(f(0),\partial\Omega)

holds for
|z|=r\leq r^{f}_{N}
. The largest such
r^{f}_{N}
is called the Bohr-Rogosinski radius.

2.
Generalized Bohr’s sum for Ma-Minda starlike functions

We here solve the Problem
1.4
. But as we do not have sharp coefficient’s bound for each
a_{n}
for the given class in general. Thus to solve it, we need the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

The families
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
and
\mathcal{C}(\psi)
are normal and compact.

Proof.

From Montel’s Theorem
[
27
]
, we see that the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
is a normal family. Now let us prove that
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
is compact.
Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of functions from
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
. Suppose that
\{f_{n}\}
be convergent. Then it is well-known that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}:=f\in\mathcal{S}.

We show that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
. If possible, suppose that there exists a nonzero point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
such that

g(z_{0})\not\in\psi(\mathbb{D}),

where
g(z)=zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)
. Note that the corresponding sequence
\{g_{n}\}
converges to
g
, where
g_{n}(z)=zf^{\prime}_{n}(z)/f_{n}(z)
. Now let

\epsilon=dist(g(z_{0}),\partial\psi(\mathbb{D})).

Then the open ball

B(g(z_{0}),\epsilon)\not\subset\psi(\mathbb{D}).

Since
g_{n}\rightarrow g
, in particular,
g_{n}(z_{0})\rightarrow g(z_{0})
. There exists
n(\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}

such that

g_{n}(z_{0})\in B(g(z_{0}),\epsilon),\quad\forall n\geq n(\epsilon),

which implies

g_{n}(z_{0})\not\in\psi(\mathbb{D}),\quad\forall n\geq n(\epsilon).

But as
f_{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
,

g_{n}(z_{0})=\frac{z_{0}f^{\prime}_{n}(z_{0})}{f_{n}(z_{0})}\in\psi(\mathbb{D}),\quad\forall n.

Hence, we must have
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
, that means the family
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
is compact. With the similar arguments, it is easy to see that the family
\mathcal{C}(\psi)
is also compact. ∎

Remark 2.1
.

(
Existence of sharp coefficients Bounds)

Let us consider the real-valued functional
\mathcal{J}
defined on
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
as

\mathcal{J}(f)=\max\{|a_{n}|\}\quad\text{\it for every}\quad f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi),

where
n
is fixed and
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. From Lemma
2.1
,
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
is normal and compact.
Further, since
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{*}
, we have
|a_{n}|\leq n
, that means
\mathcal{J}
is a bounded functional. Hence, following the discussion in the Goodman’s Book
[
27
, page no. 44-45]
, we conclude that

\mathcal{J}(f)=\max\{|a_{n}|\}\quad\text{\it exists in the family}\quad\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi).

Thus, let us say that

\max_{f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)}\{|a_{n}|\}:=M(n)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For instance,
M(n)=n
for the class of univalent starlike functions. For the Janowski starlike functions, it is given by
[
5
, Theorem 3]
.

We can now state our result in a general setting whose complement is Theorem
2.3
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{\phi_{n}(r)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of non-negative continuous functions in
(0,1)
such that the series

\phi_{1}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}M(n)\phi_{n}(r)

converges locally uniformly with respect to each
r\in[0,1)
.
If for
\beta\in[0,1]

(2.1)

\beta f^{\prime}_{0}(r^{m})+(1-\beta)f_{0}(r^{m})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(n)\phi_{n}(r)<-f_{0}(-1).

and the function
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
.
Then the following inequality

\beta|f^{\prime}(z^{m})|+(1-\beta)|f(z^{m})|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\phi_{n}(r)\leq d(0,\partial{\Omega})

holds for
|z|=r\leq r_{0}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega=f(\mathbb{D})
and
r_{0}
is the smallest positive root of the equation:

(2.2)

\beta f^{\prime}_{0}(r^{m})+(1-\beta)f_{0}(r^{m})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(n)\phi_{n}(r)=-f_{0}(-1),

where
M(1)=1
and

f_{0}(z)=z\exp\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\psi(t)-1}{t}dt.

In case when
f_{0}
or it’s rotation serves as an extremal for the coefficient’s bounds
M(n)
, then the radius
r_{0}
is sharp.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.1
and Remark
2.1
, we see that: (a)
\mathcal{J}
is a bounded real valued continuous functional, (b)
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
is a normal family, and (c)
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
is a compact family in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus, the sharp bounds for each
a_{n}
exists. In view of Remark
2.1
, we have

|a_{n}|\leq M(n),

which yields that

(2.3)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\phi_{n}(r)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(n)\phi_{n}(r).

The Koebe-radius for the functions in
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\psi)
satisfies

(2.4)

d(0,\partial f(\mathbb{D}))\geq-f_{0}(-1).

Now combining it with the growth and distortion theorems
[
17
]
, and using the condition
2.1
, the inequalities (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) gives

\displaystyle\beta|f^{\prime}(z^{m})|

\displaystyle+(1-\beta)|f(z^{m})|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|\phi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\beta f^{\prime}_{0}(r^{m})+(1-\beta)f_{0}(r^{m})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(n)\phi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\leq d(0,\partial{\Omega}),

which holds in
|z|=r\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is the minimal positive root of the equation (
2.2
). Existence of the root
r_{0}
follows from the Intermediate value theorem for continuous function in
(0,1)
.
To see the sharpness case, let us consider the function

f_{0}(z)=z\exp\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\psi(t)-1}{t}dt.

such that it’s Taylor series coefficients
a_{n}(f_{0})
satisfies
|a_{n}(f_{0})|=M(n)
. For this function we have

d(0,\partial f(\mathbb{D}))=-f_{0}(-1),

and the following equality holds for
|z|=r_{0}
:

\beta f^{\prime}_{0}(r^{m})+(1-\beta)f_{0}(r^{m})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(n)\phi_{n}(r)=d(0,\partial f(\mathbb{D})),

and therefore, if
f_{0}
is extremal for each coefficient’s bound, then the radius
r_{0}
can not be improved. ∎

Question 2.2
.

What if we do not have
M(n)
?

For such cases, the following result complements Theorem
2.1
:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\{\phi_{n}(r)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a non-negative sequence of continuous functions in
[0,1]
such that

\phi_{1}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f^{(n)}_{0}(0)}{n!}\right|\phi_{n}(r)

converges locally uniformly with respect to each
r\in[0,1)

\mathcal{C}_{c}(\psi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{A}:\frac{(zf^{\prime}(z))^{\prime}}{(f(z)+\overline{f(\bar{z})})^{\prime}}\prec\psi(z)\right\}.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\psi)
. If
g\in S_{f}(\psi)
. Then

|g(z^{m})|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|b_{k}||z|^{k}\leq d(0,\partial{\Omega})

holds for
|z|=r_{b}\leq\min\{\frac{1}{3},r_{0}\}
, where
m,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega=f(\mathbb{D})
and
r_{0}
is the minimal positive root of the equation:

(3.17)

k_{\psi}(r^{m})+R^{N}(r)+k_{\psi}(-1)=0,

where

R^{N}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}{M_{t}^{N}(k^{\prime}_{\psi}).M_{t}^{N}(\psi)}dtds,

and
k_{\psi}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}l_{n}z^{n}
is given by (
3.16
).
The result is sharp when
r_{b}=r_{0}
and
l_{n}>0
.

Proof.

Consider the function

G(z)=\frac{f(z)+\overline{f(\bar{z})}}{2}.

Then
G\in\mathcal{C}(\psi)
. Now from Definition
3.7
, we see that

(3.18)

(zf^{\prime}(z))^{\prime}=G^{\prime}(z)\psi(\omega(z)).

This gives

(3.19)

f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}G^{\prime}(t)\psi(\omega(t))dtdy.

As
G^{\prime}\prec k^{\prime}_{\psi}
, see Lemma
3.2
, it follows using Lemma
1.1
that

(3.20)

M_{r}^{N}(G^{\prime})\leq M_{r}^{N}(k^{\prime}_{\psi})\quad\text{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

Hence, using (
3.18
), (
3.19
) and (
3.20
)

\displaystyle|g(z^{m})|+M_{r}^{N}(f)

\displaystyle\leq k_{\psi}(r^{m})+\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}M_{t}^{N}(G^{\prime})M_{t}^{N}(\psi\circ\omega)dtdy

\displaystyle\leq k_{\psi}(r^{m})+\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{y}\int_{0}^{y}M_{t}^{N}(k^{\prime}_{\psi})M_{t}^{N}(\psi)dtdy

\displaystyle\leq-k_{\psi}(-1)

\displaystyle\leq d(0,\partial\Omega),

holds for
|z|=r\leq r_{b}=\{1/3,r_{0}\}
, where
r_{0}
is minimal root of the equation (
3.17
). The existence of
0<r_{0}<1
can be seen by Intermediate value theorem for continuous function in
[0,1]
. The case of equality

|g(z^{m})|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}|b_{k}||z|^{k}=d(0,\partial{\Omega})

follows with the choice
g=f=k_{\psi}
, whenever
r_{b}=r_{0}
and
l_{n}>0
. ∎

Definition 3.10

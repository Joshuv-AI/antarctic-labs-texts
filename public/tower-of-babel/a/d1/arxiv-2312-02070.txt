Genus bounds from unrolled quantum groups at roots of unity
Daniel López Neumann and Roland van der Veen

Abstract
. For any simple complex Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , we show that the degrees of the “ADO” link polynomials coming from the unrolled restricted quantum group U ¯ q H ​ ( 𝔤 ) \overline{U}^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) at a root of unity give lower bounds to the Seifert genus of the link. We give a direct simple proof of this fact relying on a Seifert surface formula involving universal 𝔲 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) \mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) -invariants, where 𝔲 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) \mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) is the small quantum group. We give a second proof by showing that the invariant P 𝔲 q ​ ( 𝔟 ) θ ​ ( K ) P_{\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})}^{\theta}(K) of our previous work [ 21 ] coincides with such ADO invariants, where 𝔲 q ​ ( 𝔟 ) \mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}) is the Borel part of 𝔲 q ​ ( 𝔤 ) \mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}) . To prove this, we show that equivariantizations of relative Drinfeld centers of crossed products essentially contain unrolled restricted quantum groups, a fact that could be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

The quantum invariants of knots and links form a general class of topological invariants built from the theory of braided tensor categories, the main source being the representation categories of quantum groups
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, which depend on a parameter
q\in\mathbb{C}
. These categories are semisimple if and only if
q
is not a root of unity, and this fact leads to two different families of link polynomials. The generic case leads to the well-known Jones, HOMFLY and Kauffman polynomials and their colored versions. When
q
is a root of unity, one obtains the Akutsu-Deguchi-Ohtsuki (ADO) invariants
[
1
]
or the Costantino-Geer-Patureau (CGP) invariants
[
4
]
. While the former family has been intensively studied since their introduction, interest in the latter family of “non-semisimple” invariants seems to have grown only quite recently as they appear in a new kind of TQFTs
[
2
]
and are conjectured to be related to the
q
-series predicted by physicists
[
13
,
14
,
12
]
.

In our previous work
[
21
]
, we found a structural difference between the above two families of invariants. While relations between quantum invariants at generic
q
and geometric topology are at most the subject of various conjectures
[
17
,
7
]
, we showed that the ADO invariants of
[
1
]
(for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
) provide lower bounds to the Seifert genus. This was part of a general theorem giving genus bounds from finite dimensional Hopf algebras. Previous to our work, the only genus bound known to come from quantum invariants (and that is not simply the Alexander bound) was that of Ohtsuki for the 2-loop part of the Kontsevich invariant
[
23
]
. After our work, genus bounds for link polynomials coming from the quantum supergroup of
\mathfrak{sl}(2|1)
(a.k.a. Links-Gould invariants) were found in
[
18
]
.

We now explain our results in detail. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple complex Lie algebra of rank
n
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
a set of simple roots and let
\Delta^{+}
be the set of positive roots. Write

\sum_{\beta\in\Delta^{+}}\beta=k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+k_{n}\alpha_{n}

where
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
q
be a primitive root of unity of order
r\geq 2
and let
r^{\prime}=r
if
r
is odd and
r^{\prime}=r/2
if
r
is even. We further suppose
r
coprime to the determinant of the Cartan matrix. If
f
is a Laurent polynomial in variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
(possibly with half-integer powers) and if
N_{i}
(resp.
n_{i}
) denotes the maximal power (resp. minimal power) of
t_{i}
appearing in some monomial in
f
, then we define
\text{deg}_{t_{i}}f:=N_{i}-n_{i}
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is simply-laced. If
L\subset S^{3}
is an
s
-component link, then there is an isotopy invariant

\text{ADO}_{\mathfrak{g},r}^{\prime}(L)\in(t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{k_{n}})^{\frac{(r^{\prime}-1)(s-1)}{2}}\cdot\mathbb{Q}(q)[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]

satisfying

\text{deg}_{t_{i}}\text{ADO}_{\mathfrak{g},r}^{\prime}(L)\leq(2g(L)+s-1)(r^{\prime}-1)k_{i}

for every
i=1,\dots,n
, where
g(L)
is the Seifert genus of
L
.

The definition of
\text{ADO}_{\mathfrak{g},r}^{\prime}(L)
is based on the category
\mathcal{C}^{H}
of weight modules over the unrolled restricted quantum group
\overline{U}^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
at a root of unity, which is a ribbon category
[
8
,
9
,
25
]
. In this case, there are finite-dimensional weight modules
V_{\lambda}
with highest weight
\lambda
for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
\text{ADO}_{\mathfrak{g},r}^{\prime}(L)|_{t_{i}=q^{2\lambda(H_{i})}}
is the result of applying the Reshetikhin-Turaev construction
[
24
]
to any
(1,1)
-tangle with closure
L
in which all components have the same color
V_{\lambda}
. We denote it by
\text{ADO}^{\prime}
, since the original ADO
[
1
]
and generalizations
[
4
]
are certain normalizations of ours. Our proof is based on a Seifert surface formula involving the action of a universal
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-invariant

on
V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}
, where we can apply the degree bound of Proposition
2.11
. For the non-simply-laced case see Theorem
3.3
.

The invariant
\text{ADO}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2},4}
is the 1-variable Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}

[
19
]
and the genus bound for
\text{ADO}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2},r}(K)
was shown in
[
21
]
. The invariant
\text{ADO}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{3},4}
was studied by Harper in
[
16
]
and shown to distinguish the Kinoshita-Terasaka from the Conway knot. As an example, we show that
\text{ADO}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{3},4}
actually gives the correct genus bounds for these two knots.

In our second theorem, we explain the above bound through our previous work. Based on
[
20
]
, we built in
[
21
]
an
n
-variable knot polynomial

out of a
\mathbb{N}^{n}
-graded Hopf algebra

gives lower bounds to the Seifert genus. This construction relied on the relative Drinfeld center of a crossed product

. When

and one gets a braided

-equivariantization of

is a braided category in the usual sense. Kauffman-Radford pairs on
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
(see Subsection

(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{H},c^{\chi})
be the ribbon category of Subsection

.

Theorem 2
.

The category
(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}^{H},c^{\chi})
embeds as a full ribbon subcategory of the
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
-equivarianti-
zation of
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}))}(\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}))\rtimes\mathfrak{h}^{*})

\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})

We find interesting that the braided and ribbon structure of

can be deduced from that of the small quantum group by the above theorem. Relative Drinfeld centers appear in Turaev-Virelizier’s work on HQFT
[
27

, so our theorem might be useful in order to define TQFTs for ADO or CGP invariants in a Turaev-Viro style. From the above theorem we deduce the following (which was shown in
[
21
]
for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
).

Theorem 3
.

The multivariable polynomial invariant
P_{\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})}^{\theta}(K)
of
[
21
]
equals
\text{ADO}^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{g},r}(K)
.

Note that the genus bound of
[
21
]
was only for the total degree and only for knots in
S^{3}
, so Theorem
1
is still more general that what can be deduced via Theorem
3
and
[
21
]
.

The plan of the paper is the following. Section
2
contains some preliminaries on unrolled quantum groups and weight modules. The main result here is the degree bound of Proposition

. In Section
3
we define the

invariants of links, give a Seifert surface formula for them in terms of universal

-invariants and prove Theorem

. We also give some computations in the case

and

2
and
3

Unrolled restricted quantum groups

In this section we recall some standard facts from unrolled quantum groups: PBW basis, restricted quantum groups, weight modules, braiding, pivotal and ribbon structure, etc. We follow mainly

2.1.
Unrolled quantum groups

Let
U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
be the algebra with generators
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}^{\pm 1},H_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
and relations

\displaystyle[H_{i},E_{j}]

\displaystyle=a_{ij}E_{j},

\displaystyle[H_{i},F_{j}]

\displaystyle=-a_{ij}F_{j},

\displaystyle[H_{i},K_{j}^{\pm 1}]

\displaystyle=0=[H_{i},H_{j}],

\displaystyle K_{i}E_{j}

\displaystyle=q^{a_{ij}}E_{j}K_{i},

\displaystyle K_{i}F_{j}

\displaystyle=q^{-a_{ij}}F_{j}K_{i}

\displaystyle K_{i}K_{j}

\displaystyle=K_{j}K_{i},

\displaystyle[E_{i},F_{j}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\frac{K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle K_{i}K_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle=K_{i}^{-1}K_{i}=1,

for every
i,j
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k}\left[\begin{matrix}1-a_{ij}\\
k\end{matrix}\right]_{q}E_{i}^{1-a_{ij}-k}E_{j}E_{i}^{k}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k}\left[\begin{matrix}1-a_{ij}\\
k\end{matrix}\right]_{q}F_{i}^{1-a_{ij}-k}F_{j}F_{i}^{k}

\displaystyle=0,

for
i\neq j
. This is a Hopf algebra if we define

\displaystyle\Delta(E_{i})

\displaystyle=E_{i}\otimes K_{i}+1\otimes E_{i},

\displaystyle\Delta(F_{i})

\displaystyle=K_{i}^{-1}\otimes F_{i}+F_{i}\otimes 1,

\displaystyle\Delta(K_{i})

\displaystyle=K_{i}\otimes K_{i},

\displaystyle\Delta(H_{i})

\displaystyle=H_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes H_{i}

for every
i
. For
\gamma=a_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+a_{n}\alpha_{n}\in L_{R}
we denote
K_{\gamma}:=K_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots K_{n}^{a_{n}}
.

We denote by
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\subset U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
the subalgebra generated by
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}^{\pm 1}
. We denote by
U(\mathfrak{h})
the subalgebra of
U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by the
H_{i}
’s. Let
V_{+}
(resp.
V_{-}
) be the subalgebra generated by
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}
(resp.
F_{1},\dots,F_{n}
).

The algebra
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is
L_{R}
-graded if we set
d(E_{i})=\alpha_{i},d(F_{i})=-\alpha_{i}
and we extend by multiplicativity. This way

becomes a
L_{R}
-graded Hopf algebra.

2.2.
PBW basis

Let
\Delta^{+}
be the set of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}
. The root vectors
E_{1},\dots,E_{n}
correspond to the simple roots
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
. To define root vectors for all positive roots consider a reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{N}}
. Then all positive roots occur exactly once in

\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1}},\beta_{2}=s_{i_{2}}(\alpha_{i_{2}}),\dots,\beta_{N}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{N-1}}(\alpha_{i_{N}}).

Then we define

E_{\beta_{k}}=T_{i_{1}}\dots T_{i_{k-1}}(E_{i_{k}}),\ \ F_{\beta_{k}}=T_{i_{1}}\dots T_{i_{k-1}}(F_{i_{k}}).

where the
T_{i}
’s are determined by the braid action of
B(\mathfrak{g})
on
U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

[
3
, Theorem 8.1.2]
:

\displaystyle T_{i}(E_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k-a_{ij}}\frac{q^{-k}}{[-a_{ij}-k]_{q}![k]_{q}!}E_{i}^{-a_{ij}-k}E_{j}E_{i}^{k},

\displaystyle T_{i}(F_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{-a_{ij}}(-1)^{k-a_{ij}}\frac{q^{k}}{[-a_{ij}-k]_{q}![k]_{q}!}F_{i}^{k}F_{j}F_{i}^{-a_{ij}-k}

for
i\neq j
, and
T_{i}(E_{i})=-F_{i}K_{i},T_{i}(F_{i})=-K_{i}^{-1}E_{i}
.

Note that
T_{i}(E_{j})
has
L_{R}
-degree
\alpha_{j}-a_{ij}\alpha_{i}=s_{i}(\alpha_{j})
. It follows that
E_{\beta_{k}}
has degree
s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{k-1}}(\alpha_{i_{k}})=\beta_{k}
. Thus,
d(E_{\beta})=\beta
and similarly
d(F_{\beta})=-\beta
for all
\beta\in\Delta^{+}
.

Now for every
I:\Delta^{+}\to\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
set

E^{I}:=E_{\beta_{N}}^{I(\beta_{N})}\cdots E_{\beta_{1}}^{I(\beta_{1})},\ \ F^{I}:=F_{\beta_{N}}^{I(\beta_{N})}\cdots F_{\beta_{1}}^{I(\beta_{1})}.

If for every
I
we denote

d(I)=\sum_{\beta\in\Delta^{+}}I(\beta)\beta\in L_{R},

then
d(E^{I})=d(I)
and
d(F^{I})=-d(I)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[

]
)
For each positive root

and

2.3.
Unrolled restricted and small quantum groups

The
restricted
unrolled quantum group is the quotient
\overline{U}^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
of
U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
by the two-sided ideal generated by

and
F_{\beta}^{r^{\prime}}

\beta\in\Delta^{+}
. Here

if

r^{\prime}=r/2
if

is even. Note that this ideal is a Hopf ideal, this is because
E_{i}\otimes K_{i}
and

q^{2}
-commute for all

\overline{U}^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by

and simply called the restricted quantum group. The

\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is the quotient of
\overline{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
by setting

for all

. The
small Borel
is the subalgebra
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

.

Proposition 2.2
.

\{K_{\gamma}E^{I}F^{J}\ |\ \gamma\in L_{R}\text{ and }I,J:\Delta^{+}\to\{0,\dots,r^{\prime}-1\}\}

\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

2.4.
Weight modules

A
weight module
is a
\overline{U}^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module for which
U(\mathfrak{h})
acts diagonalizably and
K_{i}
acts as
q^{H_{i}}
for every
i
. Let
V
be a weight module. For each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
let

V(\lambda)=\{v\in V\ |\ Hv=\lambda(H)v,H\in\mathfrak{h}\}.

Then
E_{i}(V(\lambda))\subset V(\lambda+\alpha_{i})
and
F_{i}(V(\lambda))\subset V(\lambda-\alpha_{i})
. Thus, for any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
\oplus_{\gamma\in L_{R}}V(\lambda+\gamma)
is a submodule of
V
. Let
G=\mathfrak{h}^{*}/L_{R}
and let
\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[\lambda]}
be the category of weight modules whose weights are in the class of
[\lambda]:=\lambda+L_{R}\in G
. Then the whole category
\mathcal{C}^{H}
of weight modules splits as a direct sum

\mathcal{C}^{H}=\bigoplus_{[\lambda]\in G}\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[\lambda]}.

Moreover, one has
\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[\lambda]}\otimes\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[\mu]}\subset\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[\lambda]+[\mu]}
for each
[\lambda],[\mu]\in G
.
The neutral component consists of modules with weights in
L_{R}
. Modules in
\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[0]}
are modules over the small quantum group
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(where
K_{i}^{r}=1
for all
i
) with a choice of logarithm for the weights (the weights of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
are only defined mod
r
). Thus, there is a forgetful functor
F:\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[0]}\to\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}))
.

2.6.
Pivotal structure

If
V

For

\displaystyle j_{V_{\lambda}}(v^{\prime\prime}_{A})=q^{(r^{\prime}-1)(\lambda-d(A),2\rho)}v_{A}

2.7.
Braiding on weight modules

The category of weight

\mathcal{H}

\overline{\mathcal{K}}=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in Q_{r}}q^{-(\alpha,\beta)}K_{\alpha}\otimes K_{\beta}

where
Q_{r}=\{c_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+c_{n}\alpha_{n}\ |\ 0\leq c_{i}\leq r-1\}\subset L_{R}
and
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})=a_{ij}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V(\lambda)
and
w\in W(\mu)
where
\lambda=\sum_{i}c_{i}\alpha_{i}\in L_{R}
and
\mu=\sum_{i}d_{i}\alpha_{i}\in L_{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{K}}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in Q_{r}}q^{-(\alpha,\beta)+(\alpha,\lambda)+(\beta,\mu)}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l}\in(\mathbb{Z}/r)^{n}}q^{-\boldsymbol{k}^{t}A\boldsymbol{l}+\boldsymbol{k}^{t}A\boldsymbol{c}+\boldsymbol{d}^{t}A\boldsymbol{l}}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}q^{\boldsymbol{k}^{t}A\boldsymbol{c}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{l}}q^{(-\boldsymbol{k}^{t}+\boldsymbol{d}^{t})A\boldsymbol{l}}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}q^{\boldsymbol{k}^{t}A\boldsymbol{c}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}}q^{(-\boldsymbol{k}^{t}+\boldsymbol{d}^{t})\boldsymbol{x}}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{n}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}q^{\boldsymbol{k}^{t}A\boldsymbol{c}}r^{n}\delta_{-\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{d},0}(v\otimes w)

\displaystyle=q^{\boldsymbol{d}^{t}A\boldsymbol{c}}(v\otimes w)=q^{(\lambda,\mu)}(v\otimes w)=\mathcal{H}(v\otimes w).

Note that in the fourth equality we used our assumption that
A
is invertible mod
r
and in the fifth equality we used that
\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/r)^{n}}q^{\boldsymbol{y}^{t}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}=r^{n}\delta_{\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{0}}
since
q
is a primitive
r
-th root of unity.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

The forgetful functor
F:\mathcal{C}^{H}_{[0]}\to\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}))
is braided.

Here we consider
\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}))
with the braiding given by
c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=\tau_{V,W}(\overline{\mathcal{K}}\cdot\overline{\Theta}(v\otimes w)).

2.9.
Lemmas on PBW

We now prove some simple lemmas concerning the algebra structure on

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
I,J
one can write
F^{I}F^{J}=\sum c_{A}(q)F^{A}
for some
c_{A}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
and
d(A)=d(I)+d(J)
.

Proof.

This is obvious. This lemma only says that
V_{-}
is graded by the root lattice and multiplication preserves the grading (and that
F^{I}
has degree
-d(I)
).
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

If
I
is not a simple root then
F^{I}=\sum c_{A,B}(q)F^{A}F^{B}
where
c_{A,B}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
and
d(A)+d(B)=d(I)
with
0<d(A),d(B)<d(I)
.

Proof.

Clearly, we only need to show this when
F^{I}=F_{\beta_{k}}
and
\beta_{k}
is not a simple root. Note first that from the above formula of
T_{i}(F_{j})
one sees that
a_{ij}=0
implies
T_{i}(F_{j})=F_{j}
and
a_{ij}<0
implies
T_{i}(F_{j})
(whose degree is
\alpha_{j}-a_{ij}\alpha_{i}
) is a sum of products of
F_{l}
’s of smaller
L_{R}
-degree. Indeed, note that if
0<k\leq-a_{ij}
then
0<k\alpha_{i},(-a_{ij}-k)\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}<\alpha_{j}-a_{ij}\alpha_{i}
and if
k=0
,
0<\alpha_{j},-a_{ij}\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{j}-a_{ij}\alpha_{i}
.
Now, if
\beta_{k}
is a non-simple positive root, then there is an index
i_{l}
(in the decomposition
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{N}}
) with
1\leq l\leq k-1
such that
a_{i_{l},i_{l+1}}<0
. Since the
T_{i}
’s are algebra automorphisms, it follows from the previous discussion that
F_{\beta_{k}}=T_{i_{1}}\dots T_{i_{k-1}}(F_{i_{k}})
is a sum of products of
F_{i}
’s of smaller
L_{R}
-degree.

∎

Denote
[q^{n}K_{i}]=\frac{q^{n}K_{i}-q^{-n}K_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}
. Note that
K_{i}F^{A}=q^{-(d(A),\alpha_{i})}F^{A}K_{i}
so that
[q^{n}K_{i}]F^{A}=F^{A}[q^{n-(d(A),\alpha_{i})}K_{i}]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

For every
i
and
A:\Delta^{+}\to\{0,\dots,r^{\prime}-1\}
we have

E_{i}F^{A}=F^{A}E_{i}+\sum_{B}c_{B}(q)F^{B}[q^{n_{B}}K_{i}]

for some
c_{B}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
and
n_{B}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

If
A
is a simple root this is obvious. If not, we can use Lemma
2.7
to write
F^{A}=\sum c_{B,C}(q)F^{B}F^{C}
with
d(B),d(C)<d(A)
. By induction

\displaystyle E_{i}F^{A}

\displaystyle=\sum c(q)E_{i}F^{B}F^{C}

\displaystyle=\sum c(q)(F^{B}E_{i}+\sum_{D}d(q)F^{D}[q^{n_{D}}K_{i}])F^{C}

\displaystyle=\sum c(q)F^{B}E_{i}F^{C}+\sum f(q)F^{D}F^{C}[q^{n_{D}-(d(C),\alpha_{i})}K_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum c(q)F^{B}(F^{C}E_{i}+\sum e(q)F^{I}[q^{n_{I}}K_{i}])+\sum f(q)F^{D}F^{C}[q^{n_{D}-(d(C),\alpha_{i})}K_{i}]

\displaystyle=F^{A}E_{i}+\sum g(q)F^{B}F^{I}[q^{n_{I}}K_{i}]+\sum f(q)F^{D}F^{C}[q^{n_{D}-(d(C),\alpha_{i})}K_{i}].

The proof is finished by writing each
F^{B}F^{I},F^{D}F^{C}
as a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-linear combination of
F^{J}
’s (Lemma
2.6
).
∎

2.10.
Lemmas on Verma modules

The Verma
V_{\lambda}
has basis the vectors
v_{A}:=F^{A}v_{0}
where
A:\Delta^{+}\to\{0,\dots,r^{\prime}-1\}
. We will consider
V^{*}_{\lambda}
with the dual basis
v^{\prime}_{A}
, that is,
\langle v^{\prime}_{A},v_{B}\rangle=\delta_{A,B}
and
X=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}
with the tensor product basis. We refer to these bases (and tensor products of these) as standard bases. We consider
q^{\lambda(H_{1})},\dots,q^{\lambda(H_{n})}
as independent variables. For simplicity, we denote
\lambda_{i}=\lambda(H_{i})
.

Lemma 2.9
.

In the standard basis of
V_{\lambda}
,
E_{i}
acts by a matrix with coefficients of the form
a(q)q^{\lambda_{i}}+b(q)q^{-\lambda_{i}}
with
a(q),b(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
. In the dual basis of
V_{\lambda}^{*}
,
E_{i}
acts by a matrix with coefficients of the form
c(q)+d(q)q^{-2\lambda_{i}}
,
c(q),d(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
.

Proof.

Using
E_{i}v_{0}=0
,
K_{i}v_{0}=q^{\lambda_{i}}v_{0}
and Lemma
2.8
we find:

\displaystyle E_{i}v_{A}

\displaystyle=E_{i}F^{A}v_{0}=F^{A}E_{i}v_{0}+\sum_{B}c_{B}(q)F^{B}[q^{n_{B}}K_{i}]v_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B}c_{B}(q)[n_{B}+\lambda_{i}]_{q}v_{B}=\sum_{B}c^{\prime}_{B}(q)(q^{n_{B}+\lambda_{i}}-q^{-n_{B}-\lambda_{i}})v_{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B}(a_{B}(q)q^{\lambda_{i}}+b_{B}(q)q^{-\lambda_{i}})v_{B}

This shows the first part of the lemma. Now, the matrix representing
E_{i}
acting on
V_{\lambda}^{*}
in the dual basis is the transpose of the matrix of
S(E_{i})=-E_{i}K_{i}^{-1}
acting on
V_{\lambda}
. But
K_{i}^{-1}
is represented by a diagonal matrix with coefficients of the form
q^{m_{B}-\lambda_{i}},m_{B}\in\mathbb{Z}
hence multiplying this with the previous matrix of
E_{i}
, we get a matrix with coefficients of the desired form.
∎

In the following lemmas we consider
X=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}
as a
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module.

Lemma 2.10
.

In the standard basis of
X
,
E_{i}
acts by matrices whose coefficients have the form
c(q)+d(q)q^{-2\lambda_{i}}
with
c(q),d(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
. In the same basis of
X
,
F_{i}
acts by a matrix with
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-coefficients.

Proof.

Indeed,
K_{i}v^{\prime}_{B}=q^{-\lambda_{i}+m_{B}}v^{\prime}_{B}
for some
m_{B}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence the matrix representing the action of
E_{i}\otimes K_{i}
on
X
has coefficients of the form

(a(q)q^{\lambda_{i}}+b(q)q^{-\lambda_{i}})q^{-\lambda_{i}+m_{B}}=c(q)+d(q)q^{-2\lambda_{i}}

for some
c(q),d(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
. Now, by the previous lemma,
E_{i}
acts on
V_{\lambda}^{*}
by a matrix of the same form, hence so does the matrix of
1\otimes E_{i}
on
X
. Thus,
E_{i}\otimes K_{i}+1\otimes E_{i}
acts by such a matrix, and this is exactly the matrix of the action of
E_{i}
on
X
. The second assertion follows from
\Delta(F_{i})=K_{i}^{-1}\otimes F_{i}+F_{i}\otimes 1
and
S(F_{i})=-K_{i}F_{i}
: the
q^{-\lambda_{i}}
’s of the action of
K_{i}^{-1}
on
V_{\lambda}
cancel with the
q^{\lambda_{i}}
’s of the action of
-K_{i}F_{i}
on
V_{\lambda}
, hence only
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-coefficients appear.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
z\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and consider its action on the module
X=V_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda}^{*}
. Then, in the standard basis of
X
, each coefficient of the matrix representing
z
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}(q)[q^{-2\lambda_{1}},\dots,q^{-2\lambda_{n}}]
where the power of each
q^{-2\lambda_{i}}
appearing in some monomial is bounded above by
(r^{\prime}-1)k_{i}
, where
k_{i}
is the coefficient of
\alpha_{i}
in
2\rho
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this when
z
is a PBW basis element. Since any
K_{\gamma}
and
F^{I}
acts on
X
with
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-coefficients, we can further suppose
z=E^{I}
for some
I:\Delta^{+}\to\{0,\dots,r^{\prime}-1\}
. If
d(I)=l_{1}\alpha_{1}+\dots+l_{n}\alpha_{n}
, then
E^{I}
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-linear combination of products
E_{i_{1}}E_{i_{2}}\dots E_{i_{m}}
where there are
l_{i}
repetitions of
i
among the
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{m}
. By the previous lemma, the matrix representing
E_{i_{1}}\dots E_{i_{m}}
has coefficients of the form

(c_{1}(q)+d_{1}(q)q^{-2\lambda_{i_{1}}})\dots(c_{m}(q)+d_{m}(q)q^{-2\lambda_{i_{m}}}).

In this product, there are at most
l_{i}
terms with a
q^{-2\lambda_{i}}
for every
i
. Thus, in every monomial appearing when expanding this product, the power of
q^{-2\lambda_{i}}
is bounded above by
l_{i}
. Since

for every

the proposition follows.
∎

\text{ADO}_{\mathfrak{g},r}^{\prime}(L)=\text{ADO}_{\mathfrak{g},r}(L)=\langle L_{o}\rangle=q^{(r^{\prime}-1)(\lambda,2\rho)(2g+s-1)}P(q^{-2\lambda_{1}},\dots,q^{-2\lambda_{n}})

Now setting

, the above is written as

4.
Unrolled restricted quantum groups as doubles of crossed products

In this section we define all the elements appearing in Theorem
2
: crossed products, relative Drinfeld centers, equivariantization. Our proof relies on Hopf algebra descriptions of these objects, for this we introduce twisted Drinfeld doubles and semi-direct products. The proof of Theorem
2
comprises Subsections
4.7
to
4.10
and is summarized in Subsection
4.11
. Finally, we prove Theorem
3

4.12
.

During the whole section we let
G
be an abelian group.

4.1.
G
-graded categories

A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is
G
-graded
if it splits as a disjoint union of full subcategories
\mathcal{C}=\coprod_{\lambda\in G}\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathcal{C}_{\mu}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\lambda+\mu}
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
G
-crossed
if
G
acts on
\mathcal{C}
by monoidal autoequivalences
\phi_{\lambda}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
such that
\phi_{\lambda}(\mathcal{C}_{\mu})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
for each
\lambda,\mu\in G
. We will only consider
strict
monoidal actions, that is, the isomorphisms
\phi_{\lambda+\mu}(X)\cong\phi_{\lambda}(\phi_{\mu}(X))
and
\phi_{\lambda}(X\otimes Y)\cong\phi_{\lambda}(X)\otimes\phi_{\lambda}(Y)
are all identity maps of vector spaces (our categories will be representation categories of Hopf group-coalgebras).
A
braided
G
-crossed category
is a
G
-crossed category with natural isomorphisms
c_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to\phi_{\lambda}(Y)\otimes X
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda},Y\in\mathcal{C},\lambda\in G
satisfying certain axioms. For more details, see
[
26
]
.

Remark 4.1
.

If
G
is abelian, a
G
-graded category
\mathcal{C}
might be braided in two senses. On the one hand,
\mathcal{C}
might carry a
G
-action and be
G
-braided in the above sense. On the other hand, it might be braided in the usual sense (no
G
-action involved).

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is
G
-graded and braided in the usual sense. If
\chi:G\times G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
is a bicharacter, then it is easy to see that
c^{\chi}_{X,Y}=\chi(\lambda,\mu)c_{X,Y}
is a braiding, where
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda},Y\in\mathcal{C}_{\mu}
. If
(\mathcal{C},c,\theta)
is ribbon, then
(\mathcal{C},c^{\chi},\theta^{\chi})
is ribbon where
\theta^{\chi}_{X}=\chi(\lambda,\lambda)\theta_{X}
if
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
. It is easy to see that the unframed link invariants obtained from
(\mathcal{C},c^{\chi})
are the same as those of
(\mathcal{C},c)
: each crossing multiplies the invariant by a
\chi(\lambda,\lambda)^{\pm 1}
, but these cancel out when multiplying by the framing factor.

Suppose
G
acts on monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
by monoidal autoequivalences
\phi_{\lambda}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
. Then we define the
crossed product

\mathcal{C}\rtimes G
as the category whose objects are pairs
(X,\lambda)
with
X\in\mathcal{C}
and
\lambda\in G
and
\text{Hom}\,_{\mathcal{C}\rtimes G}((X,\lambda),(Y,\mu))=\text{Hom}\,_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\delta_{\lambda,\mu}
. This is a
G
-graded category in a natural way. It is a
G
-graded monoidal category if we define

(X,\lambda)\otimes(Y,\mu)=(X\otimes\phi_{\lambda}(Y),\lambda+\mu).

The
relative Drinfeld center
of
\mathcal{C}\rtimes G
is the category consisting of pairs
(X,\lambda,\sigma)
where
(X,\lambda)
is an object of
\mathcal{C}\rtimes G
and
\sigma=\{\sigma_{Y}\}_{Y\in\mathcal{C}}
is a

of
(X,\lambda)
, that is, a collection of isomorphisms

\sigma_{Y}:(Y,0)\otimes(X,\lambda)\to(X,\lambda)\otimes(Y,0)

satisfying certain axioms. We denote it by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{C}\rtimes G)
, this is a braided
G
-crossed category in a natural way, see
[
11
]
or
[
20
]
for more details.

4.3.
Hopf group-coalgebras

A
Hopf
G
-coalgebra
is a family
\underline{D}=\{D_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in G}
of algebras with unit
D_{\lambda}
together with coproducts
\Delta_{\lambda,\mu}:D_{\lambda+\mu}\to D_{\lambda}\otimes D_{\mu}
, a counit
\epsilon:D_{0}\to\mathbb{C}
and antipodes
S_{\lambda}:D_{\lambda}\to D_{-\lambda}
for each
\lambda,\mu\in G
satisfying a generalization of the usual Hopf algebra axioms. From the axioms it follows that
\text{Rep}(\underline{D})=\coprod_{\lambda\in G}\text{Rep}(D_{\lambda})
is a
G
-graded monoidal category with tensor product induced by
\Delta_{\lambda,\mu}
. The antipode induces a duality on this category. See
[
26
,
28
]
for more details.

We say that
\underline{D}
is
G
-crossed
if
G
acts on each
D_{\lambda}
by algebra automorphisms
\phi_{\nu}
and
(\phi_{\nu}\otimes\phi_{\nu})\Delta_{\lambda,\mu}=\Delta_{\lambda,\mu}\phi_{\nu}
for each
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in G
. It follows that
\text{Rep}(\underline{D})
is
G
-crossed if for each
X\in\text{Rep}(D_{\lambda})
we define
\phi_{\nu}(X)
to be
X
as a vector space but with
D_{\lambda}
-module structure
h\cdot_{\nu}x:=\phi_{\nu}(h)x
(
h\in D_{\lambda},x\in X
).

We say
\underline{D}
is
G
-braided
if it is endowed with a family
R=\{R_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{\lambda,\mu\in G}
of invertible elements of
D_{\lambda}\otimes D_{\mu}
with the following property: if
X\in\text{Rep}(D_{\lambda}),Y\in\text{Rep}(D_{\mu})
then

c_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to\phi_{\lambda}(Y)\otimes X,\ x\otimes y\mapsto\tau(R_{\lambda,\mu}\cdot x\otimes y)

defines a
G
-braiding on
\text{Rep}(\underline{D}).
Here
\tau
denotes the usual transposition of tensor factors.

4.4.
Drinfeld twists

A
Drinfeld twist
on a Hopf
G
-coalgebra
\underline{D}
consists of a family
J=\{J_{\lambda,\mu}\}
of invertible elements of
D_{\lambda}\otimes D_{\mu}
satisfying

(1_{\lambda}\otimes J_{\mu,\nu})\cdot[\text{id}\otimes\Delta_{\mu,\nu}(J_{\lambda,\mu+\nu})]=(J_{\lambda,\mu}\otimes 1_{\nu})\cdot[\Delta_{\lambda,\mu}\otimes\text{id}(J_{\lambda+\mu,\nu})].

This guarantees that
\Delta^{J}_{\lambda,\mu}:=J_{\lambda,\mu}\Delta_{\lambda,\mu}J^{-1}_{\lambda,\mu}
is a new associative coproduct on
\underline{D}
. If
\underline{D}
is
G
-braided and
J
is a Drinfeld twist, then
(\underline{D},\Delta^{J})
is
G
-braided with

R^{J}_{\lambda,\mu}=\tau(J_{\mu,\lambda})\cdot R_{\lambda,\mu}\cdot J_{\lambda,\mu}^{-1}.

Applying a Drinfeld twist does not change the associated monoidal categories up to equivalence. This requires the more general notion of monoidal equivalence: a
monoidal functor

F:(\mathcal{C},\otimes)\to(\mathcal{D},\otimes^{\prime})
between monoidal categories
(\mathcal{C},\otimes),(\mathcal{D},\otimes^{\prime})
is a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
together with a family of isomorphisms

J_{X,Y}:F(X\otimes Y)\to F(X)\otimes^{\prime}F(Y)

satisfying hexagon axioms (see
[
5
]
), and two monoidal categories are
monoidally equivalent
if there are monoidal functors
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and
G:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
which are inverses to each other. If
(\mathcal{C},c),(\mathcal{D},c^{\prime})
are braided, a monoidal equivalence is braided if
c^{\prime}_{F(X),F(Y)}J_{X,Y}=J_{Y,X}F(c_{X,Y})
.

In the case
\mathcal{C}=\text{Rep}(\underline{D},\Delta)
and
\mathcal{D}=\text{Rep}(\underline{D},\Delta^{J})
, the identity functor
F:\text{Rep}(\underline{D},\Delta)\to\text{Rep}(\underline{D},\Delta^{J})
is a monoidal equivalence with
J_{X,Y}(x\otimes y)=J_{\lambda,\mu}\cdot x\otimes y
if
X\in\text{Rep}(D_{\lambda}),Y\in\text{Rep}(D_{\mu})
. If
\underline{D}
is braided and
\underline{D}^{J}
has the above
R
-matrix, then the equivalence is braided. Thus:

Lemma 4.3

If

4.5.
Semidirect product and equivariantization

Let
\{D_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in G}
be a
G
-braided Hopf
G
-coalgebra. Since
G
acts on each
D_{\lambda}
we can define the semidirect product
D^{G}_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{C}[G]\ltimes D_{\lambda}
, thus, in
D^{G}_{\lambda}
one has
\mu\cdot x=\phi_{\mu}(x)\cdot\mu
for each
\mu\in G,x\in D_{\lambda}
. Then
\underline{D}^{G}=\{D^{G}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in G}
is a Hopf
G
-coalgebra with the structure tensors of
\underline{D}
and the usual Hopf structure on
\mathbb{C}[G]
. The category
\text{Rep}(\underline{D}^{G})
is braided (in the usual sense) with

(9)

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}:X\otimes y\to Y\otimes X,\ x\otimes y\mapsto\tau(R^{G}_{\lambda,\mu}\cdot x\otimes y)

if
X\in\text{Rep}(D_{\lambda})^{G}
,
Y\in\text{Rep}(D_{\mu}^{G})
and
R^{G}_{\lambda,\mu}=(1\otimes\phi_{\lambda})\cdot R_{\lambda,\mu}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

The equivariantization
\text{Rep}(\underline{D})^{G}
is braided equivalent to
\text{Rep}(\underline{D}^{G})
.

Proof.

Let
(V,f)\in\mathcal{C}^{G}
, say
V\in\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
. Since
\phi_{\mu}(V)=V
as vector spaces, we can define a
G
-action on
V
by
\mu\cdot v=f_{\mu}(v)
. To say that
f_{\mu}:\phi_{\mu}(V)\to V
is a morphism is equivalent to
f_{\mu}(\phi_{\mu}^{-1}(h)v)=hf_{\mu}(v)
for each

or, in terms of the
G
-action,
\mu\cdot(\phi_{\mu}^{-1}(h)v)=h(\mu\cdot v)
. This is equivalent to
V
being a module over
\mathbb{C}[G]\ltimes D_{\lambda}
. It is easy to see that the braiding

defined in (
4.2

∎

4.6.

Suppose
G=(\mathbb{C}^{n},+)

H

G

(h_{i})

(h^{i})\subset H^{*}

]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

The category
\coprod_{\lambda\in G}\text{Rep}(D(H)_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\begin{split}R^{J}_{\lambda,\mu}&=\tau(J_{\mu,\lambda})R^{G}_{\lambda,\mu}J_{\lambda,\mu}^{-1}\\

4.10.
Ribbon structure

Let
\Lambda_{l}
be the left cointegral of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
and
\mu_{r}\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{*}
be the right integral. Let
a\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}),\alpha\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{*}
be the corresponding distinguished group-likes. A generalization of the usual Kauffman-Radford theorem (see
[
20
, Prop. 2.3]
) states that ribbon structures on the twisted Drinfeld double
\underline{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}))}
are classified by elements
b\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}),\beta\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{*}
and
p:\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
satisfying
b^{2}=a,\beta^{2}=\alpha
,
\phi_{\lambda}(\Lambda_{l})=p(\lambda)^{2}\Lambda_{l}
for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
and
S^{2}=\text{ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\circ\text{ad}_{b}
. We call
(\beta,b,p)
a
Kauffman-Radford triple
(and
(\beta,b)
a Kauffman-Radford pair). The associated pivotal element is then given by
g_{\lambda}=p(\lambda)^{-1}\beta\otimes b
.

Let’s find
(\beta,b,p)
for
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
. The left cointegral is given by

(12)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{l}=\left(\sum_{\gamma\in Q_{r}}k_{\gamma}\right)e^{I_{max}}

where
I_{max}
is defined by
I_{max}(\beta)=r^{\prime}-1
for all
\beta\in\Delta^{+}
. The right integral is defined by
\mu_{r}(k_{2\rho}^{1-r^{\prime}}e^{I_{max}})=1
and
\mu_{r}=0
on any other PBW basis element. From this, one can see the distinguished group-likes of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
are given by

\displaystyle a=k_{2\rho}^{1-r^{\prime}},\ \alpha(k_{i})=q^{2}

and
\alpha(e_{i})=0
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Set

(13)

\displaystyle\beta(k_{i})=q^{1-r^{\prime}},\beta(e_{i})=0,\ b=k_{2\rho}^{\frac{1-r^{\prime}}{2}}=k_{\rho}^{1-r^{\prime}},\ \ p(\lambda)=q^{(r^{\prime}-1)(2\rho,\lambda)},

for all
i
, we claim that
(\beta,b,p)
is a Kauffman-Radford triple for
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
. Clearly
\beta^{2}=\alpha
and
b^{2}=a
. We claim that
b\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
: if
r^{\prime}
is odd this is clear. If
r
is even, since we assume
r
coprime to
\det(A)
it must be
\det(A)
odd, but this only happens for
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
with even
n
. But then

k_{2\rho}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}^{i(n-i+1)}

and all powers of the
k_{i}
’s are seen to be even. Hence
b\in\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
as desired. One has
be_{i}b^{-1}=q^{(1-r^{\prime})(\rho,\alpha_{i})}e_{i}=q^{1-r^{\prime}}e_{i}
since
(2\rho,\alpha_{i})=(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})
for all
i
(in the simply-laced case). Also
\text{ad}_{\beta^{-1}}(e_{i})=\beta(k_{i})e_{i}=q^{1-r^{\prime}}e_{i}
. Hence
S^{2}=\text{ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\circ\text{ad}_{b}
as desired. Moreover, one has
\phi_{\lambda}(\Lambda_{l})=q^{2(r^{\prime}-1)(2\rho,\lambda)}\Lambda_{l}
hence
\phi_{\lambda}(\Lambda_{l})=p(\lambda)^{2}\Lambda_{l}
as desired.

The
k^{\prime}_{i}
’s in
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{*}\subset D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}))
are characterized by
\langle k^{\prime}_{i},k_{j}\rangle=q^{-a_{ij}}
. Hence, one has
\beta=(k^{\prime}_{\rho})^{r^{\prime}-1}
in the
k^{\prime}_{i}
’s since
\langle k^{\prime}_{\rho},k_{j}\rangle=q^{-(\rho,\alpha_{j})}=q^{-1}
. It follows that
\beta
maps to
k_{\rho}^{1-r^{\prime}}
in
\mathfrak{u}_{\lambda}
so that the pivot
g_{\lambda}=p(\lambda)^{-1}\beta\otimes b
maps to
p(\lambda)^{-1}k_{2\rho}^{1-r^{\prime}}
. This is exactly
\boldsymbol{K}_{2\rho}^{1-r^{\prime}}
in the rescaled generators. Passing to
\underline{A}
and then
\underline{A}^{J}
does not change the pivot (see
[
21
, Sect. 5]
). Thus, we have shown that the embedding of Proposition
4.8
preserves the pivotal structures. Since the ribbon structure is determined by the associated pivotal structure and viceversa, this implies:

Proposition 4.9
.

The Kauffman-Radford triple
(\beta,b,p)
above induces a ribbon structure on
\mathcal{Z}_{rel}(\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b}))\rtimes\mathfrak{h}^{*})
and its equivariantization that makes the embedding of Proposition
4.8
a ribbon embedding.

4.12.
Recovering
P_{\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})}^{\theta}(K)

In
[
21
, Subs. 3.5]
, given an oriented knot

, we defined a multivariable polynomial invariant by

P_{\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})}^{\theta}(K)=t_{1}^{w|\Lambda_{l}|_{1}/2}\cdots t_{n}^{w|\Lambda_{l}|_{n}/2}\epsilon_{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime})}(Z^{\theta}_{\underline{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime})}}(K_{o})).

Here
K_{o}
is a framed long knot whose closure is
K
,

is a “twisted universal invariant” build from the twisted Drinfeld double of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime}=\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})\otimes\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{m}^{\pm 1}]
(the same TDD we defined above but where

\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime}
defined by
\theta(e_{i})=t_{i}e_{i},\theta(k_{i})=k_{i}
for every
i
,
w=w(K)
is the writhe of
K_{o}
and
(|\Lambda_{l}|_{1},\dots,|\Lambda_{l}|_{n})
is the
\mathbb{N}^{n}
-degree of the cointegral
\Lambda_{l}
of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})
. Here by twisted universal invariant we mean
Z^{\theta}_{\underline{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime})}}(K_{o})
takes care of the
G
-crossed structure of
\underline{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime})}
. However, this is computed from the usual universal invariant defined out of
\underline{A}
by

(14)

\displaystyle Z_{\underline{A}}^{\theta}(K_{o})=Z^{\theta}_{\underline{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime})}}(K_{o})\cdot\theta^{w}

see
[
21
, Subs. 3.3]
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Set
t_{i}=q^{2\lambda_{i}}
for every
i
, then
\theta=\phi_{\lambda}
. We will denote the universal invariants

. Then
\underline{D(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{b})^{\prime})}
and
\underline{A}
are the same objects considered in this paper. By

R

over

by Theorem
2
and the

but multiplied by

(15)

\displaystyle Z_{\underline{A}^{J}}^{\lambda}(K_{o})v=q^{w(\lambda,\lambda)}\langle K_{o}\rangle v.

Now, it is easy to see that

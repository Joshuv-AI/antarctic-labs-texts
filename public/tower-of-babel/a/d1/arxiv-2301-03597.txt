On the Minimax Regret for Linear Bandits in a wide variety of Action Spaces
Debangshu Banerjee, Aditya Gopalan

Abstract
As noted in the works of Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020 , it has been mentioned that it is an open problem to characterize the minimax regret of linear bandits in a wide variety of action spaces. In this article we present an optimal regret lower bound for a wide class of convex action spaces.



1
Introduction

Minimax regret bounds in bandit environments are a well studied problem and results typically are limited to a particular action set, namely the
l_{1}
and
l_{\infty}
balls in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We include in this article that display that the methods introduced by
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
in Chapter 24 can indeed be generalized to a wide variety of action spaces, namely to any
l_{p}
ball where
p
is in the range
(1,\infty)
.

2
Key Result

Note that the result we include in
2.1
, is optimal in the bandit setting, in sense that algorithms like LinUCB achieve this.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be the
L^{p}
ball defined as
\mathcal{X}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;:\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}\leqslant c\}
, where
1<p<\infty
. Assume
d\leqslant(2cn^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}
. Then there exists a parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{(c4\sqrt{3})^{p}}\frac{d^{2}}{n^{\frac{p}{2}}}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\theta)\geqslant\frac{d\sqrt{n}}{16\sqrt{3}}
.

Proof.

We chose
\theta\in\{+\Delta,-\Delta\}^{d}
and note that the regret, defined as,
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\theta)
, is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{n}(\theta)=\sum_{t=1}^{n}x^{*}{{}^{\top}}\theta-x_{t}^{\top}\theta\\
=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}x^{*}_{i}\theta_{i}-x_{ti}\theta_{i}\\
=\Delta\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\\
\geqslant\frac{\Delta d^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2c}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\bigg)^{2}\;,\end{split}

(1)

where the third equality follows from Lemma
A.1
and the last inequality follows from Lemma
A.2
. The remainder of the proof follows the same idea as that presented in the proof of the Unit Ball in Section 24.2 of
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
. We present it here for the sake of completeness.
We define a stopping time
\tau_{i}=n\wedge\min{\{t\;:\;\sum_{s=1}^{t}x_{si}^{2}\geqslant\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}\}}
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\theta)\geqslant\frac{\Delta d^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2c}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\bigg(\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\bigg)^{2}\;.

Define a Random Variable
U_{i}(\sigma)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\bigg(\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}\sigma\bigg)^{2}
where
\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}
and note that

\displaystyle U_{i}(1)=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\bigg(\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}\bigg)^{2}\leqslant 2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\frac{c^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+2\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}x_{ti}^{2}\leqslant\frac{4nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+2\;,

(2)

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
\tau_{i}
.

Now we fix an
i
, and make a perturbed version of
\theta^{\prime}
, which is the same as
\theta
except in the
i^{th}
position where
\theta^{\prime}_{i}=-\theta_{i}
. Thus, applying Pinsker’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[U_{i}(1)]\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}[U_{i}(1)]-\bigg(\frac{4nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+2\bigg)\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}||\mathbb{P}_{\theta^{\prime}})}

(3)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}[U_{i}(1)]-\frac{\Delta}{2}\bigg(\frac{4nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+2\bigg)\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}x_{ti}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}[U_{i}(1)]-\frac{\Delta}{2}\bigg(\frac{4nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+2\bigg)\sqrt{\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+1}

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}[U_{i}(1)]-\frac{4\sqrt{3}n\Delta c^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}\sqrt{\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}}\;,

where the last inequality follows under the assumption
d\leqslant(2nc^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[U_{i}(1)]+\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}[U_{i}(-1)]\geqslant\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}[U_{i}(1))+U_{i}(-1)]-\frac{4\sqrt{3}n\Delta c^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}\sqrt{\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}=2\mathbb{E}_{\theta^{\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\tau_{i}c^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}+\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}x_{ti}^{2}\bigg]-\frac{4\sqrt{3}n\Delta c^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}\sqrt{\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}}\geqslant\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}\;,\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
\tau_{i}
and setting the value of
\Delta
as
\frac{1}{4\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{\frac{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}{nc^{2}}}
.
Using an average hammering trick

\displaystyle\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm\Delta\}^{d}}\mathcal{R}_{n}(\theta)\geqslant\frac{\Delta d^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2c}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{\theta\in\{\pm\Delta\}^{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[U_{i}(sign(\theta_{i})]

\displaystyle\begin{split}=\frac{\Delta d^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2c}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{\theta_{-i}\in\{\pm\Delta\}^{d-1}}\sum_{\theta_{i}\in\{\pm\Delta\}}\mathbb{E}_{\theta}[U_{i}(sign(\theta_{i})]\\
\geqslant\frac{\Delta d^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2c}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{\theta_{-i}\in\{\pm\Delta\}^{d-1}}\frac{nc^{2}}{d^{\frac{2}{p}}}=2^{d-2}\Delta ncd^{1-\frac{1}{p}}.\end{split}

Hence there exists a
\theta
in
\{\pm\Delta\}^{d}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}(\theta)\geqslant\frac{nc\Delta d^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{4}=\frac{d\sqrt{n}}{16\sqrt{3}}.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

The results in
2.1
are interesting because with regards to the dimensionality
d
and time horizon
n
dependence it is exact.

Remark 2.3
.

This result also shows that the minimum eigen value of the design matrix and regret are fundamentally different quantities
Banerjee et al. 2022
. For example note that for
l_{p}
balls for
p>2
, the minimum eigen value can grow at a rate lower than
\Omega(\sqrt{n}
, whereas, the minimax regert remains bounded as
\Omega(\sqrt{n}
.

3
Conclusion

We expect that similar results can hold for general convex bodies and not just for
l_{p}
balls.

Appendix A
Appendix

A.1
Technical Lemmas

Lemma A.1

(Optimal Reward in
L^{p}
Ball)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be the
L^{p}
ball defined as
\mathcal{X}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;:\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}\leqslant c\}
. We compute the optimal reward for the linear bandit model

\displaystyle\max x^{\top}\theta\;\;\;s.t.\;\;\;x\in\mathcal{X}

(4)

The solution to the optimization problem
4
is
\frac{1}{\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\theta_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}c|\theta_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

Proof.

Note that the solution
x^{*}
satisfies the following relation for any
i\in[d]

\displaystyle x^{*}_{i}=\Big(\frac{|\theta_{i}|}{\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}sign(\theta_{i})\;,

(5)

where
\lambda\geqslant 0
is the Lagrangian variable.
Solving for
\lambda
using the constraint equation of the problem with now equality instead of inequality. (Because the optimal solution lies at the boundary)

\displaystyle\lambda=\Big(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\theta_{i}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}{c^{p}}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\;.

(6)

Now solving for
x^{*}{{}^{\top}}\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x^{*}_{i}\theta_{i}
gives the result.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\geqslant\frac{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}{2c}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\bigg)^{2}

(7)

Proof.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\bigg)^{2}\\
=c^{2}d^{1-\frac{2}{p}}-2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{ti}^{2}\\
\leqslant 2c^{2}d^{1-\frac{2}{p}}-2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\\
=\frac{2c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{c}{d^{\frac{1}{p}}}-x_{ti}sign(\theta_{i})\;,\end{split}

(8)

where the inequality follows from Lemma
A.3
. Rearranging gives the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

If
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{p}\leqslant c
, then
\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leqslant c^{2}d^{1-\frac{2}{p}}

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}\leqslant(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{p})^{\frac{2}{p}}d^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant c^{2}d^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\;,

(9)

where the first inequality follows from Holder’s inequality and the second inequality follows from the hypothesis.
∎

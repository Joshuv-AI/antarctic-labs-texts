Fibre functors and reconstruction of Hopf algebras
Simon Lentner and Martín Mombelli

Abstract
. The main objective of the present paper is to present a version of the Tannaka-Krein type reconstruction Theorems: If F : ℬ → 𝒞 F:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{C}} is an exact faithful monoidal functor of tensor categories, one would like to realize ℬ {\mathcal{B}} as category of representations of a braided Hopf algebra H ⁡ ( F ) H(F) in 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} . We prove that this is the case iff ℬ {\mathcal{B}} has the additional structure of a monoidal 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} -module category compatible with F F , which equivalently means that F F admits a monoidal section. For Hopf algebras, this reduces to a version of the Radford projection theorem. The Hopf algebra is constructed through the relative coend for module categories. We expect this basic result to have a wide range of applications, in particular in the absence of fibre functors, and we give some applications. One particular motivation was the logarithmic Kazhdan-Lusztig conjecture.

Our approach

The point of view of this paper owes a lot to
[
22
]
. However, we shall work only with finite categories. Let
{\Bbbk}
be an arbitrary field, and let
{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{C}}
be finite
{\Bbbk}
-linear abelian categories. Assume both
{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{B}}
are rigid monoidal categories, and
F:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
is a monoidal functor. If

has a
section

G:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{B}}

as monoidal functors, then it is possible to endow

with an action of

is a monoidal
{\mathcal{C}}
-module functor. This action behaves well together with the monoidal product of

, in a sense that we call

Under these conditions, we aim at constructing a Hopf algebra

and obtaining a kind of Radford projection Theorem in this categorical setting.

The reconstruction of such Hopf algebra is given in some steps, that we describe as follows. If

is a right
{\mathcal{C}}
-module category,

is an exact faithful module functor, we construct a coalgebra

Here

stands for the

, a new tool, developed in
[
3

, in the context of module categories. This tool is one of the new features that we incorporate in these reconstruction theorems. The coproduct and counit of

are defined using universal properties of the dinatural transformations associated with the relative coend. See Proposition
6.2
. Moreover, we show that the functor
F
factorizes as

\textstyle{{\mathcal{B}}\;\;\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widehat{F}}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{{}^{C}{\mathcal{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{C}}.}

Here

is the forgetful functor, and
\widehat{F}:{\mathcal{B}}\to{}^{C}{\mathcal{C}}
is an equivalence of
{\mathcal{C}}
-module categories. The existence of
\widehat{F}
is stated in Proposition

, and the proof that it is an equivalence of categories is given in Theorem

If in addition

is a braided rigid monoidal

-module category,
{\mathcal{B}}
is a rigid monoidal category, and

is a monoidal functor, then we endow the coalgebra

with a product, turning it into a bialgebra in

. Rigidity of

The contents of the paper are the following. In Section

Throughout this paper,
{\Bbbk}

We shall denote by

-vector spaces

\bullet:\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}\times{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}.

2.
Representations of tensor categories

2.1.
Finite tensor categories

A finite tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
is a monoidal rigid category, with simple unit object
\mathbf{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}
. We refer to
[
10
]
for more details on finite tensor categories. Without loss of generality, we shall assume that tensor categories in this work are strict.

If
{\mathcal{C}},{\mathcal{D}}
are monoidal categories, a monoidal functor is a functor
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
equipped with natural isomorphisms

\xi_{A,B}:F(A){\otimes}F(B)\to F(A{\otimes}B),

such that

(2.1)

\xi_{A,B{\otimes}C}(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{F(A)}{\otimes}\xi_{B,C})=\xi_{A{\otimes}B,C}(\xi_{A,B}{\otimes}\mbox{\rm id\,}_{F(C)}).

If
(F,\xi),(\widetilde{F},\widetilde{\xi}):{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
are two monoidal functors, a monoidal
natural transformation
between
F
and
\widetilde{F}
is a natural transformation
\alpha:F\to\widetilde{F}
such that

(2.2)

\alpha_{A{\otimes}B}\xi_{A,B}=\widetilde{\xi}_{A,B}(\alpha_{A}{\otimes}\alpha_{B}),

for any
A,B\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a category, for any
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
we shall denote by

\mbox{ev}_{X}:X^{*}{\otimes}X\to\mathbf{1},\quad\mbox{coev}_{X}:\mathbf{1}\to X{\otimes}X^{*}

the evaluation and coevaluation. By abuse of notation, we shall also denote by

\mbox{ev}_{X}:X{\otimes}{}^{*}X\to\mathbf{1},\quad\mbox{coev}_{X}:\mathbf{1}\to{}^{*}X{\otimes}X

the evaluation and coevaluation for the left duals. We will use the following basic result.
If
f:X\to Y
is an isomorphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
then

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{{}^{*}X}{\otimes}f)\mbox{coev}_{X}=&({}^{*}f{\otimes}\mbox{\rm id\,}_{Y})\mbox{coev}_{Y},\\
\mbox{ev}_{Y}(f{\otimes}\mbox{\rm id\,}_{Y})=&\mbox{ev}_{X}(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{X}{\otimes}{}^{*}f).\end{split}

It is well known that for any pair of objects
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
there are canonical natural isomorphisms

(2.4)

\phi^{r}_{X,Y}:Y^{*}{\otimes}X^{*}\to(X{\otimes}Y)^{*},

(2.5)

\phi^{l}_{X,Y}:{}^{*}Y{\otimes}{}^{*}X\to{}^{*}(X{\otimes}Y).

These isomorphisms allow us to compute coevaluation and evaluation maps of duals; more precisely we shall need the following identity

(2.6)

\mbox{ev}_{{}^{*}V}={}^{*}(\mbox{coev}_{X})\phi^{l}_{{}^{*}X,X}.

We are going to make use, very often, of the canonical natural isomorphisms

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi^{Z}_{X,Y}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(X{\otimes}{}^{*}Y,Z)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(X,Z{\otimes}Y),\\
\psi^{Z}_{X,Y}(f)=(f{\otimes}\mbox{\rm id\,}_{Y})(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{X}{\otimes}\mbox{coev}_{Y}).\end{split}

And its inverse

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\psi}^{Z}_{X,Y}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(X,Z{\otimes}Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(X{\otimes}{}^{*}Y,Z)\\
\bar{\psi}^{Z}_{X,Y}(g)=(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{Z}{\otimes}\mbox{ev}_{Y})(g{\otimes}\mbox{\rm id\,}_{{}^{*}Y}).\end{split}

We shall also need the following basic fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any pair
V,W\in{\mathcal{C}}
the evaluation of
V{\otimes}W
is given by

(2.9)

\mbox{ev}_{V{\otimes}W}=\mbox{ev}_{V}(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{V}{\otimes}\mbox{ev}_{W}{\otimes}\mbox{\rm id\,}_{{}^{*}V})(\mbox{\rm id\,}_{V{\otimes}W}{\otimes}(\phi^{l}_{V,W})^{-1}).\qed

6.
Fiber functors and reconstruction results

It is a classical result that, out of a fiber functor, which is a monoidal, exact and faithful functor
F:{\mathcal{B}}\to\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}
, one can reconstruct a (usual) Hopf algebra
H
such that
{\mathcal{B}}
is monoidally equivalent to the category of left
H
-comodules. We shall generalize these reconstruction theorems for a fiber functor
F:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}
is an arbitrary braided tensor category acting monoidally on
{\mathcal{B}}
. The reconstruction of the Hopf algebra from these data will be described in a similar way as the case
{\mathcal{C}}=\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}
. See for example
[
19
]
,
[
22
]
. The main new ingredient will be the use of the relative coend, introduced in
[
3
]
, and the splitting condition; that is, the existence of a section
G:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
of
F
. While dealing with the relative (co)end, the primary new challenge will be demonstrate that some dinatural transformations satisfy Equation (
3.5
).

7.1.

The next example was treated along the paper. At the end we proved that the reconstructed Hopf algebra from the forgetful functor

Example 7.1
.

Let

and consider the tensor category

Example 7.2
.

Let

be Hopf algebras in
\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}
with

the inclusion and assume there is a left-inverse Hopf algebra map

We now discuss how this is related to the classical Radford Projection Theorem
[

]
. This result states that the existence of a Hopf algebra projection

implies that

is isomorphic to a Radford biproduct

where

is a Hopf algebra in

. In our case we have assumed that

is quasitriangular and
\iota^{*}
lifts to a braided functor to the center of
{\mathcal{B}}
. This gives rise to a choice of a functor
\operatorname{Rep}(H)\to{{}^{H}_{H}}{\mathcal{Y}D}(\mbox{\rm Vect\,}_{\Bbbk})
and shows

to be in the image - more explicitly the
R
-matrix of
H
determines the
H
-coaction from the
H
-action. Then this

is precisely the dual of our Hopf algebra

.

7.3.
Lifting and cocycles deformations

In the classification of (e.g. pointed) Hopf algebras
H
the strategy in the Andruskiewisch-Schneider program
[
2
]
is to consider the coradical
H_{0}
(which is assumed to be a Hopf algebra, for example a group ring) and classify the possible Nichols algebras
R
, and then obtain
H
as a lifting of
\mathrm{gr}(H)=R\#H_{0}
. It is an important question to determine whether this lifting is a
2
-cocycle twist, and the observation is that this holds in almost all cases
[
1
]
.

We view this problem in our setting: Since a main assumption is that
H_{0}\subset H
we have a tensor functor
\iota^{*}:\,\mathrm{Comod}(H_{0})\to\mathrm{Comod}(H)
. The lifting is a
2
-cocycle twist precisely iff there is a tensor functor
\mathrm{Comod}(H_{0})\leftarrow\mathrm{Comod}(H)
, if the
2
-cocycle is trivial then it comes from a Hopf algebra projection
\pi
, and then our relative coend is
R^{*}
.

While this view does not allow to decide the difficult question when a lifting is a
2
-cocycle twist, it shows the natural categorical context of this question and it produces general statements, for example if
R
as a coalgebra has a trivial lifting, then there is a monoidal section and hence the lifting is tensor functor and thus the lifting comes from a
2
-cocycle twist.

7.4.
The logarithmic Kazhdan Lusztig conjecture

In conformal field theory, there is in good situations a modular tensor category of representations of a vertex algebra
\mathcal{V}
, which reflects the analytic properties for example of solution spaces to certain differential equations (e.g. the braiding reflects the monodromy around the singularity
z=0
). One is often confronted with the very difficult question to determine the representation category of representations of a vertex subalgebra
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
if the representation theory of
\mathcal{V}
is known, for example being a free-field realization. A brief introduction, references and an account for the statements below can be found in
[
6
]
.

Categorically, one can understand as
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{W})
being a modular tensor category and
A=\mathcal{V}
being a commutative algebra in this category and
{\mathcal{C}}=\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{V})
is the category of local
A
-modules
\operatorname{Rep}^{loc}(A)
. Then by the results in
[
6
]
Section
3
we have under suitable conditions that
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{W})
is a relative Drinfeld center of
\operatorname{Rep}(A)
with respect to the subcategory
{\mathcal{C}}=\operatorname{Rep}^{loc}(A)
. In the cases relevant to logarithmic (i.e. nonsemisimple) conformal field theory, it is often the case that all
simple
modules in
\operatorname{Rep}(A)
are already in
\operatorname{Rep}^{loc}(A)
. This gives rise via the socle filtration to a monoidal section functor, see
[
6
]
Section 4

\operatorname{Rep}^{loc}(A)\rightleftarrows\operatorname{Rep}^{loc}(A)

Then the results in our paper produce a Hopf algebra
R^{*}
, such that

\operatorname{Rep}(A)={{}^{R^{*}}}{\mathcal{C}}

and in this case the mentioned equivalence to the relative Drinfeld center means explicitly (see Section
4.2
)

\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{W})\cong{{}^{R}_{R}}{\mathcal{Y}D}({\mathcal{C}})

For example the celebrated and notoriously difficult
logarithmic Kazhdan Lusztig conjecture
considers the lattice vertex algebra
\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda}
of the root lattice of a semisimple complex finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
rescaled by an integer

, whose category of representations is the category of vector spaces graded by an abelian group

Then it asserts that

contains as kernel of screening operators a certain vertex algebra
\mathcal{W}_{p}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathcal{V}_{\Lambda}
, whose category of representation is conjectured to be equivalent to the category of representations of the small quantum group
u_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
for

. In the first authors paper
[
6
]
the approach is to view the small quantum group category as a category of Yetter-Drinfeld modules over the Nichols algebra

\operatorname{Rep}(u_{q}(\mathfrak{g}))\cong{{}^{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{N}}}{\mathcal{Y}D}(\mbox{\rm Vect\,}_{\Lambda^{*}/\Lambda})

and develop the categorical tools above to reduce the equivalence in question to proving that

\operatorname{Rep}(A)\cong{{}_{\mathcal{N}}}\mbox{\rm Vect\,}_{\Lambda^{*}/\Lambda}

In the case
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
the abelian category could be determined and this gives a systematic proof of the conjecture in this case (initially we have used more complicated proof methods for the case
\mathfrak{sl}_{2},p=2
in
[
6
]
, while
[
11
]
have used very different arguments for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2},p
). A main motivation for writing the present paper is that now we have the clear statement that the category
\operatorname{Rep}(A)
is for abstract reasons given as category of representations of a Hopf algebra
\mathcal{N}
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and it now remains to determine that
\mathcal{N}
is indeed the expected Nichols algebra, namely the Nichols algebra of screenings.

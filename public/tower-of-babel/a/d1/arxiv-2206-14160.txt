Local well-posedness of the 1d compressible Navier–Stokes system with rough data
Ke Chen, Ruilin Hu, Quoc-Hung Nguyen

Abstract
This paper presents a new approach to the local well-posedness of the 1d compressible Navier–Stokes systems with rough initial data. Our approach is based on establishing some smoothing and Lipschitz-type estimates for the 1d parabolic equation with piecewise continuous coefficients.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we study the compressible Navier–Stokes equations in Lagrangian coordinates, which can be written as (see
[
16
]
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
&u_{t}+p_{x}=\left(\frac{\mu u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x},\\
&(e+\frac{1}{2}u^{2})_{t}+({p}u)_{x}=\left(\frac{\kappa}{v}\theta_{x}+\frac{\mu}{v}uu_{x}\right)_{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here we denote
v
the specific volume,
u
the velocity,
p
the pressure,
e
the specific internal energy,
\theta
the absolute temperature. And
\mu,\kappa>0
are viscosity and heat conductivity coefficients. The above equations describe the conservation of mass, momentum and energy, respectively.

We will consider two systems. The first system is the so-called
p-
system (see
[
16
]
), which is a general model of isentropic gas dynamics(when
p(v)=Av^{-\gamma}
). In the isentropic case, the temperature is held constant hence energy must be added to the system, whence the conservation
of energy is absent. The
p-
system can be written as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
&u_{t}+(p(v))_{x}=(\frac{\mu u_{x}}{v})_{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

In this paper we consider a more general system which only requires
p\in W^{2,\infty}
.

Another system studied in this paper is (
1.1
) for the polytropic ideal gas which has the constitutive relations

p(v,\theta)=\frac{K\theta}{v},\ \ \ e=\mathbf{c}\theta,

where
K
and heat capacity
\mathbf{c}
are both positive constants. The system can be written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
&u_{t}+(p(v,\theta))_{x}=\left(\frac{\mu u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x},\\
&\theta_{t}+\frac{p(v,\theta)}{\mathbf{c}}u_{x}-\frac{\mu}{\mathbf{c}v}(u_{x})^{2}=\left(\frac{\kappa}{\mathbf{c}v}\theta_{x}\right)_{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

Let us give a review of classical results. The study about compressible Navier-Stokes equations in gas dynamics started with Nash in
[
15
]
, where he considered general elliptic and parabolic equations. Then Itaya
[
5
,
6
]
solved the compressible Navier-Stokes equations for initial data in Hölder space. Kazhikhov
[
9
]
established a priori estimates and proved the existence of weak and classical solutions. For the equation of 3-d ideal gas, Nishida and Matsumura in
[
12
]
applied the energy method to give the existence of a global solution with data in Hölder space. The result of large-time behavior of global solution was provided by Kawashima
[
8
]
. Using the energy method, Hoff
[
4
]
proved the existence of a global solution for discontinuous data. The results in the book of Lions
[
10
]
and the book of Feireisl
[
2
]
are applicable to a larger space of initial data.

Recently, Liu and Yu
[
11
]
studied the system (
1.2
) with
BV\cap L^{1}
data, under the assumption
p^{\prime}(v)<0
. Based on exact analysis of the associated linear equations and their Green’s functions, they initiated a new method, which converts the differential equations into integral equations, and proved that for initial data

v_{0},u_{0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|v_{0}-1\|_{L^{1}},\|v_{0}\|_{BV},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|u_{0}\|_{BV}<\delta\ll 1,

there exist
t_{\sharp}>0
and
C_{\sharp}>0
such that
the system (
1.2
) admits a unique weak solution
(v,u)
for
t\in(0,t_{\sharp})
, and

\displaystyle\|v(t,\cdot)-1\|_{L^{1}}+\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{BV}+\|\sqrt{t}u_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2C_{\sharp}\delta,\ \ \ \forall t\in(0,t_{\sharp}).

Moreover, they also established global result for polytropic gases
p(v)=Av^{-\gamma}
with
1\leq\gamma<e
.
Their result was extended to the full compressible Navier–Stokes system (
1.3
) by Wang, Yu, and Zhang
[
18
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the local well-posedness of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) with rough data, which allows
v_{0}
to have a finite jump. The idea to study the elliptic problem with jump coefficients comes from
[
13
,
14
]
. More precisely, without loss of generality, let
x=0
be the jump point. Suppose there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle|v_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)|\leq\delta,\ \ \ \text{if}\ |x-y|\leq\varepsilon\ \text{and}\ xy>0,

(1.4)

for some
\delta>0
that will be fixed later.

Throughout the paper, we fix two constants
0<\alpha<\gamma\ll 1
. Define the norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}(E)}=\sup_{0<s<T}s^{\sigma}\|f(s)\|_{L^{p}(E)}+\sup_{0<s<t<T}s^{\sigma+\alpha}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}(E)}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Y_{T}(E)}=\sup_{0<s<T}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(E)}+\sup_{0<s<t<T}s^{\alpha}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(E)}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{T}(E)}=\|f\|_{L^{p}_{T}(E)}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E}|f(t,x)|^{p}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for any measurable set}\ E\subseteq\mathbb{R},

and we omit the domain if
E=\mathbb{R}
. We write several norms together to denote the sum of them. Moreover, we denote
A\lesssim B
if there exists a universal constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
.
We will solve the system (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) by the fixed point theorem in the Banach space equipped with the norms above. We note that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Y_{T}}
contain Hölder derivatives in time.

For the system (
1.2
), we impose the following conditions for initial data

\displaystyle v_{0}\in L^{\infty},\ \inf_{x}v_{0}\geq\lambda_{0}>0,\ u_{0}=\partial_{x}\bar{u}_{0}\ \operatorname{with}\ \bar{u}_{0}\in\dot{C}^{2\gamma}.

(1.5)

Simplified versions of our main results are stated in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1

Assume
p\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},u_{0})
satisfies the conditions (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) with
\delta=\delta_{0}
, then the system (
1.2
) admits a unique local solution
(v,u)
in
[0,T]
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(t,x)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2},\ \ \ \|v\|_{Y_{T}}\leq 2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\ \ \ \ \|\partial_{x}u\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq M,

for some
T,M>0
.

For the full N–S system (
1.3
), we use
L^{p}
setting, and set the following norm and corresponding Banach space

\displaystyle\|(w,\vartheta)\|_{Z_{T}}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\star\in\{L^{2}_{T},X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2},X_{T}^{\frac{3}{4},\infty}\}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{\star}+\sum_{\star\in\{L^{2}_{T},X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}\}}\|\vartheta\|_{\star}+\sum_{\star\in\{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T},X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}\}}\|\partial_{x}\vartheta\|_{\star}.

The above norms have the same scaling in the parabolic setting. The norms
\|\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}_{T}}
,
\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}_{T}}
and
\|\partial_{x}\vartheta\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}
are imposed in view of the parabolic structure of the equation of
(u,\theta)
. Other norms
\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}},\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{3}{4},\infty}},\|\vartheta\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}},\|\partial_{x}\vartheta\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}}
are necessary to obtain local estimates of
v
.

We assume the initial data satisfies that

\displaystyle\inf_{x}v_{0}(x)\geq\lambda_{0}>0,\ \ \ \|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,\ \ \|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty,\ \ \|\theta_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{-\frac{2}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}<\infty.

(1.6)

The following result is the local well-posedness of system (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.2

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})
satisfies (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) with
\delta=\delta_{0}
, then the system (
1.3
) has a unique local solution
(v,u)
in
[0,T]
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(t,x)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2},\ \ \ \|v\|_{Y_{T}}\leq 2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\ \ \ \ \|(u,\theta)\|_{Z_{T}}\leq B.

for some
T,B>0
.

Note that we do not need
u_{0},\theta_{0}
decay at infinity. Hence the condition (
1.6
) can be relaxed to local
L^{p}
norms(see Theorem
5.2
):

\displaystyle\inf_{x}v_{0}(x)\geq\lambda_{0}>0,\ \ \ \|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,\ \ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{0}\chi_{z}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty,\ \ \sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\|\theta_{0}\chi_{z}\|_{\dot{W}^{-\frac{2}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}<\infty.

(1.7)

Here
\chi_{z}
is a smooth cutoff function satisfying
\mathbf{1}_{[z-1,z+1]}\leq\chi_{z}\leq\mathbf{1}_{[z-2,z+2]}
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the indicator function.

Our method is based on analysis of heat equation with jump coefficient
\phi(x)
satisfying (
3.2
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f(t,x)-\partial_{x}(\phi(x)\partial_{x}f(t,x))=\partial_{x}F(t,x)+R(t,x),

\displaystyle f(0,x)=f_{0}(x).

We convert the differential equations into integral equations and give two different formulas of solution. One formula (
3.9
) is suitable for estimates near jump points, and another one (
3.37
) behaves well for estimates far from jump points. Our main results rely on Lemma
3.1
.

We make some remarks about the choice of initial data. For the isentropic system (
1.2
), we requires
u_{0}\in\dot{B}^{-1+2\gamma}_{\infty,\infty}
. Generally in Schauder theory of parabolic/elliptic equations, to control the Lipschitz norm of solution, it requires the coefficient to be at least Dini (see
[
7
]
). Due to the roughness of coefficient, the space
\dot{B}^{-1+2\gamma}_{\infty,\infty}
is optimal in the sense that we can take
\gamma>0
arbitrarily close to 0. For the full N–S system (
1.2
), we require
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and
\theta_{0}\in\dot{W}^{-\frac{2}{3},\frac{6}{5}}
. Taking a glance at the equation of
\theta
, we need
(u_{x})^{2},\theta u_{x}\in L^{1}_{T}
to make
\theta
well-defined. By classical theory of heat equation,
u_{x}\in L^{2}_{T}
requires
u_{0}\in L^{2}
, and
\theta\in L^{2}_{T}
requires
\theta_{0}\in\dot{H}^{-1}
. Taking force terms into consideration, we finally choose
\theta_{0}\in\dot{W}^{-\frac{2}{3},\frac{6}{5}}\subset\dot{H}^{-1}
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. We introduce some primary properties for heat equations and heat kernel in Section
2
. We establish the main lemma
3.1
in Section
3
. In Section 4 we apply Lemma
3.1
to obtain local well-posedness for system (
1.2
) and prove Theorem
1.1
. Section 5 is devoted to prove local wellposedness for system (
1.3
) and prove Theorem
1.2
.

2
Preliminaries

We introduce the following estimates for the standard heat kernel
\mathbf{K}(t,x)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}
in
\mathbb{R}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t,x)-\partial_{xx}\mathbf{K}(t,x)=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\mathbf{K}(t,x)=\mathbf{Dirac}(x).

The following estimates for heat kernel will be used frequently in our proof.

Lemma 2.1

There holds

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}^{j}\partial_{x}^{m}\mathbf{K}(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x|)^{1+2j+m}},

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{x}^{m}\mathbf{K}(t,x)|^{p}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim|x|^{\frac{2}{p}-1-m},\ \ \ \ \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}^{m}\mathbf{K}(t,x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-1-m)},

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{t}^{j}\partial_{x}^{m}\mathbf{K}(t,x)|^{p}|x|^{\sigma p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{j+{\frac{1}{2}(m-\frac{1}{p}+1-\sigma)}}},

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{t}^{j}\partial_{x}^{m}\mathbf{K}(t+a,x)-\partial_{t}^{j}\partial_{x}^{m}\mathbf{K}(t,x)|^{p}|x|^{\sigma p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{j+{\frac{1}{2}(m-\frac{1}{p}+1-\sigma)}}}\min\{1,\frac{a}{t}\},

for any
m,j=0,1,2
, any
\sigma,a,t\geq 0
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
.

It is easy to check the above estimates by the definition of heat kernel and the fact that
b^{m}e^{-b}\lesssim_{m}1
,
\forall b>0,m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. We omit details here.

Lemma 2.2

Let
h\in\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\sigma\in(0,1),p\in[1,\infty)
. Define
\mathbf{h}(x)=h(x)\mathbf{1}_{x\geq 0}+h(-x)\mathbf{1}_{x<0}
and
\bar{\mathbf{h}}(x)=(h(x)-h(-x))\mathbf{1}_{x\leq 0}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathbf{h}(x)-\mathbf{h}(x-z)|^{p}\frac{dxdz}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\bar{\mathbf{h}}(x)-\bar{\mathbf{h}}(x-z)|^{p}\frac{dxdz}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}\lesssim\|h\|_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}}^{p}.

Proof.
By definition, one can check that

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{h}(x)-\mathbf{h}(x-z)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{x\geq z}\frac{|{h}(x)-{h}(x-z)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}+\mathbf{1}_{x<z}\frac{|{h}(x)-{h}(z-x)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|{h}(x)-{h}(x-z)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}+\frac{|{h}(x)-{h}(z-x)|^{p}}{|2x-z|^{1+\sigma p}},

\displaystyle\frac{|\bar{\mathbf{h}}(x)-\bar{\mathbf{h}}(x-z)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{x<z}\frac{|{h}(x-z)-{h}(z-x)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|{h}(x)-{h}(x-z)|^{p}}{|z|^{1+\sigma p}}+\frac{|{h}(x)-{h}(z-x)|^{p}}{|2x-z|^{1+\sigma p}}.

The result is standard in view of the fact that

\|h\|_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}}^{p}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{1+\sigma p}}dxdy.

This completes the proof.

We introduce the following Schauder type lemma, the idea can be found in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.3

Let
\mathbf{K}
be the heat kernel, and let

\displaystyle g(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)f(\tau,y)dyd\tau.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}},\ \ \ \forall T>0,\ \sigma\in(0,1-\alpha),\ p\in[1,\infty].

(2.1)

Proof.
One has

\displaystyle g(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)(f(\tau,y)-f(t,y))dyd\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)f(t,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle:=g_{1}(t,x)+g_{2}(t,x).

By Lemma
2.1
we get

\displaystyle\|g_{1}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\|f(\tau)-f(t)\|_{L^{p}}d\tau\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\tau^{-\sigma-\alpha}d\tau\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim t^{-\sigma}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}.

Moreover, observe that
\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)d\tau=K(t,x-y)-\mathbf{Dirac}(x-y)
, hence

g_{2}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{K}(t,x-y)f(t,y)dy-f(t,x).

Then

\displaystyle\|g_{2}(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(1+\|\mathbf{K}(t)\|_{L^{1}})\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim t^{-\sigma}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}.

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}s^{\sigma}\|g(s)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}.

(2.2)

In the following we will denote
a=t-s>0
for convenience, and we denote
\delta_{\beta}f(s,x)=f(s+\beta,x)-f(s,x)
. We write

\displaystyle g(t)-g(s)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\delta_{a}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(s-\tau)\ast f(\tau)d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau)\ast f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\delta_{a}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(s-\tau)\ast(f(\tau)-f(s))d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau)\ast(f(\tau)-f(t))d\tau

\displaystyle+\big(\int_{0}^{s}\delta_{a}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(s-\tau)\ast f(s)d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau)\ast f(t)d\tau\big)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{s}\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}\frac{1}{(s-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\tau^{\sigma+\alpha}}d\tau\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{-\sigma-\alpha}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}},

where we use the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}\frac{1}{(s-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\tau^{\sigma+\alpha}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\frac{s}{2}}\frac{1}{(s-\tau)\tau^{\sigma+\alpha}}d\tau+s^{-\sigma-\alpha}\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{s}\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}\frac{1}{(s-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}d\tau\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{-\sigma-\alpha}.

(2.3)

For
I_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}}\frac{1}{\tau^{\sigma+\alpha}}d\tau\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{-\sigma-\alpha}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}.

For
I_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{3}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f(s)\ast\mathbf{K}(t-s)-f(s)\ast\mathbf{K}(t)-f(s)+f(s)\ast\mathbf{K}(s)+f(t)-f(t)\ast\mathbf{K}(t-s)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}+\|f(s)\ast(\mathbf{K}(t)-\mathbf{K}(s))\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim s^{-\sigma-\alpha}(t-s)^{\alpha}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}},

where we applied Lemma
2.1
to get
\|\mathbf{K}(t)-\mathbf{K}(s)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\frac{(t-s)^{\alpha}}{s^{\alpha}}
in the last inequality.
Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s<t<T}s^{1-\gamma+\alpha}\frac{\|g(t)-g(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}.

(2.4)

By (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), we get (
2.1
). The proof is complete.

Remark 2.4

The result in Lemma
2.3
is still true for
g(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{G}(t-\tau,x-y,x)f(\tau,y)dy
, where the kernel
\mathbf{G}(t,z,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{z}|\partial_{t}\mathbf{G}(t,z,x)|\lesssim|\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(\zeta t,x)|,

\displaystyle\sup_{z}|\partial_{t}\mathbf{G}(t,z,x)-\partial_{t}\mathbf{G}(s,z,x)|\lesssim|\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(\zeta s,x)-\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(\zeta t,x)|,

for a universal constant
\zeta>0
.

Lemma 2.5

Let
0\leq\beta<1-\alpha
; and
\tilde{\sigma}<1
,
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
,
1+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q}
,
1+\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}-\beta\geq 0
. Suppose that
G(t,y,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{x}\|G(t,\cdot,x)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim t^{-\beta},\ \ \ \sup_{x}\|G(t,\cdot,x)-G(s,\cdot,x)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim s^{-\beta}\min\{1,\frac{t-s}{s}\},

(2.5)

for any
0<s<t<T
.
Then for
g(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int G(t-\tau,x-y,x)f(\tau,y)dyd\tau
, we have

\|g\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim T^{1+\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}-\beta}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}.

Proof.
By Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|g(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\sup_{x}\|G(t-\tau,\cdot,x)\|_{L^{r}}\|f(\tau)\|_{L^{q}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta}}\frac{1}{\tau^{\tilde{\sigma}}}d\tau\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}\lesssim t^{1-\beta-\tilde{\sigma}}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}t^{\sigma}\|g(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim T^{1-\beta+\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}.

(2.6)

Moreover, for any
0<s<t<T
, denote
a=t-s
,

\displaystyle g(t)-g(s)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{a}G(s-\tau,x-y,x)f(\tau,y)dyd\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-\tau,x-y,x)f(\tau,y)dyd\tau.

By (
2.5
) and Young’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle\|g(t)-g(s)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}}{(s-\tau)^{\beta}}\frac{1}{\tau^{\tilde{\sigma}}}d\tau+\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\beta}}\frac{1}{\tau^{\tilde{\sigma}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{intt}}{\lesssim}a^{\alpha}s^{-\sigma-\alpha}T^{1-\beta+\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}}\|f\|_{X_{T}^{\tilde{\sigma},q}}.

Combining this with (
2.6
), we obtain the result.

Lemma 2.6

Suppose

g(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)R(\tau,y)dyd\tau.

Let
l=0,1
. Then for any
\frac{1}{1+2\alpha}<p<\frac{1}{2\alpha}
and
\sigma\geq\frac{1+l}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}
, there holds

\|\partial_{x}^{l}g\|_{X_{T}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim T^{\sigma-\frac{1+l}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}(\|R\|_{L_{T}^{1}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}).

Proof.
Note that

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{l}g(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)R(\tau,y)dyd\tau.

By Young’s inequality and Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}g(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau)\|_{L^{p}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau.

We divide the integral into two parts. When
\tau\in[0,{t/2}]

. This yields that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}g(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}(\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}).

(2.7)

It remains to check the Hölder norms. Let
0<s<t<T
and
a=t-s
, one has

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{l}g(t,x)-\partial_{x}^{l}g(s,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\delta_{a}\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}(s-\tau,x-y)R(\tau,y)dyd\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)R(\tau,y)dyd\tau:=\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}.

By Young’s inequality and Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{s}\|\delta_{a}\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}(s-\tau)\|_{L^{p}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau\lesssim\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau.

We discuss
\tau\in[0,s/2]
and
\tau\in[s/2,s]

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha}\int_{0}^{s/2}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau+s^{-1}\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\int_{s/2}^{s}(s-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}d\tau.

By a change of variable, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{s/2}^{s}(s-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\min\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\}d\tau=\int_{0}^{s/2}\tau^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\min\{1,\frac{a}{\tau}\}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbf{1}_{a\leq s/2}\left(\int_{0}^{a}\tau^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}d\tau+a\int_{a}^{s/2}\tau^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-1}d\tau\right)+\mathbf{1}_{a\geq s/2}\int_{0}^{s}\tau^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbf{1}_{a\leq s/2}a^{\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1-l}{2}}+\mathbf{1}_{a\geq s/2}s^{\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1-l}{2}}\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1-l}{2}-\alpha},

provided
\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1-l}{2}-\alpha\geq 0
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha}(\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}).

(2.8)

Then we deal with
\mathcal{R}_{2}
. By Young’s inequality,

\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau)\|_{L^{p}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau.

We consider two cases. If
s<t/2
, then one has
a\sim t
. Hence

\int_{s}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}(\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}})\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha}(\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}),

provided
\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha<0

If
s\geq t/2
, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}\|R(\tau)\|_{L^{1}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}}d\tau t^{-1}\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}\lesssim a^{\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1-l}{2}}t^{-1}\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha}\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}.

We conclude that

\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim a^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha}(\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}).

Combining this with (

) yields that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}g(t)-\partial_{x}^{l}g(s)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1+l}{2}-\alpha}(\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}+\|R\|_{X_{T}^{1,1}}).

(2.9)

Then we complete the proof in view of (

2.9

Lemma 3.4

For any non-negative measurable function

, there holds for any

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)\frac{dx}{x+y}\right)^{p}dy\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)^{p}dx.

[
3
]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)\frac{dx}{x+y}\right)^{p}dy\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{y}h(x){dx}\right)^{p}\frac{dy}{y^{p}}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{y}^{\infty}h(x)\frac{dx}{x}\right)^{p}{dy}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{\varepsilon})}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{\varepsilon})}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{1}(1+\ell^{-1})\|\bar{f}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}+\tilde{C}_{1}\|F\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}+\tilde{C}_{1}T^{\frac{1}{4}}\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}

\displaystyle+\tilde{C}_{1}(\ell C_{\phi}+T^{\frac{1}{3}}\ell^{-\frac{3}{4}})(\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{T}}).

Similarly, when
R=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{6}_{T}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{\varepsilon})}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{\varepsilon})}\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{1}(1+\ell^{-1})\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\tilde{C}_{1}\|F\|_{L^{2}_{T}}

\displaystyle+\tilde{C}_{1}(\ell C_{\phi}+\ell^{-1}T^{\frac{1}{4}})(\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{T}}+\|f\|_{L^{6}_{T}}).

Then we obtain the result by taking
\ell={(10+\tilde{C}_{1}+C_{\phi})^{-10}}
and
T=\ell^{10}
.
This completes the proof.

Remark 3.11

We have two ways to prove Lemma
3.10
. One is to approximate the kernel (see (
3.37
)), and the other is to cut off in space. We choose the latter one for simplicity. It is equivalent to approximate the kernel piece wisely, namely, approximate the kernel by
\sum_{n}\mathbf{H}(t,x,z_{n})\chi_{n}^{l}(x)
.

4
Well-posedness for the isentropic Navier–Stokes equations

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem of (
1.2
) with initial data

(v(0,x),u(0,x))=(v_{0},u_{0})
satisfying

\displaystyle v_{0}\in L^{\infty},\ \ \inf_{x}v_{0}\geq\lambda_{0}>0,\ \ u_{0}=\partial_{x}\bar{u}_{0}\ \text{with}\ \bar{u}_{0}\in\dot{C}^{2\gamma}.

(4.1)

The initial data
v_{0}
is allowed to have finite jump. More precisely, we assume there exists a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
satisfying

\displaystyle\min_{j\neq k}|a_{j}-a_{k}|\geq\sigma>0,\ \ \ \ \|v_{0}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

(4.2)

for some constants
0<\varepsilon,\eta\ll\sigma
. Here

\displaystyle b_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N+1}\left(v_{0}\ast\rho_{\eta}\mathbf{1}_{(a_{n-1}+\varepsilon,a_{n}-\varepsilon)}+(v_{0}\ast\rho_{\eta})(a_{n}+\varepsilon)\mathbf{1}_{[a_{n},a_{n}+\varepsilon]}+v_{0}\ast\rho_{\eta}(a_{n}-\varepsilon)\mathbf{1}_{[a_{n}-\varepsilon,a_{n}]}\right),

(4.3)

where we take
a_{0}=-\infty
and
a_{N+1}=+\infty
.

The main result is the following

Theorem 4.1

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},u_{0})
satisfies (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), then the system (
1.3
) admits a unique local solution
(v,u)
in
[0,T]
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(t,x)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2},\ \ \ \|v\|_{Y_{T}}\leq 2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\ \ \ \ \|\partial_{x}u\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq M.

for some
T,M>0
.

Clearly, the theorem (
1.1
) is as a consequece of Theorem
4.1
.

Define the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{T,M}:=\{w:w(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq M\}.

Consider
w\in\mathcal{E}_{T,M}
, we define a map
\mathcal{T}w=u
, where
u
is a solution to the equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mu(\frac{\partial_{x}u}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})_{x}

\displaystyle=-(p(v))_{x}+\mu((\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})\partial_{x}w)_{x}=:\tilde{F}(v,w)_{x},

(4.4)

where
v
is defined by

\displaystyle v(t,x)=v_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}w(s,x)ds.

(4.5)

The following is a key lemma to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Lemma 4.1

For any initial data

v_{0}\in L^{\infty},\ \ \inf_{x}v_{0}\geq\lambda_{0}>0,\ \ u_{0}=\partial_{x}\bar{u}_{0}\ \text{with}\ \bar{u}_{0}\in\dot{C}^{2\gamma},

there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if (
4.2
) holds, then

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq M,\ \ \forall w\in\mathcal{E}_{T,M},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w_{1}-\mathcal{T}w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}},\ \ \forall w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{T,M},

for some
T,M>0
depending on
\varepsilon_{0},\lambda_{0},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|\bar{u}_{0}\|_{\dot{C}^{2\gamma}}
.

Proof.
We first show the estimates of
v
. By definition (
4.5
), it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v_{0}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|w_{x}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\lesssim\|v_{0}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}+T^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}},

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s<t<T}s^{\alpha}\frac{\|v(t)-v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim\sup_{0<s<t<T}s^{\alpha}\frac{\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\quad\ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \lesssim\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\alpha}\frac{t^{\gamma}-s^{\gamma}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim T^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\leq\|v_{0}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}+T^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\overset{\eqref{conccc}}{\leq}\varepsilon_{0}+T^{\gamma}M.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{s\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(s,x)\geq\inf_{x}v_{0}(x)-\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|v(s)-v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\geq\lambda_{0}-T^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\geq\lambda_{0}-T^{\gamma}M.

We can take
T
small enough such that
T^{\gamma}M\leq\frac{1}{10}\min\{\varepsilon_{0},\lambda_{0}\}
, then

\displaystyle\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0},\ \ \ \ \ \inf_{s\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(s,x)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}.

(4.6)

Applying Lemma
3.1
to (
4.4
), there exists
T_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\lesssim\|\bar{u}_{0}\|_{\dot{C}^{2\gamma}}+\|\tilde{F}(v,w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}},\ \ \ \forall 0<T<T_{1}.

(4.7)

We have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{F}(v,w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\lesssim\|p(v)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}+\left\|(\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})\partial_{x}w\right\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

Note that
p\in W^{2,\infty}
, hence

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}t^{1-\gamma}\|p(v)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim T^{1-\gamma},

\displaystyle\frac{\|p(v)(t)-p(v)(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{\|v(t)-v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim s^{-\alpha}\|v-b_{\varepsilon}\|_{Y_{T}},\ \ \forall 0<s<t<T.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|p(v)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\lesssim T^{1-\gamma}(1+\|v-b_{\varepsilon}\|_{Y_{T}}).

(4.8)

Denote
q(t,x)=(\frac{1}{v(t,x)}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}(x)})\partial_{x}w(t,x)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|q(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v(t)-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{\gamma-1}\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.9)

Moreover, for
0<s<t<T
, we have

\displaystyle q(t,x)-q(s,x)=(\frac{1}{v(t)}-\frac{1}{v(s)})\partial_{x}w(t)+(\frac{1}{v(s)}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})(\partial_{x}w(t)-\partial_{x}w(s)).

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|(\frac{1}{v(t)}-\frac{1}{v(s)})\partial_{x}w(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v(t)-v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}w(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \lesssim(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\gamma-1-\alpha}\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}},

\displaystyle\left\|(\frac{1}{v(s)}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})(\partial_{x}w(t)-\partial_{x}w(s))\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v(s)-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w(t)-w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\lesssim(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\gamma-1-\alpha}\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

Then one gets

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s<t<T}s^{1-\gamma+\alpha}\frac{\left\|q(t)-q(s)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.10)

Hence we get from (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) that

\displaystyle\|q\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\lesssim\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

Combining this with (
4.8
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{F}(v,w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}T^{1-\gamma}(1+\|v-b_{\varepsilon}\|_{Y_{T}})+C_{1}\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.11)

We conclude from (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.11
) that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|\bar{u}_{0}\|_{\dot{C}^{2\gamma}}+C_{1}T^{1-\gamma}(1+2\varepsilon_{0})+2C_{1}\varepsilon_{0}M,\ \ \ \ \ \forall w\in\mathcal{E}_{T,M}.

Then we obtain
\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w)\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq M
by taking
M=2C_{1}\|\bar{u}_{0}\|_{\dot{C}^{2\gamma}}+1
,
\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{1}{(1+C_{1})^{10}}
and
T<\min\{\frac{1}{(1+C_{1})^{10}},T_{1}\}
. This implies that
\mathcal{T}
maps
\mathcal{E}_{T,M}
to itself.

It remains to prove that
\mathcal{T}
is contraction. Consider
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{T,M}
, let
v_{m}(t,x)=v_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}w_{m}(s,x)ds
,
m=1,2
. Then it is easy to check that both
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
satisfy (
4.6
), and

\displaystyle\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y_{T}}\lesssim T^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.12)

Thanks to Lemma
3.1
, there exists
T_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w_{1}-\mathcal{T}w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\lesssim\|\tilde{F}(v_{1},w_{1})-\tilde{F}(v_{2},w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}},\ \ \forall 0<T<T_{2}.

(4.13)

Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(v_{1},w_{1})-\tilde{F}(v_{2},w_{2})=p(v_{2})-p(v_{1})+\mu\left(\left(\frac{1}{v_{1}}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)\partial_{x}w_{1}-\left(\frac{1}{v_{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)\partial_{x}w_{2}\right).

We first estimate
p(v_{2})-p(v_{1})
. Note that
p\in W^{2,\infty}
, hence

\displaystyle\|p(v_{2}(t))-p(v_{1}(t))\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y_{T}},

and

\displaystyle\|p(v_{2}(t))-p(v_{1}(t))-(p(v_{2}(s))-p(v_{1}(s)))\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(v_{2}-v_{1})(t)-(v_{2}-v_{1})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{2}(t)-v_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|v_{1}(t)-v_{1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{2}(t)-v_{2}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}})

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{-\alpha}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y_{T}}(1+\|v_{1}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}+\|v_{2}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}).

Hence

\displaystyle\|p(v_{2})-p(v_{1})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{1-\gamma}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y_{T}}(1+\|v_{1}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}+\|v_{2}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{difv}}{\lesssim}T\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.14)

Then we estimate
\mathcal{Q}=\left(\frac{1}{v_{1}}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)\partial_{x}w_{1}-\left(\frac{1}{v_{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)\partial_{x}w_{2}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\left(\frac{1}{v_{1}}-\frac{1}{v_{2}}\right)\partial_{x}w_{1}+\left(\frac{1}{v_{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2}).

For any
0<t<T
, one has

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|(v_{1}-v_{2})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{\gamma-1}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w_{1}\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.15)

For any
0<s<t<T
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}(t)-\mathcal{Q}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(v_{1}-v_{2})(t)-(v_{1}-v_{2})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\|(v_{1}-v_{2})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|v_{1}(t)-v_{1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{2}(t)-v_{2}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\partial_{x}w_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\|(v_{1}-v_{2})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}(t)-w_{1}(s))\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{2}(t)-v_{2}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ +\|v_{2}(s)-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})(t)-\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\gamma-1-\alpha}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}w_{1}\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}(1+\|v_{1}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}+\|v_{2}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+(t-s)^{\alpha}s^{\gamma-1-\alpha}\|v_{2}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.16)

Combining (
4.12
), (
4.15
), (
4.16
) with the fact that
\|v_{1}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}+\|v_{2}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}
and
\|\partial_{x}w_{1}\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq M
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\lesssim MT^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

(4.17)

We conclude from (
4.13
), (
4.14
) and (
4.17
) that

\|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}w_{1}-\mathcal{T}w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq C_{2}(MT^{\gamma}+T)\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{1-\gamma,\infty}}.

Here we fix
T=\min\left\{\frac{1}{(1+C_{1}+C_{2})^{10}},T_{1},T_{2}\right\}
. This completes the proof.

5
Well-posedness for the full Navier–Stokes system

In this section, we consider the Cauchy problem of (
1.3
) with initial data

(v,u,\theta)(0,x)=(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})(x)
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{x}v_{0}(x)\geq\lambda_{0}>0,\ \ \ \|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,\ \ \|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty,\ \ \|\theta_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{-\frac{2}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}<\infty.

(5.1)

Moreover, we suppose that there exist
0<\varepsilon,\eta\ll 1
such that
v_{0}
satisfies the condition (
4.2
) for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
that will be fixed later in our proof.

Theorem 5.1

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})
satisfies (
5.1
) and (
4.2
), then the system (
1.3
) admits a unique local solution in
[0,T]
satisfying

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(t,x)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2},\ \ \ \|v\|_{Y_{T}}\leq 2\|v_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\ \ \ \ \|(u,\theta)\|_{Z_{T}}\leq B.

for some
T,B>0.

Clearly, the theorem (
1.2
) is as a consequece of Theorem
5.1
.

Define the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{T,B}=\{(w,\vartheta):(w,\vartheta)|_{t=0}=(u_{0},\theta_{0}),\|(w,\vartheta)\|_{Z_{T}}\leq B\}.

Let
(w,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{X}_{T,B}
, we define a map
\mathcal{M}(w,\vartheta)=(u,\theta)
, where
u,\theta
solve the equations

\displaystyle u_{t}-\mu(\frac{u_{x}}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})_{x}+(p(v,\theta))_{x}=\mu((\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})\partial_{x}w)_{x},\ \ \ \ u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle\theta_{t}-\frac{\kappa}{\mathbf{c}}\left(\frac{\theta_{x}}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)_{x}=-\frac{p(v,\vartheta)}{\mathbf{c}}w_{x}+\frac{\mu}{\mathbf{c}v}\left(w_{x}\right)^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\mathbf{c}}((\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})\partial_{x}\vartheta)_{x},\ \ \ \theta(0,x)=\theta_{0}(x),

where
v
is fixed by

\displaystyle v(t,x)=v_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}w(s,x)ds,

and the function
b_{\varepsilon,\eta}
is defined in (
4.3
).

The following is the key lemma to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 5.1

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})
satisfies (
5.1
) and (
4.2
), then we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}(w,\vartheta)\|_{Z_{T}}\leq B,\ \ \forall(w,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{X}_{T,B},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}(w_{1},\vartheta_{1})-\mathcal{M}(w_{1},\vartheta_{2})\|_{Z_{T}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|(w_{1}-w_{2},\vartheta_{1}-\vartheta_{2})\|_{Z_{T}},\ \ \forall(w_{k},\vartheta_{k})\in\mathcal{X}_{T,B},\ k=1,2.

for some
T,B>0
.

Proof.

1.
Estimate
v

We have

\displaystyle\|v(t)-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|v_{0}-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|w_{x}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau,

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(t,x)\geq\inf_{x}v_{0}(x)-\int_{0}^{t}\|w_{x}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|w_{x}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\leq CT^{\frac{1}{4}}\|w_{x}\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{3}{4},\infty}}\leq CT^{\frac{1}{4}}B.

We take
T
small enough such that
CT^{\frac{1}{4}}B\leq\frac{1}{100}\min\{\lambda_{0},\varepsilon_{0}\}
, and

\displaystyle\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{Y_{T}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0},\ \ \ \ \ \inf_{s\in[0,T]}\inf_{x}v(s,x)\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}.

(5.2)

2.
Estimate
\theta

\displaystyle\theta_{t}-\frac{\kappa}{\mathbf{c}}\left(\frac{\theta_{x}}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}}\right)_{x}=-\frac{p(v,\vartheta)}{\mathbf{c}}w_{x}+\frac{\mu}{\mathbf{c}v}\left(w_{x}\right)^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{\mathbf{c}}((\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon,\eta}})\partial_{x}\vartheta)_{x}:=R(v,w,\vartheta)+\partial_{x}\tilde{F}(v,\vartheta).

Applying Lemma
3.1
with the equation above for
\theta
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\star\in\{L^{2}_{T},X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}\}}\|\theta\|_{\star}+\sum_{\star\in\{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T},X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}\}}\|\theta_{x}\|_{\star}\lesssim\|\bar{\theta}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}+\sum_{\star\in\{L_{T}^{\frac{6}{5}},X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}\}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{\star}+T^{\frac{1}{4}}\sum_{\star\in\{L^{1}_{T},X_{T}^{1,1}\}}\|R\|_{\star}.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sum_{\star\in\{L_{T}^{\frac{6}{5}},X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}\}}\|\tilde{F}\|_{\star}\lesssim\|v-b_{\varepsilon,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}}(\|\partial_{x}\vartheta\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{6}{5}}}+\|\partial_{x}\vartheta\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}})\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}B,

\displaystyle\sum_{\star\in\{L^{1}_{T},X_{T}^{1,1}\}}\|R\|_{\star}\lesssim\|w_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}^{2}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}_{T}}^{2}+\|w_{x}\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}}^{2}+\|\vartheta\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}}^{2}\lesssim B^{2}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{L^{2}_{T}}+\|\theta_{x}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}+\|\theta\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}}+\|\theta_{x}\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{5}{6},\frac{6}{5}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \ \leq\tilde{C}_{0}\|\bar{\theta}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}+\tilde{C}_{0}\varepsilon_{0}B+\tilde{C}_{0}T^{\frac{1}{4}}B^{2}\leq\frac{B}{3},

(5.3)

provided
\|\bar{\theta}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}\leq\frac{B}{\tilde{C}_{0}}
,
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{10(1+\tilde{C}_{0})^{10}}
and
T\leq\frac{1}{(1+\tilde{C}_{0}+B)^{10}}
.

3.
Estimate
u

Recall the equation

\displaystyle u_{t}-\mu\left(\frac{u_{x}}{b_{\varepsilon}}\right)_{x}=-(p(v,\theta))_{x}+\mu((\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{b_{\varepsilon}})w_{x})_{x}=:\partial_{x}F(v,w,\theta).

Applying Lemma
3.1
with the equation above for
u
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}+\|u_{x}\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}}+\|u_{x}\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{3}{4},\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|F\|_{L^{2}_{T}}+\|F\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{1}{2},2}}+\|F\|_{X_{T}^{\frac{3}{4},\infty}}.

It is easy to check that

by a change of variable,

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{l}f_{L1}(t,x)|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{0}(t,z)|\Delta_{z}\bar{f}_{0}(x)|dz.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}f_{L1}\|_{L^{p}_{T}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{0}(t,z)|\Delta_{z}\bar{f}_{0}(x)|dz\right)^{p}dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{0}(t,z)\|\Delta_{z}\bar{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{x}}dz\right)^{p}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{0}(t,z)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(1+|z|^{2}t^{-1})^{\frac{\gamma p}{p-1}}dz\right)^{p-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|\Delta_{z}\bar{f}_{0}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}}{(1+|z|^{2}t^{-1})^{\gamma p}}dz\right)dt,

where we applied Minkowski’s inequality in the first inequality, and Hölder’s inequality in the second inequality.
It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{0}(t,z)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(1+|z|^{2}t^{-1})^{\frac{\gamma p}{p-1}}dz\right)^{p-1}\lesssim t^{\frac{-(l+1)p-1}{2}},

where we fix
\gamma=\frac{1+l}{2}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}f_{L}(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{T}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{-(l+1)p-1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|\Delta_{z}\bar{f}_{0}(x)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}}{(1+|z|^{2}t^{-1})^{\gamma p}}dz\right)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\|\Delta_{z}\bar{f}_{0}(x)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}}{|z|^{(1+l)p-1}}dz=c\|\bar{f}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{p-2}{p}+l,p}}^{p}.

(6.3)

Hence we obtain that

\displaystyle\|f_{L1}\|_{L^{6}_{T}}\lesssim\|\bar{f}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{2}{3},6}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}\bar{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \|\partial_{x}f_{L1}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{\infty}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\bar{f}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{3},\frac{6}{5}}}.

(6.4)

Combining this with (
6.2
) and (
6.4
), one has

\displaystyle\|f_{L1}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}+\|\partial_{x}f_{L1}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}\lesssim\|\bar{f}_{0}\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{3},\frac{6}{5}}},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\|f_{L1}\|_{L^{6}_{T}}+\|\partial_{x}f_{L1}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}\bar{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Then we estimate
f_{L2}
. By Parseval’s identity, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}f_{L2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|^{2}|e^{-|\xi|^{2}\tau}\mathcal{F}(\tilde{f}_{0})(\xi)|^{2}d\xi d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}(\tilde{f}_{0})(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, by Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|f_{L2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim\|\mathbf{K}\|_{L^{2}_{t,T}L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{x}}\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}},

\displaystyle\|f_{L2}\|_{L^{6}_{T}}\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\mathbf{K}(x-y)\|_{L^{6}_{t,T}}|\tilde{f}_{0}(y)|dy\right\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\tilde{f}_{0}(y)|\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\lesssim\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}f_{L2}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{t,T}L^{1}_{x}}\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}.

Hence we obtain that

\displaystyle\|f_{L2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}+\|\partial_{x}f_{L2}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{T}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\|f_{L2}\|_{L^{6}_{T}}+\|\partial_{x}f_{L2}\|_{L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim\|\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Then we estimate the forced term
f_{N}
. We can write

\partial_{t}f_{N}-\partial_{x}^{2}f_{N}=\partial_{x}F_{0},\ \ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\ \ \ \ \text{where}\ \ F_{0}=F\mathbf{1}_{t>0}.

We take Fourier transform in
(t,x)
and obtain that

\displaystyle f_{N}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x}\left(\frac{\xi\mathcal{F}_{T}(F_{0})(\tau,\xi)}{i\tau+\xi^{2}}\right),

where we denote
\mathcal{F}_{t,x}
the Fourier transform with respect to
(t,x)
, and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{T}
its inverse. It follows from the parabolic Calderon-Zygmund theory (see
[
17
]
) that

\left\|\partial_{x}f_{N}\right\|_{L^{p}_{T}}\lesssim\|F_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{T}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{p}_{T}},\ \ \ \ p\in(1,\infty).

On the other hand, we have

\left|\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(t,x)\right|\lesssim{(t^{\frac{1}{2}}+|x|)^{-2}}.

Then by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)F(\tau,y)dyd\tau\right\|_{L^{p}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(\cdot,x-y)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{t,T}}\|F(y)\|_{L^{\frac{3p}{3+p}}_{t,T}}dy\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|F(y)\|_{L^{\frac{3p}{3+p}}_{t,T}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{\frac{2}{3}}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\frac{3p}{3+p}}_{T}}.

Hence we obtain that for
l=0,1
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{l}f_{N}\|_{L^{p}_{T}}=\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{1+l}\mathbf{K}(t-\tau,x-y)F(\tau,y)dyd\tau\|_{L^{p}_{T}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{\frac{3p}{3+(1-l)p}}_{T}}.

(6.7)

Finally, we estimate
f_{R}
. By Hölder’s inequality we have

\|\partial_{x}^{l}f_{R}\|_{L^{p}_{T}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}\|_{L^{p}_{T}}\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{4}}\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}},\ \ (l,p)\in\{(0,2),(1,6/5)\},

and

\|\partial_{x}^{l}f_{R}\|_{L^{p}_{T}}\lesssim\|\partial_{x}^{l}\mathbf{K}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2+p}}_{T}}\|R\|_{L^{1}_{T}}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|R\|_{L^{2}_{T}},\ \ (l,p)\in\{(0,6),(1,2)\}.

Combining this with (
6.5
), (
6.6
) and (
6.7
), we finish the proof.

Consider the parabolic equation with jump coefficients,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{\pm}-c_{\pm}f_{xx}^{\pm}=F,\ \ \ \text{in}\ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{\pm},

(6.8)

\displaystyle f^{+}(t,0)=f^{-}(t,0),\quad\quad\ \ c_{+}\partial_{x}f^{+}(t,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f^{-}(t,0)=0,\ \ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle f(0,x)=f_{0}(x),\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 6.2

Solution to (
6.8
) has formula

f=f^{+}\mathbf{1}_{x\geq 0}+f^{-}\mathbf{1}_{x<0}
, where

f^{\pm}(t,x)=f_{M}^{\pm}(t,x)+f_{B}^{\pm}(t,x).

with

\displaystyle f_{M}^{\pm}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\pm}}(\mathbf{K}(c_{\pm}t,x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{\pm}t,x+y))f_{0}(y)dy

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\pm}}(\mathbf{K}(c_{\pm}(t-\tau),x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{\pm}(t-\tau),x+y))F(\tau,y)dyd\tau,

(6.9)

\displaystyle f_{B}^{\pm}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\frac{-2\sqrt{c_{\pm}}}{\sqrt{c_{+}}+\sqrt{c_{-}}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{K}(c_{\pm}(t-\tau),x)(c_{+}\partial_{x}f_{M}^{+}(\tau,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f_{M}^{-}(\tau,0))d\tau.

(6.10)

Proof.
By superposition principle, we have
f^{\pm}(t,x)=f_{M}^{\pm}(t,x)+f_{B}^{\pm}(t,x)
, where

f_{M}^{\pm}
solves the following equation on half space

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}f_{M}^{+}-c_{+}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{M}^{+}=F,\ \ \ \text{in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
&f_{M}^{+}(0,x)=f_{0}(x),\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
&f_{M}^{+}(t,0)=0,\ \ \ t\in(0,T).\end{cases}\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}f_{M}^{-}-c_{-}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{M}^{-}=F,\ \ \ \text{in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{-},\\
&f_{M}^{-}(0,x)=f_{0}(x),\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{-},\\
&f_{M}^{-}(t,0)=0,\ \ \ t\in(0,T).\end{cases}

And
f_{B}^{\pm}
solves the system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{B}^{\pm}-c_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{B}^{\pm}=0,\ \ \ \text{in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{\pm},

(6.11)

\displaystyle f_{B}^{+}(0,x)=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{+},\ \ f_{B}^{-}(0,x)=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{-},

\displaystyle f_{B}^{+}(t,0)=f_{B}^{-}(t,0),\ \ \ t\in(0,T),

\displaystyle c_{+}\partial_{x}f_{B}^{+}(t,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f_{B}^{-}(t,0)=-(c_{+}\partial_{x}f_{M}^{+}(t,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f_{M}^{-}(t,0)),\ \ \ t\in(0,T).

Indeed, integrate by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x+y))F(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x+y))(\partial_{t}f_{M}^{+}-c_{+}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{M}^{+})(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle=f_{M}^{+}(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\mathbf{K}(c_{+}t,x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{+}t,x+y))f_{0}(y)dy

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\partial_{t}(\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x+y))f_{M}^{+}(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle\quad\quad-c_{+}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\partial_{x}^{2}(\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x+y))f_{M}^{+}(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle=f_{M}^{+}(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}(\mathbf{K}(c_{+}t,x-y)-\mathbf{K}(c_{+}t,x+y))f_{0}(y)dy,

where we use the fact that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{K}(c_{+}t,x)-c_{+}\partial_{x}^{2}\mathbf{K}(c_{+}t,x)=0,\ \ \forall\ t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

We also get similar estimate for
f^{-}_{M}
. Hence we obtain formula (
6.9
).

It remains to check that formula (
6.10
) satisfies equation (
6.11
). For simplicity, we only check the Neumann boundary condition

\displaystyle c_{+}\partial_{x}f_{B}^{+}(t,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f_{B}^{-}(t,0)=-(c_{+}\partial_{x}f_{M}^{+}(t,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f_{M}^{-}(t,0)):=h(t),\ \ \ t\in(0,T).

(6.12)

And other conditions are trivial.
Note that

\displaystyle c_{+}\partial_{x}f_{B}^{+}(t,0)-c_{-}\partial_{x}f_{B}^{-}(t,0)=

\displaystyle\frac{2c_{+}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{c_{+}}+\sqrt{c_{-}}}\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x)h(\tau)d\tau

(6.13)

\displaystyle-\frac{2c_{-}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{c_{+}}+\sqrt{c_{-}}}\lim_{x\to 0^{-}}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(c_{-}(t-\tau),x)h(\tau)d\tau.

Note that the heat kernel has scaling
\mathbf{K}(t,x)=t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{K}(1,\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}})
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x)h(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{x}{2c_{+}\tau}K(c_{+}\tau,x)h(t-\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{x}{2(c_{+}\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}}K(1,\frac{x}{\sqrt{c_{+}\tau}})h(t-\tau)d\tau.

Let
\tilde{\tau}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{c_{+}\tau}}
, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x)h(\tau)d\tau=c_{+}^{-1}\int_{\frac{x}{\sqrt{c_{+}t}}}^{\infty}\mathbf{K}(1,\tilde{\tau})h(t-\frac{x^{2}}{c_{+}\tilde{\tau}^{2}})d\tilde{\tau}.

We can write

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{t}}^{\infty}\mathbf{K}(1,\tilde{\tau})h(t-\frac{x^{2}}{c_{+}\tilde{\tau}^{2}})d\tilde{\tau}=\left(\int_{\tilde{t}^{1/2}}^{\infty}+\int_{\tilde{t}}^{\tilde{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\mathbf{K}(1,\tilde{\tau})h(t-\frac{x^{2}}{c_{+}\tilde{\tau}^{2}})d\tilde{\tau},

where we denote
\tilde{t}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{c_{+}t}}
.
We have
\tilde{t}\to 0
when
x\to 0
, and the integral on
[\tilde{t},\tilde{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}]
tends to 0. Hence,

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(c_{+}(t-\tau),x)h(\tau)d\tau=c_{+}^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{K}(1,\tilde{\tau})d\tilde{\tau}h(t)=\frac{1}{2c_{+}}h(t).

Similarly, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0^{-}}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{x}\mathbf{K}(c_{-}(t-\tau),x)h(\tau)d\tau=-\frac{1}{2c_{-}}h(t).

(6.14)

Then we obtain (
6.12
) from (
6.13
)-(
6.14
).
This completes the proof.

Consider the following parabolic equation with variable coefficient,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f-\partial_{x}(\phi(x)f_{x})=F,\quad\quad\ \ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R},

(6.15)

\displaystyle f(0,x)=f_{0}(x).

Lemma 6.3

the
Solution
f
to (
6.15
) satisfies

\displaystyle f(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{H}(t,x-y,x)f_{0}(y)dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)F(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)(\phi(x)-\phi(y))f_{x}(\tau,y)dyd\tau,

where we denote
\mathbf{H}(t,x,y)=\mathbf{K}({\phi(y)}{t},x)
,
\partial_{1}\mathbf{H}(t,x,y)=\partial_{x}\mathbf{H}(t,x,y)
.

Proof.
Integrate by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)F(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)(\partial_{t}f-\partial_{x}(\phi f_{x}))(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle=f(t,x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{H}(t,x-y,x)f_{0}(y)dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)f(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)(\phi(x)-\phi(y))f_{x}(\tau,y)dyd\tau

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\phi(x)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{1}^{2}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)f(\tau,y)dyd\tau.

It is easy to check that

\partial_{t}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)-\phi(x)\partial_{1}^{2}\mathbf{H}(t-\tau,x-y,x)=0.

Then we obtain the result.

The Geometry of Three-Forms on Symplectic Six-Manifolds
Teng Fei

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the geometries associated with 3-forms of various orbital types on a symplectic 6-manifold. We show that there are extremely rich geometric structures attached to certain unstable 3-forms arising naturally from degeneration of Calabi-Yau structures, which in turn provides us a new perspective towards the SYZ conjecture. We give concrete examples and demonstrate that the limiting behavior of the Type IIA flow can be used to detect canonical geometric structures on symplectic manifolds.



1
Introduction

The classification 3-forms in dimension six has been known for a long time
[
Rei07
,
Gur35
,
Gur64
,
Cha98
,
Bry06
]
. A distinguished feature of 3-forms in dimension six is that there are two open (a.k.a. stable) orbits under the natural general linear group action. This fact has been utilized by many authors, such as Hitchin
[
Hit00
]
and Donaldson-Lehmann
[
DL24b
,
DL24c
,
DL24a
]
, to study various geometric and deformation problems in complex and Calabi-Yau geometry.

In this paper, we systematically investigate various geometric structures associated with 3-forms on a 6-dimensional manifold, including those 3-forms from unstable orbits under the general linear group action. We introduce several natural integrability conditions on 3-forms and study the relationships among them. We examine in detail the geometry of certain unstable 3-forms arising naturally from degeneration of Calabi-Yau structures. In particular, we show that such manifolds possess a canonical Lagrangian foliation as a result of degeneration of complex structures. Moreover, we prove that the leaves of this foliation naturally admit a pair of dual Hessian structures, where the local potentials of the metric automatically solve a real Monge-Ampère equation, hence demonstrating a close relationship with the Strominger-Yau-Zaslow conjecture
[
SYZ96
]
. As explicit examples, we study special 3-forms on certain nilmanifolds and solvmanifolds, where we also apply the Type IIA flow to detect canonical geometric structures.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the classification of 3-forms in dimension 6 under both the general linear group and the symplectic group action. We introduce certain homogeneous equivariant polynomials to describe the orbits of 3-forms. Section 3 is devoted to integrability conditions on 3-forms. In particular, we introduce the notion of
F
-harmonic 3-forms as an ideal integrability condition. In Section 4, we study the geometry associated to an
F
-harmonic 3-form lying in the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{+}
. Such 3-forms arises naturally from the degeneration of Calabi-Yau structures and they induce extremely rich geometric structures on the underlying symplectic manifolds, including a canonical Lagrangian foliation with leaves equipped with dual Hessian structures with an invariant volume form. In Section 5 we work with certain symplectic nilmanifolds and solvmanifolds and examine the space of special 3-forms on them. We also investigate the limiting behavior of the Type IIA flow of 3-forms and use it as a tool to detect canonical structures on such manifolds.

Classification of three-forms

Let
V
be a real vector space of dimension
6
. We denote by
\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
the space of 3-form on
V
. The group
\mathrm{GL}(V)
naturally acts on
\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
. This action is “stable” in the sense that it has open orbits. In fact, all orbits of this action have been classified classically, see for example
[
Rei07
,
Gur35
,
Gur64
,
Cha98
,
Bry06
]
.

To better describe these orbits, we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

A 3-form
\varphi\in\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
is called
stable
if it lies in an open orbit under the
\mathrm{GL}(V)
-action.

For any 3-form
\varphi\in\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
, its kernel
\ker\varphi
is defined to be

\ker\varphi=\{v\in V:\iota_{v}\varphi=0\}.

Definition 2.2
.

A 3-form
\varphi
is called
non-degenerate
if
\ker\varphi=\{0\}
.

As
\varphi
induces a non-degenerate 3-form
\bar{\varphi}\in\bigwedge^{3}(V/\ker\varphi)^{*}
, it is easy to see that the only possible dimensions for
\ker\varphi
are
0
,
1
,
3
, and
6
.

For later use, we introduce several equivariant homogeneous polynomials on the space
\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}
:

(a).

K
is an equivariant homogeneous degree 2 polynomial of
\varphi
such that
K(\varphi)\in\mathrm{End}~V\otimes\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
for any
\varphi
. It is defined by

K(\varphi)(v)=-\iota_{v}\varphi\wedge\varphi\in\bigwedge\nolimits^{5}V^{*}\cong V\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{6}V^{*}

for any
v\in V
.

(b).

F
is an equivariant homogeneous degree 3 polynomial of
\varphi
such that
F(\varphi)\in\bigwedge^{3}V^{*}\otimes\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
for any
\varphi
. It is defined by

F(\varphi)(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=-2\varphi(K(\varphi)(v_{1}),v_{2},v_{3})

for any
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V
. Modulo tensoring the one dimensional space
\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
,
F(\varphi)
is also a 3-form. Therefore it makes sense to talk about
\ker F(\varphi)
. By this definition, we know that

\ker F(\varphi)=K(\varphi)^{-1}(\ker\varphi\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{6}V^{*}).

(c).

Q
is an equivariant homogeneous degree 4 polynomial of
\varphi
such that
Q(\varphi)\in(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 2}
for any
\varphi
. It is defined by

Q(\varphi)=-\varphi\wedge F(\varphi).

Definition 2.3
.

An element of
(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 2}
is called
positive
if it can be written as
\tau^{2}
for
0\neq\tau\in\bigwedge^{6}V^{*}
. An element of
(\bigwedge^{6}V^{*})^{\otimes 2}
is called
negative
if its negative is positive.

F(\varphi)&=2\hat{\varphi}\cdot\sqrt{-\lambda(\varphi)},\\
Q(\varphi)&=4\lambda(\varphi).\end{split}

\mathrm{d}Q(\varphi)=0

It is not surprising that these notions of integrability are related to each other. To explore this aspect, let us first establish a series of useful identities.

Lemma 3.2

For any vector fields

and

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)=-\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\iota_{X}(\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)),

(3)

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)+\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)=0,

(4)

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)=\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi).

(5)

Proof.

We shall prove this lemma by direct calculation. It is noteworthy that we have the following equations from the definition of
K
,
F
, and

:

\displaystyle\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\varphi,

(6)

\displaystyle\iota_{X}F(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\iota_{KX}\varphi,

(7)

\displaystyle\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Q(\varphi)\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!},

(8)

\displaystyle K^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q(\varphi)}{4}\mathrm{id}.

(9)

Using (

-\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)=2\varphi\wedge\iota_{KX}\varphi=-2\iota_{K^{2}X}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=-\frac{Q}{2}\iota_{X}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}.

Therefore

\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)-\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)=\iota_{X}(\varphi\wedge F(\varphi))=-\iota_{X}Q\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=-2\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)

and we prove (

Take

\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}(\varphi\wedge F(\varphi))=\iota_{Y}(\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi))=\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi).

As this expression is anti-symmetric in

Take
\iota_{Y}
of left and middle sides of (

\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)=-\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi).

Plug in (

∎

Next we establish an identity relating the Nijenhuis tensor

with other quantities like

Theorem 3.3

The Nijenhuis tensor

\displaystyle\iota_{N_{K}(X,Y)}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)-\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi)+2\varphi\wedge(\iota_{Y}\iota_{KX}-\iota_{X}\iota_{KY})\mathrm{d}\varphi

(10)

\displaystyle+\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi).

Proof.

From (
6
) and (
7
) we know

\displaystyle-\iota_{[KX,KY]}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=-\iota_{\mathcal{L}_{KX}(KY)}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{KX}\iota_{KY}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}+\iota_{KY}\mathcal{L}_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{KX}\iota_{KY}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}+\mathcal{L}_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}-d\iota_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{KX}(\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\varphi)-\mathcal{L}_{KY}(\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\varphi)-d\iota_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}_{KX}\iota_{Y}\varphi-\mathcal{L}_{KY}\iota_{X}\varphi\right)\wedge\varphi-d\iota_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}+\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi

Similarly, we have that

\displaystyle\iota_{K([KX,Y]+[X,KY])}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=-\iota_{[KX,Y]}\varphi\wedge\varphi-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{L}_{KX}\iota_{Y}\varphi+\iota_{Y}\mathcal{L}_{KX}\varphi)\wedge\varphi-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\mathcal{L}_{KX}\iota_{Y}\varphi+\iota_{Y}\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}F(\varphi)\right)\wedge\varphi-(X\leftrightarrow Y),

and

\displaystyle-\iota_{K^{2}[X,Y]}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=\iota_{K[X,Y]}\varphi\wedge\varphi=-\frac{1}{2}\iota_{[X,Y]}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-\iota_{Y}\mathcal{L}_{X}F(\varphi))\wedge\varphi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}+\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}-\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}\iota_{X})F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi.

Adding (

\displaystyle\iota_{N_{K}(X,Y)}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\iota_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi

\displaystyle+\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi+\iota_{Y}\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\varphi+\frac{1}{4}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi-(X\leftrightarrow Y).

Notice that

\displaystyle\iota_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=-\iota_{KY}(\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\iota_{KY}\varphi\wedge\varphi-\iota_{X}\wedge\iota_{KY}\varphi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi),

Consequently we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\iota_{KY}\iota_{KX}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi+\frac{1}{4}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)\wedge\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-\frac{1}{4}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi).

Plug (

\displaystyle\iota_{N_{K}(X,Y)}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{d}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)+\frac{1}{4}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)\wedge\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle+\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi+\iota_{Y}\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\varphi+\frac{1}{4}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)\wedge\varphi-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge(\mathcal{L}_{Y}F(\varphi)-\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi))-\frac{1}{4}(\mathcal{L}_{X}\varphi-\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi)\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)+\frac{1}{4}\iota_{X}\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)\wedge\mathrm{d}\varphi

Observe that

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathcal{L}_{Y}F(\varphi)-\mathcal{L}_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathcal{L}_{Y}F(\varphi)+\mathcal{L}_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y}(\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi))-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)-\varphi\wedge\mathcal{L}_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{X}\iota_{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\iota_{X})\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge(\mathcal{L}_{X}\iota_{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\iota_{X})F(\varphi),

\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)=0

\mathcal{L}_{X}\iota_{Y}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\iota_{X}=\iota_{[X,Y]}+\iota_{Y}\mathcal{L}_{X}-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\iota_{X}=\iota_{[X,Y]}+\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X},

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathcal{L}_{Y}F(\varphi)-\mathcal{L}_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)-\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)-\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi).

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathcal{L}_{Y}F(\varphi)-\mathcal{L}_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)-\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)-\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi).

Substitute it back into (

\displaystyle\iota_{N_{K}(X,Y)}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}(\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)-\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)-\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi))

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)+\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi),

\displaystyle-\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)-(X\leftrightarrow Y)

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)+\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi),

\displaystyle\iota_{Y}\varphi\wedge\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi-\iota_{Y}\iota_{KX}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\varphi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Y}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{X}F(\varphi)-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi).

\displaystyle\iota_{N_{K}(X,Y)}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)+\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge F(\varphi)-\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}F(\varphi)+2\varphi\wedge(\iota_{Y}\iota_{KX}-\iota_{X}\iota_{KY})\mathrm{d}\varphi.

As a corollary, we have proved

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\varphi
is integrable and
F
-integrable, then it is
K
-integrable.

Remark 3.5
.

Notice that
K
-integrability implies that

[KX,KY]=K([KX,Y]+[X,KY]-K[X,Y])\in\mathrm{Im}~K.

It follows from the Frobenius theorem that the distribution
\mathrm{Im}~K(\varphi)
is integrable. However, it seems that
K
-integrability is a stronger condition than that
\mathrm{Im}~K(\varphi)
is integrable as a distribution. For example, if
\varphi
belongs to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{\pm}
pointwise, then
\mathrm{Im}~K(\varphi)=\ker F(\varphi)
, hence the integrability of
\mathrm{Im}~K(\varphi)
is guaranteed solely by the
F
-integrability of
\varphi
.

Next, we also prove that

Theorem 3.6
.

If
\varphi
is integrable and
F
-integrable, then it is
Q
-integrable.

Proof.

We need to prove that
Q(\varphi)
is a constant on
M
under the condition that
\mathrm{d}\varphi=\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)=0
. We can decompose
M
as

M=\{Q<0\}\coprod\{Q=0\}\coprod\{Q>0\}.

As
\{Q<0\}
and
\{Q>0\}
are both open subsets of
M
, we only need to show that in every connected component of the set
\{Q\neq 0\}
,
Q
is a constant. Let us assume that
U
is a connected open subset of
\{Q<0\}
. In this case, we know that
\varphi
defines an almost complex structure
J
compatible with
\omega
and that
F(\varphi)=|\varphi|^{2}\hat{\varphi}
on
U
. Since
\mathrm{d}\varphi=0
, in the language of
[
FPPZ21a
]
, we get Type IIA structure
(\omega,\varphi)
on
M
. In
[
FPPZ21a
, pp. 791-792]
we have shown that
\mathrm{d}\hat{\varphi}
is a
(2,2)
-form with respect to
J
. Consequently, we know that

\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)=\mathrm{d}|\varphi|^{2}\wedge\hat{\varphi}+|\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\hat{\varphi}

is exactly the decomposition of
\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)
into its
(3,1)+(1,3)
and
(2,2)
components. Therefore
\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)=0
implies that both
\mathrm{d}|\varphi|^{2}=0
and
\mathrm{d}\hat{\varphi}=0
, which further implies that
Q=-|\varphi|^{4}
is a constant and
J
is integrable.

The situation that
U
is contained in
\{Q>0\}
is parallel, see the analogous construction outlined in
[
Fei15
]
.
∎

To summarize, we have proved that

Theorem 3.7
.

If
\varphi
is integrable and
F
-integrable, then it is also
K
-integrable and
Q
-integrable. Moreover,
\varphi
is either pointwise stable or pointwise unstable. It is impossible for
\varphi
to be stable at some points while unstable at other places.

Remark 3.8
.

There are examples of
\varphi
being both integrable and
F
-integrable with
Q(\varphi)=0
everywhere such that
\varphi
belongs to different non-stable orbits at different points.

Remark 3.9
.

Being both integrable and
F
-integrable is a very natural condition to impose. For example, when
\varphi
belongs to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{-}^{+}(\mu)
pointwise, being both integrable and
F
-integrable is equivalent to that the associated metric
q(\omega,\varphi)
is both Kähler and Ricci-flat. If the underlying manifold is a Kähler Calabi-Yau 3-fold, such a
\varphi
can always be found by running the Type IIA flow with the real part of any holomorphic volume form as initial data, see
[
FPPZ21a
, Theorem 9]
. This condition also fits well into what Hitchin calls the “nonlinear Hodge theory”. In fact, on a closed symplectic 6-manifold
(M,\omega)
, one can introduce the a functional
I
on the space of closed 3-forms within a fixed cohomology class:

I(\varphi)=\int_{M}Q(\varphi)\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}.

One can show that the critical points of
I
are exactly those 3-forms that are both integrable and
F
-integrable. Therefore it is naturally to introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.10
.

We say a primitive 3-form on
(M,\omega)
is
F
-harmonic if it is both integrable and
F
-integrable.

4
Lagrangian foliations and degeneration of Calabi-Yau structures

In this section, we examine how certain Lagrangian foliations can be regarded as degeneration of Calabi-Yau structures and discuss its potential interactions with the Stroinger-Yau-Zaslow (SYZ) conjecture
[
SYZ96
]
.

Roughly speaking, the SYZ conjecture predicts that near the large complex structure limit, which is a place where the complex structure of a Calabi-Yau manifold has maximal degeneration, the Calabi-Yau manifold admits a fibration of special Lagrangian tori with singularities. Moreover, the base of the fibration carries a pair of dual Hessian structures (with singularities!) such that the local potentials solve a real Monge-Ampère equation. The SYZ conjecture has served as a guiding principle to understand the mirror symmetry from a geometric point of view. There has been numerous work devoted to various aspects of the SYZ conjecture. We recommend Yang Li’s survey
[
Li22
]
and references therein for its recent developments.

The usual approach to the SYZ conjecture factors through the semi-flat geometry, which serves as an asymptotic model of the Ricci-flat Kähler metrics near the large complex structure limit. In this section, we shall consider a weaker version of the SYZ picture, namely how a Lagrangian foliation naturally arises from certain degeneration of complex structures on a 6-dimensional Calabi-Yau manifold, where semi-flatness is an automatic consequence of the special structures of ambient geometry.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a fixed compact symplectic 6-manifold with vanishing first Chern class. Let
J_{t}
be a family of integrable complex structures on
M
that are compatible with
\omega
. Moreover, we require that
(M,J_{t})
has holomorphically trivial canonical bundle so we get a family of Calabi-Yau structures. Such a family can be obtained by applying the Moser’s trick to a usual polarized degeneration of Calabi-Yau manifolds. Let
\Omega_{t}=\varphi_{t}+\sqrt{-1}\hat{\varphi}_{t}
be a family of holomorphic volume forms on
(X,J_{t})
. From Section 2, we know that all the information encoded in the degeneration of complex structures can be recovered from the family
\varphi_{t}
, which is a family of real primitive 3-forms on
M
belonging to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{-}^{+}(\mu)
pointwise. In fact, we shall further impose that
\varphi_{t}
is Ricci-flat, meaning that we have
\mathrm{d}\hat{\varphi}_{t}=0
and
|\varphi_{t}|^{2}
is a constant on
M
. This can always be achieved by running the Type IIA flow
[
FPPZ21a
, Theorem 9]
. Consequently
F(\varphi_{t})=|\varphi_{t}|^{2}\hat{\varphi}_{t}
is also
\mathrm{d}
-closed.

Suppose the limit
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi_{t}=\varphi_{\infty}
exists smoothly on
M
, where
\varphi_{\infty}
is a primitive 3-form belonging to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{+}
or
\mathcal{O}_{3}
pointwise on an open dense subset
U
of
M
. Since
\varphi_{t}
is
F
-harmonic and the convergence is smooth, we see that
\varphi_{\infty}
is also
F
-harmonic. In view of Proposition
2.12
and Remark
3.5
, we know that there is canonically a Lagrangian foliation associated to
\varphi_{\infty}
on
U
, which can be viewed as a Lagrangian foliation with singularities on
M
.

Example 4.1
.

Let
T=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the standard 2-torus equipped with the symplectic form
\omega=\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y
. For any
\tau
in the upper half plane, consider the complex structure
J_{\tau}
on
T
defined by the holomorphic
(1,0)
-form
\mathrm{d}x+\tau\mathrm{d}y
on
T
. It is not hard to see that as a Riemann surface,
(T,J_{\tau})
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau)
, the elliptic curve with period
\tau
. For this reason we shall write
E_{\tau}
to denote the Calabi-Yau 1-fold
(T,\omega,J_{\tau})
.

Now let
M_{\tau}=E_{\tau}\times E_{\tau}\times E_{\tau}
with product complex and symplectic structures. It is easy to see that
M_{\tau}
is a Calabi-Yau 3-fold with Käher Ricci-flat metric. Moreover, it is well-known that the family
M_{\tau}
with
\tau\to\sqrt{-1}\infty
is a model of large complex structure limit. For example, it is indicated in
[
Leu05
]
that under the limit
\tau\to\sqrt{-1}\infty
, the geometry of
M_{\tau}
collapses to a 3-dimensional base.

Now let us consider the following holomorphic volume form
\Omega_{\tau}
on
M_{\tau}
:

\Omega_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}(\mathrm{d}x^{1}+\tau\mathrm{d}y^{1})\wedge(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\tau\mathrm{d}y^{2})\wedge(\mathrm{d}x^{3}+\tau\mathrm{d}y^{3}),

with
\varphi_{\tau}=\textrm{Re}~\Omega_{\tau}
. In this set-up we have

\lim_{\tau\to\sqrt{-1}\infty}\varphi_{\tau}=\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{3}+\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{1}+\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{2}=:\varphi_{\infty}

is a member of
\mathcal{O}_{3}^{+}
. Associated to
\varphi_{\infty}
there is a natural Lagrangian foliation

L=\ker K_{\varphi_{\infty}}=\mathrm{Span}\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{2}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{3}}\right\}.

In fact, in this example
L
is a Lagrangian torus fibration over a 3-torus base.

The case that
\varphi_{\infty}
belonging to the
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{+}
is the more desired situation. Because in this case, the other sequence
F(\varphi_{t})
satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}F(\varphi_{t})=F(\varphi_{\infty}),

where
F(\varphi_{\infty})
belongs to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{3}
.

For this reason, to understand the degeneration of Calabi-Yau structures, we shall focus on the geometry associated to the triple
(M,\omega,\varphi)
, where
(M,\omega)
is a symplectic 6-manifold, and
\varphi
is a primitive 3-form on
M
belonging to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{+}
pointwise that is also
F
-harmonic.

Summarizing results from Proposition
2.12
and Theorem
3.7
, we have the following basic description of some fundamental geometric structures associated to
(M,\omega,\varphi)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic 6-manifold and
\varphi
an
F
-harmonic primitive 3-form on
M
belonging to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{+}
pointwise. Then
K=K(\varphi)
is a smooth section of
\mathrm{End}~TM
such that
K^{2}=0
and
\mathcal{L}:=\ker K=\mathrm{Im}~K
defines a Lagrange foliation on
(M,\omega)
. In addition, the Nijenhuis tensor
N_{K}
of
K
vanishes, namely

\displaystyle N_{K}(X,Y)=K[KX,Y]+K[X,KY]-[KX,KY]=0

(19)

for any vector fields
X
and
Y
. The natural bilinear symmetric form
g(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(\cdot,K\cdot)
induces a Riemannian metric on the vector bundle
TM/\mathcal{L}
. The bundle
TM/\mathcal{L}
is canonically oriented so the volume form of the above Riemannian metric is exactly given by
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}F(\varphi)
. Through the bundle isomorphism
K:TM/\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{L}
, we also get a natural Riemannian metric
g_{\mathcal{L}}
with orientation on the Lagrangian foliation
\mathcal{L}
that

g_{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)=\omega(K^{-1}X,Y)

for any vector fields tangent to
\mathcal{L}
. Clearly the definition of
g_{\mathcal{L}}
is independent of the choice of preimage
K^{-1}X
of
X
since
\mathcal{L}
is Lagrangian.

It turns out that there are very rich geometric structures on the leaves of the Lagrangian foliation
\mathcal{L}
. In particular we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
L
be a leaf, namely a maximal integral (immersed) submanifold, of the Lagrangian foliation
\mathcal{L}
. Then
L
is naturally oriented. Moreover,
L
carries a canonical affine structure such that the natural Riemannian metric induced from
g_{\mathcal{L}}
is a Hessian metric with parallel volume form. In other words, in any local affine coordinate system on
L
, the metric is the Hessian of a locally defined convex function
h
such that it solves a real Monge-Ampère equation with constant right hand side.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}=\ker F(\varphi)
, we know that
F(\varphi)
naturally defines an orientation on the vector bundle
TM/\mathcal{L}
. Therefore we also get a natural orientation on
\mathcal{L}
, hence every leaf of
\mathcal{L}
is naturally oriented as well.

Now let us introduce a partial connection
D
on
\mathcal{L}
as follows: for any vector fields
X
and
Y
that are tangent to
\mathcal{L}
everywhere, we define

\displaystyle D_{X}Y=K[X,K^{-1}Y].

(20)

Suppose we have two choices
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
of
K^{-1}Y
, since
K(Z_{1})=K(Z_{2})=Y
, we know that
Z_{1}-Z_{2}
is a section of
\ker K=\mathcal{L}
. As
\mathcal{L}
is integrable and
X
is tangent to
\mathcal{L}
, so
[X,Z_{1}-Z_{2}]
is also tangent to
\mathcal{L}
hence
K[X,Z_{1}-Z_{2}]=0
. Therefore
D
is well defined as
D_{X}Y
does not depend on the choice of
K^{-1}Y
. One can check that
D
satisfies the usual properties of a connection. For any function
f
, we have

D_{fX}Y=K[fX,K^{-1}Y]=K(f[X,K^{-1}Y])-(K^{-1}Yf)KX=fD_{X}Y,

and

\begin{split}D_{X}(fY)&=K[X,K^{-1}(fY)]=K[X,fK^{-1}Y]=K(f[X,K^{-1}Y])+Xf\cdot K(K^{-1}Y)\\
&=fD_{X}Y+Xf\cdot Y.\end{split}

Moreover, by vanishing of the Nijenhuis tensor (
19
), we have

\begin{split}D_{X}Y-D_{Y}X=&K([K(K^{-1}X),K^{-1}Y]+[K^{-1}X,K(K^{-1}Y)])=[K(K^{-1}X),K(K^{-1}Y)]\\
=&[X,Y].\end{split}

The Jacobi identity further suggests that

\begin{split}&D_{X}(D_{Y}Z)-D_{Y}(D_{X}Z)-D_{[X,Y]}Z\\
=&K[X,[Y,K^{-1}Z]]-K[Y,[X,K^{-1}Z]]-K[[X,Y],K^{-1}Z]\\
=&0.\end{split}

Consequently the restriction of
D
to each leaf
L
is a torsion-free and flat connection on
TL
, hence we get a canonical affine structure on every leaf.

Let
g_{L}
be the induced Riemannian metric from
g_{\mathcal{L}}
. Then for any tangent vectors
Y
and
Z
of
L
, we have
g_{L}(Y,Z)=\omega(K^{-1}Y,Z)
,
hence

D_{X}g_{L}(Y,Z)=X\omega(K^{-1}Y,Z)-\omega([X,K^{-1}Y],Z)-\omega(K^{-1}Y,D_{X}Z).

On the other hand,
\omega
is a symplectic form, so

\begin{split}0=\mathrm{d}\omega(X,K^{-1}Y,Z)=&X\omega(K^{-1}Y,Z)+K^{-1}Y\omega(Z,X)+Z\omega(X,K^{-1}Y)\\
&-\omega([X,K^{-1}Y],Z)-\omega([K^{-1}Y,Z],X)-\omega([Z,X],K^{-1}Y),\end{split}

which can be translated to

D_{X}g_{L}(Y,Z)=D_{Z}g_{L}(Y,X),

hence
Dg_{L}
is a totally symmetric tensor. In a local affine coordinate system
\{x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
, we can write
g_{L}=h_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{j}
. It follows that

\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}h_{ij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}h_{ik}

for any indices
i,j
and
k
. Therefore locally we can find a potential function
h
such that
h_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial^{2}h}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}}
hence
g_{L}
is a Hessian metric. Let
\mathrm{vol}_{g_{L}}
be the volume form of the metric
g_{L}
. By a pointwise computation using the normal form, we have

\mathrm{vol}_{g_{L}}(X,Y,Z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}F(\varphi)(K^{-1}X,K^{-1}Y,K^{-1}Z).

Since
\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)=0
and
\mathcal{L}=\ker F(\varphi)
, we get

\begin{split}&WF(\varphi)(K^{-1}X,K^{-1}Y,K^{-1}Z)-F(\varphi)([W,K^{-1}X],K^{-1}Y,K^{-1}Z)\\
&-F(\varphi)(K^{-1}X,[W,K^{-1}Y],K^{-1}Z)-F(\varphi)(K^{-1}X,K^{-1}Y,[W,K^{-1}Z])=0\end{split}

for any
X,Y,Z,W
tangent to
\mathcal{L}
. This equation translates to

D_{W}\mathrm{vol}_{g_{L}}(X,Y,Z)=0.

Therefore we know that
\mathrm{vol}_{g_{L}}
is parallel under
D
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It is not hard to write down a locally
D
-parallel frame of
TL
. By a theorem of Weinstein
[
Wei71
]
, near any point
p
of
M
, there exists locally a Darboux coordinate system
\{x^{1},y^{1},x^{2},y^{2},x^{3},y^{3}\}
such that

\omega=\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{1}+\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{2}+\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}y^{3},

and the leaves of
\mathcal{L}
are locally defined by
y=(y^{1},y^{2},y^{3})
are constants. In this local coordinates,
\left\{K\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y^{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{3}
gives rise to a
D
-parallel frame on
TL
.

On each leaf
L
, the pair
(g_{L},D)
forms what is known as a Hessian structure
[
Shi07
, Chap. 2]
. By performing the Legendre transform locally, one can generate a dual Hessian structure
(g_{L},D^{\prime})
on
L
, where
D^{\prime}
is a torsion-free and flat connection on
TL
such that

D+D^{\prime}=2\nabla,

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection associated to
g_{L}
. The same metric
g_{L}
is also Hessian with respect to
D^{\prime}
and the volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{g_{L}}
is also parallel under
D^{\prime}
. On the other hand, for any Lagrangian foliation, there is another canonical affine structure on its leaves characterized by the Bott (partial) connection
\nabla^{B}

[
Bot72
]
. In this setting, see for example
[
HK23
, Chap. 4]
, the Bott (partial) connection satisfies

\displaystyle\omega(\nabla^{B}_{X}Y,Z)=X\omega(Y,Z)+\omega([X,Z],Y)

(21)

for any
X,Y
tangent to
\mathcal{L}
and arbitrary vector field
Z
. It is not surprising to expect the following theorem:

Theorem 4.5
.

The Bott connection
\nabla^{B}
is dual to
D
in the sense of Hessian structures, namely
\nabla^{B}=D^{\prime}
. In particular, with respect to the affine structure determined by
\nabla^{B}
, the metric
g_{L}
on each leaf
L
is Hessian and its local potential functions all solve a real Monge-Ampère equation with constant right hand side.

Proof.

We only need to prove that

Xg_{L}(Y,Z)=g_{L}(D_{X}Y,Z)+g_{L}(Y,\nabla^{B}_{X}Z)

for any vector fields
X,Y,Z
tangent to
\mathcal{L}
. This follows directly from that
g_{L}(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(K^{-1}\cdot,\cdot)
and the definitions of
D
(
20
) and
\nabla^{B}
(
21
).
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

The natural metric
g_{L}
on any leaf
L
has nonnegative Ricci curvature. If the leaf
L
is compact, then it must be a flat torus.

Proof.

In an affine coordinate, the Ricci curvature
R_{jk}
of a Hessian metric
g
is given by

\displaystyle R_{jk}=\frac{1}{4}h^{st}h^{lp}(h_{jps}h_{klt}-h_{jkt}h_{pls}),

(22)

where
h
is the local potential function for the Hessian metric
g
and
h_{ijk}
is the short-handed notation for
\dfrac{\partial^{3}h}{\partial x^{i}\partial x^{j}\partial x^{k}}
, the 3rd derivative of
h
with respect to an affine coordinate system. If in addition that the volume form is parallel under the affine connection, then
\det[h_{pl}]
is a constant, hence

\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{s}}\log\det[h_{pl}]=h^{lp}h_{pls}=0.

Plugging it in (
22
), we get

\displaystyle R_{jk}=\frac{1}{4}h^{st}h^{lp}h_{jps}h_{klt}

(23)

is semi-positive definite. In other words, for any vector field
X
, we have

\displaystyle\textrm{Ric}(X,X)=\frac{1}{4}|D_{X}g|^{2}.

(24)

It follows that the scalar curvature
S
has the expression

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{4}h^{st}h^{ik}h^{jl}h_{sil}h_{tkj}=\frac{1}{4}|Dg|^{2}\geq 0.

(25)

As in Theorem (
4.3
) we have proved that for any leaf
L
, it has a Hessian structure with parallel volume form, so we know its Ricci curvature is nonegative from (
24
).

Now suppose that
L
is a compact leaf. It is well-known that
L
must be a torus. By a famous theorem of Schoen-Yau
[
SY79
]
and Gromov-Lawson
[
GLJ80
]
, we know that any Riemannian metric on a torus with nonnegative scalar curvature must be flat.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

In general, the two affine structures determined by
D
and
\nabla^{B}
are different. This follows from the fact that the metric
g_{L}
on each leaf is in general not flat. See the example below.

Example 4.8
.

Let
(N,g)
be an oriented 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Over
N
, we consider its cotangent bundle
T^{*}N
, namely the bundle of 1-forms, and also the bundle of 2-forms
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
. It is well-known that
T^{*}N
carries a tautological 1-form, whose exterior derivative gives rise to the canonical symplectic structure
\omega
on
T^{*}N
. Let
\{x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
be a local coordinate chart on
N
, and let
\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}
be the corresponding coordinate in the cotangent direction, we know that
\omega
takes the Darboux form

\omega=\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{1}+\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{2}+\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{3}.

Similarly, the total space of
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
carries a tautological 2-form
\alpha
defined as follows: Let
(p,\lambda)
be a point in
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
where
p\in N
and
\lambda\in\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}_{p}N
. For any
X,Y\in T_{(p,\lambda)}\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
, we define that

\alpha_{(p,\lambda)}(X,Y)=\lambda(\pi_{*}X,\pi_{*}Y),

where
\pi:\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N\to N
is the projection map. Over a local coordinate chart
\{x^{1},x^{2},x^{3}\}
of
N
, any 2-form on
N
can be written as
t^{1}\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}+t^{2}\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{1}+t^{3}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}
. If we use
\{x^{1},t^{1},x^{2},t^{2},x^{3},t^{3}\}
as a local coordinate chart on
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
, we can express
\alpha
as

\alpha=t^{1}\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}+t^{2}\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{1}+t^{3}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}.

By taking the exterior derivative, we get the canonical 3-form
\mathrm{d}\alpha
on
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
:

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\alpha&=\mathrm{d}(t^{1}\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}+t^{2}\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{1}+t^{3}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2})\\
&=\mathrm{d}t^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}+\mathrm{d}t^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{1}+\mathrm{d}t^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}.\end{split}

The Riemannian metric
g
on
N
provides us an isomorphism
T^{*}N\cong\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
via the Hodge star operator. In the local coordinates above, we have

t^{j}=y_{k}g^{kj}\sqrt{\det g},

or

y_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}g_{kj}t^{j}.

Under this identification, we can think of
\omega
as a symplectic form on
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
with the expression

\omega=\frac{g_{kj}}{\sqrt{\det g}}\mathrm{d}x^{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}t^{j}+t^{j}\mathrm{d}x^{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{g_{kj}}{\sqrt{\det g}}\right).

It is not hard to find out that

\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}t^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}t^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}t^{3}.

Let
r
be the norm square function on
\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
, namely
r(p,\lambda)=|\lambda|^{2}_{g_{p}}
. In local coordinates we have that

r(x,t)=\frac{t^{j}t^{k}}{\det g}g_{jk}.

A straightforward calculation shows that

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\omega=\frac{\sqrt{\det g}}{2}\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}\wedge\mathrm{d}r.

(26)

It follows that
\mathrm{d}\alpha\wedge\omega=\mathrm{d}(\alpha\wedge\omega)=0
, hence
\mathrm{d}\alpha
is primitive.

Let
f
be a smooth single variable function of
r
, and we define

\varphi_{f}=\mathrm{d}(f\alpha)=f\mathrm{d}\alpha+f^{\prime}\mathrm{d}r\wedge\alpha.

It follows from (
26
) that
\varphi_{f}
is a primitive 3-form for any such
f
. Moreover, direct calculation reveals that

\begin{split}&K(\varphi_{f})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{j}}\right)=0,\\
&K(\varphi_{f})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=2f\sqrt{\det g}\left[(f+2rf^{\prime})\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{j}}-f^{\prime}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{j}}\mathcal{E}\right],\end{split}

where
\mathcal{E}=t^{j}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t^{j}}
is the local Euler vector field. It follows that

F(\varphi_{f})=-4f^{2}(f+2rf^{\prime})\sqrt{\det g}\cdot\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}.

We are mainly interested in the case that

\displaystyle f(r)=r^{-\frac{1}{2}}(r^{\frac{3}{2}}+C)^{\frac{1}{3}}

(27)

for some constant
C
, so that

f^{2}(f+2rf^{\prime})=1.

With the above choice (
27
) of
f
, we consider
M=\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N
when
C=0
or
M=\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N\setminus N
when
C>0
and
M=\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N\setminus\{r\leq(-C)^{2/3}\}
when
C<0
. It follows that
(M,\omega,\varphi_{f})
is a triple such that
(M,\omega)
is a symplectic 6-manifold and
\varphi_{f}
is a primitive 3-form on
M
belonging to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{0}^{+}
pointwise. In addition,
\varphi_{f}
is
F
-harmonic.

It is obvious that the Lagrangian foliation
\mathcal{L}
in this case is simply given by the vertical foliation of the fibration
\pi:\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N\to N
. On one hand, the affine structure associated to the Bott connection
\nabla^{B}
is the standard affine structure on the fibers of the vector bundle
\pi:\bigwedge^{2}T^{*}N\to N
. To understand the affine structure associated to
D
, we let

V_{j}=K(\varphi_{f})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}\right)=2f\sqrt{\det g}\left[(f+2rf^{\prime})\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{j}}-f^{\prime}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{j}}\mathcal{E}\right],

for
j=1,2,3
. By Remark
4.4
we know that the restriction of
V_{j}
to any leaf is parallel under
D
, hence gives rise to a local affine coordinate on the Lagrangian leaf. In this local affine coordinate, the induced metric
g_{\mathcal{L}}
takes the Hessian form

h_{jk}=2f^{-1}g_{jk}-ff^{\prime}\det g\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{j}}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{k}}.

By direct computation we have

\det[h_{jk}]=8\det g

is constant in
t
, as predicted. In addition, we have

h^{jk}=\frac{f}{2}\left[g^{jk}+\frac{f^{\prime}\det g}{2f}g^{jp}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{p}}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{q}}g^{qk}\right],

and

h_{jkl}=-4f^{\prime}\sqrt{\det g}\left(g_{jk}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{l}}+g_{kl}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{j}}+g_{lj}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{k}}\right)-\frac{C(5r^{\frac{3}{2}}+2C)}{2r^{4}(r^{\frac{3}{2}}+C)^{\frac{2}{3}}}(\det g)^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{j}}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{k}}\frac{\partial r}{\partial t^{l}}.

To demonstrate that
D
and
\nabla^{B}
induces different affine structures on the leaves, we only need to show that the induced metric
g_{L}
on the leaves are not flat, as
D
and
\nabla^{B}
define dual Hessian structures on every leaf. This can be done by computing the scalar curvature of every leaf
L
. By (
25
) we know that

S=\frac{1}{4}|Dg|^{2}>0

because
h_{jkl}\neq 0
.

Alternatively, we notice that in this example the induced metric on the leaves are rotationally symmetric. If we let
r=\rho^{2}
, then this metric can be written as

g_{L}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{2(\rho^{3}+C)^{2/3}}(\mathrm{d}\rho)^{2}+\frac{\rho(\rho^{3}+C)^{1/3}}{2}g_{S^{2}}.

It follows that the scalar curvature of
g_{L}
is

S=\frac{5C^{2}}{\rho^{4}(\rho^{3}+C)^{4/3}}.

Remark 4.9
.

Much of the discussion in this section can be placed in a more general setting such as in higher dimensions or with weaker assumption on integrability conditions. We shall not pursue this kind of generality in this paper. The general theory will be developed elsewhere.

5
Detecting geometrical structures on symplectic 6-manifolds

In a series of papers
[
FPPZ21a
]
,
[
FPPZ23
]
,
[
FPPZ21b
]
, we introduce and investigate the Type IIA flow as an intrinsically defined geometric flow on symplectic 6-manifolds. In the language of Section 2, the Type IIA flow on a fixed symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is the following evolution equation of 3-forms

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi),

(28)

where
\Lambda_{\omega}
is the Lefschetz operator of contraction with respect to the symplectic form
\omega
. Usually we require the initial data
\varphi\big|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}
to be a
\mathrm{d}
-closed 3-form that belongs to the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{-}^{+}(\mu)
pointwise so we have the short-time existence and uniqueness. However, in principle Equation (
28
) makes sense for any initial data though additional effort is in need for its existence.

In this section, We also use the Type IIA flow as a tool to detect geometric structures on certain symplectic 6-manifolds, notably those with large symmetry, from two perspectives which are complementary to each other.

On one hand, we would like to find integrable and closed stationary points of the Type IIA flow, namely primitive 3-forms
\varphi
satisfying

\begin{cases}&\mathrm{d}\varphi=0,\\
&\mathrm{d}\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{d}F(\varphi)=0.\end{cases}

In particular, these stationary points include those
\varphi
that are
F
-harmonic. Presumably, the set of all
F
-harmonic 3-forms encode rich geometric and topological information about the underlying symplectic manifold. Moreover, the structure of this set also tells us what kind of geometric structures can be supported on this manifold. For example, by the result in
[
FPPZ21b
]
, we know such
\varphi
cannot pointwise lie in the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{-}^{+}(\mu)
if the underlying manifold does not support any Kähler structure.

On the other hand, we would like to study the limits of the Type IIA flow. Suppose we have a Type IIA flow
\varphi(t)
with initial data from
\mathcal{O}_{-}^{+}(\mu)
on a maximal time interval
[0,T)
. In general, we do not expect its convergence when
t\to T
as the underlying manifold
(M,\omega)
may be non-Kähler. However, it is possible that after a choice of suitable normalizations, the sequence
C(t)\varphi(t)
has a smooth limit
\varphi_{\infty}
when
t\to T
, where
C(t)
is a constant depending on
t
. In this case, natural geometric structures associated to
\varphi_{\infty}
would be of great interest, as they are special in the sense that they can be detected by the intrinsically defined Type IIA flow.

In this section, we shall test these ideas on certain 6-dimensional Lie groups with left-invariant symplectic structures and their quotients by cocompact discrete subgroups. In one aspect, these manifolds are rich enough to demonstrate various phenomena in Type IIA flow
[
FPPZ21a
, Section 9.3.2]
. On the other hand, their large group of local symmetries allows us to reduce the Type IIA flow to an ODE system that sometimes can be solved explicitly. Moreover, as we have the existence theorems about solutions to ODE, we could even include degenerate and non-closed 3-forms as out initial data.

For simplicity, we shall consider only those 6-dimensional symplectic Lie groups with cocompact discrete subgroups. It is well-known (see for example
[
Mil76
]
) that any Lie group admitting a cocompact discrete subgroup must be unimodular. By a theorem of Chu
[
Chu74
]
, any unimodular symplectic Lie group must be solvable. Though the classification is not fully complete, the structure of 6-dimensional symplectic solvable Lie groups is well-understood from the works
[
GB87
,
GK96
,
Sal01
,
KGM04
,
CS09
,
BC16
]
. In particular, it contains many more examples compared to 6-dimensional Lie groups with left-invariant symplectic half-flat structures
[
CT07
,
FMOU13
]
. In this section, we shall not attempt to exhaust all the possible cases. Instead, we present a more refined analysis of the two examples given in
[
FPPZ21a
, Section 9.3.2]
. Further examples will be left for future studies.

It is convenient to have the following lemma for computational purpose.

Lemma 5.1
.

Choose a basis
\{e^{1},e^{2},\dots,e^{6}\}
of
V^{*}
such that the symplectic form
\omega
takes the standard form

\omega=e^{12}+e^{34}+e^{56}.

A general primitive 3-form
\varphi
on
V
can be written as

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ae^{135}+Be^{136}+Ce^{145}+De^{146}+Ee^{235}+Fe^{236}+Ge^{245}+He^{246}

where

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}F(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{A}e^{135}+\hat{B}e^{136}+\hat{C}e^{145}+\hat{D}e^{146}+\hat{E}e^{235}+\hat{F}e^{236}+\hat{G}e^{245}+\hat{H}e^{246}

(30)

\displaystyle+(\hat{I}e^{1}+\hat{J}e^{2})(e^{34}-e^{56})+(\hat{K}e^{3}+\hat{L}e^{4})(e^{12}-e^{56})+(\hat{M}e^{5}+\hat{N}e^{6})(e^{12}-e^{34}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(AH-BG-CF-DE+2IJ+2KL+2MN)

\displaystyle-2(BM^{2}+CK^{2}+EI^{2}-BCE+2IKM),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(AH+BG-CF+DE+2IJ-2KL+2MN)

\displaystyle-2(-AL^{2}+DM^{2}+GI^{2}+ADG+2ILM),

\displaystyle\hat{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(-AH-BG-CF+DE+2IJ-2KL-2MN)

\displaystyle-2(-BL^{2}-CN^{2}+HI^{2}-BCH+2ILN),

and

\displaystyle\hat{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(AH+BG+CF-DE-2IJ+2KL+2MN)

\displaystyle-2(-AJ^{2}+FM^{2}+GK^{2}+AFG+2JKM),

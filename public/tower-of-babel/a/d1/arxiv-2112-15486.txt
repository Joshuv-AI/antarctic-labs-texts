Efficient and Reliable Overlay Networks for Decentralized Federated Learning Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Yifan Hua, Kevin Miller, Andrea L. Bertozzi, Chen Qian, Bao Wang

Abstract
We propose near-optimal overlay networks based on d d -regular expander graphs to accelerate decentralized federated learning (DFL) and improve its generalization. In DFL a massive number of clients are connected by an overlay network, and they solve machine learning problems collaboratively without sharing raw data. Our overlay network design integrates spectral graph theory and the theoretical convergence and generalization bounds for DFL. As such, our proposed overlay networks accelerate convergence, improve generalization, and enhance robustness to clients failures in DFL with theoretical guarantees. Also, we present an efficient algorithm to convert a given graph to a practical overlay network and maintaining the network topology after potential client failures. We numerically verify the advantages of DFL with our proposed networks on various benchmark tasks, ranging from image classification to language modeling using hundreds of clients.

Theory of DFedAvg

An important notion in DFL
is the
mixing matrix
, which is associated with an undirected connected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
, with vertex set
\mathcal{V}=\{1,2,\cdots,N\}:=[N]
and edge set
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
,
and the edge
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
represents a communication channel between clients
i
and
j
.

Definition 1
(Mixing matrix).

A matrix
{{\bm{M}}}=[m_{i,j}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
is a mixing matrix, if it satisfies 1. (Graph) If
i\neq j
and
(i,j)\notin\mathcal{E}
, then
m_{i,j}=0
, otherwise,
m_{i,j}>0
; 2. (symmetry)
{{\bm{M}}}={{\bm{M}}}^{\top}
; 3. (Null space property)
null\{{{\bm{I}}}-{{\bm{M}}}\}=span\{\mathbf{1}\}
where
{{\bm{I}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times N}
and
\mathbf{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
are the identity matrix and the vector whose entries are all
1
s; 4. (Spectral property)
{{\bm{I}}}\succeq{{\bm{M}}}\succ-{{\bm{I}}}
, where
{{\bm{I}}}\succeq{{\bm{M}}}
means
{{\bm{I}}}-{{\bm{M}}}
is positive semi-definite and
{{\bm{M}}}\succ-{{\bm{I}}}
stands for
{{\bm{M}}}+{{\bm{I}}}
is positive definite.

Given the adjacency matrix of a network, its maximum-degree matrix and metropolis-hastings matrix are both mixing matrices
[
4
]
. The symmetric property of
{{\bm{M}}}
indicates that its eigenvalues are real and can be sorted in the non-increasing order. Let
\lambda_{i}({{\bm{M}}})
denote the
i^{th}
largest eigenvalue of
{{\bm{M}}}
, then we have
\lambda_{1}({{\bm{M}}})=1>\lambda_{2}({{\bm{M}}})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}({{\bm{M}}})>-1
based on the spectral property of the mixing matrix.
The mixing matrix also serves as a probability transition matrix of a Markov chain.
An important constant
is
\lambda=\lambda({{\bm{M}}}):=\max\{|\lambda_{2}({{\bm{M}}})|,|\lambda_{N}({{\bm{M}}})|\}
, which describes the speed of the Markov chain, induced by the mixing matrix
{{\bm{M}}}
, converges to its stable state.

We consider DFL using the following update on client
i

{\small{\bm{w}}^{t,k+1}_{i}={\bm{w}}^{t,k}_{i}-\eta_{t}\nabla f_{i}({\bm{w}}^{t,k}_{i};\xi^{t,k}_{i})+\beta({\bm{w}}^{t,k}_{i}-{\bm{w}}^{t,k-1}_{i}),}

(2)

where
t
is the communication round,
k
is the local iteration, and
\xi^{t,k}_{i}=({\bm{x}}^{t,k}_{i},y^{t,k}_{i})\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}
.
After the
K^{th}
local iteration, communication happens according to the graph topology of the mixing matrix,
{{\bm{M}}}
; that is, we have for each
i\in[N]
:

{\bm{w}}^{t+1,0}_{i}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}m_{i,\ell}{\bm{w}}^{t,K}_{\ell}.

To ensure well-defined iterations, we set
{\bm{w}}^{t,-1}_{i}={\bm{w}}^{t,0}_{i}
for each
i
. These iterations are referred to as DFedAvgM (Decentralized Federated Averaging with Momentum)
[
41
]
.

To guarantee convergence of generalization of DFedAvgM, we collect below the necessary assumptions on the local functions
f_{i}
and global function
f
:

Assumption 1
(L-smooth).

f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
are all L-smooth, i.e.
f_{i}({\bm{w}})\leq f_{i}({\bm{v}})+\langle\nabla f_{i}({\bm{v}}),{\bm{w}}-{\bm{v}}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|{\bm{w}}-{\bm{v}}\|_{2}^{2}
for all
{\bm{w}},{\bm{v}}
.

Assumption 2
(Bounded Local Gradient Variance (BLGV)).

Let
\xi^{t}_{i}:=({\bm{x}}^{t,k}_{i},y^{t,k}_{i})
be sampled from the
i^{th}
device’s local data
\mathcal{D}_{i}
uniformly at random. Then for all
i\in[N]
:
\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f_{i}({\bm{w}}^{t,k}_{i};\xi^{t,k}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}({\bm{w}}^{t,k}_{i})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
, i.e. the stochastic gradients have bounded variance.

Assumption 3
(Bounded Global Gradient Variance (BGGV)).

The global variance is bounded, i.e.
\|\nabla f_{i}({\bm{w}})-\nabla f({\bm{w}})\|^{2}\leq\zeta^{2}
.

Assumption 4
(Bounded Local Gradient Norm (BLGN)).

At each node
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, the norm of the gradients is uniformly bounded, i.e.
\max_{{\bm{w}}}\|\nabla f_{i}({\bm{w}})\|\leq B
.

While convergence guarantees for FL and DFL have been studied extensively
[
45
,
39
,
14
,
41
]
, we provide stability analysis for DFedAvgM to give generalization guarantees under Assumptions
1
-
4
. Along with related convergence guarantees, our work here elucidates the importance of beneficial graph topology design.

2.1
Convergence of DFedAvgM

We state convergence results for DFedAvgM and highlight the effect of graph topology on convergence rates in DFL. This result analyzes the convergence of the sequence

\min_{1\leq t\leq T}\ \mathbb{E}\|\nabla f(\bar{{\bm{w}}}^{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\bar{{\bm{w}}}^{1}-2\min f}{\gamma(K,\eta)T}+\alpha(K,\eta)+\frac{\Xi(K,\eta)}{(1-\lambda)^{2}},

(3)

where

In this result we clearly see the convergence of the auxiliary sequence depends on the value of

2.2
Generalization of DFedAvgM

In this section, we will establish a generalization bound of DFedAvgM.
Given an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
that acts on data
\mathcal{D}
with output
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D})
, the generalization error is given by
{\epsilon}_{gen}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}}[F(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D}))-F_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D}))]
, where
F({\bm{x}})=\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}}f({\bm{x}};\xi)
is the
“true” risk
and
F_{\mathcal{D}}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f({\bm{x}};\xi)/N
is the
empirical risk
of the machine learning model for input
{\bm{x}}
with loss function
f
. Uniform stability is a useful property used to bound the generalization error
{\epsilon}_{gen}
, see e.g.
[
12
,
7
]
.

Definition 3
.

A randomized algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is
{\epsilon}
-uniformly stable if for any two data sets
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
with
N
samples each that differ in one example we have

\sup_{\xi}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{A}}[f(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D});\xi)-f(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime});\xi)]\leq{\epsilon}.

With this definition in hand, it has been proven that uniform stability implies bounded generalization error:

Lemma 4
(
[
12
]
).

Let
\mathcal{A}
be
{\epsilon}
-uniformly stable, then it follows that

|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}}[F(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D}))-F_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{D}))]|\leq{\epsilon}.

Therefore, to ensure the generalization bound of a given random algorithm
\mathcal{A}
, we simply compute the uniform stability bound
{\epsilon}
. To establish this result, we additionally require Assumption
4
,
i.e. boundedness of the local gradients.

The following theorem summarizes our result of uniform stability for DFedAvgM given the assumptions stated previously; the proof can be found in the appendix.

Theorem 5
(Uniform stability).

Under Assumptions
1
-
4
, we have that for any
T
if the step size
\eta_{t}\leq\frac{c}{t}
and
c
is small enough, then DFedAvgM satisfies uniform stability with

\displaystyle\epsilon\leq T^{\frac{cLK}{1+cLK}}\left(\frac{(\sup f)K(cLK)^{\frac{1}{1+cLK}}}{n}+\frac{\frac{2\sigma B}{NL}}{(cLK)^{\frac{cLK}{1+cLK}}}\right)+\frac{B(\sigma+B)\left(cK+2C_{\lambda}\right)}{cLK},

(4)

where
\sup f<\infty
is the uniform bound on the size of the non-negative global loss function
f
,
n
is the local data set size, and

C_{\lambda}:=2\lambda^{2}+4\lambda^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+2\lambda+\frac{2}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}

is a constant depending on the graph topology.

Per Lemma
4
, we have that the generalization error for DFedAvgM is bounded by the same constant that bounds the uniform stability,
\epsilon
. Again, we note here the
explicit dependence
of the generalization error on the corresponding value of
\lambda
for the mixing matrix
{\bm{M}}
of the graph topology.
C_{\lambda}
is an increasing function of
\lambda\in(0,1)
which implies that the bound in (
4
) improves with smaller
\lambda
.

3
Network Topology Design

The results of Section
2
show that the network topology has a profound impact on both optimization and generalization of DFedAvgM. According to Theorems
2
and
5
, the closer
\lambda
is to
1
the slower DFedAvgM converges (Theorem
2
) and the worse it generalizes (Theorem
5
). To improve DFedAvgM, we propose a theoretically efficient and practical
sparse network topology
whose
\lambda
is far away from
1
.

For the sake of notation, we recall graph definitions and properties to introduce network construction. Given an undirect, connected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
we define the
graph Laplacian

{\bm{L}}={\bm{D}}-{\bm{A}}
,
where
{\bm{A}}=[a_{i,j}]
(with
a_{i,j}=1
if
(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
) is the adjacency matrix and
{\bm{D}}_{i,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{i,j}
is the diagonal degree matrix of
\mathcal{G}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is undirected, we have that both
{\bm{A}}
and
{\bm{L}}
are symmetric. Note that
{\bm{L}}
is positive semidefinite, with a trivial eigenvalue of
0
occurring with multiplicity reflecting the number of connected components in
\mathcal{G}
. As we assume that
\mathcal{G}
is connected, this means that only the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}({\bm{L}})=0
, and we can order the rest of the eigenvalues as
0=\lambda_{1}({\bm{L}})<\lambda_{2}({\bm{L}})\leq\lambda_{3}({\bm{L}})\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{N}({\bm{L}})
. Define the
reduced condition number
of
{\bm{L}}
as

\kappa({\bm{L}}):=\frac{\lambda_{N}({\bm{L}})}{\lambda_{2}({\bm{L}})},

(5)

which is a measure of graph connectivity because a
smaller

\kappa({\bm{L}})
corresponds to a graph with
higher
connectivity. This is an important constant that allows us to quantify how useful a given graph topology is for the purposes of improving convergence and generalization of DFedAvgM.

We apply the mixing matrix used in
[
6
]

{{\bm{M}}}={\bm{I}}-\frac{2}{(1+\theta)\lambda_{N}({\bm{L}})}{\bm{L}},\quad\theta\in[0,1),

which allows us to quantify the associated value of
\lambda
.
The eigenvalues of this mixing matrix
{{\bm{M}}}
have a straightforward relationship with eigenvalues of
{\bm{L}}
:

\lambda_{i}({{\bm{M}}})=1-\frac{2}{(1+\theta)\lambda_{m}({\bm{L}})}\lambda_{i}({\bm{L}}).

Then it is clear that

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\max\{|\lambda_{2}({{\bm{M}}})|,|\lambda_{m}({{\bm{M}}})|\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left|1-\frac{2}{(1+\theta)\lambda_{m}({\bm{L}})}\lambda_{2}({\bm{L}})\right|,\left|1-\frac{2}{(1+\theta)\lambda_{m}({\bm{L}})}\lambda_{m}({\bm{L}})\right|\right\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\frac{\left|1+\theta-\frac{2}{\kappa({\bm{L}})}\right|}{1+\theta},\frac{1-\theta}{1+\theta}\right\}.

:

\lambda

for the expander graph as a function of
\theta

\kappa({\bm{L}})
.
Lower

\kappa
leads to a
higher
value of
\theta^{\ast}(\kappa)
, which in turn leads to a
lower
value of
\lambda
, which is desired.

For a fixed
\kappa({\bm{L}})
, we can view
\lambda
as a function of
\theta
that can be optimized to lead to the lowest value of
\lambda
. In Fig.
2
, we have plotted the function
|\lambda_{m}({\bm{M}})|=(1-\theta)/(1+\theta)
along with
|\lambda_{2}({\bm{M}})|=|1+\theta-2/\kappa({\bm{L}})|/(1+\theta)
for two values of
\kappa({\bm{L}})
. For each fixed
\kappa({\bm{L}})
, the corresponding lowest value of
\lambda(\theta)
occurs when
|\lambda_{2}({\bm{M}})|=|\lambda_{m}({\bm{M}})|
, shown in Fig.
2
as stars; that is, when
\theta=\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))
. It is clear that

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}({\bm{M}})|

\displaystyle=|\lambda_{m}({\bm{M}})|

\displaystyle\iff\frac{1+\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))-\frac{2}{\kappa({\bm{L}})}}{1+\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))}{1+\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))}

\displaystyle\iff\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))-\frac{2}{\kappa({\bm{L}})}

\displaystyle=-\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))

\displaystyle\iff\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa({\bm{L}})},

as long as
2/\kappa({\bm{L}})-\theta^{\ast}(\kappa({\bm{L}}))\leq 1
, which is reasonable since most
\kappa({\bm{L}})\geq 2
.

It is straightforward then that choosing a graph structure with a smaller value of
\kappa({\bm{L}})
gives
\mathcal{G}
better connectivity properties. Somewhat in competition with this connectivity is the communication cost of a given graph topology; that is, better connectivity of a graph structure generally corresponds to more edges in the graph which increases the communication cost. Each node sends its updated model to each of its neighbors, and so an increased number of edges results in more communication that must happen between nodes.

We propose using
d
-regular expander graphs to balance this connectivity communication tradeoff
. A
d
-regular graph has a fixed number degree
d
for each node; i.e.
d(i)=d
for all
i
. Expander graphs are sparse graphs that have strong connectivity properties, of which
d
-regular expander graphs (and the special case of
Ramanujan graphs
) are in a sense “optimal” graph connectivity structures (captured in the constant
\kappa(L)
) with fixed communication cost. While Ramanujan graphs are not known for every value of total nodes
N
and degree
d
, with high probability most
d
-regular graphs are approximately Ramanujan for large enough
N

[
6
]
.

For
d
-regular graphs, there exists a convenient upper bound for
\kappa({\bm{L}})
. This bound involves the first non-trivial eigenvalue,
\lambda_{1}({\bm{A}})
, of the corresponding adjacency matrix

\kappa({\bm{L}})\leq\frac{d+\lambda_{1}({\bm{A}})}{d-\lambda_{1}({\bm{A}})}.

If the
d
-regular graph in question is Ramanujan, then we can bound
\kappa({\bm{L}})
as

\kappa^{R}({\bm{L}})\leq\frac{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}{d-2\sqrt{d-1}}.

(6)

As the right-hand side of (
6
) is a
decreasing
function of
d
, this would suggest to choose larger
d
in order to minimize
\kappa^{R}({\bm{L}})
. However, increasing
d
will incur greater communication costs. One can in practice choose the value of
d
according to a prescribed bound on the total communication cost.

3.1
Comparison to Ring and Erdös-Rényi graphs

We show that other graph topologies are in a sense suboptimal for the purposes of DFedAvgM, highlighting two common examples: Ring and Erdös-Rényi graphs. We emphasize that using
d
-regular Ramanujan graphs are in a sense “optimal”
by possessing
strong connectivity
properties in the graph topology while requiring
low communication cost
for local node neighborhood communication (i.e. sparsity).

Ring graphs – poor connectivity.

Ring graph is an extremely sparse, but still connected,
2
-regular graph structure where the graph structure constitutes a ring (see Fig.
1
(b)). While a very simple and sparse topology to impose on the nodes of the graph, ring graphs possess poor connectivity properties that we can directly compare with
d
-regular Ramanujan graphs.

It is well-known that the eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian
{\bm{L}}_{ring}
of the ring graph on
N
nodes are given by

\{\lambda_{k}({\bm{L}}_{ring})\}_{k=0}^{\frac{N}{2}}=\left\{2-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)\right\}_{k=0}^{\frac{N}{2}},

each with geometric multiplicity
2
, except for the first eigenvalue
\mu_{0}({\bm{L}}_{ring})=0
which has geometric multiplicity 1; if
N
is even, the last eigenvalue
\lambda_{N/2}({\bm{L}}_{ring})
has multiplicity 1 as well. Therefore, we can straightforwardly see that the reduced condition number for a Ring graph on
N
nodes is

\displaystyle\kappa^{ring}({\bm{L}}_{ring})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{N/2}({\bm{L}}_{ring})}{\lambda_{1}({\bm{L}}_{ring})}=\frac{2-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi N}{2N}\right)}{2-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{2-2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)}\geq\frac{4}{2-2\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^{2}\right)}=\frac{4N^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}=\frac{N^{2}}{\pi^{2}}.

Therefore, we see that with this
lower bound
for the Ring graph has a
\kappa({\bm{L}}_{ring})
grows
quadratically
with the size
N
of the graph! The corresponding value for
\lambda
approaches 1 for increasing values of
N
, which implies slower convergence rates (Theorem
2
) and worse generalization bounds (Theorem
5
). It is clear then that

\kappa^{R}({\bm{L}})\leq\frac{d+2\sqrt{d-1}}{d-2\sqrt{d-1}}\ll\frac{N^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\leq\kappa^{ring}({\bm{L}}_{ring}),

which shows superior convergence properties of Ramanujan expander graphs compared to the sparse Ring graph structure.

Erdös-Rényi graph – high communication cost.

Another type of graph topology one could impose for DFedAvgM is an Erdös-Rényi (ER) random graph structure, wherein each edge
(i,j)\in\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
is sampled independently and indentically distributed with probability
p\in(0,1)
. It is well-known that as long as
p=\mathcal{O}(\ln N/N)
, then the resulting graph
\mathcal{G}
is connected with high probability
[
8
]
.

While the connectivity properties of ER graphs are nearly guaranteed to be better than
d
-regular Ramanujan graphs (with
d
is relatively small),
the communication cost of ER graphs is prohibitively large
for large network size
N
. To see this, the expected degree
d_{i}
of a node
i\in\mathcal{V}
in an ER graph with large enough edge probability
p
is simply
\bar{d}=Np=\mathcal{O}(\ln N)
, which grows with the size of the graph
N
. This incurs a much larger communication cost than the constant cost of
d
-regular expander graphs as it is assumed that
d\ll N
, with
d<\ln N
as
N
is large.

In Section
5
,
we empirically verify the superior
connectivity-communication cost
balance exemplified by the
d
-regular expander graph structure compared to Ring and ER graphs for DFedAvgM. These
d
-regular expander graphs have better connectivity properties than Ring graphs while at the same time being sparser (i.e. lower communication costs) than ER graphs.

4.1
Network recovery from node failures

To maintain a correct DFL topology for a dynamic set of nodes, protocols should be designed to recover errors from node failures and leaves. Here an error is defined as a node that has a wrong neighbor set compared to a correct DFL network topology. If a node
x
fails from the network, in each virtual space
i
, its adjacent nodes
y_{i}
and
z_{i}
should remove
x
from their neighbors and add each other as a new neighbor. To recover from such single-node failure, the proposed recovery protocol allows each node to store the IP addresses of the two-hop neighbors. Hence if a node is detected to fail, its two adjacent nodes can directly connect as new neighbors.

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we presented the theoretical advantages of expander graph-based overlay networks and their practical construction. We numerically verified the efficacy in accelerating training, improving generalization, and enhancing robustness to client failures of decentralized federated learning by using expander graph-based overlay networks on various benchmarks. How to establish the theoretical robustness guarantees of the expander graph-based overlay networks to the node failure is an interesting future direction.

\ln(1/\lambda)>0
and so we can simplify the integrals as

\int_{1}^{t/2}\frac{\lambda^{-x}}{x}dx\leq\frac{2}{t}\int_{1}^{t/2}\lambda^{-x}dx=\frac{2}{t\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\left(\lambda^{-t/2}-\lambda^{-t}\right)\leq\frac{2\lambda^{-t/2}}{t\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}

and

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t/2}^{t}\frac{\exp(x\ln(\frac{1}{\lambda}))}{x}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{t/2}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda})^{k}x^{k}}{k!}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{t/2}\frac{1}{x}dx+(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda})\int_{1}^{t/2}1dx+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda})^{k}}{k!}\int_{1}^{t/2}x^{k-1}dx

\displaystyle=1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda})^{k}}{(k)k!}\left(\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{k}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda})^{k}}{k!}\left(\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)^{k}-1\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{t/2}}-1+\frac{t}{2}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}-\left[\frac{1}{\lambda}-1+\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{t/2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{t/2}}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}.

Plugging this result into (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{t}\eta_{t-j}\lambda^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{t-1}+\lambda^{t}\left(1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{t/2}}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}+\frac{2\lambda^{-t/2}}{t\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{t-1}}{2}+\lambda^{t}\left(1-\frac{4}{t^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}(t-2)\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)+\lambda^{t/2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{t\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\left[\lambda^{t}\left(t+\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}t^{2}\right)+\lambda^{t/2}\left(t+\frac{2}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right)\right],

(16)

where in the last line we have used that
t-1\geq t/2
for
t\geq 2
.

Seeking a uniform bound over
t=2,3,\ldots
, we bound each of the last two terms of the right hand side of the above equation. It is easy to check that

\displaystyle t\lambda^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{2\lambda^{2},\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\},

\displaystyle t^{2}\lambda^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{4\lambda^{2},\frac{4}{\left(\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)^{2}}\lambda^{\frac{2}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\},

\displaystyle t\lambda^{t/2}

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{2\lambda,\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\},

where we have noted that each of these functions are decreasing functions of
t
. Therefore, our bound becomes:

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{j=1}^{t}\eta_{t-j}\lambda^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}\left[\min\left\{2\lambda^{2},\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\}+\min\left\{4\lambda^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda},\frac{4}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{2}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\}+\min\left\{2\lambda,\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2}{t\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\left[2\lambda^{2}+4\lambda^{2}\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{2}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}+\min\left\{2\lambda,\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{1}{\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}}}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=:\frac{C_{\lambda}}{t}.

We note that all terms of
C_{\lambda}
except for
2/\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}
are
uniformly bounded
on
\lambda\in(0,1)
. It is true that
2/\ln\frac{1}{\lambda}\rightarrow\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow 1^{-}
, but for each
\lambda<1
this bound
C_{\lambda}
is valid.

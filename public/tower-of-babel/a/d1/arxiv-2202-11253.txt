Abstract
. We study the limits of the additive and derivative martingales of one-dimensional branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment. Then we prove the existence of pulsating travelling wave solutions of the corresponding F-KPP equation in the supercritical and critical cases by representing the solutions probabilistically in terms of the limits of the additive and derivative martingales. We also prove that there is no pulsating travelling wave solution in the subcritical case. Our main tools are the spine decomposition and martingale change of measures.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

A classical branching Brownian motion (BBM) in
\mathbb{R}
is constructed as follows. Initially there is a single particle at the origin of the real line. This particle moves as a standard Brownian motion
B=\{B(t),t\geq 0\}
and produces a random number of offspring,

1+L
, after an exponential time
\eta
. We assume that
L
has distribution
\{p_{k},k\geq 0\}
with
m:=\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k}<\infty
and
\eta
is exponentially distributed with parameter
\beta>0
. Starting from their points of creation, each of these children evolves independently.

McKean
[
25
]
established the connection between BBM and
the Fisher-Kolmogorov-Petrovskii-Piskounov (F-KPP)
reaction-diffusion equation

(1.1)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial x^{2}}+\beta({\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{u}})-{\mathbf{u}}),

where
{\mathbf{f}}(s)={\mathbf{E}}(s^{L+1})
and
{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
.
The F-KPP equation has been studied intensively by both analytic techniques (see, for example, Kolmogorov et al.
[
19
]
and Fisher
[
9
]
) and probabilistic methods
(see, for instance, McKean
[
25
]
, Bramson
[
2
,
3
]
, Harris
[
10
]
and Kyprianou
[
20
]
).

Particular attention has been paid to solutions of the form

{\mathbf{u}}(t,x)=\Phi_{c}(x-ct).

Substituting this into (
1.1
) shows that
\Phi_{c}
satisfies

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{c}^{\prime\prime}+c\Phi_{c}^{\prime}+\beta({\mathbf{f}}(\Phi_{c})-\Phi_{c})=0,

and such a solution
\Phi_{c}
is known as a travelling wave solution of speed
c
.
Kyprianou
[
20
]
, using
the additive and derivative martingales of BBM,
gave a probabilistic representation of travelling wave solutions and also gave probabilistic proofs, different from Harris
[
10
]
, for the existence, asymptotics and uniqueness of travelling wave solutions. Inspired by
[
20
]
, we consider similar problems for BBMs in a periodic environment.

BBM in a periodic environment (BBMPE) is constructed in the same way as BBM,
except that the constant branching rate is replaced by
a space-dependent rate function
\mathbf{g}
, where we assume
\mathbf{g}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is strictly positive and 1-periodic.
More precisely, initially there is a single particle
v
at

x\in\mathbb{R}
, performing standard Brownian motion, and
let
b_{v}
and
d_{v}
be the birth time and death time of the particle
v
, respectively, and
X_{v}(s)
be the location of the particle
v
at time
s
, then

(1.3)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(d_{v}-b_{v}>t\;|\;b_{v},\{X_{v}(s):s\geq b_{v}\}\right)=\exp\left\{-\int_{b_{v}}^{b_{v}+t}\mathbf{g}(X_{v}(s))\mathrm{d}s\right\}.

Here, we fictitiously extend
X_{v}(s)
beyond its death time
d_{v}
.
We still assume that
1+L

is the number of offspring produced by one individual and
L
has distribution
\{p_{k}:k\geq 0\}
with
m=\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k}<\infty
.
We always assume that
m>0
.
The F-KPP equation related to BBMPE has the following form:

(1.4)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial x^{2}}+\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{u}).

In this case, travelling wave solutions, that is solutions satisfying (
1.2
), do not exist. However, we can consider so-called pulsating travelling waves, that is, solutions
\mathbf{u}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
to (
1.4
) satisfying

(1.5)

\mathbf{u}(t+\frac{1}{\nu},x)=\mathbf{u}(t,x-1),

as well as the boundary condition

(1.6)

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=0,\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=1,

when
\nu>0
, and

(1.7)

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=1,\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=0,

when
\nu<0
.
\nu
is called the wave speed.
It is known that there is a constant
\nu^{*}>0
such that
when
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
no such solution exists, whereas for each
|\nu|\geq\nu^{*}

there exists a unique, up to time-shift, pulsating travelling wave (see Hamel et al.
[
16
]
).

Recently, Lubetzky, Thornett and Zeitouni
[
22
]
established the connection
between F-KPP equation (
1.4
) with BBMPE in the case of binary branching
and studied the the maximum of BBMPE.
In this paper we first study limits of additive and derivative martingales of BBMPE.
Then we use these limits to give
probabilistic representations of pulsating travelling wave solutions of (
1.4
) with speed

\nu
satisfying
|\nu|\geq\nu^{*}
, where
\nu^{*}
is a constant defined below.
In the rest of this paper,

|\nu|>\nu^{*}
is called the supercritical case, and
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
the critical case.
We also prove that there is no pulsating travelling wave solution of (
1.4
) with speed satisfying
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
(called the subcritical case).
The asymptotic behavior and uniqueness of the pulsating travelling wave solution of (
1.4
) will be studied in
the companion paper
[
27
]
.
Therefore, we extend the results of Kyprianou
[
20
]
for classical BBM to BBMPE.
It turns out that most of the general deas in Kyprianou
[
20
]
still work for BBMPE. However, as we will see, carrying out the actual argument is much more difficult.

Before we state our main results, we first introduce the minimal speed
\nu^{*}
.
As in
[
16
]
, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\gamma(\lambda)
and
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
be the principal eigenvalue and the corresponding positive eigenfunction of the periodic problem: for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.8)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\psi_{xx}(x,\lambda)-\lambda\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\psi(x,\lambda)&=\gamma(\lambda)\psi(x,\lambda),\\
\psi(x+1,\lambda)&=\psi(x,\lambda).\end{split}

We normalize
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
such that
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(x,\lambda)dx=1
. Define

(1.9)

\nu^{*}:=\min_{\lambda>0}\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda},\quad\lambda^{*}:=\underset{\lambda>0}{\arg\min}\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

\nu^{*}
is the minimal wave speed (see
[
16
]
) and the existence of
\lambda^{*}
is proved in
[
22
]
.

Let
N_{t}
be the set of particles alive at time
t
and
X_{u}(s)
be the position of the particle
u
or its ancestor at time
s
for any
u\in N_{t}
,
s\leq t
. Define

Z_{t}=\sum_{u\in N_{t}}\delta_{X_{u}(t)},

and
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(Z_{s}:s\leq t)
.

\{Z_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is called a branching Brownain motion in a periodic environment
(BBMPE).
Let
\mathbb{P}_{x}
be the law of
\{Z_{t}:t\geq 0\}
when the initial particle starts at
x\in\mathbb{R}
, that is
\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z_{0}=\delta_{x})=1
and
\mathbb{E}_{x}
be expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{x}
.
For simplicity,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{0}
will be written as
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{E}
, respectively. Notice that the distribution of
L
does not depend on the spatial location.
In the remainder of this paper, expectations with respect to
L
will be written as

\mathbf{E}
.

1.2.
Main results

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, define

(1.10)

W_{t}(\lambda)=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda).

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{(W_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{x}\}
is a martingale.
The limit
W(\lambda,x):=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}W_{t}(\lambda)
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

\mathrm{(i)}
If
|\lambda|>\lambda^{*}
then
W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

\mathrm{(ii)}
If
|\lambda|=\lambda^{*}
then
W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

\mathrm{(iii)}
If
|\lambda|<\lambda^{*}
then
W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely when
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)=\infty
, and
W(\lambda,x)
is an
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
-limit when
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
.

\{(W_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{x}\}

is called the additive martingale of the BBMPE starting from
x
.

Remark 1
.

For BBM, there is no essential difference between the case when the initial ancestor starting from
x
and the case starting from the origin, whereas things are different for BBMPE because the branching rate depends on the position. This is why we write
W(\lambda,x)
as the almost sure limit of
W_{t}(\lambda)
, instead of
W(\lambda)
. Nevertheless, it can be proved that
(W(\lambda,y),\mathbb{P}_{y})\overset{d}{=}(e^{-\lambda(y-x)}W(\lambda,x),\mathbb{P}_{x})
, if
y-x\in\mathbb{Z}
, due to the 1-periodicity of
\mathbf{g}
.

We will show that both
\gamma(\lambda)
and
\psi(x,\lambda)
are differentiable with respect to
\lambda
, so we can define

(1.11)

\partial W_{t}(\lambda):=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}W_{t}(\lambda)=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\bigg(\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+X_{u}(t))-\psi_{\lambda}(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\bigg).

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\{(\partial W_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{x}\}
is a martingale.
For all
|\lambda|\geq\lambda^{*}
, the limit
\partial W(\lambda,x):=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\partial W_{t}(\lambda)
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

\mathrm{(i)}
If
|\lambda|>\lambda^{*}
then
\partial W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

\mathrm{(ii)}
If
|\lambda|=\lambda^{*}
then
\partial W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely when

\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})=\infty
, and
\partial W(\lambda,x)\in(0,\infty)

(respectively
\partial W(\lambda,x)\in(-\infty,0)
)
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely when
\lambda>0
(respectively
\lambda<0
) and
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
.

\{(\partial W_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{x}\}

is called the derivative martingale of the BBMPE starting from
x
.

Pulsating travelling waves have been studied analytically in many papers,
see, for example,
[
4
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.
It is known that there is no pulsating travelling waves for
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
, whereas if
|\nu|\geq\nu^{*}
then there exists a unique, up to time-shift, pulsating travelling wave (see
[
16
, P. 467]
).
In this paper, we use limits of the additive and derivative martingales to give probabilistic representations of pulsating travelling wave in the supercritical case of
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and the critical case of
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
.
Thus we give probabilistic proofs of the existence of pulsating travelling waves in the supercritical and critical cases.
We also give a probabilistic proof of the non-existence of pulsating travelling waves in the subcritical case
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
.
In
[
27
]
, we will give a probabilistic proof of the asymptotic behavior and uniqueness of the pulsating travelling waves.

Theorem 1.3
.

\mathrm{(i)}

Supercriticality case.

If
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
,

(1.12)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}W(\lambda,x)\right\}\right)

is a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu
,
where
|\lambda|\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is such that

\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Criticality case.

If
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
,

(1.13)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda^{*})t}\partial W(\lambda^{*},x)\right\}\right)

is a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu^{*}
, and

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda^{*})t}\partial W(-\lambda^{*},x)\right\}\right)

is a pulsating travelling wave with speed
-\nu^{*}
.

\mathrm{(iii)}

Subcriticality case.

There is no pulsating travelling wave when
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
.

Since
\gamma(\lambda)
is an even function (see Lemma
2.1
below),
in the remainder of the paper we will deal only with the case that
\lambda\geq 0
(i.e.,
\nu\geq 0
) unless otherwise stated. The case
\lambda<0
(i.e.,
\nu<0
) follows from symmetry and the results are the same.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of principal eigenvalue and eigenfunction

We first discuss some properties of
\gamma(\lambda)
,

Lemma 2.1
.

(1) The function
\gamma
is
analytic and strictly convex on
\mathbb{R}
.
There exists a unique
\lambda^{*}>0
such that

\nu^{*}=\frac{\gamma(\lambda^{*})}{\lambda^{*}}=\min_{\lambda>0}\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}>0.

Furthermore

(2.1)

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow-\infty}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)=-\infty,\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)=+\infty.

(2) The function
\gamma
is even on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The analyticity, convexity of
\gamma
, and the existence and uniqueness of
\lambda^{*}
are contained in
[
22
, Lemma 2.5]
.
For the analyticity and convexity of
\gamma
, one can also see
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
.
Now we show (
2.1
).
Define
\phi(x,\lambda)=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
. A direct calculation shows that
\phi
satisfies

(2.2)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda).

By the Feynman-Kac formula,

\phi(x,\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\left[\phi(B_{t},\lambda)e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right],\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence,

(2.3)

\psi(x,\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\left[\psi(B_{t},\lambda)e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda(B_{t}-x)+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right],\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
\mathbf{g}
is 1-periodic and continuous, we can assume that
0<\alpha\leq\mathbf{g}(x)\leq\beta<\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Notice that
\Pi_{x}[e^{-\lambda B_{t}}]=e^{-\lambda x+\frac{\lambda^{2}t}{2}}
, (
2.3
) implies that

(2.4)

\gamma(\lambda)\in\bigg[\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}+m\alpha,\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}+m\beta\bigg].

Combining this with the analyticity and convexity of
\gamma
, we get (
2.1
).

If there exists
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{1})=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{2})
. The convexity of
\gamma
would imply that
\gamma^{\prime}
is constant on
[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]
, and then analyticity would imply that
\gamma^{\prime}
is a constant, which contradicts (
2.1
). Therefore,
\gamma^{\prime}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing on
\mathbb{R}
, that is,
\gamma(\cdot)
is strictly convex.

(2) Let
\psi(x):=\psi(x,\lambda)
satisfy (
1.8
), i.e.,
\psi

is the positive eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\gamma(\lambda)
.
Let
\bar{\psi}=\psi(x,-\lambda)

be the positive eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\gamma(-\lambda)
, then
\bar{\psi}(x)

satisfies, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}_{xx}(x)+\lambda\bar{\psi}_{x}(x)+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\bar{\psi}(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma(-\lambda)\bar{\psi}(x),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(x+1)

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}(x).

Multiplying (
2.5
) by
\psi
and integrating over
(0,1)
, we get that

\displaystyle\gamma(-\lambda)\int_{0}^{1}\bar{\psi}\psi\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}_{xx}\psi+\lambda\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\bar{\psi}\psi\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}_{x}(1)\psi(1)-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}_{x}(0)\psi(0)+\lambda\bar{\psi}(1)\psi(1)-\lambda\bar{\psi}(0)\psi(0)

\displaystyle\,+\int_{0}^{1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{x}-\lambda\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\bar{\psi}\psi\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}_{x}\psi_{x}-\lambda\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\bar{\psi}\psi\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}\psi_{xx}-\lambda\bar{\psi}\psi_{x}+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\bar{\psi}\psi\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(\lambda)\int_{0}^{1}\bar{\psi}\psi\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\psi,\bar{\psi}>0
, we obtain that
\gamma(\lambda)=\gamma(-\lambda)
.
∎

We compare the values of
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)
and
\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{*})=\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda^{*})}{\lambda^{*}}
.
(2) If
0<\lambda<\lambda^{*}
,
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)<\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.
(3) If
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
,
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)>\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Put
f(\lambda)=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.
(1) Note that

(2.7)

f^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)-\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}}{\lambda}.

Since
f(\lambda^{*})=\min_{\lambda>0}f(\lambda)
, we have
f^{\prime}(\lambda^{*})=0
, that is,
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{*})=\frac{\gamma(\lambda^{*})}{\lambda^{*}}
.

(2) If there were
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda^{*}
satisfying
f^{\prime}(\lambda_{1})\geq 0
, then, by the uniqueness of
\lambda^{*}
,

\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{1})\geq\frac{\gamma(\lambda_{1})}{\lambda_{1}}>\frac{\gamma(\lambda^{*})}{\lambda^{*}}=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{*}).

This contradicts the convexity of
\gamma
.

(3) If there were
\lambda_{2}>\lambda^{*}
satisfying
f^{\prime}(\lambda_{2})<0
, let
\lambda_{3}=\sup\{\lambda:\lambda<\lambda_{2}\text{ and }f^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq 0\}
. Then by the continuity of
f^{\prime}
,
f^{\prime}(\lambda_{3})=0
and
\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{2}
.
By the definition of
\lambda_{3}
,

\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{3})=\frac{\gamma(\lambda_{3})}{\lambda_{3}}=f(\lambda_{3})>\frac{\gamma(\lambda_{2})}{\lambda_{2}}>\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{2}),

which contradicts the convexity of
\gamma
again.

Suppose there were
\lambda_{4}>\lambda^{*}
satisfying
f^{\prime}(\lambda_{4})=0
.
For any
\delta\in(0,\lambda_{4}-\lambda^{*})
, we have

(2.8)

\int_{\lambda_{4}-\delta}^{\lambda_{4}}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)-\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{\lambda_{4}-\delta}^{\lambda_{4}}f^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}\lambda=f(\lambda_{4})-f(\lambda_{4}-\delta).

We claim that
f(\lambda_{4})-f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)>0
. In fact, since
f^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq 0
for
\lambda\in[\lambda_{4}-\delta,\lambda_{4}]
, we have
f(\lambda_{4})-f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)\geq 0
. If
f(\lambda_{4})-f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)=0
,
then
f^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
for
\lambda\in[\lambda_{4}-\delta,\lambda_{4}]
. Thus we have

\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{4})=\frac{\gamma(\lambda_{4})}{\lambda_{4}}=f(\lambda_{4})=f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)=\frac{\gamma(\lambda_{4}-\delta)}{\lambda_{4}-\delta}=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{4}-\delta),

which contradicts
the strict convexity of
\gamma
. Thus the claim is true.

For
\lambda\in[\lambda_{4}-\delta,\lambda_{4}]
, it holds that
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda_{4})=f(\lambda_{4})
, and that
f(\lambda)\geq f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)
because we have proved
f^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq 0
when
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle f(\lambda_{4})-f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{4}-\delta}^{\lambda_{4}}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)-\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}}{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\lambda\leq\delta\frac{f(\lambda_{4})-f(\lambda_{4}-\delta)}{\lambda^{*}}>0,

which implies that
\lambda^{*}\leq\delta
for any
\delta>0
.
This contradicts the fact that
\lambda^{*}>0
. The proof of (3) is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
\widetilde{\psi}(\cdot,\lambda)
is a positive eigenfunction of
(
1.8
) with
\widetilde{\psi}(0,\lambda)=1
. Then
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
Moreover,
\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)
, the derivative of
\widetilde{\psi}
with respective to
\lambda
, satisfies, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda xx}(x,\lambda)-\widetilde{\psi}_{x}(x,\lambda)-\lambda\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda x}(x,\lambda)+\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x)\right)\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)+\lambda\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\lambda)\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda).

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)
is 1-periodic,
to prove that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
,
it suffices to verify that, for any
x\in[0,1]
,

\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)
is continuous with respect to
\lambda
and
continuously differentiable with respect to
\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Define

(2.10)

\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda):=e^{-\lambda x}\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda).

Then
\widehat{\psi}
satisfies

(2.11)

\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\psi}_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore,
\widehat{\psi}
is the solution of the following boundary value problem

(2.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\psi}_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda),\quad x\in(0,1),\\
&\widehat{\psi}(1,\lambda)=e^{-\lambda},\quad\widehat{\psi}(0,\lambda)=1.\end{array}\right.

By the probabilistic representation of the solution to the above boundary value problem, we have

(2.13)

\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\left[e^{-\lambda B_{\tau}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right],\quad x\in[0,1],

where
\tau=\inf\{t>0:B_{t}\notin(0,1)\}
.
By (
2.10
), we only need to prove that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\widehat{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.

Note that for any
x\in[0,1]
,

\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t},B_{\tau}=0\right]+e^{-\lambda}\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t},B_{\tau}=1\right]<\infty.

Thus for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the gauge function

(2.14)

g(x,\lambda):=\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]

is bounded for
x\in[0,1]
.

\gamma(\lambda)
is analytic, convex and even, so
\gamma(0)
is the minimum of value of
\gamma(\lambda)
and for any
\lambda\neq 0
,
\gamma(\lambda)>\gamma(0)
. Hence,

(2.15)

\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda)\leq g(x,\lambda)\leq g(x,0)=\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(0))\mathrm{d}t}\right]\leq\sup_{x\in[0,1]}g(x,0)<\infty,\quad x\in[0,1].

Using the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain that

\widehat{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})
.

Now we prove
\widehat{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. We only need to deal with the case
\lambda>0
. Fix a
\lambda_{0}>0
.
Since
B_{\tau}e^{-\lambda B_{\tau}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\leq e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(0))\mathrm{d}t}
, and we have
for
\lambda\in[\lambda_{0}/2,3\lambda_{0}/2]
,

(2.16)

\tau e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\leq Me^{(\gamma(\lambda)-\gamma(0))\tau}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}=Me^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(0))\mathrm{d}t},

for some large constant
M
.
Thanks to the dominated convergence theorem,

\widehat{\psi}(x,\cdot)
is differentiable at
\lambda_{0}
. Since
\lambda_{0}>0
is arbitrary,
\widehat{\psi}(x,\cdot)
is differentiable on
(0,\infty)
.
Taking the partial derivative of
\widehat{\psi}
with respect to
\lambda
, we obtain

(2.17)

\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)=-\Pi_{x}\left[B_{\tau}e^{-\lambda B_{\tau}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\Pi_{x}\left[\tau e^{-\lambda B_{\tau}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right].

Using the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)
is continuous.

Using the Markov property of Brownian motion for the second term of the right hand side of (
2.17
), we have

\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)=-\Pi_{x}\left[B_{\tau}e^{-\lambda B_{\tau}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\Pi_{x}\left[\int^{\tau}_{0}e^{\int_{0}^{s}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\widehat{\psi}(B_{s},\lambda)\mathrm{d}s\right].

From the classical theory of Schrödinger equations, we obtain that

\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}(\cdot,\lambda)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}
satisfies

(2.18)

\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda xx}+(m\mathbf{g}-\gamma(\lambda))\widehat{\psi}_{\lambda}=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\widehat{\psi}.

One can easily check that

\widetilde{\psi}=e^{\lambda x}\widehat{\psi}(\lambda,x)
satisfies (
2.9
).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
is a positive eigenfunction of
(
1.8
) with
\psi(0,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\psi(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
Moreover,

\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)
satisfies

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\psi_{\lambda xx}(x,\lambda)-\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)-\lambda\psi_{\lambda x}(x,\lambda)+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)+\lambda\psi(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\lambda)\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\psi(x,\lambda).

Proof.

Since
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
is 1-periodic, it suffices to verify
\frac{\partial\psi(x,\lambda)}{\partial\lambda}
exists for
x\in[0,1]
.
Define

(2.20)

\phi(x,\lambda):=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
\phi
satisfies

(2.21)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore,
\phi
is the solution of the following boundary value problem

(2.22)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda),\quad x\in(0,1)\\
&\phi(1,\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\psi(0,\lambda),\phi(0,\lambda)=\psi(0,\lambda).\end{array}\right.

By the probabilistic representation of the solution to the above boundary value problem, we have

(2.23)

\phi(x,\lambda)=\psi(0,\lambda)\Pi_{x}\left[e^{-\lambda B_{\tau}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right],\quad x\in[0,1],

where
\tau=\inf\{t>0:B_{t}\notin(0,1)\}
.
Let
\widehat{\psi}
be defined by (
2.13
).
Then

(2.24)

\phi(x,\lambda)=\psi(0,\lambda)\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda),\quad x\in[0,1].

In Lemma
2.3
, we proved that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\widehat{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
By (
2.20
) and the assumption that
\psi(0,\lambda)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
Since
\widehat{\psi}
satisfies (
2.18
), we have

(2.25)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\lambda xx}+m\mathbf{g}\phi_{\lambda}=\gamma(\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\phi.

(
2.19
) follows easily from (
2.20
).
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

There is a positive eigenfunction
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
of
(
1.8
) with
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(x,\lambda)dx=1
and
\psi(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)
satisfies (
2.19
).

Proof.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
let
\widetilde{\psi}(\cdot,\lambda)
be a positive eigenfunction of the periodic problem (
1.8
) with
\widetilde{\psi}(0,\lambda)=1
.
By Lemma
2.3
, for any fixed
x\in[0,1]
,
\widetilde{\psi}(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
. Put

\widetilde{c}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)\mathrm{d}x.

Define
\psi(x,\lambda):=\widetilde{c}(\lambda)^{-1}\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)
. Then
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(x,\lambda)dx=1
. By Lemma
2.4
, we only need to prove that
\widetilde{c}(\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
We only need to deal with the case
\lambda>0
. So in the remainder of this proof, we assume
\lambda>0
.
By the proof of Lemma
2.3
,
\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)\leq e^{\lambda}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}g(x,0)<\infty
.
By the bounded convergence theorem,
\widetilde{c}(\cdot)
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}
. We now show
\widetilde{c}(\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
.
By symmetry, it suffices to show
\widetilde{c}(\cdot)\in C^{1}((0,\infty))
.
For any
\lambda>0
and
\epsilon>0
with
\lambda-\epsilon>0
, define
D_{\epsilon}:=\{(x,\bar{\lambda}):0\leq x\leq 1,\,\lambda-\epsilon\leq\bar{\lambda}\leq\lambda+\epsilon\}
.
Note that

\frac{\partial\widetilde{\psi}}{\partial\lambda}(x,\lambda)=xe^{\lambda x}\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda)+e^{\lambda x}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\widehat{\psi}(x,\lambda).

Combining (
2.17
) and (
2.16
) we know that
\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)
is bounded in
D_{\epsilon}
. Therefore, by the bounded convergence theorem, we have for
|h|<\epsilon

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\widetilde{c}(\lambda+h)-\widetilde{c}(\lambda)}{h}=\int_{0}^{1}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda+h)-\widetilde{\psi}(x,\lambda)}{h}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)\mathrm{d}x.

Hence,
\widetilde{c}(\cdot)
is differentiable
and
\widetilde{c}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)dx
. By the bounded convergence theorem again, we get that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{c}^{\prime}(\lambda+h)=\int_{0}^{1}\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda+h)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)\mathrm{d}x=\widetilde{c}^{\prime}(\lambda).

This shows the continuity of
\widetilde{c}^{\prime}(\lambda)
.
∎

2.2.
Measure change for Brownian motion

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
\{B_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{x}\}
is a Brownian motion starting from
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

(2.26)

\Xi_{t}(\lambda):=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}x}\psi(B_{t},\lambda).

Then
\{\Xi_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{x}
-martingale.

Proof.

\psi(x,\lambda)
is strictly positive, so by Itô’s formula, we obtain

\frac{\psi(B_{t},\lambda)}{\psi(B_{0},\lambda)}=\exp\bigg\{\log\psi(B_{t},\lambda)-\log\psi(B_{0},\lambda)\bigg\}=\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\psi_{x}}{\psi}\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\psi_{xx}\psi-\psi_{x}^{2}}{\psi^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg\},

where we have written
\psi=\psi(B_{t},\lambda)
,
\psi_{x}=\psi_{x}(B_{t},\lambda)
and
\psi_{xx}=\psi_{xx}(B_{t},\lambda)
for short. Thus,

(2.27)

\displaystyle\frac{\Xi_{t}(\lambda)}{\Xi_{0}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\psi_{x}}{\psi}-\lambda\right)\mathrm{d}B_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}\psi-\psi_{x}^{2}}{2\psi^{2}}+m\mathbf{g}(B_{s})-\gamma(\lambda)\right)\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\psi_{x}}{\psi}-\lambda\right)\mathrm{d}B_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\psi_{x}}{\psi}-\lambda\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg\},

where the second equality follows from (
1.8
).
For fixed
\lambda
, by periodicity,
\frac{\psi_{x}}{\psi}-\lambda
is bounded, and so Novikov’s condition is satisfied.
Therefore,
\{\Xi_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{x}
-martingale.

∎

Since
\frac{\Xi_{t}(\lambda)}{\Xi_{0}(\lambda)}
is a non-negative martingale of mean
1
, we can define a probability measure
\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}
by

(2.28)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{x}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}=\frac{\Xi_{t}(\lambda)}{\Xi_{0}(\lambda)},

where
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}:t\geq 0\}

is the natural filtration of Brownian motion.
Define

\phi(x,\lambda):=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

A direct calculation shows that
\phi
satisfies

(2.29)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda)

and

\frac{\phi_{x}(x,\lambda)}{\phi(x,\lambda)}=\frac{\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}-\lambda.

By (
2.27
) and Girsanov’s theorem,

B_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi_{x}(B_{s},\lambda)}{\phi(B_{s},\lambda)}\mathrm{d}s
is a
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-Brownian motion.
In other words, under
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
,

\{B_{t},t\geq 0\}
satisfies

(2.30)

\mathrm{d}B_{t}=\frac{\phi_{x}(B_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(B_{t},\lambda)}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{t},\quad B_{0}=x,

where
\{\widehat{B}_{t},\,t\geq 0;\,\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a Brownian motion.
Hence,

\{B_{t},\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a diffusion with infinitesimal generator

(2.31)

(\mathcal{A}f)(x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f(x)}{\partial x^{2}}+\left(\frac{\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}-\lambda\right)\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x}.

Since
\frac{\phi_{x}(\cdot,\lambda)}{\phi(\cdot,\lambda)}
is 1-periodic, the law of
\{B_{t}-B_{0}:t\geq 0\}
under
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
is the same as under
\Pi_{x+1}^{\lambda}
.

In the remainder of this paper, we always assume
that
\{Y_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a diffusion with infinitesimal generator (
2.31
).

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need some properties of
Y_{t}
under
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
.

[
22
, Lemma 2.6 and Corollary 2.7]

obtained the strong law of large numbers of
\{Y_{t}\}
under
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda^{*}}
for binary branching in the critical case
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
and their proofs also work for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
and thus
Y_{t}/t\rightarrow-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)
,
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-almost surely. Now we prove the analog for our general case.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mu_{t}
be the law of
\{\frac{Y_{t}}{t}\}
under
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
. Then
\{\mu_{t}\}
satisfies a large deviation principle with good rate function

(2.32)

I(z):=\gamma^{*}(z)+\{\lambda z+\gamma(\lambda)\},

where
\gamma^{*}(z)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\{\eta z-\gamma(\eta)\}
denotes the Fenchel-Legendre transform of
\gamma
.

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
,
t>0
and any measurable function
F:C[0,t]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.33)

\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left[F(\{Y_{s}\}_{s\leq t})\right]=\Pi_{x}\bigg[\frac{\psi(B_{t},\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda(B_{t}-x)+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}F(\{B_{s}\}_{s\leq t})\bigg].

Since
\psi
is strictly positive and bounded, setting
F\equiv 1
in the previous equation, we get that

(2.34)

\Pi_{x}\left[e^{-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right]=e^{-\lambda x+\gamma(\lambda)t+O(1)},

where for fixed
\lambda
,
O(1)
is uniformly bounded.

By (
2.33
), for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\log\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}[e^{\eta Y_{t}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\log\Pi_{x}\bigg[\frac{\psi(B_{t},\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda(B_{t}-x)+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}e^{\eta B_{t}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\log\Pi_{x}\bigg[\frac{\psi(B_{t},\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}e^{-(\lambda-\eta)B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\bigg]+\frac{\lambda x}{t}-\gamma(\lambda).

It follows from (
2.34
) that as
t\to\infty
,

(2.35)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}[e^{\eta Y_{t}}]=\gamma(\lambda-\eta)-\gamma(\lambda).

The Fenchel-Legendre transform of
\eta\mapsto\gamma(\lambda-\eta)-\gamma(\lambda)
is given by

(2.36)

\displaystyle I(z):

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{\eta z-[\gamma(\lambda-\eta)-\gamma(\lambda)]\big\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{(\eta-\lambda)z-\gamma(\lambda-\eta)]\big\}+\lambda z+\gamma(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{*}(z)+\{\lambda z+\gamma(\lambda)\},

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\gamma
is an even function.
Note that
\gamma(\lambda-\eta)-\gamma(\lambda)
is
differentiable with respect to
\eta
by Lemma
2.1
.
By the Gärtner-Ellis theorem (for example, see
[
7
, §2.3]
),
\{\mu_{t}\}
satisfies a large deviation principle with good rate function
I(z)
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that
\frac{Y_{t}}{t}\rightarrow-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)

\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-almost surely.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
(1),
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)
is strictly increasing. Next, note that
I(z)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{\eta z-[\gamma(\lambda-\eta)-\gamma(\lambda)]\big\}
and that the
derivative of
\eta z-\gamma(\lambda-\eta)
with respect to
\eta
is

z+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda-\eta)
. For fixed
z
, define
\eta_{z}
such that
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda-\eta_{z})=-z
.
Here the existence of
\eta_{z}
is guaranteed by (
2.1
). By the convexity of
\gamma
,
\eta_{z}
is an increasing function of
z
. Moreover, because
\gamma^{\prime}
is strictly increasing, we have

(2.37)

I(z)=-\eta_{z}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda-\eta_{z})-[\gamma(\lambda-\eta_{z})-\gamma(\lambda)],

where
I(z)
is equal to
0
when
\eta_{z}=0
or
equivalently
z=-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)
. Thus

\frac{\mathrm{d}I(z)}{\mathrm{d}\eta_{z}}=\eta_{z}\gamma^{\prime\prime}(\lambda-\eta_{z})\geq 0

(respectively
\frac{\mathrm{d}I(z)}{\mathrm{d}\eta_{z}}\leq 0
) when
\eta_{z}>0
(respectively
\eta_{z}<0
). Using (
2.37
) and the fact that
\gamma^{\prime}
is strictly increasing, we have for any
\epsilon>0
,

(2.38)

\delta:=\inf\{I(z):|z+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)|\geq\epsilon\}=I(-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)-\epsilon)\wedge I(-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)+\epsilon)>0.

Applying the large deviation principle of
\{\mu_{t}\}
, we get

(2.39)

\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left(\left|\frac{Y_{t}}{t}+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon\right)\leq Ce^{-\delta t/2}.

By (
2.33
), there is a constant
C_{1}(\lambda)>0
such that

\displaystyle P(x,T):

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left(\max_{t\in[0,1]}|Y_{t}-Y_{0}+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t|>T\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}\left[\frac{\psi(B_{1},\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)-\lambda(B_{1}-x)+m\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{1}_{\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|B_{t}-x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t|>T\epsilon\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\lambda)\Pi_{x}\left[e^{-\lambda(B_{1}-x)}\mathbf{1}_{\{\max_{t\in[0,1]}|B_{t}-x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t|>T\epsilon\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\lambda)\Pi_{0}\left[e^{\lambda B_{1}^{*}}\mathbf{1}_{\{B_{1}^{*}>T\epsilon-|\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)|\}}\right],\quad x\in[0,1],

where
B_{1}^{*}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\{|B_{t}|\}
. Since, under
\Pi_{0}
,
B_{1}^{*}

has the same distribution as
|B_{1}|
and
\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{1}{x}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}
for
x>0
, there is a constant
C_{2}(\lambda)>0
such that

(2.40)

P(x,T)\leq C_{2}(\lambda)e^{-\delta T/2},\quad x\in[0,1].

Thus, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

\displaystyle\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\bigg(\bigcup_{t\in[n,n+1]}\{|Y_{t}+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t|>2t\epsilon\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left(|Y_{n}+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)n|>n\epsilon\right)+\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left(\max_{t\in[0,1]}|Y_{n+t}-Y_{n}+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t|>n\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-n\delta/2}+\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left[P(Y_{n},n)\right]\leq Ce^{-n\delta/2},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
P(x,T)
is 1-periodic in
x
.
Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, by the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we obtain

\frac{Y_{t}}{t}\rightarrow-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)

\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-almost surely.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
\{B_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{x}\}
is a Brownian motion starting from
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Define

(2.41)

\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda):=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\left(\psi(B_{t},\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})-\psi_{\lambda}(B_{t},\lambda)\right).

Then
\{\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{x}
-martingale.

Proof.

For convenience,
put
J_{t}:=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}
.
A straightforward computation using Itô’s formula yields

\mathrm{d}J_{t}=-\lambda J_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}-\gamma(\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})\right)J_{t}\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\psi(B_{t},\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})-\psi_{\lambda}(B_{t},\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\psi_{x}(B_{t},\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})+\psi(B_{t},\lambda)-\psi_{\lambda x}(B_{t},\lambda)\right]\mathrm{d}B_{t}

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{1}{2}\psi_{xx}(B_{t},\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})+\psi(B_{t},\lambda)\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)+\psi_{x}(B_{t},\lambda)-\frac{1}{2}\psi_{\lambda xx}(B_{t},\lambda)\right]\mathrm{d}t,

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left[J_{t}(\psi(B_{t},\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})-\psi_{\lambda}(B_{t},\lambda)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{t}\left[(\psi_{x}-\lambda\psi)(B_{t},\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})+\lambda\psi_{\lambda}(B_{t},\lambda)+\psi(B_{t},\lambda)-\psi_{x\lambda}(B_{t},\lambda)\right]\mathrm{d}B_{t}

\displaystyle+J_{t}(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})\left(\frac{1}{2}\psi_{xx}-\lambda\psi_{x}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}-\gamma(\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})\right)\psi\right)(B_{t},\lambda)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-J_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\psi_{\lambda xx}-\psi_{x}-\lambda\psi_{\lambda x}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}-\gamma(\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})\right)\psi_{\lambda}+\lambda\psi-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\psi\right)(B_{t},\lambda)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{t}\left[(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+B_{t})(\psi_{x}-\lambda\psi)+\lambda\psi_{\lambda}+\psi-\psi_{\lambda x}\right](B_{t},\lambda)\mathrm{d}B_{t},

where in the last equality we used (
1.8
) and (
2.19
).
Note that
\psi
,
\psi_{\lambda}
and
\psi_{\lambda x}
are 1-periodic in
x
, so they are bounded for fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,

\{\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{x}
-martingale.
∎

The martingale

\{(\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\Pi_{x}\}

may take negative values.
Now we introduce a related non-negative martingale.
Before giving its definition, we first give some properties of the function
h
defined by

(2.42)

h(x):=x-\frac{\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}.

Clearly
h(x)
is continuous and satisfies
h(x+1)=h(x)+1
.
Recall that we always suppose
\lambda>0
unless explicitly stated otherwise.
Since
\phi(x,\lambda)=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda)
, we have

(2.43)

h(x)=-\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)}{\phi(x,\lambda)}.

It is easy to see that
h^{\prime}
is 1-periodic and continuous.
Thus,
h^{\prime}
is bounded.

Lemma 2.10
.

h^{\prime}
is strictly positive.

Proof.

Recall that
\phi(x,\lambda)=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda)
satisfies

(2.44)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda).

For any
-\infty\leq y<z\leq\infty
, define

(2.45)

\tau_{(y,z)}:=\inf\{t>0:B(t)\notin(y,z)\},

and
\tau_{y}:=\inf\{t>0:B(t)=y\}
.
By the probabilistic representation for the solution of the Schrödinger equation, we have for any
x\in(y,z)
,

\displaystyle\phi(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}\left[\phi(B_{\tau_{(y,z)}},\lambda)e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{(y,z)}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}\left[\phi(y,\lambda)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{y}<\tau_{z}\}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]+\Pi_{x}\left[\phi(z,\lambda)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{z}<\tau_{y}\}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{z}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right].

By the monotone convergence theorem, the first term on the right-hand side of the last equation converges
to

\Pi_{x}\left[\phi(y,\lambda)e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]

as
z\rightarrow\infty.

The second term of the last equation is equal to

e^{-\lambda z}\Pi_{x}\left[\psi(z,\lambda)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{z}<\tau_{y}\}}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{z}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right],

thus bounded by
Ce^{-\lambda z}
. So it converges to zero when
z\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore we have shown for any
x>y
,

(2.46)

\phi(x,\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\left[\phi(y,\lambda)e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right].

Hence,

(2.47)

\ln\phi(x,\lambda)=\ln\phi(y,\lambda)+\ln\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right].

Differentiating both sides of the previous equation with respect to
\lambda
gives

(2.48)

\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)}{\phi(x,\lambda)}=\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(y,\lambda)}{\phi(y,\lambda)}-\frac{\Pi_{x}\left[\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\tau_{y}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]}{\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]}<\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(y,\lambda)}{\phi(y,\lambda)}.

In other words,
\phi_{\lambda}(\cdot,\lambda)/\phi(\cdot,\lambda)
is strictly decreasing. Thus by (
2.43
),
h
is strictly increasing.

By the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we know the gauge function
g(\cdot,\lambda)
is bounded. Therefore,

(2.49)

C=\max_{y\in[x-1,x]}\Pi_{x}\left[e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]<+\infty.

It is well known, see
[
6
, Theorem 8.5.7]
for example, that for any
b>0
,

(2.50)

\Pi_{x}[e^{-b\tau_{y}}]=e^{-(x-y)\sqrt{2b}}.

Differentiating both sides of the previous equation with respect to
b
, we get

(2.51)

\Pi_{x}\left[\tau_{y}e^{-b\tau_{y}}\right]=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{2b}}e^{-(x-y)\sqrt{2b}}.

Recall that
0<\alpha\leq\min_{x\in[0,1]}\mathbf{g}(x)
and (
2.4
) implies
\gamma(\lambda)>m\alpha
. Thus we have

(2.52)

\displaystyle h(x)-h(y)

\displaystyle\geq\Pi_{x}\left[\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\tau_{y}e^{\int_{0}^{\tau_{y}}(m\mathbf{g}(B_{t})-\gamma(\lambda))\mathrm{d}t}\right]/C

(2.53)

\displaystyle\geq\Pi_{x}\left[\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\tau_{y}e^{(m\alpha-\gamma(\lambda))\tau_{y}}\right]/C

(2.54)

\displaystyle=\frac{x-y}{C\sqrt{2(\gamma(\lambda)-m\alpha)}}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)e^{-(x-y)\sqrt{2(\gamma(\lambda)-m\alpha)}}.

Therefore

(2.55)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{h(x)-h(y)}{x-y}\geq\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)}{C\sqrt{2(\gamma(\lambda)-m\alpha)}}>0.

This completes the proof.
∎

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, define

(2.56)

\tau^{x}_{\lambda}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\;h(B_{t})\leq-x-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t\right\}.

Since
h(x)
is strictly increasing, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we may rewrite the definition of
\tau_{\lambda}^{x}
as

\displaystyle\tau^{x}_{\lambda}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:B_{t}\leq h^{-1}(-x-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t)\right\}.

Hence,
\tau^{x}_{\lambda}
is an
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}\}
-stopping time.

Define

(2.57)

\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}:=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\psi(B_{t},\lambda)\left(x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(B_{t})\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}>t\}}.

\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}
is nonnegative.
We now prove that
\{\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)},t\geq 0\}
is a martingale.

Lemma 2.11
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
y>h^{-1}(-x)
,
\{\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)},t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{y}
-martingale.

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.6
and
2.9
,

\{\Xi_{t}(\lambda):t\geq 0\}
and

\{\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda):t\geq 0\}

are
\Pi_{y}
-martingales.
Put
F(B_{t}):=\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda)+x\Xi_{t}(\lambda)
.
Then
\{F(B_{t}):t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{y}
-martingale.
The fact
\tau^{x}_{\lambda}
is an
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}\}
-stopping time yields that
\{F(B_{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}\wedge t}):t\geq 0\}
is a

\Pi_{y}
-martingale.
Note that

\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}=\left(\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda)+x\Xi_{t}(\lambda)\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}>t\}}=F(B_{t})\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}>t\}}

and

F(B_{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}})=0.

Thus

F(B_{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}\wedge t})=F(B_{t})\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}>t\}}+F(B_{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}})\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}\leq t\}}=\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)},

and hence
\{\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)},t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{y}
-martingale.
∎

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
y>h^{-1}(-x)
, consider a new probability measure
\Pi^{(x,\lambda)}_{y}
defined by

(2.58)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}=\frac{\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\Lambda_{0}^{(x,\lambda)}}.

Recall that under
\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}
,
\{Y_{t}\}
satisfies
\mathrm{d}Y_{t}=\frac{\phi_{x}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{t}
, where
\{\widehat{B}_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}\}
is a Brownian motion starting from
x
.
The following lemma, which says that

h(Y_{t})+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t

is a martingale under
\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}
, is crucial to study the behavior of the motion under
\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Define

(2.59)

M_{t}:=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(Y_{t})-h(Y_{0}),\quad t\geq 0.

Then
\left\{M_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}\right\}
is a martingale. Moreover, there exist two constants
c_{2}>c_{1}>0

such that the quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle_{t}\in[c_{1}t,c_{2}t]
.

Proof.

By (
2.43
),
h(Y_{t})=-\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}
.
Using (
2.30
) and Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\right)\mathrm{d}Y_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\right)\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{t}+\bigg[\frac{\phi_{x}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left(\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(Y_{t},\lambda)}{\phi(Y_{t},\lambda)}\right)\bigg]\mathrm{d}t,

where
\{\widehat{B}_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a Brownian motion.
Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda),

(2.60)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\lambda xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda)+\gamma(\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda).

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{x}}{\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\phi_{\lambda}}{\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\frac{\phi_{\lambda}}{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{x}}{\phi}\frac{\phi_{\lambda x}\phi-\phi_{\lambda}\phi_{x}}{\phi^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{\lambda xx}}{\phi}-\frac{\phi_{\lambda x}\phi_{x}}{\phi^{2}}+\frac{\phi_{\lambda}\phi_{x}^{2}}{\phi^{3}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{\lambda}\phi_{xx}}{\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{\lambda xx}}{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{\lambda}\phi_{xx}}{\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\phi+\gamma(\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}-m\mathbf{g}\phi_{\lambda}}{\phi}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi_{\lambda}\phi_{xx}}{\phi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)-\frac{\phi_{\lambda}}{\phi^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}+(m\mathbf{g}-\gamma(\lambda))\phi\right]=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda).

This yields

(2.61)

\mathrm{d}\left(h(Y_{t})\right)=h^{\prime}(Y_{t})\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{t}-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{d}t.

It follows from the boundedness of
h^{\prime}
and Lemma
2.10
that
h^{\prime}(x)\in[\sqrt{c_{1}},\sqrt{c_{2}}]
for two constants
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
.
Integrating both sides of (
2.61
) gives

h(Y_{t})-h(Y_{0})=\int_{0}^{t}h^{\prime}(Y_{s})\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{s}-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t.

Hence,

M_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}h^{\prime}(Y_{s})\mathrm{d}\widehat{B}_{s}

is a martingale with quadratic variation

(2.62)

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(h^{\prime}(Y_{s})\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\in[c_{1}t,c_{2}t].

∎

Let
T(s)=\inf\{t>0:\langle M\rangle_{t}>s\}
.
Thanks to the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem,
\widetilde{B}_{t}:=M_{T(t)}
is a standard Brownian motion.
Note that for
y>h^{-1}(-x)
,

(2.63)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi^{\lambda}_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}\times\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi^{\lambda}_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}

(2.64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(Y_{t})}{x+h(y)}\textbf{1}_{\{\forall s\leq t:\;x+h(y)+M_{s}>0\}}

(2.65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x+h(y)+M_{t}}{x+h(y)}\mathbf{1}_{\{\forall s\leq t:\;x+h(y)+M_{s}>0\}}.

Put

(2.66)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda}_{t}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{T(t)}^{(x,\lambda)}=\left(x+h(y)+M_{T(t)}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\forall s\leq t:\;x+h(y)+M_{T(s)}>0\}}

(2.67)

\displaystyle=\left(x+h(y)+\tilde{B}_{t}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\forall s\leq t:\;x+h(y)+\tilde{B}_{s}>0\}}

and
\mathcal{G}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{T(t)}^{B}
. We have

(2.68)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi^{\lambda}_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}=\frac{x+h(y)+M_{T(t)}}{x+h(y)}\mathbf{1}_{\{\forall s\leq t:\;x+h(y)+M_{T(s)}>0\}},

that is,

(2.69)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi^{\lambda}_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{G}_{t}}=\frac{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{t}}{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{0}}.

By
[
17
]
,

\left\{x+h(y)+\tilde{B}_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}\right\}

is a standard Bessel-3 process starting at

x+h(y)
, i.e.,

\left\{x+h(y)+M_{T(t)},t\geq 0;\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}\right\}

is a standard Bessel-3 process
starting at
x+h(y)
.

2.3.
Martingales for branching Brownian motion

In this subsection we give three martingales, that will play important roles for BBMPE.
First we prove that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{W_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0\}
is a martingale.
For this, we need the following many-to-one lemma (see
[
13
]
,
[
22
]
and
[
24
, §2.3]
).

Lemma 2.13

(Many-to-one Lemma)
.

Let
t>0
and

F:C[0,t]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function such that
F\left(B_{s},s\in[0,t]\right)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}
-measurable. Then

(2.70)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sum_{u\in N_{t}}F\left(X_{u}(s),s\in[0,t]\right)\right]=\Pi_{x}\left[e^{m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}F\left(B_{s},s\in[0,t]\right)\right].

Lemma 2.14
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\left\{W_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0;\mathbb{P}_{x}\right\}
is a non-negative martingale and the limit
W(\lambda,x):=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}W_{t}(\lambda)
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.13

and the fact that

\{\Xi_{t}(\lambda):t\geq 0\}
is a martingale, we get

(2.71)

\mathbb{E}_{x}W_{t}(\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\left[e^{m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}}\psi(B_{t},\lambda)\right]=\Pi_{x}\Xi_{t}(\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\Xi_{0}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda).

Thus by the branching property, for
s<t
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[W_{t}(\lambda)\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{v\in N_{s}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\gamma(\lambda)(t-s)}\sum_{u\in N_{t},u>v}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{v\in N_{s}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{v}(s)}W_{t-s}^{(v)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{v\in N_{s}}e^{-\lambda X_{v}(s)}\psi(X_{v}(s),\lambda)=W_{s}(\lambda),

where
u>v
denotes that
u
is a descendant of
v
and
W_{t-s}^{(v)}(\lambda)
is the additive martingale for the BBMPE starting from
X_{v}(s)
.
Therefore,
\{W_{t}(\lambda):t\geq 0\}
is a non-negative martingale and the limit
W(\lambda,x)=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}W_{t}(\lambda)
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.
∎

The second martingale is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.15
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{\partial W_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0;\mathbb{P}_{x}\}
is a martingale.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.13
that

(2.72)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\partial W_{t}(\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\Upsilon_{t}(\lambda)=\Pi_{x}\Upsilon_{0}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda x}\left(x\psi(x,\lambda)-\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)\right).

Using the branching property and the Markov property, it is easy to show that for any
t>s>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\partial W_{t}(\lambda)\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{v\in N_{s}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{v}(s)}\left(\partial W_{t-s}^{(v)}(\lambda)+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)sW_{t-s}^{(v)}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{v\in N_{s}}\left(e^{-\lambda X_{v}(s)}\left(X_{v}(s)\psi(X_{v}(s),\lambda)-\psi_{\lambda}(X_{v}(s),\lambda)\right)+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)se^{-\lambda X_{v}(s)}\psi(X_{v}(s),\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial W_{s}(\lambda),

where, for each
v\in N_{s}
,
W_{t-s}^{(v)}(\lambda)
and
\partial W_{t-s}^{(v)}(\lambda)
are respectively the additive and derivative martingales for the BBMPE starting from
X_{v}(s)
.
Therefore,
\{\partial W_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0;\mathbb{P}_{x}\}
is a martingale.
∎

The martingale
\{\partial W_{t}(\lambda)\}
may take negative values.
To study its limit we need to consider a related non-negative martingale. Note that

(2.73)

\displaystyle\partial W_{t}(\lambda)+xW_{t}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\left(x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+X_{u}(t)-\frac{\psi_{\lambda}(X_{u}(t),\lambda)}{\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)}\right)

(2.74)

\displaystyle=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\left(x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(X_{u}(t))\right).

Put

\widetilde{N}_{t}^{x}=\left\{u\in N_{t}:\forall s\leq t,\;x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)s+h(X_{u}(s))>0\right\}.

In the spirit of
[
20
]
, we define for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.75)

V_{t}^{x}(\lambda)=\sum_{u\in\widetilde{N}_{t}^{x}}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\left(x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(X_{u}(t))\right).

Now we show that
V_{t}^{x}(\lambda)
is a martingale.

Lemma 2.16
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
y>h^{-1}(-x)
,
\{V_{t}^{x}(\lambda),t\geq 0;\mathbb{P}_{y}\}
is a martingale with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

On the space-time half plane
\{(z,t):z\in\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}
,
consider the barrier

\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(-x,\lambda)}

described by
z=h^{-1}(-x-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t)
. By stopping the lines of descent the first time they hit this barrier we produce a random collection
\mathbf{C}(-x,\lambda)
of individuals.
Let
\sigma_{u}
denote the first hitting time for each

u\in\mathbf{C}(-x,\lambda)
.
Consider the stopping “line”

\mathcal{L}(t)=\left\{u\in\mathbf{C}(-x,\lambda):\sigma_{u}\leq t\right\}\cup\widetilde{N}_{t}^{x}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{L}(t)}
be the natural filtration
generated by the spatial paths and the number of offspring of the individuals before hitting the stopping line
\mathcal{L}(t)
.
By the strong Markov branching property of
\{Z_{t},t\geq 0\}
(see Jagers
[
18
, Theorem 4.14]
, also see Dynkin
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
for the corresponding property for superprocesses, where this property is called the special Markov property),

(2.76)

\mathbb{E}_{y}\left[\partial W_{t}(\lambda)+xW_{t}(\lambda)\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{L}(t)}\right]=V_{t}^{x}(\lambda).

Thus

\mathbb{E}_{y}V_{t}^{x}(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\left[\partial W_{t}(\lambda)+xW_{t}(\lambda)\right]=e^{-\lambda y}\psi(y,\lambda)\left(x+h(y)\right).

Hence we have for
0\leq s\leq t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{y}\left[V_{t}^{x}(\lambda)\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=

\mathbb{P}_{y}(\gamma^{(-x,\lambda)})\rightarrow 1\quad\mbox{as }x\rightarrow\infty.

Combining these with Theorem
4.2
, we get
\partial W(\lambda,y)=0

\mathbb{P}_{y}
-almost surely when
V^{x}(\lambda)=0

\mathbb{P}_{y}
-almost surely.
It follows from Proposition
4.1
that
\mathbb{P}_{y}(\partial W(\lambda,y)=0)=0
or
1
. Therefore, when

is an

-limit, we have

implies Theorem

∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

It was proved analytically in
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
14
, Proposition 1.2]
that pulsating travelling waves
exist if and only if
|\nu|\geq\nu^{*}
.
In this section, we will use probabilistic methods to prove the existence in the supercritical case
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and critical case
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
, and the nonexistence in the subcritical case
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
.

5.1.
Existence in the supercritical case (
\nu>\nu^{*}
)

In this subsection, we consider the case
\nu>\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
. By (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.2
,
\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
strictly decreases from
+\infty
to
\nu^{*}
on
[0,\lambda^{*}]
. Therefore, for any
\nu>\nu^{*}
there exists a unique
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
such that
\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.
Recall that the additive martingale
W_{t}(\lambda)
is defined in (
1.10
). Using the periodicity of
g(\cdot)
and
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
, we get that for
y-x\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(5.1)

(W(\lambda,y),\mathbb{P}_{y})\overset{d}{=}(e^{-\lambda(y-x)}W(\lambda,x),\mathbb{P}_{x}).

Note that for any
t>s>0
,

W_{t}(\lambda)=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{u\in N_{s}}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)(t-s)}\sum_{v\in N_{t},v>u}e^{-\lambda X_{v}(t)}\psi(X_{v}(t),\lambda).

It is easy to see that, under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
,

(5.2)

W_{t}(\lambda)\overset{d}{=}\sum_{u\in N_{s}}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}W_{t-s}^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s)),

where
W_{t-s}^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s))
is the additive martingale of the BBMPE
starting from
X_{u}(s)
, and given
\mathcal{F}_{s}
,
\{W_{t-s}^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s)),u\in N_{s}\}
are independent. Hence, letting
t\rightarrow\infty
, we have under
\mathbb{P}_{x}

(5.3)

W(\lambda,x)\overset{d}{=}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{u\in N_{s}}W^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s)),

where
W^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s))
is the limit of the additive martingale for the
BBMPE starting from
X_{u}(s)
, and given
\mathcal{F}_{s}
,
\{W^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s)):u\in N_{s}\}
are independent.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
.
Define

(5.4)

\mathbf{u}(t,x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}W(\lambda,x)\right\},

where
|\lambda|\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is such that
\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.
Then
\mathbf{u}
is a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu
.

Proof.

We assume that
\lambda\geq 0
. The case
\lambda<0
can be analyzed by symmetry.
By (
5.3
) and the Markov property,
we have, for any
t\geq s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}W(\lambda,x)\right\}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\bigg\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)s}\sum_{u\in N_{s}}W^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s))\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\exp\big\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)(t-s)}\sum_{u\in N_{s}}W^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s))\big\}\;\bigg|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\prod_{u\in N_{s}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{u}(s)}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)(t-s)}W^{(u)}(\lambda,X_{u}(s))\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\prod_{u\in N_{s}}\mathbf{u}(t-s,X_{u}(s)).

In particular, setting
s=t
, we have

(5.5)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\prod_{u\in N_{t}}\mathbf{u}(0,X_{u}(t)).

An argument similar to the one used in
[
25
]
shows that
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
satisfies

\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial x^{2}}+\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{u}).

Moreover, since
\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}=\nu
, by (
5.1
),
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(t+\frac{1}{\nu},x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)(t+\frac{1}{\nu})}W(\lambda,x)\right\}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t+\lambda}W(\lambda,x)\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x-1}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}W(\lambda,x-1)\right\}=\mathbf{u}(t,x-1).

In order to prove that
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is a pulsating travelling wave, it remains to show that

(5.6)

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=0,\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=1.

Let
\lfloor x\rfloor
denote the integral part of
x
. By (
5.1
),

(5.7)

\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x-\lfloor x\rfloor}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}e^{-\lambda\lfloor x\rfloor}W(\lambda,x-\lfloor x\rfloor)\right\}.

Since
\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}e^{-\lambda\lfloor x\rfloor}=0
and
y=x-\lfloor x\rfloor\in[0,1)
, we have

(5.8)

e^{-\lambda n}W(\lambda,y)\to 0,\quad\mbox{ $\mathbb{P}_{y}$-almost surely as }n\to\infty.

It follows from the bounded dominated convergence theorem that for fixed
t\geq 0
,

(5.9)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,y+n)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}e^{-\lambda n}W(\lambda,y)\right\}=1.

By Dini’s theorem, we have

(5.10)

\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=1.

To prove
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=0
, we need to verify that

(5.11)

\mathbb{P}_{x}(W(\lambda,x)=0)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Thanks to (
5.1
) and (
5.3
), an argument similar to the one used in the proof of Proposition
4.1
shows that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(W(\lambda,x)=0)=\mathbb{P}_{y}(W(\lambda,y)=0)
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
q
denote this common value.
It follows from (
5.3
) that
q
must satisfy
q=\mathbb{E}_{x}q^{|N_{s}|}
for any
s>0
. Note that
|N(s)|\geq 1
almost surely and
\mathbb{P}_{x}(|N(s)|>1)>0
, thus
q=\mathbb{E}_{x}q^{|N_{s}|}

implies
q=0
or
1
.
By Theorem
1.1
,
W(\lambda,x)
is an
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
-limit and hence
q<1
. So
q=0
. Therefore
\mathbf{u}
defined by (
5.4
) is a pulsating travelling wave.
∎

Proof of Theorem

The following result about the extremes of BBMPE is a consequence of Theorem

and (

).

Lemma 5.3

Let
\widetilde{m}_{t}:=\max\{X_{u}(t):u\in N_{t}\}

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{\widetilde{m}_{t}}{t}=\nu^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{m_{t}}{t}=-\nu^{*},\quad\mathbb{P}_{x}\mbox{-a.s.}

Proof.

We first show that

(5.16)

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{-m_{t}}{t}\leq\nu^{*}.

If this were not true, there would exist
\lambda>\lambda^{*}
such that

(5.17)

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{-m_{t}}{t}>\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}>\nu^{*}.

Hence,

Then we have

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}W_{t}(\lambda)\geq\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}e^{\lambda t\left(\frac{-m_{t}}{t}-\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}\right)}\psi(m_{t},\lambda)=+\infty,

which contradicts Theorem
1.1
.

Next we show that

(5.19)

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{-m_{t}}{t}\geq\nu^{*}.

For any small
\delta,\epsilon>0
,
let
\lambda=\lambda^{*}-\delta
. By the mean value theorem, there
exists
\tilde{\lambda}\in(\lambda-\epsilon,\lambda)
with

(5.20)

\gamma(\lambda)-\gamma(\lambda-\epsilon)=\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})\epsilon.

For any fixed
\lambda
and
\lambda-\epsilon
,
there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\leq\psi(x,\lambda),\psi(x,\lambda-\epsilon)\leq C_{2}
for any

.
Using an argument similar to that of
[
21
, Corollary 3.2]
, we get that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\;

\displaystyle e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\textbf{1}_{\{X_{u}(t)\geq(-\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})+\epsilon)t\}}

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}C_{2}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-(\lambda-\epsilon)X_{u}(t)}e^{-\epsilon X_{u}(t)}\textbf{1}_{\{X_{u}(t)\geq(-\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})+\epsilon)t\}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}C_{2}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-(\lambda-\epsilon)X_{u}(t)}e^{-\epsilon(-\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})+\epsilon)t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}C_{2}e^{-(\gamma(\lambda)-\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})\epsilon)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-(\lambda-\epsilon)X_{u}(t)}e^{-\epsilon^{2}t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}e^{-\gamma(\lambda-\epsilon)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-(\lambda-\epsilon)X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda-\epsilon)e^{-\epsilon^{2}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}e^{-\epsilon^{2}t}W_{t}(\lambda-\epsilon)=0,

where in the last inequality we used (
5.20
).
Therefore,

(5.21)

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\textbf{1}_{\{X_{u}(t)<(-\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})+\epsilon)t\}}=W(\lambda,x),\quad\mathbb{P}_{x}\mbox{-a.s.}

By (
5.11
),
\mathbb{P}_{x}(W(\lambda,x)=0)=0
.
Thus the previous limit implies that

(5.22)

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\exists\;u\in N_{t}:X_{u}(t)<(-\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})+\epsilon)t\}}>0.

This yields

(5.23)

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{-m_{t}}{t}\geq\gamma^{\prime}(\tilde{\lambda})-\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon,\delta
are arbitrary and
\gamma^{\prime}
is continuous, we obtain

\liminf\limits_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{-m_{t}}{t}\geq\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{*})=\nu^{*}
.
Thus

(5.24)

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{m_{t}}{t}=-\nu^{*}.

Using the evenness of
\gamma(\lambda)
and an argument similar as above, we can easily get that

\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{\widetilde{m}_{t}}{t}=\nu^{*}
.
∎

Twistor theory of the Chen–Teo gravitational instanton
Maciej Dunajski

Abstract
Toric Ricci–flat metrics in dimension four correspond to certain holomorphic vector bundles over a twistor space. We construct these bundles explicitly, by exhibiting and characterising their patching matrices, for the five–parameter family of Riemannian ALF metrics constructed by Chen and Teo. The Chen–Teo family contains a two–parameter family of asymptotically flat gravitational instantons. The patching matrices for these instantons take a simple rational form.

Introduction

In
[
5
]
Chen and Teo constructed a five–parameter family of Riemannian Ricci–flat four–manifolds with torus symmetry. In the limiting cases this family contains
the Riemannian Plebański–Demiański solutions
[
13
]
and some multi–centred Gibbons–Hawking metrics

[
10
]
, as well as a new two–parameter family of asymptotically flat gravitational instantons
[
4
]

on the four–manifold
\mathbb{CP}^{2}{\setminus}S^{1}
.

It was shown by Louis Witten
[
16
]
, and in a different form by Richard Ward
[
15
]
, that any Lorentzian Ricci flat toric metric in four–dimensions corresponds to a static and axi–symmetric solution of the anti–self–dual Yang Mills equations
(ASDYM) with gauge group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
on flat space–time. These field equations are integrable by the twistor
correspondence of Ward
[
14
]
, and their solutions
are encoded in rank–two holomorphic vector bundles over the twistor space
PT\equiv\mathbb{CP}^{3}\setminus\mathbb{CP}^{1}
which are trivial on twistor lines. Any such bundle can be defined in terms
a patching matrix, and the Ward correspondence
gives an algorithm for recovering the ASDYM connection,
and eventually the toric vacuum solution from such a matrix. This construction has primarily focused
on Lorentzian toric vacuum solutions.

The aim of this paper is to adapt this construction to Riemannian signature, and use it to
put the Chen–Teo metrics in the twistor framework. In the next section we shall recall
the Chen–Teo metrics. In §
3
we shall
use the rod structure of these metrics to construct
the patching matrix for the Ward bundle. It is
a rational matrix with three simple poles, and
asymptotics encoding conserved charges.
In §
4
we discuss the reconstruction
of the Chen–Teo patching matrix from a general patching matrix with three simple poles. The Ward correspondence for the reduced ASDYM equations is summarised in the Appendix.

ds^{2}=2(dZd\widetilde{Z}-dWd\widetilde{W}),\quad\mbox{vol}=dW\wedge d\widetilde{W}\wedge dZ\wedge d\widetilde{Z}.

(A.1)

Let
A
be an
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
–valued connection one–form with the curvature
F=dA+A\wedge A
and a covariant derivative
D=d+A
. The ASDYM condition
F=-\star F
takes the form

F_{WZ}=0,\quad F_{\widetilde{W}\widetilde{Z}}=0,\quad F_{W\widetilde{W}}-F_{Z\widetilde{Z}}=0.

(A.2)

These equations arise as the compatibility conditions
[L,M]=0
for an over–determined
linear system of PDEs

L\Psi=0,\quad M\Psi=0,\quad\mbox{where}\quad L=D_{\widetilde{Z}}-\lambda D_{W},\quad M=D_{\widetilde{W}}-\lambda D_{Z}.

(A.3)

Here
\lambda\in\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is the spectral parameter, and
\Psi=\Psi(W,Z,\widetilde{W},\widetilde{Z},\lambda)
takes its values in the group
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
(other gauge groups are also possible).

The first equation in (
A.2
) implies the existence of a gauge such that
A_{W}=A_{Z}=0
. The second equation then implies the existence of an
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
valued function
J=J(W,Z,\widetilde{W},\widetilde{Z})
such that

A=J^{-1}\partial_{\widetilde{W}}Jd\widetilde{W}+J^{-1}\partial_{\widetilde{Z}}Jd\widetilde{Z}.

(A.4)

Finally the last equation in (
A.2
) becomes the so–called Yang equation

\partial_{Z}(J^{-1}\partial_{\widetilde{Z}}J)-\partial_{W}(J^{-1}\partial_{\widetilde{W}}J)=0.

(A.5)

Therefore the Yang equation is equivalent to ASDYM together with a particular choice of gauge.

A.2
Twistor correspondence

The ASDYM equations (
A.2
) are equivalent to the flatness of the connection
A
on self–dual
totally null two–planes (
\alpha
–planes in twistor terminology). All such planes in
M_{\mathbb{C}}
are
of the form

\alpha=W+\lambda\widetilde{Z},\quad\beta=Z+\lambda\widetilde{W}

(A.6)

and the space of these planes is the projective twistor space
PT\equiv\mathbb{CP}^{3}\setminus\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with affine coordinates
(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)
covering the open set where
\lambda\neq\infty
. The vector space of parallel sections of the Yang–Mills
bundle
{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{C}}

on each
\alpha
–plane defines a fibre of a holomorphic rank–2 vector bundle
E\rightarrow PT
. For any fixed point

p\in M_{\mathbb{C}}
the equations (
A.6
) define a rational curve

L_{p}\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}
in the twistor space. The sections
of
\mathcal{E}
constant on all
\alpha
–planes through
p
can be compared at
p
, and thus the
twistor bundle
E
is trivial on all lines (
A.6
).

Conversely, let
E\longrightarrow PT
be a rank–2 vector bundle trivial on lines. Cover
PT
with two open sets:
U
where
\lambda\neq\infty
, and
\widetilde{U}

where
\lambda\neq 0
. Let
P_{U\widetilde{U}}=P(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)
be the patching matrix
of the bundle which is holomorphic on
U\cap\widetilde{U}
. Restrict
P
to a twistor line (
A.6
) where the bundle is trivial so that a Riemann–Hilbert splitting

P_{U\widetilde{U}}=P_{U}{{P}_{\widetilde{U}}}^{-1}

(A.7)

can be performed with
P_{U}
and
P_{\widetilde{U}}

holomorphic and non–singular in
U
and
\widetilde{U}
respectively. The patching matrix
P_{U\widetilde{U}}
is constant
over the
\alpha
–planes spanned by

l=\partial_{\widetilde{Z}}-\lambda\partial_{W}
, and
m=\partial_{\widetilde{W}}-\lambda\partial_{Z}
. Therefore the quantities

{P_{U}}^{-1}l(P_{U})={P_{\widetilde{U}}}^{-1}l(P_{\widetilde{U}}),\quad{P_{U}}^{-1}m(P_{U})={P_{\widetilde{U}}}^{-1}m(P_{\widetilde{U}})

are global on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, and by the application
of the Liouville theorem there exist

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
valued one–form
A

on the complexified Minkowski space such that

P_{U}^{-1}l(P_{U})={P_{\widetilde{U}}}^{-1}l(P_{\widetilde{U}})=A_{\widetilde{Z}}-\lambda A_{W},\quad P_{U}^{-1}m(P_{U})={P_{\widetilde{U}}}^{-1}m(P_{\widetilde{U}})=A_{\widetilde{W}}-\lambda A_{Z}.

(A.8)

Setting
H=P_{U}(\lambda=0),\widetilde{H}=P_{\widetilde{U}}(\lambda=\infty)
then gives

A=H^{-1}\partial_{Z}H\;dZ+H^{-1}\partial_{W}H\;dW+\widetilde{H}^{-1}\partial_{\widetilde{Z}}\widetilde{H}\;d\widetilde{Z}+\widetilde{H}^{-1}\partial_{\widetilde{W}}\widetilde{H}\;d\widetilde{W}

which is gauge–equivalent to (
A.4
) with
J=\widetilde{H}H^{-1}
. Applying
l
and
m
to the first and the second relation in (
A.8
) and commuting derivatives then implies that
A
is a solution of ASDYM, or equivalently that
J
satisfies the Yang equation.

A.3
Symmetry reduction

We shall use the cylindrical polar coordinates

Z=t+z,\quad\widetilde{Z}=t-z,\quad W=re^{i\theta},\quad\widetilde{W}=re^{-i\theta}

so that

ds^{2}=2(dt^{2}-dz^{2}-dr^{2}-r^{2}d\theta^{2}),

and assume that the gauge potential is invariant under a 2–dimensional Abelian group generated
by a translation
\partial_{t}
and a rotation
\partial_{\theta}
. We chose
J=J(r,z)
in which case the Yang equation (
A.5
) reduces to equation (
3.3
) locally characterising the toric Ricci–flat four–manifolds.
The Killing vectors
{\mathcal{K}_{1}}=\partial_{t}
and
{\mathcal{K}_{2}}=\partial_{\theta}
on
M_{\mathbb{C}}
give rise to holomorphic vector fields
({\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}},{\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})

on
PT
. These vector fields have zeros, so the reduced twistor space defined to be the corresponding quotient
of
PT
is a one–dimensional non–Hausdorff complex manifold
[

,
9
,
17
]
. Rather than taking this quotient we can consider the holomorphic vector bundle
E\rightarrow PT
as defined in §
A.2

such that the patching matrix (
A.7
) depends only on one complex variable
\gamma
constant along
({\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{1}},{\hat{\mathcal{K}}_{2}})
. The incidence relation (
A.6
) then reduces to

\gamma=z+\frac{1}{2}r\Big(\lambda-\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big).

Therefore knowing the patching matrix at a rod corresponding to a fixed value of

Construction of inclusions with vanishing generalized polarization tensors by imperfect interfaces Thanks: This study was supported by National Research Foundation of Korea (NRF) grant funded by the Korean government (MSIT) (NRF-2021R1A2C1011804).
Doosung Choi, Mikyoung Lim

Abstract
We investigate the problem of planar conductivity inclusion with imperfect interface conditions. We assume that the inclusion is simply connected. The presence of the inclusion causes a perturbation in the incident background field. This perturbation admits a multipole expansion of which coefficients we call the generalized polarization tensors (GPTs), extending the previous terminology for inclusions with perfect interfaces. We derive explicit matrix expressions for the GPTs in terms of the incident field, material parameters, and geometry of the inclusion. As an application, we construct GPT-vanishing structures of general shape that result in negligible perturbations for all uniform incident fields. The structure consists of a simply connected core with an imperfect interface. We provide numerical examples of GPT-vanishing structures obtained by our proposed scheme.

Introduction

We consider the planar conductivity (or anti-plane elasticity) problem, where an inclusion is inserted into an infinite homogeneous background. The inclusion, having a different conductivity from that of the background, generally induces perturbations to the background fields.
However, certain types of inclusions do not cause any perturbation to the uniform background fields, known as neutral inclusions. Neutral inclusions have been extensively studied due to their possible applications in designing invisibility cloaking structures with metamaterials in various contexts, including acoustics, elasticity, electromagnetics, and microwaves
[
1
,
5
,
30
,
32
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
.
Most results on neutral inclusions deal with perfect boundary conditions.
On the other hand, this paper addresses neutral inclusions with imperfect interfaces. More precisely, we provide a construction scheme for neutral inclusions of general shape with imperfect interfaces conditions where the core has finite low conductivity.

Well-known examples of neutral inclusions (with perfect interfaces) that do not disturb incident uniform fields are coated disks and spheres with isotropic material parameters
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
23
,
35
,
36
]
.
Coated ellipses and ellipsoids exhibit neutrality to uniform fields of all directions when the conductivities of the core, shell, and matrix are appropriately selected (potentially anisotropic)
[
16
,
29
,
33
,
40
,
39
]
. It is worth noting that these are the only shapes that can achieve the neutral property for all uniform fields
[
25
,
26
,
34
]
. Non-elliptical coated inclusions can achieve neutrality for a single uniform field
[
21
,
34
]
(see also
[
31
]
).

One can design neutral inclusions using the concept of the generalized polarization tensors (GPTs), which are the coefficients of the multipole expansion for the perturbed potential caused by the inclusion; this concept extends the polarization tensor, introduced by Schiffer and Szegö
[
38
]
, to higher orders.
GPT-vanishing structures–inclusions with vanishing values for the leading-order terms of the GPTs–are neutral inclusions in the asymptotic sense. Multi-coated concentric disks or balls have been reported to be GPT-vanishing structures
[
4
,
43
]
(see also
[
3
,
5
]
for results in the context of Helmholtz and Maxwell’s equations).
GPT-vanishing structures of general shape were also obtained using a shape optimization approach
[
15
,
22
,
28
]
(see also
[
11
]
).

Imperfect interfaces are characterized by discontinuity in either the flux or the potential, in contrast to perfectly bonding boundaries of which both the flux and potential are continuous. The interface parameter, possibly a non-constant function, accounts for the discontinuity of the flux or the potential on the interface.
In
[
37
]
, Ru investigated neutral inclusions for the two-dimensional elastic problem with imperfect interfaces when the inclusion is stiffer than the background medium.
For the conductivity problem, Benveniste and Miloh found neutral inclusions with imperfect interfaces for a single uniform field
[
8
]
.
In
[
27
]
, Kang and Li constructed PT-vanishing structures of general shape with imperfect interfaces, assuming that the core is simply connected and perfectly conducting.

In this paper, we propose a new construction scheme of neutral inclusions (of general shape) for the planar conductivity problem with imperfect interfaces. The inclusions have discontinuity in the potential, where the core have the arbitrary constant conductivity.
Different from
[
27
]
, the constant potential condition in the core is no longer assumed. Consequently, the construction of the interface parameter satisfying the neutrality condition becomes challenging.
To address this problem, we find GPT-vanishing structures by employing the concept of the Faber polynomials polarization tensors (FPTs) (see
[
11
]
).
The FPTs are linear combinations of GPTs with coefficients given by Faber polynomials associated with the inclusion. We also refer to
[
13
,
14
]
for details of Faber polynomials.
The concept of FPTs has been successfully applied to design semi-neutral inclusions
[
11
]
(see also
[
9
,
10
,
24
]
). In this paper, we derive an explicit matrix expression of the FPTs for an inclusion with imperfect interface conditions and, by using the matrix expression, determine the interface parameter that vanishes several leading terms of the FPTs.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section

2
Problem formulation

We let
D
be a planar, simply connected domain with an analytic boundary. The core region
D
has the constant conductivity
\sigma_{c}
and the exterior region
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus D
is occupied by another homogeneous isotropic material with the constant conductivity
\sigma_{m}>0
.
We consider the potential problem with imperfect interface condition:

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\sigma}\nabla u=0\qquad&\mbox{in }\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\\
\displaystyle p\big(u\big|^{+}-u\big|^{-}\big)=\sigma_{m}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|^{+}\qquad&\mbox{on }\partial D,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\sigma_{m}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|^{+}=\sigma_{c}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|^{-}\qquad&\mbox{on }\partial D,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle(u-H)(x)=O({|x|^{-1}})\qquad&\mbox{as }|x|\to\infty,\end{cases}

where
p
is the interface parameter that is a real-valued function on
\partial\Omega
and
H
is an arbitrary entire harmonic function. The conductivity distribution
{\sigma}
has the form

{\sigma}=\sigma_{c}\chi_{D}+\sigma_{m}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}},

where
\chi
is the characteristic function.

2.1
Generalized polarization tensors

For a Lipschitz domain
D
, we define the single and double layer potentials with a density
\varphi\in L^{2}(\partial D)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\Gamma(x-y)\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}[\varphi](x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{y}}\Gamma(x-y)\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\partial D,

where
\Gamma
is the fundamental solution to the Laplacian,
i.e.
,
\Gamma(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|
, and
\nu_{y}
denotes the outward unit normal vector on
\partial D
. We also define the so-called Neumann–Poincaré (NP) operator

\mathcal{K}_{\partial D}[\varphi](x)=p.v.\,\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{y}}\Gamma(x-y)\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\partial D,

and its
L^{2}
-adjoint operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}[\varphi](x)=p.v.\,\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{x}}\Gamma(x-y)\varphi(y)\,d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\partial D.

Here,
p.v.
stands for the Cauchy principal value. The operator
\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\partial D)
.
We identify
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
z=x_{1}+\mathrm{i}x_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.
We set

\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z):=\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi](x).

Likewise, we define
\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z)
and
\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z)
.

On the interface
\partial D
, the layer potentials satisfy the jump relations

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi]\Big|^{+}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi]\Big|^{-},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[\varphi]\Big|^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left(\pm\frac{1}{2}I+\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}\right)[\varphi],

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}[\varphi]\Big|^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left(\mp\frac{1}{2}I+\mathcal{K}_{\partial D}\right)[\varphi],

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}[\varphi]\Big|^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}[\varphi]\Big|^{-}.

Here,
I
denotes the identity operator on
L^{2}(\partial D)
.

Let
\Re[\cdot]
and
\Im[\cdot]
denote the real and imaginary part of a complex number, respectively. The solution
u
of the system (
2.1
) can be represented by

(2.2)

u(z)=H(z)+\left(\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}\,p-\tau\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}\right)[\varphi](z),\quad(\Re(z),\Im(z))\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus{\partial D}

with
\lambda=\frac{\sigma_{c}+\sigma_{m}}{2(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})}
and
\tau=\frac{\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}}{\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m}},

where
p
denotes the multiplication operator by
p
(by abuse of the notation) and the density function
\varphi
is the solution to the integral equation

(2.3)

\left(\left(\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}\right)p+\tau\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}\right)\left[\varphi\right]=\frac{\partial H}{\partial\nu}.

This representation was introduced in
[
27
]
for the case
\sigma_{c}=\infty
(that is,
\tau=1
) with arbitrary smooth function
p>0
.
Following the proof in
[
27
]
, one can show that the operator
\left(\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}\right)p+\tau\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}
is invertible for
0<\sigma\neq 1<\infty
.
By the jump relations, the solution (
2.2
) meets all conditions of the system (
2.1
).

Definition 1
.

Set
Z_{n}(\eta)=\eta^{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(1)}(D,p,\sigma_{c},\sigma_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\bigg(Z_{n}(\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial Z_{n}(\eta)}{\partial\nu}\bigg)\bigg((\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D})p+\tau\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}}{\partial\nu}\bigg)^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial Z_{m}}{\partial\nu}\right](\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta),

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(2)}(D,p,\sigma_{c},\sigma_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\bigg(Z_{n}(\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial Z_{n}(\eta)}{\partial\nu}\bigg)\bigg((\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D})p+\tau\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}}{\partial\nu}\bigg)^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial\overline{Z_{m}}}{\partial\nu}\right](\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta),

where
\lambda=\frac{\sigma_{c}+\sigma_{m}}{2(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})}
and
\tau=\frac{\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}}{\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m}}
.
We call
\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(1)}
and
\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(2)}
the (complex) generalized polarization tensors (GPTs) corresponding to the inclusion
D
with the interface function
p
and the conductivities
\sigma_{c}
,
\sigma_{m}
.

The GPTs generalize the polarization tensor (PT) that was introduced by Schiffer and Szegö
[
38
]
. The concept of the PT, the GPTs for
m=n=1
, is applicable to the potential problem with imperfect interface condition.

2.2
Multipole expansion

Using this Taylor expansion, the solution expression (
2.2
) and the definition of the GPTs, one can show the following multipole expansion for the inclusion problem with imperfect interface (refer to
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
42
]
for the perfect interface problem).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
u
be the solution to (
2.1
) for
H(z)=\Re\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{m}z^{m}\right]
with complex coefficients
\alpha_{m}
. Let
\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(1)}
and
\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(2)}
be given by Definition
1
. We have

\displaystyle u(z)=H(z)-\Re\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4\pi n}\left(\alpha_{m}\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(1)}+\overline{\alpha_{m}}\,\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(2)}\right){z^{-n}}\right],\quad|z|>\sup\left\{|y|:y\in D\right\}.

Proof.

By the Taylor expansion of the logarithmic function, we get as
|z|\to\infty
,

\Gamma(z-\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Re\left[\ln(z-\eta)\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Re\bigg[\ln z-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{\eta^{n}}{z^{n}}\bigg]=\Gamma(z)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi n}\Re\left[Z_{n}(\eta)\,z^{-n}\right].

Using this expansion and (
2.2
), the solution
u
satisfies

\displaystyle u(z)

\displaystyle=H(z)+\mathcal{S}_{\partial D}[p\varphi](z)-\tau\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}[\varphi](z)

\displaystyle=H(z)+\int_{\partial D}\left[\Gamma(z-\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial\Gamma(z-\eta)}{\partial\nu_{\eta}}\right]\varphi(\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta)

\displaystyle=H(z)+\int_{\partial D}\Gamma(z)\,p(\eta)\,\varphi(\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-n}}{2\pi n}\int_{\partial D}\Re\left[Z_{n}(\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial Z_{n}(\eta)}{\partial\nu_{\eta}}\right]\varphi(\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=H(z)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{-n}}{2\pi n}\int_{\partial D}\Re\left[Z_{n}(\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial Z_{n}(\eta)}{\partial\nu_{\eta}}\right]\varphi(\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta).

The last equation holds because
\int_{\partial D}p(\eta)\,\varphi(\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta)=0
. Due to (
2.3
), we have

\varphi=\left(\left(\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D}\right)p+\tau\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}\right)^{-1}\left[\Re\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{m}\frac{\partial Z_{m}}{\partial\nu}\right)\right],

and substituting this
\varphi
into (
2.4
) leads the desired result.

\Box

In
[
27
]
, the interface function
p
was constructed such that the corresponding PT, i.e.,
\mathbb{N}_{11}^{(1)}
and
\mathbb{N}_{11}^{(2)}
, vanishes under the assumption that
\sigma_{c}=\infty
(that is, the core is occupied with perfectly conducting material).

In the present paper, we construct the interface function
p
such that the GPTs vanish up to some finite orders (including the PT), where the core
D
is a given domain with arbitrary constant conductivity
\sigma_{c}
.
In view of Proposition
2.1
, the resulting inclusion is a neutral inclusion in the asymptotic sense.
For the construction of
p
, we will apply the complex potential approach in
[
11
]
, where the concept of the Faber polynomial polarization tensors was employed to build semi-neutral inclusions of general shape.

3
Faber polynomial polarization tensors (FPTs)

From the Riemann mapping theorem, there uniquely exist
\gamma>0
and conformal mapping
\Psi
from
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|>\gamma\}
onto
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{D}
such that

(3.1)

\Psi(w)=w+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{w}+\frac{a_{2}}{w^{2}}+\cdots.

The quantity
\gamma
is called the conformal radius of
D
.
One can numerically compute the numerical computation of
a_{n}
from a given parametrization of
\partial\Omega
(see, for instance,
[
24
]
). As an univalent function,
\Psi
defines the Faber polynomials
\{F_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
by the relation
[
13
]

(3.2)

\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(w)}{\Psi(w)-z}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{F_{m}(z)}{w^{m+1}},\quad z\in{\overline{D}},\ |w|>\gamma.

The Faber polynomials
F_{m}
are monic polynomials of degree
m
that are uniquely determined by the conformal mapping coefficients
\{a_{n}\}_{0\leq n\leq m-1}
via the recursive relation
[
12
]
:

(3.3)

F_{m+1}(z)=zF_{m}(z)-ma_{m}-\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{n}F_{m-n}(z),\quad m\geq 0.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle F_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=1,\quad F_{1}(z)=z-a_{0},\quad F_{2}(z)=z^{2}-2a_{0}z+a_{0}^{2}-2a_{1},

\displaystyle F_{3}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{3}-3a_{0}z^{2}+3(a_{0}^{2}-a_{1})z-a_{0}^{3}+3a_{0}a_{1}-3a_{2}.

For
z=\Psi(w)
with
|w|>r
, it holds that

(3.4)

F_{m}(\Psi(w))=w^{m}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{mn}{w^{-n}}

with the so-called Grunsky coefficient
c_{mn}
.
Recursive relations for the Faber polynomial coefficients and the Grunsky coefficients are well-known (see, for instance,
[
12
,
17
]
).

We introduce the modified polar coordinates
(\rho,\theta)\in[\rho_{0},\infty)\times[0,2\pi)
,
\rho_{0}=\ln\gamma
, for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus D
via the relation

z=\Psi(w)=\Psi(e^{\rho+i\theta}).

We denote the scale factor as
h=\left|\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\rho}\right|=\left|\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\theta}\right|=e^{\rho}\left|\Psi^{\prime}\right|.
One can easily see that
d\sigma(z)=h(\rho_{0},\theta)d\theta
on
\partial D
. For
z=\Psi(w)\in\partial D
, we have

(3.5)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|_{\partial D}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{h(\rho_{0},\theta)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}u(\Psi(w))\Big|_{\rho\rightarrow\rho_{0}^{\pm}}.

We assume that
\Omega
has an analytic boundary, that is,
\Psi
can be conformally extended to
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|>\gamma-\delta\}
for some
\delta>0
. Then (
3.2
) holds with a modified conformal radius and domain. The Grunsky coefficients satisfy the so-called
strong and week Grunsky inequalities
. In particular, it holds that

\left|c_{mn}\right|\leq 2m\gamma^{m+n}.

The same relation holds with
\gamma-\delta
instead of
\gamma
, that is,

(3.6)

\left|c_{mn}\right|\leq 2m(\gamma-\delta)^{m+n}.

The Faber polynomials form a basis for complex analytic functions in
D

[
12
]
.
Let
v
be a complex analytic function in
D
and continuous in
\overline{D}
. By applying the Cauchy integral formula to
v
and applying (
3.2
), it follows that

v(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}F_{m}(z)\quad\mbox{in }D

with

b_{m}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=\gamma}\frac{v(\Psi(w))}{w^{m+1}}\,dw.

Hence, we have

(3.7)

|b_{m}|\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial D)}\gamma^{-m}.

3.1
FPTs and FPTs-vanishing structure

Since the Faber polynomials form a basis for complex analytic functions,
H
is given by

(3.8)

H(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Re\big[\alpha_{m}F_{m}(z)\big]

with some complex coefficients
\alpha_{m}
.

Let
u
be the solution to the imperfect interface problem (
2.1
). We can expand
u\big|_{D}
into the real parts of the Faber polynomials. Furthermore, as the solution to (
2.1
) linearly depends on the background solution
H
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle u(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Re\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{mn}F_{n}(z)\bigg]\quad\mbox{in }D

with some constants
\beta_{mn}
.
As
u-H
is decaying near infinity and
\Psi
is conformal, it holds that for
z=\Psi(w)
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle u(z)=H(z)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Re\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{mn}w^{-n}\bigg]\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}

with some constants
s_{mn}
. Following
[
11
]
, we call the expansion in (
3.10
) the
geometric multipole expansion
of
u
.
For each
m
, both
\beta_{mn}
and
s_{mn}
are determined by
\alpha_{m}
. In particular,
\beta_{mn}=s_{mn}=0
if we give
\alpha_{m}=0
for some
m
in (
3.8
). In fact, we can decompose the coefficient
s_{mn}
by using some quantities defined as below.

Definition 2
.

Let
F_{n}
be the Faber polynomial for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(1)}(D,p,\sigma_{c},\sigma_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\bigg(F_{n}(\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial F_{n}(\eta)}{\partial\nu}\bigg)\bigg((\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D})p+\tau\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}}{\partial\nu}\bigg)^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial F_{m}}{\partial\nu}\right](\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta),

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(2)}(D,p,\sigma_{c},\sigma_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial D}\bigg(F_{n}(\eta)\,p(\eta)-\tau\frac{\partial F_{n}(\eta)}{\partial\nu}\bigg)\bigg((\lambda I-\mathcal{K}^{*}_{\partial D})p+\tau\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}_{\partial D}}{\partial\nu}\bigg)^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial\overline{F_{m}}}{\partial\nu}\right](\eta)\,d\sigma(\eta),

where
\lambda=\frac{\sigma_{c}+\sigma_{m}}{2(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})}
and
\tau=\frac{\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}}{\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m}}
. We call
\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(1)}
and
\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(2)}
the Faber polynomial polarization tensors (FPTs) for an inclusion with an imperfect interface, by extending the definition for an inclusion with perfect interface conditions in
[
11
]
.

For
z=\Psi(w)
, integrating the expansion (
3.2
) with respect to
w
yields that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(z-\eta)=-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{F_{m}(\eta)}{2\pi m}\frac{1}{w^{m}}+\text{constant},\quad\eta\in{\overline{D}},\ |w|>\gamma.

By using the linear dependence of
u
on
H
and the above series expansion, we can decompose
s_{mn}
as

(3.11)

s_{mn}=-\alpha_{m}\frac{\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(1)}}{4\pi n}-\overline{\alpha_{m}}\,\frac{\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(2)}}{4\pi n},

where
\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(1)}
and
\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(2)}
are the FPTs independent of
\alpha_{m}
, namely,

(3.12)

\displaystyle u(z)=H(z)-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Re\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4\pi n}\left(\alpha_{m}\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(1)}+\overline{\alpha_{m}}\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(2)}\right)w^{-n}\bigg]\quad\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D},

where
H
is given by an entire Faber series
H(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\Re\big[\alpha_{m}F_{m}(z)\big]
. We omit the derivation of (
3.12
) because it is very similar with the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

The first-order terms of the FPTs correspond to the PTs considered in
[
27
]
.
More precisely, the polarization tensor
M=\{m_{ij}\}_{i,j=1,2}
admits the expression

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m_{11}&m_{12}\\
m_{21}&m_{22}\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\Re\left[\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(1)}+\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(2)}\right]&\displaystyle\Im\left[\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(1)}+\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(2)}\right]\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\Im\left[\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(1)}-\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(2)}\right]&\displaystyle\Re\left[\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(2)}-\mathbb{F}_{11}^{(1)}\right]\end{array}\right).

We can express the Faber polynomial
F_{m}(z)
as

(3.13)

F_{m}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}q_{mn}z^{n},

where for a fixed
m
, the coefficient
q_{mn}
depends only on
\{a_{k}\}_{0\leq k\leq m-1}
. One can easily obtain recursive formulas for
q_{mn}
from (
3.3
).
From (
3.13
) and the definition of the FPTs, it holds for each
m,n
that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{n}q_{mk}\,q_{nl}\,\mathbb{N}_{kl}^{(1)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{mn}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\overline{q_{mk}}\,q_{nl}\,\mathbb{N}_{kl}^{(2)}.

From the relations of the GPTs and FPTs, we can then obtain matrix factorizations for the GPTs.
Set
Q=(q_{mn})_{m,n=1}^{\infty}
with
q_{mn}
given by (
3.13
).
We can rewrite relations (
3.14
) in matrix form as

\displaystyle B_{2}&=(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})\gamma^{-\mathcal{N}}P^{-}\gamma^{\mathcal{N}}+\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}\,\mathcal{N},\end{cases}

which gives
A_{1}=\gamma^{2\mathcal{N}}\overline{B_{1}}
and
A_{2}=\gamma^{2\mathcal{N}}\overline{B_{2}}-2\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}\gamma^{2\mathcal{N}}\mathcal{N}
. It then follows from Theorem
4.5
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(1)}=8\pi mn\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}\gamma^{2m}\left[\overline{B_{2}}^{-1}\overline{B_{1}}\left(B_{2}-B_{1}\overline{B_{2}}^{-1}\overline{B_{1}}\right)^{-1}\right]_{mn},

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(2)}=4\pi n\gamma^{2m}\delta_{mn}-8\pi mn\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}\gamma^{2m}\left[\left(B_{2}-B_{1}\overline{B_{2}}^{-1}\overline{B_{1}}\right)^{-1}\right]_{mn}.

If we set
p_{n}=0
for all
n\neq 0
, then
P^{+}=O
and
P^{-}=p_{0}I
, and thus,

B_{1}=O,\quad B_{2}=(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})p_{0}I+\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}\mathcal{N}.

We then get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

For a disk
D
, if
p_{n}=0
for all
n\neq 0
, namely, if the interface function is constant, then GPTs of the interface problem (
2.1
) are

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(1)}=0,\quad\mathbb{N}_{mn}^{(2)}=4\pi n\gamma^{2m}\delta_{mn}\frac{(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})p_{0}-\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}m}{(\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m})p_{0}+\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}m}.

By this corollary,
\mathbb{N}_{1n}^{(1)}=\mathbb{N}_{1n}^{(2)}=0
for all
n\geq 1
if

p_{0}=\frac{\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}}{\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m}}.

In other words, by using (
4.11
) and
h(\rho_{0},\theta)=\gamma
for the disk
\Omega
, if we set
p
as

p=\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\sigma_{c}\sigma_{m}}{\sigma_{c}-\sigma_{m}}

the inclusion is perfectly neutral and does not perturb the incident field at all when the incident field is unform
[
41
]
.

5
GPT-vanishing inclusions with imperfect interfaces

The computation in this section is very similiar with the numerical part in
[
11
]
. However, main difference is that we iterate the coefficients of the interface function instead of conductivites of inclusions.
We utilize FPTs to construct imperfect interfaces that vanish GPTs. Specifically, we determine the interface condition function
p
for a given pair
(D,\sigma_{c})
such that FPTs vanish up to a finite order. Then, GPTs would also vanish up to the same finite order.

5.1
Numerical scheme

For a given
D
, we use the quantities
\gamma
,
\Psi(w)
, and
(\rho,\theta)
as defined in Section
3
. The interface function,
p
, and its coefficient matrices,
P^{+}
and
P^{-}
, are defined by (
4.11
), (
4.15
), and Figure
4.1
. Let us assume that
\sigma_{m}=1
and
\sigma_{c}
is fixed. The incident field is assumed to be a uniform field, which means that we only need to consider FPTs for
m=1
.

Our objective is to find
\boldsymbol{p}=(p_{0},\dots,p_{N-1})
such that the leading terms of the FPTs vanish. To accomplish this, we aim to solve the following equation:

(5.1)

\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{p})=\boldsymbol{0},

where
\boldsymbol{f}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{2N}
is a nonlinear vector function mapping
\boldsymbol{p}\longmapsto(f_{1},...,f_{2N})
. Here,
f_{n}
is given by:

\displaystyle f_{n}=\begin{cases}&\left|\dfrac{\mathbb{F}^{(1)}_{1n}}{4\pi n}\right|,\quad 1\leq n\leq N,\\[14.22636pt]
&\left|\dfrac{\mathbb{F}^{(2)}_{1n}}{4\pi n}\right|,\quad N+1\leq n\leq 2N,\end{cases}

In order to achieve
\boldsymbol{p}
with (
5.1
), we iterate the multidimensional Newton method:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{p}^{(k+1)}=\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)}-\alpha\,\boldsymbol{J}^{\dagger}\big[\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)}\big]\,\boldsymbol{f}\big[\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)}\big],

where the learning rate is denoted by
\alpha
, while
k
represents the iteration number. Additionally,
\boldsymbol{J}^{\dagger}
refers to the pseudo-inverse of the Jacobian matrix of
\boldsymbol{f}
. We start with seed input
\boldsymbol{p}^{(0)}=(p_{0}^{(0)},\dots,p_{N-1}^{(0)})
, with each coefficient having a magnitude less than 2.

We use Theorem
4.4
to calculate
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)})
, truncating all semi-infinite matrices to finite matrices with size
100\times 100
. To compute
\boldsymbol{J}^{\dagger}[\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)}]
, we apply the finite difference method to the partial derivatives of
\boldsymbol{f}
based on Matlab R2022b. We continue applying the multidimensional Newton method until the coefficients of the interface function meet the stopping criterion:

\frac{|\boldsymbol{p}^{(k+1)}-\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)}|}{|\boldsymbol{p}^{(k)}|}<10^{-15}.

We give three different boundary conditions that are perfectly bonding as (

For perfectly bonding case, we set

\sigma_{m}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|^{+}=\sigma_{c}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Big|^{-}=p(u\big|^{+}-u\big|^{-})\qquad\mbox{on }\partial D

is equivalent to

(5.3)

which are the typical transmission conditions.

For GPTs of order 1 vanishing case, namely, PT-vanishing structure, we give

(5.4)

P(w)=p_{0}+p_{2}w^{2}+\overline{p_{2}}\gamma^{4}w^{-2}.

Since
p_{0}
and
p_{2}
are free variables, we have the degree of freedom as 3. This is enough to vanish two quantities and we could make the weakly neutral inclusion satisfying
\mathbb{N}_{11}^{(1)}=\mathbb{N}_{11}^{(2)}\approx 0
.

For GPTs of order up to 2 vanishing structure, we give

(5.5)

P(w)=p_{0}+p_{1}w+\overline{p_{1}}\gamma^{2}w^{-1}+p_{2}w^{2}+\overline{p_{2}}\gamma^{4}w^{-2}+p_{3}w^{3}+\overline{p_{3}}\gamma^{6}w^{-3}.

Now we have four free variables, which enable us to control four quantities that vanish. Thus, provided that
P
is given as (
5.5
), we can make the GPTs of order up to 2 vanishing structures satisfying
\mathbb{N}_{11}^{(1)}=\mathbb{N}_{11}^{(2)}=\mathbb{N}_{12}^{(1)}=\mathbb{N}_{12}^{(2)}\approx 0
. This is quite surprising because the solution
u
in (
2.1
) could decay faster than the weakly neutral inclusion as follows:

u(x)=a\cdot x+O(|x|^{-3}),\quad|x|\to\infty.

6
Conclusion

We propose a novel concept of GPT-vanishing structures for inclusions with arbitrary conductivity and imperfect interfaces. We show that inclusions of general shape can achieve nearly neutral behavior for the background field by carefully selecting a proper imperfect interface parameter. We employ exterior conformal mappings and FPT techniques to compute the coefficients of the interface parameter. Furthermore, we provide two distinct examples that highlight the effect of the vanishing FPTs, demonstrating the practical implications of our proposed concept.

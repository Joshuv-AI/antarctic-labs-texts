Introduction

Let
n\geq 3
be the dimension,
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
,
\mu=\pm 1
, and
k>0
. We are interested in the solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
of the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equations (shortened as INLS) defined as below. When
k>1
, we would want to assume a weaker initial condition
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is explained later in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

2.2
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &iu_{t}+\Delta u=\mu|x|^{-b}|u|^{k}u\\
&u(0)=\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

The equation (
1.1
) is a model for mainly two physical topics: the study of diluted Bose-Einstein condensation with two- and three-body interactions considered and the study of laser guiding in an axially non-uniform plasma channel. (See
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
23
]
, and
[
35
]
for detail.)
INLS thus has many general forms, but we limit our attention to a relatively simple case of having one nonlinear power with a smooth (possibly except at origin) decaying multiplier, especially
|x|^{-b}
.

We start with introducing common terminologies in the studies of nonlinear Schrödinger equations and their relatives.
The time interval
I
in which the solution
u
is defined is called its
lifespan
.
For the sign parameter
\mu
, the equation (
1.1
) is said to be
defocusing
if
\mu=+1
and
focusing
if
\mu=-1
.
For the nonlinearity power parameter
k
, we consider together a value called the
critical regularity
defined by
\gamma_{c}=n/2-(2-b)/k
. We note that both the equation (
1.1
) and the solution’s
\dot{H}_{x}^{\gamma_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm are preserved under the
scaling
transformation

u(t,x)~\mapsto~u_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda^{\frac{n-2\gamma_{c}}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\quad(\lambda>0).

We also introduce two main quantities conserved throughout the lifespan of the solution
u\in C_{t}(I,H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. One is the
mass

M[u(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|u(t,x)\big|^{2}dx

and the other is the
energy

E[u(t)]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\big|\nabla u(t,x)\big|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{k+2}|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{k+2}~dx.

Based on the fact that
M[u(t)]^{1-\gamma_{c}}E[u(t)]^{\gamma_{c}}
is preserved under the scaling, we say that (
1.1
) is
mass-critical
if
\gamma_{c}=0
,
energy-critical
if
\gamma_{c}=1
, and
intercritical
if
0<\gamma_{c}<1
.

We lastly note one
coercivity
property for the defocusing case.
It is the uniform boundedness of the solution’s
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm in time, both above and below, in terms of the energy
E[\phi]
.
More precisely, every solution
u\in C_{t}(I,\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
of (
1.1
) with initial data
\phi
satisfies the bounds

c\min(E[\phi],E[\phi]^{c^{\prime}})\leq\left\|{u(t)}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\max(E[\phi],E[\phi]^{C^{\prime}})

for every
t\in I
, where
0<c<C
and
0<c^{\prime}<1<C^{\prime}
are absolute constants depending only on
n
and
b
.
(As a side note, the focusing case also has a similar formulation for coercivity, but this is valid only when
E[\phi]
is below a threshold.)

In this paper, our main interest lies in the case
\mu=+1
and
k=(4-2b)/(n-2)
, and hence we have
defocusing energy-critical INLS
as featured in the title. From now on, we limit our view to the defocusing energy-critical case unless stated otherwise.

When
b=0
, (
1.1
) turns into the usual nonlinear Schrödinger equations (shortened as NLS), whose global existence and scattering results have been studied extensively for decades.
We mainly look at the defocusing energy-critical NLS as it is one of the two main inspirations for our result.
For the radial data, Bourgain
[

For the general data, Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka, and Tao
[

[

resolved

As a side note, we also look at some notable results for other parameter setups of NLS.
For the focusing energy-critical case, Kenig and Merle

For the focusing intercritical case, Dinh

For the defocusing energy-supercritical case, Bulut

Regarding these backgrounds, we present the following two main theorems. In Theorem
1.1
, we estimate a solution’s spacetime norms for the defocusing energy-critical ILNS (

We note that the spacetime norm estimate in Theorem

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer and
b>0
obey the following range restriction.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &0<b<3/2&&\text{if }n=3,\\
&0<b<1&&\text{if }n=4,\\
&0<b<1/2\text{\> or \>}5/4<b<2&&\text{if }n=5,\\
&0<b<2&&\text{if }n\geq 6.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
[T_{-},T_{+}]
be a compact interval, and let
u
be the unique spherically symmetric solution of (
1.1
) associated with the initial data
\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and lying in the solution space

C_{t}([T_{-},T_{+}],H_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L_{t}^{p_{*}}([T_{-},T_{+}],W_{x}^{1;q_{*},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

where
p_{*}=2(n+2)/(n-2+2b)
and

. Then we have

\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([T_{-},T_{+}],L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq C\exp(CE^{C})

where
E=E[\phi]
is the solution’s energy and

are absolute constants depending only on
n
and
b
.

In particular, if
n=3,4,5
and
0<b<(6-n)/2
, the same estimate holds when we replace the inhomogeneous Sobolev-Lorentz spaces with homogeneous ones:
we can assume

to only lie in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
in which case
the solution only lies in
C_{t}([T_{-},T_{+}],\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L_{t}^{p_{*}}([T_{-},T_{+}],\dot{W}_{x}^{1;q_{*},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n
,
b
,
p_{*}
,

and

be defined as in Theorem
1.1
. Then the solution

is global and scatters in
\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in the sense that

in fact belongs to

C_{t}(\mathbb{R},\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L_{t}^{p_{*}}(\mathbb{R},\dot{W}_{x}^{1;q_{*},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

and there exist profiles
U_{+},U_{-}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left\|{u(t)-e^{it\Delta}U_{+}}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}=\lim_{t\to-\infty}\left\|{u(t)-e^{it\Delta}U_{-}}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}=0.

Remark
.

In Theorem
1.1
, the range conditions
n=3,4,5
and
0<b<(6-n)/2
correspond to the nonlinearity power
k>1
, high enough for the discussion of well-posedness in homogeneous Sobolev-Lorentz spaces.
Theorem
1.2
can be understood as a minimal result embracing both cases

and
k\leq 1
. In fact, if the inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces are used in Theorem
1.1
, then the scattering result in

holds in Theorem
1.2
.

Ideally, we expect Theorem
1.1
to hold for every
n\geq 3
and
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
as this is the whole range where the local theory of the energy-critical case is discussed. However, the scattering problem remains open for two missing ranges
1\leq b<2
for
n=4
and
1/2\leq b\leq 5/4
for
n=5
. Both cases have the low dimension

and the low nonlinearity power

at the same time, making it challenging to apply the variant bootstrapping arguments by Tao
[
36
, Appendix]
; we will see the detailed issues in Section
4

On the bright side, we still cover most of our target parameter range, especially all of
0<b<\min(2,n/2)
when

or

.
Another interesting aspect is that we do not utilize the concentration compactness method of
[
29
]
, despite it being widespread for the scattering results of several dispersive equations nowadays.
Lastly, we can quantitatively bound the solution’s size (in spacetime norms) using only the solution’s energy and a few absolute parameters.

Throughout this paper,
C>0
and
c>0
stand for several large and small constants that depend only on the parameters
n
and
b
(unless stated otherwise). When several absolute constants exist in a single context, we may add prime symbols or subscripts for disambiguation.

To illustrate the arguments of Tao
[
36
, Section 3]
, we start with a small parameter
\eta
, which preferably depends polynomially on the solution’s energy
E
, and then divide the large time interval
[T_{-},T_{+}]
into many smaller subintervals
I_{1},I_{2},\cdots,I_{J}
such that
L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(I_{j},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})
norm of the solution

is comparable to
\eta^{c}
for every
j=1,\cdots,J
.
Later, it happens that one of the two scenarios happens for every
j
:

1.

At least one of the two long-time approximants
u_{\pm}=e^{i(t-T\pm)\Delta}u(T_{\pm})
has significantly large
L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(I_{j},L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}})
norm, especially greater than
\eta^{C_{1}}
.

2.

There exists a point
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the solution forms a bubble of concentration on the spacetime slab
I_{j}\times B(x_{j},C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2})
. In particular,
L_{x}^{2}
norm of
u(t)
is bounded below by
c\eta^{CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}
uniformly in
t\in I_{j}
.

There are only a finite number of subintervals
I_{j}
falling in the first scenario, called
bad
(or
exceptional
) subintervals in
[
36
]
and this paper. In particular, that number is polynomially bounded in
1/\eta
and thus eventually negligible.
For all the other
I_{j}
, called
good
(or
unexceptional
) intervals, we can take
x_{j}=0
thanks to the radial data assumption. The Morawetz inequality of
[
5
]
has a variant for the defocusing energy-critical INLS, so we can extract a finite sequence of good subintervals rapidly concentrating to a single time. However, since the approximate local mass conservation law provides a lower bound for the local mass in each bubble of concentration, if
J
is too large (beyond an exponential bound in
E
), then the summation of the local masses leads to a contradiction against the energy conservation law after Hölder’s inequality.

As the distinction from
[
36
]
, we select the solution spaces differently from the usual
L^{p}
-based ones. Unlike NLS, INLS has a
L^{p,\infty}
type multiplier

. Since we work on the endpoint regularity of

data assumption, it is necessary to use Lorentz spaces as in
[
2
]
or weighted Sobolev spaces as in
[
12
]
.

Lorentz spaces have two significant occurrences in this paper: a priori estimates and “
h
-difference” estimates.
A priori estimates are relatively straightforward due to the good properties of Lebesgue spaces shared with Lorentz spaces, and we only have to choose appropriate exponents.
The
h
-difference estimates refer to estimating the forms
f(x+h)-f(x)
, where

is small and

is any sufficiently smooth function in
x
relevant to INLS, such as the multiplier
|x|^{-b}
or the solution
u(t,x)
at a fixed time
t
.
The real interpolation of Lorentz spaces and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus are helpful for these estimates.

Most of the difficulties in this paper lie in a technical bootstrapping lemma, Lemma
3.2
.
The usual approach as in the short proof of
[
36
, Lemma 3.2]
works only for high nonlinearity powers (
k>1
, i.e.

), and this is where
[
36
, Appendix]
comes in.
After differentiating (
1.1
) in

, we find a way to bootstrap

by Neumann series approximation.
To make use of the arguments’ iterative nature, one special lemma (Lemma

) is necessary, which the technical conditions

and

for the remaining case

arise from and explains the strange-looking loss of ranges in

2
Local Well-posedness and Other Basic Estimates

2.1
Definitions and Properties of Sobolev-Lorentz Spaces

We start with some basic definitions for Lorentz spaces, Sobolev-Lorentz spaces, the Lorentz-type Strichartz norms, and the scattering behavior of a solution. For the differential operators like
|\nabla|^{\gamma}
to make sense, we also assume that all the functions in this paper are tempered distributions in the
x
variable.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty,~1\leq q\leq\infty
. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be in the
Lorentz space

L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if its
Lorentz quasinorm

\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{-1/p}f^{*}(t)\big)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/q}

is finite, where
f^{*}
is the decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R},~1<p<\infty,~1\leq q\leq\infty
. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be in the
homogeneous Sobolev-Lorentz space

\dot{W}^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\left\|{f}\right\|_{\dot{W}^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\big\||\nabla|^{\gamma}f\big\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,

or in the
inhomogeneous Sobolev-Lorentz space

W^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\left\|{f}\right\|_{W^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\big\|\langle\nabla\rangle^{\gamma}f\big\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty

where
\langle x\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
, and
2\leq q<\infty
. A pair
(p,q)
is said to be
\dot{H}^{\gamma}
-
admissible
if
\dfrac{2}{p}+\dfrac{n}{q}=\dfrac{n}{2}-\gamma
, and the
Strichartz space

\dot{S}^{\gamma}(I)
refers to the set of all functions
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
whose
Strichartz norms
, defined as below, are finite. (Usual modification if
p=\infty
.)

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{S}^{\gamma}(I)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\text{$L^{2}$-admissible }(p,q)}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{t}^{p}(I,\dot{W}_{x}^{\gamma;q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\text{$L^{2}$-admissible }(p,q)}\Big(\int_{I}\big\||\nabla|^{\gamma}u(t)\big\|_{L_{x}^{q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\,dt\Big)^{1/p}

Remark
.

Lorentz spaces
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are reflexive for every
1<p<\infty
and
1<q<\infty
, and thus so are Sobolev-Lorentz spaces
\dot{W}^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

[

When

.
In the notation of norms, we often omit

We lastly note the following equivalent characterizations of Sobolev-Lorentz spaces for positive integers

\displaystyle c\leq\frac{\left\|{\nabla^{\gamma}f}\right\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{\dot{W}^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\leq C\text{\quad for every $0\neq f\in\dot{W}^{\gamma;p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$},

which can be derived from the real interpolation on Hörmander-Mikhlin multiplier theorem.

We now look at several norm inequalities widely known for Lebesgue spaces. Apart from possibly worse proportionality constants, the inequalities work for Lorentz spaces too.

Proposition 2.1

(Hölder’s inequality;
[
30

,
[

)
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a
\sigma

1<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
, and

1<p=\dfrac{1}{1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}}<\infty,\quad 1\leq s=\dfrac{1}{1/s_{1}+1/s_{2}}\leq\infty.

Then for every

\left\|{fg}\right\|_{L^{p,s}(X)}\leq C\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p_{1},s_{1}}(X)}\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{p_{2},s_{2}}(X)}

where

,
p_{2}
,
s_{1}
, and

.

(Sobolev embeddings;
[

)

Let

\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{\tilde{p},s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|{f}\right\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha;p,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where

is an absolute constant depending only on

,
s
,

, and
\alpha
.

Proposition 2.3

(Strichartz estimates;
[

)
.

(i)

Let

\left\|{e^{it\Delta}f}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C|t|^{-n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime},2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

Morawetz Inequality for INLS

This subsection extends Morawetz inequality introduced by Bourgain

We start with the following variant of Morawetz identity.

Lemma 2.6

(Morawetz identity for INLS)
.

Let
V,a:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be two non-negative smooth functions with compact support.
Suppose that
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is a solution of the equation

iu_{t}+\Delta u=V(x)|u|^{k}u

and define

Z(t)=2\,\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla a~dx.

Then the following identity holds.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z(t)

\displaystyle=4\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{Re}(\overline{\partial_{x_{j}}u}{\partial_{x_{k}}u})\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}a~dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{2}\Delta^{2}a~dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2k}{k+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V|u|^{k+2}\Delta a~dx-\frac{4}{k+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{k+2}\,\nabla V\cdot\nabla a~dx

To construct an inequality from this identity, choose
V(x)=|x|^{-b}
and
a(x)=(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}\chi(x)
, where
\epsilon>0
is small and
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a radially decreasing bump function with
\chi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
\chi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
.
We observe the convexity of
a
on
|x|\leq 1
, the non-negativity of the following three functions on
|x|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle x\cdot\nabla a

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\Delta a

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{3/2}}

\displaystyle-\Delta^{2}a

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{3/2}}+\frac{6(n-3)\epsilon^{2}}{(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{5/2}}+\frac{15\epsilon^{4}}{(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{7/2}}

and the uniform boundedness of
a
and its derivatives on
1\leq|x|\leq 2
with respect to
\epsilon
. Ignoring some positive terms, we see that

\partial_{t}Z(t)\geq c\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{|u|^{k+2}}{|x|^{b}(|x|^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}dx-CE^{C}.

Integrating this in time, using the scaling invariance, and sending
\epsilon\to 0
, we obtain the following variant of Morawetz inequality.

Lemma 2.7

(Morawetz inequality for INLS, localized in space)

Let

be a solution for the equation (

).
Then we have the inequality

\int_{I}\int_{|x|\leq A|I|^{1/2}}\frac{|u|^{2(n-b)/(n-2)}}{|x|^{b+1}}dx\,dt\leq CE^{C}A|I|^{1/2}

for every interval

and

.

Proof of Lemma

for lower dimensions.

Duhamel’s formula on

Let us consider two endpoint linear evolutions
u_{-},\,u_{+}:[T_{-},T_{+}]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
u_{\pm}(t)=e^{i(t-T_{\pm})\Delta}u(T_{\pm})
, which supposedly approximate the long-time behavior of the solution

We categorize the time subintervals

into two groups:

\int_{I_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{\pm}|^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}dx\,dt>\eta^{C_{1}}

\int_{T_{-}}^{T_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{\pm}|^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}dx\,dt\leq CE^{C},

there are at most

exceptional subintervals, meaning we only have to focus on estimating the number of good subintervals.

We now look at a proposition describing a lower bound of the solution’s local mass in a good subinterval. It is used to establish a certain relationship for the lengths of good subintervals, which later helps the estimation of

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
I_{j}
be a good subinterval. Then there exists
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathrm{Mass}(u(t);B(x_{j},C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}))\geq c\eta^{CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}

for every
t\in I_{j}
.

The radiality condition becomes essential afterward.
Adjusting the absolute constants
C
(but not
C_{0}
and
\eta
) in the proposition if necessary, we can reduce the cases to
x_{j}=0
for every good subinterval
I_{j}
by the radiality condition.
The idea is that if
|x_{j}|
is not adequately bounded, then the data becomes too large in
L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
on a disjoint union of the ball
B(x_{j},C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2})
and its selected rotations, violating the energy conservation. See
[
36
, Corollary 3.5]
for the proof.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
I_{j}
be a good subinterval and

be spherically symmetric. Then

\mathrm{Mass}(u(t);B(0,C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}))\geq c\eta^{CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}

t\in I_{j}
.

Combining Corollary
3.4
and Lemma
2.7
, we deduce the following relation for the lengths of good subintervals, similar to
[
36
, Corollary 3.6]
. The proof is provided separately as the Morawetz inequality for INLS differs slightly from the case of standard NLS.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let the solution
u
be spherically symmetric. Then for any interval
I\subset[T_{-},T_{+}]
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J\\[0.75346pt]
I_{j}\subset I\mathrm{\>good}\end{subarray}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}\leq C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I|^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
R_{0}=C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}
.
Taking Hölder’s inequality on Corollary
3.4
, we have the following lower bounds for every good subinterval
I_{j}
and
R\geq R_{0}
. We especially consider the case
R=C\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I|^{1/2}
.

\displaystyle\quad\int_{I_{j}}\int_{|x|\leq R}\frac{|u|^{2(n-b)/(n-2)}}{|x|^{b+1}}dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geq|I_{j}|R_{0}^{-(b+1)}\inf_{t\in I_{j}}\int_{|x|\leq R_{0}}|u(t)|^{2(n-b)/(n-2)}dx

\displaystyle\geq|I_{j}|R_{0}^{-(b+1)}(CR_{0}^{n})^{-(2-b)/(n-2)}\inf_{t\in I_{j}}\Big(\int_{|x|\leq R_{0}}|u(t)|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{n-b}{n-2}}

\displaystyle\geq c\eta^{CC_{0}}|I_{j}|^{1/2}

Meanwhile, Lemma

yields the upper bound

\int_{I}\int_{|x|\leq R}\frac{|u|^{2(n-b)/(n-2)}}{|x|^{b+1}}dx\,dt\leq CE^{C}\eta^{-CC_{0}}|I|^{1/2}.

Combining the upper bound and the summation of lower bounds yields the claim.
∎

Proposition 3.7

Let the solution
u

n=4
, except that we do not need the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus since we have already resolved all the necessary
h
-difference forms.

We only need to look at two more things. First, for every
0<\theta<1
, we have

\left\|{|x+h|^{-b}-|x|^{-b}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+\theta},1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C|h|^{\theta}

(3.17)

where
C
is an absolute constant depending only on
n
,
b
, and
\theta
. We can find an explicit bound for the decreasing rearrangement of
|x+h|^{-b}-|x|^{-b}
to deduce (
3.17
).
Second and last, we have

\left\|{u^{(h)}-u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{3,2}}\leq C\left\|{u^{(h)}-u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{1/2}\left\|{u^{(h)}-u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{6,2}}^{1/2}\leq C(CE^{C}|h|)^{1/2}(CE^{C})^{1/2},

which comes from the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus on the
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
factor and Sobolev embedding on the
L_{x}^{6,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
factor.
This completes the proof of Lemma
3.8
.

4
Proof of Lemma
3.2
for Higher Dimensions

This last section is dedicated to applying the arguments of Tao
[
36
, Appendix]
to INLS. The usual bootstrapping arguments work directly on INLS and use Duhamel’s formula singly, while these variant arguments work on the first spatial derivative of INLS and use Duhamel’s formula iteratively by Neumann approximation. In this way, the previously unreachable higher-dimensional parameter setups,
n+2b\geq 6
, become reachable.

When
n=4
or
5
, the effect of having both the low dimension and the low nonlinearity power leads to some technical difficulties; it is only possible to additionally resolve
5/4<b<2
for
n=5
, and never for
n=4
. For
n\geq 6
, the dimension is high enough to eliminate the negative effect for every
0<b<2
.

We start with differentiating (
1.1
) once in
x
and obtain the following equation.
Compared to the standard NLS, we work with one more nonlinear term due to the non-constant multiplier
|x|^{-b}
.

i\partial_{t}\nabla u+\Delta\nabla u=-b|x|^{-(b+2)}x\cdot|u|^{k}u+\frac{k+2}{2}|x|^{-b}|u|^{k}\nabla u+\frac{k}{2}|x|^{-b}|u|^{k-2}u^{2}\overline{\nabla u}

(4.1)

Fix
u
and let
A
be a linear operator defined on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued functions
w\in\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])
by

Aw(t,x):=-i\int_{t_{1}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}A_{0}w(s)\,ds

where
A_{0}w
is defined as below. Note that
A_{0}\nabla u
is exactly the right side of (
4.1
).

\displaystyle A_{0}w:=A_{0}^{1}w+A_{0}^{2}w+A_{0}^{2^{\prime}}w

\displaystyle\bigg(A_{0}^{1}w:=b|x|^{-(b+2)}x|u|^{k}\frac{1}{|\nabla|^{2}}\nabla\cdot w,\quad A_{0}^{2}w:=\frac{k+2}{2}|x|^{-b}|u|^{k}w,\quad A_{0}^{2^{\prime}}w:=\frac{k}{2}|x|^{-b}|u|^{k-2}u^{2}\overline{w}\bigg)

Recalling the assumption
u_{1}(t,x)=e^{i(t-t_{1})\Delta}u(t_{1})
, we have a simplified Duhamel’s formula

\nabla u=\nabla u_{1}+A\nabla u

by the definition of
A
. We also observe that
A
is a contraction mapping on
\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])
in view of the estimate

\left\|{Aw}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}\leq C\eta^{\frac{2-b}{n+2}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}.

(4.2)

The contraction property comes from the following estimate of
A_{0}w
followed by Strichartz estimates. To understand negative Sobolev-Lorentz spaces and the norm bounds in
\eta
, we recall the
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness of Riesz transforms (
1<p<\infty
) and the assumption (
3.2
) respectively. Reusing the exponents
p_{\beta}
,
q_{\beta}
, and
\tilde{q}_{\beta}
defined in Subsection
2.2
, we see that

\displaystyle\quad\left\|{A_{0}w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([t_{1},t_{2}];L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n+2},2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{|x|^{-(b+1)}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+1},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{0}}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\tilde{q}_{0}})}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{b}}([t_{1},t_{2}],\dot{W}_{x}^{-1;\tilde{q}_{b},2})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left\|{|x|^{-b}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{0}}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\tilde{q}_{0}})}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{b}}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{q_{b},2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{\frac{2-b}{n+2}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}.

Let
M
be a positive integer. We write down the
M
th
order truncation of the Neumann series

{\nabla u-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}={A^{M}\nabla u_{1}}+{A\bigg(\nabla u-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}A^{m}\nabla u_{1}\bigg)}.

Since
\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}\leq CE^{C}
by Strichartz estimate, we bootstrap on
\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])
to have

\left\|{\nabla u-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}\leq CE^{C}(C\eta^{\frac{2-b}{n+2}})^{M}.

Let
X=L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([t_{1},t_{2}],\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Here, we take
M
as the least integer greater than or equal to
4(n+2)/(2-b)
. By Sobolev embedding
\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])\hookrightarrow X
and taking
\eta
possibly smaller, we can tidy the inequality as

\left\|{\nabla u-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}\leq C\eta.

The assumption (
3.2
) says
\left\|{\nabla u}\right\|_{X}\geq c\eta^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+2)}}
, so by subtraction we have

c\eta^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+2)}}\leq\left\|{\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\|{A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}.

(4.3)

At this point, we introduce one special lemma whose proof shall be given shortly.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
0<\theta<1
depending only on
n
and
b
such that

\left\|{Aw}\right\|_{X}\leq CE^{C}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}^{1-\theta}\left\|{w}\right\|_{X}^{\theta}.

for every
\mathbb{C}^{n}
-valued function
w\in\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])
.

We iterate Lemma
4.1
with
w=A^{m}\nabla u_{1}
over
m
. To simplify the estimating process, we note that
\left\|{A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}\leq CE^{C}
uniformly in
m
. Eventually, we see that

\left\|{A^{m}\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}\leq(CE^{C})^{(2-\theta)\sum_{\nu=0}^{m-1}\theta^{\nu}}\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}^{\theta^{m}}\leq(CE^{C})^{\frac{2-\theta}{1-\theta}}\max\Big(\!\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X},\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}^{\theta^{M}}\!\Big)

for every
m=0,1,\cdots,M-1
. Then (
4.3
) is turned into

c\eta^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+2)}}\leq M(CE^{C})^{\frac{2-\theta}{1-\theta}}\max\Big(\!\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X},\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}^{\theta^{M}}\!\Big)

and therefore, we conclude with the following estimate for small
\eta
.

\left\|{\nabla u_{1}}\right\|_{X}\geq c^{\prime}E^{-C^{\prime}}\eta^{\frac{n-2}{2(n+2)}\theta^{-M}}\geq c^{\prime\prime}\eta^{\frac{n-2}{4(n+2)}\theta^{-M}}

This finishes the proof of Lemma
3.2
, minus Lemma
4.1
.

4.1
Proof of Lemma
4.1

We start with normalization
\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}=1
and
\left\|{w}\right\|_{X}=\alpha
as in the proof of
[
36
, Lemma 4.1]
. By Sobolev embedding, we have
\alpha\leq C
. It is enough to show that
\left\|{Aw}\right\|_{X}\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta}
. Since

\displaystyle\quad\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}Aw}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}Aw}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\frac{2}{n}}\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}Aw}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n}}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\frac{2n(n+2)}{n^{2}-2n-4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}Aw}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\frac{2}{n}}\left\|{Aw}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}Aw}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([t_{1},t_{2}],L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{\frac{2}{n}}

by Hölder’s inequality, Sobolev embedding and (
4.2
), we only have to show the existence of a small constant
0<\theta^{\prime}<n/2
such that for every
t_{1}\leq t\leq t_{2}
,

\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}Aw(t)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}.

We set
t=0
by time translation and then let
v(x)=Aw(0,x)
. Let
{M}
be the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function

{M}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy.

It suffices to prove the following pointwise Hedberg-type inequality (See
[
27
]
for the inequalities involving maximal functions by Hedberg.)

\big||\nabla|^{-1}v\big|(x)\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}({M}v(x))^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

(4.4)

so that we can conclude by Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality and (
4.2
) that

\left\|{|\nabla|^{-1}v}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}\left\|{{M}v}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}.

We assume
0<{M}v(x)<\infty
, since (
4.4
) is trivial when
{M}v(x)
is either
0
or
\infty
. From here, we also set
x=0
by space translation. Let
R=({M}v(0))^{-2/n}
. We can rewrite (
4.4
) as

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v(y)}{|y|^{n-1}}dy\right|\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}R^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}.

(4.5)

We note that for every
r>0
,

\int_{B(0,r)}|v(y)|\,dy\leq C\min(R^{-n/2}r^{n},r^{n/2}).

(4.6)

One upper bound
R^{-n/2}r^{n}
comes from the definition of
{M}
, and the other
r^{n/2}
comes from Hölder’s inequality and
\left\|{v}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\left\|{Aw}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},t_{2}])}\leq 1
.
For a small absolute constant
0<c_{0}<1
, with help of (
4.6
) and the layer cake decomposition, we can prove (
4.5
) restricted on the region
|y|\leq\alpha^{c_{0}}R
or
|y|\geq\alpha^{-c_{0}}R
. Therefore, after we dyadically decompose the remaining region
\alpha^{c_{0}}R\leq|y|\leq\alpha^{-c_{0}}R
and take
c_{0}
possibly smaller, it suffices to prove that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(y)\phi\Big(\frac{y}{r}\Big)dy\right|\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}r^{n/2}

for every
r>0
, where
\phi
is a fixed real-valued bump function. We assume
r=1
as the general case can be obtained by the scaling
u(t,x)\mapsto r^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u(r^{2}t,rx)
and
w(t,x)\mapsto r^{\frac{n}{2}}w(r^{2}t,rx)
.
Expanding
v(x)=Aw(0,x)
, our goal is rewritten as

\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(A_{0}^{1}w(t)+A_{0}^{2}w(t)+A_{0}^{2^{\prime}}w(t)\big)e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\,dt\right|\leq CE^{C}\alpha^{\theta^{\prime}}.

(4.7)

We divide the left side of (
4.7
) into two time regions, the distant past
[t_{1},-\tau]
and the recent past
[-\tau,0]
, where
\tau=C\alpha^{-\theta^{\prime\prime}}
with
\theta^{\prime\prime}>0
determined later.
Estimating over the distant past is relatively easy, and even omissible when
t_{1}\geq-\tau
.
After some computations, with the exponents
p_{\beta}
,
q_{\beta}
, and
\tilde{q}_{\beta}
in Subsection
2.2
reused, we see that

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}^{1}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{|x|^{-(b+1)}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+1},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{0}}([t_{1},-\tau];L_{x}^{\tilde{q}_{0}})}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{b}}([t_{1},-\tau];\dot{W}_{x}^{-1;\tilde{q}_{b},2})}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([t_{1},-\tau];L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2},2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{\frac{2-b}{n+2}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},-\tau])}\tau^{-1/2}\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{\theta^{\prime\prime}/2}

and similarly

\displaystyle\quad\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{-\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}^{2}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{|x|^{-b}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{0}}([t_{1},-\tau];L_{x}^{\tilde{q}_{0}})}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{p_{b}}([t_{1},-\tau];L_{x}^{q_{b},2})}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{t}^{2}([t_{1},-\tau];L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2},2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{\frac{2-b}{n+2}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{S}^{0}([t_{1},-\tau])}\tau^{-1/2}\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{\theta^{\prime\prime}/2}.

The integral of
A_{0}^{2^{\prime}}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi
is estimated similarly to that of
A_{0}^{2}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi
.

It remains to prove (
4.7
) over the recent past. We modify the
h
-difference approach used in the proof of Lemma
3.8
.
Let
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and write
f^{(h)}(t,x):=f(t,x+h)
for any measurable function
f(t,x)
.
At a fixed time
-\tau\leq t\leq 0
, we integrate the
h
-difference of
A_{0}^{1}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi
but with
w
left unperturbed.
For any absolute constant
0<\delta<1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{x+h}{|x+h|^{b+2}}|u^{(h)}|^{k}(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi^{(h)}-\frac{x}{|x|^{b+2}}|u|^{k}(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi\Big)\frac{\nabla\cdot w}{|\nabla|^{2}}\,dx\right|

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\frac{x+h}{|x+h|^{b+2}}-\frac{x}{|x|^{b+2}}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+1+\delta}}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2n}{n-2}}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-4-2\delta}}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|{|x|^{-(b+1)}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+1},\infty}}\left\|{|u^{(h)}|^{k}-|u|^{k}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{k}}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2n}{n-2}}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n(n-2)}{n(n-6)+4b},1}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|{|x|^{-(b+1)}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+1},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2n}{n-2}}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}(\phi^{(h)}-\phi)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-4},1}}

\displaystyle\leq CE^{C}\langle t\rangle^{-2}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([-\tau,0],\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2n}{n-2}})}(|h|^{\delta}+|h|^{k}+|h|)

\displaystyle\leq CE^{C}\langle t\rangle^{-2}|h|^{\min(\delta,k)}

provided that
n-4-2\delta\geq 0
and
n(n-6)+4b>0
.

We note a few things to justify (
4.8
). First, we can obtain

\left\|{\frac{x+h}{|x+h|^{b+2}}-\frac{x}{|x|^{b+2}}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+1+\delta}}}\leq C(n,b,\delta)|h|^{\delta}<\infty

as a variant of (
3.17
). Second, we also observe that

\left\|{|u^{(h)}|^{k}-|u|^{k}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{k}}}\leq C\left\|{u^{(h)}-u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{k}\leq CE^{C}|h|^{k},

which comes from the pointwise inequality
\big||u^{(h)}|^{k}-|u|^{k}\big|\leq C|u^{(h)}-u|^{k}
(since
0<k\leq 1
when
n+2b\geq 6
) and by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus on
u^{(h)}-u
.
Lastly, all the factors involving
e^{it\Delta}\phi
are estimable by the dispersive estimate and the smoothness of
\phi
.
All three Lorentz space exponents associated with
e^{it\Delta}\phi
,

2n/(n-4),\ 2n/(n-4-2\delta),\text{ and }2n(n-2)/(n(n-6)+4b),

should lie in
[2n/(n-4),\infty)
to support the time decay
\langle t\rangle^{-2}
. This is possible when
0<\delta\leq 1/2
and either of
n\geq 6
with
0<b<2
or
n=5
with
5/4<b<2
holds. (
2n/(n-4-2\delta)=\infty
is permitted.) For a similar reason, our arguments in this section are discontinued when
n=4
.

We next estimate the integral of the
h
-difference of
A_{0}^{2}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi
.
Under the same conditions on
n
,
b
, and
\delta
, for every
-\tau\leq t\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{|x+h|^{b}}|u^{(h)}|^{k}(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi^{(h)}-\frac{1}{|x|^{b}}|u|^{k}(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi\Big)\overline{w}\,dx\right|

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\frac{1}{|x+h|^{b}}-\frac{1}{|x|^{b}}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b+\delta}}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-4-2\delta}}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|{|x|^{-b}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b},\infty}}\left\|{|u^{(h)}|^{k}-|u|^{k}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2}{k}}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n(n-2)}{n(n-6)+4b},1}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|{|x|^{-b}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{k}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}(\phi^{(h)}-\phi)}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-4},1}}

\displaystyle\leq CE^{C}\langle t\rangle^{-2}\left\|{w}\right\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}([-\tau,0],L_{x}^{2})}(|h|^{\delta}+|h|^{k}+|h|)

\displaystyle\leq CE^{C}\langle t\rangle^{-2}|h|^{\min(\delta,k)}.

The integral is similarly estimable when the integrand involves
A_{0}^{2^{\prime}}w
in place of
A_{0}^{2}w
.

Adding the estimates (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and its
A_{0}^{2^{\prime}}w
version, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(w-w^{(-h)})e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\right|\leq CE^{C}\langle t\rangle^{-2}|h|^{\min(\delta,k)}

for every
-\tau\leq t\leq 0
. Taking the average on
|h|<r
here, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}(w-w^{\rm av})e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\right|\leq CE^{C}r^{\min(\delta,k)}\langle t\rangle^{-2}

(4.10)

where we define
\displaystyle w^{\rm av}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi(y)w(t,x+ry)\,dy
and
0<r<1
is determined later.

Meanwhile, Young’s inequality applied to
\displaystyle w^{\rm av}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\chi\Big(\frac{y^{\prime}-x}{r}\Big)w(t,y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}
yields

\left\|{w^{\rm av}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}}\leq Cr^{-1}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}}

and hence for every
-\tau\leq t\leq 0
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}w^{\rm av}e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\right|

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{|x|^{-b}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{n}{b},\infty}}\left\|{u}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}}^{k}\left\|{w^{\rm av}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}}\left\|{e^{it\Delta}\phi}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2n(n+2)}{(n+4)(n-2)},1}}

\displaystyle\leq CE^{C}r^{-1}\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}\left\|{w}\right\|_{\dot{W}_{x}^{-1,\frac{2(n+2)}{n-2}}}.

Therefore, adding the two estimates (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) integrated in time, we have

\left|\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}A_{0}w(t)e^{it\Delta}\phi\,dx\,dt\right|\\
\leq CE^{C}(r^{\min(\delta,k)}+r^{-1}\tau^{\frac{n+6}{2(n+2)}}\alpha)

where we recall that
\tau=C\alpha^{-\theta^{\prime\prime}}\geq C
. Taking
\theta^{\prime\prime}>0
small enough, we can optimize the last inequality in
r
to finish the proof of (
4.7
) and hence Lemma
4.1
.

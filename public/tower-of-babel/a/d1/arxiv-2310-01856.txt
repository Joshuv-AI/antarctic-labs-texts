Nonlocal Contributions to the Turbulent Cascade in Magnetohydrodynamic Plasmas
J. Friedrich, M. Wilbert, R. Marino

Abstract
We present evidence for nonlocal contributions to the turbulent energy cascade in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) plasmas. Therefore, we revisit a well-known result derived directly from the MHD equations, i.e., the Politano & Pouquet (P&P) law for the transfer of kinetic and magnetic energy in scale. We propose adding a term that accounts for nonlocal transfer and represents the influence of fluctuations from large scales due to the Alfvén effect. Supported by direct numerical simulations of homogeneous and isotropic MHD turbulence, we verify that in some plasma configurations, neglecting the additional nonlocal term might consistently overestimate energy dissipation rates and, thus, the contributions of turbulent energy dissipation potentially affecting solar wind heating; a central puzzle in space plasma physics that motivates the present work.



I
Introduction

The physical mechanisms underlying the solar wind - a continuous flow of charged particles emitted from the solar corona - have been hypothesized since the beginning of the past century. Based on Biermann’s observations of comet tail motion relative to the Sun
Biermann 1951
, Parker provided the first comprehensive theoretical description of the solar wind as a rapidly expanding outer coronal atmosphere overcoming the Sun’s gravitational field
Parker 1958
. Assuming an adiabatic solar wind expansion, Parker also considered the radial temperature profile
T(r)
as a function of the heliocentric distance
r
. Nonetheless, a purely adiabatic expansion of the solar wind, which suggests a radial temperature profile of proton temperature as
T(r)\sim r^{-4/3}
, differs from actual solar wind plasma measurements that exhibit a much slower decay with increasing distances
r

Matthaeus et al. 1999
;
Richardson and Smith 2003
;
Hundhausen 2012
.

A possible mechanism that would supply heat to the expanding solar wind - and thus explain the slower decay of the temperature profile - is the dissipation of turbulent kinetic and magnetic energy into heat predicted by a phenomenological description of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence
Marino et al. 2008
(we also refer the reader to the recent review article on scaling laws in solar wind turbulence
Marino and Sorriso-Valvo 2023
).
The concept of turbulent cascades, which has been put forth in the context of hydrodynamic turbulence by Kolmogorov
Kolmogorov 1941
, Heisenberg
Heisenberg 1948
, von Weizsäcker
v. Weizsäcker 1948
, and Onsager
Onsager 1949
, highlights the fact that turbulent motions are essentially transport processes of energy in scale. The existence of energy cascades in fluid and plasma turbulence has been assessed experimentally (e.g., in channel flows and fusion plasmas
Danaila et al. 2001
;
Conway 2008
), as well as by atmospheric
Vierinen et al. 2019
, oceanic
Balwada et al. 2022
, and space plasma measurements
Telloni 2023
. A central quantity in phenomenological descriptions of such cascade processes is the averaged energy dissipation rate
\langle\varepsilon_{kin}\rangle
, which is assumed to entirely characterize the transfer of energy from the injection scale, where the turbulent flow is stirred, to small scales where energy is dissipated into heat.

In a conducting fluid, the rate at which kinetic and magnetic energy is dissipated is determined by the local kinetic and magnetic energy dissipation rates

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{kin}({\bf x},t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial u_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\;,

(1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{mag}({\bf x},t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial h_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial h_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\;,

(2)

where
{\bf u}({\bf x},t)
denotes the velocity field,
{\bf h}({\bf x},t)=\sqrt{\mu/4\pi\rho}\;{\bf H}({\bf x},t)
the rescaled magnetic field,
\rho
the density of the fluid,
\mu
the permeability,
\nu
the kinematic viscosity, and
\lambda
the magnetic diffusivity (see Appendix
A
for further details). Fig.
1
(a) depicts a two-dimensional cut through the total energy dissipation rate field
\varepsilon_{tot}({\bf x},t)=\varepsilon_{kin}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon_{mag}({\bf x},t)
from a direct numerical simulation (DNS) of MHD turbulence (see Tab.
1
for further details). The dissipation field is highly fluctuating and exhibits strong gradients organized in current sheets in contrast to hydrodynamic turbulence
Marino et al. 2022
; see Fig.
1
(b), where the peaks are more localized, hinting at the presence of vortex tubes
Ishihara et al. 2009
;
Tanaka and Kida 1993
.

The solar wind is weakly collisional, which implies that energy cascades from the MHD scales further down to proton and electron scales
Matthaeus et al. 2020
. Due to the limited resolution of the instruments onboard most spacecraft and the presence of measurement noise, strong gradients of the energy dissipation at kinetic scales can typically not be resolved directly from
in situ
solar wind observations. Nonetheless, the scale-wise energy transfer at the scales at which the interplanetary plasma has a fluid-like behavior can be estimated indirectly from third-order moments of velocity and magnetic fields
Carbone et al. 2009
;
Marino and Sorriso-Valvo 2023
.

This can best be illustrated by considering the limit of vanishing magnetic field in the MHD equations, i.e., the Navier-Stokes equation. Under the assumption of homogeneity, isotropy, and statistical stationarity of the flow, the third-order moment of the longitudinal velocity increment
\delta_{r}u=[{\bf u}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{\bf u}({\bf x},t)]\cdot\frac{{\bf r}}{r}
is related to the averaged kinetic energy dissipation rate according to

S_{r\,r\,r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r)=\left\langle(\delta_{r}u)^{3}\right\rangle=-\frac{4}{5}\left\langle\varepsilon_{kin}\right\rangle r\;,

(3)

where angle brackets
\langle\ldots\rangle
denote a suitable averaging procedure, e.g., ensemble averages
Monin and Yaglom 2007
. A similar law has been derived from the MHD equations based on the Elsässer fields by Politano and Pouquet
Politano and Pouquet 1998a
;
Politano and Pouquet 1998b
and will hereafter be referred to as P&P law. The P&P law, which has been successfully used to assess the significance of solar wind heating
Marino et al. 2008
;
Stawarz et al. 2010
, suggests that - similar to hydrodynamic turbulence - the nonlinear interactions during energy transfer are purely
local
in scale
r
. The localness of interactions, however, in MHD turbulence is broken by the Alfvén effect
Biskamp 2003
, which implies that small-scale fluctuations are susceptible to large-scale magnetic field structures and behave approximately as Alfvén waves
Alfvén 1950
- a central assumption in both phenomenological models put forth by Iroshnikov
Iroshnikov 1964
and Kraichnan
Kraichnan 1965
. The Alfvénisation of small-scale fluctuations is also implied in the phenomenological model by Boldyrev
Boldyrev 2005
, who suggests a scale-dependent alignment between velocity and magnetic field that effectively suppresses nonlinear transfer at smaller scales (we also refer to
Alexakis and Biferale 2018
;
Molokov et al. 2007
;
Nazarenko and Schekochihin 2011
for further discussions as well as the incorporation of potential anisotropies captured by the Goldreich-Sridhar model
Goldreich and Sridhar 1995
). The solar wind can be highly Alfvénic, supporting the propagation of outward and inward Alfvén waves
Adhikari et al. 2015
;
D’Amicis and Bruno 2015
(with respect to the Sun). At the same time, heliospheric plasmas develop a strong turbulent state leading to the apparent paradox known as Alfvénic turbulence
Dobrowolny et al. 1980
;
Bavassano and Bruno 1989
. This condition, characterized by the simultaneous presence of strong turbulence and non-negligible correlations between velocity and the frozen-in magnetic field, is routinely observed in the solar wind
Bavassano et al. 1998
. Alfvénicity, as well as homogeneity, isotropy, and compressibility, are space plasma features that vary throughout the heliosphere and in time, thus with heliocentric distance, heliolatitude, and the solar activity itself
Stawarz et al. 2010
;
Marino et al. 2011
;
Marino et al. 2012
;
Coburn et al. 2012
. In other words, solar wind turbulence explores a vast parameter space of the magnetohydrodynamic cascade, driven by local nonlinear couplings
Aluie and Eyink 2010
;
Mininni 2011
. In specific conditions, for instance, due to the presence of large-scale shear and structures propagating from the Sun, cascade processes might be affected by interactions that are nonlocal in scale. Such effects should be reproduced by the third-order model we propose here - at least in the locally isotropic and homogeneous case. Beyond the MHD regime,
turbulent heating in the solar wind results from different contributions, for instance, Landau-type damping
Cerri et al. 2016
and non-resonant damping mechanisms (such as stochastic heating) occurring at small scales due to the existence of propagating plasma waves, but also the dissipation related to the presence of coherent structures originating from magnetic reconnection
Cerri and Califano 2017
and currents
(
Vasquez et al. 2007
)
. Such kinetic effects, as well as anisotropy and compressibility, have been incorporated in recent modifications of the scaling laws used to assess solar wind heating
Banerjee and Galtier 2013
;
Galtier et al. 2005
. Furthermore, strong turbulence dominated by nonlinear coherent structures such as Orszag-Tang vortices
(
Orszag and Tang 1979
;
Foldes et al. 2023
)
and Alfvén vortices
(
Shukla et al. 1985
;
Roberts et al. 2016
)
imply the possibility of nonlocal couplings at MHD scales and thus contribute to energy transfer and dissipation in space plasmas.

This article presents new evidence for nonlocal contributions to kinetic and magnetic energy transfer. To this end, we first revisit the derivation of the P&P law
Politano and Pouquet 1998a
in Sec.
II
, obtaining from first principles an additional nonlocal term that accounts for the influence of magnetic fields induced at large scales on fluctuations at small scales as suggested by the Iroshnikov-Kraichnan phenomenology. The importance of this nonlocal term for estimations of turbulent energy transfer based on third-order moments is then assessed by DNS of homogeneous and isotropic MHD turbulence in Sec.
III
, and the validity of the model proposed is demonstrated in the plasma under study. Although the original P&P law has been verified in numerous DNS of two- and three-dimensional MHD turbulence
Mininni and Pouquet 2009
;
Sorriso-Valvo et al. 2002
;
Yousef et al. 2007
(see also
Marino and Sorriso-Valvo 2023
for further references), here, we provide analytical and numerical evidence that there can be plasma configurations for which considering the nonlocal contributions to the MHD cascade is critical to correctly infer the plasma heating rate through the third-order law approach. These configurations can be representative of heliospheric plasmas, whose highly dynamical state is such that solar wind may at times be characterized by enhanced turbulence and nonlocal couplings of kinetic and magnetic modes, due to magnetic reconnection
Cerri et al. 2022
, magnetic switchbacks
Hernandez et al. 2021
;
Telloni et al. 2022
or the interaction between fast and slow solar wind streams in the ecliptic
Marino et al. 2011
.

II
Revisiting the P&P law using the invariant theory of MHD turbulence

In this section, we revisit the P&P law
Politano and Pouquet 1998a
;
Politano and Pouquet 1998b
using the invariant theory of MHD turbulence devised by Chandrasekhar
Chandrasekhar 1951
.
Following standard statistical treatments of turbulent flows
Monin and Yaglom 2007
, we introduce ensemble averages denoted by the brackets
\langle\ldots\rangle
. Under the assumption of homogeneity, it is straightforward to derive an evolution equation for the total energy
E_{tot}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\langle u_{i}u_{i}+h_{i}h_{i}\rangle
according to

\dot{E}_{tot}(t)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}t}\left(u_{rms}^{2}+h_{rms}^{2}\right)=-\langle\varepsilon_{tot}\rangle\;,

(4)

where we imply summation over identical indices and introduce the root mean square velocity and magnetic fields (please see Appendix
A
for further details on the derivation of Eq. (
4

In his seminal work, Chandrasekhar generalized the work of von Kármán and Howarth
de Karman and Howarth 1938
to an invariant theory of homogeneous and isotropic MHD turbulence
Chandrasekhar 1951

Here, we consider evolution equations for the velocity and magnetic field correlation tensors,
C_{i\;j}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}({\bf r},t)=\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\right\rangle
and
C_{i\;j}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}({\bf r},t)=\left\langle h_{i}h_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\right\rangle
, which can be derived from the MHD equations (under the assumption of homogeneity, see Appendix
B
and
C

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle-

\displaystyle 2\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{k}}\left\langle(u_{i}u_{k}-h_{i}h_{k})u_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle=2\nu\nabla_{{\bf r}}^{2}\left\langle u_{i}u_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle\;,

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left\langle h_{i}h_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle-

\displaystyle 2\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{k}}\left\langle(h_{i}u_{k}-h_{k}u_{i})h_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle=2\lambda\nabla_{{\bf r}}^{2}\left\langle h_{i}h_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle\;.

(6)

5

C_{(ik)j}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}{\mathbf{u}}}({\bf r},t)=\left\langle u_{i}u_{k}u_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle
and

[ik]

\displaystyle A_{[ki]j}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}({\bf r},t)=

\displaystyle\left\langle(h_{i}u_{k}-h_{k}u_{i})h_{j}^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}(r,t)\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r}\delta_{jk}-\frac{r_{k}}{r}\delta_{ij}\right)\;.

(7)

Furthermore, the indices
r
and

denote the longitudinal and transverse projections,

0=-\frac{4}{5}\langle\varepsilon_{tot}\rangle r+\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[\nu S_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r)+\lambda S_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r)\right]\;,

(23)

or

S_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r)+\textrm{Pm}S_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r)=\frac{\langle\varepsilon_{tot}\rangle}{15\lambda}r^{2}\;,

(24)

where
\textrm{Pm}={\nu}/{\lambda}
denotes the magnetic Prandtl number. In the following section, we will verify the modified scaling law (
18
) in direct numerical simulations of MHD turbulence.

III
Verification of the third-order law via direct numerical simulations of MHD turbulence

To verify Eq. (
18
), we performed DNS of homogeneous and isotropic MHD turbulence in a box of
1024^{3}
grid points with characteristic turbulence parameters summarized in Tab.
1
. The forcing scheme consists of a divergence-free random forcing
Alvelius 1999
that is applied to the evolution equation of the velocity field (i.e., the evolution equation for the magnetic field is not actively forced). As shown in Fig.
2
, after an initial phase, the forcing scheme leads to nearly constant kinetic and magnetic energy dissipation rates
\langle\varepsilon_{kin}\rangle
and
\langle\varepsilon_{mag}\rangle
over time. Fig.
3
depicts the third-order moments that enter Eq. (
18
). The lowest curve corresponds to the third-order velocity structure function
S_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r)
. Interestingly, this curve exhibits three distinct scaling regions: In the dissipation range, we observe the predicted scaling
\sim r^{3}
followed by a region that scales in agreement with the Iroshnikov-Kraichnan phenomenology
\sim r^{3/4}
whereas a linear increase seems to dominate at larger
r
. Furthermore, in the dissipation range, we can directly verify the relation between the symmetric and anti-symmetric correlations (
22
). In the case under study, the original prediction
Politano and Pouquet 1998a
;
Politano and Pouquet 1998b
without the additional nonlocal source term overestimates the total energy dissipation rate
\langle\varepsilon_{tot}\rangle
(dashed line) and does not vanish for small
r
, therefore, violating energy conservation. This becomes even more apparent in the compensated plot in Fig.
3
(b), which suggests that including the source term results in the correct scaling in the inertial range in between the largest Taylor scale
\lambda_{kin}
and the smallest integral length scale
L_{mag}

Politano and Pouquet 1998a
;
Politano and Pouquet 1998b
(green curve) would plateau before this inertial range and overestimate the

by a factor of 1.71.
We must emphasize that this discrepancy between the original P&P law and (
18

;
Sorriso-Valvo et al. 2002
;
Yousef et al. 2007
due for instance to low magnetic and fluid Reynolds numbers and signatures of the large-scale anisotropic forcing scheme (e.g., by Taylor-Green vortices). Indeed, MHD and kinetic simulations reproduce plasma dynamics and features peculiar to the set of governing parameters defined for specific runs, which may differ significantly from actual solar wind parameters. Due to the insufficient computational power of today’s supercomputers, constraints in reproducing the solar wind numerically are technical but also inherent since solar wind dynamics develop in a vast parameter space, hardly reproducible even with large ensembles of simulations.

This entails that the influence of the additional nonlocal term on cascade processes can be sub-dominant to the third-order correlation stemming from the Lorentz force
C_{r\;r\;r}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)
or that the assumptions of isotropy and homogeneity in the derivation of Eq. (
18
) are not strictly guaranteed, in some of the simulations appearing in the literature, and also in many cases within the actual solar wind. Nonetheless, for some settings, e.g., strong Alfvénic turbulence, this term might play an important, non-negligible role.

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we have derived from first principles an additional nonlocal term in the equation for the energy cascade directly from the incompressible MHD equations, assuming isotropy and homogeneity only. This additional term can potentially improve the heating rate predictions by the original P&P law when nonlocal interactions become essential in the overall plasma dynamics. We have verified by numerical simulations of MHD turbulence forced at large scales that this additional nonlocal term is critical in describing the energy transfer from large to small scales for the set of parameters chosen.

The exact law established by Politano and Pouquet
Politano and Pouquet 1998a
;
Politano and Pouquet 1998b
assumes homogeneity, isotropy, and incompressibility - conditions that are fulfilled perhaps only locally in interplanetary space plasmas - representing to date the most robust statistical framework to characterize energy transfer and heating in the solar wind
Marino and Sorriso-Valvo 2023
;
Marino et al. 2008
.
Without diminishing its validity, our findings suggest that in some solar wind samples, the current analyses of observational data by the original P&P law and some of its surrogates might substantially overestimate the rate at which energy is dissipated (or further transferred across sub-proton scales) at the bottom of the MHD turbulent cascade developing in the solar wind. This leads to improved third-order moment law predictions characterizing the observed space plasma heating.

As mentioned in Sec.
II
, the additional nonlocal term arises due to the advection of the magnetic field by the velocity field and the corresponding transformation of the electric field
{\bf E}^{\prime}={\bf E}+\mu{\bf u}\times{\bf H}
. Hence, it would also be interesting to assess other transformations of the electric field, e.g., in the context of Hall MHD
Galtier 2008
;
Foldes et al. 2023
.
Future work will be devoted to applying the law (
18
) and assessing the influence of nonlocality in solar wind measurements. Moreover, we aim at reconstructing velocity and magnetic fields from partial measurements by extending current velocity field reconstructions
Friedrich et al. 2022
;
Lübke et al. 2023
to MHD turbulence
Friedrich 2020
;
Friedrich and Grauer 2020
;
Lübke et al. 2024
, exploiting observations of state-of-art ongoing
(
Owen et al. 2020
;
Fox et al. 2016
)
and future multi-spacecraft solar wind mission
(
Klein et al. 2023
;
Pecora et al. 2023a
;
Pecora et al. 2023b
;
Retino et al. 2022
)
.

Appendix A
Derivation of the Equation of Total Energy Balance in MHD Turbulence

In this section, we derive an evolution equation for the total energy (i.e., the sum of kinetic and magnetic energy) in MHD turbulence. Therefore, we consider the MHD equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{i}({\bf x},t)+u_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{i}({\bf x},t)-h_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{i}({\bf x},t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}P({\bf x},t)+\nu\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}u_{i}({\bf x},t)\;,

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h_{i}({\bf x},t)+u_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{i}({\bf x},t)-h_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{i}({\bf x},t)

\displaystyle=\lambda\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}h_{i}({\bf x},t)\;,

(26)

where summation over identical indices is implied. Here,
P({\bf x},t)=p({\bf x},t)+\frac{\rho}{2}|{\bf h}|^{2}
denotes the sum of hydrodynamic pressure
p({\bf x},t)
and
the magnetic pressure,
\rho
the density (which will be set to one),
\nu
the kinematic
viscosity and
\lambda
the magnetic diffusivity of the conducting fluid. Furthermore, it should be noted that in this formulation of the MHD equations, the magnetic field
{\bf H}
is measured in units of the velocity field

{\bf h}({\bf x},t)=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{4\pi\rho}}{\bf H}({\bf x},t)\;.

(27)

The total energy can now be defined as the sum of kinetic and magnetic energy according to

E_{tot}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle u_{i}u_{i}+h_{i}h_{i}\right\rangle=\frac{3}{2}\left(u_{rms}^{2}+h_{rms}^{2}\right)\;,

(28)

where
u_{rms}
and
h_{rms}
denote the root mean square velocity and magnetic field of a three-dimensional MHD flow. Moreover, the brackets
\langle\ldots\rangle
represent suitable averages, e.g., ensemble averages. We scalar multiply Eq. (
25
) by
u_{i}
and Eq. (
26
) by
h_{i}

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\bf u}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{k}{\bf u}^{2}-u_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{k}h_{i}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{i}P

(29)

\displaystyle+u_{i}\nu\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}u_{i}\;,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\bf h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{k}{\bf h}^{2}-h_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{k}u_{i}=

\displaystyle h_{i}\lambda\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}h_{i}\;,

(30)

where we used the incompressibility conditions for both velocity and magnetic field, i.e.,
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{k}=0
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{k}=0
. Here, the viscous terms can be reformulated according to

\displaystyle u_{i}\nu\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}u_{i}=\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{i}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}-\nu\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}{\bf u}^{2}-\frac{\nu}{2}\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}+\nu\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\nabla^{2}_{\bf x}{\bf u}^{2}-\varepsilon_{kin}+\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}u_{i}\;.

(31)

The same procedure applies to the diffusive terms in Eq. (
30
). Furthermore, local kinetic and magnetic energy dissipation rates are defined according to

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{kin}({\bf x},t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial u_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\;,

(32)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{mag}({\bf x},t)=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{i,k}\left(\frac{\partial h_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial h_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\;.

(33)

We observe that nonlinear terms which are advected by the magnetic field (third terms on l.h.s of Eqs. (
29
) and (
30

29
) and (
30

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}e_{tot}({\bf x},t)+\nabla_{{\bf x}}\cdot{\bf J}^{tot}({\bf x},t)=-\varepsilon_{tot}({\bf x},t)\;,

(34)

where we defined

\displaystyle e_{tot}({\bf x},t)=\frac{{\bf u}^{2}({\bf x},t)+{\bf h}^{2}({\bf x},t)}{2}\;,

(35)

\displaystyle{\bf J}^{tot}({\bf x},t)={\bf u}({\bf x},t)\left(\frac{{\bf u}^{2}({\bf x},t)+{\bf h}^{2}({\bf x},t)}{2}+\frac{P({\bf x},t)}{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle-{\bf h}({\bf x},t)[{\bf u}({\bf x},t)\cdot{\bf h}({\bf x},t)]-\frac{\nu}{2}\nabla_{\bf x}{\bf u}^{2}({\bf x},t)-\frac{\lambda}{2}\nabla_{\bf x}{\bf h}^{2}({\bf x},t)

\displaystyle-\nu{\bf u}({\bf x},t)\cdot\nabla_{{\bf x}}{\bf u}({\bf x},t)-\lambda{\bf h}({\bf x},t)\cdot\nabla_{{\bf x}}{\bf h}({\bf x},t)\;,

(36)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{tot}({\bf x},t)=\varepsilon_{kin}({\bf x},t)+\varepsilon_{mag}({\bf x},t)\;.

(37)

The two first terms in the brackets of (
A
denote the kinetic and magnetic energy transported throughout the surface. The second term is the work from the fluid against the total pressure. The next term is the cross helicity
{\bf u}({\bf x},t)\cdot{\bf h}({\bf x},t)
which is transported anti-parallel along the lines of force. Performing the averaging procedure, we obtain a temporal evolution equation for the total energy
E_{tot}(t)=\langle e_{tot}({\bf x},t)\rangle
as

\dot{E}_{tot}(t)=-\langle\varepsilon_{tot}({\bf x},t)\rangle\;,

(38)

where we assumed homogeneity to neglect the transport terms
\nabla_{\bf x}\cdot\langle{\bf J}^{tot}({\bf x},t)\rangle=0
. Hence, the total energy is only changed by viscous and magnetic dissipation. Here, we only considered decaying MHD turbulence; otherwise, the temporal evolution

would be influenced by additional forcing mechanisms in Eqs. (
25

38

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q_{kin}(r,t)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}C_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}(r,t)\right),\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}Q_{mag}(r,t)=\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}C_{r\;r}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}}(r,t)\right),

(69)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{k}}J_{k}^{kin}({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[r^{4}\left(C_{r\;r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}-C_{r\;r\;r}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}{\bf u}}\right)\right]\right)\;,\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{k}}J_{k}^{mag}({\bf r},t)=\frac{2}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}(r,t)\right)\;,

(70)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bf r}Q_{kin}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}Q_{kin}(r,t)\right)\;,\qquad\Delta_{\bf r}Q_{mag}(r,t)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}Q_{mag}(r,t)\right)\;.

(71)

Inserting these relations into Eq. (
67
) and (
68
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\Bigg[\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{4}\Bigg(C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)-C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)

\displaystyle+2\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial r}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)\Bigg)\Bigg]\;,

(72)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r,t)=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{r}A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r,t)+2\lambda\frac{1}{r^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r,t)\;.

(73)

Eq. (
72
) is the generalization of the von Kármán-Howarth equation to MHD turbulence with the additional third-order correlation function
C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)
that is due to the Lorentz force in Eq. (
25
). The evolution equation for the second-order longitudinal correlation function for the magnetic field (
73
) possesses a different differential form, which is due to the anti-symmetry of the tensor in Eq. (
64
).

Appendix D
On the Equivalence of the Mixed Third-Order Correlations for Small Scale Separations

As first recognized by Chandrasekhar
Chandrasekhar 1951
, the defining scalars of the mixed third-order correlation functions
C_{(ki)j}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)=\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x}^{\prime},t)\right\rangle
and
A_{[ki]j}^{{\bf u}{\bf h}{\bf h}}({\bf r},t)=\left\langle(h_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)-h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t))h_{j}({\bf x}^{\prime},t)\right\rangle
are related to each other for small scale separations
{\bf r}
. This can be shown as follows: First, we consider the derivative of the symmetric tensor defined by Eq. (
63
)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial C_{(ki)j}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)}{\partial r_{i}}\right|_{{\bf r}=0}=\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial u_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)}{r}+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\partial C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right]_{r=0}\delta_{jk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}\left.\frac{\partial C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right|_{r=0}\delta_{jk}\;.

(74)

Similarly, we derive the tensorial form of the anti-symmetric third-order correlation tensor (
64
) and obtain

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial A_{[ki]j}^{{\bf u}{\bf h}{\bf h}}({\bf r},t)}{\partial r_{i}}\right|_{{\bf r}=0}

\displaystyle=\left\langle(h_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)-h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t))\frac{\partial h_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.2\frac{\partial A_{[rt]t}^{{\bf u}{\bf h}{\bf h}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right|_{{r}=0}\delta_{jk}\;.

(75)

Next, we derive the following identity

\displaystyle\left\langle(h_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)-h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t))\frac{\partial h_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial h_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle-\left\langle h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial h_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle}_{=0,\;\textrm{homogeneity}}-\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial u_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\underbrace{\frac{\partial h_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}}_{=0}h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left\langle h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle}_{=0,\;\textrm{homogeneity}}+\left\langle h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial h_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\underbrace{\frac{\partial u_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}}_{=0}h_{j}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial u_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle+\left\langle h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial h_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle\;.

(76)

Interchanging
j
and
k
in Eq. (
75
) yields

\displaystyle\left\langle(h_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)-h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t))\frac{\partial h_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle}_{=0,\;\textrm{homogeneity}}

\displaystyle-\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial u_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\underbrace{\frac{\partial h_{i}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}}_{=0}h_{k}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{i}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial h_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle\;.

(77)

Adding Eqs. (
76
) and (
77
) yields

\displaystyle 4\left.\frac{\partial A_{[rt]t}^{{\bf u}{\bf h}{\bf h}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right|_{{r}=0}\delta_{jk}=-\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial u_{k}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t)\frac{\partial u_{j}({\bf x},t)}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rangle\underbrace{=}_{\textrm{Eq.}(\ref{eq:ident0})}-5\left.\frac{\partial C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right|_{r=0}\delta_{jk}\;.

(78)

Hence, for small-scale separations
r
, the defining scalars of symmetric and anti-symmetric mixed third-order tensors are related according to

\left.\frac{\partial A_{[rt]t}^{{\bf u}{\bf h}{\bf h}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right|_{{r}=0}=-\frac{5}{4}\left.\frac{\partial C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)}{\partial r}\right|_{r=0}\;.

(79)

Appendix E
Loitsiansky Invariants and their Implications for the Additional Source Term

In this section, we discuss further implications of Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
), namely the existence of certain invariants. As shown by Loitsiansky
Loitsianskii 1939
, in the hydrodynamic limit, the von Kármán-Howarth equation admits the invariant

\Lambda^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{d}r\;r^{4}C_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}(r,t)\;.

(80)

As shown by Chandrasekhar
Chandrasekhar 1951
, this quantity should also be conserved in MHD turbulence, which can be seen by multiplying Eq. (
8
) by
r^{4}
and subsequent integration

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{0}^{r}\textrm{d}r^{\prime}\;r^{\prime 4}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{4}\left(C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)-C_{r\;r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)+2\nu\frac{\partial}{\partial r}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}}(r,t)\right)\;.

(81)

Hence, if the bracketed terms on the r.h.s. decay more rapidly than
\sim r^{-4}
in the limit of
r\rightarrow\infty
, then
\Lambda^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}
is a conserved quantity in MHD turbulence as well. Nonetheless, due to the anti-symmetry of the third-order correlation in Eq. (
9
), one cannot establish a similar line of reasoning for the magnetic Loitsiansky invariant

\Lambda^{{\bf h}{\bf h}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{d}r\;r^{4}C_{r\;r}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}}(r,t)\;.

(82)

Following Chandrasekhar
Chandrasekhar 1951
, we introduce the vector potential
{\bf a}({\bf x},t)
according to

{\bf b}({\bf x},t)=\nabla\times{\bf a}({\bf x},t)\;,

(83)

The anti-symmetric tensor (
64
) can thus be re-cast as

\displaystyle A_{[ki]j}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}({\bf r},t)=\langle(u_{k}({\bf x},t)h_{i}({\bf x},t)-u_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t))h_{j}({\bf x}^{\prime},t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{jlm}\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{l}}\langle(u_{k}({\bf x},t)h_{i}({\bf x},t)-u_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t))a_{j}({\bf x}^{\prime},t)\rangle\;.

(84)

The corresponding tensor is skew and possesses the following form

\displaystyle A_{[ki]j}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{a}}}({\bf r},t)=\langle(u_{k}({\bf x},t)h_{i}({\bf x},t)-u_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{k}({\bf x},t))a_{j}({\bf x}^{\prime},t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{a}}}({r},t)\varepsilon_{ijk}+r\frac{\partial A_{[rt],t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{a}}}({r},t)}{\partial r}\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r}\varepsilon_{jkl}\frac{r_{l}}{r}+\frac{r_{k}}{r}\varepsilon_{jil}\frac{r_{l}}{r}\right)\;.

(85)

Inserting this tensorial form in Eq. (
84
) thus yields

\displaystyle A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}({r},t)=-\frac{1}{r^{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{4}\frac{\partial A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{a}}}({r},t)}{\partial r}\right)\;,

(86)

which upon insertion into Eq. (
9
) yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r,t)=\frac{1}{r^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left[r^{4}\left(\frac{\partial A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{a}}}({r},t)}{\partial r}+2\lambda\frac{\partial}{\partial r}C_{r\;r}^{\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}}(r,t)\right)\right]\;.

(87)

Hence, similar arguments as the ones used in Eq. (
81
) lead to
\lambda^{{\bf h}{\bf h}}=const.
, and Chandrasekhar further concluded
\lambda^{{\bf h}{\bf h}}=0
. From Eq. (
9
), we can thus derive

\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{d}r^{\prime}r^{\prime 3}A_{[rt]t}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}({r},t)=0\;.

(88)

Appendix F
Longitudinal and Transverse Correlation Functions

In this Appendix, we derive tensorial forms for second and third-order correlation functions under the assumptions of homogeneity and isotropy. To this end,
we consider the velocity fields
{\bf u}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)
at point
{\bf x}+{\bf r}
and
{\bf u}({\bf x},t)
at point
{\bf x}
(a similar treatment applies for magnetic field). We can divide the vector
{\bf u}={\bf u}_{r}+{\bf u}_{t}
into a part
{\bf u}_{r}
parallel to
{\bf r}
, and a transverse part
{\bf u}_{t}
.
These parts are thereby given as

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{r}=\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\left(\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\cdot{\bf u}\right)\;,\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;{\bf u}_{t}=-\left(\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\times\left(\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\times{\bf u}\right)\right).

(89)

The longitudinal correlation function

C_{r\,r}(r,t)=\langle{\bf u}_{r}({\bf x},t)\cdot{\bf u}_{r}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle\;,

(90)

can be calculated by multiplying the two-point correlation tensor

C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle\;,

(91)

by
\frac{r_{i}}{r_{j}}
and
\frac{r_{j}}{r}
.
Assuming that
C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle
is isotropic and mirror-symmetric
Monin and Yaglom 2007
its general form is given by

C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=C_{1}(r,t)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}+C_{2}(r,t)\delta_{ij}\;,

(92)

where the defining scalars
C_{1}(r,t)
and
C_{2}(r,t)
can now be expressed in terms of the longitudinal (
90
) and transverse correlation functions

C_{t\,t}(r,t)=\frac{1}{2}\langle{\bf u}_{t}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\cdot{\bf u}_{t}({\bf x},t)\rangle\;.

(93)

Here, the factor
\frac{1}{2}
has been included in three dimensions: two transverse and only one longitudinal direction. Multiplying Eq. (
92
) by
\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}
thus yields

C_{rr}(r,t)=C_{1}(r,t)+C_{2}(r,t)\;.

(94)

Decomposing the correlation tensor (
98
) yields

\displaystyle C_{ij}({\bf r},t)=

\displaystyle\langle{\bf u}_{r}({\bf x},t)\cdot{\bf u}_{r}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\langle{u}_{t,i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t){u}_{t,j}({\bf x},t)\rangle\;.

(95)

Hence, we can identify

\displaystyle\langle{u}_{t,i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t){u}_{t,j}({\bf x},t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{(C_{1}(r,t)-C_{rr}(r,t))}_{\textrm{Eq.}(\ref{eq:long})}\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}+C_{2}(r,t)\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2}(r,t)\left(\delta_{ij}-\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}\right)\;.

(96)

Summing Eq. (
96
) over
i=j
thus yields

2C_{tt}(r,tt)=2C_{2}(r,t)\;,

(97)

and we can express the correlation tensor (
92
) in terms of its longitudinal and transverse correlation functions according to

C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=\left(C_{r\,r}(r,t)-C_{t\,t}(r,t)\right)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}+C_{t\,t}(r,t)\delta_{ij}.

(98)

The bilinear form (
92
) can now be extended to a trilinear form
Robertson 1940
. A general isotropic and mirror-symmetric tensor of order three can be defined as

\displaystyle C_{ijk}({\bf r},t)=

\displaystyle C_{1}(r,t)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}r_{k}}{r^{3}}+C_{2}(r,t)\frac{r_{k}}{r}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+C_{3}(r,t)\frac{r_{j}}{r}\delta_{ik}+C_{4}(r,t)\frac{r_{i}}{r}\delta_{jk}\;.

(99)

Whereas a bilinear form is always symmetric in
i
and
j
, we can now impose further symmetry conditions on this tensor. E.g., the third-order correlation function

C_{(ij)k}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)=\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle\;,

(100)

is symmetric in
i
and
j
, which implies
C_{3}(r,t)=C_{4}(r,t)
. This also applies to the third-order correlation
C_{(ij)k}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}{\bf u}}=\langle h_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle
, which stems from the Lorentz force. Both of these tensors can thus be expressed as

\displaystyle C_{(ij)k}({\bf r},t)

\displaystyle=C_{1}(r,t)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}r_{k}}{r^{3}}+C_{2}(r,t)\frac{r_{k}}{r}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+C_{3}(r,t)\left(\frac{r_{j}}{r}\delta_{ik}+\frac{r_{i}}{r}\delta_{jk}\right)\;,

(101)

where the coefficients will be specified later on in terms of longitudinal and transverse correlation functions. In contrast, the third-order tensor

\displaystyle A_{[ij]k}^{\mathbf{u}\mathbf{h}{\mathbf{h}}}({\bf r},t)

(102)

\displaystyle=\langle(u_{i}({\bf x},t)h_{j}({\bf x},t)-u_{j}({\bf x},t)h_{i}({\bf x},t))h_{k}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\rangle\;,

is anti-symmetric in
i
and
j
, which implies
C_{1}(r,t)=C_{2}(r,t)=0
and
C_{3}(r,t)=-C_{4}(r,t)=A_{1}(r,t)
. Hence, an anti-symmetric tensor of order three obeys the trilinear form

A_{[ij]k}({\bf r},t)=A_{1}(r,t)\left(\frac{r_{j}}{r}\delta_{ik}-\frac{r_{i}}{r}\delta_{jk}\right)\;.

(103)

Appendix G
The Correlation Functions for Incompressible, Isotropic, and Homogeneous Fields

G.1
Correlation Functions of Second Order

Due to the incompressibility condition, it is possible to reduce the tensorial form of
C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)
to a dependence of the longitudinal structure function
C_{r\,r}(r,t)
only. The incompressibility condition is used according to

\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{i}}C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=\left\langle\frac{\partial u_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)}{\partial r_{i}}u_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle=0\;.

(104)

Using the relation
\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{i}}=\frac{r_{i}}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{i}}C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(C_{r\,r}(r,t)-C_{t\,t}(r,t))\frac{r_{j}}{r}

(105)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{r}(C_{r\,r}(r,t)-C_{t\,t}(r,t))\frac{r_{j}}{r}+\frac{\partial}{\partial r}C_{t\,t}(r,t)\frac{r_{j}}{r}=0\;,

and results in the von Kármán-Howarth relation

C_{t\,t}(r,t)=\frac{1}{2r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}C_{r\,r}(r,t)\right)\;.

(106)

The correlation function
C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)
can therefore be described solely in terms of the longitudinal correlation function
C_{r\,r}(r,t)
. Furthermore, summing
C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)
over equal indices
i=j
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=j}C_{i\,j}({\bf r},t)=

\displaystyle C_{r\,r}(r,t)+2C_{t\,t}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{3}C_{r\,r}(r,t)\right)\;.

(107)

G.2
Correlation Functions of Third Order

Applying the incompressibility condition

\frac{\partial}{\partial r_{k}}C_{(ij)k}({\bf r},t)=0\;,

(108)

to the third-order correlation function (
101
) yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}C_{1}(r,t)\right)+2r\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\frac{C_{3}(r,t)}{r}\right)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}C_{2}(r,t)\right)+2\frac{C_{3}(r,t)}{r}\right)\delta_{ij}=0\;.

(109)

Since both brackets in (
109
) have to vanish identically to satisfy the equation, we obtain two equations along with (
101
) for the three pre-factors
C_{1}(r,t)
,
C_{2}(r,t)
and
C_{3}(r,t)
. This system of equations is solved by

\displaystyle C_{1}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)}{r}\right)\;,

(110)

\displaystyle C_{2}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)}{2},

(111)

\displaystyle C_{3}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)\right)\;.

(112)

Therefore, the third-order correlation function can be written in terms of
C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)
only

\displaystyle C_{(ij)k}({\bf r},t)=-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)}{r}\right)\frac{r_{i}r_{j}r_{k}}{r^{3}}

(113)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)\right)\left(\frac{r_{i}}{r}\delta_{jk}+\frac{r_{j}}{r}\delta_{ik}\right)-\frac{C_{r\,r\,r}(r,t)}{2}\frac{r_{k}}{r}\delta_{ij}\;.

Appendix H
Structure Functions of Incompressible, Isotropic, and Homogeneous Fields

We consider the corresponding moments of velocity increments to calculate the structure functions introduced in Sec.
II
.

H.1
Structure Functions of Second Order

The second-order longitudinal structure functions are defined as

\displaystyle S_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}(r,t)=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\left[{\bf u}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{\bf u}({\bf x},t)\right]\cdot\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\;,

(114)

\displaystyle S_{r\;r}^{{\bf h}{\bf h}}(r,t)=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\left[{\bf h}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{\bf h}({\bf x},t)\right]\cdot\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\right)^{2}\right\rangle\;.

(115)

These longitudinal structure functions can be related to the longitudinal correlation functions, which will be shown here based on the longitudinal velocity correlation function
C_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}(r,t)
defined by Eq.(
90
). We obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\left[{\bf u}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{\bf u}({\bf x},t)\right]\cdot\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\right)^{2}\right\rangle

(116)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{i}}{r}\left\langle{u}_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t){u}_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)+{u}_{i}({\bf x},t){u}_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle\frac{r_{j}}{r}

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{i}}{r}\left\langle{u}_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t){u}_{j}({\bf x},t)-{u}_{i}({\bf x},t){u}_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\right\rangle\frac{r_{j}}{r}.

Due to homogeneity and isotropy, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)u_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\right\rangle=C_{i\;j}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf 0},t)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)\right\rangle=\left\langle u_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle=C_{i\;j}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)\;.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}(r,t)=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{i}}{r}\left[2C_{i\;j}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf 0},t)-2C_{i\;j}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)\right]\frac{r_{j}}{r}

(117)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[C_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}({0},t)-C_{r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}}({r},t)\right]\;.

(118)

The same treatment applies to the magnetic structure function of the second order.

H.2
Structure Functions of Third Order

The third-order longitudinal velocity structure function

S^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}_{r\;r\;r}(r,t)=\left\langle\left(\left[{\bf u}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{\bf u}({\bf x},t)\right]\cdot\frac{{\bf r}}{r}\right)^{3}\right\rangle

(119)

can be related to the third-order longitudinal correlation function as

\displaystyle S^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}_{r\;r\;r}(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left[{u}_{i}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{u}_{i}({\bf x},t)\right]\left[{u}_{j}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{u}_{j}({\bf x},t)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left[{u}_{k}({\bf x}+{\bf r},t)-{u}_{k}({\bf x},t)\right]\right\rangle\frac{r_{i}r_{j}r_{k}}{r^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[C_{(ij)k}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)+C_{(jk)i}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)+C_{(ik)j}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}({\bf r},t)\right]\frac{r_{i}r_{j}r_{k}}{r^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6C_{r\;r\;r}^{{\bf u}{\bf u}{\bf u}}(r,t)\;.

(120)

Here, we used statistical homogeneity and isotropy and the relation
\left\langle u_{i}({\bf x},t)u_{j}({\bf x},t)u_{k}({\bf x},t)\right\rangle=0
.

Non-semisimple 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} quantum invariants of fibred links
Daniel López Neumann and Roland van der Veen

Abstract
. The Akutsu-Deguchi-Ohtsuki (ADO) invariants are the most studied quantum link invariants coming from a non-semisimple tensor category. We show that, for fibered links in S 3 S^{3} , the degree of the ADO invariant is determined by the genus and the top coefficient is a root of unity. More precisely, we prove that the top coefficient is determined by the Hopf invariant of the plane field of S 3 S^{3} associated to the fiber surface. Our proof is based on the genus bounds established in our previous work, together with a theorem of Giroux-Goodman stating that fiber surfaces in the three-sphere can be obtained from a disk by plumbing/deplumbing Hopf bands.



1.
Introduction

A big open problem in quantum topology is to find connections between quantum invariants and geometric topology. This problem has been mostly studied for quantum invariants coming from semisimple tensor categories, such as quantum groups at generic parameter. For the colored Jones polynomials (which come from
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
at generic
q
), there are several conjectures in this direction
[
19
,
11
,
39
]
. For other tensor categories this problem is largely unexplored.

In our previous work
[
26
,
27
]
, we showed that quantum link invariants coming from certain non-semisimple tensor categories
structurally differ from their semisimple counterparts. For instance, if
N_{p}(L,x)
is the Akutsu-Deguchi-Ohtsuki (ADO) invariant of a link
L\subset S^{3}

[
2
,
25
]
(which comes from
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
at a
2p
-th root of unity
q
), we showed that:

(1)

\displaystyle\text{deg}_{x}N_{p}(L,x)\leq(2g(L)+s-1)(p-1)

where
g(L)
is the minimal genus of a connected Seifert surface of
L
and
s
is the number of components of
L
. This generalizes the well-known genus bound for the Alexander polynomial, which is the case
p=2
by
[
24
]
. No similar result is known for the colored Jones polynomials. Note that the ADO invariants are at the source of various recent developments in quantum topology, such as non-semisimple TQFTs
[
3
]
, the
q
-series invariants predicted by physicists
[
15
,
14
]
and vertex operator algebras
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the ADO invariants for fibred links. Recall that a link
L\subset S^{3}
is
fibred
if there exists a fibration
S^{3}\setminus L\to S^{1}
in which each fiber is a Seifert surface. Generalizing the well known result that the Alexander polynomial of a fibered knot is monic and the genus bound is sharp we prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a fibred
s
-component link with fiber surface
F
. Then, for any
p\geq 2
,
N_{p}(L,x)
has degree
(2g(L)+s-1)(p-1)
and its top coefficient is given by

(-q)^{2g(L)+s-1-2\lambda(\xi_{F})}

where
\lambda(\xi_{F})
is the Hopf invariant of the plane field
\xi_{F}
of
S^{3}
associated to
F
.

To the knowledge of the authors, Theorem
1
is the first theorem relating quantum link invariants to fibredness. No relation seems to be known for the “semisimple” link polynomials, such as colored Jones, HOMFLY and Kauffman polynomials, or even their categorifications such as Khovanov homology. It is worth noting that certain obstructions to fibredness can be obtained from the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev TQFT
[
12
,
1
]
.

Theorem
1
follows from Theorem
4.10
below, which studies the effect of Hopf plumbing on (the top term of) ADO invariants, together with a result of Giroux-Goodman
[
13
]
that states that all fiber surfaces in
S^{3}
are obtained from a disk by plumbing/deplumbing Hopf bands (see Subsection
2.2
and Figure
1
). The proof of Theorem
4.10
is purely algebraic and relies on the genus bounds as established in
[
27
]
. Note that, in contrast, the proof of the Giroux-Goodman theorem of
[
13
]
relies on contact geometry techniques.

Our method turns out to apply to other non-semisimple quantum invariants as well, such as the Links-Gould invariant or analogues of ADO invariants for other quantized Lie algebras at roots of unity (this will be carried out in a separate paper). Indeed, all these invariants are defined in a similar way (using Verma modules of arbitrary complex highest weight) and they all satisfy a genus bound
[
27
,
21
]
. The key point, and the main difference with the generic
q
case, is the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-graded structure of the tensor categories behind the construction of these invariants (or the “equivariant” nature of such categories, as in
[
27
, Theorem 2]
).

2.
Fibred links and fiber surfaces

2.1.
Fibred links

By a
fibred link
in
S^{3}
we mean an oriented link
L
together with a fibration
S^{3}\setminus L\to S^{1}
in which the closure of each fiber is a Seifert surface for
L
. If
L
is fibred, then the fibration (and hence the fiber surface) is unique up to isotopy, hence being fibred is a property of links and not an additional structure. Moreover, it is well-known that the fiber surface of a fibred link is the unique Seifert surface of minimal genus.

Let
H_{+}
and
H_{-}
be the positive and negative Hopf links respectively, these are fibred links. Let
A^{+}
(resp.
A^{-}
) be an unknotted, oriented annulus embedded in
S^{3}
with a negative (resp. positive) full twist. Then
\partial A^{\pm}=H_{\pm}
as oriented links,
A^{\pm}
are the fiber surfaces for the respective Hopf links. We call
A^{+}
(resp.
A^{-}
) a positive (resp. negative)
Hopf band
.

2.2.
Plumbing Hopf bands

Let
F\subset S^{3}
be an oriented compact surface with boundary and

\alpha\subset F
a properly embedded simple arc. We say that a compact surface
F^{\prime}\subset S^{3}
is obtained from
F
by
plumbing
a Hopf band
A^{\pm}
along
\alpha

F^{\prime}=F\cup A^{\pm}
in such a way that:

(1)

the intersection
A^{\pm}\cap F
is a tubular neighborhood of
\alpha
in
F
;

(2)

the core curve of
A^{\pm}
bounds a disk in
S^{3}\setminus F
that near
\alpha
lies on the positive side of
F
.

F^{\prime}

\alpha\subset F
. The green arrows are normal vectors indicating the orientation of
F
, these force the disk bounded by the core of the new annulus to locally be above
F
.

We also say that
F
is obtained by
deplumbing
the Hopf band
A^{\pm}
to
F^{\prime}
. See Figure
3
. The following theorem had been conjectured by Harer in
[
17
]
and was proved by Giroux-Goodman in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
13
]

Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a fibred link with fiber surface
F
. Then

2.3.
Plane fields and the Hopf invariant

A fibred link induces an oriented plane field
\xi_{F}
of
S^{3}
as follows
[
13
,
29
,
32
]
. First, the tangent planes to the fiber surfaces induce a plane field on
S^{3}\setminus N(L)
, where
N(L)
is a tubular neighborhood of
L
. This plane field can be extended through
N(L)
as follows: let
(r,\theta,z)
be oriented cylindrical coordinates around
L
. Then consider the plane field on
N(L)
which is the kernel of
f(r)dz+r^{2}d\theta
where
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is smooth,
f\equiv 1
near 0 and
f\equiv 0
near 1. At
\partial N(L)
this coincides with the tangent planes to the fibers, so the fields glue to an oriented plane field
\xi_{F}
on the whole three-sphere.

Now let
\xi_{0}
be the oriented plane field of
S^{3}
associated to the unknot with the trivial (disk) fiber. If
\xi_{F}
and
\xi_{0}
are in general position, then

C_{\pm}=\{p\in S^{3}\ |\ \xi_{F}(p)=\pm\xi_{0}(p)\}

are oriented links in
S^{3}
, the orientation being determined by the co-orientation of the plane fields induced from the orientation of
S^{3}
. We denote

\lambda(\xi_{F}):=lk(C_{+},C_{-}),

this is the obstruction to homotope
\xi_{F}
onto
\xi_{0}
. Under a trivialization of
TS^{3}
,
\xi_{F}
corresponds to a map
f_{\xi_{F}}:S^{3}\to S^{2}
whose homotopy class in
\pi_{3}(S^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is determined by the
Hopf invariant

H(f_{\xi_{F}})\in\mathbb{Z}
. The number
H(f_{\xi_{F}})-H(f_{\xi_{0}})
is independent of the trivialization and coincides with
\lambda(\xi_{F})

[
29
]
. This is called the “enhanced Milnor number” in
[
29
,
32
]
, but we prefer to simply call it the
Hopf invariant
of the plane field.

It is shown, e.g. in
[
13
]
, that
\lambda(\xi_{A^{+}})=0
and
\lambda(\xi_{A^{-}})=1
. Since
\lambda
is additive under plumbing
[
28
]
, it follows that if a fiber surface
F
is obtained by plumbing
n_{\pm}
copies of
A^{\pm}
and deplumbing
m_{\pm}
copies of
A^{\pm}
, then

\lambda(\xi_{F})=n_{-}-m_{-}.

3.
ADO invariants

In what follows we let
q
be a primitive
2p
-th root of unity, say
q=e^{\pi i/p}
. For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
denote
[\lambda]=\frac{q^{\lambda}-q^{-\lambda}}{q-q^{-1}}
. Here
q^{a+ib}=e^{\pi(-b+ia)/p}
for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. For more details, see
[
25
,
4
]
.

3.1.
Unrolled and small quantum
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

The
unrolled restricted quantum group

\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with generators
E,F,K^{\pm 1},H
and relations

\displaystyle[H,E]

\displaystyle=2E,

\displaystyle[H,F]

\displaystyle=-2F,

\displaystyle[H,K^{\pm 1}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle KE

\displaystyle=q^{2}EK,

\displaystyle KF

\displaystyle=q^{-2}FK,

\displaystyle KK^{-1}

\displaystyle=K^{-1}K=1,

\displaystyle[E,F]

\displaystyle=\frac{K-K^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}},

\displaystyle E^{p}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle F^{p}

\displaystyle=0.

This is a Hopf algebra if we define

\displaystyle\Delta(E)

\displaystyle=E\otimes K+1\otimes E,

\displaystyle\epsilon(E)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle S(E)

\displaystyle=-EK^{-1},

\displaystyle\Delta(F)

\displaystyle=K^{-1}\otimes F+F\otimes 1,

\displaystyle\epsilon(F)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle S(F)

\displaystyle=-KF,

\displaystyle\Delta(K)

\displaystyle=K\otimes K,

\displaystyle\epsilon(K)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle S(K)

\displaystyle=K^{-1},

\displaystyle\Delta(H)

\displaystyle=H\otimes 1+1\otimes H,

\displaystyle\epsilon(H)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle S(H)

\displaystyle=-H.

The
small quantum group

\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is obtained from
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
by taking the subalgebra generated by
E,F,K^{\pm 1}
and modding out by
K^{2p}=1
. Thus,
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is finite-dimensional of dimension
2p^{3}
. We will consider the (ribbon) extension
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
obtained by adding a group-like
k
of order
4p
such that
k^{2}=K
and
kE=qEk
and
kF=q^{-1}Fk
.

3.2.
The ribbon category
\mathcal{C}

A
weight module
over
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is a
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-module
V
with diagonalizable
H
-action such that
K=q^{H}
as operators on
V
. The eigenvalues of
H
are the
weights
. We will denote by
\mathcal{C}
the category of finite-dimensional weight
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-modules. This is braided with
c_{V,W}:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
defined by

c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=\tau_{V,W}(\mathcal{H}\overline{\Theta}(v\otimes w))

where

\overline{\Theta}=\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}c_{m}\cdot E^{m}\otimes F^{m}

with

(2)

\displaystyle c_{m}=\frac{(q-q^{-1})^{m}}{[m]!}q^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}},

\mathcal{H}
denotes the operator
V\otimes W\to V\otimes W
defined by

\mathcal{H}(v\otimes w)=q^{\lambda\mu/2}v\otimes w

if
v
has weight
\lambda
and
w
has weight
\mu
, and
\tau_{V,W}
is the transposition
v\otimes w\mapsto w\otimes v
. The category
\mathcal{C}
is pivotal with left evaluations and coevaluations the usual ones of vector spaces, and with right evaluations and coevaluations given by

\overrightarrow{\text{ev}}_{V}(v\otimes v^{\prime})=v^{\prime}(K^{1-p}v),\ \ \overrightarrow{\text{coev}}_{V}(1)=\sum v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes K^{p-1}v_{i}.

Here
(v_{i})
is a basis of
V
and
(v_{i}^{\prime})
is the dual basis of
V^{*}
. It follows that there is an isomorphism
j_{V}:V^{**}\to V
such that

j_{V}^{-1}(v)(v^{\prime})=v^{\prime}(K^{1-p}v).

The pivotal and braided structure are compatible, hence define a ribbon category structure on
\mathcal{C}
. For any object
X
of
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\theta_{X}:X\to X
the twist.

3.3.
Verma modules

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the Verma module
V_{\lambda}
is the
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-module with basis
v_{0},\dots,v_{p-1}
and action given by

\displaystyle Ev_{i}

\displaystyle=[\lambda-i+1]v_{i-1},

\displaystyle Fv_{i}

\displaystyle=[i+1]v_{i+1},

\displaystyle Hv_{i}

\displaystyle=(\lambda-2i)v_{i},

\displaystyle Kv_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{\lambda-2i}v_{i},

see
[
25
]
. The dual
V_{\lambda}^{*}
is a
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-module with

\displaystyle Ev^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=-q^{-\lambda+2(i+1)}[\lambda-i]v^{\prime}_{i+1},

\displaystyle Fv^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=-q^{\lambda-2i}[i]v^{\prime}_{i-1},

\displaystyle Hv^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=(-\lambda+2i)v^{\prime}_{i},

\displaystyle Kv^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle=q^{-\lambda+2i}v^{\prime}_{i}.

Here
(v^{\prime}_{i})
denote the dual basis of
V_{\lambda}^{*}
. We will refer to the above basis of
V_{\lambda}
and
V_{\lambda}^{*}
as
standard basis
. Any tensor product of standard basis will also be called a standard basis of the corresponding vector space (a tensor product of
V_{\lambda}
’s and
V_{\lambda}^{*}
’s).

For
V_{\lambda}
one has

(3)

\displaystyle j_{V_{\lambda}}(v^{\prime\prime}_{i})=q^{(p-1)(\lambda-2i)}v_{i}

where
v^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in V_{\lambda}^{**}
is the basis dual to
(v^{\prime}_{i})
. The twist is given by

\theta_{V_{\lambda}}=q^{\lambda^{2}-(p-1)\lambda}\cdot\text{id}_{V_{\lambda}}.

3.4.
Relation to
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\prime}
-modules

Let
\mathcal{C}_{[0]}
be the subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of modules with integer weights. If
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{[0]}
, then
K^{2p}
acts by the identity on
Y
, so that
Y
is a
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\prime}
-module. This defines a functor
F:\mathcal{C}_{[0]}\to\text{Rep}(\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\prime})
. The Hopf algebra
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\prime}
is ribbon
[
8
]
and the functor
F
is a ribbon functor. In particular, if
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{[0]}
, the twist
\theta_{Y}
of
\mathcal{C}
coincides with left multiplication by the ribbon element of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})^{\prime}
, that is

\theta_{Y}=\frac{1}{4p}\sum_{i,j=0}^{4p-1}\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}c_{m}q^{-ij/2}k^{i}F^{m}K^{1-p}k^{j}E^{m}

for the
c_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
as in (
2
). The inverse ribbon is given by

\theta_{Y}^{-1}=\frac{1}{4p}\sum_{i,j=0}^{4p-1}\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}c_{m}q^{-ij/2}S(k^{j}E^{m})K^{1-p}k^{i}F^{m}.

This applies in particular to the
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
module
Y=V_{\lambda}^{*}\otimes V_{\lambda}
(for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
) which clearly has integer weights.

3.5.
ADO invariants

We will assume the reader is familiar with the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant
F(T)
of framed, oriented,
\mathcal{C}
-colored tangles
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,1]
, see
[
38
]
. If all components of
T
have the same color
V\in\mathcal{C}
, we will denote the invariant by
F(T,V)
. If
L
is a (framed, oriented) link in
S^{3}
and
L_{o}
is a
(1,1)
-tangle (oriented upwards at the endpoints) obtained by opening
L
at a strand, then
F(L_{o},V_{\lambda})
is a
\overline{U}_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-morphism
V_{\lambda}\to V_{\lambda}
. Since
V_{\lambda}
is simple (for generic
\lambda
), it must be
F(L_{o},V_{\lambda})=\alpha_{\lambda}\cdot\text{id}_{V_{\lambda}}
for some
\alpha_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}
. This turns out to be independent of the place where the cut is happening, hence it is a link invariant that we will denote by
N_{p}^{\prime}(L,\lambda)
. The ADO invariant of the unframed, oriented link
L
is

N_{p}(L,\lambda):=q^{(p-1)\lambda\cdot w(L)}N_{p}^{\prime}(L,\lambda)

where
w(L)
is the writhe of the (framed) link
L
. Setting
t=q^{2\lambda}
this becomes a Laurent polynomial in
t
(up to an overall fractional power of
t
) which we denote
N_{p}(L,t)
. More precisely

N_{p}(L,t)\in t^{\frac{(p-1)(s-1)}{2}}\mathbb{Z}[q^{2}][t^{\pm 1}]

where
s
is the number of components of
L

[
27
]
. Here
\mathbb{Z}[q]\subset\mathbb{C}
is the ring of cyclotomic integers. This is not symmetric in
t
(i.e. invariant under
t\mapsto t^{-1}
), but after the change of variable
t=q^{-2}x
it becomes symmetric in
x
, see
[
23
]
.

Let
H_{+}
and
H_{-}
be the positive and negative Hopf links respectively. Their ADO invariants are given by (see
[
25
]
):

(4)

\displaystyle N_{p}(H_{+},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}t^{\frac{p-1-2i}{2}}q^{-2i}=q^{2}t^{-(p-1)/2}+\dots+t^{(p-1)/2}

\displaystyle N_{p}(H_{-},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}t^{\frac{p-1-2i}{2}}q^{-2(1+i)}=t^{-(p-1)/2}+\dots+q^{-2}t^{(p-1)/2}.

In
x=q^{2}t
;

(5)

\displaystyle N_{p}(H_{+},x)

\displaystyle=-qx^{-(p-1)/2}+\dots-qx^{(p-1)/2}

\displaystyle N_{p}(H_{-},x)

\displaystyle=-q^{-1}x^{-(p-1)/2}+\dots-q^{-1}x^{(p-1)/2}.

4.1.
The top term of the ribbon element

We set up to understand the effect of Hopf plumbing on ADO invariants. This is done in Proposition
4.6
below. We need a couple lemmas for this.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

(1)

E^{p-1}(v_{i}^{\prime}\otimes v_{j})=0
if
j<i
.

(2)

F^{p-1}(v_{i}^{\prime}\otimes v_{j})=0
if
j>i
.

Proof.

Each standard basis vector
v^{\prime}_{k}\otimes v_{l}
has weight
w=2(k-l)
.
Since
E
increases weight by 2,
E^{p-1}(v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes v_{j})
has weight
2(i-j+p-1)>2(p-1)
as
j<i
. Since there are no vectors with such weight, this must be zero. A similar argument applies to
F^{p-1}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

E^{p-1}(v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes v_{i})=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{s=0}^{p-2}[\lambda-s]\right)v^{\prime}_{p-1}\otimes v_{0}.

Proof.

For any
l,k
we have

(6)

E^{l}v_{i}=[\lambda-i+1]\dots[\lambda-i+l]v_{i-l}

and

(7)

E^{k}v^{\prime}_{i}=(-1)^{k}q^{-k\lambda+2ki+k(k+1)}[\lambda-i]\dots[\lambda-(i+k-1)]v^{\prime}_{i+k}.

By the
q
-binomial theorem

E^{p-1}(v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes v_{i})=\sum_{l}\binom{p-1}{l}_{q^{-2}}E^{l}v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes K^{l}E^{p-1-l}v_{i}.

The
l
-th term on the right hand side is a scalar multiple of
v^{\prime}_{i+l}\otimes v_{i+l-(p-1)}
. This is nonzero only for
i+l=p-1
hence

E^{p-1}(v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes v_{i})=\binom{p-1}{p-1-i}_{q^{-2}}E^{p-1-i}v^{\prime}_{i}\otimes K^{p-1-i}E^{i}v_{i}.

Now, using (
6
) and (
7
) with
l=i
and
k=p-1-i
, and that
\binom{p-1}{k}_{q^{-2}}=(-1)^{k}q^{k(k+1)}

4.4.
Special classes of fibred links

A link in
S^{3}
is
strongly quasi-positive
(SQP) if it is the closure of a braid that is a product of elements of the form

(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j+1}\dots\sigma_{i-1})\sigma_{i}(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j+1}\dots\sigma_{i-1})^{-1}.

Equivalently (and more geometrically), a link is SQP if it is the boundary of a quasi-positive surface, that is, a subsurface
F
of the fiber
F_{T}
of a positive torus link with the property that
\pi_{1}(F)\to\pi_{1}(F_{0})
is injective
[
33
]
.

Corollary 4.13
.

Suppose
L
is fibred and strongly quasi-positive. Then
N_{p}(L)
has maximal degree and its top coefficient is
(-q)^{2g(L)+s-1}
.

Proof.

Fibred strongly-quasipositive links are precisely the links that can be obtained by plumbing/deplumbing positive Hopf bands: this is because a plumbing of two surfaces is strongly quasi-positive if and only if each of the two surfaces is so
[
34
]
, and the positive Hopf band is quasi-positive while the negative Hopf band is not
[
33
]
. Hence,
n_{-}=m_{-}=0
for a fibred SPQ link so that
\lambda(F)=0
and the corollary follows from Theorem
1
.
∎

18
]
. Also, deplumbing is necessary in the class of SQP knots (see e.g.
[
7
]
).

Another class of links that are fibered are homogeneous braid closures, as shown in
[
36
]
. A braid
\beta\in B_{n}
is said to be
homogeneous
if it can be written as a product of the standard generators
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
in a such a way that every generator appears and all appearances of a generator
\sigma_{i}
have the same sign (in particular, positive braids are homogeneous).

Corollary 4.15
.

If
L
is the closure of a homogeneous braid
\beta\in B_{n}
, then
N_{p}(L,x)
has maximal degree and its top coefficient is
(-q)^{w(\beta)-k_{+}+k_{-}}
where
w(\beta)
is the writhe of the braid and
k_{+}
(resp.
k_{-}
) is the number of generators that appear with positive (resp. negative) sign.

Proof.

Let
F
be the surface obtained by applying Seifert’s algorithm to the braid. Then, as shown in
[
36
]
,
F
can be obtained by plumbing
n_{+}=N_{+}-k_{+}
positive Hopf bands and
n_{-}=N_{-}-k_{-}
negative Hopf bands, where
N_{+}
(resp.
N_{-}
) is the number of positive (resp. negative) appearances of generators in
\beta
. Since no deplumbing is needed, we have
m_{+}=m_{-}=0
and hence

2g(L)+s-1-2\lambda(\xi_{F})=n_{+}-n_{-}-m_{+}+m_{-}=N_{+}-N_{-}-k_{+}+k_{-}=w(\beta)-k_{+}+k_{-}

so the corollary follows from our theorem.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Note that positive braid closures are both fibred SQP and homogeneous, so both corollaries above apply: for
\beta\in B_{n}
the top coefficient of
N_{p}(\widehat{\beta})
is
(-q)^{w-n+1}=(-q)^{2g+s-1}
where
w
is the writhe. Positive knots are not necessarily fibred (e.g.
5_{2}
), but if they are, then they are SQP by
[
35
]
, hence the top coefficient of their
N_{p}
’s are as in Corollary
4.13
above.

∗ \ast -Autonomous Envelopes and Conservativity
Michael Shulman

Abstract
We prove 2-categorical conservativity for any { 𝟎 , ⊤ } \{\mathbf{0},\top\} -free fragment of MALL over its corresponding intuitionistic version: that is, that the universal map from a closed symmetric monoidal category to the ∗ \ast -autonomous category that it freely generates is fully faithful, and similarly for other doctrines. This implies that linear logics and graphical calculi for ∗ \ast -autonomous categories can also be interpreted canonically in closed symmetric monoidal categories. In particular, every closed symmetric monoidal category can be fully embedded in a ∗ \ast -autonomous category, preserving both tensor products and internal-homs. In fact, we prove this directly first with a Yoneda-style embedding (an enhanced “Hyland envelope” that can be regarded as a polycategorical form of Day convolution), and deduce 2-conservativity afterwards from Hyland–Schalk double gluing and a technique of Lafont. The same is true for other fragments of ∗ \ast -autonomous structure, such as linear distributivity, and the embedding can be enhanced to preserve any desired family of nonempty limits and colimits.

In addition, the multicategorical Chu construction itself has a simple universal property, which generalizes and strictifies that of
[
Pav97
]
.
Let
\spoly
denote the category of
\ast
-polycategories, and
\supoly
that of co-subunary polycategories.

Theorem 4.3
.

\mathsf{Chu}
is right adjoint to the forgetful
\ssU:\spoly\to\supoly
, and the adjunction is comonadic.

Proof.

The counit
\ssU\mathsf{Chu}(\cE)\to\cE
extracts
A^{+}
from
A
,
f_{1}^{+}
from a morphism
f:(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\to B_{1}
, and
\underline{\smash{f}}
from a morphism
f:(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\to()
.
The unit
\cP\to\mathsf{Chu}(\ssU(\cP))
sends an object
A
to
(A,A^{*},\ep_{A})
, where
\ep_{A}\in\cP(A,A^{*};\,)
is the duality counit, and a morphism
f\in\cP(\Gamma;\Delta)
to the family of all its co-subunary duality images.
The coalgebras for the induced comonad are
co-subunary
\ast
-polycategories
, which by
[
Shu20
, §7]
are equivalent to ordinary
\ast
-polycategories.
∎

5
Envelopes

Let
\cP
be a polycategory; we now describe Hyland’s Yoneda-type embedding of
\cP
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
Hyl02
, §5]
)
.

The
envelope
of
\cP
is the Chu construction

\envp=\mathsf{Chu}(\modp,\cP).

Thus, an object of
\envp
is two modules
\cU
,
\cV
with a module map
(\cU,\cV)\to\cP
.
By
4.2
,
\envp
is
\ast
-autonomous
, and contains
\modp
as a symmetric monoidal full subcategory via
\cU\mapsto(\cU,\cU\multimap\cP,\mathsf{ev})
.
Hence it also contains
U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP)
as a full sub-multicategory via
A\mapsto(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A},{}_{A}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}},\gamma_{A})
, where
\gamma_{A}:(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A},{}_{A}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}})\to\cP
is composition in
\cP
.
In fact, Hyland showed:

Theorem 5.2

(
[
Hyl02
, §5]
)
.

The assignment
A\mapsto(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A},{}_{A}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}},\gamma_{A})
extends to a full embedding of
polycategories

\cP\hookrightarrow\envp
.

Proof.

A morphism
g:(\gamma_{A_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{A_{m}})\to(\gamma_{B_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{B_{n}})
in
\envp
is a compatible family:

\displaystyle g^{+}_{j}:

\displaystyle(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{1}},\dots,\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{m}},{}_{B_{1}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}},\dots,\widehat{{}_{B_{j}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}},\dots,{}_{B_{n}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}})

\displaystyle\to\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{B_{j}}

\displaystyle g^{-}_{i}:

\displaystyle(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{1}},\dots,\widehat{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{i}}},\dots,\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{m}},{}_{B_{1}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}},\dots,{}_{B_{n}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}})

\displaystyle\to{}_{A_{i}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}

\displaystyle\ug:

\displaystyle(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{1}},\dots,\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{m}},{}_{B_{1}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}},\dots,{}_{B_{n}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}})

\displaystyle\to\cP.

By (
3.5
), each of these is equivalent to a morphism
(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})\to(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
in
\cP
, and the compatibility conditions say they all correspond to the same such morphism.
Functoriality is straightforward.
∎

Thus, any polycategory
\cP
embeds fully-faithfully in a
\ast
-autonomous category, and indeed in a Chu construction.
An explicit way to extract a morphism in
\cP
from a morphism
g:(\gamma_{A_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{A_{m}})\to(\gamma_{B_{1}},\dots,\gamma_{B_{n}})
is to evaluate the component

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{1}}(A_{1};)\times\cdots\times\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{m}}(A_{m};)\times{}_{B_{1}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}(;B_{1})\times\cdots\times{}_{B_{n}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}(;B_{n})\to\cP(A_{1},\dots,A_{m};B_{1},\dots,B_{n})}

of
\ug
at the identities
(1_{A_{1}},\dots,1_{A_{m}},1_{B_{1}},\dots,1_{B_{n}})
.

Remark 5.3
.

If
\cP
is an ordinary category regarded as a unary co-unary polycategory, then the categories
[\cP^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{Set}]
and
[\cP,\mathsf{Set}]
are equivalent to the categories of modules whose only nonempty values have the form
\cU(A;\,)
and
\cU(\,;A)
, respectively.
The functor
(-)\multimap\cP
restricts to Isbell conjugation interchanging these two subcategories, and the Hyland envelope contains the Isbell envelope
[
Isb66
]
.

6
Preserving Tensors and Cotensors

Hyland’s envelope does not preserve any tensor or cotensor products that exist in
\cP
,
but we can modify it to do so along the lines of
[
FK72
]
and
[
Kel82
, §3.12]
.

Suppose
\cP
is equipped with a set
\cJ
of tensor and cotensor products that exist (potentially including the nullary cases of a unit and/or counit), which we call
distinguished
.
Our intended example is
\cP=\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
from
section

2
, with the tensor products and unit coming from
\cC
, and the cotensor products
(\overline{A}\invamp B)=(A\multimap B)
.

Definition 6.1
.

A
(\cP,\cJ)
-
module
is a module that respects the distinguished tensor and cotensor products.
E.g. if
(A\ten B)\in\cJ
, the induced maps such as

\cU(\Gamma,A\ten B;\Delta)\to\cU(\Gamma,A,B;\Delta)

are isomorphisms.

Let
\modpj\subseteq\modp
consist of the
(\cP,\cJ)
-modules.
Of course,
\cP
is a
(\cP,\cJ)
-module, as is any shift of a
(\cP,\cJ)
-module; thus
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A},{}_{A}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}\in\modpj
.

Theorem 6.2
.

The embedding
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!:U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP)\to\modpj
preserves distinguished tensor products.
Dually, the embedding
{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}:U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP^{\mathrm{op}})\to\modpj
takes distinguished cotensor products (which are tensor products in
\cP^{\mathrm{op}}
) to tensor products.

Proof.

Let
\cU
be a
(\cP,\cJ)
-module and
\Gamma
a list of
(\cP,\cJ)
-modules. Then by (
3.5
) and the assumption on
\cU
we have natural bijections:

\modp(\Gamma,\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A},\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{B}\mathbin{;}\cU)\cong\modp(\Gamma\mathbin{;}\cU[A,B;\,])\cong\modp(\Gamma\mathbin{;}\cU[A\ten B;\,])\cong\modp(\Gamma,\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A\ten B}\mathbin{;}\cU).

Thus,
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A\ten B}
is a tensor product
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A}\ten\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{B}
. The dual statement is similar.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

(i)

\modpj
is a reflective subcategory of
\modp
.

(ii)

If
\cU\in\modp
and
\cV\in\modpj
then
(\cU\multimap\cV)\in\modpj
.

(iii)

\modpj
has a closed symmetric monoidal structure such that the reflector is strong monoidal and the inclusion preserves internal-homs.

Proof.

For a list of objects
\Gamma
, write
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma}
for the tensor product of all their representable modules
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A}
, and similarly
{}_{\Gamma}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
for the tensor product (not a cotensor product!
\modp
has no cotensors) of their dual representables
{}_{A}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
.
In particular,
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
is the ordinary representable presheaf at the object
(\Gamma,\overline{\Delta})\in F_{\ten}U_{\mathrm{multi}}\mathord{\Asterisk}\cP
, so by the ordinary Yoneda lemma, a morphism
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}\to\cU
is the same as an element of
\cU(\Gamma;\Delta)
.
It follows that
\cU
respects a tensor product
A\ten B
if and only if every morphism
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma,A,B}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}\to\cU
extends uniquely to a morphism
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma,A\ten B}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}\to\cU
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma,A,B}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cU}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma,A\ten B}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!}

and similarly for cotensor products.
Thus,
\modpj
is a
small-orthogonality class
in
\modp
, so its reflectivity follows from a standard “small object argument” iterative construction as in
[
AR94
, 1.36–1.38]
.
(Since
\modp
is locally finitely presentable and the objects
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma,A,B}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
and
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{\Gamma,A\ten B}\ten{}_{\Delta}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
are finitely presentable, this construction requires only countably many steps and is fully constructive.)

This proves
(i)
.
Now by definition

(\cU\multimap\cV)(\Gamma;\Delta)=\modp(\cU,\cV[\Gamma;\Delta]),

so
(ii)
follows since
\cV
is a
(\cP,\cJ)
-module.
Finally,
(iii)
follows formally
[
Day72
]
; the tensor product
\mathbin{\ten_{\cJ}}
of
\modpj
is the reflection of that of
\modp
.
∎

Example 6.4
.

By the formula for
\cU\ten\cV
on p28 of
[
Hyl02
]
, any elements
u\in\cU(A;\,)
and
v\in\cV(B;\,)
induce an element of
(\cU\ten\cV)(A,B;\,)
, hence of
(\cU\mathbin{\ten_{\cJ}}\cV)(A\ten B;\,)
and thence
(\cU\mathbin{\ten_{\cJ}}\cV)(C;\,)
for any
C\to A\ten B
in
\cP
.
Thus we have a map

\cP(C,A\ten B)\times\cU(A;\,)\times\cV(B;\,)\overset{}{\longrightarrow}(\cU\mathbin{\ten_{\cJ}}\cV)(C;\,)

showing that
\mathbin{\ten_{\cJ}}
is similar to Day convolution
[
Day70
]
.

Definition 6.5
.

Given
(\cP,\cJ)
, its
envelope
is the Chu construction

\envpj=\mathsf{Chu}(\modpj,\cP).

Theorem 6.6
.

\envpj
is
\ast
-autonomous, contains
\cP
as a full sub-polycategory, and the inclusion preserves the distinguished tensor and cotensor products.

Proof.

Since
\modpj
is a full sub-multicategory of
\modp
,
\envpj
is a full sub-polycategory of
\envp
.
Since it contains the image of
\cP
, which is a full sub-polycategory of
\envp
by
5.2
, the inclusion
\cP\hookrightarrow\envpj
is also full.

Now the embedding of
\modpj
in
\envpj
, like that of any monoidal category in its Chu construction, preserves tensor products.
Since the embedding of
U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP)
in
\modpj
preserves distinguished tensor products by
6.2
, so does the composite
U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP)\hookrightarrow\modpj\hookrightarrow\envpj
.
Dually, the composite embedding
U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP)\hookrightarrow\modpj^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\envpj
preserves distinguished cotensor products, and by
section

3
the two embeddings coincide.
∎

Combining
2.1
and
6.6
, we obtain our first new embedding theorem.

Theorem 6.7
.

Any closed symmetric monoidal category
\cC
can be fully embedded in a
\ast
-autonomous category by a strong symmetric monoidal closed functor.

Proof.

By
2.1
,
\cC
embeds fully in
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
preserving tensor products and taking
A\multimap B
to
\overline{A}\invamp B
.
Let
\cJ
consist of these tensor and cotensor products, and embed
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
in the
\ast
-autonomous category
\mathsf{Env}_{(\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC,\cJ)}
, which by
6.6
preserves these tensor and cotensor products.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

The use of
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
is not strictly necessary: we have
\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC}\simeq\mathsf{Mod}_{\cC}
, and the preservation of cotensors
\overline{A}\invamp B
can be expressed directly in terms of a
\cC
-module as a preservation of internal-homs,
\cU(\Gamma;\Delta,A\multimap B)\cong\cU(\Gamma,A;\Delta,B)
.
However, it is convenient to prove
6.6
by separate reduction to
6.2
and its dual, which requires expressing internal-homs as cotensor products.

7
Preserving Limits and Colimits

By a

in a polycategory
\cP
we mean a cone of unary morphisms such that

\cP(\Gamma;\Delta,\llim_{i}A_{i})\to\llim_{i}\cP(\Gamma;\Delta,A_{i})

A
colimit
is a limit in
\cP^{\mathrm{op}}
.
Since limits of modules are pointwise,

\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{(\llim_{i}A_{i})}\cong\llim_{i}(\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A_{i}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad{}_{(\colim_{i}A_{i})}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}\cong\llim_{i}({}_{A_{i}}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}),

but in
\modp
or
\modpj

\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{(\colim_{i}A_{i})}
or
{}_{(\llim_{i}A_{i})}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
.
Now let
(\cP,\cJ)
be as in
section

6
, and
\cK
a set of distinguished limit and colimit cones in
\cP
.

Remark 7.1
.

We have been ignoring size, but
\cP
should everywhere be a
small
polycategory, and
\cK
should be a small set.
(
\cJ
is automatically small once
\cP

Definition 7.2
.

A
\pjk

(\cP,\cJ)
-module that in addition respects the distinguished limits and colimits in
\cK

\cU(\Gamma;\Delta,\llim_{i}D_{i})\to\llim_{i}\cU(\Gamma;\Delta,D_{i})

is an isomorphism for all distinguished limit cones, and dually for colimits.

The tautological module
\cP
is a
\pjk
-module, as is any shift of a
\pjk
-module; hence so are
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!_{A}
and
{}_{A}{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}
.
Let
\modpjk\subseteq\modp
consist of the
\pjk
-modules.

Theorem 7.3
.

(i)

\modpjk
is a reflective subcategory of
\modp
.

(ii)

If
\cU\in\modp
and
\cV\in\modpjk
then
(\cU\multimap\cV)\in\modpjk
.

(iii)

\modpjk

(iv)

The embedding
\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!:U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP)\to\modpjk

Dually, the embedding
{\reflectbox{\!\text{\char 136\relax}\!}}:U_{\mathrm{multi}}(\cP^{\mathrm{op}})\to\modpjk

(v)

The embeddings

Proof.

Parts

To express respect for a limit as an orthogonality property, we use colimits of modules as in the original
[

Note that if the limits and colimits in

For

, it remains to show that

To see this, let

Definition 7.4
.

Given
\pjk

is the Chu construction

\envpjk=\mathsf{Chu}(\modpjk,\cP).

Theorem 7.5
.

\envpjk
is a complete and cocomplete
\ast
-autonomous category, contains
\cP
as a full sub-polycategory, and the inclusion preserves the distinguished tensor and cotensor products and the distinguished limits and colimits.

Proof.

Just like
6.6
except for the final claim, which follows from the formulas for limits and colimits in a Chu construction and
7.3
(v)
:

\displaystyle\llim_{i}(A^{+}_{i},A^{-}_{i},e_{i})

\displaystyle=(\llim_{i}A^{+}_{i},\colim_{i}A^{-}_{i},\underline{\hskip 4.30554pt})

\displaystyle\colim_{i}(A^{+}_{i},A^{-}_{i},e_{i})

\displaystyle=(\colim_{i}A^{+}_{i},\llim_{i}A^{-}_{i},\underline{\hskip 4.30554pt})\qed

We want to combine this with
2.1
as before, but there is a complication.
For any limit cone in a closed symmetric monoidal category
\cC
, we might expect

\displaystyle\text{\textquestiondown}\qquad\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC(\Gamma,\overline{\Pi};\Delta,\overline{\Sigma},\llim_{i}A_{i})

\displaystyle=\cC(\Gamma,\Sigma;\Delta,\Pi,\llim_{i}A_{i})

\displaystyle\cong\llim_{i}\cC(\Gamma,\Sigma;\Delta,\Pi,A_{i})

\displaystyle=\llim_{i}\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC(\Gamma,\overline{\Pi};\Delta,\overline{\Sigma},A_{i})\qquad?

yielding a limit in
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
.
However, the isomorphism in the second line is only valid if
\Delta=\Pi=\emptyset
, whereas to have a limit in
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
the composite isomorphism must hold for all
\Delta
and
\Pi
.
Of course, if
\Delta\cup\Pi
is nonempty, then
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC(\Gamma,\overline{\Pi};\Delta,\overline{\Sigma},\llim_{i}A_{i})
is empty, as is each
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC(\Gamma,\overline{\Pi};\Delta,\overline{\Sigma},A_{i})
— but the latter only implies that their limit is empty
if the diagram is nonempty!

And indeed, a terminal object in
\cC
need not be terminal in
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC
: the latter requires
\mathord{\Asterisk}\cC(\Gamma,\overline{\Pi};\Delta,\overline{\Sigma},1)=1
always, but the former only ensures this when
\Pi=\Delta=\emptyset
.
Similar considerations apply for colimits, so the best enhancement of
6.7
we can manage is:

Theorem 7.6
.

Any closed symmetric monoidal category
\cC
can be fully embedded in a
\ast
-autonomous category by a strong symmetric monoidal closed functor, which preserves any chosen family of
nonempty
limits and colimits that exist in
\cC
.∎

With other choices of
(\cP,\cJ,\cK)
, we can embed other kinds of structures into
\ast
-autonomous categories as well.
For instance:

•

Any linearly distributive category can be fully embedded in a
\ast
-autonomous category, preserving tensors, cotensors, and any set of colimits that are preserved in each variable by
\ten
and limits that are preserved in each variable by
\invamp
.

•

Any ordinary category can be fully embedded in a
\ast
-autonomous category preserving any set of nonempty limits and colimits.
(By
section

5
, this is closely related to the Isbell envelope.)

Remark 7.7
.

In the special case when
\cC
is
cartesian
closed, of course the terminal object
1
is also the monoidal unit.
Since the embedding
\cC\hookrightarrow\envcjk
preserves the monoidal unit, the image of
1\in\cC
in
\envcjk
is again the monoidal unit, but it will no longer be terminal.
And if we include binary products in
\cK
, then this embedding preserves both tensor products and binary cartesian products, which coincide in
\cC
; hence tensor products and binary cartesian products
of objects in the image of
\cC
also coincide in
\envcjk
.
However,
\envcjk
is not itself cartesian monoidal: tensor products and cartesian products of objects not in the image of
\cC
need not coincide.

8
Conservativity

Since closed symmetric monoidal and
\ast
-autonomous categories model intuitionistic and classical linear logic, respectively,
7.6
implies a (1-)conservativity result.

Theorem 8.1
.

Classical linear logic with
(-)^{\bot},\ten,\invamp,\mathbbb{1},\bot,\multimap,\&,\oplus
is conservative over intuitionistic linear logic with
\ten,\mathbbb{1},\multimap,\&,\oplus
(but not
\mathbf{0},\top
).

Proof.

Let MA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL and IMA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL denote the given fragments, and let
T
be a theory in IMA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL.
By imposing an appropriate equivalence relation on proofs from
T
in IMA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL and MA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL respectively, we obtain a closed symmetric monoidal category
\cC_{T}
and a
\ast
-autonomous category
\cD_{T}
, both with binary products and coproducts, and each freely generated by a model of
T
.

By
7.6
,
\cC_{T}
embeds fully in
\mathsf{Env}_{\cC_{T}}
preserving all the structure.
Since
\mathsf{Env}_{\cC_{T}}
is a
\ast
-auto
nomous model of
T
, this embedding factors up to isomorphism through
\cD_{T}
.
Now any sequent in the language of IMA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL over
T
that is provable in MA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL yields a morphism in
\cD_{T}
between objects in the image of
\cC_{T}
.
Hence such a morphism also exists in
\mathsf{Env}_{\cC_{T}}
, and thus also in
\cC_{T}
.
∎

Similarly, we can show that MA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL is conservative over any smaller fragment of IMA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL, and that full MALL is conservative over any of its fragments.
(IMALL is not a fragment of MALL in this sense, since its judgmental structure is different.)

Perhaps surprisingly,
8.1
is almost best possible.
By
[
Sch91
]
, classical linear logic with
\mathbf{0}
and
\multimap
is
not
conservative over intuitionistic linear logic with the same connectives: the sequent

C\multimap((\mathbf{0}\multimap X)\multimap A),\,(C\multimap B)\multimap\mathbf{0}\vdash A

is provable in the former but not the latter.
Thus, not every closed symmetric monoidal category with initial object embeds monoidally into a
\ast
-autonomous category preserving the initial object.

Conservativity of MA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL over IMA
\!{}^{\raisebox{1.5pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}
LL says we can use the former to reason about the latter without changing the theorems that are provable.
Semantically, this means that we can assume a closed symmetric monoidal poset (with binary meets and joins) is
\ast
-autonomous (and hence use the syntax of classical linear logic) without changing the inequalities between objects of the original poset.

To a certain extent,
7.6
allows us to similarly use syntaxes for
\ast
-autonomous categories (e.g. term syntaxes for linear logic, or graphical calculi such as proof nets and circuit diagrams—see the references cited in
section

1
) to reason about a closed symmetric monoidal category
\cC
.
For instance, since
\cC\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Env}_{\cC}
is faithful and isomorphism-reflecting, if two morphisms in
\cC
can be proven equal using
\ast
-autonomous syntax, they were already equal in
\cC
, and if a morphism in
\cC
can be proven invertible using
\ast
-autonomous syntax, it was already invertible in
\cC
.

Since
\cC\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Env}_{\cC}
is full, we can also construct morphisms in
\cC
in this way; but
a priori
the
particular
morphism in
\cC
that we obtain might depend on the particular embedding of
\cC
into its envelope.
And while the envelope seems “canonical”, it is by no means the
unique
closed monoidal embedding of any given
\cC
in a
\ast
-autonomous category (for instance, if
\cC
has a zero object, then
\cC\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Chu}(\cC,0)
suffices).

As noted in the introduction, the most sensible way to avoid this is to show that the
universal
functor
\Phi:\cC\to\cD
from
\cC
to a
\ast
-autonomous category is fully faithful, which we call
2-conservativity
.
In this case, since any fully faithful embedding of
\cC
in a
\ast
-autonomous category factors through the universal one, all interpretations of
\ast
-autonomous syntax in
\cC
must coincide.

7.6
implies that
\Phi
is faithful and conservative, since
\cC\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Env}_{\cC}
factors through it.
We cannot show full-faithfulness of
\Phi
in the same way, but we can use a general technique introduced by Lafont
[
Laf88
]
that combines Artin gluing along a restricted Yoneda embedding (a.k.a. a “Kripke logical relation”), as generalized to the
\ast
-autonomous case by
[
Tan98
,
HS03
,
Has99
]
using
double gluing
.

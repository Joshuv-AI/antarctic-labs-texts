Abstract
. In [ 11 ] , Min Ru and Paul Vojta, among other things, proved the so-called general theorem (arithmetic part) which can be viewed as an extension of Schmidt’s subspace theorem. In this note, we extend their result by replacing the divisors by closed subschemes.



1.
Introduction and Statements

Let
X
be a complete variety.
Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line sheaf and let
D
be a nonzero effective
Cartier divisor on
X
. We define

\beta(\mathscr{L},D)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m\geq 1}h^{0}(\mathscr{L}^{N}(-mD))}{Nh^{0}(\mathscr{L}^{N})}\;.

In
[
11
]
, Min Ru and Paul Vojta proved the following theorem which can be viewed as an extension of the Schmidt’s subspace theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
11
]
, General theorem (Arithmetic Part))
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over a number field
k
, and
let
D_{1},\dots,D_{q}
be nonzero effective Cartier divisors
intersecting properly on
X
. Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line sheaf on
X
.
Let
S\subset M_{k}
be a finite set of places. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,
the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{i})m_{D_{i},S}(\mathfrak{p})\leq(1+\epsilon)h_{\mathscr{L}}(\mathfrak{p})

holds for all
k
-rational points
\mathfrak{p}
outside a proper Zariski-closed
subset of
X
.

Here,
m_{D,S}(\mathfrak{p}):=\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{D,v}(\mathfrak{p})
, where
\lambda_{D,v}
is a Weil function with respect to the divisor
D
and the place
v
.

The purpose of this note is to extend the above theorem to closed subschemes.
We first introduce some notations.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line sheaf and let
Y
be a closed subscheme
on a complete variety
X
. We define

\beta(\mathscr{L},Y)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m\geq 1}h^{0}(\mathscr{L}^{N}\otimes{\mathscr{I}}_{Y}^{m})}{Nh^{0}(\mathscr{L}^{N})},

where
{\mathscr{I}}_{Y}
is the ideal sheaf defining
Y
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be the blow up along
Y
and
E
be the exceptional divisor.
If
X
is smooth (or just Cohen Macaulay) and
Y
is a locally complete intersection, then

h^{0}(\mathscr{L}^{N}\otimes{\mathscr{I}}_{Y}^{m})=h^{0}(\pi^{*}\mathscr{L}^{N}(-mE))

for all
m\geq 1
.
See
[
6
, Remark 4.3.17]
. Therefore, in this case, we have

\beta(\mathscr{L},Y)=\beta(\mathscr{L},E):=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m\geq 1}h^{0}(\pi^{*}\mathscr{L}^{N}(-mE))}{Nh^{0}(\mathscr{L}^{N})}\;.

The main result of our theorem is stated as follows.

Main Theorem
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over a number field
k
, and
let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q}
be
closed subschems of
X
over
k

intersecting properly on
X
. Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line sheaf on
X
.
Let
S\subset M_{k}
be a finite set of places. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,
the inequality

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta(\mathscr{L},Y_{i})m_{Y_{i},S}(\mathfrak{p})\leq(1+\epsilon)h_{\mathscr{L}}(\mathfrak{p})

holds for all
k
-rational points
\mathfrak{p}
outside a proper Zariski-closed
subset of
X
.

Here,
m_{Y,S}(\mathfrak{p}):=\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(\mathfrak{p})
, where
\lambda_{Y,v}
is a Weil function with respect to the closed subscheme
Y
and the place
v
.
Closed subschems
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q}

are said to be
intersecting properly on
X
if
for any subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,q\}
such that
\cap_{i\in I}{\rm Supp}Y_{i}\neq\emptyset
, the sequence

\{\phi_{i,1}\ldots\phi_{i,\epsilon_{i}},i\in I\}
is a regular sequence in the local ring
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p},X}
for
\mathfrak{p}\in\cap_{i\in I}{\rm Supp}Y_{i}
,
where, for each
1\leq i\leq q
,
\phi_{i,1}\ldots\phi_{i,\epsilon_{i}}
are the local functions in
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{p},X}
defining
Y_{i}
. Therefore,
\epsilon_{i}\geq{\rm codim}~Y_{i}
, and
\sum_{i\in I}\epsilon_{i}\leq\dim X
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the same context, we say that
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q}
are in general position if
{\rm codim}\left(\cap_{i\in I}Y_{i}\right)\geq\sum_{i\in I}{\rm codim}~Y_{i}
for any
I\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
. Here we use the convention that
\dim\emptyset=-\infty
.
If
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q}
are of locally complete intersection closed subschemes intersecting properly on
X
, then they are in general position. The converse holds if
X
is Cohen-Macaulay by
[
8
, Theorem 17.4]
.

We note that our main theorem extends Theorem
1.1
and its
counter-part in Nevanlinna theory can also be obtained. For details of such correspondence, see
[
11
]
or
[
12
]
.

We now introduce some background on the study of Schmidt’s subspace theorem for closed subschemes. In
[
12
]
, the authors observed a connection between the work of Mckinnon and Roth in
[
9
]
and the arithmetic general theorem in
[
11
]
, as well as provided a simpler proof of the result of Mckinnon-Roth (see
[
9
]
).
The following is the statement.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety defined over
k
and

Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{q}
be closed subschemes of
X
defined over
k
such that at most
\ell
of the closed subschemes meet at any point of
X
. Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on
X
and let

(2)

\displaystyle\beta_{A,Y_{i}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\tilde{X}_{i},N\pi_{i}^{*}A-mE_{i})}{Nh^{0}(X,NA)},\quad 1\leq i\leq q,

where
\pi_{i}:\tilde{X}_{i}\to X
is the blowing-up of
X
along
Y_{i}
, with the associated exceptional divisor
E_{i}
.
Then, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{i=1}^{q}m_{Y_{i},S}(\mathfrak{p})\leq\ell(\max_{1\leq i\leq q}\{\beta_{A,Y_{i}}^{-1}\}+\epsilon)h_{A}(\mathfrak{p})

holds for all
\mathfrak{p}
outside a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z
of
X(k)
.

When the closed subschemes
Y_{i}=y_{i}
are distinct points in
X
, one may take
\ell=1
in Theorem
1.5
, ane the connection between Theorem
1.5
and
[
9
]
is due to the following inequality shown by Mckinnon and Roth:

\beta_{A,y_{i}}\geq\frac{n}{n+1}\epsilon_{y_{i}}(A),

where
\epsilon_{y_{i}}(A)
is the Seshadri constant defined below.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of a projective variety
X
and let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be the blowing-up of
X
along
Y
with the exceptional divisor
E
. Let
A
be a nef Cartier divisor on
X
. The
Seshadri constant
\epsilon_{Y}(A)
of
Y
with respect to
A
is the real number

\epsilon_{Y}(A)=\sup\{\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\,|\,\pi^{*}A-\gamma E\text{ is }\mathbb{Q}{\rm-nef}\}.

We note that when
Y
is a Cartier divisor on
X
, then
\epsilon_{Y}(A)
can be defined similarly without blowing up.

Recently, G. Heier and A. Levin also generalized the result in
[
9
]
from points to close subschmes.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over a number field
k
, and
let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{q}
be closed subschems of
X
defined over
k
, and in general position.
Let
A
be an ample Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
S\subset M_{k}
be a finite set of places. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
,
the inequality

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\epsilon_{Y_{i}}(A)m_{Y_{i},S}(\mathfrak{p})\leq(n+1+\epsilon)h_{A}(\mathfrak{p})

holds for all
\mathfrak{p}
outside a proper Zariski-closed
subset
Z
of
X(k)
.

They also proved (
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
)

(4)

\displaystyle\beta_{A,Y}\geq\frac{r}{n+1}\epsilon_{Y}(A),

where
\beta_{A,Y}
is as in (
2
).
Therefore, when
X
is smooth and the
Y_{i}
are of locally complete intersection, our main theorem implies
Theorem
1.7
by Remark
1.3
and
1.4
. Here, we let
\mathscr{L}=\mathcal{O}(A)
.

Note that the statement of Theorem
1.5
is weaker than the Main Theorem although Theorem
1.5
dealt with more general situation that
“at most
\ell
of the closed subschemes meet at any point of
X
”. Theorem
1.7
is also special since it only deals with
the Seshadri constant
\epsilon_{Y_{i}}(A)
rather than
\beta(\mathcal{O}(A),Y_{i})
.

The following is an example coming from
[
3
, Corollary 2]
. We use it to compare these two constants.

Example 1.8
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3},L_{4}
be four lines in general position
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and choose three points
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
, with
P_{i}\in L_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
,
P_{i}\notin L_{j}
if
j\neq i
. Let
X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be the blow-up of the three points
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3}
and let
D_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 4
be the strict transform of
L_{i}
. Let
A=\ell D_{1}+\ell D_{2}+\ell D_{3}+D_{4}
, where
\ell
is a positive integer. It is easy to compute that
A^{2}=6\ell^{2}+6\ell+1
,
(A.D_{i})=2\ell+1
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. (See also
[
3
, Proposition 2]
.)

It is explained in the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 5.6]
that

\beta(\mathcal{O}(A),D_{i})=\frac{\frac{2}{3}\xi_{i}A^{2}-\frac{1}{3}(A.D_{i})\xi_{i}^{2}}{A^{2}}.

Here,
\xi_{i}
is the constant such that
(A-\xi_{i}D_{i})^{2}=0
and
\xi_{i}=\frac{A^{2}}{2(A.D_{i})}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. (See also
[
3
, Proposition 2]
)
These datas give

\beta(\mathcal{O}(A),D_{i})=\frac{6\ell^{2}+6\ell+1}{8\ell+4}\geq\frac{3\ell}{4}.

On the other hand, it is clear that
A
is ample and the Seshadri constant
\epsilon_{D_{i}}(A)=\ell
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
Then the right hand side of (
4
) is
\frac{\ell}{3}
and left hand side is at least
\frac{3\ell}{4}
.

2.
Preliminary

Number field and valuations
.
For a number field
k
, recall that
M_{k}
denotes the set of places of
k
, and
that
k_{\upsilon}
denotes the completion of
k
at a place
\upsilon\in M_{k}
.
Norms
\|\cdot\|_{\upsilon}
on
k
are normalized so that

\|x\|_{\upsilon}=|\sigma(x)|^{[k_{\upsilon}:\mathbb{R}]}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\|p\|_{\upsilon}=p^{-[k_{\upsilon}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}

if
\upsilon\in M_{k}
is an archimedean place corresponding to an embedding

\sigma\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
or a non-archimedean place lying
over a rational prime
p
, respectively.

An
M_{k}
-constant is a collection
(c_{v})_{v\in M_{k}}
of real constants such
that
c_{v}=0
for all but finitely many
v
.
Heights are logarithmic and relative to the number field used as a base field,
which is always denoted by
k
. For example, if
P
is a point
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
with homogeneous coordinates
[x_{0}:\dots:x_{n}]
in
k
, then

h(P)=h_{\mathscr{O}(1)}(P)=\sum_{\upsilon\in M_{k}}\log\max\{\|x_{0}\|_{\upsilon},\dots,\|x_{n}\|_{\upsilon}\}\;.

Weil function for subschemes

We first recall some basic properties of local Weil functions associated to closed subschemes from
[
12

[
13
, Section 2]
.
We assume that the readers are familiar with the notion of Weil functions associated to divisors. (See

Let

V

.
It uniquely corresponds to the ideal sheave
{\mathscr{I}}_{Y}

J. Silverman showed in

.
We state some properties which we needed.

Lemma 2.1

(
[

]

.

The Weil function

satisfy the following condtions(up to

-constants):

(

)

(

If

\lambda_{X\cap Y}=\min\{\lambda_{X},\lambda_{Y}\}
.

(

If

\lambda_{X+Y}=\lambda_{X}+\lambda_{Y}

(

The Weil function can be expressed locally by the generators as follows.

We first recall some definitions and results from

]

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of natural numbers. A subset
N\subset\mathbb{N}^{r}
is said to be

saturated
if
{\bf a}+{\bf b}\in N
for any

{\bf a}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\bf b}\in N
.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 3.2,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
A
be a local ring and
(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r})
be a regular sequence of

A
. Let
M
and
N
be two saturated subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Then

{\mathcal{I}}(M)\cap{\mathcal{I}}(N)={\mathcal{I}}(M\cap N),

where, for
N\subset\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
{\mathcal{I}}(N)
is the ideal of
A
generated by

\{\phi_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\phi_{r}^{b_{r}}~|~{\bf b}\in N\}
.

Let
\square_{\ell}=(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{\ell}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
For each
{\bf t}\in\square_{\ell}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, let

N_{\ell}({\bf t},x)=\{{\bf b}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\mid t_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}b_{\ell}\geq x\}.

Let
m
,
r
and
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{r}
be positive integers such that
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\epsilon_{j}=m
(In application, we will use
m\leq\dim X
).
Let
A
be a local ring and
(\phi_{11},\dots,\phi_{1\epsilon_{1}},\dots,\phi_{r1},\dots,\phi_{r\epsilon_{r}})
be a regular sequence of
A
.
Then
{\mathcal{I}}(N_{m}({\bf t},x))
is the ideal of
A
generated by

\{\phi_{11}^{b_{11}}\cdots\phi_{1\epsilon_{1}}^{b_{1\epsilon_{1}}}\cdots\phi_{r1}^{b_{r1}}\cdots\phi_{r\epsilon_{r}}^{b_{r\epsilon_{r}}}~|~(b_{11},\dots,b_{1\epsilon_{1}},\dots,b_{r1},\dots,b_{r\epsilon_{r}})\in N_{m}({\bf t},x)\}.

Let
I_{j}
be the ideal of

\phi_{j1},\dots,\phi_{j\epsilon_{j}}
, where

.
Denote by
{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf t},x))

The following is our main lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in\square_{r}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then

{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf t},x))={\mathcal{I}}(N_{m}({\bf\tilde{t}},x)),

{\bf\tilde{t}}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{1},\ldots,t_{r},\ldots,t_{r})\in\square_{m}

Proof.

It suffices to check the inclusions on generators on both ideals.
The generators of
{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf t},x))
come from

with
{\bf b}\in N_{r}({\bf t},x)

Therefore, they are in the following form:

(5)

\displaystyle\phi_{11}^{b_{11}}\cdots\phi_{1\epsilon_{1}}^{b_{1\epsilon_{1}}}\cdots\phi_{r1}^{b_{r1}}\cdots\phi_{r\epsilon_{r}}^{b_{r\epsilon_{r}}},

where the

for

.
Since
{\bf b}\in N_{r}({\bf t},x)

\displaystyle t_{1}b_{11}+\cdots+t_{1}b_{1\epsilon_{1}}+\cdots+t_{r}b_{r1}+\cdots+t_{r}b_{r\epsilon_{r}}=t_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+b_{r}t_{r}\geq x.

Consequently,

(b_{11},\ldots,b_{1\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,b_{r1},\ldots,b_{r\epsilon_{r}})\in N_{m}({\bf\tilde{t}},x)

{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf t},x))
is a subset of
{\mathcal{I}}(N_{m}({\bf\tilde{t}},x))

On the other hand, the generator of

{\mathcal{I}}(N_{m}({\bf\tilde{t}},x))
are of the form as in (

(6)

\displaystyle t_{1}b_{11}+\cdots+t_{1}b_{1\epsilon_{1}}+\cdots+t_{r}b_{r1}+\cdots+t_{r}b_{r\epsilon_{r}}\geq x.

For each
1\leq j\leq r
, we let

\phi_{j1}^{b_{j1}}\cdots\phi_{j\epsilon_{j}}^{b_{j\epsilon_{j}}}\in I_{j}^{b_{j}}

{\mathcal{I}}(N_{m}({\bf\tilde{t}},x))

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let

{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf t},x))\cap{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf u},x))\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}(\lambda{\bf t}+(1-\lambda){\bf u},\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y))

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf t},x))\cap{\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}({\bf u},x))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{I}(N_{r}(\lambda{\bf t}+(1-\lambda){\bf u},\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)).

We will construct a filtration corresponding to the closed subschemes
Y_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq q
. We recall the following definition and result concerning filtration.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
W
be a vector space of finite dimension.
A

of

is a family of subspaces

{\mathcal{F}}=({\mathcal{F}}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
of subspaces of

W
such that
\mathcal{F}_{x}\supseteq\mathcal{F}_{y}
whenever
x\leq y
,
and such that
{\mathcal{F}}_{x}=\{0\}
for
x
big enough.
A basis
{\mathcal{B}}
of
W

is said to be
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
if

{\mathcal{B}}\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{x}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{F}}_{x}
for every real number
x\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.6

(Corvaja–Zannier
[
2

,
Levin
[
7
]
, Autissier
[
1
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
be two filtrations of
W
.
Then there exists a basis
of
W
which is adapted to both
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line sheaf, and let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{r}
be closed subschemes of
X
over
k

X

\cap_{i=1}^{r}\mbox{Supp}Y_{j}\neq\emptyset
.
For any fixed
{\bf t}\in\square_{r}
, we construct a filtration of

as follows:
for

,
one defines the ideal sheaf

(7)

Then

Good ultrafilters and highly saturated models: a friendly explanation
Paul E. Lammert

Abstract
. Highly saturated models are a fundamental part of the model-theoretic machinery of nonstandard analysis. Of the two methods for producing them, ultrapowers constructed with the aid of κ + \kappa^{+} -good ultrafilters seems by far the less popular. Motivated by the hypothesis that this is partly due to the standard exposition being somewhat dense, a presentation is given which is designed to be easier to digest.



1.
Should you read this?

The most difficult piece of the model-theoretic machinery underlying nonstandard analysis
(NSA) is certainly the construction of
polysaturated
models
(more generally,
\kappa
-saturated for
\kappa>\omega^{+}
).
There are two main approaches:
the ultralimit method and the good ultrafilter method.
Stroyan and Luxemburg
[
5
]
and
the recent volume
[
4
]
edited by Loeb and Wolff contain detailed
proofs using ultralimits. This method has the advantage that at least the general
idea seems clear enough. Lindstrøm’s lectures
[
3
]
is very
unusual in adopting the good ultrafilter method, which allows
use of plain ultrapower construction by relying on special properties of the ultrafilter.
This facilitates using ultrapowers to directly reason about nonstandard universes;
the ultralimit construction is too unwieldy for that.
Lindstrøm essentially copies the proof of Chang & Keisler
[
1
]

(itself a fleshing-out of Kunen
[
2
]
) of the existence of

\kappa^{+}
-saturated ultraproducts,
describing it as “an interesting, but quite technical piece of infinitary
combinatorics”. To me, “opaque” seemed an apt description.
The following attempt at clarification is offered firstly
to others who are not model theorists or set theorists, but are
interested in using nonstandard analysis (or model theory more generally)
in their home fields.
In mathematical essence, it follows §6.1 of Chang & Keisler.
The difference is primarily one of presentation.

Here is a brief description of the body of the paper, highlighting novel elements.
§
2.1
and
2.2
very briefly recall
ultraproducts and saturation.
Rather than just showing that the definition of
good ultrafilter
works,
§
2.3
follows some intution vaguely inspired by modal logic
to deduce a condition on an ultrafilter which endows an untraproduct with

\kappa
-saturation.
The condition is reformulated and abstracted into an order-theoretic definition
of
good
in §
2.4
in §
2.4
and used in the
first main Thm.
2.3
.
That may seem to be gratuitous generalization, but it has some naturality,
and the domain side of the abstract version is maintained through the
second main Thm.
3.1
, clarifying a little what is involved,
with no cost in complication.
In §
3
, the construction of a
\kappa^{+}
-good
ultrafilter on
\kappa
is given, structured as a transfinite recursion with
some complications. We add sets step-by-step to a filter to produce
an ultrafilter, simultaneously building in goodness for a set of functions.
(Which set is determined by the finished ultrafilter!)
The recursion also requires a supply of a certain auxiliary raw
material — partitions of
\kappa
with special properties, which are simply
consumed, not built into the final product in any meaningful sense.
The construction of this supply, which is completely independent of everything else,
is segregated in §
4
.

2.3.
Sufficient conditions on
{\mathcal{U}}
for saturation

Suppose the ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
guarantees the condition

\Sigma(x)

{\mathcal{U}}

Then, any mod-
{\mathcal{U}}

is another language with cardinality less than

and condition S immediately delivers what is required.

which guarantees condition S.

To that end, suppose

with
|\Sigma|<\kappa
is finitely satisfiable
in
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}{\mathcal{A}}_{i}

p:{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(\Sigma)\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}::\Theta\mapsto\{i\in I\,|\,{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\models\exists x(\wedge\Theta)\}.

(2.1)

p(\Theta)
is the set of indices of models in which the finite set of
formulas
\Theta
is satisfiable.
A binary relation-style notation is also convenient: ‘
i\,p\,\Theta
’
is synonymous with ‘
i\in p(\Theta)
’.
Clearly,
p
satisfies the condition

\Theta\subseteq\Theta^{\prime}\Rightarrow p(\Theta)\supseteq p(\Theta^{\prime}),\;\text{ equivalently }\;i\,p\,(\Theta\cup\Theta^{\prime})\Rightarrow i\,p\,\Theta\text{ \& }i\,p\,\Theta^{\prime}.

(Psb)

defining the notion of a
possibility function
(or binary relation).

With
p
denoting the possibility relation associated with
\Sigma
as above,

\Sigma
is satisfiable if there exists
(\Phi_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(\Sigma))_{i\in I}

actualizing

in the sense that

\text{(a): }i\,p\,\Phi_{i},\text{ and }\text{(b): }\text{for all }\theta\in\Sigma,\,\{i\in I\,|\,\theta\in\Phi_{i}\}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

This is because, according to (a),
p
certifies the existence of
an element
[(a_{i}\,|\,i\in I)]
of the ultraproduct
such that
{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\models\Phi_{i}[a_{i}]
.
Clause (b) then shows that
[(a_{i}\,|\,i\in I)]
satisfies every formula in
\Sigma
.
As linguistic aspects of
\Sigma
are untouched here,
we might as well ask for criteria guaranteeing that
every possibility function on any set
X

|X|<\kappa
,
has an actualization.

Now, by basic properties of ultrafilters, clause (b) in the definition implies
that
\{i\in I\,|\,\Theta\subseteq\Phi_{i}\}\in{\mathcal{U}}

for every
\Theta\in{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(X)
.
Thus, defining
p_{\Phi}
by
i\,p_{\Phi}\,\Theta

iff
\Theta\subseteq\Phi_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(X)
,

is a
possibility relation in its own right.
It satisfies the possibility condition (
Psb
), and the
strenghtened form of clause (b) shows that

p_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(X)\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
.
Such a possibility relation

has some special properties:

q\text{ consistent: }\quad i\,q\,(\Theta\cup\Theta^{\prime})\Leftarrow i\,q\,\Theta\text{ \& }i\,q\,\Theta^{\prime},

(Con)

which is inverse to the implication in (
Psb
), and

q\text{ locally finite: }\quad\text{ for all }i\in I,\;\sup\{|\Theta|\,|\,i\,q\,\Theta\}<\infty.

(Lfn)

(
Con
) is equivalent to the statement that

q^{-1}i=\{\Theta\in{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(X)\,|\,i\,q\,\Theta\}

is closed under finite union.
Thus, if
q
satisfies (
Psb
,
Con
,
Lfn
),
then
\bigcup q^{-1}i
is finite, hence itself a member of
q^{-1}i
,
for all
i\in I
. And, in that case

is an actualization of

.
So, a locally finite consistent possibility relation has an actualization.

Finally, suppose that
p^{\prime}\leq p

[meaning
p^{\prime}(\Theta)\subseteq p(\Theta)
for
\Theta\in{\mathcal{P}_{\omega}}(X)
].
Then, an actualization of
p^{\prime}
is also an actualization of
p

[only clause (a) sees
p
], and
p^{\prime}
is locally finite if
p
is.
Thus, if for every possibility relation
p
, there were two others,

Tp\leq p
locally finite, and
Mp\leq p
consistent,
then

satisfies
(

,

,
Lfn
), hence
has an actualization which is also an actualization of
p
.
Summing up,

guarantees saturation if the operators

and

If the ultrafilter

2.4.
Goodness

If

Given

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\alpha
is an infinite cardinal and

,
then
\alpha<\mathrm{cf}(\lambda^{\alpha})
.

Proof.

We prove this using the following

Claim (König’s theorem): if

\mu<\mu^{\,\mathrm{cf}\,\mu}
.

Suppose the proposition is false, so

\alpha\geq\mathrm{cf}\,\lambda^{\alpha}=\mathrm{cf}\,\mu
,

\mu=\lambda^{\alpha}
.
Then, using the Claim in the first inequality that follows,

\mu<\mu^{\mathrm{cf}\,\mu}\leq\mu^{\alpha}=\lambda^{\alpha\cdot\alpha}=\lambda^{\alpha}=\mu.

Diagonalize out of the enumeration as follows.

For, by unboundedness of

Suppose

Proof.

If
h

\gamma\in\hat{h}(x\wedge y)

Thus

∎

This result is immediately suggestive. Given

in

, we attempt to get a multiplicative

to a disjoint map

Chopping up the image of

Definition 3.1

(partition, large, polycell, independent)
.

A

is a set of disjoint subsets of
X
(
cells
of

),
which cover
X

P
is

|P|=|X|
.
If

is a collection of partitions, a

of a finite number of cells from distinct partitions

P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\in\Pi

\Pi
is denoted
\mathrm{cell}^{*}(\Pi)

\Pi
is

(or, “a set of independent partitions”)

\emptyset\not\in\mathrm{cell}^{*}(\Pi)

Definition 3.2

(consistent, polyconsistent)
.

If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a filter on
Y
, then

\mathrm{con}\,{\mathcal{F}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\{y\in Y\,|\,x\cap y\neq\emptyset,\forall x\in{\mathcal{F}}\}

(3.2)

is the set of subsets of
Y

consistent with

{\mathcal{F}}
.
A subset of (function into)
\mathcal{P}(Y)
is called consistent with
{\mathcal{F}}
if
it (its range) is a subset of
\mathrm{con}\,{\mathcal{F}}
.
A collection
\Pi
of partitions is
polyconsistent
with
{\mathcal{F}}
if

\mathrm{cell}^{*}(\Pi)\subseteq\mathrm{con}\,{\mathcal{F}}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The notion of consistency is central, as Lemmata
3.4
,

3.5
will show.
A few points to note:

\mathrm{con}\,{\mathcal{F}}
is closed under taking supersets.

{\mathcal{F}}\cup A
generates a filter iff the
\cap
-semilattice generated by
A

(finite intersections) is in
\mathrm{con}\,{\mathcal{F}}
.
Finally,
Y\setminus(\mathrm{con}\,{\mathcal{F}})
is closed under union.
For, if

, then

(a\cup b)\cap(x\cap y)=(a\cap x\cap y)\cup(b\cap x\cap y)=\emptyset
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Pi

polyconsistent with the

Since

Lemma 4.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\beta\leq\kappa
and

Proof.

By the axiom of Choice, there is a bijection between

inducing a (henceforth

Let

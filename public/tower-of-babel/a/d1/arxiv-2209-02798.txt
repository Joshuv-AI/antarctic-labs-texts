Abstract
. We discuss invariants of Cohen-Macaulay local rings that admit a canonical module ω \omega . Attached to each such ring R R , when ω \omega is an ideal, there are integers–the type of R R , the reduction number of ω \omega –that provide valuable metrics to express the deviation of R R from being a Gorenstein ring. In [ 6 ] and [ 7 ] we enlarged this list with the canonical degree and the bi-canonical degree. In this work we extend the bi-canonical degree to rings where ω \omega is not necessarily an ideal. We also discuss generalizations to rings without canonical modules but admitting modules sharing some of their properties.

1.
Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d
that has a canonical module
\omega
. Our central viewpoint is to look
at the properties of
\omega
as a way to refine our understanding
of
R
. We recall that
R
is Gorenstein when
\omega
is isomorphic to
R
. In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, if the canonical module
\omega
is an ideal, we treated metrics aimed at measuring the deviation from
R
being Gorenstein.
More precisely, two integers arise by considering the following lenghts (when
R
has dimension one):

(1)

If
(a)
is a minimal reduction of
\omega
,
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)=\lambda(\omega/(a)).

(2)

If
{\omega}^{**}
is the bidual of
\omega
,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(\omega^{**}/\omega).

The canonical degree
\mbox{\rm cdeg}(R)
is the focus of
[
6
]
and the bi-canonical degree
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
is the focus of
[
7
]
.
They are extended to higher dimensions (see Definition
2.1
and Definition
2.9
).

We note that both degrees are invariants of the ring and they vanish if and only if
R
is Gorenstein in codimension one.
One of the main points is that the minimal values of these degrees lead to a stratification of the Cohen-Macaulay property, such as almost Gorenstein rings
[
6
, 2.4, 2.5, 3.2, 3.3, 6.5]
and Goto rings
[
7
, 3.1, 4.2, 5.1]
. For instance, one dimensional almost Gorenstein rings are characterized by having the smallest possible canonical degree.

An interesting broad question is how to extend these degrees to more general settings. In this paper we focus on the generalization of the bi-canonical degree (the more suitable degree for computations) to two important cases, outlined below.

First, in Section
2
we give a brief survey of the canonical degree and the bi-canonical degree. These are recently introduced degrees but also descendants of a long line of degree functions established by Vasconcelos. His extensive research on arithmetic degrees (adeg, gdeg, jdet) and cohomological degrees (hdeg, bdeg) demonstrated
a good understanding of algebraic structures of rings and developed rich applications to normalization of algebras, ideals, and modules. In Section
3

we extend the bi-canonical degree to a Cohen-Macaulay local ring whose canonical module is not necessarily an ideal. The definition involves the classical theory developed by Auslander
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

(Definition
3.1
).
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. The
bi-canonical degree
of
R
is

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R))+\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R)),

where
{\displaystyle D(\omega)}
is the Auslander dual of
\omega
and
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rule{8.53581pt}{0.56905pt}$\;\!$})
is the multiplicity associated with the
\mathfrak{m}
–adic filtration.

Note that the condition that the canonical module
\omega
is an ideal is equivalent to the completion of R being generically Gorenstein. Without the constraint of
\omega
being an ideal, our new definition and results of bi-canonical degree can be applied to more broad classes of rings.
In Proposition
3.2
we discuss conditions under which the canonical module is an ideal, and we see that
the above definition coincides with the classical one in such case. We also have that
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
if and only if
R
is Gorenstein in codimension one (Proposition
3.3
). In Proposition
3.4
we compute the free presentation of the Alexander dual when
R
is the quotient of a regular local ring. This is helpful in computing bi-canonical degrees in explicit examples.
In Proposition
3.8
we extend a result of
[
7
]
relating the bi-canonical degree to the trace of the canonical module. We also propose some open questions, inspired by results of
[
7
]
.

In Section
4

we consider Cohen-Macaulay local rings that do not necessarily have a canonical module.
We look for rings that have ideals with properties similar to those of a canonical ideal. Ideals with similar sets of conditions have appeared in the literature, notably among them closed ideals
[
4
]
and semidualizing (spherical) modules
[
16
]
.
We define a class of ideals in this setting.

Definition 1.2
.

(Definition
4.1
)
Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. An
R
-ideal
\mathcal{P}
is called a precanonical ideal if it is closed (
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P})=R
) and

We prove conditions under which a precanonical ideal is canonical.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorem
4.2
)

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring that has a canonical ideal.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be an
\mathfrak{m}
–primary precanonical ideal of

If

\mathcal{P}/(c)\simeq(R/\mathfrak{m})^{n}

We define the

of

as

\mbox{\rm bideg}_{\mathcal{P}}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{P}^{**}/\mathcal{P})
. It is interesting to ask which properties we can obtain from this metric to understand the structures of the ideal and the ring. We end the section by showing in Proposition

3.
Bi-canonical Degrees of Rings with Canonical Modules

We propose an extension of the bi-canonical degree to a Cohen–Macaulay local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
whose canonical module
\omega
is not necessarily an ideal. Let
k
be the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
. Recall that a finitely generated
R
–module
\omega
is called a canonical module of
R
if

\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(k,\omega)\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if}\;\;i\neq\dim(R),\\
k&\mbox{if}\;\;i=\dim(R).\end{array}\right.

In particular, if
\dim(R)=0
, then the injective hull of
k
is the canonical module of
R

[
3
, Proposition 3.1.14, Lemma 3.2.7]
.

For an
R
–module
E
, we denote the dual of
E
by
{\displaystyle E^{*}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(E,R)}
.
By dualizing the finite free presentation of
\omega
,

F_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{0}\rightarrow\omega\rightarrow 0,

we obtain the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\omega^{*}\rightarrow F_{0}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{1}^{*}\rightarrow D(\omega)\rightarrow 0,

where
{\displaystyle D(\omega)=\mbox{\rm coker}(\varphi^{*})}
is the Auslander dual of
\omega
.
The module
D(\omega)
depends on the chosen presentation but the values of
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{i}(D(\omega),R)}
, for
i\geq 1
, are independent of the presentation. By
[
1
, Proposition 2.6]
, there exists an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R)\longrightarrow\omega\longrightarrow\omega^{**}\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R)\longrightarrow 0.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. The
bi-canonical degree
of
R
is

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(D(\omega),R))+\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{2}(D(\omega),R)),

where
{\displaystyle D(\omega)}
is the Auslander dual of
\omega
and
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mbox{\rule{8.53581pt}{0.56905pt}$\;\!$})
is the multiplicity associated with
\mathfrak{m}
–adic filtration.

An
R
–module
E
is said to be
torsionless
if the natural homomorphism
{\displaystyle\sigma:E\rightarrow E^{**}}
is injective. Recall that a finite
R
–module
E
is torsionless if and only if it is a submodule of a finite free module. The following proposition shows that if
R
possesses a canonical ideal
\mathcal{C}
, then Definition
2.9
and Definition
3.1
coincide.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. Then
\omega
is torsionless if and only if
\omega
is isomorphic to an ideal of
R
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) is clear. (
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
\omega
is torsionless. Then there exists a finite free
R
–module
F
and an exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\omega\rightarrow F}
. Let
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}}
be a minimal prime of
R
. Then
{\displaystyle\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is the canonical module of
{\displaystyle R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
.
Since
{\displaystyle R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is Artinian,
{\displaystyle\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is isomorphic to the injective hull
{\displaystyle E(k({\mathfrak{p}}))}
, where
{\displaystyle k({\mathfrak{p}})=R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}/{\mathfrak{p}}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
. In particular,
\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is injective.
Then the exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\rightarrow F_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
splits. Hence
{\displaystyle\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is a finite free
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
–module. Moreover, as the injective hull
{\displaystyle E(k({\mathfrak{p}}))}
,
{\displaystyle\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is indecomposable. Thus,
\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\simeq R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Hence
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is Gorenstein. By
[
3
, Proposition 3.3.18]
,
\omega
can be identified with an ideal in
R
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

(Cf. Proposition
2.10
)

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. Then
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
if and only if

Proof.

By Definition

,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=0
if and only if
\omega
is reflexive. By

, this is equivalent to

is Gorenstein for all primes
{\mathfrak{p}}
with height

.

\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(G_{g-1},S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tiny\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(\sigma,S)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(G_{g},S)\rightarrow\omega_{R}\rightarrow 0.

By tensoring this with
R=S/I
, we obtain the finite free presentation for
\omega_{R}
as an
R

F_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{0}\rightarrow\omega_{R}\rightarrow 0,

where
{\displaystyle F_{1}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(G_{g-1},S)\otimes_{S}R}
,
{\displaystyle F_{0}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(G_{g},S)\otimes_{S}R}
, and
{\displaystyle\varphi=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(\sigma,S)\otimes R}
. In particular,

\omega_{R}\simeq\mbox{\rm coker}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(\sigma,S)\otimes R)=\mbox{\rm coker}(\sigma^{T}\otimes R).

Note that

\varphi^{*}=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\varphi,R)=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(\sigma,S)\otimes R,R)=\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{S}(\sigma,S),S)\otimes R=(\sigma^{T})^{T}\otimes R=\sigma\otimes R.

Thus, we have

D(\omega_{R})=\mbox{\rm coker}(\varphi^{*})=\mbox{\rm coker}(\sigma\otimes R).

Let
{\displaystyle G_{g}=S^{p}}
. Then
F_{0}=R^{p}

∎

Let

0\longrightarrow S^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{3}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow 0,\;\mbox{where}\;\sigma=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}X^{2}&Y^{2}\\
YZ&ZX\\
XZ&XY\end{array}\right].

Let

R^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}R^{2}\rightarrow\omega_{R}\rightarrow 0,

where
{\displaystyle\varphi=\sigma^{T}\otimes R=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}x^{2}&yz&xz\\

Remark 3.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring with a canonical module
\omega
. If there exists an injective homomorphism
\varphi:R\rightarrow\omega
, then
\omega
is isomorphic to an ideal. In particular, if
R
is almost Gorenstein then the canonical module is isomorphic to an ideal.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\omega\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0}
, where
C=\mbox{\rm coker}(\varphi)
. If
C=0
, then
R\simeq\omega
and we are done. Suppose
C\neq 0
. Let
{\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}}}
be a minimal prime of
R
. Then
{\displaystyle\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\simeq\omega_{R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}\simeq E(k({\mathfrak{p}}))}
, where

{\displaystyle\omega_{R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}}
is the canonical module of
R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
and

is the injective hull of the residue field of

.
Hence by Matlis Duality
[

{\displaystyle{\lambda}(R_{{\mathfrak{p}}})={\lambda}(\omega_{{\mathfrak{p}}})}
. Therefore
C_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=0
. This means

for every minimal prime, that is,

is generically Gorenstein. By
[
3
, Proposition 3.3.18]
,
\omega
can be identified with an ideal.
∎

Question 3.7
.

Let
R
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of dimension
1
with a canonical module
\omega
. When is
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=1
?

Let
{\displaystyle\Gamma:\omega\otimes\omega^{*}\rightarrow R}
be the natural map given by
{\displaystyle\Gamma(x\otimes\alpha)=\alpha(x)}
. The
trace
of
\omega
is the image of
\Gamma
in
R
and is denoted by
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)}
. The trace degree
\mbox{\rm tdeg}(R)
of
R
was introduced in
[
11
]
.
When the canonical module is isomorphic to an ideal, a standard metric is
\mbox{\rm tdeg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega))
.
In dimension
1
,
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)
equals
\mbox{\rm tdeg}(R)

[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
.
A generalization is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
R
be a
1
–dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
.

(1)

If
\omega
is torsionless, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega))}
.

(2)

If
\omega
is not torsionless, then
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}\big(\mbox{\rm Hom}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega),\omega)\big)}
.

Proof.

(1) If
\omega
is torsionless, then
\omega
is isomorphic to an ideal and the assertion was proved in
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
.

(2) Let
{\displaystyle\Gamma:\omega\otimes\omega^{*}\rightarrow R}
be the natural map and let
{\displaystyle K=\mbox{\rm ker}(\Gamma)}
. Consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\omega\otimes\omega^{*}\rightarrow\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\rightarrow 0

Set
{\displaystyle E^{\vee}=\mbox{\rm Hom}(E,\omega)}
. Dualizing the above sequence with respect to
\omega
we obtain

0\rightarrow\big(\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\big)^{\vee}\rightarrow\big(\omega\otimes\omega^{*}\big)^{\vee}\rightarrow K^{\vee}=0.

Therefore,

\big(\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\big)^{\vee}\simeq\mbox{\rm Hom}(\omega^{*}\otimes\omega,\omega)\simeq\mbox{\rm Hom}(\omega^{*},\mbox{\rm Hom}(\omega,\omega))\simeq\mbox{\rm Hom}(\omega^{*},R)=\omega^{**}.

By dualizing the sequence
{\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\rightarrow R\rightarrow R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\rightarrow 0}
with respect to
\omega
, we obtain the following.

0\rightarrow\big(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\big)^{\vee}\rightarrow\omega\rightarrow\big(\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)\big)^{\vee}\simeq\omega^{**}\rightarrow\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega),\omega)\rightarrow 0.

Therefore, we have

\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}\big((R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega))^{\vee}\big)+\mbox{\rm deg}\big(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega),\omega)\big).

Since
\omega
is not isomorphic to an ideal, the height of
\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega)
is
0

[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
. Therefore,
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}(R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega),\omega)=0}
and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}\big((R/\mbox{\rm tr}(\omega))^{\vee}\big)}
.
∎

4.
Precanonical Ideals

In this section we consider a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
R
that does not necessarily have a canonical module. We look for rings that have ideals with properties similar to those of a canonical ideal. We start by recalling some useful definitions.

An ideal
I
is said to be
closed
if
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,I)=R
. An
R
–module
E
is said to be
semidualizing
(or
spherical
) if
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(E,E)=R}
and
{\displaystyle\mbox{\rm Ext}^{i}_{R}(E,E)=0}
for all
i\geq 1
(
[
16
]
,
[
5
]
). We refer to
[
14
]
for a survey of semidualizing modules. In particular, the canonical module is semidualizing.

If
R
is not Gorenstein, principal ideals generated by a non-zero divisor are semidualizing. Examples of rings with non trivial semidualizing ideals are constructed in
[
15
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of dimension
d>0
. An
R
–ideal
\mathcal{P}
is called a
precanonical
ideal if it is closed and
\mbox{\rm Ext}_{R}^{1}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P})=0
.

For instance, canonical ideals and semidualizing ideals are precanonical ideals. The following result shows when a precanonical ideal is canonical.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring that has a canonical ideal.
Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
–primary precanonical ideal of
R
.
If
c\in I
is such that
I/(c)\simeq(R/\mathfrak{m})^{n}
, for some
n
, then
I
is a canonical ideal.

Proof.

By
[
7
, Proposition 3.4]
, it is enough to show that
I
is irreducible. Let
k=R/\mathfrak{m}
.
Consider the exact sequence of natural maps

0\longrightarrow(c)\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow I/(c)\simeq k^{n}\rightarrow 0.

Applying
\mbox{\rm Hom}(\mbox{\rule{8.53581pt}{0.56905pt}$\;\!$},I)
to the exact sequence, we obtain

0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow c^{-1}I\longrightarrow\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{R}(k,I)\right)^{n}\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore we have

\left(\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(k,I/cI)\right)^{n}\simeq\left(\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{R}(k,I)\right)^{n}\simeq c^{-1}I/R\simeq I/(c)\simeq k^{n}.

Thus,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(k,I/cI)=k
.
Since
R/I\simeq(c)/cI
embeds in
I/cI
, the socle of
R/I
is
k
. Hence
I
is irreducible. ∎

Question 4.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring possessing a precanonical ideal
\mathcal{P}
. Define the
bi-canonical degree
of
R

relative to

\mathcal{P}
as

\mbox{\rm bideg}_{\mathcal{P}}(R)=\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{P}^{**}/\mathcal{P}).

Which are the properties we can obtain from this metric to understand the structures of the ideal and the ring?

Recall that if
\mathcal{C}
is a canonical ideal of a
1
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring then
\mbox{\rm bideg}(R)=\lambda(\mathcal{C}^{**}/\mathcal{C})=0
(i.e.,
\mathcal{C}
is reflexive) if and only if
R
is Gorenstein (i.e.,
\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to
R
). We now study reflexivity in more generality.

First, we prove a lemma inspired by
[
11
, Lemma 1.1]
and
[
13
, Lemma 2.9]
that has interest in its own right.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R
be a
1
–dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an ideal that contains a non–zero divisor.
Then

\mbox{\rm tr}(I)^{*}\cong(I\otimes_{R}I^{-1})^{*}\cong(I\otimes_{R}I^{*})^{*}\cong\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I,I^{**}).

Proof.

By
[
11
, Lemma 1.1]
, one has
\mbox{\rm tr}(I)=I\cdot I^{-1}.

Let
K
be the kernel of the evaluation map
I\otimes_{R}I^{*}\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm tr}(I),
and consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow I\otimes_{R}I^{*}\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm tr}(I)\longrightarrow 0.

Let
Q(R)
be the total ring of fractions of
R
, which is a flat
R
-module.
Since
I
contains a non–zero divisor, the map

ev\otimes 1\colon(I\otimes_{R}I^{*})\otimes_{R}Q(R)\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm tr}(I)\otimes_{R}Q(R)

is an isomorphism. Therefore,
K\otimes_{R}Q(R)=0.

As
K
is a torsion
R
–module,
\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(K,R)=0
, so dualizing the above exact sequence yields

0\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(\mbox{\rm tr}(I),R))\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(I\otimes_{R}I^{*},R)\longrightarrow\mbox{\rm Hom}_{R}(K,R)=0

and the lemma follows.
∎

The following is an improvement of
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
R
be a
1
–dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an ideal that contains a non-zero-divisor. If
I
is closed and reflexive, then
I
is a principal ideal.

Proof.

Let
{\displaystyle L=\mbox{\rm tr}(I)=I\cdot I^{*}.}

Then by Lemma
4.4
and the fact that
I
is closed and reflexive we have (realizing
L^{*}
as a fractional ideal in the quotient ring of
R
)

L^{*}=\mbox{\rm Hom}(L,R)=\mbox{\rm Hom}(I\cdot I^{*},R)=\mbox{\rm Hom}(I\otimes I^{*},R)=\mbox{\rm Hom}(I,\mbox{\rm Hom}(I^{*},R))=\mbox{\rm Hom}(I,I)=R.

But since
R
is
1
–dimensional, we have that the grade of
L
is less than or equal to one, so
L
is a free
R
–module.
Hence
I
is an invertible ideal and as
R
is local we have that
I
is principal.
∎

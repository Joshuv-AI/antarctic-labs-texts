Letter-braiding: bridging combinatorial group theory and topology
Nir Gadish

Abstract
. We define invariants of words in arbitrary groups, measuring how letters in a word are interleaving, perfectly detecting the dimension series of a group. These are the letter-braiding invariants. On free groups, braiding invariants coincide with coefficients in the Magnus expansion. In contrast with Magnus’ coefficients, our invariants are defined on all groups and over any PID. They respect products in the group and are a complete invariant of the dimension series, so they are the coefficients of a universal multiplicative finite-type invariant, depending functorially on the group. Letter-braiding invariants arise from the bar construction on a cochain model of a space with a prescribed fundamental group. This approach specializes to simplicial presentations of a group as well as to more geometric contexts, which we illustrate in examples. As an application, we define variants of the Johnson filtration and the Johnson homomorphism on the automorphisms of arbitrary groups, and use them to constrain automorphisms of finite p p -groups.

1.
Introduction

How can one detect whether a particular element in a group belongs to the
k
-th term in the lower central series?
In this work we describe a collection of topologically motivated invariants of words in arbitrary groups, called
letter-braiding invariants
. They are explicitly computable from the sequences of letters defining the word and a presentation of the group. The invariants distinguish between linear combinations of group elements with coefficients over any PID up to powers of the augmentation ideal in the group ring, and thus give obstructions to being a
k
-fold commutator.

Our invariants grew out of work of Monroe–Sinha
[
MS22
]
, who discovered that ideas from classical linking theory generalize to letters in words
w\in F_{n}
– the free group on
n
generators.
For example, in
the commutator
(xyx^{-1}y^{-1})
the
x
’s link with the
y
’s once.
They introduced combinatorial linking of letters, with degree
k
linking invariants defined on the
k
-th term in the lower central series
\gamma_{k}F_{n}
, and pairing perfectly with
\nicefrac{{\gamma_{k}F_{n}}}{{\gamma_{k+1}F_{n}}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. They furthermore show that, when defined, their linking invariants coincide with coefficients in the classical Magnus expansion of
F_{n}

[
Mag35
]
.

Below, we give an “open-string” generalization of these letter-linking invariants, which we therefore call
letter-braiding
1
1

1

Recall that links are closures of braids – the former consisting of closed strings while the latter having open strands. Similarly, Monroe–Sinha’s letter-linking is defined when all ‘strings of letters’ are closed, while we consider strands with ends.
. These vastly extend the scope of Mornoe–Sinha’s letter-linking, as well as Magnus’ expansion. They also give a simplicial analog to Chen’s celebrated iterated integrals
[
Che71
]
– one that is combinatorial and can detect torsion phenomena.

1.1.
Main Results

Let
\Gamma
be a
group and
let
X
be any connected simplicial set with fundamental group
\Gamma
.
In applications, choices of
X
can range
from small combinatorial models
to functorial models and the singular simplicial set of
a topological space. Let
A
be a PID.

The cohomology ring of
X
with coefficients in
A
is computed by the associative algebra of simplicial cochains
C^{*}_{\cup}(X;A)
. The
A
-valued letter-braiding invariants of
\Gamma
are parametrized by the
0
-th (associative) Bar cohomology of
C^{*}_{\cup}(X;A)
. This is denoted by

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A):=\mathrm{H}^{0}\left(Bar(C^{*}_{\cup}(X;A))\right)

and is a subquotient of the tensor (co)algebra
\bigoplus_{k}C^{1}_{\cup}(X;A)^{\otimes k}
, see §
2.1
for definitions.

Every
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A)
defines an
A
-valued invariant
\ell_{T}
of elements
w\in\Gamma
, as follows. Representing
w
by a map
\tilde{w}\colon S^{1}\to X
, it defines a pullback on Bar constructions

\tilde{w}^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(S^{1};A).

We exhibit a canonical invariant
\ell\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(S^{1};A)\to A
, through which one obtains the
letter braiding number
by composition
\ell_{T}(w):=\ell(\tilde{w}^{*}(T))\in A
. A combinatorial formula for
\ell_{T}(w)
on free groups is this: for homomorphisms
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}\colon\Gamma\to A
and generators
s_{1},\ldots s_{n}\in\Gamma
,

T=[H_{1}|\ldots|H_{k}]\text{ and }w=s_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}}s_{2}^{\epsilon_{2}}\ldots s_{n}^{\epsilon_{n}}\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad\ell_{T}(w)=\sum_{i_{1}<_{w}\ldots<_{w}i_{k}}H_{1}(s_{i_{1}}^{\epsilon_{i_{1}}})H_{2}(s_{i_{2}}^{\epsilon_{i_{2}}})\ldots H_{k}(s_{i_{k}}^{\epsilon_{i_{k}}}),

where order relation
i<_{w}j
between natural numbers is the standard
<
relation when
\epsilon_{i}=1
and
\leq
when
\epsilon_{i}=-1
.

This collection of functions
\ell_{T}(\bullet)
for
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A)
turns out to depend only on the group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(X,*)
, as explained in §
4
, and is thus denoted
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
. Extending
A
-linearly,

Definition 1.1
.

The letter-braiding numbers define an
A
-bilinear pairing with the group ring
A[\Gamma]
, called the
letter-braiding pairing
,

(1)

\langle-,\bullet\rangle\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)\otimes A[\Gamma]\to A\quad\text{given on group elements by}\quad\langle T,w\rangle:=\ell_{T}(w).

When
\Gamma
is presented by generators and relations, computing both
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
and its pairing on words is given by explicit, simple algorithms. When
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of some manifold or variety, braiding invariants have compelling geometric interpretations.

The letter-braiding pairing is compatible with the natural structures on
A[\Gamma]
and
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
. Recall that
A[\Gamma]
is a ring under the convolution product, and it is filtered by powers of the augmentation ideal
I=\ker(\epsilon\colon A[\Gamma]\to A)
. This filtered algebra structure is functorial with respect to group homomorphisms.

Our main result, proved in §
5
and §
6
, is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Fundamental theorem of letter-braiding)
.

Let
A
be a principal ideal domain. For any group
\Gamma
, its
Bar cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
is a filtered coalgebra, functorial in
\Gamma
. Under the letter-braiding pairing from (
1
), this structure is dual to the filtered algebra structure on
A[\Gamma]
, in the following sense.

(1)

Functoriality: for any group homomorphism
h\colon\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
,

\langle h^{*}(T),w\rangle=\langle T,h_{*}(w)\rangle.

(2)

Product-coproduct duality
2
2

2

Thinking of letter-braiding numbers as coefficients in a power series expansion, product–coproduct duality is equivalent to the statement that this expansion is
multiplicative
. E.g. the Kontsevich integral on pure braids is a multiplicative expansion, while Mostovoy–Willerton’s
\mathbb{Z}
-valued expansion is not. The
\mathbb{Z}
-valued braid invariant produced by our procedure would be multiplicative, but it would take some work to find the ring over which it is defined – see Example
4.21
for coefficients of degree
\leq 3
.
: setting
\langle T_{1}\otimes T_{2},x\otimes y\rangle:=\langle T_{1},x\rangle\langle T_{2},y\rangle
, the deconcatenation coproduct
\Delta
on
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
is dual to the convolution product on
A[\Gamma]
,

\langle\Delta T,x\otimes y\rangle=\langle T,x\cdot y\rangle.

(3)

Filtrations: let
I^{n}\leq A[\Gamma]
denote the
n
-th power of the augmentation ideal, then

(I^{n})^{\perp}=\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)_{<n},

the sub-coalgebra of weight
<n
tensors.

(4)

Extending Magnus: when
\Gamma
is a free group, the letter-braiding pairing coincides with coefficients in the classical Magnus expansion (see Example
1.4
).

Note that functoriality and the evaluation formula on free groups together determine the letter braiding pairing uniquely. Moreover, letter braiding invariants are maximal given the constraints enforced by the last theorem, as explained next.

Theorem 1.3

(Completeness/Universal finite-type invariant)
.

When
\Gamma
is finitely generated, the letter-braiding pairing defines an isomorphism of filtered coalgebras

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)\xrightarrow{\sim}\varinjlim\text{Hom}_{A}(A[\Gamma]/I^{n},A).

This map is defined and injective even without assuming finite generation (see Remark
6.2
).

For context on Theorem
1.3
, recall that in braid theory, an invariant of the braid group
B_{n}
has
finite type
(also called a
Vassiliev invariant
) whenever it vanishes on some power of
I\leq\mathbb{Z}[B_{n}]
(see
[
MW02
]
for this perspective). Extending this to general groups, Property (
3
) of the pairing says that every
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
defines a finite type invariant
\ell_{T}(\bullet)\colon\Gamma\to A
.
Furthermore, a finite type invariant
\Psi\colon\Gamma\to R
is
universal
if every finite type invariant of
\Gamma
factors uniquely through
\Psi
. Theorem
1.3
says that every
A
-valued finite type invariant is of the form
\ell_{T}(\bullet)
for a unique
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
for
\Gamma
finitely generated, exhibiting universality.

Nearly a century ago, Magnus discovered a universal finite-type invariant for free groups
[
Mag35
]
, valued in a noncommutative power series ring. Later, Fox developed his ‘free differential calculus’ to compute the coefficients of this power series
[
Fox53
]
. These two works lead to an explosion of activity and countless applications spanning topology and group theory.

A generalization of Magnus’ expansion to other groups has until now been elusive, and was only known in a small number of special classes of ‘sufficiently free’ groups: right-angled Artin groups (RAAGs) are partially free and partially commutative, and a Magnus expansion was constructed by Duchamp–Krob
[
DK92
]
; while pure braid groups are semi-direct products of free groups, and Magnus expansions over
\mathbb{Z}
were found by Mostovoy–Willerton
[
MW02
]
and Papadima
[
Pap02
]
, but their expansions are not
multiplicative
in that they do not respect the product structure on braids. The most general construction of invariants for general presented groups might still be that of Chen–Fox–Lyndon
[
CFL58
]
, who give a word-rewriting algorithm that computes the lower central series of groups from a presentation. Their algorithm produces functionals on the lower central series subquotients of a group, but
it is inductive with equations that must be solved at each stage.

The construction in this paper explains why expansions for arbitrary groups have been hard to find, by exhibiting the obstruction: as discussed in §
4.1
, expansion coefficients of degree
k
require knowledge of all Massey products
\mu_{n}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)^{\otimes n}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;A)
of size
n\leq k
. These products are often unknown and hard to compute. For RAAGs and braid groups all higher Massey products vanish as a consequence of formality over
\mathbb{Q}
, so their expansions are easier to determine completely.

We discuss and compare with older work throughout, but let us highlight some advantages of our approach over previous ones.

•

General groups: every group
\Gamma
can be probed by a collection of letter-braiding invariants, which are well-defined even on non-free or infinitely generated groups.

•

Globally defined: all letter-braiding invariants are well-defined on the entire group, not just high-order commutators or elements of dimension
subgroups.

•

Cohomological interpretation: letter-braiding invariants are built from combinations of classes in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X;A)
, for any space with
\pi_{1}(X,*)\cong\Gamma
, and thus carry topological meaning alongside their combinatorial manifestation.

•

Coefficients in PIDs: invariants can be made to take values in any PID, and thus detect torsion elements.

1.2.
First examples, motivation and background

Letter-braiding invariants enjoy an explicit and effective combinatorial formulation, but they come from intersection theory in the geometry of loop spaces. If
M
is a manifold, classes
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(M;\mathbb{Q})
are represented by counting transverse intersections of loops with real hypersurfaces in
M
. Given a sequence of such hypersurfaces, one may track the order in which intersections
occur and count series of intersections in prescribed orders – this is morally what letter-braiding invariants are measuring (compare with Hain’s construction using iterated integrals
[
Hai85
]
). Such ordered intersection counts are generally not homotopy invariant, e.g. when the hypersurfaces intersect, but one can find linear combinations of counts that are invariant through analysis of such intersections, as reflected by cup products and Massey products.

The algebraic framework for this analysis is governed by the classical bar construction, which has been used to model cochains on (a completion of) loop spaces for over sixty years
[
Ada56
,
EM66
,
Dwy75a
]
.
From this perspective, one is studying the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X,x)
through the zeroth cohomology of the loop space
\Omega_{x}X
.

In this introduction, we will recall several classical approaches to studying fundamental groups using the bar construction, but few of those give rise to invariants of words in groups that are explicit enough to, say, be calculated on a computer. Letter-braiding invariants achieve this: they give concrete and fast algorithms for obtaining
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
and its letter-braiding pairing with
\Gamma
, as
implemented in Sage
by Aydin Ozbek, Ben Walter and others
3
3

3

Since the first draft of this paper, Ozbek has tragically passed away. His original code
is still available
.
.

Example 1.4
.

The free group
F_{n}=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\rangle
is the fundamental group of a wedge

W_{n}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}S^{1}

whose
1
-cochains are
C^{1}(W_{n};A)=A^{n}
with trivial cup product. Its letter-braiding invariants are parametrized by the tensor (co)algebra

T(A^{n})=A\langle S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}\rangle

of noncommutative polynomials in the standard basis elements
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}\in A^{n}
.

Fox calculus
[
Fox53
]
supplies operators on the group algebra
A[F_{n}]
called ‘derivatives’ with which every group element is expanded as a Taylor series. Theorem
5.9
shows that for every word
w\in F_{n}
, the letter-braiding number associated with the monomial
(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\ldots S_{i_{k}})
is

\ell_{(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k}})}(w)=\epsilon\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i_{k}}}w\right),

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}
is the
i
-th Fox derivative from
[
Fox53
]
and
\epsilon\colon A[F_{n}]\to A
is the standard augmentation map. Equivalently, this letter-braiding number coincides with the coefficient of
t_{i_{1}}\ldots t_{i_{k}}
in the classical Magnus expansion for
F_{n}
(see
[
Mag35
]
), which is the same as Fox’s Taylor coefficient.
In this way, letter-braiding invariants are a posteriori seen to be functorial extensions of the classical Magnus expansion to arbitrary groups and coefficients in any PID. At the same time, they are substantially easier to calculate than Fox’ or Magnus’ invariants.

In §
3.2
we give a computational approache to letter-braiding on free groups, inspired by Monroe–Sinha’s weight reduction procedure
[
MS22
]
. This leads to fast and flexible calculations, with one implementation resulting in the following closed form expression.

Proposition 1.5
.

On the free group
F_{n}=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\rangle
, the letter braiding numbers
\ell_{(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k}})}(\bullet)
count the number of

-increasing subsequences of letters

where
S_{i}(s_{j})=\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta, and
r<_{w}r^{\prime}
is the order relation
r<r^{\prime}
when
\epsilon_{i}=1
and it is

\epsilon_{i}=-1

Example 1.6

(Torsion commutators)
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathscr{H}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
be the Heisenberg group of matrices

\begin{pmatrix}1&X&Z\\
0&1&Y\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{Gl}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2}).

The functions
X,Y,Z\colon\mathscr{H}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
determine cochains in
C^{1}(\mathscr{H}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2});\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and Example
5.11
shows
XY+Z\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\mathscr{H}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2});\mathbb{F}_{2})

\mathscr{H}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\

.

, but can be defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and detect torsion elements.

1.3.
Hopf invariants

In a direct predecessor of this work, Sinha–Walter
[
SW13
]
use the Bar construction, and a variant for commutative algebras called the
Harrison complex
, to define invariants of maps
\alpha\colon S^{n}\to X
with
n\geq 2
using higher linking numbers in spheres
S^{n}
. They call these
Hopf invariants
and show that they distinguish rational homotopy classes of maps into simply connected spaces. The present paper grew out of a joint effort to define Hopf invariants of maps
S^{1}\to X
using ‘linking’ of
0
-manifolds in
S^{1}
, together with with A. Ozbek, D. Sinha, and B. Walter
[
GOSW24
]
. The theory we develop there is complementary to letter-braiding, starting with a commutative model for cochains in characteristic zero, and some of its benefits are the following.

•

Hopf invariants of maps
S^{1}\to X
detect and obstruct the Malcev Lie algebra of the group
\pi_{1}(X,*)
. The Harrison complex is much smaller than the Bar complex, and it is naturally a Lie coalgebra. As such, it removes redundancy from functionals on the group ring, and computes a maximal Lie coalgebraic dual of the Malcev Lie algebra of any
\Gamma
.

•

The theory is naturally geometric: de Rham forms supported near submanifolds (a.k.a. Thom forms) realize integration and wedge products as intersection of submanifolds. As such, letter-braiding theory is restated in terms of the meeting times of a smooth loop with with various hypersurfaces, as described above.

•

Hopf invariants specialize to Chen’s
[
Che71
]
theory of iterated integrals, on the one hand, while on the other hand having a combinatorial formulation relative to group presentations.

Hopf invariants differ subtly from letter-braiding invariants studied here, which are based on simplicial cochains with their noncommutative cup product. In
[
GOSW24
]
we prove that the former are obtained from the latter as the invariants of
\log(w)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}(w-1)^{n}
, as expected from functionals on the Malcev Lie algebra.

Topological applications of this theory include the following (see
[
GOSW24
]
for terminology).

Proposition 1.7
.

Milnor invariants of links are computed from the intersections of a link component with any collection of Seifert surfaces for the link, along with further secondary surfaces: they are the letter-braiding numbers of the intersection pattern.

Further invariants are well-defined beyond the weights which Milnor initially considered, and for any element in the link group.

Here, Seifert surfaces are allowed to intersect, at the cost of adding secondary surfaces. In ongoing work in progress, we use this additional freedom to give algorithms for calculating Milnor invariants from a planar projection of the link.

1.4.
Applications

The invariants
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
are determined by any space or simplicial set
X
with
\pi_{1}(X,*)\cong\Gamma
, thus different choices of model give invariants of distinct flavors, blending Algebra, Combinatorics, Geometry and Topology.

1.4.1.
Combinatorial group theory

A presentation
\Gamma=\langle S|R\rangle
determines a
2
-complex
X_{S|R}
whose
1
-cells are labeled by
S
and
2
-cells come from relations
R
. Its letter-braiding invariants are therefore presented in terms of the generators and relation,
as follows.

For any set
S
, let
F_{S}
denote the free group generated by
S
. Its letter-braiding invariants are computed from the wedge
X_{S}=\vee_{s\in S}S^{1}
and are simply the tensor (co)algebra

T(A^{S})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}(A^{S})^{\otimes k}.

As mentioned, letter-braiding numbers of words
w\in F_{S}
coincide with the output of Fox’ classical derivatives on free groups (see Theorem
5.9
). We thus think of them as differential operators:
f\in A^{S}\longleftrightarrow\sum_{s\in S}f(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
.

Now, the inclusion
X_{S}\hookrightarrow X_{S|R}
identifies
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
as a sub-coalgebra of
T(A^{S})
, consisting of possibly nonhomogeneous differential operators, so that the letter-braiding numbers of
w\in\langle S|R\rangle
are given by the Magnus expansion coefficients of any lift
\tilde{w}\in F_{S}
.

But to get well-defined invariants of words in
\langle S|R\rangle
, we may only consider differential operators that happen to kill all relations
\langle R\rangle\leq F_{S}
. Examples
4.5
,
4.6
discuss basic cases of relations constraining the collection of invariants. In fact, we show that vanishing on relations, suitably understood,
is the only constraint.

Theorem 1.8
.

For
\Gamma=\langle S|R\rangle
, the quotient map
p\colon F_{S}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma
induces an inclusion

p^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)\hookrightarrow T(A^{S})

whose image is the maximal sub-coalgebra of differential operators that vanish on all
r\in R
.

In other words, a differential operator on
\Gamma
must annihilate
all relations
r\in R
, but this by itself is insufficient – all of its iterated coproducts must annihilate the relations as well. Theorem
6.3
gives a precise and useful generalization of this theorem. With it, one can compute the letter-braiding invariants of
\Gamma
as follows: start with the linear differential operators that vanish on
r\in R
; then find among their degree
2
compositions those linear combinations that continue to vanish on
r\in R
; and so on.

1.4.2.
Geometric topology

Suppose
\Gamma
arises as the fundamental group of a manifold
M
rather than from a presentation. Then its letter-braiding invariants are expressible in geometric terms. Examples include surface groups, Artin groups, knot and link groups and more general 3-manifold groups, and (finite-index subgroups of) mapping-class groups.

Lemma
4.11
recounts that
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(M;A)
is determined by sums of tensors of cohomology classes
\mathrm{H}^{1}(M;A)^{\otimes k}
, all of whose cup and higher Massey products vanish – see Examples
4.9
,
4.10
. By Poincaré duality, classes
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(M;A)
are represented by intersection with a dual hypersurface
H_{\alpha}\subset M
. A word
w\in\Gamma
is represented by some loop in
M
, which intersects those hypersurfaces at various times. The letter-braiding numbers of
w
are then determined by the interleaving of these intersections – see §
3.2
. With
A=\mathbb{R}
coefficients, this description was given in
[
Hai85
]
.

However, different choices of representative loops for
w
might meet the hypersurfaces
H_{\alpha}
in a different order. The condition of vanishing cup and Massey products turns out to be equivalent to the resulting letter-braiding number being a homotopy invariant. Since cup products are dual to intersection, relations in
\pi_{1}(M,*)
constrain the collections of hypersurfaces that may define invariants, and therefore force nontrivial intersection patterns of hypersurfaces.

For example, the fundamental group of the Borromean ring complement is

\Gamma_{3}=\langle x,y,z\mid[x,[y^{-1},z]],[y,[z^{-1},x]],[z,[x^{-1},y]]\rangle.

whose double-commutator relations are detected by the invariant
XYZ
of
F_{3}=\langle x,y,z\rangle
, reflecting a triple Massey product
\langle X,Y,Z\rangle\neq 0
. Geometrically,
X,Y
and
Z
are dual to Seifert surfaces of the three link components. Thus, the Massey product implies that no choice of Seifert surfaces can be made disjoint.

1.4.3.
Positive characteristic

Taking coefficients rings
A
of positive charactertistic, letter-braiding can detect torsion elements and commutators in finite groups. Recall that the lower exponent
p
-central series of a group
\Gamma
is the filtration induced by
w\in F_{k}\Gamma\iff(w-1)\in I^{k}\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\Gamma]
. It follows that
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-valued letter-braiding invariants obstruct this
p
-central series. They are particularly informative when
\Gamma
is a finite
p
-group, since in this case the lower
p
-central series terminates and, therefore, any two elements
w\neq w^{\prime}\in\Gamma
can be distinguished by letter-braiding invariants.

Conversely, the structure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\Gamma]
provides information about Massey products in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology.

Example 1.9
.

For
\Gamma=C_{p}
, the cyclic group of order
p
, the group ring is
p
-dimensional, while
\mathrm{H}^{1}(C_{p};\mathbb{F}_{p})=\mathbb{F}_{p}\cdot T
generated by the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group homomorphism
T(i)=i
. The braiding invariants for
\Gamma
are thus spanned by
T^{k}+(\text{lower weight correction terms})
for
0\leq k\leq p-1
, see Example
7.10
for explicit cochain representatives for which

\langle T^{k}+\text{ (lower weights) },\,i\rangle=\frac{i^{k}}{k!}\mod{p}.

That these already span the dual of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[C_{p}]
means that
T^{p}
does not extend to an invariant.

Corollary 1.10
.

For any space
X
with
\pi_{1}(X,*)\cong C_{p}
, the generator
T\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(X;\mathbb{F}_{p})
has vanishing cup and Massey powers up to order
p-1
, but its
p
-th Massey power

\langle\underbrace{T,\ldots,T}_{p\text{ times}}\rangle\neq 0.

For
C_{p^{2}}
the same
p
-fold Massey power is
0
, and
T^{p}
detects the nontrivial element
p\in C_{p^{2}}
.

See §
7.3
for a generalization to all finite groups.

1.4.4.
Relative Massey products vs. relations

The previous two subsections exhibit a dictionary between Magnus expansions of relations in
\Gamma
and Massey products on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)
. That such a connection exists has been well-known, going back to Dwyer
[
Dwy75b
]
and Fenn–Sjerve
[
FS87
]
.

We complete this dictionary in §
6.4
by defining
relative Massey products
. Given a pair of spaces
(Y,X)
there are higher product operations, that in suggestive simple cases become

\mu_{n}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(X;A)^{\otimes n}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,X;A).

When
Y
is obtained from
X
by attaching ‘relation’
2
-discs along a set
R\subseteq\pi_{1}(X,*)
, the relative cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,X;A)\cong A^{R}
, recording functions on
R
, and Proposition
6.22
shows that the relative Massey product
\mu_{n}
computes the letter-braiding numbers of all
r\in R
. Our setup thus vastly extends the picture painted by Fenn-Sjerve
[
FS87
]
in a number of ways, listed at the end of §
6.4
.

1.4.5.
Algebraic models for spaces

Mandell
[
Man06
]
showed that when
X
is a nilpotent space of finite type, its cochains
C^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
with their
E_{\infty}
-algebra structure determine the homotopy type of
X
, and in particular
\pi_{1}(X,*)
.
More constructively, building on Adams’ classical theorem
[
Ada56
]
, Rivera–Zeinalian
[
RZ18
, Corollary 9.2]
explains that the coBar construction on the singular chains
C_{*}^{Sing}(X)
models the chains of the based loop space
\Omega_{*}X
and thus its
\mathrm{H}_{0}
is computes the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X,*)]
. However, their proofs use rather abstract homotopy theory, and do not provide any means for measuring words
w\in\pi_{1}(X,*)
.

In contrast, our proofs are essentially elementary, with algebraic rather than homotopy theoretic flavor. Consequently, we get a simple constructive proof of the following.

Corollary 1.11

(Compare Mandell
[
Man06
]
, and Dwyer
[
Dwy75a
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
(X,*)
be a pointed connected simplicial set (or topological space) with
\pi_{1}(X,*)
finitely generated but perhaps infinitely related, and let
A
be any PID. Then the filtered coalgebra of
A
-valued functionals of finite type
\varinjlim\text{Hom}_{A}(A[\pi_{1}(X,*)]/I^{n},A)
is determined by the
2
-truncation of the augmented simplicial (or singular) cochain algebra
C^{*\leq 2}_{\Delta}(X;A)
, via letter-braiding.

Compare this further to Rivera–Zeinalian
[
RZ21
]
, who prove that the singular chains of a connected space
X
determine the entire fundamental group – no nilpotency hypotheses necessary. However, one major obstacle in applying their theory here is that one cannot simply pass to a small chain model for calculations, as their construction is not quasi-isomorphism invariant (instead, they use a finer notion of
\Omega
-equivalence).

1.4.6.
Johnson homomorphisms

Work of Kawazumi
[
Kaw05
]
on Johnson filtrations indicates that Magnus expansions define generalizations of the classical Johnson homomorphism of the Torelli group. Since our setup is dual to Magnus expansions, and is defined for general groups, it lets us define dual Johnson homomorphisms in general.

For any group
\Gamma
, the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is filtered by subgroups
J^{A}(k)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
respectively fixing
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
pointwise – a generalized Johnson filtration. Via the letter-braiding pairing, one obtains a Johnson-like homomorphism on these groups.

Theorem 1.12
.

Denote
H=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)
, and let
K_{n}\leq\mathrm{H}^{\otimes n}
be the tensors on which the
n
-th Massey product is defined and vanishes. Then for
\varphi\in J^{A}(k)
, the endomorphism
(T-\varphi^{*}(T))
of
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)_{\leq k+1}

determines an
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
-equivariant ‘dual Johnson’ homomorphism

J^{A}(k)\to\text{Hom}_{A}(K_{k+1},H),

Furthermore, its kernel always contains
J^{A}(k+1)
, and is exactly this subgroup whenever
\Gamma
is finitely generated and
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
is
A
-torsion free, e.g. when
A
is a field.

With
A=\mathbb{Z}
and
\Gamma=F_{n}
a free group,
K_{k}=H^{\otimes k}
and the integral Johnson filtration
J^{\mathbb{Z}}(k)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
is the classical Andreadakis filtration. In this case our dual Johnson homomorphism maps
J(k)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(F_{n})
to
(H^{*})^{\otimes k+1}\otimes H
(compare this with the Johnson homomorphism in
[
Kaw05
, Theorem 6.1]
). And when
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g},*)
, the mapping class group
\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma_{g,1})
acts on
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g},*)
, sending the classical Johnson filtration into the our mod
A
Johnson filtration. We have not found tools for calculating the dual Johnson homomorphism, and leave it as an open problem to relate it with the classical ones.

Theorem
1.12
is proved in §
7.2
. With it, we get a new proof of the following classical theorem of Burnside.

Theorem 1.13
.

If
\Gamma
is a finite
p
-group, then
J^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(1)\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
is a
p
-group. In particular, if
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
does not have
p
-power order, then
\varphi
acts nontrivially on the maximal elementary abelian quotient
\Gamma^{ab}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

1.5.
Related work

A number of constructions of a similar flavor to ours appeared in the near and far past. Most close in spirit are the Chen integrals
[
Che71
]
, which attach
\mathbb{R}
- or
\mathbb{C}
-valued invariants to words
w\in\Gamma
via integration: if
M
is a smooth manifold with
\pi_{1}(M,*)\cong\Gamma
and
\tilde{w}\colon[0,1]\to M
is a loop representing the homotopy class of
w
, Chen took

\langle(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})\,,\,w\rangle=\int_{0\leq t_{1}\leq\ldots\leq t_{n}\leq 1}\tilde{w}^{*}\alpha_{1}(t_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge\tilde{w}^{*}\alpha_{n}(t_{n})

for any tensor of de-Rham
1
-forms in the Bar construction
\mathrm{H}^{0}(Bar(\Omega^{*}_{dR}(M))
. He further proved that this defines an isomorphism of Hopf algebras with
\varinjlim\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]/I^{n},\mathbb{R})
. This construction informed later integral invariants, such as the Kontsevich integral, but it has not been made so explicit as to produce combinatorial invariants of words in groups.

Our weight reduction algorithm (Proposition
3.19
) gives a discrete analog of Chen’s iterated integral, see Corollary
3.25
. With it, the resulting invariants are made combinatorial, while also being defined over general PIDs. In addition, weight reduction manifests substantial freedom to choose the order in which
1
-forms integrated, of which Chen’s ‘time-ordered’ formalism is but one choice: for most words, other orderings yield more efficient calculations.

Concurrently with Chen, Stallings
[
Sta75
]
studied quotients
I^{n}/I^{n+1}
of the augmentation ideal in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]
. He introduced a spectral sequence converging to
I^{\bullet}/I^{\bullet+1}
, which is conceptually dual to the spectral sequence we use in the proof of this paper’s main technical theorem, Theorem
6.1
.

On the interaction between Massey products on
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;R)
and relations in
\Gamma
, work of Dwyer
[
Dwy75b
]
interprets ‘Massey systems’ as homomorphisms from a group of upper triangular matrices
\Gamma\to U_{n}(R)
, and Fenn–Sjerve
[
FS87
]
relate the evaluation of Massey products on
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
with Magnus coefficients of relations.
Dually, Stavros–Levine
[
GL05
]
relate Massey products with lower central series quotients of a group. They show that a homomorphism
F_{n}\to\Gamma
that induces an isomorphism on
H_{1}
, further induces an isomorphism modulo the
k
-th step of the lower central series and a surjection on the
k+1
-st quotient iff all Massey products of order
\leq k
vanish. At the
k+1
-st step, the difference between the two groups is measured by a homological dual Massey product. Our setting pushes this interaction further, by considering braiding invariants past the degree at which the first Massey products appear.

Recently, Porter–Suciu
[
PS22
,
PS23
]
introduced an integral version of rational homotopy theory for studying fundamental groups. Their construction makes explicit use the Steenrod’s cup-1 operation, and requires a binomial structure on the coefficient ring. With this, they develop a theory of
1
-minimal models that give explicit access to fundamental groups, including their torsion, starting from a model for singular cochains over a binomial ring. Around the same time, Horel
[
Hor22
]
found that binomial rings are a good setup for integral homotopy theory, and exhibited a fully faithful functor between nilpotent spaces and cosimplicial binomial rings.
Our theory is not trying to pick out
\pi_{1}
from the group ring
A[\pi_{1}]
, since braiding invariants are already defined at the group ring level, and therefore we can avoid the cup-1 operations and binomial structures.

2.
(Co)algebraic preliminaries

Let
A
be a PID and recall the category of graded
A
-modules with objects
B=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{n}
where every
B_{n}
is an
A
-submodule. The
shift
of
B
by
k
is the graded modules
B[k]
given by
(B[k])_{n}:=B_{n+k}.

Morphisms
f\colon B\to B^{\prime}
are
A
-linear maps that preserve grading. A homomorphism
f\colon B\to B^{\prime}[k]
is said to be a morphism of degree
k

Also recall that the tensor product of two graded modules has graded components

(B\otimes B^{\prime})_{n}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}\otimes_{A}B^{\prime}_{n-i}.

Throughout this paper we consider conilpotent associative coalgebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A
differential graded coalgebra
is a graded module
B
equipped with a coassociative comultiplication

\Delta\colon B\to B\otimes B,\text{ a counit }\eta\colon B\to A

and a coderivation
d\colon B\to B
of degree
+1
obeying the Koszul sign convention (see
[
LV12
, §1.5.3]
). That is,

(\Delta\otimes\Id)\circ\Delta=(\Id\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta,\quad(\Id\otimes\eta)\circ\Delta=(\eta\otimes\Id)\circ\Delta=\Id\quad\text{ and }

\quad\Delta\circ d=(d\otimes\Id+(-1)^{n}\Id\otimes d)\circ\Delta

when evaluated on
B_{n}
.

A homomorphism of dg coalgebras is a morphism
h\colon B\to B^{\prime}
such that

(h\otimes h)\circ\Delta_{B}=\Delta_{B^{\prime}}\circ h,\quad\eta_{B^{\prime}}\circ h=\eta_{B},\quad\text{and}\quad h\circ d_{B}=d_{B^{\prime}}\circ h.

Denote iterated coproducts by
\Delta^{0}=\Id
and

\Delta^{n+1}=(\Delta\otimes\Id^{\otimes n})\circ\Delta^{n}.

The ring
A
itself has a tautological dg coalgebra structure, with
\Delta\colon A\to A\otimes_{A}A
the canonical isomorphism,
\eta\colon A\to A
the identity and
d=0
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
augmentation
of a dg coalgebra
B
is a coalgebra homomorphism
\epsilon\colon A\to B
. Denote
1\in B
for the element
\epsilon(1)
. This splits
B
as
\overline{B}\oplus A\cdot 1
, and we call
\overline{B}=\ker(\eta)
the
reduced
coalgebra of
B
. The
reduced comultiplication

\overline{\Delta}\colon\overline{B}\to\overline{B}
is

\overline{\Delta}(b)=\Delta(b)-1\otimes b-b\otimes 1.

Define

\overline{\Delta}^{n+1}=(\overline{\Delta}\otimes\Id^{\otimes n})\circ\overline{\Delta}^{n}.

We say that
B
is
conilpotent
if for every
b\in B
we have
\overline{\Delta}^{N}(b)=0
for some
N\geq 0
.

Filtrations of coalgebras will play a significant role in this work. These are most naturally understood through quotients rather than subspaces.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose
(B,\Delta,\eta,d)
is a differential coalgebra. A
filtration
on
B
is a sequence of subcomplexes

0=F_{-1}B\leq F_{0}B\leq F_{1}B\leq\ldots\leq F_{p}B\leq\ldots\leq B

such that every quotient
B/F_{p}B
is
A
-torsion free, and for all
i\leq p
the comultiplication factors as indicated by

\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Delta}
\scriptstyle{q_{p}}
\textstyle{B\otimes B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{i}\otimes q_{p-i}}
\textstyle{\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists\Delta^{p}_{i}}
\textstyle{\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i}B}}\otimes\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-i}B}}.}

Equivalently,
(q_{i}\otimes q_{p-i})\circ\Delta
vanishes on
F_{p}B
.

The
associated graded
coalgebra is

\mathrm{gr}~{B}=\bigoplus\mathrm{gr}_{p}~{B}\text{ where }\mathrm{gr}_{p}~{B}:=\ker\left(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-1}}}\twoheadrightarrow\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}}\right)\cong\nicefrac{{F_{p}B}}{{F_{p-1}B}}

equipped with the well-defined coproduct
\Delta\colon\bigoplus_{p}\mathrm{gr}_{p}~B\to\bigoplus_{i\leq p}\mathrm{gr}_{i}~B\otimes\mathrm{gr}_{p-i}~B
consisting of

\mathrm{gr}_{p}~B\subseteq\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-1}B}}\xrightarrow{\oplus\Delta^{p-1}_{i}}\bigoplus_{i\leq p-1}\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i}B}}\otimes\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-1-i}B}},

whose image lands in
\bigoplus_{i\leq p}\mathrm{gr}_{i}~B\otimes\mathrm{gr}_{p-i}~B
.

Remark 2.4
.

Since all
A
-modules in question are torsion free, the sequence

0\to\mathrm{gr}_{i}~B\otimes\mathrm{gr}_{p-i}~B\to\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i-1}B}}\otimes\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-i-1}B}}\to\left(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i}B}}\otimes\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-i-1}B}}\right)\oplus\left(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i-1}B}}\otimes\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-i}B}}\right)

is left-exact. Thus, a quick diagram chase shows that
\Delta^{p-1}_{i}
restricts to
\mathrm{gr}_{p}~B\to\mathrm{gr}_{i}~B\otimes\mathrm{gr}_{p-i}~B
, giving a coassociative coproduct on
\mathrm{gr}~{B}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
B=(B,\Delta,\eta,d)
is a dg-coalgebra. Let
\mathrm{H}^{*}(B)
denote its cohomology. Suppose that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
is torsion-free, then
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
has a natural coalgebra structure

\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{H}^{0}(\Delta)}\mathrm{H}^{0}(B\otimes B)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)

where the second arrow is inverse to the Künneth isomorphism.

Moreover, given a filtration
F_{\bullet}\leq B
such that all
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}})
are torsion-free, the cohomology coalgebra
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
is naturally filtered by

F_{p}\mathrm{H}^{0}(B):=\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}})\right).

Proof.

The Künneth formula shows that whenever
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
is torsion-free, the natural homomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(B\otimes B)
is invertible. Its inverse gives a coproduct structure on
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
. Coassociativity follows immediately from the naturality of the Künneth map.

Now, we claim that
F_{\bullet}\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
is a filtration of coalgebras. Indeed, the quotients
\nicefrac{{\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)}}{{F_{p}\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)}}
are torsion-free as submodules of
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}})
, and using the Künneth formula again,

\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(B\otimes B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(\Delta_{i}^{p})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i}B}}\otimes\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-i}B}})}
\textstyle{\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{i}B}})\otimes\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p-i}B}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}

so after inverting the Künneth isomorphisms we indeed find that the comultiplication factors through
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}})
, as required.
∎

The filtered coalgebra structure on
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)
is obviously functorial.

Definition 2.6
.

Any augmented coalgebra
B
with no
A
-torsion is equipped with the
weight filtration
as follows. Let
F_{p}B=\ker\left(\overline{\Delta}^{p}\colon B\to B^{\otimes p+1}\right)
where
\overline{\Delta}
extends to
B
via the projection
B\to\overline{B}
given by
b\mapsto b-\eta(b)\cdot 1
. Coassociativity of
\overline{\Delta}
implies that the comultiplication factors through
\nicefrac{{B}}{{F_{p}B}}
, as required. Clearly, any homomorphism of coalgebras preserves this filtration.

2.1.
Bar construction

Coalgebras play a central role in our theory through the following construction.

Definition 2.7

(Bar construction)
.

Let
(C^{*},d,\mu)
be an augmented associative dg algebra, and for
c\in C^{i}
denote
|c|=i
. The augmentation ideal of
C
is denoted by
\overline{C}
. The
Bar construction
of
C
,
denoted
Bar(C)
, is the totalization of the 2nd quadrant bicomplex

Bar^{-p,q}(C)=(\overline{C}^{\otimes p})^{q},

where the vertical
q
-grading is the internal cohomological grading of the tensor product complex
\overline{C}^{\otimes p}
. Tensors
\overline{C}^{\otimes p}
are said to have
weight

p
.

Remark 2.8
.

An equivalent way to construct the totalization
Bar(C)
is as the tensor (co)algebra

T(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\overline{C})=\bigoplus_{p=0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\overline{C})^{\otimes p},

where
\mathbf{s}^{-1}\overline{C}:=\overline{C}[1]
is the
desuspension
of
\overline{C}
, i.e. it is the shifted complex
\overline{C}^{i+1}
. Indeed, totalization
(\operatorname{tot}E)^{*}=\bigoplus_{p=0}^{\infty}E^{-p,*+p}
effectively identifies horizontal shifts with shifts in cohomological degree, equivalent to a desuspension operation.

The differential on the Bar construction is the sum of two differentials. The ‘vertical’ differential
d_{1}
of bidegree
(0,1)
is the extension of
d
to a graded coderivation,

d_{1}(c_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes c_{p})=-\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{(i-1)+|c_{1}|+\ldots+|c_{i-1}|}c_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes dc_{i}\otimes\ldots\otimes c_{p}.

The ‘horizontal’ differential
d_{2}
of degree
(1,0)
is defined by multiplying consecutive elements,

d_{2}(c_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes c_{p})=-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{i+|c_{1}|+\ldots+|c_{i}|}c_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu(c_{i},c_{i+1})\otimes\ldots\otimes c_{p}.

Both differentials satisfy the Koszul sign convention, and they anti-commute. The (total)
Bar differential
is
d_{Bar}=d_{1}+d_{2}
. Let
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(C)
denote its cohomology, which we call the
Bar cohomology of
C
.

Definition 2.9

(Coalgebra structure)
.

The Bar construction is naturally a conilpotent dg coalgebra, with respect to the deconcatenation coproduct

(2)

\Delta(c_{1}|\ldots|c_{p})=\sum_{i=0}^{p}(c_{1}|\ldots|c_{i})\otimes(c_{i+1}|\ldots|c_{p}).

where we replaced the tensor symbols by bars. The augmentation is given by the
p=0
summand
(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\overline{C})^{\otimes 0}=A
concentrated in cohomological degree
0
, so that

\overline{Bar}(C)=\bigoplus_{p\geq 1}(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\overline{C})^{\otimes p}

with reduced coproduct omitting the
i=0
and
p
summands from (
2
). Consequently, the weight of a tensor
x
agrees with the order of vanishing of
\overline{\Delta}^{n}(x)
.

Remark 2.10
.

When
C
is a commutative algebra, there is a further product structure on
Bar(C)
, given by the shuffle product, making
Bar(C)
into a Hopf algebra. This structure is significant in the context of rational homotopy theory, giving rise for example to Malcev Lie algebras of fundamental groups.

Bar cohomology is a quasi-isomorphism invariant of a dg algebra
C
, in the following sense.

Lemma 2.11

(
[
LV12
, Proposition 2.2.3]
)
.

If
C\to C^{\prime}
is a quasi-isomorphism of augmented dg algebras, and both underlying
A
-modules are torsion-free, then the induced map

Bar(C)\to Bar(C^{\prime})

is a quasi-isomorphism.

Proof.

The argument of
[
LV12
, Proposition 2.2.3]
applies verbatim, with the observation that torsion-free
A
-modules are flat. Recounting the argument, one filters the bicomplexes
Bar(C)
and
Bar(C^{\prime})
by weight. Their associated graded consist of tensor power complexes

(C^{\otimes p},d_{1})\to((C^{\prime})^{\otimes p},d_{1}).

Since tensor products of complexes of flat modules are quasi-ismorphism invariant, the map induces an isomorphism of the
E^{1}
-pages of the associated spectral sequence. It therefore induces an isomorphism on Bar cohomology.
∎

Definition 2.12
.

An augmented dg algebra
C^{*}
is said to be
connected
if it is quasi-isomorphic to an augmented algebra
\tilde{C}
with
\tilde{C}^{0}=A
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
C^{*}
be a dg algebra whose underlying
A
-module is torsion-free. Let
Bar_{\leq p}(C)
denote the sub-coalgebra of tensors of weight
\leq p
. Then the quotients

Bar_{>p}(C):=Bar(C)/Bar_{\leq p}(C)

are torsion-free so
Bar_{\leq p}(C)
defines a natural filtration on
Bar(C)
.

If
C
is connected, then
\mathrm{H}^{0}(Bar_{>p}(C))
is torsion-free for all
p\geq 0
, so that these induce a natural filtration on
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(C)
.

Proof.

The fact that
Bar_{>p}(C)
is torsion-free is clear, and it gives a filtration of
Bar(C)
as a special case of the filtration of a conilpotent coalgebra by weight.

To see that in the connected case
\mathrm{H}^{0}(Bar_{>p}(C))
is torsion-free, assume without loss of generality that
C^{0}=A
. Then the augmentation ideal
\overline{C}
is concentrated in positive degrees, and consequently
Bar^{0}(C)
is the lowest degree in which
Bar(C)
is nontrivial. It follows that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(Bar_{>p}(C))
is a sub-coalgebra of
Bar_{>p}(C)
, characterized by the vanishing of
d_{Bar}
, thus torsion-free.
∎

We summarize the main results of this section as follows.

Corollary 2.14
.

The assignment
C^{*}\mapsto\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(C)
is a functor which sends connected dg algebras, whose underlying
A
-module is torsion-free, to filtered coassociative coalgebras. Quasi-isomorphisms of algebras are sent to isomorphism of coalgebras.

2.2.
Group rings

Let
\Gamma
be a group. We recall basic definitions.

Definition 2.15
.

The
group ring

A[\Gamma]
is the module of
A
-linear combinations of elements
w\in\Gamma
. It forms an augmented (Hopf) algebra, with product

m\colon A[\Gamma]\otimes A[\Gamma]\to A[\Gamma]

extending
m(w_{1}\otimes w_{2})=w_{1}w_{2}
to an
A
-linear map, with coproduct
\Delta(w)=w\otimes w
, and with antipode
s(w)=w^{-1}
for every
w\in\Gamma
.

The augmentation
\epsilon\colon A[\Gamma]\to A
sends every
w\in\Gamma
to
1
, so that

\epsilon(\sum\lambda_{i}\cdot w_{i})=\sum\lambda_{i}.

Let
I\lhd A[\Gamma]
denote the augmentation ideal, which is the kernel of
\epsilon
, generated by
(w-1)
for
w\in\Gamma
. Then
A[\Gamma]
is filtered by powers
\ldots\leq I^{2}\leq I\leq A[\Gamma]
.

The association
\Gamma\mapsto A[\Gamma]
is a functor, sending groups to augmented (Hopf) algebras.

Recall the dimension series of a group.

Definition 2.16

(
[
Los60
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group and
A
any ring. The
A
-
dimension series
of
\Gamma
modulo
A
is the descending central series of subgroups

\ldots\leq\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;A)\leq\ldots\leq\gamma_{2}(\Gamma;A)\leq\gamma_{1}(\Gamma;A)=\Gamma

defined by

w\in\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;A)\iff(w-1)\in I^{k}\subseteq A[\Gamma].

Recall that the
lower central series
of
\Gamma
, defined recursively as
\gamma_{1}\Gamma=\Gamma
and
\gamma_{k+1}\Gamma=[\gamma_{k}\Gamma,\Gamma]
, is the minimal central series. It follows that
\gamma_{k}\Gamma\subseteq\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;A)
for every ring
A
.

Example 2.17
.

For
A=\mathbb{Z}
, Passi
[
Pas68
]
described classes of groups for which
\gamma_{k}\Gamma=\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
. However, starting with
k=4
there exist groups for which
\gamma_{k}\Gamma\subsetneq\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;A)
, as found by Rips
[
Rip72
]
.
For
A=\mathbb{Q}
, the dimension series is constructed recursively by the rule

\gamma_{k+1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Q})=\{w\in\Gamma\mid\exists n\geq 1\text{ s.t. }w^{n}\in[\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Q}),\Gamma]\},

and for
A=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the dimension series is the
lower exponent
p
central series
constructed recursively by

\gamma_{k+1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})=[\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p}),\Gamma]\cdot\gamma_{k}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{p}.

Definition 2.18
.

Given an
A
-module
M
, let
M^{*}
denote
\text{Hom}_{A}(M,A)
, the module of
A
-linear functionals on
M
.
For every
p\geq 1
define an evaluation pairing

(M^{*})^{\otimes p}\otimes M^{\otimes p}\to A\quad\text{via}\quad\langle\varphi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\varphi_{p}\;,\;m_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes m_{p}\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\varphi_{i}(m_{i}).

Given a function
f\colon\Gamma\to A
, its
linear extension
to
A[\Gamma]
is the
A
-linear map

f\colon A[\Gamma]\to A\quad\text{defined by}\quad f\left(\sum\lambda_{i}\cdot w_{i}\right)=\sum\lambda_{i}\cdot f(w_{i}),

defining an isomorphism
A[\Gamma]^{*}\cong A^{\Gamma}
.

2.3.
Tensors and infinite cartesian powers

The following technical fact will be crucial for constructing elements in group rings that evaluate nontrivially on invariants of words.

Lemma 2.19

(
[
Goo72

)
.

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring. Then for any two sets

and

A^{S}\otimes A^{T}\to A^{S\times T}

Proof.

[

shows that for any

3.
Free groups

We start by defining letter-braiding polynomials of words in free groups. In §
6.1
we will find which of these remain well-defined once relations have been introduced. Our first approach in §
3.1
is topological, with few calculations, which are then the focus of §
3.2
.

3.1.
Topological construction

The invariants we construct are grounded in the Bar construction for simplicial cochains on simplicial sets.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a pointed simplicial set
X_{\bullet}
, let
C^{*}_{\cup}(X;A)
denote its simplicial cochain complex with coefficients in
A
. This is the complex of functions on nondegenerate simplices of
X
(or dual to the normalized chains). The cup product makes this into an associative algebra, and the basepoint provides an augmentation. Denote

Bar(X;A):=Bar(C^{*}_{\cup}(X;A)),

and
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(X;A)
for its cohomology.

The following is a standard application of the Quillen adjunction between the realization and singular functors and the quasi-isomorphism invariance of the Bar construction.

Lemma 3.2
.

The assignment
X\mapsto Bar(X;A)
is a contravariant functor from simplicial sets to filtered dg coalgebras.

A pointed continuous map on geometric realizations
g\colon|X|\to|Y|
induces a homomorphism of filtered coalgebras
g^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(Y;A)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(X;A)
that furthermore satisfies

(g_{2}\circ g_{1})^{*}=g_{1}^{*}\circ g_{2}^{*}\quad\text{ and }\quad f\sim g\text{ homotopic }\implies f^{*}=g^{*}.

If
g
is the geometric realization of a simplicial map
|f|
, then
f^{*}=g^{*}
on Bar cohomology.

The definition of letter braiding requires calculations on simplicial models for circles and wedges of circles.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\Delta[1]
be the standard
1
-simplex, with endpoints
\partial_{0}\Delta[1]
and
\partial_{1}\Delta[1]
.

The
standard circle
is the pointed simplcial set

S^{1}_{\bullet}:=\Delta[1]/(\partial_{0}\Delta[1]\sim\partial_{1}\Delta[1]).

Let
F_{S}
be a free group on basis
S
.
The
presentation complex of
F_{S}
is the pointed simplicial set

(X_{S})_{\bullet}=\bigvee_{s\in S}S^{1}_{\bullet},

having one standard loop for every generator
s\in S
of
F_{S}
.

The letter-braiding number is defined given the following choice of isomorphism.

Lemma 3.4
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of filtered augmented coalgebras

\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(S^{1}_{\bullet};A)\cong A[t],

with
|t|=0
, and
A[t]
considered as the cofree coalgebra generated by
t
.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in C^{1}(S^{1}_{\bullet};A)
be the cochain that assigns the value
1
to the unique nondegenerate
1
-simplex of
S^{1}
. The complex
C^{*}_{\cup}(S^{1}_{\bullet};A)
is the augmented algebra
A\cdot 1\oplus A\tau
with
d(\tau)=0=\tau^{2}
. Therefore, its Bar construction is the cofree coalgebra

Bar(A[\tau]/(\tau^{2}))=T(A\cdot\mathbf{s}^{-1}\tau)=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}A(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\tau)^{\otimes p}

with vanishing differentials. Denoting
t=\mathbf{s}^{-1}\tau
, this coalgebra is polynomial in
t
.
∎

More generally, for the wedge of circles
X_{S}
, the cochain algebra naturally consists of a unit along with

C^{1}_{\cup}(X_{S};A)\cong A^{S},

the
A
-valued functions on the set
S
with trivial cup product. This is in turn naturally interpreted as
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{S};A)\cong\text{Hom}(F_{S},A)
.
As in the last observation, the Bar construction is concentrated in degree
0
, with vanishing differentials. The Bar cohomology is thus naturally identified with the cofree coalgebra

\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(X_{S};A)\cong T(A^{S})=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\left(A^{S}\right)^{\otimes p}.

Definition 3.5
.

Recall that elements
w\in F_{S}
are in bijection with based homotopy classes of pointed maps
|S^{1}|\to|X_{S}|
. Abusing notation, let
w
denote a representative of the respective homotopy class.

The
letter-braiding polynomials
of the element
w\in F_{S}
are the composition

L_{\bullet}(w)\colon T(A^{S})\cong H^{0}_{Bar}(X_{S};A)\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(S^{1};A)\cong A[t].

The
letter-braiding numbers
of
w
are the linear coefficient of the respective polynomials

\ell_{\bullet}(w):=\frac{d}{dt}L_{\bullet}(w)|_{t=0}\colon T(A^{S})\longrightarrow A.

Since induced maps on Bar cohomology depend only on homotopy class, the polynomials
L_{\bullet}(w)
are independent of the choice of representative for
w
.

Example 3.6
.

The easiest calculation of braiding polynoimals is that of a generator
s\in S
. In this case, given functionals
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in A^{S}
, we have

L_{(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})}(s)=\alpha_{1}(s)\alpha_{2}(s)\ldots\alpha_{n}(s)\cdot t^{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad L_{1}(s)=1.

Indeed, let
i_{s}\colon S^{1}_{\bullet}\to(X_{S})_{\bullet}
be the inclusion of the edge labeled by
s
. On
1
-cochains it defines the pullback
i_{s}^{*}\colon C^{1}(X_{S};A)\to C^{1}(S^{1};A)
given by restriction
A^{S}\to A^{\{s\}}
. That is,
i_{s}^{*}(\alpha)=\alpha(s)\cdot\tau
, where
\tau\in A^{\{s\}}
is the functional
s\mapsto 1
. Recalling that the desuspension of
\tau
in the Bar complex of
S^{1}_{\bullet}
is
t
, the general calculation follows.

The braiding number is therefore

\ell_{(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})}(s)=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}(s)&n=1\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Recall that
A[t]
is cofreely cogenerated by
t
, which satisfies
\Delta(t^{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}t^{i}\otimes t^{n-i}
. With this, the coproduct of braiding polynomials is readily computable.

Theorem 3.7
.

The letter-braiding polynomial is a homomorphism of augmented filtered coalgebras, meaning that
L_{(T+a\cdot T^{\prime})}(s)=L_{T}(w)+a\cdot L_{T^{\prime}}(w)
and if
\Delta(T)=\sum_{i}T_{1}^{i}\otimes T_{2}^{i}
then

\Delta L_{T}(w)=\sum_{i}L_{T_{1}^{i}}(w)\otimes L_{T_{2}^{i}}(w)\quad\text{and}\quad L_{1}(w)=1.

Furthermore, the braiding polynomial is determined by the braiding number, in that

L_{T}(w)=\eta(T)\cdot 1+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\ell(w)^{\otimes k+1})_{\overline{\Delta}^{k}(T)}\cdot t^{k+1}

where
\overline{\Delta}^{k}
is the iterated reduced coproduct
(\overline{\Delta}\otimes\operatorname{Id}^{\otimes k-1})\circ\ldots\circ(\overline{\Delta}\otimes\operatorname{Id})\circ\overline{\Delta}
, and
(\ell(w)\otimes\ell(w))_{T_{1}\otimes T_{2}}=\ell_{T_{1}}(w)\cdot\ell_{T_{2}}(w)
.

Proof.

Functoriality of the Bar construction implies that the letter braiding polynomial
L(w)
of any
w\in F_{S}
is a coalgebra homomorphism. Thus, for every
T
with
\Delta(T)=\sum_{i}T_{1}^{i}\otimes T_{2}^{i}
,

(\Delta\circ L(w))_{T}=(L(w)\otimes L(w))\circ\Delta(T)=\sum_{i}(L(w)\otimes L(w))_{T_{1}^{i}\otimes T_{2}^{i}}=\sum_{i}L_{T_{1}^{i}}(w)\otimes L_{T_{2}^{i}}(w)

as claimed.

Now, suppose
L_{T}(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}t^{k}
. Applying the reduced coproduct
k
times to
t^{k+1}
gives the pure tensor
\underbrace{(t\otimes\cdots\otimes t)}_{k+1\text{ terms}}
. On the other hand,

a_{k}\cdot(t\otimes\cdots\otimes t)+\text{ higher order terms }=\overline{\Delta}^{k}L_{T}(w)=L(w)^{\otimes k+1}({\overline{\Delta}^{k}(T)}),

so the coefficient
a_{k}

We next unpack the sense in which braiding numbers are functorial with respect to group homomorphisms of free groups.
On Bar cohomology

for every

Theorem 3.8
.

Given a homomorphism of free groups

Proposition 3.19

(0-linking and weight reduction)

Every element

\delta_{0}

. In the notation below, we identify
\delta_{0}
with
t
.

The polynomial can be computed via the following weight-reduction procedure. Given a tensor
(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})
such that every
\alpha_{i}
is either

or

be

(1)

If the modified weight is
0

t

(preferably one with

and proceed.

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\int_{w}fdx\right)\cdot&(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{k-1}|t|\alpha_{k+1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})\\
-&(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{k-2}|(\alpha_{k-1}\smile d^{-1}(fdx))|\alpha_{k+1}|\alpha_{k+2}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})\\

By convention, if

These cup products are given by the following rules:

\displaystyle t\smile d^{-1}(fdx)=-\left(\int_{w}fdx\right)\cdot t\quad

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad d^{-1}(fdx)\smile t=0

\displaystyle gdx\smile d^{-1}(fdx)=-\left(\int_{\partial_{1}(\bullet)}^{n+1}fdx\right)g\cdot dx\quad

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad d^{-1}(fdx)\smile gdx=-\left(\int_{\partial_{0}(\bullet)}^{n+1}fdx\right)g\cdot dx,

where
\partial_{0}(\bullet)
(or
\partial_{1}(\bullet)
) is the function on segments that returns the initial vertex (or terminal vertex), and we define

.

(3)

The summands obtained in Step (2) have strictly smaller modified weight than the original. Collect like terms and return to Step (1) for each resulting summand.

More generally, let
\gamma_{n}F_{S}
be the
n
-th step of the lower central series of
F_{S}
, recalled in §
2.2
.

Proposition 3.23

(Invariants of commutators)
.

For a nested commutator
w\in\gamma_{n}F_{S}
and any generator
s\in S
, the commutator
[w,s]
has invariants

(5)

\ell_{(\alpha_{0}|\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})}([w,s])=\ell_{(\alpha_{0}|\ldots|\alpha_{n-1})}(w)\cdot\alpha_{n}(s)-\alpha_{0}(s)\cdot\ell_{(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})}(w).

The proof is deferred to Remark
5.7
below.

Weight reduction can be treated as a recursive procedure. The
k
-th step of the weight-reduction process shows that any tensor
(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})\in Bar^{0}(\tilde{w};A)
is cohomolgous to a linear combination of tensors of the form
(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n-k}|t^{d})
for
d\leq k
. Suppose we already completed a weight-reduction calculation for
(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n-k})
, then we can use the following.

Lemma 3.24
.

If
T\in Bar^{0}(\tilde{w};A)
has weight reduction
P(t)\in A[t]
, then
T|t^{d}
has weight reduction
P(t)\cdot t^{d}
.

Proof.

If
T=P(t)+d_{Bar}(\eta)
for some polynomial
P(t)\in{A[t]}
and for
\eta\in Bar^{-1}(w;A)
, then we claim that

T|\underbrace{t|\ldots|t}_{d\text{ times}}=P(t)\cdot t^{d}+d_{Bar}(\eta|\underbrace{t|\ldots|t}_{d\text{ times}}).

Indeed, since every
f\in\overline{C}^{0}(\tilde{w};A)
satisfies
f(0)=0
, it follows that
f\smile t=0
. Thus,

d_{Bar}(\eta|\underbrace{t|\ldots|t}_{d\text{ times}})=d_{Bar}(\eta)|\underbrace{t|\ldots|t}_{d\text{ times}}

demonstrating the claimed equality.
∎

One particular choice of weight reduction gives rise to the following discrete analog of Chen’s iterated integrals in
[
Che71
]
.

Corollary 3.25

(Letter braiding as iterated sum)
.

For functions
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in A^{S}
, the letter-braiding number of a word
\tilde{w}\colon[n]\to S\cup S^{-1}
is

(6)

\ell_{(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{r})}(w)=\sum_{i_{1}<_{w}i_{2}<_{w}\ldots<_{w}i_{r}}\alpha_{1}(\tilde{w}(i_{1}))\ldots\alpha_{r}(\tilde{w}(i_{r}))

where
i<_{w}j
is the relations
i<j
when
\tilde{w}(i)\in S
, and
i\leq j
when
\tilde{w}(i)\in S^{-1}
.

In particular, when the
\alpha_{j}
’s are indicator forms, this sum counts the (signed) number of occurrences of a sequence of generators and their inverses, in order, within a word. Both the signs and the “edge cases” specified by
<_{w}
are critical for invariance. For example, in
F_{\{x,y,z\}}
the invariant
\ell_{X|Y|Z}(w)
counts the number of times the generator
x^{\pm}
appears before
y^{\pm}
which in turn appears before
z^{\pm}
in the word
w
(see Example
1.4
).

Proof.

First, observe that for any
\alpha\in A^{S}
, writing
\tilde{w}^{*}\alpha=fdx
we have

\tilde{w}^{*}(\alpha)_{i}=\alpha(\tilde{w}(i))=f(i).

The result thus follows from the next characterization of weight-reduced polynomials: let
\sim
denote the relation of differing by coboundary, then for all functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
we have
(f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx)\sim\ell\cdot t+t^{2}P(t)
for some polynomial
P(t)
and

(7)

\ell=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}\leq i_{2}\leq\ldots\leq i_{r}\leq n\\
\text{s.t. }\forall j:\,\partial_{1}(i_{j})\leq i_{j+1}\end{subarray}}f_{1}(i_{1})f_{2}(i_{2})\ldots f_{r}(i_{r}),

where
\partial_{1}(i)
is the terminal vertex of the
i
-th edge in
S^{1}(\tilde{w})
. Indeed, the condition
\partial_{1}(i)\leq j
is equivalent to
i<_{w}j
.

The equality is proved by induction on
r
, with
r=1
being the equality in Example
3.22
. Now, denote the iterated sum in (
7
) by
\int_{w}(f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx)
and assume by induction that for all
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})

(f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx)\sim t^{2}\cdot P(t)+t\cdot\int_{w}(f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx).

Then for
r+1
we have

f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx|f_{r+1}dx\sim\left(\int_{w}f_{r+1}dx\right)\cdot(f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx|t)-(f_{1}dx|\ldots|f_{r}dx\smile d^{-1}(f_{r+1}dx)).

Lemma
3.24
shows that the first term has polynomial invariant divisible by

and thus does not contribute to the linear coefficient. And by the cup product formulas in Proposition
3.19
,

f_{r}dx\smile d^{-1}(f_{r+1}dx)=-\bigg(\sum_{i_{r+1}\geq\partial_{1}(\bullet)}f_{r+1}(i_{r+1})\bigg)f_{r}(\bullet)dx.

The inductive hypothesis now implies that the linear coefficient is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{r}\leq n\\
\forall j:\,\partial_{1}(i_{j})\leq i_{j+1}\end{subarray}}f_{1}(i_{1})\ldots f_{r-1}(i_{r-1})\cdot\bigg(f_{r}(i_{r})\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{r}\leq i_{r+1}\\
\partial_{1}(i_{r})\leq i_{r+1}\end{subarray}}f_{r+1}(i_{r+1})\bigg).\qed

.

.

.

Let

For

4.
General groups

We extend letter-braiding invariants to general groups, first recalling definitions.
Define the fundamental group of a pointed simplicial set
(Y_{\bullet},*)
as that of its geometric realization. Let
\pi_{1}(Y,*)=\Gamma
. Letter-braiding invariants of
\Gamma
are assigned to the coalgebra
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A)
. Every
w\in\Gamma
determines a continuous map
w\colon|S^{1}|\to|Y|
, well-defined up to homotopy, and thus induces a pullback

w^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(S^{1};A).

Using the canonical isomorphism
L\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(S^{1};A)\to A[t]
, we define the following.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A)
. For every
w\in\Gamma
the
letter-braiding polynomial
of
w
associated to
T
is

L_{T}(w)=L(w^{*}(T))\in A[t].

The
letter-braiding number

\ell_{T}(w)\in A
is the linear coefficient of
L_{T}(w)
.

By relating these letter-braiding polynomials to the ones in §
3
, we will get formulas for computing them and obstructions for their existence.

The following claim shows that the particular space that realizes a group
\Gamma
as its fundamental group is immaterial. Consequently, any such space
(Y,*)
gives a distinct viewpoint on the braiding invariants of words in
\Gamma
, in some cases by a geometric description of the ring
\mathrm{H}^{*}(Y;A)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
(Y,*)
and
(Y^{\prime},*^{\prime})
are connected pointed simplicial sets and
f\colon(|Y|,*)\to(|Y^{\prime}|,*^{\prime})
is a pointed map of their realizations that induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{1}
, then the pullback

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y^{\prime};A)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A)

given by Lemma
3.2
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

By quasi-isomorphism invariance of
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(-;A)
and Lemma
3.2
, it is sufficient to prove the claim for a simplicial map
(Y,*)\to(Y^{\prime},*^{\prime})
. Let
Z
be the mapping cone of
f
. By Van Kampen’s theorem
Z
is simply connected, and thus the long exact sequence in cohomology has

\cancel{\tilde{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(Z;A)}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{1}(Y^{\prime};A)\overset{f^{*}}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{1}(Y;A)\to\cancel{\mathrm{H}^{1}(Z;A)}\overset{d}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y^{\prime};A)\overset{f^{*}}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y;A),

so that
f^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{*}(Y^{\prime};A)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}(Y;A)
is an isomorphism when
*\leq 1
and injective when
*=2
. At the cochain level, this means that the pullback
f^{*}\colon C^{*}(Y^{\prime};A)\to C^{*}(Y;A)
is
1
-connected, so that after desuspension it becomes
0
-connected on
\mathbf{s}^{-1}{\overline{C}(-;A)}
.

Since the complexes here consist of torsion-free and thus flat
A
-modules, it follows that on every tensor power

(f^{*})^{\otimes p}\colon\mathbf{s}^{-1}{\overline{C}(Y^{\prime};A)}^{\otimes p}\to\mathbf{s}^{-1}{\overline{C}(Y;A)}^{\otimes p}

is also
0
-connected.
Passing to Bar constructions, we imitate the proof that
Bar(-)
is a quasi-isomorphism invariant (Lemma
2.11
) and consider the spectral sequence of the filtration by weight. Its
E_{0}
page is simply the direct sum of complexes
\mathbf{s}^{-1}{\overline{C}(-;A)}^{\otimes p}
, and thus
f
induces a
0
-connected map on the
E_{0}
-page. Since this connectivity property passes through the pages of the spectral sequence, it follows that the map

f^{*}\colon Bar(Y^{\prime};A)\to Bar(Y;A)

is already
0
-connected. It therefore induces an isomorphism on
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(-;A)
and an injection on
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{Bar}(-;A)
.
∎

In the present work, our preferred choice of simplicial set with fundamental group
\Gamma
is the standard simplicial model for the classifying space.

Definition 4.3
.

Recall, the simplicial Bar construction of a group
\Gamma
is the simpilicial set

B\Gamma_{\bullet}\colon[p]\mapsto\Gamma^{p}

with face maps
\partial_{0}
and
\partial_{p}
forgetting the respective extremal coordinate, internal face maps given by multiplication in
\Gamma
of adjacent terms, and degeneracies inserting copies of the unit. Its fundamental group is canonically isomorphic to
\Gamma
.
Denote

Bar(\Gamma;A):=Bar(B\Gamma;A),\text{ and }\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)=\mathrm{H}^{*}(Bar(B\Gamma;A)).

The functoriality of the construction
\Gamma\mapsto B\Gamma
implies the following.

Lemma 4.4
.

The assignment
\Gamma\mapsto Bar(\Gamma;A)
is a contravariant functor from the category of groups to the category of bigraded dg-coalgebras. Thus
\Gamma\mapsto\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)

is a contravariant functor taking values in the category of filtered coalgebras.

Moreover, any connected
(Y,*)
admits a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\pi_{1}(Y,*);A)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A).

Proof.

Recalling that
|B\Gamma_{\bullet}|
is a
K(\Gamma,1)
space, homotopy classes of pointed maps
(X,*)\to|B\Gamma_{\bullet}|
are in bijection with group homomorphisms
\pi_{1}(X,*)\to\Gamma
. In particular, for any topological space
(X,*)
there exists a pointed map
i\colon X\to|B(\pi_{1}(X,*))_{\bullet}|
, unique up to homotopy, inducing the identity on
\pi_{1}
. Applied to
X=|Y|
, Lemma
3.2
gives a canonical pullback

i^{*}\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\pi_{1}(Y,*);A)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A),

which by Lemma
4.2
is an isomorphism. Functoriality and naturality are clear.
∎

4.1.
Letter braiding and vanishing (Massey) products

The pages of the spectral sequence of the bicomplex
Bar(\Gamma;A)
give approximations to
H^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
. The first page the tensor (co)algebra on
H^{1}(\Gamma;A)=\text{Hom}_{grp}(\Gamma,A)
, which is already the full answer when
\Gamma
is free as we saw in §
3
. The second page of the sequence in degree
0
is the
0
-th Bar cohomology of the graded ring
H^{*}(B\Gamma;A)
. We see immediately
that, in weight two,
H^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
is
computed by the kernel of the cup product

H^{1}(B\Gamma;A)\otimes H^{1}(B\Gamma;A)\to H^{2}(B\Gamma;A)

In general constructing letter-braiding invariants for
\Gamma
relies on understanding the cohomology ring, including its Massey products, of
B\Gamma
or any other
X
with
\pi_{1}(X,*)\cong\Gamma
in
degrees one and two. We will observe that introducing relations to a group simultaneously gives obstructions for braiding invariants to be well-defined as well as creates Massey products in this cohomology ring, both encoded by
d_{Bar}\colon Bar^{0}\to Bar^{1}
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
F_{\{e,f\}}
be the free group on two generators, with letter-braiding invariants indexed by noncommutative polynomials
\mathbb{Z}\langle E,F\rangle
. For example,
\ell_{E}(-)
counts the signed number of
e
’s in words.

Now, introduce the relation
e=f
, so a letter
e
may be replaced by an
f
. With this, the functional
\ell_{E}(-)
is no longer well-defined, e.g.
eefe=fffe
under this relation, but

\ell_{E}(eefe)=3\neq 1=\ell_{E}(fffe).

So
E
and
F
are lost as invariants of the group. However,
E+F
still defines an invariant.

At the level of the Bar complex, the effect of introducing the relation is to add a
2
-cell
D^{2}_{e^{-1}f}
, so that
dE=-dF=[D^{2}_{e^{-1}f}]
are no longer closed. But their sum
E+F
is closed, and represents a braiding invariant.

Example 4.6
.

A more interesting example is obtained by introducing the relation
ef=fe
to
F_{\{e,f\}}
, modeled by a
2
-torus. Now,
E
and
F
still define invariants of words, but
(E|F)
does not: it counts occurrences of
e^{\pm}

before

f^{\pm}
, which changes upon application of the relation.

Accordingly,
(E|F)
is not a Bar cocycle, because
E\smile F\neq 0
in cohomology. Anti-symmetry of the cup product means that
(E|F)+(F|E)
is an invariant.

Remark 4.7
.

The loss of the invariant
(E|F)
in the last example is reflected by the fact that in the abelian group
F_{\{e,f\}}/\langle[e,f]\rangle
, any two distinct elements are already distinguished by their evaluation on
E
and
F
, so no additional invariants are needed. Still, higher weight invariants exist and serve to distinguish linear combinations of elements in the group ring, as discussed in Example
5.8
.

On the cohomological side, the Bar cohomology features cochains with vanishing products, as explained next.

Example 4.8
.

Let
Y
be any pointed simplicial set. Given
1
-cochains
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in C^{1}(Y;A)
, let’s examine when the tensor
T=(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})\in Bar^{0}(Y;A)
is a cocycle. The differential in the Bar construction is
d_{Bar}=d_{1}+d_{2}
, with
d_{1}
internal to
C^{*}(Y;A)
and
d_{2}
given by cup products of cochains. So
d_{Bar}T=0
iff

•

every
\alpha_{i}
is closed in
C^{1}(Y;A)
, and

•

every cup product
\alpha_{i}\smile\alpha_{i+1}
vanishes in
C^{2}(Y;A)
.

When these conditions are satisfied,
T
represents a class in
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A)
.

Now, let
w\colon S^{1}(w)\to Y
be a simplicial map representing some
w\in\pi_{1}(Y,*)
. Then

w^{*}(T)=(w^{*}(\alpha_{1})|\ldots|w^{*}(\alpha_{n}))\in Bar^{0}(w;A)

can be weight-reduced to a polynomial
L_{T}(w)\in{A[t]}
, thus giving the letter-braiding polynomial of
w
.
Most importantly, due to relations in
\pi_{1}(Y,*)
there might be different words representing the same group element. But, Lemma
3.2
guarantees that the letter-braiding polynomial only depends on the pointed homotopy class
S^{1}\to Y
, and is thus an invariant of
w
.

To better understand the condition
d_{Bar}(T)=0
, consider the following two examples.

Example 4.9
.

Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in C^{1}(Y;A)
are
1
-cocycles whose cup product vanishes in cohomology. Then
d_{Bar}(\alpha|\beta)=-\alpha\smile\beta=d\eta
for some
\eta\in C^{1}(Y;A)
but might not be
0
. However, the in-homogeneous linear combination
(\alpha|\beta)+\eta
satisfies,

d_{Bar}\colon(\alpha|\beta)+\eta\longmapsto-\alpha\smile\beta-d\eta=0

giving a Bar
0
-cocycle of weight
2
. We thus get a well-defined letter-braiding invariant, by weight reducing
(w^{*}(\alpha)|w^{*}(\beta))+w^{*}(\eta)\in Bar^{0}(w;A)
, while neither of the summands is by itself a homotopy invariant.

Summarising, cup products need only vanish at the level of cohomology to give invariants, but one must then include a correction term of lower weight ‘witnessing’ this.

Example 4.10

To construct a weight

5.
Group rings

As discussed above, letter-braiding numbers define functionals

, for every

. These extend
A
-linearly to the group ring

.

Definition 5.1
.

The letter-braiding pairing

\langle-,\bullet\rangle\colon\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)\otimes A[\Gamma]\to A

is the
A
-linear extension of
\langle T,w\rangle=\ell_{T}(w)+\eta(T)
from
\Gamma
to

, where

is the counit. It further extends to a pairing between their tensor powers as in §

.

The various properties proved in §

are most naturally stated in terms of this pairing, matching the functorial filtered coalgebra structure of

with the filtered algebra structure of

, as stated in Theorem

.

Remark 5.2

(
Diagonal
)
.

Recall that
A[\Gamma]
is furthermore equipped with a diagonal coproduct
\Delta(w)=w\otimes w
for
w\in\Gamma
, making it into a Hopf algebra. In contrast, the Bar construction of a commutative dg algebra has a shuffle product, making it into a Hopf algebra as well. Chen
[
Che71
]
matches these structures on
\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]
and
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Omega_{dR}(M))
under his pairing by iterated integrals. However, since our cochain algebras are rarely commutative, the shuffle product does not commute with

and does not induce a Hopf algebra structure on

.

Instead, there is an
E_{\infty}
-algebra structure on cochains, as instrumental in Mandell’s theory
[
Man06
]
. For our low dimensional applications, only the first layer of this homotopy commutativity structure is needed: the cup-1 operation, as central to Porter–Suciu work on 1-minimal models
[
PS22
]
.

Problem 5.3

Describe the product structure on

dual to the coproduct on

.

For the proof of Theorem

Lemma 5.4

Let

denote the canonical augmentation

Definition 5.5
.

For words

Proposition 5.6
.

Let

be any sequence of elements. The multi-evaluation
\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n}})
satisfies the following.

(1)

It is a linear combination of letter-braiding invariants of products,

\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n}})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq n}\ell_{\bullet}({w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{i_{k}}}).

(2)

In terms of the letter-braiding pairing,

\ell_{T}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n}})=\langle T\;,\;(w_{0}-1)\cdots(w_{n}-1)\rangle.

(3)

For every

(\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{i}})\otimes\ell_{\bullet}({w_{i+1}|\ldots|w_{n}}))\circ\overline{\Delta}=\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n}}).

Proof.

The third statement follows immediately from coassociativity of
\Delta
. Next, the first and the second equations are evidently equivalent, so let us prove the latter by induction on

. Consider

and assume that

\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n}})=\langle\bullet,x\rangle

\langle\bullet,{x(w_{n+1}-1)}\rangle=\cancel{\epsilon(w_{n+1}-1)}\cdot\langle\bullet,x\rangle+\cancel{\epsilon(x)}\cdot\langle\bullet,w_{n+1}-1\rangle+\langle\overline{\Delta}(\bullet),x\otimes(w_{n+1}-1)\rangle,

=\langle\overline{\Delta}(\bullet),x\otimes w_{n+1}\rangle.

The last expression is
(\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n}})\otimes\ell_{\bullet}{(w_{n+1})})\circ\overline{\Delta}
and thus agrees with
\ell_{\bullet}({w_{0}|\ldots|w_{n+1}})
.

Remark 5.10
.

In light of the last theorem, our setup can be understood as an extension of the Magnus expansion on free groups in the following ways.

•

The group
\Gamma
needs not be free, where now the only well-defined Fox derivatives are inhomogeneous linear combinations that exhibit vanishing cup and Massey products.

•

The group
\Gamma
need not be finitely generated.

•

The coefficient ring
A
need not be
\mathbb{Z}
. E.g. it may be a field of characteristic
p>0
.

•

The calculus is independent of a presentation for
\Gamma
. Instead, the only necessary input is a presentation of the cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)\cong\text{Hom}(\Gamma,A)
with its Massey products

\mu_{n}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)^{\otimes n}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;A).

Let us illustrate a major improvement afforded by allowing coefficients in general PIDs. While rational homotopy theory gives insight into the ranks of the lower central series quotients of a group, it can never detect words in finite groups. More generally, any invariant theory with values in a torsion-free ring
A
will necessarily have
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)=0
for any finite group
\Gamma
.

Example 5.11
.

Take
A=\mathbb{F}_{2}
and consider the Heisenberg group
\Gamma=\mathscr{H}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
of matrices

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&X&Z\\
&1&Y\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}\text{ s.t. }X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{F}_{2}\right\}\subseteq\operatorname{Gl}_{3}(\mathbb{F}_{2}).

It has
8

\langle x,y,z\mid x^{2},y^{2},z^{2},[x,y]z^{-1}\rangle
, and

given by the sub-diagonal entries.

On the group cochain level, the functions

and

are defined in

is not closed. Instead,

A well-defined invariant is therefore

Now,

[x,y]=z

More generally, finite

6.
Completeness of letter-braiding

We next show that letter-braiding invariants furnish a
complete
coalgebra of functionals that are continuous with respect to the augmentation ideal topology on the group ring. Recall that the group ring
A[\Gamma]
is filtered by powers of the augmentation ideal
I\lhd A[\Gamma]
. The
I
-adic completion is inverse limit

A[\Gamma]^{\wedge}_{I}=\varprojlim A[\Gamma]/I^{n}

topologized so that all quotients
A[\Gamma]^{\wedge}_{I}\twoheadrightarrow A[\Gamma]/I^{n}
are continuous, when the target has the discrete topology. In particular, a functional
\varphi\colon A[\Gamma]^{\wedge}_{I}\to A
is
continuous
if and only if it vanishes on some power
I^{N}
.

Theorem 6.1

(
Completeness of letter-braiding invariants
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. The letter-braiding pairing defines a natural isomorphism of filtered coalgebras

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{Hom}_{cts}(A[\Gamma]^{\wedge}_{I},A)=\varinjlim_{n}\text{Hom}_{A}(A[\Gamma]/I^{n},A)

where the
A[\Gamma]^{\wedge}_{I}
is the
I
-adic completion of the group ring equipped with the
I
-adic topology, and
\text{Hom}_{cts}(-,-)
denotes continuous linear functionals.

In other words, for every functional
\varphi\colon A[\Gamma]\to A
that vanishes on some power
I^{n}
, there exists a unique
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
of weight
\leq n
such that

\langle T,\bullet\rangle=\varphi(\bullet).

Remark 6.2
.

\Gamma

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
is always a coalgebra, while duals of infinite dimensional algebras are not – this is due to the failing of

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)
is the better invariant of

The last step of the proof is the most elaborate, first requiring substantial terminology.

6.2.

To complete the proof of the lifting criterion of Theorem

In the construction below, a ‘space’

In the special case of a presentation

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

For a finitely generated free group

generated by
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\}
, we claim that the truncated group ring

is the free

(x_{i_{1}}-1)(x_{i_{2}}-1)\cdots(x_{i_{d}}-1)

for sequences
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})
of length

. Indeed, every generator

can be written as

, its inverse is
x_{i}^{-1}=1-(x_{i}-1)+(x_{i}-1)^{2}-\ldots+(-1)^{n-1}(x_{i}-1)^{n-1}+\ldots
, and every element in the free group can be written as a product of

so expressible as a linear combination of shifted monomials. Freeness will follow from the following observation.

Let
X_{i}\colon F_{r}\to A
be the linear functional that counts instances of

with sign, so that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{r};A)=\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{r}\rangle
. Then the letter-braiding invariants of

are labeled by the tensor coalgebra

of noncommutative polynomials in the

\langle X_{i_{1}}|\ldots|X_{i_{d}}\,,\,(x_{j_{1}}-1)\cdots(x_{j_{d}}-1)\rangle=\ell_{x_{j_{1}}}(X_{i_{1}})\ldots\ell_{x_{j_{d}}}(X_{i_{d}})=\delta_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}),(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})}

gives a dual basis to degree

that define a dual basis to the monomials. This proves that letter-braiding

is surjective and thus an isomorphism (it also gives a recipe for constructing the Taylor-Maclaurin expansion of every group ring element, i.e. writing it as a linear combination of monomials).

Now, let
F_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma
be a presentation of some group. Then the Cartesian square in Corollary

shows that
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)_{\leq n}\to(A[\Gamma]/I^{n})^{*}
is also an isomorphism.
∎

6.4.
Relative Massey products measure relations

In Lemma
4.11
we saw that the obstructions to having a tensor
T(\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A))
define a letter-braiding invariant of
\Gamma
is that all its cup and Massey products must vanish. In the last section we gave an alternative characterization as having vanishing defect, as measured by evaluations on relations. The relationship between Massey products and relations in a group has a long history, going back to the work of Dwyer
[
Dwy75b
]
and Fenn–Sjerve
[
FS87
]
), but they only cover a somewhat narrow set of examples: ones in which the Massey product of cohomology classes
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
is defined, and it pairs with relations that are commutators.

However, even the most elementary examples we considered above, of surface groups and braid groups (Examples
4.14
,
4.21
), exhibit interesting invariants in which one must consider a linear combination of tensors from
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;A)
to detect various group elements. In this section we expand the scope of Dwyer’s analysis, giving a full picture of how Massey products coincide with letter-braiding numbers of relations, including non-commutators, via a
relative Massey product
.

Let
\Gamma=\langle S|R\rangle
be a group presentation. Applying the construction
Y\mapsto Y_{|R}
from §
6.1
to the wedge of circles
X_{S}
from §
3
, we obtain a presentation
2
-complex
X_{S|R}
whose fundamental group is
\Gamma
. It contains a subcomplex
p\colon X_{S}\hookrightarrow X_{S|R}
, inducing the surjection
F_{S}\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma
. Observe that the
1
-simplices of
X_{S}
are in bijection with the set of generators
S\subseteq\Gamma
, while
X_{S|R}
has additional edges coming from the relations.
1
-cochains are
A
-valued functions on those edges.

Observation 6.19
.

Restricting
1
-cochains of
X_{S|R}
to the generators
S\subseteq\Gamma
gives a surjection of coalgebras
p^{*}\colon Bar(X_{S|R};A)\twoheadrightarrow Bar(X_{S};A)=T(A^{S})
, where the target is the coalgebra of letter-braiding invariants on
F_{S}
from §
3
. So every letter-braiding invariant of
F_{S}
lifts to a Bar cochain of
\Gamma
by extending the functions from
X_{S}
to the interiors of relation discs
D^{2}(r)\subseteq X_{S|R}
. But those extensions will typically not be
d_{Bar}
-closed: their coboundary involves a
2
-cochain of
X_{S|R}
, detecting some of the relations.
Let us quantify the obstruction to closed lifting.

Consider then the short exact sequence

(19)

0\to(K)_{Bar}\to Bar(X_{S|R};A)\to T(A^{S})\to 0

where
(K)_{Bar}
is a coalgebraic coideal. In analogy with relative cochains
C^{*}(X_{S|R},X_{S};A)
, denote this coideal by
Bar(X_{S|R},X_{S};A)
. More generally,

Definition 6.20
.

When
p\colon X\hookrightarrow Y
is a subcomplex inclusion, denote

Bar(Y,X;A)=\ker\left(p^{*}\colon Bar(Y;A)\twoheadrightarrow Bar(X;A)\right),

the coideal cogenerated by
K=C^{*}(Y,X;A)
in
T(\overline{C^{*}(Y;A)})
. Its cohomology will be denoted
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(Y,X;A)
.

An alternative equivalent definition proceeds by recalling that a surjection
Bar(C)\twoheadrightarrow Bar(C_{0})
is quasi-isomorphic to the injection
Bar(C)\hookrightarrow Bar(C,K)
. Then
Bar(Y,X;A)
could be defined as
\mathrm{coker}(Bar(C)\hookrightarrow Bar(C,K))
– the quotient by the defect
0
subcomplex – up to a degree shift.

Proposition 6.21
.

An inclusion
X\hookrightarrow Y
defines an exact sequence

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(Y;A)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A)\xrightarrow{\mu}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{Bar}(Y,X;A),

with
\mu(-)
measuring the obstruction of lifting Bar cocycles from
X
to
Y
. We call
\mu(-)
the
relative Massey product
, for reasons explained below.

In particular, a tensor
T\in T(A^{S})
extends to a closed cochain in
Bar^{0}(X_{S|R};A)
if and only if its relative Massey product
\mu(T)\in\mathrm{H}_{Bar}^{1}(X_{S|R},X_{S};A)
vanishes.

Proof.

The snake lemma applied to

0\to Bar(Y,X;A)\to Bar(Y;A)\to Bar(X;A)\to 0

gives the claimed exact sequence. It follows that
T\in T(A^{S})
lifts to a class
\tilde{T}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X_{S|R};A)
if and only if
\mu(T)=0
.
∎

For
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A)
its the relative Massey product is computed by picking any lift
\tilde{T}\in Bar^{0}(Y;A)
and computing its Bar differential:

\mu(T)=[d_{Bar}(\tilde{T})]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{Bar}(Y,X;A).

When
\mu(T)
has weight
1
, it is a relative cohomology class
\mu(T)\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y,X;A)
whose image in the absolute cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{2}(Y;A)
is the usual Massey product of the lift
\tilde{T}
, as explained in Proposition
4.13
. Thus
\mu(T)
is a refinement of the Massey product that remembers that
T
came from
X
.

To see how the relative Massey product detects relations in a group, return to the special case
(X_{S|R},X_{S})
. Then
\mu(T)
takes values in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{S|R},X_{S};A)\cong A^{R}
, and is therefore exactly a function on relations.

Proposition 6.22

(
Relative Massey = letter-braiding of relations
)
.

Suppose
T\in T(A^{S})
is a letter-braiding invariant of
F_{S}
whose relative Massey product has weight
1
, that is
\mu(T)\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{S|R},X_{S};A)\cong A^{R}
. Then for every relation
r\in R
,

(20)

\ell_{T}(r)=\langle\mu(T),[D^{2}(r)]\rangle,

where
[D^{2}(r)]\in H_{2}(X_{S|R},X_{S};A)
is the relative homology class of the relation disc of
r
.

Proof.

This is essentially a special case of Proposition
6.16
. With the interpretation of
\mathrm{H}^{*}_{Bar}(Y,X;A)
using the defect filtration as in the paragraph preceding Proposition
6.21
, the condition on
\mu(T)
is equivalent to saying that
T
has defect
1
. The proof of (
18
) here works, using the case of the universal relation
(D^{2},S^{1})
: the relative Massey product in the exact sequence

\cancel{\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(D^{2};A)}\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(S^{1};A)\xrightarrow{\mu}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{Bar}(D^{2},S^{1};A)\to\cancel{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{Bar}(D^{2};A)}

is an isomorphism, identifying the letter-braiding invariant with the evaluation on the relative fundamental class
[D^{2}]\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(D^{2},S^{1};A)
.
∎

The absolute version of this proposition follows as a special case, and it appears e.g. in
[
FS87
, Main theorem]
. They evaluate the absolute Massey product
\mu_{n}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})^{\otimes n}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
on
H_{2}(\Gamma)
, which by Hopf’s formula is generated by
R\cap[\Gamma,\Gamma]
– relations that happen to also be commutators. These correspond to disc classes
[D^{2}(r)]
that come from
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X_{S|R};\mathbb{Z})
as they have vanishing boundary. Fenn–Sjerve show that the evaluation coincides with Magnus expansion coefficients, which by Theorem
5.9
is the same as
\ell_{T}(r)
. Proposition
6.22
therefore subsumes their result and extend it in the following directions:

•

While older work considers Massey products
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
, which are
\mu_{n}(\alpha_{1}|\ldots|\alpha_{n})
on rank
1
tensors, many groups require considering linear combinations of tensors, which our theory accommodates – see Examples
4.14
,
4.21
.

•

Our generalization sees the entire set of relations
R
, and also considers products of cochains
C^{1}(\Gamma;A)^{\otimes n}
that are no a priori closed. In fact, the pairing of
\alpha\in C^{1}(\Gamma;A)
with a non-commutator
r\in R
is exactly the obstruction to
\alpha
being closed, with (
20
) specializing to

\ell_{\alpha}(r)=d\alpha([D^{2}(r)])

so
d\alpha=0
iff
\ell_{\alpha}(r)=0
for all
r\in R
.

•

While we only discussed
\mu(T)
of weight
1
above, higher weights appear and are significant obstructions to lifting as well. This is necessary because
T(A^{S})
is a coalgebra, and one might attempt to lift braiding invariants
T
that split into several factors with nontrivial relative Massey products. This situation is quantified by the defect map and Proposition
6.16
.

7.
Topological applications

So far, topology provided us with invariants of groups. In the other direction, we get a constructive proof the following fact.

7.1.
Cochains determine fundamental group ring

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
X
be any connected pointed CW-complex (or simplicial set) with a finitely generated fundamental group. Then with coefficients in any PID, the
2
-truncation of its singular (or simiplicial) cochain algebra
C^{*\leq 2}_{\cup}(X;A)
, along with its augmentation, determines the completed group ring
A[\pi_{1}(X,*)]_{I}^{\wedge}
explicitly via letter-braiding invariants.

Proof.

Given a filtered coalgebra, its dual is naturally a completed algebra, and this operation is inverse to the continuous dual (see e.g.
[
SS15
, Subsection (2.5.3)]
). In light of Theorem
6.1
, it follows that
A[\pi_{1}(X,*)]_{I}^{\wedge}
is isomorphic to the complete algebra of
A
-linear functionals on
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(X;A)
, where their pairing is the letter-braiding pairing. But the latter coalgebra is computed from
C^{*}_{\cup}(X;A)
in degrees
\leq 2
along with its augmentation coming form evaluation at the basepoint.
∎

To get ones hands on the (completion of the) group
\pi_{1}(X,*)
itself, additional structure is needed: the diagonal
X\to X\times X
, inducing the Hopf algebra structure on
A[\pi_{1}(X,*)]
. This involves the
E_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
C^{*}(X;A)
, and is not present in our discussion.

\overline{\Delta}(T)=\sum T_{1}^{i}\otimes T_{2}^{i}

\overline{\Delta}(T-\varphi^{*}(T))=\sum T_{1}^{i}\otimes T_{2}^{i}-\varphi^{*}(T_{1}^{i})\otimes\varphi^{*}(T_{2}^{i})=\sum(T_{1}^{i}-\varphi^{*}(T_{1}^{i}))\otimes T_{2}^{i}+\varphi^{*}(T_{1}^{i})\otimes(T_{2}-\varphi^{*}(T_{2}^{i})).

But since both
T_{1}^{i}
and
T_{2}^{i}
have weight
\leq k
, they are fixed by
\varphi^{*}
and the coproduct vanishes.
∎

Proposition 7.4
.

For
k\geq 1
, the assignment
\varphi\mapsto\tau_{\varphi}
is a group homomorphism
J^{A}(k)\to\text{Hom}_{A}(K_{k+1},H)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in J^{A}(k)
be two automorphisms of
\Gamma
. Then

\tau_{\varphi\psi}=T-(\varphi\psi)^{*}(T)=T-\psi^{*}\varphi^{*}(T)=T-\psi^{*}(T)+\psi^{*}(T-\varphi^{*}(T)).

But since
T-\varphi^{*}(T)
has weight
1
, it is fixed by
\psi^{*}
and we get
\tau_{\varphi\psi}=\tau_{\psi}+\tau_{\varphi}
.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

The Johnson homomorphism
J^{A}(k)\to\text{Hom}_{A}(K_{k+1},\mathrm{H})
is
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
-equivariant, where the action on
J^{A}(k)
is by conjugation.

Proof.

For any
\chi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
,

\tau_{\chi\varphi\chi^{-1}}(T)=T-(\chi^{-1})^{*}\varphi^{*}\chi^{*}(T)=(\chi^{-1})^{*}(\chi^{*}(T)-\varphi^{*}(\chi^{*}(T)))=(\chi^{-1})^{*}\tau_{\varphi}(\chi^{*}(T))

which is exactly the action of
\chi
on
\text{Hom}_{A}(K_{k+1},\mathrm{H})
.
∎

Proposition 7.6
.

The kernel of the Johnson homomorphism
J^{A}(k)\to\text{Hom}_{A}(K_{k+1},\mathrm{H})
contains
J^{A}(k+1)
. When
\Gamma
is finitely generated and
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
is
A
-torsion free (e.g. when
A
is a field), this is the entire kernel.

Proof.

Any
\varphi\in J^{A}(k+1)
stabilizes all of
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)_{\leq k+1}
and thus
\tau_{\varphi}=0
. Conversely, if
\varphi\in J^{A}(k)
has
\tau_{\varphi}=0
then
T=\varphi^{*}(T)
for all
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)_{\leq k}
. We claim that, for
\Gamma
finitely generated with
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
is
A
-torsion-free,
\varphi
fixes all of
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
.

Indeed, since
\Gamma
is finitely generated, the
A
-module
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
is also finitely generated and we assume it to be torsion-free, thus it is in fact free. If
x\neq\varphi_{*}(x)\in A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
, then there exists a functional
f\colon A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}\to A
such that
f(x-\varphi(x))\neq 0
. But, by completeness of letter-braiding, there exists
T\in\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;A)_{\leq k}
such that
\langle T,\bullet\rangle=f
. Now,

f(x)=\langle T,x\rangle=\langle\varphi^{*}(T),x\rangle=\langle T,\varphi_{*}(x)\rangle=f(\varphi_{*}(x))

contradicting our choice of
f
. It therefore must be that
\varphi_{*}=Id
on
A[\Gamma]/I^{k+1}
.
∎

7.3.
Massey products of finite groups

For finite groups, our theory leads to the following fact about Massey products in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(-;\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group, and
p
any prime number such that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq 0
. Then
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq 0
and nontivial Massey products
\mu_{k}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\otimes k}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})
exist.

Quantitatively, denote
b_{1}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})
, if
k\geq 2
is the length shortest nontrivial Massey product

0\neq\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\rangle\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p}),

which is necessarily well-defined with no indeterminacy, then

(21)

k<\begin{cases}|\Gamma|+1&b_{1}=1\\
\log_{b_{1}}|\Gamma|+2&b_{1}>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\Gamma]
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space of dimension
|\Gamma|
, and the letter-braiding invariants
\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})
inject into its linear dual, they must also have dimension
\leq|\Gamma|
. Now, suppose
k\geq 2
is the smallest length of a nontrivial Massey product in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then by the weight spectral sequence,

\mathrm{gr}~\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})_{\leq k-1}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\mathrm{H}^{1}\oplus(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\otimes 2}\oplus\ldots\oplus(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\otimes k-1},

so in particular
|\Gamma|\geq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{0}_{Bar}(\Gamma;\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq 1+b_{1}+b_{1}^{2}+\ldots+b_{1}^{k-1}
. The claimed inequalities for

follow.
∎

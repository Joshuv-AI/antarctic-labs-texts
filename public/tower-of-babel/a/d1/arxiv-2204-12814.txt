Bounds for Synchronizing Markov Decision Processes
Laurent Doyen, Marie van den Bogaard

Abstract
We consider Markov decision processes with synchronizing objectives, which require that a probability mass of 1 − ε 1-\varepsilon accumulates in a designated set of target states, either once, always, infinitely often, or always from some point on, where ε = 0 \varepsilon=0 for sure synchronizing, and ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 for almost-sure and limit-sure synchronizing. We introduce two new qualitative modes of synchronizing, where the probability mass should be either positive , or bounded away from 0 0 . They can be viewed as dual synchronizing objectives. We present algorithms and tight complexity results for the problem of deciding if a Markov decision process is positive, or bounded synchronizing, and we provide explicit bounds on ε \varepsilon in all synchronizing modes. In particular, we show that deciding positive and bounded synchronizing always from some point on, is coNP-complete.

Introduction

Markov decision processes (MDP) are finite-state probabilistic systems with
controllable (non-deterministic) choices.
They play a central role
in several application domains for practical purpose
[
15
,
20
]
,
and in theoretical computer science as a basic model
for the analysis of stochastic transition systems
[
2
,
11
]
.

In the traditional state-based semantics,
we consider the
sequences of states
that form a path in the underlying graph of the MDP.
When a control policy (or strategy) for the non-deterministic choices is fixed,
we obtain a purely stochastic process that induces a probability measure
over sets of paths
[
2
,
9
]
.

In the more recent distribution-based semantics,
the outcome of a stochastic process is a sequence of distributions
over states
[
3
,
19
]
. This alternative semantics has received
some attention recently for theoretical analysis of probabilistic bisimulation
[
17
]

and is adequate to describe large populations of agents
[
14
,
10
]
with applications
in system biology
[
19
,
1
]
.
The behaviour of an agent is modeled as an MDP with some state space
Q
,
and a large population of identical agents is described by a (continuous)
distribution
d:Q\to[0,1]
that gives the fraction
d(q)
of agents in the
population that are in each state
q\in Q
.
The control problem is to construct a strategy for the agents
that guarantees a specified global outcome of the agents,
defined in terms of
sequences of distributions
. Specifications of interest
include safety objectives
[
1
]
and synchronization objectives
[
14
]
.
A distribution is
p
-synchronized in a set
T
of states if
it assigns to the states in
T
a mass of probability at least
p
.
Synchronization objectives require
that
p
-synchronized distributions occur in the outcome sequence,
either, at some position (eventually), at all positions (always),
infinitely often (weakly), or always from some point on (strongly),
where synchronization is sure winning for
p=1
, and almost-sure
or limit-sure winning for
p
arbitrarily close to
1

[
14
]
.

Consider eventually synchronizing as an illustration.
Formally, denoting by
d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)
the probability
mass in a set
T
under strategy
\sigma
at position
i

in a given MDP, the three winning modes for eventually synchronizing
correspond to the following three possible orders of the quantifiers:

•

\forall\varepsilon>0\cdot\exists\sigma\cdot\exists i:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)\geq 1-\varepsilon
,
for limit-sure winning,

•

\exists\sigma\cdot\forall\varepsilon>0\cdot\exists i:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)\geq 1-\varepsilon
,
for almost-sure winning,

•

\exists\sigma\cdot\exists i\cdot\forall\varepsilon>0:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)\geq 1-\varepsilon
,
for sure winning.

Note that the formula
\forall\varepsilon>0:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)\geq 1-\varepsilon

is equivalent to
d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)=1
in the case of sure winning.
Defining the value of a strategy
\sigma
as

\mathrm{val}(\sigma)=\sup_{i}d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)
,
the question for limit-sure winning is analogous to the
cutpoint isolation problem for value
1
, i.e. whether
the value
1
can be approached arbitrarily closely
[
21
,
4
]
.
Previous work
[
14
]
shows that the above three questions are PSPACE-complete,
and presents a construction of the (existentially quantified) strategy
\sigma

when one exists.

In this paper, we consider dual synchronization objectives obtained
either by taking the negation of the synchronization objectives, or
by replacing the existential quantifier on strategies by a universal quantifier.

1. Taking the negation corresponds to the control player having no
strategy to satisfy the synchronization objective. In that case, we
show that a more precise information can be derived, namely bounds
on the value of
\varepsilon
, which is existentially quantified,
and we construct explicit values for the four synchronizing modes.
These values give bounds on the isolation distance of the value
1
.
For instance, the negation of limit-sure eventually synchronizing in
T
is
given by the formula:

\exists\varepsilon>0\cdot\forall\sigma\cdot\forall i:d^{\sigma}_{i}(T)\leq 1-\varepsilon.

We show that the statement holds for a value
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{e}(n,\alpha,\alpha_{0})

that depends on the number
n
of states of the MDP,
the smallest positive probability
\alpha
in the transitions of the MDP,
and the smallest positive probability
\alpha_{0}
in the initial distribution
d_{0}

(see Theorem
3.1
).
The most interesting case is when limit-sure weakly synchronizing does not hold,
that is:

\exists\varepsilon>0\cdot\forall\sigma\cdot\exists N\cdot\forall i\geq N:d^{\sigma}_{i}(T)\leq 1-\varepsilon.

Given the value
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{w}
that satisfies this condition (see Theorem
4.1
),
the value of
N
can be arbitrarily large (depending on the
strategy
\sigma
). Nevertheless, we can effectively construct a constant
N_{w}

such that, for all strategies
\sigma
, in the sequence
(d^{\sigma}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

there are at most
N_{w}
distributions that are
(1-\varepsilon_{w})
-synchronized in
T
.

2. Replacing the existential strategy quantifier by a universal quantifier
corresponds to an adversarial MDP where all strategies need to satisfy the requirement,
or after taking the negation, to the existence of a strategy that violates
a dual of the synchronizing requirement.
Note that there is no more alternation of quantifiers on
\varepsilon
and on
\sigma

(
\forall\varepsilon\cdot\forall\sigma
is the same as

\forall\sigma\cdot\forall\varepsilon
), which gives rise to only two
new winning modes in existential form:

•

\exists\sigma\cdot\exists\varepsilon>0\cdot\forall i:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)\geq\varepsilon
,
that we call
bounded
winning,

•

\exists\sigma\cdot\forall i\cdot\exists\varepsilon>0:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)\geq\varepsilon
,
that we call
positive
winning (since this is equivalent to

\exists\sigma\cdot\forall i:d_{i}^{\sigma}(T)>0
).

We establish the complexity of deciding bounded and positive winning

Adversarial MDPs are a special case of two-player stochastic games
[
9
]

in which only the second player (the adversary of the first player)
is non-trivial. The results of this paper will be useful for the
analysis of adversarial MDPs obtained from a game
by fixing a strategy of the first player.
The complexity results are summarized in Table
2
.
For positive winning, memoryless winning strategies exist (playing all
actions uniformly at random is sufficient), and the problem can be solved
by graph-theoretic techniques on Markov chains.
For bounded winning, the most challenging case is strongly synchronizing,
where we show that a simple form of strategy with memory is winning,
and that the decision problem is coNP-complete. We give
a structural characterization of bounded strongly synchronizing MDPs,
and show that it can be decided in coNP.
Note that the coNP upper bound is not obtained by guessing a strategy,
since the coNP lower bound holds in the case of Markov chains where
strategies play no role.

Definitions

A
probability distribution
over a finite set
Q
is a
function
d:Q\to[0,1]
such that
\sum_{q\in Q}d(q)=1
.
The
support
of
d
is the set
{\sf Supp}(d)=\{q\in Q\mid d(q)>0\}
.
We denote by
{\cal D}(Q)
the set of all probability distributions over
Q
.
Given a set
T\subseteq Q
, let
d(T)=\sum_{s\in T}d(s)
.

A
Markov decision process
(MDP) is a tuple
{\cal M}=\langle Q,{\sf A},\delta\rangle

where
Q
is a finite set of states,
{\sf A}
is a finite set of labels called actions,
and
\delta:Q\times{\sf A}\to{\cal D}(Q)
is a probabilistic transition function.
A
Markov chain
is an MDP with singleton action set
\lvert{\sf A}\rvert=1
.
Given a state
q\in Q
and an action
a\in{\sf A}
, the successor state of
q

under action
a
is
q^{\prime}
with probability
\delta(q,a)(q^{\prime})
.

Given
X,Y\subseteq Q
, let

{\sf APre}(Y,X)=\{q\in Q\mid\exists a\in{\sf A}:{\sf Supp}(\delta(q,a))\subseteq Y\land{\sf Supp}(\delta(q,a))\cap X\neq\varnothing\},

be the set of states from which there is an action to ensure that all successor
states are in
Y
and that with positive probability the successor state is in
X
,
and for
X=Q
, let

{\sf Pre}(Y)={\sf APre}(Y,Q)=\{q\in Q\mid\exists a\in{\sf A}:{\sf Supp}(\delta(q,a))\subseteq Y\}

be the set of states from which there is an action to ensure (with probability
1
)
that the successor state is in
Y
.
For
k>0
, let
{\sf Pre}^{k}(Y)={\sf Pre}({\sf Pre}^{k-1}(Y))
with
{\sf Pre}^{0}(Y)=Y
.
Note that the sequence
{\sf Pre}^{k}(Y)
of iterated predecessors is ultimately periodic,
precisely there exist
k_{1}<k_{2}\leq 2^{\lvert Q\rvert}
such that
{\sf Pre}^{k_{1}}(Y)={\sf Pre}^{k_{2}}(Y)
.

Strategies.

A (randomized)
strategy
in
{\cal M}
is a function
\sigma:(Q{\sf A})^{*}Q\to{\cal D}({\sf A})

that, given a finite sequence
\rho=q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}a_{1}\dots q_{k}
,
chooses the next action
a_{k}
with probability
\sigma(\rho)(a_{k})
.
We write
\sigma_{1}\subseteq\sigma_{2}
if
{\sf Supp}(\sigma_{1}(\rho))\subseteq{\sf Supp}(\sigma_{2}(\rho))

for all
\rho\in(Q{\sf A})^{*}Q
.
A strategy
\sigma
is
pure
if for all
\rho\in(Q{\sf A})^{*}Q
,
there exists an action
a\in{\sf A}
such that
\sigma(\rho)(a)=1
.
In all problems considered in this paper, it is known that pure strategies
are sufficient
[
14
]
. However, the bounds we provide in case
there is no winning strategy hold for all strategies, pure or randomized.

Given an initial distribution
d_{0}\in{\cal D}(Q)
and a strategy
\sigma
in
{\cal M}
,
the probability of a finite sequence
\rho=q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}a_{1}\dots q_{k}
is
defined by

\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\rho)=d_{0}(q_{0})\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\sigma(q_{0}a_{0}\dots q_{j})(a_{j})\cdot\delta(q_{j},a_{j})(q_{j+1}).

For an initial distribution
d_{0}
such that
d_{0}(q_{0})=1
,
we sometimes write
\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\cdot)
and say that

q_{0}
is the initial state.
We say that
\rho
is
compatible
with
\sigma
and
d_{0}
if

\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\rho)>0
. By extension, an infinite sequence

\pi\in(Q{\sf A})^{\omega}
is compatible with
\sigma
and
d_{0}
if
all prefixes of
\pi
that end in a state are compatible.
It is standard to extend (in a unique way)
\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}
over Borel sets of infinite
paths in
(Q{\sf A})^{\omega}
(called events), by assigning probability
\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\rho)

to the basic cylinder set containing all infinite paths with prefix
\rho

[
23
,
2
]
.
Given a set
T\subseteq Q
of target states, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we define the following events (sometimes called objectives):

•

\Box T=\{q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}\dots\in(Q{\sf A})^{\omega}\mid\forall i:q_{i}\in T\}

the safety event of staying in
T
;

•

\Diamond T=\{q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}\dots\in(Q{\sf A})^{\omega}\mid\exists i:q_{i}\in T\}

the event of reaching
T
;

•

\Diamond^{k}\,T=\{q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}\dots\in(Q{\sf A})^{\omega}\mid q_{k}\in T\}
the event
of reaching
T
after exactly
k
steps;

A distribution
d_{0}
is
almost-sure winning
for an event
\Omega

if there exists a strategy
\sigma
such that
\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\Omega)=1
,
and
limit-sure winning
if
\sup_{\sigma}\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\Omega)=1
,
that is the event
\Omega
can be realized with probability arbitrarily close to
1
.
Finally
d_{0}
is
sure winning
for
\Omega
if there exists
a strategy
\sigma
such that all paths compatible with
\sigma
and
d_{0}

belong to
\Omega
.

Safety and reachability events are dual, in the sense that
\Diamond T

and
\Box(Q\setminus T)
form a partition of
(Q{\sf A})^{\omega}
.
It is known for safety objectives
\Box T
that the three
winning regions (sure, almost-sure winning, and limit-sure winning)
coincide in MDPs, and for reachability objectives
\Diamond T
,
almost-sure and limit-sure winning coincide
[
12
]
.
It follows that if the negation of almost-sure reachability holds, that is

\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\Diamond T)<1
for all strategies
\sigma
,
then equivalently
\inf_{\sigma}\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\Box(Q\setminus T))>0
(note the strict inequality),
the probability mass that remains always outside
T
can be bounded.
An explicit bound can be obtained from the classical characterization
of the winning region for almost-sure reachability
[
11
]
.

Lemma 1

If a distribution
d_{0}
is not almost-sure winning for a reachability
objective
\Diamond T
in an MDP
{\cal M}
, then for all strategies
\sigma
,
for all
i\geq 0
,
we have

Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\Diamond^{i}\,T)\leq 1-\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{n}

where
n=\lvert Q\rvert
is the number of states and

\alpha
the smallest positive probability in
{\cal M}
,
and
\alpha_{0}=\min\{d_{0}(q)\mid q\in{\sf Supp}(d_{0})\}
is the smallest
positive probability in the initial distribution
d_{0}
.

Proof

The set of almost-sure winning states for the reachability objective

\Diamond T
can be computed
by graph-theoretic algorithms
[
11
]
, and can be expressed succinctly
by the fixpoint
\mu
-calculus formula

\varphi_{{\sf AS}}=\nu Y.\mu X.~\!T\cup{\sf APre}(Y,X)
.
We briefly recall the interpretation of
\mu
-calculus formulas
[
6
,
7
]

based on Knaster-Tarski theorem.
Given a monotonic function
\psi:2^{Q}\to 2^{Q}

(i.e., such that
X\subseteq Y
implies
\psi(X)\subseteq\psi(Y)
),
the expression
\nu Y.\psi(Y)
is the (unique) greatest fixpoint of
\psi
,
which can be computed as the limit of the sequence
(Y_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

Y_{0}=Q
, and
Y_{i}=\psi(Y_{i-1})
for all
i\geq 1
.
Dually, the expression
\mu X.~\psi(X)
is the (unique) least fixpoint of
\psi
,
and the limit of the sequence
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

X_{0}=\varnothing
, and
X_{i}=\psi(X_{i-1})
for all
i\geq 1
.
If
\lvert Q\rvert=n
, then it is not difficult to see that the
limit of those sequences is reached after at most
n
iterations,

X_{n}=X_{n+1}
and
Y_{n}=Y_{n+1}
.

Intuitively, the formula
\varphi_{{\sf AS}}
computes the largest set
S
of states
such that every state
q\in S
has a strategy to ensure reaching
T
with
positive probability, while at the same time staying in
S
with probability
1
.
It follows that
S
is the set of all states from which there exists a strategy
to reach
T
with probability
1
.

Now consider the states
q_{0}\not\in\varphi_{{\sf AS}}
that are not almost-sure
winning, and let the rank of
q_{0}
be the least integer
i
such that

q_{0}\in Y_{i}
and
q_{0}\not\in Y_{i+1}
.
Let
\Omega_{i}=Y_{i}\setminus Y_{i+1}
for all
i\geq 0
be the set of states of rank
i
, and for
\mbox{$\sim$}\!\in\!\{<,\leq\}

let
\Omega_{\sim i}=\bigcup_{j\sim i}\Omega_{j}
.
It is easy to show by induction that
Q\setminus Y_{i}=\Omega_{<i}
since
\Omega_{<i}=(Y_{0}\setminus Y_{1})\cup(Y_{1}\setminus Y_{2})\cup\dots\cup(Y_{i-1}\setminus Y_{i})
and
Y_{0}=Q
. Moreover since
T\subseteq Y_{i}

we have
\Omega_{i}\cap T=\varnothing
.

For
q_{0}\in\Omega_{i}
, we show that

\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box\Omega_{i}\cup\Diamond\Omega_{<i})\geq\alpha
for all
strategies
\sigma
(where
\alpha
is the smallest positive probability in the transitions of the MDP).
Since
q_{0}\not\in Y_{i+1}=\mu X.~T\cup{\sf APre}(Y_{i},X)
, we have

q_{0}\not\in{\sf APre}(Y_{i},Y_{i+1})
.
Then for all actions
a\in{\sf A}
we have:

\text{either }{\sf Supp}(\delta(q_{0},a))\not\subseteq Y_{i},\text{ or }{\sf Supp}(\delta(q_{0},a))\cap Y_{i+1}=\varnothing,

that is

\text{either }{\sf Supp}(\delta(q_{0},a))\cap\Omega_{<i}\neq\varnothing,\text{ or }{\sf Supp}(\delta(q_{0},a))\subseteq\Omega_{\leq i}.

It follows in both cases
that if not all successors of
q_{0}
on action
a
are in
\Omega_{i}
,
then at least one of them is in
\Omega_{<i}
, which entails that

\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box\Omega_{i}\cup\Diamond\Omega_{<i})\geq\alpha
.
For
i=0
, since
\Omega_{<0}=\varnothing
, we have

\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box\Omega_{0})=1
and thus
\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box(Q\setminus T))=1

for all
q_{0}\in\Omega_{0}
and all strategies
\sigma
. Inductively, if
\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box(Q\setminus T))\geq p_{i}
for all

q_{0}\in\Omega_{<i}
and all strategies
\sigma
, then
\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box(Q\setminus T))\geq\alpha\cdot p_{i}
for all
q_{0}\in\Omega_{i}

and all strategies
\sigma
.

It follows that if
q_{0}\not\in\varphi_{{\sf AS}}
is not almost-sure winning
for the reachability objective
\Diamond T
, then

q_{0}\in\Omega_{<n}
and
\Pr^{\sigma}_{q_{0}}(\Box(Q\setminus T))\geq\alpha^{n}

for all strategies
\sigma
, which entails the result of the lemma. ∎

In Lemma
1
it is crucial to notice that the bound

\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{n}
is independent of the number
i
of steps.

Synchronizing objectives.

We consider MDPs as generators of sequences of probability distributions

]

Given an initial distribution

and a strategy
\sigma
in
{\cal M}
,
the sequence

Hence,

Informally, synchronizing objectives require that the probability of some set

in the sequence
({\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
either always, once, infinitely often, or always after some point
[

]
.
Given a target set

,

(b)

is

) for some

(c)

is
p
-synchronized (in

) for infinitely many
i
’s;

(d)

strongly

-synchronizing

is
p
-synchronized (in
T
) for all but finitely many
i
’s.

Given an initial distribution
d_{0}
,
we say that for the objective of {always, eventually, weakly, strongly} synchronizing
from

•

is
{always, eventually, weakly, strongly}

•

{always, eventually, weakly, strongly}

For

End-components.

Given a state
q\in Q
and a set
S\subseteq Q
,
let
{\sf A}_{S}(q)
be the set of all actions
a\in{\sf A}
such
that
{\sf Supp}(\delta(q,a))\subseteq S
.
A
closed
set in an MDP is a set
S\subseteq Q
such
that
{\sf A}_{S}(q)\neq\varnothing
for all
q\in S
.
A set

is an

[
12
,
2
]
if
(i)

is closed, and
(ii) the graph
(S,E_{S})
is strongly connected
where
E_{S}=\{(q,q^{\prime})\in S\times S\mid\delta(q,a)(q^{\prime})>0\text{ for some }a\in{\sf A}_{S}(q)\}

denote the set of edges given the actions.
In the sequel, end-components should be considered
maximal
, that is
such that no strict superset is an end-component.
We denote by
{\cal E}
the union of all end-components,
and for
q\in{\cal E}
, we denote by
{\cal E}(q)
the maximal end-component
containing
q
.
A fundamental property of end-components is that under arbitrary strategies,
with probability
1
the set of states visited infinitely often along a
path is an end-component.

Lemma 2
(
[
11
,
12
]
)

Let
{\cal M}
be an MDP. For all strategies
\sigma
, we have
\liminf_{i\to\infty}{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}({\cal E})=1
.

Tracking counter in MDPs.

It will be useful to track the number of steps (modulo a given number

in MDPs.
Given a number

{\cal M}\times[r]=\langle Q_{r},{\sf A},\delta_{r}\rangle

\delta_{r}(\langle q,i\rangle,a)(\langle q^{\prime},j\rangle)=\begin{cases}\delta(q,a)(q^{\prime})&\text{ if }j=i-1\!\!\mod r,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

For a distribution

and
0\leq t<r
,
we denote by

the distribution defined, for all
q\in Q
,
by

, and

Given a finite sequence

in

,
and

\rho^{\prime}=\langle q_{0},k_{0}\rangle a_{0}\langle q_{1},k_{1}\rangle a_{1}\dots\langle q_{n},k_{n}\rangle

Since the sequence

and the paths in

In the sequel, we freely omit to apply and mention this bijection.
In particular, we often consider that a strategy

3
Eventually synchronizing

In the rest of this paper, fix an MDP
{\cal M}=\langle Q,{\sf A},\delta\rangle

and let
n=\lvert Q\rvert
be the size of
{\cal M}
, and let
\alpha
be the
smallest positive probability in the transitions of
{\cal M}
.
We first consider eventually synchronizing, that is synchronization
must happen once.

3.1
Sure winning

We recall the following characterization of the sure-winning region for
eventually synchronizing
[
14
, Lemma 7]
:

d\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)

if and only if there exists
k\geq 0
such that
{\sf Supp}(d)\subseteq{\sf Pre}^{k}(T)
.
Intuitively, from all states in
{\sf Pre}^{i}(T)
, there exists an action to ensure
that the successor is in
{\sf Pre}^{i-1}(T)
(for all
i>0
),
and therefore there exists a strategy to ensure that the probability mass in

{\sf Pre}^{k}(T)
reaches
T
in exactly
k
steps.
Now, if
q_{0}\not\in{\sf Pre}^{k}(T)
, then for all sequences of actions
a_{0},\dots,a_{k-1}

there is a path
q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}a_{1}\dots q_{k}
of length
k
that ends in
q_{k}\in Q\setminus T
.
It is easy to derive the following result from this characterization.

Lemma 3

If
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
is not sure eventually synchronizing in
T
,
then for all strategies
\sigma
, for all
i\geq 0
, we have:

{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}(T)\leq 1-\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i}

where
\alpha_{0}
is the
smallest positive probability in
d_{0}
.

Note that the bound
1-\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i}
tends to
1
as
i\to\infty
,
which is unavoidable since MDPs that are not sure eventually synchronizing
may be almost-sure eventually synchronizing
[
14
]
. The following variant of Lemma
3
will be useful
in the sequel.

Remark 1

If
\{q_{0}\}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
and we take

\alpha^{\prime}_{0}=d_{0}(q_{0})
, then from the initial distribution
d_{0}
we have,
for all strategies
\sigma
, for all
i\geq 0
:

{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}(T)\leq 1-\alpha^{\prime}_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i}.

3.2
Limit-sure winning

If the MDP
{\cal M}
is not limit-sure winning for eventually synchronizing
in

from

We give an explicit bound

3.3
Almost-sure winning

A simple argument shows that the almost-sure winning region
for eventually synchronizing consists of the union of the
sure winning region for eventually synchronizing and
the almost-sure winning region for weakly synchronizing
[
22
, Section 5.1.2]
,
that is
\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)\cup\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{weakly}}(T)
.

It follows that if
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
, then both

d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
and
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{weakly}}(T)
,
and we can use both the results of
Lemma
3
and Theorem
4.1
.

4
Weakly synchronizing

We now consider weakly synchronizing, which intuitively requires that
synchronization happens infinitely often.

4.1
Sure winning

We recall the following characterization of the sure-winning region for
weakly synchronizing
[
14
, Lemma 18]
:
for all distributions
d_{0}\in{\cal D}(Q)
, we have
d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{weakly}}(T)

if and only if there exists a set
S\subseteq T
such that

{\sf Supp}(d)\subseteq{\sf Pre}^{k}(S)
for some
k\geq 0
,
and
S\subseteq{\sf Pre}^{r}(S)
for some

.

Lemma 4

If
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{weakly}}(T)
is not sure weakly synchronizing in
T
,
then for all strategies
\sigma
,
in the sequence
{\cal M}^{\sigma}
there are at most
2^{n}
distributions that
are
1
-synchronized in
T
, that is

{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}(T)=1

2^{n}
values of

Proof

Consider the support

(where
i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots
)
of the distributions in the sequence
{\cal M}^{\sigma}
that are

1
-synchronized in
T
. We show by contradiction,
that all
S_{j}
are distinct, which establishes the result
since they can take at most
2^{n}
different values.

If

for some

then it is easy to show that
{\cal M}
is sure-winning for eventually
synchronizing in

from

, and

is also

of the lemma. ∎

4.2
Limit-sure and almost-sure winning

The winning region for limit-sure and almost-sure weakly synchronizing
coincide
[
14
, Theorem 7]
. Therefore, in the sequel we treat
them interchangeably.
We recall the following characterization of almost-sure weakly
synchronizing.

Lemma 5
(
[
14
, Lemma 23,Theorem 7]
)

For all distributions
d_{0}
, the following equivalence holds:

d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{weakly}}(T)

if and only if there exists a set
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
such that:

d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{limit}}^{\mathit{event}}(T^{\prime})\ \text{ and }\ T^{\prime}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{limit}}^{\mathit{event}}({\sf Pre}(T^{\prime})).

The condition in Lemma
5
ensures that from
d_{0}

almost all the probability mass (namely
1-\varepsilon
for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
)
can be injected in a set
T^{\prime}
of target

0
or more steps, and that from
T^{\prime}
almost all the probability
mass can be injected in
{\sf Pre}(T^{\prime})
, thus also in
T^{\prime}
(but, in at least
1

Intuitively, by successively halving the value of

construct a strategy that ensures almost all the
probability mass loops through
T^{\prime}
,
thus a limit-sure weakly synchronizing strategy (which is equivalent
to the existence of an almost-sure weakly synchronizing strategy).

If

we use Lemma

is limit-sure eventually synchronizing in

,
then

is not limit-sure eventually synchronizing in

(i.e.,

). This implies that
a bounded number of distributions in the sequence

can be

-synchronized in

(for sufficiently small

).

5
Always and Strongly Synchronizing

We summarize the key insights for always and strongly synchronizing (i.e.,
synchronizing always from some point on),
and give the bounds in Theorem
5.1
.

Always synchronizing.

For always synchronizing, all winning modes coincide
\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{limit}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)
and it is easy to see that sure winning
for always synchronizing is equivalent to sure winning for the safety objective

\Box T

[
14
, Lemma 2]
.
A standard analysis of MDPs shows that if a sure safety objective
\Box T
is violated,
then for all
pure
strategies
\sigma
there exists a path compatible with

\sigma
from some state in the support of the initial distribution
to a state in
Q\setminus T
, of length at most
\lvert Q\rvert
and thus of probability
at least
\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{n}
where
\alpha_{0}
is the smallest positive
probability in the initial distribution, and
\alpha
is the smallest positive
probability in the transitions of
{\cal M}
. Under a
randomized
strategy
\sigma
,
a probability mass
\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{n}
also reaches
Q\setminus T

within
n
steps, but possibly along paths of different lengths.
By a pigeonhole principle, at some length (smaller than
n
)
the probability mass must be at least
\frac{\alpha_{0}}{n}\cdot\alpha^{n}
in
Q\setminus T
.

Strongly synchronizing.

For strongly synchronizing, the almost-sure and limit-sure winning modes
coincide
[
14
, Corollary 28]
. On the other hand, the sure and almost-sure
winning modes are equivalent to, respectively, sure, and almost-sure winning
for the reachability objective
\Diamond S

where
S
is the sure-winning region for the safety objective
\Box T

[
14
, Lemma 27]
.
It follows that:

•

if
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{sure}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)

is not sure winning for strongly synchronizing in
T
,
then for all
pure
strategies
\sigma

there exists an infinite path
q_{0}a_{0}q_{1}a_{1}\dots

compatible with
\sigma

from some state
q_{0}
in the support of
d_{0}

that remains outside
S
, that is
q_{i}\in Q\setminus S

for all
i\geq 0
. The corresponding probability mass is at least

\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i}
, and hence for all
randomized
strategies

\sigma
, a probability mass at least
\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i}
is also
in
Q\setminus S
at step
i
(but not necessarily in a single state).

By definition of
S
, and using an argument similar to the case
of always synchronizing, for all
i\geq 0
,
there exists a length
\ell_{i}\leq n
such that
a probability mass at least
\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n}

is in
Q\setminus T
at step

.
We construct the indices
i_{0},i_{1},\dots
inductively as follows.
Let
i_{0}=\ell_{0}

and for all
j\geq 0
let
i_{j+1}=i_{j}+1+\ell_{i_{j}+1}
.
Then
{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i_{j}}(T)<1
is not
1
-synchronized in
T

(in fact
{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i_{j}}(T)\leq 1-\alpha_{0}\cdot\alpha^{i_{j-1}+n}
)
for all
j\geq 0
.
Note that
i_{0}\leq n
and
0<i_{j+1}-i_{j}\leq n
for all
j\geq 0
.

•

if
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{almost}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)
, then

d_{0}
is not almost-sure winning for the reachability objective
\Diamond S
,
and by Lemma
1

for all (randomized) strategies
\sigma
and all
i\geq 0
,
there exists a state
q_{i}\in Q\setminus S
such that

\Pr^{\sigma}_{d_{0}}(\Diamond^{i}\{q_{i}\})\geq\alpha_{0}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n}
.
Since
q_{i}\not\in S
is not in the winning region for sure safety
\Box T
,
there is a length
\ell_{i}\leq n
as above,
such that the probability mass in
Q\setminus T
at length
i+\ell_{i}

is at least
\alpha_{0}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{2n}}{n^{2}}
.
For the indices
i_{0}=\ell_{0}
and
i_{j+1}=i_{j}+1+\ell_{i_{j}+1}

for all

, we get
{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i_{j}}(T)\leq 1-\alpha_{0}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{2n}}{n^{2}}
.

Theorem 5.1

Given an initial distribution
d_{0}
,
let
\alpha_{0}

and let

6
Adversarial Synchronizing Objectives

In an adversarial MDP the strategies are universally quantified,
which corresponds to satisfying an objective regardless
of the choice of strategies by an adversary.
Replacing
\exists\sigma
by
\forall\sigma
in the definition
of the three winning modes gives, after taking the negation
to get existentially quantified strategies,
the following new winning modes.

Given a set
T\subseteq Q
,
we say that a sequence
d_{0}d_{1}\dots
of probability distributions is:

•

positively
{always, eventually, weakly, strongly} winning
if
d_{i}(T)>0
for, respectively, all
i\geq 0
, some
i\geq 0
, infinitely many
i
’s,
all but finitely many
i
’s.

•

boundedly
{always, eventually, weakly, strongly} winning
if there exists
\varepsilon>0

such that
d_{i}(T)>\varepsilon
for, respectively, all
i\geq 0
, some
i\geq 0
, infinitely many
i
’s,
all but finitely many
i
’s.

For
\lambda\in\{always,event,weakly,strongly\}
,
we denote by
\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{\lambda}}(T)
(resp.,
\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{\lambda}}(T)
)
the set of initial distributions
d_{0}
from which
there exists a strategy
\sigma
such that
the sequence
{\cal M}^{\sigma}
is positively (resp., boundedly)
\lambda
-synchronizing in
T
,
and we say that
\sigma
is positively (resp., boundedly)
\lambda
-synchronizing in
T

from
d_{0}
.

Table
1
summarizes the new definitions.
Note that replacing the existential quantification on strategies
in boundedly winning mode by a supremum gives the same question,
since
\exists\sigma:f(\sigma)>0
is equivalent to
\sup_{\sigma}f(\sigma)>0
.
For the same reason, we have the identity
\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
.
It is easy to show that the definitions imply the identity

\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)\cap\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)
,
which we also obtain as a corollary of Lemma
6
below.

Remark 3

It immediately follows from the definitions that
for all synchronizing modes
\lambda\in\{always,event,weakly,strongly\}
,
and
\mu\in\{positive,bounded\}
:

•

\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{\mu}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)\subseteq\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{\mu}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)\subseteq\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{\mu}}^{\mathit{weakly}}(T)\subseteq\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{\mu}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
,

•

\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{\lambda}}(T)\subseteq\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{\lambda}}(T)
,

and moreover,

•

\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{event}}(T)
, and

•

\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)=\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)\cap\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)
.

It is easy to see that if there exists a strategy
\sigma
that
is positively
\lambda
-synchronizing in
T
, then the
strategy
\sigma_{{\sf u}}
that plays at every round all actions uniformly
at random is also positively
\lambda
-synchronizing in
T
,
because the condition
d_{i}(T)>0
is equivalent to

{\sf Supp}(d_{i})\cap T\neq\varnothing
, and because we have
\sigma\subseteq\sigma_{{\sf u}}
,
which implies that

{\sf Supp}({\cal M}_{i}^{\sigma})\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}_{i}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}})

for all
i\geq 0
.

Hence, in all four synchronization modes,
the question is equivalent to the same question in Markov chains
(obtained from the given MDP by fixing the strategy
\sigma_{{\sf u}}
)
which can be solved as follows.
Given a Markov chain, consider the underlying directed graph
\langle Q,E\rangle

where
(q,q^{\prime})\in E
if
\delta(q,a)(q^{\prime})>0
(where
{\sf A}=\{a\}
).
For positive eventually synchronizing, it suffices to find a state in
T

that is reachable in that graph, and for positive weakly synchronizing,
it suffices to find a state in
T
that is both reachable and can reach itself.
These questions are NL-complete.
For positive always and strongly synchronizing, the question is equivalent
to the model-checking problem for the formulas
G\exists T

and
FG\exists T
in the logic CTL+Sync, which are both
coNP-complete
[
8
, Lemma 2 & Section 3]
.

For boundedly winning, we show that one strategy is good enough
in all four synchronization modes, like for positive winning.
The strategy plays like
\sigma_{{\sf u}}
for the first
2^{n}
rounds,
and then switches to a strategy
\sigma_{{\cal E}}
that,
in the states
q\in{\cal E}
, plays uniformly at random
all actions that stay in the end-component
{\cal E}(q)
of
q

(thus all actions in
{\sf A}_{{\cal E}(q)}
),
and in the transient states
q\not\in{\cal E}
, plays
all actions uniformly at random. We call this strategy
the
freezing
strategy. Intuitively we use
\sigma_{{\sf u}}

to scatter the probability mass in all end-components of the MDP,
and then
\sigma_{{\cal E}}
to maintain a bounded probability
in each end-component.

Lemma 6

Let
{\cal M}
be an MDP with
n
states and initial distribution
d_{0}
, and let
T
be a target set.
Consider the following conditions:

(1)

\forall i\geq 0:{\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i}(T)>0
(2)
\forall i\geq 2^{n}:{\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i}({\cal E}\cap T)>0

Then, the following equivalences hold:

(a)

d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{positive}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)
if and only if Condition
(1)
holds;

(b)

d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)
if and only if Condition
(2)
holds;

(c)

d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{always}}(T)
if and only if Conditions
(1)
and
(2)
hold;

Proof

Equivalence
(a)
follows from the definition of positive always synchronizing,
and from the fact that the uniform strategy
\sigma_{{\sf u}}
is sufficient for positive
winning.

We show Equivalence
(b)
as follows.
First, if Condition
(2)
does not hold, then

{\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i}({\cal E}\cap T)=0
for some
i\geq 2^{n}
,
and thus also for infinitely many
i
’s (since the sequence
{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i})

is ultimately periodic, after at most
2^{n}
steps).
For arbitrary strategy
\sigma
, we have
{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i})\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i})

for all
i\geq 0
, therefore
{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}({\cal E}\cap T)=0
for
infinitely many
i
’s.
By Lemma
2
, we have
\liminf_{i\to\infty}{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}({\cal E})=1

which entails that
\limsup{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}({\cal E}\setminus T)=1

and
\limsup{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}(Q\setminus T)=1
,
that is
\liminf{\cal M}^{\sigma}_{i}(T)=0
.
Since this holds for arbitrary strategy
\sigma
,
it follows that
d_{0}\not\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)
.

For the converse direction, assuming Condition
(2)
holds, we show that

d_{0}\in\langle\!\langle 1\rangle\!\rangle_{\mathit{bounded}}^{\mathit{strongly}}(T)
, witnessed by the freezing strategy
\sigma_{f}

(which plays like
\sigma_{{\sf u}}
for the first
2^{n}
rounds,
and then switches to the strategy
\sigma_{{\cal E}}
).

We show the key property that

{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i})\cap{\cal E}={\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{f}}_{i})\cap{\cal E}\text{ for all }i\geq 2^{n}.

Fix an arbitrary
i\geq 2^{n}
and let
p
be a period of the sequence

{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}})
such that
i-p\leq 2^{n}
and

{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i})={\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i-p})
.
Consider the Markov chain
{\cal M}_{{\cal E}}
obtained by
fixing the strategy
\sigma_{{\cal E}}
in
{\cal M}
.
From the basic theory of Markov chains, each
end-component
C
in
{\cal M}
is a recurrent class in
{\cal M}_{{\cal E}}
.
For each
i\geq 2^{n}
, either all or none of the states in a
periodic class of
C
are in the support of
{\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i}
.

To show the key property, first consider a state
q\in{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i})\cap{\cal E}

for
i\geq 2^{n}
,
and show that
q\in{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{f}}_{i})\cap{\cal E}
.
Let
S
be the periodic class of
{\cal E}(q)
containing
q
(in
{\cal M}_{{\cal E}}
), and thus

S\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i})\cap{\cal E},\text{ and thus }S\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{{\sf u}}}_{i-p})\cap{\cal E}.

Since
\sigma_{{\sf u}}
and
\sigma_{f}
coincide on the first
2^{n}
rounds,
we have

S\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{f}}_{i-p})\cap{\cal E}.

Now consider the strategy
\sigma_{{\cal E}}
and an initial distribution
with support
S
, and denote by
S+j
the support of the probability
distribution after playing
\sigma_{{\cal E}}
for
j
steps. Then,
since
\sigma_{{\cal E}}\subseteq\sigma_{f}\subseteq\sigma_{u}
,

S+p\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{f}}_{i})\cap{\cal E},\text{ and }S+p\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{u}}_{i})\cap{\cal E}.

We can repeat the same argument with
S+p
instead of
S
,
and show by induction that
S+j\cdot p\subseteq{\sf Supp}({\cal M}^{\sigma_{f}}_{i})\cap{\cal E}

for all

. In particular, by taking

the period of the

q
, we get

\sigma_{f}\subseteq\sigma_{{\sf u}}
).

From the theory of Markov chains, in every end-component state
q\in{\cal E}
,
the positive probability mass is bounded away from
0
in
{\cal M}^{{\cal E}}
,
that is there exists a bound
\varepsilon>0
such that
for all

and thus

.

(c)
follows the same steps as above to

and
(2)
imply

for the first

.
The converse direction immediately follows from the first part

and

. ∎

We extract explicit bounds from the proof of Lemma

All end-components are reached within a most

\varepsilon_{a}
in

.

The conditions

and
(2)

(1)
we guess an index

(in

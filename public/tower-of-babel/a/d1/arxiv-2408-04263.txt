Poincaré’s lemma for formal manifolds
Fulin Chen, Binyong Sun, Chuyun Wang

Abstract
. This is a paper in a series that studies smooth relative Lie algebra homologies and cohomologies based on the theory of formal manifolds and formal Lie groups. In two previous papers, we develop the basic theory of formal manifolds, including generalizations of vector-valued distributions and generalized functions on smooth manifolds to the setting of formal manifolds. In this paper, we establish Poincaré’s lemma for de Rham complexes with coefficients in formal functions, formal generalized functions, compactly supported formal densities, or compactly supported formal distributions.

1.
Introduction

This is a sequel to
[
CSW1
]
and
[
CSW2
]
in a series to study the theory of formal manifolds and formal Lie groups, with the ultimate goal of establishing a smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory.

1.1.
The motivation

Motivated by recent developments in the theories of Lie group representations and automorphic forms, there is a growing interest in establishing a (co)homology theory for smooth representations of general Lie pairs. Here, by a Lie pair, we mean a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra and a (not necessarily compact) Lie group, together with additional structures that satisfy the same compatibility conditions as that of the usual pair
(\mathfrak{g},K)
for a reductive Lie group
G
(see
[
KV
, (1.64)]
for example), where
\mathfrak{g}
is the complexified Lie algebra of
G
and
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. We call this (co)homology theory the
smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory, which unifies the smooth (co)homology theory for Lie groups (see
[
HM
]
and
[
BW
]
) with the algebraic theory of relative Lie algebra (co)homologies (see
[
KV
]
).

In the 1970s, Zuckerman proposed a homological construction of Harish-Chandra modules on certain relative Lie algebra (co)homology spaces (see
[
KV
]
), which played an important role in the representation theory of Lie groups.
We expect that there is an analytic analog of Zuckerman’s functor based on the smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory so that it produces interesting smooth
representations of reductive Lie groups through cohomological induction in a similar way.

Starting with
[
CSW1
]
, we formulate and study a notion of what we call formal manifolds, inspired by the notion of formal schemes in algebraic geometry.
In a forthcoming paper, we will further explore formal
Lie groups, defined as group objects in the category of formal manifolds. Particularly, we
will prove therein that there is an equivalence of categories between the category of formal
Lie groups and that of general Lie pairs.
Then we plan to establish the smooth relative Lie algebra (co)homology theory by studying (co)homologies of “suitable” representations for formal Lie groups.

In the smooth (co)homology theory of Lie groups, the standard (projective or injective) resolutions of a smooth representation
can be constructed by using various functions spaces(see
[
HM
,
BW
,
KS
]
).
In
[
CSW2
]
we introduce and study various functions spaces on formal manifolds, including generalizations of
vector-valued generalized functions and vector-valued distributions on smooth
manifolds to the setting of formal manifolds.
As a stone step towards the (co)homologies of representations for formal Lie groups, we need to construct standard resolutions of the trivial representation. To achieve this goal, an important work is to define the de Rham complexes for formal manifolds with coefficients in various function spaces and prove the corresponding Poincaré’s lemma. This is the main motivation of the present paper.

In this paper, by an LCS,

2.1.
The sheaves
\mathcal{D}\mathrm{er}({\mathcal{O}})

{\mathcal{O}}_{E}

In this subsection, we recall the notion of derivations of

(see
[
CSW1

For every

\mathrm{Der}(A):=\{X\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(A,A)\,:\,X(a_{1}a_{2})=X(a_{1})a_{2}+a_{1}X(a_{2})\ \text{for all}\ a_{1},a_{2}\in A\}.

By a derivation of

, we mean
a family

D=\{(D_{U}:{\mathcal{O}}(U)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(U))\in\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}}(U))\}_{U\textrm{ is an open subset of }M}

Write
\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}})

By

With the obvious restriction maps, the assignment

is naturally a sheaf of

{\mathcal{O}}(U)\times\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}}|_{U})\rightarrow\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}}|_{U}),\quad\quad(f,D)\mapsto f\circ D,

where

is an open subset of

De Rham complexes I

Let

with the point-wise convergence topology. Namely, a net

in

if and only if

converges to

for all

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

and let

be an open subset of

. Then the restriction map

\Omega^{r}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E)\rightarrow\Omega^{r}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(U;E)

is continuous.

Proof.

The case when

, Lemma 6.13]
. Now we assume that

.
For each

, choose open neighborhoods

Take a formal function

on

such that (see

, Corollary 2.4]
)

Lemma 2.5

Let

The case when

, Lemma 6.14]

. Lemma

implies that the map (

) is continuous,

) is injective and has closed image.

As usual, for

, we define the wedge product

(2.4)

\wedge:\ \Omega^{r}_{\mathcal{O}}(M)\times\Omega^{s}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E)\rightarrow\Omega^{r+s}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E),\quad(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\mapsto\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}

by setting

\displaystyle(\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2})(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{r+s})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r!s!}\sum_{\sigma}\mathrm{sign}(\sigma)\omega_{1}(X_{\sigma(1)},X_{\sigma(2)},\dots,X_{\sigma(r)})\cdot\omega_{2}(X_{\sigma(r+1)},X_{\sigma(r+2)},\dots,X_{\sigma(r+s)}),

where
(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{r+s})\in\mathrm{Der}({\mathcal{O}})^{r+s}
, and

\sigma
runs over all the permutations of
\{1,2,\dots,r+s\}
.
By using the fact that
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is a topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra (see
[

, Proposition 4.8]

{\mathcal{O}}(M)\times{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(M),\quad(f,g)\rightarrow fg

with the direct sum topology.
Then

is naturally an

-module such that the module structure map

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Omega_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M)\times\Omega_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E)&\rightarrow&\Omega_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E),\\
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})&\mapsto&\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}\end{array}

is bilinear and continuous. Moreover,

is a graded topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra, which is super-commutative in the sense that

For each

by setting

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{rl}&(d\omega)(X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{r})\\
:=&\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq r}(-1)^{i}(X_{i}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{E})(\omega(X_{0},X_{1},\dots,\hat{X_{i}},\dots,X_{r}))\\
+&\sum\limits_{0\leq i<j\leq r}(-1)^{i+j}(\omega([X_{i},X_{j}],X_{0},X_{1},\dots,\hat{X_{i}},\dots,\hat{X_{j}},\dots,X_{r})),\end{array}

where

denotes the identity map on

, and “

” indicates the omission of a term. As usual, we have the following straightforward results (

[

•

for

and
\omega_{2}\in\Omega^{s}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E)

d(\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2})=(d\omega_{1})\wedge\omega_{2}+(-1)^{r}\omega_{1}\wedge(d\omega_{2}).

These imply that

is a differential graded topological
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra.
Meanwhile, it is routine to check that the family

(2.7)

\{d:\ \Omega^{r}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(U;E)\rightarrow\Omega^{r+1}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(U;E)\}_{\text{$U$ is an open subset of $M$}}

is a
{\mathbb{C}}_{M}
-homomorphism between the sheaves

Example 2.6

In the setting of Example
2.1
,
for

and
1\leq j\leq k

(2.9)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Omega^{r}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M;E)&\rightarrow&\prod\limits_{(I,J)\in\Lambda_{n,k}^{r}}{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(M),\\

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M,{\mathcal{O}})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})
be a morphism of formal manifolds.
Then there is a unique homomorphism

(2.13)

of sheaves of graded algebras such that

\varphi^{\natural}|_{\overline{\varphi}^{-1}\Omega^{0}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}}=\varphi^{*}:\ \overline{\varphi}^{-1}\Omega^{0}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}=\overline{\varphi}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\rightarrow\Omega^{0}_{{\mathcal{O}}}={\mathcal{O}}
; and

.

Furthermore, the homomorphism

\varphi^{\natural}:\Omega_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathcal{O}}(M)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

.
Assume first that

,
where

be a homomorphism that fulfills the requirements in the proposition. Let

, respectively. Then we have that

for

.
Thus, for every

the following equality holds (see Example
2.6
):

(2.14)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\natural}(fdx_{I}dy_{J})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\varphi^{*}(x_{i_{1}}))\wedge\cdots\wedge d(\varphi^{*}(x_{i_{s}}))\wedge d(\varphi^{*}(y_{j_{1}}))\wedge\cdots\wedge d(\varphi^{*}(y_{j_{r-s}}))\wedge\varphi^{*}(f).

This proves the uniqueness of

.
On the other hand, the desired homomorphism

is defined by using (

For the general case,

\varphi^{\natural}
be a homomorphism that fulfills the requirements of this proposition. Then for each chart

of

Then for each

Then we obtain a homomorphism

which satisfies the requirements in the proposition.
This completes the proof of the first assertion in the proposition.

Now we turn to prove that the map

and

\begin{CD}\Omega^{r}_{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})@>{{\varphi^{\natural}}}>{}>\Omega^{r}_{\mathcal{O}}(M)\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\

Appendix A
Topological cochain complexes

In this appendix, we collect some basics on topological cochain complexes which are needed in
Sections
2
-
4
.

A.1.
Topological cochain complexes

We start with the following definition.

Definition A.1
.

A topological cochain complex
I^{\bullet}
is a cochain complex

\cdots\rightarrow I^{i}\xrightarrow{d}I^{i+1}\xrightarrow{d}I^{i+2}\rightarrow\cdots

of Hausdorff LCS with continuous linear coboundary maps
d
.

Let
I^{\bullet}
and
J^{\bullet}
be two topological cochain complexes, and let
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. By a continuous map
f:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
of degree
p
, we mean a sequence

(A.1)

f=\{f_{n}:I^{n}\rightarrow J^{n+p}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

of continuous linear maps.
As usual, a continuous map of degree
0
that commutes with the coboundary maps is called a continuous complex map. A continuous complex map
f:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
is said to be a topological complex isomorphism if there is a continuous complex map
f^{\prime}:J^{\bullet}\rightarrow I^{\bullet}
such that
f\circ f^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}
and
f^{\prime}\circ f=\mathrm{id}_{I^{\bullet}}
. Here
\mathrm{id}_{I^{\bullet}}
and
\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}
denote the identity maps of complexes.

Let
f,g:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
be two continuous complex maps.
We say that
f
is topologically homotopic to
g
, denoted by
f\sim_{\mathrm{h}}g
, if there
is a continuous map
h:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
of degree
-1
such that
d\circ h+h\circ d=f-g
.
The map
f
is said to be a topological homotopy equivalence if there is a continuous complex map
f^{\prime}:J^{\bullet}\rightarrow I^{\bullet}

such that
f\circ f^{\prime}\sim_{\mathrm{h}}\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}
and
f^{\prime}\circ f\sim_{\mathrm{h}}\mathrm{id}_{I^{\bullet}}
.

A.2.
Tensor products of topological cochain complexes

Let
E
and
F
be two LCS. As discussed in
[
G
]
, the algebraic tensor product
E\otimes F
admits at least three valuable locally convex topologies: the inductive tensor product
E\otimes_{\mathrm{i}}F
, the projective tensor product
E\otimes_{\pi}F
, and the epsilon (injective) tensor product
E\otimes_{\varepsilon}F
. Explicit definitions can be found in
[
G
]
.
In this context, as mentioned in the Introduction, we denote the quasi-completions and completions of these topological tensor product spaces as follows:

\text{$E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F$,
$E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F$, $E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F$\quad and\quad$E\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F$,
$E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F$, $E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F$}.

When
E
or
F
is nuclear, it is a classical result of Grothendieck that their projective tensor product coincides with the epsilon tensor product (see
[
T
, Theorem 50.1]
).
In this case, we
will simply write

E\widetilde{\otimes}F:=E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}F=E\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F\quad\text{and}\quad E\widehat{\otimes}F:=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F.

Given two continuous linear maps
\phi_{1}:E_{1}\rightarrow F_{1}
and
\phi_{2}:E_{2}\rightarrow F_{2}
between LCS, we use
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}
to denote the continuous linear map on various topological tensor products (or their quasi-completions or completions) obtained by the tensor product of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
. For example, we have the maps

\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}:\ E_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{2}\rightarrow F_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}:\ E_{1}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}E_{2}\rightarrow F_{1}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\mathrm{i}}F_{2}.

Let
I^{\bullet}
and
J^{\bullet}
be two topological cochain complexes. We define

I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}
and
I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}
to be the topological cochain complexes such that

(I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet})^{i}=\bigoplus_{r,s\in\mathbb{Z};r+s=i}I^{r}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{s},\quad(I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet})^{i}=\bigoplus_{r,s\in\mathbb{Z};r+s=i}I^{r}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{s}

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

d(u\otimes v)=(du)\otimes v+(-1)^{r}u\otimes(dv)

for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
,
u\in I^{r}
and
v\in J^{s}
. Here the notation
\otimes_{\Box}
stands for one of the topological tensor products
\otimes_{\pi}
,
\otimes_{\varepsilon}
and
\otimes_{\mathrm{i}}
. Then both
I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}
and
I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}
are topological cochain complexes.

We say that a topological cochain complex
I^{\bullet}
is nuclear if the LCS
I^{i}
is nuclear for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
I^{\bullet}
or
J^{\bullet}
is nuclear, we
will simply write

I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}J^{\bullet}:=I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\pi}J^{\bullet}=I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}J^{\bullet}\quad\text{and}\quad I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}J^{\bullet}:=I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}J^{\bullet}=I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}J^{\bullet}.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
I^{\bullet}
,
J^{\bullet}
and
K^{\bullet}
be topological cochain complexes, and
let
f_{1},f_{2}:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow K^{\bullet}
be two continuous complex maps. If
f_{1}\sim_{\mathrm{h}}f_{2}
,
then

f_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}\sim_{\mathrm{h}}f_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}:\quad I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}\rightarrow K^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet},

and

f_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}\sim_{\mathrm{h}}f_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}:\quad I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}\rightarrow K^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}.

Proof.

Choose a continuous map
h:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow K^{\bullet}
of degree
-1
such that
d\circ h+h\circ d=f_{1}-f_{2}.

Then

h\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}:I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}\rightarrow K^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}\quad(\text{resp.}\ h\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}:I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}J^{\bullet}\rightarrow K^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}J^{\bullet})

is a continuous map of degree
-1
such that
d\circ(h\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}})+(h\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}})\circ d
and
f_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}-f_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{J^{\bullet}}

agree on the strictly dense (resp. dense) subspace
I^{r}\otimes J^{s}
of
I^{r}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{s}
(resp.
I^{r}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{s}
), where
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The lemma then follows.

f\otimes g:I^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}\rightarrow I_{1}^{\bullet}\widetilde{\otimes}_{\Box}J_{1}^{\bullet}\quad\text{and}\quad f\otimes g:I^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J^{\bullet}\rightarrow I_{1}^{\bullet}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Box}J_{1}^{\bullet}

are topological homotopy equivalences as well.

Proof.

This easily follows from Lemma
A.2

A.3.
Transposes of topological cochain complexes

Let
E
and
F
be two LCS. As mentioned in the Introduction, let
E^{\prime}
denote the space of all continuous linear maps from
E
to
{\mathbb{C}}
, which is endowed with the strong topology. For a continuous linear map
\phi:E\rightarrow F
, let

(A.3)

{}^{t}\phi:\ F^{\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime},\quad u^{\prime}\mapsto(v\mapsto\langle u^{\prime},\phi(v)\rangle)

denote the transpose of
\phi
. It is well-known that
{}^{t}\phi
is a continuous linear map.

Let
I^{\bullet}
be a topological cochain complex. Write
({}^{t}I)^{\bullet}
for
the topological cochain complex, to be called the transpose of
I^{\bullet}
,
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

({}^{t}I)^{i}:=(I^{-i})^{\prime}

and

\langle d(u^{\prime}),v\rangle=(-1)^{i}\langle u^{\prime},d(v)\rangle\quad(u^{\prime}\in({}^{t}I)^{i},v\in I^{-i-1}).

Let
f:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
be a continuous complex map.
As in (
A.3
), we define the transpose
{}^{t}f:({}^{t}J)^{\bullet}\rightarrow({}^{t}I)^{\bullet}
of
f
in an obvious way (by taking the term-wise transpose), which
is still a continuous complex map. Furthermore, we have the following result.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
f,g:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
be two continuous complex maps which are topologically homotopic to each other. Then

{}^{t}f,\ {}^{t}g:\quad({}^{t}J)^{\bullet}\rightarrow({}^{t}I)^{\bullet}

are topologically homotopic to each other as well. Moreover, if
f:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
is a topological homotopy equivalence,
then so is
{}^{t}f:({}^{t}J)^{\bullet}\rightarrow({}^{t}I)^{\bullet}
.

Proof.

Let
h:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow J^{\bullet}
be a continuous map of degree
-1
such that
d\circ h+h\circ d=f-g.

Then

{}^{\#}h:\ ({}^{t}J)^{\bullet}\rightarrow({}^{t}I)^{\bullet},\quad u^{\prime}\mapsto(v\mapsto(-1)^{i}\langle u^{\prime},h(v)\rangle)\quad(u^{\prime}\in({}^{t}J)^{i+1},v\in I^{-i},i\in\mathbb{Z})

is a continuous map of degree
-1
such that
d\circ{}^{\#}h+{}^{\#}h\circ d={}^{t}f-{}^{t}g
.
This proves the first assertion. The second assertion easily follows from the first one.
∎

A.4.
Strong exactness of topological cochain complexes

Recall from the Introduction that a topological cochain complex is said to be strongly exact if it is exact and all the coboundary maps are strong.
For every Hausdorff LCS
E
, form the topological cochain complex concentrated at degree
0
:

(A.4)

\iota^{\bullet}(E):\quad\cdots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\iota^{0}(E):=E\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots.

We say that a topological cochain complex
I^{\bullet}
is positive (resp. negative) if
I^{i}=0
for all
i<0
(resp.
i>0
).
The following two results are useful in checking the strong exactness of positive/negative topological cochain complexes.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
I^{\bullet}
be a positive topological cochain complex, and let
E
be
a Hausdorff LCS. Suppose that
there is a topological homotopy equivalence
f:\iota^{\bullet}(E)\rightarrow I^{\bullet}
.
Then the induced topological cochain complex

(A.5)

\cdots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow E\xrightarrow{f}I^{0}\xrightarrow{d}I^{1}\xrightarrow{d}I^{2}\rightarrow\cdots

is strongly exact.

Proof.

The exactness of (
A.5
) is obvious, and we need to prove that the continuous linear maps

f:E\rightarrow I^{0}
and
d:I^{r}\rightarrow I^{r+1}
(
r\geq 0
) are strong.
Let
g:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow\iota^{\bullet}(E)
be a continuous complex map such that
f\circ g\sim_{\mathrm{h}}\mathrm{id}_{I^{\bullet}}
and
g\circ f\sim_{\mathrm{h}}\mathrm{id}_{\iota^{\bullet}(E)}
.
Since
g\circ f=\mathrm{id}_{\iota^{\bullet}(E)}
, we have that
f=f\circ g\circ f:E\rightarrow I^{0}
.

We take a continuous map
h:I^{\bullet}\rightarrow I^{\bullet}
of degree
-1
such that

d\circ h+h\circ d=\mathrm{id}_{I^{\bullet}}-f\circ g
.
Since
g\circ d=0
on
I^{r}
, we find that

d\circ h\circ d=(\mathrm{id}_{I^{r+1}}-f\circ g-h\circ d)\circ d=d

as continuous linear maps from
I^{r}
to
I^{r+1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Similar to Lemma
A.5
, we also have the following result.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
J^{\bullet}
be a negative topological cochain complex, and let
E
be
a Hausdorff LCS. Suppose that
there is a topological homotopy equivalence
g:J^{\bullet}\rightarrow\iota^{\bullet}(E)
.
Then the topological cochain complex

(A.6)

\cdots\rightarrow J^{-2}\xrightarrow{d}J^{-1}\xrightarrow{d}J^{0}\xrightarrow{g}E\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots

is strongly exact.

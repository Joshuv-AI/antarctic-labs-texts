Abstract
. The main objective of this paper is to demonstrate the uniform large deviation principle (UDLP) for the solutions of two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (SNSE) in the vorticity form when perturbed by two distinct types of noises. We first consider an infinite-dimensional additive noise that is white in time and colored in space and then consider a finite-dimensional Wiener process with linear growth coefficient. In order to obtain the ULDP for 2D SNSE in the vorticity form, where the noise is white in time and colored in space, we utilize the existence and uniqueness result from B. Ferrario et. al., Stochastic Process. Appl., 129 (2019), 1568–1604, and the uniform contraction principle . For the finite-dimensional multiplicative Wiener noise, we first prove the existence of a unique local mild solution to the vorticity equation using a truncation and fixed point arguments. We then establish the global existence of the truncated system by deriving a uniform energy estimate for the local mild solution. By applying stopping time arguments and a version of Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we conclude the global existence and uniqueness of a solution to our model. We employ the weak convergence approach to establish the ULDP for the law of the solutions in two distinct topologies. We prove ULDP in the C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , L p ​ ( 𝕋 2 ) ) \mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})) topology, for p > 2 p>2 , taking into account the uniformity of the initial conditions contained in bounded subsets of L p ​ ( 𝕋 2 ) \mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2}) . Finally, in C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] × 𝕋 2 ) \mathrm{C}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2}) topology, the uniformity of initial conditions lying in bounded subsets of C ⁡ ( 𝕋 2 ) \mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{2}) is considered.



1.
Introduction

We consider the following two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (SNSE):

(1.1)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}}{\partial t}(t,x)+(\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)-&\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)+\nabla p^{\epsilon}(t,x)\\
&=\sqrt{\epsilon}\boldsymbol{\eta},\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)&=0,\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\\
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(0,x)&=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}(x),\ x\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\end{aligned}}}\right.

which describe the motion of a viscous incompressible fluid in a domain
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here,
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)=(u_{1}^{\epsilon},u_{2}^{\epsilon})(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
represents the velocity field at time
t
and position
x
,
p^{\epsilon}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure field,
\nu>0
is the viscosity,
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}
us the initial velocity and
\boldsymbol{\eta}
is the stochastic forcing term. We consider our domain to be a torus, with
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}=[0,2\pi]^{2}
and periodic boundary conditions in place.

1.1.
Vorticity formulation

In this article, we discuss the vorticity formulation of the model in (
1.1
) with two distinct types of noises.

1.1.1.
Additive infinite-dimensional noise

Taking
curl
on both sides of the first equation of the system (
1.1
), one obtains the vorticity formulation, where the unknown is the vorticity

\xi^{\epsilon}(t,x)=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)\equiv\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}^{\epsilon}(t,x)-\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1}^{\epsilon}(t,x),\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}}.

We know that the
curl
of a planar vector field has only one significant component
\xi^{\epsilon}
, which is orthogonal to the plane. Therefore, with enough regularity of solutions, the system (
1.1

(1.2)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\xi^{\epsilon}}{\partial t}(t,x)+\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)\cdot\nabla\xi^{\epsilon}(t,x)-&\nu\Delta\xi^{\epsilon}(t,x)\\
&=\sqrt{\epsilon}\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}x),\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(t,x)&=0,\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\\
\xi^{\epsilon}(0,x)&=\xi_{0}(x),\ x\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\end{aligned}}}\right.

with the periodic boundary conditions. For the sake of convenience, we are assuming
\nu=1
. Here,
\mathrm{W}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the formal notation for some Gaussian perturbation defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. The

provides a relationship between the velocity field

We interpret the system (

[
51
]

be the fundamental solution to the heat equation on the flat torus
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
. Then, the random field
\xi^{\epsilon}=\{\xi(t,x):(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\}
is a solution to the system (
1.2
) if it satisfies the following integral equation

\displaystyle\xi^{\epsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t,x,y)\xi_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nabla_{y}G(t-s,x,y)\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(s,y)\xi^{\epsilon}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(1.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t-s,x,y)\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y),\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
.

\displaystyle\xi^{\epsilon}(t,x)

1.3.
Objectives and novelties of the paper

This work aims to analyze the asymptotic behavior of the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations in the vorticity form with a stochastic forcing term (to be specified later). To the best of our understanding, this analysis presented in this article has not been previously investigated in available literature. This paper is structured into two parts, which focus on the noise structure:

In the first part, we assume that the equation is driven by an infinite-dimensional additive noise, which is white in time and colored in space.

•

The noise structure of this part resembles that of the work
[
23
]
. Minor modifications to the well-posed result presented in
[
23
]
can be used to obtain the global solvability of the system (
1.2
). Our objective is to demonstrate that ULDP holds in two distinct topologies:
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))
with
p>2
, where the uniformity is measured over initial conditions belonging to bounded subsets of
\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})
, and
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
with uniformity over initial conditions belonging to bounded subsets of
\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. The proof of the ULDP in both cases are based on the uniform contraction principle established in
[
52
]
.

We assume in the second part that the equation is driven by a multiplicative finite-dimensional Wiener process with a linear growth of the noise coefficient.

•

We use the approach from
[
25
,
28
,
29
,
32
]
, etc. to ensure the well-posedness of the system (
1.4
). We prove the existence of a local solution to the integral equation (
1.1.2
) by utilizing a truncation and fixed point argument. With the help of stopping time arguments, tightness properties and Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we establish the uniform global energy estimates of the local solution and consequently, the global existence of the solution.

•

Our final goal is to verify the ULDP for the system (
1.4
). Given the initial conditions in a bounded subsets of an appropriate space, it is not sufficient to establish ULP to obtain UDLP without any additional assumption as discussed in
[
43
]
. The motivation for the ULDP comes from the work of
[
43
]
, which provides a sufficient condition for the verification of EULP, leading to ULDP in two distinct topologies:
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))
with
p>2
, where the uniformity is measured over initial conditions belonging to bounded subsets of
\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})
, and
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
with uniformity over initial conditions belonging to bounded subsets of
\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. We use the embedding
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))
, in the proof of ULDP in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
topology.

\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2}):=\mathbb{H}\cap\mathbb{L}_{\#}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, which forms Banach spaces with the norms inherited from the space
\mathbb{L}_{\#}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Similarly, we set the vector space,
\mathbb{H}^{a,p}=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}):\mathrm{A}^{\frac{a}{2}}\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})\}
. These are Banach spaces with the usual norm, and for
p=2
they become Hilbert spaces and we denote them by
\mathbb{H}^{a}
. For
a<0
, we define
\mathbb{H}^{a}
as the dual space of
\mathbb{H}^{-a}
with respect to the
\mathbb{H}
-scalar product. Moreover, the Poincaré inequality holds. The zero mean value assumption provides that the norms
\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{a,p}}=\|\mathrm{A}^{\frac{a}{2}}\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}
and
\big(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}}^{p}+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{a,p}}^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}
are equivalent.

We use the following Rellich-Kondrachov compactness Theorem in the sequel (see
[
4
, Theorem 9.16]
):

(1)

For every
p\in(2,\infty)
, the space
\mathbb{H}^{1,p}
is compactly embedded in
\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})
, that is, there exists a constant
C_{p}
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\infty}}\leq C_{p}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1,p}}.

(2)

The space
\mathrm{H}^{a}
is compactly embedded in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})
for
a>1
.

Let
(\mathbb{U},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{U}})
and
(\mathbb{V},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{V}})
be any two normed vector spaces. The space of all linear bounded operators from
\mathbb{U}
into
\mathbb{V}
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V})
. We use the notation
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{U}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{U}}
for scalar product and duality pairing between
\mathbb{U}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{U}
, respectively.

2.2.
The heat kernel

We interpreted system (
1.2
) in the sense of Walsh (see
[
51
]
) as expressed in (
1.1.1
), with the heat kernel
G
. We need to make accurate estimations of
G
to complete our calculations, as it is a critical factor.

The operator
\mathrm{A}
generates a semigroup
\mathrm{S}(t)=e^{-t\mathrm{A}}
, for
\xi\in\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{\#}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle[\mathrm{S}(t)\xi](x)=\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{-t|\eta|^{2}}(\xi,e_{\eta})_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}e_{\eta}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(\xi,e_{\eta})_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}e^{-t|\eta|^{2}+i\eta\cdot x}.

Moreover, the action of the semigroup
\mathrm{S}(\cdot)
on the vorticity
\xi
can be represented by the convolution

(2.4)

\displaystyle[\mathrm{S}(t)\xi](x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t,x,y)\xi(y)\mathrm{d}y,

where
G
is the heat kernel of the following problem

(2.5)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)&=0,\ \ (t,x)\in(0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\\
v(t,\cdot)&\text{ is periodic },\ \ t\in[0,T],\\
v(0,x)&=\delta_{0}(x-y),\ \ x,y\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}},\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\delta_{0}(\cdot)
is the Dirac delta distribution centered at
(0,0)
. Using Fourier series expansion, we obtain the following explicity form of heat kernel
G

(2.6)

\displaystyle G(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{-t|\eta|^{2}+i\eta\cdot(x-y)}.

For our calculations, we need another form of heat kernel which can be derived using the method of images (see
[
22
, Chapter 2.7, Section 5 or Chapter 2.11, Section 3]
or
[
47
, Chapter 7, Section 2]
)

(2.7)

\displaystyle G(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi t}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y+2\eta\pi|^{2}}{4t}}.

From (
2.6
) or (
2.7
), one can observe the following properties of the heat kernel
G
for all
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
:

(1)

Symmetry:

G(t,x,y)=G(t,y,x)
,

(2)

G(t,x,y)=G(t,0,x-y)
.

Let us recall a result from
[
23
]
which gives useful estimates on the heat kernel and its gradient in the two-dimensional case.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
23
, Theorem 4]
)
.

For fixed
s\in(0,t)
and
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2},
the following estimates hold:

(1)

For
\beta\in(0,4/3)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\beta}
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla_{y}G(s,x,y)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}s^{1-\frac{3\beta}{2}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla_{y}G(s,x,y)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}t^{2-\frac{3\beta}{2}}.

(2)

For every
\beta\in(0,2)
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\beta}
such that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|G(s,x,y)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}s^{1-\beta},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|G(s,x,y)|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}t^{2-\beta}.

The following result shows the regularizing effect of convolution with the gradient of the heat kernel
G
. Let
J
be the linear operator defined as

(2.12)

\displaystyle(J\boldsymbol{\phi})(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nabla_{y}G(t-s,x,y)\cdot\boldsymbol{\phi}(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,\ \ \text{ for }\ \ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}},

and
\boldsymbol{\phi}
smooth enough. We have the following result from
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
23
, Lemma 6]
)
.

The following hold:

(1)

Let
p\geq 1,\ \alpha\geq 1,\ \beta\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1,\frac{4}{3}}}\right),\ \gamma>\frac{2\beta}{2-\beta}
such that
\frac{1}{\beta}=1+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Then,
J
is a bounded linear operator from
\mathrm{L}^{\gamma}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
into
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C_{\beta}
such that

\displaystyle\|(J\boldsymbol{\phi})(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{3}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{\phi}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}\mathrm{d}s

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}t^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|\boldsymbol{\phi}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}},

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

(2)

Let
p>4
and
\gamma>\frac{2p}{p-2}
. Then,
J
is a bounded linear operator from

\mathrm{L}^{\gamma}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
into
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C_{p,T}
such that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|(J\boldsymbol{\phi})(t,x)|\leq C_{p,T}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|\boldsymbol{\phi}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}}^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.

2.3.
The Biot-Savart law

Let us discuss the Biot-Savart law, which provides an explicit expression of the velocity field
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}
in terms of the vorticity
\xi^{\epsilon}
, as described in
[
36
, Section 2.4.1]
and
[
37
, Section 1.2]
. Taking
curl
on both side of the relation
\xi^{\epsilon}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}
, we find

(2.15)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}&=\nabla^{\perp}\xi^{\epsilon},\\
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}&=0,\\
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}&\text{ is periodic}.\end{aligned}}}\right.

The above system allows us to express velocity field
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}
in terms of vorticity
\xi^{\epsilon}
. In terms of the Fourier series, if
\xi^{\epsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\xi_{\eta}^{\epsilon}e^{i\eta\cdot x}
, then

(2.16)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(x)=-\frac{i}{2\pi}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\frac{\eta^{\perp}}{|\eta|^{2}}\xi_{\eta}^{\epsilon}e^{i\eta\cdot x}.

It is clear from the above relation that the velocity field
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}
has one order more of regularity in view of vorticity field
\xi^{\epsilon}
, that is, if
\xi^{\epsilon}\in\mathrm{H}^{a-1,p}
then
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{H}^{a,p}
. In particular, the norms
\|\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{a,p}}
and
\|\xi^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{a-1,p}}
are equivalent.

From
[
37
, Section 1.2]
, we know that the Biot-Savart law allows us to write the velocity field
\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}
in terms of the vorticity
\xi^{\epsilon}
as

(2.17)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}(x)=(\boldsymbol{k}*\xi^{\epsilon})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\boldsymbol{k}(x-y)\xi^{\epsilon}(y)\mathrm{d}y,

where
\boldsymbol{k}
is the Biot-Savart kernel given by

(2.18)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{k}=\nabla^{\perp}G=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{2}},\frac{\partial G}{\partial x_{1}}}}\right),

and
G
is the Green function of
-\Delta
on the torus with mean zero.

Let us recall some basic properties of the Biot-Savart kernel from
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
6
, Lemma 2.17]
)
.

For every
p\in[1,2)
, the map
\boldsymbol{k}
defined in (
2.18
) is an
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
divergence free (in the distributional sense) vector field.

We have some useful estimates which play a crucial role. Using (
2.16
), the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{H}^{1,p}\subset\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for
p>2
, and the equivalency of the norms
\|\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1,p}}
and
\|\xi^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}
, we obtain for any
p>2

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{k}*\xi^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\infty}}=\|\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\infty}}\leq C_{p}\|\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1,p}}\leq C_{p}\|\xi^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}},

for some positive constant
C_{p}
.

Using (
2.17
), Lemma
2.3
, Young’s inequality for convolution (with exponent
p\geq 1,\alpha\in[1,2)
, and
\beta\geq 1
, and
1+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}
), we obtain

(2.20)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{k}*\xi^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}}=\|\boldsymbol{u}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{k}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}}\|\xi^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\beta}}.

2.4.
A short note on stochastic forcing term

Let us discuss the stochastic term appearing in the system (
1.2
). Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a given stochastic basis.
For any given
T>0
, let
\mathrm{Q}:\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{\#}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\to\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{\#}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
be a positive symmetric bounded linear operator. Set
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
as the completion of the space of all square integrable, zero mean-value, periodic functions
\phi:{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to the scalar product
(\phi,\psi)_{\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}}=(\mathrm{Q}\phi,\psi)_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}
, for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{T}=\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}))
denote the real separable Hilbert space, with the scalar product

\displaystyle(g_{1},g_{2})_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}=\int_{0}^{T}(g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t))_{\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}(\mathrm{Q}g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t))_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\mathrm{d}t.

Let us now consider the isonormal Gaussian process
\mathrm{W}=\{\mathrm{W}(h);h\in\mathcal{H}_{T}\}
(for more details see
[
41
]
). The map
h\mapsto\mathrm{W}(h)
provides a linear isometry from
\mathcal{H}_{T}
onto
\mathcal{H}
, which is a closed subset of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
, consisted of zero-mean Gaussian random variables and the isometry can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{W}(h)\mathrm{W}(g)]=(h,g)_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}.

The stochastic term appearing in (
1.2
) should be understand in the following sense, for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{T}
, we set

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(h)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}h(s,y)\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y),

namely, the above random variable is a zero-mean Gaussian random variable with the covariance
\mathbb{E}[(\mathrm{W}(h))^{2}]=\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}^{2}
.

The stochastic term above, expressed through linear isometry
\mathrm{W}
, can be interpreted in the framework of Walsh (as detailed in
[
51
]
or
[
16
]
). One can express
\mathrm{W}(h)
as

(2.22)

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}(h)=\sum_{i}\int_{0}^{T}(h(s),\tilde{e}_{i})_{\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\beta_{s}(\tilde{e}_{i}),

where
\{\tilde{e}_{i}\}
is a complete orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{Q}}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\beta_{s}(\tilde{e}_{i})=\mathrm{W}(\chi_{[0,s]}\tilde{e}_{i})
, hence
\{\beta(\tilde{e}_{i})\}
is a sequence of independent standard 1-dimensional Brownian motions on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
adapted to the filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Setting
\mathrm{M}_{t}(A):=\mathrm{W}(\chi_{[0,t]}\chi_{A})
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we construct a martingale measure with the covariance operator
\mathrm{Q}
and (
2.22
) coincides with the stochastic integral in the sense of Walsh (
[
51
]
). Moreover, the isonormal Gaussian process
\mathrm{W}
can be associated to a
\mathrm{Q}
-Wiener process
\mathscr{W}_{t}
on
\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{\#}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
(see
[
16
]
) in the following way

(2.23)

\displaystyle(\mathscr{W}_{t},h)_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=\mathrm{W}(\chi_{[0,t]}h),\ \ \text{ for all }\ \ h\in\mathring{\mathrm{L}}_{\#}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}),

and (
2.21
) coincides with the integral with respect to
\mathscr{W}
, in a sense made precise in
[
17
, Section 3.4]
. The stochastic convolution term appearing on the right-hand side of (
1.1.1
) is meaningful in the ways described above. The stochastic forcing term is periodic with zero-mean in the space variable. It is not unexpected that we need the covariance operator
\mathrm{Q}
to have some regularizing effect when working in a two-dimensional space, as stated in
[
16
, Pages 144-146]
. To address this issue, one can consider using the covariance operator
\mathrm{Q}
with an appropriate regularizing effect. For example, one can choose a covariance operator of the form

(2.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}=(-\Delta)^{-a},\ \ \text{ for some}\ \ a>0.

Thus, we have

\mathrm{Q}e_{\eta}=\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2a}}e_{\eta},\ \text{for all}\ \eta\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2},
and
\{\tilde{e}_{\eta}\}
is a complete orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
defined as
\tilde{e}_{\eta}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}|\eta|^{a}\cos(\eta\cdot x)
and
\tilde{e}_{-\eta}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}|\eta|^{a}\sin(\eta\cdot x)
, for
\eta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
. From
[
23
, Eq
n
(2.24)]
, we know that the stochastic term
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathrm{G}(t-s,x,y)\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)
is well-defined for
a>0
, which is equivalent to have
G(t-\cdot,x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}_{t}
, for every
t>0
. It can justified as

\displaystyle\|G(t-\cdot,x,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2+2a}},

the above series is convergent if and only if
a>0
. Thus, the hypothesis
a>0
is sufficient for space-time continuity of the stochastic convolution’s trajectories (see
[
15
, Theorem 2.13]
). The noise formulation of our work is similar to
[
23
]
, where the author considered two-dimensional SNSE (
1.1
) in the vorticity form (
1.2
) with
\epsilon=1
, and established the existence and uniqueness of the solution
\xi
of (
1.2
) in the spaces
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})),
for
p>2
and
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
whenever the initial data
\xi_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\xi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, respectively.

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(t-s,x,y)\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y),\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

for all

.

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s+1}}\|\xi^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2},

where we have used (

), Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities.

, we consider the solution to the system (

) according to Walsh (see

]
) being provided by the integral equation (

).

Let us recall the the existence and uniqueness result for the solution to the system (

]
.

(

.

, for

, and

in (

-valued

3.
Uniform Large Deviation Principle: Additive Noise

In this section, we first recall the basics of large deviation theory. Then, we discuss the concept of uniform large deviation principle (ULDP) and establish it for the law of the solution of the system (
1.2
).

3.1.
Large deviation principle

Let
\mathcal{E}
denote a Polish space (complete separable metric space) with the Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E})
. Let
\{\mu^{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
be a family of probability measures on
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{E}))
. We provide some basic definitions related to large deviation principle.

Definition 3.1

(Rate function)
.

A function
\mathrm{I}:\mathcal{E}\to[0,\infty]
is called a
rate function
on
\mathcal{E}
if it is a lower semicontinuous in
\mathcal{E}
. A rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
on
\mathcal{E}
is said to be a
good rate function
on
\mathcal{E}
if for every
M\in[0,\infty)
, the level set
K:=\{x\in\mathcal{E}:\mathrm{I}(x)\leq M\}
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 3.2

(Large deviation principle)
.

Let
\mathrm{I}:\mathcal{E}\to[0,\infty]
be a rate function on a Polish space
\mathcal{E}
. The sequence
\{\mu^{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
satisfies the LDP on the space
\mathcal{E}
with the rate function
\mathrm{I}(\cdot)
, if the following hold:

(1)

For any closed subset

If

Definition 3.3

(FWULDP)

A family of
\mathcal{E}
-valued random variables

with the speed

and the rate function

if the following hold:

(1)

For any
\mathrm{E}\in\mathscr{T},\ t_{0}\geq 0,
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\inf_{\xi_{0}\in\mathrm{E}}\inf_{\psi\in\Lambda_{\xi_{0}}(t_{0})}\bigg\{b(\epsilon)\log\mu^{\epsilon}\big(d(\mathrm{Y}_{\xi_{0}}^{\epsilon},\psi)<\delta\big)+\mathrm{I}_{\xi_{0}}(\psi)\bigg\}\geq 0.

(2)

For any
\mathrm{E}\in\mathscr{T},\ t_{0}\geq 0,
and
\delta>0

\displaystyle\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{\xi_{0}\in\mathrm{E}}\sup_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}\bigg\{b(\epsilon)\log\mu^{\epsilon}\big(\mathrm{dist}_{\mathcal{E}}(\mathrm{Y}_{\xi_{0}}^{\epsilon},\Lambda_{\xi_{0}}(s))\geq\delta\big)+s\bigg\}\leq 0.

Proposition 3.4

(

\displaystyle=\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\nabla_{y}G(t-s,x,y)\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{q}_{R}(\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)-\boldsymbol{q}_{R}(\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))\big)(y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{q}_{R}(\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)-\boldsymbol{q}_{R}(\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))\|_{\mathbb{L}_{\sigma}^{p}}\mathrm{d}s,

for all

. From the above inequality and Lemma

(see (

)), we obtain for all

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{2}\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{A}_{2}\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R,p}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{-\lambda pt}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{R,p}T^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}e^{-\lambda s}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\big]\mathrm{d}s\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C_{R,p}T^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\right)\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{p}=C_{R,p}T^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\right)\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{p},

for

, where

Using similar arguments as in (

4.1
(Lipschitz continuity of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{A}_{3}\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(t)-\mathcal{A}_{3}\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\big]

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|G(t-s,\cdot,\cdot)*(\sigma_{j,R}(s,\cdot,\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))-\sigma_{j,R}(s,\cdot,\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)))\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|G(t-s,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}^{2}\|\sigma_{j,R}(s,\cdot,\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))-\sigma_{j,R}(s,\cdot,\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma_{j,R}(s,\cdot,\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot))-\sigma_{j,R}(s,\cdot,\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s,\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon,n,p,L}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon,n,p,L}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{p}}}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon,n,p,L}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\big]\bigg\}^{\frac{2}{p}}\mathrm{d}s}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon,n,p,L}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\lambda}(e^{2\lambda t}-1)\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\bigg\{e^{-\lambda ps}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\big]\bigg\},

for
p\geq 2
. From the above inequality, we obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{3}\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{A}_{3}\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{p}\leq C_{\epsilon,n,p,L}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\lambda}(1-e^{-2\lambda t})\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\|\xi_{R,1}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{R,2}^{\epsilon}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{p}.

Combining (

)-(
4.15
), and choosing

\displaystyle C_{R,p}T^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}}}\right)+C_{\epsilon,n,p,L}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\lambda}(1-e^{-2\lambda T})\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}<1,

for

. For the above value of

Therefore an application of the Banach fixed point theorem yields the existence of a unique fixed point for the operator

∎

Lemma 4.5

For each

Remark 4.6
.

One can show the existence of solution in the following way also: We first consider the solution
\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}
of the truncated integral equation (
4.1
) for any
R\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us define a sequence of stopping times

\displaystyle\tau_{R}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:\|\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\geq R\}\wedge T,\ \ \text{ for }\ \ R\in\mathbb{N}.

From Proposition
4.4
, we know that
\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
is the unique solution of the integral equation (
1.1.2
) for any
t\in[0,\tau_{R}]
. By the uniqueness of solution, we infer that for any
P\geq R
,
\tau_{R}\leq\tau_{P}
and
\xi_{P}^{\epsilon}(t)=\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t)
for
t\leq\tau_{R}
. Let us define
\xi^{\epsilon}(t)=\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t)
for
t\leq\tau_{R}
. Using the similar arguments we are able to construct a solution
\xi^{\epsilon}(t)
of the integral equation (
1.1.2
) in the random interval
[0,\tau_{\infty}]
, where
\tau_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{R\geq 0}\tau_{R}
.
Now, our aim is to establish that
\tau_{\infty}=T,\ \mathbb{P}
-a.s., which is equivalent to show that
\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\tau_{R}<T\big)=0
.

From the uniform energy estimate (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]\leq C\big(\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}+1\big),

for
p>2
. Since
\tau_{R}\leq\tau_{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\tau_{\infty}<T\big)\leq\mathbb{P}\big(\tau_{R}<T\big)=\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}>R^{p}\bigg).

Applying Markov’s inequality in the above inequality, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\tau_{R}<T\big)\leq\frac{1}{R^{p}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi_{R}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]\leq\frac{C\big(\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}+1\big)}{R^{p}}.

Passing
R\to\infty
leads to our required result, that is,
\tau_{\infty}=T,\ \mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(t)-\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}>\delta\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\varrho+\sup_{\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sqrt{\epsilon}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{3,\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}>\frac{\delta}{C}\bigg).

Using the linear growth of
\sigma_{j}
(see Hypothesis
4.1
), we have for any
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sigma_{j}(t,\cdot,\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot))\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]

(5.19)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(1+\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]\bigg)<\infty.

Using
[
29
, Corollary 3.6]
, we obtain for any
\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N
and
v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
the family

\displaystyle\{I_{3,\epsilon}:\epsilon\in(0,1]\}

is tight in
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))
, for
p>2
. By Prokhorov’s Theorem (see
[
3
, Section 5]
), we find that for any sequence
\epsilon_{m}\to 0,\ v_{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
and
\|\xi_{0,m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N
, there exists a subsequence (denoted by
(\epsilon_{m},v_{m},\xi_{0,m})
) such that
I_{3,\epsilon_{m}}(t,x)
converges in distribution. Now, we multiply by the subsequence
\sqrt{\epsilon_{m}}\downarrow 0
, then it is obvious that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\epsilon_{m}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{3,\epsilon_{m}}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon_{m}}}\Rightarrow 0\ \ \text{ as }\ \ m\to\infty,

where “
\Rightarrow
” stands for convergence in distribution. Thus, the original sequence

\sqrt{\epsilon}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{3,\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}\Rightarrow 0\ \text{ as }\ \epsilon\to 0.

As we can see that the sequential limit is zero, which is a constant, therefore the sequence converges in probability also, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sqrt{\epsilon}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|I_{3,\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}>\frac{\delta}{C}\bigg)=0.

Due to arbitrary choice of
\epsilon_{m}\downarrow 0,\ v_{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
, and
\|\xi_{0,m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N
, we deduce

(5.20)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))}>\delta\bigg)\leq\varrho.

The choice of
\varrho
is arbitrary, hence (
5.10
) follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.7
.

Combining Theorem
5.3
and Corollary
5.11
, we obtain the required result.
∎

Let us now move to the proof of Theorem
5.8
, that is, ULDP in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
topology. For that, we need the following intermediate result.

Lemma 5.12
.

For any
\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N
, the family

\displaystyle\{\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}:v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M},\epsilon\in(0,1]\}

is tight in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
.

Proof.

From (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(t,x)-

\displaystyle\xi_{v}^{0}(t,x)=:I_{1,\epsilon}+I_{2,\epsilon}+\sqrt{\epsilon}I_{3,\epsilon}.

Using similar arguments as in (
5.2
), we obtain

(5.21)

\displaystyle\|I_{1,\epsilon}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}+\|\xi_{v}^{0}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\big)^{2}\mathrm{d}s.

From Corollary
5.10
, we conclude that the terms appearing in the right hand side of the estimate (
5.21
) are uniformly bounded in probability over the bounded subsets of initial conditions and for all
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. Therefore, by
[
29
, Corollary 3.2]
, for all
v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, the family

\displaystyle\{I_{1,\epsilon}:\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N\}

are tight in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. Using
[
29
, Corollary 3.6]
, we have

\displaystyle\{I_{3,\epsilon}:\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N,\ v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M},\ \epsilon\in(0,1]\}

is tight in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
.

We consider the penultimate term
I_{2,\epsilon}
and estimate it using
[
29
, Lemma 3.1(iv)]
(cf.
[
45
, Lemma 2]
), with
\gamma=2,\ q>\frac{4}{3},\ p=\frac{q}{q-1},\ \kappa_{p}=1-\frac{1}{q}
and any
\beta\in\big(0,\beta_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\big)
, where
\beta_{0}=\min\big\{2-\frac{2}{q},1\big\},
for
x,x+y\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
,
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle|I_{2,\epsilon}(t,x)-I_{2,\epsilon}(t,x+y)|

\displaystyle\leq C|y|^{\beta}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{v}^{0}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}}^{2}|v(s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CM|y|^{\beta}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{v}^{0}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}}.

Similarly, the Hölder’s continuity in time
t
follows form
[
29
, Lemma 3.1(ii)]
. For any
q>2
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{2,\epsilon}(t)-I_{2,\epsilon}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C|t-s|^{\alpha}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{v}^{0}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}}^{2}|v(s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CM|t-s|^{\alpha}\sup_{ts\in[0<T]}\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}(s)-\xi_{v}^{0}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}}.

For large enough
q
, we observe that
I_{3,\epsilon}
is Hölder’s continuous in both space and time variables and the Hölder norm is uniformly bounded in probability over
\epsilon\in(0,1],\ v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
and
\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N
. Therefore, the family
\{I_{2,\epsilon}:\ \|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N,\ \epsilon\in(0,1],\ v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}\}
is also tight in the space
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
.
∎

Theorem 5.13

(Convergence in probability in supremum norm)
.

For any
T>0,\ \delta>0,\ N>0
and
M>0

(5.22)

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}}\sup_{v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})}>\delta\bigg)=0.

Proof.

In Lemma
5.12
, we established that for any
\|\xi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N
, the family

\displaystyle\{\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}:v\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M},\epsilon\in(0,1]\}

is tight in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})
. Again, by Prokhorov’s Theorem (see
[
3
, Section 5]
), we obtain that for any sequence
\epsilon_{m}\to 0,\ v_{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
and
\|\xi_{0,m}\|_{\mathrm{C}({\mathbb{T}^{2}})}\leq N
, there exists a subsequence (denoted by
(\epsilon_{m},v_{m},\xi_{0,m})
) such that
\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}
converges in distribution. Let
\xi^{*}
be the limit.

Since we have the embedding
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))
, the above convergence gives
\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}\Rightarrow\xi^{*}
, in the space
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{2}}))
topology also. Therefore, Corollary
5.11
yields

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\xi^{*}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}=0,

with probability 1 which implies that
\|\xi^{*}\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})}=0
with probability 1. Since
\|\xi_{v}^{\epsilon}-\xi_{v}^{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{2}})}
is converging to
0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to 0}\mathbb{P}\big(\|\xi_{v_{m}}^{\epsilon_{m}}-\xi_{v}^{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}([0,T];{\mathbb{T}^{2}})}>\delta\big)=0.

Due to arbitrary choice of
\epsilon_{m}\downarrow 0,\ v_{m}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
, and
\|\xi_{0,m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N
, we obtain the required result (
5.22
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.8
.

Combining Theorems
5.3
and
5.13
, we obtain the required result.
∎

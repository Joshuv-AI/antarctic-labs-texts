Effective codescent morphisms of n n -quasigroups and n n -loops
Dali Zangurashvili

Abstract
. Effective codescent morphisms of n n -quasigroups and of n n -loops are characterized. To this end, it is proved that, for any n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , every codescent morphism of n n -quasigroups (resp. n n -loops) is effective. This statement generalizes our earlier results on qusigroups and loops. Moreover, it is shown that the elements of the amalgamated free products of n n -quasigroups (resp. n n -loops) have unique normal forms, and that the varieties of n n -quasigroups and n n -loops satisfy the strong amalgamation property. The latter two statements generalize the corresponding old results on quasigroups and loops by Evans. Key words and phrases : effective codescent morphism; n n -quasigroup; n n -loop; normal form of an element of the amalgamated free product; strong amalgamation property. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 18E50, 18C20, 20N15, 20N05, 08B25, 68Q42.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
Q
be a finite 1-quasigroup. Then any
\{f\}
-congruence on
Q
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-congruence.

Proof.

Let
\theta
be an
\{f\}
-congruence on
Q
. Let
C_{a}
denote the
\theta
-class of an element
a\in Q
. One obviously has

f(C_{a})\subseteq C_{f(a)}.

(3.2)

Inequality (3.2) implies that we have the increasing chain of natural numbers:

card(C_{a})\leq card(C_{f(a)})\leq card(C_{f^{2}(a)})\leq...

Since
Q
is finite, this chain stabilizes at some step
k\geq 0
(assuming that
f^{0}=id_{Q}
). Inequality (3.2) implies that, for any
m\geq k
, we have

f(C_{f^{m}(a)})=C_{f^{m+1}(a)}.

(3.3)

Assume now that
k\geq 1
. Since
Q
is finite and no distinct
\theta
-classes have non-empty intersection, (3.3) implies that there is the smallest
l\geq k
with

f(C_{f^{l}(a)})=C_{f^{l}(a)}.

Since
l\geq 1
and
f
is a bijection, we arrive to the contradiction. Therefore,
k=0
, and we have

f(C_{a})=C_{f(a)}.

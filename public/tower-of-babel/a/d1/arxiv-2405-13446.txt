Effective gonality theorem on weight-one syzygies of algebraic curves
Wenbo Niu, Jinhyung Park

Abstract
. In 1986, Green–Lazarsfeld raised the gonality conjecture asserting that the gonality gon ⁡ ( C ) \gon(C) of a smooth projective curve C C of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 can be read off from weight-one syzygies of a sufficiently positive line bundle L L on C C , and also proposed possible least degree of such a line bundle. In 2015, Ein–Lazarsfeld proved the conjecture when deg ⁡ L \deg L is sufficiently large, but the effective part of the conjecture remained widely open and was reformulated explicitly by Farkas–Kemeny. In this paper, we establish an effective vanishing theorem for weight-one syzygies, which implies that the gonality conjecture holds if deg ⁡ L ≥ 2 ​ g + gon ⁡ ( C ) \deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C) or deg ⁡ L = 2 ​ g + gon ⁡ ( C ) − 1 \deg L=2g+\gon(C)-1 and C C is not a plane curve. As Castryck observed that the gonality conjecture may not hold for a plane curve when deg ⁡ L = 2 ​ g + gon ⁡ ( C ) − 1 \deg L=2g+\gon(C)-1 , our theorem is the best possible and thus gives a complete answer to the gonality conjecture.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over the field
\mathbf{C}
of complex numbers. Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
, and
B
and
L
be line bundles on
C
. Suppose that
L
is globally generated, and write
S:=\Sym H^{0}(C,L)
for the polynomial ring as the symmetric algebra of the vector space
H^{0}(C,L)
. The associated section module

R(C,B;L):=\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbf{Z}}H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q})

is a finitely generated graded
S
-module and admits a minimal graded free resolution over
S
:

\cdots\longrightarrow E_{p}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow E_{2}\longrightarrow E_{1}\longrightarrow E_{0}\longrightarrow R(C,B;L)\longrightarrow 0.

Each graded free
S
-module
E_{p}
in the resolution has the form

E_{p}=\bigoplus_{q\in\mathbf{Z}}K_{p,q}(C,B;L)\otimes_{\mathbf{C}}S(-p-q),

where
K_{p,q}(C,B;L)
is the
Koszul cohomology
defined as the cohomology at the middle of the Koszul-type complex

\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q-1})\longrightarrow\wedge^{p}H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q})\longrightarrow\wedge^{p-1}H^{0}(L)\otimes H^{0}(B\otimes L^{q+1}).

We may regard
K_{p,q}(C,B;L)
as the space of
p
-th syzygies of weight
q
. For more details on Koszul cohomology, we refer the reader to the pioneering work
[
13
,
14
]
of Green.

One of the most interesting cases is when
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}
, in which case we simply write the associated section module as
R(C;L)
and its Koszul cohomology groups as
K_{p,q}(C;L)
. It is elementary to see that
K_{i,0}(C;L)=0
if and only if
i\geq 1
, and if
L
is nonspecial, i.e.,
H^{1}(C,L)=0
, then
K_{i,j}(C;L)=0
for
j\geq 3
. Thus there are basically only two nontrivial weights for syzygies:
K_{i,1}(C;L)
(weight-one) and
K_{i,2}(C;L)
(weight-two). There has been a great deal of interests in the past decades to understand syzygies of algebraic curves with considerable effort on weight one and two especially. It turns out that they often reveal surprising connection between the intrinsic/extrinsic geometry and algebraic properties of the curves. Among others, generalizing classical results of Castelnuovo, Mattuck, Mumford, Saint-Donat, Fujita on equations defining algebraic curves, Green
[
13
, Theorem 4.a.1]
proved his celebrated
(2g+1+p)
-theorem:
if
\deg L\geq 2g+1+p
, then
K_{i,2}(C;L)=0
for
0\leq i\leq p
.
Green–Lazarsfeld
[
16
]
classified the cases of
K_{p+1,2}(C;L)\neq 0
when
\deg L=2g+1+p
.
Geometrically, as
L
is very ample, it gives an embedding

C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,L)=\mathbf{P}^{r},~~\text{ where }~~r=r(L):=h^{0}(C,L)-1,

and Green’s theorem in particular means that
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}^{r}
is projectively normal when
\deg L\geq 2g+1
and the defining ideal
I_{C|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
is generated by quadrics when
\deg L\geq 2g+2
.

It is natural to further ask what one can expect for weight-one syzygies
K_{i,1}(C;L)
in the setting of Green’s theorem. To continue our discussion leaving out the trivial cases, we assume
g\geq 2
since for
g=0
or
1
the minimal graded free resolution of
R(C;L)
can be explicitly computed. Note that
K_{0,1}(C;L)=0
and the dimension of
K_{1,1}(C;L)
is the number of minimal quadric generators of
I_{C|\mathbf{P}^{r}}
. For the rest part in weight-one syzygies, Green–Lazarsfeld’s nonvanishing theorem
[
13
, Appendix]
implies that

if
\deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C)-2
, then
K_{r(L)-\gon(C),1}(C;L)\neq 0
and hence
K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0
for
1\leq p\leq r(L)-\gon(C)
.
Recall that
\gon(C)
is the
gonality
of the curve
C
defined as the minimal degree of a branched covering
C\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
.
The important issue here is whether weight-one syzygies actually vanish beyond the range above, and Green–Lazarsfeld’s gonality conjecture
[
15
, Conjecture 3.7]
predicted that this is exactly the case. Precisely, in 1986, Green–Lazarsfeld conjectured that if
\deg L\gg 0
, then

(1.1)

K_{p,1}(C;L)=0~~\text{ for $p\geq r(L)-\gon(C)+1$}.

The conjecture was first verified for the case of very small value of gonality
[
13
,
21
]
or for a general curve of each gonality
[
3
,
4
]
. In 2015, Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
10
]
made a breakthrough by fully proving the gonality conjecture when
\deg L\gg 0
. However, the last piece of the whole puzzle, the effective gonality problem seeking an effective sharp bound for
\deg L
to satisfy (
1.1
), has remained widely open since then. Indeed, Green–Lazarsfeld
[
15
, page 87]
originally proposed that
\deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C)-1
would suffice for (
1.1
). Farkas–Kemeny
[
12
, page 3]
explicitly conjectured that this would be the case when
g\geq 4
, and verified their conjecture for general curves of each gonality. However, Castryck
[
6
]
pointed out that Farkas–Kemeny’s effective gonality conjecture is too optimistic: if
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(CH)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve and
L:=\omega_{C}\otimes H
, then
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-1
but
K_{r(L)-\gon(C)+1,1}(C;L)\neq 0
. Moreover, Farkas–Kemeny noticed that if
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
, where
\xi
is any effective divisor of degree
\gon(C)
, then
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-2
but
K_{r(L)-\gon(C)+1,1}(C;L)\neq 0
. In somewhat slightly different flavor, Rathmann
[
18
]
showed that the gonality conjecture holds when
\deg L\geq 4g-3
, and noticed an example of a plane quartic curve for which the degree bound cannot be improved to
4g-4
. This is actually the only exception for
\deg L=4g-4
when
L
is very ample, as discussed in details in
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we prove the following effective gonality theorem, which completely resolves the effective gonality problem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
.
If
\deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C)-2
, then

K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~1\leq p\leq r(L)-\gon(C)

except for the following two cases:

(1)

C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
\geq 4
, and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
. In this case,
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-1
.

(2)

C
is arbitrary, and
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for an effective divisor
\xi
of degree
\gon(C)
with
\dim|\xi|=1
. In this case,
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-2
.

In the exceptional cases, we have

K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~1\leq p\leq r(L)-\gon(C)+1.

The theorem unifies and clarifies all the previously known results on the gonality conjecture. It includes all aforementioned exceptional examples observed by Castryk and Farkas–Kemeny, and says that the desired vanishing (
1.1
) of
K_{p,1}(C;L)
holds without any exceptional cases as soon as
\deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C)
. It is interesting to point out that Castryck’s example is the only exception for Green–Lazarsfeld’s original expectation and Farkas–Kemeny’s effective gonality conjecture, and Farkas–Kemeny’s example is the only exception for the gonality conjecture when
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-2
.

As remarked by Green–Lazarsfeld
[
15
, page 87]
, one certainly wants a bound on the degree of
L
independent of
\gon(C)
for the gonality conjecture. Since
g\geq\gon(C)
, Theorem
1.1
can be applied to the case when
\deg L\geq 3g-2
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
. If
\deg L\geq 3g-2
, then

K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~1\leq p\leq r(L)-\gon(C)

except for the following two cases:

(1)

C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane quartic curve and
L=\omega_{C}^{2}
or
L=\omega_{C}^{2}(-x)
for a point
x\in C
. In this case,
g=3
, and
\deg L=3g-1=8
or
\deg L=3g-2=7
.

(2)

C
is a curve of genus
g=2
and
L=\omega_{C}^{2}
. In this case,
\deg L=3g-2=4
.

One can compute the whole Betti tables of the exceptional cases in Corollary
1.2
. Notice that there is no exception when
\deg L\geq 3g
. Thus one can read off
\gon(C)
from the minimal graded free resolution of
R(C;L)
for one line bundle
L
of degree
3g
. This was also expected by Green–Lazarsfeld
[
15
, page 87]
. On the other hand, Green’s
(2g+1+p)
-theorem is sharp: Green–Lazarsfeld
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
showed that
K_{p+1,2}(C;L)\neq 0
when
\deg L=2g+1+p
and
H^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
. In other words,

K_{p,2}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~r(L)-g\leq p\leq r(L)-1.

The required conditions automatically hold as soon as
\deg L\geq 3g-2
. Thus our result completes determining the vanishing and nonvanishing of all Koszul cohomology
K_{p,q}(C;L)
and thereby the overall shape of the minimal graded free resolution of
R(C;L)
when
\deg L\geq 3g-2
.

Theorem
1.1
follows from a more general effective vanishing theorem for weight-one syzygies of the module
R(C,B;L)
. Indeed, by the duality theorem (cf.
[
13
, Theorem 2.c.1]
), we have

K_{i,1}(C;L)=K_{r(L)-i-1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)^{\vee}.

It is well-known that
\gon(C)-2\geq p
if and only if
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample. Thus the gonality conjecture can be restated as that
if
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample, then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
.
Recall that a line bundle
B
on
C
is said to be
p
-very ample
if the restriction map

H^{0}(C,B)\longrightarrow H^{0}(\xi,B|_{\xi})

is surjective for every effective divisor
\xi
of degree
p+1
(in other words,
\xi
imposes independent conditions on the global sections of
B
). From this perspective, to prove the gonality conjecture, Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
10
, Theorem B]
actually established the vanishing
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
when
B
is
p
-very ample and
\deg L
is sufficiently large. Soon after, Rathmann
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
further showed that this vanishing holds if
h^{1}(C,L)=h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=0
. In this paper, we prove the following effective vanishing theorem for weight-one syzygies, which significantly improves those vanishing results and from which the effective gonality theorem follows as a consequence.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
, and
B
and
L
be line bundles on
C
. Assume that
B
is
p
-very ample. If

(1.2)

h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1,

then
L
is
p
-very ample and
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
.

Our approach to prove the theorem is to conduct computation of the Koszul cohomology
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
on the symmetric product
C_{p+1}
of the curve
C
. This idea was introduced by Voisin
[
19
,
20
]
and then extensively exploited by Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
10
]
, Rathmann
[
18
]
, and many others in the study of syzygies of algebraic curves
[
2
,
9
]
and their secant varieties
[
7
,
11
]
. Specifically, there is a tautological bundle
E_{p+1,B}
on
C_{p+1}
whose fiber over an effective divisor
\xi\in C_{p+1}
of degree
p+1
is
H^{0}(\xi,B|_{\xi})
and whose global sections
H^{0}(C_{p+1},E_{p+1,B})=H^{0}(C,B)
. The
p
-very ampleness of
B
gives rise to a surjective evaluation map

\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B}\colon H^{0}(C,B)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\longrightarrow E_{p+1,B}.

We can define the kernel bundle
M_{p+1,B}
as the kernel of
\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B}
. On the other hand, there is a line bundle
N_{p+1,L}=\det E_{p+1,L}
on
C_{p+1}
associated to
L
with
H^{0}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L})=\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(C,L)
. The key observation made by Voisin (see e.g.,
[
10
, Lemma 1.1]
) is that
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
if and only if the multiplication map

(1.3)

H^{0}(C,B)\otimes H^{0}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L})\longrightarrow H^{0}(C_{p+1},E_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})

is surjective. The surjectivity of the multiplication map follows from the cohomology vanishing

(1.4)

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0.

If
L
is sufficiently positive as in the asymptotic situation treated by Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
10
]
, then
N_{p+1,L}
is also sufficiently positive so that Fujita–Serre vanishing theorem immediately yields the desired cohomology vanishing (
1.4
). However, making the vanishing effectively becomes much more subtle, and one really needs to develop a more sophisticated technique.

Our argument to prove Theorem
1.3
is summarized as follows.
First, we realize the dual to the above cohomology (
1.4
) as a term

E_{2}^{p,0}=H^{p}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{r(B)-p-1}M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1}})

of the Leray spectral sequence for the projection
\pr_{2}\colon C_{r(B)-p-1}\times C_{p+1}\to C_{p+1}
associated to a line bundle
(N_{r(B)-p-1,B}\boxtimes N_{p+1,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1}})(-D_{r(B)-p-1,p+1})
, where

D_{r(B)-p-1,p+1}:=\{(\xi,\eta)\in C_{r(B)-p-1}\times C_{p+1}\mid\Supp(\xi)\cap\Supp(\eta)\neq\emptyset\}

is an effective divisor on
C_{r(B)-p-1}\times C_{p+1}
. If
E_{2}^{p,0}=E_{\infty}^{p,0}
and
E^{p}=0
, then
E_{2}^{p,0}=0
. The required conditions can be deduced from the cohomology vanishing of line bundles

H^{i}(C_{j}\times C_{p+1},(N_{j,B}\boxtimes N_{p+1,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1}})(-D_{j,p+1}))=0

for
0\leq j\leq r(B)-p-1
and
0\leq i\leq p
. Next, we realize the dual to this cohomology as the converging term

E^{j+p+1-i}=H^{j+p+1-i}(C_{j}\times C_{p+1},(S_{j,\omega_{C}\otimes B^{-1}}\boxtimes S_{p+1,L\otimes B^{-1}})(D_{j,p+1}))

of the Leray spectral sequence for the projection
\pr_{1}\colon C_{j}\times C_{p+1}\to C_{j}
. A pleasant geometric consequence of the condition (
1.2
) is that

\dim\underbrace{\{\xi\in C_{j}\mid h^{1}(C,(L\otimes B^{-1})(\xi))\geq\ell\}}_{=:\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\ell}(\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})}\leq p+j-i-\ell~~\text{ for $\ell\geq 1$}.

As
\Supp\big(R^{\ell}\pr_{1,*}(S_{j,\omega_{C}\otimes B^{-1}}\boxtimes S_{p+1,L\otimes B^{-1}})(D_{j,p+1})\big)\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{j}^{\ell}(\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})
, we get
E_{2}^{j+p+1-i-\ell,\ell}=0
and hence
E^{j+p+1-i}=0
.

Bearing in mind the multiplication map (
1.3
), one may think of Theorem
1.3
as a far-reaching generalization of Green’s
H^{0}
-Lemma (
[
13
, Theorem 4.e.1]
), which is just
p=0
case of our theorem. As a consequence of
H^{0}
-Lemma, Green
[
13
, Corollary 4.e.4]
obtained that if

\deg L\geq 2g+1~~\text{ and }~~\deg B+\deg L\geq 4g+2,

then
K_{0,1}(C,B;L)=0
. Based on the method of Green–Lazarsfeld
[
15
]
for projective normality of algebraic curves, Butler
[
5
, Theorem 2]
and Pareschi
[
17
, Theorem 3 in Appendix]
significantly improve Green’s result: roughly speaking, under some additional hypothesis on
B
and
L
, if

\deg B+\deg L\geq 4g+1-2h^{1}(C,B)-2h^{1}(C,L)-\Cliff(C),

then
K_{0,1}(C,B;L)=0
. Here
\Cliff(C)
is the
Clifford index
of
C
defined by the minimum of

\Cliff(A):=\deg A-2h^{0}(C,A)+2

for a line bundle
A
on
C
with
h^{0}(C,A)\geq 2
and
h^{1}(C,A)\geq 2
(if
g=2
or
g=3
, there is no such a line bundle
A
, so we set
\Cliff(C):=\Cliff(\omega_{C})=0
).
In this spirit, we show the following theorem for vanishing and nonvanishing of weight-one syzygies using Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
B
and
L
be line bundles on
C
. Assume that
B
is
p
-very ample and
L
is globally generated. If

\deg L\geq 2g+p+1-h^{1}(C,B)~~\text{ and }~~\deg B+\deg L\geq 4g+2p-2h^{1}(C,B)-\Cliff(C),

then
K_{i,1}(C,B;L)=0
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
and
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
except for the following cases:

(1)

|B|
is a base point free pencil
(p=0)
and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=1
. In this case,
K_{0,1}(C,B;L)=H^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\neq 0
.

(2)

C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,B)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
\geq 4

(p=1)
and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=1
. In this case,
K_{0,1}(C,B;L)=0
, and
K_{1,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
.

(3)

\deg L=2g+p+h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-h^{1}(C,B)-1
,
\deg B+\deg L=4g+2p-2h^{1}(C,B)-\Cliff(C)
, and
L\otimes B^{-1}
computes
\Cliff(C)
. If
B=\omega_{C}
, then
\gon(C)=p+2
and either
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)
is a plane curve of degree
p+3\geq 5
and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H

(\deg L=2g+p+1)
or
C
is arbitrary with
g\geq 4
and
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)

(\deg L=2g+p)
for an effective divisor
\xi
of degree
p+2
with
\dim|\xi|=1
. In this case,
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
.

If furthermore
B
is not
(p+1)
-very ample and
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
, then
K_{p+1,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
.

This theorem almost contains the effective gonality theorem (Theorem
1.1
) as a special case when
B=\omega_{C}
and fully recovers Green’s
(2g+1+p)
-theorem when
B=L
as
K_{p,2}(C;L)=K_{p,1}(C,L;L)
. Thus this somewhat technical-looking theorem provides a unified and strengthened statement on syzygies of algebraic curves. Moreover, a conjectural variant –
e.g., if
B
is
p
-very ample and
L
is
(p+1)
-very ample with
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1-2h^{1}(C,B)-\Cliff(C)
, and

\deg B+\deg L\geq 4g+2p+2-2h^{1}(C,B)-2h^{1}(C,L)-2\Cliff(C),

then
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
except for a few cases
– would solve Green’s conjecture
[
13
, Conjecture 5.1]
and the Green–Lazarsfeld secant conjecture
[
15
, Conjecture 3.4]
.

This paper is organized as follows. We begin in Section
2
with collecting relevant facts on symmetric products of algebraic curves. Section
3
is devoted to the proof of our effective vanishing theorem for weight-one syzygies (Theorem
1.3
). After discussing effective nonvanishing results for weight-one syzygies in Section
4
, we finally establish Theorem
1.4
and the effective gonality theorem (Theorem
1.1
) in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries on symmetric products of algebraic curves

In this section, we prepare the necessary techniques relating Koszul cohomology to vector bundles on symmetric products of algebraic curves. For more detailed discussion along this direction, we refer the reader to
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
.

Let
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
. For an integer
m\geq 0
, denote by
C_{m}
the
m
-th symmetric product of
C
, which parameterizes effective divisors of degree
m
on
C
. It is well-known that
C_{m}
is a smooth projective variety of dimension
m
. Now, for integers
m,n\geq 1
, there is an addition map

\sigma_{m,n}\colon C_{m}\times C_{n}\longrightarrow C_{m+n},~~(\xi,\xi^{\prime})\longmapsto\xi+\xi^{\prime},

which is a finite morphism.
The incidence subvariety
D_{m,n}
in the product
C_{m}\times C_{n}
is an effective divisor defined to be the image of the map

j\colon C_{m-1}\times C_{n-1}\times C\longrightarrow C_{m}\times C_{n},~~(\xi,\xi^{\prime},x)\longmapsto(\xi+x,\xi^{\prime}+x).

As a set,
D_{m,n}=\{(\xi,\eta)\in C_{m}\times C_{n}\mid\Supp(\xi)\cap\Supp(\eta)\neq\emptyset\}
.

Consider the special case when
n=1
. In this case,
C_{m-1}\times C=D_{m,1}\subseteq C_{m}\times C
is the universal family over
C_{m}
, as indicated in the following diagram

\textstyle{D_{m,1}=C_{m-1}\times C\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sigma_{m-1,1}}
\scriptstyle{j}
\textstyle{C_{m}\times C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pr_{1}}
\textstyle{C_{m}}

in which
\pr_{1}
is the projection map. Given a line bundle
L
on
C
, the
tautological bundle
associated to
L
is a vector bundle of rank
m
on
C_{m}
defined by

E_{m,L}:=\sigma_{m-1,1,*}(\mathscr{O}_{C_{m-1}}\boxtimes L).

Note that
H^{0}(C,E_{m,L})=H^{0}(C,L)
.
Next, we define a line bundle

N_{m,L}:=\det(E_{m,L}).

There is a divisor
\delta_{m}
on
C_{m}
such that
\mathscr{O}_{C_{m}}(-\delta_{m})=N_{m,\mathscr{O}_{C}}
. Note that
\sigma_{m-1,1}^{*}\delta_{m}=D_{m-1,1}\subseteq C_{m-1}\times C
. Since
E_{m,\omega_{C}}
is the cotangent bundle of
C_{m}
, we have
\omega_{C_{m}}=N_{m,\omega_{C}}
.
Now, consider a line bundle
S_{m,L}
in
C_{m}
which is the invariant descent of the
m
-fold box product

L^{\boxtimes m}:=\underbrace{L\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L}_{\text{$m$ times}}

from the
m
-th Cartesian product
C^{m}
to the
m
-th symmetric product
C_{m}
under the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. We have
N_{m,L}=S_{m,L}(-\delta_{m})
. The line bundles
N_{m,L}
and
S_{m,L}
are playing critical roles in the computation of Koszul cohomology on the symmetric products of
C
. Fortunately, the cohomology of these two line bundles are well understood as summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
,
[
11
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

For any
i\geq 0
, we have

H^{i}(C_{m},N_{m,L})=\wedge^{m-i}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,L)~\text{ and }~H^{i}(C_{m},S_{m,L})=S^{m-i}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes\wedge^{i}H^{1}(C,L).

Given a line bundle
B
on
C
, the fiber of
E_{p+1,B}
over
\xi\in C_{p+1}
is
E\otimes k(\xi)=H^{0}(\xi,B|_{\xi})
. Thus the restriction map
H^{0}(C,B)\rightarrow H^{0}(\xi,B|_{\xi})
is globalized to an evaluation map

\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B}\colon H^{0}(C,B)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\longrightarrow E_{p+1,B}.

Note that
B
is
p
-very ample if and only if
\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B}
is surjective if and only if
E_{p+1,B}
is globally generated. Following Voisin
[
19
,
20
]
and Ein–Lazarsfeld
[
10
]
, we briefly explain how one can carry out the computation of the Koszul cohomology
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
on
C_{p+1}
. Formally, consider the Koszul-type complex

\wedge^{p+1}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes H^{0}(C,B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{p+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\wedge^{p}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes H^{0}(C,B\otimes L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{p}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\wedge^{p-1}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes H^{0}(C,B\otimes L^{2}).

The Koszul cohomology group
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
can be defined as the quotient
\Ker(d_{p})/\uIm(d_{p+1})
. Suppose now that
B
is
p
-very ample so that the evaluation map
\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B}
is surjective. Let
M_{p+1,B}:=\ker(\operatorname{ev}_{p+1,B})
be the kernel bundle, which fits into a short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\longrightarrow M_{p+1,B}\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,B)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\longrightarrow E_{p+1,B}\longrightarrow 0.

The key observation due to Voisin (see e.g.,
[
10
, Lemma 1.1]
) is that the multiplication map
m_{p+1}
in the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,B)\otimes H^{0}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m_{p+1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{0}(C_{p+1},E_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})

derived by taking cohomology of (
2.1
) is exactly the Koszul differential map
d_{p+1}
and the space
H^{0}(C_{p+1},E_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})
is exactly the space
\Ker(d_{p})
.
Thus
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
is the cokernel of the multiplication map
m_{p+1}
, or equivalently one has the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K_{p,1}(C,B;L)\longrightarrow H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})\longrightarrow H^{0}(C,B)\otimes H^{1}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L}).

In particular, if
H^{1}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L})=0
(e.g., this holds when
H^{1}(C,L)=0
by Lemma
2.1
), then

K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})
.
In general, we obtain the following lemma, which will be used to prove our effective vanishing theorem for weight-one syzygies (Theorem
1.3
).

Lemma 2.2

(
[
10
, Lemma 1.1]
)
.

Assume that
B
is a
p
-very ample line bundle and
L
is a line bundle on
C
. If
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0
, then
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
.

The vanishing of
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})
implies the vanishing of
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
but the converse needs not be true. Under suitable conditions, one may assume
H^{1}(C,L)=0
and hence
H^{1}(C_{p+1},N_{p+1,L})=0
, so
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0
if and only if
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
. However, roughly speaking, as it is hard to control the kernel bundle
M_{p+1,B}
, an effective result for
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0
is usually quite difficult to obtain directly. The next lemma will serve our purpose to manage the kernel bundle
M_{p+1,B}
and overcome such difficulty. It provides a way to lift a vector bundle of the form
\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}
on
C_{p+1}
to a line bundle of the form
(\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})
on
C_{p+1}\times C_{n}
, and therefore successfully enables us to transfer the vanishing of cohomology of vector bundle
\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L}
to the vanishing of cohomology of line bundle
(N_{p+1,L}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})
.

Lemma 2.3

(cf.
[
7
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Assume that
B
is a
p
-very ample line bundle on
C
. Let
\pr_{1}\colon C_{p+1}\times C_{n}\to C_{p+1}
be the projection map.
Then there is a natural isomorphism

R^{i}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)\cong\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)~~\text{ for $i\geq 0$}.

Proof.

Notice that for
\xi\in C_{p+1}
, the restriction of the sheaf
(\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})
to the fiber
\pr^{-1}_{1}(\xi)\cong C_{n}
is
N_{n,B(-\xi)}
. By Lemma
2.1
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}H^{i}(C_{n},N_{n,B(-\xi)})&=&\wedge^{n-i}H^{0}(C,B(-\xi))\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B(-\xi))\\
&\subseteq&\wedge^{n-i}H^{0}(C,B)\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)\,=\,H^{i}(C_{n},N_{n,B}).\end{array}

As
B
is
p
-very ample, both
h^{0}(C,B(-\xi))
and
h^{1}(C,B(-\xi))
are constants independent on the choice of
\xi
and in addition
H^{1}(C,B(-\xi))=H^{1}(C,B)
. By the base change, there is a natural isomorphism

u^{i}_{\xi}\colon R^{i}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)\otimes\mathbf{C}(\xi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}(C_{n},N_{n,B(-\xi)}).

Now, push down the inclusion map

\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B}(-D_{p+1,n})\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B}

by
\pr_{1}
to yield

\delta^{i}\colon R^{i}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)\longrightarrow\underbrace{H^{i}(C_{p+1},N_{n,B})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}}_{\mathclap{=\wedge^{n-i}H^{0}(C,B)\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}}}~~\text{for $i\geq 0$}.

Tensoring
\delta^{i}
with the residue field
\mathbf{C}(\xi)
for each point
\xi\in C_{p+1}
induces a map

\delta^{i}_{\xi}\colon R^{i}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)\otimes\mathbf{C}(\xi)\longrightarrow\wedge^{n-i}H^{0}(C,B)\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)

which factors through the map
u^{i}_{\xi}
and has the image
H^{i}(C_{n},N_{n,B(-\xi)})
.
On the other hand, the short exact sequence (
2.1
) induces an inclusion

\alpha^{i}\colon\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\wedge^{n-i}H^{0}(C,B)\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}.

We claim that the map
\delta^{i}
factors through
\alpha^{i}
. Indeed, for
\xi\in C_{p+1}
, we have

\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)\otimes\mathbf{C}(\xi)=\wedge^{n-i}H^{0}(C,B(-\xi))\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,,B)=H^{i}(C_{n},N_{n,B(-\xi)}).

This means that

\delta^{i}_{\xi}\Big(R^{i}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)\otimes\mathbf{C}(\xi)\Big)=\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)\otimes\mathbf{C}(\xi).

Thus
\delta^{i}
maps
R^{i}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{O}_{C_{p+1}}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)
surjectively onto
\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes S^{i}H^{1}(C,B)
, and it must be an isomorphism since both are vector bundles of the same rank.
∎

3.
Effective vanishing for weight-one syzygies of algebraic curves

We continue to assume that
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
and
B,L
are line bundles on
C
. This section is devoted to proving Theorem
1.3
. For this purpose, we develop a general technique to show the vanishing of the cohomology groups of the form
H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})
. The basic idea is that such vanishing can be transferred to the vanishing of certain cohomology groups of the sheaves
N_{p+1,L}\boxtimes N_{p+1,B}(-D_{p+1,n})
on
C_{p+1}\times C_{n}
. Then these vanishing are controlled by the dimensions of suitable loci in the space
C_{n}
and eventually can be controlled by the dimension of global sections of
L
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
B
be a
p
-very ample line bundle on
C
, and
\mathscr{E}
be a locally free sheaf on
C_{p+1}
. For integers
m,n\geq 0
, we have

H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})=0

provided that the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

H^{m}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n},(\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n}))=0
.

(2)

H^{m-j}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n-i},(\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n-i,B})(-D_{p+1,n-i}))=0
for all
i,j
with
1\leq i\leq m-1
and
i+1\leq j\leq 2i
.

Proof.

Write
\pr_{1}\colon C_{p+1}\times C_{n}\rightarrow C_{p+1}
for the projection map. By Lemma
2.3
, pushing down the sheaf
(\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})
by
\pr_{1}
yields

R^{j}\pr_{1,*}\big((\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})\big)=S^{j}H^{1}(C,B)\otimes\wedge^{n-j}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E}~~\text{ for $j\geq 0$},

and gives rise to a Leray spectral sequence

E^{i,j}_{2}:=S^{j}H^{1}(C,B)\otimes H^{i}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n-j}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})

converging to

E^{i+j}:=H^{i+j}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n},\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n,B}(-D_{p+1,n})).

To prove the proposition, we just need to show
E^{m,0}_{2}=0
.
From the convergence of the spectral sequence, we observe that the vanishing
E^{m,0}_{2}=0
holds if
E^{m}=0
and
E^{m,0}_{2}=E^{m,0}_{\infty}
. The former condition
E^{m}=0
is nothing but the one given in
(1)
, while the latter condition
E^{m,0}_{2}=E^{m,0}_{\infty}
is implied by the vanishing

E^{m-2,1}_{2}=E^{m-3,2}_{2}=\cdots=E^{0,m-1}_{2}=0.

Thus we reduce the problem to showing that

H^{m-1-i}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})=0~~\text{ for $1\leq i\leq m-1$}.

To this end, for each
i
, we argue exactly in the same way above by utilizing the Leray spectral sequence for pushing down of the sheaf
(\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n-i,B})(-D_{p+1,n-i})
by the projection map
\pr_{1}\colon C_{p+1}\times C_{n-i}\to C_{p+1}
. It turns out that the vanishing of
H^{m-1-i}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n-i}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})
can be reduced to showing the vanishing

H^{m-2-k}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n-k}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})=0~~\text{ for $i+1\leq k\leq m-2$}

and the vanishing

H^{m-1-i}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n-i},\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n-i,B}(-D_{p+1,n-i}))=0.

But the latter one is already given in condition
(2)
for
j=i+1
.
So if we continue to repeat the above arguments to handle vanishing for all the terms of the form
\wedge^{*}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E}
created in the process, eventually we are able to transfer all required cohomology vanishing on
C_{p+1}
to the ones exactly given in condition
(2)
.
Therefore, we conclude
H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})=0
as desired.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

(1) When
m=0
or
1
in Proposition
3.1
, the second vanishing hypothesis becomes empty so that the vanishing condition
H^{m}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n},\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n,B}(-D_{p+1,n}))=0
alone implies the vanishing
H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})=0
.

(2) If
H^{1}(C,B)=0
, then the term
E^{i,j}_{2}
of the Leray spectral sequence for the projection map
\pr_{1}\colon C_{p+1}\times C_{n}\rightarrow C_{p+1}
vanishes whenever
j\geq 1
. Thus

H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes\mathscr{E})=H^{m}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n},(\mathscr{E}\boxtimes N_{n,B})(-D_{p+1,n})).

Now, given a line bundle
L
on the curve
C
, we consider the locus

\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\ell}(L):=\{\xi\in C_{n}\mid h^{0}(C,L(-\xi))\geq\ell\}~~\text{ for integers $n,\ell\geq 0$}.

When
\ell\geq 1
and
n\geq r(L)-\ell+1
, the locus
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\ell}(L)
coincides with the secant space
V_{n}^{r(L)-\ell}(L)
of
n
-secant
(r(L)-\ell)
-planes to
C
in
\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,L)
(see
[
8
, Definition 1.1]
). In general, the locus
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\ell}(L)
is a closed subset of
C_{n}
and there is a natural stratification

C_{n}=\mathcal{L}^{0}_{n}(L)\supseteq\mathcal{L}^{1}_{n}(L)\supseteq\mathcal{L}^{2}_{n}(L)\supseteq\cdots.

When
n=0
, the locus
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{0}(L)
either contains a point and has dimension zero, or else is empty and has dimension
-1
by convention.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
L
be a line bundle on
C
. Fix integers
m,n,p\geq 0
, and suppose that

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)\leq p+n-m-\ell

for all integers
\ell\geq 0
with
p+1-m\leq\ell\leq p+1+n-m
.
Then we have the following:

(1)

m\leq p
.

(2)

If
\mathscr{E}
is a locally free sheaf on
C_{n}
, then

H^{m}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n},(N_{p+1,L}\boxtimes\mathscr{E})(-D_{p+1,n}))=0.

(3)

If
\mathscr{E}
is a locally free sheaf on
C_{n-i}
for an integer
i\geq 0
, then

H^{m-j}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n-i},(N_{p+1,L}\boxtimes\mathscr{E})(-D_{p+1,n-i}))=0~~\text{ for $i\leq j\leq 2i$}.

Proof.

(1)
If
m\geq p+1
, we can take
\ell=0
and then
n=\dim\mathcal{L}^{0}_{n}(L)\leq p+n-m\leq n-1
, which is a contradiction.

(2)
Let
\pr_{2}\colon C_{p+1}\times C_{n}\rightarrow C_{n}
be the projection map. Recall that the dualizing sheaf of
C_{p+1}\times C_{n}
is
\omega_{C_{p+1}\times C_{n}}=N_{p+1,\omega_{C}}\boxtimes N_{n,\omega_{C}}
. By Serre duality, the cohomology in
(2)
is dual to

(3.1)

H^{p+1+n-m}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n},(S_{p+1,\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1}}\boxtimes(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes N_{n,\omega_{C}}))(D_{p+1,n})).

The Leray spectral sequence for
\pr_{2}
yields

E_{2}^{p+1+n-m-\ell,\ell}:=H^{p+1+n-m-\ell}(C_{n},\mathscr{E}^{\vee}\otimes N_{n,\omega_{C}}\otimes R^{\ell}\pr_{2,*}(S_{p+1,\omega\otimes L^{-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{n}}(D_{n,p+1})))

converging to the cohomology in (
3.1
). It is enough to show that

E_{2}^{p+1+n-m-\ell,\ell}=0~~\text{ for $0\leq\ell\leq p+1+n-m$}.

By
(1)
, we get
E_{2}^{p+1+n-m,0}=0
. For
\ell\geq 1
, it suffices to show that

(3.2)

\dim\Supp R^{\ell}\pr_{2,*}(S_{p+1,\omega\otimes L^{-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{n}}(D_{p+1,n}))\leq p+n-m-\ell.

To this end, note that the restriction of the sheaf
(S_{p+1,\omega\otimes L^{-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{n}})(D_{p+1,n})
to the fiber
\pr^{-1}_{2}(\xi)\cong C_{p+1}
over
\xi\in C_{n}
is
S_{p+1,\omega\otimes L^{-1}(\xi)}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have

H^{\ell}(C_{p+1},S_{p+1,\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1}(\xi)})=S^{p+1-\ell}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1}(\xi))\otimes\wedge^{\ell}H^{1}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1}(\xi))=0

if
h^{1}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1}(\xi))=h^{0}(C,L(-\xi))<\ell
. This suggests that

\Supp R^{\ell}\pr_{2,*}(S_{p+1,\omega\otimes L^{-1}}\boxtimes\mathscr{O}_{C_{n}}(D_{p+1,n}))\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L).

By the assumption on the dimension of
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)
, we obtain the inequality in (
3.2
) and hence conclude that
E_{2}^{p+1+n-m-\ell,\ell}=0
.

(3)
In view of
(2)
, it is sufficient to show that

(3.3)

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n-i}(L)\leq p+n-i-m+j-\ell

for all integers
\ell\geq 0
with
p+1-m+j\leq\ell\leq p+1+n-i-m+j
. Recall that the addition map
\sigma:=\sigma_{n-i,1}\colon C_{n-i}\times C\to C_{n-i+1}
sending
(\xi,x)
to
\xi+x
is a finite morphism. Observe that

\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n-i+1}(L)\subseteq\sigma(\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n-i}(L)\times C)\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\ell-1}_{n-i+1}(L),

which in particular implies that

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n-i}(L)\leq\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell-1}_{n-i+1}(L)-1.

Iterating this inequality, we obtain

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n-i}(L)\leq\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell-i}_{n}(L)-i.

Recall that
\ell
is an integer with
p+1-m+j\leq\ell\leq p+1+n-i-m+j
. Since
p\geq m
and
i\leq j\leq 2i
, we get
p+1-m\leq\ell-i\leq p+1+n-m
and
\ell\geq i+1
. We deduce (
3.3
) from the given assumption as follows:

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n-i}(L)\leq\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell-i}_{n}(L)-i\leq p+n-m-\ell\leq p+n-i-m+j-\ell.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
B
be a
p
-very ample line bundle and
L
a line bundle on
C
. Fix integers
m,n\geq 0
and suppose that

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)\leq p+n-m-\ell

for all integers
\ell\geq 0
with
p+1-m\leq\ell\leq p+1+n-m
. Then

H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, it suffices to check that

H^{m-j}(C_{p+1}\times C_{n-i},(N_{p+1,L}\boxtimes N_{n-i,B})(-D_{p+1,n-i}))=0

for all
i,j
with
0\leq i\leq m-1
and
i\leq j\leq 2i
. But these cohomology vanishing follow from Proposition
3.3
.
∎

We are ready to prove the following theorem which is the main technical result of this section. Notice that it does not require any positivity condition on
L
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
B
be a
p
-very ample line bundle and
L
a line bundle on
C
. Fix integers
m,n\geq 0
with
m\leq p
. If

h^{0}(C,L)\leq p+n-m
, then

H^{m}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{n}M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0.

Proof.

It is enough to verify the hypothesis on the dimension of
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)
in Corollary
3.4
. To derive a contradiction, suppose that there exits an integer
\ell
with
p+1-m\leq\ell\leq p+1+n-m
such that

\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)\geq p+n-m-\ell+1.

Set
d:=\deg L
, and consider the closed subset

C^{\ell}_{d-n}:=\{\xi^{\prime}\in C_{d-n}\mid h^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(\xi^{\prime}))\geq\ell\}\subseteq C_{d-n}.

Write
\sigma_{n,d-n}\colon C_{n}\times C^{\ell}_{d-n}\rightarrow C_{d}
for the addition map given by
(\xi^{\prime},\xi)\mapsto\xi^{\prime}+\xi
, and consider the projection map
\pr_{1}\colon C_{n}\times C^{\ell}_{d-n}\rightarrow C_{n}
. Observe that

\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)=\pr_{1}(\sigma_{n,d-n}^{-1}(|L|)).

For any
\xi\in\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)
, we can identify
\pr_{1}^{-1}(\xi)=C^{\ell}_{d-n}\subseteq C_{d-n}
.
Then

\pr_{1}^{-1}(\xi)\cap\sigma_{n,d-n}^{-1}(|L|)=|L(-\xi)|

which has dimension
\geq\ell-1
. This means that the projection from
\sigma_{n,d-n}^{-1}(|L|)
to
\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)
has fibers of dimensions at least
\ell-1
. Since
\sigma_{n,d-n}
is a finite morphism, we can count dimensions:

\dim|L|\geq\sigma^{-1}_{n,d-n}(|L|)\geq\dim\mathcal{L}^{\ell}_{n}(L)+\ell-1\geq p+n-m.

This gives a contradiction to the assumption that
h^{0}(C,L)\leq p+n-m
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
B
be a
p
-very ample line bundle and
L
a line bundle on
C
. Assume that
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1
. Then we have

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0.

Proof.

Recall that the kernel bundle
M_{p+1,B}
is defined in the short exact sequence (
2.1
).
Note that
\rank M_{p+1,B}=r(B)-p
and
\det M_{p+1,B}=N_{p+1,B}^{-1}
.
By Serre duality, it is enough to prove

H^{p}(C_{p+1},\wedge^{r(B)-p-1}M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1}})=0.

But the assumption
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1
implies

h^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})=h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1=p+(r(B)-p-1)-p,

so the assertion follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The upper bound condition imposed on the specialty of
L\otimes B^{-1}
in Corollary
3.6
and Theorem
1.3
can be transferred to one on the degree of
L
, which turns out to be convenient in applications. Indeed, by Riemann–Roch,

h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=g-1-\deg L+\deg B+h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1}),

and

r(B)-p-1=\deg B-g+1+h^{1}(C,B)-p-2.

Therefore the inequality
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1
is equivalent to the inequality

(3.4)

\deg L\geq 2g+p+h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-h^{1}(C,B).

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We first show that
L
is
p
-very ample. By Remark
3.7
, the assumption (
1.2
) is equivalent to (
3.4
). If
B
is nonspecial, then it is clear that
L
is
p
-very ample. If
B
is special, we prove by contradiction by assuming
L
is not
p
-very ample. Thus there exists an effective divisor
\xi
of degree
p+1
such that
L(-\xi)
is special. So we may write
B=\omega_{C}(-\alpha)
and
L=\omega_{C}(\xi-\beta)
for some effective divisors
\alpha
and
\beta
on
C
. We then have

2g-1+p-\deg\beta=\deg L\geq 2g+p+h^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(\xi-\beta+\alpha))-h^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(\alpha))\geq 2g+p-\deg\beta,

which is a contradiction. Thus in particular
L
is globally generated. Now, the theorem is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.2
and Corollary
3.6
.
∎

In the rest part of this section, we present several applications of Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
B
be a
p
-very ample line bundle and
L
a line bundle on
C
. Assume that
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq 1
. Then

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})\neq 0
if and only if
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=1
and one of the following holds:

(1)

p=0
and
|B|
is a base point free pencil.

(2)

p=1
,
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,B)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
\geq 3
, and
H^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\neq 0
.

(3)

p\geq 2
,
g=1
,
\deg B=p+2
, and
L=B
.

Proof.

Suppose that
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})\neq 0
. By Corollary
3.6
,

r(B)-p\leq h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq 1,

so
r(B)\leq p+1
. If
r(B)\leq p
, then
\rank M_{p+1,B}=0
so that
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0
. Thus
r(B)=p+1
, and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=1
.

Let us consider first the case when
C=\mathbf{P}^{1}
. In this case,
B=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p+1)
and
L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p-1)
. Notice that if
p\geq 1
, then
L
is globally generated and

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=K_{p,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},B;L)=H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\wedge^{p+1}M_{L}\otimes B)=0.

Hence the only possibility is that
p=0
and
B=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1)
defines a base point free pencil. Next we consider arbitrary curves in the sequel by assuming
g\geq 1
. By
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
, we must have (1)
|B|
is a base point free pencil, (2)
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,B)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
\geq 3
, or (3)
g=1
,
\deg B=p+2
for
p\geq 2
. In the last case, the condition
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=1
forces
L=B
or
B(-x)
for any
x\in C
. We will exclude the case of
L=B(-x)
.

Finally, it only remains to show that
(i)

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})\neq 0
really occurs under the circumstances (1), (2), (3) and
(ii)

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=0
when
g=1,\deg B=p+2,L=B(-x)
for
x\in C
.
This comes from the observation that
\rank M_{p+1,B}=1
in all these cases. By Lemma
2.1
,

H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})=H^{1}(C_{p+1},S_{p+1,L\otimes B^{-1}})=S^{p}H^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\otimes H^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1}).

Thus
H^{1}(C_{p+1},M_{p+1,B}\otimes N_{p+1,L})\neq 0
if and only if either
p=0
or
H^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\neq 0
.
∎

Corollary 3.9

(Green’s
(2g+1+p)
-theorem)
.

If
L
is a line bundle on
C
and
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
, then
K_{p,2}(C;L)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\deg(L)\geq 2g+p+1
, it follows from Riemann–Roch theorem that
L
is
p
-very ample and
r(L)\geq g+p+1
. Note that
K_{p,2}(C;L)=K_{p,1}(C,L;L)
. Since
h^{1}(C,L\otimes L^{-1})=g\leq r(L)-p-1
, the assertion follows from Theorem
1.3
.
∎

The following is a well-known generalization of Castelnuovo’s base point free pencil trick.

Corollary 3.10

(Base point free pencil trick)
.

Let
B
be an effective divisor and
L
a nonspecial globally generated line bundle. Then

H^{1}(C,\wedge^{w+1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)=0~~\text{ for $w\leq\deg L-2g$}.

unless
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}
, in which case
H^{1}(C,\wedge^{w}M_{L}\otimes L)=0
for
w\leq\deg L-2g
.

Proof.

Note that
H^{1}(C,B\otimes L)=0
.
We may assume that
w\geq 0
. As
\deg(L\otimes B)\geq 2g+w
, we see that
L\otimes B
is
w
-very ample. If
B\neq\mathscr{O}_{C}
, then

\deg L\geq 2g+w+\underbrace{h^{0}(C,L\otimes(L\otimes B)^{-1})}_{=0}-\underbrace{h^{1}(C,L\otimes B)}_{=0}.

By Theorem
1.3
and Remark

, we have
H^{1}(C,\wedge^{w+1}M_{L}\otimes B\otimes L)=K_{w,1}(C,B\otimes L;L)=0
.
If

, then Green’s
(2g+1+p)
-theorem yields
H^{1}(C,\wedge^{w}M_{L}\otimes L)=K_{w-1,2}(C;L)=0
when

.
∎

4.
Effective nonvanishing for weight-one syzygies of algebraic curves

In this section, we discuss the nonvanishing of the Koszul cohomology
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
. Here as in previous sections, let
B
and
L
are line bundles on a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
. We are trying to relate positivity of line bundles with nonvanishing of the Koszul cohomology.

We start by recalling the following very useful nonvanishing theorem for
K_{p,1}(C;L)
proved by Green–Lazarsfeld. The original theorem holds for arbitrary smooth projective varieties, but we only state the version here for curves.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
13
, Appendix]
)
.

If
L=M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
for some line bundles
M_{1},M_{2}
on
C
such that
r_{1}:=r(M_{1})\geq 1,r_{2}:=r(M_{2})\geq 1
, then
K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0
for
1\leq p\leq r_{1}+r_{2}-1
.

Together with Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume
g\geq 2
.
Suppose that
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample and
\deg L\geq 2g+p
. Then

K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0~\text{ if and only if }~h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\geq g-p-1.

Furthermore, we have the following:

(1)

If
\omega_{C}
is not
(p+1)
-very ample, then
K_{p+1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
except for the case when
g=2
,
p=0
, and
\deg L=4
, in which case,
K_{1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
.

(2)

If
\deg L=2g+p
, the condition
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\geq g-p-1
is equivalent to
L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}
computes the gonality
\gon(C)=p+2
.

Proof.

If
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\leq g-p-2
, then Theorem
1.3
shows that
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
. Conversely, suppose that
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\geq g-p-1
. Set
d:=\deg L
. As
h^{0}(C,L)=d-g+1
, the duality of Koszul cohomology (
[
13
, Theorem 2.c.1]
) gives

K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=K_{d-g-p-1,1}(C;L)^{\vee}.

Consider two line bundles
M_{1}:=\omega_{C}
and
M_{2}:=L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}
. We compute that
r(M_{1})=g-1
and

r(M_{2})=\deg(L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})-g+h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}).

The hypothesis imposed on
\deg L
and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})
gives
r(M_{2})\geq d-p-2g+1\geq 1
. By Green–Lazarsfeld’s nonvanishing theorem (Theorem
4.1
), we find
K_{d-p-g-1,1}(C;L)\neq 0
and therefore
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
as desired.

Now, we prove the statement (1) by assuming that
\omega_{C}
is not
(p+1)
-very ample. There exists an effective divisor
\xi
of degree
p+2
such that
h^{0}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(\xi))=h^{1}(C,\omega_{C}(-\xi))=2
. As before, the duality of Koszul cohomology (
[
13
, Theorem 2.c.1]
) gives

K_{p+1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=K_{d-g-p-2,1}(C;L)^{\vee}.

For
M_{1}:=\mathscr{O}_{C}(\xi)
and
M_{2}:=L(-\xi)
, we have
r(M_{1})=1
and
r(M_{2})\geq d-p-2-g
. If
d-p-2-g\geq 1
, then Theorem
4.1
shows
K_{d-p-g-2,1}(C;L)\neq 0
. On the other hand, note that
d-p-2-g\geq g-2
. Thus if
d-p-2-g=0
, then
g=2
and
p=0
. In this case,
d=\deg L=4
. The duality above then gives
K_{1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=K_{0,1}(C;L)^{\vee}=0
.

Finally, we prove the statement (2) by assuming that
\deg L=2g+p
. Then
\deg(L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})=p+2
. By
Riemann–Roch,

h^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})=p+g+3+h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}).

Thus
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\geq g-p-1
if and only if
h^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\geq 2
.
However, as
\gon(C)\geq p+2
, we have
h^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\leq 2
. Hence
h^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\geq 2
is equivalent to that the gonality
\gon(C)=p+2
and
L\otimes\omega^{-1}_{C}
computes the gonality. ∎

Using the corollary, we may prove the effective gonality theorem (Theorem
1.1
) right away, but we will derive it from a more general statement (Theorem
1.4
) in the next section. In order to prove Theorem
1.4
, in the rest part of this section, we will prepare effective nonvanishing results for the Koszul cohomology
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)
when
B
is neither
\mathscr{O}_{C}
nor
\omega_{C}
, which are the situations where Green–Lazarsfeld’s theorem says nothing.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
B
and

B

h^{0}(C,L(-x_{1}))=1
, then
L=\mathscr{O}_{C}(x_{1})
. But
L
is also globally generated, so we must have
C=\mathbf{P}^{1}
and
L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1)
, and thus
K_{1,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}};\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(1))=0
. If
h^{0}(C,L(-x_{1}))\geq 2
, then
h^{0}(C,L(-2x_{1}))>0
. By Lemma
4.5
,
K_{0,1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C}(-x_{1});L(-x_{1}))\neq 0
, and thus, by Lemma
4.3
, we conclude
K_{1,1}(C,\mathscr{O}_{C};L)\neq 0
.

Case
2:
p\geq 1
,
C=\mathbf{P}^{1}
and
B=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p)
. In this case,
L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m)
with
m\geq p+1
. If
m=p+1
, then we have
K_{p+1,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p);\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p+1))=0
. If
m\geq p+2
, then
K_{0,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},B(-\xi);L(-\xi))=K_{0,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-1);\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m-p-1))\neq 0
. Corollary
4.4
gives
K_{p+1,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p);\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(m))\neq 0
.

Now, we consider the case when
h^{0}(C,B)\geq p+2
. The line bundle
B(-\xi)
is effective and the linear system
|B(-\xi)|
has nonempty base locus. By Lemma
4.5
,
K_{0,1}(C,B(-\xi);L(-\xi))\neq 0
, and thus, by Corollary
4.4
, we conclude
K_{p+1,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
.
∎

Now, we obtain a slight improvement of
[
1
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
B
and
L
be line bundles on
C
. Suppose that
B
is
p
-very ample but not
(p+1)
-very ample.
If
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
then
K_{p+1,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
except for the following:

(1)

g=0
,
B=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p)
,
L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p+1)
with
p\geq 1
. In this case,
K_{p+1,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},B;L)=0
.

(2)

g=1
,
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}
,
\deg L=3
. In this case,
K_{1,1}(C;L)=0
.

Proof.

There exist points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+1}\in C
such that
B(-\xi_{p+1})
is not globally generated, where
\xi_{i}:=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p+1
and
\xi_{0}:=0
. Note that
L(-\xi_{p+1})
is globally generated and
H^{1}(C,L(-\xi_{p+1}))=0
. Thanks to Proposition
4.6
, we only need to check that

(4.1)

H^{1}(C,\wedge^{p+2-i}M_{L(-\xi_{i})}\otimes B(-\xi_{i-1})\otimes L(-\xi_{i}))=0~~\text{ for every $1\leq i\leq p+1$}.

Note that
B(-\xi_{i-1})
is effective and
B(-\xi_{i-1})\otimes L(-\xi_{i})
is
(p+1-i)
-very ample. We have

\deg L(-\xi_{i})\geq 2g+(p+1-i).

If
B(-\xi_{p})\neq\mathscr{O}_{C}
, then Corollary
3.10
yields (
4.1
) as desired. On the other hand, if
B(-\xi_{p})=\mathscr{O}_{C}
, then
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}(\xi_{p})
and
h^{0}(C,B)=p+1
. By
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
, we have either
p\geq 1
,
C=\mathbf{P}^{1}
,
B=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p)
or
p=0
,
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}
. For the former case, Case 2 in the proof of Proposition
4.6
yields that
K_{p+1,1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},B;L)=0
if and only if
L=\mathscr{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(p+1)
. For the latter case, consider the Koszul-type complex

\wedge^{2}H^{0}(C,L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{0}(C,L)\otimes H^{0}(C,L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{0}(C,L^{2})

computing
K_{1,1}(C;L)
. Here
\delta_{1}
is clearly injective, and
\delta_{2}
is surjective by Green’s
(2g+1+p)
-theorem.
Putting
d:=\deg L\geq 2g+1
, we can directly compute

\dim K_{1,1}(C;L)=\frac{(d-g)^{2}-d-g}{2}\geq\frac{g^{2}-g}{2}.

Thus
K_{1,1}(C;L)=0
if and only if
g=1
and
d=3
.
∎

Now, we want to apply Corollary
4.4
to study when
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
under the assumption that
B
is
p
-very ample.
Note that
B(-\xi)
is globally generated for every
\xi:=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{p}\in C_{p}
. Suppose that
L
satisfies the conditions in Corollary
4.4
. We have

K_{0,1}(C,B(-\xi);L(-\xi))=K_{0,1}(C,L(-\xi);B(-\xi))=H^{1}(C,M_{B(-\xi)}\otimes L(-\xi)).

Theorem
1.3
says that if
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\leq r(B)-p-1
, then
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)=0
. Thus the problem is to find a condition for
K_{0,1}(C,L(-\xi);B(-\xi))\neq 0
assuming that

h^{1}(C,L(-\xi)\otimes B(-\xi)^{-1})=h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq r(B)-p=r(B(-\xi)).

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
B
and
L
be globally generated line bundles on
C
. Assume that
h^{1}(C,L)=0
and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq r(B)
.

(1)

If
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}
, then
K_{0,1}(C;L)=0
.

(2)

If
B\neq\mathscr{O}_{C}
and
H^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
, then
K_{0,1}(C,B;L)=K_{0,1}(C,L;B)\neq 0
.

Proof.

As the assertion (1) is trivial, we only need to prove the assertion (2).
We have

K_{0,1}(C,L;B)=H^{1}(C,M_{B}\otimes L)=H^{0}(C,\wedge^{r(B)-1}M_{B}\otimes\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})^{\vee}.

As
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes L^{-1})=0
, we have

H^{0}(C,\wedge^{r(B)-1}M_{B}\otimes\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})=K_{r(B)-1,0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1};B).

Since
h^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})=h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq r(B)
, we can choose linearly independent sections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r(B)}\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})
. We can also take a nonzero section
t\in H^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{r(B)}(-1)^{i}(s_{1}t\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{s_{i}t}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{r(B)}t)\otimes s_{i}\in\wedge^{r(B)-1}H^{0}(C,B)\otimes H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1})

is killed by the Koszul differential, so this yields
K_{r(B)-1,0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes B\otimes L^{-1};B)\neq 0
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
B
be a
p
-very ample line bundle with
r(B)\geq p+1
and
L
a line bundle on
C
with
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq r(B)-p
. Assume that there exist not necessarily distinct points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}\in C
such that if
\xi_{i}:=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
and
\xi_{0}:=0
, then
L(-\xi_{p})
is globally generated with
H^{1}(C,L(-\xi_{p}))=0
,
H^{0}(C,L(-\xi_{p})\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
, and

(4.2)

H^{1}(C,\wedge^{p+1-i}M_{L(-\xi_{i})}\otimes B(-\xi_{i-1})\otimes L(-\xi_{i}))=0~~\text{ for every $1\leq i\leq p$.}

Then
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
. In particular, if
\deg L\geq 2g+p
with
H^{0}(C,L(-\xi)\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
for some
\xi\in C_{p}
, then
K_{p,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Note that
h^{0}(C,B)\geq p+2
implies
B(-\xi_{p})\neq\mathscr{O}_{C}
.
The first part of the corollary then follows from Corollary
4.4
and Lemma
4.8
. For the second part, write
\xi=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{p}
. It is enough to check (
4.2
) when
\xi_{i}:=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq p
and
\xi_{0}:=0
. But this follows from Corollary
3.10
since
\deg L(-\xi_{i})\geq 2g+(p-i)
.
∎

As an interesting quick application, we recover one part of Green–Lazarsfeld’s result
[

, Theorem 1.2]
on the failure of
N_{p}
-property for a line bundle of large degree.

Corollary 4.10

(Green–Lazarsfeld)
.

Assume that
g\geq 1
. Let
L
be a line bundle on
C
of degree
2g+p+1
. If
H^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
, then
K_{p+1,2}(C;L)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Notice that
L
is
(p+1)
-very ample,
r(L)=g+p+1\geq p+2
, and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes L^{-1})=r(L)-(p+1)
. As
L\otimes\omega^{-1}_{C}
is effective and has degree

, there is an effective divisor
\xi\in C_{p+1}
such that

5.
Effective gonality theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
and deduce Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
from it. We assume that
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

As the assertion is trivial when
B=\mathscr{O}_{C}
, we assume that
B\neq\mathscr{O}_{C}
. The line bundle
B
is
p
-very ample, so we have
r(B)\geq p+1
by the classification in
[
9
, Proposition 3.4]
. Observe that if
\xi
is an effective divisor of degree
p
, then
|B(-\xi)|
is a nonzero base point free linear system and thus
\gon(C)\leq\deg B-p
. This particularly implies that
\Cliff(C)\leq\deg B-p-2
(recall that Coppens–Martens
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
proved that
\Cliff(C)=\gon(C)-2
or
\gon(C)-3
). Using this observation and Riemann–Roch theorem applied for the line bundle
B
, we deduce from the second inequality in the hypothesis the following inequality

(5.1)

\deg L\geq 2g+3p+4-2h^{0}(C,B).

By Theorem
1.3
and Remark
3.7
, if

\deg L\geq 2g+p+h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-h^{1}(C,B),

then we immediately have
K_{i,1}(C,B;L)=0
for
0\leq i\leq p
. Thus in the sequel, we focus on the case that

(5.2)

\deg L\leq 2g+p+h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-h^{1}(C,B)-1,

which is equivalent to
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq r(B)-p
by Remark
3.7
. Then
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq 1
.
Furthermore, as
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1-h^{1}(C,B)
, the inequality (
5.2
) also implies that
h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq 2
.

If
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=1
, then Corollary
3.8
gives the exceptional cases (1) and (2) in the theorem. Notice also that in both cases the line bundle
L
is nonspecial because of the degree bound of
L
in (
5.1
).

Now, we consider the case when
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq 2
. So the line bundle
L\otimes B^{-1}
contributes to the Clifford index
\Cliff(C)
of
C
(the existence of a line bundle contributing
\Cliff(C)
implies
g\geq 4
). This means that

(5.3)

(\deg L-\deg B)-2h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})+2=\Cliff(L\otimes B^{-1})\geq\Cliff(C).

Rewriting (
5.2
) as

(5.4)

h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq\deg L-2g-p+1+h^{1}(C,B)

and combinng this with (
5.3
), we find

(5.5)

(\deg L-\deg B)-\Cliff(C)+2\geq 2h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})\geq 2\deg L-4g-2p+2+2h^{1}(C,B).

Hence we obtain

(5.6)

4g+2p-2h^{1}(C,B)-\Cliff(C)\geq\deg L+\deg B.

However, the assumption on
\deg B+\deg L
in the hypothesis of the theorem forces (
5.6
) to be an equality. Consequently, all the inequalities in (
5.5
), (
5.4
), (
5.3
), (
5.2
) are equalities, and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=r(B)-p
. In particular,
\deg L=2g+p+h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-h^{1}(C,B)-1
,
\deg B=2g+p-h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-h^{1}(C,B)+1-\Cliff(C)
, and
L\otimes B^{-1}
computes
\Cliff(C)
. This is the case (3) stated in the theorem. Furthermore, as
h^{1}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=r(B)-p
, we obtain
K_{i,1}(C,B;L)=0
for
0\leq i\leq p-1
by Theorem
1.3
.

Turning to the exceptional case
(3)
with
B=\omega_{C}
, we have

2g-2=\deg\omega_{C}=2g+p-h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})-\Cliff(C)\leq 2g-2+(p-\Cliff(C)).

Thus
\Cliff(C)\leq p
. As
p+2\leq\gon(C)
, we get
p-1\leq\Cliff(C)\leq p
(recall that
\Cliff(C)=\gon(C)-2
or
\gon(C)-3
). Consider the case when
\Cliff(C)=p
. Then
h^{0}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})=2
, and
\deg L=2g+p
. Hence
\deg(L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})=p+2
, so
\gon(C)=p+2
. Since
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})=g-p-1
, Corollary
4.2
shows that
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for some
\xi\in C_{p+2}
with
\dim|\xi|=1
. Consider the case when
\Cliff(C)=p-1
(hence
\gon(C)=p+2
). Then
h^{0}(C,L\otimes B^{-1})=3
, and
\deg L=2g+p+1
. We have
\gamma(C)=2
. Here

\gamma(C):=\min\{r(A)\mid\text{$A$ computes $\Cliff(C)$}\}

is the
Clifford dimension
of
C
. It is well-known that
\gamma(C)=1
if and only if
\Cliff(C)=\gon(C)-2
. Since
H:=L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1}
computes
\gamma(C)
, it follows that
H
gives an embedding
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
. Then
\omega_{C}=H^{p}
, and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
.
Now, in any event,
\deg L\geq 2g+p
and
h^{1}(C,L\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})=g-p-1
. Furthermore,
r(L)\geq g+p\geq p+1
and
H^{0}(C,L(-\xi)\otimes\omega_{C}^{-1})\neq 0
for some
\xi\in C_{p}
. Thus Corollary
4.9
yields
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
(instead one can also apply Corollary
4.2
to conclude
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
).

Finally, if
B
is not
(p+1)
-very ample and
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
, Corollary
4.7
shows that
K_{p+1,1}(C,B;L)\neq 0
since we assume
g\geq 2
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Theorem
1.4
holds true even when
g=0
or
1
. However, more exceptional cases occur such as Corollaries
3.8
and
4.7
. We leave the details to the interested readers.

The following is essentially equivalent to the effective gonality theorem (Theorem
1.1
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
.
Assume that
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample.

(1)

If
\deg L\geq 2g+p+2
, then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
.

(2)

If
\deg L=2g+p+1
, then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
if and only if
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
p+3\geq 4
and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
. In this case,
p=\gon(C)-2
.

(3)

If
\deg L=2g+p
, then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
if and only if
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for some
\xi\in C_{p+2}
with
\dim|\xi|=1
. In this case,
p=\gon(C)-2
.

Proof.

As
\gon(C)\geq p+2
, we have
\Cliff(C)\geq p-1
.
When
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
, we have

\deg\omega_{C}+\deg L\geq 4g+p-1\geq 4g+2p-2-\Cliff(C).

By Theorem
1.4
,
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
if and only if
\deg L=2g+p+1
,
\Cliff(C)=p-1
, and
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
p+3\geq 4
with
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
. We have shown
(1)
and
(2)
. When
\deg L=2g+p
, the assertion (3) follows from Corollary
4.2
.
∎

The following is a reformulation of the above theorem in the light of
[
15
, Conjecture 3.7]
. Our result is a refinement of Green–Lazarsfeld’s original expectation that if
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
, then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
if and only if
\omega_{C}
is not
p
-very ample (see
[
15
, page 87]
).

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
with
\deg L\geq 2g+p
. Then
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

\omega_{C}
is not
p
-very ample.

(2)

C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
p+3\geq 4
and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
. In this case,
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample and
\deg L=2g+p+1
.

(3)

C
is arbitrary and
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for some
\xi\in C_{p+2}
with
\dim|\xi|=1
. In this case,
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample and
\deg L=2g+p
.

Proof.

If
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample, then the corollary follows from Theorem
5.2
. If
\omega_{C}
is not
p
-very ample, then Corollary
4.7
shows that
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0

∎

Now, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the duality theorem (
[
13
, Theorem 2.c.1]
), it is enough to show that if
\deg L\geq 2g+\gon(C)-2
, then

K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq\gon(C)-2
and
K_{\gon(C)-1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0

except for the cases (1) and (2) in which

K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
for
0\leq p\leq\gon(C)-3
and
K_{\gon(C)-2,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0

under the assumption that
K_{\gon(C)-1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq K_{0,1}(C;L)^{\vee}
.
If
K_{\gon(C)-1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=K_{0,1}(C;L)^{\vee}=0
, then
g=2

(\gon(C)=2)
and
\deg L=4
(cf. Corollary
4.2

(1)
). In this case, we only need to show that
K_{0,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
if and only if
L=\omega_{C}^{2}
. But this is a special case of Theorem
5.2
. Now, assume that
K_{\gon(C)-1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq K_{0,1}(C;L)^{\vee}
. For
0\leq p\leq\gon(C)-3
, as
\deg L\geq 2g+p+1
and
p\neq\gon(C)-2
, we get
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
by Theorem
5.2
. For
p=\gon(C)-2
, note that
\deg L\geq 2g+p
and
\omega_{C}
is
p
-very ample but not
(p+1)
-very ample. Then Corollary
4.2
implies
K_{p+1,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)\neq 0
, and Theorem
5.2
determines exactly when
K_{p,1}(C,\omega_{C};L)=0
.
∎

We conclude this section by deriving a degree bound for
L
proposed by Rathmann from Theorem
1.1
. In his journey of exploring an optimal bound for the gonality conjecture, Rathmann believes that the most natural degree bound would look like as
2g+g/2+\epsilon
together with some exceptional cases. It turns out that his intuition is indeed the case, and we confirm it in the following corollary, which also quickly implies Corollary
1.2
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
, and
L
be a line bundle on
C
.
If
\deg L\geq 2g+\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
, then

K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~1\leq p\leq r(L)-\gon(C)

except for the following two cases:

(1)

C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
4,5,6
, and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
.
(
In this case,
(g,\gon(C),\deg L)=(3,3,8),(6,4,15),(10,5,24)
, respectively.
)

(2)

C
has maximal gonality, i.e.,
\gon(C)=\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor
, and
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for an effective divisor
\xi
of degree
\gon(C)
with
\dim|\xi|=1
.

In the exceptional cases, we have

K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0~~\Longleftrightarrow~~1\leq p\leq r(L)-\gon(C)+1
.

Proof.

As
\gon(C)\leq\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor
, we have
\deg L\geq 2g+\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor\geq 2g+\gon(C)-2
. By Theorem
1.1
, we only need to show the following:

(1)

If
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,H)=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane curve of degree
d\geq 4
and
L=\omega_{C}\otimes H
, then
d=4,5,6
.

(2)

If
L=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for an effective divisor
\xi
of degree
\gon(C)
with
\dim|\xi|=1
, then
\gon(C)=\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor
.

Suppose that we are in Case (1). We have
g=(d-1)(d-2)/2
and
\gon(C)=d-1
. Since
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-1\geq 2g+\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
, it follows that
d-2\geq\lfloor(d^{2}-3d)/4\rfloor
. This implies that
d=4,5,6
. Suppose that we are in Case (2). As
\deg L=2g+\gon(C)-2\geq 2g+\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
, we get
\gon(C)\geq\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor
and hence
\gon(C)=\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Note that
g\geq\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor
. Thus
\deg L\geq 3g-2\geq 2g+\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor
. Suppose that we are in the exceptional case (1) of Corollary
5.4
. Then
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
is a plane quartic curve (
g=3
and
L=\omega_{C}
), and
L=\omega_{C}^{2}
. Suppose that we are in the exceptional case (2) of Corollary
5.4
. Then
\deg L=2g+\lfloor(g-1)/2\rfloor\geq 3g-2
, and hence,
\lfloor(g+3)/2\rfloor=g
. This yields
g=3
or
2
. If
g=3
(
\gon(C)=3
), then
C\subseteq\mathbf{P}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})=\mathbf{P}^{2}
is a plane quartic curve. In this case, for any
\xi\in C_{3}
with
\dim|\xi|=1
, there is
x\in C
such that
\mathscr{O}_{C}(\xi)=\omega_{C}(-x)
. Thus
L=\omega_{C}^{2}(-x)
. If
g=2
(
\gon(C)=2
), then
\omega_{C}=\omega_{C}(\xi)
for any
\xi\in C_{2}
with
\dim|\xi|=1
. Thus
L=\omega_{C}^{2}
. Now, the corollary follows from Corollary
5.4
.
∎

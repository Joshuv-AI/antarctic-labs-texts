Mod p p local-global compatibility for GSp 4 ⁡ ( ℚ p ) \operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) in the ordinary case
John Enns, Heejong Lee

Abstract
Let F F be a totally real field of even degree in which p p splits completely. Let r ¯ : G F → GSp 4 ⁡ ( 𝔽 ¯ p ) \overline{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}) be a modular Galois representation unramified at all finite places away from p p and upper-triangular, maximally nonsplit, and of parallel weight at places dividing p p . Fix a place w w dividing p p . Assuming certain genericity conditions and Taylor–Wiles assumptions, we prove that the GSp 4 ⁡ ( F w ) \operatorname{GSp}_{4}(F_{w}) -action on the corresponding Hecke-isotypic part of the space of mod p p automorphic forms on a compact mod center form of GSp 4 \operatorname{GSp}_{4} with infinite level at w w determines r ¯ | G F w \overline{r}|_{G_{F_{w}}} .

Introduction

The mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for the group
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
gives a tight connection between continuous Galois representations
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and admissible smooth
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(see
[
CDP14
,
CEG+18
]
) It is hoped that this result generalizes in some form to the group
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K)
for any finite extension
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. However, mod
p
representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K)
remain poorly understood and the eventual form of the correspondence (should one exist) is still mysterious outside the cases
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
or
n=1
. Going further still, in accordance with the general Langlands philosophy one may speculate about the relationship between continuous Galois representations
G_{K}\rightarrow{}^{L}G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
(suitably interpreted) and admissible
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-representations of
G(K)
for any reductive group
G
over
K
. This paper establishes a concrete connection between certain special classes of these objects when
G=\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
and
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

If
F
is a number field having
K
as one of its
p
-adic completions and
G
is defined over
F
, then generally one hopes that the correspondence should be compatible with the association of Galois representations of
G_{F}
to automorphic representations of
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
– that is, mod
p
local-global compatibility. More concretely, if for example
\bar{\rho}:G_{K}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is a local component of a
modular
Galois representation
\bar{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
then using spaces of mod
p
automorphic forms (or étale cohomology) it is possible to construct admissible
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-representations
\Pi(\bar{r})
of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K)
naturally associated with
\bar{r}
. The question of mod
p
local-global compatibility is then to study the relationship between
\bar{\rho}
and
\Pi(\bar{r})
. In particular one could ask whether
\Pi(\bar{r})
only depends on
\bar{\rho}
as opposed to all of
\bar{r}
. This question has been answered in the affirmative for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by
[
Eme
]
using the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, but in no other cases are there results to this effect. Indeed the question is closely related to the existence of a mod
p
local Langlands correspondence.

Instead, one can ask the opposite question of whether
\Pi(\bar{r})
uniquely determines
\bar{\rho}
. This is what we do in this paper for
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Previous work on this question has considered the cases
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}})
(
[
BD14
]
and
[
DL21
]
),
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}})
(
[
HLM17
]
and
[
LMP18
]
for
f=1
,
[
Enn18
]
for
f>1
),
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(
[
PQ
]
), and recently
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}})
(
[
LHM+
]
). Except in the most recent work
[
LHM+
]
, which was not yet available at the time this paper was developed, one considers a family of representations
\bar{\rho}
which are upper-triangular with fixed diagonal characters in the Fontaine-Laffaille range (or similar families). One then gives a very explicit recipe using certain group algebra operators in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[G(K)]
acting on
\Pi(\bar{r})
which pick out the isomorphism class of
\bar{\rho}
within its family. This knowledge gives us clues as to how the elusive mod
p
local Langlands correspondence should behave.

The main idea used in previous work (except
[
LHM+
]
) is that
\Pi(\bar{r})
admits a characteristic 0 lift
\tilde{\Pi}
, which is closely related to automorphic representations of
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. The
p
-adic Galois representations associated to these automorphic representations lift
\bar{r}
by construction and one uses their
p
-adic Hodge-theoretic properties at the completion
K
in order to show that the generic fibre of
\tilde{\Pi}
recovers
\bar{\rho}
. More specifically, one locates the parameters of
\bar{\rho}
within potentially crystalline deformation rings of
\bar{\rho}
of various prescribed types and then uses classical local-global compatibility of automorphic representations to transfer this data to the generic fibre of
\tilde{\Pi}
. Crucially, one must then show that this information can be “reduced mod
p
” and is not lost in
\tilde{\Pi}
. This typically involves some form of Taylor-Wiles patching and some subtle modular representation theory with group algebra operators.

In this paper, we work with a version of the patched module
M_{\infty}
first constructed for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(K)
in
[
CEG+16
]
instead of
\tilde{\Pi}
. This is an upgrade of
\Pi(\bar{r})
in the sense that it is an
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\square}[G(K)]
-module, where
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\square}
is the universal lifting ring of
\bar{\rho}
, which determines
\Pi(\bar{r})
modulo the maximal ideal of
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\square}
. The combined
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\square}
- and
G(K)
- actions essentially allow us to axiomatize the relationship between deformations of
\bar{\rho}
and classical local-global compatibility described in the previous paragraph. This makes
M_{\infty}
convenient for expressing local-global compatibility. As a result,
\bar{\rho}
can be recovered by the eigenvalues of certain “normalized Hecke operators” acting on the generic fibre of
M_{\infty}
. In particular, we avoid the complicated arguments with group algebra operators. An optimistic hope is that
M_{\infty}
actually realizes the mod
p
and
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence in general (see
[
CEG+16
, §6]
), so its use here is completely natural.

By taking quotient modulo the maximal ideal of
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\square}
, we recover
\bar{\rho}
from the eigenvalues of “normalized Hecke operators” acting on a certain Iwahori eigenspace in
\Pi(\bar{r})
.
The most subtle part of our argument is proving the non-triviality of the operator.
Namely, we need to show that the Iwahori eigenspace is non-zero and the “normalized Hecke operator” acts on it non-trivially. In the case of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, the former follows from a standard argument using Taylor–Wiles patching and combinatorics between types and weights. Its naive generalization does not work for
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
, due to the fixed similitude character in the patching argument. Instead, we construct
a congruent pair of patched modules
, a pair of patched modules with different similitude characters that are congruent modulo
p
. This allows us to perform the usual patching argument in our case.

Another tool we introduce is the Jantzen filtration. The Jantzen filtration arises to compute
p
-divisibility of the image of Carter–Lusztig intertwiners. We use it to describe the image of the mod
p
reduction of “normalized Hecke operators” and thus to determine when the image is non-zero.

\overline{r}
is unramified at all finite places away from

p
. We also assume that
(\bar{r},\chi)
is
potentially diagonalizably automorphic
(Definition
4.2.5

F,\overline{r},
and

4.4.1

v|p

\displaystyle\overline{r}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}\omega^{a_{3}}\mathrm{nr}_{\xi_{3}}&*_{1}&*&*\\

the

of

. We choose the weight

We now give a more precise description of the contents of this article. Let
\bar{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
be a continuous upper triangular, maximally nonsplit, and strongly generic representation.
The first part of the argument is to find Fontaine-Laffaille parameters of
\bar{\rho}
inside certain symplectic potentially crystalline deformation rings of
\bar{\rho}
of type
(\eta,\tau)
, where
\eta=(3,2,1,0)
is a Hodge type and
\tau
a tame inertial type. In fact, it will suffice for us to consider the single tame principal series type
\tau_{0}
. We begin in §
2.1
by giving an explicit description of these deformation rings by adapting the work of
[
LLHLM18
]
and
[
LLHL19
]
(for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
) to the case of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
using some ideas of
[
KM
]
about duality for Kisin modules. Using this, we prove that the strong genericity of
\bar{\rho}
implies
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\eta,\tau_{0}}
is formally smooth (Theorem
2.3.10
). (We actually work with a fixed similitude character as well.)

The Fontaine-Laffaille parameters of
\bar{\rho}
can be found as the reduction modulo the maximal ideal of certain “universal Frobenius eigenvalues” in
R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\eta,\tau_{0}}
. We make this completely explicit in Theorem
2.3.10
. In order to apply this result in the setting of the patched module
M_{\infty}
, we express these universal eigenvalues in terms of a morphism
\Theta_{\bar{\rho},\tau_{0}}:\mathcal{H}(\sigma(\tau_{0}))\rightarrow R_{\bar{\rho}}^{\eta,\tau_{0}}[1/p]
which interpolates the local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
in the Bernstein block corresponding to
\tau_{0}
. Here
\mathcal{H}(\sigma(\tau_{0}))
is the Hecke algebra of the
K
-type corresponding to the inertial type
\tau_{0}
. This follows ideas of
[
CEG+16
]
and is described in §
2.2
.

In §
3.3
we prove an abstract local-global compatibility result (Theorem
3.3.8
) assuming the existence of a congruent pair of patched modules
(M_{\infty},M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{cris}})
satisfying certain axioms
(PM1)
–
(PM6)
.
This result shows how the Fontaine–Laffaille invariants of
\bar{\rho}
may be recovered from the admissible smooth
\mathbb{F}[\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]
-representation
\pi
related to
M_{\infty}
. See §
3.3
for precise construction of
\pi
using
M_{\infty}
. One of the axioms (which follows from classical local-global compatibility of automorphic representations in the construction of
M_{\infty}
) states that the universal Frobenius eigenvalues may be recovered as the eigenvalue of a certain “
U_{p}
-operator”
T_{\lambda}
in the pro-
p
Iwahori–Hecke algebra acting on well-chosen Iwahori eigenspaces of (the dual of)
M_{\infty}
, up to powers of
p
. One therefore needs to pick out the leading term of these eigenvalues modulo
p
. To do this, one writes the action of
T_{\lambda}
on
M_{\infty}
as the composite of a Carter–Lusztig intertwiner and an Iwahori normalizing element in
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(see (
3.3.6
)). The power of
p
by which the Carter–Lusztig intertwiner is divisible is described by the Jantzen filtration of principal series representations of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. We show in Theorem
3.3.8
that the Carter–Lusztig intertwiner is divisible by the “correct” power of
p
on these Iwahori eigenspaces and consequently that the Fontaine–Laffaille invariant can be recovered from
\pi
. To do this, we show that there is a unique modular Serre weight occurring in
\JH(\overline{\sigma(\tau_{0})})
. This requires the combinatorics between types and weights using patching argument. On the Galois side, this requires both potentially crystalline and crystalline lifts of
\bar{\rho}
. However, it is necessary to fix the similitude character in the patching argument. That is why we have the second patched module
M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{cris}}
designed for the crystalline setting, and the congruence between the two patched modules
(PM5)
allows us to prove the uniqueness of the modular Serre weight.
Finally, we show that the modular Serre weight appears in the correct layer of the Jantzen filtration. This requires a somewhat tedious calculation with the Jantzen sum formula. We also interpret this theorem in terms of group algebra operators in Remark
3.3.10
. All this is contained in §
3.3
. The computation with the Jantzen filtration is contained in §
3.1
and §
3.2
.

The goal of §
4
is to show the existence of a congruent pair of patched modules
(M_{\infty},M_{\infty}^{\mathrm{cris}})
obeying the axioms of §
3.3
. We accomplish this under certain conditions by ultrapatching spaces of
p
-adic automorphic forms on a compact mod centre form
\mathcal{G}
of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
over a totally real field

, essentially following
[
CEG+16
]
but with ideas from
[
BCGP21
]
for the symplectic case. The congruence between patched modules is obtained by choosing the same Taylor–Wiles datum and ultrafilter.
In order to attach Galois representations to a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathcal{G}
, we need to apply Jacquet–Langlands for
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
proven in
[
Sor09
]
under stable and tempered assumptions. In Lemma

, we show that automorphic representations of

of our concern are indeed stable and tempered by using various results on Arthur multiplicity formula
[
Art13
,
Taï19
,
GT19
]
.
In §
4.4
, we assume that
\bar{\rho}
is a local component of a Galois representation
\overline{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
modular with respect to
\mathcal{G}
satisfying several assumptions
(A1)
–
(A5)
including standard Taylor–Wiles type assumptions, but also an unramifiedness away from
p
assumption that is made mainly for simplicity. In Corollary
4.4.7
, we prove the modularity of the obvious weight
(PM6)
by the change of weight argument using the main result of
[
PT21
]
. We also show the existence of such globalization
F
and
\overline{r}
for given
\bar{\rho}
by following
[
EG14
]
; see Corollary

We expect that most of this article should generalize with the same methods to
\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}}
for any
f\geq 1
. However, for simplicity we have tended to state results only in the generality that we need.

Recently,
[
LHM+
]
proved mod
p
local-global compatibility for
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}})
and generic Fontaine–Laffaille (but not necessarily upper-triangular)
\bar{\rho}
using a more geometric argument. We expect that one can adapt this idea to
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}})
and prove mod
p
local-global compatibility in a similar generality. We plan to investigate this further in our future work.

Notation and preliminaries

Throughout we fix a prime
p>2
. Most of our results require

to be larger than this due to genericity assumptions.

We let
E
denote a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
which will serves as a field of coefficients. We always assume that

We sometimes write
K
instead of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
denote a compatible choice of
p^{n}

-p

p_{0}=-p

. Let

\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
denote the reductive group over

Lemma 2.3.3

Proof.

If

Corollary 4.1.1
.

Stable and tempered automorphic representations of
\mathcal{G}
occur with multiplicity one in the discrete spectrum.

Proof.

Automorphic representations of

of general type (meaning its Arthur parameter is a

∎

We finish this subsection by proving that all automorphic representations of

Let
\pi
be a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
or
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. We say that a continuous representation
\bar{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
is
attached to
\pi
if for almost all finite places

at which
\pi
and
\bar{r}
are unramified, we have

\displaystyle\iota\circ\operatorname{WD}(\bar{r}|_{G_{F_{v}}})\simeq\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})\mod\varpi.

Lemma 4.1.6
.

Let
\pi^{\prime}
be a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
. Suppose that there exists a continuous semisimple representation
\bar{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{F})
attached to
\pi^{\prime}
. Then there exists a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
which is a weak Jacquet–Langlands transfer of
\pi^{\prime}
. In other words, we have
\pi_{v}\simeq\pi^{\prime}_{v}
for almost all finite places
v
of
F
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
be the derived subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is an inner form of
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}
which splits at all finite places. By a Theorem of Hiraga–Saito (
[
GT19
, Theorem 5.1.2]
),
\pi^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}
contains a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
which we denote by
\tilde{\pi}^{\prime}
. By
[
Taï19
, Theorem 4.0.1]
, there exists a self-dual regular algebraic discrete automorphic representation
\Pi
of
\operatorname{GL}_{5}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
which is a transfer of
\tilde{\pi}^{\prime}
. It is moreover cuspidal by
[
Ram08
, Theorem A]
. Then by
[
Art13
, Theorem 1.4.1]
, there exists a regular algebraic discrete automorphic representation
\tilde{\pi}
of
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
whose transfer to
\operatorname{GL}_{5}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
is
\Pi
. Since
\Pi
is algebraic,
\Pi_{\infty}
is essentially tempered by
[
Clo90
, Lemma 4.9]
, and
\pi_{\infty}
is also essentially tempered, so that
\pi
is cuspidal by
[
Wal84
, Theorem 4.3]
. Note that at all finite places
v
,
\tilde{\pi}^{\prime}_{v}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{v}
have the same
L
-parameter (these
L
-parameters are constructed in
[
Art13
]
).

By
[
Pat19
, Proposition 3.1.4]
, there exists a cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
lifting
\tilde{\pi}
and whose central character is equal to the central character of
\pi^{\prime}
. For any finite place
v
of
F
,
{\mathrm{std}}^{\prime}(\bar{r})_{v}
and
{\mathrm{std}}^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{\pi,p,\iota})_{v}
are isomorphic, as they are mod
p
reduction of
L
-parameters attached to
\tilde{\pi}^{\prime}_{v}
and
\tilde{\pi}_{v}
respectively (here, we use the compatibility between the local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
and
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}
upon restriction; see
[
GT10
, Main Theorem (v)]
). By Brauer–Nesbitt Theorem, we have
{\mathrm{std}}^{\prime}(\bar{r})\simeq{\mathrm{std}}^{\prime}(\bar{r}_{\pi,p,\iota})
. Since
\bar{r}_{\pi,p,\iota}
and
\bar{r}
also have the same similitude character, we have
\bar{r}\simeq\bar{r}_{\pi,p,\iota}\otimes\theta
for some quadratic character
\theta:G_{F}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
. By twisting
\pi
by
\theta^{{-1}}
, we can ensure that
\bar{r}_{\pi,p,\iota}\simeq\bar{r}
.

Let
v
be a finite place of
F
at which
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
are unramified. Then
\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})
and
\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi^{\prime}_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})
have the same similitude character and are isomorphic after composed with
{\mathrm{std}}^{\prime}
(again, by using the local Langlands for
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}
). Thus,
\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})\simeq\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi^{\prime}_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})\otimes\theta_{v}
for some quadratic character
\theta_{v}:W_{F_{v}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{\times}
. However, by the previous paragraph, we have
\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})\simeq\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi^{\prime}_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})\mod\varpi
, which means
\theta_{v}
is trivial modulo
\varpi
. Since
\theta_{v}
has order at most 2 and
p>2
, this implies that
\theta_{v}
is trivial and
\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})\simeq\mathrm{rec}_{\mathrm{GT}}(\pi^{\prime}_{v}\otimes|\nu_{\mathrm{sim}}|^{-3/2})
. Since the
L
-packet of an unramified
L
-parameter valued in
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
is a singleton, we have
\pi_{v}\simeq\pi^{\prime}_{v}
.
∎

Lemma 4.1.7
.

Let
\pi
be a regular cuspidal automorphic representation of
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
where
G=\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
or
G=\mathcal{G}
. Suppose that there exists a continuous irreducible representation
\bar{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
attached to
\pi
, then
\pi
is stable and tempered.

Proof.

If
G=\mathcal{G}
, then we can apply Lemma
4.1.6
to replace
\pi
by a regular cuspidal automorphic representation of
\operatorname{GSp}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
to which
\bar{r}
is attached. Since being stable and tempered is characterized by components at almost all finite places, it suffices to prove the claim for
G=\operatorname{GSp}_{4}
, and
\bar{r}\simeq\bar{r}_{\pi,p,\iota}
in this case. By
[
BCGP21
, Theorem 2.9.3]
, the irreducibility of
\bar{r}
implies that
\pi
is of general type, which implies that
\pi
is stable and tempered by Arthur’s classification (see item (a)-(f) at the end of
[
Art04
]
).

To be more precise, suppose that
\pi
is not stable, i.e.
\pi
is either CAP or endoscopic (again, in the sense of
[
Sor09
]
). If
\pi
is CAP, then by the results of Piatetski-Shapiro and Soudry
[
PS83
,
Sou88
]
,
\pi
can be realized as a theta lift (such
\pi
corresponds to either Soudry type or Saito–Kurokawa type in
[
Art04
]
). Using this, one can easily see that
\bar{r}
has to be reducible. If
\pi
is endoscopic, it corresponds to Yoshida type in
loc. cit.
, and
\bar{r}
in this case has to be reducible as well.

To show that
\pi
is tempered, note that the transfer
\Pi
of
\pi
to
\operatorname{GL}_{4}
is cuspidal. By Ramanujan conjecture (proven in
[
Shi11
]
in this case),
\Pi_{v}
is tempered for all places
v
. This implies that the
L
-parameter attached to
\Pi_{v}
has bounded image. By
[
GT11
, Main Theorem (vii)]
, the
L
-packet containing
\pi_{v}
has a unique generic tempered element. Then for any finite place
v
at which
\pi
is unramified,
\pi_{v}
is generic tempered because the
L
-packet containing it is a singleton.
∎

4.2
p
-adic automorphic forms

We now define integral models of spaces of automorphic forms on
\mathcal{G}
over
\mathcal{O}
. Fix a choice of isomorphism
\iota:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and let
\psi:=\chi_{p,\iota}\epsilon^{-3}

\iota
, so we omit it from the notation. If
V
is an
\mathcal{O}

Definition 4.2.1
.

We say that a compact open subgroup

as above is

\mathcal{O}
-module

. Then

\iota

4.3
A patching lemma

In order to clarify the patching method used in the next section, we present here a formalized version. In this section
G
denotes a locally profinite group having a countable fundamental system of open neighbourhoods at the identity.

Definition 4.3.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and
\mathfrak{j}=\{\mathfrak{j}_{1}\supseteq\mathfrak{j}_{2}\supseteq\cdots\}
a system of ideals. A
(G,A,\mathfrak{j})
-inverse system
is an inverse system
\mathfrak{M}=\{M_{r}(H)\}_{r\geq 1,H\leq_{\mathrm{c.o.}}G}
of finite

-modules together with a collection of morphisms of inverse systems
g_{*}:M_{r}(H)\rightarrow M_{r}(gHg^{-1})
for each

satisfying
(gh)_{*}=g_{*}h_{*}
and
\mathrm{id}_{*}=\mathrm{id}
. We assume that the transition maps have the property that they induce isomorphisms

M_{r+1}(H)/\mathfrak{j}_{r}\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{r}(H)

and

M_{r}(H^{\prime})_{H}\xrightarrow{\sim}M_{r}(H)

for all
r\geq 1,H\leq_{\mathrm{c.o.}}G
, and any open normal subgroup
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
. (In particular, all transition maps are surjective.) These objects form a category in an obvious way.

We say that
\mathfrak{M}
is
projective
if
M_{r}(H^{\prime})
is a projective
(A/\mathfrak{j}_{r})[H/H^{\prime}]
-module whenever

is an open normal subgroup.

Remark 4.3.2
.

If

is another system of ideals of

such that
\mathfrak{b}_{r}\supseteq\mathfrak{j}_{r}
then
(M_{r}(K)/\mathfrak{b}_{r})_{r,K}
is a (projective)
(G,A,\mathfrak{b})
-inverse system.

Definition 4.3.3

(cf.
[
Eme10
]

Let

-algebra with finite residue field. We let

-modules

-action extends to an action of

into a linear topological

-module for the profinite topology on

Lemma 4.3.4
.

Let

be a complete local noetherian
\mathcal{O}
-algebra with finite residue field, and suppose that the topology determined by

is the max-adic one. The functor

-inverse systems and

Abstract
. The coadjoint
representation of the BMS group in four dimensions is constructed
in a formulation that covers both the sphere and the punctured
plane. The structure constants are worked out for different
choices of bases. The conserved current algebra of non-radiative
asymptotically flat spacetimes is explicitly interpreted in these
terms.

1
Introduction

The BMS group
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
is
the symmetry group of four-dimensional asymptotically flat spacetimes at null
infinity. The precise form of this group changes when one replaces the celestial
sphere by a different Riemann surface
[
6
,
7
]
. Whereas
unitary irreducible representations of the BMS group are directly relevant for
the quantum theory
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, the coadjoint
representation is intimately connected to classical solution space through the
momentum map. Unitary irreducible representations come later, after the
classification of coadjoint orbits, via geometric quantization.

In this paper, we provide the detailed construction of the coadjoint
representation of the BMS group and of the algebra on the celestial sphere and
the punctured plane. In the case of the sphere, we explicitly identify the
coadjoint representation in the gravitational data of non-radiative spacetimes.

In order to set the stage, we start in section
2

by providing the (corrected) commutation relations of the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}

algebra on the celestial sphere. Since the structure of the BMS group is the
same as that of the Poincaré group in the sense that both are semi-direct
product groups with an abelian ideal, we recall in section

3
the structure of the coadjoint representation of such
groups and algebras
[
11
]
.

In a next step in section
4
, we provide a description of the
coadjoint representation of the BMS group in four dimensions in terms of
suitably weighted functions on a two-dimensional surface by focussing on local
aspects. For the presentation, after some generic considerations based on

[
12
,
13
]
, we will use a Weyl covariant derivative instead
of the standard “eth” operator
[
5
,
14
,
15
]
. Note
that our conventions for these derivatives differ somewhat from those originally
introduced in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
for related
reasons. The description applies both to the “global” and “local” versions
of the algebra

[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
, which are
studied explicitly in sections
5
and

6
,
respectively. In the former section, we use extensively results of sections 4.14
and 4.15 of
[
17
]
. Besides the standard choice of rotation and
boost generators as used originally in
[
3
]
, we also provide
explicit commutation relations adapted to the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\times\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
decomposition of the Lorentz algebra. In the latter
section, our expansions follow the standard conventions used in the context of
two-dimensional conformal field theories.

In section
7
, we briefly comment the case of the
cylinder. Finally, in the case of the sphere and for non-radiative
asymptotically flat spacetimes, we explicitly construct the equivariant map from
the free gravitational data at
{\mycal I}^{+}
to the coadjoint representation in
section
8
.

The coadjoint representation of the BMS group in three dimensions

[
23
]
(see also
[
24
,
25
]
) has
been investigated in
[
26
]
. In that case, the abelian factor
can be identified with the Lie algebra of the non abelian factor acted upon by
the adjoint representation, which simplifies the classification of coadjoint
orbits considerably. Furthermore, central extensions are the familiar ones
directly related to the Virasoro group and algebra. Neither of these
simplifications occur in four dimensions. As a consequence, we will not discuss
the classification of coadjoint orbits in this paper. Also, central extensions
that are relevant in the gravitational context are of a different nature

[
27
]
, and will not be considered here.

Poincaré and BMS algebras on the celestial sphere

The structure constants of the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}
algebra on the celestial sphere
have been worked out in
[
3
]
(see also
[
28
]
for
corrections, and
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
for reviews).
More details on the geometric interpretation can be found in

[
5
,
15
]
(see also

[
31
,
17
,
18
,
32
,
33
]
). In
this section, we start by providing the standard commutation relations of the

\mathfrak{bms}_{4}
algebra in terms of rotation and boost generators.

Let
x^{a}
,
a=0,\dots 3
, be Cartesian coordinates on Minkowski spacetime where

\eta_{ab}
=
\text{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
and its inverse
\eta^{ab}
are used to
lower and raise indices. The starting point is the Poincaré algebra with
generators

L^{ab}=L^{[ab]}=-(x^{a}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{b}}$}\kern 1.0pt-x^{b}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{a}}$}\kern 1.0pt),\quad P^{a}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{a}},

(2.1)

satisfying

\begin{split}[L^{ab},L^{cd}]&=-(\eta^{bc}L^{ad}-\eta^{ac}L^{bd}-\eta^{bd}L^{ac}+\eta^{ad}L^{bc}),\\
[P^{a},L^{bc}]&=-(\eta^{ab}P^{c}-\eta^{ac}P^{b}).\end{split}

(2.2)

When splitting into suitable combinations of rotation and boost generators and
of translation generators,

\begin{split}L_{z}&=L^{12},\quad L^{\pm}=\pm iL^{23}+L^{13},\quad K_{z}=L^{30},\quad K^{\pm}=\mp iL^{20}-L^{10},\\
H&=P^{0},\quad P_{z}=-\frac{1}{2}P^{3},\quad P^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(iP^{2}\pm P^{1}),\end{split}

(2.3)

the non-vanishing commutation relations of the Poincaré algebra become

\begin{split}[L^{+},L^{-}]&=2iL_{z},\quad[L_{z},L^{\pm}]=\pm iL^{\pm},\quad[K^{+},K^{-}]=-2iL_{z},\\
[K_{z},K^{\pm}]&=L^{\pm},\quad[L^{+},K^{-}]=2K_{z},\quad[L^{-},K^{+}]=2K_{z},\\
[L_{z},K^{\pm}]&=\pm iK^{\pm},\quad[L^{\pm},K_{z}]=-K^{\pm},\\
[K_{z},H]&=2P_{z},\quad[K^{\pm},H]=\pm 2P^{\pm},\quad[L^{\pm},P_{z}]=\mp P^{\pm},\quad[K_{z},P_{z}]=\frac{1}{2}H,\\
[L_{z},P^{\pm}]&=\pm iP^{\pm},\ [L^{+},P^{-}]=-2P_{z}=-[L^{-},P^{+}],\\
[K^{+},P^{-}]&=-H=-[K^{-},P^{+}].\end{split}

(2.4)

In terms of spherical coordinates and a retarded time coordinate,

r=\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}+(x^{3})^{2}},\quad u=x^{0}-r,\quad r\cos\theta=x^{3},\quad r\sin\theta e^{i\phi}=x^{1}+ix^{2},

(2.5)

the Poincaré generators read

\begin{split}L_{z}&=\partial_{\phi},\\
L^{\pm}&=-e^{\pm i\phi}\big[\partial_{\theta}\pm i\cot\theta\partial_{\phi}\big],\\
K_{z}&=-(1+\frac{u}{r})\cos\theta(r\partial_{r})+\cos\theta(u\partial_{u})+(1+\frac{u}{r})\sin\theta\partial_{\theta},\\
K^{\pm}&=e^{\pm i\phi}\big[(1+\frac{u}{r})\sin\theta(r\partial_{r})-\sin\theta(u\partial_{u})+(1+\frac{u}{r})\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}\pm(1+\frac{u}{r})\frac{i}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\big],\\
H&=\partial_{u},\\
-2P_{z}&=\cos\theta(-\partial_{r}+\partial_{u})+\frac{1}{r}\sin\theta\partial_{\theta},\\
\pm 2P^{\pm}&=e^{\pm i\phi}\big[\sin\theta(\partial_{r}-\partial_{u})+\frac{1}{r}\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}\pm\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\big].\end{split}

(2.6)

As may be shown on general grounds or explicitly checked, the Poincaré algebra
in the form (
2.4
) may also be represented in terms of these generators
restricted to the surface
r=cte\to\infty
,

\begin{split}L_{z}&=\partial_{\phi},\quad L^{\pm}=-e^{\pm i\phi}\big[\partial_{\theta}\pm i\cot\theta\partial_{\phi}\big],\\
K_{z}&=\cos\theta(u\partial_{u})+\sin\theta\partial_{\theta},\quad K^{\pm}=e^{\pm i\phi}\big[-\sin\theta(u\partial_{u})+\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}\pm\frac{i}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\big],\\
H&=\partial_{u},\quad-2P_{z}=\cos\theta\partial_{u},\quad\pm 2P^{\pm}=-e^{\pm i\phi}\sin\theta\partial_{u}.\end{split}

(2.7)

The next step is to represent the Poincaré algebra at
u=0
. This can be done
by simply restricting the Lorentz generators to that surface, and by
representing the translation generators by suitable functions on that surface,

\begin{split}L_{z}&=\partial_{\phi},\quad L^{\pm}=-e^{\pm i\phi}\big[\partial_{\theta}\pm i\cot\theta\partial_{\phi}\big],\\
K_{z}&=\sin\theta\partial_{\theta},\quad K^{\pm}=e^{\pm i\phi}\big[\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}\pm\frac{i}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\phi}\big],\\
H&=1={}_{0}Z_{0,0},\quad P_{z}=-\frac{1}{2}\cos\theta={}_{0}Z_{1,0},\quad P^{\pm}=\mp\frac{1}{2}e^{\pm i\phi}\sin\theta={}_{0}Z_{1,\pm 1},\end{split}

(2.8)

while in addition, defining for any function
f
on the sphere,

\begin{split}[L_{z},f]&=L_{z}(f),\quad[L^{\pm},f]=L^{\pm}(f),\\
[K_{z},f]&=K_{z}(f)-\cos\theta f,\quad[K^{\pm},f]=K^{\pm}(f)+e^{\pm i\phi}\sin\theta f.\end{split}

(2.9)

When applied to the four functions in the last line of (
2.8
), this
reproduces the commutation relations of the Lorentz with the translation
generators in the Poincaré algebra, i.e., the last two lines of (
2.4
).
The general expression for the unnormalized spherical harmonics
{}_{s}Z_{j,m}

are explicitly given in Appendix
A
.

How the Poincaré algebra is enhanced to the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}
algebra in the
context of asymptotically flat spacetimes at null infinity is discussed in the
references at the beginning of this section. Besides the original reference

[
3
]
, we also refer to the re-derivation in
[
20
]

for more details.

In the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}
algebra, the commutation relations for the Lorentz
sub-algebra are unchanged and given in the first three lines of (
2.4
).
The commutation relations for the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}
algebra are then completed
by choosing a basis for functions on the sphere. Whatever basis is chosen, the
supertranslation generators
\mathcal{T}^{G}_{A}
commute,

[\mathcal{T}^{G}_{A},\mathcal{T}^{G}_{A^{\prime}}]=0.

(2.10)

To get explicit structure constants for the commutators involving Lorentz and
supertranslation generators, one may start with an unnormalized basis involving
associated Legendre functions,

\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}=P_{j}^{m}(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi},\quad P^{m}_{j}(x)=(-1)^{m}(1-x^{2})^{\frac{m}{2}}\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}P_{j}(x).

(2.11)

The action of the Lorentz generators on the
\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}
is then worked
out according to (
2.9
) by using suitable properties of associated
Legendre functions (see e.g. 8.733 and 8.735 of
[
34
]
),

\displaystyle[L_{z},\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}]=im\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m},

(2.12)

\displaystyle[L^{+},\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}]=-\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m+1},

(2.13)

\displaystyle[L^{-},\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}]=(j-m+1)(j+m)\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m-1},

(2.14)

\displaystyle[K_{z},\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}]=\frac{(j-1)(j-m+1)}{2j+1}\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j+1,m}-\frac{{(j+2)(j+m)}}{2j+1}\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j-1,m},

(2.15)

\displaystyle[K^{+},\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}]=\frac{j-1}{2j+1}\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j+1,m+1}+\frac{j+2}{2j+1}\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j-1,m+1},

(2.16)

[K^{-},\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j,m}]=-\frac{(j-1)(j-m+1)(j-m+2)}{2j+1}\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j+1,m-1}\\
-\frac{(j+2)(j+m)(j+m-1)}{2j+1}\mathcal{T}^{U}_{j-1,m-1}.

(2.17)

For a normalized basis in terms of standard spherical harmonics,

\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m}={}_{0}Y_{j,m}=\sqrt{\frac{(2l+1)(l-m)!}{4\pi(l+m)!}}P_{j}^{m}(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi},

(2.18)

one finds instead

\displaystyle[L_{z},\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m}]=im\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m},

(2.19)

\displaystyle[L^{\pm},\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m}]=\mp\sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m\pm 1},

(2.20)

[K_{z},\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m}]=(j-1)\sqrt{\frac{(j+m+1)(j-m+1)}{(2j+1)(2j+3)}}\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j+1,m}\\
-(j+2)\sqrt{\frac{(j+m)(j-m)}{(2j-1)(2j+1)}}\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j-1,m},

(2.21)

[K^{\pm},\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j,m}]=\pm(j-1)\sqrt{\frac{(j\pm m+2)(j\pm m+1)}{(2j+1)(2j+3)}}\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j+1,m\pm 1}\\
\pm(j+2)\sqrt{\frac{(j\mp m)(j\mp m-1)}{(2j-1)(2j+1)}}\mathcal{T}^{S}_{j-1,m\pm 1}.

(2.22)

This is the form under which the commutation relations between Lorentz
and supertranslation generators usually appear in the literature (with
due care devoted to various conventions and correction of misprints).

3
Coadjoint representations of semi-direct product groups and
algebras

For a semi-direct product group of the form
G\ltimes_{\sigma}A
, with

G
a Lie group and
A
an abelian Lie group seen as a vector space
with the addition, the group law is given by

(f,\alpha)\cdot(g,\beta)=(f\cdot g,\alpha+\sigma_{f}(\beta)),

(3.1)

while
\sigma
is a representation of
G
on
A
. The associated Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{g}\oright_{\Sigma}A
, the Lie algebra of
A
being identified with

A
itself, and

[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)]=([X,Y],\Sigma_{X}\beta-\Sigma_{Y}\alpha),

(3.2)

where
\Sigma
is the differential of
\sigma
. The adjoint actions of
the group and the algebra are then given by

{\rm Ad}_{(f,\alpha)}(X,\beta)=({\rm Ad}_{f}X,\sigma_{f}\beta-\Sigma_{{\rm Ad}_{f}X}\alpha),

(3.3)

{\rm ad}_{(X,\alpha)}(Y,\beta)=([X,Y],\Sigma_{X}\beta-\Sigma_{Y}\alpha).

(3.4)

The dual space to the Lie algebra is given by
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus A^{*}
, with non-degenerate pairing denoted by

\langle(j,p),(X,\alpha)\rangle=\langle j,X\rangle+\langle p,\alpha\rangle,

(3.5)

and coadjoint actions

\displaystyle\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{(f,\alpha)}(j,p),(Y,\beta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(j,p),{\rm Ad}_{(f,\alpha)^{-1}}(Y,\beta)\rangle,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\langle{\rm ad}^{*}_{(X,\alpha)}(j,p),(Y,\beta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(j,p),-{\rm ad}_{(X,\alpha)}(Y,\beta)\rangle.

(3.7)

Defining
\times:A\oplus A^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by

\langle\alpha\times p,X\rangle=\langle p,\Sigma_{X}\alpha\rangle,

(3.8)

and
\sigma^{*}
to be the dual representation associated with
\sigma
,

\sigma^{*}:G\times A^{*}\to A^{*}
,

\langle\sigma^{*}_{f}p,\alpha\rangle=\langle p,\sigma_{f^{-}1}\alpha\rangle,

(3.9)

the coadjoint representations are given by

{\rm Ad}^{*}_{(f,\alpha)}(j,p)=({\rm Ad}^{*}_{f}j+\alpha\times\sigma^{*}_{f}p,\sigma^{*}_{f}p),

(3.10)

{\rm ad}^{*}_{(X,\alpha)}(j,p)=({\rm ad}^{*}_{X}j+\alpha\times p,\Sigma^{*}_{X}p).

(3.11)

In terms of generators,
(e_{A},e_{\alpha})
of
\mathfrak{g}\oright_{\Sigma}A
, with
(e_{*}^{A},e_{*}^{\alpha})
the associated dual basis of

\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus A^{*}
,

[e_{A},e_{B}]=f_{AB}^{C}e_{C},\quad[e_{A},e_{\alpha}]=f^{\beta}_{A\alpha}e_{\beta},\quad[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]=0,

(3.12)

the coadjoint representation of the algebra (
3.11
) becomes

{\rm ad}^{*}_{e_{A}}e_{*}^{B}=-f^{B}_{AC}e_{*}^{C},\quad{\rm ad}^{*}_{e_{\alpha}}e_{*}^{B}=0,\quad{\rm ad}^{*}_{e_{A}}e_{*}^{\beta}=-f^{\beta}_{A\gamma}e_{*}^{\gamma},\quad{\rm ad}^{*}_{e_{\alpha}}e_{*}^{\beta}=-f^{\beta}_{\alpha C}e_{*}^{C}.

(3.13)

4
General structure of the coadjoint representation of BMS4

4.1
Background structure

4.1.1
Extended conformal transformations

Consider an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with coordinates

x^{\alpha}
and metric
g_{\alpha\beta}(x)
which transforms under
invertible coordinate transformations
x^{\prime\alpha}=x^{\prime\alpha}(x)
as

g^{\prime}_{\gamma\delta}(x^{\prime})=g_{\alpha\beta}(x)\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial x^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\prime\gamma}}$}\kern 1.0pt\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial x^{\beta}}{\partial x^{\prime\delta}}$}\kern 1.0pt.

(4.1)

Conformal coordinate transformations are such invertible coordinate
transformations for which

g^{\prime}_{\gamma\delta}(x^{\prime})=g_{\gamma\delta}(x^{\prime})\Omega^{2}(x^{\prime}).

(4.2)

Consider then a two-dimensional surface
\mathcal{S}
with coordinates

x^{\alpha}=(\xi,\bar{\xi})
and a conformally flat metric

ds^{2}=g_{\alpha\beta}dx^{\alpha}dx^{\beta}=-2(P\bar{P})^{-1}d\xi d\bar{\xi},

(4.3)

for some nowhere vanishing
P(x)
. In this case, coordinate
transformations of the form

\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime}(\xi),\quad\bar{\xi}^{\prime}=\bar{\xi}^{\prime}(\bar{\xi})

(4.4)

are conformal coordinate transformations with

\Omega(x^{\prime})=\left[\frac{(P\bar{P})(x^{\prime})}{(P\bar{P})(x)}J\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad J=\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}.

(4.5)

For such conformal transformations, the transformation law

P^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=P(x)\frac{\partial\xi^{\prime}}{\partial\xi},\quad\bar{P}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\bar{P}(x)\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}{\partial\bar{\xi}},

(4.6)

induces the transformation (
4.2
) of the metric components. The
more general transformation law

P^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=P(x)\frac{\partial\xi^{\prime}}{\partial\xi}e^{-E(x^{\prime})},\quad\bar{P}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\bar{P}(x)\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}{\partial\bar{\xi}}e^{-\bar{E}(x^{\prime})},

(4.7)

with
E
a complex scalar field can be understood as follows. Writing the metric
with suitable zweibeins as,

ds^{2}={e^{A}}_{\alpha}dx^{\alpha}\eta_{AB}{e^{B}}_{\beta}dx^{\beta},\quad\eta_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad{e_{1}}^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}=P\partial,\quad{e_{2}}^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}=\bar{P}\bar{\partial},

(4.8)

they correspond to the transformations of the zweibeins under conformal
coordinate transformations. At the same time, the imaginary part
iE_{I}
produces
a local rotation of the zweibeins while the real part of
E_{R}
generates the
Weyl rescaling of the metric. The associated conformal factor is

\Omega(x^{\prime})=\left[\frac{(P\bar{P})(x^{\prime})}{(P\bar{P})(x)}J\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{E_{R}(x^{\prime})}.

(4.9)

Three relevant subclasses of the extended transformations
(
4.7
) are

(i)
Conformal coordinate transformation:
taking
E=0=\bar{E}
in (
4.7
) leads back to (
4.6
), and thus,
when applied to the metric components, to the same transformations
(
4.2
) as those coming from the conformal coordinate
transformation applied to the metric tensor.

(ii)
Complex Weyl rescaling:
taking
\xi^{\prime}=\xi
,

\bar{\xi}^{\prime}=\bar{\xi}
in (
4.7
) gives

P^{\prime}(x)=P(x)e^{-E(x)},\quad\bar{P}^{\prime}(x)=\bar{P}(x)e^{-\bar{E}(x)}.

(4.10)

This induces a real local Weyl rescaling on the metric,

g^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}(x)=e^{2E_{R}}g_{\alpha\beta}(x).

(4.11)

When the real part
E_{R}=0
, the metric is unchanged.

(iii)
Fixed conformal factor:
fixing as in

[
5
,
15
]
the conformal factor to be a prescribed function of
its arguments,
P(x)=P_{F}(x)
,
\bar{P}(x)=\bar{P}_{F}(x)
and
P^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=P_{F}(x^{\prime})
,
\bar{P}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\bar{P}_{F}(x^{\prime})
in (
4.7
), implies that complex Weyl
rescalings are frozen to

e^{E(x^{\prime})}=\frac{P_{F}(x)}{P_{F}(x^{\prime})}\frac{\partial\xi^{\prime}}{\partial\xi}\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}e^{E_{R}(x^{\prime})}=J^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\frac{(P_{F}\bar{P}_{F})(x)}{(P_{F}\bar{P}_{F})(x^{\prime})}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\\
e^{iE_{I}(x^{\prime})}=\left[\frac{(P_{F}/\bar{P}_{F})(x)}{(P_{F}/\bar{P}_{F})(x^{\prime})}\left(\frac{\partial\xi^{\prime}/\partial\xi}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}/\partial\bar{\xi}}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{array}\right.

(4.12)

where
E=E_{R}+iE_{I}
. We will mostly be interested in 2 particular cases
below. The first is when
\mathcal{S}
is a 2-sphere of radius
R
with metric

ds^{2}=-R^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2})
and

\xi=\bar{\zeta}=\cot{\frac{\theta}{2}}e^{-i\phi}
, so that

P_{S}=\frac{1+\xi\bar{\xi}}{R\sqrt{2}}=\bar{P}_{S},

(4.13)

see
[
17
]
section 4.15 for details.

The second is the punctured complex plane, the complex plane with the
origin removed
\mathbb{C}_{0}=\mathbb{C}-\{0\}
, with standard metric

{ds}^{2}=-2dzd\bar{z}
so that

P=1={\bar{P}}.

(4.14)

4.1.2
Conformal fields and weighted scalars

Under conformal coordinate transformations and complex Weyl rescalings,
fields
\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}}
of conformal
dimensions
(h,\bar{h})
and Weyl weights
(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})

transform as

{\phi^{\prime}}^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}}(x^{\prime})=e^{\lambda E(x^{\prime})}e^{\bar{\lambda}\bar{E}(x^{\prime})}\Big(\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}$}\kern 1.0pt\Big)^{h}\Big(\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}$}\kern 1.0pt\Big)^{\bar{h}}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}(x).

(4.15)

It follows from (
4.7
) that the conformal dimensions and Weyl
weights of
P
are both
(-1,0)
whereas those of
\bar{P}
are both

(0,-1)
. These quantities can be used to map the fields

\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}}
into scalars
\eta^{s,w}

\eta^{s,w}=P^{h}\bar{P}^{\bar{h}}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}},

(4.16)

of spin and conformal weights
[s,w]
,

s=(h-\bar{h})-(\lambda-\bar{\lambda}),\quad w=-(h+\bar{h})+(\lambda+\bar{\lambda}),

(4.17)

which transform under conformal coordinate transformations and complex
Weyl rescalings as

\eta^{\prime s,w}(x^{\prime})=e^{wE_{R}(x^{\prime})}e^{-isE_{I}(x^{\prime})}\eta^{s,w}(x).

(4.18)

4.1.3
Derivative operators

The only non-vanishing components of the Levi-Civita connection
associated with (
4.3
) are

\Gamma^{\xi}_{\xi\xi}=-\partial\ln(P\bar{P}),\quad\Gamma^{\bar{\xi}}_{\bar{\xi}\bar{\xi}}=-\bar{\partial}\ln(P\bar{P}),

(4.19)

where
\partial=\partial_{\xi},\bar{\partial}=\partial_{\bar{\xi}}
. Equation (
4.7
) induces
their transformation law under conformal coordinate transformations
combined with Weyl rescalings,

\Gamma^{\prime\xi^{\prime}}_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=\Gamma^{\xi}_{\xi\xi}(x)\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\partial\xi^{\prime}}{\partial\xi}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}\partial\xi^{\prime}}+2\partial^{\prime}E_{R}(x^{\prime}),

(4.20)

with a similar transformation law for
\Gamma^{\bar{\xi}}_{\bar{\xi}\bar{\xi}}
. In
addition to the conformally flat metric (
4.3
), one supposes that

\mathcal{S}
is endowed with a Weyl connection
({W},\bar{{W}})
(see
e.g.
[
12
]
) that transforms as

{W}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\Big(\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\Big){W}(x)+2\partial^{\prime}E_{R}(x^{\prime}),\quad\bar{{W}}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\Big(\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}\Big)\bar{{W}}(x)+2\bar{\partial}^{\prime}E_{R}(x^{\prime}).

(4.21)

Using
P
,
\bar{P}
,
{W}
,
\bar{{W}}
, one can define

\begin{split}&K=\frac{1}{2}(\partial\ln\mu-\Gamma^{\xi}_{\xi\xi})+{W}=\partial\ln\bar{P}+{W},\quad O=\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma^{\xi}_{\xi\xi}-\partial\ln\mu)=-\partial\ln\bar{P}\quad\\
&\bar{K}=\frac{1}{2}(\bar{\partial}\ln\bar{\mu}-\Gamma^{\bar{\xi}}_{\bar{\xi}\bar{\xi}})+\bar{{W}}=\bar{\partial}\ln P+\bar{{W}},\quad\bar{O}=\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma^{\bar{\xi}}_{\bar{\xi}\bar{\xi}}-\bar{\partial}\ln\bar{\mu})=-\bar{\partial}\ln P,\end{split}

(4.22)

where
\mu=\frac{\bar{P}}{P}
is a Beltrami differential. These
objects transform as

\begin{split}K^{\prime}(x^{\prime})&=\Big(\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\Big)K(x)+\partial^{\prime}E(x^{\prime}),\quad O^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\Big(\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\Big)O(x)+\partial^{\prime}\bar{E}(x^{\prime}),\\
\bar{K}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})&=\Big(\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}\Big)\bar{K}(x)+\bar{\partial}^{\prime}\bar{E}(x^{\prime}),\quad\bar{O}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\Big(\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}\Big)\bar{O}(x)+\bar{\partial}^{\prime}E(x^{\prime}).\end{split}

(4.23)

The Weyl covariant derivative can then be defined as

\begin{split}D\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}&=[\nabla+(h-\lambda)K+(h-\bar{\lambda})O]\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}},\\
\bar{D}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}&=[\bar{\nabla}+(\bar{h}-\bar{\lambda})\bar{K}+(\bar{h}-\lambda)\bar{O}]\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}},\end{split}

(4.24)

where
\nabla\equiv\nabla_{\xi}
and

\bar{\nabla}\equiv\nabla_{\bar{\xi}}
are the components of the
covariant derivative associated to the Levi-Civita connection
(
4.19
). Notice that the field
P
(
\bar{P}
) of Weyl weights

(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})=(-1,0)
(resp.

(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})=(0,-1)
) and conformal dimensions

(h,\bar{h})=(-1,0)
(resp.
(h,\bar{h})=(0,-1)
) are
holomorphic (resp. anti-holomorphic) with respect to the Weyl
covariant derivative, namely
\bar{D}P=0
(resp.
D\bar{P}=0
). Under conformal coordinate transformations and complex Weyl
rescalings, we have

\begin{split}(D\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}})^{\prime}(x^{\prime})&=e^{\lambda E(x^{\prime})}e^{\bar{\lambda}\bar{E}(x^{\prime})}\Big(\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\Big)^{h+1}\Big(\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}\Big)^{\bar{h}}(D\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}})(x),\\
(D\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}})^{\prime}(x^{\prime})&=e^{\lambda E(x^{\prime})}e^{\bar{\lambda}\bar{E}(x^{\prime})}\Big(\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}\Big)^{h}\Big(\frac{\partial\bar{\xi}}{\partial\bar{\xi}^{\prime}}\Big)^{\bar{h}+1}(\bar{D}\phi^{\lambda,\bar{\lambda}}_{h,\bar{h}})(x).\end{split}

(4.25)

Therefore, the operator
D
(
\bar{D}
) acts on fields
of Weyl weights
(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})
and conformal dimensions

(h,\bar{h})
to produce fields of Weyl weights
(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})
and conformal dimensions
(h+1,\bar{h})
(resp.
(h,\bar{h}+1)
).

In the following we will assume that the only fields carrying non-vanishing Weyl
weights are
P,\bar{P}
. All other fields are thus of the form
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{0,0}
with associated scalars
\eta^{s,w}=P^{h}\bar{P}^{\bar{h}}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{0,0}
so that

s=h-\bar{h},\quad w=-(h+\bar{h}),\quad h=\frac{s-w}{2},\quad\bar{h}=-\frac{s+w}{2}.

(4.26)

If

\eth\eta^{s,w}=P^{h+1}\bar{P}^{\bar{h}}(\nabla\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{0,0}),\quad\bar{\eth}\eta^{s,w}=P^{h}\bar{P}^{\bar{h}+1}(\bar{\nabla}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}^{0,0}),

(4.27)

then

\begin{split}\eth\eta^{s,w}=P\bar{P}^{-s}\partial(\bar{P}^{s}\eta^{s,w})=P(\partial-sO)\eta^{s,w},\\
\bar{\eth}\eta^{s,w}=\bar{P}P^{s}\bar{\partial}(P^{-s}\eta^{s,w})=\bar{P}(\bar{\partial}+s\bar{O})\eta^{s,w}.\end{split}

(4.28)

in agreement with expressions (4.14.34) and (4.14.33) of

[
17
]
. Under conformal coordinate transformations and
complex Weyl rescalings,

\begin{split}(\eth\eta^{s,w})^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=e^{(w-1)E_{R}(x^{\prime})}e^{-i(s+1)E_{I}(x^{\prime})}\big[\eth+(w-s)P\partial E_{R}(x^{\prime}(x))\big]\eta^{s,w}(x),\\
(\bar{\eth}\eta^{s,w})^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=e^{(w-1)E_{R}(x^{\prime})}e^{-i(s-1)E_{I}(x^{\prime})}\big[\bar{\eth}+(w+s)\bar{P}\bar{\partial}E_{R}(x^{\prime}(x))\big]\eta^{s,w}(x).\end{split}

(4.29)

Hence, the scalars
\eth\eta^{s,w}
and
\bar{\eth}\eta^{s,w}
transform as
scalars of weights
[s+1,w-1]
respectively
[s-1,w-1]
only if
w=s\iff h=0

respectively
w=-s\iff\bar{h}=0
. Alternatively, one may limit oneself to
complex Weyl rescalings with
E_{R}=0
so that only rotations of the zweibeins are
allowed, with no Weyl rescaling of the metric. In this case, only spin weight

s
is relevant.

When using the Weyl covariant derivative
D
instead of the covariant derivative

\nabla
associated to the Christoffel connection, this issue does not arise.
Denoting
\mathcal{D}\eta^{s,w}
and

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}\eta^{s,w}&=\left[\eth+\left(\frac{s-w}{2}\right)(\mathcal{O}+\mathcal{K})\right]\eta^{s,w}=P\left[\partial-\frac{s}{2}(O-K)-\frac{w}{2}(O+K)\right]\eta^{s,w},\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}\eta^{s,w}&=\left[\bar{\eth}-\left(\frac{w+s}{2}\right)(\bar{\mathcal{O}}+\bar{\mathcal{K}})\right]\eta^{s,w}=\bar{P}\left[\bar{\partial}+\frac{s}{2}(\bar{O}-\bar{K})-\frac{w}{2}(\bar{O}+\bar{K})\right]\eta^{s,w},\end{split}

(4.30)

where
\mathcal{O}=PO
,
\mathcal{K}=PK
,

\bar{\mathcal{O}}=\bar{P}\bar{O}
,

\bar{\mathcal{K}}=\bar{P}\bar{K}
. Under conformal coordinate

\begin{split}(\mathcal{D}\eta^{s,w})^{\prime}(x^{\prime})&=e^{(w-1)E_{R}(x^{\prime})}e^{-i(s+1)E_{I}(x^{\prime})}(\mathcal{D}\eta^{s,w})(x),\\
(\bar{\mathcal{D}}\eta^{s,w})^{\prime}(x^{\prime})&=e^{(w-1)E_{R}(x^{\prime})}e^{-i(s-1)E_{I}(x^{\prime})}(\bar{\mathcal{D}}\eta^{s,w})(x).\end{split}

(4.31)

Therefore, the operator
\mathcal{D}

(
\bar{\mathcal{D}}
) acts on weighted scalars
[s,w]
to produce
weighted scalars
[s+1,w-1]
(resp.
[s-1,w-1]

The following property holds:

(4.32)

Note that

4.2.1
Weighted scalars

In terms of above ingredients, the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}
algebra may be
defined by triplets
s=(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{T})
with the commutation
relations

[(\mathcal{Y}_{1},\bar{\mathcal{Y}}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1}),(\mathcal{Y}_{2},\bar{\mathcal{Y}}_{2},\mathcal{T}_{2})]=(\hat{\mathcal{Y}},\hat{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}},\hat{\mathcal{T}}),

(4.34)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} \hat{\mathcal{Y}}&=\mathcal{Y}_{1}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Y}_{2}-\mathcal{Y}_{2}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\,,\\
\hat{\mathcal{T}}&=\mathcal{Y}_{1}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Y}_{1}\mathcal{T}_{2}-(1\leftrightarrow 2)+{\rm c.c.}\,.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(4.35)

Elements of the type
(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}},0)
form a
sub-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. As usual, we identify the individual
entries of the triplets/doublets with the triplets/doublets where all

\eta^{s,w}

\mathfrak{g}
is defined by

\mathcal{Y}\cdot\eta^{s,w}=\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}\eta^{s,w}+\frac{s-w}{2}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Y}\eta^{s,w},\quad\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\cdot\eta^{s,w}=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\eta^{s,w}-\frac{s+w}{2}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\eta^{s,w}.

(4.36)

At this stage, we note that this representation, and also the
\mathfrak{bms}_{4}

algebra above and the coadjoint representation below, may also be written with

\eth,\bar{\eth}
instead of
\mathcal{D},\bar{\mathcal{D}}
because the additional
terms cancel.

In the notation of section
3
, we thus have

X=(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}})
,
\alpha=\mathcal{T}
, and

\Sigma_{X}\alpha=(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}})\cdot\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{T}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{T}+{\rm c.c.}\,.

(4.37)

Elements of
\mathfrak{bms}^{*}_{4}
are denoted by triplets
([\mathcal{J}],[\bar{\mathcal{J}}],\mathcal{P})
where the pairing is given by

\langle([\mathcal{J}],[\bar{\mathcal{J}}],\mathcal{P}),(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{T})\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{S}}d\mu\,[\bar{\mathcal{J}}\mathcal{Y}+\mathcal{J}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}+\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}].

(4.38)

The measure

d\mu(\xi,\bar{\xi})=\frac{iC}{P\bar{P}}d\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi},

(4.39)

for some normalization constant
C
, has dimensions
(0,0)
and weights
[0,2]
.
At this stage, we assume that the integral annihilates total
\mathcal{D}
and

\bar{\mathcal{D}}
derivatives. Furthermore, we require the pairing to be
non-degenerate, which can only be the case when taking quotients with respect to
the equivalence relations
{\mathcal{J}}\sim{\mathcal{J}}+\mathcal{D}{\mathcal{L}}
and

{\bar{\mathcal{J}}}\sim{\bar{\mathcal{J}}}+\bar{\mathcal{D}}{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}

realizations where these assumptions hold will be discussed below.

From the definition of the coadjoint representation (
3.7
), it then
follows that

\begin{split}{\rm ad}^{*}_{(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{T})}\mathcal{J}&=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{J}+2\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{J}+\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{J})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{T}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{P}+\frac{3}{2}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{P},\\
{\rm ad}^{*}_{(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{T})}\mathcal{P}&=\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{P}+\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{P}+{\rm c.c.}\,,\end{split}

(4.40)

where the third term in the first of the above equations does not appear but can

transformation law consistent with the conformal dimensions of
\mathcal{J}
.

Remarks:

(i) The definition makes sense on the level of equivalence classes,

(4.41)

because

(4.42)

(ii) Since the inner product involves complex conjugation, we have

{\rm ad}^{*}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{J}=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{J}+2\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{J},\quad{\rm ad}^{*}_{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}}\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{J})\sim 0,\quad{\rm ad}^{*}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{P}=\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{P}+\frac{3}{2}\bar{\mathcal{D}}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{P},

(4.43)

together with the complex conjugates of these relations.

(iii) In the notation of section
3

j=([\mathcal{J}],[\bar{\mathcal{J}}])
,

(4.45)

equation (

(4.47)

(4.56)

(4.58)

where

denotes the complex conjugate of the expression to the left
of the comma.

Remarks:

(i) As usual for diffeomorphisms, the adjoint and coadjoint representations of
the algebra discussed previously are the differentials of those of the group
discussed in this section up to an overall minus sign.

(ii) As usual in conformal field theory, on the level of the algebra, we will
consider not only the Lie algebra of the globally well-defined conformal
transformations but also the algebra of infinitesimal local conformal
transformations.

(iii) The formulas for the group can also be used to understand how the coadjoint
representation behaves under conformal mappings. We briefly discuss the
standard map from the punctured plane to the cylinder below.

The structure described above is covariant with respect to conformal coordinate

In particular, the above descriptions are valid for all conformal factors

and
\bar{P}
and all two-dimensional surfaces
\mathcal{S}
such that total

\mathcal{D},\bar{\mathcal{D}}

cases:
(i)
the sphere

of radius
R

(ii)

5
Realization on the sphere

5.1
Generalities

If
\xi=\cot\frac{\theta}{2}e^{-i\phi}
, the standard metric on the sphere of
radius
R
is

ds^{2}=-2(P_{S}\bar{P}_{S})^{-1}d\xi d\bar{\xi},\quad P_{S}=\frac{1+\xi\bar{\xi}}{R\sqrt{2}}.

(5.1)

The globally well-defined conformal coordinate
transformations for the sphere are the fractional linear unimodular
transformations

\xi^{\prime}=\frac{a\xi+b}{c\xi+d},\quad ad-bc=1,\quad a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}.

(5.2)

In particular,

\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\prime}}=(c\xi+d)^{2}.

(5.3)

Under combined conformal coordinate transformations and Weyl rescalings, the
metric takes the standard form in the new coordinates if one freezes the Weyl
transformations as in equation (
4.12
). For
P_{F}=P_{S}
,
we have
[

15

e^{E(x^{\prime})}=\frac{P_{S}(x)}{P_{S}(x^{\prime})}\raisebox{0.5pt}{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi^{\prime}}{\partial\xi}$}\kern 1.0pt\iff e^{E_{R}(x^{\prime})}=\frac{1+\xi\bar{\xi}}{|a\xi+b|^{2}+|c\xi+d|^{2}},\quad e^{iE_{I}(x^{\prime})}=\frac{\bar{c}\bar{\xi}+\bar{d}}{c\xi+d}.

(5.4)

In this context,
w
is referred to as the boost weight.

The derivative operators (
4.28
) now take the explicit form (cf. section
4.15 of
[
17
]
)

\eth\eta^{s,w}=P_{S}^{1-s}\partial(P_{S}^{s}\eta^{s,w}),\quad\bar{\eth}\eta^{s,w}=P_{S}^{1+s}\bar{\partial}(P_{S}^{-s}\eta^{s,w}).

(5.5)

The pairing on the sphere is defined between scalars
\eta^{s,w}
of weights
[s,w]

and
\kappa^{s,-w-2}
of weights
[s,-w-2]
as follows,

\langle\kappa^{s,-w-2},\eta^{s,w}\rangle=\frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}}\int_{S^{2}}\frac{id\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}}{P_{S}\bar{P}_{S}}\ \widebar{\kappa^{s,-w-2}}\eta^{s,w},

(5.6)

where the normalization

\frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}}\int_{S^{2}}\frac{id\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}}{P_{S}\bar{P}_{S}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}\frac{id\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}}{(1+\xi\bar{\xi})^{2}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\sin\theta\int^{2\pi}_{0}d\phi=1.

(5.7)

When compared to (
4.38
), we thus
have

\langle([\mathcal{J}],[\bar{\mathcal{J}}],\mathcal{P}),(\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{T})\rangle=\langle\mathcal{J},\mathcal{Y}\rangle+\langle\bar{\mathcal{J}},\bar{\mathcal{Y}}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{P},\mathcal{T}\rangle,\quad d\mu(\xi,\bar{\xi})=\frac{id\xi\wedge d\bar{\xi}}{4\pi R^{2}P_{S}\bar{P}_{S}}.

(5.8)

This pairing has all the required properties.

6
Realization on the punctured complex plane

6.1
Generalities

Since the whole structure is Weyl invariant, one may start from the sphere with
radius
R
and perform a Weyl rescaling as in (
4.10
) with

e^{-E(\xi,\bar{\xi})}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+\xi\bar{\xi}},

(6.1)

followed by the (conformal) coordinate transformations that consists of a simple
rescaling
\xi=R^{-1}z
,
\bar{\xi}=R^{-1}\bar{z}
, so that the metric becomes

ds^{2}=-2dzd\bar{z}.

(6.2)

The next step is to remove the points at infinity and at the origin to go to the
1-punctured complex plane
\mathbb{C}_{0}
. This changes the allowed space of
functions. Conformal coordinate transformations are of the form

z^{\prime}=z^{\prime}(z),\quad\bar{z}^{\prime}=\bar{z}^{\prime}(\bar{z}),

(6.3)

where the globally well-defined ones that are connected to the identity are

z^{\prime}=az,a\in\mathbb{C},a\neq 0
. The derivative operators
\eth
and

\bar{\eth}
defined in (
4.28
) simply become
\partial
and

\bar{\partial}
, respectively. There is no difference between conformal fields
and weighted scalars. Indeed, freezing the conformal factor as in (
4.12
) with
P_{F}=1=\bar{P}_{F}
yields

e^{E(x^{\prime})}=\frac{\partial z^{\prime}}{\partial z}~\Longleftrightarrow~e^{E_{R}(x^{\prime})}=\left(\frac{\partial z^{\prime}}{\partial z}\frac{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}}{\partial\bar{z}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~e^{iE_{I}(x^{\prime})}=\left(\frac{\partial z^{\prime}/\partial z}{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}/\partial\bar{z}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(6.4)

which implies that conformal fields (of vanishing Weyl weights) and their
associated weighted scalars through the map (
4.16
) are equal and
transform in the same way (compare (
4.15
) and (
4.18
) by taking
(
6.4
) into account). In the following, we use the notation for
conformal fields
\phi_{h,\bar{h}}
.

We assume here that conformal fields on the punctured complex plane may be
expanded in series as

\phi_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{k,l}a_{k,l}\ {}_{h,\bar{h}}\tilde{Z}_{k,l},\quad{}_{h,\bar{h}}\tilde{Z}_{k,l}=z^{-h-k}\bar{z}^{-\bar{h}-l},

(6.5)

where the coefficients
a_{k,l}\in\mathbb{C}
and satisfy suitable conditions
that we will not discuss in detail here (see e.g.
[
36
]
for more
details). We also assume that
h,\bar{h}
are either integer or half-integer. In
the former case
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, whereas in the latter case
k,l\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
. Other choices are also possible. The reason we are
choosing Neveu-Schwarz conditions here is that, up to factors of
(1+z\bar{z})
,
the functions that appear here then include those that have appeared naturally
in the case of the sphere.

Residues with respect to
z
and
\bar{z}
are defined as

\text{Res}_{z}[\phi_{h,\bar{h}}](\bar{z})=\sum_{l}a_{1-h,l}\bar{z}^{-\bar{h}-l},\quad\text{Res}_{\bar{z}}[\phi_{h,\bar{h}}](z)=\sum_{k}a_{k,1-\bar{h}}z^{-h-k}.

(6.6)

This allows one to define pairing

\langle\psi_{-\bar{h}+1,-h+1},\phi_{h,\bar{h}}\tilde{\rangle}=\text{Res}_{z}\text{Res}_{\bar{z}}[\overline{\psi_{-\bar{h}+1,-h+1}}\phi_{h,\bar{h}}].

(6.7)

This pairing is non-degenerate, and since
\text{Res}_{z}[\partial\phi]=0=\text{Res}_{\bar{z}}[\bar{\partial}\phi]
, it annihilates total
derivatives
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
, as it should.
The pairing can then be defined as

\langle([\tilde{\mathcal{J}}],[\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{J}}}],\tilde{\mathcal{P}}),(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}},\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}},\tilde{\mathcal{T}})\tilde{\rangle}=\langle\tilde{\mathcal{J}},\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\tilde{\rangle}+\langle\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{J}}},\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}}\tilde{\rangle}+\langle\tilde{\mathcal{P}},\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\rangle}.

(6.8)

6.2
Adjoint and coadjoint representations of the group

The formulas for the adjoint and coadjoint representations of the group are the
same than those for the conformal fields on the sphere, except for the general
Jacobians
\partial z/\partial z^{\prime},\partial\bar{z}/\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}(z^{\prime})=\big(\frac{\partial z}{\partial z^{\prime}}\big)^{-1}\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}(z),\\
&\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}}^{\prime}(\bar{z}^{\prime})=\big(\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}}\big)^{-1}\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}}(z),\\
&\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\big(\frac{\partial z}{\partial z^{\prime}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\tilde{\beta}-\big(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}\partial\tilde{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\alpha}\partial\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}+{\rm c.c.}\big)\Big)(x),\end{split}

(6.9)

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\big(\frac{\partial z}{\partial z^{\prime}}\big)^{1}\big(\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\Big(\tilde{\mathcal{J}}+(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\bar{\partial}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}+\frac{3}{2}\bar{\partial}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\mathcal{P}})\Big)(x)\\
&\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{J}}}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\big(\frac{\partial z}{\partial z^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\big(\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}}\big)^{1}\Big(\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{J}}}+(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\partial\tilde{\mathcal{P}}+\frac{3}{2}\partial\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\tilde{\mathcal{P}})\Big)(x)\\
&\tilde{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})=\big(\frac{\partial z}{\partial z^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}\big(\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial\bar{z}^{\prime}}\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(x).\end{split}

(6.10)

6.3

(6.14)

is
real. For the coadjoint representation, one finds from (

(6.15)

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{j}_{m}\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{m}_{*},\quad\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{J}}}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{\bar{j}}_{m}\tilde{\bar{\mathcal{Y}}}_{*}^{m},\quad\tilde{\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{k,l\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{p}_{k,l}\mathscr{\tilde{T}}^{k,l}_{*},

(6.16)

where

Let

\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{1+\xi\bar{\xi}}}\begin{pmatrix}-1&\xi\\
\bar{\xi}&1\end{pmatrix}.

(A.1)

Let also
s
be integer or half-integer,
j\pm s\in\mathbb{N}
,

j\pm m\in\mathbb{N}
,
|m|\leq j
,
|s|\leq j
, and consider

{}_{s}Z_{j,m}=\sum_{r}\frac{(j+m)!(j-m)!(j+s)!(j-s)!\alpha^{r}\beta^{j-m-r}\gamma^{j+s-r}\delta^{r+m-s}}{(2j)!r!(j-m-r)!(j+s-r)!(r+m-s)!},

(A.2)

where the summation extends over integer values of
r
in the range
\max(0,s-m)\leq r\leq\min(j-m,j+s)
.

The spin-weighted spherical harmonics
{}_{s}Y_{j,m}
are then defined by

{}_{s}Y_{j,m}=(-1)^{j+m}~_{s}Z_{j,m}\sqrt{\frac{(2j+1)!(2j)!}{4\pi(j+s)!(j-s)!(j+m)!(j-m)!}}.

(A.3)

For
s=0
, one recovers the usual spherical harmonics functions, i.e.,
{}_{0}Y_{j,m}
. The following properties hold:

•

For each
s
, the
{}_{s}Z_{j,m}
form an orthogonal basis for the
spin-weighted scalars
\eta^{s}
on the sphere with

\langle{}_{s}Z_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}},{}_{s}Z_{j,m}\rangle=\frac{(j+s)!(j-s)!(j+m)!(j-m)!}{(2j+1)!(2j)!}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}},

(A.4)

for the pairing (
5.6
). The
{}_{s}Y_{j,m}
form an orthonormal
basis with

4\pi\langle{}_{s}Y_{j^{\prime},m^{\prime}},{}_{s}Y_{j,m}\rangle=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}.

(A.5)

•

The behavior under complex conjugation is

\overline{{}_{s}Z_{j,m}}=(-1)^{m+s}~_{-s}Z_{j,-m},\quad\overline{{}_{s}Y_{j,m}}=(-1)^{3m+s}~_{-s}Y_{j,-m}.

(A.6)

•

The action of the operators
\eth
and
\bar{\eth}
defined in (
5.5
) is explicitly given by

\eth\ {}_{s}Z_{j,m}=-\left(\frac{j-s}{R\sqrt{2}}\right){}_{s+1}Z_{j,m},\quad\bar{\eth}\ {}_{s}Z_{j,m}=\left(\frac{j+s}{R\sqrt{2}}\right){}_{s-1}Z_{j,m}.

(A.7)

\begin{split}\eth\ _{s}Y_{j,m}&=-\sqrt{\frac{(j+s+1)(j-s)}{2R^{2}}}{}_{s+1}Y_{j,m},\\
\bar{\eth}\ {}_{s}Y_{j,m}&=\sqrt{\frac{(j-s+1)(j+s)}{2R^{2}}}{}_{s-1}Y_{j,m}.\end{split}

(A.8)

•

The
{}_{s}Z_{j,m}
and
{}_{s}Y_{j,m}
are eigenfunctions of the
operator
\bar{\eth}\eth
:

\bar{\eth}\eth_{s}Z_{j,m}=-(j+s+1)(j-s)\frac{1}{2}~_{s}Z_{j,m},\quad\bar{\eth}\eth_{s}Y_{j,m}=-(j+s+1)(j-s)\frac{1}{2}~_{s}Y_{j,m}.

(A.9)

•

Products of spin-weighted spherical harmonics can be decomposed
as

\begin{split}{}_{s_{1}}Z_{j_{1},m_{1}}~{}_{s_{2}}Z_{j_{2},m_{2}}&=\sqrt{\frac{(j_{1}+s_{1})!(j_{1}-s_{1})!(j_{1}+m_{1})!(j_{1}-m_{1})!}{(2j_{1})!(2j_{1})!}}\\
&\times\sqrt{\frac{(j_{2}+s_{2})!(j_{2}-s_{2})!(j_{2}+m_{2})!(j_{2}-m_{2})!}{(2j_{2})!(2j_{2})!}}\\
&\times\sum_{j}(-1)^{j_{1}+j_{2}+j}~_{(s_{1}+s_{2})}Z_{j,(m_{1}+m_{2})}\\
&\times\sqrt{\frac{(2j)!(2j)!}{(j+s_{1}+s_{2})!(j-s_{1}-s_{2})!(j+m_{1}+m_{2})!(j-m_{1}-m_{2})!}}\\
&\times\langle j_{1},s_{1};j_{2},s_{2}|j,(s_{1}+s_{2})\rangle~\langle j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}|j,(m_{1}+m_{2})\rangle,\end{split}

(A.10)

or

\begin{split}{}_{s_{1}}Y_{j_{1},m_{1}}~{}_{s_{2}}Y_{j_{2},m_{2}}=&\sum_{j}\sqrt{\frac{(2j_{1}+1)(2j_{2}+1)}{4\pi(2j+1)}}~_{(s_{1}+s_{2})}Y_{j,(m_{1}+m_{2})}\\
&\times\langle j_{1},s_{1};j_{2},s_{2}|j,(s_{1}+s_{2})\rangle~\langle j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}|j,(m_{1}+m_{2})\rangle,\end{split}

(A.11)

where the summation extends over integer values of
j
in the range

\max(|j_{1}-j_{2}|,|s_{1}+s_{2}|,|m_{1}+m_{2}|)\leq j\leq j_{1}+j_{2}
, and where

\langle j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}|j,(m_{1}+m_{2})\rangle
is a Clebsch-Gordan
coefficient of the rotation group (see e.g.
[
15
,
100
]
in
this context).

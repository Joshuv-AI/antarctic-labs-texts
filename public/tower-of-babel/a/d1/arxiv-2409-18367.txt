A Framework for Gluing Harmonic Maps
Shaozong Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the gluing construction of the extended harmonic maps between Riemannian manifolds. Harmonic maps are critical points of the energy functional. We construct the gluing map of the extended harmonic maps from Riemann surfaces to some Riemannian manifold N N under certain conditions.

1.6.
Outline

In Section
2
, we introduce the setup of the problem. We define the harmonic map operator and compute the linearization. Then we use the same idea as in Donaldson and Kronheimer
[
9
, Proposition 7.2.28]
to construct a surjective operator. In Section
3
, we write out the details of the construction of the gluing map. We introduce the Implicit Function Theorem used in the proof, and the pregluing construction. Then we construct the approximate inverse and from that construct the real right inverse. Finally, we check the conditions needed in the Implicit Function Theorem and show the existence of the gluing map. The appendix contains technical details such as norm and derivative estimates, and related facts in elliptic PDE theory and functional analysis.

2.2.
The Harmonic Map Operator

Let us follow McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Chapter 10]
and consider gluing harmonic maps on
S^{2}
(the Riemann sphere) or any Riemannian manifolds of dimension two into
N
. For simplicity, we consider the case when
\Sigma_{1}=S^{2},\Sigma_{2}=S^{2}
. However, we will see that the gluing can be used for general Riemann manifolds of dimension two.

First, let us consider the general definition of harmonic maps. Suppose
M
,
N
are Riemannian manifolds and
f
is a smooth map from the domain manifold
M
to the target manifold
N
. We say
f
is a harmonic map if it satisfies the harmonic map equation. There are multiple ways of writing the harmonic map equation. One would be

(10)

P(f):=\triangle_{g}f+A(f)(df,df),

where we consider an isometric embedding
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
A
is the second fundamental form of the embedding.

However, the above will depend on the ambient Euclidean space. In particular, since we are using the implicit function theorem later, the above will make it difficult to utilize the surjectivity onto the tangent space. An alternate form would be

\mathrm{tr}_{g}(\nabla df)=0.

In coordinates, this is

g^{\alpha\beta}\left(f^{k}_{\alpha\beta}-(\Gamma^{M})^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}f^{k}_{\gamma}+(\Gamma^{N})^{k}_{ij}(f)f^{i}_{\alpha}f^{j}_{\beta}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}=0,

where
x^{\alpha},x^{\beta}
are coordinates on
S^{2}
,
y^{i},y^{j}
are coordinates on
N
, and we are denoting
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\alpha}}
by
f_{\alpha}
. We know that the above is a well defined section of
f^{-1}TN
, independent of choice of coordinates on
M
and
N
. We can also verify this by directly computing the transformation law in different coordinates.

We define the operator
P
as

P(f):=g^{\alpha\beta}\left(f^{k}_{\alpha\beta}-(\Gamma^{M})^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}f^{k}_{\gamma}+(\Gamma^{N})^{k}_{ij}(f)f^{i}_{\alpha}f^{j}_{\beta}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{k}}.

It can be verified directly by coordinate change that the above is a
(0,1)
tensor.

Next, we consider the linearization of
P
. The idea is that, for a perturbation of
f
and
x\in M
, the operator will give a vector that belongs to a different fiber in the tangent bundle of
N
. Thus we have to parallel translate the vectors to the same fiber before comparing them. Similar computation of linearization has been done in the proof of Proposition 3.1 on page 42 of McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Section 3.1]
.

Here are the details: Given
\xi\in W^{2,p}(\Sigma,f^{-1}TN)
, from page 85 of Adams and Fournier
[
3
, Theorem 4.12]
we know that for
1<p<2
,
W^{2,p}
is embedded into
C^{0,\gamma}
where
\gamma=2-\frac{2}{p}
. Let

(11)

\Phi_{f}(\xi):f^{-1}TN\rightarrow(\exp_{f}(\xi))^{-1}TN

be the parallel transport. Set

(12)

\mathcal{F}_{f}(\xi):=\Phi_{f}(\xi)^{-1}P(\exp_{f}(\xi))

and compute

(13)

D_{f}:=d\mathcal{F}_{f}(0).

The following computation of linearization is due to T. Parker. Let
F:M\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
be a two-parameter variation of a map
f:M\rightarrow N
(not assumed to be harmonic). Write
F(x,s,t)
as
f_{s,t}(x)
, so
f=f_{0,0}
and set

X=f_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\quad Y=f_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\quad Z_{\alpha}=f_{*}e_{\alpha}

where
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
is a local orthonormal frame of
TM
. Then

(14)

D_{f}(Y)=\nabla^{*}\nabla Y+\sum_{\alpha}R^{N}(Z_{\alpha},Y)Z_{\alpha}.

The proof is by T. Parker and will be written out in Appendix
D
.

It is well known that a second order elliptic operator from
W^{2,p}
to
L^{p}
spaces of sections of vector bundles on compact manifolds, such as
D_{f}
, is Fredholm (refer to Hörmander
[
17
]
). We write it as the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
C^{2}
map
f:\Sigma\rightarrow N
, where
\Sigma
is a Riemann surface and
N
a closed Riemannian manifold, the operator
D_{f}
defined in (
13
) is a Fredholm operator.

2.3.
Structure of the Moduli Space

This part follows from Donaldson and Kronheimer
[
9
, Section 4.2.4]
or McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Section A.4]
. From Lemma

we know that

D_{f^{0}}:W^{2,p}\left(S^{2},\left(f^{0}\right)^{-1}TN\right)\rightarrow L^{p}\left(S^{2},\left(f^{0}\right)^{-1}TN\right)

is Fredholm. It follows that the kernel and image of
D_{f^{0}}
are closed and admit topological complements. For simplicity, we write

\displaystyle U:=W^{2,p}\left(S^{2},\left(f^{0}\right)^{-1}TN\right),

\displaystyle V:=L^{p}\left(S^{2},\left(f^{0}\right)^{-1}TN\right).

So we can write
U=U_{0}\oplus F
,
V=V_{0}\oplus G
, where
F
and
G
are finite-dimensional linear spaces, and
D_{f^{0}}
is a linear isomorphism from
U_{0}
to
V_{0}
.

Consider a connected open neighborhood of
0
in
U
. Since the linearization of
\mathcal{F}_{f^{0}}
is Fredholm, we know
\mathcal{F}_{f^{0}}
is Fredholm. If
D_{f^{0}}
is surjective, then by the implicit function theorem (refer to page 541 of McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Theorem A.3.3]
), we know there is a diffeomorphism
\psi
from one neighborhood of
0
in
U
to another, such that
\mathcal{F}\circ\psi=D_{f^{0}}
.

Now consider the general case when
D_{f^{0}}
is not necessarily surjective. Consider projection of
V
onto
V_{0}
, we will have the derivative be surjective. The following is similar to Theorem A.4.3 on page 546 of McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Theorem A.4.3]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

The Fredholm map
D_{f^{0}}
from a neighborhood of
0
is locally right equivalent to a map of the form

\tilde{\mathcal{F}}:U_{0}\times F\rightarrow V_{0}\times G,\ \tilde{F}(\xi,\eta)=(D_{f^{0}}(\xi),\alpha(\xi,\eta))

where
D_{f^{0}}
is a linear isomorphism from
U_{0}
to
V_{0}
,
F
and
G
are finite-dimensional, and the derivative of
\alpha
vanishes at

.

Note that elements in
Z(\mathcal{F})
are smooth by elliptic regularity (use partition of unity to reduce the case on manifolds to that on Euclidean space).

We know there exists a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism
g
from some open set
W
containing
0\in W^{2,p}(S^{2},(f^{0})^{-1}TN)
, such that

g(0)=0,\quad dg(0)=id

and

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)\cap g(W)=g(W\cap\ker D)

From the above we can get a coordinate chart for

by the isomorphism between

and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where

is the dimension of the kernel. Thus we can view the vectors in the kernel as tangent vectors of the moduli space. Furthermore, a smooth path

in the open neighborhood can be represented as

.

2.4.

In McDuff and Salamon
[

Given

Recall the representatives of the cokernel defined in Definition

3.
Existence of the Extended Gluing Map

3.1.
The implicit function theorem

We first state a version of implicit function theorem for general Banach spaces:

Proposition 3.1

(Implicit Function Theorem for General Banach Spaces)
.

(See McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Proposition A.3.4, p. 542]
)

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces,
U\subset X
be an open set, and
f:U\rightarrow Y
be a continuously differentiable map. Let
x_{0}\in U
be such that
D:=df(x_{0}):X\rightarrow Y
is surjective and has a (bounded linear) right inverse
Q:Y\rightarrow X
. Choose positive constants
\delta
and
c
such that
\|Q\|\leq c
,
B_{\delta}(x_{0};X)\subset U
, and

(15)

\|x-x_{0}\|<\delta\ \Rightarrow\|df(x)-D\|\leq\frac{1}{2c}.

Suppose that
x_{1}\in X
satisfies

(16)

\|f(x_{1})\|<\frac{\delta}{4c},\ \|x_{1}-x_{0}\|<\frac{\delta}{8}.

Then there exists a unique
x\in X
such that

(17)

f(x)=0,\ x-x_{1}\in\text{im}Q,\ \|x-x_{0}\|<\delta.

Moreover,
\|x-x_{1}\|\leq 2c\|f(x_{1})\|
.

\displaystyle-\left(R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2}\right)^{2}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}(\beta_{\delta,R}(\xi^{0}-\xi_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\beta(z)}{\partial s^{2}}(\xi^{0}-\xi_{0})+2\frac{\partial\beta(z)}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}+\beta(z)\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{0}}{\partial s^{2}}\right)

where the equality holds because
f^{R}
is constant for
\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R
.

From Equation (
28
) we know

\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\beta(z)}{\partial s^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{2}\log(1/\delta)}.

Furthermore, note that we have control over the
C^{0,\gamma}
norm of
\xi^{0}
by the
W^{2,p}
norm. We consider the Sobolev embedding of
C^{0,\mu}
into
W^{2,p}
where
1<p<2
and
\mu=2-\frac{2}{p}
. For every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and every
\xi\in W^{2,p}(B)
, we have

(32)

\displaystyle z_{0},z_{1}\in B\ \Rightarrow\ |\xi(z_{1})-\xi(z_{0})|\leq C(p)\|\xi\|_{W^{2,p}(B)}|z_{1}-z_{0}|^{2-\frac{2}{p}}.

Note that in the Sobolev embedding, for balls of different radius, the constant remains the same. Thus, under the weighted norm (
7
),

\displaystyle\left\|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s^{2}}(\xi^{0}-\xi_{0})\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\frac{C}{r^{2}\log(1/\delta)}(\xi^{0}-\xi_{0})\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{R}}(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{-2}\left|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\frac{C}{r^{2}\log(1/\delta)}(\xi^{0}-\xi_{0})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{R}}\frac{(1+\delta_{0}^{4})^{2p-2}C}{r^{2p}(\log(1/\delta))^{p}}|\xi^{0}-\xi_{0}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\delta_{0}^{4})^{2-2/p}C^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\log(1/\delta)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\|\xi^{0}\|_{W^{2,p}(B_{{1}/{R}})}.

In the above, the first inequality comes from (
34
) and (
30
), and the second inequality comes from (
33
), and the last inequlity uses (
32
).

\displaystyle\left\|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R}(R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2})^{-2}\left|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R}(R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2})^{2p-2}\left|\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta_{0}^{4})^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\left\|\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right\|_{L^{p}(\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R)}

by the fact that

(33)

R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2}\leq\left({\delta\delta_{0}}\right)^{2}+1\leq\delta_{0}^{4}+1

for
\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R
.

We can consider using the Sobolev embedding on the manifold, which, in our case, is the two-dimensional sphere. First, we use the Hölder’s inequality to get:

(34)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{R})}\left\|\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right\|_{L^{q}(\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{R})}

where
q=\frac{2p}{2-p}>2
.

For the
L^{q}
norm term in Equation (
34
), we want to use the Sobolev embedding on the sphere. From page 35 of Aubin
[
4
, Section 2.3]
we know that for compact manifold the Sobolev embedding holds. Note that here we can treat
\xi^{0}
in the same way as real-valued functions on the sphere.
\left|\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right|\leq C\left|\nabla\xi^{0}\right|
, where
C
is univeral. Thus we have

(35)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right\|_{L^{q}(\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{R})}\leq C\|\xi^{0}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta^{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}

if we apply the forthcoming Lemma
D.2
. Here we are considering
S^{2}
with the round metric of radius one.

We can compute using (
27
) that

\left\|\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\right\|_{L^{2}(\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{R})}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)}}.

Furthermore, note that,

\displaystyle\|\xi^{0}\|_{W^{2,p}(B_{1/R})}\leq C\|\xi^{0}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta^{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta\|_{0,p,R},

where the second inequality comes from (
21
) and (
23
), and the third inequality comes from (
22
).

Now we are left with

\displaystyle\left\|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\beta_{\delta,R}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{0}}{\partial s^{2}}\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R}(R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2})^{-2}\left|(R^{-2}+R^{2}r^{2})^{2}\beta_{\delta,R}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{0}}{\partial s^{2}}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R}(R^{-2}+R^{2}|z|^{2})^{2p-2}\left|\frac{\partial\beta_{\delta,R}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial\xi^{0}}{\partial s}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta_{0}^{4})^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{0}}{\partial s^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R)}.

Similar as in Equation (
35
), we have

\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{0}}{\partial s^{2}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/R)}\leq C\|\xi^{0}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta^{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}.

Thus by choosing small enough
\delta_{0}
only depending on
c,p,N,\mathcal{U}
, we have the desired result.
∎

The following Lemma
3.2
corresponds to an estimate in the proof of Proposition 10.5.1 on page 382 of McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Proposition 10.5.1]
. This estimate covers the part of the neck that was not in Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
(\tilde{f}^{0},\tilde{f}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{M}(c,p)
, we can choose a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{M}(c,p)
, and
\delta_{0}
small enough only depending on
c,p,N,\mathcal{U}
, such that for any
(f^{0},f^{\infty})\in\mathcal{U}
and any
(\delta,R)\in\mathcal{A}(\delta_{0})
, the approximate right inverse
T_{f^{R}}
defined in Definition
3.1
satisfies:

\|(D_{f^{R}}\oplus\sigma)T_{f^{R}}\eta-\eta\|_{0,p,R,\Omega_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\eta\|_{0,p,R,\Omega_{2}}

for every
\eta\in L^{p}(S^{2},(f^{R})^{-1}TN)
, where
\Omega_{2}=\{1/R\leq|z|\leq 1/\delta R\}
.

Proof.

We still use the coordinates as in Equation (
26
). Here we have

\xi^{R}(z)=\xi^{0}(z)+\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0}).

We know that
D_{f^{R}}\xi^{0}=D_{f^{0,r}}\xi^{0}=\eta^{0}=\eta
. We also know that
D_{f^{R}}\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)=D_{f^{\infty,r}(R^{2}\cdot)}\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)
. Again, we use
(s,t)
coordinates for
1/R\leq|z|\leq 1/\delta R
(
z=s+it
). We use
(\tilde{s}=R^{2}s,\ \tilde{t}=R^{2}t)
coordinates for
R\leq R^{2}|z|\leq R/\delta
. We know that in the annulus
1/R\leq|z|\leq 1/\delta R
, we have

f^{R}(z)=f^{\infty,r}(R^{2}z)=f^{0}(0)=f^{\infty}(\infty)=y.

Similar as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, the error is

-(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\left(\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}+\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}\right)\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0})\right).

We only need to estimate the
0,p,R
norm of

\displaystyle(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0})+2\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z))+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z))\right).

We compute that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r\log(1/\delta)},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2}\log(1/\delta)},

where
C
is a universal constant. Let’s first estimate

\displaystyle\left\|(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{1/R}^{1/(\delta R)}rdr\ (1+r^{2})^{2p-2}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{\delta R}\right)^{2}\right)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{1/R}^{1/(\delta R)}rdr\ \left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right|^{p}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{\delta R}\right)^{2}\right)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{A}\ \left(\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}\left(\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p}}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}

where
A
denotes the annulus
1/R\leq|z|\leq 1/(\delta R)
. The last inequality comes from Hölder’s inequality.

For the integral involving the cutoff function, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}\ \left(\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{1/R}^{1/(\delta R)}rdr\ \frac{C}{r^{2}(\log(1/\delta))^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\delta)}}

where
C
is a universal constant.

Here are some thoughts about measuring the norm of the derivative of
\xi^{\infty}
. Let us note that with the coordinate chart given by stereographic projection, there is a weight for the derivative of
\xi^{\infty}
, since in the coordinate chart, at the points away from the origin, say
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
, the vector is dilated with a ratio of
(1+|w|^{2})^{-1}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z))=R^{2}\frac{\partial\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s}(R^{2}z)\leq(1+|R^{2}z|^{2})\frac{\partial\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s}(R^{2}z)

Recall that using the coordinates from the stereographic projection of the Riemann sphere
S^{2}
, the
W^{2,p}
norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2},f^{-1}TN)}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{S^{2}}|\xi|^{p}+|\nabla\xi|^{p}+|\nabla^{2}\xi|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+r^{2})^{-2}|\xi|^{p}+(1+r^{2})^{p-2}(|\nabla_{s}\xi|^{p}+|\nabla_{t}\xi|^{p})+\right.

\displaystyle\left.(1+r^{2})^{2p-2}\left(\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi}{\partial s^{2}}\right|^{p}+\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi}{\partial s\partial t}\right|^{p}+\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi}{\partial t^{2}}\right|^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Note that here we cannot directly consider the range of
\xi
to be
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By the definition of the
W^{2,p}
norm for maps mapping to vector bundles, we need to consider the local trivialization of that vector bundle. However, for
\frac{\delta}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{\delta R}
, since
f(z)\equiv y
, these two definitions are equivalent.

Denote
\frac{2p}{2-p}
by
p^{*}
. Since
1<p<2
, we know
p^{*}>2
. Thus it is easy to see that, in the annulus
\frac{1}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{\delta R}
,

\displaystyle R^{2p^{*}}\leq R^{4}(1+R^{2})^{p^{*}-2}\leq R^{4}(1+R^{4}|z|^{2})^{p^{*}-2}

We know that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}\left(\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p}}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}\left|R^{2}\frac{\partial\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s}(R^{2}z)\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}R^{4}(1+R^{4}|z|^{2})^{p^{*}-2}\left|\frac{\partial\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s}(R^{2}z)\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}R^{4}(1+|R^{2}z|^{2})^{-2}\left|(1+|R^{2}z|^{2})\frac{\partial\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s}(R^{2}z)\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{R\leq|w|\leq R/\delta}(1+|w|^{2})^{-2}\left|(1+|w|^{2})\frac{\partial\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s}(w)\right|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\xi^{\infty}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(S^{2})}

where
C
is universal.

Then we use the Sobolev embedding theorem for closed Riemann manifolds, as well as Lemma
D.2
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla\xi^{\infty}\|_{L^{p^{*}}}\leq C\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta^{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(S^{2})}

From the definition of
\eta^{\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta^{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(S^{2})}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left|\eta\left(\frac{z}{R^{2}}\right)\right|^{p}(1+|z|^{2})^{-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(R^{4}\int_{B_{{1}/{R}}(0)}\left|\eta(w)\right|^{p}(1+R^{4}|w|^{2})^{-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{{1}/{R}}(0)}\left|\eta(w)\right|^{p}(R^{-2}+R^{2}|w|^{2})^{-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}

Thus we have

\displaystyle\left\|(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{\delta R}\right)^{2}\right)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\log(1/\delta)}}\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left\|(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(1+|z|^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\left(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)\right)\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{1/R\leq|z|\leq 1/(\delta R)}(1+|z|^{2})^{2p-2}R^{4p}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s^{2}}(R^{2}z)\right|^{p}dsdt\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{1/R\leq|z|\leq 1/(\delta R)}(1+|z|^{2})^{2p-2}R^{4p}(1+|R^{2}z|^{2})^{-2p}\left|(1+|R^{2}z|^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s^{2}}(R^{2}z)\right|^{p}dsdt\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\delta^{2}R^{2}}\right)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{R\leq|w|\leq R/\delta}R^{4p-4}(1+|w|^{2})^{2-2p}\left|(1+|w|^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi^{\infty}}{\partial s^{2}}(w)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\delta_{0}^{2})^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left\|\left((1+r^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right)(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0})\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{C(1+r^{2})^{2}}{r^{2}\log(1/\delta)}(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0})\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}(1+r^{2})^{-2}\left|\frac{C(1+r^{2})^{2}}{r^{2}\log(1/\delta)}(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\log(1/\delta)}\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{\delta R}\right)^{2}\right)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\left(\int_{A}\frac{1}{r^{2p}}|\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

We can consider changing coordinates
w={1}/{R^{2}z}
. Since
\frac{1}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{1}{\delta R}
, we have
\frac{\delta}{R}\leq\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\leq\frac{1}{R}
. Also, in the new coordinate system,
\xi_{0}=\xi^{\infty}(0)
. We have

(36)

\displaystyle|\xi^{\infty}(w)-\xi^{\infty}(0)|\leq C\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}|w|^{2-\frac{2}{p}}

where
C
is a universal constant.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A}\frac{1}{r^{2p}}|\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{R}}^{\frac{1}{\delta R}}\frac{1}{r^{2p}}\left|\frac{1}{R^{2}r}\right|^{2p-2}rdr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{R}}^{\frac{1}{\delta R}}(rR)^{4-4p}\frac{1}{r}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{R}}^{\frac{1}{\delta R}}\frac{1}{r}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\log(1/\delta)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Similar as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we know

\displaystyle\|\xi^{\infty}\|_{W^{2,p}(S^{2})}\leq C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}

Thus we get

\displaystyle\left\|\left((1+r^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s^{2}}\left(\beta_{\delta,R}\left(\frac{1}{R^{2}z}\right)\right)\right)(\xi^{\infty}(R^{2}z)-\xi_{0})\right\|_{0,p,R}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C(c,p,N,\mathcal{U})}{\log(1/\delta)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{\delta R}\right)^{2}\right)^{2-\frac{2}{p}}\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}

Hence we can choose
\delta_{0}
small enough only depending on
c,p,N,\mathcal{U}
to get the desired inequality.
∎

[

, Proposition 10.5.1]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

(Compare McDuff and Salmon
[
25
, Proposition 10.5.1, p. 382]
for the analogous statement for J-holomorphic curves)

For any
(\tilde{f}^{0},\tilde{f}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{M}(c,p)
, we can choose a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{M}(c,p)
, and we can choose
\delta_{0}>0
,
c_{0}>0
only depending on
c,p,N,\mathcal{U}
, such that for any
(f^{0},f^{\infty})\in\mathcal{U}
and any
(\delta,R)\in\mathcal{A}(\delta_{0})
, the approximate right inverse
T_{f^{R}}
defined in Definition
3.1
satisfies:

(37)

\|(D_{f^{R}}\oplus\sigma)T_{f^{R}}\eta-\eta\|_{0,p,R}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|_{0,p,R},\quad\|T_{f^{R}}\eta\|_{2,p,R,V}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{2}\|\eta\|_{0,p,R}

for every
\eta\in L^{p}(S^{2},(f^{R})^{-1}TN)
.

Recall that the
2,p,R,V
norm is defined in Definition
2.3
.

Proof.

We have
T_{f^{R}}\eta=(\xi^{R},\tilde{v})
for each
\eta\in L^{p}_{f^{R}}
, and must prove that

(38)

\|D_{f^{R}}\xi^{R}+v-\eta\|_{0,p,R}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|_{0,p,R},

where
v=\sigma(\tilde{v})
.

Since
D_{f^{0,r}}\xi^{0}+v=\eta^{0}
and
D_{f^{\infty,r}}\xi^{\infty}+v=\eta^{\infty}
, the term on the left hand side vanishes for
|z|\geq 1/\delta R
and for
|z|\leq\delta/R
. For
\delta/R\leq|z|\leq 1/\delta R
, we can apply Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
. The first equality is proved.

Before we prove the second inequality, we take a closer look of how the
W^{2,p}
norm is defined on the weighted sphere.

We still consider the stereographic projection of the weighted
S^{2}
(which is the connected sum of the original two
S^{2}
s). We consider
\xi\in W^{2,p}(S^{2},(f^{R})^{-1}TN)
.

For
|z|\geq\frac{1}{R}
, we have

f^{R}(z)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f^{0}(z),\ &&|z|\geq\frac{2}{\delta R}\\
&\exp_{y}(\rho(\delta Rz)\zeta^{0}(z)),\ &&\frac{1}{R}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2}{\delta R}\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\mathcal{U}
is precompact, we know that the norm of
d\exp_{y}
and
d\exp_{y}^{-1}
are uniformly bounded. Thus, there exists

First, for

Lemma A.1

Consider

be the operator defined in (
). Choose any

Proof.

We claim that the conclusion of the lemma holds for any operator such that the target space is

, and the cokernel is finite dimensional, and the image is closed. We use mathematical induction to prove this.

Suppose that the cokernel has dimension 0, there is nothing to prove.

Suppose that the cokernel has dimension 1, we prove it by contradiction. Assume that for any

as a representative of the quotient space that is only supported on

Now suppose that the conclusion is true for cokernel dimension less than or equal to

. We prove the conclusion for cokernel dimension

.

may not be supported away from a neighborhood of

be the identity map of the space spanned by

, then we know that the image of

is closed (note that it is easy to show that a closed subspace direct sum with a one-dimensional space is closed). Now we can choose

\sigma_{1}
be the identity map of the space spanned by

. Now consider

and we can find other representatives that are supported away from a neighborhood of

∎

Now we prove that

\gamma(0)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle(V(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))

\displaystyle\mapsto V(1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(0)

Lemma D.2

For any
(\tilde{f}^{0},\tilde{f}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{M}(c,p)
, where

\mathcal{U}

\mathcal{M}(c,p)
and positive constants

, the following holds for

, we have

\|\xi\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq c_{0}(\|D_{0,\infty,r}\xi\|_{L^{p}}+\|\xi\|_{L^{p}}).

and

(ii) For every

, we have

D_{0,\infty,r}\xi+\sigma(\tilde{v})=0\ \Rightarrow\ \|\xi\|_{W^{2,p}}+\|\tilde{v}\|_{\tilde{V}}\leq c_{0}\|(\xi,\tilde{v})\|_{L^{2}}.

(iii) For every
\eta=(\eta^{0},\eta^{\infty})\in L^{p}_{f^{0,r}}\times L^{p}_{f^{\infty,r}}
we have

\|Q_{0,\infty,r}\eta\|_{W^{2,p}\times\tilde{V}}\leq c_{0}\|\eta\|_{L^{p}},

where
Q_{0,\infty,r}
is defined as in Equation (
20
).

Proof.

The proof follows the same ideas as in McDuff and Salamon
[
25
, Lemma 10.6.1, p. 392]
. Note that the part for
f^{0}
and the part for
f^{\infty}
are symmetric, so we only need to consider one, and the other can be proved in the same way. Let us consider
f^{0}
. For any
\xi^{0}\in W^{2,p}_{f^{0,r}}
, note that
f^{0,r}
only differs from
f^{0}
in
|z|\leq\frac{2}{\delta R}
. In
|z|\leq\frac{2}{\delta R}
, we know that
f^{0}
and
f^{0,r}
are both close to
y
, so we can do a parallel transport of
\xi^{0}
to get
\tilde{\xi^{0}}\in W^{2,p}_{f^{0}}
. From Lemma
D.1
we know that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\xi^{0}}\|_{W^{2,p}}\leq c_{0}(\|D_{0}\tilde{\xi^{0}}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\tilde{\xi^{0}}\|_{L^{p}})

From the previous section we know that the first estimate is proved.

To prove the remaining estimates we consider the following abstract functional analytic setting. Suppose we have a surjective Fredholm operator

D:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}

of index
d
between two Banach spaces (think of the case
\mathcal{W}=W^{2,p}_{f^{0,\infty}}\times\tilde{V}
, and
\mathcal{L}=L^{p}_{f^{0}}\times L^{p}_{f^{\infty}}
, and
D=D_{0,\infty}\oplus\sigma
). We assume
\mathcal{W}
is equipped with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and denote the corresponding norm by

\|\xi\|_{L^{2}}:=\sqrt{\langle\xi,\xi\rangle}.

(think of the
L^{2}
inner product on
W^{2,p}_{f^{0,\infty}}
and let
\tilde{V}
be orthogonal to
W^{2,p}_{f^{0,\infty}}
) We assume further that there are positive constants
c
and
c_{D}
such that

(44)

\|\xi\|_{L^{2}}\leq c\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}

for every
\xi\in\mathcal{W}
(this is true because the
L^{2}
norm of the
W^{2,p}_{f^{0,\infty}}
component is controlled by the
W^{2,p}_{f^{0,\infty}}
norm) and

(45)

D\xi=0\ \Rightarrow\ \|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq c_{D}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}}.

This holds in our setting because the kernel of
D
is finite dimensional. Denote by
Q:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
the right inverse of
D
whose image is the orthogonal complement of the kernel with respect to the above inner product. Note that here
W^{2,p}_{f^{0,\infty}}\times\tilde{V}
is just a subspace of the Hilbert space
L^{2}
. We can consider
\ker D
, which is finite dimensional, and its orthogonal space in
\mathcal{W}
, which we denote by
(\ker D)^{\perp}
. It is easy to show that
(\ker D)^{\perp}
is closed (in
\mathcal{W}
),
\ker D\cap(\ker D)^{\perp}=\{0\}
, and
\ker D+(\ker D)^{\perp}=\mathcal{W}
. Thus we have
\mathcal{W}=\ker D\oplus(\ker D)^{\perp}
. Then we can construct the
Q
the same way as for Hilbert spaces.

Now we prove the norm of
Q
is bounded: Since we already have
\mathcal{W}=\ker D\oplus(\ker D)^{\perp}
, we can consider
D
on
(\ker D)^{\perp}
and we know the inverse will be bounded by the open mapping theorem.

Now suppose that
D^{\prime}:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
is another bounded linear operator (
D_{0,\infty,r}\oplus\sigma
for example) such that

\epsilon:=\|D^{\prime}-D\|\|Q\|<1.

Since
DQ=id
we have
\|D^{\prime}Q-id\|<1
and so
D^{\prime}
is surjective with right inverse
Q(D^{\prime}Q)^{-1}
. However, we wish to understand the right inverse
Q^{\prime}:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
whose image is the orthogonal complement of the kernel of
D^{\prime}
.

As a first step we observe that, if
D^{\prime}\zeta=0
, then
\|QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\epsilon\|\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\|\zeta-QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\geq(1-\epsilon)\|\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}
. Hence

(46)

D^{\prime}\zeta=0\ \Rightarrow\ \|QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\|\zeta-QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Since
\zeta-QD\zeta\in\ker D
, we find
\|\zeta-QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq c_{D}\|\zeta-QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq c_{D}\|\zeta\|_{\mathcal{L}}
. The last inequality holds because
\zeta-QD\zeta
is the orthogonal projection of
\zeta
onto the kernel of
D
(note that the image of
Q
is
(\ker D)^{\perp}
.) But we saw above that
\|(1-\epsilon)\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\|\zeta-QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}
. Hence

D^{\prime}\zeta=0\ \Rightarrow\ \|\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\frac{c_{D}}{1-\epsilon}\|\zeta\|_{L^{2}}.

This proves (ii).

Now assume that
\xi\in\mathcal{W}
is orthogonal to the kernel of
D^{\prime}
. Let
e_{1},\cdots,e_{d}
be an orthonormal basis of
\ker D
and consider the basis
e_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,e_{d}^{\prime}
of
\ker D^{\prime}
defined by

e_{i}^{\prime}-QDe_{i}^{\prime}=e_{i},\ i=1,\cdots,d.

(The map
\ker D^{\prime}\rightarrow\ker D:\zeta\mapsto\zeta-QD\zeta
is an isomorphism between the two kernels. We can see that since in the above argument, we have, if
D^{\prime}\zeta=0
, then
\|QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\epsilon\|\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\|\zeta-QD\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\geq(1-\epsilon)\|\zeta\|_{\mathcal{W}}
. Note that the index of Fredholm operators stays the same if we have
\|D^{\prime}-D\|<\epsilon
for fixed
D
and small enough
\epsilon
. Since the index is the same for
D^{\prime}
and
D
, we know the map is surjective.)

Since
\xi-QD\xi\in\ker D
we have

\xi-QD\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle\xi,e_{i}\rangle e_{i}.

Moreover,
\langle\xi,e_{i}\rangle=\langle\xi,e_{i}-e_{i}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\xi,-QDe_{i}^{\prime}\rangle
and hence, by (
46
),

|\langle\xi,e_{i}\rangle|\leq c\|QDe_{i}^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\frac{c\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\|e_{i}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\frac{cc_{D}\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Combining this with the previous identity we find

\|\xi-QD\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq c_{D}\|\xi-QD\xi\|_{L^{2}}=c_{D}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\langle\xi,e_{i}\rangle^{2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}cc_{D}^{2}\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\leq\|QD\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\xi-QD\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q\|\|D^{\prime}\xi\|_{\mathcal{L}}+\|Q\|\|D^{\prime}-D\|\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\xi-QD\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\leq\|Q\|\|D^{\prime}\xi\|_{\mathcal{L}}+\epsilon\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{d}cc_{D}^{2}}{1-\epsilon}\right)\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}.

If
\epsilon\leq 1/2
and
\epsilon(1+2\sqrt{d}cc_{D}^{2})\leq 1/2
we deduce that
\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq 2\|Q\|\|D^{\prime}\xi\|_{\mathcal{L}}
for every
\xi\in\mathcal{W}
that is orthogonal to the kernel of
D^{\prime}
.

Now recall that
\|D^{\prime}-D\|=\epsilon/\|Q\|
and that
D^{\prime}
is surjective, so that
D^{\prime}\xi
runs over all elements in
\mathcal{L}
. It follows that there is a constant
\delta>0
such that

\|D^{\prime}-D\|<\delta\ \Rightarrow\ \|Q^{\prime}\|\leq 2\|Q\|.

How small
\delta
must be chosen depends only on the operator norm of
Q
and the constants
d,c,c_{D}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

(Compare McDuff and Salamon
[

denotes the operator norm.

Proof.

By choosing small enough
\mathcal{U}

c_{0}>0

Denote

\Phi_{f^{R}}(\xi+t\xi^{\prime})\mathcal{F}_{f^{R}}(\xi+t\xi^{\prime})=P(\exp_{f^{R}}(\xi+t\xi^{\prime}))

covariantly at

\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{0}

Now we are left to estimate

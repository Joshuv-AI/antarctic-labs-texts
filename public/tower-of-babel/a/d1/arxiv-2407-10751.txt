On Green’s function of the vorticity formulation for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations
Igor Kukavica, Fei Wang, Yichun Zhu

Abstract
. We give a novel vorticity formulation for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Via a resolvent argument, we obtain Green’s function and establish an upper bound, which is the 3D analog of [ 24 ] . Moreover, we prove similar results for the corresponding Stokes problem with more general mixed boundary conditions.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=0

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u=0

(1.3)

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}=u_{0}

on the half-space

(1.4)

\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)
, with the
no-slip boundary condition

(1.5)

\displaystyle u|_{\partial\mathbb{H}}=0.

Here,
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity. Formally setting
\nu=0
in (
1.1
)–(
1.3
) we arrive at the 3D incompressible Euler equations, with the so called
slip boundary condition
given by
u\cdot n|_{\partial\mathbb{H}}=0
. It is well-known that the vorticity plays an important role in studying the Navier-Stokes equations. For instance, in the domains without boundaries, the Navier-Stokes equations can be equivalently considered using the vorticity instead of the velocity. A benefit of such formulation is eliminating the pressure, which is replaced by the Biôt-Savart law. However, when considering the Navier-Stokes equations in the presence of boundaries, there is a difficulty with the boundary conditions for the vorticity, and thus the vorticity formulation is not as commonly used. This difficulty was overcome by Anderson, who introduced in
[
1
]
the boundary conditions for the vorticity in the 2D case. Using Anderson’s boundary conditions
[
1
]
, Maekawa in
[
19
]
reformulated the 2D Navier-Stokes equation using the vorticity by a semigroup approach. The reformulation allowed him to obtain a result stating that the inviscid limit holds in the half-space provided the initial vorticity is compactly supported.

The Anderson-Maekawa vorticity formulation
[
1
,
19
]
was further explored by Nguyen and Nguyen
[
24
]
, who computed Green’s function and obtained an upper bound for its derivatives. This paper is the starting point for our main results. Namely, the main goal of the present paper is to obtain a new formulation of the 3D Navier-Stokes vorticity setting (see (
2.19a
)–(
2.19c
)), derive the corresponding Green’s function (see (
4.1
)), and obtain upper bounds for the Green’s function (see Theorem
4.3
). In particular, we show that the Navier-Stokes equations with no-slip boundary conditions can be written as

(1.6)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\omega-\nu\Delta\omega=\omega\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle\nu(\partial_{z}+\Lambda_{\tau})\omega_{\tau}+\nu\nabla_{\tau}\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}\nabla_{\tau}\cdot\omega_{\tau}

(1.7)

\displaystyle\quad{}=\partial_{z}(-\Delta_{\text{D}})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{\tau}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{\tau})+\nabla_{\tau}(-\Delta_{\text{N}})^{-1}\left(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{3}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{z}\right)\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{H}$},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}=0\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{H}$},

where
\tau
indicates the tangential components;
see Section
2
for the definitions
of
(-\Delta_{\text{D}})^{-1}
and
(-\Delta_{\text{N}})^{-1}
.
For the corresponding Stokes problem, we get a decomposition of the Green function mode by mode in Fourier frequency

\displaystyle G_{\xi}(t,y;z)=H_{\xi}(t,y;z)+R_{\xi}(t,y;z),

where
H_{\xi}(t,y;z)
is the heat kernel with homogeneous boundary condition in positive half line and the remainder term
R_{\xi}(t,y;z)
satisfies a bound as the heat kernel with a fast decay tail (see (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) for more details). After finding Green’s function for the problem (
1.6
)–(
1.8
), we obtain the upper bounds (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) where
G
satisfies (
4.6
). In 2D, such upper bounds were obtained by Nguyen and Nguyen in
[
24
]
; the bounds proved to be extremely useful in the inviscid limit problem (
[
2
,
15
,
16
,
24
]
). The form of the upper bounds allows one to obtain the 3D analog of the result from
[
15
,
16
]
on the inviscid limit for data that are analytic in a neighborhood of the boundary and Sobolev away from the boundary.

We point out that in the paper
[
13
]
, Kosaka and Maekawa obtained a vorticity boundary conditions, which agree with (
1.7
) for the horizontal components of the vorticity; however, the condition
\partial_{3}\omega_{3}+\nabla^{\prime}\cdot\omega^{\prime}=0
from
[
13
]
is replaced by the simple Dirichlet condition
\omega_{3}=0
. Our simplification of the boundary conditions allows for a simpler treatment of the Green’s function and easier derivation of the upper bounds stated in Theorem
4.3
. Besides simplifying the boundary conditions, the derivation of the conditions also appears to be new. Instead of the explicit verification, we justify our conditions by a new uniqueness argument; see the proof of Theorem
2.5
(see the argument after the proof of Lemma
2.4
). Finally, note that our last section provides an upper bound for the semigroup under general conditions on the Stokes problem.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we prove that all solutions of the Stokes system satisfy the Neumann/Dirichlet boundary conditions. Note that the basic step is the uniqueness argument presented at the end of the section. Section
3
introduces the analytic semigroup using the resolvent inequality, stated in Theorem
3.1
. Section
4
introduces Green’s function with Theorem
4.2
, which is the sum of the classical heat Neumann and residual kernels. The following statement, Theorem
4.3
contains an upper bound for the spatial derivatives of the residual kernel. The proof of this statement is inspired by
[
24
]
. The upper bounds apply to the inviscid limit problem for the half-space, which states that the inviscid limit holds locally in time for data that is analytic close to the boundary and Sobolev regular elsewhere. We do not present the details since they follow
[
16
]
(see also
[
30
]
for an alternative approach). In the last part of the section, we prove Duhamel’s formula. Section
5
then contains the derivation of the resolvent and Green’s function upper bounds in the case of general boundary conditions.

2.
Vorticity formulation

2.1.
A derivation of the vorticity formulation

In this section, we provide the derivation of the new vorticity formulation.
Throughout the paper,
we use
\tau
to denote the tangential component of a vector.
We denote by
(\Delta_{\text{D}})^{-1}f
the solution

h

to the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle-\Delta h

\displaystyle=f\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{in~$\mathbb{H}$}

\displaystyle\gamma(h)

\displaystyle=0,

where both
f
and
h
are smooth and
with the necessary decay for
h
at infinity;
we denote by
\gamma
the trace of the function on
\partial\mathbb{H}
,
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, depending on the context.
Similarly, we denote by
(\Delta_{\text{N}})^{-1}f
the solution
h
to the Neumann problem

\displaystyle-\Delta h

\displaystyle=f\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{in~$\mathbb{H}$}

\displaystyle\gamma(\partial_{z}h)

\displaystyle=0

with

\lim_{z\to\infty}h(z)=0
.
Also, we introduce the operator

(2.1)

\Phi(h)=\left((-\Delta_{\text{D}})^{-1}h_{\tau},(-\Delta_{\text{N}})^{-1}h_{n}\right).

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
h\colon\overline{\mathbb{H}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
smooth
with compact support in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, is such that

h=0
on
\partial\mathbb{H}
and
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits h=0

in
\mathbb{H}
, then we have
\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))=h
in
\mathbb{H}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

First, observe that

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{H}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h(x)\phi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{H}}h(x)\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\phi(x)\,dx,

for all
h,\phi\in\left(C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})\right)^{3}
such that
h_{\tau}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{H}
.
Using the Helmholtz decomposition, for
every
f\in\left(C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})\right)^{3}

there exist
\widetilde{\phi}\colon\overline{\mathbb{H}}\to\mathbb{R}

and
\phi\colon\overline{\mathbb{H}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
f=\nabla\widetilde{\phi}+\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\phi

and
(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\phi)_{3}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{H}
;
the functions
\phi
and
\widetilde{\phi}
belong to all
W^{k,p}
,
where
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
; see
[
22
]
.
Thus we have

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))(x)\cdot f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))(x)\cdot\bigl(\nabla\widetilde{\phi}+\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\phi)\bigr)\,dx.

Let
g=\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h)
, and note that by the definition of
\Phi
, we have
g_{\tau}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{H}
. Since

\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\nabla\widetilde{\phi})=0

and

(2.4)

\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\phi=-\Delta\phi+\nabla(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\phi),

we obtain, using
(
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))(x)\cdot f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(-\Delta g(x)+\nabla(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits g)(x)\right)\cdot\phi(x)\,dx.

By the definition of
\Phi
, we have

-\Delta g(x)=\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h.

Since
h_{\tau}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}=0
on
\partial\mathbb{H}
,
we may use (
2.2
) again to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))(x)\cdot f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\bigl(h(x)\cdot\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\phi(x)+\nabla(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits g)(x)\cdot\phi(x)\bigr)\,dx.

On the other hand, since
\Delta(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits g)=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\Delta g)=-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h)=0
and

g_{\tau}=0\quad\text{and}\quad{\partial_{3}}g_{3}=0,

we get
\Delta(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits g)=0
and

\gamma(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits g)=0
,
which implies
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits g=0
. Hence, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))(x)\cdot f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}h(x)\cdot\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits\phi(x)\,dx

Finally, since
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits h=0
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}h(x)\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\phi}(x)dx=0.

Therefore,
using also
f=\nabla\widetilde{\phi}+\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\phi)
,
we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits(\Phi(\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits h))(x)\cdot f(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}h(x)\cdot f(x)\,dx,

which proves the result.
∎

We also need the following identities.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a smooth function
f
, we have

(2.5)

\gamma(\partial_{3}\Delta_{\text{D}}^{-1}\Delta f)=\gamma(\partial_{3}f)+\Lambda_{\tau}(\gamma(f))

and

(2.6)

\gamma(\Delta_{\text{N}}^{-1}\Delta f)=\gamma(f)+\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}(\gamma(\partial_{3}f)),

where
\Lambda_{\tau}=(-\Delta_{\tau})^{1/2}
and
\gamma
is the trace operator restricted at
z=0
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

The function

g=\Delta_{\text{D}}^{-1}\Delta f
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Delta g=\Delta f\\
&\gamma(g)=0.\end{split}

Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Delta(g-f)=0\\
&\gamma(g-f)=-\gamma(f).\end{split}

By the definition of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map
\mathop{\text{DN}}
,
we have

\gamma(\partial_{3}(g-f))=\mathop{\text{DN}}(\gamma(f))

(note that the normal derivative on
\partial\mathbb{H}
equals
-\partial_{3}
),
from where we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma(\partial_{3}g)&=\gamma(\partial_{3}f)+\mathop{\text{DN}}(\gamma(f))=\gamma(\partial_{3}f)+\Lambda_{\tau}(\gamma(f)),\end{split}

which gives (
2.5
) since
\gamma(\partial_{3}g)
equals
the left-hand side in (
2.5
).

To get (
2.6
), we set

g=\Delta_{\text{N}}^{-1}\Delta f
so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Delta g=\Delta f\\
&\gamma(\partial_{3}g)=0.\end{split}

This leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Delta(g-f)=0\\
&\gamma(\partial_{3}(g-f))=-\gamma(\partial_{3}f).\end{split}

Denoting by
\mathop{\text{ND}}=\mathop{\text{DN}}^{-1}
the Neumann-to-Dirichlet map, we have

\gamma((g-f))=\mathop{\text{ND}}(\gamma(\partial_{3}f))

(again noting that the normal derivative and
\partial_{3}
have opposite signs)
and thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma(g)&=\gamma(f)+\mathop{\text{ND}}(\gamma(\partial_{3}f))=\gamma(f)+\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}(\gamma(\partial_{3}f)),\end{split}

from where (
2.6
) follows.
∎

Now, we are ready to derive the vorticity formulation of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations.
Applying the curl to the equation (
1.1
) gives

\omega_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\omega-\nu\Delta\omega=\omega\cdot\nabla u.

By
Lemma
2.1
, the Biôt-Savart law for the mixed Dirichlet-Neumann condition reads

(2.7)

u=\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits W,\quad\text{where~}W=\Phi(\omega)

We next obtain the boundary conditions for

\omega
.
For the third component of the vorticity, we have

\omega_{3}=\partial_{1}u_{2}-\partial_{2}u_{1}=0\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{H}$}.

To get the boundary conditions for the horizontal component

\omega_{2}
, first note that

(2.8)

\displaystyle u_{1}=\partial_{2}W_{3}-\partial_{3}W_{2}=\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{\text{N}})^{-1}\omega_{3}-\partial_{3}(-\Delta_{\text{D}})^{-1}\omega_{2}.

Using (
1.1
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\partial_{t}u_{1}&=\bigl(\partial_{3}(-\Delta_{\text{D}})^{-1}\partial_{t}\omega_{2}\bigr)-\bigl(\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{\text{N}})^{-1}\partial_{t}\omega_{3}\bigr)\\
&=\Bigl(\partial_{3}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{2}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{2}+\nu\Delta\omega_{2})\Bigr)\\
&\quad{}-\Bigl(\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{3}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{3}+\nu\Delta\omega_{3})\Bigr)\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{H}$}.\end{split}

By (
2.5
), we have

(2.10)

\gamma(\partial_{3}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}\Delta\omega_{2})=-\gamma(\partial_{3}\omega_{2}+\Lambda_{\tau}\omega_{2})

while (
2.6
) implies

(2.11)

\gamma(\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}\Delta\omega_{3})=-\gamma(\partial_{2}\omega_{3}+\partial_{2}\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}\partial_{3}\omega_{3}).

Using
\gamma(u_{1})=0
, along with (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) in (
2.9
),
we get

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\nu\gamma((\partial_{3}+\Lambda_{\tau})\omega_{2})-\nu\gamma(\partial_{2}\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}\partial_{3}\omega_{3})\\
&\quad{}=\gamma\bigl(\partial_{3}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{2}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{2})\bigr)-\gamma\bigl(\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{3}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{3})\bigr).\end{split}

Analogously,
we obtain the boundary condition for
\omega_{1}
as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\nu\gamma((\partial_{3}+\Lambda_{\tau})\omega_{1})-\nu\gamma(\partial_{1}\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}\partial_{3}\omega_{3})\\
&\quad{}=\gamma\bigl(\partial_{3}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{1}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{1})\bigr)-\gamma\bigl(\partial_{2}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{3}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{3})\bigr).\end{split}

Using also the divergence-free condition

\partial_{3}\omega_{3}=-\nabla_{\tau}\cdot\omega_{\tau}
,
in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we obtain
the vorticity formulation for the 3D Navier-Stokes equations with mixed boundary conditions,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\omega_{t}+u\cdot\nabla\omega-\nu\Delta\omega=\omega\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle\nu(\partial_{z}+\Lambda_{\tau})\omega_{\tau}+\nu\nabla_{\tau}\Lambda_{\tau}^{-1}\nabla_{\tau}\cdot\omega_{\tau}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\quad{}=\partial_{z}(-\Delta_{D})^{-1}(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{\tau}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{\tau})+\nabla_{\tau}(-\Delta_{N})^{-1}\left(-u\cdot\nabla\omega_{3}+\omega\cdot\nabla u_{z}\right)\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{H}$},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}=0\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{H}$}.

2.2.
Equivalence of the vorticity and velocity formulations

Next, we show that the vorticity system obtained above is equivalent to the original Navier-Stokes system.

Theorem 2.3
.

Consider the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
)–(
1.3
)
with the initial data
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{H})

with the classical compatibility conditions.
Then the system is equivalent to (
2.14
)–(
2.16
).

Note that the

(see

We prove Theorem

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider the Stokes problem (

)

Proof of Theorem

.

The argument above, starting with (

),
carries over to the Stokes case. Namely, the argument shows that if
u
is a solution of (
2.17a
)–(
2.17d
),
then
\omega=\mathop{\rm curl}\nolimits u
solves (
2.18a
)–(
2.18d
).
Conversely, assuming that
\omega
solves the equations (
2.18a
)–(
2.18d
),
and letting
u=\Phi(\omega)
,
we claim that
u
solves (
2.17a
)–(
2.17d
).
Consider the solution
\widetilde{u}
of the problem (
2.17a
)–(
2.17d
); we claim that

. The vorticity

∎

Proof of Theorem

The proof follows directly from Theorem

3.
Analytic semigroup

In this section, we study the horizontal components of the system (
2.18
), which leads to the resolvent equations

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lambda\omega_{\xi}-\nu\Delta_{\xi}\omega_{\xi}=f\\
&-(\partial_{3}+|\xi|)\omega_{\xi}|_{z=0}+\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot\omega_{\xi}|_{z=0}=0,\end{split}

for fixed
\xi
. And the vertical component satisfies the heat equation with the Dirichlet boundary condition, for which the analysis is standard.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A_{\xi}
be the realization of the Laplace operator
\nu\Delta_{\xi}
with the boundary condition

-\gamma(\partial_{3}+|\xi|)u+\gamma\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot u=0,

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where
\gamma
denotes the trace operator at
\{z=0\}
. We define the domain of
A_{\xi}
by

D(A_{\xi}):=\left\{u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):-\gamma(\partial_{3}+|\xi|)u+\gamma\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot u=0\right\}.

Above and in the sequel, we do not distinguish in notation between vector and scalar valued function spaces; for example, we write

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for

(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2}
.

3.1.
Resolvent equation in
L^{2}

We first solve the resolvent equation

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\lambda-\nu\Delta_{\xi})u

\displaystyle=f,

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\gamma(\partial_{3}+|\xi|)u+\gamma\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot u

\displaystyle=0.

for
f\in L^{2}{(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
, where
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}
is fixed and

\lambda\in(-|\xi|^{2}\nu,+\infty)\setminus\{0\}
.
It is straight-forward to check that the results obtained below extend
to
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus((-\infty,-|\xi|^{2}\nu]\cup\{0\})
.
To start, we extend
f
evenly to
\bar{f}
on
\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\bar{f}(z)=\begin{cases}f(z),&z\in[0,+\infty)\\
f(-z),&z\in(-\infty,0]\end{cases}

and solve the non-homogeneous equation

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\lambda-\nu\Delta_{\xi})v=\bar{f}

in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and thus

\bar{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we may take the Fourier transform of (
3.3
) in
z
and obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}))\mathcal{F}(v)=\mathcal{F}(\bar{f}),

with
\zeta
denoting the corresponding Fourier variable. Hence, we get

{v}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}))^{-1}\mathcal{F}(\bar{f})\right)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}))^{-1}\right)\star\bar{f},

where
\star
denotes the convolution in
z
variable.
Letting

(3.5)

\mu=\nu^{-1/2}\sqrt{\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}}

results in

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}))^{-1}\right)(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}}e^{iz\cdot\zeta}\,d\zeta=\frac{1}{\nu\mu}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+\zeta^{2}}e^{iz\mu\cdot\zeta}\,d\zeta.

Recalling the Fourier transform of the Poisson kernel, we have

\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}))^{-1}\right)(z)=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}e^{-\mu|z|}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}v(z)&=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\mu|z^{\prime}-z|}\bar{f}(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\mu|z^{\prime}-z|}+e^{-\mu|z+z^{\prime}|}\right){f}(z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}.\end{split}

The difference
w=u-v
satisfies

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\lambda-\nu\Delta_{\xi})w=0\\
&-\gamma(\partial_{z}+|\xi|)w+\gamma\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot w=\gamma(\partial_{z}+|\xi|)v-\gamma(\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot v)\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}$}.\end{split}

In order to obtain a solution in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
we note that, based on the first equation in (
3.6
),
w
has the form

(3.7)

\displaystyle w(z)=c_{0}e^{-\mu z},

where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
To find
c_{0}
, we first compute the value of
(\partial_{z}+|\xi|)w+\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot w
at the boundary,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma(-(\partial_{z}+|\xi|)w+\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot w)&=\gamma(\mu c_{0}e^{-\mu z}-|\xi|c_{0}e^{\mu z}+\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot c_{0}e^{\mu z})\\
&=\mu c_{0}-|\xi|c_{0}+\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot c_{0}.\end{split}

From (
3.8
), we thus obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle Bc_{0}=\gamma((\partial_{z}+|\xi|)v-\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot v),

where the matrix
B
is given by

(3.10)

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}\mu-|\xi|+\xi_{1}^{2}|\xi|^{-1}&\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\xi|^{-1}\\
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\xi|^{-1}&\mu-|\xi|+\xi_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

It is easy to check

\displaystyle\det B=\mu(\mu-|\xi|),

and hence
B
is invertible when

\mu\neq 0
and
\mu\neq|\xi|
, which holds
if
\lambda\neq-\nu\xi^{2}
and
\lambda\neq 0
.
We then obtain

c_{0}
from (
3.9
), which then gives
w
by (
3.7
).
Then

u=v+w
satisfies the system (
3.1
) with the boundary condition (
3.2
).
For a solution
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, denoting

\displaystyle u=R(\lambda;A_{\xi})f:=(\lambda-A_{\xi})^{-1}f,

then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&R(\lambda;A_{\xi})f=v+w\end{split}

where

(3.11)

v(y)=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-\mu|z-y|}+e^{-\mu|z+y|}\right){f}(z)dz

and

(3.12)

w(y)=B^{-1}\gamma((\partial_{3}+|\xi|)v-\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot v)e^{-\mu y},

with
\mu
as in (
3.5
).

Next, we show that the operator
A_{\xi}
is sectorial and thus generates an analytic semigroup

(3.13)

\displaystyle S_{\xi}(t):=\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda;A_{\xi})d\lambda,

where
\Gamma\subseteq\rho(A_{\xi})
is a path which starts
as
\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\lambda\to-\infty
as
\mathop{\rm\mathbb{I}m}\nolimits\lambda\to-\infty
,
encircles
0
on the right side and ends
as
\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\lambda\to-\infty
and
\mathop{\rm\mathbb{I}m}\nolimits\lambda\to\infty
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The operator
A_{\xi}
is sectorial and generates an analytic semigroup
S_{\xi}
on
L^{2}
. The resolvent set
\rho(A_{\xi})
of
A_{\xi}
contains
\mathbb{C}\setminus\left((-\infty,-\nu|\xi|^{2}]\cup\{0\}\right)
. Moreover, we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C_{\theta}{\left|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}\right|^{-1}}

and

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\|_{L^{2}\to H^{1}}\leq C_{\theta}\nu^{-1/2}|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|^{-1/2}

for

\lambda\in S_{\theta,\xi}=\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda^{\prime}|\geq c_{0}|\xi|^{2},\ |\arg\lambda^{\prime}|<\theta\},

where
\theta\in(0,\pi)
is a constant and
c_{0}>0
is arbitrarily small.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

With
\lambda u-A_{\xi}u=f
, we aim to bound
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
by
\|f\|_{L^{2}}
. Recall that
u(y)=v(y)+w(y)
, where

v
and
w
are given in (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), respectively.
By (
3.4
) and Plancherel’s identity, we have

(3.16)

\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=c\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{|\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2})|^{2}}|\mathcal{F}(\bar{f})(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta\lesssim\frac{1}{|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
c
is a constant.
For
w
, note that

B^{-1}=\frac{1}{|\xi|\mu(\mu-|\xi|)}\begin{pmatrix}\mu|\xi|-\xi_{1}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\mu|\xi|-\xi_{2}^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}

and

(\partial_{3}+|\xi|)v-\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot v=|\xi|v-\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot v\hbox{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\hbox{on~$\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}$}.

Hence, we have

w(z)=\frac{1}{|\xi|(\mu-|\xi|)}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\end{pmatrix}\gamma(\xi_{2}v_{1}-\xi_{1}v_{2})e^{-\mu z}.

Since also

|v(0)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu\mu\sqrt{\mu}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

we get

\|w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{|\xi|}{|\sqrt{\mu}(\mu-|\xi|)|}|v(0)|\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu\mu^{2}}\frac{|\xi|}{|\mu-|\xi||}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

where the norms are taken in the
z
variable.
Recalling the definition of
\mu
from (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.17)

\|w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{|\xi|}{|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|\,|\mu-|\xi||}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{(\sqrt{1+{\lambda}/{\nu|\xi|^{2}}}-1)|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|}.

Since
\lambda\in S_{\theta,\xi}
, there exists a constant
C_{\theta,\omega}
such that

(3.18)

\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{\theta,\omega}}{|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), we obtain (
3.14
).
Next, by Young’s inequality, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}&=\|\zeta\mathcal{F}(v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\nu}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\nu|\zeta|^{2}}{|\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2})|^{2}}|\mathcal{F}(\bar{f})(\zeta)|^{2}\,d\zeta\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\nu|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Similarly,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|w\|_{H^{1}}&\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|}{|(\mu-|\xi|)|}|v(0)|\lesssim\frac{|\xi|}{\nu\mu|\mu-|\xi||}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\sqrt{1+{\lambda}/{\nu|\xi|^{2}}}-1)|\lambda+\nu|\xi|^{2}|^{1/2}\sqrt{\nu}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Combining (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), we then obtain (
3.15
).
∎

3.2.
Extension of
G_{\lambda,\xi}
and
S_{\xi}(t)

We now extend our definition of

R(\lambda,A_{\xi})
and
S_{\xi}(t)
from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to the space of
finite Borel measures
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

The operator
R(\lambda;A_{\xi})
is symmetric for all
\lambda\in\rho(A_{\xi})
.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

For any
u,v\in D(A_{\xi})\subseteq C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
we denote

f=(\lambda-A_{\xi})u
and
g=(\lambda-A_{\xi})v
.
Integrating by parts gives

\displaystyle\langle f,R(\lambda;A_{\xi})g\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle(\lambda-A_{\xi})u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle u,({\lambda}+\nu|\xi|^{2})v\rangle_{L^{2}}-\nu\partial_{z}u(z)\cdot\bar{v}(z)|_{z=0}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\nu u(z)\cdot\partial_{z}\bar{v}(z)|_{z=0}-\int_{0}^{+\infty}u(z)\cdot\nu\partial_{z}^{2}\bar{v}(z)\,dz.

Since
u,v\in D(A_{\xi})
, applying the boundary condition gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle(\lambda-A_{\xi})u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}&=\langle u,({\lambda}+|\xi|^{2})v\rangle_{L^{2}}+u\cdot\nu(|\xi|\bar{v}+\partial_{z}\bar{v}-\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot\bar{v})|_{z=0}\\
&\quad{}-\int_{0}^{+\infty}u(z)\cdot\nu\partial_{z}^{2}\bar{v}(z)dz,\end{split}

from where it follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle f,R(\lambda;A_{\xi})g\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle(\lambda-A_{\xi})u,v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle u,(\lambda-A_{\xi})v\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle R(\lambda;A_{\xi})f,g\rangle_{L^{2}},\end{split}

and the proof is complete.
∎

For
\phi\in\mathcal{M}
, we consider the linear functional
T_{\phi}\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

T_{\phi}(f)=\langle\phi,R({\lambda};A_{\xi})f\rangle{\rm,\qquad{}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

Since
R({\lambda};A_{\xi})f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\subseteq C_{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
we obtain by (
3.15
), that

T_{\phi}
is a bounded linear functional on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Therefore,
for
\phi\in\mathcal{M}
,
we may define
R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\phi
as an element in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Definition 3.2
.

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{M}
, we define

R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\phi
to be an element in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

\displaystyle\langle R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\phi,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=\langle\phi,R({\lambda};A_{\xi})f\rangle_{\mathcal{M}\times C_{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\lambda\in\rho(A_{\xi})
.

Since
S_{\xi}(t)
is an analytic semigroup, we have
S_{\xi}(t)f\in D(A_{\xi})
for

and

, and hence

. Therefore,
for
\phi\in\mathcal{M}
,
we may similarly define

as an element in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
as follows.

Definition 3.3
.

For any
\phi\in\mathcal{M}

and

, we define
S_{\xi}(t)\phi
as an element in

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle S_{\xi}(t)\phi,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=\langle\phi,S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle_{\mathcal{M}\times C_{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.\end{split}

for any

.

Remark 3.3
.

Due to analyticity of the semigroup, we immediately obtain that

for

and

.

4.
Green’s Function

4.1.
Analytic representation of Green’s function

Now, we introduce Green’s function

(4.1)

G_{\lambda,\xi}(z):=R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\text{diag}{(\delta_{z},\delta_{z}}).

From Definition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G_{\lambda,\xi}(y),f\rangle=\langle\text{diag}{(\delta_{y},\delta_{y})},R(\lambda;A_{\xi})f\rangle{\rm,\qquad{}}f\in(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))^{2},\end{split}

where the duality
\mathcal{M}
-
C_{\text{b}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is understood.
By (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we have

(4.2)

\langle G_{\lambda,\xi}(y),f\rangle=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-\mu|y-z|}+e^{-\mu|y+z|})f(z)dz+\int_{0}^{\infty}M(z)f(z)e^{-\mu y}\,dz

where
M(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
solves the equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}BMf(y)&=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}(\partial_{z}+|\xi|)(e^{-\mu|y-z|}+e^{-\mu|y+z|})f|_{z=0}\\
&\quad{}-\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot(e^{-\mu|y-z|}+e^{-\mu|y+z|})f|_{z=0},\end{split}

and
B
is given in (
3.10
).
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mu-|\xi|+\xi_{1}^{2}|\xi|^{-1}&\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\xi|^{-1}\\
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}|\xi|^{-1}&\mu-|\xi|+\xi_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}M(z)f=\frac{e^{-\mu z}}{\nu\mu}\begin{pmatrix}(\xi_{2}^{2}f_{1}-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}f_{2})|\xi|^{-1}\\
(-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}f_{1}+\xi_{1}^{2}f_{2})|\xi|^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

for any
f\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
which implies that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{pmatrix}\mu|\xi|-\xi_{2}^{2}&\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\mu|\xi|-\xi_{1}^{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}M(z)=\frac{e^{-\mu z}}{\nu\mu}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

Computing
M(z)
from (
4.3
) then leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}M(z)&=\frac{e^{-\mu z}}{\nu\mu}\frac{1}{\mu|\xi|^{2}(\mu-|\xi|)}\begin{pmatrix}\mu|\xi|-\xi_{1}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\mu|\xi|-\xi_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\frac{e^{-\mu z}}{\nu\mu}\frac{1}{|\xi|(\mu-|\xi|)}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

and replacing this in (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G_{\lambda,\xi}(y),f\rangle&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}(e^{-\mu|y-z|}+e^{-\mu|y+z|})\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(z)\\
f_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}dz\\
&\quad{}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu\mu}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\frac{1}{|\xi|(\mu-|\xi|)}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(z)\\
f_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\,dz.\end{split}

Finally, we note that

\frac{1}{\nu\mu|\xi|(\mu-|\xi|)}=\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu|\xi|(\nu\mu^{2}-\nu|\xi|^{2})}=\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu|\xi|\lambda}.

Thus we obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu=\nu^{-1/2}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}\nu}
where

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,-|\xi|^{2}\nu]

and
\lambda\neq 0
. There holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G_{\lambda,\xi}(y),f\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)f(z)\,dz{\rm,\qquad{}}f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\end{split}

and the kernel
G_{\lambda,\xi}(z,y)
is given by

G_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)=H_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)+R_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z),

where

H_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)=\frac{1}{2\nu\mu}(e^{-\mu|y-z|}+e^{-\mu|y+z|})I_{2}

and

(4.4)

R_{\lambda,\xi}(z,y)=\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu\lambda|\xi|}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}e^{-\mu|y+z|},

with
I_{2}
denoting the
2\times 2
identity matrix.

Next, we calculate the time-dependent
kernel
G_{\xi}(t,y)=S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y}
, where

t>0
. By Definition
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},f\rangle=&\langle\delta_{y},S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle{\rm,\qquad{}}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).\end{split}

Using Definitions
3.2
and
3.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle G_{\xi}(t,y),f\rangle=\langle S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},f\rangle=\langle\delta_{y},S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle=\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}\langle\delta_{y},R(\lambda;A_{\xi})f\rangle\,d\lambda=\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}\langle R(\lambda;A_{\xi})\delta_{y},f\rangle\,d\lambda,

where
\Gamma
is a path as in after (
3.13
).
By Lemma
4.1
and Fubini’s Theorem, we arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle G_{\xi}(t,y),f\rangle=&\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)f(z)\,dz\,d\lambda=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}G_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)\,d\lambda\right)f(z)\,dz.\end{split}

Defining

(4.5)

G_{\xi}(t,y;z):=\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}G_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)\,d\lambda,

we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

The kernel
G_{\xi}(t,y;z)
has the form

(4.6)

\displaystyle G_{\xi}(t,y;z)=H_{\xi}(t,y;z)+R_{\xi}(t,y;z),

where

(4.7)

\displaystyle H_{\xi}(t,y,z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu t}}\left(e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{4\nu t}}+e^{-\frac{|z+y|^{2}}{4\nu t}}\right)e^{-\nu|\xi|^{2}t}I_{2}

and

(4.8)

\displaystyle R_{\xi}(t,y;z)=\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu\lambda|\xi|}\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2}^{2}&-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\\
-\xi_{1}\xi_{2}&\xi_{1}^{2}\end{pmatrix}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\,d\lambda,

with
\mu=\nu^{-1/2}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}\nu}

and
I_{2}
denoting the
2\times 2
identity matrix.
The matrix function (
4.6
) solves the system

(4.9a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}G_{\xi}(t,y;z)-\nu\Delta_{\xi}G_{\xi}(t,y;z)

\displaystyle=0{\rm,\quad{}}t>0

(4.9b)

\displaystyle-\nu(\partial_{z}+|\xi|)G_{\xi}(t,y;z)|_{z=0}+\nu\xi|\xi|^{-1}\xi\cdot G_{\xi}(t,y;z)|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.9c)

\displaystyle G_{\xi}(t,y;z)|_{t=0}

\displaystyle{=\delta_{y}(z)I_{2}}{\rm,\qquad{}}z>0,

for
y>0
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

We get the equation (
4.7
) by solving the heat equation with the homogeneous Neumann condition, while (
4.8
) follows directly from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). By Lemma
4.1
, both
G_{\xi}
and
H_{\xi}
are continuous with respect to

, and (
4.9b
) holds. To prove (
4.9a
), it suffices to check that
\langle\partial_{t}G_{\xi}(t,y)-A_{\xi}G_{\xi}(t,y),f\rangle=0
, for any
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
Indeed, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle A_{\xi}G_{\xi}(t,y),f\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(-\xi^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2})G_{\xi}(t,y;z)f(z)\,dz=\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\xi}(t,y;z)(-\xi^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2})f(z)\,dz.\end{split}

Hence, we have

\langle\partial_{t}G_{\xi}(t,y)-A_{\xi}G_{\xi}(t,y),f\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},f\rangle-\langle G_{\xi}(t,y),\Delta_{\xi}f\rangle.

By the absolute convergence of the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Gamma}\lambda e^{\lambda t}G_{\lambda,\xi}(y,z)\,d\lambda f(z)\,dz,

we have

\langle\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},f\rangle=\langle\delta_{y},\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\partial_{t}G_{\xi}(t,y)-A_{\xi}G_{\xi}(t,y),f\rangle=&\langle\delta_{y},\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle-\langle S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},A_{\xi}f\rangle\\
=&\langle\delta_{y},\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle-\langle\delta_{y},A_{\xi}S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle.\end{split}

By
the analytic semigroup property of
S_{\xi}
in
L^{2}
, there holds

\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)f-A_{\xi}S_{\xi}(t)f=0,

from where

\langle\delta_{y},\partial_{t}S_{\xi}(t)f-A_{\xi}S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle=0,

establishing (
4.9a
).

The boundary condition (
4.9b
) follows by a direct computation, and we omit further details.

Finally, we check (
4.9c
). For
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we have

\langle S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},f\rangle:=\langle\delta_{y},S_{\xi}(t)f\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to 0}S_{\xi}(t)f=f,

by Definition
3.3
,
where the convergence holds uniformly on compact sets.
Therefore, we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\langle S_{\xi}(t)\delta_{y},f\rangle=f(y),

which implies (
4.9c
).
∎

4.2.
An estimate for Green’s function

In this section, we prove a result of Nguyen-Nguyen type, providing an upper bound for Green’s function.

Theorem 4.3
.

The residual kernel
R_{\xi}

may be decomposed as

(4.10)

R_{\xi}=R^{(1)}_{\xi}+R^{(2)}_{\xi}

\theta_{0}>0

.
The implicit constants in (

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

We first consider the case when
\nu|\xi|^{2}\leq 1
and,
as in

decompose the contour as
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{\text{c}}\cup\Gamma_{+}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Gamma_{-}:=\left\{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu iab-iM,\ b\in(-\infty,0]\right\},\\
&\Gamma_{\text{c}}:=\left\{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu a^{2}+Me^{i\theta},\ \theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\right\},\\
&\Gamma_{+}:=\left\{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu iab+iM,\ b\in[0,\infty)\right\},\end{split}

where
a={|y+z|}/{2\nu t}

and
M>0
is large enough so that
0
is on the left-hand side of the contour
\Gamma
.

We first consider the integral on
\Gamma_{\text{c}}
.
Recalling (
3.5
),
we have

\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\mu\geq\sqrt{\frac{\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\lambda}{\nu}+|\xi|^{2}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\lambda}}{2}\left(|\xi|+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\right)

and

\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\mu\geq\sqrt{\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\lambda/\nu}\geq a.

Since
|\lambda|\gtrsim 1
on
\Gamma_{\text{c}}
, there exists a constant
\theta_{0}
, depending on the choice of
\Gamma_{\text{c}}
, such that

\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\mu\geq\theta_{0}\mu_{0}+\frac{a}{2}.

For the case
\nu|\xi|^{2}\leq 1
, we set

(4.13)

R^{(1)}_{\xi}(t,y,z)=\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda

and

(4.14)

R^{(2)}_{\xi}=\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda.

Then we have

\left|R^{(1)}_{\xi}(t,y,z)\right|\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{Mt}e^{\nu a^{2}t}e^{-\frac{a}{2}|y+z|}e^{-\theta_{0}\mu_{0}|y+z|}\left|\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu}\right||\xi|\,|d\lambda|.

Recalling that
\mu=\nu^{-1/2}\sqrt{\lambda+|\xi|^{2}\nu}
from (

obtaining

\left|\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(t,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim\mu_{0}e^{\theta_{0}\mu_{0}|y+z|}.

For the integrals on
\Gamma_{-}
and
\Gamma_{+}
, we have

\left|e^{\lambda t}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}{\rm,\qquad{}}\lambda\in\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+},

which gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\leq\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\left|\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu\lambda}|\xi|\right||d\lambda|.\end{split}

Since on

\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu iab\pm iM
, we obtain

and thus

\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu\lambda}\,d\lambda=\pm\frac{2i(a+bi)}{\mu(\mu-|\xi|)}\,db.

Since
\mu^{2}=\lambda/\nu+|\xi|^{2}

\mu^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+(a+ib)^{2}\pm iM\nu^{-1},

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim&\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\frac{|\xi|\cdot|a+bi|}{|\mu(\mu-|\xi|)|}db\\
\lesssim&\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\frac{|\xi|\cdot|\mu|}{|\mu(\mu-|\xi|)|}db.\end{split}

Let

, and note that

on

, we may write

\frac{|\xi|}{|\mu-|\xi||}\leq\frac{1}{|\sqrt{1+r\lambda}-1|}\lesssim\frac{1+\sqrt{r|\lambda|}}{r|\lambda|}\lesssim 1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}db\lesssim(\nu t)^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}.

For the derivatives, we have

\left|\partial_{z}^{k}\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}\mu^{k}e^{-\theta_{0}\mu_{0}|y+z|}\left|\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu}\right||\xi|\,|d\lambda|.

\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}

We now address the case

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{\xi}(t,y;z):=R_{\xi}^{(1)}(t,y;z)+R_{\xi}^{(2)}(t,y;z)=R_{\xi}^{(1)}(t,y;z)+\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\frac{\mu+|\xi|}{\mu\lambda}|\xi|d\lambda,\end{split}

where

if

On

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\theta a+ib+|\xi|}{(\theta a+ib)\nu(\theta a+ib+|\xi|)(\theta a+ib-|\xi|)}|\xi|2\nu(\theta a+ib)\right||db|

,
with the two kernels satisfying the bounds

(5.12)

|\partial_{z}^{k}R^{(1)}_{\xi,\tau}(t,y,z)|\lesssim\mu_{0}^{k+1}e^{-\theta_{0}\mu_{0}(y+z)}e^{\nu((\alpha+\beta)^{2}-|\xi|^{2})t}{\rm,\qquad{}}k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

and

(5.13)

|\partial_{z}^{k}R^{(2)}_{\xi,\tau}(t,y,z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\nu t)^{(k+1)/2}}e^{-\frac{(y+z)^{2}}{\nu t}}e^{-\frac{\nu|\xi|^{2}t}{8}}{\rm,\qquad{}}k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

where

\theta_{0}>0
is a constant and
the boundary remainder coefficient
is given by
\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}(\xi,\nu)=|\xi|+\sqrt{\nu}^{-1}
.
The implicit constants in (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) depend on
k
.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that this result should be seen as a generalization of Theorem
4.3
. In fact, if we further assume that
\alpha+\beta\leq|\xi|
, then the
two results
coincide.

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Since

\frac{1}{\nu\mu(\mu-(\alpha+\beta))}=\frac{\mu+(\alpha+\beta)}{\mu\left(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2})\right)},

we have

R_{\xi,\tau}(t,y;z):=\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\frac{\mu+\alpha+\beta}{\mu\left(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2})\right)}D(\xi)\,d\lambda.

By Hypothesis
1
, there holds

|D(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|.

We first consider the case when
\nu|\xi|^{2}\leq 1
and decompose the contour as
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{\text{c}}
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Gamma_{-}:=\left\{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu iab-iM,\ -b\in[0,\infty)\right\},\\
&\Gamma_{\text{c}}:=\left\{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu a^{2}+Me^{i\theta},\ \theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\right\},\\
&\Gamma_{+}:=\left\{\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu iab+iM,\ +b\in[0,\infty)\right\},\end{split}

for some positive number
M
large enough so that a ball
B
centered at
\nu((\alpha+\beta)^{2}-|\xi|^{2})
with radius
2C_{0}
is on the left-hand side of the contour
\Gamma
, where
C_{0}>0
is as in Hypothesis
1
and

(5.14)

a=\frac{|y+z|}{2\nu t}.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we have

\mathop{\rm\mathbb{R}e}\nolimits\mu\geq\theta_{0}\mu_{0}+\frac{a}{2}.

Recalling

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)=e^{-\mu|y+z|}\frac{\mu+\alpha+\beta}{\mu(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2}))}D(\xi),\end{split}

we obtain

\left|R^{(1)}_{\xi}(t,y,z)\right|=\left|\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{Mt}e^{\nu a^{2}t}e^{-\frac{a}{2}|y+z|}e^{-\theta_{0}\mu_{0}|y+z|}\frac{|\mu|+|\xi|}{|\mu|}|\xi|d\lambda

where we used that
\nu|\xi|^{2}\leq 1
.
Hence, we get

\left|\int_{\Gamma_{\text{c}}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(t,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim\mu_{0}e^{\theta_{0}\mu_{0}|y+z|}e^{\nu((\alpha+\beta)^{2}-|\xi|^{2})t}.

For the integral on
\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}
, we have

\left|e^{\lambda t}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\right|\leq e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t},

which gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\leq\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\left|\frac{\mu+\alpha+\beta}{\mu(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2}))}\right||\xi|d\lambda.\end{split}

Since
\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu iab\pm iM
on
\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}
, we have

d\lambda=-2\nu i(a+bi)db.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we have
|a+ib|\lesssim|\mu|
. Therefore, we may write

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\frac{|\mu+\alpha+\beta||\xi|}{|\mu^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2}|}db\\
\lesssim&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\frac{|\xi|}{|\mu-\alpha-\beta|}db.\end{split}

Since
2C_{0}\leq|\lambda|
on
\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}
, we have

\frac{|\xi|}{|\mu-|\alpha+\beta||}\leq\frac{1}{|\sqrt{1+\left(\nu|\xi|^{2}\right)^{-1}\lambda}-C_{0}|}\lesssim 1.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{+}}e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)\,d\lambda\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}\,db\lesssim(\nu t)^{-1/2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}.

The proof for the derivatives is the same as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

We now consider the case when
\nu|\xi|^{2}\geq 1
using a contour

\Gamma:=\left\{\lambda=-\nu|\xi|^{2}+\nu(\theta^{2}a^{2}-b^{2})+2\nu i\theta ab,\ b\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

similar to the one in the proof of Theorem
4.3
,
where
\theta=1
when
a/(\alpha+\beta)\in(-\infty,1/2)\cup(3/2,\infty)
and
\theta=1/2
when
a/(\alpha+\beta)\in(1/2,3/2)
,
where
a
is defined in (
5.14
).
Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{\xi}(t,y;z):&=R_{\xi}^{(1)}(t,y;z)+R_{\xi}^{(2)}(t,y;z)\\
&=R_{\xi}^{(1)}(t,y;z)+\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\frac{\mu+\alpha+\beta}{\mu(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2}))}D(\xi)d\lambda\end{split}

where
R_{\xi}^{(2)}(t,y;z)
is the residue of
e^{\lambda t}R_{\xi}(\lambda,y,z)
at the pole
\lambda=\nu(-|\xi|^{2}+(\alpha+\beta)^{2})
if
\nu(-|\xi|^{2}+(\alpha+\beta)^{2})
is on the right of the contour
\Gamma
and
0
otherwise.
On
\Gamma
, we have

\mu=\theta a+ib,\ \lambda=\nu(\theta a+ib+|\xi|)(\theta a+ib-|\xi|)

and

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mu+\alpha+\beta}{\mu(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2}))}\right||d\lambda|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\theta a+ib+\alpha+\beta}{(\theta a+ib)\nu(\theta a+ib+\alpha+\beta)(\theta a+ib-\alpha-\beta)}2\nu(\theta a+ib)\right||db|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\theta a+ib-\alpha-\beta|}|db|\lesssim|db|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|R_{\xi}^{(2)}(t,y;z)|&\leq\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}e^{-\mu|y+z|}\left|\frac{\mu+\alpha+\beta}{\mu(\lambda+\nu(|\xi|^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)^{2}))}\right||D(\xi)||d\lambda|\\
&\leq C_{0}e^{-\nu|\xi|^{2}t}e^{-\frac{|y+z|^{2}}{4\nu t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\nu b^{2}t}db.\end{split}

When
\nu|\xi|^{2}\geq 1
, a direct calculation gives

|R_{\xi}^{(1)}(t,y;z)|\leq e^{\nu((\alpha+\beta)^{2}-|\xi|^{2})t}2|\xi|e^{-|\xi||y+z|}\leq 2\mu_{0}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}|y+z|}e^{\nu((\alpha+\beta)^{2}-|\xi|^{2})t},

proving (
5.12
) and (
5.13
) for
k=0
.
The estimates for
k>0
follows similarly as in the previous case,
concluding the proof.
∎

The Average of Some Irreducible Character Degrees This work was done while the first author was a PhD student at Kent State University and this paper appears as a part of his PhD dissertation under the supervision of the second author.
Ramadan Elsharif, Mark L. Lewis

Abstract
We are interested in determining the bound of the average of the degrees of the irreducible characters whose degrees are not divisible by some prime p p that guarantees a finite group G G of odd order is p p -nilpotent. We find a bound that depends on the prime p p . If we further restrict our average by fixing a subfield k k of the complex numbers and then compute the average of the degrees of the irreducible characters whose degrees are not divisible by p p and have values in k k , then we will see that we obtain a bound that depends on both p p and k k . Moreover, we find examples that make those bounds best possible. Keywords : Character Degrees, p p -nilpotence, averages. MSC(2010) : 20C15

Introduction

In this paper, all groups are finite, and for a group
G
,
{\rm Irr}(G)
is the set of (complex) irreducible characters of
G
. The average of the (complex) irreducible character degrees of
G
is

\displaystyle{\rm acd}(G)=\frac{\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr(G)}}\chi(1)}{|{\rm Irr}(G)|}.

The influence of character degrees on the structure of finite groups has been a subject of interest for many scholars. For instance, the average of the character degrees can determine the solvability of a group. Indeed, there is interest in determining the best bound for the average of the irreducible character degrees that will guarantee solvability, nilpotency, or supersolvability of the group.

The story of the average of irreducible character degrees starts when Magaard and Tong-Viet prove in Theorem 1.4 of
[
8
]
that if
{\rm acd}(G)\leq 2
, then
G
is solvable. They conjecture that this bound should be
3
. In the same direction, Isaacs, Loukaki, and Moretó in Theorem A of
[
4
]
prove this conjecture, and they conjecture the stronger statement: if the average is less than
\frac{16}{5}
, then
G
is solvable. Moretó and Hung settle this in Theorem A of
[
10
]
by proving the conjecture. In addition, they show that
A_{5}
meets this bound; so the bound is best.

We now establish some more notation. Let
p
be a prime, and take
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
to be the set of (complex) irreducible characters whose degrees are not divisible by
p
. Write
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
for the average of the degrees of characters in
{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
. Similarly, when
k
is a field, write
{\rm Irr}_{k}(G)
for the set of irreducible characters in
G
whose values lie in
k
and
{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)
for the set of irreducible characters in
G
with values lying in
k
and whose degrees are not divisible by
p
. We write
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)
for the average of the degrees of the characters in
{\rm Irr}_{k}(G)
and
{\rm acd}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)
for the average of the degrees of the characters in
{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)
. When
k=\mathbb{C}
, we use
{\rm acd}(G)
in place of
{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
and
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
in place of
{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{C},p^{\prime}}(G)
.

Moretó and Hung look at this problem from another angle. They suggest in
[
10
]
that one can look at a subset of these irreducible characters and still get information about the structure of a group. In particular, they define
{\rm acd}_{even}(G)
to be the average of the degrees of the irreducible characters whose degrees are even. They show that if
{\rm acd}_{even}(G)<\frac{16}{5}
, then
G
is solvable, see
[
10
]
. Further, they establish the idea of looking at the irreducible characters having degrees that are not divisible by
p
where
p
is some prime that divides the order of
G
. They ask in that paper if the condition that
{\rm acd}_{3^{\prime}}(G)<3
or
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)<\frac{16}{5}
when
p>5
implies that
G
must be solvable. In Theorem 1.2 of
[
3
]
, Hung settles this problem by showing: if
{\rm acd}_{3^{\prime}}(G)<3
,
{\rm acd}_{5^{\prime}}(G)<\frac{11}{4}
, or
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)<\frac{16}{5}
when
p>5
, then
G
is solvable. He also presents examples that illustrate that these bounds are best possible.

We study a condition on the average value of
k
-valued irreducible characters of a group of odd order that guarantees that the group contains a normal
p
-complement for some prime
p
. A theorem of Thompson (Theorem 1 of
[
2
]
or Corollary 12.2 of
[
5
]
) asserts that
G
has a normal
p
-complement if the degree of every irreducible character of
G
is 1 or divisible by
p
. This theorem is reformulated by Hung in
[
3
]
as follows: if
G
is a group and
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
, then
G
has a normal
p
-complement. Furthermore, Hung improves Thompson’s theorem in Theorem 1.1 of
[
3
]
: if
{\rm acd}_{2^{\prime}}(G)<3/2
, then
G
is
2
-nilpotent, and if
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)<4/3
, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent when
p
is an odd prime. Furthermore, Lewis pushes this further in Corollary 1.2 of
[
7
]
. Let
G
be a group and let
p
be an odd prime. If
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)<2(p+1)/(p+3)
, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent.

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
by a
p^{th}
root of unity. In Theorem 1.5 of
[
7
]
, Lewis proves that if
G
is a group of odd order and if
p=7
and
{\rm acd}_{7^{\prime}}(G)<9/5
or
p\neq 7
and
{\rm acd}_{p^{\prime}}(G)<2
or
{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p},p^{\prime}}(G)<2
or
{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(G)<2
, then
G
is
p
-nilpotent. In this paper, we are able to find an upper bound that depends on the odd prime
p
for any extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that contains the primitive
p^{th}
roots of unity. In particular, for the fields
k=\mathbb{C}
and
k=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
we have the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group of odd order and
p
an odd prime. Assume one of the following holds:

1.

{\rm acd}(G)<3(p+2)/(p+8)~
if
~p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
;

2.

{\rm acd}(G)<3(p^{2}+2)/(p^{2}+8)~
if
~p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
(p-1)/2
is even;

3.

{\rm acd}(G)<3(p-1)/(p+3)
if
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
(p-1)/2
is odd;

4.

{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(G)<3p^{2}/(p^{2}+2)~
if
~p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
(p-1)/2
is odd;

5.

{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(G)<3p/(p+2)~
if
~p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
;

6.

{\rm acd}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(G)<3p^{2}/(p^{2}+2)~
if
~p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
(p-1)/2

Then
G
is
p
-nilpotent.

This paper is organized as follows: in Section 1, we introduce the motivation and results related to the average character degree problem. Within Section 2, we state various basic results. In Sections 3 and 4, we prove the result in the critical restricted cases. Section 5 features the statement of the main result of the paper, the proof of the main theorem, and several examples.

Basic Lemmas

In this chapter, we present some basic results. The proof of Lemma
2.1
follows using the usual techniques from calculus for finding the minimum value of a continuous function on a closed interval.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x(x+p^{a}-1)}{x^{2}+p^{a}-1}

\displaystyle g(x)=\frac{xp^{a}}{x+p^{a}-1}
.

1.

If
p^{a}\geq 7
, then the minimum value for f(x) on
[3,\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}]
is
\displaystyle\frac{3(p^{a}+2)}{p^{a}+8}
.

2.

If
p^{a}\geq 11
, then the minimum value for
f(x)
on
[5,\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}]
is
\displaystyle\frac{3(p^{a}-1)}{p^{a}+3}
.

3.

If
p^{a}\geq 29
, then the minimum value for
f(x)
on
[5,\frac{p^{a}-1}{4}]
is
\displaystyle\frac{5(p^{a}+4)}{p^{a}+24}
.

4.

If
p^{a}\geq 11
, then the minimum value for
g(x)
on
[3,\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}]
is
\displaystyle\frac{3p^{a}}{p^{a}+2}
.

5.

If
p^{a}\geq 11
, then the minimum value for
g(x)
on
[5,\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}]
is
\displaystyle\frac{5p^{a}}{p^{a}+4}
.

[

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Then the functions
f(x)=3(p^{x}+2)/(p^{x}+8)
,
g(x)=3p^{x}/(p^{x}+2)
and
h(x)=3(p^{x}-1)/(p^{x}+3)
are increasing functions on the interval
[1,\infty)
and so their minimum values on that interval are
f(1)=3(p+2)/(p+8)
,

, and

respectively.

[

. Let

{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)\cap{\rm Irr}(G/G^{\prime})={\rm Irr}(G/A^{k}(G))

if

,

,

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a normal subgroup of
G
so that
K\cap G^{\prime}=1
. If
\varphi\in{\rm Irr}(K)
, then
\varphi
extends to
\tilde{\varphi}\in{\rm Irr}(G)
and the map
f:{\rm Irr}(G/K)\longrightarrow{\rm Irr}(G|\varphi)
defined by

is a bijection such that
f(\chi)(1)=\chi(1)
for all
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G/K)
.

We can use the same idea in the proof of Lemma 2.5 in

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
k
is a field,
G
is a group of odd order, and
p
is a prime. Assume

is a minimal normal subgroup of

3
When
H
is abelian

In Sections 3 and 4, we consider a group
G
with odd order and having the following structure. Suppose that
G=HV
and
H\cap V=1
for subgroups
H
and
V
where
V
is an elementary abelian normal
p
-group for some prime
p
and
V
can be viewed as a module for
H
that is faithful and irreducible. In Lemma
3.1
of this section we will consider the case when

is a nontrivial abelian group, and we will see that the lower bound for the average value of the degrees of the
k
-valued irreducible characters is
3(p+2)/(p+8)
when

. We derive lower bounds in other circumstances.

In the following lemma, we use the fact that when
H
is abelian that the group
G
is a Frobenius group. Observe that all of the values of the characters in
{\rm Irr}(V)
are
p^{th}
roots of unity, so they have values in

. By Theorem 6.34 of
[
5
]
the nonlinear irreducible characters of

are induced from the non-principal irreducible characters of

. This implies that
G
has exactly
(|V|-1)/|H|
nonlinear
k
-valued irreducible characters and these all have degree equal to
|H|
and vanish on

(see Problem 2.18 of
[
5
]
). Furthermore,
|H|
must divide
|V|-1
, which implies that
|H|
is co-prime to

. By Theorem 6.15 of
[
5
]
, this implies that every character in
{\rm Irr}_{k}(G)
has

-degree. In particular, we can write that
{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)={\rm Irr}_{k}(G)
and
{\rm acd}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)={\rm acd}_{k}(G)
.

Suppose
|V|=p^{a}=3
or
p^{a}=5
. We know
|H|
divides
|V|-1=p^{a}-1
, and

is odd and not 1. Therefore, this leads to a contradiction. Hence, we may assume that

Note that we set

to be the least common multiple of the orders of the roots of unity in

Assume

{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq\frac{3(p+2)}{p+8}~~~~~~

~~~~~p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
~3

{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq\frac{3(p^{2}+2)}{p^{2}+8}~~~~~

,
~\frac{p-1}{2}\nmid t~

~3

Proof.

We have
G/V\cong H

. Because

G^{\prime}\neq 1
, and because

. Moreover, we see that

[

]

is a Frobenius group in this case. Now, Lemma 3.1 of
[
7
]

-valued irreducible characters by the following equation

{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=\frac{|H|(|H:A^{k}(H)|+p^{a}-1)}{|H||H:A^{k}(H)|+p^{a}-1}.

Suppose that
3
divides
t
. Set
l=|H:A^{k}(H)|
. Now, because
H
is cyclic, there is a linear character of

whose order is

, and it is clear that this character must have at least one value that is a root of unity of order

. It follows that

will divide both

and

. Let

divides

. Since

is cyclic, it follows that

. Hence, the values of

, and so,

divides

. We conclude that

.

We know that

that have values in

. This implies that the index

is the greatest number that divides

and the number of the

is the greatest common divisor of

The equation of

becomes
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=|H|(l+p^{a}-1)/(l|H|+p^{a}-1)
, where

, so that

is co-prime to

\displaystyle{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{l(l+p^{a}-1)}{l^{2}+p^{a}-1}~~~~~if~~~~~3\leq l\leq\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}\\

\frac{5p^{a}}{p^{a}+4}~~~~~~~~~if~~~~~~~~~l=1\\

f(l)\geq 5(p+4)/(p+24)
. This yields
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 5(p+4)/(p+24)
. And, since
3p/(p+2)\leq 5(p+4)/(p+24)
for all values of
p
, we obtain the desired result that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 3p/(p+2)
.

Finally, assume that
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
. We first handle the case when
l=1
. Thus,
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 3p^{a}/(p^{a}+2)
. Since
G
is a Frobenius group and
3
does not divides
p-1
, we know that
|V|\geq p^{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
, we can see that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 3p^{2}/(p^{2}+2)
.

We now suppose
5\leq l
. There are two cases. The first case is
(p-1)/2
does not divide
t
. As before, in this case, we have
l\leq(p-1)/4
. By Lemma
2.1
(3) and Lemma
2.2
, we see that
f(l)\geq 5(p+4)/(p+24)
. Since
3p^{2}/(p^{2}+2)\leq 5(p+4)/(p+24)
, we may conclude that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 3p^{2}/(p^{2}+2)
.

The second case is
(p-1)/2
does divide
t
. In this case, we have
l\leq(p-1)/2
. Thus, Lemma
2.1
(2) and Lemma
2.2
give us that
f(l)\geq 3(p-1)/(p+3)
. Since
3(p-1)/(p+3)\leq 3p^{2}/(p^{2}+2)
, we conclude that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 3(p-1)/(p+3)
. ∎

We now consider the odd prime
p=3
in the following lemma and we will use a different method to prove our results.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
H
acts faithfully on an irreducible module
V
of characteristic
3
, and suppose that
G=HV
is a group of odd order. Suppose that
k
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that contains the primitive
3^{rd}
roots of unity. Let
H
be a nontrivial abelian group. Then,
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq{}^{182}\!/_{61}
except if
A^{k}(H)=1
and
|H|=13
, then
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq{}^{13}\!/_{5}
.

Proof.

Recall the equation of the average value of the degrees of the
k
-valued irreducible characters and set
p=3
:

{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=\frac{|H|(|H:A^{k}(H)|+3^{a}-1)}{|H||H:A^{k}(H)|+3^{a}-1}.

Now, notice that
H
acts faithfully and irreducibly on
V
, and
|H|
divides
|V|-1
. Since
|V|=3^{a}
, this implies that
|H|
is not divisible by
3
, and so,
5\leq|H|\leq(3^{a}-1)/2
. First, we handle the case when
A^{k}(H)=1
. In this case the equation becomes
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=|H|(|H|+3^{a}-1)/(|H|^{2}+3^{a}-1)
and the function
f(x)=x(x+3^{a}-1)/(x^{2}+3^{a}-1)
is increasing on the interval
5\leq x\leq(3^{a}-1)/2
. Consider that
|H|=5
. This implies
|V|=3^{a}\geq 3^{4}
and
f(5)=5(3^{a}+4)/(3^{a}+24)
is at least
5(3^{4}+4)/(3^{4}+24)=85/21
for all
a\geq 4
. We can conclude that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 85/21\geq 182/61
. Consider
|H|=7
or
11
. This implies
|V|\geq 3^{6}
or
3^{5}
. In the same way we can see that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 81/17\geq 182/61
in both cases. Now, assume
|H|=13
. This implies
|V|\geq 3^{3}
. We can show that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 13/5
which is the exception. Finally, assume
|H|\geq 17
. This implies
|V|\geq 3^{6}
and the function
f(x)=x(x+3^{a}-1)/(x^{2}+3^{a}-1)
has a minimum value
3(3^{a}-1)/(3^{a}+3)
at
x=(3^{a}-1)/2
. Again, we see that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 182/61
.

For the rest of the proof, assume that
|A^{k}(H)|\neq 1
. Notice, since
|H|
is not divisible by
3
, that we have
|A^{k}(H)|\geq 5
. Consider
|H|=5
or
7
. This implies
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=|H|3^{a}/(|H|+3^{a}-1)
. Then, we see that
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=5(3^{a})/(3^{a}+4)
or
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)=7(3^{a})/(3^{a}+6)
. So, we obtain the desired result
{\rm acd}_{k}(G)\geq 182/61
in both cases. Now, assume that
|H|\geq 11
(we may assume
|H|\neq 9
, because
|H|
is not divisible by 3). We can write
(187/81)|H|-11\geq 106/81|H|
, and
(|H|-81/17)\geq 106/187|H|
. Also, we have that
|A^{k}(H)|\geq 5
and
(3^{a}-1)/|H|\geq 2
. This implies
(3^{a}-1)\geq 10|H:A^{k}(H)|
. Thus, we compute

\displaystyle(|H|-81/17)(3^{a}-1)\geq{}^{64}\!/_{17}|H||H:A^{k}(H)|.

Finally, we compute

\displaystyle|H|(|H:A^{k}(H)|+3^{a}-1)

\displaystyle=|H||H:A^{k}(H)|+(|H|-{}^{81}\!/_{17})(3^{a}-1)+{}^{81}\!/_{17}(3^{a}-1)

\displaystyle\geq|H||H:A^{k}(H)|+{}^{64}\!/_{17}|H||H:A^{k}(H)|+{}^{81}\!/_{17}(3^{a}-1)

\displaystyle={}^{81}\!/_{17}(|H||H:A^{k}(H)|+3^{a}-1).

Hence, we obtain the desired result. ∎

4
When
H
is nonabelian

In Section 4, we present some lemmas to handle the case when
H
is a nonabelian subgroup of the group
G=HV
that appeared early in Section 3. In the following lemma we will see that every
H
-orbit on
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
has length at least
7
, and at least one such orbit has length that is not divisible by
p
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
H
acts faithfully on an irreducible module
V
of characteristic
p
, and suppose that
G=HV
is a group of odd order. Let
H
be a nonabelian group. Then, every
H
-orbit on
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
has length at least
7
, and at least one such orbit has length that is not divisible by
p
. Furthermore, among the
H
-orbits whose length is not divisible by
p
, either there are at least two
H
-orbits of length
7
or there is at least one
H
-orbit of length at least
9
, and when
p=3
, this orbit must have length at least 11.

Proof.

The set
{\rm Irr}(V)
may be viewed as a faithful irreducible module for
H
of order
p^{a}
. Suppose
\alpha\in{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
, and let
T
be the stabilizer of
\alpha
in
H
. Since
H
acts faithfully and irreducibly on
V
, we see that
H
acts faithfully and irreducibly on
{\rm Irr}(V)
. In particular,
{\rm Irr}(V)
as a module will be generated by the elements in the orbit of
\alpha
. Since the action of
H
is faithful,
\cap_{h\in H}T^{h}=1
and thus,
T
is core-free in
H
. Also, we see that
H
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
S_{n}
where
n=|H:T|
and
n
is odd. Since
H
is nonabelian, it must be that
n>2
. Also,
n\neq 3
or
5
, because
S_{3}
and
S_{5}
do not have any nonabelian subgroups of odd order. This implies that
n=|H:T|
is at least
7
. Hence, every
H
-orbit on
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
has length at least
7
. Also, the lengths of the
H
-orbits of
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
must sum to
p^{a}-1
, so that at least one of these orbits must have length that is not divisible by
p
.

Assume that the last conclusion is not true. This implies that there is only one
H
-orbit whose length is not divisible by
p
and the length of this orbit is equal
7
. Let
\alpha
be a representative of the
H
-orbit whose length is not divisible by
p
. Let
T
be the stabilizer of
\alpha
in
H
and we know that
|H:T|=7
. Now, we know that
{\rm Irr}(V)
is the union of
H
-orbits, the orbit of the principal character
1_{V}
, the orbit of
\alpha
, and the remaining orbits (whose lengths are divisible by
p
). This yields
p^{a}=|V|=1+7+pr
for some positive integer
r
, and then
p^{a}\equiv 8\pmod{p}
. This implies that
p
divides
8
which is a contradiction since
p
is an odd prime. Note that the smallest length of an orbit whose length is not divisible by 3 will not be 9, so it must be that 11 is the smallest such length when
p=3
.
∎

In Theorem 3.3 of
[
7
]
, Lewis demonstrates conclusion (1) of the following lemma, and the proofs of conclusions (2) and (3) are also motivated by the proof of Theorem 3.3 of
[
7
]
. For any prime
p
and a field
k
, define
{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)
to be the set of nonlinear irreducible characters in
G
with values in
k
and whose degree are not divisible by
p
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
H
acts faithfully on an irreducible module
V
of characteristic
p
, and suppose that
G=HV
is a group of odd order. Suppose that
k
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that contains the primitive
p^{th}
roots of unity. Let
H
be a nonabelian group. Then

1.

\displaystyle|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)|=|H:A^{k}(H)|+|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|
,

2.

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|
,
p\neq 3
,

3.

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+5|{\rm nl}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(T_{i})|
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
,
H
has an orbit on
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
whose length is not divisible by
p
and that length is at least
7
. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{l}
be representatives for the
H
-orbits of
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
whose lengths are not divisible by
p
. Let
T_{i}
be the stabilizer of
\alpha_{i}
in
H
. Furthermore, we apply Theorem 1.4 of
[
6
]
(the Fundamental Counting Principle) to conclude that
|H:T_{i}|
will equal the length of the
H
-orbit of
\alpha_{i}
.

Now arguing as in Theorem 3.3 of
[
7
]
, we obtain Conclusion (1):

|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)|=|H:A^{k}(H)|+|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

Using Theorem 6.11 of
[
5
]
, we have
{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G|\alpha_{i})=\{(\alpha_{i}\times\tau)^{G}|\tau\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})\}.
Since
|G:VT_{i}|=|H:T_{i}|
, it follows that for any
\psi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G|\alpha_{i})
we have
\psi(1)=|H:T_{i}|\tau(1)
where
\tau\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)=\sum_{\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)}\gamma(1)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}|(\sum_{\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})}\theta(1)).

Also, since
|H|
is odd and
\gamma(1)
divides
|H|
, we see that
\gamma(1)\geq 3
for all
\gamma\in{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)
. Thus,

|H:A^{k}(H)|+\sum_{\gamma(1)\neq 1,\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)}\gamma(1)=\sum_{\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)}\gamma(1)\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|,

and

\sum_{\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})}\theta(1)\geq|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

This yields Conclusion (2):

\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

Finally, assume
p=3
. From the above work, it is still true that

|{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(G)|=|H:A^{k}(H)|+|{\rm nl}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(T_{i})|,

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)=\sum_{\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)}\gamma(1)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}|(\sum_{\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(T_{i})}\theta(1)).

Since
H
acts faithfully on the irreducible module
V
of characteristic 3, then we have
|H|\geq 5
, and
\gamma(1)
divides
|H|
. This implies
\gamma(1)\geq 5
for all
\gamma\in{\rm nl}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)
. Thus, we compute that

|H:A^{k}(H)|+\sum_{\gamma(1)\neq 1,\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)}\gamma(1)=\sum_{\gamma\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)}\gamma(1)\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+5|{\rm nl}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)|,

and observe that

\sum_{\theta\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(T_{i})}\theta(1)\geq|{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

Hence, Conclusion (3) follows:

\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+5|{\rm nl}_{k,3^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,3^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

∎

Now, we are ready to present the proof of the case where
H
is nonabelian. In the following lemma we will consider the nonabelian case for the subgroup
H
. And we will show that the
{\rm acd}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)\geq 3
for
p\neq 3
, and
{\rm acd}_{k,3^{\prime}}(G)\geq 81/17
. These values are the best lower bounds that we found for any field
k
such that
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\subseteq k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. We can do better for other circumstances.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
H
acts faithfully on an irreducible module
V
of characteristic
p
, and suppose that
G=HV
is a group of odd order. Suppose that
k
is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that contains the primitive
p^{th}
roots of unity. Let
H
be a nonabelian group. Then the following holds.

1.

If
p\neq 3
, then
{\rm acd}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)\geq 3
.

2.

If
p=3
, then
{\rm acd}_{k,3^{\prime}}(G)\geq{}^{81}\!/_{17}
.

Proof.

Working as in Lemma
4.1
, take
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},...,\alpha_{l}
to be representatives for the
H
-orbits of
{\rm Irr}(V)\setminus\{1_{V}\}
whose lengths are not divisible by
p
. For all
i
, let
T_{i}
be the stabilizer of
\alpha_{i}
in
H
and note that
|H:T_{i}|
is not divisible by
p
and
|H:T_{i}|\geq 7
. Also, we have that
T_{i}
is core-free in
H
for all
i
.

First, we notice that
A^{k}(T_{1})<A^{k}(H)
. Otherwise, we have
A^{k}(T_{1})=A^{k}(H)
, and this implies that
1\leq A^{k}(H)\leq T_{1}
which contradicts the fact that
T_{1}
is core-free. Thus, we have
|H:A^{k}(T_{1})|\geq 3|H:A^{k}(H)|
. Note that the number of linear characters of
T_{1}
with values in
k
is
|T_{1}:A^{k}(T_{1})|
, and the linear characters of
T_{1}
with values in
k
all lie in
{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})
. This yields
|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|\geq|T_{1}:A^{k}(T_{1})|
.

Now, we will work by cases on the length of
H
-orbits on
V
whose lengths are not divisible by
p
. As shown in Lemma
4.1
, we have only two cases for
p\neq 3
:

Case(1):
There are at least two
H
-orbits whose lengths are not divisible by
p
and their lengths equal
7
. Among the
\alpha_{i}
relabel the subscripts so that
|H:T_{i}|=7
and
|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|\leq|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{2})|
for
i=1
,
2
. Thus, we have that
3|H:T_{1}|=7+2|H:T_{1}|
and
\displaystyle|H:T_{1}|-\frac{7}{3}=\frac{2}{3}|H:T_{1}|
. We can combine this with the previous facts that
|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|\geq|T_{1}:A^{k}(T_{1})|
and
\frac{1}{3}|H:A^{k}(T_{1})|\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|
to calculate the following

\displaystyle\left(|H:T_{1}|-\frac{7}{3}\right)|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|\geq\frac{2}{3}|H:A^{k}(T_{1})|\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|.

In addition we compute

\displaystyle|H:T_{1}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|

\displaystyle=\left(|H:T_{1}|-\frac{7}{3}\right)|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|+\frac{7}{3}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|

\displaystyle\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|+\frac{7}{3}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|.

The existence of
\alpha_{2}
guarantees that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|\neq 0
. With this in mind and the fact that
|H:T_{i}|\geq 7
for all
i
, we can calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|+{}^{7}\!/_{3}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|+7\sum_{i=2}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|+3\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

Finally, by Lemma
4.2
(2) we compute

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)

\displaystyle\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+2|H:A^{k}(H)|+3\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq 3\left(|H:A^{k}(H)|+|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|\right).

Hence, by applying Lemma
4.2
(1) we get the desired result in this case that
{\rm acd}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)\geq 3
.

Case(2):
There is at least one
H
-orbit whose length is not divisible by
p
and that length is at least
9
. Among the
\alpha_{i}
, relabel the subscripts so that
|H:T_{1}|\geq 9
. Thus, we can write that
3|H:T_{1}|\geq 9+2|H:T_{1}|
and
\displaystyle|H:T_{1}|-3\geq{}^{2}\!/_{3}|H:T_{1}|
. Recall that
|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|\geq|T_{1}:A^{k}(T_{1})|
and
{}^{1}\!/_{3}|H:A^{k}(T_{1})|\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|
. Thus, we derive

\displaystyle(|H:T_{1}|-3)|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|\geq{}^{2}\!/_{3}|H:A^{k}(T_{1})|\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|.

Also, we calculate

\displaystyle|H:T_{1}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|

\displaystyle=(|H:T_{1}|-3)|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|+3|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|

\displaystyle\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|.

Notice that for all
i
we have
|H:T_{i}|\geq 7
, and then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{1})|+7\sum_{i=2}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq 2|H:A^{k}(H)|+3\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|.

Thus, by Lemma
4.2
(2) we compute

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)}\chi(1)

\displaystyle\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|H:T_{i}||{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq|H:A^{k}(H)|+3|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+2|H:A^{k}(H)|+3\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|

\displaystyle\geq 3\left(|H:A^{k}(H)|+|{\rm nl}_{k,p^{\prime}}(H)|+\sum_{i=1}^{l}|{\rm Irr}_{k,p^{\prime}}(T_{i})|\right).

In a similar way we obtain the desired result
{\rm acd}_{k,p^{\prime}}(G)\geq 3.

For the rest of the proof, assume that
p=3
. In similar way, we will work by cases on the length of such
H
-orbits on
V
whose lengths are not divisible by
3
. Again, by Lemma
4.1
we have the following two cases.

Case(1):
Consider that we have at least two
H
-orbits whose lengths are not divisible by 3 and their lengths are equal to 7. Among the
\alpha_{i},
relabel the subscripts so that
|H:T_{i}|=7
for
i=1
,
2
. In the second paragraph of this proof, we showed that
A^{k}(T_{i})<A^{k}(H)
. So by The Diamond Isomorphism Theorem of
[
1
]
we can derive that
|A^{k}(H):A^{k}(T_{i})|\geq 7
and
|H:A^{k}(T_{i})|\geq 7|H:A^{k}(H)|
for
i=1
,
2
. Also, the fact that
|H:T_{i}|=7
implies
|H:T_{i}|\geq\frac{7(81)}{87}
for
i=1
,
2
. Then for
i=1
,
2
, we see that
\frac{87}{17}|H:T_{i}|-\frac{7(81)}{17}\geq 0
and
7|H:T_{i}|-\frac{7(81)}{17}\geq\frac{32}{17}|H:T_{i}|
. This implies the following inequality for
i=1
,
2
:

|H:T_{i}|-\frac{81}{17}\geq\frac{32}{7(17)}|H:T_{i}|.

Also, for
i=1
,
2
, we know that every linear character of

Main Theorem

In Section 5, we prove the Main Theorem. We consider a field

roots of unity such that

. Suppose that

is the least common multiple of the orders of the roots of unity in

having order dividing
|G|
. We will show that the optimal upper bound for groups of odd order depends on the odd prime

]

Theorem 5.1
.

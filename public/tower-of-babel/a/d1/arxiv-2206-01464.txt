Pointwise semicommutative rings
Sanjiv Subba †, Tikaram Subedi †∗, A. M. Buhphang ††

Abstract
. We call a ring R R pointwise semicommutative if for any element a ∈ R a\in R either l ⁡ ( a ) l(a) or r ⁡ ( a ) r(a) is an ideal of R R . A class of pointwise semicommutative rings is a strict generalization of semicommutative rings. Since reduced rings are pointwise semicommutative, this paper studies sufficient conditions for pointwise semicommutative rings to be reduced. For a pointwise semicommutative ring R R , R R is strongly regular if and only if R R is left SF; R R is exchange if and only if R R is clean; if R R is semiperiodic then R / J ⁡ ( R ) R/J(R) is commutative.

Definition 2.1

We call a ring

Proposition 2.3
.

Let R be a pointwise semicommutative ring. Then:

(1)

R is directly finite.

(2)

R is left min-abel.

Proof.

(1)

Let

Proposition 2.6

Let

Proposition 2.7

Proposition 2.9
.

Let

is 2-primal.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let R be a pointwise semicommutative exchange ring, then R is clean.

Proof.

Let

satisfying

and

. So

and

,

. Then
(w-(1-e))(h-k)=wh-wk-(1-e)h+(1-e)k=wh+(1-w)k-(1-e)h-ek=1-(1-e)h-ek
. Since

is pointwise semicommutative, either

or

is an ideal of

. If

is an ideal of

, then

is an ideal of

(

A ring

Lemma 2.11
.

Let

be a pointwise semicommutative ring. If

is semiperiodic then

Proof.

Suppose

for some positive integer

, then

is right quasi-regular. If

, then

is nilpotent, and so

is right quasi-regular. Assume that

. Then by
[
1
, Lemma 2.3(iii)]
, there exist a positive integer

and

such that
(wx)^{p}=(wx)^{p}e
and

for some

. Observe that
e=wy=ewy=ew(1-e)y+ewey=ew(1-e)y+ew^{2}y^{2}=...=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}ew^{i}(1-e)y^{i}+ew^{k}y^{k}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}ew^{i}(1-e)y^{i}
. Since

is pointwise semicommutative,

or

is an ideal of

. If

is an ideal of the

, then

. If

is an ideal of

, that is,

, that is,

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
R
be a pointwise semicommutative ring. If

is semiperiodic, then:

(1)

is commutative.

(2)

R
is

.

(3)

(1)

By Lemma

. Write

( see Lemma

), that is,

. Suppose

, then

. If

, then
\bar{w}\bar{R}\bar{w}=0
. Since
\bar{R}
is semiprime,
\bar{w}=0
. Assume, if possible, that

. By
[

, there exist a positive integer

and

such that

and

for some

. Hence

, that is,

(2)

Let

and

. By Lemma

. By
[

,

. This implies that

and hence

is an ideal.

(3)

By

. This yields that

The following examples show that the skew polynomial ring and the polynomial ring over a pointwise semicommutative ring need not be pointwise semicommutative.

Example 2.13
.

(1)

Let

\sigma(h,w)=(w,h)

(2)

Take
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Cohomology and deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras
Jia Zhao, Yu Qiao, Senrong Xu*

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras and establish the cohomology theory of crossed homomorphisms via the Yamaguti cohomology. Consequently, we use this cohomology to characterize linear deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We show that if two linear or formal deformations of a crossed homomorphism are equivalent, then their infinitesimals are in the same cohomology class in the first cohomology group. Moreover, we show that an order n n deformation of a crossed homomorphism can be extended to an order n + 1 n+1 deformation if and only if the obstruction class in the second cohomology group is trivial.

1.2.
Crossed homomorphisms

The notion of crossed homomorphisms on Lie algebras was introduced when nonabelian extension of Lie algebras was studied
[
21
]
. An example of crossed homomorphisms is a differential operator of weight
1
, and a flat connection 1-form of a trivial principle bundle is also a crossed homomorphism. In
[
25
]
, authors showed that the category of weak representations (resp. admissible representations) of Lie-Rinehart algebras (resp. Leibniz pairs) is a left module category over the monoidal category of representations of Lie algebras using crossed homomorphisms. Later, cohomology and deformations of crossed homomorphisms on
3
-Lie algebras were also studied in
[
14
]
. Thus it is natural to consider cohomology and deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

More precisely, for a crossed homomorphism
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
, the most important step is to establish the cohomology theory of
H
. Our strategy is as follows: First we introduce linear maps
\rho_{H}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
and
\mu_{H}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
via
H
, and prove that
(\mathfrak{h};\rho_{H},\mu_{H})
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on the vector space
\mathfrak{h}
. Consequently, we obtain a corresponding Yamaguti cohomology of Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho_{H},\mu_{H})
. Note that Yamaguti cohomology stars from
1
-cochains. Thus the second step is to construct
0
-cochains and the corresponding coboundary maps, which is a difficulty to overcome. Once the cohomology theory is established, we are able to explore the relationship between cohomology and deformations of crossed homomorphisms. For this purpose, we intend to investigate three kinds of deformations: linear, formal, and higher order deformations.

Note that a Lie-Yamaguti algebra can be reduced to a Lie triple system when the ternary bracket is trivial, thus the notion of crossed homomorphisms and the cohomology and deformation theory of those between Lie triple systems can be obtained directly from the present paper.

1.3.
Outline of the paper

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations, and cohomology. In Section 3, we introduce the notion of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between crossed homomorphisms and Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphisms. In Section 4, we establish the cohomology of crossed homomorphisms on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and examine a functorial property of the cohomology theory. Finally in Section 5, we explore three kinds of deformations, and show that the infinitesimal of linear and formal deformations can be governed by cohomology and that the extension of a higher deformation is characterized by a special cohomology class. This is why we call this cohomology class the obstruction class.

In this paper, all vector spaces are assumed to be over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and finite-dimensional.

2.
Preliminaries: Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and cohomology

In this section, we recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and their cohomology theories.
The notion of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was introduced by Yamaguti in
[
32
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
16
]

A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and a trilinear bracket
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, which meet the following conditions: for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

\displaystyle[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket[x,y],z,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[y,z],x,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[z,x],y,w\right\rrbracket=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,[z,w]\right\rrbracket=[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w]+[z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket],

(4)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket.

In the sequel, we denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. Define a trilinear bracket

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

by

\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket:=[[x,y],z],\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then by a direct computation, we know that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

The following example is even more interesting.

Example 2.3
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold with an affine connection, and denote by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of vector fields on
M
. For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, set

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-T(x,y),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-R(x,y)z,

where
T
and
R
are torsion tensor and curvature tensor respectively. It turns out that the triple

(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. See
[
24
]
for more details.

Next, we recall the notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 2.4
.

[
33
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space
V
equipped with a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and a bilinear map
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, which meet the following conditions: for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(5)

\displaystyle\mu([x,y],z)-\mu(x,z)\rho(y)+\mu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\mu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\mu(x,z)+\rho(z)\mu(x,y)=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],

(8)

\displaystyle\mu(z,w)\mu(x,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(x,z)-\mu(x,\left\llbracket y,z,w\right\rrbracket)+D_{\rho,\mu}(y,z)\mu(x,w)=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+\mu(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\mu(z,w)],

where the bilinear map
D_{\rho,\mu}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is given by

(10)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y):=\mu(y,x)-\mu(x,y)+[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y]),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is obvious that
D_{\rho,\mu}
is skew-symmetric, and we write
D
in the sequel without ambiguities. We denote a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
by
(V;\rho,\mu)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\rho=0
and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie tripe system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, then the representation reduces to that of the Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
:
(V;\mu)
. If
\mu=0
,
D=0
and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, then the representation reduces to that of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
:
(V;\rho)
. Hence a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a natural generalization of that of a Lie algebra or of a Lie triple system.

By a direct computation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. Then the following equalities are satisfied:

\displaystyle D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)=0;

\displaystyle D(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+D(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D(x,y),D_{\rho,\mu}(z,w)];

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)=\mu(x,w)\mu(z,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(z,x)-\mu(z,w)D(x,y).

Example 2.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. We define linear maps
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{R}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by
x\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{x}
and
(x,y)\mapsto\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}
respectively, where
\mathrm{ad}_{x}z=[x,z]
and
\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket
for all
z\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
forms a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, where
\mathfrak{L}:=D_{\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}=\mathfrak{R}_{y,x}-\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}+[\mathrm{ad}_{x},\mathrm{ad}_{y}]-\mathrm{ad}_{[x,y]},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

By (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall z\in\mathfrak{g}.

In this case,
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
is called the
adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let us recall the cohomology theory on Lie-Yamaguti algebras given in
[
33
]
. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote the set of
p
-cochains by
C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)~(p\geqslant 1)
, where

\displaystyle C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\triangleq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n},V)\times\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V),&\forall n\geqslant 1,\\
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V),&n=0.\end{cases}

In the sequel, we recall the coboundary map of
p
-cochains:

•

If
n\geqslant 1
, for any
(f,g)\in C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, the coboundary map

\delta=(\delta_{\rm I},\delta_{\rm II}):C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{n+2}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad(f,g)\mapsto(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g))

is given as follows:

(11)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\rho(x_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})-\rho(y_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[x_{n+1},y_{n+1}])\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}D(\mathfrak{X}_{k})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1}),

(12)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\mu(y_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})-\mu(x_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k+1}D(\mathfrak{X}_{k})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},\left\llbracket x_{k},y_{k},z\right\rrbracket),

where
\mathfrak{X}_{i}=x_{i}\wedge y_{i}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}~(i=1,\cdots,n+1),~z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l}:=\left\llbracket x_{k},y_{k},x_{l}\right\rrbracket\wedge y_{l}+x_{l}\wedge\left\llbracket x_{k},y_{k},y_{l}\right\rrbracket
.

•

If
n=0
, for any
f\in C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, the coboundary map

\delta:C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{2}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),

\qquad\qquad\qquad f\mapsto(\delta_{\rm I}(f),\delta_{\rm II}(f))

is defined to be

(13)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f)\Big)(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)f(y)-\rho(y)f(x)-f([x,y]),

(14)

\displaystyle~\Big(\delta_{\rm II}(f)\Big)(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)f(z)+\mu(y,z)f(x)-\mu(x,z)f(y)-f(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Yamaguti showed the following fact.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
33
]

With the notations above, for any
f\in C^{1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm I}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f)),\delta_{\rm II}(f)\Big)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta_{\rm II}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f)),\delta_{\rm II}(f)\Big)=0.

Moreover, for all
(f,g)\in C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),~(p\geqslant 2)
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm I}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad\delta_{\rm II}\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)=0.

Thus the cochain complex
(C^{\bullet}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\bigoplus\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}C^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),\delta)
is well defined. For convenience, we call this cohomology the

in this paper.

Definition 2.9
.

With the above notations, let
(f,g)
in

(resp.

for

) be a
p
-cochain. If it satisfies
\delta(f,g)=0
(resp.
\delta(f)=0
), then it is called a
p
-cocycle. If there exists
(h,s)\in C^{p-1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, (resp.

, if

) such that
(f,g)=\delta(h,s)
(resp.

), then it is called a

-coboundary (

). The set of

-cocycles and that of

and

respectively. The resulting

H^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)=Z^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)/B^{p}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V).

In particular, we have

3.
Crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and show that a crossed homomorphism can be seen as a homomorphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Moreover, a crossed homomorphism corresponds to a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1.
Before this, we introduce the notion of center of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Denote the
center
of
\mathfrak{g}
by

C(\mathfrak{g}):=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}|[x,y]=0,\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}\}\cap\Big(\{x\in\mathfrak{g}|\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket=0,\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g}\}\cup\{x\in\mathfrak{g}|\left\llbracket y,x,z\right\rrbracket=0,\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g}\}\Big).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Let
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on the vector space
\mathfrak{h}
, i.e., linear maps
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
,
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
, and
D:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
are given by Eqs. (
5
)-(
10
). If for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, the following conditions are satisfied

\displaystyle\rho(x)u,\mu(x,y)u\in C(\mathfrak{h}),

\displaystyle\rho(x)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mu(x,y)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,

\displaystyle\rho(x)\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mu(x,y)\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}=0.

then we say that
(\rho,\mu)
is an
action
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Let
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. By (
10
), we deduce that

D(x,y)u\in C(\mathfrak{g}),~D(x,y)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}=D(x,y)\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

The following proposition shows that an action of Lie-Yamaguti algebras can be used to characterize semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Let
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
, then there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on the direct sum
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
defined by

\displaystyle[x+u,y+v]_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+D(x,y)w+\mu(y,z)u-\mu(x,z)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
. This Lie-Yamaguti algebra is called the
semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebra
with respect to the action
(\rho,\mu)
, and is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

It is a direct computation, and we omit the details.
∎

The following definition is standard.

Definition 3.3
.

[
27
,
28
]

Suppose that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
are two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A
homomorphism
from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
that preserves the Lie-Yamaguti algebra structures, that is, for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\displaystyle\phi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\phi(x),\phi(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle~\phi(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\phi(x),\phi(y),\phi(z)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}.

If, moreover,
\phi
is a bijection, it is then called an
isomorphism
.

Now we are ready to introduce the notion of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Let
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. A linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is called a
crossed homomorphism
from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to
(\rho,\mu)
, if

(15)

\displaystyle H[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)H(y)-\rho(y)H(x)+[Hx,Hy]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(16)

\displaystyle\ \ H\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)H(z)+\mu(y,z)H(x)-\mu(x,z)H(y)+\left\llbracket Hx,Hy,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Remark 3.5
.

If the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
is trivial, then any crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism as in Definition
3.3
; if
\mathfrak{h}
is commutative, then any crossed homomorphism is a derivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a
4
-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
a basis. The nonzero brackets are given by

[e_{1},e_{2}]=2e_{4},\quad\quad\left\llbracket e_{1},e_{2},e_{1}\right\rrbracket=e_{4}.

It is obvious that the center of
\mathfrak{g}
is spanned by
\{e_{3},e_{4}\}
, and that the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
is an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself. Then

\displaystyle H=\begin{pmatrix}O&A\\
B&C\end{pmatrix}

is a crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}
, where
A
and
C
have the form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1}&0\\
\lambda_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The following theorem shows that a crossed homomorphism can be seen as a homomorphism between Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
on another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
. Then a linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
if and only if the linear map
\phi_{H}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism, where

\phi_{H}(x):=(x,Hx),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{H}\Big([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},H[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big),

\displaystyle~[\phi_{H}(x),\phi_{H}(y)]_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(x,Hx),(y,Hy)]_{\rho,\mu}=\Big([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x)Hy-\rho(y)Hx+[Hx,Hy]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle\phi_{H}\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}},H\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big),

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket\phi_{H}(x),\phi_{H}(y),\phi_{H}(z)\right\rrbracket_{\rho,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}},D(x,y)H(z)+\mu(y,z)H(x)-\mu(x,z)H(y)+\left\llbracket Hx,Hy,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Thus, we have that the linear map
\phi_{H}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism if and only of the following two equalities hold:

\displaystyle H[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)Hy-\rho(y)Hx+[Hx,Hy]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle\ \ \ ~H\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)H(z)+\mu(y,z)H(x)-\mu(x,z)H(y)+\left\llbracket Hx,Hy,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

which implies that the linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
. This completes the proof.
∎

\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

At the end of this section, we reveal the relationship between crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We give the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
\lambda

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
its representation. A linear map

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u+\lambda[u,v]\Big),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v+\lambda\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket\Big),\quad\forall u,v,w\in V.

Relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight on Lie algebras stem from the classical Yang-Baxter equation and have many applications on mathematical physics. Here, we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weight on Lie-Yamaguti algebras and explore its relation with crossed homomorphisms.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(\rho,\mu)
be an action of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
on another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
. An invertible linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to
(\rho,\mu)
if and only if
H^{-1}:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the representation
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
.

Proof.

Assume that the invertible linear map
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a crossed homomorphism, then for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, by (
15
), we have

\displaystyle[H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=H^{-1}\Big(H[H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{-1}\Big(\rho\big(H^{-1}(u)\big)v-\rho\big(H^{-1}(v)\big)w+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Similarly, by (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\left\llbracket H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v),H^{-1}(w)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{-1}\Big(H\left\llbracket H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v),H^{-1}(w)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{-1}\Big(D\big(H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v)\big)w+\mu\big(H^{-1}(v),H^{-1}(w)\big)u-\mu\big(H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(w)\big)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Thus
H^{-1}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
.

Conversely, let
H^{-1}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
. For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, there exist
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, such that
x=H^{-1}(u),y=H^{-1}(v)
, and
z=H^{-1}(w)
. Then we have

\displaystyle H[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=H[H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\circ H^{-1}\Big(\rho\big(H^{-1}(u)\big)v-\rho\big(H^{-1}(v)\big)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)H(y)-\rho(y)H(x)+[Hx,Hy]_{\mathfrak{h}},

and

\displaystyle H\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}=H[H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v),H^{-1}(w)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\circ H^{-1}\Big(D\big(H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(v)\big)w+\mu\big(H^{-1}(v),H^{-1}(w)\big)u-\mu\big(H^{-1}(u),H^{-1}(w)\big)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)H(z)+\mu(y,z)H(x)-\mu(x,z)H(y)+\left\llbracket Hx,Hy,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},

which implies that
H
is a crossed homomorphism.
∎

\displaystyle(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}})\Big(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\Big(D(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}()y)H\big(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\big)+\mu(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y),\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z))H\big(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\big)-\mu(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z))H\big(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big)

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y),H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\Big)+\mu(y,z)\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)\Big)-\mu(x,z)\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y),\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},

which implies that the linear map
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}^{-1}\circ H\circ\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
with respect to the action
(\rho,\mu)
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
5.5
:
For any Nijenhuis element
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathsf{Nij}(H)
, we define

\mathfrak{H}:=\delta(\mathfrak{X}).

By Definition
5.4
, for any
t
,
H_{t}=H+t\mathfrak{H}
satisfies that

\displaystyle H\circ\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big)\circ H_{t},

\displaystyle~\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big)\circ\rho(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})(y)\Big)\circ\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big),

\displaystyle~\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big)\circ\mu(y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})(y),{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})(z)\Big)\circ\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big),\quad\forall y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

For
t
sufficiently small, we see that
{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})
and
{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})
are Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphisms. Thus, we have

H_{t}=\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big)^{-1}\circ H\circ\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})\Big).

By Lemma
5.6
, we deduce that
H_{t}
is a crossed homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{h}
for
t
sufficiently small. Thus
\mathfrak{H}=\delta(\mathfrak{X})
generates a linear deformations of
H
. It is easy to see that this deformation is trivial. This completes the proof.∎

5.2.
Formal deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we study formal deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Let
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
be a ring of power series of one variable
t
. If
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, then there is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure over the ring
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
on
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]]
given by

\displaystyle\Big[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}t^{j}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j=s}\Big[x_{i},y_{j}\Big]t^{s},

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}t^{j},\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z_{k}t^{k}\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j+k=s}\left\llbracket x_{i},y_{j},z_{k}\right\rrbracket t^{s},\quad\forall x_{i},y_{j},z_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}.

For any action
(\rho,\mu)
of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
on another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
, there is a natural action of the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
on the
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{h}[[t]]
given by

\displaystyle\rho\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}v_{k}t^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+k=s}\rho(x_{i})v_{k}t^{s},

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}t^{i},\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}y_{j}t^{j}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}v_{k}t^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i+j+k=s}\mu(x_{i},x_{j})v_{k}t^{s},\quad\forall x_{i},y_{j}\in\mathfrak{g},~v_{k}\in V.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to anther Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Consider the power series

(32)

\displaystyle H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i},\quad\mathfrak{H}_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}),

that is,
H_{t}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})[[t]]=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}[[t]])
.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to anther Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Suppose that
H_{t}
is given by (
32
) with
\mathfrak{H}_{0}=H
, and
H_{t}
also satisfies

(33)

\displaystyle H_{t}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)(H_{t}y)-\rho(y)(H_{t}x)+[H_{t}x,H_{t}y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(34)

\displaystyle H_{t}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)(H_{t}z)+\mu(y,z)(H_{t}x)-\mu(x,z)(H_{t}y)+\left\llbracket H_{t}x,H_{t}y,H_{t}z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
. We say that
H_{t}
is a
formal deformation
of
H
.

Substituting the Eq. (
32
) into Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{s}~(\forall s\geqslant 0)
, we have for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=s,\atop i,j\geqslant 0}\Big(\rho(x)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}y)-\rho(y)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}x)+[\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\mathfrak{H}_{s}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)t^{s}=0,

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=s,\atop i,j,k\geqslant 0}\Big(D(x,y)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}z)+\mu(y,z)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}x)-\mu(x,z)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}y)

\displaystyle~~~~~\quad\quad+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y,\mathfrak{H}_{k}z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}-\mathfrak{H}_{s}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)t^{s}=0.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
. If
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
is a formal deformation of
H
, then
\delta^{H}\mathfrak{H}_{1}=0
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{H}_{1}\in\mathfrak{C}^{1}_{H}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a
1
-cocycle of
H
.

Proof.

When
s=1
, Eqs. (
35
) and (
36
) are equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{1}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)(\mathfrak{H}_{1}y)-\rho(y)(\mathfrak{H}_{1}x)+[Hx,\mathfrak{H}_{1}y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\mathfrak{H}_{1}x,Hy]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{1}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)(\mathfrak{H}_{1}z)+\mu(y,z)(\mathfrak{H}_{1}x)-\mu(x,z)(\mathfrak{H}_{1}y)

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{H}_{1}x,Hy,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}+\left\llbracket Hx,\mathfrak{H}_{1}y,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}+\left\llbracket Hx,Hy,\mathfrak{H}_{1}z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

which implies that
\delta^{H}(\mathfrak{H}_{1})=0
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{H}_{1}
is a
1
-cocycle of
\delta^{H}
.
∎

Definition 5.9
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
. The
1
-cocycle
\mathfrak{H}_{1}
is called the
infinitesimal
of the formal deformation
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
of
H.

In the sequel, let us give the notion of equivalent formal deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Two formal deformations
\bar{H}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{\mathfrak{H}_{i}}t^{i}
and
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
, where
\bar{\mathfrak{H}_{0}}=\mathfrak{H}_{0}=H
are said to be
equivalent
if there exist
\mathfrak{X}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},~\phi_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\varphi_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}),~i\geqslant 2,
such that for

(37)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}={{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\phi_{i}t^{i},~\quad\varphi_{t}={{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\varphi_{i}t^{i},

the following conditions are satisfied:

(38)

\displaystyle[\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{t}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\left\llbracket\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{t}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},

(39)

\displaystyle[\varphi_{t}(u),\varphi_{t}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\varphi_{t}[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\left\llbracket\varphi_{t}(u),\varphi_{t}(v),\varphi_{t}(w)\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}=\varphi_{t}\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h},

(40)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}\rho(x)u=\rho(\phi_{t}(x))(\varphi_{t}(u)),\quad\varphi_{t}\mu(x,y)u=\mu(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))(\varphi_{t}(u)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u\in\mathfrak{h},

and

(41)

\displaystyle H_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}=\phi_{t}\circ\bar{H_{t}}

as
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-module maps.

The following theorem is the second key conclusion in this section.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
. If two formal deformations
\bar{H}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{\mathfrak{H}_{i}}t^{i}
and
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
are equivalent, then their infinitesimals are in the same cohomology classes.

Proof.

Let
(\phi_{t},\varphi_{t})
be the maps defined by (
37
), which makes two deformations
\bar{H}_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\bar{\mathfrak{H}_{i}}t^{i}
and
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
equivalent. By (
41
), we have

\bar{\mathfrak{H}_{1}}z=\mathfrak{H}_{1}z+\mu(y,z)(Hx)-\mu(x,z)(Hy)+\left\llbracket Hx,Hy,Hz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{H}_{1}z+\partial(\mathfrak{X})(z),\quad\forall z\in\mathfrak{h},

which implies that
\bar{\mathfrak{H}}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{H}_{1}
are in the same cohomology class.
∎

5.3.
Order
n
deformations of crossed homomorphisms between Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we introduce a special cohomology class associated to an order
n
deformation of a crossed homomorphism, and show that a deformation of order
n
of a crossed homomorphism is extendable if and only if this cohomology class in the second cohomology group is trivial. This is why we call this special cohomology class the obstruction class of a deformation of order
n
being extendable.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
. If
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
with
\mathfrak{H}_{0}=H
,
\mathfrak{H}_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, defines a
\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^{n+1})
-module from
\mathfrak{g}[t]/(t^{n+1})
to the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{h}[t]/(t^{n+1})
satisfying

(42)

\displaystyle H_{t}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)(H_{t}y)-\rho(y)(H_{t}x)+[H_{t}x,H_{t}y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(43)

\displaystyle H_{t}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)(H_{t}z)+\mu(y,z)(H_{t}x)-\mu(x,z)(H_{t}y)+\left\llbracket H_{t}x,H_{t}y,H_{t}z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
. we say that
H_{t}
is an
order
n
deformation
of
H
.

Remark 5.13
.

The left hand sides of Eqs. (
42
) and (
43
) hold in the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t]/(t^{n+1})
and the right hand sides of Eqs. (
42
) and (
43
) make sense since
H_{t}
is a
\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^{n+1})
-module map.

Definition 5.14
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Let
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
H
. If there exists a
1
-cochain
\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
such that
\widetilde{H_{t}}=T_{t}+\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
is an order
n+1
deformation of
H
, then we say that
H_{t}
is
extendable
.

The following theorem is the third key conclusion in this section.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
. Let
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
H
. Then
H_{t}
is extendable if and only if the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{H}]\in\mathcal{H}_{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is trivial, where
\mathsf{Ob}^{H}=(\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{H},\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{H})\in\mathfrak{C}_{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{H}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop 0<i,j<n+1}[\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{H}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1,\atop 0<i.j.k<n+1}\left\llbracket\mathfrak{H}_{i}x_{1},\mathfrak{H}_{j}x_{2},\mathfrak{H}_{k}x_{3}\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{H_{t}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
be an extension of
H_{t}
, then for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(44)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H_{t}}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x)(\widetilde{H_{t}}y)-\rho(y)(\widetilde{H_{t}}x)+[\widetilde{H_{t}}x,\widetilde{H_{t}}y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(45)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H_{t}}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(x,y)(\widetilde{H_{t}}z)+\mu(y,z)(\widetilde{H_{t}}x)-\mu(x,z)(\widetilde{H_{t}}y)+\left\llbracket\widetilde{H_{t}}x,\widetilde{H_{t}}y,\widetilde{H_{t}}z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Expanding the Eq.(
44
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
yields that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\rho(x)(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}y)-\rho(y)(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}x)+\sum_{i+j=n+1}[\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y]_{\mathfrak{h}},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\rho(x)(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}y)-\rho(y)(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}x)+[\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}x,Hy]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[x,\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}y]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\sum_{i+j=n+1\atop 0<i,j<n+1}[\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0.

i.e.,

(46)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{H}+\delta_{\rm I}^{T}(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1})=0.

Similarly, expanding the Eq.(
45
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
yields that

(47)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{H}+\delta_{\rm II}^{T}(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1})=0.

From (
46
) and (
47
), we get

\mathsf{Ob}^{H}=\delta^{H}(-\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}).

Thus, the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{H}]
is trivial.

Conversely, suppose that the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{H}]
is trivial, then there exists
\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}\in\mathfrak{C}_{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, such that
\mathsf{Ob}^{H}=-\delta^{H}(\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}).
Set
\widetilde{H_{t}}=H_{t}+\mathfrak{H}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
. Then for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant n+1
, and for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\widetilde{H_{t}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=s}\Big(\mathfrak{H}_{s}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}-\big(\rho(x)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}y)-\rho(y)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}x)+[\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y]_{\mathfrak{h}}\big)\Big)=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=s}\Big(\mathfrak{H}_{s}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-\big(D(x,y)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}z)+\mu(y,z)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}x)-\mu(x,z)(\mathfrak{H}_{s}y)+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{H}_{i}x,\mathfrak{H}_{j}y,\mathfrak{H}_{k}z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\big)\Big)=0,

which implies that
\widetilde{H_{t}}
is an order
n+1
deformation of
H
. Hence it is an extension of
H_{t}
.
∎

Definition 5.16
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
with respect to an action
(\mathfrak{h};\rho,\mu)
, and
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{H}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
H
. Then the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{H}]\in\mathcal{H}_{H}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
defined in Theorem
5.15
is called the
obstruction class
of
H_{t}
being extendable.

Corollary 5.17
.

Let
H:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
be a crossed homomorphism from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})

, then every
1
-cocycle in

.

Global existence and multiplicity of solutions for logarithmic Schrödinger equations on graphs
Mengqiu Shao

Abstract
We consider the following logarithmic Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + h ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = u ​ log ⁡ u 2 -\Delta u+h(x)u=u\log u^{2} on a locally finite graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , where Δ \Delta is a discrete Laplacian operator on the graph, h h is the potential function. Different from the classical methods in Euclidean space, we obtain the existence of global solutions to the equation by using the variational method from local to global, which is inspired by the works of Lin and Yang in [ 17 ] . In addition, when the potential function h h is sign-changing, we prove that the equation admits infinitely many solutions with high energy by using the symmetric mountain pass theorem. We extend the classical results in Euclidean space to discrete graphs.

Introduction

In recent years, the partial differential equation on graphs has become a hot topic in the field of discrete geometry and nonlinear analysis. For example, Lin and Wu
[
16
]
studied the existence and nonexistence of global solutions for a semilinear heat equation on graphs. Hua, Keller, Schwarz and Wirth
[
12
]
investigated the eigenvalue growth of infinite graphs with
discrete spectrum. Cushing, Kamtue, Liu and Peyerimhoff
[
5
]
reformulated the Bakry-Émery curvature on a weighted graph. Moreover, the existence and asymptotic behavior of solutions to elliptic equations have been widely studied on graphs, see
[
14
,
15
]
for more details. In particular, Lin and Yang
[
17
]
considered various equations on locally finite graphs and established a variational method from local to global on graphs. More recently, Chang, Wang and Yan
[
7
]
first investigated the existence of ground state solutions of a Schrödinger equation with logarithmic nonlinearity on locally finite graphs. Later, Chang et al.
[
8
]
considered the existence of least energy sign-changing solutions of a logarithmic Schrödinger equation on locally finite graphs. The above works arouse our great research interest. In this paper, we are focus on the following logarithmic Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+h(x)u=u\log u^{2}

(1)

on a locally finite graph
G=(V,E)
, where
\Delta
is the discrete Laplacian on graphs and
h
is the potential function satisfying certain conditions. On the Euclidean space, equation (
1
) is closely related to the time-dependent logarithmic Schrödinger equation

i\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\Delta u+h(x)u-u\log u^{2}=0\ \ \hbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

which has important applications in quantum mechanics, quantum optics, nuclear physics, transport and diffusion
phenomena, open quantum systems, effective quantum gravity, theory of superfluidity, and Bose-Einstein condensation (see
[
4
,
6
,
23
,
25
]
for more details).

When
V=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the equation (
1
) is formally associated with the energy functional
J:W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(h(x)+1)u^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dx.

However, in general, this functional fails to be finite and
C^{1}
smooth on
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
because there exists
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\log u^{2}dx=-\infty
. Thus the classical critical point theory cannot be applied for
J
. To overcome this obstacle, several approaches have been developed on the Euclidean space. For instance, Cazenave
[
6
]
worked in an Orlicz space endowed with Luxemburg type norm in order to make the associated functional well defined and
C^{1}
smooth. By applying non-smooth critical point theory for lower semi-continuous functionals, Squassina and Szulkin
[
20
]
proved the existence of multiple solutions to a logarithmic Schrödinger equation with periodic potential. By using the penalization technique, Tanaka and Zhang
[
21
]
obtained infinitely many multi-bump geometrically distinct solutions for a logarithmic Schrödinger equation. For a more non-smooth variational framework to the logarithmic Schrödinger equation, we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
,
11
,
19
]
.

It is worth pointing out that the general form of the equation (
1
) is the following Schrödinger equation

-\Delta u+h(x)u=f(x,u),\ \ x\in V.

(2)

On a lattice graphs, when the potential function
h
is periodic or bounded, Hua and Xu
[
13
]
proved the existence of ground state solutions for the equation (
2
) by using the Nehari manifold method.
When
h(x)=\lambda a(x)+1
and
f(x,u)=|u|^{p-1}u
,
p\geq 2
, Zhang and Zhao
[
24
]
proved the existence and convergence of ground state solutions to the equation (
2
) on locally finite graphs.
Under the following Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz (or (A-R) for short) condition

(f_{1})

there exists a constant
\alpha>2
such that for all
x\in V
and
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,

0<\alpha F(x,s)=\alpha\int^{s}_{0}f(x,t)dt\leq sf(x,s)

and some growth conditions, Grigor’yan, Lin and Yang
[
10
]
obtained that the equation (
2
) admits a positive solution on graphs, where the (A-R) condition
(f_{1})
plays an important role in proving the boundedness of
(PS)
sequence.

However, the logarithmic nonlinearity
u\log u^{2}
in the equation (
1
) not satisfies the (A-R) condition, which brings some difficulties when we use the variational method to study such equations on graphs. On the other hand, similarly to the case in Euclidean space, the corresponding energy functional of the equation (
1
) is not well defined on
W^{1,2}(V)
. Particularly, Chang, Wang and Yang
[
7
]
gave the following example

u(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(|x|\log|x|)^{-1},&|x|\geq 3,\\[6.45831pt]
0,&|x|\leq 2\end{array}\right.

and verified
u\in W^{1,2}(V)
but
\int_{V}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu=-\infty
, where
x_{0}\in V
is some fixed point and
|x|:=\rho(x,x_{0})
denotes the distance between
x
and
x_{0}
on graphs. To deal with this limitation, they restricted
u^{2}\log u^{2}\in L^{1}(V)
and applied the direction derivative and the Nehari manifold method to obtain the existence of ground state solutions of the equation (
1
) in
[
7
]
.

Inspired by the above works, this paper is devoted to study the existence and multiplicity of solutions to the logarithmic Schrödinger equation (
1
) on the locally finite graph
G=(V,E)
. Specifically, our first aim is to obtain the existence of global solutions to the equation (
1
) by using the variational method from local to global, which is different from the classical methods in Euclidean space and the analysis techniques in
[
7
]
. Roughly speaking, we first get sequences of local solutions
u_{k}
for equation (
1
) on
W^{1,2}(B_{k})
and derive uniform estimates for those local solution sequences. Then we obtain the global solutions by extracting convergent sequence of local solutions. We have to point out that one of the difficulties is to prove the nontrivial property of the global solutions. On the other hand, under the assumption that the potential function
h
allows changing sign, we also prove that the equation (
1
) admits infinitely many solutions with high energy by using the symmetric mountain pass theorem. To the best of our knowledge, there seem no results concerned with infinitely many solutions to such equations on graphs.

To describe our main results in details, we first give some notations and assumptions.
Throughout this paper, we always assume that
G=(V,E)
is a connected locally finite graph and its measure
\mu:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a finite positive function satisfying

\mu(x)\geq\mu_{min}>0\ \ \hbox{for\ \ all}\ \ x\in V.

(3)

For an edge
xy\in E
, the weight
\omega_{xy}
is positive and symmetric, namely

\omega_{xy}=\omega_{yx}>0.

(4)

The distance
\rho(x,y)
of two vertices
x,y\in V
is defined by the minimal number
of edges which connect these two vertices. For any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
x\in V
, the Laplacian of
u
at
x
is defined by

\Delta u(x):=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\underset{y\sim x}{\sum}\omega_{xy}(u(y)-u(x)).

For any fixed
x\in V
, the gradient of
u
at
x
is given by

\nabla u(x)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{xy_{1}}}{2\mu(x)}}(u(y_{1})-u(x)),\cdots,\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{xy_{\ell_{x}}}}{2\mu(x)}}(u(y_{\ell_{x}})-u(x))\right),

where
\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{\ell_{x}}\}
is the set of all neighbors of
x
for some positive integer
\ell_{x}
. Then
\nabla u(x)
can be seen as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell_{x}}
.

\nabla u\nabla v
at
x\in V
is denoted by

\nabla u\nabla v(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\underset{y\sim x}{\sum}\omega_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

The length of the gradient for
u
is denoted by

|\nabla u(x)|=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\underset{y\sim x}{\sum}\omega_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, an integral of
u
over
V
is defined by

\int_{V}ud\mu=\underset{x\in V}{\sum}\mu(x)u(x).

For any
1\leq s<\infty
, we denote by

L^{s}(V):=\left\{u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int_{V}|u|^{s}d\mu<\infty\right\}

the set of integrable functions on
V
with the respect to the measure
\mu
. For
s=\infty
, let

L^{\infty}(V):=\left\{u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\underset{x\in V}{\sup}|u(x)|<\infty\right\}.

Usually, we use
\|\cdot\|_{s}
to denote the
L^{s}(V)
norm. Define

W^{1,2}(V):=\left\{u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int_{V}(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2})d\mu<+\infty\right\}

with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}:=\left(\int_{V}(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2})d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Clearly,
W^{1,2}(V)
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

(u,v)_{W^{1,2}(V)}=\int_{V}(\nabla u\nabla v+uv)d\mu,\ \ \forall u,v\in W^{1,2}(V).

Let
h(x)\geq h_{0}>0
for all
x\in V
, we define a subspace
of
W^{1,2}(V)
, which is also a Hilbert space, namely

\mathcal{H}:=\{u\in W^{1,2}(V):\int_{V}h(x)u^{2}d\mu<+\infty\}

(5)

with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\left(\int_{V}(|\nabla u|^{2}+h(x)u^{2})d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Our first result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected locally finite graph satisfying (
3
) and (
4
). Assume that
h
satisfies the following conditions:

(
h_{1}
)

there exists a constant
h_{0}>0
such that
h(x)\geq h_{0}
for all
x\in V
;

(
h_{2}
)

h^{-1}\in L^{1}(V)
.

Then the equation (
1
) possesses a nontrivial solution.

Remark 1.2
.

The assumptions
(h_{1})
and
(h_{2})
were originally introduced on graphs by Grigor’yan, Lin and Yang in
[
10
]
which is used to guarantee the compactness of the embedding
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow L^{s}(V)
,
s\in[1,+\infty]
. Especially, this embedding theorem plays an important role in proving the nontrivial property of the global solutions.

Moreover, we consider the existence of infinitely many solutions to the equation (
1
) when
h
satisfies the following assumptions.

(
h^{\prime}_{1}
)

\underset{x\in V}{\inf}h(x)\geq h_{1}
for some constant
h_{1}\in(-1,0)
.

(
h^{\prime}_{2}
)

There exists
\alpha>0
such that
h^{-1}\in L^{1}(V\setminus V_{\alpha})
, where
V_{\alpha}=\{x\in V:h(x)\leq\alpha\}
and the volume of
V_{\alpha}
is finite, i.e.

Vol(V_{\alpha})=\underset{x\in V_{\alpha}}{\sum}\mu(x)<\infty.

Note that the equation (
1
) is formally associated with the energy functional
\mathcal{J}:W^{1,2}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

\mathcal{J}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\frac{1}{2}\int_{V}(h(x)+1)u^{2}d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\int_{V}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu.

(6)

To study the multiplicity of solutions to the equation (
1
), we consider a function space

\mathcal{W}:=\{u\in W^{1,2}(V):\int_{V}(h(x)+1)u^{2}d\mu<+\infty\}

(7)

with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}}:=\left(\int_{V}[|\nabla u|^{2}+(h(x)+1)u^{2}]d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
h
satisfies
(h^{\prime}_{1})
and
(h^{\prime}_{2})
.
It follows from Lemma
3.3
in Section 3 that
\mathcal{J}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{W},\mathbb{R})
.

Our second result is as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected locally finite graph satisfying (
3
) and (
4
). Suppose that
(h^{\prime}_{1})
and
(h^{\prime}_{2})
hold. Then the equation (
1
) admits a sequence of solutions
\{u_{n}\}
with
\mathcal{J}(u_{n})\rightarrow+\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 1.4
.

It should be pointed out that the hypotheses
(h^{\prime}_{1})
and
(h^{\prime}_{2})
were proposed by Chang, Wang and Yang in
[
7
]
, where they got the existence of ground state solutions to the equation (
1
) on graphs. Different from their results in
[
7
]
, by using the symmetric mountain pass theorem, we obtain infinitely many solutions with high energy of the equation (
1
). On the other hand, we extend the classical results in Euclidean space to discrete graphs.

This article is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, we prove the existence of the nontrivial solutions of equation (
1
) from local to global. In Sect. 3, we prove Theorem
1.3
by using the symmetric mountain pass theorem.

2
Existence of global solutions

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
by using variational method from local to global. Firstly, we present some preliminaries and basic functional settings. Let
\Omega
be a connected finite subset of
V
.
The boundary of
\Omega
in
V
is defined by

\partial\Omega:=\{y\notin\Omega:\exists x\in\Omega\ \ \hbox{such that}\ \ xy\in E\}.

(8)

The space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega):=W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
endowed with the inner product

(u,v)_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\int_{\Omega}h(x)u^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is a Hilbert space, where
\overline{\Omega}=\Omega\cup\partial\Omega
.
Fixed some point
O\in V
. For any
x\in V
,
\rho(x)=\rho(x,O)
denotes the distance between
x
and
O
. For any integer
k\geq 1
, we denote a ball centered at
O
with radius
k
by

B_{k}=B_{k}(O)=\{x\in V:\rho(x)<k\}.

The boundary of
B_{k}
written as

\partial B_{k}=\{x\in V:\rho(x)=k\}.

It is easy to see that if
\Omega=B_{k}
, then
\partial\Omega
defined in (
8
) is equal to
\partial B_{k}
.

Now we consider the following locally equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u+h(x)u=u\log u^{2},&\text{in}\ \ B_{k},\\
u=0,&\text{on}\ \ \partial B_{k},\end{cases}

(9)

where
h
satisfies
(h_{1})
and
(h_{2})
.
It is suitable to study (
9
) in the space
\mathcal{H}(B_{k})
under the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}=\left(\int_{\overline{B}_{k}}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\int_{B_{k}}h(x)u^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The functional related to (
9
) is

\mathcal{I}_{k}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{k}}u^{2}d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{k}}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu.

Note that for any
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

|t^{2}\log t^{2}|\leq C_{\varepsilon}(|t|^{2-\varepsilon}+|t|^{2+\varepsilon}),\ \ \forall t\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}.

(10)

By (
10
) and a standard argument, we have
\mathcal{I}_{k}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{H}(B_{k}),\mathbb{R})
, and

\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{k}(u),v\rangle=\int_{\overline{B}_{k}}\nabla u\nabla vd\mu+\int_{B_{k}}h(x)uvd\mu-\int_{B_{k}}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu.

The following lemma gives the compactness of the space
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
. In addition, we prove an embedding inequality where the embedding constant is independent of
\Omega
. For brevity, we denote the
L^{q}
norms on
\Omega
by
\|\cdot\|_{q,\Omega}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected locally finite graph satisfying (
3
) and (
4
),
\Omega
be a connected finite subset of
V
. Assume that
(h_{1})
hold. Then
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
L^{q}(\Omega)
for any
q\in[2,+\infty]
. In particular, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
h_{0}
,
\mu_{\min}
and
q
such that for any
u\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{q,\Omega}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}.

(11)

Moreover,
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is pre-compact.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is inspired by Theorem 7 in
[
10
]
. Since
\Omega
is a connected finite subset of
V
,
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is a finite dimensional space. Hence,
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is pre-compact. We are left to prove (
11
). Indeed, for any
u\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
and vertex
x_{0}\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu+\int_{\Omega}h(x)u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}h(x)u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=\underset{x\in\Omega}{\sum}h(x)u(x)^{2}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq h_{0}\mu_{\min}u(x_{0})^{2},

which gives

u(x_{0})\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}\mu_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}.

Then

\|u\|_{\infty,\Omega}\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}\mu_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)},

and thus for any

,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}|u|^{q-2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{h_{0}\mu_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\|u\|^{q-2}_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq(h_{0})^{\frac{-q}{2}}(\mu_{\min})^{\frac{2-q}{2}}\|u\|^{q-2}_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}h(x)u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq(h_{0})^{\frac{-q}{2}}(\mu_{\min})^{\frac{2-q}{2}}\|u\|^{q}_{\mathcal{H}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, for any
2\leq q\leq+\infty
,

\|u\|_{q,\Omega}\leq C\|u\|_{H(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
h_{0}
,
\mu_{\min}
and
q
.
∎

Next, we verify that the functional
\mathcal{I}_{k}
satisfies the mountain pass geometry
in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exist constants
\delta>0
and
r>0
satisfying

\mathcal{I}_{k}(u)\geq\delta
for all
u
with
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}=r
.

Proof.

It follows from (
10
) and Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{k}}u^{2}d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{k}}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{x\in B_{k}:u(x)\geq 1\}}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{k}}C_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2+\varepsilon}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}-\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{2}\|u\|^{2+\varepsilon}_{2+\varepsilon,B_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}-\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{2}C\|u\|^{2+\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}\left(1-C_{\varepsilon}C\|u\|^{\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{H}(B_{k})}\right),

where

depends on

,

and

.
Setting

for all

with

∎

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists some

\mathcal{I}_{k}(u_{n})\rightarrow c\ \ \hbox{and}\ \ \mathcal{I}_{k}^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0\ \ \hbox{as}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty.

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{x\in V:u\geq 1\}}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}-\frac{1}{2}C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|^{2+\varepsilon}_{2+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}-\frac{1}{2}C_{\varepsilon}C_{4}\|u\|^{2+\varepsilon}_{\mathcal{W}}.

Taking
\varrho=\left(\frac{1}{2C_{\varepsilon}C_{4}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
, then we have

\mathcal{J}(u)|_{\partial B_{\varrho}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\varrho^{2}:=\sigma>0.

(ii)
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
be a finite dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{W}
. Suppose
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\setminus\{0\}
and setting
\phi:=\frac{u}{\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}}}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{V}u^{2}\log u^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}}{2}\left(\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}-\int_{V}\phi^{2}\log\phi^{2}d\mu-\int_{V}\phi^{2}\log\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}}{2}\left(1+C_{\varepsilon}\|\phi\|^{2-\varepsilon}_{2-\varepsilon}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\phi\|^{2+\varepsilon}_{2+\varepsilon}-\|\phi\|^{2}_{2}\log\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{W}}\right).

Therefore, there exists an
R=R(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}})
such that
\mathcal{J}\leq 0
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}\setminus B_{R(\widetilde{\mathcal{W}})}
.

From the above argument, we know that the functional
\mathcal{J}
satisfies all the conditions of Lemma
3.1
. Therefore, the equation (
1
) has a sequence of solutions
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{W}
such that
\mathcal{J}(u_{n})\rightarrow+\infty

1.
Introduction

Intersection local times (including self-intersection local time and mutual intersection local time) of stochastic processes are a fundamental concept in stochastic
analysis, which also play an important role in the study of the quantum field theory, random polymer models, stochastic partial differential
equations and the models of polymers. Our goal in this note is to
establish the small deviation for the mutual intersection local time

\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr,\hskip 14.45377ptt>0

driven by two independent 1-dimensional Brownian motions
B_{s}
and

\tilde{B}_{s}
with
B_{0}=\tilde{B}_{0}=0
, where
\delta_{0}(\cdot)
is the Dirac delta
function on
\mathbb{R}
(see, e.g.,
[
1
, Chapter 2]
for the detailed construction of intersection local times).
The mutual intersection local time measures the intensity of path intersection between two
independent Brownian trajectories.
The following is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists constants
0<c\leq C<\infty
such that for small
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.1)

c\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\leq\varepsilon\bigg\}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}.

The small deviation (or, small ball probability) is an interesting subject
for stochastic models. An interested reader is referred to
[
6
]

for a survey of general development in this direction.
The goal is to find the decay rate of the probability

P\{\|G\|\leq\varepsilon\}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, where
G(\cdot)
is
a stochastic process or a random field that is properly embedded
in a Banach space endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|
. In our setting,

\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}L(1,x)\tilde{L}(1,x)dx\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Delta}}\|G\|,

where
L(t,x)
and
\tilde{L}(t,x)
(
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
) are local times of
B
an
\tilde{B}
, respectively;
and the random field
G(x)=L(1,x)\tilde{L}(1,x)
(
x\in\mathbb{R}
) is embedded in the space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Due to technical limitations, the random fields
G(\cdot)

in the literature of small ball probability are predominantly
Gaussian. Theorem
1.1
represents a rare setting
for the non-Gaussian situation. Another similar example of the non-Gaussian is the small deviation for
the self-intersection local time (see (
1.3
) below).

In addition, this work is also motivated by a practical matter arising from the area of stochastic
partial differential equations:
The integrability

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\bigg]^{-p}<\infty\text{ for some }p>0,

plays an important rule when studying the regularity of the probability distribution for the solution of 1-dimensional parabolic Anderson equation (driven by a
space-white Gaussian noise) in
[
4

p

1.1

Corollary 1.2

The negative-moment-integrability (

if and only if

The large and small deviations are two research subjects concerning the probabilities of the rare events
representing two extreme behaviors
of the stochastic system: the random variable takes
unusually large values and the random variable takes unusually small
values. For the purpose of comparison in terms of large and small deviations, we introduce the self-intersection
local time

\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-B_{r})dsdr,\hskip 14.45377ptt>0.

By Theorem 1.1 in
[
2
]
(
m=1

p=2

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-B_{r})dsdr\geq\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg\}=-{3\over 2};

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\geq\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg\}=-3.

It is noticeable that the large deviations for two different types
of intersections take
the forms close to each other. The story behind is the following deterministic relation:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}L(1,x)\tilde{L}(1,x)dx\leq{1\over 2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}L^{2}(1,x)dx+{1\over 2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tilde{L}^{2}(1,x)dx

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-B_{r})dsdr+{1\over 2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(\tilde{B}_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr

where
L(t,x)
and
\tilde{L}(t,x)
are, respectively, the local times of

B_{t}
and
\tilde{B}_{t}
.
In the game of large deviations, the
Brownian trajectories that make mutual intersection large are those
that make
L(1,\cdot)
and
\tilde{L}(1,\cdot)
close to each
other so that “
\leq
”
is replaced by “
\approx
” in the above deterministic relation.

The small ball probability for the self-intersection local time
(

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{2}\log P\left\{\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-B_{r})dsdr\leq\varepsilon\right\}=-c

with constant

. A striking difference
between (

lies in the disparity between exponential decay and power decay.
The typical paths that the Brownian motion takes for maximizing
self-avoiding are the ones that are close to straight lines, i.e.,
B_{t}\approx c\epsilon^{-1}t,t\in[0,1].
For the mutual intersection local time,
the small deviation means a totally different game: Two independent Brownian paths
tend to avoid meeting each other right after they set out from the same starting point.

In the following we outline
the idea in our proof: By the Brownian scaling property and the relation

\delta_{0}(\lambda x)=\lambda^{-1}\delta_{0}(x)
for any
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}t^{3/2}\int_{0}^{1}\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr,\hskip 14.45377pt\forall t>0,

where “

(1.5)

\displaystyle c{1\over t}\leq P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\leq a\bigg\}\leq C{1\over t}

for large

In the proof of the lower bound, we separate the Brownian paths

into two disjoint and distanced

Consequently, the only unavoidable intersections are the ones happening
on the time square
[0,1]^{2}

In other words, the strategy lies in the comparison
between the probability of “low intersection” and the probability of “no intersection”.
Indeed, the steps taken in (

\displaystyle P\{\hbox{low intersection on $[0,t]^{2}$}\}\geq cP\{\hbox{no intersection on $[1,t]^{2}$}\}.

Since the probability on the right-hand side is not hard to compute
(only for 1-dimensional Brownian motions, of course),
the proof of the lower bound (see Section
2

3

As for the upper bound, the real challenge is to fill the gap between “low intersection” and “no intersection” suggested by (

Lower bound

In this section, all we need is to prove the lower bound in (

(2.1)

P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\leq 1\bigg\}\geq\frac{c}{t}

\displaystyle P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\leq 1\bigg\}

\{B_{1}\geq 2\}

\displaystyle P_{B_{1}}\bigg\{\min_{0\leq s\leq t-1}B_{s}>1\bigg\}\geq P\bigg\{1+\min_{0\leq s\leq t-1}B_{s}>0\bigg\}

\displaystyle=P\bigg\{|B_{t-1}|\leq 1\bigg\}\sim{1\over\sqrt{2\pi t}}\hskip 14.45377pt(t\to\infty),

and similarly, using (

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{t}\,P\bigg\{\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{0}(B_{s}-\tilde{B}_{r})dsdr\leq 1,\hskip 3.61371pt\min_{0\leq s\leq 1}B_{s}\geq-1,\hskip 3.61371ptB_{1}\geq 2,\hskip 3.61371pt\max_{0\leq s\leq 1}\tilde{B}_{s}\leq 1,\hskip 3.61371pt\tilde{B}_{1}\leq-2\bigg\},

Lemma 2.1
.

For any

\displaystyle P\{x+B_{s}>0;\hskip 3.61371pt\forall s\leq t\}=P\{|B_{t}|\leq x\},

\displaystyle P\{-x+B_{s}<0;\hskip 3.61371pt\forall s\leq t\}=P\{|B_{t}|\leq x\}.

Proof.

Denote

where the last equality follows from the reflection principle

. This yields (
2.5

\{l\}\times[1,l]
,
or on both. An intersection
\{S_{j}=\tilde{S}_{k}\}
is called an early intersection, if there is no
(j_{1},k_{1})\not=(j,k)

such that
j_{1}\leq j
,
k_{1}\leq k
, and
S_{j_{1}}=\tilde{S}_{k_{1}}
. In general, there may be multiple early intersections
due to the multi-dimension of time.
On
\{\sigma=l\}
, early intersections happen on
[1,l]\times\{l\}
or on
\{l\}\times[1,l]
, or on both, i.e.,

\displaystyle\{\sigma=l\}=

\displaystyle\left\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $[1,l]\times\{l\}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\bigcup\left\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $\{l\}\times[1,l]$}\right\}.

Therefore, for

,

(3.13)

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\{\sigma=l,\hskip 3.61371ptH_{n}\leq a\}

\displaystyle\leq P_{\mu}\left\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $[1,l]\times\{l\},\hskip 3.61371ptH_{n}\leq a$}\right\}

\displaystyle+P_{\mu}\left\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $\{l\}\times[1,l],\hskip 3.61371ptH_{n}\leq a$}\right\}

\displaystyle=2P_{\mu}\left\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $[1,l]\times\{l\},\hskip 3.61371ptH_{n}\leq a$}\right\}.

On the event
\big\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $[1,l]\times\{l\}$}\big\}
, there is
a unique early intersection on
[1,l]\times\{l\}

happening at

(3.14)

F_{\rho,l}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Delta}}\Big\{\Lambda\Big(\big([1,\rho]\times[1,l]\big)\setminus(\rho,l)\Big)=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371ptS_{\rho}=\tilde{S}_{l}\Big\},\hskip 14.45377pt\rho=1,\cdots,l.

Thus, we have

\left\{\Lambda([0,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $[1,l]\times\{l\}$}\right\}\subset\bigcup_{\rho=1}^{l}F_{\rho,l}\,.

Here we mention that, when
\rho=l
, the fact that “
S_{l}=\tilde{S}_{l}
” is an early intersection requires, in addition to
“
S_{1}\not=\tilde{S}_{l},\cdots,S_{l-1}\not=\tilde{S}_{l}
”,

” (and therefore
\Lambda\left(\left([1,l]\times[1,l]\right)\setminus(l,l)\right)=\emptyset

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\left\{\Lambda([1,l-1]^{2})=\emptyset,\hskip 3.61371pt\hbox{early intersection happens on $[1,l]\times\{l\}$},\hskip 3.61371ptH_{n}\leq a\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\rho=1}^{l}P_{\mu}\big\{F_{\rho,l}\cap\{H_{n}\leq a\}\big\}=\sum_{\rho=1}^{l}P_{\mu}\big\{F_{\rho,l}\cap\{\xi_{\rho,l}\leq a\}\cap\{H_{n}\leq a\}\big\}.

\displaystyle H_{n}\geq\sum_{j=\rho+2}^{n}\sum_{k=l+2}^{n}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{S_{j}=\tilde{S}_{k}\}}\xi_{j,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-\rho-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-l-1}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{(S_{\rho+1+j}-S_{\rho+1})+(S_{\rho+1}-S_{\rho})=(\tilde{S}_{l+1+k}-\tilde{S}_{l+1})+(\tilde{S}_{l+1}-\tilde{S}_{l})\}}\xi_{\rho+1+j,l+1+k}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j,k=1}^{n-l-1}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{(S_{\rho+1+j}-S_{\rho+1})+(S_{\rho+1}-S_{\rho})=(\tilde{S}_{l+1+k}-\tilde{S}_{l+1})+(\tilde{S}_{l+1}-\tilde{S}_{l})\}}\xi_{\rho+1+j,l+1+k}

Lemma 3.1
.

There is a constant

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\{Q_{n}=0\}\leq{C\over n},\hskip 14.45377ptn=1,2,\cdots,

Proof.

Under
P_{\mu}
,
S_{0}=\pm 1
and
\tilde{S}_{0}=\pm 1
. When
S_{0}\not=\tilde{S}_{0}

\{Q_{n}=0\}\subset\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-S_{0},\hskip 3.61371pt\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\tilde{S}_{k}\leq-\tilde{S}_{0}\Big\}\bigcup\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\tilde{S}_{k}\geq-\tilde{S}_{0},\hskip 3.61371pt\max_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\leq-S_{0}\Big\}.

When
S_{0}=\tilde{S}_{0}
,

\{Q_{n}=0\}\subset\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq S_{0},\hskip 3.61371pt\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\tilde{S}_{k}\leq\tilde{S}_{0}\Big\}\bigcup\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\tilde{S}_{k}\geq\tilde{S}_{0},\hskip 3.61371pt\max_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\leq S_{0}\Big\}.

By sub-additivity, symmetry and independence, we get

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\{Q_{n}=0\}

\displaystyle\leq P_{1}\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-1\Big\}P_{-1}\Big\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\leq 1\Big\}+P_{1}\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq 1\Big\}P_{1}\Big\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\leq 1\Big\}

\displaystyle=\bigg(P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-2\Big\}\bigg)^{2}+\bigg(P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq 0\Big\}\bigg)^{2}\leq 2\bigg(P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-2\Big\}\bigg)^{2}.

As for the hitting time, we have

\displaystyle P_{\mu}\{T_{z}\geq n\}={1\over 2}P\{T_{z-1}\geq n\}+{1\over 2}P\{T_{z+1}\geq n\}

\displaystyle={1\over 2}P\{T_{-|z-1|}\geq n\}+{1\over 2}P\{T_{-|z+1|}\geq n\}

\displaystyle\leq{1\over 2}P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-|z-1|\Big\}+{1\over 2}P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-|z+1|\Big\},

where the second equality follows from symmetry.

Therefore, both (
3.28
) and (
3.29

P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-z\Big\}\leq{C_{z}\over\sqrt{n}}\,.

The bound like this looks classic and must exist somewhere in literature. For the

Since the probability on the left is non-decreasing in
z
, we may assume that

. By our construction of the simple
random walk,
|B_{t}-S_{k}|\leq 1
for
\tau_{k}\leq t\leq\tau_{k+1}

\begin{aligned} &P\Big\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}S_{k}\geq-z\Big\}\leq P\Big\{\min_{1\leq s\leq\tau_{n}}B_{s}\geq-(z-1)\Big\}\\
&\leq P\Big\{\min_{1\leq s\leq(1-\delta)n\mathbb{E}\tau_{1}}B_{s}\geq-(z-1)\Big\}+P\{\tau_{n}\leq(1-\delta)n\mathbb{E}\tau_{1}\}\\

2.1

3.3

\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}\sum_{l=1}^{n-2}\bigg(\sum_{\rho=1}^{n-1-l}P_{\mu}\{F_{\rho,n-l-1}\}\bigg)P_{\mu}\{H_{l}\leq a\}\leq C+5\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{H_{n}\leq a\}.

Plugging this into (
3.31
), we have

\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{H_{n}\leq a\}\leq C+\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{Q_{n}=0\}+20P\{\xi_{1,1}\leq a\}\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{H_{n}\leq a\},

or equivalently,

(3.33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{H_{n}\leq a\}\leq\Big(1-20P\{\xi_{1,1}\leq a\}\Big)^{-1}\Big\{C+\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{Q_{n}=0\}\Big\}\,.

In view of (
3.28
) in Lemma
3.1
, the sequence

\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{Q_{n}=0\}\leq\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}\frac{C}{n}\leq C\left(\log 2^{k+1}-\log 2^{k}\right)\leq C

is uniformly bounded in
k
. Therefore,
(
3.33
) yields that there exists a constant
C
independent of
k
such that

\sum_{n=2^{k}+1}^{2^{k+1}}P_{\mu}\{H_{n}\leq a\}\leq C\hskip 7.22743pt\text{ for all }k=1,2,\dots,

which leads to, noting the monotonicity of
P_{\mu}\{H_{n}\leq a\}
in
n
,

P_{\mu}\{H_{2^{k+1}}\leq a\}\leq C{1\over 2^{k}}\hskip 7.22743pt\text{ for all }k=1,2,\dots

By the monotonicity of the random sequence
\{H_{n}\}
,
this leads to (
3.10
). ∎

Well-posedness and stability for a class of fourth-order nonlinear parabolic equations
Xinye Li Christof Melcher

Abstract
In this paper we examine well-posedness for a class of fourth-order nonlinear parabolic equation ∂ t u + ( − Δ ) 2 ​ u = ∇ ⋅ F ⁡ ( ∇ u ) \partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{2}u=\nabla\cdot F(\nabla u) , where F F satisfies a cubic growth conditions. We establish existence and uniqueness of the solution for small initial data in local BMO spaces. In the cubic case F ⁡ ( ξ ) = ± | ξ | 2 ​ ξ F(\xi)=\pm\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi , we also examine the large time behavior and stability of global solutions for arbitrary and small initial data in VMO \mathrm{VMO} , respectively. Keywords fourth-order parabolic equation, bounded mean oscillation, stability, gradient nonlinearity, epitaxial growth MSC 35K25, 35K55, 35B35

In this section, we review some fundamental properties on the biharmonic heat kernel and local BMO spaces.

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{B(0,1)\times[0,1]}|\nabla\Phi|^{4}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\|\Phi(t)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}(\eta^{2}\Phi)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,1])}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CK_{T}^{2}\|w\|_{X_{T}}.

This finishes the proof of (
19
) which implies (
20
).
∎

Using a fixed-point iteration argument, we conclude existence and uniqueness of small local or global mild solution of (
1
).

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
f
satisfies (
2
). There exists a
\rho>0
with the following property:

(i)

For all
T>0
, if
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}<\rho
, then there exists a unique small mild solution
u
in
X_{T}
of (
1
) to initial data

, which satisfies

\|u\|_{X_{T}}<2C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}.

Moreover, if
u_{0}\in\overline{\mathrm{VMO}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then there exists a terminal time
T_{0}>0
such that there exists a unique small mild solution
u\in X_{T_{0}}
of (

u(0)=u_{0}
.

(ii)

If
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\rho
, then there exists a unique small global mild solution
u\in X
of (
1
) with

.

Proof.

We define

u_{1}=S(t)u_{0},\quad u_{j+1}=u_{1}+G(u_{j})

(25)

and choose
\rho>0
, such that
4C_{1}^{2}C_{2}\rho^{2}<\frac{1}{2}
. For
T\in(0,+\infty]
, if
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}<\rho

\|u_{1}\|_{X_{T}}<\frac{\delta}{2}

(26)

for

Note that if
u_{0}\in\overline{\mathrm{VMO}}
, according to (
18
) in Lemma
5
there exists a

such that (
26

T=T_{0}
.

We prove by induction that

\|u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}<2C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}\quad\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

This is true for
j=1
since Lemma
5
. For the induction step, it follows by (
20
) and our choice of

that

\displaystyle\|u_{j+1}\|_{X_{T}}\leq C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}+C_{2}\|u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}^{3}\leq 2C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}.

Therefore we have, for all

\|u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}<2C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}<\delta.

(

\|G(u_{j+1})-G(u_{j})\|_{X_{T}}\leq C_{2}\delta^{2}\|u_{j+1}-u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}<\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j+1}-u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}

and hence

\|u_{j+1}-u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}=\|G(u_{j})-G(u_{j-1})\|_{X_{T}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{j}-u_{j-1}\|_{X_{T}}\leq\cdots\leq 2^{-j}\|u_{1}-u_{0}\|_{X_{T}}.

For

\displaystyle\|u_{k}-u_{i}\|_{X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}\|u_{j+1}-u_{j}\|_{X_{T}}\leq\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}2^{-j}\|u_{1}-u_{0}\|_{X_{T}}\leq C\delta 2^{-i}.

Therefore

is a Cauchy sequence in

and converges to a mild solution

of (
1
) and

\|u\|_{X_{T}}<2C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}.

For

, we obtain by (

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\|G(u)\|_{X_{t}}\leq C\lim_{t\searrow 0}\|u\|_{X_{t}}^{3}\leq C\lim_{t\searrow 0}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{t^{1/4}}}^{3}=0

and therefore

.
∎

The above argument only guarantees the uniqueness of mild solutions that are sufficiently small in
X_{T}
. Now we study the uniqueness of general mild solutions in

.
A straightforward calculation shows the following result similar to Lemma 7 in

]
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
0<t<t^{\prime}<+\infty
and
\tilde{u}=u(t+\cdot)
, defined on

N_{k,c,t^{\prime}-t}(\tilde{u})\leq\left(\ln\frac{t^{\prime}}{t}\right)^{\frac{k}{4}}N_{k,\infty}(u)\quad\text{and}\quad N_{k,\infty,t^{\prime}-t}(\tilde{u})\leq\left(1-\frac{t}{t^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{k}{4}}N_{k,\infty}(u)

for

.

We apply this lemma to prove uniqueness of mild solutions in
X_{0,T}
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
T>0
. Suppose
u,v\in X_{0,T}
are mild solutions to (
1
) for identical initial conditions
u_{0}\in\mathrm{BMO}
, then
u
and
v
coincide in
X_{0,T}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
be a fixed constant, whose value will be decided later. It follows from Lemma
7
that, there exist
J\in\mathbb{N}
and
J+1
overlapping intervals
I_{j}=(t_{j},t_{j}^{\prime})
covering
(0,T)
, with
t_{0}=0
,
t_{J}^{\prime}=T
and
t_{j}<t_{j-1}^{\prime}
for any

, such that

\lVert\tilde{u}_{j}\rVert_{X_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}},\lVert\tilde{v}_{j}\rVert_{X_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}}\leq\delta\quad\text{for any }0\leq j\leq J

for

\tilde{u}_{j}(t)=u(t_{j}+t),\quad\tilde{v}_{j}(t)=v(t_{j}+t)\quad t\in(0,t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}).

Since

u(t)=S(t-t_{j})u(t_{j})+\int_{0}^{t-t_{j}}S(t-t_{j}-s)\nabla\cdot F(\nabla u(t_{j}+s))\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{for}\quad t\in(t_{j},t_{j}^{\prime}),

we have

\tilde{u}_{j}(t)=S(t)u(t_{j})+G(\tilde{u}_{j})(t)\quad\text{for}\quad t\in(0,t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}).

Set
\tilde{w}_{0}:=\tilde{u}_{0}-\tilde{v}_{0}
. Since
u(t_{0})=v(t_{0})=u_{0}

\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{X_{t_{0}^{\prime}-t_{0}}}=\lVert G(\tilde{u}_{0})-G(\tilde{v}_{0})\rVert_{X_{t_{0}^{\prime}-t_{0}}}\leq C_{2}\delta^{2}\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{X_{t_{0}^{\prime}-t_{0}}}.

Taking small enough

, we deduce

\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{X_{t_{0}^{\prime}-t_{0}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\tilde{w}_{0}\rVert_{X_{t_{0}^{\prime}-t_{0}}},

so that

and

, in particular
u(t_{1})=v(t_{1})
. Repeating this process inductively for

∎

3.3
Higher regularity

If in addition
F\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the solution

obtained in Theorem

satisfies

N_{k,\infty,T}(u)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\,t^{\frac{k}{4}}\lVert\nabla^{k}u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\quad\text{for any}\quad k\geq 1.

The proof follows from an upgrade of the fixed point argument above, cf.
[
7
]

for Navier-Stokes. In the following we outline the main idea and give a sketch of proof.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and

we define the space

of functions

\lVert u\rVert_{X_{T}^{\ell}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}N_{k,\infty,T}(u)+N_{k,c,T}(u)<\infty

It follows by similar arguments as in the proof Lemma

that

N_{k,\infty,T}(S(\cdot)u_{0}),N_{k,c,T}(S(\cdot)u_{0})\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}_{T^{1/4}}}\quad\text{for all}\quad k\leq\ell.

Following the idea in
[
7
]
, one can adapt the argument in the proof of Lemma

to prove that

N_{\ell+1,\infty,T}(G(u)),N_{\ell+1,c,T}(G(u))\leq C\lVert u\rVert_{X_{T}^{\ell}}^{3}\quad\text{for all}\quad\ell\geq 2.

By induction we prove that the sequence

defined in (

where

.

4
Stability of global solutions

4.1
Main idea

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2
, i.e., asymptotics and stability of global solutions in the case of cubic nonlinearities
F(\xi)=\pm\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
. Key is a linearization principle by Auscher, Dubois and Tchamitchian’s in
[
1
]
introduced in the context
of global solutions to the Navier-Stokes equation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to initial data in
\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}
. An essential feature of their method is the cancellation property of the trilinear term specific to Navier-Stokes. The trilinear term
\nabla\cdot F(\nabla u)
in our equation does not possess any similar cancellation property. However, we will prove that, under certain conditions, global solutions of (
1
) to initial data in
\mathrm{VMO}
possess some asymptotic behaviour, which is the key to the stability of the solutions. Depending on the property of energy functional, we distinguish the following two cases:

(a)

For the coercive case
F(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
, we derive an energy-type inequality and use interpolation to deduce the asymptotics of global solutions of (
1
) to initial data in
\mathrm{VMO}
without a size restriction.

(b)

For the non-coercive case
F(\xi)=-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
, though there is no similar energy-type inequality, we use a fixed-point argument to obtain the asymptotic result of global solutions, for initial data small enough in
\mathrm{VMO}
.

We define the trilinear operator

\Psi(f,g,h)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\psi(f(s),g(s),h(s))\,\mathrm{d}s

where

\psi(f,g,h):=\nabla\cdot\left((\nabla f\cdot\nabla g)\nabla h\right)=(\nabla f\cdot\nabla g)\Delta h+\nabla f\cdot\nabla^{2}g\cdot\nabla h+\nabla g\cdot\nabla^{2}f\cdot\nabla h

and we denote

\nabla f\cdot\nabla^{2}g\cdot\nabla h=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k}f\cdot\partial_{i}\partial_{k}g\cdot\partial_{i}h.

Then mild solutions of (
1
) for
F(\xi)=\pm\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
are characterized by

u(t)=S(t)u_{0}\pm\Psi(u,u,u)(t).

The strategy of proof is to show that for any initial data
v_{0}\in\mathrm{BMO}
near
u_{0}
, (
1
) has a solution
v\in X
. This is equivalent to solving the following equation for
w=u-v
to small initial data
w_{0}:=u_{0}-v_{0}

w(t)=S(t)w_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{u,u}w(t)+\mathcal{B}_{u}(w,w)(t)-\Psi(w,w,w)(t)

(27)

in terms of linear and bilinear operators

\mathcal{L}_{a,b}f:=\pm(\Psi(f,a,b)+\Psi(a,b,f)+\Psi(b,f,a))

and

\mathcal{B}_{a}(f,f):=\mathcal{L}_{a,f}f,

respectively, acting on
f\in X
for
a,b\in X
.
Making use of the asymptotic behaviour of
u
, we prove that the operator
I+\mathcal{L}_{u,u}
is invertible on
X
. Then the equation (
27
) is equivalent to

w(t)=(I+\mathcal{L}_{u,u})^{-1}S(t)w_{0}+(I+\mathcal{L}_{u,u})^{-1}\left[\mathcal{B}_{u}(w,w)(t)-\Psi(w,w,w)(t)\right],

which is solvable in
X
for
w_{0}
small enough in
\mathrm{BMO}
according to the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
\mathcal{L}
a linear operator,
\mathcal{B}
a bilinear operator and
\Psi
a trilinear operator, continuous on
X
with bounded operator norm. Suppose
I+\mathcal{L}
is invertible. Then for
z\in X
such that

\lVert(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}z\rVert_{X}<\frac{1}{4(\lVert\mathcal{B}\rVert+\lVert\Psi\rVert)\lVert(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}\rVert},

there is a solution
w\in X
to the equation

w=z-\mathcal{L}w+\mathcal{B}(w,w)-\Psi(w,w,w).

(28)

Furthermore this solution satisfies

\lVert w\rVert_{X}\leq C_{z}\lVert z\rVert_{X},

where
C_{z}>0
is a constant depending on
z
.

Proof.

First we assume
\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert<1
and define a sequence

w_{1}=\tilde{z},\quad\tilde{w}_{n+1}=\tilde{w}_{1}+\mathcal{L}\tilde{w}_{n}+B(\tilde{w}_{n},\tilde{w}_{n})+\Psi(\tilde{w}_{n},\tilde{w}_{n},\tilde{w}_{n}).

It is straightforward to prove that, if
\lVert\tilde{z}\rVert_{X}\leq\frac{(1-\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert)^{2}}{4(\lVert B\rVert+\lVert\Psi\rVert)}
, then the sequence
\lVert\tilde{w}_{n}\rVert_{X}
is uniformly bounded and the telescopic series
\sum\lVert\tilde{w}_{n+1}-\tilde{w}_{n}\rVert_{X}
converges. This gives the solution
\tilde{w}=\sum(\tilde{w}_{n+1}-\tilde{w}_{n})
to (
28
).

In the general case, when
I+\mathcal{L}
is invertible, equation (
28
) is equivalent to

w=(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}z+(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}B(w,w)-(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}\Psi(w,w,w).

Applying above result on
\mathcal{L}=0
and
\tilde{z}=(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}z
yields solution of (
28
).
∎

4.2
The linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}

In preparation for the proof of stability, we first prove boundedness of trilinear operator
\Psi
in
X_{T}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
T\in(0,+\infty]
.

(i)

\Psi
is well-defined and continuous on
X_{T}\times X_{T}\times X_{T}
with

\lVert\Psi(f,g,h)\rVert_{X_{T}}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert h\rVert_{X_{T}}.

(ii)

Let
a,b\in X_{T}
. Then
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
is continuous on
X_{T}
with

\lVert\mathcal{L}_{a,b}f\rVert_{X_{T}}\leq C\lVert a\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert b\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}.

Proof.

Let
f,g,h\in X_{T}
. Taking into account the beta integral

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}s^{-\beta}\,\mathrm{d}s=ct^{1-\alpha-\beta}\quad\text{for }\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)

(29)

and (
11
), we obtain for
k=1,2
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla^{k}S(t-s)\Psi(f,g,h)(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{k+1}{4}}\bigg(\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\nabla g(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\nabla h(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{k+1}{4}}s^{-\frac{3}{4}}\,\mathrm{d}sN_{1,\infty,T}(f)N_{1,\infty,T}(g)N_{1,\infty,T}(h)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{-\frac{k}{4}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert h\rVert_{X_{T}},

hence, for the estimate of
\lVert\nabla\Psi(f,g,h)\rVert_{L^{4}(B(x,r)\times(0,r^{4}))}
, we can assume
x=0
and
r=1
due to the invariance under translation and scaling. We note that
\Psi(f,g,h)
solves (
23
) for
\phi=\psi(f,g,h)
.
It follows by the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\lVert\psi(f,g,h)\rVert_{L^{1}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\leq

\displaystyle C(\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\lVert\nabla g\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\lVert\Delta h\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}

\displaystyle+\lVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\lVert\nabla^{2}g\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\lVert\nabla h\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}

\displaystyle+\lVert\nabla^{2}f\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\lVert\nabla g\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{2}\times[0,1])}\lVert\nabla h\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{2}\times[0,1])})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert h\rVert_{X_{T}}.

Using the same argument to prove (
22
) in Lemma
6
with help of a smooth cutoff function of
B_{1}
, we obtain

\lVert\nabla^{2}\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{1}\times[0,1])}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert h\rVert_{X_{T}}

and

\lVert\nabla\Psi\rVert_{L^{4}(B_{1}\times[0,1])}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert h\rVert_{X_{T}}.

Hence

\lVert\Psi(f,g,h)\rVert_{X_{T}}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert h\rVert_{X_{T}}

where
C
is a universal constant independent of
f,g,h
and
T
. Thus (i) follows and from this directly (ii).
∎

The operator
\Psi
preserves BMO norm in the following sense:

Lemma 10
.

Let
a,b,f\in X_{T}
for some
T\in(0,+\infty]
. Then
\Psi(a,b,f)\in\mathrm{BMO}
for any
t\in(0,T)
and

\lVert\Psi(a,b,f)(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C\lVert a\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert b\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}

for some universal constant
C>0
independent of
a,b,f
and

.

Proof.

Due to the embedding

, it is enough to show

\lVert\Psi(a,b,f)(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\lVert a\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert b\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}

(30)

for some uniform constant
C>0
. It follows by (

) and (

) that

\displaystyle\lVert\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\psi(f(s),a(s),b(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla S(t-s)\left((\nabla f\cdot\nabla a)\nabla b\right)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{4}}s^{-\frac{3}{4}}\bigg[\left(s^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla a(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(s^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla b(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(s^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla f(s)\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\lVert a\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert b\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert f\rVert_{X_{T}}.

Therefore we have proved (

∎

We need an auxiliary space
L_{T}^{p}
for
T\in(0,+\infty]
and

\lVert f\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}:=\sup_{0<t\leq T}\left(\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+t^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla^{2}f(t)\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)<+\infty.

Lemma 11
.

Let
a,b\in X_{T}
for some
T\in(0,+\infty]
. Then

(i)

\Psi(\cdot,a,b),\Psi(b,\cdot,a),\Psi(a,b,\cdot):L_{T}^{p}\to L_{T}^{p}
are well-defined and continuous with

\lVert\Psi(f,a,b)\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}},\lVert\Psi(b,f,a)\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}},\lVert\Psi(b,a,f)\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}\leq C_{3}\lVert a\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert b\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}

for a uniform constant
C_{3}>0
.

(ii)

\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
is continuous on
L_{T}^{p}
with

\lVert\mathcal{L}_{a,b}f\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}\leq C_{4}\lVert a\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert b\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert f\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}

for a uniform constant
C_{4}>0
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L_{T}^{p}
. It follows by (
11
) and (

) that, for any

and

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla^{k}\Psi(f,a,b)(t)\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\nabla^{k+1}S(t-s)(\nabla f(s)\cdot\nabla a(s))\nabla b(s))\rVert_{L^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}C(t-s)^{-\frac{k+1}{4}}\lVert(\nabla f(s)\cdot\nabla a(s))\nabla b(s)\rVert_{L^{p}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{k+1}{4}}s^{-\frac{3}{4}}\bigg[\left(s^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla a(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(s^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla b(s)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(s^{\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla f(s)\rVert_{L^{p}}\right)\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{k}{4}}N_{1,\infty,T}(a)N_{1,\infty,T}(b)\lVert f\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}},

thus

\lVert\Psi(f,a,b)\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}\leq CN_{1,\infty,T}(a)N_{1,\infty,T}(b)\lVert f\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}.

The corresponding estimates for
\Psi(a,b,f)
and
\Psi(b,f,a)
follows similarly and implies the continuity of
\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
on
L_{T}^{p}
.
∎

Solving (
27

\mathcal{L}_{a,b}
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
a,b\in X_{0,T}
for some
T\in(0,+\infty]
, with the property that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert a(t+\cdot)\rVert_{X}=0\quad\text{or}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\lVert b(t+\cdot)\rVert_{X}=0

.

We may only consider the case

. For

, we can extend

and

by

. We want to show the equation

(32)

has a unique solution in
X
for any

. The strategy is to construct the global solution to (
32
) from finite many local solutions. Let
\delta>0
be a fixed constant, whose value will be decided later. We deduce from Lemma

a,b\in X_{0,T}
, the existence of an integer

and

covering
(0,+\infty)
, with
t_{0}=0
,
t_{J}^{\prime}=+\infty
and
t_{j}<t_{j-1}^{\prime}
for any
1\leq j\leq J
, such that

\lVert a_{j}\rVert_{X_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}},\lVert b_{j}\rVert_{X_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}}\leq\delta\quad\text{for any }0\leq j\leq J

for

a_{j}(t)=a(t_{j}+t),\quad b_{j}(t)=b(t_{j}+t)\quad t\in(0,t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}).

According to Lemma
9
, we may choose
\delta
small enough such that

\lVert\mathcal{L}_{a_{j},b_{j}}\rVert_{X_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{for }j=0,1,\cdots,J,

(33)

thus
I+\mathcal{L}_{a_{j},b_{j}}
is invertible on
X_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}
.
Now we construct the global solution to (
32
) in
X
by starting with (
32
) restricted on
I_{0}=(0,t_{0}^{\prime})
. According to (
33
), there exists a unique
f_{0}\in X_{t_{0}^{\prime}}
such that

f_{0}(t)+\mathcal{L}_{a_{0},b_{0}}f_{0}(t)=g(t)\quad\forall t\in I_{0}.

Again using (
33
), there exists a unique
\tilde{f_{1}}\in X_{t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}}
such that

\tilde{f}_{1}(\tau)+L_{a_{1},b_{1}}\tilde{f}_{1}(\tau)=g(t_{1}+\tau)+S(\tau)L_{a_{0},b_{0}}f_{0}(t_{1})\quad\forall\tau\in(0,t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}).

Note that
L_{a_{0},b_{0}}f_{0}(t_{1})\in\mathrm{BMO}
by Lemma
10
, hence the right hand side of above equation is in
X_{t_{1}^{\prime}-t_{1}}
according to Lemma
4
. Now we can define

f_{1}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{0}(t),&\text{for }t\in I_{0}\\
f_{1}(t)=\tilde{f}_{1}(t-t_{1}),&\text{for }t\in I_{1}\end{array}\right.

which is a unique solution to (
32
) on
I_{0}\cup I_{1}
and
f_{1}\in X_{t_{1}^{\prime}}
. Iterating this process
J-1
times, we obtain the unique solution
f=f_{J}\in X
to (
32
) on
(0,+\infty)
.
∎

4.3
Asymptotics of
u

In this section we consider the asymptotic behaviour of the global solution. This implies that (
31
) is fulfilled for
a=b=u
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed and
u_{0}\in\mathrm{VMO}
generating a
unique global solution
u\in X_{0}
of (
1
) to initial data
u_{0}
, cf. Proposition
2
. According to the definition of VMO space, there exists a decomposition:

u_{0}=f_{0}+g_{0},\quad f_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad g_{0}\in\mathrm{VMO},

where
\lVert g_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}
is small enough, so that there exists a unique global solution
g\in X_{0}
of (
1
) to initial data
g_{0}
, cf. Theorem
1
.
By Lemma
13
below,
g(t)
stays in
\mathrm{BMO}
and
\lVert g(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C\varepsilon
. For
f=u-g
, if there exists a time
T^{\prime}>0
at which
\lVert f(T^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq\varepsilon
, then
\lVert u(T^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C\varepsilon
. Provided
\varepsilon
is small enough, applying the existence and uniqueness result Theorem
1
to initial data
a=u(T^{\prime})
implies
\lVert u(T^{\prime}+\cdot)\rVert_{X}\leq C\varepsilon
, thus the desired asymptotic behaviour (
7
) of
u
.

First we show the persistence of
g(t)
in
\mathrm{BMO}
for small initial data
g_{0}
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\lVert g_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}<\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon<\min\{1,\rho\}
. Then the global solution
g\in X
of (
1
) to initial data
g_{0}
satisfies
g(t)\in\mathrm{BMO}
for any
t\in(0,\infty)
with

\lVert g(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}<C\varepsilon

for a constant
C>0
independent of
t
.

Proof.

First we note that for
F(\xi)=\pm\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
, the solution is characterized by

g(t)=S(t)g_{0}\pm\Psi(g,g,g)(t).

It follows by Lemma
10
and Proposition
1
that

\lVert g(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C\left(\lVert g_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}+\lVert g\rVert_{X}^{3}\right)\leq C\lVert g_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}.

Hence the claim for some constant
C>0
independent of
t
.
∎

In the following we prove that there exists a time
T^{\prime}>0
at which
\lVert f(T^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq\varepsilon
, under the condition (a) or (b) of Theorem
2
separately.

4.3.1
The coercive case

For the coercive nonlinearity
F(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
, we first show that the perturbation
f=u-g
fulfils an energy inequality. Then we derive its decay property.

We first prove that
f=u-g
possesses following regularity property:

Lemma 14
.

f\in L_{T}^{p}
for any
T\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We note that
f
satisfies the equation

f(t)=S(t)f_{0}-\mathcal{L}_{u,g}f(t)+\Psi(f,f,f)(t).

(34)

This equation can be rewritten as

f(t)=S(t)f_{0}-\mathcal{L}f(t)

where
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{u,g}-\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{L}_{f,f}
for
u,g,f\in X_{T}
. Since
f_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
S(t)f_{0}\in L^{p}_{T}
. Due to Lemma
7
and
11
, we can cover interval
(0,T)
by finitely many overlapping intervals
I_{j}=(t_{j},t_{j}^{\prime})
with
t_{0}=0
,
t_{J}^{\prime}=T
and
t_{j}<t^{\prime}_{j-1}
for any
1\leq j\leq J
, such that

\lVert\mathcal{L}\rVert_{L^{p}_{t_{j}^{\prime}-t_{j}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Note that since
T<\infty
, we do not need the asymptotic assumption (
31
). Then following a similar argument as in Lemma
12
, we can prove that
I+\mathcal{L}
is invertible on
L_{T}^{p}
. Therefore
f(t)=(I+\mathcal{L})^{-1}S(t)f_{0}
holds in
L_{T}^{p}
.
∎

This regularity property of
f
is local in time. Now we exploit the coercivity of the nonlinearity to derive global
L^{2}
-regularity of
f
. We infer from (
34
) that
f
solves the following equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+(-\Delta)^{2}f

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\left(\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2}\nabla f\right)+\lvert\nabla g\rvert^{2}\Delta f+\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2}\Delta g+2(\nabla g\cdot\nabla f)\Delta g+2(\nabla g\cdot\nabla f)\Delta f

\displaystyle+2\nabla g\cdot\nabla^{2}g\cdot\nabla f+2\nabla g\cdot\nabla^{2}f\cdot\nabla g+2\nabla g\cdot\nabla^{2}f\cdot\nabla f

\displaystyle+2\nabla f\cdot\nabla^{2}g\cdot\nabla g+2\nabla f\cdot\nabla^{2}g\cdot\nabla f+2\nabla f\cdot\nabla^{2}f\cdot\nabla g.

Due to Lemma
14
, we can multiply this equation by
f
and integrate it over
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(T,T^{\prime})
for any
0<T<T^{\prime}<+\infty
. Regularity in time follows from the properties of semigroup and spatial regularity. Then applying partial integration and Hölder’s inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert f(T^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert f(T)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\bigg[-\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{4}}^{4}-5\lVert\nabla g(t)\cdot\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+N_{2,\infty}(g)t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}+2N_{1,\infty}(g)N_{1,\infty}(f)t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+2N_{1,\infty}^{2}(g)t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}t.

It follows from Young’s inequality and the smallness of
g
that

\displaystyle\lVert f(T^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

(35)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f(T)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\left[8N_{1,\infty}^{2}(g)N_{1,\infty}^{2}(f)+8N_{1,\infty}^{4}(g)+\frac{1}{4}N_{2,\infty}^{2}(g)\right]t^{-1}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f(T)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{u}\,\varepsilon^{2}\sup_{T\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\ln\frac{T^{\prime}}{T},

where
C_{u}>0
is a constant depending on
u_{0}
. Setting
t_{k}=e^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\lVert f(T^{\prime})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{t_{k}}^{T^{\prime}}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\lVert f(t_{k})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{u}\,\varepsilon^{2}\sup_{t_{k}\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\quad\forall T^{\prime}\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]

and

\sup_{t_{k}\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\lVert f(t_{k})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

if
\varepsilon\leq 1/\sqrt{C_{u}}
, hence

\lVert f(t_{k+1})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq(1+2C_{u}\varepsilon^{2})\lVert f(t_{k})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This implies

\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ct^{\beta},\qquad\forall t\geq 1\quad\text{where }\beta=\ln(1+2C_{u}\varepsilon^{2}),

where
\varepsilon
is the upper bound of
\lVert g(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}
. Since
f=u-g\in X
, we have

\lVert\nabla^{2}f(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}

and by the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{n}}\leq C\lVert\nabla^{2}f(t)\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{n}{n+4}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{n+4}}\leq Ct^{\frac{4\beta-n}{2(n+4)}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty

for any
n\geq 4
, provided
\varepsilon\leq\sqrt{(e^{n/4}-1)/2C_{u}}
.

For
n=2,3
, we note that it follows from (
35
)

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\lVert f(t_{k})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{u}\,\varepsilon^{2}\sup_{t_{k}\leq t\leq T^{\prime}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

and thus

\int_{1}^{T}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq CT^{\beta}\quad\forall T>1.

Then by Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{n}}^{\frac{8}{n}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{1}^{T}\lVert\Delta f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-n}{n}\frac{8}{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq CT^{3\beta-1}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad T\to\infty

provided
\varepsilon\leq\sqrt{(e^{\frac{1}{3}}-1)/2C_{u}}
.

Therefore we have
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}=0
for any
n\geq 2
, due to the Poincáre inequality
\lVert a\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lVert\nabla a\rVert_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

4.3.2
The non-coercive case

Now we consider the nonlinearity
F(\xi)=-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\xi
, in which case
f=u-g
does not satisfy an energy-type inequality as above. But if
u_{0}
is small enough, the asymptotic property of
f
still holds.

Suppose
u_{0}\in\mathrm{VMO}
satisfies
\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}<\rho
and
\lVert g_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}<\varepsilon<\rho
where
\rho>0
is the constant in Theorem
1
. Then the corresponding solutions
u,g\in X
satisfy
\lVert u\rVert_{X}<2C_{1}\rho
and
\lVert g\rVert_{X}<2C_{1}\varepsilon
. In the following, we deduce the global
L^{p}
-regularity of
f
.

Lemma 15
.

Under the above conditions with possibly reduced
\rho
, we have
f\in L_{T}^{p}
for
T=+\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}<\rho
, there exists a sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
obtained by the fixed point iteration (
25
), which converges to
u
in
X_{T}
and satisfies
\lVert u_{k}\rVert_{X_{T}}<2C_{1}\rho
.
We define

f_{1}=S(t)f_{0},\qquad f_{k+1}=u_{k+1}-g\quad\text{for }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Using the notation of the trilinear operator
\Psi
, we can reformulate
f_{k}
as

f_{k+1}=u_{k+1}-g=f_{1}-\mathcal{L}_{u_{k},g}f_{k}-\Psi(f_{k},f_{k},f_{k}).

Since
f_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}
, it follows from (
11
) that
for
t>0

\lVert\nabla S(t)f_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\lVert\nabla f_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}}

and

\lVert\nabla^{2}S(t)f_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}\lVert\nabla f_{0}\rVert_{L^{p}},

hence
f_{1}\in L_{T}^{p}
. Letting
\mu:=\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}
, then if
\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}<2\mu
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, Lemma
11
implies

\displaystyle\lVert f_{k+1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}+\lVert\mathcal{L}_{u_{k},g}f_{k}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}+\lVert\Psi(f_{k},f_{k},f_{k})\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert f_{1}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}+\left(C_{4}\lVert u_{k}\rVert_{X_{T}}\lVert g\rVert_{X_{T}}+C_{3}\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{X_{T}}^{2}\right)\lVert f_{k}\rVert_{L_{T}^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu+\left(4C_{1}^{2}C_{4}\rho\varepsilon+4C_{1}^{2}C_{3}(\rho+\varepsilon)^{2}\right)\mu<2\mu

provided
\rho^{2}<\dfrac{1}{4C_{1}^{2}(C_{4}+4C_{3})}
. Therefore the sequence
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L_{T}^{p}
, hence its limit
f\in X_{T}\cap L_{T}^{p}
.
∎

According to the definition of
L_{T}^{n}
, we know that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\lVert\nabla f(t)\rVert_{L^{n}}=0
, hence
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}=0
.

Remark 2
.

Applying Lemma
15
to
g=0
, we conclude the following result:
If additionally the initial data
u_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the solution
u
obtained in Theorem
1
is in
L_{T}^{p}
for any
p\in[0,\infty]
.

Now we put everything together to prove Theorem
2
:

Proof of Theorem
2
.
Under the condition (a) or (b), for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a time
T^{\prime}>0
at which
\lVert f(T^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq\varepsilon
. Together with Lemma
13
, this yields
\lVert u(T^{\prime})\rVert_{\mathrm{BMO}}\leq C\varepsilon
. Hence by the well-posedness theory to small initial data established in Theorem
1
, we infer that
\lVert u(T^{\prime}+\cdot)\rVert_{X}\leq C\varepsilon
, i.e., the large time behaviour of global solution in Theorem
2
(i).

Then we can invoke Lemma
12
with
a=b=u
and conclude that
I+\mathcal{L}_{u,u}
is invertible on
X
. According to Lemma
8
, equation (
27
) is solvable for small enough perturbation
w_{0}=u_{0}-v_{0}
, which implies the stability of global solutions and completes the proof of Theorem
2
(ii).

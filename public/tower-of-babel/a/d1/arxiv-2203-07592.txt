Abstract
Finite p p -groups with a unique 𝒜 2 \mathcal{A}_{2} -subgroup are classified up to isomorphism. A problem proposed by Berkovich and Janko is solved. Keywords Finite p p -groups, 𝒜 t \mathcal{A}_{t} -groups, 𝒜 2 \mathcal{A}_{2} -subgroups 2020MSC 20D15

Introduction

In this paper,
p
is a prime, all groups considered are finite
p
-groups (in brief,
p
-groups), where
p
-groups are the groups of prime power order. A finite group
G
is said to be minimal non-abelian if
G
is non-abelian but all its proper subgroups are abelian. Berkovich and Janko in their long paper
[
5
]
introduced a more general concept than that of minimal non-abelian
p
-groups.
A finite non-abelian
p
-group is called an
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-group,
t
is a positive integer, if it has a non-abelian subgroup of index
p^{t-1}
but all its subgroups of index
p^{t}
are abelian. Obviously,
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-groups are exactly minimal non-abelian
p
-groups, which were classified by R
{\rm\acute{e}}
dei in
[
9
]
. For
t=2,3
,
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-groups were classified by Zhang et al. in
[
16
,
17
]
, respectively. Obviously, given a non-abelian
p
-group
G
, there is a
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G
is an
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-group. Hence the study of finite non-abelian
p
-groups can be regarded as that of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-groups for
some
t\in\mathbb{N}
. We use
G\in\mathcal{A}_{t}
to denote
G
is an
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-group.

As numerous results show, the structure of a
p
-group depends essentially on its
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroups. Indeed, the structure of a
p
-group also depends essentially on the
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroups contained there. Recently, some scholars studied the structure of
p
-groups by imposing restrictions on their
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroups, see
[
6
,
14
,
15
]
. In this paper, we study the structure of
p
-groups by imposing restriction on the number of their
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroups.

Following the notation of
[
3
]
,
\alpha_{k}(G)
denotes the number of
\mathcal{A}_{k}
-subgroups of a non-abelian
p
-group
G
.
An and Qu et al. in a series of papers
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
classified the
p
-groups with an
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of index
p
. Naturally, we wish to classify the
p
-groups with an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of index
p
. Such a class of
p
-groups contains at least
\mathcal{A}_{3}
-groups as its subclass. Hence such a class of
p
-groups is quite large, and this classification is an enormous work!
In this paper, we classify the
p
-groups with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup. In other words, we classify the
p
-groups with
\alpha_{2}(G)=1
. Thus
[
4
, Problem 895]
are solved.
Due to the classification of
\mathcal{A}_{3}
-groups and the classification of
the
p
-groups obtained by An and Qu et al., the classification of the
p
-groups with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup is completed in a very short length.

2
Preliminaries

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group. We use
c(G)
,
\exp(G)
and
d(G)
to denote the nilpotency class, the exponent and the minimal cardinality of generating set of
G
, respectively. We use
{\rm C}_{p^{n}}
and
{\rm C}_{p^{n}}^{m}
to denote the cyclic group of order
p^{n}
and the direct product of
m
copies of
{\rm C}_{p^{n}}
, respectively. The group
G
is the central product,

G=A\ast B
, of the two subgroups
A
and
B
if
G=AB
and
[A,B]=1
.

For any positive integer
s
, we define

\Omega_{s}(G)=\langle a\in G\mid a^{p^{s}}=1\rangle~{\rm and}~\mho_{s}(G)=\langle a^{p^{s}}\mid a\in G\rangle.

The Frattini subgroup
\Phi(G)
of
G
is equal to
G^{\prime}\mho_{1}(G)
.

We use
{\rm M}_{p}(n,m)
to denote the
p
-groups

\langle a,b\bigm|a^{p^{n}}=b^{p^{m}}=1,~[a,b]=a^{p^{n-1}}\rangle,~{\rm where}~n\geqslant 2,~m\geqslant 1.

We use
{\rm M}_{p}(n,m,1)
to denote the
p
-groups

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{p^{n}}=b^{p^{m}}=c^{p}=1,~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=[c,b]=1\rangle,

{\rm where}~n\geqslant m\geqslant 1,~{\rm and~if}~p=2,~{\rm then}~m+n\geqslant 3
.

For any undefined notations and terminologies, the reader is referred to
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
13
, Lemma 2.2]
)

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group. Then the following propositions are equivalent
:

(1)

G
is a minimal non-abelian
p
-group
;

(2)

d(G)=2
and
|G^{\prime}|=p;

(3)

d(G)=2
and
Z(G)=\Phi(G).

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
2
, Corollary 2.4]
)

(1)
Let
M
be an
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-group and

A
be an abelian group of order
p^{k}
. Then
G=M\times A
is an
\mathcal{A}_{t+k}
-group.

(2)
Let
M
be an
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-group with
|M^{\prime}|=p
,
G=M\ast A
, where
A
is abelian with order
p^{k+1}
and
M\cap A=M^{\prime}
. Then
G
is an
\mathcal{A}_{t+k}
-group.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
17
, Lemma 2.15]
)
Suppose that
G
is a finite
p
-group.
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are two distinct maximal subgroups of
G
. Then

|G^{\prime}|\leqslant p|M_{1}^{\prime}M_{2}^{\prime}|
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
3
, Lemma 1.4]
)
Let
G
be a
p
-group and
N\trianglelefteq G
. If
N
has no abelian
G
-invariant subgroups of type
(p,p)
, then
N
is either cyclic or a
2
-group of maximal class. If, in addition,
N\leq\Phi(G)
, then
N
is cyclic.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
3
, Proposition 1.13]
)
Let
G
be a
p
-group and let
N\leq\Phi(G)
be
G
-invariant. If
Z(N)
is cyclic, then
N
is also cyclic.

3
A classification of
p
-groups with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup

In the following we always assume
G
is an
\mathcal{A}_{t}
-group with
t\geqslant 3
. We discuss in two cases:
|G^{\prime}|=p
and
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group with
|G^{\prime}|=p
. Then
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup if and only if
G=H\ast C
, where
H
is a non-abelian subgroup of order
p^{3}
and
C
is cyclic.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Since
|G^{\prime}|=p
,
M^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
for any non-abelian subgroup
M
of
G
.
Let
L/G^{\prime}=\Omega_{1}(G/G^{\prime})
. Then
L/G^{\prime}\cong{\rm C}_{p}^{3}
.
Let
K
be an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
. Then
d(K)\geqslant 3
by Lemma
2.1
.
It follows that
|\Omega_{1}(K/K^{\prime})|\geqslant p^{3}
.
This forces that
\Omega_{1}(K/G^{\prime})=\Omega_{1}(K/K^{\prime})=\Omega_{1}(G/G^{\prime})
.
Hence
L/G^{\prime}\leq K/G^{\prime}
. Thus
L\leq K
. Notice that
H<L
. We have
H<K
.
Since
K
is an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-group,
|K/H|=p
. This forces
K=L
. That is,
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup.

(\Rightarrow)

Suppose that
H
is a non-abelian subgroup of minimum order of
G
. Since
|G^{\prime}|=p
,
H^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
. In particular,
H\unlhd G
.
If
K/H
is a subgroup of
G/H
of order
p
, then
|K:H|=p
. By the minimality of
H
, each subgroup of
K
of index
\geqslant p^{2}

is abelian. Hence
K
is an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-group.
Since
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup,
G/H
has a unique subgroup of order
p
. Obviously,
G/H
is abelian. Hence
G/H
is cyclic.
By the minimality of
H
,
d(H)=2
. Thus
d(G)\leqslant 3
. By Lemma
2.1
,
d(G)\neq 2
. Hence
d(G)=3
.
Fortunately, the
p
-groups
G
with
d(G)=3
and
|G^{\prime}|=p
were classified, which are listed in
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
. The conclusion follows by a simple checking.

\Box

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup
K
and
|G^{\prime}|=p
. Then
K\not\leq\Phi(G)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup
K
and
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
. Then
K\nleq\Phi(G)
if and only if
G
has an
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of index
p
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Let
M
be an
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of index
p
of
G
. Then
K\nleq M
. Hence
K\nleq\Phi(G)
.

(\Rightarrow)

Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of
G
. If
M
is neither abelian nor minimal non-abelian, then
K\leq M
. Moreover,
K\Phi(G)\leq M
.
Let
d(G)=d
and
d(G/K\Phi(G))=k
. Since
K\nleq\Phi(G)
,
k<d
. Notice that
G
has
1+p+\cdots+p^{d-1}
maximal subgroups and
G/K\Phi(G)
has

1+p+\cdots+p^{k-1}
maximal subgroups. Thus
G
has at least
p^{d-1}
maximal subgroups which are abelian or minimal non-abelian.
It follows from
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
and Lemma
2.3
that
G
has at most one abelian maximal subgroup.
Thus
G
has at least
(p^{d-1}-1)

\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of index
p
.

\Box

By the proof process
(\Rightarrow)
of Lemma
3.3
, we have

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume a
p
-group
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup
A
of index
p
and
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
. If
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup, then
G
is a two-generator
2
-group with an abelian maximal subgroup. In particular,
G/A^{\prime}
has at least two abelian maximal subgroups.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
G
is a finite
p
-group,
d(G)=3
,
\Phi(G)\leq Z(G)
and
G^{\prime}\cong{\rm C}_{p}^{3}
. Let
\overline{G}=G/G^{\prime}=\langle\overline{a_{1}},~\overline{a_{2}},~\overline{a_{3}}\rangle
, where
o(\overline{a_{i}})=p^{m_{i}}
,
i=1,2,3
and
m_{1}\geqslant m_{2}\geqslant m_{3}
.
Let
\omega(G)=(\omega_{ij})
be a
3\times 3
matrix over
GF(p)
and
a_{i}^{p^{m_{i}}}=x^{\omega_{i1}}y^{\omega_{i2}}z^{\omega_{i3}}
, where
x=[a_{2},a_{3}],~y=[a_{3},a_{1}]
,
z=[a_{1},a_{2}]
. If
\omega(G)
has at least two principal minors to be equal
0
, then
G
has at least two
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroups.

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume
{\scriptsize\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{11}&\omega_{12}\\
\omega_{21}&\omega_{22}\\
\end{array}\right|}=

{\scriptsize\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{22}&\omega_{23}\\
\omega_{32}&\omega_{33}\\
\end{array}\right|}=0
.
Let
H=\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
. Notice that
G^{\prime}\leq\Phi(G)\leq Z(G)
. We have
H^{\prime}=\langle[a_{1},a_{2}]\rangle
. Moreover,
|H^{\prime}|=p
. Then
H\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
by Lemma
2.1
.
We claim
H\cap G^{\prime}=\langle a_{1}^{p^{m_{1}}},a_{2}^{p^{m_{2}}},z\rangle
. In fact,
take
g\in H\cap G^{\prime}
. Then
g=a_{1}^{i}a_{2}^{j}z^{k}
. Moreover,
\overline{1}=\overline{g}=\overline{a_{1}}^{i}\overline{a_{2}}^{j}
.
It follows from
\langle\overline{a_{1}},~\overline{a_{2}},~\overline{a_{3}}\rangle
being a basis of
\overline{G}
that
\overline{a_{1}}^{i}=\overline{a_{2}}^{j}=\overline{1}
. Hence
p^{m_{1}}|i
and
p^{m_{2}}|j
. Thus
g\in\langle a_{1}^{p^{m_{1}}},a_{2}^{p^{m_{2}}},z\rangle
. That means
H\cap G^{\prime}\leq\langle a_{1}^{p^{m_{1}}},a_{2}^{p^{m_{2}}},z\rangle
. Obviously,
\langle a_{1}^{p^{m_{1}}},a_{2}^{p^{m_{2}}},z\rangle\leq H\cap G^{\prime}
. Thus
H\cap G^{\prime}=\langle a_{1}^{p^{m_{1}}},a_{2}^{p^{m_{2}}},z\rangle
.
Since
{\scriptsize\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{11}&\omega_{12}\\
\omega_{21}&\omega_{22}\\
\end{array}\right|}=0
,

|\langle a_{1}^{p^{m_{1}}},a_{2}^{p^{m_{2}}},z\rangle|\leqslant p^{2}
.
Since
G^{\prime}\cong{\rm C}_{p}^{3}
, there exists
c\in G^{\prime}\setminus H
. By Lemma
2.2
(1)
,
H\times\langle c\rangle
is an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
.
Similarly, there exists
d\in G^{\prime}\setminus\langle a_{2},a_{3}\rangle
such that
\langle a_{2},a_{3}\rangle\times\langle d\rangle
is also an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
.
Clearly,
\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\neq\langle a_{2},a_{3}\rangle\times\langle d\rangle
. Thus
G
has at least two
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroups.

\Box

In following we classify the
p
-groups
G
with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup
K
in two cases:
K\nleq\Phi(G)
and
K\leq\Phi(G)
.

If
K\nleq\Phi(G)
and
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
, then, by Lemma
3.3
,

G
has an
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of index
p
. Fortunately, such
p
-groups have been classified up to isomorphism in
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
The classification results are listed in
[
1
, Theorem 7.1]
,
[
2
, Theorem 4.12]
,
[
10
, Lemma 2.12, Corollary 3.5, Theorem 3.8, 3.9]
,
[
11
, Theorem 3.1, 4.1, 5.1, 6.1, 7.1, 7.6]
and
[
12
, Theorem 5.1, 5.3]
. In this case, what we need to do is to pick out the groups with a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup from the groups listed in the theorems mentioned above. Although there are a lot of the
p
-groups with an
\mathcal{A}_{1}
-subgroup of index
p
, the process of checking can be greatly reduced by Corollary
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
. For the remaining cases, the process of checking is simple but tedious. The details are omitted.
Now we list the groups with
K\nleq\Phi(G)
and
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
as follows. In particular, we also list some information of these groups, which are used in the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group with
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
. Then
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup
K
and
K\nleq\Phi(G)
if and only if
G
is one of the following non-isomorphic groups
:

(1)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{4}=b^{4}=c^{2}=1,~[b,c]=1,~[c,a]=b^{2},~[a,b]=a^{2}\rangle;
moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{5}
,
K=\langle ab,c\rangle\times\langle a^{2}\rangle\cong{\rm D}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(2)

\langle a,b,c,x\bigm|a^{4}=b^{2}=x^{2}=1,~[a,b]=x,~[a,c]=a^{2}=c^{2},~[b,c]=[x,a]=[x,b]=[x,c]=1\rangle;
moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{5}
,
K=\langle a,c\rangle\times\langle x\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(3)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{8}=c^{2}=1,~b^{2}=a^{4},~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=b^{2},~[c,b]=1\rangle;
moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{5}
,
K=\langle a^{2},b\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(4)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{8}=c^{4}=1,~b^{2}=a^{4},~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=a^{4},~[c,b]=c^{2}\rangle;
moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{6}
,
K=\langle a^{2},b,c\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{2}(2,2,1).{\rm C}_{2}
.

(5)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{2^{n+1}}=b^{2}=c^{2}=1,~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=a^{2^{n}},~[c,b]=1\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 2;
moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{n+3}
,
K=\langle a^{2},b\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{2}(n,1)\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(6)

\langle a,b,c,d\bigm|a^{2^{n}}=b^{2}=c^{2}=d^{2}=1,~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=d,~[c,b]=[d,a]=[d,b]=1\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 2;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{n+3}
,
K=\langle a^{2},b\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{2}(n-1,1,1)\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(7)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{2^{n}}=b^{4}=c^{2}=1,~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=b^{2},~[c,b]=1\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 3;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{n+3}
,
K=\langle b,a^{2}\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{2}(2,n-1)\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(8)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{8}=b^{4}=c^{4}=1,~[b,c]=a^{4},~[c,a]=b^{2},~[a,b]=b^{2}c^{2}\rangle;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=2^{7}
,
K=\langle b,c\rangle\ast\langle a^{2}\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{2}(2,2,1)\ast{\rm C}_{4}
.

(9)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{4}=b^{4}=c^{4}=1,~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=b^{2},~[c,b]=c^{2}\rangle;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{4},~|G|=2^{6}
,
K=\langle c,a^{2}b\rangle\times\langle b^{2}\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(10)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|b^{2}=c^{4}=1,~a^{2^{n}}=c^{2},~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=[c,b]=c^{2}\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 2;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{n+1},~|G|=2^{n+3}
,
K=\langle c,b\rangle\ast\langle a^{2^{n-1}}\rangle\cong{\rm D}_{8}\ast{\rm C}_{4}
.

(11)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{2^{n}}=b^{2}=c^{4}=1,~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=[c,b]=c^{2}\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 2;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{n+1},~|G|=2^{n+3}
,
K=\langle c,b\rangle\times\langle a^{2^{n-1}}\rangle\cong{\rm D}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(12)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{2^{n}}=c^{4}=1,~b^{2}=c^{2},~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=[c,b]=c^{2}\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 2;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{n+1},~|G|=2^{n+3}
,
K=\langle c,b\rangle\times\langle a^{2^{n-1}}\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(13)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{4}=b^{2^{n+1}}=c^{4}=1,~[b,c]=1,~[c,a]=a^{2}=c^{2},~[a,b]=b^{2^{n}}\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 2;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{n+2},~|G|=2^{n+4}
,
K=\langle a,c\rangle\times\langle b^{2^{n}}\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(14)

\langle a,b,c\bigm|a^{2^{n+1}}=b^{4}=1,~c^{2}=b^{2},~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=a^{2^{n}},~[c,b]=c^{2}\rangle
, where
n\geqslant 3;

moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{n+2},~|G|=2^{n+4}
,
K=\langle c,b\rangle\times\langle a^{2^{n}}\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
.

(15)

\langle a,b,c,d\bigm|a^{9}=c^{3}=d^{3}=1,~b^{3}=a^{3},~[a,b]=c,~[c,a]=d,~[c,b]=a^{3},~[d,a]=[d,b]=1\rangle
;
moreover,
G\in\mathcal{A}_{3},~|G|=3^{5}
,
K=\langle b,c\rangle\times\langle d\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{3}(2,1)\times{\rm C}_{3}

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group with
|G^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
. Then
G
has a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup
K
and
K\leq\Phi(G)
if and only if

G=\langle a,b,c,d\bigm|a^{4}=b^{4}=c^{2}=d^{2}=1,~[b,a]=c,~[c,a]=d,~[d,a]=b^{2},~[c,b]=[d,b]=[d,c]=1\rangle
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)

In fact,
G=A\rtimes_{\varphi}\langle a\rangle
, where
A=\langle b\rangle\times\langle c\rangle\times\langle d\rangle\cong{\rm C}_{4}\times{\rm C}_{2}^{2}
,
a^{\varphi}=\alpha
:

b^{\alpha}=bc
,
c^{\alpha}=cd
,
d^{\alpha}=db^{2}
and
\alpha\in\hbox{\rm Aut\,}(A)
. Now we have
|G|=2^{6}
and
\Phi(G)=\langle a^{2},c\rangle\times\langle d\rangle\cong{\rm D}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
is an
\mathcal{A}
2
-subgroup of
G
. It follows that
G
is an
\mathcal{A}
4
-group and all maximal subgroups of
G
are
\mathcal{A}
3
-groups.
Hence the
\mathcal{A}
2
-subgroups of
G
must have order
16
. It is easy to verify
G
satisfies the hypothesis of the theorem by calculations.

(\Rightarrow)
We discuss in two cases:
p=2
and
p>2
.

Case 1.

p=2

Using Magma
[
8
]
to check the SmallGroups Database
[
7
]
, we know that if
|G|<2^{8}
, then
G
is exactly the group in the theorem.
Assume
|G|\geqslant 2^{8}
. We will prove there is no group satisfying the hypothesis in the theorem by counterexample.

Assume that
G
is a counterexample of minimal order.
Let
M
be a maximal subgroup of
G
. We assert that
K\nleq\Phi(M)
. If not, since
K
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
,
K
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
M
. By Corollary
3.2
,
|M^{\prime}|\geqslant 2^{2}
. Thus
M
satisfies the conditions of the theorem.
By the minimality of
G
,
M
satisfies the conclusion of the theorem. Thus
M
is the group in the theorem. That is,
|M|=2^{6}
. This contradicts
|G|\geqslant 2^{8}
. Thus the assert holds. Now we have
M
is one of the groups in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.6
.
By Lemma
2.5
,
Z(K)
is non-cyclic. Thus
M
is one of the groups in Theorem
3.1
with
H\cap C=1
or the groups
(5-8)
and
(11-14)
in Theorem
3.6
.

We assert that
M
cannot be the group
(5)
in Theorem
3.6
. If not, then
|M|=2^{n+3}
and
K=\langle a^{2},b,c\rangle\cong{\rm M}_{2}(n,1)\times{\rm C}_{2}
. It follows from
|M|\geqslant 2^{7}
that
n\geqslant 4
.
Since
K
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
and
K\leq\Phi(G)
,
K
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of any maximal subgroup
T
of
G
.
Same as
M
,
T
is one of the groups in Theorem
3.1
with
H\cap C=1
or the groups
(5-8)
and
(11-14)
in Theorem
3.6
.
Now
T
and
K
are known. From this we know only the
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of the group (5) is isomorphic to
{\rm M}_{2}(n,1)\times{\rm C}_{2}
, where
n\geqslant 4
. Thus
T
is isomorphic to the group
(5)
. By calculation,
Z(T)
is cyclic.
On the other hand, since
K
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
,
K\unlhd G
. By
K\leq\Phi(G)
and Lemma
2.4
,
there exists
N\unlhd G
and
N\leq K
such that
N\cong{\rm C}_{2}\times{\rm C}_{2}
. Notice that
G/C_{G}(N)\lesssim\hbox{\rm Aut\,}(N)
. We have
|G:C_{G}(N)|\leqslant 2
. Thus there exists a maximal subgroup
M_{1}
of
G
such that
N\leq M_{1}\leq C_{G}(N)
.
Moreover,
N\leq Z(M_{1})
and hence
Z(M_{1})
is non-cyclic. This contradicts
Z(T)
is cyclic for any maximal subgroup
T
of
G
.

We assert that
M
cannot be the groups
(6)
and
(7)
in Theorem
3.6
. If not, then
K=\langle a^{2},b,c\rangle
and
|M:K|=2
. By calculation,
\mho_{2}(K)=\langle a^{8}\rangle
and
|K:\mho_{2}(K)|=2^{5}
.
Let
\overline{G}=G/\mho_{2}(K)
. Then
|\overline{G}|=2^{7}
.
Since
K\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
,
\overline{K}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}(i\leqslant 2)
.
Clearly,
|\overline{K}^{\prime}|=2
and
d(\overline{K})=3
. Moreover,
\overline{K}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
.
We assert that
\overline{K}
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
\overline{G}
.
If not, let
\overline{A}
be an
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
\overline{G}
and
\overline{A}\neq\overline{K}
.
It follows from
\overline{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
that
A\in\mathcal{A}_{t}(t\geqslant 2)
.
Since
K
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
G
,
K\leq A
. Moreover,
\overline{K}\leq\overline{A}
.
This contradicts that
\overline{A}
and
\overline{K}
are two different
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroups of
\overline{G}
.
Thus
\overline{K}
is a unique
\mathcal{A}_{2}
-subgroup of
\overline{G}
.
Since
{K}\leq{\Phi(G)}
,
\overline{K}\leq\overline{\Phi(G)}=\Phi(\overline{G})
. By Corollary
3.2
,
|\overline{G}^{\prime}|\geqslant p^{2}
. Thus
\overline{G}
satisfies the conditions of the theorem.
By the minimality of
G
,
\overline{G}
satisfies the conclusion of the theorem. Thus
\overline{G}
is the group in the theorem. That is,
|\overline{G}|=2^{6}
. This contradicts
|\overline{G}|=2^{7}
.

If
M
is one of the groups in Theorem
3.1
with
H\cap C=1
or the groups
(8)
and
(11-14)
in Theorem
3.6
, then
Z(K)\leq Z(M)
and
\mho_{1}(K)\leq Z(M)
.

K
is isomorphic to one of the following groups
:

(i)

H\times\langle x\rangle
, where

or
{\rm D}_{8}
,

(ii)

H\ast\langle x\rangle
, where

,

and

.

Same as

,

Z(K)\leq Z(L)
and

for any maximal subgroup

of

. It follows that

and
\mho_{1}(K)\leq Z(G)
.

If
K
is isomorphic to the group (i), then, let

. Thus
\overline{K}\cong H
.
If

is isomorphic to the group (ii), then, let

. Thus

\overline{K}

{K}\leq{\Phi(G)}
,
\overline{K}\leq\overline{\Phi(G)}=\Phi(\overline{G})
. Now it follows from Lemma
2.5
that
\overline{K}
is cyclic. This is a contradiction.

To sum up, the counterexample of minimal order does not exist. The conclusion follows.

Case 2.

p>2

In this case, we prove that there is no group satisfying the conditions in Theorem
3.7
. Let
L
be a subgroup of minimum order of
G
such that

. Then

for any maximal subgroup

of

. Hence

is one of the groups in Theorem

.

,

(26-28)

and

:

or

(ii)

,

that
\overline{K}

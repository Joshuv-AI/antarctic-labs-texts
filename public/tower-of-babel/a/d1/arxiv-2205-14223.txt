A short note on inf-sup conditions for the Taylor-Hood family Q k Q_{k} – Q k − 1 Q_{k-1}
Walter Zulehner

Abstract
We discuss two types of discrete inf-sup conditions of the Stokes problem for the Taylor-Hood family Q k Q_{k} – Q k − 1 Q_{k-1} for all k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} with k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 in 2D and 3D. While in 2D all results hold for a general class of quadrilateral meshes, the results in 3D are restricted to meshes of parallelepipeds. The analysis is based on an element-wise technique as opposed to the widely used macroelement technique. It covers Stokes problems with pure Dirichlet boundary conditions, pure Neumann boundary conditions, and mixed boundary conditions.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a bounded, connected and open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
.
We consider the following Stokes problem: Find the velocity
u
and the pressure
p
such that

\displaystyle-\mu\,\Delta u+\nabla p=f,\quad\operatorname{div}u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma_{D},

\displaystyle\mu\,\partial_{n}u-p\,n

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma_{N},

where
\mu
is the dynamic viscosity,
f
is a given force density,
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}
with
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
,
\partial_{n}
indicates the directional derivative with respect to the unit outer normal vector
n
to
\partial\Omega
.
The boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{N}
are also known as ”do-nothing” boundary conditions, see
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
.

The standard mixed variational formulation of this problem reads: Find
u\in V
and
p\in Q
, such that

\displaystyle a(u,v)

\displaystyle+b(v,p)

\displaystyle=\langle F,v\rangle

\displaystyle\text{for all}\ v\in V,

\displaystyle b(u,q)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{for all}\ q\in Q,

where

a(u,v)=\mu\int_{\Omega}\nabla u:\nabla v\ dx,\quad b(v,q)=-\int_{\Omega}q\ \operatorname{div}v\ dx,\quad\langle F,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v\ dx,

with
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and the function spaces

(a)

for pure Dirichlet boundary conditions (
\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
):

V=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d},\quad Q=\left\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}q\ dx=0\right\}=:L_{0}^{2}(\Omega),

(b)

for mixed boundary conditions (
\Gamma_{D}
,
\Gamma_{N}
have positive measure, i.e.,
|\Gamma_{D}|,|\Gamma_{N}|>0
):

V=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\colon v=0\ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{D}\}=:H_{0,\Gamma_{D}}^{1}(\Omega)^{d},\quad Q=L^{2}(\Omega),

(c)

for pure Neumann boundary conditions (
\Gamma_{D}=\emptyset
):

V=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\colon\int_{\Omega}v\ dx=0\right\}=:\hat{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{d},\quad Q=L^{2}(\Omega).

Here and throughout the paper we use
L^{2}(D)
and
H^{1}(D)
to denote the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces of functions on
D
. Moreover,
H_{0}^{1}(D)
is the subspace of functions in
H^{1}(D)
with vanishing trace on
\partial D
, its dual space is indicated by
H^{-1}(D)
. The inner product in
L^{2}(D)
is denoted by
(.,.)_{0,D}
, the norms in
L^{2}(D)
,
H^{1}(D)
,
H^{-1}(D)
are denoted by
\|.\|_{0,D}
,
\|.\|_{1,D}
,
\|.\|_{-1,D}
, respectively. The same symbols are used for inner product and norms
in the vector-valued versions of the spaces. For
D=\Omega
we drop the subscript
D
.
The Euclidean vector norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is indicated by
\|.\|
(without any subscript).

It is well-known that the famous inf-sup condition (also known as LBB condition) is satisfied for this mixed variational problem: There exists a constant
\beta>0
such that

\sup_{0\neq v\in V}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1}}\geq\beta\,\|q\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in Q.

(1)

The LBB condition is essential for the well-posedness of the Stokes problem. Its verification for the case (a) is typically done by an indirect proof using a compactness argument. We are aware of only one reference, namely
[
1
]
, which contains a proof for the case
|\Gamma_{N}|>0
. In Appendix
A
an alternative proof for this case is given, which follows very closely the standard indirect proof.

A popular class of finite element methods for discretizing the Stokes problem is the generalized Taylor-Hood family
P_{k}
–
P_{k-1}
on triangular/tetrahedral meshes with continuous approximations for both velocity and pressure. The discrete analog of (
1
), the discrete LBB condition, which is essential for the stability of the discretization and for error estimates, is well understood for this family, see
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
, and also
[
5
]
for additional references.

In this paper we focus on the related generalized Taylor-Hood family
Q_{k}
–
Q_{k-1}
on quadrilateral/hexahedral meshes under the following assumptions.

(i)

\Omega
is a polygonal/polyhedral domain, i.e., we do not consider domains with curved boundaries.

(ii)

\mathcal{T}_{h}
is a subdivision of
\Omega
into convex quadrilaterals/hexahedrons
K
, which satisfies the standard compatibility conditions.

(iii)

Each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is of the form

K=F_{K}(\hat{K})

with the reference element
\hat{K}=[0,1]^{d}
, bijective and continuously differentiable mappings
F_{K}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
for each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and the following standard shape regularity conditions. There are positive constants
c_{i}
such that

c_{1}\,h_{K}^{d}\leq|\det J_{K}({\hat{x}})|\leq c_{2}\,h_{K}^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{3}\,h_{K}^{2}\,I_{d}\leq J_{K}({\hat{x}})^{\top}J_{K}({\hat{x}})\leq c_{4}\,h_{K}^{2}\,I_{d}

for all
{\hat{x}}\in\hat{K}
with the identity matrix
I_{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here
h_{K}
denotes the diameter of
K
,
J_{K}
denotes the Jacobian of
F_{K}
with determinant
\det J_{K}
, for which we assume that
\det J_{K}({\hat{x}})>0
. The mesh size of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is introduced by
h=\max\{h_{K}\colon K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
.

(iv)

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, at least one of its vertices does not lie on
\Gamma_{D}
. This excludes only some very degenerate meshes.

The family of generalized Taylor-Hood elements is given by the following pairs of finite element spaces for
k\geq 2
:

\displaystyle V_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in V\colon v\circ F_{K}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d}\ \text{for all}\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

(2)

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{q\in Q\cap C(\bar{\Omega})\colon q\circ F_{K}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})\ \text{for all}\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}

with the following standard notation of polynomial spaces: Let
P_{k}(D)

denote the set of polynomials on
D
with total degree
\leq k
,
and let
P_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(D)
denote the set of polynomials on
D
with degree
\leq k_{i}
with respect to the
i^{\text{th}}
variable, for
i=1,\ldots,d
. Finally, we set
Q_{k}(D)=P_{k,\ldots,k}(D)
.

The discrete LBB condition associated to these finite element spaces reads as follows:

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

(3)

Here and throughout the paper we use the following notation:
A\lesssim B
iff there is a constant
c>0
such that
A\leq c\,B
,
A\gtrsim B
iff
B\lesssim A
, and
A\sim B
iff
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
. The involved constants are meant to be independent of the mesh size
h
(or the element size
h_{K}
for local estimates on some element
K
), but may depend on
k
and the shape regularity constants.

A natural condition for the local mappings
F_{K}
is

F_{K}\in Q_{1}(\hat{K})^{d}\quad\text{for all}\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

(4)

in order to allow for general quadrilateral/hexahedral meshes on polygonal/polyhedral domains.
Under Condition (
4
) the discrete LBB condition was shown for
d=2
in
[
14
]
.
We are not aware of any valid proof of the discrete LBB condition for the Taylor-Hood elements for
d=3
under Condition (
4
). However, the proof of the discrete LBB condition in
[
14
]
extends to
d=3
under the stronger condition
F_{K}\in P_{1}(\hat{K})^{3}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
A consequence of this stronger restriction is that for
d=3
only meshes of parallelepipeds are covered.
The discrete LBB condition could also be shown for the isogeometric generalized Taylor-Hood family, see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
.
The proof there relies on a continuously differentiable parametrization of the domain
\Omega
on each of a fixed number of patches, which does not cover general quadrilateral/hexahedral meshes.

In this paper we will stick to the same conditions as needed in
[
14
]
, namely

F_{K}\in\begin{cases}Q_{1}(\hat{K})^{2}&\quad\text{for}\ d=2,\\
P_{1}(\hat{K})^{3}&\quad\text{for}\ d=3\end{cases}

(5)

for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
since we rely as in
[
14
]
on the exact representation of the bilinear form
b(v,q)
on
V_{h}\times Q_{h}
by the composite Gauss-Lobatto quadrature rule. In more details, we have

b(v,q)=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}b_{K}(v,q)

with

\displaystyle b_{K}(v,q)

\displaystyle=-\int_{K}q\,\operatorname{div}v\ dx=\int_{K}v\cdot\nabla q\ dx-\int_{\partial K}v\cdot n\,q\ d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{K}}\hat{v}_{K}\cdot J_{K}^{-\top}\nabla\hat{q}_{K}\,\det J_{K}\ d{\hat{x}}-\int_{\partial\hat{K}}\hat{v}_{K}\cdot J_{K}^{-\top}\hat{n}_{K}\,\hat{q}_{K}\,\det J_{K}\ d\hat{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{K}}\hat{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J_{K})\nabla\hat{q}_{K}\ d{\hat{x}}-\int_{\partial\hat{K}}\hat{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J_{K})\hat{n}_{K}\,\hat{q}_{K}\ d{\hat{\sigma}}

(6)

for all
v\in H^{1}(K)^{d}
and
q\in H^{1}(K)

with
\hat{v}_{K}=v\circ F_{K}
,
\hat{q}_{K}=q\circ F_{K}
, and the outward unit normal vector
\hat{n}_{K}
to
\partial\hat{K}
.
Here we use the
(\det J)\,J^{-\top}=\operatorname{cof}(J)
, where
\operatorname{cof}(J)
denotes the cofactor matrix of a matrix
J
.
By elementary calculations it can be shown that Condition (
5
) implies that

\hat{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J_{K})\nabla\hat{q}_{K}\in Q_{2k-1}(\hat{K}),\quad\hat{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J_{K})\hat{n}_{K}\,\hat{q}_{K}\in Q_{2k-1}(\hat{f})

for all
\hat{v}_{K}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d}
,
\hat{q}_{K}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})
, and on each edge/face
\hat{f}\subset\partial\hat{K}
. These conditions allow to represent the integrals in (
6
)
for
\hat{v}_{K}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d}
and
\hat{q}_{K}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})

by the tensor product
(k+1)
-point Gauss-Lobatto quadrature rule exactly. In the case
d=3

the weaker Condition (
4
) only implies that
\hat{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J_{K})\nabla\hat{q}_{K}\in Q_{2k}(\hat{K})
,
\hat{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J_{K})\hat{n}_{K}\,\hat{q}_{K}\in Q_{2k}(\hat{f})
and one loses the exact representation by the tensor product
(k+1)
-point Gauss-Lobatto quadrature, in general.

In this paper we present an alternative proof for the discrete LBB condition, which covers not only the case of pure Dirichlet conditions as it was previously done but also mixed boundary conditions and pure Neumann boundary conditions.

Moreover, we will also study a second type of discrete inf-sup condition:

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{0}}\gtrsim\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

(7)

This condition was discussed by Bercovier and Pironneau in
[
2
]
, which turned out as an enabler of (
3
), see
[
15
]
. We will refer to this inf-sup condition as the discrete BP condition.
In
[
2
]
the proof was given for
k=2
and for meshes made of rectangles for
d=2
and bricks for
d=3
. We will extend this result for all
k\geq 2
and all meshes satisfying Condition (
5
).
Our interest in (
7
) is not its role for showing the discrete LBB condition but its role in the analysis of the smoothing property of some multigrid method for the Stokes problem, see
[
12
]
.

2
T
-coercivity

A standard technique to show a discrete inf-sup condition is the macroelement technique, see
[
14
]
.
A macroelement
M
is a connected set of (a few) elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. An essential step of this technique is the verification of an inf-sup condition for pairs
V_{M}
,
Q_{M}
of local finite element spaces associated to a collection of macroelements
M
which cover
\Omega
.

In this paper we will consider instead the pairs

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{K}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in H^{1}(K)^{d}\colon v\circ F_{K}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d},\ v=0\ \text{on}\ \partial K\cap\Gamma_{D}\right\},

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{K}

\displaystyle=\left\{q\in C(K)\colon q\circ F_{K}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})\right\}

associated to each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
as local counterparts of the spaces

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in H_{0,\Gamma_{D}}^{1}(\Omega)^{d}\colon v\circ F_{K}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d}\ \text{for all}\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{h}

\displaystyle=\left\{q\in C(\bar{\Omega})\colon q\circ F_{K}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})\ \text{for all}\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}.

Note that the spaces
\bar{V}_{h}
,
\bar{Q}_{h}
coincide with the spaces
V_{h}
,
Q_{h}
up to the scaling conditions for pure Dirichlet and pure Neumann boundary conditions.

The following analysis was stimulated by the discussion in
[
16
]
, where Taylor-Hood elements for
k=2
on meshes of mixed type consisting of hexahedrons, prisms, pyramids, and tetrahedrons are considered, and where it is claimed that, for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, there is a linear mapping
T_{K}\colon\bar{Q}_{K}\to\bar{V}_{K}
such that

b_{K}(T_{K}q,q)\gtrsim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T_{K}q\|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}

for all
q\in\bar{Q}_{K}
, see
[
16
, Lemma 3]
.
In literature such a property is often called
T
-coercivity.
The proof was given for
k=2
and only on reference elements
\hat{K}
.

Here this result is extended to all
k\geq 2
and all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
but only for quadrilateral/hexahedral elements.

Lemma 1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, let
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for
K
.
Then there is a linear mapping
T_{K}\colon\bar{Q}_{K}\to\bar{V}_{K}
such that

b_{K}(T_{K}q,q)\gtrsim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}

and

\|T_{K}q\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K},\quad\|T_{K}q\|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}

for all
q\in\bar{Q}_{K}
.

Proof.

We will restrict ourselves to the case
d=3
, the case

First of all, we introduce a series of notation related to the local degrees of freedom of
\hat{v}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d}
and
v\in\bar{V}_{K}
(for the analysis only, not for the implementation).

•

Let
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
be the set of all faces of
\hat{T}
. We have
\hat{\mathcal{F}}=\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{1,2}\cup\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{1,3}\cup\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{2,3}
with the pairwise disjoint sets
\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{i,j}

\hat{K}
which are parallel to the
{\hat{x}}_{i}
-axis and the
{\hat{x}}_{j}
-axis.
Let
\hat{\mathcal{E}}
be the set of all edges of
\hat{T}

\hat{\mathcal{E}}=\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\cup\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\cup\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{3}

\hat{\mathcal{E}}_{i}

\hat{K}

\hat{K}

\hat{\mathcal{V}}

•

As nodal degrees of freedom for the set

\hat{K}

The sets

•

As nodal degrees of freedom for

\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K)

\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j})

•

Finally, to indicate that nodal points on

are excluded we write
\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(.)
instead of
\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(.)
.

Let
q\in\bar{Q}_{K}
. The nodal values of the element

are fixed on

as follows.

v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}=\begin{cases}\left(J\nabla\hat{q}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K),\\[8.61108pt]
\left(J_{i}\,\partial_{i}\hat{q}+J_{j}\,\partial_{j}\hat{q}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j}),\\[8.61108pt]
\left(J_{i}\,\partial_{i}\hat{q}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i}),\\[8.61108pt]
0&\quad\text{for all other}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K),\end{cases}

with
{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}=F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{a}})
,

By using the property

\left(\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})=\begin{cases}\left(\det J\,\|\nabla\hat{q}\|^{2}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K),\\[8.61108pt]
\left(\det J\,(|\partial_{i}\hat{q}|^{2}+|\partial_{j}\hat{q}|^{2})\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j}),\\[8.61108pt]
\left(\det J\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}|^{2}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i}),\\[8.61108pt]

and

\left(\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\hat{n}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})=0\quad\text{for all}\ {\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\partial\hat{K},

with
\hat{v}=v\circ F
.

By using Condition (
5
),
estimates based on the exactness of the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature (see Appendix
B

and
\det J\sim h_{K}^{3}
it follows that

\displaystyle b_{K}(v,q)=-\int_{K}q\,\operatorname{div}v\ dx=\int_{\hat{K}}\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q}\ d{\hat{x}}\sim\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}(\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q})({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

\displaystyle\quad\sim\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K)}\left(\det J\,\|\nabla\hat{q}\|^{2}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j})\\
i,j\in\{1,2,3\},i<j\end{subarray}}\left(\det J\,(|\partial_{i}\hat{q}|^{2}+|\partial_{j}\hat{q}|^{2})\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\qquad{}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i})\\
i\in\{1,2,3\}\end{subarray}}\left(\det J\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}|^{2}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

\displaystyle\quad\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\underbrace{\Big(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j})\\
i,j\in\{1,2,3\},i<j\end{subarray}}\left(|\partial_{i}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}+|\partial_{j}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i})\\
i\in\{1,2,3\}\end{subarray}}|\partial_{i}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\Big)}_{\displaystyle=:|\hat{q}|_{A}^{2}}.

(8)

For the
L^{2}
-norm of
\nabla q
on
K
we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim h_{K}\,\|\nabla\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2}\sim h_{K}\,\underbrace{\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}}_{\displaystyle=:|\hat{q}|_{B}^{2}}.

(9)

Note that
|.|_{A}
and
|.|_{B}
are semi-norms on
Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})
.
Next we will study the kernels of these two semi-norms.
From the assumptions on
K
it follows that at least one of the vertices of
K
does not lie on the boundary part
\Gamma_{D}
. Without loss of generality we assume this vertex corresponds to the node
{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}=(0,0,0)
.
Then all nodes from the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}

\displaystyle=\{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}=(a_{k_{1}},a_{k_{2}},a_{k_{3}})\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})\colon 1\leq k_{1}\leq k-1,\ 0\leq k_{2}\leq k-1,\ 0\leq k_{3}\leq k-1\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{2}

\displaystyle=\{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}=(a_{k_{1}},a_{k_{2}},a_{k_{3}})\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})\colon 0\leq k_{1}\leq k-1,\ 1\leq k_{2}\leq k-1,\ 0\leq k_{3}\leq k-1\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{3}

\displaystyle=\{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}=(a_{k_{1}},a_{k_{2}},a_{k_{3}})\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})\colon 0\leq k_{1}\leq k-1,\ 0\leq k_{2}\leq k-1,\ 1\leq k_{3}\leq k-1\}

correspond to nodes in the interior of
K
, or in the interior of some face, or in the interior of some edge of
K
, but none of them corresponds to nodes on the boundary
\Gamma_{D}
.

Assume now that
|\hat{q}|_{A}=0
for some
\hat{q}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})
.
Then

\partial_{1}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})=0\quad\text{for all}\ {\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{1}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\in P_{k-2,k-1,k-1}(\hat{K}).

Since
\mathcal{N}_{1}
is a contiguous block of
(k-1)\cdot k\cdot k
nodes, where the function
\partial_{1}\hat{q}\in P_{k-1,k,k}(\hat{K})
vanishes,
it immediately follows that
\partial_{1}\hat{q}
vanishes everywhere. The same arguments apply to
\partial_{2}\hat{q}
and
\partial_{3}\hat{q}
with the sets
\mathcal{N}_{2}
and
\mathcal{N}_{3}
, respectively, which eventually show that
\nabla\hat{q}=0
, i.e.,
\hat{q}
is constant.
So the kernel of the semi-norm
|.|_{A}
is
Q_{0}(\hat{K})
. It is obvious that the kernel of the semi-norm
|.|_{B}
is
Q_{0}(\hat{K})
, too.
Since both semi-norms are defined on the finite-dimensional space
Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})
and have the same kernel, they are equivalent. Therefore, we immediately obtain from the equivalence of the two semi-norms, (
8
), and (
9
) that

b_{K}(v,q)\sim h_{K}^{3}\,|\hat{q}|_{A}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,|\hat{q}|_{B}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}.

In order to estimate the norms of
v
observe that

\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{0,K}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\|\hat{v}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}^{5}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}=h_{K}^{5}\,|\hat{q}|_{B}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{4}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{1,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}\,\|\hat{v}\|_{1,\hat{K}}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim h_{K}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}=h_{K}^{3}\,|\hat{q}|_{B}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

From this local result on

we easily obtain the following global version.

Corollary 1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then there is a linear mapping
T_{h}\colon\bar{Q}_{h}\to\bar{V}_{h}
such that

b(T_{h}q_{h},q_{h})\gtrsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}

and

\|T_{h}q_{h}\|_{0}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{4}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad\|T_{h}q_{h}\|_{1}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

for all
q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}
.

Proof.

Let

.
It is easy to see that the definition of the nodal values of
v
in the proof of Lemma

on common vertices, edges and faces of neighboring elements is consistent. This results in a well-defined and continuous finite element function

for which we have

\displaystyle b(v_{h},q_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}b_{K}(T_{K}(q_{h}|_{K}),q_{h})\sim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}

(10)

and

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{4}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}

\displaystyle\|v_{h}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v_{h}\|_{1,K}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}.

∎

The results in Lemma
1
and Corollary
1
suffice to discuss the discrete inf-sup conditions for the case (a) of pure Dirichlet boundary conditions. If
|\Gamma_{N}|>0
, we need an additional result, for which we introduce the following notation

{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}=\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\colon f\subset\Gamma_{N}\ \text{for some face}\ f\ \text{of}\ K\}.

For
K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}
, the set of all faces of
K
which lie on
\Gamma^{N}
are denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{N}(K)
.
The set
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}^{N})
is formed by all nodes (as introduced in the proof of Lemma
1
) on faces from
\mathcal{F}^{N}(K)
.
The set
\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})
is formed by all nodes in the (relative) interior of faces from
\mathcal{F}^{N}(K)
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, let
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for
K
.
Then there is a linear mapping
T_{K}^{N}\colon\bar{Q}_{K}\to\bar{V}_{K}
such that

b_{K}(T_{K}^{N}q,q)\gtrsim h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}-h_{K}^{3/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}

and

\|T_{K}^{N}q\|_{1,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{1/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}

for all
q\in\bar{Q}_{K}
,
with the (
L^{2}
-like) semi-norm
\vvvert.\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}
given by

\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}=h_{K}^{d-1}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}.

Proof.

We will restrict ourselves to the case
d=3
, the case
d=2
is a simple adaptation of the arguments used for
d=3
and is omitted.

Let
q\in\bar{Q}_{K}
. The nodal values of the element
v=T_{K}^{N}q\in\bar{V}_{K}
are fixed on
K\in\mathcal{T}
as follows.

v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}=\begin{cases}-\left(\hat{q}\,J\hat{n}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N}),\\[4.30554pt]
0&\quad\text{for all other}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K),\end{cases}

with
{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}=F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{a}})
,
\hat{q}=q\circ F
, the Jacobian
J
of
F
.

By using the property
J^{\top}\operatorname{cof}(J)=(\det J)\,I_{d}
, it is easy to see that

\left(\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})=\begin{cases}-\left(\det J\,(\hat{q}\,\hat{n}\cdot\nabla\hat{q})\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N}),\\[4.30554pt]
0&\quad\text{for all other}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K),\end{cases}

and

-\left(\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\hat{n}\,q\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})=\begin{cases}\left(\det J\,|\hat{q}|^{2}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})&\quad\text{for}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N}),\\[4.30554pt]
0&\quad\text{for all other}\ {\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K),\end{cases}

with
\hat{v}=v\circ F
.

We have

\displaystyle b_{K}(v,q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{K}}\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q}\ d{\hat{x}}-\int_{\partial\hat{K}\cap\Gamma_{N}}\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\hat{n}\,\hat{q}\ d{\hat{\sigma}}.

From estimates based on the exactness of the Gauss-Lobatto quadrature (see Appendix
B
for details),
and
\det J\sim h_{K}^{3}
, it follows that

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial\hat{K}\cap\Gamma_{N}}\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\hat{n}\,\hat{q}\ d{\hat{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\left(\det J\,|\hat{q}|^{2}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

\displaystyle\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}=h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert^{2}.

(11)

Since
\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q}\in Q_{2k-1}
, and
\det J\sim h_{K}^{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\hat{K}}\hat{v}\cdot\operatorname{cof}(J)\nabla\hat{q}\ d{\hat{x}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\left(\det J\,|\hat{q}|\,\|\nabla\hat{q}\|\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

\displaystyle\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\left(|\hat{q}|\,\|\nabla\hat{q}\|\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

\displaystyle\leq h_{K}^{3}\,\Big(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=h_{K}^{2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert\,\Big(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

(12)

where the last estimate follows from the Cauchy inequality.
Since

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}\leq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}=|\hat{q}|_{B}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{-1}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2},

(13)

it follows from (
11
), (
12
), (
13
) that

\displaystyle b_{K}(v,q)\gtrsim h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}-h_{K}^{3/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}.

In order to estimate the norm of
v
observe that

\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{2}\,|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{1,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}\,\|\hat{v}\|_{1,\hat{K}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}=h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

.

b(T_{h}^{N}q_{h},q_{h})\gtrsim\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

and

\|T_{h}^{N}q_{h}\|_{1}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

for all
q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}

Proof.

Let
q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}

It is easy to see that the definition of the nodal values of

in the proof of Lemma
2

v_{h}=T_{h}q_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}\quad\text{with}\quad v_{h}|_{K}=T_{K}(q_{h}|_{K}),

for which we have

\displaystyle b(v_{h},q_{h})

3
Discrete inf-sup conditions

3.1
The discrete LBB condition

We start the discussion with an element-wise version of the discrete LBB condition, for which we need the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, let
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for
K
.
Then

\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1,K}}\gtrsim h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in\bar{Q}_{K}.

If, additionally,
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}
, then

\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1,K}}\gtrsim\Big(h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\,\|q\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in\bar{Q}_{K}.

Proof.

The first estimate follows immediately from Lemma
1
:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1,K}}\geq\frac{b_{K}(T_{K}q,q)}{\|T_{K}q\|_{1,K}}\gtrsim\frac{h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}}{h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}}=h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}.

(14)

If
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}
, then we have by Lemma
2

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1,K}}\geq\frac{b_{K}(T_{K}^{N}q,q)}{\|T_{K}^{N}q\|_{1,K}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{h_{K}\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}-h_{K}^{3/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}}{h_{K}^{1/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}}

\displaystyle=h_{K}^{1/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}-h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}.

(15)

From (
14
) and (
15
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1,K}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim h_{K}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{1/2}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in\bar{Q}_{K}.

It remains to show that

h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\,\|q\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}.

Now we have

\displaystyle h_{K}\,\|q\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}

\displaystyle=h_{K}\,\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{N}(K)}\|q\|_{0,f}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{N}(K)}\|\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{f}}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}.

Together with (
9
) we obtain

\displaystyle h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\,\|q\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\underbrace{\Big[\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}+\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\Big]}_{\displaystyle=:\|\hat{q}_{h}\|_{C}^{2}}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}+h_{K}\,\vvvert q\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\underbrace{\Big[\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}+\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(\mathcal{F}^{N})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\Big]}_{\displaystyle=:\|\hat{q}_{h}\|_{D}^{2}}.

Since both
\|.\|_{C}
and
\|.\|_{D}
are norms on the finite-dimensional space
Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})
, they are equivalent, which completes the proof.
∎

From Lemma
3
the element-wise version of the discrete LBB condition easily follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, let
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for
K
.
Then

\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1,K}}\gtrsim\|q\|_{0,K}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in\bar{Q}_{K}\ \text{with}\ \int_{K}q\ dx=0.

If, additionally,
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}
, then

\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{1,K}}\gtrsim\|q\|_{0,K}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in\bar{Q}_{K}.

Proof.

The estimates follow immediately from Lemma
3
and the Friedrichs/Poincaré-type inequalities

\displaystyle\|q\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{-d}\,\Big(\int_{K}q(x)\ dx\Big)^{2}+h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\|q\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}\,\|q\|_{0,f}^{2}+h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2},

where
f
is some face of
K
with
f\subset\Gamma_{N}
.
The proofs of these estimates are analogous to the proof of Lemma 1.1.3 in
[
13
]
.
∎

For the global version of the discrete LBB condition the following global version of Lemma

Corollary 3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for all

.
Then

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\|q_{h}\|_{\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}.

Proof.

The proof follows closely the proof on Lemma
3
.
By Corollary
1
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\geq\frac{b(T_{h}q_{h},q_{h})}{\|T_{h}q_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\frac{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}}{\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}}=\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(16)

By Corollary
2
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\geq\frac{b(T_{h}^{N}q_{h},q_{h})}{\|T_{h}^{N}q_{h}\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\gtrsim\frac{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}}{\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}-\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

(17)

From (
16
) and (
17
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\vvvert q_{h}\vvvert_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\|q_{h}\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}.

∎

In order to continue we apply the so-called Verfürth trick. We need a slightly modified variant compared to
[

,
14
,
16
]

Lemma 4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, connected and open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary. Let
V_{h}\subset V\subset H_{0,\Gamma_{D}}^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
and
Q_{h}\subset Q\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
with
Q\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be closed subspaces.
Assume that the LBB condition is satisfied on
V
and
Q
and that there exists a linear operator
\Pi_{h}\colon V\to V_{h}

such that

\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{-2}\,\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}^{-1}\,\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim\|v\|_{1}

and

\|\Pi_{h}v\|_{1}\lesssim\|v\|_{1}\quad\text{for all}\ v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}.

Then

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}-\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\|q_{h}\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
. From the continuous LBB condition it follows that there exists a
\bar{v}\in V
with

\frac{b(\bar{v},q_{h})}{\|\bar{v}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}.

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{b(\Pi_{h}\bar{v},q_{h})}{\|\Pi_{h}\bar{v}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\frac{b(\Pi_{h}\bar{v},q_{h})}{\|\bar{v}\|_{1}}=\frac{b(\bar{v},q_{h})}{\|\bar{v}\|_{1}}+\frac{b(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v},q_{h})}{\|\bar{v}\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}+\frac{b(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v},q_{h})}{\|\bar{v}\|_{1}}.

Now we have

\displaystyle b(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v},q_{h})=\int_{\Omega}\nabla q_{h}\cdot(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v})\ dx-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}q_{h}\,(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v})\cdot n\ d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla q_{h}\cdot(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v})\ dx-\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}\int_{\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}q_{h}\,(\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v})\cdot n\ d\sigma

\displaystyle\quad\geq-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}\,\|\bar{v}-\Pi_{h}\bar{v}\|_{0,K}-\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}\,\|\Pi_{h}\bar{v}-\bar{v}\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq-\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\|q_{h}\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{-2}\,\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}^{-1}\,\|v-\Pi_{h}v\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\gtrsim-\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\|q_{h}\|_{0,\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,\|v\|_{1}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}-\Big(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{N}}}h_{K}\,\|q_{h}\|_{\partial K\cap\Gamma_{N}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

∎

Finally, we obtain

Theorem 2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}

for
V_{h}
and
Q_{h}
given by (
2
).

Proof.

For the linear operator
\Pi_{h}
in Lemma
4
we choose a Scott-Zhang operator. Then Lemma
4
and Corollary

easily imply the following discrete inf-sup condition

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{1}}\gtrsim\|q_{h}\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}.

(18)

Then the discrete LBB condition easily follows for the case (a) of pure Dirichlet boundary conditions and case (b) of mixed boundary conditions, since

and

in these cases.

For the case (c) of pure Neumann boundary conditions it follows from (

and

3.2
The discrete BP condition

Next we will discuss inf-sup conditions for a different pair of norms. First we show a local version of
[
2
, Proposition 1]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, let
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for
K
.
Then

\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{0,K}}\gtrsim\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in\bar{Q}_{K}.

Proof.

By using Lemma
1
we obtain

\sup_{0\neq v\in\bar{V}_{K}}\frac{b_{K}(v,q)}{\|v\|_{0,K}}\geq\frac{b_{K}(T_{K}q,q)}{\|T_{K}q\|_{0,K}}\gtrsim\frac{h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}}{h_{K}^{2}\,\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}}=\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}.

∎

The next result is a global version of Theorem
3
and can be seen as an extension of
[
2
, Proposition 1]
, where the proof was given for
k=2
and for meshes made of rectangles for
d=2
and bricks for
d=3
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
and assume that Condition (
5
) holds for all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Then

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in V_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{0}}\gtrsim\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

Proof.

We will restrict ourselves to the case
d=3
, the case
d=2
is a simple adaptation of the arguments used for
d=3
and is omitted.

In a first step we show the discrete BP condition in spaces without scaling conditions, i.e.,

\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})}{\|v_{h}\|_{0}}\gtrsim\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0}\quad\text{for all}\ q_{h}\in\bar{Q}_{h}.

(19)

Let
\mathcal{N}_{h}
be the set of all nodes of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
let
v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}
and
\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}
be the nodal value and the nodal basis function associated to a node
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
, respectively.
Then

\|v_{h}\|_{0}^{2}\sim\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}\quad\text{for}\ v_{h}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\,\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}.

For

it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})^{2}}{\|v_{h}\|_{0}^{2}}\sim\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{\left[\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\,\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})\right]^{2}}{\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\,\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\sup_{0\neq v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})^{2}}{\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}}.

We discuss three cases for the node
{\boldsymbol{a}}
.

1.

Assume that
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K)
for some
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
By setting

v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}=\left(J\nabla\hat{q}_{h}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

we obtain as in the proof of Lemma
1

b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})=b_{K}(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})\sim\,h_{K}^{3}\,\|\nabla\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}

and

\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}=\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{3}\,\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{5}\,\|\nabla\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2},

which implies

\sup_{0\neq v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})^{2}}{\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}}\gtrsim h_{K}\,\|\nabla\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}.

2.

Next assume that
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i})
for some
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
By setting

v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}=\left(J_{i}\,\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}\right)({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

we obtain as in the proof of Lemma
1

b_{K}(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})\sim\,h_{K}^{3}\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}

and

\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{5}\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}.

Analogous estimates follow for all other elements
K^{\prime}
with
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in K^{\prime}
. This leads to

b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})}b_{K}(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})\sim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})}h_{K}^{3}\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}

and

\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})}\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})}h_{K}^{5}\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2},

with
\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})=\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\colon{\boldsymbol{a}}\in K\}
.
It easily follows that

\sup_{0\neq v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})^{2}}{\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}^{2}\|_{0}}\gtrsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})}h_{K}\,|\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}.

3.

Finally, assume that
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j})
for some
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Then it follows completely analogously to the previous case that

\sup_{0\neq v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{b(v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}},q_{h})^{2}}{\|v^{({\boldsymbol{a}})}\varphi_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\|_{0}^{2}}\gtrsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}({\boldsymbol{a}})}h_{K}\,\left(|\partial_{i}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}+|\partial_{j}\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\right).

In summary, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v_{h}\in\bar{V}_{h}}\frac{b(v_{h},q_{h})^{2}}{\|v_{h}\|_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}\,\bigg(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}(K)}\|\nabla\hat{q}_{h}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{i,j})\\
i,j\in\{1,2,3\},i<j\end{subarray}}\left(|\partial_{i}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}+|\partial_{j}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad{}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathcal{N}_{\text{int}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i})\\
i\in\{1,2,3\}\end{subarray}}|\partial_{i}\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}\bigg)\sim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}=\|\nabla q_{h}\|_{0}^{2},

which completes the proof of (
19
).

Then the discrete BP condition easily follows for the case (a) of pure Dirichlet boundary conditions and the case (b) of mixed boundary conditions, since
V_{h}=\bar{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}\subset\bar{Q}_{h}
in these cases. The discrete BP condition for the case (c) of pure Neumann boundary conditions can be shown completely analogously as for the discrete LBB condition in Theorem

.
∎

4
Conclusions and open problems

Previous work on the discrete LBB condition of the Stokes problem for the generalized Taylor-Hood family
Q_{k}
–
Q_{k-1}
on quadrilateral/hexahedral meshes focused on the case of pure Dirichlet boundary conditions. In this paper we extended the analysis to the case, where ”do-nothing” boundary conditions are prescribed on part of the boundary or on the whole boundary.
This was one of two aims of the paper. A second aim was the analysis of the discrete BP condition, which plays an important role in the analysis of the smoothing property of some multigrid method for the discretized Stokes problem.

From the technical point of view the major difference to previous work is the element-wise approach as opposed to the widely used macroelement technique. The element-wise approach allowed to derive element-wise discrete LBB and BP conditions.

The present paper suffers from the same rather severe restrictions on hexahedral meshes in 3D as in previous work. The analysis of discrete inf-sup conditions for general hexahedral meshes remains an open problem. Another open problem is the analysis of isoparametric generalized Taylor-Hood families in 2D and 3D to cope with curved boundaries. Perturbation arguments similar to those used in
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
for isogeometric generalized Taylor-Hood families seem to be a promising approach for this open problem.

Appendix A
The continuous LBB condition

The LBB condition (
1
) can be rewritten as

\|q\|_{0}\leq c\,\|\nabla_{V}\,q\|_{V^{*}}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in Q

with
c=1/\beta
, the linear functional

\langle\nabla_{V}\,q,v\rangle:=-(q,\operatorname{div}v)_{0}\quad\text{for}\ v\in V,

and the dual norm

\|\nabla_{V}\,q\|_{V^{*}}=\sup_{0\neq v\in V}\frac{\langle\nabla_{V}\,q,v\rangle}{\|v\|_{1}}.

If
V=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
the linear functional
\nabla_{V}\,q
can be identified with the gradient of
q
in the distributional sense. In this case we omit the subscript
V
and simply write
\nabla q
.

We have the following important inequality:

Lemma 5

(Nečas)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary. Then there exists a constant
c_{N}>0
, such that

\|q\|_{0}\leq c_{N}\,(\|q\|_{-1}+\|\nabla q\|_{-1}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in L^{2}(\Omega).

(20)

Proof.

See
[
13
]
, under stronger assumptions also
[
8
]
.
∎

The LBB condition for
V=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
and
Q=L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
follows by standard compactness arguments.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, connected and open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary. Then there exists a constant
c>0
, such that

\|q\|_{0}\leq c\,\|\nabla q\|_{-1}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega).

(21)

For later reference we recall the well-known proof.

Proof.

Assume the inequality (
21
) is not valid. Then there exists a sequence
(q_{k})
in
L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
with
\|q_{k}\|_{0}=1
and

\|\nabla q_{k}\|_{-1}\to 0
.
Since the embedding
i\colon H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\longrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact, the (adjoint) embedding
i^{*}\colon L^{2}(\Omega)\longrightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
is also compact.
Therefore, there exists a convergent sub-sequence
(q_{k}^{\prime})
in

H^{-1}(\Omega)
. From (
20
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|q_{k}^{\prime}-q_{\ell}^{\prime}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq c_{N}\,(\|q_{k}^{\prime}-q_{\ell}^{\prime}\|_{-1}+\|\nabla(q_{k}^{\prime}-q_{\ell}^{\prime})\|_{-1})

\displaystyle\leq c_{N}\,(\|q_{k}^{\prime}-q_{\ell}^{\prime}\|_{-1}+\|\nabla q_{k}^{\prime}\|_{-1}+\|\nabla q_{\ell}^{\prime}\|_{-1})\to 0,

if
k,\ell\to\infty
. So
(q_{k}^{\prime})
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and, therefore,
q_{k}^{\prime}\to q
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for some
q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

For

we have:

\displaystyle|(q,\operatorname{div}v)_{0}|=\lim_{k\to\infty}|(q_{k}^{\prime},\operatorname{div}v)_{0}|=\lim_{k\to\infty}|\langle\nabla q_{k}^{\prime},v\rangle|\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla q_{k}^{\prime}\|_{-1}\,\|v\|_{1}=0.

Hence

(q,\operatorname{div}v)_{0}=0\quad\text{for all}\ v\in\left(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{d},

i.e., the generalized gradient of
q
vanishes, which implies that
q
is constant.
Since
q\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
it follows
q=0
, in contradiction to
\|q\|_{0}=\|q_{k}\|_{0}=1
.
∎

(22)

Assume the inequality (

(q_{k})
in

with
\|q_{k}\|_{0}=1
and
\|\nabla_{V}\,q_{k}\|_{V^{*}}\to 0
. Since

As in the proof of Theorem it follows that

Lemma 6

Under the assumptions of Theorem

]

, such that

\|q\|_{0}\leq c\,\|\nabla_{V}\,q\|_{V^{*}}\quad\text{for all}\ q\in L^{2}(\Omega).

The LBB condition for this case of pure Neumann boundary conditions can be shown completely analogously as for the discrete LBB condition, see Theorem
2
, and is omitted.

Appendix B
Some useful relations

For the convenience of the reader we recall several useful relations, which are used throughout the paper.

Norms under the change of variables

For all
q\in H^{1}(K)
:

\|\nabla q\|_{0,K}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{d-2}\,\|\nabla\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2},\quad\|q\|_{0,f}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{d-1}\,\|\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{f}}

For all
v\in H^{1}(K)^{d}
:

\displaystyle\|v\|_{0,K}^{2}\sim h_{K}^{d}\,\|\hat{v}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2},\quad\|v\|_{1,K}^{2}\lesssim h_{K}^{d-2}\,\|\hat{v}\|_{1,\hat{K}}^{2}

Norms in terms of nodal values

For all
\hat{q}\in Q_{k-1}(\hat{K})
:

\displaystyle\|\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2}\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2},\quad\|\nabla\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2}\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})}\|\nabla\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2},\quad\|\hat{q}\|_{0,\hat{f}}^{2}\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{f})}|\hat{q}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|^{2}

For all
\hat{v}\in Q_{k}(\hat{K})^{d}
:

\displaystyle\|\hat{v}\|_{0,\hat{K}}^{2}\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})}\|\hat{v}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2},\quad\|\hat{v}\|_{1,\hat{K}}^{2}\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})}\|\hat{v}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\|^{2}

Relations based on Gauss-Lobatto quadrature

For all
\hat{g}\in Q_{2k-1}(\hat{K})
with
\hat{g}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\geq 0
for all
{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{K}}\hat{g}\ d\hat{x}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{K})}\hat{g}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

For all
\hat{g}\in Q_{2k-1}(\hat{f})
with
\hat{g}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})\geq 0
for all
{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{f})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{f}}\hat{g}\ d\hat{\sigma}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{f})}\hat{g}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})

For all
\hat{g}\in Q_{2k-1}(\hat{f})
:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\hat{f}}\hat{g}\ d\hat{\sigma}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathcal{N}(\hat{f})}|\hat{g}({\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}})|

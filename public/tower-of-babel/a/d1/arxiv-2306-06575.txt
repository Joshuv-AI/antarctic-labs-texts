1.
Introduction

Let
f
be a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism on a compact Riemannian manifold
M
. A
physical measure
of
f
is an invariant Borel probability
\mu
on
M
for which there exists a positive Lebesgue measure set of points
x\in M
such that for any continuous
\varphi:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
one has

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varphi(f^{i}(x))=\int\varphi d\mu.

The set of points
{\mathcal{B}}(\mu,f)
for which the above convergence holds is called the basin of
\mu
w.r.t.
f
. Physical measures were firstly discovered by
Sinai, Ruelle and Bowen in uniformly hyperbolic systems
[
18
,
17
,
4
,
5
]
.
In smooth ergodic theory, the physical measures are usually produced from
Sinai-Ruelle-Bowen (SRB)
measures.
One says that an invariant measure is an SRB measure if it admits positive Lyapunov exponents almost everywhere, and its conditional measures along Pesin unstable manifolds are absolutely continuous w.r.t Lebesgue measures on these manifolds.
It is well known that every ergodic SRB measure with non-zero Lyapunov exponents is a physical measure. It remains to be a changeable problem in finding physical measures for systems beyond hyperbolicity ( see
[
2
, Chapter 11]
and
[
15
,
20
,
22
]
for related conjectures ).

In this paper, we are interested in studying physical (SRB) measures for partially hyperbolic systems (see
\lx@sectionsign

2.1
for more details) under the hyperbolicity of
Gibbs
u
-states
. By Gibbs
u
-state, we mean an invariant measure whose disintegration along strong unstable manifolds are absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue measures on these manifolds.
The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
f
be a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism with a partially hyperbolic splitting

TM=E^{u}\oplus_{\succ}E_{1}^{c}\oplus_{\succ}\cdots\oplus_{\succ}E_{k}^{c}\oplus_{\succ}E^{s},

where
{\rm dim}E_{i}^{c}=1,~1\leq i\leq k,~k\geq 1
.
If every Gibbs
u
-state of
f
is hyperbolic
1
1

1

An invariant measure is hyperbolic if all its Lyapunov exponents are non-zero.
,
then there are finitely many ergodic physical(SRB) measures of
f
.

According to
[
10
]
, partially hyperbolic splitting as in Theorem
A
is abundant among diffeomorphisms away from homoclinic tangencies. Partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms in Theorem
A
sometimes inherit some properties in uniform hyperbolicity (see e.g.
[
11
,
21
]
).

We would like to list some related results below:

•

In
[
7
, Theorem C]
, the authors prove the existence of (ergodic) SRB measures for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with multi 1-D centers.

•

Under the hyperbolicity of Gibbs
u
-state, for case
k=1
, it is shown in
[
12
]
that there exist finitely many physical measures whose basins cover a full Lebesgue measure subset.

•

Without the restriction on dimension of center direction, the existence and finiteness of physical/SRB measures have been achieved for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms for which the central Lyapunov exponents of Gibbs
u
-states are all negative, or all positive; including diffeomorphisms with mostly contracting center
[
6
]
; with mostly expanding center
[
1
]
; with mostly expanding center and mostly expanding center simultaneously
[
14
]
.

Observe that every SRB measure must be a Gibbs
u
-state, using the result of
[
7
]
, the hyperbolicity on Gibbs
u
-states guarantees the existence of ergodic physical measures for diffeomorphisms in Theorem
A
. Hence, the main effort on Theorem
A
is to show the finiteness of ergodic physical measures.
Let us remark that in the previous work
[
6
,
1
,
14
]
, the signs of central Lyapunov exponents w.r.t. Gibbs
u
-states are definite. Our assumption on Gibbs
u
-states is more flexible: different Gibbs
u
-states may admit different signs of Lyapunov exponents on the same central sub-bundle.

In
[
12
]
, besides the finiteness of ergodic physical measures, the authors also show the basin covering property of these physical measures, i.e., Lebesgue almost every point belongs to the basin of some ergodic physical measure.
It is natural to expect that the basin covering property can hold also for the general case
k>1
.
The main difference between the special case
k=1
and the general case
k>1
is that when
k=1
, the non-uniform behavior occurs only in one fixed center.

Our strategy of the proof of Theorem
A
is to decompose the ergodic physical/SRB measures into different levels by their signs of Lyapunov exponents on centers (see Theorem
4.1
), and then show the finiteness of these measures on each level. To achieve this, we will make an effort on getting the closed property of Gibbs
cu
-states, which is done by showing the uniformity on central Lyapunov exponents for Gibbs
cu
-states on the same level(see Theorem
3.4
).

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Dominated splitting and partially hyperbolicity

Let
f
be a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism on compact Riemannian manifold
M
. Given two
Df
-invariant subbundles
E
and
F
, we say
E

dominates

F
if
for every
x\in M

u\in E(x)
and

with
\|u\|=\|v\|=1

\|Df(x)v\|<\frac{1}{2}\|Df(x)u\|.

We write
E\oplus_{\succ}F
to denote this domination. A
Df
-invariant splitting
TM=E\oplus_{\succ}F
of the tangent bundle is called a
dominated splitting
if
E
dominates

.

One says that

is a
partially hyperbolic
diffeomorphism if there exists a dominated splitting

TM=E^{u}\oplus_{\succ}E_{1}\oplus_{\succ}\cdots\oplus_{\succ}E_{k}\oplus_{\succ}E^{s}

on the tangent bundle such that

•

E^{u}
is uniformly expanding and
E^{s}
is uniformly contracting.

•

at least one of
E^{u}
and
E^{s}
is nontrivial.

Gibbs

Let

be a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism with partially hyperbolic splitting

.
Recall that an
f
-invariant measure is a Gibbs

We list following properties of Gibbs

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

(1)

The ergodic components of any Gibbs

u
-states.

(2)

The set of Gibbs

(3)

For Lebesgue almost every

, any limit measure of

u

Recall the following entropy formula for diffeomorphisms with dominated splittings, see
[
9

[

, Theorem A]

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f
be a diffeomorphism with dominated splitting

, any limit measure
\mu
of
\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{i}(x)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

h_{\mu}(f)\geq\int\log|{\rm det}Df|_{E}|{\rm d}\mu.

Recall another notion called

-states

Let

Since we need to deal with various Gibbs

.

Similar to property (

Proposition 2.3

Assume that

diffeomorphism with a dominated splitting

\mu
is a Gibbs

E
-state.

We will use the following fact several times, which can be deduced by the absolute continuity of Pesin stable lamination, see
[

Proposition 2.4

Assume that

. If

The result below is abstract from

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

-states, assume by contradiction that there are infinitely many ergodic Gibbs
F_{k}
-states. This implies that there exists a sequence of different ergodic Gibbs
F_{k}
-states, which converges to an invariant measure
\mu
. By Theorem
3.4
,
\mu
must be a Gibbs
F_{k}
-state as well. However, from Proposition
3.2
we get that
\mu
have positive Lyapunov exponents along
E^{s}
, which is a contradiction. Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

As a byproduct of Theorem
3.4
, we also have the following interesting result, which asserts the uniformity on Lyapunov exponents of Gibbs
F_{i}
-states along
F_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
f
be a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism as in Theorem
3.4
, then there exists
\alpha>0
such that for every Gibbs
F_{i}
-state
\mu
,
one has that

\lambda^{c}_{i}(x)>\alpha,\quad\mu\textrm{-a.e.}~x.

Proof.

Assuming contrary, there exists
1\leq i\leq k
and a sequence of Gibbs
F_{i}
-states
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for which there exists an ergodic component
\nu_{n}
of
\mu_{n}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda^{c}_{i}(\nu_{n})=0.

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to some
f
-invariant measure
\nu
when
n\to+\infty
. Note that
x\mapsto\log\|Df|_{E_{i}^{c}(x)}\|
is continuous. By the weak
∗
convergence we have

(6)

\lambda^{c}_{i}(\nu)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda^{c}_{i}(\nu_{n})=0.

By Proposition
2.3
, we know each
\nu_{n}
is an ergodic Gibbs
F_{i}
-state for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence,
\nu
is a limit measure of the set of ergodic Gibbs
F_{i}
-states. By Theorem
3.4
, there exists
a>0
such that

\lambda^{c}_{i}(x)>a,\quad\nu\textrm{-a.e.}~x.

Noting
{\rm dim}E_{i}^{c}=1
, by applying Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem (see e.g.
[
23
, Theorem 1.14]
) one obtains

\lambda_{i}^{c}(\nu)=\int\lambda_{i}^{c}(x){\rm d}\nu>a,

which is in contradiction with convergence (
6
).
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
A

Now we begin to prove Theorem
A
. Assume that
f\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(M)
.
For every
0\leq i\leq k
, put

{\mathcal{G}}_{i}=\left\{\mu:\mu\in G_{i}~\textrm{is ergodic and}~\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu)<0\right\}.

Recall that
G_{i}
is the space of Gibbs
F_{i}
-states for every
0\leq i\leq k
, respectively; and

\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu)=\int\log\|Df|_{E_{i+1}^{c}}\|{\rm d}\mu.

Note that here we set
E_{k+1}^{c}=E^{s}
, and

\lambda^{c}_{k+1}(\mu)
denotes the maximal Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
along
E^{s}
, which is negative automatically as the uniform contraction on
E^{s}
.

To give the proof of Theorem
A
, we need the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the setting of Theorem
A
, the set of ergodic physical(SRB) measures coincides with
\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq k}{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
.

Proof.

To begin with, we show the existence of ergodic physical measures.
From the result
[
7
, Theorem C]
, there exists an ergodic SRB measure
\mu
for
f
, which is a Gibbs
u
-state by definition. We know also that
\mu
is hyperbolic by assumption. Consequently,
\mu
is an ergodic physical measure by using the absolute continuity of Pesin stable lamination.

By definition, for any
0\leq i\leq k
, any invariant measure in
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
is a hyperbolic ergodic SRB measure, which is also a physical measure by Proposition
2.4
. Thus, it suffices to show that every ergodic physical measure is contained in
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq k
.

By item (
3
) of Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
, there is a subset
\Delta
of
M
with full Lebesgue measure such that for any
x\in\Delta
, any limit measure
\eta
of
\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{i}(x)}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a Gibbs
u
-state and satisfies

(7)

h_{\eta}(f)\geq\int\log|{\rm det}Df|_{F_{i}}|{\rm d}\eta,\quad\forall~1\leq i\leq k.

Assume that
\mu
is an ergodic physical measure, thus
{\mathcal{B}}(\mu,f)
admits positive Lebesgue measure. Thus
{\mathcal{B}}(\mu,f)\cap\Delta
exhibits positive Lebesgue measure. By taking
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mu,f)\cap\Delta
, one gets form definition of
{\mathcal{B}}(\mu,f)
that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\delta_{f^{j}(x)}=\mu.

So,
\mu
is a Gibbs
u
-state and satisfies (
7
) form the definition of
\Delta
.

Since any Gibbs
u
-state is hyperbolic by assumption,
\mu
is an ergodic hyperbolic Gibbs
u
-state. Consequently, one can take
i\in\{0,\cdots,k\}
such that all the Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
along
F_{i}
are positive and
\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu)<0
. By Ruelle’s inequality, one has

h_{\mu}(f)\leq\int\log|{\rm det}Df|_{F_{i}}|{\rm d}\mu.

On the other hand, inequality (
7
) says that

h_{\mu}(f)\geq\int\log|{\rm det}Df|_{F_{i}}|{\rm d}\mu.

Therefore,

h_{\mu}(f)=\int\log|{\rm det}Df|_{F_{i}}|{\rm d}\mu.

By the result of Ledrappier-Young
[
13
, Theorem A]
,
\mu
is an SRB measure, which is contained in
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
by its sign of Lyapunov exponents.
∎

Now, we can give the proof of Theorem
A
.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

In view of Theorem
4.1
, it suffices to show that for each
0\leq i\leq k
,
{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
is a finite set. We know from Theorem
3.1
that
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
is finite. Now we assume

0\leq i\leq k-1
.

By contradiction, there exists a sequence of measures
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
and some invariant measure
\mu
such that

\mu_{n}\xrightarrow{weak^{*}}\mu,\quad\textrm{as}~n\to+\infty.

By Theorem
3.1
,
\mu
is a Gibbs
F_{i}
-state, which satisfies

(8)

\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(x)>0,\quad\mu\textrm{-a.e.}~x\in M.

On the other hand, since
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
, we know by definition that
\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu_{n})<0
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The convergence
\mu_{n}\xrightarrow{weak^{*}}\mu
yields

(9)

\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu_{n})\leq 0.

By Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, we also have

\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(\mu)=\int\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(x){\rm d}\mu.

This together with (
9
) yields that there exists a subset
A\subset M
such that
\mu(A)>0
and

\lambda^{c}_{i+1}(x)<0,\quad\forall~x\in A,

which contradicts (
8
) and we complete the proof.

∎

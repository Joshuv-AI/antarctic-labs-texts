Flat F-theory and friends
Ruben Minasian

Abstract
We discuss F-theory backgrounds associated to flat torus bundles over Ricci-flat manifolds. In this setting the F-theory background can be understood as a IIB orientifold with a large radius limit described by a supersymmetric compactification of IIB supergravity on a smooth, Ricci flat, but in general non-spin geometry. When compactified on an additional circle these backgrounds are T-dual to IIA compactifications on smooth non-orientable manifolds with a Pin − \text{Pin}^{-} structure.



1
Introduction

Given a type IIB string theory compactified on a manifold
Y\times{\mathbb{R}}^{1,d-1}
with a discrete symmetry action
G
, we can orientifold the construction to obtain a compactification
Y\times{\mathbb{R}}^{1,d-1}/H
, where
H=G^{\prime}\cup G\Pi
, and
\Pi
denotes a worldsheet parity operation
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Typically the
G
-action will have a non-empty fixed locus on
Y
, and this locus will mark the locations of orientifold planes. The orientifold planes source a tadpole that can be canceled by adding D-branes on top of the orientifold planes, and the resulting theory can preserve some reduced amount of supersymmetry. As shown in the classic work
[
4
,
5
]
, such constructions can often be identified with certain limits of related F-theory compactifications.

The introduction of open string degrees of freedom is a central feature of this construction that leads to much of the beauty and intricate structure of the resulting theory. In this note we point out an interesting corner of the orientifold landscape: when
G
acts freely on
Y
no orientifold planes are required, so that the resulting theories can be understood without additional D-branes or open strings. Furthermore, in the large radius limit the effective theory is described as compactification of IIB supergravity on
X=Y/G
—a smooth space that need not admit a spin structure. These compactifications also have an F-theory interpretation in terms of an elliptically fibered Calabi-Yau manifold
\pi:Z\to X
, where the fibration corresponds to a flat
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
bundle, so that the F-theory elliptic fiber is locally constant.

While well-known to experts since the inception of F-theory—see for example sections 6.3 and 6.4 of
[
6
]
, there are a number of reasons why these examples deserve more attention. First, they provide very simple examples of supersymmetric string compactification on non-spin manifolds in IIB or non-orientable manifolds in IIA, where a crucial role is played by the lift of a bosonic duality group, such as
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
of the ten-dimensional IIB string theory, to the action on the full theory, including the spacetime fermion fields
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Second, they illustrate the importance of global topological features, such as certain non-trivial torsion classes in cohomology, and allow us to probe these features directly on the string worldsheet. Third, they provide additional examples of perturbative string compactifications with reduced supersymmetry in various dimensions. Finally, their simple structure makes these vacua ideal for detailed explorations of string duality.

In the rest of this note we first illustrate the idea with a well-known example based on
[
10
]
. We then describe a number of related examples in diverse dimensions, point out a relationship to compactification of the IIA string on non-orientable manifolds, and we conclude with a brief outlook. There is a small geometric appendix.

2
The Enriques orientifold

2.1
The Enriques geometry as a quotient

The starting point for our discussion is compactification of IIB string theory on a smooth K3 surface
Y
. The massless spectrum is well-known
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
: it is (2,0) supergravity with
21
chiral tensor multiplets and a moduli space
{\cal M}_{5,21}\simeq\operatorname{Gr}(5,21)/\operatorname{O{}}(\Gamma_{5,21})
, where
\operatorname{Gr}(n_{1},n_{2})
is the Grassmannian

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gr}(n_{1},n_{2})=\operatorname{O{}}(n_{1},n_{2})/\operatorname{O{}}(n_{1})\times\operatorname{O{}}(n_{2})~,

(2.1)

and
\operatorname{O{}}(\Gamma_{n_{1},n_{2}})
is the group of isometries of the integral lattice
\Gamma_{n_{1},n_{2}}
with signature
(n_{1},n_{2})
.

Working in the RNS formulation, the string-perturbative regime is described by a worldsheet theory with (4,4) superconformal symmetry and central charges
c_{\text{\tiny{L}}}=c_{\text{\tiny{R}}}=6
, and if we further take a large radius limit, then the worldsheet theory has a description as a non-linear sigma model with target space
Y
. This SCFT has moduli space
{\cal M}_{4,20}
defined in an analogous fashion to
{\cal M}_{5,21}

[
14
,
15
]
. In the large radius limit we can decompose
{\cal M}_{4,20}
into the moduli space of Einstein volume
1
metrics
g
on K3—this is isomorphic to
{\cal M}_{3,19}
, a choice of closed B-field
B\in H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{R}})/H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
, and the K3 volume
V

[
16
]
.
Y
is a hyper-Kähler manifold with holonomy
\operatorname{SU}(2)=\operatorname{Sp}(1)
and consequently has
3
covariantly constant forms
j\in H^{1,1}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})
,
\omega\in H^{2,0}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})
, and
\overline{\omega}\in H^{0,2}(Y,{\mathbb{C}})
which obey

\displaystyle j\wedge j

\displaystyle={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\omega\wedge\overline{\omega}=\operatorname{dVol}_{g}(Y)~,

\displaystyle j\wedge\omega

\displaystyle=\omega\wedge\omega=\overline{\omega}\wedge\overline{\omega}=0~.

(2.2)

It is useful to think of the holonomy group of
Y
in terms of the reduction of the holonomy of
Y
from the generic
\operatorname{SO}(4)=\left\{\operatorname{SU}(2)_{1}\times\operatorname{SU}(2)_{2}\right\}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Here the quotient is by the diagonal central element, and we can identify the holonomy action with
\operatorname{SU}(2)_{2}
. The forms
j,\omega,\overline{\omega}
are singlets with respect to
\operatorname{SU}(2)_{2}
but transform in
{{\boldsymbol{3}}}
of
\operatorname{SU}(2)_{1}
, which describes the hyper-Kähler rotations. We can think of
Y
as a Calabi-Yau manifold with trivial canonical bundle
K_{Y}
and
h^{1}({\cal O}_{Y})=0
.

There is a locus in the metric moduli space
{\cal M}_{E}\subset{\cal M}_{3,19}
, where
Y
admits a freely-acting Kähler isometry group
G_{E}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

[
17
]
, and its generator
U_{E}
acts on the triplet of forms as

\displaystyle U_{E}\cdot j

\displaystyle=j~,

\displaystyle U_{E}\cdot\omega

\displaystyle=-\omega~,

\displaystyle U_{E}\cdot\overline{\omega}

\displaystyle=-\overline{\omega}~.

(2.3)

This free action is known as the Enriques involution, and the quotient
X=Y/G_{E}
is an Enriques surface— a smooth compact Kähler Ricci-flat surface with a torsion canonical bundle:
K_{X}^{\otimes 2}\simeq{\cal O}_{X}
, but
K_{X}\neq{\cal O}_{X}
. Recall that a complex manifold
X
is spin if and only if
c_{1}(K_{X})=0\mod 2
(see, e.g.
[
18
]
); thus, the Enriques surface
X
is not spin. We can see this explicitly in the quotient construction, as the action of
U_{E}
on the hyper-Kähler forms and on the covariantly constant spinors is by the element
i\sigma_{3}\in\operatorname{SU}(2)_{1}
. Letting
J^{3}
and
J^{\pm}
be the generators of
\operatorname{SU}(2)_{1}
with the non-zero commutators

\displaystyle{[J^{3},J^{\pm}]}

\displaystyle=\pm 2J^{\pm}~,

\displaystyle{[J^{\pm},J^{\mp}]}

\displaystyle=\pm J^{3}~,

(2.4)

the action can also be expressed as
e^{i{\textstyle\frac{\pi}{2}}J^{3}}
. It is no coincidence that the quotient also enlarges the holonomy group: we have
\pi_{1}(X)={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, and the quotient metric
g_{X}
has holonomy group
\text{Hol}(X,g_{X})=\operatorname{SU}(2)\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. The first factor is the null-homotopic holonomy inherited from
Y
, while the second factor is associated to the holonomy around a loop
\gamma
that generates
\pi_{1}(X)\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

1

1

The semi-direct product in the holonomy group is discussed in the appendix.

The last geometric point we will need is the
G_{E}
action on the lattice
H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})=\Gamma_{3,19}
with respect to the Enriques action

10

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3,19}=(\Gamma_{0,8}+\Gamma_{1,1})+(\Gamma_{0,8}+\Gamma_{1,1})+\Gamma_{1,1}~,

the generator acts as

\displaystyle U_{E}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&{\mathbbm{1}}_{10}&0\\
{\mathbbm{1}}_{10}&0&0\\
0&0&-{\mathbbm{1}}_{2}\end{pmatrix}~,

(2.6)

and consequently the spaces of dual and anti-self-dual forms decompose into even and odd components as

\displaystyle H^{2}_{+}(Y,{\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{R}}_{\text{e}}\oplus\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\right)_{\text{o}}~,

\displaystyle H^{2}_{-}(Y,{\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbb{R}}^{9}\right)_{\text{e}}\oplus\left({\mathbb{R}}^{10}\right)_{\text{o}}~.

(2.7)

2.2
The Enriques symmetry in the SCFT

Denote by
{\cal S}[Y]
the superconformal theory associated to the non-linear sigma model on
Y
equipped with Einstein metric
G=\sqrt{V}g
, where
g
is an Einstein metric with volume
1
, and a closed
B
-field
B
. Choosing
g\in{\cal M}_{E}
and setting
B=0
, we expect
{\cal S}[Y]
to admit a global
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
symmetry, which we will also call
G_{E}
.
2
2

2

It is not necessary to set
B=0
. The most general choice is to choose
B
so that the difference
g_{E}\cdot B-B
is contained in the image of
H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}})
inside
H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Its generator is a unitary operator which we will also denote
U_{E}
. Although we will use some features of the large radius limit, we will phrase our discussion as much as possible in terms of the SCFT properties to emphasize that in principle we have a construction that works at generic points on the Enriques locus. This includes solvable points, such as
T^{4}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
orbifolds and Gepner points.

In the NS-NS sector the action of the generator
U_{E}
is a combination of two operations. The first, which we call
x_{E}
, is a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
action on the (4,4) primary fields of the SCFT, while the second is a rotation generated by the global charges of the
\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{su}}}(2)_{\text{\tiny{L}}}\oplus\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{su}}}(2)_{\text{\tiny{R}}}
Kac-Moody algebra contained in the superconformal algebra:

\displaystyle\exp\left[{\textstyle\frac{i\pi}{2}}J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{L}}0}+{\textstyle\frac{i\pi}{2}}J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{R}}0}\right]~.

(2.8)

The symmetry generator is then

\displaystyle U_{E}

\displaystyle=x_{E}\exp\left[{\textstyle\frac{i\pi}{2}}J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{L}}0}+{\textstyle\frac{i\pi}{2}}J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{R}}0}\right]~.

(2.9)

It is not hard to see that
U_{E}^{2}={\mathbbm{1}}
in the NS-NS sector. The only subtlety is in the factor
\exp\left[{\textstyle\frac{i\pi}{2}}J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{L}}0}+{\textstyle\frac{i\pi}{2}}J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{R}}0}\right]
. To see that it squares to identity note that the primary fields in the NS-NS sector transform either in
({{\boldsymbol{1}}},{{\boldsymbol{1}}})
or
({{\boldsymbol{2}}},{{\boldsymbol{2}}})
of
\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{su}}}(2)_{\text{\tiny{L}}}\oplus\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{su}}}(2)_{\text{\tiny{R}}}
.
3
3

3

At
c=6
unitarity only allows the representations
{{\boldsymbol{1}}}
and
{{\boldsymbol{2}}}
to appear, and by considering a limiting case such as
Y=T^{4}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, where the theory is solvable, we see that in fact the only representations possible are
({{\boldsymbol{1}}},{{\boldsymbol{1}}})
or
({{\boldsymbol{2}}},{{\boldsymbol{2}}})
. Unitarity constraints on representations of the N=4 superconformal algebra are explored in detail in
[
19
]
.
In the latter case the action on the (4,4) primary field is determined by specifying the action of
x_{E}
on, say, the state of charge
+1
with respect to
J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{L}}0}
and
-1
with respect to
J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{R}}0}
.

h_{\text{\tiny{L}}}=h_{\text{\tiny{R}}}=1/2

({{\boldsymbol{2}}},{{\boldsymbol{2}}})

2.3
Some RNS details

In order to obtain supersymmetric vacua based on the Enriques orbifold we will need to enlarge the action of
G_{E}
to act on the entire world-sheet theory, including the Minkowski degrees of freedom and the superconformal ghosts. To describe the structure we will use the covariant formulation of the RNS string, and we will not need too many details beyond the usual textbook treatment

20
]
.

The first additional ingredient we will need is a description of the Ramond sectors associated to the
{\cal S}[Y]
SCFT. These are easily constructed by spectral flow once we pick a (2,2) subalgebra of the (4,4) algebra. In our case there is a preferred choice of a (2,2) sub-algebra — the sub-algebra that commutes with the action of
U_{E}
. Then, by the usual construction
[
21
]
, we obtain an isomorphism between the Ramond ground states of the theory and the chiral primary operators in the NS sector. Since the theory has chiral spectral flow, we can do this separately on the left and right. For example, left spectral flow of the identity operator and
J^{+}_{\text{\tiny{L}}}
leads to spin fields
\Sigma^{\pm}_{\text{\tiny{L}}}
which have the OPE

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\text{\tiny{L}}}^{A}(z_{1})\Sigma_{\text{\tiny{L}}}^{B}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{{\epsilon}^{AB}}{\sqrt{z_{12}}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{z_{12}}\delta^{A,B}J_{\text{\tiny{L}}}^{B}~.

(2.16)

Moreover these are primary with respect to
\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{su}}}(2)_{\text{\tiny{L}}}
, transforming in a doublet. These universal spin fields will play a role in constructing spacetime supercharges and a universal tensor multiplet in spacetime. On the other hand, chiral primary fields with
h_{\text{\tiny{L}}}=1
lead to left-moving Ramond ground states that are singlets with respect to
\widehat{\operatorname{\mathfrak{su}}}(2)_{\text{\tiny{L}}}
. More generally, every NS-NS field has an image in the R-NS sector determined by the left-moving spectral flow, and we have the same construction on the right. Finally, the internal theory has chiral fermion number operators that are represented by

\displaystyle e^{i\pi F_{\text{\tiny{L}}}^{\text{int}}}

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{L}}0}}~,

\displaystyle e^{i\pi F_{\text{\tiny{R}}}^{\text{int}}}

\displaystyle=e^{i\pi J^{3}_{\text{\tiny{R}}0}}~.

(2.17)

Next we describe the Minkowski degrees of freedom: these are bosons

2.4
Worldsheet parity and a supersymmetric Enriques action

The Enriques action in the NS-NS sector has a lift to the R-NS and NS-R sectors, and because the internal spin fields transform in doublets of the
\operatorname{SU}(2)_{\text{\tiny{L}}}\times\operatorname{SU}(2)_{\text{\tiny{R}}}

An orientifold without open strings and its supergravity limit

We now use the
\widetilde{U}_{E}
action on the IIB string worldsheet to define a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
orientifold. The
\widetilde{U}_{E}
–even modes include the spacetime supercharges
{\cal Q}_{\text{e}}^{A}
transforming in a doublet of
\operatorname{SU}(2)_{\text{e}}
, and taking the projection in the untwisted sector leads to a massless spectrum consisting of (1,0) supergravity coupled to
9
tensor multiplets and
12
hypermultiplets—exactly the massless spectrum of F-theory compactification on
Z=(Y\times T^{2})/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

[
6
,
24
]
—the Enriques Calabi-Yau
3
-fold familiar from the Borcea-Voisin construction of mirror pairs
[
25
,
26
]
and introduced in the context of string duality in
[
10
]
. The same spectrum arises in the closed string sector of the orientifold introduced in
[
27
]
(there it is also accompanied by open string modes and a non-trivial gauge symmetry of rank at least
8
), and the same spectrum is reproduced in the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
orientifold of
T^{4}
studied, among other models, in
[
28
]
. Because our theory has neither orientifold planes or ordinary orbifold singularities, by contrast with both of these constructions, the orientifold we discuss is a consistent closed unoriented string (as is the model in
[
28
]
), but it is also entirely smooth, and thus has a supergravity description as a supersymmetric compactification of the IIB string on the Enriques surface
X
, a non-spin manifold.

To understand how it is possible to have a supersymmetric supergravity compactification on
X
, we recall that the fermions in IIB supergravity are not really spinors due to the non-trivial action of the duality group
[
7
]
. Though it is often left unstated, this point is at the heart of orientifold and F-theory constructions. The former have been discussed in great generality in
[
29
,
30
]
. Our worldsheet construction is an important special case of that discussion, in that the background preserves supersymmetry and also allows a complementary spacetime point of view, which we develop next.

The duality group of IIB has been recently discussed in
[
8
,
9
,
31
]
, and the basic point relevant to us is that the bosonic duality symmetry
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
is enlarged to
\text{Pin}^{+}\operatorname{GL}(2,{\mathbb{Z}})
when the action on the fermions is taken into account. Considering the Lorentz symmetry
\operatorname{SO}(1,9)
together with the duality group, we find the corresponding faithful action on the fermions by a group we term
{\operatorname{Spin}_{\text{d}}}(1,9)

\displaystyle(M_{\alpha\beta},A_{\beta\alpha})\in{\operatorname{Spin}^{\prime}_{\text{d}}}(1,9)~.

3
Further examples

The conclusion of the previous section is that it is possible to compactify IIB string theory on the Enriques surface by using
{\operatorname{Spin}^{\prime}_{\text{d}}}
fermions. Moreover, it is possible to analyze the resulting theory both from supergravity and from the worldsheet, and the latter does not require any non-perturbative degrees of freedom. In this section we will discuss a few other examples of these sorts of “flat F-theory” backgrounds.

3.1
Toroidal generalizations

The first idea one might have is to replace the K3 geometry with the simpler choice of

4.2
Pinors and spacetime supersymmetry

The worldsheet construction makes it clear that the IIA string yields a supersymmetric compactification on

4.3
M-theory lift

in the IIA string. On the other hand, the GSO projection in the IIA worldsheet description requires the reflection in the

This construction can be compared with M-theory compactified on the Klein bottle

5
Outlook

In this short note we pointed out a simple class of supersymmetric IIB string compactifications that share a number of interesting properties: first, they have a perturbative closed string orientifold description; second, they are described as flat F-theory backgrounds, where the axio-dilaton profile remains constant over a Kähler base; finally, they have a supergravity description as compactifications of IIB supergravity on non-spin manifolds. The latter feature depends on the non-trivial transformations of IIB fermion fields under the IIB duality group. With the addition of a circle the backgrounds also have a IIA interpretation as compactification on unoriented manifolds.

While these backgrounds are rather trivial from the point of view of generic F/M-theory compactifications, they deserve a closer look precisely because, while preserving various amounts of supersymmetry, they have a perturbative string-theoretic description and a geometric interpretation. They thus offer a perfect framework for exploring aspects of F/M-theory away from the large radius limit, as well as string duality along the lines of
[
45
]
. For instance, it may be possible to find dual descriptions of some of our
5
-dimensional examples as heterotic compactification on compact flat manifolds that has been recently studied in
[
46
]
. Perhaps they may help to explore new spacetime perspectives on string orbifold constructions such as that pursued in
[
47
]
.

We envision several ways to explore and expand on these observations. Perhaps the most immediate would be to explore the appropriate generalization of spacetime T-duality that would account for the non-trivial transformation from a compactification on a manifold with non-vanishing
w_{2}
Stiefel-Whitney class to a T-dual compactification on a non-orientable manifold. While the appearance of non-spin manifolds has been discussed in the context of topological T-duality, for example in

]
, the resolution of the attending supersymmetry puzzle involved a non-trivial flux and a corresponding
\text{Spin}_{\text{c}}
structure. It would be very useful to rephrase our T-dual pairs in a purely spacetime language that generalizes the results of

It will also be good to have a better understanding of the F- and M-theory lifts of these string backgrounds. In each case we see that the lift gives a “geometrization” of the action of a spacetime gauge symmetry, such as
\Pi e^{i\pi{{{\boldsymbol{F}}}}_{\text{\tiny{L}}}}
in the string compactification, resulting in a locally constant but globally non-trivial fibration of the M-theory circle or the F-theory torus. What are the general rules?

In addition it will be interesting to look for string dual descriptions. Are there manifestations of the “non-spin” nature of these compactifications in the dual description? It would also be interesting to explore the constructions more systematically. For example, one could use the classification of compact flat Kähler manifolds
[
49
]
to identify suitable non-spin manifolds and study in detail the resulting orientifolds.

More generally, the global structures studied here can also arise in less trivial examples that also involve fluxes and might lack an explicit worldsheet description. To handle such situations it would be useful to develop a theory akin to that of G-structures that would also incorporate the global effects. Finally, it should also be possible to find other routes to non-spin compactifications. For instance, by compactifying the heterotic string on
T^{2}
one also obtains a duality group action, and further compactification could make use of this. Studying such compactifications, their geometries, non-geometric limits, and dual descriptions should provide valuable markers in the string landscape.

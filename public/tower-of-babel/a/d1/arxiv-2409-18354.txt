Gaussian JN p \textup{JN}_{p} spaces
Jorge J. Betancor 1, Estefanía Dalmasso 2, Pablo Quijano 2

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce the John-Nirenberg’s type spaces JN p \textup{JN}_{p} associated with the Gaussian measure d γ ( x ) = π − d / 2 e − | x | 2 d x d\gamma(x)=\pi^{-d/2}e^{-|x|^{2}}dx in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} where 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . We prove a John-Nirenberg inequality for JN p ​ ( ℝ d , γ ) \textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma) . We also characterize the predual of JN p ​ ( ℝ d , γ ) \textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma) as a Hardy type space.

1.
Introduction

In
[
12
]
, John and Nirenberg introduced the well-known space
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of functions with bounded mean oscillation. Also, they considered a variant of the
BMO
condition. This other condition is used to define the space of functions
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1<p<\infty
, as follows. Let
Q_{0}
be a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1<p<\infty
. We always assume that the cubes have sides parallel to the coordinate axis and they are open. A function
f\in L^{1}(Q_{0})
is said to be in
\textup{JN}_{p}(Q_{0})
when

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}(Q_{0})}\coloneqq\sup\left(\sum_{i}|Q_{i}|\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{i}|}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|dx\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all the countable families
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of pairwise disjoint cubes in
Q_{0}
and
f_{Q_{i}}
stands for the average of
f
over the cube
Q_{i}
.
Similarly, a function
f\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is in
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is defined analogously.

\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces were considered in the context of interpolation by Campanato
[
3
]
and Stampacchia
[
19
]
. More recently, in the last decade a number of papers have investigated about
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
17
]
, for instance). Related with the
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces are the dyadic
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces (
[
13
]
), the John-Nirenberg-Campanato spaces (
[
25
]
), localized versions of
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces (
[
23
]
) and the sparse
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces (
[
6
]
), among others.

Other definitions of
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces appear when the cubes are replaced by other classes of sets in more general measure metric spaces. Depending on the overlapping properties of the chosen sets we can obtain different spaces.

In
[
11
]
, John studied
BMO
spaces using medians instead of integral averages. From the results in
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
it can be deduced that
BMO
spaces defined by using medians and averages coincide. Recently, median-type John-Nirenberg spaces in metric measure spaces have been studied in
[
18
]
.

It is not hard to see that
L^{p}\subset\textup{JN}_{p}
. Also we have that
\textup{JN}_{p}\subset L^{p,\infty}
. Further, both of these inclusions are strict. An example of a function
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(I)\setminus L^{p}(I)
where
I
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}
was defined in
[
5
]
. Previously, some results related to the nonequality
L^{p}\neq\textup{JN}_{p}
were contained in
[
15
]
. In
[
1
]
, it was proved that
\textup{JN}_{p}\neq L^{p,\infty}
. Other examples of functions in
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces have been constructed in
[
24
]
.

Our objective in this paper is to introduce and to study the
\textup{JN}_{p}
spaces associated with the Gaussian measure
d\gamma(x)=\pi^{-d/2}e^{-|x|^{2}}dx
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that we name
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
with
1<p<\infty
.

We consider the function
m
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

m(x)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }x=0,\\
\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{|x|}\right\}&\mbox{if }x\neq 0.\end{cases}

If
B
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
c_{B}
and
r_{B}
the center and the radius of
B
, respectively. Let
a>0
. By
\mathcal{B}_{a}
we represent the family of balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
r_{B}\leq am(c_{B})
. It is usual to name the balls in
\mathcal{B}_{a}

admissible balls
with parameter
a
. The Gaussian measure has not the doubling property. However, the Gaussian measure is doubling on
\mathcal{B}_{a}
but the doubling constant depends on
a
(
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
).

The bounded mean oscillation function space associated with
\gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in short
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
, was introduced in
[
16
]
. A function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is said to be in
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
when

\|f\|_{\star,\mathcal{B}_{1}}=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}\frac{1}{\gamma(B)}\int_{B}|f-f_{B}|d\gamma<\infty,

where, for a function
f
and a ball
B
,
f_{B}=\frac{1}{\gamma(B)}\int_{B}fd\gamma
. The space
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}+\|f\|_{\star,\mathcal{B}_{1}},\quad f\in\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma).

Thus,
(\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma),\|\cdot\|_{\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)})
is a Banach space.

In
[
16
, Proposition 2.4]
it was proved that if we define the space
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
associated to the family
\mathcal{B}_{a}
with
a\neq 1
instead of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
we obtain again the same space and the corresponding norms are equivalent.

If
Q
is a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
c_{Q}
and
\ell_{Q}
the center and the side length of
Q
respectively. The family
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
consists of all those cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\ell_{Q}\leq am(c_{Q})
.
If we consider the family
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
instead of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
to define
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
the new space coincides with that defined using
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and the corresponding norms are equivalent.

The main properties of the space
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
were established in
[
16
]
(see also
[
4
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
26
]
).

Let
a>0
and
1<p<\infty
. A function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is said to be in
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
when

K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(f)=\sup\left(\sum_{i}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all the countable collections
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of pairwise disjoint cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. The space
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}+K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(f),\quad f\in\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma).

We will prove in Proposition
2.1
that
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
actually does not depend on
a>0
. Then we will write in the sequel
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
to name
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
,
a>0
. We also prove that
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is contained in
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
,
1<p<\infty
, and appears when
p\rightarrow\infty
in
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
(see Proposition
2.2
).

The following property is a John-Nirenberg type inequality for
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a>0
and
1<p<\infty
. There exists
C>0
such that, for every
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
,
\sigma>0
and
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
,

\gamma\left(\{x\in Q:|f-f_{Q}|>\sigma\}\right)\leq C\left(\frac{K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p}.

It is a celebrated result due to Fefferman and Stein (
[
7
]
) that the Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the predual of
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In the Gaussian setting, the predual of the space
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
was characterized in
[
16
, Theorem 5.2]
as a Hardy type space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
defined by using atoms whose support is contained in admissible balls. In
[
5
, §6]
it was defined a Hardy type space
H^{p^{\prime}}(Q)
whose dual coincide with
\textup{JN}_{p}(Q)
, where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
and
1<p<\infty
. The ideas in
[
5
]
inspired the duality properties for John-Nirenberg-Campanato spaces (
[
23
]
).

Our main result characterizes a new Hardy type space as the dual of
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
,
1<p<\infty
.

For every
1\leq s\leq\infty
and every cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
L^{s}_{0}(Q,\gamma)
the space consisting of all those
f\in L^{s}(Q,\gamma)
such that
\int_{Q}fd\gamma=0
. For
1<q\leq\infty
and
a>0
we say that a function
b\in\mathcal{A}(q,a,Q)
if
b
is supported on a cube
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
and
b\in L_{0}^{q}(Q,\gamma)
.

Let
a>0
and
1<p<q\leq\infty
. We consider a measurable function
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by
g=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}
, where, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{j}\in\mathcal{A}(q,a,Q_{j})
being
Q_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
and the sequence
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is pairwise disjoint. We say that
g
is a
(p,q,a)
-polymer
when

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|b_{j}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}<\infty.

(1.1)

Note that the series defining
g
is pointwise convergent because
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is pairwise disjoint. We also define

\|g\|_{(p,q,a)}=\inf\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|b_{j}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}

where the infimum is taken over all the sequences
\{b_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
as above such that
{g=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}}
and (
1.1
) holds.
By using Jensen inequality we can see that if
g
is a
(p,q,a)
-polymer defined as above, then
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and

\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|b_{j}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}.

(1.2)

Observe that
g=0
a.e. provided that
\|g\|_{(p,q,a)}=0
. The above estimate implies that if
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
(p,q,a)
-polymers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p,q,q)}<\infty
, then the series
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}
converges in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

When
q=\infty
the above expressions are understood in the usual way.

We now introduce a Hardy type space as follows. A measurable function
g
is in
H_{p,q,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
when
g=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}
, where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,
g_{i}
is a
(p,q,a)
-polymer for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p,q,a)}<\infty
. The convergence of the series is understood in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Note that if
g\in H_{p,q,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
then
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Observe that
c_{0}
is actually unique, since each polymer
g_{i}
can be written in terms of functions
b_{ij}\in\mathcal{A}(q,a,Q_{ij})
and all of them have zero integral with respect to the Gaussian measure.

We define the following quantity

\|g\|_{H_{p,q,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}=|c_{0}|+\inf\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p,q,a)},

where the infimum is taken over all the sequences
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
(p,q,a)
-polymers such that
g=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}
with
c_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}gd\gamma
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p,q,a)}<\infty
. The functional
\|\cdot\|_{H_{p,q,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}
is a norm for
H_{p,q,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

Given
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
, we define the functional
\Lambda_{f}
by

\Lambda_{f}g:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{N}gd\gamma,

(1.3)

where for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

f_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&if $|f(x)|\leq N$\\
N\operatorname{sgn}(f(x)),&if $|f(x)|>N$.\end{cases}

The functional
\Lambda_{f}
is well-defined, as we shall see in the proof of Theorem
1.2
(a)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1<q<p<\infty
and
a>0
.

(a)

Let
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Then
\Lambda_{f}\in(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}
and

\|\Lambda_{f}\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}\leq C\|f\|_{\textup{JN}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)},

where
C>0
does not depend on
f
.

(b)

If
\Lambda\in(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}
there exists a unique
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
such that
\Lambda=\Lambda_{f}
, defined as in (
1.3
), and

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq C\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}

where
C>0
does not depend on
\Lambda
.

Note that from Theorem
1.2
and
[
10
, Lemma 4.14]
we can deduce that
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is a Banach space for every
1<p<\infty
. Moreover, as we shall see in Proposition
4.3
,
H_{p,q,a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)=H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
whenever
a_{1},a_{2}>0
and
1<p<q<\infty
.

2.
Some properties of the
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
spaces

We first prove that the space
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
does not depend on
a>0
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
a_{1},a_{2}>0
. We have that

\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)=\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)

algebraically and topologically.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
0<a_{2}<a_{1}
. It is clear that
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)\subseteq\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
since
K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(f)\leq K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(f)
for every
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and, therefore,

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)},\quad f\in\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma).

We are going to see the other inclusion. Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}
. As in the proof of
[
16
, Proposition 2.3]
, there exist
N
cubes
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{N}\in\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}
contained in
Q
and a positive constant
C
such that
\gamma(Q_{j})\leq\gamma(Q)\leq C\gamma(Q_{j})
for every
j=1,\dots,N
, and

\frac{1}{\gamma(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|d\gamma\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|f-f_{Q_{j}}|d\gamma.

Here,
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
do not depend on the cube
Q
.

Consider now a family
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}
such that
Q_{i}\cap Q_{j}=\emptyset
for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
,
i\neq j
. For a fixed
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the collection of cubes
\{Q_{i,1},\dots,Q_{i,N}\}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}
associated with
Q_{i}
as above. Hence, there exists
C>0
for which

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i,j})}\int_{Q_{i,j}}|f-f_{Q_{i,j}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i,j})}\int_{Q_{i,j}}|f-f_{Q_{i,j}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i,j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i,j})}\int_{Q_{i,j}}|f-f_{Q_{i,j}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq CN\left(K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(f)\right)^{p}.

Taking the supremum on the pairwise disjoint families
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}
, we get that

K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(f)\leq C_{p}N^{1/p}K_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(f),

which gives
\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)\subseteq\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and the inclusion is also continuous.
∎

The following proposition establishes some relations between
\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(a)

\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is continuously contained in
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
for every
1<p<\infty
.

(b)

For every
a>0
and
f\in\textup{BMO}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
,

\lim_{p\to\infty}\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}=\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}.

Here,
\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}}\frac{1}{\gamma(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|d\gamma+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}
, for
{f\in\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}
.

Proof.

(a)

Let
f\in\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
, and suppose
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a pairwise disjoint family of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
. Thus, for every
1<p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}^{p}.

Then,

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)},\quad 1<p<\infty.

(b)

We adapt an idea given in the proof of
[
25
, Proposition 2.6]
. Let
a>0
,
1<p<\infty
,
f\in\textup{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
, and consider
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. We have that

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\geq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}+\gamma(Q)^{1/p}\frac{1}{\gamma(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|d\gamma.

Thus,

\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\geq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}+\frac{1}{\gamma(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|d\gamma,

and we obtain that

\liminf_{p\to\infty}\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\geq\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}.

(2.1)

On the other hand, by proceeding as in
(a)
, for every
1<p<\infty
,

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}.

(2.2)

From (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), it follows that

\lim_{p\to\infty}\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}=\|f\|_{\textup{BMO}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}

as desired. ∎

3.
A John-Nirenberg inequality for
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)

We now prove Theorem
1.1
. Let
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. We denote by
\lambda
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Proceeding as in the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 3]
we can see that

\lambda\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f(x)-f_{Q,\lambda}\right|>\sigma\right\}\right)\leq C\left(\frac{\mathbb{K}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p},

(3.1)

for
\sigma>0
where

\mathbb{K}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)=\sup\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\lambda(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i},\lambda}|d\lambda\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}

and the supremum is taken over all the pairwise disjoint sequences
\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. Here,

f_{H,\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda(H)}\int_{H}fd\lambda
for every measurable set
H
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To see this, suppose that
H
is a cube contained in
Q
. We have that

|c_{H}|\leq|c_{H}-c_{Q}|+|c_{Q}|\leq\sqrt{d}\ell_{Q}+|c_{Q}|\leq a\sqrt{d}m(c_{Q})+|c_{Q}|.

Then,

\begin{split}m(c_{H})^{-1}&=\max\{1,|c_{H}|\}\leq\max\{1,|c_{Q}|\}+a\sqrt{d}m(c_{Q})\\
&\leq m(c_{Q})^{-1}+a\sqrt{d}m(c_{Q})\leq m(c_{Q})^{-1}+a\sqrt{d}.\end{split}

Therefore

m(c_{Q})\leq m(c_{H})(1+a\sqrt{d}m(c_{Q}))\leq m(c_{H})(1+a\sqrt{d})

and it follows that

\ell_{H}\leq\ell_{Q}\leq am(c_{Q})\leq a(1+a\sqrt{d})m(c_{H}).

According to
[
12
, Lemma 3]
we deduce that

\lambda\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f(x)-f_{Q,\lambda}\right|>\sigma\right\}\right)\leq C\left(\frac{\mathbb{K}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a(1+a\sqrt{d})}}_{p}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p},

for
\sigma>0
and proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
we obtain that

\mathbb{K}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a(1+a\sqrt{d})}}_{p}(f)\leq C\mathbb{K}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)

and (
3.1
) is proved.

If
H\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
, by using
[
16
, Proposition 2.1(i)]
we get

\frac{1}{\lambda(H)}\int_{H}|f-f_{H,\lambda}|d\lambda\leq\frac{2}{\lambda(H)}\int_{H}|f-f_{H}|d\lambda\leq\frac{C}{\gamma(H)}\int_{H}|f-f_{H}|d\gamma.

Also,
[
16
, Proposition 2.1(i)]
implies that if
b>0
there exists
C>0
such that for every measurable set
B\subset D
with
D\in\mathcal{Q}_{b}

C^{-1}\gamma(B)\leq e^{-|c_{D}|^{2}}\lambda(B)\leq C\gamma(B).

It follows that, for
\sigma>0
,

\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f(x)-f_{Q,\lambda}\right|>\sigma\right\}\right)\leq C\left(\frac{{K}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p}.

(3.2)

Let
\sigma>0
. We have that

\begin{split}\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f(x)-f_{Q}\right|>\sigma\right\}\right)\leq&\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f(x)-f_{Q,\lambda}\right|>\sigma/2\right\}\right)\\
&+\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f_{Q,\lambda}-f_{Q}\right|>\sigma/2\right\}\right).\end{split}

As above, we can write

\left|f_{Q,\lambda}-f_{Q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda(Q)}\int_{H}|f-f_{Q}|d\lambda\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\gamma(Q)}\int_{H}|f-f_{Q}|d\gamma\leq C_{0}\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f),

for certain
C_{0}>0
.

Then

\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f_{Q,\lambda}-f_{Q}\right|>\sigma/2\right\}\right)\leq\begin{cases}\gamma(Q)&\text{ if }0<\sigma\leq 2C_{0}\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f),\\
0&\text{ if }\sigma<2C_{0}\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f).\end{cases}

We obtain

\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f_{Q,\lambda}-f_{Q}\right|>\sigma/2\right\}\right)\leq C\gamma(Q)\left(\frac{\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p}\leq C\left(\frac{K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p}

Using this and estimate (
3.2
) we conclude that

\gamma\left(\left\{x\in Q:\left|f(x)-f_{Q}\right|>\sigma\right\}\right)\leq C\left(\frac{K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)}{\sigma}\right)^{p}

and the proof of Theorem
1.1
is finished.

4.
Duality

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. In order to do so, we will establish some preliminary results related to the spaces involved and a covering lemma of admissible cubes.

4.1.
Properties of function spaces

First we consider the space
\textup{JN}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
as follows. Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
a>0
. A function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is said to be in
\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
when

K_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(f):=\sup\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all the pairwise disjoint sequences
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. The space
\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}
defined by

\|f\|_{\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}+K_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(f),\quad f\in\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma).

When
a>0
and
1\leq q<p
,
\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
actually does not depend on
a
and
q
, as shown below.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a>0
and
1\leq q<p
. Then,
\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)=\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
algebraically and topologically.

Proof.

By using Hölder inequality with
q
and
q^{\prime}
we easily get
\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)\subseteq\textup{JN}_{p,1}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)=\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
for any
a>0
, and the inclusion is continuous. Here, we have used Proposition
2.1
.

We will now prove the other inclusion. Let
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. Since
1\leq q<p
,
L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)
is continuously contained in
L^{q}(Q,\gamma)
, and there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
Q
such that

\|g\|_{L^{q}(Q,\gamma)}\leq C\gamma(Q)^{1/q-1/p}\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)},\quad g\in L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma).

Indeed, given
g\in L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)
we can write, for
t=\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}
,

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{q}(Q,\gamma)}^{q}

\displaystyle=q\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{q-1}\gamma(\{x\in Q:|g(x)|>\sigma\})d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq q\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{q-1}\gamma(Q)d\gamma+\int_{t}^{\infty}\sigma^{q-1}\gamma(\{x\in Q:|g(x)|>\sigma\})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(Q)t^{q}+q\int_{t}^{\infty}\sigma^{q-1-p}\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}^{p}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\gamma(Q)t^{q}+q\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}^{p}\frac{t^{q-p}}{p-q}

\displaystyle=\gamma(Q)^{1-q/p}\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}^{q}+\frac{q}{p-q}\gamma(Q)^{1-q/p}\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{p-q}\gamma(Q)^{1-q/p}\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}^{q}.

Hence,

\|g\|_{L^{q}(Q,\gamma)}\leq C_{p,q}\gamma(Q)^{1/q-1/p}\|g\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}^{q}.

Now, let
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(Q,\gamma)
. By proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
we can deduce that

\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}\leq CK^{\mathcal{Q}_{a(1+\sqrt{d}a)}}_{p,Q}(f)\leq CK^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p,Q}(f),

where

K^{\mathcal{Q}_{b}}_{p,Q}(f):=\sup\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

and the supremum is taken over all the pairwise disjoint sequences
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
contained in
Q
.

Then,
f-f_{Q}\in L^{q}(Q,\gamma)
and

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,q}\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}\|f-f_{Q}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(Q,\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\gamma(Q)^{-1/p}K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p,Q}(f).

Suppose that
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a pointwise sequence of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. From the above inequality we have

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p,Q_{i}}(f)\right)^{p}.

Let
\epsilon>0
. For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we choose a pairwise disjoint sequence
\{Q_{i,j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
for which

\left(K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p,Q_{i}}(f)\right)^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i,j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i,j})}\int_{Q_{i,j}}|f-f_{Q_{i,j}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}+\frac{\epsilon}{2^{i}}.

We get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i,j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i,j})}\int_{Q_{i,j}}|f-f_{Q_{i,j}}|d\gamma\right)^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(K^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f)\right)^{p}+\epsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\epsilon>0
allows us to obtain

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{i})}\int_{Q_{i}}|f-f_{Q_{i}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}\leq CK^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}_{p}(f).

This implies that
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is continuously contained in
\textup{JN}_{p,q}^{\mathcal{Q}_{a}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
so the proof is now finished.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
1<r<s<\infty
and
a>0
. The linear space

A_{a,s}:=\operatorname{span}\left\{\left(\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}}L_{0}^{s}(Q,\gamma)\right)\cup\{c\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:c\in\mathbb{C}\}\right\}

is dense in
H_{r,s,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
g
is an
(r,s,a)
-polymer. We can write
g=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}
where, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\supp b_{j}\subset Q_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
and
b_{j}\in L_{0}^{s}(Q_{j},\gamma)
, being
\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of pairwise disjoint cubes. Also, we have that (
1.1
) holds.

We can write

\left\|g-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\right\|_{(r,s,a)}\leq\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|b_{j}|^{s}d\gamma\right)^{r/s}\right)^{1/r}.

Due to the convergence of the series, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|g-\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}b_{j}\right\|_{(r,s,a)}<\epsilon.

Given now
g\in H_{r,s,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
, where
g=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}
with
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,
g_{i}
is an
(r,s,a)
-polymer for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(r,s,a)}<\infty
, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|g-c_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}g_{i}\right\|_{H_{r,s,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\sum_{i=i_{0}+1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(r,s,a)}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

For each of these
(r,s,a)
-polymers
g_{i}
, we have that
g_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{ij}
as above. Therefore, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
l_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\|g_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l}b_{ij}\right\|_{(r,s,a)}<\frac{\epsilon}{2i_{0}},\quad\text{for every }l\geq l_{i}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|g-c_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}b_{ij}\right\|_{H_{r,s,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|g-c_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}g_{i}\right\|_{H_{r,s,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}+\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}\left(g_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}b_{ij}\right)\right\|_{H_{r,s,a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}\left\|g_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}b_{ij}\right\|_{(r,s,a)}<\epsilon.

The proof is now concluded.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2}>0
and
1<p<q<\infty
. Then

H_{p,q,a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)=H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)

algebraically and topologically.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
0<a_{2}<a_{1}
.
Since
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}
it is immediate that
H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
is continuously contained in
H_{p,q,a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

We now prove the converse inclusion.

Let us fix
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}
and
v\in L^{q}_{0}(Q,\gamma)
. We consider, as in
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
, the family of
2^{d}
cubes
P_{i}
contained in
Q
with sides parallel to the axes, each having sidelength
\ell_{P_{i}}=\frac{2}{3}\ell_{Q}
and a vertex in common with the cube
Q
. They verify that

\bigcap_{i=1}^{2^{d}}P_{i}=P_{0},

where
P_{0}
is a cube with
c_{P_{0}}=c_{Q}
and
\ell_{P_{0}}=\frac{1}{3}\ell_{Q}
. As in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 2.3]
, it can be obtained that
P_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{2}{3}a_{1}(1+\sqrt{d}\frac{a_{1}}{2})}
for each
i=0,\dots,2^{d}
. Consequently,

\gamma(P_{i})\leq C_{d,a_{1}}\gamma(P_{0}),\quad i=1,\dots,2^{d}.

(4.1)

We define the functions and scalars given also in the aforementioned proof. For every
i=1,\dots,2^{d}
,

\psi_{i}=\frac{\chi_{P_{i}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{2^{d}}\chi_{P_{k}}},\quad\lambda_{i}=\frac{1}{\gamma(P_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\psi_{i}d\gamma,

where
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of the measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and set

v_{i}=v\psi_{i}-\lambda_{i}\chi_{P_{0}},\quad v_{0}=v-\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d}}v_{i}.

For every
i=0,\dots,2^{d}
it is clear that
\supp v_{i}\subset P_{i}
and
\int_{P_{i}}v_{i}d\gamma=0
. Moreover, for
i=1,\dots,2^{d}
, by Hölder inequality and (
4.1
) we get

\displaystyle\|v_{i}\|_{L^{q}_{0}(P_{i},\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{q}(P_{i},\gamma)}+\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(P_{0})}\int_{P_{i}}|v||\psi_{i}|d\gamma\right)\gamma(P_{0})^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{q}(P_{i},\gamma)}+\|v\|_{L^{q}(P_{i},\gamma)}\frac{\gamma(P_{i})}{\gamma(P_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{q}(P_{i},\gamma)},

and

\|v_{0}\|_{L^{q}_{0}(P_{0},\gamma)}\leq\|v\|_{L^{q}(P_{0},\gamma)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{q}(P_{0},\gamma)}\leq(1+C2^{d})\|v\|_{L^{q}(P_{0},\gamma)}.

If
\frac{2}{3}a_{1}(1+\sqrt{d}\frac{a_{1}}{2})\leq a_{2}
, we have
P_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}
for every
i=0,\dots,2^{d}
, so we are done. If, otherwise, there exists some
P_{i}
not in
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}
, we repeat the previous construction for each of these cubes not belonging to
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}.
If necessary, we iterate the argument.

Notice that it will suffice to repeat this construction at most
n
times, where

n:=\min\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{k}a_{1}\left(1+\sqrt{d}\tfrac{a_{1}}{2}\right)\leq a_{2}\right\}.

This process produce a decomposition of
Q
into a family of cubes
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{i_{0}}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}
, a decomposition of
v
into a family of functions
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{i_{0}}
for some
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
with
i_{0}\leq(1+2^{d})^{n}
such that, for every
i=1,\dots,i_{0}
,
\supp v_{i}\subset P_{i}
,
v_{i}\in L^{q}_{0}(P_{i},\gamma)
and
\|v_{i}\|_{L^{q}_{0}(P_{i},\gamma)}\leq(1+C2^{d})^{n}\|v\|_{L^{q}_{0}(Q,\gamma)}
. According to
[
16
, Proposition 2.1(i)]
, for every
i=0,\dots,i_{0}
,
\gamma(P_{i})\sim\gamma(Q)
, where the equivalence does not depend on
Q
.

Let
g
be a
(p,q,a_{1})
-polymer, that is,
g=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}v_{j}
where, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\supp v_{j}\subset Q_{j}\in\mathcal{Q}_{a_{1}}
and
v_{j}\in L^{q}_{0}(Q_{j},\gamma)
, and the sequence of cubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is pairwise disjoint with

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|v_{j}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}<\infty.

(4.2)

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We work as before with
v_{j}
and
Q_{j}
. Hence, we obtain a family of cubes
\{P_{ij}\}_{i=1}^{i_{0}}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{a_{2}}
and a collection
\{v_{ij}\}_{i=1}^{i_{0}}
of functions satisfying that

(a)

Q_{j}=\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{i_{0}}P_{ij}\right)\cup E_{j}
, where
|E_{j}|=0
;

(b)

\supp v_{ij}\subset P_{ij}
,
v_{ij}\in L^{q}_{0}(P_{ij},\gamma)
and
\|v_{ij}\|_{L^{q}_{0}(P_{ij},\gamma)}\leq(1+C2^{d})^{n}\|v_{j}\|_{L^{q}_{0}(Q_{j},\gamma)}
for every
i=1,\dots,i_{0}
;

(c)

there exists
C=C(a_{1},a_{2},d)>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\gamma(P_{ij})\leq\gamma(Q_{j})\leq C\gamma(P_{ij}),\quad i=1,\dots,i_{0};

(d)

v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}v_{ij}
.

Consequently, for any representation of
g=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}v_{j}
as above, we have another representation of
g=\sum_{ij}v_{ij}=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}V_{i}
satisfying the properties
(a)
-
(d)
. Moreover, for each
i=1,\dots,i_{0}
,
V_{i}
is a
(p,q,a_{2})
-polymer since

\displaystyle\|V_{i}\|_{(p,q,a_{2})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(P_{ij})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(P_{ij})}\int_{P_{ij}}|v_{ij}|^{q}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|v_{j}|^{q}\right)^{p/q}<\infty,

and thus

\|g\|_{H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}\|V_{i}\|_{(p,q,a_{2})}\leq C\ i_{0}\|g\|_{(p,q,a_{1})}

being
g\in H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Notice that the constant does not depend on
g
. Then,

\|g\|_{H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq C\|g\|_{(p,q,a_{1})}.

Suppose now that
g=c_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g_{j}
where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and
g_{j}
is a
(p,q,a_{1})
-polymer for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|g_{j}\|_{(p,q,a_{1})}<\infty
.
We can represent each
g_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}V_{ij}
where
V_{ij}
is a
(p,q,a_{2})
-polymer. Then, as before we can estimate

\sum_{i=1}^{i_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|V_{ij}\|_{(p,q,a_{2})}\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|g_{j}\|_{(p,q,a_{1})}<\infty.

We conclude that
g\in H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
with

\|g\|_{H_{p,q,a_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq C\|g\|_{H_{p,q,a_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}

and the constant does not depend on
g
.
∎

4.2.
A covering lemma

We now introduce a covering by cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that will be very useful in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a sequence of cubes
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

(a)

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\cup_{n\in N}Q_{n}\cup E
with
E
having null Lebesgue (equivalently Gaussian) measure.

(b)

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
, where
A_{d}=2\sqrt{d}
.

(c)

There exists
C_{d}
depending only on the dimension
d
such that

\#\{j\in\mathbb{N}:Q_{n}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset\}\leq C_{d},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(d)

There exists, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 2
, a subfamily of cubes in the covering, named the
k
-th layer
L_{k}
, such that
\#L_{k}\leq Ck^{d-1}
where
C
only depends on
d
, and
if
Q\in L_{k}
then
m(c_{Q})\leq\ell_{Q}\leq 2\sqrt{d}m(c_{Q})
and there exists a constant
M
, independent of
k
, such that
\frac{1}{M}\sqrt{k}\leq|c_{Q}|\leq Mk^{d/2}
.

Proof.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A>1
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{A}
such that
m(c_{Q})\leq\ell_{Q}
and
|c_{Q}|>1/2
. Let
B
be the ball inscribed in
Q
,
M(B)
and
M(Q)
the ball and the cube obtained in the construction above,
B^{\prime}
the larger ball contained in
M(B)\cap B
and
Q^{\prime}
the cube circumscribed around
B^{\prime}
. Then there exists
K>0
independent of
Q
such that

\frac{\gamma(M(Q))}{\gamma(Q^{\prime})}\leq K.

Also if
|c_{Q}|\leq D\sqrt{d}
for some constant
D
, then
K_{Q}\leq D^{2}d
.

Proof.

Notice that if
M(B)\subset B
, then
M(Q)=Q^{\prime}
and

\frac{\gamma(M(Q))}{\gamma(Q^{\prime})}=1.

If
M(B)\subsetneq B
we distinguish 3 cases. First, suppose that
|c_{M(B)}|\geq 1
. Then

\begin{split}\ell_{M(Q)}-\ell_{Q}&=2(r_{M(B)}-r_{B})\leq m(c_{M(B)})-m(c_{B})\\
&=\frac{1}{|c_{M(B)}|}-\frac{1}{|c_{B}|}=\frac{|c_{B}|-|c_{M(B)}|}{|c_{B}||c_{M(B)}|}\\
&=\frac{r_{M(B)}}{|c_{B}||c_{M(B)}|}\leq 4r_{M(B)}^{2}r_{B}=\frac{\ell^{2}_{M(Q)}\ell_{Q}}{2}.\end{split}

Thus

\ell_{M(Q)}\leq\ell_{Q}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{2}_{M(Q)}}{2}\right).

Now,

\ell_{Q}\leq A\ell_{M(Q)}\leq A\ell_{Q}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{2}_{M(Q)}}{2}\right)\leq A\ell_{Q}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}(c_{M(Q)})}{2}\right)\leq\frac{3A}{2}\ell_{Q}.

Therefore

\frac{2}{3}\ell_{M(Q)}\leq\ell_{Q}\leq A\ell_{M(Q)}.

Since
2\ell_{Q^{\prime}}=\ell_{Q}
the conclusion follows.

Second, suppose that
M(B)\subsetneq B
and
|c_{M(B)}|\leq|c_{B}|\leq 1
. In this case,
r_{B}=2r_{B^{\prime}}\geq r_{M(B)}
and the conclusion follows immediately.

Suppose now that
|c_{M(B)}|\leq 1\leq|c_{B}|
. Then, proceeding similarly to the first case

\begin{split}\ell_{M(Q)}-\ell_{Q}\leq 2r_{M(B)}r_{B}=\frac{\ell_{M(Q)}\ell_{Q}}{2}.\end{split}

Thus

\ell_{M(Q)}\leq\ell_{Q}\left(1+\frac{\ell_{M(Q)}}{2}\right).

Now,

\ell_{Q}\leq A\ell_{M(Q)}\leq A\ell_{Q}\left(1+\frac{\ell_{M(Q)}}{2}\right)\leq A\ell_{Q}\left(1+\frac{m(c_{M(Q)})}{2}\right)\leq\frac{3A}{2}\ell_{Q}.

Therefore

\frac{2}{3}\ell_{M(Q)}\leq\ell_{Q}\leq A\ell_{M(Q)}.

Since
2\ell_{Q^{\prime}}=\ell_{Q}
the conclusion follows.

Finally, assume that
|c_{Q}|\leq D\sqrt{d}
, then

\ell_{M^{j}(Q)}=m(c_{M^{j}(Q)})\geq m(c_{Q})\geq\frac{1}{2|c_{Q}|}\geq\frac{1}{2D\sqrt{d}}.

Thus,

K_{Q}\leq 4D\sqrt{d}|c_{Q}|\leq D^{2}d.\qed

\Lambda\in(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}
.

Note first that for any
1<p^{\prime}<q^{\prime}<\infty
,
a>0
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
,
L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)
is continuously contained in
H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Indeed, let
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
. Any
g\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)
happens to be a
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)
-polymer and

\|g\|_{H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq\gamma(Q)^{1/p^{\prime}-1/q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{Q}|g|^{q^{\prime}}d\gamma\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\leq\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)}.

(4.9)

Then,
\left.\Lambda\right|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)}\in(L^{q^{\prime}}(Q,\gamma))^{\prime}
and there exists
h_{q^{\prime},Q}\in L^{q}(Q,\gamma)
such that

\Lambda(g)=\int_{Q}gh_{q^{\prime},Q}d\gamma,\quad g\in{L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)}.

Replacing
h_{q^{\prime},Q}
by
h_{q^{\prime},Q}-\gamma^{-1}(Q)\int_{Q}h_{q^{\prime},Q}d\gamma
shows that we can consider
h_{q^{\prime},Q}\in L^{q}_{0}(Q,\gamma)
.

If
H
is a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
h
is a measurable function defined on
H
we say that
h
represents
\Lambda
on all the cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
contained in
H
when for every cube
R\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
contained in
H
,
\left.h\right|_{R}
represents
\left.\Lambda\right|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(R,\gamma)}
. According to this definition we have that
h_{q^{\prime},Q}
represents
\Lambda
on all the cubes
R\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
contained in
Q
. Note that also, for every
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
h_{q^{\prime},Q}+c
represents
\Lambda
on all the cubes
R\in\mathcal{Q}_{a}
contained in
Q
.

The arguments developed in
[
16
, steps I, II and II, p. 300-301]
allow us to define a function
h^{\Lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which represents
\Lambda
on all the cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
.

Our objective is to prove that
h^{\Lambda}\in\textup{JN}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Then, by Proposition
4.1
we can conclude that
h^{\Lambda}\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. To do this, we claim first that
h^{\Lambda}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

By Proposition
4.3
it suffices to pick any value
a\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
a=A_{d}
as in Lemma
4.4
(b)
and consider the function
h^{\Lambda}
that represents
\Lambda
on all the cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
. Given
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
, there exists
\alpha_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\left.h^{\Lambda}\right|_{Q}=h_{q^{\prime},Q}+\alpha_{Q}.

Using (
4.9
),

\begin{split}\|h_{q^{\prime},Q}\|_{L^{1}(Q,\gamma)}&\leq\gamma(Q)^{1/q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{Q}|h_{q^{\prime},Q}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{1/q}\\
&\leq\gamma(Q)^{1/q^{\prime}}\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)}\leq 1}\left|\int_{Q}h_{q^{\prime},Q}gd\gamma\right|\\
&\leq\gamma(Q)^{1/q^{\prime}}\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q,\gamma)}\leq 1}\|g\|_{H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\\
&\leq\gamma(Q)^{1/q^{\prime}}\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}.\end{split}

We get

\begin{split}\|h^{\Lambda}\|_{L^{1}(Q,\gamma)}&\leq\|h_{q^{\prime},Q}\|_{L^{1}(Q,\gamma)}+|\alpha_{Q}|\gamma(Q)\\
&\leq\gamma(Q)^{1/q^{\prime}}\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}+|\alpha_{Q}|\gamma(Q).\end{split}

By proceeding as in
[
16
, p. 302]
we deduce that

|\alpha_{Q}|\leq C{K_{Q}}\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}

Then,

\|h^{\Lambda}\|_{L^{1}(Q,\gamma)}\leq C\left(\gamma(Q)^{1/q^{\prime}}+K_{Q}\gamma(Q)\right)\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}.

Now, we consider the covering
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given in Lemma
4.4
and write

\begin{split}\|h^{\Lambda}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}&\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|h^{\Lambda}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{n},\gamma)}\\
&\leq C\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\gamma(Q_{n})^{1/q^{\prime}}+K_{Q_{n}}\gamma(Q_{n})\right).\end{split}

According to
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
there exists
C>1
such that for every
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
and every
x\in Q

\frac{1}{C}\leq e^{|c_{Q}|^{2}-|x|^{2}}\leq C.

It follows that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

\gamma(Q_{n})\leq Ce^{-|c_{Q_{n}}|^{2}}\ell_{Q_{n}}^{d}

By Lemmas
4.4

(d)
and
4.5
, if
Q
is a cube in the
k
-th layer of
\{Q_{n}\}
we get

\gamma(Q)\leq Ce^{-ck}k^{-d/2},\quad\text{and}\quad K_{Q}\leq Ck,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Also by Lemma
4.4

(d)
, the number of cubes in the
k
-th layer of
\{Q_{n}\}
is controlled by
Ck^{d-1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that the constant
C
in the last three appearances only depend on the dimension
d
.

Therefore, we obtain

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{n})^{1/q^{\prime}}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(e^{-ck}k^{-d/2}k^{d-1}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}<\infty

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{Q_{n}}\gamma(Q_{n})\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-ck}k^{-d/2}k^{d-1}<\infty.

Thus, we have proved that
h^{\Lambda}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

Using the ideas in
[
5
, p. 601-602]
, we are going to see now that

K^{\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}}_{p,q}(h^{\Lambda})\leq C\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}},

where
C
does not depend on
\Lambda
.

We consider a finite pairwise disjoint family of cubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
. We can write, for every
j=1,\dots,N
,

\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{1/q}=\sup\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}h^{\Lambda}b_{j}d\gamma,

where the supremum is taken over all the functions
b_{j}\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q_{j})
such that

\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|b_{j}|^{q^{\prime}}d\gamma=1.

(4.10)

Let
\epsilon>0
. We choose, for every
j=1,\dots,N
,
b_{j}\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q_{j},\gamma)
such that (
4.10
) holds and

\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{1/q}=\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}h^{\Lambda}b_{j}d\gamma+\frac{\epsilon}{\lambda_{j}N\gamma(Q_{j})}.

Here
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset(0,\infty)
is such that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\lambda_{j}^{p^{\prime}}=1
and

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{1/q}.

We define
g=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}b_{j}
. It follows that

\begin{split}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}&=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\int_{Q_{j}}h^{\Lambda}b_{j}d\gamma+\epsilon\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}b_{j}d\gamma+\epsilon\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}gd\gamma+\epsilon\\
&=\Lambda(g)+\epsilon.\end{split}

The function
g
is a
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},A_{d})
-polymer and

\displaystyle\|g\|_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},A_{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|\lambda_{j}b_{j}|^{q^{\prime}}d\gamma\right)^{p^{\prime}/q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\lambda_{j}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}=1.

Then,
\|g\|_{H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},A_{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)}\leq 1
and
|\Lambda(g)|\leq\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},A_{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}
. We obtain

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}\leq\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},A_{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}+\epsilon.

It follows that

\inf\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma(Q_{j})\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}|h^{\Lambda}-h^{\Lambda}_{Q_{j}}|^{q}d\gamma\right)^{p/q}\right)^{1/p}\leq\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},A_{d}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}}+\epsilon,

where the infimum is taken over all the families
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of pairwise disjoint cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
, with
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The arbitrariness of
\epsilon>0
allow us to conclude that

K^{\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}}_{p,q}(h^{\Lambda})\leq C\|\Lambda\|_{(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}},

and, by virtue of Proposition
4.1
, that
h_{\Lambda}\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
.

We are going to see that there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Lambda=\Lambda_{f}
where
{f=h^{\Lambda}+\alpha}
. Let
g\in H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)\setminus\{0\}
.

Suppose that
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and consider
g=c_{0}
. We have

\Lambda(g)=c_{0}\Lambda(1)=c_{0}\left(\Lambda(1)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}d\gamma\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}gh^{\Lambda}d\gamma.

We define
\alpha=\Lambda(1)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}d\gamma
, so it is clear that
f=h^{\Lambda}+\alpha\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and
\Lambda_{f}(g)=\Lambda(g).

Suppose now that
g
is a nonconstant function in
\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Therefore,
A:=\inf\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)}>0
, where the infimum is taken over all the sequences
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)
-polymers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)}<\infty
and
g=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}
, for some
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Thus, there exists a sequence
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)
-polymers such that
g_{i}\not\equiv 0
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
g=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}
, with

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|g_{i}\|_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)}\leq 2A.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
g_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{ij}
, where for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{ij}\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{0}(Q_{ij},\gamma)
and
\supp b_{ij}\subset Q_{ij}
, being
\{Q_{ij}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a family of pairwise disjoint cubes in
\mathcal{Q}_{A_{d}}
such that

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma(Q_{ij})\left(\frac{1}{Q_{ij}}\int_{Q_{ij}}|b_{ij}|^{q^{\prime}}d\gamma\right)^{p^{\prime}/q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\leq 2\|g_{i}\|_{(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a)}.

By proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
we can see that there exists two sequences
\{i_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{j_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of nonnegative integers such that

c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{i_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{k}}b_{ij}\longrightarrow g,

as
k\rightarrow\infty
, in
H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
. Since
\Lambda\in(H_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},a}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma))^{\prime}
we get

\begin{split}\Lambda(g)&=c_{0}\Lambda(1)+\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{j_{k}}\Lambda(b_{ij})\\
&=c_{0}\Lambda(1)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}b_{ij}d\gamma,\end{split}

where the series is absolutely convergent (see part
(a)
of this proof). Then,

\Lambda(g)=c_{0}\left(\Lambda(1)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}d\gamma\right)+\Lambda_{h^{\Lambda}}(g).

We define
f=h^{\Lambda}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}d\gamma+\Lambda(1):=h^{\Lambda}+\alpha.
It is clear that
f\in\textup{JN}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\gamma)
and we also have that

\begin{split}\Lambda_{f}(g)&=c_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fd\gamma+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fb_{ij}d\gamma\\
&=c_{0}\Lambda(1)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h^{\Lambda}b_{ij}d\gamma\\
&=\Lambda(g).\end{split}

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is now complete.

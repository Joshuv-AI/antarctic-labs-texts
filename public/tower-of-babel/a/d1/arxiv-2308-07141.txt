In this paper, we are interested in the following fractional
p
-Laplacian equation

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u&=\lambda|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{p_{s}^{*}-2}u&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\

[formula]

where

,

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}
denotes the fractional

-Laplacian operator, and when

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u(x)=2\penalty\ \underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}}dy,

(-\Delta)^{s}
when

[
13

be a Carathéodory mapping, consider the general fractional
p
-Laplacian equation

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u&=f(x,u)\penalty\ \penalty\ \,\penalty\ \text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\

(1.1)

To give a weak formulation of (
1.1
), we denote the Gagliardo seminorm by

[u]_{s,p}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

Let

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):[u]_{s,p}<\infty\}

be endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}}:=(|u|_{p}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p})^{1/p},

where
|\cdot|_{p}
denotes the usual norm of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Denote the subspace

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\;\text{a.e.\penalty\ in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\},

equivalently renormed with
\|u\|=[u]_{s,p}
(see
[
13

), it is well known that
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a uniformly convex Banach space. Furthermore, the embedding
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
is continuous for

and compact for

[

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}
can be variationally regarded as an operator from

into its dual space

as follows,

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\frac{J_{u}(x,y)(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}}dxdy,\quad\forall\;v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega),

where
J_{u}(x,y)=|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))
.

We call

a weak solution (respectively weak subsolution, weak supersolution) of (
1.1

f(x,u)\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)^{*}
and

\langle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u,v\rangle=(\text{respectively}\penalty\ \leq,\,\geq)\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)vdx,\quad\forall\;v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega),v\geq 0.

For simplicity, from now on, we omit the term

in the rest of our paper. If

|f(x,t)|\leq C_{0}(1+|t|^{r-1})\quad\mbox{for }\;C_{0}>0,1<r\leq p_{s}^{*}

are disjoint, there holds

|\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}+Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{q}^{q}=|\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}|_{q}^{q}+|Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{q}^{q},

(4.11)

and

|\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}+Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}=|\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}+|Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}.

(4.12)

By a direct computation,

|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{q}^{q}=\int_{B_{\theta\delta}}u_{\varepsilon,\delta}^{q}(y)dy\geq\int_{B_{\delta}}U_{\varepsilon}(y)^{q}dy=\varepsilon^{n-\frac{(n-sp)q}{p}}\int_{B_{\delta/\varepsilon}}U(y)^{q}dy.

It follows from Lemma

,
\delta/\varepsilon\geq 2

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta/\varepsilon}}U(y)^{q}dy

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{1}^{\delta/\varepsilon}U(r)^{q}r^{n-1}dr\geq C\int_{1}^{\delta/\varepsilon}\frac{1}{r^{(n-sp)q/(p-1)}}r^{n-1}dr\geq C.

Thus,

|u_{\varepsilon,\delta}|_{q}^{q}\geq C\varepsilon^{n-\frac{(n-sp)q}{p}}.

(4.13)

By (
4.11
), (
4.13
), we have that (
4.9
) holds. Using (
4.12
) and (

), we get (
4.10
).

Step 3.

Conclusion
.

Combining (
4.4
), (
4.9
) with (

), we deduce that

\displaystyle{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}+Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta})\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda})+\frac{R^{p}}{p}\left[S^{\frac{n}{sp}}+C\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)^{(n-sp)/(p-1)}\right]-\frac{R^{p_{s}^{*}}}{p_{s}^{*}}\left[S^{\frac{n}{sp}}-C{\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)}^{\frac{n}{p-1}}\right]

\displaystyle-C_{1}R^{q}\varepsilon^{n-\frac{(n-sp)q}{p}}-C_{1}R^{p-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{n-sp}{p}}+\frac{C_{2}R^{p-2}}{\delta^{sp}}\varepsilon^{n-\frac{(n-sp)(p-2)}{p}}\left(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon}\right)^{n-\frac{(n-sp)(p-2)}{p-1}}.

Taking

with

in the above inequality, then

\displaystyle{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}+Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta})=

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda})+g_{\delta}(R),

(4.14)

with

\displaystyle g_{\delta}(R)=

\displaystyle\frac{R^{p}}{p}\left(S_{s,p}^{\frac{n}{sp}}+C\delta^{k(n-sp)/(p-1)}\right)-\frac{R^{p_{s}^{*}}}{p_{s}^{*}}\left(S_{s,p}^{\frac{n}{sp}}-C{\delta}^{\frac{kn}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle-C_{1}R^{q}\delta^{(k+1)\left[n-\frac{(n-sp)q}{p}\right]}-C_{1}R^{p-1}\delta^{\frac{(k+1)(n-sp)}{p}}+{C_{2}R^{p-2}}{\delta^{(n-sp)\left[1-\frac{(p-2)(p-k-1)}{p(p-1)}\right]}}.

Using (

\underset{R\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}{\max}g_{\delta}(R)\leq\frac{s}{n}S_{s,p}^{\frac{n}{sp}}-C_{1}R_{\delta}^{q}\delta^{(k+1)\left(n-\frac{(n-sp)q}{p}\right)}+O\left(\delta^{k(n-sp)/(p-1)}\right).

\displaystyle{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(\eta_{\delta}u_{\lambda}+Ru_{\varepsilon,\delta})=c_{s,p}-C\delta^{(k+1)\left[n-\frac{(n-sp)q}{p}\right]}+O\left(\delta^{k(n-sp)/(p-1)}\right)+O(\delta^{n-sp}).

By

We get then

(4.20)

if

Next, we proceed to check that the functional
{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition at the level

. Recall that the functional
{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}
is said satisfying the Palais-Smale condition at a level

(for short

) if any sequence
\{u_{j}\}\subset W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
such that

{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(u_{j})\rightarrow c\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\widetilde{I}}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{j})\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ in }\;W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)^{*}

admits a subsequence which is convergent in

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
1<q<p
,
n>sp
and
u_{\lambda}
be the minimal positive solution to
(P_{\lambda})
in Proposition
3.4
. Assume that
{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}
has only two critical points
0
and

{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}
satisfies the
(PS)_{c}
condition for all

Proof.

Let
\{u_{j}\}\subset W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
be a
(PS)_{c}
sequence of
{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}
with
c<c_{s,p}
, i.e.

{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(u_{j})=\frac{1}{p}\lVert u_{j}\lVert^{p}-\frac{\lambda}{q}|u^{+}_{j}|^{q}_{q}-\frac{1}{p_{s}^{*}}|u^{+}_{j}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}=c+o(1)

(4.21)

and

\displaystyle\langle{\widetilde{I}}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{j}),v\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\frac{J_{u_{j}}(x,y)(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}}dxdy-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u^{+}_{j})^{q-1}vdx

(4.22)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(u^{+}_{j})^{p_{s}^{*}-1}vdx=o(1)\lVert v\lVert\text{\penalty\ \penalty\ for\penalty\ all\penalty\ }v\in W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega).

Taking
v=u_{j}
in (
4.22
), by (

, when

\infty

\begin{split}p_{s}^{*}c+o(1)\|u_{j}\|+o(1)=&p_{s}^{*}{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(u_{j})-\langle{\widetilde{I}}^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{j}),u_{j}\rangle\\

\geq&\left(\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{p}-1\right)\|u_{j}\|^{p}-\left(\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{q}-1\right)\lambda C\|u_{j}\|^{q}.\\
\end{split}

Hence,

is bounded in

. Therefore there is a renamed subsequence of

, which converges weakly to some

and

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

in several steps.

Step 1.
We claim that

\lim_{j\to\infty}(\lVert u_{j}-u\lVert^{p}-\lVert u_{j}\lVert^{p}+\lVert u\lVert^{p})=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\liminf_{j\to\infty}(|u_{j}-u|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}-|u^{+}_{j}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}+|u^{+}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}})\geq 0.

Consider

\mathbb{R}^{2n}

[

), the first claim is done. Moreover, as

and

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Step 2.
For any

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\frac{J_{u_{j}}(x,y)(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}}dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}{\frac{J_{u}(x,y)(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}}dxdy.

Indeed, define

and

where

. Then
\{\varPhi_{j}\}
is bounded in

, and

converges weakly to

in

Step 3.
By Step 2, it is easy to see that
u
is a critical point of
{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}
, so

\lVert u\lVert^{p}=\lambda|u^{+}|_{q}^{q}+|u^{+}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}.

(4.24)

Setting

\lVert\widehat{u}_{j}\lVert^{p}=\lVert u_{j}\lVert^{p}-\lVert u\lVert^{p}+o(1)\;\;\text{and}\;\;|\widehat{u}_{j}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}\geq|u^{+}_{j}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}-|u^{+}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}+o(1).

(4.25)

Taking

is bounded in

and
u_{j}\rightarrow u

(4.26)

It follows from (

4.26

\displaystyle\|\widehat{u}_{j}\|^{p}=|{u}_{j}^{+}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}-|{u}^{+}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}+o(1)\leq|\widehat{u}_{j}|_{p_{s}^{*}}^{p_{s}^{*}}+o(1)\leq\frac{\lVert\widehat{u}_{j}\lVert^{p_{s}^{*}}}{S_{s,p}^{p_{s}^{*}/p}}+o(1),

(4.27)

so

(4.28)

By (

\displaystyle{\widetilde{I}}_{\lambda}(u_{j})

As we assume that

Using (

) and (

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and
\varphi
be a
C^{1}
functional on
X
. Taking
u,v\in X
and consider

c=\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma}{\inf}\Big[\underset{0\leq t\leq 1}{\max}\varphi(\gamma(t))\Big]

where

. Assume that
F
is a closed subset of

such that for any

\gamma([0,1])\cap\{x\in F:\varphi(x)\geq c\}\neq\varnothing
. Then there exists a sequence
\{x_{j}\}\subset X
satisfying

(i)

\underset{j\to\infty}{\lim}\mbox{dist}(x_{j},F)=0
;

(ii)

\underset{j\to\infty}{\lim}\varphi(x_{j})=c
;

(iii)

\underset{j\to\infty}{\lim}\|\varphi^{\prime}(x_{j})\|_{X^{*}}=0
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

small. Consider

Assume that

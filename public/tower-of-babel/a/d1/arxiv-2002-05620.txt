Gushel–Mukai varieties: intermediate Jacobians
Olivier Debarre, Alexander Kuznetsov

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
X
be a Gushel–Mukai variety of dimension
3
or
5
. If
A\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
is the Lagrangian subspace associated with
X
,
we prove that the intermediate Jacobian of
X
is isomorphic to the Albanese variety
of the canonical double covering
of any of the two dual Eisenbud–Popescu–Walter surfaces
Y^{\geq 2}_{A}
and
Y^{\geq 2}_{A^{\perp}}
. As an application, we describe the period maps for Gushel–Mukai varieties of dimension
3
or
5
.

Keywords.
Fano varieties; Gushel–Mukai varieties; intermediate Jacobians; EPW sextics; Albanese varieties; period maps; Abel–Jacobi maps; Grassmannians.

1. Introduction

This article is an addition to the series
[
DK18
,
DK19
,
DK20b
,
KP18
]
on the geometry of Gushel–Mukai varieties. For an introduction, we recommend the survey
[
Deb20
]
.

A smooth complex
Gushel–Mukai
(
GM
for short)
variety
of dimension
n\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}

is a smooth dimensionally transverse intersection

(1.1)

X=\CGr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W)\cap Q,

where
V_{5}
is a 5-dimensional complex vector space,
\CGr(2,V_{5})\subset{\bf P}({\bf C}\oplus\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})
is the cone
over the Grassmannian
\Gr(2,V_{5})
in its Plücker embedding,
W\subset{\bf C}\oplus\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}
is a linear subspace of dimension
n+5
,
and
Q\subset{\bf P}(W)
is a quadratic hypersurface.

GM varieties of dimension 2 are Brill–Noether general K3 surfaces of genus 6. GM varieties of dimension 4 or 6 are Fano varieties but they share some properties with K3 surfaces. For instance, their derived categories have a component of K3-type (
[
KP18
, Propositions 2.6 and 2.9]
)
and their vanishing cohomology of middle dimension is isomorphic
to a Tate twist of the primitive second cohomology of a certain hyperkähler fourfold
associated with their K3 category (
[
DK19
, Theorem 5.1]
). This allowed us to describe the period map for GM varieties of dimension 4 or 6 in
[
DK19
, Proposition 5.27]
.

GM varieties of dimension 3 or 5 are also Fano varieties but they behave differently. The nontrivial components of their derived categories
bear some of the features of the derived category of a curve (
[
KP18
, Proposition 2.9]
)
and the Hodge structure on their middle cohomology defines their intermediate Jacobian,
a 10-dimensional principally polarized abelian variety (
[
DK19
, Proposition 3.1]
). The main goal of this article is to describe the intermediate Jacobians and period maps of GM varieties of dimension 3 or 5.

The key object we use to study a GM variety
X
is its associated Lagrangian data set constructed in
[
DK18
]
. It is a triple
(V_{6}(X),V_{5}(X),A(X))
(or
(V_{6},V_{5},A)
for short) that consists
of a 6-dimensional vector space
V_{6}
, a hyperplane
V_{5}\subset V_{6}
,
and a subspace
A\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
which is Lagrangian with respect to the symplectic form on
\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
given by exterior product,
and contains no decomposable vectors (this means no nonzero products
v_{1}\wedge v_{2}\wedge v_{3}
, where
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in V_{6}
). A GM variety can be reconstructed from its Lagrangian data set (
[
DK18
, Theorem 3.6]
). Moreover, Lagrangian data sets can be used to describe the moduli stack of smooth GM varieties and its coarse moduli space (
[
DK20b
]
).

It is not surprising then that
many geometric properties of a GM variety can be described in terms of its Lagrangian data set,
particularly in terms of the
Eisenbud–Popescu–Walter
(EPW for short)
varieties

Y_{A}^{\geq 3}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 2}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 1}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 0}={\bf P}(V_{6}),

where
Y_{A}^{\geq 1}
is a sextic hypersurface (called an
EPW sextic
) with singular locus
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
, itself an integral surface with singular locus the finite set
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
,
and the
dual EPW varieties

Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 3}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 2}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 1}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 0}={\bf P}(V_{6}^{\vee})

associated with the Lagrangian subspace
A^{\bot}\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}^{\vee}
(see Section
2.2
for the definitions).

Let
A
be a Lagrangian with no decomposable vectors (such as
A(X)
and
A(X)^{\bot}
). O’Grady constructed a canonical double covering

\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 1}\longrightarrow Y_{A}^{\geq 1},

étale away from the surface
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
. When
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
is empty (this holds for
A
general),
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 1}
is a hyperkähler fourfold
called a
double EPW sextic
. When
X
is a GM variety of even dimension, the double EPW sextic
\widetilde{Y}_{A(X)}^{\geq 1}
is the hyperkähler fourfold mentioned above
whose primitive second cohomology is isomorphic to a Tate twist of the vanishing middle cohomology of
X
.

We also defined in
[
DK20a
, Theorem 5.2(2)]
a canonical double covering

\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}\longrightarrow Y_{A}^{\geq 2},

étale away from the finite set
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
, where
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
is an integral surface (called a
double EPW surface
)
which has an ordinary double point over each point of
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
and is smooth elsewhere;
in particular,
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
is smooth for
A
general. It has a 10-dimensional Albanese variety
\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A})
which, when
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
is singular,
can be defined as the Albanese variety of any desingularization.

The first main result of this article is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any Lagrangian subspace
A\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
with no decomposable vectors,
the Albanese variety
\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A})
has a canonical principal polarization such that there is an isomorphism

(1.2)

\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A})\simeq\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A^{\perp}})

of principally polarized abelian varieties.

If
X
is a smooth GM variety of dimension
n\in\{3,5\}
, with intermediate Jacobian
\Jac(X)
and associated Lagrangian subspace
A
,
there is a canonical isomorphism

(1.3)

H_{n}(X,{\bf Z})\simeq H_{1}({\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A}},{\bf Z})

of polarized Hodge structures. It induces an isomorphism

(1.4)

\Jac(X)\simeq\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A})

of principally polarized abelian varieties.

We prove this in Theorem
4.4
for GM threefolds
X
with
Y_{A(X)}^{\geq 3}=\varnothing

and in Theorem
5.3
for GM fivefolds
X
with
Y_{A(X)}^{\geq 3}=\varnothing
. In particular, we use the natural principal polarization of the intermediate Jacobian
\Jac(X)

to produce a principal polarization on
\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)})
and we deduce the isomorphism (
1.2
)
from a birational isomorphism (a line transform) between two GM threefolds
X
and
X^{\prime}

such that
A(X^{\prime})=A(X)^{\perp}
(such pairs are called
period duals
in
[
DK18
]
). The extension to arbitrary GM threefolds and fivefolds is given in Section
6
.

Remark 1.2
.

We are not aware of
a direct proof of the isomorphism (
1.2
). It can be thought of as a Hodge-theoretic incarnation of the equivalence
between the nontrivial components of derived categories of odd-dimensional GM varieties
conjectured in
[
KP18
, Conjecture 3.7]
and proved in
[
KP19
, Corollary 6.5]
. It would be interesting to extract the principal polarization on
\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A})

from the categorical data and to deduce the isomorphism (
1.2
) from the equivalence of categories.

One can also think of the principal polarization on
\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A})

as of an element of

H^{2}(\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A}),{\bf Z})=\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{H}^{1}(\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A}),{\bf Z})=\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{H}^{1}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},{\bf Z}).

The latter group maps, by cup-product, to
H^{2}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},{\bf Z})^{+}
(the invariant space for the canonical covering involution on
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
)
with finite (but nontrivial) cokernel. The principal polarization maps to a class
3\nu
in
\NS(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2})^{+}
,
where
\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}}(1)=2\nu
(thus,
\nu
is the class of one of the components of the curve (
1.5
) discussed below);
this follows from Welters’ work on the variety of lines on quartic double solids
(see the proof of Proposition
2.5
and
[
Wel81
, (3.32), Proposition (3.60), and p. 70]
).

Let
X
be a smooth GM variety of dimension
n\in\{3,5\}
and assume
Y^{3}_{A(X)}=\varnothing
. To prove the isomorphisms (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), it is natural to construct a subscheme or a cycle

Z\subset X\times\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}

of codimension
\tfrac{n+1}{2}

and use the Abel–Jacobi map
\AJ_{Z}\colon H_{1}(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)},{\bf Z})\to H_{{n}}(X,{\bf Z})
. For this, one needs an interpretation of the double EPW surface
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
(or some other closely related surface)
as a moduli space of sheaves or as a parameter space of cycles on
X
.

When
X
is a GM threefold, the most natural moduli space of sheaves to consider is the Hilbert scheme of conics on
X
. This scheme was thoroughly studied in
[
Log12
]
and
[
DIM12
, Section 6]
; in
[
DK20+
]
,
we prove that it is isomorphic to the blow up of a point of the dual double EPW surface
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)^{\perp}}
. Similarly, for a GM fivefold
X
, one could use the Hilbert scheme of quadric surfaces in
X
;
we proved in
[
DK20+
]
that it has a connected component isomorphic to a
{\bf P}^{1}
-bundle over
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)^{\perp}}
. In the case of GM threefolds, it is claimed in
[
IM11
, Section 5.1]
and
[
IM07
, Theorem 9]

that the Clemens–Letizia degeneration method can be applied to prove that
the Abel–Jacobi map given by the universal conic is an isomorphism;
however, it is not clear whether this method would work in the case of GM fivefolds, so we need a different approach.

Another possible approach in the case of a GM threefold
X
would be to use the moduli space
\mathscr{M}_{X}(2;1,5)

of Gieseker semistable rank-2 torsion-free sheaves on
X
with
{\mathrm{c}}_{1}=1
,
{\mathrm{c}}_{2}=5
, and
{\mathrm{c}}_{3}=0
. This space was shown in
[
DIM12
, Section 8]
to be birational to the Hilbert scheme of conics on
X^{\prime}
,
a line transform of
X
(see Section
4.5
), hence to the double EPW surface
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
;
the natural correspondence is provided by the second Chern class of the universal sheaf on the product
X\times\mathscr{M}_{X}(2;1,5)
. We use a small modification of this construction in which the moduli space of sheaves is kept implicit. We explain it below.

If
X
is a GM threefold and
L_{0}\subset X
is a line,
the corresponding (inverse) line transform
X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X
takes a general conic
C^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}

to a rational quartic curve
C\subset X
to which the line
L_{0}
is bisecant
(the corresponding rank-2 sheaf on
X
can then be obtained by Serre’s construction
applied to
C\cup L_{0}
; in particular, the curve
C\cup L_{0}
represents the second Chern class of this sheaf). We consider the union
C\cup L_{0}
as a quintic curve of arithmetic genus 1 on
X
containing the line
L_{0}

and construct the correspondence
Z
as the closure of a family of such curves
parameterized by an open subscheme of
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
.

To prove that the Abel–Jacobi map
\AJ_{Z}
associated with this family of curves is an isomorphism,
we make the crucial observation that over the curve

(1.5)

Y^{\geq 2}_{A(X),V_{5}(X)}:=Y^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}\cap{\bf P}(V_{5}(X)),

the double covering
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}\to Y^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
splits,
and that over a general point
y
of one of the components of its preimage, there is a relation

Z_{y}+L_{y}=S_{y}\cap X
in the Chow group
\operatorname{CH}_{1}(X)
of 1-cycles. Here
Z_{y}
is the fiber of the correspondence
Z
over
y
,

L_{y}
is a line on
X
, and
S_{y}
is a cubic surface scroll on the fourfold
M_{X}:=\CGr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W)
. Moreover, the curve (
1.5
) is birational to the Hilbert scheme
F_{1}(X)
of lines on
X

and the line
L_{y}
comes from the universal family of lines over
F_{1}(X)
.

From these observations and from the vanishing of the odd cohomology of
M_{X}
, it follows that for
X
general,
there is a morphism
\phi\colon F_{1}(X)\to\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}

such that the composition

H_{1}(F_{1}(X),{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\ \phi_{*}\ }H_{1}(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)},{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\ \AJ_{Z}\ }H_{3}(X,{\bf Z})

is the opposite of the Abel–Jacobi map defined by the universal family of lines. The latter map is surjective by an argument of Clemens–Tyurin (see Section
3.3
),
hence
\AJ_{Z}
is surjective as well. It is not hard to check that the source and target of
\AJ_{Z}
are free abelian groups of rank 20,
hence
\AJ_{Z}
is an isomorphism.

A similar argument works for GM fivefolds:
rational quartic curves are replaced by rational quartic surface scrolls,
reducible quintic curves by reducible quintic del Pezzo surfaces,
the Hilbert scheme of lines by a component of the Hilbert scheme of planes,
and a higher-dimensional analogue of the Clemens–Tyurin argument is applied.

For GM fivefolds
X
, the isomorphism (
1.4
) may be proved by a completely different topological argument. When
X
is general, we consider the double cover
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X),V_{5}(X)}

of the curve (
1.5
)
induced by the double covering
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}\to Y^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
; in contrast with the case of GM threefolds, this is a smooth curve of genus 161. Using classical monodromy arguments, we prove that its Jacobian has three simple factors:
the Jacobian of the curve
Y^{\geq 2}_{A(X),V_{5}(X)}
(of dimension 81),
the Albanese variety of the surface
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
(of dimension 10),
and a simple factor of dimension 70. The curve
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X),V_{5}(X)}
parameterizes planes on
X
(see Section
2.5.1
)
and the corresponding Abel–Jacobi map

H_{1}(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X),V_{5}(X)},{\bf Z})\longrightarrow H_{5}(X,{\bf Z})

is surjective by a generalization of the Clemens–Tyurin argument. The induced surjective morphism

\Jac(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X),V_{5}(X)})\longrightarrow\Jac(X)

therefore has connected kernel. The description of the simple factors implies that it has to be isogeneous to the product of the 81-dimensional and 70-dimensional factors. Therefore,
\Jac(X)
is isomorphic to the remaining 10-dimensional factor
\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)})
.

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
and to describe the period maps for GM varieties of dimension 3 or 5,
we investigate the rational map

(1.6)

\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}=\LGr(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6})/\!\!/\PGL(V_{6})\dashrightarrow\mathbf{A}_{10}

from the coarse moduli space of EPW sextics (constructed in
[
O’Gr08a
, Section 6]
)
to the coarse moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension 10
defined by

{[A]\longmapsto[\Alb(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2})]}

when
A
has no decomposable vectors and
Y^{{\geq 3}}_{A}=\varnothing
.

Let
\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}_{\mathrm{ndv}}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}
be the open subset parameterizing Lagrangian subspaces with no decomposable vectors
and let
\mathbf{r}
be the involution of
\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}_{\mathrm{ndv}}
defined by
[A]\mapsto[A^{\perp}]
(see
[
O’Gr08b
]
). We show in Proposition
6.2

that the map (
1.6
) extends to a regular morphism

\bar{\wp}\colon\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}_{\mathrm{ndv}}/\mathbf{r}\longrightarrow\mathbf{A}_{10}

such that
\bar{\wp}([A])
is the Albanese variety of (any desingularization of) the double EPW surface
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
.

Let now
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}
be the coarse moduli space of GM varieties of dimension
n
(see
[
DK20b
]
and Section
2.3
). We use the above result to prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\in\{3,5\}
, the period map
\wp_{n}\colon\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}\to\mathbf{A}_{10}
factors as the composition

\wp_{n}\colon\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}\longrightarrow\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}_{\mathrm{ndv}}\longrightarrow\mathbf{M}^{\rm EPW}_{\mathrm{ndv}}/\mathbf{r}\xrightarrow{\ \bar{\wp}\ }\mathbf{A}_{10},

where the first map is given by
[X]\mapsto[A(X)]
and the second map is the canonical projection. In particular,
\wp_{n}([X])=[\Alb(\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)})]
.

This factorization
of the period map for GM threefolds was discussed in the introduction of
[
DIM12
]
(see also
[
DIM12
, Remark 7.5]
);
moreover, it was conjectured there that the map
\bar{\wp}
is generically injective
(the computation in
[
DIM12
, Theorem 5.1]
shows that it has finite fibers).

The story of GM threefolds is very similar to the story of quartic double solids. The articles
[
Wel81
,
Voi88
]
were an inspiration to us;
in particular, we took the idea of using the Clemens–Tyurin argument from
[
Wel81
]
.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the theory of GM varieties, EPW varieties, and their double covers. In particular, we describe the Hilbert scheme of lines on GM threefolds, the Hilbert scheme of
\sigma
-planes on GM fivefolds,
and we identify double EPW varieties with the canonical covers of degeneracy loci for the family of quadrics containing a GM variety.

In Section
3
, we recall basic facts about Abel–Jacobi maps,
prove a generalization of the Clemens–Tyurin argument,
and discuss the endomorphism ring of intermediate Jacobians;
in particular, we check that the intermediate Jacobian of a very general GM variety of odd dimension is simple and has Picard number 1
(this had already been proved for GM threefolds by a different argument in
[
DIM12
, Corollary 5.3]
).

In Section
4
, we construct, for any GM threefold
X
, a cycle
Z\subset X\times\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A(X)}
, and prove that the Abel-Jacobi map defined by
Z
is an isomorphism when
Y^{\geq 3}_{A(X)}=\varnothing
. We also describe how the line transform of GM threefolds acts on their coarse moduli spaces. In Section
5
, we prove analogous results for GM fivefolds. Finally, we describe in Section
6
the period map for GM threefolds and fivefolds
and prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

2. Gushel–Mukai and Eisenbud–Popescu–Walter varieties

We work over the field of complex numbers. Given a subvariety
X
of a projective space, we denote by
F^{k}(X)
the Hilbert scheme
parameterizing linear spaces of dimension
k
in
X
.

2.1. Geometry of
\Gr(2,5)

Let
V_{5}
be a 5-dimensional vector space. A subspace of
V_{5}
of dimension
k
will usually be denoted by
V_{k}
or
U_{k}
. We denote by

\Gr(2,V_{5})\subset{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})

the Grassmannian of 2-dimensional vector subspaces in
V_{5}
in its Plücker embedding.

We recall some standard facts about its geometry. It has codimension 3 and degree 5 and is the intersection, for
v\in V_{5}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, of the
Plücker quadrics

(2.1)

Q_{v}:=\Cone_{{\bf P}(v\wedge V_{5})}(\Gr(2,V_{5}/{\bf C}v))\subset{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}).

In the next lemma, we describe Hilbert schemes of linear subspaces on
\Gr(2,V_{5})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have the following isomorphisms:

(a)

F^{1}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))\simeq\Fl(1,3;V_{5})
; the line corresponding to a flag
V_{1}\subset V_{3}\subset V_{5}

is the set of all
[U_{2}]\in\Gr(2,V_{5})
such that
V_{1}\subset U_{2}\subset V_{3}
.

(b)

F^{2}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))=\Fl(1,4;V_{5})\sqcup\Gr(3,V_{5})
;
the plane
corresponding to a flag
V_{1}\subset V_{4}\subset V_{5}

is the set of all
[U_{2}]\in\Gr(2,V_{5})
such that
V_{1}\subset U_{2}\subset V_{4}
,
and the plane
corresponding to a subspace
V_{3}\subset V_{5}

is the set of all
[U_{2}]\in\Gr(2,V_{5})
such that
U_{2}\subset V_{3}
.

(c)

F^{3}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))\simeq{\bf P}(V_{5})
;
the linear
3
-space corresponding to a subspace
V_{1}\subset V_{5}

is the set of all
2
-spaces
[U_{2}]\in\Gr(2,V_{5})
such that
V_{1}\subset U_{2}
.

(d)

F^{4}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))=\varnothing
: there are no linear
4
-spaces on
\Gr(2,V_{5})
.

Planes on
\Gr(2,V_{5})
parameterized by the two components in Lemma
2.1
(b)
are traditionally known as
\sigma
-planes
and
\tau
-planes
. We use the notation

F^{2}_{\sigma}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))\simeq\Fl(1,4;V_{5})

for the connected component of
F^{2}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))
parameterizing
\sigma
-planes.

If a finite morphism
\gamma\colon X\to\Gr(2,V_{5})
is compatible with the polarizations,
it induces a morphism
F^{k}(\gamma)\colon F^{k}(X)\to F^{k}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))
between Hilbert schemes
and we denote by

(2.2)

F^{2}_{\sigma}(X)\subset F^{2}(X)

the preimage of
F^{2}_{\sigma}(\Gr(2,V_{5}))
.

We will need the following classical result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V_{2}\subset V_{5}
be a
2
-dimensional subspace. We have the equality

\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(V_{2}\wedge V_{5})=\Cone_{{\bf P}({\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.28453pt{V}_{2})}({\bf P}(V_{2})\times{\bf P}(V_{5}/V_{2}))

in
{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})
, where the right side is the cone over the cubic scroll.

Typically, an intersection
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}^{5}
is 2-dimensional (and is a quintic del Pezzo surface). In the next lemma, we discuss some pathological intersections.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
{\bf P}^{5}\subset{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})
is a linear subspace such that
\dim(\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}^{5})=3
. The only possible
3
-dimensional component of
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}^{5}
of even degree is a hyperplane section of some
\Gr(2,V_{4})\subset\Gr(2,V_{5})
.

Proof.

Write
{\bf P}^{5}={\bf P}(W_{6})
, where
W_{6}\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}
. Let
W_{6}^{\perp}\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}^{\vee}
be the orthogonal complement of
W_{6}

and set
T:={\bf P}(W_{6}^{\perp})\cap\Gr(2,V_{5}^{\vee})
. This is a subscheme of
{\bf P}(W_{6}^{\perp})\simeq{\bf P}^{3}
, hence
\dim(T)\leq 3
. We discuss all the possibilities for
\dim(T)
and check the claim in each case.

Assume first
\dim(T)\leq 1
. We then have
{\bf P}(W_{8}^{\perp})\cap\Gr(2,V_{5}^{\vee})=\varnothing
for a general subspace
W_{8}\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}
containing
W_{6}
: hence
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{8})
is a smooth quintic del Pezzo fourfold (
[
DK18
, Proposition 2.24]
)
not contained in a hyperplane. Its Picard group is
{\bf Z}
, hence for any subspace
W_{7}\subset W_{8}
containing
W_{6}
,
its hyperplane section
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{7})
is an irreducible threefold not contained in a hyperplane. Therefore, the hyperplane section
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})
of this threefold has no 3-dimensional components.

Assume now
\dim(T)=2
. Since
\Gr(2,V_{5}^{\vee})
is an intersection of quadrics, so is
T
, hence
T

is either an irreducible quadric surface or contains a plane.

If
T
contains a plane, we have, by Lemma
2.1
(b),
either
{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{2}^{\perp})\subset T
, in which case
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})
is a hyperplane section
of
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(V_{2}\wedge V_{5})
hence, by Lemma
2.2
, an irreducible threefold of degree 3,
or
{\bf P}(V_{1}^{\perp}\wedge V_{4}^{\perp})\subset T
, in which case
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})
is a hyperplane section
of
\Gr(2,V_{4})\cup{\bf P}(V_{1}\wedge V_{4})
, hence is the union of a hyperplane section of
\Gr(2,V_{4})
and of a linear 3-space.

If
T
is an irreducible quadric, we have
{\bf P}(W_{6}^{\perp})\subset{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{1}^{\perp})

and
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})
is the union of
{\bf P}(V_{1}\wedge V_{5})
and two planes (or a double plane)
corresponding to the intersection of
\Gr(2,V_{5}/V_{1})
with the line
{\bf P}^{1}
given by the orthogonal of
W_{6}^{\perp}\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{1}^{\perp}
.
Therefore, the only 3-dimensional component of
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})
has degree 1.

Finally, assume
\dim(T)=3
. By Lemma
2.1
(c), we have
W_{6}^{\perp}=V_{4}^{\perp}\wedge V_{5}^{\vee}

and
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})=\Gr(2,V_{4})
has dimension 4.

Therefore, the only case when
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W_{6})
has a 3-dimensional component of even degree
is the case when
T
contains a
\sigma
-plane, and in this case, this component is a hyperplane section of
\Gr(2,V_{4})
.
∎

We will also need the following standard locally free resolution
for the cone
\CGr(2,V_{5})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There is an exact sequence

0\to\mathscr{O}(-5)\to V_{5}^{\vee}\otimes\mathscr{O}(-3)\to V_{5}\otimes\mathscr{O}(-2)\to\mathscr{O}\to\mathscr{O}_{\CGr(2,V_{5})}\to 0

of coherent sheaves on

2.2. Eisenbud–Popescu–Walter varieties and their double coverings

Let
V_{6}
be a 6-dimensional vector space. We consider subspaces
A\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
that are Lagrangian for the symplectic form given by exterior product. Those that contain no decomposable vectors (that is, such that
{\bf P}(A)\cap\Gr(3,V_{6})=\varnothing
) are parameterized by the complement

(2.3)

\LGr_{\rm ndv}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6})\subset\LGr(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6})

of a hypersurface in the Lagrangian Grassmannian
\LGr(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6})
.

Given a Lagrangian subspace
A\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
, one defines its
EPW varieties
; they form
a chain

Y_{A}^{\geq 4}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 3}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 2}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 1}\subset Y_{A}^{\geq 0}={\bf P}(V_{6})

of closed subschemes, where
Y_{A}^{\geq k}

is, at least set-theoretically, the set of points
[v]
in
{\bf P}(V_{6})
such that
\dim(A\cap(v\wedge\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}))\geq k

(the scheme structures were defined in
[
DK20a
, (18)]
).

The Lagrangian subspace
A\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}
defines a Lagrangian subspace
{A^{\perp}}\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}^{\vee}

hence, as above,
dual EPW varieties

Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 4}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 3}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 2}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 1}\subset Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 0}={\bf P}(V_{6}^{\vee}).

Set-theoretically, the variety
Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq k}
is the set of points
[V_{5}]
in
{\Gr(5,V_{6})={\bf P}(V_{6}^{\vee})}

such that
\dim(A\cap\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})\geq k
. We also use the notation

Y^{k}_{A}=Y^{\geq k}_{A}\smallsetminus Y^{\geq k+1}_{A}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad Y^{k}_{A^{\perp}}=Y^{\geq k}_{A^{\perp}}\smallsetminus Y^{\geq k+1}_{A^{\perp}}.

Assume for the rest of this section that
A
contains no decomposable vectors. A combination of results of O’Grady (see
[
DK18
, Theorem B.2]
) gives the following:

•

Y_{A}:=Y_{A}^{\geq 1}
is a normal sextic hypersurface (called an
EPW sextic
);

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 2}=\Sing(Y_{A})
is a normal integral surface of degree 40 (called an
EPW surface
);

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 3}=\Sing(Y_{A}^{\geq 2})
is a finite scheme, empty when
A
is general;

•

Y_{A}^{\geq 4}
is empty.

Note that the Lagrangian subspace
{A^{\perp}}
also contains no decomposable vectors and analogous statements hold for dual EPW varieties.

EPW varieties have canonical double coverings. First, there is a double covering

\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 0}_{A}\longrightarrow Y^{\geq 0}_{A}={\bf P}(V_{6})

branched along the EPW sextic
Y_{A}
. Next,
O’Grady constructed in
[
O’Gr13
, Section 1.2]
a canonical double covering

\widetilde{Y}_{A}\longrightarrow Y_{A}

étale away from
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
. When
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}=\varnothing
, the scheme

\widetilde{Y}_{A}
is a smooth hyperkähler fourfold (called a
double EPW sextic
). Finally, in
[
DK20a
, Theorem 5.2(2)]
, we constructed a canonical double covering

(2.4)

\pi_{A}\colon\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}\longrightarrow Y_{A}^{\geq 2}

étale away from
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}
, where
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
is an integral normal surface (called a
double EPW surface
),
and proved an isomorphism

(2.5)

\pi_{A*}\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{Y_{A}^{\geq 2}}\oplus\omega_{Y_{A}^{\geq 2}}(-3).

The double coverings
\pi_{A}
are the main characters of this article. We now prove some results about the surface
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
that will be needed later on.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a Lagrangian subspace with no decomposable vectors and assume
Y_{A}^{\geq 3}=\varnothing
,
so that
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
and
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
are smooth connected projective surfaces. One has

(2.6)

H^{1}(Y_{A}^{\geq 2},\mathscr{O}_{Y_{A}^{\geq 2}})=0,\qquad H^{1}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}})\simeq A,

where the isomorphism is canonical, and the abelian group
H_{1}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},{\bf Z})
is free of rank
20
.

Proof.

From (
2.5
) and Serre duality, we deduce that there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{1}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{1}(Y_{A}^{\geq 2},\mathscr{O}_{Y_{A}^{\geq 2}})\oplus H^{1}(Y_{A}^{\geq 2},\omega_{Y_{A}^{\geq 2}}(-3))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{1}(Y_{A}^{\geq 2},\mathscr{O}_{Y_{A}^{\geq 2}})\oplus H^{1}(Y_{A}^{\geq 2},\mathscr{O}(3))^{\vee}.

From the table in
[
DK19
, Corollary B.5]
, we see that the first summand vanishes,
whereas the second summand is canonically isomorphic to
A
. This proves the statements (
2.6
) of the proposition.

To prove that
H_{1}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},{\bf Z})
is torsion-free, we use a degeneration argument. Let
S\subset{{\bf P}^{3}}

be a smooth quartic surface containing no lines. Ferretti proved in
[
Fer12
, Proposition 4.1 and Corollary 4.2]
that there is a smooth deformation
of the surface
Y_{A}^{\geq 2}
to the surface
\Bit(S)\subset{\Gr(2,4)}
of bitangent lines to
S
.

Let
X\to{\bf P}^{3}
be the double solid branched over
S
. The variety
F_{1}(X)
of lines on
{X}
is a connected surface (
[
Wel81
, Remark (3.58)a)]
)
and the canonical map
F_{1}({X})\to\Bit(S)
is a double étale cover (
[
Wel81
, Corollary (1.3)]
)
whose associated order-2 line bundle on
\Bit(S)
is
{\omega}_{\Bit(S)}(-3)
(
[
Wel81
, Propositions (3.1)a) and (3.35)]
). It then follows from (
2.5
) that the surface
F_{1}({X})
is a smooth deformation of the surface
\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2}
. They are therefore diffeomorphic. The statement that
H_{1}(\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{\geq 2},{\bf Z})
is free of rank
20
then follows
from the analogous statement for
H_{1}(F_{1}({X}),{\bf Z})
proved in
[
Wel81
, Section 6, Proposition, p. 71]
.
∎

2.3. Gushel–Mukai varieties

Let
n\in\{3,4,5,6\}
. As recalled in the introduction (see (
1.1
)), a Gushel–Mukai variety of dimension
n

is a dimensionally transverse intersection

X=\CGr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W)\cap Q.

It is the intersection in
{\bf P}(W)
of the 6-dimensional space
V_{6}(X)\subset\Sym^{2}(W^{\vee})

of quadrics containing
X
, generated by the space

V_{5}(X):=V_{5}=\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{4}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}^{\vee}\subset\Sym^{2}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}^{\vee})

of (the restrictions to
W
of) Plücker quadrics and the quadric
Q
. In particular,
one can replace
Q
by any other quadric in the space
V_{6}(X)\smallsetminus V_{5}(X)
. The intersection

(2.7)

M_{X}:=\CGr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W)

is called the
Grassmannian hull
of
X
.
There are two types of GM varieties:

•

if
M_{X}
does not contain the vertex of the cone
\CGr(2,V_{5})
,
then
M_{X}\simeq\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W)
is a linear section of
\Gr(2,V_{5})
and
X=M_{X}\cap Q
is a quadratic section of
M_{X}
;
these GM varieties are called
ordinary
;

•

if
M_{X}
contains the vertex of the cone
\CGr(2,V_{5})
,
then
M_{X}
is a cone over
M^{\prime}_{X}=\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}(W^{\prime})
, a linear section of
\Gr(2,V_{5})
,
and
X\to M^{\prime}_{X}
is a double covering branched along a quadratic section
X^{\prime}=M^{\prime}_{X}\cap Q^{\prime}
;
these GM varieties are called
special
.

When
X
is a special GM variety of dimension
n
, the variety
X^{\prime}
is an ordinary GM variety of dimension
n-1
;
the varieties
X
and
X^{\prime}
are called
opposite
GM varieties.

With every GM variety
X
, we associated in
[
DK18
, Section 3.2]
a
Lagrangian data set

(V_{6}(X),V_{5}(X),A(X))
consisting of

•

the 6-dimensional space
V_{6}(X)
of quadrics containing
X
,

•

the hyperplane
V_{5}(X)\subset V_{6}(X)
of Plücker quadrics,

•

a Lagrangian subspace
A(X)\subset\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6}(X)
.

The Lagrangian data sets of a GM variety and of its opposite GM variety coincide.

Many properties of
X
are related to properties of its Lagrangian data set. For instance, when
X
is smooth and
\dim(X)\geq 3
,
the space
A(X)
contains no decomposable vectors (
[
DK18
, Theorem 3.16]
) and
\dim(A(X)\cap\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5}(X))\leq 3
.

Conversely, if
(V_{6},V_{5},A)
is a Lagrangian data set such that
A

contains no decomposable vectors and
\ell=\dim(A\cap\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})\leq 3
,
there are exactly two smooth GM varieties
X
such that

(V_{6}(X),V_{5}(X),A(X))=(V_{6},V_{5},A):

one ordinary GM variety
of dimension
5-\ell
and one special GM variety
of dimension
6-\ell
(
[
DK18
, Theorem 3.10]
);
they are opposite of one another.

In
[
DK20b
]
, we upgraded the above constructions to a description
of the moduli stack
\mathfrak M_{n}^{\rm GM}
of smooth GM varieties of dimension
n

and its coarse moduli space
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}
. In particular, we showed in
[
DK20b
, Theorem 5.15(a)]

that the coarse moduli space of smooth GM varieties of dimension

is the quasiprojective GIT quotient

(2.8)

\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}=\{(A,V_{5})\in\LGr_{\rm ndv}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6})\times{\bf P}(V_{6}^{\vee})\mid\dim(A\cap\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})\in\{5-n,6-n\}\}/\!\!/\PGL(V_{6})

(see (

) for the notation). In particular, as explained in
[
DK20b
, Section 6.1]
, there is a map

(2.9)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}\colon\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\LGr_{{\rm ndv}}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{6})/\!\!/\PGL(V_{6})

\displaystyle{[X]}

\displaystyle\longmapsto{[A(X)]}

and

(2.10)

\mathfrak{p}_{n}^{-1}([A])\simeq(Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 5-n}\smallsetminus Y_{A^{\perp}}^{\geq 7-n})/\!\!/\PGL(V_{6})_{A}

(when
n=6
, the right side is
Y_{A^{\perp}}^{0}/\!\!/\PGL(V_{6})_{A}

\PGL(V_{6})

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth GM variety of odd dimension
n\in\{3,5\}
.

•

The even cohomology
H^{\mathrm{even}}(X,{\bf Z})
is pure Tate of ranks
(1,1,1,1)
when
n=3
, and
(1,1,2,2,1,1)
when
n=5
.

•

For the odd cohomology, we have

H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X,{\bf Z})=H^{n}(X,{\bf Z})\simeq{\bf Z}^{20},\qquad F^{(n+3)/2}H^{n}(X,{\bf C})=0.

If
X
is moreover ordinary, so that its Grassmannian hull

is smooth,

•

the even cohomology
H^{\mathrm{even}}(M_{X},{\bf Z})
is pure Tate of ranks
(1,1,2,1,1)
when

,
and
(1,1,2,2,2,1,1)

•

the odd cohomology

∎

We will also need the following result.

Lemma 2.7
.

A smooth GM fivefold contains no quadric threefold whose image in
\Gr(2,V_{5})
is a hyperplane section of some
\Gr(2,V_{4})
.

Proof.

Let
Q\subset\CGr(2,V_{5})
be a quadric threefold contained in a smooth GM fivefold
X
. Then
Q
does not contain the vertex of the cone
\CGr(2,V_{5})
(because
X
does not),
hence its projection from the vertex to
\Gr(2,V_{5})
is well defined. Assume it is
a hyperplane section of some
\Gr(2,V_{4})
. Then
Q
is a local complete intersection and its normal bundle splits as

N_{Q/\!\CGr(2,V_{5})}\simeq\mathscr{U}^{\vee}\oplus\mathscr{O}(1)^{\oplus 2},

where
\mathscr{U}
is the restriction of the tautological bundle of
\Gr(2,V_{5})
. Any GM fivefold
X
is the intersection of a hyperplane and a quadric in
\CGr(2,V_{5})
. If
X
contains
Q
, the differentials of the equations of
X
give a morphism

N_{Q/\!\CGr(2,V_{5})}\to\mathscr{O}(1)\oplus\mathscr{O}(2).

Clearly,
X
is singular at any degeneracy point of that morphism. If
X
is smooth, this morphism is therefore surjective, hence its kernel is a vector bundle of rank 2. This is absurd, since a simple computation shows that its third Chern class is nonzero. Therefore,
X
cannot contain
Q
.
∎

If

containing

\mathscr{Q}\subset{\bf P}(W)\times{\bf P}(V_{6}(X))

Lemma 2.8

Proof.

By
[
DK20a
, Theorem 5.2]
, the

\mathscr{A}_{1}=A\otimes\mathscr{O}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathscr{A}_{2}=\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{T}_{{\bf P}(V_{6})}(-3)

over
{\bf P}(V_{6})
. To identify the double coverings, we will show that the family of quadrics (

can be related to this pair by the isotropic reduction procedure of
[
DK20a
, Section 4.2]

[
DK20a

3. Topological preliminaries

In
[
Tju72
, Section 4.3]
, Tyurin gave a beautiful argument (which he attributed to Clemens) proving the surjectivity of the Abel–Jacobi map
given by the universal line on a threefold. In this section, we recall this argument and prove the generalization
on which our results about GM threefolds and fivefolds are based. In Section
3.4
, we use Picard–Lefschetz theory
to show that intermediate Jacobians of very general GM varieties of dimensions 3 or 5
have trivial endomorphism rings.

3.1. Abel–Jacobi maps

We start by recalling a few properties of Abel–Jacobi maps.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth proper varieties of respective dimensions
d_{X}
and
d_{Y}
,
let
Z
be an algebraic cycle of dimension
d_{Y}+c
on
X\times Y
, and let
k
be an integer. The Abel–Jacobi map

\AJ_{Z}\colon H_{k}(Y,{\bf Z})\longrightarrow H_{k+2c}(X,{\bf Z})

is defined as the composition

H_{k}(Y,{\bf Z})\isomlra H^{2d_{Y}-k}(Y,{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\ p_{Y}^{*}\ }H^{2d_{Y}-k}(X\times Y,{\bf Z})\\
\xrightarrow{\ \cdot\,\smile\,[Z]\ }H^{2d_{Y}+2d_{X}-2c-k}(X\times Y,{\bf Z})\isomlra H_{k+2c}(X\times Y,{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\ p_{X*}\ }H_{k+2c}(X,{\bf Z}),

where
p_{X}
and
p_{Y}
are the projections from
X\times Y
onto the factors and the isomorphisms are given by Poincaré duality
and the middle map is the cup-product with the cohomology class of the cycle
Z
.

We will use the following functoriality properties of the Abel–Jacobi map.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
i\colon X\to X^{\prime}
and
j\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
be morphisms of smooth proper varieties.

(a)
We have

and

.

(b)
If

is a cycle on

.

(c)

All these statements follow from base change and the projection formula.

3.3. The Clemens–Tyurin argument

The following result is a generalization of

(see also
[

, Lemma (4.6)]
);
the original result is the case

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
2m+2
and let

be a smooth hyperplane section of

. Let

be a smooth closed subscheme of the Hilbert scheme of
m

let

be the closed subscheme parameterizing projective spaces contained in

and let

3.4. Intermediate Jacobians and their endomorphisms

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
2m-1
. We consider the middle cohomology
H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Z})
with its natural Hodge structure of weight
2m-1
. The complex torus

\Jac(X)=H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})/(F^{m}H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})+H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Z})),

where
F^{m}H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})\subset H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})
is part of the Hodge filtration,
is called the (Griffiths)
intermediate Jacobian
of
X
(see
[
BL99
, Chapter 4]
). Poincaré duality induces a hermitian form on
H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})
which is not necessarily positive definite
but defines (in the terminology of
[
BL99
, Chapter 2]
) a canonical
nondegenerate line bundle
on
\Jac(X)
,
making it into a
nondegenerate torus.
If moreover

(3.2)

F^{m+1}H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})=0,

the hermitian form is positive definite,
the line bundle is ample, and it defines a principal polarization on the abelian variety
\Jac(X)
.

More generally, a polarized rational Hodge structure of odd weight defines an isogeny class of complex tori
which, under a vanishing assumption analogous to (
3.2
), becomes an isogeny class of abelian varieties.

Let now
M
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
2m
. For a smooth hypersurface
{\jmath}\colon X\hookrightarrow M
, we denote by

H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})_{\text{van}}:=\Ker\bigl({\jmath}_{*}\colon H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})\to H^{2m+1}(M,{\bf Q})\bigr)

the
vanishing cohomology
. By
[
Voi03
, Proposition 2.27]
, there is an orthogonal direct sum decomposition

(3.3)

H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})=H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})_{\textnormal{van}}\oplus{\jmath}^{*}H^{2m-1}(M,{\bf Q}).

In particular, the Hodge structure
H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})_{\text{van}}
acquires a polarization from Poincaré duality on
X
and we denote by

\Jac(X)_{\text{van}}

the corresponding isogeny class of nondegenerate complex tori.

We will say that the endomorphism ring of a complex torus
T
is
trivial

if any endomorphism of
T
is the multiplication by an integer. If
T\neq 0
, this means that the endomorphism ring
\End(T)
is isomorphic to
{\bf Z}

or, equivalently, that the rational endomorphism ring
\End(T)\otimes{\bf Q}
is isomorphic to
{\bf Q}
;
so we can extend this terminology to isogeny classes of complex tori.

If
T
is a nonzero and nondegenerate complex torus with trivial endomorphism ring,
it is indecomposable with Picard number
1
(
[
BL99
, Propositions 1.7.3 and 2.3.7]
).

The next result is an old statement made by Severi and proved in
[
CvG92
, Theorem (1.1)]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
2m
. If
\jmath\colon X\hookrightarrow M
is a very general hyperplane section,
the endomorphism ring of
\Jac(X)_{\textnormal{van}}
is trivial.

Proof.

Assume first that there is a rational map
f\colon M\dashrightarrow{\bf P}^{1}
,
which we resolve by blowing up a smooth codimension-2 subvariety
to obtain a morphism
\tilde{f}\colon\widetilde{M}\to M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\dashrightarrow}}{\bf P}^{1}
with critical values
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\in{\bf P}^{1}
,
and that the strict transform of
X
is the fiber over
0\in{\bf P}^{1}\smallsetminus\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\}
. Let
{\tilde{\jmath}}\colon X\hookrightarrow\widetilde{M}
be the embedding and let

\rho\colon\pi_{1}({\bf P}^{1}\smallsetminus\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\},0)\longrightarrow\Sp(H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q}))

be the
monodromy representation
.

Assume moreover that the only singularities of the fibers of
\tilde{f}
are nodes. As explained in
[
Voi03
, Sections 2.2.2 and 2.3.1]
, one can then attach to each singular point of a fiber
a (noncanonically defined)
vanishing cycle
in
H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})_{\text{van}}

and the vanishing cycles span the vector space
H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})_{\text{van}}

(
[
Voi03
, Lemma 2.26]
; this reference deals with the case where each singular fiber
has a single node but the proofs extend to the general case).

Assume that
f\colon M\dashrightarrow{\bf P}^{1}
is a Lefschetz pencil. For each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, the singular fiber
X_{t_{i}}
has a single node
and there exists an element
\gamma_{i}
of
\pi_{1}({\bf P}^{1}\smallsetminus\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\},0)
that acts on
H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})
,
via the monodromy representation, as the transvection

T_{\delta_{i}}\colon x\longmapsto x-(x\cdot\delta_{i})\delta_{i},

where
\delta_{i}\in H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf Q})_{\text{van}}
is a vanishing cycle (
[
Voi03
, Theorem 3.16]
). The main results of Picard–Lefschetz theory are:

•

the vanishing cycles
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{r}

are in the same orbit for the monodromy representation (
[
Voi03
, Corollary 3.24]
);

•

the monodromy representation is absolutely irreducible (
[
Voi03
, Theorem 3.27]
or
[
PS83
, Lemma 3.13]
).

It follows that the monodromy is “big”:
the Zariski closure of its image is the full symplectic group
\Sp(H^{2m-1}(X,{\bf C})_{\textnormal{van}})
(
[
PS03
, Lemma 4]
). As in the proof of
[
PS03
, Theorem 17]
, for
t\in{\bf P}^{1}
very general, any endomorphism of
\Jac(X_{t})_{\textnormal{van}}

intertwines every element of the monodromy group, hence every element of the symplectic group. It must therefore be a multiple of the identity:
the endomorphism ring of
\Jac(X_{t})_{\textnormal{van}}
is trivial.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
M
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
2m
with
H^{2m-1}(M,{\bf Q})=0

and let
\jmath\colon X\hookrightarrow M
be a very general hyperplane section. The endomorphism ring of
\Jac(X)
is then trivial.

These results apply to intermediate Jacobians of GM threefolds and fivefolds.

Corollary 3.6
.

If
X
is a GM variety of dimension
n\in\{3,5\}

the intermediate Jacobian
\Jac(X)
is a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
10
. If, moreover,
X
is very general, we have

\End(\Jac(X))\simeq{\bf Z}.

In particular, the Picard number of
\Jac(X)
is
1
.

Proof.

For any GM variety
X
of dimension 3 or 5, condition (
3.2
) holds by Proposition
2.6
,
hence the abelian variety
\Jac(X)
is principally polarized; its dimension is 10 again by Proposition
2.6
.

For the second statement, we may assume that
X
is ordinary; it is then a very ample divisor in its Grassmannian hull
M_{X}
,
which is the Grassmannian
\Gr(2,V_{5})
when
n=5
or the fixed smooth fourfold
\Gr(2,V_{5})\cap{\bf P}^{7}\subset{\bf P}(\textstyle{\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{2}}\hskip 0.56905pt{V}_{5})
when
n=3
. We have
H^{n}(M_{X},{\bf Q})=0
in both cases by Proposition
2.6
,
so Corollary
3.5
implies the first claim. A standard result then implies that the Picard number of
\Jac(X)
is 1.
∎

has codimension 3 and degree 5. Therefore,

contains no surfaces of degrees less than 10 (

is a local complete intersection curve in

of degree 5. This also proves the inclusion

Since the curve

is a dimensionally transverse linear section of

the resolution of Lemma

restricts on

to a resolution

hence

is a connected curve of arithmetic genus 1;
in particular, its Hilbert polynomial is

is flat over
\widetilde{S}_{0}
. Finally,

and

Let

where the curve

To prove such a relation, we will assume that the Hilbert scheme of lines

Lemma 4.13
.

Assume that

is smooth. For a general point

in

, the fiber

of

is a smooth cubic surface scroll
and the lines

and

Proof.

We saw at the end of the proof of Proposition

that

hence

) and

and a vector

is the projection from

. Since

is an integral curve of degree 40, its image by

only if the line connecting

with a general point of

intersects

hence the same is true for its hyperplane section

and the curve

would then contain the cone

Therefore, for

general in
\widetilde{S}^{\prime}_{0,V_{5}}
, we are in case (a).

By (

),
the lines

and

are contained in fibers of the map

Since the curve

Proposition 4.14

Assume that

is smooth. There is a dense open subscheme

such that

Since we only need an equality over a dense open subset of

we may base change both sides along the open embedding

. The left side can then be rewritten as

By Proposition

and Lemma

, the morphism

is a flat family of surfaces whose general fiber is a smooth cubic surface scroll. Since

contains no surfaces of degrees less than 10 (

), it contains no

. Therefore, the morphism

is the intersection

of the smooth cubic surface scroll

with any non-Plücker quadric

containing

. Such an intersection is a connected curve of degree 6 and arithmetic genus 2. Since the lines

are contained both in the scroll

and the quadric

, they are components of

To describe the remaining components, we denote by

of the scroll

and by

the class of a fiber of its ruling. We have

contains a line, the class of this line is

(the class of a fiber of the ruling), or

(the class of the exceptional section

of

). If it is

4.4. Abel–Jacobi maps

Let
X
be a smooth GM threefold with associated Lagrangian
A
. Assume that
Y^{3}_{A}=\varnothing
, so that
\widetilde{Y}^{\geq 2}_{A}
is a smooth surface,
and that the Hilbert scheme of lines

is smooth. The subscheme

. Consider the universal line

Proposition 4.15
.

Let

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
(a), it is enough to check that the Abel–Jacobi map given by the image of
[Z]

with respect to the pullback map

is equal to

. Equality (
4.19
) implies that there is a cycle

supported on

where the scheme

Let us show that the Abel–Jacobi map defined by the right side of this equality is zero.

By Lemma
3.1
(b), the Abel–Jacobi map corresponding to the cycle
(i\times\Id_{F_{1}(X)})^{*}([Z^{\prime}_{F}])

is equal to the composition

H_{1}(F_{1}(X),{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\ \AJ_{Z^{\prime}_{F}}\ }H_{5}(M_{X},{\bf Z})\xrightarrow{\ i^{*}\ }H_{3}(X,{\bf Z}).

Since

by Proposition

, this map vanishes. Similarly, the Abel–Jacobi map corresponding to the cycle

In this section, we prove Theorems

and
1.3

\displaystyle\wp_{n}\colon\mathbf{M}_{n}^{\rm GM}

Consider the universal EPW variety

\textstyle{\mathscr{Y}^{5-n}_{\mathscr{A}^{\perp}}\times_{\LGr_{\rm ndv}({\bigwedgeop\displaylimits\hskip-2.56073pt^{3}}\hskip 0.28453pt{V}_{6})}\mathscr{Y}^{5-n}_{\mathscr{A}^{\perp}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

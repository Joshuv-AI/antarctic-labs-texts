Non-minimal couplings to 𝑼 ⁡ ( 𝟏 ) U(1) -gauge fields and asymptotic symmetries
Oscar Fuentealba, Marc Henneaux, and Jules Mas

Abstract
We analyse the asymptotic symmetries of electromagnetism non-minimally coupled to scalar fields, with non-minimal couplings of the Fermi type that occur in extended supergravity models. Our study is carried out at spatial infinity where minimal and non-minimal couplings exhibit very different asymptotic properties: while the former generically cannot be neglected at infinity, the latter can. Electromagnetic non-minimal couplings are in that respect similar to gravitational minimal couplings, which are also asymptotically subdominant. Because the non-minimally interacting model is asymptotic to the free one, its asymptotic symmetries are the same as the ones of the free theory, i.e., described by angle-dependent u ⁡ ( 1 ) u(1) gauge transformations. We also analyse the duality symmetry and show that it is broken to its compact subgroup by the asymptotic conditions. Finally, we consider logarithmic gauge transformations and use them to simplify the symmetry algebra.

This is very easy to see by mere inspection of the covariant derivatives which read, in the example of a complex one-form

The same is true for the Yang-Mills curvatures where “minimal coupling” yields

It is interesting to note that the same important difference between the asymptotic properties of gravity and Yang-Mills gauge models plays a striking role in at different (but also asymptotic) context, that of the boost problem of Christodoulou and O’Murchadha

As stressed by these authors, while the Einstein equations linearize at infinity, the Yang-Mills equations exhibit a radically different behaviour which does not guarantee that asymptotically flat initial data have a regular development which includes complete spacelike hypersurfaces boosted relative to the initial one.

There exist other types of couplings to abelian gauge fields that have been considered in the literature. These are of non-minimal Fermi-type and play for instance an important role in extended supergravity models

The supergravity models enjoy the further interesting feature of being invariant under a “hidden” duality symmetry

In Section

2.3
Poincaré transformations

The above boundary conditions in which one allows explicitly an angle-dependent
\mathcal{O}(1)
gauge transformation term in the asymptotic form of the fields have been tailored to incorporate an infinite-dimensional angle-dependent
u(1)
symmetry. A central difficulty with these relaxed boundary conditions, however, is that they conflict with Poincaré invariance. This occurs in a subtle way, in the sense that the boundary conditions themselves are Poincaré invariant. The difficulty has to do with an additional condition that a transformation should fulfill to define an “invariance”, that of leaving the symplectic form strictly invariant.

This is a subtle question in the present case because the invariance of the Lagrangian density under Poincaré transformations up to a divergence
\partial_{\mu}k^{\mu}
guarantees the invariance of the symplectic form, but only up to a surface term at spatial infinity. The whole question is then to check that this surface term vanishes with the chosen boundary conditions. The point is that it does not with (
18
)-(
21
).

This can be cured in the pure Maxwell case. To understand why the above non-minimal couplings do not spoil the curing procedure, we go step-by-step over it, following closely
Henneaux:2018gfi
;
Henneaux:2018hdj
.

exactly as in
Henneaux:2018gfi
, with no additional contributions coming from the interactions with the scalar sector. Because of this key property, one can rescue boost invariance in the same way as in the free case, as we will now show.

This is in sharp contrast with minimal couplings, which modify the leading asymptotic terms in the variations of the fields, leading to obstructions, as explained in Appendix
A
.

2.3.3
Re-establishing boost invariance

To re-establish invariance under Lorentz boosts, the method of
Henneaux:2018gfi
introduces a surface degree of freedom

\Psi

at infinity and modifies the symplectic structure by a surface term that involves it,

\Omega_{\text{surface}}=-\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,d_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{r}d_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}\,.

(36)

The new field

\Psi

can be extended into the bulk, by incorporating it as the leading order coefficient in the fall-off of the time component of the vector potential
A_{0}
and adding its corresponding conjugate momentum
\pi^{0}
, which must be weakly zero. This amounts to consider the Hamiltonian formulation of the theory as it comes from the strict application of the Dirac procedure, in which one keeps the “primary constraint”
\pi^{0}\approx 0
and its Lagrange multiplier which we denote by
\lambda
(see e.g.
Dirac1967
;
Henneaux:1992ig
). The Hamiltonian action principle with
\pi^{0}
and
\lambda
included is given by

S_{H}=S^{s}_{H}[\phi,\chi;\pi_{\phi},\pi_{\chi}]+S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A_{\mu};\pi^{\mu};\lambda]\,,

(37)

where
S^{s}_{H}
is the same as in (
9
) and the vector Hamiltonian action
S^{v}_{H}
now reads

\displaystyle S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A_{\mu};\pi^{\mu};\lambda]

\displaystyle=\int dtd^{3}x\left(\pi^{i}\dot{A}_{i}+\pi^{0}\dot{A}_{0}-\mathcal{H}^{v}-\lambda\pi^{0}\right)-\oint dtd^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{r}\dot{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,,

(38)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\left(e^{\phi}\pi_{i}\pi^{i}+e^{\phi}\chi\epsilon^{ijk}\pi_{i}F_{jk}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{4}\left(e^{\phi}\chi^{2}+e^{-\phi}\right)F_{ij}F^{ij}+A_{0}\mathcal{G}-\partial_{i}\pi_{0}A^{i}\,.

(39)

We have modified for convenience the vector Hamiltonian by adding the constraint term
-\partial_{i}\pi_{0}A^{i}
, which is of course permissible.
Variation of the action principle with respect to the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
enforces the constraints
\pi^{0}\approx 0
, while Gauss’s law appears now as a “secondary constraint”.

We can further extend the formalism by introducing an independent Lagrange multiplier
\psi
for the secondary constraint
\mathcal{G}
, yielding Dirac’s extended formulation that makes all symmetries manifest
Dirac1967
;
Henneaux:1992ig
. This amounts to modifying the vector action as

S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A_{\mu};\pi^{\mu};\lambda]\rightarrow S^{v}_{E}[\phi,\chi,A_{\mu};\pi^{\mu};\lambda,\psi]=S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A_{\mu};\pi^{\mu};\lambda]-\int dtd^{3}x\psi\mathcal{G}\,.

(40)

The two formulations are physically equivalent and one goes from the extended formulation to the non-extended one by imposing the gauge condition
\psi=0
.

The fall-off (in Cartesian coordinates) of the time component of the vector potential and its conjugate momentum are given by

A_{0}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,\quad\pi^{0}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{\Psi}}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-3}\right)\,,

(41)

with parity conditions on the leading orders that read

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(-n^{i})=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}(n^{i})\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{\Psi}(-n^{i})=\pi_{\Psi}(n^{i})\,.

(42)

The Poincaré transformation laws are modified in the vector sector of the theory by adding gauge transformations in such a way that the symplectic form is invariant (see next subsection). This is of course also permissible. Explicitly, we take

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}A_{0}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\left(\xi A^{i}\right)+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}A_{0}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}A_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}e^{\phi}\pi_{i}+\frac{\xi}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}e^{\phi}\gamma_{ij}\chi\epsilon^{jkl}F_{kl}+\partial_{i}\left(\xi A_{0}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{i}}A_{i}\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi^{0}

\displaystyle=\xi\partial_{i}\pi^{i}+\partial_{i}\left(\xi^{i}\pi^{0}\right)\,,

(45)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi^{i}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{j}\left(\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}e^{\phi}\chi\epsilon^{ijk}\pi_{k}\right)-\partial_{j}\left[\sqrt{\gamma}\xi\left(e^{\phi}\chi^{2}+e^{-\phi}\right)F^{ij}\right]+\xi\nabla^{i}\pi^{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{i}}\pi^{i}\,.

(46)

2.3.4
Poincaré charges

One can check that the new symplectic form

\Omega=\int d^{3}x\left(d_{V}\pi_{\phi}d_{V}\phi+d_{V}\pi_{\chi}d_{V}\chi+d_{V}\pi^{i}d_{V}A_{i}+d_{V}\pi^{0}d_{V}A_{0}\right)-\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,d_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{r}d_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{0}\,,

(47)

is then invariant under Lorentz boosts, and thus under the whole Poincaré group, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega=0\,.

(48)

The Poincaré canonical generator can then be obtained by direct application of the equation

\iota_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\Omega=-d_{V}P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\,.

(49)

Then, we obtain that

P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}=\int d^{3}x\left(\xi\mathcal{H}+\xi^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right)+\oint d^{2}x\left[b\left(\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{r}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}\right)+Y^{A}\left(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}+\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{r}\right)\right]\,,

(50)

where the energy and momentum densities read

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{s}+\mathcal{H}^{v}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{i}

\displaystyle=F_{ij}\pi^{j}-\partial_{j}\pi^{j}A_{i}+\pi^{0}\partial_{i}A_{0}+\pi_{\phi}\partial_{i}\phi+\pi_{\chi}\partial_{i}\chi\,,

(52)

respectively, with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\left(\pi_{\phi}^{2}+e^{-2\phi}\pi_{\chi}^{2}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{2}\gamma^{ij}\left(\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi+e^{2\phi}\partial_{i}\chi\partial_{j}\chi\right)\,,

(53)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\left(e^{\phi}\pi_{i}\pi^{i}+e^{\phi}\chi\epsilon^{ijk}\pi_{i}F_{jk}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{4}\left(e^{\phi}\chi^{2}+e^{-\phi}\right)F_{ij}F^{ij}+A_{0}\mathcal{G}-\nabla_{i}\pi_{0}A^{i}\,.

(54)

2.4
Angle-dependent
u(1)
asymptotic symmetries

The asymptotic conditions are also invariant under the gauge transformations generated by the parameters

\displaystyle\epsilon=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,\quad\mu=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(55)

with
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}(-n^{i})=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}(n^{i})
and
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}(-n^{i})=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}(n^{i})
. Transformation laws of the fields read

\delta_{\mu}A_{0}=\mu\,,\quad\delta_{\epsilon}A_{i}=\partial_{i}\epsilon\,,

(56)

where the remaining fields do not transform under gauge transformations. The canonical generator of the asymptotic symmetries is then given by

G_{\mu,\epsilon}=\int d^{3}x\left(\mu\pi^{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{G}\right)+\oint d^{2}x\left(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\pi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}-\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{r}\right)\,.

(57)

The algebra of the asymptotic symmetries is then the semi-direct sum of Poincaré and the infinite-dimensional set of
u(1)
charges, that is to say,

\displaystyle\big\{P_{\xi_{1},\xi_{1}^{i}},P_{\xi_{2},\xi_{2}^{i}}\big\}

\displaystyle=P_{\hat{\xi},\hat{\xi}^{i}}\,,

(58)

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\mu,\epsilon},P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\big\}

\displaystyle=G_{\hat{\mu},\hat{\epsilon}}\,,

(59)

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\mu_{1},\epsilon_{1}},G_{\mu_{2},\epsilon_{2}}\big\}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(60)

where the components of the Poincaré vector field transform as

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}^{i}\partial_{i}\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}^{i}\partial_{i}\xi_{1}\,,

(61)

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}^{i}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}^{j}\partial_{j}\xi_{2}^{i}-\xi_{2}^{j}\partial_{j}\xi_{1}^{i}+g^{ij}\left(\xi_{1}\partial_{j}\xi_{2}-\xi_{2}\partial_{j}\xi_{1}\right)\,.

(62)

The gauge parameters are boosted and rotated as follows

\hat{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}}=-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}\right)\,,\quad\hat{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}}=-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}-b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}\,.

(63)

3
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
-case: Duality-invariant formulation

3.1
Action principle and boundary conditions

We now turn to the description of the duality symmetry, broken to
SO(2)
by the asymptotic conditions. In order to exhibit it, we go to the two-potential formulation along the Hamiltonian lines of
Bunster:2010kwr
.

The Gauss’s constraint is solved, by introducing a new vector potential
Z_{i}

Deser:1976iy
,

\pi^{i}=-\epsilon^{ijk}\partial_{j}Z_{k}\,.

(64)

We assume the absence of sources, i.e., no electric or magnetic charges, which would otherwise need the introduction of electric and magnetic Dirac strings
Deser:1997mz
.

Then, up to boundary terms, the vector action takes the form

S^{v}_{H}=\frac{1}{2}\int dtd^{3}x\left(\epsilon_{ab}B^{ai}\dot{A}^{b}_{i}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}G_{ab}(\phi,\chi)B^{ai}B^{b}_{i}\right)\,,

(65)

where the index
a
takes the values
(1,2)
and
A^{a}_{i}=(A_{i},Z_{i})
. The field strength associated with the double vector potential
A^{a}_{i}
is given by

B^{ai}=\epsilon^{ijk}\partial_{j}A^{a}_{k}\,.

(66)

The matrix

G_{ab}(\phi,\chi)=\begin{bmatrix}e^{\phi}\chi^{2}+e^{-\phi}&-\chi e^{\phi}\\
-\chi e^{\phi}&e^{\phi}\end{bmatrix}\,,

(67)

is such that the Hamiltonian in (
65
) is invariant under
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
duality transformations of the fields

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}\phi

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\alpha}\xi^{\phi}_{\alpha}(\phi,\chi)\,,\qquad\delta_{\varepsilon}\chi=\varepsilon^{\alpha}\xi^{\chi}_{\alpha}(\phi,\chi)\,,

(68)

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}A^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\alpha}(X_{\alpha})^{a}_{\,\,\,b}A^{b}_{i}\,,\quad\delta_{\varepsilon}B^{a}_{i}=\varepsilon^{\alpha}(X_{\alpha})^{a}_{\,\,\,b}B^{b}_{i}\,,

(69)

where
\alpha,\beta=\pm,0
. The phase space vectors
\xi_{\alpha}(\phi,\chi)=\xi^{\phi}_{\alpha}(\phi,\chi)\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}+\xi^{\chi}_{\alpha}(\phi,\chi)\frac{\partial}{\partial\chi}
and the matrices
X_{\alpha}
fulfill both the
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
algebra,

[\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}]=(\alpha-\beta)\xi_{\alpha+\beta}\,,\qquad[X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}]=(\alpha-\beta)X_{\alpha+\beta}\,.

(70)

Explicit expressions for these quantities can be found for instance in
Bunster:2010kwr
.

For the scalar fields and their conjugate momenta we will adopt the asymptotic (and parity) conditions previously written. For the gauge potential, we will take the obvious generalization of (
19
), i.e.,

A^{a}_{r}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,\quad A^{a}_{A}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{A}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,.

(71)

This implies the following fall-off for the corresponding field strength

B^{ar}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,\quad B^{aA}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(72)

where

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}=\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}e^{AB}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{B}\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}=\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}e^{AB}\partial_{B}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}\,,

(73)

with
\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,e^{AB}\equiv\epsilon^{rAB}
.
We will assume the twisted parity conditions of
Henneaux:2020nxi

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}(-n^{i})=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}(n^{i})\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{A}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a\text{even}}_{A}+\partial_{A}\lambda^{a}\,,

(74)

where
\lambda^{a}(-n^{i})=\lambda^{a}(n^{i})
, which guarantee the finiteness of the symplectic structure. Since only
\partial_{A}\lambda^{a}
appears in the asymptotic conditions, one can assume that these gauge functions have no zero mode.

3.2
Poincaré invariance

Rewritten in the two-potential formulation, the Poincaré transformations of the fields explicitly read

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\pi_{\phi}+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}\phi\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\chi

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}e^{-2\phi}\pi_{\chi}+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}\chi\,,

(76)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi_{\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}e^{-2\phi}\pi_{\chi}^{2}+\partial_{i}\left(\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma^{ij}\xi\partial_{j}\phi\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma^{ij}\xi e^{2\phi}\partial_{i}\chi\partial_{j}\chi-\frac{\xi}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\frac{\partial G_{ab}}{\partial\phi}B^{ai}B^{b}_{i}+\partial_{i}\left(\xi^{i}\pi_{\phi}\right)\,,

(77)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\pi_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}(\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma^{ij}\xi e^{2\phi}\partial_{j}\chi)-\frac{\xi}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\frac{\partial G_{ab}}{\partial\chi}B^{ai}B^{b}_{i}+\partial_{i}\left(\xi^{i}\pi_{\chi}\right)\,,

(78)

\displaystyle\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}A^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\epsilon^{ab}G_{bc}B^{c}_{i}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{i}}A^{a}_{i}\,.

(79)

and are easily verified to preserve the asymptotic conditions.

The variation of the symplectic form under Poincaré transformations takes again the form

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}d_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(bd_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{bA}\right)\,,

(80)

with no contribution from the interaction terms, which are subleading.

The resolution to the Lorentz boost problem – the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\Omega\not=0
– proceeds then as in
Henneaux:2020nxi
.
We consider first the Hamiltonian action principle with additional degrees of freedom
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}
at infinity,

S_{H}=S^{s}_{H}[\phi,\chi;\pi_{\phi},\pi_{\chi}]+S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A^{a}_{i},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}]\,.

(81)

The scalar action
S^{s}_{H}
is the same as in (
9
), but the vector Hamiltonian action
S^{v}_{H}
in (
65
) is modified as follows

\displaystyle S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A^{a}_{i},\Psi^{a}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int dtd^{3}x\left(\epsilon_{ab}B^{ai}\dot{A}^{b}_{i}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}G_{ab}(\phi,\chi)B^{ai}B^{b}_{i}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\oint dtd^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}\dot{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}^{b}\,.

(82)

The transformations under Lorentz boosts of the canonical variables are adjusted by new contributions involving
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}
, in such a way that the symplectic form corresponding to the extended action,

\Omega=\int d^{3}x\left(d_{V}\pi_{\phi}d_{V}\phi+d_{V}\pi_{\chi}d_{V}\chi+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ab}d_{V}B^{ai}d_{V}{A}^{b}_{i}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}d_{V}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}d_{V}{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}^{b}\,,

(83)

is invariant under Poincaré transformations.
One finds that the transformation laws of the scalar fields remain unchanged. The ones of the vector potentials, however, are modified by gauge transformation terms (as always permissible):

\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}A^{a}_{i}=-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\epsilon^{ab}G_{bc}B^{c}_{i}+\partial_{i}\left(\xi A^{a}_{0}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{i}}A^{a}_{i}\,.

(84)

As previously, the fields
A^{a}_{0}
can be thought as extensions into the bulk of the asymptotic boundary degrees of freedom
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}
, i.e.,

A^{a}_{0}=\frac{1}{r}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,.

(85)

Its Poincaré variation reads

\delta_{\xi,\xi^{i}}A^{a}_{0}=\nabla_{i}\left(\xi A^{ai}\right)+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}A^{a}_{0}\,,

(86)

from which it follows that

\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}\right)+2b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\,.

(87)

The Poincaré canonical generators, which exist because
\mathcal{L}_{X_{\xi}}\Omega=d_{V}(\iota_{X_{\xi}}\Omega)
now vanishes, are obtained from
\iota_{X_{\xi}}\Omega=-d_{V}P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}
and found to be

5
Log-gauge transformations - Rewriting the algebra as a direct sum

5.1
Asymptotic conditions, charges and algebra

5.1.1
Action principle with log-relaxed asymptotic conditions

A noticeable feature of the symmetry algebra is that the Lorentz generators are not invariant under asymptotic
\mathcal{O}(1)
gauge symmetries (although they are of course invariant under gauge transformations that vanish at infinity). This is because the Poisson brackets of the corresponding generators with the Lorentz generators are non-zero, due to the fact that the gauge generators are in a non-trivial representation of the Lorentz algebra. Technically, the dependence under “improper”
Benguria:1976in
gauge transformations of the Lorentz generators follows from the surface term that must be included to make them well-defined when the more flexible boundary conditions allowing
\mathcal{O}(1)
gauge symmetries at infinity are imposed. This surface term involves the bare potentials.

The gauge-dependence of the Lorentz generators leads to an ambiguity in the values of the angular momentum and boost generators. In the standard single-potential formulation, one can redefine the Lorentz generators in a manner that makes them invariant under all (proper and improper) gauge transformations
Fuentealba:2023rvf
. This is done by adding to the Lorentz generators nonlinear terms given (roughly) by the product of the generators of the improper gauge symmetries by the asymptotic fields

\Psi

or

\Phi

. These fields can furthermore be shown to be equal (on the constraint surface) to the generators of a new type of gauge transformations, namely, gauge transformations that grow logarithmically in
r
at infinity.

The approach can be straightforwardly extended to the duality invariant formulation with non-minimal couplings to scalar fields considered in this paper. Ultimately, this is again because the non-minimal couplings are negligeable (to leading order) at infinity, so that the asymptotic computations of the free theory can be repeated without change.

We will thus only sketch here the procedure to arrive at an ambiguity-free angular momentum, following
Fuentealba:2023rvf
, and considering only the case of a single Maxwell field (in the double potential formulation). This is sufficient to convey the key ideas.

To allow the possibility to perform gauge transformations that behave asymptotically as
\ln r
(“log-gauge transformations"), we take as asymptotic conditions for the gauge potentials:

\displaystyle A^{a}_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(119)

\displaystyle A^{a}_{A}

\displaystyle=\ln r\,\partial_{A}\Phi^{a}_{\log}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{A}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,

(120)

\displaystyle A^{a}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln r}{r}\Psi^{a}_{\log}+\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,.

(121)

This leads to the following fall-off for the field strengths:

B^{ar}=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,\quad B^{aA}=\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(122)

where

\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}=\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}e^{AB}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{B}\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}=\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}e^{AB}\partial_{B}\left(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}-\Phi^{a}_{\log}\right)\,.

(123)

We impose as in
Fuentealba:2023rvf
the parity conditions

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}(-n^{i})

\displaystyle=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}(n^{i})\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{A}=\left(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{A}\right)^{\text{even}}+\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\,,

(124)

and

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(-n^{i})

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(n^{i})\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(-n^{i})=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(n^{i})\,,

(125)

\displaystyle\Phi^{a}_{\log}(-n^{i})

\displaystyle=-\Phi^{a}_{\log}(n^{i})\,,\quad\Psi^{a}_{\log}(-n^{i})=\Psi^{a}_{\log}(n^{i})\,.

(126)

We can assume that the even functions
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}
have no zero mode, and hence also the even functions
\Psi^{a}_{\log}
which are their conjugates in the action (

The action principle is modified by a surface term involving the new fields

S_{H}=S^{s}_{H}[\phi,\chi;\pi_{\phi},\pi_{\chi}]+S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A^{a}_{i},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a},\Psi^{a}_{\log}]\,,

(127)

yielding the vector Hamiltonian action
S^{v}_{H}

\displaystyle S^{v}_{H}[\phi,\chi,A^{a}_{i},\Psi^{a}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int dtd^{3}x\left(\epsilon_{ab}B^{ai}\dot{A}^{b}_{i}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}G_{ab}(\phi,\chi)B^{ai}B^{b}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\oint dtd^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\left(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}\dot{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}}^{b}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\dot{\Psi}^{b}_{\log}\right)\,.

(128)

5.1.2
Symmetries and charges

The preservation under Poincaré transformations of the symplectic form implies that the asymptotic fields should transform as

\displaystyle\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\Phi^{a}_{\log}

\displaystyle=b\Psi^{a}_{\log}+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\Phi^{a}_{\log}\,,

(129)

\displaystyle\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\Psi^{a}_{\log}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\Phi^{a}_{\log}\right)+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\Psi^{a}_{\log}\,,

(130)

and

\displaystyle\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}

\displaystyle=b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\,,

(131)

\displaystyle\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}\right)+2b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}+Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\,,

(132)

with zero modes that can be projected out in
\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}
and
\delta_{b,Y^{A}}\Psi^{a}_{\log}
.
The Poincaré canonical generators are then given by

P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}=\int d^{3}x\left(\xi\mathcal{H}+\xi^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right)+B_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\,,\\

(133)

where the boundary term reads

\displaystyle B_{\xi,\xi^{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\oint d^{2}x\left[b\left(\epsilon_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}+\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\Psi^{a}_{\log}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}+\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{r}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+Y^{A}\left(\epsilon_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{A}+\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\Psi^{a}_{\log}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{A}+\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{r}\right)\right]\,.

(134)

The asymptotic conditions are also invariant under gauge transformations generated by the parameters

\displaystyle\epsilon^{a}=\ln r\,\epsilon^{a}_{\log}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-1}\right)\,,\quad\mu^{a}=\frac{\ln r}{r}\mu^{a}_{\log}+\frac{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}}{r}+\mathcal{O}\left(r^{-2}\right)\,,

(135)

with

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(-n^{i})

\displaystyle=\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(n^{i})\,,\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(-n^{i})=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}(n^{i})\,,

(136)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{a}_{\log}(-n^{i})

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{a}_{\log}(n^{i})\,,\qquad\mu^{a}_{\log}(-n^{i})=\mu^{a}_{\log}(n^{i})\,.

(137)

The transformation laws of the fields read

\delta_{\mu}A^{a}_{0}=\mu^{a}\,,\quad\delta_{\epsilon}A^{a}_{i}=\partial_{i}\epsilon^{a}\,,

(138)

The canonical generator of the asymptotic symmetries is then given by

\displaystyle G_{\mu^{a},\epsilon^{a}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\oint d^{2}x\left[\left(\epsilon_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}+\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\Psi^{a}_{\log}\right)\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}-\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{r}\right]\,,

(139)

\displaystyle G_{\mu^{a}_{\log},\epsilon^{a}_{\log}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\delta_{ab}\left(\epsilon^{a}_{\log}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\mu^{b}_{\log}\right)\,.

(140)

The zero mode gauge transformations are proper gauge symmetries with zero charge. We can therefore assume here also that the even gauge parameters
\epsilon^{a}
and
\mu^{a}_{\log}
have no zero mode and this will be done in the description of the symmetry algebra.

To conclude, we have four independent groups of angle-dependent

-sector as above, and two new ones in the log-sector (all of which with harmonic number
\ell>0

Finally, the canonical generator of

-duality rotations is given by

\displaystyle R_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\rho\int d^{3}x\left[2\pi_{\phi}\chi+\pi_{\chi}\left(e^{-2\phi}-\chi^{2}-1\right)-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}B^{ai}A^{b}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\rho}{2}\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\,\epsilon_{ab}\left(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{r}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}+\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\Psi^{b}_{\log}\right)\,.

(141)

The computation of the Poisson bracket algebra of the generators is direct.

The brackets of the
u(1)
-conserved charges and Poincaré charges read

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\mu_{\log},\epsilon_{\log}},P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\big\}

\displaystyle=G_{\hat{\mu}_{\log},\hat{\epsilon}_{\log}}\,,

(142)

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\mu,\epsilon},P_{\xi,\xi^{i}}\big\}

\displaystyle=G_{\hat{\mu},\hat{\epsilon}}\,,

(143)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}}^{a}

\displaystyle=-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}-b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}\,,

(145)

\displaystyle=-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\epsilon^{a}_{\log}-b\mu^{a}_{\log}\,,

(146)

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}^{a}_{\log}

\displaystyle=-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\mu^{a}_{\log}-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\epsilon^{a}_{\log}\right)\,.

(147)

The brackets of the
u(1)
-conserved charges with the
SO(2)
-rotation generator are given by

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\mu,\epsilon},R_{\rho}\big\}

\displaystyle=G_{\hat{\mu},\hat{\epsilon}}\,,

(148)

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\mu_{\log},\epsilon_{\log}},R_{\rho}\big\}

\displaystyle=G_{\hat{\mu}_{\log},\hat{\epsilon}_{\log}}\,,

(149)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}}^{a}

\displaystyle=-\rho\epsilon^{ab}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{b}\,,\quad\hat{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}}^{a}=-\rho\epsilon^{ab}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{b}\,,

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{C}_{(\mu_{\log},\epsilon)}=\frac{1}{2}\oint d^{2}x\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}\delta_{ab}\mu^{a}_{\log}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}\,,

(154)

5.2
Decoupling of the soft charges in the algebra – General considerations

Denoting the generators of the homogeneous Lorentz generators, spacetime translations,
\mathcal{O}(1)
-gauge symmetries, log-gauge symmetries and
SO(2)
-duality rotations respectively by
M_{I}
,
T_{j}
,
U^{a}_{\alpha}
,
L^{\alpha}_{a}
and
R
, the algebra of the symmetries has the following structure,

\displaystyle\left[M_{I},M_{J}\right]

\displaystyle=f^{K}_{IJ}M_{K}\,,

(155)

\displaystyle\left[M_{I},T_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=R^{k}_{Ij}T_{k}\,,

(156)

\displaystyle\left[M_{I},U^{a}_{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=G^{\beta}_{I\alpha}U_{\beta}\,,

(157)

\displaystyle\left[M_{I},L^{\alpha}_{a}\right]

\displaystyle=-G^{\alpha}_{I\beta}L^{\beta}_{a}\,,

(158)

\displaystyle\left[R,U^{a}_{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{a}_{b}U^{b}_{\alpha}\,,

(159)

\displaystyle\left[R,L^{\alpha}_{a}\right]

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{b}_{a}L^{\alpha}_{b}\,,

(160)

\displaystyle\left[L^{\alpha}_{a},U^{b}_{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta^{b}_{a}\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,,

(161)

where
R
commutes with Poincaré generators.
Written in this way, the algebra takes exactly the form analysed in
Fuentealba:2023hzq
, where it was shown that the presence of an invertible central charge among a set of generators
\{q^{i},p_{j}\}
enables one to decouple them from the rest of the algebra. More precisely, through (nonlinear) redefinitions, one can rewrite the algebra as a direct sum involving as one of its summands the subalgebra generated by the
q^{i}
’s and
p_{j}
’s. This method was adopted first in the context of gravity to provide a supertranslation-independent definition of the angular momentum
Fuentealba:2022xsz
.

For the model considered here, one must redefine the Lorentz and
SO(2)
-duality rotation generators as

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{I}

\displaystyle=M_{I}-G^{\beta}_{I\alpha}U^{a}_{\beta}L^{\beta}_{a}\,,

(162)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}

\displaystyle=R-\epsilon^{a}_{b}U^{b}_{\alpha}L^{\alpha}_{a}\,.

(163)

One then finds that the soft charges
U^{a}_{\alpha}
and
L^{\beta}_{b}
have vanishing Poisson brackets with the new generators. Furthermore, these have unchanged brackets among themselves,

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{M}_{I},\tilde{M}_{J}\right]

\displaystyle=f^{K}_{IJ}\tilde{M}_{K}\,,

(164)

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{M}_{I},T_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=R^{k}_{Ij}T_{k}\,,

(165)

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{M}_{I},U^{a}_{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\tilde{M}_{I},L^{\alpha}_{a}\right]=0\,,

(166)

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{R},U^{a}_{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\tilde{R},L^{\alpha}_{a}\right]=0\,,

(167)

\displaystyle\left[L^{\alpha}_{a},U^{b}_{\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta^{b}_{a}\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,.

(168)

In order to carry out this computation, the following identities are extremely useful:

•

From

\left[M_{I},\left[M_{J},U^{a}_{\alpha}\right]\right]+\left[M_{J},\left[U^{a}_{\alpha},M_{I}\right]\right]+\left[U^{a}_{\alpha},\left[M_{I},M_{J}\right]\right]=0\,,

(169)

we get that

G^{\beta}_{J\alpha}G^{\gamma}_{I\beta}-G^{\beta}_{I\alpha}G^{\gamma}_{J\beta}=f^{K}_{IJ}G^{\gamma}_{K\alpha}\,.

(170)

•

From

\left[M_{I},\left[M_{J},T_{k}\right]\right]+\left[M_{J},\left[T_{k},M_{I}\right]\right]+\left[T_{k},\left[M_{I},M_{J}\right]\right]=0\,,

(171)

we get that

R^{k}_{Jj}R^{i}_{Ik}-R^{k}_{Ij}R^{i}_{Jk}=f^{K}_{IJ}R^{i}_{Kj}\,.

(172)

5.3
Decoupling of the soft charges in the algebra – Explicit computations

We now put in practice the general considerations of the previous subsection to the case at hand.

The redefinitions of the symmetry generators can equivalently be viewed as redefinitions of the corresponding symmetry parameters. Applying the general formulas, one finds that the desired redefinition for the Lorentz and
SO(2)
generators is implemented by performing simultaneously additional
u(1)
-gauge transformations generated by the following parameters

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\epsilon$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{(b,Y^{A},\rho)}

\displaystyle=-b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}-\rho\epsilon^{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{b}\,,

(173)

\displaystyle\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\mu$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}_{(b,Y^{A},\rho)}

\displaystyle=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{aA}\right)-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{a}-\rho\epsilon^{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Psi$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{b}\,,

(174)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{b}_{\log(b,Y^{A},\rho)}

\displaystyle=-b\left(\frac{\epsilon^{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{a}}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}+\Psi^{b}_{\log}\right)-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{r}-\rho\epsilon^{bc}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{cr}\,,

(175)

\displaystyle\mu^{b}_{\log(b,Y^{A},\rho)}

\displaystyle=-\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}_{A}\left(b\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$D$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{A}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$A$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}_{r}\right)-Y^{A}\partial_{A}\left(\frac{\epsilon^{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{r}_{a}}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}+\Psi^{b}_{\log}\right)-\rho\epsilon^{bc}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{ac}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}}{\sqrt{\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\gamma$\kern 0.0pt}}}}}+\Psi^{\log}_{c}\right)\,.

(176)

The generators take then the following form

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\xi,\xi^{i}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}x\left(\xi\mathcal{H}+\xi^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right)\,,

(177)

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\rho\int d^{3}x\left[2\pi_{\phi}\chi+\pi_{\chi}\left(e^{-2\phi}-\chi^{2}-1\right)-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}B^{ai}A^{b}_{i}\right]+\frac{\rho}{2}\oint d^{2}x\delta_{ab}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$B$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{ar}\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.29167pt\hbox{\kern 0.0pt$\Phi$\kern 0.0pt}}}^{b}\,.

(178)

It is interesting to note that in the enlarged context where logarithmic gauge transformations are included, the functional
\int d^{3}x\left(\xi\mathcal{H}+\xi^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right)
is well-defined as a generator, without surface terms.

We can explicitly check that the only non-vanishing brackets of the canonical generators are given by
\tilde{P}_{\xi,\xi^{i}}=\int d^{3}x\left(\xi\mathcal{H}+\xi^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right)
is invariant under all gauge transformations, proper and improper. This automatically implies that it has vanishing brackets with the
u(1)
generators.

\displaystyle\big\{\tilde{P}_{\xi_{1},\xi_{1}^{i}},\tilde{P}_{\xi_{2},\xi_{2}^{i}}\big\}

\displaystyle=\tilde{P}_{\hat{\xi},\hat{\xi}^{i}}\,,

(179)

\displaystyle\big\{G_{\epsilon_{1}},G_{\epsilon_{2}}\big\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})}\,.

(180)

The vanishing of the Poisson brackets of the Poincaré generators with the generators of improper gauge symmetries is in particular obvious, since
\tilde{P}_{\xi,\xi^{i}}
is manifestly invariant under both proper and improper gauge transformations.

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have investigated the asymptotic structure at spatial infinity of electromagnetism coupled to scalar fields through non-minimal couplings of Fermi type. We have shown that the rich structure found for the free Maxwell theory is unaffected by these couplings, contrary to minimal couplings which do have a non trivial impact asymptotically. In the asymptotic context, non minimal electromagnetic couplings are similar to minimal gravitational couplings.

Our study also shows the interplay between asymptotic symmetries and duality symmetries, which arise as “hidden symmetries” in supergravity
Cremmer:1978ds
. It would be interesting to push the analysis further and consider (conjectured) infinite-dimensional hidden symmetries such as
E_{10}

Julia1980
;
Damour:2002cu
, which could be broken to its “compact” subgroup
K(E_{10})
by the relevant boundary conditions.

Appendix A
Conflict between Lorentz invariance and angle-dependent
u(1)
transformations in the case of minimal coupling (at spatial infinity)

In this appendix, we consider the action describing the minimal coupling of a complex scalar field
\phi
to electromagnetism

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}\phi)^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\phi)\right),

(181)

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}
is the gauge covariant derivative and
\dagger
refers to the complex conjugate.

The momenta that are conjugate to the vector potential
A_{i}
read

\pi^{i}=\sqrt{\gamma}F_{0}^{\ i},

(182)

while the ones conjugate to the scalar field
\phi
and its complex conjugate
\phi^{\dagger}
are

\pi_{\phi}=\sqrt{\gamma}(D_{0}\phi)^{\dagger},\quad\pi_{\phi^{\dagger}}=\sqrt{\gamma}(D_{0}\phi)=\pi_{\phi}^{\dagger}.

(183)

From them, one can derive the following Hamiltonian action

S_{H}[\phi,\phi^{\dagger},A_{i};\pi_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}^{\dagger},\pi^{i}]=\int dtd^{3}x(\pi_{\phi}\dot{\phi}+\pi_{\phi}^{\dagger}\dot{\phi}^{\dagger}+\pi^{i}\dot{A}_{i}-\mathcal{H}-A_{0}\mathcal{G})\,,

(184)

where
\mathcal{G}=-\partial_{i}\pi^{i}+ie(\phi\pi_{\phi}-\phi^{\dagger}\pi_{\phi}^{\dagger})
and the Hamiltonian density is given by

\mathcal{H}=\frac{\pi^{i}\pi_{i}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\pi_{\phi}\pi_{\phi}^{\dagger}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}+\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{2}(D_{i}\phi)^{\dagger}(D^{i}\phi).

(185)

The transformation laws of the dynamical fields under boosts and time translations parametrized by
\xi
are defined up to a gauge transformation generated by
\zeta
and read

\delta_{\xi}A_{i}=\frac{\xi\pi_{i}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}+\partial_{i}\zeta,\quad\delta_{\xi}\phi=\frac{2\xi\pi_{\phi}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}+ie\zeta\phi,\quad\delta_{\xi}\phi^{\dagger}=\frac{2\xi\pi_{\phi}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}-ie\zeta\phi^{\dagger},

(186)

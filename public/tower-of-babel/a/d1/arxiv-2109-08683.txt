Generalized Characteristics for Finite Entropy Solutions of Burgers’ Equation Thanks: *Corresponding author
Andres A. Contreras Hip, Xavier Lamy, Elio Marconi*

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove the existence of generalized characteristics for weak, not necessarily entropic, solutions of Burgers’ equation

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\frac{u^{2}}{2}=0,

whose entropy productions are signed measures.
Such solutions arise in connection with large deviation principles for the hydrodynamic limit of interacting particle systems. The present work allows to remove a technical trace assumption in a recent result by the two first authors about the
L^{2}
stability of entropic shocks among such non-entropic solutions.
The proof relies on the Lagrangian representation of a solution’s hypograph, recently constructed by the third author. In particular, we prove a decomposition formula for the entropy flux across a given hypersurface, which is valid for general multidimensional scalar conservation laws.

Keywords:
generalized characteristics, finite entropy solutions, Burgers’ equation, Lagrangian representation,
L^{2}
stability of shocks.

MSC (2010): 35L60.

1.
Introduction

We consider bounded weak (not necessarily entropy) solutions of Burgers’ equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}\frac{u^{2}}{2}=0,

or more generally a scalar conservation law

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0,\quad t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R},

with strictly convex flux
f^{\prime\prime}\geq\alpha>0
.
For any entropy-flux pair
(\eta,q)
i.e.
\eta^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
and
q^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}f^{\prime}
, the corresponding entropy production of a bounded weak solution
u
is the distribution

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta}=\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\partial_{x}q(u).

Entropy solutions are weak solutions whose entropy production is nonpositive, i.e.
\mu_{\eta}\leq 0
for all convex entropies
\eta
, and given any bounded initial condition
u_{0}(x)
there exists a unique entropy solution
[
11
]
.

Here in contrast we consider weak solutions whose entropy productions do not necessarily have a sign: we call
finite-entropy solution
any bounded weak solutions of (
1.1
) such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta}\text{ is a Radon measure for all convex }\eta,

where
\mu_{\eta}
is the entropy production defined in (
1.2
).
This larger class of solutions is relevant in
the study of large deviation principles for the hydrodynamic limit of asymmetric interacting particle systems
[
7
,
17
]
, or for scalar conservation laws with appropriately small random forcing
[
16
,
2
]
. Known tools fail at completing the large deviation analysis because finite-entropy solutions do not in general have bounded variation (
BV
). Similar issues arise in the study of the so-called Aviles-Giga energy (see
[
12
, Introduction]
for more details). In the past years, several works have proved
BV
-like structural properties for finite-entropy solutions
[
13
,
6
,
14
,
15
]
but the large deviation principle still seems out of reach.

A key progress would be to obtain good estimates on the distance to entropy solutions in terms of the positive part of the entropy production. Inspired by
[
9
]
, the two first authors recently proposed a
relative entropy
approach to that question
[
4
]
, but they had to assume the existence of
generalized characteristics
: Lipschitz curves
x(t)
such that
x^{\prime}(t)=f^{\prime}(u^{\pm}(t,x(t)))
for a.e.
t
at which
u^{+}(t,x(t))=u^{-}(t,x(t))
, where
u^{\pm}(t,x(t))
are the left and right traces of
u
along
(t,x(t))
. It is well-known that
BV
solutions admit generalized characteristics starting at any value
x(0)=x_{0}

[
5
, § 10.2]
, but the argument uses a stronger notion of traces than the one available for finite-entropy solutions (see the discussion in the introduction of
[
4
]
).
Existence of generalized characteristics is also crucial in several recent works using relative entropy methods for hyperbolic systems of conservation laws (see e.g.
[
8
,
3
,
10
]
).

Our main result Theorem
1.1
establishes the existence of generalized characteristics for finite-entropy solutions. As a corollary, the results in
[
4
]
are valid for any finite-entropy solution of Burgers’ equation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a finite-entropy weak solution of (
1.1
) with strictly convex flux
f
.
For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a generalized characteristic of
u
starting at
x_{0}
, that is, a Lipschitz curve
x\colon[t_{0},T]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
x(0)=x_{0}
and

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(t)=f^{\prime}(u^{\pm}(t,x(t)))\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T]\text{ s.t. }u^{+}(t,x(t))=u^{-}(t,x(t)),

where
u^{\pm}(t,x(t))
denote the left and right traces of
u
along
(t,x(t))
.

Remark 1.2
.

Finite-entropy solutions have traces which are reached strongly in
L^{1}
:

\displaystyle\esslim_{y\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{T}\left|u(t,x(t)\pm y)-u^{\pm}(t,x(t))\right|\,dt=0.

This is proved in
[
18
]
for entropy solutions, but the proof uses only a kinetic formulation which is also valid for finite-entropy solutions
[
6
]
.
In particular, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
such that
u^{+}(t,x(t))\neq u^{-}(t,x(t))
we have the Rankine-Hugoniot condition

x^{\prime}(t)=\frac{f(u^{+}(t,x(t)))-f(u^{-}(t,x(t)))}{u^{+}(t,x(t))-u^{-}(t,x(t))}.

The strategy of proof relies on the Lagrangian representation recently introduced by the third author
[
14
,
15
]
.
A central ingredient is valid for general multidimensional scalar conservation laws and is of independent interest: in Theorem
1.4
we obtain a formula for the entropy flux across a hypersurface in terms of the Lagrangian representation. In order to state this result, we consider finite-entropy solutions of

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\nabla_{x}\cdot f(u)=0,\quad t\in(0,T),\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where the flux
f
is now any
C^{2}
function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the finite-entropy condition amounts to

\displaystyle\mu_{\eta}:=\partial_{t}\eta(u)+\nabla_{x}\cdot q(u)\quad\text{ is a Radon measure for all convex }\eta,

and associated entropy flux
q
given by
q^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}f^{\prime}
.
In
[
14
]
the third author proves the existence of a Lagrangian representation of the hypograph of
u
, that is, a nonnegative finite measure
\omega_{h}
on the set of curves

\displaystyle\Gamma=\left\{\gamma=(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{v})\in BV([0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]\colon\gamma_{x}\text{ is Lipschitz}\right\},

with the following three properties.

•

For all
t\in[0,T)
, the pushforward
(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{h}
by the evaluation map
e_{t}\colon\gamma\mapsto\gamma(t^{+})
is uniform on the hypograph of
u(t)
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{h}=\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\lfloor\left\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]\colon v<u(t,x)\right\}.

•

The measure
\omega_{h}
is concentrated on curves
\gamma\in\Gamma
satisfying the characteristic differential equation

(1.6)

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}^{\prime}(t)=f^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T).

In particular
\gamma_{x}
is
S
-Lipschitz for
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
, where
S=\sup|f^{\prime}|([0,1])
is the maximal speed.

•

The total variation of
\gamma_{v}
is controlled by

(1.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{TotVar}\>\gamma_{v}\,d\omega_{h}<\infty.

Remark 1.3
.

One can also define a Lagrangian representation
\omega_{e}
of the epigraph of
u
. This representation satisfies the same properties as the representation of the hypograph, where (
1.5
) is replaced by

\displaystyle(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{e}=\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\lfloor\left\{(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1]\colon v>u(t,x)\right\}.

We will sometimes loosely refer to typical curves chosen according to the measure
\omega_{h}

(respectively
\omega_{e}
), as curves of the hypograph (respectively epigraph).

Our second main result is a decomposition formula for the entropy flux across a given hypersurface, along the curves of the Lagrangian representation.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
u
be a finite-entropy solution of (
1.4
) with Lagrangian representation
\omega_{h}
of its hypograph. Let
\Sigma\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Lipschitz hypersurface.
Then
\omega_{h}
-almost every curve
\gamma\in\Gamma
intersects
\Sigma
at most a finite number of times, in the sense that

\displaystyle\left\{t\in(0,T)\colon(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in\Sigma\right\}\quad\text{ is finite.}

For any open set
U\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
U\setminus\Sigma
has two connected components
\Sigma^{\pm}
, denote by
\nu
the unit normal vector to
\Sigma
pointing from
\Sigma^{-}
to
\Sigma^{+}
and by
u^{\pm}
the traces of
u
on the corresponding sides. Then, for any entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,q)
with

\displaystyle\eta(0)=0,\quad q(0)=0,

the entropy flux across
\Sigma
from
\Sigma^{-}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\nu\cdot(\eta(u^{-}),q(u^{-}))\,\Phi\,d\mathcal{H}^{d}=\int_{\Gamma}\langle F^{-}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)\quad\text{for all }\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U),

where

(1.8)

\displaystyle\langle F^{-}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in I^{+}_{\gamma}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{-}))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))-\sum_{t\in I_{\gamma}^{-}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{+}))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{t\in B_{\gamma}^{-}}(\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{-}))-\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{+})))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))

Here
I_{\gamma}^{\pm}
and
B_{\gamma}^{-}
are disjoint subsets of the intersection times of
(t,\gamma_{x}(t))
with
U\cap\Sigma
,
I_{\gamma}^{+}
corresponding to crossings from
\Sigma^{-}
to
\Sigma^{+}
,
I_{\gamma}^{-}
to crossings from
\Sigma^{+}
to
\Sigma^{-}
, and
B_{\gamma}^{-}
to bounces on
\Sigma
from
\Sigma_{-}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Specifically, the sets
I^{\pm}_{\gamma}
,
B_{\gamma}^{-}
appearing in Theorem
1.4
are given by

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}^{+}=\left\{t\in(0,T)\colon(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in U\cap\Sigma,\,(t\pm\delta,\gamma_{x}(t\pm\delta))\in\Sigma^{\pm}\text{ for }0<\delta\ll 1\right\},

\displaystyle I_{\gamma}^{-}=\left\{t\in(0,T)\colon(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in U\cap\Sigma,\,(t\pm\delta,\gamma_{x}(t\pm\delta))\in\Sigma^{\mp}\text{ for }0<\delta\ll 1\right\},

\displaystyle B_{\gamma}^{-}=\left\{t\in(0,T)\colon(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in U\cap\Sigma,\,(t\pm\delta,\gamma_{x}(t\pm\delta))\in\Sigma^{-}\text{ for }0<\delta\ll 1\right\}.

Remark 1.6
.

For the Lagrangian representation
\omega_{e}
of the epigraph of
u
(see Remark
1.3
), the identity of Theorem
1.4
becomes,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(\eta(u^{-}),q(u^{-}))\cdot\nu\,\Phi(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d}(t,x)=-\int_{\Gamma}\langle F^{-}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle\,d\omega_{e}(\gamma),

provided
\eta(1)=0
and
q(1)=0
.

Once the first assertion of Theorem
1.4
is established, that typical curves of the hypograph have finite intersection with
\Sigma
, the flux formula (
1.8
) follows from rather natural manipulation, using the Lagrangian property (
1.5
) that
(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{h}=\mathbf{1}_{v<u(t,x)}\,dxdv
in order to link values of
u
with the Lagrangian representation. The finite intersection property is a consequence of a transverse intersection property: tangential intersections are negligible, essentially thanks to the fact that
\Sigma
is of codimension 1 while
(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{h}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
uses the flux formula of Theorem
1.4
and the property, established in
[
15
]
, that for Burgers’ equation (
1.1
) curves of the hypograph cannot cross from the left curves of the epigraph. This enables us to construct
x(t)
as a curve that cannot be crossed from the left by any curve of the hypograph, nor from the right by curves of the epigraph. This implies, via the flux formula (
1.8
) from which some terms can then be dropped, inequalities on the entropy flux across
x(t)
that can only be satisfied by a generalized characteristic.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we prove Theorem
1.1
as a consequence of Theorem
1.4
, whose proof is given in Section
3
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
, that is, the existence of characteristics for finite-entropy solutions of Burgers. Aside from the flux formula from Theorem
1.4
, the main tool is the existence of a curve
x(t)
that cannot be crossed from the left by (typical) curves of the hypograph, nor from the right by curves of the epigraph. This is the only place where we need the strict convexity of the flux
f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a finite-entropy solution of (
1.1
) with strictly convex flux
f
.
For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a Lipschitz curve
x\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
x(0)=x_{0}
, and

(2.1)

\omega_{h}(\{\gamma\in\Gamma\colon\exists t_{1}<t_{2},\;\gamma_{x}(t_{1})<x(t_{1}),\gamma_{x}(t_{2})>x(t_{2})\})=0,

and

(2.2)

\omega_{e}(\{\gamma\in\Gamma\colon\exists t_{1}<t_{2},\;\gamma_{x}(t_{1})>x(t_{1}),\gamma_{x}(t_{2})<x(t_{2})\})=0.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

For any
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
we define a curve
\hat{\gamma}_{t,x}\colon[t,T]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\hat{\gamma}_{t,x}(s)=\inf\{y\in\mathbb{R}:\omega_{h}\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma_{x}(t)<x,\gamma_{x}(s)>y\}=0\}.

In other words,
\hat{\gamma}_{t,x}(s)
is the right-most value of
y
that can be attained at time
s\geq t
by curves of the hypograph passing left from
x
at time
t
. Note that
\hat{\gamma}_{t,x}
is
S
-Lipschitz because
\gamma_{x}
is
S
-Lipschitz for
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
.

To obtain a curve that cannot be crossed from the left by any curves of the hypograph, we iterate this construction on small time intervals. For
\delta>0
we let
t_{k}=k\delta
and define an
S
-Lipschitz curve
x^{\delta}\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
iteratively on
(t_{0},t_{1}]
,
(t_{1},t_{2}]
, etc., by setting

\displaystyle x^{\delta}(0)

\displaystyle=x_{0},

\displaystyle x^{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{\gamma}_{t_{k},x^{\delta}(t_{k})}(t)\quad\text{for }t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}]\cap[0,T],\quad k\delta\leq T.

For all
t\in[0,T]
, the sequence
x^{1/2^{n}}(t)
is monotone, and we set

\displaystyle x(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}x^{1/2^{n}}(t)=\sup_{n>0}x^{1/2^{n}}(t).

From the definitions of
x^{\delta}
and
\hat{\gamma}_{t,x}
we have that, for any
m\geq n>0
,

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(\left\{\gamma\colon\exists t_{1}\in 2^{-n}\mathbb{N},\,t_{2}>t_{1},\,\gamma_{x}(t_{1})<x^{1/2^{m}}(t_{1}),\,\gamma_{x}(t_{2})>x^{1/2^{m}}(t_{2})\right\}\right)=0,

and since
x(t)=\lim x^{1/2^{m}}(t)
we deduce that

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(\left\{\gamma\colon\exists t_{1}\in\bigcup_{n>0}(2^{-n}\mathbb{N}),\,t_{2}>t_{1},\,\gamma_{x}(t_{1})<x(t_{1}),\,\gamma_{x}(t_{2})>x(t_{2})\right\}\right)=0.

Property (
2.1
) follows because
x
is Lipschitz, and so is
\gamma_{x}
for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Property (
2.2
) is a consequence of the fact, proven in
[
15
, Proposition 6]
, that curves of the epigraph cannot cross from the right curves of the hypograph, because
f
is strictly convex. Specifically, exchanging the roles of
\omega_{e}
and
\omega_{h}
in
[
15
, Proposition 6]
, we have that
\omega_{e}
is concentrated on a set
\Gamma_{e}
such that, for any
\bar{\gamma}\in\Gamma_{e}
and
t_{1}<t_{2}\in[0,T]
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(\left\{\gamma\colon\gamma_{x}(t_{1})<\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t_{1}),\,\gamma_{x}(t_{2})>\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t_{2})\right\}\right)=0.

Assuming that
\Gamma_{e}
has non-empty intersection with the set in (
2.2
) and using the definition of
x(t)
, we would obtain a curve
\bar{\gamma}\in\Gamma_{e}
and
t_{1}<t_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t_{1})>x^{1/2^{n}}(t_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t_{2})<x^{1/2^{n}}(t_{2}),

for some large enough
n
, and so there is
k\geq 0
,
t_{k}=k/2^{n}
, and
t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}]
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t_{k})\geq x^{1/2^{n}}(t_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t)<x^{1/2^{n}}(t)=\hat{\gamma}_{t_{k},x^{1/2^{n}}(t_{k})}(t).

By definition of
\hat{\gamma}_{t,x}
this implies that

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(\left\{\gamma\colon\gamma_{x}(t_{k})<\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t_{k}),\,\gamma_{x}(t)>\bar{\gamma}_{x}(t)\right\}\right)>0,

thus contradicting (
2.3
) and concluding the proof of (
2.2
).
∎

The rest of Theorem
1.1
’s proof consists in showing that the curve
x(t)
provided by Lemma
2.1
is a generalized characteristic.
Thanks to the property (
2.1
) ensuring that curves of the hypograph typically do not cross
x(t)
from the left, in the flux formula (
1.8
) for the flux
F_{\gamma}^{-}
along a curve
\gamma
across
x(t)
, there will be no contribution of the set
I_{\gamma}^{+}
(times of crossings from left to right). Moreover, at typical times
t
where the traces
u^{\pm}(t,x(t))
agree, the contribution of the set
B_{\gamma}^{-}
(times of bounces from the left) will be negligible. As a consequence, for monotone entropies
\eta
the flux across
x(t)
will have a sign, providing a lower bound on
x^{\prime}(t)
. The matching upper bound is then obtained similarly by using the property (
2.2
) that curves of the hypograph typically do not cross
x(t)
from the right.

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, let us be more specific about why the contribution of
B_{\gamma}^{-}
will be negligible at points where the traces agree. This is due to the fact that entropy dissipation can be decomposed along the Lagrangian representation, and jumps in
\gamma_{v}
create an amounts of entropy dissipation that is incompatible with the absence of jump
u^{+}(t,x(t))=u^{-}(t,x(t))
. We recall here the relevant result from
[
14
]
. We denote by
\nu
the total entropy dissipation

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nu=\bigvee_{|\eta^{\prime\prime}|\leq 1}|\mu_{\eta}|,

where
\bigvee
stands for the lowest upper bound of a family of measures (as defined e.g. in
[
1
, Definition 1.68]
). As a consequence of
[
14
, Propositions 5.11 & 5.12]
there is a Lagrangian representation
\omega_{h}
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|D\gamma_{v}|(\{t\in A\colon\gamma_{x}(t)\in B\})\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)=\nu(A\times B),

for any borelian sets
A\subset[0,T]
,
B\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is proved in
[
15
]
that for Burgers equation the measure
\nu
is actually equal to
|\mu_{\eta_{0}}|
for
\eta_{0}(u)=u^{2}/2
, but we won’t need it here.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We assume without loss of generality that
u
takes values in
[0,1]
and show that the curve
x(t)
from Lemma
2.1
is a generalized characteristic. As a consequence of
[
6
]
, at almost every
t_{0}\in[0,T]
such that the traces
u^{\pm}(t_{0},x(t_{0}))
are equal, we must have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\nu(B_{r}(t_{0},x_{0}))}{r}=0,

where
x_{0}=x(t_{0})
and
\nu
is the total entropy dissipation (
2.4
). Hence we fix
t_{0}
a Lebesgue point of
x^{\prime}
such that
(t_{0},x_{0})
is a Lebesgue point of
u^{\pm}
satisfying (
2.6
), and prove that
x^{\prime}(t_{0})=f^{\prime}(u_{0})
, where
u_{0}=u^{+}(t_{0},x(t_{0}))=u^{-}(t_{0},x(t_{0}))\in(0,1)
.

Let
\eta
be a nondecreasing
C^{2}
entropy such that
\eta(0)=0
and
q
be the associated entropy flux with
q(0)=0
.
Given a non-negative test function
\Phi
and applying the flux formula of Theorem
1.4
to the curve
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}q(u^{-}(t,x(t)))-x^{\prime}(t)\eta(u^{-}(t,x(t)))\,\Phi(t,x(t))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{t\in I_{\gamma}^{+}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{-}))\Phi(t,x(t))\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)-\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{t\in I_{\gamma}^{-}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{+}))\Phi(t,x(t))\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{t\in B_{\gamma}^{-}}(\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{-}))-\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t_{+})))\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma).

The first term in the right-hand side is zero thanks to the property (
2.1
) that typical curves of the hypograph don’t cross
x(t)
from the left. The second term is nonpositive because
\eta
is non decreasing, so we deduce

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}q(u^{-}(t,x(t)))-x^{\prime}(t)\eta(u^{-}(t,x(t)))\Phi(t,x(t))dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{t\in B_{\gamma}^{-}}(\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t^{-}))-\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t_{+})))\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma).

Next we choose

\displaystyle\Phi(t,x)=\chi_{\delta}(t)\varphi(x),\quad\chi_{\delta}(t)=\frac{1}{\delta}\chi\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{\delta}\right),

where
\chi
is a smooth cut-off function with
0\leq\chi(t)\leq\mathbf{1}_{|t|\leq 1}
and
\int\chi=1
, and
\varphi
is any smooth compactly supported non-negative function such that
\varphi(x_{0})=1
. Using the Lebesgue point properties of
t_{0}
we may pass to the limit
\delta\to 0
in the left-hand side of (
2.7
) and obtain

\displaystyle q(u_{0})-x^{\prime}(t_{0})\eta(u_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\Gamma}|D\gamma_{v}|(\{t\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta)\colon\gamma_{x}(t)=x(t)\})\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma).

Since
x
is
S
-Lipschitz and
x(t_{0})=x_{0}
, using (
2.5
) to further estimate the right-hand side we infer

\displaystyle q(u_{0})-x^{\prime}(t_{0})\eta(u_{0})\leq\|\eta^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\nu(B_{C\delta}(t_{0},x_{0})),

where
C=\sqrt{1+S^{2}}
.
Recalling (
2.6
) we deduce

q(u_{0})-x^{\prime}(t_{0})\eta(u_{0})\leq 0,

for any nondecreasing
C^{2}
entropy
\eta
with
\eta(0)=0
, and associated entropy flux
q
with
q(0)=0
. Approximating by
C^{2}
functions, this is valid for any nondecreasing
\eta
with
\eta(0)=0
. Choosing

\eta(x)=(x-a)1_{x\geq a},q(x)=(f(x)-f(a))1_{x\geq a}

for any
a\in[0,u_{0})
, we deduce

\displaystyle x^{\prime}(t_{0})\geq\frac{f(u_{0})-f(a)}{u_{0}-a},

and letting
a\to u_{0}
this implies
x^{\prime}(t_{0})\geq f^{\prime}(u_{0})
.

Using curves of the epigraph (see Remark
1.6
) and the property (
2.2
) that typical curves of the epigraph cannot cross
x(t)
from the right, we similarly obtain that

\displaystyle q(u_{0})-x^{\prime}(t_{0})\eta(u_{0})\geq 0

for all nonincreasing entropy
\eta
with
\eta(1)=0
, and associated entropy flux
q
with
q(1)=0
, and applying this to

\eta=(a-x)1_{x\leq a},q(x)=(f(a)-f(x))1_{x\leq a},

for any
a\in(u_{0},1]
we deduce the opposite inequality
x^{\prime}(t_{0})\leq f^{\prime}(u_{0})
.
∎

3.
Proof of the Flux Formula

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
. As in its statement, we fix
u
a finite-entropy solution of (
1.4
) with Lagrangian representation
\omega_{h}
of its hypograph,
\Sigma\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
a Lipschitz hypersurface, and
an open set
U\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
U\setminus\Sigma
has two connected components
\Sigma^{\pm}
. We denote by
\nu
the unit normal vector to
\Sigma
pointing from
\Sigma^{-}
to
\Sigma^{+}
and by
u^{\pm}
the traces of
u
on the corresponding sides.

We start by establishing a first decomposition formula for the flux, where the flux along a curve
\gamma
is not yet in the geometrically meaningful form of
F_{\gamma}^{-}
in Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any entropy-entropy flux pair
(\eta,q)
with
\eta(0)=0
,
q(0)=0
we have

(3.1)

\int_{\Sigma}(\eta(u^{-}),q(u^{-}))\cdot\nu\,\Phi(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d}(t,x)=\int_{\Gamma}\langle\widetilde{F}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle d\omega_{h}(\gamma)\qquad\forall\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U),

where

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{F}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in\overline{\Sigma^{+}}}\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))D(\eta^{\prime}\circ\gamma_{v})(dt)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in\overline{\Sigma^{+}}}\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))(\partial_{t}\Phi+f^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))\cdot\nabla_{x}\Phi)(t,\gamma_{x}(t))dt.

Proof.

For small enough
\delta>0
we may find a
C/\delta
-Lipschitz function
G_{\delta}\colon U\to[0,1]
, with
C>0
independent of
\delta
, such that

\displaystyle G_{\delta}=1\text{ on }\Sigma^{+},\quad G_{\delta}=0\text{ on }\left\{(t,x)\in\Sigma^{-}:\dist((t,x),\Sigma^{+})\geq\delta\right\}

and

\displaystyle\nabla G_{\delta}\to\nu\otimes\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\lfloor\Sigma\cap U}\text{ as }\delta\to 0.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(\eta(u^{-}),q(u^{-}))\cdot\nu\,\Phi(t,x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{d}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int(\eta(u),q(u))\cdot\nabla G_{\delta}(t,x)\,\Phi(t,x)\,dtdx

Since
\eta(0)=0
and
q(0)=0
we may write
\eta(u)=\int\mathbf{1}_{v<u}\eta^{\prime}(v)\,dv
and
q(u)=\int\mathbf{1}_{v<u}\eta^{\prime}(v)f^{\prime}(v)\,dv
. Combining this with property (
1.5
) of the Lagrangian representation,
(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{h}=\mathbf{1}_{v<u(t,x)}\,dx\,dv
, we find

\displaystyle\int(\eta(u),q(u))\cdot\nabla G_{\delta}(t,x)\,\Phi(t,x)\,dtdx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(1,f^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t)))\cdot\nabla G_{\delta}(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\,\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\,dt\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}\left[G_{\delta}(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\right]\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\,dt\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Gamma}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T}G_{\delta}(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\,D(\eta^{\prime}\circ\gamma_{v})(dt)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\int_{0}^{T}G_{\delta}(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))(\partial_{t}\Phi+f^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))\cdot\nabla_{x}\Phi)(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\,dt\Bigg]\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma).

For the second equality we used the fact that
\omega_{h}
is concentrated on curves satisfying the characteristic equation (
1.6
).
Note that
G_{\delta}(t,x)\to\mathbf{1}_{(t,x)\in\overline{\Sigma^{+}}}
for all
(t,x)\in U
as
\delta\to 0
, and that the Lagrangian representation satisfies

\int_{\Gamma}(1+\mathrm{TotVar}\>\gamma_{v}\,d\omega_{h}(\gamma)<\infty
(
1.7
). Hence by dominated convergence we deduce

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int(\eta(u),q(u))\cdot\nabla G_{\delta}(t,x)\,\Phi(t,x)\,dtdx=\int_{\Gamma}\langle\widetilde{F}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle d\omega_{h}(\gamma),

which concludes the proof of (
3.1
).
∎

Next we check that
\widetilde{F}_{\gamma}=F_{\gamma}^{-}
provided
\gamma
intersects
\Sigma
at most a finite number of times.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma
intersects
\Sigma
at most a finite number of times, that is,

\displaystyle N_{\Sigma}(\gamma)=\mathrm{card}\>\left\{t\in(0,T)\colon(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in\Sigma\right\}<\infty,

then

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{F}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle=\langle F_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle\qquad\forall\eta\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\Phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U),

where
\widetilde{F}_{\gamma}
and
F_{\gamma}^{-}
are defined in Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

Setting

(3.2)

\displaystyle\theta(t)=\mathbf{1}_{(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in\overline{\Sigma^{+}}},\qquad\psi(t)=\eta^{\prime}(\gamma_{v}(t))\Phi(t,\gamma_{x}(t)),

we rewrite
\widetilde{F}_{\gamma}
as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{F}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle=-\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)D\psi(dt).

Since
\gamma
has a finite number of intersections with
\Sigma
, we know that the
\{0,1\}
-valued function
\theta
is
BV
. Its jump set can be decomposed as

\displaystyle J_{\theta}=I_{\gamma}^{+}\cup I_{\gamma}^{-},

where
I_{\gamma}^{\pm}
are as in Theorem
1.4
the sets of intersection times where
\gamma
crosses
\Sigma
from
\Sigma^{\mp}
to
\Sigma^{\pm}
. They correspond to positive and negative jumps of
\theta
. Note that
\theta(t)=1
for all
t\in J_{\theta}
. Moreover, the set
B_{\gamma}^{-}
of intersection times where
\gamma
bounces on
\Sigma
from
\Sigma^{-}
corresponds to the non-jump points of
\theta
at which its pointwise value is different from its left and right limits: for
t\in B_{\gamma}^{-}
we have
\theta(t^{+})=\theta(t^{-})=0
but
\theta(t)=1
.

The function
\psi
is also
BV
, and its jump set is included in
J_{\gamma}
, the jump set of
\gamma
. We use the product rule in
BV
for
D(\theta\psi)
in order to rewrite (
3.3
).
As in
[
1
]
we denote by
\tilde{D}\theta
the sum of the absolutely continuous and Cantor parts of the measure
D\theta
.
Since
\widetilde{D}\theta=0
and
\psi
vanishes at the boundary of
(0,T)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{F}_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\theta(t)\tilde{D}\psi(dt)-\sum_{t\in J_{\gamma}}\theta(t)(\psi(t^{+})-\psi(t^{-}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in J_{\theta}\cup J_{\gamma}}(\theta(t^{+})\psi(t^{+})-\theta(t^{-})\psi(t^{-}))-\sum_{t\in J_{\gamma}}\theta(t)(\psi(t^{+})-\psi(t^{-}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in J_{\theta}\setminus J_{\gamma}}\psi(t)(\theta(t^{+})-\theta(t^{-}))+\sum_{t\in J_{\gamma}\cap J_{\theta}}(\theta(t^{+})\psi(t^{+})-\theta(t^{-})\psi(t^{-}))

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{t\in J_{\gamma}\cap J_{\theta}}(\psi(t^{+})-\psi(t^{-}))-\sum_{t\in J_{\gamma}\cap B_{\gamma}^{-}}(\psi(t^{+})-\psi(t^{-}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in I_{\gamma}^{+}}\psi(t^{-})-\sum_{t\in I_{\gamma}^{-}}\psi(t^{+})+\sum_{t\in B_{\gamma}^{-}}(\psi(t^{-})-\psi(t^{+})),

and recalling the definitions of
\theta,\psi
(
3.2
) this corresponds exactly to
\langle F_{\gamma},\eta\otimes\Phi\rangle
.
∎

Now Theorem
1.4
follows directly from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, provided we show that
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
intersects
\Sigma
a finite number of times:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a Lipschitz hypersurface. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma\colon N_{\Sigma}(\gamma)=\infty\right\}\right)=0.

Proposition
3.3
follows from the following:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
h:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz and

\Sigma=\{(t,\hat{x},h(t,\hat{x}))\colon(t,\hat{x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\},

then
N_{\Sigma}(\gamma)<\infty
for
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

First, we know that
\Sigma
is a locally finite union of Lipschitz graphs of the form
x_{i}=h(\hat{x}_{i},t)
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, where
\hat{x}_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{d})
or of the form
t=g(x)
. Moreover for the graphs over
x
we can assume that
|\nabla g|<1/S
, where
S
is the maximal speed for curves
\gamma_{x}
. Therefore,
\omega_{h}
-a.e
\gamma
can have at most one intersection with such a graph. For the rest of the graphs, we apply Lemma
3.4
to each graph, and we conclude that
N_{\Sigma}(\gamma)
is finite for
\omega_{h}-
a.e.
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
∎

We will now prove Lemma
3.4
. The strategy is to rule out non-transverse intersections, but this makes sense only at points of the hypersurface which admit a tangent space. Hence we need a preliminary result allowing us to leave out the non-differentiable points:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
E\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)=0.
Then

\omega_{h}(\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\exists t\in[0,T]\text{ such that }(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in E\})=0.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E)=0,
for any
\varepsilon>0
there is a sequence of balls
B((t_{i},x_{i}),r_{i})
such that

E\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}B((t_{i},x_{i}),r_{i}),\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{i}^{d}<\varepsilon.

For
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
, since
\gamma_{x}
is
S
-Lipschitz we have the implication

\displaystyle\Big(\exists t\in[0,T],\;(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in B((t_{i},x_{i}),r_{i})\Big)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\gamma_{x}(t_{i})\in B(x_{i},(1+S)r_{i}),

and using the property (
1.5
) that
(e_{t})\sharp\omega_{h}=\mathbf{1}_{v<u(t,x)}\,dxdv
we deduce

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(\left\{\gamma\colon\exists t\in[0,T],\,(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in B((t_{i},x_{i}),r_{i}))\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(e_{t_{i}})\sharp\omega_{h}\left(B(x_{i},(1+S)r_{i})\times[0,1]\right)\leq Cr_{i}^{d},

for some constant
C>0
. Summing over
i
this implies

\omega_{h}(\{\gamma\colon\exists t\in[0,T],\;(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in E\})\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{i}^{d}<C\varepsilon,

and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
concludes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.
∎

With Lemma
3.5
at hand we now prove Lemma
3.4
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
R,\varepsilon>0,
and
\delta>0.
Since
\Sigma
is Lipschitz, it has a tangent plane at almost every point. We let
\bar{\Sigma}
be the set of points on
\Sigma
that have a tangent plane. Then by Lemma
3.5
, we see that

\omega_{h}(\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\exists t\in[0,T],\mbox{ s.t. }(t,\gamma_{x}(t))\in\Sigma\setminus\bar{\Sigma}\})=0,

so we only need to show that
N_{\bar{\Sigma}}(\gamma)<\infty
for
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
. We obtain this by proving that for
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
, all intersections with
\bar{\Sigma}
must be transverse, i.e.

(3.4)

\displaystyle\omega_{h}\left(X^{-}\right)=\omega_{h}\left(X^{+}\right)=0,\quad\text{where }

\displaystyle X^{-}=\left\{\gamma\colon\exists t_{0}\in(0,T],\;(t_{0},\gamma_{x}(t_{0}))\in\bar{\Sigma},\;(1,\gamma_{x}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{-}))\in T_{(t_{0},\gamma_{x}(t_{0}))}\Sigma\right\},

\displaystyle X^{+}=\left\{\gamma\colon\exists t_{0}\in[0,T),\;(t_{0},\gamma_{x}(t_{0}))\in\bar{\Sigma},\;(1,\gamma_{x}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{+}))\in T_{(t_{0},\gamma_{x}(t_{0}))}\Sigma\right\}.

Granted (
3.4
) we directly deduce that for
\omega_{h}
-a.e.
\gamma
the set of intersection times with
\bar{\Sigma}
is discrete, and therefore finite.

It remains to prove (
3.4
). We will prove only
\omega_{h}\left(X^{-}\right)=0
, the argument for
X^{+}
being analogous.First, we remark that the set
X^{-}
satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle X^{-}\subset\bigcup_{R>0}\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\bigcup_{\delta>0}X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta},\quad\text{where }

\displaystyle X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta}=\Big\{\gamma\in\Gamma\colon|\gamma_{x}(0)|\leq R\text{ and }\exists t_{0}\in[0,T],\;(t_{0},\gamma_{x}(t_{0}))\in\bar{\Sigma},

\displaystyle\hskip 70.0001pt\mathrm{dist}((t,\gamma_{x}(t)),\Sigma\cap(\{t\}\times B_{R+ST}))\leq\varepsilon(t_{0}-t)\;\forall t\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0})\Big\}.

This follows directly from the definitions of
\gamma_{x}^{\prime}
and
T\Sigma
.
Let indeed
\gamma\in X^{-}
. There obviously is an
R>0
such that
|\gamma_{x}(0)|<R
, and then
\gamma_{x}(t)\in B_{R+ST}
for all
t
since
|\gamma_{x}^{\prime}|\leq S
.
Suppose that
\gamma
intersects
\bar{\Sigma}
tangentially at time
t_{0}
, that is,
\gamma_{x}(t_{0})=x_{0}=(\hat{x}_{0},h(t_{0},\hat{x}_{0}))
, where
\hat{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
denotes the first
(d-1)
components of
x_{0}
, and

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{-})=(\hat{y}_{0},\nabla h(t_{0},\hat{x}_{0})\cdot(1,\hat{y}_{0}))\quad\text{for some }\hat{y}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. By definition of
\gamma_{x}^{\prime}(t_{0}^{-})
and
\nabla h(t_{0},x_{0})
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for all
s\in(-\delta,0]
,

\displaystyle|\gamma_{x}(t_{0}+s)-x_{0}-(s\hat{y}_{0},\nabla h(t_{0},\hat{x}_{0})\cdot(s,s\hat{y}_{0}))|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|s|,

\displaystyle|h(t_{0}+s,\hat{x}_{0}+s\hat{y}_{0})-h(t_{0},\hat{x}_{0})-\nabla h(t_{0},\hat{x}_{0})\cdot(s,s\hat{y}_{0})|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|s|,

which implies

\displaystyle|(t_{0}+s,\gamma_{x}(t_{0}+s))-(t_{0}+s,\hat{x}_{0}+s\hat{y}_{0},h(\hat{x}_{0})+h(t_{0}+s,\hat{x}_{0}+s\hat{y}_{0})|\leq\varepsilon|s|,

and therefore

\mathrm{dist}((t,\gamma_{x}(t)),\Sigma\cap(\{t\}\times B_{R^{\prime}}))\leq\varepsilon(t_{0}-t)
for all
t\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}]
, proving (
3.5
).

Next we claim that the sets
X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta}
defined in (
3.5
) satisfy

(3.6)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\omega_{h}(X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta})\leq C\varepsilon,

for some constant
C>0
depending only on
S
,
R
,
T
and
\Sigma
. Since the union
\bigcup_{\delta}X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta}
is nondecreasing, this implies via (
3.5
) that
\omega_{h}(X^{-})=0
and concludes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Recall the definition

\displaystyle X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta}=\Big\{\gamma\in\Gamma\colon

\displaystyle|\gamma_{x}(0)|\leq R\text{ and }\exists t_{0}\in[0,T],\;(t_{0},\gamma_{x}(t_{0}))\in\bar{\Sigma},

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}((t,\gamma_{x}(t)),\Sigma\cap(\{t\}\times B_{R+ST}))\leq\varepsilon(t_{0}-t)\;\forall t\in(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0})\Big\}.

and let
N:=\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor
. For any
\gamma\in X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta}
, there exists
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that

\mathrm{dist}((k/N,\gamma_{x}(k/N)),\Sigma\cap(\{k/N\}\times B_{R+ST}))<\varepsilon\delta,

and therefore

X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta}\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\left(\frac{k}{N},\gamma_{x}\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\in\Sigma\cap(\{k/N\}\times B_{R+ST})+B(0,\varepsilon\delta)\right\}.

Using the Lagrangian property (
1.5
), we know that
e_{k/N}\sharp\omega_{h}\leq\mathcal{L}^{d+1},
and thus

\displaystyle\omega_{h}(X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}^{d+1}\left(\left(\Sigma\cap\left(\left\{\frac{k}{N}\right\}\times B_{R+ST}\right)+B(0,\varepsilon\delta)\right)\times[0,1]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}^{d}\left(\Sigma\cap\left(\left\{\frac{k}{N}\right\}\times B_{R+ST}\right)+B(0,\varepsilon\delta)\right).

Letting
L
denote the Lipschitz constant of
h
, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma\cap\left(\left\{\frac{k}{N}\right\}\times B_{R+ST}\right)+B(0,\varepsilon\delta)

\displaystyle\subset\left\{x=(\hat{x},x_{d})\in B_{R+ST}\colon|x_{d}-h(k/N,\hat{x})|\leq(L+1)\varepsilon\delta\right\},

and we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{d}\left(\Sigma\cap\left(\left\{\frac{k}{N}\right\}\times B_{R+ST}\right)+B(0,\varepsilon\delta)\right)\leq c(L+1)\varepsilon\delta\,(R+ST)^{d-1},

for some absolute constant
c>0
.
Therefore we have

\omega_{h}(X_{R,\varepsilon}^{\delta})\leq c(L+1)\varepsilon\,(R+ST)^{d-1}\,N\delta\leq c(L+1)\,(R+ST)^{d-1}T\,\varepsilon,

which implies (
3.6
).
∎

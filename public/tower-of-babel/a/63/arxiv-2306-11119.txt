Bounds for Smooth Theta Sums with Rational Parameters
Francesco Cellarosi, Tariq Osman

Abstract
We provide an explicit family of pairs ( 𝜶 , 𝜷 ) ∈ ℝ k × ℝ k ({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k} such that for sufficiently regular f f , there is a constant C C for which the theta sum bound | ∑ 𝒏 ∈ ℤ k f ⁡ ( 1 N ​ 𝒏 ) ​ exp ⁡ { 2 ​ π ​ i ​ ( ( 1 2 ​ ‖ 𝒏 ‖ 2 + 𝜷 ⋅ 𝒏 ) ​ x + 𝜶 ⋅ 𝒏 ) } | ≤ C ​ N k / 2 , \displaystyle\left|\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N}{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\right)\exp\{2\pi i((\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\|^{2}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$n$}})x+{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$n$}})\}\right|\leq CN^{k/2}, holds for every x ∈ ℝ x\in\mathbb{R} and every N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} . Central to the proof is realising that, for fixed N N , the theta sum normalised by N k / 2 N^{k/2} agrees with an automorphic function | Θ f | |\Theta_{f}| evaluated along a special curve known as a horocycle lift. The lift depends on the pair ( 𝜶 , 𝜷 ) ({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}) , and so the bound follows from showing that there are pairs such that | Θ f | |\Theta_{f}| remains bounded along the entire horocycle lift.

Introduction

Let
\mathrm{e}\!\left(z\right):=e^{2\pi iz}
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Schwartz function. We consider the
generalised quadratic Weyl sum
(or
generalised theta sums
)

\displaystyle S^{f}_{N}(x;{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}):=\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}f\!\left(\tfrac{1}{N}{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\right)\mathrm{e}\!\left((\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\|^{2}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$n$}})x+{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\right),

(1.1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We can think of
f
as a smooth cut-off function.
We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Main Theorem, for Schwartz cut-offs.)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
be a Schwartz function and let
{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. If
{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
,
{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n})
, and at least one of
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})=(\tfrac{a}{2m},\tfrac{b}{2m})\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
is such that
\gcd(a,b,m)=1
, and
a
,
b
, and
m
are all odd, then

\displaystyle\left|S_{N}^{f}(x;{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})\right|\ll_{k,m,{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}},f}N^{k/2},

(1.2)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

In (
1.2
) and in the rest of the paper we use Vinogradov’s “
\ll
” notation (which is equivalent to Landau’s
O
-notation) and stress the dependence of the implied constants upon the parameters written as subscripts.
As we shall see, we can relax the assumption that
f
is Schwartz, see Theorem
4.9
.
When
k=1
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\alpha=\tfrac{a}{2m}
and
\beta=\tfrac{b}{2m}
, with
a
,
b
, and
m
all odd, and such that
\gcd(a,b,m)=1
. Then

\displaystyle|S_{N}^{f}(x;\alpha,\beta)|\ll_{m,\beta,f}\sqrt{N},

(1.3)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The only previously known instance of Corollary
1.2
is due to Marklof when
m=1
, i.e.
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, see Section 5.2 of
[
18
]
. Furthermore, it follows from Theorem 1.4 (i) in
[
4
]
and Theorem 1.0.7 in
[
5
]
that the limiting distribution of
|N^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}S_{N}(x;\alpha,\beta)|
as
N\to\infty
for any pair
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

not
of the form given in Corollary
1.2
, is heavy tailed. Therefore, the rational pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
in Corollary
1.2
are
the only pairs
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which the bound (
1.3
) holds for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

In Section
5.1
, as an illustration of the main theorem, we also obtain bounds for the classical Jacobi theta function

\displaystyle\vartheta_{3}(z;w)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{2\pi inz+\pi n^{2}w},

(1.4)

where
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
w\in\mathfrak{H}:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:y>0\}
. When
w
approaches the boundary in
\mathfrak{H}
and
z
is of the form
\alpha+\beta\operatorname{Re}w
with
(\alpha,\beta)
as in Corollary
1.2
, we have the following

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
(\alpha,\beta)=(\tfrac{a}{2m},\tfrac{b}{2m})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
\gcd(a,b,m)=1
and
a,b
and
m
all odd.
Then

\displaystyle\left|\vartheta_{3}(\alpha+\beta x,x+i\varepsilon)\right|\ll_{m}\varepsilon^{-1/4}.

(1.5)

Figure

1.4
for
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6})
(that is
a=3,b=1,m=3
) for various values of

and of
\varepsilon=N^{-2}
. For contrast, Figure

\varepsilon^{1/4}\vartheta_{3}(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{x}{3},x+i\varepsilon)
as

decreases. In this case the pair

There are many results in the literature concerning upper bounds for generalised theta sums as defined in (
1.1
). These estimates have modern applications, for instance, in
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
they are used to understand the value distribution of quadratic forms.

Estimates for
S_{N}:=S_{N}^{f}
where
f=\mathds{1}_{(0,1]},k=1
have received much attention over the years. In the case where
x,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
,
S_{N}
reduces to a quadratic Gauss sum for which various bounds are classical, e.g. if
\gcd(a,q)=1
and
N\leq q
then
|S_{N}(\tfrac{a}{q};0,0)|=O(\sqrt{q})
. See
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
for further details.

The detailed study of
S_{N}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
was initiated by Hardy and Littlewood in
[
11
]
, who were attracted by its “interesting and beautiful properties”. In particular, they prove an approximate functional equation which they use to obtain various bounds for
S_{N}
, typically with some restriction on
x
, e.g. for
x
of bounded type they prove that
S_{N}(x;\alpha,0)\ll_{x}\sqrt{N}
.

For generic
x
it is known that
|S_{N}(x;\alpha,\beta)|\ll_{x}\sqrt{N}\log N
, first in the case where
(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)

by Fiedler, Jurkat and Körner
[
7
]
, and then for every
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
by Flaminio and Forni
[
8
]
, even with a reduction of the power of
\log N
, see Remark
5.4
. Analogous bounds for theta sums in higher rank, including the were obtained by Cosentino and Flaminio
[
6
]
, with recent improvements by Marklof and Welsh
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
. For bounds on Weyl sums of arbitrary degree (not just theta sums, in which the degree of the polynomial in the exponential sum is
2
), see the recent work of Flaminio and Forni
[
9
]
and the references therein.

In light of these results, we see that the behaviour of
|S^{f}_{N}(x;{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})|
for
({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})
as in Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
is far from typical. In particular, the implied constant in (
1.3
) is independent of
x
. Therefore, for
(\alpha,\beta)
as in the statement of Corollary
1.2
there exists
R_{0}>0
such that for any
R>R_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leb}\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}S^{f}_{N}(x;\alpha,\beta)>R\}=0,

(1.6)

for every
N
. It follows that for
R>R_{0}

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{Leb}\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}S^{f}_{N}(x;\alpha,\beta)>R\}=0,

(1.7)

and so the limiting distribution (as
N\to\infty
) of
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}S^{f}_{N}(x;\alpha,\beta)
for random
x
must be compactly supported in this case. In this way we see that Theorem
1.1
implies Theorem 1.0.7 (i) in
[
5
]
.

Our general approach aligns with that of
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
which interprets
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}|S^{f}_{N}|
as an automorphic function
|\Theta_{f}|
evaluated along special curves, known as horocycle lifts.

In Section
2
we outline the construction of the (projective) Shrödinger-Weil representation, a unitary representation of Jacobi group on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. We then use this representation in Section
3
to define a special real-valued function
|\Theta_{f}|
over the group
G:=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2k}
, which is a subgroup of the Jacobi group, and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is some sufficiently regular weight function.
The sum
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}|S^{f}_{N}|
agrees with
|\Theta_{f}|
along special curves
\mathcal{C}^{({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})}_{N}
, known as horocycle lifts. Special invariance properties of
|\Theta_{f}|
imply that it can be viewed as a function on a non-compact, finite volume homogeneous space
\Gamma\backslash G
.
In Section
4
we prove a uniform bound for
|\Theta_{f}|
, provided certain parameters avoid certain (explicit) regions of growth in
\Gamma\backslash G
. We then give a simple, explicit condition on
({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})
so that the curve
\Gamma\mathcal{C}^{({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})}_{N}
(a horocycle lift viewed as a curve in
\Gamma\backslash G
) is bounded away from the regions of growth, uniformly in
N
. In Section
4.3
we give explicit conditions on the pair
({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})
so that
\Gamma\mathcal{C}^{({\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})}_{N}
avoids regions of growth, yielding Theorem
1.1
.

The final Section
5
contains applications of our main theorem to produce a bound for the classical Jacobi theta function
\vartheta_{3}
(Theorem
1.4
) and a bound of the form
|S_{N}(x;\alpha,\beta)|\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{1/2+\varepsilon}
uniformly in
x
and
N
(Corollary
5.2
) for the rational pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
we consider.

2
Representation-Theoretical Preliminaries

In this section we briefly introduce the necessary representation-theoretical ingredients needed to define (in Section
3
) the Jacobi theta function
\Theta_{f}
for any sufficiently regular weight function
f
. For further details, we refer the reader to
[
17
]
.

2.1
The Heisenberg Group its Schrödinger Representation

Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{2k}\times\mathbb{R}^{2k}\to\mathbb{R}
be the standard symplectic form,

\displaystyle\omega\!\left(\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}^{{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.}}\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}^{{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.}}\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}_{1}\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}_{2}\end{array}\right)\right)={\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}_{2}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2},

where
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1},{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2},{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}_{1},{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

We define the Heisenberg group as
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})=\mathbb{R}^{2k}\times\mathbb{R}
with multiplication law

\displaystyle({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})=({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime},\zeta+\zeta^{\prime}+\tfrac{1}{2}\omega({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime})).

(2.5)

Note that
\mathbb{R}^{2k}
is isomorphic to the quotient of
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
by its centre.
The
Schrödinger representation

W
is a representation of
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
on
\mathcal{U}(\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
, the group of unitary operators on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Specifically, using the fact that each element in
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\left(\!\begin{pmatrix}{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\\
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\zeta\right)=\left(\!\begin{pmatrix}{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix},0\right)\left(\!\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\end{pmatrix},0\right)\left(\!\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix},\zeta-\frac{{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}}{2}\right),

(2.6)

we define
W
via

\displaystyle\left[W\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\!\\
\!\mathbf{0}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),0\right)f\right]({\text{\boldmath$w$}})=\mathrm{e}\!\left(({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\right)f({\text{\boldmath$w$}}),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left[W\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!\mathbf{0}\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),0\right)f\right]({\text{\boldmath$w$}})=f({\text{\boldmath$w$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left[W\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!\mathbf{0}\!\\
\!\mathbf{0}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),\zeta\right)f\right]({\text{\boldmath$w$}})=\mathrm{e}\!\left(\zeta\right)f({\text{\boldmath$w$}}),

(2.9)

where
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. For further details on this construction, see Section 1.2 of
[
13
]
.

2.2
\mathrm{Sp}(k,\mathbb{R})
and its Projective Shale-Weil Representation

For any
M\in\mathrm{Sp}(k,\mathbb{R})
we may define a new representation of
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
as
W_{M}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta):=W(M{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)
where
W
is the Schrödinger representation defined in (
2.7
)-(
2.9
). By the Stone-von Neumann theorem any such representation is irreducible, and unitarily equivalent. Therefore, there exists a unitary operator
R(M)
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
such that

\displaystyle R(M)W({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)R(M)^{-1}=W_{M}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta).

(2.10)

By Schur’s Lemma the map
M\mapsto R(M)
is unique up to a phase, that is

\displaystyle R(M_{1}M_{2})=c(M_{1},M_{2})R(M_{1})R(M_{2})

(2.11)

where
|c(M_{1},M_{2})|=1
. The map
R:\mathrm{Sp}(k,\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{U}(\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))
is therefore a projective unitary representation of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, known as the
projective Shale-Weil representation
. The phase
c(M_{1},M_{2})
can be computed explicitly (see Theorem 1.6.11 in
[
13
]
), but we shall not need it in what follows.

2.3
The Jacobi Group and its Projective Schrödinger-Weil Representation

We define the Jacobi group as
\mathrm{Sp}(k,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
whose group law is

\displaystyle(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)(M^{\prime};{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})=(MM^{\prime};{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}+M{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime},\zeta+\zeta^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}\omega({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},M{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime})),

(2.12)

where
\omega
is as in (
2.1
). Using the Schrödinger representation
W
of
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
from Section
2.1
and the projective Shale-Weil representation
R
of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
from Section
2.2
, we can define the
projective Schrödinger-Weil representation
of the Jacobi group as

\displaystyle R(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta):=W({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)R(M).

(2.13)

3
The Jacobi Theta Function

Observe that we may embed
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
in
\mathrm{Sp}(k,\mathbb{R})
via
\bigr(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\mapsto\bigr(\begin{smallmatrix}aI_{k}&bI_{k}\\
cI_{k}&dI_{k}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
where
I_{k}
is the
k\times k
identity matrix. Given a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
we define (up to a phase) a theta function
\Theta_{f}:\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\Theta_{f}(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta):=\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}[R(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)f]({\text{\boldmath$n$}}),

(3.1)

provided the series in (
3.1
) converges absolutely (we shall make sufficient assumptions for this to hold, see Section
3.2
). The function is defined up to a phase because
R(M)
in (
2.10
), and therefore
R(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)
in (
2.13
), are defined up to a phase. To properly define
\Theta_{f}
, one can pass to the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
as done in
[
14
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
when
k=1
. However, since our aim is to study
|\Theta_{f}|
, this definition of
\Theta_{f}
will suffice. Let us see that
|\Theta_{f}|
may be viewed as a real-valued function on the group

G:=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2k}
,
with group law

\displaystyle(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})(M^{\prime};{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime})=(MM^{\prime};{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}+M{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{\prime}).

(3.2)

In fact, by
(
2.13
)
, we see that

R(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)=W(\mathbf{0},\zeta)W({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},0)R(M)
.
Therefore, by (
2.9
) and (
3.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)|=\left|e(\zeta)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}R(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},0)f({\text{\boldmath$n$}})\right|=|\Theta_{f}(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},0)|,

(3.3)

and so we may define
|\Theta_{f}|:G\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}|(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}):=|\Theta_{f}(M;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},0)|.

(3.4)

3.1
Writing
|\Theta_{f}|
in coordinates on
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2k}

Through the embedding of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
in
\mathrm{Sp(k,\mathbb{R})}
, we obtain an action of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{2k}
coming from the group law (
3.2
) of
\mathrm{Sp}(k,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2k}
, namely

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\!a{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}+b{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\\
\!c{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}+d{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\end{array}\right).

By Iwasawa decomposition, any
M\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
may be written as

\displaystyle M=n_{x}a_{y}k_{\phi}

(3.9)

where
x+iy\in\mathfrak{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
,
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
, and

\displaystyle n_{x}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),\>\>\>\>a_{y}:=\left(\begin{matrix}y^{1/2}&0\\
0&y^{-1/2}\end{matrix}\right),\>\>\>\>k_{\phi}:=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\phi&-\sin\phi\\
\sin\phi&\cos\phi\end{matrix}\right).

(3.10)

It can be shown (see, e.g., Section 1.6 of
[
13
]
) that

\displaystyle[R(n_{x})f]({\text{\boldmath$w$}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\|^{2}x\right)f({\text{\boldmath$w$}}),

(3.11)

\displaystyle[R(a_{y})f]({\text{\boldmath$w$}})

\displaystyle=y^{\frac{k}{4}}f\!\left(y^{\frac{1}{2}}{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\right),

(3.12)

\displaystyle[R(k_{\phi})f]({\text{\boldmath$w$}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}f({\text{\boldmath$w$}})&if $\phi=0\!\!\!\pmod{2\pi}$,\\
f(-{\text{\boldmath$w$}})&if $\phi=\pi\!\!\!\pmod{2\pi}$,\\
|\sin\phi|^{-\frac{k}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mathrm{e}\!\left(\tfrac{\frac{1}{2}(\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\|^{2}+\|{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\|^{2})\cos\phi-{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$v$}}}{\sin\phi}\right)f({\text{\boldmath$v$}})\>\mathrm{d}{\text{\boldmath$v$}}&if $\phi\neq 0\!\!\!\pmod{\pi}$.\end{cases}

(3.13)

In view of (
3.9
), formulæ  (
3.11
)–(
3.13
) are enough to define the projective Shale-Weil representation restricted to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Define

\displaystyle f_{\phi}:=R(k_{\phi})f.

(3.14)

Identifying
(M,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2k}
with
(z,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathfrak{H}\times[0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{R}^{2k}
via (
3.9
), the modulus of the theta function
|\Theta_{f}|
may be written in ‘Iwasawa coordinates’ as

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}(z,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})|=y^{k/4}\left|\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}f_{\phi}(({\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})y^{1/2})\mathrm{e}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\|^{2}x+n{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\right)\right|,

(3.15)

We now see how the generalised quadratic Weyl sums (
1.1
) can be related to certain values of
|\Theta_{f}|
. By choosing
(z,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})=(x+iN^{-2},0;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}x\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right))
we have

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}(x+\tfrac{i}{N^{2}},0;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}x\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right))|

\displaystyle=N^{-k/2}\!\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}f(\tfrac{1}{N}{\text{\boldmath$n$}})\,\mathrm{e}\!\left((\tfrac{1}{2}\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\|^{2}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$n$}})x+{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\right)

\displaystyle=N^{-k/2}S^{f}_{N}(x;{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}).

(3.16)

Hence, in light of (
3.16
), bounds of the form
|S_{N}^{f}(x,{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})|\ll N^{k/2}
can be obtained by bounds
|\Theta_{f}(x+\tfrac{i}{N^{2}},0;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}x\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right))|\ll 1
.

3.2
Regular Cut-off Functions

When we introduced
S_{N}^{f}
in (
1.1
), we assumed that
f
is a Schwartz function. We now relax this assumption. It is apparent from (
3.15
) that
|\Theta_{f}|:\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2k}\to\mathbb{R}
is not necessarily well defined pointwise for arbitrary
f
.
For instance, as pointed out in Section 2.6 of
[
4
]
, taking
k=1
and
f=\mathds{1}_{(0,1]}
we see that
\Theta_{f}(z,0;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)
converges since it is a finite sum, but
f_{\pi/2}(w)
decays too slowly as
|w|\to\infty
, and hence the series defining
\Theta_{f}(z,\pi/2;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}},\zeta)
does not converge absolutely.
Define

\displaystyle\kappa_{\eta}(f):=\sup_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}},\phi}(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\|^{2})^{\eta/2}|f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}})|,

(3.17)

and consider the function space

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k}):=\{f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}:\kappa_{\eta}(f)<\infty\}.

(3.18)

We say that a cut-off function
f:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is
regular
if it belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
for some
\eta>k
. Note that the regularity of
f
is sufficient to
guarantee that the series (
3.15
) defining
|\Theta_{f}|
is absolutely convergent for every
(z,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathfrak{H}\times[0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{R}^{2k}
.
Regular cut-off functions generalise Schwartz functions. In fact, it can be shown that Schwartz functions belong to
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
for every
\eta>1
, see Lemma 4.3 in
[
17
]
.

The following lemma
will be useful in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
h:=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),c\right)\in\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. If
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
for some
\eta>1
then
W(h)f\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
.
More precisely,

\displaystyle\kappa_{\eta}(W(h)f)\leq 3^{\eta}(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\|^{2}+\|{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\|^{2})^{\eta/2}\,\kappa_{\eta}(f).

(3.19)

Proof.

Due to (
2.6
) and (
2.9
), it is enough to consider
h=(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\!\\
\!\mathbf{0}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),0)(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!\mathbf{0}\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),0)
. Using (
2.10
) and the fact that
W_{k_{\phi}}(h)=\ W\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cos\phi-{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\sin\phi\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\sin\phi+{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\cos\phi\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),\tfrac{{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$b$}}}{2}\right)
, we get

\displaystyle|[W(h)f]_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}})|=\left|\left[R(k_{\phi})W\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),0\right)f\right]\!({\text{\boldmath$w$}})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|W\!\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cos\phi-{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\sin\phi\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\sin\phi+{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\cos\phi\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),0\right)\cdot f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}})\right|

(3.20)

\displaystyle=|f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}}-({\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cos\phi+{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\sin\phi))|.

(3.21)

Now, note that

\displaystyle\kappa_{\eta}(h\cdot f)

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}},\phi}\>(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\|^{2})^{\eta/2}|f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}}-({\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cos\phi+{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\sin\phi))|

(3.22)

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}},\phi}\>(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}+({\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cos\phi+{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\sin\phi)\|^{2})^{\eta/2}|f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}})|.

(3.23)

Applying the triangle inequality and the AM-GM inequality to the multiplier in (
3.23
) we have

\displaystyle\sup_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}},\phi}\>(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}+({\text{\boldmath$a$}}\cos\phi+{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\sin\phi)\|^{2})^{\eta/2}|f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}})|

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{\eta}\sup_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}},\phi}\>((1+\|{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\|^{2}+\|{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\|^{2})+\|{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\|^{2})^{\eta/2}|f_{\phi}({\text{\boldmath$w$}})|

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{\eta}(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\|^{2}+\|{\text{\boldmath$b$}}\|^{2})^{\eta/2}\kappa_{\eta}(f),

(3.26)

which proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem 3.2

4.1
Uniform Bounds for fixed
\xi

We begin with a lemma along the lines of Lemma 2.1 in
[
4
]
.
Define, for
{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\theta_{k}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}):=\min_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\|,

(4.1)

i.e. Euclidean distance to the closest integer point.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2k}
is such that
\theta_{k}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})>0
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
with
\eta>k
. Then there exits a constant
C=C(k,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2},\eta)>0
such that

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}(x+iy,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})|\leq C\,\kappa_{\eta}(f).

(4.2)

for every
y\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, and every
x,\phi
and
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}
.

Proof.

As
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
it follows that

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}(x+iy,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})|

\displaystyle\leq y^{k/4}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}|f_{\phi}(({\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})y^{1/2})|

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\eta}(f)\,y^{k/4}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\frac{1}{(1+\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\|^{2}y)^{\eta/2}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{\eta}(f)\,y^{-\frac{2\eta-k}{4}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\frac{1}{\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\|^{\eta}}.

(4.5)

As
y\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
it follows that
y^{-\frac{2\eta-k}{4}}\leq 2^{\frac{2\eta-k}{4}}
since
\eta>k
. Let

\displaystyle C(k,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2},\eta):=2^{\frac{2\eta-k}{4}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\frac{1}{\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\|^{\eta}},

(4.6)

which is a convergent series for
\eta>k
, as
\theta_{k}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})>0
.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}:=\{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}|=\theta_{k}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})\}
. Note that
1\leq|\mathcal{M}|\leq 2^{k}
. The series

\displaystyle\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}}\frac{1}{\|{\text{\boldmath$n$}}-{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\|^{\eta}}=\frac{|\mathcal{M}|}{\theta_{k}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})^{\eta}}+O_{\eta}(1),

(4.7)

and so the closer
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}
is to an integer, the larger the constant
C(k,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2},\eta)
in (
4.6
) becomes.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose that
(x+iy;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{(\infty)}
where
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\!\\
\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2k}
is such that
\theta_{k}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2})>0
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
with

. Then

\displaystyle|\Theta_{f}(x+iy,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})|\leq C(k,{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{2},\eta)\,\kappa_{\eta}(f),

(4.8)

where

is as in Lemma
4.1
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemma
4.1

since
y\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
for any

. ∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose
(x+iy;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{(1)}

{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{1}\!\\

\displaystyle|S_{N}^{f}(x;{\text{\boldmath$\alpha$}},{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}})|\ll_{k,m,\eta,\beta,f}N^{k/2}.

, and

Characterizing bipartite distance-regularized graphs with vertices of eccentricity 4
Blas Fernández, Marija Maksimović, Sanja Rukavina

Abstract
The characterization of bipartite distance-regularized graphs, where some vertices have eccentricity less than four, in terms of the incidence structures of which they are incidence graphs, is known. In this paper we prove that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the incidence graphs of quasi-symmetric SPBIBDs with parameters ( v , b , r , k , λ 1 , 0 ) (v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0) of type ( k − 1 , t ) (k-1,t) with intersection numbers x = 0 x=0 and y > 0 y>0 , where 0 < y ≤ t < k 0<y\leq t<k , and bipartite distance-regularized graphs with D = D ′ = 4 D=D^{\prime}=4 .



Mathematics Subject Classifications: 05C75, 05B05

Keywords: distance-regularized graph, bipartite graph, incidence graph, special partially balanced incomplete block design

Lemma 2.1

Let

Special partially balanced incomplete block designs

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
1
-
(v,b,r,k,\lambda)
design and let
(s,t)
be a pair of non-negative integers. A flag (a non-flag) of
\mathcal{D}
is a point-block pair
(p,B)
such that
p\in B
(
p\notin B
). We say that
\mathcal{D}
is a
special partially balanced incomplete block design
(SPBIBD for short) of type
(s,t)
if there are constants
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
with the following properties:

(i)

Any two points are contained in either
\lambda_{1}
or
\lambda_{2}
blocks.

(ii)

If a point-block pair
(p,B)
is a flag, then the number of points in
B
which occur with
p
in
\lambda_{1}
blocks is
s
.

(iii)

If a point-block pair
(p,B)
is a non-flag, then the number of points in
B
which occur with
p
in
\lambda_{1}
blocks is
t
.

In this case, we say that
\mathcal{D}
is a
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
SPBIBD of type
(s,t)
.

The intersection numbers of a
1
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design are the cardinalities of the intersection of any two distinct blocks.
Let
x
and
y
be non-negative integers with
x<y
. A design
\mathcal{D}
is called a (proper)
quasi-symmetric design
with intersection numbers
x
and
y
if any two distinct blocks of
\mathcal{D}
intersect in either
x
or
y
points, and both intersection numbers are realized. That is, if
|B\cap B^{\prime}|\in\{x,y\}
for any pair of distinct blocks
B,B^{\prime}
and both intersection numbers occur.

Recall that the dual of a design
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
is the structure
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}=(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I^{\prime}})
such that
(B,p)\in\mathcal{I^{\prime}}
if and only if
(p,B)\in\mathcal{I}
for every
(p,B)\in\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{P}
. The dual of a quasi-symmetric SPBIBD is an SPBIBD (see
[
13
, Theorem 4.39]
).

A
partial geometry
(r,k,t)
is a
1
-
(v,b,r,k,\lambda)
design such that:

•

any two points are incident with at most one block

•

for every non-flag
(x,B)
, there exist exactly
t
blocks
that are incident with
x
and intersect
B

(1\leq t\leq r,1\leq t\leq k)
.

Notice that partial geometries are exactly quasi-symmetric
(v,b,r,k,1,0)
SPBIBDs of type
(k-1,t)
with intersection numbers
x=0
and
y=1
. A partial geometry for which
t=1
is called a
generalized quadrangle
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0)
SPBIBD of type
(k-1,t)
. If
r=b
, then every point belongs to every block, which is a trivial case that we will not consider. Therefore, we will assume
r<b
. Also, if
t=0
, then every two different blocks do not interesect, and if
t=k
, then
\mathcal{D}
is a
2
-design.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
be a quasi-symmetric
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0)
SPBIBD of type
(k-1,t)
with intersection numbers
x=0
and
y>0
. Then
y\leq t<k
. If
y>1
, then
t>y
and
\lambda_{1}<t\lambda_{1}/y<r
.

Proof.

Let
(p,B)
be a non-flag and let
B_{1}
be a block such that
p\in B_{1}
and
B_{1}
intersects
B
in
y
points. From this it follows that the number of points in
B
which occur with
p
in
\lambda_{1}
blocks is greater or equal than
y
, that is
y\leq t
. Also
t<k
, since from
t=k
it follows that every two points are together in
\lambda_{1}
blocks.

Let
y>1
and
y=t
. For a non-flag
(p,B)
let
\{p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{t}\}\subset B
be the set of points in
B
which occur with
p
in
\lambda_{1}
blocks. Every block
B_{1}
containing

and
p_{1}
will intersect
B
in points
p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{t}
, and, since

, it follows that
\lambda_{1}=1
. Also, in that case there are at least

blocks (
B
and
B_{1}
) containing

and
p_{2}
, which is not possible. It follows that

. Furthermore, for a non-flag
(p,B)
there are exactly

blocks containing

and intersecting

. Since

it follows
\lambda_{1}<t\lambda_{1}/y
. Also

, because in the contrary every two block will intersect.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For the quasi-symmetric

4
The incidence graph of a SPBIBD

The incidence graph of a design
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
is a
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B})
-bipartite graph where the point
x\in\mathcal{P}
is adjacent to the block
B\in\mathcal{B}
if and only if
x
is incident with
B
. In this section, we mention some properties about the incidence graph of certain SPBIBDs. We also study the
2
-
\mathcal{P}
-homogeneous and
2
-
\mathcal{B}
-homogeneous conditions in these graphs.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
be a
1
-
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0)
SPBIBD of type
(k-1,t)
where
0<t<k
. Let
\Gamma
denote the incidence graph of
\mathcal{D}
. Then, every vertex
p\in\mathcal{P}
has eccentricity
4
in
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Notice that for any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
, there are
r
blocks containing
p
and so, which are at distance
1
from
p
. Moreover, since
r<b
there exists a block
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
(p,B)
is not a flag.

Let
S
denote the set of all points incident with
B
which are at distance greater than
2
from
p
. We observe that
S
is nonempty and has size
k-t<k
. Pick
q\in S
. Since
\Gamma
is bipartite, in this case we have that
\partial(p,q)\geq 4
. Note also that there are
t
points in
B
which are at distance
2
from
p
. Let
w
be such a point. For a block
B^{\prime}
containing both
p
and
w
, it follows that
\left[p,B^{\prime},w,B,q\right]
is a
pq
-path of length
4
, meaning that
\partial(p,q)=4
and
\partial(p,B)=3
. Hence, from the above comments, every point
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
has eccentricity
4
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
be a
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0)
SPBIBD of type
(k-1,t)
, where

Let
\Gamma
denote the incidence graph of
\mathcal{D}
. Then, every vertex
p\in\mathcal{P}
is distance-regularized. Moreover,
\Gamma
is distance-semiregular with respect to
\mathcal{P}
with the following intersection numbers:

\displaystyle c_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\hskip 14.22636ptc_{1}=1,\hskip 34.14322ptc_{2}=\lambda_{1},\hskip 34.14322ptc_{3}=t,\hskip 49.79231ptc_{4}=r.

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r,\hskip 14.22636ptb_{1}=k-1,\hskip 14.22636ptb_{2}=r-\lambda_{1},\hskip 14.22636ptb_{3}=k-t\hskip 34.14322ptb_{4}=0.

Proof.

Note that
\Gamma
is bipartite with bipartitions
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{B}
. As every point is contained in
r
blocks and every block has size
k
, it is easy to see that
\Gamma
is
(r,k)
-biregular. We notice that every pair of points either are contained in exactly
\lambda_{1}
blocks or are not contained in any block. Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
every point in
\mathcal{P}
has eccentricity equal to
4
.

We will now show that every point in
\mathcal{P}
is distance-regularized. Pick
p\in\mathcal{P}
. For every

, consider the numbers

,
a_{i}(p,z)
and
c_{i}(p,z)
. Recall that
a_{i}(p,z)=0
as the graph
\Gamma
is bipartite. Note that
b_{0}(p,z)=|\Gamma(p)|
,
c_{0}(p,z)=0
,
c_{1}(p,z)=1
and
b_{4}(p,z)=0
. As every block has size

, every block in

contains

points different from

. Similarly, as every pair of points that appears in a block is contained in
\lambda_{1}
blocks we have

and, since every point is contained in

blocks, we have
b_{2}(p,z)=r-\lambda_{1}
. Moreover, for a block

we have
t

which occur with

in
\lambda_{1}
blocks. This yields

and
b_{3}(p,z)=k-t
, since every block has size
k
. Furthermore,

as every point appears in
r
blocks. Thus, the numbers

and

do not depend on the choice of

is distance-regular around

is distance-semiregular with respect to

.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
be a quasi-symmetric
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0)
SPBIBD of type
(k-1,t)
with intersection numbers
x=0
and
y>0
. Then, for every
B\in\mathcal{B}
there exist
B_{1},B_{2}\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
|B\cap B_{1}|=0
and
|B\cap B_{2}|=y
.

Proof.

Let
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Since
k<v
, there exists
p\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that
(p,B)
is a non-flag. Note also that there are
t
points in
B
which are at distance
2
from
p
. Let
q
be such a point. Then, there exists a block
B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
such that

. Thus,

and therefore,
|B\cap B_{2}|=y
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
denotes the dual od
\mathcal{D}
. Since
\mathcal{D}
is quasi-symmetric,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is an SPBIBD, let say of type
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
. Now, suppose that
B
intersects all the other blocks in
{\mathcal{B}}
. This means that in the incidence graph of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
there exists a point
p^{\prime}
which is at distance
2
from any other point.
If
p
is contained in all blocks of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, then
k=v
, which is not possible. So, there exists a block
B^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that the pair
(p,B^{\prime})
is a non-flag. Since
p^{\prime}
is at distance
2
from any other point then the number of points in
B^{\prime}
that are at distance
2
from
p^{\prime}
equals the size of a block. That is,

.

Now, let

be a point of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. Then, any other point
q^{\prime\prime}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is either in a block with
q^{\prime\prime}
or not. In the first case, the distance between

and
q^{\prime\prime}
is

. In the second case, there exists a block
B^{\prime\prime}
of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
such that

and the pair
(q^{\prime},B^{\prime\prime})
is a non-flag. Since

equals the size od the block in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, we have that
q^{\prime\prime}
is at distance
2
from

. This shows that

is at distance two from any other point. Therefore, any two points of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
are at distance 2. In the other words, any two blocks of
\mathcal{D}
intersect, which contradicts

. Hence, there must be a block

such that

. This finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I})
be a quasi-symmetric
(v,b,r,k,\lambda_{1},0)
SPBIBD of type
(k-1,t)
with intersection numbers
x=0
and
y>0
. Then

.

Proof.

Assume that

. Pick
B\in\mathcal{B}.
Then for every

and

such that

is a non-flag and
(p,B^{\prime})
is a flag, it follows that

. Hence, there is no

such that

.
∎

Lemma 4.5

Let

Pick

Pick now

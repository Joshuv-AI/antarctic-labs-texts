Globally stable cylinders for hyperbolic CAT(0) cube complexes
Nir Lazarovich and Michah Sageev

Abstract
Rips and Sela [ 6 ] introduced the notion of globally stable cylinders and asked if all Gromov hyperbolic groups admit such. We prove that hyperbolic cubulated groups admit globally stable cylinders.



Globally stable cylinders.

Rips and Sela
[
6
]
introduced the notion of globally stable cylinders in their work on solutions of equations over groups. In the context of a
\delta
-hyperbolic group, the idea is as follows. Given two points
x
an
y
in a
\delta
-hyperbolic space
X
, one can choose a geodesic
[x,y]
joining them. Then given three vertices
x,y
and
z
, one has that the two geodesics
[x,y]
and
[x,z]
“fellow travel” (i.e. are within
\delta
of each other) up to some median point from which they diverge. The idea of stable cylinders is to thicken the geodesics into “cylinders” which are not just close to one another, but actually agree for most of the time they fellow travel.

More precisely, let
\theta\geq 0
. A
\theta
-cylinder
C(x,y)
of
x,y\in X
is a subset that satisfies
[x,y]\subseteq C(x,y)\subseteq N_{\theta}([x,y])
for every geodesic
[x,y]
connecting
x,y
.

A choice of
\theta
-cylinders
C:X\times X\to 2^{X}
for every
x,y\in X
is called globally stable if there exist
k,R\geq 0
such that:

1.

inversion invariance:

C(x,y)=C(y,x)
for all
x,y\in X
, and

2.

(k,R)
-stability:
for all
x,y,z\in X
there exists
k

R-
balls
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
in
X
such that

(C(x,y)\cap B(x,\rho))-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}=(C(x,z)\cap B(x,\rho))-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B_{i}

(
\ast
)

where
\rho=(y.z)_{x}=\frac{1}{2}(d(x,y)+d(x,z)-d(y,z))
is the Gromov product (see Figure
1
).

(k,R)
-uniformly stable cylinders

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group. The group
G

admits globally stable cylinders
if some geodesic hyperbolic space
X
on which
G
acts properly cocompactly admits globally stable cylinders which are
G
-invariant
, i.e,
C(gx,gy)=gC(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
and

. For completeness we show in Proposition
9
that this is a group property, and does not depend on the space

Rips and Sela
[
6
]
showed that hyperbolic
C^{\prime}(1/8)
-small cancellation groups have globally stable cylinders. In fact, they show that such groups are
(1,R)-
stable.
They also asked if all hyperbolic groups admit globally stable cylinders.
Recently, Kharlampovich-Sklinos
[
4
]
used the globally stable cylinders of
C^{\prime}(1/8)
-small cancellation groups to study the first-order theory of random groups.
Globally stable cylinders are also used by the first author
[
5
]
to study the connection between complexity and volume of hyperbolic groups.

In this paper, we prove the existence of globally stable cylinders for hyperbolic cubulated groups.

Theorem
.

Let
X
be a hyperbolic
d
-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex. Then,
X
admits globally stable cylinders which are
\Aut(X)
-invariant.

In particular, every hyperbolic cubulated group admits globally stable cylinders.

Note that in the Theorem,

Preliminaries on CAT(0) cube complexes.

We briefly recall standard material in CAT(0) cube complexes, as well as introducing some new definition which we will employ. For a more extensive discussion on CAT(0) cube complexes see e.g
[
1
]
.
Let
X
be a hyperbolic
d
-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex.
Let
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(X)
be its set of hyperplanes.
Every hyperplane
\hat{h}
separates
X
into two components. In each component there is a unique maximal subcomplex
h
of
X
which we call a halfspace bounded by
\hat{h}
.
The halfspaces of
X
are convex and the collection of all of them is denoted by
\mathcal{H}(X)
.
The set
\mathcal{H}(X)
has a natural involution
h\mapsto h^{*}
mapping a halfspace to its complementary halfspace. Moreover, we denote by
\hat{\;}:\mathcal{H}(X)\to\hat{\mathcal{H}}(X)
the map
h\mapsto\hat{h}
that assigns to a halfspace its bounding hyperplane.
For
x,y\in X^{(0)}
, define the set of halfspaces separating
x
from
y
as

\vec{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=\{k\in\mathcal{H}(X)|x\notin k\ni y\},

and its corresponding set of hyperplanes by

\hat{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=\{\hat{k}|k\in\mathcal{H}(x,y)\}.

All other hyperplanes are called
peripheral
to
x,y
, and the set of
peripheral halfspaces
is defined as

\breve{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=\{h\in\mathcal{H}(X)|x,y\in h^{*}\}.

The interval between
x
and
y
is the union of all
\ell_{1}
geodesics between
x
and
y
. It turns out that the interval is also the intersection of all the halfspaces that contain both
x
and
y
. That is, it is the set

I(x,y)=\{z\in X|d(x,y)=d(x,z)+d(z,y)\}=\bigcap_{h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)}h^{*}

where
d
denotes the
\ell_{1}
-metric on
X
.
The interval
I(x,y)
is a convex subcomplex of
X
, and is isomorphic to the dual cube complex to
\mathcal{H}(I(x,y))
, the pocset of halfspaces associated to the set of hyperplanes in
\vec{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)
.
The nearest-point projection
\pi_{I(x,y)}:X\to I(x,y)
can be expressed using the language of ultrafilters as
\pi_{I(x,y)}(v)=v\cap\mathcal{H}(I(x,y))
.

Observation 1
.

Note that in the case that
X
is
\delta
-hyperbolic, any two
\ell_{1}
-geodesics between
x
and
y
are contained in
\delta
-neighborhoods of one another. Thus the interval
I(x,y)
is contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of any
\ell_{1}
-geodesic joining
x
and
y
.

The median
m(x,y,z)
of
x,y,z
in
X
is the unique point in
I(x,y)\cap I(y,z)\cap I(x,z)
. Alternatively,
m(x,y,z)
is the vertex
\pi_{I(x,y)}(z)
.

We denote
\hat{h}\pitchfork\hat{k}
if the hyperplanes
\hat{h},\hat{k}\in\hat{\mathcal{H}}(X)
intersect.
A nested sequence of halfspaces
h_{1}<\ldots<h_{n}
is called a
pencil of halfspaces
and the corresponding sequence of bounding hyperplanes will be called a
pencil of hyperplanes
.

We now want to refine the notion of peripheral hyperplanes and halfspaces to those that do not run too long parallel to a given interval. For a subset
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{H}
and a halfspace
h\in\mathcal{H}
define their intersection number as the maximal size of a pencil of hyperplanes in
\mathcal{K}
that intersect
\hat{h}
, i.e,

i(h,\mathcal{K})=\max\{n|\exists k_{1}<\ldots<k_{n}\in\mathcal{K}:\hat{h}\pitchfork\hat{k_{i}}\forall 1\leq i\leq n\}.

Note that
i(h,\mathcal{K})
may be infinite.
We then define the following subset of
\breve{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)
,

\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)=\{h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)|i(h,\vec{\mathcal{H}}(x,y))\leq D\},

We call these the
D
-peripheral halfspaces
of
[x,y]

and correspondingly

I_{D}(x,y)=\bigcap_{h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)}h^{*}.

Observation 2
.

If
h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)
then
\diam(\pi_{I(x,y)}h)\leq Dd
. Otherwise, there are points
u,v\in\pi_{I(x,y)}(h)
of distance
>Dd
. By the pigeonhole principle out of the
Dd+1
hyperplanes separating
u,v
there are
D+1
hyperplanes satisfying
k_{1}<\ldots<k_{D+1}\in\vec{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)
. But, by the properties of the nearest point projection,
\hat{k}_{1},\ldots,\hat{k}_{D+1}
are transverse to
\hat{h}
, contradicting the assumption that
h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)
.

Henceforth, we will assume that
X
is a
\delta
-hyperbolic CAT(0) cube complex of dimension
d
.
A
grid of size
m
is a pair of pencils of hyperplanes
\hat{h}_{1},\ldots,\hat{h}_{m}
and
\hat{k}_{1},\ldots,\hat{k}_{m}
such that
\hat{h}_{i}\pitchfork\hat{k}_{j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
.

Lemma 3
.

There exists a maximal number

such that

Proof.

Assume

for all

Let

h_{D}\cap k_{D},h_{1}^{*}\cap k_{D},h_{1}^{*}\cap k_{1}^{*},h_{D}\cap k_{1}^{*}
.
Consider a geodesic quadrilateral

\delta
-hyperbolic, every quadrilateral has to be

.
∎

Henceforth, let

Our goal is to prove that the choice

-uniformly stable choice of

We begin by showing that

Lemma 4
.

Let
\theta=Dd
. Then
I(x,y)\subset I_{D}(x,y)\subseteq N_{\theta}(I(x,y))
.

Proof.

By definition

.
To prove the second inclusion, assume for contradiction that there exists a vertex

for which the distance

. Since

is a convex subcomplex, this means that

.
By the pigeonhole principle there are
D+1

such that

and

. By definition of

the hyperplane

\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)
and thus there exist
k_{1}<\ldots<k_{D+1}\in\vec{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)

\hat{h}_{D+1}\pitchfork\hat{k}_{j}
for all

. Since each
\hat{k}_{i}
intersects

, and
\hat{h}_{i}

\hat{h}_{i}\pitchfork\hat{k}_{j}
for all

.
∎

Let

\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho}(x,y)=\{h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)|d_{\max}(x,\pi_{I(x,y)}(h))<\rho\}

. Roughly speaking, this is the collection of

The key step in proving the existence of globally stable cylinders is to bound the number of projections of

-peripheral halfspaces for
[x,y]
which are not

Proposition 5
.

There exists
M=M(\delta,d)\geq 0
such that for all
x,y,z\in X

|\{\pi_{I(x,y)}(h)\mid h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho-Dd}(x,y)-\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,z)\}|\leq M

\rho=(y.z)_{x}
.

Proof.

First, we show that
\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho-Dd}(x,y)
is contained in the full peripheral set of hyperplanes for the pair
(x,z)
.

Claim 6
.

\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho-Dd}(x,y)\subset\breve{\mathcal{H}}(x,z)

Proof.

Note that
\rho=d(x,m)
where
m=m(x,y,z)
is the median of

in
X

Assume for contradiction that
h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho-Dd}(x,y)-\breve{\mathcal{H}}(x,z)

It follows that

Whence

But then

\rho=d(x,m)<d_{\max}(x,\pi_{I(x,y)}(h))<\rho-Dd

a contradiction.

To prove the proposition, we will assume that the number of projections

of halfspaces in

In carrying out this strategy, we will need to make use of the following two technical “pigeonhole claims”, which ensure that our objects of interest are disjoint. We abuse notation and let

Claim 7

For every
m,R,d
there exists
N_{1}=N_{1}(m,R,d)
such that for every
N_{1}

Proof.

Let
K=K(d,R)
be the number of subcomplexes in
[0,R]^{d}
.
Set

.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of

distinct subcomplexes of

of diameter

Claim 8

Let

Set

A standard application of the Pigeonhole Principle shows that one can now find a strictly monotonic subsequence.
∎

w^{\prime}
belongs to
C(x,y)
.

Let
w^{\prime\prime}=\pi_{I(x,y)}(w)
. Since
w^{\prime}\in C(x,y)
we have
d(w,w^{\prime\prime})\leq d(w,w^{\prime})\leq\theta
, and it follows that
d(x,w^{\prime\prime})\leq d(x,w^{\prime})+d(w^{\prime},w)+d(w,w^{\prime\prime})\leq\rho-R+3\theta
.

Now, there exists
h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,y)-\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D}(x,z)
such that
w\in h
. By Observation
2
,
\diam(\pi_{I(x,y)}(h))\leq Dd
. Since
w^{\prime\prime}\in\pi_{I(x,y)}(h)
,

d_{\max}(x,\pi_{I(x,y)}(h))\leq d(x,w^{\prime\prime})+Dd\leq\rho-R+3\theta+Dd\leq\rho-Dd.

Hence
h\in\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho-R}(x,y)-\breve{\mathcal{H}}_{D,\rho}(x,z)
and
w^{\prime\prime}\in P
.
Since
d(w,w^{\prime\prime})\leq\theta=Dd\leq R
we get that
w\in B(w^{\prime\prime},R)\subseteq\mathcal{B}
as desired.
∎

Stable cylinders for cubulated groups.

As discussed in the introduction, stability of cylinders does not depend on the space on which
G
acts properly and cocompactly.

Proposition 9
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group. Let
X,X^{\prime}
be graphs on which
G
acts properly and cocompactly. The space
X
admits
G
-globally stable cylinders if and only if
X^{\prime}
does.

Proof.

Assume
X
has
G
-globally stable cylinders.

Let
F
be a finite fundamental domain for
G\curvearrowright X
. Define the map
\phi:X\to 2^{X^{\prime}}
by sending each
x\in F
to a non-empty set
\phi(x)
of diameter
\diam(\phi(x))\leq D
stabilized by the finite group
\Stab_{G}(x)
, and extend to
X

G
-equivariantly.
By the Milnor-Svarc Lemma the coarse map
\phi
is a quasi-isometry. By replacing
\phi(x)
by
N_{r}(\phi(x))
for some large enough
r
, we assume that
\phi
is “surjective” on
X^{\prime}
in the sense that for all
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
there exists
x\in X
such that
x^{\prime}\in\phi(x)
.

By the Morse Lemma for
X^{\prime}
, let
R
be the constant such that
N_{R}(\phi([x,y]))
contains any geodesic between
x^{\prime}\in\phi(x)
and
y^{\prime}\in\phi(y)
. Define the cylinder
C^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
for
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
by

C^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=N_{R}(\bigcup\{\phi(z)\in X^{\prime}\mid\forall x,y,z:x^{\prime}\in\phi(x),y^{\prime}\in\phi(y),z\in C(x,y)\}).

It is straightforward to verify that
C^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are
G
-invariant globally stable cylinders for
X^{\prime}
.
∎

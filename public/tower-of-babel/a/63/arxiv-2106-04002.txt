Universal Radial Approximation in Spaces of Analytic Functions
Konstantinos Maronikolakis

Abstract
Recently, Charpentier showed that there exist holomorphic functions f f in theunit disk such that, for any proper compact subset K K of the unit circle,any continuous function ϕ \phi on K K and any compact subset L L of the unit disk, there exists an increasing sequence ( r n ) n ∈ ℕ ⊆ [ 0 , 1 ) (r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[0,1) converging to 1 such that | f ⁡ ( r n ​ ( ζ − z ) + z ) − ϕ ⁡ ( ζ ) | → 0 \left\lvert f\left(r_{n}\left(\zeta-z\right)+z\right)-\phi(\zeta)\right\rvert\to 0 as n → ∞ n\to\infty uniformly for ζ ∈ K \zeta\in K and z ∈ L z\in L (see [ Charpentier1 ] ). In this paper, we give analogues of this result for the Hardy spaces H p ​ ( 𝔻 ) , 1 ≤ p < ∞ H^{p}(\mathbb{D}),1\leq p<\infty . In particular, our main result implies that, if we fix a compact subset K K of the unit circle with zero arc length measure, then there exist functions in H p ​ ( 𝔻 ) H^{p}(\mathbb{D}) whose radial limits can approximate every continuous function on K K . We give similar results for the Bergman and Dirichlet spaces.

Firstly, we will look at the universal Taylor series established by Nestoridis in 1996, which have been shown to possess interesting boundary properties. We define

2
Universal radial limits in spaces of analytic functions

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We denote by
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
the space of
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
that satisfy

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{p}}:=\sup_{0<r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left\lvert f\left(re^{i\theta}\right)\right\rvert^{p}d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

This space, endowed with the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{p}}
, is a Banach space and so Baire’s Theorem is at our disposal. Let
\rho
be a subset of
[0,1)
with 1 as a limit point and
K
a compact subset of
\mathbb{T}
with
m(K)=0
, where
m
is the arc length measure.

Definition 2.1
.

We define
\mathcal{V}_{H^{p}}^{K}(\mathbb{D},\rho)
to be the set of
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
that satisfy the following property: Given any continuous function
\phi
on
K
and any compact subset
L
of
\mathbb{D}
, there exists an increasing sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\rho
converging to 1 such that

\sup_{\zeta\in K}\sup_{z\in L}\left\lvert f\left(r_{n}\left(\zeta-z\right)+z\right)-\phi(\zeta)\right\rvert\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Remark 2.2
.

We notice that the definition cannot be improved by asking that
m(K)>0
. This is true because any
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
has non-tangential limits
m
-almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
and so it cannot have the universal boundary property described above on a set of positive measure.

Theorem 2.3
.

The set
\mathcal{V}_{H^{p}}^{K}(\mathbb{D},\rho)
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

In order to prove Theorem
2.3
, it suffices to write
\mathcal{V}_{H^{p}}^{K}(\mathbb{D},\rho)
as a countable intersection of dense open sets and use Baire’s Theorem.

Let
\left(P_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an enumeration of complex polynomials whose coefficients have rational coordinates. For
j\in\mathbb{N},s\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
r\in[0,1)
, we introduce the set

U(j,s,r):=\left\{f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D}):\sup_{\zeta\in K}\sup_{z\in\overline{D\left(0,\frac{s}{s+1}\right)}}\left\lvert f\left(r\left(\zeta-z\right)+z\right)-P_{j}(\zeta)\right\rvert<\frac{1}{s}\right\}.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have that

\mathcal{V}_{H^{p}}^{K}(\mathbb{D},\rho)=\bigcap_{j,s}\ \bigcup_{r\in\rho}U(j,s,r).

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathcal{V}_{H^{p}}^{K}(\mathbb{D},\rho)\subseteq\bigcap_{j,s}\ \bigcup_{r\in\rho}U(j,s,r)
holds trivially. So, let
f
belong to the right-hand side. Let
\phi
be a continuous function on
K,L
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Firstly, we can choose
s
large enough such that
L\subseteq\overline{D\left(0,\frac{s}{s+1}\right)}
and
\frac{1}{s}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. By Mergelyan’s Theorem, there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{\zeta\in K}\left\lvert P_{j}(\zeta)-\phi(\zeta)\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By assumption, there exists
r\in\rho
such that

\sup_{\zeta\in K}\sup_{z\in\overline{D\left(0,\frac{s}{s+1}\right)}}\left\lvert f\left(r\left(\zeta-z\right)+z\right)-P_{j}(\zeta)\right\rvert<\frac{1}{s}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

and so

\sup_{\zeta\in K}\sup_{z\in L}\left\lvert f\left(r\left(\zeta-z\right)+z\right)-\phi(\zeta)\right\rvert<\varepsilon.

This proves that
f\in\mathcal{V}_{H^{p}}^{K}(\mathbb{D},\rho)
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{N},s\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and any
r\in[0,1)
, the set
U(j,s,r)
is open in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in U(j,s,r)
. Let also
K_{r,s}
be the set

K_{r,s}=\left\{r(\zeta-z)+z:\zeta\in K,z\in\overline{D\left(0,\frac{s}{s+1}\right)}\right\}.

Clearly, the set
K_{r,s}
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
, so there exists
C_{r,s,p}>0
such that
|\phi(z)|\leq C_{r,s,p}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{p}}
for every
\phi\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and
z\in K_{r,s}
(see
[
Duren1
]
p.36). We set

\varepsilon:=\frac{\frac{1}{s}-\sup_{\zeta\in K}\sup_{z\in\overline{D\left(0,\frac{s}{s+1}\right)}}\left\lvert f\left(r\left(\zeta-z\right)+z\right)-P_{j}(\zeta)\right\rvert}{2C_{r,s,p}}>0.

It suffices to show that any function
g\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
with
\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{H^{p}}<\varepsilon
belongs to
U(j,s,r)
. Indeed, we get that

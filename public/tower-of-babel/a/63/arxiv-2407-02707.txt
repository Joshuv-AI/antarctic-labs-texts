Abstract
. We compute the exact thermal
partition functions of a massive scalar field on flat spacetime backgrounds
of the form
\mathbb{R}^{d-q}\times\mathbb{T}^{q+1}
and show that they possess an

{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
symmetry. Non-trivial relations between equivalent
expressions for the result are obtained by doing the computation using
functional, canonical and worldline methods. For
q=1
, the results exhibit
modular symmetry and may be expressed in terms of massive Maass-Jacobi
forms. In the complex case with chemical potential for
{\rm U}(1)
charge
turned on, the usual discussion of relativistic Bose-Einstein condensation
is modified by the presence of the small dimensions.

Introduction

{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})

This leads one directly to the question addressed in this follow-up paper: what
is the effect of mass on
{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
invariance, and for
q=1

modular invariance?

While there is a considerable amount of literature on how to compute such

[

for massive free fermions on a torus, and

[
19
]
for a massive complex scalar in odd spacetime dimensions,

q=0,d=2L
). In this work, we revisit relativistic Bose-Einstein condensation on

\mathbb{R}^{d-q}\times\mathbb{T}^{q+1}

{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})

2
Complex massive scalar field on

\mathbb{R}^{d-q}\times\mathbb{T}^{q+1}

In this section, we describe the setup we use for studying thermal partition
functions of a massive complex scalar field in a box with
q
small and
d-q

large spatial dimensions with certain chemical potentials turned on. We discuss
the non-trivial part,
\mathbb{T}^{q+1}
, of the background in terms of adapted
coordinates and a constant frame and provide the action and Hamiltonian of the
scalar field as well as different conserved charges for which chemical
potentials are turned on.

2.2
Lagrangian action, Hamiltonian, and Noether charges

In

coordinates, the information about the geometry of the torus is
encoded in the skewed periodicities determined by the frame vectors

{e}^{{{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle S^{E}_{L}[\Phi,\bar{\Phi};m]=\int_{V_{d+1}}d^{d+1}x\,[\partial_{I}\bar{\Phi}\partial^{I}\Phi+\delta^{ab}\partial_{a}\bar{\Phi}\partial_{b}\Phi+m^{2}\bar{\Phi}\Phi].

(2.3)

There is a global

\delta_{\alpha}\Phi=i\alpha\Phi

(2.5)

with
\epsilon_{AB}

\epsilon_{12}=1

P_{i}=-\int_{V_{d}}d^{d}x\,\partial^{d+1}\phi_{A}\partial_{i}\phi^{A}.

(2.6)

In the canonical formalism, the Hamiltonian and Noether charges are given
by

\begin{split}H&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{V_{d}}d^{d}x\,[\pi^{A}\pi_{A}+\partial_{I}\phi^{A}\partial^{I}\phi_{A}+\delta^{ij}\partial_{i}\phi^{A}\partial_{j}\phi_{A}+m^{2}\phi^{A}\phi_{A}],\\

(2.7)

3
Representations of the partition function

Our aim in the following section is to compute and study the properties of the partition
function

Z_{d,q}(e,m,\nu)=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{-S^{E}_{L}[\phi;e,m,\nu]}.

(3.1)

This can be done in any parametrization; the ADM parametrization is only needed
in order to interpret the result in the operator approach as in (
2.28
).

Since the Lagrangian is quadratic,

S^{E}_{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\Gamma}\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma\Delta}\phi^{\Delta}
, the path
integral (
3.1
) is formally given by a functional determinant

\ln Z_{d,q}(e,m,\nu)=-\frac{1}{2}\ln{\rm Det}\,\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma\Delta}=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\ln\mathcal{D}_{\Gamma\Delta}.

(3.2)

3.1
{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
invariance of the partition function

As in the massless case, the partition function of a massive real scalar field
may be expected to be invariant under SL
(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
transformations of the
lattice frame vectors

e^{{{\prime}{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.30556pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.30556pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.34749pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[0.9625pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha}}}=S^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\beta}}}_{{{\alpha}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.77156pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.97969pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}e^{{{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.77156pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.97969pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\beta}}},\quad S^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\beta}}}_{{{\alpha}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.77156pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.97969pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\in{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z}).

(3.3)

This invariance can be seen by the following argument which is same as for the massless case in
[
3
]
, section 4.2. The action (
2.31
) is diffeomorphism invariant where derivatives, fields, metric, and vielbeins are transformed appropriately under diffeomorphisms. In particular, the mass term is also invariant by itself. However, this invariance is broken to down to
SL
(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
transformations due to the periodic boundary conditions in
y^{\alpha}
. Assuming that the path integral measure remains invariant, this implies that the partition function is invariant

Z_{d,q}[e,m,\nu]=Z_{d,q}[e^{\prime},m,\nu],

(3.4)

with
{\rm det}\,g^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}={\rm det}\,g_{\alpha\beta}
. More details can be found in

[
3
]
.

3.2
Worldline approach

In the worldline formalism, the trace in equation (
3.2
) can be
represented as an integral over Schwinger proper time,
t
. In these terms,
the partition function admits the representation

\begin{split}&\ln Z_{d,q}(e,m,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{dt}{t}\ {\rm Tr}\ e^{-t\hat{H}^{P}},\\
&\hat{H}^{P}=(\hat{p}_{I}\hat{p}^{I}+\hat{p}_{\alpha}g^{\alpha\beta}\hat{p}_{\beta}+m^{2}-\nu^{2})\delta_{AB}+2\nu e^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[17.92989pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[17.92989pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[8.8115pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[6.29393pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha}}}_{{{d+1}\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\hat{p}_{\alpha}\epsilon_{AB},\end{split}

(3.5)

where one has to properly remove zero modes. The first quantized Hamiltonian

\hat{H}^{P}
acts in the tensor product of a free particle Hilbert space with a
2-dimensional internal space in order to take into account the real and the
imaginary part of the scalar field as well as the
{\rm U}(1)
charge.

The associated heat kernel,

K_{\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{T}^{q+1}}(x^{\prime I},y^{\prime\alpha},x^{I},y^{\alpha};t)_{AB}=\langle x^{\prime I},y^{\prime\alpha},A|e^{-t\hat{H}^{P}}|x^{I},y^{\alpha},B\rangle

(3.6)

and partition function are worked out in Appendix
B
in
terms of the redefined parameters,

\rho_{L}=\frac{m}{2\pi}(\text{V}_{q+1})^{\frac{1}{q+1}},\quad\nu_{L}=\frac{\nu}{2\pi}(\text{V}_{q+1})^{\frac{1}{q+1}},\quad w^{\pm}_{\alpha}=\mp i\nu_{L}{\hat{e}}^{{{d+1}\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[17.92989pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[17.92989pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[8.8115pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[6.29393pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha}}}.

(3.7)

After a suitable subtraction, one finds that the partition function admits the
following integral representation in terms of the Riemannn theta function
defined in (
A.1
),

\ln Z_{d,q}(e,m,\nu)=\frac{V_{d-q}}{2(\text{V}_{q+1})^{\frac{d-q}{q+1}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{\frac{d+1}{2}-1}e^{-\pi\frac{{\rho_{L}^{2}}}{t}}\sum_{\pm}\big(\vartheta_{q+1}(w^{\pm}|it\hat{g})-1\big).

(3.8)

This expression is manifestly invariant under the
{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})

transformations (
3.3
) that only affects the lattice vectors

e^{{{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha}}}
,

\ln Z_{d,q}(e^{\prime},m,\nu)=\ln Z_{d,q}(e,m,\nu).

(3.9)

Indeed, the Riemann theta function is invariant since,

m^{\alpha}\hat{g}^{\prime}_{\alpha\beta}m^{\beta}=m^{\alpha}S^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\gamma}}}_{{{\alpha}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.20052pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.20052pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.53687pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.81206pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\hat{g}_{\gamma\delta}S^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.77156pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.97969pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\delta}}}_{{{\beta}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.63474pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.63474pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.17776pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55554pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}m^{\beta}=m^{\prime\alpha}\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta}m^{\prime\beta},\quad w^{\prime\pm}_{\alpha}m^{\alpha}=w^{\pm}_{\beta}S^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\beta}}}_{{{\alpha}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.77156pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.97969pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}m^{\alpha}=w^{\pm}_{\alpha}m^{\prime\alpha},

(3.10)

with
m^{\prime\alpha}=S^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.53441pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.77156pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.97969pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\alpha}}}_{{{\beta}\mathchoice{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.19876pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.13454pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.23895pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}m^{\beta}
. Hence, the transformation
amounts to a relabeling of the lattice points and, since the Riemann theta
function involves a sum over the entire
\mathbb{Z}^{q+1}
lattice, it is
invariant under SL
(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
. Furthermore, both
\text{V}_{q+1}
and

\rho_{L}
are invariant because the volume is preserved.

The subtraction, discussed in detail in Appendix
B
,
corresponds to the

in the formula above and removes the

mode of the Riemann theta function. The associated integral (

diverges in odd spatial dimensions. However we choose to subtract it in even

invariance.

3.3
Functional approach

In the functional approach to computing partition functions

,

,

where

and the eigenvalue matrix,

, is defined through

(3.13)

(3.15)

3.4
Canonical approach

We now want to directly evaluate (
2.28
) in the operator approach. An
orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions is now

f_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V_{d}}}e^{ik_{I}x^{I}}e^{2\pi in_{\iota}\theta^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.66785pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.66785pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[0.86128pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\iota}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.74887pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.74887pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.23877pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[0.88484pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}x^{i}},\qquad k_{I}=\frac{2\pi n_{I}}{L_{I}},\qquad n_{I}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p},\qquad n_{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q},

(3.16)

where
V_{d}=V_{d-q}\text{V}_{q}
and the orthonormality condition is

(f_{n_{I},n_{\iota}},f_{n^{\prime}_{I},n^{\prime}_{\iota}})=\int_{V_{d}}\text{d}^{d}x\,f^{*}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}(x)f_{n^{\prime}_{I},n^{\prime}_{\iota}}(x)\\

(3.17)

The fields and their momenta may be expanded as

\phi^{A}(x)=\sum_{(n_{I},n_{\iota})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\phi^{A}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}f_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}(x),\quad\pi_{A}(x)=\sum_{(n_{I},n_{\iota})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\pi_{An_{I},n_{\iota}}f_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}(x),

(3.18)

Defining the oscillator variables,

a^{A}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}}{2}}\big[\phi^{A}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}+\frac{i}{\omega_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}}\pi^{A}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}\big],\quad\omega_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}=\sqrt{k_{I}k^{I}+(2\pi)^{2}n_{\iota}h^{\iota\kappa}n_{\kappa}+m^{2}},

(3.19)

these expansions become

\begin{split}\phi^{A}(x)&=\sum_{(n_{I},n_{\iota})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}}}(a^{A}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}f_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}(x)+{\rm c.c.}),\\
\pi_{A}(x)&=-i\sum_{(n_{I},n_{\iota})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}}{2}}(a_{A,{n_{I},n_{\iota}}}f_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}(x)-{\rm c.c.}).\end{split}

(3.20)

Contrary to the massless case treated in
[
3
]
, the term with

(n_{I},n_{\iota})=(0,\dots,0,0,\dots,0)
can be included because the associated
angular frequency
\omega_{0}=m
does no longer vanish.

Inserting this mode expansion into
H(h,m)
and
P_{i}
, one finds for the
quantized Hamiltonian, the linear momenta in the small dimensions and the
U(1)

charge,

\begin{split}\hat{H}(h)&=\sum_{(n_{I},n_{\iota})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}\hat{a}^{A\dagger}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}\hat{a}_{An_{I},n_{\iota}}+2E^{d,q}_{0}(h,m),\quad\hat{Q}=-i\epsilon_{AB}\sum_{n_{I},n_{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}a^{A\dagger}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}a^{B}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}},\\
\hat{P}_{i}&=\theta_{i}{}^{\iota}\sum_{(n_{i},n_{\iota})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(2\pi in_{\iota})\hat{a}^{A\dagger}_{n_{I},n_{\iota}}\hat{a}_{An_{I},n_{\iota}},\end{split}

(3.21)

where
E^{d,q}_{0}(h,m)
is the renormalized Casimir energy of a single massive
scalar field on
\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{T}^{q}
. Note that, contrary to the
massless case, the contribution to the Hamiltonian of the degree of freedom
associated to
(n_{I},n_{\iota})=(0,\dots,0,0,\dots,0)
is not that of a free
particle but that of another oscillator with angular frequency
\omega_{0}=m
.

The result for the partition function is worked out in Appendix

D
. In terms of the dimensionless parameter,

\rho_{H}=\frac{m}{2\pi}(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}=\rho_{L}b^{-\frac{1}{q+1}},

(3.22)

one finds

\ln Z_{d,q}(\beta,\mu^{j},\theta^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\iota}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.95952pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.95952pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.73428pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.23877pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}},m,\nu)=\frac{V_{d-q}}{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\Big[\\
2b\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m^{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{H}}{\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}\Big(2\pi\rho_{H}\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}\Big)\\
+4b^{-\frac{d-q-1}{2}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\sum_{n_{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n_{\iota}\hat{h}^{\iota\kappa}n_{\kappa}+\rho_{H}^{2}}}{l}\Big)^{\frac{d+1-q}{2}}K_{\frac{d+1-q}{2}}\Big(2\pi lb\sqrt{n_{\iota}\hat{h}^{\iota\kappa}n_{\kappa}+\rho_{H}^{2}}\Big)\\
e^{2\pi iln_{\iota}N^{\iota}}\cosh{(l\beta\nu)}\Big],

(3.23)

where the terms on the second line correspond to
-2\beta E^{d,q}_{0}(h,m)
,
with

E_{0}^{d,q}(h,m)=-\frac{V_{d-q}}{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{d+1-q}{q}}}\Big[\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m^{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{H}}{\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}\Big(2\pi\rho_{H}\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}\Big)\Big].

(3.24)

When taking into account that the
b
-dependent Bessel functions on the third
line are exponentially suppressed, cf. equation (
A.11
), the expression
for the partition function in (
3.23
) is directly adapted to a
low-temperature/small volume expansion

when the chemical potential

\nu
vanishes. When
\nu
doesn’t vanish, the expression is still adapted to a
low temperature expansion, but the term with
n_{\iota}=\{0,\dots,0\}
is no
longer exponentially suppressed. This will be analyzed in detail in the
discussion on Bose-Einstein condensation in section
5
below.

3.5
High temperature expansion

For a high temperature/large volume expansion

starts from expression (

After a suitable use of Poisson resummation, and the same subtraction discussed

\ln Z_{d,q}(\beta,\mu^{j},\theta^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\iota}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.95952pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.95952pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.73428pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.23877pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}},m,\nu)=\frac{\text{V}_{d-q}}{(\text{V}_{q}^{D})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\Big[\\

(3.26)

5
Bose-Einstein condensation

In this section, we briefly discuss Bose-Einstein condensation from the
perspective of the exact expressions of the partition function of a complex
charged relativistic massive scalar field.

5.1
Low temperature regime

The charge density is defined as

\delta_{d,q}=\frac{\langle Q\rangle}{V_{d-q}}=\frac{1}{{V_{d-q}}\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\ln Z_{d,q}(e,m,\nu).

(5.1)

Note that the maximum value of
|\nu|
is
m
. This can be most easily seen
from (
D.2
) and (
D.3
) since there is a branch point at

|\nu|=m
and the partition function ceases to be real for
|\nu|>m
. The
fact that this is a special value can also be understood from (
2.31
) and
(
3.5
) because the effective mass squared,
m^{2}-\nu^{2}
, becomes
negative.

Using (
3.23
), it is explicitly given by

\delta_{d,q}=\frac{4b^{-\frac{d-q-1}{2}}}{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\Big[\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\sum_{n_{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{n_{\iota}\hat{h}^{\iota\kappa}n_{\kappa}+\rho_{H}^{2}}}{l}\Big)^{\frac{d+1-q}{2}}lK_{\frac{d+1-q}{2}}\Big(2\pi lb\sqrt{n_{\iota}\hat{h}^{\iota\kappa}n_{\kappa}+\rho_{H}^{2}}\Big)\\
e^{2\pi iln_{\iota}N^{\iota}}\sinh{(l\beta\nu)}\Big].

(5.2)

In the low temperature regime,
b\gg 1
and
\beta m\gg 1
. Also, using

n_{\iota}\hat{h}^{\iota\kappa}n_{\kappa}\geq 1,\;\forall n_{\iota}\neq(0,\cdots,0)
,
2\pi lb\rho_{H}=l\beta m
, and the
asymptotic behavior of Bessel functions (
A.11
), the terms with

n_{\iota}\neq(0,\cdots,0)
are suppressed by a factor
e^{-2\pi lb(\sqrt{1+\rho_{H}^{2}}-\rho_{H})}

with respect to those with
n_{\iota}=0
. Hence, to leading order

\delta_{d,q}\approx\frac{4b^{-\frac{d-q-1}{2}}}{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{H}}{l}\Big)^{\frac{d+1-q}{2}}lK_{\frac{d+1-q}{2}}(l\beta m)\sinh{(l\beta\nu)}.

(5.3)

Note that, if there are only large dimensions (
q=0
), this is the exact result
since there are no terms with
n_{\iota}\neq(0,\cdots,0)
,

\delta_{d,0}=4\beta^{-d}\big(\frac{\beta m}{2\pi}\big)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}l^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}(l\beta m)\sinh{(l\beta\nu)}.

(5.4)

In the general case
q\neq 0
, up to terms of order

\mathcal{O}\big((\beta m)^{-1}\big)
, the leading order can be expanded
further as

|\delta_{d,q}|\approx{2}\beta^{-(d-q)}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\Big(\frac{\beta m}{2\pi l}\Big)^{\frac{d-q}{2}}e^{-l\beta(m-|\nu|)},

(5.5)

where the sign of
\delta_{d,q}
follows that of
\nu
. The critical density is

|\delta^{c}_{d,q}|=|\delta_{d,q}(|\nu|=m)|\approx{2}\Big(\frac{m}{2\pi\beta}\Big)^{\frac{d-q}{2}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\Big(\frac{1}{l}\Big)^{\frac{d-q}{2}}.

(5.6)

The sum over
l
converges if and only if
d-q>2
. There should thus be at
least
3
large dimensions to achieve Bose-Einstein condensation, and

|\delta^{c}_{d,q}|\approx{2}\Big(\frac{m}{2\pi\beta}\Big)^{\frac{d-q}{2}}\zeta({d-q\over 2}),\quad d-q>2.

(5.7)

The critical temperature is

T_{c}\approx{2\pi\over m}\Big({|\delta_{d,q}|\over 2}\zeta({d-q\over 2})\Big)^{2\over d-q}.

(5.8)

The condensate is formed when the charge density is greater than the critical
charge density and the charge density of the ground state/condensate is

\delta^{G}_{d,q}=\delta_{d,q}-\delta^{c}_{d,q}

\begin{cases}T>T_{c}:\delta^{G}_{d,q}=0,\\
T<T_{c}:\frac{\delta^{G}_{d,q}}{\delta_{d,q}}=1-(\frac{T}{T_{c}})^{d-q\over 2}\quad{\rm and}\;\nu=\pm m.\end{cases}

(5.9)

5.2
High temperature regime

In the high temperature/large volume expansion, the charge density
\delta_{d,q}
can
be found using (
3.26
) and the identity (
A.8
) at
n=1
,

\delta_{d,q}=\frac{1}{(\text{V}_{q}^{D})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\Big[4b_{D}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}l\sinh{(l\beta\nu)}\Big(\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{lb_{D}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}\Big(2\pi l\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{b_{D}}\Big)\\
-b_{D}^{\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{\pm}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m^{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\pm\Big(\frac{\sqrt{(\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{b_{D}})^{2}+(n\pm i\frac{\beta\nu}{2\pi})^{2}}}{\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}}m^{\iota}N_{\iota}^{D}\\
K_{\frac{d}{2}}\Big(2\pi b_{D}\sqrt{(\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{b_{D}})^{2}+(n\pm i\frac{\beta\nu}{2\pi})^{2}}\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}\Big)e^{2\pi im^{\iota}N_{\iota}^{D}n\mp m^{\iota}N_{\iota}^{D}\beta\nu}\\

K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\Big(2\pi b_{D}\sqrt{(\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{b_{D}})^{2}+(n\pm i\frac{\beta\nu}{2\pi})^{2}}\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}\Big)e^{2\pi im^{\iota}N_{\iota}^{D}n\mp m^{\iota}N_{\iota}^{D}\beta\nu}\Big].

(5.10)

The high temperature/large volume regime may be defined through

b_{D}=\frac{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}}{\beta(1+\mu_{i}\mu^{i})^{\frac{q+1}{2q}}}\gg 1,\ \rho_{H}^{D}=\frac{m(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}}{2\pi(1+\mu_{i}\mu^{i})^{\frac{1}{2q}}}\gg 1,\ {\rho_{H}^{D}\over b_{D}}=\frac{\beta m}{2\pi}\sqrt{1+\mu_{i}\mu^{i}}\ll 1.

(5.11)

We will now argue that the first line of (
5.10
) is the dominant one
in this regime. When using the asymptotic behavior of Bessel functions
(
A.11
), in both of the sums over
n
, the terms with
n\neq 0
are
exponentially suppressed as compared to the term with
n=0
. When these are
neglected, the arguments of the Bessel functions in the third and fifth line of
(
5.10
) become

2\pi\rho_{H}^{D}\sqrt{1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{m^{2}(1+\mu_{i}\mu^{i})}}{\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}}.

(5.12)

If this argument is large, these terms are still exponentially suppressed. A
necessary condition for the argument to become small is that

\frac{\nu^{2}}{m^{2}(1+\mu_{i}\mu^{i})}
is very close to unity, and that
|\nu|
is close to

m
with all
\mu^{i}
close to zero. For simplicity, let us assume in the
following that
\mu^{i}=0
, which implies
N_{\iota}^{D}=0
so that the terms on the
second and third lines vanish. We then have

\delta_{d,q}\approx\frac{1}{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\Big[4b_{D}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}l\sinh{(l\beta\nu)}\Big(\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{lb_{D}}\Big)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}K_{\frac{d+1}{2}}\Big(2\pi l\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}}{b_{D}}\Big)\\
+2b_{D}^{2}(\frac{\beta\nu}{2\pi})\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m^{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\Big(\frac{\rho_{H}^{D}\sqrt{1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{m^{2}}}}{\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\\
K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\Big(2\pi\rho_{H}^{D}\sqrt{1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{m^{2}}}\sqrt{m^{\iota}\hat{h}^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa}}\Big)\Big].

(5.13)

Let us now assume that one may do a Taylor expansion around
0
of each of the
terms in the series using (
A.7
), even if the arguments of the
Bessel functions end up being large for sufficiently large
l
or
m^{\iota}
when

|\nu|<m
. The validity of this assumption is discussed in Appendix
I
. One finds

\delta_{d,q}\approx\frac{1}{(\text{V}_{q})^{\frac{d-q}{q}}}\Big[\frac{2b_{D}^{d}\nu\beta}{\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\Gamma(\dfrac{d+1}{2})\left(1-\mathcal{O}(m^{2}\beta^{2})\right)\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{1}{l^{d-1}}\\
+\frac{{b^{2}_{D}}\nu\beta}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}-1)\left(1-\mathcal{O}(\beta^{2}(m^{2}-\nu^{2}))\right)\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m^{\iota}\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}\frac{1}{(m^{\iota}h^{D}_{\iota\kappa}m^{\kappa})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}}\Big].

(5.14)

Note that the sum in the second term converges when
d-q>2
. There should thus
be at least
3
large dimensions to achieve Bose-Einstein condensation. This is
in agreement with the analysis in the low-temperature expansion. Moreover, the
first line dominates for
d>2
. So, the critical density is

\delta^{c}_{d,q}=\delta_{d,q}|_{\nu=\pm m}\approx\pm\frac{2m\text{V}_{q}}{\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}\beta^{d-1}}\Gamma(\dfrac{d+1}{2})\zeta(d-1)~,\quad d-q>2.

(5.15)

The critical temperature is

T_{c}\approx\Big(\frac{\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}|\delta_{d,q}|}{2m\text{V}_{q}\Gamma(\dfrac{d+1}{2})\zeta(d-1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}.

(5.16)

The condensate is formed when the charge density is greater than the critical
charge density and the charge density of the ground state/condensate is

\delta^{G}_{d,q}=\delta_{d,q}-\delta^{c}_{d,q}

\begin{cases}T>T_{c}:\delta^{G}_{d,q}=0,\\
T<T_{c}:\frac{\delta^{G}_{d,q}}{\delta_{d,q}}=1-(\frac{T}{T_{c}})^{d-1}\quad{\rm and}\;\nu=\pm m.\end{cases}

(5.17)

For
3
large dimensions,
d=3,q=0
, one recovers the well-known results.

6
Conclusions

We have studied the partition function of free massive scalar field theory on
locally flat manifolds of the form
\mathbb{R}^{d-q}\times\mathbb{T}^{q+1}
with two
different types of chemical potentials turned on. The main goal of turning on
the first type of chemical potentials,
\mu^{i}
for linear momentum in the small
directions, was to study the effect of mass on
{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})

invariance (and on modular invariance for
q=1
) by computing exact partition
functions. We found that, for
q=1
, the free energy
F=-\beta^{-1}\ln Z
is
described by modular invariant massive deformations of Maass-Jacobi forms
(
4.15
) recently studied in
[
20
]
. For
q>1
, we
obtain higher dimensional generalizations of these forms (
3.15
) which are

{\rm SL}(q+1,\mathbb{Z})
invariant. Making use of this invariance, we obtained
the high and low-temperature expansions of the free energy.

By introducing a second chemical potential,
\nu
for the charge of
U(1)

rotations of a complex massive scalar, we generalized some of the known results
on relativistic Bose-Einstein condensation by Kapusta, Haber and Weldon

[
8
,
10
,
28
,
29
]
to include arbitrary
number of small dimensions. For even
d
, these types of partition functions
have recently been shown to be related to conformal graphs

[
19
]
. It would be interesting to explore the connection
between the recursion relations of
[
19
]
to those of
Haber-Weldon given in equation (
H.16
). More generally, one might try to
understand whether there is a direct connection between these scalar field
partition functions and string theory scattering amplitudes, where these modular
invariant forms and their massive deformations also appear.

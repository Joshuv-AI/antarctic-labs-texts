Scale for codimension growth of Poisson PI-algebras
Victor Petrogradsky

Abstract
. A.Regev proved that the codimension growth of an associative PI-algebra is at most exponential. The author established a scale for the codimension growth of Lie PI-algebras, which includes a series of functions between exponential and factorial functions. We prove that the same scale stratifies the ordinary codimension growth of Poisson PI-algebras satisfying Lie identical relations. As a byproduct, we obtain a new bound on the codimension growth of Lie PI-algebras in terms of complexity functions. We also study very fast codimension growth of some Poisson algebras without Lie identities. We essentially use techniques of exponential generating functions and growth of respective fast growing entire functions.

This sequence proved to be useful to study associative algebras.
So, A.Regev proved that the tensor product of associative PI-algebras is a PI-algebra

A. Giambruno and M. Zaicev proved that a ”precise” exponent

1.2.
Codimension growth of Lie PI-algebras

Let

Theorem 1.2

Let

Yu.P.Razmyslov suggested to consider an exponential generating function, called

\mathcal{C}(L,z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{c_{n}(L)}{n!}z^{n}

The result above was equivalently rewritten as:

Theorem 1.3

(Yu.P.Razmyslov
[
33
]
)

Let
L
be a Lie algebra satisfying a nontrivial identity.
Then
\mathcal{C}(L,z)
is an entire function of complex variable.

Recall standard definitions of varieties of Lie algebras
[
1
]
.
One defines the

\gamma_{1}(L)=L

\gamma_{n+1}(L)=[\gamma_{n}(L),L]

In particular,

A Lie algebra is

\gamma_{s+1}(L)=0

\gamma_{s}(L)\neq 0

All Lie algebras nilpotent of class at most

\mathbf{N}_{s}

A Lie algebra

is
solvable

provided that there exists
a chain of ideals
0=L_{q+1}\subset L_{q}\subset\dots\subset L_{2}\subset L_{1}=L

such that
L_{i}^{2}\subset L_{i+1}

i=1,\dots,q
, and

is minimal with these conditions.
All Lie algebras solvable of length at most

is a variety denoted by
\mathbf{A}^{q}

More generally, a Lie algebra

is

(s_{q},\dots,s_{2},s_{1})

provided that there exists a chain of ideals

Denote by

As the particular case

we get the variety
{\mathbf{A}}^{q}
of solvable Lie algebras of length

Let us make the following observation. First, any polynilpotent Lie algebra is solvable.
Second, the

Lie algebras (the tuple being fixed) are particular interesting examples of solvable Lie algebras.

The author constructed the following hierarchy for the codimension growth of Lie algebras that consists
of a series of subfactorial functions

\Psi^{q}_{\alpha}(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\big(n!\big)^{1-1/\alpha},&\alpha\geq 1,&q=2;\\
\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(\ln^{(q-2)}n)^{n/\alpha}},&\alpha>0,&q=3,4,\dots\end{array}\right.

(1)

The origin of these functions is due to the following result.
It is derived from the study of the respective complexity functions and their growth
(see Theorem

Theorem 1.4

(
[

,
28
]
)

Let
\mathbf{A}^{q}
be the variety of solvable Lie algebras of length

c_{n}(\mathbf{A}^{q})=\frac{n!}{(\ln^{(q-2)}n)^{n}}\left(1+o(1)\right)^{n},\qquad n\to\infty.

Recall that the class of solvable Lie algebras

of length

is a particular case of the variety of polynilpotent Lie algebras

A more general statement is as follows.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
28

)

Consider a tuple of integers
(s_{q},\ldots,s_{1})

Our scale is complete in the sense that the codimension growth of a nontrivial variety
of Lie algebras is bounded by some of these functions.

[

Let

Then there exists an infinitesimal such that

1.3.
Poisson algebras

A
K
-vector space
A
is called a
Poisson algebra

if
A
is supplied with two
K
-bilinear operations:

•

A
is a commutative associative algebra with unit and multiplication
a\cdot b
(or
ab
), where
a,b\in A
;

•

A
is a Lie algebra which product is traditionally denoted by the
Poisson bracket

\{a,b\}
, where
a,b\in A
;

•

these two operations are related by the Leibnitz rule:

\{a\cdot b,c\}=a\cdot\{b,c\}+b\cdot\{a,c\},\qquad a,b,c\in A.

Let
L
be a Lie algebra over an arbitrary field
K
, and

\{U_{n}|n\geq 0\}
the natural filtration of its universal enveloping algebra
U(L)
.
Consider the
symmetric algebra

S(L):=\gr U(L)=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}U_{n}/U_{n-1}
.
Recall that
S(L)
is identified with the polynomial ring
K[v_{i}\,|\,i\in I]
,
where
\{v_{i}\,|\,i\in I\}
is an arbitrary
K
-basis of
L
(see e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
2
]
).
Set
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}=[v_{i},v_{j}]
for all
i,j\in I
.
Extending to the whole of
S(L)
by linearity and using the Leibnitz rule, one obtains the Poisson bracket:

\{v_{i}\cdot v_{j},v_{k}\}=v_{i}\cdot\{v_{j},v_{k}\}+v_{j}\cdot\{v_{i},v_{k}\},\qquad i,j,k\in I.

Now,
S(L)
has a structure of a Poisson algebra, called the
symmetric algebra
of
L
.

1.4.
Free Poisson algebras

Consider the free Lie algebra

Then,

is a

For example, let

We obtain the free Poisson algebra

(2)

the number of non-zero powers

above being finite.

2.
Main results: Scale for codimension growth of Poisson algebras

We distinguish
two types
of elements of the free Poisson algebra and identities they yield:

•

The elements of the free Lie algebra
L(X)
, which are naturally contained in
F(X)=S(L(X))
,
we call
(pure) Lie identities
, e.g. such as
the identity of Lie nilpotence of class
s
:

\{\{X_{1},X_{2}\},\ldots X_{s+1}\}\equiv 0
.

•

The remaining elements are called
mixed identities
. For example, consider:

\{X_{1},X_{2}\}\cdot\{X_{3},X_{4}\}\equiv 0.

(4)

Our goal is to show that the scale for the codimension growth for Lie algebras (
1
)
also stratifies the ordinary codimension growth of Poisson PI-algebras of the first type, e.g.
satisfying a non-trivial Lie identity
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{V}
is a variety of Lie algebras. Then by
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}
we denote the variety of Poisson algebras
defined by the Lie identical relations of
\mathbf{V}
.
So, we obtain the variety of solvable Poisson algebras
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}^{q}
and the
variety of Lie polynilpotent Poisson algebras
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}}
.

Let
P_{n}=P_{n}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
be the span of all multilinear monomials in
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}

in the free Poisson algebra
F(X)=S(L(X))
generated by
X=\{X_{i}|i\in\mathbb{N}\}
,
i.e. we consider multilinear products of Lie commutators, for example, using the basis of the free Poisson algebra (
2
).
Let
F(\mathbf{V},X)
be the relatively free algebra of a variety of Poisson algebras
\mathbf{V}
and

\phi:F(X)\to F(\mathbf{V},X)
the natural epimorphism.
We define the
ordinary codimension sequence

c_{n}(\mathbf{V}):=\dim\phi(P_{n})
,
n\geq 0
.
We warn that in case of Poisson algebras, the
customary codimension growth
is also studied,
defined as the dimension of the space of multilinear customary polynomials (
3
)
in a relatively free algebra, see
[
15
,
16
,
25
]
.
In this paper we study the ordinary codimension growth only, the term ”ordinary” can be omitted.

We formulate the first main result of the paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a variety of Poisson algebras satisfying a nontrivial
Lie identity
of degree
m\geq 3
and

\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W},z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(\mathbf{W})}{n!}z^{n}
its complexity function, the field being arbitrary. Then

1)

The following coefficientwise bound holds:

\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W},z)\preceq\underbrace{\exp(z\exp(z\exp(\cdots(z\exp(z\exp}\limits_{m-1\text{\rm{ times }}\exp}z))\cdots))).

2)

There exists an infinitesimal such that

c_{n}(\mathbf{W})\leq\frac{n!}{(\ln^{(m-2)}n)^{n}}(1+o(1))^{n},\qquad n\to\infty.

The second main result specifies the place of solvable Poisson algebras
\mathbf{P}\mathbf{A}^{q}
on our scale.
We prove our result in generality of free Lie polynilpotent Poisson algebras as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider a tuple of integers
(s_{q},\ldots,s_{1})
, where
q\geq 3
, and the variety of Lie polynilpotent Poisson algebras

\mathbf{P}\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}}
, a field
K
being arbitrary.
Then the ordinary codimension growth has the same parameters as the respective variety of polynilpotent Lie algebras

\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}}
(see Theorem
1.5
):

c_{n}(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}})=\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(\ln^{(q-2)}n)^{n/s_{1}}}\left(\frac{s_{2}+o(1)}{s_{1}}\right)^{n/s_{1}},\qquad n\to\infty.

Our approach is based on study of exponential generating functions of algebras and sets
and the technique of growth of fast growing entire functions developed by the author in
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
,
all necessary results are given in Section
3
.

As an intermediate result, we obtain a new bound on the codimension growth of Lie algebras
in terms of complexity functions (Theorem
4.5
).
To prove the second theorem we use a resent result of S.Siciliano and H.Usefi
[
42
]
(Theorem
6.1
),
due to which we turn to center-by-metabelian Lie algebras, which were extensively studied before.
Now we need to know the respective complexity function,
for which we find explicit formulas in case of an arbitrary field, see Theorem
5.1
.
Finally, we show that the ordinary codimension growth of Poisson algebras without
Lie identical relations can be very high (Theorem
7.1
).

Remark 1

We expect the results will be applied to study overexponential codimension growth of varieties of Jordan algebras started in
[

]
.
We conjecture that scale (

) (or similar to it) will appear in case of Jordan PI-algebras as well.

Complexity functions and fast growing entire functions

In this section, we present facts on exponential generating functions of varieties of linear algebras,

3.1.
Codimension growth of varieties of linear algebras and complexity functions

Let
F(\mathbf{V},X)
be the relatively free algebra of a variety of linear algebras
\mathbf{V}
generated by countable set
X=\{x_{i}|i\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Let
P_{n}\subset F(\mathbf{V},X)
be the subspace of multilinear polynomials of degree
n
in
variables
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. One defines so called

codimension growth sequence
:
c_{n}(\mathbf{V})=\dim P_{n}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It was introduced by A. Regev in order to study identical relations of associative algebras
[
34
]
.
After Yu.P.Razmyslov
[
33
]
, we consider the

complexity function
.
This is the exponential generating function
[
13
]
of the sequence
c_{n}

\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{V},z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(\mathbf{V})}{n!}\,z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Let us give a more general setting in case of sets of monomials,
which are naturally extended to the case of vector spaces and algebras.
Let
A
be a set of monomials in letters
X=\{x_{i}\,|\,i\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Let
\widetilde{X}=\{x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n}}\}\subset X
;
by
P_{n}(A,\widetilde{X})
denote the set
of all multilinear elements of degree
n
in
\widetilde{X}
belonging to
A
.
Suppose that the number of elements
c_{n}(A,\widetilde{X})

does not depend on the choice of
\widetilde{X}
, but depends only on
n
.
In this case we write
c_{n}(A)=c_{n}(A,\widetilde{X})
and say that

A
is
X
-
uniform
and define the

complexity function with respect to

X
:

{\mathcal{C}}_{X}(A,z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(A)}{n!}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

Examples
.

1)

\mathbf{A}
— variety of all associative algebras

\displaystyle P_{n}(\mathbf{A},\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})

\displaystyle=\langle x_{\pi(1)}\cdots x_{\pi(n)}\mid\pi\in S_{n}\rangle,\quad c_{n}(\mathbf{A})=n!,\quad n\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{A},z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{n!}z^{n}=\frac{1}{1-z}.

(5)

2)

\mathbf{M}
— variety of commutative associative algebras (
XY-YX\equiv 0
)

\displaystyle P_{n}(\mathbf{M},\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})

\displaystyle=\langle x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\rangle,\quad c_{n}(\mathbf{M})=1,\quad n\geq 0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{M},z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}z^{n}=\exp(z).

3)

\mathbf{L}
— variety of all Lie algebras

\displaystyle P_{n}(\mathbf{L},\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\})

\displaystyle=\langle[x_{n},x_{\pi(1)},\ldots,x_{\pi(n-1)}]\rangle,\quad c_{n}(\mathbf{L})=(n-1)!,\quad n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{L},z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}z^{n}=-\ln(1-z).

(6)

4)

Let
\mathbf{P}
be the variety of all Poisson algebras over an arbitrary field.
By construction of the free Poisson algebra
F(X)\cong S(A(X))
, we get

c_{n}(\mathbf{P})=n!,\quad n\geq 0;\qquad\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{P},z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{n!}z^{n}=\frac{1}{1-z}.

(7)

The language of exponential generating functions is natural to study
codimension growth because of the following facts.

Lemma 3.1

(
[

,
28
]
)
.

Consider sets of monomials

,
B
in

let there exist complexity functions:

\mathcal{C}(A,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(A)}{n!}z^{n},\quad\mathcal{C}(B,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(B)}{n!}z^{n}.

Then for the formal concatenation product of monomials

\mathcal{C}(A\cdot B,z)=\mathcal{C}(A,z)\mathcal{C}(B,z)
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[

,

,

,

]
)
.

Let a Lie algebra

be uniform with respect to the generating set

Then the complexity function of its universal enveloping algebra satisfies:

\mathcal{C}(U(L),z)=\exp\mathcal{C}(L,z).

3.2.
Growth of fast growing entire functions, applications

We start with observations on growth of fast growing entire functions. Fix notations

\displaystyle\ln^{(1)}x:=\ln x,\quad\quad\ \ln^{(q+1)}x:=\ln(\ln^{(q)}x);

\displaystyle\exp^{(1)}x:=\exp x,\quad\exp^{(q+1)}x:=\exp(\exp^{(q)}x),\quad q\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
f(z)
be an entire function of complex variable.
We use standard notation
{\rm M}_{f}(r):=\max\limits_{|z|=r}|f(z)|
for
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Fix an integer
q\geq 1
.
We define the
order
and
type
of a
level

q
(the latter we define provided that
0<\rho=\ord_{q}f<\infty
) introduced by the author in
[
28
]
:

\displaystyle\ord_{q}f

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\rho\mid{\rm M}_{f}(r)\mathrel{\mathop{\leq}\limits^{\rm a}}\exp^{(q)}(r^{\rho})\}=\operatorname*{\overline{lim}}_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\ln^{(q+1)}{\rm M}_{f}(r)}{\ln r};

\displaystyle\typ_{q}f

\displaystyle:=\inf\{\sigma\mid{\rm M}_{f}(r)\mathrel{\mathop{\leq}\limits^{\rm a}}\exp^{(q)}(\sigma r^{\rho})\}=\operatorname*{\overline{lim}}_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\ln^{(q)}{\rm M}_{f}(r)}{r^{\rho}}.

Let
f(z)
be an entire function and for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
we have
0<\rho=\ord_{q}f<\infty
and
0<\sigma=\typ_{q}f<\infty
.
Then
{\rm M}_{f}(r)
behaves like
\exp^{(q+1)}(\sigma r^{\rho})
as

.

In the particular case

, we get the classical notions of the order
\ord f
and type
\typ f
of an entire function
[
7
]
.
Let

, then

is said of

.
Classical Adamar’s formula connects the growth of an entire function of finite order with an asymptotic of its Taylor coefficients
[
7

), we get the first claim.

Now, let
L=F(\mathbf{C},X,\mathbb{Z})
be the free centre-by-metabelian Lie ring.
Kuzmin proved that
L^{\prime\prime}
is a direct sum of a free abelian group and an elementary 2-group
[
21
]
.
Later, his computations of both the free component and the torsion subgroup were corrected in
[
22
]
and
[
19
]
.
Following
[
22
]
, the monomials

[[x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}}],[x_{i_{3}},x_{i_{4}},x_{i_{5}},\ldots,x_{i_{n}}]],\quad x_{i_{j}}\in X,\quad n\geq 5;\\

(15)

are called
Kuzmin elements
if

i_{1}>i_{2}
,
i_{3}>i_{4}
,
i_{1}\geq i_{3}
, and
i_{4}\leq i_{2}\leq i_{5}\leq i_{6}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n}
.
Building on the results of
[
22
]
it was shown in
[
19
, Theorem 8.1]

that the multilinear component
P_{n}(L^{\prime\prime})
of even degree
n
is a free abelian group that is freely generated
by the multilinear Kuzmin elements of degree
n
and the element

[[x_{3},x_{2}],[x_{4},x_{1},x_{5},x_{6},\ldots,x_{n}]],\quad n\geq 6,

while in the case of odd degree
n\geq 5
, the multilinear component
P_{n}(L^{\prime\prime})
is a direct sum
of a free abelian group that is freely generated by the multilinear Kuzmin elements of degree
n
and a cyclic
group of order 2 that is generated by the element

[[x_{1},x_{2}],[x_{3},x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}]]+[[x_{2},x_{3}],[x_{1},x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}]]+[[x_{3},x_{1}],[x_{2},x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}]].

The component of degree 4 was not considered in
[
21
,
22
]
.
The identity (
12
) is of degree 5, so
P_{4}(L^{\prime\prime})
is the same as that for the free Lie ring, namely

\langle[[x_{\pi(1)},x_{\pi(2)}],[x_{\pi(3)},x_{\pi(4)}]]\mid\pi\in S_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
,
which has rank 3. Its basis corresponds to two Kuzmin elements (
15
) and the additional element above.
Thus, the description above applies to the degree 4 as well.

Now consider the case of the algebra over a field
K
.
The number of multilinear Kuzmin elements (
15
) is equal to
\frac{n(n-3)}{2}
, see
[

, Lemma 7.1, Theorem 7.1]
.
In case
\ch K=2
, by arguments above each multilinear component obtains one more element,
we get the same formula for all integers (this fact follows also from
[

]
)

c_{n}(L^{\prime\prime})=1+\frac{n(n-3)}{2}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}=d_{n}
for

, the same value as above, yielding the second claim.
Let
\ch K\neq 2
, then the 2-torsion elements disappear, the latter having the complexity function

\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}=\mathop{\mathrm{sh}}(z)-z-\frac{z^{3}}{6}.

It remains to subtract this value from the function of claim 2.
(These values
c_{n}(L^{\prime\prime})=\frac{n(n-3)}{2}
or
c_{n}(L^{\prime\prime})=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}
for odd and even

are also shown in
[
1
, 4.8.6]
in case of
\mathbb{C}
).
∎

6.
Codimension growth of solvable Poisson algebras

Now we show that there exist Poisson PI-algebras, satisfying some Lie identical relations,
that correspond to the functions of our scale for the codimension growth (
1
).
We shall use the following recent result of S.Siciliano and H.Usefi.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
42
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
P
be a Poisson algebra that is Lie solvable of length
n
.
Then the Poisson ideal generated by all elements

\{\{\{x_{1},x_{2}\},\{x_{3},x_{4}\}\},x_{5}\}
is
associative nilpotent of index bounded by a function of
n
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We start with a general remark.
Denote by
X
a countable set.
Let
\mathbf{V}
be a multilinear variety of Lie algebras. Consider the free Poisson algebra

F(X)=S(L(X))
and the relatively free Poisson algebra, defined by all Lie identities of
\mathbf{V}
,
as the factor algebra by the verbal ideal
F(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V},X)=F(S(L(X)))/\mathbf{V}(F(S(L(X))))
,
see definitions and properties in
[
1
,
5
]
).
Let
h(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\in\mathbf{V}
be a multilinear Lie identity.
By substitutions of Lie elements
X_{i}=a_{i}\in L(X)
,
i=1,\ldots,m
we get
h(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathbf{V}(L(X))
.
If at least one of the elements above is a product
a_{1}=b_{1}\cdots b_{k}
,
b_{i}\in L(X)
, where
k\geq 2
,
then by Leibnitz rule,
h(b_{1}\cdots b_{k},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in(L(X))^{k}S(L(X))
.
Let
\tilde{L}(X)\subset F(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{V},X)
be the subalgebra generated by
X
using Lie bracket only, then
by our arguments
\tilde{L}(X)\cong F(\mathbf{V},X)
, the relatively free algebra of the variety of Lie algebras
\mathbf{V}
.

Now let
P:=F(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}},X)
be
the relatively free Poisson algebra defined by
the identity of Lie polynilpotency corresponding to the tuple
(s_{q},\ldots,s_{1})
.
Consider the Lie subalgebra
L
generated by
X
. By arguments above,

L\cong F(\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}},X)\subset P
.
Consider the verbal ideal determined by the centre-by-metabelian identity
(i.e. the ideal generated by all substitutions of elements of
L
into identity (
12
)).
We obtain a multihomogeneous ideal
H:=\mathbf{C}(L)\triangleleft L
.
Choose well-ordered multihomogeneous bases
\{v_{i}|i\in I\}
for
L/H
and
\{w_{j}|j\in J\}
for
H
.
The following monomials span the Poisson algebra
P
:

P=\langle v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{a}}w_{j_{1}}\cdots w_{j_{b}}\mid i_{s}\in I,j_{s}\in J,i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{a},j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{b},\ a,b\geq 0\rangle_{K}.

(16)

To evaluate the number of the first factors above remark that
L/H\in\mathbf{C}
, so

\mathcal{C}(L/H,z)\preceq\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{C},z)
, and use Theorem
3.2
.
Denote
f(z)=\mathcal{C}(L,z)=\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}},z)
.
Since
L
is solvable, by Theorem
6.1
, there exists
N
such that
b\leq N
in (
16
).
Using machinery of exponential generating functions (Lemma
3.1
),
and the bound on the number of the seconds factors (
16
), we get

\mathcal{C}(P,z)\preceq\exp(\mathcal{C}(L/H,z))(\mathcal{C}(H,z))^{N}\preceq\exp(\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{C},z))(\mathcal{C}(L,z))^{N}\preceq\exp(\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{C},z))(f(z))^{N}.

(17)

Below, we substitute
z=r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
into the series with nonnegative coefficients, turning
\preceq
into
\leq
.
By Theorem
3.4
, we have a bound

f(r)\leq\exp^{(q-1)}\Big(\Big(\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}+o(1)\Big)r^{s_{1}}\Big),\quad r\to+\infty.

(18)

By (
17
), (
18
), Theorem
5.1
, and using that
q\geq 3
, we get

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{C}(P,r)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{C},r)+N\ln f(r)\leq\frac{r^{2}}{2}\exp(r)+r+N\exp^{(q-2)}\Big(\Big(\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}+o(1)\Big)r^{s_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq r^{2}\exp^{(q-2)}\Big(\Big(\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}+o(1)\Big)r^{s_{1}}\Big),\qquad r\to+\infty;

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\ln^{(q-1)}{\mathcal{C}(P,r)}}{r^{s_{1}}}=\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}.

Recall that
\mathcal{C}(P,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{n}(P)}{n!}z^{n}
. Applying Theorem
3.3
, where
\lambda=s_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\overline{lim}}_{r\to+\infty}{\frac{\ln^{(q-1)}{\mathcal{C}(P,r)}}{r^{s_{1}}}}=\operatorname*{\overline{lim}}_{n\to\infty}\Big(\frac{c_{n}(P)}{n!}\Big)^{s_{1}/n}\ln^{(q-2)}n;

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{c_{n}(P)}{n!}\Big)^{s_{1}/n}\ln^{(q-2)}n\leq\frac{s_{2}}{s_{1}}+o(1),\qquad n\to\infty;

\displaystyle c_{n}(P)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\frac{n!}{(\ln^{(q-2)}n)^{n/s_{1}}}\Big(\frac{s_{2}+o(1)}{s_{1}}\Big)^{n/s_{1}},\qquad n\to\infty.

Finally, the lower bound follows by
c_{n}(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}})=c_{n}(P)\geq c_{n}(L)=c_{n}(\mathbf{N}_{s_{q}}\cdots\mathbf{N}_{s_{1}})

and the lower bound of Theorem
1.5
.
∎

7.
Wild codimension growth in case of mixed identities

In this section we show that
the codimension growth of Poisson PI-algebras without Lie identities can be very high.
Recall that (
7
), the codimension growth of the free Poisson algebra is
c_{n}(\mathbf{P})=n!
.
Unlike varieties with Lie identities (see our Theorem
2.1
),
there exist Poisson algebras with mixed identities
such that
c_{n}(\mathbf{W}_{s})\geq(n-1)!
, see below.

Let us compute the complexity function of the
variety of Poisson algebras
\mathbf{W}_{s}
, defined by the mixed identity:

\{X_{1},X_{2}\}\cdots\{X_{2s-1},X_{2s}\}\equiv 0,\quad s=2,3,\ldots.

(19)

Theorem 7.1
.

The complexity function of the variety of Poisson algebras
\mathbf{W}_{s}
is equal to

1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{s})=\exp(z)\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}\frac{1}{k!}\big(-z-\ln(1-z)\big)^{k}\bigg).

2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{s})=\frac{1}{1-z}-\exp(z)\bigg(\sum_{k=s}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\Big)^{k}\bigg).

Proof.

Let
R
be a Hall basis of the free Lie algebra
L(X)\subset F(X)=S(L(X))
and

R=\cup_{n\geq 1}R_{n}
, where
R_{n}
are monomials of length
n
in the generators
X
.
In particular,
R_{1}=X
, and
\mathcal{C}(R_{1},z)=z
.
Consider the commutators subalgebra:
L^{\prime}=[L,L]=\langle R_{n}|n\geq 2\rangle_{K}
.
Using (
6
), we get a function with non-negative coefficients:

h(z):=\mathcal{C}(L^{\prime},z)=\mathcal{C}(L,z)-\mathcal{C}(X,z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}=-z-\ln(1-z).

(20)

The free Poisson algebra
F(X)
has the PBW-basis

x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{a}}\cdot w_{j_{1}}\cdots w_{j_{b}},\qquad x_{i_{j}}\in X,\quad w_{i_{j}}\in\mathop{\cup}\limits_{n\geq 2}R_{n},

(21)

where all multiplicands above are nondecreasing with respect to a liner order on
R(X)
.
We consider multilinear elements above.

We claim that the verbal ideal
\mathbf{W}_{s}(F(X))
is spanned by monomials (
21
) with
b\geq s
.
First, clearly, all such products belong to the verbal ideal.
Second, consider a substitution into
\{X_{1},X_{2}\}
of products (
21
).
By Leibnitz rule we get a sum of products each containing a factor
\{w_{i},w_{j}\}
, where
w_{i},w_{j}\in R
,
thus yielding a factor
w_{l}\in\cup_{n\geq 2}R_{2}
.
So, the verbal ideal contains at least
s
such factors.
Finally, a basis of
F(\mathbf{W}_{s},X)\cong F(X)/\mathbf{W}_{s}(F(X))
consists of monomials (
21
) with
b=0,1,\dots,s-1
.

Consider a formal set of nonordered(!) products of length
k
and use Lemma
3.1
and (
20
):

\displaystyle U_{k}:

\displaystyle=\{w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{i_{k}}\mid w_{i_{j}}\in\mathop{\cup}_{n\geq 2}R_{n}\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(U_{k},z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}(\cup_{n\geq 2}R_{n},z)^{k}=\mathcal{C}(L^{\prime},z)^{k}=h(z)^{k}.

Now we pass to ordered multilinear products:

\displaystyle\tilde{U}_{k}:

\displaystyle=\{w_{i_{1}}\cdots w_{i_{k}}\mid w_{i_{j}}\in\cup_{n\geq 2}R_{n},\quad i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\tilde{U}_{k},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\mathcal{C}(U_{k},z)=\frac{1}{k!}h(z)^{k}.

(22)

By (
5
), the first factor (
21
) has the complexity function
\exp(z)
.
We count the complexity function of the second factor in (
21
) summing (
22
) for
k=0,\ldots,s-1

and using (
20
), thus yielding the first claim.

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{s}),z

\displaystyle=\exp(z)\Big(1+\sum_{k=1}^{s-1}\frac{h^{k}(z)}{k!}\Big)=\exp(z)\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{h^{k}(z)}{k!}-\sum_{k=s}^{\infty}\frac{h^{k}(z)}{k!}\Big)

\displaystyle=\exp(z)\Big(\exp(h(z))-\sum_{k=s}^{\infty}\frac{h^{k}(z)}{k!}\Big)=\frac{1}{1-z}-\exp(z)\bigg(\sum_{k=s}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\Big)^{k}\bigg).\qed

Examples.
We provide some computations of the series:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{2},z)

\displaystyle=1+z+z^{2}+z^{3}+\frac{7}{8}z^{4}+\frac{17}{24}z^{5}+\frac{41}{72}z^{6}+\frac{169}{360}z^{7}+\frac{51}{128}z^{8}+\frac{25133}{72576}z^{9}+\frac{556037}{1814400}z^{10}+\cdots

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{3},z)

\displaystyle=1+z+z^{2}+z^{3}+z^{4}+z^{5}+\frac{47}{48}z^{6}+\frac{15}{16}z^{7}+\frac{1021}{1152}z^{8}+\frac{43249}{51840}z^{9}+\frac{509}{648}z^{10}+\cdots

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{4},z)

\displaystyle=1+z+z^{2}+z^{3}+z^{4}+z^{5}+z^{6}+z^{7}+\frac{383}{384}z^{8}+\frac{1141}{1152}z^{9}+\frac{5641}{5760}z^{10}+\cdots

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{5},z)

\displaystyle=1+z+z^{2}+z^{3}+z^{4}+z^{5}+z^{6}+z^{7}+z^{8}+z^{9}+\frac{3839}{3840}z^{10}+\cdots

We obtain a version of the bound of S.Ratseev
[
36
, Theorem 1]
.

Corollary 7.2
.

c_{n}(\mathbf{W}_{s})\geq[e(n-1)!]-1
for
s\geq 2
, where
e=2,71828...
.

Proof.

By Theorem,

\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{s},z)\succeq\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{W}_{2},z)=\exp(z)(1-z-\ln(1-z))=\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\Big)\Big(1+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\Big)\\
=1+z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}z^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{n\cdot 0!}+\frac{1}{(n-1)1!}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2(n-2)!}+\frac{1}{n!}\Big)\\
\succeq 1+z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n}\Big(\frac{1}{0!}+\frac{1}{1!}+\cdots+\frac{1}{(n-2)!}+\frac{1}{n!}\Big).

Hence,

c_{n}(\mathbf{W}_{s})\geq(n-1)!(e-\frac{1}{(n-1)!}-\sum_{k>n}\frac{1}{k!})\geq[(n-1)!e]-1
.
∎

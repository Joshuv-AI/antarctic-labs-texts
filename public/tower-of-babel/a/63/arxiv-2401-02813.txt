Abstract
In a previous paper [ 24 ] , Yakubovich and the author of this article proved that certain shifted combinations of completed Dirichlet series have infinitely many zeros on the critical line. Here we provide some lower bounds for the number of critical zeros of a subclass of shifted combinations.

2
Lemmas for the proof of Theorem
1.1

We start by recalling Stirling’s formula for the Gamma function,

\Gamma(\sigma+it)=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,t^{\sigma+it-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}-it+\frac{i\pi}{2}(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})}\left(1+\frac{1}{12(\sigma+it)}+O\left(\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\right),\,\,\,\,t\rightarrow\infty,

(2.1)

valid whenever
-\infty<\sigma_{1}\leq\sigma\leq\sigma_{2}<\infty
.
A similar formula can be written for
t<0
as
t
tends to
-\infty

by using the fact that
\Gamma(\overline{s})=\overline{\Gamma(s)}
.
Of course, a direct consequence of this exact version is

|\Gamma(\sigma+it)|=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,|t|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{|t|}\right)\right),\,\,\,\,\,|t|\rightarrow\infty.

(2.2)

Let us now recall some basic facts about the Dirichet series
\zeta_{\alpha}(s)
.
It is well-known that the theta function
\vartheta_{3}(\tau):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\pi in^{2}\tau}

is a modular form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
with a multiplier system
with respect to the theta group
\Gamma_{\theta}
(see
[
17
]
,
p. 15-16 for details). Therefore, for any
\alpha>0
,
\vartheta_{3}^{\alpha}(\tau)

is a modular form of weight
\alpha/2
with a multiplier system on
the same group. By definition,
r_{\alpha}(n)
are the Fourier coefficients
of the expansion of
\vartheta_{3}(\tau)
at the cusp
i\infty
,
this is (cf.
[
24
]
and
[
17
]
for a clearer explanation
of this expansion)

\vartheta_{3}^{\alpha}(ix):=\theta^{\alpha}(x)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{\alpha}(n)\,e^{-\pi n\,x},\,\,\,\,\,x>0.

(2.3)

The order of growth of
r_{\alpha}(n)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
is
determined by classical estimates due to Petersson and Lehner
[
17
,
18
]
. These estimates show that

r_{\alpha}(n)\ll_{\alpha}\begin{cases}n^{\alpha/2-1}&\alpha>4\\
n^{\alpha/2-1}\log(n)&\alpha=4\\
n^{\alpha/4}&0<\alpha<4.\end{cases}

(2.4)

Thus, we can rigorously define the Dirichlet series (
1.6
)
in the form

\zeta_{\alpha}(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r_{\alpha}(n)}{n^{s}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{Re}(s)>\sigma_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha}{2}&\alpha\geq 4\\
1+\frac{\alpha}{4}&0<\alpha<4\end{cases}.

(2.5)

Mimicking Riemann’s paper, one can easily show that
\zeta_{\alpha}(s)

can be analytically continued to the entire complex plane as a meromorphic
function with a simple pole located at
s=\frac{\alpha}{2}
, whose
residue is
\text{Res}_{s=\alpha/2}\zeta_{\alpha}(s)=\frac{\pi^{\alpha/2}}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)}
.
Moreover, it satisfies Hecke’s functional equation

\eta_{\alpha}(s):=\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\,\zeta_{\alpha}(s)=\pi^{-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-s\right)}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-s\right)\,\zeta_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-s\right):=\eta_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-s\right).

(2.6)

It also follows from the Phragmén-Lindelöf principle that
\zeta_{\alpha}(s)

obeys to the convex estimate

\zeta_{\alpha}\left(\sigma+it\right)\ll_{\alpha}|t|^{\sigma_{\alpha}-\sigma+\delta},\,\,\,\,\frac{\alpha}{2}-\sigma_{\alpha}-\delta<\sigma<\sigma_{\alpha}+\delta,

(2.7)

for any
\delta>0
and
\sigma_{\alpha}
defined by (
2.5
).

In order to estimate the series given in (
1.20
),
we need an asymptotic formula for the confluent hypergeometric function
valid when
|s|\rightarrow\infty
. Following the reasoning in [
[
6
]
,
p. 379], recall that the Whittaker function
M_{\lambda,\mu}(z)

has the asymptotic formula, [
[
19
]
, p.
341, eq. (13.21.1)]

M_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\frac{z^{1/4}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\lambda^{-\mu-\frac{1}{4}}\Gamma\left(2\mu+1\right)\,\cos\left(2\sqrt{\lambda z}-\frac{\pi}{4}-\mu\pi\right)+O\left(|\lambda|^{-\mu-\frac{3}{4}}\right)

(2.8)

as
|\lambda|\rightarrow\infty
and
z
such that
|\arg(\lambda z)|<2\pi
.
Furthermore, since

M_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=z^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-z/2}\,_{1}F_{1}\left(\mu-\lambda+\frac{1}{2};\,2\mu+1;\,z\right),

(2.9)

we see that, as
|t|\rightarrow\infty
and fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
the substitutions in (
2.8
) and (
2.9
)
give the bound

\left|{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{4}-it;\,\frac{\alpha}{2};\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right|=\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{\frac{z^{2}}{8}}\left\{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\sqrt{\frac{|z|}{2\pi}}\,|t|^{\frac{1-\alpha}{4}}\,\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{|t|}{2}}\,|z|\right)+O\left(|t|^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{4}}\right)\right\},\,\,\,\,|t|\rightarrow\infty.

(2.10)

This estimate is more than enough to justify most of the steps in
this paper. By Stirling’s formula and
(
2.7
), one can see that

\left|\eta_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{4}+it\right)\right|\ll_{\alpha}\,|t|^{A(\alpha)}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|},\,\,\,\,\,|t|\rightarrow\infty,

(2.11)

where
A(\alpha)=\sigma_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}
,
\sigma_{\alpha}

being given by (
2.5
).
When combined with (
2.10
), the convex estimate (
2.11
) yields the bound

\left|\tilde{F}_{z,\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{4}+it\right)\right|\leq\sum_{j\neq 0}\left|c_{j}\,\eta_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{4}+i\,(t+\lambda_{j})\right)\text{Re}\left(\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{4}-i\,(t+\lambda_{j});\,\frac{\alpha}{2};\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right)\right|\ll_{\alpha,z}C_{\lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|\,|t|^{B(\alpha)}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|+|z|\sqrt{|t|}},

(2.12)

where we have used the fact that
\left(\lambda_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

is a bounded sequence and
\left(c_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{1}
.
The term
C_{\lambda}
only stands for a positive constant which
depends on the bounds of the sequence
\left(\lambda_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Next, let us note that an explicit way of writing the first equality

1.2

\displaystyle 1+2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\,\left(\sqrt{\pi x}\,z\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{\alpha}(n)\,n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\,e^{-\pi n\,x}\,J_{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(\sqrt{\pi n\,x}\,z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{4}}}{x^{\alpha/2}}\left\{1+2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\,\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{x}}\,z\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{\alpha}(n)\,n^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi n}{x}}\,I_{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi n}{x}}\,z\right)\right\},

(2.13)

whenever
\text{Re}(x)>0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Since the summation
formula (
2.13
) transforms a generalized

\psi-
function involving the Bessel functions of the first kind,
it will be useful in our next argument to have a bound for the modified
Bessel function,
I_{\nu}(z)
,
\nu>-1
. In the previous paper [
[
24
]
,
p. 42] we have used the famous Hankel expansion for
I_{\nu}(z)
,

|z|\rightarrow\infty
. However, for our alternative argument we
need a bound for

that is somewhat uniform in
\nu

and

.

The following simple bound will be useful: first, let us note that,
if
\nu\geq 0
and

,

\Gamma(k+\nu+1)=\Gamma(\nu+1)(\nu+1)...(\nu+k)\geq k!\,\Gamma(\nu+1),

which gives, after the use of the power series of

|I_{\nu}(z)|\leq\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{\nu}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2k}}{2^{2k}k!\,\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}\leq\frac{(|z|/2)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}}\leq\frac{(|z|/2)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{k}}{2^{k}k!}\right\}^{2}=\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{\nu}\frac{e^{|z|}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)},\,\,\,\,\,\nu\geq 0.

(2.14)

If, on the other hand, we want to extend (
2.14
)
to
-1<\nu<0
, we begin to note that, for

,

\Gamma(k+\nu+1)=\Gamma(\nu+2)\,(\nu+2)...(\nu+k)\geq(k-1)!\,\Gamma(\nu+2).

Hence, if

,

|I_{\nu}(z)|\leq\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{\nu}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2k}}{2^{2k}k!\,\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}\leq\frac{\left(|z|/2\right)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}+\frac{\left(|z|/2\right)^{\nu+2}}{\Gamma(\nu+2)}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}}<\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{\nu}\frac{e^{|z|}}{\Gamma(\nu+2)}\left(1+\frac{|z|^{2}}{4}\right).

(2.15)

(2.16)

As stated above, the transformation formula for

,

2.13
), will play a major role in

[
24
]

(2.17)

from the Fourier expansion at the cusp

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

Proof.

\left[\frac{d^{k}}{du^{k}}\left(1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right]_{u=u_{0}}=\frac{k!}{2\pi i}\,\intop_{C_{\lambda u_{0}}(u_{0})}\,\frac{1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iw},z\right)}{(w-u_{0})^{k+1}}\,dw.

(2.26)

Now, let us bound trivially the integral above: to do it, it will

with

\displaystyle 1+\psi_{\alpha}(ie^{-2iw},z)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\left(\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-w\right)}z\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\,\frac{e^{-\frac{ie^{-iw}z^{2}}{8\sin(w)}}}{2e^{-iw}\sin(w)}

\displaystyle\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)e^{-\frac{\pi i}{2}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\,e^{-\frac{\pi}{2\tan(w)}\,\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}I_{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi(n+\frac{\alpha}{4})\,}e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}}z}{2\sin(w)}\right).

(2.27)

We will track each factor independently. Clearly, for any

\left|2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\,\left(\sqrt{\pi}\,e^{i\frac{\pi}{4}}e^{-iw}z\right)^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right|\leq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\pi^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{4}\left|1-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right|}|z|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}},

where we have noted that

. Next,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{e^{-\frac{ie^{-iw}z^{2}}{8\sin(w)}}}{2e^{-iw}\sin(w)}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}-\text{Im}(w)}\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}}{8}\text{Im}\left(\frac{1}{\tan(w)}\right)\right)}{2\sqrt{\sin^{2}\left(\text{Re}(w)\right)+\sinh^{2}\left(\text{Im}(w)\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}-\text{Im}(w)}\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{8}\cdot\frac{\sinh(2\text{Im}(w))}{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}\right)}{2\sqrt{\sin^{2}\left(\text{Re}(w)\right)+\sinh^{2}\left(\text{Im}(w)\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\lambda u_{0}}}{2\sin\left(\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{8}\cdot\frac{\sinh(2\text{Im}(w))}{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}\right),

where in the last step we just have used the fact that, for any
w\in C_{\lambda u_{0}}(u_{0})
,

|\text{Im}(w)|\leq\lambda u_{0}
. Moreover, we have used the explicit
expression

\frac{1}{\tan(w)}=\frac{\sin(2\text{Re}(w))-i\,\sinh(2\text{Im}(w))}{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}.

(2.28)

Using now the elementary Jordan inequality
\sin(x)>\frac{2x}{\pi}
,

0<x<\frac{\pi}{2}
, and the fact that
(1-\lambda)u_{0}\leq\text{Re}(w)\leq(1+\lambda)u_{0}<\frac{\pi}{2}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{e^{-\frac{ie^{-iw}z^{2}}{8\sin(w)}}}{2e^{-iw}\sin(w)}\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{\pi e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\lambda u_{0}}}{4\text{Re}(w)}\,\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{8}\cdot\frac{\sinh(2\text{Im}(w))}{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\lambda u_{0}}}{4(1-\lambda)u_{0}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{z^{2}}{8}\cdot\frac{\sinh(2\text{Im}(w))}{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}\right),\,\,\,\,w\in C_{\lambda u_{0}}(u_{0}).

(2.29)

We now address each term of the series on the right-hand side of (
2.27
).
Since we first aim at proving (
2.24
), we assume
here that
\alpha>2
, so that
\frac{\alpha}{2}-1>0
. Using the
first uniform inequality for
I_{\nu}(z)
, (
2.14
), we have that the term involving the Bessel function admits the bound

\displaystyle\left|I_{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi(n+\frac{\alpha}{4})\,}e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}}z}{2\sin(w)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{2^{\alpha-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)|\sin(w)|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}\,\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,|z|}{2\,|\sin(w)|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{2^{\alpha-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\sin^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,|z|}{\sqrt{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{\frac{3\alpha}{4}-\frac{3}{2}}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{2^{\frac{3\alpha}{2}-3}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\left(1-\lambda\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}u_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}\,\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,|z|}{\sqrt{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}}\right).

(2.30)

In the second inequality above we have used the fact that
|\sin(w)|\geq|\sin\left(\text{Re}(w)\right)|

and in the third inequality we have once more invoked Jordan’s inequality

\sin(x)>\frac{2x}{\pi}
,
0<x<\frac{\pi}{2}
, together with the
fact that
(1-\lambda)u_{0}\leq\text{Re}(w)\leq(1+\lambda)u_{0}<\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Returning to (
2.27
) and recalling once more (
2.28
), from (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) we deduce

\left|1+\psi_{\alpha}(ie^{-2iw},z)\right|<d_{\alpha}\,\frac{\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\lambda u_{0}}}{2^{\alpha}\left(1-\lambda\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)|\,\exp\left\{-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,P_{w}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}\right)\right\},

(2.31)

where
d_{\alpha}
is some constant depending on
\alpha
and
P_{w}\left(X\right)

is the real-valued polynomial

P_{w}(X):=X^{2}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{|z|}{\sin(2\text{Re}(w))}\,\sqrt{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,X+\frac{z^{2}}{4\pi}\,\frac{\sinh(2\text{Im}(w))}{\sin(2\text{Re}(w))}.

(2.32)

The inequality (
2.31
) is valid for any
w\in C_{\lambda u_{0}}(u_{0})
. Taking out the first term of the series on (
2.31
) and using the fact that
\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0):=2^{\alpha}
, we get from (
2.31
)

\displaystyle\left|1+\psi_{\alpha}(ie^{-2iw},z)\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{d_{\alpha}\,\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}+\frac{u_{0}}{2}}}{\left(1-\lambda\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,P_{w}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)}{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0)}\right|\exp\left[-Q_{w}(n)\right].

(2.33)

The exponent showing up in the infinite series is explicitly

Q_{w}(n):=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\left[P_{w}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}\right)-P_{w}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right],

where
P_{w}(X)
is the polynomial given by (
2.32
). We will show below that there is a choice of
\lambda
such that, for any
z
satisfying (
2.23
),
Q_{w}(n)>0,\,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
|\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)|<A_{\alpha}\,n^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
(see [
[
24
]
,
p. 38]), the infinite series that appears on the right-hand side of
(
2.33
) is convergent. The remaining part of our proof will be to find a uniform bound for it, with a constant depending on
\lambda
and
\alpha
.
We want to find an upper bound for

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)}{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0)}\right|\,\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\left\{n-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,|z|\frac{\sqrt{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}}{\sin(2\text{Re}(w))}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\}\right].

Since
(1-\lambda)u_{0}\leq\text{Re}(w)\leq(1+\lambda)u_{0}
, we
know from Jordan’s inequality

\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))\geq 1-\cos\left(2(1-\lambda)u_{0}\right)=\intop_{0}^{2(1-\lambda)u_{0}}\sin(t)\,dt>\frac{2}{\pi}\,\intop_{0}^{2(1-\lambda)u_{0}}t\,dt=\frac{4(1-\lambda)^{2}}{\pi}u_{0}^{2},

which implies

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}}<\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2(1-\lambda)u_{0}}.

(2.34)

On the other hand, we can find the upper bound

\displaystyle\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))

\displaystyle=\intop_{0}^{2\text{Im}(w)}\sinh(t)\,dt+\intop_{0}^{2\text{Re}(w)}\sin(t)\,dt\leq\intop_{0}^{2\text{Im}(w)}t\,e^{\frac{t^{2}}{6}}dt+\intop_{0}^{2\text{Re}(w)}t\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\text{Im}(w)^{2}\,e^{\frac{2}{3}\text{Im}(w)^{2}}+2\text{Re}(w)^{2}<4e^{\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{3}u_{0}^{2}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}u_{0}^{2},

(2.35)

where we have used the fact that
(1-\lambda)u_{0}\leq\text{Re}(w)\leq(1+\lambda)u_{0}
,

-\lambda u_{0}\leq\text{Im}(w)\leq\lambda u_{0}
. We have also used
the known inequality

\sinh(x)\leq x\,e^{\frac{x^{2}}{6}},\,\,\,\,x>0.

(2.36)

Since
(1-\lambda)u_{0}\leq\text{Re}(w)\leq(1+\lambda)u_{0}<\frac{\pi}{2}
,
another application of Jordan’s inequality gives

\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)>\begin{cases}\frac{4}{\pi}\text{Re}(w),&0<\text{Re}(w)<\frac{\pi}{4}\\
2-\frac{4}{\pi}\text{Re}(w),&\frac{\pi}{4}\leq\text{Re}(w)<\frac{\pi}{2}\end{cases}\,\,\geq\frac{4}{\pi}\left(1-\lambda\right)u_{0}.

(2.37)

Thus, combining (
2.35
) with (
2.37
) and recalling that
0<u_{0}<\frac{\pi}{4}
, we get the inequality

\frac{\sin(2\text{Re}(w))}{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}>\frac{\left(1-\lambda\right)}{\pi e^{\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{3}u_{0}^{2}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}u_{0}}>\frac{\left(1-\lambda\right)}{\pi e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}u_{0}}.

(2.38)

Thus, the infinite series on the right-hand side of (
2.33
) admits the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)}{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0)}\right|\,

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\left\{n-\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,|z|\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)}{\sin(2\text{Re}(w))}\sqrt{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle<\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)}{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0)}\right|\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{u_{0}}\left\{\frac{n(1-\lambda)}{2e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi\,|z|}{2\sqrt{2}(1-\lambda)}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\}\right].

Using the condition (
2.23
), one sees that the exponent in the previous expression is bounded by

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{u_{0}}\left\{\frac{n(1-\lambda)}{2e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi\,|z|}{2\sqrt{2}(1-\lambda)}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[-\frac{1}{u_{0}}\left\{\frac{n(1-\lambda)}{2e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\alpha}}{24(1-\lambda)}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[-\frac{n}{u_{0}}\left\{\frac{1-\lambda}{2e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{24(1-\lambda)}\right\}\right],

where in the last inequality we have used the mean value theorem for
the function
f(x)=\sqrt{x+\frac{\alpha}{4}}
. If we select the value
\lambda=0.01
, we have that

\frac{1-\lambda}{2e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{24(1-\lambda)}>\frac{1}{4},

and so, since
0<u_{0}<\frac{\pi}{4}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)}{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0)}\right|\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{u_{0}}\left\{\frac{n(1-\lambda)}{2e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi\,|z|}{2\sqrt{2}(1-\lambda)}\left(\sqrt{n+\frac{\alpha}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(n)}{\tilde{r}_{\alpha}(0)}\right|\,\exp\left[-\frac{n}{4u_{0}}\right]<\frac{A_{\alpha}}{2^{\alpha}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{n}{\pi}\right]\leq M_{\alpha}.

(2.39)

Combining (
2.39
) with (
2.33
) and using the fact that
0<u_{0}<\frac{\pi}{4}
, the following bound holds (note that we are now selecting
\lambda
as equal to
0.01
)

\left|1+\psi_{\alpha}(ie^{-2iw},z)\right|<\frac{d_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}e^{\frac{\pi}{8}}\,\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}}}{(2u_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,P_{w}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right],

(2.40)

which is almost (
2.24
) with
k=0
. To conclude
the proof in this direction, we just need to bound uniformly the term

\exp\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,P_{w}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right],\,\,\,\,\,\,w\in C_{\lambda u_{0}}(u_{0})

under the hypothesis on
z
(
2.23
) and the
choice
\lambda=0.01
. This can be done by appealing to (
2.36
),
(
2.34
) and (
2.38
),
which give

\displaystyle\exp

\displaystyle\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sin\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}{\cosh\left(2\text{Im}(w)\right)-\cos\left(2\text{Re}(w)\right)}\,P_{w}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right]<\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha}{8}\,\frac{\left(1-\lambda\right)}{e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}u_{0}}+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\,\frac{\pi\,|z|}{4(1-\lambda)u_{0}}+\frac{\pi z^{2}\lambda e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}}{16(1-\lambda)^{2}u_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left[-\frac{\alpha}{u_{0}}\left\{\frac{1-\lambda}{8e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{48(1-\lambda)}-\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}}{1152(1-\lambda)^{2}}\right\}\right].

(2.41)

However, for
\lambda=0.01
, a numerical confirmation gives

\frac{1-\lambda}{8e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}\left(1+\lambda\right)^{2}}-\frac{\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{48(1-\lambda)}-\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda e^{\frac{\pi^{2}\lambda^{2}}{24}}}{1152(1-\lambda)^{2}}>0.004,

and so, returning to (
2.40
), we get from the previous inequality that

\left|1+\psi_{\alpha}(ie^{-2iw},z)\right|<\frac{d_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}e^{\frac{\pi}{8}}\,\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}e^{-\frac{z^{2}}{8}}}{(2u_{0})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{0.04\,\alpha}{u_{0}}\right]=\frac{C}{u_{0}^{\alpha/2}}\,e^{-\frac{A}{u_{0}}}

(2.42)

where
C
and
A:=0.04\,\alpha
only depend on
\alpha
. This proves
(
2.24
) for
k=0
. For the remaining cases, let
us invoke Cauchy’s integral formula (
2.26
)
and use (
2.42
) to get

\left|\left[\frac{d^{k}}{du^{k}}\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right]_{u=u_{0}}\right|\leq\frac{k!}{2\pi\left(\lambda u_{0}\right)^{k+1}}\,\intop_{C_{\lambda u_{0}}(u_{0})}\,|1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iw},z\right)|\,|dw|<C\,\frac{k!}{u_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+k}\lambda^{k}}e^{-\frac{A}{u_{0}}}.

(2.43)

Finally, under the choice
\lambda=0.01
, we know
\frac{1}{\lambda}<2^{7}
,
and so we derive our estimate (
2.24
) in its final
form.

To get the second formula (
2.25
),
we use the bound (
2.15
) for
I_{\nu}(z)
,
-1<\nu<0
, and we invoke the same kind of inequalities as in (
2.30
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left|I_{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi(n+\frac{\alpha}{4})\,}e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}}z}{2\sin(w)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}{2^{\alpha-2}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+1\right)|\sin(w)|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-1}}\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,|z|}{\sqrt{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\pi^{3}(n+\frac{\alpha}{4})|z|^{2}}{64(1-\lambda)^{2}u_{0}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{A}\,\frac{\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}}{2^{\frac{3\alpha}{2}-3}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+1\right)\left(1-\lambda\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}u_{0}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}}\exp\left(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,|z|}{\sqrt{\cosh(2\text{Im}(w))-\cos(2\text{Re}(w))}}\right),

(2.44)

for some absolutely large constant
\mathcal{A}
. There is no formal
difference between (
2.44
) and (
2.30
),
the only exception being in the power of
1/u_{0}
. Thus, the same
argument carries through with an extra factor of
1/u_{0}^{2}
. This
gives (
2.25
) and finishes the
proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Of course, the condition (
1.22
) may be
improved if we take a more careful choice of the parameter
\lambda

in the previous proof. Moreover, when
\nu>-\frac{1}{2}
, the uniform
bounds (
2.14
) and (
2.15
)
can be replaced by the better inequality,

\left|I_{\nu}(z)\right|\leq\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{\nu}\frac{e^{|\text{Re}(z)|}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)},\,\,\,\,\,\nu>-\frac{1}{2},

(2.45)

which can be obtained via the integral representation [
[
19
]
,
p. 252, eq. (10.32.2)]

I_{\nu}\left(z\right)=\frac{\left(z/2\right)^{\nu}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,\intop_{-1}^{1}\left(1-t^{2}\right)^{\nu-\frac{1}{2}}e^{zt}\,dt,\,\,\,\,\,\nu>-\frac{1}{2}.

Using (
2.45
) instead
of (
2.14
) in the proof above, one may enlarge
the interval
\left[-\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}},\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}}\right]

in the condition (
2.23
) to
\left[-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}},\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}}\right]

when
\alpha\geq 1
. Therefore, there are some cases where technical
improvements lead to stronger results than those stated in our Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

We now give another lemma that will be crucial in the proof of our
result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
h:\,\mathbb{C}\longmapsto\mathbb{C}
be an analytic function.
If
z\in\left[-\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}},\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}}\right]

then, for every
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the following relation holds

\frac{d^{2p}}{du^{2p}}\left\{h(u)\,\left(e^{-z^{2}/8}+e^{z^{2}/8}\,\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right\}=-2\sinh\left(\frac{z^{2}}{8}\right)h^{(2p)}(u)+\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,p}(u,z),

(2.46)

where, for any
0<u<\frac{\pi}{4}
,
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,p}(u,z)

satisfies the inequalities

|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,p}(u,z)|<D\frac{2^{14p}(2p)!e^{-\frac{A}{u}}}{u^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+2p}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{1}(0))},\,\,\,\,\alpha>2,

(2.47)

|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,p}(u,z)|<D\frac{2^{14p}(2p)!e^{-\frac{A}{u}}}{u^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+2+2p}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{1}(0))},\,\,\,\,0\leq\alpha\leq 2,

(2.48)

with
A
and
D
depending only on
\alpha
.

Proof.

First observe that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2p}}{du^{2p}}\left\{h(u)\,\left(e^{-z^{2}/8}+e^{z^{2}/8}\,\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=-2\sinh\left(\frac{z^{2}}{8}\right)h^{(2p)}(u)+e^{\frac{z^{2}}{8}}\,\frac{d^{2p}}{du^{2p}}\,\left\{h(u)\,\left(1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle:=-2\sinh\left(\frac{z^{2}}{8}\right)h^{(2p)}(u)+\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,p}(u,z).

(2.49)

By the well-known Cauchy’s estimates for the coefficients
of analytic functions and (
2.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|e^{\frac{z^{2}}{8}}\,\frac{d^{2p}}{du^{2p}}\,\left\{h(u)\,\left(1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{\pi\alpha}{576}}\,\sum_{k=0}^{2p}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2p\\
k\end{array}\right)\,\left|h^{(2p-k)}(u)\right|\,\left|\frac{d^{k}}{du^{k}}\left(1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{\pi\alpha}{576}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(D(0,1))}\,\sum_{k=0}^{2p}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2p\\
k\end{array}\right)(2p-k)!\,\left|\frac{d^{k}}{du^{k}}\left(1+\psi_{\alpha}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle<Ce^{\frac{\pi\alpha}{576}}\,\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(D(0,1))}\frac{(2p)!e^{-\frac{A}{u}}}{u^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{2p}\frac{2^{7k}}{u^{k}}

\displaystyle<C^{\prime}\,\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{1}(0))}\frac{(2p)!e^{-\frac{A}{u}}}{u^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+2p}}\,\frac{(128)^{2p+1}-u^{2p+1}}{128-u}

\displaystyle<C^{\prime\prime}\,\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{1}(0))}\frac{2^{14p}(2p)!e^{-\frac{A}{u}}}{u^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+2p}}

where we have invoked the maximum modulus principle. This completes the proof
of (
2.47
). The proof of (
2.48
) is analogous.
∎

To conclude this section, let us recall the useful notation introduced
in
[
8
]
. From now on, it will be convenient to write the terms of
the sequence
\left(\lambda_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in polar
coordinates

\frac{i\alpha}{2}-2\lambda_{j}:=r_{j}\,e^{i\theta_{j}},\,\,\,0<\theta_{j}<\pi.

(2.50)

According to our assumption

b_{2\ell}:=\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<...<r_{\ell}}\,\frac{1}{r_{1}^{4}\cdot...\cdot r_{\ell}^{4}}=\frac{2^{2\ell+1}\pi^{4\ell}}{(4\ell+2)!}\implies a_{2\ell}\leq\mathscr{B}\,\frac{2^{2\ell+1}\pi^{4\ell}}{h^{2\ell}(4\ell+2)!}.

(5.7)

Our proof will be concluded by seeing that (

)
contradicts (

) and the bounds found
in Lemma

above. Recall that, under the assumption

has the same
odd zeros as this function. Hence, by this construction,
i^{-k/2}\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\,\varphi_{f,z}(t)

must be a real and even function with constant sign for any

.
Considering the continuous

will now show that this cannot be true if the bound (

)
takes place.

If we use the power series (
5.2
) for

\varphi_{f,z}(t)
, we see that

can be written as an
infinite series of the form

\displaystyle Q_{f}(u)

\displaystyle=\intop_{0}^{\infty}i^{-\frac{k}{2}}\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\,\varphi_{f,z}(t)\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}a_{2\ell}\,\intop_{0}^{\infty}i^{-\frac{k}{2}}\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\,t^{2\ell}\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt.

\displaystyle\intop_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}|a_{2\ell}|t^{2\ell}\,\left|\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\right|\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)

\displaystyle\,dt\leq\mathscr{B}\,\intop_{0}^{\infty}\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2\ell+1}\pi^{4\ell}}{h^{2\ell}(4\ell+2)!}t^{2\ell}\,\left|\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\right|\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathscr{B}\,\intop_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\,\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2t}{h}}\right)^{\ell}\left|\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\right|\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle<2\mathscr{B}\,\intop_{0}^{\infty}\left|\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\right|\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2t}{h}}+\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\ll_{k,z,\Lambda}\,\intop_{0}^{\infty}|t|^{B(k)}\,\exp\left(-2ut+\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2}{h}}+|z|\right)\sqrt{t}\right)\,dt<\infty,

where in the last step we have used the estimate (
4.1
).
Having assured that we can perform the operation (
5.8
),
it now follows from Lemma

above that

\displaystyle Q_{f}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}a_{2\ell}\,\intop_{0}^{\infty}i^{-k/2}\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\,t^{2\ell}\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}a_{2\ell}\,\frac{2\pi}{2^{2\ell}}\,e^{\frac{z^{2}}{4}}\,\text{Re}\,\left(i^{-\frac{k}{2}}\frac{d^{2\ell}}{du^{2\ell}}\left\{\sum_{j\neq 0}c_{j}\,e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-u\right)k-\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)\lambda_{j}}\,\psi_{f}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right\}\right).

(5.9)

Using Lemma
4.3
together with the estimate for
a_{2\ell}
(
5.7
),
we can bound uniformly the previous series with respect to

. Indeed,
since the function

g(u)=e^{i\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-u\right)k}\,\sum_{j\neq 0}c_{j}\,e^{-\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)\lambda_{j}}

is analytic and
\left\|g(u)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(C_{0}(1))}\leq\mathcal{M}

with

only depending on the sequence
\sum_{j\neq 0}|c_{j}|

(see (

(

\left|\frac{d^{2\ell}}{du^{2\ell}}\left\{g(u)\,\psi_{f}\left(ie^{-2iu},z\right)\right\}\right|<\mathcal{D}\frac{2^{14\ell}(2\ell)!e^{-\frac{A}{u}}}{u^{k+2\ell}},

(5.10)

for some constant

depending on the weight of the cusp

\left(c_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and

\left(\lambda_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

Thus, returning to (

5.7

\displaystyle|Q_{f}(u)|

\displaystyle<2\pi\mathcal{D}e^{\frac{z^{2}}{4}}\frac{e^{-A/u}}{u^{k}}\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}|a_{2\ell}|\frac{2^{12\ell}(2\ell)!}{u^{2\ell}}\leq 2\pi\mathscr{C}e^{\frac{z^{2}}{4}}\frac{e^{-A/u}}{u^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{2^{14\ell+1}\pi^{4\ell}(2\ell)!}{h^{2\ell}(4\ell+2)!}\cdot\frac{1}{u^{2\ell}}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{C}\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{\frac{z^{2}}{4}}\frac{e^{-A/u}}{u^{k}}\,\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{10\ell}\pi^{4\ell}}{\Gamma\left(2\ell+\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(2\ell+1\right)\left(hu\right)^{2\ell}}<\mathscr{C}\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{\frac{z^{2}}{4}}\frac{e^{-A/u}}{u^{k}}\,\cosh\left(\frac{32\pi^{2}}{hu}\right)

). From

\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left|Q_{f}(u)\right|<\mathscr{C}\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{\pi/36}\,\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}u^{-k}\,\exp\left(-\left(A-\frac{32\pi^{2}}{h}\right)\frac{1}{u}\right)=0

which contradicts the fact that

is positive decreasing.
Consequently, we have found

such that

for infinitely many values of

. This shows (

)
and so we complete the proof of our Theorem. Finally, we note that we can replace the value of

in (

(calculated in the proof of Lemma

In order to consider the case where

, the argument
is the same with a small modification. Instead of considering

we will study the similar function,

P_{f}(u):=\intop_{0}^{\infty}i^{-\frac{k}{2}}\,t\,\tilde{G}_{z,f}\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)\varphi_{f,z}(t)\,\cosh\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-2u\right)t\right)\,dt,\,\,\,\,0<u<\frac{\pi}{4},

\blacksquare

6
Concluding Remarks

In this paper we have presented estimates for the number of critical
zeros of the arbitrary shifted combinations

\tilde{F}_{z,\alpha}(s):=\sum_{j\neq 0}c_{j}\,\eta_{\alpha}\left(s+i\lambda_{j}\right)\,\left\{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-s-i\lambda_{j};\,\frac{\alpha}{2};\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)+\,_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\overline{s}+i\lambda_{j};\,\frac{\alpha}{2};\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right\},

(6.1)

\tilde{G}_{z,f}(s):=\sum_{j\neq 0}c_{j}\,\eta_{f}\left(s+i\lambda_{j}\right)\,\left\{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(k-s-i\lambda_{j};\,k;\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)+\,_{1}F_{1}\left(k-\overline{s}+i\lambda_{j};\,k;\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}.

(6.2)

The proofs of both estimates relied on the summation formulas (
2.21
)
and (
4.2
). One may wonder how
to extend our results in different directions. For example, one may
ask if we can relax the class of hypergeometric functions appearing
in the combinations (
6.1
) and (
6.2
).
Note that the second parameter of the hypergeometric functions above
depends on the Dirichlet series that preceeds them: in the first case,
it is equal to
\alpha/2
and in the second is
k
. Therefore, one
may consider the study of the critical zeros of the functions,

\tilde{F}_{z,\alpha}(s;\nu):=\sum_{j\neq 0}c_{j}\,\eta_{\alpha}\left(s+i\lambda_{j}\right)\,\left\{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-s-i\lambda_{j};\,\nu+1;\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)+\,_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\overline{s}+i\lambda_{j};\,\nu+1;\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right\},

(6.3)

\tilde{G}_{z,f}(s;\nu):=\sum_{j\neq 0}c_{j}\,\eta_{f}\left(s+i\lambda_{j}\right)\,\left\{{}_{1}F_{1}\left(k-s-i\lambda_{j};\,\nu+1;\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)+\,_{1}F_{1}\left(k-\overline{s}+i\lambda_{j};\,\nu+1;\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right\},

(6.4)

where
\nu
is now a real parameter independent of
\alpha
(in
the first case) and of
k
in the second.
We remark that it is indeed possible to get analogues of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to the shifted combinations (
6.3
)
and (
6.4

\nu\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}-1

in the first case and
\nu\geq k-1
in the second. However, instead
of using the summation formulas (
2.21
)
and (
4.2
), we will need more
general transformations. Indeed, it is not difficult to show

Another direction that this work can take concerns even more general
shifted combinations of completed Dirichlet series. To continue, recall
again that
\eta(s):=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta(s)
. In the paper

[

if one considers a combination of shifted products of the form

H_{z}(s):=\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}c_{m,n}\,\eta\left(s+i\lambda_{m}\right)\,\eta\left(s+i\lambda_{n}\right)\,\text{Re}\left({}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-s-i\lambda_{m}}{2};\,\frac{1}{2};\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right)\,\text{Re}\left({}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-s-i\lambda_{n}}{2};\,\frac{1}{2};\,\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right)\right),

\text{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}

\lambda_{m}=m,\,\,\,1\leq m\leq M

and assume that

, then
due to the non-negativity of

, we have that

\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i(\tau+m)\right)=0,\,\,\,\,\,\text{for }1\leq m\leq M.

(6.10)

But this is impossible for
N
large enough because every arithmetical
sequence contains infinitely many elements which are not zeros of

\zeta(z)
.

\lambda_{m}:=\frac{2\pi m}{\log(2)}

1
Main results

Let
f:Y\to X
be a morphism between smooth complex projective varieties, and let
Z
be the closure of
f(Y)
. By the work of Deligne
[
Del74
, Proposition 8.2.7]
, the kernel of the map from
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
to

H^{*}(Y,\mathbb{C})
induced by
f
is the same as that of
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{*}(Z,\mathbb{C})
. In this paper, we will study this phenomenon in the context of non-abelian Hodge theories.
Our first theorem is as follows.

Theorem A

(=
Theorems

3.4
and
3.9
)
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a morphism between smooth quasi-projective varieties and
Z
be the closure of the image
f(Y)
. Let
\iota:Z\hookrightarrow X
be the natural inclusion. Let
K
be any field and let
k\subset K
be the minimal field contained in
K
, which is
\mathbb{Q}
if
{\rm char}\,K=0
and is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
if
{\rm char}\,K=p>0
. Then there exists finitely many semisimple representations
\{\tau_{i}:\pi_{1}(Z)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\overline{k})\}_{i=1,\ldots,\ell}
with
\tau_{i}(\pi_{1}(Z))
being finite groups such that

(i)

if
{\rm char}\,K=0
and
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
is a semisimple representation with
f^{*}\varrho=1
, then
\iota^{*}\varrho:\pi_{1}(Z)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
is conjugate to some
\tau_{i}:\pi_{1}(Z)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. In particular,
\iota^{*}\varrho(\pi_{1}(Z))
is a finite group.

(ii)

if
{\rm char}\,K=p>0
and
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
is a linear representation such that the semisimplification of
f^{*}\varrho
is trivial, then the semisimplification of
\iota^{*}\varrho:\pi_{1}(Z)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
is conjugate to some
\tau_{i}:\pi_{1}(Z)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, and
\iota^{*}\varrho(\pi_{1}(Z))
is a finite group.

We remark that the above theorem generalizes
[
LR96
, Theorem 1.1]
to the quasi-projective cases. Moreover, we provide a more explicit description of the finite quotient group factoring through in
[
LR96
]
even when
X
is projective.

It is noteworthy that Deligne’s theorem also has some analogue in terms of morphisms between character varieties. Let
X
be a complex quasi-projective variety. The
{\rm GL}_{N}
-character variety of the topological fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
in characteristic zero, denoted by
M_{\rm B}(X,N)
, is an affine
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme of finite type such that its
\mathbb{C}
-points
M_{\rm B}(X,N)(\mathbb{C})
is identified with the conjugate classes of semisimple representations
\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. In the case of characteristic
p>0
, there exists also the corresponding character variety
M_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, which is an affine
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme of finite type such that for any algebraically closed field
K
with
{\rm char}\,K=p
, the
K
-points
M_{\rm B}(X,N)(K)
is identified with the conjugate classes of semisimple representations
\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(K)
. If
f:Y\to X
is a morphism between smooth complex quasi-projective varieties,
it induces morphisms
j_{Y}:M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)\to M_{\mathrm{B}}(Y,N)
and
j_{Y}:M_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\to M_{\rm B}(Y,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.
Our second result is as follows.

Theorem B

(=
theorems

4.1
and
4.3
)
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a morphism between smooth quasi-projective varieties, and
Z
be the closure of
f(Y)
. If
{\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(Z)\to\pi_{1}(X)]
is a finite index subgroup of
\pi_{1}(X)
, then both

and
j_{Y}:M_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\to M_{\rm B}(Y,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
are quasi-finite.

In characteristic zero, this result generalizes
[
Las95
, Theorem 6.1]
to the quasi-projective case. The case for a positive characteristic is new even in the projective setting. We remark that our proof is quite different and much simpler than that in
[

]
(see

[

,
CDY22
,
DYK23
,

]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In

[

]
. In
Section

in characteristic

Section

is devoted to the proof of

2
Technical preliminaries

2.1
Character varieties

In this section, we briefly recall the definition and properties of the character varieties in any characteristic. For more details and proof of these results, we refer the readers to
[
Ses77
,
LM85
]
.

Assume that
X
is a complex quasi-projective variety and
\pi_{1}(X)
is its topological fundamental group. Even though we can construct a variety of representations and so on for any finitely generated group, we restrict to the fundamental groups of algebraic varieties which is enough for our purpose.

Let
N
be a fixed positive integer. The homomorphisms from
\pi_{1}(X)
into
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
is represented by an affine scheme
R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}
of finite type defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. For any commutative ring
A
, we have
R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}(A)={\rm Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),{\rm GL}_{N}(A))
. Note that
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
acts on
R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}
by the conjugation and such an action is algebraic. Let
\pi:R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}\to M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}
be the GIT quotient. Note that
\pi
is a surjective morphism between affine schemes of finite type defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. We call
M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}
the
character variety
of
\pi_{1}(X)
into
{\rm GL}_{N}
.

Now assume that
A
is a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero. Consider
M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N):=M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}\times_{{\rm Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}{\rm Spec}\,\mathbb{Q}
, which is an affine
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme of finite type. For
\varrho\in R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{K})
, we shall denote its image in
M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)(\mathbb{K})
by
[\varrho]:=\pi(\varrho)
. Notice that for an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero, the points of
M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)(\mathbb{K})
parametrize conjugation classes of semisimple representations
\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
. For any
\varrho\in R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)(\mathbb{K})
, we denote its semi-simplification as
\varrho^{ss}
. Then two linear representations
\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\bar{\mathbb{K}})
satisfies
[\varrho_{1}]=[\varrho_{2}]
if and only if
\varrho_{1}^{ss}\sim\varrho_{2}^{ss}
, where
\sim
means that they differ by a conjugation by an element in
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
.

The case of character variety in the positive characteristic is similar. Let
p
be a prime number and write
R_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}:=R_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{Z}}\times_{{\rm Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}{\rm Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Note that the general linear group
{\rm GL}_{N,\mathbb{F}_{p}}:={\rm GL}_{N}\times_{{\rm Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}{\rm Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_{p}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
acts on
R_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
by conjugation. Let
M_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
be the GIT quotient of
R_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
by
{\rm GL}_{N,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
. Then
M_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is an affine
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme of finite type. For any algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
p
, the
\mathbb{K}
-points
M_{\rm B}(X,N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\mathbb{K})
are identified with the conjugacy classes of semisimple representations
\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
.

2.2
Bounded representations

We first recall the following definition of bounded subsets of affine schemes over a non-archimedean local field (see
[
DYK23
, Definitions 3.3]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a non-archimedean local field.

(i)

Let
X
be an affine
\mathbb{K}
-scheme of finite type. A subset
B\subset X(\mathbb{K})
is
bounded
if for every
f\in\mathbb{K}[X]
, the set
\{\nu(f(b))\mid b\in B\}
is bounded below, where
\nu:\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{R}
is the non-archimedean valuation of
\mathbb{K}
.

(ii)

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated group. A representation
\varrho:\Gamma\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
is
bounded
if its image
\varrho(\Gamma)
is bounded.

Bounded subsets and compact subsets are closely related as follows (see
[
KP23
, Fact 2.2.3]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a non-archimedean local field and
X
be an affine
\mathbb{K}
-scheme of finite type. A closed subset
B
is bounded if and only if
B
is compact with respect to the analytic topology of
X(\mathbb{K})
. If
f:X\to Y
is a morphism of affine
\mathbb{K}
-schemes of finite type, then
f
carries bounded subsets of
X(\mathbb{K})
to bounded subsets in
Y(\mathbb{K})
. ∎

The following property of bounded representations is essential for our proof.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
DYK23
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a non-archimedean local field. Let
x\in M_{\rm B}(X,N)(\mathbb{K})
. If
\{\varrho_{i}:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\bar{\mathbb{K}})\}_{i=1,2}
are two linear representations such that
[\varrho_{1}]=[\varrho_{2}]=x\in M_{\rm B}(X,N)(\bar{\mathbb{K}})
, then
\varrho_{1}
is bounded if and only if
\varrho_{2}
is bounded. ∎

In the case of characteristic zero, we need the following lemma due to Yamanoi.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Yam10
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic zero. Let
R_{0}\subset R_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)(\mathbb{K})
be the subset whose points are bounded representations. Let
M_{0}\subset M_{\mathrm{B}}(X,N)(\mathbb{K})
be the image of
R_{0}
under the natural projection. Then
M_{0}
is compact with respect to the analytic topology. ∎

2.3
A reduction theorem

Based on the previous work
[
BDDM22
]
on the extension of Gromov-Schoen theory
[
GS92
]
to quasi-projective varieties, Cadorel, Yamanoi, and the first author
[

]
established the following theorem for the representation of fundamental groups of quasi-projective varieties into algebraic groups defined over non-archimedean local fields.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
CDY22
, Theorem 0.10]
)
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth quasi-projective variety, and let
\varrho:\pi_{1}(X)\to{\rm GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
be a reductive representation where
\mathbb{K}
is a non-archimedean local field. Then for any connected Zariski closed subset

(a)

the image
\varrho({\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(T)\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is a bounded subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
.

(b)

For every irreducible component
T_{o}
of
T
, the image
\varrho({\rm Im}[\pi_{1}(T_{o}^{\rm norm})\to\pi_{1}(X)])
is a bounded subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{K})
. Here
T_{o}^{\rm norm}
means the normalization of
T_{o}
.∎

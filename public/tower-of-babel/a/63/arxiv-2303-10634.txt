Uniqueness criteria for the Vlasov–Poisson system and applications to semiclassical analysis
Laurent Lafleche, Chiara Saffirio

Abstract
. We review some uniqueness criteria for the Vlasov–Poisson system, emerging as corollaries of stability estimates in strong or weak topologies, and show how they serve as a guideline to solve problems arising in semiclassical analysis. Different topologies allow to treat different classes of quantum states.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
81Q20, 35Q55, 35Q83 (82C10, 82C05).

1.
Introduction

We consider the Vlasov–Poisson equation

(1)

\partial_{t}f+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E_{f}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f=0,

where
f=f(t,x,\xi)
is a real-valued non-negative time-dependent function defined on the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{6}
,
E_{f}(t,x)=(\nabla K*\rho_{f})(t,x)
is a self-induced force field generated by the spatial density
\rho_{f}(t,x)=\int f(t,x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
. To simplify the presentation, in the rest of the paper we will focus on
d=3
and

K(x)=\frac{\pm 1}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}.

In this setting, the well-posedness theory for the Cauchy problem associated with Equation (
1
) has been established by Pfaffelmoser
[
19
]
and Lions and Perthame
[
16
]
, respectively proving global existence and uniqueness of classical solutions with compactly supported initial data, and existence of global weak solutions assuming that the initial data
f^{\mathrm{in}}
are such that
f^{\mathrm{in}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
and
\int\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{m}f^{\mathrm{in}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
is finite for some
m>3
. A sufficient condition for uniqueness of measure-valued solutions with spatial density satisfying
\rho_{f}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
was proven by Loeper in
[
17
]
, and more recently generalized by Miot
[
18
]
including spatial densities such that
\sup_{p\geq 1}\frac{1}{p}\left\lVert\rho_{f}\right\rVert_{L^{p}}
is finite, uniformly in time, and by Holding and Miot
[
5
]
considering spatial densities in some Orlicz space.

The above mentioned uniqueness criteria deal with assumptions at positive time on the spatial density
\rho_{f}
. In this paper we collect three stability estimates for the Vlasov–Poisson equation leading to uniqueness criteria (non optimal in terms of conditions on
\rho_{f}
) in
weak-strong
sense. By weak-strong stability we indicate that some regularity conditions are assumed only on one of the two solutions involved in the stability estimate, thus allowing the other solution to satisfy only the minimal existence requirements. We will show how such a weak-strong form in our estimates (cf. Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
) can be used to deduce results in the context of the semiclassical limit from the Hartree equation (Equation (
5
) below) towards the Vlasov–Poisson system (
1
). We will also look at the weak topology induced by the 2-Wasserstein distance (cf. Section
4
) and show how one can get a semiclassical estimate as an application of a stability result à la Loeper. The weak topology allows to treat a class of quantum states that were not included in the analysis with the weak-strong method and that are relevant at zero temperature (so-called pure states), however it does not provide a weak-strong estimate. More precisely, we will review the results in
[
13
,
2
,
9
]
and
[
9
,
10
]
, giving a unified picture of the methods.

1.1.
Notations

For functions on the phase space of the form
f=f(x,\xi)
, we use the shorthand notation
L^{p}_{x}L^{q,r}_{\xi}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},L^{q,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, where
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
L^{q,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denote respectively the Lebesgue and Lorentz spaces. We also use the notation
\mathscr{P}=\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
for the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{6}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{6}
be a multi-index with
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=\sum_{i=1}^{6}\alpha_{i}
, and
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x_{1}}\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}\dots\partial_{x_{3}}^{\alpha_{5}}\partial_{\xi_{3}}^{\alpha_{6}}
, then the weighted Sobolev norm in the Sobolev space
W^{k,p}_{n}
can be defined by the formula

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{k,p}_{n}}=\Big(\sum_{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\leq k}\left\lVert\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{n}\partial^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with
\left\langle z\right\rangle=\sqrt{1+\left\lvert z\right\rvert^{2}}
, with the usual associated notations
H_{n}^{k}=W_{n}^{k,2}
and
L^{p}_{n}=W^{0,p}_{n}
.

Using the correspondence of classical and quantum mechanics and using the symbol
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
and
\left[{\cdot,\cdot}\right]
to denote respectively the Poisson brackets and the commutator, we recall that the quantum analogue of

\nabla_{x}f=\left\{-\xi,f\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{\xi}f=\left\{x,f\right\}

are respectively

\quad\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\boldsymbol{\rho}:=\left[{\nabla,\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right]\ \qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}:=\left[{\frac{x}{i\hbar},\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right],

where
\hbar=h/(2\pi)
is the reduced Plank constant.

The quantum analogue of Lebesgue norms and weighted Sobolev norms of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
, a bounded operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, are the quantum Lebesgue and Sobolev norms

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}

\displaystyle=h^{\frac{3}{p}}\tr\!\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{W}^{k,p}_{n}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathsf{m}\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\lVert\mathsf{m}\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\left\lVert\mathsf{m}\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}

with
\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rvert=\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\rho}^{*}\boldsymbol{\rho}}
and
\mathsf{m}:=1+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{n}
, with
\boldsymbol{p}=-i\hbar\nabla
. When
p=\infty
,
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}
denotes the operator norm.

To connect functions on the phase space and operators acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we define the Weyl quantization as follows: to every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
, the Weyl quantization of
f
is the operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)
with kernel

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-2i\pi(y-x)\cdot\xi}\,f\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2},h\xi\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

Observe that, if
f
is a probability density on the phase space (hence nonnegative), its Weyl quantization is not necessarily a non-negative operator. For this reason it is convenient to introduce an alternative quantization of the function
f
, called the Töplitz quantization, or the Wick quantization, or the Husimi quantization, given by

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)=\frac{1}{h^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(z)\ket{\psi_{z}}\bra{\psi_{z}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z,

where

\psi_{z}(y):=\left(\pi\hbar\right)^{-3/4}e^{-\left\lvert y-x\right\rvert^{2}/(2\hbar)}\,e^{iy\cdot\xi/\hbar}.

It follows from this formula that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)
is a positive operator whenever
f\geq 0
.

One can also define the inverse transformation of the Weyl quantization. Namely, let
\boldsymbol{\rho}
be an operator with a regular kernel
\boldsymbol{\rho}(x,y)
, then

f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-2\pi\,i\,\xi\cdot y/h}\boldsymbol{\rho}\left(x+\frac{y}{2},x-\frac{y}{2}\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

is the Wigner transform of the operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}
.

If not otherwise stated, we will denote by
C
a positive constant independent of
\hbar
and will use that, for
a,b\geq 0
,
a\lesssim b
if and only if there exists
C>0
such that
a\leq C\,b
.

2.
L^{1}
weak-strong uniqueness criterion

In this section we present a
L^{1}
weak-strong stability estimate for the Vlasov–Poisson equation, where regularity is required only on one of the two solutions of (
1
) and show an application of this criterion to the semiclassical limit from the Hartree equation to the Vlasov–Poisson system.

2.1.
L^{1}
weak-strong stability criterion for the Vlasov–Poisson equation

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be two positive solutions of the Vlasov–Poisson equation (
1
) verifying
f_{1},\,f_{2}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6}))
, for some
T>0
. Then, if

(2)

\lambda_{f_{2}}(t):=\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3,1}_{x}L^{1}_{\xi}}

is finite for every
t\in[0,T]
, the following stability estimate holds

\displaystyle\left\lVert f_{1}-f_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}\leq\left\lVert f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}-f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})}e^{\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t)},

where
\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t)=C\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{f_{2}}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s
with
C=\left\lVert\nabla K\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{3}{2},\infty}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Notice that the regularity condition (
2
) on
f_{2}
reads

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\in L^{3,1}_{x}.

In particular, for each
t\in[0,T]
and for some
\varepsilon\in(0,2]
, it holds by real interpolation

(3)

\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right\rVert_{L^{3,1}_{x}}\leq\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right\rVert_{L^{3+\varepsilon}_{x}}+\left\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right\rVert_{L^{3-\varepsilon}_{x}}.

Indeed it is known that solutions of Vlasov–Poisson are regular (see e.g.
[
6
,
16
,
19
,
20
]
). This is one of the advantages of the weak-strong method, that requires regularity only on one of the solutions.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let
f:=f_{1}-f_{2}
and define for
k=1,2
,
\rho_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{k}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
,
\rho:=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}
and
E_{k}=-\nabla K*\rho_{k}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E_{1}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f=(E_{2}-E_{1})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f_{2},

whence

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left(\nabla K*\rho\cdot\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right)\sign\!\left(f\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho\,\nabla K\,\dot{*}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sign\!\left(f\right)\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right)

where the notation
\dot{*}
indicates that we perform the scalar product of vectors inside the convolution. This implies

(4)

\partial_{t}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert\nabla K*\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}f_{2}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

By Hölder’s inequality for Lorentz spaces (cf.
[
7
, Formula (2.7)]
), since
\nabla K\in L^{\frac{3}{2},\infty}
, we have

\left\lVert\nabla K*\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{\xi}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla K(z-\cdot)\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{\xi}}\right\rvert\leq C\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L_{x}^{3,1}L^{1}_{\xi}}

and Grönwall’s Lemma concludes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Applications to the limit from Hartree to Vlasov–Poisson: convergence in trace norm

In this section we consider the time-dependent Hartree equation

(5)

i\,\hbar\,\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\rho}=\left[{H_{\boldsymbol{\rho}},\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right]

describing the time evolution of
\boldsymbol{\rho}=\boldsymbol{\rho}(t)
, the nonnegative and bounded density operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}=1
. The Hamiltonian is given by the sum of the Laplace operator and the multiplication operator
V_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=K*\diag{(\boldsymbol{\rho})}

(6)

H_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\Delta+V_{\boldsymbol{\rho}},

where
\hbar=h/(2\pi)
is the reduced Planck constant and

\diag\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)(x):=h^{3}\,\boldsymbol{\rho}(x,x).

We recall the following result from
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
13
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
f
be a nonnegative solution to the Vlasov–Poisson equation (
1
) and
\boldsymbol{\rho}
a solution to the Hartree equation (
5
) with initial data

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle\in W_{m}^{\sigma+1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\cap H_{\sigma}^{\sigma+1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{L}^{1}

with
m>3
and
\sigma>m+6
. Then there exist two nonnegative real-valued continuous functions
\Lambda_{f}(t)
and
C_{f}(t)
independent of
\hbar
such that

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f^{\mathrm{in}}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+C_{f}(t)\,\hbar\right)e^{\Lambda_{f}(t)}.

Remark 2.2
.

Recalling that for
(y,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}

\mathcal{F}[f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}](y,w)=h^{3}\tr\!\left(e^{-2i\pi\left(y\cdot x+w\cdot\boldsymbol{p}\right)}\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)\,

where
x
and
\boldsymbol{p}
are the position and momentum operator respectively and
\mathcal{F}[g]
denotes the Fourier transform of the function
g
, we obtain

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}[f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}]-\mathcal{F}[f]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}.

As a corollary of Theorem
2.1
, this gives convergence in
L^{\infty}
for the Fourier transform of the difference between the Wigner transforms of the solution to the Hartree equation and the solution to the Vlasov–Poisson equation in the semiclassical limit, if the initial data are close for
\hbar\to 0
, providing an explicit rate of convergence, that is supposed to be optimal in
\hbar
. This is a generalization in a stronger topology with explicit rate of the result obtained by Lions and Paul in
[
15
]
.

The rest of this section is devoted to highlighting the analogy between the proof of Proposition
2.1
and the proof of Theorem
2.1
, aiming at conveying the idea that, given a weak-strong uniqueness stability estimate, there is a quite general method leading to the proof of the semiclassical limit in the correspondent topology for operators.

2.2.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
part I: reduction to stability estimates

Let
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)
be the Weyl quantization of
f
and
U_{t,s}
,
t>s
, be the two parameter semigroup generated by the Hartree Hamiltonian (
5
), i.e.

i\,\hbar\,\partial_{t}U_{t,s}=H_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(t)\,U_{t,s},\quad\quad U_{s,s}=1.

By conjugating the difference of
\boldsymbol{\rho}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)
by
U_{t,s}
and performing the time derivative we obtain

i\,\partial_{t}U_{t,s}^{*}(\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right))\,U_{t,s}=U_{t,s}^{*}\left[{K*(\rho-\rho_{f}),\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)}\right]\,U_{t,s}+U_{t,s}^{*}B\,U_{t,s}

with
B_{t}
the time-dependent operator with integral kernel

(7)

B(x,y)=\left((K*\rho_{f})(x)-(K*\rho_{f})(y)-(\nabla K*\rho_{f})\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)\cdot(x-y)\right)\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}(x,y)

and
\rho_{f}(x)=\diag(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right))(x)
. By Duhamel’s formula and using the unitarity of
U_{t,s}
we get

(8)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}&\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f^{\mathrm{in}}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert B_{s}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\rho(x)-\rho_{f}(x)\right\rvert\left\lVert\left[{K(\cdot-x),\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)}\right]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

The structure of Equation (
8
) is clear in light of the proof of Proposition
2.1
: the second line in Equation (
8
) is the quantum analogue of Inequality (
4
) and has to be treated in the same spirit of a weak-strong stability estimate, whereas the second term in the right-hand side of the first line represents the error due to the fact that we are considering two different dynamics, namely the Hartree and the Vlasov ones. The error term is easily bounded in
[
13
, Proposition 4.4]
by

\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq C\,\hbar^{2}\left\lVert\rho_{f}\right\rVert_{L^{1}\cap H^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}^{2}f\right\rVert_{H^{2n}_{2n}},\quad\quad n>\frac{3}{2}.

2.2.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
part II: the commutator inequality

To bound the second line in Equation (
8
), we proceed as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, taking the
L^{\infty}
norm in the variable
z
in the commutator term. This yields

(9)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\rho(x)-\rho_{f}(x)\right\rvert&\left\lVert\left[{K(\cdot-x),\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)}\right]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\ \ \leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lVert\left[{K(\cdot-x),\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)}\right]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}.\end{split}

As is the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we need to bound the commutator
{\left[{K(\cdot-x),\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)}\right]}
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
, uniformly in
x
and in
\hbar
. This is achieved in
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
, that we report here for completeness.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
\boldsymbol{\rho}
be a self-adjoint bounded operator and
K(x)=\frac{1}{\left\lvert x\right\rvert}
, then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,2]
, there exists
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\left[{K(\cdot-x),\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq\,C\,\hbar\,\left(\left\lVert\diag(|\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}|)\right\rVert_{L^{3-\varepsilon}}+\left\lVert\diag(|\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}|)\right\rVert_{L^{3+\varepsilon}}\right).

Applying Proposition
2.2
to Equation (
9
), we are left with bounding
\diag(|\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)|)
in
L^{p}
, for
p\in\{3-\varepsilon,3+\varepsilon\}
. This is the quantum analogue of Inequality (
3
). Mimicking the classical interpolation inequality

\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{x}(L^{1}_{\xi})}\leq\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x,\xi}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}f\right\rvert\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{3(p-1)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

the boundedness of the operator
\diag(|\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)|)
and the propagation of quantum moments
h^{3}\tr(|\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)|\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{n})
, for some integer
n
, yield

(10)

\left\lVert\diag\!\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rvert\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(h^{3}\tr\!\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rvert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{3(p-1)}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By the Boulkhemair generalization of the Calderón–Vaillancourt theorem for the Weyl operators
[
1
]
, it holds

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\leq C\,\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}f\right\rVert_{W^{4,\infty}}

uniformly in
\hbar
, for some
C>0
. Furthermore, by
[
13
, Proposition 3.3]
, the quantum velocity moments are bounded by

h^{3}\tr\!\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!\xi}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(f\right)\right\rvert\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{3(p-1)}\right)\leq C\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}f\right\rVert_{H_{\sigma}^{\sigma}}.

Grönwall’s Lemma concludes the proof.

3.
L^{2}
weak-strong uniqueness criterion

In this section we review the proof of
[
2
]
to obtain an optimal bound in
L^{2}
for the semiclassical limit from the Hartree to the Vlasov–Poisson by mimicking the proof of a weak-strong stability criterion for solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system in
L^{2}
.

3.1.
L^{2}
weak-strong stability criterion for the Vlasov–Poisson equation

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
be two positive solutions of the Vlasov–Poisson equation verifying
\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\infty}
. If

\lambda_{f_{2}}(t)=\left\lVert{\rho_{f_{2}}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}\sqrt{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{\xi}}+C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}\sqrt{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{3,1}_{x}L^{1}_{\xi}}

is finite for each
t\in[0,T]
, the following stability estimate holds

\Big\lVert\sqrt{f_{1}}-\sqrt{f_{2}}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\Big\lVert\sqrt{f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}}-\sqrt{f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}e^{\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t)}

where
\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t)=C\,\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{f_{2}}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s
, for some
C>0
. In particular,

\left\lVert f_{1}-f_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq 2\,C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}}-f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{\Lambda_{f_{2}}(t)}.

Proof.

Let
v_{1}=\sqrt{f_{1}}
,
v_{2}=\sqrt{f_{2}}
and
v:=v_{1}-v_{2}
. Then
v
satisfies the equation

\big(\partial_{t}+\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}+E_{v_{1}^{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\big)v=\big(E_{v_{2}^{2}}-E_{v_{1}^{2}}\big)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}=\left(\nabla K*\rho_{\left(v_{2}+v_{1}\right)v}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}v_{2},

where we use the notations
\rho_{g}=\int g\,d\xi
and
E_{g}=\nabla K*\rho_{g}
, for any function
g
. The fact that the vector field
(\xi,E_{v_{1}^{2}})
is divergence-free yields

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}v\left(\nabla K*\rho_{\left(v_{2}+v_{1}\right)v}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{12}}\big(\left\lvert v(z^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}+2\,v_{2}(z^{\prime})\,v(z^{\prime})\big)\nabla K(x-x^{\prime})\cdot\left(v(z)\,\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}(z)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}\\
&=:I_{1}+2\,I_{2}.\end{split}

We bound the first term by Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\Big\lVert\nabla K*\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\,\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\Big\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Big\lVert\left\lvert\nabla K\right\rvert*\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\lvert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rvert\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\Big\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

and by applying Hölder’s inequality for the Lorentz spaces we get

I_{1}\leq\left\lVert\nabla K\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{3}{2},\infty}_{x}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3,1}_{x}L^{1}_{\xi}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

To bound
I_{2}
we use the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, to obtain the bound

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\,\Big\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\,v_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\,\Big\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\,\Big\lVert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\,\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\,\Big\rVert_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x}}\\
&\leq C\left\lVert\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\left\lVert v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\left\lVert\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}_{x}}.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality, we finally obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{\xi}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{\xi}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=C\left\lVert{\rho_{f_{2}}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{\xi}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

that combined with the bound on
I_{1}
leads to

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\,\big(\left\lVert{\rho_{f_{2}}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}_{x}}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3}_{x}L^{2}_{\xi}}+\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\left\lVert\nabla_{\xi}v_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{3,1}_{x}L^{1}_{\xi}}\big)\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using that
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}=2\,C_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, Grönwall’s lemma concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Quantum Optimal transport pseudo-distance

The strategies of Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
are quite general and they generalizes to the relativistic case (cf.
[
14
]
). Application of these methods to other models are certainly possible. Although quite general, the weak-strong type criterion requires some regularity on the solution of the Vlasov–Poisson equation (
1
), that is not always compatible with certain classes of quantum states, such as pure states. To treat this important set of initial conditions, one can look at weaker topologies, in which almost no regularity conditions on derivatives of solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson are required.

4.1.
Classical optimal transport stability estimate

Among the classical metrics on the set of probability measures are the Wasserstein–(Monge–Kantorovich) distances. For
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
two probability distributions on the phase space
\mathbb{R}^{6}
, one defines the set of couplings by
\mathcal{C}(f_{1},f_{2})
as the set of probability measures
\gamma
on
\mathbb{R}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
with first marginal
f_{1}
and second marginal
f_{2}
, that is
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\gamma(\cdot,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y)=f_{1}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\gamma(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\cdot)=f_{2}
. Then, for any
p\geq 1
, the Wasserstein distance of order
p
is defined by

W_{p}(f_{1},f_{2})=\left(\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(f_{1},f_{2})}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\gamma(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{2})\right)^{1/p}.

It gives a natural distance to compare two solutions of the Vlasov equation. To explain this, let us define the characteristic flow
z(t,z_{0})=(x(t,z_{0}),\xi(t,z_{0}))
associated to a solution
f
of the Vlasov equation by
z(0,z_{0})=z_{0}
and

\left\{\ \begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}x(t,z_{0})=\xi(t,z_{0})\\
&\partial_{t}\xi(t,z_{0})=E_{f}(x(t,z_{0}))\end{aligned}\right.

and it verifies for any bounded continuous function
\varphi

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(z)\,f(t,z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(x(t,z_{0}),\xi(t,z_{0}))\,f^{\mathrm{in}}(z_{0})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{0}.

Then, if
E_{f_{1}}
and
E_{f_{2}}
are sufficiently close and regular, one observes that two initially close characteristic trajectories
z_{1}=z_{1}(t,z_{0})
following the flow of
f_{1}
and
z_{2}=z_{2}(t,z^{\prime}_{0})
following the flow of
f_{2}
will remain close since

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)\cdot\left(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right)+2\left(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right)\cdot\left(E_{f_{1}}(x_{1})-E_{f_{2}}(x_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\omega_{f_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})^{2}\right)\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert^{2}+3\left\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert E_{f_{1}}(x_{2})-E_{f_{2}}(x_{2})\right\rvert^{2}

where

\omega_{f_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}}\frac{\left\lvert E_{f_{1}}(x_{1})-E_{f_{1}}(x_{2})\right\rvert}{\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert}

so that one should expect that
\left\lvert z_{1}(t)-z_{2}(t)\right\rvert
does not grow too fast. Hence, to control an initially small quantity of the form

(17)

\mathcal{E}_{p}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\left\lvert z_{1}-z_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\gamma(t,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{2})

one can choose
\gamma
such that

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})\,\gamma(t,z_{1},z_{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{2}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{6}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(z_{1}(t,z),z_{2}(t,z^{\prime}))\,\gamma^{\mathrm{in}}(z,z^{\prime})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}

or equivalently,
\gamma
solving

\partial_{t}\gamma+\left(\xi_{1}\cdot\nabla_{x_{1}}+E_{f_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi_{1}}\right)\gamma+\left(\xi_{2}\cdot\nabla_{x_{2}}+E_{f_{2}}\cdot\nabla_{\xi_{2}}\right)\gamma=0.

with initial condition
\gamma(0,\cdot,\cdot)=\gamma^{\mathrm{in}}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Taking the initial coupling
\gamma^{\mathrm{in}}
to be the optimal coupling then ensures that (
17
) is initially small if
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are initially close in
W_{p}
. In the case of the Coulomb potential and when
\rho_{f_{1}}
and
\rho_{f_{2}}
are uniformly bounded, then
E_{f_{1}}
is log-Lipshitz, that is
\omega_{f_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq C\max\!\left(1,-\ln(\left\lvert x_{1}-x_{2}\right\rvert)\right)
, and it was proved by Loeper
[
17
]
that
\left\lvert E_{f_{1}}(x_{2})-E_{f_{2}}(x_{2})\right\rvert
is controlled by
W_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})
. As proved in
[
17
]
, this leads to an inequality of the form

(18)

\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)\leq C(t)\,\mathcal{E}_{2}(t)\max\!\left(1,-\ln(\mathcal{E}_{2}(t))\right).

where
C(t)>0
depends on the growth of
\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
. One then concludes by Gronwall’s lemma to a stability estimate of the form

W_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})\leq W_{2}(f_{1}^{\mathrm{in}},f_{2}^{\mathrm{in}})^{e^{\theta\lambda(t)}}\,e^{e^{\lambda(t)}}

where
\theta=\sign(\ln W_{2}(f_{1},f_{2}))
and
\lambda(t)>0
depends on
C(t)
, and this implies uniqueness for the Vlasov–Poisson equation. Let us mention that using a different weight for the phase space variables (see
[
8
]
), one can also obtain an inequality with a square root of the logarithm instead of a logarithm in Inequality (
18
).

4.2.
Quantum optimal transport

To obtain an application to semiclassical estimates, one can use the semiclassical analogue of the optimal transport distance defined by Golse and Paul
[
3
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of density operators, that is the set of positive trace class operators verifying
h^{3}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)=1
. As in the classical case, one first introduces the notion of a coupling between a density operator
\boldsymbol{\rho}
and a classical kinetic density
f
as an operator valued function
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6},\mathcal{P})
such that

\displaystyle h^{3}\tr\!\left(\boldsymbol{\gamma}(z)\right)=f(z),\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}(z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=\boldsymbol{\rho}.

Denoting by
\mathcal{C}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})
the set of semiclassical couplings, the semiclassical optimal transport pseudometric of order
2
is defined by

(19)

W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho}):=\left(\inf_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in\mathcal{C}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}h^{3}\tr\!\left(\mathbf{c}_{\hbar}(z)\boldsymbol{\gamma}(z)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
,
\boldsymbol{c}_{\hbar}(z)
is an operator defined by it action on functions
\varphi
of the form
\boldsymbol{c}_{\hbar}(z)\varphi(y)=(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert\xi-\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{2})\varphi(y)
, with
z=(x,\xi)
and
\boldsymbol{p}=-i\hbar\nabla_{y}
. This is not a distance, as it verifies for example
W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})^{2}\geq 3\,\hbar
, but it is comparable to the classical Wasserstein distance
W_{2}
between the Husimi transform
\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
of the quantum density operator and the normal kinetic density
f
in the sense that it verifies
[
3
]

(20)

W_{2}(f,\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}})^{2}\leq W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})^{2}+3\,\hbar,

and in the special case when
\boldsymbol{\rho}
is the Wick quantization of some function
g
,

(21)

W_{2,\hbar}(f,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\rho}}_{\hbar}^{W}\!\!\left(g\right))^{2}\leq W_{2}(f,g)^{2}+3\,\hbar.

Moreover, it verifies approximate triangle inequalities
[
4
]
. For general density matrices, it can be proved that
W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})\leq W_{2}(f,\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}})+\sqrt{3\hbar}+\hbar\,\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}
(see
[
12
]
). In particular, this allows to treat more singular states such as some classes of projection operators verifying
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{2}=\boldsymbol{\rho}\geq 0
for which in general one can obtains bounds of the form
\hbar\,\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\sqrt{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}=\hbar\,\lVert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\rho}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C\,\hbar^{\theta}
for some
\theta\in[0,1/2]
(see
[
11
]
).

4.3.
Application to the semiclassical limit

The same strategy as in the classical case was used in
[
9
,
10
]
to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
9
, Theorem 5]

Let
f
be a solution of the Vlasov–Poisson equation (
1
) and
\boldsymbol{\rho}
be a solution of the Hartree equation (
5
) equation with respective initial conditions
f^{\mathrm{in}}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}}
verifying

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathscr{P}\cap L^{1}_{n_{0}}\cap L^{\infty}_{n}\qquad\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{L}^{1}_{16}\cap\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{4}.

uniformly in
\hbar
for some
(n_{0},n)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
verifying
n_{0}>6
and
n>3
. Then there exists
T>0
such that

(22)

h^{3}\tr\!\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{16}\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)\in L^{\infty}([0,T])\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\boldsymbol{\rho}\in L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{4}),

uniformly in
\hbar
, and there exists a constant
C_{T}
depending only on the initial conditions and independent of
\hbar
such that

(23)

W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})\leq W_{2,\hbar}(f^{\mathrm{in}},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}})^{e^{\theta\lambda(t)}}\,e^{e^{\lambda(t)}}

where
\theta=\sign(\ln W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho}))
and
\lambda(t)\in C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}_{+})
is an increasing function of time depending on the growth of
\left\lVert\rho_{f}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
and verifying
\lambda(0)=0
.

The function
\lambda(t)
is detailed in
[
10
, Theorem 5]
. The above theorem implies in particular that if initially there exists a constant
C
independent of
\hbar
such that
W_{2,\hbar}(f^{\mathrm{in}},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}})\leq C\sqrt{\hbar}\leq 1
, then

W_{2}(f,\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\rho}})\leq\hbar^{e^{-\lambda(t)}}\,e^{e^{\lambda(t)}}.

Steps of the proof.

Several steps are needed to get the complete result. As in the classical case, the stability estimate in the optimal transport metric uses the fact that both
\rho_{f}
and
\rho
are bounded (uniformly in
\hbar
). To get this property for the Hartree equation, one proceeds in three steps.

—

First, one proves the propagation of moments of the form
h^{3}\tr\!\left(\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{n}\boldsymbol{\rho}\right)
with
n\in 2\mathbb{N}

[
9
, Theorem 3]
. The proof uses the fact that moments of order two are bounded by the energy conservation, the fact that
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}
is conserved and Lieb–Thirring type inequalities. This is at this step that only a local in time propagation is proved, which prevents the theorem to hold for all times
T>0
.

—

The second point is to prove the propagation of weighted Schatten norms of the form
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\,\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}
uniformly with respect to
p
and
\hbar

[
9
, Proposition 5.3]
using the previously proved boundedness of moments.

—

The last point consists in taking
p\to\infty
to get that
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\rho}\,\left(1+\left\lvert\boldsymbol{p}\right\rvert^{n}\right)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}
is bounded uniformly in
\hbar
, from which one deduces that
\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded uniformly in
\hbar
.

Once these points are proved, the proof of the theorem follows as in
[
3
]
by adapting the classical proof of stability for the Vlasov equation but replacing one of the solutions by a solution of the Hartree equation. More precisely, for any coupling
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathrm{in}}\in\mathcal{C}(f^{\mathrm{in}},\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mathrm{in}})
, one looks at the solution
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t,z)
to

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\left(\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}+E_{f}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\right)\boldsymbol{\gamma}-\frac{1}{i\hbar}\left[{H_{\boldsymbol{\rho}},\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\right]

and obtains a Grönwall’s type inequality on (the logarithm of)

\mathcal{E}_{2,\hbar}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}h^{3}\tr\!\left(\mathbf{c}_{\hbar}(z)\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t,z)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

Since
W_{2,\hbar}(f,\boldsymbol{\rho})\leq\mathcal{E}_{2,\hbar}
, the result follows by minimizing over all couplings
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathrm{in}}
.

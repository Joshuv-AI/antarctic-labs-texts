Class Number of the Imaginary Quadratic Field and Quadratic Residues Identities
Jorge Garcia

Abstract
A formula for the sum of quadratic residues modulus a prime p = 4 ​ n − 1 p=4n-1 is studied. We relate some terms on this formula with roots of quadratics and provide an exhaustive analysis of new concepts based on these roots. A number of formulas for the sum of the quadratic residues are obtained. We finalize the paper by obtaining several identities involving h ⁡ ( − p ) h(-p) the class number of the imaginary quadratic field ℚ ⁡ ( − p ) . {\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p}).

Consider

Definition 2.1

Let

r_{q}(x)\in\{0,1,2,...,q-1\}
satisfies

x=m\cdot q+r_{q}(x),

. Clearly,

For

Theorem 2.3

Let
p=4n-1
be prime. Using Notation

we have

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}r_{p}\left(k^{2}\right)=p\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\lfloor R_{m}\right\rfloor+\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\left\lfloor Q_{m}\right\rfloor\right)-Mp(2n-1)+\frac{p\cdot(n^{2}+n)}{6}.

A concept arises naturally when we study the term

. This term is the positive root of the quadratic polynomial
x^{2}-x+2-3n-mp
which is the same as
(x-1)^{2}+x+1-3n-mp.

Definition 2.4

Let
p=4n-1
be a prime and
0\leq k\leq p
. The total residue of

is defined and denoted by

\Gamma(k)=r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)+r_{p}\left(k+1-3n\right).

We also say that

or more, i.e. if

\Gamma(k)\geq p.

, we need to subtract

Therefore

. Secondly, the amount of jumps in the interval

will allow us to establish a formula to compute the terms

The following three lemmas allow us to identify some jumps and when they occur.
For notation purposes we define
{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}=\{0,1,2,...\}.

Lemma 2.5

Let
p=4n-1
be a prime and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}.
Then

\left\lfloor Q_{m}\right\rfloor<Q_{m}.

Proof.

Assume there is
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\,\displaystyle{j=\left(1+\sqrt{4mp+12n-7}\right)/2.}\,
Then
\,j^{2}-j+2-3n=mp.\,
Taking
\,x_{0}=2n-j\,
gives

x_{0}^{2}=4n^{2}-n+mp+j-4nj+n+3n-2=(n+m-j+1)p-1,

hence
\,x_{0}^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{p}\,
which contradicts Fermat Little Theorem as
p=4n-1.

\Box

Remark 2.6

By using the same argument, there is no integer
k
with
k^{2}-3k+4-3n=mp,
else taking
k-1=j
we obtain
\,j^{2}-j+2-3n=mp\,
which leads to a contradiction.

Lemma 2.7

Let
p=4n-1
be a prime with

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}
with

0\leq m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}-4n+5}{p}\right\rfloor\textit{\ \ and\ \ }k_{m}=1+\left\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{4mp+12n-7}}{2}\right\rfloor.

(i)

\,3\leq k_{m}\leq n+2.\,

(ii)

\,1+\sqrt{4mp+12n-7}<2k_{m}<3+\sqrt{4mp+12n-7}.\,

(iii)

k_{m}
is a jump and

(iv)

\,\displaystyle{3n-k_{m}-1<r_{p}\left((k_{m}-1)^{2}\right)=(k_{m}-1)^{2}-mp\leq 3n+k_{m}-4.}\,

Proof.

(i)

Notice that
\,4mp+12n-7\leq 4n^{2}+4n+1,\,
hence
\,k_{m}\leq n+2.\,
Since
2\leq n,\ 9\leq 4mp+12n-7.
Therefore

(ii)

Notice that

hence

k_{m}^{2}-k_{m}+2-3n>mp.

(2.3)

Now

\,2\leq n\,

(k_{m}-1)^{2}-mp=k_{m}^{2}-k_{m}+2-3n-mp+3n-k_{m}-1\geq 3n-k_{m}.

(2.4)

From Lemma
2.5
,
\,k_{m}<\left(3+\sqrt{4mp+12n-7}\right)/2.\,
The other inequality is obvious.

(iii)

From (ii),
k_{m}
is less than the positive root of

, hence
k_{m}^{2}-3k_{m}+4-mp-3n\leq-1.
Therefore

(k_{m}-1)^{2}-mp\leq 3n+k_{m}-4\leq 4n.

(2.5)

It is impossible that

otherwise
(k_{m}-1)^{2}-1=(m+1)p
, hence

would divide

which forces

Hence

For

\begin{split}C_{<}&=\left\{k\in\{2,...,n+2\}\ :\ r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)<3n-k-1\,\right\},\\

Lemma 3.4

Let
n>3,\ p=4n-1
prime and
M_{0}=\left\lfloor(n^{2}-4n+5)/p\right\rfloor
. Define
\ell_{m}=1+\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp+p-1}\right\rfloor
and consider
k_{m}
defined as in Lemma
2.7
. Let
2\leq k\leq n+2
and
0\leq m\leq M_{0}-1.

If
k<k_{0},\,k>\ell_{M_{0}}
or
\ell_{m}<k\leq k_{m+1}
then
k\notin C_{\geq}.
Also if
k<k_{0}
or
\ell_{m}<k<k_{m+1}
then
k
is not a jump.

Proof.

Consider
k<k_{0}=1+\left\lfloor(1+\sqrt{12n-7})/2\right\rfloor
. Then
(2k-1)^{2}<12n-7
from which
(k-1)^{2}<3n-k-1\leq 4n-1.
Hence
r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)=(k-1)^{2}<3n-k-1.
Therefore by Lemma
2.10
,
k\in C_{<}
and
k
is not a jump.

Consider
k>\ell_{M_{0}}.
Notice that
\ell_{M_{0}}=1+\left\lfloor\sqrt{Mp}\right\rfloor\geq n-1.
By Lemma
3.2
, either
k\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}
or
k\in C_{<}.
If
k=k_{m+1},
by Lemma
2.7
(iv),
k\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}.

Finally consider
\ell_{m}<k<k_{m+1}.
Then

1+\sqrt{(m+1)p}<k<\frac{1+\sqrt{4(m+1)p+12n-7}}{2}.

Hence

(m+1)p<(k-1)^{2}<(m+1)p+3n-k-1.

Therefore
r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)=(k-1)^{2}-(m+1)p<3n-k-1.
By Lemmas
2.10
and
3.2
,
k
is not a jump and
k\in C_{<}.

\Box

Lemma 3.5

Consider
n,p
and
M_{0}
as in Lemma
2.7
. Then
k\in C_{\geq}
if and only if there is
m\in\{0,1,...,M_{0}\}
such that
k_{m}<k\leq 1+\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp+p-1}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.8
, if
k_{m}<k\leq\ell_{m}
then
k
is a jump and
r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)>3n+k-3.
Note that
2\leq k_{0}<k.
Since
m\leq M_{0}
we have
\ell_{m}\leq n+2,
hence
2\leq k\leq n+2.
Therefore
k\in C_{\geq}.

Conversely, let
k\in C_{\geq}.
Then
2\leq k\leq n+2
and
r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)\geq 3n+k-3.
Observe that

[2,n+2]=[2,k_{0}]\cup(k_{0},\ell_{0}]\cup(\ell_{0},k_{1}]\cup(k_{1},\ell_{1}]\cup\cdots\cup(k_{M_{0}},\ell_{M_{0}}]\cup(\ell_{M_{0}},n+2].

If
k<k_{0},\,k>\ell_{M_{0}}
or
\ell_{m}<k\leq k_{m+1}
, by Lemma
3.4
,
k\notin C_{\geq}.

This forces
k
to be in
(k_{m},\ell_{m}]
for some
m\in\{0,...,M_{0}\},
which is what we wanted.
\Box

4
Counting the Jumps

In this section we will relate the jumps in the different sets
C_{<},C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)},C_{\geq},D_{<},D_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}
and
D_{\geq}.
We will compute different cardinalities when possible. In this section we consider
n>3
and
p=4n-1
a prime number.

Theorem 4.1

The function
f:C_{\geq}\longrightarrow D_{<}
defined by

f(k)=2n+2-k,

is well-defined and bijective.

Proof.

Consider
k\in C_{\geq}.
By Lemmas
2.8
,
3.5
and Observation
2.9
, there is
m\in\{0,1,...,M_{0}\}
with
M_{0}=\left\lfloor(n^{2}-4n+5)/p\right\rfloor
such that
k_{m}<k\leq 1+\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp+p-1}\right\rfloor
and
3n+k-3\leq(k-1)^{2}-mp=r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right),
where
k_{m}
is the jump given in Lemma
2.10
. Hence

\displaystyle 0\leq k^{2}-3k-3n+4-mp,

(4.6)

\displaystyle k^{2}-2k+1-mp\leq 4n-2.

(4.7)

Let
k_{f}=f(k)=2n+2-k.
We will first prove that
k_{f}\in D_{<}.
Now

(k_{f}-1)^{2}=(n-k+m+2)p+(k-1)^{2}-mp+n-k+1-p.

Let
w=(k-1)^{2}-mp+n-k+1-p.
From Inequality
4.6
,
w\geq-1.
From Remark
2.6
,
k^{2}-3k+4-3n-mp\neq 0,
hence
w\geq 0.

Note that in the case of equality in Inequality
4.7
, we would have
(k-1)^{2}=mp+p-1,
hence the congruency
x^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{p}
would have a solution, which is impossible. Therefore

k^{2}-2k+1-mp<4n-2.

(4.8)

From Inequality
4.7
,

w=k^{2}-2k+1-mp+2-k-3n<n-k.

(4.9)

Clearly
n-k\leq 4n-2,
hence
w<p-1.
Therefore
r_{p}\left((k_{f}-1)^{2}\right)=w.

From Inequality
4.9
,
0\leq w<n-k,
this forces
k\leq n-1
. Also from Inequality
4.9
, since
k_{f}-n-2=n-k,
we conclude that
r_{p}\left((k_{f}-1)^{2}\right)=w<k_{f}-n-2.

Finally,
2\leq k\leq n-1
implies
n+3\leq k_{f}\leq 2n,
hence
f
is well defined as
k_{f}\in D_{<}.

Clearly
f
is injective. Take now
{\widehat{k}}\in D_{<}.
Then
n+3\leq{\widehat{k}}\leq 2n
and

r_{p}\left(({\widehat{k}}-1)^{2}\right)<{\widehat{k}}-n-2.

(4.10)

Consider
k=2n+2-{\widehat{k}}
and
{\widehat{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with

{\widehat{m}}\leq({\widehat{k}}-1)^{2}<{\widehat{m}}\cdot p+p.

(4.11)

Then
2\leq k\leq n-1
and

(k-1)^{2}=(n-{\widehat{k}}+{\widehat{m}})\cdot p+(\widehat{k}-1)^{2}-\widehat{m}p+n-\widehat{k}+1-p.

Consider
m=n-{\widehat{k}}+{\widehat{m}}
and
u=(\widehat{k}-1)^{2}-\widehat{m}p+n-\widehat{k}+1-p.

Since
{\widehat{k}}\leq 5n,
Inequality
4.11
implies

0\leq n-{\widehat{k}}+1-p\leq(k-1)^{2}-\widehat{m}p+n-\widehat{k}+1-p.

(4.12)

Also from Inequality
4.10
, we have
(k-1)^{2}-\widehat{m}p+n-\widehat{k}+1-p<p-1,
therefore
0\leq u<p-1.
Hence
r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)=(k-1)^{2}-mp=u.

Finally Inequality
4.12
implies
u\geq 5n-{\widehat{k}}\geq 5n-1-{\widehat{k}}=3n+k-3.

Therefore
r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)\geq 3n+k-3,
i.e.
k\in C_{\geq}.
Clearly
f(k)={\widehat{k}}
, then
f
is bijective.
\Box

Lemma 4.2

Let
y,z
be the last two elements in
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}.
If
k\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}-\{y,z\}
then

(4.13)

where
m=m(k)=\left\lfloor(k-1)^{2}/p\right\rfloor.

Proof.

(a)

Case
n=4\ell+2
or

By Lemma
3.2
,
k\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}-\{y,z\}
implies
k\leq n-3.
Hence
m\leq\ell-2=\left\lfloor(n-4)^{2}/p\right\rfloor.

If
n=4\ell+2
then
mp+p\leq(\ell-1)(16\ell+7)\leq 16\ell^{2}+1=(n-2)^{2}+1.

If
n=4\ell+3
then
mp+p\leq(\ell-1)(16\ell+11)\leq 16\ell^{2}+8\ell+2=(n-2)^{2}+1
.

(b)

Case
n=4\ell+1
or
n=4\ell+4,\ \ell\geq 4.
By Lemma
3.2
,
k\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}-\{y,z\}
implies
k\leq n-2.

If
n=4\ell+1
then
m\leq\left\lfloor(n-3)^{2}/p\right\rfloor=\ell-2.
Hence
mp+p\leq(\ell-1)(16\ell+3)\leq 16\ell^{2}-8\ell+2=(n-2)^{2}+1.

If
n=4\ell+4
then
m\leq\left\lfloor(n-3)^{2}/p\right\rfloor=\ell-1.
Hence

(c)

The only left cases are

and 18 as the choices

do not provide prime numbers.

(i)

If
n=5
or
6
then
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}
has only two elements. Hence Inequality
4.13
is trivial.

(ii)

If
n=8
then
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}=\{6,8,10\}
and
k=6.
Therefore
m(k)=0=\left\lfloor(k-1)^{2}/p\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor 25/31\right\rfloor
clearly satisfies Inequality
4.13
.

(iii)

If
n=11
then
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}=\{7,10,12\}
and
k=7.
Therefore
m(k)=0
clearly satisfies Inequality
4.13
.

(iv)

If
n=12
then
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}=\{7,10,12,14\}.
If

then

clearly satisfies Inequality

as

. Clearly
m(7)
also does.

(v)

If
n=15
then
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}=\{8,11,14,16\}.
If
k=11,
then

clearly satisfies Inequality
4.13
as

. Clearly

(vi)

If

then
n+1\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}=\{8,12,15,17,19\}.
If

4.13

Lemma 4.3

k\in D_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}
if and only if there is an integer
m
with
1\leq m\leq\left\lfloor(n^{2}-4n+5)/p\right\rfloor
and

k=2n-\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp-1}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

\Rightarrow
)

Let
k\in D_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}
and define
\alpha=2n+1-k.
Hence
1\leq\alpha\leq n-2
and

(k-1)^{2}=4n^{2}+\alpha^{2}-4n\alpha=(n-\alpha+m)p+\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha-mp,

where
m
satisfies
mp\leq\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha<(m+1)p.
Therefore

r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)=\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha-mp.
Since
k-n-2\leq r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)<3n-k-1,\ \ mp-1\leq\alpha^{2}
and
(\alpha-1)^{2}<mp-1.

Hence
\sqrt{mp-1}\leq\alpha<\sqrt{mp-1}+1.
Since
x^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{p}
has no solution,
\sqrt{mp-1}
is not an integer. Therefore
\alpha=\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp-1}\right\rfloor+1.

Then
k=2n-\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp-1}\right\rfloor
.

Since
0\leq\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha,\ m\geq 0.
Now
(\alpha-1)^{2}<mp-1
implies
1\leq m.
Clearly
mp\leq\alpha^{2}+1\leq(n-2)^{2}+1.
Therefore
m\leq(n^{2}-4n+5)/p.

\Leftarrow
)

Consider an integer
m
with
1\leq m\leq(n^{2}-4n+5)/p
and

k=2n-\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp-1}\right\rfloor.

Since
mp-1\leq(n-2)^{2},\ \ n+2\leq k.

Clearly
n+2=k
leads us to an integer solution of
x^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{p},
therefore
n+3\leq k.
Also
1\leq m
implies
k\leq 2n.

Consider
\alpha=\left\lfloor\sqrt{mp-1}\right\rfloor+1.
Then
\sqrt{mp-1}\leq\alpha<\sqrt{mp-1}+1.
Clearly
\alpha\neq\sqrt{mp-1}+1
(otherwise
x^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{p}
has an integer solution), then
\sqrt{mp-1}<\alpha<\sqrt{mp-1}+1
.

Hence
mp-1<\alpha^{2}
and
(\alpha-1)^{2}<mp-1.
Since
\alpha<n-1,\ mp\leq\alpha^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha<n+\alpha-2+mp<mp+p.
Since

(k-1)^{2}=(2n-\alpha)^{2}=(n-\alpha+m)p+\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha-mp,

r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)=\alpha^{2}+n-\alpha-mp.
Finally, from
k-n-2=n-\alpha-1<r_{p}\left((k-1)^{2}\right)<n+\alpha-2=3n-k-1,
we conclude that
k\in D_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}.

\Box

Theorem 4.4

Let
y,z
the last two elements of
C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}.
For
k\in C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}-\{y,z\}
, consider
m=\left\lfloor(k-1)^{2}/p\right\rfloor
and
u_{0}=u_{0}(k)
the first integer less than or equal to
n+2
such that
x=u_{0}
satisfies

(m+1)\,p\leq(k+x-1)^{2}+1.

(4.14)

Then, the function
f:C_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}-\{y,z\}\longrightarrow D_{[\hskip-2.13387pt-\!\!-\!\!)}
defined by

f(k)=k_{f}=2n+2-u_{0}-k,

is well-defined and bijective.

Proof.

First, we will prove that
f
is well-defined. It is not hard to check that
u_{0}=\left\lfloor\sqrt{(m+1)p}\right\rfloor+2-k
. The definition of
u_{0}

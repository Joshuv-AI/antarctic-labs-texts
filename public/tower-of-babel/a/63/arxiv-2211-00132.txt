Large solutions of elliptic semilinear equations non-degenerate near the boundary
G. Díaz

Abstract
In this paper we study the so-called large solutions of elliptic semilinear equations with non null sources term, thus solutions blowing up on the boundary of the domain for which reason they are greater than any other solution whenever Weak Maximum Principle holds. The main topic about large solutions is uniqueness results and their behavior near the boundary. It is much less than being simple. The structure of the semilinear equations considered includes the well known Keller-Osserman integral and an assumption on the ellipticity of the leading part of the differential operator. In our study an uniform ellipticity near the boundary is required. We consider source terms in the PDE whose boundary explosion is consistent with the Keller-Osserman condition. Extra Keller-Osserman explosions on the source are also studied, showing in particular that in some cases the PDE only admits large solutions.

Keller-Osserman type growth of the sources on the boundary

As it was pointed out, the Keller-Osserman condition (
1.8
)

\int^{+\infty}\dfrac{ds}{\sqrt{{\rm G}(s)}}<+\infty\quad\hbox{where $\displaystyle{\rm G}(s)=\int^{s}_{0}\beta(r)dr$}

[
24

Lemma 2.1
(Lemma A.1 of

In some cases we replace (

Lemma 2.2

Under (

Proof
. Let us assume

4
Comparison, uniqueness and existence of solutions

As it was pointed out in Section
1
the eventual degeneracy inside the domain of the leading part of the equation prevents, in general, to consider
{\cal C}^{2}
solutions. They are replaced by viscosity solutions that solve the PDE more intrinsically. We send to the mongraphy
[
11
]
for a full introduction of the Viscosity Solution Theory. In short, we recall that for any function
u:{\cal O}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},~{\cal O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{{\rm N}}
, locally bounded from above, one defines the upper semi-continuous envelope

u^{*}(x)=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x\atop y\in{\cal O}}u(y),\quad x\in{\cal O}

and the superjets

{\cal J}^{2,+}_{{\cal O}}u^{*}(x)=\big\{({\bf p},{\rm X})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{\rm N}}\times{\cal S}^{{\rm N}}:~u^{*}(y)\leq u^{*}(x)+\displaystyle{\langle{\bf p},y-x\rangle}+\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\langle{\rm X}(y-x),y-x\rangle}+o(|y-x|^{2})\big\}.

In particular, a locally bounded from above function
u:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a (viscosity) subsolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u+\beta(u)=f\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$}

if

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(x){\rm X}+\beta\big(u^{*}(x)\big)\leq f(x)

for all
({\bf p},{\rm X})\in{\cal J}^{2,+}_{\Omega}u^{*}(x),~x\in\Omega
. By simplicity, sometimes we say that
u
is a solution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u+\beta(u)\leq f\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$}.

Remark 4.1

From the above definition we deduce a property near the well known verification results of the Optimal Control Problems. More precisely any (viscosity) subsolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u+\beta(u)=f\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$}

satisfies

u^{*}(x)\leq\inf_{{\rm V}\in{\cal V}_{+}^{u}}{\rm V}(x):,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega},

(4.1)

where

{\cal V}_{+}^{u}=\sup\big\{{\rm V}\in{\cal C}^{2}\hbox{ supersolution in $\Omega$ such that $u^{*}\leq{\rm V}$ on $\partial\Omega$}\big\}.

Indeed, given a such function
{\rm V}
, if there exists a positive interior maximum point of
u^{*}-{\rm V}
at some
x_{0}\in\Omega
one has

u^{*}(x)-u^{*}(x_{0})\leq{\rm V}(x)-{\rm V}(x_{0})=\displaystyle{\langle{\rm D}{\rm V}(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle}+\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\langle{\rm D}^{2}{\rm V}(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle}+o(|x-x_{0}|^{2})^{,}

thus
\big({\rm D}{\rm V}(x_{0}),{\rm D}^{2}{\rm V}(x_{0})\big)\in{\cal J}^{2,+}_{\Omega}u^{*}(x_{0})
for which we derive the contradiction

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(x_{0}){\rm D}^{2}{\rm V}(x_{0})+\beta\big(u^{*}(x_{0})\big)\leq-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(x_{0}){\rm D}^{2}{\rm V}(x_{0})+\beta\big({\rm V}(x_{0})\big).

Therefore

u^{*}(x)-{\rm V}(x)\leq u^{*}(x_{0})-{\rm V}(x_{0})\leq 0,\quad x\in\overline{\Omega}

and (
4.1
) holds.
\quad{}_{\Box}

Analogously, for
v:{\cal O}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},~{\cal O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{{\rm N}}

Theorem 7.1
(Weak Maximum Principle without coercivity term (I))

\varepsilon>0
. So that, we have obtained the inequality

for
x\in\Omega
. The result follows sending
\varepsilon\searrow 0.\hfill\mbox{$\quad{}_{\Box}$}

Lemma 7.1
(Hopf Lemma)

Assume (
4.12
) and (
1.7
).
Let
u
be a discontinuous subsolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u=f,\quad x\in\Omega

and
v
be a discontinuous supersolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}v=f,\quad x\in\Omega

where
f
is a uniformly continuous function. Suppose that
\Omega
is an open set such that there exists
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
for which

{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\subset\Omega\quad\hbox{and}\quad\partial{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\cap\partial\Omega=\{x_{0}\}\quad\hbox{{\rm(}interior sphere condition at $x_{0}\in\partial\Omega${\rm)}}

and

\big(u^{*}-v_{*}\big)(x)\leq\big(u^{*}-v_{*}\big)(x_{0}),\quad x\in{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)

hold. Then

\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\dfrac{\big(u^{*}-v_{*}\big)(x_{0})-\big(u^{*}-v_{*}\big)(x)}{|x-x_{0}|}>0.

Proof
. The smooth and positive function

{\rm W}(x)=e^{-\alpha|x-y|^{2}}-e^{-\alpha{\rm R}^{2}},\quad x\in{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\subset\Omega

satisfies
{\rm D}{\rm W}(x)=-2\alpha e^{-\alpha|x-y|^{2}}(x-y)
whence

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha{\rm R}e^{-\alpha{\rm R}^{2}}\leq|{\rm D}{\rm W}(x)|=2\alpha e^{-\alpha|x-y|^{2}}|x-y|\leq 2{\rm R}\alpha e^{-\alpha\frac{{\rm R}^{2}}{4}},\\[4.26773pt]
\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm D}^{2}{\rm W}(x)&\hskip-5.69046pt=-2\alpha e^{-\alpha|x-y|^{2}}{\rm I}+4\alpha^{2}e^{-\alpha|x-y|^{2}}\big(x-y\big)\otimes\big(x-y\big)\\[4.26773pt]
&\geq-\dfrac{2}{{\rm R}}|{\rm D}{\rm W}(x)|{\rm I}+\alpha{\rm R}\dfrac{{\rm D}{\rm W}(x)\otimes{\rm D}{\rm W}(x)}{|{\rm D}{\rm W}(x)|}\end{array}\end{array}\right.\qquad x\in{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\setminus{\bf B}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y).

Then from (
1.7
) we have

{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(x){\rm D}^{2}{\rm W}(x)\geq 0,\quad x\in{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\setminus{\bf B}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y),

for some provided
\alpha
. Therefore

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}(u^{*}+\varepsilon{\rm W})\leq f\quad\hbox{in ${\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\setminus{\bf B}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y)$},

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover

(u^{*}-v_{*}+\varepsilon{\rm W})(x)\leq(u^{*}-v_{*})(x_{0}),\quad x\in\partial{\bf B}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y)

if

\varepsilon=\dfrac{(u^{*}-v_{*})(x_{0})-\sup_{x\in\partial{\bf B}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y)}(u^{*}-v_{*})(x)}{e^{-\alpha\frac{{\rm R}^{2}}{4}}-e^{-\alpha{\rm R}^{2}}}>0.

So that, the Weak Maximum Principle (Theorem
7.1
) applied to the set
{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\setminus\overline{{\bf B}}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y)
leads to

(u^{*}-v_{*})(x_{0})-(u^{*}-v_{*})(x)\geq{\rm W}(x),\quad{\bf B}_{{\rm R}}(y)\setminus\overline{{\bf B}}_{\frac{{\rm R}}{2}}(y).

Sinces
{\rm W}(x_{0})=0
one concludes

\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}\dfrac{(u^{*}-v_{*})(x_{0})-(u^{*}-v_{*})(x)}{|x-x_{0}|}\geq\varepsilon\displaystyle{\langle{\rm D}{\rm W}(x_{0}),{\bf n}({\rm R})\rangle}=\varepsilon\alpha e^{-\alpha{\rm R}^{2}}>0,

(7.1)

where
{\bf n}({\rm R})
is the outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}.\hfill\mbox{$\quad{}_{\Box}$}

Remark 7.1

In fact, straightforward computations show that there exists
\delta>0
for which

\liminf_{x\rightarrow x_{0}\atop\angle(x_{0}-x,{\bf n}({\rm R}))<\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta}\dfrac{\big(u^{*}-v_{*}\big)(x_{0})-\big(u^{*}-v_{*}\big)(x)}{|x-x_{0}|}>0.

\quad{}_{\Box}

Remark 7.2

Even in the broader case in which
\alpha
is large enough, assumption (
1.7
) is more restrictive to (
1.3
).
\quad{}_{\Box}

Theorem 7.2
(Strong Maximum Principle without coercivity term)

Assume (
1.6
)
and (
1.7
). Let
u
be a discontinuous subsolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u=0,\quad x\in\Omega

and
v
be a discontinuous supersolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}v=0,\quad x\in\Omega,

Then if
u^{*}-v_{*}
achieves its maximum in the interior of
\Omega
, then

u^{*}-v_{*}
is constant in
\Omega
.

Before the proof of Theorem
7.2
we collect some useful results. First we recall a property of any convex function,
\psi
, defined in
{\cal O}
: it attains a local maximum at
z_{0}\in{\cal O}
then
{\rm D}\psi(z_{0})={\bf 0}
. More precisely

Lemma 7.2
(DM)

Let
\psi
be a function achieving a local maximum at some
z_{0}\in{\cal O}
. Assume that there exists a function
\widehat{\psi}
defined in
{\cal O}
such that
\widehat{\psi}(z_{0})=0,~\Psi=\psi+\widehat{\psi}
is convex on
{\cal O}
and

\widehat{\psi}(x)\leq{\rm K}|x-z_{0}|^{2},\quad x\in{\cal O}~\hbox{with $|x-z_{0}|$ small,}

for some constant
{\rm K}>0
. Then the function
\psi
is differentiable at
z_{0}
and
{\rm D}\psi(z_{0})={\bf 0}
.

Proof
. By simplicity we can take
z_{0}=0\in{\cal O}
. By applying the Convex Separation Theorem there exists
{\bf p}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{{\rm N}}
such that

\Psi(x)\geq\Psi(0)+\langle{\bf p},x\rangle=\psi(0)+\langle{\bf p},x\rangle,\quad x\in{\cal O},~\hbox{with $|x|$ small}.

Then we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi(x)&\hskip-5.69046pt=\Psi(x)-\widehat{\psi}(x)\geq\psi(0)+\langle{\bf p},x\rangle-{\rm K}|x|^{2}\\[4.26773pt]
&\hskip-5.69046pt\geq\psi(x)+\langle{\bf p},x\rangle-{\rm K}|x|^{2},\quad x\in{\cal O}~\hbox{with $|x|$ small}\end{array}

(7.2)

whence

\langle{\bf p},x\rangle\leq{\rm K}|x|^{2},\quad x\in{\cal O}\hbox{ with $|x|$ small}.

For
\tau>0
small enough we can choose
x=\tau{\bf p}\in{\cal O}
and
\tau{\rm K}<1
, for which

\tau|{\bf p}|^{2}\leq{\rm K}\tau^{2}|{\bf p}|^{2}.

Therefore
{\bf p}={\bf 0}
. Finally, (
7.2
) leads to

0\geq\psi(x)-\psi(0)\geq-{\rm K}|x|^{2},\quad x\in{\cal O}~\hbox{ with $|x|$ small},

and the result follows.
\quad{}_{\Box}

Remark 7.3

As it was pointed out the result is immediate if
\psi
is convex for which we can choose
\widehat{\psi}\equiv~0.\hfill\mbox{$\quad{}_{\Box}$}

As is in the uniform ellipticity case, the proof of Theorem
7.2
rests on a contradiction generated on the differentiability at a local interior maximum. It is more direct whenever (DM) property holds. We make it by regularizing
u^{*}
and
v_{*}
by sup- and inf- convolution

u_{\varepsilon}(x)\doteq\sup_{y\in\Omega}\left\{u^{*}(y)-\dfrac{|y-x|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad v^{\varepsilon}(x)\doteq\inf_{y\in\Omega}\left\{v_{*}(y)+\dfrac{|y-x|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right\}.

By construction,
u_{\varepsilon}
and
v^{\varepsilon}
are semiconvex and semiconcave function, respectively, in a slightly smaller domain (still denote by
\Omega
in the sequel for simplicity) where we argue. Moreover, the magical properties of the regularization implies that they are sub and supersolution of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u=0,\quad x\in\Omega.

respectively (see
[
11
]
). In fact, each of them satisfies the Strong Maximum Principle as follows from

Corollary 7.1

Assume (
1.7
). Let
w
be a subsolution (respectively supersolution) of

-{\bf trace}~{\cal A}(\cdot){\rm D}^{2}u=0,\quad x\in\Omega.

Assume that
w
achieves a local maximum (resp. a local minimum) at
z_{0}\in\Omega
then
w
is constant in a neighborhood of
z_{0}
.

Proof
. For simplicity we only treat the case of a subsolution. First of all

w_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\geq w^{*}(z_{0})\geq u^{*}(y)-\dfrac{|y-x|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\quad\hbox{for all $y,~x$}

w_{\varepsilon}(z_{0})\geq w_{\varepsilon}(x)
, thus

z_{0}

must be constant in a ball
{\bf B}(z_{0})
. Then denoting

the point such that

w_{\varepsilon}(x)=w^{*}(\widehat{x})-\dfrac{|\widehat{x}-x|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}

Therefore

and
w^{*}(z_{0})=w^{*}(x),~x\in{\bf B}(z_{0}).\hfill\mbox{$\quad{}_{\Box}$}

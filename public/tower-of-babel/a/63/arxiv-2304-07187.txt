A Proof that Coarse Correlated Equilibrium Implies Nash Equilibrium in Two-Player Zero-Sum Games
Revan MacQueen

Abstract
We give a simple proof of the well-known result that the marginal strategies of a coarse correlated equilibrium form a Nash equilibrium in two-player zero-sum games. A corollary of this fact is that no-external-regret learning algorithms that converge to the set of coarse correlated equilibria will also converge to Nash equilibria in two-player zero-sum games. We show an approximate version: that ϵ \epsilon -coarse correlated equilibria imply 2 ​ ϵ 2\epsilon -Nash equilibria.

Background

It is well-known that the marginal strategies of a coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE) are Nash equilibria in two-player zero-sum games. We present a simple proof of this fact, which may be useful to others.

This fact is useful, since a no-external-regret learning algorithm’s average strategy profile will approach the set of CCE
(
Greenwald and Jafari 2003
)
, and so will compute an approximate Nash equilibrium in two-player zero-sum games. Hence, the marginal strategies of a CCE in two-player zero-sum games have the desirable properties of Nash equilibrium, including bounded exploitability
(
Fudenberg and Tirole 1991
)
.

2
The Proof

Here we show a simple proof that CCE imply Nash in two-player zero-sum games. We begin showing that if a player
i
deviates from their CCE recommendations, if
-i
continues to play their CCE recommendations, this is equivalent to
-i
playing their marginal strategy for the CCE.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mu\in\Delta(\mathrm{P})
, then in a two-player game, for any deviation
s_{i}
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(s_{i},\rho_{-i})\right]=u_{i}(s_{i},s^{\mu}_{-i})
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(s_{i},\rho_{-i})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}}\sum_{\rho_{-i}\in\mathrm{P}_{-i}}s_{i}(\rho_{i})\mu_{-i}(\rho_{-i})u_{i}(\rho_{i},\rho_{-i}),

where
\mu_{-i}(\rho_{-i})\doteq\sum_{\rho_{-i}\in\mathrm{P}_{-i}}\mu(\rho_{i},\rho_{-i})
. Note, however, that
\mu_{-i}(\rho_{-i})=s^{\mu}_{-i}(\rho_{-i})
so

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(s_{i},\rho_{-i})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}}\sum_{\rho_{-i}\in\mathrm{P}_{-i}}s_{i}(\rho_{i})s^{\mu}_{-i}(\rho_{-i})u_{i}(\rho_{i},\rho_{-i})

\displaystyle=u_{i}(s_{i},s^{\mu}_{-i}).

∎

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\mu
is an
\epsilon
-CCE of a two-player constant-sum game, then
|\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})|\leq\epsilon

Proof.

Suppose not, then
\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})\right|>\epsilon
which means either

\displaystyle u_{i}(s^{\mu})-\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]>\epsilon

(1)

or

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})>\epsilon.

(2)

Consider (
1
). By Lemma
2.1
we have
u_{i}(s^{\mu})=\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(s_{i}^{\mu},\rho_{-i})\right]
, which means

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(s_{i}^{\mu},\rho_{-i})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]>\epsilon,

which contradicts the fact that
\mu
is an
\epsilon
-CCE, since
s^{\mu}_{i}
is a deviation that is more than
\epsilon
-profitable for player
i
. Next, consider (
2
); since the game is two-player zero-sum, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})>\epsilon

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{-i}(\rho)\right]-(-u_{-i}(s^{\mu}))>\epsilon

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle u_{-i}(s^{\mu})-\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{-i}(\rho)\right]>\epsilon.

At this point we may repeat the steps above to show that
\mu
is not an
\epsilon
-CCE, since
s^{\mu}_{-i}
is a deviation that is more than
\epsilon
-profitable for player
-i
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\mu

\epsilon
-CCE of a two-player constant-sum game
G
, then

2\epsilon
-Nash equilibrium.

Proof.

Choose player

or
u_{i}(s^{\mu})<\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]

\epsilon

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},\rho_{-i})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]\leq\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}.

But since
u_{i}(s^{\mu})\geq\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},\rho_{-i})\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})\leq\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}.

Which by Lemma
2.1
means

\displaystyle u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},s^{\mu}_{-i})-u_{i}(s^{\mu})\leq\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}.

Thus
s^{\mu}
is an
\epsilon
-Nash. Next, suppose
u_{i}(s^{\mu})<\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]
. By Proposition
2.2
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})\leq\epsilon

(3)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]\leq u_{i}(s^{\mu})+\epsilon.

(4)

Then, starting from the definition of
\epsilon
-CCE and applying (
4

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},\rho_{-i})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho)\right]\leq\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},\rho_{-i})\right]-(u_{i}(s^{\mu})+\epsilon)\leq\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho\sim\mu}\left[u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},\rho_{-i})\right]-u_{i}(s^{\mu})\leq 2\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}.

By Lemma
2.1

\displaystyle u_{i}(\rho_{i}^{\prime},s^{\mu}_{-i})-u_{i}(s^{\mu})\leq 2\epsilon\quad\forall\rho_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}_{i}.

∎

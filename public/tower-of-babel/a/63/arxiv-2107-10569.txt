Schatten classes and commutators of Riesz transform on Heisenberg group and applications
Zhijie Fan, Michael Lacey, Ji Li

Abstract
. We study commutators with the Riesz transforms on the Heisenberg group ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} . The Schatten norm of these commutators is characterized in terms of Besov norms of the symbol. This generalizes the classical Euclidean results of Peller, Janson–Wolff and Rochberg–Semmes. The method of proof extends the earlier methods, allowing us to address not just the Riesz transforms, but also the Cauchy–Szegő projection and second order Riesz transforms on ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} among other settings.

47B10, 42B20, 43A85

1.
Introduction

The commutators with the Riesz transforms are bounded and compact on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1<p<\infty
, if and only if the symbol
b
is in the BMO space and VMO space. This is well known, see
[
9
,
39
]
. A finer property of the commutators
quantifies the Schatten norms, that is the
\ell^{p}
norm of the singular values.
This was studied by Peller, for the Hilbert transform on
\mathbb{R}

[
28
]
(see also
[
29
]
).
And in higher dimensions
by Janson–Wolff in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2

[
21
]
, and later on by Rochberg–Semmes
[
33
,
34
]
.
The Schatten norm is characterized by the symbol being in certain Besov spaces.
To summarize the known results are as follows. Let
H
denote the Hilbert transform and let
R_{\ell}
denote the
\ell
-th Riesz transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

•

If
n=1
and
0<p<\infty
, then
[b,H]
is in Schatten class
S^{p}
if and only if the symbol
b
is in the Besov space
B_{p,p}^{1/p}(\mathbb{R})
.
[
28
,
29
]
.

•

Suppose
n\geq 2
and
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. When
p>n
,
[b,R_{\ell}]\in S^{p}
if and only if
b\in B_{p,p}^{{n}/{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
; when
0<p\leq n
,
[b,R_{\ell}]\in S^{p}
if and only if
b
is a constant
[
21
,
34
]
.

Notice that the cases of dimensions
n=1
and
n>2
differ somewhat. This is due to the distinguished nature of the Hilbert transform,
particularly its close connection to analyticity. Similar results have been demonstrated in
[
14
]
for Szegő projection, big and little Hankel operators on the unit ball and Heisenberg group, in
[
2
]
for the big Hankel operator on Bergman space of the disk, and in
[
41
]
for Hankel operators on the Bergman space of the unit ball.

The Janson–Wolff inequality has bearing on the a quantised derivative of Alain Connes introduced in
[
10
, IV]
.
In this setting, the (weak) Schatten norm of the commutator is relevant
[
25
]
. See also some recent progresses in different settings
[
1
,
13
,
20
,
27
,
31
,
32
]
.

In this paper we extend the Janson–Wolff result to commutators of Riesz transforms on Heisenberg groups.
This requires us to revisit the methods of Janson–Wolff and Rochberg–Semmes,
replacing Fourier analytic methods they used with more robust real variable arguments.
Our result not only recovers the result of Janson–Wolff
[
21
]
and Rochberg–Semmes
[
34
]
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

, with the quantitative estimate of the Schatten norm (which was not showed explicitly before), but also opens the door to the study of commutator with certain Calderón–Zygmund operators in other important settings beyond

. Examples of such Calderón–Zygmund operators include

(1)

the Cauchy–Szegő projection from Siegel upper half space to its boundary (identified with Heisenberg group), see
[

, Chapter 12, Section 2.4]
and
[
14
]
;

(2)

certain second order Riesz transforms, such as the well-known Beurling–Ahlfors operator on the complex plane

and second order Riesz transforms on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Details will be provided in the last section;

(3)

Riesz transforms in the Bessel setting
[
4
,
19
]
and Neumann Laplacian setting
[
26
]
, which will be addressed in subsequent papers.

To be more explicit on our result,
let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be Heisenberg group. It is a nilpotent Lie group with underlying manifold
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}=\{[z,t]:z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\}
, the multiplication law

(1.1)

\displaystyle[z,t][z^{\prime},t^{\prime}]=[z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},t][z_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,z_{n}^{\prime},t^{\prime}]:=\Big[z_{1}+z_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,z_{n}+z_{n}^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+2{\rm Im}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}\overline{z}_{j}\Big)\Big]

and
the homogeneous norm
\rho(g)
(details on the notation will be given in Section
2
).

For any
\ell=1,2,\ldots,2n
, let
R_{\ell}
be the Riesz transform on Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}

and the commutator with
R_{\ell}
is defined as follows.

[b,R_{\ell}](f)(x):=b(x)R_{\ell}(f)(x)-R_{\ell}(bf)(x).

Next we recall the definition of the homogeneous Besov space in the following form.

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose
1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\alpha<1
. Let
f\in L_{\rm loc}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Then we say that
f
belongs to Besov space
B_{p,q}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
if

\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{\|f(g\cdot)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{q}}{\rho(g)^{2n+2+q\alpha}}dg<\infty.}

We recall the definition of the Schatten class
S^{p}
. Note that if
T
is any compact operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, then
T^{*}T

is compact, symmetric and positive. It is diagonalizable. For
0<p<\infty
, we say that
T\in S^{p}
if
\{\lambda_{n}\}\in\ell^{p}
, where
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is the sequence of square roots of eigenvalues of
T^{*}T
(counted according to multiplicity).

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
0<p<\infty
and
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Then for any
\ell\in\{1,2,\cdots,2n\}
, one has
[b,R_{\ell}]\in S^{p}

if and only if

(1)

b\in B_{p,p}^{\frac{2n+2}{p}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, if
p>2n+2
; in this case we have
\|b\|_{B_{p,p}^{\frac{2n+2}{p}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\approx\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}
;

(2)

b
is a constant, if
0<p\leq 2n+2
.

In the Euclidean setting, the Riesz transforms have an explicit form,
\Omega(x)\over|x|^{n}
where
n
is the dimension of the underlying space and
\Omega(x)
is a smooth homogeneous function of degree 0.
This leads to arguments highly dependent on the form of the kernel.
However, the Riesz transform kernel on Heisenberg group has no such convenient form.
And, our argument depends upon recent developments.
A pointwise lower bound of the Riesz transform kernel on stratified Lie groups (which covers the Heisenberg group) was established in
[
11
]
to characterize the boundedness of the commutator of Riesz transform.
We have to further develop this theme to prove the main result. See Theorem
3.1
below.
Indeed, Theorem
3.1
is key to our proof, a canonical ‘non-degenerate’ condition.
It depends upon the kernel of the Riesz transforms only being zero on a set of zero measure,
and being suitably large.
In addition, the property aligns well with the martingale structure on Heisenberg groups.
Verifying this property should be central in settings beyond the Euclidean. We return to this point in §
6
.

Our proof uses a natural martingale structure on the Heisenberg group, and an associated Haar basis,
and crucially a notion of
nearly weakly orthogonal
due to Rochberg–Semmes
[
34
]
.
It is very well adapted to the analysis of Schatten norms in Harmonic Analysis settings. See (
2.9
).
As with other methods, the median of the symbol on the atoms of the martingale is important.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the tilings and Haar Basis on Heisenberg group and characterization of Schatten class. In Section
3
we recall the basic property for Riesz transform and then prove the pointwise lower bound for the Riesz kernel (Theorem
3.1
). In Sections
4
and
5
, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
for the cases
p>2n+2
and
0<p\leq 2n+2
, respectively, which lies in Propositions
4.17
,
4.21
and
5.13
. In Section
6
, we provide the applications of our approach to some well-known Calderón–Zygmund operators beyond the Euclidean setting.

. Other norms (such as the Besov norm or Schatten norm) are given explicitly in the context. The
indicator function of a subset

2.
Preliminaries on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a Heisenberg group, which is a nilpotent Lie group with underlying manifold
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}=\{[z,t]:z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\}
and multiplication law as in (
1.1
).
Then the identity of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the origin and the inverse is given by
[z,t]^{-1}=[-z,-t]
. In addition to the Heisenberg group multiplication law, for each positive number
\lambda
, non-isotropic dilations
\delta_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are given by

\delta_{\lambda}(g):=\delta_{\lambda}[z,t]:=[\lambda z,\lambda^{2}t].

Besides, the norm structure
\rho
on
\mathbb{H}
is defined by

\rho(g)=\rho([z,t])=\max\{|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}},|t|^{1/2}\},

where
|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{2}
. The Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
coincides with Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. For any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
|E|
denotes its Haar measure. It is direct to see that
\rho(g^{-1})=\rho(-g)=\rho(g)
and
\rho(\delta_{\lambda}(g))=\lambda\rho(g)
.

The
2n+1
vector fields

X_{\ell}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}-2y_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ Y_{\ell}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{\ell}}+2x_{\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ \mathcal{T}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ \ell=1,2,\cdots,n

form a natural basis for the Lie algebra of left-invariant vector field on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. For convenience, we set
X_{n+\ell}:=Y_{\ell},\ell=1,2,\cdots,n
and set
X_{2n+1}:=\mathcal{T}
. The standard sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
on the Heisenberg group is defined by
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2n}X_{\ell}^{2}
. For any multi-index
I=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{2n+1})\in\mathbb{N}^{2n+1}
, we set
X^{I}:=X_{1}^{i_{1}}X_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots X_{2n+1}^{i_{2n+1}}
and further set

|I|:=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2n+1}\quad\textup{ and}\quad\ d(I):=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{2n}+2i_{2n+1}.

The integers
I
and
d(I)
are said to be the topological degree and homogeneous degree of the differential
X^{I}
, respectively.

2.1.
Tiles on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

We recall the metrics and tilings in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
summarized in
[
7
]
.
We shall use the
gauge distance

(2.1)

\displaystyle d(g,g^{\prime}):=\left\|g^{\prime-1}\cdot g\right\|=\left\|g^{-1}\cdot g^{\prime}\right\|,\qquad\forall g,g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

where

is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|(z,t)\right\|:=\max\left\{|x_{1}|,|y_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|,|y_{n}|,|t|^{1/2}\right\}\qquad\forall(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

It is easy to see that

is equivalent to the homogeneous norm

for a discussion.
We write

for the ball in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with center

and radius

We also use balls in the (algebraic) center of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which may be identified with

Tubes are sets of the form

, which are images of products of balls in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
under the multiplication in (

).
We recall that
T(g,r,s)
is defined as

(2.3)

T(g,r,s):=g\cdot B(o,r)\cdot B^{*}(0,s)=B(g,r)\cdot B^{*}(0,s).

We use the work of
[
37
,
38
]
on self-similar tilings to find a “nice” decomposition of

, analogous to the decomposition of

into dyadic cubes in classical harmonic analysis, and describe an analogue of a lemma of Journé
[
23
]

We identify
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
|z|_{\infty}

Theorem 2.1

(
[

,
38
]
)

There is a measurable function

\frac{1}{4n(n+1)}\leq f(z)\leq\frac{2n+1}{4n(n+1)}\qquad\forall z\in Q_{0},

The definitions of

and the metrics that we use show that

\displaystyle T_{o}\subset\left\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:|z|_{\infty}\leq 1/2,|t|\leq 3/8\right\}\subseteq\bar{B}(o,1/2)\cdot\bar{B}^{*}(o,1/8)=\bar{T}(o,1/2,1/8),

We note that

Definition 2.5
.

We define

\mathfrak{T}_{0}:=\{g\cdot T_{o}:g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}\},\qquad\mathfrak{T}_{j}:=\delta_{(2n+1)^{j}}\mathfrak{T}_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{T}:=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{T}_{j}.

We call the sets

If

And we define

(
[

,
37
]
)

Let
\mathfrak{T}_{j}
and

Then the following hold:

(1)

(3)

for each

(4)

Every tile is a dilate and translate of the basic tile

Hence each tile in

2.3.
Characterization of Schatten class

The Schatten norm is defined in a non-linear fashion. Estimating it above, and below, is not necessarily straight forward.
Operators with kernels, such as commutators, admit general upper bounds in terms of norms on the kernels.
These general facts are recalled, and used, in §
4.2
.

Characterizations of Schatten norms for general operators are well known, and frequently expressed in terms of
supremums, or infimums, over all choices of orthonormal bases for the Hilbert space in question.

Rochberg and Semmes
[
34
]
proposed a notion of
nearly weakly orthogonal (NWO)
sequences of functions.
This notion is closely connected to Carleson measures. For our purposes, we do not need to recall the full definition
of NWO sequences. With the development of tiles in §
2.1
, we have the inequality below, for any
bounded compact operator
A
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
:

(2.9)

\Bigl[\sum_{T\in\mathfrak{T}}\lvert\langle Ae_{T},f_{T}\rangle\rvert^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\lesssim\lVert A\rVert_{S^{p}},

where
\{e_{T}\}_{T}
and
\{f_{T}\}_{T}
are function sequences satisfying
\lvert e_{T}\rvert,\lvert f_{T}\rvert\leq\lvert T\rvert^{-1/2}\chi_{T}
.
This inequality can be found in
[
34
, (1.10), §3]
.

3.
Lower bound of the Riesz transform kernel on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

For any
\ell=1,2,\ldots,2n
, The the Riesz transform on Heisenberg groups
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by
R_{\ell}=X_{\ell}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1/2}
.
It is well known that the heat kernel
p_{h}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
has this
form (cf.
[
16
]
): for
g=[z,t]\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle p_{h}(g)=\frac{1}{2(4\pi h)^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp{\Big(\frac{\lambda}{4h}(t\,\char 16\relax-|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\coth{\lambda})\Big)}\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\sinh{\lambda}}\Big)^{n}\,d\lambda,\quad\char 16\relax^{2}=-1.

Moreover,
p_{h}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
satisfies (c.f. for example
[
15
, Equation (1.73)]
)

(3.2)

\displaystyle p_{h}(g)=h^{-n-1}p(\delta_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}}(g)),\qquad\forall h>0,\ g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

The kernel of the
\ell^{{\rm th}}
Riesz transform
R_{\ell}
(
1\leq\ell\leq 2n
) is written simply as
K_{\ell}(g)
. It is well-known that

K_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
, and it satisfies the scaling condition

(3.3)

\displaystyle\ K_{\ell}(\delta_{r}(g))=r^{-2n-2}K_{\ell}(g),\quad\forall g\neq o,\ r>0,\ 1\leq\ell\leq 2n.

Indeed, this follows from the relationship between the Riesz transform and heat kernel (
3.2
) given by

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(g)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}X_{\ell}p_{h}(g)\,dh=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-n-2}\left(X_{\ell}p\right)(\delta_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}}(g))\,dh.

We recall that
by the classical estimates for heat kernel and its derivations on stratified groups (see for example
[
40
]
), it is well-known that (e.g.
[
15
]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k}(2n+1)^{(2n+2)k}\|b-E_{k+1}(b)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{k}(2n+1)^{(2n+2)k}\|E_{k+1}(b)-E_{k}(b)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k}(2n+1)^{(2n+2)(k-1)}\|b-E_{k}(b)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{k}(2n+1)^{(2n+2)k}\|E_{k+1}(b)-E_{k}(b)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-(2n+2)/p}\left(\sum_{k}(2n+1)^{(2n+2)k}\|b-E_{k}(b)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{k}(2n+2)^{(2n+2)k}\|E_{k+1}(b)-E_{k}(b)\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=:{\textup{Term}_{1}}+{\textup{Term}_{2}}.

Since

, we see that
{\textup{Term}_{1}}
can be absorbed into

.
Lemma
4.2
controls
{\textup{Term}_{2}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
2n+2<p<\infty
and suppose that
b\in L_{{\rm loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|b\|_{B_{p,p}^{\frac{2n+2}{p}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq C\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}.

Proof.

To begin with, we note that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|b(g)-b({\hat{g}})|^{p}}{d(g,{\hat{g}})^{2(2n+2)}}dgd\hat{g}\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(2n+1)^{2(2n+2)k}\iint_{d(g,{\hat{g}})\leq(2n+1)^{-k-1}}|b(g)-b({\hat{g}})|^{p}dgd{\hat{g}}.

Hence, it suffices to show that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=L}^{M}(2n+1)^{2(2n+2)k}\iint_{d(g,\hat{g})\leq(2n+1)^{-k-1}}|b(g)-b({\hat{g}})|^{p}dgd{\hat{g}}\leq C\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}^{p},

where
C
is a constant independent of
L<M\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Recall that a tile
T
in
\mathfrak{T}_{-k}
is approximately a Heisenberg ball of radius
(2n+1)^{-k}
. Fix a Heisenberg ball
B
centered at the origin with radius
(2n+1)^{-L+A}
for a large fixed integer
A
, and then denote
b_{\tilde{g}}(g):=b(\tilde{g}g)
for
\tilde{g}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then
the left-hand side of (
4.19
) is dominated by a constant times

\displaystyle{1\over|B|}\int_{B}\sum_{k=L}^{M}\sum_{T\in\mathfrak{T}_{-k}}

\displaystyle(2n+1)^{2(2n+2)k}\int_{T}\int_{T}|b_{\tilde{g}}(g)-b_{\tilde{g}}({\hat{g}})|^{p}\,d{\hat{g}}\,dg\,d\tilde{g}

\displaystyle\lesssim{1\over|B|}\int_{B}\sum_{k=L}^{M}\sum_{T\in\mathfrak{T}_{-k}}(2n+1)^{(2n+2)k}\int_{T}|b_{\tilde{g}}(g)-E_{k}(b_{\tilde{g}})(g)|^{p}\,dg\,d\tilde{g}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim{1\over|B|}\int_{B}C\|[b_{\tilde{g}},R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}^{p}\,d\tilde{g},

where in the first inequality we added and subtracted the term
E_{k}(b_{\tilde{g}})
by noting that for
g,\hat{g}\in T\in\mathfrak{T}_{-k}

E_{k}(b_{\tilde{g}})(g)=E_{k}(b_{\tilde{g}})(\hat{g})
, and in the second inequality we use Lemma
4.15
. Next, as the Riesz transform is convolution,
\|[b_{\tilde{g}},R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}=\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}
, we obtain that the right-hand side of (
4.1
) is bounded by

C\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}^{p}
. Hence, (
4.19
) holds.

Therefore, the proof of Proposition
4.17
is complete.

4.2.
Proof of the sufficient condition

Proposition 4.21
.

Suppose
\ell\in\{1,2,\cdots,2n\}
,
2n+2<p<\infty
and
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. If
b\in B_{p,p}^{\frac{2n+2}{p}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, then
[b,R_{\ell}]\in S^{p}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[
21
]
, which relies upon general estimates for Schatten norms of integral operators.
For the convenience of the readers, we briefly sketch the proof here. We first recall that
[b,R_{\ell}]
is compact
[
8
]
when
b\in B_{p,p}^{\frac{2n+2}{p}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\subset{\rm VMO}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Note that Russo (
[
35
]
) proved that for general measure space
(X,\mu)
, if
p>2
and
K(x,y)\in L^{2}(X\times X)
, then the integral operator
T
associated to the kernel
K(x,y)
satisfies the following bound:

\displaystyle\|T\|_{S^{p}}\leq\|K\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime}}}^{1/2}\|K^{*}\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime}}}^{1/2},

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate index of
p
,
K^{*}(x,y)=\overline{K(y,x)}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime}}}
denotes the mixed-norm:

\|K\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime}}}:=\big\|\|K(x,y)\|_{L^{p}(dx)}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(dy)}.

Later on Goffeng (
[
18
]
) showed that the condition
K(x,y)\in L^{2}(X\times X)
in the above statement can be removed.

Moreover, Janson–Wolff (
[
21
, Lemma 1 and Lemma 2]
) extended the above statement to the corresponding weak-type version general measure space
(X,\mu)
: if
p>2
and
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
, then

(4.22)

\displaystyle\|T\|_{S^{p,\infty}}\leq\|K\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}^{1/2}\|K^{*}\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}^{1/2},

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}
denotes the mixed-norm:

\|K\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}:=\big\|\|K(x,y)\|_{L^{p}(dx)}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime},\infty}(dy)}.

Next, back to our setting on Heisenberg group, we note that by weak-type Young’s inequality, for
1/q=1-2/p
,

\displaystyle\left\|(b(g)-b(\hat{g}))K(g,\hat{g})\right\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{b(g)-b(\hat{g})}{d(g,\hat{g})^{2n+2}}\right\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{b(g)-b(\hat{g})}{d(g,\hat{g})^{2(2n+2)/p}}\right\|_{L^{p},L^{p}}\left\|\frac{1}{d(g,\hat{g})^{(2n+2)(1-2/p)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty},L^{q,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{B_{p,p}^{(2n+2)/p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Similarly,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\left\|(b(g)-b(\hat{g}))\overline{K(\hat{g},g)}\right\|_{L^{p},L^{p^{\prime},\infty}}\leq C\|b\|_{B_{p,p}^{(2n+2)/p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},

Combining the inequalities (
4.2
), (
4.24
) and then applying the weak-type Russo’s inequality (
4.22
), we see that

\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p,\infty}}\leq C\|b\|_{B_{p,p}^{(2n+2)/p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Since this inequality holds for all
2n+2<p<\infty
, we can apply the interpolation
(S^{p_{1}},S^{p_{2}})_{\theta_{p}}=S^{p}
and
(B_{p_{1},p_{1}}^{(2n+2)/p_{1}},B_{p_{2},p_{2}}^{(2n+2)/p_{2}})_{\theta_{p}}=B_{p,p}^{(2n+2)/p}
, where
\frac{1-\theta_{p}}{p_{1}}+\frac{\theta_{p}}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}
, to obtain that

\displaystyle\|[b,R_{\ell}]\|_{S^{p}}\leq C\|b\|_{B_{p,p}^{(2n+2)/p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

This finishes the proof of sufficient condition for the case
2n+2<p<\infty
.
∎

To formulate our argument simplicity, we will usually identity

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a positive integer

Consider first

2.6

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

Lemma 5.3

Lemma 5.10
.

A function
b\in L_{{\rm loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is constant if

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{h\in B(o,1)}\left\|\left\{\fint_{T}\fint_{T}\left|E_{k+B_{0}}(\tau^{h}b)(g^{\prime})-E_{k+B_{0}}(\tau^{h}b)(g^{\prime\prime})\right|dg^{\prime}dg^{\prime\prime}\right\}_{T\in\mathfrak{T}}\right\|_{\ell^{2n+2}}<+\infty.

, and both

and

vary. And

denotes translation by

Proof.

The assumption is that

Denote
\psi_{\epsilon}(g):=\epsilon^{-2n-2}\psi(\delta_{\epsilon^{-1}}g)

\epsilon

The point is that

Proposition 5.13
.

Suppose
b\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
p=2n+2
. Then
for any
\ell\in\{1,2,\cdots,2n\}
, the commutator
[b,R_{\ell}]\in S^{2n+2}
if and only if
b
is a constant.

Proof.

A constant function
b
is associated with the zero commutator. So, we only consider the
direction in which we assume
[b,R_{\ell}]\in S^{2n+2}
. And, then, we need to verify that (
5.11
) holds.
That inequality has the supremum over translations. The Riesz transforms are themselves convolution operators,
so that it suffices to verify (
5.11
) without translations. That is,

(5.14)

\left\|\left\{\fint_{T}\fint_{T}\left|E_{k+B_{0}}(b)(g^{\prime})-E_{k+B_{0}}(b)(g^{\prime\prime})\right|dg^{\prime}dg^{\prime\prime}\right\}_{T\in\mathfrak{T}}\right\|_{\ell^{2n+2}}\lesssim\lVert[b,R_{\ell}]\rVert_{S^{2n+2}}<\infty.

(In the display,
T\in\mathfrak{T}_{k}
, and both
T
and
k
vary).

This is in fact a corollary to Lemma
4.2
, and is seen by way of a general remark.
For a random variable
Z
, we have for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\lVert Z-\mathbb{E}Z\rVert_{p}\simeq\lVert Z-Z^{\prime}\rVert_{p},

where
Z^{\prime}
is an independent copy of
Z
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\lVert Z-\mathbb{E}Z\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\lVert Z-\mathbb{E}Z^{\prime}\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert Z-Z^{\prime}\rVert_{p}\leq 2\lVert Z-\mathbb{E}Z\rVert_{p}.

The first inequality is by convexity and the second by the triangle inequality.

Thus, Lemma

\left\|\left\{\fint_{T}\left|E_{k+B_{0}}(b)(g)-E_{k}(b)(g)\right|\,dg\right\}_{T\in\mathfrak{T}}\right\|_{\ell^{2n+2}}\lesssim\lVert[b,R_{\ell}]\rVert_{S^{2n+2}}

6.
Applications

As stated in the introduction, our approach depends upon a standard non-degeneracy condition on the kernel of the
singular integral operator, and then on robust real variable techniques. (In particular, no Fourier analysis.)
The approach applies to the following non-Euclidean Calderón–Zygmund operators.

(1) The Cauchy–Szegő projection
\mathcal{C}

[
36
, Chapter 12, Section 2.4]
is an important singular integral on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
It recovers an analytic function in the Siegel upper half space from its boundary value.
Its restriction to the boundary is a convolution operator, that is,
\mathcal{C}(f)(g)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(g^{\prime})k_{CS}((g^{\prime})^{-1}g)dg^{\prime}
, and the convolution kernel
k_{CS}
is given by

(6.1)

k_{CS}(g)=\frac{c}{(|z|^{2}+\char 16\relax\,t)^{n+1}},\quad\char 16\relax^{2}=-1,\qquad\forall g=(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

It is well-known that this
k_{CS}
is a Calderón–Zygmund kernel. From the explicit kernel, we see that the non-degeneracy condition in our Theorem
3.1
holds for
\mathcal{C}
. Hence, Theorem
1.1
holds for
[b,\mathcal{C}]
. This recovers the Theorem A obtained by Feldman–Rochberg
[
14
]
where they relied on the Cayley transform and Fourier transform.

(2) Second order Riesz transforms appear naturally in the study of PDEs (see for instance
[
17
]
) and have been extensively studied in literature. They are mostly interpreted as iterations of Riesz transforms and their adjoints, or second derivatives of the fundamental solution operator for the Laplacian:
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(-\Delta)^{-1}
. On Euclidean spaces, second order Riesz transforms are well understood as Calderón–Zygmund singular integrals and have bounded
L^{p}
norm for
1<p<\infty
.

(2a) A particular interesting example is the classical Beurling–Ahlfors operator
\mathcal{B}
on the complex plane defined by (see for example
[
3
,
30
]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(f)(z)

\displaystyle={\rm p.v.}{1\over\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}{f(w)\over\big(z-w\big)^{2}}\ dw.

Equivalently, we have

\mathcal{B}=\partial^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1},

where
\partial={\partial\over\partial x_{1}}-\char 16\relax{\partial\over\partial x_{2}}
is the Cauchy–Riemann operator and
\Delta
is the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Note that the kernel of
\mathcal{B}
is homogeneous and smooth away from the diagonal. Hence, the Schatten class
[b,\mathcal{B}]
was covered by Rochberg–Semmes
[
34
]
. Our approach can also be applied to
[b,\mathcal{B}]
, to have the explicit quantitative estimate for the Schatten norm.

(2b) Second order Riesz transform
\mathcal{T}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(recall that
\mathcal{T}={1\over 4}(X_{j}X_{n+j}-X_{n+j}X_{j})
).

By using functional calculus for
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
, it is direct to see that

\mathcal{T}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}e^{h\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\,dh,

which gives that the kernel
K
of
\mathcal{T}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
is a convolution kernel. Together with the size and smoothness estimates for the heat kernel
[
40
]
, we obtain that for
g\not=[0,0]
,

|K(g)|\lesssim{1\over\rho(g)^{2n+2}}\quad{\rm and}\quad|X_{\ell}K(g)|\lesssim{1\over\rho(g)^{2n+3}}

for
\ell=1,2,...,2n
. Hence,
\mathcal{T}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We now verify the
non-degeneracy condition in our Theorem
3.1
.

We follow the idea in Section 7 in
[
12
]
.
Recall that (
[
16
]
, see also Section 3.1) the explicit expression of heat kernel on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
as follows: for
g=[z,t]\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle p_{h}(g)={1\over 2(4\pi h)^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big({\lambda\over 4h}\big(\char 16\relax t-|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\coth\lambda\big)\Big)\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}d\lambda,\quad\char 16\relax^{2}=-1.

For any
g=[z,t]\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, by using the explicit expression of the heat kernel above and by Fubini’s theorem, we have that for
g\not=[0,0]
,

\displaystyle K(g)

\displaystyle={1\over 2(4\pi)^{n+1}}{\partial\over\partial t}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big({\lambda\over 4h}\big(\char 16\relax t-|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\coth\lambda\big)\Big)\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}\ d\lambda\ dh

\displaystyle={1\over 2(4\pi)^{n+1}}{\partial\over\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-n-1}\exp\Big({\lambda\over 4h}\big(\char 16\relax t-|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\coth\lambda\big)\Big)dh\ \Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{1}{\partial\over\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\lambda\coth\lambda-\char 16\relax\lambda t\big)^{-n}\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\lambda\coth\lambda-\char 16\relax\lambda t\big)^{-n-1}\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}\lambda\ d\lambda,

where in the next to the last equality we applied Cauchy integral formula to deform the ray on right-half complex plane
\mathbb{C}_{+}
into the real axis. Here we also note that

(6.3)

\displaystyle C_{2}=-n\,\char 16\relax C_{1}=-\frac{n\,\char 16\relax}{8\pi^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-n-1}e^{-s^{-1}}ds\neq 0.

Observe that

\displaystyle|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\lambda\coth\lambda-\char 16\relax\lambda t

\displaystyle={\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}d_{K}^{2}(g)\bigg({|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}\over d_{K}^{2}(g)}\cosh\lambda-\char 16\relax{t\over d_{K}^{2}(g)}\sinh\lambda\bigg)={\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}d_{K}^{2}(g)\cosh(\lambda-\char 16\relax\phi),

where
d_{K}
is the Korányi metric given by
d_{K}(g)=(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{4}+t^{2})^{1\over 4}
for
g=[z,t]\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and

(6.4)

\displaystyle-{\pi\over 2}\leq\phi=\phi(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}},t)\leq{\pi\over 2},\quad e^{\char 16\relax\phi}=d_{K}^{-2}(g)(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}+\char 16\relax\,t).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle K(g)

\displaystyle=C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}d_{K}^{2}(g)\cosh(\lambda-\char 16\relax\phi)\bigg)^{-n-1}\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}\lambda\ d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{2}d_{K}^{-2n-2}(g)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda-\char 16\relax\phi)\big)^{-n-1}\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{-n-1}\Big({\lambda\over\sinh\lambda}\Big)^{n}\lambda\ d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{2}d_{K}^{-2n-2}(g)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda-\char 16\relax\phi)\big)^{-n-1}\ \sinh\lambda\ d\lambda

\displaystyle=C_{2}d_{K}^{-2n-2}(g)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-1}\ \sinh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)\ d\lambda,

where the last equality follows from Cauchy integral formula again. We now define

F(g):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-1}\ \sinh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)\ d\lambda.

Then we have
K(g)=C_{2}F(g)d_{K}^{-2n-2}(g)
. We now investigate the function

\mathfrak{F}(w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-1}\ \sinh(\lambda+w)\ d\lambda,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}.

Then we have
F(g)=\mathfrak{F}(\char 16\relax\phi)
with
g=[z,t]\not=0
and
\phi=\phi(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}},t)
such that
e^{\char 16\relax\phi}=d_{K}^{-2}(g)(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}^{2}+\char 16\relax\,t)
. Note that

\mathfrak{F}(w)
is analytic in some domain in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, which contains the line segment
[-{\pi\over 2}\char 16\relax,{\pi\over 2}\char 16\relax]
in the imaginary axis, and that
\mathfrak{F}({\pi\over 4}\char 16\relax)\not=0
.

Thus,

\mathfrak{F}(w)
has at most a finite number of zero points on
[-{\pi\over 2}\char 16\relax,{\pi\over 2}\char 16\relax]
, i.e., there exist
\{\phi_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{N}\subset[-{\pi\over 2},{\pi\over 2}]
such that
\mathfrak{F}(\char 16\relax\phi_{\ell})=0
. From the mapping in (
6.4
), we see that for each
\ell=1,\ldots,N
,
\phi_{\ell}

corresponds to a hyperplane
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
defined by

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}:=\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\ \phi_{\ell}=\phi(|z|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}},t)\}.

Let

\mathcal{H}=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{N}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}.

Then we see that
\{g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:F(g)=0\}\subset\mathcal{H}
, and that
\mathcal{H}
has measure zero.
Consequently, the measure of the set
\{g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:F(g)=0\}
is zero.

Hence, we see that the convolution kernel
K(g)
is homogeneous of degree
-2n-2
and that

K(g)=C_{2}F(g),\quad g\in\mathbb{S}^{n},

which is non-zero almost everywhere on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(the unit sphere in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
). Thus,
non-degeneracy condition in Theorem
3.1
holds.

(2c) Second order Riesz transform
X_{j}X_{k}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
j,k\in\{1,2,\ldots,2n\}
.

Again, by using functional calculus for
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
, it is direct to see that

X_{j}X_{k}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}X_{j}X_{k}e^{h\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}\,dh,

which together with the size and smoothness estimates for the heat kernel
[
40
]
, shows that
X_{j}X_{k}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
is a Calderón–Zygmund operator on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Denote the kernel of
X_{j}X_{k}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{H}})^{-1}
by
K_{j,k}(g)
. We now verify the
non-degeneracy condition in our Theorem
3.1
.

In fact, this follows from similar approach as we used in (2b). Without lost of generality, we take

X_{j}={\partial\over\partial x_{j}}+2x_{n+j}{\partial\over\partial t},\ \ j<n\quad{\rm and}\quad X_{k}={\partial\over\partial x_{k}}+2x_{n-k}{\partial\over\partial t},\ \ k>n.

Then based on the formula (
6.2
) for heat kernel, we get that for
g\not=[0,0]
,

\displaystyle K_{j,k}(g)=

\displaystyle\,d_{K}^{-2n-4}(g)\Big(F_{1}(g)+\char 16\relax F_{2}(g)+F_{3}(g)+\char 16\relax F_{4}(g)\Big),

where

\displaystyle F_{1}(g)

\displaystyle=C_{3}x_{j}x_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-2}\cosh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)^{2}\,d\lambda,

\displaystyle F_{2}(g)

\displaystyle=-C_{3}x_{n+j}x_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-2}\cosh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)\sinh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)^{2}\,d\lambda,

\displaystyle F_{3}(g)

\displaystyle=C_{4}x_{n-k}x_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-2}\ \cosh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)\sinh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)^{2}\ d\lambda,

\displaystyle F_{4}(g)

\displaystyle=-C_{4}x_{n-k}x_{n+j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\cosh(\lambda)\big)^{-n-2}\ \sinh(\lambda+\char 16\relax\phi)^{2}\ d\lambda,

with
\phi
defined as in (
6.4
),
C_{3}:=2n(2n+2)C_{1}
,
C_{4}:=2(2n+2)C_{2}
and
C_{1},C_{2}
are as in (
6.3
). By resorting to the analytic continuation as in (2b) and using the isolated zero point, we get that
K_{j,k}(g)\not=0
a.e.
g\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Thus,
non-degeneracy condition in Theorem
3.1
holds.

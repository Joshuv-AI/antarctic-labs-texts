1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the initial value problem for the derivative nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) equation

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=i\lambda\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}u),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

which was introduced in connection to the compressible magneto-hydrodynamic equation in the
presence of the Hall effect and the propagation of circular polarized Alfvén
waves in magnetized plasma (
[
17
,
18
]
).

Local and global well-posedness results for the Cauchy problem
(
1
) have been extensively analysed in Sobolev spaces
(see for instance

[
1
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
15
,
21
,
22
]
),
where the optimal results in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
were deduced by
H. Takaoka
[
22
]
;
H. Bahouri and G. Perelman
[
1
]
, respectively.

Motivated by the seminal work of T. Kato
[
14
]
, the solvability of many dispersive nonlinear equations
has also been recently studied in the weighted Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)
, aiming to
control better the decay at infinity of the solutions (see for example

[
2
,
7
,
12
,
13
,
19
,
20
]

and the references therein).
In particular in
[
3
,
4
]
the authors
analyzed the behaviour of solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger–Airy equation

\partial_{t}u+i\,a\,\partial_{x}^{2}u+b\,\partial_{x}^{3}u+i\,c\,|u|^{2}u+d\,|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u+e\,u^{2}\partial_{x}\overline{u}=0

in weighted Sobolev spaces, assuming
b\neq 0
and hence not covering the case of the DNLS. Inspired by the work of T. Ozawa
[
21
]
, our main goal is the following persistency property.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in(0,1]
and

u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)
.
Then, there exist
T>0
and a unique
solution
u
of the initial value problem (
1
) verifying that

(2)

u(\cdot,t)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx),\quad t\in(0,T].

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce the notation and recall some properties that will be often used in this work.

We write
A\lesssim B
when there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
if simultaneously
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
.

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
refers to the Schwartz space consisting on smooth functions whose derivatives are rapidly decreasing.

We denote the Fourier transform of a square-integrable function
f
by

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx,

which allows to define the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, of regularity
s\in\mathbb{R}
, via the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\Big)^{1/2}.

The inclusion

(3)

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}},\quad s\leq s^{\prime},

the product estimate

(4)

\|fg\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}\,\|g\|_{H^{s}},\quad s>1/2,

and the embedding

(5)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}},\quad s>1/2,

are standard properties that will be very helpful.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the fractional derivative
D_{x}^{\alpha}
as the Fourier multiplier given by

(D_{x}^{\alpha}f)^{\wedge}(\xi):=|\xi|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi).

Analogously, consider

\big((1+D_{x}^{2})^{\alpha}f\big)^{\wedge}(\xi):=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi).

Hence, Plancherel’s identity yields to

\|f\|_{H^{s}}\sim\|(1+D_{x}^{2})^{s/2}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\|D_{x}^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, the
L^{2}
-weighted space
L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)
is
given by

\|f\|_{L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)}=\||x|^{r}f\|_{L^{2}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}|x|^{2r}\,dx\Big)^{1/2}.

Notice the connection

\|f\|_{L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)}\sim\|D^{r}_{x}\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, for a function of two variables

f:\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

and
1\leq p,q<\infty
, let’s
define the mixed norm

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{p}_{x}}:=\Big(\int^{T}_{0}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{q/p}\,dt\Big)^{1/q},

with the usual modifications involving the essential supremum when
p=\infty

or
q=\infty
. It is convenient to introduce

(6)

{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|f|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}:=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+\|f\|_{L^{4}_{T}L^{\infty}_{x}},

which is a norm recurrently used along this paper.

Next, we turn our attention to the initial value problem for the linear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=0,&x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

whose solution can be written as

u(x,t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}u_{0}(x)
, where
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}
denotes the Fourier multiplier given by

(e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}u_{0})^{\wedge}(\xi):=e^{-it\xi^{2}}\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi).

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
16
, Th. 4.2]
) that

(7)

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}_{x}}dt\Big)^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}},

provided that

(8)

2\leq p\leq\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2}{q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}.

Furthermore (
[
16
, Corollary 4.1]
), for any
T>0
,

(9)

\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}f(\cdot,t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\Big\|^{q_{1}}_{L_{x}^{p_{1}}}dt\Big)^{1/{q_{1}}}\lesssim\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(\cdot,t^{\prime})\|^{q^{\prime}_{0}}_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}_{0}}}dt\Big)^{1/{q^{\prime}_{0}}},

for pairs
(p_{0},q_{0})
, and
(p_{1},q_{1})
satisfying the condition (
8
) and with conjugated exponents
p_{0}^{\prime}
,
q_{0}^{\prime}

such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{0}}+\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{q_{0}^{\prime}}=1.

In
[
20
, Lemma 2]
it was also established that, for any

r\in(0,1)
and
t>0
,

(10)

\||x|^{r}e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim t^{r/2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}+t^{r}\|D_{x}^{r}f\|_{L^{2}}+\||x|^{r}f\|_{L^{2}}.

3.
System of nonlinear Schrödinger equations

Consider the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) system of equations

(11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}\varphi+\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi=-i\lambda\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi},\\
i\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{x}^{2}\psi=i\lambda\psi^{2}\overline{\varphi},\\
\varphi(x,0)=\varphi_{0}(x),\\
\psi(x,0)=\psi_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

and also set the constraint

(12)

\psi=\partial_{x}\varphi+i\frac{\lambda}{2}|\varphi|^{2}\varphi.

The following remark connects the constrained NLS system
(
11
)-(
12
) with the DNLS equation (
1
)
(see for example
[
21
, pp. 147-148]
).

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be solutions of (
11
) with constraint (
12
) and

\varphi_{0}(x):=\exp\Big(-i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{x}|u_{0}(y)|^{2}dy\Big)u_{0}(x).

Then, if we define

u(x,t):=\exp\Big(i\lambda\int_{-\infty}^{x}|\varphi(y,t)|^{2}dy\Big)\varphi(x,t),

it solves the initial value problem (
1
). Conversely, if
u
is a solution to (
1
), it solves the constrained NLS system (
11
)-(
12
)

3.1.
Unconstrained NLS system

The next result is a modification of

[
21
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in(0,1]
and

\varphi_{0},\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx)
. Then, there exist
T>0
and unique solutions
\varphi
,
\psi
of the NLS system (
11
)
verifying that

(13)

\varphi(\cdot,t),\psi(\cdot,t)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(|x|^{2r}dx),\quad t\in(0,T].

Proof.

We start considering the system of integral equations

(14)

\varphi(x,t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\varphi_{0}(x)+i\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},

(15)

\psi(x,t)=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}\psi_{0}(x)+i\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(-\psi^{2}\overline{\varphi})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},

which can be combined using vector notation as

(16)

(\varphi,\psi)=\Phi(\varphi,\psi),

for

(17)

\Phi(\varphi,\psi):=e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})(x)+i\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi},-\psi^{2}\overline{\varphi})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}.

Notice that, by using Duhamel’s principle, any solution of the differential equations (
11
) is also a solution of the integral equations (
16
). The converse is not true in general. However, for initial data
\varphi_{0},\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
one has that a solution of (
16
) also satisfies the NLS system (
11
) (proceed as in
[
16
, Corollary 5.5]
).
Therefore, to prove this Proposition we are going to show the existence and uniqueness of solutions of (
16
) verifying the persistency property (
13
).

For some constants
a,T>0
, that will be conveniently fixed later, consider the space

X^{a}_{T}:=\{\varphi\text{ : }\|\varphi\|_{X_{T}}\leq a\},

endowed with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{X_{T}}:=\|\varphi\|_{Y_{T}}+\||x|^{r}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}},

where

\|\varphi\|_{Y_{T}}:={|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\varphi|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}+{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\partial_{x}\varphi|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}+{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\partial^{2}_{x}\varphi|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}

with
{|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\cdot|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|}
introduced in (
6
).
Moreover, we define

\|(\varphi,\psi)\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}}:=\|\varphi\|_{X_{T}}+\|\psi\|_{X_{T}},

and say that
(\varphi,\psi)\in X^{a}_{T}\times X^{a}_{T}
provided that

\|(\varphi,\psi)\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}}\leq 2a.

By the Banach fixed-point theorem, it is enough to establish that the mapping
\Phi
given in (
17
) is a contraction on the complete metric space
X^{a}_{T}\times X^{a}_{T}
.

Step 1:
\Phi
is well defined
.
Our first goal is to see that
\Phi:X^{a}_{T}\times X^{a}_{T}\longmapsto X^{a}_{T}\times X^{a}_{T}
, that is, for
\varphi,\psi\in X^{a}_{T}
we need to show that

(18)

\|\Phi(\varphi,\psi)\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}}\leq 2a.

Recalling well-known results for the unweighted case
r=0
(see for example

[
21
, Proposition 2.2]
and the references therein) one can deduce that

(19)

\|\Phi(\varphi,\psi)\|_{Y_{T}\times Y_{T}}\lesssim\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+T^{\theta}(\|\varphi\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}\,\|\psi\|_{Y_{T}}+\|\varphi\|_{Y_{T}}\,\|\psi\|_{Y_{T}}^{2}),

for certain
\theta>0
.
According to
(
14
) and (
15
), it remains to control the sum of the terms

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\||x|^{r}e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}},

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\||x|^{r}e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}(\psi_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}},

\displaystyle I_{3}:=\Big\||x|^{r}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}},

\displaystyle I_{4}:=\Big\||x|^{r}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\psi^{2}\overline{\varphi})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}.

By symmetry, it is enough to consider
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
.
For the analysis of
I_{1}
, we simply use
(
10
), and (
3
) to get

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{r/2}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+T^{r}\|D^{r}_{x}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\||x|^{r}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

(20)

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{r/2}+T^{r})\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\||x|^{r}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

As for
I_{3}
, once again (
10
) produces

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\Big\||x|^{r}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi})(x,t^{\prime})\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\,dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{1+r/2}\,\|\varphi^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+T^{1+r}\,\|D_{x}^{r}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+T\,\||x|^{r}\varphi^{2}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{1+r/2}+T^{1+r})\,\|\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}+T\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L_{x}^{\infty}}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L_{x}^{\infty}}\,\||x|^{r}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{1+r/2}+T^{1+r})\,\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}^{2}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}_{x}}+T\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H_{x}^{1}}\,\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H_{x}^{1}}\,\||x|^{r}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L_{x}^{2}}

(21)

\displaystyle\lesssim(T^{1+r/2}+T^{1+r}+T)\|\varphi\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\,\|\psi\|_{X_{T}}

where we also used the Sobolev properties
(
4
)
and
(
5
).

Combining
(
19
),
(
3.1
) and
(
3.1
)
(and taking into consideration the analogous estimates for
I_{2}
and
I_{4}
) we deduce that,
for every
\varphi,\psi\in X^{a}_{T}
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\varphi,\psi)\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big((1+T^{\theta})(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}})+\||x|^{r}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\||x|^{r}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\,T^{\theta}\,a^{3},

for some
C,\theta>0
.

Finally, set

a:=C(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\||x|^{r}\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\||x|^{r}\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}})

and chose
T
small enough verifying

(22)

C\,T^{\theta}\,(\|\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{2}})+C\,T^{\theta}\,a^{3}\leq a,

to conclude (
18
).

Step 2:
\Phi
is a contraction
.
Now the task is to show the existence of a constant
0<K<1

verifying that

\|\Phi(\varphi,\psi)-\Phi(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}}\leq K\|(\varphi,\psi)-(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}},

for
\varphi,\widetilde{\varphi},\psi,\widetilde{\psi}\in X^{a}_{T}
.

It is known that (see

[
21
, Proposition 2.2]
),
for some
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\varphi,\psi)-\Phi(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})\|_{Y_{T}\times Y_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\theta}\big(\|\varphi\|_{Y_{T}}+\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{Y_{T}}+\|\psi\|_{Y_{T}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{Y_{T}}\big)^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle\quad\times\|(\varphi,\psi)-(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})\|_{Y_{T}\times Y_{T}}.

Hence, we concentrate on the terms

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle:=\Big\||x|^{r}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi}-\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\widetilde{\psi}})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}},

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle:=\Big\||x|^{r}\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\partial_{x}^{2}}(\psi^{2}\overline{\varphi}-\widetilde{\psi}^{2}\overline{\widetilde{\varphi}})(x,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}.

We only analyze

,
J_{2}
can be treated analogously.

Next, observe that we can rewrite the difference of nonlinear factors as follows

\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi}-\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}=\varphi^{2}(\overline{\psi}-\overline{\widetilde{\psi}})+\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}(\varphi+\widetilde{\varphi})(\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}).

Then, proceeding as in
(
3.1
)
and
(
3.1
)
above, we get

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim T\,\||x|^{r}(\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi}-\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\widetilde{\psi}})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}+(T^{1+r/2}+T^{1+r})\,\|\varphi^{2}\overline{\psi}-\widetilde{\varphi}^{2}\overline{\widetilde{\psi}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq T\,\Big(\||x|^{r}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\||x|^{r}\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\||x|^{r}\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}_{x}}\,\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(T^{1+r/2}+T^{1+r})\Big(\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}^{2}\,\|\psi-\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}+\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}

(25)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\,\|\varphi-\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}_{x}}\Big).

Putting together (
3.1
),
(
3.1
) and the corresponding symmetric estimate for

we get, for
\varphi,\widetilde{\varphi},\psi,\widetilde{\psi}\in X^{a}_{T}
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\varphi,\psi)-\Phi(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq CT^{\theta}a^{2}\|(\varphi,\psi)-(\widetilde{\varphi},\widetilde{\psi})\|_{X_{T}\times X_{T}},

for certain

.
Therefore, it is possible to pick

) and
CT^{\theta}a^{2}<1

Proof of (

From

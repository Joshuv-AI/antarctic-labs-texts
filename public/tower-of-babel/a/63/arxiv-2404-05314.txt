A measure for the stability of structures immersed in a 2D laminar flow
Edoardo Bocchi, Filippo Gazzola

Abstract
We introduce a new measure for the stability of structures, such as the cross-section of the deck of a suspension bridge, subject to a 2D fluid force, such as the lift exerted by a laminar wind. We consider a wide class of possible flows, as well as a wide class of structural shapes. Within a suitable topological framework, we prove the existence of an optimal shape maximizing the stability. Applications to engineering problems are also discussed.

Introduction

Let
L>H>0
and consider the rectangle
R=(-L,L)\times(-H,H)
. Let
B\subset R
be a compact domain having barycenter at the origin
(x_{1},x_{2})=(0,0)
and such that
\ diam(B)\ll L,H
. We study the behavior of a stationary laminar (horizontal) fluid flow crossing
R
and filling the domain
\Omega=R\setminus B
with possibly asymmetric inflow and outflow. The fluid is governed by the steady 2D Navier-Stokes equations with inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega=\partial B\cup\partial R
, see Figure
1
.

Figure 1
:
The fluid domain
\Omega=R\setminus B
and the laminar inflow-outflow.

A parameter
\lambda\geq 0
, proportional to the Reynolds number, measures the strength of the inflow-outflow.
For
U\in\{0,1\}
, we consider the boundary-value problem

\displaystyle-\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=0,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle{u}_{|_{\partial B}}={u}_{|_{\Gamma_{b}}}

\displaystyle=0,\quad{u}_{|_{\Gamma_{t}}}=\lambda Ue_{1},\quad{u}_{|_{\Gamma_{l}}}=\lambda V_{\rm in}e_{1},\quad{u}_{|_{\Gamma_{r}}}=\lambda V_{\rm out}e_{1},

where
\Gamma_{b},\,\Gamma_{t},\,\Gamma_{l},\,\Gamma_{r}
denote the sides of
R
(respectively, bottom, top, left, right) and the couple

(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
belongs to a suitable class of compatible inflow-outflow shapes, see Definition
2.1

in Section
2
. Throughout the paper we refer to
\lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
as the
flow
, to
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
as
the
flow shape
, and to
\lambda
as the
flow magnitude
.

It is known
Païdoussis et al. (2010)
that if the solution
(u,p)
to (
1.1
) is regular enough, which is the case when
B
is of class
C^{1,1}
, the (transversal) lift force exerted by the fluid on the body
B
may be computed through the formula

\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)=-e_{2}\cdot\int_{\partial B}\mathbb{T}(u,p)n,

(1.2)

where
\mathbb{T}(u,p)=\nabla u+\nabla u^{T}-p\mathbb{I}
is the fluid stress tensor and
n
is the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\Omega
which, on
\partial B
, points towards the interior of
B
. If the solution
(u,p)
is not regular, for instance when
B
is merely Lipschitz, the integral in (
1.2
) is not defined but one can still compute the lift in a weak sense (see
Gazzola and Sperone (2020)
;
Bocchi and Gazzola (2023)
) by

\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)=-e_{2}\cdot\langle\mathbb{T}(u,p)n,1\rangle_{\partial B},

(1.3)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\partial B}
denotes the duality pairing between
W^{-2/3,3/2}(\partial B)
and
W^{2/3,3}(\partial B)
.

An apparent paradox in fluid mechanics shows that a perfectly symmetric laminar flow hitting a perfectly symmetric body may generate
a transversal lift force, orthogonal to the flow direction. This paradox is only apparent because it is the
asymmetric
vortex shedding arising leeward
which generates the transversal force. In
Gazzola and Sperone (2020)
an “anti-paradox” was shown, namely that there is no
lift force if the flow has sufficiently small Reynolds number, with an explicit (but far from being sharp) upper bound: the Reynolds number needs
to be so small that it may also be undetectable numerically. Subsequently,
this anti-paradox was used to study equilibrium configurations for fluid-structure interactions (FSI) problems in perfectly symmetric frameworks

Bonheure et al. (2020)
;
Gazzola and
Patriarca (2022)
;
Patriarca (2022)
;
Berchio et al. (2024)
.
Recently, in
Bocchi and Gazzola (2023)
we considered asymmetric frameworks and we determined a threshold for the Reynolds
number under which the equilibrium configuration is unique, although not symmetric. What is missing to give up invoking the idea of paradox is to establish whether an asymmetric body under the action of an asymmetric flow can maintain its barycenter at the original symmetric position in
x_{2}=0
.

The present paper should be seen as a connection between theoretical results and applications. A first approach in the study of stability of suspension bridges, or other structures interacting with air flows, consists in wind tunnel experiments where artificial laminar flows
simulating winds are created and interact with a scaled model of a symmetric structure. Typically, the flows vanish at the top
\Gamma_{t}
and
the bottom
\Gamma_{b}
of the inlet-outlet, as for Poiseuille-type flows. Mathematically, this translates into the boundary-value problem
(
1.1
) with
U=0
. However, this artificial “best-case” scenario does not occur in nature because neither winds nor the shape of
bridges are perfectly symmetric and one is led to investigate more general configurations. In fact, for winds hitting real bridges a
Couette-type flow appears more appropriate, which mathematically translates into
U=1
in (
1.1
).

The paper is divided in three parts. In Section
2
we introduce the topological tools needed to rigorously tackle the other two parts.
We define the classes of admissible bodies and flow shapes and we show their compactness in a suitable sense. Then, we derive continuous dependence results for the solution to (
1.1
) and the associated lift (
1.3
) with respect to variations of both the
body shape
B
and the flow
\lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
.

If the body has a symmetric shape (with respect to the horizontal line
x_{2}=0
) and the flow has an even shape, there is no lift exerted by the fluid when the flow magnitude
\lambda
is small, see
Gazzola and Sperone (2020)
. But if the flow shape is asymmetric, a nonzero lift may appear also on symmetric bodies
Bocchi and Gazzola (2023)
.
In Section
3
we prove a punctual zero-lift result, which has a direct application in wind tunnel experiments. For a given flow magnitude,
we construct asymmetric bodies and flow shapes for which the lift (
1.3
) vanishes.

The impossibility to prevent the appearance of the lift on a prescribed body shape independent of the flow

\lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
, suggests to characterize the shapes of the body
B
that are more stable.
In Section
4
we define a new measure for the instability of a structure immersed in a planar fluid, we introduce a quantity capturing the instability of a structure in terms of the lift acting on it in a given range of flow magnitudes
\lambda
.
Stability is meant as minimization of the absolute value of the lift (
1.3
) considering a suitable class of flows
\lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
with a prescribed range of magnitudes. We prove the existence of an optimal body shape,
minimizing the instability in the class of admissible body shapes. The application to bridge design is straightforward: the optimal shape
corresponds to the most stable cross-section for the deck of a suspension bridge.

2
Preliminary topological setting

2.1
Admissible flow and body shapes

We define the classes of flow and body shapes that will be used throughout the paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
U\in\{0,1\}
. We say that
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
is an admissible flow shape with controlled norm
r>0
if

(1)

(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
with
\|V_{\rm in}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)}+\|V_{\rm out}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)}\leq r
,

(2)

V_{\rm in}(-H)=V_{\rm out}(-H)=0
and
V_{\rm in}(H)=V_{\rm out}(H)=U
,

(3)

\int_{-H}^{H}V_{\rm in}(x_{2})dx_{2}=\int_{-H}^{H}V_{\rm out}(x_{2})dx_{2}=1
.

The set of the admissible flow shapes with controlled norm
r>0
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
.

Physically, the controlled norm
r>0
in Definition
2.1
measures the maximum strength of the flow by taking into account also its gradient.
By the compact embedding
W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}\Subset L^{\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
and the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm, the set
\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
is closed in the weak-
\ast
topology.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
U\in\{0,1\}
and
r>0
. Assume that a sequence
\{(f_{n},g_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
converges weakly-
\ast

in
W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
to some
(f,g)\in W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
. Then
(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
.

Next, we confine the bodies to a proper subregion of
R
. This choice is motivated by the applications to wind-bridges interactions:
the range of possible shapes of the cross-section of a bridge deck lies in a confined region, typically a rectangle. Moreover, in wind
tunnel experiments the deck has to be built far away from the walls
\partial R
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
D\subset R
be a closed rectangle homothetic to
R
and let
0<\alpha<|D|
.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{D}
be the set of nonempty compact convex domains contained in
D
.
We say that
B\in\mathcal{C}_{D}
is an admissible body shape if
|B|=\alpha
and we then write
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
.

Let
d^{H}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the Hausdorff distance between two sets. We say that a family of sets
\{B_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}

converges to
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in the sense of Hausdorff if

d^{H}(B_{\varepsilon},B)\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0\qquad\mbox{(notation: $B_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{H}B$).}

Physically,
\alpha>0
measures the amount of concrete used to build the cross-section of the deck.

Remark 2.4
.

In Definition
2.3
, the rectangle
D
can be replaced by any compact subdomain of
R
without
altering the results in this paper. For instance, the fact that all the domains of the family
\{B_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
are
contained in a compact set
D\subset R
still allows to obtain uniform bounds in the proofs of Theorems
2.8

and
2.9
below.
Also the convexity request on the admissible body shapes can be relaxed by considering, instead,
uniformly Lipschitz shapes since the next compactness result holds also within this class. Convexity is assumed in order
to avoid pathological limit configurations with no interest for applications in wind-bridges interactions.

This class of admissible body shapes has a crucial compactness property:

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
D\subset R
and
0<\alpha<|D|
be as in Definition
2.3
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{C}_{D}
for the Hausdorff metric.

Proof.

It follows by combining
(
Henrot and Pierre,
, Section 2)
with
(
Schneider,
, Theorem 1.8.20)
.∎

In the next subsections, we prove some continuity results of the lift (
1.3
) with respect to admissible flow and body shapes.

2.2
Continuity of the lift with respect to the flow

The functional spaces needed to establish well-posedness for (
1.1
) are the Sobolev spaces
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of vector fields and

H^{1}_{*}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ |\ u=0\ \mbox{ on }\ \partial B\},

defined as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{R}\setminus B)
with respect to the Dirichlet norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Since the Poincaré inequality holds in this space, see
Gazzola and Sperone (2020)
, the Dirichlet semi-norm is a norm also in
H^{1}_{*}(\Omega)
.
We also introduce the associated Sobolev constants

\mathcal{S}_{*}(\Omega)=\min\limits_{u\in H^{1}_{*}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}},\qquad\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega)=\min\limits_{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}}.

(2.1)

Note that
\mathcal{S}_{*}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega)
and that both constants are non-increasing with respect to the inclusion of domains. We then consider their subspaces of solenoidal vectors

V_{*}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}_{*}(\Omega)\ |\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\},\qquad V(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ |\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\}

and we recall that for
f\in V_{*}(\Omega)\cup V(\Omega)
,
g\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
h\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot\nabla g)\cdot h=-\int_{\Omega}(f\cdot\nabla h)\cdot g\,.

(2.2)

The scalar pressure in (
1.1
) is defined up to the addition of a constant and, hence, will be sought within the space of
zero-mean functions

L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)=\left\{p\in L^{2}(\Omega)\ \bigg|\ \int_{\Omega}p\ dx=0\right\}.

We may now state well-posedness of (
1.1
) for small flow magnitudes:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
,
\Omega=R\setminus B
and
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
with
U\in\{0,1\}
.
There exists
\Lambda=\Lambda(r,D)>0
such that, for
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]
,
(
1.1
) admits a unique weak solution
(u,p)\in V_{*}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with flow
\lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
,
satisfying both the bounds

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\lambda,\qquad\forall\lambda\in[0,\Lambda],

for
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega)
as in (
2.1
) and some
C=C(r,D)>0
. In particular, the first bound gives rise to a uniform bound with respect to
\Omega
.

Proof.

From
(
Gazzola and Sperone, 2020
, Theorem 3.1)
we know that there exists a unique solution to (
1.1
) satisfying the first bound of the proposition. Since
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
, we have that
R\setminus D\subset\Omega
and
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{S}_{0}(R\setminus D)
, hence yielding a uniform bound.

In
(
Bocchi and Gazzola, 2023
, Theorem 2.2)
we showed uniqueness
for
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]
with
\Lambda=\Lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out},\Omega)>0
and derived the bound

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\lambda

for some
C=C(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out},\Omega)>0
. More precisely, both
\Lambda
and
C
continuously depend on the
W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
-norm of
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
and, for
U=1
, also on the inverse of the Euclidean distance
d(\partial B,\partial R)
.
Since
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
and
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
, we have that
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
and
d(\partial B,\partial R)\geq d(\partial D,\partial R)
. Thus, we obtain a uniform uniqueness threshold
\Lambda(r,D)
and the second bound of the proposition follows.∎

In view of Proposition
2.6
, unless otherwise specified, from now on by
solution
of (
1.1
) we intend a weak solution

(u,p)\in V_{*}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.7
.

The uniqueness threshold
\Lambda=\Lambda(r,D)
in Proposition
2.6
is a map defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{C}_{R}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is the set of nonempty homothetic rectangles contained in
R
ordered by the inclusion of domains. We have that
\Lambda(\cdot,D)
is a decreasing function, with
\Lambda(r,D)\rightarrow 0
as

, and that
\Lambda(r,\cdot)
is a non-increasing function. Moreover, when

,
\Lambda(r,D)\rightarrow 0
as

approaches

.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let

) admits a unique solution

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|\mathcal{L}_{B}(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}=0.

(2.4)

Proof.

Using the compact embedding

2.3
Continuity of the lift with respect to the body shape

We study here how the lift
\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)
in (
1.3
), associated with the unique solution
(u,p)
to (
1.1
), behaves if we perturb the body shape but
not
its measure. Consider a family of domains
\{B_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
and the related problems, set in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=R\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla p_{\varepsilon}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(2.6)

\displaystyle{u_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}}={u_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\Gamma_{b}}}

\displaystyle=0,\quad{u_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\Gamma_{t}}}=\lambda Ue_{1},\quad{u_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\Gamma_{l}}}=\lambda V_{\rm in}e_{1},\quad{u_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\Gamma_{r}}}=\lambda V_{\rm out}e_{1}.

We prove the continuity of the lift with respect to the Hausdorff convergence of the body shapes on which the lift acts.

Theorem 2.9
.

Consider a family
\{B_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
and
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
such that
B_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{H}B
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=R\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
,
\Omega=R\setminus B

(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
with

and
\Lambda
as in Proposition
2.6
. Given
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]
, let
(u,p)\in V_{*}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be the unique solution to (
1.1
). Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, (
2.6
) admits a unique solution
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in V_{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
with
\lambda
in the same interval. Moreover,

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{L}_{B_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p).

(2.7)

Proof.

Since
\{B_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
for any
\varepsilon>0
, Proposition
2.6
yields the existence of a unique solution
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})\in V_{*}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times L^{2}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
to (
2.6
) for any

\varepsilon

We study the convergence of
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
to
(u,p)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Let us first consider the case when
B
is a domain of class
C^{1,1}
.
Then we know from
(
Bocchi and Gazzola, 2023

that the unique solution to (

solution, that is,

(u,p)\in H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)

Since
B_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{H}B
without an “order” (for instance,
B_{\varepsilon}

(u,p)
may not be

\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and/or the solution
(u_{\varepsilon},p_{\varepsilon})
may not be defined in

and
u_{\varepsilon}
, we extend them by zero, respectively, in

In particular,

(2.8)

Arguing as in
Gazzola and Sperone (2020)
and using the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\nu}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
\nu\in(0,1)
, we obtain

which guarantees that there exists

,

\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{\varepsilon})}\leq\sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, there exists a solenoidal vector field
s_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and a constant

, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

{s_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\partial R}}=0,\qquad{s_{\varepsilon}}_{|_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}}=u_{|_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}}},\qquad\|\nabla s_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\sigma_{\varepsilon}.

(2.9)

Let
v:=u-u_{\varepsilon}-s_{\varepsilon}\in V(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
. Since

and
u_{\varepsilon}
weakly solve, respectively, (
1.1
) and (
2.6
), we infer that

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla v:\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(v\cdot\nabla v)\cdot\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\nabla s_{\varepsilon}:\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in V(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle\Phi:\quad\{B_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,1]}\times\{(V^{\delta}_{\rm in},V^{\delta}_{\rm out})\}_{\delta\in[0,1]}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\qquad\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\left(B_{\varepsilon},V^{\delta}_{\rm in},V^{\delta}_{\rm out}\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\quad\mathcal{L}_{B_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon,\delta},p_{\varepsilon,\delta}).

By (
3.1
) and the continuous dependence results in Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
, the generalized Bolzano Theorem yields the existence of
(\widetilde{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\delta})\in(0,1)\times(0,1)
such that

\Phi(B_{\widetilde{\varepsilon}},V^{\widetilde{\delta}}_{\rm in},V^{\widetilde{\delta}}_{\rm out})=0
.
Equivalently, denoting
\widetilde{B}=B_{\widetilde{\varepsilon}}
and
(\widetilde{V}_{\rm in},\widetilde{V}_{\rm out})=(V^{\widetilde{\delta}}_{\rm in},V^{\widetilde{\delta}}_{\rm out})
, there exists a unique solution
(u_{\widetilde{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\delta}},p_{\widetilde{\varepsilon},\widetilde{\delta}})
to (
FSI
) in
\widetilde{\Omega}=R\setminus\widetilde{B}
with flow
\lambda(\widetilde{V}_{\rm in},\widetilde{V}_{\rm out})
.

Using analogous arguments, the same result holds also
when
B
is symmetric and
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})
is non-even and when
B
is asymmetric and
(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})

is even.∎

4
A new measure for the stability of bridges

Theorem
3.1
states that, for a fixed flow magnitude
\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]
, in wind tunnel experiments one may create asymmetric flow
shapes and/or asymmetric body shapes in order to “artificially” obtain an equilibrium configuration in which no lift acts on the body. This local result is only partially satisfactory since the target is to find a global result, namely the construction of a body shape minimizing
the lift action in a given range of flow magnitudes. In this section we address this query.

Consider a fixed body shape
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
. From
Bocchi and Gazzola (2023)
we know that the lift
\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)
in (
1.3
)
belongs to
C([0,\Lambda])
, with
\Lambda>0
possibly smaller than the uniqueness threshold in Proposition
2.6
.
Fix
U\in\{0,1\}
and consider the functional

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{B}:\quad\quad\mathcal{F}_{r,U}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\qquad\qquad C([0,\Lambda])

(4.1)

\displaystyle(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})

\displaystyle\mapsto\quad\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\lambda(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})]=\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)

which maps the flow shapes to the lift exerted on
B
, seen as a continuous function of
\lambda
.
We restrict (
4.1
) to flow shapes that belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
, introduced in Definition
2.1
.
This set has an evident physical interpretation: if a bridge of cross-section
B
is built in some region where the winds are controlled by
the parameter
r>0
(not necessarily small), then
\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
contains all the “expected winds”.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
r>0
,
U\in\{0,1\}
and let
D\subset R
,
0<\alpha<|D|
. We call
instability measure
of the body shape
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
the positive number

\gamma_{r,U}(B):=\sup\limits_{(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}}\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}.

(4.2)

The main result of this paper is the existence of a body shape of given area minimizing the instability measure.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
r>0
,
U\in\{0,1\}
and let
D\subset R
,
0<\alpha<|D|
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
be as in Definition
2.3
.
There exists
B^{*}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
such that

\gamma_{r,U}(B^{*})\leq\gamma_{r,U}(B)\qquad\mbox{for all}\ B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}.

(4.3)

Proof.

We first claim that the supremum in (
4.2
) is attained, namely that, for a given
B\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
,
there exists
(\overline{V}_{\rm in},\overline{V}_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
such that

\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (\overline{V}_{\rm in},\overline{V}_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}=\max\limits_{(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}}\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}.

(4.4)

To this end, consider a maximizing sequence
\{(V^{n}_{\rm in},V^{n}_{\rm out})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
, that is,

\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (V^{n}_{\rm in},V^{n}_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}\rightarrow\sup\limits_{(V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})\in\mathcal{F}_{r,U}}\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (V_{\rm in},V_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

Since
\{(V^{n}_{\rm in},V^{n}_{\rm out})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
, from the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem
we know that, up to a subsequence,
(V^{n}_{\rm in},V^{n}_{\rm out})
weakly-
\ast
converges to some
(\overline{V}_{\rm in},\overline{V}_{\rm out})\in W^{1,\infty}(-H,H)^{2}
, which also belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{r,U}
due to Proposition
2.2
.
By Theorem
2.8
we then know that

\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (V^{n}_{\rm in},V^{n}_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}\rightarrow\|\mathfrak{L}_{B}[\ \cdot\ (\overline{V}_{\rm in},\overline{V}_{\rm out})]\|_{C([0,\Lambda])}\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

and that (
4.4
) holds.

By Theorem
2.9
, we have that the lift
\mathcal{L}_{B}(u,p)
is a continuous function of
B
in the class
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
, therefore
\gamma_{r,U}(\cdot)
is a continuous function in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
. By Proposition
2.5
, the generalized Weierstrass Theorem guarantees the existence of a body shape
B^{*}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
that minimizes
\gamma_{r,U}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
, which proves (
4.3
).∎

The instability measure
\gamma_{r,U}(B)
, defined in (
4.2
), appears suitable to quantify the instability of
B
since,
for a given range of flow magnitudes, it computes the maximum strength of the lift exerted on
B
. Theorem
4.2
shows that,
within the class
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,D}
, an optimal shape
B^{*}
minimizing
\gamma_{r,U}(\cdot)
exists. Correspondingly, this
shows that an optimal cross-section for the deck of the bridge exists (maximizing its stability) but leaves open how to find it or, at least, some qualitative features of it. In view of the results in
Pironneau (1974)
, a conjecture could be that the optimal profile has a front end shaped like a wedge of right angle, see
also
Montoya et al. (2018)
for the engineering point of view. Since in real applications the wind flow is of Couette-type, that is,
U=1
, we do not expect a symmetric body shape minimizing
\gamma_{r,U}
.

On the ADM mass of Kähler scalar flat ALE metrics
Claudio Arezzo and Karen Corrales

Abstract
In this paper we study the behaviour of scalar flat Kähler ALE spaces and their ADM mass under blow ups. In particular we prove that by blowing up sufficiently many points at sufficiently big mutual distance one can produce scalar flat metrics with arbitrarily large ADM mass. A general machinery for producing scalar flat non Ricci flat ALE spaces of zero ADM mass is also presented, using and integrating previous work by Rollin-Singer ( [ 17 ] ) and Hein-LeBrun ( [ 11 ] ).

The existence and geometric properties of Ricci and scalar flat metrics on ALE Kähler spaces with a given structure group at infinity have been subject of intense research in past few decades (see e.g.

Given the fact that such spaces do not carry holomorphic vector fields (which are the key obstruction for this theory) it is not hard to get the existence of scalar-flat metrics on blow ups of scalar flat ALE spaces (Theorem
3.1
). While not the main focus of this paper, we point out that the new ALE scalar flat spaces
produced in Theorem

can in turn be used as new models for the gluing theory, hence getting, even starting from compact cscK manifolds, infinitely may new families of cscK manifolds of different topological types.

The new interesting problem studied in this paper is to estimate the maximal size of an exceptional divisor that can be inserted in the original space. It is known that in general this question can be very subtle on compact manifolds, carrying implicitily the difficulties hidden in the notion of
K
-stability and in the Tian-Yau-Donaldson Conjecture (see for example Section
7.2
point (2) in
[

]
and
[

). On top of its clear geometric interest, the main motivation for this question comes from the recent and beautiful work by Hein-LeBrun (
[
11
]
) connecting various geometric and topological quantities of ALE spaces to their ADM mass, another natural invariant arising from General Relativity recalled in Section 2.2 (
[
4
]
).

Hein-LeBrun’s topological formula for the ADM mass (Theorem
2.2
) immediately implies that the effect of blowing up one point on the ADM mass is to increase it by an amount proportional to the volume of the exceptional divisor that one can insert, while keeping the scalar curvature zero, hence pointing again to the problem mentioned above.

The main result of this paper (Theorem
4.1
) guarantees that this quantity cannot decrease when performing a second blow up at a point sufficiently far away from the first one. Iterating this scheme for multiple blow ups at sufficiently many points at sufficiently big mutual distance, we get the main applications in terms of ADM mass.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,{\bf{\Gamma}},h,\omega)
be an ALE scalar flat Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
and ADM mass
e\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for every
\tilde{e}\geq e
there exists an ALE scalar flat Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
with ADM mass
\tilde{e}
. Such manifold can be obtained from
X
by blowing up sufficiently many points at sufficiently big mutual distance.

The above theorem indicates that, while
\Gamma
does not determine the ADM mass of an ALE scalar flat Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
(something easy to observe even for trivial
\Gamma
), the interesting quantity that can be algebraically determined by the structure group is

inf\{\mbox{ADM mass}(X,{\bf{\Gamma}},h,\omega)\,|\,(X,{\bf{\Gamma}},h,\omega){\mbox{ ALE scalar flat K\"{a}hler\ with structure group }}\,\bf{\Gamma}\}.

Theorem
1.1
gives also a general framework to produce scalar flat, non Ricci flat, ALE spaces with vanishing ADM mass. Specific examples were known thanks to Rollin-Singer (
[
17
]
, Section 6.7) and Hein-LeBrun (
[
11
]
, Theorem 4.7), but our result gives a general machinery to produce them.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(X,{\bf{\Gamma}},h,\omega)
be an ALE scalar flat Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
and ADM mass
e<0
. Then there exists an ALE scalar flat Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
with vanishing ADM mass. Such manifold can be obtained from
X
by blowing up sufficiently many points at sufficiently big mutual distance.

Preliminaries

2.1
The Kähler potential of a scalar flat ALE Kähler resolution.

We start by recalling the concept of Asymptotically Locally Euclidean (ALE for short) Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
. Let
{\bf{\Gamma}}=(\Gamma_{j}),j=1...,k
be a collection of finite subgroups of the unitary group

U(m)
each acting freely away from the origin. We say that a complete noncompact Kähler manifold
(X,{\bf{\Gamma}},h,\omega)
of complex dimension
m
, where
h
is the Kähler metric and
\omega
is the Kähler form, is an ALE Kähler manifold with structure group
\bf{\Gamma}
if

•

there exist a compact subset
K
such that the
j
-th connected component of
X\setminus K
is biholomorphic via a map
\pi_{j}
to

(\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus B)/\Gamma_{j}
, where
B
is a closed ball with positive radius
R>0
, and

•

on each component in standard Euclidean coordinates the metric
\pi_{*}h
satisfies

\left|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\left(\big(\pi_{*}h)_{i\bar{j}}\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\delta_{i\bar{j}}\right)\right|\,\,=\,\,\mathcal{O}\left(|x|^{-\tau-|\alpha|}\right)\,,

(2.1)

for some
\tau>0
and every multindex
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
.

Note that we are identifying the complex structure outside a compact subset with the standard one. This puts a restriction (widely studied) on the class of spaces allowed since in general its complex structure could be only asymptotic to the standard one and in fact the complex structure could not even admit holomorphic coordinates at infinity as shown for example by Honda (
[
12
]
) also in the scalar flat case.

While the definition allows for an ALE space to have multiple components, a remarkable Theorem by Hein-LeBrun (Propositions
1.5
and
4.2
in
[
11
]
) shows that only the case
k=1
can actually occur. We can then assume it thoughtout the paper.

The behaviour of the Kähler potential of a scalar flat ALE Kähler metric is quite well understood (see e.g.
[
2
]
Section
7
and
[
1
]
for further details)

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\Gamma,h,\omega)
be a scalar flat ALE Kähler manifold with structure group
\Gamma
. Then for
R>0
large enough, we have that on
X\setminus\pi^{-1}(B_{R})
the Kähler form can be written as

•

if
dim(X)=m>2
, then, for some real constant
e_{X}
,

\omega\,\,=\,\,i\partial\overline{\partial}\left(\,\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\,+\,e_{X}\,|x|^{4-2m}+\,\psi_{\eta}\left(x\right)\right)\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\psi_{\eta}\,=\,\mathcal{O}(|x|^{3-2m})\,,

(2.2)

•

if
dim(X)=2
, then for some real constant
e_{X}
,

\omega\,\,=\,\,i\partial\overline{\partial}\left(\,\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}\,+\,e_{X}\,log|x|+\,\psi_{\eta}\left(x\right)\right)\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\psi_{\eta}\,=\,\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-1})\,,

(2.3)

2.2
The ADM mass of a Kähler potential of an ALE Kähler manifold with structure group
\Gamma
.

The key quantity studied in this paper is the celebrated ADM mass introduced by Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (
[
4
]
), whose
geometrical nature has been clarified by Bartnik (
[
5
]
) and Chruściel (
[
8
]
).

In the context on ALE Kähler manifolds it has been the center of the beautiful paper by Hein-LeBrun (
[
11
]
) which is the original motivation of our work.

Following Hein-LeBrun’s normalization conventions are chosen so that, at a given end, the mass of an ALE manifold is given by

m(X,h)=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\bf{\Gamma}}(\frac{m}{2})}{4(m-1)\pi^{\frac{m}{2}}}\int_{S_{R}/\Gamma}[h_{kl,k}-h_{kk,l}]{\bf{n}}^{l}da_{E}

where
S_{R}
is the Euclidean coordinate sphere of radius
R
,
da_{E}
is the
(m-1)
-dimensional volume form induced on this sphere by the Euclidean metric, and
\bf{n}
is the outward-pointing Euclidean unit normal vector.

Let us also denote by
\clubsuit\colon H^{2}\rightarrow H^{2}_{c}(X)
the inverse of the isomorphism induced by the standard inclusion of compactly supported forms into all differential forms.

Restricting our attention to the scalar flat case, their key result is the following equivalence:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(X,\Gamma,h,\omega)
be a scalar flat ALE Kähler manifold with structure group
\Gamma
. Then

m(X,h)=-\frac{\langle\clubsuit(c_{1}(X),\omega^{m-1}\rangle}{(2m-1)\pi^{m-1}}=e_{X}\,\,.

The first equality is a special case of Theorem C in
[
11
]
, while the equality
m(X,h)=e_{X}
is a classical observation due to LeBrun (
[
15
]
) and Rollin-Singer (
[
17
]
, Section 6.8) in complex dimension
2
, whose proof extends readily to any dimension.

3
Blowing up points

In order to fix notations needed in the sequel,
for every point
p\in X
we denote by
(z_{1},\dots z_{m})
normal coordinates centred at
p
, so that near
p
the metric
\omega
is of the form

\omega=\omega_{E}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{1}(z),\quad\varphi_{1}(z)=\mathcal{O}(|z|^{4}),

(3.1)

specifically,
\varphi_{1}(z)\leq c|z|^{4},
where

depends on the curvature of
\omega
at

It is also convenient to isolate, among all ALE scalar flat Kähler manifolds,
(Bl_{0}\mathbb{C}^{m},\{Id\},h_{BS},\eta_{BS})
, where
h_{BS}
denotes the well-know Burns-Simanca metric, normalized so to have volume of the exceptional divisor

equal to
1

Its asymptotic expansion at infinity can be written as

\displaystyle\eta_{BS}

\displaystyle=\omega_{E}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{2}(w),\quad\varphi_{2}(w)=\mathcal{O}(|w|^{4-2m}),\quad\mbox{for}\;m>2

(3.2)

\displaystyle\eta_{BS}

\displaystyle=\omega_{E}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{2}(w),\quad\varphi_{2}(w)=\log(|w|^{2}),\quad\mbox{for}\;m=2

in terms of the standard coordinates on
Bl_{0}\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus E\cong\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
.

On the other hand,
[
2
, Proposition 5.4]
implies that there is no nonzero holomorphic vector field vanishing somewhere on
X
and then it is easy to observe that the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
readily extends to the ALE case. More precisely we have

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,\Gamma,h,\omega)
be a scalar flat ALE Kähler manifold with structure group
\Gamma
. Given finitely many smooth points
p_{1},\dots p_{k}\in X
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}(X,p_{1},\dots,p_{k}):=\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the blow-up of
X
at
p_{1},\dots p_{k}
carries zero scalar curvature Kähler forms

\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\pi^{*}[\omega]-\varepsilon^{2}(PD[E_{1}]+\dots+PD[E_{k}]),

where the
PD[E_{j}]
are the Poincaré duals of the
(2m-2)
-homology classes of the exceptional divisors of the blow-up at
p_{j}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
.

What is crucial for the scope of this paper is to make, as explicit as possible, the dependence of
\varepsilon_{0}
on all the geometric data built in this theorem, such as the position of the points
p_{j}
and the geometry of
X
and of
Bl_{0}\mathbb{C}^{m}
.

Let us then recall that in order to construct the scalar flat metric on
Bl_{p_{1},\dots p_{k}}X
, the idea is to replace the metric
\omega
on a small neighbourhood of
p_{i}
with a suitably
scaled down copy of
\eta_{BS}
·

Precisely, for a fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
r_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{m-1/m}
, we will glue
\omega
with
\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{BS}
on the annulus
B_{2r_{\varepsilon}}\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}
around each
p_{i}
, under the change of variables
z=\varepsilon w.

Following (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), the metric on
X\setminus\{p_{1}\dots,p_{k}\}
, which naturally extends to a metric on
Bl_{p_{1},\dots p_{k}}X
, is defined by

\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}=\begin{cases}\omega\quad\mbox{on}\quad X\setminus{\cup_{i=1}^{k}B_{2r_{\varepsilon}}(p_{i})}\\
\omega_{E}+i\partial\bar{\partial}(\gamma_{1}(z)\varphi_{1}(z)+\varepsilon^{2}(1-\gamma_{1}(z))\varphi_{2}(\varepsilon^{-1}z))\quad\mbox{on}\quad B_{2r_{\varepsilon}}(p_{i})\setminus B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(p_{i})\\
\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{BS}\quad\mbox{on}\quad B_{r_{\varepsilon}}(p_{i})\setminus\{p_{i}\}\end{cases}

(3.3)

where
\gamma_{1}
is a suitable cutoff function.

Once this pre-glued metric is built the problem is to find a function
\varphi
such that the metric
\omega_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}+i\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi
has zero scalar curvature. In order to solve that equation, the most important step is to show that
the linear operator
L_{\tilde{\omega}_{\varepsilon}}
associated to the scalar curvature is invertible and to obtain bounds on the norm of the inverse in suitable Banach spaces: weighted Hölder spaces, which now have to take into “weighted” account the behaviour of function near the point
p_{j}
, with weight
\delta
, and at infinity, with weight
\delta_{\infty}
.

The weighted spaces where to carry out the proof of Theorem

are the same one as those used in
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
with the extra weight

If we denote
X^{*}:=X\setminus\{p_{1}\dots,p_{k}\}
,
X_{r}:=X\setminus{\cup_{i=1}^{k}B_{r}(p_{i})}
and
N_{p_{i}}
by the identification of

.

Given

\displaystyle=\|\varphi\|_{C^{l,\alpha}_{\delta_{\infty}}(X_{2r_{\varepsilon}})}+\|\varphi\|_{C^{l,\alpha}_{\delta}(N_{p_{i}})}

\displaystyle+\max_{i=1...k}\left\{\sup_{r\in(r_{\varepsilon},2r_{\varepsilon})}\left(r^{-\delta}\|\varphi(rz)\|_{C^{l,\alpha}(B_{2}(p_{i})\setminus B_{1}(p_{i}))}\right)\right\}

Remark 4.4

5
ADM mass under blow ups, proof of the main results

We now analyze the effects of the previous results on the ADM mass of a scalar flat ALE space.
Thanks to the formula due to Hein-LeBrun (
[
11
]
) (Theorem
2.2
), this is easily achieved by the following

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
m(X,h)
and
m(Bl_{p}X,h_{\varepsilon})
be the ADM mass of
(X,\Gamma,h,\omega)
and
Bl_{p}X
respectively, where
h_{\varepsilon}
is the scalar flat metric produced out of
h
by Theorem
3.1
. Then,

m(Bl_{p}X,h_{\varepsilon})=m(X,h)+\frac{(m-1)\varepsilon^{2(m-1)}}{(2m-1)\pi^{m-1}}.

Proof.

Since
\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\pi^{*}[\omega]-\varepsilon^{2}PD[E_{p}]
and we have normalized the Burns-Simanca metric in a way that
[\eta_{BS}]=-PD[E_{p}]
then

[\omega_{\varepsilon}]=[\omega]+\varepsilon^{2}[\eta],\quad c_{1}(Bl_{p}X)=\pi^{*}(c_{1}(X))-(m-1)PD[E_{p}].

By Theorem
2.2
we have

\displaystyle m(Bl_{p}X,h_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\langle\clubsuit(c_{1}(Bl_{p}X)),[\omega_{\varepsilon}]^{m-1}\rangle}{(2m-1)\pi^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\langle\clubsuit(c_{1}(X)-(m-1)PD[E_{p}]),([\omega]-\varepsilon^{2}PD[E_{p}])\rangle}{(2m-1)\pi^{m-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(X,h)+\frac{(m-1)\varepsilon^{2(m-1)}}{(2m-1)\pi^{m-1}}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

Proposition
5.1
shows that each time we blow up a point
p
we can increase the ADM mass of any amount up to

\frac{(m-1)\varepsilon_{0}(p)^{2(m-1)}}{(2m-1)\pi^{m-1}}.

Theorem
4.1
shows that iterating the process taking a second point sufficiently far away from the first one, we can increase again the ADM mass by the same amount (at least). Of course, blowing up points does not change the structure groups at infinity of the original ALE space. Given that the process can be iterated infinitely many times, the theorem is proved.
∎

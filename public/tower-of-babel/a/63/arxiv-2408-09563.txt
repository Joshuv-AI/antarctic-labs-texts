Analogues of Fourier quasicrystals for a strip
Sergii Yu.Favorov

Abstract




Abstract.

We study a certain family of discrete measures with unit masses on a horizontal strip as an analogue of Fourier quasicrystals on the real line. We prove a one-to-one correspondence
between supports of measures from this family and zero sets of exponential polynomials with imaginary frequencies. This result is the special case of a general result on measures
whose supports correspond to zero sets of absolutely convergent Dirichlet series with bounded spectrum.

AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 52C23, 30B50, 42A38

Keywords: Fourier quasicrystal, almost periodic set, zero set, exponential polynomial, Dirichlet series

The Fourier transform on the complex plane

For
\psi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
put

\hat{\psi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t)e^{-2\pi ixt}dt.

If

(10)

\psi(t)=O\left(e^{-K|t|}\right),\qquad K>2\pi H,\qquad|t|\to\infty,

then the function

(11)

\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi(t)e^{-2\pi izt}dt=\widehat{(\psi(t)e^{2\pi yt})}(x),\quad z=x+iy,

is the holomorphic extension of
\hat{\psi}(x)
to a neighborhood of
\mathcal{S}_{H}
. Extending to the strip the well-known equality for real
x
, we get for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
and
\psi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
which satisfies (
10
)

(12)

\widehat{(\varphi\star\psi)^{c}}(z)=\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\hat{\psi}^{c}(z),\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}.

If
\psi
is an
m

10

\psi
and all its derivatives
\psi^{(k)},\,k\leq m
, then

(13)

\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)=(2\pi iz)^{-m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi^{(m)}(t)e^{-2\pi itz}dt,\quad z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}\setminus\{0\}.

For all
z\in\mathcal{S}_{H}
the integral is finite, hence,

(14)

|\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)|\leq C(\max\{1,|z|\})^{-m},\qquad C=C(\psi,m)<\infty.

Applying (
13
) to
t^{k}\psi(t)
, we get inequalities similar to (
14
) for
(\hat{\psi}^{c})^{(k)}(z)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Note that (
5
) is equivalent to the estimate

(15)

M_{\mu}(r)=O(r^{N}),\qquad r\to\infty.

with some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Using (
14
) for
\varphi\in\mathcal{D},\,m=N+1
and integrating by parts, we get

\left|\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},|z|>R}\hat{\varphi}^{c}(z)\mu(dz)\right|\leq C\int_{z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},|z|>R}|z|^{-N-1}|\mu|(dz)\leq C(N+1)\int_{R}^{\infty}r^{-N-2}M_{\mu}(r)dr.

The last integral tends to zero as

. Therefore the integral in (
6
) is finite, and the distribution
\hat{\mu}^{c}
is well-define.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathcal{S}_{H}
satisfying (

), and let
\psi
be a

-function such that all its derivatives satisfy (

If

|\hat{\mu}^{c}|(-r,r)=O(e^{Lr}),\qquad 2\pi H<L<K,\qquad r\to\infty,

\psi

Proof
. Let
\eta(t)\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\eta(0)=1

\eta_{n}(t)=\eta(t/n)
.
Replacing
\psi

(\widehat{\eta_{n}\psi})^{c}(z)\to\hat{\psi}^{c}(z),\qquad n\to\infty.

Next, it is easy to see that

|(\eta_{n}(t)\psi(t))^{(N+1)}|\leq Ce^{-K|t|},\quad C<\infty,

uniformly in

|(\widehat{\eta_{n}\psi})^{c}(z)|\leq C^{\prime}(\max\{1,|z|\})^{-N-1},\quad z\in\mathcal{S}_{H},\quad C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\eta,\psi,N)<\infty.

Since the integral

\int|(\widehat{\eta_{n}\psi})^{c}(z)||\mu|(dz)\leq C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\max\{1,r\})^{-N-1}M_{\mu}(dr)=C^{\prime}(N+1)\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{-N-2}M_{\mu}(r)dr

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\mu,(\widehat{\eta_{n}\psi})^{c})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int(\widehat{\eta_{n}\psi})^{c}(z)\mu(dz)=\int\hat{\psi}^{c}(z)\mu(dz)=(\mu,\hat{\psi}^{c}).

3.
Dirichlet series on the real line

Denote by
\mathcal{W}
the algebra of all Dirichlet series on
{\mathbb{R}}

Q(x)=\sum_{n}q_{n}e^{2\pi i\omega_{n}x},\,\omega_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}},

with the finite Wiener’s norm
\|Q\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\sum_{n}|q_{n}|
. The spectrum of
Q\in\mathcal{W}
is the set
\spec Q=\{\omega_{n}:\,q_{n}\neq 0\}
.
For each
Q\in\mathcal{W}
and each analytic function
h(z)
on a neighborhood of the set
\overline{\{Q(x):\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}\}}
we have
h(Q(x))\in\mathcal{W}
(see
[
19
]
, Ch.VI). In particular,
\exp Q\in\mathcal{W}
,
if
\inf_{\mathbb{R}}|Q(x)|>0
then
1/Q\in\mathcal{W}
, and if
\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|Q(x)|<1
then
\log(1+Q(x))\in\mathcal{W}
.

When the spectrum
\spec Q
is non-negative and locally finite
\spec Q
the set
\spec Q^{n}\cap(0,r)
for all
n,\,r<\infty
is a subset of all sums of
at most
\frac{r}{\min\{\spec Q\setminus\{0\}\}}
possibly identical elements of
\spec Q\cap(0,r)
.
Therefore, the sets
\spec Q^{n}\cap(0,r)
for all
n
are subsets of one finite set, which depends only on
r
, and the equalities

\exp{Q(x)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q^{n}(x)/n!\quad\text{for}\quad\|Q\|_{\mathcal{W}}<\infty,\qquad\log(1+Q(x))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}Q^{n}(x)/n\quad\text{for}\quad\|Q\|_{\mathcal{W}}<1,

imply the following proposition:

Proposition 2
.

If the spectrum of
Q\in\mathcal{W}
is non-negative and locally finite, then so is the spectrum
\spec\exp Q
. If, in addition,
\|Q\|_{\mathcal{W}}<1
and
0\not\in\spec Q
,
then
\spec\log(1+Q)
is strictly positive and locally finite too.

Almost periodic measures and sets on a strip

Definition 1

]

A continuous function

\varepsilon

and some

For example, any function

Definition 2

]

A continuous function

for all

and some

depending on
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\varepsilon

Every holomorphic function, which is bounded on

and almost periodic on one line

(see

For example, every Dirichlet series with finite Wiener’s norm and bounded spectrum (

A measure

In our investigation we consider only almost periodic measures with supports in closed horizontal strips of finite width. In this case the definition of almost periodic measures can be simplified:

.

5.
Entire functions with almost periodic zeros

By Hadamard’s Theorem, every entire function
g(z),\,z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, of exponential growth (i.e.,
\log|g(z)|\leq O(|z|)
as
|z|\to\infty
) with zeros
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}

has the form

(18)

g(z)=e^{dz}\prod_{n}(1-z/a_{n})e^{z/a_{n}},\quad d\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Note that each
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
can be repeated any finite number of times in the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
.

Further, zeros of
g
satisfy the conditions

(19)

\#\{n:\,|a_{n}|\leq r\}=O(r),\qquad r\to\infty,

and

(20)

\sum_{n:\,|a_{n}|\leq r}\frac{1}{a_{n}}=O(1),\qquad r\to\infty.

On the other hand, if a sequence
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
satisfies these conditions,
then the function (
18
) is an entire function of exponential growth (Lindelöf’s Theorem (see
[
10
]
)).

Proposition 5
.

Let
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{H}\setminus\{0\}
be an almost periodic set. Then the points
a_{n}
satisfy conditions (
19
), (
20
), and under suitable numeration the infinite product

(21)

f(z)=(1-z/a_{0})\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(1-z/a_{n})(1-z/a_{-n})

converges uniformly on compact sets in
{\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, the sum

(22)

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\frac{1}{z-a_{0}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{z-a_{n}}+\frac{1}{z-a_{-n}}\right]=\\
\frac{1}{x+iy-\phi(0)}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{x+iy-\rho n-\phi(n)}+\frac{1}{x+iy+\rho n-\phi(-n)}\right]

converges absolutely and uniformly (after discarding a finite number of members) on the sets
D_{R}:=\{z=x+iy:\,|x|\leq R,\,|y|\geq M+1\}
, where
M:=1+\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\phi(n)|
and
R<\infty
is arbitrary.

Proof
. By Proposition
4
, we can renumber
a_{n}
such that
a_{n}=\rho n+\phi(n)
,

,
with a bounded complex-valued function
\phi(n)

.

. We get

\widehat{(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z))^{c}}=\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon z),\quad|\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon z)|\leq e^{2\pi H},\quad\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon z)\to 1\,\text{ as }\,\varepsilon\to 0.

The functions
e_{\zeta}(t),\,e_{\zeta}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)
and all their derivatives satisfy (
10
). By Proposition
3
we have
\mu_{A}(-r,r)=O(r)
.
It follows from Proposition
1
that (
6
) holds for the function
e_{\zeta}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}
. The equality (
12
) yields

(25)

(\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c},e_{\zeta}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon})=(\mu_{A},\hat{e}^{c}_{\zeta}\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}^{c})

We have

(26)

(\mu_{A}(z),\hat{e}^{c}_{\zeta}(z)\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(z))=\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(a_{0})}{\zeta-a_{0}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(a_{-n})\left[\frac{1}{\zeta-a_{n}}+\frac{1}{\zeta-a_{-n}}\right]+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{n})-\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{-n})}{\zeta-a_{n}}.

By Proposition
5
, the first sum is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Then,

|\zeta-a_{n}|\geq\max\{|\mbox{Im }\zeta-\mbox{Im }\phi(n)|,|\mbox{Re }\zeta-\rho n-\mbox{Re }\phi(n)|\}\geq\max\{K/2\pi-H,||\mbox{Re }\zeta-\rho n|-M|\}.

The series

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\max\{K/2\pi-H,||\mbox{Re }\zeta-\rho n|-M|\}]^{-2}

represents a periodic function on the line
\mbox{Im }\zeta=K/2\pi

S:=\sup\left\{\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\zeta-a_{n}|^{-2}:\,\mbox{Im }\zeta=K/2\pi\right\}<\infty.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz-Bunyakovskii inequality, we obtain

(27)

\left|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{n})-\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{-n})}{\zeta-a_{n}}\right|\leq S^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{n})-\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{-n})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

Next, taking into account that
\hat{\varphi}^{c}
is even, we get

\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{n})-\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{-n})\leq\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon\rho n+\varepsilon\phi(n))-\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon\rho n-\varepsilon\phi(-n))\leq 2M\varepsilon\max\{|(\hat{\varphi}^{c})^{\prime}(z)|:\,z\in I_{n}\},

where the number
M
is the same as in Proposition

and

is the segment
[\varepsilon\rho n-\varepsilon\phi(-n),\varepsilon\rho n+\varepsilon\phi(n)]
.
It follows from (

) that

\max\{|(\hat{\varphi}^{c})^{\prime}(z)|:\,z\in I_{n}\}\leq C\max_{I_{n}}\min\{1,|z|^{-1}\}\leq\min\{1,(\varepsilon\rho n-\varepsilon M)^{-1}\}.

Hence,

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}|\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{n})-\hat{\varphi}^{c}(\varepsilon a_{-n})|^{2}\leq 4\varepsilon^{2}M^{2}C^{2}\left[\sum_{1\leq n\leq 1/\varepsilon\rho+M/\rho}1+\sum_{n>1/\varepsilon\rho+M/\rho}(\varepsilon\rho n-\varepsilon M)^{-2}\right].

The first sum in the right-hand side is
O(1/\varepsilon)
, and second one is
o(1/\varepsilon^{2})
. Therefore, (
27
) tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and (
26
) is uniformly bounded for small
\varepsilon
.
Applying Lebesgue’s Dominate Convergence Theorem, we can take the limit in (
26
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We obtain

(28)

(\mu_{A}(z),\hat{e}^{c}_{\zeta}(z)\hat{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}^{c}(z))\to\frac{1}{\zeta-a_{0}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{\zeta-a_{n}}+\frac{1}{\zeta-a_{-n}}\right],\qquad\varepsilon\to 0.

n(s)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma:\,0<\gamma<s}|b_{\gamma}|.

By (
23
),

(29)

\sum_{\gamma\geq r}|b_{\gamma}|e^{-2\pi\gamma y}=\int_{r}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi sy}n(ds)\leq\lim_{T\to\infty}n(T)e^{-2\pi Ty}+2\pi y\int_{r}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi sy}n(s)ds<\infty.

Therefore the series
\sum_{\gamma>0}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma\zeta}
converges absolutely and uniformly on the line
\mbox{Im }\zeta=K/2\pi
.

Next, we have for
\varepsilon>0

(30)

\frac{i}{2\pi}(\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}(t),e_{\zeta}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t))=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma\zeta}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\gamma}e^{-2\pi is\zeta}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds.

It is easy to see that all integral on the right-hand side do not exceed

1/2,&\text{if }\gamma=0,\end{cases}

\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\gamma}e^{-2\pi is\zeta}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(s)ds=\int_{-1}^{\max\{\gamma/\varepsilon,-1\}}e^{-2\pi i\varepsilon s\zeta}\varphi(s)ds\to 0,\quad\text{if }\gamma<0.

(\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}(t),e_{\zeta}\star\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t))\to-2\pi i\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap(0,+\infty)}b_{\gamma}e^{2\pi i\gamma\zeta}-\pi ib_{0},\qquad\varepsilon\to 0.

(
22
), (
25
), and (
28
) yield (
24
).
\square

Proof of Theorem

Let
A\subset\mathcal{S}_{H}\setminus\{0\}
be an almost periodic set,
\mu_{A}
be the measure (
7
), and the pure point measure
\hat{\mu}_{A}^{c}
satisfy (
9
) and (
23
) with
L>2\pi H
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
0\not\in A
. We integrate the equality (
24
) over the segment
[iy_{0},x+iy_{0}]
with

and change the order of summation and integration. We get

\log f(x+iy_{0})-\log f(iy_{0})=\int_{iy_{0}}^{x+iy_{0}}\frac{f^{\prime}(\zeta)}{f(\zeta)}d\zeta=-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma\cap(0,+\infty)}b_{\gamma}\frac{(e^{2\pi i\gamma x}-1)e^{-2\pi\gamma y_{0}}}{\gamma}-ib_{0}\pi x.

f(x+iy_{0})e^{ib_{0}\pi x}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\beta_{\omega}e^{2\pi i\omega x},\qquad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}|\beta_{\omega}|<\infty,

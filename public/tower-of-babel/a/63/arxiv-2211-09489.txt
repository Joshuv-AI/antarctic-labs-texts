How does SU( N )-natural inflation isotropize the universe? Preprint: RUP-22-23
Tomoaki Murata, Tomohiro Fujita, Tsutomu Kobayashi

Abstract
We study the homogeneous and anisotropic dynamics of pseudoscalar inflation coupled to an SU( N N ) gauge field. To see how the initially anisotropic universe is isotropized in such an inflation model, we derive the equations to obtain axisymmetric SU( N N ) gauge field configurations in Bianchi type-I geometry and discuss a method to identify their isotropic subsets which are the candidates of their late-time attractor. Each isotropic solution is characterized by the corresponding SU(2) subalgebra of the SU( N N ) algebra. It is shown numerically that the isotropic universe is a universal late-time attractor in the case of the SU(3) gauge field. Interestingly, we find that a transition between the two distinct gauge-field configurations characterized by different SU(2) subalgebras can occur during inflation. We clarify the conditions for this to occur. This transition could leave an observable imprint on the CMB and the primordial gravitational wave background.



I
Introduction

It is widely believed that
inflation
Starobinsky:1980te
;
Guth:1980zm
;
Sato:1980yn
,
a prolonged phase of the quasi-de Sitter expansion,
occurred in the early universe.
The inflationary mechanism solves the problems concerning the
initial conditions of the standard Big Bang model, i.e.,
the flatness and horizon problems, and thus
naturally explains the homogeneity and isotropy of the universe.
Inflation can also generate primordial density fluctuations
that are consistent with observations such as the CMB anisotropies.

For a given inflationary model to be successful,
it is required that no particular fine-tuned initial conditions
are imposed at the beginning of inflation. For example,
inflation would be spoiled if initial anisotropies were not diluted away
in the early universe. Fortunately, according to Wald’s cosmic no-hair
theorem
Wald:1983ky
,
all Bianchi models except type IX isotropize toward de Sitter
in the presence of a positive cosmological constant.
However, one has to be careful when applying this theorem to
models of inflation, because the actual inflationary expansion
is caused not by a cosmological constant but by a potential energy
of a dynamical scalar field.
In the case of power-law inflation, this point was addressed in Refs.
Moss:1986ud
;
Kitada:1991ih
;
Rendall:2004ic
.
The inflationary isotropization process is particularly nontrivial
in the presence of vector fields.
In fact, it is possible to construct an inflationary model
with a U(1) gauge field coupled to a scalar field
through a particular coupling function
that violates the cosmic no-hair theorem, though
the amount of remaining anisotropies is slow-roll suppressed
Watanabe:2009ct

(see Ref.
Yamamoto:2012tq
for the
extension of multiple U(1) gauge fields).
This anisotropic inflationary model has been extended
to the cases of a SU(2) gauge field
Maeda:2013daa

and a SU(3) gauge field
Gao:2021qwl
coupled to
a scalar field.
These examples show that an inflationary universe does not
always isotropize in the presence of a vector field
Maleknejad:2011jr
;
Maleknejad:2012as
.

The chromo-natural inflation (CNI) model
Adshead:2012kp

is a model of inflation well motivated by particle physics in which a pseudo-scalar axion and an SU(2) gauge field are
coupled through the Chern-Simons coupling
(see Ref.
Maleknejad:2012fw
for a review).
It has been shown in Refs.
Maleknejad:2013npa
;
Wolfson:2020fqz
;
Wolfson:2021fya

that the Bianchi type-I metric in the CNI model generically isotropize
and the isotropic solution is an attractor in favor of the cosmic no-hair theorem.
Although the CNI model is an interesting class of inflationary models,
the model was already excluded according to observations
Adshead:2013nka

(see, however, some extended models compatible with observations
Dimastrogiovanni:2016fuu
;
Adshead:2016omu
).

The CNI model has been extended to the case with
a general SU(
N
) gauge field in Ref.
Fujita:2021eue
.
It has been shown that this SU(
N
)-natural inflation model
admits an isotropic configuration of the gauge field
forming an SU(2) subalgebra in SU(
N
)
on a homogeneous and isotropic spacetime
Fujita:2021eue
.
The amplitude and energy density of the gauge field
depend on the gauge coupling
g_{A}
and a parameter

\lambda
, which characterizes the embedding of an SU(2) subalgebra,
through the combination of
g_{A}\lambda
.
Linear perturbations of SU(
N
)-natural inflation
have been investigated in Ref.
Fujita:2022fff
, showing that
the perturbation dynamics and linear predictions depend on
\lambda

only through the same combination
g_{A}\lambda
.
As the dynamics of SU(
N
)-natural inflation is thus obtained simply
by replacing
g_{A}
of CNI with
g_{A}\lambda
,
the former model cannot be distinguished observationally from the latter
unless, for instance, a gauge-field configuration of one of the SU(2) subalgebras
is unstable and transits to another isotropic configuration
during inflation.

In this paper, we study the homogeneous dynamics of SU(
N
)-natural inflation
in more detail, improving the previous analysis of
Fujita:2021eue

in two respects.
First, we investigate whether or not
the isotropic solution is the late-time attractor when starting from
anisotropic initial configurations. Our analysis is more rigorous than
that of Ref.
Fujita:2021eue
because
we solve the Einstein equations and the equations of motion for the gauge fields and
the scalar field consistently.
Second, we clarify the conditions for the transition from
an isotropic gauge-field configuration of one of the SU(2) subalgebras
to another to occur.

This paper is organized as follows.
In the next section, we review the isotropic solution of the
SU(
N
)-natural inflation model.
We then derive the conditions for a configuration of SU(
N
) gauge fields
to be axisymmetric, and consider the isotropic limit
as a candidate for the late-time attractor
in Sec.
III
.
We also show that the spacetime metric is istropized once the
x
,
y
, and
z
-components of the gauge fields have the same norm.
In Sec.
IV
,
by restricting ourselves to the case of SU(3),
we provide several examples of axisymmetric gauge field configurations.
In Sec.
V
, we
present the results of our numerical analysis on the
dynamics of the axisymmetric SU(3)-natural inflation.
On the basis of our numerical results,
we draw our conclusions in Sec.
VI
.

II
Review of SU(
N
)-natural inflation

In this section, we review the SU(
N
)-natural inflation model and introduce
its homogeneous and isotropic cosmological solution
Fujita:2021eue
.
The SU(
N
)-natural inflation model is described by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F_{a}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial\phi\right)^{2}-V(\phi)-\frac{\phi}{4f}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}_{a},

(1)

where
\phi
is the inflaton scalar field,
V(\phi)
is its potential,
and
f
is a constant parameter.
The field strength
F_{\mu\nu}^{a}
of the SU(
N
) gauge field
A_{\mu}^{a}

and its dual
\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}_{a}
are defined by

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}^{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{a}+g_{A}f^{abc}A_{\mu}^{b}A_{\nu}^{c},

(2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}F_{\rho\lambda}^{a},

(3)

where
f^{abc}
is the structure constant of the SU(
N
) algebra,

g_{A}
is the gauge coupling constant, and

\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\lambda}/\sqrt{-g}

is the Levi-Cività tensor with
\epsilon^{0123}=1
.

Let us consider homogeneous and isotropic cosmology in this model. The Friedmann‐Lemaître‐Robertson-Walker metric is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2}+\mathrm{d}z^{2}\right),

(4)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor.
Here we assume the spatial flatness. It is nontrivial to include the spatial curvature in the universe with a similar gauge-field configuration
Murata:2021vnb
.
Throughout the paper, we use the temporal gauge,
A_{0}^{a}=0
.
It has been shown in Ref.
Fujita:2021eue

that the SU(2) subalgebra of the SU(
N
) algebra allows for an isotropic solution.
The isotropic gauge field configuration can be expressed as

\displaystyle A_{0}(t)=0,\quad A_{i}(t)=a(t)\psi(t){\cal T}_{i},

(5)

where
{\cal T}_{i}
is the generator of the SU(2) subalgebra.
The SU(2) generators can be expressed as a linear combination of
the SU(
N
) generators
T^{a}
:
{\cal T}_{i}=n^{a}_{i}T^{a}
.
The SU(2) generators satisfy the commutation relation and the normalization condition,

\displaystyle\left[{\cal T}_{i},{\cal T}_{j}\right]=i\lambda\epsilon_{ijk}{\cal T}_{k},\quad\operatorname{Tr}\left({\cal T}_{i}{\cal T}_{j}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij},

(6)

where
\lambda

is a number that is
different depending on the choice of the SU(2) subalgebra
of SU(
N
).

Using the ansatz (
5
), we obtain
the equations of motion for
\phi
and
\psi
as

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+V_{,\phi}=-\frac{3g_{A}\lambda}{f}\psi^{2}\left(\dot{\psi}+H\psi\right),

(7)

\displaystyle\ddot{\psi}+3H\dot{\psi}+\left(2H^{2}+\dot{H}\right)\psi+2g_{A}^{2}\lambda^{2}\psi^{3}=\frac{g_{A}\lambda}{f}\dot{\phi}\psi^{2},

(8)

where the dot represents differentiation with respect to
t

and
H:=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter.
The same equations can be obtained just by replacing
g_{A}
with
g_{A}\lambda

in the inflaton and gauge-field equations of motion in
original SU(2) chromo-natural inflation
Adshead:2012kp
.
Therefore, one would expect qualitatively the same dynamics of
homogeneous and isotropic cosmology as that in the previous chromo-natural model.
Assuming slow roll, one obtains a solution for the gauge field
staying at the minimum of its effective potential
Adshead:2012kp
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\rm min}^{3}=-\frac{fV_{,\phi}}{3g_{A}\lambda H}.

(9)

The solution can also be derived by dropping all the time derivatives
in Eq. (
7
).

To simplify the following analysis,
we use the linear potential,

\displaystyle V(\phi)=-\frac{\mu^{4}}{f}\phi,

(10)

where
\mu
is a constant having the dimension of mass.
In this simplified setup,
we would expect that
the isotropic solution for the gauge field
in the slow-roll approximation reduces to

\displaystyle H^{1/3}\psi_{\rm min}=\left(\frac{\mu^{4}}{3g_{A}\lambda}\right)^{1/3}=\textrm{const}.

(11)

Since
\mu
and
g_{A}
are constant model parameters, the slow-roll value of
H^{1/3}\psi
depends only on
\lambda
as
H^{1/3}\psi_{\rm min}\propto\lambda^{-1/3}
.

Having introduced the SU(
N
)-natural inflation model and reviewed the isotropic solution, let us move to discuss axisymmetric cosmological solutions in that model.

III
Axisymmetric configurations of SU(
N
) gauge fields

One of the purposes of this paper is to discuss the robustness of
the isotropic solution in SU(
N
)-natural inflation to initial anisotropies.
This point was studied in Ref.
Fujita:2021eue

for randomly oriented initial gauge field configurations
with the
fixed
scalar-field evolution on a
fixed

de Sitter background. In this paper, we improve the previous analysis
by consistently solving all the relevant equations, i.e., the Einstein equations and
the equations of motion for the gauge fields and the inflaton.
To handle this generalization, we focus on an axisymmetric setup.
In this section, we rigorously derive the equations to obtain
axisymmetric SU(
N
) gauge field configurations and discuss a method to
identify their isotropic subsets which are the candidates of their late-time attractor.

III.1
Conditions for axisymmetric SU(
N
) gauge fields

Following Refs.
Murata:2011wv
;
Gao:2021qwl
,
let us consider axisymmetric gauge-field configurations
in the axisymmetric Bianchi type-I geometry,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left[e^{-4\sigma(t)}\mathrm{d}x^{2}+e^{2\sigma(t)}\left(\mathrm{d}y^{2}+\mathrm{d}z^{2}\right)\right],

(12)

where
\sigma(t)
characterizes the deviation from the isotropic space.
Note that the metric (
12
) is isotropic when

\dot{\sigma}=0
, because constant
\sigma
can always be absorbed
into a rescaling of the spatial coordinates.

Since we consider homogeneous SU(
3
) gauge fields in the temporal gauge (
A_{0}^{a}=0
),
the gauge fields can be written as

\displaystyle A=A_{x}(t)\mathrm{d}x+A_{y}(t)\mathrm{d}y+A_{z}(t)\mathrm{d}z.

(13)

We assume that the
principal axes of metric and gauge-field anisotropies point to the same direction.
An infinitesimal rotational transformation around this axis is generated by
the Killing vector
\xi=-z\partial_{y}+y\partial_{z}
.
Under this rotational transformation, the gauge field transforms as

\displaystyle A^{a}\mapsto A^{a}+\epsilon\mathscr{L}_{\xi}A^{a},\qquad\mathscr{L}_{\xi}A^{a}=A_{z}^{a}\mathrm{d}y-A_{y}^{a}\mathrm{d}z,

(14)

where
\epsilon
is an infinitesimal parameter.
Even if the gauge field configuration is not invariant under the above transformation, the configuration is regarded to be axisymmetric as long as there exists a gauge transformation that cancels the effect of the rotational transformation.
There is a residual global gauge symmetry,
A\to UAU^{\dagger}
,
and in the case of an infinitesimal transformation
with
U=1+i\epsilon u^{a}T^{a}
the gauge field transforms as

\displaystyle A^{a}\mapsto A^{a}+\epsilon\delta_{g}A^{a},\qquad\delta_{g}A^{a}=-f^{abc}u^{b}A^{c},

(15)

where
u^{a}
is a constant vector.
To maintain the rotational invariance,
we require that the two transformations are compensated:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\xi}A^{a}+\delta_{g}A^{a}=0.

(16)

We thus obtain the conditions for the axisymmetric configuration as

\displaystyle f^{abc}u^{b}A_{x}^{c}=0,\quad f^{abc}u^{b}A_{y}^{c}=A_{z}^{a},\quad f^{abc}u^{b}A_{z}^{c}=-A_{y}^{a}.

(17)

For given
u^{a}
, the above equations can be used to determine the possible configurations of
A
.
We see that

A_{x}^{a}
is determined independently.
Eliminating
A_{z}^{a}
, we get the equation solely for
A_{y}^{a}
:

\displaystyle A_{y}^{a}=M^{ab}A_{y}^{b},\quad M^{ab}:=f^{ace}f^{bde}u^{c}u^{d}.

(18)

The explicit form of the

(N^{2}-1)\times(N^{2}-1)
matrix
M^{ab}
depends on
u^{a}
.
This equation may be considered as an eigenvalue equation, and then
A_{y}^{a}
must be an eigenvector with eigenvalue one.
We shall solve the above equations explicitly
in the case of SU(3) gauge fields in the next section.

III.2
Conditions for isotropic subsets

In the previous subsection, we derived the conditions for
axisymmetric configurations of the SU(
N
) gauge field.
In this subsection, we
further discuss conditions for the isotropic configurations,
which are included in the axisymmetric SU(
N
) configurations as subsets.
In the isotropic solution (
5
), the gauge field takes the form of
A_{i}=\sqrt{2}{\cal R}\mathcal{T}_{i}
with the common norm
\sqrt{2}{\cal R}:=a\psi

and the generators
\mathcal{T}_{i}
of the SU(2) subalgebra.
Multiplying the commutation relation (
6
)
of the SU(2) generators by the squared norm
2{\cal R}^{2}
,
we find the condition satisfied by the isotropic solution,

\displaystyle\left[A_{i},A_{j}\right]=\sqrt{2}i\lambda\epsilon_{ijk}{\cal R}A_{k}.

(19)

This condition enables us to remove anisotropic components and identify the isotropic configuration in the axisymmetric configurations.
In the next section, we will use Eq. (
19
) in
some cases of axisymmetric SU(3) gauge field configurations
to identify their isotropic subsets.

III.3
Isotropization of the metric

We now show that the spatial metric is isotropized when the norms of the gauge field are equal in the following sense.
In an axisymmetric Bianchi type-I universe, we decompose the gauge field in the temporal gauge
(A^{a}_{0}=0)
as

\displaystyle A_{i}^{a}\mathrm{d}x^{i}=\psi_{x}^{a}(t)\left[a(t)e^{-2\sigma(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}x+\psi_{y}^{a}(t)\left[a(t)e^{\sigma(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}y+\psi_{z}^{a}(t)\left[a(t)e^{\sigma(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}z,

(20)

where
i=1,2,3
and
a=1,\dots,N^{2}-1
.
Note that
\psi_{i}^{a}
does not include the metric variables (i.e.
a
and
\sigma
).
The above equation can be understood as a redefinition of the gauge field
A_{i}^{a}
into
\psi_{i}^{a}
, and its benefit will be seen below.
We define the norm of the gauge field for each
i
as

\displaystyle R_{i}(t):=\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}\left(\psi_{i}^{a}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\quad(\text{no sum over }i).

(21)

This expression implies that
\sqrt{2}R_{i}
corresponds to the radial coordinate in the
\left(N^{2}-1\right)
-dimensional spherical coordinate system when
\psi_{i}^{a}
is viewed as the Cartesian coordinate.
One can thus parametrize the components of
the SU(
N
) gauge field
using the spherical coordinates for each
i
as

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{a}=\sqrt{2}R_{i}(t)n_{i}^{a}(t),\qquad n_{i}^{a}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\cos\theta^{a}_{i}\prod_{b=1}^{a-1}\sin\theta^{b}_{i}&(a=1,\dots,N^{2}-2)\\
\displaystyle\prod_{b=1}^{N^{2}-2}\sin\theta^{b}_{i}&(a=N^{2}-1)\end{array}\right.\quad(\text{no sum over }i),

where
0\leq\theta^{a}_{i}(t)\leq\pi
(
a=1,\dots,N^{2}-3
) and

0\leq\theta^{N^{2}-2}_{i}(t)<2\pi
.
Furthermore, it is convenient to decompose
R_{i}
into
the geometric mean and the anisotropic part as

\displaystyle R_{i}=\bar{R}(t)e^{\beta_{i}(t)},\quad\bar{R}:=\left[R_{x}R_{y}R_{z}\right]^{1/3},\quad\sum_{i}^{3}\beta_{i}=0.

(24)

From the symmetry, we assume that
R_{y}=R_{z}
.
To summarize, we can express the component of the gauge field as

\displaystyle A^{a}_{i}=\psi^{a}_{i}ae^{\Sigma_{i}}=\sqrt{2}a\bar{R}e^{\beta_{i}+\Sigma_{i}}n_{i}^{a}\quad(\text{no sum over }i),

(25)

where we introduced the vector
\Sigma_{i}=\left(-2\sigma,\sigma,\sigma\right)

that represents the anisotropic part of the spatial metric.
Thanks to the redefinition (
20
), we now easily extract the terms involving the anisotropic part of the spatial metric
\sigma

from our Lagrangian to get

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\dot{\sigma}^{2}-2\dot{\sigma}\partial_{t}\left(R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}\right)-4H\dot{\sigma}\left(R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}\right)+\dot{\sigma}^{2}\left(4R_{x}^{2}+2R_{y}^{2}\right).

(26)

Since there is a symmetry under a constant shift
of the anisotropic part of the spatial metric,
\sigma\to\sigma+\,
const,
we have a conserved quantity (denoted as
{\cal D}
).
One can thus integrate the equation of motion for
\sigma

to obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}=\frac{{\cal D}/a^{3}+\partial_{t}\left(R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}\right)+2H\left(R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}\right)}{3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}+4R_{x}^{2}+2R_{y}^{2}},

(27)

If the anisotropic part of the gauge field decays away,

\beta_{i}\to 0
, then one has
R_{i}\to\bar{R}
,
and hence
\dot{\sigma}\to 0
at late times.
The spatial metric is therefore isotropized
if the norm of the gauge field defined by Eq. (
21
)
settles down to
R_{x}=R_{y}=R_{z}
regardless of
n_{i}^{a}
.
We can extend this result to the general Bianchi type-I case (see Appendix
B
).

In Sec.
III.2
, we discussed the condition for the isotropic solution forming the SU(2) subgroup. That condition not only requires that the three norms are equal
R_{i}=\bar{R}
but also determines the internal configuration of the gauge field
n^{a}_{i}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{i}=n_{i}^{a}T^{a}
.
However, we have seen above that requiring only the former condition leads to the isotropic space.
Therefore, the condition of the SU(2) subgroup (
19
) is in fact a sufficient condition for the metric isotropization.

Here we stress that
\sigma
can be disregarded in some cases.
One can always find a coordinate system in which
\sigma(t)=0
once
\sigma
stops evolving, since we have the freedom to shift the value of
\sigma
by a coordinate rescaling.
In this convenient coordinate system, we can easily study some conditions for the isotropic gauge field in the isotropized universe without having
\sigma
.
In what follows, therefore, when we discuss the isotropic configuration of the gauge field, we work in such a coordinate system where
\sigma
vanishes, given that the universe has been isotropized. In such cases, we simply have
A_{i}^{a}=a\psi_{i}^{a}
.
In contrast, when we discuss axisymmetric configurations of the gauge field, we cannot eliminate
\sigma

and will take it into account.
This is because axisymmetric configurations will be used as an initial condition of our numerical calculations in which the universe is still anisotropic, and then
A_{i}^{a}
contains nonzero
\sigma
as in Eq. (
20
).

IV
Examples of axisymmetric configurations of SU(3) gauge field and their isoropic subsets

In this section,
we compute axisymmetric configurations and their isotropic subsets of
the SU(3) gauge field by solving the conditions derived in Sec.
III
.
In principle, by solving Eq. (
18
) for generic
u^{a}

one can find the
general axisymmetric configuration of the SU(
N
) gauge field.
However, it is difficult to analytically find the general solution to Eq. (
18
). For this reason, we consider the SU(3) case with
some simple choices of
u^{a}
.
See Appendix
A
for a brief summary of the SU(3) algebra.

In the next section, we will perform numerical calculations to clarify whether and how the universe is isotropized in SU(3)-natural inflation by using axisymmetric configurations as the initial conditions.
It is known that the SU(2) subgroups in SU(3) are classified into two types characterized respectively by
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=1/2
.
The axisymmetric configurations desirable for our purpose contain the isotropic solutions with both
\lambda=1
and
1/2
as the subsets, and we therefore seek such configurations that have a parameter allowing us to switch between the two possible types. Fortunately, we can find such
a case with a simple choice of
u^{a}
, as we will see below.

IV.1
Example 1:
u^{1}=2
and others
\,=0

Let us consider the case
where
u^{1}
is nonvanishing, but the other
u^{a}
’s are zero,
namely,

u^{a}=(k,0,0,0,0,0,0,0),

(28)

with
k
being a real number.
Then, the matrix
M
appearing in Eq. (
18
)
has only its diagonal components as

\displaystyle M^{ab}={\rm diag.}\left(0,k^{2},k^{2},\frac{k^{2}}{4},\frac{k^{2}}{4},\frac{k^{2}}{4},\frac{k^{2}}{4},0\right).

(29)

As discussed below Eq. (
18
), the solution
A_{y}^{a}
of Eq. (
18
) may be regarded
as an eigenvector whose eigenvalue is unity.
Therefore, we have two branches of the solutions:
k=\pm 1
and
k=\pm 2
.
Note that the cases with a minus sign only invert the sign of
A_{z}
,
and hence it suffices to focus on the cases with a plus sign.

It is pedagogical to describe the calculation for
u^{1}=k=2
in detail. In this case,
since the fourth, fifth, sixth, and seventh diagonal components of the matric
M
are unity,
only
A_{y}^{4},A_{y}^{5},A_{y}^{6},A_{y}^{7}
are nonzero and the other components of
A_{y}
vanish.
Then,
A_{x}
and
A_{z}
can be easily obtained by solving Eq. (
17
). One finds the axisymmetric configuration of the SU(3) gauge field for
u^{1}=k=2
as

\displaystyle A_{x}=A_{x}^{1}T^{1}+A_{x}^{8}T^{8},\quad A_{y}=A_{y}^{4}T^{4}+A_{y}^{5}T^{5}+A_{y}^{6}T^{6}+A_{y}^{7}T^{7},\quad A_{z}=-A_{y}^{7}T^{4}+A_{y}^{6}T^{5}-A_{y}^{5}T^{6}+A_{y}^{4}T^{7},

(30)

where the six coefficients,
A_{x}^{1},A_{x}^{8},A_{y}^{4},A_{y}^{5},A_{y}^{6},A_{y}^{7}
, are arbitrary.
We have thus obtained an example of the axisymmetric configurations of the SU(3) gauge field.

To determine the isotropic solutions within the axisymmetric configuration (
30
),
we substitute Eq. (
30
) into the commutation relation (
19
). Two branches of the solution are found. One branch has
A_{x}^{8}\neq 0
and the other has
A_{x}^{8}=0
:

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}{\cal R}^{2}=2\left(A_{x}^{1}\right)^{2}=\left(A_{y}^{4}\right)^{2}+\left(A_{y}^{5}\right)^{2},\quad A_{x}^{8}=\pm\sqrt{3}A_{x}^{1},\quad A_{y}^{6}=\pm A_{y}^{4},\quad A_{y}^{7}=\pm A_{y}^{5},

\displaystyle(A_{x}^{8}\neq 0)

(31)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}{\cal R}^{2}=\frac{1}{8}\left(A_{x}^{1}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{8}\left[\left(A_{y}^{4}\right)^{2}+\left(A_{y}^{5}\right)^{2}+\left(A_{y}^{6}\right)^{2}+\left(A_{y}^{7}\right)^{2}\right],\quad A_{y}^{4}A_{y}^{6}+A_{y}^{5}A_{y}^{7}=0,

\displaystyle(A_{x}^{8}=0)

(32)

where the common norm of
A_{i}
is given by

{\cal R}^{2}={\cal R}_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{8}\left(A_{i}^{a}\right)^{2}\quad(\text{no sum over }i).

(33)

Using this expression for
{\cal R}
, one finds that Eqs. (
31
)
and (
32
) correspond to
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=1/2
, respectively.

Specializing the axisymmetric configuration (
30
)
to the case of Eq. (
31
) (
\lambda=1
),
we obtain

\displaystyle A_{x}=A_{x}^{1}\big(T^{1}+\sqrt{3}T^{8}\big),

(34)

\displaystyle A_{y}=A_{y}^{4}\left(T^{4}+T^{6}\right)+A_{y}^{5}\left(T^{5}+T^{7}\right),

(35)

\displaystyle A_{z}=-A_{y}^{5}\left(T^{4}+T^{6}\right)+A_{y}^{4}\left(T^{5}+T^{7}\right),

(36)

To rewrite the above expression in the manifestly isotropic form,
A_{i}=\sqrt{2}{\cal R}\mathcal{T}_{i}
, we define the SU(2) generators

{\cal T}_{i}
as

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{1}+\sqrt{3}T^{8}}{2},

(37)

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{2}

\displaystyle=\cos{\theta}\,\frac{T^{4}+T^{6}}{\sqrt{2}}+\sin{\theta}\,\frac{T^{5}+T^{7}}{\sqrt{2}},

(38)

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{3}

\displaystyle=-\sin{\theta}\,\frac{T^{4}+T^{6}}{\sqrt{2}}+\cos{\theta}\,\frac{T^{5}+T^{7}}{\sqrt{2}},

(39)

where we introduced spherical coordinates
A_{y}^{4}={\cal R}\cos{\theta},A_{y}^{5}={\cal R}\sin{\theta}
with
{\cal R}=\sqrt{2}A_{x}^{1}
.
It is easy to see that
{\cal T}_{i}
satisfies the properties of the SU(2) generators, Eq. (
6
), with
\lambda=1
.

We repeat the same procedure in the case of
Eq. (
32
) (
\lambda=1/2
) and find that
the gauge field can be written in the form

A_{i}=\sqrt{2}{\cal R}\mathcal{T}_{i}
with

\displaystyle A_{x}^{1}=\sqrt{2}{\cal R},\quad A_{y}^{4}=A_{x}^{1}\cos{\theta}\cos{\varphi},\quad A_{y}^{5}=A_{x}^{1}\cos{\theta}\sin{\varphi},\quad A_{y}^{6}=-A_{x}^{1}\sin{\theta}\sin{\varphi},\quad A_{y}^{7}=A_{x}^{1}\sin{\theta}\cos{\varphi}.

(40)

The SU(2) generators here are given by

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{1}=T^{1},

(41)

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{2}=\cos{\theta}\cos{\varphi}\,T^{4}+\cos{\theta}\sin{\varphi}\,T^{5}-\sin{\theta}\sin{\varphi}\,T^{6}+\sin{\theta}\cos{\varphi}\,T^{7},

(42)

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{3}=-\sin{\theta}\cos{\varphi}\,T^{4}-\sin{\theta}\sin{\varphi}\,T^{5}-\cos{\theta}\sin{\varphi}\,T^{6}+\cos{\theta}\cos{\varphi}\,T^{7}.

(43)

These generators satisfy the SU(2) subalgebra (
6
) with

\lambda=1/2
.

As we have seen, the present example admits the two SU(2) subgroups, which is exactly what we want.

IV.2
Example 2:
u^{1}=1
and others
\,=0

Let us consider again the case where
u^{a}
is
of the form of Eq. (
28
) and
hence the matrix is given by Eq. (
29
),
but now
k=1
.
The calculation can be done in a similar way to the case with
k=2
.
The axisymmetric configuration for
u^{1}=k=1
reads

\displaystyle A_{x}=A_{x}^{1}T^{1}+A_{x}^{8}T^{8},\quad A_{y}=A_{y}^{2}T^{2}+A_{y}^{3}T^{3},\quad A_{z}=-A_{y}^{3}T^{2}+A_{y}^{2}T^{3}.

(44)

Having thus obtained the axisymmetric configuration,
we further impose the conditions (
19
) for the isotropic subsets,
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(A_{x}^{1}\right)^{2}=\left(A_{y}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(A_{y}^{3}\right)^{2},\quad A_{x}^{8}=0,

(45)

and then
the isotropic solution of the form
A_{i}=\sqrt{2}{\cal R}{\cal T}_{i}

can be obtained with the SU(2) generators

\displaystyle{\cal T}_{1}=T^{1},\quad{\cal T}_{2}=\cos{\theta}\,T^{2}+\sin{\theta}\,T^{3},\quad{\cal T}_{3}=-\sin{\theta}\,T^{2}+\cos{\theta}\,T^{3}.

(46)

These SU(2) generators satisfy the commutation relation (
6
) with
\lambda=1
, and no other isotropic solution is available in the

u^{1}=1
case. Thus, the axisymmetric configuration for
u^{1}=1
does not include the isotropic solution with
\lambda=1/2
.

IV.3
Comments on the other cases

In the previous two subsections, we consider

u^{a}
with the single nonvanishing component,
u^{1}\neq 0
.
Let us comment on the other cases.
In the case where only a single component of
u^{a}

other than
u^{1}
and
u^{8}
is nonvanishing,
we have essentially the same results
as in the above two examples.
Therefore, for
u^{a}=2
(
a=2,\dots,7
) there is an axisymmetric
configuration that involves both
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=1/2

isotropic solutions.
In contrast,
in the case where only
u^{8}
is nonvanishing,
we have an isotropic configuration forming only
the
\lambda=1
SU(2) subalgebra.
One could also consider more general cases
where multiple components of
u^{a}
are nonvanishing.
There are some (rather simpler) cases
that are analytically tractable,
but in general the analysis would be so complicated
that an exhaustive investigation is hindered.
It should be emphasized, however, that
we have already obtained the physically interesting
axisymmetric configuration that admits
both
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=1/2
isotropic limits
in the case of
u^{1}=2
.

To address this question, we run numerical calculations.
Interestingly, there exists a nontrivial variety of the ways how the SU(3) gauge field isotropizes the universe, as we will see below.

vs. the “time spent close to

The initial conditions for

.

VI
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we have studied the homogeneous
and anisotropic dynamics of SU(
N
)-natural inflation.
We have focused mainly on axisymmetric gauge-field configurations
in the axisymmetric Bianchi type-I geometry to see whether and
how the anisotropies dilute in SU(
N
)-natural inflation.
This simplified axisymmetric setup allows us to solve
the Einstein equations and the equations of motion for
the inflaton and gauge fields consistently
instead of relying on the test field approximation
in the fixed cosmological background,
thus improving the earlier analysis of Ref.
Fujita:2021eue
.

We have derived the equations to obtain axisymmetric configurations of
the SU(
N
) gauge field and discussed the conditions under which
they are isotropic, and thus identified the candidates of
their late-time attractor.
On the basis of this argument, we have presented
an interesting explicit example
of axisymmetric configurations of the SU(3) gauge field
admitting two different SU(2) subalgebras characterized by
a number
\lambda
appearing in the commutation relation
of the SU(2) generators. (In this case, we have
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=1/2
.)
We have performed numerical calculations to see
the dynamics of this system in detail.
First, we have confirmed that SU(
N
) natural inflation
does indeed isotropize the universe.
Second, and more interestingly, we have found that
under certain conditions the isotropic solution with
the
\lambda=1/2
SU(2) subalgebra exhibits a transition to
the isotropic
\lambda=1
solution which is therefore more stable.
This feature was not seen in the numerical study of the previous work
Fujita:2021eue
, essentially because of a simplifying assumption
\dot{\phi}=\,
const there. By performing a linear stability analysis, we found the condition for the transition (
54
) and the growth rate of the instability (
56
).

The study of cosmological perturbations in SU(
N
)-natural inflation
has shown that the linear predictions depend on
\lambda

through
\lambda g_{A}
, where
g_{A}
is the gauge coupling constant,
implying that one cannot tell which isotropic solution is realized
at the level of linear perturbations
Fujita:2022fff
.
This degeneracy, however, is broken if the transition occurs
and
\lambda
changes its value
during the observable period of inflation.
Since the amplitude of gravitational waves sourced by a SU(
N
) gauge field is exponentially sensitive to the background gauge field amplitude
Fujita:2022fff
and the different isotropic solutions, such as
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=1/2
in the SU(3) case, have different amplitudes, the transition of the background solutions would lead to a step-like feature in the spectrum of the sourced gravitational waves.
Therefore, the transition could leave a detectable signature on the B-mode polarization of the cosmic microwave background radiation or the stochastic gravitational wave background at the interferometer frequency range depending on when it occurs. It would be interesting to further study these potential observable imprints of SU(
N
)-natural inflation and we leave it for future work.

Appendix B
THE CONDITIONS FOR ISOTROPIZATION IN GENERAL BIANCHI TYPE-I GEOMETRY

We extend the result of Sec.
III.3
to the general Bianchi type-I universe.
The general Bianchi type-I metric is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left[e^{-4\sigma_{+}(t)}\mathrm{d}x^{2}+e^{2\sigma_{+}(t)+2\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-}(t)}\mathrm{d}y^{2}+e^{2\sigma_{+}(t)-2\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-}(t)}\mathrm{d}z^{2}\right].

(60)

In general Bianchi type-I universe, we decompose the SU(
N
) gauge field in the temporal gauge
(A^{a}_{0}=0)
as

\displaystyle A_{i}^{a}\mathrm{d}x^{i}=\psi_{x}^{a}(t)\left[a(t)e^{-2\sigma_{+}(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}x+\psi_{y}^{a}(t)\left[a(t)e^{\sigma_{+}(t)+\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-}(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}y+\psi_{z}^{a}(t)\left[a(t)e^{\sigma_{+}(t)-\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-}(t)}\right]\mathrm{d}z,

(61)

where
i=1,2,3
and
a=1,\dots,N^{2}-1
.
We define the norm of the gauge field for each
i
as

\displaystyle R_{i}(t):=\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{N^{2}-1}\left(\psi_{i}^{a}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\quad(\text{no sum over }i).

(62)

This expression implies that
\sqrt{2}R_{i}
corresponds to the radial coordinate in the
\left(N^{2}-1\right)
-dimensional spherical coordinate system when
\psi_{i}^{a}
is viewed as the Cartesian coordinate.
One can thus parametrize the components of
the SU(
N
) gauge field
using the spherical coordinates for each
i
as

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{a}=\sqrt{2}R_{i}(t)n_{i}^{a}(t),\qquad n_{i}^{a}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\cos\theta^{a}_{i}\prod_{b=1}^{a-1}\sin\theta^{b}_{i}&(a=1,\dots,N^{2}-2)\\
\displaystyle\prod_{b=1}^{N^{2}-2}\sin\theta^{b}_{i}&(a=N^{2}-1)\end{array}\right.,\qquad(\text{no sum over }i),

where
0\leq\theta^{a}_{i}(t)\leq\pi
(
a=1,\dots,N^{2}-3
) and

0\leq\theta^{N^{2}-2}_{i}(t)<2\pi
.
Furthermore, it is convenient to decompose
R_{i}
into
the geometric mean and the anisotropic part as

\displaystyle R_{i}=\bar{R}(t)e^{\beta_{i}(t)},\quad\bar{R}:=\left[R_{x}R_{y}R_{z}\right]^{1/3},\quad\sum_{i}^{3}\beta_{i}=0.

(65)

From the symmetry we assume that
R_{y}=R_{z}
.
To summarize, we can express the component of the gauge field as

\displaystyle A^{a}_{i}=\psi^{a}_{i}ae^{\Sigma_{i}}=\sqrt{2}a\bar{R}e^{\beta_{i}+\Sigma_{i}}n_{i}^{a},\qquad(\text{no sum over }i),

(66)

where we introduce the vector as
\Sigma_{i}=\left(-2\sigma_{+},\sigma_{+}+\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-},\sigma_{+}-\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-}\right)

that represents the metric anisotropy.

The Chern-Simons term in the action can then be written as

\displaystyle F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}_{a}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\frac{g_{A}}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{t}\left(\epsilon^{ijk}f_{abc}A^{a}_{i}A^{b}_{j}A^{c}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\sqrt{2}g_{A}}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{t}\left[\left(a\bar{R}\right)^{3}f_{abc}n^{a}_{x}n^{b}_{y}n^{c}_{z}\right],

(67)

from which
we see that,
while
\bar{R}(t)
and the directions in the internal space,
n_{i}^{a}(t)
,
come into play,
the anisotropic part
\beta_{i}

of the gauge field does not make any contributions to the Chern-Simons term.
The anisotropic part of the gauge field
\beta_{i}
is not sourced by this term.
It implies that the inflaton’s energy is not directly transferred to the anisotropic part and one naively expects that the anisotropic part simply decays as the universe expands in the same way as free gauge fields. Nevertheless, there is a possibility that the isotropic part of the gauge field sources it through self-interaction. To check this possibility we performed numerical calculations in the axisymmetric cases. As shown in the bulk of this paper (see Fig.
2
), our numerical results indicate the anisotropic part is not sourced but quickly decays in the axisymmetric cases.
Therefore, we can also conjecture that the norm of the gauge field is isotropized
R_{i}\to\bar{R}
in the general Bianchi type-I geometry.

We now extract the terms involving the metric anisotropy
\sigma_{\pm}

from our Lagrangian to get

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\sigma_{+}}

\displaystyle=3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\left(\dot{\sigma}_{+}^{2}+\dot{\sigma}_{-}^{2}\right)-\dot{\sigma}_{+}\partial_{t}\left(2R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)+\sqrt{3}\dot{\sigma}_{-}\partial_{t}\left(R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle~~-2H\dot{\sigma}_{+}\left(2R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)+2\sqrt{3}\dot{\sigma}_{-}\left(H+\dot{\sigma}_{+}\right)\left(R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)+\dot{\sigma}_{+}^{2}\left(4R_{x}^{2}+R_{y}^{2}+R_{z}^{2}\right)+3\dot{\sigma}_{-}^{2}\left(R_{y}^{2}+R_{z}^{2}\right).

(68)

Since there is a symmetry under a constant shift
of the metric anisotropy,
\sigma_{\pm}\to\sigma_{\pm}+\,
const,
we have a conserved quantity (denoted as
{\cal D_{\pm}}
).
One can thus integrate the equation of motion for
\sigma_{\pm}

to obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{+}=\frac{{\cal D}_{+}/a^{3}+\partial_{t}\left(2R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)/2+H\left(2R_{x}^{2}-R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)-\sqrt{3}\sigma_{-}\left(R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)}{3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}+4R_{x}^{2}+R_{y}^{2}+R_{z}^{2}},

(69)

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}_{-}=\frac{{\cal D}_{-}/a^{3}+\sqrt{3}\partial_{t}\left(R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)/2+\sqrt{3}\left(H+\dot{\sigma}_{+}\right)\left(R_{y}^{2}-R_{z}^{2}\right)}{3\left(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}+R_{y}^{2}+R_{z}^{2}\right)},

(70)

If the anisotropic part of the gauge field decays away,

\beta_{i}\to 0
, then one has
R_{i}\to\bar{R}
,
and hence
\dot{\sigma}_{\pm}\to 0
at late times.
The spatial metric is therefore isotropized
if the norm of the gauge field defined by Eq. (
21
)
settles down to
R_{x}=R_{y}=R_{z}
regardless of
n_{i}^{a}
.

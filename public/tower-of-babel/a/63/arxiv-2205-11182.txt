A note on a stable algorithm for computing the fractional integrals of orthogonal polynomials
P. Amodio, L. Brugnano, F. Iavernaro

Abstract
In this note we provide an algorithm for computing the fractional integrals of orthogonal polynomials, which is more stable than that using the expression of the polynomials w.r.t. the canonical basis. This algorithm is aimed at solving corresponding fractional differential equations. A few numerical examples are reported. Keywords: fractional differential equations, fractional integrals, orthogonal polynomials, Legendre polynomials, Chebyshev polynomials. AMS-MSC: 65L05, 65L03.

2
Computing the fractional integrals

As is well-known, the family of orthonormal polynomials (
5
)-(
6
) satisfies a 3-term recurrence

\displaystyle P_{0}(c)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 1,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad c\in[0,1],

\displaystyle P_{1}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}c-b_{1})P_{0}(c),

(14)

\displaystyle P_{j}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{j}c-b_{j})P_{j-1}(c)-d_{j}P_{j-2}(c),\qquad j\geq 2,

for suitable coefficients
a_{j},b_{j},d_{j}
,
j\geq 1
(with
d_{1}=0
).

As an example, the coefficients

a_{1}=2,\quad b_{1}=1,\qquad a_{j}=4,\quad b_{j}=2,\quad d_{j}=1,\qquad j\geq 2,

(15)

provide the shifted and scaled Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind, corresponding to the choice of the weighting function
\omega(c)=\left(\pi\sqrt{c(1-c)}\right)^{-1}
.

Another relevant example is provided by the shifted and scaled Legendre polynomials, corresponding to the weighting function
\omega(c)\equiv 1
, for which:

b_{j}=\sqrt{4-j^{-2}},\quad a_{j}=2b_{j},\quad j\geq 1,\qquad d_{j}=\frac{j-1}{j}\sqrt{\frac{2j+1}{2j-3}},\quad j\geq 2.

(16)

The following preliminary result holds true.

Lemma 1

For any given
\alpha>0
and function
g(c)
, and with reference to the function
\Psi^{\alpha}g(t)
defined in (
3
), one has:

\Psi^{\alpha}cg(c)=c\Psi^{\alpha}g(c)-\Psi^{\alpha+1}g(c).

Proof
In fact, from (
3
) one has:

\displaystyle\Psi^{\alpha}cg(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}(c-x)^{\alpha-1}xg(x)\mathrm{d}x~=~\int_{0}^{c}(c-x)^{\alpha-1}(x-c+c)g(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\int_{0}^{c}(c-x)^{\alpha-1}g(x)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{c}(c-x)^{\alpha}g(x)\mathrm{d}x~\equiv~c\Psi^{\alpha}g(c)-\Psi^{\alpha+1}g(c).\mbox{~$\Box{~}$}

Consequently, from the last property in (
4
), Lemma
1
, and with reference to (
13
), the following results follows (we omit their straightforward proofs, for sake of brevity).

Theorem 1

The fractional integrals of the polynomials (
14
) are given by

I^{\alpha}P_{j}(c)=\Psi^{\alpha}P_{j}(c)/\Gamma(\alpha),\qquad j\geq 0,

where:

\displaystyle\Psi^{\alpha}P_{0}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c^{\alpha}}{\alpha},

\displaystyle\Psi^{\alpha}P_{1}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}c-b_{1})\Psi^{\alpha}P_{0}(c)-a_{1}\Psi^{\alpha+1}P_{0}(c),

(17)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\alpha}P_{j}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{j}c-b_{j})\Psi^{\alpha}P_{j-1}(c)-a_{j}\Psi^{\alpha+1}P_{j-1}(c)-d_{j}\Psi^{\alpha}P_{j-2}(c),\qquad j\geq 2.

Corollary 1

For computing the fractional integrals

I^{\alpha}P_{0}(c),~I^{\alpha}P_{1}(c),~\dots,~I^{\alpha}P_{s-2}(c),~I^{\alpha}P_{s-1}(c),

(18)

by means of the recurrence scheme (
17
), one needs to compute the following triangular table:

\begin{array}[]{llllll}\Psi^{\alpha}P_{0}(c)\\
\Psi^{\alpha+1}P_{0}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha}P_{1}(c)\\
\Psi^{\alpha+2}P_{0}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha+1}P_{1}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha}P_{2}(c)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\Psi^{\alpha+s-2}P_{0}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha+s-3}P_{1}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha+s-4}P_{2}(c)&\dots&\Psi^{\alpha}P_{s-2}(c)\\
\Psi^{\alpha+s-1}P_{0}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha+s-2}P_{1}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha+s-3}P_{2}(c)&\dots&\Psi^{\alpha+1}P_{s-2}(c)&\Psi^{\alpha}P_{s-1}(c)\\
\end{array}

(19)

whose diagonal provides, upon division by
\Gamma(\alpha)
, the integrals (
18
).

4
Conclusions

In this note, we have devised a 3-term recurrence for computing fractional integrals of orthogonal polynomials. The corresponding algorithm has been used for solving fractional differential equations, and proved to be more effective than the standard approach based on the canonical basis.

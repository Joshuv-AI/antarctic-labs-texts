Abstract

Atoms with magnetic field-insensitive transitions are appealing for their use in quantum information science
Crain et al. 2014
(QIS) and precision measurement
Lewty et al. 2012
;
Pizzocaro et al. 2017
.
For ions with a single valence electron and half-integer nuclear spin, there is a ground state qubit composed of two total spin projection
m_{F}=0
states separated by the hyperfine splitting.
These states are first order-insensitive to magnetic field noise and are well-suited to laser driven gates
Ballance et al. 2016
;
Boguslawski et al. 2023
.
For laser-coolable ions with nuclear spin
I=1/2
, the qubit is straightforward to initialize and control optically or with microwaves.
This hyperfine qubit is a feature of
171
Yb
+

Balzer et al. 2006
;
Ma et al. 2022
and led to the development of the radioactive
133
Ba
+
qubit
Hucul et al. 2017
.
The hyperfine qubit’s features also benefit optical clocks based on
{}^{171}\mathrm{Yb}^{+}

Tamm et al. 2014
;
Huntemann et al. 2016
.
The radium-225 ion has nuclear spin
1/2
, a low charge to mass ratio, and optical transitions at convenient wavelengths
Fan et al. 2019
;
West et al. 2019
, making it a good optical clock candidate
Holliman et al. 2022
. The ion’s large hyperfine splittings make it possible to run an optical clock with only two infrared wavelengths
Holliman et al. 2023
, which is amenable to integrated photonic devices.

Searches for time reversal symmetry violation are motivated by the observed baryon asymmetry of the universe and the strong
CP
problem
Pospelov and Ritz 2005
. Radium-225 has an octupole-deformed nucleus that enhances its sensitivity to hadronic sources of
T
-violation
Auerbach et al. 1996
;
Dobaczewski and Engel 2005
;
Bishof et al. 2016
.
The sensitivity can be further enhanced by incorporating radium-225 into a molecule
Yu and Hutzler 2021
, which can be produced with laser-cooled
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}

Fan et al. 2021
.

Radium is famous for its radioactivity and radium-225 is no exception with only a
15\text{\,}\mathrm{d}
half-life.
Despite this apparent obstacle we have worked with laser-cooled
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
ions in a sealed vacuum system since
June 6
{}^{\text{th}}
, 2023
.
The key to seamless operation is the oven design based on
Fan
et al.

Fan et al. 2023

7800\text{\,}\mathrm{\text{~yr}}
half-life
Essex et al. 2018
).

To laser cool
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
we drive optical transitions from hyperfine levels of the
{}^{2}S_{1/2}

The
{}^{2}S_{1/2}

The ground state hyperfine splitting was re-measured
Neu et al. 1989

47\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
(

With laser-cooled
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
we measured the hyperfine structure of all three states used for laser cooling and state detection, improving the precision of the
{}^{2}S_{1/2}
and
{}^{2}P_{1/2}

The
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}

8\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro\text{Ci}}
of
{}^{229}\mathrm{Th}
deposited in a titanium crucible.
The oven is heated to between

and

Radium ions are loaded into the trap with a two-stage process: neutral radium is driven to the
{}^{1}P_{1}

Radium ions are consistently trapped within
15\text{\,}\min

The ions are held in a linear Paul trap with characteristic radial and axial dimensions
r_{0}=

0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}
and

2.5\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}

Fan et al. 2021

The radial electrodes are driven at
8.205\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
and dc offsets of
+200\text{\,}\mathrm{mV}
and

We can trap chains of ions, see Fig.

Ions are laser-cooled with four lasers to address all hyperfine levels in the
{}^{2}S_{1/2}

{}^{2}D_{3/2}

The frequency and power of each laser is independently controlled with an acousto-optic modulator set up in a double-pass configuration.
Decays from the

638(\pm 10)\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}

The metastable state hyperfine levels are repumped with 1079 nm light.
Both 468 nm lasers and one repump laser are stabilized to an optical cavity, while the second repump laser is offset-locked

We use the first-order sidebands of fiber electro-optic modulators (EOMs) to scan the 468 nm and 1079 nm light across the hyperfine states and also use the 468 nm EOM for state preparation, following

Olmschenk et al. 2007

We drive magnetic dipole transitions between the ground state hyperfine levels with microwaves emitted from a horn positioned
10\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}
from the trap center.
The microwaves are generated by mixing a fixed tone at
27.3\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
with a tunable rf source from a direct digital synthesizer, which is frequency doubled to near
385\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
.

Each hyperfine splitting measurement consists of Doppler cooling the ion, preparing the target state, scanning the transition, and state detection.
During state detection we drive the
S_{1/2}(F=1)\leftrightarrow~P_{1/2}(F=0)
and
D_{3/2}(F=1)\leftrightarrow~P_{1/2}(F=0)
transitions.
The
{}^{2}S_{1/2}(F=1)
and
{}^{2}D_{3/2}(F=1)
states are referred to as the bright states, highlighted in blue in Fig.
1
b
.
When the ion is in one of the dark states,
{}^{2}S_{1/2}(F=0)
or
{}^{2}D_{3/2}(F=2)
, light is not scattered as the dark states do not decay and are far off resonance from the state detection lasers.
Scattered 468 nm photons are focused onto a photomultiplier tube (PMT) and camera by an objective.

We characterize the hyperfine qubit by measuring the state preparation and measurement (SPAM) fidelity.
After preparing the dark ground state, we transfer the population to the bright ground state with a
235\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro s}
resonant microwave pulse and perform a state detection.
This is compared to a state detection after only dark state preparation (no microwave pulse).
From the SPAM measurement we find a
2.5\text{\,}
photon
threshold to discriminate between bright events (
11.6\text{\,}
photons on average
) and dark events (
0.04\text{\,}
photons on average
) in a
1.7\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}
detection time, see Fig.
2(a)
.
The threshold minimizes the average false detection fraction, given by
1/2\left(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1}\right)

Christensen et al. 2020
, where
\epsilon_{0}
(
\epsilon_{1}
) is the fraction of dark (bright) events falsely identified as bright (dark) events, and is related to the fidelity by
\mathcal{F}=1-1/2\left(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1}\right)
.
We determine the fidelity of
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
in our system from an average of
13
separate measurements.
Our measured average SPAM fidelity is
\mathcal{F}=

0.9951(\pm 0.0009)\text{\,}
, with false identification fractions of
\epsilon_{0}=
0.0035(\pm 0.0008)\text{\,}
and
\epsilon_{1}=

0.007(\pm 0.002)\text{\,}
.
Our result is limited by off-resonant scattering, PMT dark counts, and the
0.2241(\pm 0.0011)\text{\,}\%
light collection efficiency.

The
S_{1/2}(F=1,m_{F}=0)\leftrightarrow~S_{1/2}(F=0,m_{F}=0)\

\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{QZ}}=C_{2}B^{2}\ ,

where

is the quadratic Zeeman shift coefficient and

We measured
\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{QZ}}

, and from the fit we extract the quadratic Zeeman shift coefficient,
C_{2}=

142.3(\pm 1.0)\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{G}}}^{-2}
.
, which is consistent with the calculated value of
141\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{G}}}^{-2}

Versolato et al. 2011

To measure the ground state hyperfine splitting, we prepare the
{}^{2}S_{1/2}(F=0,m_{F}=0)
dark state and then scan a
10\text{\,}\mathrm{ms}
microwave pulse across the
\pi

S_{1/2}(F=0,m_{F}=0)\leftrightarrow~S_{1/2}(F=1,m_{F}=0)

2(c)

To obtain the unperturbed
\pi

\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{QZ}}(B=

3.0\text{\,}\mathrm{\text{G}}
)
is subtracted from the scanned frequency.
We average
18

A\left(S_{1/2}\right)=
-27.684\,511\,056(\pm 0.000\,000\,009)\text{\,}\mathrm{GHz}
.

The
0.9\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
statistical uncertainty is negligible compared to the
9\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
systematic uncertainty.

The dominant source of uncertainty of the
{}^{2}S_{1/2}
hyperfine splitting comes from the second-order Zeeman shift, which shifts the frequency of the measured transition by
\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{QZ}}(B)

To determine the magnetic field
B
at the ion, we perform Rabi spectroscopy on the
\sigma
transitions,
(F=0,m_{F}=0)\leftrightarrow(F=1,m_{F}=\pm 1)
,
and compute

The average frequency of the
\sigma

\pi

\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{QZ}}(B)

The uncertainties for the quadratic Zeeman shift data points are derived from fits to the transitions, and contribute
9\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
to the measured ground state hyperfine splitting.

The second largest contribution to the systematic uncertainty is inaccuracy of the rf source, which is locked to a GPS-disciplined reference (SRS FS740).
The short-term stability of this reference is
1\text{\times}{10}^{-11}\text{\,}
, and we assign an uncertainty of
0.3\text{\,}\mathrm{Hz}
to this systematic.

We investigated frequency shifts due to the ac magnetic field of the rf trap drive by measuring the
\pi
transition frequency for a range of trap drive powers.
We also tested for ac Stark shifts from probe laser light leakage.
We did not observe any frequency shifts due to either of these effects.

We compare our result with previous measurements and a theoretical calculation of
A\left(S_{1/2}\right)
, see Fig.
3
. Of the two previous
A\left(S_{1/2}\right)
hyperfine constant measurements, our result agrees with the first measurement
Ahmad et al. 1983
. The contribution to hyperfine splittings due to the nuclear magnetization distribution is known as the Bohr-Weisskopf (BW) effect. For
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
the BW effect is relatively large but challenging to compute, and dominates the
380\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
uncertainty for the calculated value of
A\left(S_{1/2}\right)

Ginges et al. 2017

The
{}^{2}D_{3/2}

The 468 nm EOM sideband drives the

We reference the laser carrier frequency by performing spectroscopy on the
D_{3/2}(F=2)\leftrightarrow P_{1/2}(F=1)

To measure the hyperfine splitting of the

{}^{2}P_{1/2}

{}^{2}D_{3/2}

The resulting uncertainty is

for the

The hyperfine levels are frequency-shifted in the presence of laser light due to the ac Stark effect.
We investigated frequency shifts of the
{}^{2}P_{1/2}
and
{}^{2}D_{3/2}
states due to the probe lasers by varying the 468 nm laser power from
1.7\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro W}11.0\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro W}
and varying the 1079 nm laser power from

We did not observe ac Stark shifts for either the
{}^{2}P_{1/2}
or
{}^{2}D_{3/2}

10\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
, well below the statistical uncertainty.

In summary, we have operated a radium-225 ion trap for over a year in a sealed vacuum system and measured the hyperfine splitting of the electronic states used for laser cooling and state detection.
The demonstrated long-term source of short-lived
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}
isotopes enables the development of a
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
ion optical clock, which has field-insensitive states that suppress magnetic field noise compared to a nuclear spin-zero ion optical clock
Holliman et al. 2022
, as well as experiments to test fundamental symmetries with radium-bearing molecules. Radium-225 ions are also a potential route to trapped actinium ions, as
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}

\beta
-decays
to
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ac}
, and therefore
{}^{225}\mathrm{Ra}^{+}
decays could result in trapped
225
Ac
2+
, which supports laser cooling and state detection in two independent manifolds.

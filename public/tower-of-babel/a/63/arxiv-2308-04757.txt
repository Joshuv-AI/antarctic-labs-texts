1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a real-valued random variable and set
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
to be independent copies of
X
. Denote by
F(t)=\mathbb{P}(X\leq t)
the
distribution function
of
X
and let

F_{m}(t)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i})

be the
empirical distribution function
.
A fundamental question in probability theory and (nonparametric) statistics is whether and to what extent the empirical distribution function
F_{m}
can serve as a proxy for the true (unknown) distribution function
F
.
A cornerstone result in that direction is the celebrated
Glivenko-Cantelli
theorem
[
2
,
5
]
:
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|
tends to zero almost surely as the sample size
m
tends to infinity.
A powerful generalization of the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem is the
Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz (DKW)
inequality
[
3
]
which describes the speed of convergence.
The following formulation of the DKW inequality is taken from
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
\Delta>0
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-2\Delta m)
,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq\sqrt{\Delta}.

It is well known that Theorem
1.1
is optimal, but at the same time it is reasonable to expect that the deviation
|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|
is much smaller than
\sqrt{\Delta}
when
F(t)
is significantly smaller or significantly larger than
1/2
.
Specifically, let

\sigma^{2}(t)=\mathrm{\mathbb{V}ar}\left(1_{(-\infty,t]}(X)\right)=F(t)(1-F(t)),

and observe that

\mathrm{\mathbb{V}ar}\left(F_{m}(t)\right)=\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{m}.

Therefore, a natural conjecture is that the typical behaviour of
|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|
is of the order of
\sigma(t)/\sqrt{m}
, and the purpose of this note is show that such an estimate is (almost) true:

Theorem 1.2
.

There are absolute constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
such that the following holds.
For every

\Delta\geq c_{0}\frac{\log\log m}{m},

with probability at least
1-2\exp(-c_{1}\Delta m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
we have

\left|F_{m}(t)-F(t)\right|\leq\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

Remark 1.3
.

The focus of Theorem
1.2
is on the range
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
in which the behaviour of
|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|
is governed by cancellations.
For smaller values of
\sigma^{2}(t)
(relative to
\Delta
) no cancellations are to be expected, and if
F(t)\ll\Delta
then

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\sim\max\left\{F_{m}(t),F(t)\right\}.

The ‘no cancellations’ version of Theorem
1.2
is studied in Section
5
.

At a first glance, the restriction
\Delta\gtrsim\frac{\log\log m}{m}
in Theorem
1.2
might appear unnatural, especially as it prevents obtaining a uniform error of
\sigma(t)/\sqrt{m}
.
Note that for a
fixed

t
that satisfies
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
, it follows from Bernstein’s inequality that

\mathbb{P}\left(|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\geq\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}\right)\leq 2\exp(-c\Delta m),

which is nontrivial when
\Delta\gtrsim\frac{1}{m}
.
On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
holds
uniformly
in
t
, and then the condition that
\Delta\gtrsim\frac{\log\log m}{m}
is necessary:

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that there is a positive sequence
(\Delta_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
and numbers
\alpha,\beta>0
for which the following holds:
For every
\Delta\geq\Delta_{m}
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\alpha\Delta m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
,

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq\beta\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

Then there are constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
that depend only on
\alpha
and
\beta
which satisfy that for any
m\geq c_{0}
,

\Delta_{m}\geq c_{1}\frac{\log\log m}{m}.

Moreover, not only is the restriction on
\Delta
in Theorem
1.2
necessary, the probability estimate is optimal as well and cannot be improved even for a fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

There are absolute constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that the following holds.
For every
\Delta\geq\frac{c_{1}}{m}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
, we have that with probability at least
2\exp(-c_{1}\Delta m)
,

\left|F_{m}(t)-F(t)\right|\geq c_{2}\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

We end the introduction with a word about
notation.

Throughout,
c,c_{0},c_{1},\dots
denote strictly positive absolute constants that may change their value in each appearance.
We write
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq cB
for an absolute constant and
A\sim B
if
c_{1}A\leq B\leq c_{2}A
for absolute constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.
If a constant
c
depends on a parameter
\alpha
, that is denoted by
c=c(\alpha)
.

We denote by
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
iid Bernoulli random variables (that is, symmetric random variables that satisfy
\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{1}=1)=\frac{1}{2}
) that are independent of
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
.
The expectation only with respect to
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
(resp.
\varepsilon
) is denoted by
\mathbb{E}_{X}
(resp.
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}
), and the same convention applies to
\mathbb{P}_{X}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

To simplify the presentation, we assume in what follows that
X
is absolutely continuous.
The modifications needed in the general case are straightforward.
In addition, we may assume without loss of generality that
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{4}
(because
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sigma^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{4})
) and we consider only
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfy
F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}
; in particular,

\frac{1}{2}F(t)\leq\sigma^{2}(t)\leq F(t).

The case where
F(t)>\frac{1}{2}
follows from the same arguments and is omitted.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
presented here is not the most basic possible, but it is the shortest that we are aware of.
It is based on Talagrand’s concentration inequality for empirical process
[
10
]
.
The version used here can be found, for example, in
[
1
, Theorem 12.5]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists an absolute constant
c
such that the following holds.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a class of functions that are bounded by
1
and set
\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\|h\|_{L_{2}}
.
Then for
\lambda>0
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\lambda)
,

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}h(X)\right|\leq c\left(\mathbb{E}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}h(X)\right|+\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{m}}+\frac{\lambda}{m}\right).

Since
\Delta\leq\frac{1}{4}
, we have that
\log_{2}\frac{1}{\Delta}\geq 2
.
For every
1\leq\ell\leq\log_{2}\frac{1}{\Delta}
, let
t_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
F(t_{\ell})=2^{-\ell}
and set
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}=\{1_{(-\infty,t]}:t\in(t_{\ell+1},t_{\ell}]\}
.
In particular, for every
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
,
2^{-(\ell+1)}\leq\mathbb{E}h^{2}(X)\leq 2^{-\ell}
.

With Theorem
2.1
in mind, let us begin by showing that for every
1\leq\ell\leq\log_{2}\frac{1}{\Delta}
,

(2.1)

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}=\mathbb{E}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}h(X)\right|\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{2^{-\ell}}{m}}.

Set

\mathcal{V}_{\ell}=\left\{(h(X_{i}))_{i=1}^{m}:h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\right\}

and let

\sharp_{\ell}=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}\left|\{i:h(X_{i})=1\}\right|=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i}).

Denote by
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
It is straightforward to verify that for every realization of
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
there is a permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\dots,m\}
for which

(2.2)

\mathcal{V}_{\ell}\subset\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}e_{\pi(i)}:j\leq\sharp_{\ell}\right\}\cup\{0\}.

By the Giné-Zinn symmetrization inequality
[
4
]
(see also
[
1
, Lemma 11.4]
) and (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m}\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\ell}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}v_{i}\right|\leq\frac{2}{m}\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq j\leq\sharp_{\ell}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\varepsilon_{i}\right|,

where we set
\max_{\emptyset}=0
.
Invoking Lévy’s maximal inequality (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Proposition 2.3]
),

\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq j\leq\sharp_{\ell}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{j}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{X}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\sharp_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{\sharp_{\ell}}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{E}_{\ell}\leq\frac{4}{m}\mathbb{E}_{X}\sqrt{\sharp_{\ell}}\leq\frac{4}{m}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\sharp_{\ell}},

and

\mathbb{E}\sharp_{\ell}=\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq t_{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i})=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{m}1_{(-\infty,t_{\ell}]}(X_{i})\leq m2^{-\ell},

proving (
2.1
).

Next, let
1\leq\ell\leq\log_{2}\frac{1}{\Delta}
.
By Talagrand’s concentration inequality for
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
and
\lambda=2\Delta m
, it is evident that with probability at least
1-2\exp(-2\Delta m)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}h(X)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2^{-\ell}}{m}}+\sqrt{2^{-\ell}\Delta}+2\Delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sqrt{2^{-\ell}}\sqrt{\Delta},

where the last inequality holds because
2^{-\ell}\geq\Delta
.
Moreover, if
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
then
\mathbb{E}h^{2}(X)\geq 2^{-(\ell+1)}
, and therefore, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-2\Delta m)
, for every
t\in(t_{\ell+1},t_{\ell}]

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left|F_{m}(t)-F(t)\right|\leq c_{3}\sqrt{2F(t)\Delta}\leq c_{4}\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

If
\Delta m\geq c\log\log m
for a suitable constant
c
, then it follows from the union bound over
1\leq\ell\leq\log_{2}\frac{1}{\Delta}
that with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\Delta m)
, (
2.3
) holds for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
F(t)\geq\Delta
.
In particular, (
2.3
) holds when
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

We end this section by establishing two simple observations:
That the restriction
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
in Theorem
1.2
may be omitted by a simple monotonicity argument; and that a high probability error estimate (similar to the one in Theorem
1.2
) trivially implies a corresponding estimate on the expected error, via tail-integration.

Corollary 2.2
.

There are absolute constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
such that the following holds.
For every
\Delta\geq c_{0}\frac{\log\log m}{m}
,
with probability at least
1-2\exp(-c_{1}\Delta m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|F_{m}(t)-F(t)\right|\leq\Delta+\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

Proof.

As before, we will only consider the case
F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
By Theorem
1.2
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-c\Delta m)
, if
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
then

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

Fix a realization of
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
in that high probability event, and let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy that
\sigma^{2}(t_{0})=\Delta
.
If
\sigma^{2}(t)<\Delta
(and
F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}
) then
t\leq t_{0}
, and by the monotonicity of
F_{m}
and
F
,

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq F_{m}(t_{0})+F(t_{0})\leq 2F(t_{0})+\sigma(t_{0})\sqrt{\Delta}\leq 5\Delta.\qed

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\Delta_{m}\geq\frac{1}{m}
and assume that there are numbers
\alpha,\beta>0
such that the following holds.
For every
\Delta\geq\Delta_{m}
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\alpha\Delta m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
for which
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
we have that
|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq\beta\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}
.
Then

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta_{m}}\left|\frac{F_{m}(t)-F(t)}{\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}}\right|\leq c_{1}

where
c_{1}
is a constant that depends only on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta\geq\Delta_{m}
.
Just as in the proof of Corollary
2.2
, it follows from the monotonicity of
F
and
F_{m}
that with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\alpha\Delta m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq(4+\beta)\Delta+\beta\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta}.

Moreover, set

Z=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|}{(4+\beta)\Delta_{m}+\beta\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}}

so that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta_{m}}\frac{|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|}{\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}}\leq c(\beta)Z.

For every
\lambda\geq 1
consider
\Delta=\lambda\Delta_{m}
and observe that
\mathbb{P}(Z>\lambda)\leq 2\exp(-\alpha\lambda\Delta_{m}m)
.
Indeed, with probability at least

1-2\exp(-\alpha\lambda\Delta_{m}m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|

\displaystyle\leq(4+\beta)\lambda\Delta_{m}+\beta\sigma(t)\sqrt{\lambda\Delta_{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left((4+\beta)\Delta_{m}+\beta\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}\right),

implying that
Z\leq\lambda
.
Therefore, by tail-integration,

\mathbb{E}Z\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(Z>\lambda\right)\,d\lambda\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}2\exp(-\alpha\lambda\Delta_{m}m)\,d\lambda=1+\frac{2}{\alpha}.\qed

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

The key to the proof of Theorem
1.4
is the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that there is a number
\gamma>0
and a positive sequence
(\Delta_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
such that

(3.1)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta_{m}}\left|\frac{F_{m}(t)-F(t)}{\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}}\right|\leq\gamma.

Then there are constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
that depend only on
\gamma
and for any
m\geq c_{0}
,
\Delta_{m}\geq c_{1}\frac{\log\log m}{m}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
(given Proposition
3.1
).

By our assumption and Corollary
2.3
(applied with
\Delta_{m}^{\prime}=\max\{\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{m}\}
),

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta_{m}^{\prime}}\left|\frac{F_{m}(t)-F(t)}{\sigma(t)\sqrt{\Delta_{m}^{\prime}}}\right|\leq c_{1}(\alpha,\beta).

Proposition
3.1
implies that
\Delta_{m}^{\prime}\geq c_{2}\frac{\log\log m}{m}
for any
m\geq c_{3}
, where the constants
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
depend only on
c_{1}
.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We may and do assume without loss of generality that

C_{0}\leq\Delta_{m}m\leq C_{1}\log\log m

for well chosen absolute constants
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
.

Set

Z_{m}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,F(t)\in[2\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{2}]}\left|\frac{F_{m}(t)-F(t)}{\sqrt{F(t)}\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}}\right|

so that by our assumption,
\sup_{m}\mathbb{E}Z_{m}\leq\gamma
.
We will show that cannot be the case if
\Delta_{m}\lesssim\frac{\log\log m}{m}
thanks to a lower estimate on each
\mathbb{E}Z_{m}
.

Our starting point is the following uniform ‘isomorphic’ relation between
F_{m}
and
F
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists an absolute constant
c
such that the following holds.
For every
\Delta\geq\frac{1}{m}
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-c\Delta m)
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
\sigma^{2}(t)\geq\Delta
we have

\left|F_{m}(t)-F(t)\right|\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}(t)}{4}.

The proof of Lemma
3.2
follows the same path as that of Theorem
1.2
.
It is outlined here for the sake of completeness.

Sketch of proof of Lemma
3.2
.

For
1\leq\ell\leq\log_{2}\frac{1}{\Delta}
, let
F(t_{\ell})=2^{-\ell}
and set
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}=\{1_{(-\infty,t]}:t\in(t_{\ell+1},t_{\ell}]\}
.
By Talagrand’s concentration inequality and (
2.1
), for every
\lambda\geq 0
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\lambda)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}h(X)\right|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2^{-\ell}}{m}}+\sqrt{2^{-\ell}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{m}}+\frac{\lambda}{m}\right).

The proof is completed by setting
\lambda=c_{2}2^{-\ell}m\sim c_{2}F(t_{\ell})m
for a suitable absolute constant
c_{2}
, followed by the union bound over
\ell
.
∎

Invoking Lemma
3.2
, it is evident that if
C_{0}
is a sufficiently large constant and
\Delta_{m}m\geq C_{0}
, then there is an event
\Omega
of probability at least
1/2
on which

(3.2)

\frac{3}{4}F(t)\leq F_{m}(t)\leq\frac{5}{4}F(t)

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
that satisfies
F(t)\in[2\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{2}]
.

Let

\mathcal{H}_{m}=\left\{\frac{1_{(-\infty,t]}}{\sqrt{F(t)}\sqrt{\Delta_{m}}}:t\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }F(t)\in\left[2\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{2}\right]\right\}

and therefore,

Z_{m}=\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}h(X)\right|.

By the Giné-Zinn symmetrization inequality,

\mathbb{E}Z_{m}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}h(X_{i})\right|-\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}\frac{|\mathbb{E}h(X)|}{\sqrt{m}}.

Note that

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}\frac{|\mathbb{E}h(X)|}{\sqrt{m}}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,F(t)\in[2\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{2}]}\frac{F(t)}{\sqrt{F(t)\Delta_{m}m}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta_{m}m}}

and

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}h(X_{i})\right|=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,F(t)\in[2\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{2}]}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\Delta_{m}}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\frac{1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i})}{\sqrt{mF(t)}}\right|=\mathcal{U}_{m}.

Fix
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\in\Omega
and therefore (
3.2
) is satisfied.
Set
4m\Delta_{m}\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}
and let
r_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy that
F_{m}(r_{\ell})=\frac{\ell}{m}
.
It follows from (
3.2
) that
F(r_{\ell})\in[2\Delta_{m},\frac{1}{2}]
and

\beta_{\ell}=\frac{F_{m}(r_{\ell})}{F(r_{\ell})}\in\left[\frac{1}{2},2\right].

If
\pi
is the permutation of
\{1,\dots,m\}
satisfying that
X_{\pi(1)}\leq X_{\pi(2)}
and so on, then for every
4m\Delta_{m}\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}
,

\left(\frac{1_{(-\infty,r_{\ell}]}(X_{\pi(i)})}{\sqrt{mF(r_{\ell})}}\right)_{i=1}^{m}=\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{\ell}}{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}e_{i}.

Set

V=\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{\ell}}{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}e_{i}:4m\Delta_{m}\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}\right\},

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{U}_{m}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{X}1_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\Delta_{m}}}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\max_{v\in V}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}v_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m\Delta_{m}}}\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\max_{4m\Delta_{m}\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{\ell}}{\ell}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|.

Since
\beta_{\ell}\geq\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\max_{4m\Delta_{m}\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta_{\ell}}{\ell}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{E}\max_{4m\Delta_{m}\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq\ell\leq\frac{m}{4}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq\ell\leq 4m\Delta_{m}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|

\displaystyle=(1)-(2).

By Höffding’s inequality (see, e.g.
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
) and the union bound, it is evident that

(2)\leq c_{1}\sqrt{\log(m\Delta_{m})}.

Moreover, we prove in Theorem
3.3
that if
m\geq e^{e}
, then

(1)\geq c_{2}\sqrt{\log\log m}.

Since
m\Delta_{m}\leq C_{1}\log\log m
, if
m
is sufficiently large then

(1)-(2)\geq\frac{c_{2}}{2}\sqrt{\log\log m}.

Combining these observations shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z_{m}\geq\mathbb{E}\,\mathcal{U}_{m}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta_{m}m}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1)-(2)}{4\sqrt{m\Delta_{m}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta_{m}m}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{2}}{8}\sqrt{\frac{\log\log m}{m\Delta_{m}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta_{m}m}}.

Finally, as
\Delta_{m}m\geq 1
and
\mathbb{E}Z_{m}\leq\gamma
, it follows that
\Delta_{m}m\geq c_{3}(\gamma)\log\log m
, as claimed.
∎

Next, let us turn to the proof of the wanted estimate on
(1)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

There is an absolute constant
c
such that for every
r\geq e^{e}
,

\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq\ell\leq r}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\geq c\sqrt{\log\log r}.

Remark 3.4
.

The
law of the iterated logarithm
implies that, with probability 1,

\limsup_{r\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2r\log\log r}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\varepsilon_{i}\right|=1.

Hence, for any
r\geq r_{0}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\max_{1\leq\ell\leq r}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ell\log\log\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}.

However, (
3.3
) is a weaker statement than the one in Theorem
3.6
(where we have
\log\log r
instead of
\log\log\ell
), and it does not suffices for the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The proof of Theorem
3.3
is based on a lower bound on the tail of the sum of iid Bernoulli random variables.

Lemma 3.5
.

There are absolute constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
such that the following holds.
Let
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
be independent, symmetric
\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables and set
g
to be the standard gaussian random variable.
Then for
0\leq\lambda\leq c_{0}n^{1/6}
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\geq\lambda\right)\geq\mathbb{P}(g\geq\lambda)\cdot\exp\left(c_{1}\frac{\lambda^{3}}{n^{1/2}}\right)\left(1+c_{2}\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right).

The proof of Lemma
3.5
can be found, for example, in
[
9
, Theorem 5.23]
.

By a tail estimate on the standard gaussian random variable, it is evident that for
\lambda>0

\mathbb{P}(g\geq\lambda)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\lambda}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{c}{\lambda^{2}}\right).

Therefore, there are absolute constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
such that for any
C_{1}\leq\lambda\leq C_{2}n^{1/6}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}\geq\lambda\right)\geq\frac{C_{3}}{\lambda}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right).

Lemma 3.6
.

There are integers
\xi,\eta,s_{0}\geq 2
such that the following holds.
Set
m_{s}=\xi^{s}
.
Then for any
s\geq s_{0}
, with probability at least
0.9
,

\max_{s\leq\ell\leq\eta s}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\ell}\log\log m_{\ell}}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Proof.

Let

D_{\ell}=\sum_{i=m_{\ell-1}+1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}

and thus

S_{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\ell-1}}\varepsilon_{i}+\sum_{i=m_{\ell-1}+1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}=S_{\ell-1}+D_{\ell}.

Consider the events

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{D_{\ell}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m_{\ell}\log\log m_{\ell}}\right\}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\left\{|S_{\ell-1}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{m_{\ell}\log\log m_{\ell}}\right\},

and note that on the intersection of

\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{s\leq\ell\leq\eta s}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}=\bigcap_{s\leq\ell\leq\eta s}\mathcal{B}_{\ell},

there exists
s\leq\ell\leq\eta s
for which
\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\ell}\log\log m_{\ell}}}S_{\ell}\geq\frac{1}{4}
. Thus,

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{s\leq\ell\leq\eta s}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\ell}\log\log m_{\ell}}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}),

and it suffices to establish that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})\geq 0.9
.

To show that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\geq 0.99
, observe that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{\ell})\geq\kappa/\ell
for a constant
\kappa(\xi)\sim\frac{1}{\log\xi}
.
Indeed, set

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\ell}}{m_{\ell}-m_{\ell-1}}\log\log m_{\ell}}

and therefore

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{\ell})=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\ell}-m_{\ell-1}}}\sum_{i=m_{\ell-1}+1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}\geq\lambda\right).

Since
\xi\geq 2
,

\frac{m_{\ell}}{m_{\ell}-m_{\ell-1}}=\frac{\xi}{\xi-1}\in[1,2],

and in particular
C_{1}\leq\lambda^{2}\leq\log\log m_{\ell}
.
Moreover, for
\ell\geq s\geq s_{0}(\xi)
we have that
\lambda\leq C_{2}(m_{\ell}-m_{\ell-1})^{1/6}
.
Thus, by (
3.4
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{3}}{\lambda}\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{3}}{\sqrt{\log\log m_{\ell}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\log\log m_{\ell}\right)\geq\frac{\kappa(\xi)}{\ell}

for
\kappa(\xi)\sim\frac{1}{\log\xi}
, as claimed.

Now, by the independence of the sets
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=1-\prod_{\ell=s}^{\eta s}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{\ell}^{c})\geq 1-\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=s}^{\eta s}\log\left(1-\frac{\kappa}{\ell}\right)\right),

and if
s\geq 5\kappa
then
\log(1-\frac{\kappa}{\ell})\geq-2\frac{\kappa}{\ell}
for every
\ell\geq s
.
Hence,
\sum_{\ell=s}^{\eta s}\log(1-\frac{\kappa}{\ell})\geq-c_{1}\kappa\log\eta
and

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\geq 1-\exp(-c_{1}\kappa\log\eta)\geq 0.99,

where the last inequality holds if
\log\eta\geq c_{2}(\kappa)
.

To prove that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})\geq 0.99
one may use Höffding’s inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=s}^{\eta s}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}_{\ell}^{c})=\sum_{\ell=s}^{\eta s}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\ell-1}}}S_{\ell-1}\right|>\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\xi\log\log m_{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{\ell=s}^{\eta s}\exp\left(-c_{3}\xi\log\log\xi^{\ell}\right)\leq 0.01

provided that
\xi
is sufficiently large (and note that the choice of
\xi
is independent of the choices of
\eta
and
s
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Since
\mathbb{E}|\varepsilon_{1}|=1
, it suffices to prove the claim only for
r\geq c_{0}
.
Using the notation of Lemma
3.6
, let
c_{0}
be sufficiently large to ensure that the largest integer
s
for which
m_{\eta s}\leq r
satisfies
s\geq s_{0}
; in particular,
r\leq m_{\eta(s+1)}
.
It follows that

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq r}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\right|\geq\max_{s\leq\ell\leq\eta s}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m_{\ell}}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{\ell}}\varepsilon_{i}\right|=(\ast),

and by Lemma
3.6
, with probability at least
0.9
,
(\ast)\geq\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\log\log m_{s}}
.

The final observation is straightforward:
If
c_{0}
(and hence

\displaystyle\log\log r\leq\log\log m_{\eta(s+1)}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\eta(s+1)\log\xi\right)

∎

4.
Proof of Proposition
1.5

The proof of Proposition
1.5
can be established using well-known lower bounds on the binomial distribution. The proof presented here follows a slightly different path and is based on a result due to Montgomery-Smith
[
8
]
.
To formulate that result, denote by
\lfloor\lambda\rfloor
the largest integer smaller than
\lambda
, and for
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
let
w^{\ast}
be the monotone non-increasing rearrangement of
(|w_{i}|)_{i=1}^{m}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

There are absolute constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
such that for every
0<\lambda<m-1
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, with probability at least
c_{1}\exp(-c_{2}\lambda)
,

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}w_{i}\right|\geq c_{3}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\lambda\rfloor}w_{i}^{\ast}+\sqrt{\lambda}\left(\sum_{i=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor+1}^{m}(w_{i}^{\ast})^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right).

Again, we only consider the case
F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and in particular
\frac{1}{2}F(t)\leq\sigma^{2}(t)\leq F(t)
.

Let
(X_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{m}
be independent copies of
X
that are also independent of
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
and for
1\leq i\leq m
set

W_{i}=1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i})-1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i}^{\prime}).

Note that if
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are independent random variables with the same distribution and
\lambda\geq 0
, then
\mathbb{P}(|Z-Z^{\prime}|\geq 2\lambda)\leq 2\mathbb{P}(|Z|\geq\lambda)
; therefore,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i})-\mathbb{E}1_{(-\infty,t]}(X)\right|\geq\lambda\right)&\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}W_{i}\right|\geq 2\lambda\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}W_{i}\right|\geq 2\lambda\right),\end{split}

where the equality holds by the symmetry and independence of the
W_{i}
s.

Clearly,

\mathbb{P}(|W_{i}|=1)=2F(t)(1-F(t))\geq F(t)\geq\Delta,

and if
\Delta m\geq 8
, then by Markov’s inequality, with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
,

|\{i:|W_{i}|=1\}|\geq\frac{1}{2}F(t)m.

Denote that event by
\Omega
, fix a realization
(W_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\in\Omega
and apply Lemma
4.1
for
w=(W_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{4}\Delta m
. Hence,

\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}W_{i}\right|\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\sqrt{F(t)\Delta}\right)\geq c_{2}\exp\left(-\frac{c_{3}}{4}\Delta m\right),

and by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}W_{i}\right|\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\sqrt{F(t)\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{Z}1_{\Omega}\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\left(\left|\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}W_{i}\right|\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\sqrt{F(t)\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{2}}{2}\exp\left(-\frac{c_{3}}{4}\Delta m\right).\hfill

Recalling that
F(t)\sim\sigma^{2}(t)
, the claim follows from (
4.1
).
∎

5.
The ‘no cancellation’ range

Next, let us turn to the regime in which
\sigma^{2}(t)
is small relative to
\Delta
.
In that range there are no significant cancellations between
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}1_{(-\infty,t]}(X_{i})
and
F(t)=\mathbb{E}1_{(-\infty,t]}(X)
, and as a result,

\displaystyle|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\sim\begin{cases}\max\{F_{m}(t),F(t)\}&\text{if }F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\max\{1-F_{m}(t),1-F(t)\}&\text{if }F(t)>\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Specifically, we have the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

There are absolute constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
such that the following holds.
For every
\Delta\geq c_{0}\frac{\log\log m}{m}
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-c_{1}\Delta m)
,

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\leq c_{2}\begin{cases}\dfrac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/\sigma^{2}(t))}&\text{if }\sigma^{2}(t)\in\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{10}\right],\\
\\
\sigma^{2}(t)&\text{if }\sigma^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m).\end{cases}

Remark 5.2
.

As we show in what follows, in the range
\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{1}{10}\Delta\right]
the dominant term is
F_{m}(t)
, while in
(0,\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m)]
the dominant term is
F(t)
.

Theorem
5.1
is complemented by a lower bound that holds for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
for which
\sigma^{2}(t)\leq\frac{\Delta}{10}
.
In particular, the uniform upper estimate in Theorem
5.1
cannot be improved in a rather strong sense.

Proposition 5.3
.

There are absolute constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
such that the following holds.
Let
\Delta\geq\frac{c_{0}}{m}
and consider
t\in\mathbb{R}
for which
\sigma^{2}(t)\leq\frac{\Delta}{10}
.
Then, with probability at least
2\exp(-c_{1}\Delta m)
,

\left|F_{m}(t)-F(t)\right|\geq c_{2}\begin{cases}\dfrac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/\sigma^{2}(t))}&\text{if }\sigma^{2}(t)\in\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{10}\right],\\
\\
\sigma^{2}(t)&\text{if }\sigma^{2}(t)\leq\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m).\end{cases}

Remark 5.4
.

Just as in the proofs of Theorem
5.1
and Proposition
5.3
, we restrict the presentation to absolutely continuous random variables and only consider the case
F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}
—implying that
\frac{1}{2}F(t)\leq\sigma^{2}(t)\leq F(t)
.
In particular,

\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2},\,\sigma^{2}(t)\in[a,b]\right\}\subset\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:F(t)\leq\frac{1}{2},\,F(t)\in[a,2a]\right\}.

As will become clear in what follows, it is convenient to split the range
(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{10}]
to

\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{10}\exp(-\Delta m)\right]\cup\left(\frac{\Delta}{10}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{10}\right],

noting that if
\Delta m\geq 10
then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/\sigma^{2}(t))}\sim\frac{1}{m}\quad\text{for }\sigma^{2}(t)\in\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{10}\exp(-\Delta m)\right].

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
5.1

\bullet
The range

F(t)\in\left(\frac{\Delta}{10}\exp(-\Delta m)),\frac{\Delta}{5}\right]
.

Since
\frac{\Delta}{F(t)}\geq 5
and
\log(\lambda)\leq\lambda
for
\lambda\geq 1
, we have that

F(t)\leq\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/F(t))}.

Hence, it suffices to show that
F_{m}(t)\leq c\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/F(t))}
.

For every
\ell\geq 1
, let
x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
F(x_{\ell})=2^{-\ell}\Delta
.
By Bennett’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 2.9]
), for every
\ell
, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-3\Delta m)
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left|F_{m}(x_{\ell})-F(x_{\ell})\right|\leq c_{1}\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/F(x_{\ell}))}.

Moreover, if
\Delta m\geq 10
then by the restriction on
F(t)
,

\left|\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{N}:F(x_{\ell})=\Delta 2^{-\ell}\geq\frac{\Delta}{10}\exp(-\Delta m))\right\}\right|\leq 2\Delta m.

Invoking the union bound over those integers
\ell
, it follows that with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\Delta m)
(
5.2
) holds uniformly.

Now fix a realization
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
in that high probability event, set
t\in\mathbb{R}
in the range in question and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
for which
t\in(x_{\ell+1},x_{\ell}]
.
By the monotonicity of
F_{m}
and (
5.2
),

F_{m}(t)\leq F_{m}(x_{\ell})\leq(c_{1}+1)\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/F(x_{\ell}))}\leq c_{2}\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/F(t))},

where the last inequality holds because
F(x_{\ell})\leq 2F(t)
.

\bullet
The range

F(t)\in\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{5}\exp(-\Delta m)\right]
.

Let
t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

p=F(t_{1})=\frac{\Delta}{5}\exp(-\Delta m).

If
\lambda\geq 3
, then
\lambda\exp(-\lambda)\leq\exp(-\frac{\lambda}{2})
; hence if
\Delta m\geq 3
, then

(5.3)

\displaystyle pm\leq\frac{1}{5}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\Delta m\right)\leq\frac{1}{5}.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{1}
the event in which
|\{i:X_{i}\leq t_{1}\}|\leq 1
.

By (
5.1
), it suffices to show that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{1})\geq 1-\exp(-c\Delta m)
.
Indeed, on that event,

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,F(t)\leq\frac{\Delta}{5}\exp(-\Delta m)}F_{m}(t)\leq F_{m}(t_{1})\leq\frac{1}{m},

and obviously
F(t)\leq F(t_{1})\leq\frac{1}{5m}
.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{c})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}p^{\ell}(1-p)^{m-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\exp\left(\ell\log\frac{em}{\ell}+\ell\log p\right)

(5.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\exp\left(-\ell\log\frac{\ell}{emp}\right),

and since

, we have that

\ell\log\frac{\ell}{emp}\geq\frac{1}{2}\ell\Delta m

for every
\ell\geq 1
.
Comparing (
5.4
) to a geometric progression shows that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{c})\leq\exp(-c\Delta m)
.

\bullet
The range

F(t)\leq\frac{1}{5m}\exp(-\Delta m)
.

Let
t_{2}
satisfy that
p=F(t_{2})=\frac{1}{5m}\exp(-\Delta m)
.
In particular,
t\leq t_{2}
for every
t
in the range in question.
Denote the event in which
|\{i:X_{i}\leq t_{2}\}|=0
by
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

Observe that

\log(1-p)\geq-2p
(since
p\leq\frac{1}{5}
) and that
\exp(-\lambda)\geq 1-\lambda
for
\lambda\geq 0
. Hence,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)=(1-p)^{m}&=\exp(m\log(1-p))\\
&\geq\exp\left(-2mp\right)\\
&\geq 1-2mp\geq 1-\exp(-\Delta m).\end{split}

For every realization
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
t\leq t_{2}
, we have
F_{m}(t)\leq F_{m}(t_{2})=0
.
Therefore,

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|=F(t)\sim\sigma^{2}(t),

which concludes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

\bullet
The range

F(t)\in\left(\frac{\Delta}{10}\exp(-\Delta m)),\frac{\Delta}{5}\right]
.

Fix
t
in that range and set
p=F(t)
.
Since
\frac{\Delta}{p}\geq 1
and
\log\lambda\leq\lambda
for
\lambda\geq 1
, we have that

p\leq\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/p)}.

Moreover, set

k=4\frac{\Delta m}{\log(\Delta/p)}

and note that by the restriction on
p
,
k\geq\max\{1,4mp\}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{1}
be the event in which

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\binom{m}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{m-k}

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(k\log\frac{m}{k}+k\log p+(m-k)\log(1-p)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-k\log\frac{k}{mp}-2mp\right),

where we used that
\log(1-p)\geq-2p
and that
\frac{k}{mp}\geq 1
.

By the definition of
k

k\log\frac{k}{mp}=4\frac{\Delta m}{\log(\Delta/p)}\log\frac{4\Delta}{p\log(\Delta/p)}\leq 8\Delta m,

2pm\leq\Delta m

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}_{1})\geq\exp(-9\Delta m)
.

Finally, for a realization
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
,

\displaystyle F_{m}(t)-F(t)\geq\frac{k}{m}-p

\displaystyle=4\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/p)}-p

\displaystyle\geq 3\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/p)}\sim\frac{\Delta}{\log(\Delta/\sigma^{2}(t))},

as claimed.

\bullet
The range

F(t)\in\left(\frac{1}{10m}\exp(-\Delta m),\frac{\Delta}{5}\exp(-\Delta m)\right]
.

Fix
t
in that range and set
p=F(t)
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{2}
be the event in which
|\{i:X_{i}\leq t\}|\geq 1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}_{2})=mp\geq\frac{1}{10}\exp(-\Delta m).

On the other hand, it follows from the restriction on
p
that if

then
F(t)=p\leq\frac{1}{5m}
(see (

)).
Therefore, in
\mathcal{B}_{2}
,

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|\geq\frac{1}{m}-F(t)\geq\frac{4}{5m}.

By (
5.1
), the wanted estimate follows.

\bullet
The range

F(t)\leq\frac{1}{5m}\exp(-\Delta m)
.

Fix
t
in that range.
It was shown in (
5.5
) that with probability at least
1-\exp(-\Delta m)
,
|\{i:X_{i}\leq t\}|=0
.
In that event,
F_{m}(t)=0
and

|F_{m}(t)-F(t)|=F(t)\geq\sigma^{2}(t)

as claimed.
This concludes the proof of Proposition
5.3
.

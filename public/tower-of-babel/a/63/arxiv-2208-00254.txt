Bertini theorems admitting base changes
Hiromu Tanaka

Abstract
. Given a base point free linear system on an algebraic variety, many classes of singularities are stable under taking suitable members after enlarging the base field. We establish analogous results when the base ring is an excellent ring.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14G17, 14D06.

1.
Introduction

Given a smooth projective variety
X
over an infinite field,
the classical Bertini theorem asserts that general hyperplane sections of
X
are smooth
[
14
, Ch. II, Theorem 8.18]
.
In other words, if
L
is a very ample divisor on
X
, then general members of its complete linear system are smooth.
In characteristic zero, the same conclusion holds for arbitrary base point free linear systems
[
14
, Ch. III, Corollary 10.9]
,
whilst such a generalisation is known to fail in positive characteristic (cf. Example
4.23
and Example
4.24
).
This pathological phenomenon is one of typical obstructions to
extend results in characteristic zero to ones in positive characteristic.

The purpose of this paper is to establish results of Bertini type for base point free linear systems in positive and mixed characteristic.
For a base point free linear system, all the members can have bad singularities.
On the other hand,
we shall prove that the linear system will have good members after taking a suitable base change.
To this end, we first introduce purely transcendental extensions
R(t_{1},...,t_{n})

for arbitrary rings
R
:

R(t_{1},...,t_{n}):=U_{R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]/R}^{-1}(R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]),

where
R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]
denotes the polynomial ring over
R
and

U_{R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]/R}:=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R}(R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]\setminus\mathfrak{p}R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]).

When
R
is a field,
R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
coincides with the purely transcendental extension in the usual sense.
After taking the base change
X\mapsto X\times_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
,
base point free linear systems have good members as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.22
, Theorem
4.7
)
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Let
R
be a noetherian ring and set
R(n):=R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
.
Then there exists a hyperplane
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R(n)}
such that the following holds.

Let
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R}
be an
R
-morphism from a scheme
X
of finite type over
R
.
Set
X_{R(n)}:=X\times_{R}R(n)
.
Let (P) be a property for noetherian schemes which satisfies the following properties (I)–(III)
(e.g. (P) can be regular, reduced, klt, or strongly
F
-regular).

(I)

Let
Y
be a noetherian scheme and let
Y=\bigcup_{i\in I}Y_{i}
be an open cover.
Then
Y
is (P) if and only if
Y_{i}
is (P) for every
i\in I
.

(II)

For a noetherian ring
A
,
if
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,A
is (P), then also
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,A[t]
is (P),
where
A[t]
denotes the polynomial ring over
A
with one variable.

(III)

For a noetherian ring
A
and a multiplicatively closed subset
S
of
A
,
if
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,A
is (P), then also
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,S^{-1}A
is (P).

If
X
is (P), then also
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
is (P), where

X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
is the scheme-theoretic inverse image of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}
,
i.e. the following diagram is cartesian:

\begin{CD}X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}@>{}>{}>X_{R(n)}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\varphi\times_{R}R(n)}V\\
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}@>{}>{}>\mathbb{P}^{n}_{R(n)}.\end{CD}

Remark 1.2
.

We shall establish analogous results for pairs
(X,\Delta)
(Theorem
4.15
, Theorem
4.17
).

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
is folklore for the case when
R
is a field.
Indeed, we can apply a similar argument to the one of
[
7
, Theorem 1]
for this case.

Theorem
1.1
assures the existence of good members
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
.
If
k
is an infinite field, then the classical Bertini theorem states that
general hyperplane sections are good.
We shall establish the following analogous result.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.3
, Remark
5.5
)
.

Let
k
be a field.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
let
k^{\prime}
be a purely transcendental field extension with
n+1\leq{\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}<\infty
.
Then there exists a dense subset
\Lambda\subset(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k^{\prime}})^{*}(k^{\prime})

with respect to the Zariski topology such that the following holds.

Let (P) be a property for noetherian schemes which satisfies the properties (I)–(III)
listed in Theorem
1.1
.
Let
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a
k
-morphism from a
k
-scheme
X
of finite type.
If
X
is (P) and
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then also
X^{\lambda}_{\varphi}
is (P), where

X^{\lambda}_{\varphi}
denotes the scheme-theoretic inverse image of the hyperplane

H^{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k^{\prime}}
corresponding to
\lambda
,
i.e. the following diagram is cartesian:

\begin{CD}X^{\lambda}_{\varphi}@>{}>{}>X\times_{k}k^{\prime}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{\varphi\times_{k}k^{\prime}}V\\
H^{\lambda}@>{}>{}>\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k^{\prime}}.\end{CD}

Theorem

Then it is natural to compare

In this direction, we shall establish the following properties under the assumption that

(iii)

R\to R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
is essentially smooth and faithfully flat (Proposition

).
In particular,
X
is regular (resp. Cohen-Macaulay, resp. reduced) if and only if

.

(iv)

is integral (resp. connected) if and only if so is
X\times_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})

).

(v)

We here give a sketch of the proof only for (v).
By (ii), we may assume that
n=1
. Set
t:=t_{1}
,
The key part is to determine the fibres of
X\times_{R}R(t)\to X
over the closed points of
X
.
More specifically, we show that
\kappa\otimes_{R}R(t)\simeq\kappa(t)

if
\kappa
is a field which is a finitely generated
R
-algebra (Theorem
3.18
).
We now describe the outline of how to prove this.
By the formula
(R/I)\otimes_{R}R(t)\simeq(R/I)(t)
(Proposition
3.5
),
we may assume that
R
is a subring of
\kappa
.
Applying Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz and the Artin–Tate lemma,
the problem is reduced to the case when
\kappa
is the field of fractions of
R
:

\kappa={\operatorname{Frac}}\,R
.
Since
{\operatorname{Frac}}\,R
is a finitely generated
R
-algebra,
it follows from Ratliff’s existence theorem that
R
is a semi-local ring with
\dim R\leq 1
.
Taking the normalisation
R^{N}
of
R
, the problem is finally reduced to the case when

R
is a principal ideal domain (Lemma
3.16
).
In this case, we can directly check the required equation
\kappa\otimes_{R}R(t)\simeq\kappa(t)
(Lemma
3.17
).
For more details, see Subsection
3.2
.

In Section
4
, we prove a main theorem: Theorem
1.1
.
We now explain the main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
,
which is similar to the one of
[
7
, Theorem 1]
.
For simplicity, we assume that
k:=R
is a field and (P) is regular.
We have the following diagram in which each square is cartesian:

\begin{CD}X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}@>{}>{}>X_{k(n)}@>{}>{}>X\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}@>{}>{}>\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k(n)}@>{}>{}>\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}.\\
\end{CD}

In particular, it suffices to find a hyperplane

(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}(\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}_{k(n)})
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k(n)}
such that
the induced morphism
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
is essentially smooth.
For the coordinates
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}={\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[x_{0},...,x_{n}]
and

(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}={\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[s_{0},...,s_{n}]
, we set

(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{{\rm id}}:=\{s_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+s_{n}x_{n}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\times_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}.

Identifying
k(n)=k(t_{1},...,t_{n})
with the function field of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}
,
we define
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}
as the generic fibre, i.e. the following cartesian square

\begin{CD}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}@>{}>{}>(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{{\rm id}}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,k(n)@>{}>{}>(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}.\end{CD}

Then it is almost straightforward to prove that
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}

is essentially smooth. For more details, see Proposition
4.6
.

In Section
5
, we prove Theorem
1.4
.
We now overview the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Assume that
X
is (P).
For the universal family
X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{\varphi}\subset X\times_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}
,
we consider the generic fibre
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
of
X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{\varphi}\to(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}
.
Set

\Lambda:=\{\lambda\in(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k^{\prime}})^{*}(k^{\prime})\,|\,{\rm(I),(II)}\}.

(I)

The image of
\lambda
to
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}
is equal to the generic point of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}
.

(II)

The field extension
K((\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*})\subset\kappa(\lambda)
induced by (I) is a purely transcendental extension.

Then it suffices to prove the following.

(1)

\Lambda
is a dense subset of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k^{\prime}})^{*}(k^{\prime})
.

(2)

If
\lambda\in\Lambda
, then
X^{\lambda}_{\varphi}
is (P).

(1)
Fix a complement of a hyperplane:
\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}={\operatorname{Spec}}\,k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]\subset(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}
.
For a transcendental basis
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of the purely transcendental extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
,
it follows from
{\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}\geq n+1
that
|I|\geq n+1
.
Then there exist distinct elements
i_{0},i_{1},...,i_{n}\in I
.
We define
\Lambda^{\prime}
as the set of the maximal ideals
\lambda
of
k^{\prime}[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
of the form

\lambda=(z_{1}-t_{i_{1}}-t_{i_{0}}^{d_{1}},...,z_{n}-t_{i_{n}}-t_{i_{0}}^{d_{n}})

for
(d_{1},...,d_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})^{n}
.
Then we can check that
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
, which implies that
\Lambda
is dense in
(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k^{\prime}})^{*}(k^{\prime})
.

(2)
Fix
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
By the definition of
\Lambda
,

X^{\lambda}_{\varphi}
is obtained as the base change of
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
by the
purely transcendental field extension
K(\mathbb{P}(V))\subset\kappa(\lambda)
, i.e.
X^{\lambda}_{\varphi}=X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}\times_{K(\mathbb{P}(V))}\kappa(\lambda)
.
Since (P) is stable under taking base changes by purely transcendental field extensions of finite degree,
it is enough to show that
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
is (P).
This is guaranteed by Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.2

We collect some basic results on localisation of rings.
Although the results in this subsection might be well known,

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A
be a ring.
Let
S
and
T
be multiplicatively closed subsets of
A

such that an
A
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:S^{-1}A\to T^{-1}A
exists:

\theta^{\prime}:A\xrightarrow{\theta}S^{-1}A\xrightarrow{\varphi}T^{-1}A,

where
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
denote the induced ring homomorphisms.
If
\theta^{\prime}:A\to T^{-1}A
is injective,
then also
\varphi:S^{-1}A\to T^{-1}A
is injective.

Proof.

Take
a/s\in S^{-1}A
such that
\varphi(a/s)=0
in
T^{-1}A
, where
a\in A
and
s\in S
.
We also have

0=\varphi(a/1)=\varphi(\theta(a))=\theta^{\prime}(a).

Since
\theta^{\prime}
is injective, we get
a=0
in
A
.
Hence,
a/s=0
holds in
S^{-1}A
, as required.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A
be a ring.
Let
S
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
A
and
let
T
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
S^{-1}A
.
Then there exists a multiplicatively closed subset
U
of
A
such that there is an isomorphim

T^{-1}(S^{-1}A)\simeq U^{-1}A
of
A
-algebras.

Proof.

Set

•

T^{\prime}:=\{t^{\prime}\in A\,|\,\frac{t^{\prime}}{s^{\prime}}\in T\text{ for some }s^{\prime}\in S\}
and

•

U:=ST^{\prime}:=\{st^{\prime}\in A\,|\,s\in S,t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}\}
.

Then
T^{\prime}
is a multiplicatively closed subset of
A
.
Hence, also
U
is a multiplicatively closed subset of
A
.

We now prove that the induced ring homomorphism

\theta:A\to T^{-1}(S^{-1}A)

factors through the induced one
A\to U^{-1}A
.
Pick
st^{\prime}\in U
with
s\in S,t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
.
We have
t^{\prime}/s^{\prime}\in T
for some
s^{\prime}\in S
.
Then we obtain

\theta(st^{\prime})=\frac{st^{\prime}/1}{1/1}=\frac{(ss^{\prime})/1}{1/1}\cdot\frac{t^{\prime}/s^{\prime}}{1/1},

which is contained in
(T^{-1}(S^{-1}A))^{\times}
, as required.
Therefore, we get the induced ring homomorphism:

\theta^{\prime}:U^{-1}A\to T^{-1}(S^{-1}A),\qquad\frac{a}{u}\mapsto\theta(u)^{-1}\theta(a).

for
a\in A
and
u\in U
.

Let us show that
\theta^{\prime}
is injective.
Fix
a\in A
and assume that
\theta^{\prime}(a/1)=0
.
Then there exists
t\in T
such that
t\cdot(a/1)=0
holds in
S^{-1}A
.
We can write
t=t^{\prime}/s^{\prime}
for some
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}
.
Therefore, we obtain
s^{\prime\prime}t^{\prime}a=0
in
A
for some
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
and
s^{\prime\prime}\in S
.
By
s^{\prime\prime}t^{\prime}\in U
, we get
a/1=0/1
in
U^{-1}A
.
Thus
\theta^{\prime}
is injective.

Let us show that
\theta^{\prime}
is surjective.
Pick
\zeta\in T^{-1}(S^{-1}A)
.
We can write
\zeta=\frac{a/s}{t}
for some
a\in A,s\in S,t\in T
.
We further have
t=t^{\prime}/s^{\prime}
for some
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime},s^{\prime}\in S
.
It holds that

\zeta=\frac{a/s}{t}=\frac{a/s}{t^{\prime}/s^{\prime}}.

We can check that
\zeta=\theta^{\prime}(as^{\prime}/st^{\prime})
for
as^{\prime}\in A,st^{\prime}\in U
, as required.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
be a ring and let
S
be a multiplicatively closed subset of

.
Set

•

M(S):={\rm Im}(\theta^{*}:{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S^{-1}A\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,A)
, and

•

\overline{S}:=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in M(S)}(A\setminus\mathfrak{p})
,

where
\theta^{*}:{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S^{-1}A\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,A
is defined by
\theta^{*}(\mathfrak{n}):=\theta^{-1}(\mathfrak{n})

for the natural ring homomorphism
\theta:A\to S^{-1}A,a\mapsto a/1
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

The inclusion

holds.
Furthermore, the induce
A
-algebra homomorphism

is an isomorphism.

(2)

Let

and
S_{2}
be multiplicatively closed subsets of

.
If

, then an
A
-algebra isomorphism

S_{1}^{-1}A\simeq S_{2}^{-1}A
holds.

Proof.

Since (1) immediately implies (2), it suffices to show (1).
Let us show the inclusion
S\subset\overline{S}
.
Fix
s\in S
and
\mathfrak{p}\in M(S)
.
Note that
\mathfrak{p}S^{-1}A
is a maximal ideal of

.
It follows from

that

Therefore, we obtain

.
Since

is chosen to be an arbitrary element of

,
we get

.

We then obtain an

-algebra homomorphism

.

Therefore, we obtain

, as required.
∎

.

Fix

.

(1)

(

(2)

We say that a morphism

of schemes is
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-

(a)

is affine and

(b)

there exists an affine open cover

of

such that

of

-localising.

Proposition 2.7
.

Fix

.

(1)

Let

\begin{CD}X^{\prime}@>{f^{\prime}}>{}>Y^{\prime}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\

be a cartesian diagram of schemes.

(a)

If

is an
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising morphism, then also

is an
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising morphism.

(b)

If
f
is a strongly
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising morphism and

is an affine scheme, then also
f^{\prime}
is a strongly
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising morphism.

(2)

Let
\varphi:A\to B
be a ring homomorphism.
Then
\varphi
is an
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising ring homomorphism
if and only if the induced morphism

is strongly
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising.

(3)

Let

be ring homomorphisms.
If
\varphi
is
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising and

is
\mathbb{A}^{m}
-localising, then

\psi\circ\varphi
is
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}
-localising.

(4)

Let

be morphisms of schemes.
If

is strongly
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising and

is
\mathbb{A}^{m}
-localising,
then

is
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}
-localising.

(5)

Let
f_{0}:X_{0}\to Y_{0}
be a morphism of affine schemes such that
the induced ring homomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{0}}(Y_{0})\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}(X_{0})
is
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising.
Let

\begin{CD}X@>{f}>{}>Y\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
X_{0}@>{f_{0}}>{}>Y_{0},\end{CD}

be a cartesian diagram of schemes.
If

is an
\mathbb{A}^{m}
-localising morphism of schemes,
then the composite morphism

is an
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}
-localising morphism.

Proof.

Let us show (1).
The assertion (a) is clear.
We now show (b).
Replacing

by an affine open subset of

, we may assume that

is an affine scheme.
Then all the schemes

and

are affine, and hence the assertion (b) holds,
which completes the proof of (1).
The assertion (2) directly follows from (b) of (1).

Let us show (3).
We have

B=S^{-1}(A[x_{1},...,x_{n}])=S^{-1}(A[\underline{x}])\quad\text{and}\quad C=T^{-1}(B[y_{1},...,y_{m}])=T^{-1}(B[\underline{y}]),

and

of

where each of

and

denotes the induced map.
Then it is easy to see that

Therefore, we obtain

C=T^{-1}((S^{-1}(A[\underline{x}]))[\underline{y}])\simeq T^{-1}(\widetilde{S}^{-1}(A[\underline{x},\underline{y}]))\simeq U^{-1}(A[\underline{x},\underline{y}])

for some multiplicatively closed subset

of

(Lemma

).
Thus (3) holds.

Let us show (4).
Let
Z=\bigcup_{i\in I}Z_{i}
be an affine open cover of

such that
if we set

,
then
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(Z_{i})\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Y_{i})
is
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising for any

.
Set
X_{i}:=f^{-1}(Y_{i})
for any

.
As

is strongly
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising,

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Y_{i})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{i})
is
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising.
Then (3) implies that
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(Z_{i})\to\mathcal{O}_{Y}(Y_{i})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{i})
is
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}
-localising.
Hence (4) holds.

Let us show (5).
By (b) of (1),
f
is strongly
\mathbb{A}^{n}
-localising.
Then (4) implies that

is
\mathbb{A}^{n+m}
-localising.
∎

3.1.

In this subsection,
we first introduce purely transcendental extensions for rings (Definition

):

which generalise purely transcendental extensions for fields.
We then establish some foundational properties as follows:

(1)

(Proposition

).

(2)

is faithfully flat and essentially smooth (Proposition

(3)

The map

(4)

(Proposition

).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
R
be a ring.
Set

(1)

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

be a noetherian ring and let

.
Then the induced ring homomorphism

\theta:R(t_{1},...,t_{n})/IR(t_{1},...,t_{n})\to(R/I)(t_{1},...,t_{n})

Let
\underline{t}:=(t_{1},...,t_{n})

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring.
Then the following hold.

(1)

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime ideal of

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R(t_{1},...,t_{n})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-1}(\mathfrak{n})

In what follows,
\underline{t}
denotes the multi-variable
(t_{1},...,t_{n})

for some maximal ideal

of

such that

for any

Note that both sides are integral normal schemes such that
the induced morphisms to
X\times_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
are finite morphisms.
Hence these are isomorphic over
X\times_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
.
∎

3.2.2.
Fibres

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
R
be a noetherian integral domain such that
K:={\operatorname{Frac}}\,R
is a finitely generated
R
-algebra.
Then
R
is a semi-local ring with
\dim R\leq 1
.

Proof.

We have

K=R\left[\frac{r^{\prime}_{1}}{r_{1}},...\frac{r^{\prime}_{m}}{r_{m}}\right]=R\left[\frac{1}{r_{1}},...\frac{1}{r_{m}}\right]=R\left[\frac{1}{f}\right]

for some
r_{1},...,r_{m},r^{\prime}_{1},...,r^{\prime}_{m}\in R
and
the product
f:=r_{1}\cdots r_{m}
.
In other words, the equation
D_{f}=\{(0)\}
holds in
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R
.
By taking the complements of both hands, we get

{\operatorname{Spec}}R\setminus\{(0)\}=V(f)=V(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\cup\cdots\cup V(\mathfrak{p}_{r}),

where
\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{r}
are the minimal prime ideals of
fR
, and hence of height one.
In particular,
R
has
only finitely many primes ideals of height one.
We then obtain
\dim R\leq 1
by Ratliff’s existence theorem
[
20
, Theorem 31.2]
.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral extension of noetherian
integral domains.
Then the following induced ring homomorphism is an isomorphism:

S\otimes_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})\xrightarrow{\simeq}S(t_{1},...,t_{n}).

Proof.

Let
\underline{t}:=(t_{1},...,t_{n})
be the multi-variable.
Note that
both sides are localisations of
S[\underline{t}]
.
In particular, also
S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})\to S(\underline{t})

is a localisation.

We claim that the following hold.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is a maximal ideal of
S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
, then its pullback
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap S
is a maximal ideal of
S
, i.e. we have the following map

(3.16.1)

{\operatorname{Spm}}\,(S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t}))\to{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S,\qquad\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\mapsto\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap S.

(2)

The map (
3.16.1
) is bijective.

For now, we complete the proof under assuming (1) and (2).
Since
{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S(\underline{t})\to{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S,\mathfrak{l}\mapsto\mathfrak{l}\cap R
is bijective (Theorem
3.7
), we get the bijection by (1) and (2):

{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S(\underline{t})\to{\operatorname{Spm}}\,(S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})),\qquad\mathfrak{l}\mapsto\mathfrak{l}\cap S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t}).

Since
S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})\to S(\underline{t})

is a localisation, this is faithfully flat, and hence an isomorphism.

Therefore, it is enough to show (1) and (2).
Let us show (1).
Fix
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,(S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t}))
and take its pullbacks:

(3.16.2)

\begin{CD}S@>{}>{}>S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})\\
@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
R@>{}>{}>R(\underline{t})\end{CD}\qquad\qquad\begin{CD}\mathfrak{n}@<{}<{}<\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\mathfrak{m}@<{}<{}<\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}.\end{CD}

All the arrows in the diagram (
3.16.2
) are injective.
Indeed, since
R\to R(\underline{t})
is faithfully flat, also
S\to S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})

is faithfully flat, and hence injective.
Since
R(\underline{t})
is reduced and

{\operatorname{Spec}}\,(S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t}))\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R(\underline{t})
is surjective,

R(\underline{t})\to S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
is injective.
In what follows, we consider all the rings in the diagram (
3.16.2
)
as subrings of
S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
.
Furthermore, these rings are integral domains since so is
S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
(Theorem
3.13
(1)).
Since
R\subset S
is an integral extension,
so is
R(\underline{t})\subset S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
, and hence
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap R(\underline{t})
is a maximal ideal.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\cap R
is a maximal ideal (Theorem
3.7
).
Since
R\to S
is an integral extension of integral domains, also

\mathfrak{n}:=\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap S
is a maximal ideal.
Thus (1) holds.

Let us show (2).
It suffices to give the inverse map of the map (
3.16.1
).
Pick
\mathfrak{n}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,S
.
Then
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{n}\cap R
and
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{m}R(\underline{t})
are maximal ideals (Theorem
3.7
).
We get a prime ideal
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}\,(S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t}))
such that
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap S=\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cap R(\underline{t})=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
.
Since
R(\underline{t})\to S\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
is an integral extension of integral domains,
\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
is a maximal ideal. Then the map
\mathfrak{n}\mapsto\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}
given here is nothing but the inverse map of (
3.16.1
).
Thus (2) holds.
∎

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
R
be a principal ideal domain.
Set
K:={\operatorname{Frac}}\,R
. Then the following induced ring homomorphism
is an isomorphism:

K\otimes_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})\xrightarrow{\simeq}K(t_{1},...,t_{n})

Proof.

Let
\underline{t}:=(t_{1},...,t_{n})
be the multi-variable.
If
\dim R=0
, i.e.
R=K
, then the assertion is clear.
Hence we may assume that
\dim R=1
.
Since the induced ring homomorphism
K\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})\to K(\underline{t})

is injective (Lemma
3.11
),
we may consider
K\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})
is a subring of
K(\underline{t})
.
Inside the field
K(\underline{t})
, we have that

R(\underline{t})\otimes_{R}K=\left\{\frac{f(\underline{t})}{ag(\underline{t})}\in K(\underline{t})\,\middle|\,a\in R\setminus\{0\},f(\underline{t})\in R[\underline{t}],g(\underline{t})\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R}R[\underline{t}]\setminus\mathfrak{m}R[t]\right\}.

Pick
h(\underline{t})\in R[\underline{t}]\setminus\{0\}
.
For a greatest common divisor
a\in R\setminus\{0\}
of the coefficients of
h(\underline{t})
,
we have that
h(\underline{t})=ag(\underline{t})
for some
g(\underline{t})\in\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R}R[\underline{t}]\setminus\mathfrak{m}R[\underline{t}]
, which implies
R(\underline{t})\otimes_{R}K=K(\underline{t})
.
∎

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
R
be an excellent ring and let
\kappa
be a field which is a finitely generated
R
-algebra.
Then the following induced ring homomorphism is an isomorphism:

\kappa\otimes_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\kappa(t_{1},...,t_{n}).

Proof.

Let
\underline{t}:=(t_{1},...,t_{n})
be the multi-variable.
By Proposition
3.5
,
we have
\kappa\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})\simeq\kappa\otimes_{R^{\prime}}R^{\prime}(\underline{t})

for the image
R^{\prime}:={\rm Im}(R\to\kappa)
.
Hence, after replacing
R
by
R^{\prime}
,
we may assume that
R
is a subring of
\kappa
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
3.16
and the assumption that
R
is excellent,
the problem is reduced to the case when
R
is integrally closed.
For
K:={\operatorname{Frac}}\,R
, we have

R\subset K\subset\kappa.

Since
\kappa
is a finitely generated
R
-algebra,
\kappa
is also a finitely generated
K
-algebra.
Then Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz implies that
K\subset\kappa
is a finite extension.
By the Artin–Tate lemma
[
1
, Proposition 7.8]
, also
K
is a finitely generated
R
-algebra.
Then Lemma
3.15
implies that

R
is a semi-local ring with
\dim R\leq 1
.
Therefore,
R
is
either a field or a Dedekind domain with finitely many prime ideals, and hence
R
is a principal ideal domain.
We then obtain

\displaystyle\kappa\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t})\simeq\kappa\otimes_{K}(K\otimes_{R}R(\underline{t}))\simeq\kappa\otimes_{K}K(\underline{t})\simeq\kappa(\underline{t}),

where the second isomorphism holds by Lemma
3.17

and the last one follows from Lemma
3.16
.
∎

3.2.3.
Dimension

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
A
be a noetherian ring with
d:=\dim A<\infty
.
Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a maximal ideal of
A[x]
with
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n})=d+1
.
Then
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{n}\cap A
is a maximal ideal of
A
with
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{m})=d
.

Proof.

By
d=\dim A
, it suffices to prove that the prime ideal
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}\cap A
satisfies
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{m})=d
.
We have
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{m})\leq\dim A=d
.
Suppose that
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{m})<d
.
It suffices to derive a contradiction.
By
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{n}\cap A
, we have that
\mathfrak{n}\cap S_{\mathfrak{m}}=\emptyset
for
S_{\mathfrak{m}}:=A\setminus\mathfrak{m}
.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{n}
comes from
S^{-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A[x])=A_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]
, i.e.

\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}S^{-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A[x])\cap A[x]
.
We have

{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n})={\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n}S^{-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A[x]))\leq\dim S^{-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(A[x])=\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}[x]=\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}+1={\rm ht}(\mathfrak{m})+1<d+1,

which contradicts
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n})=d+1
.
∎

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
R
be an excellent ring and let
A
be a finitely generated
R
-algebra.
Then
\dim A\otimes_{R}R(t)\leq\dim A
.

Proof.

If
\dim A=\infty
, then there is nothing to show.
Hence we may assume that
\dim A<\infty
.
Fix a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
A\otimes_{R}R(t)
.
It suffices to show
{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n})\leq\dim A
.
Set
\mathfrak{l}:=\mathfrak{n}\cap A[t]
and
\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{n}\cap A
:

A\to A[t]\to A\otimes_{R}R(t),\qquad\mathfrak{m}\leftarrow\mathfrak{l}\leftarrow\mathfrak{n}.

We now treat the case when
\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}<\dim A
.
We have

{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n})={\rm ht}(\mathfrak{l})\leq\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}[t]=\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}+1<\dim A+1,

where the first equality holds by

A[t]_{\mathfrak{l}}\simeq(A\otimes_{R}R(t))_{\mathfrak{n}}
.
This completes the proof for the case when
\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}<\dim A
.

We may assume that
\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}=\dim A
.
In particular,
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
A
.
Since
A_{\mathfrak{m}}\to(A\otimes_{R}R(t))_{\mathfrak{n}}
is faithfually flat,
it holds by
[
20
, Theorem 15.1]
that

{\rm ht}(\mathfrak{n})=\dim(A\otimes_{R}R(t))_{\mathfrak{n}}=\dim A_{\mathfrak{m}}+\dim(A\otimes_{R}R(t)/\mathfrak{m}(A\otimes_{R}R(t)))_{\mathfrak{n}}

\leq\dim A+\dim((A/\mathfrak{m})\otimes_{R}R(t))=\dim A,

where the last equality
follows from Theorem
3.18
.
∎

.

Let
R
be an excellent ring,
X
a scheme, and
f:X\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R
a morphism of finite type.
Then it holds that

\dim X=\dim(X\times_{R}R(t_{1},...,t_{n})).

By Proposition

, we may assume that

.

is faithfully flat (Proposition

),
we obtain

[
20

.
The opposite inequality

3.21
.
∎

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
R
be an excellent one-dimensional principal ideal domain.
Set
K:={\operatorname{Frac}}\,R
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

R(t)
is an excellent one-dimensional principal ideal domain.

(2)

R(t)\otimes_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})\simeq(R/\mathfrak{m})(t)
for any
\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R
.

(3)

R(t)\otimes_{R}K\simeq K(t)
.

(4)

The induced morphism
{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R(t)\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R
is bijective.

In particular,
\mathbb{Z}(t)
is a one-dimensional principal ideal domain,

{\operatorname{Spec}}\,\mathbb{Z}(t)\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,\mathbb{Z}
is bijective,
\mathbb{Z}(t)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\simeq\mathbb{Q}(t)
,
and
\mathbb{Z}(t)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F}_{p}\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
for any prime number
p
.

Proof.

We have the induced ring homomorphisms

Note that
\dim R(t)=\dim\,R=1
(Theorem
3.22
).
Since
R[t]
is a unique factorisation domain,

is a one-dimensional unique factorisation domain,
and hence a principal ideal domain. Thus (1) holds.

3.5
and

3.17
that (2) and (3) hold, respectively.
Then (4) holds by (2) and (3).
∎

As the following example shows,
the localisation functor
A\mapsto S^{-1}A
does not commute with the purely transcendental extension functor
R\mapsto R(t)
.

Proposition 3.24
.

Let
k
be a field.
Set

R:=k[x,y]\quad{\rm and}\quad S:=R\setminus\{0\}.

Then
S^{-1}(R(t))
and
(S^{-1}R)(t)
are not isomorphic as rings.

Proof.

Set
K:=S^{-1}R=k(x,y)
.
We have
(S^{-1}R)(t)=(k(x,y))(t)=k(x,y,t)
.
In particular, it suffices to show that
S^{-1}(R(t))
is not a field.
As a subring of
{\rm Frac}(R[t])=k(x,y,t)
,

S^{-1}(R(t))
can be written as follows:

S^{-1}(R(t))=S^{-1}(U^{-1}_{k[x,y][t]/k[x,y]}k[x,y,t])

=\left\{\frac{f(x,y,t)}{s(x,y)u(x,y,t)}\,\middle|\,f(x,y,t)\in k[x,y,t],s(x,y)\in S,u(x,y,t)\in U_{k[x,y][t]/k[x,y]}\right\}.

We then have
S^{-1}(R(t))=V^{-1}k[x,y,t]
for

V:=\{s(x,y)u(x,y,t)\,|\,s(x,y)\in S,u(x,y,t)\in U_{k[x,y][t]/k[x,y]}\}.

Set
\mathfrak{p}:=(x+ty)k[x,y,t]
, which is a nonzero prime ideal of
k[x,y,t]
.
It suffices to show that
\mathfrak{p}\cap V=\emptyset
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\cap V\neq\emptyset
.
Pick
\zeta\in\mathfrak{p}\cap V
.
We have

\zeta=(x+ty)f(x,y,t)=s(x,y)u(x,y,t)\neq 0

for some
f(x,y,t)\in k[x,y,t],s(x,y)\in S,u(x,y,t)\in U_{k[x,y][t]/k[x,y]}
.
Since
x+ty
is a prime element of
k[x,y,t]
and
s(x,y)\not\in(x+ty)k[x,y,t]
,
there exists
g(x,y,t)\in k[x,y,t]
such that

u(x,y,t)=(x+ty)g(x,y,t)\in\mathfrak{m}k[x,y,t],

where
\mathfrak{m}:=(x,y)k[x,y]
.
This contradicts

u(x,y,t)\in U_{k[x,y][t]/k[x,y]}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{n}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,k[x,y]}k[x,y,t]\setminus\mathfrak{n}k[x,y,t]\subset k[x,y,t]\setminus\mathfrak{m}k[x,y,t].

∎

in Theorem

no longer holds if

Proposition 3.25
.

Let

be a field.
Then
\dim(B\otimes_{k}k(t))>\dim B

.

\displaystyle=

Proposition 3.26
.

Let
k
be a field. Set
R:=k[x]
.
Then
the following hold.

(1)

R(t)
is an essentially finitely generated
k(t)
-algebra.

(2)

R(t)
is not a finitely generated

-algebra.

Since (1) is obvious, let us show (2).
Suppose that
R(t)=(k[x])(t)
is a finitely generated

-algebra.
We have the induced injective

-algebra homomorphism:

\varphi:k(t)[x]\hookrightarrow(k[x])(t),

is a constructible set.
Since

is injective,
\varphi^{*}
is dominant.
Therefore,

Hence

3.4.
A generalisation

In order to treat a gluing problem of our construction (cf. Remark
4.2
),
we give a slight generalisation of our purely transcendental extensions (Definition
3.27
) and establish a auxiliary result (Proposition
3.28
).

Definition 3.27
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
S
be an
R
-algebra
such that
\mathfrak{m}S
is a prime ideal of
S
for any
\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R
.
We set

U_{S/R}:=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{p}\in{\operatorname{Spec}}\,R}(S\setminus\mathfrak{p}S)=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R}(S\setminus\mathfrak{m}S)

and

In particular, if
S=R[t_{1},...,t_{n}]
, then
R(S)=R(t_{1},...,t_{n})
(Definition
3.1
).

Proposition 3.28
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
S
be an
R
-algebra
such that
\mathfrak{m}S
is a prime ideal of
S
for any
\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R
.
Take
f\in U_{S/R}
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

For any
\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R
,
\mathfrak{m}S_{f}
is a prime ideal of
S_{f}
.

(2)

The composition
S\xrightarrow{\theta}S_{f}\xrightarrow{\beta}R(S_{f})
of the induced ring homomorphisms factors through the induced ring homomorphism
\alpha:S\to R(S)
:

\begin{CD}S@>{\theta}>{}>S_{f}\\
@V{}V{\alpha}V@V{}V{\beta}V\\
R(S)@>{\theta^{\prime}}>{}>R(S_{f}).\end{CD}

Furthermore,
\theta^{\prime}:R(S)\to R(S_{f})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The assertion (1) follows from
f\in U_{S/R}\subset S\setminus\mathfrak{m}S
.

Let us show (2). We first show that
there exists a ring homomorphism
\theta^{\prime}:R(S)\to R(S_{f})

such that
\theta^{\prime}\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\theta
.
Take
u\in U_{S/R}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R}(S\setminus\mathfrak{m}S)
.
It suffices to prove
\theta(u)\in U_{S_{f}/R}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R}(S_{f}\setminus\mathfrak{m}S_{f})
.
Fix
\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R
.
Suppose
\theta(u)\in\mathfrak{m}S_{f}
.
Let us derive a contradiction.
We have
f^{r}u\in\mathfrak{m}S
for some
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Since
\mathfrak{m}S
is a prime ideal of
S
, we have
f^{r}\in\mathfrak{m}S
or
u\in\mathfrak{m}S
.
By
f\not\in\mathfrak{m}S
, we obtain
u\in\mathfrak{m}S
, which contradictis
u\in U_{S/R}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in{\operatorname{Spm}}\,R}(S\setminus\mathfrak{m}S)
.
Therefore, we get a ring homomorphism
\theta^{\prime}:R(S)\to R(S_{f})
such that
\theta^{\prime}\circ\alpha=\beta\circ\theta
.

We now prove that
\theta^{\prime}:R(S)\to R(S_{f})
is injective.
Take
s/u\in R(S)
with
\theta^{\prime}(s/u)=0
, where
s\in S
and
u\in U_{S/R}
.
Then we have
\theta^{\prime}(s/1)=0
.
Hence we obtain
v\cdot(s/1)=0
in
S_{f}
for some
v\in U_{S_{f}/R}
.
We can write

.
By

Let us prove that

4.6.1.
Positive characteristic

Example 4.23
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0

and set
X:=X^{\prime}:=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
.
Let
F_{X/k}:X=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\to X^{\prime}=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be the relative Frobenius morphism

F_{X/k}:\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},\qquad[a_{0}:\cdots:a_{n}]\mapsto[a_{0}^{p}:\cdots:a_{n}^{p}],

which is a
k
-morphism.
In what follows, we use the identifications via the following isomorphisms

•

(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}={\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[s_{0},...,s_{n}]
, and

•

{\operatorname{Frac}}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}\simeq{\operatorname{Frac}}({\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[s_{0},...,s_{n}])=k(t_{1},...,t_{n})
with
t_{i}:=s_{i}/s_{0}

We have the following diagram in which each square is cartesian (Remark
4.5
):

\begin{CD}X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{F_{X/k}}@>{}>{}>X^{\prime{\operatorname{gen}}}_{{\rm id}}@>{}>{}>{\operatorname{Spec}}\,k(n)={\operatorname{Spec}}\,k(t_{1},...,t_{n})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{F_{X/k}}@>{}>{}>X^{\prime{\operatorname{univ}}}_{{\rm id}}@>{}>{}>(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
X\times_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}@>{F_{X/k}\times{\rm id}}>{}>X^{\prime}\times_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
X@>{F_{X/k}}>{}>X^{\prime}.\end{CD}

By

X^{\prime{\operatorname{univ}}}_{{\rm id}}=\{s_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+s_{n}x_{n}=0\}\subset{\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[x_{0},...,x_{n}]\times_{k}{\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[s_{0},...,s_{n}]=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\times_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*},

we obtain

X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{F_{X/k}}=\{s_{0}x_{0}^{p}+\cdots+s_{n}x_{n}^{p}=0\}\subset{\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[x_{0},...,x_{n}]\times_{k}{\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[s_{0},...,s_{n}]=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\times_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}

and

X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{F_{X/k}}=\{x_{0}^{p}+t_{1}x_{1}^{p}+\cdots+t_{n}x_{n}^{p}=0\}\subset{\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[x_{0},...,x_{n}]\times_{k}k(t_{1},...,t_{n})=\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}\times_{k}k(t_{1},...,t_{n}).

Note that
X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{F_{X/k}}
is a smooth projective variety over
k

(Theorem
4.7
(1)) and

X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{F_{X/k}}
is a regular projective variety over
k(n)=k(t_{1},...,t_{n})
(Theorem
4.9
(4)).
Although the generic member
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{F_{X/k}}
is regular,
any member of (the linear system corresponding to) the morphism

is not reduced.
Indeed, given
\lambda:{\operatorname{Spec}}\,k\to(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\operatorname{Proj}}\,k[s_{0},...,s_{n}]
and

, the corresponding member

X^{\lambda}_{F_{X/k}}:=X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{F_{X/k}}\times_{(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k})^{*},\lambda}{\operatorname{Spec}}\,k

X^{\lambda}_{F_{X/k}}=\{a_{0}x^{p}_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}x^{p}_{n}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}.

Example 4.24
.

We work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
k
and let

Proposition 4.25
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
R
be a discrete valuation ring
of mixed characteristic
(0,p)
whose residue field is a perfect field.
Set

•

\mathbb{P}^{2}_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}}:={\operatorname{Proj}}\,R[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}](=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{R}),

•

\mathbb{P}^{2}_{y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}}:={\operatorname{Proj}}\,R[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}](=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{R}),
and

•

X:=\{x_{0}y_{0}^{p}+x_{1}y_{1}^{p}+x_{2}y_{2}^{p}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}}\times_{R}\mathbb{P}^{2}_{y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}}
.

Then the following hold.

(1)

X
is regular.

(2)

Let
H
be a hyperplane of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}}
over
R
.
For the induced morphism

\varphi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}}=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{R},

the scheme-theoretic inverse image
\varphi^{-1}(H)
is not regular.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be a uniformiser of
R
and set
\mathfrak{m}:=\pi R
and
\kappa:=R/\mathfrak{m}
.
Let
v_{\pi}:R\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the additive
\pi
-adic valuation with
v_{\pi}(\pi)=1
.

Let us show (1).
By the Jacobian criterion for smoothness,
we can check that
X\times_{R}\kappa
is smooth over
\kappa
.
Since an effective Cartier divisor
X\times_{R}\kappa
on
X
is regular,

X
is regular around
X\times_{R}\kappa
.
The non-regular locus
X_{{\rm non-reg}}
of
X
is a closed subset of
X
because
R
is an excellent ring
[
13
, Scholie 7.8.3(iii)]
.
Therefore, if
X_{{\rm non-reg}}\neq\emptyset
, then

X_{{\rm non-reg}}\cap(X\times_{R}\kappa)\neq\emptyset
, which is a contradiction.
Hence
X
is regular.
Thus (1) holds.

Let us show (2).
We can write
H=\{a_{0}x_{0}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}}
for some
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in R
.
Since
H
is flat over
R
(cf. Subsection
2.1
(
15
)),
we have that
v_{\pi}(a_{0})=0,v_{\pi}(a_{1})=0
, or
v_{\pi}(a_{2})=0
.
Hence we may assume that
a_{0}=-1
, i.e.
H=\{a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}=x_{0}\}
.
Since
\kappa
is a perfect field, we can find
a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2}\in R
such that

v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})\geq 1
and
v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2})\geq 1
.
We may assume that
v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2})\geq v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})\geq 1
.

We then obtain

\varphi^{-1}(H)=\{(a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2})y_{0}^{p}+x_{1}y_{1}^{p}+x_{2}y_{2}^{p}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}_{x_{1},x_{2}}\times_{R}\mathbb{P}^{2}_{y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}}.

Consider the following open subset of
\varphi^{-1}(H)

\varphi^{-1}(H)\cap D_{+}(x_{2})\cap D_{+}(y_{0})=\{f:=x_{1}y_{1}^{p}+y_{2}^{p}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{3}_{x_{1},y_{1},y_{2}},

where the resulting polynomial
x_{1}y_{1}^{p}+y_{2}^{p}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}
is obtained by the substitution

x_{2}=y_{0}=1
.
For
y^{\prime}_{1}:=y_{1}+a^{\prime}_{1}
and
y^{\prime}_{2}:=y_{2}+a^{\prime}_{2}
, the following holds:

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}y_{1}^{p}+y_{2}^{p}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(y^{\prime}_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+(y^{\prime}_{2}-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(y^{\prime}_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}-x_{1}(-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+(y^{\prime}_{2}-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}-(-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})x_{1}+((-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})x_{1}+((-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2})\mod I^{2},

where
I:=(\pi,y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2})
.
We treat the following two cases separately:

(i)

v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})\geq 2
.

(ii)

v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})=1
.

(i) Assume
v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})\geq 2
.
We then obtain
v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2})\geq v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})\geq 2
.
In particular, we get
(-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1}\in I^{2}
and
(-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2}\in I^{2}
.
Hence it holds that
f\in\mathfrak{m}^{2}
for the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}:=(\pi,x_{1},y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2})
of
R[x_{1},y_{1},y_{2}]
.
Then
\varphi^{-1}(H)
is not regular at
\mathfrak{m}
in this case.

(ii)
Assume
v_{\pi}((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})=1
.
We can write
(-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1}=\pi a^{\prime\prime}_{1}
and
(-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2}=\pi a^{\prime\prime}_{2}

for some
a^{\prime\prime}_{1}\in R^{\times}
and
a^{\prime\prime}_{2}\in R
.
We then have

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle((-a^{\prime}_{1})^{p}+a_{1})x_{1}+((-a^{\prime}_{2})^{p}+a_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(a^{\prime\prime}_{1}x_{1}+a^{\prime\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 0\mod\mathfrak{n}^{2}

for the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}:=(\pi,a^{\prime\prime}_{1}x_{1}+a^{\prime\prime}_{2},y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2})
of
R[x_{1},y_{1},y_{2}]
.
Hence,
\varphi^{-1}(H)
is not regular at
\mathfrak{n}
.
Thus (2) holds.
∎

5.

5.1.
Density of good members

If a linear system
V\subset H^{0}(X,L)
, which is a finite-dimensional
k
-vector subspace, is base point free,
then we have observed in Section
4
that its generic member has good singularities.
The purpose of this subsection is to prove that many members of

V\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}\subset H^{0}(X_{k^{\prime}},L_{k^{\prime}})
has a similar property
for a purely transcendental field extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
with
{\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}\gg 0
,
where
X_{k^{\prime}}:=X\times_{k}k^{\prime}
and
L_{k^{\prime}}
denotes the pullback of
L
to
X_{k^{\prime}}

(Theorem
5.3
, Theorem
5.4
).
The key result is the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Set
Z:=\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}={\operatorname{Spec}}\,k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
.
Let
k\subset k^{\prime}
be a purely transcendental extension (whose transcendental degree is not necessarily finite).
Assume that one of the following holds.

(a)

k
is an infinite field and
{\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}\geq n
.

(b)

{\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}\geq n+1
.

For
Z_{k^{\prime}}:=Z\times_{k}k^{\prime}
, we set

\Lambda:=\{\lambda\in Z_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})\,|\,{\rm(I)}\text{ and }{\rm(II)}\text{ hold for }\lambda\}.

(I)

The image of
\lambda
to
Z
is the generic point of
Z
.

(II)

The field extension
K(Z)\subset\kappa(\lambda)
induced by (I) is a purely transcendental extension.

Then
\Lambda
is a dense subset of
Z_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
with respect to the Zariski topology.

Proof.

Assume (a).
Fix a transcendental basis
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for the purely transcendental extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
, i.e.
k^{\prime}=k(\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I})
and
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is algebraically independent over
k
.
By
|I|={\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}\geq n
, we can take distinct elements
t_{i_{1}},...,t_{i_{n}}
. Set

\Lambda^{\prime}:=\{(z_{1}-c_{1}t_{1},...,z_{n}-c_{n}t_{n})\in{\operatorname{Spm}}k^{\prime}[z_{1},...,z_{n}]\,|\,(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in(k^{\times})^{n}\}.

Then
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset Z_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
.
It suffices to show that

(i)

If
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}
, then (I) holds for
\lambda
.

(ii)

If
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}
, then (II) holds for
\lambda
.

(iii)

\Lambda^{\prime}
is dense in
Z_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
.

Indeed, (i) and (ii) imply that
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
.
Then, combining with (iii), we see that
\Lambda
is dense in
Z_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
.

Fix
\mathfrak{m}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
.
We can write
\mathfrak{m}=(z_{1}-c_{1}t_{i_{1}},...,z_{n}-c_{n}t_{i_{n}})

for some
(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in(k^{\times})^{n}
.
Let us show (i).
To this end, it suffices to prove that
\mathfrak{m}\cap k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]=\{0\}
.
Pick an element
f(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in\mathfrak{m}\cap k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
. Then

f(z_{1},...,z_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(z_{j}-c_{j}t_{i_{j}})g_{j}(z_{1},...,z_{n})

for some
g_{j}(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in k^{\prime}[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
.
Substituting
z_{1}=c_{1}t_{i_{1}},...,z_{n}=c_{n}t_{i_{n}}
, we obtain the equation

f(c_{1}t_{i_{1}},...,c_{n}t_{i_{n}})=0
in
k^{\prime}
.
By
f(z_{1},...,z_{n})\in k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
and the fact that
c_{1}t_{1},...,c_{n}t_{n}
are algebraically independent over
k
,
we obtain
f(z_{1},...,z_{n})=0
in
k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]
,
which implies
\mathfrak{m}\cap k[z_{1},...,z_{n}]=\{0\}
.
Thus (i) holds.

Let us show (ii).
By (i), we have the induced field extension:

K(Z)=k(z_{1},...,z_{n})\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{m})=k^{\prime}[z_{1},...,z_{n}]/\mathfrak{m}\xrightarrow{\simeq}k^{\prime},\qquad z_{j}\mapsto c_{j}t_{i_{j}}.

Since
c_{1}t_{i_{1}},...,c_{n}t_{i_{n}}
is a subset of a transcendental basis for the purely transcendental extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
,
the induced field extension
K(Z)\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{m})
is a purely transcendental extension.
Thus (ii) holds.

Let us show (iii).
Fix a proper closed subset
W
of
Z_{k^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})
.
Since
k^{\times}
is an infinite set, there exists
c_{1}\in k^{\times}
such that

\{c_{1}t_{i_{1}}\}\times_{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{A}_{k^{\prime}}^{n-1}\not\subset W.

By induction on
n
, we see that
(c_{1}t_{i_{1}},...,c_{n}t_{i_{n}})\not\in W
for some
(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in(k^{\times})^{n}
.
Hence (iii) holds.
This completes the proof for the case when (a) holds.

Assume (b).
Fix a transcendental basis
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for the purely transcendental extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
.
By
|I|={\rm tr.deg}_{k}k^{\prime}\geq n+1
, we can take distinct elements
t_{i_{0}},t_{i_{1}},...,t_{i_{n}}
.
Set

\Lambda^{\prime}:=\{(z_{1}-t_{i_{1}}-t_{i_{0}}^{d_{1}},...,z_{n}-t_{i_{n}}-t_{i_{0}}^{d_{n}})\in{\operatorname{Spm}}k^{\prime}[z_{1},...,z_{n}]\,|\,(d_{1},...,d_{n})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{>0})^{n}\}.

Then the same argument as in (a) works after replacing
\Lambda^{\prime}
by
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

The purpose of this subsection is to recall the definitions and some fundamental properties of the universal families

.

,
the scheme-theoretic inverse image of the closed subscheme

Y^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{L_{Y},V_{Y}}=\{t_{0}\psi_{0}+\cdots+t_{n}\psi_{n}=0\}
is equal to

X^{{\operatorname{univ}}}_{L_{X},V_{X}}=\{t_{0}\varphi_{0}+\cdots+t_{n}\varphi_{n}=0\}
.
∎

5.3.
Generic members vs General members

In this subsection, we summarise which property is stable under taking general members.

5.3.1.
Characteristic zero

We first discuss the case of characteristic zero.
In this case, almost all the properties are stable under taking general members.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,
X
a scheme of finite type over
k
,

L
an invertible sheaf, and
V\subset H^{0}(X,L)
a finite-dimensional
k
-vector subspace which is base point free.
Let (P) be one of (1)–(25) in Theorem
4.9
,
Theorem
4.15
, and Theorem
4.17

except for (11)–(12), (15)–(16), and (23)–(25).
If
X
is (P), then general members of
V
are (P) (note that the latter five notions (15)–(16) and (23)–(25) are defined only in positive characteristic).

Proof.

It is well known that the cases (1)–(8) hold true.
As for (9)–(10), see
[
7
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2, and the paragraph immediately after Theorem 2]

(note that seminormality coincides with weak normality in characteristic zero).
It follows from
[
17
, Lemma 5.17]
that (17)–(22) hold.

Let us treat the case (13). Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a resolution of singularities.
Assume that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=0
for any
i>0
.
Fix a general member
D
of
V
, so that
D^{\prime}:=f^{*}D
is smooth.
By an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-D^{\prime})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\otimes f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}\to 0,

it holds that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{D}
and
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}=0
for
i>0
, i.e.
D
has rational singularities.
A similar argument works also for the case (14) by using Schwede’s characterisation
of Du Bois singularities
(Subsubsection
2.2.1
(
6
)).
∎

5.4.
Base points

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k
,
L
an invertible sheaf, and
\theta:V\to H^{0}(X,L)
a
k
-linear map from a finite-dimensional
k
-vector space
V
with
\overline{V}:=\theta(V)\neq 0
.
Set
K:=K(\mathbb{P}(V))
and
X_{K}:=X\times_{k}K
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

Fix a closed point
x\in X
and let
x^{\prime}
be its inverse image to
X\times_{k}K
.
Note that
x^{\prime}
is a closed point of
X_{K}
(cf. Lemma
3.16
).
Then
x\in{\operatorname{Bs}}(\overline{V})
if and only if
x^{\prime}\in X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}
.

(2)

If
\overline{V}
is base point free, then
Y\times_{k}K\not\subset X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}

for any non-empty closed subscheme
Y
of
X
(in particular,
X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}
does not contain the inverse image
of an arbitrary closed point on
X
).

(3)

If
k
is algebraically closed and
\overline{V}
is not base point free, then

X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}
contains a
K(\mathbb{P}(V))
-rational point.

Proof.

Let us show (1).
It is clear that
x\in{\operatorname{Bs}}(\overline{V})
implies
x^{\prime}\in X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}
.
Assuming that
x\not\in{\operatorname{Bs}}(\overline{V})
, let us show
x^{\prime}\not\in X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}
.
Replacing
X
by
X\setminus{\operatorname{Bs}}(\overline{V})
,
the problem is reduced to the case when
\overline{V}
is base point free, and hence

X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}=X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{\varphi}
for the morphism
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}

induced by the linear system
\overline{V}
(Remark
5.8
).
We then obtain
x^{\prime}\not\in X^{{\operatorname{gen}}}_{L,V}
by Theorem
4.12
.
This completes the proof of (1).

The assertions (2) and (3) immediately follow from (1).
∎

Remark 5.16
.

We use the same notation as in Proposition

.

, we have

.

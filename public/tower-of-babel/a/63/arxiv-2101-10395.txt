Representations of closed quadratic forms associated with Stieltjes and inverse Stieltjes holomorphic families of linear relations
Yu.M. Arlinskiĭ, S. Hassi

Abstract
. In this paper holomorphic families of linear relations which belong to the Stieltjes or inverse Stieltjes class are studied. It is shown that in their domain of holomorphy ℂ ∖ ℝ + {\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{+} the values of Stieltjes and inverse Stieltjes families are, up to a rotation, maximal sectorial. This leads to a study of the associated closed sesquilinear forms and their representations. In particular, it is shown that the Stieltjes and inverse Stieltjes holomorphic families of linear relations are of type (B) in the sense of Kato. These results are proved by using linear fractional transforms which connect these families to holomorphic functions that belong to a combined Nevanlinna-Schur class and a key tool then relies on a specific structure of contractive operators.

2.1.
Sectorial linear relations

Let

be a linear relation (l.r.)
in the Hilbert space

Each

Observe, in particular, that the case

A\geq 0

Let

A

Note that

W(\exp{-i(\beta+\alpha)}A)\subseteq\left\{{\mathbb{C}}:\;|\arg z|\leq(\beta-\alpha)/2\right\}=S((\beta-\alpha)/2),

A similar definition can be given for sectorial sesquilinear forms. A sectorial form

-sectorial l.r.’s

-sectorial l.r.

2.3.
Operator-valued functions of the class

Definition 2.3
.

[
7
]
.
Let
{\mathfrak{M}}

If

[
10

(see also
[
7
]
)
there exists (up to unitary equivalence) a unique minimal passive selfadjoint system

\tau=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}D&C\cr C^{*}&F\end{bmatrix},{\mathfrak{M}},{\mathfrak{M}},{\mathfrak{K}}\right\}

\Omega(z)=\Omega_{\tau}(z)=D+zC(I-zF)^{-1}C^{*},\;z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{(-\infty,-1]\cup[1,+\infty)\}.

Moreover, if

T=\begin{bmatrix}D&C\cr C^{*}&F\end{bmatrix}:\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathfrak{M}}\\
\oplus\\
{\mathfrak{K}}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathfrak{M}}\\
\oplus\\
{\mathfrak{K}}\end{array}

{\rm\overline{span}\,}\left\{F^{n}C^{*}{\mathfrak{M}},\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}\right\}={\mathfrak{M}}\oplus{\mathfrak{K}}.

The latter is equivalent to the fact that the operator

Observe, that if

Recall that
S(\varphi)
stands for the sector

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
be a Nevanlinna family in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{M}}
which is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is a Stieltjes family;

(ii)

the following inequality holds

{\rm Re\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]+\cfrac{{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{|{\rm Im\,}\lambda|}\,\,|{\rm Im\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]|\geq 0,\;f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda),\;{\rm Im\,}\lambda\neq 0;

(iii)

\lambda{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is a Nevanlinna family in
{\mathfrak{M}}
.

Furthermore, a Stieltjes family
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{M}}
admits the following properties:

If
\arg\lambda\in[-\pi,\pi)
, then

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm Re\,}\lambda<0\Rightarrow|{\rm Im\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]|\leq\frac{|{\rm Im\,}\lambda|}{-{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{\rm Re\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)\\[8.53581pt]
\qquad\qquad\Longleftrightarrow W({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda))\subset S(\pi-|\arg\lambda|);\\[8.53581pt]
{\rm Re\,}\lambda=0\Rightarrow{\rm Re\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]\geq 0\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)\Longleftrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)\quad\mbox{is an m-accretive l.r.};\\[8.53581pt]
{\rm Re\,}\lambda>0,\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm Im\,}\lambda>0\Rightarrow W\left(\exp(-i(\pi-|\arg\lambda|)/2){\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)\right)\subset S({\frac{\pi-|\arg\lambda|}{2}}),\\[8.53581pt]
{\rm Im\,}\lambda<0\Rightarrow W\left(\exp(i(\pi-|\arg\lambda|)/2){\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)\right)\subset S({\frac{\pi-|\arg\lambda|}{2}}).\end{array}\right.\end{array}

In particular, if
\beta\in[0,\pi/2)
, then

\begin{array}[]{l}\arg\lambda\in[\pi-\beta,\pi+\beta]\Longrightarrow W({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda))\subset S(\beta),\\
\arg\lambda\in[\beta,\pi/2]\;(\beta\neq 0)\,\Longrightarrow W({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda))\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}:\arg\xi\in[0,\pi-\beta]\},\\
\arg\lambda\in[-\pi/2,-\beta]\;(\beta\neq 0)\,\Longrightarrow W({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda))\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}:\arg\xi\in[\pi+\beta,2\pi]\}.\end{array}

Proof.

First the equivalence of assertions (i) – (iii) is shown.

(i)
\Longleftrightarrow
(ii) Denote
z=\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
Then equivalently
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\left((-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty)\right)
and one has

(3.1)

\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{{\rm Im\,}z}=-\frac{2{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{{\rm Im\,}\lambda},\quad{\rm Im\,}\lambda\neq 0.

By Lemma
2.8

{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is given by the transform (
2.12
) for some
\Omega\in\mathcal{RS}({\mathfrak{M}})
,
in fact,

\Omega(z)=\left\{\left\{\left(I_{\mathfrak{M}}+{\mathcal{Q}}\left(\lambda\right)\right)h,\left(\Omega\left(\lambda\right)-I_{\mathfrak{M}}\right)h\right\}:\;h\in{\mathfrak{M}}\right\},\quad\lambda=\cfrac{z-1}{z+1}.

Therefore,
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
precisely when
\{g,g^{\prime}\}:=\{f+f^{\prime},f^{\prime}-f\}\in\Omega(z)
.
Since
\|g\|^{2}+\|g^{\prime}\|^{2}=4{\rm Re\,}(f^{\prime},f)
and
{\rm Im\,}(g^{\prime},g)=2{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)
we conclude from
Theorem
2.4
that the inequality (
2.6
) with
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in\Omega(z)
is equivalent to

{\rm Re\,}(f^{\prime},f)+\frac{{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{{\rm Im\,}\lambda}\,{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)\geq 0.

Since
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is a Nevanlinna family, one has
\dfrac{{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)}{{\rm Im\,}\lambda}\geq 0
.
This proves the claim.

(ii)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii) Let
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
.
Then equivalently
\{f,\lambda f^{\prime}\}\in\lambda{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
and

{\rm Im\,}(\lambda f^{\prime},f)={\rm Re\,}\lambda\;{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)+{\rm Im\,}\lambda\;{\rm Re\,}(f^{\prime},f).

Therefore the inequality in (ii) means that
{{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda):=\lambda{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)

is dissipative for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and accumulative for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.
Since
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is a Nevanlinna family, one has
{{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda)^{*}={{\mathcal{M}}}(\bar{\lambda})
,
\lambda\in{{\mathbb{C}}\backslash{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Moreover,
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
is maximal dissipative for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, i.e.,

{\rm ran\,}({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)+\mu)={\mathfrak{M}}
for some, equivalently for all,

\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and
({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)+\mu)^{-1}
is holomorphic as a function of
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
.
Then also
{\rm ran\,}({{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda)+\lambda\mu)=\lambda\,{\rm ran\,}({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)+\mu)={\mathfrak{M}}
and

({{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda)+\lambda\mu)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\lambda}({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)+\mu)^{-1}
is holomorphic at
\lambda
,
when
\mu
is chosen such that
0<\arg\mu<\pi-\arg\lambda
. One concludes that
{{\mathcal{M}}}(\lambda)=\lambda{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)

is a Nevanlinna family if and only if
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
satisfies the inequality in (ii).
Hence, the equivalence of (i) – (iii) is shown.

Now the remaining assertions can be easily proved. The statements with
{\rm Re\,}\lambda<0
and
{\rm Re\,}\lambda=0
are clear from
the inequality in (ii).

Assume that
{\rm Re\,}\lambda>0
. Since
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
and
\lambda{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
are Nevanlinna families
one has
{\rm Im\,}({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)f,f)\geq 0
and
{\rm Im\,}(\lambda\,{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f])\geq 0
for all
f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
when
{\rm Im\,}\lambda>0
.
In this case
0\leq\arg{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]+\arg\lambda\leq\pi
or, equivalently,

-\frac{\pi-\arg\lambda}{2}\leq\arg\left(\exp(-i(\pi-\arg\lambda)/2){\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]\right)\leq\frac{\pi-\arg\lambda}{2},

where
0<\arg\lambda<\pi/2
. Similarly, if
{\rm Im\,}\lambda<0
then
-\pi\leq\arg{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]+\arg\lambda\leq 0
or, equivalently,

-\frac{\pi-|\arg\lambda|}{2}\leq\arg\left(\exp(i(\pi-|\arg\lambda|)/2){\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f]\right)\leq\frac{\pi-|\arg\lambda|}{2},

where

. This gives the assertions for

The last three implications concerning the numerical range of

are clear.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
be a Nevanlinna family in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{M}}
which is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
is an inverse Stieltjes family;

(ii)

the following inequality holds

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm Re\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]-\cfrac{{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{{\rm Im\,}\lambda}\,\,{\rm Im\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]\leq 0\\
\Longleftrightarrow{\rm Re\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])+\cfrac{{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{|{\rm Im\,}\lambda|}\,\,|{\rm Im\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])|\geq 0,\;f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda),\;{\rm Im\,}\lambda\neq 0;\end{array}

(iii)

\dfrac{{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)}{\lambda}
is a Nevanlinna family in
{\mathfrak{M}}
.

Furthermore, an inverse Stieltjes family
{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{M}}
admits the following properties:

If
\arg\lambda\in[-\pi,\pi)
, then

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm Re\,}\lambda<0\Rightarrow|{\rm Im\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])|\leq\frac{|{\rm Im\,}\lambda|}{-{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{\rm Re\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\\[8.53581pt]
\iff W(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda))\subset S({\pi-|\arg\lambda|});\\[8.53581pt]
{\rm Re\,}\lambda=0\Longrightarrow{\rm Re\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])\geq 0\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\Longleftrightarrow-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\quad\mbox{is an m-accretive l.r.};\\[8.53581pt]
{\rm Re\,}\lambda>0,\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm Im\,}\lambda>0\Longrightarrow W\left(\exp(-i(\pi+|\arg\lambda|)/2){\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\right)\subset S({\frac{\pi-|\arg\lambda|}{2}}),\\[8.53581pt]
{\rm Im\,}\lambda<0\Longrightarrow W\left(\exp(i(\pi+|\arg\lambda|)/2){\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\right)\subset S({\frac{\pi-|\arg\lambda|}{2}}).\end{array}\right.\par\end{array}

In particular, if
\beta\in[0,\pi/2)
, then

\begin{array}[]{l}\arg\lambda\in[\pi-\beta,\pi+\beta]\Longrightarrow W({\mathcal{R}}(\lambda))\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}:\arg\lambda\in[\pi-\beta,\pi+\beta]\},\\
\arg\lambda\in[\beta,\pi/2]\;(\beta\neq 0)\,\Longrightarrow W({\mathcal{R}}(\lambda))\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}:\arg\xi\in[\beta,\pi]\},\\
\arg\lambda\in[-\pi/2,-\beta]\;(\beta\neq 0)\,\Longrightarrow W({\mathcal{R}}(\lambda))\subset\{\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}:\arg\xi\in[-\pi,-\beta]\}.\end{array}

Proof.

Since the proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
only the main steps are pointed out here.

Using formula (
2.13
) in Lemma
2.8
it is seen that

\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
precisely when
\{g,g^{\prime}\}:=\{f+f^{\prime},f-f^{\prime}\}\in\Omega(z)
.
Since
\|g\|^{2}+\|g^{\prime}\|^{2}=4{\rm Re\,}(f^{\prime},f)
and
{\rm Im\,}(g^{\prime},g)=-2{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)
we conclude from
Theorem
2.4
and the formula
3.1
that the inequality (
2.6
) with
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in\Omega(z)
is equivalent to

{\rm Re\,}(f^{\prime},f)-\frac{{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{{\rm Im\,}\lambda}\,{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)\geq 0,

i.e.,

{\rm Re\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])-\cfrac{{\rm Re\,}\lambda}{{\rm Im\,}\lambda}\,\,{\rm Im\,}(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])\geq 0,\quad f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda),\;{\rm Im\,}\lambda\neq 0.

This yields the equivalence (i)
\Longleftrightarrow
(ii).

On the other hand, with
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
one has

\{f,1/\lambda f^{\prime}\}\in\frac{{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)}{\lambda}
and

{\rm Im\,}(1/\lambda f^{\prime},f)={\rm Im\,}\left(\overline{\lambda}(f^{\prime},f)\right)={\rm Re\,}\lambda\;{\rm Im\,}(f^{\prime},f)-{\rm Im\,}\lambda\;{\rm Re\,}(f^{\prime},f),

or, equivalently,

{\rm Im\,}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]}{\lambda}\right)={\rm Re\,}\lambda\;{\rm Im\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]-{\rm Im\,}\lambda\;{\rm Re\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f],\quad f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda),\;{\rm Im\,}\lambda\neq 0.

Therefore the inequality in (ii) means that
\frac{{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)}{\lambda}

is dissipative for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and accumulative for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
,
which then yields the equivalence (ii)
\Longleftrightarrow
(iii).

As to the remaining assertions, observe that for
{\rm Im\,}\lambda>0
one has
0\leq\arg{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]\leq\pi
and

0\leq\arg\left(\frac{{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]}{\lambda}\right)=\arg{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]-\arg\lambda\leq\pi,

for all
f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
or, equivalently,

(3.2)

-\pi+\arg\lambda\leq\arg(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])\leq 0.

Similarly
{\rm Im\,}\lambda<0
one has
-\pi\leq\arg{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]\leq 0
,

-\pi\leq\arg{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f]-\arg\lambda\leq 0
, and

(3.3)

0\leq\arg(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[f])\leq\pi-|\arg\lambda|.

All the statements in the second part of the theorem follow from the inequalities in (ii), (
3.2
), and (
3.3
).

\alpha\in[0,\pi/2),
the families
{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)
and
-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)
are maximal sectorial l.r.’s with the vertex at the origin and the semi-angle
\alpha
;

(2)

if
\lambda=r\exp(i\psi)
, where
\psi\in(0,\pi/2],
then

\begin{array}[]{l}\arg({\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)[f])\in[0,\pi-\psi]\;\forall f\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{Q}}(\lambda)\setminus\{0\},\\
\arg(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[g])\in[\psi-\pi,0]\;\forall g\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\setminus\{0\};\end{array}

(3)

if

\arg(-{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)[g])\in[0,\pi+\psi]\;\forall g\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathcal{R}}(\lambda)\setminus\{0\}.\end{array}

Thus, for an arbitrary

such that

(respect.

and inverse Stieltjes family

on

determines a unique (in general nondensely defined) closed sectorial form (up to a rotation) via the closure of

,

and the representing l.r.’s

.

form holomorphic families of the type (B) in sense of Kato

]

4.
Constructions of Stieltjes and inverse Stieltjes families

In this section we give some explicit constructions of Stieltjes and inverse Stieltjes families.
In the first subsection the case of operator functions whose values are bounded operators on a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{M}}
is considered.
These results are used in second subsection to construct Stieltjes and inverse Stieltjes functions whose values are unbounded operators or, more generally, l.r.’s in
{\mathfrak{M}}
. In the last subsection special behaviour of these functions in the unbounded case is pointed out
by means of an example. The constructions given in this section are shown to be of general nature in Section
5
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{M}}
and

\oplus\\
{\mathfrak{K}}\end{array}\to\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathfrak{M}}\\
\oplus\\
{\mathfrak{K}}\end{array}

(

Using (
4.1

4.2

4.3

The equalities in (
4.5

4.2

4.3

,

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
L(x)
,
x\in(a,b)
be a function
whose values are bounded nonnegative selfadjoint operators acting on
H
.
Suppose that

(1)

L(x)
is non-decreasing on
(a,b)
,

(2)

L(b):=s-\lim\limits_{x\uparrow b}L(x)
exists as a bounded operator,

(3)

{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)={\mathcal{R}}_{0}
is constant for
x\in(a,b)
.

Then

(6.1)

\lim\limits_{x\downarrow a}L^{-1}(x)[f]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}L^{-1}(a)[f],\;f\in{\mathcal{D}}[L^{-1}(a)],\\
+\infty,\qquad f\in{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\setminus{\mathcal{D}}[L^{-1}(a)],\end{array}\right.

(6.2)

\lim\limits_{x\uparrow b}L^{-1}(x)[f]=L^{-1}(b)[f],\;f\in{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}[L^{-1}(b)].

Proof.

By assumption (1) the strong limit value
L(a)
exists as a bounded operator. Clearly.

L(a)\leq L(x)\leq L(b)
for each
x\in(a,b)
. Hence,
{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\subseteq{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(b)
. The inverses
L^{-1}(x)
,
L^{-1}(a)
and

L^{-1}(b)
are in general nonnegative selfadjoint l.r.’s
The corresponding closed sesquilinear forms are defined on
{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
,

{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)
, and
{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(b)
, respectively. Since

L(x_{1})\leq L(x_{2})
if
x_{1}<x_{2}
, then
L^{-1}(x_{1})\geq L^{-1}(x_{2})
.
In addition
L^{-1}(a)\geq L^{-1}(x)\geq L^{-1}(b)
.
Let us prove that

(6.3)

\sup\limits_{x\in(a,b)}L^{-1}(x)[f]<\infty\iff f\in{\mathcal{D}}[L^{-1}(a)]={\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a).

If
f\in{\mathcal{D}}[L^{-1}(a)]
, then
L^{-1}(x)[f]\leq L^{-1}(a)[f]<\infty
.
Conversely, assume that

L^{-1}(x)[f]\leq C\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in(a,b)\quad\mbox{and for some}\quad f\in{\mathcal{D}}[L^{-1}(x)]={\mathcal{R}}_{0}.

Then there exists a sequence of numbers
\{x_{n}\}\subset(a,b)
such that the sequence of vectors
\{g_{n}:=L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x_{n})f\}

converges weakly to some vector
\varphi\in\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}
, i.e.,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(g_{n},h)=(\varphi,h)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad h\in\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}_{0}.

Here
L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)=\left(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)\right)^{-1}
is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse.
Since

\lim\limits_{x\downarrow a}L(x)h=L(a)h\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lim\limits_{x\downarrow a}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)h=L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h

for all
h
, one gets

\begin{array}[]{l}(f,h)=(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x_{n})g_{n},h)=(g_{n},L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x_{n})h)\\
\qquad\quad=(g_{n},L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h)+(g_{n},(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x_{n})-L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a))h)\to(\varphi,L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h)\;(n\to\infty)\end{array}

It follows that
f=L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)\varphi.

Next it is shown that

\lim\limits_{x\downarrow a}||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||=||h||,\;h\in{\rm\overline{ran}\,}L(a).

Since
{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)\subseteq{\rm ran\,}L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)
, one has

\sup\limits_{g\in H}\cfrac{|(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h,g)|^{2}}{(L(x)g,g)}=||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2}.

Then for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
there exists
g_{\varepsilon}

such that

||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2}-\varepsilon\leq\cfrac{|(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h,g_{\varepsilon})|^{2}}{(L(x)g_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})}\leq||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2}.

Let

C:=\sup\limits_{x\in(a,b)}||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2}.

Then by (
6.3
)
C<\infty
. Since

\lim\limits_{x\downarrow a}||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2}=\sup\limits_{x\in(a,b)}||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2},

we get

C-\varepsilon\leq\cfrac{|(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h,g_{\varepsilon})|^{2}}{(L(a)g_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon})}\leq C

It follows that

||h||^{2}=\sup\limits_{g\in H}\cfrac{|(L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h,g)|^{2}}{(L(a)g,g)}=C.

Thus

\lim\limits_{x\downarrow a}||L^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(x)L^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(a)h||^{2}=||h||^{2},\;h\in{\rm\overline{ran}\,}L(a).

Relation (
6.1
) is proved. Relation (
6.2
) can be
proved similarly.
∎

Integrated Relative-Measurement-Based Network Localization and Formation Maneuver Control (Extended Version)
Xu Fang, Lihua Xie, Xiaolei Li

Abstract
This paper studies the problem of integrated distributed network localization and formation maneuver control. We develop an integrated relative-measurement-based scheme, which only uses relative positions, distances, bearings, angles, ratio-of-distances, or their combination to achieve distributed network localization and formation maneuver control in ℝ d ​ ( d ≥ 2 ) \mathbb{R}^{d}(d\geq 2) . By exploring the localizability and invariance of the target formation, the scale, rotation, and translation of the formation can be controlled simultaneously by only tuning the leaders’ positions, i.e., the followers do not need to know parameters of the scale, rotation, and translation of the target formation. The proposed method can globally drive the formation errors to zero in finite time over multi-layer d + 1 d\!+\!1 -rooted graphs. A simulation example is given to illustrate the theoretical results.

Assumption 1

For each follower

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, among the neighbors of follower
i

d\!+\!1
neighbors
{j_{0}},\cdots,{j_{d}}\!\in\!\mathcal{N}_{i}

p^{*}(t)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}(p_{i}(t)-\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}}\hat{p}_{j}(t))=\!\mathbf{0},\ i\in\mathcal{V}_{f},\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Remark 3
.

From (

p^{*}_{f}(t)\!+\!(\Omega_{f\!f}^{-1}\Omega_{fl}\otimes I_{d})p^{*}_{l}(t)\!=\!\mathbf{0}
. The leaders know their own positions, i.e.,
\hat{p}_{l}(t)=p_{l}(t)
. Based on the first and second subequations of (

) is equivalent to
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}[p_{f}(t)+(\Omega_{f\!f}^{-1}\Omega_{fl}\otimes I_{d})p^{*}_{l}(t)]\!=\!\mathbf{0}
, i.e.,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}[p_{i}(t)-p^{*}_{i}(t)]\!=\!\mathbf{0},i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
.

Based on (
34
),
define the tracking error
\bar{e}_{i}
and position estimation error
\hat{e}_{i}
of follower
i\in\mathcal{V}_{f}
as

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{i}(t)=p_{i}(t)-\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}}{\hat{p}}_{j}(t).\

(35)

\displaystyle\hat{e}_{i}(t)=\hat{p}_{i}(t)-p_{i}(t).

(36)

As shown in (

and its

neighbors

can form a displacement constraint, i.e.,

\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}h_{ij}(t)e_{ij}(t)\!=\!\mathbf{0}\Rightarrow h_{ii}(t)p_{i}(t)\!=\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}h_{ij}(t)p_{j}(t),

(37)

where
h_{ii}(t)\!=\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}h_{ij}(t)
, and the displacement parameters

can be calculated by the relative positions, bearings, distances, angles, ratio-of-distances, or their combination among agent
i
and its

neighbors
{j_{0}},\cdots,{j_{d}}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
(the details can be found in Appendix and
[

]
).
Under Assumption
1
,

h_{ii}(t)\!\neq\!0
if

neighbors
{j_{0}},\cdots,{j_{d}}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
reach their target positions, and then (

p_{i}(t)\!=\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{h_{ij}(t)}{h_{ii}(t)}p_{j}(t).

(38)

Although we can guarantee the localizability of the target formation

(\mathcal{G},p^{*}(t))
, there is no guarantee on the localizability of follower agents during the transient before all agents reach the target formation. To tackle this problem, two operation modes (maneuvering mode and maintaining mode) are designed for each follower. A remarkable advantage of the two operation modes is that we do not require that all the followers be always localizable by their neighbors before all agents reach the target formation.

Definition 6
.

Follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
is in the maneuvering mode at time instant

if
its

neighbors
{j_{0}},\cdots,{j_{d}}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
reach their target positions.

Definition 7
.

Follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
is in the maintaining mode at time instant

if at least one of its
d\!+\!1
neighbors
{j_{0}},\cdots,{j_{d}}\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
does not reach its target position.

The immediate question in Definition
6
and Definition
7
is how each agent knows whether it has arrived at its target position? Since the leaders know their own positions and target positions, each leader
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{l}
can know whether it has arrived at its target position.
Next, we will introduce how each follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
knows whether
it achieves self-localization and has arrived at its target position.
Although the followers do not know their target positions,
each follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
under Assumption
1
can know that its task is completed
if and only if

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}(\frac{h_{ij}(t)}{h_{ii}(t)}\!-\!\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}})\hat{p}_{j}(t)=\mathbf{0},\\
\hat{p}_{i}(t)-\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{h_{ij}(t)}{h_{ii}(t)}\hat{p}_{j}(t)\!=\!\mathbf{0},\\
\hat{p}_{j}(t)=p_{j}(t)=p^{*}_{j}(t),\ j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(39)

Remark 4
.

Each follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
needs to communicate with its neighbor
j\!\in\!\mathcal{N}_{i}
to obtain its neighbor’s position estimate
\hat{p}_{j}
and to know whether its neighbor
j\!\in\!\mathcal{N}_{i}
satisfies the third subequation of (
39

The position estimates
\hat{p}_{i},\hat{p}_{j}
and control inputs

of each follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}
and its neighbor
j\!\in\!\mathcal{N}_{i}
will be designed in Section
V

to guarantee that the condition (
39
) holds.
If the third subequation of
(
39
) holds, we can know from (

the first and second subequations of
(

) are equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\hskip-11.38092pt\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\!\!(\frac{h_{ij}(t)}{h_{ii}(t)}\!-\!\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}})\hat{p}_{j}(t)\!=\!p_{i}(t)\!-\!\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}}p^{*}_{j}(t)\!=\!p_{i}(t)\!-\!p^{*}_{i}(t)\!=\!\mathbf{0},\\
&\hskip-11.38092pt\hat{p}_{i}(t)-\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\!\!\frac{h_{ij}(t)}{h_{ii}(t)}\hat{p}_{j}(t)=\hat{p}_{i}(t)-p_{i}(t)=\mathbf{0}.\end{array}

(40)

Thus, we can know from (

In the multi-layer
d\!+\!1
-rooted graphs, the followers will sequentially make their own conditions (

d\!+\!1
-rooted graphs.

From the above analysis, each leader or follower can know whether
it has arrived at its target position. Then,
each follower
i\!\in\!\mathcal{V}_{f}

.

V-A

Control Design for the Leaders

We first define a continuous function
\text{sig}(\cdot)
as

\text{sig}^{l}(x)\!=\![\text{sgn}(x_{1})|x_{1}|^{l},\cdots,\text{sgn}(x_{d})|x_{d}|^{l}]^{T},

(41)

where
x\!=\![x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}]^{T}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{d},l\!>\!0
, and
\text{sgn}(\cdot)
is the signum function defined component-wise. Then, it has

\begin{array}[]{ll}&x^{T}\text{sig}^{l}(x)=|x_{1}|^{l\!+\!1}+\cdots+|x_{d}|^{l\!+\!1}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ =(x_{1}^{2})^{\frac{l\!+\!1}{2}}+\cdots+(x_{d}^{2})^{\frac{l\!+\!1}{2}}.\end{array}

(42)

If
0<l<1

[
25
]
, it has

\begin{array}[]{ll}&x^{T}\text{sig}^{l}(x)=(x_{1}^{2})^{\frac{l\!+\!1}{2}}+\cdots+(x_{d}^{2})^{\frac{l\!+\!1}{2}}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \geq(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d}^{2})^{\frac{l\!+\!1}{2}}=\|x\|_{2}^{l\!+\!1}.\end{array}

(43)

Let
\tilde{e}_{i}=p_{i}-p^{*}_{i}
be the tracking error of leader
i
.
To achieve its control objective (
5
), the control protocol of leader
i\in\mathcal{V}_{l}
is given by

u_{i}=-a_{1}\tilde{e}_{i}-a_{2}\text{sig}^{a_{3}}(\tilde{e}_{i})+\dot{p}^{*}_{i},

(44)

where
a_{1},a_{2}>0,0<a_{3}<1
are positive control gains.

Lemma 3
.

Under Assumption
1
, the leaders will drive their tracking errors to zero in finite time under controller (
44
).

Proof.

Consider a Lyapunov function candidate
V_{1}\!=\!\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\tilde{e}}^{T}_{i}{\tilde{e}}_{i}
. It has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\begin{array}[]{ll}&\dot{V}_{1}\!=\!-2a_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\tilde{e}}^{T}_{i}{\tilde{e}}_{i}\!-\!2a_{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\tilde{e}_{i}^{T}\text{sig}^{a_{3}}(\tilde{e}_{i})\\
&\ \ \ \ \!\leq\!-2a_{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\tilde{e}_{i}\|_{2}^{a_{3}\!+\!1}\!\leq\!-2a_{2}V_{1}^{\frac{a_{3}\!+\!1}{2}}.\end{array}\end{array}

(45)

From Lemma
1
of
[
26
]
, the tracking errors of the leaders will converge to zero in finite time, i.e., there exists a finite time
T,0<T<\infty
such that
\tilde{e}_{i}(t)=0,t\geq T,i\in\mathcal{V}_{l}
, where
T
is determined by the initial tracking errors
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}{\tilde{e}}^{T}_{i}(0){\tilde{e}}_{i}(0)
.
∎

V-B

Control Design for the Followers under Maintaining Mode

From Section
IV
, if follower
i
is in the maintaining mode, it may not be localized by its neighbors.
Hence,
it should keep its formation error bounded before switching to the maneuvering mode. To keep its formation error bounded,
the controller of follower
i\in\mathcal{V}_{f}
is designed as

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{i}\!=\!-a_{1}\hat{p}_{i}\!+\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}}(a_{1}{\hat{p}}_{j}\!+\!{\hat{v}}_{j}),\\
\dot{\hat{p}}_{i}\!=\!-2a_{1}\hat{p}_{i}\!+\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}}(2a_{1}{\hat{p}}_{j}\!+\!{\hat{v}}_{j}),\end{array}\right.\end{array}

(46)

where
a_{1}>0
is a positive control gain, and
{\hat{v}}_{j}\!=\!\dot{\hat{p}}_{j}
is the estimated velocity.

Theorem 2
.

Follower
i\in\mathcal{V}_{f}
under the maintaining mode will keep its formation error bounded under controller (
46

Proof.

Under controller (
46
), it has

\dot{e}_{i}\!=\!-D_{a}e_{i},\ i\!=\!m\!+\!1,\cdots,n,

(47)

where
e_{i}=(\bar{e}_{i}^{T},\hat{e}_{i}^{T})^{T}
is the formation error of follower
i
and
D_{a}\!=\!\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{1}&a_{1}\\
a_{1}&a_{1}\end{array}\right]\!\otimes\!I_{d}.
The formation error
e_{i}
of follower
i
consists of the tracking error
\bar{e}_{i}
(
35
) and position estimation error
\hat{e}_{i}
(
36
).
Note that the matrix
D_{a}
is positive semidefinite. Consider a Lyapunov function candidate
V_{2}=\frac{1}{2}e^{T}_{i}e_{i}
. It has
\dot{{V}}_{2}\!=\!-e_{i}^{T}D_{a}e_{i}\!\leq\!0.

Hence, we obtain
\|e_{i}(t)\|_{2}\!\leq\!\|e_{i}(0)\|_{2},t\!\geq\!0

of follower

will be no more than its initial formation error

∎

V-C

Control Design for the Followers under Maneuvering Mode

From Section
IV
, if follower
i
is in the maneuvering mode, it must be localized by its neighbors. Hence,
if follower
i
is in the maneuvering mode, it should achieve self-localization
and get to its target position. To achieve self-localization
and get to its target position,
the control protocol of follower
i\in\mathcal{V}_{f}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{i}\!=\!\eta_{i}\!+\!\sum\limits_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\frac{w_{ij}}{w_{ii}}{\hat{v}}_{j}\!+\!\text{sig}^{a_{3}}(\eta_{i}),\\

(48)

From Lemma

of

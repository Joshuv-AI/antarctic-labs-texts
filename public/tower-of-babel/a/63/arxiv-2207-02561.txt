Durfee squares, symmetric partitions and bounds on Kronecker coefficients Thanks: August 8, 2026 Thanks: ⋆ Department of Mathematics, UCLA, Los Angeles, CA 90095. Email: pak@math.ucla.edu Thanks: † Department of Mathematics, USC, Los Angeles, CA 90089. Email: gpanova@usc.edu

Abstract
. We resolve two open problems on Kronecker coefficients g ⁡ ( λ , μ , ν ) g(\lambda,\mu,\nu) of the symmetric group. First, we prove that for partitions λ , μ , ν \lambda,\mu,\nu with fixed Durfee square size, the Kronecker coefficients grow at most polynomially. Second, we show that the maximal Kronecker coefficients g ⁡ ( λ , λ , λ ) g(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda) for self-conjugate partitions λ \lambda grow superexponentially. We also give applications to explicit special cases.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Foreword

How do you approach a massive open problem with countless cases to consider?
You start from the beginning, of course, trying to resolve either
the most natural, the most interesting or the simplest yet out of reach
special cases. For example, when looking at the billions and billions of
stars
contemplating the immense challenge
of celestial cartography, you start
with the
closest
(Alpha Centauri and Barnard’s Star), the

brightest
(Sirius and Canopus), or the
most useful

(Polaris aka North Star),
but not with the
galaxy far, far away.

The same principle applies to the
Kronecker coefficients

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
.
Introduced by Murnaghan in 1938, they remain among the great mysteries
of Algebraic Combinatorics. In part due to the fact that they lack a
combinatorial interpretation, even the most basic questions present seemingly
insurmountable challenges, while even the simplest examples are already
hard to compute. Yet, this should not prevent us from pursuing both.

In our previous paper
[
PP20
]
, we briefly surveyed the dispiriting state of art
on Kronecker bounds, and identified two promising problems which are
both interesting, simple looking, yet not immediately approachable with the tools previously used:

(1)
give upper bounds for
g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
, where

\hskip 0.85355pt\lambda,\mu,\nu
have a
small Durfee square
,

(2)
give lower bounds for the maximal
g(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)
, where
\lambda
is
symmetric
:
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.

We largely resolve both problems, getting estimates
up to a constant in the leading terms of the asymptotics.
For the
small Durfee square problem

(1)
, we
employ symmetric functions technology and obtain new estimates
on the
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
which
are of independent interest. For the
fully
symmetric Kronecker problem

(2)
,
we use a combinatorial argument based on the
monotonicity property
.
We then use this argument to derive the first nontrivial lower bounds
in several explicit examples.

1.2.
Small Durfee square problem

For a partition
\lambda\vdash n
, denote by
\ell(\lambda)
the
length
of
\lambda
, i.e. the number of rows in the Young diagram
\lambda
.
Denote by
d(\lambda)
the
Durfee square size
, i.e. the size of the largest square which fits
\lambda
. Clearly,
d(\lambda)\leq\ell(\lambda)
.

The
Kronecker coefficients

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\in\mathbb{N}
are defined as the structure constants in the ring of characters of
S_{n}\hskip 0.85355pt
:

{\chi^{\mu}}\hskip 1.70709pt\cdot\hskip 1.70709pt\chi^{\nu}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\hskip 1.70709pt\chi^{\lambda}\,,\quad\text{where \ \ $\mu,\hskip 1.70709pt\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\vdash\hskip 0.85355ptn$}

and
\chi^{\alpha}
denotes the character of the irreducible representation (Specht module) indexed by the partition
\alpha
.
Note that
g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
are symmetric with respect to permutations of three partitions.

It is known that
g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\leq\min\bigl\{f^{\lambda},f^{\mu},f^{\nu}\bigr\}
,
where
f^{\alpha}:=\chi^{\alpha}(1)
is the dimension of the Specht module, see
[
PPY19
]
, but there are no other general
bounds.
On the other hand, for partitions with few rows, we have the following general upper bound:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
PP20
]
)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n
, such that
\ell(\lambda)=\ell
,
\ell(\mu)=m
,
and
\ell(\nu)=r
. Then:

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\ \leq\ \Bigg(1\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{\ell mr}{n}\Bigg)^{n}\hskip 1.70709pt\Bigg(1\hskip 1.70709pt+\,\frac{n}{\ell mr}\Bigg)^{\ell mr}.

In particular, we have:

Corollary 1.2

(see
\lx@sectionsign
2.5
)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n
, such that
\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu),\ell(\nu)\leq k
. Then:

(1.1)

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\,\leq\,n^{k^{3}}\hskip 0.85355pt.

In other words, for partitions with fixed number of rows, the Kronecker coefficients
are bounded polynomially.

2.1.
Partitions

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
be a

of

n:=|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{\ell}
, where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{\ell}\geq 1
. We write

\lambda\vdash n
in this case. As in the introduction,
let
\ell(\lambda):=\ell
be the

of
\lambda
, and let
d(\lambda)=\max\{k~:~\lambda_{k}\geq k\}
denote the
Durfee square size
.

Denote by
p(n)
the number of partitions
\lambda\vdash n
.
Let
\lambda^{\prime}
denote the
conjugate partition
of
\lambda
.
Let
\lambda+\mu
denote the
sum of partitions
:
(\lambda_{1}+\mu_{1},\lambda_{2}+\mu_{2},\ldots)
. Similarly, let
\lambda\cup\mu
denotes the

defined as
\lambda\cup\mu:=(\lambda^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime})^{\prime}

We write

if
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for all

.
The

\lambda/\mu
is the difference of two straight shapes
(Young diagrams).

We use
(a^{b})=(a,\ldots,a)

, and
\rho_{\ell}=(\ell-1,\ldots,2,1)

2.2.
Basic inequalities

Throughout the paper we will use several basic inequalities allowing us to reach the polynomial bounds. By
AM–GM
we will refer to the Arithmetic vs Geometric mean inequality

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}}{m}\ \geq\ \left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\right)^{1/m}

for nonnegative real numbers
x_{i}
. In particular, applying this with
x_{i}=N+1-i
gives

(2.1)

k!\hskip 1.70709pt\binom{N}{k}\ =\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}(N+1-i)\ \leq\ \left(\frac{\sum_{I=1}^{k}N+1-i}{k}\right)^{k}\ =\ \left(N-\frac{k-1}{2}\right)^{k}

We will also use the fact that
\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n}
is an increasing sequence in
n
with limit
e^{x}
.

We will also use the log-concavity of binomial coefficients, namely

(2.2)

\binom{x}{k}^{2}\ \geq\ \binom{x-1}{k}\binom{x+1}{k}\ \geq\ \cdots\ \geq\ \binom{x-r}{k}\binom{x+r}{k}.

2.3.
Partition inequalities

We draw partitions as Young diagrams in the English notation. For example, we use

for
\lambda=(4,3,1)
.
We will use several bounds on the number of partitions which can be easily seen from this graphical representation.

The Young diagram of a partition can be determined by its boundary, which is a North-East monotone lattice path. If the partition has length
k
and at most
n
boxes, then, after removing its first column, it fits in an
k\times(n-k)
rectangle and we can bound it by the number of lattice paths as

(2.3)

\#\{\lambda\vdash n\,:\,\ell(\lambda)=k\}\ \leq\ \binom{n}{k}.

If
\lambda\vdash n
and
\ell(\lambda)\leq k
, then the number of partitions can be bounded by the (unsorted) number of weak compositions of
n
into
k
parts, given by
\binom{n+k-1}{k-1}
and so

(2.4)

\#\{\lambda\vdash n\,:\,\ell(\lambda)\leq k\}\ \leq\ \binom{n+k-1}{k-1}\ =\ O(n^{k-1}),

where the last equality holds when
k
is fixed and
n\to\infty
.

For a partition
\gamma\subset\lambda
with
\ell(\lambda)=k
and
\lambda\vdash n
, then
\gamma
can be viewed as a lattice path inside the
k\times(n-k+1)
rectangle bounding
\lambda
. This gives:

\#\{\gamma\,:\,\gamma\subseteq\lambda\}\ \leq\ \binom{n-k+1}{k}.

For
\lambda\vdash n
and
d(\lambda)\leq k
, we have then:

(2.5)

\#\{\gamma\,:\,\gamma\subseteq\lambda\}\ \leq\ (n/2)^{2k}.

This follows by considering
\gamma
as a NE lattice path from the lower left corner of
\lambda
at
(0,-\ell(\lambda))
, through a point on the diagonal
(i,-i)
with
i\in[1,k]
, to
(\lambda_{1},0)
. For
\ell(\lambda)=\ell
, then
\lambda_{1}\leq n+1-\ell
, and the total lattice path count gives

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{\ell}{i}\binom{n+1-\ell}{i}\ \leq\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{i}\binom{\lceil n/2\rceil}{i}\ \leq\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{(n/2)^{2i}}{(i!)^{2}}\ \leq\ (n/2)^{2k}.

Here the first inequality follows from (
2.2
), and the last inequality is easily seen by induction on
k
.

2.6.

Recall the

The Kronecker coefficients can be equivalently defined as follows:

(2.8)

s_{\lambda}[xy]\,:=\,\sum_{\mu,\nu\vdash n}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\hskip 0.85355pts_{\mu}(x)\hskip 0.85355pts_{\nu}(y)\quad\text{for all}\quad\lambda\vdash n\hskip 0.85355pt,

where
[xy]:=(x_{1}y_{1},x_{1}y_{2},\ldots,x_{i}y_{j},\ldots)
denote all pairwise products of variables. The last identity can be written as the
tripple Cauchy identity
given by

(2.9)

\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)s_{\lambda}(x)s_{\mu}(y)s_{\nu}(z)=\prod_{i,j,k}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}z_{k}}.

We will also need
Littlewood’s identity

[
Lit58
]
:

(2.10)

s_{\lambda}*(s_{\alpha}s_{\beta})\,=\,\sum_{\theta\vdash|\alpha|,\eta\vdash|\beta|}\hskip 1.70709ptc^{\lambda}_{\theta\eta}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(s_{\alpha}*s_{\theta}\big)\big(s_{\beta}*s_{\eta}\big)

where
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
denote the
Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
, and “
*
” denotes
the
Kronecker product of symmetric functions
:

s_{\mu}\cdot s_{\nu}\,=\,\sum_{\lambda\vdash|\mu|+|\nu|}\hskip 1.70709ptc^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\hskip 1.70709pts_{\lambda}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{\mu}*s_{\nu}\,=\,\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\hskip 1.70709pts_{\lambda}\,,\quad\text{for all}\quad\mu,\hskip 0.85355pt\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\vdash n\hskip 0.85355pt.

3.
Bounds on Kostka numbers and Littlewood–Richardson coefficients

In this section, we obtain bounds on the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
in terms of Durfee square size of partitions. We begin with the
skew Kostka numbers
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew shape,
|\lambda/\mu|=m
, and let
\alpha\vdash m
.
Suppose
d(\lambda)\leq k
and
\ell(\alpha)\leq r
. Then:

K_{\lambda/\mu,\alpha}\,\leq\,2^{2r}\left(\frac{m}{r}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k}{2}\right)^{r(k-1)}.

Proof.

Recall the
Pieri rule
for
s_{\mu}\cdot h_{a}
. We have:

K_{\lambda/\mu,\alpha}\ =\ \#\big\{\mu\hskip 1.70709pt\subset\hskip 1.70709pt\lambda^{(1)}\subset\hskip 1.70709pt\ldots\hskip 1.70709pt\subset\hskip 1.70709pt\lambda^{(r)}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\lambda\big\},

where the set has sequences of partitions
\lambda^{(1)},\ldots,\lambda^{(r)}
, such that
\lambda^{(i)}/\lambda^{(i-1)}
is a horizontal strip of size
\alpha_{i}
, which can be zero if
\ell(\alpha)<r
.
Such a strip can have at most
k
rows of total size
k
, which are below the diagonal in our English notation, and then at most
k
rows of total size at most
\alpha_{i}
. The first number is bounded by
2^{k}
and the second number is bounded by the number of weak compositions of
\alpha_{i}
into
k
parts, i.e.
\binom{\alpha_{i}+k-1}{k-1}
.

Overall, we have:

K_{\lambda/\mu,\alpha}\,\leq\,2^{rk}\,\prod_{i=1}^{r}\hskip 1.70709pt\binom{\alpha_{i}+k-1}{k-1}\,\leq\,\frac{2^{rk}}{((k-1)!)^{r}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left[\hskip 1.70709pt(\alpha_{i}+1)\cdots(\alpha_{i}+k-1)\right].

Applying the AM–GM inequality as (
2.1
) to each product term at the end we get the bound
\left(\alpha_{i}+\frac{k}{2}\right)^{r}
. Another application of AM–GM gives

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(\alpha_{i}+\frac{k}{2}\right)\leq\left(\frac{rk/2+\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}}{r}\right)^{r}=\left(\frac{m}{r}+\frac{k}{2}\right)^{r(k-1)}

for the big product. Since
2^{k-2}\leq(k-1)!
, the first factor is bounded by
2^{2r}
. Putting it all together we obtain the result.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
,
\mu\vdash n-m
and
\nu\vdash m
, such that
\ell(\lambda)\hskip 0.85355pt\leq\hskip 0.85355ptk
. Then:

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\,\leq\,\left(\frac{2\hskip 0.85355ptm}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k+1}{3}\right)^{\binom{k}{2}}

Proof.

Recall that
c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
is equal to the number of Littlewood–Richardson tableaux of shape

\lambda/\mu
and type
\nu
. These are characterized by having only
1
’s in the first row,
only
1
’s and
2
’s in the second row, etc. Thus, we have:

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\ \leq\ \tbinom{\lambda_{2}-\mu_{2}+1}{1}\hskip 1.70709pt\tbinom{\lambda_{3}-\mu_{3}+2}{2}\hskip 1.70709pt\cdots\hskip 1.70709pt\tbinom{\lambda_{k}-\mu_{k}+k-1}{k-1}\hskip 0.85355pt.

By the AM–GM applied to each product term in the numerators of the binomial coefficients, the right-hand side is bounded by

\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}i!}\left(\tfrac{\sum_{i=2}^{k}(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i})(i-1)\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\binom{k+1}{3}}{\binom{k}{2}}\right)^{\binom{k}{2}}\ \leq\ \left(\frac{2\hskip 0.85355ptm}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k+1}{3}\right)^{\binom{k}{2}},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma
3.2
is slightly sharper than the upper bound
in Theorem 4.14 of
[
PPY19
]
. Both upper bounds are the same asymptotically
when the length
k
is fixed and
n\to\infty
, and match
the lower bound in the same theorem. The proof of the lemma
is concise and very different from that in
[
PPY19
]
,
so we included it for completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
such that
d(\lambda)\leq k
. Then for every
\mu,\nu\subseteq\lambda
with
|\mu|+|\nu|=n
, we have:

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\,\leq\,\left(\frac{n}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709ptk\right)^{2k^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\nu=\alpha\cup\beta^{\prime}
, where
\alpha=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k})
and
\beta^{\prime}=(\nu_{k+1},\ldots)
.
Since
d(\nu)\leq d(\lambda)\leq k
, we have
\ell(\beta)\leq k
.
Then we have:

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\,=\,\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}\hskip 0.85355pts_{\nu}\rangle\,\leq\,\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}\hskip 0.85355pth_{\alpha}\hskip 0.85355pte_{\beta}\rangle\hskip 0.85355pt.

By definition, we have

s_{\mu}\hskip 1.70709pth_{\alpha}\,=\,\sum_{\gamma}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\gamma/\mu,\alpha}\,s_{\gamma}

Multiplying by
e_{\beta}
, expanding in the Schur basis and extracting the
s_{\lambda}
term, we get:

\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}\hskip 0.85355pth_{\alpha}\hskip 0.85355pte_{\beta}\rangle\,=\,\sum_{\gamma}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\gamma/\mu,\alpha}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\lambda^{\prime}/\gamma^{\prime},\beta}\hskip 1.70709pt.

Applying the bounds from Lemma
3.1
and using the fact that
\ell(\alpha),\ell(\beta)\leq k
, we obtain

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\,\leq\,\sum_{\gamma\vdash m}\hskip 1.70709pt2^{4k}\hskip 1.70709pt\left(\frac{m}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k}{2}\right)^{k(k-1)}\left(\frac{n-m}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k}{2}\right)^{k(k-1)},\quad\text{where \ \ $m\hskip 1.70709pt:=\hskip 1.70709pt|\alpha|$.}

Observe that the number of partitions
\gamma\subset\lambda
is bounded above by
(n/2)^{2k}
from inequality (
2.5
).
Note also that, by the AM–GM inequality, we have:

\left(\frac{m}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k}{2}\right)\left(\frac{n-m}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k}{2}\right)\ \leq\ \frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{n}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709ptk\right)^{2}.

Putting everything together, after some cancellations we obtain:

c^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}\ \leq\ (n/2)^{2k}\,\frac{2^{4k}}{4^{k}}\hskip 1.70709pt\left(\frac{n}{k}+k\right)^{2k(k-1)}\ \leq\ \left(\frac{n}{k}+k\right)^{2k^{2}},

as desired.
∎

In this section we prove Theorem

4.1.
Upper bounds

We approach the problem gradually, starting from the case where two partitions
have bounded number of rows (Lemma
4.2
),
then just one (Lemma

Before we proceed with those, we need to add a Lemma complementing the results in

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n
and
\ell(\lambda)\leq a
,
\ell(\mu)\leq b
,
\ell(\nu)\leq c
. Then

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu^{\prime})\leq 2^{abc}
.

Proof.

From the triple Cauchy identity (
2.9
), applying the involution
\omega
on the symmetric functions in
z
we get that
\omega(s_{\nu}(z))=s_{\nu^{\prime}}(z)
on the left-hand side, so

\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}g(\lambda,\mu,\nu^{\prime})s_{\lambda}(x)s_{\mu}(y)s_{\nu}(z)=\prod_{i,j,k}(1+x_{i}y_{j}z_{k}).

Expanding both sides in monomials in
x,y,z
and taking the coefficient at
x^{\lambda}y^{\mu}z^{\nu}
on both sides we get

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu^{\prime})\leq[x^{\lambda}y^{\mu}z^{\nu}]\prod_{i,j,k=1}^{a,b,c}(1+x_{i}y_{j}z_{k})

as only the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},y_{1},\ldots,y_{b},z_{1},\ldots,z_{c}
can appear. The right-hand side is the generating function of
a\times b\times c
binary arrays
B
, where for each coordinate
(i,j,k)
we have a multiplicative weight
(x_{i}y_{j}z_{k})^{B_{ijk}}
.
Thus, the coefficient on the right-hand side above is bounded by the total number of such binary arrays, which is
2^{abc}
. ∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n
, such that
\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu)\leq k
and

Then

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\,\leq\,2^{k^{3}}n^{k^{3}+k^{2}+3k}.

Proof.

Let
\nu=\alpha\cup\beta^{\prime}

\alpha=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k})
and
\beta^{\prime}=(\nu_{k+1},\ldots)
.
Again, since
d(\nu)\leq d(\lambda)=k

\ell(\beta)\leq k

Let
m=|\alpha|
, so
n-m=|\beta|

Since
s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}=s_{\nu}+\cdots
is a Schur positive sum containing
s_{\nu}
, we have:

(4.1)

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\,=\,\langle s_{\lambda}*s_{\nu},s_{\nu}\rangle\,\leq\,\big\langle s_{\lambda}*(s_{\alpha}s_{\beta^{\prime}}),s_{\mu}\big\rangle.

Applying Littlewood’s identity (
2.10
), we get:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\big\langle s_{\lambda}*(s_{\alpha}s_{\beta^{\prime}}),s_{\mu}\big\rangle\ =\ \sum_{\theta,\eta,\xi,\gamma}\hskip 1.70709ptc^{\lambda}_{\theta\eta}\hskip 1.70709ptc^{\mu}_{\gamma\xi}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\theta,\alpha,\gamma)\hskip 1.70709ptg(\eta,\beta^{\prime},\xi)\hskip 1.70709pt.

Since
c^{\mu}_{\gamma\xi}>0

\gamma,\xi\subset\mu

c^{\lambda}_{\theta,\eta}>0

\theta,\eta\subset\lambda

it follows that all partitions in the right-hand side above have length at most

We now apply previous results to estimate the right-hand side of (

By Corollary

g(\theta,\alpha,\gamma)\,\leq\,m^{k^{3}}.

For the term

\ell(\eta),\ell(\xi)\leq k

g(\eta,\beta^{\prime},\xi)\neq 0

[
Reg80

\ell(\beta^{\prime})\leq k^{2}

Applying Lemma
3.2

Indeed, denote by

. Then for the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients in (

Therefore, equations (

\displaystyle g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\ \leq\ \sum_{\theta,\eta,\xi,\gamma}\hskip 1.70709pt\left(\frac{2r}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k+1}{3}\right)^{2\binom{k}{2}}\hskip 1.70709pt2^{k^{3}}\hskip 1.70709ptm^{k^{3}}

The sum above is over

of sizes

,
n-m
,
and over

of sizes

The number of such pairs can be bound following (

\binom{m+k-1}{k-1}\binom{n-m+k-1}{k-1}\leq_{\eqref{eq:binom_concave}}\binom{n/2+k-1}{k-1}^{2}

\displaystyle g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\ \leq\ \binom{n/2+k-1}{k-1}^{4}\left(\frac{2r}{k}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709pt\frac{k+1}{3}\right)^{2\binom{k}{2}}2^{k^{3}}m^{k^{3}}\ \leq\,2^{k^{3}}n^{k^{3}+k^{2}+3k}\ \leq\ C_{k}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{(k+1)^{3}},

∎

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n

As before, let

\alpha=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})

we have:

(4.3)

g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\,=\,\langle s_{\lambda}*s_{\mu},s_{\nu}\rangle\,\leq\,\big\langle s_{\lambda}*(s_{\alpha}\hskip 0.85355pts_{\beta^{\prime}}),s_{\nu}\big\rangle.

Applying Littlewood’s identity (

(4.4)

\big\langle s_{\lambda}*(s_{\alpha}\hskip 0.85355pts_{\beta^{\prime}}),s_{\nu}\big\rangle\ =\ \sum_{\theta,\eta,\gamma,\xi}\hskip 1.70709ptc^{\lambda}_{\theta\eta}\hskip 1.70709ptc^{\nu}_{\gamma\xi}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\theta,\alpha,\gamma)\hskip 1.70709ptg(\eta,\beta^{\prime},\xi)\hskip 0.85355pt.

On the other hand, since we only select partitions

\displaystyle g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{1}{k^{2k^{2}}}\,n^{2k^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt2k^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt6k\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt6k\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt2k^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt\binom{k}{2}}\ =\ \frac{1}{k^{2k^{2}}}\,n^{2k^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt\frac{9}{2}k^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt\frac{23}{2}k},

∎

4.2.

on both sides, where

are

(4.5)

\displaystyle[x_{1}^{a}\ldots x_{k}^{a}\hskip 1.70709pty_{1}^{a}\ldots y_{k}^{a}]\hskip 0.85355pth_{a^{k}}[xy]\ =\ \sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n}\,g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\lambda,a^{k}}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\mu,a^{k}}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\nu,a^{k}}\hskip 1.70709pt.

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\in\hskip 0.85355pt\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\lambda,a^{k}}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\mu,a^{k}}\hskip 1.70709ptK_{\nu,a^{k}}\ \leq\ a^{3k^{2}-3k}\hskip 1.70709pt\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\in\hskip 0.85355pt\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\ \big|\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)\big|^{3}\hskip 1.70709pta^{3k^{2}-3k}\hskip 1.70709pt\max_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\in\hskip 0.85355pt\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)}\hskip 0.85355ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\ \leq\ a^{3k^{2}-3}\hskip 1.70709pt\max_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\in\hskip 0.85355pt\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)}\hskip 0.85355ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu),

where
\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)=\bigl\{\lambda\vdash n\hskip 1.70709pt:\hskip 1.70709pt\ell(\lambda)\leq k\bigr\}
,
so that
|\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)|=O\big(n^{k-1}\big)
. Comparing the inequalities
from (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain

\max_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\hskip 0.85355pt\in\hskip 0.85355pt\mathcal{P}_{k}(n)}\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\,\geq\,G_{k}\hskip 1.70709pta^{k^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt3k\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt3k^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt3},

as desired. ∎

5.
Kronecker bounds via the monotonicity property

5.1.
Bounds for the symmetric Kronecker problem

For all
n,k\geq 1
, define

\displaystyle\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k)

\displaystyle:=\,\max\hskip 1.70709pt\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)\ :\ \lambda\vdash n,\ \ell(\lambda)\leq k\hskip 0.85355pt\big\},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n,k)

\displaystyle:=\,\max\hskip 1.70709pt\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)\ :\ \lambda\vdash n,\ \lambda=\lambda^{\prime},\ d(\lambda)\leq k\hskip 0.85355pt\big\}.

Clearly,
\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k)\leq\textup{{{K}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n)
and
\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n,k)\leq\textup{{{K}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
, we have:

(5.1)

\textup{{{K}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(3n)\,\geq\,\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)\quad\text{and}\quad\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(3n,k)\geq\textup{{\rm{A}}}(n,k)\hskip 0.85355pt.

Proof.

Let
g(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)
, for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\vdash n
.
Let
\lambda:=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)\vdash 3n
. By the symmetry property (
2.6
) and
monotonicity property (Theorem
2.1
),
we have:

\displaystyle\textup{{{K}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(3n)

\displaystyle\geq\,g(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)\,=\,g\big(\alpha+\beta+\gamma,\hskip 1.70709pt\beta+\gamma+\alpha,\hskip 1.70709pt\gamma+\alpha+\beta\big)

\displaystyle\geq\,\max\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),\,g(\beta+\gamma,\hskip 1.70709pt\gamma+\alpha,\hskip 1.70709pt\alpha+\beta)\hskip 0.85355pt\big\}

\displaystyle\geq\,\max\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),\,g(\beta,\gamma,\alpha),\,g(\gamma,\alpha,\beta)\hskip 0.85355pt\big\}\,=\,\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n).

This proves the first inequality in (
5.1
). The second inequality follows
verbatim the argument above and the fact that
\ell(\lambda)=\max\{\ell(\alpha),\ell(\beta),\ell(\gamma)\}
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For all
k\geq 1
, there is a constant
C_{k}>0
, such that

(5.2)

\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k)\,\geq\,C_{k}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{k^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt3k^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt3k\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt3}\qquad\text{for all \ \ $n\geq 1$\hskip 0.85355pt.}

Proof.

Combining Theorem
1.5
and Lemma
5.1
, we obtain the
result for
3\hskip 0.85355pt|\hskip 0.85355ptn
. For general
n
, note that
g(\lambda+1,\mu+1,\nu+1)\geq g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
,
again by the monotonicity property. Thus, we have
\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n+1,k)\geq\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
n,k\geq 1
, we have:

(5.3)

\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(4n+k^{2},k)\,\geq\,\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k)\hskip 0.85355pt.

Proof.

Let
g(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\geq 1
, for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\vdash n
such that
\ell(\alpha),\hskip 0.85355pt\ell(\beta),\hskip 0.85355pt\ell(\gamma)\hskip 0.85355pt\leq k
.
Recall from the introduction that
g\big(\delta_{k},\delta_{k},\delta_{k}\big)\geq 1
,
where
\delta_{k}=(k^{k})
is the square shape.
By the repeated alternating application of the monotonicity property
(Theorem
2.1
) and symmetry/conjugation properties (
2.6
),
we have the following
long formula
:

\displaystyle g\big(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\big)

\displaystyle\leq\ g\big(\delta_{k}+\alpha,\delta_{k}+\beta,\delta_{k}+\gamma\big)\ =\ g\big(\delta_{k}\cup\alpha^{\prime},\delta_{k}\cup\beta^{\prime},\delta_{k}+\gamma\big)

\displaystyle\leq\ g\big((\delta_{k}+\beta)\cup\alpha^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\gamma)\cup\beta^{\prime},\delta_{k}+\gamma+\alpha\big)

\displaystyle=\ g\big((\delta_{k}+\beta)\cup\alpha^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\beta)\cup\gamma^{\prime},\delta_{k}\cup(\gamma+\alpha)^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\ g\big((\delta_{k}+\beta+\gamma)\cup\alpha^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\beta+\alpha)\cup\gamma^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\beta)\cup(\gamma+\alpha)^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle=\ g\big((\delta_{k}+\alpha)\cup(\beta+\gamma)^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\gamma)\cup(\beta+\alpha)^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\beta)\cup(\gamma+\alpha)^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\ g\big((\delta_{k}+\alpha+\beta)\cup(\beta+\gamma)^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\gamma+\alpha)\cup(\beta+\alpha)^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+\beta+\gamma)\cup(\gamma+\alpha)^{\prime}\big).

See Figure
1
below for an illustration.

Now, let
\alpha=\beta=\gamma
and suppose
g(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)=\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k)
. From above,

g\big(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha\big)\ \leq\ g\big((\delta_{k}+2\alpha)\cup(2\alpha)^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+2\alpha)\cup(2\alpha)^{\prime},(\delta_{k}+2\alpha)\cup(2\alpha)^{\prime}\big)\ =\ g(\mu,\mu,\mu),

where
\mu:=\hskip 0.85355pt\big(\delta_{k}+2\alpha\big)\cup(2\alpha)^{\prime}\hskip 0.85355pt\vdash\hskip 0.85355pt4n+k^{2}
.
Since
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
and
d(\mu)\leq k
, we conclude:

\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(4n+k^{2},k)\,\geq\,g(\mu,\mu,\mu)\,\geq\,g(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)\,=\,\textup{{\rm{A}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{s}}$}}(n,k),

as desired.
∎

5.3
.

Corollary 5.4
.

For all
k\geq 1
, there is a constant
D_{k}>0
, such that

(5.4)

\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n,k)\,\geq\,D_{k}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{k^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt18k^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355pt102k\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355pt182}\qquad\text{for all \ \ $n\geq 1$\hskip 0.85355pt.}

Proof.

Combining Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.3
, we have

(5.5)

\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(12n+k^{2},k)\,\geq\,\textup{{\rm{A}}}(n,k)\hskip 0.85355pt.

Now Theorem
1.5
implies the result for
12\hskip 0.85355pt|\hskip 0.85355pt(n-k^{2})
.

For general
n
, note that in the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we can use

\mu\hskip 1.70709pt:=\hskip 1.70709pt\big(\delta_{k+c}+2\alpha\big)\cup(2\alpha)^{\prime}\hskip 0.85355pt\vdash\hskip 0.85355pt4n+(k+c)^{2}\quad\text{for all \ $c\geq 1$ \ and \ $\ell(\alpha)\leq k$.}

We can also replace
\delta_{k+c}
with a chopped symmetric square by removing a symmetric partition of size
t\in\{0,1,3,4,\ldots,10,11,14\}
from its bottom right corner. Since
g(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)\geq 1
for all
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
, see
[
BB04
,
PPV16
]
, we can then repeat the steps in the proof of Lemma
5.3
with the chopped square instead of the
\delta_{k}
. This allows us to construct symmetric partitions
\mu
of any size modulo
12
, and note that
c=5
suffices.

We conclude that
g(\mu,\mu,\mu)\geq A(n,k)
for some
\mu\vdash 12n+(k+c)^{2}-t
with
k\leq d(\mu)\leq k+c
.
This implies that
\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n,k)\hskip 0.85355pt\geq\hskip 0.85355ptA\big(\lfloor(n-k^{2})/12\rfloor,k-5\big)
, and the bound follows.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

A curious Conjecture 5.12 in
[
BRR17
]
, claims that

g\big((\lambda+1)\cup 1,\hskip 0.85355pt(\mu+1)\cup 1,\hskip 0.85355pt(\nu+1)\cup 1\big)\,\geq\,g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\quad\text{for all}\ \hskip 1.70709pt\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n.

This would imply that
\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n+2,k)\geq\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(n,k)
and improve
the lower order terms in (
5.4
).

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
k=(2+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}
. Let us show that
for sufficiently large
n>N(\varepsilon)
, we have
\textup{{\rm{A}}}(n,k)=\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)
.
We follow
[
PPY19
]
in our presentation.

Recall that a sequence
\{\lambda\}
of partitions is called
Plancherel

if
f^{\lambda}=\sqrt{n!}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-O(\sqrt{n})}
. Suppose that
g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)=\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)
.
By Stanley’s theorem (
1.4
) and the dimension bound, we have:

\sqrt{n!}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-O(\sqrt{n})}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pt\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709ptf^{\lambda}\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt\sqrt{n!}

We conclude that all three sequences

\{\lambda\}
,
\{\mu\}
and
\{\nu\}
achieving the maximum
g(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
are
Plancherel. In fact, we can even fix two of these three sequences
(see
[
PPY19
, Thm 1.4]
).

Now, by the VKLS Theorem (see
[
PPY19
, Thm 1.3]
), all three sequences must
have
VKLS shape
. Without stating it explicitly, it follows from the
definition that

\ell(\lambda),\hskip 1.70709pt\ell(\mu),\hskip 1.70709pt\ell(\nu)\,\leq\,2\sqrt{n}\hskip 1.70709pt+\hskip 1.70709ptO(n^{1/6})\hskip 1.70709pt\leq\hskip 1.70709pt(2+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptk\hskip 0.85355pt,

for
n
large enough. Thus we have
\textup{{\rm{A}}}(n,k)=\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)
in that case.
By (
5.5
), we conclude:

\textup{{{K}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}\big((16+3\varepsilon)n\big)\,\geq\,\textup{{{K}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}(12n+k^{2})\,\geq\,\textup{{\rm{B}{\hskip-0.85355pt}${}^{\textit{fs}}$}}\big(12n+k^{2},k\big)\,\geq\,\textup{{\rm{A}}}(n,k)\,=\,\textup{{\rm{K}}}(n)\,=\,\sqrt{n!}\hskip 1.70709pte^{-O(\sqrt{n})}\hskip 0.85355pt,

for
k=(2+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n}
as above and
n
large enough.
Taking logs on both sides implies the result.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

We now use the iterated conjugation trick in the proof of Lemma
5.3

to give the first nontrivial lower bound for
g\big(\delta_{k},\delta_{k},\delta_{k}\big)
.

We start with
[
PP17
, Thm 1.2]
, which gives for
k=2s^{2}
that

g\big((2s)^{2s},(2s)^{2s},k^{2}\big)\,\geq\,C\hskip 1.70709pt2^{2s}\hskip 1.70709pt(2s)^{-9/2}>0

for some universal constant
C>0.004
.

Observe that by conjugating two partitions we get

g\big((2s)^{2s},(2s)^{2s},2^{k}\big)\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptg\big((2s)^{2s},(2s)^{2s},k^{2}\big)\hskip 1.70709pt>\hskip 1.70709pt0.

Let
s:=2^{r}
. We can repeatedly apply the combination of monotonicity and conjugation,
from
4m=k=2s^{2}
, to get

\displaystyle g\big((4m)^{4m},\hskip 0.85355pt(4m)^{4m},\hskip 0.85355pt(4m)^{4m}\big)\ \geq\ g\big((2m)^{4m},\hskip 0.85355pt(2m)^{4m},\hskip 0.85355pt(2m)^{4m}\big)\ =

\displaystyle=\ g\big((4m)^{2m},\hskip 0.85355pt(4m)^{2m},\hskip 0.85355pt(2m)^{4m}\big)\ \geq\ g\big((2m)^{2m},\hskip 0.85355pt(2m)^{2m},\hskip 0.85355pt(m)^{4m}\big)\ \geq\

\displaystyle\geq\ \ldots\ \geq\ g\big((2s)^{2s},(2s)^{2s},k^{2}\big)\ >\ C\hskip 1.70709pt2^{2s}\hskip 1.70709pt(2s)^{-9/2}.

Since
s=\sqrt{2k}=\sqrt{2}\hskip 1.70709ptn^{1/4}
, we obtain the first part of Theorem
1.8
.

For the caret shape
\tau_{k}
, note that

\tau_{k}\,=\,\big(\delta_{k+1}+2\hskip 0.85355pt\rho_{k}\big)\hskip 1.70709pt\cup\hskip 1.70709pt2\hskip 0.85355pt\rho_{k}\hskip 1.70709pt.

In notation of the proof of Lemma
5.3
, let
\alpha:=\rho_{k}
and recall from (
1.5
) that
g(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha)>0
. From the long formula in the proof, we have:

\displaystyle g\big(\tau_{k},\tau_{k},\tau_{k}\big)

\displaystyle=\ g\big((\delta_{k+1}+2\alpha)\cup 2\alpha,(\delta_{k+1}+2\alpha)\cup 2\alpha,(\delta_{k+1}+2\alpha)\cup 2\alpha\big)

\displaystyle\geq\ \max\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\alpha,\alpha,\alpha),\hskip 1.70709ptg(\delta_{k+1},\delta_{k+1},\delta_{k+1})\hskip 0.85355pt\big\}.

Now the second part of Theorem
1.8
follows from the first part. ∎

6.
Final remarks and open problems

6.1.

The importance of Durfee square size in connection with the
vanishing
of Kronecker coefficients
(i.e., whether they are nonzero),
has long been understood in the literature. We refer to

[
BB04
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
and
[
Dvir93
,
Reg80
]
for some notable examples.

6.2.

There are many special cases
of partitions with small Durfee square size (at most three),
where the Kronecker coefficients are computed exactly, see
e.g.
[
BvWZ10
,
BOR09
,
RW94
,
Tew15
]
. In all these cases the
Kronecker coefficients are bounded by a constant. This is in
sharp contrast with examples in
[
BV18
,
MRS21
]
and
our lower bound in Theorem
1.5
,
suggesting that being bounded is a small numbers phenomenon.

6.3.

Recall Murnaghan’s
stability property
: the sequence
(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)
defined as

a_{d}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pta_{d}(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\,:=\,g\big(\lambda+(d),\hskip 0.85355pt\mu+(d),\hskip 0.85355pt\nu+(d)\big)

is increasing and bounded. This phenomenon was recently
generalized by Stembridge

a_{d}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709pta_{d}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\hskip 1.70709pt\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\,:=\,g\big(\lambda\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355ptd\hskip 0.85355pt\alpha,\hskip 0.85355pt\mu\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355ptd\hskip 0.85355pt\beta,\nu\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355ptd\hskip 0.85355pt\gamma\big)\quad\text{for \ \, $g(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\hskip 0.85355pt=\hskip 0.85355pt1$. }

The nondecreasing of
\{a_{d}\}
follows from the monotonicity property (Theorem
2.1
),
while boundedness was proved by Sam and Snowden
[
SS16
]
.

It is known that
a_{d}
are a quasi-polynomial in
d

[
Man15
]
,
see also
[
BV18
,
Man16
]
.
In view of Corollary
1.2
and Theorem
1.5
,
it would be interesting to give a
combinatorial description of the degree of these quasi-polynomials.
Let us mention that
a_{d}\geq d+1
for all
g(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)>1

[
Ste14
, Prop. 3.2]
.

Similarly, one can consider more general families of Kronecker coefficients

b_{d}\hskip 1.70709pt=\hskip 1.70709ptb_{d}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\hskip 1.70709pt\alpha,\beta,\gamma;\hskip 1.70709pt\zeta,\xi,\eta)\,:=\,g\big((\lambda\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355ptd\hskip 0.85355pt\alpha)\cup(d\hskip 0.85355pt\zeta)^{\prime},\hskip 0.85355pt(\mu\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355ptd\hskip 0.85355pt\beta)\cup(d\hskip 0.85355pt\xi)^{\prime},\hskip 0.85355pt(\nu\hskip 0.85355pt+\hskip 0.85355ptd\hskip 0.85355pt\gamma)\cup(d\hskip 0.85355pt\eta)^{\prime}\big),

where
g(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),\hskip 0.85355ptg(\zeta,\xi,\eta)\geq 1
.
In view of Theorem
1.4
, we conjecture that
b_{d}
are again
quasi-polynomial in
d
, for
d
large enough. In a special case when

\alpha=\beta=\gamma=\zeta=\xi=\eta=1
, this is the
hook stability
introduced in
[
BRR17
]
.
Note that even characterizing
the cases when these quasi-polynomials are nonzero is quite challenging
(cf.
[
BRR17
,
\lx@sectionsign
6.2]
).

A more general approach to stability for Lie groups can be found in
[
Par19
]
.

6.4.

One can give explicit lower bounds on the constants
C_{k}
in (
1.3
). For that, in notation of the proof
of Theorem
1.5
, we need to use the
integral volume
G_{k}
of the

three-way transportation polytopes

T_{k}(1)
.
These polytopes are highly symmetric, so the lower bounds
are especially simple and can be found in
[
Ben14
]
,
see also
[
Bar17
]
for a survey. See also a recent explicit
lower bound in
[
BR07
, Ex. 2.6]
, on the (usual) volume of
T_{k}(1)
.
To put these bounds into context, recall the natural upper bound
used in
[
PP20
]
in this case. The main result in
[
Ben14
]

(and in greater generality in
[
Bar17
]
), is that
these upper bounds are asymptotically sharp.

6.5.

For the uniform random partitions
\lambda\vdash n
, we have
\ell(\lambda)=\Theta(\sqrt{n}\hskip 1.70709pt\log n)
and
d(\lambda)=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
,
see e.g.
[
DP19
]
and references therein. This implies that the
bounds in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are useful
only for partitions with relatively few rows and small Durfee square size,
respectfully.

6.6.

Define

\textup{{\rm{B}}}(n,k)\,:=\,\max\hskip 1.70709pt\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\ :\ \lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n\ \ \text{and}\ \ d(\lambda),d(\mu),d(\nu)\leq k\hskip 0.85355pt\big\}.

Comparing the bounds in Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.4
,
it would be natural to believe that the upper bound on
\textup{{\rm{B}}}(n,k)
in
(
1.2
) is closer to the truth than the lower bound in (
1.3
).

Conjecture 6.1
.

There is a universal constant
c>0
such that

\textup{{\rm{B}}}(n,k)\,\geq\,n^{4k^{3}\hskip 0.85355pt-\hskip 0.85355ptck^{2}}\quad\text{for all}\ \ n,\hskip 0.85355ptk\geq\hskip 0.85355pt1\hskip 0.85355pt.

6.7.

We believe that the Kronecker coefficients in Theorem
1.8
grow much
faster than our lower bounds suggest.
The following conjecture immediately implies Conjecture
1.6

improving upon Theorem
1.7
.

Conjecture 6.2
.

We have:

\displaystyle g(\rho_{k},\rho_{k},\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=\,\sqrt{n!}\,e^{-O(n)}\quad\text{where}\quad n=\tbinom{k}{2},\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle g(\delta_{\ell},\hskip 0.85355pt\delta_{\ell},\hskip 0.85355pt\delta_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\,\sqrt{n!}\,e^{-O(n)}\quad\text{where}\quad n=\ell^{2}.

6.8.

Let
n=\binom{k}{2}
. Define

\textup{{\rm{F}}}(n)\,:=\,\max\hskip 1.70709pt\big\{\hskip 0.85355ptg(\rho_{k}\hskip 0.85355pt,\hskip 0.85355pt\rho_{k}\hskip 0.85355pt,\lambda)\ :\ \lambda\vdash n\ \ \text{and}\ \ \lambda=\lambda^{\prime}\hskip 0.85355pt\big\}.

It would be interesting to find sharp lower bounds on
\textup{{\rm{F}}}(n)
.

It was shown in
[
PP17
,
\lx@sectionsign 4.2
]
, that
g(\rho_{k}\hskip 0.85355pt,\hskip 0.85355pt\rho_{k}\hskip 0.85355pt,\lambda)=e^{\Omega(\sqrt{n})}
for two-row partitions
\lambda=(n/2,n/2)
, where
n
is even. For self-conjugate
\lambda
,
it was only shown recently in
[
BBS21
,
\lx@sectionsign
5]
using
modular representation theory
,
that the
\textup{{\rm{F}}}(n)
is unbounded.

Combined with the lower bound for Littlewood–Richardson coefficients given in
[
PPY19
, Thm 1.5]
,
Theorem 5.11 in
[
BBS21
]
implies
that
\textup{{\rm{F}}}(n)=e^{\Omega(\sqrt{n})}
. This is nowhere close to Conjecture
6.2
, but
gives us a hope that there might be more tools to be discovered.

6.9.

In
[
MPP20
]
, there is a tight asymptotic bound

g\big(\delta_{2s},\delta_{2s},(n-k,k)\big)\hskip 0.85355pt=\hskip 0.85355pt\Theta\big(2^{\sqrt{2k}}/k^{3/2}\big)
in the case
when
k/n\in(0,1/2)
. However, this bound cannot be applied when
k=n/2+o(n)
,
so the bound from
[
PP17
]
is still the best known lower bound in this case.

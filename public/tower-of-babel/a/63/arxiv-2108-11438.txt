The canonical bijection between pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams
Yibo Gao, Daoji Huang

Abstract
. We present a direct bijection between reduced pipe dreams and reduced bumpless pipe dreams by interpreting reduced compatible sequences on bumpless pipe dreams and show that this bijection preserves Monk’s formula, establishing its canonical nature.



1.
Introduction

Schubert polynomials,
\{\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}\>|\>\pi\in S_{n}\}
, defined by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
9
]
, are polynomial representatives of the Schubert classes of the full flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}_{n}
. They can be defined via the divided difference operators as follows:

\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}:=\begin{cases}x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{n-2}\cdots x_{n-1}&\text{ if }w=n\ n{-}1\ \cdots\ 1,\\
\partial_{i}\mathfrak{S}_{\pi s_{i}}&\text{ if }\ell(\pi)<\ell(\pi s_{i}),\end{cases}

where
s_{i}
is the simple transposition
(i\ i{+}1)
, which acts on polynomials by swapping the variables
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
, and

\partial_{i}f:=\frac{f-s_{i}f}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}.

Schubert polynomials and their various generalizations are the central objects of interest in Schubert calculus and algebraic combinatorics, and possess very rich combinatorial structures. The Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
’s expand positively into monomials. Notable combinatorial models for this result involve
compatible sequences
by Billey-Jockusch-Stanley
[
3
]
, which are equivalent to
pipe dreams
(PDs), also called
RC-graphs
, by Bergeron-Billey
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
,
3
]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation, then
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}=\sum_{D\in\operatorname{PD}(\pi)}\operatorname{wt}(D).

We will introduce the necessary notations in Section
2
.

Relatively recently, Lam, Lee and Shimozono
[
8
]
introduced
bumpless pipe dreams
(BPDs), in the context of back stable Schubert calculus, that provide another monomial expansion of the Schubert polynomials.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation, then
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}=\sum_{D\in\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)}\operatorname{wt}(D).

Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
guarantee that there exists a weight-preserving bijection between pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams of a fixed permutation. The following question has been of interest since the discovery of bumpless pipe dreams.

Question 1.3
.

Can we explicitly describe a “natural” weight-preserving bijection between
\operatorname{PD}(\pi)
and
\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
for any permutation
\pi
?

The goal of this paper is to answer the above question affirmatively, by providing such a bijection
\varphi
(Definition
3.3
). We then show that our bijection is the “correct” bijection in a precise sense by proving that
\varphi
preserves Monk’s rule (Theorem
4.5
).

One notable consequence of Theorem
4.5
is that it unifies all attempts to define bijections between pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams via any choices of Monk’s rules. Specifically, the second author mentioned a family of bijections
[
5
]
between pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams defined using (co)transition formulas which are special cases of Monk’s rule. Each bijection in this family depends on a choice
\alpha
for each permutation
\pi
upon which the (co)transition is performed. By Theorem
4.5
, we now know that this family of bijections is in fact one single bijection (Definition
3.3
).

For the organization of this paper, we first introduce background knowledge in Section
2
. Then we describe the bijection in Section
3
. Finally, we show that the bijection preserves Monk’s rule in Section
4
, which is divided into many subsections due to the technicality the proof of our main theorem, Theorem
4.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
S_{\infty}
be the infinite symmetric group that consists of bijections from
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
to itself with all but finitely many fixed points. Namely,
S_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}S_{n}
. The group
S_{\infty}
is generated by the simple transpositions
s_{i}:=(i\ i{+}1)
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. For
a<b
, let
t_{a,b}
be the transposition
(a\ b)
. Let
\ell(w)
be the standard Coxeter length of
w
and let
\operatorname{Red}(w)
be the set of reduced words of
w
.

The Bruhat order on
S_{\infty}
is the transitive closure of the binary relation
w<wt_{a,b}
if
\ell(w)<\ell(wt_{a,b})
. The cover relation in the Bruhat order is given by
x\lessdot y
if
x=yt_{a,b}
for some
a<b
and
\ell(x)=\ell(y)-1
. Also, let
\operatorname{Des}_{L}(\pi):=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\>|\>s_{i}\pi\lessdot\pi\}
be the set of (left) descents of
\pi
.

The stability property of the Schubert polynomials allows us to work with permutations
\pi\in S_{\infty}
, instead of each symmetric group
S_{n}
separately. Recall that the stability property states that for any
m<n
,
\pi\in S_{m}
, we have
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}=\mathfrak{S}_{\iota(\pi)}
, where
\iota:S_{m}\hookrightarrow S_{n}
is the natural embedding.

2.1.
Pipe dreams and compatible sequences

Definition 2.1

(Pipe dream, RC-graph
[
1
]
)
.

For a permutation
\pi\in S_{\infty}
, a (reduced)
pipe dream

D
of
\pi
is a tiling of the square grid
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
using two kinds of tiles, the cross-tile
and the elbow-tile
, with finitely many
-tiles, forming pipes that travel from the north border to the west border, such that for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the pipe starting at column
i
, which is labeled as pipe
i
, ends at row
\pi(i)
, and no two pipes cross twice.

We typically draw a pipe dream in a staircase shape of size
n
if
\pi\in S_{n}
. Let
\operatorname{PD}(\pi)
be the set of pipe dreams of
\pi
, and for
D\in\operatorname{PD}(\pi)
, let
\operatorname{cross}(D)
denote the coordinates of its
-tiles. Then the
weight
of
D
is defined as

\operatorname{wt}(D):=\prod_{(i,j)\in\operatorname{cross}(D)}x_{i}.

Given a pipe dream
D
, we let
\operatorname{perm}(D)
denote the permutation given by
D
.

Definition 2.2

(Compatible sequence
[
3
]
)
.

For a permutation
\pi\in S_{\infty}
with
\ell(\pi)=\ell
, a tuple of integer sequences
\big(\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}),\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{\ell})\big)
is a (reduced)
compatible sequence
of
\pi
if

(1)

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell})
is a reduced word of
\pi
,

(2)

r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{\ell}
is weakly increasing,

(3)

r_{j}\leq a_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
,

(4)

r_{j}<r_{j+1}
if
a_{j}<a_{j+1}
.

There is a straightforward bijection between pipe dreams of
\pi
and compatible sequences of
\pi
, which we describe here.

We label the square grids with matrix notation and give a total order to the square grids, by going from top to bottom, and within each row, right to left. To be precise,
(i,j)<(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
if
i<i^{\prime}
or
i=i^{\prime},j>j^{\prime}
. For
D\in\operatorname{PD}(\pi)
with
\ell(\pi)=\ell
, order its
-tiles
\operatorname{cross}(D)
in this way as
(r_{1},j_{1})<\cdots<(r_{\ell},j_{\ell})
. Then its corresponding compatible sequence is
\big(\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}),\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{\ell})\big)
where
a_{k}=r_{k}+j_{k}-1
. Note that each
-tile at coordinate
(i,j)
corresponds to the simple transposition
s_{i+j-1}
. See
[
1
]
for further details on this bijection.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
D\in\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
,
\varphi(D)
is a compatible sequence of
\pi
.

Proof.

We check the conditions of compatible sequences for
\varphi(D)
as in Definition
2.2
. Condition (1) follows from
\nabla D\in\operatorname{BPD}(s_{a}\pi)
if
\operatorname{pop}(D)=(a,r)
. Condition (2) is evident from construction, as we are removing
-tiles from top to bottom. Note that if
\operatorname{pop}(D)=(a,r)
, with
D\in\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
, there are no
-tiles in the first

, meaning that
\pi(i)=i
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
, and consequently

. Thus, condition (3) follows. For condition (4), we assume the opposite that
r_{j}\geq r_{j+1}
, i.e.
r_{j}=r_{j+1}
and
a_{j}<a_{j+1}
. Since
a_{j}<a_{j+1}
, by Lemma 3.2 of
[
6
]
, the footprints of
\nabla^{j-1}D
are strictly to the S/W/SW direction of the footprints of
\nabla^{j}D
. However, the starting point of the footprints of
\nabla^{j-1}D
is directly east of the footprints of
\nabla^{j}D
as we start in the same row
r_{j}=r_{j+1}
. Contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

The map
\varphi
in Definition
3.3
is a weight-preserving bijection between
\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
and
\operatorname{PD}(\pi)
, i.e. compatible sequences of
\pi
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, we have a well-defined map
\varphi
from
\operatorname{BPD}(\pi)
to
\operatorname{PD}(\pi)
. Immediate from the construction (Definition

),
\varphi
is weight-preserving. Since each step

is invertible if a preimage exists, the map
\varphi
is injective. As both pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams enumerate the Schubert polynomial

,
\varphi
is a bijection.
∎

4.
Preserving Monk’s rule

In this section, we demonstrate why the map
\varphi
in Definition
3.3
is “canonical”, by showing that it preserves Monk’s rule.

Theorem 4.1

(Monk’s rule)
.

Let
\pi
be a permutation and
\alpha
be a positive integer,

(1)

x_{\alpha}\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s<\alpha\\
\pi t_{s,\alpha}\gtrdot\pi\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{\pi t_{s,\alpha}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l>\alpha\\
\pi t_{\alpha,l}\gtrdot\pi\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{\pi t_{\alpha,l}}.

This version of Monk’s rule is derived from the Monk’s rule that expands the product of a linear Schubert polynomial with a general Schubert polynomial. In the cases when the summation on the right-hand of Equation (
1
) is a single Schubert polynomial, the formula specializes to the
transition formula
of Schubert polynomials, which is an important inductive formula for Schubert polynomials. In the case when the summation on the left-hand side is empty, the formula specializes to the
cotransition formula

[
7
]
. In
[
2
]
, a bijective proof of the transition formula is given using pipe dreams, which can be easily extended to a proof of Monk’s rule in its general form. The second author gives a bijective proof of Monk’s rule with bumpless pipe dreams in
[
5
]
, which also generalizes to the equivariant version. It is also remarked in
[
5
]
that using either the transition or the cotransition formula, one can construct bijections inductively of pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams. As a corollary of our result, these inductive bijections agree. We review both weight-preserving bijections for Equation (
1
) on pipe dreams and bumpless pipe dreams in Section
4.1
and Section
4.2
.

We present the following maps

\begin{cases}x_{\alpha}{\rightsquigarrow}:&\operatorname{PD}(\pi)\rightarrow\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l>\alpha\\

m_{s,\beta}:&\operatorname{PD}(\pi t_{s,\beta})\rightarrow\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}l>\beta\\
\pi t_{\beta,l}\gtrdot\pi\end{subarray}}\operatorname{PD}(\pi t_{\beta,l}),\ s<\beta,\pi t_{s,\beta}\gtrdot\pi\end{cases}

such that

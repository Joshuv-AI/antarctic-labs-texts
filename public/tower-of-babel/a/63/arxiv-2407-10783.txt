Kummer theory for function fields
Félix Baril Boudreau and Antonella Perucca

Abstract
. We develop Kummer theory for algebraic function fields in finitely many transcendental variables. We consider any finitely generated Kummer extension (possibly, over a cyclotomic extension) of an algebraic function field, and describe the structure of its Galois group. Our results show in a precise sense how the questions of computing the degrees of these extensions and of computing the group structures of their Galois groups reduce to the corresponding questions for the Kummer extensions of their constant fields.

1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let
K
be a field containing a primitive
n
-th root of unity. A Kummer extension of
K
is a field extension obtained by adjoining a finite number of
n
-th roots of elements of
K
. This extension is Abelian of exponent dividing
n
. Conversely, Kummer theory says that any finite Abelian extension of
K
of exponent dividing
n
can be obtained by taking the
n
-th roots of finitely many elements of
K^{\times}
, the multiplicative subgroup of
K
. Moreover, Abelian extensions of
K
of exponent dividing
n
can then be seen to correspond bijectively with subgroups of the quotient group
K^{\times}/K^{\times n}
. In this work, we develop Kummer theory for arbitrary algebraic function fields and go beyond the classical theory: Using parameters that measure the divisibility of elements up to constants, we completely solve the problem of understanding the nature of Kummer theory over algebraic function fields by showing in a precise way that this theory reduces to the Kummer theory over the corresponding constant fields.

We consider a function field
K/k
and suppose without loss of generality that
k
is algebraically closed in
K
. For any element

\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus k^{\times}
, and any prime number
\ell\neq\mathrm{char}(k)
, we can speak of its
\ell
-divisibility parameter modulo constants over
K
. This is the largest non-negative integer
D
such that
\alpha\in kK^{\times\ell^{D}}
.

For an integer
M\geq 1
not divisible by
\mathrm{char}(k)
, and a choice
\zeta_{M}
of primitive
M
-th root of unity in a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
, the field
K(\zeta_{M})
is a cyclotomic extension of
K
. Given a finitely generated subgroup
G
of
K^{\times}
, we consider the Kummer extensions

(1)

K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[N]{G})/K(\zeta_{M})\qquad\text{where $N\mid M$}\,.

In this article we show that, to compute the degree and the structure of the Galois group of the above Kummer extensions, we may reduce to computations involving only the constant field
k
.

By Kummer theory, we may suppose that
N=\ell^{n}
for some prime number
\ell
and some integer
n\geq 1
.
We can write
G=G_{0}G^{\prime}
where
G_{0}:=G\cap k^{\times}
and where
G^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
G
such that
G^{\prime}\cap k^{\times}=\{1\}
. We denote by
r
the rank of
G^{\prime}
and we suppose that
r
is strictly positive (for
r=0
, see Remark
37
). A careful choice of basis for
G^{\prime}
, which we call
\ell
-
good basis modulo constants
(see Definition
28
), allows us to conduct a finer analysis of Galois group structures of Kummer extensions. The following result, which combines Corollary
27
and Theorem
31
, considers the so-called
geometric Kummer extension

K\bar{k}(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G})/K\bar{k}
:

Theorem 1
.

There is a basis
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
of
G^{\prime}
such that, calling
D_{i}
the
\ell
-divisibility parameter modulo constants of
\alpha_{i}
over
K(\zeta_{\ell})
, the integer
\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}
is maximal (by varying the basis of
G^{\prime}
). The multiset of the
D_{i}
’s is uniquely determined (it only depends on
K/k
and
G
), and for every
n\geq 1
we have

\Gal(K\bar{k}(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G})/K\bar{k})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\max(n-D_{i},0)}\mathbb{Z}\,.

To simplify the notation, suppose that
\zeta_{M}\in k
. We deduce the following result on the cardinality of the Kummer extensions (see Theorem
33
):

Theorem 2
.

With the above notation, let
c_{i}\in k
be such that
\alpha_{i}\equiv c_{i}\bmod K^{\times\ell^{D_{i}}}
. Then we have

[K(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G}):K]=[k,\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G_{0}},\sqrt[\ell^{\min(n,D_{1})}]{c_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[\ell^{\min(n,D_{r})}]{c_{r}}):k]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ell^{\max(n-D_{i},0)}\,.

Up to relabelling, we suppose without loss of generality that
D_{1}\leq D_{2}\leq\cdots\leq D_{r}
. Our main result gives the precise Galois group structure of the Kummer extension
K(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G})/K
(see Theorem
36
):

Theorem 3
.

With the above notation, let
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
be maximal such that the class of
c_{i}
is an
\ell^{d_{i}}
-th power in
k^{\times}/\langle G_{0},c_{j}:j>i\rangle
. Then there is a group isomorphism

\Gal\left(K\left(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G}\right)/K\right)\simeq\Gal\left(k\left(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G_{0}}\right)/k\right)\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\max(n-\min(D_{i},d_{i}),0)}\mathbb{Z}\,.

Thanks to the above results, to determine the cardinality and the structure of the Galois group of the Kummer extensions in (
1
), it suffices to compute the parameters
D_{i}
’s and the constant elements
c_{i}
’s, and this then reduces to computations involving only the constant field
k
. Such computations can be done in principle for finite fields, but there is also an explicit finite procedure to perform them for number fields and
p
-adic fields (see
[
1
,
4
]
).

The structure of the paper is as follows: Section
2
contains background theory on algebraic function fields and a lemma about rank
1
discrete valuations and
\ell
-divisibility modulo constants of elements. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to studying extensions of function fields (with some general results that can be of independent interest). The theory of divisibility modulo constants is developed in Section
5
, and our main results for Kummer theory are proven in Section
6
.

The idea of using parameters to consider the divisibility of elements in a given field comes from past works of the second-named author on Kummer theory for number fields. Nevertheless, we consider a completely new setting and we provide several auxiliary results to develop Kummer theory for function fields in full generality.

2.
Prerequisites on function fields and notation

An
algebraic function field (or function field, for short)
is a triple
(K,k,n)
, comprised of a field
K
containing a field
k
such that
K
is finitely generated over
k
of transcendence degree
\mathrm{trdeg}(K/k)
equal to some positive integer
n
. In this case, we write
K/k
(and can refer to it as a
function field in
n
variables
). Let
\{t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}\}
be a transcendence basis for
K
over
k
. The function field
K/k
is a
rational function field
if
K=k(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n})
and it is a
global function field
if
n=1
and
k
is a finite field.

In this manuscript, a
valuation on

K/k
is a surjective map
v
from
K
to
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
, with
\mathbb{Z}
the additive group of integers and
\infty
a symbol subject to the rules
n+\infty=\infty+n=\infty+\infty=\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The map
v
is satisfying for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in K^{\times}
the properties
v(f_{1}f_{2})=v(f_{1})+v(f_{2})
and
v(f_{1}+f_{2})\geq\min\{v(f_{1}),v(f_{2})\}
, is such that if
f\in k^{\times}
, then
v(f)=0
and
v(0)=\infty
. In particular, our valuations are
discrete
in the usual terminology.

The ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
of all
f\in K^{\times}
satisfying
v(f)\geq 0
, is called the
valuation ring
of
v
. It is a local ring whose maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
is the set of
f\in\mathcal{O}_{v}
satisfying
v(f)>0
. The field
k_{v}:=\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}
is called the
residue field
of
v
. It can be seen as a field extension of
k
and the transcendence degree
\mathrm{trdeg}(k_{v}/k)
of
k_{v}
over
k
will be the
dimension of

v

over

k
. A
prime divisor of

K/k
is a valuation of
K/k
of dimension
n-1
over
k
. Each prime divisor is a discrete valuation of
rank

1
(that is,
\mathcal{O}_{v}
has Krull dimension
1
) and
k_{v}
is a function field of transcendence degree
n-1
over
k
. We denote by
\mathrm{Prime}(K/k)
the set of prime divisors of
K/k
.

The
constant field
of
K/k
is the algebraic closure
\widetilde{k}
of
k
inside
K
. The extension
\widetilde{k}/k
is finite as

[\widetilde{k}:k]=[\widetilde{k}(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}):k(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})]\leq[K:k(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})]

is finite. Therefore,
K/\widetilde{k}
is an algebraic function field of
n
variables over
\widetilde{k}
.

Fixing an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
, if
M
is a positive integer not divisible by
\mathrm{char}(k)
, we denote by
\zeta_{M}
a primitive root of unity in
\overline{K}
(or inside the algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
in
\overline{K}
) of order
M
and we obtain a cyclotomic extension
k(\zeta_{M})/k
.

Throughout the text,
\ell
is a prime number different from
\mathrm{char}(k)
. We define
k(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})/k
inside
\overline{K}
as the union, over the integers
n\geq 1
, of the cyclotomic extensions
k(\zeta_{\ell^{n}})/k
.
We also write
v_{\ell}
for the
\ell
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. Also, if
G
is a group, then
G=\langle\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\rangle
means that
G
is generated by a finite set of elements
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f\in K^{\times}\setminus\widetilde{k}
and
c\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}
.

(i)

For all
v\in\mathrm{Prime}(K/k)
we have
v(c)=0
and
v(cf)=v(f)
.

(ii)

There is a finite non-empty set
S
of
\mathrm{Prime}(K/k)
such that
v(f)\neq 0
for all
v\in S
and
v(f)=0
for all
v\notin S
. More precisely,
S
has at least two elements,
v_{1},v_{2}
respectively satisfying
\mathrm{ord}_{v_{1}}(f)>0
and
\mathrm{ord}_{v_{2}}(f)<0
.

(iii)

There are only finitely many positive integers
n
such that
f\in\widetilde{k}K^{\times n}
. In particular, there is no prime number
\ell
such that
f
is infinitely
\ell
-divisible (not even up to constant elements).

Proof.

(i)

This follows immediately from the fact that
v
is also a valuation on
K/\widetilde{k}
.

(ii)

The finiteness of
S
is classical divisor theory and the rest is proven in
[
6
, p.99, Ch VI, §14 and p.175, Ch VII, §4 bis]
.

(iii)

This follows from (i) and (ii) since if
cf\in\widetilde{k}K^{\times n}
, then
n
divides
v(cf)=v(f)
for all
v\in\mathrm{Prime}(K/k)
.

∎

3.
Extensions of function fields

3.1.
General results

The following results hold for fields that are not necessarily function fields.

Definition 5
.

Let
K
and
L
be two extensions of a field
k
that are contained in some algebraically closed field. The field
K
is said to be
linearly disjoint from
L
over
k
if every finite set of elements of
K
that is linearly independent over
k
is linearly independent over
L
.

Remark that if
K
and
L
are linearly disjoint over
k
, then
K
and
L^{\prime}
are linearly disjoint over
k
for any intermediate extension
k\subseteq L^{\prime}\subseteq L
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
k
be a field that is algebraically closed in some field
K
. Let
\alpha
be an element of a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{k}
over
k
. Then
k(\alpha)
and
K
are linearly disjoint over
k
, or equivalently,
k(\alpha)\otimes_{k}K
is a field, and we have
[k(\alpha):k]=[K(\alpha):K]
. In particular, the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
K
and over
k
are the same.

Proof.

Let
m(x)
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
k
. Any constant factor of
m(x)
over
K
has its coefficients algebraic over
k
, hence in
k
. Therefore,
m(x)
is irreducible over
K
and the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 7

(
[
5
, p.253, Proposition 8.4.1]
)
.

If a field
k
is algebraically closed in
K
and
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an algebraically independent set over
K
, then
k(\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is algebraically closed in
K(\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I})
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
K/k
be a field extension such that
k
is algebraically closed in
K
. Fix some algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
and fix an algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
contained in
\overline{K}
.
Let
F/K
be a finite extension such that
F\subseteq KL
for some field
L\subseteq\overline{k}
that is separable over
k
.
Then
(F\cap\overline{k})/k
is finite and we have
F=K(F\cap\overline{k})
.

Proof.

Notice that, as
F/K
is finite we may suppose, up to replacing
L
by a subfield, that
L/k
is finite. Thus,
L/k
is primitive. Since we have
K\cap L=k
, then
K\otimes_{k}L
is a field by Lemma
6
.

We now prove the statement by induction on the degree of
F/K
. If
[F:K]=1
, the statement is trivial because
F\cap\overline{k}=k
. Now suppose that
[F:K]>1
and that the statement holds for all field extensions of lesser degree.

Suppose that
F\otimes_{k}L
is a field. Then the inclusion
K\to F
yields an injective ring morphism
\phi_{1}:K\otimes_{k}L\to F\otimes_{k}L
. We also have injective ring morphisms
\phi_{2}:F\otimes_{k}L\to\overline{K}
and
\phi:K\otimes_{k}L\to\overline{K}
, that are given on elementary tensors by
x\otimes y\mapsto xy
. One sees the three morphisms satisfy the compatibility
\phi=\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}
. Since
F\subseteq KL
, then
\mathrm{im}(\phi)=KL=\mathrm{im}(\phi_{2})
and hence
\phi_{1}
is an isomorphism. Since
L
is a
k
-module, the functor
\bullet\otimes_{k}L
is faithfully flat hence
F=K
, contradicting that
[F:K]>1
.

If
F\otimes_{k}L
is not a field, then, by Lemma
6
the field
k
is not algebraically closed in
F
and so there is a non-trivial extension
k_{1}:=F\cap\overline{k}
of
k
. So we have
[F:Kk_{1}]<[F:K]
and
F\subseteq(Kk_{1})(Lk_{1})
and
Lk_{1}/k_{1}
is separable. By induction hypothesis the field extension
(F\cap\overline{k})/k_{1}
is finite and we have
F=(Kk_{1})(F\cap\overline{k})=K(F\cap\overline{k})
.
∎

3.2.
Algebraic extensions of function fields

Given two algebraic function fields
L/k_{L}
and
K/k
, we say that the former is an
extension
of the latter if
L/K
and
k_{L}/k
are extensions of fields. Moreover, if both
L/K
and
k_{L}/k
are algebraic, we say the extension of function fields is
algebraic
. From now on, an
extension of function fields
will mean an algebraic extension of algebraic function fields. With the above notation we say that
L^{\prime}/k_{L^{\prime}}
is a
subextension
of
L/k_{L}
if
L^{\prime}/k_{L^{\prime}}
is an extension of
K/k
and
L/k_{L}
is an extension of
L^{\prime}/k_{L^{\prime}}
. Suppose that
L/k_{L}
is an extension of
K/k
. Then the transcendence degree of
L
over
k_{L}
equals the one of
K
over
k
. The constant field of
L
is
\widetilde{k}_{L}=\bar{k}\cap L
and it is algebraic over
k
(for convenience, the field
k_{L}
will not be specified if it equals
k
). In particular,
K\cap\widetilde{k}_{L}=\widetilde{k}
. We call the extension of function fields
finite
in case
L/K
and
k_{L}/k
are finite. We call the extension of function fields
Galois
if
L/K
is Galois. In this case,
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is Galois (see Lemma
10
) however
k_{L}/k
is not necessarily Galois. We call the extension of function fields
Abelian
(respectively,
of exponent

N
) if it is Galois and the Galois group of
L/K
is Abelian (respectively, of exponent
N
). Note that for an Abelian extension we have that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k
is also Abelian while for an Abelian extension of exponent
N
, we have that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k
is Abelian with exponent dividing
N
.

Remark 9
.

If
L/k_{L}
is an extension of
K/k
, then
L/K
is finite if and only if
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is finite if and only if
k_{L}/k
is finite. The second implication holds because the extensions
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k_{L}
and
\widetilde{k}/k
are both finite. For the first implication choose transcendence variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
for
K/k
, recall that the two extensions
K/\widetilde{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
L/\widetilde{k}_{L}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
are finite and write

[L:K][K:\widetilde{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})]=[L:\widetilde{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})]=[L:\widetilde{k}_{L}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})][\widetilde{k}_{L}:\widetilde{k}]\,.

Lemma 10
.

Let
L/k_{L}
be an extension of
K/k
. If
L/K
is separable, then
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is separable.
If
L/K
is normal, then
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k
and
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
are normal.
Consequently,

(i)

if
L/K
is Galois, then
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is Galois;

(ii)

if
L/K
is Galois and
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k
is separable, then
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k
is Galois;

(iii)

if
L/K
is separable, then
\widetilde{k}_{L}^{N}/k
is Galois, where
\widetilde{k}_{L}^{N}
is the normal closure of
\widetilde{k}_{L}
in
\overline{k}
.

Proof.

We first prove that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is separable, assuming that
L/K
is separable.
We may suppose that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is finite because if this extension is not separable, then there is some element
\alpha\in\widetilde{k}_{L}
such that
\widetilde{k}(\alpha)/\widetilde{k}
is finite and not separable.
We may also suppose that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is normal, up to replacing
\widetilde{k}_{L}
by its normal closure in
\bar{k}
.
Now assume that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is not separable. Up to replacing
\widetilde{k}
by a separable extension, we may suppose that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is purely inseparable. So let
p
be the finite characteristic of
\widetilde{k}
and let
\alpha\in\widetilde{k}_{L}\smallsetminus\widetilde{k}
be such that
\alpha^{p^{m}}\in\widetilde{k}
holds for some positive integer
m
. As
K\cap\widetilde{k}_{L}=\widetilde{k}
, we have
\alpha\in L\smallsetminus K
such that
\alpha^{p^{m}}\in K
, contradicting the separability of
L/K
.

We now assume that
L/K
is normal and prove that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k
is normal, which immediately implies that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is normal. Consider an element of
\widetilde{k}_{L}
, namely some
\alpha\in L
that is algebraic over
k
. The conjugates of
\alpha
are in
L
because
L/K
is normal and they are all algebraic over
k
, so they are in
\widetilde{k}_{L}
.
The last assertion is immediate.
∎

3.3.
Constant and geometric extensions

We say that an algebraic extension
L/k_{L}
of
K/k
is
constant
if
L=Kk^{\prime}
for some algebraic extension
k^{\prime}
of
k
. Note that we may replace
k^{\prime}
by
k^{\prime}\widetilde{k}
because
L=K(k^{\prime}\widetilde{k})
and
k^{\prime}\widetilde{k}/k
is algebraic. Hence, we may suppose without loss of generality that
\widetilde{k}\subseteq k^{\prime}
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
K/k
be a function field, and let
k^{\prime}
be an algebraic extension of
\widetilde{k}
. If
K
or
k^{\prime}
is separable over
\widetilde{k}
(in particular, if
\widetilde{k}
is perfect), then the constant field of
Kk^{\prime}
is
k^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Set
L:=Kk^{\prime}
and denote by
\widetilde{k}_{L}
its constant field. By Theorem

19
the extension
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k^{\prime}
is finite and purely inseparable. So it suffices to prove that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k^{\prime}
is separable.

Suppose that
k^{\prime}/\widetilde{k}
is separable and fix
\alpha\in k^{\prime}
such that
k^{\prime}=\widetilde{k}(\alpha)
. Since the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
K
divides the one over
\widetilde{k}
, we deduce that
L=K(\alpha)
is separable over
K
. To conclude remark that, for an element of
\widetilde{k}_{L}
, the minimal polynomial over
\widetilde{k}
is the same as the one over
K
by Lemma
6
.

Now suppose that
K/\widetilde{k}
is separable and let
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in K
be a transcendental basis over
\widetilde{k}
. Notice that
K/\widetilde{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
is a finite separable extension and hence
L
is a finite separable extension of
k^{\prime}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
. We deduce that
\widetilde{k}_{L}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})/k^{\prime}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
is a finite separable extension. So
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
and hence
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k^{\prime}
is separable by Lemma
10
.
∎

Lemma 12
.

If
C/K
is a finite constant Galois extension and
K^{\prime}/K
is a finite Galois extension whose maximal constant subextension is
L/K
, then
CL/K
is the maximal constant subextension of
CK^{\prime}/K
.

Proof.

Write
C=k_{C}K
and
L=k_{L}K
for some fields
k_{C},k_{L}
containing
\widetilde{k}
and contained in
\bar{k}
. The extension
CL/K
is constant because we have
CL=k_{C}k_{L}K
. If it is not maximal, let
M/K
be the maximal constant subextension of
CK^{\prime}/K
. By Galois theory, we have
M=CL^{\prime}
for some subextension
L^{\prime}/K
of
K^{\prime}/K
. Now,
L^{\prime}
strictly contains
L
, and
L^{\prime}/K
must be constant by Lemma
8
, contradicting the maximality of
L
.
∎

If
L/k_{L}
is an extension of
K/K
, then
\widetilde{k}\subseteq L\cap\overline{k}
. This extension is said to be
geometric
if the converse inclusion holds.
We call an extension
geometric
if
L\cap\overline{k}=\widetilde{k}
(remark that the inclusion
\supseteq
always holds). Notice that an algebraic extension is geometric if and only if
\widetilde{k}_{L}=\widetilde{k}
.

Remark 13
.

An algebraic extension
L/\widetilde{k}_{L}
of
K/\widetilde{k}
that is constant and geometric must be trivial. For a constant extension we have
L=Kk^{\prime}
for some
\widetilde{k}\subseteq k^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{k}
and
k^{\prime}\subseteq(L\cap\overline{k})\subseteq\widetilde{k}_{L}
so, assuming
\widetilde{k}_{L}=\widetilde{k}
, we get
k^{\prime}=\widetilde{k}
and hence
L=K
.

Remark 14
.

An algebraic separable extension of function fields of prime degree is constant if and only if it is not geometric. The “only if” direction is a consequence of Remark
13
. Conversely, suppose that the extension is not geometric. So there exists some
\gamma\in\widetilde{k}_{L}\setminus\widetilde{k}
. Since
\widetilde{k}_{L}\subseteq\overline{k}
we deduce that
\gamma\notin K
hence it is a primitive element for
L/K
, showing that
L=K\widetilde{k}(\gamma)
is constant.

Recall that any group whose order is the power of a prime
\ell
has a normal subgroup of index
\ell
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number different from
\mathrm{char}(k)
. Let
L/k_{L}
be a finite Galois extension of
K/k
such that the degree of
L/K
is a power of
\ell
. In particular, there exists a Galois subextension of
L/K
of degree
\ell
. Write
L^{\prime}/K
for the largest subextension of exponent
\ell
(namely, the compositum of all such extensions).
Then
L/k_{L}
is geometric if and only if
L^{\prime}/k_{L^{\prime}}
is geometric.

Proof.

If
\widetilde{k}_{L}=\widetilde{k}
, then
\widetilde{k}_{L^{\prime}}=\widetilde{k}
. Conversely, suppose that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/\widetilde{k}
is not trivial. This extension is finite (because
L/K
is finite) and Galois (by Lemma
10
) and its degree is a power of
\ell
(by Lemma
6
). Then it has a Galois subextension of degree
\ell
. This extension is of the form
\widetilde{k}(\alpha)/\widetilde{k}
where
\alpha\in\overline{k}\setminus\widetilde{k}
. Since
\alpha\in L^{\prime}
we deduce that
\widetilde{k}_{L^{\prime}}\neq\widetilde{k}
.
∎

3.4.
Cyclotomic extensions

An extension
L/k_{L}
of
K/k
is called
cyclotomic
if
L\subseteq K(\zeta_{m})
holds for some integer
m\geq 1
. Since
K(\zeta_{m})/K
is finite and Abelian, a cyclotomic extension of function fields is finite and Abelian.

In the following results,
\ell
is a prime number different from
\mathrm{char}(k)
and
k(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})=\cup_{n\geq 1}k(\zeta_{\ell^{n}})
.

Proposition 16
.

Let
K/k
be a function field. For every
M\geq 1
that is not divisible by
\mathrm{char}(k)
, the constant field of
K(\zeta_{M})/k
is
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})
. Moreover, the constant field of
K(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})/k
is
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})
.

Proof.

By the assumption on
M
, the field extension
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})/\widetilde{k}
is separable. Hence, by Theorem
11
the constant field of
K(\zeta_{M})
is
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})
. The field
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})
is contained in the constant field of
K(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})
because its elements are algebraic over
k
.
The converse implication holds because an element of
K(\zeta_{\ell^{\infty}})
that is algebraic over
k
is an element of
K(\zeta_{\ell^{m}})
for some integer
m\geq 1
and hence is contained in the constant field of
K(\zeta_{\ell^{m}})
, which we have shown to be
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}})
.
∎

Remark 17
.

Let
K/k
be a field extension such that
k
is algebraically closed in
K
. Fix some algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
and choose an algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
contained in
\overline{K}
.
Let
F/K
be a finite extension of degree coprime to
\mathrm{char}(k)
that is contained in
K\overline{k}
. Then Lemma
8
applies. Indeed, it is clear that
F\subset KL^{\prime}
for some finite and normal extension
L^{\prime}
of
k
. Let
L/k
be the maximal separable subextension of
L^{\prime}/k
hence
L^{\prime}/L
is purely inseparable. So

KL^{\prime}/KL
has degree a power of
\mathrm{char}(k)
and it has
FL/KL
as a subextension. This is only possible if
FL=KL
, giving
F\subseteq KL
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
f\in K^{\times}
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
g\in K\overline{k}
such that
f=g^{\ell}
.

(ii)

For some choice of
\ell
-th root
\sqrt[\ell]{f}
of
f
, the extension
K(\sqrt[\ell]{f})/K
is constant.

(iii)

For every
\ell
-th root
\sqrt[\ell]{f}
of
f
, the extension
K(\sqrt[\ell]{f})/K
is constant.

Moreover, these statements are implied by

(iv)

There exist
c\in\widetilde{k}
and
b\in K
such that
f=cb^{\ell}
.

and, if
\zeta_{\ell}\in K
(or if
f\in K^{\times\ell}
), they are equivalent to it.

Proof.

First notice that if
f\in K^{\times\ell}
then all conditions
(i)
to
(iv)
hold (remark that
K(\zeta_{\ell})/K
is constant). So now we may suppose that
f\notin K^{\times\ell}
hence the polynomial
x^{\ell}-f
is irreducible in
K[x]
by
[
3
, Theorem 9.1 of Chapter VI]
. In particular, the extension
K(\sqrt[\ell]{f})/K
has degree
\ell
for any choice of the
\ell
-th root of
f
.

To complete the proof of the last assertion we show that
(iii)
implies that there is some
c\in\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell})
and
b\in K(\zeta_{\ell})
such that
f=cb^{\ell}
. We may clearly reduce to the case
f\notin K(\zeta_{\ell})^{\times\ell}
. Recall from Proposition
16
that the constant field of
K(\zeta_{\ell})
is
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell})
.
By assumption the extension
K(\zeta_{\ell},\sqrt[\ell]{f})/K(\zeta_{\ell})

is constant, so there is an extension
k^{\prime}/\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell})
that has degree
\ell
(by Lemma
6
)
such that
K(\zeta_{\ell},\sqrt[\ell]{f})=K(\zeta_{\ell})k^{\prime}
. By Kummer theory over
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell})
and over
K(\zeta_{\ell})
there is
c\in\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell})^{\times}
such that
k^{\prime}(\zeta_{\ell})=\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{\ell},\sqrt[\ell]{c})
and we have
fc^{-1}\in K(\zeta_{\ell})^{\times\ell}
. Thus we
have
f=cb^{\ell}
where
b
is an
\ell
-th root of
fc^{-1}
in
K(\zeta_{\ell})
.

We are left to prove the equivalence of
(i)
,
(ii)
, and
(iii)
in the case where
f\notin K^{\times\ell}
, noticing that
(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
is obvious.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: By Lemma
8
(that can be applied thanks to Remark
17
) we deduce that
K(g)/K
is constant, so we conclude by choosing
\sqrt[\ell]{f}=g
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: By assumption,
K(\sqrt[\ell]{f})=Kk^{\prime}
with
k^{\prime}
a subfield of
\overline{k}
and we may take
g=\sqrt[\ell]{f}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Let
\alpha,\beta\in\overline{K}\setminus K
be such that
\alpha^{\ell}=\beta^{\ell}=f
.
Suppose that
K(\alpha)/K
is constant hence
K(\beta)/K
is contained in the constant extension
K(\alpha,\zeta_{\ell})/K
.
By Lemma
8
(that can be applied thanks to Remark
17
) we deduce that
K(\beta)/K
is constant.
∎

4.
An auxiliary result on function fields extensions

The following result generalizes
[
5
, p.254, Theorem 8.4.2]

from transcendence degree
1
to any finite transcendence degree. We let
K/k
be a function field, and we suppose that
k
is algebraically closed in
K
.

Theorem 19
.

If
k^{\prime}
is an extension of
k
, then there exists a function field
L/k_{L}
that is an extension of
K/k
such that
L=k_{L}K
and there is a
\widetilde{k}
-isomorphism
\lambda:k_{L}\to k^{\prime}
.
The above properties imply that
\widetilde{k}_{L}
is a purely inseparable finite extension of
k_{L}
.
Finally, if
\hat{L}
,
k_{\hat{L}}
,
\hat{\lambda}
are also as above, there exists a
K
-isomorphism
\hat{L}\to L
extending

\lambda^{-1}\circ\hat{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
n=\mathrm{trdeg}\left(K/k\right)
and fix a transcendence basis
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
of
K
over
k
.

Let
\{t_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a transcendence basis of
k^{\prime}
over
k
and choose elements
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
that are algebraically independent over
K
. Mapping
t_{i}\mapsto T_{i}
gives a
k
-morphism from
k^{\prime}
to some algebraic closure of
K(\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I})
. We denote its image by
k_{L}
and we define
L:=k_{L}K
.

Let
t\in K\setminus k
be transcendental over
k
, and suppose that it is algebraic over
k_{L}
. Then it is algebraic also over
k(\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I})
because the extension
k_{L}/k(\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is algebraic. Therefore, we have without loss of generality. (and up to clearing the denominators) a relation of the form

f_{r}t^{r}+\cdots+f_{0}=0

with
f_{i}\in k[T_{1},\cdots,T_{m}]
, not all zero.
Some of the
f_{i}
must be nonconstant, else
t
would be algebraic over
k
. Hence, we have a non-trivial algebraic relation over
K
of the elements
T_{1},\cdots,T_{m}
, contradicting the algebraic independence of the
T_{i}
’s. Therefore, we have
k_{L}\cap K=k

and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are algebraically independent over
k_{L}
.
Now,
k_{L}
contains
k
and we have

[L:k_{L}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})]\leq[K:k(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})]<\infty.

Hence,
L/k_{L}
is a function field.

We now prove the last assertion. Setting
\theta:=\lambda^{-1}\circ\hat{\lambda}
, we prove that we obtain a
K
-isomorphism
\rho:\hat{L}\to L
by defining

\rho\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{j}\delta_{j}}\right)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\theta(\beta_{i})}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{j}\theta(\delta_{j})}\qquad\text{ where $\alpha_{i},\gamma_{j}\in K$ and $\beta_{i},\delta_{j}\in k_{\hat{L}}$ and $\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{j}\delta_{j}\neq 0$}.

We claim that for elements
A_{i}\in K
and
B_{i}\in k_{\hat{L}}
we have

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}B_{i}=0\text{ if and only if }\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\theta(B_{i})=0\,.

Then the formula for
\rho
produces an element in
L
and different expressions for an element of
\hat{L}
lead to the same image and the map
\rho
is injective. We immediately see that
\rho
is a
K
-morphism, and it is surjective because
\theta
is invertible.

Notice that (
2
) only involves a finite number of elements and so we may assume that
k_{\hat{L}}
and
k_{L}
are finitely generated over
k
.

Let
k^{\prime}/k
have transcendence basis
t_{1},\cdots,t_{m}
and that is generated over
k(t_{1},\cdots,t_{m})
by some elements
\alpha_{i}
. Then by construction the elements
T_{i}=\lambda^{-1}(t_{i})
are a transcendence basis of
k_{L}
(and they are algebraically independent over
K
) which generate
k_{L}
together with the elements
\lambda^{-1}(\alpha_{i})
. The correspondence
t_{i}\mapsto T_{i}
maps the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
to the one of
\lambda^{-1}(\alpha_{i})
.
The analogous statement holds for
k_{\hat{L}}
, and we deduce that (
2
) holds.

If
k_{L}/k
is purely transcendental with transcendental basis
\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, by Lemma
7
the field
k_{L}=k(T_{i})_{i\in I}
is algebraically closed in
L=K(T_{i})_{i\in I}
. So the constant field of
L
is
k_{L}
.
In general,
k_{L}/k
will have an intermediate extension
k_{L}^{\prime}/k
that is purely transcendental. By applying what is above, we may replace
K/k
by
k_{L}^{\prime}K/k_{L}^{\prime}
and are left to deal with the case where
k_{L}/k
is algebraic. Since
k_{L}K/k_{L}
is a function field, the extension
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k_{L}
is finite. We are left to prove that
\widetilde{k}_{L}/k_{L}
is purely inseparable.

So write
\widetilde{k}_{L}=k(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r})
for some
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r}
algebraic over
k
. We prove the result by induction on
r
. The result holds trivially for
r=0
. Now, suppose the result holds for some
r-1\geq 0
and let
k_{1}=k(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r-1})
and
L_{1}:=Kk_{1}=K(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r-1})
. Let
k_{2}
be the algebraic closure of
k_{1}
in
L_{1}
.
To conclude, it suffices to prove that
\widetilde{k}_{L}
is purely inseparable over
k_{2}
.
Since
\gamma_{r}
is algebraic over
k
, the same is true for the coefficients of the minimal polynomial
\min(\gamma_{r},L_{1})
of
\gamma_{r}
over
L_{1}
. So
\min(\gamma_{r},L_{1})\in k_{2}[X]
and hence
\min(\gamma_{r},L_{1})=\min(\gamma_{r},k_{2})
, so we have

[L_{1}(\gamma_{r}):k_{2}]=[L_{1}(\gamma_{r}):L_{1}][L_{1}:k_{2}]=[k_{2}(\gamma_{r}):k_{2}][L_{1}:k_{2}]

and we deduce
[L_{1}:k_{2}]=[L_{1}(\gamma_{r}):k_{2}(\gamma_{r})]
.

If
c\in\widetilde{k}_{L}
and
p:=\mathrm{char}(k)
, there is an integer
u\geq 0
(taking
p^{u}=1
if
p=0
) for which the element
c^{p^{u}}
is separable over
k_{2}(\gamma_{r})
. By separability, we have
k_{2}(\gamma_{r},c^{p^{u}})=k_{2}(\delta)
for some primitive element
\delta
. We can write

[L_{1}(\gamma_{r}):k_{2}(\gamma_{r},c^{p^{u}})]=[L_{1}(\delta):k_{2}(\delta)]=[L_{1}:k_{2}]=[L_{1}(\gamma_{r}):k_{2}(\gamma_{r})],

where the second inequality is because a separable extension is linearly disjoint from an inseparable extension. Hence,
c^{p^{u}}\in k_{2}(\gamma_{r})
, so
c
is purely inseparable over
k_{L}=k_{2}(\gamma_{r})
.
∎

5.
Divisibility modulo constants

5.1.
Divisibility parameter modulo constants

Let
K/k
be a function field and let
\ell
be a prime number different from
\mathrm{char}(k)
. We will see in Lemma
20
that for any
\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\widetilde{k}^{\times}
there exists a maximal integer
D\geq 0
such that
\alpha\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}K^{\times\ell^{D}}
, which we call
\ell
-divisibility parameter modulo constants over
K
. We say that
\alpha\in K^{\times}
is
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants
if
D=0
, which means
\alpha\notin\widetilde{k}^{\times}K^{\times\ell}
.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\widetilde{k}^{\times}
. There is an integer
D\geq 0
which is the largest integer
n
for which
\alpha\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}K^{\times\ell^{n}}
.
Moreover, if
\alpha=c\beta^{\ell^{n}}
for some
c\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}
, for some
\beta\in K^{\times}
, and for some integer
n\geq 0
, we have
D=n
if and only if
\beta
is
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants.
Consequently, for all but finitely many primes
\ell
,
\alpha
is
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants.

Proof.

There is
v\in\mathrm{Prime}(K/k)
such that
v(\alpha)\neq 0
(see Lemma
4
(ii)).
This shows that
D
is well-defined and it also proves the last assertion. If
\beta
is not
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants, we can write
\beta=c_{1}\beta_{1}^{\ell}
for some
c_{1}\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}
and for some
\beta_{1}\in K^{\times}
. Then
\alpha=(cc_{1})\beta_{1}^{\ell^{n+1}}\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}K^{\times\ell^{n+1}}
, implying that
n<D
. Conversely, suppose that
n<D
and write
\alpha=c_{2}\beta_{2}^{\ell^{D}}
for some
c_{2}\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}
and for some
\beta_{2}\in K^{\times}
. We can write
\beta^{\ell^{n}}=(c_{2}c_{1}^{-1})\beta_{2}^{\ell^{D}}
hence there is
\gamma\in\overline{k}
such that
\beta=\gamma\beta_{2}^{\ell^{D-n}}
. Since
\gamma\in K\cap\overline{k}=\widetilde{k}
we deduce that
\beta
is not
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants.
∎

Lemma 21
.

Let
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, and suppose that
\zeta_{\ell}\in K
. For any
M\geq 1
not divisible by
\mathrm{char}(k)
the
\ell
-divisibility parameter
D
of
\alpha
over
K/\widetilde{k}
is the same over
K(\zeta_{M})/k(\zeta_{M})
.
If
L/k_{L}
is a finite constant extension of
K
, then the
\ell
-divisibility parameter
D
of
\alpha
over
K
is the same over
L
.

Proof.

Write
\alpha=c\beta^{\ell^{D}}
with

c\in\widetilde{k}^{\times}
,
\beta\in K^{\times}
. Since
\beta
is
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants (and a constant extension of prime degree is geometric), the extension
K(\sqrt[\ell]{\beta})
is not contained in
K\overline{k}
.
We deduce that the extension
L(\zeta_{\ell^{D+1}},\sqrt[\ell^{D+1}]{\alpha})/K
is not contained in
K\overline{k}
, which makes it impossible to have
\alpha=c_{L}g^{\ell^{D+1}}
with

c_{L}\in\widetilde{k}_{L}^{\times}
,
g\in L^{\times}
.
∎

Example 22
.

Let
K=k(t)
, let
s
be a positive integer and let
\alpha=c\prod_{i=1}^{s}P_{i}^{n_{i}}
where

,

are distinct monic irreducible polynomials in

, and

. Then the divisibility parameter of

over

is

because

is a unique factorization domain.

Suppose that
c=1
and let

be a non-zero integer pairwise coprime with each

.
Consider the function field
L/k
such that
L=K(y)
for some choice of

in an algebraic closure
\bar{K}
of

such that

(the function field

is called
superelliptic
if

and all

If
\ell
divides

-divisibility parameters of

over

is
\mathrm{ord}_{\ell}(n)
. If

over

.

Remark 23
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\widetilde{k}
, and let
n\geq 1
.
If
L/K
is a constant extension containing
\zeta_{\ell^{n}}
, then we have

[L(\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{\alpha}):L]=[L(\sqrt[\ell^{D}]{\alpha}):L]\cdot\ell^{n-D}\qquad\text{for }n>D\,.

Indeed, by Lemma

the
\ell

5.2.
Independence modulo constants

Let
K/k
be a function field and let
n\geq 1
be an integer coprime with
\mathrm{char}(K)
. If
G
is a subgroup of
K^{\times}
, then we write
K(\sqrt[n]{G})
for the extension of
K
generated by all the
\sqrt[n]{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in G
. This extension is uniquely determined by
G
, as a subfield of a fixed algebraic closure
K
. In particular, if
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\}
is a minimal subset of
K^{\times}
of generators for
G
, then by
[
3
, Corollary 6.1.16]
we have

K(\sqrt[n]{G})=K(\sqrt[n]{\alpha_{1}},\cdots,\sqrt[n]{\alpha_{r}})=K(\sqrt[n]{\alpha_{1}})\cdot\cdots\cdot K(\sqrt[n]{\alpha_{r}})

and we have a canonical group isomorphism

\mathrm{Gal}(K(\sqrt[n]{G})/K)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Gal}(K(\sqrt[n]{\alpha_{1}})/K)\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Gal}(K\sqrt[n]{\alpha_{r}})/K).

We now suppose that
n
is the power of some prime number
\ell
and hence we will suppose
that
\zeta_{\ell}\in K
(as we are interested in the Kummer extensions, we may replace
K
by
K(\zeta_{\ell})
).

Definition 24
.

We say elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\in K^{\times}
are
\ell
-independent modulo constants
if for each vector
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
not in
(\ell\mathbb{Z})^{r}
, the element
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{x_{i}}
is
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants.

Lemma 25
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}\in K^{\times}
be
\ell
-independent modulo constants. The group generated by the images of
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}
in the quotient group
\frac{K^{\times}}{\widetilde{k}^{\times}}
has rank
r
. In particular, the
\alpha_{i}
’s generate a subgroup
G
of
K^{\times}
that is torsion-free and of rank
r
.

Proof.

The latter assertion is readily seen to be a consequence of the former.
Suppose that
\eta:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{x_{i}}
for some non-zero vector
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. Up to replacing
\eta
, we may suppose without loss of generality that not all
x_{i}
are divisible by
\ell
. Since
\eta
is not
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants, this contradicts the assumption of
\ell
-independence modulo constants.
∎

The function fields appearing in the next theorem are implicitly considered as function fields over the same field
k
.

Theorem 26
.

Let
G
be a torsion-free subgroup of
K^{\times}
of rank
r>0
, and write
G=\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\rangle
with
\alpha_{i}\in K^{\times}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

The elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
are
\ell
-independent modulo constants.

(ii)

The Kummer extension
K(\sqrt[\ell]{G})/K
has degree
\ell^{r}
and it is geometric.

(iii)

For every
m\geq n\geq 1
the Kummer extension
K(\zeta_{\ell^{m}},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G})/K(\zeta_{\ell^{m}})

5.3.
Good basis modulo constants

In the following definition, we let
D_{i}
be the
\ell
-divisibility parameters of
\alpha_{i}
over
K
.

Definition 28
.

Consider a finitely generated subgroup
G
of
K^{\times}
that intersects
\widetilde{k}
only trivially and that has positive rank
r
.
Let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
be a basis of

. This basis is said to be an
\ell
-good basis modulo constants
if, equivalently:

(i)

the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{r}D_{i}
is maximal among the bases of

;

(ii)

we can write
\alpha_{i}=c_{i}\beta_{i}^{\ell^{D_{i}}}
where
c_{i}\in\widetilde{k}
and
\beta_{i}\in K
and
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{r}
are
\ell
-independent modulo constants.

The multiset of

is uniquely determined by

and

(see Remark
32
). We call it the
\ell
-divisibility parameters modulo constants of

over

.

Proposition 29
.

Definition
28
is well-posed, namely the equivalence holds true.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
By the definition of the
\ell
-divisibility parameters we can write
\alpha_{i}=c_{i}\beta_{i}^{\ell^{D_{i}}}
where
c_{i}\in\widetilde{k}
and
\beta_{i}\in K
. Suppose that the
\beta_{i}
’s are not
\ell
-independent modulo constants. So there are a non-empty subset
J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}
and integers
x_{j}
coprime to
\ell
such that

\beta:=\prod_{j\in J}\beta_{j}^{x_{j}}
is not
\ell
-indivisible modulo constants.
We may suppose without loss of generality that

and
D_{1}=\max\{D_{j}:j\in J\}
. We may additionally suppose that

(by raising

to some power

such that

and then discarding an

-th power).
Define

By inspecting the factor

, we may replace
\alpha_{1}
by
\alpha
and still get a basis of

. Moreover, by construction

\alpha\beta^{-\ell^{D_{1}}}=\prod_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}^{x_{j}\ell^{D_{1}-D_{j}}}\left(\prod_{j\in J}\beta_{j}^{x_{j}\ell^{D_{1}}}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{j\in J}c_{j}^{x_{j}\ell^{D_{1}-D_{j}}}

is a constant hence (since

(ii)
\Rightarrow

We cannot have a different basis

of

the degree of

has

∎

Theorem 30
.

Consider a finitely generated subgroup
G
of

of rank

that intersects

(ii)

6.
Reducing to cyclotomic-Kummer extensions of the constant field

Consider a function field
K/k
and a Kummer extension of the form

K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[N]{G})/K(\zeta_{M})\,,

where
G
is a finitely generated subgroup of
K^{\times}
and
M
and
N
are positive integers such that
M
is divisible by
N
but not by
\mathrm{char}(K)
. In this section, we precisely show how the questions of computing the degree of this extension and of computing the group structure of its Galois group reduce to the corresponding questions for the Kummer extension of the constant fields.

Considering the prime factorization
N=\prod_{\ell\mid N}\ell^{v_{\ell}(N)}
, by Kummer theory (notice the pairwise coprime exponents of the Kummer extensions in the product group) we have a group isomorphism

\Gal(K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[N]{G})/K(\zeta_{M}))\simeq\prod_{\ell\mid N}\Gal(K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{v_{\ell}(N)}]{G})/K(\zeta_{M}))\,.

We will therefore restrict to the case where
N=\ell^{n}

for some fixed prime number
\ell
different from
\mathrm{char}(k)
and for some integer
n\geq 1
. We may then without loss of generality replace
K
by
K(\zeta_{\ell})
. We may also suppose that
G
is not a subgroup of
\widetilde{k}^{\times}
. Hence, from now on
G_{0}:=G\cap\widetilde{k}
is a proper subgroup of
G
and we can write the set decomposition
G=G_{0}G^{\prime}
, where
G^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
G
isomorphic to
G/G_{0}
. Let
r>0
be the rank of
G^{\prime}
and let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
be the
\ell
-divisibility parameters modulo constants of
G^{\prime}
over
K
. Up to relabelling, we suppose without loss of generality that
D_{1}\leq D_{2}\leq\cdots\leq D_{r}
.
Let
\alpha_{i}=c_{i}\beta_{i}^{\ell^{D_{i}}}
be the elements of an
\ell
-good basis modulo constants of
G^{\prime}
over
K
, with
c_{i}\in\widetilde{k}
and
\beta_{i}\in K
.

The following result reduces the problem of determining the degree of the Kummer extension
K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G})/K(\zeta_{M})
to determine the degree of a Kummer extension that only depends on
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})
,
\ell^{n}
,
G_{0}
, the
c_{i}
’s and the
D_{i}
’s:

Theorem 33
.

Define
H
as the subgroup of
K^{\times}
generated by
G_{0}
and by the elements
c_{i}^{\ell^{\max(n-D_{i},0)}}
.
Then we have

[K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G}):K(\zeta_{M})]=[\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{H}):\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ell^{\max(n-D_{i},0)}\,.

Proof.

Remark that, by Lemma
6
applied to the monogenic extension
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{H})/\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M}
) we have

[\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{H}):\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})]=[K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{H}):K(\zeta_{M})]\,.

We may conclude by Corollary
27
(applied to
G^{\prime}
) because the largest constant subextension of

K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G})/K(\zeta_{M})

is generated by the
\ell^{n}
-th roots of

, in view of Lemma
12
(where

is the Kummer extension generated by

and

is the Kummer extension generated by

in

By Lemma
21
the
\ell
-divisibility parameters of

over

are the same over

and by Proposition

by an element of

This will conclude because no integer

can satisfy both

c_{i}^{x}\bmod\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})^{\times\ell^{n}}\in\langle G_{0},c_{j}:j>i\rangle\bmod\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})^{\times\ell^{n}}\,\quad\text{and}\quad c_{i}^{x}\notin\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})^{\times\ell^{n}}.

Remark 37
.

The extension
\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G_{0}})/\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})

[K(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G_{0}}):K(\zeta_{M})]=[\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M},\sqrt[\ell^{n}]{G_{0}}):\widetilde{k}(\zeta_{M})].

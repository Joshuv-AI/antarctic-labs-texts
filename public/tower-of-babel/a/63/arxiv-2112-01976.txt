Estimating the Hardy constant of nonconcave Gini means Thanks: The first author was supported by the K-134191 NKFIH Grant.
Zsolt Páles, Paweł Pasteczka

Abstract
. The extension of the Hardy-Knopp-Carleman inequality to several classes of means was the subject of numerous papers. In the class of Gini means the Hardy property was characterized in 2015 by the second author. The precise value of the associated Hardy constant was only established for concave Gini means by the authors in 2016. The determination Hardy constant for nonconcave Gini means is still an open problem. The main goal of this paper is to establish sharper upper bounds for the Hardy constant in this case. The method is to construct a homogeneous and concave quasideviation mean which majorizes the nonconcave Gini mean and for which the Hardy constant can be computed.

1.
Introduction

There are a number of recent results, obtained by the authors, which allow one to establish the Hardy constants for various families of means. Most of them are based on the so-called Kedlaya’s property
[
4
,
5
]
in the background, which unifies their assumptions. In the most natural setting, we assume that a mean is concave, homogeneous, and repetition invariant (then it is also monotone). These assumptions are relaxed for example using homogenizations techniques
[
13
,
14
]
, or by replacing repetition invariance by a weaker axiom
[
8
]
.

However we have not been able to relax the concavity assumption till now. Due to this reason, it was difficult to establish an upper estimation for the Hardy constant for Gini means which are nonconcave. We note that some lower estimation can be obtained by using some general results from
[
11
]
. In this paper we apply a certain concavization techniques to improve the trivial upper bound (which can be obtained directly from
[
11
]
) as well as a more recent bound from
[
7
]
.
For this purpose, we consider Gini means in the quasideviation framework and construct a concave and homogeneous quasideviation mean which is above the given (nonconcave) Gini mean. The problem of calculating the Hardy constant for concave quasideviation means has been solved recently in
[
14
]
. The key auxiliary results are contained in Lemma
3.2
which enables us the described construction.

2.
Means and their properties

A function
\mathscr{M}\colon\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\min(x)\leq\mathscr{M}(x)\leq\max(x)
holds for all
x
in the domain of
\mathscr{M}
is called a
mean (on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
)
.Throughout the present note all considered means are defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, thus we can omit the domain of a mean whenever convenient. We also adopt the standard convention that natural properties like convexity, homogeneity, etc. refer to the respective properties of the
n
-variable function
\mathscr{M}|_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}
to be valid for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

For a given mean
\mathscr{M}
let
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{M})
denote the smallest nonnegative extended real
number, called the
Hardy constant of
\mathscr{M}
, such that, for all sequences
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)
of positive elements,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{M}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{M})\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}.

Means with a finite Hardy constant are called
Hardy means
(cf.
[
11
]
).

2.1.
Homogeneous quasideviation means

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a class of all continuous functions
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(i)

\sign(f(t))=\sign(t-1)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(ii)

for all
x\in(0,1)
, the mapping

t\mapsto\frac{f(t)}{f(t/x)}
is strictly increasing on
(x,1)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy (i) of the previous definition and assume that, for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
f_{p}(t):=t^{p}f(t)
(
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
) is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and strictly increasing on
(0,1)
. Then
f
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, if
f
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and strictly increasing on
(0,1)
, then
f\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

We need to verify that condition (ii) is also valid for
f
. Let
x\in(0,1)
be fixed. Then

\displaystyle\frac{f(t)}{f(t/x)}=x^{p}\frac{t^{p}f(t)}{(t/x)^{p}f(t/x)}=x^{p}\frac{f_{p}(t)}{f_{p}(t/x)}=-x^{p}\frac{(-f_{p})(t)}{f_{p}(t/x)}

and, using the monotonicity property of
f_{p}
, observe that the mappings

\displaystyle t\mapsto(-f_{p})(t)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad t\mapsto\frac{1}{f_{p}(t/x)}

are positive and strictly decreasing/decreasing functions on
(x,1)
. Therefore, their product is strictly decreasing, which implies that
t\mapsto\frac{f(t)}{f(t/x)}
is strictly increasing on
(x,1)
and proves that (ii) is valid.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In view of the results os the paper
[
10
, Theorem 6]
, it turns out that a continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
if and only if, for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
f_{p}(t):=t^{p}f(t)
(
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
) is increasing and strictly increasing either on
(0,1)
or on
(1,\infty)
.

For every
f\in\mathcal{F}
and all positive-entry vector
x:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, define
e_{f,x}(y)\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle e_{f,x}(y):=f\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{y}\Big)+\dots+f\Big(\frac{x_{n}}{y}\Big).

Due to
[
9
]
, it is known that the equation
e_{f,x}(y)=0
has a unique solution, which is called the
quasideviation mean generated by the quasideviation
E_{f}(x,y):=f(x/y)
. We denote it by
y:=\mathscr{E}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. It immediately follows from this definition that
\mathscr{E}_{f}
is a homogeneous mean, i.e.,
\mathscr{E}_{f}(tx_{1},\dots,tx_{n})=t\mathscr{E}_{f}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}>0
. Let us recall two important results for this family of means.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
with
f\leq g
we have
\mathscr{E}_{f}\leq\mathscr{E}_{g}
.

Proof.

Fix a vector of positive reals
x:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then
e_{f,x}(y)\neq 0
for all
y<\mathscr{E}_{f}(x)
. Furthermore
e_{f}(\min(x))>0
. Thus, since
e_{f,x}
is continuous, we get
e_{f,x}(y)>0
for all
y<\mathscr{E}_{f}(x)
.
Consequently we get
e_{g,x}(y)\geq e_{f,x}(y)>0
for all
y<\mathscr{E}_{f}(x)
, which implies
\mathscr{E}_{f}(x)\leq\mathscr{E}_{g}(x)
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

By the results of
[
2
]
and
[
9
, Theorem 10]
, the comparison inequality
\mathscr{E}_{f}\leq\mathscr{E}_{g}
holds if and only if
f\leq ag
for some positive number
a
.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
12
]
, Theorem 3.4)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be concave such that
\sign(f(t))=\sign(t-1)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then
f
is increasing on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and strictly increasing on
(0,1)
(and hence
f\in\mathcal{F}
), and
\mathscr{E}_{f}
is a Hardy mean if and only if

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f\Big(\frac{1}{t}\Big)\>dt<+\infty.

Furthermore, its Hardy constant
c:=\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{E}_{f})
is the unique solution of the equation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c}f\Big(\frac{1}{t}\Big)\>dt=0.

2.2.
Gini means

For a given
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
, define
g_{p,q}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle g_{p,q}(t):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{t^{p}-t^{q}}{p-q}&\text{ if }p\neq q;\\
t^{p}\ln t&\text{ if }p=q.\end{cases}

Then, observe that
t\mapsto t^{-q}g_{p,q}(t)
is strictly increasing. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
, it follows that
g_{p,q}\in\mathcal{F}
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, for
p,q\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the
Gini mean
of parameter
(p,q)
by
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}:=\mathscr{E}_{g_{p,q}}
(cf.
[
3
]
). One can easily see that
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}
has the following explicit form:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{p,q}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{x_{1}^{p}+\cdots+x_{n}^{p}}{x_{1}^{q}+\cdots+x_{n}^{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-q}}&\mbox{if }p\neq q,\\[11.38109pt]
\exp\left(\dfrac{x_{1}^{p}\ln(x_{1})+\cdots+x_{n}^{p}\ln(x_{n})}{x_{1}^{p}+\cdots+x_{n}^{p}}\right)\quad&\mbox{if }p=q.\end{cases}.

Clearly, in the particular case
q=0
, the mean
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}
reduces to the
p
th Hölder mean
\mathscr{P}_{p}
. It is
also obvious that
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}=\mathscr{G}_{q,p}
. Furthermore by
[
1
]
we know that Gini means are nondecreasing in their parameters, that is
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}\leq\mathscr{G}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
for all
p,q,p^{\prime},q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
with
p\leq p^{\prime}
and
q\leq q^{\prime}
.

Due to
[
15
]
and
[
6
]
it is known that
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}
is a Hardy mean if and only if
\min(p,q)\leq 0
and
\max(p,q)<1
. In addition, its Hardy constant is related to the limit

\displaystyle H_{p,q}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathscr{G}_{p,q}\big(1,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{1}{n}\big)=\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{1-p}{1-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}&\mbox{if }p\neq q,\\
e^{\frac{1}{1-p}}&\mbox{if }p=q=0.\end{cases}

More precisely, by
[
11
]
, we have that
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})=H_{p,q}
in the case when the Gini mean
\mathscr{G}_{p,q}
is concave, i.e., when
\min(p,q)\leq 0\leq\max(p,q)<1
. In the remaining case, that is, if
\max(p,q)<0
, we only have the lower estimate
H_{p,q}\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})
. However the exact value of
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})
remains unknown in this quadrant.

There were two approaches to obtain upper estimations of this value. The comparison criterion easily implies that, for
q<p<0
, we have
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{q,0})=(1-q)^{-\frac{1}{q}}
. A sharper upper estimation is due to
[
7
]
, where it was proved that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})\leq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\big(\frac{1-q}{1-p}\big)^{\frac{1-p}{p-q}}-p}{1-p}&\text{ for }q<p<0;\\
\frac{e-p}{1-p}&\text{ for }q=p<0.\end{cases}

The main goal of this paper is to improve both of these upper bounds.

3.
Main results

Let us first prove a technical result collecting a few properties of the function
g_{p,q}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p,q\in(-\infty,0)
. Then

(i)

g_{p,q}
has a global maximum at
\tau_{p,q}\in(1,\infty)
, where

\displaystyle\tau_{p,q}:=\begin{cases}\big(\tfrac{p}{q}\big)^{\tfrac{1}{q-p}}&\text{ if }p\neq q,\\[5.69054pt]
e^{-1/p}&\text{ if }p=q;\end{cases}

(ii)

\sign(g_{p,q}(t))=\sign(t-1)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(iii)

g_{p,q}
restricted to the interval
(0,\tau_{p,q})
is strictly increasing and concave.

Proof.

We prove the assertions only in the case
p>q
. The cases
p=q
and
p<q
are fairly analogous. For the brevity, denote
g:=g_{p,q}
and
\tau:=\tau_{p,q}
. We have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{pt^{p-1}-qt^{q-1}}{p-q}\qquad\text{and}\qquad g^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{p(p-1)t^{p-2}-q(q-1)t^{q-2}}{p-q}.

Thus
g^{\prime}
has a unique zero which equals
\tau
. The second derivative of
g
also has a unique zero which equals

\eta:=\big(\frac{p(p-1)}{q(q-1)}\big)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}=\tau\big(\frac{p-1}{q-1}\big)^{\frac{1}{q-p}}
. But
q-1<p-1<0
, thus
\frac{p-1}{q-1}>1
, and
q-p>0
. Therefore
\eta>\tau>1
.

Next, observe that
g(0^{+})=-\infty
and
g(+\infty)=0
. Thus
g
is strictly increasing on
(0,\tau)
and strictly decreasing of
(\tau,\infty)
, which implies (i). In particular
g^{\prime\prime}(\tau)<0
. Consequently
g^{\prime\prime}
is negative on the interval
(0,\eta)
. Thus, since
(0,\tau)\subset(0,\eta)
, we obtain (iii).

As we have already describe the monotonicity properties of
g
, to show the property (ii) it is sufficient to verify that
g(1)=0
, which is trivial.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the crucial lemma, which binds all facts which will be used in the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p,q\in(-\infty,0)
. Define
f_{p,q}\colon(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle f_{p,q}(t):=\begin{cases}g_{p,q}(t)&\text{ for }t\leq\tau_{p,q};\\
g_{p,q}(\tau_{p,q})&\text{ for }t>\tau_{p,q}.\end{cases}

Then

(I)

f_{p,q}
is concave;

(II)

\sign(f_{p,q}(t))=\sign(t-1)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(III)

\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{E}_{f_{p,q}})=c_{p,q}
, where
c_{p,q}\in(1,+\infty)
is the unique solution of the equation

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{g_{p,q}(\tau_{p,q})}{\tau_{p,q}}+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau_{p,q}}}^{c_{p,q}}g_{p,q}\big(\tfrac{1}{t}\big)dt=0

(IV)

g_{p,q}\leq f_{p,q}
;

(V)

\mathscr{G}_{p,q}\leq\mathscr{E}_{f_{p,q}}
.

Proof.

To show (I) we need to verify that
f_{p,q}^{\prime}
is decreasing, which easily follows from the definition of
f_{p,q}
, and the Lemma
3.1
.(iii).
Next, Lemma
3.1
.(i) yields (IV). Consequently, view of Lemma
2.3
, we get (V). Property (II) is implied Lemma
3.1
.(i) and (ii).

To proceed to the proof of (III), set
c:=c_{p,q}
,
f:=f_{p,q}
,
g:=g_{p,q}
, and
\tau:=\tau_{p,q}
. It is easy to check that (
2.1
) holds. Thus, by Proposition
2.5
we know that
\mathscr{E}_{f}
is a Hardy mean and its Hardy constant
c
satisfies
\tau^{-1}<1<c
and solves the equation

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{c}f(\tfrac{1}{t})\>dt=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\tau}}f(\tfrac{1}{t})\>dt+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}f(\tfrac{1}{t})\>dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\tau}}g(\tau)\>dt+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}g(\tfrac{1}{t})\>dt=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}g(\tfrac{1}{t})\>dt,

which completes the proof
∎

Now we are ready to present the main result of this note.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
p,q\in(-\infty,0)
, then
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})\leq c_{p,q}
, where
c_{p,q}
is the unique solution
c\in(1,+\infty)
of the equation

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{c^{1-q}}{1-q}-\frac{c^{1-p}}{1-p}

\displaystyle=\left|q\right|^{\frac{1-p}{q-p}}\left|p\right|^{\frac{1-q}{p-q}}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}-\frac{1}{1-q}\Big)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }p\neq q;

\displaystyle c^{1-p}(1-\ln c^{1-p})

\displaystyle=pe^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }p=q.

Proof.

Let us denote, as previously,
g:=g_{p,q}
,
\tau:=\tau_{p,q}
, and
c:=c_{p,q}
.
Then applying Lemma
3.2
(
V
), and (
III
) we have
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{E}_{f_{p,q}})=c
, where
c
solves (
3.1
).
Thus for
p\neq q
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}g\big(\tfrac{1}{t}\big)dt=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}\frac{t^{-p}-t^{-q}}{p-q}\>dt

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\frac{1}{p-q}\cdot\Big(\frac{c^{1-p}-\tau^{p-1}}{1-p}-\frac{c^{1-q}-\tau^{q-1}}{1-q}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-q}\cdot\Big(\frac{c^{1-p}}{1-p}-\frac{c^{1-q}}{1-q}+\Big(\frac{(p-q)g(\tau)}{\tau}+\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{q-1}\Big)\Big).

Equivalently, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{c^{1-q}}{1-q}-\frac{c^{1-p}}{1-p}=\frac{(p-q)g(\tau)}{\tau}+\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{q-1}.

In order to complete the proof, observe that

\displaystyle\frac{(p-q)g(\tau)}{\tau}+\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{q-1}

\displaystyle=\tau^{p-1}-\tau^{q-1}+\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{q-1}=\frac{p\tau^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{q\tau^{q-1}}{q-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{p-1}\Big(\frac{\left|p\right|}{\left|q\right|}\Big)^{\tfrac{p-1}{q-p}}-\frac{q}{q-1}\Big(\frac{\left|p\right|}{\left|q\right|}\Big)^{\tfrac{q-1}{q-p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|p\right|}{1-p}\left|p\right|^{\tfrac{p-1}{q-p}}\left|q\right|^{\tfrac{1-p}{q-p}}-\frac{\left|q\right|}{1-q}\left|p\right|^{\tfrac{q-1}{q-p}}\left|q\right|^{\tfrac{1-q}{q-p}}

\displaystyle=\left|q\right|^{\frac{1-p}{q-p}}\left|p\right|^{\frac{1-q}{p-q}}\Big(\frac{1}{1-p}-\frac{1}{1-q}\Big).

Therefore (
3.4
) implies our assertion in the case
p\neq q
.

Now assume that
p=q<0
. Then (
3.1
) simplifies to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}g\big(\tfrac{1}{t}\big)dt=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}-\int_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}t^{-p}\ln t\>dt=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\Big[\frac{t^{1-p}(1-\ln t^{1-p})}{(p-1)^{2}}\Big]_{\frac{1}{\tau}}^{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{g(\tau)}{\tau}+\frac{c^{1-p}(1-\ln c^{1-p})}{(p-1)^{2}}-\frac{\tau^{p-1}(1-\ln\tau^{p-1})}{(p-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}\Big(\frac{g(\tau)(p-1)^{2}}{\tau}+c^{1-p}(1-\ln c^{1-p})-\tau^{p-1}(1-\ln\tau^{p-1})\Big)

Thus one gets

\displaystyle c^{1-p}(1-\ln c^{1-p})=\tau^{p-1}(1-\ln\tau^{p-1})-\frac{g(\tau)(p-1)^{2}}{\tau}.

As in the previous case it is now sufficient to simplify

\displaystyle\tau^{p-1}(1-\ln\tau^{p-1})-\frac{g(\tau)(p-1)^{2}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau^{p-1}(1-\ln\tau^{p-1})-(p-1)\tau^{p-1}\ln\tau^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\tau^{p-1}(1-p\ln\tau^{p-1})=e^{\frac{1-p}{p}}(1-p\ln e^{\frac{1-p}{p}})=pe^{\frac{1-p}{p}},

which completes the second case.
∎

We note that equations in (
3.2
) are equivalent to (
3.1
).

4.
An example

In this section we compare the known estimates for the particular Gini mean
\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}
.

— First, by
H_{p,q}\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})
, we immediately obtain
1.5\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2})
.

— In view of the trivial estimation
\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}\leq\mathscr{G}_{0,-2}
, we get
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2})\leq\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{0,-2})=\sqrt{3}\approx 1.732
.

— Using the inequality (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{p,q})\leq\dfrac{\big(\frac{3}{2}\big)^{2}+1}{2}=\frac{13}{8}=1.625.

— Finally we use Theorem
3.3
. Then
c_{0}
is a solution of the equation

\tfrac{1}{3}c^{3}-\tfrac{1}{2}c^{2}=2^{-2}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{3})
(or, after simplifications,
8c^{3}-12c^{2}-1=0
). This polynomial has a unique real root
c_{0}\approx 1.552
. Thus
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2})\leq c_{0}
, which, obviously, improves both previous upper estimations. On the other hand, this is still far from the lower estimation
1.5
.

We now somehow leave apart the Hardy property and examine the mean
\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}}
, where
f_{-1,-2}\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined in Lemma
3.2
by

\displaystyle f_{-1,-2}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{-1}-t^{-2}&\text{ for }t\leq 2;\\
\tfrac{1}{4}&\text{ for }t>2.\end{cases}

By Lemma
3.2
, we already know that
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}})=c_{0}
. We are going to show the form of this mean. Since it is symmetric, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a vector of positive numbers with
x_{1}\leq\ldots\leq x_{n}
. Put
m:=\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}}(x)
. Take the maximal
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
x_{k}\leq 2m
. Then either
2m<x_{k+1}
or
k=n
. Thus

\displaystyle 0=e_{g}(m)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{-1,-2}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{m}\Big)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}f_{-1,-2}\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{m}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\frac{m}{x_{i}}-\frac{m^{2}}{x_{i}^{2}}\Big)+\frac{n-k}{4}.

With the notation
s_{\alpha}:=x_{1}^{\alpha}+\dots+x_{k}^{\alpha}
, we have
-s_{-2}m^{2}+s_{-1}m+\tfrac{n-k}{4}=0
.
Therefore, since
m>0
, we get

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{-1}+\sqrt{s_{-1}^{2}+s_{-2}(n-k)}}{2s_{-2}}=\frac{1}{2}\Bigg(\frac{s_{-1}}{s_{-2}}+\sqrt{\Big(\frac{s_{-1}}{s_{-2}}\Big)^{2}+\frac{n-k}{s_{-2}}}\Bigg)

Finally, using the definition of Gini means, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})+\sqrt{\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}^{2}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})+\tfrac{n-k}{k}\mathscr{G}_{0,-2}^{2}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}+\sqrt{\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}^{2}+\tfrac{n-k}{k}\mathscr{G}_{0,-2}^{2}}\ \Big)(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}),

where
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
is the maximal index satisfying
x_{k}\leq 2\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}}(x)
.

Remarkably, in the particular case
x_{n}\leq 2\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}}(x)
we have
k=n
and therefore
\mathscr{E}_{f_{-1,-2}}(x)=\mathscr{G}_{-1,-2}(x)
.

p p -Linear schemes for sequences modulo p r p^{r}
Frits Beukers

Abstract
. Many interesting combinatorial sequences, such as Apéry numbers and Franel numbers, enjoy the so-called Lucas property modulo almost all primes p p . Modulo prime powers p r p^{r} such sequences have a more complicated behaviour which can be described by matrix versions of the Lucas property called p p -linear schemes. They are examples of finite p p -automata. In this paper we construct such p p -linear schemes and give upper bounds for the number of states which, for fixed r r , do not depend on p p .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study infinite sequences of integers that have special properties
when they are reduced modulo a prime
p
or a prime power
p^{r}
. The most familiar
sequences are those sequences
a_{k}
that have the so-called Lucas property modulo
almost all primes
p
,

a_{k_{m}p^{m}+\cdots+k_{1}p+k_{0}}\equiv a_{k_{m}}\cdots a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{0}}({\rm mod}\ p)

for all
k_{i}
with
0\leq k_{i}\leq p-1
. Examples are the exponential sequences like
2^{k}
,
the central binomial coefficients
\binom{2k}{k}
, the Apéry and Franel-type numbers
given by

\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}^{2}\binom{k+m}{m}^{2},\quad\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}^{\ell}

and countless others. Our first result concerns constant term sequences of the form

g_{k}=\text{ct}[g({\bf x})^{k}]\quad k\geq 0,

where
g({\bf x})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1}\cdots x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is a Laurent
polynomial in the
n
variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and ’ct’ is the operation of
taking the constant term. In this paper we denote the Newton polytope of

g({\bf x})
by
\Delta
. By
\Delta^{\circ}
we denote the topological interior of
\Delta
, i.e

\Delta
minus its proper faces.
In
[
5
, Thm 1.4]
we find the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\Delta^{\circ}
has
{\bf 0}
as unique lattice point.
Then, for any prime
p
the constant term sequence
g_{k}
has the Lucas property.

To my knowledge the first explicit occurrence of this theorem is in the case

s=1
of
[
8
, Thm 3.3]
, which is unfortunately unpublished.

In Section
3
we present a proof,
which is basically the same as in
[
5
]
.
Here are some examples of interest. In
[
5
, Example 2.3]
we find

\binom{2k}{k}=\text{ct}\left[\left(x+2+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{k}\right].

In
[
5
, Example 2.4]
we find

\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\geq 0\atop k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=k}\left(\frac{k!}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{n}!}\right)^{2}=\text{ct}\left[(1+x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n})^{k}\left(1+\frac{1}{x_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{x_{n}}\right)^{k}\right].

In
[
5
, Example 2.5]
we find

\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{m+k}{m}^{2}\binom{k}{m}^{2}=\text{ct}\left[\left(\frac{(x+y)(z+1)(x+y+z)(y+z+1)}{xyz}\right)^{k}\right].

These are the famous Apéry numbers that occur in the irrationality proof of
\zeta(3)
.
Finally we mention

\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}^{n+1}=\text{ct}\left[(1+x_{1})^{k}(1+x_{2})^{k}\cdots(1+x_{n})^{k}\left(1+\frac{1}{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}\right)^{k}\right]

which is not hard to show.

In general one cannot expect the Lucas property to hold modulo

p^{r}
for
r>1
, or when
\Delta^{\circ}
contains another interior lattice point besides
{\bf 0}
.
But one can introduce additional companion sequences, which result in matrix versions
of the Lucas property.

Definition 1.2
.

A
p
-linear scheme modulo
p^{r}
for a set of sequences
{\bf a}_{k}=(a_{1,k},\ldots,a_{s,k})^{t}
,
k=0,1,\ldots

is a set of
s\times s
-matrices
M_{\ell}
with entries in
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(1)

{\bf a}_{kp+\ell}\equiv M_{\ell}{\bf a}_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})

for every
\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
and
k\geq 0
.

We say that any constant linear combination of the sequences
a_{1,k},\ldots,a_{s,k}

is a sequence that can be generated by a
p
-linear scheme modulo
p^{r}
. We call
s

the number of states of this
p
-linear scheme.

As explained in
[
10
]
,
p
-linear schemes are special instances of finite
p
-automata.
A simple corollary of Definition
1.2
is

{\bf a}_{k_{m}p^{m}+\cdots+k_{1}p+k_{0}}\equiv M_{k_{0}}M_{k_{1}}\cdots M_{k_{m}}{\bf a}_{0}({\rm mod}\ p^{r}),

where
0\leq k_{i}\leq p-1
for all
i=0,\ldots,m
. So all one needs in order to
compute
{\bf a}_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})

for any
k
are the matrices
M_{\ell}
for
\ell=0,1,\ldots,p-1
, the vector

{\bf a}_{0}
and the base
p
expansion of
k
. Furthermore, if
r=1
,
s=1
and
a_{0}=1

then Definition
1.2
comes down to the Lucas property modulo
p
.

There is also a formulation of a
p
-linear scheme in terms of generating power series.
Let
{\bf F}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}{\bf a}_{k}t^{k}
, a vector of generating series,
and
M(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}M_{\ell}t^{\ell}
. Then (
1
)
is equivalent to

(2)

{\bf F}(t)\equiv M(t){\bf F}(t^{p})({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

As an example we display a generalization of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\circ}
be the set of lattice points in
\Delta^{\circ}
.
Define the
p
-th
Hasse-Witt matrix

H
associated to
1-tg({\bf x})
as the square matrix
indexed by
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\circ}
, whose
{\bf u},{\bf v}
-entry is given by

H_{{\bf u}{\bf v}}(t)=\text{coefficient of ${\bf x}^{p{\bf u}-{\bf v}}$ in }(1-tg({\bf x}))^{p-1}.

Note that the entries of
H
are polynomials of degree
\leq p-1
. For any
{\bf u}\in\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\circ}
define

F_{{\bf u}}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}ct[{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}g({\bf x})^{k}]t^{k}.

We prove in Section
3
the following statement.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
{\bf u}\in\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\circ}
we have

F_{{\bf u}}(t)=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\circ}}H_{{\bf u}{\bf v}}(t)F_{{\bf v}}(t^{p})({\rm mod}\ p).

A simple example for congruences modulo higher powers
p^{r}
is the 2-state

p
-linear scheme modulo
p^{2}
for the sequences
a=2^{k},b_{k}=k2^{k}
.
It follows from the following congruences, which are easily verified for any odd prime
p
,

\displaystyle a_{kp+\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2^{\ell}a_{k}+p\alpha 2^{\ell}b_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{2})

\displaystyle b_{kp+\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ell 2^{\ell}a_{k}+p2^{\ell}(1+\ell\alpha)b_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{2}),

where
\alpha\equiv\frac{2^{p-1}-1}{p}({\rm mod}\ p)
. In general
2^{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})
can be generated by
an
r
-state
p
-linear scheme.

An example of historical interest is Ira Gessel’s mod
p^{2}
congruence for the Apéry numbers

A_{k}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}^{2}\binom{m+k}{m}^{2}
in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Gessel, 1982)
.

Define for all integers
k\geq 0
,

A^{\prime}_{k}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}^{2}\binom{m+k}{m}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{m+1}+\cdots+\frac{1}{k}\right).

Then, for any prime
p
and any integers
k\geq 0
and
\ell=0,1,\ldots,p-1
we have

A_{kp+\ell}\equiv A_{\ell}A_{k}+pA^{\prime}_{\ell}kA_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{2}).

Multiply this result with
kp+\ell
to get

(kp+\ell)A_{kp+\ell}\equiv\ell A_{\ell}A_{k}+p(A_{\ell}+\ell A^{\prime}_{\ell})kA_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{2}).

So the sequences
A_{k},kA_{k}
form the two states of a
p
-linear scheme modulo
p^{2}
.

Suppose again that
g({\bf x})
is any Laurent polynomial in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and coefficients
in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. The following statement is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1

and Remark
3.2
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
p
a prime. Choose an integer
\rho
such that
\rho-\lceil\rho/p\rceil\geq r-1
.
Then the numbers
g_{k}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})
can be generated by a
p
-linear scheme with
s
states, where

s
is bounded by
\rho
times the number of lattice points in
\rho\Delta^{\circ}
.

Note that if
r=1
we can take
\rho=1
. Since
g_{0}=1
we recover Theorem
1.1

if
{\bf 0}
is the unique lattice point in
\Delta^{\circ}
.
Notice also that
\rho
can always be chosen such that
\rho\leq 2r
.

Our second result concerns coefficients of power series expansions for rational functions.
Let
P({\bf x}),Q({\bf x})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

P_{0}:=P({\bf 0})\neq 0
. Then we

. Some typical examples,

-

The diagonal elements of
(1-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{n})^{-1}
are
\frac{(kn)!}{(k!)^{n}}
.

-

The diagonal elements of
((1-x_{1}-x_{2})(1-x_{3}-x_{4})-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4})^{-1}
are
the Apéry numbers introduced above. See
[
9
]
.

In
[
11
]
we find a long list of further examples, with further references to
Sloane’s Online Encyclopedia.

In Theorem

we construct a

-linear scheme to compute the coefficients

. This time the coefficients

have

. However, it is straightforward to replace
{\bf a}_{k}
by

in Definition

. Here is a consequence of

which also follows from
[

with

.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let notations be as above and suppose that
Q({\bf x})=1
,
P_{0}=1
and the degree of

in
each variable is
1
. Then, for every prime

,

{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and

we have

a_{p{\bf k}+\bm{\ell}}\equiv a_{\bm{\ell}}a_{{\bf k}}({\rm mod}\ p).

To prove this, apply Corollary

with

(hence

)
and

. Then we get a 1-state

]

2.
Main tool

The starting point in this paper is a Laurent polynomial
f
in the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

and coefficients in a ring
R
which is either
{\mathbb{Z}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
. Let
p
be any prime and let

\sigma
be the Frobius lift on
R
, which is the identity map on
{\mathbb{Z}}
and which maps
t

to
t^{p}
in the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
.

Let
\mu
be a bounded polytope in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with a finite set of proper faces removed and such that

\Delta^{\circ}\subset\mu
. The original polytope is denoted by
\overline{\mu}
, the closure
of
\mu
.
In
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
we study the action of the Cartier
operator
\mathscr{C}_{p}
on the
R
-module
\Omega_{f}(\mu)

of rational functions of the form
(r-1)!A({\bf x})/f({\bf x})^{r}
, where
A({\bf x})
is a Laurent
polynomial with coefficients in
R
and support in
r\mu
. In Section
3

we use
\mu=\Delta^{\circ}
and in Section
4
we use an extension of
\Delta^{\circ}
,
the so-called box closure.
Note that in
[
1
]
we use only
\mu
such that
\overline{\mu}=\Delta
.
The main property of these sets is the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a bounded polytope with a finite number of proper subfaces removed. Denote the original
polytope by
\overline{\mu}
. Then, for all positive integers
a,b
we have

a\mu+b\overline{\mu}\subset(a+b)\mu.

Here we use the notation
X+Y=\{{\bf x}+{\bf y}|{\bf x}\in X,{\bf y}\in Y\}
for any two sets
X,Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and

kX
the
k
-fold sum of
k
copies of
X
.

Suppose we are given an infinite Laurent
series
\sum_{{\bf k}}a_{{\bf k}}{\bf x}^{{\bf k}}
,
a_{{\bf k}}\in R
, with support in a positive cone
with
{\bf 0}
as top. Then
\mathscr{C}_{p}
is defined by

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\sum_{{\bf k}}a_{{\bf k}}{\bf x}^{{\bf k}}\right)=\sum_{{\bf k}}a_{p{\bf k}}{\bf x}^{{\bf k}}.

The action of
\mathscr{C}_{p}
on a rational function
R({\bf x})\in\Omega_{f}(\mu)
is obtained
by expansion of
R({\bf x})
in an infinite Laurent series and then application of

\mathscr{C}_{p}
. Unfortunately the image is a Laurent series which usually does not come from
an element in
\Omega_{f}(\mu)
.
Fortunately, in
[
1
]
it is shown that the image does lie in the
p
-adic completion
of
\Omega_{f^{\sigma}}(\mu)
. Here
f^{\sigma}
is simply
f
with
\sigma
applied to its
coefficients.

In the present paper we will only need a mod
p^{r}
version of this fact and for its
derivation we need the following easy lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K({\bf x}),L({\bf x})
be two infinite Laurent series in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
supported in
the same cone with top
{\bf 0}
. Then,

\mathscr{C}_{p}(K({\bf x}^{p})L({\bf x}))=K({\bf x})\mathscr{C}_{p}(L({\bf x})).

Let
r
be a positive integer and consider the
module of rational functions
\frac{A({\bf x})}{f({\bf x})^{r}}
with support of
A({\bf x})
in
r\mu

by
M(r)
. By
M^{\sigma}(r)
we denote module generated by the elements of
M(r)
where we have applied
\sigma
to all coefficients of the rational functions.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
r
be a positive integer and
p
a prime. Choose an integer
\rho
such that

\rho-\lceil\rho/p\rceil\geq r-1
. Then the Cartier operator
\mathscr{C}_{p}
modulo
p^{r}

maps
M(\rho)
to
M^{\sigma}(\rho)
.

Proof.

Rewrite
A({\bf x})/f({\bf x})^{\rho}
as

A({\bf x})f({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho}/f({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil}
.
Then note that

f({\bf x})^{p}=f^{\sigma}({\bf x}^{p})-pG({\bf x})
for some Laurent polynomial
G

with coefficients in
R
and support in
p\Delta
. Then we use the
p
-adic expansion

\frac{A({\bf x})}{f({\bf x})^{\rho}}=\frac{A({\bf x})f({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho}}{(f^{\sigma}({\bf x}^{p})-pG({\bf x}))^{\lceil\rho/p\rceil}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}p^{m}{\lceil\rho/p\rceil+m-1\choose m}\frac{G({\bf x})^{m}}{f^{\sigma}({\bf x}^{p})^{m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil}}A({\bf x})f({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho}.

Apply
\mathscr{C}_{p}
. We find that

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{A({\bf x})}{f({\bf x})^{\rho}}\right)=\sum_{m\geq 0}p^{m}{\lceil\rho/p\rceil+m-1\choose m}\frac{Q_{m}({\bf x})}{f^{\sigma}({\bf x})^{m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil}},

where
Q_{m}({\bf x})=\mathscr{C}_{p}(A({\bf x})G({\bf x})^{m}f({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho})
. The support of

A({\bf x})G({\bf x})^{m}f({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho}
is contained in

\rho\mu+mp\Delta+(p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho)\Delta\subset\rho\mu+(mp+p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho)\overline{\mu}.

According to Lemma
2.1
this is contained in
(mp+p\lceil\rho/p\rceil)\mu
,
hence the support of
Q_{m}
lies in
(m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil)\mu
. Its coefficients are still in
R
.
The terms with
m\geq r
all vanish modulo
p^{r}
. Hence

(3)

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{A({\bf x})}{f({\bf x})^{\rho}}\right)\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{r-1}p^{m}{\lceil\rho/p\rceil+m-1\choose m}\frac{Q_{m}({\bf x})}{f^{\sigma}({\bf x})^{m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

Since
m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil\leq r-1+\lceil\rho/p\rceil\leq\rho
we see that modulo
p^{r}
the
right hand side is in
M^{\sigma}(\rho)
.
∎

3.
Constant terms of powers of Laurent polynomials

As a warm up to the proof of Theorem
3.1
we give a simple proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let notations and assumptions be as in Theorem
1.1
. Consider the equality

\frac{1}{1-tg({\bf x})}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}t^{k}g({\bf x})^{k}}{1-t^{p}g({\bf x})^{p}}\equiv\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}t^{k}g({\bf x})^{k}}{1-t^{p}g({\bf x}^{p})}({\rm mod}\ p).

Expand on both sides in a power series in
t
with Laurent polynomials in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

as coefficients. Apply the Cartier operator on both sides. We get

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-tg({\bf x})}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{1-t^{p}g({\bf x})}\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}t^{k}g({\bf x})^{k}\right)({\rm mod}\ p).

Take any monomial
{\bf x}^{{\bf w}}
in
\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}t^{k}g({\bf x})^{k}
. In order to survive
\mathscr{C}_{p}

we must have
{\bf w}=p{\bf v}
for some lattice point
{\bf v}
. Note that
p{\bf v}\in(p-1)\Delta
,
hence
{\bf v}\in\Delta^{\circ}
. By our assumption on the lattice points in
\Delta^{\circ}
,
{\bf v}={\bf 0}
.
We obtain

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{1}{1-tg({\bf x})}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{1-t^{p}g({\bf x})}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}g_{k}t^{k}({\rm mod}\ p).

Take the constant term in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
on both sides. We get

\sum_{\ell\geq 0}g_{\ell}t^{\ell}\equiv\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}g_{k}t^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}g_{m}t^{pm}\right)({\rm mod}\ p).

By comparison of the coefficients of
t^{\ell}=t^{pm+k}
on both sides we get
g_{pm+k}\equiv g_{k}g_{m}({\rm mod}\ p)
,
which proves our theorem.
∎

We now switch to the general situation.
We apply Proposition
2.3
to construct
p
-linear schemes for sequences of
the form

\text{ct}[q({\bf x})g({\bf x})^{k}]({\rm mod}\ p^{r}),

where ’ct’ is the operation of selecting the
constant term and
q({\bf x}),g({\bf x})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
are Laurent polynomials.
Let
f({\bf x})=1-tg({\bf x})
and
\mu=\Delta^{\circ}
, the euclidean interior of
the Newton polytope
\Delta
.
Let
p
be a prime,
r
a positive integer and
\rho
an
integer such that
r-1\leq\rho-\lceil\rho/p\rceil
. For any monomial

{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}\in\rho\Delta^{\circ}
expand

{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}/f({\bf x})^{\rho}
as power series in
t
and take the constant term of each
coefficient. We get a power series

F_{{\bf u}}(t):=\text{ct}\left[\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}}{(1-tg({\bf x}))^{\rho}}\right]=\sum_{k\geq 0}{\rho+k-1\choose\rho}\text{ct}[{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}g({\bf x})^{k}]t^{k},

which is the generating series of the numbers
{\rho+k-1\choose\rho}\text{ct}[{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}g({\bf x})^{k}]
.
The functions
t^{\ell}F_{{\bf u}}(t)
with
\ell=0,1,\ldots,\rho-1
and
{\bf u}\in\rho\Delta^{\circ}

will be the states of our
p
-linear scheme. Usually we are interested in
sequences of the form
\text{ct}[g({\bf x})^{k}]
, which correspond to the constant
term of
1/f({\bf x})
. However, we can use the observation

\frac{1}{f({\bf x})}=\frac{f({\bf x})^{\rho-1}}{f({\bf x})^{\rho}}.

So the constant term of
1/f({\bf x})({\rm mod}\ p^{r})
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear combination of
the states
t^{\ell}F_{{\bf v}}(t)
with
{\bf v}\in\rho\Delta^{\circ}
and
\ell=0,1,\ldots,\rho-1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p,r,\rho
and
F_{{\bf u}}{t}
be as before.
Then there exist polynomials
\lambda_{{\bf u},\ell,{\bf v},m}(t)
in
t
of degree
<p
such that

t^{\ell}F_{{\bf u}}(t)\equiv\sum_{{\bf v}\in\rho\Delta^{\circ}}\sum_{m=0}^{\rho-1}\lambda_{{\bf u},\ell,{\bf v},m}(t)(t^{p})^{m}F_{{\bf v}}(t^{p})({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

for all
{\bf u}\in(\rho\Delta^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\ell=0,1,\ldots,\rho-1
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that we have found a
p
-linear scheme with states
t^{\ell}F_{{\bf u}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})
,
where
\ell=0,1,\ldots,\rho-1
and
{\bf u}\in\rho\Delta^{\circ}
. The cardinality of

(\rho\Delta^{\circ})_{\mathbb{Z}}
is bounded polynomially in
\rho

of degree
n
. For every prime
p
the choice
\rho=2r
suffices so the number of such
states has order
O(r^{n+1})
, independent of
p
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Consider (
3
) with
A({\bf x})={\bf x}^{{\bf u}}
and expand as

(4)

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}}{f({\bf x})^{\rho}}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{f^{\sigma}({\bf x})^{\rho}}\sum_{{\bf v}\in\rho\mu}q_{{\bf u},{\bf v}}(t){\bf x}^{{\bf v}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

The coefficients
q_{{\bf u},{\bf v}}(t)
are polynomials in
t
and one easily verifies that
their degrees are at most
p(r-1)+p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho
.
Let us choose polynomials
\lambda_{{\bf u},\ell,{\bf v},m}

of degree
<p
such that

t^{\ell}q_{{\bf u},{\bf v}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\rho-1}\lambda_{{\bf u},\ell,{\bf v},m}(t)t^{pm}.

This is possible because the degree of the left hand side is at most

p(r-1)+p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho+\rho-1=p(r-1+\lceil\rho/p\rceil)-1\leq p\rho-1.

The constant terms with respect to
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
on both sides of (
4
)
are equal modulo
p^{r}
and by a straightforward computation our assertion follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Set
r=\rho=1
. Inspection of (
3
) with
A({\bf x})={\bf x}^{{\bf u}}
yields

(5)

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}}{1-tg({\bf x})}\right)\equiv\frac{1}{1-t^{p}g({\bf x})}\mathscr{C}_{p}\left({\bf x}^{{\bf u}}(1-tg({\bf x}))^{p-1}\right)({\rm mod}\ p).

The support of
{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}(1-tg({\bf x}))^{p-1}
is in
p\Delta^{\circ}
and we get

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left({\bf x}^{{\bf u}}(1-tg({\bf x}))^{p-1}\right)=\sum_{{\bf v}\in\Delta^{\circ}_{\mathbb{Z}}}H_{{\bf u}{\bf v}}(t){\bf x}^{{\bf v}}.

We complete the proof of the theorem by expansion of (
5
) as power series
in
t
and taking the constant term with respect to
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
on both sides.
∎

4.
Power series coefficients of rational functions

Our second main application of Proposition
2.3
is to the coefficients
of power series expansions of rational functions.
Let
P
be a polynomial in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
with integer coefficients and a constant
term which is not divisible by the prime
p
. Let us call this constant
P_{0}
.
Let
\Delta
be the Newton polytope of
P
.
For any polynomial
Q
with integer coefficients we can expand
Q/P
as
power series. Let us write

\frac{Q({\bf x})}{P({\bf x})}=\sum_{{\bf k}}a_{{\bf k}}{\bf x}^{{\bf k}},

and note that
a_{{\bf k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[1/P_{0}]
for all
{\bf k}
. We will show that for every
r\geq 1

there is a
p
-linear scheme which generates the numbers
a_{{\bf k}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})
.
In particular this holds for coefficients
a_{k{\bf v}}
with
{\bf v}=(1,1,\ldots,1)

and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. These are the diagonal elements of the power series.

In Theorem
4.1
we use the concept of
box closure
.
For any
{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with non-negative entries we denote

B({\bf x})=[0,x_{1}]\times\cdots\times[0,x_{n}]
where
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
We take
B({\bf x})
empty if
x_{i}<0
for at least one index
i
.
For any subset
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we define its box closure by
B(S)=\cup_{{\bf x}\in S}B({\bf x})
.

For us, the main property of box closures is that for any
\bm{\ell}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
we
have
B(S-\bm{\ell})\subset B(S)
, where
S-\bm{\ell}
is the translate of
S
over the vector
-\bm{\ell}
.

In the following theorem we heavily use
B(\Delta^{\circ})
, where
\Delta
is the Newton polytope of
P
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p
be a prime not dividing
P_{0}
. Choose
\rho
such that
r-1\leq\rho-\lceil\rho/p\rceil
.
For every
{\bf u}\in(\rho B(\Delta^{\circ}))_{\mathbb{Z}}
consider the power series

\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}}{P({\bf x})^{\rho}}=\sum_{{\bf k}}a_{{\bf u},{\bf k}}{\bf x}^{{\bf k}},

where
a_{{\bf u},{\bf k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[1/P_{0}]
. Then there exist
\lambda_{{\bf u},{\bf v},\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

with
{\bf u},{\bf v}\in(\rho B(\Delta^{\circ}))_{\mathbb{Z}}
and

\bm{\ell}\in[0,1,\ldots,p-1]^{n}
such that for every
{\bf k}

we have

a_{{\bf u},p{\bf k}+\bm{\ell}}\equiv\sum_{{\bf v}\in(\rho B(\Delta^{\circ}))_{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{{\bf u},{\bf v},\bm{\ell}}a_{{\bf v},{\bf k}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

Proof.

In this proof we shall use the convention that for any two sets
X,Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
we denote

X+Y=\{{\bf x}+{\bf y}|{\bf x}\in X,{\bf y}\in Y\}
. Furthermore
kX
with
k
a positive integer denotes
the repeated sum of
k
copies of
X
.

We apply (
3
) in Proposition
2.3
with
f=P
and
\mu=B(\Delta^{\circ})
.
For any
\bm{\ell}\in[0,1,\ldots,p-1]^{n}
and any
{\bf u}\in\rho B(\Delta^{\circ})
we have

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf u}-\bm{\ell}}}{P({\bf x})^{\rho}}\right)\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{r-1}p^{m}\binom{\lceil\rho/p\rceil+m-1}{\rho}\frac{Q_{m}({\bf x})}{P({\bf x})^{m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

with

Q_{m}({\bf x})=\mathscr{C}_{p}\left({\bf x}^{{\bf u}-\bm{\ell}}G({\bf x})^{m}P({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho}\right).

In the present context we have that
G({\bf x})
is
P({\bf x}^{p})-P({\bf x})^{p}

divided by
p
. The terms in

{\bf x}^{{\bf u}-\bm{\ell}}G({\bf x})^{m}P({\bf x})^{p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho}
have their support in

(6)

\{{\bf u}\}+\{-\bm{\ell}\}+pm\Delta+(p\lceil\rho/p\rceil-\rho)\Delta\subset\{-\bm{\ell}\}+p(m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil)B(\Delta^{\circ}).

Here we applied Lemma
2.1
with
\mu=B(\Delta^{\circ}),{\bf u}\in\rho\mu
and
\Delta\subset\overline{\mu}
.
The operator
\mathscr{C}_{p}
selects the terms whose support belongs to the set (
6
)
and which are divisible by
p
.
Since the components of
\bm{\ell}
are all
\leq p-1
such support vectors have non-negative coordinates.
By the main property of box closures the points in
\{-\bm{\ell}\}+p(m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil)B(\Delta^{\circ})

with non-negative coordinates is contained in
p(m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil)B(\Delta^{\circ})
.

Hence we conclude that the support of
Q_{m}
is in
(m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil)B(\Delta^{\circ})
. Note that by our choice of

\rho
we have
m+\lceil\rho/p\rceil\leq r-1+\lceil\rho/p\rceil\leq\rho
. Consequently there exist coefficients

\lambda_{{\bf u},{\bf v},\bm{\ell}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\mathscr{C}_{p}\left(\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf u}-\bm{\ell}}}{P({\bf x})^{\rho}}\right)\equiv\sum_{{\bf v}\in(\rho B(\Delta^{\circ}))_{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{{\bf u},{\bf v},\bm{\ell}}\frac{{\bf x}^{{\bf v}}}{P({\bf x})^{\rho}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r}).

The theorem follows by taking the power series expansion of both sides and then
selection of the coefficient of
{\bf x}^{{\bf k}}
on both sides.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
a_{{\bf k}}
be as in the beginning of this section and suppose that the numerator
Q
has its
support in
B(\Delta^{\circ})
. Let
d_{i}
be the degree of
P

in the variable
x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Let
r
be a positive integer and
\rho
an
integer such that
r-1\leq\rho-\lceil\rho/p\rceil
.
Then there is a
p
-linear scheme, with at most
\rho^{n}d_{1}\cdots d_{n}
states, which
generates the numbers
a_{{\bf k}}({\rm mod}\ p^{r})
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
4.1
. Note that the lattice points in
\rho B(\Delta^{\circ})

are contained in the box
[0,\rho d_{1}-1]\times\cdots\times[0,\rho d_{n}-1]
.
Hence the number of lattice points is bounded above by
\rho^{n}d_{1}\cdots d_{n}
.
One easily checks that
Q/P
can be written as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear combination
of
{\bf x}^{{\bf u}}/P^{\rho}
with
{\bf u}\in(\rho B(\Delta^{\circ}))_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

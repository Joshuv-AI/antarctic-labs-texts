Physics over a finite field and Wick rotation
B.Zilber

Abstract
The paper develops some mathematics supporting an earlier hypothesis that the physical universe is a finite system co-ordinatised by a huge finite field F 𝔭 \mathrm{F}_{\mathfrak{p}} which looks like the field of complex numbers to an observer. Earlier we constructed a place (’limit’ homomorphism) 𝗅𝗆 \mathsf{lm} from a pseudo-finite field F 𝔭 \mathrm{F}_{\mathfrak{p}} onto the compactified field of complex numbers. In the current paper we construct 𝗅𝗆 \mathsf{lm} in a more concrete form. In particular, 𝗅𝗆 \mathsf{lm} sends certain multiplicative subgroups ℝ + ′ ′ {}^{\prime}{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}_{+} and 𝕊 ′ ′ {}^{\prime}\mathbb{S}^{\prime} of F 𝔭 \mathrm{F}_{\mathfrak{p}} onto the non-negative reals ℝ + {\mathbb{R}}_{+} and the unit circle 𝕊 \mathbb{S} in ℂ . {\mathbb{C}}. Thus F 𝔭 , \mathrm{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}, ℝ + ′ ′ {}^{\prime}{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}_{+} and 𝕊 ′ ′ {}^{\prime}\mathbb{S}^{\prime} provide co-ordinates for physical universe. We introduce two systems of natural units corresponding to ℝ + ′ ′ {}^{\prime}{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}_{+} and 𝕊 ′ ′ , {}^{\prime}\mathbb{S}^{\prime}, respectively, on the logarithmic scale. The passage from the scale of units of ℝ + ′ ′ {}^{\prime}{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}_{+} to the scale of units of 𝕊 ′ ′ {}^{\prime}\mathbb{S}^{\prime} corresponds to a multiplication (on the logarithmic scale) by a ’huge’ (non-standard) integer 𝔦 \mathfrak{i} equal approximately to 𝔭 . \sqrt{\mathfrak{p}}. This provides an explanation to the phenomenon of Wick rotation. In the same model we explain the phenomenon of phase transition in a large finite system

For every

4.6

Corollary.

\mathsf{lm}_{\mathrm{F}}:\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{n\in\mathrm{I}}\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}(-a\frac{n^{2}}{2\mathfrak{l}}\mathbf{u})\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ax^{2}}dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}

\mathsf{lm}_{\mathrm{F}}:\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{n\in\mathrm{I}}\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}(-a\frac{n^{2}}{2\mathfrak{l}}\mathbf{v})\mapsto\lim_{l\to\infty}\int_{-l}^{l}e^{-iax^{2}}dx=\frac{e^{\frac{\pi}{4}}}{\sqrt{a}}

Proof. In both cases the right-hand side is the limit of integrals in
4.5
since
\mathrm{I}=\bigcup_{l}\mathrm{I}_{l}.
In the first case the classical Gaussian integral over the whole of
{\mathbb{R}}
converges and is equal to the limit. In the second case the right-hand side as the limit of the Fresnel integral is well-defined but the Riemann integral is not.

4.7

Discussion
. The left-hand sides of
4.2
and of
4.6
differ in the domains of summations but the right-had sides are the same. This implies that our definition of
\mathsf{lm}_{\mathrm{F}}
is such that

\mathsf{lm}_{\mathrm{F}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{n\in\mathrm{I}_{a,\mathbf{u}}\setminus\mathrm{I}}\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}(-a\frac{n^{2}}{2\mathfrak{l}}\mathbf{u})\right)=0\mbox{ and }\mathsf{lm}_{\mathrm{F}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\sum_{n\in\mathrm{I}_{a,\mathbf{v}}\setminus\mathrm{I}}\exp_{\mathfrak{p}}(-a\frac{n^{2}}{2\mathfrak{l}}\mathbf{v})\right)=0

In these “tail domains”
\mathrm{I}_{a,\mathbf{u}}\setminus\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{I}_{a,\mathbf{v}}\setminus\mathrm{I}
the respective values under exponentiation are very large, non-feasible numbers, and according to the inerpretation of
\mathrm{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
in
{\mathbb{C}}
the application of
\exp
to such values oscillates uncontrollably. R.Feynman intuition was that for this reason the sum should be considered negligible, see e.g.
[
13
]
, 2-3.

Monodromy of the families of del Pezzo and K ​ 3 K3 surfaces branching over smooth quartic curves
Adán Medrano Martín del Campo

Abstract
Two families of surfaces arise from considering cyclic branched covers of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} over smooth quartic curves. These consist of degree 2 del Pezzo surfaces with a ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} action and K ​ 3 K3 surfaces with a ℤ / 4 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} action. We compute the monodromy groups of both families. In the first case, we obtain the Weyl group W ⁡ ( E 7 ) W\left(E_{7}\right) , corresponding to the automorphisms of the 56 56 lines contained in a degree 2 2 del Pezzo surface. In the second case we obtain an arithmetic lattice: the unitary group U ⁡ ( h L − ) U\left(h_{L_{-}}\right) of a type ( 1 , 6 ) \left(1,6\right) quadratic form over ℤ ⁡ [ i ] \mathbb{Z}\left[i\right] by building on results of Kondo and Allcock, Carlson, Toledo.

Introduction

Given a smooth degree
d

C

\mathbb{P}^{2}
and an integer
k\mid d

k
cyclic branch cover
S
of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
branching over it. A natural, often difficult, question that arises from this construction is to determine the monodromy representation of the family of surfaces it induces. This has been extensively studied in the case of universal families of a given type of varieties. By considering subfamilies of the universal ones and computing their monodromy representation, one obtains a better global picture of the one associated to the universal family, as well as of its acting fundamental group (see, e.g.
[
McM13
]
). In this paper we compute the monodromy of branched covers of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
whose branch locus is a smooth quartic curve.

The parameter space of homogeneous degree
d

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{Sym}^{d}\left(\mathbb{C}^{3}\right)\right)=\mathbb{P}^{N\left(d\right)}\quad\text{where }N\left(d\right)=\frac{d\left(d+3\right)}{2}.

The

\mathbb{P}^{2}

\mathcal{U}_{d}=\mathbb{P}^{N\left(d\right)}\setminus\Delta_{d}.

Let

be its vanishing locus. Then

\mathbb{P}^{2}

is a complex codimension
1

\mathbb{P}^{2}
, so there exists a cyclic
k

X

\mathbb{P}^{2}
with branched locus equal to

is a multiple of

. It is a classical result (see

The action of

, namely:

L_{+}=\left\{x\in H^{2}\left(X;\mathbb{Z}\right)\mid T^{2}x=x\right\}\quad\quad\quad L_{-}=\left\{x\in H^{2}\left(X;\mathbb{Z}\right)\mid T^{2}x=-x\right\}.

As discussed in

and discriminant group (or

[

]
) isomorphic to
\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\oplus 8}
and the action of
T
on this group is induced by the Geiser involution on
\mathcal{P}
. By considering the branched cover
X\to\mathcal{P}
, we obtain the pullback map

H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)\to H^{2}\left(X;\mathbb{Z}\right)

and
L_{+}
is then generated by the pullbacks of generators of
H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)
. As discussed in Section 4 of
[
AS11
]
, the action
T
is purely non-symplectic, meaning that
T\omega=\pm i\omega
on the invariant line
H^{2,0}\left(X;\mathbb{C}\right)
. The Néron-Severi, or Picard, group
\text{NS}\left(X\right):=H^{2}\left(X;\mathbb{Z}\right)\cap H^{1,1}\left(X;\mathbb{C}\right)
of a generic fiber
X
is then

\text{Pic}\left(X\right)\cong\text{NS}\left(X\right)\cong L_{+}\cong\left\langle 2\right\rangle\oplus A_{1}^{\oplus 7}.

This determines the Hodge decomposition on
L_{\pm}\otimes\mathbb{C}
since
L_{+}\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset H^{1,1}\left(X;\mathbb{C}\right)
and therefore
L_{-}\otimes\mathbb{C}
contains the lines
H^{2,0}\left(X;\mathbb{C}\right),H^{0,2}\left(X;\mathbb{C}\right)
, hence
L_{-}\otimes\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{12}\oplus\mathbb{C}
.

The monodromy map
\rho_{2}
then determines the action of
\rho_{4}
on
L_{+}
, and it remains to study the action of
\rho_{4}
on
L_{-}
. To do this, we observe that the action of
\rho_{4}
commutes with the deck group action
T
, therefore having its image lie in the centralizer
C_{L_{-}}\left(T\right)
of
T
in
L_{-}
. Since
L_{-}
is equipped with an action
T
satisfying
T^{2}+I=0
, this centralizer can be regarded as a
\mathbb{Z}\left[i\right]
-module. Following
[
All02
]
and
[

]

\mathbb{Z}\left[i\right]

L_{-}\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[i\right]^{7},h_{L_{-}}\right)

where
h_{L_{-}}
is quadratic form of signature
\left(1,6\right)
given by (see
5.4
)

h_{L_{-}}=-2\left(\left|z_{0}\right|^{2}+\left|z_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|z_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|z_{3}\right|^{2}+\left|z_{4}\right|^{2}-\Re\left({z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}}+{z_{3}\overline{z_{4}}}+{z_{5}\overline{z_{6}}}\right)-\Im\left({z_{1}\overline{z_{2}}}+{z_{3}\overline{z_{4}}}+{z_{5}\overline{z_{6}}}\right)\right)

Since
\rho_{4}
preserves the intersection form on
L_{-}
, and therefore the hermitian form
h_{L_{-}}
, it follows that

\text{Im}\left(\rho_{4}\mid_{L_{-}}\right)\subseteq U\left(h_{L_{-}}\right).

Using a result by
[
Kon00
]
on the characterization of the moduli space of smooth quartic curves as a ball complement quotient, we show that these two groups coincide. Analogous results have been used to study the monodromy and fundamental group of cubic surfaces (see, e.g.
[
Lib78
]
). Furthermore, as explained in
Section

4.2
, the lattices
L_{\pm}
are pairwise orthogonal and therefore primitive within
H^{2}\left(X;\mathbb{Z}\right)
. By virtue of the relation between the actions of
\rho_{4}
on the discriminant groups of
L_{\pm}

\rho_{4}
acts trivially on
L_{-}
then it follows that the action on
L_{+}
is trivial too. This implies that the monodromy map
\rho_{4}
is completely determined by its action on the sublattice

Theorem 1.3
.

The monodromy representation

\rho_{4}:\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)\to U\left(h_{L_{-}}\right)

(4,4)

\mathbb{P}^{2}

Degree
2
del Pezzo Surfaces

2.1
General facts

Degree
2
del Pezzo surfaces are realized as blow-ups of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at
7
points in general position. Given a degree
2
del Pezzo surface
\mathcal{P}
, its anticanonical linear system
|-K_{\mathcal{P}}|
defines a rational map
p:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
which has as branch locus a smooth quartic curve
V\left(f\right)
. Such surface
\mathcal{P}
contains exactly
56
exceptional divisors corresponding to the pullbakcs of the
28
bitangents of
V\left(f\right)
. These
56
divisors can be labeled as follows:

Let
P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{7}
be the seven points at which
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is blown up to obtain
\mathcal{P}
and let
L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{7}
be the exceptional divisors corresponding to the blow-ups at these points. Let
e_{i}
be the Poincaré dual of each divisor
L_{i}
and
e_{0}=p^{\ast}\left(\text{PD}\left[H\right]\right)
be the Poincaré dual of the pullback of the hyperplane class
\left[H\right]
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then the
56
exceptional divisors contained in
\mathcal{P}
have corresponding cohomology classes given by

\displaystyle\left[L_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=e_{i}

\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq 7

\displaystyle\left[L_{i}^{\ast}\right]

\displaystyle=3e_{0}-e_{i}-(e_{1}+e_{2}+\cdots+e_{7})

\displaystyle\left[L_{i,j}\right]

\displaystyle=e_{0}-e_{i}-e_{j}

\displaystyle 1\leq i<j\leq 7

\displaystyle\left[L_{i,j}^{\ast}\right]

\displaystyle=2e_{0}+e_{i}+e_{j}-(e_{1}+e_{2}+\cdots+e_{7})

These
56
classes span
H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)
, whose intersection form with respect to the basis
\left\{e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{7}\right\}
is

\displaystyle\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{P}}:H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)

\displaystyle\times H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)\to\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathbf{a}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-I_{7}\\
\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{b}.

2.2
The Gosset and Schläfli graphs

As explained in Section
8
of
[
Dol12
]
, the classes of the
56
exceptional divisors of
\mathcal{P}
are in correspondence with the vertices of the Gosset graph, where two vertices share an edge if and only if the inner product of the corresponding cohomology classes is equal to
1
. Moreover, the induced subgraph given by the vertices adjacent to a given vertex form a graph isomorphic to the Schläfli graph, which gives the intersection pattern of the
27
lines contained in a smooth cubic surface. The automorphim group of the Schläfli and Gosset graphs are the Weyl groups
W\left(E_{6}\right)
and
W\left(E_{7}\right)
respectively. A depiction of both graphs is shown in
Figure

1
.

This phenomenon is fundamentally related to the following geometric construction in
[
Har79
]
. A degree
2
del Pezzo surface
\mathcal{P}
may be regarded as a blow-up of a cubic surface
S
over a point
Q
not contained in any of its
27
lines. The projections of the strict transforms of the
27
lines contained in
S
and the exceptional divisor over
Q
via
p:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
are in correspondence with the
28
bitangents of the quartic curve
V\left(f\right)\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Moreover, the
56
exceptional divisors contained in
\mathcal{P}
lie over each bitangent of
V\left(f\right)
in pairs. Each pair of such exceptional divisors has inner product
2
with respect to the intersection form in
H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)
and they are precisely the pairs

\left\{\left(\left[L_{i}\right],\left[L_{i}^{\ast}\right]\right)\mid 1\leq i\leq 7\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\{\left(\left[L_{i,j}\right],\left[L_{i,j}^{\ast}\right]\right)\mid 1\leq i<j\leq 7\right\}.

We call each pair of such lines
dual
and we denote the dual of a line
L
by
L^{\ast}
. Let
L_{Q}
be the exceptional divisor over
Q
,
\mathcal{S}
be the set of strict transforms of the
27
lines in the cubic surface
S
, and
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
be the set of dual lines to those in
\mathcal{S}
. A direct computation shows that

\left\langle\left[L_{Q}\right],\left[L\right]\right\rangle_{\mathcal{P}}=\begin{cases}0&L\in\mathcal{S}\\
1&L\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}\end{cases}

Moreover, the intersection pattern of the lines in
\mathcal{S}
and in
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
is given by the Schläfli graph. Hence, for any line
L
contained in
\mathcal{P}
we may define the following sets of
27
lines associated to
L
:

\mathcal{S}_{L}=\left\{L^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}\mid\left\langle\left[L\right],\left[L^{\prime}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathcal{P}}=0\right\}\quad\quad\quad\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{L}=\left\{L^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{P}\mid\left\langle\left[L\right],\left[L^{\prime}\right]\right\rangle_{\mathcal{P}}=1\right\}

where
\mathcal{S}_{L^{\ast}}=\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{L}
. With this notation, we also have that

L\in\mathcal{S}_{L^{\prime}}\iff L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{L}\quad\text{ and }\quad L\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{L^{\prime}}\iff L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{L}.

3
Computing
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)

In this section we prove
1.1
, namely, we show that

\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}:\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)\to\text{Aut}\left(H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)\right)\right)\cong W\left(E_{7}\right).

The proof consists of
3
main steps: computing the stabilizer of a line
L
in
\mathcal{P}
, restricting the
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
and showing that
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
acts transitively on th
56
lines contained in
\mathcal{P}
.

3.1
Stabilizer of a line
L

In
[
Har79
]
, Harris studies the monodromy groups of the
28
bitangents of a smooth quartic and the
27
lines in a smooth cubic surface are studied. It is a classical result of Klein and Jordan (see
[
Har79
]
for a proof) that the monodromy group of
27
lines in a cubic surface
S
is isomorphic to

W\left(E_{6}\right)\cong O^{-}_{6}\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right).

The monodromy group of the
28
bitangents of a smooth quartic curve
V\left(f\right)
is isomorphic to

W\left(E_{7}\right)^{+}\cong O_{6}\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)\cong\text{Sp}_{6}\left(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)

and moreover, we have that
W\left(E_{7}\right)\cong W\left(E_{7}\right)^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. It follows that an element of the monodromy group of the
28
bitangents of a quartic curve that fixes one bitangent is determined by its monodromy action on the
27
lines on
S
corresponding to the remaining bitangents. It is then shown in
[
Har79
]
that automorphisms of the
27
lines in
S
preserving a set of
6
of these lines generate
W\left(E_{6}\right)
and can be realized as the monodromy action given by a path in the parameter space of smooth quartic curves
\mathcal{U}_{4}
, implying the following key lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(Harris,
[
Har79
]
, Section II.4)
.

The stabilizer of any bitangent in the monodromy group of the
28
bitangents of a smooth quartic curve is isomorphic to
W\left(E_{6}\right)
.

Now we turn to the monodromy action of interest on the
56
lines in
\mathcal{P}
, proving the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

The stabilizer in
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
of any line in
\mathcal{P}
is isomorphic to
W\left(E_{6}\right)
.

Proof.

Suppose
g\in\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)
lies in the stabilizer of a line
L
in
\mathcal{P}
. Then
g
must lie in the stabilizer of its dual line
L^{\ast}
. Moreover, since the intersection form is preserved by the monodromy action, the set
\mathcal{S}_{L}
must be permuted, and its permutation completely determines that of
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{L}
, hence the action of
g
. The automorphism group of
\mathcal{S}_{L}
is then isomorphic to the automorphism group of their underlying bitangents, and by
3.1
this is precisely the stabilizer of the bitangent lying under
L
, which is isomorphic to
W\left(E_{6}\right)
.
∎

3.2
Restricting
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)

We proceed now to reduce
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
to two possibilities with the aid of the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
is contained in
W\left(E_{7}\right)
.

Proof.

We have seen that the automorphism group of the Gosset graph is isomorphic to
W\left(E_{7}\right)
and the intersection pattern of the
56
lines in
\mathcal{P}
is given by this graph. Since the monodromy action preserves the intersection form in
H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)
and the classes of the
56
lines in
\mathcal{P}
generate
H^{2}\left(\mathcal{P};\mathbb{Z}\right)
, it follows that the monodromy action of any element in
\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)
is determined by an automorphism of the Gosset graph, thus giving the desired restriction.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
contains
W\left(E_{7}\right)^{+}
.

Proof.

The action of
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
on the
56
lines in
\mathcal{P}
preserves the
28
pairs of lines lying over each bitangent of
V\left(f\right)
. Recall that the automorphism group of the
28
bitangents of
V\left(f\right)
is isomorphic to
W\left(E_{7}\right)^{+}
. As noted in
3.2
above, any such automorphism is realized by an element of
\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)
, which in turn induces an automorphism of the
28
pairs of lines in
\mathcal{P}
. This implies the desired contention.
∎

Since
W\left(E_{7}\right)^{+}
is a subgroup of index
2
in
W\left(E_{7}\right)
,
3.3
and
3.4
imply the following:

Proposition 3.5
.

\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
is isomorphic either to
W\left(E_{7}\right)^{+}
or to
W\left(E_{7}\right)
.

3.3
Transitivity on the lines contained in
\mathcal{P}

Finally, we see
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
acts transitively on the
56
lines, which will allow us to conclude our proof.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any line
L
contained in
\mathcal{P}
, the group
\text{Im}\left(\rho_{2}\right)
acts transitively on
\mathcal{S}_{L}
and on
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}_{L}
.

Proof.

By
3.2
, the stabilizer of
L
is isomorphic to

.

Proposition 5.11
.

The natural map
q_{\ast}:\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}\right)
induced by the quotient
q
is surjective.

Proof.

Consider the subvariety
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{M}_{3}
of genus
3
curves with a nontrivial automorphism group. Then
\mathcal{O}
is the union of irreducible components for each topological type of faithful finite group action on a genus
3
surface
\Sigma_{3}
. Using the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, the dimension over
\mathbb{C}
of each component is at most
5
, with equality only holding for the hyperelliptic locus
\mathscr{H}_{3}
. In particular, the remaining components distinct of
\mathscr{H}_{3}
lie within
\mathcal{M}_{3}
and are of dimension at most
4
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{3}
is
6
-dimensional, we have that

\text{codim}_{\mathcal{M}_{3}}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\geq 2\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{codim}_{\mathcal{U}_{{4}}}\left(q^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)\geq 2.

By excising
q^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)
from
\mathcal{U}_{4}
we obtain a
PGL_{3}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
fibration

q:\mathcal{U}_{4}-q^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\to\mathcal{M}_{3}-\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right).

The long homotopy exact sequence induced by this fibration is given by

\cdots\to\pi_{1}\left(PGL_{3}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}-q^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}-\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)\to\pi_{0}\left(PGL_{3}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\right)=1

implying that the map
\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}-q^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}-\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)
is surjective. Furthermore, we have

\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}\right)\cong\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}-\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)\cong\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}-q^{-1}\left(\mathcal{O}-\mathscr{H}_{3}\right)\right)

since the excised spaces are of codimension at least
2
. Therefore,
q_{\ast}:\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{U}_{4}\right)\to\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}\right)
is surjective.
∎

The composition of the maps
\mu\circ q_{\ast}
gives precisely the monodromy map
\rho_{4}^{-}
.
5.10
and
5.11
combined give surjectivity of
\rho_{4}^{-}
, concluding our computation.

Perturbations and stability conditions of k-essence and kinetic gravity braiding models in two-field measure theory
R. Cordero 1, J. De-Santiago 2,3, O. G. Miranda 3, M. Serrano-Crivelli 1

Abstract
We study cosmological perturbations for k-essence and kinetic gravity braiding models in the context of the two-field measure theory (TMT). Considering scalar perturbations and the uniform field gauge, we obtain the sound speed of the fields and present a stability analysis by means of the kinetic matrix and the mass eigenvalues. For k-essence models, in the two-field measure theory, the speed of propagation of the field is modified completely due to the new measure field and it gives rise to crucial differences with respect to the case without new measure. The stability analysis gives a physical viable model for the Universe. For the kinetic gravity braiding models in the two-field measure theory we get that, in general, the speed of perturbations is equal to the speed of light which is a consequence of the properties of the new measure field. In the later case, there is always a ghost field. Furthermore, we calculate general expressions for the mass eigenvalues and find, for an explicit example, the existence of tachyonic instabilities.

II
The two field measure theory

In this Section we describe the main features of TMT, that we will use throughout this work, following the discussions presented in
Guendelman and
Kaganovich 2006b
;
Cordero et al. 2019
. In this theory, the action has two measures: the term with the usual metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and an extra term with a new measure
\Phi
, in the same space-time,

S=\int\mathcal{L}_{1}\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{2}\Phi d^{4}x.

(3)

It is possible to construct the new measure using four scalar
fields,
\xi^{a}
(
a=1,2,3,4
),

\Phi=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ijkl}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{i}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{j}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{k}\partial_{\beta}\xi^{l}.

(4)

In the present work we consider the Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
\mathcal{L}_{2}
to be independent from the fields
\xi^{a}
and each of them to be independent of each other. The relations between the fields come from the equations of motion.

As shown in
Guendelman and Kaganovich 1998
, the variation of the action produces the equation of motion

A^{\mu}_{i}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{2}=0,

(5)

where

A^{\mu}_{i}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{ijkl}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{j}\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{k}\partial_{\beta}\xi^{l}.

(6)

Considering the relation

\det\left(A^{\mu}_{i}\right)=\frac{4^{-4}}{4!}\Phi^{3}\,,

(7)

and if we assume
\Phi\neq 0
, the constancy of the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{2}
arises.

In the following sections we will assume that the first Lagrangian contains the usual
Einstein-Hilbert action plus a matter Lagrangian which will be repeated in the second term.
In this work we will consider the matter Lagrangian to be either the
k-essence or braiding scalar field, but it can include the other matter-energy components of the Universe:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M_{p}^{2}}{2}R+\mathcal{L}_{M}

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M},

(9)

where
M_{\rm{P}}=1/\sqrt{8\pi G}
is the reduced Planck mass (
c=\hbar=1
).
The considerations of this section imply the constancy of
\mathcal{L}_{M}
.

III
K-essence model perturbations

In this section, we will first derive the cosmological gravitational perturbations for a Universe
filled with a purely kinetic k-essence field
and calculate its sound speed in the absence
of the second measure. For this we will follow De Felice and Tsujikawa
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2012

who compute the perturbations for a Universe filled with a general Horndeski’s model.
We will repeat the procedure for the model including the new measure and compare the results.

We start by considering that the action of a k-essence field
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\frac{M_{\rm{P}}^{2}}{2}R+K(\varphi,X)
with
\mathcal{L}_{2}=0
:

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\>\sqrt{-g}\>\left[\frac{M_{\rm{P}}^{2}}{2}R\;+\;K\left(\varphi,X\right)\right].

(10)

The flat Friedmann equations associated with this field are given by
Armendariz-Picon
et al. 1999
:

\displaystyle H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3M_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\epsilon\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6M_{\rm{P}}^{2}}\left(\epsilon+3P\right)\,,

(12)

with

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}=2XK_{,X}-K\,,

\displaystyle P_{k}=K\,.

(13)

We will work with a flat Friedmann-Robertson-Walker spacetime plus scalar perturbations, that
has a metric of the form

ds^{2}=-\left(1+2\psi\right)dt^{2}+2\partial_{i}\>\chi\>dt\>dx^{i}+a^{2}E_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}+a^{2}\left(1+2\phi\right)d\mathbf{x}^{2}\,.

(14)

The selection of a gauge for this metric usually involves choosing two of the perturbations as zero

(
\delta\varphi=0
)
Maldacena 2003
and, to fix the remaining gauge freedom, also set
E_{ij}=0
.

To obtain the first order equations of motion for the perturbation fields we expand the action (
10
) up to
second order as
S\approx S^{(0)}+S^{(1)}+S^{(2)}
. Using the metric (
14
)
as well as the background Friedmann equations we get the expression:

\displaystyle S^{\>(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xa^{3}\left[\left(2\omega_{1}\dot{\phi}-\omega_{2}\psi\right)\frac{\partial^{2}\chi}{a^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{3}}{3}\psi^{2}+\frac{\omega_{4}}{a^{2}}\left(\partial\phi\right)^{2}-3\omega_{1}\dot{\phi}^{2}\right.

(15)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.+\left(3\omega_{2}\dot{\phi}-2\omega_{1}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{a^{2}}\right)\psi\right],

where the
\omega_{i}
coefficients are taken from Ref.
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2012
and we
will use them to compare with the TMT case. Here they reduce to

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{4}=M_{\rm{P}}^{2},

(16)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2M_{\rm{P}}^{2}H,

(17)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\dot{\varphi}^{2}\left(K_{,X}+\dot{\varphi}^{2}K_{,XX}\right)-9M_{\rm{P}}^{2}H^{2}.

(18)

One can obtain the linear equations of motion for each scalar perturbation by varying the second order action (
15
) with respect of each of them. In general the
expressions can be quite involved, but we can use the procedure in Ref.

De Felice et al. 2017
to obtain the speed of the perturbations directly from the
terms in the action. In the following paragraphs we summarize the steps behind this procedure.

Considering some of the perturbation fields as Lagrange multipliers we can use their
equations of motion
and some integrations by parts, to arrive at the second order action in the general form:

\displaystyle S^{(2)}=\int d^{4}x\left[A_{ij}\dot{Q}_{i}\dot{Q}_{j}-C_{ij}\left(\partial Q_{i}\right)\left(\partial Q_{j}\right)-B_{ij}Q_{i}\dot{Q}_{j}-D_{ij}Q_{i}Q_{j}\right],

(19)

where
Q_{i}
are the dynamical perturbation fields of the theory and we are summing over
repeated indices. In general,
A,B,C
and
D
will be some square matrices dependent only on
background quantities. The associated Euler-Lagrange equations become

2A_{ij}\ddot{Q}_{j}+(2\dot{A}_{ij}+B_{ij}-B_{ji})\dot{Q}_{j}-2C_{ij}\partial^{2}Q_{j}+(2D_{ij}-\dot{B}_{ij})Q_{j}=0\,.

(20)

In order to find the sound speed, we are interested in perturbations inside the horizon, with
k\gg aH
. After Fourier-transforming the fields
Q_{i}
we keep only the higher terms in
\omega
and
k

arriving at

(\omega^{2}A_{ij}-k^{2}C_{ij})\widetilde{Q}_{j}(\omega,k)=0\,,

(21)

that can be solved for

\omega=c_{s}k/a
we obtain the condition

\det\left(c_{S}^{2}A-a^{2}C\right)=0,

(22)

which can be used to obtain the speed of propagation of the perturbations in the limit
of high frequencies. If
c_{s}^{2}
is possitive, then the perturbations propagate without growing, if it
is zero, the perturbations grow at the rate needed for the structure formation, if it is negative
the perturbations grow exponentially.

Coming back to the k-essence field, we can use the equation of motion for
\chi,\psi,\phi
,

\displaystyle E_{\chi}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\partial^{2}(2\omega_{1}\dot{\phi}-\omega_{2}\psi)=0\,,

(23)

\displaystyle E_{\psi}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 2\frac{\omega_{3}a^{3}}{3}\psi+3\omega_{2}a^{3}\dot{\phi}-2\omega_{1}a\partial^{2}\phi-\omega_{2}a\partial^{2}\chi=0\,,

(24)

\displaystyle E_{\phi}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\left(2\omega_{1}a\partial^{2}\chi-6\omega_{1}a^{3}\dot{\phi}+3\omega_{2}a^{3}\psi\right)-2\omega_{4}a\partial^{2}\phi-2\omega_{1}a\partial^{2}\psi=0\,,

(25)

to write the action in the form (
19

perturbation field
Q_{i}=(\phi)

The speed of sound

in this case is given by:

\displaystyle c_{S}^{2}=\frac{3\left(2\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}H-\omega_{2}^{2}\omega_{4}-2\omega_{1}^{2}\dot{\omega}_{2}\right)}{\omega_{1}\left(4\omega_{1}\omega_{3}+9\omega_{2}^{2}\right)}.

(26)

Taking into account that
\partial\epsilon_{k}/{\partial X}=K_{,X}+2XK_{,XX}
and substituting the values of
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
and

we obtained the well known result

(27)

III.2
Geodesic perturbations

As obtained in section
II
the equation of motion associated to the new measure implies the
constancy of the matter Lagrangian. In this case

K(\varphi,X)=\rm{constant},

(43)

which can be written as follows:

\nabla_{\alpha}K=K_{\varphi}\varphi_{,\alpha}+K_{X}\nabla_{\alpha}X=0\,.

(44)

We can rewrite this equation by defining the 4-velocity of the field as

U_{\alpha}\equiv\frac{\varphi_{,\alpha}}{\sqrt{2X}},

(45)

then the constancy of the Lagrangian can be written as

\left(\sqrt{2X}K_{\varphi}-K_{X}U^{\beta}\nabla_{\beta}X\right)U_{\alpha}-2XK_{X}U^{\beta}\nabla_{\beta}U_{\alpha}=0\,.

(46)

Since the two terms are linearly independent we arrive at the equations

\displaystyle\sqrt{2X}K_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{X}U^{\beta}\nabla_{\beta}X\,,

(47)

\displaystyle U^{\beta}\nabla_{\beta}U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,.

(48)

The last equation corresponds to the geodesic equation that is satisfied independently
of the k-essence Lagrangian. This result was first obtained in Ref.
Guendelman et al. 2012
by means of a different approach
and it explains that in the procedure of the previous subsection. The fact that the field obeys the geodesic equation allows it to be a good
candidate for Dark Matter as it adequately reproduces the formation of structure in the Universe.

IV

We first follow

After we expand the action up to second order in perturbations,

\displaystyle S^{\>(2)}

V
Conclusions

The nature of the late time accelerating expansion of the Universe is one of the most challenging aspects of modern cosmology. One of the more appealing characteristics of Two Field Measure Theory is the possibility to have a unifying description of dark matter and dark energy.
In this paper we have studied the perturbations of k-essence and braiding fields in the context of a Two Field Measure Theory. We have found the stability characteristics and the speed of sound of the scalars fields.

In the original Horndeski theory, the speed of perturbations is a function of the field Lagrangian
K(\varphi,X)
or
G_{3}(\varphi,X)
as well as their derivatives, as it is summarized in table
2
, presenting a rich variety in the speeds of sound depending on the particular Lagrangians. When the new measure
\Phi
is added, however, both cases present a fixed behaviour. For the k-essence models we obtained that the field follows geodesics, a result already obtained in
Guendelman et al. 2012
. This allows the field to be a good candidate for the Dark Matter as it can grow its perturbations and form the structure of the Universe.
In contrast, kinetic gravity braiding model
G_{3}\square\phi
has a unitary speed of perturbations. This characteristic is a consequence of the new measure field which fixed the value of the matter Lagrangian.

For the braiding models we studied further their stability conditions. We obtained that, in the presence of the new measure, there will always appear a ghost instability. The field perturbations of the braiding field plus new measure, can be mapped to a two field theory with a ghost field and a canonical field, both with complicated mass expressions written in Eq. (
77
). When the masses squared are negative we are in the presence of a tachyon instability.

To shed some light on the tachyonic stability of the Braiding model, we studied a particular example in which the field density evolves as a power law in the scale factor plus a cosmological constant. In that case, we computed the squared masses of the resulting fields, obtaining that they change their sign as the Universe evolves. When the density of the Braiding model is low, the tachyonic instability can be limited to the early Universe.

Both, ghost and tachyonic instabilities disfavour the braiding + TMT model as explanation for either dark energy or dark matter, since its energy is unbounded from below. An equally important problem for the braiding field to represent the dark matter is that its sound speed is equal to the speed of light, this prevents the perturbations to grow and form the structure of the Universe, despite its background density to evolve as dust with
1/a^{3}
as can be seen in Eq. (
83
) for
\gamma=-1/2
. The tachyonic and braiding instabilities might be alleviated if we had considered in our calculations a Universe with more fields besides the braiding field, however this study was outside of the scope of this work.

The dark energy nature is one of the most intriguing question in modern cosmology. The TMT theory has exciting properties. For example, any k-essence model in the context of TMT can produce a unification of dark matter and dark energy and explain the growth of structure in the Universe.

Multi-level images around Kerr-Newman black holes
Yehui Hou, Peng Liu, Minyong Guo, Haopeng Yan, Bin Chen

Abstract
We study the lensing effects of Kerr-Newman (KN) black holes and the multi-level images of luminous sources around the black holes. We obtain the analytic forms of the lens equations in KN spacetime and derive approximately their limiting forms for photon emissions near the bounded photon orbits. After deriving the expressions of three key parameters γ \gamma , δ \delta and τ \tau , we investigate the primary, the secondary and the higher-order images of spherical sources and equatorial disk-like sources. In particular, we find that the charge of KN black hole has a significant effect on the higher-order images.

In General Relativity, the most general asymptotically flat and stationary black hole solution to the Einstein-Maxwell equations is the Kerr-Newman (KN) metric, which describes charged rotating black holes with three parameters: mass
M
, electric charge
Q
and angular momentum
J=Ma
. The KN metric reduces to the Kerr metric for
Q=0
. The Kerr metric is the most relevance solution in astrophysical application, because astrophysical black holes are thought to be electrically neutral and rotating. The Kerr metric further reduces to the Schwarzschild metric for
a=0
. A Schwarzschild black hole is static and only has one parameter, thus it is the simplest model to study astrophysical problems for black holes.
Although we would not expect an astrophysical black hole to be considerably charged, it is still reasonable to assume a black hole is slightly charged, and it is thus interesting to study the effects of the charge on the observational feature of a black hole
[
36

,

The lensing effects by a Schwarzschild black hole have been revisited in
[
9
]
, where the authors have argued that the results in the Schwarzschild spacetime are qualitatively unchanged in the Kerr spacetime. In order to study the images of an astrophysical source near a black hole, it is convenient to introduce a fractional number
n
for the photon orbits from the source to an observer. The photons having
n\leq\frac{1}{2}
produce a “direct” primary image of the source, and photons having
\frac{1}{2}<n\leq 1
produce a diminished secondary image of the source surrounding the edge of the black hole shadow. We call this edge the critical curve since it corresponds to the critical photon emissions from the unstable bounded photon orbits.
The higher-order photons having
n>1
contribute to exponentially diminished successive images which approach the critical curve rapidly. That is, the higher-order images produce the observed feature of photon ring and they actually corresponds to the near-critical photon emissions.
The demagnification between two successive images is determined by the Lyapunov exponent
\gamma
, which characterizes the instability of the bounded photon orbits
[
39
,
40
,
8
,
5

Later in
[
10

\gamma
, two more parameters,
\delta
and
\tau
, are introduced to describe the successive images. These two parameters characterize the rotation and time delay of the critical photons over a polar half-libration, respectively. The primary image depicts the overall apparent shape of a given source and thus describes the source-dependent features. On the other hand, the secondary image starts to show the effects of spacetime, and the higher-order images are almost independent of the source and describe the universal feature of a black hole spacetime. The higher-order images can be characterized by the above three key parameters,
\gamma
,
\delta
and
\tau
, of the bounded photon orbits, which are functions of the black hole spin
a
. In this paper, we will study the multi-level lensed images around KN black holes, generalizing the discussions in the Kerr case, and especially study the influence of black hole charge
Q

In this paper, we studied the lensing effects of charged rotating KN black holes, closely following the work

on the lensing by Kerr black holes. Comparing with the results in Kerr case, we found the modifications in the integral form of the photon motion and three key parameters

From our studies, we noticed that for the primary and secondary images the effect of

\mathcal{C}

Q
has a nonnegligible effect on the critical curve. As a result, it is quite reasonable that charge

Appendix A
Radial integrals for near-critical rays

In this appendix, we will compute the radial integrals for near-critical rays following
[
10
]
.
We will outline the main steps and provide the final results for reference.

A.1
Asymptotic expansions

For the light rays crossing the equatorial plane we may introduce a nonnegative parameter
q=\sqrt{\eta}
for convenience. For near-critical rays, the impact parameters can be written as

\displaystyle\lambda=\tilde{\lambda}(1+\delta\lambda),\qquad q=\tilde{q}(1+\delta q),

(A.1)

with
|{\delta\lambda}|\sim|{\delta q}|\sim\varepsilon\ll 1
.
We introduce the
\delta r
-coordinate around the critical radius
\tilde{r}
as well

r=\tilde{r}(1+\delta r).

(A.2)

Then we can define the near and far regions as follows,

\displaystyle\text{Near region}:\qquad|\delta r|\ll 1,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\text{Far region}:\quad\quad\,\,\,\,|\delta r|\gg\varepsilon.

(A.4)

The far region is disjoint around
\tilde{r}
, as can be seen in Fig.
10
.
Apparently, there are overlapping regions here in which
\varepsilon\ll|\delta r|\ll 1
. This ensures the usability of the matched asymptotic expansion (MAE) method.

Figure 10:
Schematic diagram for near, far and overlapping regions with regards to
\tilde{r}
. Note that zone 1 (
\delta r<0
) and zone 2 (
\delta r>0
) are in two disjoint far regions.

In the near region, the radial potential
\mathcal{R}(r)
may be expanded in the regime
|\delta r|\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, then

\mathcal{R}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{r})\delta r^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\delta\lambda+\mathcal{R}_{q}\delta q+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3/2}),

(A.5)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{r})=\tilde{r}^{2}(6\tilde{r}^{2}+a^{2}-\tilde{\lambda}^{2}-\tilde{q}^{2}),\quad\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}=-2\tilde{\lambda}\tilde{r}\Delta(\tilde{r})\frac{2Q-3M\tilde{r}+\tilde{r}^{2}}{a(M-\tilde{r})},\quad\mathcal{R}_{q}=-2\tilde{q}^{2}\Delta(\tilde{r}).

(A.6)

It is convenient to define

\mathcal{R}_{n}(\delta r):=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{r})\delta r^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\delta\lambda+\mathcal{R}_{q}\delta q=4\tilde{r}^{4}\tilde{\chi}(\delta r^{2}-\delta r_{0}^{2}),

(A.7)

where

\delta r_{0}^{2}=\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{2\tilde{r}^{2}\tilde{\chi}}\left[\frac{\tilde{q}^{2}}{\tilde{r}^{2}}\delta q-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{a}\left(\frac{\tilde{r}-3M}{\tilde{r}-M}\right)\delta\lambda\right],\qquad\tilde{\chi}=1-\frac{M\Delta(\tilde{r})}{\tilde{r}(M-\tilde{r})^{2}}.

(A.8)

In the far region, we have
|\delta r|\gg\varepsilon
and the radial potential becomes

\mathcal{R}(r)=\mathcal{R}(\tilde{r}(1+\delta r))|_{\lambda=\tilde{\lambda},q=\tilde{q}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

(A.9)

It is convenient to define

\mathcal{R}_{f}(\delta r):=\mathcal{R}(\tilde{r}(1+\delta r))|_{\lambda=\tilde{\lambda},q=\tilde{q}}=4\tilde{r}^{4}\tilde{\chi}\delta r^{2}\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)

(A.10)

where

\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)=1+\frac{\delta r}{\tilde{\chi}}+\frac{\delta r^{2}}{4\tilde{\chi}}.

(A.11)

Similarly, we can also expand the functions
\mathcal{I}_{\phi}
and
\mathcal{I}_{t}
[Eq. (
2.14
)] in the near and far regions, respectively. Then the radial integrals
I_{i}
(
i=r,\phi,t
) [Eq. (
2.13
)] can be computed explicitly in each of these regions. Combining the near-zone and far-zone results for a physical photon trajectory by using the MAE method, one can obtain the final results for the radial integrals connecting a source to an observer.

Note that a physical light ray never encounter a turning point in the far region thus we should always have
\mathcal{R}_{f}(\delta r)>0
. On the other hand, a light ray has a turning point
r_{t}
(corresponding to the larger root of
\mathcal{R}_{n}(\delta r)=0
) in the near region if
\delta r_{0}^{2}>0
. In that case,
r_{t}=\tilde{r}(1+\delta r_{0})
. We have described in Sec.
2.2
that
\delta r_{0}^{2}
is related to the perpendicular distance
d
from the critical curve and
\delta r_{0}^{2}\gtrless 0
corresponds to the rays arriving outside/inside the critical curve, respectively.

A.2
Matched asymptotic expansion computations

In the near region, we use
I^{n}_{i}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})
to denote the leading order results in
\varepsilon
of the radial integral from
\delta r_{a}
to
\delta r_{b}
with
\delta r_{a}<\delta r_{b}\ll 1
. The radial integrals in (
2.13
) can be written as

I^{n}_{r}=\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\int_{\delta r_{a}}^{\delta r_{b}}\frac{d(\delta r)}{\sqrt{\delta r^{2}-\delta r_{0}^{2}}},\quad I_{\phi}^{n}=a\left(\frac{\tilde{r}+M}{\tilde{r}-M}+\frac{Q}{\tilde{\Delta}}\right)I_{r}^{n},\quad I_{t}^{n}=\tilde{r}^{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{r}+3M}{\tilde{r}-M}\right)I_{r}^{n}.

(A.12)

And the result for
I_{r}^{n}
is

I_{r}^{n}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})=\frac{\text{sign}(\delta r)}{2\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\log{\left(\frac{|\delta r|+\sqrt{\delta r^{2}-\delta r^{2}_{0}}}{|\delta r_{0}|}\right)}\Bigg|^{\delta r_{b}}_{\delta r_{a}}.

(A.13)

In one zone of the far region (either in zone 1 or in zone 2 in Fig.
10
), we use
I^{f}_{i}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})
to denote the radial integral from
\delta r_{a}
to
\delta r_{b}
with
\varepsilon\ll\delta r_{a}<\delta r_{b}
.
The radial integrals in (
2.13
) can be written as

I^{f}_{i}=\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\int_{\delta r_{a}}^{\delta r_{b}}\frac{\mathcal{I}^{f}_{i}(\delta r)d(\delta r)}{\sqrt{\delta r^{2}\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)}},\qquad\mathcal{I}^{f}_{i}(\delta r)=\mathcal{I}_{i}(\tilde{r}(1+\delta r))|_{\lambda=\tilde{\lambda},q=\tilde{q}},

(A.14)

where
\mathcal{I}_{i}(r)
are introduced in (
2.14
).
Following the procedure in
[
10
]
we get

\displaystyle I_{r}^{f}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})=\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\ \text{sign}(\delta r)\log{\left|\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)}+\delta r/2\tilde{\chi}+1}{\delta r}\right|}\Bigg|^{\delta r_{b}}_{\delta r_{a}},

(A.15)

\displaystyle I_{\phi}^{f}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})=a\left(\frac{\tilde{r}+M}{\tilde{r}-M}+\frac{Q}{\tilde{\Delta}}\right)I_{r}^{f}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})-\text{sign}(\delta r)\frac{Ma}{\tilde{r}^{2}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}(\delta r)\bigg|^{\delta r_{b}}_{\delta r_{a}},

(A.16)

\displaystyle I_{t}^{f}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})=\tilde{r}^{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{r}+3M}{\tilde{r}-M}\right)I_{r}^{f}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})-\text{sign}(\delta r)\frac{\tilde{r}}{2\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\delta r)\bigg|^{\delta r_{b}}_{\delta r_{a}},

(A.17)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}(\delta r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{0}+\delta r}{(\delta r_{+}-\delta r_{-})\delta r_{-}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)}}\arctanh{\mathcal{Q}_{2}(\delta r,\delta r_{-})}+(\delta r_{-}\leftrightarrow\delta r_{+}),

(A.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{t}(\delta r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\tilde{\chi}\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)}-\frac{4M\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}{\tilde{r}}\arctanh{\mathcal{Q}_{2}(\delta r,\infty)}

(A.19)

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{c_{1}(1+\delta r_{-})+c_{2}(1+\delta r_{-})^{2}+(1+\delta r_{-})^{4}}{\delta r_{-}(\delta r_{-}-\delta r_{+})\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r_{-})}}\arctanh{\mathcal{Q}_{2}(\delta r,\delta r_{-})}+(\delta r_{-}\leftrightarrow\delta r_{+})\right],\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,

with
\delta r_{\pm}
being the radii of the event horizon in the
\delta r-
coordinates, and

\displaystyle c_{0}=1-\frac{a\tilde{\lambda}}{2M\tilde{r}},\quad c_{1}=\frac{2Ma}{\tilde{r}^{3}}(a-\tilde{\lambda}),\quad c_{2}=\frac{a^{2}}{\tilde{r}^{2}},

(A.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})=\frac{2\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r_{a})\mathcal{Q}(\delta r_{b})}}{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r_{a})+\mathcal{Q}(\delta r_{b})-(\delta r_{a}-\delta r_{b})^{2}/4\tilde{\chi}}.

(A.21)

Next we use the MAE method to calculate radial integrals
for the photons connecting
|\delta r_{a}|\ll 1
in the near region to
|\delta r_{b}|\gg\varepsilon
in the far region through their overlapping region
\varepsilon\ll|\delta R|\ll 1
.
There are several distinct scenarios. For the photons arriving outside the critical curve, we have
\delta r_{a}>\delta r_{0}
such that the point is outside the turning point and we denote the integrals for this case as
I_{i,\text{out}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})
. For the photons arriving inside the critical curve, we may either have

or
\delta r_{b}<\delta r_{a}<0
and we denote the corresponding integrals as
I_{i,\text{in}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})
and
I_{i,\text{in}}^{1\rightarrow n}(\delta r_{b},\delta r_{a})
, respectively.
We have used the superscripts “
1\rightarrow n
” to denote the path integrals from zone 1 of the far region to the near region, and “
n\rightarrow 2
” to denote the path integrals from the near region to zone 2 of the far region (see Fig.
10

We have also used the subscript ”in/out” to denote the photons arriving inside or outside the critical curve, respectively.

We will take
I_{r,\text{out}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})
as an example to perform the calculation, and list the results for
I_{\phi,\text{out}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})
and

We compute the integral

by splitting it into two pieces, and read

\displaystyle I_{r,\text{out}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I^{n}_{r}(\delta r_{a},\delta R)+I^{f}_{r}(\delta R,\delta r_{b})

(A.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\left[\log{\left(\frac{|\delta r|+\sqrt{\delta r^{2}-\delta r^{2}_{0}}}{|\delta r_{0}|}\right)}\Bigg|^{\delta R}_{\delta r_{a}}+\log{\left|\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r)}+\delta r/2\tilde{\chi}+1}{\delta r}\right|}\Bigg|^{\delta r_{b}}_{\delta R}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tilde{r}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}\left[\log\left(\frac{8\tilde{\chi}}{|\delta r_{0}|\sqrt{1-\tilde{\chi}}}\right)-\arctanh\left[\frac{\sqrt{\delta r_{a}^{2}-\delta r_{0}^{2}}}{\delta r_{a}}\right]-\arctanh\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{Q}(\delta r_{b})}}{1+\delta r_{b}/2\tilde{\chi}}\right],\qquad\,\,\,\,

where in the last step
\delta R
was canceled out due to the scaling nature of an overlap region.
The results for

and

\displaystyle I_{\phi,\text{out}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})=a\left(\frac{\tilde{r}+M}{\tilde{r}-M}+\frac{Q}{\tilde{\Delta}}\right)I_{r,\text{out}}^{n\rightarrow 2}(\delta r_{a},\delta r_{b})-\frac{Ma}{\tilde{r}^{2}\sqrt{\tilde{\chi}}}[\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}(\delta r_{b})-\mathcal{Q}_{\phi}(0)],

(A.23)

(A.24)

The results for other cases can be obtained in a similar way.

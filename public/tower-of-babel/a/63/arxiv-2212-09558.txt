Graded covering of a supermanifold
Elizaveta Vishnyakova

Abstract
. We introduce and investigate the notion of a ℤ ≥ 0 \mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0} -graded covering for a supermanifold. More precisely, Donagi and Witten [ DW2 ] suggested a construction of the first obstruction class for splitting of a supermanifold via differential operators. We prove that an infinite prolongation of this construction satisfies some universal properties and can be seen as a covering of a supermanifold in the category of graded manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a connected smooth manifold. A
universal covering
of
M
consists of a connected, simply connected manifold
C
and a local diffeomorphism
p:C\to M
with some additional conditions. A classical example of this situation is the following map:
p:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
, where
p(t)=e^{it}
. A universal covering
C
of
M
is
(a)
unique up to isomorphism and
(b)
any smooth map
f:N\to M
of a simply connected, connected manifold
N
to
M
factors through
p
. Covering spaces are very important for algebraic topology, differential geometry, theory of Lie groups, Riemann surfaces, geometric group theory. More information about covering spaces and precise definitions can be found in
[
Mass
, Chapter 5]
.

There are examples of a covering spaces in algebra. (In algebra the word “cover” is preferred.) For instance if
F
is a family of modules over a certain ring
R
, then an
F
-cover
of an
R
-module
M
is a surjective homomorphism
p:C\to M
with the following properties:

•

C
is in the family
F
;

•

Any endomorphism
f
of
C
commuting with the map
p
, that is
p\circ f=p\circ\mathrm{id}
, is an automorphism. (This implies that
C
is unique up to isomorphism. An analogue of property
(a)
of a geometric covering.)

•

Any (surjective) homomorphism from a module
N\in F
to
M
factors through
C
(an analogue of property
(b)
of a geometric covering).

One of the most important examples of such covers are a torsion-free cover and a flat cover. For instance over an integral domain, every module
M
has a torsion-free cover
C
, see
[
Ban
]
. The flat cover conjecture due to Enochs
[
Eno
, Page 196, 1981]
stats that
for general rings, every module
M
has a
flat cover
C
. This conjecture was resolved positively by Bican, El Bashir and Enochs in
[
BBE
, 2001]
.

In the present paper we suggest a definition of a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-graded covering for any supermanifold
, which has properties similar to
(a)
and
(b)
of a topological and algebraic cover(ing). In more details, we show that

(a)

A
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-graded covering
\mathcal{P}
is unique up to isomorphism for any supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
.

(b)

For any graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
, we can factor the morphism
\psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded ringed spaces through the covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}
.

Further we give two constructions of a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-graded covering for any supermanifold. In other words, we prove that for any supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
, there exists a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-graded covering
\mathcal{P}
. The ringed space
\mathcal{P}
is an infinite dimensional graded manifold of the form
\mathcal{P}=\varprojlim\mathcal{P}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is a graded manifold of degree
n
. We show that a construction given by Donagi and Witten is equivalent to a construction of the graded manifold
\mathcal{P}_{2}
. The construction of
\mathcal{P}_{2}
is related also to constructions of a metric double vector bundle and a skew-symmetric double vector bundle obtained independently in
[
JL
,
CM
]
. A construction of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
for any
n
can be deduced from results
[
BGR
,
Vi1
]
and was obtained in
[
RV
]
, however the meaning and universal properties of the inverse limit
\mathcal{P}=\varprojlim\mathcal{P}_{n}
were not understood before.

We investigate the case of a Lie supergroup.
More precisely, for any Lie supergroup
\mathcal{G}
we define a graded Lie group structure on its
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-graded covering
\mathcal{P}
. Further we show that the Lie superalgebra of
\mathcal{P}
is an example of a loop algebra constructed by V. Kac, see also Allison, Berman, Faulkner, Pianzola
[
ABFP
]
and
[
Eld
]
for applications of this construction. For instance the loop algebra of a (Lie) superalgebra was used by several authors to investigate graded-simple (Lie) algebras.

Our results are especially interesting in the complex-analytic (and algebraic) category. The reason is the following. According to the Batchelor–Gawedzki Theorem any smooth supermanifold is (non-canonically) split, that is its structure sheaf is isomorphic to the wedge power of a certain vector bundle. Therefore very often we can study geometry of a split supermanifold using geometry of vector bundles. This is not the case in the complex-analytic situation. The study of non-split supermanifolds was initiated in
[
Ber
,
Gr
]
, where the first non-split supermanifold was described. Significant advances in this direction were achieved by A.L. Onishchik. Interest in this problem arose again after Donagi and Witten’s papers
[
DW1
,
DW2
]
, where they proved that the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces for
g\geq 5
is non-split (or more generally not projected) and studied other properties of non-split supermanifolds. Other examples of non-split supermanifolds are all (except of few exceptional cases) super-grassmannians and flag supermanifolds.

Our covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}
induces an embedding of the corresponding structure sheaves
\mathsf{p}^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}
. In other words the
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering
\mathcal{P}
keeps the whole information about the (non-split) supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
. Therefore we can study geometry of a non-split supermanifold in the category of graded manifolds using the tools of classical complex geometry due to the fact that geometrically a graded manifold is determined by a family of vector bundles.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Lie superalgebras and graded Lie superalgebras

Throughout the paper we work over the field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}
. More information about supergeometry and Lie superalgebras can be found in
[
Kac
,
BLMS
,
DM
,
L
,
Var
]
. A
vector superspace

V
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space, this is
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
, where
\bar{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
. Elements of
V_{\bar{i}}\setminus\{0\}
are called homogeneous of
parity

\bar{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. To
v\in V_{\bar{i}}\setminus\{0\}
we assign the parity
|v|:=\bar{i}
.

Definition 1
.

A Lie superalgebra is a vector superspace

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded bilinear operation
[\,\,,\,]:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, this is
[\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{i}},\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{j}}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{i}+\bar{j}}
, satisfying the following conditions

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,x],

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]]

\displaystyle=[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,[x,z]],

where
x
,
y
, and
z
are homogeneous elements.

We will consider Lie superalgebras with a parity-matched
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, that is
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=2q}\mathfrak{g}_{q}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=2q+1}\mathfrak{g}_{q}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space (or a Lie superalgebra). Then we put

\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{g})=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,|\,\,\mathfrak{g}_{n}\neq\{0\}\}.

In this paper most of the time we will work with non-negatively
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebras. That is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebras with

\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}:=\{0,1,\ldots,n,\ldots\}.

2.2.
Supermanifolds, graded manifolds and graded supermanifolds

2.2.1.
Supermanifolds

We consider a smooth or complex-analytic supermanifold in the sense of Berezin and Leites
[
DM
,
L
]
, see also
[
BLMS
]
. Thus, a
superdomain in
\mathbb{C}^{n|m}
is a ringed space
(U,\mathcal{F}\otimes\bigwedge(\mathbb{C}^{m})^{*})
, where
U
is an open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\mathcal{F}
is the sheaf of smooth or holomorphic functions on
U
. A supermanifold

\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
of dimension
n|m
is a locally
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
ringed space that is locally isomorphic to a superdomain in

\mathbb{C}^{n|m}
. Here the underlying space
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a complex-analytic or smooth manifold. The dimension
n
of the underlying manifold
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is called
even dimension of
\mathcal{M}
, while
m
is called
odd dimension of
\mathcal{M}
. A

morphism

F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
of two
supermanifolds is a morphism between
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded locally ringed spaces, this is, a pair
F=(F_{0},F^{*})
, where
F_{0}:\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{0}
is a holomorphic mapping and
F^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}\to(F_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
is a homomorphism of sheaves of

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded locally ringed spaces. A morphism
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is called an
isomorphism
if there exists a morphism
G:\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{M}
such that
G\circ F=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
F\circ G=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
.
A supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
is called
split
, if its structure sheaf is isomorphic to
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}
is a locally free sheaf, this is a sheaf of sections of a (holomorphic or smooth) vector bundle
\mathbb{E}
. In this case the structure sheaf is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, not only
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded. According to the Batchelor–Gawedzki Theorem any smooth supermanifold is split. This is not true in the complex-analytic case, see
[
Gaw
,
Ber
,
Gr
]
.

Let again
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O})
be a supermanifold. (Sometimes we will omit
\mathcal{M}
in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
if the meaning of
\mathcal{O}
is clear from the context.) The
tangent sheaf
of a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
is the sheaf
\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{D}er\mathcal{O}
of
derivations of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
. Its global sections are called
smooth or holomorphic vector fields
on
\mathcal{M}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
and let
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
be the maximal ideal in the local superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{x}
. Then as in the classical geometry we can define the tangent space
T_{x}(\mathcal{M})
at the point
x
as
T_{x}(\mathcal{M}):=(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{x})^{*}
. The tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}
-sheaf. If
\mathcal{U}
is a local chart on
\mathcal{M}
with even and odd coordinates
(x_{a},\xi_{b})
, then
\mathcal{T}|_{\mathcal{U}}
is a free
\mathcal{O}
-sheaf with a basis
(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{a}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{b}})
.

2.2.2.
The functor
\mathrm{gr}

To any supermanifold we can assign a split supermanifold. Let us briefly recall this construction. Let
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O})
be a supermanifold. Then its structure sheaf has the following filtration

(1)

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{J}^{0}\supset\mathcal{J}\supset\mathcal{J}^{2}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{J}^{p}\supset\cdots,

where
\mathcal{J}
is the sheaf of ideals generated by odd elements in
\mathcal{O}
. We define

\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{M}):=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}),\quad\text{where}\quad\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{O}=\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}\mathcal{J}^{p}/\mathcal{J}^{p+1}.

The supermanifold
\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{M})
is split, that is its structure sheaf is isomorphic to
\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{J}/\mathcal{J}^{2}
is a locally free sheaf. Since any morphism
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
of supermanifolds preserves the filtration (
1
), the morphism
\mathrm{gr}(F):\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}\to\mathrm{gr}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is defined. Summing up, we defined the functor
\mathrm{gr}
from the category of supermanifolds to itself.

2.2.3.
Graded manifolds and graded supermanifolds of degree
n

Let us start with a notion of a graded manifold of degree
n
,
see
[
Roy
]
for more information.
Consider the following
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector superspace
V
with
\mathrm{supp}(V)\subset\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
:

(2)

V=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}V_{i},\quad V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}},\quad V_{\bar{0}}=\bigoplus_{i=2q}V_{i},\quad V_{\bar{1}}=\bigoplus_{i=2q+1}V_{i},

where
V_{i}
is a finite-dimensional vector space for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. To any element
v
in
V_{i}\setminus\{0\}
we assign the weight
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and the parity
|v|=\bar{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Denote by
S^{*}(V)
the super-symmetric algebra of
V
. In more details,
S^{*}(V)
is the quotient of the tensor algebra of
V
by the ideal generated by
x\otimes y-(-1)^{|x||y|}y\otimes x
, where
x,y
are homogeneous elements. If
v=v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\in S^{*}(V)
is a product of homogeneous elements
v_{i}\in V_{q_{i}}\setminus\{0\}
, then we assign to
v
the weight
\sum\limits_{i}q_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and the parity
\sum\limits_{i}|q_{i}|\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. This induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading in
S^{*}(V)
.

Let us fix a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector superspace
V
over
\mathbb{K}
with
\mathrm{supp}(V)\subset\{0,\ldots,n\}
. Consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed space
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})
, where
\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset V^{*}_{0}
is an open set,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}}:=\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}\otimes_{S^{*}(V_{0})}S^{*}(V)

and

\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}
is the sheaf of smooth or holomorphic functions on
\mathcal{V}_{0}
.
We call the ringed space
\mathcal{V}
a
graded domain of degree
n
and of dimension
\{n_{i}\}
, where
n_{i}:=\dim V_{i}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
. Further, let us choose a basis
(x_{a})
,
a=1,\ldots,n_{0}
, in
V_{0}
and a basis
(\xi_{b_{i}}^{i})
, where
b_{i}=1,\ldots,n_{i}
, in
V_{i}
for any
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then the set
(x_{a},\xi_{b_{i}}^{i})
is by definition a system of local coordinates in the graded domain
\mathcal{V}
. We assign the weight
0
and the parity
\bar{0}
to
x_{a}
and the weight
i
and the parity
\bar{i}
to
\xi_{b_{i}}^{i}
. Such coordinates as
(x_{a},\xi_{b_{i}}^{i})
in
\mathcal{V}
are called
graded
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
be two graded domains with graded coordinates
(x_{a},\xi_{b_{i}}^{i})
and
(y_{c},\eta_{d_{j}}^{j})
, respectively. A
morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of graded domains
is a morphism of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces such that
\Phi^{*}|_{(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}})_{0}}:(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}})_{0}\to(\Phi_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})_{0}
is local, that is it is a usual morphism of smooth or holomorphic domains. Clearly such a morphism is determined by images of local coordinates
\Phi^{*}(y_{c})
and
\Phi^{*}(\eta_{d_{j}}^{j})
. Conversely, if we have the following set of functions

(3)

\Phi^{*}(y_{c})\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})_{0}(\mathcal{V}_{0})\quad\text{and }\quad\Phi^{*}(\eta_{d_{j}}^{j})\in(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{V}})_{j}(\mathcal{V}_{0}),\quad j>0,

such that
(\Phi^{*}(y_{1})(u),\ldots,\Phi^{*}(y_{n_{0}})(u))\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{0}
for any
u\in\mathcal{V}_{0}
, than there exists unique morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
of graded domains compatible with (
3
). A
graded manifold of degree
n
and of dimension
\{n_{i}\}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
,
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed space
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})
, that is locally isomorphic to a graded domain of degree
n
and of dimension
\{n_{i}\}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
. More precisely, we can find an atlas
\{U_{i}\}
of
\mathcal{N}_{0}
and isomorphisms
\Phi_{i}:(U_{i},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}|_{U_{i}})\to\mathcal{V}_{i}
of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces such that
\Phi_{i}\circ(\Phi_{j})^{-1}:\mathcal{V}_{j}\to\mathcal{V}_{i}
is a morphism of graded domains. A
morphism of graded manifolds
\Phi=(\Phi_{0},\Phi^{*}):\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}_{1}
is a morphism of the corresponding
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces, which is locally a morphism of graded domains.

Since
\mathcal{N}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed space, its structure sheaf is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded:
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}=\bigoplus\limits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})_{q}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{I}_{n}
the sheaf of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}

(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})_{q}
, where

. The
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ringed spaces
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}:=(\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}/\mathcal{I}_{n})

. If we have a morphism
\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}_{1}
of graded manifolds, than the induced morphism
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}\to\mathcal{N}_{1}^{(n)}
is local for any
n
.

Remark 3
.

To any graded manifold of degree

we can assign a graded manifold of degree

A definition of a graded supermanifold is the following.
Consider a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector superspace with support

:

where

.

2.3.
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded morphisms

Let
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
be a supermanifold and
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})

n
).
Let us define a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded morphism
\psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M}
. To understand the nature of this problem, consider the following example.

Example 4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{K}^{1|0}
,
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathcal{N}
be a graded domain
V_{0}\oplus V_{2}
, where

. Denote by

the standard coordinate in
\mathcal{M}

,

\mathcal{N}
with weights

Then it has to be

We see that the image of

has to be an infinite series in

Example

Let as above

Within the framework of this paper, it is natural to consider the following definition.

Definition 5
.

A morphism of a graded manifold
\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{M}
is a compatible family of morphisms

,

-graded locally ringed spaces.

Let us comment the compatibility of morphisms
\psi^{(k)}
in Definition
5
. The family of morphisms
(\psi^{(k)}:\mathcal{N}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M})
is called

if the following diagrams are commutative for any

:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}/\mathcal{I}_{k},}

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a supermanifold,
\mathcal{N}
be a graded manifold and
(\psi^{(k)}:\mathcal{N}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M})
be a morphism as in Definition
5

(1)
Any morphism
\psi^{(k)}:\mathcal{N}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M}
,
k\geq 0
, of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded locally ringed spaces is locally determined by the images of local coordinates.

(2)
The compatible family
(\psi^{(k)}:\mathcal{N}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M})
, is locally determined by the images of local coordinates.

Proof.

Clearly the second statement is the consequence of the first one. Let u sprove the first statement.
The idea of the proof is similar to
[
L
, Section 2.1.7, Proof of Theorem]
. It is sufficient to assume that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{U}=(\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}})

2.4.
Lie supergroups, graded Lie groups and super Harish-Chandra pairs

2.4.1.
Lie supergroups

A
Lie supergroup
is a group object in
the category of supermanifolds.
In other words a Lie supergroup is a supermanifold
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}})
with three morphisms:

\mu:\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}

(the multiplication morphism),
\kappa:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
(the inversion morphism),

\varepsilon:(\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{K})\to\mathcal{G}
(the identity
morphism). Moreover, these morphisms have to satisfy the usual
conditions, modeling the group axioms. The underlying manifold
\mathcal{G}_{0}
of
\mathcal{G}

is a smooth or complex-analytic Lie group. As in the theory of Lie groups and Lie algebras, we can define the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})
of a Lie supergroup
\mathcal{G}
. Let us remind this construction.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie supergroup,
e=\varepsilon_{0}(\mathrm{pt})
be the identity element of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{e}
be the maximal ideal in the local algebra
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}})_{e}
. Then
T_{e}(\mathcal{G}):=(\mathfrak{m}_{e}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{e})^{*}
is the tangent space of
\mathcal{G}
at
e
. By definition,
\mathfrak{g}:=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})
is the subsuperalgebra of the Lie superalgebra of smooth or holomorphic vector fields on

\mathcal{G}
consisting of all right invariant
vector fields on
\mathcal{G}
. Recall that a vector field
Y
on
\mathcal{G}
is called
right invariant
if
(Y\times\mathrm{id})\circ\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}\circ Y
. Any right invariant vector field
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
has the following form

Y=(v_{e}\times\mathrm{id})\circ\mu^{*}

for a
v_{e}\in(\mathfrak{m}_{e}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{e})^{*}
, see
[
Var
, Theorem 6.1.1]
. Note that as in the classical case the vector superspace
(\mathfrak{m}_{e}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{e})^{*}
may be identified with the vector superspace of all maps
D:(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}})_{e}\to\mathbb{K}
satisfying the super Leibniz rule.
Further the correspondence
v_{e}\mapsto(v_{e}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\mu^{*}
defines an isomorphism of
T_{e}(\mathcal{G})
onto
\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly we can define a
graded Lie group of degree
n
as a group object in the category of graded manifolds of degree
n
.

2.4.2.
Super Harish-Chandra pairs

To study a Lie supergroup one uses the theory of super Harish-Chandra pairs, see
[
DM
]
and also
[
CCF
,
Vi3
]
.

Definition 8
.

A super Harish-Chandra pair
(G,\mathfrak{g})
consists of
a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}
, a Lie group
G
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
, and
an action
\alpha
of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
by authomorphisms such that

•

for any
g\in G
the action
\alpha(g)
restricted to
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
coincides with the adjoint action
Ad(g)
;

•

the differential
(d\alpha)_{e}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\to\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
at the identity
e\in G
coincides with
the adjoint representation
\operatorname{ad}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Super Harish-Chandra pairs form a category.
The following theorem was proved in
[
Vi3
]
for complex-analytic Lie supergroups.

Theorem 9
.

The category of complex-analytic Lie supergroups is
equivalent to the category of complex-analytic super Harish-Chandra pairs.

A similar result in the real case was obtained in
[
Kos
]
, the algebraic case was treated by several authors, see for example
[
Gav1
,
Mas
,
MS
]
and references therein.

A super Harish-Chandra pair of degree
n
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded super Harish-Chandra pair
(G,\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra with
\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\{0,\ldots,n\}
, and
G=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
. Moreover,
\alpha(g):\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, where
g\in G
, is an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
preserving the
\mathbb{Z}
-grading and
\mathrm{d}_{e}\alpha(X)=[X,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
for any
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. In
[
JKPS
, Theorem 5.6]
it was noticed that an analogue of Theorem
9
holds true for graded Lie groups of degree
n
.

2.4.3.
Construction of the Lie supergroup corresponding to a super Harish-Chandra pair

Let us remind a reader how to construct a Lie supergroup
\mathcal{G}
(or a graded Lie group of degree
n
) using a given super (or of degree
n
) Harish-Chandra pair
(G,\mathfrak{g})
. Denote by
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h})
the universal enveloping algebra of Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
. We need to define a structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}
of
\mathcal{G}
. In the super and graded cases we, respectively, put

(7)

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathcal{F}_{G}),\quad\mathcal{O}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathcal{F}_{G}).\end{split}

Here
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is the sheaf of (smooth or holomorphic) functions on
G
. Recall that in the super- and graded cases we respectively have
G=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})
and
G=\operatorname{Lie}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
.
Using the Hopf superalgebra structure on
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
we can define explicitly the multiplication morphism
\mu
, the inversion morphism
\kappa
and the identity
\varepsilon
, see for instance
[
Vi3
]
. Indeed, assume that a super or graded of degree
n
Harish-Chandra pair
(G,\mathfrak{g})
is given. Let us define the supergroup structure of the corresponding Lie supergroup or graded Lie group
\mathcal{G}
of degree
n
. Let
X\cdot Y\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})\simeq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
, where

X
is from the first copy of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
and
Y

from the second one,
f\in\mathcal{O}
, see (
7
), and
g,\,h\in G
. The following formulas define a multiplication morphism, an
inverse morphism and an identity morphism respectively:

(8)

\begin{split}\mu^{*}(f)(X\cdot Y)(g,h)&=f(\alpha(h^{-1})(X)\cdot Y)(gh);\\
\kappa^{*}(f)(X)(g^{-1})&=f(\alpha(g^{-1})(S(X)))(g);\\
\varepsilon^{*}(f)&=f(1)(e).\end{split}

Here
S
is the antipode map in
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
considered as a Hopf superalgebra and
\alpha
is as in the definition of a super Harish-Chandra pair. Theorem
9
or
[
JKPS
, Theorem 5.6]
imply that any Lie supergroup or graded Lie group is isomorphic to a Lie supergroup with the structure sheaf (
7
) and the structure morphisms (
8
).

2.5.
Inverse limit of graded manifolds and graded Lie groups

In this paper we will work with a special type of infinite dimensional graded manifolds or graded Lie groups, more precisely with the inverse limit of graded manifolds or of graded Lie groups with fixed base space
N
. Related construction of a direct limit of (super)manifolds may be found for instance in
[
DP
,
OV
]
.

Let we have a family of graded manifolds
\mathcal{N}_{k}=(N,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{k}})
of degree
k=0,1,\ldots
. For any
k
we have a projection
\mathrm{pr}^{k}_{k-1}
of
\mathcal{N}_{k}
to a graded manifold
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{k-1}
of degree
k-1
, see Remark
3
. Assume that our family of graded manifolds
(\mathcal{N}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is compatible in the following sense. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
we have
\mathrm{pr}^{k}_{k-1}(\mathcal{N}_{k})=\mathcal{N}_{k-1}
.
Therefore for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
we have the following sequence of projections

\mathcal{N}_{0}\xleftarrow{\mathrm{pr}^{1}_{0}}\mathcal{N}_{1}\xleftarrow{\mathrm{pr}^{2}_{1}}\mathcal{N}_{2}\xleftarrow{\mathrm{pr}^{3}_{2}}\ldots\xleftarrow{\mathrm{pr}^{n}_{n-1}}\mathcal{N}_{n}.

For the corresponding structure sheaves we have the following sequence of inclusions

(9)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{0}}\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{pr}^{1}_{0})^{*}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{pr}^{2}_{1})^{*}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{2}}\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{pr}^{3}_{2})^{*}}\cdots\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{pr}^{n}_{n-1})^{*}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{n}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}.

We put

\mathcal{N}:=(N,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}),\quad\text{ where }\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{k}},

That is
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}
if and only if there exist
k
such that
f\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}
. In other words,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}
is the
direct limit
of the
direct system
of sheaves (
9
). We will write

\mathcal{N}=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{k},\quad\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}}=\varinjlim\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{k}}.

A
morphism

\Phi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{N}=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{k}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{k}
, is a family of morphisms
(\Phi_{k}:\mathcal{N}_{k}\to\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{k})

such that

\Phi_{k}\circ\mathrm{pr}^{k+1}_{k}=\mathrm{pr}^{k+1}_{k}\circ\Phi_{k+1},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

The inverse limit of graded Lie groups has a graded Lie group structure. Here we additionally assume that the projections
\mathrm{pr}^{k+1}_{k}
are graded Lie group homomorphisms.

3.
A
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a supermanifold

In this section we introduce a new concept:
a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a supermanifold
. In Introduction we gave several examples of coverings in geometry and algebra. Consider for instance a flat cover
C
of a module
M
. In this case we construct a cover of a module
M
in the category of flat modules. This paper is devoted to the following question: can a supermanifold be covered in the category of graded manifolds? Such a covering if exists we will call a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a supermanifold
.

Our idea is the following. For any supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
we define a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of
\mathcal{M}
as a certain non-negatively
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifold
\mathcal{P}
together with a covering projection
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}
. It has to satisfy the universal property similar to the geometric and algebraic cases. For instance any morphism
\psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M}
of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded manifold
\mathcal{N}
to the supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
has to factor through
\mathsf{p}
. In other words it has to exist a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded morphism
\Psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{P}
such that
\mathsf{p}\circ\Psi=\psi
.

Assume that we constructed a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering
\mathsf{p}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{M}
.
Consider a graded manifold
\mathcal{N}=(\mathrm{pt},\bigwedge(\zeta))
, where we assume that
\zeta
is an odd variable of weight
2n+1
. Let
\psi:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded morphism of locally ringed spaces and
\Psi
be a lift of
\psi
such that
\mathsf{p}\circ\Psi=\psi
. Since
\Psi
has to be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded, this implies that
\mathcal{P}
has local coordinates of weight
2n+1
. Similarly for even coordinates. This implies that
\mathcal{P}
has to be infinite dimensional, since it has local coordinates of any weight in
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
.

3.1.
Definition of a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a supermanifold

We suggest the following definition of a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering
\mathcal{P}
of a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O})
. As we saw above the
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{M}
cannot be finite dimensional.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\varprojlim\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the inverse limit of graded manifolds
\mathcal{P}_{n}
of degree
n
with the same underlying space
(\mathcal{P}_{n})_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
, see Section
2.5
. In this case the structure sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}
of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathcal{P}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded. In other words,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}=\bigoplus\limits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})_{q},\quad\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}=\bigoplus\limits_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}})_{q}.

Let again
\mathcal{I}_{k}
be the sheaf of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}
generated by all
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}})_{q}
, where
q\geq k+1
. We have the following natural isomorphism of sheaves of superalgebras

(10)

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}}/{\mathcal{I}}_{k}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}/({\mathcal{I}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}),\quad n\geq k\geq 0.

Denote

\mathcal{P}^{(k)}:=(\mathcal{P}_{0},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}}/{\mathcal{I}}_{k})\simeq(\mathcal{P}_{0},{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}}/({\mathcal{I}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}_{n}})),\quad n\geq k.

Note that we have the following natural isomorphism of sheaves of vector spaces

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}}/{\mathcal{I}}_{k}\simeq({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}})_{0}\oplus({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}})_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{P}})_{k},\quad k\geq 0.

An atlas on
\mathcal{P}=\varprojlim\mathcal{P}_{n}
is a set
\{\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}\}
of graded domains
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
, where each
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
is the inverse limit of graded domains
(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{n}
of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\{(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{n}\}
is an atlas of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
for any
n
. Let
(y_{a_{s}}^{s},\eta^{t}_{b_{t}})
, where
s
is an even non-negative integer and
t
is an odd non-negative integer, be graded coordinates in
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}=\varprojlim(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{n}
. As above the weight of
y_{a_{s}}^{s}
is equal to
s
and the weight of
\eta^{t}_{b_{t}}
is equal to
t
. We call such coordinates
adapted
, if
(y_{a_{s}}^{s},\eta^{t}_{b_{t}})_{s,t\leq n}
are graded coordinates in
(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{n}
for any
n
.
We will denote images of
y_{a_{s}}^{s}
or
\eta^{t}_{b_{t}}
,
s,t\leq k
, in the structure sheaf of
\mathcal{P}^{(k)}
also by
y_{a_{s}}^{s}
or
\eta^{t}_{b_{t}}
, respectively. For example for the image of
y_{a_{s}}^{s}
,
s\leq k
, in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{(k)}}
we use the same notation
y_{a_{s}}^{s}
.

Definition 10
.

A
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
is an infinite dimensional graded manifold
\mathcal{P}=\varprojlim\mathcal{P}_{n}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
together with a compatible family of morphisms
\{\mathsf{p}^{(k)}:\mathcal{P}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded ringed spaces such that
we can choose atlases
\{\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}\}
and
\{\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}=\varprojlim(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{n}\}
on
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{P}
, respectively, with
(\mathcal{U}_{\lambda})_{0}=(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{0}
, with even and odd coordinates
(x_{a},\xi_{b})
of
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
and with adapted graded coordinates
(y_{a}^{s},\eta^{t}_{b})
of
\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}
, where
s
is an even non-negative integer and
t
is an odd non-negative integer, such that

\mathrm{pr}_{s}\circ(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}(x_{a})=y_{a}^{s},\quad\mathrm{pr}_{t}\circ(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}(\xi_{b})=\eta^{t}_{b},\quad s,t\leq k,

where
\mathrm{pr}_{q}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{(k)}}\to(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{(k)}})_{q}
,
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, is the natural projection.

Remark 11
.

We can write locally the morphism
\mathsf{p}^{(k)}:\mathcal{P}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M}
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
, from Definition
10
without use of projections
\mathrm{pr}_{q}
. More precisely, we can write
\mathsf{p}^{(k)}:\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
in coordinates from Definition

in the following form

(11)

\begin{split}&(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}(x_{a})=y_{a}^{0}+y_{a}^{2}+y_{a}^{4}+\cdots+y_{a}^{2v}\mod\mathcal{I}_{k};\\
&(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}(\xi_{b})=\eta^{1}_{b}+\eta^{3}_{b}+\eta^{5}_{b}+\cdots+\eta_{b}^{2v+1}\mod\mathcal{I}_{k},\end{split}

where
v>k
is any integer.
Sometimes instead of (
11
) we will use the following schematic series notation omitting superscript
(k)
meaning (
11
):

(12)

\begin{split}\mathsf{p}^{*}(x_{a})=y_{a}^{0}+y_{a}^{2}+y_{a}^{4}+\cdots;\quad\mathsf{p}^{*}(\xi_{b})=\eta^{1}_{b}+\eta^{3}_{b}+\eta^{5}_{b}+\cdots.\end{split}

Note that the series on the right hand side (

) are not elements of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Remark 12
.

Note that if the morphism
\mathsf{p}^{(k)}:\mathcal{P}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{M}
,
k\geq 0
, there exists, it is locally determined by Formula (
11
). Indeed, by Formula
10
the morphism
\mathsf{p}^{(k)}
can be regarded as a morphism
\mathsf{p}^{(k)}:\mathcal{P}^{(k)}_{k}\to\mathcal{M}
, which is determined by the images of local coordinates, see Theorem
6
.

Further, if we have (

), by Theorem
7
there exists unique morphism

\mathsf{p}^{(k)}:\mathcal{V}^{(k)}_{\lambda}\simeq(\mathcal{V}_{\lambda})_{k}^{(k)}\to\mathcal{U}_{\lambda},\quad k\geq 0,

compatible with (

).

3.2.
Properties of a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a supermanifold

We will prove that our covering, if exists, satisfies the universal properties as other coverings in geometry and algebra.
Let as above
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O})
be a supermanifold and
\mathcal{N}=(\mathcal{N}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}})
be a graded manifold.
Denote

be as above. Any morphism

&\zeta_{d}^{s}=\mathrm{pr}_{s}\circ\mathsf{p}^{*}(\psi^{*}(\theta_{d}))=\mathrm{pr}_{s}\circ\mathsf{p}^{*}(G_{d}(x_{a},\xi_{b})).\end{split}

.

Let we have three superdomains

,
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}
and two moprphisms

6.1.
k
-th-order tangent bundle of a manifold
M

Let
M
be a smooth or holomorphic manifold and
p\in M
. Consider two smooth functions
f,g:W\to M
, where
W
is a neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{K}
, such that
f(0)=g(0)=p
. We say that
f
and

are
equivalent to order
k
at

of

\varphi:U\to\mathbb{K}
the
k
-th-order jets

J^{k}_{0}(\varphi\circ g)
at point

are equal, that is
J^{k}_{0}(\varphi\circ f)=J^{k}_{0}(\varphi\circ g)
. Denote the
k
-jet of a curve
f
through

to be the equivalence class of
f
under this equivalence relation. The
k
-th-order jet space
J^{k}_{0}(\mathbb{K},M)_{p}
is then the set of
k
-jets at
p
. The
k
-th-order tangent bundle

of a manifold

is a fiber bundle with the fiber
J^{k}_{0}(\mathbb{K},M)_{p}
ap
p\in M
. If
(x_{a})
are local coordinates in
U
at

, the standard local coordinates in
T^{k}(U)\subset T^{k}(M)
are

(x_{a},\dot{x}_{a},\ddot{x}_{a},\ldots,x^{(k)}_{a}),

where

\displaystyle x^{(s)}_{a}:=\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial t^{s}}\Big|_{t=0}(x_{a}\circ f(t)),\quad f:W\to U

For instance,
T^{1}(M)=T(M)
. Denote
T^{\infty}(M):=\varprojlim T^{k}(M)
.

Example 31
.

Let
(x_{a})
be local coordinates in
U
and
y_{c}=F_{c}(x_{a})
be a coordinate change. Then the transformation law for coordinates
y_{c},\dot{y}_{c},\ddot{y}_{c}
is the following

\displaystyle y_{c}=F_{c}(x_{a}),\quad\dot{y}_{c}=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial F_{c}}{\partial x_{i}}\dot{x}_{i},\quad\ddot{y}_{c}=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial F_{c}}{\partial x_{i}}\ddot{x}_{i}+\sum_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}F_{c}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\dot{x}_{i}\dot{x}_{j}.

The fiber bundle
T^{k}(M)
is a graded manifold
(M,\mathcal{O}_{T^{k}(M)})
of degree
k
assuming that
x^{(s)}_{a}
has weight
s
. Denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
the sheaf of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{T^{k}(M)}
locally generated by
x^{(2s+1)}_{a}
, where
s\geq 0
. Clearly
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is well-defined.

Proposition 32
.

Let
M
be a manifold and
T^{k}(M)
be its

-th-order tangent bundle. Then we have

In the standard local coordinates an isomorphism is given by

.

8.
About non-split supermanifolds

Recall that a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}})
is called
split
if
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\simeq\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{E}
is a locally free sheaf on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. If
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\not\simeq\bigwedge\mathcal{E}
the supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
is called
non-split
. Consider the following example.

Example 44
.

A superquadric
\mathcal{Q}
is a supermanifold of dimension
1|2
with the base space
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. This is a particular case of an isotropic super-Grassmannian introduced in
[
Man
]
. We give a description of
\mathcal{Q}
using language of local charts and atlases. For a description of
\mathcal{Q}
as an equation in the protective superspace
\mathbb{CP}^{2|2}
see
[
Oni1
, Example 2.14]
.

We can cover
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with two standard charts
\mathbb{CP}^{1}=U_{0}\cup U_{1}
. Let
x
and
y
are standard coordinates in
U_{0}
and in
U_{1}
, respectively, with
y=\frac{1}{x}
in
U_{0}\cap U_{1}
. Consider two superdomains

\mathcal{U}_{0}=(U_{0},\mathcal{F}_{U_{0}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}))\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{U}_{1}=(U_{1},\mathcal{F}_{U_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})),

where
\mathcal{F}_{U_{i}}
is the sheaf of holomorphic functions on
U_{i}
,
i=0,1
, and
\bigwedge(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})
is the Grassmann algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
. Summing up, we can regard
(x,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
as coordinates in
\mathcal{U}_{0}
and
(y,\eta_{1},\eta_{2})
as coordinates in
\mathcal{U}_{1}
. Define the supermanifold
\mathcal{Q}
in terms of local charts
\mathcal{U}_{i}
and the following transition functions in
U_{0}\cap U_{1}

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{x^{3}}\xi_{1}\xi_{2};

\displaystyle\eta_{j}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}\xi_{j},\,\,j=1,2.

The obtained supermanifold
\mathcal{Q}
is non-split.
Historically, this was one of the first examples of such supermanifolds, see
[
Ber
,
Gr
,
Man
]
.

Non-split supermanifolds plays an important rule in physics. For instance in
[
DW1
]
it was shown that for genus
g\geq 5
the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces is not split. The property of this moduli space to be non-split implies the following
[
DW1
, Abstract]
:

“
Physically, this means that certain approaches to superstring perturbation theory that are very powerful in low orders have no close analog in higher orders. Mathematically, it means that the moduli space of super Riemann surfaces cannot be constructed in an elementary way starting with the moduli space of ordinary Riemann surfaces. It has a life of its own.
”

The transition from a supermanifold
\mathcal{M}
to a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering
\mathcal{P}
resolves in a certain sense the problem of
\mathcal{M}
to be non-split and reduces the study of geometry of
\mathcal{M}
to a classical problem.

We will illustrate this in the following example.

Example 45
.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}
a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of
\mathcal{Q}
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is an infinite dimensional graded manifold, with the base space
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and which has the following transition functions

weight
0
:

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{x};

weight
1
:

\displaystyle\theta_{j}=\frac{1}{x^{2}}\zeta_{j},\,\,j=1,2;

weight
2
:

\displaystyle w=-\frac{1}{x^{2}}z+\frac{1}{x^{3}}\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2};

weight
\geq 3
:

\displaystyle\cdots

Here the coordinates
x,y
have weight
0
,
\xi_{i},\eta_{j}
have weight
1
and
z
has weight
2
. (Compare with Proposition
40
and Theorem
41
.)

The structure sheaf of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is determined by a compatible family of vector bundles over
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Let us describe
(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}})_{0}
,
(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}})_{1}
and
(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}})_{0}
explicitly, see Proposition
40
and Theorem
41
.

Graded functions of weight
0
on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}
are usual local holomorphic function on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. Further graded functions of weight
1
are local sections of a vector bundle of rank
2
isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-2)
. Graded functions of weight
2
are sections of a vector bundle
\mathcal{D}
, which is determined by the following exact sequence

with the Atiyah class

The second important observation is that the following morphism of sheaves of superalgebras

(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{(k)}_{\mathcal{Q}}},\quad k\geq 2,

is invective. In other words, the structure sheaf of a non-split supermanifold is realized as a subsheaf of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded sheaf

for any

.

The observation from Example

holds for any supermanifold.

Theorem 46
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a (non-split) supermanifold of dimension
m|n
and
\mathcal{P}
be its
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering. Then the following morphism of sheaves of superalgebras

(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}^{(k)}},\quad k\geq n,

is invective.

Proof.

Locally, for superdomains, the morphism
(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}
is defined by Formula (
16
). Therefore
(\mathsf{p}^{(k)})^{*}
is locally invective for any

. But this implies the result, since if the image of a function is
0
, there exists a superdomain where this image is
0
.
∎

Recall that the correspondence

Definition 47

from Theorem

10.1.
A

We start with the following definitions. If

, then
\bar{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the parity of
a
.

Definition 51
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-covering of a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded superalgebra Lie
\mathfrak{p}=\oplus_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{p}_{a}
together with a homomorphism
p:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{g}
of Lie superalgebras such that

p|_{\mathfrak{p}_{a}}:\mathfrak{p}_{a}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{a}}
is a bijection for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 52
.

A
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded superalgebra Lie
\mathfrak{p}=\oplus_{a\geq 0}\mathfrak{p}_{a}
with support
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
together with a
homomorphism
p:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{g}
of Lie superalgebras such that

p|_{\mathfrak{p}_{a}}:\mathfrak{p}_{a}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{a}}
is a bijection for any
a\geq 0
.

Note that in both cases the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{p}
is fully determined by the bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
. Indeed, for
X\in\mathfrak{p}_{a}
,
X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{p}_{a^{\prime}}
we have
p([X,X^{\prime}]_{\mathfrak{p}})=[p(X),p(X^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{a}+\bar{a}^{\prime}}
. Hence

[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{p}}=(p|_{\mathfrak{p}_{a+a^{\prime}}})^{-1}([p(X),p(X^{\prime})]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

Moreover,
p|_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}:\mathfrak{p}_{0}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
is a Lie algebra isomorphism and

p|_{\mathfrak{p}_{a}}:\mathfrak{p}_{a}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{a}}
is a morphism of
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
-module, where we identify the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
via
p|_{\mathfrak{p}_{0}}
.

.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie superalgebra,
\mathfrak{a}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra (or a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra with support
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
) and let

be a homomorphism of Lie superalgebras. If
\mathfrak{p}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-covering of
\mathfrak{g}
(or
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathbb{Z}
-graded homomorphism

such that the following diagram is commutative

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Define a linear map

10.2.
Loop superalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie superalgebra. We can easily see that a
\mathbb{Z}

\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
and a
\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
-covering
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a (sub)algebra of a loop superalgebra in the sense
[

, see also

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{i}}\otimes t^{i},\quad\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{i}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{i}}\otimes t^{i};\quad\mathfrak{p}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{i}}\otimes t^{i},\quad\mathfrak{p}_{i}\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{i}}\otimes t^{i}.

\displaystyle=f(p(X))(g),\quad X\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{p}}),\quad g\in\mathcal{G}_{0},

where

is as in Proposition
53
,
satisfies the universal property and to use Theorem
18
. Let us take a non-negatively graded Lie group

of degree

. By
[

]

.

]

\displaystyle\psi^{*}:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}})}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}}),\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{G}_{0}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})}(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{a}}),\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{A}_{0}});

A Donagi–Witten construction

In

\omega_{2}
see Appendix

.) Further we give an interpretation of the Donagi–Witten construction using the language of double vector bundles and graded manifolds of degree

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a (non-split) supermanifold.
In
[
DW2
, Section 2.1]
the first obstruction class
\omega_{2}
to splitting a supermanifold

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{J}
be the corresponding to
\mathcal{M}

we defined

as an element in

[

[

it was noticed that the locally free sheaf
\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{2}}

. This construction was suggested in

To get

\mathcal{U}_{1}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}

(x_{a},\xi_{b})

\begin{split}&y_{a}=F_{a}+\frac{1}{2}G_{a}^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}+\cdots\,\,\,a=1,\ldots,p;\\
&\eta_{b}=H^{j}_{b}\xi_{j}+\cdots,\,\,\,b=1,\ldots,q,\end{split}

where
F_{a}=F_{a}(x)
,

are

\{x_{a}\}

\mathcal{J}^{3}

on

as the sheaf, which is locally generated over

by

In

Let us write transition function for generators
\left[{\partial_{x_{a}}}\right]
,
\left[{\partial_{\xi_{i}}\circ{\partial_{\xi_{j}}}}\right]

We have

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{b}}

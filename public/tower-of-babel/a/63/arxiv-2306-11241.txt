Online Vector Bin Packing and Hypergraph Coloring Illuminated: Simpler Proofs and New Connections
Yaqiao Li, Denis Pankratov

Abstract
This paper studies the online vector bin packing (OVBP) problem and the related problem of online hypergraph coloring (OHC). Firstly, we use a double counting argument to prove an upper bound of the competitive ratio of F ​ i ​ r ​ s ​ t ​ F ​ i ​ t FirstFit for OVBP. Our proof is conceptually simple, and strengthens the result in [ 1 ] by removing the dependency on the bin size parameter. Secondly, we introduce a notion of an online incidence matrix that is defined for every instance of OHC. Using this notion, we provide a reduction from OHC to OVBP, which allows us to carry known lower bounds of the competitive ratio of algorithms for OHC to OVBP. Our approach significantly simplifies the previous argument from [ 1 ] that relied on using intricate graph structures. In addition, we slightly improve their lower bounds. Lastly, we establish a tight bound of the competitive ratio of algorithms for OHC, where input is restricted to be a hypertree, thus resolving a conjecture in [ 6 ] . The crux of this proof lies in solving a certain combinatorial partition problem about multi-family of subsets, which might be of independent interest.

The

Here and throughout the paper we use the notation

Let

In the

Bin packing is a fundamental problem in optimization, for which VBP is one of its many variants. The VBP has applications in virtual machine placement in cloud computing.
While upper bounds on competitive ratios of OVBP algorithms are easier to establish, it took two decades to establish strong lower bounds in the breakthrough
[

Main Results

Theorem 1
.

For the
\{0,1\}

d
-dimensional OVBP with bin size

is

<\sqrt{2d}+2
.

Secondly, we give much simpler proofs of improved lower bounds on the competitive ratio of arbitrary algorithms for OVBP. In

, though this term was not used, and it was only applied to graphs not hypergraphs. As a byproduct of applying lower bounds from OHC directly, we remove the extra

Proof of Theorem
1

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}

Viewing each

1

\mathcal{I}

. Let

denote the maximum number of
1
’s in a row of
\mathcal{I}

, then
\OPT(\mathcal{I})=\FF(\mathcal{I})=1
. So, we assume

\mathcal{I}
in

and other notations. Obviously,

.

Counting by rows of
\mathcal{I}

. Next, we count by columns of
\mathcal{I}
. In fact, we will count by bins used by
FirstFit
. We say a vector

is

for bin

if

cannot be put into bin
j
by

such that bin
j
is full (i.e., has
B

1

r

v
in row
r
is
1

Since each row contains at most
p
many
1

1
in

can be unfit for
\leq(p-1)/B

puts the online vector

into bin

must contain at least

(i-1)/((p-1)/B)=B(i-1)/(p-1)

Observe also that for

contains at least
B

contains at least

\frac{B^{2}(i-1)}{p-1}

’s.
The last bin, i.e., bin

1

Summing over all bins (i.e., counting by ‘column’) and applying the upper bound on

dp\geq w\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\FF-1}\frac{B^{2}(i-1)}{p-1}\right)+\frac{B(\FF-1)}{p-1}=\frac{1}{p-1}\cdot\left(B^{2}\cdot\frac{(\FF-1)(\FF-2)}{2}+B(\FF-1)\right).

(1)

Solving inequality (
1
), we get

\FF\leq\frac{1}{2B}\cdot\Big(\sqrt{8dp^{2}-8dp+(B-2)^{2}}+3B-2\Big)
.
Note that

holds for every

\FF<\frac{(2\sqrt{2d}p-1)+|B-2|+3B-2}{2B}
.
For

\FF<\sqrt{2d}p+0.5\leq\sqrt{2d}\cdot\OPT+0.5

For

\FF<\sqrt{2d}\cdot p/B+2(B-1.25)/B\leq\sqrt{2d}\cdot\OPT+2(B-1.25)/B

∎

By the same double counting proof, one can prove a similar bound for the

We say
\mathcal{F}
is
(p,C)
-diverse
if there exists a partition

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{q}

such that for every
1\leq i\leq q
:
(i)
|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\geq p
, and
(ii)
|T|\geq i
for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
.
We say
\mathcal{F}
is
(p,C)
-starry
if instead of condition (ii) we require (ii’)
c_{i}\in T
for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
.

Sometimes we omit the notation
(p,C)
when it is clear from the context. For convenience, we call each part in the diverse or starry partition a
block
. In the starry partition, we call block
\mathcal{F}_{i}
a
c_{i}
-star. Clearly,
\mathcal{F}
in (
6
) admits a
(4,\{1,2,3\})
-diverse partition. In fact,
\mathcal{F}
also admits a
(4,\{1,2,3\})
-starry partition:

\mathcal{F}=\big\{\{1\},\{1\},\{1,3\},\{1,3\}\big\}\cup\big\{\{2\},\{2,3\},\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\big\}\cup\big\{\{3\},\{1,3\},\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\big\}.

(7)

A
(p,C)
-starry multi-family needs not be
(p,C)
-diverse. For example,

\mathcal{F}=\big\{\{1\},\{1\},\{2\},\{2\}\big\}

is
(2,\{1,2\})
-starry but is not
(2,\{1,2\})
-diverse.

Theorem 9
.

For every
p\geq 1
, for every
C\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, every
(p,C)
-diverse multi-family is also
(p,C)
-starry.

Proof.

We use induction on
q=|C|
. The base case
q=1
is trivial. Assume the claim is true for every
C
of size
q
. Now, consider

C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{q+1}\}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
(p,C)
-diverse multi-family on
C
with a diverse partition

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{q+1}
.
From this diverse partition, we use a greedy strategy to construct a starry partition, as follows.

Respecting the order

\mathcal{F}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{F}_{q+1}
,
successively and greedily choose subsets containing
c_{1}
from each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
as many as possible, until we reach a sub-multi-family
\mathcal{G}
of cardinality
|\mathcal{G}|=p
. Note that it is always possible to find such
\mathcal{G}
of cardinality
|\mathcal{G}|=p
: this is because
\mathcal{F}
is diverse, in particular,
\mathcal{F}_{q+1}
is a sub-family of
\mathcal{F}
such that every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{q+1}
contains
c_{1}
and
|\mathcal{F}_{q+1}|\geq p
.
Now,
\mathcal{G}
is a desired
c_{1}
-star, to construct the other stars, we consider
\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G}
.
Define a map

\phi:2^{C}\to 2^{C^{\prime}}

by

\phi(T)=T-\{c_{1}\}
.
Note that

\phi(C)=C-\{c_{1}\}
,
hence,
|\phi(C)|=q
.
We use the notation
\phi(\mathcal{F})
to denote the resulting multi-family on
\phi(C)
.
Observe that it suffices to show
the multi-family
\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})
is
(p,\phi(C))
-diverse.
By induction, this implies

\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})
is
(p,\phi(C))
-starry.
Clearly, by pulling back the starry partition for
\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})
together with
\mathcal{G}
gives a
(p,C)
-starry partition for
\mathcal{F}
.

It remains to show
\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})
is
(p,\phi(C))
-diverse. Recall the diverse partition

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{q+1}
.
For each
\mathcal{F}_{i}
, write

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}\cup\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}
,
where

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}=\mathcal{F}_{i}\cap\mathcal{G}

and

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}=\mathcal{F}_{i}-\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}
.
By the choice of
\mathcal{G}
,

\sum_{i=1}^{q+1}|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}|=|\mathcal{G}|=p\geq 1
.
Let
1\leq j^{*}\leq q+1
be the largest index for which
\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}}^{g}>0
, in other words,
\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}}
is the place at which the greedy choice of
\mathcal{G}
stops.
We have

\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})=\phi(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{r})\cup\cdots\cup\phi(\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}}^{r})\cup\cdots\cup\phi(\mathcal{F}_{q+1}^{r}).

(8)

Case 1:
j^{*}+1\leq i\leq q+1
. For such
i
, by the choice of
j^{*}
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}=\emptyset
,
or equivalently,

\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}=\mathcal{F}_{i}
.
Hence,

|\phi(\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r})|=|\phi(\mathcal{F}_{i})|=|\mathcal{F}_{i}|\geq p
.
Since every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{i}
satisfies

|T|\geq i
,
we have every
\phi(T)\in\phi(\mathcal{F}_{i})
satisfies

|\phi(T)|\geq i-1
.
These

\phi(\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}+1}^{r}),\ldots,\phi(\mathcal{F}_{q+1}^{r})

will be the last
q-(j^{*}-1)
blocks for a diverse partition of
\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})

Case 2:
1\leq i\leq j^{*}
. Consider those
i<j^{*}
first. Since we choose
\mathcal{G}
greedily, this means

c_{1}\not\in T
for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}
,
since otherwise
T
would have been chosen into
\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}
. This implies

\phi(T)=T
for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}
.
Hence,

|\phi(T)|=|T|\geq i
for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}
.
Let us also note that, simply by definition,

|\phi(T)|\geq j^{*}-1
for every
T\in\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}}^{r}
.
Now, consider the following
j^{*}-1
objects:

\phi(\mathcal{F}_{1}^{r}),\ldots,\phi(\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}-1}^{r})\cup\phi(\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}}^{r})
.
What we have shown is that every
\phi(T)
in the
i
-th object in the above list satisfies

|\phi(T)|\geq i
.
However, these
j^{*}-1
objects may not satisfy Condition (i) for the diverse partition. For this purpose, we use a
backward
greedy strategy to reallocate: starting from the last (i.e., the
(j^{*}-1)
-th) object, keep arbitrarily
p
subsets, and move the rest to the next, and repeat. Note that after this reallocation, every subset in the
i
-th block still has size at least
i
. It only remains to show that this backward greedy strategy is feasible, in other words, at every step
1\leq k\leq j^{*}-1
, the current object contains at least
p
subsets. Indeed, consider

\phi(\mathcal{F}_{k}^{r}),\ldots,\phi(\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}-1}^{r})\cup\phi(\mathcal{F}_{j^{*}}^{r})
.
The total cardinality (i.e., the total number of subsets in these objects) is

\sum_{i=k}^{j^{*}}|\phi(\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r})|=\sum_{i=k}^{j^{*}}|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{r}|\geq\sum_{i=k}^{j^{*}}(p-|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}|)\geq(j^{*}-k)p,

where we used the fact that

\sum_{i=k}^{j^{*}}|\mathcal{F}_{i}^{g}|\leq|\mathcal{G}|=p
.
Since before step
k
, we have chosen exactly

(j^{*}-1-k)p

subsets, therefore, at least
p
subsets are left to be chosen at step
k
, as desired.

The blocks in Case 1 and Case 2 together give a
(p,\phi(\Omega))
-diverse partition for
\phi(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{G})
, as desired.
∎

A lower bound for online coloring uniform hypertrees

With Theorem
9
, we are ready to prove Theorem
3
. Our proof strategy is illustrated in Figure
2
.

A vertex
v
merges
F_{3}
by a greedy selection of hyperedges. Here,
k=5,m=4
. The multi-family in (

) is the multi-family of color subsets of

, where its starry partition in (

.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
T_{m}
denote the online hypertree of size
\leq k^{m-1}
that we will construct to satisfy

\ALG(T_{m})\geq m
.
We use induction on
m\geq 1
.
For base case
m=1
,
T_{1}
consists of a single vertex, and this clearly works.
For
m\geq 2
, we construct
T_{m}
inductively in two steps:

STEP 1: for every

i=1,\ldots,m-1
,
successively construct
k-1
disjoint copies of
T_{i}
, let
F_{m-1}
denote this hyperforest consisting of exactly

(m-1)(k-1)

disjoint hypertrees.
F_{m-1}
is naturally partitioned into
m-1
blocks
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m-1}
, where
B_{i}
consists of
k-1
disjoint copies of
T_{i}
.

STEP 2: create a new vertex
v
to merge the disjoint hypertrees in
F_{m-1}
into a single hypertree
T_{m}
.

By induction hypothesis, each copy of
T_{i}
has size at most
k^{i-1}
and
\ALG
uses at least
i
colors on it. Hence,
the number of vertices of
T_{m}
is at most

1+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(k-1)k^{i-1}=k^{m-1}

as desired.
Also,

\ALG(F_{m-1})\geq\ALG(T_{m-1})\geq m-1
.

Case 1:
\ALG(F_{m-1})\geq m
. In this case, STEP 2 can be done in an arbitrary way, e.g., for each block
B_{i}
, for every
T\in B_{i}
, arbitrarily choose a vertex from
T
, let
e_{i}
be the edge containing
v
together with these
k-1
vertices from block
B_{i}
. Obviously, the obtained hypergraph
T_{m}
is a hypertree satisfying

\ALG(T_{m})\geq\ALG(F_{m-1})\geq m
.

Case 2:
\ALG(F_{m-1})=m-1
. Without loss of generality, assume

\ALG(F_{m-1})=[m-1]
.
For every
1\leq j\leq k-1
,
Let
T_{i,j}
denote the set of colors used by
\ALG
on the
j
-th copy of
T_{i}
. Then,

T_{i,j}\subseteq[m-1]
,
and by induction

|T_{i,j}|\geq i
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be the multi-family consisting of all these color subsets
T_{i,j}
, for

i=1,\ldots,m-1
and
j=1,\ldots,k-1
.
Obviously, the block partition

F_{m-1}=B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{m-1}

induces a
(k-1,[m-1])
-diverse partition for the multi-family
\mathcal{T}
.
By Theorem
9
,

\mathcal{T}
is also
(k-1,[m-1])
-starry.
Consider a starry partition:

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{T}_{m-1}
.
Since every color subset in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
contains color
i
, equivalently, this means that the corresponding hypertree contains a vertex with color
i
.
Construct a
k
-uniform hyperedge
e_{i}
incident to
v
, by picking
k-1
vertices of color
i
, one vertex from each of these hypertrees corresponding to the block
\mathcal{T}_{i}
. Clearly, these
m-1
hyperedges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m-1}
force
\ALG
to use a color that is not in
[m-1]
. Hence,
\ALG(T_{m})\geq m
as desired.
∎

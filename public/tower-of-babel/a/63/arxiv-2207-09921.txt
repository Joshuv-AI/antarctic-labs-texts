Quantitative Versions of the Two-dimensional Gaussian Product Inequalities
Ze-Chun Hu, Han Zhao, Qian-Qian Zhou

Abstract
The Gaussian product inequality (GPI) conjecture is one of the most famous inequalities associated with Gaussian distributions and has attracted a lot of concerns. In this note, we investigate the quantitative versions of the two-dimensional Gaussian product inequalities. For any centered non-degenerate two-dimensional Gaussian random vector ( X 1 , X 2 ) (X_{1},X_{2}) with variances σ 1 2 , σ 2 2 \sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2} and the correlation coefficient ρ \rho , we prove that for any real numbers α 1 , α 2 ∈ ( − 1 , 0 ) \alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(-1,0) or α 1 , α 2 ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) \alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,\infty) , it holds that 𝐄 ⁡ [ | X 1 | α 1 ​ | X 2 | α 2 ] − 𝐄 ⁡ [ | X 1 | α 1 ] ​ 𝐄 ​ [ | X 2 | α 2 ] ≥ f ⁡ ( σ 1 , σ 2 , α 1 , α 2 , ρ ) ≥ 0 , {\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]-{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]\geq f(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho)\geq 0, where the function f ⁡ ( σ 1 , σ 2 , α 1 , α 2 , ρ ) f(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho) will be given explicitly by Gamma function and is positive when ρ ≠ 0 \rho\neq 0 . When − 1 < α 1 < 0 -1<\alpha_{1}<0 and α 2 > 0 , \alpha_{2}>0, Russell and Sun (arXiv: 2205.10231v1) proved the “opposite Gaussian product inequality”, of which we will also give a quantitative version. These quantitative inequalities are derived by employing the hypergeometric functions and the generalized hypergeometric functions.

1
Introduction

The Gaussian product inequality (GPI) conjecture is one of the most famous inequalities associated with Gaussian distributions, which states that for any
d
-dimensional real-valued centered Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{2m}\right]\geq\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\bf E}\left[X_{j}^{2m}\right],\ m\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.1)

In
[
10
]
, Li and Wei proposed the following improved version of the GPI conjecture:

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{d}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right]\geq\prod_{j=1}^{d}{\bf E}\left[|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right],

(1.2)

where
\alpha_{j},j=1,2,\ldots,d
, are nonnegative real numbers.

Up to now, the GPI (
1.1
) and the GPI (
1.2
) are still open, however, some special cases have been proved by using various tools. Frenkel
[
5
]
proved (
1.1
) with
m=1
(or (
1.2
) for the case
\alpha_{j}=2
) by using algebraic methods. Malicet et al.
[
11
]
gave an analytic proof to Frenkel’s result among other things. Wei
[
18
]
proved a stronger version of (
1.2
) for
\alpha_{j}\in(-1,0)
as follows:

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{d}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right]\geq{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right]{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=k+1}^{d}|X_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}\right],\ \forall 1\leq k\leq d-1.

(1.3)

By Karlin and Rinott
[
8
]
, we know that (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold for
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
when the density of
|{\bf X}|=(|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|,\ldots,|X_{d}|)
satisfies the so-called condition
MTP
2
, and for any non-degenerate 2-dimensional centered Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2}),

(|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|)
has an
MTP
2
density. Thus (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) hold for
d=2.

Lan et al.
[
9
]
used the hypergeometric functions to prove the following 3-dimensional GPI: for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and any centered Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
,

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2m_{1}}X_{2}^{2m_{2}}X_{3}^{2m_{2}}\right]\geq{\bf E}\left[X_{1}^{2m_{1}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{2}^{2m_{2}}\right]{\bf E}\left[X_{3}^{2m_{2}}\right].

(1.4)

Genest and Ouimet
[
6
]
proved that if there exists a matrix
C\in[0,+\infty)^{d\times d}
such that
(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d})=(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{d})C
in law, where
(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{d})
is a
d
-dimensional standard Gaussian random vector, the following stronger version of (
1.1
) holds:

\displaystyle{\bf E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}^{2m_{j}}\right]\geq\mathbf{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{j}^{2m_{j}}\right]\mathbf{E}\left[\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}X_{j}^{2m_{j}}\right],\ m_{j}\in\mathbb{N},j=1,\ldots,n,\ \forall 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(1.5)

Russell and Sun
[
15
]
proved among other things that (
1.5
) holds if all the correlation coefficients are nonnegative. Edelmann et al.
[
4
]
extended (
1.5
) to the multivarite gamma distributions. As to other related work, we refer to Russell and Sun
[
16
]
, Russell and Sun
[
17
]
, Genest and Ouiment
[
7
]
.

Recently, De et al.
[
3
]
obtained a range of quantitative correlation inequalities, including the quantitative version of the Gaussian correlation inequality proved by Royen
[
14
]
, and the quantitative version of the well-known Fortuin-Kasteleyn-Ginibre (FKG) inequality for monotone functions over
any finite product probability space. Motivated by
[
3
]
, we will consider the quantitative versions of the two-dimensional Gaussian product inequalities in this note.

For any centered non-degenerate two-dimensional Gaussian random vector
(X_{1},X_{2})
with variances
\sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2}
and the correlation coefficient
\rho
, we will prove that for any real numbers
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(-1,0)
or
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,\infty)
, it holds that

{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]-{\bf E}[|X_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}]{\bf E}[|X_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}]\geq f(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho)\geq 0,

where the function
f(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho)
will be given explicitly by Gamma function and is positive when
\rho\neq 0
. When
-1<\alpha_{1}<0
and
\alpha_{2}>0,
Russell and Sun
[
17
]
proved the “opposite Gaussian product inequality”, of which we will also give a quantitative version. These quantitative inequalities are derived by employing the hypergeometric functions and the generalized hypergeometric functions..

The rest of this note is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present the main results and some corollaries. In Section 3, we give the proofs. In the final section, we give some remarks.

2
Main results

Throughout this note, any Gaussian random variable is assumed to be real-valued and non-degenerate. Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 2.1

Let
(X_{1},X_{2})
be centered bivariate Gaussian random variables with
\textbf{E}[X_{1}^{2}]={\sigma_{1}}^{2}
,
\textbf{E}[X_{2}^{2}]={\sigma_{2}}^{2}
and the correlation coefficient
\rho
satifying
|\rho|<1
. Then for any real numbers
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(-1,0)
or
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]\geq f(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho),

(2.1)

where the nonnegative function
f(\cdot)
is defined by

\displaystyle f(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\rho)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{2\pi}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}),&\mbox{if}\ -1<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}<0\ \mbox{or}\ 0<\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq 2\ \mbox{or}\ \alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}>2,\\
\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}{2}),&\mbox{if}\ \alpha_{1}>2,0<\alpha_{2}<2\ \mbox{or}\ 0<\alpha_{1}<2,\alpha_{2}>2.\end{array}\right.

When the real numbers
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
in Theorem
2.1
have opposite signs, Russell and Sun
[
17
]
proved the following opposite GPI:

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]\leq\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}].

(2.3)

We have the quantitative version of the GPI (
2.3
) as follows.

Theorem 2.2

Let
(X_{1},X_{2})
be centered bivariate Gaussian random variables with
\textbf{E}[X_{1}^{2}]={\sigma_{1}}^{2}
,
\textbf{E}[X_{2}^{2}]={\sigma_{2}}^{2}
and the correlation coefficient
\rho
satifying
|\rho|<1
. Let
\alpha_{1}\in(-1,0)
and
\alpha_{2}\in(0,\infty)
.

(i) When

,

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{2\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\textbf{E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}\right]-\textbf{E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}\right]\textbf{E}\left[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{2\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)\leq 0,

(2.4)

where
F(\cdot)
is the hypergeometric function defined by

\displaystyle F(a,b;c;z):=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\cdot\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\ |z|\leq 1,

(2.5)

and, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle(\alpha)_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\alpha(\alpha+1)\dots(\alpha+n-1),&n\geq 1,\\
1,&n=0,\alpha\neq 0.\\
\end{array}\right.

(ii) When
\alpha_{2}>2
,

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{2\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\textbf{E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}\right]-\textbf{E}\left[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}\right]\textbf{E}\left[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{2\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};1\right),\,0\right\}.\quad\quad

(2.6)

By Theorem
2.1
, we have the following corollaries.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\{1,2\}
in Theorem
2.1
, then we have

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}{|X_{2}|}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}]\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\rho^{2}}{\pi},

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}{X_{2}}^{2}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}]\textbf{E}[{X_{2}}^{2}]\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}^{2}\rho^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}},

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{X_{1}}^{2}{X_{2}}^{2}]-\textbf{E}[{X_{1}}^{2}]\textbf{E}[{X_{2}}^{2}]\geq 2\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma^{2}_{2}\rho^{2}.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
, suppose that
\alpha_{2}=1
,
\alpha_{1}
is an integer satisfying
\alpha_{1}=m>2
. Then

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{m}{|X_{2}|}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{m}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}]\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\frac{(m-2)!!\,m\sigma_{1}^{m}\sigma_{2}\rho^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},&\text{if }m\;is\;even,\\

\alpha_{1}
and

are integers and
\alpha_{1}=m,\alpha_{2}=n
with

are both even integers, then

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{X_{1}}^{m}{X_{2}}^{n}]-\textbf{E}[{X_{1}}^{m}]\textbf{E}[{X_{2}}^{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(m-1)!!(n-1)!!mn\sigma_{1}^{m}\sigma_{2}^{n}\rho^{2}}{2};

(ii) if

are both odd integers, then

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{m}{|X_{2}|}^{n}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{m}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{n}]

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{m}{|X_{2}|}^{n}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{m}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(m-1)!!(n-1)!!mn\sigma_{1}^{m}\sigma_{2}^{n}\rho^{2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{i}|}^{\alpha_{i}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{i}}{|x|}^{\alpha_{i}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma_{i}^{2}}}dx=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{\alpha_{i}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2\sigma_{i}^{2}}}dx

Then

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right).

Since

[

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right),

It follows from (

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)\left[F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right)-1\right].

Since

), we get

\displaystyle F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right)-1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{2}\cdot\rho^{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2})}.

By (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{2}\cdot\rho^{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\rho^{2}}{4\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}-1}{2}\right).

The proof is complete.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
2.2

By the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we know that

\displaystyle\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]-\textbf{E}[{|X_{1}|}^{\alpha_{1}}]\textbf{E}[{|X_{2}|}^{\alpha_{2}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}}\sigma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{2}\right)\left[F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right)-1\right],

(3.16)

\displaystyle F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right)-1=\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\rho^{2}}F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};h\right)dh,

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dh}F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};h\right)=\frac{2}{3}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\right)(1-h)^{\frac{1+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}-2}F\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{5}{2};h\right).

Case I
:
0<\alpha_{2}\leq 2
. In this case
1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}\geq 0
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dh}F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};h\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Then by (
3.17
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\rho^{2}}{2}F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};1\right)\leq F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right)-1\leq\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\rho^{2}}{2}\leq 0,

which together with (
3.16
) implies (
2.2
).

Case II
:
\alpha_{2}>2
. In this case,
1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2}<0
, and thus

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dh}F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};h\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Then by (
3.17
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\rho^{2}}{2}\leq F\left(-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{1}{2};\rho^{2}\right)-1\leq\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\rho^{2}}{2}F\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2},1-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{2};\frac{3}{2};1\right),

which together with (
3.16
) implies (
2.2
).

The proof is complete.

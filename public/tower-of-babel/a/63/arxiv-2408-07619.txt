Directional Chebyshev Constants on the Boundary
Thomas Bloom, Norman Levenberg

Abstract
We prove results on existence of limits in the definition of (weighted) directional Chebyshev constants at all points of the standard simplex Σ ⊂ ℝ d \Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} for (locally) regular compact sets K ⊂ ℂ d K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d} .



1
Introduction

The multivariate transfinite diameter of a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
was introduced by Leja as a generalization of the notion defined by Fekete in the univariate case (
d=1
). Let
e_{1}(z),\ldots,e_{j}(z),\ldots
be a listing of the monomials

e_{j}(z)=z^{\alpha(j)}=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}(j)}\cdots z_{d}^{\alpha_{d}(j)}
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
where the multi-indices
\alpha(j)=(\alpha_{1}(j),...,\alpha_{d}(j))
are arranged in some order
\prec
with the property that
\alpha\prec\beta
if
|\alpha|<|\beta|
. Let
d_{n}
be the dimension of the space
\mathcal{P}_{n}
of holomorphic polynomials of degree at most
n
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. For
\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{d_{n}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, let

VDM(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{d_{n}}):=\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits[e_{i}(\zeta_{j})]_{i,j=1,...,d_{n}}=\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}e_{1}(\zeta_{1})&e_{1}(\zeta_{2})&\ldots&e_{1}(\zeta_{d_{n}})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
e_{d_{n}}(\zeta_{1})&e_{d_{n}}(\zeta_{2})&\ldots&e_{d_{n}}(\zeta_{d_{n}})\end{array}\right]

(1.1)

and for a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
let

V_{n}=V_{n}(K):=\max_{\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{d_{n}}\in K}|VDM(\zeta_{1},...,\zeta_{d_{n}})|.

(1.2)

Then

\delta(K):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}V_{n}^{1/l_{n}}

(1.3)

is the transfinite diameter of
K
where
l_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{n}}{\rm deg}(e_{j})
.

For this definition, one only requires
\alpha\prec\beta
if
|\alpha|<|\beta|
, but for what follows, it will be convenient to use a particular order
\prec
; in this paper we will take one satisfying (by abuse of notation)
z_{1}\prec z_{2}\cdots\prec z_{d}
. Zaharjuta
[
8
]
verified the existence of the limit in (
1.3
) by introducing directional Chebyshev constants
\tau(K,\theta)
and proving

\delta(K)=\exp\left(\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\int_{\Sigma^{0}}\log\tau(K,\theta)d\theta\right)

(1.4)

where
\Sigma:=\{(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:0\leq x_{i}\leq 1,\ \sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{i}=1\}
is the standard simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
;
\Sigma^{0}=\{(x_{1},...,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:0<x_{i}<1,\ \sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{i}=1\}
; and
|\Sigma|
is the
(d-1)-
dimensional measure of
\Sigma
. If we need to specify the dimension, we will write
\Sigma^{(d)}
for
\Sigma
. To define
\tau(K,\theta)
, for each multi-index
\alpha
we let

M_{\alpha}:=\{p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},...,z_{d}]:p(z)=z^{\alpha}+\sum_{\beta\prec\alpha}c_{\beta}z^{\beta}\}

(1.5)

and define the corresponding Chebyshev constants

\tau_{K,\alpha}:=\inf\{||p||_{K}:=\max_{z\in K}|p(z)|:p\in M_{\alpha}\}^{1/|\alpha|}.

Zaharjuta showed that for
\theta\in\Sigma^{0}
, the limit

\tau(K,\theta):=\lim_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}

exists and
\theta\to\tau(K,\theta)
is continuous on
\Sigma^{0}
.

For
\theta\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{0}
, the limit need not exist; an elementary example was given by Zaharjuta: let
K=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:z_{2}=0,\ |z_{1}|\leq 1\}
and
\theta=(1,0)
(see Remark 1 in
[
8
]
). Here
K
is pluripolar; utilizing this set one can easily construct nonpluripolar examples with the same property. However, If
K
is sufficiently nice, our main result is that the limit exists. Given
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
compact, for
\theta\in\Sigma
we will define

\tau(K,\theta):=\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}.

(1.6)

In fact, more generally, given a continuous, admissible weight function
w
on
K
, i.e.,
w\geq 0
with
\{z\in K:w(z)>0\}
nonpluripolar, one can define
weighted
Chebyshev constants

\tau^{w}_{K,\alpha}:=\inf\{||w^{|\alpha|}p||_{K}:p\in M_{\alpha}\}^{1\over|\alpha|}.

Then for
\theta\in\Sigma
, define

\tau^{w}(K,\theta):=\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau^{w}_{K,\alpha}.

(1.7)

It follows from
[
4
]
that for
\theta\in\Sigma^{0}
, the limit in (
1.7
) exists.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
compact and regular, if
\theta\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{0}
,

\tau(K,\theta)=\lim_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha};

i.e., the limit in (
1.6
) exists. For
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
compact and locally regular, and
w\geq 0
a continuous, admissible weight function on
K
, if
\theta\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{0}
,

\tau^{w}(K,\theta)=\lim_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau^{w}_{K,\alpha};

i.e., the limit in (
1.7
) exists.

Our interest in this result is that it provides an essential step in proving a result on convergence in probability related to random polynomials in a weighted setting
[
2
]
.

We recall these notions of regularity for
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
compact and other relevant notions in pluripotential theory in the next section. Section 3 contains the proof of Theorem
1.1
and an appendix follows with proofs of two auxiliary results.

2
Pluripotential preliminaries

Recall
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
denotes the holomorphic polynomials of degree at most
n
in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. For
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
compact, we let
V_{K}^{*}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z}V_{K}(\zeta)
where

V_{K}(z):=\sup\{u(z):u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})\ \hbox{and}\ u\leq 0\ \hbox{on}\ K\}

=\sup\{{1\over{\rm deg}(p)}\log|p(z)|:p\in\bigcup{\mathcal{P}}_{n},\ ||p||_{K}\leq 1\}.

Here
L(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is the set of all plurisubharmonic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
of logarithmic growth; i.e.,
u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})
if
u
is plurisubharmonic in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
u(z)\leq\log|z|+0(1)
as
|z|\to\infty
. It is known that
K
is pluripolar if and only if
V_{K}^{*}\equiv+\infty
; otherwise
V_{K}^{*}\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})
. A compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
is
regular
at
a\in K
if
V_{K}
is continuous at
a
;
K
is regular if it is continuous on
K
, i.e., if
V_{K}=V_{K}^{*}
. A stronger notion is that of
local regularity
:
K
is locally regular if it is locally regular at each of its points; i.e., for
a\in K
and
r>0
,
V_{K\cap B(a,r)}^{*}(a)=0
where
B(a,r):=\{z:|z-a|\leq r\}
. Finally,
K
is
polynomially convex
if
K=\widehat{K}
where

\widehat{K}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:|p(z)|\leq||p||_{K},\ p\ {\rm polynomial}\}.

Note that for any compact set
K
,
V_{K}=V_{\widehat{K}}
because
||p||_{K}=||p||_{\widehat{K}}
for any
p\in\bigcup{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
.

Next, for a continuous, admissible weight function
w
on
K
, let
Q:=-\log w
and define the weighted extremal function
V^{*}_{K,Q}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z}V_{K,Q}(\zeta)
where

V_{K,Q}(z):=\sup\{u(z):u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d}),\ u\leq Q\ \hbox{on}\ K\}

=\sup\{{1\over{\rm deg}(p)}\log|p(z)|:p\in\bigcup{\mathcal{P}}_{n},\ ||w^{deg(p)}p||_{K}\leq 1\}.

If
K
is locally regular and
w
is continuous, it is known that
V_{K,Q}=V_{K,Q}^{*}
; i.e., this function is continuous. If
K
is only regular, this conclusion may fail.

For a function
u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})
, the
Robin function
\bar{\rho}_{u}
associated to
u
is defined as

\bar{\rho}_{u}(z):=\limsup_{|\lambda|\to\infty}[u(\lambda z)-\log|\lambda|].

(2.1)

Such a function is logarithmically homogeneous:
\bar{\rho}_{u}(tz)=\bar{\rho}_{u}(z)+\log|t|
for
t\in\mathbb{C}
.
In particular, for
K
nonpluripolar, we write
\bar{\rho}_{K}:=\bar{\rho}_{V_{K}^{*}}
(similarly,
\bar{\rho}_{K,Q}:=\bar{\rho}_{V_{K,Q}^{*}}
) and define

K_{\rho}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\bar{\rho}_{K}\leq 0\}.

(2.2)

The set
K_{\rho}
is
circled
; i.e.,
z\in K_{\rho}
implies
e^{i\phi}z\in K_{\rho}
for
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
V_{K_{\rho}}=\max[0,\bar{\rho}_{K}]
.

We follow the notation and terminology of
[
1
]
. Given
K
compact and a multiindex
\alpha
, we let
t_{\alpha,K}\in M_{\alpha}
be a Chebyshev polynomial; i.e.,
||t_{\alpha,K}||=\tau_{K,\alpha}^{|\alpha|}
. More generally, if
w\geq 0
is an admissible weight on
K
, we let
t^{w}_{\alpha,K}\in M_{\alpha}
be a weighted Chebyshev polynomial; i.e.,
||t^{w}_{\alpha,K}||=(\tau^{w}_{K,\alpha})^{|\alpha|}
. For a polynomial
p(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq d}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
with at least one
c_{\alpha}\not=0
when
|\alpha|=d
, we let
\widehat{p}(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=d}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
. For such
p
, letting
u(z):=\frac{1}{d}\log|p(z)|
, we have
u\in L(\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
\bar{\rho}_{u}(z)=\frac{1}{d}\log|\widehat{p}(z)|
. Finally, given a homogeneous polynomial
Q
of degree
d
, we let
Tch_{K}Q
denote a (Chebyshev) polynomial satisfying
\widehat{Tch_{K}Q}=Q
and

||Tch_{K}Q||_{K}=\inf\{||Q+h||_{K}:h\in\mathcal{P}_{d-1}\}.

Directional Chebyshev constants on the boundary

The proof of our main result, Theorem
1.1
, consists of three steps:

1.

the limit in (
1.6
) exists for
K
circled and regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
implies the limit in (
1.6
) exists for
K
compact and regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
(Proposition
3.1
);

2.

the limit in (
1.6
) exists for
K
compact and regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
implies the limit (
1.7
) in the weighted case exists for
K
compact and locally regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
w
admissible and continuous on
K
(Proposition
3.2
);

3.

the limit (
1.7
) in the weighted case exists for
K
compact and locally regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
and
w
admissible and and continuous on
K
implies the limit in (
1.6
) exists for
K
circled and regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
(Proposition
3.4

From 1.-3. and the standard fact that, for
K\subset\mathbb{C}
compact and
w
admissible on
K
, the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{K,n}^{w}
of weighted Chebyshev constants

\tau_{K,n}^{w}:=\bigl(\inf\{||w^{n}p_{n}||_{K}:p_{n}(z)=z^{n}+...\}\bigr)^{1/n}

exists (cf., Theorem III.3.1 in
[
7
]
), Theorem
1.1
follows. We summarize the argument at the end of this section.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a compact, regular set. Our first result relates directional Chebyshev constants of
K
with those of
K_{\rho}
(see (
2.2
)). We note that if
K
is regular, it is known that
\bar{\rho}_{K}
is continuous and
K_{\rho}
is regular (cf., Corollary 4.4 of
[
5
]
). Fix
\theta\in\Sigma
. Let
\{\alpha(j)\}
for
j=1,2...

be a sequence of
d-
multi-indices with

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(j)}{|\alpha(j)|}=\theta.

(3.1)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a compact, regular set.
Suppose that for all sequences satisfying (
3.1
)

\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}_{K_{\rho}}=:\tau(K_{\rho},\theta)

exists and all the limits are equal. Then for all sequences satisfying (
3.1
)

\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K}||^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}=:\tau(K,\theta)

exists and all the limits are equal. Furthermore,
\tau(K_{\rho},\theta)
=
\tau(K,\theta)
.

Proof.

From
[
1
]
, equation (3.7), we have

||\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K_{\rho}}\leq||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}.

Since
\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),K}
is a candidate for
t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}

||t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||_{K_{\rho}}\leq||\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K_{\rho}}.

From the previous two inequalities, together with our hypothesis, we have

\tau(K_{\rho},\theta)=\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}_{K_{\rho}}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}.

(3.2)

Now
K_{\rho}
is circled; thus
t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}
may be taken to be homogeneous of degree
|\alpha(j)|
. Moreover, the extremal function for
K_{\rho}
is given by
V_{K_{\rho}}=\max[0,\bar{\rho}_{K}]
.

Thus,

\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||_{K_{\rho}}+\bar{\rho}_{K}(z);

hence

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}(z)|\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||_{K_{\rho}}+\bar{\rho}_{K}(z)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

Using the hypothesis that the limit on the right of the above equation exists, as in
[
1
]
, equation (3.11), we have

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||Tch_{K}t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||_{K}\leq\log\tau(K_{\rho},\theta).

But by the defining property of
t_{\alpha(j),K}
,

||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}\leq||Tch_{K}t_{\alpha(j),K_{\rho}}||_{K}

so that

\limsup||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}\leq\tau(K_{\rho},\theta).

(3.3)

Combining (
3.3
) and (
3.2
) we conclude.
∎

For the next result we will use notation and results from
[
3
]
, particularly Proposition 3.4. Let
K\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
be a locally regular compact set and
w=e^{-Q}
a continuous admissible weight function on
K
. Let

Z=Z(K):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:V_{K,Q}(z)\leq M=M(K):=||V_{K,Q}||_{K}\}.

From the proof of Theorem 2.5 in
[
3
]
,
V_{Z}
is continuous, i.e.,
Z
is regular, and we have
V_{K,Q}=V_{Z}+M

on
\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus Z
. By Proposition 3.4 of
[
3
]
, for
\theta\in\Sigma^{0}
we have

\tau(Z,\theta)=e^{M}\tau^{w}(K,\theta).

We will show:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K
be compact and locally regular in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and let
w=e^{-Q}
be admissible and continuous on
K
.
Suppose that for all sequences satisfying (
3.1
)

\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{Z}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}=:\tau(Z,\theta)

exists and all limits are equal. Then for all sequences satisfying (
3.1
) all the limits

\lim_{j\to\infty}||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}=:\tau^{w}(K,\theta)

exist and are equal. Furthermore,
\tau(Z,\theta)=e^{M}\tau^{w}(K,\theta)
.

Proof.

The proof follows closely the proof of Proposition 3.4 of
[
3
]
. First,
t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}\in M_{\alpha(j)}
, hence it is a candidate for
t_{\alpha(j),Z}
so that

||t_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{Z}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|(j)|}}\leq||t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{Z}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|(j)|}}.

(3.4)

Let

p_{j}(z):=\frac{t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}(z)}{||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}}.

Then

\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|p_{j}(z)|=\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}(z)|-\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}.

Since
||w^{|\alpha(j)|}p_{j}||_{K}=1
, combining this with the defining property of
Z
we obtain

\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{Z}\leq M+\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}.

Taking exponentials and combining with (
3.4
) we have

||t_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{Z}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha|(j)}}\leq||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}e^{M}.

This yields

\tau(Z,\theta)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}e^{M}.

(3.5)

The proof will be completed by showing that for all sequences satisfying (
3.1
) that

\tau(Z,\theta)\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}||w^{|\alpha(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}e^{M}.

(3.6)

To this end, note that

\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|t_{\alpha(j),Z}(z)|\leq V_{Z}(z)+\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||t_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{Z}.

Taking the Robin function of both sides of the equation
we have

\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),Z}(z)|\leq\bar{\rho}_{Z}(z)+\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||t_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{Z}.

Since
V_{K,Q}=V_{Z}+M

on
\mathbb{C}^{d}\setminus Z
we have
\bar{\rho}_{K,Q}=\bar{\rho}_{Z}+M
, hence

\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),Z}(z)|\leq\bar{\rho}_{K,Q}(z)-M+\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log||t_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{Z}.

Taking limsup of both sides and recalling that the limit exists on the right side, we obtain

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}\log|\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),Z}(z)|\leq\bar{\rho}_{K,Q}(z)-M+\log\tau(Z,\theta).

By Theorem 3.3 of
[
3
]
,

\limsup_{j\to\infty}||w^{|\alpha(j)|}Tch_{K,w}\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),Z}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha_{j}|}}\leq e^{-M}\tau(Z,\theta).

This establishes (
3.6
) since
Tch_{K,w}\widehat{t}_{\alpha(j),Z}
is a candidate for

t^{w}_{\alpha(j),K}
.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose for
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
compact and locally regular and
w=e^{-Q}
any continuous admissible weight function on
M
, we have that for any

\theta^{\prime}=(\theta^{\prime}_{2},...,\theta^{\prime}_{d})\in\Sigma^{(d-1)}
, and for any sequence of
d-1
multi-indices satisfying

\alpha^{\prime}(j)=(\alpha^{\prime}_{2}(j),...,\alpha^{\prime}_{d}(j))
with

\lim_{j\rightarrow{\infty}}\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(j)}{|\alpha^{\prime}(j)|}\rightarrow{\theta^{\prime}}
,

\lim_{j\rightarrow{\infty}}||w^{|\alpha^{\prime}(j)|}t^{w}_{\alpha^{\prime}(j),M}||_{M}^{1/{|\alpha^{\prime}(j)|}}

exists. Then for any regular, circled compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
for any sequence of
d
multi-indices
\alpha(j)=(\alpha_{1}(j),...,\alpha_{d}(j))
with
j=1,2,...
and

\frac{\alpha(j)}{|\alpha(j)|}\rightarrow\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta^{\prime})\in\Sigma^{(d)}
where
\theta_{1}<1
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}

exists.

Proof.

Since Chebyshev polynomials for
K
and
\widehat{K}
are the same, we may assume
K=\widehat{K}
. Moreover, for circled
K
,
\widehat{K}
coincides with the homogeneous polynomial hull. It follows from (
2.2
) that
K=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\bar{\rho}_{K}\leq 0\}
where
\bar{\rho}_{K}
is the logarithmic
homogeneous Robin function of
K
. For
\epsilon>0
, we set
K_{\epsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\bar{\rho}_{K}(z)\leq-\epsilon\}
. Using Lemma
4.1
in the appendix, for some
\eta=\eta(\epsilon)>0
, the polynomial convex hull

\widehat{S}
of
S=S(\epsilon,\eta):=K\setminus\{|z_{1}|<\eta\}
contains
K_{\epsilon}
. Note that
S
is compact, circled and nonpluripolar. Moreover, it is known that we can approximate
\widehat{S}
from the outside by a sequence of locally regular homogeneous polynomial polyhedra (cf., p. 93 of
[
3
]
). Thus we may assume
\widehat{S}
is locally regular and we still have the inclusions

K_{\epsilon}\subset\widehat{S}\subset\widehat{K}.

Thus we obtain the following inequalities on Chebyshev polynomials:

||t_{\alpha,K_{\epsilon}}||_{K_{\epsilon}}\leq||t_{\alpha,S}||_{S}\leq||t_{\alpha,K}||_{K}.

(3.7)

The scaling
z\rightarrow e^{-\epsilon}z
maps
K
onto
K_{\epsilon}
which yields that

||t_{\alpha,K_{\epsilon}}||_{K_{\epsilon}}=e^{-\epsilon|\alpha|}||t_{\alpha,K}||_{K}.

(3.8)

Thus if, under condition (
3.1
) that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(j)}{|\alpha(j)|}=\theta\in\Sigma^{(d)}
, we knew that

\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),S}||_{S}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}

(3.9)

exists, then
\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}
also exists, since by (
3.7
) and (
3.8
)

\liminf_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}\geq\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),S}||_{S}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}\geq\limsup_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K_{\epsilon}}||_{K_{\epsilon}}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}

Thus

is a

We let

\widehat{S}
is and

The lexicographic ordering in

\alpha^{\prime}(j)=(\alpha(j)_{2},...,\alpha(j)_{d}),\ j=1,2,..

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
be compact, circled and regular. Let
\{\alpha(j)\}
for
j=1,2...

be a sequence of
d-
multi-indices with

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\alpha(j)}{|\alpha(j)|}=(1,0,0,...,0).

Then

\lim_{j\to\infty}||t_{\alpha(j),K}||_{K}^{\frac{1}{|\alpha(j)|}}

exists and is the same for all such
\{\alpha(j)\}
.

Proof.

We begin with some general facts in Zaharjuta’s paper
[
8
]
. In Lemma 3, he shows for an arbitrary compact set
K
that for
\theta\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{0}
one has

\tau_{-}(K,\theta):=\liminf_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}=\liminf_{\theta^{\prime}\to\theta,\ \theta^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta^{\prime}).

For the reader’s convenience, we give his argument to show that

\liminf_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}\leq\liminf_{\theta^{\prime}\to\theta,\ \theta^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta^{\prime}).

(3.14)

Indeed, choosing a sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}\subset\Sigma^{0}
with
\theta_{k}\to\theta
, we can find a sequence of integers
\{i_{k}\}
with
i_{k}\to\infty
such that

|\frac{\alpha(i_{k})}{|\alpha(i_{k})|}-\theta_{k}|<1/k\ \hbox{and}\ |\tau_{K,\alpha(i_{k})}-\tau(K,\theta_{k})|<1/k.

Then

\liminf_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\tau_{K,\alpha(i_{k})}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\tau(K,\theta_{k}).

Since this holds for any sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}\subset\Sigma^{0}
with
\theta_{k}\to\theta
, (
3.14
) follows.

A similar proof shows that for
\theta\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{0}

\tau(K,\theta):=\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}\geq\limsup_{\theta^{\prime}\to\theta,\ \theta^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta^{\prime}).

(3.15)

To see this, we first choose a sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}\subset\Sigma^{0}
with
\theta_{k}\to\theta
, and we then find a sequence of integers
\{i_{k}\}
with
i_{k}\to\infty
such that

|\frac{\alpha(i_{k})}{|\alpha(i_{k})|}-\theta_{k}|<1/k\ \hbox{and}\ |\tau_{K,\alpha(i_{k})}-\tau(K,\theta_{k})|<1/k.

Then

\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to\theta}\tau_{K,\alpha}\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\tau_{K,\alpha(i_{k})}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\tau(K,\theta_{k}).

Since this holds for any sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}\subset\Sigma^{0}
with
\theta_{k}\to\theta
, (
3.15
) follows.

For easier reading, we will write our Chebyshev constants as
\tau_{\alpha}(K)
since the multiindices
\alpha
will be important. First, since
z_{1}\prec...\prec z_{d}
and
K
is circled, to compute
\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K)
, we minimize sup norms on
K
over the
single
homogeneous polynomial
z_{1}^{n}
: thus
\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K)=\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K)=\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|

which is positive since
K
is not pluripolar. Take a sequence
\alpha_{j}
with
n_{j}:=|\alpha_{j}|\to\infty
and
\theta_{j}:=\frac{\alpha_{j}}{|\alpha_{j}|}\to(1,0,...,0)
. We can write

\theta_{j}=(1-\epsilon_{j},a_{j})

where
\epsilon_{j}=|a_{j}|\to 0
. We proceed as follows. Writing
z=(z_{1},z^{\prime})=(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, competitors for
\tau_{(0,n_{j}a_{j})}(K)
are homogeneous polynomials of the form

p_{0,n_{j}a_{j}}(z_{1},z^{\prime}):=z^{\prime n_{j}a_{j}}+...+az_{1}^{n_{j}\epsilon_{j}}.

Consider
\tau_{(n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j}),n_{j}a_{j})}(K)
where we minimize sup norms on
K
over the homogeneous polynomials of the form

z_{1}^{n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j})}z^{\prime n_{j}a_{j}}+...+az_{1}^{n_{j}}.

Some of these can be factored as

z_{1}^{n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j})}z^{\prime n_{j}a_{j}}+...+az_{1}^{n_{j}}=z_{1}^{n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j})}(z^{\prime n_{j}a_{j}}+...+az_{1}^{n_{j}\epsilon_{j}});

i.e., the competitors for
\tau_{(n_{j}(1-a_{j}),n_{j}\epsilon_{j})}(K)
include
z_{1}^{n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j})}
times the competitors for
\tau_{(0,n_{j}a_{j})}(K)
. Hence

\tau_{(n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j}),n_{j}a_{j})}(K)^{n_{j}}\leq\tau_{(0,n_{j}a_{j})}(K)^{n_{j}\epsilon_{j}}\cdot[\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|^{n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j})}]

showing that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\tau_{(n_{j}(1-\epsilon_{j}),n_{j}a_{j})}(K)\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\bigl(\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|^{1-\epsilon_{j}}\cdot\tau_{(0,n_{j}a_{j})}(K)^{\epsilon_{j}}\bigr)=\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|.

Thus we have shown that

\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to(1,0,...,0)}\tau_{K,\alpha}\leq\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K).

(3.16)

We will show that

\liminf_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta)\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K).

(3.17)

Given this, from (
3.14

\liminf_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to(1,0,...,0)}\tau_{K,\alpha}=\liminf_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta)
so that

\liminf_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to(1,0,...,0)}\tau_{K,\alpha}\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K).

On the other hand, from (
3.15
),
\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to(1,0,...,0)}\tau_{K,\alpha}\geq\limsup_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta)
and (
3.16
) gave us

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K)\geq\limsup_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to(1,0,...,0)}\tau_{K,\alpha}.

Putting these last two displayed inequalities together gives us that

\lim_{\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}\to(1,0,...,0)}\tau_{K,\alpha}

exists and equals
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K)
.

It remains to prove (
3.17
). We may assume
\widehat{K}=K=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\bar{\rho}_{K}\leq 0\}
and the intersection of
K
with any complex line through the origin is a closed disk centered at the origin in this complex line of positive radius. Suppose first that for
K\cap\{z^{\prime}=0\}=\{(z_{1},0)\in K\}
we have

\max\{|z_{1}|:(z_{1},0)\in K\}=\max_{(z_{1},z)\in K}|z_{1}|=:R.

Recall
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K)=\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|=R
. We can use interior approximation by an ellipsoid to get the lower bound for the
(1,0,...,0)
Chebshev constant of
K
. To be precise, by our hypothesis, for
r<R
, the closed disk

\{(z_{1},0):|z_{1}|\leq r\}

is contained in the interior of

and hence for some constant

the ellipsoid

E=E(A,r):=\{(z_{1},z^{\prime}):|z_{1}|^{2}/r^{2}+|z^{\prime}|^{2}/A^{2}\leq 1\}

is contained in the interior of
K
. Thus for all
\theta\in\Sigma^{0}
we have

\tau(K,\theta)\geq\tau(E,\theta).

Explicit calculation gives that

\lim_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(E,\theta)=r.

Hence

\liminf_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta)\geq r

for all

so that

\liminf_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta)\geq R=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K),

establishing (
3.17
) in this case.

Finally, suppose that

\max\{|z_{1}|:(z_{1},0)\in K\}<\max_{(z_{1},z^{\prime})\in K}|z_{1}|.

Let the right-hand-side be attained at the point
(c,d^{\prime})\in K
. Consider the change of coordinates

(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}^{\prime}):=(z_{1},z^{\prime}-z_{1}d^{\prime}/c)
and let
\widetilde{K}
be the image of
K
. The Chebyshev constants of
K
and
\widetilde{K}
are the same as this transformation preserves the classes of polynomials used to define
the directional Chebyshev constants (recall
z_{1}
was first in the order
\prec
). But now for
\widetilde{K}\cap\{\widetilde{z}^{\prime}=0\}=\{(\widetilde{z}_{1},0)\in\widetilde{K}\}
we have

|c|=\max\{|\widetilde{z}_{1}|:(\widetilde{z}_{1},0)\in\widetilde{K}\}=\max_{(\widetilde{z}_{1},\widetilde{z}^{\prime})\in\widetilde{K}}|\widetilde{z}_{1}|

and we are back in the first case. Thus we have established (
3.17
) in all cases:

\liminf_{\theta\to(1,0,...,0),\ \theta\in\Sigma^{0}}\tau(K,\theta)\geq R=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{(n,0,...,0)}(K).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be a holomorphic mapping on an open set

and let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}

be the connected component of

is locally regular at

is nondegenerate in a neighborhood of

is locally regular at

Proof.

We want to show that
\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(K)\cap B(f(a),r)}(w)=0
. Fix
r_{0}
with
B(a,r_{0})\subset U
and take
M>0
with

|f(\zeta)-f(a)|\leq M|\zeta-a|\ \hbox{for all}\ \zeta\in B(a,r_{0}).

First suppose
r<Mr_{0}
and let
E:=K\cap B(a,r/M)
. Note that
\widehat{E}\subset\widehat{K}\cap B(a,r/M)
. From Theorem 5.3.9 of
[
6
]
we have

\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(E)}(w)=0.

(4.1)

Since
f(E)\subset f(K\cap B(a,r/M))
we have

V_{f(K)\cap B(f(a),r)}\leq V_{f(E)}.

(4.2)

Thus, from (
4.1
) and (
4.2

0\leq\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(K)\cap B(f(a),r)}(w)\leq\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(E)}(w)=0,

yielding the result when

.

If
r\geq Mr_{0}
, we observe that

f(K)\cap B(f(a),r_{0}M/2)\subset f(K)\cap B(f(a),r);

hence applying the previous case with
r_{0}M/2
in place of
r
we obtain

0\leq\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(K)\cap B(f(a),r)}(w)\leq\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(K)\cap B(f(a),r_{0}M/2)}(w)=0.

This shows that
\lim_{w\to f(a)}V_{f(K)\cap B(f(a),r)}(w)=0
.

∎

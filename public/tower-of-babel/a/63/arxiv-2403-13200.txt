Abstract
. The exact value of the separating Noether number of an arbitrary finite abelian group of rank two is determined. This is done by a detailed study of the monoid of zero-sum sequences over the group.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 13A50; Secondary 11B75, 20D60

1.
Introduction

Separating invariants

The
separating Noether number

\beta_{sep}(G)

of a finite group
G
was introduced in
[
12
]
as the minimal positive integer
d
such that for any
finite dimensional complex representation of
G
,
the homogeneous polynomial
G
-invariants of degree at most
d
form a separating set. The present paper fits into the program of determining
\beta_{sep}(G)
for finite abelian groups
G
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
endowed with an action of
G
via linear transformations. The algebra
\mathbb{C}[V]
of polynomial functions on
V
contains the subalgebra

\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
of
G
-invariants
(i.e. polynomial functions constant along the
G
-orbits).
By
[
4
, Theorem 3.12.1]
for any
v,w\in V
for which
Gv\neq Gw
, there exists a homogeneous polynomial invariant
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
of degree at most
|G|
with
f(v)\neq f(w)
.
Denote by
\beta_{sep}(G,V)
the minimal positive integer
d
such that for any

v,w\in V
with
Gv\neq Gw
, there exists a homogeneous
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
of degree at most
d
satisfying
f(v)\neq f(w)
. Set

\beta_{sep}(G):=\underset{V}{\sup}\{\beta_{sep}(G,V)\mid V\text{ is a finite dimensional }G\text{-module}\}.

So by
[
4
, Theorem 3.12.1]
we have
\beta_{sep}(G)\leq|G|
.

Denote by
C_{n}
the cyclic group of order
n
. Our main result gives the exact value of the separating Noether number for any finite abelian group
C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n}
of rank two:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits,n
be positive integers and denote by
p
the minimal prime divisor of
n
. Then we have:

\beta_{sep}(C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}

The definition of
\beta_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)
is modeled on the
Noether number

\beta(G)
, defined in
[
17
]
as the maximal degree in a minimal homogeneous generating system of the algebras
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
, where
V
ranges over all finite dimensional representations of
G
. The famous theorem of Noether asserts that
\beta(G)\leq|G|
.
The exact value of the Noether number or the separating Noether number is known only for a few families of groups other than those covered by
Theorem
1.1
(see
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
1
]
).

Davenport constant

By
[
5
, Corollary 2.6.]

(see Lemma
2.3
of the present paper) Theorem
1.1
translates to a question on zero-sum sequences over

C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n}
.

It is well known (see for example
[
17
]
) that for a finite abelian group
G
,
\beta(G)
coincides with the
Davenport constant

\mathsf{D}(G)
of
G
(the maximal length of an irreducible zero-sum sequence over
G
). This observation highlights an interesting connection between invariant theory and the theory of zero-sum sequences (see
[
2
]
for more information on this topic).

A long-standing open question about the Davenport constant is a conjecture stating that for the direct sum
C_{n}^{r}
of
r
copies of the cyclic group of order
n
, the equality
D(C_{n}^{r})=1+r(n-1)
(or equivalently,

\beta(C_{n}^{r})=1+r(n-1)
) holds (see
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
).
The analogous question on the separating Noether number was answered recently in

[
16
]
, where
we calculated the exact value of

\beta_{sep}(C_{n}^{r})
, see Theorem
2.8
of the present paper for the result.

On the other hand, the most common families of finite abelian groups for which the exact value of the Davenport constant (hence the Noether number) is known are the finite abelian groups of rank two and the finite abelian
p
-groups
(
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
; see also
[
8
]
for further information).
It is therefore natural to aim at computing
\beta_{sep}(G)
for rank two abelian groups as we do here.
Interestingly, this seems to be technically more involved than the
study of the separating Noether number of

C_{n}^{r}
(whose Noether number is still unknown in general).
The methods of this paper will be used in a subsequent paper
[
15
]

to determine the separating Noether number for rank three finite abelian groups.

Outline of the present paper

The interpretation of the separating Noether number of a finite abelian group
G
in terms of zero-sum sequences over
G
is reviewed in Section
2
, where we also recall some results from
[
16
]
that are used here. Key technical contributions of the present work are Proposition
3.2
in Section
3
and Lemma
4.2
in Section
4
. Their consequences are applied in Section
5
to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
g_{1},...,g_{k}
be distinct elements of the additively written finite abelian group
G
. Then

\mathcal{G}(g_{1},...,g_{k}):=\{[m_{1},...,m_{k}]\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:\sum m_{i}g_{i}=0\in G\}

is a subgroup of the additive group of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Its submonoid

\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k}):=\mathbb{N}^{k}\cap\mathcal{G}(g_{1},...,g_{k})

is called
block monoid
, see
[
9
, Definition 2.5.5]
. In the particular case when
g_{1},...,g_{k}
are all the elements of
G
, by slight abuse of notation we write
\mathcal{B}(G):=\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
.

The
length
of an element
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},...,m_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
is
|\mathsf{m}|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
. An element of the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
that can not be written as the sum of two non-zero elements of the monoid is called an
atom
. Let
\{g_{1},...,g_{k}\}\subset\{g_{0},g_{1},...,g_{k}\}
, then each element
\mathsf{m_{0}}=[m_{1},...,m_{k}]
of the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
can be identified with the element
\mathsf{m}=[0,m_{1},...,m_{k}]
of the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{0},g_{1},...,g_{k})
. If
\mathsf{m_{0}}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
is an atom, then
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{0},g_{1},...,g_{k})
is also an atom. By iterating this idea we get that each atom of a monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
can be identified with an atom of
\mathcal{B}(G)
. The maximal length of an atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}(G)
is denoted by
\mathsf{D}(G)
. Thus we get the definition of the Davenport constant of the abelian group
G
.

The support of an element is:

\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})=\{i\in\{1,...,k\}:m_{i}\neq 0\}

The size of the support is
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|
. The
i^{th}
standard basis vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
will be denoted by
\mathsf{e_{i}}
, hence
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
, where
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})
the order of
g_{i}
in
G
. This implies that
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
generates
\mathcal{G}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
as a group.

Remark
.

One can think of
\mathcal{B}(G)
as the monoid of zero-sum sequences over
G
. An element
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},...,m_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}(G)=\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
corresponds to the sequence over
G
containing
g_{i}\in G
with multiplicity
m_{i}
. For a survey on zero-sum sequences over abelian groups see
[
8
]
,
[
9
, Chapter 5]
,
[
11
, Chapter 10]
.

The following definition was introduced in
[
16
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

An element
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
is called a
group atom in

\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})

if
\mathsf{m}
can not be written as an
integral
linear combination of elements
of
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
that have length strictly smaller than
|\mathsf{m}|
.

Remark
.

(1) A group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
is obviously an atom in

\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
, but the converse is not true
(see for example
[
16
, Example 4.4]
).

(2) It may happen that an element
\mathsf{m_{0}}=[m_{1},...,m_{k}]
is a group atom in some monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
, however the element
\mathsf{m}=[0,m_{1},...,m_{k}]
is not a group atom in the larger monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{0},g_{1},...,g_{k})
(see Example
2.2
of the present paper).

Example 2.2
.

Take the group
G=C_{12}\oplus C_{4}
, and consider the element
[8,4]\in\mathcal{B}((1,1),(1,2))
. For an element
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},m_{2}]\in\mathcal{B}((1,1),(1,2))
, we have
m_{1}+2m_{2}\equiv 0\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits 4
. So
m_{1}\equiv 0\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits 2
, hence by the condition
m_{1}+m_{2}\equiv 0\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits 12
, we get that
m_{2}\equiv 0\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits 2
yielding that
m_{1}\equiv m_{2}\equiv 0\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits 4
. Hence the atoms in the monoid
\mathcal{B}((1,1),(1,2))
are exactly:
[0,12],[4,8],[8,4],[12,0]
. This shows that
[8,4]
is a group atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}((1,1),(1,2))
, since it can not be written as a linear combination of elements of lower length, as such elements do not exist. However the equality
[0,8,4]=2[2,4,4]-[4,0,4]
shows that
[0,8,4]
is not a group atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}((8,0),(1,1),(1,2))
.

The precise relation between the separating Noether number of an abelian group and the theory of zero-sum sequences
was established in
[
5
]
(see
[
6
]

for the case of not necessarily finite diagonalizable groups):

Lemma 2.3
.

[
5
, Corollary 2.6.]

The number
\beta_{sep}(G)
is the maximal length of a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
, where
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\}
ranges over all subsets of size
k\leq\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1
of the abelian group
G
.

In this paper the following convention will be used:
G
will stand for the abelian group
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus C_{n_{2}}\oplus...\oplus C_{n_{r}}
where
2\leq n_{r}\mid...\mid n_{2}\mid n_{1}
, and
r=2s
or
2s-1
, depending on its parity,
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
are fixed distinct elements of
G
, and
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
the corresponding block monoid. Note that here
r
is the
rank
(i.e. the minimal number of generators), and
n_{1}=\exp(G)
is the
exponent
(i.e. the least common multiple of the orders of the elements) of the group
G
.

Using Lemma
2.3
, the following general upper bound was given for
\beta_{sep}(G)
:

Lemma 2.4
.

[
5
, Theorem 3.10]

\beta_{sep}(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_{i}-1)+1
for all abelian groups
G
, with equality if and only if
G
is cyclic or
2=n_{s+1}=

\dots

=n_{r}
where
r=2s-1
or
r=2s
.

We shall use the following results from
[
16
]
:

Lemma 2.5
.

[
16
, Lemma 3.1.]

Let
\mathsf{m}
be a group atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
such that
|\mathsf{m}|>\max\{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mid i=1,\dots,k\}
. Then we have

(1)

2|\mathsf{m}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})

Lemma 2.6
.

[
16
, Lemma 4.3.]

Assume that
r=2s
and
n_{s+1}=\dots=n_{1}
. Let
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},\dots,m_{2s+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
, for elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1}\in G
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=n_{1}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,2s\}

(ii)

|\mathsf{m}|>sn_{1}+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}
, where
p
is the minimal prime divisor of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})
.

Then
\mathsf{m}
is not a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
16
, Lemma 5.5.]

Assume that
r=2s
and let
p
be a prime divisor of
n_{r}
. Then
\beta_{sep}(G)\geq n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}+\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
16
, Theorem 1.2.]

For positive integers
n\geq 2
and
r
denote by
C_{n}^{r}
the direct sum
C_{n}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n}
of
r
copies of the cyclic group
C_{n}
of order
n
, and let
p
be the minimal prime divisor of
n
.
Then we have

\beta_{sep}(C_{n}^{r})=\begin{cases}ns,&\mbox{ if }r=2s-1\mbox{ is odd}\\
ns+\frac{n}{p},&\mbox{ if }r=2s\mbox{ is even}.\\
\end{cases}

3.
Preparations for the odd order case

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be an abelian group of odd order,
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in G
. Suppose that for an element
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
there exists an integral linear combination
\lambda_{1}\mathsf{q}_{1}+...+\lambda_{t}\mathsf{q}_{t}+\varepsilon\mathsf{m=\mathop{b}\nolimits}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
,
\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits}=[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\dots,\mathop{b}\nolimits_{k}]
such that the following conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathsf{q}_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
,
|\mathsf{q}_{j}|<|\mathsf{m}|
for
j=1,...,t
, and
\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}

(ii)

0\leq\mathop{b}\nolimits_{i}=2\mathop{b}\nolimits_{i}^{\prime}
is even for
i=1,\dots,k
and
\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1}+\dots+\mathop{b}\nolimits_{k}<2|\mathsf{m}|

Then
\mathsf{m}
is not a group atom.

Proof.

Since the order of
G
is odd, the equality
\sum_{i=1}^{k}2\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}_{i}g_{i}=0
implies
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}_{i}g_{i}=0
, hence
\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}}=[\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
. Here
|\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}}|=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{b}\nolimits_{i}}{2}<|\mathsf{m}|
, so the equality

\varepsilon\mathsf{m}=2\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits^{\prime}}-(\lambda_{1}\mathsf{q}_{1}+...+\lambda_{t}\mathsf{q}_{t})

shows that
\mathsf{m}
can be written as an integral linear combination of elements of length strictly lower than
|\mathsf{m}|
. Hence it is not a group atom.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus C_{n_{2}}\oplus...\oplus C_{n_{r}}
be an odd order abelian group of rank at least two, such that
1<\mathop{\ell}\nolimits:=\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}
. Let
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
for
i=1,2,3
, where
n=n_{2}
. Suppose that
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
is an element such that
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
, and there is exactly one even number among
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
. Then
\mathsf{m}
is not a group atom.

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
m_{3}
is even,
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are odd, and
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
. Note that
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
,
n
and
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits
are odd, since
|G|
is odd.

Since
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
, both
ng_{1}
and
-ng_{2}
are generators of the order
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits
subgroup of the direct summand
C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}
of
G
. Hence there exists a positive integer
x\in\{0,...,\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1\}
such that
-ng_{2}=xng_{1}
. Then
\mathsf{x}=[xn,n,0]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
, moreover
|\mathsf{x}|=n(x+1)\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
. Similarly, we have a positive integer
y\in\{0,...,\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1\}
, for which
\mathsf{y}=[n,yn,0]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
, and
|\mathsf{y}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
. Since
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
, we have that
x\neq 0,y\neq 0
. Finally, by the same technique, we get a positive integer
z\in\{0,...,\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1\}
, for which
\mathsf{z}=[n,n,zn]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
. Note that if one of the equalities
x=1
;
y=1
;
z=0
holds, then the other two also hold, yielding that
\mathsf{x=y=z}=[n,n,0]
.

If
z\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-2
, then
|\mathsf{z}|\leq 2n+n(\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-2)=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
. If
z=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1
, then
|\mathsf{z}|>\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
. Note that all the elements in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
of type
[\nu_{1}n,\nu_{2}n,\nu_{3}n]
correspond to elements
[\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}]\in\mathcal{B}(ng_{1},ng_{2},ng_{3})
, where
ng_{1},ng_{2},ng_{3}
are generators of the order
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits
subgroup of the direct summand
C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}
of
G
. Since
\beta(C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits
, atoms of
\mathcal{B}(C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits})
have length at most
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits
, so atoms of
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
of type
[\nu_{1}n,\nu_{2}n,\nu_{3}n]
have length at most
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
. So if
z=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1
, then
\mathsf{z}
can be written as sum of elements of length at most
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
.

Using
\mathsf{x,y,z}
and
\mathsf{e_{i}}
for
i=1,2,3
, we construct an integral linear combination
\lambda_{1}\mathsf{q_{1}}+...+\lambda_{t}\mathsf{q_{t}}+\varepsilon\mathsf{m=\mathop{b}\nolimits}=[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
fulfilling conditions (i) and (ii) of Lemma
3.1
. Since
|\mathsf{e_{i}}|=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
,
|\mathsf{x}|<|\mathsf{m}|
,
|\mathsf{y}|<|\mathsf{m}|
and either
|\mathsf{z}|<|\mathsf{m}|
, or
\mathsf{z}
is the sum of elements of length at most
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
(as described above), the first condition is automatically satisfied. We will distinguish cases depending on the parity of
x,y
and
z
and the size of
m_{i}
compared to
n
.

(1)

x
is odd:

\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits}:=\mathsf{m+x}
, so
[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]=[xn+m_{1},n+m_{2},m_{3}]

Here
0\leq\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1}
and
0\leq\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2}
are even, since
m_{1},m_{2},x
and
n
are odd, while
0\leq b_{3}=m_{3}
is also even. Moreover,
\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1}+\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2}+\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}=|\mathsf{m}|+|\mathsf{x}|<2|\mathsf{m}|
.

(2)

y
is odd: similar to (1).

(3)

z
is even and
z<\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1
:

\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits:=m+z}
, so
[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]=[m_{1}+n,m_{2}+n,m_{3}+zn]

z\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-3
, so
|\mathsf{z}|=(z+2)n<\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n<|\mathsf{m}|
. Hence
\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1}+\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2}+\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}=|\mathsf{m}|+|\mathsf{z}|<2|\mathsf{m}|
.

From now on, we assume that
x
and
y
are even, and
z
is odd or
z=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1
.

(4)

m_{1}<n\leq m_{2}
:

\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits}:=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n\mathsf{e_{1}}+\mathsf{m-x}
, so
[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]=[m_{1}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-xn,m_{2}-n,m_{3}]

m_{1}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-xn+m_{2}-n+m_{3}=|\mathsf{m}|+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-(x+1)n<2|\mathsf{m}|
.

(5)

(a)

n\leq m_{1}\leq m_{2}
and
z
is odd:

\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits:=m-z}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n\mathsf{e_{3}}
, so
[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]=[m_{1}-n,m_{2}-n,m_{3}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-zn]

m_{1}-n+m_{2}-n+m_{3}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-zn=|\mathsf{m}|+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-(2+z)n<2|\mathsf{m}|
.

(b)

n\leq m_{1}\leq m_{2}
and
z=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits-1
:

\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits:=m-z}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n\mathsf{e_{3}}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n\mathsf{e_{3}}
, so
[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]=[m_{1}-n,m_{2}-n,m_{3}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+n]

m_{1}-n+m_{2}-n+m_{3}+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+n=|\mathsf{m}|+\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n-n<2|\mathsf{m}|
.

(6)

(a)

m_{1}\leq m_{2}<n
and
z
is odd:

\mathsf{\mathop{b}\nolimits:=}\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n\mathsf{e_{3}}+\mathsf{z-m}
, so
[\mathop{b}\nolimits_{1},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{2},\mathop{b}\nolimits_{3}]=[n-m_{1},n-m_{2},\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+zn-m_{3}]

Proposition 4.3

(ii)

(i) Since

\gcd\left(\mathop{\ell}\nolimits,\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(\sigma_{3})}\right)>1

\gcd\left(\mathop{\ell}\nolimits,\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(\sigma_{3})}\right)=1

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(\sigma_{3})\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(\sigma_{3})}=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})\mid\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits\frac{n}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(\sigma_{3})}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(\sigma_{3})

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})

Proposition 4.5

i=1,2,3
, then for each group atom
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
we have
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
|\mathsf{m}|>\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
. If
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits=2\ell^{\prime}
is even, then
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=2\ell^{\prime}n
, so
\langle\ell^{\prime}ng_{1}\rangle=\langle\ell^{\prime}ng_{2}\rangle=\langle\ell^{\prime}ng_{3}\rangle\cong C_{2}
. However this contradicts Lemma
4.2
(iii), claiming that for a group atom
\mathsf{m}
with
|\mathsf{m}|>\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})
, the intersection
\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\langle g_{2}\rangle\cap\langle g_{3}\rangle
does not contain a cyclic subgroup of order divisible by
2
.

So we can assume that
n
is even, hence its minimal prime divisor is
p=2
. Proposition
4.4
(ii) implies that inequality (
3
) holds. So
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}{2\mathop{\rm\uptheta}\nolimits}\geq|\mathsf{m}|
, hence by Proposition
4.3
(ii) we get that
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
.
∎

5.
Proof of the main theroem

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus...\oplus C_{n_{r}}
be an abelian group with
r\geq 2
, such that
1<\mathop{\ell}\nolimits:=\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}
. Let
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G
and
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}]
be an arbitrary group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
. Denote by
p
the minimal prime divisor of
n=n_{2}
. Then

|\mathsf{m}|\leq\begin{cases}\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}&\mbox{ if }r=2\\
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{2}&\mbox{ if }r>2\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose that
|\mathsf{m}|>\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
. Then Proposition
4.4
(i) implies that
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
, hence by Proposition
4.4
(ii) we get that either
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})\mid n
, or
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
for
i=1,2,3
holds.

Suppose that
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
with
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})\mid n
. Lemma
2.5
implies that
2\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})\geq 2|\mathsf{m}|
, hence
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{2}
. Moreover, if
r=2
and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})<n
then
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})|n
implies that
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})\leq\frac{n}{p}
, hence
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})}{2}<\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
. If
r=2
and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})=n
, then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
by Lemma
2.6
.
So we can turn to the case when
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
for
i=1,2,3
.

Suppose that
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
is odd. If there was exactly one even number among
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
, then
\mathsf{m}
could not be a group atom by Proposition
3.2
. If
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
were all even, then
\mathsf{m}
itself would fulfill the conditions (i), (ii) of Lemma
3.1
, while if
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
were all odd, then
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})\mathsf{e_{1}}+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})\mathsf{e_{2}}+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})\mathsf{e_{3}}-\mathsf{m}
would fulfill the conditions (i), (ii) of Lemma
3.1
. Finally, if there was exactly one odd number among
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{3}
(assume that this one was
m_{1}
), then
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})\mathsf{e_{1}}+\mathsf{m}
would fulfill the conditions (i), (ii) of Lemma
3.1
. So Lemma
3.1
would imply that
\mathsf{m}
could not be a group atom.

Hence remains the case when
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
is even. Then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
by Proposition
4.5
, so we are done.
∎

Proof of
Theorem
1.1
.

If
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits=1
, then
\beta_{sep}(C_{n}\oplus C_{n})=n+\frac{n}{p}
by Theorem
2.8
. So we can assume that
\mathop{\ell}\nolimits>1
. Lemma
2.7
implies that
\beta_{sep}(C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n})\geq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
. By Lemma
2.3
we are searching for the maximal possible length of a group atom
\mathsf{m}
in a monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},...,g_{k})
, where
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\}
ranges over all subsets of size
k\leq 3
of the group
C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n}
. If
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|=1
, then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
. If
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|=2
, then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\max\{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1}),\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2}),\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})}{2}\}\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n
by Lemma
2.5
. If
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|=3
, then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
by Theorem
5.1
.

So
\beta_{sep}(C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n})\leq\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
, giving that
\beta_{sep}(C_{\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n}\oplus C_{n})=\mathop{\ell}\nolimits n+\frac{n}{p}
.

\square

Invariant domain preserving high-order spectral discontinuous approximations of hyperbolic systems
Valentin Carlier, Florent Renac

Abstract
We propose a limiting procedure to preserve invariant domains with time explicit discrete high-order spectral discontinuous approximate solutions to hyperbolic systems of conservation laws. Provided the scheme is discretely conservative and satisfy geometric conservation laws at the discrete level, we derive a condition on the time step to guaranty that the cell-averaged approximate solution is a convex combination of states in the invariant domain. These states are then used to define local bounds which are then imposed to the full high-order approximate solution within the cell via an a posteriori scaling limiter. Numerical experiments are then presented with modal and nodal discontinuous Galerkin schemes confirm the robustness and stability enhancement of the present approach.

Introduction

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open domain with
d
the space dimension. We are interested here in high-order numerical solutions to hyperbolic systems of conservations law

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}{\bf u}+\nabla\cdot{\bf f}({\bf u})=0,&\text{ in }D\times(0,\infty),\\
{\bf u}({\bf x},0)={\bf u}_{0}({\bf x}),&\text{ in }D,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
{\bf u}({\bf x},t)
represents the vector of conserved variables with values in the set of states
\Omega^{a}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
which is assumed to be convex. The flux tensor
{\bf f}=({\bf f}_{1},\dots,{\bf f}_{d}):\Omega^{a}\ni{\bf u}\mapsto{\bf f}({\bf u})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is assumed to be smooth.

Solutions to
eq.

1
may develop discontinuities in finite time even if the initial data is smooth, therefore the equations are to be understood in the sense of distributions. Nevertheless, in this setting we lose uniqueness of the solution and
eq.

1
must be supplemented with further admissibility criteria. We here focus on entropy inequalities on some twice differentiable strictly convex function
\eta:\Omega^{a}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated with a smooth entropy flux
{\bf q}:\Omega^{a}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\prime}({\bf u})^{\top}{\bf f}_{i}^{\prime}({\bf u})={\bf q}_{i}^{\prime}({\bf u})^{\top}\quad\forall{\bf u}\in\Omega^{a},\quad 1\leq i\leq d.

(2)

A weak solution to
eq.

1
is called an entropy weak solution if for every entropy pair of
eq.

1
we have

\frac{\partial\eta({\bf u})}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot{\bf q}({\bf u})\leq 0,

(3)

in the sense of distributions. Classical (smooth) solutions respect this condition with an equality, since one can apply the chain rule with
eq.

2
. The inequality for discontinuous solutions comes from a vanishing viscosity argument by adding a parabolic perturbation to
eq.

1
, the regularizing effect allows to have a smooth unique solution in this case. Then, under some structural assumptions on
eq.

1
, vanishing viscosity approximations converge to an entropy measure valued solution to
eqs.

1
and
3

[
9
]
. This result is in particular based on the existence of convex invariant domains
\mathcal{B}\subset\Omega^{a}
for
eq.

1
: if
{\bf u}
is in
\mathcal{B}
, then it remains in
\mathcal{B}
almost everywhere in
D\times(0,\infty)

[
28
,
13
,
24
,
40
]
. This property generalizes the notion of maximum principle for scalar equations. Numerical methods keeping this property at the discrete level are called invariant domain preserving (IDP).

We are here interested in the approximation of
eq.

1
using high-order discontinuous spectral methods (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
14
,
11
,
6
,
8
]
and references therein) where the solution to
eq.

1
is sought under the form of discontinuous piecewise truncated series of analytic functions over a partition of the domain

. Such methods have been applied to a wide range of applications
[
41
,
45
]
, and have the potential to achieve high-order accuracy efficiently on modern parallel architectures
[
25
,
12
]
. Unfortunately these approximations suffer from spurious oscillations around discontinuities of the exact solution due to Gibbs phenomenon
[
10
,
17
]
that may cause the approximate solution to become locally nonphysical, leading to robustness issues. A large body of research has been proposed to address such issues with, e.g., solution and flux limiters
[
31
,
49
,
21
]
, entropy conservative subcell flux differencing
[
11
,
6
,
8
]
artificial viscosity
[

,
2
]
, shock-capturing terms
[

,

]
.

We here focus on a posteriori limiters from
[
49
,
50
]
scaling the cellwise approximate solution around its cell average, thus allowing to preserve positivity of the solution (i.e., with
\mathcal{B}=\Omega^{a}
) and maximum principles in scalar problems, while preserving conservativity of the method. Under some strong assumptions on the mesh, it is indeed possible to derive a condition on the time step of the scheme so that the cell-averaged solution remains in the invariant domains on Cartesian and simplicial grids
[
49
,
50
,
27
]
, or on unstructured quadrangular straight-sided grids
[
38
]
. We here extend this limiting technique to an IDP limiter for a broad class of spectral discontinuous methods with explicit time stepping on general unstructured meshes with possibly curved elements. Provided, the discretization method is conservative and satisfies geometric conservation laws
[
43
,
29
]
at the discrete level, we propose a condition on the time step to guaranty that the cell-averaged approximate solution is a convex combination of states lying in the required invariant domains. These states are then used to define local bounds which are then imposed to the full high-order approximate solution within the cell via the scaling limiter. This strategy is closely related to convex limiting
[
21
]
based on first-order IDP approximations defining local bounds and then forcing the high-order approximation to satisfy these bounds through flux limiting
[
4
,
48
]
. This approach has been applied to finite element approximations in
[
19
]
and discontinuous Galerkin spectral element method in
[
34
]
among others.

The objective of this paper is hence to derive a CFL condition on the time step and to propose an iterative algorithm for its evaluation. These are based first on the existence of a state in the invariant region which satisfies a trivial flux balance over each mesh cell boundaries. We call this state the pseudo-equilibrium state and then use tricks from
[
36
]
to expand the the cell-averaged approximate solution is a convex combination of states lying in the invariant domains. The CFL condition is also based on the existence of a quadrature rule to evaluate the cell-averaged solution that includes the traces of the numerical solution used to evaluate numerical fluxes in the scheme. This latter result generalized the work in
[
51
]
on triangular grids to general curved polyhedral elements.

Finally, we will use the definition of invariant domain from

2.2
Two-point numerical fluxes and approximate Riemann solvers

The discretization of
eq.

1
will rely on two-point numerical fluxes
[
32
,
22
]
for the approximation of
{\bf f}\cdot{\bf n}
and we assume them to be consistent and conservative:

{\bf h}({\bf u},{\bf u},{\bf n})={\bf f}({\bf u})\cdot{\bf n},\quad{\bf h}({\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})=-{\bf h}({\bf u}_{R},{\bf u}_{L},-{\bf n})\quad\forall{\bf u},{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R}\in\Omega^{a},

(7)

and Lipschitz continuous. We also define the notion of IDP two-point flux in the following definition.

Definition 1
.

A two-point flux is said to be invariant domain preserving (IDP) for
\mathcal{B}
an invariant domain if we have

{\bf u}-\frac{\Delta t}{h}\big({\bf h}({\bf u},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})-{\bf h}({\bf u}_{L},{\bf u},{\bf n})\big)\in\mathcal{B}\quad\forall\;{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u},{\bf u}_{R}\in\mathcal{B},

under the half CFL condition

\frac{\Delta t}{h}\max(|\lambda|({\bf u}_{L},{\bf u},{\bf n}),|\lambda|({\bf u},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n}))\leq\frac{1}{2}.

We now introduce the notion of ARS and IDP ARS, which will be used to derive IDP two-point fluxes.

Definition 2
(Approximate Riemann solver).

An ARS is a self-similar function
\mathcal{W}^{a}(\frac{x}{t};{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})
, used to approximate the solution
\mathcal{W}(\frac{x}{t};{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})

4

1

[
22
,
16
]

, we have

\frac{1}{h}\int_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}\mathcal{W}^{a}\Big(\frac{x}{\Delta t};{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n}\Big)dx=\frac{{\bf u}_{L}+{\bf u}_{R}}{2}-\Delta t\big({\bf f}({\bf u}_{R})-{\bf f}({\bf u}_{L})\big)\cdot{\bf n}.

(8)

Using consistency in
eq.

7
, and setting
\xi=\frac{x}{\Delta t}
, one defines a two-point flux from an ARS as

\displaystyle{\bf h}_{\mathcal{W}^{a}}({\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})

\displaystyle={\bf f}({\bf u}_{L})\cdot{\bf n}-\int_{-\lambda}^{0}\big(\mathcal{W}^{a}(\xi;{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})-{\bf u}_{L}\big)d\xi

(9a)

\displaystyle={\bf f}({\bf u}_{R})\cdot{\bf n}+\int_{0}^{\lambda}\big(\mathcal{W}^{a}(\xi;{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})-{\bf u}_{R}\big)d\xi,

(9b)

. Both definitions are equivalent due to

Definition 3
.

An ARS

, so for
\varepsilon_{\kappa}>0

\displaystyle\langle f\rangle_{\kappa}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})-\varepsilon_{\kappa}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa})+\varepsilon_{\kappa}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})-\varepsilon_{\kappa}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})+\varepsilon_{\kappa}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}(\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}-\varepsilon_{\kappa}\alpha_{i}^{\kappa})f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})+\varepsilon_{\kappa}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa}).

Since the
\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}
are positive, for
\varepsilon_{\kappa}=\min_{\{i:\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}>0\}}(\frac{\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}}{\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}})>0
, the
(\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}-\varepsilon_{\kappa}\alpha_{i}^{\kappa})
are nonnegative, then
eq.

17
holds with

\nu_{i}^{\kappa}=\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}-\varepsilon_{\kappa}\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}\geq 0\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq N_{v},\quad\beta_{i}^{\kappa}=\varepsilon_{\kappa}s_{i}^{\kappa}>0\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq N_{f}.

(19)

Remark 7
.

Note that
\kappa
is usually a polyhedron and
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
a polynomial space, we can subdivide
\kappa
into simplices and since there are quadrature rules integrating exactly arbitrary order polynomials on simplices, the previous lemma can be applied. Likewise, the existence of the quadrature in
lemma

6
is required only for modal methods or when the DOFs are not defined at the
{\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}
in
eq.

12
, so the present framework also holds for non polynomial nodal approximations with DOFs at the faces as in
[
8
]
.

The following corollary allows to explicitely define the quadrature
eq.

14
for modal polynomial based methods.

Corollary 8
.

Given a basis
(\phi_{j})_{1\leq j\leq N_{p}}
of
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
which is orthonormal with respect to the inner product (i.e.,
\int_{\kappa}\phi_{i}({\bf x})\phi_{j}({\bf x})dV=\delta_{ij}
where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol), then the
\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}
in
eq.

19
read

\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}=\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{p}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}s_{k}^{\kappa}\phi_{j}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})\phi_{j}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa}).

(20)

Proof.

We know that there exists
g\in\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
such that (see proof of
lemma

6
)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})g({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}.

(21)

Expanding
g\equiv\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{p}}g_{k}\phi_{k}
in the orthonormal basis and using
eq.

21
with
f\equiv\phi_{j}
we get

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}\phi_{j}({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}\phi_{j}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{p}}g_{k}\phi_{k}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})=g_{j},

for all
1\leq j\leq N_{p}
, by orthonormality of the basis. Substituting
g
in
eq.

21
by its expansion in the basis gives

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf x}_{i}^{\kappa})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}f({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{p}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}s_{k}^{\kappa}\phi_{j}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})\phi_{j}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{P}_{\kappa},

and we conclude by comparing this result with
eq.

18
.

Finally, the scheme is assumed to preserve uniform states in the following sense:

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}s_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}=0,

(22)

which is a discrete version of
\tfrac{1}{|\kappa|}\oint_{\partial\kappa}{\bf n}dS=0
for a closed contour. Relation
eq.

22
is closely related to the discrete geometric conservation laws
[
43
]
and is required for the numerical scheme to preserve free-stream states
[
29
]
. In
section

5
we will present schemes that satisfies assumptions
eq.

12
,
eq.

14
and
eq.

22
.

3.2
The pseudo-equilibrium state

We first introduce the Rusanov flux
[
39
]
:

{\bf h}_{\lambda}({\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})=\frac{{\bf f}({\bf u}_{L})\cdot{\bf n}+{\bf f}({\bf u}_{R})\cdot{\bf n}}{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}({\bf u}_{R}-{\bf u}_{L}),

(23)

for
\lambda\geq|\lambda|({\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})
defined in
eq.

5
. Note that the Rusanov flux is derived from the following ARS:

{\cal W}_{\lambda}(\xi,{\bf u}_{L},{\bf u}_{R},{\bf n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\bf u}_{L},&\xi<-\lambda,\\
\frac{{\bf u}_{L}+{\bf u}_{R}}{2}-\frac{1}{2\lambda}({\bf f}({\bf u}_{R})\cdot{\bf n}-{\bf f}({\bf u}_{L})\cdot{\bf n}),&-\lambda<\xi<\lambda,\\
{\bf u}_{R},&\lambda<\xi,\\
\end{array}\right.

so from
eq.

6
we know that it is IDP, see also
[
13
]
. We now state a result that will allow us to rewrite
eq.

12
with updates of three-point schemes.

Lemma 9
(pseudo-equilibrium state).

Suppose that the numerical scheme satisfies
eq.

12
,
eq.

14
and
eq.

22
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a invariant domain and suppose that the internal traces
{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}
are in
\mathcal{B}
, then there exists
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}={\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}(t^{(n)})
in
\mathcal{B}
and
{\lambda}_{\kappa}^{\star}={\lambda}_{\kappa}^{\star}(t^{(n)})>0
finite such that

\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}s_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf h}_{\lambda_{\kappa}^{\star}}\big({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big)=0,

(24)

with
{\bf h}_{\lambda_{\kappa}^{\star}}
defined in
eq.

23
with
\lambda=\lambda_{\kappa}^{\star}
where

\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}\geq\max_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\big(|\lambda|({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})\big),

(25)

and the pseudo-equilibrium state is defined by

{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}\bigg({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})-\frac{{\bf f}\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})\big)\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\bigg),\quad\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}:=\frac{s_{k}^{\kappa}}{{\cal S}^{\kappa}}.

(26)

Proof.

For the sake of clarity, we remove the time dependance of
{\bf u}_{h}
since all evaluations are done at
t^{(n)}
and write
{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})
for
{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})
. We first remark that from
eqs.

13
and
26
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}=1.

(27)

We introduce the following two sequences:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf u}^{\star}_{0}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),\quad\lambda_{0}=\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\big(|\lambda|({\bf u}^{\star}_{0},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})\big)\Big),\\
\lambda_{p+1}=\max\Big(\lambda_{p},\frac{1}{d({\bf u}^{\star}_{p},\partial B)}+\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\big(|\lambda|({\bf u}^{\star}_{p},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})\big)\Big),\quad p\geq 0,\\
{\bf u}^{\star}_{p+1}=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}\bigg(\frac{{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})+{\bf u}^{\star}_{p}}{2}-\frac{{\bf f}\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})\big)\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}-{\bf f}({\bf u}^{\star}_{p})\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}}{2\lambda_{p+1}}\bigg),\quad p\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

(28)

where
d({\bf u},\partial\mathcal{B})=\inf_{{\bf v}\in\partial\mathcal{B}}\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}\|
is the distance from
{\bf u}
to the boundary of
\mathcal{B}
. We will show that both sequences converge and the limits satisfy
eq.

24
and
eq.

25
. We first remark that
(\lambda_{p})
is non decreasing and therefore converges to some
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and that for all
p\geq 0
,
{\bf u}^{\star}_{p}
is in
\mathcal{B}
since
\lambda_{p+1}\geq|\lambda|({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf u}^{\star}_{p},{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})
so we can use
eq.

6
with
\Delta t=\frac{1}{2\lambda_{p+1}}
. Using successively
eq.

22
and the definition of
\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}
in
eq.

26
, then
eq.

27
, we have

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{\star}_{p+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}\bigg(\frac{{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})+{\bf u}^{\star}_{p}}{2}-\frac{{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}}{2\lambda_{p+1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bf u}^{\star}_{p}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{p+1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}.

Then the first step of the sequence
eq.

28
for
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
reads

{\bf u}^{\star}_{1}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa},

so applying the recurrence relation
p
more times, we obtain

{\bf u}^{\star}_{p+1}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p+1}\frac{1}{2^{i}\lambda_{(p+2-i)}}\times\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}.

(29)

Let show that
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{2^{i}\lambda_{(p+1-i)}}
above converges to
\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}
. Assume
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
is finite and let
\epsilon>0
, since
\lambda_{p}
converges to
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
, there exists
p_{0}
such that
|\frac{1}{\lambda_{p}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}|<\epsilon
for all
p>p_{0}
. Then for
p>p_{0}
, we set

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{2^{i}\lambda_{(p+1-i)}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p}\frac{1}{2^{(p+1-k)}\lambda_{k}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{(p+1-k)}\lambda_{k}}+\sum\limits_{k=p_{0}+1}^{p}\frac{1}{2^{(p+1-k)}\lambda_{k}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p_{0}}\frac{2^{k}}{\lambda_{k}}+\!\!\!\sum\limits_{k=p_{0}+1}^{p}\frac{2^{k}}{2^{p+1}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\!-\!\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\right)-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\bigg(1\!-\!\!\!\sum\limits_{k=p_{0}+1}^{p}\frac{2^{k}}{2^{p+1}}\bigg)

and using a triangle inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{2^{i}\lambda_{(p+1-i)}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{(p+1)}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p_{0}}\frac{2^{k}}{\lambda_{k}}+\!\!\sum\limits_{k=p_{0}+1}^{p}\frac{2^{k}}{2^{p+1}}\left|\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\bigg(1-\!\!\sum\limits_{k=p_{0}+1}^{p}\frac{2^{k}}{2^{p+1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{(p+1)}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p_{0}}\frac{2^{k}}{\lambda_{k}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-p_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\epsilon+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\bigg(1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{p-p_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{p+1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p_{0}}\frac{2^{k}}{\lambda_{k}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{p-p_{0}}}\right)\epsilon+\frac{1}{2^{p-p_{0}}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}

which clearly converges to
\epsilon
when
p\rightarrow\infty
, proving our statement and
eq.

26
.

Let now prove that
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
is finite by contradiction. Suppose that
\lambda_{p}\rightarrow+\infty
, then
{\bf u}^{\star}_{p}\rightarrow\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})
, but the application

{\bf u}\mapsto\max_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}(|\lambda|({\bf u},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}))+\frac{1}{d({\bf u},\partial B)}

is continuous in the interior of
\mathcal{B}
and is hence locally bounded around
\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})
, implying that
\lambda_{p}
is bounded and
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
is finite which is a contradiction. By
eq.

28
, we obviously have
\lambda_{p+1}\geq\max_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\big(|\lambda|({\bf u}^{\star}_{p},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})\big)
and passing the inequality to the limit we obtain
eq.

25
.

Let now prove that
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
is in
\mathcal{B}
, since for all
p\geq 0
,
{\bf u}^{\star}_{p}
is in
\mathcal{B}
, we already know that
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
is in the closure
{\bar{\mathcal{B}}}
. Now by contradiction, assuming that
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
is not in
\mathcal{B}
, we necessarily have
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
in
\partial\mathcal{B}
, so
d({\bf u}^{\star}_{p},\partial\mathcal{B})\rightarrow 0
inducing
\lambda_{p}\rightarrow+\infty
by
eq.

28
which a contradiction. It remains to prove
eq.

24
. Using
eq.

22
, we add
\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}{\bf f}({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa})\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}=0
to
eq.

26
and get

{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}\bigg({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-\frac{{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}+{\bf f}({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa})\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\bigg).

Moving
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
to the right-hand side and using
eq.

27
we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}\bigg({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}-\frac{{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}+{\bf f}({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa})\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}}{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\bigg)=0,

and multiplying the above quantity by
-\frac{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}}s_{i}^{\kappa}=-\frac{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}{2}{\cal S}^{\kappa}
we finally get

\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}s_{k}^{\kappa}\bigg(\frac{{\bf f}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}))\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}+{\bf f}({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa})\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}}{2}-\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}\frac{({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa})}{2}\bigg)=0,

which is exactly
eq.

24
from the definition of the Rusanov flux
eq.

23
.

3.3
Invariant domain preserving schemes

Using
lemma

9
we now state and prove the main result of this work in the theorem below.

Theorem 10
(Time step condition).

Assume that the numerical scheme satisfies
eq.

12
,
eq.

14
and
eq.

22
and assume that
u_{h}^{\pm}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}
and
u_{h}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})_{1\leq i\leq N_{v}}
are in
\mathcal{B}
, then under the following condition on the time step

\Delta t^{(n)}\max\limits_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\max_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\frac{s_{k}^{\kappa}}{\beta_{k}^{\kappa}}\max\Big(\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa},|\lambda|\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big)\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2},

(30)

where
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
is defined by
eq.

25
,
\langle u_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}
is also in
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Once again we remove the time dependance of
{\bf u}_{h}
for the sake of clarity, except when explicitly needed in the evaluation of
\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}
. Using
lemma

9
we add the trivial quantity
\Delta t^{(n)}\times

eq.

24
to
eq.

12
and use
eq.

14
to get

\displaystyle\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}=

\displaystyle\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n)}\rangle_{\kappa}-\Delta t^{(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}s_{k}^{\kappa}\big({\bf h}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k})-{\bf h}_{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\nu_{i}{\bf u}_{h}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\Big(\beta_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})

\displaystyle-\Delta t^{(n)}s_{k}^{\kappa}\big({\bf h}({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k})-{\bf h}_{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{v}}\nu_{i}{\bf u}_{h}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\beta_{k}^{\kappa}{\cal U}_{k}^{\kappa,n},

(31)

where, from
lemma

5
and the condition
eq.

30
, the updates

{\cal U}_{k}^{\kappa,n}:={\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa})-\frac{\Delta t^{(n)}s_{k}^{\kappa}}{\beta_{k}^{\kappa}}\Big({\bf h}\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big)-{\bf h}_{\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}}\big({\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big)\Big),

(32)

are in
\mathcal{B}
. Then by
eq.

15

\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}
is a convex combination of quantities in
\mathcal{B}
, which concludes the proof.

.

Since the set of states
\Omega^{a}
is in general a convex invariant domain,
theorem

10
can then be applied to
\mathcal{B}=\Omega^{a}
to ensure robustness of the scheme.

Remark 12
.

In the case where we are using the quadrature on the volume defined by
lemma

6
, from
eqs.

20
and
19
and the definition
\varepsilon_{\kappa}=\min_{\{i:\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}>0\}}(\frac{\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}}{\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}})
, we have

\frac{s_{k}^{\kappa}}{\beta_{k}^{\kappa}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\kappa}}=\max_{\{i:\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}>0\}}(\frac{\alpha_{i}^{\kappa}}{\varpi_{i}^{\kappa}})=\max_{1\leq i\leq N_{v}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{p}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{f}}s_{l}^{\kappa}\phi_{j}({\bf x}_{l}^{\kappa})\phi_{j}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa})\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq N_{f},

(33)

and the CFL condition
eq.

30
now reads

\Delta t^{(n)}\max\limits_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\kappa}}\max\Big(\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa},|\lambda|\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big)\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(34)

3.4
Limiting strategy

Convex limiting enforces the numerical solution to preserve invariant domains
[
21
]
through quasiconcave constraints
[
1
]
. Let recall that a function
\psi:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is quasiconcave iff. for every family of convex coefficients
(\lambda_{i})\geq 0
, with
\sum\lambda_{i}=1
, we have
\psi(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}{\bf u}_{i})\geq\min_{i}\psi({\bf u}_{i})
for all
{\bf u}_{i}
in
\mathcal{B}
. From
theorem

10
we see that for any quasiconcave function
\psi
we have

\psi(\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa})\geq m_{\kappa}^{\psi}:=\min\Big(\psi\big({\bf u}_{h}({\bf y}_{i}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})\big)_{1\leq i\leq N_{v}},\psi\big({\cal U}_{k}^{\kappa,n}\big)_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\Big),

(35)

where the updates
{\cal U}_{k}^{\kappa,n}
are defined in
eq.

32
. We now limit the solution around its cell-average
\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}
so that it satisfies the same bounds and we rely on scaling limiters introduced in
[
50
,
49
]
to enforce the bounds from quasiconcave functions to points where
{\bf u}_{h}
needs to be evaluated. The limited solution is thus defined as

\tilde{{\bf u}}_{h}^{(n+1)}\equiv(1-\theta_{\kappa}){\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}+\theta_{\kappa}\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa},

(36)

where

\theta_{\kappa}=\!\!\min\limits_{{\bf z}\in({\bf y}_{1\leq i\leq N_{v}}^{\kappa})\cup({\bf x}_{1\leq i\leq N_{f}}^{\kappa})}\!\!\max\big\{0\leq t\leq 1:\psi\big((1-t){\bf u}_{h}({\bf z},t^{(n+1)})+t\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}\big)\geq m_{\kappa}^{\psi}\big\}.

This strategy may be applied to a finite family
(\psi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n_{c}}
of
n_{c}
quasiconcave functions by using the minimum value
\theta_{\kappa}=\min\{\theta_{\kappa}(\psi_{i}):1\leq i\leq n_{c}\}
. The limiter
eq.

36
is then applied locally to each cell
\kappa
and preserves high-order accuracy of the scheme in smooth domains
[
50
]
. The cell-average is not modified,
\langle\tilde{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}=\langle{\bf u}_{h}^{(n+1)}\rangle_{\kappa}
, and cellwise discrete conservation
eq.

12
still holds.

3.5
Practical evaluation of
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}

The evaluation of
m_{\kappa}^{\psi}
in
eq.

35
requires to first evaluate both
\lambda^{\star}_{\kappa}
and
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
as limits of the sequences in
eq.

28
which might be cumbersome. Here we propose another strategy where we only need to check the convergence of
(\lambda_{p})
to ensure that
({\bf u}^{\star}_{p})
has also converged to
{\bf u}^{\star}_{\kappa}
which is in
\mathcal{B}
. Let introduce the sequences

\left\{\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{l}\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\!|\lambda|\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big),{\bf u}^{\star}_{0}=\!\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),\\

where

\displaystyle\vartheta

\displaystyle=\max\Big\{0\leq t\leq 1:{\bf u}^{\star}_{0}-\frac{t}{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\tilde{\gamma}_{k}^{\kappa}{\bf f}\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)})\big)\cdot{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\in\mathcal{B}\Big\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{1}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N_{f}}\Big(|\lambda|\big({\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf u}_{h}^{+}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big),|\lambda|\big({\bf u}^{\star}_{0},{\bf u}_{h}^{-}({\bf x}_{k}^{\kappa},t^{(n)}),{\bf n}_{k}^{\kappa}\big)\Big).

Since

, we have

and then

by definition of

. Now

and

4.3
Other methods

Properties
eqs.

12
,
14
, and
22
also hold for other discretely conservative spectral difference methods on general curved elements provided the discretization operators satisfy the metric identities at the discrete level, which imposes some limits on the order of approximation of the mesh elements compared to the approximation order of the solution
[
29
]
. The limiter
eq.

36
may hence be applied to make these methods invariant domain preserving. We list some examples below.

The skew-symmetric entropy stable modal discontinuous Galerkin methods
[
6
]
uses skew-hybrized summation-by-parts (SBP) operators allowing conservation and free-stream preservation under the standard accuracy requirements of volume and surface quadratures. In
[
8
]
multidimensional discretization schemes based on SBP operators on general curved elements generalize the staggered finite differences from
[
11
]
. The discretizations with curved elements remain accurate, conservative, and entropy stable. Spectral differences
[
33
]
and staggered Chebyshev
[
30
]
methods on curved elements belong to a same family of conservative approximations satisfying the discrete metric identities. The methods use two sets of interpolation points for the solution and fluxes and impose the discrete residuals to be satisfied at solution points. Flux points contain points at faces of the elements where two-point numerical fluxes are used. Then, the space derivatives at solution points are evaluated by differencing the polynomials interpolating the fluxes.

Let consider the compressible Euler equations of gas dynamics. The conservative variables and fluxes in

1

{\bf u}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
\rho{\bf v}\\
\rho E\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf f}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho{\bf v}^{\top}\\

\end{pmatrix},

(48)

where

denote the density, velocity vector, and specific total energy, respectively. The system is closed by defining the equation of state

with

6
Conclusions

We here investigate robustness and stability properties of discretely conservative high-order spectral discontinuous methods with explicit time stepping for the approximation of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws. We derive a condition on the time step to guaranty that the cell-averaged approximate solution is a convex combination of DOFs at preceding time step and updates of invariant domain preserving and entropy stable three-point schemes. As a consequence, the cell-averaged solution lies in some convex invariant domain and we apply a posteriori scaling limiting techniques
[
50
]
that impose to all the DOFs to satisfy the same invariant domain properties.

The condition on the time step is evaluated from the traces of the solution at faces of the mesh and can be easily evaluated on the fly. Provided the scheme satisfies the discrete metric identities, the condition is fairly general and holds for general unstructured grid with possibly curved elements. It relies on the existence of a so-called pseudo-equilibrium state which satisfies a flux balance over each mesh element, and the existence of a quadrature rule including the traces to evaluate the cell-averaged solution. We here prove their existence in the general case and provide an iterative algorithm to evaluate the pseudo-equilibrium state. We illustrate these results with the classical modal discontinuous Galerkin and DGSEM schemes. Numerical experiments in one and two space dimensions are provided to illustrate the robustness and stability of the present approach. The extension of this framework to parabolic systems of conservation laws, e.g., the compressible Navier-Stokes, is a possible direction of future research.

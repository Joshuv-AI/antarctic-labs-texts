DoF Analysis for (M, N)-Channels through a Number-Filling Puzzle
Yue Bi, Philippe Ciblat, Michèle Wigger, Yue Wu, Yue Bi 1 2 , Yue Wu 1 , Cunqing Hua 1

Abstract
We consider a 𝖪 \mathsf{K} user interference network with general connectivity, described by a matrix 𝐍 {\mathbf{N}} , and general message flows, described by a matrix 𝐌 {\mathbf{M}} . Previous studies have demonstrated that the standard interference scheme (IA) might not be optimal for networks with sparse connectivity. In this paper, we formalize a general IA coding scheme and an intuitive number-filling puzzle for given 𝐌 {\mathbf{M}} and 𝐍 {\mathbf{N}} in a way that the score of the solution to the puzzle determines the optimum sum degrees that can be achieved by the IA scheme. A solution to the puzzle is proposed for a general class of symmetric channels, and it is shown that this solution leads to significantly higher Sum-DoF than the standard IA scheme.

The puzzle:
We present the number-filling puzzle for given connectivity and message flow matrices
{\mathbf{M}}
and

Fill in the entries of a matrix
\mathsf{K}

\mathsf{K}
matrix

•

{\mathbf{G}}

{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=0\Rightarrow{\mathbf{G}}[p,q]=0

•

All non-zero entries in a column are different. I.e.,
{\mathbf{G}}[p,q]={\mathbf{G}}[p^{\prime},q]

\Rightarrow

{\mathbf{G}}[p,q]=0

The goal of the puzzle is to maximize the following score

\mathsf{S}=\frac{\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{0}}{\max_{p\in[\mathsf{K}]}\left\{\|{\mathbf{G}}[p,:]\|_{0}+g^{(p)}\right\}}

(2)

p

II
Channel model

Consider a network with
\mathsf{K}
Txs and
\mathsf{K}
Rxs labeled from
1
to
\mathsf{K}
. The connectivity of the network is described by the matrix
{\mathbf{N}}\in\{0,1\}^{(\mathsf{K}\times\mathsf{K})}
, and the message transmission is described by the matrix
{\mathbf{M}}\in\{0,1\}^{(\mathsf{K}\times\mathsf{K})}
. For simplicity, we call this network a
({\mathbf{M}},{\mathbf{N}})
-channel.
In this channel, each Rx
p
observes a linear combination of the signals sent by the Txs with
{\mathbf{N}}[p,q]=1
corrupted by Gaussian noise. Denoting Tx
q
’s slot-
t
input by
X_{q}(t)\in\mathbb{C}
and Rx
p
’s slot-
t
output by
Y_{p}(t)\in\mathbb{C}
, the input-output relation of the network is expressed as:

\displaystyle Y_{p}(t)=\sum_{\{q\colon{\mathbf{N}}[p,q]=1\}}H_{p,q}(t)X_{q}(t)+Z_{p}(t),\qquad p\in[\mathsf{K}],

(6)

where the sequences of complex-valued channel coefficients
\{H_{p,q}(t)\}
and standard circularly symmetric Gaussian noises
\{Z_{p}(t)\}
are both i.i.d. and independent of each other and of all other channel coefficients and noises.
Each coefficient
H_{p,q}(t)
has independent and identically distributed real and imaginary parts, following a specified continuous distribution over a bounded interval
[-\mathsf{H}_{\max},\mathsf{H}_{\max}]
. Importantly, these coefficients are known to all terminals even before communication commences.

The message flow in this channel is represented by the matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
, in the sense that Tx
q
transmits an independent message
a_{p,q}
to each Rx
p
for which
{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=1
. When communication is of blocklength
\mathsf{T}
, each message is uniformly distributed over
\left[2^{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{R}_{p,q}}\right]
, where
\mathsf{R}_{p,q}\geq 0
denotes the rate of transmission, and it is independent of all other messages and of all channel coefficients and noise sequences.
As a consequence, Tx
q\in[\mathsf{K}]
produces its block of channel inputs
{\mathbf{X}}_{q}\triangleq(X_{q}(1),\ldots,X_{q}(\mathsf{T}))
as

{\mathbf{X}}_{q}=f_{q}^{(\mathsf{T})}\left(\left\{a_{p,q}\colon{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=1\right\}\right)

(7)

by means of an encoding function
f_{q}^{(\mathsf{T})}
on appropriate domains and so that the inputs satisfy the block-power constraint

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathsf{T}}\sum_{t=1}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{q}(t)|^{2}\right]\leq\mathsf{P},\qquad q\in[\mathsf{K}].

(8)

Given a power
\mathsf{P}>0
, the
capacity region

\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})
is defined as the set of all rate tuples
(\mathsf{R}_{p,q}\colon p,q\in[\mathsf{K}],\;{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=1)
so that for each blocklength
\mathsf{T}
there exist
encoding functions
\{f_{q}^{(\mathsf{T})}\}_{q\in[\mathsf{K}]}
as described above and decoding functions
\{g_{p,q}^{(\mathsf{T})}\}
on appropriate domains producing the estimates

\hat{a}_{p,q}=g_{p,q}^{(\mathsf{T})}(Y_{p}(1),\ldots,Y_{p}(\mathsf{T})),\quad p,q\in[\mathsf{K}],\;{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=1,

(9)

in a way that the sequence of error probabilities

p^{(\mathsf{T})}(\textnormal{error})\triangleq\textnormal{Pr}\bigg[\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in[\mathsf{K}]\end{subarray}}\bigcup_{\{q\colon{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=1\}}\hat{a}_{p,q}\neq a_{p,q}\bigg]

(10)

tends to 0 as the blocklength
\mathsf{T}\to\infty
.

Our main interest is in the Sum-DoF:

\displaystyle\textnormal{Sum-DoF}\triangleq\varlimsup_{\mathsf{P}\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{{\mathbf{R}}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathsf{P})}\sum_{(p,q)\colon{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=1}\frac{\mathsf{R}_{p,q}}{\log\mathsf{P}}.

(11)

III
Main Results

The main results of this paper are lower bounds on the Sum-DoF of the network described in the previous Section
II
. To comprehend these results, we first present the definition of a valid precoding index matrix.

Definition 1
.

For a given message matrix
{\mathbf{M}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathsf{K}\times\mathsf{K}}
, a matrix
{\mathbf{G}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathsf{K}\times\mathsf{K}}
is called a

•

If
{\mathbf{M}}[p,q]=0
then
{\mathbf{G}}[p,q]=0
;

•

If
{\mathbf{G}}[p,q]={\mathbf{G}}[p^{\prime},q]
for
p\neq p^{\prime}
, then
{\mathbf{G}}[p,q]=0

Also denote by
{\mathbf{G}}^{(p)}
the submatrix of
{\mathbf{G}}
obtained by removing the p-th row and the columns
q\in[\mathsf{K}]
for which
{\mathbf{N}}[p,q]=0
. More formally:

{\mathbf{G}}^{(p)}\triangleq{\mathbf{G}}[p^{\prime}\neq p,\{q\colon{\mathbf{N}}[p,q]=1\}].

(12)

(Notice the dependence of
{\mathbf{G}}^{(p)}
on the connectivity matrix
{\mathbf{N}}
, which is not represented in the notation.)
Let

be equal to the number of different non-zero integers in the matrix

.

VII
Conclusion

In conclusion, our study revolves around a
\mathsf{K}
-user channel characterized by a connectivity matrix
{\mathbf{N}}
and a message matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
. To overcome this limitation and facilitate the design of new IA schemes, we introduce a formalized number-filling puzzle. Our analysis establishes a compelling relationship: the score of a puzzle solution is proportional to the sum degrees of freedom achieved by the corresponding IA scheme, given any

and
{\mathbf{N}}
. Leveraging this puzzle, we uncover new IA schemes for symmetric channels, demonstrating enhanced

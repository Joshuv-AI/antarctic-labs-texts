A simple electronic ladder model harboring ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} parafermions
Botond Osváth, Gergely Barcza, Örs Legeza, Balázs Dóra, László Oroszlány

Abstract
Parafermions are anyons with the potential for realizing non-local qubits that are resilient to local perturbations. Compared to Majorana zero modes, braiding of parafermions implements an extended set of topologically protected quantum gates. This, however, comes at the price that parafermionic zero modes can not be realized in the absence of strong interactions whose theoretical description is challenging. In the present work, we construct a simple lattice model for interacting spinful electrons with parafermionic zero energy modes. The explicit microscopic nature of the considered model highlights new realization avenues for these exotic excitations in recently fabricated quantum dot arrays. By density matrix renormalization group calculations, we identify a broad range of parameters, with well-localized zero modes, whose parafermionic nature is substantiated by their unique 8 ​ π 8\pi periodic Josephson spectrum.



Introduction.

As outlined at the turn of the century, key requirements for the realization of a quantum computer are qubits with long decoherence times and a universal set of robust quantum gates acting on them
DiVincenzo 2000
.
Topological quantum computers, where quantum information is encoded in non-local quasiparticles, promise to address these issues at the hardware level
Nayak et al. 2008
;
Aasen et al. 2016
.
Majorana fermions
Kitaev 2001
and their fractionalized counterparts, parafermions
Fendley 2012
, are such excitations in topological superconductors
Alicea 2012
;
Alicea and Fendley 2016
.
Parafermions are classified by a
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
index with
d=2
corresponding to Majorana fermions. The characteristic non-Abelian exchange statistics of parafermionic excitations can be exploited to implement topologically protected quantum gates. Braiding of
d>2
parafermion zero modes implements an extended set of quantum operations compared to Majorana zero modes
Hutter and Loss 2016
.

Blueprints for realizing parafermions rely on electron-electron interactions
Alicea and Fendley 2016
. Furthermore, some of these proposals require combining superconductivity and fractional quantum Hall edge modes, whose realization is challenging as the magnetic field needed for stabilizing the quantum Hall phase is detrimental for conventional superconductors
Clarke et al. 2013
;
Lindner et al. 2012
.
Several recent theoretical works have proposed platforms for realizing
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
parafermion zero modes circumventing this conundrum. These approaches rely on strongly interacting quantum spin Hall (QSH) insulators coupled to superconductors and do not require a substantial external magnetic field
Zhang and Kane 2014a
;
Orth et al. 2015
. In these proposals, interactions open a gap in the edge states while preserving time-reversal symmetry. Parafermion modes emerge at interfaces between edge regions gapped by interactions and proximity-coupled superconducting sections. The
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
parafermionic modes manifest a four-fold degenerate ground state and are characterized by
8\pi
-periodic fractional Josephson effect.
Interestingly, this effect can be realized without an extended region of strong interactions. In recent proposals, it was suggested that coupling an impurity quantum spin to the Josephson junction formed at a QSH edge can exhibit
8\pi
-periodicity
Peng et al. 2016
;
Vinkler-Aviv et al. 2017
.
Weak interactions in a constriction of topological insulators can also stabilize
8\pi
Josephson signal
Fleckenstein et al. 2019
. The existence of parafermionic zero modes was also investigated in spinless
Klinovaja and Loss 2014a
and spinful
Klinovaja and Loss 2014b
nano-wire setups.

All the studies above regarding the physical implementation of parafermionic phases rely on the bosonization technique, thus neglecting high energy or lattice scale effects
Giamarchi 2004
.
Alternatively, lattice models can be used to study the rich landscape defined by parafermionic excitations.
Density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) methods
White 1992
have been applied to
investigate dynamical excitations in parafermionic chains
Kaskela and Lado 2021
.
There is a natural mapping between
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
parafermionic and spinful fermionic degrees of freedom since they both encode a four-dimensional local Hilbert space. The algebraic structure of this mapping has recently been thoroughly explored
Chew et al. 2018
.
The fermionized version of parafermion chains has also been investigated, yielding an electronic lattice model with exotic components, such as three-particle interactions as well as occupation and spin-dependent hopping
Calzona et al. 2018
;
Teixeira and Dias da
Silva 2021
.

Here, we follow a more realistic approach inspired by experimentally feasible proposals utilizing the helical QSH edge modes. We construct a lattice model for spinful electrons hosting
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
parafermionic zero modes. Utilizing the DMRG approach we explore its rich phase diagram and elucidate the requirements for the stability of the anyonic phase. The simplicity of our model highlights new realization avenues of parafermionic zero modes in highly tunable quantum dot arrays.

The model.

In the following, we introduce a ladder Hamiltonian acting on spinful electrons which captures the essential properties of the edge states of two-dimensional topological insulators without explicitly treating the insulating bulk. We build on previous works where a similar lattice model was applied for modelling free fermions
Creutz 1999
;
Gholizadeh et al. 2018
;
Sticlet et al. 2013
and helical Majorana modes subject to interactions
Grover et al. 2014
;
Li et al. 2020
.
In the proposed model each electronic site has local spin degrees of freedom denoted by
{\uparrow}
and
{\downarrow}
while the left and right leg of the ladder will be referred to simply by the labels
\mathrm{L}
and
\mathrm{R}
respectively.
We write the Hamiltonian of the system as the sum of three parts:

H=H_{\text{kin}}+H_{\text{sc}}+H_{\text{int}}\,.

(1)

The first term describes the kinetic contributions, capturing propagation along the legs and hopping across the rungs of the ladder,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle H}_{\text{kin}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}c_{m}^{\dagger}\left(-\mu_{m}s_{0}\otimes\zeta_{0}+ts_{0}\otimes\zeta_{x}\right)c_{m}

(2)

\displaystyle-\frac{t}{2}\sum_{m}c_{m+1}^{\dagger}\left(is_{z}\otimes\zeta_{z}+s_{0}\otimes\zeta_{x}\right)c_{m}+\text{h.c.}\,.

Here,
s_{\alpha}
and
\zeta_{\alpha}
are Pauli matrices acting on the spin and leg degrees of freedom respectively, and
c^{\dagger}_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}c^{\dagger}_{m,\mathrm{L},{\uparrow}}&c^{\dagger}_{m,\mathrm{R},{\uparrow}}&c^{\dagger}_{m,\mathrm{L},{\downarrow}}&c^{\dagger}_{m,\mathrm{R},{\downarrow}}\end{bmatrix}
where
c^{\dagger}_{m,\zeta,s}
denotes the creation operator of an electron with spin projection
s\in\{{\uparrow},{\downarrow}\}
on-site
m
of leg
\zeta\in\{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{R}\}
.
t
serves as an overall energy scale for the system, while
\mu_{m}
is a site-dependent potential.

1
(a).

For low energies, the kinetic term (
2
) describes the propagation of helical particles which can be made more explicit if we take the Fourier transform of (
2
) along the ladder, by introducing operators
c_{k}=\sum_{m}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}mk}c_{m}
. Thus the kinetic term, for uniform values of the chemical potential
\mu_{m}=\mu
becomes
H_{\text{kin}}=\sum_{k}c^{\dagger}_{k}\mathcal{H}(k)c_{k}
. For low energies, this term can be analyzed by the envelope function approximation at
k=0
yielding

\mathcal{H}(k)\approx-\mu s_{0}\otimes\zeta_{0}+kts_{z}\otimes\zeta_{z}\,.

(3)

Crucially there are no terms mixing the two legs, thus for low-energy helical particles, the two legs are decoupled just as they would be for two spatially separated edges of a large two-dimensional topological insulator.

The second term in the Hamiltonian (
1
) describes proximity to an
s
-wave superconductor,

H_{\text{sc}}=\sum_{m,\zeta}\Delta_{m,\zeta}\left[c_{m,\zeta,{\uparrow}}^{\dagger}c_{m,\zeta,{\downarrow}}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}\right],

(4)

with a site and leg-dependent pair potential
\Delta_{m,\zeta}
.

The last term,
H_{\text{int}}
, captures a particular, albeit simple form of interaction among charge carriers in the system inspired by the bosonized calculation of Orth and coworkers
Orth et al. 2015
:

H_{\text{int}}=\sum_{m,\zeta}V_{m,\zeta}\left[c_{m,\zeta,{\uparrow}}^{\dagger}c_{m,\zeta,{\downarrow}}c_{m+1,\zeta,{\uparrow}}^{\dagger}c_{m+1,\zeta,{\downarrow}}+\text{h.c.}\right]\,.

(5)

Introducing
c^{\dagger}_{m,\zeta}=\begin{bmatrix}c^{\dagger}_{m,\zeta,{\uparrow}}&c^{\dagger}_{m,\zeta,{\uparrow}}\end{bmatrix}
and rewriting the interaction term with operators
S^{\alpha}_{m,\zeta}=c^{\dagger}_{m,\zeta}s_{\alpha}c_{m,\zeta}
we get

H_{\text{int}}=\sum_{m,\zeta}\frac{V_{m,\zeta}}{2}\left[S^{x}_{m,\zeta}S^{x}_{m+1,\zeta}-S^{y}_{m,\zeta}S^{y}_{m+1,\zeta}\right],

(6)

that is, this term describes an anisotropic symmetric exchange coupling of strength
V_{m,\zeta}
between electron spins localized to neighboring sites along a leg.
The relevance of local anisotropy and anisotropic exchange interaction in this setting was recognised in the context of impurity spins at the QSH edge
Peng et al. 2016
;
Vinkler-Aviv et al. 2017
.

An important aspect of this model is that it can be used to effectively circumvent fermion doubling
Montvay and Münster 1994
, without breaking time-reversal symmetry albeit at the cost of dismissing charge conservation. For instance in a configuration depicted in Fig.
1
(b) a fixed, large value of
\Delta
gaps the whole red region thus allowing the exploration of effects of arbitrary local interactions on the helical states localized in the blue region.

Excitation spectrum.

The phase with moderate interaction strength is endowed with one key indicator of the presence of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
parafermions, in the form of zero energy excitation.
This is evident in Fig.
3
where we show the low energy many-body excitation spectrum of our model for
\Delta^{\prime}/t=\mu/t=0
as the function of the interaction strength
V
.
For weak interaction, up to around
V/t=1.5
, the system has a well-defined ground state with even fermion parity, and the first excited state is a doubly degenerate odd state. In the thermodynamic limit, the spectrum of this phase shows a metallic character with a vanishing excitation gap
SM
.
For intermediate interaction strengths, beyond the reach of perturbation theory, a phase with a fourfold degenerate ground state emerges with a considerable gap in the excitation spectrum. This spectral structure is found to be independent of the size of the system
SM
.
At around
V/t=3
a second phase transition is observed. For stronger interaction strengths, the fourfold degeneracy is lifted and the gap increases linearly with
V
.

The phase at moderate interaction strengths with fourfold degeneracy shall be the focus for the remainder of our work.

Local characteristics.

The four-fold degeneracy of the ground state is resilient against fluctuations of the electrostatic potential.
The expectation value of the local electron density
n_{m,\zeta}=\sum_{s}c^{\dagger}_{m,\zeta,s}c_{m,\zeta,s}
, as confirmed by our calculations, is the same for all four states. The matrix element between two distinct states is exactly zero.

On the contrary, breaking time-reversal symmetry with a local magnetic field breaks the degeneracy of the ground state. For instance, as can be observed in Fig.
4
(a) and (b) the expectation value of
S^{y}_{m\zeta}
is sharply localized to the region with interactions and it differs in sign for the two states in the even subspace
|e_{1}\rangle
and
|e_{2}\rangle
. The other matrix elements related to the local spin operators were found to be negligible. Similar behaviour is observed in the odd subspace. This magnetization pattern exhibited by the ground state manifold is a clear consequence of the considered interaction (
6
) favouring an anisotropic spin configuration.

Zero energy single particle excitations, in the ground state manifold, are exponentially localized to the interfaces of the superconducting and interacting regions, as depicted in Fig.
4
(c). This is again an indicator for two parafermionic zero modes attached to the boundary of the interacting region. However in order to clearly distinguish these zero-energy excitations from time-reversal invariant pair of Majorana bound states
Haim et al. 2014
further analysis is necessary.

Josephson spectrum.

The Josephson spectrum, depicted in Fig.
5
decisively reveals the presence of parafermionic zero modes in the considered model.
The evolution of the energy of the localized four modes as the function of phase bias
\varphi
in between two superconducting terminals can be used to characterize anyonic excitations
Fu and Kane 2009
. In particular, time-reversal invariant Majorana modes show a
4\pi
periodic modulation
Zhang and Kane 2014b
;
Zhang et al. 2013
, while
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
parafermions exhibit
8\pi
periodicity
Alicea and Fendley 2016
.

The introduced model allows for the isolation of the Josephson current passing through two parafermionic excitations localized at the boundary of the interacting region
SM
.
In Fig.
5
we consider a short junction of
N=8
sites, thus the degeneracy is slightly lifted. At
\varphi=0
time-reversal symmetry protects the twofold degeneracy of states with odd parity while no such restrictions apply for even states.
Initializing the system in
|\psi_{1}\rangle
we can continuously follow its progression as the phase bias
\varphi
is tuned. At
\varphi=\pi
as the consequence of the joint manifestation of the parity anomaly and time-reversal symmetry, the spectrum exhibits two distinct degeneracies
Vinkler-Aviv et al. 2017
. Continuing further a crossing between
\varphi=\pi
and
\varphi=3\pi/2
is protected by local parity of the junction. Arriving at
\varphi=2\pi
the system evolves smoothly in
|\psi_{2}\rangle
. We need a further three cycles, that is a total of
8\pi
shift of the phase bias to recover the initial state.

New experimental avenues.

The introduced ladder model implies a new bottom-up approach for realizing parafermionic excitations in quantum dot arrays. Ladder-like geometries consisting of four
Hendrickx et al. 2021
and more recently eight quantum dots
Hsiao et al. 2023
have been fabricated where hopping, spin-orbit coupling and interactions could be controlled with high accuracy. Quantum dot arrays in conjunction with topological superconductivity have been studied theoretically
Liu et al. 2022
and realized experimentally
Dvir et al. 2023
;
Bordin et al. 2023
.
Crucially in these systems the relevant energy scales for superconducting pair potential, hopping, and spin-orbit coupling can be tuned and are roughly in the same typical energy scale of 10-100 meV, thus providing a versatile platform for implementing the proposed model.
Ultracold atomic ladders
Li et al. 2013
in combination with spin-orbit coupling mimicking mechanisms
Lin et al. 2011
;
Galitski and Spielman 2013
;
Valdés-Curiel et al. 2021
could also provide an alternative route for the realization of our model.
In line with frameworks employing the QSH phase layered van der Waals materials may provide a fresh realization pathway as all necessary ingredients can be found in these systems. The QSH effect has been observed in WSe
2
Ugeda et al. 2018
, superconductivity was measured in twisted bilayer graphene
Cao et al. 2018
and in 2H-NbS
2
samples
Devarakonda et al. 2020
. Anisotropic exchange interaction, crucial for our model, can be also realized using magnetic van der Waals materials
Telford et al. 2023
.

Conclusions.

We constructed a lattice model with explicit time-reversal symmetry which is capable of describing the edge states of a topological insulator in the presence of superconductivity and interaction.
We identified the different phases in the model, in particular, we found a parameter regime where the model hosts parafermionic excitations.
Our model is thus capable of capturing beyond Majorana physics, therefore it serves as an important stepping stone for simulating
topological quantum information processing devices.
Our model highlights new realization avenues for these exotic excitations in quantum dot arrays.

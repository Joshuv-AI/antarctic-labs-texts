Distributed quantum multiparameter estimation with optimal local measurements
L. Pezzè, A. Smerzi

Abstract
We study the multiparameter sensitivity bounds of a sensor made by an array of d d spatially-distributed Mach-Zehnder interferometers (MZIs). A generic single non-classical state is mixed with d − 1 d-1 vacuums to create a d d -modes entangled state, each mode entering one input port of a MZI, while a coherent state enters its second port. We show that local measurements, independently performed on each MZI, are sufficient to provide a sensitivity saturating the quantum Cramér-Rao bound. The sensor can overcome the shot noise limit for the estimation of arbitrary linear combinations of the d d phase shifts, provided that the non-classical probe state has an anti-squeezed quadrature variance. We compare the sensitivity bounds of this sensor with that achievable with d d independent MZIs, each probed with a nonclassical state and a coherent state. We find that the d d independent interferometers can achieve the same sensitivity of the entangled protocol but at the cost of using additional d d non-classical states rather than a single one. When using in the two protocols the same average number of particles per shot n ¯ T \bar{n}_{T} , we find analytically a sensitivity scaling 1 / n ¯ T 2 1/\bar{n}_{T}^{2} for the entangled case which provides a gain factor d d with respect to the separable case where the sensitivity scales as d / n ¯ T 2 d/\bar{n}_{T}^{2} . We have numerical evidences that the gain factor d d is also obtained when fixing the total average number of particles, namely when optimizing with respect to the number of repeated measurements.

Conclusions.—
We have studied the sensitivity bounds of an array of MZIs for the estimation of an arbitrary number
d
of phases.
We show that the QCRB can be saturated by local measurements for every value of the phase shifts.
We have analytically provided an optimization protocol to achieve the highest sensitivity allowed by the QCRB for arbitrary non-classical states, number of particles and the estimation of a generic combination of parameters.
Our analysis clarifies advantages offered by combing nonclassicality of the input state and mode-entanglement created by the quantum circuit.
We show a significant reduction of resources overhead when using the same probe states as in the separable strategy.
Furthermore, when fixing the total resources we show the possibility to reach a gain factor
d
, at best.
Analytical predictions are supported by a numerical maximum likelihood analysis.

.1.1
MS-probe scheme

The case of a MS-probe state,

\hat{U}_{\rm QC}=\mathbbm{1}
.
With the assumption that
\ket{\varphi_{j}}
has real coefficients when projected over Fock states, we have
\bra{\varphi_{j}}\hat{b}_{j}\ket{\varphi_{j}}=\bra{\varphi_{j}}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\ket{\varphi_{j}}
.
Therefore, it is immediate to see that the off diagonal terms of
\bm{F}_{Q}
in Eq (

[\bm{F}_{Q}]_{j,k}=0

The QFIM is therefore diagonal:

[\bm{F}_{Q}]_{j,k}=\big[\alpha_{j}^{2}\bra{\varphi_{j}}2\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}+1-\hat{b}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\ket{\varphi_{j}}+\bar{n}_{j}\big]\delta_{j,k},

(13)

where

is the average number of particles in the state
\ket{\varphi_{j}}

We notice that

\hat{p}_{j}=(\hat{b}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger})/i
is the
p

j

ScullyBOOK

Taking
\ket{\varphi_{j}}

\bra{\varphi_{j}}\hat{p}_{j}\ket{\varphi_{j}}=0

Therefore,
\bra{\varphi_{j}}2\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}+1-\hat{b}_{j}\hat{b}_{j}-\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\ket{\varphi_{j}}=(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}
, and we rewrite Eq. (

[\bm{F}_{Q}]_{j,k}=\big[\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}+\bar{n}_{j}\big]\delta_{j,k}.

(14)

The QCRB for the MS-probe schemes is obtained by inverting the diagonal QFIM Eq. (

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm QCRB}=\frac{\bm{v}^{T}\bm{F}_{Q}^{-1}\bm{v}}{m}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}+\bar{n}_{j}}.

.1.2
ME-probe scheme

In the manuscript we focus in the case where the input state of the QC given by the generic state
\ket{\varphi}=\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c(m)\ket{m}
in one mode and the vacuum in the other
d-1

\ket{\varphi_{j}}=\ket{\varphi}

\ket{\varphi_{k}}=\ket{0}
for
k\neq j

Without loss of generality we consider the mode
b_{1}
as the input mode of QC where the state
\ket{\varphi}

Below, we will also indicate this mode as
b

Let us first show that the QC performs a multinomial splitting of the state
\ket{\varphi}
.
We have

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{\rm QC}\big[\ket{\varphi}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}\big]=

(16a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c(m)\,\hat{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}\big[\ket{m}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}\big]

(16b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c(m)\,\frac{\hat{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}(\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger})^{m}}{\sqrt{m!}}\big[\ket{0}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}\big]

(16c)

(16e)

where we have used, respectively,

\hat{U}_{\rm QC}\big[\ket{0}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}\big]=\ket{0}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}

\hat{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{U}_{\rm QC}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}(U_{\rm QC})_{j,k}\hat{b}_{k}^{{\dagger}}

Restricting to mode transformations with real coefficients, we write

\hat{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{U}_{\rm QC}=\sqrt{P_{1}}\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}+\sqrt{P_{2}}\hat{b}_{2}^{\dagger}+...+\sqrt{P_{d}}\hat{b}_{d}^{\dagger},

where

The commutativity of different

When applied to the vacuum, taking into account that

\frac{\big(\hat{U}_{\rm QC}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{U}_{\rm QC}\big)^{m}}{\sqrt{m!}}\big[\ket{0}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}\big]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},...,m_{d}=0\\

(19)

Finally, taking into account the sum over coefficients

\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}=\hat{U}_{\rm QC}\big[\ket{\varphi}\otimes\ket{0}\otimes...\otimes\ket{0}\big]=\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},...,m_{d}=0\\

giving

where

P_{j}=\frac{\bra{\Psi_{\rm QC}}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bra{\Psi_{\rm QC}}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}},

is the probability to find a particle at the
j

Using the properties of the multinomial distribution, we calculate

\displaystyle\bra{\Psi_{\rm QC}}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}=P_{j}\bar{n},

(23a)

\displaystyle\bra{\Psi_{\rm QC}}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}=\bra{\Psi_{\rm QC}}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}\ket{\Psi_{\rm QC}}=\sqrt{P_{j}}\sqrt{P_{k}}\bar{n},

(23c)

with

(30)

.2
Saturation of the Quantum Cramer-Rao bound

For a distributed sensing scheme with unitary phase-encoding transformations
e^{-i\theta_{j}\hat{H}_{j}}
and commuting Hamiltonians
[\hat{H}_{j},\hat{H}_{k}]=0
for all
j,k=1,...,d
, Ref.
PezzePRL2017
reported necessary and sufficient conditions for which the Fisher information matrix computed with a set of projectors
\{\ket{\bm{\mu}}\bra{\bm{\mu}}\}_{\bm{\mu}}
is equal to the QFIM.
The condition that must be satisfied for all
j=1,\dots,d
is
PezzePRL2017

\lim_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}\to\bm{\theta}}\frac{\textrm{Re}[\langle\Psi_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}|\hat{H}_{j}|\bm{\mu}\rangle\langle\bm{\mu}|\Psi_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}\rangle]}{|\langle\bm{\mu}|\Psi_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}}\rangle|}=0,\qquad\text{for}\,\,\langle\bm{\mu}|\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle=0,

(31)

and

\textrm{Re}[\langle\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\hat{H}_{j}|\bm{\mu}\rangle\langle\bm{\mu}|\psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle]=|\langle\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\bm{\mu}\rangle|^{2}\langle\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\hat{H}_{j}|\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle,\qquad\text{for}\,\,\langle\bm{\mu}|\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle\neq 0.

(32)

A sufficient condition to fulfill both Eq. (
31
) and Eq. (
32
) is given

\textrm{Re}[\langle\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\hat{H}_{j}\ket{\bm{\mu}}\bra{\bm{\mu}}\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle]=0,\qquad\text{for all}\,\ket{\bm{\mu}},\,\bm{\theta}\,\text{and}\,j=1,...d.

(33)

In fact, if this condition is satisfied, then the numerator in Eq. (
31
) as well as the left-hand side of Eq. (
32
) will identically vanish.
The right-hand side of Eq. (
32
) will also vanish as a consequence of the completeness relation
\sum_{\bm{\mu}}|\bm{\mu}\rangle\langle\bm{\mu}|=1
and the fact that
\hat{H}_{j}
is Hermitian:
\langle\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\hat{H}_{j}|\psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle=\textrm{Re}[\langle\psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\hat{H}_{j}|\psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle]=\sum_{\bm{\mu}}\textrm{Re}[\langle\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}|\hat{H}_{j}|\bm{\mu}\rangle\langle\bm{\mu}|\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle]=0
.
As relevant in our case (see below), Eq. (
33
) can be fulfilled if
\bra{\bm{\mu}}\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle
is real and
\bra{\bm{\mu}}\hat{H}_{j}|\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle
is instead imaginary (for all
j
,
\ket{\bm{\mu}}\bra{\bm{\mu}}
and
\bm{\theta}
).

In our case, we take
\ket{\bm{\mu}}=\ket{n_{1}}\otimes\ket{m_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\ket{n_{d}}\otimes\ket{m_{d}}
, where
\ket{n_{j}}\otimes\ket{m_{j}}
are Fock states counting the number of particles at the
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
output ports, respectively, of the
j
th MZI.
The output state of the MZI array (namely before the final detection) is

\ket{\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}}=e^{-i\hat{\bm{H}}\cdot\bm{\theta}}\ket{\Psi}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},m_{1},...,n_{d},m_{d}\\
n_{1}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime},...,n_{d}^{\prime},m_{d}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}c(n_{1}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime},...,n_{d}^{\prime},m_{d}^{\prime})\mathcal{D}_{n_{1}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime}}^{n_{1},m_{1}}(\theta_{1})...\mathcal{D}_{n_{d}^{\prime},m_{d}^{\prime}}^{n_{d},m_{d}}(\theta_{d})\ket{n_{1}}\otimes\ket{m_{1}}...\ket{n_{d}}\otimes\ket{m_{d}},

(34)

where we have used Eq. (

.3
Optimization of the QCRB for the MS schemes

We first optimize Eq. (
15
) over the intensities
\alpha_{j}^{2}
(
j=1,...,d
) of the
d
coherent states, taking
\ket{\varphi_{j}}=\ket{\varphi}
for all
j
.
We thus have
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}=(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}
and
\bar{n}_{j}=\bar{n}/d
(we recall that
\bar{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{n}_{j}
), giving

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm QCRB}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}/d}.

(37)

Imposing a constant
\bar{n}_{c}=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}^{2}
, we write the Langrangian

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}/d}+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}^{2},

(38)

and impose
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\alpha_{j}^{2}=0
.
We obtain

\lambda=\frac{v_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}}{\big[\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}/d\big]^{2}}.

(39)

We now take the square root of both terms in Eq. (
39
), multiply by
[\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}]
and sum over
j
.
We obtain

\sqrt{\lambda}=\frac{(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|)\sqrt{(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}}}{\big[\bar{n}_{c}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}\big]}.

(40)

Considering again Eq. (
39
) we have

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}/d}=\frac{\lambda}{(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}}\big[\bar{n}_{c}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}\big].

(41)

Finally, by combining Eqs. (
37
), (
40
) and (
41
), we obtain

\min_{\alpha_{1}^{2},...\alpha_{d}^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm QCRB}=\frac{\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1}^{2}}{m\big[\bar{n}_{c}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}\big]},

(42)

where
\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1}^{2}=(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|)^{2}
.
We thus recover Eq. (
1
).
The optimal value of
\alpha_{j}^{2}
is

\big(\alpha_{j}^{(\rm opt)}\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\psi}}}\Bigg[\frac{|v_{j}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|}\big(\bar{n}_{c}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\psi}}+\bar{n}\big)-\frac{\bar{n}}{d}\Bigg].

(43)

The shot noise, Eq. (
4
), is obtained by taking
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi}}=1
, as in the case of the vacuum,
\ket{\varphi}=\ket{0}
, or a generic coherent state,
\ket{\varphi}=\ket{\alpha}
, where
\bar{n}_{T}=\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}
.

We now let the states
\ket{\varphi_{1}},...\ket{\varphi_{d}}
be different in general, and optimize Eq. (
15
) over all possible choices of
\ket{\varphi_{j}}
.
We first maximize the denominator of each terms of the sum in Eq. (
15
).
Specifically, we maximize
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}
over
\ket{\varphi_{j}}
for given
\bar{n}_{j}
.
Following Ref.
LangPRL2013
, we have that the optimal state is the squeezed-vacuum with
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}=2\bar{n}_{j}+2\sqrt{\bar{n}_{j}(\bar{n}_{j}+1)}+1
.
To summarise,

\max_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}\big[\alpha_{j}^{2}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p}_{j})_{\ket{\varphi_{j}}}+\bar{n}_{j}\big]=2\alpha_{j}^{2}\big[\bar{n}_{j}+\sqrt{\bar{n}_{j}(\bar{n}_{j}+1)}]+\alpha_{j}^{2}+\bar{n}_{j}\approx 4\bar{n}_{j}\alpha_{j}^{2}+\alpha_{j}^{2}+\bar{n}_{j},

(44)

where the last equality holds for
\bar{n}_{j}\gg 1
.
We further maximize Eq. (
44
) considering an optimal distribution of resources between the squeezed-vacuum state and the coherent state, fore fixed
\bar{n}_{T,j}=\alpha_{j}^{2}+\bar{n}_{j}
.
We take the derivative of Eq. (
44
) with respect to
\bar{n}_{j}
and use
\alpha_{j}^{2}=\bar{n}_{T,j}-\bar{n}_{j}
.
We obtain
\bar{n}_{j}=\alpha_{j}^{2}=\bar{n}_{T,j}/2
.
The right-hand side of Eq. (
44
) becomes
\bar{n}_{T,j}^{2}+\bar{n}_{T,j}\approx\bar{n}_{T,j}^{2}
,
such that the optimized QCRB is proportional to
\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}^{2}/\bar{n}_{T,j}^{2}
.
We want to further find the optimal values of
\bar{n}_{T,j}
, for a fixed
\bar{n}_{T}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{n}_{T,j}
.
To this aim, we write the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\bm{v}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{T,j}^{2}}+\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{n}_{T,j}.

(45)

The condition
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\bar{n}_{T,j}=0
gives

\lambda=\frac{kv_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{T,j}^{3}}.

(46)

Equation (
46
) can be rewritten as

\bar{n}_{T,j}=(k/\lambda)^{1/3}|v_{j}|^{2/3}.

(47)

Summing both sides of Eq. (
47
) over the index
j
gives
\bar{n}_{T}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{n}_{T,j}=(k/\lambda)^{1/3}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{2/3}
, that is

\frac{\lambda}{k}=\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|v_{j}|^{2/3}}{\bar{n}_{T}}\Bigg)^{3}

(48)

Finally, using Eq. (
46
) and (
48
), we have

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\bm{v}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{T,j}^{2}}=\frac{\lambda}{k}\bar{n}_{T}=\frac{||\bm{v}||_{2/3}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{T}^{2}},

(49)

where
\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{\kappa}=(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{\kappa})^{1/\kappa}
.
Using Eq. (
47
) and (
48
) we find the optimal

\big(\alpha_{j}^{(\rm opt)}\big)^{2}=\bar{n}_{j}^{\rm(opt)}=\frac{\bar{n}_{T,j}}{2}=\frac{\bar{n}_{T}}{2}\frac{|v_{j}|^{\tfrac{2}{3}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{\tfrac{2}{3}}}.

(50)

To conclude, we have demonstrated that

\min_{\ket{\varphi_{1}},...\ket{\varphi_{d}}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\bm{v}_{j}^{2}}{m\bar{n}_{T,j}^{k}}=\frac{||\bm{v}||_{2/3}^{2}}{m\bar{n}_{T}^{k}},

(51)

which recovers Eq. (
2
).

.4
Optimization of the QCRB for the ME schemes

Now we want to optimize Eq. (
30
) over the coefficients
P_{j}
that characterize the QC transformation
\hat{U}_{\rm QC}
and over the intensities
\alpha_{j}^{2}
of the
d
coherent states.
We demonstrate Eq. (
1
):

\min_{\alpha_{1}^{2},...,\alpha_{d}^{2}}\min_{P_{1},...,P_{d}}\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})_{\rm QCRB}=\frac{\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1}^{2}}{m\big[\bar{n}_{c}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}\big]},

(52)

where
\bar{n}_{c}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}^{2}
.
Let’s neglect, for the moment, the factor
m
.
We also consider the interesting case
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}\geq 1
.
The minimization in Eq. (
52
) is achieved by the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})^{2}_{\rm QCRB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}-\frac{(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}-1}{1+[(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}-1]\kappa}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}\alpha_{j}\sqrt{P_{j}}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)^{2}

(53a)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}-\frac{(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}-1}{1+[(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}-1]\kappa}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|v_{j}||\alpha_{j}|\sqrt{P_{j}}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)^{2}

(53b)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+[(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}-1]\kappa}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}

(53c)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+[(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}-1]\kappa}\frac{\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}}

(53d)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}+\bar{n}}.

(53e)

The first inequality, Eq. (
53b
), is obtained by noticing that
Eq. (
30
) is minimized by using

v_{j}\alpha_{j}\leq|v_{j}|\,|\alpha_{j}|,\qquad\text{which implies}\qquad-\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}\alpha_{j}\sqrt{P_{j}}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)^{2}\geq-\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|v_{j}||\alpha_{j}|\sqrt{P_{j}}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)^{2},

(54)

with equality if and only if
{\rm sign}(\alpha_{j})={\rm sign}(v_{j})
.
Equation (
53b
) follows form the fact that
0\leq\kappa\leq 1
and the assumption that
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}\geq 1
, giving
\tfrac{(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}-1}{1+[(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}-1]\kappa}\geq 0
.

The second inequality, Eq. (
53c
), is due to Cauchy-Schwartz
(\sum_{j}X_{j}Y_{j})^{2}\leq(\sum_{j}X_{j}^{2})(\sum_{j}Y_{j}^{2})
with
X_{j}=|v_{j}|/\sqrt{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}
and
Y_{j}=|\alpha_{j}|\sqrt{P_{j}}/\sqrt{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}
:

-\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{|v_{j}||\alpha_{j}|\sqrt{P_{j}}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)^{2}\geq-\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}P_{j}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg)=-\kappa~\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\Bigg).

(55)

The inequality (
53c
) follows, again, from the assumption
(\Delta^{2}\hat{p})_{\ket{\varphi}}\geq 1
, with equality if and only if
|\alpha_{j}|\sqrt{P_{j}}=\gamma_{1}|v_{j}|
, for some coefficient
\gamma_{1}
independent from
j
.

The inequality (
53d
) is also due to Cauchy-Schwartz with
X_{j}=|v_{j}|/\sqrt{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}
and
Y_{j}=\sqrt{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}
:

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{v_{j}^{2}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\geq\frac{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|\big)^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}=\frac{\lVert\bm{v}\rVert_{1}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}},

(56)

with equality if and only if
|v_{j}|=\gamma_{2}(\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n})
, for some coefficient
\gamma_{2}
independent from
j
.

The final inequality, Eq. (
53e
), is due to

\kappa=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}P_{j}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\leq\frac{\bar{n}_{c}}{\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n}}.

(57)

This follows from a maximization of
\kappa
obtained by using the method of Lagrange multipliers, with constraints
\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}=1
and
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}^{2}=\bar{n}_{c}
.
To perform the maximizatuion, we write the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}P_{j}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}+\lambda_{1}\sum_{j}P_{j}+\lambda_{2}\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}^{2}.

(58)

Taking the partial derivatives
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial n_{j}=0
and
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial P_{j}=0
gives

\lambda_{1}=-\frac{\alpha_{j}^{4}}{(\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n})^{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{2}=-\frac{P_{j}^{2}\bar{n}}{(\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n})^{2}}.

(59)

Taking the ration between
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
and defining
\lambda\equiv\sqrt{\lambda_{1}/\lambda_{2}}
, we obtain

P_{j}\sqrt{\bar{n}}=\lambda\alpha_{j}^{2}.

(60)

Taking the sum over the index
j
of both sides of Eq. (
60
) gives
\lambda=\bar{n}_{c}/\sqrt{\bar{n}}
.
Replacing this expression for
\lambda
into Eq. (
60
), we find

P_{j}=\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}}{\bar{n}_{c}}.

(61)

The corresponding (maximum) value of
\kappa
is
\bar{n}_{c}/(\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n})
.
Using Eq. (
57
) into Eq. (
53d
) gives Eq. (
53e
).

Let us finally analyze the conditions for the saturation of the chain of inequalities.
We replace Eq. (
61
) into the condition
|\alpha_{j}|\sqrt{P_{j}}=\gamma_{1}|v_{j}|
discussed above.
This gives
\alpha_{j}^{2}=\gamma_{1}|v_{j}|\sqrt{\bar{n}_{c}}
, where
\gamma_{1}
can be found by summing both terms of this equation over
j
:
\gamma_{1}=\sqrt{\bar{n}_{c}}/\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|
.
This provides

(\alpha_{j}^{\rm(opt)})^{2}=\frac{\bar{n}_{c}|v_{j}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|},\quad\text{and}\quad P_{j}^{\rm(opt)}=\frac{|v_{j}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|}.

(62)

We notice that the condition
|v_{j}|=\gamma_{2}(\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n})
is also satisfied by using Eq. (
62
) and
\gamma_{2}=(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|)^{2}/(\bar{n}_{c}+\bar{n})
, that is independent from
j
.
Finally, Eq. (
6
) is recovered from Eq. (
62
) taking into account the condition
{\rm sign}(\alpha_{j})={\rm sign}(v_{j})
discussed above, and
\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|=||\bm{v}||_{1}
.

.4.1
Quadrature variance and sensitivity

We evaluate Eq. (
1
) for different example of
\ket{\varphi}
:

•

Vacuum
. If
\ket{\varphi}=\ket{0}
, then
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=1
and
\bar{n}=0
.
In this case Eq. (
1
) recovers the shot-noise limit, Eq. (
4
), with
\bar{n}_{T}=\bar{n}_{c}
.

•

Coherent state
. If
\ket{\varphi}=\ket{\alpha}
, then
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=1
and
\bar{n}=\alpha^{2}
.
In this case, the QC does not create mode entanglement: the output state of the QC is a product of coherent states.
Also in this case Eq. (
1
) recovers the shot-noise limit, with
\bar{n}_{T}=\bar{n}_{c}+\alpha^{2}
.

•

Squeezed-vacuum states
. We consider
\ket{\varphi}=\hat{S}(r)\ket{0}
, where
\hat{S}(r)=e^{-r^{2}(\hat{b}^{2}+(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{2})}
is the squeezing operator, and
r>0
.
In this case, the QC generates mode entanglement and the sensitivity overcomes the SN.
We have
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=e^{2r}
and
\bar{n}=\sinh^{2}r
.
By expressing
e^{2r}
in terms of
\sinh^{2}r
we find

(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=2\bar{n}+1+2\sqrt{\bar{n}(\bar{n}+1)},

(63)

that is
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}\approx 4\bar{n}
for
\bar{n}\gg 1
.
We recall that the Mach-Zehnder sensor array with a single squeezed-vacuum state has been analyzed in Ref.
MalitestaPRA2023
, focusing on a method of moments for multiphase estimation.

•

Fock state.
Fock states
\ket{\varphi}=\ket{n}
are characterized by
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=2n+1
and
\bar{n}=n
.
The quadrature variance
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=2\bar{n}+1
is larger than 1 for
n>0
.

•

Schrödinger-cat states.
We consider

\ket{\varphi}=\frac{\ket{i\alpha}+\ket{-i\alpha}}{\sqrt{2(1+e^{-2\alpha^{2}})}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+e^{-2\alpha^{2}}}}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\cos(n\pi/2)\frac{\alpha^{n}e^{-\alpha^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket{n},

(64)

where
\ket{\pm i\alpha}
are coherent states,
\alpha
is a real number, and
\bra{i\alpha}-i\alpha\rangle=e^{-2\alpha^{2}}
.
Notice that this state has real coefficients when projected over Fock states.
We have

(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}=\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{1+e^{-2\alpha^{2}}}+1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{n}=\alpha^{2}\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha^{2}}}{1+e^{-2\alpha^{2}}}.

(65)

For
\alpha^{2}\gg 1
, we have
(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}\approx 4\bar{n}
.

.5
Details on the numerical analysis

The numerics shown in the main text refer to a maximum likelihood analysis.
The key ingredient for the analysis is the conditional probability

P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\theta})=|\bra{\bm{\mu}}\Psi_{\bm{\theta}}\rangle|^{2}=\bigg|\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c(m)\sum_{n_{1}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime},...,n_{d}^{\prime},m_{d}^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{m^{\prime}!}{m_{1}^{\prime}!...m_{d}^{\prime}!}}P_{1}^{m_{1}^{\prime}/2}...P_{d}^{m_{d}^{\prime}/2}\delta_{m,m_{1}^{\prime}+...+m^{\prime}_{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{n_{j}^{\prime}}e^{-\alpha_{j}^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{n_{j}^{\prime}!}}\mathcal{D}_{n_{1}^{\prime},m_{1}^{\prime}}^{n_{1},m_{1}}(\theta_{1})...\mathcal{D}_{n_{d}^{\prime},m_{d}^{\prime}}^{n_{d},m_{d}}(\theta_{d})\bigg|^{2}

(66)

to observe a measurement occurrence
\bm{\mu}=\{n_{1},m_{1},...,n_{d},m_{d}\}
for a fixed value of
\bm{\theta}
.
Equation (
66
) is obtained from Eqs. (
9
), (
21
) and (
34
).
Considering Fock states
\ket{\varphi}=\ket{n}
, corresponding to
c(m)=\delta_{n,m}
in Eq. (
66
) avoids an additional summation and simplifies the calculations.
We also set the coherent state intensities and the probabilities
P_{j}
equal to the optimal values given in Eq. (
6
), depending on
\bm{v}
.
To simulate experimental measurements, we randomly sample the probability
P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\theta})
.
For each sequence of
m
measurements, with outcome
\bm{\mu}_{m}=\{\bm{\mu}^{(1)},...,\bm{\mu}^{(m)}\}
, we calculate the maximum likelihood estimator according to Eq. (
8
).

There are a few technical aspects in these numerics.
First, we place a cutoff on the number of possible measurement events (which are, in principle infinite) in order to compute Eq. (
66
).
The cutoff is such that
1-\sum_{\bm{\mu}}P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\theta})\approx 10^{-4}
.
Second, the computation of the maximum according to Eq. (
8
) requires computing
P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\varphi})
on a
d
-dimensional grid
\bm{\varphi}\in[0,\pi]^{d}
.
A careful computation of the maximum-likelihood estimator requires the numerical grid to be much smaller than the typical width of the likelihood distribution
P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\varphi})
as a function of
\bm{\varphi}
, otherwise the sensitivity of the maximum-likelihood estimator is dominated by the grid size.
One of the main difficulties is that the likelihood function
P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\varphi})
shrinks around
\bm{\theta}
by increasing
m
.
To avoid the computational slowing down we search for the maximum of
P(\bm{\mu}|\bm{\varphi})
using a gradient descent algorithm.
Third, a single measurement sequence
\bm{\mu}_{m}=\{\bm{\mu}^{(1)},...,\bm{\mu}^{(m)}\}
provides a single maximum-likelihood estimate
\bm{\Theta}
.
In order to obtain
P(\bm{\Theta}|\bm{\theta})
and extract mean values and variances, we repeat the numerical experiment hundreds of times.
In all the numerical results reported in the manuscript, we compute

\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\Theta})=\int d^{d}\bm{\Theta}\,P(\bm{\Theta}|\bm{\theta})\,\big[\bm{v}\cdot(\bm{\Theta}-\bm{\theta})]^{2}-\bigg(\int d^{d}\bm{\Theta}\,P(\bm{\Theta}|\bm{\theta})\,\big[\bm{v}\cdot(\bm{\Theta}-\bm{\theta})\big]\bigg)^{2}.

(67)

corresponding to the mean squared fluctuations with respect to the true value
\bm{\theta}
.
This quantity takes into account not only the width of
P(\bm{\Theta}|\bm{\theta})
but also the possible bias of the mean of the distribution,
\int d^{d}\bm{\Theta}\,P(\bm{\Theta}|\bm{\theta})\bm{\Theta}
, from
\bm{\theta}
.

P(\bm{\Theta}|\bm{\theta})
obtained for different optimal settings of the multiparameter estimation for

.6
Sum of variances as figure of merit

Many authors have considered, as figure of merit of multiparameter estimation, the sum of variances:

V\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Delta^{2}\theta_{j}\geq V_{\rm QCRB}\equiv\frac{{\rm Tr}[\bm{F}_{Q}^{-1}]}{m},

(74)

where
V_{\rm QCRB}
is the corresponding QCRB.
Using Eq. (
29
) we find

V_{\rm EQCRB}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\bigg(1-\frac{(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}-1}{1+[(\Delta\hat{p})^{2}_{\ket{\varphi}}-1]\kappa}\frac{\alpha_{j}^{2}P_{j}}{\alpha_{j}^{2}+P_{j}\bar{n}}\bigg).

(75)

The optimization of this equation is beyond the scope of our discussion.
Here we want to compare ME-L and MS-L strategies when using the same numerical data and parameters as in Fig.
3
(b) and taking
V
as figure of merit.
The results of the analysis are reported in Fig.
6
.
While in Fig.
3
(b), using
\Delta^{2}(\Theta_{1}-\Theta_{2})
, we observe an advantage of the entangled strategy over the separable one, when using
\Delta^{2}\Theta_{1}+\Delta^{2}\Theta_{2}
, we observe the opposite: the separable strategy overcomes the entangled one.
The reason is that the information included in the off diagonal terms in the QFIM (and its inverse) is not entering when considering the sum of variances.
The off diagonal terms instead play an important role when taking
\Delta^{2}(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{\theta})
as figure of merit, in which case the entangled strategy overcomes the separable one, as shown in the main text.

Cutoff in the Bernoulli-Laplace Model With Unequal Colors and Urn Sizes
Thomas Griffin, Bailey Hall, Jackson Hebner, David Herzog, Denis Selyuzhitsky, Kevin Wong, John Wright

Abstract
We consider a generalization of the Bernoulli-Laplace model in which there are two urns and n n total balls, of which r r are red and n − r n-r white, and where the left urn holds m m balls. At each time increment, k k balls are chosen uniformly at random from each urn and then swapped. This system can be used to model phenomena such as gas particle interchange between containers or card shuffling. Under a reasonable set of assumptions, we bound the mixing time of the resulting Markov chain asymptotically in n n with cutoff at log ⁡ n \log{n} and constant window. Among other techniques, we employ the spectral analysis of [ 4 ] on the Markov transition kernel and the chain coupling tools of [ 1 ] and [ 6 ] .

Introduction

In the classical Bernoulli-Laplace model, there are a total of
n
balls split evenly between a left and a right urn. Of these balls,
{n\over 2}
are red and
{n\over 2}
are white. At each time increment,
k
balls are selected uniformly at random without replacement from each urn and then swapped between the urns.

We consider a generalization of this model where the left urn holds
m
balls and the right urn
n-m
balls, and where
r
of the balls are red and
n-r
of the balls are white. As in the classical case,
k
balls are selected from both urns uniformly and without replacement to swap. Note that implicitly,
m,r,k
are sequences in
n
but we will suppress the reliance. We will let
X_{t}
denote the number of red balls in the left urn after
t
swaps. Transition probabilities for this process are given by

p(x,y)=\mathbb{P}(x-H_{1}^{x}+H_{2}^{x}=y)

(1)

where
H_{1}^{x}
and
H_{2}^{x}
are independent hypergeometric random variables corresponding to the number of red balls moved out of the left urn and into the left urn during the swap, respectively. More precisely,

\displaystyle H_{1}^{x}\sim\mathrm{Hyp}(m,x,k)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636ptH_{2}^{x}\sim\mathrm{Hyp}(n-m,r-x,k).

(2)

This process is an irreducible, aperiodic Markov chain on the finite state space

\mathcal{X}=\{\max(0,r+m-n),\max(0,r+m-n)+1,...,\min(m,r)\}

and so converges to a stationary distribution
\pi
. Given an initial distribution
\mu
for
X_{0}
, we define the
total variation distance
between the law of
X_{t}
and the stationary distribution by

\|\mu P^{t}-\pi\|_{TV}=\sup_{A\subset\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\mu P^{t}(A)-\pi(A)\right\rvert,

where
P
is the Markov transition kernel corresponding to the chain. We may then define the
mixing time at
\varepsilon
by

t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\inf\{t:\|\delta_{x}P^{t}-\pi\|_{TV}\leq\varepsilon\},

where
\delta_{x}
is the point distribution at
x
(meaning the chain starts at
X_{0}=x\in\mathcal{X}
).

We say a sequence of Markov chains
exhibits cutoff
in total variation if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t^{(n)}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon)}{t^{(n)}_{\mathrm{mix}}(1-\varepsilon)}=1\hskip 11.38109pt\text{ for all fixed }\hskip 11.38109pt\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Further, we say a sequence
a_{n}
is a
cutoff window
if there exists a constant
c(\varepsilon)
for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

a_{n}=o\left(t^{(n)}_{\mathrm{mix}}\left({1\over 2}\right)\right)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\left\lvert t^{(n)}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon)-t^{(n)}_{\mathrm{mix}}(1-\varepsilon)\right\rvert\leq c(\varepsilon)a_{n}\hskip 11.38109pt\text{ for all }\hskip 11.38109ptn.

This paper analyzes the mixing times of sequences
\{X_{t}^{(n)}\}_{n}
of generalized Bernoulli-Laplace chains. To make this manageable, we introduce the following assumption.

Assumption 1.1
.

For each
n
, assume without loss of generality that
r,m\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Suppose
\lim_{n\to\infty}{k\over n}=\gamma
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}{m\over n}=h
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{r\over n}=\eta
, and that

\displaystyle 0<\gamma\leq h\leq\frac{1}{2},\hskip 11.38109pt0<\eta\leq{1\over 2},\hskip 11.38109pt\gamma\neq h(1-h),\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\gamma\neq\frac{1}{2}\text{ if }h={1\over 2}.

Remark 1.2
.

When proving asymptotic results, Assumption
1.1
allows us to make the following assumptions without loss of generality

\displaystyle m,n-m\geq 2,\hskip 11.38109ptk\neq\frac{m(n-m)}{n},\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{if }k=m\text{ then }m\neq\frac{n}{2}.

Further,

\mathcal{X}=\{0,\dots,\min(m,r)\}.

For completeness, we explicitly provide the stationary distributions of the chains. The proof is simple and follows, for example, from the logic in
[
7
]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

The stationary distribution of
X_{t}^{(n)}
is
\pi^{(n)}\sim\mathrm{Hyp}(n,r,m)
, with probability mass function

\pi^{(n)}(j)=\frac{{r\choose j}{n-r\choose m-j}}{{n\choose m}}.

We now define for each
n

t_{n}:=\frac{-\log{n}}{2\log{\left\lvert 1-\frac{kn}{m(n-m)}\right\rvert}}=\frac{\log n}{2\left\lvert\log\left\lvert 1-\frac{kn}{m(n-m)}\right\rvert\right\rvert}.

(3)

Notation
. Let
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
be sequences in
\mathbb{R}
. We write
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
if there exists a constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert\leq C\left\lvert b_{n}\right\rvert
for all
n
large enough. If
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}=1
, we write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
. To denote the probability of transitioning from a state
x
into a set
S
in
t
steps, we write

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,S):=\sum_{y\in S}p_{t}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in S),

where
\mathbb{P}_{x}
denotes the probability measure induced by the Markov chain starting from a state
X_{0}=x\in\cal X
. We may also sometimes write
X_{t}^{x}
to refer to a random variable induced by the same starting criterion, especially in the context of two chains started at different initial states.

Our main result bounds
t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)
within a constant distance of
t_{n}
and is given below.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\{X_{t}^{(n)}\}_{n}
be a sequence of generalized Bernoulli-Laplace chains satisfying Assumption
1.1
. Then there exist constants
N_{1},N_{2},c
and
C
, all depending on
\varepsilon,\gamma,\eta,h
, such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
,

t_{n}-c\leq t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon),

and for all
n\geq N_{2}
,

t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{n}+C,

with
t_{n}
as in (
3
).

Remark 1.5
.

By taking the maximums of a finite number of constants, Theorem
1.4
implies that for any sequence
\{X_{t}^{(n)}\}_{n}
of Bernoulli-Laplace chains satisfying Assumption
1.1
, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}
such

t_{n}-C^{\prime}\leq t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{n}+C^{\prime}

for all
n
. This further implies that
t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)
exhibits cutoff at
t_{n}
with constant window.

Remark 1.6
.

If we assume further that

\left\lvert\frac{1}{\log\left\lvert 1-\frac{\gamma}{\eta(1-\eta)}\right\rvert}-\frac{1}{\log\left\lvert 1-\frac{kn}{m(n-m)}\right\rvert}\right\rvert=O\left(\frac{1}{\log n}\right),

then Theorem
1.4
implies (for possibly different
C^{\prime}
) that

\frac{-\log{n}}{2\log\left\lvert 1-\frac{\gamma}{\eta(1-\eta)}\right\rvert}-C^{\prime}\leq t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{-\log{n}}{2\log\left\lvert 1-\frac{\gamma}{\eta(1-\eta)}\right\rvert}+C^{\prime},

(4)

and so
t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)
exhibits cutoff at order
\log n
with constant window.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.4
fails when
\gamma=h(1-h)
. In that case,
t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)
is usually a bounded sequence. For details, see Section
4
.

2.1
Spectral Lemmata

All of the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions for the Bernoulli-Laplace model are known (see
[
4
]
), but here we only need the first two. This is similar to the approach in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Markov transition has first two right eigenfunctions

\displaystyle s_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n}{rm}x

(5)

\displaystyle s_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2(n-1)}{rm}x+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{r(r-1)m(m-1)}x(x-1)

(6)

with respective eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{nk}{m(n-m)}

(7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2(n-1)k}{m(n-m)}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)k(k-1)}{m(m-1)(n-m)(n-m-1)}.

(8)

That is,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[s_{1}(X_{1})]=\lambda_{1}s_{1}(x)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathbb{E}_{x}[s_{2}(X_{1})]=\lambda_{2}s_{2}(x).

Proof.

This can be checked by a direct calculation.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

For all
t\geq 0,x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[s_{1}(X_{t})]

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{t}s_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[s_{2}(X_{t})]

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}^{t}s_{2}(x)

by the Markov property.

Doing some algebra, we find that

\displaystyle s_{1}^{2}(x)=b_{0}+b_{1}s_{1}(x)+b_{2}s_{2}(x),

where

\displaystyle b_{0}={(n-m)(n-r)\over(n-1)rm},\hskip 14.22636ptb_{1}=-{(n-2r)(n-2m)\over(n-2)rm},\hskip 14.22636ptb_{2}={n^{2}(r-1)(m-1)\over(n-1)(n-2)rm}.

Corollary 2.3
.

For any
x,t,
we have

\mathbb{E}_{x}[s_{1}^{2}(x)]=b_{0}+b_{1}\lambda_{1}^{t}s_{1}(x)+b_{2}\lambda_{2}^{t}s_{2}(x).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be as above. Then
\lambda_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}=O({1\over n}).

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{nk}{m(n-m)}\right)^{2}-\left(1-\frac{2(n-1)k}{m(n-m)}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)k(k-1)}{m(m-1)(n-m)(n-m-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2k}{m(n-m)}+\left({n^{2}k^{2}\over m^{2}(n-m)^{2}}-{(n-2)(n-1)k(k-1)\over(n-m)(n-m-1)m(m-1)}\right).

The claim follows.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\lambda_{2}
be as above,
t_{n}
as in (
3
), and suppose
\gamma\neq h(1-h)
. Then
\lambda_{2}^{t_{n}}=O({1\over n})
.

Proof.

We first show that

\lambda_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{2}=\frac{2k}{m(n-m)}+\left({n^{2}k^{2}\over m^{2}(n-m)^{2}}-{(n-2)(n-1)k(k-1)\over(n-m)(n-m-1)m(m-1)}\right)\geq 0.

Notice that for
n\geq 4
, we have

\frac{4k^{2}}{m^{2}(n-m)^{2}}\leq\frac{2k}{m(n-m)}.

It thus suffices to show that

\displaystyle{(n^{2}+4)k^{2}\over m^{2}(n-m)^{2}}-{(n-2)(n-1)k(k-1)\over(n-m)(n-m-1)m(m-1)}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

which is equivalent to

\frac{(n^{2}+4)k}{m(n-m)}-\frac{(n-2)(n-1)(k-1)}{(n-m-1)(m-1)}\geq 0.

It is easy to check that the claim holds for
k=1
and
k=m
. Since the left-hand side is linear in
k
, this implies that the claim holds for all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

The condition
\gamma\neq h(1-h)
implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{2}>0
. We may thus assume without loss of generality that
\lambda_{2}=\left\lvert\lambda_{2}\right\rvert
. Therefore
\log{(\lambda_{1}^{2})}-\log{\left\lvert\lambda_{2}\right\rvert}=:a_{n}\geq 0
, and so

\displaystyle\left\lvert\lambda_{2}^{t_{n}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\log{\left\lvert\lambda_{2}\right\rvert}\frac{\log n}{|\log(\lambda_{1}^{2})|}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\left[\log\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}\right)-a_{n}\right]\frac{\log n}{\left\lvert\log(\lambda_{1}^{2})\right\rvert}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\log n\right)\exp\left(\frac{-a_{n}\log n}{\left\lvert\log(\lambda_{1}^{2})\right\rvert}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}.

∎

Bounds under Assumption

3.1
Lower Bound

In this section, we prove the lower bound of Theorem
1.4
, which we restate below.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{X_{t}^{(n)}\}_{n}
be a sequence of generalized Bernoulli-Laplace chains satisfying Assumption
1.1
. Then there exist constants
N_{1}:=N_{1}(\varepsilon,\gamma,\eta,h)
and
c:=c(\varepsilon,\gamma,\eta,h)
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
,

t_{n}-c\leq t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon).

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle s(x)=\sqrt{n-1}s_{1}(x)

and calculate the mean and variance of
s(X_{t})
both in the case where the chain is started from
0
and with respect to the stationary distribution. We also let
t=t_{n}-c
, where
c
is to be determined later.

Clearly,
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[s(X_{t})]=0
and

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{\pi}(s(X_{t}))

\displaystyle=(n-1){n^{2}\over r^{2}m^{2}}\text{Var}_{\pi}(X_{t})={(n-m)(n-r)\over rm}\leq{(1-h)(1-\eta)\over\eta h}+2

for sufficiently large
n
. Now,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]|

\displaystyle=|\sqrt{n-1}\lambda_{1}^{t}s_{1}(0)|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n-1}|\lambda_{1}|^{t_{n}}|\lambda_{1}|^{-c}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n-1\over n}|\lambda_{1}|^{-c}.

To calculate

\displaystyle\text{Var}_{0}[s(X_{t})]

\displaystyle=(n-1)\mathbb{E}_{0}[s_{1}(X_{t})^{2}]-\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]^{2},

we first write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}[s_{1}(X_{t})^{2}]

\displaystyle=b_{0}+b_{1}\lambda_{1}^{t}+b_{2}\lambda_{2}^{t}

\displaystyle={(n-m)(n-r)\over(n-1)rm}-{(n-2m)(n-2r)\over(n-2)rm}\lambda_{1}^{t_{n}}\lambda_{1}^{-c}+{(m-1)(r-1)n^{2}\over rm(n-1)(n-2)}\lambda_{2}^{t_{n}}\lambda_{2}^{-c}.

By Lemma
2.5
,
\lambda_{2}^{t_{n}}=O({1\over n})
. Now consider these three terms in
(n-1)\mathbb{E}_{0}[s_{1}(X_{t})^{2}]
in the limit as
n\to\infty
. The first,

{(n-1)(n-m)(n-r)\over(n-1)rm}\to{(1-h)(1-\eta)\over h\eta}.

The second vanishes, and the third is asymptotically bounded by
1
. It follows that

\text{Var}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\leq{(1-h)(1-\eta)\over h\eta}+2=:K

for sufficiently large
n
.
We now define the sets

A_{\alpha}=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\left\lvert s(x)\right\rvert\leq\alpha\sqrt{K}\right\}

and

\displaystyle B_{d,c}

\displaystyle=\left\{x:\min(|s(x)-\lambda_{1}^{-c}|,|s(x)+\lambda_{1}^{-c}|)\leq d\sqrt{K}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x:|s(x)-\lambda_{1}^{-c}|\leq d\sqrt{K}\right\}\cup\left\{x:|s(x)+\lambda_{1}^{-c}|\leq d\sqrt{K}\right\}.

The two sets reflect the two possibilities for the mean. Using Chebyshev’s inequality, we calculate

\displaystyle\pi_{n}(A_{\alpha})\geq 1-{1\over\alpha^{2}}

For
B_{d,c}
, we have

\displaystyle P_{t}(0,B_{d,c})

\displaystyle\geq P_{t}\left(0,\left\{\left\lvert s(X_{t})-\sqrt{n\over n-1}\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\right\rvert\leq d\sqrt{K}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{t}\left(0,\left\{\left\lvert s(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\right\rvert\leq d\sqrt{K}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+P_{t}\left(0,\left\{\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]-d\sqrt{K}\leq s(X_{t})<\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\sqrt{n\over n-1}-d\sqrt{K}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-P_{t}\left(0,\left\{\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]+d\sqrt{K}<s(X_{t})\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\sqrt{n\over n-1}+d\sqrt{K}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq P_{t}\left(0,\left\{\left\lvert s(X_{t})-\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\right\rvert\leq d\sqrt{K}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-P_{t}\left(0,\left\{\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]+d\sqrt{K}<s(X_{t})\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\sqrt{n\over n-1}+d\sqrt{K}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-{1\over d^{2}}-{\left\lvert\left(\sqrt{n\over n-1}-1\right)\mathbb{E}_{0}[s(X_{t})]\right\rvert\over{n\sqrt{n-1}\over rm}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-{1\over d^{2}}-{K^{\prime}\over\sqrt{n}}

eventually, where
K^{\prime}
is a constant.
Notice that if

|\lambda_{1}|^{-c}-d\sqrt{K}\geq 2\alpha\sqrt{K}

(10)

we have that
A_{\alpha}
and
B_{d,c}
are disjoint, implying that
P_{t}(0,A_{\alpha})\leq{1\over d^{2}}+{K^{\prime}\over\sqrt{n}}
. Choosing
\alpha
and

such that
{1\over\alpha^{2}}
and

are less than

, and then

such that (

) is satisfied, we have

\displaystyle||P_{t}(0,\cdot)-\pi_{n}||_{TV}

\displaystyle\geq|P_{t}(0,A_{\alpha})-\pi_{n}(A_{\alpha})|

\displaystyle\geq 1-{1\over\alpha^{2}}-{1\over d^{2}}-{K^{\prime}\over\sqrt{n}}

Observe that equality is (asymptotically) achieved in (

.

3.2

We now begin the proof of the upper bound in Theorem

.

Definition 3.2
.

Define the following:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\left|x-\frac{rm}{n}\right|\leq\kappa\sqrt{n}\right\}

\displaystyle F_{n}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa)^{2}:\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\kappa^{3}}\right\}

3.2.1
Path Coupling Contraction

We use a generalization of the coupling introduced in
[
6
]
. Let
Y_{1,t}
and
Y_{2,t}
be generalized Bernoulli-Laplace chains with parameters
m,r,n
. We define a coupling between these chains as
follows: In each chain, label the balls in the left urn with
\{1,\dots,m\}
and the balls in the right urn with
\{m+1,\dots,n\}
such that the red balls in any given urn have lower indices than the white balls in the same urn. Now, choose subsets
A\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
and
B\subset\{m+1,\dots,n\}
uniformly at random such that
\left\lvert A\right\rvert=\left\lvert B\right\rvert=k
. Then, in both chains, move the balls with labels in
A
to the right urn and labels in
B
to the left urn. Relabel the balls and repeat.

This is a coupling because each individual chain,
Y_{1,t}
or
Y_{2,t}
, has the same behavior as
X_{t}
. However, there is a tendency for
Y_{1,t}
and
Y_{2,t}
to approach one another because the same indices are being swapped in each chain and indices are not independent of color. This is formalized below.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
. Then for all
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}[|Y_{t}^{x}-Y_{t}^{y}|]\leq\left(1-\frac{k(n-2k)}{m(n-m)}\right)^{t}|x-y|.

That is, each application of this path coupling’s Markov transition is a strict contraction with coefficient

1-\frac{k(n-2k)}{m(n-m)}\in(0,1).

Proof.

The proof is very similar to that of Lemma 3.4 in
[
1
]
. We may assume without loss of generality that
x>y
. By the Markov property, it suffices to prove for
t=1
. Now consider the case when
x-y=1
. Then
Y_{1}^{x}-Y_{1}^{y}
can only takes values
-1,0,\text{ and }1
. We directly calculate that

\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}^{x}-Y_{1}^{y}=-1)=\frac{k}{m}\left(\frac{k}{n-m}\right)

and

\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}^{x}-Y_{1}^{y}=1)=\left(\frac{m-k}{m}\right)\left(\frac{n-m-k}{n-m}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}^{x}-Y_{1}^{y}|]

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{m}\left(\frac{k}{n-m}\right)+\left(\frac{m-k}{m}\right)\left(\frac{n-m-k}{n-m}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{k(n-2k)}{m(n-m)}.

When

, we use the triangle inequality to get

\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}^{x}-Y_{1}^{y}|]\leq\sum_{i=0}^{x-y-1}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{1}^{y+i}-Y_{1}^{y+i+1}|]\leq(x-y)\left(1-\frac{k(n-2k)}{m(n-m)}\right).

∎

Now we state the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose Assumption
1.1
is satisfied. Then for any

such that

\displaystyle\kappa^{4}\left(1-{\gamma(1-2\gamma)\over h(1-h)}\right)^{\kappa}\leq{1\over\kappa^{2}},

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x,y}(\kappa)>t_{n}+\kappa)=O\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\right).

Proof.

First observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau_{x,y}(\kappa)>t_{n}+\kappa)\leq\mathbb{P}(X_{t_{n}}^{x}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa))+\mathbb{P}(X_{t_{n}}^{y}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa))+\mathbb{P}(X_{t_{n}+\kappa}^{x}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa))

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}(X_{t_{n}+\kappa}^{y}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa))+\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{t_{n}}^{x},Y_{t_{n}}^{y}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa),\left\lvert Y_{t_{n}+\kappa}^{x}-Y_{t_{n}+\kappa}^{y}\right\rvert>\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\kappa^{3}}\right),

where we may use

X
and
Y
share the same distributions by the coupling. We now work to asymptotically bound these terms by

. For any constants

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z}(X_{t}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(\left|X_{t}-{rm\over n}\right|>\kappa\sqrt{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{z}\left(s_{1}^{2}(X_{t})>\kappa^{2}{n^{3}\over r^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{1\over\kappa^{2}}{r^{4}\over n^{3}}\mathbb{E}_{z}[s_{1}^{2}(X_{t})]

\displaystyle={1\over\kappa^{2}}{r^{4}\over n^{3}}\left(b_{0}+b_{1}\lambda_{1}^{t}s_{1}(z)+b_{2}\lambda_{2}^{t}s_{2}(z)\right).

Two of the terms involve

\lambda_{1}
and

are bounded. For the final term, notice that
b_{2}
and
s_{2}(z)
is bounded. It therefore suffices to show that

2.5

\mathbb{P}_{z}(X_{t}\not\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa))=O\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\right).

We now bound the last term. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{t_{n}}^{x},Y_{t_{n}}^{y}\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa),\left|Y_{t_{n}+\kappa}^{x}-Y_{t_{n}+\kappa}^{y}\right|>{\sqrt{n}\over\kappa^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x,y\in\mathcal{I}_{n}(\kappa)}{\kappa^{3}\over\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{\kappa}^{x}-Y_{\kappa}^{y}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa^{4}\left(1-{k(n-2k)\over m(n-m)}\right)^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=O\left({1\over\kappa^{2}}\right).

∎

\displaystyle 2\|\mu_{H}-\mu_{Z}\|_{TV}

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=L}^{R}\left|{\phi(x_{j})\over\sigma}-{\phi(x_{j})\over\mathcal{N}\sigma}\right|+\sum_{j=L}^{R}\left|\mathbb{P}(H=j)-{\phi(x_{j})\over\sigma}\right|

Let
\{X_{t}^{(n)}\}_{n}
be a sequence of generalized Bernoulli-Laplace chains satisfying Assumption
4.1
. Then there exist constants
N:=N(\varepsilon,\gamma,\eta,h)
and
C:=C(\varepsilon,\gamma,\eta,h)
such that for all
n\geq N
,

t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{(n)}(\varepsilon)\leq q_{n}+C.

We now employ this proposition with the rate of convergence assumption (
13
) to prove the upper bound of Theorem
4.2
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
q_{n}
is bounded when Assumption
4.1
holds. This is trivially satisfied for all
n
such that
\lambda_{2}=0
, so assume
\left\lvert\lambda_{2}\right\rvert\neq 0
. Using Lemma
2.4
, we see that

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{2}+O\left({1\over n}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left({1\over\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}+O\left({1\over n}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left({1\over n}\right).

That is to say, there exists a
K
such that
\left\lvert\lambda_{2}\right\rvert\leq{K\over n}
. Thus

\log\left\lvert\lambda_{2}\right\rvert\leq\log(K)-\log(n).

Consider only
n
large enough such that
\log(K)-\log(n)<0.
Then

\left\lvert\log|\lambda_{2}|\right\rvert\geq\log(n)-\log(K).

This implies that

q_{n}=\frac{\log(n)}{\left\lvert\log|\lambda_{2}|\right\rvert}\leq\frac{\log(n)}{\log(n)-\log(K)},

which is clearly bounded.
∎

5
Conclusion

5.1
Generalizations

Theorem
1.4
generalizes pre-existing work on the two-color, two-urn Bernoulli-Laplace model. In particular, we extend the work from
[
6
]
on the lower bound and
[
1
]
on the upper bound to a model with uneven distributions of colors and urn sizes. We also show that mixing time is bounded under certain conditions in Theorem
4.2
, which we believe to be a new result.

Another possible generalization comes from letting there be more than 2 colors. The complete spectrum of the Markov transition in this case is still known (see
[
4
]
), but the more complicated state space is harder to work with, and many proofs (e.g. that of Proposition
3.5
) breaks down.

Another case still unproven (though analogous to
[
2
]
) is when
\gamma=0
. We put forth the following conjecture, which is the analogue of the result in
[
6
]
and notably lacks constant window.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
\{X_{t}^{(n)}\}_{n}
be a sequence of Bernoulli-Laplace chains. For each
n
, assume without loss of generality that
r,m\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Suppose
\lim_{n\to\infty}{k\over n}=0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}{m\over n}=h
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}{r\over n}=\eta
. Then there exists constants
N,c,c^{\prime},
and
c^{\prime\prime}
all depending on
\varepsilon,\eta,\gamma,
and
h
such that for all
n\geq N
,

\frac{n\log n}{c(\varepsilon,\eta,\gamma,h)k}-\frac{k}{n}c^{\prime}(\varepsilon,\eta,\gamma,h)\leq t_{mix}(\varepsilon)\leq t_{n}+\frac{c^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon,\eta,\gamma,h)n}{k}\log\log n.

The last generalization we propose is to let
k
and
r
be random variables such that convergence
{k\over n}\to\gamma
and
{r\over n}\to\eta
in distribution. We are not aware of any previous literature on such a model.

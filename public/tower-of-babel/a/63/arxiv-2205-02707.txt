A DG method for a stress formulation of the elasticity eigenproblem with strongly imposed symmetry Thanks: This research was supported by Ministerio de Ciencia e Innovación, Spain, Project PID2020-116287GB-I00.
Salim Meddahi

Abstract
We introduce a pure–stress formulation of the elasticity eigenvalue problem with mixed boundary conditions. We propose an H(div)-based discontinuous Galerkin method that imposes strongly the symmetry of the stress for the discretization of the eigenproblem. Under appropriate assumptions on the mesh and the degree of polynomial approximation, we demonstrate the spectral correctness of the discrete scheme and derive optimal rates of convergence for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. Finally, we provide numerical examples in two and three dimensions.

Introduction

The finite element determination of the vibration characteristics (natural frequencies and mode shapes) of elastic bodies is of great interest in structural mechanics. For example, the knowledge of the eigenfrequencies keeps the forced oscillations safe from resonance regimes, and the eigenmodes can be used to expand the solution of transient elastodynamic problems in a Fourier series. We approach this topic from the perspective of the mixed formulation derived from the Hellinger-Reissner variational principle. Namely, we are interested in variational formulations in which the Cauchy stress tensor prevails as the main unknown. In addition to the fact that accurate approximations of the stress are of paramount importance in many applications, it is well known that mixed formulations are immune to locking in the case of nearly incompressible materials.

In recent years, the theory of Descloux–Nassif–Rappaz
[
11
,
12
]
for non-compact operators has been successfully applied to the mixed finite element analysis of eigenvalue problems in elasticity
[
26
,
23
,
25
]
. The same approach allowed to deal with mixed formulations of the Stokes eigenproblem formulated in terms of a pseudo-stress
[
27
,
22
]
or the Cauchy stress tensor
[
25
]
. The symmetry requirement for the stress tensor, which reflects the conservation of angular momentum, is a specific feature of the Hellinger-Reissner variational principle. The imposition of this restriction in association with H(div)-conformity gives rise to conforming Galerkin methods with a very large number of degrees of freedom, and which are difficult to implement
[
2
,
20
]
. A common practice to overcome this drawback consists in enforcing the symmetry constraint variationally through a Lagrange multiplier. In this context,
[
26
,
25
]
validated the use of the weakly symmetric mixed finite elements
[
5
,
4
,
9
,
18
]
for the stress formulation of the elasticity eigenproblem.

Motivated by the ability of DG methods to handle efficiently
hp
-adaptive strategies and to facilitate the implementation of high order methods, an H(div)-based interior penalty version of
[
26
]
(that retains the weak imposition of the symmetry) has been introduced in
[
23
]
. Nevertheless, on account of
[
3
,
19
,
33
]
, it is known that relaxing H(div)-conformity by using non-conforming or DG approximations for the elasticity source problem allows the incorporation of the symmetry constraint in the energy space at a reasonable computational cost. To our knowledge, the eigenvalue numerical analysis of such non-conforming/DG mixed methods is not yet available. In this work, our main issue is to determine whether a strong imposition of the symmetry constraint in the scheme introduced in
[
23
]
provides a correct eigenvalue approximation.

The resulting DG-method approximates the stress by symmetric tensors with piecewise polynomial entries of degree
k\geq 1
, in 2D and 3D. We note that, the stress/displacement DG formulation introduced in
[
33
]
for the elasticity source problem relays on the same discrete space for the stress. However, the displacement field is not present as an independent variable in our DG formulation because it is eliminated via the momentum balance equation. The same equation can be used to post-process the displacement at the discrete level. We prove that the inf-sup stability of the Scott-Vogelius element
[
31
]
for the Stokes problem (see Assumption
2
below) is a sufficient condition for the spectral correctness of our DG method. We also obtain optimal error estimates for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions in an adequate DG norm.

We finally highlight that, unlike
[
26
,
23
]
, our analysis does not rely on any extra Sobolev regularity of an auxiliary elasticity source problem. Hence, our analysis remains valid for eigenproblems posed in general domains, with mixed boundary conditions and with minimal requirements on material coefficients.

Outline.

The contents of this paper have been organized in the following manner. The remainder of this section contains notational conventions and definitions of Sobolev spaces. Section

Notations and Sobolev spaces.

We denote the space of real matrices of order
d\times d
by
\mathbb{M}
and let
\mathbb{S}:=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{M};\ \boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathtt{t}}\}
be the subspace of symmetric matrices, where
\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\mathtt{t}}:=(\tau_{ji})
stands for the transpose of
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau_{ij})
. The component-wise inner product of two matrices
\boldsymbol{\sigma},\,\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{M}
is defined by
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\boldsymbol{\tau}:=\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{ij}\tau_{ij}
. We also introduce
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{ii}
and denote by
I
the identity in
\mathbb{M}
.
Along this paper we convene to apply all differential operators row-wise. Hence, given a tensorial function
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{M}
and a vector field
\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set the divergence
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the gradient
\nabla\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{M}
, and the linearized strain tensor
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}):\Omega\to\mathbb{S}
as

(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma})_{i}:=\sum_{j}\partial_{j}\sigma_{ij}\,,\quad(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})_{ij}:=\partial_{j}u_{i}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\boldsymbol{u}+(\nabla\boldsymbol{u})^{\mathtt{t}}\right).

Let
\Omega
be a polyhedral Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d=2,3)
, with boundary
\partial\Omega
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
H^{s}(\Omega,E)
stands for the usual Hilbertian Sobolev space of functions with domain
\Omega
and values in E, where
E
is either
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{M}
. In the case

we simply write
H^{s}(\Omega)
. The norm of
H^{s}(\Omega,E)
is denoted
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{s,\Omega}
and the corresponding semi-norm
|\cdot|_{s,\Omega}
, indistinctly for
E=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{M}
. We use the convention
H^{0}(\Omega,E):=L^{2}(\Omega,E)
and let
(\cdot,\cdot)
be the inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega,E)
, for
E=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{M}
, namely,

(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}):=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\boldsymbol{\tau}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M}).

We consider the space
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,E)
of tensors
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{2}(\Omega,E)

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})

\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert^{2}_{H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)}:=\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}
, where
E

\mathbb{M}
or
\mathbb{S}
. Let
\boldsymbol{n}
be the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
. Let
\boldsymbol{\tau}
be a sufficiently regular symmetric tensor, Green’s formula

(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v}))+(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{v})=\int_{\partial\Omega}\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\qquad\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(1.1)

can be used to extend the normal trace operator
\boldsymbol{\tau}\to(\boldsymbol{\tau}|_{\partial\Omega})\boldsymbol{n}
to a linear continuous mapping
(\cdot|_{\partial\Omega})\boldsymbol{n}:\,H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})\to H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the dual of
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Throughout this paper, we shall use the letter

to denote a generic positive constant independent of the mesh size

2
A stress formulation of the elasticity eigenproblem

Our aim is to determine the natural frequencies
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
of an elastic structure with mass density
\varrho
and occupying a polyhedral Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) . This amounts to solve the eigenproblem

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\omega^{2}\varrho(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u})\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

(2.2)

where
\boldsymbol{u}:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the displacement field and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{S}
is the Cauchy stress tensor. The symmetric and positive-definite 4
th
-order tensor
\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{x}):\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{S}
involved in the linear material law (
2.2
) is known as the compliance tensor. We assume that there exist
a^{+}>a^{-}>0
such that

a^{-}\boldsymbol{\zeta}:\boldsymbol{\zeta}\,\leq\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{x})\boldsymbol{\zeta}:\boldsymbol{\zeta}\leq a^{+}\,\boldsymbol{\zeta}:\boldsymbol{\zeta}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in\mathbb{S},\quad\text{a.e. in $\Omega$}.

We also suppose that there exists a polygonal/polyhedral disjoint partition
\big\{\bar{\Omega}_{j},\ j=1,\ldots,J\big\}
of
\bar{\Omega}
such that
\varrho|_{\Omega_{j}}:=\varrho_{j}>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,J
and let
\varrho^{+}:=\max_{j}\varrho_{j}
and
\varrho^{-}:=\min_{j}\varrho_{j}
.

We impose the boundary condition
\boldsymbol{u}=\mathbf{0}
on a subset
\Gamma_{D}\subset\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
of positive surface measure and let the structure free of stress on
\Gamma_{N}:=\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{D}
. Here, we opt for combining the equilibrium equation (
2.1
) with the constitutive law (
2.2
) to eliminate the displacement field
\boldsymbol{u}
and impose
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
as a primary variable. This procedure leads to the following eigensystem: find eigenmodes
0\neq\boldsymbol{\sigma}:\Omega\to\mathbb{S}
and eigenfrequencies
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
such that,

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\left(\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)&=\omega^{2}\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\quad\text{ in $\Omega$},\\
\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}&=0\quad\text{ on $\Gamma_{D}$},\\
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\boldsymbol{n}&=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{ on $\Gamma_{N}$},\end{split}

(2.3)

where
\boldsymbol{n}
stands for the exterior unit normal vector on
\Gamma
.

In the following, we write
H
for the space
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})
endowed with the
\mathcal{A}
-weighted inner product
\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}:=\left(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)
and denote the corresponding norm
\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\left(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)
. The eigenfunctions
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
will be sought in the closed subspace
X
of
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
defined by

X:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S});\quad\left\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=0\quad\text{$\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})$,\, $\boldsymbol{\phi}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\mathbf{0}$}\right\},

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\Gamma}
holds for the duality pairing between
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We introduce the symmetric and positive semidefinite bilinear form
c:\,X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
given by

c(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}):=\left(\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)

and endow
X
with the Hilbertian inner product
a\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right):=\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}+c(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau})
. We denote the corresponding norm
\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert^{2}_{X}:=a(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\tau})
.

Testing the first equation of (
2.3
) with
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X
and applying Green’s formula (
1.1
) we deduce, after a shift argument, the following pure–stress variational formulation of the eigenproblem: find
0\neq\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X
and
\kappa=1+\omega^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

a\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)=\kappa\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X.

(2.4)

We introduce the source operator
\tilde{T}:\,L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})\to X
corresponding to the variational eigenproblem (
2.4
); which is defined for any
\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})
by

a\left(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)=\left(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X.

(2.5)

Obviously,
\tilde{T}
is linear and bounded, actually it holds,

\left\lVert\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rVert_{X}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{f}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{f}\in H.

(2.6)

We denote the
H^{1}
-Sobolev space with incorporated Dirichlet boundary conditions on either
\Gamma_{D}
or
\Gamma_{N}
by

H^{1}_{\star}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d});\ v|_{\Gamma_{\star}}=\mathbf{0}\right\},\quad\star\in\left\{D,N\right\}.

It is important to notice that testing (
2.5
) with a tensor
\boldsymbol{\tau}:\Omega\to\mathbb{S}
whose entries are indefinitely differentiable and compactly supported in
\Omega
proves that
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}))=\mathcal{A}(\tilde{T}-I)\boldsymbol{f}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})
. Hence, by virtue of Korn’s inequality,
\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and it follows readily from Green’s formula (
1.1
) that
\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})
vanishes on
\Gamma_{D}
. In other words,
\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and there exists
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}\leq C\lVert\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{f}\in H.

(2.7)

The operator
T:=\tilde{T}|_{X}:\,X\to X
is relevant in our analysis because its eigenvalues and those of problem (
2.4
) are reciprocal to each other and the corresponding eigenfunctions are the same. A full description of the spectrum of
T
will then solve problem (
2.4
).

We consider the direct sum decomposition
X=K\oplus K^{\bot}
into closed subspaces

K:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X;\ \mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}=0\ \text{in $\Omega$}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad K^{\bot}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X;\ \left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=0,\ \forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in K\right\},

which are orthogonal with respect to both
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{A}}
and
a(\cdot,\cdot)
. It is clear that
\kappa=1
is an eigenvalue of (
2.4
) with associated eigenspace
K
. Consequently, as
K
is not a finite-dimensional subspace of
X
,
T
is not a compact operator.

Lemma 2.1
.

The orthogonal projection
P
in
X
onto
K^{\bot}
is characterized, for any
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X
, by
P\boldsymbol{\sigma}:=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}})
and
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the unique solution of

\left(\mathcal{A}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}),\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{v})\right)=-\left(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{v}\right),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.8)

Proof.

We first point out that Korn’s inequality provides the stability estimate

\left\lVert\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}\leq C\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

(2.9)

By definition, (
2.9
) also ensures that
\left\lVert P\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{1}\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}
. Moreover,
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits P\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}
by construction, which ensures that
P:\,X\to X
is bounded. Moreover, it is clear that
P\circ P=P
and
\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}\nolimits{P}=K
. It remains to show that the range of
P
coincides with
K^{\bot}
. To this end, we notice that, for any
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X
,

\left(P\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}),\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)=(\nabla\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{\tau})=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in K,

which proves that
P(X)\subset K^{\bot}
. The reciprocal inclusion is a consequence of
K^{\bot}=P(K^{\bot})+(I-P)K^{\bot}=P(K^{\bot})\subset P(X)
, where we used that
(I-P)X\subset K
, and the result follows.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The inclusions
P(X)\hookrightarrow H
and
P(X)\cap T(X)\hookrightarrow X
are compact.

Proof.

Let
\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}\right\}_{n}
be a weakly convergent sequence in
X
. The continuiuty of
P:X\to X
implies that the sequence
\left\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}\right\}_{n}:=\left\{P\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}\right\}_{n}
is also weakly convergent in
X
. By definition,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n})
, where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n}\in H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
solves (
2.8
) with right-hand side
-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}
. It follows from (
2.9
) that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the compactness of the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
implies that
\left\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n}\right\}_{n}
admits a subsequence (denoted again
\left\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{n}\right\}_{n}
) that converges strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Next, we deduce from Green’s identity

\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{q},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{q}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{p}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{q}),\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{q}\right)=-\left(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{p}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{q},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{q})\right),

that
\left\{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}\right\}_{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H
, which implies that the embedding
P(X)\hookrightarrow H
is compact.

Finally, it follows from (
2.7
) that

T(X)\cap P(X)\subset\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in P(X);\ \tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\},

and the compactness of the embedding
T(X)\cap P(X)\hookrightarrow X
is a consequence of the fact that the inclusion
\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in P(X);\ \tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}\subset X
is compact.

We point out that
\tilde{T}
is symmetric with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{A}}
, which implies that
P(X)=K^{\bot}
is
T
-invariant. Consequently, it holds
T(P(X))\subset P(X)\cap T(X)
and Lemma

-symmetric and positive definite operator

.

Theorem 2.1
.

The spectrum
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(T)
of

is given by
\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(T)=\{0,1\}\cup\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

3
Definitions and auxiliary results

We consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h}
of shape-regular simplicial meshes that subdivide the domain
\bar{\Omega}
into simplices
K
of diameter
h_{K}
. The parameter
h:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\{h_{K}\}
represents the mesh size of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We assume that
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is aligned with the partition
\bar{\Omega}=\cup_{j=1}^{J}\bar{\Omega}_{j}
and that
\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega_{j}):=\left\{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h};\ K\subset\Omega_{j}\right\}
is a shape-regular mesh of
\bar{\Omega}_{j}
for all
j=1,\cdots,J
and all
h
.

For all
s\geq 0
, we consider the broken Sobolev space

H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j}):=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega);\ v|_{\Omega_{j}}\in H^{s}(\Omega_{j}),\ \forall j=1,\ldots,J\right\}

corresponding to the partition
\bar{\Omega}=\cup_{j=1}^{J}\bar{\Omega}_{j}
.
Its vectorial and tensorial versions are denoted
H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M})
, respectively. Likewise, the broken Sobolev space with respect to the subdivision of
\bar{\Omega}
into
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is

H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},E):=\left\{\boldsymbol{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega,E):\quad\boldsymbol{v}|_{K}\in H^{s}(K,E)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},\quad\text{for $E\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{M}\}$}.

For each
\boldsymbol{v}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}_{K}\}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}:=\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}\}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
the components
\boldsymbol{v}_{K}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{K}
represent the restrictions
\boldsymbol{v}|_{K}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}|_{K}
. When no confusion arises, the restrictions of these functions will be written without any subscript.

Hereafter, given an integer
m\geq 0
and a domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{P}_{m}(D)
denotes the space of polynomials of degree at most
m
on
D
. We introduce the space

\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}

of piecewise polynomial functions relatively to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We also consider the space
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h},E)
of functions with values in
E
and entries in
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, where
E
is either
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{M}
or
\mathbb{S}
.

Let us introduce now notations related to DG approximations of
H(\text{div})
-type spaces. We say that a closed subset
F\subset\overline{\Omega}
is an interior edge/face if
F
has a positive
(d-1)
-dimensional measure and if there are distinct elements
K
and
K^{\prime}
such that
F=\bar{K}\cap\bar{K}^{\prime}
. A closed subset
F\subset\overline{\Omega}
is a boundary edge/face if there exists

such that
F
is an edge/face of
K
and
F=\bar{K}\cap\Gamma
. We consider the set
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}
of interior edges/faces, the set

Given

For any

If it happens that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{*}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}+\left\lVert\varrho_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}.

It is important to notice that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert_{X}
for all

.

The following discrete trace inequality is useful in our analysis.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{\textup{tr}}>0
independent of
h
and
\varrho
such that

\left\lVert\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}\{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho}}\boldsymbol{v}\}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\leq C_{\textup{tr}}\left\lVert\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho}}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.1)

Proof.

By definition of
\varrho_{\mathcal{F}}
, for any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}h^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{F}}\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\boldsymbol{v}\}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}h_{F}\left\lVert\varrho^{\frac{1}{2}}_{F}\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\boldsymbol{v}\}_{F}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,F}\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}h_{F}\left\lVert\{\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho}}\boldsymbol{v}\}_{F}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,F}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}\left\lVert\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho}}\boldsymbol{v}_{K}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\partial K}.

Applying in the last inequality the well-known estimate (see for example
[
13
]
)

h_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{0,\partial K}\leq C_{\textup{tr}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{0,K}\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(K),

(3.2)

where
C_{\textup{tr}}>0
is independent of
h
, we obtain the result.
∎

For all
\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X^{DG}(h)
and for a large enough given parameter
\mathtt{a}>0
, we consider the symmetric bilinear form

c_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right):=c(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau})+\mathtt{a}\left(\varrho_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rrbracket,\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}-\left(\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\},\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}-\left(\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\},\llbracket\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}

and let

a_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right):=\left(\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)+c_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right).

For all
\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X^{DG}(h)

satisfying
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

,
a straightforward application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\left|a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau})\right|\leq 2{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}.

Moreover, if we take in the last estimate
\boldsymbol{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG}
, we deduce from Lemma
3.1
that,

\left|a_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\right|\leq M\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},

(3.3)

with
M:=2\sqrt{1+C_{\textup{tr}}^{2}}
.

The bilinear form
c_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the DG-norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
are designed in such a way that the coercivity of the bilinear form
a_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
X_{h}^{DG}
can be achieved with a stability parameter
\mathtt{a}
that is independent of the material coefficients, as shown in the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
\mathtt{a}^{*}>0
, independent of
\varrho
and
\mathcal{A}
, such that
if
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}^{*}
, then

c_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\tau})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lVert\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\delta_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\right),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X_{h}^{DG}.

(3.4)

Proof.

By definition, we have

c_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\tau})=\left\lVert\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\mathtt{a}\left\lVert\varrho_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}-2\left(\{\varrho^{-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\},\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}

(3.5)

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Young’s inequality together with the discrete trace inequality (
3.1
) we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}2\left|\left(\{\varrho^{-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\},\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{0}}\right|&\leq 2\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\{\varrho^{-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq 2C_{\textup{tr}}\left\lVert\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\varrho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+2C_{\textup{tr}}^{2}\left\lVert\gamma_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

Combining (
3.6
) and (

) gives the result with

.
∎

The pure–stress DG scheme

We are now in a position to introduce the following mixed DG discretization of (
2.4
): Find
0\neq\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG}
and
\kappa_{h}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

a_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)=\kappa_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X_{h}^{DG}.

(4.1)

Remark 4.1
.

We are only considering via (
4.1
) the symmetric interior penalty DG method (SIP) because the non-symmetric DG versions, known in the literatura as NIP and IIP, have sup-optimal rates of convergence for the eigenvalues
[
1
,
22
]
.

In all what follows, we make the following stability assumption.

Assumption 1
.

The parameter
\mathtt{a}
is greater than or equal to
\mathtt{a}^{*}
:
\mathtt{a}\geq\mathtt{a}^{*}:=2C_{\textup{tr}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}
.

Under this assumption, Proposition permits us to guaranty the well–posedness of the discrete source operator
\tilde{T}_{h}:H\to X_{h}^{DG}
given, for any

, by

a_{h}(\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=\left(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG}.

(4.2)

Actually,
\tilde{T}_{h}
is uniformly bounded, namely,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 2\lVert\boldsymbol{f}\rVert_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{f}\in H.

(4.3)

Similarly to the continuous case, we observe that
(1\neq\kappa_{h},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\in\mathbb{R}\times X_{h}^{DG}
is a solution of problem (
4.1
) if and only if
(\frac{1}{\kappa_{h}},\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})
,
is an eigenpair of

, i.e.,
T_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{h}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
. Moreover, it is clear that
\kappa_{h}=1
is an eigenvalue common to (
4.1
) and
T_{h}
with corresponding eigenspace

K_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG};\ c_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=0\right\}.

(4.4)

The following result establishes a Céa estimate for the DG approximation (
4.2
) of (
2.5
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Under Assumption
1
, for all
\boldsymbol{f}\in H
, it holds

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq(1+2M)\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*},

(4.5)

with
M
as in (
3.3
).

Proof.

We already know from (
2.7
) that
\boldsymbol{u}:=\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})\in H_{D}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, using the integration by parts (
1.1
) elementwise in

a(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau})=\left(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X

gives

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)=-\left(\boldsymbol{u},\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)+\left(\{\boldsymbol{u}\},\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X_{h}^{DG}.

Substituting back
\boldsymbol{u}=\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})
into the last expression we get

\left(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}+\left(\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}),\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)-\left(\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f})\},\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}\rrbracket\right)_{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}=\left(\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X_{h}^{DG}.

Combining the last identity with (

) yields the following consistency property

a_{h}((\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=0\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{f}\in H.

(4.6)

Now, by virtue of (
3.3
), (

) and (

), it holds

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a_{h}(\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=a_{h}(\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq M\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{T}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\tilde{T}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG},

and the result follows from the triangle inequality.

4.1
The operator
\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s}

For technical reasons, we want to consider here the
H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{S})
-conforming finite element space given by
X_{h}^{c}:=\mathcal{P}_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap X
. The goal of this section is to prove that, under certain conditions on the mesh and on the polynomial degree
k
, it holds

\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{c}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{when $h\to 0$},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X.

(4.7)

The main obstacle in performing this task is the symmetry constraint. Let us ignore this constraint and discuss, in a first step, approximation properties of the Brezzi-Douglas-Marini (BDM) mixed finite element discretization of

H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M}):=\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M});\quad\left\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{\phi}\right\rangle_{\Gamma}=0\quad\text{$\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})$,\, $\boldsymbol{\phi}|_{\Gamma_{D}}=\mathbf{0}$}\right\}.

Given
s>1/2
the tensorial version of the canonical BDM finite element interpolant
\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}:H_{N}(\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega,\mathbb{M})\to H_{N}(\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
satisfies the following classical error estimate,
[
7
, Proposition 2.5.4]
,

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}-\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{\min\{s,k+1\}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}\right\rVert_{s,\Omega_{j}}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega,\mathbb{M}),\quad s>1/2,

(4.8)

Moreover, we have the well-known commutativity property,

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}=Q^{k-1}_{h}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega,\mathbb{M}),\quad s>1/2,

(4.9)

where
Q^{k-1}_{h}
stands for the
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, if
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H^{s}(\cup_{j}\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we obtain

\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\tau}-\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau})\rVert_{0,\Omega}=\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}-Q^{k-1}_{h}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{\min\{s,k\}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\rVert_{s,\Omega_{j}}.

(4.10)

We point out that one can actually extend the domain of the canonical interpolation operator
\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}
to
H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap H^{s}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})
, for any
s>0
. In the case of a constant function
\varrho
and a constant tensor
\mathcal{A}
, classical regularity results
[
10
,
17
]
ensure the existence of
\hat{s}\in(0,1]
(depending on
\Omega
on the boundary conditions and on the Lamé coefficients) such that the solution
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
of problem (
2.8
) belongs to
H^{1+s}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H^{1}_{D}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
s\in(0,\hat{s})
. However, our aim here is to avoid relying on regularity results that may be difficult to establish for the elasticity system in the case of general domains, boundary conditions and material properties. For this reason, we resort to the following smoothed projector recently introduced by Licht
[
21
, Theorem 6.3]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

There exists a bounded and linear operator
\mathcal{J}_{h}:\,L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})\to H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
such that

i)

The exists
C>0
independent of
h
such that

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})

ii)

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}=Q_{h}^{k-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}
for all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
.

The operator
\mathcal{J}_{h}
doesn’t preserve symmetric. To remedy this drawback, we follow
[
15
,
18
,
32
]
and use a symmetrisation procedure that requires the stability the Scott-Vogelius element
[
31
]
for the Stokes problem. We refer to
[
14
, Section 55.3]
for a detailed account on the conditions (on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
k
) under which this stability property is guaranteed in 2D and 3D. The analysis that follows from now on is based on the following assumption.

Assumption 2
.

The pair
\left\{\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H_{N}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\right\}
is stable for the Stokes problem on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
: there exists
\beta>0
independent of
h
such that

\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap H_{N}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{(\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\phi_{h})}{\lVert\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\rVert_{1,\Omega}}\geq\beta\lVert\phi_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

(4.11)

Lemma 4.1
.

Under Assumption
2
, there exists a linear operator

\mathcal{S}_{h}:\,\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\to\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap X

such that, for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
,

i)

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=\mathbf{0}
in
\Omega
,

ii)

and

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega},\quad\text{with $C>0$ independent of $h$}.

Proof.

We only sketch the proof given in
[
32
, Lemma 5.2]
and adapt it to our boundary conditions, see also
[
15
,
18
]
. In the case
d=2
, given
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
, it follows from Assumption
2
that there exists
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in H^{1}_{N}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying
\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\tau_{h,21}-\tau_{h,12}
and

\lVert\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\rVert_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\tau_{h,12}-\tau_{h,21}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

(4.12)

We recall that all differential operators are applied row-wise and let
\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+\nabla^{\bot}\,\boldsymbol{w}_{h}
, where
\nabla^{\bot}:=(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1})^{\mathtt{t}}
is the rotated gradient. By construction,
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=\mathbf{0}
and thanks to (
4.12
) it holds
\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}
. Moreover, it is easy to check that
\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=(\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})^{\mathtt{t}}
. It remains to show that
\mathcal{S}_{h}
preserves the boundary condition on
\Gamma_{N}
. This follows from the fact that
\nabla^{\bot}\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\boldsymbol{n}=(\partial_{\tau}w_{h,1},\partial_{\tau}w_{h,2})^{\mathtt{t}}
and the tangential derivatives
\partial_{\tau}w_{h,j}:=\partial_{1}w_{h,j}n_{2}-\partial_{2}w_{h,j}n_{1}
,
j=1,2
, vanish on
\Gamma_{N}
since
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in H^{1}_{N}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This finishes the proof of the result in the two dimensional case.

In the case
d=3
, we let
\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+\nabla\times\boldsymbol{w}_{h}
, with
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\mathbf{z}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}-(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits{\mathbf{z_{h}}})I
, where the tensor
\mathbf{z}_{h}\in H^{1}_{N}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
satisfies
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathbf{z}_{h}=(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h,23}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h,32},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h,31}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h,13},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h,12}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h,21})^{\mathtt{t}}
and
\lVert\mathbf{z}_{h}\rVert_{1,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}
. The existence of
\mathbf{z}_{h}
is ensured by Assumption
2
. In this way, we also have
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=\mathbf{0}
and
\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}^{\mathtt{t}}\rVert_{0,\Omega}
. The proof of the symmetry property
\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=(\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})^{\mathtt{t}}
is a little more involved in this case, as shown in
[
32
, Lemma 5.2]
. Finally, we point out that
(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{w}_{h})\boldsymbol{n}=(\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}^{1}\times\boldsymbol{n}),\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}^{2}\times\boldsymbol{n}),\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits_{\Gamma}(\boldsymbol{w}_{h}^{3}\times\boldsymbol{n}))^{\mathtt{t}}
on
\Gamma
, where
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}^{j}
,
j=1,2,3
stand for the rows of
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}
and
\mathop{\textup{div}}\nolimits_{\Gamma}
represents the divergence operator on the surface
\Gamma
. Taking into account that,
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}|_{\Gamma_{N}}=\mathbf{0}
, we deduce that
\mathcal{S}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})\cap X
for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})\cap H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})
, which finishes the proof of the result in the tree-dimensional case.
∎

We are able to state now the counterpart of Theorem
4.2
for
\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}:=\mathcal{S}_{h}\circ\mathcal{J}_{h}:\,L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{M})\to X_{h}^{c}
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Under Assumption
2
,
\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}:\,L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})\to X_{h}^{c}
satisfies

i)

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq C\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})

with
C
independent of
h
,

ii)

and
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}=Q_{h}^{k-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}
for all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X
.

Proof.

The commuting property for
\mathcal{J}^{s}
follows from the corresponding property for
\mathcal{J}_{h}
and from the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{h}
preserves the divergence of tensors, as stated in Lemma
4.1
i).
In addition, as a consequence the property given by Lemma
4.1
ii), for any
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{S})
, it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}&\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{S}_{h}(\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\\[4.30554pt]
&\lesssim\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}-(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma})^{\mathtt{t}}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega},\end{split}

(4.13)

and the first statement of the Corollary follows from Theorem

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Using the density of smooth functions in
H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})

[

and the interpolation error estimates satisfied by the BDM projector, we deduce from Corollary

that, for all
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X
,

\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{c}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert_{X}\lesssim\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}-Q_{h}^{k-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\to 0

when

).

4.2
The operator
P_{h}

In what follows, the norm of a linear and continuous operator
L:\,V_{1}\to V_{2}
between two Hilbert spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
is denoted
\lVert L\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(V_{1},V_{2})}:=\sup_{v\in V_{1},\lVert v\rVert_{V_{1}}=1}\lVert Lv\rVert_{V_{2}}
.

It is crucial to notice that (under Assumption
1
) Proposition
3.1
provides the following equivalent characterization of
K_{h}

K_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{c};\ \mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=0\ \text{in $\Omega$}\right\}\subset K,

and that its
a(\cdot,\cdot)
-orthogonal complement
K_{h}^{\bot}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\in X_{h}^{c};\ \left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h},\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=0,\ \forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in K_{h}\right\}
is not a subset of
K^{\bot}=P(X)
. Let
P_{h}:X_{h}^{c}\to K_{h}^{\bot}
be the
X
-orthogonal projection in
X_{h}^{c}
onto
K_{h}^{\bot}
. The following result provides an estimate for the operator
(P-P_{h})|_{X^{c}_{h}}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, it holds

\left\lVert P-P_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X^{c}_{h},X)}\leq 2\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h})P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,H)}.

Proof.

Let us first notice that, by definition of
P
and
\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}
, for any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{c}
,

\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{J}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-Q_{h}^{k-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-Q_{h}^{k-1}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\mathbf{0},

which proves that
(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P)X_{h}^{c}\subset K_{h}
. Hence, it follows from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\lVert(P-P_{h})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}&\leq\left\lVert P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}+\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h})P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\\[4.30554pt]
&=\left\lVert P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}+\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h})P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{c},\end{split}

(4.14)

where we took into account that
P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in K_{h}
and

P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in K.

To estimate the first term in the right-hand side of (
4.14
), we take advantage of the inclusion
K_{h}\subset K
to write

\displaystyle\left(P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}=\left(P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h},P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{A}}

and we deduce from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\left\lVert P_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h}P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h})P\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}.

Plugging the last estimate in (
4.14
) gives the result.
∎

5
Spectral correctness of the DG scheme and error estimates

5.1
The main result

Even in the case of conforming Galerkin approximations of eigenproblems, it is well-known
[
6
]
that when the source operator is not compact, a convergent discrete scheme for the source problem doesn’t necessarily provide a correct approximation of the spectrum. A fortiori, in our case, Theorem
4.1
is not enough to prevent (
4.1
) from producing spurious eigenvalues. The procedure introduced in
[
11
,
12
]
to analyse the spectral approximation of non compact operators has been recently adapted in
[
23
, Section 5]
to a DG context (cf. also
[
8
]
). It is shown that the main ingredient to prove the spectral correctness of the method is the uniform convergence of
\tilde{T}_{h}
to
\tilde{T}
with respect to the following
h
–dependent norm,

\lVert\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h}\rVert_{h}:=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{S})}\frac{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{when $h\to 0$}.

(5.1)

We need the following technical result to prove (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Under Assumption
2
, there exists a projector
\mathcal{X}^{s}_{h}:\,X^{DG}_{h}\to X_{h}^{c}
such that

h\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1/2}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG},

(5.2)

with
C>0
independent of
h
.

Proof.

It is proved in
[
24
, Proposition 5.2]
that there exists a projector
\mathcal{X}_{h}:\,X^{DG}_{h}\to H_{N}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits,\Omega,\mathbb{M})\cap\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathbb{M})
such that

h\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{X}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{X}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch\left\lVert h_{\mathcal{F}}^{-1/2}\llbracket\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\rrbracket\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}^{*}_{h}}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG},

(5.3)

By construction of
\mathcal{S}_{h}
, the operator
\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}:=\mathcal{S}_{h}\circ\mathcal{X}_{h}
satisfies
\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{X}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}
. Morover, using property ii) of Lemma
4.1
and reasoning as for estimate (
4.13
) yields

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\lesssim\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}-\mathcal{X}_{h}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}.

It follows that (
5.2
) is a direct consequence of (
5.3
).
∎

We point out that the stability of
\mathcal{X}^{s}_{h}:\,X^{DG}_{h}\to X_{h}^{c}
follows directly from the triangle inequality and (
5.2
), namely,

\left\lVert\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\leq C{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG},

(5.4)

with
C>0
independent of
h
.

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this article.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, it holds

\left\lVert\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h}\right\rVert_{h}\leq C\left(h+\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h})P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,H)}+\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}\right),

with
C
independent of
h
.

Proof.

For any
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X_{h}^{DG}
, we consider the splitting
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=(I-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+P_{h}\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+(I-P_{h})\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}
and exploit the fact that
(I-P_{h})\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in K_{h}\subset K
is in the kernel of
(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})
to obtain

\displaystyle(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}

\displaystyle=(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})P_{h}\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}

\displaystyle=(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})(I-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})(P_{h}-P)\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}+(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})P\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}.

It follows from the triangle inequality, (
2.6
) and (
4.3
) that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 3\left\lVert(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}+3\lVert(P-P_{h})\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})P\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(5.5)

Using (
5.2
), we can bound the first term in the right-hand side of (
5.5
) as follows,

\displaystyle\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\leq a^{+}\lVert(I-\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s})\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(5.6)

For the second term, (
5.4
) and Lemma
4.2
yield

\displaystyle\left\lVert(P-P_{h})\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert P-P_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X^{c}_{h},X)}\left\lVert\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\lesssim\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}^{s}_{h})P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,H)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(5.7)

To bound the third term in the right-hand side of (
5.5
), we begin by applying Céa estimate (
4.5
) to obtain

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h})P\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq(1+2M){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}.

(5.8)

Let us introduce the notation
\boldsymbol{u}:=\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})\in H_{D}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and notice that, by virtue of (
2.7
) and (
5.4
),

\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert_{1,\Omega}\leq C\left\lVert P\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\leq C_{1}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(5.9)

Moreover, taking into account that
\varrho
is piecewise constant and using Corollary
4.1
ii) we can write
\frac{1}{\varrho}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}-\frac{1}{\varrho}Q_{h}^{k-1}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h})=(I-Q_{h}^{k-1})\boldsymbol{u}
and it follows that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{*}=\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{X}+\left\lVert\sqrt{\varrho_{\mathcal{F}}h_{\mathcal{F}}}\{(I-Q_{h}^{k-1})\boldsymbol{u}\}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}}.

(5.10)

Now, from the one hand,

\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\leq\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}\left\lVert\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right\rVert_{X}\lesssim\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

(5.11)

and from the other hand, a classical scaling argument combined with (
5.9
) yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sqrt{h_{\mathcal{F}}}(\boldsymbol{u}-Q_{h}^{k-1}\boldsymbol{u})\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}}\lesssim h|\boldsymbol{u}|_{1,\Omega}\leq Ch{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(5.12)

Using (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) in (
5.8
) gives the estimate

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\mathcal{X}_{h}^{s}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}\leq C\left(h+\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

(5.13)

Finally, plugging (
5.6
), (
5.7
), and (
5.13
) in (
5.5
) gives the result.

∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, it holds

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert\tilde{T}-\tilde{T}_{h}\right\rVert_{h}=0.

Proof.

The pointwise convergence of
I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s}:\,X\to X
to zero (ensured by Corollary
4.1
) and the compactness of
P:\,X\to H
and
TP:\,X\to X
imply that the operators
(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})P:\,X\to H
and
(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP:\,X\to X
are uniformly convergent to zero; namely,

\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,H)}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert(I-\mathcal{J}_{h}^{s})TP\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(X,X)}=0,

and the result follows directly from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

5.2
Spectral correctness and convergence

For the sake of completeness, in the remainder of this section we show (by applying a number of results from
[
23
, Section 5]
) how to exploit property (
5.1
) to derive the correct spectral convergence of (
4.1
). Let us first introduce some notations. For
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in X^{DG}(h)
and
E
and
F
closed subspaces of
X^{DG}(h)
, we set
\delta(\boldsymbol{\sigma},E):=\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in E}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
,
\delta(E,F):=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in E:\,{\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\kern-0.75346pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|\kern-0.75346pt\right|}=1}\delta(\boldsymbol{\sigma},F)
, and
\widehat{\delta}(E,F):=\max\{\delta(E,F),\delta(F,E)\}
, the latter being the so called
gap
between subspaces
E
and
F
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
be an arbitrary compact set with smooth boundary
\partial\Lambda
satisfying
\partial\Lambda\cap\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(T)=\emptyset
. We assume that there are
m
eigenvalues
\eta^{\Lambda}_{1},\ldots,\eta^{\Lambda}_{m}
of
T
(repeated according to their algebraic multiplicities) inside
\partial\Lambda
. The following result shows that the resolvent
\left(zI-\tilde{T}\right)^{-1}:X^{DG}(h)\longrightarrow X^{DG}(h)
is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
and
z\in\partial\Lambda
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
h
such that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(zI-\tilde{T})\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\geq C\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X^{DG}(h),

for all
z\in\partial\Lambda
.

Proof.

See
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]

∎

We deduce from Lemma
5.2
that the operator
\mathcal{E}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Lambda}\left(zI-\tilde{T}\right)^{-1}\,dz:X^{DG}(h)\longrightarrow X^{DG}(h)
is well-defined and bounded uniformly in
h
. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}|_{X}:\,X\to X
is a projector onto the finite dimensional space
\mathcal{E}(X)
spanned by the generalized eigenfunctions associated with the finite set of eigenvalues of
T
contained in
\Lambda
. Actually, it is easy to check that
\tilde{T}:X^{DG}(h)\to X^{DG}(h)
and
T:X\to X
have the same eigenvalues in
\Lambda
and that
\mathcal{E}(X^{DG}(h))=\mathcal{E}(X)
.

The next step consists in combining Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.1
to deduce that the discrete resolvent
\left(zI-\tilde{T}_{h}\right)^{-1}:X^{DG}(h)\longrightarrow X^{DG}(h)
is also uniformly bounded, provided
h
is small enough, cf.
[
23
, Lemma 5.1]
for more details.

Lemma 5.3
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, there exists
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h\leq h_{0}
,

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(zI-\tilde{T}_{h})\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\geq C\,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\tau}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\quad\text{for all $\boldsymbol{\tau}\in X^{DG}(h)$ and $z\in\partial\Lambda$}

with
C>0
independent of
h
.

Here again, it follows from Lemma
5.3
that, for
h
small enough, the linear operator

\mathcal{E}_{h}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Lambda}\left(zI-\tilde{T}_{h}\right)^{-1}\,dz:X^{DG}(h)\longrightarrow X^{DG}(h)

is uniformly bounded in
h
. Likewise,
\mathcal{E}_{h}|_{X^{DG}_{h}}:X^{DG}_{h}\to X^{DG}_{h}
is a projector onto the
\tilde{T}_{h}
-invariant subspace
\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}(h))=\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}_{h})
corresponding to the eigenvalues of
T_{h}:\,X^{DG}_{h}\to X^{DG}_{h}
contained in
\Lambda
.

The approximation properties of the eigenfunctions of problem (
2.4
) by means of those of problem (
4.1
) are obtained as a consequence of the following estimate of the distance between
\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}_{h})
and
\mathcal{E}(X)
, measured in terms of the gap
\widehat{\delta}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, there exists
h_{0}>0
such that for all
h\leq h_{0}
,

\widehat{\delta}(\mathcal{E}(X),\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}_{h}))\leq C\left(\lVert\tilde{T}-T_{h}\rVert_{h}+\delta(\mathcal{E}(X),X^{DG}_{h})\right),

(5.14)

with
C>0
independent of
h
.

Proof.

See
[
23
, Theorem 5.1]
.
∎

We point out that since
\mathcal{E}(X)
is a finite dimensional subspace of
X
, Remark
4.2
and Theorem
5.1
ensure the convergence of
\widehat{\delta}(\mathcal{E}(X),\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}_{h}))
to zero when
h\to 0
. This is the main ingredient in the proof of the following Theorem, cf.
[
23
, Theorem 5.2]
for more details.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,1\}
be an arbitrary compact set with smooth boundary
\partial\Lambda
satisfying
\partial\Lambda\cap\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(T)=\emptyset
. We assume that there are
m
eigenvalues
\eta^{\Lambda}_{1},\ldots,\eta^{\Lambda}_{m}
of
T
(repeated according to their algebraic multiplicities) contained in
\Lambda
. We also consider the eigenvalues
\eta^{\Lambda}_{1,h},\ldots,\eta^{\Lambda}_{m(h),h}
of
T_{h}:\,X^{DG}_{h}\to X^{DG}_{h}
lying in
\Lambda
and repeated according to their algebraic multiplicities. Then, there exists
h_{0}>0
such that
m(h)=m
for all
h\leq h_{0}
and

\lim_{h\to 0}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|\eta_{i}^{\Lambda}-\eta^{\Lambda}_{i,h}|=0.

Moreover, if
\mathcal{E}(X)
is the
T
-invariant subspace of
X
spanned by the generalized eigenfunctions corresponding to the set of eigenvalues
\{\eta^{\Lambda}_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,m\}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}_{h})
is the
T_{h}
-invariant subspace of
X^{DG}_{h}
spanned by the eigenspaces corresponding to
\{\eta^{\Lambda}_{i,h},\ i=1,\ldots,m\}
then
\widehat{\delta}(\mathcal{E}(X),\mathcal{E}_{h}(X^{DG}_{h}))\to 0
as
h\to 0
.

5.3
Error estimates for eigenvalues and eigenfunctions

Theorem
5.3
guaranties that the discontinuous Galerkin scheme (
4.1
) does not pollute the spectrum of
T
with spurious modes. Moreover, it proves the convergence of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions with correct multiplicity. However, in practice the space
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X)
of generalized eigenfunctions corresponding to a given isolated eigenvalue
\eta\neq 1
enjoys individual smoothness properties and the term
\lVert\tilde{T}-T_{h}\rVert_{h}
in (
5.14
) prevents from taking advantage of this specific regularity. For this reason, we are going to show now that the gap between the continuous and discrete eigenspaces corresponding to a particular eigenvalue
\eta\neq 1
can be bounded only in terms of

\delta^{*}(\mathcal{E}(X),X^{DG}_{h}):=\sup_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{E}(X),\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rVert_{X}=1}\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X^{DG}_{h}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}.

Hence, hereafter we focus on a particular isolated eigenvalue
\eta\neq 1
of
T
of algebraic multiplicity
m
and let
D_{\eta}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a closed disk centered at
\eta
with boundary
\gamma
such that
D_{\eta}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{sp}}\nolimits(T)=\{\eta\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\left(zI-\tilde{T}\right)^{-1}\,dz:X\rightarrow X
the projector onto the eigenspace
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X)
of
\eta
and we define, for
h
small enough, the projector by

\mathcal{E}_{\eta,h}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}\left(zI-\tilde{T}_{h}\right)^{-1}\,dz:X^{DG}_{h}\rightarrow X^{DG}_{h}
onto the
T_{h}
-invariant subspace
\mathcal{E}_{\eta,h}(X^{DG}_{h})
corresponding to the
m
eigenvalues of
T_{h}:\,X^{DG}_{h}\to X^{DG}_{h}
contained in
\gamma
. A straightforward adaptation of
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
gives the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. For
h
small enough, there exists a constant
C
independent of
h
such that

\widehat{\delta}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X),\mathcal{E}_{\eta,h}(X^{DG}_{h})\big)\leq C\delta^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X),X^{DG}_{h}).

(5.15)

We conclude with the following rates of convergence for eigenfunctions and eigenvalues.

Theorem 5.5
.

Assume that Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied. Let
r>0
be such that
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X)\subset\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H^{r}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{M});\ \mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\tau}\in H^{1+r}(\cup_{j}\Omega_{j},\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}
. Then, there exists
C>0
independent of
h
such that, for
h
small enough,

\widehat{\delta}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X),\mathcal{E}_{\eta,h}(X^{DG}_{h})\big)\leq Ch^{\min\{r,k\}}.

(5.16)

Moreover, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
independent of
h
such that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|\kappa-\kappa_{i,h}|\leq C^{\prime}\,h^{2\min\{r,k\}},

(5.17)

where
\kappa:=1/\eta
and
\kappa_{i,h}:=1/\eta_{i,h}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

For any
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X)
, taking into account (
4.9
), it holds

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X^{DG}_{h}}

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}

\displaystyle=\left(\left\lVert(I-\Pi^{\texttt{BDM}}_{h})\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{A}}+\left\lVert\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\varrho}}(I-Q_{h}^{k-1})\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\varrho_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\{\tfrac{1}{\varrho}(I-Q_{h}^{k-1})\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{*}}\right)^{1/2}.

Using (
4.8
), (
4.10
) for the first and second terms of the last identity, respectively, and employing classical scaling arguments for the last one we deduce that

\inf_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\in X^{DG}_{h}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}\lesssim h^{\min\{r,k\}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r,\Omega_{j}}+\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right\rVert^{2}_{r,\Omega_{j}}\right)^{1/2},

and (
5.16
) follows from the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X)
is a finite dimensional subspace of
X
.

The deduction of (
5.17
) from (
5.16
) is obtained in the classical way, see
[
23
, Theorem 6.2]
for more details.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

The displacement field corresponding to a given
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(X)
is
\boldsymbol{u}=-\frac{1}{\varrho(\kappa-1)}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\sigma}
. Let us associate to
\boldsymbol{u}
the discrete displacement
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}:=-\frac{1}{\varrho(\kappa_{h}-1)}\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}
, where
\kappa_{h}:=(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\kappa_{i,h})/m
. By virtue of the triangle inequality and Theorem
5.5
, for
h
small enough, we have the error estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\leq\frac{1}{\varrho^{-}}\Big(\frac{1}{\kappa-1}\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h})\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left|\frac{1}{\kappa-1}-\frac{1}{\kappa_{h}-1}\right|\left\lVert\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits_{h}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}\Big)\lesssim h^{\min\{r,k\}}.

6
Numerical results

We point out that the inf-sup condition (
4.11
) corresponding to the Scott–Vogelius element is only known to be satisfied under certain conditions on the mesh and the polynomial degree
k
. Namely, in two dimensions, Assumption
2
holds true on shape-regular triangulations with no singular vertices for
k\geq 3
, cf.
[
31
]
and in dimendion three, it is satisfied on uniform simplicial meshes for
k\geq 5

[
36
]
. For lower values
1\leq k<2d-1
of the polynomial degree, we can ensure Assumption
2
by considering shape-regular meshes with barycentric refinements (Alfeld splits), see
[
30
,
34
]
. Similar results have been proved for meshes of Powell–Sabin type
[
35
,
37
]
, which wil not be employed here.

To our knowledge, the stability results mentioned so far for the Scott–Vogelius element have only be obtained for homogeneous Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions on
\Gamma
. However, there is numerical evidence that the stability and optimal accuracy of this finite element method also occur on barycentric refinements of shape-regular meshes when mixed boundary conditions are imposed
[
29
,
16
]
.

In what follows, we say that
\mathcal{T}^{\mathtt{bary}}_{h}
is a simplicial barycentric (
d+1
)-sected mesh of size
h
if
\mathcal{T}^{\mathtt{bary}}_{h}
is obtained after refinement of a shape-regular simplicial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of size
h
by subdividing each simplex in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
into
d+1
sub-simplices by connecting the barycenter with the
d+1
vertices, see Figure
6.1
.

The numerical results presented in this section have been implemented using the finite element library
Netgen/NGSolve

[
28
]
.

Remark 6.1
.

In principle, for
k=2
, we need to perform a barycentric refinement of the mesh
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
to ensure Assumption
2
. However, the optimal order of convergence reported in Table
6.9
in the quadratic case seems to confirm the results obtained in Table
6.4
. In other words, our numerical tests suggest that, in the two-dimensional case, the DG scheme (
4.1
) of quadratic order provides a spectrally correct approximation of (
2.4
) with optimal rates of convergence for the eigenvalues on shape-regular triangulations. This statement is not supported by our theory since (
4.11
) is not known to be satisfied for

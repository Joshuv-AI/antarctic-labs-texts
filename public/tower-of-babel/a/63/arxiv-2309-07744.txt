Random Turán and counting results for general position sets over finite fields
Yaobin Chen, Xizhi Liu, Jiaxi Nie, Ji Zeng

Abstract
Let α ⁡ ( 𝔽 q d , p ) \alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p) denote the maximum size of a general position set in a p p -random subset of 𝔽 q d \mathbb{F}_{q}^{d} . We determine the order of magnitude of α ⁡ ( 𝔽 q 2 , p ) \alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p) up to polylogarithmic factors for all possible values of p p , improving the previous results obtained by Roche-Newton–Warren and Bhowmick–Roche-Newton. For d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 we prove upper bounds for α ⁡ ( 𝔽 q d , p ) \alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p) that are essentially tight within certain ranges for p p . We establish the upper bound 2 ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ q 2^{(1+o(1))q} for the number of general position sets in 𝔽 q d \mathbb{F}_{q}^{d} , which matches the trivial lower bound 2 q 2^{q} asymptotically in the exponent. We also refine this counting result by proving an asymptotically tight (in the exponent) upper bound for the number of general position sets with a fixed size. The latter result for d = 2 d=2 improves a result of Roche-Newton–Warren. Our proofs are grounded in the hypergraph container method, and additionally, for d = 2 d=2 we also leverage the pseudorandomness of the point-line incidence graph of 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q}^{2} .



1
Introduction

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\mathbb{F}
be a field. A point set
P
in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
is in
general position
if no
d+1
points of
P
are contained in a hyperplane, where a
hyperplane
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{F}^{d}
. There are several central problems closely related to general position sets in extremal combinatorics and discrete geometry. For example, the famous
no-three-in-line problem
raised by Dudeney
[
8
]
in 1917 asks if there exists a general position set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that contains
2n
points from the grid
[n]\times[n]
. This question and its variations have been extensively studied for a long time, see
[
15
,
11
,
24
,
19
,
32
]
for some latest results. A closely related problem posted by Erdős
[
10
]
asks for the maximum size of a general position set contained in an arbitrary point set of size
n
without collinear quadruples in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In 2018, a breakthrough result by Balogh and Solymosi
[
3
]
using the hypergraph container method shows that
n^{5/6+o(1)}
is an upper bound for Erdős’ question.

In the present work, we focus on extremal problems related to general position sets in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the finite field whose cardinality is the prime power
q
. More specifically, we study the maximum size of a general position set contained in a
p
-random subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and the number of general position sets in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Our results are presented in the following two subsections.

1.1
Random Turán results

The random Turán-type problem is a well-studied branch in extremal and probabilistic combinatorics concerning the maximum value of certain parameters in random structures. For example, see
[
17
,
7
,
28
,
16
,
30
,
20
,
22
,
21
]
for some results of this flavor. In this subsection, we study random Turán-type problems for general position sets in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
.

Given a real number
p\in[0,1]
, we use
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)
to denote the maximum size of a general position set that is contained in a
p
-random set

\mathbf{S}_{p}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Here, by “
p
-random” we mean that every point of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is sampled into
\mathbf{S}_{p}
independently with probability
p
.

Note that determining
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},1)
is equivalent to answering the following fundamental question: How large can a general position set in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
be? Erdős
[
26
]
observed that the
moment curve
, which consists of all points
(x,x^{2},\dots,x^{d})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, is in general position. Hence we have the lower bound
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},1)\geq q
. On the other hand, since
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is a disjoint union of
q
hyperplanes and a general position set can contain at most
d
points from each of them, we obtain the upper bound
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},1)\leq dq
. In fact, it follows from a more involved argument that
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},1)\leq(1+o(1))q
, see e.g. our Lemma
4.1
. Overall, we have
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},1)=(1+o(1))q
.

For
d=2
and
p\neq 1
, Roche-Newton–Warren
[
25
]
and Bhowmick–Roche-Newton
[
5
]
established essentially tight bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p)
when
p
lies in the intervals
[0,q^{-1}]
and
[q^{-1/3},1]
respectively. We improve their results by determining the order of magnitude of
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p)
up to polylogarithmic factors for all possible values of
p
(see Figure
1
).

Figure 1:
The behaviour of
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p)
in terms of
p
.

Theorem 1.1
.

As the prime power
q\to\infty
, asymptotically almost surely, we have

\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Theta(pq^{2}),~~~&q^{-2+o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-3/2-o(1)},\\
&q^{1/2+o(1)},~~~&q^{-3/2-o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-1/2+o(1)},\\
&\Theta(pq),~~~&q^{-1/2+o(1)}\leq p\leq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, all
q^{o(1)}
factors here are polylogarithmic.

Similar to the previous works
[
25
,
5
]
, our proof is grounded in the hypergraph container method. The novelty in our argument is a balanced supersaturation result for collinear triples in reasonably large subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, where the “balanced” property is achieved by leveraging the pseudorandomness of the point-line incidence graph of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
.

In higher dimensions, there are new difficulties arising in determining
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)
for all
p
(see our remark in Section
6
). Nevertheless, we extend the results in
[
25
,
5
]
to every dimension
d\geq 3
by establishing essentially tight bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)
when
p
lies in certain intervals (see Figure
2
). Our proof utilizes a notion of “critical coplanarity” (see Lemma
5.1
) to partially overcome the technical challenges that arise only in higher dimensions.

Figure 2:
Lower and upper bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)
in terms of
p
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
be a fixed integer. As the prime power
q\to\infty
, asymptotically almost surely, we have

\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Theta(pq^{d}),~~&q^{-d+o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-d+\frac{1}{d}-o(1)},\\
&q^{\frac{1}{d}+o(1)},~~&q^{-d+\frac{1}{d}-o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-d+\frac{2}{d}+o(1)},\\
&\Theta(pq),~~&q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}+o(1)}\leq p\leq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, when
q^{-d+\frac{2}{d}+o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}+o(1)}
, we have

\max\left\{q^{\frac{1}{d}-o(1)},~\Omega(pq)\right\}\leq\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)\leq\min\left\{p^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{d}{2}+o(1)},~q^{\frac{d}{d+1}+o(1)}\right\}.

Moreover, all
q^{o(1)}
factors here are polylogarithmic.

Note that Theorem
1.2
determines the asymptotic value of
\log(\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p))
when
p
lies in the intervals
[q^{-d},q^{-d+2/d+o(1)}]
and
[q^{-1/(d+1)+o(1)},1]
, which leaves a gap between the lower and upper bounds when
p
lies in the interval
[q^{-d+2/d+o(1)},q^{-1/(d+1)+o(1)}]
. It is an interesting problem to close this gap.

1.2
Counting results

In this subsection, we focus on the following two counting problems: How many general position sets does
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
contain? And how many of them are of a fixed size
m
? Using the moment curve defined in the previous subsection, one can easily obtain the lower bounds
2^{q}
and
\binom{q}{m}
to these questions respectively.

For the upper bound, Bhowmick–Roche-Newton
[
5
]
proved that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
contains at most
2^{(1+o(1))q}
general position sets. Note that the exponents of their upper bound and the lower bound
2^{q}
match asymptotically. The following theorem extends this result to all dimensions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a fixed integer and the prime power
q
tend to infinity. The number of general position sets in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is at most
2^{q+q^{\frac{2d}{2d+1}+o(1)}}
.

For counting general position sets with a fixed size, Bhowmick–Roche-Newton
[
5
]
proved that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
contains at most
\binom{(1+o(1))q}{m}
general position sets of size
m
for
m\in[q^{2/3+o(1)},q]
, and this matches the lower bound
\binom{q}{m}
asymptotically in the exponent. We also extend this result to all dimensions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a fixed integer and the prime power
q
tend to infinity. For every integer
m\geq q^{d/(d+1)}(\log q)^{3}
, the number of general position sets of size
m
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is at most
\binom{\left(1+o(1)\right)q}{m}
.

For
m
much smaller than
q^{d/(d+1)}
, the upper and lower bounds remain widely far. In this direction, Roche-Newton–Warren
[
25
]
proved that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
contains at most
\binom{q^{2+o(1)}/m}{m}
many general position sets of size
m
for
m\geq q^{1/2+o(1)}
. The following theorem greatly improves this result.

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for sufficiently large prime power
q
and every integer
m\geq q^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}
, the number of general position sets of size
m
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
is at most
\binom{cq}{m}
.

It is known that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
contains at least
\binom{q^{2-o(1)}}{m}
many general position sets of size
m
for
m\ll q^{1/2}
(see Theorem 1 of
[
25
]
). Hence, one cannot hope for a polynomial relaxation of the condition “
m\geq q^{1/2+o(1)}
” in Theorem
1.5
.

Our proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
generalize those in
[
5
]
while overcoming some technical challenges that arise only in higher dimensions (mostly in upper bounding codegrees during the construction of containers). On the other hand, our argument for Theorem
1.5
crucially relies on the pseudorandomness of the point-line incidence graph of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
just like Theorem
1.1
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some useful preliminary results, especially the hypergraph container lemma. In Section
3
, we utilize the pseudorandomness of the point-line incidence graph of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
to prove Theorem
1.1
. Section
4
is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, and
1.5
. In Section
5
, we use a supersaturation result for critical coplanar tuples to establish Theorem
1.2
. Finally, we include some remarks in Section
6
.

Throughout the paper, the asymptotic notations are always understood with the prime power
q
tending to infinity. We say that a sequence of events
\textbf{A}_{q}
happens
asymptotically almost surely
(a.a.s.) if
\text{Pr}[\textbf{A}_{q}]\rightarrow 1
as
q\rightarrow\infty
. We omit floors and ceilings whenever they are not crucial for the sake of clarity in our presentation. The functions
\exp(x)
and
\log(x)
are both in base
2
. We only consider unordered tuples of points in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, hence a “
k
-tuple” is equivalent to a point set of size
k
.

2
Preliminaries

We present some preliminary results in this section. Within our proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we shall make free use of Markov’s inequality and Chernoff’s bound (see e.g. Theorem 22.6 in
[
12
]
). For convenience, we call a
k
-dimensional affine subspace in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
a
k
-flat
. The following counting result for the number of
k
-flats in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
will be useful for us.

Lemma 2.1

(Goldman–Rota
[
13
]
)
.

The number of
k
-flats in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is
q^{d-k}\binom{d}{k}_{q}
, and
the number of
k
-flats in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
containing a fixed point is
\binom{d}{k}_{q}
, where

\binom{d}{k}_{q}:=\frac{(1-q^{d})(1-q^{d-1})\cdots(1-q^{d-k+1})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{k})}=(1+o(1))q^{k(d-k)}.

One of the key tools in our proofs will be the following well-known hypergraph container lemma, proved independently by Balogh–Morris–Samotij
[
2
]
and Saxton–Thomason
[
27
]
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer. An
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of
r
-subsets (called edges) of some finite ground set
V
, which is called the vertex set of
\mathcal{H}
. We identify an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
with its edge set and use
V(\mathcal{H})
to denote its vertex set. A subset of
V(\mathcal{H})
is said to be an
independent set
if it contains no edges of
\mathcal{H}
. Given a subset
U\subset V(\mathcal{H})
, the
degree

\delta(U)
of
U
is the number of edges in
\mathcal{H}
containing
U
. For
i\in[r]
, let
\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{i}
be the collection of all
i
-subsets of
V(\mathcal{H})
and

\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H}):=\max\left\{\delta(U):~U\in\binom{V(\mathcal{H})}{i}\right\},

and for every real number
\tau>0
, define

\Delta(\mathcal{H},\tau):=\frac{2^{\binom{r}{2}-1}|V(\mathcal{H})|}{r|\mathcal{H}|}\sum_{i=2}^{r}\frac{\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H})}{2^{\binom{i-1}{2}}\tau^{i-1}}.

Lemma 2.2

(Balogh–Morris–Samotij
[
2
]
, Saxton–Thomason
[
27
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-graph and
\varepsilon,\tau\in(0,1/2)
be real numbers.
Suppose that
\tau<1/(200\cdot r\cdot r!)
and
\Delta(\mathcal{H},\tau)\leq\varepsilon/(12\cdot r!)
, then there exists a collection
\mathcal{C}
of subsets (called containers) of
V(\mathcal{H})
such that

(i)

every independent set of
\mathcal{H}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}
,

(ii)

\log|\mathcal{C}|\leq 1000\cdot r\cdot(r!)^{3}\cdot|V(\mathcal{H})|\cdot\tau\cdot\log(1/\varepsilon)\cdot\log(1/\tau)
, and

(iii)

for every
C\in\mathcal{C}
the induced subgraph
\mathcal{H}[C]
has size at most
\varepsilon|\mathcal{H}|
.

3
Random Turán results in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}

We aim to prove Theorem
1.1
in this section.

3.1
Pseudorandomness and balanced supersaturation in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}

In this subsection, we use the pseudorandomness of the point-line incidence graph of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
to establish a balanced supersaturation result for collinear triples in large point sets.

Recall that the
adjacency matrix

A_{G}
of an
n
-vertex graph
G
is the
n\times n
symmetric matrix where

A_{G}(i,j):=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }\{i,j\}\in E(G),\\
0,&{\rm otherwise.}\end{cases}

We use
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}(G)
to denote the eigenvalues of
A_{G}
. It is well-known that if
G
is bipartite, then
\lambda_{i}(G)=-\lambda_{n-i+1}(G)
holds for all
i\in[n]
. A bipartite graph
G
with bipartition
V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
is said to be
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
-regular
if the degree
\delta_{G}(v)=\delta_{i}
for all
v\in V_{i}
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. A simple linear algebra argument tells us that every
n
-vertex
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
-regular bipartite graph
G
satisfies
\lambda_{1}(G)=-\lambda_{n}(G)=\sqrt{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}
.

The seminal expander mixing lemma
[
1
,
14
,
18
]
relates the edge distribution of a graph with its second largest eigenvalue. One of the key tools in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following bipartite extension of the expander mixing lemma (see e.g. Lemma 3.7 in
[
6
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
-regular bipartite graph with bipartition
V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
.
Then for every
S\subset V_{1}
and
T\subset V_{2}
, the number of edges between
S
and
T
, denoted by
e(S,T)
, satisfies

\left|e(S,T)-\frac{\delta_{2}}{|V_{1}|}|S||T|\right|\leq\lambda_{2}(G)\sqrt{|S||T|}.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}
denote the collection of all hyperplanes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. We define the
point-hyperplane incidence graph
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
as the bipartite graph
I_{q,d}
with bipartition
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\sqcup\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}
such that for every
p\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\pi\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}
, the pair
\{p,\pi\}
is an edge if and only if
p\in\pi
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The bipartite graph
I_{q,d}
is
\left(\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1},q^{d-1}\right)
-regular with
\lambda_{2}(I_{q,d})=q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.1
,
|\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}|=\frac{q(q^{d}-1)}{q-1}
, and
I_{q,d}
is
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
-regular with
\delta_{1}=\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}
and
\delta_{2}=q^{d-1}
. Hence it suffices to show that
\lambda_{2}(I_{q,d})=q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
. To prove this, we will show

A^{3}=(\delta_{1}-1)q^{d-2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&J\\
\hline\cr J^{t}&0\end{array}\right]+\delta_{2}A,

(3.1)

where
A
is the adjacency matrix of
I_{q,d}
and
J
is the
|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}|\times|\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}|
all-one matrix. Given this identity, it follows from a simple linear algebra argument that every eigenvalue of
A
except for
\pm\sqrt{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}
satisfies the equation
x^{3}=\delta_{2}x
. In particular,
\lambda_{2}(I_{q,d})=\sqrt{\delta_{2}}=q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
as claimed.

To prove (
3.1
), observe that the
(u,v)
-entry of
A^{3}
equals the number of length-
3
walks from
u
to
v
. Therefore,
A^{3}(u,v)=0
if
u,v
are both in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
or
\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}
. Since
A
is a symmetric matrix, we can assume that
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
v\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}
. Let
uv^{\prime}u^{\prime}v
be a length-
3
walk from
u
to
v
in
I_{q,d}
. If
\{u,v\}\not\in E(I_{q,d})
, then there are
\delta_{1}-1
choices for
v^{\prime}
, and for each chosen
v^{\prime}
there are
q^{d-2}
choices for
u^{\prime}
(since the intersection of two hyperplanes is either empty or a
(d-2)
-flat). If
\{u,v\}\in E(I_{q,d})
, then in addition to the above
(\delta_{1}-1)q^{d-2}
choices, we can also choose
v^{\prime}=v
and in this case there are
\delta_{2}
more choices for
u^{\prime}
(i.e. the neighbors of
v
). Hence we conclude the proof.
∎

Using Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we obtain the following crucial property of the point-hyperplane incidence relation of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any point set
P
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, we have

\left|\left\{\pi\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}:~|\pi\cap P|\not\in\left[\frac{|P|}{2q},\frac{2|P|}{q}\right]\right\}\right|\leq\frac{8q^{d+1}}{|P|}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{<}=\left\{\pi\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}:~|\pi\cap P|<{|P|}/{(2q)}\right\}
and
\mathbb{H}^{>}=\left\{\pi\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{d}:~|\pi\cap P|>{2|P|}/{q}\right\}
. Applying Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
to
I_{q,d}
with
S=P
and
T=\mathbb{H}^{<}
, we obtain

e\left(P,\mathbb{H}^{<}\right)\geq\frac{\delta_{2}}{|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}|}|P||\mathbb{H}^{<}|-\lambda_{2}(I_{q,d})\sqrt{|P||\mathbb{H}^{<}|}=\frac{|P||\mathbb{H}^{<}|}{q}-q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{|P||\mathbb{H}^{<}|}.

Notice that
e\left(P,\mathbb{H}^{<}\right)\leq|\mathbb{H}^{<}||P|/{(2q)}
. Combining these two inequalities we can solve

\frac{|P||\mathbb{H}^{<}|}{2q}\leq q^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{|P||\mathbb{H}^{<}|},

which implies that
|\mathbb{H}^{<}|\leq{4q^{d+1}}/{|P|}
. Similarly, we can show that
|\mathbb{H}^{>}|\leq{4q^{d+1}}/{|P|}
, hence we conclude the proof.
∎

Our balanced supersaturation result for collinear triples is a corollary of Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any point set
P
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, there is a collection
\mathcal{S}
of collinear triples in
P
such that

(i)

|\mathcal{S}|\geq\left(q(q+1)-8q^{3}/|P|\right)\binom{|P|/(2q)}{3}
,

(ii)

every point
v\in P
is contained in at most
(q+1)\binom{2|P|/q}{2}
elements in
\mathcal{S}
, and

(iii)

every pair
\{u,v\}\subset P
is contained in at most
2|P|/q
elements in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Let

L=\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{2}:~|\ell\cap P|\in\left[\frac{|P|}{2q},\frac{2|P|}{q}\right]\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{\ell\in L}\binom{\ell\cap P}{3}.

According to Lemmas
2.1
and
3.3
, we have
|L|\geq q(q+1)-8q^{3}/|P|
. By the definition of
L
, every line
\ell\in L
contains at least
|P|/(2q)
points in
P
, so we obtain

|\mathcal{S}|\geq\left(q(q+1)-\frac{8q^{3}}{|P|}\right)\binom{|P|/(2q)}{3},

and this concludes the proof of (i).

To prove (ii), notice that every point
v\in P
is contained in at most
q+1
lines in
L
, and moreover, there are at most
\binom{2|P|/q-1}{2}
collinear triples in
\mathcal{S}
containing
v
in each line
\ell\in L
. So
v
is contained in at most
(q+1)\binom{2|P|/q}{2}
elements in
\mathcal{S}
.

Finally, let
\ell_{uv}
be the unique line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
determined by a pair
\{u,v\}\subset P
. If
\ell_{uv}\not\in L
, then no element in
\mathcal{S}
contains
\{u,v\}
. If
\ell_{uv}\in L
, then by the definition of
L
, there are at most
2|P|/q-2
elements in
\mathcal{S}
containing
\{u,v\}
. This proves (iii).
∎

3.2
The first container theorem

In this subsection, we prove the following container theorem for general position sets in
\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
using Lemma
3.4
.

Theorem 3.5
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for sufficiently large prime power
q
, we have a collection
\mathcal{C}
of point sets (called containers) in
\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
satisfying

(i)

every general position set of
\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}
,

(ii)

|C|\leq 9q
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}
, and

(iii)

|\mathcal{C}|\leq\exp\left(c\cdot q^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}\right)
.

Our proof will be an iterative application of Lemmas
2.2
and
3.4
. We start with the container collection
\mathcal{C}
consisting of the space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
alone. We repeatedly apply Lemma
3.4
with each large member
C\in\mathcal{C}
to obtain a triple system
\mathcal{S}_{C}
, then apply Lemma
2.2
with
\mathcal{S}_{C}
to obtain a new collection of containers
\mathcal{C}_{C}
, and replace
C
in
\mathcal{C}
with all members in
\mathcal{C}_{C}
. This iteration process ends when every member in
\mathcal{C}
becomes small, and we can verify that the final collection satisfies the claimed properties.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

We set
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\}
and
\varepsilon=1/e
. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{j}
has been defined for some integer
j\geq 0
. Let

\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\left\{C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}:~|C|>9q\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\leq}=\left\{C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}:~|C|\leq 9q\right\}.

If
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\emptyset
, then we let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{j}
. Otherwise, for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
we set

k_{C}={|C|}/{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{C}=10^{8}\cdot k_{C}^{-1}q^{-1/2}.

We can apply Lemma
3.4
with
P=C
and obtain a collection
\mathcal{S}_{C}
of collinear triples in
C
satisfying

•

|\mathcal{S}_{C}|\geq\left(q(q+1)-8q^{3}/|C|\right)\binom{|C|/(2q)}{3}\geq 10^{-3}k_{C}^{3}q^{2}
,

•

\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{C})\leq(q+1)\binom{2|C|/q}{2}\leq 4k_{C}^{2}q
, and

•

\Delta_{2}(\mathcal{S}_{C})\leq 2|P|/q=2k_{C}
.

Here, the fact
|C|>9q
is used in the first property. As a consequence, we can compute

\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{S}_{C},\tau_{C})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\binom{3}{2}-1}|C|}{3|\mathcal{S}_{C}|}\left(\frac{\Delta_{2}(\mathcal{S}_{C})}{\tau_{C}}+\frac{\Delta_{3}(\mathcal{S}_{C})}{2\tau_{C}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4k_{C}q}{3\cdot 10^{-3}k_{C}^{3}q^{2}}\left(\frac{2k_{C}}{10^{8}k_{C}^{-1}q^{-1/2}}+\frac{1}{2\left(10^{8}k_{C}^{-1}q^{-1/2}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3\cdot 10^{5}q^{1/2}}+\frac{4}{6\cdot 10^{13}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{12\cdot 3!}.

Additionally, since
q
is sufficiently large, we have
\tau_{C}\leq\frac{1}{200\cdot 3\cdot 3!}
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
, applied to
\mathcal{S}_{C}
, that there exists a collection
\mathcal{C}_{C}
of subsets of
C
such that

(a)

every independent set of
\mathcal{S}_{C}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}_{C}
,

(b)

the size of
\mathcal{C}_{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{C}|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(1000\cdot 3\cdot(3!)^{3}\cdot|C|\cdot\tau_{C}\cdot\log(1/\varepsilon)\cdot\log(1/\tau_{C})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(10^{18}\cdot k_{C}q\cdot k_{C}^{-1}q^{-1/2}\cdot\log e\cdot\log\left(k_{C}q^{1/2}/10^{8}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(10^{18}\cdot q^{1/2}\log q\right),~\text{and}

(c)

for every
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{C}
the induced subgraph
\mathcal{S}_{C}[C^{\prime}]
has size at most
\varepsilon|\mathcal{S}_{C}|
.

Having constructed
\mathcal{C}_{C}
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
, we define

\mathcal{C}_{j+1}=\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\leq}~\cup\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}}\mathcal{C}_{C},

and repeat this process to
\mathcal{C}_{j+1}
. Recall that our iteration stops with
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{j}
when
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\emptyset
.

We prove that
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the claimed properties. First, using statement (a) above, we can argue by induction on
j
that every general position set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}_{j}
. Indeed, a general position set is either contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\leq}
or is an independent set in
\mathcal{S}_{C}
for some
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
. Also, it is obvious from our construction that
|C|\leq 9q
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}
.

It suffices for us to bound the size of the output
\mathcal{C}
. We claim that the iterative process above must stop after at most
x=\frac{\log(9/q)}{\log(1-10^{-4})}
steps. Indeed, statement (c) above implies that for every
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{C}
we have

\sum_{v\in C\setminus C^{\prime}}d_{\mathcal{S}_{C}}(v)\geq(1-\varepsilon)|\mathcal{S}_{C}|.

Combining this with the properties of
\mathcal{S}_{C}
concluded above, we have

|C\setminus C^{\prime}|\geq\frac{(1-\varepsilon)|\mathcal{S}_{C}|}{\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{C})}\geq\frac{k_{C}q}{10^{4}}=\frac{|C|}{10^{4}}.

Hence we have
|C^{\prime}|\leq\left(1-10^{-4}\right)|C|
. This implies that every container would have size at most
\left(1-10^{-4}\right)^{x}q^{2}\leq 9q
after
x
steps, so the process should not continue beyond. Combining this fact with statement (b) above, we obtain

|\mathcal{C}|\leq\left(\exp\left(10^{18}\cdot q^{1/2}\log q\right)\right)^{x}\leq\exp\left(\frac{10^{18}}{\log((1-10^{-4})^{-1})}\cdot q^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}\right),

and this concludes our proof.
∎

3.3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
in this subsection. First, we prove the following upper bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2},~~&0\leq p\leq q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2},\\
&cpq,~~&q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2}\leq p\leq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

Furthermore, we have
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\leq(1+o(1))pq^{2}
a.a.s. for
p\geq q^{-2}\log q
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{p}
be a
p
-random set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
and
\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)
denote the number of general position sets of size
m
in
\mathbf{S}_{p}
. It follows from Chernoff’s bound that
|\mathbf{S}_{p}|\leq(1+o(1))pq^{2}
a.a.s. for
p\geq q^{-2}\log q
. The “Furthermore” part of our claim is due to the fact
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\leq|\mathbf{S}_{p}|
. So it suffices to prove the first two inequalities.

By Theorem
3.5
, there exists a collection
\mathcal{C}
of point sets in
\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
satisfying

(i)

every general position set of
\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}
,

(ii)

|C|\leq 9q
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}
, and

(iii)

|\mathcal{C}|\leq\exp\left(cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}\right)
.

Here,
c
is an absolute constant and without loss of generality, we may assume
c\geq 36e
.

First we consider the range
p\geq q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2}
. We set
m=cpq
. By the linearity of expectation and statements (i)-(iii) above, we can estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)]\leq\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\binom{|C|}{m}p^{m}\leq|\mathcal{C}|\binom{9q}{m}p^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}\right)\left(\frac{9eq}{m}\right)^{m}p^{m}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}+m\cdot\log\left(\frac{9epq}{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(m+m\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp(-cpq)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad q\rightarrow\infty.

So it follows from Markov’s inequality that

\Pr\left[\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\geq m\right]=\Pr\left[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)\geq 1\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{m}(p)]\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad q\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, a.a.s. we have
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)<m=cpq
.

Now we consider the range
p\leq q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2}
. Set
m=cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}
. Since
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)]
is nondecreasing in
p
, it follows from (
3.2
) that

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}_{m}\left(q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(-cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}\right)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad q\rightarrow\infty.

Similarly, it follows from Markov’s inequality that a.a.s.
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)<m=cq^{1/2}(\log q)^{2}
.
∎

To finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to prove the lower bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},p)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{p}
be a
p
-random set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
and
\mathbf{T}(p)
denote the number of collinear triples in
\mathbf{S}_{p}
.

We first consider the range
q^{-2}\log q\leq p\leq q^{-3/2}/\log q
. Since the total number of collinear triples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
is
(1+o(1))q^{5}/6
(see Lemma
2.1
), we have
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{T}(p)]=(1+o(1))p^{3}q^{5}/6
. So it follows from Markov’s inequality that

\Pr\left[\mathbf{T}(p)\geq\frac{pq^{2}}{2}\right]\leq(1+o(1))p^{2}q^{3}/3\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad q\rightarrow\infty.

On the other hand, it follows from Chernoff’s bound that a.a.s.
|\mathbf{S}_{p}|=(1+o(1))pq^{2}
.
Therefore, by deleting a point from each collinear triple in
\mathbf{S}_{p}
, we obtain a general position subset of
\mathbf{S}_{p}
of size at least
(\frac{1}{2}+o(1))pq^{2}
. Hence, we have a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)pq^{2}.

(3.3)

For the range
q^{-3/2}/\log q\leq p\leq q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2}
, it follows from the nondecreasingness of
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)
and (
3.3
) that a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\geq\alpha\left(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},\frac{q^{-3/2}}{\log q}\right)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\frac{q^{1/2}}{\log q}.

Finally we consider the range
q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{2}\leq p\leq 1
. Let
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{S}_{p}\cap\left\{(x,x^{2}):~x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}
. By Chernoff’s bound, we have a.a.s.
|\mathbf{Y}|=(1+o(1))pq
. As a consequence,
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{2}_{q},p)\geq|\textbf{Y}|=(1+o(1))pq
a.a.s. in this range.

These lower bounds together with Proposition
3.6
complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

4
Counting general position sets in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

We aim to prove Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, and
1.5
in this section.

4.1
Supersaturation for coplanar tuples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

The main result of this subsection is the following supersaturation result for coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in large point sets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Recall that a
coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple
consists of
d+1
points contained in some hyperplane.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds for all prime power
q\geq 2(d+1)
. Suppose
P
is a point set of size at least
q+2(d+1)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, then the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples contained in
P
is at least

c\cdot\min\left\{\frac{|P|}{q}-1,1\right\}\cdot\frac{|P|^{d+1}}{q}.

Proof.

Let
P
be as given in our hypothesis. For each
(d-1)
-tuple
A\subset P
, we fix a
(d-2)
-flat
F_{A}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
that contains
A
. Notice that each
F_{A}
is contained in exactly
q+1
hyperplanes, denoted by
\pi_{A}^{1},\pi_{A}^{2}\dots,\pi_{A}^{q+1}
, and these hyperplanes cover the space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Hence we have

|F_{A}\cap(P\setminus A)|+\sum_{i=1}^{q+1}|(\pi_{A}^{i}\setminus F_{A})\cap P|=|P\setminus A|.

Observe that every pair of points in
\pi_{A}^{i}\setminus F_{A}
or
F_{A}\setminus A
together with
A
forms a coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple. By Jensen’s inequality, the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
P
containing
A
is at least

\binom{|F_{A}\cap(P\setminus A)|}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{q+1}\binom{|(\pi_{A}^{i}\setminus F_{A})\cap P|}{2}\geq(q+2)\binom{\frac{|P\setminus A|}{q+2}}{2}=\frac{\left(|P|-d+1\right)\left(|P|-d-1-q\right)}{2(q+2)}.

Note that
|P|\geq q+2(d+1)
and
q\geq 2(d+1)
by our hypothesis. If
|P|\leq 2q
, we can check

|P|-d-1-q\geq\frac{|P|}{2}-\frac{q}{2}=\left(\frac{|P|}{q}-1\right)\frac{q}{2}\geq\left(\frac{|P|}{q}-1\right)\frac{|P|}{4}.

If
|P|\geq 2q
, then we can check
|P|-d-1-q\geq{|P|}/{4}
. In both cases, we have

|P|-d-1-q\geq\min\left\{\frac{|P|}{q}-1,1\right\}\cdot\frac{|P|}{4}.

Combining above inequalities, we conclude that the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
P
containing
A
is at least
\min\left\{{|P|}/{q}-1,1\right\}\cdot|P|^{2}/(32q)
.

Averaging over all
(d-1)
-tuples in
P
, the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples contained in
P
is at least

\frac{\binom{|P|}{d-1}}{\binom{d+1}{d-1}}\cdot\min\left\{\frac{|P|}{q}-1,1\right\}\cdot\frac{|P|^{2}}{32q}\geq c\cdot\min\left\{\frac{|P|}{q}-1,1\right\}\cdot\frac{|P|^{d+1}}{q},

where
c>0
is a sufficiently small constant depending only on
d
.
∎

In order to apply the supersaturation result succinctly in upcoming computations, we introduce some further notations and prove a refined version of Lemma
4.1
. We use
\mathcal{H}_{q,d}
to denote the
(d+1)
-graph whose vertices are all the points of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and edges are all the coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples. Given an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
and two real numbers
c,\tau>0
, we say
\mathcal{H}
is
(c,\tau)
-bounded
if

\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{c|\mathcal{H}|}{|V(\mathcal{H})|}\tau^{i-1}\quad\text{for all}\quad 2\leq i\leq r.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds for all prime power
q\geq 2(d+1)
. Suppose
P
is a point set of size at least
q+2(d+1)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, then the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}_{q,d}
on
P
is
(c,\tau)
-bounded, where

\tau=\left(\min\{k-1,1\}\right)^{-\frac{d}{d+1}}k^{-1}q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}}\quad\text{and}\quad k={|P|}/{q}.

Proof.

Let
P
be as given in our hypothesis,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{q,d}[P]
be the induced subgraph, and
c_{1}>0
be the constant guaranteed by Lemma
4.1
. For every
j\in[d-1]
, we claim that the intersection of
P
and a
j
-flat
F_{j}
has size at most
(d+1)|\mathcal{H}|^{1/(d+1)}
. Otherwise, since every
(d+1)
-tuple in
F_{j}
is coplanar, we would have

|\mathcal{H}|\geq\binom{(d+1)|\mathcal{H}|^{1/(d+1)}}{d+1}>|\mathcal{H}|,

which is a contradiction.

We consider the upper bound for
\Delta_{2}(\mathcal{H})
first. Fix a pair of points
v_{1},v_{2}\in P
, for each sequence
(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d+1})
that forms a coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple in
P
, we define
\psi(v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d+1})
to be the smallest integer
i
such that
v_{1},\dots,v_{i}
are affinely dependent. It follows from the claim above that the preimage
\psi^{-1}(i)
has size at most
(d+1)|\mathcal{H}|^{{1}/{(d+1)}}|P|^{d-2}
. Combined with Lemma
4.1
, we see that the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples containing
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{i=3}^{d+1}|\psi^{-1}(i)|

\displaystyle\leq(d-1)\cdot(d+1)|\mathcal{H}|^{\frac{1}{d+1}}|P|^{d-2}

\displaystyle=(d^{2}-1)|\mathcal{H}|^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\cdot\frac{\left(c_{1}\cdot\min\left\{k-1,1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\cdot|P|^{d}/q^{\frac{d}{d+1}}}{\left(c_{1}\cdot\min\left\{k-1,1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\cdot|P|^{2}/q^{\frac{d}{d+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq(d^{2}-1)|\mathcal{H}|^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{H}|^{\frac{d}{d+1}}}{\left(c_{1}\cdot\min\left\{k-1,1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\cdot|P|\cdot(kq)/q^{\frac{d}{d+1}}}

\displaystyle=(d^{2}-1){c_{1}^{-\frac{d}{d+1}}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|P|}\tau.

Now we consider
\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{H})
for
j\geq 3
. Using the fact
\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{H})\leq|P|^{d+1-j}
and Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}\cdot\min\left\{k-1,1\right\}\cdot|P|^{d+1}/q}{c_{1}\cdot\min\left\{k-1,1\right\}\cdot|P|^{j}/q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{c_{1}\cdot\min\left\{k-1,1\right\}\cdot|P|\cdot(kq)^{j-1}/q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}\cdot\min\{k-1,1\}^{1-\frac{(j-1)d}{d+1}}q^{j-2-\frac{j-1}{d+1}}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|P|}\tau^{j-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|P|}\tau^{j-1},

where the last inequality follows from
1-\frac{(j-1)d}{d+1}\leq 0
and
j-2-\frac{j-1}{d+1}\geq 0
. Hence we conclude the proof by taking
c
to be a sufficiently large constant depending on
d
and
c_{1}
.
∎

4.2
The second container theorem

We prove the following container theorem for general position sets in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for sufficiently large prime power
q
and real number
k\geq 1+cq^{-1/d}
, we have a collection
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(q,k)
of point sets (called containers) in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
satisfying

(i)

every general position set in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}
,

(ii)

|C|\leq kq
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}
, and

(iii)

|\mathcal{C}|\leq\exp\left(c\cdot\left(q/\min\left\{k-1,~1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}\right)
.

It is worth highlighting that in Theorem
4.3
(ii), we have the flexibility to minimize the container size to as little as
q+cq^{\frac{d-1}{d}}=(1+o(1))q
, while in Theorem
3.5
(ii) we can only guarantee an upper bound
9q
for the container size. However, the upper bound for the total number of containers guaranteed by Theorem
4.3
(iii) is worse than that in Theorem
3.5
(iii).

Proof.

Let
k>1+cq^{-1/d}
be fixed with
c
being a sufficiently large constant (depending on
d
). We set
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\}
and
\varepsilon=1/e
. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{j}
has been defined for some integer
j\geq 0
. Let

\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\left\{C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}:~|C|>kq\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\leq}=\left\{C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}:~|C|\leq kq\right\}.

If
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\emptyset
, then we let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{j}
. Otherwise, for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
we set

k_{C}={|C|}/{q},\quad\tau_{C}=\left(\min\{k_{C}-1,1\}\right)^{-\frac{d}{d+1}}k_{C}^{-1}q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\tau}_{C}=c_{1}\cdot 2^{\binom{d+1}{2}}\cdot 12(d+1)!\cdot\tau_{C},

where
c_{1}>0
is the constant guaranteed by Lemma
4.2
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{H}_{C}=\mathcal{H}_{q,d}[C]
be the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}_{q,d}
on
C
, and recall that
\mathcal{H}_{q,d}
is the hypergraph defined by coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Since
|C|>kq\geq q+2(d+1)
, it follows from Lemma
4.2
that
\mathcal{H}_{C}
is
(c_{1},\tau_{C})
-bounded. By the definitions of
(c_{1},\tau_{C})
-boundedness and
\Delta\left(\mathcal{H}_{C},\hat{\tau}_{C}\right)
, we can compute

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\mathcal{H}_{C},\hat{\tau}_{C}\right)=\frac{2^{\binom{d+1}{2}-1}|C|}{(d+1)|\mathcal{H}_{C}|}\sum_{i=2}^{d+1}\frac{\Delta_{i}\left(\mathcal{H}_{C}\right)}{2^{\binom{i-1}{2}}\hat{\tau}_{C}^{i-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\binom{d+1}{2}-1}|C|}{(d+1)|\mathcal{H}_{C}|}\sum_{i=2}^{d+1}\frac{|\mathcal{H}_{C}|}{|C|}\frac{c_{1}\tau_{C}^{i-1}}{2^{\binom{i-1}{2}}\hat{\tau}_{C}^{i-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\binom{d+1}{2}-1}}{d+1}\sum_{i=2}^{d+1}\frac{1}{2^{\binom{i-1}{2}}\cdot 2^{\binom{d+1}{2}}\cdot 12(d+1)!}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{12(d+1)!}.

In addition, since
c
and
q
are sufficiently large, we can check that
\hat{\tau}_{C}\leq\frac{1}{200\cdot(d+1)\cdot(d+1)!}
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
, applied to
\mathcal{H}_{C}
, that there exists a collection
\mathcal{C}_{C}
of subsets of
C
such that

(a)

every independent set of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}_{C}
,

(b)

the size of
\mathcal{C}_{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{C}|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(1000\cdot(d+1)\cdot\left((d+1)!\right)^{3}\cdot|C|\cdot\hat{\tau}_{C}\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{\hat{\tau}_{C}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(c_{2}\cdot\left(q/\min\left\{k-1,~1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\log q\right),

where
c_{2}
is a large constant depending on
d
and the inequality follows from the definitions of
\hat{\tau}_{C}
and
k_{C}
, and

(c)

for every
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{C}
the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
on
C^{\prime}
has size at most
\varepsilon|\mathcal{H}_{C}|
.

Having constructed
\mathcal{C}_{C}
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
, we define

\mathcal{C}_{j+1}=\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\leq}~\cup\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}}\mathcal{C}_{C},

and repeat this process to
\mathcal{C}_{j+1}
. Recall that our iteration stops with
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{j}
when
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\emptyset
.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.5
, we can verify the statements (i) and (ii) claimed for our container collection
\mathcal{C}
. So it suffices for us to bound the size of the output
\mathcal{C}
. We claim that the iterative process above must stop after at most
x=d(d+1)\cdot\log q
steps. Indeed, statement (c) above implies that the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}_{q,d}
on any
C\in\mathcal{C}_{x}^{>}
has size at most
\varepsilon^{x}|\mathcal{H}_{q,d}|<e^{-x}\left(q^{d}\right)^{d+1}=1
. On the other hand, Lemma
4.1
implies that this induced subgraph contains at least one edge when
q
is sufficiently large. This contradiction means the process should not continue beyond
x
steps. Combining this fact with statement (b) above, we obtain

|\mathcal{C}|\leq\exp\left(c_{2}\cdot\left(q/\min\left\{k-1,~1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\log q\cdot x\right)\leq\exp\left(c\cdot\left(q/\min\left\{k-1,~1\right\}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\left(\log q\right)^{2}\right),

where the last inequality follows from
c
being sufficiently large.
∎

Let

. Fix

\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}2^{|C|}\leq|\mathcal{C}|2^{kq}\leq\exp\left(c\left(q^{\frac{1}{2d+1}}\cdot q\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}+\left(1+q^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}}q\right)\right)=2^{q+q^{\frac{2d}{2d+1}+o(1)}}.\qed

.

Let
m
be as given in our hypothesis and we assume

\epsilon=m^{-\frac{d+1}{2d+1}}q^{\frac{d}{2d+1}}(\log q)^{\frac{2d+2}{2d+1}}.

Using this definition and the bounds on

\left(\frac{q}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}=\epsilon m\quad\text{and}\quad q^{-\frac{1}{2d+1}}\left(\frac{\log q}{2}\right)^{\frac{d+1}{2d+1}}\leq\epsilon\leq\left(\log q\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2d+1}}.

Hence we set

and

. By the other properties of

Here we used the the inequality

5
Random Turán results in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

We aim to prove Theorem
1.2
in this section.

5.1
Supersaturation for critical coplanar tuples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}

We say a coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is
critical
if every proper subset of it is affinely independent. The main result of this subsection is the following supersaturation result for critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds for all prime power
q
. Suppose
P
is a point set of size at least
4dq^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, then
P
contains at least
c|P|^{d+1}/q
critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples.

Proof.

Let
P
be as given in our hypothesis,
N=q^{d-k}\binom{d}{k}_{q}=(1+o(1))q^{d}
(see Lemma
2.1
), and label all hyperplanes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
as
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{N}
. A simple double-counting and Lemma
2.1
give

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|P\cap\pi_{i}|=\sum_{v\in P}\text{ number of hyperplanes containing $v$ }=\binom{q}{d-1}_{q}|P|\geq q^{d-1}|P|.

Define the function

f(x):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(d+1)!}x\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{d-2}\left(x-q^{i}\right)\cdot\left(x-dq^{d-2}\right),&\quad\text{if}\quad x\geq 2dq^{d-2},\\
0,&\quad\text{if}\quad x<2dq^{d-2}.\end{cases}

By choosing points one by one, it is easy to see that if a hyperplane
\pi
contains
x
points from
P
, then there are at least
f(x)
critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
P\cap\pi
. Therefore, it follows from the convexity of
f(x)
and Jensen’s inequality that the number of critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
P
is at least

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}f\left(|P\cap\pi_{i}|\right)

\displaystyle\geq N\cdot f\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}|P\cap\pi_{i}|}{N}\right)\geq N\cdot f\left(\frac{q^{d-1}|P|}{N}\right)\geq q^{d}\cdot f\left(\frac{|P|}{2q}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q^{d}}{(d+1)!}\left(\frac{|P|}{4q}\right)^{d+1}=\frac{1}{4^{d+1}(d+1)!}\cdot\frac{|P|^{d+1}}{q}.\qed

We remark that the number of critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples guaranteed by Lemma
5.1
is tight up to some constant factor. To see this, consider a
p
-random set
\textbf{S}_{p}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. By Chernoff’s bound, a.a.s.
|\textbf{S}_{p}|=(1+o(1))pq^{d}
as long as
p\gg 1/q^{d}
. Since the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples inside
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is
(1+o(1))q^{d+(d-1)(d+1)}
(because there are
q^{d}
hyperplanes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
each one contains
(1+o(1))q^{(d-1)(d+1)}
coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples), the number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
\textbf{S}_{p}
is a.a.s.
(1+o(1))q^{d+(d-1)(d+1)}p^{d+1}=O\left({|\textbf{S}_{p}|^{d+1}}/{q}\right)
.

We also prove a refined version of Lemma
5.1
which is more handy in later computations. We use
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{q,d}
to denote the
(d+1)
-graph whose vertices are all the points of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and edges are all the critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds for all prime power
q
. Suppose
P
is a point set of size at least
4dq^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, then the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{q,d}
on
P
is
(c,\tau)
-bounded, where

\tau=\max\left\{k^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{d}-1},~k^{-2}q^{d-2}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad k={|P|}/{q}.

Proof.

Let
P
be as given in our hypothesis,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{q,d}[P]
be the induced subgraph, and
c_{1}>0
be the constant guaranteed by Lemma
5.1
.

Let
2\leq j\leq d
be an integer and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\in P
be affinely independent. To upper bound
\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{H})
it suffices to count the number of choices for points
v_{j+1},\ldots,v_{d+1}\in P
such that
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d+1}\}
is a critical
(d+1)
-tuple. We use the trivial upper bound
|P|
for each point in
\{v_{j+1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
. And once they are chosen the
(d+1)
-th point must lie in the unique
(d-1)
-flat containing
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
, so there are at most
q^{d-1}
choices for
v_{d+1}
. Combined with the fact
|P|\leq q^{d}
and Lemma
5.1
, we can compute

\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{H})\leq|P|^{d-j}q^{d-1}\leq\left(\frac{q^{d}}{|P|}\right)^{j-2}|P|^{d-j}q^{d-1}=\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot c_{1}\frac{|P|^{d}}{q}\cdot\left(\frac{q^{d-2}}{k^{2}}\right)^{j-1}\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|P|}\tau^{j-1}.

To get the upper bound of
\Delta_{d+1}(\mathcal{H})
, notice that
\Delta_{d+1}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1
, so we have

\Delta_{d+1}(\mathcal{H})\leq 1\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot c_{1}\frac{|P|^{d}}{q}\left(\frac{1}{kq^{1-\frac{1}{d}}}\right)^{d}\leq\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{|P|}\tau^{d}.\qed

5.2
The third container theorem

We prove the following container theorem for general position sets in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
using Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for sufficiently large prime power
q
and real number
k\in[4dq^{d-2},q^{d-1-\frac{1}{d}}]
, we have a collection
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(q,k)
of point sets (called containers) in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
satisfying

(i)

every general position set of
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}
,

(ii)

|C|\leq kq
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}
, and

(iii)

|\mathcal{C}|\leq\exp\left(ck^{-1}q^{d-1}(\log q)^{2}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
k\in[4dq^{d-2},q^{d-1-\frac{1}{d}}]
be fixed. We set
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}\}
and
\varepsilon=1/e
. Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{j}
has been defined for some integer
j\geq 0
. We define
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\leq}
in the same way as the proof of Theorem
4.3
. If
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}=\emptyset
, then we let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{j}
. Otherwise, for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
we set

k_{C}={|C|}/{q},\quad\tau_{C}=\max\left\{k_{C}^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{d}-1},~k_{C}^{-2}q^{d-2}\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\tau}_{C}=c_{1}\cdot 2^{\binom{d+1}{2}}\cdot 12(d+1)!\cdot\tau_{C},

where
c_{1}>0
is the constant guaranteed by Lemma
5.2
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{H}_{C}=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{q,d}[C]
be the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{q,d}
on
C
, and recall that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{q,d}
is the hypergraph defined by coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. Since
|C|>kq\geq 4dq^{d-1}
, it follows from Lemma
5.2
that
\mathcal{H}_{C}
is
(c_{1},\tau_{C})
-bounded. Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we can check that
\Delta\left(\mathcal{H}_{C},\hat{\tau}_{C}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{12(d+1)!}
. In addition, since
q
is sufficiently large, we have
\hat{\tau}_{C}\leq\frac{1}{200\cdot(d+1)\cdot(d+1)!}
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
, applied to
\mathcal{H}_{C}
, that there exists a collection
\mathcal{C}_{C}
of subsets of
C
such that

(a)

every independent set of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
is contained in some member of
\mathcal{C}_{C}
,

(b)

the size of
\mathcal{C}_{C}
satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{C}|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(1000\cdot(d+1)\cdot\left((d+1)!\right)^{3}\cdot|C|\cdot\hat{\tau}_{C}\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{\hat{\tau}_{C}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(c_{2}\cdot\max\left\{\frac{1}{q^{1-\frac{1}{d}}},~\frac{q^{d-2}}{k_{C}}\right\}q\log q\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(c_{2}\cdot\max\left\{\frac{1}{q^{1-\frac{1}{d}}},~\frac{q^{d-2}}{k}\right\}q\log q\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(c_{2}k^{-1}q^{d-1}\log q\right),

where
c_{2}
is a large constant depending on
d
and the inequality follows from the definitions of
\hat{\tau}_{C}
and
k_{C}
, and

(c)

for every
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{C}
the induced subgraph of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
on
C^{\prime}
has size at most
\varepsilon|\mathcal{H}_{C}|
.

Having constructed
\mathcal{C}_{C}
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{>}
, we define
\mathcal{C}_{j+1}
and proceed with the iteration in the same way as in the proofs of Theorems
3.5
and
4.3
.

Finally, we can verify the statements (i) and (ii) claimed for our final container collection
\mathcal{C}
similarly to the proof of Theorem
3.5
. Moreover, similar to the argument in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, using statements (c) above and Lemma
5.1
, we can show that the iterative process above must stop after at most
d(d+1)\cdot\log q
steps. Combining this fact with statement (b) above, we obtain
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\exp\left(ck^{-1}q^{d-1}(\log q)^{2}\right)
with
c
being a large constant depending on
d
. Hence we conclude the proof.
∎

5.3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
in this subsection. First, we prove the following upper bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)
with
p\leq q^{-d+\frac{2d}{d+1}+o(1)}
using Theorem
5.3
.

Proposition 5.4
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)\leq\begin{cases}cq^{\frac{1}{d}}(\log q)^{2},&\quad\text{if}\quad 0\leq p\leq q^{-d+\frac{2}{d}}(\log q)^{2},\\
cp^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{d}{2}}\log q,&\quad\text{if}\quad q^{-d+\frac{2}{d}}(\log q)^{2}\leq p\leq q^{-d+\frac{2d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{p}
be a
p
-random set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)
denote the number of general position sets of size
m
in
\mathbf{S}_{p}
. Also let
c>0
be the constant guaranteed by Theorem
5.3
. By enlarging
c
if necessary, we can assume
c\geq 4e
without loss of generality.

First we consider the range
q^{-d+\frac{2}{d}}(\log q)^{2}\leq p\leq q^{-d+\frac{2d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}
. We set
k=p^{-\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\log q
and
m=cpkq
. We can check that

4dq^{d-2}\ll q^{d-2+\frac{1}{d+1}}\leq k\leq q^{d-1-\frac{1}{d}}.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(q,k)
be the container collection outputted by Theorem
5.3
. By the properties of
\mathcal{C}
guaranteed by Theorem
5.3
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)]\leq\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\binom{|C|}{m}p^{m}\leq|\mathcal{C}|\binom{kq}{m}p^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(ck^{-1}q^{d-1}(\log q)^{2}\right)\left(\frac{ekq}{m}\right)^{m}p^{m}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(ck^{-1}q^{d-1}(\log q)^{2}+m\cdot\log\left(\frac{epkq}{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(m+m\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp(-cp^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{d}{2}}\log q)\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad q\rightarrow\infty.

Same as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we can use Markov’s inequality to argue that a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)\leq m=cp^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{d}{2}}\log q.

For
p\leq q^{-d+\frac{2}{d}}(\log q)^{2}
, the argument is again similar to the proof of Proposition
3.6
. Combining the “nondecreasingness” of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)]
and (
5.1
), we can argue that
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)<cq^{\frac{1}{d}}(\log q)^{2}
a.a.s. in this range.
∎

Next, we use Theorem
4.3
to establish a different type of upper bounds for
\alpha(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d},p)
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For every integer
d\geq 2
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)\leq\begin{cases}cq^{\frac{d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2},&\quad\text{if}\quad p\leq q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2},\\
cpq,&\quad\text{if}\quad q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}\leq p\leq 1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{p}
be a
p
-random set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
and
\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)
denote the number of general position sets of size
m
in
\mathbf{S}_{p}
. Also let
c>0
be the constant guaranteed by Theorem
4.3
. By enlarging
c
if necessary, we can assume
c\geq 8e
.

First we consider the range
p\geq q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}
. We set
m=cpq
and let
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(q,k)
be the container collection outputted by Theorem
4.3
with
k=2
. By the properties of
\mathcal{C}
guaranteed by Theorem
4.3
, we can estimate

\mathbb{E}[\textbf{X}_{m}]\leq\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\binom{|C|}{m}p^{m}\leq|\mathcal{C}|\binom{2q}{m}p^{m}\leq\exp\left(cq^{\frac{d}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}+m\cdot\log\left(\frac{2epq}{m}\right)\right)\leq\exp(cpq).

Same as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we can use Markov’s inequality to argue that a.a.s.

\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)\leq m=cpq.

Also similar to the proof of Proposition
3.6
, our upper bound for the range
p\leq q^{-\frac{1}{d+1}}(\log q)^{2}
follows from the “nondecreasingness” of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{m}(p)]
as a function of
p
.
∎

Finally, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Due to its similarity to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we only give a brief summary of our arguments.

Proof Sketch of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{p}
be a
p
-random set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
. First we consider the upper bounds. During the range

and Chernoff’s bound. During the range

5.4
. And during the range
q^{-d+\frac{2d}{d+1}+o(1)}\leq p\leq 1
, we can apply Proposition
5.5
to obtain the claimed upper bound.

Next we consider the lower bounds. During the range
q^{-d+o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-d+\frac{1}{d}+o(1)}
, we can check that the expected number of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
\mathbf{S}_{p}
is much smaller than the expected number of
|\mathbf{S}_{p}|
, and our lower bound follows from deleting a point from each coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple. During the range
q^{-d+\frac{1}{d}+o(1)}\leq p\leq q^{-\frac{1}{d}+o(1)}
, it follows from the “nondecreasingness” of
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)
as a function of
p
. And during the range
q^{-\frac{1}{d}+o(1)}\leq p\leq 1
, the lower bound comes from the intersection of
p
-random set and the moment curve
\textbf{Y}=\textbf{S}_{p}\cap\left\{(x,\ldots,x^{d}):~x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}
.
∎

6
Concluding Remarks

•

The problem of determining
\alpha(\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q},p)
remains open for
d\geq 3
in the range
q^{-d+2/d}\leq p\leq q^{-1/(d+1)}
. We believe that the lower bounds are closer to the truth. One obstacle in generalizing our proof for
d=2
is the lack of optimal supersaturation when using the expander mixing lemma. The reason is that the intersection of hyperplanes could be as large as
q^{d-2}
, resulting in a large number of overcounting of coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples. This problem might be overcome with some extra ideas. As the first step towards resolving this problem, we suggest the following question for
d=3
.

Problem 6.1
.

Is it true that for any point set
P
in
\mathbb{F}^{3}_{q}
with size
1000q
, there is a collection
\mathcal{S}
of coplanar
4
-tuples in
P
such that

|\mathcal{S}|=\Omega\left(q^{3}\right),~\Delta_{1}(\mathcal{S})=O\left(q^{2}\right),~\Delta_{2}(\mathcal{S})=O\left(q^{4/3}\right),~\text{and}~\Delta_{3}(\mathcal{S})=O\left(q^{2/3}\right).

•

More generally, we say a point set
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is
(k,c)
-evasive
if every
k
-flat of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
contains at most
c
points in
S
. Notice that a general position set in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
is simply a
(d-1,d)
-evasive set. Extremal problems for evasive sets are extensively studied (see e.g.
[
9
,
4
,
31
,
32
]
), but the asymptotic behaviour of many related extremal quantities are still unknown. It seems plausible to extend results in this paper to
(k,c)
-evasive sets in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
for certain values of
d
and
(k,c)
, and we hope to return to this topic in future work.

•

It seems possible that the assumption
|P|\geq 4dq^{d-1}
in Lemma
5.1
can be relaxed to
|P|\gg q^{d-2}
, which (if true) would be tight up to some constant factor, since a
(d-2)
-flat has size
q^{d-2}
but contains no critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples. As weak evidence for such a relaxation, we prove that every point set
P
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
with
|P|\gg q^{d-2}
contains at least one critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple. We also have the following theorem confirming our speculation for
d=3
. See our proofs in the Appendix.

Theorem 6.2
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that if
P
is a point set of size at least
cq
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
, then
P
contains at least
\Omega\left({|P|^{4}}/{q}\right)
critical coplanar
4
-tuples.

Appendix A
Extremal results for critical coplanar tuples

We first prove a Turán-type result for critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
.

Theorem A.1
.

For every integer
d\geq 3
there exists a constant
c>0
such that if
P
is a point set of size at least
cq^{d-2}
in
\mathbb{F}^{d}_{q}
, then there exists a critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple contained in
P
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
d
. First we consider the
d=3
case. Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
can be covered by
q
parallel hyperplanes, there exists one hyperplane
F_{2}
such that
|F_{2}\cap P|\geq 4
(assuming
c
is sufficiently large). If these
4
points do not form a critical coplanar
4
-tuple, there exists a line
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
containing at least
3
vertices of
P
. Notice that there are
q+1
hyperplanes containing
F_{1}
. Hence by the pigeonhole principle, there is a hyperplane
F_{2}^{\prime}
containing
F_{1}
and

|F_{2}^{\prime}\cap(P\setminus F_{1})|\geq\frac{|P\setminus F_{1}|}{q+1}\geq\frac{cq-q}{q+1}\geq 2.

Hence, let
u,v
be two points in
F_{2}^{\prime}\cap(P\setminus F_{1})
and
\ell_{uv}
be the line determined by them. By the fact that
|F_{1}\cap P|\geq 3
, we have
|(F_{1}\cap P)\setminus\ell_{uv}|\geq 2
. Then let
w,z
be two points in
(F_{1}\cap P)\setminus\ell_{uv}
, and notice that
\{u,v,w,z\}
is a critical coplanar
4
-tuple (there are no collinear triples among them).

Now we assume
d\geq 4
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
can be covered by
q
parallel hyperplanes, there exists one hyperplane
F_{d-1}
such that
|F_{d-1}\cap P|\geq cq^{d-3}
. By the inductive hypothesis, there exists a
d
-tuple
T
that is critical coplanar inside the
(d-1)
-dimensional affine space
F_{d-1}
. As a consequence,
T
spans a
(d-2)
-flat
F_{d-2}\subset F_{d-1}
. Notice that there are
q+1
hyperplanes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
containing
F_{d-2}
. So there is a hyperplane
F_{d-1}^{\prime}
containing
F_{d-2}
and

|F_{d-1}^{\prime}\cap(P\setminus F_{d-2})|\geq\frac{|P\setminus F_{d-2}|}{q+1}\geq\frac{cq^{d-2}-q^{d-2}}{q+1}\geq\frac{c}{2}q^{d-3}.

Write
S=F_{d-1}^{\prime}\cap(P\setminus F_{d-2})
and fix a point
u\in S
. For each point
v\in S\setminus\{u\}
, if the union of either
(d-1)
-tuple in
T
and
\{u,v\}
is not a critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
, then we say that
v
is a
bad vertex
. It suffices for us to show that not all of
S\setminus\{u\}
are bad vertices. To do this, we need the following claim.

Claim A.2
.

Suppose
v\in S\setminus\{u\}
is a bad vertex, then any
(d-1)
-tuple in
T
contains a
(d-2)
-tuple
\tau
such that
\tau\cup\{u,v\}
lies on a
(d-2)
-flat.

Proof.

Fix a
(d-1)
-tuple
T^{\prime}\subset T
. By definition,
T^{\prime}\cup\{u,v\}
is not a critical coplanar
(d+1)
-tuple. So
T^{\prime}\cup\{u,v\}
contains a
d
-tuple
\tau^{\prime}
that is affinely dependent. Since
T
is critical coplanar inside the
(d-1)
-dimensional affine space
F_{d-1}
, the affine span of
T^{\prime}
coincides with the affine span of
T
, which is
F_{d-2}
. Meanwhile,
F_{d-2}
does not contain
u
or
v
. So the affine span of
T^{\prime}\cup\{u\}
or
T^{\prime}\cup\{v\}
has dimension
d-1
. As a consequence,
\tau^{\prime}
cannot be either
T^{\prime}\cup\{u\}
or
T^{\prime}\cup\{v\}
. Hence, there exists a
(d-2)
-tuple
\tau
in
T^{\prime}
such that
\tau^{\prime}=\tau\cup\{u,v\}
, and we know that
\tau^{\prime}
lies on a
(d-2)
-flat.
∎

Now let
v
be a bad vertex and
\ell_{uv}
be the line determined by
u,v
. By Claim
A.2
, there exists a
(d-2)
-tuple
\tau
such that
\tau\cup\{u,v\}
lies on a
(d-2)
-flat
F^{\prime}_{d-2}
. Next, we choose a
(d-1)
-tuple
T^{\prime}\subset T
such that
\tau\not\subset T^{\prime}
. By Claim
A.2
, there exists a
(d-2)
-tuple
\tau^{\prime}\subset T^{\prime}
such that
\tau^{\prime}\cup\{u,v\}
lies on a
(d-2)
-flat
F^{\prime\prime}_{d-2}
. We claim that
F^{\prime}_{d-2}\neq F^{\prime\prime}_{d-2}
. Indeed, since
|\tau\cup\tau^{\prime}|\geq d-1
and
T
is critical planar in
F_{d-1}
, the span of
\tau\cup\tau^{\prime}
coincides with the span of
T
, which is
F_{d-2}
. If
F^{\prime}_{d-2}=F^{\prime\prime}_{d-2}
, then we can conclude
F_{d-2}=F^{\prime}_{d-2}
, which contradicts to the fact
u,v\not\in F_{d-2}
. Thus
v
lies on a
(d-3)
-flat
F^{\prime}_{d-2}\cap F^{\prime\prime}_{d-2}
and this
(d-3)
-flat only depends on
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\subset T
. Since there are
\binom{d}{d-2}
choices for a
(d-2)
-tuple in
T
, it follows that there are at most
\binom{d}{d-2}^{2}q^{d-3}
possible locations for a bad vertex
v
. Recall that
|S|\geq\frac{c}{2}q^{d-3}
. Hence, we can find
v\in S\setminus\{u\}
which is not a bad vertex given that
c
is sufficiently large.
∎

Next we prove Theorem
6.2
, the “asymptotically best possible” supersaturation result for critical coplanar
4
-tuples in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
. We restate the theorem here.

Theorem A.3
.

There exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that if
P
is a point set of size at least
cq
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}
, then
P
contains at least
\Omega\left({|P|^{4}}/{q}\right)
critical coplanar
4
-tuples.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary pair
\{u,v\}\subset P
and let
\ell
be the line determined by
u,v
. Notice that there are
q+1
planes
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{q+1}
containing
\ell
. First, we discard all
\pi_{i}
such that
\pi_{i}\setminus\ell
contains less than
|P|/(10q)
points from
P
. Notice that at least
|P|/10
points are not in any discarded
\pi_{i}
(here we assume
c
is sufficiently large and use
|P|\geq cq
). Next, we label each remaining hyperplane
\pi_{i}
as follows: if there exists a line
\ell_{i}
such that
u\in\ell_{i}\subset\pi_{i}
and at least a
1/3
-fraction of points from
(\pi_{i}\setminus\ell)\cap P
lie in
\ell_{i}
, then we label
\pi_{i}
with
u
; if there exists a line
\ell_{i}
such that
v\in\ell_{i}\subset\pi_{i}
and at least a
1/3
-fraction of points from
(\pi_{i}\setminus\ell)\cap P
lie in
\ell_{i}
, then we label
\pi_{i}
with
v
; otherwise we label
\pi_{i}
with
*
. Then, by the pigeonhole principle, there are planes
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}
sharing the same label such that their union contains at least
|P|/30
points from
P\setminus\ell
. Write
x_{i}=|(\pi_{i}\setminus\ell)\cap P|
for
i\in[k]
, then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\geq\frac{|P|}{30}\text{\quad and \quad}k\leq q+1.

If the planes
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}
are all labeled by
*
, then there are
x_{i}^{2}/3
critical coplanar
4
-tuples containing
\{u,v\}
on each
\pi_{i}
. Indeed, we first choose a point
w\in\pi_{i}\setminus\ell
out of
x_{i}
choices. Then we discard all the points on the lines
\overline{uw}
and
\overline{vw}
. Because the plane
\pi_{i}
is labelled by
*
, there are at least
x_{i}/3
points in
P\cap\pi_{i}
remaining, so we can choose any
z
from them and
\{u,v,w,z\}
will be critical coplanar. Hence, we have at least
\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}^{2}/3
many critical coplanar
4
-tuples. By Jensen’s inequality, there are at least
\Omega(|P|^{2}/q)
critical coplanar
4
-tuples containing
\{u,v\}
. In this particular case, we say that
\{u,v\}
is a
good pair
.

If the planes
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}
are all labeled by
u
(for example), we say that
u
is the
pivot
of the
bad pair

\{u,v\}
. Notice that for
1\leq i<j\leq k
, there are
\binom{x_{i}/3}{2}\binom{x_{j}/3}{2}
many critical coplanar
4
-tuples
\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3},w_{4}\}
such that the two lines
\overline{w_{1}w_{2}}
and
\overline{w_{3}w_{4}}
intersect at
u
. Indeed, we can just choose
w_{1},w_{2}
from
\ell_{i}\cap P
and
w_{3},w_{4}
from
\ell_{j}\cap P
. In this way, the number of such
4
-tuples we produced is at least, up to a constant factor,

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}.

(A.1)

The following claim, whose proof we postpone, optimizes this quantity in terms of
|P|
.

Claim A.4
.

When
\{u,v\}
is a bad pair, the quantity (
A.1
) is at least
\Omega(|P|^{3}/q)
.

We consider the above process for all pairs
u,v\in P
. There are
\Omega(|P|^{2})
pairs and each one is either good or bad. If there are
\Omega(|P|^{2})
many good pairs, we produce
\Omega((|P|^{2})\cdot(|P|^{2}/q))
critical coplanar
4
-tuples, where each distinct tuple is repeated at most
\binom{4}{2}
times. Indeed, for each critical coplanar
4
-tuple, there are
\binom{4}{2}
ways to determine
\{u,v\}
.

If there are
\Omega(|P|^{2})
many bad pairs, we produce
\Omega((|P|^{2})\cdot(|P|^{3}/q))
critical coplanar
4
-tuples, where each distinct tuple is repeated at most
3|P|
times. Indeed, for each critical coplanar
4
-tuple, there are
3
candidates for the pivot. Once the pivot is determined, there are at most
|P|
choices for the bad pair
\{u,v\}
.

Overall, we have counted
\Omega(|P|^{4}/q)
critical coplanar
4
-tuples in
P
. Hence it suffices for us to justify Claim
A.4
. Without loss of generality, we assume
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\dots\geq x_{k}
. We check that, fixing
x_{1}
, the quantity (
A.1
), considered as a function of
x_{2},\dots,x_{k}
, is Schur-convex using the Schur-Ostrowski criterion (see
[
29
,
23
]
).

For any
2\leq i\neq j\leq k
, we notice that

f(x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{k}):=\eqref{eq:critical_supersaturation_Fq3}=x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}+(x_{i}^{2}+x_{j}^{2})\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\iota\neq i,j\\
1\leq\iota\leq k\end{subarray}}x_{\iota}^{2}\right)+\text{other terms independent of $x_{i}$ and $x_{j}$}.

Then we can compute

(x_{i}-x_{j})\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right)=2(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\iota\neq i,j\\
1\leq\iota\leq k\end{subarray}}x_{\iota}^{2}-x_{i}x_{j}\right)\geq 0,

where the last inequality follows from
x_{1}^{2}\geq x_{i}x_{j}
. Hence
f
satisfies the Schur-Ostrowski criterion.

Because we have the hypothesis that
\{u,v\}
is a bad pair, we have a special condition
x_{1}/3\leq q
since at least a
1/3
-fraction of points from
(\pi_{i}\setminus\ell)\cap P
is contained in a line. So we have
\sum_{i=2}^{k}x_{i}\geq|P|/100
(here we use
|P|\geq cq
). Therefore, by Schur-convexity, the quantity (
A.1
) is lower bounded by when
x_{i}
’s (with
2\leq i\leq k
) are roughly equal, which means

\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq k}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\geq\Omega\left(\left(\frac{|P|}{k}\right)^{4}(k)^{2}\right)\geq\Omega(|P|^{3}/q).

Here we used
|P|\geq cq
and
k\leq q+1
for the last inequality. This proves Claim
A.4
and concludes our proof.
∎

When the geodesic becomes rigid in the directed landscape
Zhipeng Liu

Abstract
When the value L L of the directed landscape at a point ( 𝐩 ; 𝐪 ) (\mathbf{p};\mathbf{q}) is sufficiently large, the geodesic from 𝐩 \mathbf{p} to 𝐪 \mathbf{q} is rigid and its location fluctuates of order L − 1 / 4 L^{-1/4} around its expectation. We further show that at a midpoint of the geodesic, the location of the geodesic and the value of the directed landscape after appropriate scaling converge to two independent Gaussians.

The goal of this paper is to investigate one property of the geodesic in the directed landscape using the formula obtained in
[
Liu21
]
.
We first introduce some notations and properties about the directed landscape and its geodesic. Let
\mathcal{L}(x,s;y,t)
be the directed landscape. It satisfies the metric composition law

\mathcal{L}(x,r;y,t)=\max_{z\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathcal{L}(x,r;z,s)+\mathcal{L}(z,s;y,t)\right)

(1.1)

for any fixed
r<s<t
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, and hence it has the reverse triangle inequality

\mathcal{L}(x,r;y,t)\geq\mathcal{L}(x,r;z,s)+\mathcal{L}(z,s;y,t).

(1.2)

The geodesic in the directed landscape
\mathcal{L}
from
(x,r)
to
(y,t)
is a continuous path
\pi=\pi_{x,r;y,t}
which maximizes the following length with respect to
\mathcal{L}

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{r=s_{0}<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{k}=t}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{L}(\pi(s_{i-1}),s_{i-1};\pi(s_{i}),s_{i}).

(1.3)

It has been proved in
[
DOV18
]
that such a directed geodesic exists and is unique almost surely. Moreover, one can prove that the geodesic has the following properties

\pi_{x,r;y,t}((1-s)r+st)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle law}}{{=}}(t-r)^{2/3}\pi_{0,0;0,1}(s)+((1-s)x+sy),\quad s\in[0,1],

(1.4)

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle law}}{{=}}
means that the two sides have the same law, and

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(\pi_{x,r;y,t}((1-s_{1})r+s_{1}t),(1-s_{1})r+s_{1}t;\pi_{x,r;y,t}((1-s_{2})r+s_{2}t),(1-s_{2})r+s_{2}t)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle law}}{{=}}(t-r)^{1/3}\mathcal{L}(\pi_{0,0;0,1}(s_{1}),s_{1};\pi_{0,0;0,1}(s_{2}),s_{2})\\
&\quad-2(t-r)^{-1/3}(\pi_{0,0;0,1}(s_{2})-\pi_{0,0;0,1}(s_{1}))(y-x)-\frac{s_{2}-s_{1}}{t-r}(y-x)^{2}\end{split}

(1.5)

for any
s_{1},s_{2}
satisfying
0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq 1
. In other words, the goedesic
\pi_{x,r;y,t}
has the same law as
\pi_{0,0;0,1}
after rescaling, and the directed landscape along the geodesic
\pi_{x,r;y,t}
has the same law as along
\pi_{0,0;0,1}
after shifting and rescaling. These two properties can be obtained straightforwardly using the formula (
1.3
) and the stationarity and rescaling properties of
\mathcal{L}
described in
[
DOV18
, Lemma 10.2]
. Hence we do not provide details here.

In this paper, we will fix the point
(0,0;0,1)
. Denote
\Pi(s)=\pi_{0,0;0,1}(s)
,
0\leq s\leq 1
, the geodesic from
(0,0)
to
(0,1)
. We also denote
\mathcal{L}(s)=\mathcal{L}(0,0;\Pi(s),s)
for
0\leq s\leq 1
. We remark that the fact
\Pi(s)
is on the geodesic implies
\mathcal{L}(s)+\mathcal{L}(\Pi(s),s;0,1)=\mathcal{L}(1)
.

The main result of this paper is about the fluctuations of
\Pi(s)
and
\mathcal{L}(s)
when
\mathcal{L}(1)=L
becomes large. In the following theorem, the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}\left(A\mid\mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)
should be understood as
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0+}\mathbb{P}\left(A\mid\mathcal{L}(1)-L\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\right)
, for an event
A
.

Theorem 1.1

(Rigidity of the geodesic)
.

For any fixed
s\in(0,1)
and
x_{1},x_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
x_{1}<x_{2}
and
\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}&\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{2{L}^{1/4}\Pi(s)}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\in(x_{1},x_{2}),\frac{\mathcal{L}(s)-s{L}}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}{L}^{1/4}}\in(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\>\Bigg|\>\mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)\\
&=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}p(x)\mathrm{d}x\int_{\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}p(\ell)\mathrm{d}\ell,\end{split}

(1.6)

where
p
is the probability density function of the standard Gaussian distribution

p(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.7)

Equivalently, the conditional joint density of
\frac{2\mathcal{L}^{1/4}\Pi(s)}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}},\frac{\mathcal{L}(s)-s\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}\mathcal{L}^{1/4}}
given
\mathcal{L}(1)=L
converges to the product of two standard Gaussian densities as
L\to\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
implies that
\Pi(s)
becomes very rigid when
L
becomes large. It fluctuates of order
L^{-1/4}
and has Gaussian distribution after rescaling

\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{2{L}^{1/4}\Pi(s)}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\in(x_{1},x_{2})\>\Bigg|\>\mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}p(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(1.8)

The rigidity of the geodesic conditioned on large
L
is not surprising. For example in
[
BG19
]
, the authors were able to show that in the directed last passage percolation model with i.i.d. exponential weights, if the last passage time is
(4+\delta)n
, then the geodesic fluctuates of order
n^{1/2+o(1)}
which is far smaller than the typical fluctuation order
n^{2/3}
when the last passage time is about
4n+\mathcal{O}(n^{1/3})
. If we write
\delta=cn^{-2/3}L
, then
\Pi(s)\cdot n^{2/3}
has an order
n^{1/2}
. So heuristically
\Pi(s)
fluctuates as
\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/6})=\mathcal{O}(L^{-1/4})
which is consistent with our result above. However, the limiting distribution of
\Pi(s)
was not known to our best knowledge.

Theorem
1.1
also implies that
\mathcal{L}(s)
fluctuates of order
L^{1/4}
and has Gaussian distribution after rescaling

\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\mathcal{L}(s)-s{L}}{{L}^{1/4}}\in(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\>\Bigg|\>\mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)=\int_{\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}p(\ell)\mathrm{d}\ell.

(1.9)

Both the fluctuation order and the limiting distribution were not known to our best knowledge.

Remark 1.3
.

One can show that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{2{L}^{1/4}\Pi(s)}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\in(x_{1},x_{2}),\frac{\mathcal{L}(s)-s{L}}{\sqrt{s(1-s)}{L}^{1/4}}\in(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\>\Bigg|\>\mathcal{L}(1)=L\right)\\
&=\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}p(x)\mathrm{d}x\int_{\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}p(\ell)\mathrm{d}\ell+L^{-3/4}\frac{2s-1}{2\sqrt{s(1-s)}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}p(x)\mathrm{d}x\int_{\ell_{1}}^{\ell_{2}}\ell p(\ell)\mathrm{d}\ell+\mathcal{O}(L^{-3/2})\end{split}

when
L
becomes large. The error terms can be explicitly evaluated by a more careful calculation using the same argument of this paper. However, the calculation is quite cumbersome so we do not include it here.

By combing the equations (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), and Theorem
1.1
, we have the following general result for the geodesic from
(x,r)
to
(y,t)
, conditioned on
\mathcal{L}(x,r;y,t)
goes to infinity.

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, and
r,t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
are all fixed. Assume that
r<t
. Conditioned on
\mathcal{L}(x,r;y,t)=L
goes to infinity, we have

\frac{2L^{1/4}\left(\pi_{x,r;y,t}((1-s)r+st)-((1-s)x+sy)\right)}{(t-r)^{3/4}\sqrt{s(1-s)}}

and

\frac{\mathcal{L}\left(x,r;\pi_{x,t;y,t}((1-s)r+st),(1-s)r+st\right)-sL}{(t-r)^{1/4}\sqrt{s(1-s)}L^{1/4}}

converge to two independent standard Gaussians in distribution.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

It is well known that
\mathcal{L}(1)
has the GUE Tracy-Widom distribution
F_{GUE}(L)
, which can be defined by

F_{GUE}(L)=\exp\left(-\int_{L}^{\infty}\left(\ell-L\right)u^{2}(\ell)\mathrm{d}\ell\right)

(3.1)

in terms of the Hastings-McLeod solution
u=u(\ell)
to the Painlevé-II equation

u^{\prime\prime}=\ell u+2u^{3},\qquad u(\ell)\to\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}\ell^{1/4}}e^{-\frac{2}{3}\ell^{3/2}}(1+o(1)),\quad\text{as}\quad\ell\to\infty.

(3.2)

It is known from the above formulas (see, for example
[
BBD08
, equation (25)]
for the expansion of the squared root of
{F_{GUE}(L)}
) that
F_{GUE}(L)
has the following right tail behavior

F_{GUE}(L)\approx 1-\frac{1}{16\pi L^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}},\quad\text{as }L\to\infty,

(3.3)

and its density function
f_{GUE}(L)
has

f_{GUE}(L)\approx\frac{1}{8\pi L}e^{-\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}},\quad\text{as }L\to\infty.

(3.4)

Here and afterwards, we use
\approx
to denote the leading order asymptotics if the next order term is not needed in our analysis.

Now we come to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For fixed
s\in(0,1)
and
x,\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote

\begin{split}X=X(L)&:=\frac{x\sqrt{s(1-s)}}{L^{1/4}},\\
L_{1}=L_{1}(L)&:=sL+\sqrt{s(1-s)}L^{1/4}\ell+\frac{x^{2}(1-s)}{4L^{1/2}},\\
L_{2}=L_{2}(L)&:=(1-s)L-\sqrt{s(1-s)}L^{1/4}\ell+\frac{x^{2}s}{4L^{1/2}}.\end{split}

(3.5)

In order to show Theorem
1.1
, it is sufficient to prove, by using Proposition
2.1
, the following asymptotic result holds uniformly for
x
and
\ell
on compact intervals

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{s(1-s)\mathrm{p}\left(L_{1},L_{2},X;s\right)}{f_{GUE}(L)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}

(3.6)

where

is the probability density function of

Inserting (

), we find that (

\mathrm{p}(L_{1},L_{2},X;s)=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}s(1-s)L}e^{-\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{2}+x^{2})}+o(1)\right),\quad\text{as}\quad L\to\infty

(3.7)

uniformly for
x
and
\ell
on compact intervals.

Recall the definition (
2.2
) of

. We have the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

With the scaling (

), we have

\oint_{0}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{z}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(1-\mathrm{z})^{2}}\mathrm{T}_{1,1}(\mathrm{z};L_{1},L_{2},X;s)=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}s(1-s)L}e^{-\frac{4}{3}L^{3/2}}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{2}+x^{2})}+o(1)\right)

(3.8)

as

.

Suppose

is fixed. With the scaling (

for all

&\quad+\oint_{|\mathrm{z}|=r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{z}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(1-\mathrm{z})^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3\\
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(k_{1}!k_{2}!)^{2}}\mathrm{T}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\mathrm{z};L_{1},L_{2},X;s).\end{split}

By Lemma

k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\end{subarray}}e^{-\frac{4(1-\epsilon)}{3}(sk_{1}+(1-s)k_{2})L^{3/2}}\end{split}

(3.11)

for sufficiently large

C^{\prime}=\oint_{|\mathrm{z}|=r}\frac{|\mathrm{d}\mathrm{z}|}{2\pi|1-\mathrm{z}|^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3\\
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(k_{1}!k_{2}!)^{2}}k_{1}^{k_{1}/2}k_{2}^{k_{2}/2}(k_{1}+k_{2})^{(k_{1}+k_{2})/2}C^{k_{1}+k_{2}}.

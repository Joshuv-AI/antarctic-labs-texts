Abstract
We consider a flavour conserving two Higgs doublet model that consists of a type I (or X) quark sector and a generalized lepton sector where the Yukawa couplings of the charged leptons to the new scalars are not proportional to the lepton masses. The model, previously proposed to solve both muon and electron g − 2 g-2 anomalies simultaneously, is also capable to accommodate the ATLAS excess in p ​ p → S → τ + ​ τ − pp\rightarrow S\rightarrow\tau^{+}\tau^{-} with gluon-gluon fusion production in the invariant mass range [0.2; 0.6] TeV, including all relevant low and high energy constraints. The excess is reproduced taking into account the new contributions from the scalar H, the pseudoscalar A, or both. In particular, detailed numerical analyses favoured the solution with a significant hierarchy among the vevs of the two Higgs doublets, t β ∼ 10 t_{\beta}\sim 10 , and light neutral scalars satisfying m A > m H m_{\mathrm{A}}>m_{\mathrm{H}} with sizable couplings to τ \tau leptons. In this region of the parameter space, the muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly receives one and two loop (Barr-Zee) contributions of similar size, while the electron anomaly is explained at two loops. An analogous ATLAS excess in b b -associated production and the CMS excess in ditop production are also studied. Further New Physics prospects concerning the anomalous magnetic moment of the τ \tau lepton and the implications of the CDF M W M_{W} measurement on the final results are discussed.

The model

The 2HDM Yukawa Lagrangian in the
Higgs basis

63

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}_{\rm Y}=&-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\bar{Q}_{L}\left(H_{1}{\rm M}_{d}+H_{2}{\rm N}_{d}\right){d}_{R}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\bar{Q}_{L}\left(\tilde{H}_{1}{\rm M}_{u}+\tilde{H}_{2}{\rm N}_{u}\right){u}_{R}\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\bar{L}_{L}\left(H_{1}{\rm M}_{\ell}+H_{2}{\rm N}_{\ell}\right){\ell}_{R}+\text{H.c.}\,,\end{split}

(3)

with
v^{2}=v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}=(\sqrt{2}G_{F})^{-1}\simeq(246\ \mathrm{GeV})^{2}
. The fermion fields in eq. (
3

(

{\rm N}_{u}=t_{\beta}^{-1}{\rm M}_{u},\quad{\rm N}_{d}=t_{\beta}^{-1}{\rm M}_{d},\quad{\rm N}_{\ell}=\text{diag}(n_{e},n_{\mu},n_{\tau})\,,

(4)

with

5

5

Henceforth,
t_{\beta}^{-1}\equiv\cot{\beta}

It is clear that our I-g
\ell
FC model consists of a type I (or type X) quark sector and a general Flavour Conserving lepton sector. The

The physical scalar-fermion interactions are obtained once the neutral scalars are rotated into their mass eigenstates, which are determined by the scalar potential. On that respect, we consider a CP conserving scalar sector shaped by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

All in all, the Yukawa couplings of neutral scalars read

\begin{split}\mathscr{L_{\rm neutral}}=&-\frac{m_{u_{j}}}{v}\left(s_{\alpha\beta}+c_{\alpha\beta}t_{\beta}^{-1}\right)\mathrm{h}\,\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}-\frac{m_{d_{j}}}{v}\left(s_{\alpha\beta}+c_{\alpha\beta}t_{\beta}^{-1}\right)\mathrm{h}\,\bar{d}_{j}d_{j}\\
&-\frac{m_{\ell_{j}}}{v}\left(s_{\alpha\beta}+c_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\text{Re}\left(n_{\ell_{j}}\right)}{m_{\ell_{j}}}\right)\mathrm{h}\,\bar{\ell}_{j}\ell_{j}\\
&-\frac{m_{u_{j}}}{v}\left(-c_{\alpha\beta}+s_{\alpha\beta}t_{\beta}^{-1}\right)\mathrm{H}\,\bar{u}_{j}u_{j}-\frac{m_{d_{j}}}{v}\left(-c_{\alpha\beta}+s_{\alpha\beta}t_{\beta}^{-1}\right)\mathrm{H}\,\bar{d}_{j}d_{j}\\
&-\frac{m_{\ell_{j}}}{v}\left(-c_{\alpha\beta}+s_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\text{Re}\left(n_{\ell_{j}}\right)}{m_{\ell_{j}}}\right)\mathrm{H}\,\bar{\ell}_{j}\ell_{j}\\
&+i\frac{m_{u_{j}}}{v}t_{\beta}^{-1}\mathrm{A}\,\bar{u}_{j}\gamma_{5}u_{j}-i\frac{m_{d_{j}}}{v}t_{\beta}^{-1}\mathrm{A}\,\bar{d}_{j}\gamma_{5}d_{j}-i\frac{\text{Re}\left(n_{\ell_{j}}\right)}{v}\mathrm{A}\,\bar{\ell}_{j}\gamma_{5}\ell_{j}\,,\\
\end{split}

(5)

which are flavour conserving, and those corresponding to charged scalars are

\begin{split}\mathscr{L_{\rm charged}}=&\ \frac{\mathrm{H}^{-}}{\sqrt{2}v}\,\bar{d}_{i}\,V_{ji}^{*}t_{\beta}^{-1}\left[(m_{u_{j}}-m_{d_{i}})+(m_{u_{j}}+m_{d_{i}})\gamma_{5}\right]u_{j}\\

\end{split}

(6)

with

The ATLAS excess in
pp\to S\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}

In
[
59
]
the ATLAS collaboration presented results concerning the search for heavy scalars decaying into two tau leptons over a mass range
[0.2;2.5]
TeV. Although overall the data do not disagree significantly from the background prediction of the SM, the observed upper limits on the production cross section times branching fraction are larger than the corresponding expectation for masses in the
[0.2;0.6]
TeV range. This happens in gluon-gluon fusion (ggF) production and in
b
-associated production. For the analyses presented in this work, we consider that an explanation of this excess is obtained when the production cross section times branching ratio
\sigma(pp\to S)\times\text{Br}\left(S\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)
for at least one
S
in
\{\mathrm{H},\mathrm{A}\}
has a value between the expected ATLAS bound and the observed ATLAS bound for the corresponding mass of the scalar, that is

[\text{Observed bound}]\geq\sigma(pp\to S)\times\text{Br}\left(S\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)\geq[\text{Expected bound}].

(7)

We may have (i) an explanation due to
\mathrm{H}
with
m_{\mathrm{H}}\in[0.2;0.6]
TeV without regard to
m_{\mathrm{A}}
, (ii) an explanation due to
\mathrm{A}
with
m_{\mathrm{A}}\in[0.2;0.6]
TeV without regard to
m_{\mathrm{H}}
. In addition, since (i) and (ii) are not mutually exclusive, we might even have “a double explanation”, that is two local excesses for both
m_{\mathrm{H}},m_{\mathrm{A}}\in[0.2;0.6]
TeV.
One may worry that for
m_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq m_{\mathrm{A}}
the approach is inconsistent on two respects. First, for a given production mode,
pp\to\mathrm{H}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
and
pp\to\mathrm{A}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
can in principle interfere, and then one should combine both processes at the amplitude level. As discussed in precedence,
\mathrm{H}
is a scalar and
\mathrm{A}
a pseudoscalar and thus there is no such interference. Second, although eq. (
7

S=\{\mathrm{H},\mathrm{A}\}

m_{\mathrm{H}}\simeq m_{\mathrm{A}}

(8)

,
\delta a_{\mu}
when

[

that for

is obtained from a combination of one loop and two loop contributions, while

is two loop dominated. Within the two loop contributions, the ones with virtual quarks are

6.1
Other LHC excesses

Besides the ATLAS excess in
\sigma(pp\to S)_{\rm[ggF]}\times\text{Br}\left(S\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)

•

For

\sim\frac{m_{f}}{v}

\frac{\sigma(pp\to S)_{\rm[ggF]}\times\text{Br}\left(S\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)}{\sigma(pp\to S)_{[b-{\rm assoc}]}\times\text{Br}\left(S\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)}=\frac{\sigma(pp\to S)_{\rm[ggF]}^{\rm SM}}{\sigma(pp\to S)_{[b-{\rm assoc}]}^{\rm SM}}\,.

Conclusions

The recent ATLAS excesses in the search of new scalar bosons decaying into a pair of
\tau
leptons, together with the
(g-2)_{e,\mu}
anomalies pointing towards lepton flavour universality violation, might require an extension of the SM scalar sector and a non-trivial flavour structure. Aiming to address these NP hints in a common framework, we consider a type I (or X) 2HDM with a modified lepton sector, one loop stable under renormalization, that leads to the breaking of lepton flavour universality beyond the mass proportionality. The ability of the model to solve the muon
g-2
anomaly together with the different scenarios one can consider for the electron anomaly, related to the Cs or the Rb determination of the fine structure constant, was successfully addressed in
[
54
]
and
[
55
]
. In this context, we further incorporate to the analyses the apparent disagreement between the observed upper limits on
\sigma(pp\rightarrow S)_{\mathrm{[ggF]}}\times\text{Br}\left(S\rightarrow\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)
and the corresponding expectation, in the invariant mass range [0.2; 0.6] TeV, presented by ATLAS. A detailed numerical analysis, taking into account all relevant low and high energy constraints, is performed. We obtain an explanation of the excess in terms of the scalar H contribution, the pseudoscalar A contribution, or both at the same time. In particular, the allowed regions in the parameter space include scalar masses
m_{\mathrm{H}}\in[0.2;0.6]
TeV and
m_{\mathrm{A}}\in[0.4;1.0]
TeV with
m_{\mathrm{A}}>m_{\mathrm{H}}
, values of
t_{\beta}\in[7;10]
and scalar couplings to
\tau
leptons larger than 120 GeV. In this region of the parameter space, the muon
g-2
anomaly receives important one loop H mediated contributions, together with similar size two loop contributions coming from Barr-Zee diagrams with a top quark or a tau lepton in the closed fermion loop. The Cs electron anomaly is explained at two loops with equally important contributions from virtual top quarks and tau leptons. The main results of our discussions would not be substantially altered if one had considered other scenarios for the electron anomaly. On the other hand, the analogous ATLAS excess in
b
-associated production and the CMS excess in ditop production cannot be accommodated in this very framework. Further NP prospects concerning the anomalous magnetic moment of the
\tau
lepton have been analysed. In particular, considering a light scalar H that couples significantly to
\tau
leptons, a one loop dominated contribution to the
(g-2)_{\tau}
anomaly of
\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
can be generated, which is within reach of the level of the expected sensitivity at Belle-II. Finally, the disagreement between the CDF measurement of
M_{W}
and its SM prediction is translated into a change in the oblique parameters
(\Delta S,\Delta T)\neq(0,0)
. However, this has no significant impact on the regions of interest to explain “the ATLAS excess”.

Appendix A

Model building

The general 2HDM is built as an extension of the SM where a second Higgs doublet is added. The corresponding Yukawa Lagrangian reads

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}_{\rm Y}=&-\bar{Q}_{L}^{0}\left(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}Y_{d1}+\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}Y_{d2}\right){d}_{R}^{0}-\bar{Q}_{L}^{0}\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}Y_{u1}+\tilde{\Phi}_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}Y_{u2}\right){u}_{R}^{0}\\
&-\bar{L}_{L}^{0}\left(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}Y_{\ell 1}+\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}Y_{\ell 2}\right){\ell}_{R}^{0}+\text{H.c.}\,,\end{split}

(13)

where
\tilde{\Phi}_{j}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\equiv i\sigma_{2}\Phi_{j}^{\ast}
. Electroweak spontaneous symmetry breaking is obtained with non-zero vevs

\langle 0|\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}|0\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\langle 0|\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}|0\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(14)

and thus the fields can be expanded around the vacuum as

\Phi_{j}^{\phantom{\dagger}}=e^{i\theta_{j}}\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{j}^{+}\\
(v_{j}+\rho_{j}+i\eta_{j})/\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(15)

It is always possible to rotate the scalar fields into a basis where just one of the doublets presents a non-vanishing vev. This is the so-called Higgs basis
[
61
,
62
,
63
]
defined by

\begin{pmatrix}H_{1}\\
H_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{R}_{\beta}^{\phantom{T}}\,\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\theta_{1}}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\\
e^{-i\theta_{2}}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\quad\mathcal{R}_{\beta}^{\phantom{T}}=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}c_{\beta}&s_{\beta}\\
-s_{\beta}&c_{\beta}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(16)

where
c_{\beta}\equiv\cos\beta\equiv v_{1}/v
and
s_{\beta}\equiv\sin\beta\equiv v_{2}/v
. The ratio among the vevs is given by
t_{\beta}\equiv\tan\beta=v_{2}/v_{1}
and
v^{2}=v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}\simeq(246\ \mathrm{GeV})^{2}
. In the Higgs basis, the Yukawa Lagrangian

\begin{split}\mathscr{L}_{\rm Y}=&-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\bar{Q}_{L}^{0}\left(H_{1}{\rm M}_{d}^{0}+H_{2}{\rm N}_{d}^{0}\right){d}_{R}^{0}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\bar{Q}_{L}^{0}\left(\tilde{H}_{1}{\rm M}_{u}^{0}+\tilde{H}_{2}{\rm N}_{u}^{0}\right){u}_{R}^{0}\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{v}\bar{L}_{L}^{0}\left(H_{1}{\rm M}_{\ell}^{0}+H_{2}{\rm N}_{\ell}^{0}\right){\ell}_{R}^{0}+\text{H.c.}\,\end{split}

(17)

has the simplest interpretation:
{\rm M}_{f}^{0}{}
are the non-diagonal fermion mass matrices, coupled to the only Higgs doublet that acquires a vev, and
{\rm N}_{f}^{0}{}
are a new set of flavour structures for the different fermions
f=d,\,u,\,\ell
.

In the quark sector the model is shaped by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry which transforms

\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\mapsto\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\ \Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\mapsto-\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\ {d}_{R}^{0}\mapsto-{d}_{R}^{0},\ {u}_{R}^{0}\mapsto-{u}_{R}^{0},\ {Q}_{L}^{0}\mapsto{Q}_{L}^{0}.

(18)

This corresponds to a type I 2HDM (in a type II 2HDM, one would have
{d}_{R}^{0}\mapsto{d}_{R}^{0}
in eq. (
18
)), and leads to
Y_{d1}=0
,
Y_{u1}=0
and

{\rm N}_{d}^{0}=t_{\beta}^{-1}{\rm M}_{d}^{0}\,,\quad{\rm N}_{u}^{0}=t_{\beta}^{-1}{\rm M}_{u}^{0}\,.

(19)

On the other hand, in the lepton sector we only require that
Y_{\ell 1}
and
Y_{\ell 2}
are simultaneously bidiagonalized with the resulting diagonal
{\rm N}_{\ell}^{0}
arbitrary. This condition cannot be enforced by a symmetry. However, these requirements on the Yukawa sector turn out to be respected by one loop RGE, and thus scalar flavour changing neutral couplings do not arise at this level.

In the Higgs basis, the expansion of the doublets around the vevs reads

H_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}G^{+}\\
\frac{v+{H}^{0}+iG^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{pmatrix},\quad H_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{H}^{+}\\
\frac{{R}^{0}+i{I}^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(20)

We can readily identify the would-be Goldstone bosons
G^{\pm}
and
G^{0}
, the new physical charged Higgs fields
\mathrm{H}^{\pm}
and three neutral scalars
\{{H}^{0},\,{R}^{0},\,{I}^{0}\}
that are rotated into the mass eigenstates by

\begin{pmatrix}{H}^{0}\\
{R}^{0}\\
{I}^{0}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{R}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{h}\\
\mathrm{H}\\
\mathrm{A}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(21)

where the matrix

\mathcal{R}=\begin{pmatrix}s_{\alpha\beta}&-c_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
c_{\alpha\beta}&\phantom{-}s_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,

(22)

is block diagonal if we assume that there is no CP violation in the scalar sector. On that respect, we recall that the scalar potential is shaped by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry that is softly broken. As previously mentioned,
s_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\sin(\alpha+\beta)
and
c_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\cos(\alpha+\beta)
, being
\pi/2-\alpha
the mixing angle that parametrizes the change of basis from the fields in eq. (
15
) to the mass eigenstates in eq. (
21
).

The scalar potential is

V(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})=\mu_{11}^{2}\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\mu_{22}^{2}\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\mu_{12}^{2}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}}+\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})+\lambda_{1}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}+\lambda_{2}(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}\\
+2\lambda_{3}(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})+2\lambda_{4}(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})+\lambda_{5}((\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}+(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}})^{2}),

(23)

where
\mu_{ij}^{2}
,
ij=11,22,12
, and
\lambda_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,5
are real. As mentioned,
V(\Phi_{1}^{\phantom{\dagger}},\Phi_{2}^{\phantom{\dagger}})
is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric (see eq. (
18
)), except for the soft breaking term with
\mu_{12}^{2}
. Instead of the 8 parameters
\{\mu_{ij}^{2},\lambda_{j}\}
in eq. (
23
), it is convenient to use
v
,
m_{\mathrm{h}}
,
m_{\mathrm{H}}
,
m_{\mathrm{A}}
,
m_{\mathrm{H}^{\pm}}
,
t_{\beta}
,
c_{\alpha\beta}
and
\mu_{12}^{2}
, and fix
v\simeq 246
GeV,
m_{\mathrm{h}}\simeq 125
GeV (
\mathrm{h}
is the SM-like Higgs). The remaining free parameters of the model, necessary for the numerical analysis, are just
n_{e}
,
n_{\mu}
and
n_{\tau}
.

Unit Reducible Fields and Perfect Unary Forms
Alar Leibak, Christian Porter, Cong Ling

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notion of unit reducibility for number fields, that is, number fields in which all positive unary forms attain their nonzero minimum at a unit. Furthermore, we investigate the link between unit reducibility and the number of homothety classes of perfect unary forms for a given number field, and prove an open conjecture about the number of classes of perfect unary forms in real quadratic fields, stated by D. Yasaki.

Introduction

Let
K
be a real algebraic number field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Associate to
K
the canonical embeddings
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}
into
\mathbb{R}
. A quadratic form
f:K^{m}\to K
, defined by

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\sum_{k,l=1}^{m}f_{kl}x_{k}x_{l},\quad f_{kl}=f_{lk}\in K,

of rank
m
is said to be positive definite if

\sigma_{i}(f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\sum_{k,l}\sigma_{i}(f_{kl})x_{k}x_{l}

is positive definite for each
i=1,\dots,n
. Throughout the paper, we will simply refer to positive definite quadratic forms as quadratic forms, unless we need to clarify.

If
f
is defined by a single variable in
K
, the corresponding quadratic form is called a
unary quadratic form
. If
f
generates a positive-definite quadratic form, we say that the element
f
is
totally positive
. We denote by
K_{>>0}
the set of totally positive elements of
K
, i.e
K_{>>0}=\{a\in K\ |\ \sigma_{i}(a)>0,\ i=1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\omega_{1}=1,\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{n}
be an integral basis of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Note that for all
a\in K_{>>0}
,

\displaystyle q_{a}:K\to\mathbb{Q},\hskip 5.69054pt

\displaystyle q_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a(x_{1}\omega_{1}+x_{2}\omega_{2}+\ldots+x_{n}\omega_{n})^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a\omega_{k}\omega_{l})x_{k}x_{l}

corresponds to an
n
-dimensional quadratic form with rational coefficients, known as the trace quadratic form. For a totally positive
a\in K
, we will denote by

\mu(a)=\min_{x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus\{0\}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})=\min_{(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}q_{a}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),

and
\mathcal{M}(a)=\{x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}:\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})=\mu(a)\}
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
denote the unit group of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. We say that a unary form defined by
a\in K_{>>0}
is
reduced
if
\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\leq\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(au^{2})
, for all
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. We denote the set of all
a\in K_{>>0}
such that
a
corresponds to a reduced unary form by
\mathcal{F}_{K}
, and call this set the
reduction domain
of unary forms in
K
. Note that every unary form is equivalent to a reduced unary form.

Finally, we give the following important definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
K
be a totally real number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and unit group
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
. We say that
K
is
unit reducible
if, for all
a\in K_{>>0}
,

\displaystyle\mu(a)=\min_{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(au^{2}).

Unit reducibility is a useful property in the study of lattice-based cryptography
[
10
]
. The security of many lattice-based cryptosystems is underpinned by the so-called “shortest vector problem” (SVP), which asks the adversary to find a shortest nonzero vector of a lattice given an arbitrary lattice basis. Lattices defined over number fields are increasingly used in lattice-based cryptography due to their efficiency. Lattice reduction over number fields is a common strategy to find short vectors in such lattices
[
5
,
7
]
. Some lattices are constructed from principal ideals of number fields
[
3
]
, and so the problem translates to finding the shortest nonzero arithmetical value of a unary quadratic form. When the field is unit reducible, this suggests that finding the smallest arithmetical nonzero value of a unary quadratic form corresponds also to finding the shortest generator of the principal ideal. Although at high dimensions the shortest generator is unlikely to be a shortest vector of a principal ideal lattice, this can be true at small dimensions. Finding unit reducible number fields can lead to algorithms of lattice reduction at high dimensions, the latter being an ongoing subject of study.

A unary form
ax^{2}
is said to be
perfect
if it is uniquely determined by
\mu(a),\mathcal{M}(a)
. It is immediately clear that if
ax^{2}
is perfect, then for any
\lambda>0\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\mu(\lambda a)=\lambda\mu(a)
,
\mathcal{M}(\lambda a)=\mathcal{M}(a)
, and so perfect unary forms can be considered by their homothety classes. We denote by
n_{K}
the number of
\text{GL}_{1}(\mathcal{O})
-inequivalent homothety classes of perfect forms of a real number field
K
. In this paper, we establish a method of determining the number of classes of perfect forms by studying unit reducible fields. The main result of the paper is as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
be a real quadratic field for some positive, square-free integer

not equal to

. Then
n_{K}=1
if and only if
d
is of one of the following types:

•

T1
:
d=m^{2}+1
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m
odd,

•

T2
:
d=m^{2}-1
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m
even,

•

T3
:
d=m^{2}+4
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m
odd,

•

T4
:
d=m^{2}-4
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

Theorem 3

2.1
Real quadratic fields

Let

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}=\begin{cases}&4d\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptd\equiv 2,3\mod 4,\\
&d\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptd\equiv 1\mod 4\end{cases},

\displaystyle\omega=\begin{cases}&\sqrt{d}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptd\equiv 2,3\mod 4,\\
&\frac{1+\sqrt{d}}{2}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptd\equiv 1\mod 4\end{cases}.

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(x_{1}+x_{2}\sqrt{d})=x_{1}-x_{2}\sqrt{d},

Lemma 4

(
[
8
]
, Lemma 10)
.

Let
K
be a real quadratic field and denote by

is reduced if and only if

\displaystyle\emph{\text{Tr}}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\leq\emph{\text{Tr}}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(au^{2}),

\displaystyle\emph{\text{Tr}}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\leq\emph{\text{Tr}}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(au^{-2}).

Using the notation in Lemma
4
, set
a=a_{1}+a_{2}\sqrt{d}\in K_{>>0}
,
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}

, so

is reduced if and only if

\displaystyle 2a_{1}\leq 2u_{1}a_{1}-2du_{2}|a_{2}|,

and so rearranging gives

\displaystyle\frac{|a_{2}|}{a_{1}}\leq\frac{u_{1}-1}{du_{2}}.

We are now equipped to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

A real quadratic field

1

Proof.

Let
a=a_{1}+a_{2}\sqrt{d}

a
is reduced and that
a_{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\leq\frac{u_{1}-1}{du_{2}},

(1)

where

is the fundamental unit of
K
satisfying
u>1

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a(x_{1}+x_{2}\sqrt{d})^{2})=2(x_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})a_{1}+4dx_{1}x_{2}a_{2}.

Assume first that
K
is of the type
T_{1}
or
T_{2}

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\setminus\{0\}}\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(ax^{2})=\min_{(0,0)\neq(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\{2(x_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})a_{1}+4dx_{1}x_{2}a_{2}\right\}\triangleq 2g(x_{1},x_{2}).

Now, set
h(x_{1},x_{2})=g(x_{1}-kx_{2},x_{2})

\displaystyle h(x_{1},x_{2})=a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+2(da_{2}-ka_{1})x_{1}x_{2}+((k^{2}+d)a_{1}-2dka_{2})x_{2}^{2}.

\displaystyle 2|da_{2}-ka_{1}|\leq(k^{2}+d)a_{1}-2dka_{2}.

(2)

In turn, proving that

\displaystyle h(x_{1},x_{2})=a_{1}x_{1}^{2}+(a_{1}+da_{2}-2ka_{1})x_{1}x_{2}+\left(\left(\frac{1+d}{4}+k^{2}\right)a_{1}+\frac{d}{2}a_{2}-k(a_{1}+da_{2})\right)x_{2}^{2}.

\displaystyle a_{1}+da_{2}-2ka_{1}\leq\left(\frac{1+d}{4}+k^{2}\right)a_{1}+\frac{d}{2}a_{2}-k(a_{1}+da_{2}),

Lemma 6

Let
a=a_{1}+a_{2}\sqrt{d}\in K_{>>0}

\displaystyle\frac{|a_{2}|}{a_{1}}\leq\frac{\tanh(R_{K})}{\sqrt{d}}

\displaystyle(a_{1}+a_{2}\sqrt{d})e^{2R_{K}}+(a_{1}-a_{2}\sqrt{d})e^{-2R_{K}}\geq a_{1}+a_{2}\sqrt{d}+a_{1}-a_{2}\sqrt{d}=2a_{1},

\displaystyle a_{1}(\cosh(2R_{K})-1)+a_{2}\sqrt{d}\sinh(2R_{K})\geq 0,

\displaystyle a_{1}(\cosh(2R_{K})-1)-a_{2}\sqrt{d}\sinh(2R_{K})\geq 0,

\displaystyle a_{1}(\cosh(2R_{K})-1)\geq|a_{2}|\sqrt{d}\sinh(2R_{K}).

The inequality in the statement of the lemma immediately follows.

\displaystyle\text{Vol}(\mathcal{S})=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\frac{d}{4}\left(1-\frac{a_{2}^{2}d}{a_{1}^{2}}\right)}},

and so if

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{d}{4}\left(1-\frac{a_{2}^{2}d}{a_{1}^{2}}\right)}\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}\sqrt{1-\tanh(R_{K})^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{K}}}{2\cosh(R_{K})},

and so if inequality (
8
) holds,

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}>\frac{\sqrt{\Delta_{K}}}{2\cosh(R_{K})},

as required.

We have therefore verified that if inequality (
4
) holds, then the field

is not unit reducible, for any square-free positive integer

. Let

denote the fundamental unit of

. Then

\displaystyle 2\cosh(R_{K})=e^{R_{K}}+e^{-R_{K}}=v_{1}+v_{2}\sqrt{d}+\frac{1}{v_{1}+v_{2}\sqrt{d}}=\begin{cases}&2v_{1},\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u)=1,\\
&2v_{2}\sqrt{d}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{if}\hskip 5.69054pt\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u)=-1\end{cases},

so that inequality (

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}>\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2v_{1}},\hskip 5.69054pt&\text{if}\hskip 5.69054ptd\equiv 1\mod 4,\hskip 2.84526pt\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u)=1;\\

Unit Reducibility in General Totally Real Number Fields

Throughout this section, for any totally real number field
K
, we will denote by
K_{\mathbb{R}}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}
. It is easy to see that
K_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=n
. Though we have so far focused on specific families of number fields, we may also ascertain some results for arbitrary number fields.

Definition 3.1
.

A
lattice

\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If

\displaystyle\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{Z}\mathbf{b}_{i},

for some
\mathbf{b}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
m\leq n
. We say that
\Lambda
is full-rank if
m=n
, and we will assume that all lattices are full-rank from now on. We will denote by
\det(\Lambda)
the absolute value of the determinant of the matrix composed of the basis vectors of
\Lambda
. Denote by

\displaystyle J_{p}\triangleq\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|\mathbf{x}\|_{p}\leq 1\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denotes the
l_{p}
norm of an element in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we define the
i
th successive minima with respect to the
l_{p}
-norm of a lattice
\Lambda
by

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{(p)}(\Lambda)\triangleq\argmin_{\lambda>0}\{\lambda J_{p}\hskip 2.84526pt\text{contains $i$ linearly independent lattice vectors.}\}

When considering the
l_{2}
norm, we will simply write
\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
instead of
\lambda_{i}^{(2)}(\Lambda)
.

From Hadamard’s inequality one can show the following lemma:

Lemma 9
.

For all full-rank lattices
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)\geq\det(\Lambda)^{1/n}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{b}_{1},\mathbf{b}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{b}_{n}
denote the basis for
\Lambda
, and denote by
\mathbf{b}_{i}(i)
the vector
\mathbf{b}_{i}
after being orthogonalised with respect to the space generated by
\mathbf{b}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{b}_{i-1}
, for all
1<i\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\det(\Lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|\mathbf{b}_{i}(i)\|,

where
\mathbf{b}_{1}(1)=\mathbf{b}_{1}
. Assume that

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{(i)}\mathbf{b}_{j},

for some
x_{j}^{(i)}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
, for all
1\leq i\leq n
. We may assume without loss of generality that the
\mathbf{b}_{i}
are arranged so that the largest
j
such that
x_{j}^{(i)}
is nonzero is greater than or equal to
i
, otherwise we may readjust the order until this holds, since
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
must be linearly independent (e.g. we cannot have three
\mathbf{v}_{i}
expressible as the nonzero integer sum of only two
\mathbf{b}_{i}
, or else they would not be linearly independent, which is a contradiction). Now, suppose that for some
i
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}^{(i)}\mathbf{b}_{j},

where
i<k\leq n
and
x_{k}^{(i)}\neq 0

Lemma 10

Let

Theorem 12

Suppose that

Suppose that

\sqrt{\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)}

, where
\mathcal{B}_{n}
denotes the ball of unit radius. By Minkowski’s theorem, since the volume of the lattice generated by

is
\sqrt{|\Delta_{K}|\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)}
,

is not unit reducible if we can find an

and so we need

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)>\frac{4}{\pi}|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\text{Nm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a)^{\frac{1}{n}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{2}{n}},

(11)

for some

Asymptotics of the deformed higher order Airy-kernel determinants and applications
Jun Xia, Yi-Fan Hao, Shuai-Xia Xu, Lun Zhang, Yu-Qiu Zhao

Abstract
We study the one-parameter family of Fredholm determinants det ( I − ρ 2 ​ 𝒦 n , x ) \det(I-\rho^{2}\mathcal{K}_{n,x}) , ρ ∈ ℝ \rho\in\mathbb{R} , where 𝒦 n , x \mathcal{K}_{n,x} stands for the integral operator acting on L 2 ​ ( x , + ∞ ) L^{2}(x,+\infty) with the higher order Airy kernel. This family of determinants represents a new universal class of distributions which is a higher order analogue of the classical Tracy-Widom distribution. Each of the determinants admits an integral representation in terms of a special real solution to the n n -th member of the Painlevé II hierarchy. Using the Riemann-Hilbert approach, we establish asymptotics of the determinants and the associated higher order Painlevé II transcendents as x → − ∞ x\to-\infty for 0 < | ρ | < 1 0<|\rho|<1 and | ρ | > 1 |\rho|>1 , respectively. In the case of 0 < | ρ | < 1 0<|\rho|<1 , we are able to calculate the constant term in the asymptotic expansion of the determinants, while for | ρ | > 1 |\rho|>1 , the relevant asymptotics exhibit singular behaviors. Applications of our results are also discussed, which particularly include asymptotic statistical properties of the counting function for the random point process defined by the higher order Airy kernel. 2010 mathematics subject classification: 33E17; 34E05; 34M55; 41A60 Keywords and phrases: Painlevé II hierarchy, higher order Airy point processes, Fredholm determinants, asymptotic expansions, large gap asymptotics, Riemann-Hilbert approach

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with a family of integral kernels defined through the following double contour integral:

K_{n}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\gamma_{R}}\int_{\gamma_{L}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{(-1)^{n+1}(P_{2n+1}(u)-P_{2n+1}(v))-xu+yv}}{u-v}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v,~~x,y\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

where

P_{2n+1}(z)=\frac{z^{2n+1}}{2n+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\tau_{k}}{2k+1}z^{2k+1},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2)

is an odd polynomial of degree
2n+1
, the contours
\gamma_{R}
and
\gamma_{L}
are symmetric with respect to the imaginary axis and are asymptotic to the straight lines with arguments
\pm\frac{n}{2n+1}\pi
at infinity; see Figure
1
for an illustration. We call
K_{n}
the higher order Airy kernels as it reduces to the prominent Airy kernel if
n=1
. Moreover, if
P_{2n+1}
in (
1.1
) is a monomial
z^{2n+1}/(2n+1)
,
K_{n}
is equal to the generalized Airy kernel
[
35
]
defined by

K_{\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}(x+s)\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}(y+s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(1.3)

where

\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\cos\left(\frac{s^{2n+1}}{2n+1}+xs\right)\mathrm{d}s

(1.4)

is the higher order Airy function and satisfies the ordinary differential equation

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2n}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2n}}y(x)=(-1)^{n+1}xy(x).

Figure 1:
The contours
\gamma_{R}
and
\gamma_{L}
in the definition (
1.1
) of
K_{n}
.

Determinantal point processes associated with the kernels
K_{n}
are believed to be universal objects in describing local statistics for a variety of interacting particle systems arising from random matrix theory and statistical physics. The Airy process characterizes the limiting distribution of the normalized largest eigenvalues for a large class of random matrices
[
37
]
, while the generalized Airy process corresponding to the kernel
K_{\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}}
describes the multicritical edge statistics for the momenta of fermions trapped in a non-harmonic potential
[
35
]
. We also refer to
[
32
,
33
]
for the connections between point processes of higher order Airy kernels and random partitions obeying the Schur measure.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n,x}
be the integral operator acting on
L^{2}(x,+\infty)
with the higher order Airy kernel
K_{n}
. A central object of the present work is the Fredholm determinants

\displaystyle F_{n}(x;\rho):=\det(I-\rho^{2}\mathcal{K}_{n,x}),\qquad\rho\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.5)

From the theory of determinantal point processes
[
30
,
41
]
, it follows that
F_{n}(x;\pm 1)
is the probability distribution of the largest particle
in the process. If
|\rho|<1
, the deformed determinant
F_{n}(x;\rho)
can be interpreted as the probability distribution of the largest particle in the associated thinned process. The thinned process is obtained from the original one by removing each particle independently with probability
1-\rho^{2}
, and is a classical operation in the studies of point processes; cf.
[
5
,
29
]
. It has been shown in
[
11
]
that
F_{n}(x;\rho)
indeed defines a distribution function for
|\rho|\leq 1
.

A celebrated result of Tracy and Widom established in
[
42
]
shows that the Airy-kernel determinant
F_{1}(x;\pm 1)
admits an integral representation in terms of the Hastings-McLeod solution
[
26
]
of the Painlevé II equation

\displaystyle q^{\prime\prime}(x)=2q^{3}(x)+xq(x).

(1.6)

The same integral formula holds for the deformed Airy-kernel determinant
F_{1}(x;\rho)
,
|\rho|<1
, but involves the Ablowitz-Segur solution
[
1
,
39
]
of (
1.6
) therein; cf.
[
4
,
6
]
. Analogous results have been obtained in
[
11
]
for higher order Airy-kernel determinants
F_{n}
, where one instead encounters the Painlevé II hierarchy. The Painlevé II hierarchy is obtained from
the mKdV hierarchy via a self-similar reduction
[
24
]
; see also
[
16
,
34
,
36
]
. It is defined by a sequence of ordinary differential equations and the
n
-th member is given by

\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}+2q\right)\mathcal{L}_{n}\left[q^{\prime}-q^{2}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\tau_{i}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}+2q\right)\mathcal{L}_{i}\left[q^{\prime}-q^{2}\right]=xq,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.7)

where the prime denotes the derivative with respect to
x
, the parameters
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}
are real, and
\mathcal{L}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, stands for the Lenard operator defined via the recursive relation

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\mathcal{L}_{k+1}h=\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{~d}x^{3}}+4h\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}+2h^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{L}_{k}h,\quad\mathcal{L}_{0}h=\frac{1}{2},\quad\mathcal{L}_{k}0=0.

(1.8)

The Painlevé II hierarchy (
1.7
) includes the Painlevé II equation (
1.6
) as the first member, and the second member is

q^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)=10q(x)(q^{\prime}(x))^{2}+10q^{2}(x)q^{\prime\prime}(x)-6q^{5}(x)-\tau_{1}(q^{\prime\prime}(x)-2q^{3}(x))+xq(x).

(1.9)

By
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
, it comes out that the determinant
F_{n}
is directly related to the Painlevé II hierarchy via the relation

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}\ln F_{n}(x;\rho)=-q_{n}^{2}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right),

(1.10)

where
q_{n}\left(x;\rho\right)=q_{n}(x;\rho;\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1})
is a
real solution for the
n
-th member of (
1.7
) with asymptotic behavior

q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)=O\left(\exp\left(-Cx^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}\right)\right)\quad\mathrm{as}~~x\to+\infty,

(1.11)

for some
C>0
.
Here the error term is uniform for
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
in any compact subset of the real axis and
q_{n}(x;\rho)
is pole-free on real axis for
|\rho|\leq 1
.
If
\tau_{1}=\cdots=\tau_{n-1}=0
, a refinement of (
1.11
) is

q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)=\rho\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}(x)(1+o(1))\quad\mathrm{as}~~x\to+\infty,

(1.12)

where
\mathrm{Ai}_{2n+1}
is defined in (
1.4
); see
[
11
, Equation (1.13)]
. It is worth noting that although the formulas (
1.10
)–(
1.12
) are derived in
[
11
]
for
|\rho|\leq 1
, the results can be generalized to all real
\rho
without any difficulty. If
|\rho|\leq 1
,
q_{n}(x;\rho)
is a natural generalization of the Hastings-McLeod solution (for
|\rho|=1
) or the
Ablowitz-Segur solution (for
|\rho|<1
) to the Painlevé II equation (
1.6
), which is pole free on the real axis. By integrating
(
1.10
) twice, one further obtains the following
Tracy-Widom type formula

F_{n}(x;\rho)=\exp\left\{-\int_{x}^{+\infty}(s-x)q_{n}^{2}\left((-1)^{n+1}s;\rho\right)\mathrm{d}s\right\}.

(1.13)

This result has been extended to several disjoint intervals in
[
12
]
, which is related to a vector-valued Painlevé II hierarchy. We also refer to
[
7
]
for the studies of higher order Airy process at finite temperature, where the law is governed by a Painlevé II integro-differential hierarchy.

The main aim of this paper is to derive asymptotic behaviors of
q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)
and
F_{n}(x;\rho)
as
x\to-\infty
for
|\rho|\neq 1
. If
|\rho|=1

[

]
. More precisely, by
[

q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;1\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{2n}\theta_{i}|x|^{\frac{1-2i}{2n}}+\frac{\mathsf{d}_{n}}{2\theta_{0}}|x|^{-2-\frac{1}{2n}}+O\left(|x|^{-2-\frac{1}{n}}\right),

and

F_{n}(x;\pm 1)=\mathsf{C}_{n}|x|^{\mathsf{d}_{n}}\exp\left(-\sum_{j=0,j\neq n+1}^{2n}\frac{n^{2}}{(n+1-j)(2n+1-j)}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\theta_{i}\theta_{j-i}|x|^{\frac{2n-j+1}{n}}\right)\left(1+o(1)\right),

where

\mathsf{d}_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\frac{1}{8},&\hbox{$n=1$,}\\
-\frac{1}{2},&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

\theta_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}2n\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-\frac{1}{n}}
,
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1},n)
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, are constants depending explicitly on
n
and the parameters
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1}
(see
[
11
, Equation (1.16)]
), and

is an undetermined constant. As we will see in what follows, the asymptotics of

be the determinant defined in (
1.5
). If
\rho>1
, we have, as
x\to-\infty
,

\displaystyle q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}}{\sin\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{2na_{k}}{1+2(n-k)}|x|^{\frac{1+2(n-k)}{2n}}-\frac{2n+1}{2n}\kappa\ln|x|+\varphi\right)}+O\left(|x|^{-\frac{1}{2n}}\right),

(2.19)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\ln F_{n}(x;\rho)

\displaystyle=\Bigg[2\kappa-\cot\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{2na_{k}|x|^{\frac{1+2(n-k)}{2n}}}{1+2(n-k)}-\frac{2n+1}{2n}\kappa\ln|x|+\varphi\right)\Bigg]|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}+O\left(|x|^{-\frac{1}{2n}}\right),

(2.20)

where
a_{k}
,
k=0,\ldots,n
, are defined in (
2.2
), the parameters
\kappa
and
\varphi
are related to
\rho
by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\kappa=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(\rho^{2}-1),\\
&\varphi=-\kappa\ln(8n)+\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\kappa\right)+\frac{\pi}{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.21)

The error terms in the asymptotic expansions are uniformly valid for
x
bounded away from the singularities appearing on the right-hand sides of (
2.19
) and (
2.20
).

Remark 2.10
.

In the case that
\tau_{1}=\cdots=\tau_{n-1}=0
, the error terms in (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) can be improved to be

\displaystyle q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}}{\sin\left(\frac{2n}{2n+1}|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}-\frac{2n+1}{2n}\kappa\ln|x|+\varphi\right)}+O\left(|x|^{-1}\right),

(2.22)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\ln F_{n}(x;\rho)

\displaystyle=\Bigg[2\kappa-\cot\left(\frac{2n}{2n+1}|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}-\frac{2n+1}{2n}\kappa\ln|x|+\varphi\right)\Bigg]|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}+O\left(|x|^{-1}\right).

(2.23)

If
n=1
, the results (
2.22
) and (
2.21
) were first obtained in
[
31
]
using the isomonodromy method; see also
[
9
]
.

It is readily seen from the asymptotic formula (
2.19
) that
q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)
possesses a sequence of real simple poles clustering at negative infinity. As an application, we could approximate the locations of these poles as stated below.

Corollary 2.11
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.9
and
with

\tau_{1}=\cdots=\tau_{n-1}=0
, the function
q_{n}\left((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho\right)
has infinitely many simple poles
\{p_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
on the real axis, such that

\displaystyle p_{m}=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{2n+1}{2n}\pi\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}m^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}\bigg[1+\frac{2n\kappa}{(2n+1)\pi}\frac{\ln{m}}{m}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2n}{(2n+1)\pi}\left(\kappa\ln\frac{(2n+1)\pi}{2n}-\varphi\right)\frac{1}{m}+O\left(\frac{\ln^{2}m}{m^{2}}\right)\bigg],\quad m\to\infty,

(2.24)

where
\kappa
and
\varphi
are given by (
2.21
).

Following the proof of Corollary
2.11
, we also have

p_{m}=-\left(\frac{2n+1}{2n}\pi\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}m^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}+O\left(m^{\frac{2(n-1)}{2n+1}}\right),

for general parameters
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
. More terms in the expansion can also be obtained but the expression is more complicated
and we do not pursue this here.

The rest of this paper is devoted to the proofs of our main results, which is based on a Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent analysis
[

,
22
,
23
]
of the associated Riemann-Hilbert (RH) problem. In Section

in (

and

are carried out in Sections

3

and

The starting point of our analysis is the following RH problem for the Painlevé II hierarchy (

RH problem for
\Psi

(1)
\Psi(z):=\Psi(z,x;\rho)
is analytic for

, where
\Gamma_{+}
and
\Gamma_{-}
are symmetric with respect to the real line, and

\infty\mathrm{e}^{\frac{4n+1}{4n+2}\pi i}
and ends at
\infty\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{4n+2}\pi i}
; see Figure
2
for an illustration.

Figure 2:
The jump contours
\Gamma_{+}
and
\Gamma_{-}
of the RH problem for
\Psi

(2)
\Psi(z)
satisfies the jump condition

\Psi_{+}(z)=\Psi_{-}(z)J_{\Psi}(z),

where

J_{\Psi}(z)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{2\theta(z)}&1\end{pmatrix},&&z\in\Gamma_{+},\\
&\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\theta(z)}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},&&z\in\Gamma_{-},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

with

\displaystyle\theta(z):

\displaystyle=\theta(z;x,\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1})=i\frac{(2z)^{2n+1}}{4n+2}+i\sum^{n-1}_{j=1}\frac{(-1)^{n+j}\tau_{j}}{4j+2}(2z)^{2j+1}+ixz

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2}P_{2n+1}(2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi i}{2}}z)+ixz,

(3.2)

and where
P_{2n+1}
is defined in (

).

(3) As
z\to\infty
, we have

\Psi(z)=I+\frac{\Psi_{1}(x)}{z}+O\left(z^{-2}\right)

(3.3)

for some function
\Psi_{1}
.

Recall that
q_{n}
is the solution of the
n
-th member of the Painlevé II hierarchy (
1.7
) determined through (
1.10
). It is related to the above RH problem via the formulas

q_{n}(x;\rho)=\begin{cases}2i\left(\Psi_{1}(x)\right)_{12}=-2i\left(\Psi_{1}(x)\right)_{21},&\text{if}~n~\text{is~odd},\\
2i\left(\Psi_{1}(-x)\right)_{12}=-2i\left(\Psi_{1}(-x)\right)_{21},&\text{if}~n~\text{is~even}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

where
\Psi_{1}
is the coefficient of
1/z
in (
3.3
) and
A_{ij}
stands for the
(i,j)
-th entry of a matrix
A
; see
[
11
, Equation (2.27)]
. Note that
\Psi
satisfies the symmetry relation

\sigma_{3}\Psi(z,x;\rho)\sigma_{3}=\Psi(z,x;-\rho),\qquad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

(3.5)

it is then easily seen from (
3.4
) that
q_{n}(x;-\rho)=-q_{n}(x;\rho)
, as claimed in (
2.3
).

We also have the following connections between the Fredholm determinant
F_{n}
defined in (
1.5
) and the RH problem for
\Psi
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The Fredholm determinant
F_{n}(x;\rho)
satisfies the following differential identities:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\ln{F_{n}(x;\rho)}

\displaystyle=2i(\Psi_{1}(x))_{11},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\ln{F_{n}(x;\rho)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Psi^{-1}(z)\Psi^{\prime}(z)(J_{\Psi}(z)-I)\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i},

(3.7)

where
\Psi_{1}
is defined in (
3.3
) and
J_{\Psi}
is given in (
3.1
).

Proof.

The identity (
3.6
) follows directly from
[
11
, Equations (2.19) and (2.25)]
. To show (
3.7
), we see from the results in
[
2
, Section 5.1]
that

\partial_{\rho}\ln{F_{n}(x;\rho)}=\int_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Psi^{-1}_{-}(z)\Psi^{\prime}_{-}(z)\partial_{\rho}(J_{\Psi}(z))J_{\Psi}^{-1}(z)\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i}.

(3.8)

In view of
J_{\Psi}
in (
3.1
), it is readily seen that

\partial_{\rho}(J_{\Psi}(z))J_{\Psi}^{-1}(z)=\frac{1}{\rho}(J_{\Psi}(z)-I),

which implies

\partial_{\rho}\ln{F_{n}(x;\rho)}=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Psi_{-}^{-1}(z)\Psi_{-}^{\prime}(z)(J_{\Psi}(z)-I)\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i}.

We could remove the subscript
-
in the above formula by noting that

\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Psi_{-}^{-1}(z)\Psi_{-}^{\prime}(z)(J_{\Psi}(z)-I)\right]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\Psi_{+}^{-1}(z)\Psi_{+}^{\prime}(z)(J_{\Psi}(z)-I)\right],\qquad z\in\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-},

which leads to (
3.7
).
∎

4
Asymptotic analysis of the RH problem for
\Psi
with
0<\rho<1

In this section, we will perform a Deift-Zhou nonlinear steepest descent analysis to the RH problem for
\Psi
as
x\to-\infty
with the parameter
0<\rho<1
. It consists of a series of explicit and invertible transformations which leads
to an RH problem tending to the identity matrix for large negative
x
.

4.1
First transformation:
\Psi\to X

The first transformation is a rescaling, which is defined by

X(z)=\Psi\left(|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}z\right),\qquad x<0.

(4.1)

Thus,
X
satisfies the following RH problem.

RH problem for
X

(1)
X(z)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\gamma_{+}\cup\gamma_{-})
, where
\gamma_{\pm}:=|x|^{-\frac{1}{2n}}\Gamma_{\pm}
with
\Gamma_{\pm}
depicted in Figure
2
.

(2)
X(z)
satisfies the jump condition

X_{+}(z)=X_{-}(z)J_{X}(z),

where

J_{X}(z)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{2tg(z)}&1\end{pmatrix},&&z\in\gamma_{+},\\
&\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\,\mathrm{e}^{-2tg(z)}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},&&z\in\gamma_{-},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

and where
t=|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}
,

g(z):=g(z;x,\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n-1})=i\frac{(2z)^{2n+1}}{4n+2}-iz+i\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n+j}\tau_{j}}{4j+2}(2z)^{2j+1}|x|^{\frac{j-n}{n}}.

(4.3)

(3) As
z\to\infty
, we have

X(z)=I+\frac{\Psi_{1}(x)}{|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}z}+O\left(z^{-2}\right),

(4.4)

where
\Psi_{1}
is defined in (
3.3
).

4.2
Second transformation:
X\to Y

The second transformation involves a deformation of the jump contour of the RH problem for
X
.
To proceed, we begin with studies of the saddle points of the phase function
g
in (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

For sufficiently large negative
x
,
g(z)
has exactly two real saddle points
z_{+}>0
and
z_{-}=-z_{+}
. Moreover,
z_{+}
admits the following expansion

z_{+}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}a_{k}|x|^{-\frac{k}{n}},\qquad x\to-\infty,

(4.5)

where the coefficients
a_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, are given in (
2.2
).

Proof.

From (
4.3
), it follows that

|x|g^{\prime}(z)=i\left(Q\left(2z|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}\right)-|x|\right),\qquad x<0,

(4.6)

where
Q
is the even polynomial defined in (
2.1
).
Thus, we have
g^{\prime}(z)=0
if and only if
Q\left(2z|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}\right)=|x|
.
Since
Q(z)
is a polynomial with real coefficients and behaves like
z^{2n}
as
z\to\infty
, there exists a constant
M>0
such that
Q(z)
is strictly increasing on
[M,\infty)
. Suppose
|x|
is large enough such that
Q(z)<|x|
for all
z\in[0,M]
, it is then easily seen that the equation
Q(z)=|x|
has a unique positive solution
z_{0}\in[M,\infty)
.
Furthermore, according to
[
11
, Lemma 4.3]
, the solution
z_{0}
has the following fractional power series expansion

z_{0}=|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}b_{k}|x|^{-\frac{k}{2n}},\qquad x\to-\infty,

(4.7)

where
b_{0}=0
and

b_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\mathop{\rm Res}\nolimits\limits_{z=\infty}Q^{\frac{k}{2n}}(z),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.8)

Since
Q
is an even polynomial, we have

b_{2m}=0,\quad m\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad Q(-z_{0})=|x|.

(4.9)

Hence, for sufficiently large negative
x
, there exist exactly two real saddle points
z_{\pm}
of
g(z)
, which are symmetric with respect to the origin and

z_{+}=\frac{1}{2}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2n}}z_{0}.

Inserting (
4.7
)–(
4.9
) into the above formula gives us (
4.5
).
∎

\Sigma_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,5

of the RH problem for

.

We next deform the jump contours

and
\gamma_{-}
for

,

. Here, the rays
\Sigma_{1,4}
and

are emanating from the two saddle points

of

, respectively, and
\Sigma_{5}=[z_{-},z_{+}]
; see Figure

for an illustration. We choose the rays
\Sigma_{i}
,

, such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits g(z)<0,&\hbox{$z\in\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}$,}\\
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits g(z)>0,&\hbox{$z\in\Sigma_{3}\cup\Sigma_{4}$.}\end{array}\right.

(4.10)

The possibility of this choice follows from the fact that

as

with

and the signature of

as depicted in Figure

is then defined by

Y(z)=X(z)\left\{\begin{aligned} &\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

&I,&&\textrm{elsewhere.}\end{aligned}\right.

(4.12)

In view of the RH problem for

, it is direct to check that

satisfies the following RH problem.

RH problem for
Y

(1)
Y(z)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\cup_{1}^{5}\Sigma_{k})
, where the rays
\Sigma_{k}
,

(2)

Y_{+}(z)=Y_{-}(z)J_{Y}(z),

where the jump matrix

(3) As

(4.13)

where

is defined in (

).

We conclude this subsection by showing the expansion of

as

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
z_{+}
be the positive saddle point of
g
. We have, as

2ig(z_{+})|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{2na_{k}}{1+2(n-k)}|x|^{\frac{1+2(n-k)}{2n}},

where

,

, are given in (

From (

), it follows that

g(z)|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}=\theta\left(|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}z;x\right),\qquad x<0.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(g(z_{+})|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}\right)

into the polynomial (

Comparing (

) with (

) leads to

.

Third transformation:

, except for

is highly oscillatory for large

along
(z_{-},z_{+})

Using again the signature of
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits ig_{0}
as illustrated in Figure

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits g(z)<0

&I,&&\mathrm{elsewhere}.\end{aligned}\right.

,

.

(2)

is shown in Figure

.

(3) As

, we have

where

is defined in (

).

4.4
Global parametrix

As
x\to-\infty
,
J_{T}\to I
exponentially fast except for
z\in(z_{-},z_{+})
. It is then natural to consider the following
RH problem for the global parametrix
P^{(\infty)}
.

RH problem for
P^{(\infty)}

(1)
P^{(\infty)}(z)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[z_{-},z_{+}]
.

(2)
P^{(\infty)}(z)
satisfies the jump condition

{P}^{(\infty)}_{+}(z)={P}^{(\infty)}_{-}(z)\begin{pmatrix}1-\rho^{2}&0\\
0&(1-\rho^{2})^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad z\in(z_{-},z_{+}).

(4.23)

(3) As

, we have
{P}^{(\infty)}(z)=I+O(z^{-1})
.

The solution to this RH problem is explicitly given by

{P}^{(\infty)}(z)=\left(\frac{z-z_{-}}{z-z_{+}}\right)^{\nu\sigma_{3}},\qquad\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits(z-z_{\pm})\in(-\pi,\pi),

(4.24)

where

Since

. A direct computation shows that

P^{(\infty)}(z)=I+\frac{2z_{+}\nu\sigma_{3}}{z}+O(z^{-2}),\qquad z\to\infty.

(4.26)

4.5
Local parametrices at

Since the convergence of
J_{T}
to the identity matrix is not uniform near

, we have to construct two local parametrices

6.4
Proof of Corollary

To estimate locations of the poles of

, we need the following result about the zeros of a function (see
[

, Theorem 1]
).

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
J=[\lambda_{0}-\delta,\lambda_{0}+\delta]
. Assume that
f(\lambda)=h(\lambda)+\varepsilon(\lambda)
, where
f(\lambda)
is continuous,
h(\lambda)
is differentiable,
h(\lambda_{0})=0
,
l=\min|h^{\prime}(\lambda)|>0
, and

M_{\varepsilon}:=\max|\varepsilon(\lambda)|<\min\Big\{|h(\lambda_{0}-\delta)|,~|h(\lambda_{0}+\delta)|\Big\}.

Then, there exists a zero
\lambda_{z}
of
f(\lambda)
in the interval
J
such that
|\lambda_{z}-\lambda_{0}|\leq M_{\varepsilon}/l
.

To make use of the above lemma, we note from (
2.22
) that, if
\tau_{1}=\cdots=\tau_{n-1}=0
,

L(x):=\frac{|x|^{\frac{1}{2n}}}{q_{n}((-1)^{n+1}x;\rho)}=\sin\Theta+O\left(|x|^{-\frac{2n+1}{2n}}\right),~~\mathrm{as}~~x\to-\infty,

(6.70)

where

\Theta=\Theta(x):=\frac{2n}{2n+1}|x|^{\frac{2n+1}{2n}}-\frac{2n+1}{2n}\kappa\ln|x|+\varphi,

(6.71)

and where the constants
\kappa
and
\varphi
are given in (
2.21
). The error bound in (
6.70
) is uniformly valid for
\Theta
bounded away from
m\pi
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
6.71
), it is readily seen that
x\sim-(\frac{2n+1}{2n})^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}\Theta^{\frac{2n}{2n+1}}
for large negative

. Thus,
\Theta
can be taken as a large parameter. Applying Lemma

to the function

in (

Equivalently, this means there exists a simple pole

Appendix A
The parabolic cylinder parametrix

The parabolic cylinder parametrix

\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}(\eta)=\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}(\eta;\nu)
with
\nu
being a real or complex parameter is a solution of the
following RH problem.

RH problem for
\Phi_{(\mathrm{PC})}

(1)
\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}(\eta)
is analytic for
\eta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(\cup^{5}_{k=1}\Gamma_{k})
, where

\Gamma_{k}=\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}\mid\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\eta=\frac{k\pi}{2}\right\},\quad k=1,\cdots,4,\qquad\Gamma_{5}=\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}\mid\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\eta=-\frac{\pi}{4}\right\};

see Figure
7
for an illustration.

Figure 7:
The jump contours
\Gamma_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,5
, of the RH problem for
\Phi_{(\mathrm{PC})}
.

(2)
\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}(\eta)
satisfies the jump condition

\Phi_{(\mathrm{PC}),+}(\eta)=\Phi_{(\mathrm{PC}),-}(\eta)\left\{\begin{aligned} &H_{0},\quad&&\eta\in\Gamma_{1},\\
&H_{1},\quad&&\eta\in\Gamma_{2},\\
&H_{2},\quad&&\eta\in\Gamma_{3},\\
&H_{3},\quad&&\eta\in\Gamma_{4},\\
&\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\nu\sigma_{3}},&&\eta\in\Gamma_{5},\end{aligned}\right.

(A.1)

where

H_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
h_{0}&1\end{pmatrix},\ H_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&h_{1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\ H_{n+2}=\mathrm{e}^{i\pi\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sigma_{3}}H_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sigma_{3}},\ n=0,1

with

h_{0}=-i\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\Gamma(\nu+1)},\qquad h_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\Gamma(-\nu)}\mathrm{e}^{i\pi\nu},\qquad 1+h_{0}h_{1}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\nu}.

(A.2)

(3)
As
\eta\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}(\eta)=\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{\nu(\nu+1)}{2\eta^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\eta^{4}}\right)&\frac{\nu}{\eta}+O\left(\frac{1}{\eta^{3}}\right)\\[5.69046pt]
\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{(\nu+1)(\nu+2)}{2\eta^{3}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\eta^{5}}\right)&1-\frac{\nu(\nu-1)}{2\eta^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\eta^{4}}\right)\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{\left(\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}-\nu\ln\eta\right)\sigma_{3}}.

(A.3)

According to
[
25
, Chapter 9]
, the solution to above RH problem can be built explicitly in terms of the parabolic cylinder functions (cf.
[
38
, Chapter 12]
)
D_{\nu}
and
D_{-\nu-1}
. More precisely, we have

\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}(\eta)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\eta}{2}&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D_{-\nu-1}(i\eta)&D_{\nu}(\eta)\\[5.69046pt]
D_{-\nu-1}^{\prime}(i\eta)&D_{\nu}^{\prime}(\eta)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{e}^{i\frac{\pi}{2}(\nu+1)}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits{\eta}\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{4},0\right).

(A.4)

For
\eta
in other sectors of the complex plane,
\Phi_{\mathrm{(PC)}}
is determined by (
A.4
) and the jump relation (
A.1
).

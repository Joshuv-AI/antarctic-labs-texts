Poincare Series and Miraculous Duality
Kevin Lin

Abstract
. In the setting of global geometric Langlands, we show that miraculous duality on the stack of principal bundles on a curve intertwines the functor of Poincare series with the dual functor to Whittaker coefficients. We construct, for arbitrary parabolic subgroups, Jacquet functors controlling constant terms of Poincare series and Whittaker coefficients of Eisenstein series in local terms.

1.2.
Upgraded version

1.2.1.

Our main theorem has a
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,\mathrm{Ran}}

\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{\mathrm{Ran}}

1.2.2.
Global Whittaker model

Let
x
be a finite set of points of
X

\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{x}\simeq\mathrm{Whit}\left(\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,x}^{\mathrm{pol}}\right)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}\left(\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,x}^{\mathrm{pol}}\right).

It is naturally a module over the spherical Hecke category
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
, equipped with a pair of
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-linear adjoint functors

\mathrm{Poinc}_{!}:\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{x}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\qquad\mathrm{coeff}_{*}:\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\to\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{x}.

Here, as before,
\mathrm{Poinc}_{!}
is simply
!
-pushforward of the underlying sheaf to
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}

\mathrm{Whit}_{-\psi}(G)_{x}\otimes\mathrm{Whit}_{\psi}(G)_{x}\to\mathrm{Vect}

is a perfect pairing.

1.2.3.
Left and right modules

To formulate the sense in which our dualities are
\mathrm{Sph}_{G}
-linear, recall that every convolution stack comes with a canonical involution

\sigma:X\underset{S}{\times}X\to X\underset{S}{\times}X

given by swapping the two factors. For example, this involution on
G\simeq\mathrm{pt}\underset{\mathbf{B}G}{\times}\mathrm{pt}
is group inversion. We obtain an involutive anti-automorphism

\sigma:\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}^{\mathrm{rev}}.

Now let us view
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
as a right
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-module. Then
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})^{\vee}
is naturally a left
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-module, but the involution
\sigma
allows us to identify left and right modules for
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
. Therefore, it makes sense to say that the functor

\mathrm{PsId}_{!}:\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})^{\vee}\to\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})

is canonically
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-linear. This is easy to check using the ULA property of Hecke kernels and the fact that
\sigma
interchanges the geometrically defined left and right
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
actions on
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
. It is tautological that the duality

\mathrm{Whit}_{-\psi}(G)_{x}^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{Whit}_{\psi}(G)_{x}

is
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-linear.

1.3.1.

1.3.3.

Our proof that

is an isomorphism is based on the decomposition of
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
into its cuspidal and Eisenstein parts. Recall that the full subcategory
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})_{\mathrm{cusp}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
of cuspidal objects has the following equivalent characterizations:

which are annihilated by

for every proper parabolic

–

\mathscr{F}
which are annihilated by
\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}
for every proper parabolic
P
.

–

It is the right orthogonal to
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})_{\mathrm{Eis}}
.

Here the full subcategory
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})_{\mathrm{Eis}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})
is characterized by the following equivalent characterizations:

–

It is the full subcategory generated by the images of
\mathrm{Eis}_{P,!}
for all proper parabolics

–

It is the left orthogonal to
\mathrm{D}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})_{\mathrm{cusp}}

The category

contains but is not usually generated by the images of

for all proper parabolics

1.3.3 Lemma.

1.3.3 Lemma.

2.
Construction of the map

2.0.1.

In this section we will construct the map

\eta_{G}:\mathrm{Poinc}_{!}\cdot\mathrm{PsId}_{\mathrm{Whit}}^{-1}\to\mathrm{PsId}_{!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{*}^{\vee}[2\delta_{G}].

2.1.
Reminders on Vinberg’s semigroup

2.1.1.

We write

T_{\mathrm{ad}}=T/Z_{G}\simeq(\mathbf{G}_{m})^{\Delta}

for the adjoint torus. We write

T_{\mathrm{ad}}^{+}=(\mathbf{A}^{1})^{\Delta},

viewed as a semigroup. The points in
T_{\mathrm{ad}}^{+}
with zero-one coördinates are in natural bijection with subsets of
\Delta
and hence standard parabolics. We write
c_{P}
for the point corresponding to the parabolic
P
. The
T
orbit of
c_{P}
identifies with
(T_{M})_{\mathrm{ad}}\simeq T/Z_{M}
, and these orbits form a stratification of
T_{\mathrm{ad}}^{+}
.

2.1.2.
Vinberg’s semigroup

Consider the group homomorphism

G\overset{Z_{G}}{\times}T\to T_{\mathrm{ad}}.

This map is naturally
G\times T\times G
equivariant, where
G\times G
acts on the left and right on the source and trivially on the target. Vinberg’s semigroup
\mathrm{Vin}_{G}
is an algebraic semigroup equipped with a
G\times G\times T
-equivariant semigroup morphism

\mathrm{Vin}_{G}\to T_{\mathrm{ad}}^{+}

extending the above group homomorphism. This morphism has a canonical “unit section”

s:T^{+}_{\mathrm{ad}}\to\mathrm{Vin}_{G}

whose orbit under
G\times G
is an open subscheme denoted
\mathrm{Vin}_{G}^{\circ}
.

2.1.3.
Stratification

The fiber of

T_{\mathrm{ad}}^{+}\to\mathrm{Vin}_{G}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Vin}_{G}

over
c_{P}
is canonically identified with

(2.1.3.1)

\mathrm{pt}\simeq P^{-}/U_{P}^{-}\overset{M}{\times}U_{P}\backslash P\to G/U_{P}^{-}\overset{M}{\times}U_{P}\backslash G\hookrightarrow\overline{G/U_{P}^{-}\overset{M}{\times}U_{P}\backslash G}

with the natural
G\times G
action. The residual action of
Z_{M}
is descended from the right and left actions on
G/U_{P}^{-}
and
U_{P}\backslash G
, respectively.

2.1.4.
Generic maps

Let us recall the notion of generic maps from [J. Barlev]. For any scheme

, an open subset

is called a domain if it is open dense after any base change

. For any target

, we define the prestack

\mathrm{Maps}^{\mathrm{gen}}(X,\mathscr{Y}):S\mapsto\lim_{U}\mathrm{Maps}(U,\mathscr{Y})

where the limit is taken over all domains

. Given a map
\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}
, we define
\mathrm{Maps}^{\mathrm{gen}}(X,\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2})
as the fiber product:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Maps}^{\mathrm{gen}}(X,\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Maps}(X,\mathscr{Y}_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Maps}^{\mathrm{gen}}(X,\mathscr{Y}_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Maps}^{\mathrm{gen}}(X,\mathscr{Y}_{1})}

2.1.5.

We define

\mathrm{Vin}\mathrm{Bun}_{G}=\mathrm{Maps}^{\mathrm{gen}}\left(X,G\backslash\mathrm{Vin}^{\circ}_{G}/G\hookrightarrow G\backslash\mathrm{Vin}_{G}/G\right).

This a stack over
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}\times\mathrm{Bun}_{G}\times T_{\mathrm{ad}}^{+}
, and we may take the quotient by

to obtain

which is a stack over

Reminders on

and
\mathrm{PsId}_{!}

There is a sequence of maps

4.2.
Some invariant theory

4.2.1.

Construction

is based on some elementary facts about the invariant theory of

4.2.2.
Notation

For a classical affine scheme
X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
with an action of a group

, we write

for the naive GIT quotient. Here we are taking invariants in the underived sense. So, for example, we have
\overline{G/N}=G/\!\!/N
in this notation.

4.2.3.

Recall that the closure of

is a reductive monoid containing
M
as its group of units. We will denote it by
M^{+}
. Similarly, the closure of

M^{\mathrm{ab}}\simeq P/[P,P]\hookrightarrow G/\!\!/[P,P]

is a reductive monoid containing
M^{\mathrm{ab}}
as its group of units. We denote it

.

4.2.3 Proposition.

Consider the commutative diagram

(4.2.3.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{P}^{-}\backslash\!\!\backslash P^{-}\overset{M}{\times}P/\!\!/N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{P}^{-}\backslash\!\!\backslash G\overset{G}{\times}G/\!\!/N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{P}^{-}\backslash\!\!\backslash P^{-}\overset{M}{\times}P/\!\!/[P,P]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{P}^{-}\backslash\!\!\backslash G/\!\!/[P,P]}

of affine schemes with
M\times T
action.

(i)

This diagram is Cartesian.

(ii)

The lower row identifies with
M^{\mathrm{ab}}\hookrightarrow M^{\mathrm{ab},+}
.

4.2.4.
Order of operations

Our convention is that GIT quotient takes precedence over balanced product.

4.2.5.
Proof of Proposition
4.2.3
.(ii)

Actually (ii) is a consequence of a more precise assertion, which we state separately:

4.2.5 Proposition.

The composite

M^{\mathrm{ab},+}\to G/\!\!/[P,P]\to U_{P}^{-}\backslash\!\!\backslash G/\!\!/[P,P]

is an isomorphism.

4.2.6.
Proof

Consider the diagram below obtained by functoriality.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/\!\!/U_{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{P}^{-}\backslash\!\!\backslash G/\!\!/U_{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{+}/\!\!/[M,M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G/\!\!/[P,P]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{P}\backslash\!\!\backslash G/\!\!/[P,P]}

According to
[
Wan17
, Theorem 3.2.8]
, the upper row is a retract. Therefore, the lower row is also a retract. In fact, it is a retract of affine schemes, so the lower left arrow is a closed immersion. Therefore,
M^{+}/\!\!/[M,M]
contains
M^{\mathrm{ab},+}
as a closed subscheme. But
M
maps dominantly onto both, so they are equal.

4.2.7.

In order to prove Proposition
4.2.3
.(i), it will be useful to have some generalities on GIT quotients. Let
\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X
be integral affine schemes with
G
-action. We will say that the pair
(\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X,G)
is
good
if the following square is Cartesian:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}/\!\!/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/\!\!/G}

We will further say that the pair is
quite good
if the lower arrow is a distinguished open affine.

4.2.8.
Example

Let
\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{A}^{2}
be the complement of a line through the origin, and let
\mathbf{G}_{m}
act by dilation. Then the pair
(\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X,\mathbf{G}_{m})
is not good.

4.2.8 Proposition.

Let
X
be an integral affine scheme with
G
-action.

(i)

For any distinguished open affine
V\hookrightarrow X/\!\!/G
, the pair

\left(V\!\underset{X/\!\!/G}{\times}\!X\hookrightarrow X,G\right)

is quite good.

(ii)

Suppose that
(\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X,G)
is quite good. For any subgroup
H\hookrightarrow G
, the pair
(\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X,H)
is quite good and the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}/\!\!/H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/\!\!/H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}/\!\!/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/\!\!/G}

is Cartesian.

(iii)

Let
H\hookrightarrow G
be a normal subgroup. If
(\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X,H)
is good and
(\mathscr{U}/\!\!/H\hookrightarrow X/\!\!/H,G/H)
is quite good, then
(\mathscr{U}\hookrightarrow X,G)
is quite good.

4.2.9.
Proof of (i)

Let
\mathscr{U}
denote the fiber product indicated by the diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}/\!\!/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/\!\!/G}

We need to check that the dashed arrow is an isomorphism. Write

and let

be a function cutting out the complement of

. Then the dashed arrow corresponds to the inclusion of rings

A^{G}[\tfrac{1}{f}]\hookrightarrow A[\tfrac{1}{f}]^{G}.

This is an isomorphism.

4.2.10.
Proof of (ii)

Write

for the fiber product appearing in the following Cartesian diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}/\!\!/H}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Whit}_{\psi}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Whit}_{\psi}(M)_{\mathrm{Ran}_{I}}}

and an identification

\mathrm{Poinc}_{\mathrm{Conf}_{I},!}\simeq\mathrm{Poinc}_{\mathrm{Ran}_{I},!}\cdot\mathrm{sprd}_{*}

Here

.

8.3.3.
Pole-adding

Let
\mathscr{Y}\to X^{I}
be any prestack equipped with an action of the constant monoid scheme
(\Lambda^{\mathrm{pos}})^{I}
. We will think of and refer to such actions as
zero-adding
. Inverting this action gives a new prestack denoted
\mathscr{Y}^{\mathrm{pol}}
. All of our examples will have the feature that the action maps

\lambda:\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{Y}\qquad\lambda\in(\Lambda_{G,P}^{\mathrm{pos}})^{I}.

are closed immersions.

8.3.4.

Let us list a number of actions to which we will apply the pole-adding procedure. In many cases, the action will factor through the map
\Lambda^{\mathrm{pos}}\twoheadrightarrow\Lambda_{G,P}^{\mathrm{pos}}
.

–

We give
\mathrm{Bun}_{G,I}
and
\widetilde{\mathrm{Bun}}_{P^{-},I}
the trivial actions. So we will not write
\mathrm{Bun}_{G,I}^{\mathrm{pol}}
or
\widetilde{\mathrm{Bun}}_{P^{-},I}^{\mathrm{pol}}
.

–

We give
\mathrm{Conf}_{G,P}\times X^{I}
the
(\Lambda_{G,P}^{\mathrm{pos}})^{I}
-action of

\lambda\cdot(D,x)=(D+\lambda\cdot x,x).

To reduce notation, we write

\mathrm{Conf}_{I}=(\mathrm{Conf}_{G,P}\times X^{I})^{\mathrm{pol}}.

–

We give
\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,I}
the usual action of
(\Lambda^{\mathrm{pos}})^{I}
by adding zeros. Note that the map
\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,I}\to\mathrm{Bun}_{G,I}
is equivariant for this action. We warn that this action is not compatible with the formation of Whittaker categories.

–

In the same way,

\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N_{M},\mathrm{Conf}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\times X^{I}

has an action of
\Lambda_{G,P}^{\mathrm{pos}}
by adding zeros for which the projection to
\mathrm{Conf}\times X^{I}
is equivariant. We write

\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N_{M},\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\to\mathrm{Conf}_{I}

for the result of applying pole-adding to this map.

–

The open substack

\mathscr{Z}_{\overline{N},\widetilde{P}^{-},I}\hookrightarrow\left(\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N}\underset{\mathrm{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\widetilde{\mathrm{Bun}}_{P^{-}}\right)_{I}

is stable under zero-adding, and the map

\mathscr{Z}_{\overline{N},\widetilde{P}^{-},I}\to\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N_{M},\mathrm{Conf}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\times X^{I}

is equivariant for this action.

8.3.5.

The pole-adding procedure then gives a polar variant of (
4.1.6.1
).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Z}_{\overline{N},\widetilde{P}^{-},I}^{\mathrm{pol}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,I}^{\mathrm{pol}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathrm{Bun}}_{P^{-},I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N_{M},\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}^{\mathrm{pol}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bun}_{G,I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bun}_{M,I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}

We define

\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{Conf}}:\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{I}\to\mathrm{Whit}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}\qquad\mathscr{W}\mapsto u_{*}\cdot\left(\mathscr{W}\overset{!}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\right)_{\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}}[2\delta_{G}]

where the formation of
\overset{!}{\boxtimes}
is with respect to the fiber square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\square}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,I}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bun}_{G}}

The fact that this formula produces an object in the Whittaker category is checked in the same way as Proposition
4.1.8
. Now we have as before the natural transformations

\displaystyle\mathrm{CT}_{I,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,I,!}

\displaystyle\overset{\alpha}{\longleftarrow}\pi_{!}\cdot u_{!}\left(-\overset{*}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\right)_{\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}\pi_{!}\cdot u_{*}\left(-\overset{*}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\right)_{\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}}\overset{\gamma}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Poinc}_{M,I,!}\cdot\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{Conf},I}.

8.3.5 Proposition.

All of these maps are isomorphisms.

8.3.6.
Proof

The vanishing argument used to prove Proposition
4.1.6
shows that
\alpha
is an isomorphism. Also
\beta
is an isomorphism because
u
is proper: it was obtained from a proper map via the pole-adding procedure. We will prove that
\gamma
is an isomorphism by use of the following reductions.

8.3.7.
First reduction: to a point

The four functors appearing above are colax linear over
\mathrm{D}(X^{I})
. We claim that they are actually strictly linear. For
\mathrm{CT}_{I,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,I,!}
, this follows from the second adjointness
\mathrm{CT}_{I,!}^{-}\simeq\mathrm{CT}_{I,*}
and the fact that
\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,I,!}
is even
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,I}
-linear. Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are isomorphisms, it remains to observe
\mathrm{Poinc}_{M,I,!}\cdot\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{Conf}}
is linear because both of its factors are. Therefore, to check that
\gamma
is an equivalence, we may do so stratawise.

8.3.8.
Second reduction: to
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{vac},x}

The restriction of
\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{I}
to each stratum of
X^{I}
is actually constant, so it suffices to check that
\gamma
is an equivalence pointwise. For this it is enough to show that the purity morphism

\left(-\overset{*}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\right)_{\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}}\to\left(-\overset{!}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\right)_{\widetilde{\mathscr{Z}}}\,[2\delta_{G}]

defines an isomorphism of functors

\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{x}\to\mathrm{D}\left(\mathscr{Z}_{\overline{N},\widetilde{P},x}^{\mathrm{pol}}\right).

The first functor is
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-linear by the ULA property of Hecke operators and the second is obviously so. Therefore, the result follows from Lemma
4.1.7
, which says that the purity morphism is an isomorphism on the object
\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{vac},x}
.

8.3.9.
Dualities

As before, we have a
\mathrm{D}(X^{I})
-linear perfect pairing

\mathrm{Whit}_{\psi}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}^{\mathrm{glob}}\otimes\mathrm{Whit}_{-\psi}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}^{\mathrm{glob}}\to\mathrm{D}(X^{I})

given by

-tensoring the underlying sheaves followed by
*
-pushforward along the map

\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}^{\mathrm{pol}}\to\mathrm{Conf}_{I}\to X^{I}.

We take
\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{co}}
to be the descent

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{D}\left(\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{N,I}^{\mathrm{pol}}\right)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Whit}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{I}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{co}}}

\mathscr{W}\mapsto v_{*}\left(t^{!}\cdot j_{P,!}^{-}\overset{!}{\otimes}u^{!}\cdot\mathscr{W}\right)[2\delta_{M}].

It is exchanged with

under the above duality.

9.
Proof of the main theorem

9.0.1.

In this final section we will prove the main theorem in the following form:

9.0.1 Theorem.

The natural transformation
\eta_{G,\mathrm{Ran}}
is an isomorphism.

9.1.
Proof of Theorem
9.0.1

9.1.1.

Our proof proceeds by induction on
G
. We make a series of reductions.

9.1.2.
First reduction

We already know that
\eta_{G,\mathrm{Ran}}
has Eisenstein cofiber. Therefore, it suffices to show that the cofiber of
\eta_{G,\mathrm{Ran}}
is cuspidal. By Hecke linearity, it suffices to show that the cofiber of

\eta_{G}\simeq\eta_{G,\mathrm{Ran}}(\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{vac}})

is cuspidal.

9.1.3.
Second reduction

Fix a point
x\in X
and choose
\lambda
so that

\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\lambda\cdot x}=\mathrm{Bun}_{B}\underset{\mathrm{Bun}_{T}}{\times}\{\omega^{{\check{\rho}}}(\lambda\cdot x)\}

is a scheme. Write

\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}=\mathrm{Sat}_{x}(V_{\lambda})\star\mathscr{W}_{\mathrm{vac}},

viewed a sheaf on
\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\lambda\cdot x}
. Since
\mathbf{1}\to V_{\lambda}^{\vee}\otimes V_{\lambda}
is a direct summand, it suffices by
\mathrm{Sph}_{G,x}
-linearity to show that the cofiber of

\mathrm{Sat}_{x}(V_{\lambda})\star\eta_{G}\simeq\eta_{G,\mathrm{Ran}}(\mathscr{W}_{\lambda})

is cuspidal. In other words, we need to show for every proper parabolic
P
that the morphism

(9.1.3.1)

\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,!}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\to\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{PsId}_{G,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}

is an isomorphism.

9.1.4.
Third reduction

Let us rewrite the source of (
9.1.3.1
) by use of the Jacquet functor

\mathrm{Jac}_{x}:\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{x}\overset{\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{Conf}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Whit}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{x}}\overset{\mathrm{sprd}_{*}}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Whit}(M)_{\mathrm{Ran}_{x}}

and the coJacquet functor

\mathrm{co}\mathrm{Jac}_{x}:\mathrm{Whit}(M)_{\mathrm{Ran}_{x}}\overset{\mathrm{sprd}^{!}}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Whit}(M)_{\mathrm{Conf}_{x}}\overset{\mathrm{Jac}_{\mathrm{Conf},\mathrm{co}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Whit}(G)_{x}.

We obtain

(9.1.4.1)

\begin{split}&\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,x,!}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\\
&\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{Poinc}_{M,\mathrm{Ran}_{x},!}\cdot\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\\
&\qquad\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{M,\mathrm{Ran}_{x},*}^{\vee}\cdot\left(\mathrm{Jac}_{x}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\right)_{\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{M}]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{M,\mathrm{Ran}_{x},*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathrm{co}\mathrm{Jac}_{x}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\end{split}

The second arrow is induced the map
\eta_{M,\mathrm{Ran}_{x}}
, which is an isomorphism by induction. On the other hand, by rewriting the target of
9.1.3.1
, we obtain

(9.1.4.2)

\begin{split}&\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,x,!}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\\
&\qquad\to\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{PsId}_{G,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{G,x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\\
&\qquad\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot(\mathrm{Eis}_{P,!}^{-})^{\vee}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{G,x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{M,\mathrm{Ran}_{x},*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathrm{Jac}_{x,\mathrm{co}}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}].\end{split}

Therefore, it remains to perform the following:

9.1.4 Construction.

There is an identification of the morphism
(\ref{eqn:3_morphism_1})
with
(\ref{eqn:3_morphism_2})
.

9.2.
Construction of the identification

9.2.1.

We will compare (
9.1.4.1
) with (
9.1.4.2
) by describing both in explicit geometric terms. Consider the open substack

\left(\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\lambda\cdot x}\underset{\mathrm{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\widetilde{\mathrm{Bun}}_{P^{-}}\underset{\mathrm{Bun}_{M}}{\times}\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{M}\right)^{\mathrm{tr}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\lambda\cdot x}\underset{\mathrm{Bun}_{G}}{\times}\widetilde{\mathrm{Bun}}_{P^{-}}\underset{\mathrm{Bun}_{M}}{\times}\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{M}

cut out by the conditions:

–

the
N
and
P^{-}
structures are generically transverse, and

–

the induced generic
N(M)
structure is transverse to the
\overline{\mathrm{Bun}}_{M}
-structure.

This stack is equipped with with a natural projection
\pi
onto
\mathrm{Bun}_{M}
.

9.2.2.
Description of (
9.1.4.1
)

Using the fact that
\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}
is a clean extension along the inclusion
\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\lambda\cdot x}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\mathrm{pol}\,x}
, it follows from a routine diagram chase that (
9.1.4.1
) identifies with

\pi_{!}\left(\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\overset{*}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\overset{*}{\boxtimes}r_{M,!}\right)^{\mathrm{tr}}\to\pi_{*}\left(\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\overset{!}{\boxtimes}j_{P,!}^{-}\overset{!}{\boxtimes}r_{M,!}\right)^{\mathrm{tr}}[2\delta_{G}+2\delta_{M}].

We shall identify (
9.2.4.2
) with this morphism.

9.2.3.
Functors defined by kernels

To handle (
9.1.4.2
), we re-interpret the morphism

\eta_{G}:\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,!}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\to\mathrm{PsId}_{G,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]

in terms of functors defined by kernels. Namely, we view

as functors defined by kernels

\mathrm{Sh}(\mathrm{Bun}_{G})\to\mathrm{Sh}(\mathrm{pt}).

By the assumption that
\mathrm{Bun}_{N}^{\lambda\cdot x}
is a scheme, there is a canonical morphism

\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,!}\to\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,*}\,[2\delta_{G}].

Then
\eta_{G}
identifies with the morphism

(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,!})\cdot\Delta_{G,!}\to(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,*})\cdot\Delta_{G,!}\,[2\delta_{G}].

9.2.4.
Description of (
9.1.4.2
)

Now, factor (
9.1.4.2
) as the composite of

(9.2.4.1)

\begin{split}&\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot(\mathrm{Poinc}_{G,!}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda})\\
&\qquad\to\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot(\mathrm{PsId}_{G,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}})\,[2\delta_{G}]\\
&\qquad\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}(\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}^{-}\cdot\mathrm{PsId}_{G,!})\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot(\mathrm{Eis}_{P,!}^{-})^{\vee}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\end{split}

and

(9.2.4.2)

\begin{split}&\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot(\mathrm{Eis}_{P,!}^{-})^{\vee}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{x,*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\\
&\qquad\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{PsId}_{M,!}\cdot\mathrm{coeff}_{\mathrm{Ran}_{x},*}^{\vee}\cdot\mathrm{co}\mathrm{Jac}_{x}^{\vee}\cdot\mathscr{W}_{\lambda,\mathrm{co}}\,[2\delta_{G}]\end{split}

Then (

) identifies with the upper circuit of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}\boxtimes\mathbf{1})\cdot(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,!})\cdot\Delta_{G,!}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}\boxtimes\mathbf{1})\cdot(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,*})\cdot\Delta_{G,!}\,[2\delta_{G}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,!})\cdot(\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}\boxtimes\mathbf{1})\cdot\Delta_{G,!}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,*})\cdot(\mathrm{CT}_{P,!}\boxtimes\mathbf{1})\cdot\Delta_{G,!}\,[2\delta_{G}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,!})\cdot(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{Eis}_{P,!}^{-})\cdot\Delta_{M,!}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{coeff}_{\lambda,*})\cdot(\mathbf{1}\boxtimes\mathrm{Eis}_{P,!}^{-})\cdot\Delta_{M,!}\,[2\delta_{G}]}

\pi_{!}\left\{\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\overset{*}{\boxtimes}\left(j_{P,!}^{-}\overset{*}{\boxtimes}r_{M,!}\right)\right\}^{\mathrm{tr}}\to\pi_{*}\left\{\mathscr{W}_{\lambda}\overset{*}{\boxtimes}\left(j_{P,!}^{-}\overset{!}{\boxtimes}r_{M,!}\right)\right\}^{\mathrm{tr}}[2\delta_{G}].

A further diagram chase identifies (

Composing these identifications completes Construction

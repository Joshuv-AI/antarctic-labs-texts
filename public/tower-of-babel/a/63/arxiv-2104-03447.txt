Boundary layer solution of the Boltzmann equation for diffusive reflection boundary conditions in half-space
Feimin Huang, Yong Wang

Abstract
. We study steady Boltzmann equation in half-space, which arises in the Knudsen boundary layer problem, with diffusive reflection boundary conditions. Under certain admissible conditions and the source term decaying exponentially, we establish the existence of boundary layer solution for both linear and nonlinear Boltzmann equation in half-space with diffusive reflection boundary condition in L x , v ∞ L^{\infty}_{x,v} when the far-field Mach number of the Maxwellian is zero. The continuity and the spacial decay of the solution are obtained. The uniqueness is established under some constraint conditions.

Introduction

In the present paper, we consider the steady Boltzmann equation

(1.1)

v_{3}\cdot\partial_{x}F=Q(F,F)+\mathcal{S},\quad\ (x,v)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

with
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
.
The Boltzmann collision term
Q(F_{1},F_{2})
on the right is defined in terms of the following bilinear form

\displaystyle Q(F_{1},F_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-u,\theta)F_{1}(u^{\prime})F_{2}(v^{\prime})\,{d\omega du}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-u,\theta)F_{1}(u)F_{2}(v)\,{d\omega du}

(1.2)

\displaystyle:=Q_{+}(F_{1},F_{2})-Q_{-}(F_{1},F_{2}),

where the relationship between the post-collision velocity
(v^{\prime},u^{\prime})
of two particles with the pre-collision velocity
(v,u)
is given by

u^{\prime}=u+[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v^{\prime}=v-[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega,

for
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which can be determined by conservation laws of momentum and energy

u^{\prime}+v^{\prime}=u+v,\quad|u^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}=|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}.

The Boltzmann collision kernel
B=B(v-u,\theta)
in (
1
) depends only on
|v-u|
and
\theta
with
\cos\theta=(v-u)\cdot\omega/|v-u|
. Throughout this paper, we consider the hard sphere model, i.e.,

B(v-u,\theta)=|(v-u)\cdot\omega|

There have been many studies on the half-space problem of the steady Boltzmann equation in the literature. The existence, uniqueness and properties of asymptotic behavior were proved in
[
1
]
for the linearized Boltzmann equation of a hard sphere gas for the Dirichlet type boundary condition, see
[
4
]
for a classification of well-posed kinetic boundary layer problem. Later, the existence of nonlinear boundary layers with small magnitudes and Dirichlet boundary condition for the hard sphere model was established in
[
14
]
, it was shown that the existence of a solution depends on the Mach number of the far field Maxwellian, and an implicit solvability conditions yielding the co-dimensions of the boundary data (see also
[
3
]
), and we refer
[
15
,
6
]
for the time-asymptotic stability of such boundary layer solution. Wu
[
16
]
established the existence of unique solution for a modified boundary layer solution with Dirichlet boundary condition in
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
space which is used to prove the Hilbert expansion (diffusive scaling) in a disk. Recently,
Bernhoff-Golse
[
2
]
offered the existence and uniqueness of a uniformly decaying boundary layer type solution in the situation that gas is in contact with its condensed phase.
For the specular reflection condition and the solution tends to a global Maxwellian with zero Mach number at the far field, Golse-Perthame-Sulem
[
10
]
and Huang-Jiang-Wang
[
13
]
proved the existence, uniqueness and asymptotic behavior in different functional space, respectively.

For the diffusive reflection boundary condition, the existence of steady nonlinear Boltzmann solution is proved
[
5
,
7
]
in weighted
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
in smooth bounded domain, and the time-asymptotic stability of such steady solutions is also obtained. For half-space problem, Coron-Golse-Sulem
[
4
]
proved the existence of solution for linear Boltzmann equation in half-space with zero source term in the functional space
L^{\infty}(e^{\gamma x}dx;L^{2}(|v_{3}|dv))
. To the best of our knowledge, there is no result on the existence of solution to the nonlinear boundary layer problem (
1.1
) under diffusive reflection boundary condition in half-space with continuity. The purpose of the present paper is to establish the existence and continuity of solution of (
1.1
) in half-space with diffusive reflection boundary condition in
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
when the Mach number of far-field Maxwellian is zero. In fact, the continuity of boundary layer solution is very important to close the Hilbert expansion of Boltzmann equation in the initial boundary value problem, see
[
12
]
for instance.

We supplement the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) with the perturbed diffusive reflection boundary conditions

(1.3)

\displaystyle F(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=\sqrt{2\pi}\mu(v)\int_{u_{3}<0}F(0,u)|u_{3}|du+R(v)

and

(1.4)

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}F(x,v)=p_{E}^{0}\mu(v),

where
p_{E}^{0}>0
is some given positive constant, and
\mu(v)
is the normalized global Maxwellian

(1.5)

\mu(v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}.

The boundary function
R(v)
is defined only for
v_{3}>0
, and one can extend its definition to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by taking
R(v)=0
when
v_{3}\leq 0
. The positive constant
p_{E}^{0}
appear in general when we derive the Knudsen boundary layers in the process of Hilbert expansion of Boltzmann equation (compressible Euler scaling) with physical boundary. In fact, the
p_{E}^{0}
involves with the boundary pressure of Euler solution.

In general, for given source term
\mathcal{S}(x,v)
and boundary perturbed term
R(v)
, the boundary value problem (BVP) (
1.1
) (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) may be not solvable. In fact, for given
\mathcal{S}
and
R
, we have to replace the far-field condition (
1.4
) by a new one. Hence the main difficulty is to construct the new far-field state and obtain uniform estimates for both the new far-field state and the solution. To achieve our goal, we shall use the
L^{2}_{x,v}
-
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
-framework initiated by Guo
[
11
]
.

We search the solution of (
1.1
) in the following form

(1.6)

F(x,v)=p_{E}^{0}\mu(v)+\sqrt{\mu(v)}\,f(x,v).

Then (
1.1
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
) are rewritten as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\displaystyle v_{3}\cdot\partial_{x}f+p_{E}^{0}\mathbf{L}f=\Gamma(f,f)+\mathfrak{S},\\
&\displaystyle f(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=P_{\gamma}f(0,v)+\mathfrak{R}(v),\\
&\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x,v)=0,\end{cases}

where we have used the notations

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathcal{S}}{\sqrt{\mu(v)}},\qquad\mathfrak{R}:=\frac{R}{\sqrt{\mu(v)}},

(1.9)

\displaystyle P_{\gamma}f(v)

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{2\pi\mu(v)}\int_{u_{3}<0}|u_{3}|\sqrt{\mu(u)}f(u)du,

and the linearized operator
\mathbf{L}
is given by
\mathbf{L}=\nu(v)-K
with
K=K_{2}-K_{1}
and

\displaystyle(K_{1}f)(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\sqrt{\mu(v)\mu(u)}f(u)\,d\omega du,

\displaystyle(K_{2}f)(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-u)\cdot\omega|\sqrt{\mu(u)\mu(u^{\prime})}f(v^{\prime})\,d\omega du,

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-u,\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)\mu(v^{\prime})}f(u^{\prime})\,d\omega du,

\displaystyle\nu(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}B(v-u,\theta)\mu(u)\,d\omega du\cong 1+|v|,

\displaystyle\Gamma(f,f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(v)}}\,Q(\sqrt{\mu}f,\,\sqrt{\mu}f).

The null space of the operator
\mathbf{L}
is the 5-dimensional space of collision invariants:

\mathcal{N}=\mbox{Ker}\,\mathbf{L}=span\Big\{\sqrt{\mu},\quad v\sqrt{\mu},\quad\frac{1}{2}(|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{\mu}\Big\}.

And let
\bf{P}
denote the projection operator from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
\mathcal{N}
. We list some useful properties of
\mathbf{L}
and
K,\Gamma
in Appendix
A
.

For later use we define the velocity weight function

(1.10)

w(v)=(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}e^{\varpi|v|^{2}},\quad 0\leq\varpi<\frac{1}{4}.

In general, for given
\mathfrak{S}
and
\mathfrak{R}
, the boundary value problem (
1.7
) may be not solvable. In fact, our main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Recall the weight function
w(v)
in (
1.10
), and let

and

. Let

be suitably small. Assume

\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w\mathfrak{S}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|w\mathfrak{R}|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+})}:=\delta\leq\delta_{0},

then there is a unique function

We now briefly comment on the analysis of the present paper. The main part of this paper is to prove Theorem
1.5
, that is to prove the existence of solution for the linear boundary layer problem of Boltzmann equation with diffusive reflection boundary conditions. We start with the construction of approximate solutions of the truncated problem (
2.1
) with penalization. Firstly we establish
a priori
uniform
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
-estimate which is independent of both truncation parameter
d\geq
and penalization parameter
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, see Lemma
2.4
for details. With the help of the uniform
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
-estimate, and by similar arguments as in
[
5
]
, we can obtain the solution
f
of approximate problem (
2.120
) with

(1.29)

\displaystyle\|wf\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wf|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}\leq C_{d}\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\},

see Lemmas
2.5
-
2.9
for details.

We note that the bound on the right hand side (RHS) of (
1.29
) depends on
d
. However, to construct the boundary layer solution of Boltzmann equation in half-space, we need some uniform estimates independent of
d
so that we can take the limit
d\to+\infty
. We define

\bar{f}(x,v):=f(x,v)-\sqrt{2\pi\mu(v)}\,z_{\gamma_{+}}(f),

where
\displaystyle z_{\gamma_{+}}(f):=\int_{v_{3}<0}|v_{3}|\sqrt{\mu(v)}f(0,v)dv
. Clearly,
z_{\gamma_{+}}(\bar{f})=\int_{v_{3}<0}|v_{3}|\sqrt{\mu(v)}\bar{f}(0,v)dv=0
and the equation of
\bar{f}
has the same form as
f
, see (
2.132
).
Then, by energy estimate, we can prove

\displaystyle|(I-P_{\gamma})\bar{f}(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}+\int_{0}^{d}e^{2\sigma_{1}x}\|({\bf I-P})\bar{f}(x)\|_{\nu}^{2}dx

(1.30)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{|r|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{-})}+\int_{0}^{d}e^{2\sigma_{1}x}\|g(x)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}dx\Big\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\sigma_{1}\in[0,\sigma_{0}].

) is independent of

. To obtain the uniform in

estimate for macroscopic term, motivated by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v_{3}\partial_{x}\tilde{f}+p_{E}^{0}\mathbf{L}\tilde{f}=g,\quad(x,v)\in\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\tilde{f}(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=P_{\gamma}\tilde{f}(0,v)+(I-P_{\gamma})\Phi+r(v),\\

Then we can get the uniform

-estimate for

with

is independent of

\Phi
. Applying the

-

estimate to

\displaystyle\|e^{\sigma x}w\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|e^{\sigma x}w\tilde{f}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}

(1.32)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|w\big((I-P_{\gamma})\Phi+r\big)|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\},

see Lemma
2.13
for details.
From the proof of Lemma

2.
Existence for the Linearized problem

We denote
\Omega_{d}:=(0,d)
with
d\geq 1
and denote the phase boundary of
\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
as

\gamma=\partial\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We split
\gamma
into three disjoint parts, outgoing
boundary
\gamma_{+},
the incoming boundary
\gamma_{-},
and the
singular boundary
\gamma_{0}
for grazing velocities
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{+}

\displaystyle=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v>0\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v<0\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}

\displaystyle=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v=0\}.

where
\vec{n}(x)
is the outward unit normal. It is direct to know that
\partial\Omega_{d}=\{0,d\}
,
\vec{n}(0)=(0,0,-1)
and
\vec{n}(d)=(0,0,1)
.

To construct a solution of the linearized boundary layer problem in half-space, we first consider the truncated problem with penalized term

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\varepsilon f^{\varepsilon}+v_{3}\cdot\partial_{x}f^{\varepsilon}+p_{E}^{0}\mathbf{L}f^{\varepsilon}=g,\quad(x,v)\in\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=(P_{\gamma}f)(0,v)+r(v),\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle f^{\varepsilon}(d,v)|_{v_{3}<0}=f^{\varepsilon}(d,\mathcal{R}v),\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\mathcal{R}v:=(v_{\shortparallel},-v_{3})
with
v_{\shortparallel}:=(v_{1},v_{2})
. We define

h^{\varepsilon}(x,v):=w(v)f^{\varepsilon}(x,v),

then (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\varepsilon h^{\varepsilon}+v_{3}\cdot\partial_{x}h^{\varepsilon}+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v)h^{\varepsilon}=p_{E}^{0}K_{w}h^{\varepsilon}+wg,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle h^{\varepsilon}(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=\frac{1}{\tilde{w}(v)}\int_{u_{3}<0}h^{\varepsilon}(0,u)\tilde{w}(u)d\sigma+(wr)(v),\\[5.69054pt]
h^{\varepsilon}(d,v)|_{v_{3}<0}=h^{\varepsilon}(d,\mathcal{R}v),\end{cases}

where

(2.3)

\tilde{w}(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\mu(v)}w(v)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad d\sigma=\sqrt{2\pi}\mu(v)|v_{3}|dv.

It is easy to check that

\int_{v_{3}<0}d\sigma=\int_{v_{3}<0}\sqrt{2\pi}\mu(v)|v_{3}|dv=1.

Hereafter
K_{w}h=wK(\frac{h}{w})
and

(2.4)

\displaystyle K_{w}h(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}k_{w}(v,u)h(u)du\quad\mbox{with}\quad k_{w}(v,u)=w(v)k(v,u)w(u)^{-1}.

2.50

\displaystyle B_{2}\leq\frac{C}{N}\|h^{i-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+C_{N,k}\left\|\frac{\sqrt{\nu}h^{i-1}}{w}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

\displaystyle B_{3}+B_{4}+B_{5}\leq\frac{C}{N}\|h^{i-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+C_{N,k}\left\|\frac{\sqrt{\nu}h^{i-1}}{w}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

2.2.
Approximate solutions and uniform estimates

Now we are in a position to construct solutions of (
2.1
) or equivalently (
2.2
). First of all, we consider the following approximate problem

(2.56)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\varepsilon f^{n}+v_{3}\partial_{x}f^{n}+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v)f^{n}-p_{E}^{0}Kf^{n}=g,\quad(x,v)\in\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[4.2679pt]
\displaystyle f^{n}(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=(1-\frac{1}{n})P_{\gamma}f^{n}(0,v)+r(v),\\[4.2679pt]
\displaystyle f^{n}(d,v)|_{v_{3}<0}=(1-\frac{1}{n})f^{n}(d,\mathcal{R}v)\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
is arbitrary and
n>1
is an integer. For later use, we choose
n_{0}>1
large enough such that

\frac{1}{8}(1-\frac{2}{n}+\frac{3}{2n^{2}})^{-2k_{0}-1}\leq\frac{1}{2},

for any
n\geq n_{0}
, where
k_{0}>0
is the one fixed in Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0,d\geq 1
,
n\geq n_{0}
, and
\beta\geq 3
. Assume
\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}<\infty
.
Then there exists a unique solution
f^{n}
to (
2.56
) satisfying

\displaystyle\|wf^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wf^{n}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,n}\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\},

where the positive constant
C_{\varepsilon,n}>0
depends only on
\varepsilon
and
n
. Moreover, if
g is continuous in
\Omega_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
r(v)
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, then
f^{n}
is continuous away from grazing set
\gamma_{0}
.

Proof.
We consider the solvability of the following boundary value problem

(2.57)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f:=\varepsilon f+v_{3}\partial_{x}f+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v)f-p_{E}^{0}\lambda Kf=g,\\
\displaystyle f(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=(1-\frac{1}{n})P_{\gamma}f+r(v),\\
\displaystyle f(d,v)|_{v_{3}<0}=(1-\frac{1}{n})f(d,\mathcal{R}v)\end{cases}

for
\lambda\in[0,1]
. For brevity we denote
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{-1}
to be the solution operator associated to the problem, meaning that
f:=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{-1}g
is a solution to the BVP (
2.57
). Our idea is to prove the existence of
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}
, and then extend to obtain the existence of
\mathcal{L}_{1}^{-1}
by a continuous argument on
\lambda
. Since the proof is very long, we split it into four steps.

Step 1.
In this step, we prove the existence of
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-1}
. We consider the following approximate sequence

(2.58)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}f^{i+1}=\varepsilon f^{i+1}+v_{3}\partial_{x}f^{i+1}+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v)f^{i+1}=g,\\
\displaystyle f^{i+1}(0,v)|_{v_{3}>0}=(1-\frac{1}{n})P_{\gamma}f^{i}+r(v),\\
\displaystyle f^{i+1}(d,v)|_{v_{3}<0}=(1-\frac{1}{n})f^{i}(d,\mathcal{R}v)\end{cases}

for
i=0,1,2,\cdots
, where we have set
f^{0}\equiv 0
. We will construct
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
solutions to (
2.58
) for
i=0,1,2,\cdots
, and
establish
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
-estimates.

Firstly, we will solve inductively the linear equation (
2.58
) by the method of characteristics. Let
h^{i+1}(x,v)=w(v)f^{i+1}(x,v)
. For almost every
(x,v)\in\bar{\Omega}_{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash(\gamma_{0}\cup\gamma_{-})
, one can write

\displaystyle h^{i+1}(x,v)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{\{v_{0,3}>0\}}e^{-(\varepsilon+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v))t_{\mathbf{b}}}\cdot w(v)\big[(1-\frac{1}{n})P_{\gamma}f^{i}(0,v)+r(v)\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbf{1}_{\{v_{0,3}<0\}}e^{-(\varepsilon+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v))t_{\mathbf{b}}}\cdot w(v)(1-\frac{1}{n})f^{i}(d,\mathcal{R}v)

(2.59)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t-t_{1}}^{t}e^{-(\varepsilon+p_{E}^{0}\nu(v))(t-s)}(wg)(x-v_{0,3}(t-s),v){\rm d}s,

where
t_{1}=t-t_{\mathbf{b}}(x,v)
. Noting the definition of
P_{\gamma}f
, we have

(2.60)

|wP_{\gamma}f(0)|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\leq C|f(0)|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}.

We consider (
2.2
) with
i=0
. Since
f^{0}\equiv 0
,
it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\|h^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|h^{1}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}\leq C\Big\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}<\infty.

Therefore we have obtained the solution to (
2.58
) with
i=0
. Assume that we have already solved (
2.58
) for
i\leq l
and obtained

(2.61)

\|h^{l+1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|h^{l+1}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}\leq C_{l+1}\Big\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}<\infty.

We now consider (
2.58
) for
i=l+1
. Noting (
2.61
), then we can solve (
2.58
) by using (
2.2
) with
i=l+1
. We still need to prove

\displaystyle\|h^{l+2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+{|h^{l+2}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}}

\displaystyle\leq C|h^{l+1}|_{L^{\infty}{(\gamma_{+})}}+C|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}+C\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{l+2}\Big\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}<\infty.

Therefore, inductively, we have solved (
2.58
) for
i=0,1,2,\cdots
and obtained

(2.62)

\displaystyle\|h^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|h^{i}|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{+})}\leq C_{i}\Big\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}<\infty.

The positive constant

may increase to infinity as

.

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|f^{i+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\frac{1}{2}\{|f^{i+1}(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}+|f^{i+1}(d)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}\}+\|f^{i+1}\|^{2}_{\nu}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{n}|r|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{-})}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\|f^{i+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{2}{n}+\frac{3}{2n^{2}})\{|f^{i}(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}+|f^{i}(d)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}\}

(2.63)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{n}|r|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{-})}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\|f^{i+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\|f^{i+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\{|f^{i+1}(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}+|f^{i+1}(d)|^{2}_{L^{2}(\gamma_{+})}\}+2\|f^{i+1}\|^{2}_{\nu}

2.66

2.62

Hence it follows from (

) and (

(2.68)

\displaystyle\|h^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|h^{i}|_{L^{\infty}{(\gamma_{+})}}\leq C_{k_{0},\varepsilon,n,d}\Big\{\|\nu^{-1}wg\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|wr|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\},\quad\mbox{for}\ i\geq 1.

Taking the difference

and then applying Lemma

, we have that for

We can look at (
3.8
) as a linear problem. Taking
\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}\leq\sigma<\sigma_{0}
, then we apply (
1.26
) to (
3.8
) to have

\displaystyle\|e^{\sigma x}w(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|e^{\sigma x}(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})(0)|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}+|(\mathfrak{b}_{1,1}^{\infty}-\mathfrak{b}_{2,1}^{\infty},\,\mathfrak{b}_{1,2}^{\infty}-\mathfrak{b}_{2,2}^{\infty},\,\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{\infty}-\mathfrak{c}_{2}^{\infty})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\hat{C}_{1}}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}\Big\{\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w\Gamma(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2},\mathfrak{f}_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w\Gamma(\mathfrak{f}_{2},\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w(\mathfrak{S}_{1}-\mathfrak{S}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|w(\mathfrak{R}_{1}-\mathfrak{R}_{2})|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\hat{C}_{1}}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}\Big\{\big[\|e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}x}w\mathfrak{f}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\|e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}x}w\mathfrak{f}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\big]\cdot\|e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}x}w(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w(\mathfrak{S}_{1}-\mathfrak{S}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|w(\mathfrak{R}_{1}-\mathfrak{R}_{2})|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}.

Taking
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}
, by a direct calculation, we have from (
3
) that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{0}x}w(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq\frac{8\hat{C}_{1}}{\sigma_{0}}\Big\{\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w(\mathfrak{S}_{1}-\mathfrak{S}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|w(\mathfrak{R}_{1}-\mathfrak{R}_{2})|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\gamma_{-})}\Big\}.

Substituting (
3.10
) into (
3

\displaystyle\|e^{\sigma x}w(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|e^{\sigma x}(\mathfrak{f}_{1}-\mathfrak{f}_{2})(0)|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+|(\mathfrak{b}_{1,1}^{\infty}-\mathfrak{b}_{2,1}^{\infty},\,\mathfrak{b}_{1,2}^{\infty}-\mathfrak{b}_{2,2}^{\infty},\,\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{\infty}-\mathfrak{c}_{2}^{\infty})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\hat{C}_{1}}{\sigma_{0}-\sigma}\Big[1+\frac{64\hat{C}_{1}^{2}\delta}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\Big]\Big\{\|e^{\sigma_{0}x}\nu^{-1}w(\mathfrak{S}_{1}-\mathfrak{S}_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+|w(\mathfrak{R}_{1}-\mathfrak{R}_{2})|_{L^{\infty}_{v}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+})}\Big\},

which proves (
1.1
). Therefore we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

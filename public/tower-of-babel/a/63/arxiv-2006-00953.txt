Existence and Hölder regularity of infinitely many solutions to a p p -Kirchhoff type problem involving a singular nonlinearity without the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz (AR) condition
Debajyoti Choudhuri

Abstract
We carry out an investigation of the existence of infinitely many solutions to a fractional p p -Kirchhoff type problem with a singularity and a superlinear nonlinearity with a homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition. Further the solution(s) will be proved to be bounded and a weak comparison principle has also been proved. A ‘ C 1 C^{1} versus W 0 s , p W_{0}^{s,p} ’ analysis has also been discussed. Keywords : singularity, non-Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition, Cerami condition, multiplicity, symmetric Mountain-Pass theorem. AMS Classification : 35J35, 35J60.

2
A simple physical motivation

This section is devoted to a physical motivation to the problem considered in this article. The explanation is physically heuristic but nevertheless gives a strong mathematical motivation to take up this problem. We will restrict to the one dimensional case of the model of an elastic string of finite length fixed at both the ends. The vertical displacement of the string will be represented by
u:[-1,1]\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Then, mathematically, the end point constraints can be expressed as

u(0,t)=u(2,t)=0

for all
t\geq 0
. In order to identify this finite string with an infinite string one can consider

u(x,t)=0

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[0,2]
,
t\geq 0
.
Thus, the acceleration
\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}
of the vertical displacement
u
of the vibrating string must be balanced (thanks to Newton’s laws) by the elastic force of the string and by the external force field
f
. Therefore we have

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}=m\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+f,~\text{for all}~x~\text{in}~[0,2],~t\geq 0.

When the steady case is considered, we have

m\frac{\partial^{2}u(x)}{\partial x^{2}}=f(x),~\text{in}~[0,2].

We quote here G.F. Carrier
[
15
]
-
it is well known that the classical linearized analysis of the vibrating string can lead to results which are reasonably accurate only when the minimum (rest position) tension and the displacements are of such magnitude that the relative change in tension during the motion is small
. Taking this into account one can suppose that the tension due to small deformation is linear in form, then we have the following expression.

\mathfrak{M}(l)=m_{0}+2Cl

where
l
is the increment in the length of the string with respect to its mean position, i.e.

l=\int_{0}^{2}\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^{2}}dx-2,

C>0
is a constant of proportionality.
Thus for small deformations we have

\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^{2}}=1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^{2}.

Hence,

l=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^{2}.

Therefore, the problem now boils down to a Kirchhoff type problem

\displaystyle\left(m_{0}+C\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^{2}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}u(x)}{\partial x^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x);~\text{for all}~x~\text{in}~[0,2],

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}\setminus[0,2].

(2.1)

In other words it models the vibration of a string.

Technical preliminaries and functional analytic set up

We begin by giving the conditions of the function
f
which is assumed to have a superlinear growth. We quickly will recall the AR condition, which is as follows.

\displaystyle(AR):~

\displaystyle\text{there exist constants}~r>0,1<\eta<\theta~\text{such that}

\displaystyle 0<\theta F(x,t)\leq tf(x,t)~\text{for any}~x\in\Omega,t\in\mathbb{R}~\text{and}~|t|\geq r.

(3.1)

Here
F(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,t)dt
. From (
3
) we have that
F(x,t)\geq c_{1}|t|^{\theta}-c_{2}
, for any
(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\theta>\eta
for
c_{1},c_{2}>0
constants. However, there are still many functions which are superlinear at infinity yet not satisfying the AR condition. We make note of another form which is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F(x,t)}{|t|^{\eta}}=\infty~\text{uniformly for}~x\in\Omega.

(3.2)

A closer observation shows that the nonlinearity
f
, under the condition (
3.2
), is also superlinear at infinity. Clearly, for example, the function
f(x,t)=t^{\eta-1}\log(1+t)
satisfies (
3.2
) but does not satisfy (
3
). Some important works that has proved the existence of infinitely many solutions for nonlocal equations of fractional Laplacian type driven by a superlinear term and with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary data, but without the AR condition can be found in
[
38
]
. We now give the assumptions made on the function
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

\displaystyle(f_{1})

\displaystyle\exists C>0~\text{and}~q\in(p,p_{s}^{*})~\text{such that}~|f(x,t)|\leq C(1+|t|^{q-1})

\displaystyle(f_{2})

\displaystyle f(x,-t)=-f(x,t)\forall(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}~\text{and}~f\in C(\Omega\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle(f_{3})

\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F(x,t)}{|t|^{2p}}=\infty~\text{uniformly for all}~x\in\Omega

\displaystyle(f_{4})

\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\rightarrow 0}\frac{f(x,t)}{|t|^{p-1}}=0~\text{uniformly for all}~x\in\Omega

\displaystyle(f_{5})

\displaystyle\exists\overline{t}>0~\text{such that}~t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{2p-1}}~\text{is decreasing if}~t\leq-\overline{t}<0

\displaystyle~\text{and increasing if}~t\geq\overline{t}>0\forall x\in\Omega

\displaystyle(f_{6})

\displaystyle\exists\sigma\geq 1~\text{and}~T\in L^{1}(\Omega)~\text{satisfying}~T(x)\geq 0~\text{such that}~\mathfrak{G}(x,s)\leq\sigma\mathfrak{G}(x,t)+T(x)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\Omega~\text{and}~0\leq|s|\leq|t|,~\text{where}~\mathfrak{G}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2p}tf(x,t)-F(x,t).

\displaystyle\begin{split}&u_{n}\rightharpoonup u~\text{in}~X_{0},\\
&u_{n}\rightarrow u~\text{in}~L^{r}(\Omega),1\leq r<p_{s}^{*},\\
&u_{n}\rightarrow u~\text{a.e. in}~\Omega.\end{split}

(4.1)

All we need to prove is that

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq\int_{\Omega}|f(x,u_{n})|(u_{n}-u)dx&\leq\int_{\Omega}C(1+|u_{n}|^{q-1})(u_{n}-u)dx\\
&\leq C(|\Omega|^{\frac{q-1}{q}}+\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q-1})\|u_{n}-u\|_{q}.\end{split}

(4.2)

By (

Lemma 4.4
.

(Lemma 5.1 in

) For all

&\leq(a|a|_{k}^{\rho-1}-b|b|_{k}^{\rho-1})(a-b)^{p-1}\end{split}

with the assumption that

Proof.

Define

\frac{p}{\rho}\text{sgn}(t)k^{\frac{\rho}{p}-1},&|t|\geq k.\end{cases}

Observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{b}^{a}m(t)dt&=\frac{p}{\rho}(a|a|_{k}^{\frac{\rho}{p}-1}-b|b|_{k}^{\frac{\rho}{p}-1}).\end{split}

Similarly,

On using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we obtain

Thus

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{p^{p}}{\rho^{p}}(a|a|_{k}^{\frac{\rho}{p}-1}-b|b|_{k}^{\frac{\rho}{p}-1})^{p}\\
&=\left(\int_{b}^{a}m(t)dt\right)^{p}\\
&\leq(a-b)^{p-1}\int_{b}^{a}m(t)^{p}dt\\
&\leq\frac{(a-b)^{p-1}}{\rho+1-p}(a|a|_{k}^{\rho-p}-b|b|_{k}^{\rho-p}).\end{split}

Lemma 4.6

(Weak Comparison Principle)
.

Let

. Suppose,

\displaystyle|(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))|

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)||v(x)-v(y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}+|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}}{2}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\mathfrak{m}(\|u\|^{p})\left(\int_{Q}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\}dxdy\right)

\displaystyle+p\mathfrak{m}(\|v\|^{p})\left(\int_{Q}|v(x)-v(y)|^{p-2}\{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2}\mathfrak{m}(\|u\|^{p})\left(\int_{Q}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}\{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\right.

(4.47)

\displaystyle\left.-|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\}dxdy\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{p}{2}\mathfrak{m}(\|v\|^{p})\left(\int_{Q}|v(x)-v(y)|^{p-2}\{|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\right.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0<\gamma<1
,
\lambda,\mu>0
. Then the following problem

\displaystyle\left(a+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}K(x-y)dxdy\right)\mathfrak{L}_{p}^{s}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu h(x)u^{-\gamma},~\text{in}~\Omega

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,~\text{in}~\Omega

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

has a unique weak solution in
X_{0}
. This solution is denoted by
\underline{u}_{\mu}
, satisfies
\underline{u}_{\mu}\geq\epsilon_{\mu}v_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega
, where
\epsilon_{\mu}>0
is a constant.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[
19
]
. Firstly, we note that an energy functional on
X_{0}
formally corresponding to (
5.2
) can be defined as follows.

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{b}{2p}\|u\|^{2p}-\frac{\mu}{1-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}h(x)(u^{+})^{1-\gamma}dx

(5.2)

for
u\in X_{0}
. By the Poincaré inequality, this functional is coercive and continuous on
X_{0}
. It follows that
E
possesses a global minimizer
u_{0}\in X_{0}
. Clearly,
u_{0}\neq 0
since
E(0)=0>E(\epsilon v_{0})
for sufficiently small

and some

in
\Omega
.

Secondly, we have the decomposition
u=u^{+}-u^{-}
. Thus if
u_{0}
is a global minimizer for

, then so is
|u_{0}|
, by
E(|u_{0}|)\leq E(u_{0})
. Clearly enough, the equality holds iff
u_{0}^{-}=0
a.e. in
\Omega
. In other words we need to have
u_{0}\geq 0
, i.e.
u_{0}\in X_{0}
where

X_{0}^{+}=\{u\in X_{0}:u\geq 0~\text{a.e. in}~\Omega\}

is the positive cone in
X_{0}
.

Third, we will show that
u_{0}\geq\epsilon v_{0}>0
holds a.e. in
\Omega
for small enough

. Observe that,

\displaystyle\begin{split}E^{\prime}(tv_{0})|_{t=\epsilon}=&a\epsilon^{p-1}\|v_{0}\|^{p}+b\epsilon^{2p-1}\|v_{0}\|^{2p}-\mu\epsilon^{-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}h(x)v_{0}^{1-\gamma}dx<0\end{split}

(5.3)

whenever
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{\mu}
for some sufficiently small
\epsilon_{\mu}
. We now show that
u_{0}\geq\epsilon_{\mu}v_{0}
. On the contrary, suppose
w=(\epsilon_{\mu}v_{0}-u_{0})^{+}
does not vanish identically in
\Omega
. Denote

\Omega^{+}=\{x\in\Omega:w(x)>0\}.

We will analyse the function
\zeta(t)=E(u_{0}+tw)
of

. This function is convex owing to its definition over
X_{0}^{+}
being convex. Further
\zeta^{\prime}(t)=\langle E^{\prime}(u_{0}+tw),w\rangle
is nonnegative and nondecreasing for

. Consequently for

we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq\zeta^{\prime}(1)-\zeta^{\prime}(t)&=\langle E^{\prime}(u_{0}+w)-E^{\prime}(u_{0}+tw),w\rangle\\
&=\int_{\Omega^{+}}E^{\prime}(u_{0}+w)dx-\zeta^{\prime}(t)\\
&<0\end{split}

(5.4)

by inequality (
5.3
) and
\zeta^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
with
\zeta^{\prime}(t)
being nondecreasing for every

, which is a contradiction. Therefore

\Omega

u_{0}\geq\epsilon_{\mu}v_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega

Finally, the functional

is the only critical point of

with the property
\underset{V}{\text{ess}\inf}u_{0}>0

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

The redefined functional
\bar{I}
given in (

) verifies the mountain pass geometry for
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
with
\mu_{0}<\infty

Proof.

By the Sobolev embedding we obtain

\bar{I}(u)\geq\frac{a}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{b}{2p}\|u\|^{2p}-\frac{\lambda\|g\|_{\infty}C_{1}}{p}\|u\|^{p}-\frac{\mu\|h\|_{\infty}C_{2}}{1-\gamma}\|u\|^{1-\gamma}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)dx.

where

u
and

. Now for a pair

, sufficiently small
\mu>0
say
\mu_{0}
, we have that
\frac{a}{p}\|u\|^{p}+\frac{b}{2p}\|u\|^{2p}-\frac{\mu\|h\|_{\infty}C_{2}}{1-\gamma}\|u\|^{1-\gamma}>0

Conclusions

Existence of infinitely many solutions to the problem in (
1
) has been proved. In addition, a weak comparison result has been proved. It has also been shown that the solutions are in

when

. Further for this particular kernel

).

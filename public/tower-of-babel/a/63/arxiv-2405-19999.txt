The spectral radius and the distance spectral radius of complements of block graphs
Xu Chen İD, Dongjun Fan, Rongxiao Shao, Guoping Wang 1. School of Statistics and Data Science, Xinjiang University of Finance and Economics, Ürümqi, Xinjiang 830012, P.R.China;2. School of Mathematical Sciences, Xinjiang Normal University, Ürümqi, Xinjiang 830017, P.R.China

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we determine the graphs whose spectral radius and distance spectral radius attain maximum and minimum among all complements of clique trees.
Furthermore, we also determine the graphs whose spectral radius and distance spectral radius attain minimum and maximum among all complements of block graphs, respectively.

Key words:
Spectral radius; Distance spectral radius; Complements; Clique trees; Block graphs.

Proof.

&=x^{T}A(\widetilde{C}_{T}^{c})x\end{split}

By Rayleigh’s theorem we have

Thus, we have

We suppose for a contradiction that

\begin{split}\lambda_{1}(A(B^{c}))&=x^{T}(J_{n}-I_{n})x-x^{T}A(B)x\\

By Rayleigh’s theorem we have

The distance spectral radius of the complements of block graphs

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
B
and
C_{B}
be two graphs of order
n
.
Set
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}
to be a perron vector of
D(B^{c})
with respect to
\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))
.
If
B
contains some two cut vertices which do not belong to the same block, then

\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))\leq\lambda_{1}(D(C_{B}^{c})).

The equality holds if and only if
B\cong C_{B}
.

Proof.

Connecting all pairs of vertices in
B^{i}
(
i=1,2,\cdots,n
) which are not adjacent in
B
, we get a graph isomorphic to
C_{B}
.
Obviously, from equation (1) we have
x^{T}A(B)x=\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(B)}x_{i}x_{j}\leq\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(C_{B})}x_{i}x_{j}=x^{T}A(C_{B})x
.

Since
B
contains some two cut vertices which do not belong to the same block, we have
d(B)\geq d(C_{B})>3
. From Lemma 4.1 we have

\begin{split}\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))&=x^{T}D(B^{c})x\\
&=x^{T}(J_{n}-I_{n})x+x^{T}A(B)x\\
&\leq x^{T}(J_{n}-I_{n})x+x^{T}A(C_{B})x\\
&\leq x^{T}D(C_{B}^{c})x.\end{split}

By Rayleigh’s theorem we have
\lambda_{1}(C_{B}^{c})\geq x^{T}D(C_{B}^{c})x
.
Then
\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))\leq\lambda_{1}(D(C_{B}^{c}))
.

Suppose for a contradiction that
B\not\cong C_{B}
.
Note that
\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))=\lambda_{1}(D(C_{B}^{c}))
. Then we have

\begin{split}0&=\lambda_{1}(D(C_{B}^{c}))-\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))\\
&=x^{T}(A(C_{B})-A(B))x\\
&=\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in(E(C_{B})-E(B))}x_{i}x_{j}.\end{split}

By hypothesis we have
E(C_{B})-E(B)\neq\emptyset
, and so
\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in(E(C_{B})-E(B))}x_{i}x_{j}>0
.
Thus, the contradiction shows that the necessity holds. ∎

Combining Theorem 4.5 and Lemma 5.1 we get the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose
B
is a block graph of order
n
with
s
blocks such that some two cut vertices do not belong to the same block. Then

\lambda_{1}(D(B^{c}))\leq\lambda_{1}(D(\mathbb{S}^{c}(s-2,1))).

The equality holds if and only if
B\cong\mathbb{S}(s-2,1)
.

Higher-order spring-coupled multilevel Monte Carlo method for invariant measures Thanks: ⋆ Corresponding author: S. Ragunathan (ragunathan@uq.rwth-aachen.de)
Håkon Hoel, Sankarasubramanian Ragunathan ⋆

Abstract
. A higher-order change-of-measure multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) method is developed for computing weak approximations of the invariant measures of SDE with drift coefficients that do not satisfy the contractivity condition. This is achieved by introducing a spring term in the pairwise coupling of the MLMC trajectories employing the order 1.5 strong Itô–Taylor method. Through this, we can recover the contractivity property of the drift coefficient while still retaining the telescoping sum property needed for implementing the MLMC method. We show that the variance of the change-of-measure MLMC method grows linearly in time T T for all T > 0 T>0 , and for all sufficiently small timestep size h > 0 h>0 . For a given error tolerance ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 , we prove that the method achieves a mean-square-error accuracy of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}) with a computational cost of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 2 ​ | log ⁡ ϵ | 3 / 2 ​ ( log ⁡ | log ⁡ ϵ | ) 1 / 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{3/2}(\log\left|\log\epsilon\right|)^{1/2}) for uniformly Lipschitz continuous payoff functions and 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 2 ​ | log ⁡ ϵ | 5 / 3 + ξ ) \mathcal{O}\big(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{5/3+\xi}\big) for discontinuous payoffs, respectively, where ξ > 0 \xi>0 . We also observe an improvement in the constant associated with the computational cost of the higher-order change-of-measure MLMC method, marking an improvement over the Milstein change-of-measure method in the aforementioned seminal work by M. Giles and W. Fang. Several numerical tests were performed to verify the theoretical results and assess the robustness of the method.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we construct a higher-order multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) method for weak approximations of the stationary distribution of
d
-dimensional stochastic differential equations of the form

(1)

\begin{split}\di X(t)&=a(X(t))\di t+\di W(t)\\
X(0)&=x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{split}

Here,
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the drift coefficient,
W(t)
is a
d
-dimensional standard Wiener process on a filtered, complete probability space
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}\big)
, and the deterministic initial condition
x_{0}
is given. More precisely, we develop a higher-order change-of-measure numerical method for computing expected values of the form

\mathbb{E}\big[\varPhi(X_{\infty})\big]\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varPhi(x)\,\pi(\di x)=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\varPhi(X_{t})\big],

where
\varPhi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
represents a quantity of interest (QoI) and
\pi
denotes the invariant distribution of (
1
). Further details on the regularity and contractivity of the drift coefficient, ensuring that the invariant distribution is well-defined, are provided in Section
2
.

The main contribution of this work is to show that, under sufficient regularity conditions, the higher-order change-of-measure MLMC method weakly approximates
\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{\infty})]
with a mean-square-error accuracy of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
at the computational cost of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{3/2}(\log\left|\log\epsilon\right|)^{1/2})
, and
\mathcal{O}\big(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{5/3+\xi}\big)
for all
\xi>0
, for a payoff function
\varPhi
that is uniformly Lipschitz continuous, and discontinuous, respectively. The higher-order change-of-measure scheme also has a better constant associated with the computational cost than the Milstein scheme. This is an improvement from
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{2})
obtained in
[
9
, Theorem 5]
.

Computing weak approximations of the invariant measure have various applications in physics, biology, statistical mechanics and other fields alike. In molecular dynamics, ergodicity plays an important role in determining the properties of the system
[
12
]
. In quantum mechanics, the ergodic measure provides a statistical description of the spacing between the energy levels of many-body quantum systems
[
5
,
23
]
. In molecular biology, the ergodic measure relates to the motion of bio-molecules and dictates the essential functions of a cell
[
25
]
.

The invariant measure can be computed either using Monte Carlo techniques, by the numerical simulation of SDE paths, or by solving the associated partial differential equation; the Fokker–Planck equation
[
33
, equation (8.6)]
. In high-dimensional state space, computing the closed-form analytical solution to the Fokker–Planck equation is oftentimes not tractable, if not impossible, due to the curse of dimensionality. Since we are only interested in computing weak approximations of the invariant measure, we use Monte Carlo simulations because it is numerically tractable and scales well to higher dimensions.

Note that the naive Monte Carlo method can be computationally expensive as one might need many Monte Carlo samples to achieve
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
mean-squared error of the estimated QoI. In
[
13
,
14
]
, the MLMC method is formulated to compute weak approximations of a QoI w.r.t the measure of an SDE. For the case of a uniformly Lipschitz continuous payoff function, and using the Euler–Maruyama scheme, it was shown that a mean-squared error of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
can be achieved using a computational cost of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{2})
, which is an improvement from
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
using the naive Monte Carlo method. The study was later extended to the adaptive Euler-Maruyama scheme with non-uniform and path-dependent time-step sizes in
[
18
,
19
]
. The results from these works form the basis for the development of an MLMC method to compute weak approximations of invariant measures.

Following the idea above, the MLMC method was extended to compute weak approximations of invariant measures in
[
16
]
for SDE with drift coefficients that satisfy the contractivity condition, i.e. the drift coefficient is one-sided Lipschitz continuous with a negative one-sided Lipschitz constant. The authors show that under appropriate pairwise contracting coupling of the trajectories of the MLMC estimator, one can obtain a uniform-in-time estimate of the MLMC variance. The Milstein scheme is proposed in
[
34
]
to compute weak approximations of invariant measures of SDE. Assuming that the diffusion coefficient satisfies the commutativity condition
[
22
, equation (3.13)]
and the drift coefficient satisfies the contractivity condition, the authors prove a strong convergence rate of order 1. The authors also show that the probability density function associated with the numerical solution converges exponentially to the true invariant distribution.

Higher-order numerical methods for approximating the ergodic measure of SDE is developed in
[
1
]
. Sufficient conditions necessary to weakly approximate ergodic measure of SDE with a high order of accuracy, irrespective of the weak order of the numerical scheme, are developed. The convergence properties of a wide class of adaptive numerical approximations of SDE are studied in
[
11
]
. The authors show that as long as the adaptive numerical method does not introduce any bias in the computation of the
“Lévy area”
, the numerical approximation converges to the actual SDE dynamics. The seminal works provide a framework for computing weak approximations of invariant measures of SDE and to establish convergence results for methods employing adaptive numerical integrators. This is of interest to our current work and for future extensions.

Explicit adaptive time-stepping schemes employing the Euler–Maruyama scheme were developed in
[
21
,
24
]
to compute the expected value of QoIs that depend on the solution of SDE with non-uniformly Lipschitz continuous drift coefficients. The fundamental idea behind adaptive time-stepping is to use a time-step size that depends on the drift term. Using a smaller time-step size as the magnitude of the drift term becomes large ensures that the numerical solution is stable and bounded. The idea was extended in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
for the MLMC method. The authors prove theoretically that using adaptive time-stepping, under suitable pairwise contracting coupling of the MLMC trajectories, the
p
-th moment of the difference between the true SDE solution and the numerical solution is uniformly bounded in time
T
for all
T>0
, provided the drift coefficient satisfies the contractivity condition.

In all the above works, it is assumed that the drift coefficient satisfies the contractivity condition. In practice, only a small class of SDE have drift coefficients that satisfy the contractivity condition. This impedes the effective implementation of the above methods for solving SDE that do not satisfy the assumption. A change of measure technique has hence been developed in
[
9
]
to extend the invariant measure computation to drift coefficients that satisfy the one-sided Lipschitz continuity condition. The contractivity property of the drift coefficient is recovered by constructing a numerical scheme with a
“spring”
term. The authors also show a linear growth in the variance of the change-of-measure MLMC estimator with respect to time
T
for all
T>0
. A similar approach is utilized in
[
3
]
and
[
10
]
to develop coupled particle filters that work for Itô diffusion that have unstable dynamics, and to compute pathwise sensitivities of SDE, respectively.

2
and
3
introduce the necessary notation, regularity assumptions, and the main theoretical results on convergence and computational cost associated with the higher-order change-of-measure MLMC scheme, respectively. In Section
4
, we will look at how the higher-order change-of-measure algorithm performs by considering different numerical test cases. In Section
5
, we will discuss the conclusions that can be drawn from the current work and the possible future extensions. Finally, Section
6

3
.

2.
Notation and the higher-order change-of-measure method

In this section, we state the necessary notation, and assumptions on the SDE drift coefficient and its derivatives to ensure the existence and uniqueness of a solution to the SDE (
1
), and to ensure that it has an invariant measure. We also state the higher-order change-of-measure scheme and the corresponding theoretical results for the convergence and the computational cost.

2.1.
Notation and assumptions

Let
\left\langle v,w\right\rangle
denote the inner product between two vectors
v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left\langle v,w\right\rangle\coloneqq v_{1}w_{1}+v_{2}w_{2}+\cdots+v_{d}w_{d},

\left\|v\right\|\coloneqq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}^{2}},\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The definition of the standard Euclidean norm can be extended similarly to a
k

\left\|A\right\|\coloneqq\sqrt{\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}=1}^{d}\left|A_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}\right|^{2}},\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\},

where
\otimes
denotes the tensor outer product
[
31
]
.

For any integer
k\geq 1
, let
C^{k}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of scalar-valued functions with continuous and uniformly bounded partial derivatives up to and including order
k
. We make the following assumption on the regularity of the drift coefficient of SDE (
1

Assumption 1

(Regularity of
a(x)
)
.

The components of the drift coefficient
a_{i}\in C_{b}^{3}\big(\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
, for all
i=1,\ldots,d
. We also assume that there exists a constant
K_{b}>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max\Bigg(\left|\frac{\partial a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\right|,\left|\frac{\partial^{2}a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(x)\right|,\left|\frac{\partial^{3}a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}(x)\right|\Bigg)\eqqcolon K_{b}<\infty,\quad\forall i,j,k=1,\ldots,d.

From Assumption
1
, using the Mean value theorem,
the following implications hold for all
i=1,\ldots,d

\begin{split}\left\|a(x)-a(y)\right\|&=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|a_{i}(x)-a_{i}(y)\right|^{2}}\leq\sqrt{d}K_{b}\left\|x-y\right\|,\\
~\left\|Da(x)-Da(y)\right\|&\leq dK_{b}\left\|x-y\right\|,\\
~\left\|D^{2}a(x)-D^{2}a(y)\right\|&\leq d^{3/2}K_{b}\left\|x-y\right\|,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle Da(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\partial a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\;e_{i}\otimes e_{j},

\displaystyle Da[u,v]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}u_{i}v_{j},

\displaystyle D^{2}a(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{2}a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(x)\;e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{k},

\displaystyle D^{2}a[u,v,w]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}u_{i}v_{j}w_{k},

\displaystyle D^{3}a(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{3}a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}(x)\;e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\otimes e_{k}\otimes e_{l},

\displaystyle D^{3}a[u,v,w,z]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{3}a_{i}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}u_{i}v_{j}w_{k}z_{l}

for all
u,v,w,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

denotes the standard d-dimensional basis vector, i.e. a column vector with 1 in the
k
-th entry and 0 everywhere else, and
\otimes
denotes the tensor product. To keep things concise, we also introduce the short-hand notation
Da(x)u\coloneqq Da(x)[\cdot,u]
and
(Da(x))^{\top}u\coloneqq Da(x)[u,\cdot]
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Similarly, we also introduce the notation
D^{2}a(x)uv\coloneqq D^{2}a(x)[.,u,v]
and
D^{2}a(x)u^{2}\coloneqq D^{2}a(x)[.,u,u]
for all

Assumption
1
ensures that there exists a unique solution to the SDE (

\displaystyle\left\langle x-y,a(x)-a(y)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left\|x-y\right\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\lambda

Assumption 2

(uniform Lipschitz continuity of
Da(x)a(x)
)
.

There exists a constants

\left|\Big(a_{i}\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big)(x)-\Big(a_{i}\frac{\partial a_{k}}{\partial x_{i}}\Big)(y)\right|\leq L\left\|x-y\right\|,\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the infinitesimal generator of the Itô process (
1

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}=a(x)\cdot D+\frac{1}{2}\Delta,

where

represents the Laplacian operator. The Laplacian applied to the vector-valued drift coefficient

\displaystyle\Delta a(x)\coloneqq\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}a_{1}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(x),\cdots,\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}a_{d}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(x)\Bigg)^{\top}.

The infinitesimal generator is needed to construct the order 1.5 strong Itô–Taylor scheme and occurs naturally due to the Itô–Taylor expansion.

Proposition 1

(uniformly Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{A}
)
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2

\widehat{L}>0

\left\|\mathcal{A}a(x)-\mathcal{A}a(y)\right\|\leq\widehat{L}\left\|x-y\right\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Consider the operator
\mathcal{A}
applied to the
k
-th component of the drift coefficient
a
. We then obtain

\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{A}a_{k}(x)-\mathcal{A}a_{k}(y)\right|&=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg(a_{i}(x)\frac{\partial a_{k}(x)}{\partial x_{i}}-a_{i}(y)\frac{\partial a_{k}(y)}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}a_{k}(x)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}a_{k}(y)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\bigg)\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|a_{i}(x)\frac{\partial a_{k}(x)}{\partial x_{i}}-a_{i}(y)\frac{\partial a_{k}(y)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|\frac{\partial^{2}a_{k}(x)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}a_{k}(y)}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}L\left\|x-y\right\|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}K_{b}\left\|x-y\right\|\\
&=\widehat{L}_{k}\left\|x-y\right\|,\end{split}

where
\widehat{L}_{k}\coloneqq d\Big(L+\frac{1}{2}K_{b}\Big)
. We then have

\begin{split}\left\|\mathcal{A}a(x)-\mathcal{A}a(y)\right\|=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left|\mathcal{A}a_{k}(x)-\mathcal{A}a_{k}(y)\right|^{2}}\leq\underbrace{d^{3/2}\Big(L+\frac{1}{2}K_{b}\Big)}_{\eqqcolon\widehat{L}}\left\|x-y\right\|\end{split}

∎

The one-sided Lipschitz property of the operator

acting on the drift coefficient

\left\langle x-y,\mathcal{A}a(x)-\mathcal{A}a(y)\right\rangle\leq\widehat{\lambda}\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}.

\left\langle x,a(x)\right\rangle\leq-\alpha\left\|x\right\|^{2}+\beta,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

2.2.
The higher-order change-of-measure method

Having made the necessary assumptions needed to ensure the well-posedness of a solution to the SDE (
1
), and the existence of an invariant measure
\pi(x)
, the expected value of a QoI under the
\mathbb{Q}
measure, given by
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\varPhi(X_{T})]
, can be approximated using the standard MLMC method as

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T}^{L})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T}^{0})]+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T}^{f,\ell})-\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T}^{c,\ell-1})]

where
L
denotes the total number of levels in the MLMC estimator,
T>0
represents the terminal time, and
\overline{X}^{f}
and
\overline{X}^{c}
denote the fine and the coarse trajectories, respectively. Both the fine and the coarse trajectories are numerically simulated using the order 1.5 strong Itô–Taylor scheme given by

Fine trajectory,
\mathbb{Q}
-measure:

(4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n+1})&=\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n})+ha(\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n}))+\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{Q}}+Da(\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n}))\Delta Z_{2n}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n})),\end{split}

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n+2})&=\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n+1})+ha(\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n+1}))+\Delta W_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{Q}}+Da(\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n+1}))\Delta Z_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{Q}}\\
&\quad+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\overline{X}^{f}(t_{2n+1})),\end{split}

Coarse trajectory,
\mathbb{Q}
-measure:

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{X}^{c}(t_{2n+2})&=\overline{X}^{c}(t_{2n})+2ha(\overline{X}^{c}(t_{2n}))+\Big(\Delta W_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big)\\
&\quad+Da(\overline{X}^{c}(t_{2n}))\Big(\Delta Z_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\Delta Z_{2n}^{\mathbb{Q}}+h\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big)+2h^{2}\mathcal{A}a(\overline{X}^{c}(t_{2n})),\end{split}

for
n=0,1,\ldots,N/2-1
where
t_{n}=nh
,
N=T/h\in\mathbb{N}
,
h=2^{-\ell}h_{0}
denotes the uniform time-step size at level
\ell
of the multilevel estimator,
h_{0}
denotes the time-step size at level
\ell=0
,
\overline{X}^{f}(0)=\overline{X}^{c}(0)=x_{0}
, and
\Delta Z_{n}
is a random variable defined as

\Delta Z_{n}^{k}\coloneqq\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{t_{n}}^{s}\di W_{s}^{k}\di s,\qquad\forall k=1,\ldots,d.

The tuple of correlated random variables
(\Delta W_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}},\Delta Z_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}})
, under the
\mathbb{Q}
measure, can be generated by

\Delta W_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq\Delta U_{1,n},\qquad\Delta Z_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}h\Big(\Delta U_{1,n}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Delta U_{2,n}\Big),

where
\Delta U_{1,n},\Delta U_{2,n}
are independent identically distributed (i.i.d.)
\mathcal{N}^{\mathbb{Q}}(0,h\Id)
samples, and
\Id
denotes the
d\times d
identity matrix. Note that

\mathbb{E}\big[\left\langle\Delta W_{i}^{\mathbb{Q}},\Delta W_{j}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right\rangle\big]=dh\delta_{ij},\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\left\langle\Delta Z_{i}^{\mathbb{Q}},\Delta Z_{j}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right\rangle\big]=\frac{dh^{3}}{3}\delta_{ij},\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\left\langle\Delta W_{i}^{\mathbb{Q}},\Delta Z_{j}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right\rangle\big]=\frac{dh^{2}}{2}\delta_{ij},

where

\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&i=j\\
0,&i\neq j\end{cases}

represents the Kronecker delta function.

We cannot use the standard MLMC algorithm for computing the weak approximations of the ergodic measure as the fine and the coarse trajectories of the MLMC estimator may drift away from each other. This can lead to an exponential growth in the multilevel variance w.r.t. the terminal time
T
, which can lead to an increase in the computational cost needed to achieve good variance estimates
[
8
,
9
]
. To circumvent this issue, we introduce a spring term in both the fine and the coarse trajectories.

Let
S>0
denote the spring constant. The order 1.5 strong Itô–Taylor scheme, after the introduction of the spring term, for both the fine and the coarse trajectories can be written as

Fine trajectory,
\mathbb{P}
-measure:

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+1})&=\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n})+Sh\big(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n})-\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n})\big)+ha(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n}))+\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}},\\
&\quad+Da(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n}))\Delta Z_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n})),\end{split}

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+2})&=\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+1})+Sh\big(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n+1})-\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+1})\big)+ha(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+1}))+\Delta W_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{P}},\\
&\qquad+Da(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+1}))\Delta Z_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{P}}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n+1})),\end{split}

Coarse trajectory,
\mathbb{P}
-measure:

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n+1})&=\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n})+Sh\big(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n})-\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n})\big)+ha(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n}))+\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}}\\
&\quad+Da(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n}))\Delta Z_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n})),\end{split}

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n+2})&=\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n})+2Sh\big(\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n})-\widehat{Y}^{f}(t_{2n})\big)+2ha(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n}))+(\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}}+\Delta W_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{P}})\\
&\qquad+Da(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n}))(\Delta Z_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{P}}+\Delta Z_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}}+h\Delta W_{2n}^{\mathbb{P}})+2h^{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}^{c}(t_{2n})),\end{split}

for
n=0,1,\ldots,N/2-1
. The tuple of random variables
(\Delta W_{n}^{\mathbb{P}},\Delta Z_{n}^{\mathbb{P}})
under the
\mathbb{P}
measure can be generated as

(11)

\Delta W_{n}^{\mathbb{P}}\coloneqq\Delta V_{1,n},\qquad\Delta Z_{n}^{\mathbb{P}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}h\Big(\Delta V_{1,n}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Delta V_{2,n}\Big),

where
\Delta V_{1,n},\Delta V_{2,n}\sim\mathcal{N}^{\mathbb{P}}(0,h\Id)
are i.i.d. samples. Note that both the fine and the coarse trajectories have the same initial value, i.e.
\widehat{Y}^{f}(0)=\widehat{Y}^{c}(0)=x_{0}
.

Note that the order 1.5 strong Itô–Taylor scheme with the spring term included, defined by the equations (
7
)-(
10
), does not solve the original SDE (
1
). Using Girsanov’s theorem, we can compute weak approximations of the invariant measure of the original SDE with numerical schemes that include the
“spring”
term. Girsanov’s theorem states that there indeed exists a transformation from the
\mathbb{P}
measure, associated with the numerical process with the
“spring”
term, to the
\mathbb{Q}
measure, associated with the original SDE (
1
)
[
33
, Theorem 9.2]
.

Consider the fine trajectory of the numerical scheme with and without the
“spring”
term, sampled from the measures
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}^{f}
, respectively, where
\mathbb{Q}^{f}
denotes the measure associated with the fine trajectory of the numerical scheme without the
“spring”
term. Under the transformation

(12)

\begin{split}\Delta U_{1,n}^{\mathbb{Q}^{f}}&=\widehat{S}^{f}_{n}h+\Delta V_{1,n}^{\mathbb{P}}\\
\Delta U_{2,n}^{\mathbb{Q}^{f}}&=-\sqrt{3}\widehat{S}^{f}_{n}h+\Delta V_{2,n}^{\mathbb{P}},\end{split}

we have that

\widehat{Y}_{n}^{f}(\omega)=\overline{X}_{n}^{f}(\omega),\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega

for all
n=0,1,\ldots,N
, where

(13)

\begin{split}\widehat{S}^{f}_{n}\coloneqq S\Big(\widehat{Y}_{n}^{c}-\widehat{Y}_{n}^{f}\Big).\end{split}

The Radon–Nikodym derivative for the fine trajectory, according to Girsanov’s theorem, is given by

(14)

R^{f}_{T}=\frac{\di\mathbb{Q}^{f}}{\di\mathbb{P}}=\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}\exp\Bigg(-\left\langle\widehat{S}^{f}_{n},\Delta V_{1,n}\right\rangle+\sqrt{3}\left\langle\widehat{S}^{f}_{n},\Delta V_{2,n}\right\rangle-2h\left\|\widehat{S}^{f}_{n}\right\|^{2}\Bigg).

Similarly, consider the coarse trajectory of the numerical scheme with and without the
“spring”
term, sampled from the measures
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}^{c}
, respectively, where
\mathbb{Q}^{c}
denotes the measure associated with the coarse trajectory of the numerical scheme without the
“spring”
term. Under the transformation

(15)

\begin{split}\Delta U_{1,2n}^{\mathbb{Q}^{c}}&=\widehat{S}^{c}_{2n}h+\Delta V_{1,2n}^{\mathbb{P}}\\
\Delta U_{2,2n}^{\mathbb{Q}^{c}}&=-2\sqrt{3}\widehat{S}^{c}_{2n}h+\Delta V_{2,2n}^{\mathbb{P}},\end{split}

we obtain

\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}(\omega)=\overline{X}_{2n}^{c}(\omega),\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega

for all
n=0,\ldots,N/2
, where

(16)

\begin{split}\widehat{S}^{c}_{2n}\coloneqq S\Big(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\Big).\end{split}

The Radon–Nikodym derivative for the coarse trajectory is then given by

(17)

\begin{split}R^{c}_{T}=\frac{\di\mathbb{Q}^{c}}{\di\mathbb{P}}&=\prod_{n=0}^{N/2-1}\exp\Bigg(-\left\langle\widehat{S}^{c}_{2n},\Delta V_{1,2n}+\Delta V_{1,2n+1}\right\rangle+2\sqrt{3}\left\langle\widehat{S}^{c}_{2n},\Delta V_{2,2n}+\Delta V_{2,2n+1}\right\rangle\\
&\qquad-13h\left\|\widehat{S}^{c}_{2n}\right\|^{2}\Bigg).\end{split}

Using the Radon–Nikodym derivatives given by the equations (
14
) and (
17
), the MLMC estimator under the change-of-measure can be written as

(18)

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T}^{L})\Big]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T}^{0})\Big]+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\Big[\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f,\ell})R_{T}^{f,\ell}-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c,\ell})R_{T}^{c,\ell}\Big].

Theorems
5
and
6
show that
\varPhi
is
L^{p}(\Omega)
-stable under the
\mathbb{P}
measure. We also make the assumption that
\varPhi
is atleast
L_{1}(\Omega)
-stable under the
\mathbb{Q}
measure, i.e.

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\Big[\left|\varPhi(\overline{X}_{T})\right|\Big]<\infty,\quad\forall T>0.

From the above assumption, and the results from Theorems
5
and
6
, we have that equation
18
is well-defined for all
T>0
.

Lemma 1

(Probability of distance measure)

For all
\delta>0

, assuming that there exists a constant

Proof.

Note that the event

Using the assumption that the volume of the set

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\Big(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}\in G_{\delta}\Big)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}\in G_{\delta}}\Big]&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbbm{1}_{x\in G_{\delta}}\rho_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}}(x)\di x\\
&=\int_{G_{\delta}}\rho_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}}(x)\di x\\
&\leq\int_{G_{\delta}}\sup_{T>1}\sup_{h\in(0,h_{\max}(T)]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}}(x)\di x\\
&\leq C_{\rho}\Big(\underbrace{\int_{G_{\delta}}\di x}_{\eqqcolon\vol(G_{\delta})}\Big)\leq\underbrace{C_{\rho}C_{v}}_{\eqqcolon C_{b}}\delta.\end{split}

5.
Conclusion and future scope

In this paper, we developed a tractable higher-order numerical scheme to compute weak approximations of the invariant measure of an SDE using the MLMC method, that does not satisfy the contractivity condition. The fundamental idea is similar to one in
[
9
]
, where we introduce a spring term in the 1.5 strong Itô–Taylor scheme that pulls the fine and the coarse trajectories of the multilevel estimator close to each other. This ensures that the fine and the coarse trajectories do not drift far away from each other while recovering the contractivity property of the SDE needed for the efficient implementation of MLMC method. We then use Girsanov’s transformation to compute the weak approximations w.r.t. the new measure.

We theoretically proved that the fine and the coarse trajectories of the multilevel estimator are
L^{p}(\Omega)
stable. We proved that the pairwise coupled difference between the fine and the coarse trajectories of the multilevel estimator has
L^{p}(\Omega)
convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\big(\min(p^{1/2}h^{1/2},ph,p^{3/2}h^{3/2})\big)
for all
p\geq 1
, and for all sufficiently small
h>0
. We proved the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norm convergence of the multilevel estimator to be
\mathcal{O}\big(T^{1/2}p^{5/2}h^{3/2}\big)
for a uniformly Lipschitz continuous payoff function. For a discontinuous payoff function defined by equation
19
, the
p
-th moment of the multilevel estimator was proved to be
\mathcal{O}\Big(T^{p/2}p^{2p}h^{\frac{3}{2}-\xi}\Big)
for all
p\geq 1
and for all
\xi>0
. Provided that the SDE under consideration satisfies Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
, we proved that the computational cost involved in the implementation of the higher-order MLMC scheme is
\mathcal{O}\big(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{3/2}(\log\left|\log\epsilon\right|)^{1/2}\big)
, and
\mathcal{O}\big(\epsilon^{-2}\left|\log\epsilon\right|^{5/3+\xi}\big)
, where
\xi>0
, for all uniformly Lipschitz continuous payoffs, and all discontinuous payoffs of the form given by equation (
19
), respectively.

There are several ways in which the current work can be extended. Firstly, we can extend the analysis of the higher-order change-of-measure scheme to SDE with state-dependent diffusion coefficients. Another possible extension would be to use adaptive time-stepping based on the idea devised in
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
. By choosing time-steps adapted to the state of the process, and using the new higher-order change-of-measure scheme, we can extend the computation of weak approximations of invariant measure of SDE with non-uniformly Lipschitz continuous drift coefficients. It is important to note that the coupling between the coarse and the fine trajectories and the computation of the corresponding Radon–Nikodym derivatives for the higher-order change-of-measure scheme are not straightforward unlike the Milstein scheme due to the higher-order noise terms. Further analysis is needed to develop an optimal coupling and to compute the corresponding Radon–Nikodym derivatives. This poses an interesting problem for future research.

The authors of
[
20
]
construct a higher-order adaptive method for strong approximations of exit times of Itô stochastic differential equations (SDE). The fundamental idea is to use a smaller time-step size as the numerical solution gets closer to the boundary of the domain, and to use higher-order integration schemes for better approximation of the state of the Itô-diffusion. The authors prove that by using a higher-order order numerical integrator along with adaptive time-steps leads to a better strong error convergence rate of exit-time computations. Combining the ideas of adaptive timestepping for exit-time problems with the higher-order change-of-measure scheme may help in computing weak approximations of quantities of interest (QoI) that depend on the exit-time and the state of the process. This makes an interesting problem for future research.

Proof of Theorem
1

We adopt a methodology similar to one in
[
9
]
to prove the stability of the numerical trajectories with the spring constant. To keep the notation concise, we denote the tuple of random variables
(\Delta W_{n}^{\mathbb{P}},\Delta Z_{n}^{\mathbb{P}})=(\Delta W_{n},\Delta Z_{n})
for all
n=1,\ldots,N-1
. We introduce the constants
C,\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta}>0
that depend only on
S,d
, and the constants from Assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
. The constants
C,\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta}
may change from line to line in the proof argument.

Computing the square of the Euclidean norm of the fine and the coarse trajectories at the odd time-step, we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|Sh\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}+(1-Sh)\bigg\{\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}+\frac{A_{2n}^{f}}{1-Sh}\bigg\}\right\|^{2},\\
\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{c}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|Sh\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}+(1-Sh)\bigg\{\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}+\frac{A_{2n}^{c}}{1-Sh}\bigg\}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}A_{2n}^{f}\coloneqq ha(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})+\Delta W_{2n}+Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}),\\
A_{2n}^{c}\coloneqq ha(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})+\Delta W_{2n}+Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\Delta Z_{2n}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}).\end{split}

Note that for the given spring constant
S>0
, and for all sufficiently small
h>0
,

\frac{1}{1-Sh}\leq 2.

Since the Euclidean norm squared is a convex function, from Jensen’s inquality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\theta x_{1}+(1-\theta)x_{2}\right\|^{2}\leq\theta\left\|x_{1}\right\|^{2}+(1-\theta)\left\|x_{2}\right\|^{2},\qquad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\;\theta\in(0,1).

Using this inequality, we obtain

(22)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}\right\|^{2}&\leq Sh\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+(1-Sh)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}+\frac{A_{2n}^{f}}{1-Sh}\right\|^{2}\\
&\leq Sh\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+(1-Sh)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+2\left\|A_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},A_{2n}^{f}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Let
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
. From Hölder’s inequality, we then have

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\right\|^{p}\leq m^{p-1}\Biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|b_{i}\right\|^{p}\Biggr),\qquad\forall p\geq 1.

Using the above inequality, we can bound
\left\|A_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}
as

(23)

\begin{split}\left\|A_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}&\leq 4\bigg\{h^{2}\left\|a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\frac{h^{4}}{4}\left\|\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\right\|^{2}\bigg\}\\
~&\leq C\bigg\{h^{2}\Big(1+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}\Big)+\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Using Young’s inequality, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and Assumption
3
, the inner product term
\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},A_{2n}^{f}\right\rangle
can be bounded as

(24)

\begin{split}\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},A_{2n}^{f}\right\rangle&=h\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},\Delta W_{2n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\right\rangle\\
~&\leq-\widetilde{\alpha}h\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},\Delta W_{2n}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n}\right\rangle+\widetilde{\beta}h.\end{split}

Combining equations (
23
) and (
24
) with (
22
), we obtain

(25)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}\right\|^{2}&\leq Sh\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+(1-Sh-2\widetilde{\alpha}h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+C\Big\{\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}\Big\}\\
&\quad+2\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},\Delta W_{2n}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f},Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n}\right\rangle+2\widetilde{\beta}h.\end{split}

Similarly, we have the following upper bound for the coarse path at the odd time-step

(26)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq Sh\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+(1-Sh-2\widetilde{\alpha}h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+C\Big\{\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}\Big\}\\
&\quad+2\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\Delta W_{2n}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\Delta Z_{2n}\right\rangle+2\widetilde{\beta}h.\end{split}

Using the similar approach as above, we can write the square of the Euclidean norm for the fine and the coarse paths at the even time-step as

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|Sh\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{c}+(1-Sh)\bigg\{\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}+\frac{A_{2n+1}^{f}}{1-Sh}\bigg\}\right\|^{2},\\
~\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|2Sh\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}+(1-2Sh)\bigg\{\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}+\frac{A_{2n+1}^{c}}{1-2Sh}\bigg\}\right\|^{2},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}A_{2n+1}^{f}&\coloneqq ha(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})+\Delta W_{2n+1}+Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n+1}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}),\\
~A_{2n+1}^{c}&\coloneqq 2ha(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})+(\Delta W_{2n}+\Delta W_{2n+1})\\
&\quad+Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})(\Delta Z_{2n+1}+\Delta Z_{2n}+h\Delta W_{2n})+2h^{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}).\end{split}

Using Young’s inequality, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and Assumption
3
, we obtain

(27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}\right\|^{2}&\leq Sh\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{c}\right\|^{2}+(1-Sh-2\widetilde{\alpha}h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}\right\|^{2}\\

\end{split}

(28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq 2Sh\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+(1-2Sh-4\widetilde{\alpha}h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+C\Big\{\left\|\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}\\

Substituting equations (
25
) and (
26
) into (

\begin{split}\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}&=(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})+(2-Sh)h\big(a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)+\widehat{I}_{2n+1},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\widehat{I}_{2n+1}&\coloneqq h\big(a(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\big)+(1-Sh)\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)\Delta Z_{2n}\\
&\quad+\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\big)\Delta Z_{2n+1}+\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)\Delta Z_{2n+1}\\
&\quad-hDa(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\Delta W_{2n}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}(2-Sh)\big(\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)\\
&\quad+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\big(\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})-\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\big)-h^{2}\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}).\end{split}

Taking the square of the Euclidean norm on both sides of equation (
39
), using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the one-sided Lipschitz property of the drift coefficient, we can for sufficiently small
h>0
write

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})+(2-Sh)h\big(a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)+\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\\
~&\leq(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})^{2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+4h^{2}\left\|a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}+2h(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})(2-Sh)\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\right\rangle\\
&\quad+2(1+2\sqrt{d}K_{b}h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|\\
~&\leq(1-4(2S-\lambda)h+Ch^{2})\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+2(1+2\sqrt{d}K_{b}h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|,\end{split}

where
C>0
is a constant that depends only on
S,d
, and the constants from Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
. The constant
C
may change from line to line in the proof argument. From the above inequality, we observe that for all sufficiently small
h>0
, there exists a
\gamma\in(0,2S-\lambda)
such that the following holds

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq(1-4\gamma h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+3\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|+\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

Further applying Young’s inequality to

\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|\leq\frac{2\gamma h}{3}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+\frac{3}{8\gamma h}\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2},

we obtain

(40)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq(1-2\gamma h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+\frac{C}{h}\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}.\end{split}

Using Hölder’s inequality, and Assumptions
1
,
3
, and
2
, the term
\widehat{I}_{2n+1}
can be bounded as follows

(41)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}&\leq 8C\Bigg\{h^{2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+h^{2}\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+h^{4}\Big(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}\Big)+h^{4}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

From equation (
7
), we have

(42)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{p}&\leq 6^{p-1}C\Bigg\{h^{p}\bigg(1+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{p}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{p}\bigg)+\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{p}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{p}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Substituting equation (
42
) in (
41
), for
p=2
, we obtain

(43)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}&\leq C\Bigg\{h^{4}\bigg(1+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}\bigg)+h^{2}\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Substituting equation (
43
) in (
40
), we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq(1-2\gamma h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+C\Bigg\{h^{3}\bigg(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}\bigg)+h\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{h}\Big(\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\Big)+h^{3}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Multiplying both sides of the above inequality by
\e^{\gamma t_{2n}}
, noting that
1-2\gamma h\leq e^{2\gamma h}\leq 2
holds for all sufficiently small
h>0
, and from recursive summation of the inequality over multiple time-steps, we obtain

\begin{split}\e^{\gamma t_{2n}}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq C\Bigg\{h^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\bigg(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{c}\right\|^{2}\bigg)+h\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{h}\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\left\|\Delta Z_{k}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}h^{3}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Raising both sides of the above inequality to the power
p/2
, taking the supremum over
n\in[0,N/2]
, and taking the expectation on both sides of the inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sup_{0\leq n\leq N/2}\e^{\gamma pt_{2n}/2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{p}\Bigg]&\leq\widetilde{C}\Bigg\{J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}\Bigg\},\end{split}

where
\widetilde{C}\coloneqq\max(4^{p/2-1}C^{p/2},1)
, and

\begin{aligned} J_{1}&=h^{3p/2}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\Big(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{c}\right\|^{2}\Big)\right|^{p/2}\Bigg],\\
J_{3}&=h^{3p/2}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\right|^{p/2},\end{aligned}\qquad\begin{aligned} J_{2}&=h^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|^{2}\right|^{p/2}\Bigg],\\
J_{4}&=h^{-p/2}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}\left\|\Delta Z_{k}\right\|^{2}\right|^{p/2}\Bigg].\end{aligned}

To keep the argument concise, we introduce the constants
C_{J_{i}}>0
for
i=1,2,3,4
that depend only on
S,d
,
\gamma
, and the constants from Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
. Using Theorem
1
, term
J_{1}
yields

\begin{split}J_{1}&=h^{p}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}h\Big(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{c}\right\|^{2}\Big)\right|^{p/2}\Bigg]\\
&\leq h^{p}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}h\right|^{p/2-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}h\Big(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{c}\right\|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}\Bigg]\\
&\leq 2^{p/2-1}h^{p}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\e^{\gamma t}\di t\right|^{p/2-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\e^{\gamma t_{k}}h\mathbb{E}\Big[\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{f}\right\|^{p}+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{k}^{c}\right\|^{p}\Big]\\
&\leq C_{J_{1}}^{p}p^{p/2}h^{p}\e^{\gamma pT/2}.\end{split}

The terms
J_{2}
,
J_{3}
, and
J_{4}
can be bounded in a similar way as in Theorem
1
yielding the bounds

J_{2}\leq C_{J_{2}}^{p}p^{p/2}h^{p/2}\e^{\gamma pT/2},\qquad J_{3}\leq C_{J_{3}}^{p}h^{p}\e^{\gamma pT/2},\qquad J_{4}\leq C_{J_{4}}^{p}p^{p/2}h^{p/2}\e^{\gamma pT/2}.

Combining all the estimates together, there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{1}>0
that is independent of
T
and
p
, such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq n\leq N/2}\e^{\gamma pt_{2n}/2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{p}\Big]&\leq\widetilde{C}_{(1)}^{p}p^{p/2}h^{p/2}\e^{\gamma pT/2}.\end{split}

Since the above inequality holds for any arbitrary
T>0
, there exists a constant
\kappa_{3}>0
, such that for any
0<h<\kappa_{3}
,

(44)

\sup_{0\leq n\leq N/2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{p}\Big]\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}^{p}p^{p/2}h^{p/2}.

Having obtained a loose upper bound on the difference between the fine and the coarse trajectories, we aim to sharpen the bound obtained in equation (
44
). Taking the square of the Euclidean norm on both sides of equation (
39
) and using the one-sided Lipschitz property of the drift coefficient, we can write

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&=\left\|(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})+(2-Sh)h\big(a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)+\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\\
~&\leq(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})^{2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+6h^{2}\left\|a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+3\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}+2h(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})(2-Sh)\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\right\rangle\\
&\quad+2(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\rangle\\
~&\leq(1-4(2S-\lambda)h+Ch^{2})\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+3\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+2(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

As in the first approach, there exists a
\gamma\in(0,2S-\lambda)
, for all sufficiently small
h>0
, such that

(45)

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq(1-4\gamma h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+3\left\|\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\\
&\qquad+2(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\widehat{I}_{2n+1}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Using the Mean value theorem, we can write

(46)

\begin{split}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})-a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})&=Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Big(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\Big)+R_{2},\end{split}

where

denotes the second-order remainder from the Mean value theorem. From Assumption
1
, it follows that
\left\|R_{2}\right\|\leq d^{3/2}K_{b}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}
.

Substituting equation (
46
) in (
45
), using Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality, and equation (
42
), we have

\begin{split}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{2}&\leq(1-4\gamma h)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+C\Bigg\{\Big(h^{2}\left\|\Delta W_{2n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\Big)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c}\right\|^{2}+h^{2}\left\|\Delta Z_{2n}\right\|^{2}+h^{4}\Big(1+\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big(h^{4}+\left\|\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\|^{2}\Big)\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{2}+h^{2}\left\|R_{2}\right\|^{2}\Bigg\}\\
&\quad+2(1-4Sh+2S^{2}h^{2})\Bigg\{h\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},R_{2}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+h\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)\Delta W_{2n}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+h\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\Delta Z_{2n}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+(1-Sh)\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)\Delta Z_{2n}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\big)\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\big(Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})-Da(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c})\big)\Delta Z_{2n+1}\right\rangle\\
&\quad+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f})-\mathcal{A}a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\right\rangle-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{c},\Delta a(\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f})\right\rangle\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Multiplying both sides of the above inequality by
\e^{2\gamma t_{2n}}
, noting that
\e^{4\gamma h}\leq 2
for all sufficiently small
h>0
, summing over multiple time-steps, raising both sides of the inequality to the power

, taking supremum over

, taking the expectation on both sides of the inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq n\leq N/2}\e^{\gamma pt_{2n}}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n+2}^{c}\right\|^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\sum_{m=1}^{16}J_{m},

where
\widetilde{C}\coloneqq\max(16^{p/2-1}C^{p/2},1)
and the terms

for

are defined and bounded below.
For the term

, we have

\begin{split}J_{1}&\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\e^{2\gamma t_{2k}}h^{2}\left\|\Delta W_{2k}\right\|^{2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2k}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2k}^{c}\right\|^{2}\right|^{p/2}\Bigg]\\
&\leq\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\e^{2\gamma t_{2k}}h\right|^{p/2-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\e^{2\gamma t_{2k}}h^{1+p}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{2k}\right\|^{p}}{h^{p/2}}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2k}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2k}^{c}\right\|^{p}\Bigg]\\

Using equation (

), we can write

\begin{split}J_{1}&\leq C_{J_{1}}^{p}p^{p}h^{3p/2}\e^{\gamma pT}.\end{split}

Similarly, the terms

, yield the bounds

where, using the result from Theorem

(47)

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2n+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2n}^{f}\right\|^{p}\bigg]\leq C_{f}^{p}p^{p/2}h^{p/2},

for all

, where

\begin{split}J_{7}&\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\e^{2\gamma t_{2k}}\left\|\Delta Z_{2k+1}\right\|^{2}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{2k+1}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{2k}^{f}\right\|^{2}\right|^{p/2}\Bigg]\leq C_{J_{7}}^{p}p^{p}h^{3p/2}\e^{\gamma pT},\\
~J_{8}&\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N/2-1}\e^{2\gamma t_{2k}}h^{2}\left\|R_{2}\right\|^{2}\right|^{p/2}\Bigg]\leq C_{J_{8}}^{p}p^{p}h^{3p/2}\e^{\gamma pT}.\end{split}

, using Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality, Jensen’s inequality, and Young’s inequality for any

The terms
J_{12}

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})\right|^{2p}\bigg]^{1/2}\leq C^{p}p^{p/2},\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})\right|^{2p}\bigg]^{1/2}\leq C^{p}p^{p/2}.\end{split}

To bound the term
\widetilde{J}_{2}
, we define
E^{f}
as

E^{f}\coloneqq-\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\langle\widehat{S}^{f}_{n},\Delta V_{1,n}\right\rangle+\sqrt{3}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\langle\widehat{S}^{f}_{n},\Delta V_{2,n}\right\rangle-2h\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\|\widehat{S}^{f}_{n}\right\|^{2},

where
\widehat{S}^{f}_{n}
is as defined in equation (
13
). Using the mean value theorem, there exists a
\xi=\theta x+(1-\theta)y
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, and any
\theta\in(0,1)
, such that

R_{T}^{f}=\exp(E^{f})=1+\exp(\xi)E^{f},

where
\left|\xi\right|\leq\left|x\right|
for all
\theta\in(0,1)
. Using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|1-R_{T}^{f}\right|^{2p}\bigg]&=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\big(\exp(\xi)\left|E^{f}\right|\big)^{2p}\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\exp(4p\xi)\bigg]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|E^{f}\right|^{4p}\bigg]^{1/2}.\end{split}

Using the result from Theorem
4
, we have

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\exp(4p\xi)\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max(\exp(4pE^{f}),1)\bigg]\leq\mathbb{E}[\exp(4pE^{f})]+1\leq\eta_{1},

for all
T\leq C_{0}h^{-2}\left|\log h\right|^{-1}
, where
C_{0}
is a constant that is independent of

.

From Jensen’s inequality, and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|E^{f}\right|^{4p}\bigg]\leq 2^{4p-1}S^{4p}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\langle\widehat{Y}_{n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{n}^{c},\Delta V_{3,n}\right\rangle\right|^{4p}\Bigg]+2^{8p-1}S^{8p}h^{4p}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{n}^{c}\right\|^{2}\right|^{4p}\Bigg],\end{split}

where
\Delta V_{3,n}\coloneqq\sqrt{3}\Delta V_{2,n}-\Delta V_{1,n}
. Using Theorem
3
, and Burkholder–Davis–Gundy’s inequality, there exists a constant
C_{\text{BDG}}>0
independent of
T
and
p
, such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|E^{f}\right|^{4p}\bigg]&\leq 2^{12p-1}S^{4p}C_{\text{BDG}}^{2p}p^{2p}h^{2p}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{n}^{c}\right\|^{2}\right|^{2p}\Bigg]\\
&\qquad+2^{8p-1}S^{8p}h^{4p}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\left\|\widehat{Y}_{n}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{n}^{c}\right\|^{2}\right|^{4p}\Bigg]\\
~&\leq C_{3}^{6p}T^{2p}p^{8p}h^{6p}+C_{3}^{8p}T^{4p}p^{12p}h^{12p}\\
~&\leq C_{3}^{8p}T^{2p}p^{8p}h^{6p}\big(1+T^{2p}p^{4p}h^{6p}\big).\end{split}

Then, for all
T\leq C_{0}h^{-3}
, and for all sufficiently small
h>0
, there exists a constant
C_{5}>0
independent of
T
and
p
, such that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|E^{f}\right|^{4p}\bigg]\leq C_{5}^{8p}T^{2p}p^{8p}h^{6p},

which then yields

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|1-R_{T}^{f}\right|^{2p}\bigg]\leq\sqrt{3}C_{5}^{4p}T^{p}p^{4p}h^{3p}.

A similar result can obtained for the term
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|1-R_{T}^{c}\right|^{2p}\bigg]
to yield a bound for the term
\widetilde{J}_{3}
.

Combining the estimates for the terms
\widetilde{J}_{1}
,
\widetilde{J}_{2}
, and
\widetilde{J}_{3}
, for all
T\leq C_{0}h^{-2}\left|\log h\right|^{-1}
,

\kappa_{7}>0
, independent of

,

, for all sufficiently small

, we obtain

(58)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})R_{T}^{f}-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})R_{T}^{c}\right|^{p}\Bigg]\leq\kappa_{7}^{p}T^{p/2}p^{\frac{5}{2}p}h^{\frac{3}{2}p}.\end{split}

Proof of Theorem
6

Following the same steps as in Theorem
5
, using Hölder’s inequality, and Jensen’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})R_{T}^{f}-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})R_{T}^{c}\right|^{p}\Bigg]\\
&\quad=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\Bigg|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})R_{T}^{f}-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})+\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})+\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})R_{T}^{c}\Bigg|^{p}\Bigg]\\
~&\quad\leq 3^{p-1}\Bigg\{\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})\right|^{p}\bigg]}_{\widetilde{J}_{1}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})\right|^{2p}\bigg]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|1-R_{T}^{f}\right|^{2p}\bigg]^{1/2}}_{\widetilde{J}_{2}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})\right|^{2p}\bigg]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|1-R_{T}^{c}\right|^{2p}\bigg]^{1/2}}_{\widetilde{J}_{3}}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

The terms
\widetilde{J}_{2}
and
\widetilde{J}_{3}
can be bounded in a similar way as in Theorem
5
. To bound
\widetilde{J}_{1}
, we begin the analysis by bound the term
\left|\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}\in G}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\in G}\right|
. Following a similar methodology as in
[
15
]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\left|\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}\in G}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\in G}\right|&\leq\mathbbm{1}_{\left\|\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\right\|>d_{\partial G}(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})}\\
&=\mathbbm{1}_{\left\|\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\right\|>d_{\partial G}(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})}\bigg(\mathbbm{1}_{d_{\partial G}(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})\leq\delta}+\mathbbm{1}_{d_{\partial G}(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})>\delta}\bigg)\\
&\leq\mathbbm{1}_{d_{\partial G}(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})\leq\delta}+\mathbbm{1}_{\left\|\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\right\|>\delta}.\end{split}

Using Hölder’s inequality, taking the expected value on both sides of the inequality, and using Markov’s inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}\in G}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\in G}\right|^{p}\bigg]&\leq 2^{p-1}\bigg(\mathbb{P}\Big(d_{\partial G}(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})\leq\delta\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\left\|\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\right\|>\delta\Big)\bigg)\\
~&\leq 2^{p-1}\bigg(C_{b}\delta+\delta^{-q}\mathbb{E}\Big[\left\|\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}-\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\right\|^{q}\Big]\bigg).\end{split}

Using the result from Theorem
3
, for the choice of
\delta=q^{\frac{3q+2}{2(q+1)}}h^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}
,
\widetilde{J}_{1}
yields the bound

\begin{split}\widetilde{J}_{1}=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})\right|^{p}\bigg]&=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f}\in G}-\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c}\in G}\right|^{p}\bigg]\\
~&\leq C^{p}\bigg(\delta+\delta^{-q}q^{3q/2}h^{3q/2}\bigg)\\
~&\leq C^{p}(1+q)q^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}h^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}.\end{split}

Note that for all
h\in(0,1)
, and for all
\xi\geq\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{q+1}
,
q\geq 1
, the following inequality holds true

\widetilde{J}\leq C^{p}(1+q)q^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}h^{\frac{3}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}\leq C^{p}(1+q)q^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}h^{\frac{3}{2}-\xi}.

Combining the estimates for the terms
\widetilde{J}_{1}
,
\widetilde{J}_{2}
, and
\widetilde{J}_{3}
, for all

,

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left|\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f})R_{T}^{f}-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c})R_{T}^{c}\right|^{p}\bigg]\leq\kappa_{8}^{p}T^{p/2}p^{2p}(1+q)q^{\frac{1}{2}\frac{q}{q+1}}h^{\frac{3}{2}-\xi}.

Proof of Theorem
7

The MSE, using Hölder’s inequality, can be split into three components based on the source of error

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\widehat{\varPhi}-\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{\infty})]\big)^{2}\Big]&=\mathbb{E}\Big[\big((\widehat{\varPhi}-\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\varPhi}])+(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\varPhi}]-\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{T})])+(\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{T})]-\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{\infty})])\big)^{2}\Big]\\
~&\leq\underbrace{\mathbb{V}(\widehat{\varPhi})}_{\widetilde{J}_{1}}+\underbrace{2\big(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\varPhi}]-\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{T})]\big)^{2}}_{\widetilde{J}_{2}}+\underbrace{2\big(\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{T})]-\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{\infty})]\big)^{2}}_{\widetilde{J}_{3}},\end{split}

where
\mathbb{V}(\widehat{\varPhi})
denotes the variance of the MLMC estimator. Recall that we want the MSE to be bounded from above by
\epsilon^{2}
. This can be achieved by splitting the error contribution equally among the three error components, i.e.

\widetilde{J}_{k}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{3},\qquad\forall k\in\{1,2,3\}.

From equation (
3
), we know that

\begin{split}\left|\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{T})]-\mathbb{E}[\varPhi(X_{\infty})]\right|\leq\mu^{\star}\e^{-\lambda^{\star}T}.\end{split}

Then for the choice of

T=\left\lceil\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}}\log(\epsilon^{-1})+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\star}}\log(\sqrt{6}\mu^{\star})\right\rceil,

we achieve the bound
\widetilde{J}_{3}\leq\epsilon^{2}/3
.

We consider two different cases based on the regularity of the payoff function

Lipschitz continuous payoff function:

5

\left|\mathbb{E}\big[\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{f,\ell})R_{T}^{f,\ell}-\varPhi(\widehat{Y}_{T}^{c,\ell})R_{T}^{c,\ell}\big]\right|\leq C_{1}\sqrt{T}(2^{-\ell}h_{0})^{\frac{3}{2}},\qquad\forall\ell\geq 1

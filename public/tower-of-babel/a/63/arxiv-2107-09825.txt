Abstract
. We investigate the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic p p -Laplace equation ∂ t u ⁡ ( x , t ) − Δ p ​ u ​ ( x , t ) + ℒ ​ u ​ ( x , t ) = 0 , \displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\Delta_{p}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0, where Δ p \Delta_{p} is the local p p -Laplace operator and ℒ \mathcal{L} is the nonlocal p p -Laplace operator. Based on the combination of suitable Caccioppoli-type inequality and Logarithmic Lemma with a De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration, we establish the local boundedness and Hölder continuity of weak solutions for such equations.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the local behaviour of weak solutions to the following mixed problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\Delta_{p}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0\quad\text{in }Q_{T},\quad 1<p<\infty,

(1.1)

where
Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
with
T>0
and
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This kind of evolution equations arises from the Lévy process, image processing etc; see
[
14
]
and references therein.
The local
p
-Laplace operator
\Delta_{p}
is defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}u:=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u),

and
\mathcal{L}
is a nonlocal
p
-Laplace operator given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y,t)|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))\,dy,

(1.2)

where the symbol
\mathrm{P.V.}
stands for the Cauchy principle value. Here
K
is a symmetric kernel fulfilling

K(x,y,t)=K(y,x,t)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\leq K(x,y,t)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

(1.3)

with
\Lambda\geq 1
and
0<s<1
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in(0,T)
.

Before stating our main results, let us mention some known results. For the nonlocal parabolic equations of
p
-Laplacian type,

\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=0,

(1.4)

the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions were verified by Vázquez
[
23
]
, where the author studied the long time behaviours as well. Mazón-Rossi-Toledo
[
21
]
established the well-posedness of solutions to Eq. (
1.4
) together with the asymptotic property. When it comes to regularity theory for this equation, Strömqvist
[
22
]
obtained the existence and local boundedness of weak solutions provided
p\geq 2
. Hölder regularity with specific exponents in the case
p\geq 2
was proved by Brasco-Lindgren-Strömqvist
[
4
]
. Furthermore, Ding-Zhang-Zhou
[
10
]
showed the local boundedness and Hölder continuity of weak solutions to the nonhomogeneous case under the conditions that
1<p<\infty
and
2<p<\infty
respectively. We refer the readers to
[
5
,
16
,
20
,
24
,
25
]
and references therein for more results.

In the mixed local and nonlocal setting, for the case
p=2
,

-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=0,

(1.5)

Foondun
[
17
]
have derived Harnack inequality and interior Hölder estimates for nonnegative solutions, see also
[
7
]
for a diverse approach. In addition, the Harnack inequality regarding the parabolic version of (
1.5
) was established in
[
1
,
6
]
, where however the authors only proved such inequality for globally nonnegative solutions. Very recently, Garain-Kinnunen
[
19
]
proved a weak Harnack inequality with a tail term for sign changing solutions to the parabolic problem of (
1.5
). For what concerns maximum principles, interior sobolev regularity along with symmetry results among many other quantitative and qualitative properties for solutions to (
1.5
), one can see for instance
[
2
,
3
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. In the nonlinear framework (i.e.,
p\neq 2
), Garain-Kinnunen
[
18
]
developed the local regularity theory for

-\Delta_{p}u+\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\,dy=0

with
K(x,y)\simeq|x-y|^{-(N+sp)}
, involving boundedness, Hölder continuity, Harnack inequality as well as lower/upper semicontinuity of weak supersolutions/subsolutions.
Nonetheless, to the best of our knowledge, there are little results concerning on the mixed local and nonlocal nonlinear parabolic problems. To this end, we aim to establish the interior Hölder regularity and boundedness of weak slutions to Eq. (
1.1
), which could be regarded as an analogue of
[
10
,
18
]
. It is noteworthy that our results are new even for the case
p=2
.

Before giving the notion of weak solutions to (
1.1
), let us recall the tail space

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{v\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(x)|^{q}}{1+|x|^{N+\alpha}}\,dx<+\infty\right\},\quad q>0\text{ and }\alpha>0.

Then we define the tail appearing in estimates throughout this article,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,I)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,t_{0}-T_{1},t_{0}+T_{2})

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t\in I}\left(r^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|v(x,t)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}},

(1.6)

where
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
and the interval
I=\left[t_{0}-T_{1},t_{0}+T_{2}\right]\subseteq(0,T)
. This is a parabolic counterpart to the tail introduced in
[
9
]
. It is easy to check that
\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,I)
is well-defined for any
v\in L^{\infty}(I;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in L^{p}(I;W_{\rm{loc}}^{s,p}(\Omega))\cap C(I;L_{\rm{loc}}^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(I;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
is a local weak subsolution (super-) to (
1.1
) if for any closed interval
I:=[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq(0,T)
, there holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(x,t_{2})\varphi(x,t_{2})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}u(x,t_{1})\varphi(x,t_{1})\,dx-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t)\,dt\leq(\geq)0,

(1.7)

for every nonnegative test function
\varphi\in L^{p}(I;W^{s,p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
with the property that
\varphi
has spatial support compactly contained in
\Omega
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle\Big[|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))

\displaystyle(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))K(x,y,t)\Big]\,dxdy.

A function
u
is a local weak solution to (
1.1
) if and only if
u
is a local weak subsolution and supersolution.

We now are in a position to state the main contribution of this work. First, we provide the local boundedness of weak solutions in the cases that
p>\frac{2N}{N+2}
and
1<p\leq\frac{2N}{N+2}
. For two real number, set

\displaystyle a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\},\quad a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\},\quad a_{-}:=-\min\{a,0\}.

Theorem 1.2

(Local boundedness)
.

Let
p>2N/(2+N)
and
q:=\max\{p,2\}
. Assume that
u
is a local weak subsolution to (
1.1
). Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{T}
,
R\in(0,1)
and
Q_{R}^{-}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0})
such that
\overline{B}_{R}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
and
[t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0}]\subseteq(0,T)
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{Q_{R/2}^{-}}u\leq\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}\left(u_{+};x_{0},R/2,t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0}\right)+C\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{R}^{-}}u_{+}^{q}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{N(p\kappa-q)}}\vee 1,

where
\kappa:=1+2/N
and
C>0
only depends on
N,p,s
and
\Lambda
.

In the scenario that
1<p\leq 2N/(N+2)
, assuming that the weak subsolution have the following constructions: for
m>\max\left\{2,\frac{N(2-p)}{p}\right\}
, there exists a sequence of
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
whose components are
bounded subsolutions of (
1.1
) such that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}\|_{L_{\rm{loc}}^{\infty}(0,T;L_{sp}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq C

(1.8)

and

\displaystyle u_{k}\rightarrow u\quad\text{in }L_{\rm{loc}}^{m}(Q_{T})\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

(1.9)

Theorem 1.3

(Local boundedness)
.

Let
1<p\leq 2N/(N+2)
,
\kappa=1+2/N
and
m>\max\left\{2,\frac{N(2-p)}{p}\right\}
. Suppose that
u\in L_{\rm{loc}}^{m}(Q_{T})
with the properties (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) is a local weak subsolution of (
1.1
). Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{T}
,
R\in(0,1)
and
Q_{R}^{-}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0})
such that
\overline{B}_{R}(x_{0})\subseteq\Omega
and
[t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0}]\subseteq(0,T)
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{Q_{R/2}^{-}}u

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}\left(u_{+};x_{0},R/2,t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{R}^{-}}u_{+}^{m}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{(N+p)(m-2-\beta)}}\vee\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{R}^{-}}u_{+}^{m}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{p}{(N+p)(m-p-\beta)}},

where
\beta=N(m-p\kappa)/(p+N)
and
C>0
only depends on
N,p,s,m
and
\Lambda
.

Based on the boundedness result (Theorem
1.2
), we are able to deduce that the weak solutions are locally Hölder continuous for
p>2
.

Theorem 1.4

(Hölder continuity)
.

Let
p>2
. Assume that
u
is a local weak solution of (
1.1
). Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{T}
,
R\in(0,1)
and
Q_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0}+R^{p})
such that
\overline{Q}_{R}\subseteq Q_{T}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,p/(p-1))
such that for every
\rho\in(0,R/2)
,

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,osc}_{Q_{\rho,d\rho^{p}}}u<C\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}\left[\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}\left(u;x_{0},R/2,t_{0}-R^{p},t_{0}+R^{p}\right)+\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{Q_{R}}|u|^{p}\,dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\vee 1\right]

with
d\in(0,1)
and
C\geq 1
depending on
N,p,s
and
\Lambda
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first give some notations for clarity and then provide some important inequalities to be used later.

2.1.
Notation

Let
B_{\rho}(x)
be the open ball with radius
r
and centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We denote the parabolic cylinders by
Q_{\rho,r}(x,t):=B_{\rho}(x)\times(t-r,t+r)
,
Q_{\rho}(x,t):=Q_{\rho,\rho^{p}}(x,t)=B_{\rho}(x)\times(t-\rho^{p},t+\rho^{p})
and
Q_{\rho}^{-}(x,t):=Q_{\rho,\rho^{p}}^{-}(x,t)=B_{\rho}(x)\times(t-\rho^{p},t)
with
r,\rho>0
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
. If not important, or clear from the context, we simply write these symbols by
B_{\rho}=B_{\rho}(x)
,
Q_{\rho,r}=Q_{\rho,r}(x,t)
,
Q_{\rho}=Q_{\rho}(x,t)
and
Q_{\rho}^{-}=Q_{\rho}^{-}(x,t)
. Moreover for
g\in L^{1}(V)
, we denote the integral average of
g
by

\displaystyle(g)_{V}:={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{V}g(x)\,dx:=\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}g(x)\,dx.

Define

\displaystyle J_{p}(a,b)=|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)

for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
We also use the notation

\displaystyle d\mu=d\mu(x,y,t)=K(x,y,t)\,dxdy.

It is worth mentioning that the constant
C
represents a general positive constant which may differ from each other.

Next, we will show several fundamental but very useful Sobolev inequalities.

2.2.
Sobolev inequalities

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p
,
\ell\leq q<\infty
satisfy
\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{q}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\theta\left(1-\frac{N}{p}+\frac{N}{q}\right)+(1-\theta)\left(\frac{N}{q}-\frac{N}{\ell}\right)=0

with
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
only depending on
N,p,q,\ell
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{1})}\leq C\|Du\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{\ell}(B_{1})}^{1-\theta}

(2.1)

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})\cap L^{\ell}(B_{1})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<N
. Then for every
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})
, there holds that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\frac{Np}{N-p}}(B_{1})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{1})},

where
C>0
only depends on
N
and
p
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then for every

\displaystyle u\in L^{p}\left(t_{1},t_{2};W^{1,p}(B_{r})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(B_{r})\right),

it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(r^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}},

(2.2)

where
C>0
only depends on
p
and
N
.

Proof.

We divide this proof into two cases.

Case 1:

1<p<N
. Applying Lemma
2.2
and Hölder inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{\frac{2p}{N}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{\frac{pN}{N-p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-p}{N}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{p}+|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt.

By scaling argument, we get

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(r^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}.

Case 2:

p\geq N
. We can easily find that

\displaystyle 1\geq\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N}{p(1+\frac{2}{N})},\quad p\left(1+\frac{2}{N}\right)>2

and

\displaystyle\theta\left(1-\frac{N}{p}+\frac{N}{p\left(1+\frac{2}{N}\right)}\right)+(1-\theta)\left(\frac{N}{p\left(1+\frac{2}{N}\right)}-\frac{N}{2}\right)=0

with
\theta=\frac{N}{N+2}
. Thus by Lemma
2.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p\left(1+\frac{2}{N}\right)}(B_{1})}\leq C\|Du\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{\frac{N}{N+2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{\frac{2}{N+2}}

for any
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
. Using scaling argument, we have

\displaystyle{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dx\leq Cr^{p}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\times\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}

for any
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
. Integrating the above inequality over
(t_{1},t_{2})
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\times\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then for every

\displaystyle u\in L^{p}\left(t_{1},t_{2};W^{1,p}(B_{r})\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(t_{1},t_{2};L^{p}(B_{r})\right),

it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{p}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\left(r^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}},

(2.3)

where
C>0
only depends on
p
and
N
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p\left(1+\frac{p}{N}\right)}(B_{1})}\leq C\|Du\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{\frac{N}{N+p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{\frac{p}{N+p}}

for all
t\in(t_{1},t_{2})
, where
C>0
only depends on
p
and
N
. Using scaling argument, we have

\displaystyle{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{p}{N})}\,dx\leq Cr^{p}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\times\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}.

Integrating the above inequality over
(t_{1},t_{2})
, we get

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p(1+\frac{p}{N})}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\times\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{p}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dxdt\times\left(\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{t_{1}<t<t_{2}}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}|u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{N}}.

∎

3.
Energy Estimates

In this section, we will establish the Caccioppoli inequality and Logarithmic form inequality for mixed local and nonlocal type equation (
1.1
). The first step of the proof should be the regularization procedure with respect to the time variable, which can be
performed by straightforward adaptation of standard reasonings as used in
[
4
,
10
]
. We omit this step here.

Lemma 3.1

(Caccioppoli-type inequality)
.

Let
p>1
and
u
be a local subsolution of (
1.1
). Let
B_{r}\equiv B_{r}(x_{0})
satisfy
\overline{B}_{r}\subseteq\Omega
and
0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}
,
\ell>0
satisfy
\left[\tau_{1}-\ell,\tau_{2}\right]\subseteq(0,T).
For any nonnegative functions
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{r})
and
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\eta(t)\equiv 0
if
t\leq\tau_{1}-\ell
and
\eta(t)\equiv 1
if
t\geq\tau_{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{\tau_{1}-\ell}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla w_{+}(x,t)|^{p}\psi^{p}(x)\eta^{2}(t)\,dxdt+\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{\tau_{1}<t<\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}w_{+}^{2}(x,t)\psi^{p}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\tau_{1}-\ell}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}|w_{+}(x,t)\psi(x)-w_{+}(y,t)\psi(y)|^{p}\eta^{2}(t)\,d\mu dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\tau_{1}-\ell}^{\tau_{2}}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{p}w_{+}^{p}(x,t)\eta^{2}(t)\,dxdt

Abstract
. We consider the one-dimensional outer stochastic Stefan problem with reflection. The problem admits maximal solutions as long as the velocity of the moving boundary remains bounded, [ 3 , 9 , 10 ] . We apply Malliavin calculus to the transformed equation and first prove that its maximal solution u u has continuous paths a.s. In the case of the unreflected problem, the previous enables the localization of a proper approximating sequence of the maximal solution. Then, we derive there locally the differentiability of maximal u u in the Malliavin sense. The novelty of this work, apart from the derivation of continuity of the paths for the maximal solution with reflection, is that for the unreflected case we introduce a localization argument on maximal solutions and define efficiently the relevant sample space. More precisely, we prove the local (in the sample space) existence of the Malliavin derivative and, under a non-degeneracy condition on the noise coefficient, the absolute continuity of the law of the solution with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Stochastic Stefan problem

We consider two well separated phases
\Omega_{{\rm Sol}}
and
\Omega_{{\rm Liq}}
, the solid and liquid phase respectively, occupying a static bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}
defined
by the interval
\Omega:=(a,b)
. Let
\Omega_{{\rm Sol}}(t):=(s^{-}(t),s^{+}(t))
be the solid phase at time
t
, and

\Omega_{{\rm Liq}}(t)=\Omega\setminus[s^{-}(t),s^{+}(t)],

(1.1)

so

\Omega=\Omega_{{\rm Liq}}(t)\cup\{s^{-}(t),s^{+}(t)\}\cup\Omega_{{\rm Sol}}(t).

We assume that the initial solid phase is in
\Omega
and far from its boundary, i.e., that
\lambda\gg s^{+}(0)-s^{-}(0)
, for
\lambda:=b-a
the length of
\Omega
.

The outer parabolic stochastic Stefan problem is defined by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w=\alpha\Delta w+\sigma\big({\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega_{\rm{Sol}})\big)\dot{W}_{s}(y,t)+\dot{\eta}_{s}(y,t),\qquad y\in\Omega_{\rm{Liq}}(t),\quad t>0\quad\mbox{(`liquid' phase)},\\
&w(y,t)=0,\qquad y\in\overline{\Omega_{\rm{Sol}}(t)},\quad t>0,\qquad\mbox{(`solid' phase)},\\
&V=-\nabla w|_{\partial\Omega_{\rm{Sol}}(t)},\quad t>0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{(Stefan condition)},\\
&w(a,t)=w(b,t)=0,\quad t>0,\\
&\partial\Omega_{\rm{Sol}}(0)=\{s^{-}(0),s^{+}(0)\}={\rm given}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Here,
w=w(y,t)
is a density,
\alpha>0
is a positive constant, and
\sigma
is a function of the distance of
y
from the solid phase boundary,
{\rm dist}(y,\partial\Omega_{\rm{Sol}}(t)):=\min\{|y-s^{+}(t)|,|y-s^{-}(t)|\}
. The noise term is given by

\dot{W}_{s}(y,t):=\dot{W}(y-s^{+}(t),t)\;\;\text{if}\;\;y\geq s^{+}(t),\;\;\dot{W}_{s}(y,t):=\dot{W}(-y+s^{-}(t),t)\;\;\text{if}\;\;y\leq s^{-}(t),

where
\dot{W}(\pm y\mp s^{\pm}(t),t)
is a space-time white noise,
see for example in
[
13
,
5
]
. The initial condition
w(y,0)=w_{0}(y)
is a sufficiently smooth deterministic function compactly supported in the liquid phase. The moving boundary of (
1.2
) is the union for all
t\geq 0
of the curves
y=s^{+}(t),\;\;y=s^{-}(t)
enclosing the solid phase with midpoint
s(t):=(s^{-}(t)+s^{+}(t))/2
and length
s^{+}(t)-s^{-}(t)
defining the spread. The solution
w(y,t)
vanishes when
y
is in the closure of the solid phase. The Stefan condition describes the
change of liquidity;
V
there is the velocity of the interface, and for
(\nabla\cdot)^{\pm}
denoting the derivative from the right (
y>s^{+}
) and left (
y<s^{-}
) it follows that

V(s^{+}(t),t):=\partial_{t}s^{+}(t)=-(\nabla w)^{+}(s^{+}(t),t),\;\;\;\;V(s^{-}(t),t):=\partial_{t}s^{-}(t)=-(\nabla w)^{-}(s^{-}(t),t).

The term on (
1.2
) is given by

\dot{\eta}_{s}(y,t):=\dot{\eta}_{1}(y-s^{+}(t),t)\quad\text{if}\quad y\geq s^{+}(t),\qquad\dot{\eta}_{s}(y,t):=\dot{\eta}_{2}(-y+s^{-}(t),t)\quad\text{if}\quad y\leq s^{-}(t),

where
\eta_{1}
,
\eta_{2}
are reflections.

1.2.
The transformed problem

The liquid phase consists of two separate bounded linear segments which enables the splitting of the Stefan problem equation in two equations posed for on
y\geq s^{+}
and on
y\leq s^{-}
. After the
change of variables

x:=y-s^{+}(t)\;\;\text{if }y\geq s^{+}(t),\qquad x:=-y+s^{-}(t)\;\;\text{if }y\leq s^{-}(t),

cf.
[
3
]
, the stochastic equation of (
1.2
) is transformed by the use of the Stefan condition into two independent ones each posed on the fixed space domain
\mathcal{D}:=(0,\lambda)
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The value
x=0
occurs when the price
y
is
s^{\pm}
, while
x=\lambda
when the spread is zero and
s^{+}=s^{-}
hits the boundary of
\Omega
. These equations are of the form

u_{t}(x,t)=\alpha u_{xx}(x,t)\mp u_{x}(0^{+},t)u_{x}(x,t)\pm\sigma(x)\dot{W}(x,t)+\dot{\eta}(x,t),\;\;x\in\mathcal{D},\;\;t>0,

(1.3)

with
\eta(dx,dt)
a random measure on
D\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
(reflection) keeping
u
a.s. non-negative, and a space-time white noise
\dot{W}(x,t)
where
W=W(x,t)
is a Wiener process. We also note that when a system is considered in place of the Stefan problem (
1.2
) with different liquidity coefficients
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
on the diffusion term
\Delta w
, a problem analyzed in
[
9
]
when the spread is zero, the same equation of the above general form (
1.3
) will appear after the change of variables for
\alpha=\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
.

Without restricting the generality, we consider one of the cases for the s.p.d.e. which yields the following transformed initial and boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{t}(x,t)=\alpha u_{xx}(x,t)-u_{x}(0,t)u_{x}(x,t)+\sigma(x)\dot{W}(x,t)+\dot{\eta}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}\times(0,T),\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\;\;\forall x\in\overline{\mathcal{D}},\\
&u(0,t)=u(\lambda,t)=0,\;\;\forall t\in[0,T].\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

As in
[
3
]
, we assume that

\sigma:\overline{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\;\mbox{ is a function
in}\;\;C(\overline{\mathcal{D}}),\;\;\mbox{and differentiable
at}\;x=0,\;\;\mbox{ with }\;\sigma(0)=\sigma(\lambda)=0,

(1.5)

and, since
w_{0}
is compactly supported on the initial liquid
phase, that

u_{0}\;\;\mbox{is a deterministic and non-negative function in}\;\;C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}).

(1.6)

The reflection measure
\eta
keeps
u
non-negative and satisfies

\begin{split}&\mbox{for all}\mbox{ measurable functions }\psi:\;\overline{\mathcal{D}}\times(0,T)\rightarrow[0,\infty)\\
&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\psi(x,s)\eta(dx,ds)\;\;\mbox{is}\;\;\mathcal{F}_{t}-\mbox{measurable}\end{split}

(1.7)

for
\mathcal{F}_{t}
the filtration generated by
\{W(x,s):0\leq s\leq t,x\in\mathcal{D}\}
, and the constraint

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{D}}u(x,s)\eta(dx,ds)=0.

(1.8)

The problem (
1.4
), (
1.7
), with
\sigma,u_{0}
satisfying (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) respectively, has a unique maximal solution
(u,\eta)
in the time interval
[0,T^{*})
, where

T^{*}:=\displaystyle{\sup_{M>0}}\tau_{M},

(1.9)

for

\tau_{M}:=\inf\Big\{T\geq 0:\;\displaystyle{\sup_{r\in[0,T)}}|u_{x}(0^{+},r)|\geq M\Big\}\equiv\inf\Big\{T\geq 0:\;\displaystyle{\sup_{r\in[0,T)}}u_{x}(0^{+},r)\geq M\Big\},

see in
[
3
]
. An analogous result for the same stopping time
T^{*}:=\sup_{M>0}\tau_{M}
was derived in
[
9
]
for a system of such SPDEs when
\alpha=\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
.

The solution
u
of problem (
1.4
) is written in integral representation

\forall x\in\mathcal{D},\ t\in[0,T^{*})
as

\begin{split}u(x,t)=&\int_{\mathcal{D}}u_{0}(y)\,G(x,y,t)\,dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}u_{y}(0,s)\,G_{y}(x,y,t-s)\,u(y,s)\,dy\,ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G(x,y,t-s)\sigma(y)\,W(dy,ds)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G(x,y,t-s)\,\eta(dy,ds),\end{split}

(1.10)

where
G
is the Green’s function of the Dirichlet problem on

\overline{\mathcal{D}}
of the heat equation
v_{t}=\alpha v_{xx}
.

We recall the Banach space

(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}})
, introduced in
[
9
]
,
where

\mathcal{H}:=\big\{f\in C(\overline{\mathcal{D}}):\;\exists\ f^{\prime}(0),\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\ f(0)=f(\lambda)=0\big\},\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\;\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\displaystyle{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}}\bigg|\dfrac{f(x)}{x}\bigg|.

(1.11)

Let us consider
M>0
deterministic and
T>0
deterministic, and for a positive integer
n
define the stopping time

\tilde{\tau}_{n}:=\inf\Big\{T\geq 0:\;\displaystyle{\sup_{r\in[0,T)}}\|u(\cdot,r)\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\displaystyle{\sup_{r\in[0,T)}}\,\displaystyle{\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}}\,\bigg|\dfrac{u(x,r)}{x}\bigg|\geq n\Big\},

(1.12)

it then holds that, cf.

[
9
]
,

E\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\sup_{n>0}\tilde{\tau}_{n}\})}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\Big)\leq C(T,M,p)<\infty,

(1.13)

where we used that in our problem

h(u_{y}(0,s))=u_{y}(0,s)
which has a linear growth as in the
result of
[
9
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that the bound of the above expectation depends on the choice of
M
. In the case where the term
u_{y}(0,s)
was replaced by some
h(u_{y}(0,s))
uniformly bounded for any
M>0
then the
solution
u
would be global, cf. Corollary 5.7 of
[
9
]
, and it would hold that

E\Big(\displaystyle{\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{T,\sup_{M>0}\tau_{M},\sup_{n>0}\tilde{\tau}_{n}\})}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}\Big)\leq C(T,p)<\infty.

(1.14)

We fix now
T>0
deterministic and
M>0
deterministic, and define

\Omega_{M}:=\Big\{\omega\in\Omega:\;\displaystyle{\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M}\})}}|u_{x}(0^{+},t,\omega)|<M\Big\},

(1.15)

and for any integer
n>0
, we define

\Omega_{M}^{n}:=\Big\{\omega\in\Omega_{M}:\;\displaystyle{\sup_{t\in[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M}\})}}\|u(\cdot,t,\omega)\|_{\mathcal{H}}<n\Big\}.

(1.16)

It then follows that

\Omega_{M}^{1}\subseteq\Omega_{M}^{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Omega_{M},

and

\Omega_{M}^{n}\uparrow\Omega_{M}
as

n\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

As we assume that the initial condition
u_{0}
satisfies (
1.6
), then it follows that for
t=0

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
satisfies the bounds in the definitions of
\Omega_{M}
, and of
\Omega_{M}^{n}
for
M
large enough and for all
n\geq n_{0}
respectively. This yields that the sets
\Omega_{M}
and
\Omega_{M}^{n}
are not empty for
M
large enough such that
\nabla u_{0}(0)<M
and for all
n\geq n_{0}
for
n_{0}
large enough such that
\|u_{0}(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}<n_{0}
, where of course

,

depend on the initial condition
u_{0}
.

1.3.
Green’s function

Throughout this paper, we denote by
G
the Green’s function for the heat equation
u_{t}(x,t)=\alpha u_{xx}(x,t)
on
[0,\lambda]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions, which is given by

G(x,y,t-s)=\frac{2}{\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sin(\tfrac{k\pi}{\lambda}y)\,\sin(\tfrac{k\pi}{\lambda}x)\,e^{-\alpha(\pi/\lambda)^{2}k^{2}(t-s)},

(1.17)

for
s<t.
An equivalent expression is

G(x,y,t-s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\alpha(t-s)}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\exp\Big(-\frac{(y-x+2k\lambda)^{2}}{4\alpha(t-s)}\Big)-\exp\Big(-\frac{(x+y+2k\lambda)^{2}}{4\alpha(t-s)}\Big)\right].

(1.18)

We will use the following estimates of
G
and its derivative
G_{y}
(see
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
). We have, for some constants
c,C>0,

|G(x,y,t-s)|\leq C\,|t-s|^{-1/2}\,\exp\Big(\!-c\,\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{|t-s|}\Big),

and so

|G(x,y,t-s)|^{p}\leq C\,|t-s|^{-\frac{p}{2}}\,\exp\Big(\!-c\,\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{|t-s|}\Big).

(1.19)

We also have

|G_{y}(x,y,t-s)|\leq c|t-s|^{-1}\exp\Big(\!-c\,\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{|t-s|}\Big),

(1.20)

and so

\int_{\mathcal{D}}|G_{y}(x,y,t-s)|dy\leq c|t-s|^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\exp\Big(\!-c\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{|t-s|}\Big)dy\leq c|t-s|^{-1/2},

(1.21)

where we used the Gaussian integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big(-c\,\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{|t-s|}\Big)dy=\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{c}}\;|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.22)

1.4.
Main Results

In Section 2, we consider the problem with reflection and prove that the paths of the sample space
\Omega_{M}
, where
M>0
is an arbitrary deterministic constant, are space-time continuous, i.e., for all realizations in
\Omega_{M}
where as proven in
[
9
,
3
]
the solution exists, it is also space-time continuous. Section 3 is devoted to the localization of
u
in the given sample space
\Omega_{M}
when the unreflected problem is considered. We present there some important definitions of the spaces where we investigate the Malliavin differentiability. We then define a proper approximation
u_{n}
of
u
for which
u_{n}=u
on
\Omega_{M}^{n}
and prove that
u_{n}
exists uniquely as a solution of a cut-off spde. Finally, in Section 4, we consider
M_{\rm d}>0
deterministic and arbitrary. We prove that the Malliavin derivative of
u
exists for times up to which the Malliavin derivative of the term in the definition of
\Omega_{M}
(i.e.,
u_{x}(0^{+},t,\omega)
) not only is bounded by
M
but also stays differentiable in the Malliavin sense with derivative upper bounded by
M_{\rm d}
. We additionally prove some estimates for the derivative and establish the absolute continuity of the law of the solution with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}.

Lastly, regarding the process

From all the above arguments, we conclude that

L_{n}(t):=E\left(\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{p}dyds\right)\;,

the inequality (
4.28
) becomes

L_{n}(t)\leq c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}+c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}+c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,L_{n}(\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{s}.

(4.29)

By (
4.29
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,L_{n}(\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,(\tilde{s}-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\,d\tilde{s}+c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,(\tilde{s}-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\,d\tilde{s}

(4.30)

\displaystyle+c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\int_{b-\epsilon}^{\tilde{s}}|\tilde{s}-\tau|^{-\beta p}\,L_{n}(\tau)\,d\tau\,d\tilde{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\,\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,d\tilde{s}+c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\,\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,d\tilde{s}

\displaystyle+c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\int_{b-\epsilon}^{\tilde{s}}|\tilde{s}-\tau|^{-\beta p}\,L_{n}(\tau)\,d\tau\,d\tilde{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{1-\beta p}\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}+1-\beta p}\,+\,\frac{c}{1-\beta p}\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1+1-\beta p}

\displaystyle+c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,|\tilde{s}-\tau|^{-\beta p}\,d\tilde{s}\Big]\,L_{n}(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{5-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+4}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}L_{n}(\tau)\,d\tau\,,

where we used that (see (
4.13
))

\int_{\tau}^{t}|t-\tilde{s}|^{-\beta p}\,|\tilde{s}-\tau|^{-\beta p}\,\,d\tilde{s}\leq C<+\infty.

Using (
4.30
) in the last term of (
4.29
), we obtain

L_{n}(t)\leq c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}+c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\,+c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{5-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+4}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}L_{n}(\tau)\,d\tau\;.

(4.31)

By Grönwall’s lemma, we conclude that, for
t\leq b<\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\},

L_{n}(t)\leq c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}+c(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\,+c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{5-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+4}{2}-\beta p}\,,

that is

\displaystyle E\left(\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{p}\,dy\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}

(4.32)

\displaystyle+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{5-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+4}{2}-\beta p}.

Using that
D_{y,s}\bigl(u_{n}(x,t)\bigr)=0
for
s>t
and any
x\in\mathcal{D}
, and (
4.32
), we obtain, for
b-\epsilon\leq t\leq b,

\displaystyle E\left(\int_{b-\epsilon}^{b}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{p}\,dy\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\left(\int_{b-\epsilon}^{t}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{p}\,dy\,ds\right)

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}

\displaystyle+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{5-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,(t-b+\epsilon)^{\frac{p+4}{2}-\beta p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\,+\,c\,\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}+1}\,+\,c\,\epsilon^{\frac{5-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,\epsilon^{\frac{p+4}{2}-\beta p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\epsilon^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\,,

for
\beta\in((3-p)/(2p),1/(2p)).
Taking the supremum in (
4.33
) over
t\in[b-\epsilon,b],
we get (
4.18
).

Furthermore, using (
4.1
), (
4.26
), (
4.27
) with
b=t
and (
4.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle E\biggl(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\Big[\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)-G(x,y,t-s)\sigma(y)|^{p}\,dy\Big]ds\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{p-1}E\left(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\bigg|\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})\ [u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s})\mathcal{G}_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})D_{y,s}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})]\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}\bigg|^{p}dy\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\;2^{p-1}E\left(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\sup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|A|^{p}\,dy\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,\epsilon^{1+\frac{3-p}{2}-\beta p}\,+\,c\,\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\,\epsilon^{1+\frac{3-p}{2}-\beta p},

which proves the estimate (
4.19
) with
\delta:=\beta p-(3-p)/2.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Consider a given deterministic
T>0,
and
x\in\mathcal{D}
with
\sigma(x)\neq 0.
Then, for any
t\in[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\})
, we have that

P\big(\omega\in\Omega_{M}^{n}:\;\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}(u_{n}(\omega;x,t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}\times[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}))}>0\big)=1,

(4.34)

where
u_{n}
is the unique solution of (
3.2
).

Proof.

We keep
t
less than
T
deterministic and the stopping times
used in the previous Proposition. Recall (
4.1
) i.e.,

\begin{split}D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)=\,&G(x,y,t-s)\sigma(y)+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s})\ \mathcal{G}_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})D_{y,s}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}\\
&+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})D_{y,s}(u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s}))\tilde{y}T_{n}(\tilde{y}^{-1}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s}))\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}.\end{split}

In the above, taking absolute value and then raising to the second power, we get

\displaystyle|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{2}=\bigg|G(x,y,t-s)\sigma(y)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s})\ \mathcal{G}_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})D_{y,s}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})D_{y,s}(u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s}))\tilde{y}T_{n}(\tilde{y}^{-1}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s}))\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}\biggl|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}A^{2}-B^{2},

where

A(x,y,t,s):=G(x,y,t-s)\sigma(y),

and

\displaystyle B(x,y,t,s):=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s})\ \mathcal{G}_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})D_{y,s}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s})\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G_{\tilde{y}}(x,\tilde{y},t-\tilde{s})D_{y,s}(u_{\tilde{y}}(0,\tilde{s}))\tilde{y}T_{n}(\tilde{y}^{-1}u_{n}(\tilde{y},\tilde{s}))\,d\tilde{y}\,d\tilde{s}.

Then, we integrate to obtain

\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{2}\,dy\,ds\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}A^{2}\,dy\,ds\,-\,\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}B^{2}\,dy\,ds.

(4.35)

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.35
), we have (cf.
[
6
, Lemma B.2.3]
), for small
\epsilon,

\displaystyle\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}A^{2}\,dy\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{D}}G^{2}(x,y,\tau)\,\sigma^{2}(y)\,dy\,d\tau

(4.36)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{x-\sqrt{\epsilon}}^{x+\sqrt{\epsilon}}G^{2}(x,y,\tau)\,\sigma^{2}(y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}(x)}{2}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{x-\sqrt{\epsilon}}^{x+\sqrt{\epsilon}}G^{2}(x,y,\tau)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{1}\,\epsilon^{1/2}.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.35
), we use (
4.1
) and (
4.19
), to obtain that

\displaystyle E\biggl(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}B^{2}\,dy\,ds\biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\biggl(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Big|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)-G(x,y,t-s)\sigma(y)\Big|^{2}\,dy\,ds\biggr)

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\,\epsilon^{1-\delta},

for any
\delta\in(0,1).
By (
4.35
)-(
4.37
) and Markov inequality, we get, for
0<\delta<1/2,

\displaystyle P\biggl(\int_{0}^{\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t)|^{2}dyds>0\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}A^{2}dyds-\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}B^{2}dyds>0\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P\biggl(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}B^{2}\,dy\,ds<\frac{c_{1}}{2}\epsilon^{1/2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-c\,\epsilon^{-1/2}E\biggl(\int_{t-\epsilon}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{D}}B^{2}\,dy\,ds\biggr)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1\,-\,c\,\epsilon^{-1/2}\,\epsilon^{1-\delta}\rightarrow 1,\qquad\text{as}\ \epsilon\rightarrow 0,

which yields (
4.34
).
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
1.10
). Then, for any
(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}\times(0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\})
with
\sigma(x)\neq 0,
we have that

P\big(\{\omega\in\Omega_{M}:\;\left\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}(u(\omega;x,t))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}\times[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}))}>0\}\big)=1,

and the law of
u(x,t)
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

Using that
\Omega_{M}^{1}\subseteq\Omega_{M}^{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\Omega_{M}
so that
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P(\Omega_{M}^{n})=P(\Omega_{M}),

the fact that
D_{y,s}u(x,t,\omega)=D_{y,s}u_{n}(x,t,\omega)
on
\Omega_{M}^{n}
a.s., and (
4.34
), we obtain that

\displaystyle P\big(\{\omega\in\Omega_{M}:\;\left\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}(u(\omega;x,t))\right\|

{}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}\times[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}))}>0\}\big)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P\big(\{\omega\in\Omega_{M}^{n}:\;\left\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}(u(\omega;x,t))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}\times[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}))}>0\}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P\big(\{\omega\in\Omega_{M}^{n}:\;\left\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}(u_{n}(\omega;x,t))\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{D}\times[0,\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}))}>0\}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

therefore,

P\Big(\int_{0}^{\min\{T,\tau_{M},\tau_{M_{\rm d}}\}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}|D_{y,s}u(x,t)|^{2}\,dy\,ds>0\Big)=1.

(4.38)

We also have
u(x,t)\in D^{1,2}_{\text{loc}}\subset D^{1,1}_{\text{loc}}.
Therefore, (
4.38
) and
[
11
, Theorem 2.1.3]
imply that the law of
u(x,t)
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}.

∎

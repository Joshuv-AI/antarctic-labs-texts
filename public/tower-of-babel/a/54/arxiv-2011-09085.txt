Implicative algebras II: completeness w.r.t. Set-based triposes
Alexandre Miquel

Abstract
. We prove that all 𝐒𝐞𝐭 \mathbf{Set} -based triposes are implicative triposes.



1.
Introduction

In
[
3
]
, we introduced the notion of
implicative
algebra
, a simple algebraic structure that is intended to factorize
the model constructions underlying forcing and realizability, both in
intuitionistic and classical logic.
We showed that this algebraic structure induces a large class of
(
\mathbf{Set}
-based) triposes—the
implicative triposes
—, that
encompasses all (intuitionistic and classical) forcing triposes, all
classical realizability triposes (both in the sense of
Streicher
[
6
]
and Krivine
[
3
]
) as well as all the
intuitionistic realizability triposes induced by partial combinatory
algebras (in the style of Hyland, Johnstone and Pitts
[
2
]
).

The aim of this paper is to prove that the class of implicative
triposes actually encompasses all
\mathbf{Set}
-based triposes, in the sense
that:

Theorem 1.1
.

Each
\mathbf{Set}
-based tripos is (isomorphic to) an implicative tripos.

For that, we first recall some notions about triposes and
implicative algebras.

1.1.
\mathbf{Set}
-based triposes

In what follows, we write:

•

\mathbf{Set}
the category of sets equipped with all functions;

•

\mathbf{Pos}
the category of posets equipped with monotonic
functions;

•

\mathbf{HA}
the category of Heyting algebras equipped with
the corresponding morphisms.

In the category
\mathbf{Set}
, we write:

•

1
the terminal object (i.e. a fixed singleton);

•

1_{X}:X\to 1
the unique map from a given set
X
to
1
;

•

X\times Y
the Cartesian product of two sets
X
and
Y
,
and

•

\pi_{X,Y}:X\times Y\to X
and
\pi^{\prime}_{X,Y}:X\times Y\to Y

the associated projections.

•

Finally, given two functions
f:Z\to X
and
g:Z\to Y
,
we write
\langle f,g\rangle:Z\to X\times Y
the unique function such that

\pi_{X,Y}\circ\langle f,g\rangle=f
and
\pi^{\prime}_{X,Y}\circ\langle f,g\rangle=g
.

To conclude this presentation of triposes, we recall a few facts about
left and right adjoints:

Lemma 1.6
.

For all maps
f:X\to Y
and for all predicates
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}{X}
, we
have:

\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad\qquad}l}\exists{f}(p\lor p^{\prime})~=~\exists{f}(p)\lor\exists{f}(p^{\prime})&\exists{f}(\bot_{X})~=~\bot_{Y}\\[3.0pt]
\forall{f}(p\land p^{\prime})~=~\forall{f}(p)\land\forall{f}(p^{\prime})&\forall{f}(\top_{X})~=~\top_{Y}\\
\end{array}

Proof.

Let us treat the case of
\exists{f}
.
For all
q\in\mathsf{P}{Y}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{r@{\quad}c@{\quad}l}\exists{f}(p\lor p^{\prime})\leq q&\text{iff}&p\lor p^{\prime}\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(q)\\
&\text{iff}&p\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(q)~{}~\text{and}~{}~p^{\prime}\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(q)\\
&\text{iff}&\exists{f}(p)\leq q~{}~\text{and}~{}~\exists{f}(p^{\prime})\leq q\\
&\text{iff}&\exists{f}(p)\lor\exists{f}(p^{\prime})\leq q\\
\end{array}

hence
\exists{f}(p\lor p^{\prime})=\exists{f}(p)\lor\exists{f}(p^{\prime})
.
Moreover, we have
\bot_{X}\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(\bot_{Y})
, hence

\exists{f}(\bot_{X})\leq\bot_{Y}
, and thus

\exists{f}(\bot_{X})=\bot_{Y}
.
The proofs of the corresponding properties for
\forall{f}

proceed dually.

:

\exists{f}~=~\forall{f}~=~\mathsf{P}{f^{-1}}~=~(\mathsf{P}{f})^{-1}\,.

(2)

If
f
has a right inverse, then
\exists{f}
and
\forall{f}

are left inverses of
\mathsf{P}{f}
:

\exists{f}\circ\mathsf{P}{f}~=~\forall{f}\circ\mathsf{P}{f}~=~\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{Y}}\,.

(3)

If
f
has a left inverse, then
\exists{f}
and
\forall{f}

are right inverses of
\mathsf{P}{f}
:

\mathsf{P}{f}\circ\exists{f}~=~\mathsf{P}{f}\circ\forall{f}~=~\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{X}}\,.

Proof.

(1) If
f:X\to Y
is bijective, then for all
p\in\mathsf{P}{X}
and

q\in\mathsf{P}{Y}
, we have

•

\exists{f}(p)\leq q~{}~\text{iff}~{}~p\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(q)~{}~\text{iff}~{}~(\mathsf{P}{f})^{-1}(p)\leq q
, hence:
\exists{f}=(\mathsf{P}{f})^{-1}(p)=\mathsf{P}{f^{-1}}(p).

•

q\leq\forall{f}(p)~{}~\text{iff}~{}~\mathsf{P}{f}(q)\leq p~{}~\text{iff}~{}~q\leq(\mathsf{P}{f})^{-1}(p)
, hence:
\forall{f}=(\mathsf{P}{f})^{-1}(p)=\mathsf{P}{f^{-1}}(p).

(2) Let
g:Y\to X
such that
f\circ g=\mathrm{id}_{Y}
.
By functoriality, we get
\mathsf{P}{g}\circ\mathsf{P}{f}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{Y}}
, hence the
map
\mathsf{P}{f}:\mathsf{P}{Y}\to\mathsf{P}{X}
is an embedding of posets.
For all
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}{Y}
, we thus have:

•

\exists{f}(\mathsf{P}{f}(q))\leq q^{\prime}~{}~\text{iff}~{}~\mathsf{P}{f}(q)\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(q^{\prime})~{}~\text{iff}~{}~q\leq q^{\prime}
, hence
\exists{f}\circ\mathsf{P}{f}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{Y}}
.

•

q^{\prime}\leq\forall{f}(\mathsf{P}{f}(q))~{}~\text{iff}~{}~\mathsf{P}{f}(q^{\prime})\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(q)~{}~\text{iff}~{}~q^{\prime}\leq q
, hence
\forall{f}\circ\mathsf{P}{f}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{Y}}
.

(3) Let
g:Y\to X
such that
g\circ f=\mathrm{id}_{X}
.
By functoriality, we get

\forall{g}\circ\forall{f}=\exists{g}\circ\exists{f}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{X}}
,
hence
\exists{f},\forall{f}:\mathsf{P}{X}\to\mathsf{P}{Y}
are embeddings of
posets.
For all
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathsf{P}{X}
, we thus have:

•

p^{\prime}\leq\mathsf{P}{f}(\exists{f}(p))~{}~\text{iff}~{}~\exists{f}(p^{\prime})\leq\exists{f}(p)~{}~\text{iff}~{}~p^{\prime}\leq p
, hence
\mathsf{P}{f}\circ\exists{f}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{X}}
.

•

\mathsf{P}{f}(\forall{f}(p))\leq p^{\prime}~{}~\text{iff}~{}~\forall{f}(p)\leq\forall{f}(p^{\prime})~{}~\text{iff}~{}~p\leq p^{\prime}
, hence
\mathsf{P}{f}\circ\forall{f}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{P}{X}}
.∎

1.2.
Implicative algebras

Recall that:

•

An
implicative structure
is a complete (meet-semi)lattice

(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq})
equipped with a binary operation
({\to}):\mathscr{A}^{2}\to\mathscr{A}

such that:

(1)

If
a^{\prime}\preccurlyeq a
and
b\preccurlyeq b^{\prime}
, then
(a\to b)\preccurlyeq(a^{\prime}\to b^{\prime})
.

(2)

For all
a\in\mathscr{A}
and
B\subseteq\mathscr{A}
, we have:
\displaystyle a\to\bigcurlywedge_{b\in B}\!\!b~=~\bigcurlywedge_{b\in B}\!(a\to b)
.

•

A
separator
of an implicative structure

(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to})
is a subset
S\subseteq\mathscr{A}
such that:

(1)

If
a\in S
and
a\preccurlyeq a^{\prime}
, then
a^{\prime}\in S
.

(2)

\bigcurlywedge_{a,b\in\mathscr{A}}(a\to b\to c)~{}~({=}~\mathbf{K}^{\mathscr{A}})~\in~S
and

\bigcurlywedge_{a,b,c\in\mathscr{A}}((a\to b\to c)\to(a\to b)\to a\to c)~{}~({=}~\mathbf{S}^{\mathscr{A}})~\in~S
.

(3)

If
(a\to b)\in S
and
a\in S
, then
b\in S
.

Moreover, we say that a separator
S\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is

classical
when

(4)

\bigcurlywedge_{a,b\in\mathscr{A}}(((a\to b)\to a)\to a)~{}~({=}~\mathbf{cc}^{\mathscr{A}})~\in~S
.

•

An
implicative algebra
is a quadruple

(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to},S)
where
(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to})
is an implicative
structure and where
S\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is a separator.
An implicative algebra is
classical
when the underlying
separator
S\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is classical.

In
[
3
, §4]
, we have seen that each implicative algebra

(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to},S)
induces a
\mathbf{Set}
-based tripos

\mathsf{P}:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{HA}
that is defined as follows:

•

For each set
X
, the corresponding Heyting algebra
\mathsf{P}{X}
is
defined as the poset reflection of the preordered set

(\mathscr{A}^{X},\vdash_{S[X]})
whose preorder
\vdash_{S[X]}
is given by

[formula]

a\vdash_{S[X]}b\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\bigcurlywedge_{x\in X}(a_{x}\to b_{x})~\in~S

The quotient
\mathscr{A}^{X}/{\mathrel{{\dashv}{\vdash}}_{S[X]}}~({=}~\mathsf{P}{X})
is also written

\mathscr{A}^{X}/S[X]
.

•

For each map
f:X\to Y
, the corresponding substitution map

\mathsf{P}{f}:\mathsf{P}{Y}\to\mathsf{P}{X}
is the (unique) morphism of Heyting algebras
that factors the map
\mathscr{A}^{f}:\mathscr{A}^{Y}\to\mathscr{A}^{X}
through the quotients

\mathsf{P}{Y}=\mathscr{A}^{Y}/S[Y]
and
\mathsf{P}{X}=\mathscr{A}^{X}/S[X]
.

The aim of this paper is thus to show that all
\mathbf{Set}
-based triposes
are of this form.

2.1.

From now on, we work with a fixed tripos

.

From the definition,
\mathsf{P}
has a
generic predicate
, that is: a
predicate
\textit{tr}_{\Sigma}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma
(for some set

) such that for
each set

, the corresponding ‘decoding map’ is surjective:

\begin{array}[]{r@{~{}~}c@{~{}~}l}\llbracket\_\rrbracket_{X}~:~\Sigma^{X}&\to&\mathsf{P}{X}\\

\end{array}

Intuitively,
\Sigma
can be thought of as the set of (codes of)
propositions, whereas

can be thought of as the set of

propositional functions over
X
.
The above condition thus expresses that each predicate

is
represented by at least one propositional function

\sigma\in\Sigma^{X}
such that
\llbracket\sigma\rrbracket_{X}=p
, which we shall
call a

for the predicate

.

.

, the predicate

.

, we write

indexed by the singleton
1
, and
\llbracket\xi\rrbracket_{\_\in 1}\in\mathsf{P}{1}
the
associated predicate.

Remarks 2.3

(Non-uniqueness of generic predicates)
.

It is important to observe that in a
\mathbf{Set}
-based tripos
\mathsf{P}
, the
generic predicate is never unique.

•

Indeed, given a generic predicate
\textit{tr}_{\Sigma}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma

and a surjection
h:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
, we can always construct
another generic predicate
\textit{tr}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma^{\prime}
by letting

\textit{tr}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}=\mathsf{P}{h}(\textit{tr}_{\Sigma})
1
1

1

To prove that
\textit{tr}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma^{\prime}
is another
generic predicate, we actually need a right inverse of

h:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
, which exists by (AC).
Without (AC), the same argument works by replacing ‘surjective’
with ‘having a right inverse’.
.

•

More generally, if
\textit{tr}_{\Sigma}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma
and

\textit{tr}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma^{\prime}
are two generic predicates of the same
tripos
\mathsf{P}
, then there always exist two conversion maps

h:\Sigma^{\prime}\to\Sigma
and
h^{\prime}:\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
such that

\textit{tr}_{\Sigma^{\prime}}=\mathsf{P}{h}(\textit{tr}_{\Sigma})
and

\textit{tr}_{\Sigma}=\mathsf{P}{h^{\prime}}(\textit{tr}_{\Sigma^{\prime}})
.

In what follows, we will work with a fixed generic predicate

\textit{tr}_{\Sigma}\in\mathsf{P}\Sigma
.

Let us treat (for instance) the case of implication:

\end{array}

And similarly for

Remark 2.8
.

At this stage, it would be tempting to see the set

3.
Extracting the implicative algebra

In Section
2.4
, we have seen that the structure of
the tripos
\mathsf{P}:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{HA}
can be fully characterized from the
binary operations

({\mathbin{\dot{{\land}}}}),({\mathbin{\dot{{\lor}}}}),({\mathbin{\dot{{\to}}}}):\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\Sigma

and the infinitary operations

({\mathbin{\dot{{\bigvee}}}}),({\mathbin{\dot{{\bigwedge}}}}):\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to\Sigma
via some subset

\Phi\subseteq\Sigma
(the ‘pseudo-filter’).
However, these operations fail to endow the set
\Sigma
itself with
the structure of a complete Heyting algebra, for they lack the most
basic algebraic properties that are required by such a structure.

In this section, we shall construct a particular implicative structure

\mathscr{A}=(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to})
from the set
\Sigma
equipped with the only
operations
({\mathbin{\dot{{\to}}}}):\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\Sigma
and

({\mathbin{\dot{{\bigwedge}}}}):\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\to\Sigma

reminiscent from the construction of graph models

]
.
As we shall see, the main interest of such a construction is that:

(1)

The carrier set
\mathscr{A}
can be used as an alternative set of
propositions, for it possesses its own generic predicate

\textit{tr}_{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathsf{P}\mathscr{A}
.

(2)

The two operations
({\to}):\mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
and

({\bigcurlywedge}):\mathfrak{P}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathscr{A}
that naturally come with the
implicative structure
\mathscr{A}
constitute codes (in the sense of the
new generic predicate
\textit{tr}_{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathsf{P}\mathscr{A}
) for implication and
universal quantification in the tripos
\mathsf{P}
.

(3)

The ordering on each Heyting algebra
\mathsf{P}{X}
(for
X\in\mathbf{Set}
)
can be characterized from the above two operations via a particular
separator
S\subseteq\mathscr{A}
.

From the above properties, we shall easily conclude that the
tripos
\mathsf{P}:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{HA}
is isomorphic to the tripos induced by
the implicative algebra
(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to},S)
.

2

2

Remark 3.2

(Relationship with graph models)
.

In the theory of graph models
[
1
]
, the set of atoms

[formula]

\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{A}_{0}\quad::=\quad\dot{\xi}\quad|\quad\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\mapsto\beta

that is, as the least solution of the set-theoretic equation

\mathscr{A}_{0}=\Sigma+\mathfrak{P}_{\text{fin}}(\mathscr{A}_{0})\times\mathscr{A}_{0}

However, such a construction would yield an

in the left-hand side of the

actually prevents
defining an implication with the desired properties.

To fix this problem, it would be natural to relax the condition of

\mathscr{A}_{0}=\Sigma+\mathfrak{P}(\mathscr{A}_{0})\times\mathscr{A}_{0}

Alas, such an equation has no solution (for obvious cardinality
reasons), so that the trick consists to replace arbitrary subsets of

\mathscr{A}_{0}
(in the left-hand side of the construction

by arbitrary subsets of
\Sigma
, using the fact that the subsets
of
\mathscr{A}_{0}
can always be converted (element-wise) into subsets
of
\Sigma

So that in the end, we obtain the set-theoretic equation

\mathscr{A}_{0}=\Sigma+\mathfrak{P}(\Sigma)\times\mathscr{A}_{0}
, whose least solution is
precisely the set
\mathscr{A}_{0}
we defined above.

3.7.
Constructing the isomorphism

Let us now write
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}:\mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{HA}
the tripos induced by the
implicative algebra
(\mathscr{A},{\preccurlyeq},{\to},S)
(Section
1.2
).
Recall that for each set
X
, the Heyting algebra
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}{X}:=\mathscr{A}^{X}/S[X]

is the poset reflection of the preordered set
(\mathscr{A}^{X},\vdash_{S[X]})

whose preorder
\vdash_{S[X]}
is given by

[formula]

a\vdash_{S[X]}b\quad\text{iff}\quad\bigcurlywedge_{x\in X}(a_{x}\to b_{x})~\in~S

It now remains to show that:

Proposition 3.15
.

The implicative tripos
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the tripos
\mathsf{P}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the family of maps

\rho_{X}:=\langle\mskip-4.0mu\langle\_\rangle\mskip-4.0mu\rangle_{X}:\mathscr{A}^{X}\to\mathsf{P}{X}
, which is clearly natural
in the parameter set
X
.
From Prop.
3.14
, we have

[formula]

a\vdash_{S[X]}b\quad\text{iff}\quad\bigcurlywedge_{x\in X}(a_{x}\to b_{x})~\in~S\quad\text{iff}\quad\rho_{X}(a)\leq\rho_{X}(b)

hence
\rho_{X}:\mathscr{A}^{X}\to\mathsf{P}{X}
induces an embedding of
posets
\tilde{\rho}_{X}:\mathsf{P}^{\prime}{X}\to\mathsf{P}{X}
through the quotient

\mathsf{P}^{\prime}{X}:=\mathscr{A}^{X}/S[X]
.
Moreover, the map
\tilde{\rho}_{X}:\mathsf{P}^{\prime}{X}\to\mathsf{P}{X}
is surjective
(since
\rho_{X}
is), therefore it is an isomorphism
from the tripos
\mathsf{P}^{\prime}
to the tripos
\mathsf{P}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
p.
1.1
is now complete.

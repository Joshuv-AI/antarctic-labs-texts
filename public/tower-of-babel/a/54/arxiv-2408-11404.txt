Towards Brill-Noether Theory for Spectral Curves
Clemens Nollau

Abstract
We study Brill-Noether loci of three kinds of spectral curves: classical spectral curves as introduced by Hitchin, spectral curves over the projective line and double covers whose branch locus is a canonical divisor. Our techniques are based on the Beauville-Narasimhan-Ramanan correspondence: We push down line bundles on the spectral curve to the base curve and then we study the Higgs bundles obtained in this way. For the first kind we study the spaces of pencils in the Picard variety of a classical spectral curve in detail. In the case of spectral curves over the projective line we deal with their splitting loci which refine the Brill-Noether loci in the Picard variety. We compute their dimensions and investigate whether they are smooth. For the third kind we determine the gonality sequence when the rank of the linear system is much smaller than the genus. For this the base curve and the branch divisor are assumed to be general.

Introduction

The study of curves and their linear systems is a classical topic in algebraic geometry and has been a driving force in its modern developement. The description of all complete linear systems on a general curve in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
began in the 19th century but rigorous proofs of the Brill-Noether theorems were only available in the 1980s. For an in-depth treatment see
[
1
]
.

Today one also seeks to describe the spaces
W^{r}_{d}(C)
on curves
C
which are very much not general. Recently the Brill-Noether theorems have been extended to curves
C
together with a branched cover
\psi:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
n
by Hannah Larson in
[
12
]
. More precisely,
\psi:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
should be a general element of the Hurwitz space
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
. She introduced the splitting loci of
C
:

U^{(e,m)}(C)=\left\{L\in\operatorname{Pic}(C):\psi_{*}L\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}\right\}.

The splitting type
(e,m)
is the vector
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{l};m_{1},\ldots,m_{l})
. They stratify the classical Brill-Noether loci because every vector bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
splits as a direct sum of line bundles. We remark that our labeling of splitting loci differs from the one used in
[
12
]
. The main result of
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
can be summarized as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
12
,
11
, Larson, Larson, Vogt]

Let
\psi:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a general cover of degree
d
and genus
g
. Then
U^{(e,m)}(C)
has dimension

\rho(g,e,m):=g(C)-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}-1)m_{i}m_{j}.

if
\rho(g,e,m)
is non-negative. Otherwise the splitting locus is empty. Furthermore
U^{(e,m)}(C)
is irreducible if
\rho(g,e,m)>0
.

It is also interesting to look at other classes of special curves and attempt a similar description. Here we start treating spectral curves. These are curves
\widetilde{C}
which are cut out by a single equation in the global space
\mathbb{A}(N)
of a line bundle
N
over a curve
C
. Our approach rests upon the Hitchin correspondence between line bundles on
\widetilde{C}
and Higgs bundles with a fixed characteristic polynomial on
C
. All we need to know about spectral curves will be reviewed in section
2
.

In Section
3
we deal with spectral covers
\psi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
over the projective line. Inspired by Theorem
1.1
we prove the following statement about these loci:

Theorem A
.

Let
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be a general element of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(ik))
and denote by
\widetilde{C}
the associated spectral curve. Write
V
for the vector bundle
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}
.
Then

\dim U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})\leq h^{0}(\End(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(k))+1-\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}+1)m_{i}m_{j}-d-k\binom{n+1}{2}

(1)

holds for a splitting type
(e,m)
if the right hand side is nonnegative. This is either an equality or the splitting locus
U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})
is empty.
Take
(e,m)
to be a splitting type satisfying
e_{1}-e_{l}+k\geq-1
. We have

\dim U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})=g(\widetilde{C})-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}-1)m_{i}m_{j}

(2)

if the right hand side is nonnegative.
Furthermore in this case the space
U{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})
is smooth.

While our result gives a weaker result about general covers of a given degree it has the advantage of being quite simple: Instead of degenerating to a chain of elliptic curves like in
[
12
]
we work directly with smooth curves. For a more precise comparison see Remark
3.5
.

In the rest of the paper we study branched covers where the base curve has positive genus. In section
5
we treat spectral curves as introduced by Hitchin in
[
9
]
. They are spectral curves
\widetilde{C}
of degree
2
over a curve
C
defined with respect to the line bundle
N=K_{C}
. We refer to them as
classical spectral curves
. Inspired by the splitting loci which we have just introduced we stratify the spaces of pencils
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
. If
L
is a line bundle on the spectral curve with
h^{0}(\widetilde{C},L)\geq 2
, we distinguish cases based on whether the pushforward of
L
to
C
is stable, strictly semistable or unstable. Diagram
1
illustrates this approach.
Note that if
g
is the genus of
C
then
\widetilde{C}
has genus
4g-3
. Hence all line bundles in
\operatorname{Pic}^{4g-2}(\widetilde{C})
have two linearly independent global sections. We find the dimensions of all strata in the range
0\leq d\leq 4g-3
. Stating the result turns out to be quite involved so we restrict ourselves in the introduction to the most important stratum: Consider the open subscheme
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})_{s}
of
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
consisting of line bundles whose pushforward is stable. In Theorem
5.7
, we prove among other things:

Theorem B
.

Let
C
be a general curve of genus
g\geq 3
. Let
\psi:\widetilde{C}\to C
be a spectral cover corresponding to a general element
(s_{1},s_{2})
of the Hitchin base
H^{0}(K_{C})\oplus H^{0}(K_{C}^{2})
.
The stratum
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})_{s}
is empty if
d<2g+1
. Assume
2g+1\leq d\leq 4g-3
. Then the dimension of
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})_{s}
is
2d-4g+1
. This is the Brill-Noether number
\rho(g(\widetilde{C}),1,d)
.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that for
d=2g
we have
2d-4g+1=1
but
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
is still empty. The explanation for this is the following: Pushing forward a line bundle on
\widetilde{C}
of degree
2g
gives a vector bundle
V
of rank
2
and degree
2
. Then
V
can not be stable and satisfy
h^{0}(C,V)\geq 2
by
[
19
]
.

As a consequence of describing the dimensions of all strata we obtain for
\widetilde{C}
as in Theorem B the following:

Corollary 1.3
.

The gonality of
\widetilde{C}
is
2\lfloor\frac{g+3}{2}\rfloor
. The equality
\dim W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})=\rho(g(\widetilde{C}),1,d)
holds if and only if
3g-3\leq d\leq 4g-2
. If
3g-3<d
, then
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
is the closure of
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})_{s}
.

Hence we can say that
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
behaves as predicted by classical Brill-Noether theory if
d\geq 3g-3
.
To prove that the stratum
W^{1}_{d}(\widetilde{C})_{s}
is actually nonempty for a general spectral curve

requires some technical work which is done in section
4
. It turns out that we need to consider for certain vector bundles
V
of rank
2
on
C
the following map:

h_{V}:H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C})\to H^{0}(K_{C}^{2}),\phi\mapsto\det\phi.

Here
\End_{0}(V)
denotes the trace-free endomorphisms of
V
. We refer to
h_{V}
as the
restricted Hitchin map
. We give partial answers to the following question

Question 1.4
.

Is
h_{V}
dominant for all stable vector bundles

of rank
2
on a curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
?

The approach in section
4
might be of independent interest in the study of the Hitchin system.

In section
6
we look at spectral curves defined with respect to a theta characteristic instead of the canonical line bundle. The double covers we obtain can be defined as follows:

.

A double cover between smooth curves
\psi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
is called a

if the branch divisor of

For the source curve
\widetilde{C}
of a general canonical cover, we determine the gonality and more generally the gonality sequence

. Here

is the smallest integer

such that a

exists on

. The gonality

is related to the study of syzygies via the Green-Lazarsfeld conjecture. We determine these invariants for canonical covers where the target curve and the branch divisor are general in most of the cases:

.

Let

be a canonical cover.
Assume the curve

to be Brill-Noether general of genus

Assume

has gonality

(ii)

Let
\psi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
be a canonical cover where

is assumed to be general of genus

and

by

and

respectively. If

2
Preliminaries on Spectral Curves

Throughout the text
\Bbbk
denotes a fixed algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. We use Grothendieck’s convention for projectivization: If
V
is a vector space the points of
\mathbb{P}V
are one dimensional quotients of
V
. We recall the framework for working with spectral curves.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth curve and
n
a natural number. Let
N
be an ample line bundle on
C
and
s_{i}\in H^{0}(C,N^{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. We call
(C,N,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
the data for a spectral curve.

Here is how to construct a spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
out of this data. Take
\mathbb{A}(N):=\gSpec_{C}\operatorname{Sym}^{\bullet}(N^{-1})
to be the global space of the line bundle
N
. It comes with a map
\pi:\mathbb{A}(N)\to C
. By the projection formula we have

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}(N)}\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}N^{-i},\qquad\pi_{*}\pi^{*}N^{m}\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}N^{m-i}.

We denote the section
1\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C})\subset H^{0}(\pi^{*}N)
by
\tau
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(C,N,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be the data for a spectral curve. The associated spectral curve is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}:=V(\tau^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{d-i}\tau^{i})\subset\mathbb{A}(N).

The restriction of the natural projection
\mathbb{A}(N)\rightarrow C
to
\widetilde{C}
will be called the spectral cover
\psi
.

Remark 2.3
.

The data
(C,N,(s_{i}))
and
(C,N^{\prime},(s_{i}^{\prime}))
define an equivalent spectral curve if there is an isomorphism
\iota:N\rightarrow N^{\prime}
and a nonzero scalar
\lambda\in\Bbbk
such that
\iota(s_{i}^{\prime})=\lambda^{i}s_{i}
.

Now take any line bundle
M
on
C
. The following applications of the projection formula will be used very often:

\displaystyle\psi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}}\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-1}N^{-j},\qquad\psi_{*}\psi^{*}M\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n-1}M\otimes N^{-j}.

(3)

For an arbitrary line bundle
L
on
\widetilde{C}
its pushforward is a vector bundle of rank
n
on
C
. Its determinant is given by

\det\psi_{*}L\cong\operatorname{Nm}(L)\otimes\det(\psi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}})^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Nm}(L)\otimes N.

(4)

Here the norm is defined as follows: If
L=\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
for a divisor
D
, then
\operatorname{Nm(L)}:=\mathcal{O}(f_{*}D)
. See for example
[
8
, IV, Exercise 2.6]
. As a consequence the degree of
\psi_{*}L
is

\displaystyle\deg\psi_{*}L=\deg L-\binom{n}{2}\deg N

(5)

One way to get the genus of the spectral curve is to compute
h^{1}(\widetilde{C},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{C}})
via equation (
3
):

g(\widetilde{C})=n(g(C)-1)+1+\binom{n}{2}\deg N.

(6)

Our approach for understanding line bundles
L
on
\widetilde{C}
is based on studying Higgs bundles of rank
n
on
C
as we explain now. The map
L\to L\otimes\psi^{*}N
induced by multiplication with
\tau_{|\widetilde{C}}
pushes down to a so called twisted endomorphism

\phi:\psi_{*}L\to\psi_{*}L\otimes N.

From the data
(\psi_{*}L,\phi)
one can recover the line bundle
L
. To state this result we need a definition.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
N
and
V
be vector bundles on a smooth curve
C
of rank
1
and
n
respectively. Let
\phi
be a twisted endomorphism of
V
with respect to
N
i.e, a map
\phi:V\to V\otimes N
. The vector bundle
V
together with
\phi
will be called an
N
-valued
Higgs bundle
. Two Higgs bundles
(V,\phi)
and
(V^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
with respect to
N
will be considered equivalent if there is an isomorphism
\nu:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V\otimes N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V^{\prime}\otimes N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu\otimes id_{N}}

For a Higgs bundle
(V,\phi)
we define the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{\phi}
to be

\displaystyle\chi_{\phi}=T^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}s_{n-i}T^{i},\quad\text{with $s_{i}=(-1)^{i}\operatorname{tr}\left(\wedge^{i}\phi\right)\in H^{0}(C,N^{i})$.}

We next single out a class of well-behaved Higgs bundles:

Definition 2.5
.

A Higgs bundle
(V,\phi)
with respect to a line bundle
N
on a curve
C
will be called
stable
if
V
does not admit a proper subbundle
M
satisfying
\phi(M)\subset M\otimes N
and
\mu(V)\leq\mu(M)
. Here
\mu(M)
is the slope
\frac{\deg M}{\operatorname{rk}M}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Recall the distinction between subsheaves and subbundles of a vector bundle
V
on a smooth curve. We say that a subsheaf
M
of
V
is a subbundle if and only if
V/M
is locally free.

The next result is sometimes called the Hitchin correspondence or Beauville- Narasimhan-Ramanan correspondence (BNR) after the pioneerring works
[
9
]
and
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(C,N,s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be the data for a spectral cover
\psi:\widetilde{C}\to C
such that
\widetilde{C}
is smooth. There is a bijective correspondence between line bundles
L
on
\widetilde{C}
and those Higgs bundles of rank
n

(V,\phi)
with respect to
N
which have characteristic polynomial
T^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}s_{i}T^{i}
. The bijection is given by sending
L
to
\psi_{*}L
. The map
\phi
is induced by multiplication with
\tau_{|C}
.

Furthermore if
(V,\phi)
comes from a line bundle
L
on
\widetilde{C}
then
\phi
leaves invariant no proper subbundle of
V
, in particular it is a stable Higgs bundle.

Proof.

See
[
4
, Proposition 3.6]
. The second assertion is
[
15
, Lemma 5.1]
.
∎

The following relation between twisted endomorphisms and usual endomorphisms of a vector bundle will be useful to us in the last section.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(V,\phi)
be a Higgs bundle with respect to a line bundle
N
. Assume that the spectral curve
\widetilde{C}
defined by
\chi_{\phi}
is irreducible. Then the following holds:

\{\rho\in\operatorname{End}(V):(\rho\otimes\operatorname{id}_{N})\circ\phi=\phi\circ\rho\}=\{\lambda\operatorname{id}_{V}:\lambda\in\Bbbk\}.

The same conclusion holds if
(V,\phi)
is a stable Higgs bundle.

Proof.

The proof is based on
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
. Take
\rho
to be an endomorphism of
V
which commutes with
\phi
. Because
\rho
is an endomorphim its characteristic polynomial has constant coefficients. Hence
\rho
has an eigenvalue
\lambda\in\Bbbk
. Then
\rho-\lambda\operatorname{id}_{V}
commutes with
\phi
as well. Write
V_{1}
for the sub vector bundle
\operatorname{ker}(\rho-\lambda\operatorname{id})
. It is invariant under
\phi
and we have two induced twisted endomorphisms
\phi^{\prime}:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{1}\otimes N
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}:V/V_{1}\rightarrow V/V_{1}\otimes N
. In terms of characteristic polynomials we get

\chi_{\phi}=\chi_{\phi^{\prime}}\chi_{\phi^{\prime\prime}}.

Because
\chi_{\phi}
cuts out
C
in
\mathbb{A}(N)
it is irreducible and we conclude
V=V_{1}
. Hence
\rho=\lambda\operatorname{id}_{V}
.

If
(V,\phi)
is stable, we obtain
V_{1}
as before. Because
V_{1}
is invariant under
\phi
we have
\mu(V_{1})<\mu(\phi)
. But
V/V_{1}\cong\operatorname{im}(\rho-\lambda\operatorname{id}_{V})
is also
\phi
-invariant. If this subsheaf were nonzero, we would we have
\mu(V/V_{1})<\mu(V)
. This contradicts
\mu(V_{1})<\mu(V)
and we conclude
V=V_{1}
.
∎

Let us define the moduli problem for Higgs bundles.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
C
be a curve and
N
a line bundle on it. Furthermore fix positive integers
n
and
d
. Define the
Higgs-bundle functor

Sch_{\Bbbk}\rightarrow Set
for the line bundle
N
, rank
n
and degree
d
as follows. A scheme
T
is mapped to the set of equivalence classes of pairs
(V_{T},\phi_{T})
. Here
V_{t}
is a vector bundle of rank
n
and degree
d
on
C\times T
and
\phi_{T}
is a morphism of coherent sheaves
V_{T}\rightarrow V_{T}\otimes\pi_{C}^{*}N
. We demand that for all closed points
t\in T
the pair

(V_{T|t},\phi_{|t}:V_{T|t}\to V_{T|t}\otimes N)

is a stable Higgs bundle with respect to
N
.
Two pairs
(V_{T},\phi_{T}),(V^{\prime}_{T},\phi^{\prime}_{T})
are said to be equivalent if for all
t\in T

(V_{t},\phi_{t})\cong(V^{\prime}_{t},\phi_{t}^{\prime})
holds. The restriction maps are given by pulling back vector bundles and twisted endomorphisms.

Theorem 2.10
.

There is a coarse moduli space for the Higgs-bundle functor with respect to
(N,n,d)
, which we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{C}(N)(n,d)
.

Proof.

See
[
16
, Theorem 5.10]
.
∎

We now define Brill-Noether loci in this moduli space:

Definition 2.11
.

Take
C,N,n
and
d
as before and

to be a nonnegative integer. Define the
Brill-Noether locus

W^{r}_{C}(N)(n,d)
to be the locus in
\mathcal{H}_{C}(N)(n,d)
consisting of Higgs-bundles
(V,\phi)
satisfying
h^{0}(V)\geq r+1
.

The stability of a Higgs bundle

\deg M\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\deg V+\deg N\right).

(7)

In case of equality the Higgs bundle
(V,\phi)
corresponds to the line bundle of the form
\psi^{*}M
on
\widetilde{C}
. Here
\psi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
is the spectral cover associated to
(V,\phi)
.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\deg M\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg V
. By stability of

, the subbundle

We now outline our approach to Brill-Noether theory via Higgs bundles:

Strategy 2.16
.

Fix a smooth curve
C
and a line bundle
N
on it. Let
\psi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
be a general spectral cover of degree
n
with respect to
N
. In other words: The moduli space for these spectral covers is
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{0}(N^{j})
and
\psi
is a general element of it. We want to prove that
\widetilde{C}
does not admit a
g^{r}_{d}
. By the Beauville-Narasimhan-Ramanan correspondence we have set-theoretically

W^{r}_{d}(\widetilde{C})=h^{-1}(\widetilde{C})\cap W^{r}_{C}(N)(n,d).

Therefore
W^{r}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
is empty if and only if
h
restricted to
W^{r}_{C}(N)(n,d)
is not dominant. If instead there exists a
g^{r}_{d}
on
\widetilde{C}
we have

\dim W^{r}_{d}(\widetilde{C})=\dim W^{r}_{C}(N)(n,d)-\dim\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}H^{0}(N^{j}).

To analyze
W^{r}_{C}(N)(n,d)
we construct finitely many families of Higgs bundles
(\mathcal{V}_{T_{i}},\phi_{T_{i}})
over base schemes
T_{i}
with the property that
h^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{t})\geq r+1
for all geometric point
t\in T_{i}
. We want the induced maps
T_{i}\rightarrow W^{r}_{C}(N)(n,d)
to be jointly surjective.

We sketch how we generally build the family of Higgs bundles. First we start with a suitable family of vector bundles
\mathcal{V}_{S_{i}}
on
C
paramatrised by a scheme
S_{i}
. Then we will put

T_{i}=\left\{(V,\phi):\text{$V$ is a vector bundle in $S_{i}$ and $\phi$ is a map $V\to V\otimes N$}\right\}.

In other words
T_{i}
is the union of
H^{0}(\End(V)\otimes N)
as
V
varies over the points of
S_{i}
. This is a special case of a relative section space. For the lack of a good reference in the literature we will construct it in the next subsection.

2.1
Relative section space

Consider
\nu:X\to Y
a flat projective morphism of
\Bbbk
-schemes whose fibers are smooth projective curves of genus
g
and let
\mathcal{L}
be a coherent sheaf on

which is flat over
Y
. We will need a scheme which is glued from the spaces

as

varies over the points of

. We use the language of functors to give a precise definition of such a scheme.

.

We define the functor

:

S_{\nu,\mathcal{L}}:T\mapsto H^{0}(X_{T},\mathcal{L}_{T}).

(13)

Here
\mathcal{L}_{T}
and

denote base change along the map

.
We call this the functor of

.

First we show that the functor is representable. We will call the corresponding scheme

as well.

.

Take

to be a locally free sheaf on

flat over
Y
. Furthermore assume there is a Cartier divisor

in

which has positive relative degree.
Then the functor

is representable.

Proof.

(due to Hannah Larson and Feiyang Lin)
We first take
\mathcal{F}
to be a vector bundle on
X
flat over
Y
and assume

for all closed points

. We show that

is represented by
\mathbb{A}(\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})
. Take
(f:T\to Y)\in\operatorname{Sch}/Y
. From cohomology and base change we deduce

f^{*}\nu_{*}\mathcal{F}\cong\nu_{T*}\mathcal{F}_{T}.

(14)

Furthermore
\nu_{*}\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle.
By the universal property of the symmetric algebra
\mathbb{A}(\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})(T)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}((\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})^{\vee},f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{T})
. Furthermore

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}((\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})^{\vee},f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{T})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}(f^{*}(\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{T})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{T}}((f^{*}\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{T})

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}(f^{*}\nu_{*}\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}(\nu_{T*}\mathcal{F}_{T}).

Here we used in the second line that pullback and dualizing commute for vector bundles

. Furthermore we needed that vector bundles are reflexive.
Now we prove the original claim. Consider the sequence

is locally free.

is represented by

Now we assume that

is irreducible. To compute the dimension of a relative section space we use the following degeneracy loci:

Y_{p}:=\left\{y\in Y:h^{0}(X_{y},\mathcal{L}_{y})\leq r_{\operatorname{gen}}+p\right\}

Here

is the rank of

at a general point of

. We give these loci a natural scheme structure: Take

be the map

. Then we have

. Hence

.

The dimension of

is the following:

\dim S_{\nu,\mathcal{L}}=\operatorname{max}_{p}\left(\dim Y_{p}+r_{\operatorname{gen}}+p\right).

In particular if

for all

the dimension is
\dim Y+r_{\operatorname{gen}}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be the map from
S_{\nu,\mathcal{L}}
to
Y
.
The relative section space can be written as a disjoint union of locally closed subsets like this:

S_{\nu,\mathcal{L}}=\bigsqcup_{p=0}^{\infty}\pi^{-1}\left(Y_{p}\setminus Y_{p+1}\right).

We note that
\pi^{-1}\left(Y_{p}\setminus Y_{p+1}\right)

is the relative section space of
\mathcal{L}_{Y_{p}\setminus Y_{p+1}}
which in turn is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}\left(\nu_{Y_{p}\setminus Y_{p+1}*}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{p}\setminus Y_{p+1}}\right)
. This is a geometric vector bundle of rank

and has dimension
\dim Y_{p}+r_{\operatorname{gen}}+p

∎

.
Here we concern ourselves with smooth spectral curves
\widetilde{C}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with respect to the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(k)
. We denote the associated branched cover by
\psi:\widetilde{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We fix

. We extend Beauville’s approach to all vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We want to works towards understanding the irreducible components and the dimension of the Brill-Noether loci
W^{r}_{d}(\widetilde{C})
. We need the following stratification of the Brill-Noether loci:

Definition 3.1
.

A
splitting type
of rank
n
consists of integers
e_{1}<e_{2}<\ldots<e_{l}
and positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{l}m_{j}=n
holds. We denote it by
(e,m)
. Define the splitting locus corresponding to
(e,m)
by

U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C}):=\left\{L\in\operatorname{Pic}(\widetilde{C}):\psi_{*}L\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}\right\}.

(17)

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
11
]
a different but equivalent definition of splitting types of rank
n
is used. They consider sequences of integers
\vec{e}=(\widetilde{e}_{1}\leq\widetilde{e}_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\widetilde{e}_{n})
. These objects are in natural bijection with our splitting types.
Their notion of a splitting locus
W^{\vec{e}}(\widetilde{C})
differs from the one given above: They take all line bundles having pushforward of type
\vec{e}
or a specialization of it. In the case of a general branched cover of degree
n
this corresponds to the closure of our splitting locus.

The relation between splitting loci and the classical Brill-Noether loci is the following: Write
E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}
. Then

U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})\subset W^{h^{0}(E)-1}_{\sum_{i}e_{i}m_{i}+k\binom{n}{2}}(\widetilde{C}).

holds as a consequence of equation (
4
).

We view line bundles
L
on
\widetilde{C}
as Higgs bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
: The pushforward
\psi_{*}L
comes with a twisted endomorphism
\phi:\psi_{*}L\rightarrow\psi_{*}L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(k)
which we view as an
n\times n
-matrix whose entries are sections of line bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(e,m)
be a splitting type and

consists of the following lower triangular block matrices:

\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\
B_{21}&A_{22}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
B_{l1}&B_{l2}&\cdots&A_{ll}\end{pmatrix}

Here the matrices
A_{jj}
are elements of
GL_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
and the
B_{ij}
are arbitrary matrices with entries in
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{i}-e_{j})^{\oplus m_{i}m_{j}})
. The dimension of
\operatorname{Aut}(V)
as an algebraic group is then

\dim\operatorname{Aut}(V)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}+1)m_{i}m_{j}.

Proof.

One writes an endomorphism of
V
as a block matrix
\left(\phi_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{l}
with
\phi_{ij}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(e_{i})^{\oplus m_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}})
. If
i<j

\phi_{ij}
is zero. The determinant of such a matrix is
\prod_{j}\det A_{jj}
and needs to be nonzero. Hence
A_{jj}
is in
GL_{m_{j}}(\Bbbk)
.
∎

We define a Higgs bundle version of the splitting loci.

\displaystyle U^{(e,m)}(k)=\left\{(V,\phi:V\rightarrow V(k)):\begin{aligned} &\text{$V\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}$}\\
&\text{and $V$ does not have a $\phi$-invariant subbundle}\end{aligned}\right\}/\sim.

Here the equivalence relation
\sim
we divide out is equivalence of Higgs bundles. This is a scheme because it is a locally closed subset of the moduli space of stable Higgs-bundles
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}(k))(n,d)
. We construct this moduli space as a group quotient. For a fixed
(e,m)
abbreviate the vector bundle
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}
by
V
. Define the following Zariski-open subset of the space of its space of twisted endomorphisms:

\widetilde{U}^{(e,m)}(k)=\left\{\phi\in H^{0}(\End(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(k)):\text{there is no $\phi$-invariant subbundle}\right\}.

We denote by
\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(V)
the automorphism group of
V
modulo scalar multiples of the identity. This subgroup acts on
\widetilde{U}^{(e,m)}
:
g\cdot\phi:=g\phi g^{-1}
.
Here
g
is in
\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(V)
and
\phi
in
\widetilde{U}^{(e,m)}(k)
. All elements of
U^{(e,m)}(k)
are stable Higgs bundles. Therefore the action is free by Lemma
2.8
. Then we have

U^{(e,m)}(k)\cong\widetilde{U}^{(e,m)}(k)/\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(V).

We have the following analog of the Hitchin fibration

\displaystyle h:U^{(e,m)}(k)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(ik))

\displaystyle(V,\phi)

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi_{\phi}.

We are now prepared to show our first result.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Theorem A)
Let
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be a general element of
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(ik))
and denote by
\widetilde{C}
the associated spectral curve. Write
V
for the vector bundle
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{j})^{\oplus m_{j}}
.
Then

\dim U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})\leq h^{0}(\End(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(k))+1-\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}+1)m_{i}m_{j}-d-k\binom{n+1}{2}

(18)

holds for a splitting type
(e,m)
if the right hand side is nonnegative. This is either an equality or the splitting locus
U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})
is empty.
Take
(e,m)
to be a splitting type satisfying
e_{1}-e_{l}+k\geq-1
. We have

\dim U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})=g(\widetilde{C})-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}-1)m_{i}m_{j}

(19)

if the right hand side is nonnegative.
Furthermore in this case the space
U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})
is smooth.

Proof.

Assume that the right hand side in (
18
) is nonnegative.
The Hitchin correspondence
2.7
tells us
h^{-1}(f)\cong U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})
.
Hence if we have a general element
f
of the Hitchin base the dimension of its fiber can be bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\dim U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})

\displaystyle\leq h^{0}(\End(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(k))-\dim\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(V)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(ik)).

(20)

This is an equality if the Hitchin map is dominant. Otherwise the general fiber
h^{-1}(f)
is empty. This shows the first assertion.
To prove the second one we use the assumption on the splitting data to find

\displaystyle h^{0}(\End(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(k))=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(k))m_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}\left(h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(k+e_{i}-e_{j}))+h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(k+e_{j}-e_{i}))\right)m_{i}m_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}(k+1)m_{j}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}2(k+1)m_{i}m_{j}.

If
(e,m)
does not fulfill the assumption
h^{0}(\End(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}(k))
is instead strictly greater than this.
Plugging in the previous equation in (
20
) gives

\displaystyle\dim U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{l}km_{j}^{2}+\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(2k+1+e_{i}-e_{j})m_{i}m_{j}\right)+1-n-k\binom{n+1}{2}

\displaystyle=k\binom{n}{2}+1-n+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(e_{i}-e_{j}+1)m_{i}m_{j}

\displaystyle=g(\widetilde{C})-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq l}(e_{j}-e_{i}-1)m_{i}m_{j}.

Lastly we show smoothness. The tangent space of an equivalence class
[\phi]\in U^{(e,m)}(k)
is

T_{[\phi]}\left(\widetilde{U}^{(e,m)}/\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(V)\right)\cong T_{\phi}\widetilde{U}^{(e,m)}/\{(\rho\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{O}(k)})\circ\phi-\phi\circ\rho:\rho\in\operatorname{End}(V)\}.

By Lemma
2.8
the vector space
\{(\rho\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{O}(k)})\circ\phi-\phi\circ\rho:\rho\in\operatorname{End}(V)\}
has the same dimension as
\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(V)
. Hence the tangent spaces of
U^{(e,m)}(k)
have the same dimension as
U^{(e,m)}(k)
and this space is smooth. We now apply generic smoothness
[
20
, Theorem 21.6.6]
to find an open subset
V
of the Hitchin base such that
h_{|h^{-1}(V)}
is smooth. In particular if
\widetilde{C}
is the spectral curve of any
f\in V
the space
U^{(e,m)}(\widetilde{C})
is smooth.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let us compare this to Theorem

in the case where the splitting locus satisfies

for all

. They denote their splitting types by

is non-empty.

Remark 3.6
.

A general degree
n
spectral cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with respect to
\mathcal{O}(k)
is simply branched. To see this look at the closed subvariety
X
of

consisting of all spectral covers

which are not simply branched. It has three irreducible components

and

. The first consists of all singular spectral curves. If the source
\widetilde{C}
of the spectral cover

is instead smooth we need to have a branch point

such that the branch divisor has multiplicity

at
p
. This implies that

either has a preimage
q
having ramification order

or two preimages both of ramification order

. In analogy with plane curves we call these possibilites flex and bitangent respectively. The closure of the locus of spectral curves having a flex resp. a bitangent will be the affine varieties
X_{2}
resp.

. We sketch how to prove that

is irreducible of positive codimension. The argument for the other two is similar. Look at the incidence correspondence

The image of
\widetilde{X}_{2}
under projection on the first coordinate is

so it suffices to find the dimension and the irreducible components of
X_{2}
. Consider the projection on the second coordinate
\pi_{2}:\widetilde{X}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Consider the affine variety

of monic polynomials of degree

having a triple root. Up to the choice of a local parameter we have an evaluation function at

\displaystyle\operatorname{ev}_{p}:\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Trip}_{n}

\displaystyle((s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}),p)

\displaystyle\mapsto s_{1}(p),\ldots,s_{n}(p).

The target space is irreducible of dimension

. The evaluation map is surjective and because it is linear the fibers are of dimension

. Hence

is irreducible of dimension

and our claim about

follows.

4
The restricted Hitchin map

Throughout this chapter
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
over
\Bbbk
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
V
be a vector bundle of rank
2
on
C
and
\Gamma
an effective divisor on
C
. We denote the sheaf of tracefree endomorphisms of
V
by
\End_{0}(V)
. Then we define the
meromorphic restricted Hitchin map
to be

h_{V,\Gamma}:H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C}(\Gamma))\to H^{0}(K_{C}^{2}(2\Gamma)),\quad\phi\mapsto\det\phi.

If
\Gamma=0
we simply speak of the
restricted Hitchin map
and write
h_{V,\Gamma}=h_{V}
.

We introduce matrix notation in case
V
is a direct sum of line bundles:

Notation 4.2
.

Let
V=M\oplus N
for line bundles
M,N
. We write any
\phi\in H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C})
as
\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{11}&\phi_{12}\\
\phi_{21}&-\phi_{11}\end{pmatrix}
where
\phi_{11}\in H^{0}(K_{C}),\phi_{12}\in H^{0}(M\otimes N^{\vee}\otimes K_{C}),\phi_{21}\in H^{0}(M^{\vee}\otimes N\otimes K_{C})
. Then we have
h_{V}(\phi)=-\phi_{11}^{2}-\phi_{12}\phi_{21}
.

We fix a vector bundle
V
. We are interested if there is a twisted endomorphism of it with a given general spectral curve. The next lemma reduces this question to the study of the restricted Hitchin map.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
V
be a rank
2
vector bundle on
C
. Assume that the restricted Hitchin map
h_{V}
is dominant. Then also the following map has dense image:

H_{V}:H^{0}(\End(V)\otimes K_{C})\to H^{0}(K_{C})\oplus H^{0}(K_{C}^{2}),\phi\mapsto(\operatorname{tr}\phi,\det\phi).

Proof.

Take a general element
(s_{0},s_{1})
of
H^{0}(K_{C})\oplus H^{0}(K_{C}^{2})
. Then
s_{2}-\frac{1}{4}s_{1}^{2}
is general in
H^{0}(K_{C}^{2})
and by assumption there exists
\phi_{0}\in H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C})
such that
h_{V}(\phi)=s_{2}-\frac{1}{4}s_{1}^{2}
. Then
\phi_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{id}_{V}
has trace
s_{1}
and determinant
s_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Take
C
to be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
which is not hyperelliptic. Assume
V
is a vector bundle of rank
2
and degree
d
which does not contain line-subbundles of degree
\geq\frac{d}{2}-1
. Then
\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C}
is basepoint-free.

Proof.

Let
q
be any point of
C
. Consider the following exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\to\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C}(-q)\to\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C}\to\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C|q}\to 0.

From its long exact sequence we see that
H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C})\to H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C|q})
is surjective if
H^{1}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C}(-q))=0
. By Serre duality we have

H^{1}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes K_{C}(-q))^{\vee}\cong H^{0}(\End_{0}(V)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(q)).

We show that the right hand side is zero. Assume for contradiction there is a nonzero twisted endomorphism
\rho:V\to V(q)
. Then the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{\rho}
is in
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(q))\oplus H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(2q))\cong k^{\oplus 2}
. Here we used that
C
is not hyperelliptic. We can factor
\chi_{\rho}
as
\chi_{\rho}(t)=(t-\lambda_{1})(t-\lambda_{2})
with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Bbbk
. Then consider
\rho_{i}=\rho-\lambda_{i}\operatorname{id}^{\prime}_{V}
. Here we use the natural injective map
\mathcal{O}_{C}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}(q)
to define
\operatorname{id}^{\prime}_{V}:V\to V(q)
. Then we consider the image of
\rho_{i}
. It is a line bundle because
\chi_{\rho}(\lambda_{i})=0
. Hence
\ker(\rho_{i})
is a subbundle of
V
. We have
\deg\operatorname{im}(\rho_{i})\leq\mu(V(p))-1=\frac{d}{2}
. On the other hand
d=\deg\ker(\rho_{i})+\deg\operatorname{im}(\rho_{i})
. This implies
\deg\ker(\rho_{i})\geq\frac{d}{2}
. This contradicts our assumption on the degree of subbundles.
∎

4.0.2
The restricted Hitchin map of the trivial vector bundle of rank
2

The rest of this section is devoted to the question: For which vector bundles is
h_{V,\Gamma}
dominant? We first answer this question partially for
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus 2}
. We thank Daniele Agostini for suggesting the idea behind the proof of Proposition
4.7
.

We explain how quadrics come into play: We have the following factorization of
h_{V,\Gamma}
:

H^{0}(\End_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus 2})\otimes K_{C}(\Gamma))\xrightarrow{\delta}\operatorname{Sym}^{2}H^{0}(K_{C}(\Gamma))\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}}H^{0}(K_{C}^{2}(2\Gamma)).

(21)

The first map sends
\phi
to
-\phi_{11}^{2}-\phi_{12}\phi_{21}
, where the multiplication happens in the symmetric algebra. The second map is the usual multiplication map of sections. The image of
\delta
is the locus of quadrics of rank at most
3
denoted by
\hat{Q}_{3}
: Every image is clearly a quadric of rank
\leq 3
. On the other hand take a quadric of the form
q=\eta_{1}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}+\eta_{3}^{2}
with the
\eta_{i}\in H^{0}(K_{C})
. We can rewrite this:

q=\eta_{1}^{2}+(\eta_{2}+\sqrt{-1}\eta_{3})(\eta_{2}^{2}-\sqrt{-1}\eta_{3}).

Now it is clear that
q
is in the image. We prove that
\hat{Q}_{3}
has dimension
h^{0}(\End_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus 2})\otimes K_{C}(\Gamma))-3
. Consider the open set
U
of
\hat{Q}_{3}
consisting of quadrics of rank exactly
3
. Then
\delta^{-1}(U)
is a principal
PGL_{2}
-bundle over
U
and
\delta^{-1}(U)
consists of all
\phi
such that
\phi_{11},\phi_{12},\phi_{21}
are linearly independent. With this preparation we can prove the following Proposition.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
C
be a general curve of genus
g\geq 3
and let
\Gamma
be either
0
or a general effective divisor of degree
\geq 3
. Then the map
h_{\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus 2},\Gamma}
is dominant.

Proof.

We use the factorisation

H^{0}(\End_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus 2})\otimes K_{C}(\Gamma))\xrightarrow{\delta}\operatorname{Sym}^{2}H^{0}(K_{C}(\Gamma))\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}}H^{0}(K_{C}^{2}(2\Gamma)).

We have seen that the image of
\delta
has dimension
h^{0}(\End_{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus 2})\otimes K_{C}(\Gamma))-3=3g+3\deg\Gamma-6
. On the other hand
H^{0}(K_{C}^{2}(2\Gamma))
has dimension

. Hence by semicontinuity if we show that
\operatorname{ev}_{|\operatorname{im}\delta}^{-1}(0)
has dimension
\deg\Gamma-3
we are done. This is
\hat{Q}_{3}(C,K_{C}(\Gamma))
. If
\deg\Gamma\geq 3
we apply Lemma
4.5
. Our line bundle

has

and

. Note that

so we can apply the Theorem cited above. We find

\displaystyle\dim\operatorname{ev}_{|\operatorname{im}\delta}^{-1}(0)

\displaystyle=q(g,r,d,k)+1

\displaystyle=\binom{g+\deg\Gamma}{2}-\binom{g+\deg\Gamma-3}{2}-2(2g-2+\deg\Gamma)+g-1

\displaystyle=\deg\Gamma-3.

This is the number we wanted. If
\Gamma
is trivial we instead use Lemma

to show that
\operatorname{ev}^{-1}_{|\operatorname{im}\delta}(0)
is zero-dimensional.
∎

The above Proposition fails for hyperelliptic curves because the multiplication map

Remark 5.2
.

Some complications in the above proof arise because the following situation arises: We are given a family of vector bundles on a curve
C
i.e a vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
C\times T
where
T
is a scheme. We would like to build a moduli space out of the different extension spaces
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{|t\times C},\mathcal{O}_{C})
where
t
varies in
T
. The resulting moduli space is then naturally an Artin stack because the automorphism group of a given extension
\xi\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{|t\times C},\mathcal{O}_{C})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{F}_{m\times C},\mathcal{O}_{C})
. To keep the approach more elementary we do not pursue this point of view.

The following lemma will be needed only for the next Proposition:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0\leq d_{2}\leq d_{1}\leq g-1
between
0
and
g-1
. Let
\alpha
be the difference map
C_{d_{1}}\times C_{d_{2}}\to\operatorname{Pic}^{d_{1}-d_{2}}(C),(D_{1},D_{2})\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}-D_{2})
. For
r\geq 1
the following holds: No component of
\alpha^{-1}(W^{r-1}_{d}(C))
is entirely contained in
\alpha^{-1}(W^{r}_{d}(C))
. In particular we have

\operatorname{codim}\alpha^{-1}(W^{r}_{d}(C))\geq r+1.

Proof.

Take
(D_{1},D_{2})\in C_{d_{1}}\times C_{d_{2}}
such that
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}-D_{2}))=r+1
. We are going to perturb
(D_{1},D_{2})
slightly to
(D_{1}^{\prime},D_{2}^{\prime})
such that
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}^{\prime}-D_{2}^{\prime}))=r
. Note that either
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}))<r+1+d_{2}
or
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}))=r+1+d_{2}
hold. In the first case some point
q
of
D_{2}
is in the base locus of the linear system
|D_{1}|
. Move
q
outside of the base locus to obtain
D_{2}^{\prime}
. Then
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}-D_{2}^{\prime}))=r
.

In case
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}))=r+1+d_{2}
we perturb
D_{1}
to
D_{1}^{\prime}
such that
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}^{\prime}))=r+d_{2}
. Here we use that in general no irreducible component of
W^{r+d_{2}-1}_{d_{1}}(C)
is contained in
W_{d_{1}}^{r+d_{2}}(C)
. See
[
1
, chapter IV, Lemma 3.5]
. We can assume that no point of
D_{2}
is a base point of
|D_{1}^{\prime}|
. Hence
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(D_{1}^{\prime}-D_{2}))=r
.

The lower bound on the codimension follows by induction on
r
: Assume
\alpha^{-1}(W^{r-1}_{d}(C))
has codimension at least
r
. Because no component of
\alpha^{-1}(W^{r-1}_{d}(C))
is contained entirely in
\alpha^{-1}(W^{r}_{d}(C))
we conclude that

\operatorname{codim}\alpha^{-1}(W^{r}_{d}(C))\geq 1+\operatorname{codim}\alpha^{-1}(W^{r-1}_{d}(C))\geq r+1.

∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
C
be a general curve of genus

. Let

does not split. We get the dimension bound for the corresponding family.

\displaystyle 1+h^{0}(\End(E)\otimes\vartheta)

\displaystyle\leq 1+2h^{0}(\vartheta)+h^{0}(M^{\vee}\otimes M^{\prime}\otimes\vartheta)+h^{0}((M^{\prime})^{\vee}\otimes M\otimes\vartheta)

\displaystyle\leq 1+2+1+2=6.

This concludes the last case in the proof.
∎

7
The rest of the Gonality Sequence

We will prove Theorem C via an inequality involving the Brill-Noether number
\rho(g,r,d):=g-(r+1)(g+r-d)
. We use the assumption
r\ll g
to make the proof more illuminating.

Proof.

Take
L
to be a
g^{r}_{d}
, which does not lie in
\psi^{*}\operatorname{Pic}(C)
. Assume for contradiction that
d=2d_{r}
holds. Let
\varepsilon
be a positive number which we can choose later on as small as we want and assume
(r+1)^{2}/g<\varepsilon
. There is an injection of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
into
\psi_{*}L
. Therefore we find an effective line subbundle
M
of
\psi_{*}L
. Its degree is by Lemma
2.12
smaller than
d_{r}
. Write
M^{\prime}=\psi_{*}L/M
and observe that
s=h^{0}(M)-1
is nonnegative. The Brill-Noether theorem implies
\rho(g,s,\deg M)\geq 0
. Then

\deg M\geq\frac{sg}{s+1}+s

follows. Observe that
h^{0}(M^{\prime})\geq r-s
. We similarly get
\deg M^{\prime}\geq\frac{(r-s-1)g}{r-s}+r-s-1
. On the other hand the minimality of
d_{r}
implies
\rho(g,r,d_{r}-1)<0
and
\rho(g,r,d_{r})<r+1
. This translates into

d_{r}<\frac{rg}{r+1}+r+1\leq\frac{(r+\varepsilon)g}{r+1}.

We combine these inequalities to obtain

\displaystyle\deg L

\displaystyle=\deg\psi_{*}L+g-1

\displaystyle=\deg M+\deg M^{\prime}+g-1

\displaystyle\geq\frac{sg}{s+1}+\frac{(r-s-1)g}{r-s}+g-2+r

\displaystyle\geq 2\frac{(r+\varepsilon)g}{r+1}>2d_{r}.

The second to last inequality is seen as follows:
\frac{(r-1)g}{r+1}
lies between
\frac{sg}{s+1}
and
\frac{(r-s-1)g}{r-s}
and
\frac{(r+1)g}{r+1}<g-2+r
. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon
we have derived a contradiction to our assumption
d_{r}=2r
. The Theorem is proven.
∎

Equations for specific Canonical Covers

Let
(C,\vartheta,\omega)
be the data for a canonical cover. One way to get a projective coordinate ring for
\widetilde{C}
is to start with the section ring
R_{\vartheta}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{0}(C,\vartheta^{n})
and try to understand the section ring
R_{\psi^{*}\vartheta}
. By the projection formula one obtains the following representation of it:

R_{\psi^{*}\vartheta}\cong R_{\vartheta}[x]/(x^{2}-\omega).

(52)

The image of
x
has degree
1
in the above ring. The canonical coordinate ring of
\widetilde{C}
is the following subring of
R_{\psi^{*}\vartheta}

\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}H^{0}(\widetilde{C},K_{\widetilde{C}}^{m})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(R_{\psi^{*}\vartheta}\right)_{3m}.

Example 8.1
.

\mu(f_{j}^{*})(\sum_{i}m_{ij}e_{i})=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+j}m_{ij}\operatorname{det}M_{ij}=\operatorname{det}M=0.

We used Laplace expansion and the fact that
\operatorname{det}M

\mu\circ M
is zero by Laplace expansion as well:

\mu(\sum_{j}m_{ij}f_{j}^{*})=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}(-1)^{i+j}e_{i}^{*}\otimes m_{ij}\det M_{ij}.

This suffices to identify the natural boundary map with
f_{j}^{*}\mapsto\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+j}e_{i}^{*}\otimes\operatorname{det}M_{ij}

Example 8.3
.

A nonhyperelliptic curve
C
of genus
3
is a plane quartic. We derive from the last Lemma a presentation of the section ring of an even theta characteristic
\vartheta
:

R_{\vartheta}=\Bbbk[\overset{2}{X_{1}},\overset{2}{X_{2}},\overset{2}{X_{3}},\overset{3}{e_{1}},\ldots,\overset{3}{e_{4}}]/(\sum_{i}m_{ij}e_{i},e_{i}e_{j}-(-1)^{i+j}\operatorname{det}M_{ij}).

Represent the differential defining the branch divisor by a linear form
L(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
. We have from (
52
)

R_{\psi^{*}\vartheta}=\Bbbk[\overset{2}{X_{1}},\overset{2}{X_{2}},\overset{2}{X_{3}},\overset{3}{e_{1}},\ldots,\overset{3}{e_{4}},\overset{1}{U}]/(\sum_{i}m_{ij}e_{i},e_{i}e_{j}-(-1)^{i+j}\operatorname{det}M_{ij},U^{2}-L).

In Proposition
6.2
we have proved that a general canonical cover
\widetilde{C}
of
C
does not admit a
g^{1}_{4}
, but there are examples where the gonality is
4
. We will indicate how to verify this fact with computer algebra. To construct the example we need to give a twisted endomorphism of
E=\mathcal{O}_{C}(p)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(q)
where
p
and
q
are points on
C
such that
\vartheta(q-p)
is effective. To find these points construct a section
r\in H^{0}(\vartheta(1))
whose divisor has the form

\div r=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}

(54)

where
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}
and
q_{4}
are colinear points on
C
. Then
\vartheta(q_{4}-p_{1})\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(p_{2}+p_{3})
, hence we may put
p=p_{1},q=q_{4}
. Let us take
q_{1}=[1,0,0],q_{2}=[0,1,0],q_{3}=[1,1,0]
and

M=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}+X_{2}&X_{2}&X_{2}&X_{1}-X_{2}\\
X_{2}&X_{0}+X_{2}&X_{2}&X_{0}-X_{2}\\
X_{2}&X_{2}&X_{0}-X_{1}+X_{2}&X_{0}-X_{1}+X_{2}\\
X_{1}-X_{2}&X_{0}-X_{2}&X_{0}-X_{1}+X_{2}&X_{1}\end{pmatrix},\vec{r}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

The determinant of
M
defines a smooth quartic. Furthermore the matrix
\widetilde{M}=\begin{pmatrix}M|\vec{r}\end{pmatrix}
is singular when evaluated at the points
p_{1},p_{2}
and
p_{3}
. The six points at which
\widetilde{M}
is singular are
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},q_{1},q_{2}
and
q_{3}
. With the help of a computer one finds

p_{1}=[0,0,1],\;p_{2}=[1160,796,1],\;p_{3}=[794,1162,1].

The section
r
is then given as the image of
\vec{r}
under the map
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus 4}\rightarrow\vartheta(1)
from the resolution of
\vartheta(1)
.
Set
\phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&s_{1}\\
s_{2}&0\end{pmatrix}
, where
s_{1}\in H^{0}(\vartheta(p-q)),s_{2}\in H^{0}(\vartheta(q-p))
. The Higgs bundle
(E,\phi)
defines a
g^{1}_{3}
on the canonical cover with data
(C,\vartheta,\omega)
. Here
\omega
is an abelian differential cutting out the line through
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
. The canonical ring of
\widetilde{C}
is the subring of
R_{\psi^{*}\vartheta}
generated by all elements of degree
3
. Computations with the computer algebra systems Oscar
[
17
]
and Macaulay2
[
7
]
show that our example and canonical covers with a general branch divisor have canonical rings with different Betti numbers. This is to be expected because of Green’s conjecture and them having different Clifford indices.

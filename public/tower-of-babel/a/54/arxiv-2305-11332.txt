Equivariant cohomology of even-dimensional complex quadrics from a combinatorial point of view
Shintarô Kuroki

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to determine the ring structure of the graph equivariant cohomology of the GKM graph induced from the complex quadrics Q 2 ​ n Q_{2n} . We show that the graph equivariant cohomology is generated by two types of subgraphs in the GKM graph, namely M v M_{v} and Δ K \Delta_{K} , which are subject to four different types of relations. By utilizing this ring structure, we establish the multiplicative relation for the generators Δ K \Delta_{K} of degree 2 ​ n 2n and provide an alternative computation of the ordinary cohomology ring of Q 2 ​ n Q_{2n} , as previously computed by H. Lai. Additionally, we provide a combinatorial explanation for why the square of the half-degree generator x ∈ H 2 ​ n ​ ( Q 2 ​ n ) x\in H^{2n}(Q_{2n}) vanishes when n n is odd and is non-vanishing when n n is even.

Introduction

In the paper
[
8
]
, Goresky, Kottwiz, and MacPherson
established a framework for studying the class of manifolds with a torus action, known as
equivariantly formal
,
by using their fixed points and one-dimensional orbits.
These manifolds are now commonly referred to as
GKM manifold
.
Expanding on their work, Guillemin and Zara introduced the notion of an abstract
GKM graph
in
[
11
]
as a combinatorial counterpart of GKM manifolds, thus initiating the study of spaces with torus actions using the combinatorial structure of GKM graphs.
Since then,
the research of GKM manifolds and GKM graphs, commonly known as GKM theory, has been the subject of extensive research (e.g.,
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
22
]
).

One can view GKM theory as a methodology for computing equivariant cohomology based on the combinatorial structure of a graph.
For an equivariantly formal GKM manifold,
its equivariant cohomology is isomorphic to the
graph equivariant cohomology
of its corresponding GKM graph, see (
3.1
).
On the other hand, for abstract GKM graphs, the graph equivariant cohomology can be defined independently of geometry, leading to its study in various articles
(e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
18
,
17
,
22
]
).
In particular, in
[
22
]
, Maeda-Masuda-Panov introduced the combinatorial counterpart of a torus manifold, where a
torus manifold
is defined by a
2n
-dimensional
T^{n}
-manifold with fixed points.
This combinatorial object is called a
torus graph
, and
its properties have been extensively studied.
Notably, they established that the graph equivariant cohomology of a torus graph is isomorphic to its
face ring
, which is defined using the simplicial poset induced from the subgraphs of a torus graph, relying solely on algebraic and combinatorial arguments.
The advantage of establishing such a result for abstract GKM graphs, without relying on geometry, is that it can be applied to
a wider class of equivariantly formal GKM manifolds (or spaces) that share the same GKM graph.
This enables us to compute the equivariant cohomology of equivariantly formal GKM manifolds, even when well-known techniques for computing equivariant cohomology, such as certain methods in algebraic topology or Hamiltonian torus actions, cannot be applied.
Hence, the result in
[
22
]
can be regarded as a generalization of the computation of the equivariant cohomology ring of torus manifolds presented in
[
23
]
.

In our paper, we focus on the study of GKM graphs corresponding to
even-dimensional complex quadrics
.
An even-dimensional complex quadric
Q_{2n}
is defined by

\displaystyle Q_{2n}:=\{[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{2n+2}]\in\mathbb{C}P^{2n+1}\ |\ \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}z_{i}z_{2n+3-i}=0\},

having the natural

-action

(1.1)

\displaystyle[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{2n+2}]\mapsto[z_{1}t_{1}:z_{2}t_{2}:\cdots z_{n+1}t_{n+1}:t_{n+1}^{-1}z_{n+2}:t_{n}^{-1}z_{n+3}:\cdots t_{1}^{-1}z_{2n+2}],

\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}
, the following equation holds:

\displaystyle\alpha(ij)=-\alpha(\overline{i}\overline{j}).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha(ij)

\displaystyle=f(j)-f(i)=(x_{n}-x_{n+1}-f(\overline{j}))-(x_{n}-x_{n+1}-f(\overline{i}))

\displaystyle=f(\overline{i})-f(\overline{j})=\alpha(\overline{j}\overline{i})=-\alpha(\overline{i}\overline{j}).

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
j\in V_{2n}\setminus\{i,\overline{i}\}
, the following equation holds:

\displaystyle\alpha(ij)+\alpha(i\overline{j})=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(i).

Proof.

By definition of the axial function
\alpha:E_{2n}\to H^{2}(BT^{n+1})
and Lemma
2.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\alpha(ij)+\alpha(i\overline{j})

\displaystyle=(f(j)-f(i))+(f(\overline{j})-f(i))=f(j)+f(\overline{j})-2f(i)

\displaystyle=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(i).

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The GKM graph
\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}
is three-independent, i.e., for every vertex
i\in V_{2n}
and every distinct three vertices
j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\in V_{2n}\setminus\{i,\overline{i}\}
, the axial functions
\alpha(ij_{1}),\alpha(ij_{2}),\alpha(ij_{3})
are linearly independent.

Proof.

This is straightforward from Definition
2.1
.
∎

3.
Two generators

The
graph equivariant cohomology
of the GKM graph
\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}
is defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}):=\{h:V_{2n}\to H^{*}(BT^{n+1})\ |\ h(i)-h(j)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(ij)\ \text{for $ij\in E_{2n}$}\}.

The equation

in (

) is also called a

.
Note that

has the graded

\bigoplus_{t\geq 0}R_{t}

is the degree

In particular,
there is the injective homomorphism

(3.2)

\displaystyle\iota:H^{*}(BT^{n+1})\to H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})

such that the image of
x\in H^{*}(BT^{n+1})
, say
\iota(x):V_{2n}\to H^{*}(BT^{n+1})
, is defined by the function

\displaystyle\iota(x)(v)=x

for all
v\in V_{2n}
.
This induces the
H^{*}(BT^{n+1})
-action on
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
.

The following lemma holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

For the effective
T^{n+1}
-action on
Q_{2n}
, the following graded
H^{*}(BT^{n+1})
-algebra isomorphism holds:

\displaystyle H_{T^{n+1}}^{*}(Q_{2n})\simeq H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}).

Proof.

Because the effective
T^{n+1}
-action on
Q_{2n}
is obtained by the quotient
T^{n+1}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by the finite kernel of the action (
1.1
).
This implies that all isotropy subgroups of the effective

-action are connected.
Therefore, by using
H^{odd}(Q_{2n})=0
and
[

(also see
[

∎

Lemma

means that to compute the equivariant cohomology

is equivalent to compute the graph equivariant cohomology

.
The goal of this paper is to describe its generators and relations
by the combinatorial data of the GKM graph

; this will be proved in Theorem

.
The injective homomorphism (

) for

3.1.
Degree

We first define the degree two element, denoted by

for every

Definition 3.1

(degree 2 generators)
.

Take a vertex
v\in V_{2n}=[2n+2]
.
We define the function
M_{v}:V_{2n}\to H^{2}(BT^{n+1})
by

\displaystyle M_{v}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&j=v\\
\alpha(jv)=f(v)-f(j)&j\not=v,\overline{v}\\
\alpha(\overline{v}k)+\alpha(\overline{v}\overline{k})=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(\overline{v})&j=\overline{v}\end{array}\right.

The equality for
M_{v}(\overline{v})
is obtained by Lemma
2.3
; this means that
M_{v}(\overline{v})
does not depend on the choice of
k\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
.
The following proposition holds.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every
v\in V_{2n}
, the function
M_{v}:V_{2n}\to H^{2}(BT^{n+1})
is an element of
H^{2}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
, i.e.,

M_{v}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
.

Proof.

We claim that
M_{v}(j)-M_{v}(k)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(jk)
for every
jk\in E_{2n}
by case-by-case checking.

The case when
j=v
:

For every
k\in V_{2n}\setminus\{j,\overline{j}\}=V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
,
by Definition
3.1
we have

\displaystyle M_{v}(j)-M_{v}(k)

\displaystyle=0-(f(v)-f(k))=f(k)-f(v)=\alpha(vk)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(vk)=\alpha(jk).

The case when
j=\overline{v}
:

For every
k\in V_{2n}\setminus\{j,\overline{j}\}=V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
,
we have that

\displaystyle M_{v}(j)-M_{v}(k)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\overline{v}k)+\alpha(\overline{v}\overline{k})-\alpha(kv)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\overline{v}k)+\alpha(\overline{v}\overline{k})-\alpha(\overline{v}\overline{k})\quad({\rm by\ Lemma~\ref{easy-equation2}\ and\ Definition~\ref{effective_axial_function}})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\overline{v}k)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(\overline{v}k)=\alpha(jk).

The case when
j\not=v,\overline{v}
:

With the method similar to that demonstrated as above for the two cases (
k\not=\overline{v}
and
k=\overline{v}
), we can easily check that
M_{v}(j)-M_{v}(k)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(jk)
.

Therefore,
we have that
M_{v}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
.
∎

Before we define the higher degree generators, we introduce three properties for

For the vertices

•

•

Note that, by Definition
3.1
,
the value of
M_{v}(j)\in H^{2}(BT^{n+1})
for

coincides with the axial function
\alpha(jv)
on the vertex
j
of the full-subgraph
\Gamma_{I}
, where
I=V_{2n}\setminus\{v\}
.
We also note that the edge
jv
is the unique out-going edge of the full-subgraph
\Gamma_{I}
from the vertex
j\in I\setminus\{\overline{v}\}
.
The following proposition shows that
an element in
H^{2}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
with such a property is uniquely determined.

Proposition 3.3
.

If an element
A\in H^{2}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
satisfies that

A(j)=M_{v}(j)
for every
j\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
, then
A=M_{v}
.

Proof.

We first claim that
A(v)=0=M_{v}(v)
.
By using the congruence relations on the edges
jv
for all
j\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
, we have

\displaystyle A(j)-A(v)

\displaystyle=M_{v}(j)-A(v)=\alpha(jv)-A(v)\equiv-A(v)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(jv).

This shows that for every
j\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
there exists an integer
k_{j}
such that

\displaystyle A(v)=-k_{j}\alpha(jv)=k_{j}\alpha(vj).

In particular, for every
j_{1},\ j_{2}\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
,

\displaystyle A(v)=k_{j_{1}}\alpha(vj_{1})=k_{j_{2}}\alpha(vj_{2}).

By Lemma
2.4
,
this gives that
k_{j}=0
, thus establishing
A(v)=0
.

We next claim that
A(\overline{v})=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(\overline{v})=M_{v}(\overline{v})
.
By using the congruence relations on the edges
j\overline{v}
for all
j\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
, we have

\displaystyle A(j)-A(\overline{v})

\displaystyle=M_{v}(j)-A(\overline{v})=\alpha(jv)-A(\overline{v})\equiv 0\mod\alpha(j\overline{v}).

This shows that for every
j\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
there exists an integer
k_{j}
which satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle A(\overline{v})

\displaystyle=\alpha(jv)+k_{j}\alpha(j\overline{v})

\displaystyle=\alpha(\overline{v}\overline{j})+k_{j}\alpha(v\overline{j})\quad({\rm by\ Lemma~\ref{easy-equation2}\ and\ Definition~\ref{effective_axial_function}})

\displaystyle=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(\overline{v})-\alpha(\overline{v}j)+k_{j}\alpha(v\overline{j})\quad({\rm by\ Lemma~\ref{well-definedness-of-M}})

\displaystyle=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(\overline{v})-(1+k_{j})\alpha(\overline{v}j)\quad({\rm by\ Lemma~\ref{easy-equation2}})

In particular, this equation holds for every
j_{1},\ j_{2}\in V_{2n}\setminus\{v,\overline{v}\}
.
Therefore,
by using the similar method for the proof of
A(v)=0
, we obtain
1+k_{j}=0
, thus
k_{j}=-1
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle A(\overline{v})=\alpha(jv)-\alpha(j\overline{v})=x_{n}-x_{n+1}-2f(\overline{v}).

This establishes
A=M_{v}
.

Lemma 5.2

The homomorphism

Proof.

By the commutative diagram (

Take an element

By definition, for the vertex

\widetilde{\psi}
coincides with

on the vertex

We next put

\displaystyle f_{2}=f-g_{1}.

Then,
f_{2}(1)=0
.
So,

\displaystyle f_{2}(2)-f_{2}(1)\equiv 0\mod\alpha(21)=M_{1}(2).

Therefore, we have that

for some

.
By Proposition
3.4
,

\displaystyle x_{1}=M_{2}-M_{1},\ \ldots,\ x_{n+1}=M_{n+2}-M_{1}.

This implies that

g_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[M_{2}-M_{1},M_{3}-M_{1},\ldots,M_{n+2}-M_{1}]\subset\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{M},\mathcal{D}]

Note that it may also be regarded as

.
This shows that
g_{2}M_{1}
is in the image of
\widetilde{\psi}
.
Put

\displaystyle f_{3}=f_{2}-g_{2}M_{1}(=f-g_{1}-g_{2}M_{1}).

Then, by

n+2

\displaystyle=f_{n+1}-g_{n+1}M_{1}\cdots M_{n}

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

\displaystyle=f-(g_{1}+g_{2}M_{1}+\cdots+g_{n}M_{1}\cdots M_{n-1}+g_{n+1}M_{1}\cdots M_{n}).

f_{n+2}

\displaystyle+g_{n+2}\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}+g_{n+3}\Delta_{\{n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}+\cdots+g_{2n+2}\Delta_{\{2n+2\}}

\displaystyle=g_{n}(\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}+\Delta_{\{n+1,n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}})+X(\Delta)\quad({\rm by\ Relation~3}).

Therefore, if
f(v)=0
for every
v\in V_{2n}

can be written by the

terms only; more precisely,

(5.10)

\displaystyle f=g_{n}\Delta_{\{n+1,n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}+(g_{n}+g_{n}^{\prime})\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}+g_{n+1}\Delta_{\{n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}+\cdots+g_{2n}\Delta_{\{2n+2\}}.

We next claim that
f=0
if
f(v)=0
for every
v\in V_{2n}
.
The equality (
5.10
) implies that for the vertex

\displaystyle g_{n}(n+1)\Delta_{\{n+1,n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}(n+1)

\displaystyle=f(n+1)-\left((g_{n}+g_{n}^{\prime})\Delta_{\{n+2,\ldots,2n+2\}}+g_{n+1}\Delta_{\{n+3,\ldots,2n+2\}}+\cdots+g_{2n}\Delta_{\{2n+2\}}\right)(n+1)=0.

6.
Multiplicative formula of
\Delta_{K},\Delta_{H}
with
|K|=|H|=n+1

In this section, we show some multiplicative formula in
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
which gives a typical difference between
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
and the graph equivariant cohomology ring of a torus graph, i.e., the face ring proved in
[
22
]
.

Let
K,H\subset V_{2n}
be the subsets with the property
(\ast)
and
|K|=|H|=n+1
,
i.e., there are classes
\Delta_{K},\Delta_{H}\in H^{2n}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
.
Note that if
K\cap H\not=\emptyset
, then we can also define
\Delta_{K\cap H}\in H^{4n-2k}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
for
k=|K\cap H|-1
.
If
K\cap H=\emptyset
, then we put
\Delta_{\emptyset}=0
.
Recall that the elementary symmetric polynomial with degree
j
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{j}(r_{i}\ |\ i=1,\ldots,n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=j,\\
0\leq a_{i}\leq 1\end{subarray}}r_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots r_{n}^{a_{n}}.

Moreover,
because of Relation 2, for every
v=1,\ldots,n+1
,
we may put

\displaystyle X:=M_{v}+M_{\overline{v}}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}).

There is the following multiplicative formula in
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}]
(see Figure
12
and Figure
13
).

Theorem 6.1
.

The following formula holds:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}\cdot\Delta_{H}=\Delta_{K\cap H}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}X^{i}\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\not\in K\cup H)\right)\in H^{4n}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n}),

where
k=|K\cap H|-1
.

Proof.

If
K\cap H=\emptyset
, then the statement follows from Relation 1 and
\Delta_{\emptyset}=0
.
So we may assume
K\cap H\not=\emptyset
.

Because
\Delta_{K},\Delta_{H}\in H^{2n}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
, their multiplication satisfies
\Delta_{K}\cdot\Delta_{H}\in H^{4n}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n})
.
Moreover, the degree of each term on the right-hand side in (
6.1
) satisfies that

\displaystyle\deg\Delta_{K\cap H}+\deg X^{i}+\deg\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\not\in K\cup H)=(4n-2k)+2i+2(k-i)=2n.

For every
p\not\in K\cap H
, because
\Delta_{K}\cdot\Delta_{H}(p)=\Delta_{K\cap H}(p)=0
, the relation (
6.1
) holds.

For
p\in K\cap H
, by the definitions of
\Delta_{K}
’s and
M_{v}
’s,
it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}\cdot\Delta_{H}(p)=\Delta_{K\cap H}(p)\cdot\prod_{v\not\in K\cup H\cup\{\overline{p}\}}M_{v}(p).

Because
|K\cap H|=k+1
for
0\leq k\leq n
, we may put

\displaystyle K\cap H=\{a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\}\subset V_{2n},

where we assume
p=a_{0}
.
Because
|K|=|H|=n+1=\frac{|V_{2n}|}{2}
, we also have that

K^{c}=\{\overline{a}\ |\ a\in K\}
and
H^{c}=\{\overline{b}\ |\ b\in H\}
.
Therefore,

K^{c}\cap H^{c}=\{\overline{x}\ |\ x\in K\cap H\}=\{\overline{a_{0}},\overline{a_{1}},\ldots,\overline{a_{k}}\}
.
This shows that

v\not\in K\cup H\cup\{\overline{p}\}
if and only if
v\in(K\cup H\cup\{\overline{p}\})^{c}=(K^{c}\cap H^{c})\setminus\{\overline{p}\}=\{\overline{a_{1}},\ldots,\overline{a_{k}}\}
.
Therefore, if we put
\mathcal{A}:=\{\overline{a_{1}},\ldots,\overline{a_{k}}\}
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}\cdot\Delta_{H}(p)=\Delta_{K\cap H}(p)\cdot\prod_{v\in\mathcal{A}}M_{v}(p).

On the other hand,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}X^{i}\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\not\in K\cup H)(p)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}X(p)^{i}\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\{\overline{p}\}\cup\mathcal{A})(p).

By the definition of
M_{v}
, we have
M_{\overline{p}}(p)=X(p)
.
Therefore, for
0\leq i\leq k-1
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\{\overline{p}\}\cup\mathcal{A})(p)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\mathcal{A})(p)+X(p)\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i-1}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\mathcal{A})(p).

Substituting this into (
6.3
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}X(p)^{i}\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\{\overline{p}\}\cup\mathcal{A})(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}X(p)^{i}\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\mathcal{A})(p)+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}X(p)^{i+1}\cdot\mathfrak{S}_{k-i-1}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\mathcal{A})(p)+(-1)^{k}X(p)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{k}(M_{v}\ |\ v\in\mathcal{A}\})(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{v\in\mathcal{A}}M_{v}(p)\quad({\rm by}\ |\mathcal{A}|=k).

Combining (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), we obtain (
6.1
).
∎

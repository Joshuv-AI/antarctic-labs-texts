On the admissibility of observation operators in the context of maximal regularity
O. El Mennaoui, S. Hadd, Y. Kharou

Abstract
. We study admissible observation operators for perturbed evolution equations using the concept of maximal regularity. We first show the invariance of the maximal L p L^{p} -regularity under non-autonomous Miyadera-Voigt perturbations. Second, we establish the invariance of admissibility of observation operators under such a class of perturbations. Finally, we illustrate our result with two examples, one on a non-autonomous parabolic system, and the other on an evolution equation with mixed boundary conditions and a non-local perturbation.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{u}(t)+A(t)u(t)=0,\quad u(s)=x,&t\in[s,\tau],\cr y(t)=Cu(t),&t\in[s,\tau],\end{cases}

2.
Preliminaries on the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

Throughout this section,
X
and
D
are Banach spaces with
D\hookrightarrow_{d}X
. Moreover, we take real numbers
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\tau,\tau^{\prime}>0
such that
\tau^{\prime}<\tau
.

We recall some background about the concept of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity for non-autonomous linear systems.

Definition 2.1
.

A family
U:=(U(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is an evolution family on
X
if

(i)

U(t,s)=U(t,r)U(r,s),\quad U(s,s)=I
, for any
s,t,r\in[0,\tau]
with
0\leq s\leq r\leq t\leq\tau
,

(ii)

\left\{(t,s)\in[0,\tau]^{2}:t\geq s\right\}\ni(t,s)\mapsto U(t,s)
is strongly continuous.

The concept of evolution families is used to solve some classes of evolution
equations (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Chapter 2]
,
[
8
, Section VI.9]
and
[
22
]
).
That is, the evolution family arises as the solution operator of the well-posed non-autonomous
evolution equation

\displaystyle\dot{u}(t)+A(t)u(t)=0,\;u(s)=x\in X,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau,

where

A(t)
is (in general) an unbounded linear operator for every fixed
t
. We mention
that, in general, the function
U(t,s)x
, as a function of
t
, is not differentiable. However,
the differentiability of such a function is guaranteed if
A(t),\;t\in[0,\tau],
satisfy additional conditions (see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
24
]
).

In the following, we will introduce a situation in which one can naturally associate an evolution family to a family of unbounded operators. We first define the notion of maximal regularity for single operators.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}(D,X)
has
L^{p}
-maximal regularity (
p\in(1,\infty)
) and we write
A\in\mathscr{MR}_{p}
if for all bounded interval
(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
(
a<b
) and all
f\in L^{p}(a,b;X)
, there exists a unique
u\in W^{1,p}(a,b;X)\cap L^{p}(a,b;D)
such that

(4)

\dot{u}(t)+Au(t)=f(t)\quad t\text{-a.e. on }[a,b],\qquad u(a)=0.

According to
[
7
]
, the property of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity is independent of the bounded interval
(a,b)
, and if
A\in\mathscr{MR}_{p}
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
then
A\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}
for all
q\in(1,\infty)
. Hence, we can write
A\in\mathscr{MR}
for short. Also it is well known that if
A\in\mathscr{MR},
then
-A
generates an analytic semigroup on
X

[
17
]
. The converse is true if we work in Hilbert spaces
[
17
, Corollary 1.7]
. We also refer to
[
6
,
17
]
for the concept of
{\mathcal{R}}
-Boundedness and Fourier Multipliers applied to maximal regularity.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and define the following functional space

\displaystyle{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b):=W^{1,p}(a,b;X)\cap L^{p}(a,b;D),

which we call the space of maximal regularity. It is equipped with the following norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{p}(a,b)}:=\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(a,b;X)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(a,b;D)}\qquad\left(u\in{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)\right),

or, with the equivalent norm

\left(\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(a,b;X)}^{p}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(a,b;D)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The space
{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)
is a Banach space when equipped with one of the above norms. Moreover, we consider the
trace space
defined by

{\rm Tr_{p}}:=\left\{u(a):u\in{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)\right\},

and endowed with the norm

\|x\|_{\rm Tr_{p}}:=\inf\left\{\|u\|_{{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)}:x=u(a)\right\}.

The space
{\rm Tr_{p}}
is isomorphic to the real interpolation space
\left(X,D\right)_{1-1/p,p}

[
21
, Chapter 1]
. In particular,
{\rm Tr_{p}}
does not depend on the choice of the interval
(a,b)
and
D\hookrightarrow_{d}{\rm Tr_{p}}\hookrightarrow_{d}X
. We also note that

{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)\hookrightarrow_{d}C\left([a,b];{\rm Tr_{p}}\right),

and the constant of the embedding does not depend on the interval
(a,b)

[
2
, chap. 3]
. In fact, for each
u\in{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)
and
t\in[a,b],
we select
w(r)=u(t-r)
for a.e.
r\in[0,t]
. As
w(0)=u(t)
and
w\in{\rm MR}_{p}(0,t),
then
u(t)\in{\rm Tr_{p}}
. On the other hand, by the definition of the trace space, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\rm Tr_{p}}\leq\|w\|_{{\rm MR}_{p}(0,t)}\leq\|u\|_{{\rm MR}_{p}(a,b)}.

Consider now the non-autonomous evolution equation

(5)

\dot{u}(t)+A(t)u(t)=0\quad t\text{ a.e. on }[s,\tau],\qquad u(s)=x

for any
s\in[0,\tau)
,
x\in X
and
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to\mathcal{L}(D,X)
is a strongly measurable and bounded operator-valued function such that
A(t)\in\mathscr{MR}
for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. We say that
A(\cdot)
has
L^{p}
-maximal regularity on the bounded interval
[0,\tau]
(and we write
A(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{p}(0,\tau)
), if and only if for all
[a,b]
a sub-interval of
[0,\tau]
and all
f\in L^{p}(a,b;X)
, there exists a unique
u\in\mathrm{MR}_{p}(a,b)
such that

(6)

\dot{u}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t)\quad t\text{ a.e. on }[a,b],\qquad u(a)=0.

Note that, by compactness of
[0,\tau]
,
A(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{p}(0,\tau)
if and only if there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for all
[a,b]
a sub-interval of
[0,\tau]
with
|b-a|<\alpha_{0}
, and all
f\in L^{p}(a,b;X)
, there exists a unique
u\in\mathrm{MR}_{p}(a,b)
such that

\dot{u}(t)+A(t)u(t)=f(t)\quad t\text{ a.e. on }[a,b],\qquad u(a)=0.

In principle, one can ask if the condition
A(t)\in\mathscr{MR}
for each
t\in[0,\tau]
will imply that the evolution equation (
5
) is solved by an evolution family on
X
. This is true if
A(\cdot)
satisfies a kind of continuity.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
is relatively continuous if for all
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\delta>0
and
\eta\geq 0
such that for all
s,t\in[0,\tau]
, we have

\|A(t)x-A(s)x\|\leq\varepsilon\|x\|_{D}+\eta\|x\|\qquad(x\in D)

whenever
|t-s|\leq\delta
.

Now, if
A(t)\in\mathscr{MR}
for each
t\in[0,\tau]
and
A(\cdot)
is relatively continuous, the authors of
[
4
]
proved the existence of an evolution family
U:=(U(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau}
on the trace space
\mathrm{Tr}_{p}
. If the operators
A(t)
,
t\in[0,\tau]
are, in addition, accretive, they further showed that
U
extends to a contractive evolution family on
X
.

We need the following regularity on the map
A(\cdot)
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\nu\in(1,\infty)
. The function
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
satisfies the relative
\nu
-Dini condition if there exist
\eta\geq 0
,
\omega:[0,\tau]\to[0,\infty)
a continuous function with
\omega(0)=0
and

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\frac{\omega(t)}{t}\right)^{\nu}\mathrm{d}t<\infty

such that for all
x\in D
,
s,t\in[0,\tau]
, we have:

\|A(t)x-A(s)x\|\leq\omega\left(|t-s|\right)\|x\|_{D}+\eta\|x\|.

In the rest of this paper, we need the following condition:

(H1)

For any
t\in[0,\tau],

A(t)\in\mathscr{MR},
and
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
satisfies the relative
\nu
-Dini condition for some
\nu\in(1,\infty)
.

Remark 2.6
.

(i)

It is worth noting that if
A(\cdot)
satisfies the relative
p
-Dini condition, then
A(\cdot)
is relatively continuous.

(ii)

If
A(\cdot)
is Hölder continuous, that is,

\displaystyle\|A(t)x-A(s)x\|\leq|t-s|^{\alpha}\|x\|_{D},\qquad t,s\in[0,\tau],

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
A(\cdot)
satisfies the relative
\nu
-Dini condition for all
\nu\in(1,\frac{1}{1-\alpha})
.

(iii)

The condition
(H1)
implies that
A(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
for any
q\in(1,\infty),
see
[
4
]
.

The following result is a slightly modification of
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume that
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(D,X)
satisfies the condition
(H1)
and let
U:=(U(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau}
be the associated evolution family on the trace space
\mathrm{Tr}_{p}
for
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

U
extends to a bounded evolution family on
X
.

(ii)

For every
x\in X,
the function
u
given by

is the unique solution of the problem

\displaystyle\dot{u}(t)+A(t)u(t)=0\quad t\text{ a.e. on }[0,\tau],\qquad u(0)=x.

(iii)

For every
q\in(1,\nu),
and
x\in X
, we have
v:t\mapsto v(t)=tU(t,0)x\in{\rm MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
and

(8)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{{\rm MR}_{q}(0,\tau)}\leq M\|x\|

for a constant

, depending on

but independent of

. Moreover, the function

given by

belongs to the space

C\left([0,\tau];X\right)\cap L^{q}_{loc}\left((0,\tau];D\right)\cap W^{1,q}_{loc}\left((0,\tau];X\right).

.

The condition
(H1)
implies the existence of an evolution family

on

for any

3.
The stability of maximal
L^{p}
-regularity under non-autonomous Miyadera-Voigt kind of perturbations

In this section we assume that the function
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
satisfies the condition
(H1)
. Then we discuss the maximal regularity a
A(\cdot)+P(\cdot),
where
P(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
is a strongly measurable and bounded application such that:

(H2)
There exists
\mu\in(1,\infty)
and a constant
c>0
such that for
0\leq s<\tau^{\prime}<\tau
, we have

\left(\int_{s}^{\tau^{\prime}}\|P(t)U(t,s)x\|^{\mu}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}\leq c\|x\|,\qquad(x\in D).

The following result shows the maximal regularity of
A^{P}(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
defined in (
3
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
A(\cdot)
and
P(\cdot)
satisfy the conditions
(H1)-(H2)
. Then
A^{P}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
for every
q\in(1,\mu]
.

Proof.

According to Remark
2.6
we have
A(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
for any
q\in(1,\infty)
. Let
[a,b]
(
a<b
) be a sub-interval of
[0,\tau]
and define the operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
on
L^{q}(a,b;X)
by:

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{B}):=\left\{u\in W^{1,q}(a,b;X):u(a)=0\right\},\quad\mathcal{B}u:=\dot{u},

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A}):=L^{q}(a,b;D),\quad\mathcal{A}u:=A(s)u(s)\;\left(s\in(a,b)\right).

On the other hand, define

\displaystyle({\mathcal{P}}u)(t):=P(t)u(t),\quad u\in L^{q}([a,b],D),\quad a.e.t\in[a,b].

Observe that for any
u\in L^{q}([a,b],D)
,
t\mapsto({\mathcal{P}}u)(t)
is measurable and

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{a}\left\|(\mathcal{P}u)(t)\right\|^{q}dt\leq\left(\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|P(t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)}\right)^{q}\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}([a,b],D)}.

This shows that
{\mathcal{P}}:L^{q}([a,b],D)\to L^{q}([a,b],X)
, and

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{P}}u\|_{L^{q}([a,b],X)}\leq\|P(\cdot)\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{L^{q}([a,b],D)},\qquad(u\in L^{q}([a,b],D)).

Now let the operator
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{P}
on
L^{q}(a,b;X)
with domain
D(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{P}):=D(\mathcal{A})\cap D(\mathcal{B})
. The fact that
A(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
implies that the inverse
(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})^{-1}
exists in
L^{q}([a,b],X)
. Moreover, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}+\mathcal{P}=(I+\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})^{-1})(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}).

Let
q\in(1,\mu]
and
q^{\prime}\in(1,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. Then
P(\cdot)
satisfies also the condition
(H2)
if we replace
\mu
with
q
. Now for
f\in L^{q}(a,b;X)
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})^{-1}f\|^{q}_{L^{q}(a,b;X)}

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\|[\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})^{-1}f](t)\|^{q}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{b}\left\|P(t)\int_{a}^{t}U(t,r)f(r)\,dr\right\|^{q}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}\left(\int_{a}^{t}\left\|P(t)U(t,r)f(r)\right\|\,dr\right)^{q}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(b-a)^{q/q^{\prime}}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{a}^{t}\left\|P(t)U(t,r)f(r)\right\|^{q}\,dr\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(b-a)^{q/q^{\prime}}\int_{a}^{b}\int_{r}^{b}\left\|P(t)U(t,r)f(r)\right\|^{q}\,dt\,dr

\displaystyle\leq(b-a)^{q/q^{\prime}}c\int_{a}^{b}\left\|f(r)\right\|^{q}\,dr

\displaystyle=(b-a)^{q/q^{\prime}}c\;\|f\|^{q}_{L^{q}(a,b;X)},

by Hölder inequality, Fubini theorem and
(H2)
. By choosing a small interval
[a,b],
we can assume that
(b-a)^{q/q^{\prime}}c<1
. This ends the proof due to the compactness of
[0,\tau]
(see comments after Definition
2.3
).
∎

A similar result for the maximal regularity of

Proposition 3.3

Assume that
A(\cdot)

(9)

\displaystyle V(t,s)x=U(t,s)x+\int^{t}_{s}V(t,\sigma)P(\sigma)U(\sigma,s)xd\sigma

for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau
and

. Furthermore for any

, the function

solves the problem
belongs to the space

C\left([0,\tau];X\right)\cap L^{q}_{loc}\left((0,\tau];D\right)\cap W^{1,q}_{loc}\left((0,\tau];X\right),

for all
q\in(1,\inf\{\nu,\mu\}]
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.1
,
A^{P}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
for every
q\in(1,\mu]
. Then by
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
, to
A^{P}(\cdot)
, we associate a unique evolution family
V:=(V(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau}
on
{\rm Tr_{q}}
for
q\in(1,\mu]
. In particular, for
x\in D
, the functions
t\mapsto V(t,s)x
and
t\mapsto U(t,s)x
belong to
\mathrm{MR}_{q}(s,\tau)
for
q\in(1,p_{0}]
, so that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(V(t,s)x-U(t,s)x\right)

\displaystyle=-A^{P}(t)V(t,s)x+A(t)U(t,s)x

\displaystyle=-A^{P}(t)(V(t,s)x-U(t,s)x)-P(t)U(t,s)x.

The fact that
A^{P}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{MR}_{q}(0,\tau)
for
q\in(1,\mu]
implies that
there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

(10)

\displaystyle\|V(\cdot,s)x-U(\cdot,s)x\|_{\mathrm{MR}_{q}(s,\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|P(\cdot)U(\cdot,s)x\|_{L^{q}([s,\tau],X)}\leq\kappa\gamma\|x\|

for any
x\in X
, due to
(H2)
. By using the estimate (
10
) and the embedding
\mathrm{MR}_{q}(s,\tau)\hookrightarrow_{d}C([s,\tau],X)
(densely and continuously), there exists a constant
M>0,
independent of
t
and
s
such that

\displaystyle\|V(t,s)x-U(t,s)x\|\leq M\|x\|

for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau
and
x\in{\rm Tr}_{p}
. Now the density of
{\rm Tr}_{p}
in
X
implies that
V
has an extension to an evolution family on
X
. Now we can write

\displaystyle V(t,s)x-U(t,s)x=\int^{t}_{s}V(t,\sigma)P(\sigma)U(\sigma,s)xd\sigma

for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau
and
x\in X
.

On the other hand, let
q\in(1,\inf\{\nu,\mu\}]
and
x\in{\rm Tr}_{q}
. By the
L^{q}
-maximal regularity of
A(\cdot)
, the function
v(t):=tV(t,0)x
is the unique solution of the non-homogeneous problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{v}(t)+A^{P}(t)v(t)=V(t,0)x,&a.e.\;t\in[0,\tau],\cr u(0)=0,\end{cases}

and there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{{\rm MR}_{q}(0,\tau)}\leq\kappa\|V(\cdot,0)x\|_{L^{q}(0,\tau;X)}\leq M^{\prime}\|x\|

for a constant
M^{\prime}>0
depending on
\kappa,M
and
q
. By the density of
{\rm Tr}_{q}
in
X,
the above estimate holds also for every
x\in X
. In addition, since
{\rm MR}_{q}(0,\tau)\hookrightarrow_{d}C([0,\tau],X)
, we have

\displaystyle\|tV(t,0)x\|\leq M^{\prime}\|x\|

for almost every
t\in[0,\tau]
and
x\in X
. In particular, for every
x\in X
and every
q\in(1,\inf\{\nu,\mu\}],
we have

\displaystyle V(\cdot,0)x\in L^{q}_{loc}((0,\tau],D)\cap W^{1,q}_{loc}((0,\tau],X).

∎

4.

Throughout this section

Definition 4.1

Proposition 4.2

Let the assumption

\left(\int_{s}^{\tau}\|CU(t,s)x\|_{Y}^{\theta}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\leq\gamma\|x\|,\qquad(x\in D).

Obviously the condition (

) implies (

). Conversely, let

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{s}\|CU(r,s)x\|^{\theta}dr

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\|CU(r,s)x\|^{\theta}dr

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
be a strongly measurable and bounded function. Assume that

for every

and that there exist

and

Then

\mathscr{O}^{\theta}_{Y}(A(t_{0}))=\mathscr{O}^{\theta}_{Y}(A(t_{1}))

for every
\theta<\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}
and
t_{0},t_{1}\in[0,\tau]

Let
0<t\leq\tau
,
x\in D
and
\theta\in(1,\infty)
. Consider the function
v:[0,t]\ni r\mapsto e^{-(t-r)A(t_{0})}e^{-rA(t_{1})}x

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}v(r)

\displaystyle=A(t_{0})e^{-(t-r)A(t_{0})}e^{-rA(t_{1})}x-e^{-(t-r)A(t_{0})}A(t_{1})e^{-rA(t_{1})}x

\displaystyle=e^{-(t-r)A(t_{0})}\left(A(t_{0})-A(t_{1})\right)e^{-rA(t_{1})}x.

By integrating between
0

e^{-tA(t_{1})}x=e^{-tA(t_{0})}x+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-r)A(t_{0})}\left(A(t_{0})-A(t_{1})\right)e^{-rA(t_{1})}xdr.

5.
Examples

In this section, we give two situations that illustrate our abstract results. We choose the heat equation as model, but the results can also be applied to other physical models.

5.1.
A heat equation with Dirichlet conditions and point observation

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and

. Let

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}a_{ij}(t,x)x_{i}x_{j}\geq\beta\|x\|^{2}

for any

\displaystyle X=L^{q}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad D=W^{2,q}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega).

Define a family of unbounded operators on

\displaystyle A(t)u:=-\sum_{ij}a_{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u-b_{0}(t,\cdot)u,\quad t\in[0,\tau],\;u\in D,

]

[

and

(24)

\displaystyle|k(t,s,\xi,x)|\leq M(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\delta|\xi-x|^{2}}{t-s}+\tilde{\delta}(t-s)\right)

[
18

.

\displaystyle P(t):=(-A(t))^{\alpha}_{|D},\qquad t\in[0,\tau].

From

]

such that for

and

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\tau^{\prime}}|(-A(t))^{\alpha}U(t,s)\varphi|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{M}\int_{s}^{\tau^{\prime}}(t-s)^{-\alpha p}dt\|\varphi\|^{p}_{X}

satisfies the condition

for

. We also define an observation operator

\displaystyle C:D\to\mathbb{C},\quad C\varphi:=\varphi(c),\quad\varphi\in D.

As in

), for

\tau^{\prime}>s

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{\tau^{\prime}}|CU(t,s)\varphi|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{s}^{\tau^{\prime}}(t-s)^{-\frac{np}{2q}}dt\right)\|\varphi\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{p}\|\varphi\|^{p}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for
q>\frac{np}{2}
and a certain constant
\gamma>0
. This shows that
(C,A(\cdot))
is admissible with exponent
p
. Finally, according to Theorem
4.6
,
(C,A(\cdot)+P(\cdot))
is admissible.

5.2.
A heat equation with mixed Dirichlet and Neumann conditions and a non local perturbation

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{0}\cap\Gamma_{1}=\emptyset
. We select

\displaystyle X:=L^{2}(\Omega),\quad Y:=L^{2}(\partial\Omega),

\displaystyle D:=\left\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega):u=0\;\text{on}\;\Gamma_{0}\;\text{and}\;\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0\;\text{on}\;\Gamma_{1}\right\},

where
\nu
is the unit normal vector. Define
{\mathbb{A}}:=\Delta
with domain
D({\mathbb{A}})=D
. It is well known that
{\mathbb{A}}
is a generator of a strongly continuous semigroup on
X
, see e.g.
[
19
]
. Now consider operators
A(t):D\to X,\,t\in[0,\tau]
such that

\displaystyle A(t)u={\mathbb{A}}u+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\psi(t,\cdot,z)u(\cdot,z)dz,\qquad u\in D,

where
\psi:[0,\tau]\times\Omega\times\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function such that
\psi(t,\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\partial\Omega))
for a.e.
t\in[0,\tau]
and

(25)

\displaystyle\|\psi(t,\cdot,\cdot)-\psi(s,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\partial\Omega))}\leq\omega(t-s)

for a.e.
t,s\in[0,\tau]
, where
\omega:[0,\tau]\to[0,\infty)
is a continuous function with
w(0)=0
and satisfies the condition (
7
) for
p=2
.

Let
b\in L^{\infty}([0,\tau])
a scalar function and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Select

\displaystyle P(t):=b(t){\mathbb{A}}^{\alpha}:D\to X,\qquad t\in[0,\tau].

Moreover, we consider the observation operator

\displaystyle C:D\to Y,\quad Cu=\begin{cases}0,&\text{on}\,\Gamma_{0},\cr u,&\text{on}\,\Gamma_{1}.\end{cases}

According to
[
19
]
,
C\in\mathscr{O}^{2}_{Y}({\mathbb{A}})
. On the other hand, for any
t\in[0,\tau],
we can write
A(t)={\mathbb{A}}+B(t)C
with
B(t):Y\to X
defined by

\displaystyle B(t)g=\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi(t,\cdot,z)g(\cdot,z)dz,\qquad g\in Y.

Using (
25
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|B(t)g\|\leq\left(\|\omega\|_{\infty}+\|\psi(0,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times\partial\Omega))}\right)\|g\|_{Y}:=c\|g\|_{Y}

for any
g\in Y
and
t\in[0,\tau]
. Using the admissibility of
C
for
{\mathbb{A}}
, for any
s,\tau^{\prime}\in[0,\tau)
such that
s<\tau^{\prime}
, and
f\in D,
we have

\displaystyle\int^{\tau^{\prime}}_{s}\|B(t)Ce^{-{\mathbb{A}}(t-s)}f\|^{2}dt\leq c^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}-s}\|Ce^{-{\mathbb{A}}t}f\|^{2}dt\leq(c\gamma)^{2}\|f\|^{2},

for a constant
\gamma>0
. Now by using
[
11
]
, we associate to
A(\cdot)
an evolution family
U=(U(t,s))_{0\leq s\leq t\leq\tau}
on
X
. On the other hand, as
A\in\mathscr{MR},
then by
[
3
]
,
A(t)\in\mathscr{MR}
for any
t\in[0,\tau]
. In addition, for each
t
,
B(t)C
is admissible for the generator
{\mathbb{A}},
then by
[
12
]
,
(C,A(t))
is admissible for each
t\in[0,\tau].
Thus by Theorem
4.3
,
(C,A(\cdot))
is admissible.

Let us now discuss the admissibility of
C
for
A(\cdot)+P(\cdot)
. For any
f\in D
and
s,\tau^{\prime}\in[0,\tau)
with
s<\tau^{\prime},
and using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int^{\tau^{\prime}}_{s}\|P(t)e^{-(t-s){\mathbb{A}}}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|b(\cdot)\|_{\infty}\int^{\tau^{\prime}-s}_{0}\|(-{\mathbb{A}})^{\alpha}e^{-t{\mathbb{A}}}f\|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|b(\cdot)\|_{\infty}\int^{\tau^{\prime}}_{0}\frac{dt}{t^{\alpha}}\|f\|^{2}=\|b(\cdot)\|_{\infty}\frac{(\tau^{\prime})^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|b(\cdot)\|_{\infty}\tau^{\prime}\|f\|^{2}

for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Now by using Remark
4.7
,
P(\cdot)
satisfies the condition
(H2)
for
\mu=2
. On the other hand, for
t,s\in[0,\tau]
and
f\in D,
we have

\displaystyle\|A(t)f-A(s)f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}|\psi(t,x,z)-\psi(s,x,z)||(Cf)(z)|dz\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\psi(t,x,z)-\psi(s,x,z)|^{2}dzdx\|Cf\|^{2}_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\omega(t-s)^{2}\|Cf\|^{2}_{Y},

due to the estimate (
25
). This implies that
A(\cdot):[0,\tau]\to{\mathcal{L}}(D,X)
satisfies the relative
2
-Dini condition, so that the condition
(H1)
holds. Thus
(C,A(\cdot)+P(\cdot))
is admissible, due to Theorem
4.6
.

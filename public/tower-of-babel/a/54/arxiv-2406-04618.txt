1.
Introduction and main results

A fundamental result in the regularity of minimal surfaces is that a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is is locally perimeter minimising satisfies the thick density estimates:

(1.1)

\displaystyle|E\cap B_{R}(x)|\geq CR^{n}\text{ and }|B_{R}(x)\setminus E|\geq CR^{n}

for all
R>0
,
x\in\partial^{\ast}E
where
B_{R}(x)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centred at
x
with radius
R
and
|F|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
F
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see for example
[
Mag12
, Section 16.2]
. Here the constant
C>0
depends only on
n
. Since any sufficiently regular set
E
satisfies (
1.1
) for
R
sufficiently small and with the constant depending on the
C^{2}
regularity of
E
at
x
, the non-triviality of (
1.1
) is that it holds for all
R>0
and that the constant is universal. The assumption that
E
is locally minimising is essential to the proof of (
1.1
) since the proof takes advantage of the fact that
E\cap\partial B_{R}(x)
is a competitor for
\partial^{\ast}E\cap B_{R}(x)
.

If one only assumes that
E
has zero mean curvature, that is, it is locally stationary with respect to the perimeter functional then one can obtain the surface density estimate:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}(E;B_{R}(x))\geq CR^{n-1}

for all
R>0
,
x\in\partial^{\ast}E
, and with
C
depending only on
n
. Indeed, the monotonicity formula for sets of zero mean curvature immediately implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{Per}(E;B_{R}(x))}{R^{n-1}}\geq\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\operatorname{Per}(E;B_{r}(x))}{r^{n-1}}=n\omega_{n}

where
\omega_{n}=|B_{1}|
, see
[
Mag12
, Theorem 17.16]
. Observe that the thick density estimates (
1.1
) directly imply the surface density estimates (
1.2
) via the restricted isoperimetric inequality

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}(E;B_{R}(x))\geq C\big(\min\{|E\cap B_{R}(x)|,|B_{R}(x)\setminus E\}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

but sets with zero mean curvature do not necessarily satisfy (
1.1
), for example, if
E=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times(0,1)
then
\partial E
has zero mean curvature, but
|E\cap B_{R}|\leq|B^{n-1}_{R}\times(0,1)|\leq CR^{n-1}
.

[
CRS10
]

In analogy to the classical case, if

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}(E;B_{R})\geq CR^{n-1}\text{ for all }R>0.

The constant

The precise definition

A nice application of ?THM?
is that the fractional Sobolev inequality holds on hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with zero
s
-mean curvature. The classical Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
states that if
1\leq p<n
and
p^{\ast}:=\frac{np}{n-p}
then

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
C>0
depending only on
n
and
p
. It is also interesting to ask if the Sobolev inequality can hold on hypersurfaces
M^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. It is easy to see that (
1.4
) (with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
replaced with
M
and
\nabla=\nabla_{M}
the gradient with respect to the induced metric from
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
) cannot hold verbatim since if
M
is compact then
u=1\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
; however, a Sobolev-type inequality does hold if one adds an extra
L^{p}
error term involving the mean curvature
\mathrm{H}_{M}
:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(M)}\leq C\big(\|\nabla_{M}u\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|\mathrm{H}_{M}u\|_{L^{p}(M)}\big)

for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
. The inequality (
1.5
) is sometimes known as the Michael-Simon and Allard inequality after
[
All72
]
and
[
MS73
]
. Also, see
[
Bre21
]
where the optimal constant in (
1.5
) is obtained and
[
CM22
]
for a simple proof of (
1.5
).

Naturally, one would like to know if (
1.5
) extends to the nonlocal case. If
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq p<n/s
, and
p^{\ast}=\frac{np}{n-sp}
then it is well known that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where

(1.6)

\displaystyle[v]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

see
[
DPV12
]
and references therein. If
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set with locally finite perimeter (or sufficiently smooth boundary) and

\displaystyle[v]_{W^{s,p}(\partial^{\ast}E)}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\partial^{\ast}E}\int_{\partial^{\ast}E}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\mathop{}\!d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{y}\mathop{}\!d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{x}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

then it is currently an open problem whether the inequality

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\partial^{\ast}E)}\leq C\big([u]_{W^{s,p}(\partial^{\ast}E)}+\|\mathrm{H}_{s,E}u\|_{L^{p}(\partial^{\ast}E)}\big)

holds for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\partial^{\ast}E)
. Recently, it was shown in
[
CCC23
]
that (
1.7
) does hold provided that
E
is convex. Their argument relies on first establishing a pointwise lower bound on the
s
-mean curvature in terms of the perimeter. Unfortunately, as they point out in their paper, this pointwise inequality clearly cannot hold in the non-convex case, so the argument doesn’t easily generalise to the case of arbitrary
E
. Furthermore, in
[
CC19
, Proposition 5.2]
, it was proven via an extension of an (unpublished) argument due to Brezis (see
[
BV16
, Theorem 2.2.1]
for this argument), that the boundary density estimate (
1.2
) implies (
1.7
) without the term involving the
s
-mean curvature. Hence, via
[
CC19
, Proposition 5.2]
, ?THM?
immediately implies that (
1.7
) holds for sets of zero
s
-mean curvature:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
1\leq p<n/s
,
p^{\ast}=\frac{np}{n-sp}
, and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a measurable set with locally finite perimeter satisfying
\mathrm{H}_{s,E}=0
on
\partial^{\ast}E
. Then the Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\partial^{\ast}E)}\leq C[u]_{W^{s,p}(\partial^{\ast}E)}

holds for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\partial^{\ast}E)
. The constant
C>0
depends only on
n
and
s
.

Organisation of paper

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we fix notation and give standard definitions of the classical theory of BV functions and minimal surfaces, and definitions from the theory of nonlocal minimal surfaces. In Section 3, we prove an interpolation inequality between restricted

2.
Definitions and notation

First, we recall some notation and definitions of functions of bounded variation and the classical theory of minimal surfaces. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Given a function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
the
total variation
of
u
in
\Omega
is given by

\displaystyle|\nabla u|(\Omega)=\sup\bigg\{\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{div}\phi\mathop{}\!dx\text{ s.t. }\phi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1\bigg\}.

The space
\operatorname{BV}(\Omega)
is defined to be the set of
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
such that
|\nabla u|(\Omega)<+\infty
and the space
\operatorname{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is the set of functions in
\operatorname{BV}(\Omega^{\prime})
for all
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. Moreover, we say a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has
finite perimeter
in
\Omega
if
\chi_{E}\in\operatorname{BV}(\Omega)
where
\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of
A
. In this case, we define the
perimeter of

E
in
\Omega
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}(E;\Omega)=|\nabla\chi_{E}|(\Omega).

We also say
E
has
locally finite perimeter
in
\Omega
if
\chi_{E}\in\operatorname{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and simply
E
has locally finite perimeter if
\chi_{E}\in\operatorname{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Finally, given
E
with finite perimeter in
\Omega
, the distributional gradient of
\chi_{E}
, i.e.
\nabla\chi_{E}
, is a vector-valued Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then we can define the
reduced boundary
of
E
, denoted
\partial^{\ast}E
, as the set

\displaystyle\bigg\{x\in\partial E\text{ s.t. }|\nabla\chi_{E}|(B_{r}(x))>0\text{ for all }r>0,\text{ and }\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{\nabla\chi_{E}(B_{r}(x))}{|\nabla\chi_{E}|(B_{r}(x))}\text{ exists and is in }\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\bigg\}.

Now, let us turn to the nonlocal case. Let
s\in(0,1)
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{s}(A,B)=\int_{A}\int_{B}\frac{\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

The
s
-perimeter of a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
\Omega
is defined to be

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{I}_{s}(E\cap\Omega,E^{c}\cap\Omega)+\mathcal{I}_{s}(E\cap\Omega,E^{c}\setminus\Omega)+\mathcal{I}_{s}(E\setminus\Omega,E^{c}\cap\Omega).

We will also sometimes refer to
\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E;\Omega)
as the
restricted
s
-perimeter
. Note, we also have that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E;\Omega)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)}\frac{|\chi_{E}(x)-\chi_{E}(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx

where we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(\Omega)=(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})^{c}=(\Omega\times\Omega)\cup(\Omega\times\Omega^{c})\cup(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

A measurable set
E
is a
minimiser
of the
s
-perimeter in
\Omega
or
s
-minimal in
\Omega
if

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E;\Omega)\leq\operatorname{Per}_{s}(F;\Omega)

for all measurable set
F
such that
E\setminus\Omega=F\setminus\Omega
. Moreover, a measurable set
E
is stationary with respect to the
s
-perimeter in
\Omega
or
s
-stationary if

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!d}{\mathop{}\!dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E_{t,T};\Omega)=0

for all
T\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
where

\displaystyle E_{t,T}:=\psi_{T}(E,t)

and
\psi_{T}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{ \ }l @{\quad\: \;} l}\partial_{t}\psi_{T}&=T\circ\psi_{T},\hfil\quad\>&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\\
\psi_{T}&=T,\hfil\quad\>&\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{t=0\}.\end{array}\right.

One can also define
s
-stationary stable sets, but we do not require this definition for the current paper.

3.
An interpolation inequality

In this section, we prove an interpolation inequality which will allow us to estimate the restricted
s
-perimeter with the restricted perimeter plus an arbitrarily small error. The precise statement is as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R>0
and
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,3^{-\frac{1}{s}})
,

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{R})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx\leq C\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{1-s}{s}}R^{1-s}}{1-s}|\nabla u|(B_{(1+\varepsilon^{-1/s})R})+\frac{\varepsilon R^{n-s}}{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigg).

The constant
C>0
depends only on
n
.

For similar interpolation inequalities to ?THM?
and ?THM?
below, see
[
CSV19
, Equation 3.]
and
[
CFS24
, Lemma 3.15]
. A small, but important difference between ?THM?
and these results is the inclusion of the
\varepsilon
term. This will be essential to the proof of ?THM?
since it will allow us to absorb an error term coming from ?THM?
into the left-hand side of a chain of inequalities.

Taking
u=\chi_{E}
in ?THM?
, we immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
R>0
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set with locally finite perimeter. Then, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,3^{-\frac{1}{s}})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Per}_{s}(E;B_{R})\leq C\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon^{-\frac{1-s}{s}}R^{1-s}}{1-s}\operatorname{Per}(E;B_{(1+\varepsilon^{-1/s})R})+\frac{\varepsilon R^{n-s}}{s}\big)

The constant
C>0
depends only on
n
.

Before we give the proof of ?THM?
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}(x-ty,x+(1-t)y)\leq\chi_{\{|x|\leq|y|+1\}}(x,y).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ s.t. }|x|<1\text{ or }|y|<1\}
, if
(x-ty,x+(1-t)y)\in\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})
then either
|x-ty|<1
or
|x+(1-t)y|<1
. If
|x-ty|<1
then

\displaystyle|x|\leq t|y|+1\leq|y|+1.

Similarly, if
|x+(1-t)y|<1
then

\displaystyle|x|\leq 1+(1-t)|y|\leq 1+|y|.

∎

Now we give the proof of ?THM?
.

Proof of ?THM?
.

First, assume that
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
R=1
. Let
\rho>0
to be chosen later and write

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx=I_{\rho}+J_{\rho}

where

\displaystyle I_{\rho}=\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})\cap\{|x-y|<\rho\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx\text{ and }J_{\rho}=\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx.

We will estimate
I_{\rho}
and
J_{\rho}
separately.

For
I_{\rho}
, via the change of variables
y\to y+x
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|\chi_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}(x,y)\chi_{\{|x-y|<\rho\}}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x+y)-u(x)|\chi_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}(x,x+y)\chi_{B_{\rho}}(y)}{|y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx.

Since

\displaystyle|u(x+y)-u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|y|\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla u(x+ty)|\mathop{}\!dt,

it follows that

\displaystyle I_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\nabla u(x+ty)|\chi_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}(x,x+y)\chi_{B_{\rho}}(y)}{|y|^{n+s-1}}\mathop{}\!dt\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx

which, via the change of variable
x\to x-ty
, becomes

\displaystyle I_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla u(x)|\chi_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}(x-ty,x+(1-t)y)\chi_{B_{\rho}}(y)}{|y|^{n+s-1}}\mathop{}\!dx\mathop{}\!dt\mathop{}\!dy.

Then, from ?THM?
, it follows that

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}(x-ty,x+(1-t)y)\chi_{B_{\rho}}(y)\leq\chi_{\{|x|\leq|y|+1\}}(x,y)\chi_{B_{\rho}}(y)\leq\chi_{B_{\rho+1}}(x)\chi_{B_{\rho}}(y),

so

\displaystyle I_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho+1})}\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{\mathop{}\!dy}{|y|^{n+s-1}}\leq\frac{C\rho^{1-s}}{1-s}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho+1})}

with
C
depending only on
n
.

For
J_{\rho}
,

\displaystyle J_{\rho}\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}}\frac{\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

Assuming that
\rho>3
,
\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}\subset\big((B_{1}\times B_{1}^{c})\cup(B_{1}^{c}\times B_{1})\big)\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}
. Indeed, if there exists
(x,y)\in(B_{1}\times B_{1})\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}
then
3<\rho\leq|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|<2
, a contradiction. Hence,

\displaystyle J_{\rho}\leq 4\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\iint_{(B_{1}\times B_{1}^{c})\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}}\frac{\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

Since
|x-y|\geq|y|-|x|\geq|y|-1
and
(B_{1}\times B_{1}^{c})\cap\{|x-y|\geq\rho\}\subset B_{1}\times B_{\rho-1}^{c}
, we find that

\displaystyle J_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho-1}}\frac{\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx}{(|y|-1)^{n+s}}.

Since
\rho>3
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\rho}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho-1}}\frac{\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx}{|y|^{n+s}}\leq\frac{C(\rho-1)^{-s}}{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{C\rho^{-s}}{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

with
C
depending only on
n
.

Thus, combining the above estimates, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(B_{1})}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\mathop{}\!dy\mathop{}\!dx

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\frac{\rho^{1-s}}{1-s}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho+1})}+\frac{\rho^{-s}}{s}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\bigg)

for all
\rho>3
. Choosing
\rho=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{s}}
, we prove the result.

For a general element
u\in\operatorname{BV}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let
u_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, defined as in the proof of Theorem 2 of §5.2.2 in
[
EG92

. Then

Finally, to obtain the case for general

as required.
∎

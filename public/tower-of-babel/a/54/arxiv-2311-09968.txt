Gradient Flow in Morse and Analytic Settings
Leyang Zhang email leyangz_hawk@outlook.com

Abstract
This report is actually the in-class project of MATH489 FA2022, Dynamics and Differential Equations in University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. The report is mainly about gradient flow of a Morse-type functions (Morse functions, Morse–Bott functions, etc.) and analytic functions. Several small results that I develop during the reading are included as well. They have a focus on the convergence, limiting rate and direction of gradient flow in either setting.



1
Introduction

Given a differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the gradient vector of
f
at each
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\nabla f(p)
, points at the direction of the fastest value change. More generally, this can be formalized on a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, where
g
is the Riemannian metric. If
f
is smooth, the gradient vectors on
M
form a (smooth) vector field to which we can define a flow. This is called the gradient flow of
f
(more formal definition will be given in the next section).

Gradient vector fields and the gradient flow have received studies both in Mathematics and engineering, where various special properties have been discovered. These properties make gradient flow a widely used method in optimization for finding (local or global) minimum of a function. In this report, we will focus on gradient flows in Morse-type function and analytic function settings. In section
2
, I make the basic definitions and assumptions. In the following section, I list the Morse theories I read and present several corollaries I made from them. In section
4
, I list results related to gradient flows of Morse-Smale and Morse–Bott functions, as well as results I made when reading about them; proofs will be given. All these results are based on section
3
. In section
5
, I focus instead on functions satisfying Lojasiewicz inequality, which include (real) analytic and generalized Morse–Bott functions. Again, results I read and I developed are included.

All the results
with proofs
are what I came up with during my reading. It is believed that some of them may already exist; I still included them as the proofs could be different, and I hope that an alternative proof may help the reader gain some new understandings to these results.

Definition and Assumption

We make the following definitions and assumptions throughout the report. Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional (smooth) manifold. We say
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth map if all orders of its derivatives exist and are continuous.
p\in M
is called a critical point if all partial derivatives of
f
vanish at
p
. Moreover, given
p\in M
and a chart
(U,(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n})
,
\text{Hess}f(p):=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{j}}|_{p}\right)_{i,j}
is the Hessian of
f
. We are interested in non-degenerate critical points which we define below.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p\in M
be a critical point of
f
.
p
is called non-degenerate if
\text{Hess}f(p)
is non-singular.

Let
A
be a bilinear functional. we say the index of
A
,
\text{ind}(A)
is the maximal dimension of subspaces on which it is negative definite, and the nullity of
A
is the dimension of its kernel. Thus, a critical point
p
of
f
s non-degenerate if the nullity of
\text{Hess}f(p)
is 0. Also, we define the index of a critical point
p
of
f
to be
\text{ind}(\text{Hess}f(p))
. If
\text{ind}(\text{Hess}f(p))=0
,
p
is called a local minimum of
f
, if
\text{ind}(\text{Hess}f(p))\in(0,n)
,
p
is called a saddle and if
\text{ind}(\text{Hess}f(p))=n
,
p
is called a local maximum of
f
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth.
f
is called a Morse function if all its critical points are non-degenerate.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold with Riemannian metric
g
. Consider a smooth function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
. We are interested in the gradient flow (GF) of
f
, which is a dynamical system described by

\gamma:(a,b)\to M,\frac{\partial{d\gamma}}{\partial{dt}}+\nabla f(\gamma(t))=0

(1)

where
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
and

is the gradient vector field on

\langle\nabla f(p),v\rangle=d_{p}f(v)\quad\quad\forall\,v\in T_{p}M,\forall\,p\in M.

(2)

We write
\gamma
for both the function and its image and
\gamma_{p}
to be the solution to (
1
) with initial value
p\in M
.

Remark.
When we discuss GF on
M
, we will assume that
M
has a Riemannian metric
g
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth and consider the system (
1
). Given any critical point
p\in M
, define

W^{s}(p):=\{x\in M:\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma_{x}(t)=p\}

(3)

to be the stable manifold of

and

W^{u}(p):=\{x\in M:\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{x}(t)=p\}

(4)

to be the unstable manifold of

.

4
GF of Morse-Smale and Morse–Bott Functions

In this section, we first focus on Morse-Smale functions, which is a special kind of Morse function whose stable and unstable manifolds intersect transversely. This transversality makes GF of Morse-Smale functions nice. Then we focus on Morse–Bott functions, which is a generalization of Morse function. Many properties of Morse function can be generalized to Morse–Bott functions.

Definition 4.1

(Morse-Smale function)
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth.
f
is called a Morse-Smale function if it is a Morse function and for any critical points
p,q
of it,
W^{u}(p)
and
W^{s}(q)
intersect transversely. In this case, denote
W(p,q):=W^{u}(p)\cap W^{s}(q)
.

Remark.
For any critical point
p\in M
,
W(p,p)=\{p\}
. Indeed, for any
x\in W(p,p)
, we would have

f(p)=\lim_{t\to\infty}f(\gamma_{x}(t))\leq f(x)\leq\lim_{t\to-\infty}f(\gamma_{x}(t))=f(p).

Since
\gamma_{x}
is a GF,
f(\gamma_{x}(\cdot))
is monotonic and thus it is constant. But this means
\nabla f(\gamma_{x}(t))=0
and
\gamma_{x}(t)=p
for all
t
. In general, given critical points
p,q
of
f
, since
W^{u}(p)
and
W^{s}(p)
intersects transversely,
W(p,q)
is a smooth manifold with

\dim W(p,q)=\dim(W^{u}(p))+\dim(W^{s}(q))-n

Given a Morse-Smale function

p,q
of it. For

between
f(p)
and
f(q)

W^{t}(p,q):=W(p,q)\cap f^{-1}(t)

The transversality condition implies that each

Definition 4.4
.

Let
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth and
N\subseteq M
be a critical submanifold of
f
. Let

be the normal bundle of

\text{Hess}_{x}^{N}(f):v_{x}(N)\times v_{x}(N)\to\mathbb{R}

\text{Hess}_{x}f

N
is called a degenerate critical manifold if
\text{Hess}_{x}^{N}(f)
is non-degenerate for each

and
f
is called Morse–Bott if its critical points are disjoint union of non-degenerate critical manifolds.

Remark.
Intuitively, a Morse–Bott function is a smooth function whose cross sections along each of its critical manifolds are Morse. We can also view a Morse–Bott function as a family of parametrized Morse functions. Thus, similar as in the Morse function case, we may define the index of a connected
N
to be
\text{ind}(\text{Hess}_{x}^{N}(f))
for any
x\in N
. Note that the index map is continuous, so it does not change value on
N
.

Many results about Morse functions can be generalized to Morse–Bott functions. The first one says that a Morse–Bott function looks like a quadratic function along the normal of its critical manifolds. The second is about the stable and unstable manifolds of a critical manifold of it; which “divides” the domain of the function in to different parts with respect to limiting behavior of GF. Conversely, one may ask what points in a critical manifold can be the limit of a non-stationary GF. This is characterized by Corollary
4.1
. Finally, we show that if a GF
\gamma
converges to some
p
in a critical manifold of a Morse–Bott function, then it is “biased” towards the normal space at
p
, in the sense that the tangential change of
\gamma
can be eventually bounded by the square of its normal change (Corollary
4.2
). As a result, if
p
is a local minimum then
\gamma
converges to
p

\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=:p\in f^{-1}(0)

\varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{|\mathcal{P}(\gamma(t)-p)|}{|\mathcal{Q}(\gamma(t)-p)|^{2}}<\infty,

where
\mathcal{P}
is the orthogonal projection onto
T_{p}f^{-1}(0)
and
\mathcal{Q}=I-\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

The result is local, so by Theorem 4.3, we may for simplicity assume that
f^{-1}(0)=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-s}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-s}
, where
s
is the codimension of
f^{-1}(0)
. We may also write a point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
(y,z)
, where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-s}
. With these notations, on some compact neighborhood
U
of
p
the ODE for the GF
\gamma=:(y,z)
takes the form

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=H(z(t))y(t)+g(y(t),z(t))

(8)

\displaystyle\dot{z}(t)

\displaystyle=m(y(t),z(t)),

such that for any
(y,z)\in U
,
H(z)
is negative definite,
H(z)
changes smoothly with respect to
z
,
g(0,z)=m(0,z)=0
, and
g(y,z)=O(|y|^{2}),\quad\quad m(y,z)=O(|y|^{2})
. Therefore, we can also write

\displaystyle\dot{y}(t)

\displaystyle=H(z(t))y(t)+O(|y(t)|^{2})

\displaystyle\dot{z}(t)

\displaystyle=O(|y(t)|^{2})

for
t
sufficiently large.

Denote
p=(0,z_{p})
. What we need to show now is just

\varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{|z(t)-z_{p}|}{|y(t)|^{2}}<\infty.

Set

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\frac{|\langle H(z)h,h\rangle|}{|h|^{2}}:h\in\mathbb{R}^{s}\setminus\{0\},(0,z)\in U\right\};

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\frac{|\langle H(z)h,h\rangle|}{|h|^{2}}:h\in\mathbb{R}^{s}\setminus\{0\},(0,z)\in U\right\};

Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=p
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}g(\gamma(t))=\lim_{t\to\infty}m(\gamma(t))=0
. Thus, for each
1\leq j\leq s
,
y_{j}(t)
decreases at exponential rate. In particular, there is some
T>0
such that for any
\zeta\geq t>T
and any
1\leq j\leq s
, we have
|y_{j}(t)|<1
,
\frac{\lambda}{2}|y_{j}(t)|\leq|\dot{y}_{j}(t)|\leq 2\lambda^{\prime}|y_{j}(t)|
, and thus

D_{1}|y_{j}(t)|e^{-\mu_{1}(\zeta-t)}\leq|y_{j}(\zeta)|\leq D_{2}|y_{j}(t)|e^{-\mu_{2}(\zeta-t)}

for some
D_{1},D_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
depending only on
T
.

These observations imply that
y_{j}
is decreasing and
y_{j}=0
or the sign of
y_{j}
does not change on
(T,\infty)
. Thus, for any
t>T
and any
1\leq j\leq s
,

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}y_{j}^{2}(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=\lfloor{t}\rfloor}^{\infty}y_{j}^{2}(k)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=\lfloor{t}\rfloor}^{\infty}y_{j}(k)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\int_{\lfloor{t}\rfloor}^{\infty}\dot{y}_{j}(\zeta)d\zeta\right)^{2}=\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}y_{j}^{2}(\lfloor{t}\rfloor),

where
\lfloor{t}\rfloor
denotes the largest integer smaller than
t
. Since

|y_{j}(t)\geq D_{1}|y_{j}(\lfloor{t}\rfloor)|e^{-\mu_{1}(t-\lfloor{t}\rfloor)}\geq D_{1}e^{-\mu_{1}}|y_{j}(\lfloor{t}\rfloor)|,

we have

|y_{j}(\lfloor{t}\rfloor)|\leq\frac{e^{\mu_{1}}}{D_{1}}|y_{j}(t)|.

Since
\dot{z}(t)=m(y(t),z(t))=O(|y(t)|^{2})
, there is some
C>0
such that
|\dot{z}(t)|\leq C|y(t)|^{2}
for
t>T
. It follows that for
t>T
,

\displaystyle|z(t)-z_{p}|\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{s}\int_{t}^{\infty}y_{j}^{2}(\zeta)d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{e^{\mu_{1}}}{D_{1}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{j}^{2}(t)\right).

Letting
t\to\infty
, we see that

\varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{|z(t)-z_{p}|}{|y(t)|^{2}}\leq C\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\frac{e^{\mu_{1}}}{D_{1}}<\infty.

∎

Remark.
In
[
Kur00
]
the authors show that if
f
is analytic, the secant line

\frac{\gamma(t)-\lim_{s\to\infty}\gamma(s)}{|\gamma(t)-\lim_{s\to\infty}\gamma(s)|}

of any convergent GF
\gamma
always has a limit. Thus, the result I give above further shows that the limit must be in the normal space of the critical manifold at the limit point.

5
Lojasiewicz inequality and Gradient Flow

In the previous two sections, we discussed some properties of Morse and Morse–Bott functions, as well as the GF of them. Such function has an important property that its critical points are non-degenerate (or at least, non-degenerate in the normal space of the critical manifold for Morse–Bott function). However, not every gradient vector field has this property. For example, consider
f(x,y,z)=x^{4}+y^{2}
. On a neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the GF converges to 0 and exhibits different behaviors from Morse or Morse–Bott functions.

Lojasiewicz inequality, which holds for (real) analytic functions, can be used to deal with functions with degenerate critical points. It is a generalization of

|f^{\prime}(q)|\geq C|f(q)-f(p)|^{\theta},\quad\quad\exists\,\theta\in[1/2,1)

(9)

for a non-constant analytic function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
near any one of its critical point
p
. Indeed, we have
f(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(q-p)^{i}
whenever
q
is in a neighborhood
U
of
p
. If
i_{0}\geq 2
is the smallest positive integer with
a_{i_{0}}\neq 0
then there are
A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}>0
such that for any
p\in U
,

\displaystyle A_{1}|p-q|^{i_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq|f(q)-f(p)|\leq A_{2}|p-q|^{i_{0}};

\displaystyle B_{1}|p-q|^{i_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq|f^{\prime}(q)-0|\leq B_{2}|p-q|^{i_{0}},

from which we can easily see that equation (
9
) holds.

Theorem 5.1

(Lojasiewicz inequality)
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a (real) analytic function. For any critical point
p
of
f

U
of
p
and constants

,
\theta\in[1/2,1)
such that

|\nabla f(q)|\geq C|f(q)-f(p)|^{\theta}

(10)

for any
q\in U

]
.

Remark.
In fact, the inequality (
10
) holds for generalized Morse–Bott functions at
p
as well, which is a smooth function

such that

(a)

p

(b)

There is some

I also did some characterization of analytic (and generalized Morse–Bott) functions, some of which are listed below. The first one is a summary of several results in
[
Now02
]
. I give a proof of it.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be an analytic function and
p
be a local minimum of
f
. For any
k
sufficiently large, there is an open
U
containing
p
such that for any GF
\gamma
with initial value in
U
,
\gamma
intersects the set
Z:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f(x)-f(p)=|x-p|^{2k}\}
at most once, and is transversal to it.

Proof.

Since the result is local, we may work on a neighborhood
U
of
p
on which
f\geq f(p)
. First we show that for sufficiently large
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
|x-p|
sufficiently small, when
x\in Z
we have

\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla(f(x)-|x-p|^{2k})>0.

By Lojasiewicz inequality, there is an open
U\subseteq V
containing
x
and some
C>0
,
\theta\in[1/2,1)
such that for any
x\in U
,
|\nabla f(x)|\geq C|f(x)-f(p)|^{\theta}
. Choose
k
so large that
2k\theta<2k-1
. Then there is some open
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
containing
p
such that for any
x\in U^{\prime}\cap Z
,

|\nabla f(x)|\geq C|f(x)-f(p)|^{\theta}=C|x-p|^{2k\theta}>2k|x-p|^{2k-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla(f(x)-|x-p|^{2k})

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x)\cdot[\nabla f(x)-2k|x-p|^{2k-2}(x-p)]

\displaystyle\geq\nabla f(x)\cdot[|\nabla f(x)|-2k|x-p|^{2k-1}]

\displaystyle>0,

proving our claim.

Now let
W\subseteq U^{\prime}
be a neighborhood of
p
such that the image of any GF
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
f
with initial value in
W
is contained in
U^{\prime}
. For any such GF
\gamma
, note that the map

\tau:t\mapsto(f(\gamma(t))-f(p))-|\gamma(t)-p|^{2k}

is smooth. Moreover, if
t\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\gamma(t)\in Z
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\tau}(t)

\displaystyle=[\nabla f(\gamma(t))-2k|\gamma(t)-p|^{2k-2}(\gamma(t)-p)]\cdot\dot{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=-\nabla(f(\cdot)-2k|(\cdot)-p|^{2k})|_{\gamma(t)}\cdot f(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle<0.

This implies that
\dot{\gamma}(t)
does not lie in
T_{\gamma(t)}Z
and
\tau(t)=0
for at most one
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In other words,
\gamma
intersects
Z
at most once and the intersection is transversal.
∎

Similar to the Morse–Bott setting, in analytic setting one may also ask what points in the critical set would be the limit of a GF. The following two results deal with this question. The first one, Lemma
5.1
can indeed be immediately obtained from Lojasiewicz inequality. Then I will use it to prove Corollary
5.3
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative analytic or generalized Morse–Bott function. Suppose that
f^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
. Then the set

\{p\in f^{-1}(0):\text{ there is a GF }\gamma\text{ with }\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma(t)=p\}

is dense in
f^{-1}(0)
.

Proof.

Assume this is not true. Then there would be an open ball
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
V\cap f^{-1}(0)
contains no limit of GF that belongs to
W^{s}(f^{-1}(0))
. On the other hand, since
f|_{V}:V\to\mathbb{R}
, the restriction of
f
to
V
, is a non-negative analytic function with
f|_{V}^{-1}(0)=V\cap f^{-1}(0)
, there is some
U\subseteq V
such that for any
x_{0}\in U
, the GF of
f|_{V}
with initial value
x_{0}
converges to a point in
f|_{V}^{-1}(0)
. This is a contradiction.
∎

In fact, more can be said about the correspondence of convergent GF and points in the set of local minima of
f
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative analytic or generalized Morse–Bott function. Suppose that
N:=f^{-1}(0)
is a non-empty critical submanifold and each
p\in N
has a neighborhood
U
satisfying

(a)

For any
x_{0}\in U
,
\gamma_{x_{0}}
converges to a point in
N
.

(b)

There are some
C,\alpha>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in U
, the curve length of
\gamma_{x_{0}}
is bounded (above) by
C\text{dist}\left({x_{0}},{N}\right)^{\alpha}
.

Then for any
p\in N
, there is a non-constant GF converging to
p
as
t\to\infty
. In particular, the result holds when
f
is analytic or generalized Morse–Bott.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, there is a sequence
\{p_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and a sequence of GF
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\gamma_{j}(t)=p_{j}\in N
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}p_{j}=p
. Choose a compact neighborhood
V\subseteq U
of
p
(so
\bar{V}\subseteq U
) For each
j
, there is a largest
t_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
x_{j}:=\gamma_{j}(t_{j})\in\partial V\cap\gamma_{j}
. Since
\partial V
is compact, the sequence
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
has an accumulation point
x
in
\bar{V}
, Moreover, hypothesis (b) implies that
x\notin N
. Since
x\in U
, the GF
\gamma_{x}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
converges to a point in
N
and its curve length is bounded by
C\text{dist}\left({x},{N}\right)^{\alpha}
.

Let
l_{j}
be the curve length of
\gamma_{j}[t_{j},\infty)
and
l
be the curve length of
\gamma_{x}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle u_{j}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{j}(t)=-\frac{\nabla f(u_{j}(t))\chi_{[0,l_{j})}}{|\nabla f(u_{j}(t))|},\quad u_{j}(0)=x_{j}

and similarly, define

\displaystyle u:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\dot{u}(t)=-\frac{\nabla f(u(t))\chi_{[0,l)}}{|\nabla f(u(t))|},\quad u(0)=x.

In the formulas above,
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of
E
, namely,
\chi_{E}(x)=1
for
x\in E
and
\chi_{E}(x)=0
otherwise. Also note that the
u_{j}
’s and
u
are exactly the trajectories of GFs.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Choose any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
|x-x_{k}|<\varepsilon
. There is some
T>0
such that for any
t>T
, we have
\text{dist}\left({u_{j}(t)},{N}\right)<\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}
and
\text{dist}\left({u(t)},{N}\right)<\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}
. Then the Grownwall’s inequality and hypothesis (b) yield

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{s\to\infty}u(s)-\lim_{s\to\infty}u_{k}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\lim_{s\to\infty}u(s)-u(t)|+|u(t)-u_{k}(t)|+|u_{k}(t)-p_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq C\text{dist}\left({u(t)},{N}\right)^{\alpha}+\exp(t)\varepsilon+C\text{dist}\left({u_{k}(t)},{N}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq(2C+\exp(t))\varepsilon.

Since
\{x\}\cup\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a subset of the bounded
\partial V
, it follows that
\sup\{l,l_{1},l_{2},...\}<\infty
; in particular, there is some
T^{\prime}>0
such that the
u_{j}
’s and
u
are all constant on
[T^{\prime},\infty)
. Thus, we actually have

\left|\lim_{s\to\infty}u(s)-\lim_{s\to\infty}u_{k}(s)\right|\leq(2C+\exp(T^{\prime}))\varepsilon

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we see that
\lim_{s\to\infty}u(s)=\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{k}=p
, which means
u
, and thus
\gamma_{x}
, converges to
p
. This shows the first part of the corollary.

Now suppose that
f
is analytic or generalized Morse–Bott. Let
p\in N
. By Theorem
5.2
, there is a bounded neighborhood
U
of
p
and some
\beta>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in U
,
\gamma_{x_{0}}
converges at rate
\beta
. Since
U
is bounded and
f
is smooth,
f
is Lipschitz on

, so there is some

with

for any

. It follows that the curve length of any such

, can be estimated by

where

is any point satisfying

(

∎

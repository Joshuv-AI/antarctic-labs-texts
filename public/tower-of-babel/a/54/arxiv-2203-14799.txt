Postselection-free controlled generation of a high-dimensional orbital-angular-momentum entangled state
Suman Karan, Radhika Prasad, Anand K. Jha

Abstract
High-dimensional entangled states in orbital angular momentum (OAM) basis offer several unique advantages for quantum information applications. However, for the optimal performance of a given application, one requires a generation technique for OAM entangled states that is completely postselection-free and fully controllable. Nonetheless, despite several efforts in the past, no such technique currently exists. In this article, we propose just such a technique and experimentally demonstrate postselection-free generation of up to about 150-dimensional OAM entangled states. We report the generation accuracy, which is a measure of the control, to be more than 98 % \% for states with Gaussian and triangular OAM Schmidt spectra and up to 90 % \% for the maximally-entangled OAM states, which have rectangular spectra.



I
Introduction

A Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) mode
LG^{l}_{p}(\rho,\phi)
is characterized by indices
l
and
p
, referred to as the orbital angular momentum (OAM) mode index and the radial mode index, respectively. A photon in an LG mode with index
l
carries OAM of
l\hbar
, where
l
is an integer
Allen et al. 1992
. Thus the OAM of a photon provides a discrete infinite-dimensional basis
Yao and Padgett 2011
as opposed to the two-dimensional polarization basis
Forbes and Nape 2019
. The use of high-dimensional entangled states provides higher error tolerance for quantum-key distribution
Bechmann-Pasquinucci and Peres 2000
;
Nikolopoulos et al. 2006
, higher security against eavesdropper attacks in quantum cryptographic schemes
Cerf et al. 2002
, increased information capacity
Wang et al. 2012
;
Bozinovic et al. 2013
, enhanced robustness of communication protocols in noisy environment
Ecker et al. 2019
;
Zhu et al. 2021
, and stronger violations of generalized Bell’s inequalities
Leach et al. 2009
;
Dada et al. 2011
. High-dimensional entangled states also have important implications for dense coding
Wang et al. 2017
;
Hu et al. 2018
, quantum teleportation
Luo et al. 2019
;
Hu et al. 2020
, entanglement swapping
Zhang et al. 2017
;
Takeda et al. 2015
, and supersensitive measurements
Jha et al. 2011a
;
Chen et al. 2014
;
Asban et al. 2019
.

In the past, there have been several efforts at producing high-dimensional OAM-entangled states using spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC), in which a pump photon of higher frequency splits into two photons of lower frequencies, called the signal and idler. It is known that for the optimal performance of a quantum information protocol
Bouchard et al. 2018
;
Ding et al. 2017
, one needs to generate OAM entangled states in a completely postselection-free and controlled manner. However, all the past efforts have only yielded generation techniques that are either postselection-free but not fully controllable
Pires et al. 2010
;
Kulkarni et al. 2018
;
Torres et al. 2003
;
Kulkarni et al. 2017a
or fully controllable but not postselection-free
Leach et al. 2009
;
Dada et al. 2011
;
Wang et al. 2017
;
Hu et al. 2018
;
Luo et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2015
;
Hu et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2017
;
Takeda et al. 2015
;
Jha et al. 2011a
;
Vaziri et al. 2003
. More recently, there have been experimental
Liu et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Kovlakov et al. 2018
and theoretical
Bornman et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2022
;
Miatto et al. 2012
efforts at developing generation techniques that are fully controllable but postselection-free only with respect to
p=0
mode detection. These techniques involve single-mode-fiber based OAM detection system, which only detects photons with radial mode index
p=0

Qassim et al. 2014
;
Agrawal
. However, it is known that the proportion of
p\neq 0
radial modes in the OAM entangled state generated by SPDC is typically much larger than the
p=0
mode
Miatto et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2014
. Hence, while these techniques
Liu et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Kovlakov et al. 2018
;
Bornman et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2022
;
Miatto et al. 2012
can have limited use in some applications, they are not postselection-free. Thus, to the best of our knowledge, there is no existing technique for a truly postselection-free generation of high-dimensional OAM entangled states with full control. In this article, we experimentally demonstrate generation of truly postselection-free OAM entangled states with control.

II
Theory

The two-photon state generated by type-I SPDC in the transverse momentum basis can be written as
Karan et al. 2020
;
Torres et al. 2003
;
Walborn et al. 2010

\displaystyle|\psi_{\rm tp}\rangle=\iint V({\bf q}_{s},{\bf q}_{i})\Phi({\bf q}_{s},{\bf q}_{i})|{\bf q}_{s}\rangle_{s}|{\bf q}_{i}\rangle_{i}d^{2}{\bf q}_{s}d^{2}{\bf q}_{i},

(1)

where
p
,
s
and
i
denote pump, signal and idler, respectively.
V({\bf q}_{s},{\bf q}_{i})
represents the pump field amplitude,
\Phi({\bf q}_{s},{\bf q}_{i})
represents the phase matching function, and
|{\bf q}_{s}\rangle_{s}
and
|{\bf q}_{i}\rangle_{i}
denote the state vectors in the transverse momentum basis of the signal and idler, respectively. In the LG basis, we can write
|\psi_{\rm tp}\rangle
as

\displaystyle\qquad|\psi_{\rm tp}\rangle=\sum_{l_{s},p_{s}}\sum_{l_{i},p_{i}}C^{l_{s},p_{s}}_{l_{i},p_{i}}|l_{s},p_{s}\rangle_{s}|l_{i},p_{i}\rangle_{i},

(2)

where
l_{p}
,
l_{s}
and
l_{i}
are the OAM mode indices of the pump, signal, and idler, respectively
Mair et al. 2001
.
|l_{s},p_{s}\rangle_{s}
is the state of the signal photon with indices
l_{s}
and
p_{s}
, etc. Thus, using Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), we write
C^{l_{s},p_{s}}_{l_{i},p_{i}}
as
Miatto et al. 2011
;
Yao 2011
:

C^{l_{s},p_{s}}_{l_{i},p_{i}}=A\int\!\!\!\!\int d^{2}{\bm{q}}_{s}d^{2}{\bm{q}}_{i}V({\bm{q}}_{s},{\bm{q}}_{i})\Phi({\bm{q}}_{s},{\bm{q}}_{i})\\
\times\left[LG^{l_{s}}_{p_{s}}({\bm{q}}_{s})\right]^{*}\left[LG^{l_{i}}_{p_{i}}({\bm{q}}_{i})\right]^{*},

(3)

where
LG^{l}_{p}({\bm{q}})=\langle{\bm{q}}|l,p\rangle
is the momentum basis representation of the LG mode
Torres et al. 2003
. Now, using the cylindrical polar coordinates,
{\bm{q}}_{s}=(q_{sx},q_{sy})=(\rho_{s}{\rm cos}\phi_{s},\rho_{s}{\rm sin}\phi_{s})
,
{\bm{q}}_{i}=(q_{ix},q_{iy})=(\rho_{i}{\rm cos}\phi_{i},\rho_{i}{\rm sin}\phi_{i})
,
d^{2}{\bm{q}}_{s}=\rho_{s}d\rho_{s}d\phi_{s}
, and
d^{2}{\bm{q}}_{i}=\rho_{i}d\rho_{i}d\phi_{i}

C^{l_{s},p_{s}}_{l_{i},p_{i}}
as

\displaystyle C^{l_{s},p_{s}}_{l_{i},p_{i}}

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\int\!\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\rho_{s}\rho_{i}d\rho_{s}d\rho_{i}d\phi_{s}d\phi_{i}V(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})

\displaystyle\times\Phi(

\displaystyle\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})\left[LG^{l_{s}}_{p_{s}}(\rho_{s},\phi_{s})\right]^{*}\left[LG^{l_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\rho_{i},\phi_{i})\right]^{*}.

(4)

P^{l_{s}}_{l_{i}}
is the probability of detecting the signal and idler photons with OAM
l_{s}\hbar
and
l_{i}\hbar
, and it is given by

\displaystyle P^{l_{s}}_{l_{i}}=\sum^{\infty}_{p_{s}=0}\sum^{\infty}_{p_{i}=0}|C^{l_{s},p_{s}}_{l_{i},p_{i}}|^{2}.

(5)

When
l_{p}=0
, conservation of OAM leads to
l_{s}=-l_{i}

Mair et al. 2001
, and in this case, Eq. (
2
) takes the following Schmidt decomposed form:

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle_{\rm tp}=\sum_{l}\sqrt{S_{l}}|l\rangle_{s}|-l\rangle_{i}.

(6)

Here
S_{l}=P^{l}_{-l}
is called the OAM Schmidt spectrum. Using Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
), and employing the identity
\sum^{\infty}_{p=0}(LG)^{l}_{p}(\rho)(LG)^{*l}_{p}(\rho^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\pi}\delta(\rho^{2}-\rho^{\prime 2})

Jha et al. 2011b
, we write
S_{l}
as (for a more detailed derivation, see
Kulkarni et al. 2018
):

S_{l}=P^{l}_{-l}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{s}\rho_{i}\Bigg|\int\!\!\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}V(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})\\
\times\Phi(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})e^{-il(\phi_{s}-\phi_{i})}d\phi_{s}d\phi_{i}\Bigg|^{2}d\rho_{s}d\rho_{i}.

(7)

From Eq. (
7
), we find that
S_{l}
is determined by the pump field amplitude
V(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
and the phase-matching function
\Phi(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
. Therefore, by selecting an appropriate
V(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
and
\Phi(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
, one can generate any target OAM Schmidt spectrum. However, solving Eq. (
7
) for finding the analytical expressions for
V(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
and
\Phi(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
for a target OAM Schmidt spectrum

is mathematically a very difficult problem. So, our technique for finding the required
V(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
and
\Phi(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})
for a target spectrum is based on optimization and feedback.

We note that
S_{l}
expressed in Eq. (
7

in Eq. (
5
) has been defined as a sum over all the radial modes of the signal and idler photons. Thus
S_{l}
is the true OAM Schmidt spectrum. This is in contrast with several recent techniques
Liu et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Kovlakov et al. 2018
;
Bornman et al. 2021
;
Xu et al. 2022
;
Miatto et al. 2012
, in which the spectrum is taken as the probability of only the
p=0
mode. We find that the spectral content observed with a
p=0
mode detector is only a small fraction of the true spectrum (see Appendix
A
for a detailed study regarding this.) Thus our generation technique, which is based on measuring the
S_{l}
in Eq. (
7
) is truly postselection-free.

is the associated Laguerre polynomial,

, and

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho_{s},\rho_{i},\phi_{s},\phi_{i})=\mbox{sinc}\left(\frac{\Delta k_{z}L}{2}\right)\exp\left[i\frac{\Delta k_{z}L}{2}\right],

is called the phase mismatch parameter.

. For a
2D+1

Glantz and Slinker 2001

\displaystyle R^{2}=\left[1-\dfrac{\sum_{l=-D}^{+D}\left(S_{l}^{\rm t}-S_{l}^{\rm o}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{l=-D}^{+D}\left(S_{l}^{\rm t}-\langle S_{l}^{\rm t}\rangle\right)^{2}}\right]\times 100\%,

\displaystyle{\rm max}\{R^{2}\}\equiv G

III
Experiments

The experimental setup is depicted in Fig.
3
. We use a 100-mW Toptica TopMode ultraviolet (UV) continuous wave (CW) laser of wavelength
\lambda_{p}=405
nm that is spatially filtered and incident on a Holoeye Pluto-2-UV-099 spatial light modulator (SLM). The SLM is used for generating the pump field

of Eq. (
8
) in the form of a superposition of

radial modes using the method described by Arrizon et al.

. For

, we use modes from

to

with pump beam waist

. The pump field is made incident on a
\beta
-barium borate (BBO) crystal of transverse dimensions 10 mm
\times

mm. The crystal is kept on a goniometer to change the phase matching angle

. A Dichroic mirror (DM) is positioned just after the crystal to block UV while allowing the down-converted photon pairs to pass through. These photon pairs are incident on a Mach-Zehnder type interferometer

, and the interferogram output is captured using an Andor iXon Ultra-897 electron-multiplied charged coupled device (EMCCD) camera with 512
\times
512 pixel grids having pixel pitch of
16\times 16~\mu{\rm m}^{2}
. Lenses with focal lengths

, and
f_{2}=200~{\rm mm}
are placed in a

configuration to image the crystal plane with a magnification of four. The Fourier transform of this image plane is obtained at the camera plane using another lens of focal length
f_{3}=300
mm. For capturing the interferograms, the camera acquisition time is kept at
20
seconds. An interference filter (IF) of central wavelength 810 nm and bandwidth 10 nm is placed in front of the camera. For a target spectrum
S^{t}_{l}
, we use the optimized complex coefficients

from our numerical simulations as the starting point and produce the pump field

using the SLM. We then experimentally observe the generated spectrum

with EMCCD using the two-shot technique described in Ref.

and calculate

Nevertheless, a feedback mechanism with automated controls can make the optimization much more efficient. Therefore, with a nonlinear crystal with larger transverse size and an automated feedback mechanism, a much higher generation accuracy, even for a sharp-edged spectrum, should be easily achievable with our technique.

IV
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have proposed and demonstrated an experimental technique based on SPDC for postselection-free controlled generation of up to about 150-dimensional OAM entangled states. Ours is the first truly postselection-free technique for generating OAM-entangled states with full control. We note that the main limitation of our technique is that it works only for the OAM-entangled states in the Schmidt-decomposed form. This is due to the fact that currently there is no efficient detector for measuring a general OAM-entangled state. With the advent of such a detector, our technique could in principle be extended to general OAM-entangled states. Furthermore, it has been shown that for several quantum information applications non-maximally entangled states are preferred over the maximally entangled states
(
Bandyopadhyay and Ghosh 2012
;
Acín et al. 2012
;
Alimuddin et al. 2023
)
. Thus, our work can have important implications for high-dimensional quantum information.

\displaystyle\Delta k_{z}=k_{sz}+k_{iz}-k_{pz},

\displaystyle k_{pz}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{p}q_{px}+\eta_{p}K_{p0}-\frac{\left[\beta_{p}^{2}q^{2}_{px}+\gamma_{p}^{2}q^{2}_{py}\right]}{2\eta_{p}K_{p0}},

\displaystyle k_{sz}

\displaystyle=n_{so}K_{s0}-\frac{1}{2n_{so}K_{s0}}(q_{sx}^{2}+q_{sy}^{2}),

\displaystyle k_{iz}

\displaystyle=n_{io}K_{i0}-\frac{1}{2n_{io}K_{i0}}(q_{ix}^{2}+q_{iy}^{2}),

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}=\frac{(n^{2}_{po}-n^{2}_{pe})\sin\theta_{p}\cos\theta_{p}}{n^{2}_{po}\sin^{2}\theta_{p}+n^{2}_{pe}\cos^{2}\theta_{p}},

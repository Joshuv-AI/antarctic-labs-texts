The stability of a biadditive functional equation via a new direct method
Gang Lu ∗, Yang Liu, Yuanfeng Jin ∗, Qi Liu

Abstract
. Addressing stability in functional equations is a critical task with broad implications across mathematics and its applications. In this paper, we present a novel direct method for proving the stability of the following equation, f ⁡ ( x , y ) = α ​ f ​ ( f 1 ​ ( x , y ) ) + β ​ f ​ ( f 2 ​ ( x , y ) ) \displaystyle f(x,y)=\alpha f(f_{1}(x,y))+\beta f(f_{2}(x,y)) subjecting to certain constraints on the constants α \alpha and β \beta , as well as the functions f 1 ​ ( x , y ) f_{1}(x,y) and f 2 ​ ( x , y ) f_{2}(x,y) . We introduce the direct method to prove the stability of the following functional equation and inequality, ‖ f ⁡ ( x + y , z − w ) + f ⁡ ( x − y , z + w ) − 2 ​ f ​ ( x , z ) + 2 ​ f ​ ( y , w ) ‖ ≤ ‖ x ‖ p ​ ‖ y ‖ p ​ ‖ z ‖ p ​ ‖ w ‖ p ; \displaystyle\|f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\|\leq\|x\|^{p}\|y\|^{p}\|z\|^{p}\|w\|^{p}; ‖ f ⁡ ( x + y , z − w ) + a ​ f ​ ( x − y a , z + w ) − 2 ​ f ​ ( x , z ) + 2 ​ f ​ ( y , w ) ‖ ≤ ‖ ρ ⁡ ( f ⁡ ( x + y , z − w ) + f ⁡ ( x − y , z + w ) − 2 ​ f ​ ( x , z ) + 2 ​ f ​ ( y , w ) ) ‖ . \displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|f\left(x+y,z-w\right)+af\left(\frac{x-y}{a},z+w\right)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\|\\ \;&\leq\left\|\rho\left(f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\right)\right\|.\end{split} This technique efficiently confirms stability, outperforming traditional proof methods in simplicity and efficacy. We introduce the a direct method to obtain the Hyers-Ulam stability result for a two-variable function equations in Banach spaces.

Throughout the paper, let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of all positive integers,
\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the set of all positive reals,
Y^{X\times X}
be the family of all mappings from
X\times X
to
Y
. The main theme of the present work is to analyze the stability of the solution of the following functional equation

\displaystyle\|f(x,y)-\alpha f(f_{1}(x,y))-\beta f(f_{2}(x,y))\|\leq\mu(x,y),

(1.1)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are real numbers, and mappings
f_{1},f_{2}:X\times X\rightarrow X,\mu:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are arbitrarily given. In fact, the above inequality is still more general form.
In Section 2, we give some improvements to the approximations known so far. In Section 3, we find several applications of the stability results.
For the sake of simplicity we provide our results for functions with values in Banach spaces, but with some small additional assumptions they can be formulated in complete metric spaces or other spaces.

2.
Main results

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a linear normed space and
Y
is a Banach space.
Let
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
be a mapping satisfying

\displaystyle\|f(x,y)-\alpha f(f_{1}(x,y))-\beta f(f_{2}(x,y))\|\leq\mu(x,y)

(2.1)

for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
. If
\alpha
and
\beta
are real numbers, and the mappings
f_{1},f_{2}:X\times X\rightarrow X\times X,\mu:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are arbitrarily given,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{n}\mu)(x,y)=:\mu^{*}(x,y)<\infty

holds and
\Lambda
is a linear operator defined by

\displaystyle(\Lambda\delta)(x,y):=|A|\delta(f_{1}(x,y))+|B|\delta(f_{2}(x,y))

for
\delta:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
and
(x,y)\in X\times X
, then there exists a unique determined mapping
K:X\times X\rightarrow Y

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\alpha K(f_{1}(x,y))+\beta K(f_{2}(x,y)),(x,y)\in X\times X,

such that

\displaystyle\|f(x,y)-K(x,y)\|\leq\mu^{*}(x,y)

(2.2)

for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
.

Proof.

Let
T:Y^{X\times X}\rightarrow Y^{X\times X}

be an operator satisfying
(Tf)(x,y))=\alpha f(f_{1}(x,y))+\beta f(f_{2}(x,y))
in (
2.1
). Then we get

\displaystyle\|f(x,y)-(Tf)(x,y))\|\leq\mu(x,y).

(2.3)

At same time, we can see

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|(T\xi)(x,y)-(T\zeta)(x,y)\|\\
\;&\leq|\alpha|\|\xi(f_{1}(x,y))-\zeta(f_{1}(x,y))\|+|\beta|\|\xi(f_{2}(x,y))-\zeta(f_{2}(x,y))\|\end{split}

(2.4)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in Y^{X\times X},(x,y)\in X\times X
.
First, we get by induction that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\|(T^{n}f)(x,y))-(T^{n+1}f)(x,y)\|\leq(\Lambda^{n}\mu)(x,y),(x,y)\in X\times X.

(2.5)

Obviously, from (
2.3
), the case
n=0
holds. Now fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that the inequality (
2.5
) is valid. Then, using (
2.4
), for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|(T^{n+1}f)(x,y)-(T^{n+2}f)(x,y)\|\\
\;&\leq|\alpha|\|(T^{n}f)(f_{1}(x,y))-(T^{n+1}f)(f_{1}(x,y))\|+|\beta|\|(T^{n}f)(f_{2}(x,y))-(T^{n+1}f)(f_{2}(x,y))\|\\
\;&\leq|\alpha|(\Lambda^{n}\mu)(f_{1}(x,y))+|\beta|(\Lambda^{n}\mu)(f_{2}(x,y))=(\Lambda^{n+1}\mu)(x,y).\end{split}

(2.6)

Thus, we complete the proof of (
2.5
). For
n,k\in\mathcal{N},k>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|(T^{n}f)(x,y)-(T^{n+k}f)(x,y)\|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\|(T^{n}f)(x,y)-(T^{n+i+1}f)(x,y)\|\\
\;&\leq\sum_{i=n}^{n+k-1}(\Lambda^{i}\mu)(x,y)\leq\mu^{*}(x,y),(x,y)\in X\times X.\end{split}

(2.7)

From the convergence of the series
\sum(\Lambda^{n}\mu)(x,y)
, for every
(x,y)\in X\times X
,
\{(T^{n}f)(x,y)\}_{n\in\mathcal{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence. Since
Y
is a Banach space, we can define
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(T^{n}f)(x,y):=\psi(x,y)
. Taking
n=0
and
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.7
), we know that (
2.2
) holds, and

\displaystyle\|(T\psi)(x,y)-(T^{n+1}f)(x,y)\|\leq(\Lambda^{n+1})\mu(x,y),n\in\mathcal{N},(x,y)\in X\times X,

and thus

\displaystyle(T\psi)(x,y)=lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(T^{n+1}\psi)(x,y)=\psi(x,y),(x,y)\in X\times X.

In order to prove the uniqueness of
\psi
, suppose that
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in Y^{X\times X}
are two fixed points of
T
with
\|\psi_{i}(x,y)-f(x,y)\|\leq\mu^{*}(x,y)
for every
(x,y)\in X\times X,i=1,2.
We can easily show that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{1}(x,y)-\psi_{2}(x,y)\|=\|(T^{m}\psi_{1})(x,y)-(T^{m}\psi_{2})(x,y)\|\leq 2\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}(\Lambda^{i}\mu)(x,y),x\in X.

Thus
\psi_{1}(x,y)=\psi_{2}(x,y)
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We can easily prove

\displaystyle\left\|f(x,y)-\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(f(f_{i}(x,y)))\right\|\leq\mu(x)

for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
. Thus there exists a unique determined mapping
K:X\rightarrow Y

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}K(f_{i}(x,y)),(x,y)\in X\times X,

such that

\displaystyle\|f(x,y)-K(x,y)\|\leq\mu^{*}(x,y)

for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
.

3.
Application

In this section, we provide an example of how the various functional equations can be applied in practice.

3.1.
General two-variable functional mapping

Let us recall that a mapping
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
is biadditive provided

f(x+y,w)=f(x,w)+f(y,w),\quad f(x,z+w)=f(x,z)+f(x,w)

for all
x,y,z,w\in X
.
El-Fassi
[
13
]
investigated the stability of the following functional equation

\displaystyle f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)=2f(x,z)-2f(y,w).

The problem is of profound significance for the stability of functional equations.

Next, the type of the following function equation can be demonstrated by using a different method.

\displaystyle f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)=2f(x,z)-2f(y,w).

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a linear normed space and
Y
is a Banach space. Let
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
be a mapping satisfying

\displaystyle\|f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\|\leq\|x\|^{p}\|y\|^{p}\|z\|^{p}\|w\|^{p},

(3.1)

with
p>3
, for all
x,y,z,w\in X
. If
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
is symmetric (i.e.,
f(x,y)=f(y,x)
for
x,y\in X
),
then there is a biadditive mapping
K
such that

\displaystyle\|K(x,y)-f(x,y)\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)}{\left(\frac{12}{25}\right)^{p}\|x\|^{2p}\|y\|^{2p}}

for all
(x,y)\in X\times X
.

Proof.

From the inequality (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|f(x,y)+f\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)+2f\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)-2f\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)\right\|\leq\left(\frac{12}{25}\right)^{p}\|x\|^{2p}\|y\|^{2p}

(3.2)

for all
x,y\in X
.

Consider
T:Y^{X\times X}\rightarrow Y^{X\times X}
and
\mu:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
given as

\displaystyle T\xi(x,y)=-\xi\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)+2\xi\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)-2\xi\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)

for all
x,y\in X,\xi\in Y^{X\times X}

and

\mu(x,y)={\left(\frac{12}{25}\right)^{p}\|x\|^{2p}\|y\|^{2p}},\quad x\in X.

The inequality (
3.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\|Tf(x,y)-f(x,y)\|\leq\mu(x,y),\forall x,y\in X.

For every
g,h\in Y^{X\times X}
,
x,y\in X
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|Tg(x,y)-Th(x,y)\|\\
\;&=\left\|2g\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)-g\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)-2g\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)\right.\\
\;&\left.-2h\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)+h\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)+2h\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)\right\|\\
\;&\leq 2\left\|g\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)-h\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)\right\|\\
\;&+\left\|g\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)-h\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)\right\|+2\left\|g\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)-h\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)\right\|.\end{split}

So
T
satisfies the inequality (
2.4
)
with
f_{1}(x,y)=\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right);f_{2}(x,y)=\left(-\frac{x}{5},3y\right);f_{3}(x,y)=\left(-\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)
and
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{3}=2,\alpha_{2}=1
.
Next, we define the operator
\Lambda:\mathds{R}_{+}^{X}\rightarrow\mathds{R}_{+}^{X}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda\eta(x,y)=2\eta\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)+\eta\left(-\frac{x}{5},3y\right)+2\eta\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)

for all
x,y\in X
. In particular,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;\Lambda\mu(x,y)&=2\mu\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)+\mu\left(-\frac{x}{5},3y\right)+2\mu\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right)\\
\;&=\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)\mu(x).\end{split}

Since
\Lambda
is linear, we can get

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}\mu(x)=\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)^{n}\mu(x),x\in X,n\in\mathds{N}_{0}.

Since
\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)<1
,
the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Lambda^{n}\mu(x)
is convergent for every
x\in X
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\mu^{*}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Lambda^{n}\mu(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)^{n}\mu(x)\\
\;&=\frac{1}{1-\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)}\mu(x)\\
\;&=\frac{1}{1-\left(3\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^{2p}+2\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{2p}\right)}{\left(\frac{12}{25}\right)^{p}\|x\|^{2p}\|y\|^{2p}},x\in X.\end{split}

By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a mapping
K:X\rightarrow Y
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&K(x,y)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}T^{n}f(x,y)\\
\;&K(x,y)=-K\left(\frac{-x}{5},3y\right)+2K\left(\frac{3x}{5},y\right)-2K\left(\frac{2x}{5},2y\right).\end{split}

By
[
13
, Property 2.2]
,
K
satisfies the biadditive functional equation.
∎

3.2.
The functional inequality

In this subsection, we solve and investigate the biadditive
\rho
-functional inequality in normed spaces, which as the following form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|f\left(x+y,z-w\right)+af\left(\frac{x-y}{a},z+w\right)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\|\\
\;&\leq\left\|\rho\left(f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\right)\right\|.\end{split}

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
X
is a linear normed space,
Y
is a Banach space, and
a\in\mathds{R}
,
a\neq 1
. Let
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
be a mapping satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|f\left(x+y,z-w\right)+af\left(\frac{x-y}{a},z+w\right)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\|\\
\;&\leq\left\|\rho\left(f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\right)\right\|.\end{split}

(3.3)

for all
x,y,z,w\in X
. If
\rho<\frac{2}{5}
, then
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
is biadditive.

Proof.

Suppose that
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
satisfies (
3.3
). Letting
y=x=w=z=0
in (
3.3
), we can get

f(0,0)=0
. Similarly, assume that
x=y=w=0
in (
3.3
),
f(0,x)=0
can be obtained. By the same way, we can have
f(x,0)=0
.

Next, if
y=x,w=0
in (
3.3
), then

\|f(2x,z)-2f(x,z)\|\leq|\rho|\|f(2x,z)-2f(x,z)\|

for all
x,z\in X
. Thus
f(2x,z)=2f(x,z)
. The same technique is applied to obtain

af\left(\frac{x}{a},z\right)=f(x,z)
.

Letting
w=0
in (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|f(x+y,z)+f(x-y,z)-2f(x,z)\|\\
\;&=\left\|f\left(x+y,z\right)+af\left(\frac{x-y}{a},z\right)-2f\left(x,z\right)\right\|\\
\;&\leq|\rho|\|f(x+y,z)+f(x-y,z)-2f(x,z)\|,\end{split}

ans so
f(x+y,z)+f(x-y,z)=2f(x,z)
for all
x,y,z\in X
. Next, we can obtain that

f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
is additive in the second variable. Therefore
f(x,y)
is biadditive.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
X
is a linear normed space,
Y
is a Banach space, and
a,\rho\in\mathds{R}
with
2\rho\leq|1+a|
. Let
f:X\times X\rightarrow Y
be a mapping satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\left\|f\left(x+y,z-w\right)+af\left(\frac{x-y}{a},z+w\right)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\right\|\\
\;&\leq\left\|\rho\left(f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\right)\right\|\\
\;&+\left(\|x\|^{r}+\|y\|^{r}+\|z\|^{r}+\|w\|^{r}\right),\end{split}

(3.4)

for all
x,y,z,w\in X
. If
r>2
, then there exists a unique biadditive mapping
K:X\rightarrow Y
such that

\displaystyle\|f(x,z)-K(x,z)\|\leq\frac{2^{3+r}}{2^{r}-1}\frac{\|x/2\|^{r}+\|z/2\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|},x,z\in X.

Proof.

Letting
x=y=z=w=0
in (
3.4
), we get
f(0,0)=0
.
Letting
y=x,w=-z
in (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|f(2x,2z)+af(0,0)-2f(x,z)+2f(x,-z)\|\\
\;&\leq\|\rho(f(2x,2z)+f(0,0)-2f(x,z)+2f(x,-z))\|+2\|x\|^{r}+2\|z\|^{r},\end{split}

(3.5)

for all
x,z\in X
.

It follows from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\|f(x,z)-2f(x/2,z/2)+2f(x/2,-z/2)\|\leq\frac{2\|x/2\|^{r}+2\|z/2\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|}.

(3.6)

Consider
T:Y^{X}\rightarrow Y^{X}
and
\mu:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
given as

\displaystyle Tf(x,z)=2f\left(x/2,z/2\right)-2f\left(x/2,-z/2\right)

(3.7)

for all
x,z\in X,f\in Y^{X\times X}

and

\mu(x,z)=\frac{2\|x/2\|^{r}+2\|z/2\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|},\quad x,z\in X.

The inequality (
3.6
) becomes

\displaystyle\|(Tf)(x,z)-f(x,z)\|\leq\mu(x,z),\forall x,z\in X.

(3.8)

For every
g,h\in Y^{X\times X}
and
x,z\in X
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|Tg(x,z)-Th(x,z)\|\\
\;&=\left\|2g\left(x/2,z/2\right)-2g\left(x/2,-z/2\right)-2h\left(x/2,z/2\right)+2h\left(x/2,-z/2\right)\right\|\\
\;&\leq 2\left\|g\left(x/2,z/2\right)-h\left(x/2,z/2\right)\right\|+2\left\|g\left(x/2,-z/2\right)-h\left(x/2,-z/2\right)\right\|.\end{split}

(3.9)

Let
f_{1}(x,z)=(x/2,z/2),f_{2}(x,z)=(x/2,-z/2)
and
a_{1}(x)=a_{2}(x)=2
in Remak
2.2
.
The operator
\Lambda:\mathds{R}_{+}^{X\times X}\rightarrow\mathds{R}_{+}^{X\times X}
is given by

\displaystyle\Lambda\eta(x,z)=2\eta\left(x/2,z/2\right)+2\eta\left(x/2,-z/2\right)

(3.10)

for all
x,z\in X
. In particular,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\Lambda\mu(x,z)=2\mu\left(x/2,z/2\right)+2\mu\left(x/2,-z/2\right)\\
\;&=2\frac{2\|x/4\|^{r}+2\|z/4\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|}+2\frac{2\|x/4\|^{r}+2\|z/4|^{r}}{1-|\rho|}\\
\;&=\left(\frac{4}{2^{r}}\right)\mu(x).\end{split}

(3.11)

Since
\Lambda
is linear, we can get

\displaystyle\Lambda^{n}\mu(x,z)=\left(\frac{4}{2^{r}}\right)^{n}\mu(x,z),x,z\in X,n\in\mathds{N}_{0}.

(3.12)

Since
\left(\frac{4}{2^{r}}\right)<1
,
the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Lambda^{n}\mu(x)
is convergent for every
x\in X
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\mu^{*}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Lambda^{n}\mu(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{4}{2^{r}}\right)^{n}\mu(x)\\
\;&=\frac{4}{1-\frac{1}{2^{r}}}\mu(x,z)\\
\;&=\frac{2^{3+r}}{2^{r}-1}\frac{\|x/2\|^{r}+\|z/2\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|},x,z\in X.\end{split}

By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a mapping
K:X\rightarrow Y
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&K(x,z)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}T^{n}f(x,z)\\
\;&K(x,z)=2K\left(\frac{x}{2},\frac{z}{2}\right)-2K\left(\frac{x}{2},-\frac{z}{2}\right)\\
\;&\|f(x,z)-K(x,z)\|\leq\frac{4}{1-\frac{1}{2^{r}}}\mu(x,z)\\
\;&=\frac{2^{3+r}}{2^{r}-1}\frac{\|x/2\|^{r}+\|z/2\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|},x,z\in X.\end{split}

Next, we prove that
K
satisfies the biadditive functional equation.
Considering (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|f(x+y)+f(x-y)-Af\left(ax\right)-Bf\left(by\right)-Df\left(-y\right)\right\|\leq\theta\left(\left\|x\right\|^{p}+\left\|y\right\|^{p}\right)

(3.13)

for all
x,y\in X
.
Then, from the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|f(x+y,z-w)+f(x-y,z+w)-2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)\|\\
\;&\leq 2\left(\left\|x\right\|^{r}+\left\|z\right\|^{r}\right)+\|y\|^{r}+\|w\|^{r},x,y,z,w\in X.\end{split}

(3.14)

Hence

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|(Tf)(x+y,z-w)+(Tf)(x-y,z+w)-2(Tf)(x,z)+2(Tf)(y,w)\|\\
\;&\leq\frac{4}{2^{r}}\frac{2\|x\|^{r}+\|y\|^{r}+2\|z\|^{r}+\|w\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|}\end{split}

(3.15)

and so

\displaystyle\begin{split}\;&\|J^{n}f(x+y,z-w)+J^{n}f(x-y,z+w)-2J^{n}f(x,z)+2J^{n}f(y,w)\|\\
\;&\leq(\frac{4}{2^{r}})^{n}\frac{2\|x\|^{r}+\|y\|^{r}+2\|z\|^{r}+\|w\|^{r}}{1-|\rho|}\end{split}

(3.16)

for all
n\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
x,y\in X
. Letting

n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle K(x+y,z-w)+K(x-y,z+w)-2K(x,z)+2K(y,w)=0,x,y\in X.

(3.17)

As a result,
K(x,y)
is a biadditive mapping.
∎

4.
Conclusion

In the paper, we examined the stability of the biadditive function equation of the form

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\alpha f(f_{1}(x,y))+\beta f(f_{2}(x,y)).

The employed approach is utilized to examine several functional equations that involve multiple variables, and stability theorems have been derived. Notably, this method does not impose any constraints on the parity, domain, or range of the function under scrutiny, unlike other techniques. We provided two examples to demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach.

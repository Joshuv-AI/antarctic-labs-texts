Scale Invariant Monte Carlo under Linear Function Approximation with Curvature based Step-size
Rahul Madhavan, Hemanta Makwana

Abstract
We study the feature-scaled version of the Monte Carlo algorithm with linear function approximation. This algorithm converges to a scale-invariant solution, which is not unduly affected by states having feature vectors with large norms. The usual versions of the MCMC algorithm, obtained by minimizing the least-squares criterion, do not produce solutions that give equal importance to all states irrespective of feature-vector norm – a requirement that may be critical in many reinforcement learning contexts. To speed up convergence in our algorithm, we introduce an adaptive step-size based on the curvature of the iterate convergence path – a novelty that may be useful in more general optimization contexts as well. A key contribution of this paper is to prove convergence, in the presence of adaptive curvature based step-size and heavy-ball momentum. We provide rigorous theoretical guarantees and use simulations to demonstrate the efficacy of our ideas.

Illustrative example for issues with LS:
The least squares method provides solutions which are more skewed towards feature vectors with larger norm. We illustrate this with a toy-example as follows. Consider a two-state system with features

To address this issue, in this paper, we propose a solution calculated as per the alternative criterion:

Secondly, the solution is more robust to outlier rows. Unlike in the least-squares solution, large

Further, the solution remains unchanged even if the individual equations are re-scaled.

1.2
Our Contributions

We formulate and study the convergence of scale-invariant RL algorithms with linear function approximation in the presence of momentum and adaptive step size. Our algorithm uses a variant of the stochastic Kaczmarz method
(
Strohmer and Vershynin, 2009b
)
to seek a scale-invariant solution. Note that the original method solves overdetermined
\Phi w=V
systems that are consistent, and requires access to exact value (
V
) estimates. We provide a convergence guarantee even with only noisy samples of the value function. This is crucial in RL applications, where we get access to some noisy estimate of the value either by a one step temporal-difference (TD) or by summing rewards (Monte Carlo).

In RL systems, every state might be equally important irrespective of the feature vector norms. Our algorithms converge to a solution that satisfies this property of not being unduly influenced by outliers, or states with high feature-vector norms. Hence, we call our algorithm scale-invariant — as the scale of the input features does not matter to the output solution.

We now outline the basic (linear) framework under which our Algorithms operate.

1.2.1
The Update Rule

Consider any overdetermined linear system
\Phi w=V,
consisting of m rows of the form
\phi_{i}^{\top}w=V_{i},\kern 5.0pt\phi_{i},w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
D
be a diagonal weight matrix with entries
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}.

If we wish to solve

w^{*}=\min_{w}\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}(\phi_{i}w-V_{i})^{2}/\|\phi_{i}\|^{2}

(1)

Then the stochastic update (with say
\tau
samples) takes the form

w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\alpha_{k}\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\frac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{i}}{\|\phi_{i}\|^{2}}\phi_{i}

(2)

where the rows are sampled with probability
d_{i}
and
\alpha_{k}
is some step-size sequence.
Since each expression of the form
(\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{i})\phi_{i}/(\|\phi_{i}\|^{2})
is a projection from
w_{k}
onto the hyperplane
\phi_{i}^{\top}w=V_{i}
, we call the update map from
w_{k}
to
w_{k+1}
for all iterations
k
as Total Projections (TP) map. In general, such a map changes per iteration as a different rows of the form
\phi_{i}^{\top}w=V_{i}
are chosen. Depending on such a choice, the map at step
k
may be called
TP_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In other words,
w_{k+1}=TP_{k}(w_{k})
. This is a Kaczmarz based algorithm
(
Kaczmarz, 1937
)
which converges to a consistent solution in the presence of no noise.

For our full update rule, we need to add a momentum and our choice of step size. For the momentum part we use heavyball momentum with constant
\beta
(reasons in
Section
5
). For our step size, we use an osculating circle based step choice, which we call the curvature step (details in
Section
4.1
). We provide evidence that the step size works in
the section
1.2.2
. With these in place, we now describe our full update rule.

Let
TP_{k}(w)=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}(\phi_{i}w-V_{i})\phi_{i}/\|\phi_{i}\|^{2}
,
and
\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})
=
TP_{k}([w_{k}-TP_{k}(w_{k})])-TP_{k}(w_{k})
. Here the stochastic gradient update on
w_{k}
with respect to our error term is given by
TP_{k}(w_{k})
and
\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})
indicates the change in gradient. Our update rule is then given by:

w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\eta_{k}\dfrac{||TP_{k}(w_{k})||}{||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||}TP_{k}(w_{k})+\beta(w_{k}-w_{k-1})

(3)

where
\beta\in(0,1)
and
\eta_{k}=1/k^{p};\quad p\in(0.5,1]
. We now provide an intuition for each of the terms.

The second term in
Equation
3
indicates the gradient update. Note that
\eta_{k}
is a decreasing step-size sequence. Typically, one might use some sequence
\{\eta_{k}\}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty
. Such a requirement is satisfied by
\eta_{k}=1/k^{p}
where
p\in(0.5,1]

(
Robbins and Monro, 1951
;
Blum, 1954
)
. Recall that
TP_{k}
is a map that gives the projections over the sampled hyperplanes
\phi_{i}w=V_{i}
. Therefore
TP_{k}(w_{k})
is an update in the direction of the required (total) projection from
w_{k}
onto the hyperplanes chosen, i.e. a gradient descent update from
w_{k}
towards
w^{*}
in our chosen error metric.

The updates to
w_{k}
at discrete time steps may be assumed to be a noisy discretization to a continuous curve
\omega(t)
at some time
t
such that
\omega(t)=w_{k}
. Then
\omega^{\prime}(t)
– the tangent to the curve
\omega(t)
– may be approximated by the update
TP_{k}(w_{k})
.
The unit tangent to the curve
\omega(t)
is given by
TP_{k}(w_{k})/||TP_{k}(w_{k})||
. Further,
\omega^{\prime\prime}(t)
is approximated by the update
\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})
.
But the radius of curvature
\kappa=||dT/d\omega||
where
T(t)
is the unit tangent at time
t
, and
\omega(t)
is the parameterized curve
(
Kuhnel, 2015
)
.

Then by the Chain Rule,
\kappa=||dT/dt||/||d\omega/dt||=||dT/dt||/||\omega^{\prime}(t)||=||\omega^{\prime\prime}(t)||/||\omega^{\prime}(t)||^{2}

(
Tapp, 2016
)
.
Then we find the approximation
\kappa\simeq||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||/||TP_{k}(w_{k})||^{2}
for the discrete setting and the radius of curvature
R=1/\kappa=||TP_{k}(w_{k})||^{2}/||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||
. Then the update rule becomes
R\cdot

Unit gradient vector
=
R\cdot TP_{k}(w_{k})/||TP_{k}(w_{k})||=||TP_{k}(w_{k})||/||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||\cdot TP_{k}(w_{k})
.

The third term in
Equation
3
is a heavy-ball momentum term, where we add some constant (
\beta
) times the previous updates. This momentum term, is less useful in the context where we have no noise, but can be useful in the case of noisy updates
(
Gitman et al., 2019
;
Sutskever et al., 2013
;
Polyak, 1964
)
.

In light of the multiple expressions in the update rule given by Equation
3
,
showing convergence is not straightforward. We use the theory of stochastic approximation to establish almost sure (a.s.) convergence for the algorithms we propose. This is a key technical contribution of this work.

DISCUSSION ON STEP SIZE

In this section we cover in detail our curvature step size and choice of momentum method.

4.1
Adaptive step sizes for Total Projections Algorithm

Choice of step size is extremely important for ML practitioners. We propose a novel variation for a step size sequence.

To achieve convergence for a stochastic approximation algorithm, we need the step size sequences
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to be such that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}<\infty

(
Borkar, 2008
)
.
To achieve this, our step size sequence takes the form
\eta_{k}\cdot\theta_{k}/||TP_{k}(\cdot)||
, where
\eta_{k}=1/k^{p};\kern 5.0ptp\in(0.5,1]
. The second term
\theta_{k}
is the term of interest currently, and the third term makes the existing update term
TP_{k}(\cdot)
unit norm.

4.1.1
Idea for Curvature Step

and

4.1.2
Estimating Radius of Osculating circle

Let
w_{k}(t)
be some stochastic gradient curve we are descending, with some subset of hyperplanes fixed. Then the curvature is given by
\kappa=||w^{\prime\prime}(\cdot)||
, where w is parameterized to some unit vector in the space, and radius
R=1/\kappa
.

Note that that our updates,
TP_{k}(\cdot)
are tangents to
w(t)
. Since our estimates are not unit parameterized, we need an appropriate change of scale (re-parametrization). In other words, we divide our estimate for tangent by
||TP_{k}(w_{k})||
, to get the unit tangent. Similar re-scaling of our estimate for curvature yields
||TP_{k}(w_{k})||^{2}
in the denominator
Chappers, 2017
.

Let
\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})=TP_{k}(w_{k}-TP_{k}(w_{k}))-TP_{k}(w_{k})
. Then, our guess for the second derivative is
||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||
, which after re-parametrization gives
||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||/||TP_{k}(w_{k})||^{2}
.
Then we have
R=1/\kappa=||TP_{k}(w_{k})||^{2}/||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||
. Thus:

\begin{split}\theta_{k}&=\frac{||TP_{k}(w_{k})||^{2}}{||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||}\end{split}

(6)

Thus our update equation (without momentum) becomes:

\begin{split}w_{k+1}&=w_{k}-\eta_{k}\theta_{k}\frac{TP_{k}(w_{k})}{||TP_{k}(w_{k})||}\\
&=w_{k}-\eta_{k}\dfrac{||TP_{k}(w_{k})||}{||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||}(TP_{k}(w_{k}))\end{split}

(7)

We call the step size sequence
\alpha_{k}
as

We carried out simulations for systems with 25 states and 10 features (m=25,n=10) in the presence of noise to see efficacy of our proposed algorithm. We carry out two experiments. The first is to determine the momentum method to be used with our curvature step size method. The second experiment is to compare the efficacy of using a normal step size, using curvature step size with no momentum, and using curvature step size with (Polyak’s) heavyball momentum.
We outline these experiments below.

Momentum Method Used:

Of the various momentum optimization methods used in gradient descent algorithms
Ruder, 2017
, our comparisons (

works best (reasons in

). We use this for our step size sequence. We notice that the decrease in error using some of the momentum methods is not monotonic, meaning that there could be bad updates that are amplified by the momentum method used. In this sense, the heavy-ball momentum is conservative, and ensures convergence so long as the original iterates converge, even in the presence of noise.
We next look at whether using the heavy-ball momentum so chosen, we get better convergence rates than without using momentum, in the noisy setting.

Advantage of using curvature step and momentum with noisy updates:

6
CONCLUSIONS AND DISCUSSION

In this work, we presented a scale-invariant version of the popular Monte Carlo algorithm for reinforcement learning. We gave a rationale for why Least Squares criterion fails in many instances, and the feature-scaled version should be used in the linear-function approximation setting. We then proposed a novel adaptive step size sequence based on the curvature of the path of convergence of the iterate
w_{k}
. We provided a convergence proof for this algorithm in the presence of momentum. Finally we experimentally validated our algorithm through simulations and showed that in the presence of noise we have significant speedups over the regular algorithm. Without noise, our algorithm in fact has exponentially faster convergence than the usual stochastic gradient update rule.

A possible extension of our work would be to use the proposed step size in the context of non-linear, non-convex settings. Further, we believe there is merit in applying our scale-invariant algorithm
(rather than some least-squares variant) in various other linear settings where we wish to give equal importance to all data points – irrespective of norm.

Appendix B
DIFFERENCES BETWEEN OUR ALGORITHM AND THE KACZMARZ ALGORITHM

Our algorithm is a variation on the Randomized Kaczmarz algorithm described in
Strohmer and Vershynin, 2009b
. We note the major differences below

1.

The Randomized Kaczmarz algorithm samples the hyperplanes with a probability proportional to the square of the feature-norm, viz
||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}

Strohmer and Vershynin, 2009b
. This approach has been criticized in literature
Strohmer and Vershynin, 2009a
. (In our own simulations, this sampling did not provide any benefits). To sample proportional to the feature-norm square of the states, one needs to know the features-norms of all states, which may not be possible

2.

In the RL context, obtaining all possible features ab-initio is difficult, and so is sampling as per feature-norm square.
Natural sampling would be as per the stationary distribution of the ergodic Markov Chain and we allow for this.

3.

The original Randomized Kaczmarz method was meant for a fully determined
Ax=b
system. Therefore, in the original setup, the iterates lie on hyperplanes onto which one projects.
On the other hand, our iterates don’t lie on any hyperplane. This makes it easier to identify the sequence of iterates with a gradient field (of our error function).

4.

We obtain major speedups (up to a few orders of magnitude) over the regular Kaczmarz method due to our usage of
momentum
and step size based on
radius of osculatory-circle
.

5.

Our formulation makes the algorithm directly a gradient descent on the error function
\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||}\right]^{2}
where
d_{s}
are some positive weights corresponding the hyperplanes
\mathcal{H}_{s}\equiv\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}
. For example,
d_{s}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\kern 5.0pt\forall s\in\mathcal{S}
may correspond to a uniform sampling. Another example is where
d_{s}=\pi_{s}
where
\pi
is the stationary distribution corresponding to the Transition Matrix of a Markov Chain.

C.6
The batch version of the Total Projections algorithm converges

Now we proceed to prove convergence of the batch version (non stochastic version) of the TP algorithm. We have already shown

Proposition C.6.1
.

Let

It has been proved in literature

that a (batch) gradient descent algorithm converges to the local minimizer. Since we have shown that
G(\cdot)
is a convex function over a convex set, it has a single local minimizer, which is also the global optimum.

We start with the second order Taylor series expansion of

at some point

\displaystyle=G(w)+\nabla G(w)^{\top}(y-w)+\frac{1}{2}(y-w)^{\top}Hess(G(z))(y-w)

By

,

is bounded above by
1

\displaystyle G(y)

\displaystyle\leq G(w)+\nabla G(w)^{\top}(y-w)+\frac{1}{2}||y-w||_{2}^{2}

In gradient descent, we proceed in the opposite direction of the gradient.
\therefore y-w=-\alpha\nabla G(w)

\displaystyle G(y)

\displaystyle\leq G(w)-\alpha\nabla G(w)^{\top}\nabla G(w)+\frac{1}{2}||y-w||_{2}^{2}

Then,

\displaystyle G(y)

\displaystyle\leq G(w)-\alpha||\nabla G(w)||_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha\nabla||G(w)||_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle G(y)

\displaystyle\leq G(w)-(\alpha-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2})||\nabla G(w)||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle G(y)

\displaystyle<G(w)

\displaystyle G(w-\alpha TP(w))

\displaystyle<G(w)

If we label the successive iterates as

and

\displaystyle G(w_{k+1})

\displaystyle<G(w_{k})

\displaystyle G(w_{k+1})-G(w^{*})

\displaystyle G(w_{k+1})-G(w^{*})

\displaystyle G(w_{k+1})-G(w^{*})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{k-1}\gamma_{k}(G(w_{k-1})-G(w^{*}))

\displaystyle=\dots

\displaystyle G(w_{k+1})-G(w^{*})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}\gamma_{i}\right)(G(w_{0})-G(w^{*}))

Now we take the limit as

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(G(w_{k+1})-G(w^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(\left(\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}\gamma_{i}\right)(G(w_{0})-G(w^{*}))\right)

D.2
Putting the update equation in standard form:

Consider the update equation
w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\alpha_{k}TP_{k}(w_{k})
. Given the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\{w_{0},\dots,w_{k}\}

h_{k+1}(w)
. Let
\{1\dots\tau\}
be the set of unique hyperplanes sampled on the k’th run of trajectory. Then:

h_{k+1}(w)=\mathbb{E}\left[TP_{k}(w_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]

\tau

\displaystyle h_{k+1}(w)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k},\tau\right]\right]

By linearity of expectation, we can take the expectation inside the brackets:

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k},\tau\right]\right]

But any hyperplane

\pi_{i}

\pi

that we used in
Section

d_{s}=\pi_{s}|\mathcal{S}|
. Thus
\forall i\in\{1,\dots,\tau\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(i,\widetilde{V})}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k},\tau\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}|\mathcal{S}|\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{V}}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]

Substuting this back in (

\displaystyle h_{k+1}(w)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left[\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]

\displaystyle h_{k+1}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}

But the RHS is simply

for the Monte Carlo. Thus:

\displaystyle h_{k+1}(w)

\displaystyle=TP(w_{k})

(17)

Since the function

Now we are in a position to put our update equation in standard form. Let

Proposition D.4.3
.

\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}}\right]=\Phi^{\top}NDN\Phi

Proof.

Note that the LHS and RHS are both matrices of size

. We will show the equality explicitly for each (i,j)’th entry of this matrix.

For the LHS, the entry at position (i,j) is given by

For the RHS, first note that
NDN
is a diagonal matrix of size
|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|
. The diagonal entries are given by
\left[NDN\right]_{(s,s)}=\dfrac{\pi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}}
. Then
NDN\Phi
has
|\mathcal{S}|
rows of the form
\dfrac{\pi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}}\phi_{s}
. Finally, the entry at the (i,j)’th location of
\Phi^{\top}NDN\Phi
, which is a
n\times n
matrix is given by
\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\phi_{s}(i)^{\top}\dfrac{\pi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}}\phi_{s}(j)
. Note that this can be rewritten as
\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}(i)\phi_{s}(j)}{||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}}\right]
, which is the same as the LHS.
∎

In this case we are dealing with a vector in

For the LHS, we have a sum of

\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\dfrac{\pi_{s}V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||_{2}^{2}}\right)\phi_{s}(i)

Problem Setup

Our original stochastic approximation equation with momentum can be written as

w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\alpha_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]+\beta(w_{k}-w_{k-1})

where the notations have the usual meaning explained in
section
2
and further,
\beta\in[0,1)
. We want to show that this converges, where we have already shown that the update
w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\alpha_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]
converges.

Approach used

Traditional algorithms may attempt such a momentum under the two timescale approximation scheme. These have been considered in
Borkar, 2008
;
Lakshminarayanan and Bhatnagar, 2017
. Two time scale approximation are also considered in
Avrachenkov et al., 2020
in the context of web page change rate estimation.
We take a different approach.
First we convert the given stochastic approximation equation with momentum into a two timescale regime, with two iterates getting updated.
Then we collapse the second iterate into a perturbation on the first iterate
w_{k}
, and thus show convergence.
We detail this in the following sections.

F.2
Adapting the stochastic-approximation equation with momentum into a two timescale structure:

Proposition F.2.1
.

The update equation
w_{k+1}=w_{k}-\alpha_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]+\beta(w_{k}-w_{k-1})
can also be written as the set of equations

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}z_{k}

\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle=TP_{k}(w_{k})

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})\quad\forall i\in[1,k]

where
\zeta_{(i,k)}=\beta^{i}\frac{\alpha_{k-i}}{\alpha_{k}}

Proof.

Consider:

\displaystyle w_{k+1}

\displaystyle=w_{k}-\alpha_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]+\beta(w_{k}-w_{k-1})

(20)

Rewriting as a difference:

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]+\beta(w_{k}-w_{k-1})

We will call the term
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]
as
TP_{k}(w_{k})

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}TP_{k}(w_{k})+\beta(w_{k}-w_{k-1})

Expanding the momentum term

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}TP_{k}(w_{k})+\beta(\alpha_{k-1}TP_{k-1}(w_{k-1})+\beta(w_{k-1}-w_{k-2}))

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}TP_{k}(w_{k})+\beta\alpha_{k-1}TP_{k-1}(w_{k-1})+\beta^{2}(w_{k-1}-w_{k-2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}TP_{k}(w_{k})+

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\beta\alpha_{k-1}TP_{k-1}(w_{k-1})+\beta^{2}(\alpha_{k-2}TP_{k-2}(w_{k-2})+\beta(w_{k-2}-w_{k-3}))

\displaystyle=\quad\dots

Thus we can write the whole thing as:

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}TP_{k}(w_{k})+

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\beta\alpha_{k-1}TP_{k-1}(w_{k-1})+\beta^{2}\alpha_{k-2}TP_{k-2}(w_{k-2})+\dots+\beta^{k}\alpha_{0}TP_{0}(w_{0})

(21)

We note that this is in the form of a discounted sum of vectors, which we have to bring into a form that is the sum of two iterates
Kushner and Yin, 1997
.

We reverse the order of the second iterate set. We build
z_{k}
bottom up as follows. Let:

\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle=TP_{k}(w_{k})

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=z_{0}+\beta\frac{\alpha_{k-1}}{\alpha_{k}}TP_{k-1}(w_{k-1})

\displaystyle\dots\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle=\quad\dots

\displaystyle z_{k}

\displaystyle=z_{k-1}+\beta^{k}\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha_{k}}TP_{0}(w_{0})

Further, to simplify this set of equations, we let
\zeta_{(i,k)}
be the step size corresponding to
z_{i}
such that
\zeta_{(i,k)}=\beta^{i}\frac{\alpha_{k-i}}{\alpha_{k}}
. Then we have the set of equations as:

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}z_{k}

\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle=TP_{k}(w_{k})

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{0}+\zeta_{(1,k)}TP_{k-1}(w_{k-1})

\displaystyle\dots\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle=\quad\dots

\displaystyle z_{k}

\displaystyle=z_{k-1}+\zeta_{(k,k)}TP_{0}(w_{0})

Or more generally if
\zeta_{(i,k)}=\beta^{i}\frac{\alpha_{k-i}}{\alpha_{k}}
:

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}z_{k}

(22)

\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle=TP_{k}(w_{k})

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})\quad\forall i\in[1,k]

∎

F.3
Collapsing the two iterate stochastic approximation equations into a single iterate form:

Now wish to express the above equation in terms of an expected update and a Martingale noise term (with respect to the filtration). For
z_{0}
, such an expression is straightforward: We add and subtract the expectation to change the equation from
z_{0}=TP_{k}(w_{k})
to

z_{0}=\expectation\left[TP_{k}(w_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]+(TP_{k}(w_{k})-\expectation\left[TP_{k}(w_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right])

(23)

where the first term is the expected update term
H_{(0,k)}=\expectation\left[TP_{k}(w_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]
second term is a martingale difference noise term,
M_{(0,k)}=(TP_{k}(w_{k})-\expectation\left[TP_{k}(w_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right])
.

Now let us focus on
z_{i}
for
i\in[1,k]

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})

can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}\left[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})+\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})\right)\right]

Which can be further broken down as:

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}\bigg[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]+

(24)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\right)+\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})\right)\bigg]

Now we take an expectation of the first term over all possible
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. Thus the first term breaks into:

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}\bigg[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]+

(25)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right])+

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\right)+\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})\right)\bigg]

Note that in the filtration,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]
remains unaffected, and therefore, we can write
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]
. For ease of notation, we simply write
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]
as
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]
. Then we have:

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}\bigg[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]+(\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right])

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\right)+\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})\right)\bigg]

Notice that the third term above
TP_{k-i}(w_{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]
is actually 0 as the filtration provides the exact hyperplanes as well as
w_{k}
. Thus the expression is deterministic. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}\left[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]=TP_{k-i}(w_{k})
. Thus we finally have

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}\bigg[\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]+

(26)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right])+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})\right)\bigg]

Let

\begin{split}H_{(i,k)}&=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]\\
M_{(i,k)}&=\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]\\
\varepsilon_{(i,k)}&=TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})\end{split}

If
\zeta_{(i,k)}=\beta^{i}\frac{\alpha_{k-i}}{\alpha_{k}}
. Further, let
h_{(i,k)}(\cdot)
be some limiting o.d.e that asymptotically tracks
H_{(i,k)}(\cdot)
. Thus we have the set of equations:

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}z_{k}

(27)

\displaystyle z_{0}

\displaystyle=h_{(0,k)}+M_{(0,k)}

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=z_{i-1}+\zeta_{(i,k)}(h_{(i,k)}+M_{(i,k)}+\varepsilon_{(i,k)})\quad\forall i\in[1,k]

We collapse these now into a single equation. Since
h_{(i,k)}(w_{k})=TP(w_{k})\forall i,k
based on
proposition
F.7.1
We will label this simply as
h(w_{k})

Let
\mathring{h}(w_{k})=h(w_{k})\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}\right)
,
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}=M_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}M_{(i,k)}
and
\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}\varepsilon_{(i,k)}
, Then:

w_{k+1}-w_{k}=\alpha_{k}[\mathring{h}(w_{k})+\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}+\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}]

(28)

Now we have to show that this single equation follows the requirements for convergence. We will show each of the assumptions in order.

F.4
Showing basic properties of required for convergence:

Proposition F.4.1
.

The step size sequence
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfies
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}=\infty
and
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{2}<\infty

Proof.

The step size sequence remains the same as in
proposition
D.3.1
. Thus the proof remains the same.
∎

Proposition F.4.2
.

Let
\mathring{\zeta}_{k}:=1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}
. Then
\mathring{\zeta}_{k}
is bounded.

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle\mathring{\zeta}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}\left[\alpha_{k}+\beta\alpha_{k-1}+\beta^{2}\alpha_{k-2}+\dots\right]

Recall that
\alpha_{k}=\eta_{k}\vartheta_{k}
where
\eta_{k}=\dfrac{1}{k^{p}};\kern 5.0ptp\in(0.5,1]
and
\vartheta_{k}=\dfrac{||TP_{k}(w_{k})||}{||\Delta TP_{k}(w_{k})||}
. Further, recall that
\sup\vartheta_{k}=\overline{\vartheta}
. Then:

\displaystyle\mathring{\zeta}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{\vartheta}}{\vartheta_{k}}\left[1+\beta\left(\frac{k}{k-1}\right)^{p}+\beta^{2}\left(\frac{k}{k-2}\right)^{p}+\dots\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{\vartheta}}{\vartheta_{k}}\left[1+\beta\frac{k}{k-1}+\beta^{2}\frac{k}{k-2}+\dots\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\vartheta}}{\vartheta_{k}}[1+\beta+\frac{\beta}{k-1}+\beta^{2}+\frac{2\beta^{2}}{k-2}+\beta^{3}+\frac{3\beta^{3}}{k-3}+\dots]

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\vartheta}}{\vartheta_{k}}[(1+\beta+\beta^{2}+\dots)+(\frac{\beta}{k-1}+\frac{2\beta^{2}}{k-2}+\frac{3\beta^{3}}{k-3}+\dots)]

As
k\to\infty
, the first half of the above expression is
\frac{\overline{\vartheta}}{\vartheta_{k}(1-\beta)}
. As
k\to\infty
, the second half converges to 0
Murthy, 2021
. Thus the entire expression remains bounded.

∎

Proposition F.4.3
.

The expected update for
w
,
\mathring{h}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is Lipschitz

Proof.

We have already shown that
h(\cdot)=TP(w)
is Lipschitz (as can be seen from the fact that
\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}\top}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}w+C
where
C=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}
is linear in w). Now we will show that
\mathring{h}(\cdot)=h(\cdot)(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i})
is also Lipschitz. But we have shown that
\mathring{\zeta}_{k}=1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}
is bounded in
proposition
F.4.2
.

Thus we have that if
h(\cdot)
is Lipschitz, then
\mathring{\zeta}_{k}h(\cdot)=\mathring{h}(\cdot)
is also Lipschitz for some constant
\mathring{\zeta}_{k}
.

∎

F.5
Showing that the noise term is a martingale difference sequence:

Proposition F.5.1
.

We specifically consider
M_{(0,k)}
first.

\mathbb{E}[M_{(0,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}\left[||M_{(0,k)}||^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq K[1+||w_{k}||^{2}]

Proof.

We note that
M_{(0,k)}=TP_{k}(w_{k})-\expectation\left[TP_{k}(w_{k})\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]=TP_{k}(w_{k})-TP(w_{k})
as per
appendix section
D.2
.

Now we have already shown in
appendix section
D.3.3
that
\mathbb{E}[TP_{k}(w_{k})-TP(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
.

Further we also showed
\exists A_{(0,k)},C_{(0,k)}
such that
M_{(0,k)}=A_{(0,k)}w_{k}-C_{(0,k)}
whence
\mathbb{E}\left[||TP_{k}(w_{k})-TP(w_{k})||^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq K[1+||w_{k}||^{2}]
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}

∎

Proposition F.5.2
.

\mathbb{E}[M_{(i,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}\left[||M_{(0,k)}||^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq K[1+||w_{k}||^{2}]

Proof.

First we note that

\displaystyle M_{(i,k)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]

Note that the second expectation remains unchanged given the filtration, thus we can rewrite this as:

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]

Given such a definition,
\mathbb{E}[M_{(i,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]=0

For the second part, note that the filtration gives us the hyperplanes, say
\{1,\dots\tau\}
that have been sampled. Then:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||_{2}^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]

We obtain from
appendix proposition
F.7.1
that
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]=TP(w_{k})=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]
. Therefore:

\displaystyle M_{(i,k)}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||_{2}^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]-\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\dfrac{\phi_{i}\phi_{i}^{\top}}{||\phi_{i}||_{2}^{2}}--\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w_{k}-\left[\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\dfrac{\widetilde{V}_{i}\phi_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||_{2}^{2}}--\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{V_{s}\phi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=A_{(i,k)}w_{k}-C_{(i,k)}

which is linear in
w_{k}
with bounded coefficients. Further note that
A_{(i,k)}
is bounded above as
\frac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}
has maximum eigen value 1. Further,
C_{(i,k)}
is bounded as
\widetilde{V}
is bounded above by
\dfrac{R_{max}}{1-\gamma}
where
R_{max}
is the maximum reward and
\gamma
is the discounting factor.

Thus
||M_{(i,k)}||^{2}
is quadratic in
w_{k}
. Now it is easy to see that there would exist some K such that
\mathbb{E}\left[||M_{(i,k)}||^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq K(1+||w_{k}||^{2})

∎

Proposition F.5.3
.

Consider the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\{w_{0},\dots,w_{k}\}
. Then the sequence
\{\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\}
is a zero-mean martingale difference noise sequence. Specifically, we have that:

1.

\mathbb{E}[\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0

2.

\mathbb{E}[||\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}||^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq K_{i}(1+||w_{k}||^{2})

Proof.

For the first part, we need to show
\mathbb{E}[\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
where
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}=M_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}M_{(i,k)}
. We have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[M_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}M_{(i,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

By linearity of expectation:

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[M_{(0,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}\mathbb{E}[M_{(i,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

But we have from
proposition
F.5.2
that
\mathbb{E}[M_{(i,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
and from
proposition
F.5.1
that
\mathbb{E}[M_{(0,k)}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}|\mathcal{F}_{k}]

\displaystyle=0+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}0

\displaystyle=0

For the second part, we see this by linearity.

\displaystyle\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}

\displaystyle=M_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}M_{(i,k)}

From propositions
F.5.1
and
F.5.2
, we can write the above as:

\displaystyle=(A_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}A_{(i,k)})w_{k}-(C_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}C_{(i,k)})

Since
A_{(i,k)},C_{(i,k)}
are bounded
\forall i\in\{0,\dots,k\}
, we can write the above as:

\displaystyle\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathring{A}w_{k}-\mathring{C}

where
\mathring{A}=A_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}A_{(i,k)}
is bounded and
\mathring{C}=C_{(0,k)}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{(i,k)}C_{(i,k)}
is bounded.
Now we see that
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}
is linear in
w_{k}
with bounded coefficients.

Thus
||\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}||^{2}
is quadratic in
w_{k}
, whence

\exists K\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[||\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{k}||^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}_{k}\right]\leq K(1+||w_{k}||^{2})

∎

F.6
Showing that the momentum terms sum to a perturbation:

Proposition F.6.1

(Helper proposition for
F.6.2
)
.

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||\to 0
as
k\to\infty

Proof.

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||+\sum\limits_{i=m+1}^{\infty}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||

Now given any
\epsilon
, there
\exists m
such that
\sum\limits_{i=m+1}^{\infty}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||<\epsilon
. This is because
\zeta_{i}\sim\beta^{i}
go to 0 and
||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||
are bounded (shown separately when we show stability of iterates).

Then given any finite m, at the asymptote as
k\to\infty
, we have
||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||\to 0
as
\alpha_{k}\to 0
. Thus
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||<\epsilon
as
k\to\infty
for any arbitrary
\epsilon
.

Thus
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||\downarrow 0
as
k\to\infty

∎

Proposition F.6.2
.

\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}
are perturbation terms that satisfy

||\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}||\leq d_{k}(1+||w_{k}||)
where
{d_{k}}
are a sequence of positive scalars such that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}d_{k}=0

Proof.

First note that
\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\varepsilon_{(i,k)}
and
\varepsilon_{(i,k)}=TP_{k-i}(w_{k-i})-TP_{k-i}(w_{k})
. Thus

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{j}^{\top}w_{k-i}-V_{j}}{||\phi_{j}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{j}-\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{j}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{j}}{||\phi_{j}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{j}

Taking terms common:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{j}\phi_{j}^{\top}}{||\phi_{j}||^{2}}\right]\right)(w_{k-i}-w_{k})

\displaystyle||\varepsilon_{i}||

\displaystyle\leq||w_{k-i}-w_{k}||

(29)

Now we extend this by using
\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\varepsilon_{i}

Expanding
\varepsilon_{i}
using the inequality in (
29
)

\displaystyle||\mathring{\varepsilon}_{k}||

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}\cdot||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||

Now we note that asymptotically as
k\to\infty
, we have for finite i,
||w_{k}-w_{k-i}||\to 0
and for large i,
\zeta_{i}\to 0
. Thus by
proposition
F.6.1
the above is bounded above by some arbitrary
\epsilon
.

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon

Thus asymptotically we see that this perturbation term is o(1).
∎

F.7
Stability Criterion: Iterates remain bounded

In
Borkar, 2008
, we have to prove that the iterates of in the update equation remain bounded.
Lakshminarayanan and Bhatnagar, 2017
have provided a stability criterion to ensure that the iterates remain bounded. While we have already shown that the iterates on after the expected update remain bounded in
proposition
3.4
, here we will explicitly show the stability criterion is satisfied.

But first a basic proposition:

Proposition F.7.1
.

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]=TP(w_{k})

Proof.

Note that we are considering the expectation over all possible filtrations. Note that the random variables under consideration are
\tau
- the number of hyperplanes sampled,
i\in\{1,\dots,\tau\}
- the set of hyperplanes sampled, and
\widetilde{V}
- the value function.
The filtration gives us
w_{k}
and the set of hyperplanes chosen in a particular trajectory. Let’s label the unique hyperplanes in the trajectory by
\{1,\dots\tau\}
. Then:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}=(\tau,i,\widetilde{V})}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]\right)

By linearity we rewrite this as:

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{(i,\widetilde{V})}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]\right)

Over all possible filtrations, we can write the expectation of the inner term as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(i,\widetilde{V})}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\top}w_{k}-\widetilde{V}_{i}}{||\phi_{i}||^{2}}\phi_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}

Substituting this in the previous expression, we get:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\tau}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\tau}\left[\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]\right)

But the inner expression is now independent of
\tau
. Thus:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\left[\mathbb{E}[TP_{k-i}(w_{k})|\mathcal{F}_{k}]\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w_{k}-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\phi_{s}\right]

We note that the RHS is
TP(w_{k})

∎

Proposition F.7.2
.

Let us define the sequence of functions
\mathring{h}_{c}(w):\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathring{h}_{c}(w)=\dfrac{\mathring{h}(cw)}{c};\quad c\geq 1
. Then

1.

\mathring{h}_{c}(\cdot)\mapsto\mathring{h}_{\infty}(\cdot)
as
c\to\infty
uniformly on compact sets
Further,

2.

The limiting o.d.e,
\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{h}_{\infty}(w(t))
has a unique globally asymptotically stable equilibrium at the origin.

Proof.

First note that

\displaystyle\mathring{h}(w)

\displaystyle=h(w)(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i})

But
\mathring{\zeta}=(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i})
. Then:

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}h(w)

Expanding
h(w)
:

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{s}

Now we write
\mathring{h}_{c}(w)
from its definition:

\displaystyle\mathring{h}_{c}(w)

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}cw-V_{s}}{c||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{s}

But the constants c can be cancelled for the
w
term:

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}\left(\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w-\frac{1}{c}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{V_{s}\phi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\right)

We observe the uniform convergence of this set of functions
\mathring{h}_{c}(w)
to
\mathring{h}_{\infty}(w)
in the limit
c\to\infty
as the term
c
is only involved with a constant coefficient given by
\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{V_{s}\phi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]
. Thus the first part is proved.

For the second part, we note the following:

\displaystyle\mathring{h}_{\infty}(w)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{c\to\infty}\mathring{\zeta}\left(\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w-\frac{1}{c}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{V_{s}\phi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\right)

Now we apply the limit only on the second term:

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w-\left(\lim\limits_{c\to\infty}\frac{\mathring{\zeta}}{c}\right)\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{V_{s}\phi_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]

Evaluating the limit, we get 0 for the second term:

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w-0

Thus:

\displaystyle\mathring{h}_{\infty}(w)

\displaystyle=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w

Now consider the system
\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{h}_{\infty}(w(t))=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w
. At the equilibrium point,

\displaystyle\dot{w}(t)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]w

\displaystyle=0

But
\Phi
has full column rank (by assumption). Thus no eigen value of
\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}\phi_{s}^{\top}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]
is 0. Thus:

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=0

Thus
\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{h}_{\infty}(w(t))
has a unique globally asymptotically stable equilibrium at the origin.
∎

Proposition F.8.1
.

The globally asymptotically stable equilibrium for the limiting o.d.e
\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{h}(w(t))
that our stochastic approximation equation tracks is given by
w^{*}=\left[\left(\Phi^{\top}NDN\Phi\right)^{-1}\Phi^{\top}NDN\right]V

Proof.

The update equation,
\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{h}(w(t))
can be written as
\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{\zeta}h(w(t))=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{s}
. Considering that the states are sampled from the stationary distribution
\pi
, we have:

\displaystyle\dot{w}(t)=\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{s}

Then the equilibrium point is given by the point where:

\displaystyle\dot{w}(t)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mathring{\zeta}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{s}

\displaystyle=0

But
\mathring{\zeta}
is just a constant. Therefore:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{s}\left[\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\top}w-V_{s}}{||\phi_{s}||^{2}}\right]\phi_{s}

\displaystyle=0

(30)

But this is an equation that we have already solved in

A generalized polar-coordinate integration formula with applications to the study of convolution powers of complex-valued functions on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} .
Huan Q. Bui, Evan Randles

Abstract
In this article, we consider a class of functions on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , called positive homogeneous functions, which interact well with certain continuous one-parameter groups of (generally anisotropic) dilations. Generalizing the Euclidean norm, positive homogeneous functions appear naturally in the study of convolution powers of complex-valued functions on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . As the spherical measure is a Radon measure on the unit sphere which is invariant under the symmetry group of the Euclidean norm, to each positive homogeneous function P P , we construct a Radon measure σ P \sigma_{P} on S = { η ∈ ℝ d : P ⁡ ( η ) = 1 } S=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:P(\eta)=1\} which is invariant under the symmetry group of P P . With this measure, we prove a generalization of the classical polar-coordinate integration formula and deduce a number of corollaries in this setting. We then turn to the study of convolution powers of complex functions on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} and certain oscillatory integrals which arise naturally in that context. Armed with our integration formula and the Van der Corput lemma, we establish sup-norm-type estimates for convolution powers; this result is new and partially extends results of [ 20 ] and [ 21 ] .



Keywords:
Polar-coordinate Integration Formula, Spherical Measure, Oscillatory Integrals, Convolution Powers.

\operatorname{det}(\mathcal{A}(r:\eta))\,dr\,d\mbox{Vol}_{S_{q}}(\eta)
where

is the Riemannian volume measure on
S_{q}
and
\mathcal{A}(r;\eta)
is determined by the Riemannian curvature

along the geodesic paths
\gamma_{\eta}(r)
. The second related result appears in the context of homogeneous (Lie) groups and can be found as Proposition 1.15 of
[
13
]
. In that context,

is replaced by a homogeneous norm

on the homogeneous group

,

is replaced by the unit sphere

in the homogeneous norm, and

is replaced by the homogeneous dimension of

. Perhaps obviously, the second result on homogeneous groups shares the most in common with Theorem

but differs in context and in that we ask much less of

. In particular,

can be continuous yet nowhere differentiable (in contrast to

).

Before we discuss the ideas behind the construction of

and the proof of Theorem

Corollary 1.4
.

Given
0\leq a<b
, suppose that
f:\overline{A_{a}^{b}}\to\mathbb{C}
is continuous and define
\mathcal{I}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{C}
by

\mathcal{I}(r)=\int_{A_{a}^{r}}f(x)\,dx.

for
a\leq r\leq b
. Then
\mathcal{I}
is continuously differentiable and

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(r)=r^{\mu_{P}-1}\int_{S}f(r^{E}\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)

on
[a,b]
(or on
(0,b]
provided that
a=0
and
\mu_{P}<1
) where
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
. In particular, if
f
is a complex-valued function which is continuous on some open neighborhood of
\overline{B}
, then

\int_{S}f(\eta)\sigma_{P}(\eta)=\frac{d}{dr}\left(\int_{B_{r}}f(x)\,dx\right)\bigg|_{r=1}

where this derivative is two-sided.

Proof.

By virtue of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\mathcal{I}(r)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S}\chi_{A_{a}^{r}}(t^{E}\eta)f(t^{E}\eta)\,\sigma_{P}(d\eta)t^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dt=\int_{a}^{r}g(t)t^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dt

where

g(t)=\int_{S}f(t^{E}\eta)\,\sigma_{P}(d\eta).

By virtue of the continuity of
f
and the compactness of
S
, it is easy to see that
g(t)
is continuous on
[a,b]
. By an appeal to the fundamental theorem of calculus, it follows that
\mathcal{I}
is differentiable on
[a,b]
(or on
(0,b]
provided that
a=0
and
\mu_{P}<1
) and

\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(r)=g(r)r^{\mu_{P}-1}=r^{\mu_{P}-1}\int_{S}f(r^{E}\eta)\,\sigma_{P}(d\eta).

To prove the final assertion, we first claim that there exists
\epsilon>0
for which
\overline{B}\subseteq B_{1+\epsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
. To see this, we assume to reach a contradiction that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
x_{n}\in B_{1+1/n}\setminus\mathcal{O}
. Because
\overline{B_{2}}
is compact, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
is bounded and thus has a convergent subsequence by the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem. By a (possible) reassignment of
\{x_{n}\}
, we may therefore assume that
\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x
. Because
P
is continuous and
1\leq P(x_{n})\leq 1+1/n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
P(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(x_{n})=1
and therefore
x\in S\subseteq\overline{B}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
. This implies that
\mathcal{O}
contains an accumulation point
x
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{O}
which is impossible because
\mathcal{O}
is an open set. As a consequence, the second assertion of the corollary follows immediately from the first where the derivative at
r=1
is two-sided.
∎

As an application of the preceding corollary, information can be exchanged between the Fourier transforms
\{\widehat{\chi_{B_{r}}}\}_{r>0}

\widehat{\sigma_{P}}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{S}e^{-i\eta\cdot x}\,\sigma_{P}(d\eta)

\widehat{\sigma_{P}}(x)=\frac{d}{dr}\widehat{\chi_{B_{r}}}(x)\Big|_{r=1}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\widehat{\chi_{B}}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\sigma_{P}}(r^{E^{*}}x)r^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dr

(5)

for

where

. It is well known that decay estimates for the Fourier transform of surface-carried measures (of the form

Corollary 1.5
.

Given
g:S\to\mathbb{C}
and
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
, define
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
by

f(x)=\begin{cases}\mu_{P}\cdot\chi_{(0,1)}(P(x))g(P(x)^{-E}x)&\mbox{ for }x\neq 0\\
0&\mbox{ for }x=0\end{cases}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
where
\chi_{(0,1)}(\cdot)
is the indicator function of the interval
(0,1)
. Then
g\in L^{1}(S,\Sigma_{P},\sigma_{P})
if and only if
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and, in this case,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,dx=\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta).

(6)

Proof.

Observe that, for the
(\mathcal{L}\times\Sigma_{P})^{\prime}
-measurable function
k(r,\eta)=\chi_{(0,1)}(r)g(\eta)
,

k\circ\psi_{E}^{-1}(x)=k(P(x),P(x)^{-E}x)=f(x)

for

. By virtue of Theorem
1.3
, it follows that

is Lebesgue measurable and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}\left(\int_{(0,\infty)}|k(r,\eta)|r^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dr\right)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{S}|g(\eta)|\sigma_{P}(d\eta)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mu_{P}r^{\mu_{P}-1}\,dr\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}\absolutevalue{g(\eta)}\sigma_{P}(d\eta)

and therefore

if and only if

and
\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|g\|_{L^{1}(S)}
. By an analogous computation (for

), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,dx=\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta),

by virtue of Property

.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

In the case that
d=1
, it is easy to see that every function satisfying the hypotheses is of the form

P(x)=\begin{cases}P(1)x^{\alpha}&\mbox{ for }x\geq 0\\
P(-1)(-x)^{\alpha}&\mbox{ for }x<0\end{cases}

for some
\alpha>0
where
P(1),P(-1)>0
and
\operatorname{Exp}(P)
consists only of the linear function
x\mapsto x/\alpha
. In this setting, it is easy to see that Conditions
(a)
–
(e)
are satisfied (always and) simultaneously. We shall therefore assume that
d>1
for the remainder of the proof.

\ref{cond:SisCompact}\Rightarrow\ref{cond:PisAboveOne}
.

Given that
S
is compact, it is bounded and so we have a positive number
M
for which
P(x)\neq 1
for all
|x|\geq M
. Observe that, if for two points
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{M}
,
P(x_{1})<1<P(x_{2})
or
P(x_{2})<1<P(x_{1})
, then by virtue of the path connectedness of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{M}
and the intermediate value theorem, we would be able to find
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{B}_{M}
for which
P(x_{0})=1
, an impossibility. Therefore, to show that Condition
(b)
holds, we must simply rule out the case in which
P(x)<1
for all
|x|\geq M
. Let us therefore assume, to reach a contradiction, that this alternate condition holds. In this case, we take
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and observe that

\lim_{r\to\infty}P(r^{E}y)=\lim_{r\to\infty}rP(y)=\infty.

By virtue of our supposition, we find that
|r^{E}y|<M
for all sufficiently large
r
. In particular, there exists a sequence
r_{k}\to\infty
for which
|r_{k}^{E}y|\leq M
for all
k
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}P(r_{k}^{E}y)=\infty.

Because
\overline{\mathbb{B}_{M}}
, the closure of
\mathbb{B}_{M}
, is compact,
\{r_{k}^{E}y\}
has a convergent subsequence which we also denote by
\{r_{k}^{E}y\}
by a slight abuse of notation. In view of the continuity of
P
at
\eta=\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}^{E}y
, we have

P(\eta)=\lim_{k\to\infty}P(r_{k}^{E}y)=\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}P(y)=\infty,

which is impossible. Thus Condition
(b)
holds.
∎

\ref{cond:PisAboveOne}\Rightarrow\ref{cond:Contracting}
.

We shall prove the contrapositive statement. Suppose that, for
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
,
\{r^{E}\}
is not contracting. In this case, by virtue of Proposition
A.2
, there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
for which
r_{k}^{E}x
does not converge to zero in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If our sequence
\{r_{k}^{E}x\}
is bounded, then it must have a convergent subsequence
\{r_{k_{m}}^{E}x\}
with non-zero subsequential limit
\eta=\lim_{m\to\infty}r_{k_{m}}^{E}x
. By the continuity of
P
, we have

P(\eta)=\lim_{m\to\infty}P(r_{k_{m}}^{E}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k_{m}}P(x)=0

which cannot be true for it would violate the positive definiteness of
P
. We must therefore consider the other possibility: The sequence
\{r_{k}^{E}x\}
is unbounded. In particular, there must be some
k_{0}
for which
r_{k_{0}}<1/P(x)
and
|r_{k_{0}}^{E}x|>M
. Upon putting
y=r_{k_{0}}^{E}x
, we have
|y|>M
and
P(y)=P(r_{k_{0}}^{E}x)=r_{k_{0}}P(x)<1
which shows that Condition
(b)
cannot hold.
∎

\ref{cond:Contracting}\Rightarrow\ref{cond:ThereExistsContracting}
.

This is immediate.
∎

\ref{cond:ThereExistsContracting}\Rightarrow\ref{cond:InfiniteLimit}
.

Let
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
be such that the one-parameter group
\{r^{E}\}
is contracting and let
\{x_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}r_{k}=\infty
. Given that

is continuous and strictly positive on the compact set

, we have
\inf_{\eta\in\mathbb{S}}P(\eta)>0
and therefore

\liminf_{k}P(x_{k})=\liminf_{k}r_{k}P(\eta_{k})\geq\liminf_{k}r_{k}\left(\inf_{\eta\in\mathbb{S}}P(\eta)\right)=\infty

showing that
\lim_{k\to\infty}P(x_{k})=\infty
, as desired.
∎

\ref{cond:InfiniteLimit}\Rightarrow\ref{cond:SisCompact}
.

Because
S
is the preimage of the closed singleton
\{1\}
under the continuous function

(e)
,

is also be bounded and thus compact in view of the Heine-Borel theorem.
∎

Proposition 2.1
.

For each positive homogeneous function
P
,
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is a compact subgroup of
\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular, it is a subgroup of the orthogonal group
O(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

As discussed in the introduction, it is clear that
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To see that
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is compact, it suffices to prove that
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is closed and bounded by virtue of the Heine-Borel theorem. To this end, let
\{O_{n}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(P)
be a sequence converging to
O\in\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the continuity of
P
guarantees that

P(Ox)=P\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}O_{n}x\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(O_{n}x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(x)=P(x).

Hence,
O\in\operatorname{Sym}(P)
and so
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is closed.

We assume, to reach a contradiction, that
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is not bounded. In this case, there is a sequence
\{\eta_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}
for which
\lim_{n\to\infty}|O_{n}\eta_{n}|=\infty
. Given that
\mathbb{S}
is compact, by passing to a subsequence if needed, we may assume without loss in generality that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\eta_{n}=\eta\in\mathbb{S}
. By virtue of Proposition
1.1
and the continuity of
P
,

P(\eta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(\eta_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}P(O_{n}\eta_{n})=\infty

which is impossible. Hence
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
is bounded.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
P
be a positive homogeneous function, then

\tr E=\tr E^{\prime}>0

for all
E,E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
.

Proof.

By virtue of Propositions
1.1
and
A.7
,
\tr E>0
for all

. It remains to show that the trace map is constant on
\operatorname{Exp}(P)
. To this end, let
E,E^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
. Then, for all

and

,

P(x)=r(1/r)P(x)=rP((1/r)^{E^{\prime}}x)=P(r^{E}(1/r)^{E^{\prime}}x)=P(r^{E}r^{-E^{\prime}}x).

Thus
O_{r}=r^{E}r^{-E^{\prime}}\in\operatorname{Sym}(P)
for each

. In view of Proposition
2.1
, Proposition
A.1
and the homomorphism property of the determinant,

1=\operatorname{det}(O_{r})=\operatorname{det}(r^{E}r^{E^{\prime}})=\operatorname{det}(r^{E})\operatorname{det}(r^{-E^{\prime}})=r^{\tr E}r^{\tr E}=r^{\tr E-\tr E^{\prime}}

∎

Proposition 2.3
.

If
P
is continuous, positive definite and
\operatorname{Exp}(P)
contains an
E\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with real spectrum, then
\{r^{E}\}
is contracting and hence all of the above conditions are (simultaneously) met.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Spec}(E)
is real, the characteristic polynomial of
E
factors completely over
\mathbb{R}
and so we may apply the Jordan-Chevalley decomposition to write
E=D+N
where
D
is diagonalizable,
N
is nilpotent, and
DN=ND
. Let
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an eigenbasis of
D
whose corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{d}
satisfy
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k+1}
for all
k=1,2\dots,d-1
.

Let us assume, to reach a contradiction,
that
\{r^{E}\}
is not contracting. Repeating the same argument given in
\ref{cond:PisAboveOne}\Rightarrow\ref{cond:Contracting}
in the proof of Proposition
1.1
, leaves us with only one possibility: There is a non-zero
x=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
for which
|r_{k}^{E}x|\to\infty
. Let
n+1
denote the index of
N
, then we have

r^{E}_{k}x=r_{k}^{N+D}x=r_{k}^{N}r_{k}^{D}x=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{r_{k}^{\lambda_{i}}(\log r_{k})^{j}}{j!}\alpha_{i}N^{j}v_{i}

for all
k
. Since
\absolutevalue{r_k^E x}\to\infty
and
r_{k}\to 0
, at least one eigenvalue of
D
must be non-positive. To see this, suppose
\lambda_{i}>0
for all
i=1,2,\dots,d
, then in view of L’Hôpital’s rule we have

\lim_{r_{k}\to 0}(\log r_{k})^{j}r_{k}^{\lambda_{i}}=0

for any
j=0,1,2,\dots,n
and
i=1,2,\dots,d
, which implies that
\absolutevalue{r^E_k x}\not\to\infty
as
r_{k}\to 0
, contradicting our assumption. Thus,
\lambda_{1}=\min\{\operatorname{Spec}(D)\}\leq 0
. Let
k
be such that
N^{k}v_{1}\neq 0
but
N^{k+1}v_{1}=0
, then

r^{E}N^{k}v_{1}=r^{D}r^{N}N^{k}v_{1}=r^{D}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\log r)^{j}}{j!}N^{j}N^{k}v_{1}=r^{D}N^{k}v_{1}=N^{k}r^{D}v_{1}=r^{\lambda_{1}}N^{k}v_{1}

where we have used the fact that
DN=ND
. If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then

\infty=\lim_{r\to\infty}rP(N^{k}v_{1})=\lim_{r\to\infty}P(r^{E}N^{k}v_{1})=\lim_{r\to\infty}P(r^{0}N^{k}v_{1})=\lim_{r\to\infty}P(N^{k}v_{1})=P(N^{k}v_{1})

which is impossible since
P
is continuous at
N^{k}v_{1}
. On the other hand, if
\lambda_{1}<0
, then

\infty=\lim_{r\to\infty}rP(N^{k}v_{1})=\lim_{r\to\infty}P(r^{E}N^{k}v_{1})=\lim_{r\to\infty}P(r^{\lambda_{1}}N^{k}v_{1})=P(0)=0

which is also impossible.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
O\in\operatorname{Sym}{(P)}

\operatorname{Exp}(P)=O^{*}\operatorname{Exp}(P)O

where
O^{*}
is the transpose of
O
. In other words, the set
\operatorname{Exp}(P)
is invariant under conjugation by
\operatorname{Sym}(P)
.

Proof.

3
An application: Estimates for convolution powers

We denote by
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the set of functions
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
for which

\|\phi\|_{1}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\absolutevalue{\phi(x)}<\infty.

Given

is defined by

\left(\psi\ast\phi\right)(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(x-y)\phi(y)

for

for

where

is non-negative (and satisfies mild summability conditions), the asymptotic behavior of

is well understood and is the subject of the local (central) limit theorem. For accounts of this story and its connection to probability and random walk theory, we encourage the reader to see the excellent texts
[
17
]
and
[
24
]
(see also Section 7.6 of
[
21
]
). When

is generally complex-valued (or simply a real-valued function taking on both positive and negative values), its convolution powers can exhibit exotic behaviors not seen in the probabilistic setting. The problem of describing these behaviors dates back to Erastus L. De Forest in his study of data smoothing in the nineteenth century and was further pursued by Isaac J. Schoenberg and Thomas N. E. Greville. In the 1960’s, spurred by advancements in scientific computing, the study was reinvigorated by its application to numerical solutions to partial differential equations. The article
[
8
]
provides a full account of this history and references to the literature. Regarding recent developments, mostly in the context of one spatial dimension, we encourage the reader to see the articles
[
8
,
20
,
7

]
. Concerning global space-time estimates, the article
[

]
establishes Gaussian and sub-Gaussian estimates for the convolution powers of finitely-supported complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{Z}

[

and

3.2
is stated under weaker hypotheses than is the analogous result in
[
21
]
. Specifically, Theorem 1.4 of
[
21
]
is stated under the assumption that, given
\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
with
\sup_{\xi}|\widehat{\phi}(\xi)|=1
, every point
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi)
is of positive homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
and, in this case, the theorem gives positive constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
, for which

C^{\prime}n^{-\mu_{\phi}}\leq\absolutevalue{\phi^{(n)}(x)}\leq Cn^{-\mu_{\phi}}

(9)

for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Though we have not stated it this way, our proof of Theorem
3.2
(see Lemma
3.3
), guarantees the upper estimate in (
9
) (with
K=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) in the case that there are no points
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi)
of imaginary homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
. It is the presence of points
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi)
of imaginary homogeneous type that makes the analysis significantly more difficult (even in one dimension) and leads to the slightly weaker conclusion. It is our belief that a uniform estimate of the type (
9
) is valid when all points are either of positive homogeneous or imaginary homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
(perhaps still with some restriction on homogeneous order) but a resolution of such a conjecture will require further analysis and a thorough study of local limits. In Subsection
3.1
, we give several examples illustrating the conclusion of Theorem
3.2
, none of which satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 1.4 of
[
21
]
.

Our proof of Theorem
3.2
will make use of the Fourier inversion formula

\phi^{(n)}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi

(10)

which is valid for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; here
\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d}=\mathbb{T}^{d}+\xi_{\phi}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a representation of the
d
-dimensional torus chosen so that
\Omega(\phi)\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d})
; this can always be arranged, i.e., some
\xi_{\phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be selected, because
\Omega(\phi)
is a finite set (see Remark 3 of
[
21
]
). As discussed in
[
21
]
, the asymptotic behavior of
\phi^{(n)}
is characterized by the contributions to the above integral produced by integration over neighborhoods of points
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi).
Specifically, we shall study integrals of the form

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi

(11)

where
\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}
is some (small and to be determined) neighborhood of
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi)
. When
\xi_{0}
is of positive homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
, such integrals are very well behaved (the integrand is dominated uniformly by
e^{-nR_{\xi_{0}}(\xi)/2}
, a member of the Schwartz class). When
\xi_{0}
is of imaginary homogeneous type, such integrals are oscillatory in nature and therefore much more difficult to handle. Our first lemma below handles the “easy” case in which
\xi_{0}
is of positive homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
. This lemma appears, essentially, as Lemma 4.3 of
[
21
]
. For illustrative purposes, we have decided to present a distinct proof here which makes use of the polar coordinate integration formula in Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi)
be of positive homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
with homogeneous order
\mu_{\xi_{0}}
. Then, there exists an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathbb{T}^{d}_{\phi})
of
\xi_{0}
, which can be taken as small as desired, and a constant
C=C_{\xi_{0}}
for which

\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi\right|\leq C_{\xi_{0}}n^{-\mu_{\xi_{0}}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
R=R_{\xi_{0}}
,
\widetilde{R}=\widetilde{R}_{\xi_{0}}
and
\mu=\mu_{\xi_{0}}
. Given that
\xi_{0}
is of positive homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
, there is an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
0
for which

\left|\widehat{\phi}(\xi+\xi_{0})\right|=\left|\widehat{\phi}(\xi_{0})e^{\Gamma_{\xi_{0}}(\xi)}\right|=e^{-\left(R(\xi)+\widetilde{R}(\xi)\right)}

for
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
. Using the fact that
\widetilde{R}(\xi)=o(R(\xi))
as
\xi\to 0
in view of Proposition
2.7
, we can further restrict
\mathcal{U}
so that

\left|\widehat{\phi}(\xi+\xi_{0})\right|\leq e^{-R(\xi)/2}

for all
\xi\in\mathcal{U}
. Take
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(R)
and let
\sigma_{R}
be the surface measure on
S=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:R(\eta)=1\}
guaranteed by Theorem
1.3
. We fix an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}
of
\xi_{0}
which is as small as desired and has the property that

\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}-\xi_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{U}.

With this, we observe that

\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi+\xi_{0})e^{-ix\cdot(\xi+\xi_{0})}\,d\xi

(12)

and therefore

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left|\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi+\xi_{0})e^{-ix\cdot(\xi+\xi_{0})}\right|\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{U}}e^{-nR(\xi)/2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(n/2)R(\xi)}\,d\xi

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. By virtue of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-(n/2)R(\xi)}\,d\xi=\int_{S}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(n/2)r}r^{\mu-1}\,dr\,\sigma_{R}(d\eta)=\int_{S}\frac{2^{\mu}\Gamma(\mu)}{n^{\mu}}\,\sigma_{R}(d\eta)=2^{\mu}\Gamma(\mu)\sigma_{R}(S)n^{-\mu}

where
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function. Consequently,

\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi\right|\leq Cn^{-\mu}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
where
C=2^{\mu}\Gamma(\mu)\sigma_{R}(S)/(2\pi)^{d}.

∎

We shall now focus on the case in which
\xi_{0}\in\Omega(\phi)
is of imaginary homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
. As discussed above, (
11
) is oscillatory in nature; this is due to the fact that the “principal” behavior of
\Gamma_{\xi_{0}}(\xi)
, for small
\xi
, is characterized by the purely imaginary polynomial
iQ_{\xi_{0}}
. Our main estimate is presented in Lemma
3.7
and its proof makes use of (
4
) and the following version of the Van der Corput lemma.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
g\in C^{1}([a,b])
be complex-valued and
f\in C^{2}([a,b])
be real-valued and such that
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in[a,b]
. Then

\absolutevalue{\int_a^b e^{if(x)}g(x)\,dx}\leq\min\left\{\frac{4}{\lambda_{1}},\frac{8}{\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}}\right\}\left(\|g\|_{L^{\infty}[a,b]}+\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}[a,b]}\right).

where
\lambda_{1}=\inf_{x\in[a,b]}\absolutevalue{f'(x)}
and
\lambda_{2}=\inf_{x\in[a,b]}\absolutevalue{f''(x)}
.

For a proof of the above proposition, we refer the reader to Chapter 8 of
[
25
]
or Section 3 of
[
20
]
(see Lemma 3.4 therein). To effectively make use of the proposition above to estimate (
11
) in the case that
\xi_{0}
is of imaginary homogeneous type, we first treat two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function for which
\absolutevalue{Q}
is positive homogeneous with
\mu:=\mu_{\absolutevalue{Q}}<1
. Given a compact subset
S
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which
0\notin S
, set

\rho=\inf_{\eta\in S}|Q(\eta)|/3>0.

For an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, suppose that
\widetilde{Q}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice continuously differentiable function which is strongly subhomogeneous with respect to
E
of order
2
, set
F=E/\mu
, and define

\displaystyle f_{n,\eta,x}(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-nQ(\theta^{F}\eta)-n\widetilde{Q}(\theta^{F}\eta)-x\cdot\theta^{F}\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n\theta^{1/\mu}Q(\eta)-n\widetilde{Q}(\theta^{F}\eta)-x\cdot\theta^{F}\eta

for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\theta>0
sufficiently small so that
\theta^{F}\eta\in\mathcal{O}
. Then, given any compact set
K
, there is a
\delta>0
for which
\partial_{\theta}^{2}f_{n,x,\eta}(\theta)\neq 0
and

|\partial_{\theta}f_{n,x,\eta}(\theta)|\geq\frac{\rho}{\mu}n^{\mu}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in K
, and
\theta>0
for which
n^{-\mu}\leq\theta\leq\delta^{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let
E
and
S
be as in the statement of the lemma and write
f=f_{n,\eta,x}
. Because
{\theta^{F}}
is contracting, let
\delta_{1}>0
be such that

\absolutevalue{x\cdot\theta^F F \eta}\leq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\absolutevalue{x\cdot\theta^F (F-I)F\eta}\leq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)

(13)

for all
0<\theta<\delta_{1}^{\mu}
,
x\in K
and
\eta\in S
. By virtue of Proposition
2.8
, there exists
\delta_{2}>0
such that

\absolutevalue{\theta\partial_\theta\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)}\leq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\theta^{1/\mu}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\absolutevalue{\theta^2\partial_{\theta}^2\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)}\leq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\theta^{1/\mu}

(14)

for all
0<\theta<\delta_{2}^{\mu}
and
\eta\in S
. Set
\delta=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
. By virtue (
13
) and (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\theta^2\partial_{\theta}^2 f(\theta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\absolutevalue{n\partial_\theta^2\left( \theta^{1/\mu}Q(\eta)+\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)\right)+\partial_\theta^2\left(x\cdot\theta^F\eta\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\frac{n}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\theta^{1/\mu}Q(\eta)+n\theta^2\partial_\theta^2\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)+\left(x\cdot\theta^F(F-I)F\eta\right)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\theta^{1/\mu}\absolutevalue{Q(\eta)}-n\absolutevalue{\theta^2\partial_\theta^2\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)}-\absolutevalue{x\cdot\theta^F(F-I)F\eta}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3\rho n}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\theta^{1/\mu}-\frac{\rho n}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\theta^{1/\mu}-\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)\left(2n\theta^{1/\mu}-1\right)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in K
and
0<\theta<\delta^{\mu}
. Given that
\theta\mapsto\theta^{1/\mu}
is increasing, it follows that

\absolutevalue{\theta^2\partial_\theta^2 f(\theta)}\geq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)(2n\theta^{1/\mu}-1)\geq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)(2n(n^{-\mu})^{1/\mu}-1)=\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right)>0

and, in particular,
\partial_{\theta}^{2}f(\theta)\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in K
and
\theta>0
for which
n^{-\mu}\leq\theta\leq\delta^{\mu}
. By another appeal to (
13
) and (
14
), we find

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\partial_\theta f(\theta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{n\partial_\theta\left(\theta^{1/\mu}Q(\eta)+\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)\right)+\partial_\theta\left(x\cdot\theta^F \eta\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\frac{n}{\mu}\theta^{1/\mu-1}Q(\eta)+n\partial_\theta\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)+\theta^{-1}\left(x\cdot\theta^F F\eta\right)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3\rho n}{\mu}\theta^{1/\mu-1}-n\theta^{-1}\absolutevalue{\theta\partial_\theta\widetilde{Q}(\theta^F\eta)}-\theta^{-1}\absolutevalue{x\cdot\theta^F F\eta}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3\rho n}{\mu}\theta^{1/\mu-1}-\frac{\rho n}{\mu}\theta^{1/\mu-1}-\frac{\rho}{\mu}\theta^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(2n\theta^{1/\mu-1}-\theta^{-1}\right)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in K
and
0<\theta<\delta^{\mu}
. Given our supposition that
\mu<1
,
\theta\mapsto\left(2n\theta^{1/\mu-1}-\theta^{-1}\right)
is increasing for
\theta>0
and therefore

\absolutevalue{\partial_\theta f(\theta)}\geq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(2n\theta^{1/\mu-1}-\theta^{-1}\right)\geq\frac{\rho}{\mu}\left(2n(n^{-\mu})^{1/\mu-1}-(n^{-\mu})^{-1}\right)=\frac{\rho}{\mu}n^{\mu}

for all for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in K
and
\theta>0
for which
n^{-\mu}\leq\theta\leq\delta^{\mu}
, as was asserted.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a positive homogeneous function with
G\in\operatorname{Exp}(R)
,
\widetilde{R}:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}
be once continuously differentiable on a neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
0
which has
\widetilde{R}(0)=0
and is strongly subhomogeneous with respect to
G
of order
1
, and let
k
and
\mu
be positive real numbers. Set
F=(k/\mu)G
and, for each
\eta\in S=\{\eta:R(\eta)=1\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, set

g_{n,\eta}(\theta)=e^{-n\left(R\left(\theta^{F}\eta\right)+\widetilde{R}\left(\theta^{F}\eta\right)\right)}

for
\theta>0
which is sufficiently small so that
\theta^{F}\eta\in\mathcal{O}
. Then, for each
\beta>1
, there is
\delta>0
for which

\|g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}\leq 1

and

\|\partial_{\theta}g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{1}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}\leq\beta

uniformly for
\eta\in S
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq\delta^{\mu}
.

Proof.

By virtue of the strong subhomogeneity of
\widetilde{R}
, Proposition
2.6
, and the fact that
r^{G}
is a contracting group, we may choose
\delta>0
for which

R(r^{G}\eta)+\widetilde{R}\left(r^{G}\eta\right)=r+\widetilde{R}\left(r^{G}\eta\right)\geq(1-\epsilon)r>0

(15)

and

\absolutevalue{\partial_r\left(R(r^G\eta)+\widetilde{R}(r^G\eta)\right)}\leq 1+\epsilon

(16)

for all
0<r<\delta^{k}
and
\eta\in S
where

\epsilon=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta+1}\in(0,1).

In view of (
15
), for any
0<\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}<\delta^{\mu}
,
\eta\in S
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\|g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}\leq\sup_{0<\theta\leq\delta^{\mu}}\absolutevalue{g_{n,\eta}(\theta)}=\sup_{0<r\leq\delta^{k}}\absolutevalue{g_{n,\eta}(r^{\mu/k})}\leq\sup_{0<r\leq\delta^{k}}e^{-nr(1-\epsilon)}=1

where we have used the fact that
(r^{\mu/k})^{F}=r^{(\mu/k)F}=r^{G}
for
r>0
. By virtue of (
15
) and (
16
), we find that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\theta}g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{1}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\absolutevalue{\p_\theta g_{n,\eta}(\theta)}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}^{k/\mu}}^{\theta_{2}^{k/\mu}}\absolutevalue{\p_r \lp g_{n,\eta}(r^{\mu/k})\rp}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}^{k/\mu}}^{\theta_{2}^{k/\mu}}\absolutevalue{\p_r \left(e^{-n\left(R(r^G\eta)+\widetilde{R}(r^G\eta)\right)}\right) }\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}^{k/\mu}}^{\theta_{2}^{k/\mu}}n\absolutevalue{\partial_r\left(R(r^G\eta)+\widetilde{R}(r^G\eta)\right)}\absolutevalue{e^{-nR(r^G\eta)+\widetilde{R}(r^G\eta)}}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\theta_{1}^{k/\mu}}^{\theta_{2}^{k/\mu}}n(1+\epsilon)e^{-n(1-\epsilon)r}\,dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}\,dr=\beta

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
and
0<\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}\leq\delta^{\mu}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose that
\xi_{0}
is of imaginary homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
with associated drift
\alpha_{\xi_{0}}
and homogeneous order
\mu_{\xi_{0}}

\alpha_{\xi_{0}}=0
and
\mu_{\xi_{0}}<1

\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{0}}\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d})
of
\xi_{0}
, which can be taken as small as desired, and a constant

for which

\absolutevalue{\f{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi_0}}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi}\leq C_{\xi_{0}}n^{-\mu_{\xi_{0}}}

and

.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, we will write
Q=Q_{\xi_{0}}
,
\widetilde{Q}=\widetilde{Q}_{\xi_{0}}
,
R=R_{\xi_{0}}
,
\widetilde{R}=\widetilde{R}_{\xi_{0}}
and
\mu=\mu_{\xi_{0}}
. We fix a compact set

and let
E
and
k
as given in Definition
3.1
. In studying the proof of Lemma
3.3
and (
12
), in particular, it is evident that we may assume
\xi_{0}=0
and
\widehat{\phi}(0)=1
without loss of generality. Given that

, set

Using the positive homogeneous structure of
R
, let
\sigma_{R}
be the measure on

as guaranteed by Theorem

. By setting

\rho=\inf_{\eta\in S}|Q(\eta)|/3,

an appeal to Lemma
3.5
guarantees a

for which

\absolutevalue{\partial_{\theta}f_{n,\eta,x}(\theta)}\geq\frac{\rho}{\mu}n^{\mu}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\partial_{\theta}^{2}f_{n,\eta,x}(\theta)\neq 0

(17)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
,
x\in K
and
\theta>0
for which
n^{-\mu}\leq\theta\leq\delta_{1}^{\mu}
. An appeal to Lemma
3.6
guarantees
\delta_{2}>0
for which

\|g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}+\|\partial_{\theta}g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{1}[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]}\leq 3

(18)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\eta\in S
and

where

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S}\int_{0}^{\delta^{k}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(r^{G}\eta)e^{-ix\cdot r^{G}\eta}r^{\mu/k-1}\,dr\sigma_{R}(d\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{\mu}\int_{S}\int_{0}^{\delta^{\mu}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\theta^{F}\eta)e^{-ix\cdot\theta^{F}\eta}\,d\theta\,\sigma_{R}(d\eta)

\displaystyle\frac{k}{\mu}\int_{S}I_{n,x}(\eta)\,\sigma_{R}(d\eta)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta^{\mu}}\widehat{\phi}^{n}(\theta^{F}\eta)e^{-ix\cdot\theta^{F}\eta}\,d\theta.

,

, and

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\int_{n^{-\mu}}^{\delta^{\mu}}\widehat{\phi}^n(\theta^F\eta)e^{-ix\cdot\theta^F\eta}\,d\theta}+\int_{0}^{n^{-\mu}}\absolutevalue{\widehat{\phi}^n(\theta^F\eta)}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\int_{n^{-\mu}}^{\delta^\mu} e^{-i\left(nQ\left(\theta^F\eta\right)+n\widetilde{Q}\left(\theta^F\eta\right)+x\cdot\theta^F\eta\right)}e^{-n\left(R\left(\theta^F\eta\right)+\widetilde{R}\left(\theta^F\eta\right)\right)}\,d\theta}+n^{-\mu}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\absolutevalue{\int_{n^{-\mu}}^{\delta^\mu} e^{i f_{n,\eta,x}(\theta) } g_{n,\eta}(\theta)\,d\theta}+n^{-\mu}.

In view of (
17
) and (

), an appeal to Proposition
3.4
guarantees that, for any

,

and

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\absolutevalue{\int_{n^{-\mu}}^{\delta^\mu}e^{if_{n,\eta,x}(\theta)}g_{n,\eta}(\theta)\,d\theta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\frac{\|g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{\infty}[n^{-\mu},\delta^{\mu}]}+\|\partial_{\theta}g_{n,\eta}\|_{L^{1}[n^{-\mu},\delta^{\mu}]}}{\inf_{n^{-\mu}\leq\theta\leq\delta^{\delta}}|\partial_{\theta}f_{n,x,\eta}(\theta)|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\frac{3}{(\rho/\mu)n^{\mu}}=\frac{12\mu}{\rho}n^{-\mu}

and so

\absolutevalue{I_{n,x}(\eta)}\leq\left(\frac{12\mu}{\rho}\right)n^{-\mu}+n^{-\mu}\leq\left(\frac{12\mu}{\rho}+1\right)n^{-\mu}.

Thus, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and

,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\f{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\,d\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{k}{\mu}\absolutevalue{\int_S I_{n,x}(\eta)\,\sigma_R(d\eta)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{k}{\mu}\int_{S}\absolutevalue{I_{n,x}(\eta)}\,\sigma_{R}(d\eta)

\displaystyle Cn^{-\mu}

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. As we discussed in the paragraph preceding the theorem, the set
\Omega(\phi)
is finite and so we may write

\Omega(\phi)=\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{N},\xi_{N+1},\xi_{N+2},\dots,\xi_{M}\}

where our labeling assumes that the points
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{N}
are of imaginary homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
and the points
\xi_{N+1},\xi_{N+2},\dots,\xi_{M}
are of positive homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
. In view of the theorem’s hypotheses, for each
j=1,2,\dots,N
, the point
\xi_{j}
, which is of imaginary homogeneous type for
\widehat{\phi}
, has drift
\alpha_{\xi_{j}}=0
and homogeneous order
\mu_{j}:=\mu_{\xi_{j}}<1
. Thus, for each

, an appeal to Lemma
3.7
guarantees an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{j}}\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d})
of
\xi_{j}
and a constant
C_{j}=C_{\xi_{j}}
for which

\absolutevalue{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{O}_j}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi}\leq C_{j}n^{-\mu_{j}}

(19)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
x\in K
. For each
j=N+1,N+2,\dots M
, an appeal to Lemma
3.3
guarantees an open neighborhood
\mathcal{O}_{j}=\mathcal{O}_{\xi_{j}}\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d})
of
\xi_{j}
and a constant
C_{j}=C_{\xi_{j}}
for which

\absolutevalue{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{O}_j}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\,d\xi}\leq C_{j}n^{-\mu_{j}}

(20)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
where
\mu_{j}:=\mu_{\xi_{j}}
is the homogeneous order associated to
\xi_{j}
. As guaranteed by the lemmas, let us take this collection of open sets
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{M}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\phi}^{d}

\mathcal{G}
is a closed set which contains no elements of
\Omega(\phi)

By virtue of (
10
), (
19
), (
20
), and the disjointness of the collection
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{M}

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\phi^{(n)}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\lb\sum_{j=1}^M\frac{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{O}_j}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-x\cdot\xi}\,d\xi\rb+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{G}}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-x\cdot\xi}\,d\xi}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{M}\absolutevalue{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{O}_j}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-x\cdot\xi}\,d\xi}+\absolutevalue{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^d}\int_{\mathcal{G}}\widehat{\phi}^n(\xi)e^{-x\cdot\xi}\,d\xi}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{M}C_{j}n^{-\mu_{j}}+s^{n}

for all

and

4.1
Construction of
\sigma_{P,E}

Throughout this section, we fix

. Define
\psi_{E}:(0,\infty)\times S\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
by

\psi_{E}(r,\eta)=r^{E}\eta

(28)

for
r>0
and
\eta\in S
. As
\psi_{E}
is the restriction of the continuous function
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni(r,x)\mapsto r^{E}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
(0,\infty)\times S
, it is necessarily continuous. As the following proposition shows,
\psi_{E}
is, in fact, a homeomorphism.

Proposition 4.1
.

The map
\psi_{E}:(0,\infty)\times S\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
is a homeomorphism with continuous inverse
\psi_{E}^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to(0,\infty)\times S
given by

\psi_{E}^{-1}(x)=(P(x),(P(x))^{-E}x)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Given that
P
is continuous and positive definite,
P(x)>0
for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and the map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\ni x\mapsto(P(x))^{-E}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous. Further, in view of the homogeneity of
P
,

P\left((P(x))^{-E}x\right)=P(x)^{-1}P(x)=1

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. It follows from these two observations that

\rho(x)=(P(x),(P(x))^{-E}x),

defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, is a continuous function taking
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
into
(0,\infty)\times S
. We have

(\psi_{E}\circ\rho)(x)=\psi_{E}(P(x),(P(x))^{-E}x)=(P(x))^{E}(P(x))^{-E}x=x

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and

(\rho\circ\psi_{E})(r,\eta)=\rho(r^{E}\eta)=(P(r^{E}\eta),(P(r^{E}\eta))^{-E}(r^{E}\eta))=(rP(\eta),(rP(\eta))^{-E}(r^{E}\eta))=(r,\eta)

for every
(r,\eta)\in(0,\infty)\times S
. Thus
\rho
is a (continuous) inverse for
\psi_{E}
and so it follows that
\psi_{E}
is a homeomorphism and
\rho=\psi_{E}^{-1}
.
∎

We shall now construct the

We shall denote by

the collection of subsets

, i.e.,

Proposition 4.2
.

\Sigma_{P,E}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
S
containing the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
S
,
\mathcal{B}(S)
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we write
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P,E}
and
\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F_{E}}
for each

.
We first show that
\Sigma
is a
\sigma
-algebra. Since

and therefore Lebesgue measurable. Hence

. Let

\widetilde{F\setminus G}=\bigcup_{0<r<1}r^{E}\left(F\setminus G\right)=\bigcup_{0<r<1}\left(r^{E}F\setminus r^{E}G\right)=\left(\bigcup_{0<r<1}r^{E}F\right)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{0<r<1}r^{E}G\right)=\widetilde{F}\setminus\widetilde{G}

where we have used the fact that the collection

\widetilde{\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}}=\bigcup_{0<r<1}r^{E}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}\right)=\bigcup_{0<r<1}\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}r^{E}F_{n}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{0<r<1}r^{E}F_{n}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{F_{n}}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}

Throughout the proof, we will write

Lemma 4.5

Let

as desired.

Lemma 4.8
.

Every open subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
can be written as a countable union of open sets of the form
\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U})
where
\mathcal{U}=I\times\mathcal{O}
is an open rectangle in
(0,\infty)\times S
.

Proof.

Let
\{r_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be countably dense subsets of
(0,\infty)
and
S
, respectively. For each triple of natural numbers
j,l,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, consider the open set

\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n}=\{|r-r_{j}|<1/n\}\times\mathcal{O}_{l,n}\subseteq(0,\infty)\times S

where

\mathcal{O}_{l,n}=\{\eta\in S:|\eta-\eta_{l}|<1/n\}.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
be open. We will show that

U=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,l,n\\
\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})\subseteq U\end{subarray}}\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n}),

(33)

where, in view of Proposition
4.1
, each
\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})
is open. It is clear that any element of the union on the right hand side of (
33
) belongs to some
\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})\subseteq U
and so the union is a subset of
U
. To prove (
33
), it therefore suffices to prove that, for each
x\in U
, there exists a triple
j,l,n
with

x\in\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})\subseteq U.

To this end, fix
x\in U
and let
\delta>0
be such that
\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x)\subseteq U
. Consider
(r_{x},\eta_{x})=\psi_{E}^{-1}(x)\in(0,\infty)\times S
and set
M=\|r_{x}^{E}\|>0
and
C=\|E\|>0
. Observe that

\|I-\alpha^{E}\|=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\ln\alpha)^{k}}{k!}E^{k}\right\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\ln\alpha|^{k}}{k!}\|E\|^{k}=e^{(C|\ln\alpha|)}-1

for all
\alpha>0
. Since
\alpha\mapsto e^{(C|\ln\alpha|)}-1
is continuous and
0
at
\alpha=1
, we can choose
\delta^{\prime}>0
for which

\|I-\alpha^{E}\|<\frac{\delta}{2M(|\eta_{x}|+2)}

whenever
|\alpha-1|<\delta^{\prime}
. Fix an integer
n>\max\left\{1/\delta^{\prime}r_{x},4M/\delta\right\}
and, using the density of the collections
\{r_{j}\}
and
\{\eta_{l}\}
, let
r_{j}
and
\eta_{l}
be such that
\absolutevalue{r_j - r_x }<1/n
and
\absolutevalue{\eta_l - \eta_x}<1/n
. It follows that the corresponding open set
\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n}
contains
\psi_{E}^{-1}(x)
, or, equivalently,
x\in\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})
. Thus, it remains to show that
\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x)
. To this end, let
y=\psi_{E}(r_{y},\eta_{y})\in\psi_{E}(\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n})
and observe that

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\psi_{E}(r_{x},\eta_{x},)-\psi_{E}(r_{x},\eta_{y})|+|\psi_{E}(r_{x},\eta_{y})-\psi_{E}(r_{y},\eta_{y})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|r_{x}^{E}(\eta_{x}-\eta_{y})|+|(r_{x}^{E}-r_{y}^{E})\eta_{y}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M|\eta_{x}-\eta_{y}|+\|{r_{x}^{E}-r_{y}^{E}}\||\eta_{y}|.

Since both
(\eta_{x},r_{x}),(\eta_{y},r_{y})\in\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n}

|\eta_{x}-\eta_{y}|\leq|\eta_{x}-\eta_{j}|+|\eta_{j}-\eta_{y}|<\frac{2}{n}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pt|\eta_{y}|\leq|\eta_{y}-\eta_{x}|+|\eta_{x}|<|\eta_{x}|+\frac{2}{n}.

Also, since
|r_{x}-r_{y}|<1/n

r_{y}=\alpha r_{x}
where
|1-\alpha|<1/nr_{x}<\delta^{\prime}
by our choice of

\displaystyle|x-y|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}M+\left(|\eta_{x}|+\frac{2}{n}\right)\|{r_{x}^{E}-r_{x}^{E}\alpha^{E}}\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}M+\left(|\eta_{x}|+2\right)M\|I-\alpha^{E}\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}M+\frac{\delta M\left(|\eta_{x}|+2\right)}{2M(|\eta_{x}|+2)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}=\delta

and so we have established (

\{\mathcal{U}_{j,l,n}\}_{(j,l,n)\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{3}}
is a countable collection of open rectangles, the union in (
33
) is necessarily countable and we are done with the proof.

In our final lemma preceding the proof of Theorem
4.4
, we treat a general measure-theoretic statement which gives sufficient conditions concerning two measure spaces to ensure that their completions are isomorphic. Though we suspect that this result is well-known, we present its proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(X_{1},\Sigma_{1},\nu_{1})
and
(X_{2},\Sigma_{2},\nu_{2})
be measure spaces, let
\varphi:X_{1}\to X_{2}
be a bijection and denote by
(X_{i},\Sigma_{i}^{\prime},\nu_{i}^{\prime})
the completion of the measure space
(X_{i},\Sigma_{i},\nu_{i})
for
i=1,2
. Assume that the following two properties are satisfied:

1.

For each
A_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}
,
\varphi(A_{1})\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
and
\nu_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi(A_{1}))=\nu_{1}(A_{1}).

2.

For each

\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
and
\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(A_{2}))=\nu_{2}(A_{2})
.

Then the measure spaces
(X_{1},\Sigma_{1}^{\prime},\nu_{1}^{\prime})
and
(X_{2},\Sigma_{2}^{\prime},\nu_{2}^{\prime})

\varphi
. Specifically,

\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}=\{A_{2}\subseteq X_{2}:\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}\}

(34)

and

\nu_{2}^{\prime}(A_{2})=\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(A_{2}))

(35)

for all
A_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
A_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
. By definition,
A_{2}=G_{2}\cup H_{2}
where
G_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}
and
H_{2}\subseteq G_{2,0}\in\Sigma_{2}
with
\nu_{2}^{\prime}(A_{2})=\nu_{2}(G_{2})
and
\nu_{2}^{\prime}(H_{2})=\nu_{2}(G_{2,0})=0
. Consequently,
\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})=\varphi^{-1}(G_{2})\cup\varphi^{-1}(H_{2})
and
\varphi^{-1}(H_{2})\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(G_{2,0})
. In view of Property
2
,
\varphi^{-1}(G_{2}),\varphi^{-1}(G_{2,0})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
and we have

\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(G_{2}))=\nu_{2}(G_{2})=\nu_{2}^{\prime}(A_{2})\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(G_{2,0}))=\nu_{2}(G_{2,0})=0.

In view of the fact that
(X_{1}^{\prime},\Sigma_{1}^{\prime},\nu_{1}^{\prime})
is complete,
\varphi^{-1}(H_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
and
\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(H_{2}))=0
. Consequently, we obtain
\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})=\varphi^{-1}(G_{2})\cup\varphi^{-1}(H_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
and

\nu_{2}^{\prime}(A_{2})=\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(G_{2}))\leq\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(A_{2}))\leq\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(G_{2}))+\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(H_{2}))=\nu_{2}(G_{2})+0=\nu_{2}^{\prime}(A_{2}).

From this we obtain that
\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq\{A_{2}\subseteq X_{2}:\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}\}
and, for each
A_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
,
\nu_{2}^{\prime}(A_{2})=\nu_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(A_{2}))
. It remains to prove that

\{A_{2}\subseteq X_{2}:\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}\}\subseteq\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}.

To this end, let
A_{2}
be a subset of
X_{2}
for which
\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})\in\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
. By the definition of
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
, we have
\varphi^{-1}(A_{2})=G_{1}\cup H_{1}
where
G_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}
,
H_{1}\subseteq G_{1,0}\in\Sigma_{1}
and
\nu_{1}^{\prime}(H_{1})=\nu_{1}(G_{1,0})=0
. In view of Property
1
,
\varphi(G_{1})\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
,
\varphi(H_{1})\subseteq\varphi(G_{1,0})\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
and
\nu_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi(G_{1,0}))=\nu_{1}(G_{1,0})=0
. Because
(X_{2}^{\prime},\Sigma_{2}^{\prime},\nu_{2}^{\prime})
is complete, we have
\varphi(H_{1})\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
and so

A_{1}=\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(A_{2}))=\varphi(G_{1})\cup\varphi(H_{1})\in\Sigma_{2}^{\prime},

as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.10

We have:

(40)

Remark 2
.

This proposition can be seen as an application of Proposition
4.2
and Theorem 2.18 of
[
23
]
. The proof we give here is distinct and, we believe, nicely illustrates the utility of (

) of Theorem
4.4

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we shall write
\sigma=\sigma_{P,E}
,
\Sigma=\Sigma_{P,E}
and, for each
F\subseteq S
,
\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F_{E}}
. We remark that, by standard arguments using
G_{\delta}
and
F_{\sigma}
sets, Item
1
follows immediately from Item
2
. Also, given that
S
is compact and
\sigma
is finite, it suffices to prove (
39

and

of

To this end, let

and

. Given that
\widetilde{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
and

\widetilde{F}\subseteq U
and

. Since
\widetilde{F}
is a subset of the open set
B\setminus\{0\}
, we may assume without loss of generality that

(41)

For each

S

F

Lemma 4.12

g_{n,j}(r)=\int_{S}\phi_{n}(r^{E_{j}}\eta)\,\sigma_{j}(d\eta).

Proof.

First, we note that, for each

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{det}\left.\left(E\varphi^{-1}(u)\right|D_{u}\varphi^{-1}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}})_{\varphi^{-1}(u)}(\mathcal{E}_{E},\iota_{*}(\partial_{u^{1}}),\iota_{*}(\partial_{u^{2}}),\dots,\iota_{*}(\partial_{u^{d^{\prime}}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(d\sigma_{P}\right)_{\varphi^{-1}(u)}(\partial_{u^{1}},\partial_{u^{2}},\dots,\partial_{u^{d^{\prime}}})

for
u\in U
and so (
49
) is satisfied.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
g\in L^{1}(S,\Sigma_{P},\sigma_{P})
be supported on the domain of some chart on
S
. Then, for any
(\mathcal{U},\varphi)\in\mathcal{A}^{+}(S)
such that
\operatorname{Supp}(g)\subseteq\mathcal{U}
, the pushforward
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(g)=g\circ\varphi^{-1}
is Lebesgue measurable on
U=\varphi(\mathcal{U})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
and

\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\int_{U}(\psi^{-1})^{*}(g\,d\sigma_{P}).

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\varphi)
be a chart on
S
for which
\operatorname{Supp}(g)\subseteq\mathcal{U}
and assume the notation of Lemma
5.5
. Given
E\in\operatorname{Exp}(P)
, observe that

f(x)=\mu_{P}\,\chi_{(0,1)}(P(x))g(P(x)^{-E}x),

defined for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, is supported on
\widetilde{\mathcal{U}_{E}}=\rho_{E,\varphi}(V)
. An appeal to Corollary
1.5
guarantees that
f
is absolutely integrable on
\rho_{E,\varphi}(V)
and

\int_{S}g(\eta)\,\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\int_{\rho_{E,\varphi}(V)}f(x)\,dx.

(50)

Given that
\rho_{E,\varphi}
is a diffeomorphism, an appeal to Theorem 15.11 of
[
2
]
guarantees that

\int_{\rho_{E,\varphi}(V)}f(x)\,dx=\int_{V}f(\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|\operatorname{det}(D\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|\,dy;

(51)

in particular,
V\ni y\mapsto f(\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|\operatorname{det}(D\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))
is Lebesgue measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let us now view the Lebesgue measure
dy
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the completion of the product measure
dr\times du
on the product space
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
. By virtue of Lemma
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle f(\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|\operatorname{det}(D\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r^{E}\varphi^{-1}(u))\,r^{\mu_{P}-1}|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{P}\,\chi_{(0,1)}(r)g(\varphi^{-1}(u))\,r^{\mu_{P}-1}|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{P}\,r^{\mu_{P}-1}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|

for
y=(r,u)\in V
. By virtue of Fubini’s theorem,
dr
-almost every
r\in(0,1)
, the
r
-section

U\ni u\mapsto\mu_{P}r^{\mu_{P}-1}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|

is Lebesgue measurable and, upon recalling that
h_{E,\varphi}
is smooth and everywhere nonzero, we conclude that
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(g)=g\circ\varphi^{-1}
is Lebesgue measurable on
U
. In view of (
52
), Fubini’s theorem also guarantees that

\displaystyle\int_{V}f(\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|\operatorname{det}(D\rho_{E,\varphi}(y))|\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}\int_{U}\mu_{P}\,r^{\mu_{P}-1}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|\,du\,dr

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mu_{P}\,r^{\mu_{P}-1}\left(\int_{U}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|\,du\right)\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|\,du

By combining (
50
), (
51
) and (

), we have shown that
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}g=g\circ\varphi^{-1}
is Lebesgue measurable on
U=\varphi(\mathcal{U})
and

\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\int_{U}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|\,du.

Finally, if
(\mathcal{U},\varphi)\in\mathcal{A}_{+}(S)
, an appeal to Proposition
5.2
and (
49
) of Lemma
5.5
guarantees that

|h_{E,\varphi}(u)|=h_{E,\varphi}(u)=(d\sigma_{P})_{\varphi^{-1}(u)}(\partial_{u^{1}},\partial_{u^{2}},\dots,\partial_{u^{d^{\prime}}})>0

for all

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))h_{E,\varphi}(u)\,du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}g(\varphi^{-1}(u))\,h_{E,\varphi}(u)\,du^{1}\wedge du^{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge du^{d^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(g\cdot d\sigma_{P}),

as desired.
∎

Remark 3
.

In studying the proof of Lemma
5.6
, we deduce the (slightly) more general statement: Given any chart
(\mathcal{U},\varphi)\in\mathcal{A}(S)
and
g\in L^{1}(S,\Sigma_{P},\sigma_{P})
for which
\operatorname{Supp}(g)\in\mathcal{U}
,
(\varphi^{-1})^{*}g
is Lebesgue measurable on
U=\varphi(\mathcal{U})
and

\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U}(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(g\,d\sigma_{P})&\mbox{ if }(\mathcal{U},\varphi)\in\mathcal{A}_{+}(S)\\
&\\
-\displaystyle\int_{U}(\varphi^{-1})^{*}(g\,d\sigma_{P})&\mbox{ if }(\mathcal{U},\varphi)\in\mathcal{A}_{-}(S)\coloneqq\mathcal{A}(S)\setminus\mathcal{A}_{+}(S).\end{cases}

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

In view of Proposition
5.1
and the discussion following its proof, it remains to prove the assertions in the last two sentences in the statement of the theorem. Given any
F\in\Sigma_{P}
and chart
(\mathcal{U},\varphi)\subseteq\mathcal{A}(S)
, we have
\chi_{\varphi(F\cap\mathcal{U})}=(\varphi^{-1})^{*}\left(\chi_{F\cap\mathcal{U}}\right)
is Lebesgue measurable on
U=\varphi(\mathcal{U})
and so it follows (Exercise 4.6.2 of
[
19
]
) that
F\in\mathcal{L}(S)
. Consequently,
\mathcal{B}(S)\subseteq\Sigma_{P}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(S)
.

Let
g\in L^{1}(S,\Sigma_{P},\sigma_{P})
, which is necessarily Lebesgue measurable on
S
in view of the results of the previous paragraph. Now, let
\{(\mathcal{U}_{j},\varphi_{j})\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{+}(S)
be a countable atlas on
S
and let
\{\kappa_{j}\}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to the cover
\{\mathcal{U}_{j}\}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, observe that
\kappa_{j}g\in L^{1}(S,\Sigma_{P},\sigma_{P})
and has
\operatorname{Supp}(\kappa_{j}g)\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{j}
. By virtue of Lemma
5.6
, we have
(\varphi_{j}^{-1})^{*}(\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P})
is integrable on
U_{j}=\varphi(\mathcal{U}_{j})
and

\int_{S}\kappa_{j}(\eta)g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\int_{U_{j}}(\varphi_{j}^{-1})^{*}(\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P})

(54)

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
. With the help of Proposition
5.2
, it is easy to see that
g\,d\sigma_{P}
and
\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P}
(for
j\in\mathbb{N}
) are Lebesgue measurable
d^{\prime}
-forms on
S
. In view of (4.4.6) of
[
19
]
, (
54
) ensures that, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P}
is integrable (in the sense of forms) on
S
and

\int_{S}\kappa_{j}(\eta)g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\int_{S}\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P}.

By the monotone convergence theorem, it follows that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{S}\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P}\right|=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{S}\kappa_{j}(\eta)g(\eta)\sigma(d\eta)\right|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{S}\kappa_{j}(\eta)|g(\eta)|\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\|g\|_{L^{1}(S,\Sigma_{P},\sigma_{P})}<\infty.

Therefore, in view of the construction on p. 242 of
[
19
]
, we conclude that the
d^{\prime}
-form
g\,d\sigma_{P}
is integrable and

\int_{S}g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{S}\kappa_{j}(\eta)g(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{S}\kappa_{j}g\,d\sigma_{P}=\int_{S}g\,d\sigma_{P}

by virtue of the dominated convergence theorem; this is (
47
).

Finally, given that
\absolutevalue{\grad P}
is continuous and non-vanishing on the compact set
S
,

C_{1}:=\inf_{S}\frac{1}{|\gradient P|}\quad\mbox{and}\quad C_{2}:=\sup_{S}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\grad P }}

are both positive real numbers. For each
F\in\Sigma_{P}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(S)
, (
47
) guarantees that

\sigma_{P}(F)=\int_{S}\chi_{F}(\eta)\sigma_{P}(d\eta)=\int_{S}\chi_{F}\,d\sigma_{P}

By virtue of Proposition
5.2
, it follows that

C_{1}\operatorname{Vol}_{S}(F)=C_{1}\int_{S}\chi_{F}\,d\operatorname{Vol}_{S}\leq\int_{S}\frac{\chi_{F}}{|\gradient P|}d\operatorname{Vol}_{S}=\int_{S}\chi_{F}\,d\sigma_{P}=\sigma_{P}(F)

and

\sigma_{P}(F)=\int_{S}\chi_{F}d\sigma_{P}=\int_{S}\frac{\chi_{F}}{|\gradient P|}\,d\operatorname{Vol}_{S}\leq C_{2}\int_{S}\chi_{F}\,d\operatorname{Vol}_{S}=C_{2}\operatorname{Vol}_{S}(F)

where we have used the definition of the Riemannian volume measure on
S
, c.f.,
[
1
]
. In short, there are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
for which

C_{1}\operatorname{Vol}_{S}(F)\leq\sigma_{P}(F)\leq C_{2}\operatorname{Vol}_{S}(F)

(55)

for all
F\in\Sigma_{P}
. In particular, (
47
) holds for all
F\in\mathcal{B}(S)
and so it follows that the completions of the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(S)
with respect to
\sigma_{P}
and
\operatorname{Vol}_{S}
coincide. We know, however, that
\operatorname{Vol}_{S}
, which is defined on
\mathcal{L}(S)
, is a Radon measure (Proposition 1.5
[
1
, Chapter XII]
) and, by virtue of Proposition
4.10
, it follows that
\Sigma_{P}=\mathcal{L}(S)
and (
55
) holds for all
F
in this common
\sigma
-algebra. Thus
\sigma_{P}
and
\operatorname{Vol}_{S}
are mutually absolutely continuous and the theorem is proved.
∎

We immediately obtain the following corollary which allows us to compute the Lebesgue integral with respect to

in coordinates.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let

. Then, given any countable (or finite) atlas

This appendix amasses some facts about continuous one-parameter subgroups of

and, in particular, those which are contracting.

Proposition A.1

(see Section 8 of
[
21
]
)
.

Let
E,G\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
A\in\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Also, let
E^{*}
denote the adjoint of
E
. Then, for all
t,s>0
, the following statements hold:

\bullet

1^{E}=I

\bullet

t^{E^{*}}=(t^{E})^{*}

\bullet

(t^{E})^{-1}=t^{-E}

\bullet
If
EG=GE
, then
t^{E}t^{G}=t^{E+G}

\bullet

(st)^{E}=s^{E}t^{E}

\bullet

At^{E}A^{-1}=t^{AEA^{-1}}

\bullet

\operatorname{det}\left(t^{E}\right)=t^{\tr E}

We recall from the introduction that a continuous one-parameter group
\{T_{t}\}
is said to be contracting if
\lim_{t\to 0}\|T_{t}\|=0
. The notion of a contracting group (or semigroup) is closely related to notion of asymptotic stability of solutions to linear systems
[
5
]
. By virtue of the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, we have the following useful characterization of contracting groups.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\{T_{r}\}
be a continuous one-parameter group. Then
\{T_{r}\}
is contracting if and only if

\lim_{t\to 0}|T_{r}x|=0

(56)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

It is clear that (
56
) is a necessary condition for
\{T_{r}\}
to be contracting. We must therefore prove (
56
) also sufficient. To this end, we assume that the continuous one-parameter group
\{T_{r}\}
satisfies (
56
). By virtue of the continuity of
\{T_{r}\}
and (
56
), we have

\sup_{0<r\leq 1}|T_{r}x|<\infty

for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. From the Banach-Steinhaus theorem, it follows that
\|T_{r}\|\leq C
for all
0<r\leq 1
. Now, suppose that
\{T_{r}\}
is not contracting. In this case, one can find a sequence
\{\eta_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}
and a sequence
r_{n}\rightarrow 0
for which
\lim_{n}|T_{r_{n}}\eta_{n}|>0
. But because the unit sphere is compact,
\{\eta_{n}\}
has a convergence subsequence
\eta_{n_{k}}\rightarrow\eta
with
|\eta|=1
. Observe that, for all
n
,

|T_{r_{n}}(\eta-\eta_{n})|\leq C|\eta-\eta_{n}|

and so it follows that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|T_{t_{r_{k}}}\eta|=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|T_{r_{n_{k}}}\eta_{n_{k}}|>0,

a contradiction.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\{T_{r}\}\subseteq\mbox{Gl}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
be a continuous one-parameter group and let
E\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be its generator, i.e.,
T_{r}=r^{E}
for all
r>0
. If
\{T_{r}\}
is contracting, then
E\in\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and there is a positive constant
C
for which

\|T_{r}\|\leq C+r^{\|E\|}

for all
r>0
.

Proof.

If for some non-zero vector
\eta
,
E\eta=0
, then
r^{E}\eta=\eta
for all
r>0
and this would contradict our assumption that
\{T_{r}\}
is contracting. Hence
E\in\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and, in particular,
\|E\|>0
. From the representation
T_{r}=r^{E}
, it follows immediately that
\|T_{r}\|\leq r^{\|E\|}
for all
r\geq 1
and so it remains to estimate
\|T_{r}\|
for
r<1
. Given that
\{T_{r}\}
is continuous and contracting, the map
r\mapsto\|T_{r}\|
is continuous and approaches
0
as
r\rightarrow 0
and so it is necessarily bounded for
0<r\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition A.4
.

Let
\{T_{r}\}_{r>0}\subseteq\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a continuous one-parameter contracting group. Then, for all non-zero
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}|T_{r}x|=0\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 14.22636pt\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}|T_{r}x|=\infty.

Proof.

The validity of the first limit is clear. Upon noting that
|x|=|T_{1/r}T_{r}x|\leq\|T_{1/r}\||T_{r}x|
for all
r>0
, the second limit follows at once.
∎

Proposition A.5
.

Let
\{T_{r}\}_{r>0}
be a continuous one-parameter contracting group. There holds the following:

(a)

For each non-zero
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists
r>0
and
\eta\in\mathbb{S}
for which
T_{r}\eta=x
. Equivalently,

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}=\{T_{r}\eta:r>0\mbox{ and }\eta\in\mathbb{S}\}.

(b)

For each sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\lim_{n}|x_{n}|=\infty
,
x_{n}=T_{r_{n}}\eta_{n}
for each
n
, where
\{\eta_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}
and
r_{n}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(c)

For each sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\lim_{n}|x_{n}|=0
,
x_{n}=T_{r_{n}}\eta_{n}
for each
n
, where
\{\eta_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}
and
r_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
A.4
, the assertion
(a)
is a straightforward application of the intermediate value theorem. For
(b)
, suppose that
\{x_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is such that
|x_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In view of

, take
\{\eta_{n}\}\subseteq S
and
\{r_{n}\}\subseteq(0,\infty)
for which

\infty=\liminf_{n}|x_{n}|\leq\liminf_{n}\left(C+r_{n}^{M}\right)|\eta_{n}|\leq C+\liminf_{n}r_{n}^{M},

.
∎

Proposition A.6
.

Let
\{T_{r}\}

\{x_{n}\}

\{\zeta_{k}\}\subseteq K

t_{k}\rightarrow\infty

\zeta_{k}\notin T_{t_{k}}(\mathcal{O})

Proposition A.7
.

Let
\{T_{r}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Gl}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a continuous one-parameter group with generator
E
. If
\{T_{r}\}
is contracting, then
\tr E>0
.

Proof.

The supposition that
\{T_{r}\}
is a contracting group implies that
r^{E}\to\mathbf{0}
as
r\to 0
in the operator-norm topology on
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; here
\mathbf{0}
is zero transformation. Because the determinant is a continuous function from
\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

.
∎

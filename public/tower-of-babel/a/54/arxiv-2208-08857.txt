Logarithmic decomposition of connections on a relatively punctured disk
Phạm Thanh Tâm

Abstract
. Let R = C ​ ⟦ t ⟧ R=C\llbracket t\rrbracket be the ring of power series over an algebraically closed field C C of characteristic zero. We show that each connection on a finite flat R ⁡ ( ( x ) ) R(\!(x)\!) -module is the sum of a regular singular connection and a diagonalizable R ⁡ ( ( x ) ) R(\!(x)\!) –linear endomorphism when it admits a Turrittin-Levelt-Jordan form over R ⁡ ( ( x ) ) R(\!(x)\!) . 1.
Introduction

A module with a connection over
R(\!(x)\!)
is a finite
R(\!(x)\!)
–module equipped with a
R
–linear map obeying the Leibniz’s rule with respect to
\vartheta:=x\frac{d}{dx}
. We will call these objects by connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
. Denoted by
\mathbf{MC}(R(\!(x)\!)/R)
the category of connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
with horizontal morphisms.

The structure of arbitrary connections over
C(\!(x)\!)
is well understood in the work of Turrittin
[
Tur55
]
, Levelt
[
Lev75
]
and Katz
[
Kat87
]
. In the case of regular singular connections, the structure is described in detail by Manin
[
Ma65
]
and Deligne
[
Del87
]
which the canonical logarithmic lattices play an important tool in their works. In
[
HdST23
, Theorem 4.5]
, authors call these latices by Deligne-Manin lattices. In
[
Lev75
]
, Levelt shows that each connection
(E,\nabla)
over
C(\!(x)\!)
decomposes into a sum of a semisimple connection and a nilpotent
C(\!(x)\!)
–linear endomorphism.
We establish a decomposition

\nabla=\nabla^{\rm rs}+P_{\nabla}

which is called the logarithmic decomposition for
\nabla
, where
\nabla^{\rm rs}
is a regular singular connection on
E
, while
P_{\nabla}
is a semisimple
C(\!(x)\!)
–linear endomorphism with eigenvalues in
x_{s}^{-1}C[x_{s}^{-1}]
for some
s\in\mathds{N}
. The regular singular part allows us to define the Deligne-Manin lattice for
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})
which coincides with the
canonical lattice
of
(E,\nabla)
in the sense of
[
Mal96
, Theorem 3.3.1]
.

Recently, regular singular connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
are studied by
[
HdST23
]
and
[
HdSTT24
]
for the case
R
is a Henselian Noetherian local
C
-algebra. This work aims to establish the logarithmic decomposition for connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
in the case
R
is a complete discrete valuation domain with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{r}
. It is the continuation works in
[
HdST23
]
, we study the existence of the logarithmic decomposition of connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
. By reducing modulo
\mathfrak{r}^{\ell+1}
, each connection
(E,\nabla)
in
\mathbf{MC}(R(\!(x)\!)/R)
is associated to
(E,\nabla)|_{\ell}
in

\mathbf{MC}(R_{\ell}(\!(x)\!)/R_{\ell})
, where
R_{\ell}:=R/\mathfrak{r}^{\ell+1}
is the reduction of
R
modulo
\mathfrak{r}^{\ell+1}
. The connection
(E,\nabla)|_{\ell}
can be considered as an object in
\mathbf{MC}(C(\!(x)\!)/C)
with an action of
R_{\ell}
, and moreover, the logarithmic decomposition of
(E,\nabla)|_{\ell}
is compatible with the action of
R_{\ell}
. Using the completeness of
R
, we then go back to
R
by passing to the limit as
\ell\to\infty
. We establish the logarithmic decomposition for connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
equipped with a Turrittin-Levelt-Jordan form which is compatible with taking limits along
\mathfrak{r}
–adic topology. Additionally, these connections have a Deligne-Malgrange lattice which is compatible with the Turrittin-Levelt-Jordan decomposition.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we obtain the logarithmic decomposition of connections over
C(\!(x)\!)
in a canonical way by using Galois descent. In section
3
, we extend these results to the case of connections are equipped with an action of a local Artinian algebra. In Section
4
, Theorem
4.11
gives a condition for the existence of the logarithmic decomposition for connections over
R(\!(x)\!)
which is compatible with taking limit along the
\mathfrak{r}
–adic topology (see Theorem
4.16
).

2.2.
Jordan-Levelt decompositions

Let
{\rm dLog}:=\big\{u^{-1}\vartheta u\,:\,u\in C(\!(x)\!)\setminus\{0\}\big\}
. Then

\begin{split}{\rm dLog}&=\left\{\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}x^{n}\in C\llbracket x\rrbracket\,:\,a_{0}\in\mathds{Z}\right\}.\end{split}

For each
f\in C(\!(x)\!)
, let us define
\mathfrak{L}_{f}=(C(\!(x)\!)\mathbf{f},\mathrm{d}_{f})
by
\mathrm{d}_{f}(\mathbf{f})=f\mathbf{f}
.
Note that
\mathfrak{L}_{f}\simeq\mathfrak{L}_{g}
if and only if
f-g\in{\rm dLog}
. Let
\mathds{X}
stand for the set of isomorphism classes of rank one connections and endow it with the group structure obtained by the tensor product, it follows that

\frac{C(\!(x)\!)}{{\rm dLog}}\longrightarrow\mathds{X},\quad f+{\rm dLog}\longmapsto\mathfrak{L}_{f}

is a group isomorphism. Furthermore,
C[x^{-1}]/\mathds{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathds{X}
is isomorphic which is defined by
f+\mathds{Z}\longmapsto\mathfrak{L}_{f}
. For each
f\in C[x^{-1}]
, denote by
[f]
for its class in
C[x^{-1}]/\mathds{Z}
. Then, there exists
c_{f}\in\tau
such that
c_{f}+\mathrm{p}(f)
is a representative in
C[x^{-1}]
of
[f]
. We say that
\mathfrak{L}_{f}
is
a connection of type
[f]
.

For each
s\in\mathds{N}^{*}
, consider a new category
\mathbf{MC}_{s}(C(\!(x_{s})\!)/C)
whose objects are couples
(E,\nabla)
consisting of a finite dimensional
C(\!(x_{s})\!)
–vector space
E
equipped with a connection
\nabla
over
C(\!(x_{s})\!)
with respect to
\vartheta
.
Arrows are defined in the evident way. There is a natural functor

(-)_{(s)}:\mathbf{MC}(C(\!(x)\!)/C)\longrightarrow\mathbf{MC}_{s}(C(\!(x_{s})\!)/C),

which, on the level of objects, is
(E,\nabla)_{(s)}=(E_{(s)},\nabla)
with
E_{(s)}=C(\!(x_{s})\!)\underset{C(\!(x)\!)}{\otimes}E
and the connection obeys the rule
\nabla(g\otimes m)=\vartheta(g)\otimes m+g\otimes\nabla(m)
, where the derivation
\vartheta
on
C(\!(x_{s})\!)
is defined by
\vartheta=s^{-1}x_{s}\frac{d}{dx_{s}}

In parallel to Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
, we have:

.

We define the category

A connection
(E,\nabla)\in\mathbf{MC}(C(\!(x)\!)/C)

The following theorem is reminiscent of the Jordan-Chevalley decomposition for a linear operator. The detail proof see

Theorem 2.11

(Jordan-Levelt decomposition)
.

Let
(E,\nabla)\in\mathbf{MC}\big(C(\!(x)\!)/C\big)
. Then, there exists a unique decomposition
\nabla=S_{\nabla}+N_{\nabla}

satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

(E,S_{\nabla})
is a semi-simple connection in
\mathbf{MC}\big(C(\!(x)\!)/C\big)
.

(ii)

N_{\nabla}:E\longrightarrow E
is a
C(\!(x)\!)
–linear nilpotent endomorphism.

(iii)

S_{\nabla}
and
N_{\nabla}
commute. ∎

Definition 2.12
.

The
Turrittin index
of
(E,\nabla)
is the smallest number
s\in\mathds{N}
such that the connection
(E_{(s)},S_{\nabla})
is diagonalizable. We say that
(E,\nabla)
is
unramified
if its Turrittin index equals
1
.

2.3.
Turrittin-Levelt-Jordan decomposition

From on now
, denote by
s\in\mathds{N}
for the Turrittin index of
(E,\nabla)
. Because
S_{\nabla}
is diagonalizable, so
(E_{(s)},S_{\nabla})
is a direct sum of connections over
C(\!(x_{s})\!)
of rank one. Hence, there exists a finite set
\Phi\subset C[x_{s}^{-1}]/s^{-1}\mathds{Z}
and a decomposition

(E_{(s)},S_{\nabla})=\bigoplus\limits_{\varphi\in\Phi}(E_{\varphi},S_{\varphi})

in
\mathbf{MC}_{s}(C(\!(x_{s})\!)/C)
, where
(E_{\varphi},S_{\varphi})
is the direct sum of the subconnections of type
\varphi
of
(E_{(s)},S_{\nabla})
. Notice that
S_{\nabla}
and
N_{\nabla}
commute, so
E_{\varphi}
is closed under the action of
S_{\nabla},N_{\nabla}
and
\nabla
.
Hence, we obtain the following decomposition

(3)

(E_{(s)},{\nabla})=\bigoplus\limits_{\varphi\in\Phi}(E_{\varphi},\nabla_{\varphi})

in
\mathbf{MC}_{s}(C(\!(x_{s})\!)/C)
which is called the
the Turrittin-Levelt-Jordan decomposition
(TLJ-decomposition) of
(E_{(s)},\nabla)
, see
[
ABC20
, Theorem 16.1.2]
. In addition,
(E_{\varphi},\nabla_{\varphi})
is called the
isotypical component of type
\varphi
of
(E_{(s)},\nabla)
. For each
\varphi\in\Phi
, denote by
\mathrm{p}(\varphi)=\mathrm{p}(f_{\varphi})
, where
f_{\varphi}
is a representative in
C(\!(x_{s})\!)
of
\varphi
.

For each

of

Let

The logarithmic decomposition of a connection

Let

and

Definition 4.1
.

We say that

admits a

over

(6)

•

Each element of

of

•

The smallest number

) exists is called

of

. In addition,

is

For the article’s aim, we introduce the following notion:

.

We say that the connection

admits a

–basis

.

Remark 4.3
.

Let

and

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(E,\nabla)\in\mathbf{MC}_{\rm rs}^{\mathrm{o}}(R(\!(x)\!)/R)
be defined by
(V,A)\in\mathbf{End}_{R}^{\mathrm{o}}
. Assume that all eigenvalues of
A
and their differences are invertible in
R
. Then
(E,\nabla)
admits a TLJ-decomposition over
R(\!(x)\!)
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}
be the eigenvalues in
R
of
A
with multiplicities
q_{1},...,q_{m}
. The characteristic polynomial of
A
is
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}(T-\lambda_{i})^{q_{i}}.
Since
\big\{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\big\}_{1\leq i,j,k\leq m;i\neq j}
are invertible in
R
, so the polynomials
(T-\lambda_{i})^{q_{i}},(T-\lambda_{j})^{q_{j}}
and
T
are pairwise coprime for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
and
i\neq j
. Now apply the Chinese Remainder Theorem, there exists a unique polynomial
p(T)\in R[T]
such that
p(T)=\lambda_{i}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\quad(T-\lambda_{i})^{q_{i}})
and
p(T)\equiv 0\quad(\mathrm{mod}\quad T)
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Set
A_{s}=p(A)
and
A_{n}=A-A_{s}
, we have
[A_{s},A_{n}]=0
. Because the elements
\big\{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\big\}_{1\leq i,j\leq m;i\neq j}
are invertible in
R
, so we obtain that
1=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\prod\limits_{j\neq i}\big(\frac{T-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}\big)^{q_{i}}
.
Let
V_{i}=\ker(A-\lambda_{i})^{q_{i}}
for each
1\leq i\leq m
, we get
V=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{m}V_{i}
. The congruence
p(T)=\lambda_{i}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\quad(T-\lambda_{i})^{q_{i}})
implies that
A_{s}|_{V_{i}}=\lambda_{i}\mathrm{id}_{V_{i}}
for all
i
. By definition, each
A_{n}|_{V_{i}}
is nilpotent, then so is
A_{n}
.

Finally, according to
[
Ene06
, Theorem 3.3, p.79]
, there exists an
R
–basis
\mathbf{v}
of
V
such that
\mathrm{Mat}(A,\mathbf{v})=\mathrm{diag}(H_{r_{1}}(\lambda_{1}),...,H_{r_{m}}(\lambda_{m})),

where each
H_{r_{i}}(\lambda_{i})\in\mathrm{M}_{r_{i}}(R)
is a upper triangular matrix having all entries on the main diagonal
\lambda_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq m
.
Therefore,

\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla,1\otimes\mathbf{v})=\mathrm{diag}(H_{r_{1}}(\lambda_{1}),...,H_{r_{m}}(\lambda_{m}))

which shows that
(E,\nabla)
has a TLJ-decomposition over
R(\!(x)\!)
.

Theorem 4.11

(Logarithmic decomposition)
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be an object in the category
\mathbf{MC}^{\mathrm{o}}\big(R(\!(x)\!)/R\big)
which admits a TLJ-form over
R(\!(x)\!)
. Then there exists a unique decomposition
\nabla=\nabla^{\rm rs}+P_{\nabla}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})
belongs to
\mathbf{MC}_{\rm rs}\big(R(\!(x)\!)/R\big)
and admits a TLJ-form over
R(\!(x)\!)
.

(ii)

P_{\nabla}\in\mathrm{End}_{R(\!(x)\!)}(E)
is diagonalizable with all eigenvalues in
x^{-1}R[x^{-1}]
.

(iii)

Let
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})=\bigoplus\limits_{\varrho\in\Sigma}(E_{\varrho},\nabla_{\varrho})
be the TLJ-decomposition equipped with a TLJ-form over
R(\!(x)\!)
of
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})
, where
\Sigma\subset R/\mathds{Z}
. For each
{\varrho\in\Sigma}
, we have

(a)

P_{\nabla}(E_{\varrho})\subset E_{\varrho}
.

(b)

There exists an
R(\!(x)\!)
–basis
(\mathbf{e}_{\varrho})
of
E_{\varrho}
,
c_{\varrho}\in R
and
f_{\mathrm{\varrho}1},...,f_{\mathrm{\varrho}\ell}\in x^{-1}R[x^{-1}]
such that

\begin{cases}\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla^{\rm rs}|_{E_{\varrho}},\mathbf{e}_{\varrho})=\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\varrho}}(c_{\varrho})\\
\mathrm{Mat}({\nabla}|_{E_{\varrho}},\mathbf{e}_{\varrho})=\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{\varrho}1}}(c_{\varrho}+f_{\mathrm{\varrho}1}),...,\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{\varrho}\ell}}(c_{\varrho}+f_{\mathrm{\varrho}\ell})).\end{cases}

Proof.

Existence.
By Proposition
4.7
, there is
\Phi\subset R[x^{-1}]/\mathds{Z}
and a decomposition
(E,\nabla)=\bigoplus\limits_{\varphi\in\Phi}(E_{\varphi},\nabla_{\varphi})

such that

\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi},\mathbf{e}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\varphi}}(f_{\varphi})

for each
\varphi\in\Phi
, where
f_{\varphi}\in R[x^{-1}]
satisfies
c_{\varphi}=f_{\varphi}-\mathrm{p}(\varphi)\in\tau
and
(\mathbf{e}_{\varphi})
is an
R(\!(x)\!)
–basis of
E_{\varphi}
. For each
\varphi\in\Phi
, let
\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs}=\nabla_{\varphi}-\mathrm{p}(\varphi)
. Then,
(E_{\varphi},\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs})
is an object in
\mathbf{MC}_{\rm rs}\big(R(\!(x)\!)/R\big)
. It is obvious to see that

\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs},\mathbf{e}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\varphi}}(c_{\varphi}).

Let us define the connection
\nabla^{\rm rs}
on
E
which is defined by
\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs}
on
E_{\varphi}
for each
\varphi\in\Phi
. It is not difficult to see that
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})
admits a TLJ-form over
R(\!(x)\!)
. It is obvious that
P_{\nabla}=\nabla-\nabla^{\rm rs}
is a diagonalizable
R(\!(x)\!)-
linear endomorphism of
E
with all eigenvalues in
x^{-1}R[x^{-1}]
.

Consider the TLJ-decomposition
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})=\bigoplus\limits_{\varrho\in\Sigma}(E_{\varrho},\nabla_{\varrho})
over
R(\!(x)\!)
of
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})
. Let us fix a representative
c_{\varrho}
in
\tau
of
\varrho
. Note that
E_{\varrho}
is generated by
\bigcup\limits_{k\geq 1}\ker_{R(\!(x)\!)}\big(\nabla-c_{\varrho}\big)^{k}
over
R(\!(x)\!)
, and moreover

(E_{K(\!(x)\!)},\nabla^{\rm rs})=\bigoplus\limits_{\varrho\in\Sigma}(K(\!(x)\!)\underset{R(\!(x)\!)}{\otimes}E_{\varrho},\nabla_{\varrho})

is the TLJ-decomposition of
(E_{K(\!(x)\!)},\nabla^{\rm rs})
over
K(\!(x)\!)
. On the other hand, the isotypical component
E_{\varphi}
is generated by
\bigcup\limits_{k\geq 1}\ker_{R(\!(x)\!)}\big(\nabla-c_{\varphi}\big)^{k}
over
R(\!(x)\!)
for each
\varphi\in\Phi
, and the TLJ-decomposition

(E_{K(\!(x)\!)},\nabla^{\rm rs})=\bigoplus\limits_{\varphi\in\Phi}(K(\!(x)\!)\underset{R(\!(x)\!)}{\otimes}E_{\varphi},\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs})

of

belong to
\tau

is not regular singular.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
(E,\nabla)\in\mathbf{MC}\big(R(\!(x)\!)/R\big)
and
\nabla|_{\ell}^{\rm rs}
is the regular singular part of
\nabla|_{\ell}
for each
\ell\in\mathds{N}
. Then there exists a regular singular connection
(\dot{E},\dot{\nabla})
in
\mathbf{MC}_{\rm rs}\big(R(\!(x)\!)/R\big)
such that

(\dot{E},\dot{\nabla})|_{\ell}=(E|_{\ell},\nabla|_{\ell}^{\rm rs}).

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.4
, we obtain that
\{(E|_{\ell},\nabla|_{\ell}^{\rm rs})\}_{\ell\in\mathds{N}}
is an object in
\mathbf{MC}_{\rm{rs}}(R(\!(x)\!)/R)^{\wedge}
because
(E|_{\ell+1},\nabla|_{\ell+1}^{\rm rs})|_{\ell}=(E|_{\ell},\nabla|_{\ell}^{\rm rs})
for each
\ell\in\mathds{N}
. Hence, the limit
(\dot{E},\dot{\nabla})=\varprojlim\limits_{\ell}(E|_{\ell},\nabla|_{\ell}^{\rm rs})
is an object in
\mathbf{MC}_{\rm rs}(R(\!(x)\!)/R)
. ∎

We now arrive at

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
(E,\nabla)
be an object in
\mathbf{MC}^{\mathrm{o}}\big(R(\!(x)\!)/R\big)
which admits a TLJ-form over
R(\!(x)\!)
. Then,
(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})
and
(\dot{E},\dot{\nabla})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

According to Proposition
4.7
, there exists
\Phi\subset R[x^{-1}]/\mathds{Z}
and a decomposition
(E,\nabla)=\bigoplus\limits_{\varphi\in\Phi}(E_{\varphi},\nabla_{\varphi})

such that
\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi},\mathbf{e}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\varphi}}(f_{\varphi})

for each
\varphi\in\Phi
, where
f_{\varphi}\in R[x^{-1}]
is a representative of
\varphi
and
(\mathbf{e}_{\varphi})
is an
R(\!(x)\!)
–basis of
E_{\varphi}
. Let
P_{\nabla_{\varphi}}=P_{\nabla}|_{E_{\varphi}}
and
\nabla_{\varphi}=\nabla|_{E_{\varphi}}
. It is easy to see that

\mathrm{Mat}(P_{\nabla_{\varphi}},{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{p}(\varphi)\mathrm{I}_{n},\quad\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs},{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{\mathrm{r}_{\varphi}}(f_{\varphi}-\mathrm{p}(\varphi)).

Let write
a=f_{\varphi}-\mathrm{p}(\varphi)=a_{0}+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+\cdots
and
\mathrm{p}(\varphi)=b_{0}+b_{1}t+b_{2}t^{2}+\cdots
.
Notice that
f_{\varphi}\in R[x^{-1}]
and
a=f_{\varphi}-\mathrm{p}(\varphi)\in R
, so
a_{i}\in C
and
b_{i}\in x^{-1}C[x^{-1}]
. In addition,
{\rm ord}_{x}(b_{i})\geq{\rm ord}_{x}(f_{\varphi})
for all
i\in\mathds{N}
.

For each
\ell\in\mathds{N}
, consider the
C(\!(x)\!)
–basis
(t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})_{i\leq\ell}:=({\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi},t{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi},\ldots,t^{\ell}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})
of
E|_{\ell}
. Let
\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}=\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs}|_{\ell},t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})
and
\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}=\mathrm{Mat}(P_{\nabla_{\varphi}}|_{\ell},t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})
. We have

\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{n}(a_{0})&0&\cdots&0&0\\
a_{1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{n}(a_{0})&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{\ell-1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&a_{\ell-2}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{n}(a_{0})&0\\
a_{\ell}\mathrm{I}_{n}&a_{\ell-1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&a_{1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\mathrm{J}^{\flat}_{n}(a_{0})\end{pmatrix}

and

\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
b_{1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
b_{\ell-1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&b_{\ell-2}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0\\
b_{\ell}\mathrm{I}_{n}&b_{\ell-1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&b_{1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi}|_{\ell},t^{i}\mathbf{e}_{\varphi})=

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}+\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}=b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{(\ell+1)n}+\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}+(\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}-b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{(\ell+1)n}).

According to Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
3.4
, each
E_{\varphi}
is invariant under
\dot{\nabla}|_{\ell}
since
\dot{\nabla}|_{\ell}=\nabla|_{\ell}^{\rm rs}
. The restriction
\dot{\nabla}_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
of
\dot{\nabla}|_{\ell}
is the connection on
E_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
defined by the matrix
\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}+(\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}-b_{0}I)
with respect to
(t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})_{i\leq\ell}
. Let
({\mathbf{e}_{\varphi}^{(1)}})
be the
C(\!(x)\!)
-basis of
E_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
which is base changed from
(t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})_{i\leq\ell}
by

\mathrm{P}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathrm{I}_{n}&0\\
b^{\prime}_{\ell}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&\mathrm{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where
b^{\prime}_{\ell}\in x^{-1}C[x^{-1}]
satisfies
b_{\ell}+\vartheta(b^{\prime}_{\ell})=0
. The matrix of
\dot{\nabla}_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
with respect to
({\mathbf{e}_{\varphi}^{(1)}})
is

\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}^{(1)}:=P_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}-b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{(\ell+1)n})P_{1}+P_{1}^{-1}\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}P_{1}+P_{1}^{-1}\vartheta(P_{1}).

Note that

\mathrm{P}_{1}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathrm{I}_{n}&0\\
-b^{\prime}_{\ell}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&\mathrm{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

By computing, we obtain
\mathrm{P}_{1}^{-1}\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}\mathrm{P}_{1}=\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell};\mathrm{P}_{1}^{-1}(\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}-b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{(\ell+1)n})\mathrm{P}_{1}=\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}-b_{0}\mathrm{I}_{(\ell+1)n},
and

\mathrm{P}_{1}^{-1}\vartheta(\mathrm{P}_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\vartheta(b^{\prime}_{\ell})\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, the matrix of
\dot{\nabla}_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
with respect to the basis
({\mathbf{e}_{\varphi}^{(1)}})
is

\mathrm{B}_{\varphi,\ell}^{(1)}=\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
b_{1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
b_{\ell-1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&b_{\ell-2}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&0&0\\
0&b_{\ell-1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&\cdots&b_{1}\mathrm{I}_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Continuing this procedure, there exists an
C(\!(x)\!)
–basis
({\mathbf{e}_{\varphi}^{(\ell)}})
of
E_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
such that
\mathrm{Mat}(\dot{\nabla}_{\varphi}|_{\ell},{\mathbf{e}_{\varphi}^{(\ell)}})=\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}
. On the other hand,
\mathrm{A}_{\varphi,\ell}=\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs}|_{\ell},t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi}).
Hence,

\mathrm{Mat}(\dot{\nabla}_{\varphi}|_{\ell},{\mathbf{e}_{\varphi}^{(\ell)}})=\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla_{\varphi}^{\rm rs}|_{\ell},(t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{\varphi})_{i\leq\ell}).

By proceeding with all isotypical components, there exists a
C(\!(x)\!)
–basis
(\mathbf{e}^{(\ell)})
of
E|_{\ell}
such that
\mathrm{Mat}(\dot{\nabla}_{\ell},{\mathbf{e}^{(\ell)}})=\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla^{\rm rs}|_{\ell},t^{i}{\mathbf{e}}),
where
\dot{\nabla}_{\ell}
is the connection on
E|_{\ell}
which is defined by
\dot{\nabla}_{\varphi,\ell}
on each
E_{\varphi}|_{\ell}
. Therefore, we obtain that

(\dot{E},\dot{\nabla})|_{\ell}\simeq(E,\nabla^{\rm rs})|_{\ell}.

Because the numbers
{\rm ord}_{x}(b_{i})
are bounded from below, so are
{\rm ord}_{x}(b^{\prime}_{i})
. By constructing, the base changes above are compatible with taking the limit. This shows that there exists an
R(\!(x)\!)-
basis
(\mathbf{e}^{(\infty)})
of
\dot{E}
such that
\mathrm{Mat}(\dot{\nabla},\mathbf{e}^{(\infty)})=\mathrm{Mat}(\nabla^{\rm rs},\mathbf{e})
.
In particular, we obtain that
(\dot{E},\dot{\nabla})\simeq(E,\nabla^{\rm rs}).

∎

Compactness of harmonic maps of surfaces with regular nodes
Woongbae Park

Abstract
. In this paper, we formulate and prove a general compactness theorem for harmonic maps using Deligne-Mumford moduli space and families of curves. The main theorem shows that given a sequence of harmonic maps over a sequence of complex curves, there is a family of curves and a subsequence such that both the domains and the maps converge off the set of “non-regular” nodes. This provides a sufficient condition for a neck having zero energy and zero length. As a corollary, the following known fact can be proved: If all domains are diffeomorphic to S 2 S^{2} , both energy identity and zero distance bubbling hold.



1.
Introduction

Given a sequence of harmonic maps with uniformly bounded energy, Uhlenbeck Compactness Theorem discovered “bubble phenomenon” where bubble occurs at points of energy concentration.
Later, Parker
[
1
]
showed bubble tree extension to describe how a given sequence of harmonic maps converges over bubbles.
For fixed domain, this result is regarded as a full answer of the problem because

(1)

it shows energy identity and zero distance bubbling, and

(2)

it specifies where each bubble locates, including bubbles on the bubbles.

However, when domain is varying, we do not have above properties.
The difficulty is when complex structure (or metric) degenerates and there were several studies in this direction.
For example, Chen-Tian
[
11
]
focused on energy minimizing harmonic maps and showed compactness result, together with connecting geodesics.
Zhu
[
9
]
studied the conditions that energy identity and zero distance bubbling do not hold and Chen-Li-Wang
[
12
]
showed length formula of the neck, but their conditions contain non-geometric quantity.
There are other ways to consider change of complex structure, for example, looking at Teichmüller space.
This space restricts to the constant curvature metric and hence seems less appropriate to describe bubbles and necks than Deligne-Mumford moduli space.

In this paper we first define convergence of maps (Definition
3.2
) in terms of family of complex curves using Deligne-Mumford moduli space.
For each energy concentration points, we put additional marked points to build bigger family in which convergence becomes better in terms of
residual energy
(Definition
7.1
).
Details of this procedure will be explained in Section
4
.
Note that energy concentration may occur at regular point or nodal point, where the latter corresponds to a degeneration of complex structure.
Since not always energy identity holds in the case of degenerating complex structure, we need more refined notion of
regular node
(Definition
5.1
).
Now our main theorem can be stated in terms of regular node.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
be a sequence of harmonic maps with uniformly bounded energy defined on smooth
(g,n)
curves.

Then there is a subsequence
n_{k}
and a way of marking points
P_{k}
on
C_{k}
such that corresponding sequence
f_{n_{k}}:C^{\prime}_{n_{k}}=(C_{n_{k}},P_{n_{k}})\rightarrow X
converges to some
f_{0}:C_{0}\rightarrow X
off the singular set
S
(possibly empty) in
C^{1}
where all points in
S
are non-regular nodal points.
Furthermore,
f_{0}
is harmonic on closure of each component of
C_{0}\setminus S
separately.

With additional assumption, we can make the singular set
S
empty.

Corollary 1.2
.

With the same assumption of Theorem
1.1
, also assume that
C_{k}\rightarrow C_{0}
in a family
\mathcal{C}
and all nodes in
C_{0}
are regular.

Then the singular set
S
in the convergence in Theorem
1.1
is empty.
Furthermore, the energy identity holds and the image of
f_{0}
is connected.

Now Parker’s theorem is a corollary of the main theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth Riemann surface with genus
g
and suppose
f_{k}:\Sigma\rightarrow X
be a sequence of harmonic maps with uniformly bounded energy.

Then Corollary
1.2
can be applied with
C_{k}=C_{0}=\Sigma
.

Another corollary is when
g=0
, that is, all domains are
n
-marked sphere
S^{2}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
C_{k}
be 2-spheres with
n
-marked points and suppose
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
be a sequence of harmonic maps with uniformly bounded energy.

Then Corollary
1.2
can be applied.

Proofs will be given in Section
7
.

The remaining parts of this paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
deals with basic properties of harmonic maps and Deligne-Mumford moduli space.
In Section
3
, we develop necessary convergence terminology.
Section
4
focuses on neck analysis and Section
5
introduces regular nodes.
In Section
6
we explain the procedure of building bigger family by putting appropriate marked points.
Finally, the last section contains proof of the main theorem.

2.
Background

2.1.
Harmonic Maps

Let
(\Sigma,g)
and
(X,h)
be compact Riemannian manifolds with Riemannian metrics
g
and
h
with
\dim(\Sigma)=2
.
We use the same letter
g
to denote Riemannian metric of
\Sigma
and genus of
\Sigma
if there is no confusion.
A map
f:(\Sigma,g)\rightarrow(X,h)
is harmonic if it is a critical point of the energy functional

(2.1)

E(f)=\mathcal{L}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\lvert df\rvert^{2}dvol_{g}.

Using
f_{k}
and
g
, we can define corresponding energy density measures
e(f_{k})
on
\Sigma
by

(2.2)

e(f_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\lvert df_{k}\rvert^{2}dvol_{g}.

We summarize some important lemmas regarding harmonic maps.
Here we follow
[
6
]
and
[
1
]
.
For more results, see
[
7
]
or
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
f:(\Sigma,g)\rightarrow(X,h)
be harmonic.
Then we have the followings:

(1)

(
\varepsilon
-regularity)
There is a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending only on second fundamental form of the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that if
f
is a harmonic map on a disk
D
and if
E_{D}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\lvert df\rvert^{2}dvol_{g}<\varepsilon_{0}
, then for any
D^{\prime}\subset\subset D
,

(2.3)

\lVert df\rVert_{W^{1,p}(D^{\prime})}\leq C\lVert df\rVert_{L^{2}(D)},

where
1<p<\infty
and
C
is a constant which only depends on
p
,
D^{\prime}
and the geometry of
X
.

(2)

(Energy-gap)
There is a constant
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}>0
depending only on
(X,h)
such that if
f
is a smooth harmonic map on a compact domain
\Sigma
satisfying
E(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\lvert df\rvert^{2}dvol_{g}<\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}
, then
f
is constant.

(3)

(Removable Singularity)
If
f:D\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow X
is a
C^{1}
harmonic map with
E(f)<\varepsilon_{0}
on a punctured disk
D\setminus\{0\}
, then
f
can be extended to
D
in
C^{1}
.

(4)

(
C^{1}
-convergence)
There is a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if
\{f_{k}\}
is a family of harmonic maps on
D
and satisfying
E_{D}(f_{k})<\varepsilon_{0}
for all
k
, then there is a subsequence
f_{k}
that converges to
f
in
C^{1}
.

Using these, Uhlenbeck proved the following compactness theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Uhlenbeck Compactness Theorem,
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
or
[
1
, Lemma 1.2]
)
Suppose
\{f_{k}\}
be a sequence of harmonic maps with uniformly bounded energy.
Then there are at most finite number of points
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}\}
, called bubble points, subsequence of
\{f_{k}\}
and limit map
f_{\infty}:(\Sigma,g)\rightarrow(X,h)
such that
f_{k}\rightarrow f_{\infty}
in
C^{1}
for any compact set away from
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}\}
, and

(2.4)

e(f_{k})\rightarrow e(f_{\infty})+\sum_{i=1}^{l}m_{i}\delta_{p_{i}}

as measures where
m_{i}\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}
.

Parker
[
1
]
used iterated renormalizations to construct a so-called “bubble tree” to analyze the energy completely, i.e., all the energy comes from either the limit map or bubbles.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Bubble Tree Convergence,
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
).
Under the same assumption of Theorem
2.2
, there is a subsequence
\{f_{n}\}
and a bubble tower domain
T=\Sigma\cup\bigcup S_{I}
so that the renormalized maps

\{f_{n,I}\}:T\rightarrow X

converges in
W^{1,2}\cap C^{0}
to a smooth harmonic bubble tree map
\{f_{I}\}:T\rightarrow X
.
Moreover,

(1)

(No energy loss)

E(f_{n})
converges to
\sum E(f_{I})
, and

(2)

(Zero distance bubbling)
At each bubble point
x_{J}
(at an level in the tree), the images of the base map
f_{I}
and the bubble map
f_{J}
meet at
f_{I}(x_{J})=f_{J}(p^{-})
.

2.2.
Families of Curves

In this subsection we define a notion of convergence of complex curves
C_{k}
.
As in algebraic geometry, it is useful and important to consider not just single curves, but instead
families
of curves.
There are standard definitions of such families used by algebraic geometers.
Many details can be found at
[
15
]
.
We will use an equivalent definition that is more in the spirit of differential geometry.
For details, see
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
(g,n)
curve is defined to be a connected
n
-marked nodal curve of genus
g
, that is, a complex curve
C
of genus
g
with at most nodal singularity together with a sequence
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of distinct points of
C
.
A
(g,n)
curve is said to be stable if
2g-2+n>0
.

Definition 2.5
.

(
[
18
]
4.2,4.7)
An
n
-marked nodal family is a surjective proper holomorphic map
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow B
between connected complex manifolds with disjoint submanifolds, together with
\mathcal{N},S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
such that

(1)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(B)+1
,

(2)

\pi|_{S_{i}}
maps
S_{i}
diffeomorphically onto
B
,

(3)

\mathcal{N}=\{p\in\mathcal{C}:d\pi(p)\textrm{ not surjective}\}
, and

(4)

Each critical point
p\in\mathcal{N}
has local holomorphic coordinate chart
(x,y,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
, called a nodal chart, such that

(2.5)

\pi(x,y,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(xy,z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})

is a local holomorphic coordinate chart in a neighborhood of
\pi(p)
.

We call
\mathcal{N}
the nodal set, and
S_{i}
the marked sections.

By the holomorphic Implicit Function Theorem,
p\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{N}
has local holomorphic coordinate chart
(x,z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
, called a regular chart, such that
\pi(x,z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
is a local holomorphic coordinate chart in a neighborhood of
\pi(p)
.
Note that
\mathcal{N}
intersects each fiber
C_{b}:=\pi^{-1}(b)
in a finite set.
For each regular value
b\in B
of
\pi
the fiber
C_{b}
is a compact Riemann surface.
For each critical value
b\in B
of
\pi
the fiber
C_{b}
is a nodal curve.

Together with an isomorphism between a curve
C
and a fiber, one can consider a deformation of
C
as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

(
[
15
]
11.2.1, 11.4.2)
Let
C
be a (marked) complex curve (or a nodal curve).
A deformation of
C
is a nodal family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow(B,b_{0})
plus a given isomorphism between
C
and the central fiber
\pi^{-1}(b_{0})
that maps marked points to marked sections.

We understand the concept of deformation in terms of a germ.
Hence a restriction of a deformation containing the central fiber is regarded as equivalent as the original one.

Now we describe the Deligne-Mumford moduli space.
The Deligne-Mumford moduli space of stable
n
-marked nodal curves of genus
g
is defined by

(2.6)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}:=\{\textrm{ isomorphism classes }[C]\textrm{ of }(g,n)\textrm{ curves }\}.

The structure of (
2.6
) is well-known.
Here we point out properties of (
2.6
) that will be used in this paper.

•

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
be a moduli space of stable
n
-marked smooth curves of genus
g
.
Then
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is its compactification.

•

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a complex projective variety, and has orbifold structure.

•

There is a projection map, called forgetful map, given by

(2.7)

\Phi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

which forgets the last marked point and collapses unstable component to a point.

There are several different ways to construct
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.
Here we see a neighborhood of
[C_{0}]
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
as a quotient of Kuranishi family of
C_{0}
by a finite group
Aut(C_{0})
of automorphisms of central fiber
C_{0}
.
If
[C_{k}]\rightarrow[C_{0}]
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
with
Aut(C_{0})
being not trivial, then the quotient has a singularity at
C_{0}
.
Instead, we use Kuranishi family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow(B,0)
of one of representatives
C_{0}
of
[C_{0}]
without quotient.
Then the total space
\mathcal{C}
is a smooth manifold, but
Aut(C_{0})
acts as transformation of fibers, which destroys uniqueness of embedding of
C_{k}
into family.

Definition 2.7
.

For sequence of
(g,n)
curves
C_{k}
, we say
C_{k}\rightarrow C_{0}
in a family
\mathcal{C}
if
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow(B,0)
is a deformation of
C_{0}
with isomorphism
\varphi:C_{0}\rightarrow\pi^{-1}(0)
and isomorphisms
\varphi_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow\pi^{-1}(b_{k})
with
b_{k}\rightarrow 0
.
Since
\varphi
and
\varphi_{k}
are isomorphisms, we identify
C_{k}
with
\pi^{-1}(b_{k})
and
C_{0}
with
\pi^{-1}(0)
.

Remark 2.8
.

There is no uniqueness statement for the choice of the sequence
b_{k}
and the isomorphisms
\varphi
and
\varphi_{k}
.
However, all of the convergence statements below hold for any choice.

3.
Convergence of Maps

Now we care about a sequence of
C^{l}
(of
W^{l,p}
) maps
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
with
C_{k}\rightarrow C_{0}
in a family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow(B,0)
.
In a regular chart of the family, we can give a local coordinate of
C_{k}
by
(x,b_{k})
and
C_{0}
by
(x,0)
.
Also, assume that
E(f_{k})\leq E_{0}<+\infty
for all
k
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
C_{k}\rightarrow C_{0}
in a family
\mathcal{C}
and
p
be a node of
C_{0}
.
We say a sequence of maps
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
satisfies the zero neck property at
p
if the following holds:

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta_{1}>0
such that for any
0<\delta<\delta_{1}
and for all
k
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle E(f_{k},B(p,\delta)\cap C_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(f_{k}(B(p,\delta)\cap C_{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon

where
B(p,\delta)
is a ball of radius
\delta
centered at
p
in the family
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 3.2
.

We say
f_{k}
converges to
f_{0}:C_{0}\rightarrow X
off the set
S\subset C_{0}
in
C^{l}
(or
W^{l,p}
) if

(1)

C_{k}\rightarrow C_{0}
in a family
\mathcal{C}
,

(2)

for any node
p\notin S
,
f_{k}
satisfies the zero neck property at
p
.

(3)

in every regular chart away from
S
, the projected map

(3.1)

\tilde{f}_{k}(x):=f_{k}(x,b_{k})

converges to
\tilde{f}_{0}(x):=f_{0}(x,0)
in
C^{l}
(or
W^{l,p}
).

The set
S
is called singular set.

Lemma 3.3
.

(Energy Identity and Connected Image)
Suppose
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
converges to
f_{0}:C_{0}\rightarrow X
in
C^{1}
with empty singular set.
Then

(3.2)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}E(f_{k})=E(f_{0}).

Furthermore, if each
C_{k}
is connected, then the image of
f_{0}
is connected.

Proof.

Pick any regular point
p\in C_{0}
and consider a regular chart
\pi:U\times B\rightarrow B
with
p=(0,0)
.
The projection map
\pi_{k}:U\times\{b_{k}\}\rightarrow U
given by
\pi_{k}(x,b_{k})=x
is holomorphic, so the energy of
f_{k}
over
U\times\{b_{k}\}
is the same as the energy of
\tilde{f}_{k}
over
U
.
Since
f_{k}
converges to
f_{0}
in
C^{1}
,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}E(f_{k},U\times\{b_{k}\})=E(f_{0},U)
.
Also, because of the zero neck property, we have

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}E(f_{k},B(q,\delta)\cap C_{k})=0

for any node
q
.
Cover
C_{0}
by finitely many regular charts
\{U_{i}\times B\}
and we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}E(f_{k})=E(f_{0}).

Connectedness comes from the other condition of the zero neck property.
∎

Using this new definition of convergence, Uhlenbeck Compactness Theorem
2.2
can be rewritten as follows:
Note that in regular chart
\tilde{f}_{k}(x):=f_{k}(x,b_{k})
as in (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.4
.

(Local convergence)
Suppose a sequence of smooth
(g,n)
curves
C_{k}
converges to
C_{0}
in a family
\mathcal{C}
and
N
be a nodal set in
C_{0}
.
Let
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
be a sequence of harmonic maps with uniformly bounded energy.

Then there is a subsequence
n_{k}
, a finite set
Q=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{l}\}\subset C_{0}\setminus N
, and a harmonic map
f_{0}:C_{0}\rightarrow X
such that
f_{n_{k}}
converges to
f_{0}
off the set
S=Q\cup N
in
C^{1}
.
Also, near
p\in Q\subset C_{0}
, we have

(3.3)

e(\tilde{f}_{n_{k}})\rightarrow e(f_{0})+m_{p}\delta_{p},

where
\delta_{p}
is a Dirac-delta measure at
p
and
m_{p}\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}_{0}
.

Proof.

Cover
C_{0}\setminus N
by finitely many regular chart
\{U_{i}\times B\}
.
Since the regular chart is holomorphic coordinate chart,
\tilde{f}_{k}(x)
is also harmonic maps over
U_{i}
for each
i
.
By Uhlenbeck compactness theorem
2.2
, there is a subsequence
n_{k,1}
such that
\tilde{f}_{n_{k,1}}
converges on
U_{1}\setminus Q_{1}
where
Q_{1}
is a finite set.
Applying Theorem
2.2
again to find subsequence
n_{k,2}
of
n_{k,1}
such that
\tilde{f}_{n_{k,2}}
converges on
U_{2}\setminus Q_{2}
where
Q_{2}
is a finite set.
Repeat this process to obtain
n_{k,i}
for
U_{i}
, and choose diagonal subsequence
n_{k}=n_{k,k}
.
Then
\tilde{f}_{n_{k}}
converges on
U_{i}\setminus Q_{i}
for all

, where
Q_{i}
is a finite set.
Note that at each point

, by (

), the energy concentration at

is at least

.
Since the total energy is finite,

is at most finite.
The measure convergence (

) comes from (

).
∎

The above lemma says that energy loss may occur only at points in

.

Definition 3.5
.

In Lemma

, we say
p\in Q
a smooth bubble point and

The above lemma only says about smooth bubble points and nothing about nodes.
So the question is: does

4.
Neck analysis

In this section we will deal with sequence of harmonic maps over the neck region.
Throughout this section, we will use both nodal chart or cylindrical chart that are described below.
Many of the arguments in here can be found in
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
, or
[
5
]
.

First we set up the terminology.
Consider a sequence of harmonic maps
f_{k}:C_{k}\rightarrow X
defined on family of stable curves
C_{k}
with
C_{k}\rightarrow C_{0}
where
C_{0}
is a limit curve with a node
p
.
For any
\delta>0
, using nodal chart, we can denote
B(p,\delta)\cap C_{k}=\{x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:xy=t_{k},\lvert x\rvert,\lvert y\rvert\leq\delta\}
and
t_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Consider polar coordinate
x=(r,\theta)
, and take log by
(r,\theta)\rightarrow(t,\theta)
where
t=\ln(r/\sqrt{t_{k}})
.
This gives a cylindrical coordinate over the neck which is conformal to the original nodal chart, given by

(4.1)

\phi:[-T_{k}^{\delta},T_{k}^{\delta}]\times S^{1}\rightarrow B(p,\delta)\cap C_{k}

where
\phi(t,\theta)=(x,y)=(\sqrt{t_{k}}e^{t+i\theta},\sqrt{t_{k}}e^{-t-i\theta})
and
T_{k}^{\delta}=\ln(\delta/\sqrt{t_{k}})
.
Note that pullback of the metric to the cylinder is conformally equivalent to
dt^{2}+d\theta^{2}
, so we can consider flat metric over the cylinder.
For simplicity, we often denote
f_{k}\circ\phi
by
f_{k}
, or simply by
f
.

By
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
, the quantity

\alpha(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{t\}\times S^{1}}\lvert f_{t}\rvert^{2}-\lvert f_{\theta}\rvert^{2}d\theta

is a constant
\alpha
, which is independent of
t
. Hence, the energy can be written as

(4.2)

E(f)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{[-T_{k}^{\delta},T_{k}^{\delta}]\times S^{1}}\lvert f_{t}\rvert^{2}+\lvert f_{\theta}\rvert^{2}dt\,d\theta=2T_{k}^{\delta}\alpha+\int_{-T_{k}^{\delta}}^{T_{k}^{\delta}}\Theta dt

where

\Theta(t):=\int_{\{t\}\times S^{1}}\lvert f_{\theta}\rvert^{2}d\theta.

Moreover, by
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime\prime}>0
such that if
f:[-T_{k}^{\delta},T_{k}^{\delta}]\times S^{1}\rightarrow X
is a harmonic map and
E(f)\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime\prime}
, then

\Theta^{\prime\prime}\geq\Theta>0

and that for any
-T_{k}^{\delta}\leq T_{1}<T_{2}\leq T_{k}^{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\Theta dt

\displaystyle\leq 2(\Theta(T_{1})+\Theta(T_{2})),

\displaystyle\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\sqrt{\Theta}dt

\displaystyle\leq 4(\sqrt{\Theta(T_{1})}+\sqrt{\Theta(T_{2})}).

From the above, energy is controlled by
\alpha
and boundary values of
\Theta
.
We need one more quantity.
Let the average length of the neck be given by:

\overline{L}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{[-T_{k}^{\delta},T_{k}^{\delta}]\times S^{1}}\lvert f_{t}\rvert dtd\theta.

Then

\displaystyle\overline{L}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-T_{k}^{\delta}}^{T_{k}^{\delta}}\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\int_{S^{1}}\lvert f_{t}\rvert^{2}d\theta\right)^{1/2}dt

The assumption that
p
is a regular node is non-avoidable.
Parker
[
1
]
showed that there could be non-regular nodes by providing an example that the zero neck property fails.
In the language of Deligne-Mumford moduli space, his example can be seen as a family of torus with singular central fiber, which is a pinched torus.
Note that this node is not regular, because there is no forgetful map from this singular family to family with smaller marked points.
However, by modifying the example, one can get zero energy and zero (average) length of the neck, which satisfies the zero neck property.
(See
[
9
]
for details.)
This modification suggests the possibility that even over non-regular nodes, the zero neck property may hold, but not much research is done in this direction.

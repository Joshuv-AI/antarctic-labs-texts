Determination of a class of permutation quadrinomials
Zhiguo Ding, Michael E. Zieve

Abstract
. We determine all permutation polynomials over 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} of the form X r ​ A ​ ( X q − 1 ) X^{r}A(X^{q-1}) where, for some Q Q which is a power of the characteristic of 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , we have r ≡ Q + 1 ( mod q + 1 ) r\equiv Q+1\pmod{q+1} and all terms of A ⁡ ( X ) A(X) have degrees in { 0 , 1 , Q , Q + 1 } \{0,1,Q,Q+1\} . We then use this classification to resolve eight conjectures and open problems from the literature. Our proof makes a novel use of geometric techniques in a situation where they previously did not seem applicable, namely to understand the arithmetic of high-degree rational functions over small finite fields, despite the fact that in this situation the Weil bounds do not provide useful information.



1.
Introduction

A polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is called a
permutation polynomial
if the function
\alpha\mapsto f(\alpha)

defines a bijection of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Permutation polynomials arise in various contexts in math and engineering. They are
of particular interest when
f(X)
has a simple algebraic form, in which case the interplay between the algebraic
and combinatorial perspectives yields interesting results and challenges.

In the past several years, over
100
papers have addressed permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
having the form

f(X):=X^{r}A(X^{q-1})
where
r
is a positive integer and
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
, following the initial paper
[
48
]

which restated the permutation property of
f(X)
in terms of whether an associated rational function
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)

permutes the set of
(q+1)
-th roots of unity, or equivalently whether an associated
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
permutes

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
. The main advantage of these restatements is that even the simplest choices
for
g(X)
or
h(X)
correspond to interesting permutation polynomials
f(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. For instance, most
of the permutation polynomials in the literature having the form
X^{r}A(X^{q-1})
correspond to cases where
g(X)

and
h(X)
have degree at most
3
. Conversely, all possibilities for
g(X)
or
h(X)
having degree at most
4

have been classified
[
7
]
; we will show elsewhere that this classification quickly implies all previous results
classifying permutation polynomials of the form
X^{r}A(X^{q-1})
where
q
and the coefficients of
A(X)
can vary but

r
and the degrees of the terms of
A(X)
are prescribed.

Recently several authors have produced permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of the form
X^{r}A(X^{q-1})
for which
the corresponding permutation rational function
h(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can have arbitrarily large degree. This led to
a series of conjectures and open problems seeking classifications of all permutation polynomials for a series of
choices of
r
and the degrees of the terms of
A(X)
. In this paper we resolve all of these conjectures and open
problems, by proving the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Write
q:=p^{k}
and
Q:=p^{\ell}
where
p
is prime and
k
and
\ell
are positive integers, and let
r
be a positive
integer such that
r\equiv Q+1\pmod{q+1}
. Write
A(X):=aX^{Q+1}+bX^{Q}+cX+d
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then the polynomial

X^{r}A(X^{q-1})=aX^{r+qQ+q-Q-1}+bX^{r+qQ-Q}+cX^{r+q-1}+dX^{r}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if all of the following hold:

(1)

\gcd(r,q-1)=1
;

(2)

p=2
;

(3)

e:=a^{q+1}+b^{q+1}+c^{q+1}+d^{q+1}
is nonzero;

(4)

(ab^{q}+cd^{q})^{Q}=e^{Q-1}(ac^{q}+bd^{q})
; and

(5)

writing
m
for the largest integer of the form
2^{i}
(with
i\geq 0
) which divides
\gcd(k,\ell)
, we have

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}\Bigl(\frac{b^{q+1}+c^{q+1}}{e}\Bigr)=\frac{\lcm(k,\ell)}{m}.

Since the conditions on
a,b,c,d
in Theorem
1.1
are complicated, we now state an alternate version of the result
which is more useful in some situations (for instance, if one wishes to count the number of permutation polynomials of
this form, or to produce explicit examples). Here
\mu_{q+1}
denotes the set of
(q+1)
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
and if
n
is a positive integer then we write
\ord_{2}(n)
for the largest nonnegative integer
i
such that
2^{i}\mid n
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Write
q:=p^{k}
and
Q:=p^{\ell}
where
p
is prime and
k
and
\ell
are positive integers, and let
r
be a positive
integer such that
r\equiv Q+1\pmod{q+1}
. Write
A(X):=aX^{Q+1}+bX^{Q}+cX+d
with
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Then
X^{r}A(X^{q-1})

permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
p=2
,
\gcd(r,q-1)=1
, and
A(X)=\delta A_{0}(\gamma X)
for some
\gamma\in\mu_{q+1}
,
some
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
, and some
A_{0}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
such that one of the following holds:

(1)

\ord_{2}(k)\leq\ord_{2}(\ell)
and there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
for which

\displaystyle A_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{Q+1}+\beta)X^{Q+1}+(\alpha^{q+Q}+\beta)X^{Q}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\alpha^{qQ+1}+\beta)X+(\alpha^{qQ+q}+\beta);

(2)

\ord_{2}(k)\neq\ord_{2}(\ell)
and either
A_{0}(X)=X^{Q+1}
or there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mu_{q+1}
for which

\displaystyle A_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{qQ+q}+\beta)X^{Q+1}+(\alpha^{qQ}+\alpha\beta)X^{Q}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\alpha^{q}+\alpha^{Q}\beta)X+(1+\alpha^{Q+1}\beta);

(3)

\ord_{2}(k)\geq\ord_{2}(\ell)
and either
A_{0}(X)=X^{Q}
or there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mu_{q+1}
for which

\displaystyle A_{0}(X)

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{qQ}+\alpha^{q}\beta)X^{Q+1}+(\alpha^{qQ+1}+\beta)X^{Q}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+\alpha^{q+Q}\beta)X+(\alpha+\alpha^{Q}\beta).

We now explain how these results differ from all previous results in the subject. As noted above, the results from

[
48
]
reduce the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
to determining when an associated rational function

g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
, or equivalently an associated rational function
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
permutes

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. A standard approach to investigating permutation rational functions, dating back to
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
and
[
17
]
, argues that if
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
then the (possibly reducible) curve
h(X)=h(Y)
has
no non-diagonal
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points, which by Weil’s bounds implies that if
q
is sufficiently large compared to
\deg(h)

then the diagonal is the only geometrically irreducible component of
h(X)=h(Y)
which is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This is the key
first step in many important papers in the subject, since it enables one to use techniques from algebraic geometry,
Galois theory, and group theory; e.g., cf.
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. However, this approach is only useful when

q
is large compared to
\deg(h)
, since otherwise a geometrically irreducible component of
h(X)=h(Y)
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

can have no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points. Since our main results include cases where
q<\deg(h)
, we are forced to introduce
a completely new approach which does not rely on Weil’s bound.

As noted above, our proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
begin by using simple results from
[
47
]
and

[
48
]
to reduce to the problem of determining when an associated rational function
g(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
. Thus
the bulk of the proofs of the above results consists of the proof of the following result, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.3
.

Write
q:=p^{k}
and
Q:=p^{\ell}
where
p
is prime and
k
and
\ell
are positive integers. Pick
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
which are
not all zero, and write
A(X):=aX^{Q+1}+bX^{Q}+cX+d
and
B(X):=d^{q}X^{Q+1}+c^{q}X^{Q}+b^{q}X+a^{q}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

A(X)
has no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
and
g(X):=B(X)/A(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
;

(2)

conditions
(2)–(5)
of Theorem
1.1
all hold;

(3)

p=2
and
A(X)=\delta A_{0}(\gamma X)
for some
\gamma\in\mu_{q+1}
, some
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
, and some
A_{0}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]

such that one of the conditions
(1)–(3)
of Theorem
1.2
holds.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
proceeds in three steps. Loosely speaking, in step 1 we determine information about the geometry
of
g(X)
as a function
\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
; in step 2 we show that certain possibilities for this geometry prevent
g(X)
from permuting

\mu_{q+1}
; and in step 3 we determine all permutations coming from the remaining geometric possibilities. More formally, the steps
are as follows:

(1)

Show that if
A(X)
has no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
and
g(X)
is nonconstant then either two points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})

each have a unique
g
-preimage or one point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
has
g
-preimages with ramification indices
1
and
Q
.

(2)

Show that if some point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
has
g
-preimages with ramification indices
1
and
Q
then
g(X)
does
not permute
\mu_{q+1}
(in fact, we prove a more general result, cf. Theorem
4.1
).

(3)

Determine all choices of
A(X)
which have no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
in case
g(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
and two points of

\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
each have a unique
g
-preimage.

Each of these three steps presents new types of challenges. In the first step we go a long way towards determining the ramification
of all members of each of infinitely many four-parameter families of coverings;
a priori
it is not clear that this problem is
feasible. The second step is entirely new, in that it uses purely geometric information in order to determine when
g(X)
permutes

\mu_{q+1}
. We note that this type of conclusion can be proved when
q>4(Q+1)^{4}
by using Galois theory and Weil’s bound, but we prove
the result without any assumption on the relative sizes of
q
and
Q
, so that our proof cannot use Weil’s bound and hence requires
an entirely new approach. Finally, the third step combines geometric arguments with a series of elementary (but tricky) computations.

The hard part in our work is showing that
g(X)
is not a permutation when item (2) or (3) of Theorem
1.3
does not hold.
The converse implication is much easier, as it just involves computing the denominator of
\rho\circ X^{Q+1}\circ\sigma
for certain
degree-one
\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
, and likewise computing the product of the denominator of
\rho\circ X^{Q-1}\circ\sigma
with
the product of the numerator and denominator of
\sigma(X)
for certain degree-one
\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
. This approach allows
for a short proof that item (3) in Theorem
1.3
implies item (1) (cf.
[
49
]
). One can also use it to show that (2) implies
(1). But completely different ideas are needed in order to show that (1) implies (2) or (3). All previous results showing that (1)
implies (2) or (3) in some special case only applied when
Q=2
, and are immediate consequences of the classification of degree-
3

permutation rational functions (which is proved in a few lines in
[
7
, Thm. 1.3]
, cf. Lemma
2.13
).

We note that the permutation condition in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
immediately implies that
p=2
, since
\alpha
and
-\alpha

have the same image under
X^{r}A(X^{q-1})
. Thus, we could have assumed that
p=2
in those results without significant loss. However,
it is quite difficult to show that item (1) in Theorem
1.3
implies that
p=2
. Our perspective is that the most fundamental
objects in our study are the functions
g(X)
on
\mu_{q+1}

permute

\mu_{q+1}
in case

is odd, even though that situation cannot yield permutations of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

1.1
and
1.2
are complicated, but all the bijective functions

in Theorem
1.3
turn out to have the simple form
\rho\circ X^{n}\circ\sigma
for some

and some degree-one
\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
.

Our classification result immediately yields several further classification results of permutation polynomials
f(X)

over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

with permutation monomials and reducing mod

. Explicitly, we make the

Definition 1.4
.

We say that

are
multiplicatively equivalent
if
g(X)\equiv\beta f(\alpha X^{n})\pmod{X^{q}-X}
for some

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and some positive integer

such that
\gcd(n,q-1)=1
.

Plainly this is an equivalence relation on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, and if
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are multiplicatively equivalent then
f(X)

permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
g(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, if
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are multiplicatively equivalent and

\deg(g)<q
then
g(X)
has at most as many terms as does
f(X)
.

Remark 1.5
.

The above notion has been called “quasi-multiplicative equivalence” in some previous papers, and the term “multiplicative equivalence”
has been used for each of two different notions. However, we cannot envision any situation in which either of the previous definitions
of multiplicative equivalence would be preferable to the definition above, so we encourage subsequent authors to use the above definition.

2.1.
Self-conjugate reciprocal polynomials

We now recall some simple results about self-conjugate reciprocal polynomials, which are defined as follows.

Notation 2.1
.

For
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
we define
g^{(q)}(X)
to be the rational function obtained from
g(X)

by raising every coefficient to the
q
-th power. For any nonzero
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
we define

\widehat{A}(X):=X^{\deg(A)}A^{(q)}(1/X)
. Explicitly, if
A(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}X^{i}
with

\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
\alpha_{n}\neq 0
then
A^{(q)}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{q}X^{i}
and

\widehat{A}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{q}X^{n-i}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a nonzero
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
is
self-conjugate reciprocal
(or SCR for short)
if
\widehat{A}(X)=\alpha A(X)
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.

The next lemma is immediate from the definitions.

Lemma 2.3
.

All of the following hold:

•

If
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
is SCR then
\widehat{A}(X)/A(X)\in\mu_{q+1}
.

•

For nonconstant
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
we have
(g_{1}\circ g_{2})^{(q)}=g_{1}^{(q)}\circ g_{2}^{(q)}
.

•

If
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
is nonzero and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}^{*}
then the multiplicity of
\alpha
as a root
of
A(X)
equals the multiplicity of
\alpha^{-q}
as a root of
\widehat{A}(X)
.

•

A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
is SCR if and only if the multiset of roots of
A(X)
is preserved by the function

\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-q}
.

•

Every degree-
1
SCR polynomial has a root in
\mu_{q+1}
.

•

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
then
\alpha X^{2}+\beta X+\alpha^{q}
is SCR.

We will also use the following simple result.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
q
is even and
A(X):=\alpha X^{2}+\beta X+\alpha^{q}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then
the following are equivalent:

(1)

A(X)
has at least one root in
\mu_{q+1}
;

(2)

A(X)
has two distinct roots in
\mu_{q+1}
;

(3)

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\alpha^{q+1}/\beta^{2})=1
.

Proof.

We may assume
\alpha\neq 0
, since otherwise the result is immediate. Each root
\gamma
of
A(X)
is a root of

(\alpha/\beta^{2})A(X)=(\alpha/\beta)^{2}X^{2}+(\alpha/\beta)X+\alpha^{q+1}/\beta^{2}
, so that
(\alpha/\beta)\gamma

is a root of
X^{2}+X+\alpha^{q+1}/\beta^{2}
. Writing
\epsilon:=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\bigl(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gamma\bigr)
,
it follows that

\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gamma\Bigr)^{q}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gamma=\epsilon^{2}+\epsilon=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\gamma^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gamma\Bigr)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta^{2}}\Bigr).

Multiply by
\gamma
to get

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Bigr)^{q}\gamma^{q+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\gamma^{2}+\gamma\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta^{2}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\gamma+\frac{\alpha^{q}}{\beta}+\gamma\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta^{2}}\Bigr),

so that
\gamma^{q+1}=1
if and only if

\Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\Bigr)^{q}+\frac{\alpha^{q}}{\beta}=\gamma\biggl(1+\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{\alpha^{q+1}}{\beta^{2}}\Bigr)\biggr).

Since
\gamma\neq 0
and the left side of this equation is zero, it follows that
\gamma^{q+1}=1
if and only if
(
3
) holds. Here
\gamma
is an arbitrary root of
A(X)
, so we have shown that (
3
)
is equivalent to both (
1
) and (
2
).
∎

.

We will give a comprehensive treatment of SCR polynomials in a forthcoming paper.

2.2.
Rational functions

We make the following conventions about rational functions. Let
K
be a a field and let
g(X)=N(X)/D(X)
where

N,D\in K[X]
with
D(X)
monic. Let
C(X)
be the monic greatest common divisor of

and
D(X)
in
K[X]
,
and write

and
D(X)=C(X)D_{0}(X)
with

. We make no distinction between

and

. Thus, we view

as defining a function
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
given by

, so that in particular

is defined at elements

even if

. We refer to
N_{0}(X)
and

as the numerator and denominator of
g(X)
,
respectively, and we define
\deg(g):=\max(\deg(N_{0}),\deg(D_{0}))
if
g(X)\neq 0
. We say that a nonconstant

g(X)\in K(X)
is
separable
if the field extension
K(x)/K(g(x))
is separable, where
x
is transcendental
over
K
; it is known that
g(X)
is separable if and only if
g(X)\notin K(X^{p})
where

is the characteristic
of

(e.g., cf.
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Definition 2.6
.

We say that nonconstant
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
are
linearly equivalent
if
g=\rho\circ f\circ\sigma
for some
degree-one
\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
.

Note that if
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
are linearly equivalent then
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
g(X)

permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

For any field
K
and any degree-one
\rho(X)\in K(X)
, if we write

\rho(X):=(\alpha X+\beta)/(\gamma X+\delta)

with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in K
then we define

\rho^{-1}(X):=(\delta X-\beta)/(-\gamma X+\alpha).

This definition does not change if we multiply all of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
by a common element of
K^{*}
,
and we note that
\rho\circ\rho^{-1}=X=\rho^{-1}\circ\rho
. In particular, if
\rho\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
has degree one
then
(\rho^{(q)})^{-1}=(\rho^{-1})^{(q)}
.

We recall two simple results about degree-one rational functions from
[
48
]
:

Lemma 2.7
.

A degree-one
\rho(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if
\rho(X)=(\beta^{q}X+\alpha^{q})/(\alpha X+\beta)

for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with
\alpha^{q+1}\neq\beta^{q+1}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

A degree-one
\rho(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(X)
maps
\mu_{q+1}
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if

\rho(X)=(\delta X+\gamma\delta^{q})/(X+\gamma)
for some
\gamma\in\mu_{q+1}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Lemma 2.11

Let

2.4.
Ramification

We now introduce the notation and terminology we will use when discussing ramification.

As usual, for any nonconstant
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(X)
and any

, the
ramification index

e_{g}(\alpha)
is the multiplicity of
\alpha
as a
g
-preimage of
g(\alpha)
; explicitly, for any degree-one

\rho,\sigma\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(X)
such that
\sigma(0)=\alpha
and
\rho(g(\alpha))=0
, the positive integer
e_{g}(\alpha)

is the degree of the lowest-degree term of the numerator of
\rho\circ g\circ\sigma
. For
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})

we define the
ramification multiset
of
g(X)
over
\beta
to be the multiset
E_{g}(\beta)
consisting of the
ramification indices
e_{g}(\alpha)
with
\alpha\in g^{-1}(\beta)
. Thus
E_{g}(\beta)
is a collection of positive
integers whose sum is
\deg(g)
. We make the convention that if
g(X)
is constant then
E_{g}(\beta)
is the empty
multiset. We say that
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
is a
ramification point
of

if

,
and that
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
is a
branch point
of

if
\beta=g(\alpha)
for some ramification
point
\alpha
of
g(X)
. We will use the following consequence of the Riemann–Hurwitz genus formula for the map

g\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, or equivalently for the function field extension
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(x)/\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(g(x))
where

x
is transcendental over
\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}
; e.g., cf.
[
34
, Thm. 3.4.13 and Thm. 3.5.1]
:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
g(X)\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}(X)
be a separable rational function of degree

of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})

,

, and

. Now suppose that

has a root

. In this case we need only show that if

is even
then (

) does not hold. Suppose otherwise, so that

,

,

has a second root

.

Henceforth we assume that

Henceforth assume that

, so that

. Write

,

, respectively. If

), (

) and (

In the remaining case

Henceforth assume that

4.
Near-polynomial permutations

In this section we study permutation rational functions
h(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which are “nearly” polynomials, in the
sense that some
\gamma\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
has exactly two
h
-preimages. We determine all such permutation rational
functions when the ramification indices of the points in
h^{-1}(\gamma)
satisfy certain mild constraints. The main
result is as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
be a nonconstant rational function whose ramification multiset over some point

\gamma\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})
is
[s,t]
, where
s
and
t
are positive integers and
\gcd(t,q+1)=1
. Then
h(X)

permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
s\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

We first prove the following result about permutations of
\mu_{q+1}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Write
g(X):=X^{s}(\alpha^{q}X^{t}-1)/(X^{t}-\alpha)
where
s
is any integer,
t
is a positive integer coprime to
q+1
,
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}\setminus\mu_{q+1}
. Then
g(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if
s\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}
.

Proof.

If
s\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}
then
g(X)
induces the same map on
\mu_{q+1}
as
\rho(X)\circ X^{t}
, where

\rho(X):=(\alpha^{q}X-1)/(X-\alpha)
. Thus
g(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
since both
X^{t}
and
\rho(X)
permute
\mu_{q+1}
.

Conversely, suppose that
g(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
, so that

\sum_{\beta\in\mu_{q+1}}g(\beta)=\sum_{\beta\in\mu_{q+1}}\beta.

The right side is fixed by multiplication by any nontrivial
(q+1)
-th root of unity, and hence equals
0
.
For any
\beta\in\mu_{q+1}
we have
\beta^{t}-\alpha\neq 0
and

g(\beta)=\beta^{s}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{q}\beta^{t}-1}{\beta^{t}-\alpha}=-\beta^{s+t}\cdot\frac{\beta^{tq}-\alpha^{q}}{\beta^{t}-\alpha}=-\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\alpha^{q-1-i}\beta^{s+t(i+1)},

so that

(4.3)

0=\sum_{\beta\in\mu_{q+1}}g(\beta)=-\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\alpha^{q-1-i}\sum_{\beta\in\mu_{q+1}}\beta^{s+t(i+1)}.

For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, the summation
\sum_{\beta\in\mu_{q+1}}\beta^{j}
is nonzero if and only if
j\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}
.
In particular, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\epsilon_{i}:=\sum_{\beta\in\mu_{q+1}}\beta^{s+t(i+1)}\neq 0
if and only if

s\equiv-t(i+1)\pmod{q+1}
. Since
\gcd(t,q+1)=1
, there is exactly one integer
i
with
0\leq i\leq q

for which
\epsilon_{i}\neq 0
. By (
4.3
) this distinguished integer
i
must be
q
, so that

s\equiv-t(q+1)\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}
.
∎

The following consequence of Lemma
4.2
is not used in this paper, but is stated for its inherent interest.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
r
and
t
be positive integers with
\gcd(t,q+1)=1
. Pick
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and write

f(X):=X^{r}(X^{t(q-1)}-\alpha)
. Then
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
\gcd(r,q-1)=1
,

r\equiv t\pmod{q+1}
, and
\alpha\notin\mu_{q+1}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
4.2
, in light of Lemmas
2.9
and
2.10
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The special case
r=1
of Corollary
4.4
is
[
21
, Thm. 2]
; the special case that
t=1
and

1\leq r\leq q+1
is
[
24
, Thm. 3.1]
. The proofs in
[
21
]
and
[
24
]
involve complicated computations.

We now prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
p
be the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
\ell
be the largest nonnegative integer for which
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X^{p^{\ell}})
.
Then
h=h_{0}\circ X^{p^{\ell}}
where
h_{0}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is separable. Here
h(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
h_{0}(X)

does, and
E_{h_{0}}(\gamma)=[s_{0},t_{0}]
where
s_{0}:=s/p^{\ell}
and
t_{0}:=t/p^{\ell}
, so Theorem
4.1
holds for
h(X)
if and
only if it holds for
h_{0}(X)
. Thus we may replace
h(X)
by
h_{0}(X)
in order to assume that
h(X)
is separable.

Write
n:=\deg(h)
, so that
n=s+t
. Since there are no separable degree-
2
permutation rational functions (e.g., by

[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
), we may assume that
n\geq 3
. Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be the
h
-preimages of
\gamma
, where

\alpha
and
\beta
have ramification indices under
h(X)
being
s
and
t
, respectively. Every
\lambda\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q})

satisfies
e_{h}(\lambda)=e_{h^{(q)}}(\lambda^{q})
, so since
h(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
we have
e_{h}(\lambda)=e_{h}(\lambda^{q})
. Moreover,

h(\lambda^{q})=h(\lambda)^{q}
, so the
q
-th power map preserves the set of branch points of
h(X)
which have any prescribed
ramification multiset, and also the
q
-th power map permutes the set of
h
-preimages of any element of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

First suppose
\gamma\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then the
q
-th power map preserves
\{\alpha,\beta\}
, so that this set contains
either zero or two elements of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In particular,
h^{-1}(\gamma)\cap\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
cannot have size
1
, so
h(X)

does not permute
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Next suppose
\gamma\notin\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})
. Then
\gamma
,
\gamma^{q}
, and
\gamma^{q^{2}}
are pairwise distinct,
and they each have
h
-ramification multiset
[s,t]
. By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula (Lemma
2.12
), it follows
that
2n-2\geq 3\bigl(s+t-2\bigr)=3(n-2)
, so that
n\leq 4
, whence
n\in\{3,4\}
. If
n=3
then
\{s,t\}=\{1,2\}
,
so Corollary
2.14
implies that
h(X)
does not permute
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Thus we must have
n=4
. Since

\gcd(t,q+1)=1
by hypothesis, in particular we cannot have
s=t=2
in case
q
is odd, so that Corollary
2.16

implies that
h(X)
does not permute
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

The remaining possibility is that
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\gamma^{q}\neq\gamma
and we have

h^{-1}(\gamma^{q})=\{\alpha^{q},\beta^{q}\}
where
e_{h}(\alpha^{q})=s
and
e_{h}(\beta^{q})=t
, so that in particular
\alpha^{q}\neq\beta
.
Likewise,
h^{-1}(\gamma)=h^{-1}(\gamma^{q^{2}})=\{\alpha^{q^{2}},\beta^{q^{2}}\}
where
e_{h}(\alpha^{q^{2}})=s
and
e_{h}(\beta^{q^{2}})=t
,
so that either
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
or
\beta=\alpha^{q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, where in the latter
case
s=t
.

First suppose
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the degree-one rational functions
\rho(X):=(X-\gamma^{q})/(X-\gamma)

and
\sigma(X):=(X-\alpha^{q})/(X-\alpha)
map
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
bijectively onto
\mu_{q+1}
, so that
g:=\rho\circ h\circ\sigma^{-1}

permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if
h(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Here the poles of
g(X)
are
\infty
and

\delta:=\sigma(\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}\setminus\mu_{q+1}
, with ramification indices
s
and
t
, respectively,
and likewise the zeroes of
g(X)
are
0
and
\sigma(\beta^{q})=1/\sigma^{(q)}(\beta^{q})=1/\delta^{q}
. It follows that

g(X)=\epsilon X^{s}(\delta^{q}X-1)^{t}/(X-\delta)^{t}
for some
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}^{*}
. Since
g=\rho\circ h\circ\sigma^{-1}

maps
\mu_{q+1}
into
\mu_{q+1}
, and also
X^{s}(\delta^{q}X-1)^{t}/(X-\delta)^{t}
maps
\mu_{q+1}
into
\mu_{q+1}
,
we must have
\epsilon\in\mu_{q+1}
. Since
X^{t}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
and
X^{t}\circ g_{1}=g\circ X^{t}
where

g_{1}(X):=\epsilon_{1}X^{s}(\delta^{q}X^{t}-1)/(X^{t}-\delta)
and
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}^{t}
with
\epsilon_{1}\in\mu_{q+1}
,
we see that
h(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
g_{1}(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
. Finally, Lemma
4.2

says that
g_{1}(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if
s\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}
, which concludes the proof when

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Finally, suppose that
\beta=\alpha^{q^{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
s=t
. As above,
\rho(X):=(X-\gamma^{q})/(X-\gamma)

maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
bijectively onto
\mu_{q+1}
, so that
g:=\rho\circ h\circ\rho^{-1}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if

h(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Here the poles of
g(X)
are
\delta:=\rho(\alpha)
and
\rho(\beta)=\rho(\alpha^{q^{2}})=\delta^{q^{2}}
,
and likewise the zeroes are
1/\delta^{q}
and
1/\delta^{q^{3}}
, where each zero and pole has ramification index
t
. Writing

A(X):=(X-\delta)(X-\delta^{q^{2}})
and
g_{2}(X):=X^{2}A^{(q)}(1/X)/A(X)
, it follows that
g(X)=\epsilon g_{2}(X)^{t}
for some

\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\mathbb{F}}}$}_{q}^{*}
. Since
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, we have
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, so that

A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
and
A(X)
has no roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. By Lemma
2.10
we have
g_{2}(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq\mu_{q+1}
;
since also
g(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq\mu_{q+1}
, it follows that
\epsilon\in\mu_{q+1}
. Thus
h(X)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

if and only if
g_{2}(X)
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
, or equivalently
h_{2}(X):=\rho^{-1}\circ g_{2}\circ\rho
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
By Lemma
2.11
we have
h_{2}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
, so that
h_{2}(X)
cannot permute
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
since there do not exist
separable degree-
2
permutation rational functions (e.g., by
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
).
∎

We conclude this section with the following reformulation of Theorem
4.1
in term of permutations of
\mu_{q+1}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
has no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
, and
r,s,t
are integers with
\gcd(t,q+1)=1
. Suppose

g(X):=X^{r}A^{(q)}(1/X)/A(X)
has ramification multiset
[s,t]
over some
\gamma\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Then
g(X)

permutes
\mu_{q+1}
if and only if
s\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}

We next recall an easy known fact about greatest common divisors, whose proof we include for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
i
and
j
are positive integers then

•

\gcd(2^{i}+1,2^{j}+1)=1
if and only if
\ord_{2}(i)\neq\ord_{2}(j)
;

•

\gcd(2^{i}-1,2^{j}+1)=1
if and only if
\ord_{2}(i)\leq\ord_{2}(j)
.

Proof.

It is well known that
\gcd(2^{m}-1,2^{n}-1)=2^{\gcd(m,n)}-1
for any positive integers
m
and
n
. Since

\gcd(2^{j}-1,2^{j}+1)=1
, it follows that

\gcd(2^{i}-1,2^{j}+1)=\frac{\gcd(2^{i}-1,2^{2j}-1)}{\gcd(2^{i}-1,2^{j}-1)}=\frac{2^{\gcd(i,2j)}-1}{2^{\gcd(i,j)}-1},

which equals
1
precisely when
\gcd(i,2j)=\gcd(i,j)
, or equivalently
\ord_{2}(i)\leq\ord_{2}(j)
. Likewise

\gcd(2^{i}+1,2^{j}+1)=\frac{\gcd(2^{2i}-1,2^{j}+1)}{\gcd(2^{i}-1,2^{j}+1)}=\frac{2^{\gcd(2i,2j)}-1}{2^{\gcd(2i,j)}-1}\cdot\frac{2^{\gcd(i,j)}-1}{2^{\gcd(i,2j)}-1},

which equals
1
precisely when
\ord_{2}(i)\neq\ord_{2}(j)
.
∎

U(X)\mid A(X)
and
\theta=0
otherwise, so by (
7.10
) we have

n=Q-1
if
\theta=1
and
n=Q+1
if
\theta=0
.

Suppose that
n=Q-1
, so that
\theta=1
and
U(X)\mid A(X)
. Then (
7.7
) implies that
U(X)
has no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
, which
by Lemma
2.4
yields
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\zeta)=0
. By Lemma
6.5
, if
\ord_{2}(k)<\ord_{2}(\ell)
then
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}(\eta)=1
,
and if
\ord_{2}(k)\geq\ord_{2}(\ell)
then
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}(\eta)=k/m
. Thus (
7.6
) holds if and only if
\ord_{2}(k)\geq\ord_{2}(\ell)
,
which by Lemma
5.2
is equivalent to
\gcd(Q-1,q+1)=1
. Since
U(X)
has no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
, by (
7.9
) and
Lemma
5.1
we see that (
7.6
) and (
7.2
) are equivalent.

The remaining possibility is
n=Q+1
, so that
\theta=0
and
U(X)\nmid A(X)
. By Lemma
6.5
, if
\ord_{2}(k)<\ord_{2}(\ell)

then
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}(\eta)=0
, and if
\ord_{2}(k)\geq\ord_{2}(\ell)
then
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}(\eta)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\zeta)
.

If
U(X)
has roots in
\mu_{q+1}
then
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\zeta)=1
, and Lemma
5.1
implies that (
7.2
) holds if and only
if
\gcd(Q+1,q-1)=1

this is equivalent to
\ord_{2}(k)\leq\ord_{2}(\ell)
, which by the previous paragraph is equivalent
to (

).

Finally, if
U(X)
has no roots in
\mu_{q+1}
then
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\zeta)=0

5.1
implies that (

) holds

∎

8.
A general polynomial identity

In this section we prove an identity involving bivariate polynomials. This identity seems interesting for its own sake,
and in addition we use it in the next section to prove the conjecture from
[
30
, Rem. 2]
about permutations of

\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Unexpectedly, this identity provides a new proof of a result of Cusick and Müller
[
4
]
about
the functions
\mathbb{F}_{q}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
induced by certain polynomials that are
not
permutation polynomials.

Throughout this section we use the following notation:

•

q
is a prime power,

•

n
is a positive integer,

•

\Delta
is the set of all elements
(w^{q}-w)^{q-1}
with
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The main result in this section is as follows.

Theorem 8.1
.

The following identity holds in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y]
:

(8.2)

\begin{multlined}X^{q^{n}-1}-1+\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(1+zX-z^{q}Y)\\
=-Y\cdot\biggl(\frac{X^{q^{n}-1}-Y^{q^{n}-1}}{X^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-Y^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}}\biggr)\cdot\Big(Y^{\frac{q^{n}-q}{q-1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X^{1+\frac{q^{n}-q^{i+1}}{q-1}}Y^{\frac{q^{i}-q}{q-1}}\Big).\end{multlined}

We first factor the left side
of (
8.2
) in case
X=1
.

Lemma 8.3
.

We have
\lvert\Delta\rvert=(q^{n-1}-1)/(q-1)
and

\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(1+z-z^{q}Y)=-\biggl(\frac{Y^{q^{n}}-Y}{Y^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-1}\biggr)\cdot\prod_{u\in\Delta}(Y-u)^{q}.

Proof.

For any
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, let
\phi_{u}\colon\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be the function
z\mapsto uz^{q}-z
, and note that

\phi_{u}
is a linear map of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces. For any
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, the unique root of
1+z-z^{q}Y
is the
unique
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
for which
\phi_{u}(z)=1
. Thus the multiplicity of any prescribed
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
as a root of

\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(1+z-z^{q}Y)
equals
e_{u}:=\lvert\phi_{u}^{-1}(1)\rvert
. We now compute
e_{u}
for each
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
This is easy when
u
is not in
\Omega:=\mu_{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}
, since then
\phi_{u}
has trivial kernel and hence is
bijective, so that
e_{u}=1
. Now suppose that
u\in\Omega
, so that
u=v^{q-1}
for some
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. Then the
kernel of
\phi_{u}
is
\{\alpha/v\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
, whence
e_{u}
is in
\{0,q\}
, with
e_{u}=q
if and only
if
uz^{q}-z=1
for some
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Write
w:=vz
to restate the last condition as
w^{q}-w=v
for some
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.
Since
v\neq 0
, the last condition occurs if and only if
u\in\Delta
. Combining the above conclusions with the
equality
\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}z=-1
yields

\displaystyle\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(1+z-z^{q}Y)

\displaystyle=-\prod_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}(Y-u)^{e_{u}}

\displaystyle=-\prod_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\Omega}(Y-u)\cdot\prod_{u\in\Delta}(Y-u)^{q}

\displaystyle=-\biggl(\frac{Y^{q^{n}}-Y}{Y^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-1}\biggr)\cdot\prod_{u\in\Delta}(Y-u)^{q}.

The expression for
\lvert\Delta\rvert
follows upon equating the degrees of the two sides.
∎

Lemma
8.3
yields the following reformulation of the main result of
[
4
]
.

Corollary 8.4
.

The following identity holds in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[Y]
:

\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\bigl(Y-(z+1)z^{q-1}\bigr)=Y^{2}\cdot\biggl(\frac{Y^{q^{n}-1}-1}{Y^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-1}\biggr)\cdot\prod_{u\in\Delta}(Y-u)^{q}.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
8.3
since

\displaystyle\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\bigl(Y-(z+1)z^{q-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\bigl(Y-z^{q-1}-z^{q}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\bigl(Y-w^{1-q}-w^{-q}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\bigl(w^{-q}\cdot(w^{q}Y-w-1)\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\prod_{w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}\bigl(w^{q}Y-w-1\bigr).\qed

Lemma 8.5
.

The following identity holds in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[Y]
:

\prod_{u\in\Delta}(Y-u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y^{\frac{q^{i-1}-1}{q-1}}.

Proof.

Since both sides are monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[Y]
of the same degree, it suffices to show that the left side divides
the right side, by showing that each
u\in\Delta
is a root of
H(Y):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y^{\frac{q^{i-1}-1}{q-1}}
. Pick
u\in\Delta
,
so that
u=(w^{q}-w)^{q-1}
with
w\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and thus
H(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w^{q}-w)^{q^{i-1}-1}
. Then

(w^{q}-w)H(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w^{q}-w)^{q^{i-1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(w^{q^{i}}-w^{q^{i-1}}\bigr)=w^{q^{n}}-w=0,

so since
w\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
we conclude that
H(u)=0
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
8.1
.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Since each side of (
8.2
) is a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y]
having
X
-degree less than
q^{n}-1
, it suffices to
show that, for each of the
q^{n}-1
values
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, we obtain an equality in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[Y]
when we substitute

u
for
X
in (
8.2
). Pick any
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, and put
Z:=u^{-q}Y
. Then the left side of (
8.2
)
becomes
\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}}(1+zu-z^{q}u^{q}Z)
; upon substituting
w:=zu
, it follows from Lemma
8.3
and

8.5
that this equals

(8.6)

-\biggl(\frac{Z^{q^{n}}-Z}{Z^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-1}\biggr)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z^{\frac{q^{i}-q}{q-1}}.

After our substitutions, the right side of (
8.2
) becomes

-u^{q}Z\cdot\biggl(\frac{1-Z^{q^{n}-1}}{u^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-(u^{q}Z)^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}}\biggr)\cdot\Bigl((u^{q}Z)^{\frac{q^{n}-q}{q-1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u^{1+\frac{q^{n}-q^{i+1}}{q-1}}(u^{q}Z)^{\frac{q^{i}-q}{q-1}}\Bigr).

Since
u^{(q^{n}-1)/(q-1)}
is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, it equals its
q
-th power, so the above expression becomes

-u^{q}Z\cdot\biggl(\frac{1-Z^{q^{n}-1}}{u^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}-(uZ)^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}}}\biggr)\cdot\Bigl(u^{\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}-q}Z^{\frac{q^{n}-q}{q-1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u^{1+\frac{q^{n}-q^{2}}{q-1}}Z^{\frac{q^{i}-q}{q-1}}\Bigr),

which equals (
8.6
).
∎

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Writing any
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
as
x+\omega y

with
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
z^{q}=x+\omega^{2}y
so that
z+z^{q}=y
and
\omega^{2}z+\omega z^{q}=x
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}(z)

\displaystyle=(x+\alpha y)^{Q+1}+(\beta y)^{Q+1}+\omega(y+\alpha x)^{Q+1}+\omega(\beta x)^{Q+1}

\displaystyle=(1+\omega\alpha^{Q+1}+\omega\beta^{Q+1})x^{Q+1}+(\alpha+\omega\alpha^{Q})x^{Q}y+(\alpha^{Q}+\omega\alpha)xy^{Q}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\alpha^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1}+\omega)y^{Q+1},

which equals
\omega^{2}az^{qQ+q}+\omega bz^{qQ+1}+cz^{q+Q}+\omega^{2}dz^{Q+1}
where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1}+\alpha^{Q}+1,

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1}+\alpha^{Q}+\alpha,

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{Q}+\alpha+1,

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1}+\alpha+1.

For
z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
we have
\omega\widehat{\psi}(\omega^{2}z)=f(z)
where

f(X):=aX^{qQ+q}+bX^{qQ+1}+cX^{q+Q}+dX^{Q+1}.

Since
\omega X
and
\omega^{2}X
permute
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, it follows that
\widehat{\psi}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if
and only if
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Since
k
is odd,
r:=Q+1
is coprime to
q-1
. Now the case
r=Q+1

of Theorem
1.1
says
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if all of the following hold:

(9.8.1)

\widetilde{e}:=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}
is nonzero;

(9.8.2)

(ab+cd)^{Q}=\widetilde{e}^{Q-1}(ac+bd)
;

(9.8.3)

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\bigl((b^{2}+c^{2})/\widetilde{e}\bigr)=1
.

We compute
\widetilde{e}=((\alpha+1)^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1})^{2}=e
,
so that (
9.8.1
) says
(\alpha+1)^{Q+1}\neq\beta^{Q+1}
. Since
\gcd(q-1,Q+1)=1
, this is equivalent
to
\alpha+1\neq\beta
. One can check that (
9.8.2
) is equivalent to the condition
u^{Q}=e^{Q-1}v

in Corollary
9.6
. Next, if (
9.8.1
) and (
9.8.2
) hold then a routine computation yields

\frac{b^{2}+c^{2}}{e}=\lambda^{Q}+\lambda+1

where

\lambda:=\frac{u+e}{e}\alpha.

Since
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\lambda^{Q}+\lambda+1)=1
, it follows that (
9.8.1
) and (
9.8.2
) imply (
9.8.3
),
which concludes the proof of Corollary
9.6
.

Next, (
9.8.2
) says
H(\alpha,\beta)=0
where

\displaystyle H(X,Y):=

\displaystyle X^{2Q^{2}+2Q+1}Y+X^{2Q^{2}+Q}Y^{Q+2}+X^{2Q^{2}+1}Y+X^{Q^{2}+2Q+2}Y^{Q^{2}}

\displaystyle+X^{Q^{2}+2Q}Y^{Q^{2}}+X^{Q^{2}+2}Y^{Q^{2}}+X^{Q^{2}}Y^{Q^{2}+2Q+2}+X^{Q^{2}}Y^{Q^{2}}

\displaystyle+X^{2Q+1}Y+X^{Q+2}Y^{2Q^{2}+Q}+X^{Q}Y^{2Q^{2}+Q}+X^{Q}Y^{Q+2}

\displaystyle+XY^{2Q^{2}+2Q+1}+XY.

The special case of (
8.2
) in which
q
and
n
are replaced by
Q
and
2
says

\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}^{*}}(1+zX+z^{Q}Y)=1+X^{Q^{2}-1}+Y\Big(\frac{X^{Q^{2}-1}+Y^{Q^{2}-1}}{X^{Q+1}+Y^{Q+1}}\Big)\Big(Y^{Q}+X\Big).

Homogenize this by substituting
X/Z
and
Y/Z
for
X
and
Y
, and then multiplying by
Z^{Q^{2}-1}
, to get

\displaystyle\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}^{*}}(Z+zX+z^{Q}Y)

\displaystyle=Z^{Q^{2}-1}+X^{Q^{2}-1}

\displaystyle\quad+Y\Big(\frac{X^{Q^{2}-1}+Y^{Q^{2}-1}}{X^{Q+1}+Y^{Q+1}}\Big)\Big(Y^{Q}+XZ^{Q-1}\Big).

Multiplying both sides by
Z(X^{Q+1}+Y^{Q+1})
yields

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\cdot(X^{Q+1}+Y^{Q+1})\cdot\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}^{*}}(Z+zX+z^{Q}Y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=(Z^{Q^{2}}+X^{Q^{2}-1}Z)\cdot(X^{Q+1}+Y^{Q+1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+(X^{Q^{2}-1}Y+Y^{Q^{2}})\cdot(Y^{Q}Z+XZ^{Q}).

Now substitute
\alpha^{2}+1
for
X
,
\beta^{2}
for
Y
, and
\alpha\beta
for
Z
, in order to obtain

\displaystyle\alpha\beta

\displaystyle\Bigl((\alpha+1)^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1}\Bigr)^{2}\prod_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}^{*}}\Bigl(\alpha\beta+z(\alpha+1)^{2}+z^{Q}\beta^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl((\alpha\beta)^{Q^{2}}+(\alpha+1)^{2Q^{2}-2}\alpha\beta\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl((\alpha+1)^{Q+1}+\beta^{Q+1}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Bigl((\alpha+1)^{Q^{2}-1}\beta+\beta^{Q^{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\cdot\Bigl(\alpha\beta^{2Q+1}+(\alpha+1)^{2}(\alpha\beta)^{Q}\Bigr).

The right side of this last equation equals
H(\alpha,\beta)
.
Since (
9.8.2
) says
H(\alpha,\beta)=0
, and
\alpha,\beta\neq 0
by hypothesis, it follows that (
9.8.1
) and (
9.8.2
)
are both true if and only if
(\alpha+1)^{Q+1}\neq\beta^{Q+1}
and
\alpha\beta+z(\alpha+1)^{2}+z^{Q}\beta^{2}=0
for some
z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}^{*}
.

We now show that if
\alpha+1\neq\beta
and
z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
then
\alpha\beta+z(\alpha+1)^{2}+z^{Q}\beta^{2}\neq 0
.
Suppose otherwise. Since
\alpha\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
\alpha\beta=(\alpha\beta)^{q}
, so that

z(\alpha+1)^{2}+z^{Q}\beta^{2}=z^{q}(\alpha+1)^{2}+z^{qQ}\beta^{2},

or equivalently

(z+z^{q})(\alpha+1)^{2}=(z+z^{q})^{Q}\beta^{2}.

Since
z\in\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we have
z\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so that
z+z^{q}\neq 0
and thus

\Bigl(\frac{\alpha+1}{\beta}\Bigr)^{2}=(z+z^{q})^{Q-1}.

Here the left side is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the right side is in
\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}^{*}
, so since
\mathbb{F}_{q}\cap\mathbb{F}_{Q^{2}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}

it follows that both sides equal
1
. But then
\alpha+1=\beta
, contradicting our hypothesis.

Thus (
9.8.1
) and (
9.8.2
) both hold if and only if
(\alpha+1)^{Q+1}\neq\beta^{Q+1}
and
\alpha\beta+(\alpha+1)^{2}+\beta^{2}=0
.
In fact the latter condition implies the former: if
\alpha\beta+(\alpha+1)^{2}+\beta^{2}=0
then
\alpha+1\neq\beta
since

\alpha\beta\neq 0
, which implies
(\alpha+1)^{Q+1}\neq\beta^{Q+1}
since
X^{Q+1}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since we showed above
that (
9.8.1
) and (
9.8.2
) imply (
9.8.3
), this concludes the proof of Corollary
9.5
.
∎

Our next result refines the sufficient condition in
[
44
, Thm. 3.2]
to obtain a necessary and sufficient
condition, as discussed in
[
44
, Rem. 2]
:

Corollary 9.9
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be positive integers and write
m:=2^{\ord_{2}(\gcd(k,\ell))}
. Suppose that
q:=2^{k}
and

Q:=2^{\ell}
satisfy
\gcd(Q-1,q+1)=1
. Write
A(X):=1+aX^{u}+dX^{v}
where
a,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
u,v
are positive
integers such that
u(Q-1)\equiv Q\pmod{q+1}
and
v(Q-1)\equiv q\pmod{q+1}
. Then
f(X):=XA(X^{q-1})

permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if all of the following hold:

(9.9.1)

e:=1+a^{2}+d^{2}
is nonzero;

(9.9.2)

d^{Q}=e^{Q-1}a
;

(9.9.3)

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}\Bigl(\frac{a^{2}+d^{2}}{e}\Bigr)=0
.

Proof.

Let
s
be a positive integer such that
\gcd(s,q-1)=1
and
s\equiv Q-1\pmod{q+1}
. Then
\gcd(s,q^{2}-1)=1
,
so that
X^{s}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, and thus
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
f(X^{s})
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
We now determine the reductions mod
q^{2}-1
of the degrees of the terms of

f(X^{s})=X^{s}+aX^{su(q-1)+s}+dX^{sv(q-1)+s}.

Here
r:=sv(q-1)+s
is congruent to
s\pmod{q-1}
, and hence is coprime to
q-1
, while also

r\equiv(Q-1)v(q-1)+Q-1\equiv q(q-1)+Q-1\equiv Q+1\pmod{q+1}.

Next,

su(q-1)-sv(q-1)\equiv(Q-q)(q-1)\equiv(Q+1)(q-1)\pmod{q+1},

so that
su(q-1)-sv(q-1)\equiv(Q+1)(q-1)\pmod{q^{2}-1}
since both sides are divisible by
q-1
. Likewise,
we have
-sv(q-1)\equiv q-1\pmod{q^{2}-1}
. Thus
f(X^{s})
induces the same function on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
as does

f_{1}(X):=X^{r+q-1}+aX^{r+(Q+1)(q-1)}+dX^{r}.

By Theorem
1.1
,
f_{1}(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if (
.1
), (
.2
), and the following
equation (
9.10
) all hold:

(9.10)

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)=\frac{\lcm(k,\ell)}{m}.

It remains to show that (
9.10
) is equivalent to (
.3
). We compute

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{e}\Bigr)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}\Bigl(1+\frac{a^{2}+d^{2}}{e}\Bigr)=\frac{k}{m}+\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}}\Bigl(\frac{a^{2}+d^{2}}{e}\Bigr).

The hypothesis
\gcd(Q-1,q+1)=1
implies that
\ord_{2}(k)\geq\ord_{2}(\ell)
, so that
\lcm(k,\ell)/m\equiv k/m\pmod{2}
.
Thus indeed (
9.10
) holds if and only if (
.3
) holds.
∎

Remark 9.11
.

Theorem 3.2 of
[
44
]
asserts that the polynomial
f(X)
in Corollary
9.9
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

if certain conditions hold. These sufficient conditions include (
.1
)–(
.3
) in addition to
other conditions, the most notable of which is
(a+d)\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(d/e)=0
. Corollary
9.9
shows
that this last condition is superfluous. In fact, it can be deduced from the combination of Lemma
6.2

and Theorem
1.3
that if
\ord_{2}(k)\neq\ord_{2}(\ell)
then (
.1
)–(
.3
) imply that

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(d/e)=0
, but if
\ord_{2}(k)=\ord_{2}(\ell)
then the permutation polynomials in
Corollary
9.9
for which
(a+d)\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(d/e)\neq 0
are

X+\frac{\lambda^{Q-2}+\lambda^{Q}}{1+\lambda^{2Q-2}}X^{u(q-1)+1}+\frac{1+\lambda^{2Q}}{\lambda+\lambda^{2Q-1}}X^{v(q-1)+1}

where
\lambda\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\mu_{Q+1}
. In particular, such examples exist whenever
Q
is not a power
of
q
. Moreover, one can show that distinct
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mu_{q+1}\setminus\mu_{Q+1}
yield the same
polynomial if and only if
\lambda^{\prime}=1/\lambda
.

Our final result resolves
[
44
, Open problem]
by showing that the sufficient conditions in
[
44
, Thm. 3.1]

are also necessary:

Corollary 9.12
.

Let
k
and
\ell
be positive integers with
\gcd(2k,\ell)=1
, and write
q:=2^{k}
and
Q:=2^{\ell}
and
u:=(Q^{k+1}-1)/(Q-1)
.
Pick
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with
a+b\neq 1
. Then
f(X):=X+bX^{q}+aX^{u}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if there
exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
such that all of the following hold:

(9.12.1)

\lambda^{Q-1}=b
;

(9.12.2)

a=\epsilon\lambda^{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda^{Q-2^{i}}
;

(9.12.3)

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\frac{a}{a+b+1})=0
.

Proof.

The hypothesis
\gcd(2k,\ell)=1
implies that
\gcd(q^{2}-1,Q-1)=1
, so that
X^{Q-1}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
Thus
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
f(X^{Q-1})
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Note that

u(Q-1)-(Q-q)=(Q^{k+1}-1)-(Q-q)=Q(Q^{k}-1)+(q-1)=Q(q^{\ell}-1)+(q-1)

is divisible by
q-1
, and

Q(q^{\ell}-1)+(q-1)\equiv-2Q-2\pmod{q+1},

so that

u(Q-1)-(Q-q)\equiv(Q+1)(q-1)\pmod{q^{2}-1}.

Thus if
r
is a positive integer satisfying
r\equiv Q-q\pmod{q^{2}-1}
then
f(X^{Q-1})
induces the same function
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
as does

f_{1}(X):=X^{r+q-1}+bX^{r+Q(q-1)}+aX^{r+(Q+1)(q-1)}.

Note that
r\equiv Q+1\pmod{q+1}
and
r\equiv Q-1\pmod{q-1}
, so that
\gcd(r,q-1)=1
. Thus Theorem
1.1

implies that
f_{1}(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if all of the following hold:

(9.13.1)

e:=a^{q+1}+b^{2}+1
is nonzero;

(9.13.2)

(ab)^{Q}=e^{Q-1}a
;

(9.13.3)

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\bigl(\frac{b^{2}+1}{e}\bigr)=k
.

It remains to show that (
9.13.1
)–(
9.13.3
) are equivalent to (
.1
)–(
.3
).

First suppose that (
9.13.1
)–(
9.13.3
) hold. Since
a\neq 0
by hypothesis, (
9.13.2
) says
a^{Q-1}b^{Q}=e^{Q-1}
.
Since
b
and
e
are in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, it follows that
a^{Q-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, which since
\gcd(Q-1,q^{2}-1)=1
implies that

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Thus
e=a^{2}+b^{2}+1=(a+b+1)^{2}
, so that

\frac{b^{2}+1}{e}=1+\frac{a^{2}}{e}=1+\Bigl(\frac{a}{a+b+1}\Bigr)^{2}.

Since
\eta:=a/(a+b+1)
is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\eta^{2})=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\eta)
, so since

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(1)=k
it follows that

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{b^{2}+1}{e}\Bigr)=k+\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{a}{a+b+1}\Bigr).

Thus (
9.13.3
) implies that
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(a/(a+b+1))=0
, which is (
.3
). Writing
\lambda
for the unique
(Q-1)
-th
root of
b
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(so that (
.1
) holds), the identity
a^{Q-1}b^{Q}=e^{Q-1}
becomes
(a\lambda^{Q})^{Q-1}=e^{Q-1}
. Since

X^{Q-1}
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it follows that
a\lambda^{Q}=e
. Thus

\lambda^{2Q-2}=b^{2}=e+a^{2}+1=a\lambda^{Q}+a^{2}+1,

and dividing by
\lambda^{2Q}
yields

\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}=\frac{a}{\lambda^{Q}}+\frac{a^{2}}{\lambda^{2Q}}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2Q}}.

This says
\epsilon^{2}+\epsilon=0
where

\epsilon:=\frac{a}{\lambda^{Q}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2^{i}}},

so that
\epsilon
is in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Multiplying by
\lambda^{Q}
yields

a=\epsilon\lambda^{Q}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda^{Q-2^{i}},

which is (
.2
). We have shown that if (
9.13.1
)–(
9.13.3
) hold then (
.1
)–(
.3
) hold.

Conversely, now suppose that (
.1
)–(
.3
) hold. Condition (
.2
) implies
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Dividing (
.2
) by

\lambda^{Q}
yields

\frac{a}{\lambda^{Q}}=\epsilon+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2^{i}}}.

Add each side of this equation to its square to get

\frac{a}{\lambda^{Q}}+\frac{a^{2}}{\lambda^{2Q}}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2Q}}.

Now multiply by
\lambda^{2Q}
to conclude that

a\lambda^{Q}+a^{2}=\lambda^{2Q-2}+1,

or equivalently

(9.14)

ab\lambda=a^{2}+b^{2}+1.

Since
ab\lambda\neq 0
and
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, it follows that (
9.13.1
) holds. Raising both sides of (
9.14
)
to the
(Q-1)
-th power yields
a^{Q-1}b^{Q}=e^{Q-1}
, so that (
9.13.2
) holds. Finally, we have
(b^{2}+1)/e=1+a^{2}/e
.
Since
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(1)=k
and
\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(a^{2}/e)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}(a/(a+b+1))=0
by (
.3
), we conclude that

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{b^{2}+1}{e}\Bigr)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Bigl(1+\frac{a^{2}}{e}\Bigr)=k,

so that (
9.13.3
) holds.
∎

Remark 9.15
.

Our proofs of the above corollaries show that all permutation polynomials in the corollaries are multiplicatively
equivalent to the permutation polynomials in Theorem
1.1
. Some of the above corollaries can be generalized
to larger classes of permutation polynomials which are also multiplicatively equivalent to the polynomials in
Theorem
1.1
. For instance, Corollary
9.12
can be generalized as follows: if
q=2^{k}
and
Q=2^{\ell}

where
\gcd(2k,\ell)=1
, and
f(X):=aX^{u}+bX^{q}+cX+dX^{v}
where
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and
u:=(Q^{k+1}-1)/(Q-1)
and

v:=1+q(Q^{k}-1)/(Q-1)
, then
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
if and only if
a,b,c,d
satisfy the conditions in
Theorem
1.1
. We have not stated this as a separate result, or listed any further results along these lines,
since this result provides no new understanding and instead is merely an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

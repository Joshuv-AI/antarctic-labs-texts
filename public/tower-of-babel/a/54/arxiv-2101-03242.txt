RAP-modulated Fluid Processes: First Passages and the Stationary Distribution
Nigel G. Bean, Giang T. Nguyen, Bo F. Nielsen, Oscar Peralta

Abstract
We construct a stochastic fluid process with an underlying piecewise deterministic Markov process (PDMP) akin to the one used in the construction of the rational arrival process (RAP) in [ 2 ] , which we call the RAP-modulated fluid process. As opposed to the classic stochastic fluid process driven by a Markov jump process, the underlying PDMP of a RAP-modulated fluid process has a continuous state space and is driven by matrix parameters which may not be related to an intensity matrix. Through novel techniques we show how well-known formulae associated to the classic stochastic fluid process, such as first passage probabilities and the stationary distribution of its queue, translate to its RAP-modulated counterpart. \keywords Stochastic fluid process, rational arrival process, matrix-exponential distribution, first passage probability, stationary distribution 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 60J25, 60G17; Secondary: 60K25



1
Introduction

A
stochastic fluid model

(\mathcal{X},\varphi)=\{(X_{t},\varphi_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Markov additive process in which the background component
\varphi
is a Markov jump process with finite state space and the additive component
\mathcal{X}
is piecewise linear, with a rate that depends on the background state:

\displaystyle X_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}r_{\varphi_{s}}\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0.

The study of steady-state aspects of stochastic fluid models goes back to
[
11
,
1
,
9
]
, and its literature has been prolific from both applied and theoretical perspectives. Latouche and Nguyen
[
10
]
provide a comprehensive survey on the topic; much of the existing analysis exploits the probabilistic interpretation of the background process
\varphi
with finite state space.

A related concept is a
Markovian arrival process

(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{J})=\{(N_{t},J_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is a counting process and
\mathcal{J}
is the underlying Markov jump process with initial distribution
\bm{\alpha}
, hidden jumps according to intensity matrix
C
, and observable jumps according to according to a nonnegative intensity matrix
D
.
Jump times of the latter kind correspond to the arrival epochs of
\mathcal{N}
.
Asmussen and Bladt
[
2
]
introduce
rational arrival processes
(RAP), a generalisation of Markovian arrival processes for which
\bm{\alpha}
,

C
and
D
are not necessarily related to the parameters of a Markov jump process. They show that the arrivals associated to this algebraic generalisation are determined by an underlying continuous-state-space piecewise-deterministic Markov process which here we refer to as
an orbit process
. This orbit process evolves deterministically between its jump times, which occur according to a given space-dependent intensity function. The arrival epochs of the RAP can then be regarded as the jump times of the orbit process, allowing for a probabilistic analysis of the former using the physical interpretation of the latter.

In the present paper, we consider an extension of stochastic fluid models, which we call
RAP-modulated fluid processes
. A RAP-modulated fluid process
(\mathcal{R},\bm{\mathcal{A}})=\{(R_{t},\bm{A}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
is a Markov additive process in which the additive component
\mathcal{R}
is still piecewise linear but the background component
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
is now an orbit process. A similar generalisation was introduced in
[
3
]
, from
Quasi-Birth-Death processes
, where the additive component lives on the integers and is modulated by a Markovian jump process, to
QBD-RAP
, where the additive component still lives on the integers but is now modulated by a RAP. The class of RAP-modulated fluid processes we introduce here goes beyond stochastic fluid processes; for instance, one may use a Markov renewal process with matrix-exponential times in order to model the orbit process of a RAP-modulated fluid process.

Additional to defining the class of RAP-modulated fluid processes, the contributions of this paper include providing first passage probabilities, an expression for the stationary distribution of its queue, and an algorithm for computing the expected value of the orbit process
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
at the first downcrossing times of the level process to level
0
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. We define in Section
2
the simplest RAP-modulated fluid process, which we call a
simple RAP-modulated fluid process
. Although simplistic in its nature, this process helps us introduce the physical analysis and discuss necessary conditions of a RAP-modulated fluid process. In Section
3
, we give a precise definition of a RAP-modulated fluid process and present first passage results which exemplify the framework and techniques used throughout the manuscript. Then, we focus on the case in which the level process
\mathcal{R}
of the RAP-modulated fluid process is nowhere-constant in Section
4
, and allow
\mathcal{R}
to be piecewise-constant in Section
5
.

Our work demonstrates that several results of stochastic fluid models
translate into the framework of RAP-modulated fluid processes, although significantly more complicated techniques are required to analyze the latter.

2
Simple RAP-modulated fluid process

In the following we introduce the simplest non-trivial example of a RAP-modulated fluid process,
(\mathcal{R},\bm{\mathcal{A}})=\{(\mathcal{R}_{t},\bm{A}_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
, which we refer to as
a simple RAP-modulated fluid process
. To that end, first we define
\bm{\mathcal{A}}=\{\bm{A}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, the
(simple) orbit process
.

The process
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
is a càdlàg piecewise-deterministic Markov process (PDMP) with state space
\cup_{k\in\{+,-\}}\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
, where for each
k\in\{+,-\}
,
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
is a subset of the affine hyperplane
\{\bm{x}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{k}}:\bm{x}\bm{1}=1\}
for some fixed
m^{k}\geq 1
. Here, the elements of
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
are regarded as row vectors and
\bm{1}
is a column vector of ones of appropriate dimension. Thus,
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
is a row-vector process of varying dimension, either
m^{+}
or
m^{-}
depending whether it is in
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
or
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
at the given instant. In general
m^{+}\neq m^{-}
, but even in the case
m^{+}=m^{-}
the subsets
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
will be considered to belong to different spaces, and thus they will always be disjoint. We let the initial point
\bm{A}_{0}
be arbitrary but fixed.

Each PDMP is characterized by its motion between jumps, jump intensity and transition mechanism at its jump epochs
[
7
]
, properties which we define for
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
next. During an interval without jumps, say
[t,t+h)
with
h>0
, the orbit process
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
evolves according to the system of ordinary differential equations (ODE) given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{A}_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\bm{A}_{s}C^{+}-(\bm{A}_{s}C^{+}\bm{1})\bm{A}_{s}&\mbox{for }\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\\
\\
\bm{A}_{s}C^{-}-(\bm{A}_{s}C^{-}\bm{1})\bm{A}_{s}&\mbox{for }\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\quad s\in(t,t+h),\end{array}\right.

for some
C^{+}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{+}\times m^{+}}
and
C^{-}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{-}\times m^{-}}
. The solution to the ODE (
2
) is

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{t+r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{+}r}}{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{+}r}\bm{1}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{-}r}}{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{-}r}\bm{1}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\quad r\in[0,h).\end{array}\right.

Note that in (
2
) we implicitly assume that, for each initial point in
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
, the system of ODEs (
2
) evolves entirely within
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
.

As
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
evolves within
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
, jump epochs occur according to a location-dependent intensity function
\lambda:\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\mapsto\mathds{R}_{+}
given by

\displaystyle\lambda(\bm{A}_{t})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{A}_{t}D^{+-}\bm{1}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\\
\\
\bm{A}_{t}D^{-+}\bm{1}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\end{array}\right.

for some
D^{+-}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{+}\times m^{-}}
and
D^{-+}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{-}\times m^{+}}
. Since
\lambda(\cdot)
is assumed to be a nonnegative function, it indeed corresponds to a valid intensity function.

Condition 2.1
.

C^{+}\bm{1}+D^{+-}\bm{1}=\bm{0}
and
C^{-}\bm{1}+D^{-+}\bm{1}=\bm{0}
.

This condition implies that
\lambda
can alternatively be written as

\displaystyle\lambda(\bm{A}_{t})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-\bm{A}_{t}C^{+}\bm{1}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\\
\\
-\bm{A}_{t}C^{-}\bm{1}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}.\end{array}\right.

Using (
2
) and (
2
) it can be readily verified that

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\left(\left.\mbox{$\bm{\mathcal{A}}$ has no jumps in $[t,t+h]$}\;\right|\bm{A}_{t}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{+}h}\bm{1}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\\
\\
\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{-}h}\bm{1}&\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}.\end{array}\right.

Indeed, by

[
7
]
, the function

F(h):=\mathds{P}\left(\left.\mbox{$\bm{\mathcal{A}}$ has no jumps in $[t,t+h]$}\;\right|\bm{A}_{t},\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right)

corresponds to the unique differentiable solution of

\displaystyle\log F(h)=-\int_{0}^{h}\lambda\left(\frac{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{k}r}}{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{k}r}\bm{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{h}\left[\frac{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{k}r}}{\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{k}r}\bm{1}}\right]C^{k}\bm{1}\mathrm{d}r.

(2.16)

That
F(h)=\bm{A}_{t}e^{C^{k}h}
is a solution follows by differentiating both sides of (
2.16
).

Finally, given that a jump occurs at time
t
, the orbit process
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
will directly jump to

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{A}_{t^{-}}D^{+-}}{\bm{A}_{t^{-}}D^{+-}\bm{1}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\mbox{ if }\bm{A}_{t^{-}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\bm{A}_{t^{-}}D^{-+}}{\bm{A}_{t^{-}}D^{-+}\bm{1}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\mbox{ if }\bm{A}_{t^{-}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},

(2.17)

so that a jump originating from
\bm{x}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
will land at a deterministic point in
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
, and vice versa.

Note that the motion between jumps and jump behaviour of the PDMP
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
depend only on the matrices
C^{+},C^{-},D^{+-}
and
D^{-+}
. Moreover, these matrices implicitly dictate some requirements on the state space
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
through Equations (
2
), (
2
) and (
2.17
). Verifying if a set of matrices is
compatible
with a given state space is by no means trivial, which is a known issue in the context of matrix–exponential distributions and RAPs
[
6
]
. Below we present two examples of
valid
orbit processes.

Example 2.2

(Markov jump process)
.

Let
C^{+}
and
C^{-}
be subintensity matrices, and let
D^{+-}
and
D^{-+}
be nonnegative matrices, in such a way that

\begin{pmatrix}C^{+}&D^{+-}\\
D^{-+}&C^{-}\end{pmatrix}

corresponds to the intensity matrix of a Markov jump process. For
k\in\{+,-\}
choose

\mathfrak{Z}^{k}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{k}}:\bm{x}\geq\bm{0},\bm{x}\bm{1}=1\}.

Note that for all
\bm{b}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
and
h\geq 0
,
{\bm{b}e^{C^{k}h}}/{\bm{b}e^{C^{k}h}\bm{1}}
corresponds to a normalized probability vector, and thus belongs to
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
. The nonnegativity of
D^{+-}
and
D^{-+}
implies that the intensity function
\lambda
is always nonnegative. Finally, if
k\neq\ell\in\{+,-\}
and
\bm{b}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
, then
\bm{b}D^{k\ell}/{\bm{b}D^{k\ell}\bm{1}}
corresponds to an
m^{\ell}
–dimensional normalized probability vector, and thus belongs to
\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}
. Thus, an orbit process defined by these parameters must be valid.

Example 2.3

(Matrix-exponential renewal process)
.

For
k\in\{+,-\}
, let
(\bm{\alpha}^{k},S^{k})
be such that
\bm{\alpha}^{k}\bm{1}=1
and
F^{k}(x):=1-\bm{\alpha^{k}}e^{S^{k}x}\bm{1}
is a distribution function. Such a class of distributions is commonly known as matrix-exponential, a generalization of phase–type distributions. For
\ell\neq k
, let

C^{k}=S^{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad D^{k\ell}=(-\bm{S}^{k}\bm{1})\bm{\alpha}^{\ell},

and define

\mathfrak{Z}^{k}=\left\{\frac{\bm{\alpha}^{k}e^{C^{k}h}}{\bm{\alpha^{k}}e^{C^{k}h}\bm{1}}:h\geq 0\right\}.

If
\bm{b}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
, then
\bm{b}={\bm{\alpha}^{k}e^{C^{k}h}}/{\bm{\alpha^{k}}e^{C^{k}h}\bm{1}}
for some
h\geq 0
and thus

\displaystyle\lambda(\bm{b})=\bm{b}D^{k\ell}\bm{1}=\frac{\bm{\alpha}^{k}e^{S^{k}h}}{\bm{\alpha^{k}}e^{S^{k}h}\bm{1}}\left((-\bm{S}^{k}\bm{1})\bm{\alpha}^{\ell}\right)\bm{1}=\frac{\bm{\alpha}^{k}e^{S^{k}h}(-\bm{S}^{k}\bm{1})}{\bm{\alpha^{k}}e^{S^{k}h}\bm{1}}.

(2.18)

Since the numerator in the last expression of (
2.18
) corresponds to a probability density function and its denominator to a tail distribution,
\lambda
is indeed a nonnegative intensity function. Moreover, if a jump happens from some
\bm{b}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
, it will land in

\frac{\bm{b}D^{k\ell}}{\bm{b}D^{k\ell}\bm{1}}=\frac{\bm{b}(-\bm{S}^{k}\bm{1})\bm{\alpha}^{\ell}}{\bm{b}(-\bm{S}^{k}\bm{1})\bm{\alpha}^{\ell}\bm{1}}=\frac{\bm{\alpha}^{\ell}}{\bm{\alpha}^{\ell}\bm{1}}=\bm{\alpha}^{\ell}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell},\quad\ell\neq k.

Thus, this set of parameters corresponds to a valid orbit process whose inter-jump times are matrix-exponential.

Now that the distributional characteristics of the orbit process have been completely described, we state two further conditions on
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
and its state space.

Condition 2.4
.

The sets
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
are bounded.

In the RAP setting of
[
2

, Condition
2.4
is needed for
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
to correspond to the coefficients of a linear combination of probability measures that is itself a probability measure. In our setting, it will enable us to use the Bounded Convergence Theorem in specific places and to guarantee that, almost surely, there are only finitely many jumps on compact time intervals.

Condition 2.5
.

For
k\in\{+,-\}
, the set
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
is contained in a
minimal

(m^{k}-1)
-dimensional affine hyperplane, meaning that
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
cannot be contained in any
(m^{k}-2)
–dimensional affine hyperplane.

In the context of Example
2.2
, Condition
2.5
is equivalent to an irreducibility assumption of the Markov jump process, while in the context of Example
2.3
it is linked to the minimal dimension property of matrix-exponential distributions
[
6
]
. In our context, Condition
2.5
tells us that
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
is
rich enough
to guarantee a one-to-one correspondence between a matrix
G
and the collection
\{\bm{b}G:\bm{b}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\}
; this property follows from Lemma
2.6
below.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
k\in\{+,-\}
, there exist
m^{k}
linearly independent vectors contained in
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
is contained in a minimal
(m^{k}-1)
-dimensional affine hyperplane, there exist linearly independent vectors
\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{m^{k}-1}
and
\bm{g}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
such that
\{\bm{b}_{i}\}
are linearly independent from
\bm{g}
with

\{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{m^{k}-1}\}+\bm{g}\subset\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\subset\mbox{span}\{\bm{b}_{1},\dots,\bm{b}_{m^{k}-1}\}+\bm{g}.

Thus, let
\bm{h}_{m^{k}}=\bm{g}
and
\bm{h}_{i}=\bm{b}_{i}+\bm{g}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,m^{k}-1\}
. Then
\{\bm{h}_{i}\}\subset\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
is a collection of
m^{k}
linearly independent vectors.

3
RAP-modulated fluid process

3.1
Definition

In the following we introduce the RAP-modulated fluid process, which allows for greater generality than the simple RAP-modulated fluid process: in addition to allowing
\{R_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
to be piecewise constant, the orbit
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
is also allowed to perform jumps within each set
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
,
k\in\{+,-,0\}
, via a specific set-partitioning explained next.

Denote
\mathcal{S}=\{+,-,0\}
and fix
k\in\mathcal{S}
. We suppose that the set
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
is contained in a collection of
n^{k}\geq 0
orthogonal affine hyperplanes. Specifically, we assume that the set
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
can be partitioned in sets, say
\{\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n^{k}}
, with the following property:
T
here exists a collection
\{m^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n^{k}}\subset\{1,2,\dots\}
such that each set
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i}
is contained in the affine hyperplane

\displaystyle\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathds{R}^{\eta^{k}}:\bm{x}=(\bm{0}_{1}^{k},\dots,\bm{0}_{i-1}^{k},\bm{y},\bm{0}_{i+1}^{k},\dots,\bm{0}_{{n^{k}}}^{k})\mbox{ for some }\bm{y}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{k}_{i}}\mbox{ with }\bm{y}\bm{1}=1\right\},

(3.1)

where
\bm{0}_{i}^{k}
denotes the row-vector of zeros with
m^{k}_{i}
elements and
\eta^{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n^{k}}m^{k}_{i}
corresponds to the dimension of the space in which
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
lives.

The
(general) orbit process

\bm{\mathcal{A}}
is a càdlàg PDMP with state space
\mathfrak{Z}=\cup_{k\in\mathcal{S}}\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
and some arbitrary but fixed initial state
\bm{A}_{0}
. We describe its PDMP characteristics next. If
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
has no jumps in
[t,t+h)
for some
h>0
, then
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
follows the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{A}_{s}}{\mathrm{d}s}=\bm{A}_{s}\Gamma^{k}-(\bm{A}_{s}\Gamma^{k}\bm{1})\bm{A}_{s}\quad\mbox{for }\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k},s\in[t,t+h],

(3.2)

where
\Gamma^{k}\in\mathds{R}^{\eta^{k}\times{\eta^{k}}}
is of the form

\Gamma^{k}=\begin{pmatrix}C^{k}_{11}&&&\\
&C^{k}_{22}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&&C^{k}_{n^{k}n^{k}}\end{pmatrix}

for some
C^{k}_{ii}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{k}_{i}\times m^{k}_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq n^{k}
. The block-diagonal structure of
\Gamma^{k}
guarantees that, if
\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i_{0}}
for some
1\leq i_{0}\leq n^{k}
and
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
has no jumps in
[t,t+h]
, then
\{\bm{A}_{s}\}_{s=t}^{t+h}
is contained in the affine hyperplane (
3.1
) with
i=i_{0}
.
One can verify that the solution of (
3.2
) is given by

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{t+r}=\frac{\bm{A}_{t}e^{\Gamma^{k}r}}{\bm{A}_{t}e^{\Gamma^{k}r}\bm{1}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\quad\mbox{if }\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k},r\in[0,h].

(3.3)

If
\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i}
, then a jump to
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{j}
with
j\neq i
occurs with intensity
\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{C}^{k}_{ij}\bm{1}\geq 0
with

\widehat{C}^{k}_{ij}:=\begin{pmatrix}0^{k}_{1,1}&\cdots&0^{k}_{1,j-1}&&&&\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&&&\\
0^{k}_{i-1,1}&\cdots&0^{k}_{i-1,j-1}&&&&\\
&&&C^{k}_{ij}&&&\\
&&&&0^{k}_{i+1,j+1}&\cdots&0^{k}_{i+1,n^{k}}\\
&&&&\vdots&&\vdots\\
&&&&0^{k}_{n^{k},j+1}&\cdots&0^{k}_{n^{k},n^{k}}\end{pmatrix}

for some
C^{k}_{ij}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{k}_{i}\times m^{k}_{j}}
, where
0^{k}_{a,b}
denotes the zero matrix in
\mathds{R}^{m^{k}_{a}\times m^{k}_{b}}
. If such a jump to
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{j}
occurs at some time
s>t
, it will land at
\bm{A}_{s}=(\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\widehat{C}_{ij}^{k})/(\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\widehat{C}_{ij}^{k}\bm{1})\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{j}
.

Similarly, if
\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i}
, then a jump to
\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}_{j}
with
\ell\neq k,j\leq n^{\ell}
, occurs with intensity
\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}^{k\ell}_{ij}\bm{1}\geq 0
where

\widehat{D}^{k\ell}_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}0^{k\ell}_{1,1}&\cdots&0^{k\ell}_{1,j-1}&&&&\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&&&\\
0^{k\ell}_{i-1,1}&\cdots&0^{k\ell}_{i-1,j-1}&&&&\\
&&&D^{k\ell}_{ij}&&&\\
&&&&0^{k\ell}_{i+1,j+1}&\cdots&0^{k\ell}_{i+1,n^{k}}\\
&&&&\vdots&&\vdots\\
&&&&0^{k\ell}_{n^{k},j+1}&\cdots&0^{k\ell}_{n^{k},n^{k}}\end{pmatrix}

for some
D^{k\ell}_{ij}\in\mathds{R}^{m^{k}_{i}\times m^{\ell}_{j}}
. If such a jump occurs at some time
s>t
, it will land at
\bm{A}_{s}=(\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{k\ell})/(\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{k\ell}\bm{1})\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}_{j}
.

Condition 3.1
.

Remark 3.8
.

The paths in

.

For any

, define the

Now, let

be an

-stopping time. By the strong Markov property of PDMPs

where

.

Next we investigate a useful implication of the minimality property of

and Lemma

Corollary 3.9
.

Fix
x\geq 0
,
k\in\mathcal{S}
and let
\{\Pi(s)\}_{s\geq 0}
be a collection of matrices with identical dimensions. If
\{\bm{\alpha}\Pi(s):\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k},s\in[0,x]\}
is uniformly entrywise-bounded, then so is
\{\Pi(s):s\in[0,x]\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\exists\{s_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset[0,x]
and
j,\ell\in\{1,\dots,\eta^{k}\}
such that
\{\Pi(s_{n})_{j\ell}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is unbounded. Let
\{\bm{g}_{m}\}_{m}\subset\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
be a collection of
\eta^{k}
linearly independent vectors and let
\{c_{m}\}_{m}\subset\mathds{R}
be such that
\sum_{m}c_{m}\bm{g}_{m}=\bm{e}_{j}^{\prime}
, where
\bm{e}_{j}
denotes the unit column vector that is nonzero only at its
j
th entry. Then

\left\{\sum_{m}c_{m}(\bm{g}_{m}\Pi(s_{n}))_{\ell}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\left\{\Pi(s_{n})_{j\ell}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is unbounded, which is a contradiction.
∎

Next, we analyze the solution of a particular integral matrix-equation which is key to our analysis throughout this paper.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\Pi:\mathds{R}_{+}\mapsto\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
and
A,B\in\mathds{R}^{m\times m}
be such that

\Pi(\cdot)
is uniformly entrywise-bounded in compact intervals, and
for all
x\geq 0

\displaystyle\Pi(x)=e^{Ax}+\int_{0}^{x}e^{As}B\Pi(x-s)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.11)

Then,
\Pi(x)=e^{(A+B)x}
for all
x\geq 0
.

Proof.

The assumptions of
\Pi(\cdot)
imply that it is infinitely differentiable in
(0,\infty)
.
Premultiplying (
3.11
) by
e^{-Ax}
and differentiating gives us

\displaystyle-e^{-Ax}A\Pi(x)+e^{-Ax}\Pi^{\prime}(x)=e^{-Ax}B\Pi(x),

so that
\Pi^{\prime}(x)=(A+B)\Pi(x),

with initial condition
\Pi(0)=I
. Thus the result follows.
∎

3.3
Properties of the orbit process

In the following, we study the average behaviour of the process
\{\bm{A}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
over time restricted to certain events. By (
2
), we have that if
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
is simple and does not leave
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
by time
t
, for
k\in\{+,-\}
, then the row vector
\bm{A}_{t}
is proportional to
\bm{A}_{0}e^{C^{k}t}
. The following is an analogous result for the general orbit process.

Theorem 3.11
.

For all
k\in\{+,-,0\}
and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t]\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}t}\quad\mbox{ for }t\geq 0,

(3.12)

where
C^{k}\in\mathds{R}^{\eta^{k}\times{\eta^{k}}}
is of the form

C^{k}=\begin{pmatrix}C^{k}_{11}&\cdots&C^{k}_{1n^{k}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
C^{k}_{n^{k}1}&\cdots&C^{k}_{n^{k}n^{k}}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
s_{0}(\bm{\mathcal{A}}):=\inf\{s>0:\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\neq\bm{A}_{s}\}
be the time of the first jump of
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
, and for
t\geq 0
let

\displaystyle L_{t}:=\#\{s\in(0,t]:\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\neq\bm{A}_{s}\}

(3.13)

be the number of jumps of
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
in the interval
(0,t]
. When there is no ambiguity, we drop the dependency on
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
and write simply
L_{t}
.

First, we show, by induction, that for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t],\;L_{t}=n\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\Sigma_{n}(t)

(3.14)

for unique continuous matrices
\{\Sigma_{n}(\cdot)\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Case
n=0
.
Equation (
3.6
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t],\;L_{t}=0\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}e^{\Gamma^{k}t}.

(3.15)

Thus, (
3.14
) is true if we choose
\Sigma_{0}(t)=e^{\Gamma^{k}t}
, and this solution is unique by Lemma
3.4
.

Inductive part.
Suppose that (
3.14
) is true for some
n\geq 1
. Then, for
r\in(0,t)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t],\;L_{t}=n+1,\;s_{0}\in[r-\mathrm{d}r,r]\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[r,t],\;L_{t}\circ\theta_{r}=n\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{r}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in[r-\mathrm{d}r,r],\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{r}}\left[\bm{A}_{t-r}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t-r],\;L_{t-r}=n\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in[r-\mathrm{d}r,r],\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left(\bm{A}_{r}\Sigma_{n}(t-r)\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in[r-\mathrm{d}r,r],\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{r}\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in[r-\mathrm{d}r,r],\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]\Sigma_{n}(t-r),

where the strong Markov property is used in the second equality, and the induction hypothesis in the third. Now, for any
\bm{\alpha}^{*}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}^{*}}\left[\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}r}\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in[0,\mathrm{d}r],\;\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n^{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n^{k},\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\frac{\bm{\alpha}^{*}\widehat{C}^{k}_{ij}}{\bm{\alpha}^{*}\widehat{C}^{k}_{ij}\bm{1}}\left(\bm{\alpha}^{*}\widehat{C}^{k}_{ij}\bm{1}\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{\alpha}^{*}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}^{*}\left(C^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\right)\mathrm{d}r,

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{r}\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in(r-\mathrm{d}r,r),\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{r}\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in(r-\mathrm{d}r,r),\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{r-\mathrm{d}r}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{L_{r-\mathrm{d}r}=0\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{r-\mathrm{d}r}}\left[\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}r}\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}\in(0,\mathrm{d}r),\;\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{L_{r-\mathrm{d}r}=0\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left(\bm{A}_{r-\mathrm{d}r}\left({C}^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}r\mathds{1}{\left\{L_{r-\mathrm{d}r}=0\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}e^{\Gamma^{k}(r-\mathrm{d}r)}\left({C}^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\right)\mathrm{d}r=\bm{\alpha}e^{\Gamma^{k}r}\left({C}^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\right)\mathrm{d}r,

where (
3.15
) is used in the second last equality.
Thus,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t],\;L_{t}=n+1\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\Sigma_{n+1}(t)

with

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n+1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Gamma^{k}r}\left({C}^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\right)\Sigma_{n}(t-r)\mathrm{d}r,

(3.16)

which is continuous. The uniqueness of
\Sigma_{n+1}(\cdot)

3.4
.
Now,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t],\;L_{t}\leq m\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\Sigma_{n}(t)\quad\mbox{for all }m\geq 0.

(3.17)

The boundedness of
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
and Corollary
3.9
imply that

\{\sum_{n=0}^{m}\Sigma_{n}(s):m\geq 0,s\in[0,t]\}
is entrywise-bounded.
Moreover, since

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t],\;L_{t}\geq m\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}(t)

(3.18)

which converges to
\bm{0}
as
m\rightarrow 0

3.4
implies that
\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}(t)\rightarrow\bm{0}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. This guarantees the existence of
\Sigma_{n}(\cdot):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}(\cdot)
which is uniformly entrywise-bounded in compact intervals. Hence,

\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t]\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\Sigma(t)\quad\mbox{ for }t\geq 0.

\{\sum_{n=0}^{m}\Sigma_{n}(t):m\geq 0,s\in[0,t]\}

3.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma(t)=e^{\Gamma^{k}t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\Gamma^{k}s}\left({C}^{k}-\Gamma^{k}\right)\Sigma(t-s)\mathrm{d}s\qquad(t\geq 0).

(3.19)

Theorem
3.10

3.19

\Sigma(t)=\exp\left(\left[\Gamma^{k}+({C}^{k}-\Gamma^{k})\right]t\right)=e^{{C}^{k}t}

3.12
) follows.

Remark 3.12

First, note that

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in[0,\mathrm{d}t],\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n^{k}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq n^{\ell}}\frac{\bm{\alpha}\widehat{D}^{k\ell}_{ij}}{\bm{\alpha}\widehat{D}^{k\ell}_{ij}\bm{1}}\left(\bm{\alpha}\widehat{D}^{k\ell}_{ij}\bm{1}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}_{i}\right\}}=\bm{\alpha}D^{k\ell}\mathrm{d}t.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in[t,t+\mathrm{d}t],\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in[t,t+\mathrm{d}t],\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t]\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{t}}\left[\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in[0,\mathrm{d}t],\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t]\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left(\bm{A}_{t}D^{k\ell}\mathrm{d}t\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,t]\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}t}D^{k\ell}\mathrm{d}t.

By Theorem
3.11
and Equation (
3.9
), we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}t}=\bm{0}
. Since this holds for all
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
, Lemma
3.4
implies that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}e^{C^{k}t}=0
and so the eigenvalue of maximum real part of
C^{k}
must have a strictly negative real part. Then

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}t}D^{k\ell}\mathrm{d}t=\bm{\alpha}(-C^{k})^{-1}D^{k\ell}.

∎

Once again, while the matrices
\{\widehat{D}^{k\ell}_{ij}\}
dictate in a deterministic way where
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
lands after a jump,
D^{k\ell}
describes such landings in an
average
sense.

4.1
First-return probabilities

In this section we study the event in which the level process downcrosses its initial point. More specifically,
define

\displaystyle\tau_{-}:=\inf\left\{t>0:R_{t}\leq 0\right\},\quad\tau_{+}:=\inf\left\{t>0:R_{t}\geq 0\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{-}:=\{\omega\in\Omega:\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\tau_{-}<\infty\},\quad\Omega_{+}:=\{\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\tau_{+}<\infty\}.

The random variable
\tau_{-}
(
\tau_{+}
) corresponds to the first time the process
\mathcal{R}
visits
[0,\infty)
(
(-\infty,0]
), and
\Omega_{-}
(
\Omega_{+}
) is the set of paths in
\Omega
whose orbit starts in
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
(
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
) and whose level eventually downcrosses (upcrosses) level
0
in finite time.

We are interested in computing

\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\tau_{-}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\tau_{-}<\infty\right\}}\right]
for
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}.

To that end, we borrow ideas from the FP3 algorithm of
[
8
]
, whose probabilistic interpretation was developed in
[
4
]
and requires a particular partition of all paths in
\Omega_{-}
. Recall that we assume
\{R_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is piecewise linear with slopes
\pm 1
, which implies that for all
s,t\geq 0
and
k\in\{+,-\}
,

\displaystyle\big\{\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\big\}\cap\big\{|R_{t+s}-R_{t}|=s\big\}=\left\{\bm{A}_{u}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall u\in[t,t+s)\right\}.

(4.1)

Let

\Omega_{1}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega_{-}:\{\bm{A}_{s}\}_{s\geq 0}\mbox{ has exactly one jump from }\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\mbox{ to }\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\mbox{ during }[0,\tau_{-})\right\},

the set of sample paths whose level component returns to
0
after a single interval of increase and a single interval of decrease; in other words, there is no down-up peak before
\tau_{-}
. Then,
\Omega_{-}\setminus\Omega_{1}
is the set of sample paths whose level has one or more down-up peaks before
\tau_{-}
. Define

p:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\inf\left\{y\geq 0:\mbox{$\exists t\in[0,\tau_{-})$ such that $R_{t}=y$, $\bm{A}_{t^{-}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}$}\right\}&\mbox{if}&\mathcal{X}\in\Omega_{-}\setminus\Omega_{1},\\
\infty&\mbox{if}&\mathcal{X}\in(\Omega_{-}\setminus\Omega_{1})^{c}.\end{array}\right.

For every sample path in
\Omega_{-}\setminus\Omega_{1}
,
p
corresponds to the lowest level at which there is a down-up peak before time
\tau_{-}
, and we decompose each path at the time such lowest level is attained, denoted by
T_{2}
. Let
T_{1}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:R_{t}=p\}
be the first time the level reaches
p
and
T_{3}:=\sup\{t\geq 0:R_{t}=p,t<\tau_{-}\}
be the last time it does so before returning to level zero. Then,
T_{1}=p
by the assumption of
\pm 1
rates,
T_{2}=T_{1}+\tau_{-}\circ\theta_{T_{1}}
, and
T_{3}=T_{2}+\tau_{-}\circ\theta_{T_{2}}
.

Now, we recursively define
\Omega_{n}^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{-}
and
\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega_{-}
. Let
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}=\Omega_{1}
. For
n\geq 2
, a path
\omega\in\Omega_{-}
is an element of
\Omega_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if
\theta_{T_{1}}\omega\in\Omega_{n-1}^{\prime}\cup\Omega_{1}
and
\theta_{T_{2}}\omega\in\Omega_{n-1}^{\prime}\cup\Omega_{1}
. The collection
\{\Omega_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 1}
may be thought as a collection of paths of possibly increasing
complexity
, in the sense that the
excusion above level
p
of a path in
\Omega_{n}^{\prime}
can be decomposed, at time
T_{2}
, into two consecutive excursions above
p
(each increasing from
p
and returning to
p
) of paths in
\Omega_{n-1}^{\prime}\cup\Omega_{1}
. Figure
3
exemplifies a path in
\Omega_{n}^{\prime}
.

An example of a level process corresponding to
\omega\in\Omega_{3}^{\prime}

\Omega_{n}=\Omega_{n}^{\prime}\cup\Omega_{1}

\Omega_{-}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{n}

, we show that restricted to paths in

is characterized by a certain matrix

for unique matrices

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}

\displaystyle\Psi_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{C^{+}y}\left(D^{+-}+\Psi_{n-1}D^{-+}\Psi_{n-1}\right)e^{C^{-}y}\mathrm{d}y.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{\tau_{-}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\Omega_{1}\cap\{\rho_{+}\in(y,y+\mathrm{d}y)\}\right\}}\right]

4.2
Downward record process

For
x\geq 0
, define

\tau^{x}_{-}:=\inf\{t>0:R_{t}=-x,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\},

the first time the level process
\mathcal{R}
downcrosses level
-x\leq 0
.
Define the
downward record process

\{(\ell_{x},\bm{O}_{x})\}_{x\geq 0}
by

\displaystyle(\ell_{x},\bm{O}_{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}(R_{\tau_{-}^{x}},\;\bm{A}_{\tau_{-}^{x}})&\mbox{if}&\tau_{-}^{x}<\infty\\

where
\Delta
is some isolated cemetery state. If
\bm{O}_{x}\neq\Delta

x\geq 0

\bm{O}_{x}
corresponds to the orbit value when the level process downcrosses
\ell_{x}=-x

\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\cup\{\Delta\}

\bm{O}_{x}

\bm{O}_{x}\neq\Delta
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For all
\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}

attains a new local minima. By Assumption
3.6
, for

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}-\sigma_{n-1}=\kappa_{n+1}-\kappa_{n}-\tau_{-}\circ\theta_{\kappa_{n}}.

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{s=0}^{t}\mathds{E}\left[\mathds{E}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\chi_{t}\in(s,s+\mathrm{d}s)\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{t-s}\right]\right].

Now, focusing on the inner expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathds{E}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\chi_{t}\in(s,s+\mathrm{d}s)\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{t-s}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},Q_{t-s}=0,\bm{A}_{t-s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\bm{A}_{t-s{\color[rgb]{0.5,0,0.5}+}\mathrm{d}s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{t-s}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{t-s}}\left[\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t-s}\right\}}.

(4.29)

where
B_{t}:=\{Q_{t}=0,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\}
,
t\geq 0
. Thus, by Fubini’s Theorem, we have for

,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}{\left\{s\leq t\right\}}\cdot\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{t-s}}\left[\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t-s}\right\}}\right].

In (

\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{t}}\left[\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}\right].

Since the integrand in the expression above is computed on the event
\{\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z^{-}}\}
, w.l.o.g. suppose that

for some

\displaystyle\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{t}}\left[\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+},\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-},\bm{A}_{\mathrm{d}s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}_{t}}\left[\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}(\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{-+}\bm{1}\mathrm{d}s),

where
\bm{\alpha}_{t}:=\frac{\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}^{-+}_{ij}}{\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{-+}\bm{1}}
,

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in N}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}_{t}}\left[\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\mathrm{d}s\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}(\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{-+}\bm{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in N}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}_{t}}\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{x}}\bm{A}_{u}h+o(h)\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}(\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{-+}\bm{1}).

(4.32)

Substituting (
4.32
) into (
4.31
) gives

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\sum_{j\in N}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}_{t}}\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{x}}\bm{A}_{u}h+o(h)\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}(\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{-+}\bm{1})\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\sum_{j\in N}\bm{\alpha}_{t}(e^{C^{+}\Psi D^{-+}x}h)\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}(\bm{A}_{t}\widehat{D}_{ij}^{-+}\bm{1})+o(h)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{A}_{t}D^{-+}(e^{C^{+}\Psi D^{-+}x}h)\mathds{1}{\left\{B_{t}\right\}}+o(h)\right]

\displaystyle=c_{-}\bm{v}_{0}D^{-+}e^{(C^{+}+\Psi D^{-+})x}h+o(h),

where (
4.20
) is used in the last equality. Equation (
4.28
) follows by analogous steps and arguments in the proof of Corollary

.
∎

To compute

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\right]\bm{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}=0,\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\right\}}\right]\bm{1}\right)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pi^{+}(x)\bm{1}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pi^{-}(x)\bm{1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=c_{-}+c_{-}\bm{v}_{0}\left[D^{-+}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(C^{+}+\Psi D^{-+})x}\mathrm{d}x\bm{1}+D^{-+}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(C^{+}+\Psi D^{-+})x}\mathrm{d}x(\Psi\bm{1})\right]

\displaystyle=c_{-}\left(1-2\bm{v}_{0}D^{-+}(C^{+}+\Psi D^{-+})^{-1}\bm{1}\right),\quad\mbox{since }\Psi\bm{1}=\bm{1}.

(4.33)

By solving (

) for

and using Theorem

Corollary 4.12
.

Let

\displaystyle\pi(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\mathds{P}(Q_{t}\in(0,x)),\qquad x\geq 0,

(4.34)

which we call the

of

. Then,

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle=2c_{-}\bm{v}_{0}D^{-+}e^{(C^{+}+\Psi D^{-+})x}\bm{1},

(4.35)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{P}(Q_{t}=0,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-})

\displaystyle=c_{-}

(4.36)

with

.

5
Case

Now, we focus on the case that
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
is not empty and thus,
\mathcal{R}
may have some piecewise-constant intervals. In this framework, the concepts of up-down and down-up peaks used throughout Section
4
are not sufficient. To address this issue, for
k\in\{+,-\}
define

\rho^{*}_{k}:=\rho_{k}+\rho_{0}\circ\theta_{\rho_{k}},

where
\rho_{\ell}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:\bm{A}_{s}\notin\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\}
for
\ell\in\{+,-,0\}
. Then
\rho^{*}_{k}
corresponds to
the first exit time from
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
following an exit from
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
. Note that
\rho^{*}_{k}=\rho_{k}
if and only if
\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\notin\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k,\ell\in\{+,-\}
and let
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
. Then, for all
x\geq 0
.

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0},\;\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}x}D^{k0}(-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0\ell}\mathrm{d}x

Proof.

Using the strong Markov property and Lemma
3.13
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0},\;\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{k}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}}\left[\bm{A}_{\rho_{0}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{\rho_{0}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left(\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}(-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0\ell}\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\right](-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0\ell}

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}x}D^{k0}(-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0\ell}\mathrm{d}x.

∎

Lemma
5.1
is analogous to
censoring
in the context of stochastic fluid processes. In general terms, it concerns the inspection of certain aspects of a process before and after some interval; in the case of Lemma
5.1
such an interval is
(\rho_{k},\rho^{*}_{k})
, during which
\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
, and the aspects inspected are
\rho_{k}
and
\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}
.

When
n^{0}>0
, we say that an
up-down peak
of
\mathcal{R}
occurs at time
t>0
if there exists some
s\in(0,t)
such that
\rho^{*}_{+}\circ\theta_{s}=t-s
and
\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}_{-}
. That is, an up-down peak results from
\{\bm{A}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
exiting
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
and either going directly to
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
, or going through
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
and jumping later to
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
. Analogously, a
down-up peak
happens at time
t>0
if there exists some
s\in(0,t)
such that
\rho^{*}_{-}\circ\theta_{s}=t-s
and
\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}_{+}
.

In the following we compute the expected value of the orbit at a peak for the general setting.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
k,\ell\in\{+,-\}
,
k\neq\ell
, and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{k}\in(x,x+\mathrm{d}x),\;\bm{A}_{\rho^{*}_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}x}D^{k\ell*}\mathrm{d}x,

(5.1)

where
D^{k\ell*}=D^{k\ell}+D^{k0}(-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0\ell}.

Proof.

Condition the indicator function on the LHS of (
5.1
) on the events
\{\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{\ell}\}
and
\{\bm{A}_{\rho_{k}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\}
. The result follows by Lemma
3.13
and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Now that we have a notion for
peaks
, we develop next a way to handle the intervals between peaks in the case
n^{0}>0
. For instance, suppose that
\bm{A}_{0}\in\mathfrak{Z}_{+}
. A first step would be to compute the expected value of
\bm{\mathcal{A}}
at the instant
\mathcal{R}
reaches some level
x\geq 0
given that no peak has occured until that epoch, similar in spirit to Theorem
3.11
. Note that up to the time before the first up-down peak, the orbit process can perform jumps from/to
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
, so the analysis differs from that of Section
4
. However, it turns out that the solution to this problem has a structure similar to that of Theorem
3.11
once we censor the occupation times in
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
, as we will see next.

For
t\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle W_{t}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\right\}}\mathrm{d}s,\quad\zeta_{x}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:W_{t}>x\right\}.

Thus,
W_{t}
corresponds to the
occupation time of
\{\bm{A}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
up to time
t
, while
\zeta_{x}
corresponds to the
necessary time to reach
x
units of occupation time in
\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
. The process
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is continuous nondecreasing and
\{\zeta_{x}\}_{x\geq 0}
is càdlàg. A process analogous to
\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
has been used in the stochastic fluid model literature, it being regarded as the total amount of fluid that has flowed into or out of the system
[
5
]
.

By Assumption
3.6
, for
t,x\geq 0
and
k\in\{+,-\}

\displaystyle\{\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\}\cap\left\{\left|R_{t+\zeta_{x}\circ\theta_{t}}-R_{t}\right|=x\right\}=\left\{\bm{A}_{u}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall u\in\left[t,t+\zeta_{x}^{k}\circ\theta_{t}\right)\right\},

(5.2)

a relation similar to (
4.1
). In the following we investigate more about the event (
5.2
).

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
,
k\in\{+,-\}
and
x\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\zeta_{x}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x}]\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k*}x},

(5.3)

where
C^{k*}=C^{k}+D^{k0}(-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0k}.

Proof.

Let
s_{0}^{*}:=\inf\{y>0:\bm{A}_{\zeta_{y}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\}=\inf\{t>0:\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\}
, and for
x\geq 0
let

L_{x}^{*}:=\#\{s\in(0,\zeta_{x}]:\bm{A}_{s^{-}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k},\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\}.

We claim that there exist continuous matrices
\{\Sigma^{*}_{n}(\cdot)\}_{n\geq 0}
such that for all
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{{\zeta_{x}}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x}],\;L_{x}^{*}=n\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\Sigma_{n}^{*}(x).

(5.4)

To prove this we use induction, following analogous steps to those in the proof of Theorem
3.11
.

Case
n=0
.
As
{\zeta_{x}}=x
on
\{L^{*}_{x}=0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{{\zeta_{x}}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x}],\;L_{x}^{*}=0\right\}}\right]=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{{\zeta_{x}}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\;\forall s\in[0,x]\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}e^{C^{k}x},

where Theorem
3.11
is used in the last equality. Thus, (
5.4
) follows by choosing
\Sigma_{0}^{*}(x)=e^{C^{k}x}
, and this solution is unique by Lemma
3.4
.

Inductive part.

Suppose that (
5.4
) holds for some
n\geq 0
. Then, for
r\in(0,x)

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left[\bm{A}_{{\zeta_{x}}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x}],\;L_{x}^{*}=n+1,s_{0}^{*}\in(r-\mathrm{d}r,r)\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left.\bm{A}_{\zeta_{x}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x}],\;L_{x}^{*}=n+1\right\}}\;\right|\mathcal{F}_{\zeta_{r}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}^{*}\in(r-\mathrm{d}r,r),\;\bm{A}_{\zeta_{r}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{\zeta_{r}}}\left[\bm{A}_{\zeta_{x-r}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x-r}],\;L_{x-r}^{*}=n\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}^{*}\in(r-\mathrm{d}r,r),\;\bm{A}_{\zeta_{r}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\left(\bm{A}_{\zeta_{r}}\Sigma^{*}_{n}(x-r)\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{s_{0}^{*}\in(r-\mathrm{d}r,r),\;\bm{A}_{\zeta_{r}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\right\}}\right]

where Lemma
5.1
was used in the last equality.

Thus, (

) recursively holds for

as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n+1}^{*}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}e^{C^{k}r}D^{k0}(-C^{0})^{-1}D^{0k}\Sigma_{n}^{*}(x-r)\mathrm{d}r,

\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\zeta_{x}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall s\in[0,\zeta_{x}]\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\Sigma^{*}(x),

where

.
∎

Using Corollary
5.2
and Theorem

and the generalized concept of peaks we can develop the theory of first passages for general RAP-modulated fluid processes in virtually the same way as in Sections

and

and part of Section

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{R},\bm{\mathcal{A}})
be a general RAP-modulated fluid process with
n^{0}>0
. Let
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
.
Then

\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{A}_{\tau_{-}}\mathds{1}{\left\{\Omega_{\tau}\right\}}\right]=\bm{\alpha}\Psi^{*}

where
\Psi^{*}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{n}^{*}
and
\{\Psi_{n}^{*}\}_{n\geq 0}
are recursively computed by setting
\Psi_{0}^{*}=0
and solving

\displaystyle C^{+*}\Psi_{n+1}^{*}+\Psi_{n+1}^{*}C^{-*}=-D^{+-*}-\Psi_{n}^{*}D^{-+*}\Psi_{n}^{*}.

(5.6)

Furthermore, for
x\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\bm{O}_{x}\mathds{1}{\left\{\tau_{-}^{x}<\infty\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}\Psi^{*}e^{(C^{-*}+D^{-+*}\Psi^{*})x},

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}^{x}}\bm{A}_{u}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}e^{(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})x},

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\sum_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{x}}\bm{A}_{u}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}e^{(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})x}\Psi^{*}.

We can compute
\Pi^{+}(\cdot)
and
\Pi^{-}(\cdot)
as defined in (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) by using analogous arguments to the ones used in the proof of Theorem
4.11
, however, there is a considerable difference. In (
4.29
) we used that the event
\{Q_{t}=0,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\}
implies
\{\bm{A}_{t^{-}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\}
, however, this is no longer true in the case
n^{0}>0
. In fact,
\{Q_{t}=0,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}\}
implies that
\bm{A}_{t^{-}}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\cup\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
, so that a distinction needs to be made between occupations of
\{Q_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
in
(0,\infty)
due to jumps coming from
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
or from
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
. The first case was already addressed in the proof of Theorem
4.11
, while the second one follows by analogous arguments and by noticing that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}=0,\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{A}_{t}}\left[\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{\rho_{-}\in(0,\mathrm{d}s),\;\bm{A}_{r}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\;\forall r\in[\rho_{-},s]\right\}}\right]\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}=0,\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\left(\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\bm{A}_{t}D^{-0}e^{C^{0}s}\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}=0,\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}=0,\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\right\}}\right]D^{-0}(-C^{0})^{-1}.

Thus, the average limit orbit values in
\mathfrak{Z}^{0}
on the event
\{Q_{t}=0\}
can be computed once we compute the average limit orbit values in
\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
on the event
\{Q_{t}=0\}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. The latter is computed similarly to (
4.18
): for any
\bm{\beta}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}=0,\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}c_{-}^{*}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\bm{O}_{y}\right]=c_{-}^{*}\bm{v}_{0}^{*},

where
c_{-}^{*}:=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{P}(Q_{t}=0,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-})
and
\bm{v}_{0}^{*}
is the left eigenvector with
\bm{v}_{0}^{*}\bm{1}=1
associated to the eigenvalue
0
of
C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*}
. Analogous to Condition
4.7
, we assume that such an eigenvector
\bm{v}_{0}^{*}
is unique.

We now consider one final component
in order to compute the stationary distribution of
\{Q_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, which is

\displaystyle\Pi^{0}(x):=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\mathds{E}_{\bm{\beta}}\left[\bm{A}_{t}\mathds{1}{\left\{Q_{t}\in(0,x),\;\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\right],\quad x>0.

(5.7)

This can be computed using the following result whose proof is similar to that of Theorem
4.11
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{+}
and
h>0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathds{E}_{\bm{\alpha}}\left[\int_{s=0}^{\infty}\bm{A}_{s}\mathds{1}{\left\{s<\tau_{-},\;Q_{s}\in[x,x+h),\;\bm{A}_{s}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{0}\right\}}\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{\alpha}e^{(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})x}[D^{+0}+\Psi^{*}D^{-0}](-C^{0})^{-1}h+o(h).

The previous leads to the following characterization of the stationary behaviour of
\{Q_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\Pi^{+}
,
\Pi^{-}
and
\Pi^{0}
be defined as in (
4.25
), (
4.26
) and (
5.7
), respectively. Then, for
x>0

\displaystyle\Pi^{+}(x)

\displaystyle=c_{-}^{*}\bm{v}_{0}^{*}D^{-+*}e^{(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})x}

\displaystyle\Pi^{-}(x)

\displaystyle=c_{-}^{*}\bm{v}_{0}^{*}D^{-+*}e^{(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})x}\Psi^{*}

\displaystyle\Pi^{0}(x)

\displaystyle=c_{-}^{*}\bm{v}_{0}^{*}D^{-+*}e^{(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})x}[D^{+0}+\Psi^{*}D^{-0}](-C^{0})^{-1},

where
c_{-}^{*}:=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathds{P}(Q_{t}=0,\bm{A}_{t}\in\mathfrak{Z}^{-})
is of the form

\displaystyle c_{-}^{*}=\left(1-\bm{v}_{0}^{*}D^{-+*}(C^{+*}+\Psi^{*}D^{-+*})^{-1}(2\bm{1}+[D^{+0}+\Psi^{*}D^{-0}](-C^{0})^{-1}\bm{1})\right)^{-1}.

Furthermore, the stationary density function of
\{Q_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
as defined in (
4.34
) is given by

\pi(x)=\Pi^{+}(x)\bm{1}+\Pi^{-}(x)\bm{1}+\Pi^{0}(x)\bm{1}=2\Pi^{+}(x)\bm{1}+\Pi^{0}(x)\bm{1},\quad x>0.

Singular Value Decomposition for the X-ray Transforms on the Reduced Heisenberg Group, and a Two-Radius Theorem
Steven Flynn

Abstract
. We give an explicit Singular Value Decomposition of the sub-Riemannian X-ray transform on the Heisenberg group with compact center. By studying the singular values, we obtain a two-radius theorem for integrals over sub-Riemannian geodesics. We also state intertwining properties of distinguished differential operators. We conclude with a description of ongoing work.



1.
Introduction

The X-ray transform assigns to a function its integrals over closed geodesics. The Heisenberg group with its sub-Riemannian structure provides a rich example of such integral functionals. Of crucial importance for practical applications of the X-ray Transform is its Singular Value Decomposition
[
6
]
.

2.
Definitions

2.1.
Heisenberg Group

The Heisenberg group is
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}
with the multiplication law

\displaystyle(x+iy,t)(u+iv,s)=(x+u+i(y+v),t+s+\tfrac{1}{2}(xv-yu))

The reduced Heisenberg group is the quotient of
\mathbb{H}
by a discrete subgroup of the center

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbb{H}}:=\mathbb{H}/\Gamma,\quad\Gamma:=\{(0,k\pi)\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Heuristically, the harmonic analysis on the Heisenberg group is controlled by the center
Z(\mathbb{H})=\{0+0i\}\times\mathbb{R}
. Having compact center, analyis of the reduced Heisenberg group resembles that of the circle.

2.2.
X-ray Transform

Let
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
be a positive rational number. Of interest are curves on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
modeled by

(1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{r}(s):=\left(\sqrt{r}e^{is/\sqrt{r}},\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r}s\right)\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}\cong\mathbb{C}\times(\mathbb{R}/\pi\mathbb{Z}).

Take for granted that the set of closed unit speed
sub-Riemannian
geodesics
[
7
]
on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
is generated by left translations by
(z,t)\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
of the curves
\gamma_{r}
above, for
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
. The requirement that
r
is rational is analogous to the requirement that closed geodesics on the flat torus have rational slope. If
r
were irrational, the curve
\gamma_{r}
would not be closed, so we ignore that case.

Indeed, for
r=a/b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
coprime,

\displaystyle\gamma_{r}\text{ is a closed curve in }\overline{\mathbb{H}}\text{ with period }2\pi\sqrt{ab}.

After one period of
\gamma_{r}
with
r=a/b
for coprime
(a,b)
,

i

a\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
counts the number of times
\gamma_{r}
winds around the
S^{1}
component of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
,

ii

b\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
counts the number of counterclockwise rotations made by
\gamma_{r}
around its central axis.

Call
a
the
vertical winding number
, and
b
the
horizontal winding number
of
\gamma_{r}
in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
.

Definition 1
.

For vertical and horizontal winding numbers
a
and
b
, and
r=a/b
the X-ray transform

\displaystyle I_{r}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})

of a compactly supported smooth function
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
is

(2)

\displaystyle I_{r}f(z,t)=\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}f\left((z,t)\gamma_{r}(s)\right)ds,\quad(z,t)\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}.

We also write
If(r;z,t):=I_{r}f(z,t)
.

Remark 1
.

The operator
I
is the Heisenberg analog of the X-ray transform on a torus. It is related to the X-ray transform on the full Heisenberg group
[
3
]
by a type of torus-projection operator (as in
[
5
]
).

That
I_{r}
is well-defined is straightforward. By the Cauchy Schwartz Inequality and compactness of the domain of integration,
I_{r}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
. It is a simple matter to extend
I_{r}
to other function spaces, but we focus on the
L^{2}
theory.

The primary concern is injectivity of the X-ray transform. Written as in equation (
2
), the X-ray transform is a special case of the Pompeiu Transform
[
1
]
. However, to the author’s knowlegdge, the special case of integration over left-translates a closed sub-Riemannian geodesic has not been considered.

3.
Spectral Decomposition of
{I}_{r}
,
I_{r}^{*}
and
I_{r}^{*}I_{r}

The spectral decomposition of the X-ray transform
I_{r}
has a continuous part and a discrete part respecting the orthogonal decomposition

(3)

\displaystyle L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus{{}^{0}L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})}.

The substances in the orthogonal decomposition above are

\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{C})\cong\,

\displaystyle\{f\in L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}}):f(z,t)=f(z,0),\;\forall(z,t)\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}\}

{}^{0}L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}}):=\,

\displaystyle\{f\in L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}}):\smallint_{0}^{\pi}f(z,t)dt=0,\;\forall z\in\mathbb{C}\}.

Proposition 1
.

{I}_{r}
preserves the orthogonal decomposition above. i.e,

\displaystyle{{I}_{r}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{C})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle{{I}_{r}}:{{}^{0}}L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})\to{{}^{0}}L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}}).

The continuous part of
I_{r}
is its restriction to
L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
, which is essentially the mean value transform on the plane
M^{R}:L^{2}(\mathbb{C})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle M^{R}f(z):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(z+Re^{i\theta})d\theta,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}).

Theorem 1
.

The restriction of
{I}_{r}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a scalar multiple of the mean value transform. In particular

\displaystyle{I}_{r}f(z)=2\pi\sqrt{ab}\,M^{\sqrt{r}}f(z),\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}).

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{r}f(z)=\frac{\sqrt{ab}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}J_{0}(R|\xi|)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{iz\cdot\xi}d\xi.

Here
J_{0}
is the zeroth order Bessel function defined in (
12
).

Proof.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C})
,

\displaystyle{I}_{r}|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C})}f(z,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}f\left((z,t)(\sqrt{r}e^{is/\sqrt{r}},\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r}s)\right)ds=\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}f\left(z+e^{i\sqrt{r}s/\sqrt{r}},t+\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r}s+\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r}\textnormal{Im}\left(\overline{z}e^{is/\sqrt{r}}\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}f(z+\sqrt{r}e^{is/\sqrt{r}})ds=\sqrt{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi b}f(z+\sqrt{r}e^{is})ds=(2\pi b)\sqrt{r}M^{\sqrt{r}}f(z)=2\pi\sqrt{ab}M^{\sqrt{r}}f(z),

Furthermore

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}M^{R}g(z)e^{-iz\cdot\xi}dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(z+Re^{i\theta})e^{-iz\cdot\xi}d\theta dz=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{iRe^{i\theta}\cdot\xi}d\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(z)e^{-iz\cdot\xi}dz=J_{0}(R|\xi|)\widehat{g}(\xi)

The result follows.
∎

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\psi_{jk}^{n}=\sqrt{ab}\,\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(rn)\,c(rn,j)\psi_{j+r|n|,k}^{n}

where

\displaystyle c(m,j)=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{j!}{(j+m)!}}m^{m/2}e^{-(i+1)\pi m/2}L_{j}^{m}\left(m\right),\quad m\in\mathbb{N}

with

and

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(m)=\begin{cases}1&m\in\mathbb{Z}\\
0&m\not\in\mathbb{Z}\end{cases}.

Here
\{\psi_{jk}^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},j,k\in\mathbb{N}\}
defined in equation (

), is an orthonormal Hilbert Basis of
{{}^{0}L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})}
. The generalized Laguerre functions are given in (

Proof.

For

, and

,

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\psi_{jk}^{n}(z,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{|n|}}{\pi}{I}_{r}M_{jk}^{n}(z,t)=\frac{\sqrt{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}M_{jk}^{n}\left((z,t)\gamma_{r}(s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{|n|}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}M_{jl}^{n}\left(\gamma_{r}(s)\right)M_{lk}^{n}(z,t)ds,

\displaystyle\text{by }\eqref{product},

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}M_{jl}^{n}\left(\gamma_{r}(s)\right)ds\right]\psi_{lk}^{n}(z,t).

Now

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}M_{jl}^{n}\left(\gamma_{r}(s)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}M_{jl}^{n}\left(\sqrt{r}e^{is/\sqrt{r}},\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r}s\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sqrt{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{abr}}M_{jl}^{n}\left(\sqrt{r}e^{is},\tfrac{1}{2}rs\right)ds,

\displaystyle\text{via }s\mapsto s\sqrt{r}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi b}e^{i((j-l)+r|n|)s}M_{jl}^{n}\left(\sqrt{r},1\right)ds,

Corollary 4

(Two-Radius Theorem)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
with
I_{r_{1}}f=I_{r_{2}}f=0
for some
r_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}
. Then
f=0
provided that

1.

r_{1}/r_{2}
is not a ratio of roots of diagonal Laguerre functions
l_{j}(x):=L_{j}^{(x)}(x)
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}

2.

\sqrt{r_{1}/r_{2}}
is not a ratio of roots of the Bessel function
J_{0}
.

Proof.

For and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
let
s(n,j,r_{1})
and
s(n,j,r_{2})
be the singular values for
\psi_{jk}^{n}
corresponding to
I_{r_{1}}
and
I_{r_{2}}
respectively. Then
f=0
provided that
s(n,j,r_{1})
and
s(n,j,r_{2})
are not both zero. We have
s(n,j,r_{1})=s(n,j,r_{2})=0
if and only if
r_{j}n
is a zero of the diagonal Laguerre function
l_{j}
for
j=1,2
. In this case,
r_{1}/r_{2}
is a ratio of zeros of Laguerre polynomials. Similarly,
J_{0}(\sqrt{r_{i}}|\xi|)
vanishes if and only if
r_{j}|\xi|
is a zero of
J_{0}
. Then
r_{1}/r_{2}
is a ratio of zeros of
J_{0}
.
∎

4.
Intertwining differential operators

Note that the standard left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{H}
(or on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
) are

\displaystyle X:=\partial_{x}-\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{t}

\displaystyle Y:=\partial_{y}+\frac{1}{2}x\partial_{t}

\displaystyle T:=\partial_{t}.

Also, the standard right-invariant vector fields are

\displaystyle\tilde{X}:=\partial_{x}+\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}:=\partial_{y}-\frac{1}{2}x\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\tilde{T}:=\partial_{t}.

Let
-\mathcal{L}:=X^{2}+Y^{2}
, be the left sub-laplacian, and
-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}:=\tilde{X}^{2}+\tilde{Y}^{2}
be the right sub-laplacian. Also let
\square_{r}=\mathcal{L}+rT^{2}
on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
.

Proposition 2
.

For any right-invariant vector field
\tilde{V}
and
f\in S(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
we have

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left(\tilde{V}f\right)(z,t)=\tilde{V}{I}_{r}f(z,t).

Proof idea.

{I}_{r}
is a group convolution operator by a compactly supported measure on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
:

\displaystyle{I}_{r}f=\mu_{r}*_{\mathbb{H}}f

where

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathbb{H}}}fd\mu_{=}\int_{0}^{2\pi\sqrt{ab}}f\left(\gamma_{r}(s)\right)ds={I}_{r}f(0).

And for a right-invariant vector field on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{V}\left(\mu_{r}*_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)=\mu_{r}*_{\mathbb{H}}\left(\tilde{V}f\right).

∎

Corollary 5
.

The X-ray transform
{I}_{r}
commutes with
T
and the right sub-laplacian:

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left(Tf\right)=T\left({I}_{r}f\right)

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}f\right)=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left({I}_{r}f\right).

We use these facts to prove the most interesting intertwining property of
{I}_{r}
:

Theorem 4
.

{I}_{r}
intertwines the left sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
with the operator
\square_{r}
on
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
:

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left(\mathcal{L}f\right)=\square_{r}\left({I}_{r}f\right),\quad f\in S(\overline{\mathbb{H}}).

We first note the helical symmetry of
{I}_{r}
.

Lemma 1
.

Define the rotation map
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{*}f(z,t)=f(e^{i\theta}z,t)
. Then a straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{*}f\right)=\mathcal{R}^{*}_{\theta}{I}_{r}f\left(z,t-\tfrac{1}{2}r\theta\right)

for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
. Differentiating in
\theta
yields

I_{r}(\partial_{\theta}f)(z,t)=(\partial_{\theta}-\tfrac{1}{2}rT)I_{r}f(z,t).

Proof of Theorem
4
.

A straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}-\mathcal{L}=2\partial_{\theta}T.

Thus

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left((\mathcal{L}-\tilde{\mathcal{L}})f\right)(z,t)=-2\left(\partial_{\theta}-\tfrac{1}{2}rT\right)T{I}_{r}f(z,t),

so that

\displaystyle{I}_{r}\left(\mathcal{L}f\right)

\displaystyle={I}_{r}\left(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}f\right)+\left(-2\partial_{\theta}+r\right)T{I}_{r}f=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\left({I}_{r}f\right)+\left(-2\partial_{\theta}+r\right)T{I}_{r}f

\displaystyle=\left(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}-2\partial_{\theta}T+rT^{2}\right)\left({I}_{r}f\right)\ =\left(\mathcal{L}+rT^{2}\right)\left({I}_{r}f\right)

\displaystyle=:\square_{r}\left({I}_{r}f\right)

as desired.
∎

Remark 5
.

The functions
\psi_{jk}^{n}
are joint eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}
and
T
(see
5
). Indeed

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}\psi_{jk}^{n}&=2|n|(1+2j)\,\psi_{jk}^{n}\\
T\psi_{jk}^{n}&=2in\,\psi_{jk}^{n}.\end{cases}

So

\displaystyle\square_{r}\psi_{jk}^{n}

\displaystyle=2|n|\left(1+2(j-|n|r)\right)\psi_{jk}^{n}.

What’s more the functions
\psi_{jk}^{n}
are eigenfunctions of the right sublaplacian
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
. Indeed

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi_{jk}^{n}=2|n|(1+2k)\psi_{jk}^{n}.

5.
Ongoing Work

1.

Explicit Inversion formulas.
The two-radius theorem implies that a function
f\in L^{2}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
may be recovered form its X-ray transform
If
. We can therefore write down an inversion formula in terms of the singular values, but it is desirable to have a closed form inversion formula.

2.

Chacterize the zeros of the diagonal Laguerre functions. The following conjecture, if true, would imply a one-radius theorem.

Conjecture 1
.

The only
(j,n)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}^{*}
for which
l_{j}(n):=L_{j}^{n}(n)=0
is
(j,n)=(2,2)
.

2.

Stability estimates.
We would like to know if the recovery of
f
from
If
is stable. That is, for a suitable choice of Sobolev scale
H^{s}(\overline{\mathbb{H}})
and
H^{s}(\overline{\mathbb{H}}\times\mathbb{Q}^{+})
for which

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|If\|_{H^{s^{\prime}}}

We expect that, with respect to a natural choice of Sobolev scale based on the intertwining properties above, that
I
is one half smoothening. That is
s^{\prime}=s+\frac{1}{2}
.

•

Non-Euclidea metrics on the base.
The geodesics in (
1
) are determined by lifting the Eudliean metric
g=dx^{2}+dy^{2}
from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to the horizontal distribution of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
. What can we say about the X-ray transform associated to the lift of a non-Euclidean metric from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to a sub-Riemannian metric on the Heisenberg group?

•

Can we obtain a similar explicit spectral decomposition for the X-ray transform on more general Carnot groups such as the Engel group of free Nilpotent groups? Can we state more general two-radius theorems in these cases?

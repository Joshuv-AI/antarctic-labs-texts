Explicit geometric construction of sparse inverse mass matrices for arbitrary tetrahedral grids Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Silvano Pitassi, Francesco Trevisan, Ruben Specogna

Abstract
The geometric reinterpretation of the Finite Element Method (FEM) shows that Raviart–Thomas and Nédélec mass matrices map from degrees of freedoms (DoFs) attached to geometric elements of a tetrahedral grid to DoFs attached to the barycentric dual grid. The algebraic inverses of the mass matrices map DoFs attached to the barycentric dual grid back to DoFs attached to the corresponding primal tetrahedral grid, but they are of limited practical use since they are dense. In this paper we present a new geometric construction of sparse inverse mass matrices for arbitrary tetrahedral grids and possibly inhomogeneous and anisotropic materials, debunking the conventional wisdom that the barycentric dual grid prohibits a sparse representation for inverse mass matrices. In particular, we provide a unified framework for the construction of both edge and face mass matrices and their sparse inverses. Such a unifying principle relies on novel geometric reconstruction formulas, from which, according to a well-established design strategy, local mass matrices are constructed as the sum of a consistent and a stabilization term. A major difference with the approaches proposed so far is that the consistent term is defined geometrically and explicitly, that is, without the necessity of computing the inverses of local matrices. This provides a sensible speedup and an easier implementation. We use these new sparse inverse mass matrices to discretize a three-dimensional Poisson problem, providing the comparison between the results obtained by various formulations on a benchmark problem with analytical solution.

In this paper we provide a unified framework for the construction of both edge and face mass matrices and their sparse inverses.
The unifying principle relies on the construction of discrete Hodge operators starting from
reconstruction formulas
(that provide a representation of the discrete variables at the continuous level) defined by geometric elements of primal and dual grids. Starting from these reconstruction formulas, local mass matrix is constructed according to a well-established design strategy, as the sum of a consistent and a stabilization term

,
[

The construction of the inverse mass matrix that maps from dual edges of a barycentric dual grid to primal faces of an arbitrary tetrahedral grid and possibly inhomogeneous materials proposed in this paper is entirely new.
A key difference with respect to the approach proposed in

,

[

In addition, the recipe to construct the stabilization term adds more flexibility in the construction of the mass matrices.

In general, the major contribution of our construction is not only to have avoided the computation of the local inverses, but rather the fact that it results in symmetric expressions for both types of local mass matrices, thus highlighting the duality relationship between the pair of grids.
A key requirement of our setting would be that the material parameters are constant on dual cells associated with the nodes of the primal simplicial grid.
Instead, to deal with the general case of material parameters that are constant on the grid cells, but arbitrary discontinuous across the interfaces between the cells, two different approaches are proposed. The first one is a weighted averaging technique classically used in Finite Volumes Methods
[

]
,
[

]

3
Mass matrices

In compatible numerical methods, it is always necessary to map DoFs attached to geometric elements of the primal grid into DoFs attached to geometric elements of the dual grid, or viceversa
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
.
This process of converting one type of DoFs to another is performed by the so-called
discrete Hodge star operator

[
13
]
.
This operator is required to satisfy a set of properties.
A first requirement is
consistency
, which will be specified in the next subsections, and
symmetry
.
In addition, we require
positive definiteness
, which assures the stability of the resulting numerical scheme.

One possible method for explicitly converting one type of DoFs into another is to reconstruct the polynomial vector field in each cell starting from DoFs attached to a set of geometric elements, and then project this vector field onto the corresponding geometric elements related by duality.
We will follow this principle to design both types of local mass matrices, which give a discrete realization of the corresponding discrete Hodge operators.

In this section we show how to construct consistent mass matrices which map DoFs attached to primal geometric elements to DoFs attached to dual geometric elements.
Novel proofs are given based on Stokes’ theorem.

3.1
Primal cell reconstruction

Let us consider a pair of vector fields
\bm{u},\bm{w}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
along with a scalar field
w:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined on a given cell
c
. The following well-known integration by parts formulas hold

\int_{c}\bm{u}\cdot\gradient w\,dV=-\int_{c}\divergence\bm{u}\,w\,dV+\int_{\partial c}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\,w\,dS,

(6)

\int_{c}\bm{u}\cdot\curl{\bm w}\,dV=\int_{c}\curl\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}\,dV+\int_{\partial c}\bm{u}\times\bm{n}\cdot\bm{w}\,dS.

(7)

Lemma 1

(Reconstruction formulas)
.

The following tensor identities hold

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in F(c)}{{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\otimes\bm{f}=|{c}|\,\mathbb{I}_{3},

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(c)}{{}\tilde{\bm{f}}_{e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\otimes\bm{e}=|{c}|\,\mathbb{I}_{3}.

(9)

Proof.

Let
\bm{u},\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Thanks to
Eq.

6
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|{c}|\,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}\,&=\int_{c}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}\,dV=\int_{c}\bm{u}\cdot\gradient(\bm w \cdot(\bm x - \bdual n_\C))\,dV\\
&=\int_{\partial c}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n})\,(\bm{w}\cdot(\bm{x}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c}))\,dS\\
&=\sum_{f\in F(c)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{f})\,((\bm{b}_{f}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c})\cdot\bm{w}).\end{split}

(10)

By using the definition of
{{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
, it follows that

\displaystyle|{c}|\,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}=\sum_{f\in F(c)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{f})\,({{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\cdot\bm{w}).

(11)

Since
\bm{w}
is arbitrary,
Eq.

8
is proved.

Thanks to
Eq.

7
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}2|{c}|\,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}&=2\int_{c}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}\,dV=\int_{c}\bm{u}\cdot\curl\big(\bm w \times(\bm x - \bdual n_\C)\big)\,dV\\
&=\int_{\partial c}(\bm{u}\times\bm{n})\cdot\big(\bm{w}\times(\bm{x}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c})\big)\,dS\\
&=\sum_{f\in F(c)}\int_{f}(\bm{u}\times\bm{n}_{f})\cdot\big(\bm{w}\times(\bm{x}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c})\big)\,dS\\
&=\sum_{f\in F(c)}|{f}|\,(\bm{u}\times\bm{n}_{f})\cdot(\bm{w}\times{{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}).\end{split}

(12)

Now, by applying the same argument used in
Eq.

10
to the vector
\bm{u}\times\bm{n}_{f}
restricted to a face
f
, we obtain

|{f}|\,\bm{u}\times\bm{n}_{f}=\sum_{e\in E(f)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{e})\,(\bm{b}_{e}-\bm{p}),

(13)

where
\bm{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an arbitrary node.

Apply
Eq.

13
to every face
f
in the last term of
Eq.

12
, choosing the same node
\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c}
as the arbitrary point involved in the formula.
We obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}2|{c}|\,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}&=\sum_{f\in F(c)}\big(\sum_{e\in E(f)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{e})\,(\bm{b}_{e}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c})\big)\cdot(\bm{w}\times{{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc})\\
&=\sum_{f\in F(c)}\big({{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\times\big(\sum_{e\in E(f)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{e})\,(\bm{b}_{e}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c})\big)\big)\cdot\bm{w}\\
&=\sum_{e\in E(c)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{e})\,(({{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f_{i}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\times(\bm{b}_{e}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c})-{{}\tilde{\bm{e}}_{f_{j}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\times(\bm{b}_{e}-\tilde{\bm{n}}_{c}))\cdot\bm{w}),\end{split}

(14)

where
f_{i},f_{j}
are the unique faces of
c
such that
e=f_{i}\cap f_{j}
, for suitable indices
i=i(e)
and
j=j(e)
, and oriented so that they induce opposite orientations on edge
e
.
Now, dividing by two both members of the last term in
Eq.

14
and using the definition of
{{}\tilde{\bm{f}}_{e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
, it follows that

|{c}|\,\bm{u}\cdot\bm{w}=\sum_{e\in E(c)}(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{e})\,({{}\tilde{\bm{f}}_{e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\cdot\bm{w}).

(15)

Since
\bm{w}
is arbitrary,
Eq.

9
is proved.
∎

3.2
Local mass matrices

Let us focus on a cell
c
where two pairs of constant vector fields are defined, namely
(\bm{u},\bm{v})
and
(\bm{w},\bm{z})
. The two pairs are related by two constitutive relations

\displaystyle\bm{v}={{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{u},

(16)

\displaystyle\bm{z}={{}\mathbb{K}_{2}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{w},

(17)

where
{{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},{{}\mathbb{K}_{2}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
are two symmetric positive definite matrices of order
3
, assumed to be uniform in
c
.

Now, let us introduce the restriction of DoFs of the vector fields to
c
.
In particular, we attach DoFs to geometric elements of the primal and dual grid as follows
{\bm{u}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{u}
,
{\bm{v}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{v}
,
{\bm{w}}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\mathbb{E}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{w}
,
{\bm{z}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{z}
.

The local mass matrix
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
maps DoFs of
\bm{u}
attached to faces to DoFs of
\bm{v}
attached to dual edges of the barycentric dual grid.
We say that
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is a
consistent mass matrix
if

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{\bm{u}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}

(18)

holds exactly for any pair of constant vector fields
\bm{u},\bm{v}
satisfying
Eq.

16
.

Similarly, a local mass matrix
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
maps DoFs of
\bm{w}
attached to edges to DoFs of
\bm{z}
attached to dual faces of the barycentric dual grid.
We say that
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is a
consistent mass matrix
if

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{\bm{w}}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}

(19)

holds exactly for any pair of constant vector fields
\bm{w},\bm{z}
satisfying
Eq.

17
.

An efficient recipe to construct consistent and symmetric matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
combines the geometric identities in
Lemma

1
with the uniformity of the vector fields.

By applying
Lemma

1
and the definitions of DoFs
{\bm{v}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},{\bm{u}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
of
\bm{v}
and
\bm{u}
, we have that

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{v}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{u}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\frac{1}{|{c}|}(\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc})\bm{u}=\frac{(\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T})}{|{c}|}{\bm{u}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},

(20)

and hence, it follows that a symmetric and consistent matrix
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}}{|{c}|}.

(21)

Similarly, by applying
Lemma

1
and the definitions of DoFs
{\bm{z}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},{\bm{w}}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
of
\bm{z}
and
\bm{w}
, we have that

\displaystyle{\bm{z}}^{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{z}=\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{2}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{w}=\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{2}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\frac{1}{|{c}|}(\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc})\bm{w}=\frac{(\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{1}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T})}{|{c}|}{\bm{w}}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},

(22)

and hence, it follows that a symmetric and consistent matrix
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}=\frac{\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}_{2}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}}{|{c}|}.

(23)

The matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
, defined in
Eq.

21
and
Eq.

23
, are symmetric and consistent but are not positive definite.
To achieve this, the idea, developed in
Lemma

2
, is to add to the consistent positive definite matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
a
stability matrix
, which is symmetric and positive semidefinite.
The stability matrix coincides with one proposed in the mimetic literature
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
m
be the cardinality of
F(c)
or
E(c)
.
Let
{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
be a symmetric and positive definite matrix of order
3
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-3})\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{m-3}
be any
(m-3)
-upla of positive real numbers and let
\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}
be the diagonal matrix whose diagonal entries are
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m-3}
. Denote by
w_{\mathcal{F}}
and
w_{\mathcal{E}}
any two orthonormal basis of
\rm{im}(\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc})^{\perp}
and
\rm{im}(\mathbb{E}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc})^{\perp}
, respectively. Accordingly, we define
{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{F}}
and
{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{E}}
to be the matrices whose columns collect the vectors of the bases
w_{\mathcal{F}}
and
w_{\mathcal{E}}
. Then the following matrices

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|{c}|}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}+{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}{{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{F}}}^{T},

(24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|{c}|}\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\tilde{\mathbb{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}+{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}{{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{E}}}^{T},

(25)

are symmetric, consistent and positive definite.

Proof.

It sufficient to show that
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is positive definite. Let
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be such that
\bm{z}^{T}\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{z}=0
.
In order to prove that
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is positive-definite, we have to show that
\bm{z}=\bm{0}
. The condition
\bm{z}^{T}\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{z}=0
is equivalent to require that
(\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\bm{z})^{T}{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\,\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\bm{z}=0
and
({{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{F}}}^{T}{\bm{z}})^{T}\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}({{{}\mathbb{W}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{\mathcal{F}}}^{T}{\bm{z}})=0
.
Since
{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
is symmetric and positive definite, and each
\alpha_{i}
is positive, the latter condition is in turn equivalent to
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\bm{z}=\bm{0}
and
\bm{z}\in\mathrm{im}(\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc})
.
As a consequence,
\bm{z}=\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{y}
for some
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\bm{0}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\bm{z}=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{T}\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{y}=|{c}|\,\bm{y}
.
Thus
\bm{z}=\mathbb{F}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{y}=\bm{0}
, as desired.
∎

In the general case of a grid made of more than one cell, the corresponding global mass matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}
are obtained by assembling, cell by cell, the contributions from the local matrices
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
and
\mathbb{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
, respectively.

4
Sparse inverse mass matrices

To derive inverse mass matrices we will mimic the reasoning of
Section

3
.
In particular, we will derive a local mass matrix starting from reconstruction formulas and subsequently construct a global mass matrix by applying a standard assembling process.
The construction of inverse mass matrices is carried out only for tetrahedral grids through the introduction of specialized geometric identities.
This approach differs from the one described in
Section

3
.
Indeed, the same arguments used in the proof of
Lemma

1
cannot be applied to the barycentric dual grid since dual cells have non-planar geometric elements.

4.1
Dual cell reconstruction

In the following, we give novel proofs of reconstruction formula that provide a constant vector field defined on
{\tilde{c}}
starting from DoFs attached to dual edges and dual faces.

Let
c
be a tetrahedron and let
n
be one of its nodes.
Let
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. For
e_{i}\in E(n)\cap E(c)
, consider the unique face
f_{i}\in F(n)\cap F(c)
to which
e_{i}
does not belong. In this way, for every
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, there exists, and is unique,
r_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
such that
r_{i}\,\bm{f}_{i}\cdot\bm{e}_{i}>0
.

Lemma 3

(Tensor identity)
.

The following tensor identity holds

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}(\bm{f}_{i}\otimes\bm{e}_{i})=3|{c}|\,\mathbb{I}_{3}.

(26)

Proof.

Let
j\in\{1,2,3\}
and let
\bm{m}_{j}
be the unit vector of
\bm{f}_{j}
.
Let us prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}(\bm{f}_{i}\otimes\bm{e}_{i})\,\bm{m}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}(\bm{e}_{i}\cdot\bm{m}_{j})\,\bm{f}_{i}=3|{c}|\,\bm{m}_{j}=3|{c}|\,\mathbb{I}_{3}\bm{m}_{j},

(27)

from which the claimed identity follows, since the set of vectors
\bm{m}_{j}
are linearly independent.
Observe that if
i\neq j
, then
\bm{e}_{i}\cdot\bm{m}_{j}=0
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}(\bm{e}_{i}\cdot\bm{m}_{j})\,\bm{f}_{i}=r_{j}(\bm{e}_{j}\cdot\bm{m}_{j})\,\bm{f}_{j}=r_{j}(\bm{e}_{j}\cdot\bm{m}_{j})|{f_{j}}|\,\bm{m}_{j}=3|{c}|\,\bm{m}_{j},

(28)

where we have used the expression of the volume of a tetrahedron
[
30
]
.
∎

Now, we define the restriction of the following oriented geometric boundary terms
{{}s_{n,e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc},{{}l_{n,f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
to a tetrahedron
c
, which are in bijection with edges
e\in E(n)\cap E(c)
and faces
f\in F(n)\cap F(c)
, respectively.

Figure 2:
(a) Geometric construction of
{{}s_{n,e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
. (b) Geometric construction of
{{}l_{n,f}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
.

We first define
{{}s_{n,e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
. It decomposes into the union of two triangles,
{{}t_{n,e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{(1)}
and
{{}t_{n,e}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}^{(2)}
, associated with the two faces
f_{1},f_{2}\in F(e)
, see Fig.
2
(a).
The first has the vertices
\{\bm{b}_{e},\bm{b}_{f_{1}},\bm{p}_{n,e}\}
, the second the vertices
\{\bm{b}_{e},\bm{b}_{f_{2}},\bm{p}_{n,e}\}
, with node
\bm{p}_{n,e}
defined as follows

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{n,e}\coloneqq\frac{3}{4}\bm{n}+\frac{1}{4}\bm{n}_{1},\,n_{1}\in N(e)\setminus\{n\}.

4.3
Handling material parameter discontinuities inside dual cells

In this section we are interested in the discretization of problems with discontinuous material parameters, which may appear for example when inhomogeneous Poisson or wave propagation problems are considered.
In order to apply the setting detailed in
Section

4.2
, a key requirement is that the material parameters are uniform inside dual cells. In this case, discontinuities of the material parameters do not create any complication if the grid is chosen in such a way that the interfaces between different materials are aligned with the boundaries of the dual cells. Hence, in this case, the assignment of the material parameters must be based on the dual cells. This is a common practice in the Finite Volume (FE) literature
[
20
]
.
We observe that such an assignment is correctly defined since dual cells provide a partition of the computational domain.

However, in most cases, it is desired that the material parameters are constant on cells of the primal grid and that they can be arbitrary discontinuous across interfaces between cells.
To each
c\in C
is assigned a possibly different material parameter
{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
.
As a result, the material parameters of each dual cell are no longer uniform.
In order to handle this kind of situations we can use two different strategies which differ on how the constant vector field is reconstructed inside dual cells.
The first strategy is to use a
weighted average
of the material property in the scheme. The reconstructed vector field is uniform and the construction technique proposed in
Section

4.2
can be applied, by using a suitable choice of the material parameter.
The second strategy consists of using a
piecewise constant vector field
representation inside dual cells, which accounts for discontinuities of the vector field components due to material parameters discontinuities.

4.3.1
Weighted average

Let us consider a dual cell
{\tilde{c}}_{n}\in\tilde{C}
.
To motivate the definition of the new material parameter, let us consider a uniform vector field
\bm{u}
defined in
{\tilde{c}}
. Then, we define the vector field
\bm{v}
whose restriction to each cell
c\in C(n)
satisfy
{{}\bm{v}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\coloneqq{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}{{}\bm{u}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}
. Thus,
\bm{v}
is a piecewise constant vector field that is spatially constant in each
c\in C(n)
.
The weighted average of
\bm{v}
over
{\tilde{c}}
is

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{v}}=\frac{1}{|{{\tilde{c}}}|}\sum_{c\in C(n)}|{{{}{\tilde{c}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}}|\,{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\bm{u}=\Bigg(\frac{1}{|{{\tilde{c}}}|}\sum_{c\in C(n)}|{{{}{\tilde{c}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}}|\,{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\Bigg)\,\bm{u},

(60)

where we have used the fact that
\bm{u}
is uniform in
{\tilde{c}}
.
To each dual cell
{\tilde{c}}
we assign a new material parameter defined by a weighted average as follows

{{}\tilde{\mathbb{K}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0mu{\tilde{c}}}:=\Big(\frac{1}{|{{\tilde{c}}}|}\sum_{c\in C(n)}|{{{}{\tilde{c}}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}}|\,{{}\mathbb{K}}_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=3.01389pt\mkern 2.0muc}\Big)^{-1}.

(61)

We use
Eq.

61
as the material parameter appearing in the expressions of the local mass matrices in
Lemma

6
.
As a consequence, if the material parameter is constant over the whole computational domain, the same material parameter is assigned to every dual cell.

Let us consider a dual cell

4.3.3
Hybrid approach to handle material discontinuities inside dual cells

The proposed approach to handle discontinuities of the material parameter inside dual cells is the following. First, if material parameters are uniform inside a dual cell we can apply the construction detailed in
Section

4.2
.
Otherwise, we apply one among the approaches described in
Section

4.3.1
,
Section

4.3.2
.
We point out that these constructions are applied only to cells which lie at the intersection between regions enclosing different materials.
Thus, the required computational effort to compute local inverses in the approach described in
Section

4.3.2
is negligible in practice.

An important remark is that if we want to satisfy exactly a
multi-material patch test
(see
Section

5
), it is necessary in our hybrid approach to resort to the construction proposed in
Section

4.3.2
.
This is because, being the reconstructed field piecewise constant, it can represent exactly the discontinuities of the vector field components that appear at interfaces between different materials.
Instead, in
Section

4.3.1
, the reconstructed field is constant.

5
Numerical results

Beside all the other applications of sparse inverse mass matrices, to validate the construction proposed in this paper we concentrate on the solution of Poisson boundary value problems formulated with one unknown per element.

We first verified that the proposed technique is able to pass a patch test on grids made by general tetrahedra.
Then, a stationary current conduction problem representing a square resistor is solved. The stationary current conduction is a Poisson problem in region
\Omega
of the 3-D Euclidean space

\displaystyle\curl\bm{E}=\bm{0},

(70a)

\displaystyle\divergence\bm{J}=0,

(70b)

\displaystyle\bm{J}=\sigma\bm{E},

(70c)

where
\sigma
is the material parameter electric conductivity,
\bm{E}
and
\bm{J}
are the
conservative
electrostatic field and the current density vectors, respectively.
The electric conductivity
\sigma
is assumed to be a positive scalar value which is piecewise uniform in each material region.
The region boundary
\partial\Omega
is partitioned into a set of
N^{\mathrm{i}}
surfaces of perfect insulators
\partial\Omega^{\mathrm{i}}_{k}
, and a set of
N^{\mathrm{c}}+1
disjoint equipotential surfaces of perfect conductors
\partial\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}
(usually called electrodes):

\displaystyle\partial\Omega=\sum_{k=1}^{N^{\mathrm{i}}}\partial\Omega^{\mathrm{i}}_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{N^{\mathrm{c}}}\partial\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}.

Electrode

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have proposed a new construction of sparse inverse edge and face mass matrices for arbitrary tetrahedral grids.
The proposed framework unifies the construction of both mass matrices and their inverses and is based on novel geometric reconstruction formulas, from which, local mass matrices are defined as the sum of a consistent and a stabilization term.
The consistent term is defined geometrically and explicitly, thus providing a sensible speedup and an easier implementation, whereas the stabilization term is constructed according to a well-established design strategy.
The resulting expressions for both types of local mass matrices are highly symmetric, thus highlighting the duality relationship between the pair of grids.
Global mass matrices are then constructed using a standard assembling process.
In this way we obtain a sparse matrix representation also for the inverse mass matrix.
This avoids explicitly computing the algebraic inverse of the mass matrix which is found to be typically dense.
A key aspect of the proposed method is that being based on geometric elements of the barycentric dual grid, it can be applied to arbitrary tetrahedral grids.
Two different strategies are proposed to deal with the case of problems involving discontinuous material parameters for the case where the interfaces are aligned with the tetrahedral grid, and an hybrid approach between the two is employed in practical applications.
We tested the newly derived inverse mass matrices by solving a Poisson problem and we verified that patch test is passed, even when material discontinuities are present.
A real case problem involving a square resistor benchmark is analyzed.
Given that reconstruction formulas in each dual cell provide a first-order approximation of a vector field, we expect that first-order error estimates can be derived for the numerical scheme.

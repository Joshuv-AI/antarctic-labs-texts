General coefficient-vanishing results associated with theta series
Shane Chern, Dazhao Tang

Abstract
. There are a number of sporadic coefficient-vanishing results associated with theta series, which suggest certain underlying patterns. By expanding theta powers as linear combinations of products of theta functions, we present two strategies that will provide a unified treatment. Our approaches rely on studying the behavior of products of two theta series under the action of the huffing operator. For this purpose, some explicit criteria are given. We may use the presented methods to not only verify experimentally discovered coefficient-vanishing results, but also to produce a series of general phenomena. Keywords . Ramanujan’s theta series, vanishing coefficient, huffing operator, linear congruence, sublattice of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} . 2020MSC . 11F27, 11B65, 11J13, 11J20.

Introduction

Given a Laurent series
G(q)=\sum_{n}g_{n}q^{n}
, a particularly interesting problem is to determine if there exists an arithmetic progression
Mn+w
such that the coefficients of
G(q)
indexed by this arithmetic progression vanish, namely,
g_{Mn+w}=0
. If so, we say
G(q)
possesses the
coefficient-vanishing
property.

For
G(q)
a quotient of several
Ramanujan’s theta series
,

\displaystyle f(a,b):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{n(n+1)/2}b^{n(n-1)/2},

(1.1)

the study of its vanishing coefficients has a long history. In the
literature, such theta quotients are usually expressed in terms of the following product form of
f(a,b)
in light of Jacobi’s triple product identity
[
4
, p. 35, Entry 19]
:

\displaystyle f(a,b)=(-a,-b,ab;ab)_{\infty},

(1.2)

where the
q
-Pochhammer symbols
are defined by

\displaystyle(A;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=\prod_{k\geq 0}(1-Aq^{k}),

\displaystyle(A_{1},\ldots,A_{\ell};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=(A_{1};q)_{\infty}\cdots(A_{\ell};q)_{\infty}.

The very first consideration along this line was initiated by Richmond and Szekeres
[
24

, who proved with the use of the circle method that
h(4n+2)=0
for the
Ramanujan–Göllnitz–Gordon continued fraction

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}h(n)q^{n}:=\dfrac{(q,q^{7};q^{8})_{\infty}}{(q^{3},q^{5};q^{8})_{\infty}}.

(1.3)

Their result was subsequently extended by Andrews and Bressoud
[
2
]
to arbitrary moduli with recourse to Ramanujan’s
{}_{1}\psi_{1}
summation formula. Through a similar analysis, a more general result was discovered by Alladi and Gordon
[

Another important source of coefficient-vanishing properties associated with theta series is a recent paper of Hirschhorn
[
15

\gamma_{1,1,5}(5n+2)=\gamma_{1,1,5}(5n+4)=0

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\gamma_{1,1,5}(n)q^{n}:=(-q,-q^{4};q^{5})_{\infty}(q,q^{9};q^{10})_{\infty}^{3}.

The basic idea of Hirschhorn’s approach relies on reformulating this

1.1

[

Moreover, in a series of papers of Tang

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\alpha_{i,j,r,\ell,m}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{(q^{i},q^{r-i};q^{r})_{\infty}^{\ell}}{(q^{j},q^{r-j};q^{r})_{\infty}^{m}},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\beta_{i,j,r,\ell,m}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{(q^{i},q^{r-i};q^{r})_{\infty}^{\ell}}{(-q^{j},-q^{r-j};q^{r})_{\infty}^{m}},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\gamma_{i,j,r,\ell,m}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle:=(-q^{i},-q^{r-i};q^{r})_{\infty}^{\ell}(q^{j},q^{2r-j};q^{2r})_{\infty}^{m},

(1.6)

\displaystyle:=(q^{i},q^{r-i};q^{r})_{\infty}^{\ell}(-q^{j},-q^{2r-j};q^{2r})_{\infty}^{m},

(1.7)

\displaystyle:=(q^{i},q^{r-i};q^{r})_{\infty}^{\ell}(q^{j},q^{2r-j};q^{2r})_{\infty}^{m}.

2.
Outline

We outline the basic idea of our approach in this section. Let us begin with Entry 29 of Chapter XVI in Ramanujan’s
Notebooks

[
23
, p. 199]
:

Entry 29.

If
ab=cd
, then

\displaystyle f(a,b)f(c,d)+f(-a,-b)f(-c,-d)=2f(ac,bd)f(ad,bc),

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle f(a,b)f(c,d)-f(-a,-b)f(-c,-d)=2af(b/c,ac^{2}d)f(b/d,acd^{2}).

(2.2)

From this entry, it is clear that products of two theta functions can be reformulated as linear combinations of other theta products. In fact, results of this nature can be traced at least back to Schröter’s 1854 dissertation
[
25
]
, which is recorded as Lemma
3.1
in Section
3
. Setting
a=c=q^{k}
and
b=d=q^{-k+M}
, and then summing (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle f\big(q^{k},q^{-k+M}\big)^{2}=f\big(q^{2k},q^{-2k+2M}\big)f\big(q^{M},q^{M}\big)+q^{k}f\big(q^{2k+M},q^{-2k+M}\big)f\big(1,q^{2M}\big).

Recall that the
\mathbf{H}
-operator has a property that for any given series
F
in
q^{M}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(G(q)\cdot F(q^{M})\big)=F(q^{M})\cdot\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(G(q)\big).

Based on this fact, we observe that on the right-hand side of the above reformulation of
f\big(q^{k},q^{-k+M}\big)^{2}
, there is only one effective factor in each summand under the action of
\mathbf{H}_{M}
, namely,
f\big(q^{2k},q^{-2k+2M}\big)
and
q^{k}f\big(q^{2k+M},q^{-2k+M}\big)
.

More generally, we know from a recent result of Mc Laughlin
[
19
]
that the above treatment can be embedded into a broader setting. In particular, for any theta power or any product of two theta powers, we may expand it as a linear combination of the form
\sum\mathscr{A}\mathscr{F}
, where
\mathscr{A}
is a theta series, usually times a power of
-1
and a power of
q
, and
\mathscr{F}
is a series in
q^{M}
for a certain
M
. Therefore, under the action of
\mathbf{H}_{M}
, only
\mathscr{A}
is effective in each summand. These results are recorded in Theorems
3.5
and
3.8
.

A surprising fact of the aforementioned linear expansion of theta powers is its hidden symmetry. To be precise, if we write the theta power

f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{m}=\sum\mathscr{A}\mathscr{F}

as above, then for each summand
\mathscr{A}\mathscr{F}
with only one exception, there exists a companion summand
\mathscr{A}^{\prime}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
such that
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
. In other words, we may pair the summands and write the theta power as

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{m}=\mathscr{A}_{0}\mathscr{F}_{0}+\sum\big(\mathscr{A}_{I}+\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}\big)\mathscr{F}.

Meanwhile, the quintuple product identity (see
[
14
, p. 99, Eq. (10.1.4)]
or
[
9
, p. 119, Eq. (1.9)]
) tells us that

\displaystyle\frac{f\big({-q^{2k}},-q^{\mu-2k}\big)f\big({-q^{\mu}},-q^{2\mu}\big)}{f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{\mu-k}\big)}

\displaystyle=f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{3k+\mu},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-3k+2\mu}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\kappa+1}q^{k}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{3k+2\mu},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-3k+\mu}\big).

With the linear expansion formula for products of two theta powers, we find that a similar symmetry also holds true for powers of the theta quotient on the left-hand side of the above, and particularly, the exceptional unpaired summand vanishes. Namely,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f\big({-q^{2k}},-q^{\mu-2k}\big)}{f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{\mu-k}\big)}\right)^{m}=\sum\big(\mathscr{A}_{I}+\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}\big)\mathscr{F}.

These results are recorded in Corollaries
3.6
,
3.7
and
3.9
.

With the above preparation in mind, it is easily seen that the theta products or quotients in Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\left(\sum\mathscr{A}\mathscr{F}\right)\cdot\left(\sum\mathscr{B}\mathscr{G}\right),

or with our pairing process,

\displaystyle\left(\mathscr{A}_{0}\mathscr{F}_{0}+\sum\big(\mathscr{A}_{I}+\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}\big)\mathscr{F}\right)\cdot\left(\mathscr{B}_{0}\mathscr{G}_{0}+\sum\big(\mathscr{B}_{I}+\mathscr{B}_{I\!I}\big)\mathscr{G}\right),

where
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{G}
(including
\mathscr{F}_{0}
and
\mathscr{G}_{0}
) are series in
q^{M}
for a certain
M
. We remark that
\mathscr{A}_{0}
or
\mathscr{B}_{0}
may vanish.

Our next observation comes from the known coefficient-vanishing results in the literature. Briefly speaking, if a coefficient-vanishing phenomenon appears, we either encounter a series of cancelations according to the pairing process:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{0}\mathscr{B}_{0}\big)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{0}\mathscr{B}_{I}\big)

\displaystyle=\pm\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{0}\mathscr{B}_{I\!I}\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{B}_{0}\mathscr{A}_{I}\big)

\displaystyle=\pm\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{B}_{0}\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{I}\mathscr{B}_{I}\big)

\displaystyle=\pm\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}\mathscr{B}_{I\!I}\big),

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{I}\mathscr{B}_{I\!I}\big)

\displaystyle=\pm\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}\mathscr{B}_{I}\big),

or we uniformly have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{A}\mathscr{B}\big)=0.

The above situations lead us to two different strategies for proving a given coefficient-vanishing problem. In particular, Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
fall into the former circumstance, and Theorem
1.4
requires the latter strategy. Detailed discussions and proofs are presented in Section
6
.

What remains is a unified verification of the above relations. Since our main focus revolves around the behavior of

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)

\displaystyle:=q^{w}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\ \quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+B^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+(B-B^{\prime})M}\big)

under the action of
\mathbf{H}_{M}
, an effective way is to use the summation form of theta series:

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{\kappa m+\lambda n}q^{AM\binom{m}{2}+A^{\prime}Mm+BM\binom{n}{2}+B^{\prime}Mn+um+vn+w}.

It turns out that what plays a central role under the action of
\mathbf{H}_{M}
is the factor
q^{um+vn+w}
. This requires us to determine the solution set
\{(m,n):m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of the linear congruence

\displaystyle um+vn+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

For this purpose, we begin with the homogeneous case:

\displaystyle um+vn\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

Our target is to represent its solution set as a sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Then for the inhomogeneous case, we only need to make a shift on the previously obtained sublattice. See Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
for details.

Finally, with the knowledge of the structure of the solution set of
um+vn+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}
, we make substitutions for the summation indices
m
and
n
, and obtain an explicit double summation formula for
\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=\mathbf{H}_{M}\left(\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\star\cdots\star)\right)=\sum_{s,t\in\mathbb{Z}}(\star\cdots\star),

in two new indices
s
and
t
over
\mathbb{Z}
. This reformulation allows us to determine for which
\mathscr{H}(q)
, the relation
\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=0
holds true. Also of interest are two companions of
\mathscr{H}(q)
:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)

\displaystyle:=q^{\hat{w}}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\ \quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+(B-B^{\prime})M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+B^{\prime}M}\big),

\displaystyle\check{\mathscr{H}}(q)

\displaystyle:=q^{\check{w}}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+(A-A^{\prime})M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+A^{\prime}M}\big)

\displaystyle\ \quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+(B-B^{\prime})M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+B^{\prime}M}\big).

We provide some effective criteria to check whether
\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)
equals
\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)
or
\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\check{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)
. The related results are presented in Corollary
5.2
and Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
.

3.
Expanding and pairing powers of theta series

3.1.
Schröter and Mc Laughlin

As we have pointed out in the previous section, reformulations of products of Ramanujan’s theta series, such as (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), have been widely studied. Among these results, a particularly interesting identity comes from Schröter’s 1854 dissertation
[
25
]
; see also
[
5
, p. 111]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Schröter)
.

We have

\displaystyle f\big(q^{A}x,q^{A}/x\big)f\big(q^{B}y,q^{B}/y\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{A+B-1}q^{An^{2}}x^{n}f\big(q^{A+B+2An}x/y,q^{A+B-2An}y/x\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{AB(A+B+2n)}(x^{B}y^{A}),q^{AB(A+B-2n)}/(x^{B}y^{A})\big).

(3.1)

In particular,

\displaystyle f\big(qz,q/z\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{1}q^{n^{2}}z^{n}f\big(q^{2+2n},q^{2-2n}\big)f\big(q^{2+2n}z^{2},q^{2-2n}/z^{2}\big).

(3.2)

In a recent paper of Mc Laughlin
[
19
]
, Schröter’s identity was generalized to products of an arbitrary number of theta series. In particular, the following formula is stated in
[
19
, Eq. (4.4)]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Mc Laughlin)
.

For any
m\geq 3
,

\displaystyle f\big(qz,q/z\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{1}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{2}\cdots\sum_{n_{m-1}=0}^{m-1}z^{n_{m-1}}q^{n_{1}^{2}+(n_{2}-n_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(n_{m-1}-n_{m-2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{2+2n_{1}},q^{2-2n_{1}}\big)f\big(q^{m+2n_{m-1}}z^{m},q^{m-2n_{m-1}}/z^{m}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=2}^{m-1}f\big(q^{i(i+1)+2(i+1)n_{i-1}-2in_{i}},q^{i(i+1)-2(i+1)n_{i-1}+2in_{i}}\big).

(3.3)

In fact, the above identity is a particular case of
[
19
, Corollary 4.2]
, which may also be specialized as follows.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
with
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
,

\displaystyle f\big(qxy,q/(xy)\big)^{m_{1}}f\big(qx/y,qy/x\big)^{m_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{1}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{2}\cdots\sum_{n_{m-1}=0}^{m-1}x^{n_{m-1}}y^{2n_{m_{1}}-n_{m-1}}q^{n_{1}^{2}+(n_{2}-n_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(n_{m-1}-n_{m-2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{2+2n_{1}}y^{2},q^{2-2n_{1}}y^{-2}\big)f\big(q^{m+2n_{m-1}}x^{m}y^{m_{1}-m_{2}},q^{m-2n_{m-1}}x^{-m}y^{m_{2}-m_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=2}^{m_{1}}f\big(q^{i(i+1)+2(i+1)n_{i-1}-2in_{i}}y^{-2},q^{i(i+1)-2(i+1)n_{i-1}+2in_{i}}y^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=m_{1}+1}^{m-1}f\big(q^{i(i+1)+2(i+1)n_{i-1}-2in_{i}}y^{2m_{1}},q^{i(i+1)-2(i+1)n_{i-1}+2in_{i}}y^{-2m_{1}}\big).

(3.4)

Proof.

This lemma follows upon setting
z=x
,
a_{1}=\cdots=a_{m_{1}}=y
and
a_{m_{1}+1}=\cdots=a_{m_{1}+m_{2}}=y^{-1}
in
[
19
, Corollary 4.2]
.
∎

3.2.
One theta power

We will make use of Schröter’s formula and Mc Laughlin’s generalization to expand an arbitrary theta power as a summation of theta products. Before stating our result, an auxiliary lemma is required.

Lemma 3.4
.

For each positive integer
i
, define

\displaystyle\tau_{i}(n):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $n=0$},\\
i+1-n&\text{if $n\neq 0$},\end{cases}

for
0\leq n\leq i
. Let
m
be a fixed positive integer. Then

\displaystyle\tau(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m}):=\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\tau_{2}(n_{2}),\ldots,\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)

(3.5)

is a bijection on the Cartesian product
\mathcal{I}_{m}:=\{0,1\}\times\{0,1,2\}\times\cdots\times\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
. Further, for
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})\in\mathcal{I}_{m}
, define

\displaystyle\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m};q)

\displaystyle:=q^{\frac{n_{1}^{2}+(n_{2}-n_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(n_{m}-n_{m-1})^{2}-n_{m}}{2}}f(q^{1+n_{1}},q^{1-n_{1}})

\displaystyle\ \quad\times\prod_{i=2}^{m}f\big(q^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}+(i+1)n_{i-1}-in_{i}},q^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}-(i+1)n_{i-1}+in_{i}}\big).

(3.6)

Then

\displaystyle\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m};q)=\sigma\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\tau_{2}(n_{2}),\ldots,\tau_{m}(n_{m});q\big).

(3.7)

Proof.

The first part of this lemma is obvious. For the second part, we apply induction on
m
. Notice that the
m=1
case is trivial. This is because
\tau_{1}(n)=n
for
n\in\{0,1\}
. Let us assume that (
3.7
) holds true for
m-1
with
m\geq 2
. That is,

\displaystyle\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m-1};q)=\sigma\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\tau_{2}(n_{2}),\ldots,\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1});q\big).

(3.8)

We start by noting that

\displaystyle\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m};q)

\displaystyle\quad=\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m-1};q)q^{\frac{(n_{m}-n_{m-1})^{2}-(n_{m}-n_{m-1})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)n_{m-1}-mn_{m}},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)n_{m-1}+mn_{m}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad=:\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m-1};q)\cdot g(n_{m-1},n_{m}).

(3.9)

Now we claim that

\displaystyle g\big(\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1}),\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)=g(n_{m-1},n_{m}).

(3.10)

To prove this relation, we consider the following four cases:

\blacktriangleright

If
n_{m-1}=0
and
n_{m}=0
, then

\displaystyle g\big(\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1}),\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)=g(0,0)=g(n_{m-1},n_{m}).

\blacktriangleright

If
n_{m-1}\neq 0
and
n_{m}=0
, then

\displaystyle g\big(\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1}),\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)=g\big(m-n_{m-1},0\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(-m+n_{m-1})^{2}-(-m+n_{m-1})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})}\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(-m+n_{m-1})^{2}-(-m+n_{m-1})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})}\big)^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})}\big)^{\frac{s(s-1)}{2}}

(
s\mapsto-1-s
)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(-n_{m-1})^{2}-(-n_{m-1})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)n_{m-1}}\big)^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)n_{m-1}}\big)^{\frac{s(s-1)}{2}}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(-n_{m-1})^{2}-(-n_{m-1})}{2}}f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)n_{m-1}},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)n_{m-1}}\big)

\displaystyle=g(n_{m-1},0)=g(n_{m-1},n_{m}).

\blacktriangleright

If
n_{m-1}=0
and
n_{m}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle g\big(\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1}),\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)=g\big(0,m+1-n_{m}\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(m+1-n_{m})^{2}-(m+1-n_{m})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-m(m+1-n_{m})},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+m(m+1-n_{m})}\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(m+1-n_{m})^{2}-(m+1-n_{m})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-m(m+1-n_{m})}\big)^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+m(m+1-n_{m})}\big)^{\frac{s(s-1)}{2}}

(
s\mapsto 1-s
)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{n_{m}^{2}-n_{m}}{2}}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-mn_{m}}\big)^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+mn_{m}}\big)^{\frac{s(s-1)}{2}}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{n_{m}^{2}-n_{m}}{2}}f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-mn_{m}},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+mn_{m}}\big)

\displaystyle=g(0,n_{m})=g(n_{m-1},n_{m}).

\blacktriangleright

If
n_{m-1}\neq 0
and
n_{m}\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle g\big(\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1}),\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)=g\big(m-n_{m-1},m+1-n_{m}\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(1+n_{m-1}-n_{m})^{2}-(1+n_{m-1}-n_{m})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})-m(m+1-n_{m})},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})+m(m+1-n_{m})}\big)

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(1+n_{m-1}-n_{m})^{2}-(1+n_{m-1}-n_{m})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})-m(m+1-n_{m})})^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)(m-n_{m-1})+m(m+1-n_{m})}\big)^{\frac{s(s-1)}{2}}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(n_{m}-n_{m-1})^{2}-(n_{m}-n_{m-1})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)n_{m-1}-mn_{m}}\big)^{\frac{s(s+1)}{2}}\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)n_{m-1}+mn_{m}}\big)^{\frac{s(s-1)}{2}}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{(n_{m}-n_{m-1})^{2}-(n_{m}-n_{m-1})}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big(q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+(m+1)n_{m-1}-mn_{m}},q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}-(m+1)n_{m-1}+mn_{m}}\big)

\displaystyle=g(n_{m-1},n_{m}).

Finally, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sigma\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\tau_{2}(n_{2}),\ldots,\tau_{m}(n_{m});q\big)

(by (
3.2
))

\displaystyle=\sigma\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\tau_{2}(n_{2}),\ldots,\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1});q\big)\cdot g\big(\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1}),\tau_{m}(n_{m})\big)

(by (
3.10
))

\displaystyle=\sigma\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\tau_{2}(n_{2}),\ldots,\tau_{m-1}(n_{m-1});q\big)\cdot g(n_{m-1},n_{m})

(by (
3.8
))

\displaystyle=\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m-1};q)\cdot g(n_{m-1},n_{m})

(by (
3.2
))

\displaystyle=\sigma(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m};q).

This gives our desired result.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A
,
A^{\prime}
and
k
be integers and let
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
. For any
m\geq 1
, there exist series
\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A})

(0\leq s\leq m-1)
in
q^{A}
, depending only on
s
and
m
, such that

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{ks}f\big((-1)^{\kappa m}q^{km+As+A^{\prime}m},(-1)^{\kappa m}q^{-km-As+(A-A^{\prime})m}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{A^{\prime}s}\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A}).

(3.11)

Further, for
1\leq s\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A})=\mathscr{M}_{m-s}^{(m)}(q^{A}).

(3.12)

Proof.

The case of
m=1
is trivial. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{0}^{(1)}(q^{A})=1.

(3.13)

For
m=2
, we replace
q
by
q^{\frac{A}{2}}
and choose
z=(-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+(A^{\prime}-\frac{A}{2})}
in (
3.2
). Then

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{1}(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{ks}f\big(q^{2k+As+2A^{\prime}},q^{-2k-As+2(A-A^{\prime})}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{A^{\prime}s}q^{\frac{s^{2}-s}{2}A}f\big(q^{(1+s)A},q^{(1-s)A}\big).

Thus, for
0\leq s\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(2)}(q^{A})=q^{\frac{s^{2}-s}{2}A}f\big(q^{(1+s)A},q^{(1-s)A}\big).

(3.14)

For any
m\geq 3
, we replace
q
by
q^{\frac{A}{2}}
and choose
z=(-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+(A^{\prime}-\frac{A}{2})}
in (
3.2
). Then

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{ks}f\big((-1)^{\kappa m}q^{km+As+A^{\prime}m},(-1)^{\kappa m}q^{-km-As+(A-A^{\prime})m}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{A^{\prime}s}\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{1}\cdots\sum_{n_{m-2}=0}^{m-2}q^{\frac{n_{1}^{2}+(n_{2}-n_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(n_{m-2}-n_{m-3})^{2}+(s-n_{m-2})^{2}-s}{2}A}

\displaystyle\quad\times f(q^{(1+n_{1})A},q^{(1-n_{1})A})

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{i=2}^{m-2}f\big(q^{(\frac{i(i+1)}{2}+(i+1)n_{i-1}-in_{i})A},q^{(\frac{i(i+1)}{2}-(i+1)n_{i-1}+in_{i})A}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\times f\big(q^{(\frac{m(m-1)}{2}+mn_{m-2}-(m-1)s)A},q^{(\frac{m(m-1)}{2}-mn_{m-2}+(m-1)s)A}\big).

Recalling the notation in Lemma
3.4
, we find that for
0\leq s\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2})\in\mathcal{I}_{m-2}}\sigma\big(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2},s;q^{A}\big).

(3.15)

Therefore, (
3.11
) is established.

For (
3.12
), we notice that the
m=1
and
2
cases are trivial. Assuming that
m\geq 3
, we deduce from the above that for
1\leq s\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}_{m-s}^{(m)}(q^{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2})\in\mathcal{I}_{m-2}}\sigma\big(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2},m-s;q^{A}\big)

(
\tau
in (
3.5
) is bijective)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2})\in\mathcal{I}_{m-2}}\sigma\big(\tau_{1}(n_{1}),\ldots,\tau_{m-2}(n_{m-2}),\tau_{m-1}(s);q^{A}\big)

(by (
3.7
))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2})\in\mathcal{I}_{m-2}}\sigma\big(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-2},s;q^{A}\big)

(by (
3.15
))

\displaystyle=\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A}),

thereby concluding our proof.
∎

Finally, (
3.12
) allows us to pair terms in (
3.11
).

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}
be as in Theorem

. Then

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{m}=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{P}_{s}\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A}),

(3.16)

where

(3.17)

and for
1\leq s\leq m-1
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{(k+A^{\prime})s}

(3.18)

Proof.

Recalling (
3.12
), we deduce from (
3.11
) that

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+(A-A^{\prime})}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=f\big((-1)^{\kappa m}q^{km+A^{\prime}m},(-1)^{\kappa m}q^{-km+(A-A^{\prime})m}\big)\mathscr{M}_{0}^{(m)}(q^{A})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times q^{A^{\prime}s}\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{ks}f\big((-1)^{\kappa m}q^{km+As+A^{\prime}m},(-1)^{\kappa m}q^{-km-As+(A-A^{\prime})m}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times q^{A^{\prime}s}\mathscr{M}_{m-s}^{(m)}(q^{A}).

For the last summation in the above, we then change the indices by
s\mapsto m-s
. The desired result therefore follows.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}
be as in Theorem
3.5
. Let
A=2A^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{k+A^{\prime}},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-k+A^{\prime}}\big)^{m}=\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\mathscr{P}_{s}\mathscr{M}_{s}^{(m)}(q^{A}),

(3.19)

where

(3.20)

and for

,

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Big\{(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{(k+A^{\prime})s}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(-1)^{\kappa s}q^{(-k+A^{\prime})s}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\Big\{(-1)^{\kappa(m-s)}q^{(k+A^{\prime})(m-s)}

\displaystyle q^{-2km}\mathscr{A}_{s}^{(m_{1})}

\displaystyle\!=q^{-km-km_{1}+3ks}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{Mk+M\mu(2m-m_{1}+3s)},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-Mk+M\mu(m+m_{1}-3s)}\big)

\displaystyle\!+(-1)^{\kappa+1}q^{-2km+km_{1}-3ks}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{Mk+M\mu(m+m_{1}-3s)},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-Mk+M\mu(2m-m_{1}+3s)}\big).

We still observe that the two theta functions are series in
q^{M}
. Since
1\leq m_{1}\leq m-1
and
0\leq s\leq m-1
, we have
-m<3s-m_{1}<3m
. Then
-km-km_{1}+3ks\equiv 0\pmod{M}
only if
3s-m_{1}=m
. Also,
-2km+km_{1}-3ks\equiv 0\pmod{M}
only if
3s-m_{1}=m
. Therefore, when
3s-m_{1}\neq m
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(q^{-2km}\mathscr{A}_{s}^{(m_{1})}\big)=0+0=0.

When
3s-m_{1}=m
, we have

\displaystyle q^{-2km}\mathscr{A}_{s}^{(m_{1})}=\mathscr{A}=0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(q^{-2km}\mathscr{A}_{s}^{(m_{1})}\big)=0.

The above discussion therefore completes our proof.
∎

4.
Sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and the solution set of linear congruences

4.1.
Homogeneous linear congruences

We start by constructing a sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
to represent the solution set of a family of homogeneous linear congruences.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a positive integer. Let
u
and
v
be integers and write
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
. Let
d_{u}=\gcd(u,M)
and
d_{v}=\gcd(v,M)
. Also, let
u^{\prime}=u/d_{u}
and
v^{\prime}=v/d_{v}
. Assume that integers
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
b_{2}
with
\gcd(a_{1},b_{1})=1
and
\gcd(a_{2},b_{2})=1
are such that

\displaystyle u^{\prime}a_{1}+v^{\prime}a_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})},

(4.1)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}b_{1}+v^{\prime}b_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})},

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}=\pm d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}).

(4.3)

Then

\displaystyle\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:um+vn\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\big\{\big((d_{v}/d^{*})(a_{1}s+b_{1}t),(d_{u}/d^{*})(a_{2}s+b_{2}t)\big):(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\big\}.

(4.4)

In particular, if we write
d=\gcd(u,v)
, then the linear Diophantine equation

\displaystyle ud_{v}x+vd_{u}y=dM

(4.5)

is solvable. Let
(x,y)=(\alpha,\beta)
be a specific solution. Then the integers
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
b_{2}
can be chosen as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{1}&=\alpha,\\
b_{1}&=-vd^{*}/(dd_{v}),\\
a_{2}&=\beta,\\
b_{2}&=ud^{*}/(dd_{u}).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.6)

Proof.

For convenience, we write

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}:=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:um+vn\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\},

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:=\big\{\big((d_{v}/d^{*})(a_{1}s+b_{1}t),(d_{u}/d^{*})(a_{2}s+b_{2}t)\big):(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\big\}.

We start by noticing that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:u^{\divideontimes}m+v^{\divideontimes}n\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M^{\divideontimes})\},

where
u^{\divideontimes}=u/d^{*}
,
v^{\divideontimes}=v/d^{*}
and
M^{\divideontimes}=M/d^{*}
. Notice also that
\gcd(u^{\divideontimes},v^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes})=1
. Let us write

\displaystyle d_{u}^{\divideontimes}

\displaystyle=\gcd(u^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes})=\frac{d_{u}}{d^{*}},

\displaystyle d_{v}^{\divideontimes}

\displaystyle=\gcd(v^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes})=\frac{d_{v}}{d^{*}}.

Then,
\gcd(u^{\divideontimes},d_{v}^{\divideontimes})=1
and
\gcd(v^{\divideontimes},d_{u}^{\divideontimes})=1
(and so
\gcd(d_{u}^{\divideontimes},d_{v}^{\divideontimes})=1
) since
\gcd(u^{\divideontimes},v^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes})=1
. This also implies that

\frac{d^{*}M}{d_{u}d_{v}}=\frac{M^{\divideontimes}}{d_{u}^{\divideontimes}d_{v}^{\divideontimes}}

is an integer. Further, we notice that

\displaystyle\frac{u^{\divideontimes}}{d_{u}^{\divideontimes}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{d_{u}}=u^{\prime},

\displaystyle\frac{v^{\divideontimes}}{d_{v}^{\divideontimes}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{d_{v}}=v^{\prime}.

Also, since
d_{u}^{\divideontimes}=\gcd(u^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes})
and
d_{v}^{\divideontimes}=\gcd(v^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes})
, we have

\gcd(u^{\prime},d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}))=\gcd(u^{\divideontimes}/d_{u}^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes}/(d_{u}^{\divideontimes}d_{v}^{\divideontimes}))=1,

and

\gcd(v^{\prime},d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}))=\gcd(v^{\divideontimes}/d_{v}^{\divideontimes},M^{\divideontimes}/(d_{u}^{\divideontimes}d_{v}^{\divideontimes}))=1.

It can be seen that for any solution
(m,n)
to

\displaystyle u^{\divideontimes}m+v^{\divideontimes}n\equiv 0\pmod{M^{\divideontimes}},

we have
d_{v}^{\divideontimes}\mid m
and
d_{u}^{\divideontimes}\mid n
. We shall write
m^{\prime}=m/d_{v}^{\divideontimes}
and
n^{\prime}=n/d_{u}^{\divideontimes}
. Then by recalling that
u^{\prime}=u^{\divideontimes}/d_{u}^{\divideontimes}
,
v^{\prime}=v^{\divideontimes}/d_{v}^{\divideontimes}
and
d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})=M^{\divideontimes}/(d_{u}^{\divideontimes}d_{v}^{\divideontimes})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}=\{(d_{v}^{\divideontimes}m^{\prime},d_{u}^{\divideontimes}n^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:u^{\prime}m^{\prime}+v^{\prime}n^{\prime}\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}))\}.

Now it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{\prime}:=\{(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:u^{\prime}m^{\prime}+v^{\prime}n^{\prime}\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}))\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\{(a_{1}s+b_{1}t,a_{2}s+b_{2}t):(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}=:\mathscr{R}^{\prime}.

Let us fix
m^{\prime}
. Since

\displaystyle u^{\prime}m^{\prime}+v^{\prime}n^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})},

we find that all
n^{\prime}
such that
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
form a bilateral arithmetic progression of common difference
\pm d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
. Namely,

n^{\prime}\equiv-u^{\prime}\overline{v^{\prime}}m^{\prime}\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})},

where
\overline{v^{\prime}}
is such that
v^{\prime}\overline{v^{\prime}}\equiv 1\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})}
; this is doable since
v^{\prime}
is coprime to
d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
as shown in the previous discussion. Now we consider the linear Diophantine equation

\displaystyle a_{1}s+b_{1}t=m^{\prime}.

Since
\gcd(a_{1},b_{1})=1
, it is always solvable and has infinitely many solutions. Let
(s_{0},t_{0})
be a specific solution. Then the general solutions
(s,t)
satisfy

\displaystyle a_{1}(s-s_{0})+b_{1}(t-t_{0})=0.

(4.7)

Recalling that
\gcd(a_{1},b_{1})=1
, we have
s-s_{0}=kb_{1}
and
t-t_{0}=-ka_{1}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, the general solutions
(s,t)
to (
4.7
) can be parameterized as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} s&=s_{0}+kb_{1}\\
t&=t_{0}-ka_{1}\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\text{(with $k\in\mathbb{Z}$)}.

We then observe that the integers

\displaystyle a_{2}s+b_{2}t

\displaystyle=a_{2}(s_{0}+kb_{1})+b_{2}(t_{0}-ka_{1})

\displaystyle=a_{2}s_{0}+b_{2}t_{0}+k(a_{2}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{2})

also form a bilateral arithmetic progression of common difference
\pm d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
since
a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}=\pm d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
according to (
4.3
). To show that the list
\{n^{\prime}\}
is identical to the list
\{a_{2}s+b_{2}t\}
, it suffices to examine that one element in
\{a_{2}s+b_{2}t\}
is also in
\{n^{\prime}\}
. Evidently,
a_{2}s_{0}+b_{2}t_{0}
is such an element. This is because
a_{1}s_{0}+b_{1}t_{0}=m^{\prime}
by our assumption. Then

\displaystyle u^{\prime}m^{\prime}+v^{\prime}(a_{2}s_{0}+b_{2}t_{0})

\displaystyle=u^{\prime}(a_{1}s_{0}+b_{1}t_{0})+v^{\prime}(a_{2}s_{0}+b_{2}t_{0})

\displaystyle=(u^{\prime}a_{1}+v^{\prime}a_{2})s_{0}+(u^{\prime}b_{1}+v^{\prime}b_{2})t_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv 0+0=0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})}.

Therefore, ranging
m^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Z}
gives
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}=\mathscr{L}^{\prime}
, which further yields
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{L}
.

Finally, we notice that

\displaystyle\gcd(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})=\frac{d}{d^{*}}.

Recalling that
d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
is an integer, we conclude that (
4.5
), which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle u^{\prime}x+v^{\prime}y=\frac{d}{d^{*}}\cdot\frac{d^{*}M}{d_{u}d_{v}},

is solvable by Bézout’s lemma. If
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
b_{2}
are chosen as in (
4.6
), we first notice that

\displaystyle b_{1}=-\frac{vd^{*}}{dd_{v}}=-\frac{v\cdot\gcd(u,v,M)}{\gcd(u,v)\gcd(v,M)}

and

\displaystyle b_{2}=\frac{ud^{*}}{dd_{u}}=\frac{u\cdot\gcd(u,v,M)}{\gcd(u,v)\gcd(u,M)}

are integers. Now we examine the following conditions by recalling that
u^{\prime}\alpha+v^{\prime}\beta=dM/(d_{u}d_{v})
:

Eq
. (
4.1
):

\displaystyle u^{\prime}a_{1}+v^{\prime}a_{2}=u^{\prime}\alpha+v^{\prime}\beta=dM/(d_{u}d_{v})\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})};

Eq
. (
4.2
):

\displaystyle u^{\prime}b_{1}+v^{\prime}b_{2}=u^{\prime}(-v^{\prime}d^{*}/d)+v^{\prime}(u^{\prime}d^{*}/d)=0\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})};

Eq
. (
4.3
):

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}=\alpha(u^{\prime}d^{*}/d)-\beta(-v^{\prime}d^{*}/d)=d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}).

Further, if
\gcd(\alpha,v^{\prime}d^{*}/d)=\ell>1
, then
d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
is also divisible by
\ell
since
(u^{\prime}d^{*}/d)\alpha+(v^{\prime}d^{*}/d)\beta=d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
. Thus,
\ell>1
is a common divisor of
v^{\prime}d^{*}/d
and
d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
; this violates the fact that
v^{\prime}
(which is a multiple of
v^{\prime}d^{*}/d
as
d^{*}\mid d
) is coprime to
d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})
. Thus,
\gcd(\alpha,v^{\prime}d^{*}/d)=1
. Similarly, we have
\gcd(\beta,u^{\prime}d^{*}/d)=1
.
∎

4.2.
Inhomogeneous linear congruences

Theorem
4.1
allows us to analyze the inhomogeneous case.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be a positive integer. Let
u
,
v
and
w
be integers and write
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
.

(i)

Assume that
d^{*}\nmid w
. Then

\displaystyle\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:um+vn+w\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\}=\varnothing.

(4.8)

(ii)

Assume that
d^{*}\mid w
. Let
d_{u}=\gcd(u,M)
and
d_{v}=\gcd(v,M)
. Also, let
u^{\prime}=u/d_{u}
and
v^{\prime}=v/d_{v}
. Assume that integers
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
b_{2}
with
\gcd(a_{1},b_{1})=1
and
\gcd(a_{2},b_{2})=1
are such that

\displaystyle u^{\prime}a_{1}+v^{\prime}a_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})},

(4.9)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}b_{1}+v^{\prime}b_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v})},

(4.10)

and

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}=\pm d^{*}M/(d_{u}d_{v}).

(4.11)

Let
(m_{0},n_{0})
be a pair of integers such that

\displaystyle um_{0}+vn_{0}+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

(4.12)

Then

\displaystyle\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:um+vn+w\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\big\{\big((d_{v}/d^{*})(a_{1}s+b_{1}t)+m_{0},(d_{u}/d^{*})(a_{2}s+b_{2}t)+n_{0}\big):(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\big\}.

(4.13)

In particular, we write
d=\gcd(u,v)
and let
(x,y)=(\alpha,\beta)
be a specific solution of the linear Diophantine equation

\displaystyle ud_{v}x+vd_{u}y=dM.

(4.14)

Then

\displaystyle\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:um+vn+w\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\{(\alpha sd_{v}/d^{*}-vt/d+m_{0},\beta sd_{u}/d^{*}+ut/d+n_{0}):(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}.

(4.15)

Proof.

For the first part, we notice that to find a solution
(m,n)
to
um+vn+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}
, it suffices to find integers
m
,
n
and
k
such that
um+vn+Mk=-w
. By Bézout’s lemma, this linear Diophantine equation is solvable only if
w
is a multiple of
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
.

Recall that for
d^{*}\mid w
,
(m_{0},n_{0})
is a pair of integers such that
um_{0}+vn_{0}+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}
. Considering

\displaystyle\{(\tilde{m},\tilde{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:u\tilde{m}+v\tilde{n}\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\}

and

\displaystyle\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:um+vn+w\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\,M)\},

there is a trivial correspondence between
(\tilde{m},\tilde{n})
and
(m,n)
given by

\displaystyle(m,n)=(\tilde{m}+m_{0},\tilde{n}+n_{0}).

Recalling Lemma
4.1
gives the desired result.
∎

5.
Dissecting theta products

5.1.
Huffing operator

Recall from (
1.9
) that the
\mathbf{H}
-operator is defined for
G(q)=\sum_{n}g_{n}q^{n}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(G(q)\big)=\sum_{n}g_{Mn}q^{Mn}.

Throughout, we consider

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q):=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{\kappa m+\lambda n}q^{AM\binom{m}{2}+A^{\prime}Mm+BM\binom{n}{2}+B^{\prime}Mn+um+vn+w},

(5.1)

where
M\geq 1
,
A\geq 1
,
B\geq 1
,
A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
,
u
,
v
,
w
,
\kappa
and
\lambda
are integers. Notice that by the definition of Ramanujan’s theta series,

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{w}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+B^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+(B-B^{\prime})M}\big).

(5.2)

Let
d^{*}
,
d_{u}
,
d_{v}
,
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
b_{2}
,
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
be as in Theorem
4.2
. We further require that
d^{*}\mid w
. It follows from (
(ii)
) with the following changes of variables in (
5.1
):

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} m&=(d_{v}/d^{*})(a_{1}s+b_{1}t)+m_{0},\\
n&=(d_{u}/d^{*})(a_{2}s+b_{2}t)+n_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa m_{0}+\lambda n_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0}\big)+(um_{0}+vn_{0}+w)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s,t\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{(\kappa a_{1}d_{v}+\lambda a_{2}d_{u})(d^{*})^{-1}s+(\kappa b_{1}d_{v}+\lambda b_{2}d_{u})(d^{*})^{-1}t}

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{M(Aa_{1}^{2}d_{v}^{2}+Ba_{2}^{2}d_{u}^{2})(d^{*})^{-2}\binom{s}{2}+M(Ab_{1}^{2}d_{v}^{2}+Bb_{2}^{2}d_{u}^{2})(d^{*})^{-2}\binom{t}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{M\big(A\binom{a_{1}d_{v}(d^{*})^{-1}}{2}+B\binom{a_{2}d_{u}(d^{*})^{-1}}{2}\big)s+M(Aa_{1}d_{v}m_{0}+A^{\prime}a_{1}d_{v}+Ba_{2}d_{u}n_{0}+B^{\prime}a_{2}d_{u})(d^{*})^{-1}s}

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{M\big(A\binom{b_{1}d_{v}(d^{*})^{-1}}{2}+B\binom{b_{2}d_{u}(d^{*})^{-1}}{2}\big)t+M(Ab_{1}d_{v}m_{0}+A^{\prime}b_{1}d_{v}+Bb_{2}d_{u}n_{0}+B^{\prime}b_{2}d_{u})(d^{*})^{-1}t}

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{M(Aa_{1}b_{1}d_{v}^{2}+Ba_{2}b_{2}d_{u}^{2})(d^{*})^{-2}st}

\displaystyle\quad\times q^{(ua_{1}d_{v}+va_{2}d_{u})(d^{*})^{-1}s}\cdot q^{(ub_{1}d_{v}+vb_{2}d_{u})(d^{*})^{-1}t}.

(5.3)

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{H}(q)
be as in (
5.1
), namely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{\kappa m+\lambda n}q^{AM\binom{m}{2}+A^{\prime}Mm+BM\binom{n}{2}+B^{\prime}Mn+um+vn+w}

\displaystyle=q^{w}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+B^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+(B-B^{\prime})M}\big).

Let
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
,
d=\gcd(u,v)
,
d_{u}=\gcd(u,M)
and
d_{v}=\gcd(v,M)
.

(i)

Assume that
d^{*}\nmid w
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=0.

(5.4)

(ii)

Assume that
d^{*}\mid w
. Let
(m_{0},n_{0})
be a pair of integers such that

\displaystyle um_{0}+vn_{0}+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

(5.5)

Assume that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d_{u}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Av\cdot dM,\\
d_{v}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Bu\cdot dM.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.6)

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{A}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ABd^{2}M^{2}}{(d^{*})^{2}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ABd^{2}M^{2}}{2(d^{*})^{2}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}-\dfrac{AB(u+v)dM}{2d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{(A^{\prime}Bu+AB^{\prime}v)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}+\dfrac{AB(um_{0}+vn_{0})dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}{d^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{B}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}{2d^{2}}+\dfrac{Av-Bu}{2d}-\dfrac{A^{\prime}v-B^{\prime}u}{d}-\dfrac{Avm_{0}-Bun_{0}}{d}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa m_{0}+\lambda n_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0}\big)+(um_{0}+vn_{0}+w)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{s,t\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}s+\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}t}q^{\tilde{A}M\binom{s}{2}+\tilde{A}^{\prime}Ms+\tilde{B}M\binom{t}{2}+\tilde{B}^{\prime}Mn+d(d^{*})^{-1}Ms}

(5.7)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa m_{0}+\lambda n_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0}\big)+(um_{0}+vn_{0}+w)}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{d(d^{*})^{-1}M+\tilde{A}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{-d(d^{*})^{-1}M+(\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{\tilde{B}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{(\tilde{B}-\tilde{B}^{\prime})M}\big).

(5.8)

Proof.

The first part is a direct consequence of Theorem
4.2
(i). For the second part, we notice that the pair

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac{Bu\cdot dM}{d_{v}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},\frac{Av\cdot dM}{d_{u}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}\right)

satisfies

\displaystyle ud_{v}\alpha+vd_{u}\beta=dM.

By (
5.6
),
\alpha
and
\beta
are integers. In view of (
(ii)
), or (
4.6
), we may choose

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{1}&=\alpha,\\
b_{1}&=-vd^{*}/(dd_{v}),\\
a_{2}&=\beta,\\
b_{2}&=ud^{*}/(dd_{u}),\end{aligned}\right.

in (
5.1
). Making such substitutions gives (
5.7
). Furthermore, (
5.8
) follows from the definition of Ramanujan’s theta series.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\mathscr{H}(q)
be as in (
5.1
), namely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{w}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+B^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+(B-B^{\prime})M}\big).

Let
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
,
d=\gcd(u,v)
,
d_{u}=\gcd(u,M)
and
d_{v}=\gcd(v,M)
. Assume that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d^{*}&\mid w,\\
d_{u}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Av\cdot dM,\\
d_{v}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Bu\cdot dM.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.9)

Assume also that

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}=-1.

(5.10)

If there exists an integer
J
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} 2d(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid(2dMAv\cdot J-2dAvw-dAuv+dBu^{2}\\
&\quad+2dA^{\prime}uv-2dB^{\prime}u^{2}-Auv^{2}-Bu^{3}),\\
2d(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid(2dMBu\cdot J-2dBuw+dAv^{2}-dBuv\\
&\quad-2dA^{\prime}v^{2}+2dB^{\prime}uv+Av^{3}+Bu^{2}v),\end{aligned}\right.

(5.11)

then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=0.

(5.12)

Proof.

Since (
5.9
) satisfies the assumptions in Theorem
5.1
(ii), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa m_{0}+\lambda n_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0}\big)+(um_{0}+vn_{0}+w)}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{d(d^{*})^{-1}M+\tilde{A}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{-d(d^{*})^{-1}M+(\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{\tilde{B}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{(\tilde{B}-\tilde{B}^{\prime})M}\big).

We choose

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2dMBu\cdot J-2dBuw+dAv^{2}-dBuv-2dA^{\prime}v^{2}+2dB^{\prime}uv+Av^{3}+Bu^{2}v}{2d(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle n_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2dMAv\cdot J-2dAvw-dAuv+dBu^{2}+2dA^{\prime}uv-2dB^{\prime}u^{2}-Auv^{2}-Bu^{3}}{2d(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})};

both are integers according to the assumptions (
5.11
). We also find that

\displaystyle um_{0}+vn_{0}+w=JM,

so (
5.5
) is satisfied. With such a choice of
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
, as well as the assumption (
5.10
), we have

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{\tilde{B}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{(\tilde{B}-\tilde{B}^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle=f\big({-1},-q^{\tilde{B}M}\big)

\displaystyle=(1,q^{\tilde{B}M},q^{\tilde{B}M};q^{\tilde{B}M})_{\infty}=0.

This, in consequence, implies the desired result.
∎

5.2.
The first cancelation

In this part, we construct a pair of products of two theta functions such that they differ by at most a negative sign under the action of the
\mathbf{H}
-operator.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathscr{H}(q)
be as in (
5.1
), namely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{w}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+B^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+(B-B^{\prime})M}\big).

Also, define

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{\hat{w}}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{v+(B-B^{\prime})M},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{-v+B^{\prime}M}\big),

(5.13)

where

\displaystyle\hat{w}=w+\left(1-\frac{2B^{\prime}}{B}\right)v,

provided that
B\mid 2B^{\prime}v
.

Let
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
,
d=\gcd(u,v)
,
d_{u}=\gcd(u,M)
and
d_{v}=\gcd(v,M)
. Then the following statements hold true.

(i)

If
d^{*}B\mid 2B^{\prime}v
, then
w
and
\hat{w}
are simultaneously multiples of
d^{*}
, or simultaneously nonmultiples of
d^{*}
. In particular, if
w
is a nonmultiple of
d^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)=0.

(5.14)

(ii)

If
w
is a multiple of
d^{*}
, we further let
K
be such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} K&\equiv 1\pmod{M/d^{*}},\\
K&\equiv 0\pmod{d/\gcd(u,v,w)}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.15)

Assume that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d^{*}B&\mid 2B^{\prime}v,\\
d_{u}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Av\cdot dM,\\
d_{v}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Bu\cdot dM,\\
(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid(A-2A^{\prime})v^{2}-(B-2B^{\prime})uv-2Buw\cdot K,\\
B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.16)

Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=(-1)^{\epsilon}\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big),

(5.17)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\kappa\cdot\frac{(A-2A^{\prime})v^{2}-(B-2B^{\prime})uv-2Buw\cdot K}{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\cdot\frac{B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K}{B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}.

Proof.

If
d^{*}B\mid 2B^{\prime}v
, we find that

\displaystyle\hat{w}=w+v-d^{*}\cdot\frac{2B^{\prime}v}{d^{*}B}.

Noticing that
v
and
d^{*}\cdot\frac{2B^{\prime}v}{d^{*}B}
are multiples of
d^{*}
yields the first argument in the first part. Further, if
w
is a nonmultiple of
d^{*}
, and so is
\hat{w}
, we deduce (
5.14
) by Theorem
5.1
(i).

For the second part, we know from the first part that
d^{*}\mid\hat{w}
as we have assumed that
d^{*}\mid w
. In Theorem
5.1
, we replace
B^{\prime}
with
B-B^{\prime}
and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa\hat{m}_{0}+\lambda\hat{n}_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{\hat{m}_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}\hat{m}_{0}+B\binom{\hat{n}_{0}}{2}+(B-B^{\prime})\hat{n}_{0}\big)+(u\hat{m}_{0}+v\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{w})}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{d(d^{*})^{-1}M+\hat{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{-d(d^{*})^{-1}M+(\hat{\tilde{A}}-\hat{\tilde{A}}^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{\hat{\tilde{B}}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{(\hat{\tilde{B}}-\hat{\tilde{B}}^{\prime})M}\big),

(5.18)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{A}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ABd^{2}M^{2}}{(d^{*})^{2}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ABd^{2}M^{2}}{2(d^{*})^{2}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}-\dfrac{AB(u+v)dM}{2d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{(A^{\prime}Bu+A(B-B^{\prime})v)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}+\dfrac{AB(u\hat{m}_{0}+v\hat{n}_{0})dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{B}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}{d^{2}},

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{B}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}{2d^{2}}+\dfrac{Av-Bu}{2d}-\dfrac{A^{\prime}v-(B-B^{\prime})u}{d}-\dfrac{Av\hat{m}_{0}-Bu\hat{n}_{0}}{d},

and
\hat{m}_{0}
and
\hat{n}_{0}
are such that

\displaystyle u\hat{m}_{0}+v\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{w}\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

(5.19)

Recalling from (
5.5
),
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
are such that

\displaystyle um_{0}+vn_{0}+w\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

Since
w
is a multiple of
d^{*}=\gcd(u,v,M)
, the above is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{u}{d^{*}}m_{0}+\frac{v}{d^{*}}n_{0}+\frac{w}{d^{*}}\equiv 0\pmod{M/d^{*}}.

(5.20)

The assumption in (
5.15
) that
K
is a multiple of
d/\gcd(u,v,w)
implies that

\displaystyle\frac{wK}{d}=\frac{w}{\gcd(u,v,w)}\cdot\frac{K}{d/\gcd(u,v,w)}

is an integer. We then choose
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{u}{d}m_{0}+\frac{v}{d}n_{0}=-\frac{wK}{d};

(5.21)

this linear Diophantine equation in
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
is solvable since
u/d
and
v/d
are coprime. Also, we may always find such a
K
since
M/d^{*}
is coprime to
d/\gcd(u,v,w)
. This is because
d/\gcd(u,v,w)
is a divisor of
d/d^{*}
by recalling that
w
is a multiple of
d^{*}
, while in the meantime
M/d^{*}
and
d/d^{*}
are coprime. Thus, by (
5.15
) that
K\equiv 1\pmod{M/d^{*}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{u}{d^{*}}m_{0}+\frac{v}{d^{*}}n_{0}=-\frac{wK}{d}\cdot\frac{d}{d^{*}}=-K\frac{w}{d^{*}}\equiv-\frac{w}{d^{*}}\pmod{M/d^{*}},

confirming (
5.20
). Now we choose

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{0}

\displaystyle=-m_{0}+\frac{(A-2A^{\prime})v^{2}-(B-2B^{\prime})uv-2Buw\cdot K}{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}},

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=-n_{0}-\frac{B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K}{B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}.

By (
5.16
), the above two are integers. We also want to point out that the choice of
K
will not affect the last two divisibility conditions in (
5.16
) and the value of
(-1)^{\epsilon}
. This is because these
K
differ by a multiple of
dM/(d^{*}\gcd(u,v,w))
. Further, we notice that

\displaystyle\frac{2Avw\cdot\frac{dM}{d^{*}\gcd(u,v,w)}}{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\cdot\frac{w}{\gcd(u,v,w)}\cdot\frac{Av\cdot dM}{d_{u}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}\cdot\frac{d_{u}}{d^{*}},

\displaystyle\frac{2Buw\cdot\frac{dM}{d^{*}\gcd(u,v,w)}}{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\cdot\frac{w}{\gcd(u,v,w)}\cdot\frac{Bu\cdot dM}{d_{v}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}\cdot\frac{d_{v}}{d^{*}},

so both are even integers. Next, since
w
is a multiple of
d^{*}
and
K\equiv 1\pmod{M/d^{*}}
, we have

\displaystyle u\hat{m}_{0}+v\hat{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=-Kw-\left(1-\frac{2B^{\prime}}{B}\right)v

\displaystyle\equiv-w-\left(1-\frac{2B^{\prime}}{B}\right)v=-\hat{w}\pmod{M}.

Thus, (
5.19
) is satisfied. With such a choice of
\hat{m}_{0}
and
\hat{n}_{0}
, we find that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \hat{\tilde{A}}&=\tilde{A},\\
\hat{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}&=\tilde{A}^{\prime},\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} \hat{\tilde{B}}&=\tilde{B},\\
\hat{\tilde{B}}^{\prime}&=\tilde{B}-\tilde{B}^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

Also,

\displaystyle A\binom{\hat{m}_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}\hat{m}_{0}+B\binom{\hat{n}_{0}}{2}

\displaystyle+(B-B^{\prime})\hat{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0},

and

\displaystyle u\hat{m}_{0}+v\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{w}=um_{0}+vn_{0}+w.

Recall that

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\kappa\cdot\frac{(A-2A^{\prime})v^{2}-(B-2B^{\prime})uv-2Buw\cdot K}{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\cdot\frac{B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K}{B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}.

Substituting the above into (
5.2
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\epsilon}\cdot(-1)^{\kappa m_{0}+\lambda n_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0}\big)+(um_{0}+vn_{0}+w)}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{d(d^{*})^{-1}M+\tilde{A}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{-d(d^{*})^{-1}M+(\tilde{A}-\tilde{A}^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{(\tilde{B}-\tilde{B}^{\prime})M},(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{\tilde{B}^{\prime}M}\big).

Finally, it follows from (
5.8
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\hat{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)=(-1)^{\epsilon}\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big).

This implies our desired result.
∎

5.3.
The second cancelation

We construct another pair of products of two theta functions such that they differ by at most a negative sign under the action of the
\mathbf{H}
-operator.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\mathscr{H}(q)
be as in (
5.1
), namely,

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{w}f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{u+A^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-u+(A-A^{\prime})M}\big)

where

.

,

,
d_{u}=\gcd(u,M)

and
\check{w}

, or simultaneously nonmultiples of
d^{*}
. In particular, if
w
is a nonmultiple of
d^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\check{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)=0.

(5.23)

(ii)

If
w
is a multiple of
d^{*}
, we further let
K
be such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} K&\equiv 1\pmod{M/d^{*}},\\

Assume that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d^{*}AB&\mid 2(A^{\prime}Bu+AB^{\prime}v),\\
d_{u}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Av\cdot dM,\\
d_{v}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid Bu\cdot dM,\\
A(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid B(A-2A^{\prime})u^{2}+A(B-2B^{\prime})uv+2ABuw\cdot K,\\
B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})&\mid B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.24)

Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\mathscr{H}(q)\big)=(-1)^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\check{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big),

(5.25)

where

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=-\kappa\cdot\frac{B(A-2A^{\prime})u^{2}+A(B-2B^{\prime})uv+2ABuw\cdot K}{A(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\cdot\frac{B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K}{B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}.

Proof.

5.3
. For the first part, we only need to notice that

\displaystyle\check{w}=w+u+v-d^{*}\cdot\frac{2(A^{\prime}Bu+AB^{\prime}v)}{d^{*}AB}.

For the second part, the first difference is that we have to replace
A^{\prime}
with
A-A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
with
B-B^{\prime}
in Theorem
5.1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\big(\check{\mathscr{H}}(q)\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\kappa\check{m}_{0}+\lambda\check{n}_{0}}q^{M\big(A\binom{\check{m}_{0}}{2}+(A-A^{\prime})\check{m}_{0}+B\binom{\check{n}_{0}}{2}+(B-B^{\prime})\check{n}_{0}\big)+(u\check{m}_{0}+v\check{n}_{0}+\check{w})}

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{d(d^{*})^{-1}M+\check{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{(Bu\kappa+Av\lambda)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}}q^{-d(d^{*})^{-1}M+(\check{\tilde{A}}-\check{\tilde{A}}^{\prime})M}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times f\big((-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{\check{\tilde{B}}^{\prime}M},(-1)^{\frac{-v\kappa+u\lambda}{d}}q^{(\check{\tilde{B}}-\check{\tilde{B}}^{\prime})M}\big),

where

\displaystyle\check{\tilde{A}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ABd^{2}M^{2}}{(d^{*})^{2}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle\check{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ABd^{2}M^{2}}{2(d^{*})^{2}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}-\dfrac{AB(u+v)dM}{2d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{((A-A^{\prime})Bu+A(B-B^{\prime})v)dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})}+\dfrac{AB(u\check{m}_{0}+v\check{n}_{0})dM}{d^{*}(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})},

\displaystyle\check{\tilde{B}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}{d^{2}},

\displaystyle\check{\tilde{B}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{Av^{2}+Bu^{2}}{2d^{2}}+\dfrac{Av-Bu}{2d}-\dfrac{(A-A^{\prime})v-(B-B^{\prime})u}{d}-\dfrac{Av\check{m}_{0}-Bu\check{n}_{0}}{d},

and
\check{m}_{0}
and
\check{n}_{0}
are such that

\displaystyle u\check{m}_{0}+v\check{n}_{0}+\check{w}\equiv 0\pmod{M}.

The second and major difference comes when we determine
\check{m}_{0}
and
\check{n}_{0}
such that the above congruence holds true. Let
m_{0}
and

\displaystyle\check{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=-n_{0}-\frac{B(A-2A^{\prime})uv+A(B-2B^{\prime})v^{2}+2ABvw\cdot K}{B(Av^{2}+Bu^{2})};

such a choice ensures the above congruence for

and

\check{\tilde{A}}^{\prime}&=\tilde{A}^{\prime},\end{aligned}\right.\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\{\begin{aligned} \check{\tilde{B}}&=\tilde{B},\\
\check{\tilde{B}}^{\prime}&=\tilde{B}-\tilde{B}^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

Also,

\displaystyle A\binom{\check{m}_{0}}{2}+(A-A^{\prime})\check{m}_{0}+B\binom{\check{n}_{0}}{2}

\displaystyle+(B-B^{\prime})\check{n}_{0}

\displaystyle=A\binom{m_{0}}{2}+A^{\prime}m_{0}+B\binom{n_{0}}{2}+B^{\prime}n_{0},

and

\displaystyle u\check{m}_{0}+v\check{n}_{0}+\check{w}=um_{0}+vn_{0}+w.

The desired result therefore follows.
∎

6.
Coefficient-vanishing results

6.1.
Type I — Theorem
1.1

We consider in this section the three coefficient-vanishing results in Theorem
1.1
. In principle, our proof is based on a pairing-and-cancelation process. To begin with, the following preparation is necessary.

First, by Corollary
3.6
with certain common factors extracted and powers of
(-1)
modified, we have, for any
\ell\geq 1
,

\displaystyle f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{ak},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{-ak+M\mu}\big)^{\ell}=\sum_{s=0}^{\ell-1}\mathscr{A}_{s}\mathscr{F}_{s},

where each
\mathscr{F}_{\star}
is a series in
q^{M}
, and

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{0}=f\big((-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{ak\ell},(-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{-ak\ell+M\mu\ell}\big),

(6.1)

and for
1\leq s\leq\ell-1
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{s}

\displaystyle=q^{aks}f\big((-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{ak\ell+M\mu s},(-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{-ak\ell+M\mu(\ell-s)}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{ak(\ell-s)}f\big((-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{ak\ell+M\mu(\ell-s)},(-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{-ak\ell+M\mu s}\big)

\displaystyle=:\mathscr{A}_{s,I}+(-1)^{\kappa\ell}\mathscr{A}_{s,I\!I}.

More generally, we consider

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{I}

\displaystyle:=q^{a\xi k}f\big((-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{ak\ell+M\mu\xi},(-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{-ak\ell+M\mu(\ell-\xi)}\big),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{I\!I}

\displaystyle:=q^{a(\ell-\xi)k}f\big((-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{ak\ell+M\mu(\ell-\xi)},(-1)^{\kappa\ell}q^{-ak\ell+M\mu\xi}\big),

(6.3)

for generic
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Also, by Corollary
3.9
, we have, for any
m\geq 1

7.
Conclusion

The entire project began in an attempt to understand the underlying patterns in the coefficient-vanishing phenomena associated with theta series. It turns out that most of the known results on this topic can be covered by several general relations.

Also of significance is the unified strategy presented in Section
2
, which contains two aspects:

(7.1)

It provides an automatic way to verify if a coefficient-vanishing result, with or without free parameters, holds true. In fact, if such a result is discovered experimentally, one may try to fit it into the four models discussed in Section
6
, or other models of a similar nature. The remaining task then becomes verifying some relations in connection with products of two theta series under the action of the
\mathbf{H}
-operator. Finally, such a verification relies on checking certain divisibility criteria presented in Corollary
5.2
and Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
.

(7.2)

It allows us to manually construct specific or generic coefficient-vanishing results. For instance, the discovery of Theorem
1.4
relies only on one example or two presented in
[
32
]
. Roughly speaking, one may fix some of the free parameters in a generic model, and then use the divisibility conditions in Section
5
to constrain the choice of other parameters so that these conditions remain valid.

The divisibility criteria in Corollary
5.2
and Theorems
5.3
and
5.4
look very sharp, but they work effectively in practice, at least for most coefficient-vanishing results we had encountered. So we expect that our approach may light up a general theory. However, it should be pointed out that two “clouds” need to be taken into serious consideration.

First, what if one or more of the divisibility conditions fail to hold? For instance, we observe that (
1.13
) is still valid even if the latter power
4m+2
is replaced by a multiple of
4
. Namely, for
M=4\ell+6m+8
,
\sigma=-(2\ell+2m+3)k
,
\kappa\in\{1\}
,
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
and any
k
such that
\gcd(k,M)=1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{M}\Bigg(q^{\sigma}\cdot f\big((-1)^{\kappa}q^{2k},(-1)^{\kappa}q^{\mu M-2k}\big)^{2\ell+1}\bigg(\frac{f\big({-q^{2k}},-q^{\mu M-2k}\big)}{f\big((-1)^{\lambda}q^{k},(-1)^{\lambda}q^{\mu M-k}\big)}\bigg)^{4m+4}\Bigg)\overset{?}{=}0.

(7.1)

For this relation, we find that the corresponding criteria in (
5.6
) are no longer true. Thus, a subtle refinement of the results in Sections
4
and
5
is necessary.

Second, it is easily seen that the original result of Richmond and Szekeres
[
24
]
related to (
1.3
) cannot be fit into our framework. Although one may transform (
1.3
) and its generalization to the summation form by Ramanujan’s
{}_{1}\psi_{1}
formula as Andrews and Bressoud
[
2
]
had done, an obstacle occurs due to the lack of an expansion formula for the reciprocal of a generic theta power such as those in Section
3
. Also, the coefficient-vanishing phenomenon appears in series associated with other classical summations such as the Appell–Lerch sum
[
10
]
. These examples suggest one extend the glimmer of our theory to the endless Galaxy.

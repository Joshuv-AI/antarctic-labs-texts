Can we split fractional derivative while analyzing fractional differential equations?


Abstract
Fractional derivatives are generalization to classical integer-order derivatives. The rules which are true for classical derivative need not hold for the fractional derivatives, for example, we cannot simply add the fractional orders α \alpha and β \beta in D t α 0 C ​ D t β 0 C {}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta} to produce the fractional derivative D t α + β 0 C {}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha+\beta} of order α + β \alpha+\beta , in general. In this article we discuss the details of such compositions and propose the conditions to split a linear fractional differential equation into the systems involving lower order derivatives. Further, we provide some examples, which show that the related results in the literature are sufficient but not necessary conditions.

For any positive integers

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}(\cdot)=\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(\cdot)=\frac{d^{m+n}}{dx^{m+n}}(\cdot).

On the other hand, if we replace the integers

and
n
by arbitrary numbers then the resulting FDs need not hold such composition rule. Ordinary differential equation

a_{0}x(t)+a_{1}\frac{d}{dt}x(t)+a_{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}x(t)+\dots+a_{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}x(t)=0,

\begin{split}x(t)&=y_{0}(t),\\
\frac{d}{dt}y_{j}(t)&=y_{j+1}(t),\,j=0,1,\dots,n-2,\\
\frac{d}{dt}y_{n-1}(t)&=\frac{-1}{a_{n}}(a_{0}y_{0}(t)+a_{1}y_{1}(t)+a_{2}y_{2}(t)+\dots+a_{n-1}y_{n-1}(t))\end{split}

containing lower order derivatives. This is not the case with FDEs, in general.

In this article we propose the results regarding such compositions of FDs and splitting of FDEs.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Laplace transform of
f(t)=t^{b-1}E_{(\alpha-\beta,\alpha),b}(-a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta},-a_{2}t^{\alpha})
is given by,

\mathcal{L}\left\{t^{b-1}E_{(\alpha-\beta,\alpha),b}(-a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta},-a_{2}t^{\alpha});s\right\}=\frac{s^{\alpha-b}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}

(10)

Proof.

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}\left\{t^{b-1}E_{(\alpha-\beta,\alpha),b}(-a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta},-a_{2}t^{\alpha});s\right\}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=k}\frac{k!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!}\frac{(-a_{1})^{l_{1}}(-a_{2})^{l_{2}}t^{(\alpha-\beta)l_{1}+\alpha l_{2}+b-1}}{\Gamma(b+(\alpha-\beta)l_{1}+\alpha l_{2})}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=k}\frac{k!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!}\frac{(-a_{1})^{l_{1}}(-a_{2})^{l_{2}}}{S^{b+(\alpha-\beta)l_{1}+\alpha l_{2}}}\\

Can we use composition rule while analyzing fractional differential equations?

This section deals with the results regarding the composition of fractional derivatives.

Theorem 4.1
.

The solution of

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha}x(t)=\lambda x(t),\quad 0<\alpha<1

(11)

is not
C^{1}
at
t=0
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\neq 0
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
2.1
the solution of equation (
11
) is given by

x(t)=x(0)E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha}).

(12)

By using definition
2.3
, we have

\begin{split}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}\\
&=1+\frac{\lambda t^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}t^{2\alpha}}{\Gamma(2\alpha+1)}+\cdots.\end{split}

\therefore\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})=\frac{\lambda t^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}+\frac{\lambda^{2}t^{2\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(2\alpha)}+\frac{\lambda^{3}t^{3\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(3\alpha)}+\cdots.

Since
\alpha-1<0
,
t^{\alpha-1}
is not continuous at
t=0
.

\Rightarrow

\frac{d}{dt}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})
is not continuous in
[0,T]
for any
T>0
.

\Rightarrow

E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})\notin C^{1}[0,T]
.

i.e. The solution
x(t)
given by equation (
12
) is not
C^{1}
at
t=0
.
∎

Note:
Theorem
4.1
shows that the solution of FDE (
11
) does not satisfy the condition in Theorem
2.5
.
Further, the following example shows that

Example 4.1
.

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\frac{1}{4}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\frac{1}{4}}E_{\frac{1}{4}}(\lambda t^{\frac{1}{4}})=\lambda^{2}E_{\frac{1}{4}}(\lambda t^{\frac{1}{4}})

4.1
Composition rule for Mittag-Leffler function

Theorem 4.2
.

If
\alpha_{1}>0
,
\alpha_{2}>0
are real numbers satisfying
0<\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha<1
then

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})

even though
E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})
is not
C^{1}
at
t=0
.

Proof.

\begin{split}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})&={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}\left\{{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}\right\}\\
&={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}t^{\alpha k-\alpha_{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha k-\alpha_{2}+1)}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}t^{\alpha k-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}}{\Gamma(\alpha k-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}+1)}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k+1}t^{\alpha k+\alpha-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\alpha-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}+1)}\\
&=\lambda t^{\alpha-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}E_{\alpha-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}+1}(\lambda t^{\alpha}).\end{split}

In a similar manner, we find

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})=\lambda t^{\alpha-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}E_{\alpha-\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}+1}(\lambda t^{\alpha})

and

\begin{split}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})&={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t^{\alpha})^{k}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+1)}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}t^{\alpha k-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha k-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}+1)}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k+1}t^{\alpha k+\alpha-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\alpha-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}+1)}\\
&=\lambda t^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}+1}(\lambda t^{\alpha}).\end{split}

Thus,
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})
if

0<\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha<1
.
∎

5
Equivalence between FDE and a system of lower order FDEs obtained by splitting the original equation

In this section we provide the conditions for the equivalence between higher order FDE and a system of FDEs of lower order obtained by splitting the original one.

5.1
One term case:

Consider linear FDE,

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha}x(t)=\lambda x(t),\quad 0<\alpha<1.

(19)

Its general solution is,

x(t)=x(0)E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha}).

(20)

If
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha
and if we could write

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}x(t)=\lambda x(t),

(21)

then (
19
) is equivalent to a system

\begin{split}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}x(t)&=y(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}y(t)&=\lambda x(t)\\
\mathrm{or}\quad{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}y(t)&=\lambda x(t).\end{split}

(22)

Laplace transform of system (
22
) gives

\begin{split}s^{\alpha_{1}}X(s)-s^{\alpha_{1}-1}x(0)&=Y(s)\quad\mathrm{and}\\
s^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}Y(s)-s^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}-1}y(0)&=\lambda X(s).\end{split}

Solving this system for
X(s)
, we get

X(s)=x(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-1}}{s^{\alpha}-\lambda}+y(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}-1}}{s^{\alpha}-\lambda}.

Inverse Laplace transform gives

x(t)=x(0)E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha})+y(0)t^{\alpha_{1}}E_{\alpha,\alpha_{1}+1}(\lambda t^{\alpha}).

By Note 2, we have
y(0)={}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}x(0)=0
.
Thus, solution of the system (
22
) is,

x(t)=x(0)E_{\alpha}(\lambda t^{\alpha}).

(23)

Thus the equation (
19
) is equivalent to the system (
22
).

5.2

Consider linear 2-term FDE,

{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha}x(t)+a_{1}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta}x(t)+a_{2}x(t)=0,\quad 0<\beta<1,\,1<\alpha<2.

(24)

Laplace transform of the equation (

X(s)=x(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-1}+a_{1}s^{\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}+x^{\prime}(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-2}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}.

(25)

Using inverse Laplace transform, we obtain the solution of (
24
) as below:

\begin{split}x(t)&=x(0)\left[E_{(\alpha-\beta,\alpha),1}(-a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta},-a_{2}t^{\alpha})+a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta}E_{(\alpha-\beta,\alpha),\alpha-\beta+1}(-a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta},-a_{2}t^{\alpha})\right]\\
&\quad+x^{\prime}(0)tE_{(\alpha-\beta,\alpha),2}(-a_{1}t^{\alpha-\beta},-a_{2}t^{\alpha}).\end{split}

(26)

Now, consider the system

\begin{split}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta}x(t)&=y(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha-\beta}y(t)&=-a_{2}x(t)-a_{1}y(t)\end{split}

(27)

obtained by splitting the FDE (
24
).

As
1<\alpha\leq 2
, so we can write
\alpha=1+\delta
, where
0<\delta\leq 1
.

We consider following three subcases and check whether the system (
27
) is equivalent to (
24
).

Case (i):

Suppose that
0<\beta<\delta\leq 1
.
\Rightarrow 1<\alpha-\beta<2
.

Now, Laplace transform of the system (
27
) gives,

\begin{split}X(s)&=x(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-1}+a_{1}s^{\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}+y(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}\\
&\quad+y^{\prime}(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-\beta-2}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}.\end{split}

(28)

If the equation (
24
) is equivalent to the system (
27
) then their Laplace transforms (
25
) and (
28
) will match.

Comparing (
25
) and (
28
), we get

y(0)s^{-1}-y^{\prime}(0)s^{-2}=x^{\prime}(0)s^{\beta-2}

\Rightarrow y(0)s-y^{\prime}(0)=x^{\prime}(0)s^{\beta}.

(29)

Since
0<\beta<1
, the equation (
29
) cannot be solved for
y(0)
and
y^{\prime}(0)
in terms of
x^{\prime}(0)
. This shows that the Laplace transforms (
25
) and (
28
) are different.

\Rightarrow

In this case equation (
24
) is not equivalent to the system (
27
).

Case (ii):

Suppose that
0<\delta<\beta<1
.
\Rightarrow 0<\alpha-\beta<1
.

Using Laplace transform to the system (
27
), we obtain

X(s)=x(0)\left(\frac{s^{\alpha-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}+a_{1}\frac{s^{\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}\right)+y(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}.

(30)

Comparing (
25
) and (
30
), we get
\frac{x^{\prime}(0)}{y(0)}=s^{1-\beta}
. This contradiction proves that the equation (
24
) is not equivalent to the system (
27
) in this case also.

Case (iii):

Consider
0<\beta=\delta\leq 1
.
\Rightarrow\alpha-\beta=1
.

The equation (
24
) is not equivalent to the system (
27
) because
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{m}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha}\neq{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{m+\alpha},\,m=0,1,\dots
.

Now, we provide a proper way to split FDE (
24
).

5.2.1
Proper way of splitting two term FDE:

Let us split FDE (
24
) in the following system of three FDEs.

\begin{split}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta}x(t)&=y_{1}(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{1-\beta}y_{1}(t)&=y_{2}(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha-1}y_{1}(t)&=-a_{2}x(t)-a_{1}y_{1}(t)\end{split}

(31)

with initial conditions

y_{1}(0)=0,y_{2}(0)=x^{\prime}(0)

The Laplace transform of system (
31
) gives

\begin{split}X(s)&=x(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-1}+a_{1}s^{\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}+y_{1}(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}\\
&\quad+y_{2}(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-2}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}\\
&=x(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-1}+a_{1}s^{\beta-1}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}+x^{\prime}(0)\frac{s^{\alpha-2}}{s^{\alpha}+a_{1}s^{\beta}+a_{2}}.\end{split}

This expression is same as (
25
).

Therefore, the FDE (
24
) is equivalent to the system (
31
).

5.3
Multi-term case:

The following Theorem gives the proper way of splitting multi-term FDE into a system of FDEs with lower orders.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider multi-term FDE

a_{0}x(t)+a_{1}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}x(t)+a_{2}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}x(t)+\dots+a_{m}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{m}}x(t)=0,

(32)

where
k-1<\alpha_{k}\leq k
, k=1,2,…,m   subject
to initial conditions

x^{(i)}(0)=C_{i},\quad i=0,1,\dots,m-1
.

If we split equation (
32
) in a system of
2m-1
equations as

\left.\begin{split}x(t)&=y_{0}(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta_{j}}y_{j}(t)&=y_{j+1}(t),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots,2m-3,\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta_{2m-2}}y_{2m-2}(t)&=\frac{-1}{a_{m}}[a_{0}x(t)+a_{1}y_{1}(t)+a_{2}y_{3}(t)\\
&\quad+a_{3}y_{5}(t)+\dots+a_{m-1}y_{2m-3}(t)],\end{split}\right\}

(33)

where,

\begin{split}\beta_{2j}&=\alpha_{j+1}-j,\qquad j=0,1,2,\dots,m-1,\\
\beta_{2j+1}&=(j+1)-\alpha_{j+1},\qquad j=0,1,2,\dots,m-2,\end{split}

(34)

and
0<\beta_{k}\leq 1
,
k=0,1,2,\dots,2m-2
and
with initial conditions

\begin{split}y_{2i-1}(0)&=0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,i=1,2,\dots,m-1,\\
y_{2i}(0)&=x^{(i)}(0)=C_{i},\qquad i=1,2,\dots,m-1\end{split}

(35)

then the equation (
32
) is equivalent to the system (
33
).

Proof.

Laplace transform of equation (
32
) is given by,

X(s)=\frac{1}{a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}s^{\alpha_{i}}}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}x^{(i)}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}-i-1}\right)\right\}.

(36)

Similarly, the Laplace transform of the system (
33
) is

\begin{bmatrix}s^{\beta_{0}}&-1&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&s^{\beta_{1}}&-1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&s^{\beta_{2}}&-1&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&s^{\beta_{3}}&\dots&0&0\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&s^{\beta_{2m-3}}&-1\\
\frac{a_{0}}{a_{m}}&\frac{a_{1}}{a_{m}}&0&\frac{a_{2}}{a_{m}}&\dots&\frac{a_{m-1}}{a_{m}}&s^{\beta_{2m-2}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{0}(s)\\
Y_{1}(s)\\
Y_{2}(s)\\
Y_{3}(s)\\
\vdots\\
Y_{2m-3}(s)\\
Y_{2m-2}(s)\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}s^{\beta_{0}-1}y_{0}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{1}-1}y_{1}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{2}-1}y_{2}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{3}-1}y_{3}(0)\\
\vdots\\
s^{\beta_{2m-3}-1}y_{2m-3}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{2m-2}-1}y_{2m-2}(o)\\
\end{bmatrix}.

\begin{split}\Rightarrow\,Y_{0}(s)&=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}y_{2i}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{m-j+i+1}s^{\beta_{2i}+\beta_{2i+1}+\dots+\beta_{2i+2m-2j}-1}\right)}{a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}s^{\sum_{k=0}^{2i-2}\beta_{k}}}\\
&\quad+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}y_{2i-1}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{m-j+i+1}s^{\beta_{2i-1}+\beta_{2i}+\dots+\beta_{2i+2m-2j}-1}\right)}{a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}s^{\sum_{k=0}^{2i-2}\beta_{k}}}.\end{split}

(37)

Substituting (
34
) and using initial conditions (
35
) in equation (
37
) we can conclude that

Y_{0}(s)=X(s).

\Rightarrow
The FDE (
32
) is equivalent to the system (
33
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

(i) Any system with equations more than 2m-1 and obtained by splitting (
33
) is equivalent to (
32
).

(ii) Splitting equation (
32
) in a system containing equations less than 2m-1 is not allowed.

The following Theorem
5.2
illustrates this result for the case of
2m-2
equations.

Theorem 5.2
.

If we split the equation (
32
) in a system involving
2m-2
equations as

\left.\begin{split}x(t)&=y_{0}(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta_{j}}y_{j}(t)&=y_{j+1}(t),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots,2m-4\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\beta_{2m-3}}y_{2m-3}(t)&=\frac{-1}{a_{m}}[a_{0}y_{0}(t)+a_{1}y_{1}(t)+a_{2}y_{3}(t)\\
&\quad+a_{3}y_{5}(t)+\dots+a_{m-1}y_{2m-3}(t)],\end{split}\right\}

(38)

with

\begin{split}\beta_{0}&=\alpha_{1},\\
\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}&=\alpha_{2},\\
\alpha_{2}+\beta_{3}+\beta_{4}&=\alpha_{3},\\
\alpha_{3}+\beta_{5}+\beta_{6}&=\alpha_{4},\\
&\vdots\\
\alpha_{m-2}+\beta_{2m-5}+\beta_{2m-4}&=\alpha_{m-1},\\
\alpha_{m-1}+\beta_{2m-3}&=\alpha_{m}.\end{split}

(39)

then the equation (
32
) is not equivalent to the system (
38
).

Proof.

The Laplace transform of system (
38
) gives the system

\begin{bmatrix}s^{\beta_{0}}&-1&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&s^{\beta_{1}}&-1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&s^{\beta_{2}}&-1&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&0&s^{\beta_{3}}&\dots&0&0\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&s^{\beta_{2m-4}}&-1\\
\frac{a_{0}}{a_{m}}&\frac{a_{1}}{a_{m}}&0&\frac{a_{2}}{a_{m}}&\dots&0&\frac{a_{m-1}}{a_{m}}+s^{\beta_{2m-3}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{0}(s)\\
Y_{1}(s)\\
Y_{2}(s)\\
Y_{3}(s)\\
\vdots\\
Y_{2m-4}(s)\\
Y_{2m-3}(s)\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}s^{\beta_{0}-1}y_{0}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{1}-1}y_{1}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{2}-1}y_{2}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{3}-1}y_{3}(0)\\
\vdots\\
s^{\beta_{2m-4}-1}y_{2m-4}(0)\\
s^{\beta_{2m-3}-1}y_{2m-3}(o)\end{bmatrix}.

\begin{split}\therefore Y_{0}(s)&=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}y_{2i}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m-1}a_{m-j+i}s^{\sum_{k=2i}^{2(m-j+i)-2}\beta_{k}-1}+a_{m}s^{\sum_{k=2i}^{2m-3}\beta_{k}-1}\right)}{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_{j}s^{\sum_{k=0}^{2j-2}\beta_{k}}+a_{m}s^{\sum_{k=0}^{2m-3}\beta_{k}}}\\
&\quad+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}y_{2i-1}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m-1}a_{m-j+i}s^{\sum_{k=2i-1}^{2(m-j+i)-2}\beta_{k}-1}+a_{m}s^{\sum_{k=2i-1}^{2m-3}\beta_{k}-1}\right)}{\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}a_{j}s^{\sum_{k=0}^{2j-2}\beta_{k}}+a_{m}s^{\sum_{k=0}^{2m-3}\beta_{k}}}.\end{split}

(40)

Using (
39
), we get

\begin{split}Y_{0}(s)&=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}y_{2i}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-\beta_{2i-1}-1}\right)}{a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}s^{\alpha_{i}}}\\
&\quad+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}y_{2i-1}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-1}\right)}{a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}s^{\alpha_{i}}}.\end{split}

(41)

If (
32
) is equivalent to the system (
38
), then

X(s)=Y_{0}(s)
.

Comparing equations (
36
) and (
41
), we get

\displaystyle y_{2i-1}(0)=0,\quad\quad i=1,2,\dots,m-1,

\displaystyle y_{2i}(0)=x^{(i)}(0)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\beta_{2i-1}=i-\alpha_{i},\quad\quad i=1,2,\dots,m-2.

However, there is no term in equation (
41
) which matches with the term
x^{(m-1)}(0)a_{m}s^{\alpha_{m}-m}
appearing in the Laplace transform
X(s)
given by (
36
).

Hence (
32
) is not equivalent to the system (
38
).
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Consider

a_{0}x(t)+a_{1}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}x(t)+a_{2}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}x(t)+\dots a_{m}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{m}}x(t)=0,

(42)

where
p<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\dots<\alpha_{m}\leq p+1
,
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

with initial conditions
x^{(i)}(0)=C_{i},\qquad i=0,1,2,\dots,p
.

If we split equation (
42
) as

\begin{split}{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}x(t)&=y_{1}(t),\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{j+1}-\alpha_{j}}y_{j}(t)&=y_{j+1}(t),\quad j=1,2,\dots,m-2,\\
{}_{0}^{C}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{m-1}}y_{m-1}(t)&=\frac{-1}{a_{m}}[a_{0}x(t)+a_{1}y_{1}(t)+a_{2}y_{2}(t)\\
&\quad+a_{3}y_{3}(t)+\dots+a_{m-1}y_{m-1}(t)]\end{split}

(43)

with initial conditions
y_{j}(0)=0,\qquad j=1,2,\dots,m-1
,

then the equation (
42
) is equivalent to the system (
43
).

Proof.

The Laplace transform of the equation (
42
) is,

X(s)=\frac{1}{a_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}s^{\alpha_{k}}}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{p}x^{(i)}(0)\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}-i-1}\right)\right\}.

(44)

Similarly, the Laplace transform of the system (
43
) is,

\begin{bmatrix}s^{\alpha_{1}}&-1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&s^{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}&-1&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&s^{\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2}}&\dots&0&0\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
0&0&0&\dots&s^{\alpha_{m-1}-\alpha_{m-2}}&-1\\
\frac{a_{0}}{a_{m}}&\frac{a_{1}}{a_{m}}&\frac{a_{2}}{a_{m}}&\dots&\frac{a_{m-2}}{a_{m}}&\frac{a_{m-1}}{a_{m}}s^{\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{m-1}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}X(s)\\
Y_{1}(s)\\
Y_{2}(s)\\
\vdots\\
Y_{m-2}(s)\\
Y_{m-1}(s)\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{i=0}^{p}s^{\alpha_{1}-i-1}x^{(i)}(0)\\
s^{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}-1}y_{1}(0)\\
s^{\alpha_{3}-\alpha_{2}-1}y_{2}(0)\\
\vdots\\
s^{\alpha_{m-1}-\alpha_{m-2}-1}y_{m-2}(0)\\
s^{\alpha_{m}-\alpha_{m-1}-1}y_{m-1}(o)\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Simplifying, we get

\begin{split}X(s)&=\sum_{i=0}^{p}x^{(i)}(0)\frac{\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}-i-1}\right)}{a_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}s^{\alpha_{k}}}\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}y_{i}(0)\frac{\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{m}a_{j}s^{\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{i}-1}\right)}{a_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}s^{\alpha_{k}}}.\end{split}

(45)

Using initial conditions
y_{i}(0)=0
,
i=1,2,\dots,m-1
in (
45
), we get equation (
44
).
This proves the result.

6
Conclusion

In this article, we discussed the conditions for compositions of Caputo fractional derivatives. We have shown that some Mittag-Leffler functions are satisfying these rules even though the conditions in the respective Theorems given in the literature are not satisfied. Further, we proposed the results describing the proper ways to split the linear FDEs into the systems of FDEs involving lower order derivatives.

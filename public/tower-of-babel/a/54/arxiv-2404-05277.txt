Dynkin abelianisations of flag varieties
Shreepranav Varma Enugandla, Xin Fang, Ghislain Fourier and Christian Steinert

Abstract
. Cerulli Irelli and Lanini have shown that PBW degenerations of flag varieties in type 𝙰 \tt A and 𝙲 \tt C are actually Schubert varieties of higher rank. We introduce Dynkin cones to parameterise specific abelianisations of classical Lie algebras. Within this framework, we generalise their result to all degenerations of flag varieties defined by degree vectors originating from a Dynkin cone. This framework allows us to determine the extent to which a flag variety can be degenerate while still naturally being a Schubert variety of the same Lie type. Furthermore, we compute the defining relations for the corresponding degenerate simple modules in all classical types.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra of classical type with a fixed triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{-}
. The connected simply-connected semi-simple algebraic group associated with
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
G
. For a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
, the projective variety
G/P
is called a
partial flag variety
. The classical (type
\tt A
) flag variety occurs in the special case where
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
P
is the standard Borel subgroup of
G
.

The natural Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt (PBW) filtration on the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra provides an algebra filtration on
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
and, by extension, a filtration on any finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
when a highest weight generator is fixed. The induced associated graded module
V^{a}
becomes a cyclic module for the abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{a}
over the underlying vector space
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
. The PBW filtration on
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
is stable under the actions of
\mathfrak{b}:=\mathfrak{n}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, and this stability leads to an action of
\mathfrak{g}^{a}:=\mathfrak{b}\ltimes\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{a}
on
V^{a}
. These PBW degenerate modules have been studied in
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
for
\mathfrak{g}
of type
\tt A
and
\tt C
.

Consequently,
[
Fei12
]
translated this degeneration in type
\tt A
into a geometric context, by defining the PBW degenerate flag variety as the closure of the action of the algebraic group
G^{a}
corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}^{a}
on the highest weight line in
\mathbb{P}(V)
. The PBW degenerate flag variety is a flat degeneration of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}/B
. In subsequent paper
[
Fei11
]
, a realisation of type
\tt A
in terms of quiver Grassmannians was given. Here, the defining quiver representation comprises a sequence of projections, in contrast to the identity maps used for the classical flag variety. Similarly,
[
FFL14
]
considered the PBW degenerate symplectic flag variety.

In type
\tt A
, this realisation in terms of quiver Grassmannians, as used implicitly in
[
CIL15
]
, demonstrates that the PBW degenerate flag variety is in fact a Schubert variety within a partial flag variety of the same Lie type but of higher rank. Similar results in type
\tt C
are obtained using a folding procedure. They explicitly provided the Weyl group element defining the Schubert variety. In
[
CILL16
]
, similar results were rediscovered using a different construction of embedding
\mathfrak{g}^{a}
into matrices of larger sizes. It is shown that the PBW degenerate modules are Demazure modules of a Lie algebra of higher rank, and their defining relations can be naturally obtained from those of the Demazure modules.

The purpose of this project is to generalise the above results in aiming to determine, for all Lie algebras of classical type, the extent to which one can degenerate a flag variety and still naturally obtain a Schubert variety of the same Lie type (but of larger rank). We introduce a polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, describing the degrees one can associate with negative roots, resulting in an associated graded Lie algebra that is a partial abelianisation of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
. For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
we let
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
denote this partially abelianised Lie algebra. Some of such abelianisations of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
are uniquely determined by fixing a set of pairs of laced simple roots and ensuring that their root vectors commute. One can interpret this as effectively stretching the Dynkin diagram and inserting a new node between each pair of laced simple roots; hence, we call them
Dynkin abelianisations
. Denoting by
\mathbf{c}
such a set of pairs, we introduce the corresponding
Dynkin cone

F^{\mathbf{c}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, which enforces the required property while also incorporating certain additional equalities, which we call the differential operators equalities. For
\mathbf{d}
in the relative interior of the Dynkin cone
F^{\mathbf{c}}
, we denote the associated graded module of a finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module by
V^{\mathbf{d}}
.

The main result of the paper is the following, see Theorem
3.3
for a precise statement.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be of classical type
\tt X_{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
\tt X_{n+\#\mathbf{c}}
, and
V
a finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module. There exists an element
w
in the Weyl group of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
V^{\mathbf{d}}
is isomorphic to a Demazure module of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
associated to
w
as modules for
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

The
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag variety is defined similarly to the degenerate flag variety as a highest weight orbit in
\mathbb{P}(V^{\mathbf{d}})
of the action of an algebraic group associated to
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
. Consequently, we proved that

Theorem

(Corollary
3.4
)
.

For
\mathbf{d}
in the relative interior of
F^{\mathbf{c}}
, the corresponding
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag variety is isomorphic to a Schubert variety in a partial flag variety of higher rank.

A few comments should be made here about the proof. We are following the strategy of
[
CILL16
]
, using the embedding trick. We initially prove the isomorphism for the modules corresponding to fundamental weights. Then, we utilise the fact that any simple, finite-dimensional module can be embedded in a tensor product of fundamental modules. This embedding leads to a surjective map from the degenerate module to the Demazure module. Using the defining ideal for Demazure modules
[
Jos85
]
, we then show that there is a surjective map from the Demazure module to the degenerate module, thus establishing the necessary isomorphism.

Our proof yields further insights beyond the initial results. A key challenge lies in describing the defining ideal of
V^{\mathbf{d}}
. This challenge has been addressed for the PBW degenerate module in types
\tt A
and
\tt C
in
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
, and for type
\tt A
generally in
[
CI+17
]
. With our identification with the Demazure module, we conclude for all classical types:

Theorem
.

The defining ideal of
V^{\mathbf{d}}
is generated by
\mathfrak{b}
and the set
U(\mathfrak{n}_{+})\cdot f_{\alpha}^{\ell_{\alpha}}
, where
f_{\alpha}
are images of the root vectors in
\mathfrak{n}^{\mathbf{d}}
and
\ell_{\alpha}
are appropriate powers.

This concept may be familiar to the reader, as the defining relations of the finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{g}
-modules are similar.

From identifying the
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag variety with the Schubert varieties, we deduce that the
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate varieties are normal, Cohen-Macaulay, and have rational singularities. We conjecture that this statement holds even if
\mathbf{d}
is not in the relative interior, although our current methods do not extend to this scenario.

Describing monomial bases of the PBW degenerate modules is quite challenging, and this task has been accomplished for types
\tt A,C
(
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
]
), the results are known as FFLV-bases, and for type
\tt B
for almost abelian degenerations (
[
Mak19
]
). The combinatorial aspects of our results, along with those by
[
CILL16
]
, relate these bases in types
\tt A
and
\tt C
to the so-called string bases (
[
Lit98
,
BZ01
]
) as shown explicitly in
[
CFL24
]
. One future direction of our work is to adapt the string bases of the Demazure modules in type
\tt D
into FFLV-type bases.

Due to their complexity, we are restricting ourselves to classical types here. The exceptional types have been discussed to some extent by the first author in
[
Enu22
]
. Our construction, which involves degenerations by stretching the Dynkin diagram, can also extend to cases where the stretched diagram is not of finite type. In these instances, one obtains Demazure modules for affine types, though the combinatorics of the proofs still need to be worked out. Even beyond this, if the resulting stretched diagram extends beyond affine type, one must then explore Demazure modules for hyperbolic Kac-Moody algebras.

The paper is structured as follows: in Section
2
, we describe the cone of abelianisations; in Section
3
, we introduce the Dynkin cone and state the main result. Section
4
and Section
5
reduce the proof of the main theorem to fundamental modules, which are then analysed in detail in Section
6
.

2.
Partial abelianisations of Lie algebras and representations

Throughout the entire paper,
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional complex simple Lie algebra of rank
n
and fix a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{-}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\Phi
be the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots in
\Phi
and
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
the set of simple roots in
\Phi^{+}
. The coroot of a root
\beta\in\Phi
is defined as
\beta^{\vee}:=2\beta/(\beta,\beta)
. The Weyl group is denoted by
W
and the simple reflections
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
. Let
\Lambda^{+}
denote the set of dominant integral weights and
\varpi_{1},\ldots,\varpi_{n}
denote the fundamental weights.

We fix a Chevalley basis of
\mathfrak{g}

[
Hum78
, Section 25]
, which is a weighted basis

\{e_{\beta},f_{\beta},h_{i}\mid\beta\in\Phi^{+},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n\}

where
e_{\beta}
(resp.
f_{\beta}
) is of weight
\beta
(resp.
-\beta
). Results in this section do not depend on the choice of a weight basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. Nevertheless, for the main results, the existence of a Chevalley basis is used in the proofs.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, we denote by
V(\lambda)
the finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\lambda
,
v_{\lambda}
denotes a generator of the highest weight space. Further, for
w\in W
, we denote the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{b})\cdot v_{w(\lambda)}
, where
v_{w(\lambda)}\in V(\lambda)
is a generator of the one-dimensional weight space of weight
w(\lambda)
.

For a positive root
\beta
, we define its support by

\mathrm{supp}(\beta):=\{i\mid i\in[n]\text{ with }(\varpi_{i},\beta^{\vee})\neq 0\}.

2.1.
Cone of partial abelianisations

We define a polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\Phi^{+}}
by the following inequalities: a point
\mathbf{d}=(d_{\beta})_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Phi^{+}}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, if for any pair of positive roots
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
,

(2.1)

d_{\beta_{1}}+d_{\beta_{2}}\geq d_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}.

The cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
will be called the
cone of partial abelianisations
of
\mathfrak{g}
, for reasons which will become clear later. The following proposition provides polyhedral geometric properties of the cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)

The polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
is full dimensional in
\mathbb{R}^{\Phi^{+}}
.

(2)

The defining inequalities in (
2.1
) are non-redundant, that is to say, they give the facet description of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

(1)

The function
\mathbf{2}
taking value
2
at all positive roots satisfies the strict inequalities in (
2.1
), hence the cone is of full dimension.

(2)

We show that if an inequality
d_{\alpha}+d_{\beta}\geq d_{\gamma}
for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
with
\alpha+\beta=\gamma
is missing, there exists a point which is not contained in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
. For this, consider a point
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Phi^{+}}
defined by:

\mathbf{d}:=\mathbf{2}-\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}-\mathbf{e}_{\beta}+\mathbf{e}_{\gamma},

where
\mathbf{e}_{\delta}
is the coordinate function of
\delta\in\Phi^{+}
.

This point
\mathbf{d}
fulfills all inequalities in (
2.1
) except
d_{\alpha}+d_{\beta}\geq d_{\gamma}
. Indeed,
d_{\alpha}=d_{\beta}=1
but
d_{\gamma}=3
so the above inequality is violated. We look at all other inequalities in (
2.1
).

-

If
\#\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}\cap\{\alpha,\beta\}=2
then we are in the above case.

-

If
\#\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}\cap\{\alpha,\beta\}=1
, then in the inequality (
2.1
) the left hand side is larger or equal to
3
, but all coordinate of
\mathbf{d}
are less or equal to
3
.

-

If
\#\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}\cap\{\alpha,\beta\}=0
, then in the inequality (
2.1
) the left hand side is larger or equal to
4
, but all coordinate of
\mathbf{d}
are less or equal to
3
.

∎

The polyhedral cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
has an
n
-dimensional lineality space. We will not need this result so the proof is omitted.

2.2.
Filtrations on enveloping algebras

Recall that for a fixed
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
,
d_{\beta}:=\mathbf{d}(\beta)
. We interpret the cone
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
using filtration on Lie algebras.

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the following subspaces of
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
and
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
:

(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}=\mathrm{span}\{f_{\beta}\mid\,\beta\in\Phi^{+},\ d_{\beta}\leq m\},

U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}=\mathrm{span}\{f_{\beta_{1}}\cdots f_{\beta_{\ell}}\mid d_{\beta_{1}}+\ldots+d_{\beta_{\ell}}\leq m\}.

The following proposition follows from (
2.1
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For a fixed
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
,

(1)

the subspaces
\{(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}\mid m\in\mathbb{R}\}
define an
\mathbb{R}
-filtration of Lie algebra on
\mathfrak{n}_{-}
;

(2)

the subspaces
\{U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}\mid m\in\mathbb{R}\}
define an
\mathbb{R}
-filtration of algebra on
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
.

The subspaces

(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{<m}\subseteq(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}\ \ \text{and}\ \ U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{<m}\subseteq U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}

are defined in replacing the inequalities in the definitions of
(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}
and
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}
by the strict ones. The associated graded Lie algebra and the associated graded algebra are defined by:

\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}:=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}/(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{<m}\ \ \text{and}\ \ U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}:=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{R}}U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}/U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{<m}.

For
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, the class of
f_{\beta}
in
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
will be denoted by
f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

According to the following proposition, taking associated graded and taking the universal enveloping commute. In the following we will not distinguish them.

Proposition 2.3
.

The linear map
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}\to U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}
sending
f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}
to the class of
f_{\beta}
in
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}
induces an isomorphism of algebras
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})\cong U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}
.

Proof.

By (
2.1
) and the universal property of the enveloping algebra, there exists an algebra homomorphism
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})\to U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}
. The PBW theorem can be then applied to conclude.
∎

Let
\mathrm{relint}(C)
denote the relative interior of a polyhedral cone
C
.

By the following lemma, the isomorphism type of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
, when
\mathbf{d}
varies in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, depends only on the relative interior of each face.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F
be a face of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathbf{d},\mathbf{e}\in\mathrm{relint}(F)
.

(1)

The linear map

\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}\to\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{e}},\ \ f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}\mapsto f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{e}},\ \ \text{for}\ \beta\in\Phi^{+},

is an isomorphism of Lie algebras.

(2)

The induced algebra homomorphism
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}\to U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{e}}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The second part follows from the first part. To show the first part, it suffices to apply Proposition
2.1
(2).
∎

When
\mathbf{d}
is taken from the (relative) interior of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, we have:

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g}))
,
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}
is isomorphic as an algebra to the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
.

2.3.
Filtrations on modules

Given
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, the algebra filtration on
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
introduced in the previous subsection induces a vector space filtration on cyclic modules.

For any dominant integral weight
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, the simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})\cdot v_{\lambda}
is cyclic. We consider the induced
\mathbb{R}
-filtration on
V(\lambda)
:

(V(\lambda))_{\leq m}^{\mathbf{d}}=U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}\cdot v_{\lambda},

and similarly its subspace
(V(\lambda))_{<m}^{\mathbf{d}}
.
The associated graded space will be denoted by
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
,
i.e.

V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{R}}\ (V(\lambda))^{\mathbf{d}}_{\leq m}/(V(\lambda))^{\mathbf{d}}_{<m}.

The vector space
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
carries a graded
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure. Indeed, for any
k,\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})_{\leq k}^{\mathbf{d}}\cdot(V(\lambda))_{\leq\ell}^{\mathbf{d}}\subseteq(V(\lambda))_{\leq k+\ell}^{\mathbf{d}}.

Let
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
denote the image of
v_{\lambda}
in
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
. The
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
is cyclic, having
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
as a cyclic vector.

The
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
will be called a
weighted PBW degeneration
of
V(\lambda)
. Certain examples of weighted PBW degenerations are studied in type
\tt A
and
\tt C

[
Fan+19
,
BF23
]
. It is shown in these works that such degenerations are closely related to the tropicalisation of flag varieties.

2.4.
Degenerate flag varieties

We fix
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
and let
N_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}:=\mathrm{exp}(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
denote the connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

For a fixed
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, the
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag variety associated to
\lambda
is defined by:

\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda):=\overline{N^{\mathbf{d}}_{-}\cdot[v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}]}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)).

One of the goals of the paper is to study geometric properties of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
for certain
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
.

3.
Dynkin abelianisations: main result

3.1.
Dynkin cones in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})

The goal of this paper is to study the geometry of
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
for
\mathbf{d}
in certain polyhedral cones which will be termed
Dynkin
cones. The reason for the choice of such a name will become clear later.

We start from fixing notations for classical Lie algebras, following
[
Bou02
]
.

3.1.1.
Type
\tt A_{n}

For
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, we denote the positive root

\alpha_{i,j}:=\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{j}\in\Phi^{+}.

We will also use the notation
\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. In this notation, for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,j-1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
.

For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
and
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, we will write
d_{i,j}:=d_{\alpha_{i,j}}
for short.

For a fixed subset
\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\}\subseteq[n-1]
, we define a polyhedral cone
F^{\mathbf{c}}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
by the following inequalities and equalities (PA), stands for partial abelianisation:

-

for
1\leq i\leq j<\ell\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{j+1,\ell}\geq d_{i,\ell}
;

-

all other defining inequalities of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
take equality,

and the following equalities (DO), stands for differential operators:

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k<\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,k}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,k}
.

3.1.2.
Type
\tt B_{n}

The positive roots are:

-

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,j}:=\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{j}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}:=\alpha_{i,n}+\alpha_{j,n}
.

Similarly we have: for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,j-1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,n}=\varepsilon_{i}
and for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}=\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}
.

For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, we will write
d_{i,j}:=d_{\alpha_{i,j}}
and
d_{i,\overline{j}}:=d_{\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}}
. The following total order will be considered:

1<2<\ldots<n<\overline{n}<\ldots<\overline{2}.

For a fixed subset
\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\}\subseteq[n-1]
, we define a polyhedral cone
F^{\mathbf{c}}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
by the following inequalities and equalities (PA):

-

for
1\leq i\leq j<\ell\ <\overline{j+1}
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{j+1,\ell}\geq d_{i,\ell}
;

-

for
1\leq i<k<j+1\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{k,\overline{j+1}}\geq d_{i,\overline{k}}
;

-

for
1\leq k<i<j+1\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{k,\overline{j+1}}\geq d_{k,\overline{i}}
;

-

all other defining inequalities of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
take equality,

and the following equalities (DO):

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k<\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,k}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,k}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k,\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,\overline{k}}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,\overline{k}}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j<k<\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,\overline{j}}+d_{k,\overline{\ell}}=d_{i,\overline{k}}+d_{j,\overline{\ell}}=d_{i,\overline{\ell}}+d_{j,\overline{k}}
.

3.1.3.
Type
\tt C_{n}

The positive roots are:

-

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,j}:=\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{j}
;

-

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
,
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}:=\alpha_{i,n}+\alpha_{j,n-1}
.

For convenience we usually use the notation
\alpha_{i,\overline{n}}:=\alpha_{i,n}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Similarly we have: for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,j-1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,\overline{i}}=2\varepsilon_{i}
and for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}=\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}
.

For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, denote
d_{i,j}:=d_{\alpha_{i,j}}
and
d_{i,\overline{j}}:=d_{\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}}
. The following total order will be considered:

1<2<\ldots<n<\overline{n-1}<\ldots<\overline{1}.

For a fixed subset
\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\}\subseteq[n-1]
, we define a polyhedral cone
F^{\mathbf{c}}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
by the following inequalities and equalities (PA):

-

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
,
j+1\leq\ell\leq\overline{j+1}
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{j+1,\ell}\geq d_{i,\ell}
;

-

for
1\leq i\leq\ell\leq j+1\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{\ell,\overline{j+1}}\geq d_{i,\overline{\ell}}
;

-

for
1\leq\ell\leq i\leq j+1\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{\ell,\overline{j+1}}\geq d_{\ell,\overline{i}}
;

-

all other defining inequalities of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
take equality,

and the following equalities (DO):

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k<\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,k}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,k}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k,\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,\overline{k}}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,\overline{k}}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j<k<\ell\leq n-1
,
d_{i,\overline{j}}+d_{k,\overline{\ell}}=d_{i,\overline{k}}+d_{j,\overline{\ell}}=d_{i,\overline{\ell}}+d_{j,\overline{k}}
.

3.1.4.
Type
\tt D_{n}

The positive roots are:

-

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
,
\alpha_{i,j}:=\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{j}
;

-

for
1\leq i\leq n-2
,
\alpha_{i,n}:=\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{n-2}+\alpha_{n}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq n-1
,
\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}:=\alpha_{i}+\ldots+\alpha_{n-2}+\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n-2}+\ldots+\alpha_{j}
.

Similarly we have: for
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
\alpha_{i,j-1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
, for
1\leq i\leq n-2
,
\alpha_{i,n}=\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{n}
,
\alpha_{n}=\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}
and for
1\leq i<j<n
,
\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j}=\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}
.

For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
, denote
d_{i,j}:=d_{\alpha_{i,j}}
and
d_{i,\overline{j}}:=d_{\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}}
. The following total order will be considered:

1<2<\ldots<n<\overline{n-1}<\ldots<\overline{2}.

For a fixed subset
\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\}\subseteq[n-3]
, we define a polyhedral cone
F^{\mathbf{c}}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
by the following inequalities and equalities (PA):

-

for
1\leq i\leq j<\ell<\overline{j+1}
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{j+1,\ell}\geq d_{i,\ell}
;

-

for
1\leq i<k<j+1\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{k,\overline{j+1}}\geq d_{i,\overline{k}}
;

-

for
1\leq k<i<j+1\leq n
with
j\in\mathbf{c}
,
d_{i,j}+d_{k,\overline{j+1}}\geq d_{k,\overline{i}}
;

-

all other defining inequalities of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
take equality,

and the following equalities (DO):

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k<\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,k}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,k}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j\leq k,\ell\leq n
,
d_{i,\overline{k}}+d_{j,\ell}=d_{i,\ell}+d_{j,\overline{k}}
;

-

for
1\leq i<j<k<\ell\leq n-2
,
d_{i,\overline{j}}+d_{k,\overline{\ell}}=d_{i,\overline{k}}+d_{j,\overline{\ell}}=d_{i,\overline{\ell}}+d_{j,\overline{k}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The subset
F^{\mathbf{c}}
of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
is called a
Dynkin cone
of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
.

The height
\mathrm{ht}(\beta)
of a positive root
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
is by definition the number of simple roots needed to sum up
\beta
. It is clear that the point
\mathbf{h}:=(h_{\beta})_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}}
,
h_{\beta}:=\mathrm{ht}(\beta)
is in
F^{\mathbf{c}}
, while not in the relative interior in general.

Lemma 3.2
.

The Dynkin cone is a non-empty polyhedral cone in
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
.

3.2.
Main results

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra of classical type. For
\mathfrak{g}
being of type
\tt A_{n}
,
\tt B_{n}
and
\tt C_{n}
, we let
\mathbf{c}\subseteq[n-1]
and for
\mathfrak{g}
of type
\tt D_{n}
, we fix
\mathbf{c}\subseteq[n-3]
. Elements in
\mathbf{c}
are
c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}
with
1\leq c_{1}<\ldots<c_{t}\leq n
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
be the Lie algebra of the same type as
\mathfrak{g}
with rank
n+t
. Fix a triangular decomposition
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{+}:=\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}
; denote by
\widetilde{\Phi}
the set of positive roots in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
the set of positive roots and
\{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{n+t}\}
the set of simple roots. The weight lattice will be denoted by
\widetilde{\Lambda}
and the monoid of dominant integral weights is
\widetilde{\Lambda}^{+}
. Fundamental weights are denoted by
\widetilde{\varpi}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\varpi}_{n+t}
. For
\lambda\in\widetilde{\Lambda}^{+}
, the simple module of highest weight
\lambda
is
\widetilde{V}(\lambda)
. Let
W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
be the Weyl group of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
with simple reflections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+t}
.

The connected simply-connected semi-simple algebraic group associated to
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
) will be denoted by
G
(resp.
\widetilde{G}
). The corresponding Borel subgroup with Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{+}
is denoted by
\widetilde{B}
. For a root
\beta\in\Phi
(resp.
\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}
), let
U_{\beta}
(resp.
U_{\widetilde{\beta}}
) denote the root subgroup associated to
\beta
(resp.
\widetilde{\beta}
) in
G
(resp.
\widetilde{G}
).

Fix
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
, we have introduced the graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}^{\mathbf{d}}_{-}
, the weight PBW degeneration
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
as
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module and the
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag variety

\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
, embedded as a highest weight orbit in
\mathbb{P}(V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda))
.

The main result of the paper is:

Theorem 3.3
.

There exist

-

a monoid homomorphism
\Psi:\Lambda^{+}\to\widetilde{\Lambda}^{+}
,

-

a Weyl group element
w_{\mathbf{d}}\in W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
,

-

a
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure on the Demazure module
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
,

such that
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)\cong\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
as
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-modules.

As a consequence, we can realise
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
as a Schubert variety. We embed the Schubert variety in the following way:

X_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{P}_{\Psi(\lambda)}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{V}({\Psi(\lambda)}))

where
\widetilde{P}_{\Psi(\lambda)}
is the parabolic subgroup in
\widetilde{G}
stabilising the weight
\Psi(\lambda)
. The image of
X_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
is contained in
\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda)))
.

Corollary 3.4
.

For
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
, the
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag variety
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
is isomorphic to the Schubert variety
X_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
as projective varieties.

From Corollary
3.4
, for any
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
is isomorphic to a Schubert variety, hence it is normal, Cohen-Macaulay and of rational singularities. We do not know whether in general for
\mathbf{e}\in F^{\mathbf{c}}\setminus\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda)
is still isomorphic to a Schubert variety. However, we conjecture that certain geometric properties, fulfilled by the Schubert varieties, still hold true for
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda)
.

Conjecture 3.5
.

For any
\mathbf{e}\in F^{\mathbf{c}}\setminus\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda)
is normal, Cohen-Macaulay and of rational singularities.

Remark 3.6
.

(1)

Since these three geometric properties are open properties, it remains to show that for any
\mathbf{e}\in F^{\mathbf{c}}\setminus\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
and
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
, there exists a flat family over
\mathbb{C}
such that the generic fibre is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
and the special fibre is isomorphic to
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda)
.

(2)

In type
\tt B_{n}
, a similar question is asked by Makhlin in
[
Mak19
, Section 6]
.

As a second consequence of the theorem, the isomorphism types of the
\mathbf{d}
-degenerate flag varieties, as soon as
\mathbf{d}
is contained in the relative interior of a Dynkin cone, depend only on the face.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
F^{\mathbf{c}}
be a Dynkin cone of
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathbf{d},\mathbf{e}\in\mathrm{relint}(F)
. Then
\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)\cong\mathcal{F}^{\mathbf{e}}(\lambda)
as projective varieties.

4.
Dynkin abelianisation

The goal of this section is to reduce the proof of Theorem
3.3
and Corollary
3.4
to the case of fundamental weights. We keep notations as in the previous section. We sometimes drop the upper index
\mathbf{d}
from
f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}
if no confusion occurs from context.

4.1.
A Lie algebra isomorphism

Fix
\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\}
as in previous section. For convenience denote
c_{0}:=0
. We define a map
\sigma:[n]\to[n+t]
mapping
1,2,\ldots,n
to

c_{0}+1,\ldots,c_{1},c_{1}+2,\ldots,c_{2}+1,c_{2}+3,\ldots,c_{k}+k-1,c_{k}+k+1,\ldots,c_{k+1}+k,\ldots,n+t.

Here the missing elements of the image of
\sigma
in
[n+t]
are
c_{1}+1,c_{2}+2,\ldots,c_{t}+t
.

We define a map
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}:\Phi^{+}\to\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
by: for
\beta=k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}\alpha_{n}\in\Phi^{+}
with
1\leq\ell\leq n
the maximal index with
k_{\ell}\neq 0
,

\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}k_{i}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(i)}+\ldots+\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(i+1)-1})+k_{\ell}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(\ell)}.

Note that if
\beta=\alpha_{k}
is a simple root, then
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha_{k})=\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(k)}
.

Remark 4.1
.

This formulae simplifies if one denotes the positive roots in terms of the standard basis
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}
as in Section
3.1
:

(1)

Type
\tt A_{n}
:
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
.

(2)

Type
\tt B_{n}
:
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}
.

(3)

Type
\tt C_{n}
:
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(2\varepsilon_{i})=2\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}
.

(4)

Type
\tt D_{n}
:
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{n})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(n)}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(n-1)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(n)}
;
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j})=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}
. In this case note that
\mathbf{c}\subseteq[n-3]
implies that
\sigma(n-2)=n+t-2
,
\sigma(n-1)=n+t-1
and
\sigma(n)=n+t
.

The following lemma follows directly from Remark
4.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The map
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
is well-defined and it preserves the root length.

Let
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
denote the image of
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
. Then
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{1})+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{2})\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
implies that
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
and
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{1})+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{2})=\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\in\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
. In particular, the subset
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
is closed in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
in the sense of
[
Hum78
, Section 26.3]
.

Proof.

This statement holds by the formulae in Remark
4.1
. We prove it in type
\tt B_{n}
, and all other types can be proved in a similar way.

From Remark
4.1
, it is clear that
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{1})+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{2})\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
if and only if one of the following conditions are fulfilled

-

\beta_{1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
,
\sigma(j)+1=\sigma(j+1)
,
\beta_{2}=\varepsilon_{j+1}-\varepsilon_{k}
;

-

\beta_{1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
,
\sigma(j)+1=\sigma(j+1)
,
\beta_{2}=\varepsilon_{j+1}
;

-

\beta_{1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
,
\sigma(j)+1=\sigma(j+1)
,
\beta_{2}=\varepsilon_{j+1}+\varepsilon_{k}
for
k>j+1
;

-

\beta_{1}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
,
\sigma(j)+1=\sigma(j+1)
,
\beta_{2}=\varepsilon_{s}+\varepsilon_{j+1}
for
s<j+1
with
i\neq s
.

In all these cases
\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
and
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{1})+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{2})=\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})
.
∎

We define

\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}}:=\sum_{\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})_{-\widetilde{\beta}}

as the sum of root subspaces
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})_{-\widetilde{\beta}}
of weight
-\widetilde{\beta}
where
\widetilde{\beta}
runs through
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
. According to Lemma
4.3
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}}
is a Lie algebra.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
, there exists a Lie algebra isomorphism

\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}\cong\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}}.

Proof.

Recall that in the beginning of the paper we have fixed a Chevalley basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. We define
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}
to be the linear map sending
f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}
to
f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)}
for any
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
. It is clear that
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}
is an isomorphism of vector spaces.

The study of the Lie bracket will be split into the following three cases:

(1)

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}
be such that
\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi^{+}
. In this case
[f_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{d}},f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}]=0
. By Lemma
4.3
,
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)\notin\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
and hence
[f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)},f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)}]=0
.

(2)

When
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
and
d_{\alpha}+d_{\beta}>d_{\alpha+\beta}
, the Lie bracket between the root vectors
f_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{d}}
and
f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}
in
\mathfrak{n}^{\mathbf{d}}_{-}
is zero. We show that
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)\notin\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
hence
[f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)},f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)}]=0
.

First note that since
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, we can assume that
\alpha=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
. From the definition of the Dynkin cones
F^{\mathbf{c}}
, we have
j\in\mathbf{c}
. After applying
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
, by Remark
4.1
,
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
. If
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)
were a positive root,
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)
must contain the summand
\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
with a positive coefficient, this is not possible since those summands in
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)
with positive coefficients are all of form
\varepsilon_{\sigma(k)}
but it follows from
j\in\mathbf{c}
that
\sigma(j)+1\neq\sigma(j+1)
.

(3)

It remains to consider the case when
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
and
d_{\alpha}+d_{\beta}=d_{\alpha+\beta}
.

According to the property of the Chevalley basis, if
\beta-r\alpha,\ldots,\beta+q\alpha
with
q,r\geq 0
is the
\alpha
-chain through
\beta
, then
[f_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{d}},f_{\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}]=(r+1)f_{\alpha+\beta}^{\mathbf{d}}
, where

r+1=\frac{q(\alpha+\beta,\alpha+\beta)}{(\beta,\beta)}.

Similarly the Lie bracket of the two Chevalley basis elements

[f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)},f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)}]=(r^{\prime}+1)f_{\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha+\beta)},

where
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)-r^{\prime}\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha),\ldots,\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)+q^{\prime}\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)
is the
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)
-chain through
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)
and

r^{\prime}+1=\frac{q^{\prime}(\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha+\beta),\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha+\beta))}{(\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta),\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta))}.

If
q=q^{\prime}
holds, then
r+1=r^{\prime}+1
as by Lemma
4.2
,
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
preserves the root lengths.

To show that
q=q^{\prime}
we split into two cases. First notice that:
1\leq q\leq 2
and
q=2
(that is to say,
\beta+\alpha
and
\beta+2\alpha
are both positive roots) if and only if
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
\tt B_{n}
,
\beta=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
and
\alpha=\varepsilon_{j+1}
, or
\mathfrak{g}
is or type
\tt C_{n}
,
\beta=2\varepsilon_{j+1}
and
\alpha=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
. This happens only when
\alpha
and
\beta
have different root lengths.

If
q=1
, then
\alpha
and
\beta
have the same length, again by Lemma
4.2
,
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)
and
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)
have the same length, therefore
q^{\prime}\leq 1
. It remains to show that
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
. Since
\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, we can assume that
\alpha=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
and in view of the root length,
\beta=\varepsilon_{j+1}+\varepsilon_{k}
for some
k\neq i,j+1
. From
d_{\alpha}+d_{\beta}=d_{\alpha+\beta}
it follows
j\notin\mathbf{c}
and hence
\sigma(j)+1=\sigma(j+1)
. Therefore

\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(j+1)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(k)}=\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(k)}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}.

If
q=2
, in the two cases above, either
\alpha
or
\beta
is of form
\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j+1}
. The same argument as in the case
q=1
shows that
\sigma(j)+1=\sigma(j+1)
and hence
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)+\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)
and
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)+2\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\alpha)
are in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
and
q^{\prime}=2
.

As conclusion, we have shown that
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}
preserves the structure constants, it is therefore an isomorphism.
∎

4.2.
Monoid morphism

We set
\Sigma:=\sigma([n])
to be the image of
\sigma
.

Proposition 4.5
.

There exists a unique monoid homomorphism
\Psi:\Lambda^{+}\to\widetilde{\Lambda}^{+}
such that for any
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
,

(\Psi(\lambda),\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)^{\vee})=(\lambda,\beta^{\vee})\ \ \text{and}\ \ (\Psi(\lambda),\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell}^{\vee})=0

for any
\ell\in[n+t]\setminus\Sigma
.

Proof.

First notice that the values of
\Psi(\lambda)
on all simple coroots in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
are fixed by the two conditions above. The uniqueness then follows.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, we define

\Psi(\lambda):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda,\alpha_{j}^{\vee})\widetilde{\varpi}_{\sigma(j)}.

It follows from the definition that
\Psi
is a monoid homomorphism. The second equality then holds since the fundamental weights and the simple roots are dual to each other. It remains to verify the first equality. Note that for
\beta=k_{1}\alpha_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}\alpha_{n}
, the coroot

\beta^{\vee}=\frac{1}{(\beta,\beta)}\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j})\alpha_{j}^{\vee}.

It then follows

\displaystyle(\Psi(\lambda),\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda,\alpha_{j}^{\vee})(\widetilde{\varpi}_{\sigma(j)},\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta)^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta),\psi_{\mathbf{c}}(\beta))}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda,\alpha_{j}^{\vee})k_{\sigma(j)}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)},\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\beta,\beta)}\left(\lambda,\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j})\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda,\beta^{\vee}),

where we have used the fact that
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
preserves the root length, and
k_{j}=k_{\sigma(j)}
follows from definition.
∎

4.3.
Weyl group element

We study the Lie theoretic structure of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
.

Let
w\in W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Weyl group element. Denote

\widetilde{\Phi}_{w}^{+}:=\{\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}\mid w(\widetilde{\beta})<0\}

the set of positive roots which are sent to negative by
w
. If
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}
is a reduced decomposition of
w
, then

(4.1)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{w}^{+}=\{\widetilde{\alpha}_{i_{r}},s_{i_{r}}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{i_{r-1}}),\ldots,s_{i_{r}}\cdots s_{i_{2}}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{i_{1}})\}.

We define
w_{\mathbf{c}}\in W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
to be the Weyl group element

w_{\mathbf{c}}:=w_{0,t}(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t}s_{t}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1})\cdots(s_{3}\cdots s_{c_{2}+2}s_{2}\cdots s_{c_{2}+1})(s_{2}\cdots s_{c_{1}+1}s_{1}\cdots s_{c_{1}})

where
w_{0,t}
is the longest element in the subgroup of
W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
generated by
s_{t+1},\ldots,s_{n+t}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

We have:
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
.

The proof of the proposition will occupy the rest of this subsection. Before going to the proof we discuss a few consequences of the proposition which will be used later.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
be two positive roots which are not in
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
. Then
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}+\widetilde{\beta}_{2}\notin\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
.

Proof.

If
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}+\widetilde{\beta}_{2}\in\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
, we need to show that either
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}
or
\widetilde{\beta}_{2}
is in
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
. This holds by the Theorem on
[
Pap94
, Page 663]
because in the language of
loc.cit
, Proposition
4.6
implies that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
is associated to
w_{\mathbf{c}}
and associated sets form convex sets of roots.
∎

Let
I(\infty)
be the left ideal in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})
generated by
f_{\widetilde{\beta}}
for
\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}\setminus\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
.

Corollary 4.8
.

We have
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})\oplus I(\infty)
as vector spaces.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\beta}_{N}
be an enumeration of positive roots in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
with

\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}=\{\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\beta}_{m}\}.

It then follows from the PBW theorem that
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})+I(\infty)
. To show that the sum is direct, take

x=\sum_{k=m+1}^{N}x_{k}f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{k}}\in I(\infty)

where
x_{k}\in U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})
and
f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{k}}
is the fixed root vector of weight
-\widetilde{\beta}_{k}
. By Corollary
4.7
, for any PBW monomial
f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{1}}^{s_{1}}\cdots f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{N}}^{s_{N}}
appearing in
x_{k}
with a non-zero coefficient, after having written
f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{1}}^{s_{1}}\cdots f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{N}}^{s_{N}}f_{\widetilde{\beta}_{k}}
as a sum of the PBW basis, there is no PBW monomial from
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
appearing in it with a non-zero coefficient. This shows that for any
k=m+1,\ldots,N
,
x_{k}=0
and hence the sum is direct.
∎

The proof of Proposition
4.6
is a case-by-case analysis, but the proofs for type
\tt B_{n}
,
\tt C_{n}
and
\tt D_{n}
rely on the results in type
\tt A_{n}
.

4.3.1.
Type
\tt A_{n}

We start from the case where
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
\tt A_{n}
, then
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is of type
\tt A_{n+t}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For
1\leq k\leq t+1
, let

v_{k}:=(s_{k}\cdots s_{c_{k}+k-1})\cdots(s_{k}\cdots s_{c_{k-1}+k})

where
c_{0}:=0
and
c_{t+1}:=n
. Then
w_{\mathbf{c}}=v_{t+1}v_{t}\cdots v_{1}
.

Proof.

We consider the following reduced decomposition of
w_{0,t}
:

w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n}}=(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t-1})\cdots(s_{t+1}s_{t+2})s_{t+1}.

It follows then

w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n}}s_{t+1}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t}=v_{t}(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1})\cdots(s_{t+1}s_{t+2})s_{t+1},

and hence

w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n}}(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t}s_{t}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1})=v_{t+1}(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1})\cdots(s_{t+1}s_{t+2})s_{t+1}(s_{t}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1}).

Note that on the right hand side, the part without
v_{t+1}
is a reduced decomposition of the longest element in the Weyl group generated by
s_{t},s_{t+1},\ldots,s_{t+c_{t}-1}
. It means that we can write

w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n}}(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t}s_{t}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1})=v_{t+1}(s_{t}\cdots s_{c_{t}+t-1})\cdots(s_{t}s_{t+1})s_{t}.

The above procedure can be then iterated to conclude.
∎

The word
w_{\mathbf{c}}
coincides with the word appearing in
[
CI+17
, Section 5.2]
. It follows from
[
CI+17
, Proposition 9]
that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
.

For simplicity we set
\ell_{j}:=\sigma(j)
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
\ell_{0}=0
. If
j\notin\mathbf{c}
, then
\ell_{j+1}=\ell_{j}+1
; otherwise
\ell_{j+1}=\ell_{j}+2
. The permutation given by
w_{\mathbf{c}}
is explicitly described in the following lemma:

Lemma 4.10

(
[
CI+17
, Proposition 6]
)
.

The following explicit formulae for
w_{\mathbf{c}}
hold:

(1)

If
\ell_{j}=\ell_{j-1}+1
, then
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\ell_{j})=\ell_{j}+(n-2j+2)
.

(2)

If
\ell_{j}=\ell_{j-1}+2
, then
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\ell_{j}-1)=\ell_{j}-j
and
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\ell_{j})=\ell_{j}+n-j+1
.

4.3.2.
Type
\tt BC_{n}

In this case, we choose the decomposition for
w_{0,t}
as:

w_{0,t}^{\tt BC_{n}}=s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}},

where
w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}}
is defined in the proof of Lemma
4.9
. The reducedness of the decomposition is not assumed and will be proved as a consequence of Proposition
4.6
.

Since
c_{t}\leq n-1
,
c_{t}+t\leq n+t-1
, by Lemma
4.9
,
w_{\mathbf{c}}
can be written into

(4.2)

w_{\mathbf{c}}=s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})v_{t}\cdots v_{1}.

It follows that
\ell(w_{\mathbf{c}})\leq n^{2}
, where
\ell
is the length function on the Weyl group.

Since
\#\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}=n^{2}
, it remains to show that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
is a subset of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}
, i.e.,
w_{\mathbf{c}}
sends all roots in
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
to negative roots.

The Weyl group of type
\tt BC_{n}
acts on
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}
as follows: the simple reflections
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1}
acts as in the
\tt A_{n-1}
case;
s_{n}
fixes
\varepsilon_{i}
for
i\neq n
and sends
\varepsilon_{n}
to
-\varepsilon_{n}
.

It then follows that

s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})

sends
\varepsilon_{t+1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n+t}
to
-\varepsilon_{n+t},\ldots,-\varepsilon_{t+1}
, respectively.

We write again
\ell_{j}:=\sigma(j)
for
1\leq j\leq n
and have a closer look at
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)})
. If
j=n
,
\ell_{n}=\sigma(n)=n+t
hence
v_{t}\cdots v_{1}
will fix
\varepsilon_{\sigma(n)}
because the maximal index appearing in the simple reflections in
v_{t}\cdots v_{1}
is at most
c_{t}+t-1\leq n-1+t-1=n+t-2
, hence
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{\ell_{n}})=-\varepsilon_{t+1}
is a negative root.

When
1\leq j\leq n-1
, we can apply Lemma
4.10
for
\tt A_{n-1}
and obtain that

v_{t}\cdots v_{1}(\varepsilon_{\ell_{j}})=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{\ell_{j}+n-2j+1},&\text{if }\ell_{j}=\ell_{j-1}+1;\\
\varepsilon_{\ell_{j}+n-j},&\text{if }\ell_{j}=\ell_{j-1}+2,\end{cases}

where
\ell_{0}
is set to be
0
. In order to show that
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{\ell_{j}})
is a negative root, in the first case, it suffices to show that
\ell_{j}+n-2j+1\geq t+1
. Indeed, notice that
\ell_{j}+n-2j+1
is decreasing when
j
increases from
1
to
n
, hence its minimum is attained when
\ell_{n}-n+1=t+1
. For the second case, since
\ell_{j}\geq j+1
,
\ell_{j}-j+n\geq n+1>t+1
.

Since
w_{\mathbf{c}}
sends all
\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}
to negative roots, it maps all
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}
to negative roots as well.

As the word
w_{\mathbf{c}}
has the
\tt A_{n-1}
word
w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}}
as suffix, by the description of
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}
in (
4.1
), the roots of form
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
are in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}
.

As conclusion we have shown that in type
\tt B_{n}
,
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}
is a subset of
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}
. The same proof applies to type
\tt C_{n}
,
mutatis mutandis
.

4.3.3.
Type
\tt D_{n}

In this case, we choose the decomposition for
w_{0,t}
in the following way, according to the parity of
n
: when
n
is even,
w_{0,t}^{\tt D_{n}}
is

s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t-1})\cdots(s_{t+3}\cdots s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})(s_{t+2}\cdots s_{n+t-1})(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}};

when
n
is odd,
w_{0,t}^{\tt D_{n}}
has the form

s_{n+t-1}(s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+3}\cdots s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})(s_{t+2}\cdots s_{n+t-1})(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}},

where
w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}}
is defined in the proof of Lemma
4.9
. Again we do not need the reducedness of the decomposition in the proof.

Again since
c_{t}\leq n-3
,
c_{t}+t\leq n+t-3
and we can apply Lemma
4.9
to write
w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}}
into
v_{t}\cdots v_{1}
. It then follows that
\ell(w_{\mathbf{c}})\leq n(n-1)
and again we show that
w_{\mathbf{c}}
maps all positive roots in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}
to negative roots. The strategy is the same as the
\tt BC_{n}
case.

First notice that for the same reason as in the
\tt BC_{n}
case, all roots of form
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}-\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)+1}
are in
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}
.

When
n
is even, it is straightforward to check that

s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t-1})\cdots(s_{t+2}\cdots s_{n+t-1})(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})(\varepsilon_{t+k})=-\varepsilon_{n+t-k+1},

and when
n
is odd, the difference is that

s_{n+t-1}(s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+2}\cdots s_{n+t-1})(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t-2}s_{n+t})(\varepsilon_{t+k})=-\varepsilon_{n+t-k+1}

holds only for
k=2,\ldots,n
, but sends
\varepsilon_{t+1}
to
\varepsilon_{n+t}
.

If
n
is even, the same argument as in the type
\tt BC_{n}
shows that
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)})
are of form
-\varepsilon_{s}
hence
w_{\mathbf{c}}
sends roots
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(n)}
and the roots
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)}
to negative roots. If
n
is odd, what will cause problem is when
v_{t}\cdots v_{1}(\varepsilon_{\sigma(j)})=\varepsilon_{t+1}
for some
j
. But from the argument in the type
\tt BC_{n}
, this will only happen when
j=n
. For the root
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}+\varepsilon_{\sigma(n)}
,
w_{\mathbf{c}}
sends
\varepsilon_{\sigma(n)}
to itself and
\varepsilon_{\sigma(i)}
to some
-\varepsilon_{s}
for
s<\sigma(n)=n+t
. This shows that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
is a subset of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}
and the proof of Proposition
4.6
is then complete.

Corollary 4.11
.

The above decompositions of
w_{\mathbf{c}}
appeared in Lemma
4.9
, in Equation (
4.2
) and for type
\tt D_{n}
(with
w_{0,t}^{\tt A_{n-1}}
replaced by

) are reduced.

Proof.

It suffices to notice that the length of the decomposition equals to

, which coincides with

and hence equals to the length of

.
∎

4.4.
Reduction to fundamental weights

Let
\mathbf{c}
be fixed as in previous subsections and
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
. We define
w_{\mathbf{d}}:=w_{\mathbf{c}}
. According to Lemma
2.4
, the isomorphic type of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
does not depend on
\mathbf{d}
as long as
\mathbf{d}
is chosen from
\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
.

4.4.1.
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure

We start from defining a
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure on the Demazure module
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
. We temporarily write
\lambda^{\prime}:=\Psi(\lambda)
. The Demazure module admits the following description

\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{+})\cdot v_{w_{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda^{\prime})}=w_{\mathbf{d}}(w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})w_{\mathbf{d}})\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}.

Since
v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
is a highest weight vector, all positive roots act by zero on it. That is to say, only those root vectors, whose weights are contained in
w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}\cap(-\widetilde{\Phi}^{+})
, may act on
v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
in a non-zero way.

We claim that
w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}\cap(-\widetilde{\Phi}^{+})=-\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{+}
. Indeed, if a positive root
\widetilde{\beta}
satisfies
w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\beta})<0
, then
-w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\beta})
is a positive root in
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{+}
. Moreover, if a positive root
\widetilde{\beta}
satisfies
w_{\mathbf{d}}(\widetilde{\beta})<0
, then
-\beta=w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(-w_{\mathbf{d}}(\beta))
is in the set on the left hand side.

By Proposition
4.6
, we have:

\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})=w_{\mathbf{d}}U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}

where the Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}}
is defined in Section
4.1
. Let
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{-}(\lambda^{\prime}):=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
. According to Proposition
4.4
, there exists a Lie algebra isomorphism
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}\cong\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}}
. We use this isomorphism to define a
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure on
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{-}(\lambda^{\prime})
by assigning for
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
,

x\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}:=\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}(x)\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}},

and extends to
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
by the universal property. Since
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})
and
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{-}(\lambda^{\prime})
are isomorphic as vector spaces, we obtain a
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure on
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})
by pulling back the one on
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{-}(\lambda^{\prime})
.

4.4.2.
Ansatz

The goal of this subsection is to reduce the proof of Theorem
3.3
to the following proposition.

Proposition 4.12
.

For the monoid homomorphism
\Psi
, the Weyl group element
w_{\mathbf{d}}:=w_{\mathbf{c}}\in W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
, and any
1\leq k\leq n
,
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\varpi_{k})\cong\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\varpi_{k}))
as
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-modules.

The proof of the proposition will occupy the entire Section
6
. Assuming the proposition, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.3
.

4.4.3.
Defining relations

We start from defining a surjective
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module map

\varphi_{\lambda}^{-}:\widetilde{V}^{-}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))\to V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda).

Once it has been defined, composing with the
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module isomorphism
\widetilde{V}^{-}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))\cong\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
gives a surjective
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module map

\varphi_{\lambda}:\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\lambda))\to V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda).

For simplicity we write again
\lambda^{\prime}:=\Psi(\lambda)
. Note that

\widetilde{V}^{-}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cong U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})/I^{\mathbf{c}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}

where
I^{\mathbf{c}}_{\lambda^{\prime}}:=\mathrm{Ann}_{U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})}v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
is a left ideal in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
and

V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}=U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})/\mathrm{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})}v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}.

We denote
J_{\lambda}:=\sigma^{-1}_{\mathbf{d}}(I^{\mathbf{c}}_{\lambda^{\prime}})
, where
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}}\to\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}
is naturally extended to an isomorphism of universal enveloping algebras
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}:U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})\cong U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
.

Proposition 4.13
.

We have
J_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})}v^{\mathbf{d}}_{\lambda}
.

Once this proposition is proved, the isomorphism
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}
will pass to the quotient and yields the desired surjective linear map
\varphi_{\lambda}^{-}
. Since the
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure on
\widetilde{V}^{-}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})
is given by
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}
, such a linear map is a
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module map.

4.4.4.
Proof of Proposition
4.13

We start from looking at the defining ideal of the Demazure module
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})
. By
[
Jos85
]
, it is an ideal in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})
generated by

e_{\widetilde{\gamma}}^{-(w_{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda^{\prime}),\widetilde{\gamma}^{\vee})+1},\text{ for }w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\gamma})<0\text{ and }e_{\widetilde{\gamma}},\text{ for }w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\gamma})>0.

For our purpose we twist the ideal to the highest weight vector: if
w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\gamma})<0
,

U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})e_{\widetilde{\gamma}}^{-(w_{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda^{\prime}),\widetilde{\gamma}^{\vee})+1}v_{w_{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda^{\prime})}=w_{\mathbf{d}}U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}})f_{-w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\gamma})}^{-(\lambda^{\prime},w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(\widetilde{\gamma})^{\vee})+1}v_{\lambda^{\prime}},

where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}}
is the sum of the root spaces in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
whose weight
\widetilde{\beta}
satisfies
w_{\mathbf{d}}(\widetilde{\beta})>0
. By Proposition
4.6
, the positive roots appearing as weights in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}}
are those in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}\setminus\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
, hence by Corollary
4.7
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
.
Notice that we have shown in Section
4.4.1
that
-\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}=w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}\cap(-\widetilde{\Phi}^{+})
. Therefore the defining ideal of
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{-}(\lambda^{\prime})
in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
is, up to intersecting with
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
, generated by:

(4.3)

U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}})\langle f_{\widetilde{\beta}}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee})+1}\mid\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\rangle.

Since
v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
is a highest weight vector, the action of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}}
on
f_{\widetilde{\beta}}^{k}
is roughly given by derivation. The roots appearing in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}^{\mathbf{c}}
are those not in
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
, by Corollary
4.7
, for
\widetilde{\alpha}\notin\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
and
\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}
, if
\widetilde{\beta}-\widetilde{\alpha}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
, then it is contained in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}
. It follows then if
[e_{\widetilde{\alpha}},f_{\widetilde{\beta}}^{(\lambda^{\prime},\widetilde{\beta}^{\vee})+1}]
is contained in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})
, then it is contained in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
. In general with the same argument, one shows that for any
f\in U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
, if
[e_{\widetilde{\alpha}},f]
is contained in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-})
, then it is contained in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{-}^{\mathbf{c}})
.

To complete the proof of the proposition, it remains to show that there exists a subset
W
of the generating set (
4.3
), which generates the defining ideal of
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{-}(\lambda^{\prime})
, such that for any
f\in W
, there exists an element

To construct

4.4.6.
Proof of Corollary
3.4

We keep writing
\lambda^{\prime}:=\Psi(\lambda)
for short. The isomorphism
\varphi_{\lambda}
induces an isomorphism of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime}))\cong\mathbb{P}(V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda))
.

Consider an open part
\widetilde{X}^{\circ}:=\widetilde{B}\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}
of the Schubert variety
X_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})
. We show that it is isomorphic to
N_{-}^{\mathbf{d}}\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
. The corollary then follows by taking closure on both sides.

If a positive root
\widetilde{\gamma}\notin\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}}^{+}
,
e_{\widetilde{\gamma}}\cdot v_{w_{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda^{\prime})}=0
, hence

\widetilde{B}\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}=\prod_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}}^{+}}U_{\widetilde{\gamma}}\cdot v_{w_{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda^{\prime})}=w_{\mathbf{d}}\prod_{\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{+}}U_{-\widetilde{\beta}}\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}},

where for the last equality we used the identity
w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}}^{+}=-\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{+}
. Since
\varphi_{\lambda}
is a
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module isomorphism, keeping in mind the
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}^{\mathbf{d}})
-module structure on
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\lambda^{\prime})
, we obtain an isomorphism

w_{\mathbf{d}}\prod_{\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}^{+}}U_{-\widetilde{\beta}}\cdot v_{\lambda^{\prime}}\cong\prod_{\beta\in\Phi^{+}}U_{-\beta}\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}=N^{\mathbf{d}}_{-}\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}.

The proof of the corollary is then complete.

5.
Proof of Lemma
4.15

5.1.
Type
\tt A

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
\tt A_{n}
, and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is of type
\tt A_{n+t}
. Without loss of generality (using the fact that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}\in S_{\widetilde{\beta}}\implies\supp\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}\subset\supp\widetilde{\beta}
and reducing
n
if necessary) we may assume
f_{\widetilde{\beta}}=f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}
. This yields

\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}(f^{k}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}})=f^{k}_{\alpha_{1,n}}.

The condition
[e_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}},\widetilde{f}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}]\neq 0
then implies that each
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}
is either of the form
\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}
or
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t}
for some
j\in\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{t}\}
defining the Dynkin abelianisation. We may then rearrange these
e_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}}
so that
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}\preceq\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}\preceq\cdots\preceq\widetilde{\gamma}_{m}
according to the total order

\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,1}\prec\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,2}\prec\ldots\prec\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}\prec\widetilde{\alpha}_{2,n+t}\prec\widetilde{\alpha}_{3,n+t}\prec\ldots\prec\widetilde{\alpha}_{n+t,n+t}.

Therefore, we can assume that the product of
e_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}}
in the statement has the form

(5.1)

\left(\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{p_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}}\right)\left(\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{q_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t}}\right)f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}^{k},

where
p_{j},q_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
and the products are executed with respect to the above total order.

In view of
\pi(\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t})=\alpha_{j+1,n}
, the claim is then that

\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}\left(\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{p_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}}\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{q_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t}}f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}^{k}\right)\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}=\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{p_{j}}_{\alpha_{1,j}}\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{q_{j}}_{\alpha_{j+1}}f_{\alpha_{1,n}}^{k}\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}.

Again, since the monomials on both sides are acted on the highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
, the
e_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
will be treated as a differential operator on
f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}^{k}
, and from now on we will write
e_{\widetilde{\gamma}}\cdot f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}^{k}
to emphasise this action without mentioning the action on
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

From the choice of the Chevalley basis one has

e_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t}}\cdot f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,l}}=\begin{cases}f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)}},&\text{if }l=n+t;\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Thus if we act only by the second factor in the product in (
5.1
) consisting of
e_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t}}
, we obtain

\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{q_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j)+1,n+t}}\cdot f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}^{k}=\left(\prod^{\downarrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}f^{q_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)}}\right)f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,n+t}}^{r}=:\widetilde{f}

with
r:=k-(q_{c_{1}}+\ldots+q_{c_{t}})
. Note that in the product
\widetilde{f}
, we have ordered the terms so that
f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)}}
appears before
f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(l)}}
if
j>l
. We are able to do this because all these root vectors commute in
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
(we may also use a PBW argument to enforce this order, at the cost of splitting the monomial into multiple terms).

With the same argument, we also have

\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{q_{j}}_{\alpha_{j+1,n}}\cdot f_{\alpha_{1,n}}^{k}=\left(\prod^{\downarrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}f^{q_{j}}_{\alpha_{1,j}}\right)f_{\alpha_{1,n}}^{r}=:f\in U(\mathfrak{n}_{-}).

Hence, all that remains to show is that

\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}\left(\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e^{p_{j}}_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}}\widetilde{f}\right)\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}=\left(\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e_{\alpha_{1,j}}^{p_{j}}\right)f\cdot v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}.

We note that on the left side, since we have conveniently ordered the factors in
\widetilde{f}
, the only non-trivial actions of
e_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}}
are when it acts on
f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(l)}}
for
l>j
, in which case
[e_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}},f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(l)}}]=f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\sigma(j+1),\sigma(l)}}
.

Note that since the differential operators are of form
e_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(j)+1}}
and root vectors in
\widetilde{f}
are of form
f_{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\sigma(l)}}
, after action there will be no non-zero element in
\mathfrak{h}
coming out. However this will not be the case if we look at the right hand side.

On the right side, the non-trivial actions of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
are when it acts on
f_{\alpha_{1,l}}
for
l\geq j
, in which case

[e_{\alpha_{1,j}},f_{\alpha_{1,l}}]=\begin{cases}f_{\alpha_{j+1,l}},&\text{if }l>j;\\
h_{\alpha_{1,j}},&\text{if }l=j.\end{cases}

The similar argument as we deal with the second factor can be applied to show that if we gather all monomials in

\left(\prod^{\uparrow}_{j\in\mathbf{c}}e_{\alpha_{1,j}}^{p_{j}}\right)\cdot f

which is in
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
(this is well-defined by PBW theorem), they correspond via
\sigma_{\mathbf{d}}^{-1}
precisely to the terms on the left.

The final piece of the argument is then to show that: (1). all above-mentioned monomials in
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
have the same degree; (2). the monomials containing some
h_{\alpha_{1,j}}
on the right (corresponding to
l=j
) have lower degree than the degree of the monomials in
U(\mathfrak{n}_{-})
, and hence vanish when they act on
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
.

For the first point, we start by looking at the action of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
on
f
: it acts as a derivation and sends
f_{\alpha_{1,l}}
for
l>j
to
f_{\alpha_{j+1,l}}
. If
f
is a power of a single
f_{\alpha_{1,l}}
then all monomials obtained from the action of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
on
f
have the same
\mathbf{d}
-degree. Otherwise let
1\leq l<l^{\prime}\leq n
be such that
f_{\alpha_{1,l}}
and
f_{\alpha_{1,l^{\prime}}}
appears in
f
with positive exponents. The action of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
on
f_{\alpha_{1,l}}
decreases the degree by
d_{1,l}-d_{j+1,l}
; and the action of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
on
f_{\alpha_{1,l^{\prime}}}
decreases the degree by
d_{1,l^{\prime}}-d_{j+1,l^{\prime}}
. From the defining (DO) equality
d_{1,l}+d_{j+1,l^{\prime}}=d_{1,l^{\prime}}+d_{j+1,l}
of the Dynkin cone, they are the same. Since
l
and
l^{\prime}
are arbitrary, we have proved that the action of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
is homogeneous. Iterating this argument shows terminates the proof of the first point.

For the second point, we make use of the (PA) inequalities for
\mathbf{d}\in\mathrm{relint}(F^{\mathbf{c}})
. Elements in
\mathfrak{h}
acts as scalars on
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
, hence we set degree
0
to them. When
l>j
, the action of
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
on
f_{\alpha_{1,l}}
will decrease the degree by
d_{1,l}-d_{j+1,l}
. However, when
e_{\alpha_{1,j}}
acts on
f_{\alpha_{1,j}}
, the
degree will decrease by
d_{1,j}
. Since
j\in\mathbf{c}
, it follows by the definition of the Dynkin cones that

d_{1,j}>d_{1,l}-d_{j+1,l},

and hence when acted on
v_{\lambda}^{\mathbf{d}}
, the monomials containing certain
h_{\alpha_{1,j}}
can be neglected.

\displaystyle=ke_{\alpha_{j,n}}f^{k-1}_{\alpha_{1,\overline{j}}}f_{1,n}

\displaystyle=kf^{k-1}_{\alpha_{1,\overline{j}}}f_{1,j-1}+k(k-1)f^{k-2}_{\alpha_{1,\overline{j}}}f_{1,n}f_{1,n-2},

where we have once again suppressed the structure constants. A simple calculation of the degrees using (PA) inequalities shows that the second term here has lower degree, so we may safely ignore this term when we take the associated graded module. From the way we chose our order, this is the first
e_{\gamma}
we act by, and the rest of the argument is exactly the same as in type
\tt B
.

6.
Dimension counting of Demazure modules

We keep notations as in the previous sections. In this section we prove Proposition
4.12
. According to Proposition
4.13
, there exists a surjective map

\varphi_{\varpi_{k}}:\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\varpi_{k}))\to V^{\mathbf{d}}(\varpi_{k}).

We will show that for any
1\leq k\leq n
,

(6.1)

\dim\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\Psi(\varpi_{k}))\leq\dim V^{\mathbf{d}}(\varpi_{k}).

Two different methods will be used to establish this inequality, depending on the type of the Lie algebra. For types
\tt A_{n}
and
\tt C_{n}
we use the fact that
w_{\mathbf{d}}
are triangular elements; and for the orthogonal types
\tt B_{n}
and
\tt D_{n}
, we study in detail the Demazure modules for fundamental weights.

6.1.
Triangular elements: type
\tt A_{n}
and
\tt C_{n}

Triangular elements in the Weyl group of the Lie algebra of type
\tt A_{n}
and
\tt C_{n}
are introduced in
[
Fou16
,
BFK23
]
. For such an element
w
, a basis of the Demazure module
V_{w}(\lambda)
is parametrised by the lattice points in a lattice polytope.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
be the Lie algebra of type
\tt A_{n}
. For two positive roots
\alpha_{i,j},\alpha_{k,\ell}
,

(1)

their
join
is defined to be
\alpha_{i,j}\vee\alpha_{k,\ell}:=\alpha_{\min(i,k),\max(j,\ell)}
;

(2)

if the intersection of their supports is non-empty, their
meet
is defined to be

\alpha_{i,j}\wedge\alpha_{k,\ell}:=\alpha_{\max(i,k),\min(j,\ell)}.

Otherwise we say that their meet does not exist.

A subset
A\subset\Phi^{+}
is called
triangular
, if

(1)

for any
\alpha,\beta\in A
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\alpha)\cup\mathrm{supp}(\beta)
is a consecutive subset of
[n]
,
\alpha\vee\beta\in A
holds;

(2)

if the meet
\alpha\wedge\beta
exists, then it is in
A
.

An element
w\in W(\mathfrak{g})
is called
triangular
if
\Phi^{+}_{w}
is a triangular subset.

Lemma 6.1
.

The element
w_{\mathbf{c}}\in W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
is triangular.

Proof.

According to Proposition
4.6
,
\widetilde{\Phi}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}^{+}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
. It suffices to show that the image of
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
is a triangular subset in
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
. This holds because by definition,
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
preserves the join and the meet operations, and
\Phi^{+}
itself is a triangular subset.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
be the Lie algebra of type
\tt C_{n}
. We embed the Weyl group
W(\mathfrak{g})
into
W(\mathfrak{sl}_{2n})
, looked as Coxeter groups, by sending the generator
s_{i}
to
t_{i}t_{2n-i}
where
t_{1},\ldots,t_{2n-1}
is the set of generators of
W(\mathfrak{sl}_{2n})
. An element
w\in W(\mathfrak{g})
is called
triangular
if its image under the above embedding in
W(\mathfrak{sl}_{2n})
is a triangular element.

Corollary 6.2
.

The element
w_{\mathbf{c}}\in W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
is triangular.

Proof.

Note that
w_{\mathbf{c}}\in W(\mathfrak{sp}_{2(n+t)})
. We let
w
be its image in
W(\mathfrak{sl}_{2(n+t)})
under the above embedding. If
\mathbf{c}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}\}
we denote

\mathbf{c}^{\prime}:=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{t},2n-1-c_{t},\ldots,2n-1-c_{1}\}\subseteq[2n-1].

The corollary follows from the fact that
w=w_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}
, where
w_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}}
is the element in
W(\mathfrak{sl}_{2(n+t)})
associated to
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
.
∎

The set
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
of positive roots admits a poset structure by requiring:
\widetilde{\alpha}\succ\widetilde{\beta}
if
\widetilde{\alpha}-\widetilde{\beta}\in\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
. The advantage of having triangular elements is:

Proposition 6.3

(
[
BFK23
, Theorem 19]
)
.

For any
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, a linear basis of
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}(\Psi(\lambda))
is parametrised by the marked chain polytope of the poset
\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}
with the poset structure induced from that of
\widetilde{\Phi}^{+}
and the marking is given by
\Psi(\lambda)
.

Corollary 6.4
.

We have
\dim\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}(\Psi(\lambda))=\dim V(\lambda)=\dim V^{\mathbf{d}}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that the partial order
\succ
on the positive roots is preserved by
\psi_{\mathbf{c}}
. Therefore Proposition
4.5
implies that the marked poset in Proposition
6.3
coincides with the marked poset on
\Phi^{+}
with the marking given by
\lambda
. The corollary is then a consequence of the main results of
[
FFL11
,
FFL11a
,
ABS11
]
, showing that the lattice points of such a marked poset polytope parametrise a basis of
V(\lambda)
.
∎

6.2.
Type
\tt A_{n}

We give another proof of (
6.1
) for type
\tt A_{n}
. The ideas of the proofs of (
6.1
) in the orthogonal (non-spin) cases are similar and will partially rely on the combinatorics in type
\tt A_{n}
.

We fix the standard basis
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{r}
of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. The fundamental representation
V(\varpi_{k})
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
can be realised as the wedge product
\Lambda^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
of dimension
\binom{n+1}{k}
with
\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{k}
as a highest weight vector. Again we will write
\ell_{i}:=\sigma(i)
for short. Note that
\ell_{i}-i
is the number of
c_{k}
in
\mathbf{c}
with
1\leq c_{k}<i
.

Lemma 6.5
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, up to a sign, the extremal weight vector
v_{w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})}
in
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})
is given by:

v_{w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})}=\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}-i}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{n+2+\ell_{i}-2i}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{n+1+\ell_{i}-i}.

Proof.

We compute
w_{\mathbf{c}}(\{1,2,\ldots,\ell_{i}\})
. First notice that

\#(\{1,2,\ldots,\ell_{i}\}\cap\mathbf{c})=\ell_{i}-i.

Therefore from Lemma
4.10
(2), the first statement,
1,\ldots,\ell_{i}-i
are in the image. The remaining
s
elements are determined by Lemma
4.10
(1) and (2), the second statement: they are
n+2,\ldots,n+1+\ell_{i}-i
and
n+1,n,\ldots,n+2+\ell_{i}-2i
.
∎

To establish (
6.1
), notice that the Demazure module

\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})\cdot v_{w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})}.

The action of
U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})
sends some
\mathbf{e}_{p}
to some
\mathbf{e}_{q}
with
1\leq q<p
, hence sends a pure wedge product to a pure wedge product. Therefore

\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})\subseteq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}-i}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{p_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{p_{i}}\mid\ell_{i}-i+1\leq p_{1}<\ldots<p_{i}\leq n+1+\ell_{i}-i\}.

The dimension of the vector space on the right hand side is
\binom{n}{k}
since the freedoms are the choices of a subset
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{i}\}
from the set of
n
elements
\{\ell_{i}-i+1,\ldots,n+\ell_{i}-i+1\}
. This is exactly (
6.1
).

6.3.
Type
\tt B_{n}
: non-spin representations

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}
of type
\tt B_{n}
, the non-spin fundamental representations are exterior powers of the vector representation: for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
V(\varpi_{k})=\Lambda^{k}V(\varpi_{1})
and
V(\varpi_{1})\cong\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}
is the vector representation. We fix a basis
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{n},\mathbf{e}_{0},\mathbf{e}_{-n},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{-1}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}
where
\mathbf{e}_{-k}:=\mathbf{e}_{2n+1-k}
and
\mathbf{e}_{0}:=\mathbf{e}_{2n+1}
. It carries the following action of
e_{i,j}:=e_{\alpha_{i,j}}
and
e_{i,\overline{j}}:=e_{\alpha_{i,\overline{j}}}
: up to a non-zero scalar,

(1)

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
,
e_{i,j}
sends
\mathbf{e}_{j+1}
to
\mathbf{e}_{i}
and sends
\mathbf{e}_{-i}
to
\mathbf{e}_{-(j+1)}
;

(2)

for
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
,
e_{i,\overline{j}}
sends
\mathbf{e}_{-j}
to
\mathbf{e}_{i}
and sends
\mathbf{e}_{-i}
to
\mathbf{e}_{j}
;

(3)

for
1\leq i\leq n
,
e_{i,n}
sends
\mathbf{e}_{0}
to
\mathbf{e}_{i}
and
\mathbf{e}_{-i}
to
\mathbf{e}_{0}
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, the weight of
\mathbf{e}_{i}
(resp.
\mathbf{e}_{-i}
) is
\varepsilon_{i}
(resp.
-\varepsilon_{i}
), and the weight of
\mathbf{e}_{0}
is
0
.

We will use the following total order on the indices:

1<2<\ldots<n<0<-n<-(n-1)<\ldots<-1.

It follows from the description above that the action of
e_{i,j}
,
e_{i,\overline{j}}
and
e_{i,n}
decrease the index with respect to this total order when acting on some basis element
\mathbf{e}_{i}
.

Up to a non-zero scalar, the Weyl group acts on the chosen basis of
V(\varpi_{1})
in the same way as it acts on their weights.

Lemma 6.6
.

For
1\leq i\leq n-1
, up to a non-zero scalar, the extremal weight vector
v_{w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})}
in
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{c}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})
is given by:

v_{w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})}=\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}-i}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{-(2t+i+1-\ell_{i})}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{-(2t+2i-\ell_{i})}.

Proof.

According to (
4.2
), the Weyl group element
w_{\mathbf{c}}
has the following form:

w_{\mathbf{c}}=s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})v_{t}\cdots v_{1}.

The same proof as in Lemma
6.5
shows that

v_{t}\cdots v_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}})=\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}-i}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{n+1+\ell_{i}-2i}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{n+\ell_{i}-i}.

Since
s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})
sends
\varepsilon_{t+1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n+t}
to
-\varepsilon_{n+t},\ldots,-\varepsilon_{t+1}
, it follows from
\ell_{i}-i<t+1
that it sends
n+1+\ell_{i}-2i
,
\ldots
,
n+\ell_{i}-i
to
-(2t+i+1-\ell_{i})
,
\ldots
,
-(2t+2i-\ell_{i})
. The proof is then complete.
∎

For the Demazure module
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})\cdot v_{w_{\mathbf{c}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})}
, it follows from the definition of the action that
\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})
is contained in

\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\mathbf{e}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}-i}\wedge\mathbf{e}_{p_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{e}_{p_{i}}\mid\ell_{i}-i+1\leq p_{1}<\ldots<p_{i}\leq-(2t+2i-\ell_{i})\}.

There are
2n+1
elements between
\ell_{i}-i+1
and
-(2t+2i-\ell_{i})
in the above total order, and hence

\dim\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\varpi_{\ell_{i}})\leq\binom{2n+1}{i}=\dim V(\varpi_{i})=\dim V^{\mathbf{d}}(\varpi_{i}).

6.4.
Type
\tt D_{n}
: non-spin representations

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
of type
\tt D_{n}
, the non-spin fundamental representations are exterior powers of the vector representation: for
1\leq k\leq n-2
,
V(\varpi_{k})=\Lambda^{k}V(\varpi_{1})
and
V(\varpi_{1})\cong\mathbb{C}^{2n}
is the vector representation. We fix a basis
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{n},\mathbf{e}_{-n},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{-1}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
where
\mathbf{e}_{-k}:=\mathbf{e}_{2n-k}
. The total order on the indices of the basis is taken to be

1<2<\ldots<n<-n<-(n-1)<\ldots<-1.

The proof in type
\tt D_{n}
is the same as in type
\tt B_{n}
. Instead of writing down the full proof, we only point out why it is so. First, for the extremal weight vector, Lemma
6.6
holds without change. In Section
4.3.3
, the action of the Weyl group on
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n+t}
is almost the same as in the
\tt B_{n}
case. The only difference is when
n
is odd,
\varepsilon_{t+1}
is sent to
\varepsilon_{n+t}
but
-\varepsilon_{n+t}
. However in our case, the non-spin fundamental representation
V(\varpi_{i})
satisfies
1\leq i\leq n-2
, hence after having been acted by
v_{t}\cdots v_{1}
,
t+1
does not appear in the set
\{n+1+\ell_{i}-2i,\ldots,n+\ell_{i}-i\}
. Indeed, notice that for fixed
i
, the smallest element in the set is
n+1+\ell_{i}-2i
, and this number decreases with respect to
i
, hence for any
i
, the minimal number which may appear in the above set is
n+1+\ell_{n-2}-2(n-2)
. Since
\mathbf{c}\subseteq[n-3]
,
\ell_{n-2}=n-2+t
, hence

n+1+\ell_{n-2}-2(n-2)=n+1+n-2+t-2n+4=t+3>t+1.

Once this extremal weight vector is described, it suffices to notice that the action of a root vector in
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+}
will decrease the index of
\mathbf{e}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq-1
with respect to the above total order. Therefore the same formula on the inclusion of the Demazure module in the vector space generated by pure wedge products still holds without any change. The difference is that to count the dimension of the latter space, since there is no
\mathbf{e}_{0}
, which sits between
n
and
-n
in the
\tt B_{n}
case, its dimension is
\binom{2n}{i}
, which coincides with the dimension of
V^{\mathbf{d}}(\varpi_{i})
.

6.5.
Spin representations

It remains to deal with the spin representations: for type
\tt B_{n}
it is
V(\varpi_{n})
and for type
\tt D_{n}
, they are
V(\varpi_{n-1})
and
V(\varpi_{n})
. The spin representations are minuscule, and we will adapt the idea in
[
CILL16
]
to deal with the spin cases. Since in type
\tt D_{n}
it is assumed that
\mathbf{c}\subseteq[n-3]
, it follows that
\sigma(n-1)=n+t-1
and
\sigma(n)=n+t
.

We will prove that
\dim\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\varpi_{n+t})=\dim V(\varpi_{n})
for type
\tt B_{n}
. The proof for type
\tt D_{n}
is the same.

We start from the formula (
4.2
) of the reduced decomposition of
w_{\mathbf{c}}
(the reducedness holds by Corollary
4.11
)

w_{\mathbf{c}}=s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t})v_{t}\cdots v_{1}.

Let
\mathfrak{l}_{n}
be the semi-simple part of the Levi subalgebra of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
corresponding to the simple roots
\widetilde{\alpha}_{t+1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{n+t}
. It carries naturally an induced triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{l}_{n}=\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{0}\oplus\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{-}
from
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
where
\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{\pm}=\mathfrak{l}_{n}\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{\pm}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{0}=\mathfrak{l}_{n}\cap\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}_{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}
: such an isomorphism can be chosen to pair the simple root
\widetilde{\alpha}_{t+k}
with
\alpha_{k}
.

It follows that in the stabiliser
W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})_{\varpi_{n+t}}
of
\varpi_{n+t}
in
W(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}})
, the image of
w_{\mathbf{c}}
is given by

\overline{w}_{\mathbf{c}}:=s_{n+t}(s_{n+t-1}s_{n+t})\cdots(s_{t+1}\cdots s_{n+t}).

It is contained in the Weyl group
W(\mathfrak{l}_{n})_{\varpi_{n+t}}
of the Levi subalgebra modulo the stabiliser of
\varpi_{n+t}
, and moreover, it is the longest element therein. Therefore

\widetilde{V}_{w_{\mathbf{d}}}(\widetilde{\varpi}_{n+t})=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})\cdot v_{w_{\mathbf{d}}(\widetilde{\varpi}_{n+t})}=U(\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}_{+})\cdot v_{\overline{w}_{\mathbf{d}}(\widetilde{\varpi}_{n+t})}=U(\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{+})\cdot v_{\overline{w}_{\mathbf{d}}(\widetilde{\varpi}_{n+t})}.

Since
\overline{w}_{\mathbf{d}}
is the longest element,
U(\mathfrak{l}_{n}^{+})\cdot v_{\overline{w}_{\mathbf{d}}(\widetilde{\varpi}_{n+t})}\cong V(\varpi_{n})
. The statement on the dimension then follows.

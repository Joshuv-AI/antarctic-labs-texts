Capped Vertex Functions for Hilb n ​ ( ℂ 2 ) \text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
Jeffrey Ayers, Andrey Smirnov

Abstract
We obtain explicit formulas for the K K -theoretic capped descendent vertex functions of Hilb n ​ ( ℂ 2 ) \text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2}) for descendents given by the exterior algebra of the tautological bundle. This formula provides a one-parametric deformation of the generating function for normalized Macdonald polynomials. In particular, we show that the capped vertex functions are rational functions of the quantum parameter.



1
Introduction

K-theoretic capped vertex functions were defined in
[
10
]
as partition functions of relative quasimaps to Nakajima varieties.
In this paper we consider capped vertex functions for the variety
X=\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
given by Hilbert scheme of
n
-points in the plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. We consider vertex functions with a special type of descendents given by the exteriors powers of the tautological bundle. In this case we obtain an explicit combinatorial formula for the capped vertex function.

It was conjectured and proved in several special cases
[
14
,
13
,
16
]
that the partition functions corresponding to the capped vertex functions with descendents are Taylor series expansions of rational functions. The explicit formula we obtain in this paper confirms this conjecture for this special choice of descendents. In this section we overview the main definitions and results.

1.1
Content of the Paper

In
[
10
]
, Okounkov introduced the capped vertex function with descendents as the following partition function:

Definition 1.1
.

(Section 7 of
[
10
]
)
The capped vertex with descendent
\tau
for a Nakajima quiver variety
X
is the following generating function:

\displaystyle\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau)}(z):=\sum_{d}{\textsf{{ev}}}_{p_{2},*}\left(\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{2}},\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{d}_{vir}\otimes\textsf{{ev}}^{*}_{p_{1}}(\tau)\right)z^{d}\in K_{G}\left(X\right)[[z]]

(1.1)

Here
\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{2}}
denotes the moduli space of quasimaps from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to a Nakajima variety
X
relative to
p_{2}=\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. By
\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{d}_{vir}\in K_{G}(\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{2}})
we denote the symmetrized virtual structure sheaf of the moduli space
\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{2}}
. This moduli space is equipped with a proper map
{\textsf{{ev}}}_{p_{2}}:\,\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{2}}\to X
and
{\textsf{{ev}}}_{p_{2},*}
denotes the corresponding push-forward of the
G
-equivariant K-theory groups, where
G
is a certain symmetry group acting on both the moduli space and
X
. For a point
p_{1}=0\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
we also have a map
{\textsf{{ev}}}_{p_{1}}:\,\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{1}}\to[X]
where
[X]
is the quotient stack corresponding to Nakajima variety
X
(we recall that
X
is a GIT quotient, while
[X]
is a categorical quotient). Using the pull-back map
\textsf{{ev}}^{*}_{p_{1}}
for any class
\tau\in K_{G}([X])
we can construct a K-theory class
\textsf{{ev}}^{*}_{p_{1}}(\tau)
on the moduli space. The resulting vertex function is a generating function of the corresponding push-forwards over all degrees
d
. The parameter
z
counting the degrees of quasimaps is usually referred to as the
Kähler parameter
.

In this paper we study the capped vertex function for
X=\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
with descendents

\displaystyle\tau=\Lambda^{\bullet}_{u}(\mathcal{V})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{V})u^{k}

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{V}
is the tautological bundle on the Hilbert scheme.

Let
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
be a two-dimensional torus acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
by
(x,y)\to(t_{1}x,t_{2}y)

T
on
\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and the corresponding
T
-equivariant K-theory groups.
Recall that the set of
T
-fixed points is labeled by partitions of
n
:

\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{T}=\{\lambda:|\lambda|=n\}.

The classes of torus fixed points
[\lambda]
form a basis of equivariant K-theory
K_{T}\left(\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\right)
.

It is well-known that the equivariant cohomology and K-theory of the Hilbert schemes are equipped with an action of the Heisenberg algebra, see Section 8 of
[
9
]
or
[
1
]
for a construction.
Using this action

-equivariant

-theories of
\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
can be identified with an infinite dimensional polynomial space, called the Fock space. The classes of the
T
-fixed points are identified with the Macdonald polynomial normalized as in the work of Haiman
[
2
]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
2
]
)
There is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}K_{T}\left(\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\right)_{loc}=\textsf{{Fock}}:=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},...]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},t_{2})

(1.3)

where the grading on the left side is by
n
and on the right is by
\textrm{deg}(p_{k})=k
. Under this isomorphism the K-theory classes of the torus fixed points are identified with the Macdonald polynomials (in Haiman’s normalization):

[\lambda]\longrightarrow H_{\lambda}

The main result of this paper is the explicit formula of the following generating function

\displaystyle F(z,y)=\sum_{n}\widehat{\textup{V}}_{\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{(\tau)}(z)y^{n}\in\textsf{{Fock}}[[z,y]]

(1.4)

where
\widehat{\textup{V}}_{\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{(\tau)}(z)
is the capped vertex function for the Hilbert scheme
{\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}
with descendent (
1.2
). In the notations of the Fock space (
1.3
) we obtain:

Theorem 1.3
.

(Theorem
5.3
)
The generating function (
1.4
) is a Taylor series expansion of the following function:

\displaystyle F(z,y)=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{y^{k}}{k(1-t_{1}^{2k})(1-t_{2}^{2k})}((1-u^{k})\hbar^{2k}p_{k}+z^{k}\hbar^{2k}q^{-k}\dfrac{\hbar^{k}-\hbar^{-k}}{1-(z\hbar/q)^{k}}p_{k})\right)

(1.5)

where
\hbar=t_{1}t_{2}
, and
q
denoted the equivariant parameters of torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
acting on the source of the quasimaps.

When
z=0
and

this formula describes the generating function of the classes of structure sheaves
{\mathcal{O}}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}\in K_{T}(\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))
, which can be expressed as a sum of Macdonald polynomials

in the Fock space. Our formula, therefore, may be understood as a

\sum_{\lambda}\frac{H_{\lambda}}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(T_{\lambda}\textup{Hilb}^{|\lambda|}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))}y^{|\lambda|}=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{y^{k}\hbar^{2k}p_{k}}{k(1-t_{1}^{2k})(1-t_{2}^{2k})}\right)

Notice that the

) are a manifestly rational functions of
z
, and thus we get the following corollary

Corollary 1.5
.

(Corollary
5.5
)
The capped vertex function
\widehat{\textup{V}}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{(\tau)}(z)
is a Taylor series expansion of a rational function in

:

\widehat{\textup{V}}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{(\tau)}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}(t_{1},t_{2},z,q,u)

We remark also that our main result (
1.5

\displaystyle F(z,y)=\mathsf{S}^{\bullet}\left(\dfrac{y\hbar^{2}p_{1}}{(1-t_{1}^{2})(1-t_{2}^{2})}((1-u)+zq^{-1}\dfrac{\hbar-\hbar^{-1}}{1-(z\hbar/q)})\right)

(1.6)

where
\mathsf{S}^{\bullet}
denotes the
symmetric algebra or the
plethystic exponential
of the argument, for instance see Section 2.1 of
[
10
]
for notations and definitions. We note also that structurally, our formula for descendents look similar to explicit formulas for two-legs capped vertex function obtained in
[
3
]
which also have a convenient presentation as plethystic exponential of expression linear in
p_{1}
’s, see Theorem 1 in
[

) implies that the collection
\widehat{\textup{V}}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{(\tau)}(z)

labeled by
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with descendents (
1.2

factorizable
see Section 5.3.1 of
[
10
]
for definition. It would be interesting to find a direct geometric argument explaining this property.

1.2
Idea of the proof

Let us outline the main ideas used in the proof of (
1.5
). It is well known that the Hilbert scheme

\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
is a special case of a more general family of smooth symplectic varieties
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
labeled by
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, known as instanton moduli spaces. When the rank of the instantons is
r=1
, we obtain the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{M}(n,1)=\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. The moduli space
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
is an example of a Nakajima quiver variety and therefore we may study the capped vertex functions associated to
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
.
Central to us is the result of Okounkov, see Theorem 7.5.23 in
[
10
]
, on large framing vanishing. This theorem says that when
r
is sufficiently large these capped vertex functions are classical, i.e., all terms in the sum (
1.1
) with
d>0
vanish. For the descendents we study in this paper (
1.2
) this happens when
r\geqslant 2
. This means that for
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
with
r\geqslant 2
we know the corresponding capped vertex explicitly:

\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau)}_{\mathcal{M}(n,r)}(z)=\tau\in K_{G}(\mathcal{M}(n,r))

and the right side is independent of
z
.

The moduli space of rank
r=2
instantons is equipped with an action of the torus
\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{a}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that the corresponding set of fixed points has the form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(n,2)^{\mathbb{C}_{a}^{\times}}=\coprod_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\textup{Hilb}^{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times\textup{Hilb}^{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

(1.7)

Our idea is to extract the information about the capped vertex function for the Hilbert scheme from the limit of
a\to 0
of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}_{a}
-equivariant capped vertex function of
\mathcal{M}(n,2)
, where
a
denotes the equivariant parameter associated with the torus
\mathbb{C}_{a}^{*}
.

The main technical tool for computing this limit is the following result:

Theorem 1.6
.

(Section 7.4 of
[
10
]
) The capping operator factors into a product

\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau)}(z)=\Psi(z)\textup{V}^{(\tau)}(z)

where
\textup{V}^{(\tau)}(z)
is the bare vertex function with descendent
\tau
and
\Psi(z)
is the capping operator.

The bare vertex function
\textup{V}^{(\tau)}(z)
is defined similarly to (
1.1
), with the moduli space
\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{rel\,p_{2}}
replaced by the moduli space of quasimaps
\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{ns\,p_{2}}
non-singular at
p_{2}
. The bare vertex functions are much simpler to compute using equivariant localization in K-theory. Using this explicit description of the bare vertex function we obtain the following result.

Proposition 1.7
.

(Proposition
4.8
)
We have the following limit of vertex functions:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\,{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\textup{V}^{\tau,(2)}(z)={\mathcal{O}}(-1)\textup{V}^{\tau,(1)}(z(-\hbar q)^{-1})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\textup{V}^{\tau,(1)}(z(-\hbar q))

(1.8)

Here and throughout the paper we use superscript
(r)
to denote functions corresponding to
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
. For example,
V^{\tau,(2)}(z)
in the left side of (
1.8
)
denotes the
\mathbb{C}^{*}_{a}
-equivariant bare vertex function of for
\mathcal{M}(n,2)
.
The right side of (
1.8
) corresponds to the product of the vertex functions for the Hilbert schemes
\mathcal{M}(n_{i},1)=\textup{Hilb}^{n_{i}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
given by the components of components the
\mathbb{C}^{*}_{a}
-fixed set (
1.7
). In words, this proposition says that in the limit
a\to 0
, the bare vertex function of
\mathcal{M}(n,2)
factors, up to some shifts of the Kähler parameter
z
, to the product of the vertex functions for the Hilbert schemes. The appearance of the
{\mathcal{O}}(-1)
, which denotes the operator of multiplication by the line bundle

{\mathcal{O}}(-1)
in the equivariant K-theory, in the above formula is a result of a certain normalization we wish to have in the basis of fixed points. The vertex functions as defined in
[
10
]
normalized to that they have constant term of
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
. For our purposes we wish for this power series to instead begin with the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}
. The minor change in normalization is needed to ensure that the limits in the above proposition exist.

A similar factorization exists for the capping operator
\Psi(z)
. It is known that
\Psi(z)
is a fundamental solution matrix of the quantum difference equation associated with the corresponding Nakajima variety
[
12
]
. For the instanton moduli
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
this equation is the quantum dynamical equation for the quantum toroidal algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}({\ddot{\mathfrak{gl}_{1}}})
. Using this algebraic description, following
[
16
]
we obtain

Proposition 1.8
.

(Proposition
4.6
)
The normalized capping operator
\widetilde{\Psi}^{(2)}(z)
for the instanton moduli space
\mathcal{M}(n,2)
has the following limit:

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\,\widetilde{\Psi}^{(2)}(z)=Y(z)\,\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(z\hbar^{-1})\otimes\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(z\hbar)

(1.9)

where
\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(z\hbar^{-1})\otimes\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(z\hbar)
in the right side denotes the tensor product of capping operators for the Hilbert schemes corresponding to the
\mathbb{C}^{*}_{a}
- fixed components (
1.7
).

The operator
Y(z)
in this proposition is the fusion operator for the toroidal algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}({\ddot{\mathfrak{gl}_{1}}})
. In the proof of Proposition
4.6
we give an explicit description for its action on the Fock spaces:

\displaystyle Y(z)=\exp{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{n_{k}K^{-k}\otimes K^{k}}{1-z^{-k}K^{-k}\otimes K^{k}}\alpha^{0}_{-k}\otimes\alpha^{0}_{k}\right)}

(1.10)

where
\alpha^{0}_{k}
denote the generators of the horizontal Heisenberg subalgebra of
\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}({\ddot{\mathfrak{gl}_{1}}})
. These generators act on the Fock spaces in a simple way (
3.6
).

Using the factorization results summarized in the two previous propositions, in the limit
a\to 0
of the Theorem
1.6
we arrive at the following functional relation for the capped vertex functions of the Hilbert scheme:

\displaystyle\tau\otimes 1=Y(z)\left(\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau),(1)}(z)\otimes\dots\right)

(1.11)

where the left side does not have quantum corrections and therefore is known explicitly.

Finally, since the operator
Y(z)
given by (
1.10
) is invertible and acts on the Fock spaces explicitly, we can solve the linear equation (
1.11
) for the first tensor component
\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau),(1)}(z)
which gives our main result.

Background

3.1
Hilbert Schemes

The Hilbert Scheme of
n
-points on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the scheme parameterizing codimension
n
-ideals of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
:

\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})=\{\mathcal{J}\subset\mathbb{C}[x,y]:\dim(\mathbb{C}[x,y]/\mathcal{J})=n\}

Let
T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{2}
be the 2-dimensional algebraic torus acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. This action scales coordinates:

(x,y)\mapsto(t_{1}x,t_{2}y)

And this extends to an action on
\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, which scales the symplectic form
\omega\in H^{2}(\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2}),\mathbb{C})
with character
\hbar=t_{1}t_{2}
. Let
A=\ker(\hbar)\subset T
be the subtorus that fixes
\omega
.

The fixed point set of
\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{T}
is a finite set, labeled by partitions of length
n
. For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{k})
, with each
\lambda_{i}
weakly decreasing,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda|=n
, the corresponding fixed ideal is of the following form:

\mathcal{J}^{T}=\{y^{\lambda_{1}},xy^{\lambda_{2}},...,x^{k}\}

3.2
Instantons

\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
appears as the rank 1 case of a more general moduli space: the moduli space of instantons of rank
r
. We describe this moduli space as the moduli of framed torsion free sheaves of rank
r
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a framed torsion free sheaf of rank
r
over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with fixed second chern class
c_{2}(\mathcal{F})=n
. A framing of a sheaf is a choice of isomorphism

\varphi:\left.\mathcal{F}\right|_{L_{\infty}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L_{\infty}}^{\oplus r}

Where
L_{\infty}
is the line at infinity in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The moduli space of these sheaves, denoted
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
is known to arise as the Nakajima Quiver Variety of the framed double Jordan quiver. We quickly review the construction of this variety:

Let
Q
be the framed quiver with one vertex and one edge. Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{n},W=\mathbb{C}^{r}
. The representation space of

is

Rep(Q)=\text{Hom}(W,V)\oplus\text{Hom}(V,V)

This space has a natural
GL(V)
action by conjugating the endomorphisms, and multiplying the vertical maps:
X\in\text{Hom}(V,V)
,
I\in\text{Hom}(W,V)

X\mapsto gXg^{-1},\ \ I\mapsto gI

This action induces a Hamiltonian action on

T^{*}Rep(Q)=Rep(Q)\oplus Rep(Q)^{*}

with a moment map

\mu:T^{*}Rep(Q)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}

where
\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}(GL(V))
. With the choice of
GL(V)
character
\theta:g\to\det(g)
for the GIT quotient we have:

\mathcal{M}(n,r)=\mu^{-1}(0)//_{\theta}GL(V)

\mathcal{M}(n,1)=\text{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.
There is a natural torus action
A\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}

\mathbb{C}^{r}
. The
\text{i}^{th}

\mathbb{C}^{2}

\mathcal{M}(n,r)^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\coprod_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\mathcal{M}(n_{1},r_{1})\times\mathcal{M}(n_{2},r_{2}).

Acting by

\mathcal{M}(n,r)^{T}=\{(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{r}):\sum|\lambda_{i}|=n\}

The classes of fixed points

.

3.3
Quasimaps and Vertex Functions

Our main reference for this section is the notes
[
10
]
.

Let
\textsf{QM}^{d}
denote the moduli space of degree
d
quasimaps from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
. Let
\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}
be the standard torus acting on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by scaling homogeneous coordinates with weight
q
:

[x_{1}:x_{2}]\mapsto[qx_{1}:x_{2}]

There are two fixed points of this action:
p_{1}=0,p_{2}=\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, meaning that the only two nonsingular or regular points of the
\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}
-fixed quasimap may occur at these fixed points. Let
G=T\times\mathbb{C}_{q}^{\times}
be the group acting on
\textsf{QM}^{d}
. There is a subset of the moduli space we will be primarily interested: the open subset of quasimaps of degree
d
nonsingular at
p=0
or
p=\infty
. Denote this open subset
\textsf{QM}^{d}_{ns\,p}\subset\textsf{QM}^{d}
.

The fixed point set
\left(\textsf{QM}^{d}_{ns\,p}\right)^{G}
consists of pairs
(\lambda,d_{\lambda})
, where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{r})
is a tuple of partitions such that
|\lambda|=n
corresponding to a fixed point in
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
. The second element in the tuple is the degree
d_{\lambda}
, which assigns a nonnegative integer
d_{\square}
to each box
\square\in\lambda
in such a way that the data is organized into
r
-plane partitions
[
4
]
. These integers corresponding to the degree data must add up to
d
:
|d_{\lambda}|=\sum_{\square\in\lambda}d_{\square}=d

There is an evaluation map from this open subset to
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
mapping a quasimap
f\in\textsf{QM}^{d}_{ns\,p}
to
f(p)\in\mathcal{M}(n,r)
. In general as
\textsf{QM}^{d}_{ns\,p}
is not proper over
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
there is no pushforward of this map to K-theory. We can avoid this issue by using localized K-theory instead.

Definition 3.1
.

(Section 7 of
[
10
]
)
The bare vertex of
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
is the following generating function:

\textup{V}(z):=\sum_{d}\textsf{{ev}}_{p_{2},*}\left(\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{ns\,p_{2}},\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{d}_{vir}\right)z^{d}\in K_{G}\left(\mathcal{M}(n,r)\right)_{loc}[[z]]

where
\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{d}_{vir}
is the symmetrized virtual structure sheaf on
\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{ns\,p_{1}}

For
p_{1}
, we do not have an evaluation map to to
\mathcal{M}(n,r)
itself since we do not assume that
p_{1}
is nonsingular. Rather we have an evaluation map to the quotient stack:
\textsf{ev}_{p_{1}}:\textsf{QM}^{d}_{ns\,p_{2}}\rightarrow\left[\mu^{-1}(0)/\prod GL(V_{i})\right]
. For a class
\tau\in K_{G}(\left[\mu^{-1}(0)/\prod GL(V_{i})\right])
we have the following definition:

Definition 3.2
.

(Section 7 of
[
10
]
)
The bare vertex with descendent
\tau
is the generating function:

\displaystyle\textup{V}^{(\tau)}(z):=\sum_{d}\textsf{{ev}}_{p_{2},*}\left(\textsf{{QM}}^{d}_{ns\,p_{2}},\hat{\mathcal{O}}^{d}_{vir}\otimes\textsf{{ev}}_{p_{1}}^{*}(\tau)\right)z^{d}\in K_{G}\left(\mathcal{M}(n,r)\right)_{loc}[[z]]

(3.2)

Interesting to us, the classes
\tau
can be constructed as follows. We have the following inclusion of quotient stacks:

\displaystyle\left[\mu^{-1}(0)/GL(V)\right]\subset[T^{*}Rep(Q)/GL(V)]

(3.3)

Since
T^{*}Rep(Q)
is a vector space, which is contractible to a point we have

K(\left[T^{*}Rep(Q)/GL(V)\right])\cong K([pt/GL(V)])\cong K_{GL(V)}(pt)=Rep(GL(V))

where the last term is the ring of representations of
GL(V)
, i.e., is a ring of symmetric polynomials in
n
-variables. Thus, we can view such a symmetric polynomial as a K-theory class on the quotient stack
\left[\mu^{-1}(0)/GL(V)\right]
by pulling it back via inclusion (
3.3
). The descendent (

As before let

\displaystyle\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}\mathcal{O}(-1)a^{n}

\displaystyle=\hbar^{n}\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{\lambda}^{-})}{\frac{\hbar^{n_{2}}}{a^{n_{2}}}\left(1\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(1)\right)}\,\dfrac{1}{a^{n_{1}}}\,\left({\mathcal{O}}(-1)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\right)a^{n}

with repelling weights

.
Thus

. Combining all this together in the limit

we arrive at the desired expression

\lim\limits_{a\rightarrow 0}\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}\mathcal{O}(-1)a^{n}=\hbar^{n}{\left({\hbar^{n_{2}}}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\otimes 1\right)}

∎

Proposition 4.6
.

We have the following limit of the rank 2 fundamental solution matrix:

4.5
Limiting linear equation

Let
\widehat{\textup{V}}^{\tau(u)}(z)
denote the capped vertex function with descendents considered as an element of the Fock space, i.e., its degree
n
component is given by
\widehat{\textup{V}}^{\tau_{n}(u)}(z)
.

Proposition 4.9
.

In the tensor square
\textsf{{Fock}}^{\otimes 2}
we have the following identity

\displaystyle\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau(u))}(-z\hbar q)\otimes 1=(J(z)J(0)^{-1})\Big(\bar{\tau}(u)\otimes 1\Big)

(4.19)

where
\bar{\tau}(u)
is the dual descendents (
4.16
)

Proof.

From Theorem
3.4
in for rank
r=2
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(-1)c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})=\Lambda^{\bullet}(T\mathcal{M}(n,2)^{\vee})\Psi(z/q^{2})\Lambda^{\bullet}(T\mathcal{M}(n,2)^{\vee})^{-1}\textup{V}_{O}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)

(4.20)

where
\textup{V}_{O}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)
denotes the non-normalized vertex function whose expansion in the fixed point basis has the form:

\textup{V}_{O}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)={\mathcal{O}}(1)c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})+O(z).

The tangent weights decompose as follows
\Lambda^{\bullet}(T\mathcal{M}(n,2)^{\vee})=\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})\oplus\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})
, where
\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})
are the tangent weights of the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{M}(n,2)^{A}=\coprod\limits_{n_{1}+n_{2}=n}\textup{Hilb}^{n_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{2})\times\textup{Hilb}^{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})
are the weights of its normal bundle inside
\mathcal{M}(n,2)
. Thus:

{\mathcal{O}}(-1)c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})=\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})\Psi^{(2)}(z/q^{2})\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})^{-1}\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)

Or, equivalently

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(-1)c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}\Delta_{(1,1)}\Psi^{(2)}(z/q^{2})

\displaystyle\times\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})^{-1}\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})^{-1}\Delta_{(1,1)}\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)

Multiplying by
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
, and noting that this operator commutes with
\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})
and
\Delta_{(1,1)}
we obtain:

\displaystyle c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})\Delta_{(1,1)}{\mathcal{O}}(1)a^{-2n}\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}\Psi^{(2)}(z)\Lambda^{\bullet}(N_{2})^{-1}\Delta_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle\times\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})^{-1}\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)a^{2n}\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)

where we divided and multiplied factors by
a^{2n}
.
In our notation (
4.11
) this simplifies to

\displaystyle c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})\Delta_{(1,1)}{\mathcal{O}}(1)a^{-2n}\widetilde{\Psi^{(2)}}(z/q^{2})\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})^{-1}\Delta_{(1,1)}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}(-1)a^{2n}\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}(z)

To compute the limit
a\rightarrow 0
, note that the Hilbert Scheme weights
\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})
do not depend on
a
. Next, by Proposition
4.2

\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\,\Delta_{(1,1)}{\mathcal{O}}(1)a^{-2n}=\hbar^{-n}\left\{{\hbar}^{-n_{2}}{\mathcal{O}}(1)\otimes 1\right\}

and by Proposition
4.6
the limit of the modified capping operator was computed as

\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\,\widetilde{\Psi}^{(2)}(z/q^{2})=\left(J(z)J(0)^{-1}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar/q^{2})\otimes\widetilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}/q^{2})

Finally by Proposition
4.8

\lim\limits_{a\to 0}\,\textup{V}^{c_{k}(\mathcal{V}),(2)}(z)=\textup{V}^{c_{k}(\mathcal{V}),(1)}(-z\hbar q)\otimes\textup{V}^{1,(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}/q)

where we have factored out the
{\mathcal{O}}(-1)
and thus our vertex function begins with
1
and has a limit. Combining all these terms together, and using (
4.14
) in the limit
a\to 0
we arrive at

\displaystyle c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})\otimes 1=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})\times(J(0)J(-z)^{-1})^{-1}\times\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar q^{-2})\otimes\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}q^{-2})

\displaystyle\times\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})^{-1}\times\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)\otimes\textup{V}^{1,(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}/q)

In the first tensor component of the last expression we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar q^{-2})\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})}\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)=\mathsf{M}^{(1)}(-z\hbar)\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar)\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})}\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)

where we used QDE (
4.3
). Using (
4.1
) for
r=1
we also write it as:

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{(1)}(-z\hbar)\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar)\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})}\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)=\mathsf{M}^{(1)}(-z\hbar)\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)

where the last equality is (
4.17
).

\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar q^{-2})\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})}\textup{V}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})}\,\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V})),(1)}(-z\hbar q)

Similarly, in the second tensor component by (
4.1
) for
r=1

\tilde{\Psi}^{(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}q^{-2})\frac{1}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(TH^{\vee})}\textup{V}^{1,(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}/q)=\widehat{\textup{V}}^{1,(1)}(-z\hbar^{-1}/q)=1

where we used that the capped vertex for descendents

\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}))}(-z\hbar q)\otimes 1=(J(z)J(0)^{-1})\Big(c_{k}(\mathcal{V}^{*})\otimes 1\Big)

5
Derivation of Descendents formula

We find a formula for capped descendent vertex
\widehat{\textup{V}}^{(\tau(u))}(z)
by solving the identity (
4.19
) for the first tenor component. This can be done as follows.

Let
H_{\lambda}
denote the Macdonald polynomial in Haiman’s normalization. Our parameters are related to
q,t
via the relation
q=1/t_{1}^{2},t=1/t_{2}^{2}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The following identity holds in the Fock space (
1.3
):

\sum_{\lambda}\frac{H_{\lambda}}{\Lambda^{\bullet}(T_{\lambda}\text{Hilb}^{|\lambda|}(\mathbb{C}^{2}))}y^{|\lambda|}=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{y^{k}\hbar^{2k}p_{k}}{k(1-t_{1}^{2k})(1-t_{2}^{2k})}\right)

Proof.

This is the kernel identity for Macdonald scalar product. See
[
7
]
, with
Y=1
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

We have the following generating function given by
4.15
:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\bar{\tau}_{n}(u)y^{n}=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{y^{k}p_{k}}{k(1-t_{1}^{2k})(1-t_{2}^{2k})}\,\hbar^{2k}\Big(1-u^{k}\Big)\right)

(5.1)

Proof.

See corollary 6.4 in
[
7

∎

Rescaling
p_{i}\to p_{i}/a_{i}
and sending

, from this proposition we obtain the following twisted version of the above normalized Macdonald generating series:

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{O}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n}(\mathbb{C}^{2})}\,y^{n}=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{k}y^{k}\hbar^{2k}p_{k}}{k(1-t_{1}^{2k})(1-t_{2}^{2k})}\right)

(5.2)

Thus, in the tensor square of the Fock space
\textsf{{Fock}}^{\otimes 2}
we have

\sum\limits_{n_{1},n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\,(\tau_{n_{1}}(u)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\textup{Hilb}^{n_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{2})})y^{n_{1}+n_{2}}=\exp\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{y^{k}\hbar^{2k}((1-u^{k})p^{(1)}_{k}+(-1)^{k}p^{(2)}_{k})}{k(1-t_{1}^{2k})(1-t_{2}^{2k})}\right)

where the superscripts in
p^{(}i)_{k}
,
i=1,2
denote the components of the first and the second factors in the tensor product
\textsf{{Fock}}^{\otimes 2}
.

From (
4.13
) and
0
-slope Heisenberg algebra action on the Fock space (

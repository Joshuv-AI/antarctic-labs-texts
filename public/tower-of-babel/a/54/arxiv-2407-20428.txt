Bounding regularity of FI m {\mathrm{FI}}^{m} -modules
Wee Liang Gan, Khoa Ta

Abstract
. Let FI {\mathrm{FI}} be a skeleton of the category of finite sets and injective maps, and FI m {\mathrm{FI}}^{m} the product of m m copies of FI {\mathrm{FI}} . We prove that if an FI m {\mathrm{FI}}^{m} -module is generated in degree ⩽ d \leqslant d and related in degree ⩽ r \leqslant r , then its regularity is bounded above by a function of m m , d d , and r r .



Let
{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of nonnegative integers. For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we write
[n]
for the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
; in particular,
[0]
denotes the empty set
\emptyset
.
Let
{\mathrm{FI}}
be the category whose objects are the sets
[n]
for
n=0,1,2,\ldots
and whose morphisms are the injective maps between the objects. Let
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
be the product of
m
copies of the category
{\mathrm{FI}}
.

It is a well-known result of Church and Ellenberg
[
2
]
that if
V
is an
{\mathrm{FI}}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, then the (Castelnuovo-Mumford) regularity of
V
is bounded above by
d+r-1
. For
m>1
, Gan and Li
[
5
]
proved that
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules presented in finite degrees have finite regularity but their proof does not lead to a bound. The main goal of our present article is to prove that if
V
is an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, then its regularity is bounded above by a function of
m
,
d
and
r
.

The proof of our result proceeds by nested induction: the outer induction is over
m
and the inner induction is over
d
. There are two main ingredients in our argument:

•

We define a pair of spectral sequences converging to the homology of a module over a product of two categories. These two spectral sequences exist not only for
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules but in a general setting.

•

We use a generalization to
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules of the long exact sequence of Church
[
3
]
.

Let us mention some related works.
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules were studied by Casto
[
1
]
, Gadish
[
4
]
, Gan and Li
[
5
]
, Li and Ramos
[
7
]
, Li and Yu
[
8
]
, and Zeng
[
12
,
13
]
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we state our main result. In Section
2
, we define certain functors associated to modules over a product of two categories and we construct spectral sequences converging to the homology of these modules. In Section

-modules and we construct a long exact sequence following Church

]
. In Section

1.
Main result

1.1.

Throughout this article, we fix a commutative ring
k
. For any category
{\mathscr{C}}
, a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module is a functor from
{\mathscr{C}}
to the category of
k
-modules. A homomorphism from a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module
U
to a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module
V
is a natural transformation from the functor
U
to the functor
V
.

Let
{\mathscr{C}}
be a small category. We write
\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
for the set of objects of
{\mathscr{C}}
. For any
X,Y\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
, we write
{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)
for the set of morphisms in
{\mathscr{C}}
from
X
to
Y
. Let
V
be a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module. For any
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
, we write
V_{X}
for
V(X)
. For any
f\in{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)
, we write
f_{*}
for the map
V(f):V_{X}\to V_{Y}
.

Denote by
{\mathscr{C}}\Mod
the category of
{\mathscr{C}}
-modules. Recall that
{\mathscr{C}}\Mod
is an abelian category. For each
W\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
, we define a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module
M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W)
as follows:

•

for each
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
, let
M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W)_{X}
be the free
k
-module with basis
{\mathscr{C}}(W,X)
;

•

for each
g\in{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)
, let

g_{*}:M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W)_{X}\to M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W)_{Y}

be the
k
-linear map sending each
f\in{\mathscr{C}}(W,X)
to the composition
gf\in{\mathscr{C}}(W,Y)
.

It is easy to see that
M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W)
is a projective
{\mathscr{C}}
-module. We call
M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W)
a
principal projective

{\mathscr{C}}
-module.

We say that a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module is a
free

{\mathscr{C}}
-module if it is a direct sum of principal projective
{\mathscr{C}}
-modules. Every
{\mathscr{C}}
-module is a homomorphic image of a free
{\mathscr{C}}
-module, thus the abelian category
{\mathscr{C}}\Mod
has enough projectives. Every projective
{\mathscr{C}}
-module is isomorphic to a direct summand of a free
{\mathscr{C}}
-module.

1.2.

Assume that
{\mathscr{C}}
is a skeletal small category. Define a relation
\preceq
on
\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
by
X\preceq Y
if
{\mathscr{C}}(X,Y)\neq\emptyset
. We write
X\prec Y
if
X\preceq Y
but not
Y\preceq X
. We say that
{\mathscr{C}}
is
directed
if the relation
\preceq
on
\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
is a partial order.

Example 1.1
.

Recall that
{\mathscr{C}}
is an
EI-category
if every endomorphism in
{\mathscr{C}}
is an isomorphism. If
{\mathscr{C}}
is a skeletal EI-category, then it is directed; see
[
9
]
*Section 9.

Suppose now that
{\mathscr{C}}
is directed. Let
V
be a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module.
For any
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
, define a
k
-submodule
\widetilde{V}_{X}
of
V_{X}
by

\widetilde{V}_{X}=\sum_{W\prec X}\left(\sum_{f\in{\mathscr{C}}(W,X)}f_{*}(V_{W})\right).

The assignment
X\mapsto\widetilde{V}_{X}
defines a
{\mathscr{C}}
-submodule
\widetilde{V}
of
V
. Let

\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{0}:{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{C}}\Mod

be the functor defined by
\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{0}(V)=V/\widetilde{V}
. Then
\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
is a right exact functor and we can define its left derived functors. For each integer
i\geqslant 1
, let

\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{i}:{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{C}}\Mod

be the
i
-th left derived functor of
\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
. We call
\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{i}(V)
the
i
-th
{\mathscr{C}}
-homology
of
V
.

1.3.

Fix an integer
m\geqslant 1
. The category
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
is a directed skeletal small category.

Let
V
be an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module. For each
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, we set

\displaystyle|\mathbf{n}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}\in{\mathbb{N}},

\displaystyle[\mathbf{n}]=([n_{1}],\ldots,[n_{m}])\in\Ob({\mathrm{FI}}^{m}).

We write
V_{\mathbf{n}}
for
V_{[\mathbf{n}]}
. We define the
degree
of
V
by

\deg V=\begin{cases}\sup\{|\mathbf{n}|\mid V_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}&\mbox{ if }V\neq 0,\\
-1&\mbox{ if }V=0.\end{cases}

We say that
V
has
finite degree
if
\deg V<\infty
. For any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let

t_{i}(V)=\deg\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(V).

Let

{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}=\{-1\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}.

We have
\deg V\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}\cup\{\infty\}
.

For any
d\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}
, we say that
V
is
generated in degree
\leqslant d
if
t_{0}(V)\leqslant d
. Equivalently,
V
is generated in degree
\leqslant d
iff there exists an epimorphism
P\to V
where

P=\bigoplus_{j\in J}M^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}([\mathbf{n}_{j}])

for some indexing set
J
and each
\mathbf{n}_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
satisfies
|\mathbf{n}_{j}|\leqslant d
.

For any
d,r\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}
, we say that
V
is
generated in degree
\leqslant d
and
related in degree
\leqslant r
if there exists a short exact sequence

0\to U\to P\to V\to 0

such that:

•

P
is a free
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant d
,

•

U
is an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant r
.

Observe that we can choose
P
with
t_{0}(P)=t_{0}(V)
and in this case we have

(1.2)

t_{1}(V)\leqslant t_{0}(U)\leqslant\max\{t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V)\}.

We define the
regularity

\reg(V)
of
V
by

\reg(V)=\sup\{t_{i}(V)-i\mid i\geqslant 0\}.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
2
]
, the regularity of an
{\mathrm{FI}}
-module
V
is defined as
\sup\{t_{i}(V)-i\mid i\geqslant 1\}
. For our proofs below, it is more convenient to take the supremum over the range
i\geqslant 0
so that
t_{i}(V)\leqslant i+\reg(V)
for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Our notion of regularity is distinct from the notion of Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules defined by Li and Ramos in
[
7
]
. We do not know of any relation between these two notions.

1.4.

We shall define for each integer
m\geqslant 1
a function

\rho_{m}:{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}\times{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}\to{\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant-1}}.

The precise definition of
\rho_{m}
is not needed for understanding the statement of our main result, Theorem
1.6
, below.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
.

If
m=1
, then let

\rho_{m}(d,r)=\max\{d,d+r-1\}.

If
m\geqslant 2
and
d=-1
, then let

\rho_{m}(d,r)=-1.

If
m\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 0
, then let

\rho_{m}(d,r)=\max\{\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)),1+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)\}

where

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle=\max\{2+\rho_{m}(d-1,r),r\},

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)

\displaystyle=\max\{3+\rho_{m}(d-1,r),4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)\}.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
. Let
V
be an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. Then

\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r).

The proof of Theorem
1.6
will be given in Section
4
. We do not expect the bound in Theorem
1.6
to be sharp.

The following corollary gives a bound on the regularity of
V
in terms of
t_{0}(V)
and
t_{1}(V)
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
. Let
V
be an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module. Assume that
t_{0}(V)<\infty
and
t_{1}(V)<\infty
. Then

\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V)).

The proof of Corollary
1.7
is in Subsection
4.4
.

2.
Spectral sequences

2.1.

In this section, we let
{\mathscr{C}}
be a product category
{\mathscr{A}}\times{\mathscr{B}}
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
are directed skeletal small categories; in particular,
{\mathscr{C}}
is a directed skeletal small category.

Let
V
be a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module. For any
(X,Y)\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
, define
k
-submodules
V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{(X,Y)}
and
V^{\mathrm{ver}}_{(X,Y)}
of
V_{(X,Y)}
by

\displaystyle V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{(X,Y)}=\sum_{W\prec X}\left(\sum_{f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,X)}(f,\id_{Y})_{*}\left(V_{(W,Y)}\right)\right),

\displaystyle V^{\mathrm{ver}}_{(X,Y)}=\sum_{Z\prec Y}\left(\sum_{g\in{\mathscr{B}}(Z,Y)}(\id_{X},g)_{*}\left(V_{(X,Z)}\right)\right).

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

The assignment
(X,Y)\mapsto V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{(X,Y)}
defines a
{\mathscr{C}}
-submodule
V^{\mathrm{hor}}
of
V
.

(ii)

The assignment
(X,Y)\mapsto V^{\mathrm{ver}}_{(X,Y)}
defines a
{\mathscr{C}}
-submodule
V^{\mathrm{ver}}
of
V
.

(iii)

One has:
\widetilde{V}=V^{\mathrm{hor}}+V^{\mathrm{ver}}
.

Proof.

(i) Let
f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,X)
and
(g,h)\in{\mathscr{C}}((X,Y),(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}))
. Then

(g,h)(f,\id_{Y})=(gf,h)=(gf,\id_{Y^{\prime}})(\id_{W},h),

which implies

(g,h)_{*}\left((f,\id_{Y})_{*}\left(V_{(W,Y)}\right)\right)\subseteq(gf,\id_{Y^{\prime}})_{*}\left(V_{(W,Y^{\prime})}\right).

Moreover,
W\prec X
implies
W\prec X^{\prime}
. Therefore
(g,h)_{*}\left(V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{(X,Y)}\right)\subseteq V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})}
.

(ii) Similar to (i).

(iii) It is clear that
V^{\mathrm{hor}}+V^{\mathrm{ver}}\subseteq\widetilde{V}
.

Now suppose
(f,g)\in{\mathscr{C}}((W,Z),(X,Y))
where
(W,Z)\prec(X,Y)
. Then
W\prec X
or
Z\prec Y
.

If
W\prec X
, then

(f,g)_{*}\left(V_{(W,Z)}\right)\subseteq(f,\id_{Y})_{*}\left(V_{(W,Y)}\right)\subseteq V^{\mathrm{hor}}_{(X,Y)}.

If
Z\prec Y
, then

(f,g)_{*}\left(V_{(W,Z)}\right)\subseteq(\id_{X},g)_{*}\left(V_{(X,Z)}\right)\subseteq V^{\mathrm{ver}}_{(X,Y)}.

Hence
\widetilde{V}\subseteq V^{\mathrm{hor}}+V^{\mathrm{ver}}
.
∎

By the preceding lemma, we may define functors

\displaystyle\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}:{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{C}}\Mod,\qquad V\mapsto V/V^{\mathrm{hor}};

\displaystyle\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}:{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{C}}\Mod,\qquad V\mapsto V/V^{\mathrm{ver}};

moreover, there are canonical isomorphisms

(2.2)

\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}(\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}(V))\cong\HH^{\mathscr{C}}_{0}(V)\cong\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}(\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}(V)).

The functors
\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}
and
\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}
are right exact and we can define their left derived functors.
For each integer
i\geqslant 1
, let

\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{i}:{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{C}}\Mod

be the
i
-th left derived functor of
\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}
, and let

\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{i}:{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{C}}\Mod

be the
i
-th left derived functor of
\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}
. We call
\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{i}(V)
the
i
-th
horizontal homology
of
V
, and
\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{i}(V)
the
i
-th
vertical homology
of
V
.

2.2.

For each
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{A}})
, we have an inclusion functor
{\mathscr{B}}\to{\mathscr{C}}
defined on objects by
Y\mapsto(X,Y)
and on morphisms by
g\mapsto(\id_{X},g)
. Thus we obtain a restriction functor

{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{B}}\Mod,\quad V\mapsto V_{(X,-)}

where

(V_{(X,-)})_{Y}=V_{(X,Y)}\quad\mbox{ for all }Y\in\Ob({\mathscr{B}}).

Similarly, if we fix
Y\in\Ob({\mathscr{B}})
, we have a restriction functor

{\mathscr{C}}\Mod\to{\mathscr{A}}\Mod,\quad V\mapsto V_{(-,Y)}

where

(V_{(-,Y)})_{X}=V_{(X,Y)}\quad\mbox{ for all }X\in\Ob({\mathscr{A}}).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(W,Z)\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
.

(i)

For each
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{A}})
, we have

M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)_{(X,-)}\cong\bigoplus_{f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,X)}M^{{\mathscr{B}}}(Z).

(ii)

For each
Y\in\Ob({\mathscr{B}})
, we have

M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)_{(-,Y)}\cong\bigoplus_{g\in{\mathscr{B}}(Z,Y)}M^{{\mathscr{A}}}(W).

Proof.

(i) We define a homomorphism

\phi:M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)_{(X,-)}\to\bigoplus_{f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,X)}M^{{\mathscr{B}}}(Z),

as follows: for each
Y\in\Ob({\mathscr{B}})
, let

\phi_{Y}:M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)_{(X,Y)}\to\bigoplus_{f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,X)}M^{{\mathscr{B}}}(Z)_{Y}.

be the
k
-linear map sending the element
(f,g)\in{\mathscr{C}}((W,Z),(X,Y))
to the element
g\in{\mathscr{B}}(Z,Y)
in the direct summand indexed by
f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,X)
. It is easy to see that
\phi
is an isomorphism.

(ii) Similar to (i).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
V
be a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module.

(i)

Let
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{A}})
. For each
i\geqslant 0
, we have

(\HH^{{\mathrm{ver}}}_{i}(V))_{(X,-)}\cong\HH^{{\mathscr{B}}}_{i}(V_{(X,-)}).

(ii)

Let
Y\in\Ob({\mathscr{B}})
. For each
i\geqslant 0
, we have

(\HH^{{\mathrm{hor}}}_{i}(V))_{(-,Y)}\cong\HH^{{\mathscr{A}}}_{i}(V_{(-,Y)}).

Proof.

(i) The case
i=0
is obvious. The case
i>0
follows because the restriction functor is exact and, by Lemma
2.3
, the restriction of a free
{\mathscr{C}}
-module is a free
{\mathscr{B}}
-module.

(ii) Similar to (i).
∎

2.3.

The spectral sequences in the following theorem are special cases of the Grothendieck spectral sequence associated to the composition of two functors.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
V
be a
{\mathscr{C}}
-module. Then there are two convergent first-quadrant spectral sequences:

{}^{I}\!E^{2}_{pq}=\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{p}(\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{q}(V))\Rightarrow\HH^{{\mathscr{C}}}_{p+q}(V),

{}^{II}\!E^{2}_{pq}=\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{p}(\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{q}(V))\Rightarrow\HH^{{\mathscr{C}}}_{p+q}(V).

Proof.

We claim that
\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}
sends projective
{\mathscr{C}}
-modules to
\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}
-acyclic
{\mathscr{C}}
-modules. It suffices to verify the claim for principal projective
{\mathscr{C}}
-modules.

Let
(W,Z)\in\Ob({\mathscr{C}})
and let
X\in\Ob({\mathscr{A}})
.

If
X\neq W
, then

(\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}(M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)))_{(X,-)}=0.

If
X=W
, then

\displaystyle(\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}(M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)))_{(X,-)}

\displaystyle\cong M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)_{(W,-)}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{f\in{\mathscr{A}}(W,W)}M^{{\mathscr{B}}}(Z)

\displaystyle\mbox{(by Lemma \ref{restriction of free modules})}.

Thus

\displaystyle(\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}(\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}(M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z))))_{(X,-)}

\displaystyle\cong\HH^{{\mathscr{B}}}_{0}((\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}(M^{{\mathscr{C}}}(W,Z)))_{(X,-)})

(by Lemma
2.4
)

\displaystyle=0.

This proves the claim.

Using (
2.2
) and Grothendieck spectral sequence of the composition
\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{0}\HH^{\mathrm{hor}}_{0}
, we obtain the first spectral sequence. Similarly for the second spectral sequence.
∎

3.
Long exact sequence

3.1.

In this section, we give a generalization to
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-modules of the long exact sequence which Church constructed for
{\mathrm{FI}}
-modules in
[
3
]
. We start by recalling the functors
\mathbf{\Sigma}
,
\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{D}
and their basic properties following
[
8
]
.

Let
i\in[m]
. Define
\mathbf{e}_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
by

\mathbf{e}_{i}=(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0),

where
1
is in the
i
-th coordinate. There is a functor
\iota_{i}:{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}\to{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
defined on objects by

\displaystyle\iota_{i}:\Ob({\mathrm{FI}}^{m})

\displaystyle\to\Ob({\mathrm{FI}}^{m}),

\displaystyle[\mathbf{n}]

\displaystyle\mapsto[\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}],

and on morphisms by

\displaystyle\iota_{i}:{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}([\mathbf{n}],[\mathbf{r}])

\displaystyle\to{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}([\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}],[\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{i}]),

\displaystyle(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})

\displaystyle\mapsto(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}),

where

g_{j}=f_{j}
for all
j\neq i
and

g_{i}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if }t=1,\\
f_{i}(t-1)+1&\mbox{ if }t>1.\end{array}\right.

The
i
-th shift functor

\Sigma_{i}:{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}\Mod\to{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}\Mod

is defined to be the pullback via
\iota_{i}
. Thus, for any
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module
V
and
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
, we have

(\Sigma_{i}V)_{\mathbf{n}}=V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}.

For each
[\mathbf{n}]=([n_{1}],\ldots,[n_{m}])\in\Ob({\mathrm{FI}}^{m})
, let

\varpi_{i}:[\mathbf{n}]\to[\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}]

be the morphism of
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
whose
j
-th component is the identity map on
[n_{j}]
for
j\neq i
, and whose
i
-th component is the map
[n_{i}]\to[n_{i}+1]
,
t\mapsto t+1
.

Let
V
be an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module. We have a natural homomorphism

\varepsilon_{i}:V\to\Sigma_{i}V

defined at each
[\mathbf{n}]\in\Ob({\mathrm{FI}}^{m})
to be the map
V_{\mathbf{n}}\to V_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}
induced by the morphism
\varpi_{i}
.
Let
K_{i}V
and
D_{i}V
be, respectively, the kernel and cokernel of
\varepsilon_{i}:V\to\Sigma_{i}V
. Thus we have the exact sequence

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K_{i}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varepsilon_{i}}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{i}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{D_{i}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

It is easy to see that: for any

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m},

\displaystyle\mathbf{r}

Let

-module. Assume that

{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}

Hence by Corollary
3.11
, we have

(\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}}_{p}((\HH^{\mathrm{ver}}_{q}(V))_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}))_{n_{1}}=0.

∎

4.2.

We collect here some simple facts on the functions
\rho_{m}
,
\rho^{\prime}_{m}
,
\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}
(see Definition
1.5
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
. Let
x\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
x\leqslant d+1
. Then

\rho_{m}(d,r)\geqslant x+\rho_{m}(d-x,r).

Proof.

This is trivial if
d=-1
so assume that
d\geqslant 0
.

It is plain that for any integer
c\geqslant 0
, we have:

\rho_{m}(c,r)\geqslant 1+\rho_{m}(c-1,r).

The lemma now follows from taking
c=d,d-1,\ldots,d-x+1
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 1
and
d,r\geqslant-1
. Then
\rho_{m}(d,r)\geqslant d
.

Proof.

Take
x=d+1
in Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
m,d,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geqslant 2
,
d\geqslant 0
,
r\geqslant-1
. Then

\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)>\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r).

Proof.

Obviously

3+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)>2+\rho_{m}(d-1,r).

We also have:

\displaystyle 4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)

\displaystyle\geqslant 4+(d+r-1)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)

\displaystyle\geqslant 4-1+r-1-1

\displaystyle>r.

∎

4.3.

We can now prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For any integers
m\geqslant 1
and
d\geqslant-1
, denote by
\mathfrak{T}(m,d)
the following statement:

For any integer
r\geqslant-1
, if
V
is an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
, then
\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)
.

We shall prove that
\mathfrak{T}(m,d)
is true by nested induction. The outer induction is over
m
and the inner induction is over
d
.

By
[
2
]
*Theorem A, we know that
\mathfrak{T}(1,d)
is true for all
d\geqslant-1
. It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{T}(m,-1)
is true for all
m\geqslant 1
.

Now fix
m\geqslant 2
and
d\geqslant 0
. Assume that:

•

\mathfrak{T}(m-1,c)
is true for all
c\geqslant-1
;

•

\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
is true.

To prove that
\mathfrak{T}(m,d)
is true, fix an integer
r\geqslant-1
and an
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module
V
which is generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. We want to show that

\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r).

We do this in several steps.

Step 1. Bound
t_{2}(V)
.

Let
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we know that
V_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}
and
V_{(n_{1},-)}
are generated in degree
\leqslant d
and related in degree
\leqslant r
. From the base case
\mathfrak{T}(1,d)
and assumption
\mathfrak{T}(m-1,d)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\reg\left(V_{(-,n_{2},\ldots,n_{m})}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{1}(d,r),

\displaystyle\reg\left(V_{(n_{1},-)}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(d,r).

Hence by Proposition
4.1
, we have

t_{i}(V)\leqslant\max\{-1,2i+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)\}\quad\mbox{ for all }i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In particular,

(4.6)

t_{2}(V)\leqslant 4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r).

Step 2. Bound
t_{0}(\mathbf{K}V)
and
t_{1}(\mathbf{K}V)
.

By Theorem
3.10
, we have a long exact sequence

\cdots\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{3}(\mathbf{D}V)\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(\mathbf{K}V)\to\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{2}(V)\\
\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{2}(\mathbf{D}V)\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{0}(\mathbf{K}V)\to\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(V)\to\cdots

Thus

\displaystyle t_{0}(\mathbf{K}V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{t_{2}(\mathbf{D}V),\deg\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(V)\},

\displaystyle t_{1}(\mathbf{K}V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{t_{3}(\mathbf{D}V),\deg\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{2}(V)\}.

By Lemma
3.5
and assumption
\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
, we have

t_{2}(\mathbf{D}V)\leqslant 2+\rho_{m}(d-1,r).

We also have

\displaystyle\deg\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\deg\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(V)

\displaystyle=t_{1}(V)

\displaystyle\leqslant r.

We deduce that

(4.7)

t_{0}(\mathbf{K}V)\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r).

By Lemma
3.5
and assumption
\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
again, we have

t_{3}(\mathbf{D}V)\leqslant 3+\rho_{m}(d-1,r).

We also have

\displaystyle\deg\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{2}(V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\deg\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{2}(V)

\displaystyle=t_{2}(V)

\displaystyle\leqslant 4+\rho_{1}(d,r)+\rho_{m-1}(d,r)

\displaystyle\mbox{(by \eqref{eq:t2bound})}.

We deduce that

(4.8)

t_{1}(\mathbf{K}V)\leqslant\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r).

Step 3. Bound
\reg(\mathbf{K}V)
.

By (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have:

\displaystyle t_{0}(K_{1}V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),

\displaystyle t_{1}(K_{1}V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r).

Take any
x\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, we have:

(4.9)

\displaystyle t_{0}((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{-1,\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)-x\},

\displaystyle t_{1}((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{-1,\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)-x\}.

We now consider the following two cases: (1)
x\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)
, (2)
x>\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)
.

Case 1:
x\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)
.

In this case, from (
4.9
), we have:

\displaystyle t_{0}((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)-x,

\displaystyle t_{1}((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r).

Using (
1.2
) and Lemma
4.5
, we deduce that
(K_{1}V)_{(x,-)}
is generated in degree
\leqslant\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)-x
and related in degree
\leqslant\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)
. Hence by assumption
\mathfrak{T}(m-1,c)
where
c=\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)-x
, we have

\reg((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)-x,\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

Using Lemma
4.3
, it follows that

\reg((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r))-x.

Thus

x+\reg((K_{1}V)_{(x,-)})\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

Case 2:
x>\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r)
.

In this case, it follows from (
4.9
) that
(K_{1}V)_{(x,-)}=0
.

From the conclusions of the two cases above, we can apply Proposition
4.2
to
K_{1}V
and deduce that

\reg(K_{1}V)\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

Similarly, for each
s\in[m]
, we have

\reg(K_{s}V)\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

Hence,

(4.10)

\reg(\mathbf{K}V)\leqslant\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

Step 4. Bound
\reg(V)
.

Take any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Recall from Theorem
3.10
that we have a long exact sequence

(4.11)

\cdots\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i-1}(\mathbf{K}V)\to\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(V)\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(\mathbf{D}V)\to\cdots

We have

\displaystyle t_{i}(V)

\displaystyle=\deg\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(V)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\deg\mathbf{\Sigma}\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(V)

(by Lemma
3.4
)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{1+t_{i-1}(\mathbf{K}V),1+t_{i}(\mathbf{D}V)\}

(by (
4.11
)).

By (
4.10
),

1+t_{i-1}(\mathbf{K}V)\leqslant i+\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)).

By Lemma
3.5
and assumption
\mathfrak{T}(m,d-1)
, we have

1+t_{i}(\mathbf{D}V)\leqslant 1+i+\rho_{m}(d-1,r).

It follows from above that

\displaystyle t_{i}(V)

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\{i+\rho_{m-1}(\rho^{\prime}_{m}(d,r),\rho^{\prime\prime}_{m}(d,r)),1+i+\rho_{m}(d-1,r)\}

\displaystyle=i+\rho_{m}(d,r).

We conclude that
\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(d,r)
, as desired.
∎

4.4.

We now prove Corollary
1.7
.

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

There are two cases: (1)
t_{0}(V)\leqslant t_{1}(V)
, (2)
t_{0}(V)>t_{1}(V)
.

Case 1:
t_{0}(V)\leqslant t_{1}(V)
.

In this case, we know by (
1.2
) that
V
is generated in degree
\leqslant t_{0}(V)
and related in degree
\leqslant t_{1}(V)
. Hence by Theorem
1.6
, we have

\reg(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V)).

Case 2:
t_{0}(V)>t_{1}(V)
.

In this case, let

A=\{\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{m}\mid|\mathbf{n}|\leqslant t_{1}(V)\}.

Let
U
be the smallest
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-submodule of
V
such that
U_{\mathbf{n}}=V_{\mathbf{n}}
for all
\mathbf{n}\in A
. It is easy to see that

t_{0}(U)\leqslant t_{1}(V),

so we also have

(4.12)

t_{0}(U)<t_{0}(V).

Let
Q=V/U
. We have a short exact sequence

0\to U\to V\to Q\to 0.

Observe that

\displaystyle t_{0}(Q)\leqslant t_{0}(V),

\displaystyle t_{1}(Q)\leqslant\max\{t_{0}(U),t_{1}(V)\}\leqslant t_{1}(V).

We have
Q_{\mathbf{n}}=0
for all
\mathbf{n}\in A
. Thus there exists an epimorphism
\phi:P\to Q
where
P
is a free
{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}
-module such that
P_{\mathbf{n}}=0
for all
\mathbf{n}\in A
.

Let
W
be the kernel of
\phi
. Then
W_{\mathbf{n}}=0
for all
\mathbf{n}\in A
. We have a short exact sequence

0\to W\to P\to Q\to 0.

Thus we have a monomorphism
\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(Q)\to\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{0}(W)
. Hence:

\displaystyle W_{\mathbf{n}}=0\quad\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{n}\in A

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{0}(W))_{\mathbf{n}}=0\quad\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{n}\in A

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle(\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(Q))_{\mathbf{n}}=0\quad\mbox{ for all }\mathbf{n}\in A.

Since
t_{1}(Q)\leqslant t_{1}(V)
, we must have
\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{1}(Q)=0
. It follows from
[
8
]
*Theorem 1.3 that
\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(Q)=0
for all
i\geqslant 1
.

We deduce that

\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(U)\cong\HH^{{\mathrm{FI}}^{m}}_{i}(V)\quad\mbox{ for all }i\geqslant 1.

In particular,

t_{i}(U)=t_{i}(V)\quad\mbox{ for all }i\geqslant 1.

Since
t_{0}(U)\leqslant t_{1}(V)
, we have
t_{0}(U)\leqslant t_{1}(U)
. Thus by (
1.2
) we know that
U
is generated in degree
\leqslant t_{0}(U)
and related in degree
\leqslant t_{1}(U)
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\reg(U)

\displaystyle\leqslant\rho_{m}(t_{0}(U),t_{1}(U))\quad\mbox{ (by Theorem \ref{main}) }

\displaystyle<\rho_{m}(t_{0}(V),t_{1}(U))\quad\mbox{ (by \eqref{eq:t0U less than t0V} and Lemma \ref{lem:rho increasing in d}) }

\displaystyle=\rho_{m}(t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V)).

Thus for all
i\geqslant 1
, we have:

\displaystyle t_{i}(V)-i

\displaystyle=t_{i}(U)-i

\displaystyle<\rho_{m}(t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V)).

It remains to see that
t_{0}(V)\leqslant\rho_{m}(t_{0}(V),t_{1}(V))
, but this is immediate from Corollary
4.4
.
∎

Eigenvalues of Real Matrices with Prescribed Principal Minors Sign and Descartes Law of Signs
Laureano González–Vega, J. Rafael Sendra, Juana Sendra

Abstract
In this paper, using Descartes law of sign, we provide elementary proof of results on the number of real eigenvalues of real matrices of which certain properties on the signs of their principal minors are known. More precisely, we analyze P , N , Q , R , P ​ N P,N,Q,R,PN and Q ​ R QR matrices as well as their variants almost , weak and sub-zero matrices.

Preliminaries

In this section we recall Descartes law of signs and we introduce the type of matrices we analyze in the paper.

1.1
Descartes Law of Signs

For a sequence of real numbers
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
,
\mathrm{Var}(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
will denote the number of sign changes in
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
after dropping the zeros in the sequence. For a real number
a
,
{\rm sign}(a)
will denote the sign of
a
according to the following rule

{\rm sign}(a)=\begin{cases}+1&\hbox{if $a>0$},\\
\hfill 0&\hbox{if $a=0$},\\
-1&\hbox{if $a<0$}.\\
\end{cases}

Moreover, for a non-constant polynomial
C(x)=a_{0}x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}x+a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
we denote by

\mathrm{Var}(C(x))\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\rm def}}\mathrm{Var}(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}).

In this situation, the well-known Descartes law of signs is as follows; see Section 2.2.1 in
[
1
]
for a proof of the next two propositions.

Proposition 1
.

(Descartes Law of Signs)

Let

C(x)=a_{0}x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}x+a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}[x],

where
a_{0}
is nonzero. The number of positive real roots of
C(x)
is equal to
\mathrm{Var}(C(x))-2k
, for some non–negative integer
k
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
C(x)=a_{0}x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}x+a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}[x],

where
a_{0}
is nonzero. If all roots of
C
are real then the number of positive real roots of
C(x)
is equal to
\mathrm{Var}(C(x))).

Next corollary shows several consequences of these two propositions that we will use very often in what follows.

Corollary 3
.

Let

C(x)=a_{0}x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}x+a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}[x],

where
a_{0}
is nonzero. Then it holds that:

1.

If
\mathrm{Var}(C(x))=1
then
C(x)
has exactly one positive real root .

2.

If
\mathrm{Var}(C(x))=0
then
C(x)
has no positive real roots.

3.

If
\mathrm{Var}(C(-x))=1
then
C(x)
has exactly one negative real root

4.

If
\mathrm{Var}(C(-x))=0
then
C(x)
has no negative real roots.

2
Eigenvalues Through Descartes Law of Signs

In this section, we show how Descartes law of signs provides automatically information about the real eigenvalues of
P
–matrices,
Q
-matrices,
N
–matrices,
R
–matrices,
PN
–matrices and
QR
–matrices, and simplifies the usual proofs that are based on, for example for
N
–matrices, Perron-Frobenious Theorem and other results (see
[
8
]
). For the particular case of symmetric matrices we also show that the sign pattern of their eigenvalues is fixed and known in advance.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and let
C_{A}(\lambda)
be its characteristic polynomial, namely,

C_{A}(\lambda)=\det(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{n}-A)=\lambda^{n}+a_{1}\lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}\lambda+a_{n}.

(1)

We recall that the coefficients of
C_{A}(\lambda)
can be expressed by means of the principal minors. More precisely, it holds that

a_{k}=(-1)^{k}E_{k}(A)

(2)

where
E_{k}(A)
denotes the sum of all
k\times k
principal minors of
A
(see e.g
[
6
]
).

Theorem 11
.

(Eigenvalues of
Q
–matrices)

1.

If
A
is either a
Q
–matrix or a weak
Q
–matrix or a
Q_{0}
–matrix, then
A
has no negative real eigenvalues.

2.

If
A
is an almost
Q
–matrix then
A
has exactly one negative real eigenvalue.

3.

If
A
is an
n\times n
symmetric that is either a
Q
–matrix or a weak
Q
–matrix then
A
has
n
positive real eigenvalues.

4.

If
A
is an
n\times n
symmetric
Q_{0}
–matrix then
A
has
n
non negative real eigenvalues.

5.

If
A
is an
n\times n
symmetric almost
Q
–matrix, then
A
has exactly one negative real eigenvalue and
n-1
positive real eigenvalues.

Proof.

Let
C_{A}(\lambda)
be as in (
1
) and
E_{k}(A)
be as in (
2
).

1.

If
A
is a
Q
–matrix, for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
it holds that
E_{k}(A)>0
. Furthermore, by (
2
),
{\rm sign}(a_{k})=(-1)^{k}

for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Since

C_{A}(-\lambda)=(-1)^{n}\lambda^{n}+(-1)^{n-1}a_{1}\lambda^{n-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{1}a_{n-1}\lambda+a_{n},

(3)

the sequence of sign of the coefficients of
C_{A}(-\lambda)
is

\begin{array}[]{l}\{(-1)^{n},(-1)^{n-1}(-1)^{1},(-1)^{n-2}(-1)^{2},\ldots,(-1)^{1}(-1)^{n-1},(-1)^{n}\}=\\
\{(-1)^{n},(-1)^{n},\ldots,(-1)^{n},(-1)^{n}\}\end{array}

Thus
\mathrm{Var}(C_{A}(-\lambda))=0
and, according to Corollary
3
(4), we conclude that
A
has no negative real eigenvalues.

If
A
is either a weak
Q
–matrix or a
Q_{0}
matrix, reasoning as above, the first coefficient of
C_{A}(-\lambda)
is
(-1)^{n}
, and all other coefficients have sign either
(-1)^{n}
or
0
. Then
\mathrm{Var}(C_{A}(-\lambda))=0
, and
the result follows from Corollary
3
(4).

2.

If
A
is an almost
Q
–matrix, reasoning as in the previous statement, the sequence of sign of the coefficients of
C_{A}(-\lambda)
is

\{(-1)^{n},(-1)^{n},\ldots,(-1)^{n},(-1)^{n+1}\}.

Thus
\mathrm{Var}(C_{A}(-\lambda))=1
and, according to Corollary
3
(3), we conclude that
A
has exactly one negative real eigenvalue.

3.

Since
A
is symmetric, by (1) we know that all eigenvalues are non negative. Moreover, since
A
is either
R
or weak
R
, the determinant of
A
is non-zero and hence, by Statement (1), we conclude that
A
has
n
positive real eigenvalues.

4.

It follows as in the previous case but taking into account that, since
A
is
Q_{0}
, the determinant of
A
can be zero.

5.

It follows from the symmetry, from the fact that
\det(A)\neq 0
, and from statement (2).

∎

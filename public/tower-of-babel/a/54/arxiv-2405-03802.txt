A note on Hölder regularity of weak solutions to linear elliptic equations
Karthik Adimurthi

Abstract
In this paper, we show that weak solutions of − div 𝔸 ( x ) ∇ u = 0 where A ( x ) = A ( x ) T and λ | ζ | 2 ≤ ⟨ A ( x ) ζ , ζ ⟩ ≤ Λ | ζ | 2 , -\dv\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u=0\qquad\text{where}\qquad A(x)=A(x)^{T}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\lambda|\zeta|^{2}\leq\left\langle A(x)\zeta,\zeta\right\rangle\leq\Lambda|\zeta|^{2}, and 𝔸 ⁡ ( x ) ≡ 𝔸 \displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)\equiv\mathbb{A} is a constant matrix are Hölder continuous u ∈ C loc α \displaystyle u\in C^{\alpha}_{\loc} with α ≥ 1 2 ​ ( − ( n − 2 ) + ( n − 2 ) 2 + 4 ​ ( n − 1 ) ​ λ Λ ) \displaystyle\alpha\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\left(-(n-2)+\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+\tfrac{4(n-1)\lambda}{\Lambda}}\right) . This implies that the example constructed by Piccinini - Spagnolo is sharp in the class of constant matrices 𝔸 ⁡ ( x ) ≡ 𝔸 \displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)\equiv\mathbb{A} . The proof of Hölder regularity does not go through a reduction of oscillation type argument and instead is achieved through a monotonicity formula. In the case of general matrices 𝔸 ⁡ ( x ) \displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x) , we obtain the same regularity under some additional hypothesis.

Introduction

In this paper, we shall consider weak solutions
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,2}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solving

-\dv\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u=0\qquad\text{with}\qquad\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2},

(1.1)

where
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)
is assumed to be symmetric and
\displaystyle\lambda,\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
are any two fixed positive constants.

The Hölder regularity results were first proved in
[
3
,
8
]
and subsequently, a different proof was given in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. All three approaches provided the tools to prove a much stronger Harnack inequality and Hölder regularity was deduced from this. In
[
1
]
, a sharp form of Harnack inequality was proved which implied that the Hölder exponent had exponential dependence on
\displaystyle\tfrac{\lambda}{\Lambda}
. Since Harnack inequality obtained in
[
1
]
was sharp, this implied that the approaches developed in
[
3
,
8
,
5
]
could at best give the Hölder exponent that had exponential dependence on
\displaystyle\tfrac{\lambda}{\Lambda}
.

In
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a new proof using a monotonicity formula which is an optimized version of the ‘hole filling’ technique was given in
[
9
]
and they obtained the sharp Hölder exponent to be
\displaystyle\sqrt{\tfrac{\lambda}{\Lambda}}
and conjectured that in higher dimensions, the exponent should have the form

\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(-(n-2)+\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+\frac{4(n-1)\lambda}{\Lambda}}\right).

In this regard, we also mention
[
2
,
12
]
and references therein where the ‘hole filling’ technique was extended to higher dimensional linear elliptic and parabolic equations using Green’s function estimates.

In this paper, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Any local weak solutions of
Eq.

1.1
with
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)\equiv\mathbb{A}
is Hölder continuous
\displaystyle u\in C_{\loc}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(-(n-2)+\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+\frac{4(n-1)\lambda}{\Lambda}}\right).

Corollary 1.2
.

Any local weak solutions of
Eq.

1.1
with a general
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)
and
\displaystyle\err\geq 0
is Hölder continuous
\displaystyle u\in C_{\loc}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(-(n-2)+\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+\frac{4(n-1)\lambda}{\Lambda}}\right).

The quantity
\displaystyle\err
is as obtained in
Lemma

4.1
and the regularity is obtained in
Remark

4.5
.

It suffices to prove the following theorem instead:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\displaystyle S_{1}
be the sphere of unit radius in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\displaystyle B_{1}
be the unit ball in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, any weak solution of
Eq.

1.1
satisfies

\int_{S_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,d\sigma\geq\sqrt{(n-2)^{2}+\frac{4(n-1)\lambda}{\Lambda}}\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx.

Remark 1.4
.

In the rest of the paper, we will assume
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)
and
\displaystyle u
are smooth and obtain a priori estimates. Such an assumption can be made to hold by a standard approximation procedure, see
[
4
, Section 4]
for the details. The proof also shows that for general matrices
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)
, we can obtain the same regularity as
Theorem

1.1
provided
\displaystyle\err\geq 0
, where
\displaystyle\err
is as obtained in
Lemma

4.1
.

Ideas of Piccinini-Spagnolo revisited

In
[
9
]
, the authors proved the following result:

Theorem 2.1
.

When
\displaystyle n=2
, weak solutions of
Eq.

1.1
are
\displaystyle C^{\alpha}_{\loc}
regular with
\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{\tfrac{{\lambda}}{{\Lambda}}}
. In particular, the following estimate holds for any
\displaystyle r>0
:

\int_{B_{r}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\leq\frac{r{\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}}}}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,d\sigma,

(2.1)

where
\displaystyle B_{r}
is the ball of raidus
\displaystyle r
and
\displaystyle S_{r}=\partial B_{r}
is the boundary of
\displaystyle B_{r}
.

Proof.

We shall change to polar co-ordinates and use the following notation:

\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}r\cos(\theta)\\
r\sin(\theta)\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbb{J}(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta)&-\sin(\theta)\\
\sin(\theta)&\cos(\theta)\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbb{P}(x)=\mathbb{J}(\theta)^{\top}\mathbb{A}(x)\mathbb{J}(\theta),\quad\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}u_{x}\\
u_{y}\end{bmatrix}}_{:=\nabla u}=\mathbb{J}(\theta)\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}u_{r}\\
\tfrac{1}{r}u_{\theta}\end{bmatrix}}_{:=\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u}:=\begin{bmatrix}u_{N}\\
u_{T}\end{bmatrix}.

Following
[
9
]
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}g(r)&:=&\int_{B_{r}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx=\int_{S_{r}}(u-k)\left\langle\mathbb{P}(x)\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,\vec{e_{1}}\right\rangle\,d\sigma=\int_{S_{r}}(u-k)\left(p_{11}u_{\rho}+p_{12}\tfrac{1}{r}u_{\theta}\right)d\sigma,\\
g^{\prime}(r)&:=&\int_{S_{r}}\left\langle\mathbb{P}(x)\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,e_{1}\right\rangle\,d\sigma,\end{array}

(2.2)

where to obtain the first equality in the definition of
\displaystyle g(r)
, we made use of the fact that
\displaystyle u
solves
Eq.

1.1
.

With the choice
\displaystyle k=\fint_{S_{r}}u\,d\sigma
and
\displaystyle L:=\tfrac{\Lambda}{\lambda}
, we shall estimate
\displaystyle g(r)
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}g(r)&\overset{\hypertarget{a2.3a}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}a}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}}}{\leq}&\left(\int_{S_{r}}p_{11}(u-k)^{2}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{S_{r}}\left(\sqrt{p_{11}}u_{r}+\frac{p_{12}}{\sqrt{p_{11}}}\frac{1}{r}u_{\theta}\right)^{2}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\overset{\hypertarget{a2.3b}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}}}{\leq}&r\sqrt{L}\left(\lambda\int_{S_{r}}u_{T}^{2}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{S_{r}}\left(\sqrt{p_{11}}u_{N}+\frac{p_{12}}{\sqrt{p_{11}}}u_{T}\right)^{2}\,d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\overset{\hypertarget{a2.3c}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}c}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}}}{\leq}&\frac{r\sqrt{L}}{2}\left[\int_{S_{r}}\left(p_{22}-\frac{p_{12}^{2}}{p_{11}}\right)u_{T}^{2}\,d\sigma+\int_{S_{r}}\left(p_{11}u_{N}^{2}+\frac{p_{12}^{2}}{p_{11}}u_{T}^{2}+2p_{12}u_{N}u_{T}\right)\,d\sigma\right]\\
&=&\frac{r\sqrt{L}}{2}\int_{S_{r}}\left\langle P\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u\right\rangle\,d\sigma=\frac{r\sqrt{L}}{2}g^{\prime}(r),\end{array}

(2.3)

where to obtain
(
a
)
, we applied Hölder’s inequality, to obtain
(
b
)
, we applied
Proposition

3.1
along with the bound
\displaystyle\lambda\leq p_{11}(x)\leq\Lambda
and finally to obtain
(
c
)
, we made use of the matrix inequality
\displaystyle\lambda\leq p_{22}(x)-\frac{p_{12}^{2}(x)}{p_{11}(x)}\leq\Lambda
along with Young’s inequality. Recalling
Eq.

2.2
, the estimate in
Eq.

2.3
is exactly the claim in
Eq.

2.1
and this completes the proof of the theorem.

∎

3
Proof of
Theorem

1.3
when
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)=\mathbb{I}
is identity matrix - case of harmonic functions

Let us first recall the Poincaré inequality on spheres (see
[
11
, Chapter IV, Section 2]
for the details):

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u\in W^{1,2}_{\loc}(S_{r})
where
\displaystyle S_{r}
is the unit sphere in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\displaystyle n\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u=(u_{N},u_{T})
be the tangential and normal derivative of
\displaystyle u
. Then the following sharp form of Poincaré’s inequality holds:

\int_{S_{r}}(u-k)^{2}\,d\sigma\leq\left(\frac{r^{2}}{n-1}\right)\int_{S_{r}}|u_{T}|^{2}\,d\sigma,

where
\displaystyle k=\fint_{S_{r}}u\,d\sigma
.

We shall also recall the well known Pohožaev identity for harmonic functions obtained in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle u
to be a smooth, weak solution of
\displaystyle-\Delta u=0
(i.e.,
\displaystyle u
is locally harmonic), then the following identity holds:

\int_{S_{1}}|u_{T}|^{2}\,d\sigma=\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma+(n-2)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx.

Proof.

Simple calculations implies

\Delta u\left\langle\nabla u,x\right\rangle=\dv(\nabla u\left\langle\nabla u,x\right\rangle)-\frac{1}{2}\dv(|\nabla u|^{2}x)+\frac{n-2}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

(3.1)

Using
\displaystyle-\Delta u=0
along with integrating
Eq.

3.1
over
\displaystyle B_{1}
, we have the following identity

\begin{array}[]{rcl}0=\int_{B_{1}}\Delta u\left\langle\nabla u,x\right\rangle\,dx&=&\int_{S_{1}}\left\langle\nabla u,x\right\rangle^{2}\,d\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\left\langle x,x\right\rangle\,d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\\
&=&\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{1}}(|u_{T}|^{2}+|u_{N}|^{2})\,d\sigma+\frac{n-2}{2}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx,\end{array}

which completes the proof.
∎

3.1
Some discussion

If we consider solutions of
\displaystyle-\Delta u=0
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
(i.e.,
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)=I
) for
\displaystyle n\geq 3
and try to follow the calculations of
Theorem

2.1
, then we get

\int_{B_{r}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\leq\frac{r}{2\sqrt{n-1}}\int_{S_{r}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,d\sigma,

(3.2)

which fails to prove Lipschitz regularity (i.e., the constant in
Eq.

3.2
should be
\displaystyle\tfrac{r}{n}
) when
\displaystyle n=3
and for
\displaystyle n\geq 7
, we have
\displaystyle 2\sqrt{n-1}<n-2
and thus no regularity follows.

On the other hand, if we consider the simple example of harmonic functions which satisfy
\displaystyle|\nabla u|=C
for some constant
\displaystyle C
, then we have

\frac{\int_{B_{r}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx}{\int_{S_{r}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,d\sigma}=\frac{\omega_{n}r^{n}}{n\omega_{n}r^{n-1}}=\frac{r}{n},

which implies Lipschitz regularity. But the above calculations shows that the factor
\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{n}
is coming from the ratio of
\displaystyle|B_{r}|
and
\displaystyle|S_{r}|
which is a property of the fact that we are in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
and not necessarily from the property of the equation and solution.

This suggest that some additional cancellations should hold that can further improve
Eq.

3.2
and these cancellations should be obtained from the fact that we are in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We shall formalize this observation for harmonic functions in the following theorem:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle u
be a local weak solution of
\displaystyle-\Delta u=0
, then we have

\int_{B_{r}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\leq\frac{r}{n}\int_{S_{r}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,d\sigma,

holds for all
\displaystyle r>0
and in particular
\displaystyle u\in C^{0,1}_{\loc}
.

3.3
First proof

We shall use the notation
\displaystyle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u=(u_{N},u_{T})
where
\displaystyle u_{N}=\left\langle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,\vec{e_{1}}\right\rangle
and
\displaystyle u_{T}=(\left\langle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,\vec{e_{2}}\right\rangle,\ldots,\left\langle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,\vec{e_{n}}\right\rangle)
are the normal and tangential derivatives on the sphere respectively in polar coordinates. We can also take
\displaystyle r=1
since the required estimate can be obtained by scaling.

Remark 3.4
.

We will be deliberately careless in switching between
\displaystyle\nabla u
and
\displaystyle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u
, i.e., cartesian and polar coordinates in the proof, so as to better present the ideas of the proof, noting that this switch is just a transformation by orthogonal matrices and hence does not affect any of the calculations.

The proof will now proceed in several steps:

Step 1:

Since
\displaystyle u
is harmonic, we see that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx&=&\int_{S_{1}}(u-k)\left\langle\nabla u,\vec{n}\right\rangle\,d\sigma=\int_{S_{1}}(u-k)\left\langle\bm{\bar{}}{\nabla}u,\vec{e_{1}}\right\rangle\,d\sigma\\
&\overset{\hypertarget{a3.2a}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}a}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0})}}}{\leq}&\left(\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\int_{S_{1}}|u_{T}|^{2}\,d\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma\right),\\
\end{array}

(3.5)

where to obtain
(
a
)
, we applied Young’s inequality followed by
Proposition

3.1
with
\displaystyle k=\fint_{S_{1}}u\,d\sigma
.

Step 2:

We recall Pohožaev identity from
Proposition

3.2
:

\int_{S_{1}}|u_{T}|^{2}\,d\sigma=\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma+(n-2)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx.

(3.6)

Step 3:

We shall substitute
Eq.

3.6
into
Eq.

3.5
and obtain

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx\leq\frac{1}{2(n-1)}\left(\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma+(n-2)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma,

(3.7)

which after simplification, becomes

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx\leq\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma.

(3.8)

Step 4:

Let us add
\displaystyle\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma
to both sides of
Eq.

3.6
and then make use of
Eq.

3.8
to get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\int_{S_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma=\int_{S_{1}}(|u_{T}|^{2}+|u_{N}|^{2})\,d\sigma&\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{a3.7}}{=}&2\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma+(n-2)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx\\
&\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{a3.8}}{\geq}&2\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx+(n-2)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx\\
&=&n\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx.\end{array}

(3.9)

This completes the proof of the theorem.

Since

as:

\int_{S_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\sigma=2\int_{S_{1}}|u_{N}|^{2}\,d\sigma+(n-2)\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{2}\ dx.

4
Proof of
Theorem

1.3
when
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)\equiv\mathbb{A}
has constant entries

The first lemma we generalize is the Pohožaev identity from
Proposition

3.2
to variable coefficient operators of the form considered in
Eq.

1.1
. This is well known in literature and we present all the details for sake of completeness.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u
be a weak solution to
Eq.

1.1
and assume both
\displaystyle u
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}(x)
are sufficiently smooth. Then the following Pohožaev type identity holds:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\int_{S_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)x,x\right\rangle\,d\sigma&=&2\int_{S_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,x\right\rangle^{2}\,d\sigma+\int_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{i}^{n}a_{ii}(x)\right)\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\\
&&+\sum_{i,j,k,l}\int_{B_{1}}x_{j}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij})a_{kl}u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{l}}\,dx-2\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\\
&&-2\sum_{i,j,k,l}\int_{B_{1}}u_{x_{i}}a_{ij}u_{x_{k}}x_{l}(\partial_{x_{j}}a_{kl})\,dx\\
&&+\sum_{i,j,m,n}\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}x_{j}u_{x_{m}}u_{x_{n}}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{mn})\,dx\\
&=&2\int_{S_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,x\right\rangle^{2}\,d\sigma+\int_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{i}^{n}a_{ii}(x)\right)\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\\
&&-2\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u,\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx+\err,\end{array}

(4.1)

where we have denoted

\err:=\sum_{i,j,k,l}\int_{B_{1}}x_{j}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij})a_{kl}u_{x_{k}}u_{x_{l}}\,dx-2\sum_{i,j,k,l}\int_{B_{1}}u_{x_{i}}a_{ij}u_{x_{k}}x_{l}(\partial_{x_{j}}a_{kl})\,dx+\sum_{i,j,m,n}\int_{B_{1}}a_{ij}x_{j}u_{x_{m}}u_{x_{n}}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{mn})\,dx.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that all the terms of
\displaystyle\err
contain derivates of the entries of
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
and does not contain any terms from the Hessian matrix
\displaystyle[D^{2}u]
. In particular,
Eq.

4.1
already captures the cancellations of the form
\displaystyle\left\langle x,[D^{2}u]\nabla u\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla u,[D^{2}u]x\right\rangle
which is crucially used in the case of harmonic functions to prove
Proposition

3.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Simple calculations give

\begin{array}[]{l}\dv(\mathbb{A}\nabla u\left\langle x,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle)=\dv(\mathbb{A}\nabla u)\left\langle x,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,[D(\mathbb{A}\nabla u)]x\right\rangle,\\
\dv(\mathbb{A}x\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle)=\dv(\mathbb{A}x)\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D(\mathbb{A}\nabla u)]\nabla u\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D^{2}u]\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle,\end{array}

(4.2)

where
\displaystyle D(\mathbb{A}\nabla u)
is a matrix of the form

(D(\mathbb{A}\nabla u))_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{jk})u_{x_{k}}+(D^{2}u\mathbb{A})_{ij}=:(D(A)(\nabla u))_{ij}+([D^{2}u]\mathbb{A})_{ij},

(4.3)

and
\displaystyle([D^{2}u]\mathbb{A})
is the matrix product of
\displaystyle[D^{2}u]\cdot\mathbb{A}
.
Using
Eq.

4.3
in
Eq.

4.2
and noting that
\displaystyle\dv(\mathbb{A}\nabla u)=0
, we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\dv(\mathbb{A}\nabla u\left\langle x,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle)=\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,[D(\mathbb{A})(\nabla u)]x\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,[D^{2}u]\mathbb{A}x\right\rangle,\\
\dv(\mathbb{A}x\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle)=\dv(\mathbb{A}x)\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D(\mathbb{A})(\nabla u)]\nabla u\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D^{2}u]\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle,\end{array}

(4.4)

Subtracting twice the first equation in
Eq.

4.4
from the second equation in
Eq.

4.4
in order to cancel the terms containing
\displaystyle[D^{2}u]
(since
\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D^{2}u]\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,[D^{2}u]\mathbb{A}x\right\rangle
), we get

2\dv(\mathbb{A}\nabla u\left\langle x,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle)-\dv(\mathbb{A}x\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle)\\
=2\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,[D(\mathbb{A})(\nabla u)]x\right\rangle-\dv(\mathbb{A}x)\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D(\mathbb{A})(\nabla u)]\nabla u\right\rangle.

(4.5)

We see that
\displaystyle\dv(\mathbb{A}x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{ii}+\sum_{i,j}x_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}

which we substitute in
Eq.

4.5
and integrate over
\displaystyle B_{1}
to get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}2\int_{B_{1}}\dv(\mathbb{A}\nabla u\left\langle x,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle)\,dx&=&\int_{B_{1}}\dv(\mathbb{A}x\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle)\,dx+2\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\mathbb{A}\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\\
&&+2\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,[D(\mathbb{A})(\nabla u)]x\right\rangle\,dx-\int_{B_{1}}\left\langle\mathbb{A}x,[D(\mathbb{A})(\nabla u)]\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx\\
&&-\int_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{i}^{n}a_{ii}\right)\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx-\int_{B_{1}}\left(\sum_{i,j}x_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}a_{ij}\right)\left\langle\mathbb{A}\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle\,dx.\end{array}

We can now apply the divergence theorem to complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
be the matrix similar to
\displaystyle\mathbb{A}
in polar coordinates which we write as

\mathbb{P}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}p_{11}&P_{12}\\
\hline\cr P_{21}&\mathbb{P}_{22}\end{array}\right),

where
\displaystyle p_{11}
is a scalar,
\displaystyle P_{12}=P_{21}^{\top}
is
\displaystyle(n-1)
vector and
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{22}
is
\displaystyle(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix. Then the following inequality holds:

\lambda p_{11}|\xi|^{2}\leq p_{11}\left\langle\mathbb{P}_{22}\xi,\xi\right\rangle-\left\langle P_{12},\xi\right\rangle^{2}\qquad\text{for every}\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\{0\}.

(4.7)

Proof.

We have

\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-P_{21}p_{11}^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}}_{:=\mathbb{S}}\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}p_{11}&P_{12}\\
P_{21}&P_{22}\end{pmatrix}}_{=\mathbb{P}}\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1&-p_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}}_{=\mathbb{S}^{\top}}=\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}p_{11}&0\\
0&P_{22}-P_{21}p_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\end{pmatrix}}_{:=\mathbb{M}}.

From this, we see that if we consider the
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
vector
\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix}
, then using
Eq.

1.1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\lambda\frac{\left\langle P_{12},\xi\right\rangle^{2}}{p_{11}^{2}}+\lambda|\xi|^{2}&\leq&\left\langle\mathbb{P}\begin{pmatrix}-p_{11}^{-1}\left\langle P_{12},\xi\right\rangle\\
\xi\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}-p_{11}^{-1}\left\langle P_{12},\xi\right\rangle\\
\xi\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\\
&=&\left\langle\mathbb{S}\mathbb{\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{S}^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathbb{M}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\\
&=&\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}p_{11}&0\\
0&P_{22}-P_{21}p_{11}^{-1}P_{12}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\\
&=&\left\langle(\mathbb{P}_{22}-P_{21}p_{11}^{-1}P_{12})\xi,\xi\right\rangle\end{array}

In particular, we have

\lambda p_{11}|\xi|^{2}\leq p_{11}\left\langle\mathbb{P}_{22}\xi,\xi\right\rangle-\left\langle P_{12},\xi\right\rangle^{2}\qquad\text{for every}\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

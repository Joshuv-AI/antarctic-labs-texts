v v -adic periods of Carlitz motives and Chowla–Selberg formula revisited
Chieh-Yu Chang, Fu-Tsun Wei, Jing Yu

Abstract
. Let v v be a finite place of 𝔽 q ​ ( θ ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta) . In this paper, we interpret v v -adic arithmetic gamma values in terms of the v v -adic crystalline–de Rham periods of Carlitz motives with Complex Multiplication, and establish an Ogus-type Chowla–Selberg formula. Furthermore, we prove the algebraic independence of these v v -adic periods by employing the technique of “switching v v and ∞ \infty ”, and determining the dimension of relevant motivic Galois groups on the “ ∞ \infty -adic” side through an adaptation and refinement of existing methods. As a consequence, all algebraic relations among v v -adic arithmetic gamma values over 𝔽 q ​ ( θ ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta) can be derived from standard functional equations together with Thakur’s analogue of the Gross–Koblitz formula.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

This study is inspired by the Gross–Koblitz formula
[
GK79
]
and its function field analogue established by Thakur
[
T88
]
.
Classically, one interpolates the factorials
n!
to obtain the Euler gamma function, and a folklore conjecture asserts that all
the special gamma values
\Gamma(z)
, for
z\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, are transcendental.
A key reason for this conjecture comes from the geometric interpretation of these values as “abelian CM periods,” as illuminated by the Chowla–Selberg formula and further explored in Gross’s foundational work
[
Gr78
]
. This geometric viewpoint has greatly deepened our understanding of the significance of gamma values (see
[
And82
]
,
[
Co93
]
,
[
MR04
]
,
[
Yang10
]
,
[
BM16
]
, and
[
Fr17
]
, etc.).

The trancendence of the gamma values at rationals is only known when the denominator is
2
as
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
, or when the denominator is
4
or
6
. The latter case is due to Chudnovsky’s work
[
Chud84
, p. 6-8]
on the algebraic independence of a nonzero period
\varpi
and its corresponding quasi-period
\eta(\varpi)
of a CM elliptic curve defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
(see also
[
BW07
, p. 30]
).
The Lang-Rohrlich conjecture
[
La90
]
asserts that all
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebraic relations among special gamma values and
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
are explained by the standard functional equations satisfied by the Euler gamma function, which can be derived if the Shimura conjecture on period symbols is true (see
[
BCPW22
]
).

For a prime number
p
, Morita
[
Mo75
]
defined a continuous function
\Gamma_{p}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
via a
p
-adic interpolation of the factorials
n!
(up to a sign). Moreover,
Ogus’
p
-adic Chowla–Selberg-type formula in
[
O89
]
interprets
the special
p
-adic gamma values
\Gamma_{p}(z)
for
z
in
\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in terms of matrix coefficients of the isocrystal (Frobenius) action on the crystalline cohomology of the Jacobians of Fermat curves.
Note that for an odd prime
p
, Gross–Koblitz managed to express a Gauss sum explicitly in terms of a product of specific special Morita
p
-adic gamma values, which provides “extra” algebraic relations among these values other than those coming from standard functional equations.
It is a natural question whether there is a ‘
p
-adic Lang–Rohrlich phenomenon’ (namely, the known existing algebraic relations arising from the standard functional equations that Morita’s
p
-adic gamma function satisfies together with the Gross-Koblitz formula) accounting for all
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebraic relations among the special
p
-adic gamma values.
Our ultimate goal is to explore the “
v
-adic” Chowla–Selberg phenomenon through analyzing “
v
-adic periods”, and to provide an affirmative answer for this question in the positive characteristic world.

The present paper represents our first successful attempt (in this direction) to
investigate the
v
-adic arithmetic gamma function for the rational function field
k
of one variable over a finite field, after L. Carlitz
[
Car35
]
, D. Goss
[
Go80
]
and D. Thakur
[
T88
]
,
[
T91
]
, and examine the period interpretation of its special values.
Note that for the
\infty
-adic gamma values over function fields, the Chowla–Selberg phenomenon has been fully clarified from the work of
[
Sin97
]
,
[
BP02
]
,
[
ABP04
]
,
[
CPTY10
]
, and
[
Wei22
]
.
In this paper, we shift our focus to the
v
-adic case
for any finite place
v
of
k
,
and establish an Ogus-type Chowla–Selberg formula for the
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values connecting with the
v
-adic “cystalline–de Rham periods” of “Carlitz motives with complex multiplication”.
We also derive the algebraic independence of these
v
-adic periods, which enables us to show that the algebraicity of special arithmetic
v
-adic gamma values comes only from Thakur’s analogue of Gross–Koblitz.

1.2.
v
-adic periods of Carlitz motives and gamma values

Let
A:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
be the polynomial ring in one variable
\theta
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
with
q
elements, and
k:=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
be its fraction field.
Put
D_{0}:=1
, and for
i\geq 1
,
D_{i}:=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(\theta^{q^{i}}-\theta^{q^{j}})\in A
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
D_{i}
equals the product of all monic polynomials of degree
i
in
A
. Carlitz
[
Car35
]
introduced his factorial for
A
as follows:
given a non-negative integer
n
, write
n=\sum_{i\geq 0}n_{i}q^{i}
for
0\leq n_{i}<q
and put

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},\infty}(n+1):=\prod_{i\geq 0}D_{i}^{n_{i}}\in A,

which becomes the positive characteristic analogue of the factorial
n!
, see (
2.1.1
).
Taking the unit part of these factorial values, Goss
[
Go80
]
discovered that one may do interpolation in Morita style,
arriving at a continuous function:

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},\infty}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(1/\theta)\!)^{\times}
.

Goss successfully applied his strategy to all places of
k
.
Given a monic irreducible polynomial
v\in A
, let
k_{v}
be the completion of
k
at
v
.
Using
v
-adic interpolation for the Carlitz factorials, Goss
[
Go88
]
and Thakur
[
T91
]
defined a
v
-adic arithmetic gamma function

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow k_{v}^{\times},

whose precise definition is given in Section
2.2
.

Note that in the
\infty
-adic case, the period interpretation of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},\infty}(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is illustrated in
[
CPTY10
]
, which leads to the determination of all algebraic relations among these values.
More precisely, let
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be the polynomial ring in another variable
t
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given a positive integer
\ell
, we let
{\bf{C}}_{\ell}
be the Carlitz
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}[t]
-module, then
{\bf{C}}_{\ell}
is a CM Drinfeld
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
-module of rank
\ell
with CM field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}}(t)
. In
[
CPTY10
, Theorems 3.3.1 and 3.3.2]
, it is shown that periods of the dual
t
-motive associated to
{\bf{C}}_{\ell}
are expressed as products of special
\infty
-adic gamma values (up to algebraic multiples). By
[
Pe08
, Sec. 4.2]
(see also
[
CP12
, (3.4.5) and (3.4.8)]
), periods and quasi-periods of
{\bf{C}}_{\ell}
are explicitly related to the periods of the dual
t
-motive in question, and hence combining these gives a Chowla-Selberg formula for special
\infty
-adic arithmetic gamma values.

Here we shall present a
v
-adic counterpart of the phenomenon above.
Let
\matheur{C}_{\ell}
be the Carlitz
t
-motive of degree
\ell
over
A
, and denote by
\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell})

the ‘restriction of scalars’ of
\matheur{C}_{\ell}
(see Section
3.1
). We note that the terminology ‘degree
\ell
’ indicates that
\matheur{C}_{\ell}
has complex multiplication by the degree
\ell
constant field extension over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
.
Let
v
be a monic irreducible polynomial in
A
and
k_{v}
be the completion of
k
at
v
.
As
\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell})
is defined ‘integrally’ over
A
and has ‘good reduction’ at
v
, one may associate its crystalline module
H_{\textrm{cris}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v})
and de Rham module
H_{\textrm{dR}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v})
over
k_{v}
(see Remark
3.5.12
).
Moreover, the theory of Hartl–Kim in
[
HaKi20
]
provides a crystalline–de Rham comparison isomorphism (see (
3.5.13
))

\phi_{v}:H_{\textrm{cris}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{\textrm{dR}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v}).

Therefore the natural “isocrystal action”
\tau_{v}
on
H_{\textrm{cris}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v})
transforms to a
k_{v}
-linear automorphism
\varrho_{v}
on
H_{\textrm{dR}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v})
through
\phi_{v}
(see (
3.7.1
) for the precise definition).
The first theorem of this paper is to express
{\varrho}_{v}
explicitly in terms of special
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values:

Theorem 1.2.1
.

(See Theorem
3.7.7
.)

Choose the ‘global’ basis
\{\omega_{0},...,\omega_{\ell-1}\}
of the de Rham module
H_{\textrm{dR}}(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k)
as in (
3.7.4
).
Let
d:=\deg v
.
For each integer
s
with
0\leq s<\ell
, we write
s+d=s_{0}+n_{s}^{\prime}\ell
, where
s_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq s_{0}<\ell
and
n_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then we have

\varrho_{v}\cdot\omega_{s}=(-1)^{n_{s}^{\prime}}\cdot C_{s}\cdot\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\Big\langle\frac{q^{s+d}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big\rangle_{\ari}\Big)}{\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\Big\langle\frac{q^{s+d-1}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big\rangle_{\ari}\Big)^{q}}\cdot\omega_{s_{0}},\quad\text{ where }\quad C_{s}:=\begin{cases}-v,&\text{ if $s=0$,}\\
1,&\text{ if $s>0$,}\end{cases}

and
\langle z\rangle_{\ari}
is the fractional part of
z
for every
z\in\mathbb{Q}
, i.e.
0\leq\langle z\rangle_{\ari}<1
and
z-\langle z\rangle_{\ari}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 1.2.2
.

We may regard Theorem
1.2.1
as a function field analogue of Ogus’
p
-adic Chowla–Selberg formula in
[
O89
, Theorem 3.15]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2.1
is based on an explicit description of the crystalline–de Rham comparison isomorphism
\phi_{v}
and a product expansion formula for the special values of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}
in question (see Corollary
2.3.5
).
By choosing
a specific ‘global’
A[t]
-base
\mathfrak{B}
of
\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell})
as in (
3.6.2
), which induces a basis
\Lambda_{v}=\{\lambda_{v,0},...,\lambda_{v,\ell-1}\}
of
H_{\textrm{cris}}\big(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v}\big)
(in (
3.6.5
)) and a basis
\beta_{v}=\{\omega_{v,0},...,\omega_{v,\ell-1}\}
of
H_{\textrm{dR}}\big(\matheur{R}_{\tau}(\matheur{C}_{\ell}),k_{v}\big)
(in (
3.6.4
)), we particularly have

\omega_{v,s}=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{n_{s}-1}(\theta-\theta^{q^{\ell n+s}})\right)\cdot\omega_{s},&\text{ if $s=0$,}\\
\left(\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}-1}(\theta-\theta^{q^{\ell n+s}})\right)\cdot\omega_{s},&\text{ if $s>0$,}\end{cases}

given in (
3.7.5
)
(where
n_{s}
is the smallest non-negative integer so that
s+n_{s}\ell\geq\deg v
),
and the “period matrix” of the crystalline–de Rham isomorphism
\phi_{v}
with respect to the bases
\Lambda_{v}
and
\beta_{v}
is determined by the following result:

Theorem 1.2.3
.

(See Theorem
3.6.6
.)

For every integer
s
with
0\leq s<\ell
, we have

\phi_{v}(\lambda_{v,s})=\prod_{n=n_{s}}^{\infty}(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{\ell n+s}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{\ell n+s}}})\cdot\omega_{v,s}.

Here
\varepsilon=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\theta^{q^{i\deg v}}

\in k_{v}
is the Teichmüller image of
\theta\bmod v\in A/(v)
, and
n_{s}
is the smallest non-negative integer so that
s+n_{s}\ell\geq\deg v
.
Consequently,
the period matrix of the isomorphism
\phi_{v}
in (
3.5.13
) with respect to
\beta_{v}
and
\Lambda_{v}
is

\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\prod_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{\ell n}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{\ell n}}})&&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&&\displaystyle\prod_{n=n_{\ell-1}}^{\infty}(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{\ell n+\ell-1}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{\ell n+\ell-1}}})\end{pmatrix}.

Having this period matrix at hand, we are able to realize the action of
\varrho_{v}
on the basis
\beta_{v}
as in (
3.7.3
).
Combining the product formula of
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values in Corollary
2.3.5
enables us to derive Theorem
1.2.1
.

1.3.
Algebraic independence of the
v
-adic crystalline–de Rham periods

Let
\bar{k}_{v}
be an algebraic closure of
k_{v}
, and let
\bar{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
in
\bar{k}_{v}
.
By Theorem
1.2.3
, the field generated by the entries of the period matrix of the crystalline–de Rham isomorphism
\phi_{v}
over
\bar{k}
is

\bar{k}\Big(\Omega_{\ell,v,s}(v)\ \Big|\ 1\leq s\leq\ell\Big),\quad\text{ where }\quad\Omega_{\ell,v,s}(v):=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{\ell n+s}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{\ell n+s}}})\quad\in k_{v},\quad 1\leq s\leq\ell.

Rather than directly using the entries of the period matrix in Theorem
1.2.3
, we take this opportunity to introduce the infinite products
\Omega_{\ell,v,s}(v)
, whose period-theoretic interpretation will play a crucial role in Section
1.3
.
The second theorem of this paper is to determine their algebraic independence:

Theorem 1.3.1
.

Given a positive integer
\ell
,
the periods

\Omega_{\ell,v,1}(v),\cdots,\Omega_{\ell,v,\ell}(v)

are algebraically independent over
\bar{k}
.

1.3.2.
Strategy of deriving Theorem
1.3.1
.

In order to apply the powerful machinery of Papanikolas’ theory on Grothendieck’s period conjecture in
[
P08
]
,
our initial step is to regard
v
as the “infinite place” of the projective
\frac{1}{v}
-line over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
More precisely, put
\tilde{v}:=\frac{1}{v}
.
Then
k
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{v})
, and

k_{v}
is a finite extension of the Laurent series field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\frac{1}{\tilde{v}})\!)
(which is the completion of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{v})
at the place
\infty_{\tilde{v}}
corresponding to
\frac{1}{\tilde{v}}
). So
k_{v}
is an ‘
\infty_{\tilde{v}}
-adic’ field.
Let
\iota:\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra isomorphism sending
t
to
\theta
, which extends to a field isomorphism
\iota:\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
.
Put
\tilde{T}_{v}:=\iota^{-1}(\tilde{v})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
.
Then
\iota
induces an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{v}]
. To apply Papanikolas’s theory
[
P08
]
for those special values in our
\infty_{\tilde{v}}
-adic fields, we make the following replacements

\theta\leftarrow\tilde{v},\quad{}t\leftarrow\tilde{T}_{v}

to switch
\overline{k}[t]
-modules (resp. dual
t
-motives) to

\overline{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-modules (resp. dual
\tilde{T}_{v}
-motives). A detailed description of how to view
v
-adic fields as
\infty_{\tilde{v}}
-adic fields is given in Sec.
5.1
.

Let
d:=\deg v
. Our proof of Theorem
1.3.1

is outlined in the following:

(1)

Define a “Carlitz-type” Hartl–Juschka module
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
(see Definition
6.1.3
), where the infinite products
\Omega_{d\ell,v,s}(v)
in Theorem
1.3.1
(replacing
\ell
by
d\ell
), for
0<s\leq d\ell
, show up as periods of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
(see Proposition
6.3.4
).

(2)

By Papanikolas’ theorem, determining the transcendence degree of the field generated by the periods of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
is equivalent to finding the dimension of its
\tilde{T}_{v}
-motivic Galois group
\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}
(see (
6.3.6
)).

(3)

The “CM structure” of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
provides an upper bound of
\dim\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}
(cf. (
5.2.6
)).
On the other hand, from the analysis of the Hodge–Pink weights of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
, we obtain sufficiently many “Hodge–Pink cocharacters” of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
, from which we get

\dim\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}=d^{2}\ell\quad(=[\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}(t):\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{T}_{v})]),

see Theorem
6.4.4
.

(4)

Therefore, the period interpretation of
\Omega_{d\ell,v,s}(v)
in (1) above for
0<s\leq d\ell
shows their algebraic independence over
k
(see Section
6.4
).
Finally, the result follows from the identity (see (
6.4.7
)):

\Omega_{\ell,v,s}(v)=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\Omega_{d\ell,v,j\ell+s}(v),\quad 0<s\leq\ell.

Remark 1.3.3
.

Put
\tilde{\theta}_{v}:=(\theta-\varepsilon)^{-1}
, and set

\tilde{\Omega}_{\ell}(x):=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{x}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}^{q^{\ell n}}})\quad\in k_{v}[\![x]\!],\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

When
\deg v=1
, we get
v=\theta-\varepsilon
, for which
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Therefore

\Omega_{\ell,v,s}(v)=\tilde{\Omega}_{\ell}^{(s)}(x)\big|_{x=\tilde{\theta}_{v}}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{\tilde{\theta}_{v}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}^{q^{\ell n+s}}}),

and Theorem
1.3.1
can be simply derived from the algebraic independence of periods and the quasi-periods of the Carlitz module of degree
\ell
in
[
CPTY10
]
(when replacing
\theta
to
\tilde{\theta}_{v}
).
However, when
\deg v=d>1
,
the infinite product
\Omega_{d\ell,v,s}(v)
, for
0<s\leq d\ell
, is actually the
s
th Frobenius twist of
\tilde{\Omega}_{d\ell}(x)
evaluated at
x=\tilde{\theta}_{v,s}:=(\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s}})^{-1}
:

\Omega_{d\ell,v,s}(v)=\tilde{\Omega}_{d\ell}^{(s)}(x)\big|_{x=\tilde{\theta}_{v,s}}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{\tilde{\theta}_{v,s}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}^{q^{d\ell n+s}}}).

Thus, to show the algebraic independence of
\Omega_{d\ell,v,s}(v)
is equivalent to determine the algebraic relations among the evaluations of the Frobenius twists of
\tilde{\Omega}_{d\ell}(x)
at
d
distinct points
x=\tilde{\theta}_{v,1},...,\tilde{\theta}_{v,d}
.
In order to overcome this issue,
we consider the “restriction of scalars” down to
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
, so that the periods of the Hartl–Juschka
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-module
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
do include all of these evaluations.

Together with Theorem
1.3.1
, the
v
-adic Chowla–Selberg formula in Theorem
1.2.1
enables us to construct sufficiently many algebraically independent elements in the field

\bar{k}\big(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\ \big|\ z\in\mathbb{Q}\text{ with }(q^{\ell}-1)z\in\mathbb{Z}\big).

As a consequence, we are able to illustrate the Lang–Rohrlich phenomenon for
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values in the next subsection.

1.4.
Algebraic relations among
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values

In the
\infty
-adic case, the special values
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},\infty}(z)
, for
z\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, are all transcendental over
k
by Thakur
[
T96
]
and Allouche
[
All96
]
. Furthermore, using transcendence theory in terms of
t
-motives developed by Anderson-Brownawell-Papanikolas
[
ABP04
]
and Papanikolas
[
P08
]
, the period interpretation of these special values implies that all algebraic relations among these special values are shown to follow from the standard monomial relations
[
CPTY10
]
. These relations come directly from the functional equations satisfied by
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},\infty}
. Note that
\mathbb{Q}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{q^{\ell}-1}\cdot\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular, one has:

\trdeg_{k}k\big(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},\infty}(z)\ \big|\ z\in\mathbb{Q}\text{ with }(q^{\ell}-1)z\in\mathbb{Z}\big)=\ell.

In the
v
-adic case, the functional equations of
\Gamma_{\text{ari},v}
derived in
[
Go88
]
and
[
T91
]
also give rise to standard monomial relations (
2.2.3
) among
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values at rational
p
-adic integers. However for the finite place
v
, one naturally encounters the Gross–Koblitz phenomenon, as shown by Thakur
[
T88
]
.
This gives additional relations among the special values in question (see Theorem
2.2.5
and Corollary
2.2.7
).
From the period interpretation of these
v
-adic values in Theorem
1.2.1
and the algebraic independence of the
v
-adic periods in Theorem
1.3.1
, we thereby obtain:

Theorem 1.4.1
.

(See Theorem
4.1.4
) Given a positive integer
\ell
, we have that

\trdeg_{\bar{k}}\bar{k}\big(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\ \big|\ z\in\mathbb{Q}\text{ with }(q^{\ell}-1)z\in\mathbb{Z}\big)=\ell-\gcd(\ell,\deg v).

Consequently, all the
\bar{k}
-algebraic relations among
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values at rational
p
-adic integers are explained by the functional equations in Proposition
2.2.1
, the standard monomial relations (
2.2.3
) and Thakur’s analogue of Gross–Koblitz formula.

The decrease of transcendence degree for
v
-adic special values resulting from the analogue of Gross-Koblitz formula is precisely given by
\gcd(\ell,\deg v)
.
So the naturally existing monomial relations indicate that the transcendence degree specified in Theorem

, see Theorem

.
The opposite inequality is assured by the algebraic independence of the

-adic crystalline–de Rham periods in Theorem

).
Also, the above theorem complements Thakur’s theorem (cf. Remark

):

Corollary 1.4.2
.

(See Corollary

.)
Given

with
\gcd(a,b)=1
and

, we have that

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(\frac{a}{b})\text{ is algebraic over $k$}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad b\text{ divides }q^{\deg v}-1.

Remark 1.4.3
.

In

2.
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this paper, we fix the following notation. Let
p
be a prime number, and let
q
be a power of
p
. Denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field of
q
elements. Let
\theta
be a variable and let
A:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
be the polynomial ring in
\theta
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with quotient field
k:=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\theta)
. For a monic irreducible polynomial
v\in A
, we still denote by
v
the corresponding finite place of
k
when it is clear from the context. The completion of
k
at
v
is denoted by
k_{v}
, and the residue field is denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{v}
.
In particular, we may regard
\mathbb{F}_{v}
as a subfield of
k_{v}
via the Teichmüller embedding, which sends
\theta\bmod v
to
\varepsilon:=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\theta^{q^{i\deg v}}
(see
[
La02
, Chap. XII Ex. 16]
), and

k_{v}
is then identified with the Laurant series field
\mathbb{F}_{v}(\!(\theta-\varepsilon)\!)
. Note that in the case
v=\theta
we have
\varepsilon=0
, otherwise
\varepsilon
generates all the roots of unity in the local field
k_{v}
.

Choose an algebraic closure
\bar{k}_{v}
of
k_{v}
, and denote by
\mathbb{C}_{v}
the completion of
\bar{k}_{v}
.
Let
\bar{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
in
\mathbb{C}_{v}
.
Let
A_{+}
be the set of all monic polynomials in
A
. Put
D_{0}:=1
, and for
i\geq 1
,
D_{i}:=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}(\theta^{q^{i}}-\theta^{q^{j}})\in A
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
A_{+,i}
the set of all monic polynomials of degree
i
, and note that
D_{i}=\prod_{a\in A_{+,i}}a
(see
[
Car35
, p. 140]
). Carlitz
[
Car35
]
introduced the so-called
Caritz factorials
as the following. For a non-negative integer
n
, we write
n=\sum_{i\geq 0}n_{i}q^{i}
for
0\leq n_{i}<q
and define

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari}}(n+1):=\prod_{i\geq 0}D_{i}^{n_{i}}\in A.

Note that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari}}(n+1)
is analogous to the classical Euler
\Gamma
-values
\Gamma(n+1)=n!
for
n\geq 0
as one has the property that (due to W. Sinnott, see
[
Go88
, Proposition 2.1.4]
)

(2.1.1)

{\rm{ord}}_{v}\left(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari}}(n+1)\right)=\sum_{e\geq 1}\lfloor\frac{n}{q^{e\deg v}}\rfloor,

where
q^{\deg v}
is the cardinality of
A/(v)
.

2.2.
Definition of
v
-adic arithmetic gamma function

Following Goss
[
Go80
,
Go88
]
, and Thakur
[
T91
]
, we consider the
v
-adic arithmetic gamma function that interpolates the Carlitz factorials
v
-adically as follows. For each
i\geq 0
, we define

D_{i,v}:=\prod a,

where
a
in the product above runs over all monic polynomial of degree
i
with
v\nmid a
. For any
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the ring of
p
-adic integers, we express
z
as
z=\sum_{i\geq 0}z_{i}q^{i}
for
0\leq z_{i}<q
and define

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z+1):=\prod_{i\geq 0}(-D_{i,v})^{z_{i}}.

It is shown in
[
Go80
, Appendix]
that the infinite product
\prod_{i\geq 0}(-D_{i,v})^{z_{i}}
converges in
k_{v}^{\times}
, and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow k_{v}^{\times}
is a continuous function satisfying the following functional equations:

Proposition 2.2.1
.

(See
[
T04
, Thm. 4.6.1, Lem. 4.6.2, and Thm. 4.6.3]
) Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible. For each
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we have

(1)

(Reflection)

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\cdot\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-z)=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(0).

(2)

(Translation)
When
z\neq 0
, put

r=\text{\rm ord}_{q}(z):=\max\big\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ \big|\ q^{m}|z\big\}.

Then

\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z+1)}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)}=-\frac{D_{r,v}}{\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}D_{i,v}^{q-1}}.

(3)

(Multiplication)
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
p\nmid n
, we have

\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z+\frac{i}{n})=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(nz)\cdot\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(0)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

As it is known that (see
[
T91
, Theorem 4.4]
)

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\frac{1}{q-1})^{q-1}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(0)=(-1)^{\deg v-1},

the square root of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(0)
on the right hand side of the equality in Proposition
2.2.1
(3) is chosen to be

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(0)^{\frac{1}{2}}:=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\frac{1}{q-1})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}.

When taking values at a rational
p
-adic integer
z
, one has the following standard monomial relations which directly come from the
q
-adic expansion of
z
:

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible.
Given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{0},...,c_{\ell-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq c_{0},...,c_{\ell-1}<q
, we have

(2.2.3)

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{c_{i}q^{i}}{q^{\ell}-1})=\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\frac{q^{i}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{c_{i}}.

Remark 2.2.4
.

Regarding
\bar{k}
-algebraic relations among
v
-adic arimetic gamma values at rational
p
-adic integers, in
[
T91
, Sec. 2]
Thakur showed that once we have the monomial relations (
2.2.3
) together with translation (2) of Proposition
2.2.1
(for
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cap\mathbb{Q}
), then the reflection (1) and multiplication (3) of Proposition
2.2.1
are valid for
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\cap\mathbb{Q}
.

Thakur’s analogue of the Gross–Koblitz formula in
[
T88
, Theorem VI]
implies:

Theorem 2.2.5
.

(See
[
T88
, Corollary of Thm. V]
.) Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible.
For each
z\in\mathbb{Q}
with
(q^{\deg v}-1)\cdot z\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\in\bar{k}^{\times}.

Remark 2.2.6
.

In fact, Thakur’s formula in
[
T88
, Theorem VI]
shows that when
z\in\mathbb{Q}
with
(q^{\deg v}-1)\cdot z\in\mathbb{Z}
, the special value
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)
is related to Thakur’s analogue of Gauss sums.
Consequently, in this case one knows that

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg v}}(\theta)\Big(\lambda_{v},\sqrt[q^{\deg v}-1]{-v}\Big),

where
\lambda_{v}
is a non-zero
Carlitz
v
-torsion
.

As a consequence of Thakur’s analogue of the Gross–Koblitz formula, we have the following monomial relations:

Corollary 2.2.7
.

Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible.
Given a positive integer
n
with
p\nmid n
, let
r
be the order of
q^{\deg v}\bmod n
in
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
For every
z\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfying
n\cdot z\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have that

\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(q^{i\deg v}\cdot z)\in\bar{k}^{\times}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
z=1-\frac{a}{n}
with
0\leq a<n
.
The assumption of
n
indicates that
n\mid q^{r\deg v}-1
.
Write
q^{r\deg v}-1=n\cdot n^{\prime}
.
Then there exist
z_{0},...,z_{r\deg v-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq z_{0},...,z_{r\deg v-1}<q
satisfying that

\frac{a}{n}=\frac{an^{\prime}}{q^{r\deg v}-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{r\deg v-1}\frac{z_{j}q^{j}}{q^{r\deg v}-1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(q^{i\deg v}\cdot z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(q^{i\deg v}\cdot(1-\frac{a}{n})\Big)

\displaystyle\sim\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\prod_{j=0}^{r\deg v-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\frac{z_{j}q^{j+i\deg v}}{q^{r\deg v}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\sim\prod_{j=0}^{r\deg v-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}q^{j+i\deg v}}{q^{r\deg v}-1}\Big)^{z_{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{r\deg v-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\frac{q^{j}}{q^{\deg v}-1}\Big)^{z_{j}}\quad\in\bar{k}^{\times}\quad\text{(by Theorem~\ref{thm: GK-formula})}.

Here, for
x,y\in\mathbb{C}_{v}^{\times}
, we denote by
x\sim y
if
x/y\in\bar{k}^{\times}
.
∎

Remark 2.2.8
.

Since when
n\mid(q^{\deg v}-1)
, the order of
q^{\deg v}\bmod n
in
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is
1
.
Therefore Corollary
2.2.7
is in fact equivalent to Theorem
2.2.5
.

The above monomial relations among special values of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}
at rational
p
-adic integers give rise to the following upper bound of the transcendence degree in question.

Theorem 2.2.9
.

Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible.
Given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\trdeg_{\bar{k}}\bar{k}\Big(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\ \Big|\ z\in\mathbb{Q}\text{ with }(q^{\ell}-1)\cdot z\in\mathbb{Z}\Big)\leq\ell-\gcd(\ell,\deg v).

Proof.

The functional equation in Proposition
2.2.1
(2) and the monomial relation in Proposition
2.2.2
show that

\bar{k}\Big(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(z)\ \Big|\ z\in\mathbb{Q}\text{ with }(q^{\ell}-1)\cdot z\in\mathbb{Z}\Big)=\bar{k}\Big(\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\frac{q^{i}}{q^{\ell}-1})\ \Big|\ 0\leq i\leq\ell-1\Big).

Let
m=\gcd(\ell,\deg v)
and
\ell^{\prime}=\ell/m
.
By Proposition
2.2.2
and Theorem
2.2.5
, we get

(2.2.10)

\prod_{j=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\frac{q^{i+mj}}{q^{\ell}-1})=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\frac{q^{i}}{q^{m}-1})\in\bar{k},\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{Z},\ 0\leq i<m,

since
(q^{m}-1)
divides
(q^{\deg v}-1)
.
The monomial relations (
2.2.10
) for
0\leq i\leq m-1
give rise to
m
independent algebraic relations, from which the result follows.
∎

We will prove in Section
4
that the inequality stated in Theorem
2.2.9
is, in fact, an equality.
Before doing so, we first provide “period interpretations” of the
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values in question.

2.3.
Product expansions of
v
-adic arithmetic gamma values

We begin with the following observation:

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible of degree
d
.
Given
\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
n_{s}
be the smallest non-negative integer such that
s+n_{s}\ell\geq d
.
We have that

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s}}{q^{\ell}-1})}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s-1}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=n_{s}+1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell-d}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell-d}}}\bigg)}\cdot\bigg(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}}(\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}-\theta)\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 79.6678pt\cdot\begin{cases}(\theta^{q^{s+n_{s}\ell-d}}-\theta)^{-1},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell>d$,}\\
v^{-1},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell=d$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

From the definition of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}
, we get

\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s}}{q^{\ell}-1})}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s-1}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{q}}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{s+n\ell,v}}{D_{s-1+n\ell,v}^{q}}.

Note that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, one has that

\frac{D_{n}}{D_{n-1}^{q}}=(\theta^{q^{n}}-\theta)\quad\text{ and }\quad D_{n,v}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{D_{n}}{v^{q^{n-d}}D_{n-d}},&\text{ if $n\geq d$,}\\
D_{n},&\text{ if $n<d$.}\end{cases}

Thus

(2.3.2)

\frac{D_{n,v}}{D_{n-1,v}^{q}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{q^{n}}-\theta}{\theta^{q^{n-d}}-\theta}&\text{ if $n>d$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{q^{n}}-\theta}{v},&\text{ if $n=d$,}\\
\theta^{q^{n}}-\theta,&\text{ if $0<n<d$.}\end{cases}

Observe that:

(2.3.3)

\theta^{q^{n}}-\theta=(\varepsilon^{q^{n}}-\theta)\cdot\Big(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{n}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{n}}}\Big)

and

(2.3.4)

\frac{\theta^{q^{n}}-\theta}{\theta^{q^{n-d}}-\theta}=\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{n}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{n}}}\bigg)\bigg/\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{n-d}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{n}}}\bigg)\quad\text{ when $n>d$.}

As
n_{s}
is the smallest non-negative integer such that
s+n_{s}\ell\geq d
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s}}{q^{\ell}-1})}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s-1}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{q}}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{s+n\ell,v}}{D_{s-1+n\ell,v}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{n=n_{s}+1}^{\infty}\frac{D_{s+n\ell,v}}{D_{s-1+n\ell,v}^{q}}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}-1}\frac{D_{s+n\ell,v}}{D_{s-1+n\ell,v}^{q}}\right)\cdot\frac{D_{s+n_{s}\ell,v}}{D_{s-1+n_{s}\ell,v}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{n=n_{s}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{q^{s+n\ell}}-\theta}{\theta^{q^{s+n\ell-d}}-\theta}\right)\cdot\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}-1}(\theta^{q^{s+n\ell}}-\theta)\right)\cdot\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{q^{s+n_{s}\ell}}-\theta}{\theta^{q^{s+n_{s}\ell-d}}-\theta},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell>d$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{q^{s+n_{s}\ell}}-\theta}{v},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell=d$}\end{cases}

(by (
2.3.2
))

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{n=n_{s}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\displaystyle\frac{(\theta-\epsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\epsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}}{1-\displaystyle\frac{(\theta-\epsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell-d}}}{\theta-\epsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}}\right)\cdot\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}}(\epsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}-\theta)(1-\frac{(\theta-\epsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\epsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}})

\displaystyle\cdot\begin{cases}(\theta^{q^{s+n_{s}\ell-d}}-\theta)^{-1},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell>d$},\\
v^{-1},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell=d$}\end{cases}\hskip 142.26378pt\text{(by \eqref{E:th^{q^n}-th} \& \eqref{E:(th^{q^{n}}-th)/(th^{q^{n-d}}-th)})}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=n_{s}+1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell-d}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\bigg(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}}(\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}-\theta)\bigg)\begin{cases}(\theta^{q^{s+n_{s}\ell-d}}-\theta)^{-1},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell>d$,}\\
v^{-1},&\text{ if $s+n_{s}\ell=d$,}\end{cases}

where
\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}-1}\frac{D_{s+n\ell,v}}{D_{s-1+n\ell,v}^{q}}
occurring in the second identity and
\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}-1}(\theta^{q^{s+n\ell}}-\theta)
occurring in the third identity are referred to be
1
in the case when
n_{s}=0
, the forth and fifth identities come from (
2.3.4
) and (
2.3.3
) respectively.
∎

Consequently, for each
z\in\mathbb{Q}
, let
\langle z\rangle_{\ari}\in\mathbb{Q}
be the fractional part of
z
, i.e.
0\leq\langle z\rangle_{\ari}<1
and
z-\langle z\rangle_{\ari}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We get:

Corollary 2.3.5
.

Let
v\in A_{+}
be irreducible of degree
d
.
Given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
with
0\leq s<\ell
, write
s+d=s_{0}+n_{s}^{\prime}\ell
where
s_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
0\leq s_{0}<\ell
and
n_{s}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\Big\langle\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+d}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big\rangle_{\ari}\Big)}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\Big\langle\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+d-1}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big\rangle_{\ari}\Big)^{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}^{\prime}-1}(\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}-\theta)\right)\cdot\frac{\alpha_{s_{0}}}{\alpha_{s}}\cdot C_{s}^{-1},

where

(2.3.6)

\alpha_{s}:=\begin{cases}1-\displaystyle\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s}}},&\text{ if $s>0$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta-\varepsilon},&\text{ if $s=0$,}\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad C_{s}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if $s>0$,}\\
-v,&\text{ if $s=0$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall that
n_{s_{0}}
denotes the smallest non-negative integer so that
s_{0}+n_{s_{0}}\ell\geq d
.
Then
n_{s}^{\prime}\geq n_{s_{0}}
and

0\leq(n_{s}^{\prime}-n_{s_{0}})\ell=(s_{0}+n_{s}^{\prime}\ell)-(s_{0}+n_{s_{0}}\ell)\leq s+d-d=s<\ell.

Thus
n_{s}^{\prime}=n_{s_{0}}
and
s_{0}+n_{s_{0}}\ell-d=s
.

Suppose
s_{0}>0
.
Then

\displaystyle\ \frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\Big\langle\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+d}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big\rangle_{\ari}\Big)}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\Big\langle\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+d-1}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big\rangle_{\ari}\Big)^{q}}=\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s_{0}}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big)}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}\Big(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s_{0}-1}}{q^{\ell}-1}\Big)^{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\bigg(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}^{\prime}}(\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}-\theta)\bigg)\begin{cases}(\theta^{q^{s}}-\theta)^{-1},&\text{ if $s>0$,}\\
v^{-1},&\text{ if $s=0$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\displaystyle\alpha_{s_{0}}\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot(\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s}})\cdot\bigg(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}^{\prime}-1}(\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}-\theta)\bigg)\begin{cases}(\theta-\theta^{q^{s}})^{-1},&\text{ if $s>0$,}\\
(-v)^{-1},&\text{ if $s=0$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}^{\prime}-1}(\varepsilon^{q^{s_{0}+n\ell}}-\theta)\right)\cdot\frac{\alpha_{s_{0}}}{\alpha_{s}}\cdot C_{s}^{-1}.

When
s_{0}=0
, one has that
n_{s}^{\prime}=n_{s_{0}}=n_{0}>0
and
n_{\ell}=n_{0}-1
(as
s+d=0+n_{0}\ell=\ell+(n_{0}-1)\ell
).
Then

\displaystyle\ \frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+d}}{q^{\ell}-1}\right\rangle)}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\left\langle\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+d-1}}{q^{\ell}-1}\right\rangle)^{q}}=\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{1}{q^{\ell}-1})}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{\ell-1}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{q}}=(-1)\cdot\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{\ell}}{q^{\ell}-1})}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{\ell-1}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{\ell+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{\ell+n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\bigg(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{\ell}}(\varepsilon^{q^{\ell+n\ell}}-\theta)\bigg)\cdot(-1)\cdot\begin{cases}(\theta^{q^{s}}-\theta)^{-1},&\text{ if $s>0$,}\\
v^{-1},&\text{ if $s=0$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\bigg(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{0}-1}(\varepsilon^{q^{n\ell}}-\theta)\bigg)\cdot\frac{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+d}}}{\theta-\varepsilon}\cdot\begin{cases}(\theta-\theta^{q^{s}})^{-1},&\text{ if $s>0$,}\\
(-v)^{-1},&\text{ if $s=0$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{n\ell}}}\bigg)}{\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell}}}\bigg)}\cdot\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{s}^{\prime}-1}(\varepsilon^{q^{n\ell}}-\theta)\right)\cdot\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\alpha_{s}}\cdot C_{s}^{-1}.

Therefore the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.3.7
.

Let notation be given as above.
Suppose
\ell\geq d
.
Since

\displaystyle=(-D_{s,v})\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+\ell-j}}{q^{\ell}-1})}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{ari},v}(1-\displaystyle\frac{q^{s+\ell-1-j}}{q^{\ell}-1})^{q}}\right)^{q^{j}}

\displaystyle=D_{s,v}^{1-q^{\ell}}\cdot(-D_{s+\ell,v})\cdot\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1-\frac{\displaystyle(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell-j}}}{\displaystyle\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell-j}}}}{\displaystyle 1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{s+n\ell-j-d}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{s+n\ell-j}}}}\right)^{q^{j}}.

In particular, when

3.1.

For each (commutative)
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Definition 3.1.1
.

\tau

\tau\cdot(m_{0},m_{1},\cdots,m_{\ell-1})=\big((t-\theta)\cdot m_{\ell-1}^{(1)},m_{0}^{(1)},\cdots,m_{\ell-2}^{(1)}\big).

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
d
and
\ell
be two positive integers.
For every integer
i

Also, we take
i_{0}
to be the unique integer with

Lemma 3.1.4
.

Let notation be given as above. Then we have

\displaystyle\tau^{d}\cdot(m_{0},...,m_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle=(m_{0}^{\prime},...,m_{\ell-1}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\text{ where }m_{i}^{\prime}=\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{i}-1}(t-\theta^{q^{i+n\ell}})\right)\cdot m_{i_{1}}^{(d)},\quad\text{ for }0\leq i<\ell.

Proof.

For each integer
i
with
0\leq i<\ell
,
we write
i+d=i_{0}+n_{i}^{\prime}\ell
for unique integers
i_{0}
and
n_{i}^{\prime}
with
0\leq i_{0}<\ell
.
Then

m_{i_{0}}^{\prime}=\left(\prod_{n=0}^{n_{i}^{\prime}-1}(t-\theta^{q^{i_{0}+n\ell}})\right)\cdot m_{i}^{(d)}.

Based on Definition
3.1.3
, as
n_{i}^{\prime}\geq n_{i_{0}}
and

d\leq i_{0}+n_{i_{0}}\ell\leq i_{0}+n_{i}^{\prime}\ell=i+d<\ell+d,

we get

0\leq n_{i}^{\prime}\ell-n_{i_{0}}\ell<(\ell+d)-(d)=\ell,

whence
n_{i}^{\prime}=n_{i_{0}}
.
Hence
i=i_{0}+n_{i_{0}}\ell-d
=
(i_{0})_{1}
and the result holds.
∎

Remark 3.4.2

Lemma 3.4.3

4.1.

For every integer

\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]

\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{t}_{v}][\frac{1}{S}],

.
Set

(1)

\tilde{\theta}_{v,i}:=\iota(\tilde{t}_{v,i})=(\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{-i}})^{-1}

(3)

\tilde{T}_{v}=\tilde{t}_{v,0}\cdots\tilde{t}_{v,d-1}

(5.1.6)

5.2.
Hartl–Juschka Category

The Hartl–Juschka category over
\bar{k}
is defined as follows:
an object of
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}
is a free left
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-module
\mathscr{M}
of finite rank together with an invertible
\sigma
-action on
\mathit{M}:=\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{M}
so that
\mathit{M}
becomes a left
\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})[\sigma]
-module satisfying that there exists a sufficiently large integer
n
so that

(\tilde{T}_{v}-\iota(\tilde{T}_{v}))^{n}\mathscr{M}\subseteq\sigma\mathscr{M}\subseteq(\tilde{T}_{v}-\iota(\tilde{T}_{v}))^{-n}\mathscr{M}\quad(\subset\mathit{M}).

We call such an object
\mathscr{M}
a
Hartl–Juschka module over
\bar{k}
.
In particular, we say that
\mathscr{M}
is
effective
if
\sigma\mathscr{M}\subset\mathscr{M}
.
A morphism
f:\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}
is a left
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-module homomorphism so that the induced
\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})
-linear homomorphism
f:\mathit{M}=\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathit{M}^{\prime}=\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
satisfies

f(\sigma m)=\sigma f(m),\quad\forall\,m\in\mathit{M}.

Given two objects
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}
, the set
\Hom(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M}^{\prime})
of morphisms from
\mathscr{M}
to
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
has a natural
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-module structure, and elements in

\Hom^{0}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M}^{\prime}):=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{T}_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\Hom(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M}^{\prime})

are called
quasi-morphisms from
\mathscr{M}
to
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
.
The
Hartl-Juschka Category
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}
up to isogeny
has the same objects in
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}
but the morphisms in
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}
are given by quasi-morphisms.

5.2.1.
Uniformizability.

Let
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}
be the Tate algebra in the variable
\tilde{T}_{v}
over
\mathbb{C}_{v}
, i.e.

\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}:=\Big\{\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\tilde{T}_{v}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{v}[\![\tilde{T}_{v}]\!]\ \Big|\ \lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}|a_{i}|_{v}=0\Big\}.

We let
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\sigma]
be the non-commutative algebra generated by
\sigma
over
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}
subject to the relation

\sigma f=f^{(-1)}\sigma,\quad\forall\,f\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\},

where for every
f=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\tilde{T}_{v}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}
, we define

f^{(n)}:=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}^{q^{n}}\tilde{T}_{v}^{i},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Given an object
\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}
, we set

\mathbb{M}:=\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{M}
.
As
\tilde{T}_{v}-\iota(\tilde{T}_{v})=\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v}
is invertible in
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}
,
one has that
\mathit{M}\subset\mathbb{M}
, and

\mathbb{M}
becomes a left
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\sigma]
-module with the
\sigma
-action given by

\sigma\cdot(f\otimes m):=f^{(-1)}\otimes\sigma m,\quad\forall f\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\text{ and }m\in\mathscr{M}.

Put

H_{\mathrm{Betti}}(\mathscr{M}):=\{m\in\mathbb{M}\mid\sigma m=m\}.

We call
\mathscr{M}

uniformizable
(or
rigid analytically trivial
) if the natural homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}H_{\mathrm{Betti}}(\mathscr{M})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{M}

is an isomorphism.
In particular, let
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}^{\dagger}
be the algebra of rigid-analytic functions on
\mathbb{C}_{v}\setminus\{\tilde{v}^{q^{n}}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
It is known that (see
[
HaJu20
, Proposition 2.4.27]
) if
\mathscr{M}
is uniformizable, then
H_{\mathrm{Betti}}(\mathscr{M})
is contained in
\mathbb{M}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}^{\dagger}\otimes_{\bar{k}[t]}\mathscr{M}
, and the natural homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}^{\dagger}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}H_{\text{Betti}}(\mathscr{M})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{M}^{\dagger}

is an isomorphism.

5.2.2.
\tilde{T}_{v}
-Motivic Galois group.

The full subcategory of
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}
consisting of all uniformizable objects is denoted by
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}_{U}
.
Let
\mathbf{Vect}_{/\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{T}_{v})}
be the category of finite dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{T}_{v})
.
By

is a neutral Tannakian category over

.
We call

the

-motivic Galois group of

. Note that we have the following property of

by Papanikolas’ difference Galois theory
[
P08
]
.

Given an object
\mathscr{M}\in\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}_{U}

over

Then the action of

\sigma\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}\\

Since

P08

there exists a matrix

In
[
P08
]
, Papanikolas developed a difference Galois theory for the system of Frobenius difference equation (

is referred to be the field generated all entries of

over
\overline{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})

\dim\Gamma_{\mathscr{M}}={\rm{tr.deg}}_{\overline{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})}\overline{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})(\Psi)

Note that we have a faithful representation

5.2.7.
de Rham pairing and the Grothendieck period conjecture.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be an object of
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}_{U}
.
Recall that the
de Rham module of
\mathscr{M}
over
\bar{k}

is

H_{\text{dR}}(\mathscr{M},\bar{k}):=\Hom_{\bar{k}}\left(\frac{\mathscr{M}}{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\mathscr{M}},\bar{k}\right).

In particular, every differential
\omega\in H_{\text{dR}}(\mathscr{M},\bar{k})
can be extended to a
\mathbb{C}_{v}
-linear functional on

\mathbb{M}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}^{\dagger}\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{M}
vanishing on
(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\mathbb{M}^{\dagger}
.
As the uniformizability of

ensures the inclusion

,
we have the following

(5.2.8)

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{Betti}}(\mathscr{M})\times H_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathscr{M},\bar{k})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{v},

\displaystyle(\gamma,\omega)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\int_{\gamma}\omega:=\omega(\gamma).

The primary tool in this paper of dealing with transcendence problems is the following function field version of Grothendieck’s period conjecture, which was achieved by Papanikolas
[
P08
]
using the so-called ABP criterion
[
ABP04
]
.

Theorem 5.2.9
.

(See
[
P08
]
and its refined version in
[
C09
]
.)
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a uniformizable Hartl–Juschka module
\mathscr{M}
over
\bar{k}
.
Suppose that
\mathscr{M}
is effective.
Then we have that

\dim\Gamma_{\textrm{$\mathscr{M}$}}=\trdeg_{\bar{k}}\bar{k}\left(\int_{\gamma}\omega\ \bigg|\ \gamma\in H_{\mathrm{Betti}}(\mathscr{M}),\ \omega\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathscr{M},\bar{k})\right).

Proof.

Let
\Phi\in\Mat_{r}(\overline{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}])
be the matrix represented by the
\sigma
-action on
\mathscr{M}
with respect to a
\overline{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-basis
\left\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\right\}
.
Since
\mathscr{M}
is uniformizable, there exists
\Psi\in\GL_{r}(\mathbb{C}_{v}\left\{\tilde{T}_{v}\right\})
for which
\Psi^{(-1)}=\Phi\Psi
. Put
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})^{\rm{tr}}
, and note that the entries of
\Psi^{-1}\mathbf{m}
comprises an
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-basis of
H_{\mathrm{Betti}}(\mathscr{M})
(see
[
ABP04
, Lem. 4.4.12]
for their arguments without the condition that
\mathscr{M}
is free of finite rank over
\overline{k}[\sigma]

As mentioned above that

, elements of
\Psi^{-1}
are in fact in
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}^{\dagger}
.

Our hypothesis on
\det\Phi
ensures that the conditions of the refined version of the ABP criterion is satisfied (see [C09]), and so we have

{\rm{tr.deg}}_{\overline{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})}\overline{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})(\Psi)={\rm{tr.deg}}_{\overline{k}}\overline{k}(\Psi|_{\tilde{T}_{v}=\tilde{v}})={\rm{tr.deg}}_{\overline{k}}\overline{k}(\Psi^{-1}|_{\tilde{T}_{v}=\tilde{v}}),

\overline{k}(\Psi|_{\tilde{T}_{v}=\tilde{v}})
(resp.

\overline{k}
generated by all entries of
\Psi
(resp. of

) evaluated at

. It follows that by (
5.2.4
), we have

(5.2.10)

\dim\Gamma_{\mathscr{M}}={\rm{tr.deg}}_{\overline{k}}\overline{k}(\Psi^{-1}|_{\tilde{T}_{v}=\tilde{v}}).

As each

-linear functional on
\mathscr{M}

5.3.

Given an object
\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{H}\mathscr{J}^{I}_{U}
, we take a sufficiently large integer
n

(5.3.1)

(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})^{n}\mathscr{M}\subseteq\sigma\mathscr{M}\subseteq(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})^{-n}\mathscr{M}.

Let
r
be the rank of
\mathscr{M}
over
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
.
There exists a unique (non-ordered)
r
-tuple of integers
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{r})
so that

\frac{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})^{-n}\mathscr{M}}{\sigma\mathscr{M}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})^{n-w_{i}}\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}.

We call
(w_{1},\dots,w_{r})
the

\mathscr{M}
and mention that they are independent of the choices of

Let
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}\}
be a basis of the free
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]

\mathscr{M}
so that

\sigma\mathscr{M}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})^{-w_{i}}\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]\cdot m_{i}\quad\subset\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{M}.

Put

H_{\mathscr{M},\mathbb{C}_{v}}:=\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\tilde{v}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}\tilde{T}_{v},\ \mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}{\otimes}H_{\mathscr{M}},

and let

through the following identification

Then we have that:

Proposition 5.3.3
.

Keep the notation as above.

6.
Carlitz-type Hartl–Juschka modules and their periods

Throughout this section,
we fix an irreducible element
v\in A_{+}
of degree
d
as before and fix a positive integer
\ell
.
Having sufficient tools at hand,
we are now able to interpret
\Omega_{d\ell,v,i}(v)
for
0<i\leq d\ell
(occuring in Theorem
4.1.2
) as the periods of the restriction of scalars of the “Carlitz-type” Hartl–Juschka modules,
and establish their algebraic independence in this section.

6.1.
Carlitz-type Hartl–Juschka modules and restriction of scalars

Definition 6.1.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}

be the left
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma^{d\ell}]
-module
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]
whose
\sigma^{d\ell}
-action is given by

\sigma^{d\ell}\cdot m=(1-\frac{\tilde{t}_{v}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}})\cdot m^{(-d\ell)},\quad\forall m\in\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}].

Remark 6.1.2
.

By (
5.1.3
) and (
5.1.4
), one has that

\tilde{T}_{v}\equiv\tilde{v}\bmod(\tilde{t}_{v}-\tilde{\theta}_{v})\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}],

for which we get

(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}\subset\sigma^{d\ell}\cdot\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}\subset\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}.

By (
5.1.5
), we have

\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{S^{n}}\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{t}_{v}]
is not finitely generated as a
\bar{k}[\tilde{t}_{v}]
-module if
d>1
.
Since
\frac{1}{S^{n}}\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{t}_{v}]\subset\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}
is invariant by
\sigma^{d\ell}
-action for every non-negative integer
n
, we see that
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}
is not finitely generated as a
\bar{k}[\sigma^{d\ell}]
-module when
d>1
(i.e.
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}
is not an ‘Anderson dual
\tilde{T}_{v}
-motive’ in the terminology of
[
ABP04
]
).

Definition 6.1.3
.

The restriction of scalars of
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}
is the following
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma]
-module:

\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}):=\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma]\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma^{d\ell}]}\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}.

Decomposing

\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
into

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell},

we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})\supset\sigma\cdot\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})

\displaystyle=\left((1-\frac{\tilde{t}_{v}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}})\cdot 1\otimes\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}

\displaystyle\supset(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}).

Thus
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
is an effective Hartl–Juschka module
\bar{k}
.

We see from the following proposition that
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
has ‘complex multiplication’ by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]
.

Proposition 6.1.4
.

Let notation be given as above. Then we have the following
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-algebra embedding:

\phi:=\big(\alpha\mapsto\phi_{\alpha}\big):\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]\hookrightarrow\End_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma]}(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})),

where

\phi_{\alpha}:=\big(\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes m_{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes\alpha m_{i}\big).

Proof.

From the definition of
\phi
, one sees that
\phi
is a ring homomorphism. We confirm that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]
,
\phi_{\alpha}
commutes with
\sigma
as follows:

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}\circ\sigma\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes m_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\phi_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-2}\sigma^{i+1}\otimes m_{i}+1\otimes\sigma^{d\ell}m_{d\ell-1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-2}\sigma^{i+1}\otimes\alpha m_{i}+1\otimes\alpha\sigma^{d\ell}m_{d\ell-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-2}\sigma^{i+1}\otimes\alpha m_{i}+1\otimes\sigma^{d\ell}\alpha m_{d\ell-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt=\sigma\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes\alpha m_{i}\right)=\sigma\circ\phi_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes m_{i}\right).

∎

Remark 6.1.5
.

Alternatively, we may regard
\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}
as a
(\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma^{d\ell}],\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}])
-bimodule, and get the
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-algebra homomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]\hookrightarrow\End_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\sigma]}(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}))
explicitly described in Proposition
6.1.4
.

6.1.6.
Explicit descriptions of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
.

Let
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]
be the polynomial ring with variable
X
over
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
, and let
\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}(X)
be the minimal polynomial of
\tilde{t}_{v}
over
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
, i.e., by (
5.1.4
), we have

\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}(X)=X^{d}-(\tilde{\epsilon}_{d-1}\tilde{T}_{v})X^{d-1}-\cdots-(\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}\tilde{T}_{v})X-\tilde{T}_{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]\subset\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X].

Then
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]
can be identified with

\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}.

We extend the Frobenius twists by fixing
X
to
\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})[X]
as follows: for each
f(X)=\sum_{m}f_{m}X^{m}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}

f^{(n)}(X):=\sum_{m}f_{m}^{(n)}X^{m}.

We point out that when

, i.e.
f_{m}\in\bar{k}
for all

, one has that
f^{(n)}(X)=\sum_{m}f_{m}^{q^{n}}X^{m}
.
In the decomposition

\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\sigma^{i}\otimes\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell},

for each
i
with
0\leq i<d\ell
we have the

-linear isomorphism

\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-i)}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma^{i}\otimes\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}=\sigma^{i}\otimes\left(\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}\right)

defined by

\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}f_{m}X^{m}\right)\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-i)}(X)\longmapsto\sigma^{i}\otimes\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}f_{m}^{(i)}X^{m}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}(X)\right).

Therefore:

Proposition 6.1.7
.

We may identify
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
with the product space

\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(0)}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}\times\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-1)}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}\times\cdots\times\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(1-d\ell)}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]},

equipped with the
\sigma
-action given by:

\displaystyle\sigma\cdot(h_{0}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(0)},h_{1}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-1)},\cdots,h_{d\ell-1}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(1-d\ell)})

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt:=\left((1-\frac{X}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}})\cdot h_{d\ell-1}^{(-1)}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(0)},h_{0}^{(-1)}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-1)},\cdots,h_{d\ell-2}^{(-1)}\bmod\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(1-d)}\right).

Remark 6.1.8
.

As
\tilde{t}_{v,i}
is a root of
\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-i)}(X)
for each integer
i
with

, one has that

\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}]\cong\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}{\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-i)}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]}.

The above proposition allows us to identify
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
with

\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,0}]\times\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,1}]\times\cdots\times\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}],

and the
\sigma
-action is given by: for every polynomials
h_{0}(X),...,h_{d\ell-1}(X)\in\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][X]
,

\displaystyle\sigma\cdot\Big(h_{0}(\tilde{t}_{v,0}),h_{1}(\tilde{t}_{v,1}),\cdots,h_{d\ell-1}(\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\Big((1-\frac{\tilde{t}_{v,0}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}})\cdot h_{d\ell-1}^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,0}),h_{0}^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,1}),\cdots,h_{d\ell-2}^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1})\Big).

6.2.
Uniformizability

Recall that
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}
is the Tate algebra with variable
\tilde{T}_{v}
over
\mathbb{C}_{v}
.
Denote by
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v}\}
the Tate algebra with variable
\tilde{t}_{v}
over
\mathbb{C}_{v}
, i.e.

\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v}\}:=\Big\{\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\tilde{t}_{v}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{v}[\![\tilde{t}_{v}]\!]\ \Big|\ \lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}|a_{i}|_{v}=0\Big\}.

Put

\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v}]=\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}].

Lemma 6.2.1
.

We have the following inclusion

\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v}\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v}].

Proof.

For each non-negative integer
i
, by (
5.1.4
) there exist
f_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
,
0\leq j<d
, with
\deg_{\tilde{T}_{i}}f_{i,j}\leq i
such that

\tilde{t}_{v}^{i}=f_{i,0}\tilde{t}_{v}^{d-1}+\cdots+f_{i,d-1}.

Write
f_{i,j}(\tilde{T}_{v})=\sum_{s=0}^{i}u_{i,j,s}\tilde{T}_{v}^{s}
where
u_{i,j,s}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}
.
Then for each
f=\sum_{i\geq 0}a_{i}\tilde{t}_{v}^{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v}\}
, the property
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}|a_{i}|_{v}=0
implies that for
0\leq j<d
and
s\geq 0
,

b_{j,s}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=s}^{n}a_{i}u_{i,j,s}\quad\text{ converges absolutely in $\mathbb{C}_{v}$ with }\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}b_{j,s}=0.

Put

\displaystyle f_{j}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s\geq 0}b_{j,s}\tilde{T}_{v}^{s}=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=s}^{\infty}a_{i}u_{i,j,s}\right)\tilde{T}_{v}^{s}

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{i}u_{i,j,s}\tilde{T}_{v}^{s}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}f_{i,j}(\tilde{T}_{v})\quad\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}.

We then get

f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\tilde{t}_{v}^{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}f_{i,j}\tilde{t}_{v}^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}f_{j}\cdot\tilde{t}_{v}^{j}\quad\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v}]

as desired.
∎

Remark 6.2.2
.

The inclusion
\bar{k}(\tilde{T}_{v})\subset\bar{k}(\tilde{t}_{v})
induces a finite morphism
\phi
from the projective
\tilde{t}_{v}
-line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\tilde{t}_{v}}
onto the projective
\tilde{T}_{v}
-line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}
(over
\bar{k}
).
In particular,
\bar{k}[\tilde{t}_{v}]
and
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]
are the affine coordinate rings of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tilde{t}_{v}}
and
U:=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tilde{t}_{v}}\setminus\phi^{-1}(\tilde{v})
, respectively.
Since
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v}\}
and
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v}]
correspond to the the affinoid domains
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tilde{t}_{v},\leq 1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tilde{t}_{v}}(\mathbb{C}_{v})\mid|x|_{v}\leq 1\}
and
U_{\leq 1}:=\{x\in U(\mathbb{C}_{v})\mid|x|_{v}\leq 1\}
, respectively, the natural inclusion
U_{\leq 1}\subset\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\tilde{t}_{v},\leq 1}
alternatively implies
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v}\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v}]
.

To show the uniformizability of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
, we first note that the identification of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})

extends to

(6.2.3)

\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})=\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,0}]\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,1}]\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}].

In particular, Lemma
6.2.1
implies that

(6.2.4)

\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,0}\}\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,1}\}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}).

In particular,
for every
\big(h_{0}(\tilde{t}_{v,0}),...,h_{d\ell-1}(\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1})\big)\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,0}\}\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,1}\}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}\}

one has that

\displaystyle\sigma\cdot\Big(h_{0}(\tilde{t}_{v,0}),h_{1}(\tilde{t}_{v,1}),\cdots,h_{d\ell-1}(\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1})\Big)

(6.2.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\Big((1-\frac{\tilde{t}_{v,0}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}})\cdot h_{d\ell-1}^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,0}),h_{0}^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,1}),\cdots,h_{d\ell-2}^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1})\Big),

and for

Here

if

or
0

Note that since

,

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,0}\}\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,1}\}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}\}.

Then

Lemma 6.2.6
.

Via the decomposition (

), we have that

H_{\text{\rm Betti}}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})\big)=\bigoplus_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]\psi_{i,j}.

\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})

Proof.

\psi^{(-d\ell)}(\tilde{t}_{v})=(1-\frac{\tilde{t}_{v,0}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}})^{-1}\cdot\psi(\tilde{t}_{v}).

) we see that

and

.

\rank_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})=d^{2}\ell

generates
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\otimes_{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
over
\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}
.
First, from the isomorphism

\longmapsto

,

\sum_{m=0}^{d\ell-1}\alpha_{i,m}\xi^{mq^{-i^{\prime}}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if $i^{\prime}=i$,}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Put

\psi_{i,j}^{\prime}:=\sum_{m=0}^{d\ell-1}\alpha_{i,m}\psi_{m,j}=\Big(0,...,\tilde{t}_{v,i}^{j}\psi^{(-i)}(\tilde{t}_{v,i}),0,...,0\Big).

We then obtain that

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\cdot\psi_{i,j}\ =\ \sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}\cdot\psi^{\prime}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Big(\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,0}]\cdot\psi(\tilde{t}_{v,0})\Big)\times\Big(\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,1}]\cdot\psi^{(-1)}(\tilde{t}_{v,1})\Big)\times\cdots\times\Big(\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}]\cdot\psi^{(1-d\ell)}(\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,0}]\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,1}]\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}],

where the last equality comes from the observation:

\psi^{(-i)}(\tilde{t}_{v,i})\in\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{t}_{v,i}\}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{C}_{v}\{\tilde{T}_{v}\}[\tilde{t}_{v,i}]^{\times},\quad 0\leq i<d\ell.

This completes the proof.
∎

.

Since
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})

has Complex Multiplication by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]

as an

-module.

6.3.
Periods of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})

Recall that the
de Rham module of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
over
\bar{k}

is

H_{\text{dR}}(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\bar{k}):=\Hom_{\bar{k}}\left(\frac{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})},\bar{k}\right),

and the de Rham pairing is given by (see (
5.2.8
))

H_{\text{Betti}}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\text{$\tilde{\mathscr{C}}$}_{d\ell})\big)\times H_{\text{dR}}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\text{$\tilde{\mathscr{C}}$}_{d\ell}),\bar{k}\big)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{v},\quad(\gamma,\omega)\mapsto\int_{\gamma}\omega:=\omega(\gamma).

In particular, for each integer
i
with
0\leq i<d\ell
,
put

(6.3.1)

\tilde{g}_{v,i}(X):=\tilde{g}_{\tilde{T}_{v}}^{(-i)}(X)\big|_{\tilde{T}_{v}=\tilde{v}}=X^{d}-(\tilde{\epsilon}_{d-1}^{q^{(-i)}}\tilde{v})X^{d-1}-\cdots-(\tilde{\epsilon}_{1}^{q^{(-i)}}\tilde{v})X-\tilde{v}\in\bar{k}[X].

The identification in Proposition
6.1.7
leads to the following isomorphism:

(6.3.2)

\frac{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}\cong\prod_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\frac{\bar{k}[X]}{\tilde{g}_{v,i}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[X]}.

Moreover, for each integer
s
with
0\leq s<d\ell
, from (
5.1.4
) the element

\tilde{\theta}_{v,s}:=(\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{-s}})^{-1}=\iota(\tilde{t}_{v,s})\quad\in\bar{k}

is a root of

\tilde{g}_{v,s}(X)
, which gives a differential
\tilde{\omega}_{s}\in H_{\text{dR}}(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\bar{k})
induced from

(6.3.3)

\hskip-22.76228pt\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\frac{\bar{k}[X]}{\tilde{g}_{v,i}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[X]}

\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{k}[X]}{\tilde{g}_{v,s}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[X]}

\longrightarrow

\bar{k}

\Big(\bar{h}_{i}(X)\bmod\tilde{g}_{v,i}\Big)_{0\leq i<d\ell}

\longmapsto

\bar{h}_{s}(X)\bmod\tilde{g}_{v,s}

\longmapsto

\bar{h}_{s}(\tilde{\theta}_{v,s})
.

Therefore we obtain the following period interpretation:

Proposition 6.3.4
.

Recall the notation (
4.1.1
).
Given an integer
s
with
0\leq s<d\ell
, one has

\int_{\psi_{0,0}}\tilde{\omega}_{s}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{d\ell,v,d\ell-s}(v)}.

Proof.

From the definition of
\omega_{s}
and the de Rham pairing, one gets that

\displaystyle\int_{\psi_{0,0}}\tilde{\omega}_{s}

\displaystyle=\psi^{(-s)}(\tilde{t}_{v,s})\Big|_{\tilde{t}_{v,s}=\tilde{\theta}_{v,s}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{\tilde{\theta}_{v,s}}{\tilde{\theta}_{v}^{q^{d\ell i-s}}}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{-s}})^{-1}}{((\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{d\ell i-s}})^{-1}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{(\theta-\varepsilon)^{q^{d\ell i-s}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{-s}}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{d\ell,v,d\ell-s}(v)}.

∎

Remark 6.3.5
.

As
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
has CM by

The Gronthendieck period conjecture in Theorem
5.2.9
leads to

(6.3.6)

-motivic Galois group of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
.
Next, we will determine the dimension of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}
, and complete the proof of Theorem
4.1.2
.

6.4.
\tilde{T}_{v}
-Motivic Galois group of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
and the Hodge–Pink cocharacters

We let
\matheur{K}_{d\ell}:=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}(\tilde{t}_{v})
and
\matheur{F}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{T}_{v})
.
Since
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
has complex multiplication by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]
by Proposition
6.1.4

and

\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}[\tilde{T}_{v}]}\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v}]=[\matheur{K}_{d\ell}:\matheur{F}]=d^{2}\ell=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{T})}H_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})},

by (
5.2.6
)
we obtain that

\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{R}_{K_{d\ell}/F}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}})\subset\GL(H_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}),

where

\mathscr{R}_{K_{d\ell}/F}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}})
is the
restriction of scalars of
\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}
to
\matheur{F}
.
Our goal is to show

\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}=\mathscr{R}_{K_{d\ell}/F}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}})

by analysing the images of its Hodge–Pink cocharacters.

6.4.1.
Hodge–Pink cocharacters of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}
.

For
0\leq i<d\ell
,
let
\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,0}(=\tilde{\theta}_{v,i}),...,\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,d-1}
be the distinct roots of

\tilde{g}_{v,i}(X)\in\bar{k}[X]
defined in (
6.3.1
).
Comparing the minimal polynomials of
\tilde{t}_{v,i}
in (
5.1.6
) and
\tilde{g}_{v,i}(X)
, we get that

\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v}\in(\tilde{t}_{v,i}-\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,j})\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}],

whence

(6.4.2)

(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}]=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(\tilde{t}_{v,i}-\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,j})\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}].

On the other hand,
under the identification (
6.1.9
),
we get

(6.4.3)

\displaystyle\sigma\cdot\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})

\displaystyle=\Big((\tilde{t}_{v,0}-\tilde{\theta}_{v,0,0})\cdot\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,0}]\Big)\times\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,1}]\times\cdots\times\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,d\ell-1}]

\displaystyle\supset(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\cdot\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),

and the Hodge–Pink weight of
\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
is
(w_{i,j})_{0\leq i<d\ell,0\leq j<d}
with

w_{i,j}=\begin{cases}-1,&\text{ if $i=j=0$,}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Moreover precisely,
we may take a
\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}]
-base
\{m_{i,j}\mid 0\leq i<d\ell,\ 0\leq j<d\}

satisfying that
under the identification

\frac{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}\cong\prod_{i=0}^{d\ell-1}\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}]}{(\tilde{T}_{v}-\tilde{v})\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}]}\cong\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\frac{\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}]}{(\tilde{t}_{v,i}-\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,j})\bar{k}[\tilde{T}_{v}][\tilde{t}_{v,i}]}\cong\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\bar{k},

the image of
m_{i,j}
is

(\delta_{(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})})_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\in\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\bar{k},\quad\text{ where }\quad\delta_{(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $i=i^{\prime}$ and $j=j^{\prime}$,}\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

From the isomorphism (
5.3.2
), we may decompose

\displaystyle H_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\mathbb{C}_{v}}=\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\tilde{v}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}\tilde{T}_{v},\matheur{F}}{\otimes}H_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}H_{i,j}.

Notice that
for each
0\leq i<d\ell
and
0\leq j<d
,
let
\iota_{i,j}:\matheur{K}_{d\ell}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}(\tilde{t}_{v})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{v}
be the embedding satisfying that
\iota_{i,j}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{q^{-j}}
and
\iota_{i,j}(\tilde{t}_{v})=\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,j}
.
Regarding
H_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})
as a one dimensional vector space over
\matheur{K}_{d\ell}
(by Remark
6.2.7
), we have that

\alpha\cdot z=\iota_{i,j}(\alpha)\cdot z,\quad\forall z\in H_{i,j}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{d\ell}}(\tilde{t}_{v}).

Therefore under the identification

\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\tilde{v}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}\tilde{T}_{v},\matheur{F}}{\times}\mathscr{R}_{\matheur{K}_{d\ell}/\matheur{F}}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}})\cong\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\iota_{i,j},\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}{\times}\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}=\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\GL(H_{i,j}),

the cocharacter

\chi_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}^{\vee}:\mathbb{G}_{m/\mathbb{C}_{v}}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\mathbb{C}_{v}}\subset\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\tilde{v}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}\tilde{T}_{v},\matheur{F}}{\times}\mathscr{R}_{\matheur{K}_{d\ell}/\matheur{F}}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}})

is then realized as follows:

\chi_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}^{\vee}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}x^{-1}&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\in\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\iota_{i,j},\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}{\times}\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}.

Since
\Aut_{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\tilde{v})}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
acts transitively on the coordinates of
\prod_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{0\leq i<d\ell}{0\leq j<d}}\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\iota_{i,j},\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}{\times}\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}

the Hodge–Pink cocharacters of
\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}
generate the whole group
\mathbb{C}_{v}\underset{\tilde{v}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\mapsto$}}\tilde{T}_{v},\matheur{F}}{\times}\mathscr{R}_{\matheur{K}_{d\ell}/\matheur{F}}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}})
.
Consequently, we finally arrive at:

Theorem 6.4.4
.

\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}=\mathscr{R}_{\matheur{K}_{d\ell}/\matheur{F}}(\mathbb{G}_{m/\matheur{K}_{d\ell}}),

whence
\dim\Gamma_{\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell})}=[\matheur{K}_{d\ell}:\matheur{F}]=d^{2}\ell
.

Combining with (
6.3.6
), we finally arrive at:

Corollary 6.4.5
.

\displaystyle\trdeg_{\bar{k}}\bar{k}\bigg(\int_{\psi_{0,0}}\tilde{\omega}\ \bigg|\ \tilde{\omega}\in H_{\text{\rm dR}}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\bar{k}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=d^{2}\ell.

6.4.6.
Proof of Theorem
4.1.2
.

By Proposition
6.3.4
we have that

\Omega_{d\ell,v,i}(v)=\left(\int_{\psi_{0,0}}\tilde{\omega}_{d\ell-i}\right)^{-1},\quad 0<i\leq d\ell.

On the other hand,
for
0\leq i<d\ell
and
0\leq j<d
,
define
\tilde{\omega}_{i,j}\in H_{dR}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\bar{k}\big)
as follows:

\hskip-22.76228pt\displaystyle\prod_{i^{\prime}=0}^{d\ell-1}\frac{\bar{k}[X]}{\bar{g}_{v,i^{\prime}}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[X]}

\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{k}[X]}{\bar{g}_{v,i}(X)\cdot\bar{k}[X]}

\longrightarrow

\bar{k}

\Big(\bar{h}_{i^{\prime}}(X)\bmod\bar{g}_{v,i^{\prime}}\Big)_{0\leq i<d\ell}

\longmapsto

\bar{h}_{i}(X)\bmod\bar{g}_{v,i}

\longmapsto

\bar{h}_{i}(\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,j})
,

where
\tilde{\theta}_{v,i,j}
are given in the beginning of Section

.
Then

\big\{\tilde{\omega}_{i,j}\ \big|\ 0\leq i<d\ell,\ 0\leq j<d\big\}\quad\text{ is a basis of $H_{dR}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\bar{k}\big)$},

and

\tilde{\omega}_{i}=\tilde{\omega}_{i,0}
for

by comparing with
(

Since

\displaystyle\dim_{\bar{k}}H_{dR}\big(\mathscr{R}_{\sigma}(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{d\ell}),\bar{k}\big)=d^{2}\ell=\trdeg_{\bar{k}}\bar{k}\bigg(\int_{\psi_{0,0}}\tilde{\omega}_{i,j}\ \bigg|\ 0\leq i<d\ell,\ 0\leq j<d\bigg),

we obtain that

\int_{\psi_{0,0}}\tilde{\omega}_{i,j},\quad 0\leq i<d\ell,\quad 0\leq j<d,\quad\text{ are algebraically independent over $\bar{k}$.}

Finally, as for
0<i\leq\ell
, one has that

(6.4.7)

\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\Omega_{d\ell,v,j\ell+i}(v)=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{\theta_{v}^{q^{d\ell n+j\ell+i}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{j\ell+i}}})=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-\frac{\theta_{v}^{q^{\ell n+i}}}{\theta-\varepsilon^{q^{\ell n+i}}})=\Omega_{\ell,v,i}(v).

Therefore the result follows.

Uniform L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates: subsolutions to fully nonlinear partial differential equations Thanks: Work supported in part by the National Science Foundation under grants DMS-2203273, and DMS-2303508, and the collaboration grant 946730 from Simons Foundation.
Bin Guo and Duong H. Phong

Abstract
. Uniform bounds are obtained using the auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation method for solutions of very general classes of fully non-linear partial differential equations, assuming the existence of a 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} -subsolution in the sense of G. Székelyhidi and B. Guan. The main advantage over previous estimates is in the Kähler case, where the new estimates remain uniform as the background metric is allowed to degenerate.



1.
Introduction

This paper is motivated by two relatively recent developments. The first is the new theory of
{\mathcal{C}}
-subsolutions for fully non-linear equations on compact Hermitian manifolds due to G. Székelyhidi
[
24
]
and B. Guan
[
9
]
, a parabolic version of which was subsequently developed in
[
20
]
. Subsolutions are of great importance for non-linear equations, as their existence are clearly a necessary condition for the solvabiliy of the equations themselves.
A significant difficulty for equations on compact manifolds was that the naive notion may only allow trivial subsolutions, and this difficulty was only overcome recently with the notion of
{\mathcal{C}}
-subsolution introduced in
[
24
,
9
,
20
]
. The second development is the new auxiliary Monge-Ampère equation method for
L^{\infty}
estimates introduced by the authors in joint work with F. Tong for equations respectively on Kähler manifolds
[
14
]
and Hermitian manifolds
[
10
]
. A great advantage of these new methods is that they lead to
L^{\infty}
estimates which are uniform with respect to large classes of background metrics, the power of which can be appreciated from many recent applications in complex geometry
[
4
,
13
,
11
,
12
]
.
A natural question is then whether the existence of subsolutions can lead, not just
L^{\infty}
estimates, but
L^{\infty}
estimates with uniformity comparable to those in
[
13
,
11
]
. The goal of the present paper is to provide a positive answer to this question.

We begin by providing a general formulation of the equations which we shall consider.
Let
X
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
n
with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
.
Let
\omega
be another Hermitian metric and
\chi
a closed
(1,1)
-form on
X
. For a smooth function
\varphi
, let
h_{\varphi}=\omega^{-1}\cdot\chi_{\varphi}:TX\to TX
be the endomorphism defined by
\omega
and
\chi_{\varphi}=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi
, and let
\lambda[h_{\varphi}]
is the (unordered) vector of eigenvalues of this endomorphism. We consider the following fully nonlinear equation on
X
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])=e^{F},\quad{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}\varphi=0,

where
e^{F}
is a positive smooth function normalized such that

\int_{X}e^{nF}\omega^{n}=[\omega]^{n}=\int_{X}\omega^{n}.

The nonlinear operator
f:\Gamma\to{\mathbf{R}}_{>0}
is required to satisfy the following conditions:

(i):
\Gamma\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
is an open convex symmetric cone such that
\Gamma_{n}\subset\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
;

(ii):
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0
for any
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma
, which implies the ellipticity of the equation (
1.1
).

In the papers
[
14
,
2
]
, it was further assumed that
f
satisfies the structural condition

(1.2)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)\geq\hat{\gamma},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}

for some non-negative function
\hat{\gamma}
which is required to be bounded from below by a fixed positive constant. This is a natural condition, which was subsequently shown by Harvey and Lawson
[
17
]
to be satisfied by very large classes of equations, including all Garding-Dirichlet operators
[
16
]
. It is also a natural condition arising in the study of
C^{2}
estimates
[
2
,
6
,
5
]
. However, as stressed in
[
11
]
, in many important geometric applications,
\hat{\gamma}
may vanish along a complex codimension one subvariety. Another important example of geometric equation which does not obey the condition of
\hat{\gamma}
being strictly positive is the
J
equation, which admits a solution if and only if a subsolution exists
[
22
,
26
]
.

We shall be interested in obtaining
L^{\infty}
bounds for solutions of the equation (
1.1
) assuming the existence of a subsolution, even in the absence of the structural condition (
1.2
) of the operator
f
. The following notion of
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution is taken from
[
24
]
(see also
[
20
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Given
\delta>0,R>0
,
we say a
(1,1)
-form
\chi^{\prime}=\chi-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\theta
is a
(\delta,R)
-subsolution of the equation (
1.1
), if the following holds

(1.3)

(\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}}]-\delta{\mathbf{1}}+\Gamma_{n})\cap\partial\Gamma^{e^{F(z)}}\subset B(0,R)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\quad\forall z\in X,

where
{\mathbf{1}}=(1,\ldots,1)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the fixed vector,
h_{\chi^{\prime}}=\omega^{-1}\cdot\chi^{\prime}
is the endomorphism defined by
\chi^{\prime}
relative to
\omega
, and

\Gamma^{e^{F(z)}}=\{\lambda\in\Gamma~|~f(\lambda)\geq e^{F(z)}\}.

The most important case for us is the Kähler case, to be discussed in Section 2, where estimates are obtained which are uniform with respect to large sets of forms
\chi
reaching the boundary of the Kähler cone. The Hermitian case is discussed in Section 3. Finally, in Section 4, we discuss a notion of subsolution closer in spirit to numerical criteria in stabilty.

2.
The Kähler case

In this section, we assume
(X,\omega_{X})
is a Kähler manifold. We will derive
uniform estimates
for
\varphi
when a subsolution exists. Here uniform estimates refer to the ones which do not depend on a fixed reference metric, in particular, it allows the reference metrics to degenerate. For the purpose of uniform estimates, we need to consider a subset of the space of Kähler metrics on
X
. As in the joint work
[
11
]
with Song and Sturm, for a given Kähler metric
\omega
, we will denote its relative volume function as

(2.4)

e^{F_{\omega}}=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{X}^{n}},\quad V_{\omega}=\int_{X}\omega^{n}.

Given constants
A>0,K>0
,
p>n
and a continuous nonnegative function
\gamma\in C^{0}(X)
, we consider the following subset of Kähler metrics

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}(A,K,p,\gamma,\omega_{X})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big\{\omega\mbox{ a K\"{a}hler metric}~|~[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}\leq A,e^{F_{\omega}}\geq\gamma,

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{and }\,{\mathcal{N}}_{p}(\omega)=\int_{X}|F_{\omega}|^{p}e^{F_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq K\Big\}.

We will always assume that
\gamma
satisfies
{\mathrm{dim}}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\gamma^{-1}(0)<2n-1
.
When the parameters are fixed, we will simply write
{\mathcal{W}}
as this set.

Suppose
\chi^{\prime}=\chi-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\theta
is a
(\delta,R)
-subsolution to (
1.1
) as in Definition
1.1
, which satisfies the assumption:

(2.6)

-n\kappa_{1}\leq{\rm tr}_{\omega}\chi^{\prime}\leq\kappa_{2},

for constants
\kappa_{1}\geq 0,\,\kappa_{2}\geq 0
.

Our main theorem is as follows:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let the assumptions be given as above, and
\varphi
is a
C^{2}
solution to the equation (
1.1
), then there exists a constant
C>0
depending on the given parameters
n,A,K,p,\gamma
, and
\delta,R,\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
such that

(2.7)

{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}|(\varphi+\theta)-{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}(\varphi+\theta)|\leq C.

To prove this theorem, we need the following preliminary lemmas. First,
we recall an estimate on the Green’s function
G_{\omega}
associated to
\omega
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
11
,
13
]
)
.

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
which depends on
p,A,K,\gamma
and
\omega_{X}
such that

(2.8)

\sup_{x\in X}\Big(\sup_{y\in X}(-G_{\omega}(x,y))\cdot V_{\omega}+\int_{X}|G_{\omega}(x,y)|\omega^{n}(y)\Big)\leq C_{0}.

Next we have the following uniform
L^{1}
and
L^{q}
bounds:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}
be a Kähler metric, then we have

(2.9)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega^{n}\leq 2nC_{0},\quad\forall\psi\in PSH(X,\omega)\mbox{ with }\sup_{X}\psi=0,

where
C_{0}
is the constant in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.
By smooth approximation we may assume
\psi
is
C^{2}
and
\omega
-PSH. We assume
\sup_{X}\psi=\psi(x_{0})=0
for some
x_{0}\in X
. Applying Green’s formula to
\psi
at
x_{0}
gives

\psi(x_{0})=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\psi\omega^{n}+\int_{X}[G_{\omega}(x_{0},\cdot)-\inf_{z\in X}G_{\omega}(x_{0},z)](-\Delta_{\omega}\psi)\omega^{n}.

Hence by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-\psi)\omega^{n}\leq n\int_{X}\{|G_{\omega}(x_{0},\cdot)|+\frac{C_{0}}{V_{\omega}}\}\omega^{n}\leq 2nC_{0}.

Q.E.D.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}
be a Kähler metric. For any
q>n
, there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(p,A,K,\gamma,\omega_{X},q)>0
such that for any
\omega
-PSH function
\psi
with
\sup_{X}\psi=0
, we have

(2.10)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}({-\psi})^{q}\omega^{n}\leq C_{1}.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.
By Proposition 3.1 in
[
11
]
, the condition
[\omega]\cdot[\omega_{X}]^{n-1}\leq A
implies that there exists a smooth
(1,1)
-form
\tilde{\omega}\in[\omega]
such that

(2.11)

-C_{2}\omega_{X}\leq\tilde{\omega}\leq C_{2}\omega_{X}

for some
C_{2}=C_{2}(A,\omega_{X})>0
. By
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
-lemma, we can write
\omega=\tilde{\omega}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u
for an
\tilde{\omega}
-PSH function
u
normalized by
\sup_{X}u=0
. Since by (
2.11
) it holds that
\tilde{\omega}\leq C_{2}\omega_{X}
, we see that
u
is
(C_{2}\omega_{X})
-PSH, hence the
\alpha
-invariant estimates hold for
u
(see
[
15
,
25
]
), that is, there exist
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}(C_{2},\omega_{X})>0
and
C_{3}=C_{3}(C_{2},\omega_{X})>0
such that

(2.12)

\int_{X}e^{-\alpha^{\prime}u}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{3}.

For any
\omega
-PSH function
\psi
with
\sup_{X}\psi=0
,
\psi+u
is also
\tilde{\omega}
-PSH, hence
(C_{2}\omega_{X})
-PSH. Again by
\alpha
-invariant estimates, we have

(2.13)

\int_{X}e^{\alpha^{\prime}(\sup_{X}(\psi+u)-\psi-u)}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{3}.

To finish the proof, it suffices to prove a lower bound of
\sup_{X}(\psi+u)
. We note that by Young’s inequality

(2.14)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-u)\omega^{n}=\int_{X}(-u)e^{F_{\omega}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C\int_{X}e^{F_{\omega}}(1+|F_{\omega}|)\omega_{X}^{n}+C\int_{X}e^{-\alpha^{\prime}u}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{4},

where we have used (
2.12
) and
C_{4}>0
depends on
p,A,K,
and
\omega_{X}
. Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.14
) yields

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-u-\varphi)\omega^{n}\leq 2nC_{0}+C_{4}=C_{5}.

Hence
\sup_{X}(u+\varphi)\geq-C_{5}
as claimed. Together with (
2.13
) this implies that

(2.15)

\int_{X}e^{\alpha^{\prime}(-\psi)}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{3}e^{C_{5}}=:C_{6}.

To prove (
2.10
), we apply Young’s inequality again to get

(-\psi)e^{F_{\omega}/q}\leq e^{F_{\omega}/q}(1+|F_{\omega}|^{p/q})+C_{p,q}e^{-\alpha^{\prime}\psi/q}.

Taking
q
th power on both sides of the above inequality gives

(2.16)

(-\psi)^{q}e^{F_{\omega}}\leq Ce^{F_{\omega}}(1+|F_{\omega}|^{p})+C_{p,q}e^{-\alpha^{\prime}\psi}.

Integrating both sides of (
2.16
) over
X
against the measure
\omega_{X}^{n}
yields the desired inequality (
2.10
), by making use of (
2.15
). Q.E.D.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let the assumptions be as above. For any
v\in C^{2}
with
\sup_{X}v=0
satisfying
\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}v}]\in\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
, we have

(2.17)

\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-v)\omega^{n}\leq 2\kappa_{2}C_{0}=:C_{7},

where
C_{0}
is the constant in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.
The condition
\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}v}]\in\Gamma_{1}
implies that

{\rm tr}_{\omega}\chi^{\prime}+\Delta_{\omega}v>0
. By (
2.6
) this gives
\Delta_{\omega}v\geq-\kappa_{2}
. Then we can apply the Green’s formula for
v
at its maximum point
x_{0}
to get

0\leq\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}v\omega^{n}+\int_{X}[G_{\omega}(x_{0},\cdot)-\inf_{z}G_{\omega}(x_{0},z)]\kappa_{2}\omega^{n}.

Q.E.D.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
Let
\varphi
be a
C^{2}
function solving the equation (
1.1
). Denote
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi+\theta
and by adding an appropriate constant to
\varphi^{\prime}
we may assume
\sup_{X}\varphi^{\prime}=0
. We have

\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi=\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}.

Hence, we can view
\varphi^{\prime}
as a solution to (
1.1
) if
\chi
is replaced by
\chi^{\prime}
.
As in
[
14
,
10
]
, for any
s\geq 0
, we consider the sublevel set
\Omega_{s}
of
\varphi^{\prime}
by
\Omega_{s}=\{z\in X:\varphi^{\prime}(z)<-s\}
. We solve the following auxiliary complex Monge-Ampère equation which admits a smooth solution since
\omega
is a Kähler metric
[
27
]

(2.18)

(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=\frac{\tau_{k}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)}{A_{s,k}}\omega^{n},\quad\sup_{X}\psi_{s,k}=0,

where the constant
A_{s,k}
is defined so that the equation is compatible, i.e.

(2.19)

A_{s,k}:=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}\tau_{k}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)\omega^{n}\to\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)\omega^{n}=:A_{s},

as
k\to\infty
. We consider the comparison function

(2.20)

\Psi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}-\varphi^{\prime}-s,

where

(2.21)

\varepsilon=\beta_{1}A_{s,k}^{\frac{1}{n+1}},\quad\mbox{where }\beta_{1}:=\Big(\frac{n+1}{n}(R+\delta+\kappa_{1})\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}},

and
\Lambda
is given by

(2.22)

\Lambda=\beta_{2}A_{s,k},\quad\mbox{where }\beta_{2}=\frac{n}{n+1}(\frac{1}{\delta})^{n+1}(R+\delta+\kappa_{1})^{n}.

We
claim
that
\Psi\leq 0
on
X
. By definition, it suffices to check this when the maximum point of
\Psi
is achieved at
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}
. By the maximum principle, we have
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\Psi\leq 0
at
x_{0}
, which subsequently implies that at
x_{0}
,

\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}\geq\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k}+\frac{n\varepsilon}{(n+1)^{2}}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{n+2}{n+1}}\partial\psi_{s,k}\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi_{s,k}.

Therefore, adding
\chi^{\prime}
on both sides of the above we obtain

(2.23)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})

\displaystyle+\chi^{\prime}+[-\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}]\omega

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})+\chi^{\prime}-\delta\omega,

where in the last line we have applied the choice of constant
\Lambda
in (
2.22
) and the assumption (
2.6
). From the inequality (
2.23
), we readily get that at the point
x_{0}
,

(2.24)

\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}=\chi^{\prime}-\delta\omega+\rho,

for some
positive

(1,1)
-form
\rho
. In terms of eigenvalues, (
2.24
) is equivalent to

\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}}]\in\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}}]-\delta{\mathbf{1}}+\Gamma_{n}.

Moreover, from the equation (
1.1
) we also have
f(\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}}])=e^{F(x_{0})}
. Thus by the definition of a
(\delta,R)
-subsolution
\chi^{\prime}
, we conclude that the vector of eigenvalues
\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}}]|_{x_{0}}\in B(0,R)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. If we denote

\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}}]|_{x_{0}}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}),

then
|\lambda_{i}|\leq R
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
. Taking trace with respect to
\omega
on both sides of (
2.23
), together with the assumption (
2.6
), we obtain that

(2.25)

nR+n\delta+n\kappa_{1}\geq\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}{\rm tr}_{\omega}(\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})>0.

Applying the arithmetic-geometric inequality to the RHS of (
2.25
), and using the auxiliary equation (
2.18
) satisfied by
\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k}
, we get

(2.26)

\displaystyle(nR+n\delta+n\kappa_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\Big(\frac{\tau_{k}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)}{A_{s,k}}\Big)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)^{1/n}}{A^{1/n}_{s,k}}.

From the choice of the constant
\varepsilon
in (
2.21
), the inequality (
2.26
) easily yields that
\Psi(x_{0})\leq 0
. Hence we finish the proof the claim. We then obtain that on
\Omega_{s}
,

(2.27)

{(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}\leq\beta_{1}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}}(-\psi_{s,k}+\Lambda).

We fix a constant
q>n
, and by Lemma
2.3
, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that
\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}(-\psi_{s,k})^{q}\omega^{n}\leq C_{1}
. Taking
q
-th power on both sides of (
2.27
) and integrating the resulted inequality over
\Omega_{s}
yield that

(2.28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)^{q\frac{n+1}{n}}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}{A_{s,k}^{q/n}}\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{X}((-\psi_{s,k})^{q}+\Lambda^{q})\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}(C_{1}+\Lambda^{q})A_{s,k}^{q/n},

where
C_{2}
is a constant depending on
q
and
\beta_{1}
. Letting
k\to\infty
in (
2.28
) we see that this inequality remains true if
A_{s,k}
is replaced by
A_{s}
(along with the
A_{s,k}
in the constant
\Lambda
in (
2.22
)).

We define a monotone function as in
[
14
]

\phi:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\quad\mbox{by }\quad\phi(s)=\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}\omega^{n}.

From Hölder inequality, it follows that

(2.29)

\displaystyle A_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\varphi^{\prime}-s)^{\frac{q(n+1)}{n}}\omega^{n}\Big)^{\frac{n}{q(n+1)}}\phi(s)^{\frac{q(n+1)-n}{q(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C_{2}(C_{1}+\Lambda^{q})A_{s}^{q/n})^{\frac{n}{q(n+1)}}\phi(s)^{\frac{q(n+1)}{q(n+1)-n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C_{2}(C_{1}+\Lambda^{q}))^{\frac{n}{q(n+1)}}A_{s}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\phi(s)^{\frac{q(n+1)-n}{q(n+1)}}.

Hence we have

(2.30)

A_{s}\leq(C_{2}(C_{1}+\Lambda^{q}))^{\frac{1}{q}}\phi(s)^{1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}}=C_{3}\phi(s)^{1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}},

where the constant
C_{3}
is given by
(C_{2}(C_{1}+\Lambda^{q}))^{\frac{1}{q}}
and is uniformly bounded since
A_{s}
is bounded from Lemma
2.4
.
From (
2.30
) we get

(2.31)

r\phi(s+r)\leq C_{3}\phi(s)^{1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}},\quad\forall r\in(0,1)\mbox{ and }s\geq 0.

It is a classic fact due to De Giorgi
[
18
]
, that (
2.31
) implies
\Omega_{S_{\infty}}=\emptyset
, for
S_{\infty}=\frac{1}{1-2^{-nq/(q-n)}}+s_{0},
where
s_{0}>0
is a number that satisfies
\phi(s_{0})\leq(2C_{3})^{(q-n)/nq}
.

We note that

\phi(s_{0})\leq\frac{1}{s_{0}}\frac{1}{[\omega]^{n}}\int_{X}(-\varphi^{\prime})\omega^{n}\leq\frac{C_{7}}{s_{0}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.4
and
C_{7}>0
is the constant in that lemma. So we can take
s_{0}=C_{7}(2C_{3})^{-(q-n)/nq}
. Combining the above, we conclude that

(2.32)

\sup_{X}(-\varphi^{\prime})\leq S_{\infty}=C_{7}(2C_{3})^{-(q-n)/nq}+\frac{1}{1-2^{-nq/(q-n)}}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Q.E.D.

Remark 3.1.
We only require the Kähler metric
\omega
to be in the set
{\mathcal{W}}
for the purpose of verifying the two integrability conditions stated in Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
. It is important to note that the proof of Theorem
2.1
remains valid when these two lemmas are satisfied. Therefore, there exist alternative choices for the family of metrics
\omega
beyond the set
\mathcal{W}
that can ensure the validity of these lemmas, and consequently, Theorem
2.1
. For instance, in case
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
, we can impose the following assumptions on
\omega
and the subsolutions
\chi^{\prime}
:

(2.33)

\omega\leq K_{1}\omega_{X},\quad\frac{1}{V_{\omega}}\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega^{n}_{X}}\leq K_{2},\quad-\kappa_{1}\omega\leq\chi^{\prime}\leq\kappa_{2}\omega.

Under these assumptions, Lemma
2.3
holds from the
\alpha
-invariant estimates readily. Note that
\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}>0
and hence
\kappa_{2}K_{2}\omega_{X}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}>0
. Green’s formula for the fixed metric
\omega_{X}
shows
\int_{X}(-\varphi^{\prime})\omega_{X}^{n}
is bounded depending only on the given parameters. Lemma
2.4
is also true by the second inequality in (
2.33
).

3.
The Hermitian case

In this section, we will prove the
L^{\infty}
estimates when the metric
\omega
is
fixed
and only Hermitian. Let
X
be a compact complex manifold without boundary. As in the last section, we assume that
\chi^{\prime}=\chi-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\theta
is a
(\delta,R)
-subsolution to (
1.1
) which satisfies

(3.1)

-n\kappa_{1}\omega\leq\chi^{\prime}\leq\kappa_{2}\omega.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the assumptions be given as above, and
\varphi
is a
C^{2}
solution to the equation (
1.1
), then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
n,\omega
, and
\delta,R,\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
such that

(3.2)

{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}|(\varphi+\theta)-{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}(\varphi+\theta)|\leq C.

We denote
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi+\theta
and assume without loss of generality that
\sup_{X}\varphi^{\prime}=0
.
Since the metric
\omega
is fixed, there is a constant
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,\omega,X)>0
such that for any point
z_{0}\in X
, there exist holomorphic coordinates
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
at
z_{0}
such that on the ball
B(z_{0},2r_{0})=\{z:~\sum_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}<(2r_{0})^{2}\}
the metric
\omega=g_{i\bar{j}}\sqrt{-1}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j}
satisfies

(3.3)

\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}\leq g_{i\bar{j}}\leq 2\delta_{ij}.

We assume
x_{0}
is a minimum point of
\varphi^{\prime}
, i.e.
\min_{X}\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0})
, and pick the coordinates
z
at
x_{0}
such that (
3.3
) holds. Fix
s_{0}=\frac{\delta}{10}\kappa_{1}r_{0}^{2}>0
. For any
s\in(0,s_{0})
, we consider the function
u_{s}
defined on the ball
B(x_{0},r_{0})=\{z~:~|z|<r_{0}\}
as

u_{s}(z)=-\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0})+\varphi^{\prime}(z)+\frac{\delta}{4}|z|^{2}-s,

and define the level set of
u_{s}

U_{s}:=\{z\in B(x_{0},r_{0})|~u_{s}(z)<0\}.

Since
u_{s}|_{\partial B(x_{0},r_{0})}>0
, we see that
U_{s}
is relatively compact in
B(x_{0},r_{0})
. We consider the solution to the Dirichlet problem

(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=\frac{\tau_{k}(-u_{s})}{A_{s,k}}\omega^{n},\,\mbox{in }B(x_{0},r_{0}),

with
\psi_{s,k}=0
on
\partial B(x_{0},r_{0})
, where we choose the constant
A_{s,k}>0
such that

(3.4)

\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}=1

in other words

A_{s,k}:=\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}\tau_{k}(-u_{s})\omega^{n}\to A_{s}:=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-u_{s})\omega^{n}.

Note that the solution
\psi_{s,k}\leq 0
exists and is unique by
[
3
]
. We next consider the test function

\Phi=-\varepsilon(-\psi_{s,k})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}-u_{s},\mbox{ in }B(x_{0},r_{0}),

where

(3.5)

\varepsilon=(\frac{n+1}{n})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}(R+\delta+\kappa_{1})^{\frac{n}{n+1}}A_{s,k}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}=:\beta_{3}A_{s,k}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

As in the last section, we claim
\Phi\leq 0
. It suffices to prove this when the maximum of
\Phi
is achieved at some point
z_{0}\in U_{s}\subset B(x_{0},r_{0})
. By maximum principle, we have that at the point
z_{0}
,
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\Phi\leq 0
, which implies that at
z_{0}

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k}-\frac{\delta}{4}\omega_{{\mathbf{C}}^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k}-\frac{\delta}{2}\omega.

Hence at
z_{0}
it holds that

(3.6)

\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}>\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k}+\chi^{\prime}-{\delta}\omega.

This inequality shows that
\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}=\chi^{\prime}-\delta\omega+\rho
for some positive definite
(1,1)
-form
\rho
. In terms of eigenvalues, this means that

\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}}]\in\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}}]-\delta{\mathbf{1}}+\Gamma_{n}.

By the definition of the
(\delta,R)
-subsolution
\chi^{\prime}
, we have
|\lambda_{i}|\leq R
for each
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, where
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
are the eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}\cdot(\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime})|_{z_{0}}
. Again by (
3.6
) we have at
z_{0}

\displaystyle n(R+\kappa_{1}+\delta)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle{\rm tr}_{\omega}(\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}-\chi^{\prime}+\delta\omega)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}{\rm tr}_{\omega}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\Big(\frac{(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{s,k})^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\Big)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}\varepsilon}{n+1}(-\psi_{s,k})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}\frac{(-u_{s})^{1/n}}{A_{s,k}^{1/n}},

which is equivalent to
\Phi|_{z_{0}}\leq 0
by the choice of
\varepsilon
in (
3.5
). Therefore, on
U_{s}
we have

(3.7)

{(-u_{s})^{{(n+1)/}{n}}}{}\leq\beta_{3}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}A_{s,k}^{1/n}(-\psi_{s,k}).

By the uniform
\alpha
-invariant estimates (see
[
18
]
) for
\psi_{s,k}
which satisfies (
3.4
), there exist uniform constants
\alpha=\alpha(n,r_{0},\omega)
and
C=C(n,r_{0},\omega)
such that
\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}e^{-\alpha\psi_{s,k}}\omega^{n}\leq C
, hence for any
q>n
it holds that

(3.8)

\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}(-\psi_{s,k})^{q}\omega^{n}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}=C_{0}^{\prime}(n,r_{0},\omega,q).

Taking
q
-th power on both sides of (
3.7
) and integrating over
U_{s}
, we have from (
3.8
) that

(3.9)

\int_{U_{s}}(-u_{s})^{\frac{q(n+1)}{n}}\omega^{n}\leq\beta_{3}^{\frac{q(n+1)}{n}}A_{s,k}^{\frac{q}{n}}\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}(-\psi_{s,k})^{q}\omega^{n}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}\beta_{3}^{\frac{q(n+1)}{n}}A_{s,k}^{\frac{q}{n}}.

Letting
k\to\infty
, inequality (
3.9
) continues to hold when
A_{s,k}
is replaced by
A_{s}
.
By Hölder inequality, we can argue similarly as in (
2.29
) to derive

(3.10)

A_{s}\leq(C_{0}^{\prime}\beta_{3}^{\frac{q(n+1)}{n}})^{\frac{1}{q}}\hat{\phi}(s)^{1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}}=:C_{1}^{\prime}\hat{\phi}(s)^{1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}},

where
C_{1}^{\prime}=(C_{0}^{\prime}\beta_{3}^{\frac{q(n+1)}{n}})^{\frac{1}{q}}
and
\hat{\phi}(s)
is a monotone function given by

\hat{\phi}(s)=\int_{U_{s}}\omega^{n},\quad\forall s\in(0,s_{0}).

From (
3.10
) we obtain

(3.11)

t\hat{\phi}(s-t)\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\hat{\phi}(s)^{1+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}},\quad\forall\,0<t<s<s_{0}.

Then an iteration argument (see e.g. Lemma 7 in
[
10
]
) shows that there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
that depends on
n,q,C_{0}^{\prime},
and
s_{0}
such that
\hat{\phi}(s_{0})\geq c_{0}
. We observe that on
U_{s_{0}}
,

u_{s_{0}}(z)<0\quad\Rightarrow\quad-\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0})\leq-\varphi^{\prime}(z)-\frac{\delta}{4}|z|^{2}+s_{0}.

Integrating this inequality over
U_{s_{0}}
yields

(3.12)

c_{0}(-\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0}))\leq\int_{U_{s_{0}}}(-\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0}))\omega^{n}\leq\int_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}(-\varphi^{\prime})\omega^{n}+C(n,r_{0})s_{0}.

From (
3.12
), we see that

-\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0})=-{\mathrm{inf}}_{X}\varphi^{\prime}\leq C(\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})}+1),

for some constant
C>0
that depends on
n,\kappa_{1},q
and
\omega
. To complete the proof it suffices to derive an
L^{1}
estimate of
\varphi^{\prime}
. This follows from the fact that
\lambda[h_{\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}}]\in\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1}
, hence

{\rm tr}_{\omega}\chi^{\prime}+\Delta_{\omega}\varphi^{\prime}\geq 0,

where
\Delta_{\omega}\varphi^{\prime}={\rm tr}_{\omega}(\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime})
is the rough Laplacian of the Hermtian metric
\omega
. Given the assumption in (
3.1
) that
{\rm tr}_{\omega}\chi^{\prime}\leq\kappa_{2}
, we have the linear differential inequality
\Delta_{\omega}\varphi^{\prime}\geq-\kappa_{2}
on
X
. By a local covering argument (see e.g. Lemma 8 in
[
10
]
) and standard elliptic estimates
[
8
]
, we have

\int_{X}(-\varphi^{\prime})\omega^{n}\leq C,

for some
C>0
depending on
n,\omega,\kappa_{2}
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

4.
Other forms of subsolutions

In this section, we discuss another form of subsolutions in the case of
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
. To simplify the exposition, we make the convention that for a vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma_{n}
, i.e.
\lambda_{i}>0
for all
i
, we define
\tilde{\lambda}=(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}})\in\Gamma_{n}
. Abusing notation, we sometimes write
\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}
. Associated with the operator
f
which satisfies the condition (ii) in Section 1 and the condition of homogeneity of degree one, we define a new fully nonlinear operator
\tilde{f}:\Gamma_{n}\to{\mathbf{R}}_{>0}
as

(4.13)

\tilde{f}(\tilde{\lambda}):=\frac{1}{f({\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}^{-1},\ldots,{\tilde{\lambda}_{n}}^{-1})}=\frac{1}{f(\lambda)}.

Clearly
\tilde{f}
is also a homogeneous function of degree one and
\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(\tilde{\lambda})>0
for any
\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{n}
. Furthermore, we impose the extra conditions on
f
or
\tilde{f}
as follows.

(iii):
\tilde{f}
is
concave
in
\tilde{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{n}
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say a metric
\chi^{\prime}=\chi-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\theta
is a
\delta
-subsolution of (
1.1
), if

(4.14)

\sum_{j\neq i}\tilde{\mu}_{j}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{j}}(\tilde{\mu})\leq(1-\delta)e^{-F(z)},\quad\forall z\in X,\,\forall i=1,\ldots,n.

Here
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})
is an unordered vector of eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}\cdot\chi^{\prime}
, and conventionally we have defined
\tilde{\mu}_{j}=\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}
and
\tilde{\mu}=(\tilde{\mu}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\mu}_{n})
.

We observe that if
\chi^{\prime}
satisfies the equation (
1.1
), by the homogeneity of degree one condition, we necessarily have
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mu}_{j}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{j}}(\tilde{\mu})=e^{-F(z)}
. So a solution is indeed a
\delta
-subsolution for some
\delta>0
.

Given a
\delta
-subsolution
\chi^{\prime}
as in Definition
4.1
, we have the following uniform
L^{\infty}
estimates for solutions
\varphi
to (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume the operator
f
satisfies the additional condition (iii). Suppose
\varphi
is a
C^{2}
solution to the equation (
1.1
),
\omega\in{\mathcal{W}}
is a Kähler metric, and
\chi^{\prime}
is a
\delta
-subsolution as in Definition
4.1
such that
\chi^{\prime}\leq\kappa_{2}\omega
for some constant
\kappa_{2}>0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
that depends on
\kappa_{2},\delta
and the parameters in
{\mathcal{W}}
such that

{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}|(\varphi+\theta)-{\mathrm{sup}}_{X}(\varphi+\theta)|\leq C.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.
We follow the notations as in Section
2
, and denote
\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi+\theta
. Let
\psi_{s,k}
and
\Psi
be as in (
2.18
) and (
2.20
), respectively. Here the two constants
\varphi
and
\Lambda
in (
2.20
) are chosen as

\varepsilon=\Big(\frac{n+1}{n}R\Big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}A_{s,k}^{\frac{1}{n+1}},\quad\Lambda=\Big(\frac{2n\kappa_{2}\varepsilon}{(n+1)\delta}\Big)^{n+1},

and here
R:=\frac{(2-\delta)(1-\delta)}{\delta}\kappa_{2}>0
. With these choice of constants, we claim
\Psi\leq 0
. At a maximum point
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}
of
\Psi
, an analogous inequality as (
2.23
) holds, which gives

(4.15)

\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime}>(1-0.5\delta)\chi^{\prime}.

We take normal coordinates of
\omega
at
x_{0}
, and consider the Hermitian matrices
B^{\prime}=(\chi^{\prime}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi^{\prime})_{i\bar{j}}|_{x_{0}}
and
B=(\chi^{\prime})_{i\bar{j}}|_{x_{0}}
.
B^{\prime}
and
B
may not be necessarily diagonal under these coordinates. We may assume there eigenvalues are listed in an increasing order, i.e.

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n},\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0<\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{n}.

The inequality (
4.15
) means
B^{\prime}>(1-\delta)B.
It follows from an elementary fact from linear algebra (see Lemma
4.1
below) that

(4.16)

\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}(1-0.5\delta),\quad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n.

By the condition (i’) that
\tilde{f}(\tilde{\lambda})
is concave in
\tilde{\lambda}
, it follows that

(4.17)

\tilde{f}(\tilde{\lambda})\leq\tilde{f}(\tilde{\mu})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(\tilde{\mu})\cdot(\tilde{\lambda}_{i}-\tilde{\mu}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(\tilde{\mu})\cdot\tilde{\lambda}_{i},

where we have used the condition that
\tilde{f}(\tilde{\lambda})
is homogeneous of degree one. Here we need the subsolution condition (
4.14
) for
\chi^{\prime}
. For each fixed
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have from (
4.17
) that

(4.18)

e^{-F(x_{0})}\leq\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(\tilde{\mu})\cdot\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{j}}(\tilde{\mu})\cdot\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\leq\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(\tilde{\mu})\cdot\tilde{\lambda}_{i}+\frac{1-\delta}{1-0.5\delta}e^{-F(x_{0})},

which implies that

\frac{\delta}{2-\delta}e^{-F(x_{0})}\leq\frac{\partial\tilde{f}}{\partial\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}(\tilde{\mu})\cdot\tilde{\lambda}_{i}\leq\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}{\tilde{\mu}_{i}}(1-\delta)e^{-F(x_{0})}.

Hence

\lambda_{i}\leq\frac{(2-\delta)(1-\delta)}{\delta}\mu_{i}\leq\frac{(2-\delta)(1-\delta)}{\delta}\kappa_{2}=R.

The remaining arguments are the same as those in Section
2
. The proof of Theorem
4.1
is completed. Q.E.D.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
A,B
are both
n\times n
Hermitian matrices satisfying
B\geq A
, i.e.
B-A
is semi-positive definite. If we denote
\lambda_{i}(A)
the eigenvalues of
A
in the increasing order, i.e.
0<\lambda_{1}(A)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}(A)
, and similar notation for
\lambda_{i}(B)
, then

\lambda_{i}(B)\geq\lambda_{i}(A),\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,n.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.
It follows from the Courant-Fischer min-max Theorem that

\lambda_{i}(A)=\min\Big\{\max\{\langle Az,z\rangle:~z\in U,\,|z|=1\},~U\mbox{ with dim}_{{\mathbf{C}}}U=i\Big\},

where the minimum is taken over all
complex
vector spaces
U\subset{\mathbf{C}}^{n}
with dimension
i
. For each fixed vector space
U
we have

\max\{\langle Bz,z\rangle:~z\in U,\,|z|=1\}\geq\max\{\langle Az,z\rangle:~z\in U,\,|z|=1\}.

Taking minimum over such
U
’s gives the desired inequality. Q.E.D.

4.1.
Examples

4.1.1.
The
J
-equation

We consider the following form of
J
-equation:

(4.19)

(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=ne^{F}\omega\wedge(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)^{n-1},\quad\sup_{X}\varphi=0.

In the notation of
f(\lambda[h_{\varphi}])
, this equation is equivalent to

f(\lambda)=\frac{\sigma_{n}(\lambda)}{\sigma_{n-1}(\lambda)}=e^{F},

where
\lambda\in\Gamma_{n}
is the vector of eigenvalues of
\omega^{-1}\cdot(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)
. Rewriting the nonlinear operator
f
in terms of
\tilde{f}
, we see that
\tilde{f}(\tilde{\lambda})=\sigma_{1}(\tilde{\lambda})
, which satisfies the required conditions. The subsolution condition (
4.14
) for
\chi^{\prime}
with this equation states that

\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}\leq(1-\delta){e}^{-F(z)},\quad\forall\,i=1,\ldots,n,

which is just the subsolution condition introduced in
[
22
]
(see also
[
23
]
). We mention that for the
J
-equation, it is proved in
[
21
]
that the existence of a subsolution is equivalent to a Nakai-Moishezon criterion, thus confirming a conjecture of
[
19
]
.

[
7
]
, when
1\leq k\leq n

(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)^{n}=e^{F}\omega^{n-k}\wedge(\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi)^{k},\quad\sup_{X}\varphi=0.

The subsolution condition (
4.14
) for
\chi^{\prime}
with this equation is the one studied in
[
7
]
.

On Complex Intuitionistic Fuzzy Lie Sub-superalgebras


Abstract
. A complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie superalgebra is a generalization of intuitionistic fuzzy Lie superalgebra whose membership function takes values in the unit disk in the complex plane. In this research, the concepts of complex intuitionistic fuzzy sets are introduced to Lie superalgebras. We define the complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebras and complex intuitionistic fuzzy ideals of Lie superalgebras. Then, we study some related properties of complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebras and complex intuitionistic fuzzy ideals. Finally, we define the image and preimage of complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebra under Lie superalgebra anti-homomorphism. The properties of anti-complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebras and anti-complex intuitionistic fuzzy ideals under anti-homomorphisms of Lie superalgebras are investigated. AMS classification : 08A72, 03E72, 20N25.

2.
Complex intuitionistic fuzzy sets

Let
X\not=\phi
. A complex intuitionistic fuzzy set on
X
is an object having the form
A=\{(x,\lambda_{A}(x),\rho_{A}(x))\ |x\in X\}
, where the complex functions
\lambda_{A}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\rho_{A}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the degree of membership (namely
\lambda_{A}(x)
) and the degree of non-membership (namely
\rho_{A}(x)
) of each element
x\in X
to the set
A
, respectively, that assign to any element
x\in X
complex numbers
\lambda_{A}(x)
,
\rho_{A}(x)
lie within the unit circle with the property
|\lambda_{A}(x)|+|\rho_{A}(x)|\leq 1
.
For the sake of simplicity, we shall use the symbol
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
for the complex intuitionistic fuzzy set
A=\{(x,\lambda_{A}(x),\rho_{A}(x))\ |x\in X\}
.

We shall assume
\lambda_{A}(x)
,
\rho_{A}(x)
will be represented by
r_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(x)}
and
\hat{r}_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)}
, respectively, where
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
r_{A}(x),\hat{r}_{A}(x),\omega_{A}(x),\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\in[0,1]
. Thus the property of
|\lambda_{A}(x)|+|\rho_{A}(x)|\leq 1
implies
r_{A}(x)+\hat{r}_{A}(x)\leq 1
. Note that the intuitionistic fuzzy set is a special case of complex intuitionistic fuzzy set with
\omega_{A}(x)=\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)=0.

Also, if
\rho_{A}(x)=(1-r_{A}(x))e^{i2\pi(1-\omega_{A}(x))}
, then we obtain a complex fuzzy set.
Let
\alpha e^{i2\pi\beta}
and
\gamma e^{i2\pi\delta}
be two complex numbers, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in[0,1]
. By
\alpha e^{i2\pi\beta}\leq\gamma e^{i2\pi\delta}
we mean
\alpha\leq\gamma
and
\beta\leq\delta
.
In this paper,we use the symbols
a\wedge b=min\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b=max\{a,b\}
.
Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
be a complex intuitionistic fuzzy set on
X
with the degree of membership
\lambda_{A}(x)=r_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(x)}
and the degree of non-membership
\rho_{A}(x)=\hat{r}_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)}
. Then
A
is said to be a homogeneous complex intuitionistic fuzzy set if the following two conditions hold
\forall x,y\in X

(1)
r_{A}(x)\leq r_{A}(y)
if and only if
\omega_{A}(x)\leq\omega_{A}(y)
,

(2)
\hat{r}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{r}_{A}(y)
if and only if
\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{\omega}_{A}(y)
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be two complex intuitionistic fuzzy sets on the same set
X
, we say that
A
is homogeneous with
B
if the following conditions hold
\forall x,y\in X

(1)
r_{A}(x)\leq r_{B}(y)
if and only if
\omega_{A}(x)\leq\omega_{B}(y)
,

(2)
\hat{r}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y)
if and only if
\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{\omega}_{B}(y)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
V
be a
K
-vector space. A CIF set
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
of a vector space

is called a CIF vector space of

, if it satisfies the following conditions

for any

,
\alpha\in K

(1)
\lambda_{A}(x+y)\geq\lambda_{A}(x)\wedge\lambda_{A}(y)

\rho_{A}(x+y)\leq\rho_{A}(x)\vee\rho_{A}(y)

\lambda_{A}(\alpha x)\geq\lambda_{A}(x)

\rho_{A}(\alpha x)\leq\rho_{A}(x)

From this definition, we know that for any

\lambda_{A}(0)\geq\lambda_{A}(x)
and
\rho_{A}(0)\leq\rho_{A}(x)

\rho_{A}(0)=0e^{i2(\pi)0}=0

.

A+B

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF vector subspaces of a vector subspace
V
such that
A
is homogenous with
B
. Then
A+B=(\lambda_{A+B},\rho_{A+B})
is also a CIF vector subspaces of a vector subspace
V
.

Proof.

One can easily show that
A+B
is homogenous. Now, for any
x,y\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{A+B}(x)\wedge\lambda_{A+B}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{A+B}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A+B}(x)}\wedge r_{A+B}(y)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A+B}(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{A+B}(x)\wedge r_{A+B}(y))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{A+B}(x)\wedge\omega_{A+B}(x))},

and

\displaystyle r_{A+B}(x)\wedge r_{A+B}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x=a+b}\{r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b)\}\wedge\sup_{y=c+d}\{r_{A}(c)\wedge r_{B}(d)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x=a+b,y=c+d}\{(r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b))\wedge(r_{A}(c)\wedge r_{B}(d))\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x=a+b,y=c+d}\{(r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{A}(c))\wedge(r_{B}(b)\wedge r_{B}(d))\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x=a+b,y=c+d}\{r_{A}(a+c)\wedge r_{B}(b+d)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{A+B}(x+y).

Similarly,
\omega_{A+B}(x)\wedge\omega_{A+B}(y)\leq\omega_{A+B}(x+y)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lambda_{A+B}(x)\wedge\lambda_{A+B}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{A+B}(x)\wedge r_{A+B}(y))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{A+B}(x)\wedge\omega_{A+B}(x))}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r_{A+B}(x+y)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A+B}(x+y)}=\lambda_{A+B}(x+y),

and

\displaystyle\rho_{A+B}(x)\vee\rho_{A+B}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)}\vee\hat{r}_{A+B}(y)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(y)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)\vee\hat{r}_{A+B}(y))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)\vee\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x))},

and

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)\vee\hat{r}_{A+B}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{x=a+b}\{\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b)\}\vee\inf_{y=c+d}\{\hat{r}_{A}(c)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(d)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{x=a+b,y=c+d}\{(\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b))\vee(\hat{r}_{A}(c)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(d))\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{x=a+b,y=c+d}\{(\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{A}(c))\vee(\hat{r}_{B}(b)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(d))\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{x=a+b,y=c+d}\{\hat{r}_{A}(a+c)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b+d)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A+B}(x+y).

Similarly,
\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)\vee\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(y)\geq\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x+y)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\rho_{A+B}(x)\vee\rho_{A+B}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)\vee\hat{r}_{A+B}(y))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)\vee\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x))}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A+B}(x+y)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x+y)}=\rho_{A+B}(x+y).

Also, for
\alpha\in K
, we have

\lambda_{A+B}(x)=r_{A+B}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A+B}(x)}
, and

\displaystyle r_{A+B}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x=a+b}\{r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha x=\alpha a+\alpha b}\{r_{A}(\alpha a)\wedge r_{B}(\alpha b)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha x=c+d}\{r_{A}(c)\wedge r_{B}(d)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{A+B}(\alpha x).

Similarly,
\omega_{A+B}(x)\leq\omega_{A+B}(\alpha x)
. Thus,

\lambda_{A+B}(x)=r_{A+B}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A+B}(x)}\leq r_{A+B}(\alpha x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A+B}(\alpha x)}=\lambda_{A+B}(\alpha x),

and

\rho_{A+B}(x)=\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)}
, but

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{x=a+b}\{\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b)\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha x=\alpha a+\alpha b}\{\hat{r}_{A}(\alpha a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(\alpha b)\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha x=c+d}\{\hat{r}_{A}(c)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(d)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A+B}(\alpha x).

Similarly,
\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)\geq\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(\alpha x)
. Thus,

\rho_{A+B}(x)=\hat{r}_{A+B}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(x)}\geq\hat{r}_{A+B}(\alpha x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A+B}(\alpha x)}=\rho_{A+B}(\alpha x).

Hence,
A+B=(\lambda_{A+B},\rho_{A+B})
is a CIF vector subspaces of a vector subspace
V
.

4.
On lie superalgebra anti homomoiphisms

Remark: If
\phi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is a linear map between lie superalgebras such that
\phi(a_{\alpha}b_{\beta})=(-1)^{\alpha\beta}\phi(b_{\beta})\phi(a_{\alpha})
for all
a_{\alpha},b_{\beta}\in h(V)
,
\alpha,\beta=0,1
, then
\phi
is called an anti-homomorphism from
V
into
V^{\prime}
. In this case for any
a_{\alpha},b_{\beta}\in h(V)
, we have that

\displaystyle\phi([a_{\alpha},b_{\beta}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(a_{\alpha}b_{\beta}-(-1)^{\alpha\beta}b_{\beta}a_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(a_{\alpha}b_{\beta})-(-1)^{\alpha\beta}\phi(b_{\beta}a_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\alpha\beta}\phi(b_{\beta})\phi(a_{\alpha})-\phi(a_{\alpha})\phi(b_{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\phi(a_{\alpha})\phi(b_{\beta})-(-1)^{\alpha\beta}\phi(b_{\beta})\phi(a_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\phi(a_{\alpha}),\phi(b_{\beta})].

Therefore, if
x=x_{0}+x_{1},y=y_{0}+y_{1}\in V
, then

\displaystyle\phi([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([x_{0},y_{0}]+[x_{0},y_{1}]+[x_{1},y_{0}]+[x_{1},y_{1}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([x_{0},y_{0}])+\phi([x_{0},y_{1}])+\phi([x_{1},y_{0}])+\phi([x_{1},y_{1}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-([\phi(x_{0}),\phi(y_{0})]+[\phi(x_{0}),\phi(y_{1})]+[\phi(x_{1}),\phi(y_{0})]+[\phi(x_{1}),\phi(y_{1})])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\phi(x),\phi(y)].

So we have the following equivalent definition of anti-homomorphism of lie superalgebras as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

If
\phi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is a linear map between lie superalgebras
V,\ V^{\prime}
which satisfies:

(1)

\displaystyle\phi(V_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle V^{\prime}_{\alpha},\ (\alpha=0,1),

(2)

\displaystyle\phi([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\phi(x),\phi(y)]

Then
\phi
is called an anti-homomorphism of lie-superalgebras.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
be a CIF set of
V
. Then
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is called an anti-complex intuitionistic fuzzy (anti-CIF for short) lie sub-superalgebra of
V
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded CIF vector subspace of

(2)
\lambda_{A}(-[x,y])\geq\lambda_{A}(x)\wedge\lambda_{A}(y)

\rho_{A}(-[x,y])\leq\rho_{A}(x)\vee\rho_{A}(y)

If the condition(2) is replaced by (3)

and
\rho_{A}(-[x,y])\leq\rho_{A}(x)\wedge\rho_{A}(y)
, then
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is called an anti-CIF ideal of
V
.

x,y\in V^{{}^{\prime}}
. We need to show that
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])\geq\lambda_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\lambda_{\phi(A)}(y)
and
\rho_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])\leq\rho_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee\rho_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Suppose that
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<\lambda_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\lambda_{\phi(A)}(y)=r_{\phi(A)}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)}\wedge r_{\phi(A)}(y)e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)}
. Then
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])=r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])}<\{r_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge r_{\phi(A)}(y)\}e^{i2\pi\{\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)\}}
, since
\phi(A)
is homogeneous. Thus,
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge r_{\phi(A)}(y)
or
\omega_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)
. If
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge r_{\phi(A)}(y)
, then
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(x)
and
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Let
t\in[0,1]
such that
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<t<r_{\phi(A)}(x)
and
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<t<r_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Then there exist
a\in\phi^{-1}(x)
and
b\in\phi^{-1}(y)
such that
r_{A}(a)>t
,
r_{A}(b)>t
. Since
\phi([a,b])=-[x,y]
, we have that

\displaystyle r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])=\sup_{-[x,y]=\phi([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])}\{r_{A}([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r_{A}([a,b])

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{A}(b)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle t>r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])

which is a contradiction. Similarly for the case
\omega_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Therefore,
\phi(A)
is an anti-CIF lie sub-superalgebra of
V^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Now to show that
\phi(A)
is an anti-CIF ideal of
V^{{}^{\prime}}
, we need to prove that for any
x,y\in V^{{}^{\prime}}
, then
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])\geq\lambda_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee\lambda_{\phi(A)}(y)
and
\rho_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])\leq\rho_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\rho_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Suppose that
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<\lambda_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee\lambda_{\phi(A)}(y)=r_{\phi(A)}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)}\vee r_{\phi(A)}(y)e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)}
. Then, since
\phi(A)
is homogeneous,

\lambda_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])=r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])}<\{r_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee r_{\phi(A)}(y)\}e^{i2\pi\{\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)\}}
. Which implies that
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])

<r_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee r_{\phi(A)}(y)
or
\omega_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<\omega_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee\omega_{\phi(A)}(y)
. If
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(x)\vee r_{\phi(A)}(y)
, then
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(x)
or
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Suppose that
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<r_{\phi(A)}(x)
, then choose
t\in[0,1]
such that
r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])<t<r_{\phi(A)}(x)
, so there exists
a\in\phi^{-1}(x)
such that
r_{A}(a)>t
and since
\phi
is onto there exists
b\in\phi^{-1}(y)
. Now, since
\phi([a,b])=-[\phi(a),\phi(b)]=-[x,y]
then we have that

\displaystyle r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{-[x,y]=\phi([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])}\{r_{A}([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{-[x,y]=\phi([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])}\{r_{A}(a^{{}^{\prime}})\vee r_{A}(b^{{}^{\prime}})\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r_{A}(a)\vee r_{A}(b)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle t>r_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])

which is a contradiction. The other case can be proved similarly. Also, suppose that
\rho_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\rho_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\rho_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Then
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])}>\{\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(y)\}e^{i2\pi\{\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(y)\}}
, since
\phi(A)
is homogenous we have that
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(y)
or
\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(y)
. If
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(y)
, then
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(x)
or
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(y)
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(x)
, choose
t\in[0,1]
such that
\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>t>\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(x)
, then there exists
a\in\phi^{-1}(x)
such that
\hat{r}_{A}(a)<t
, because
\phi
is onto map let
b\in\phi^{-1}(y)
. Since
\phi([a,b])=-[x,y]
, we have that

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{-[x,y]=\phi([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])}\{\hat{r}_{A}([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{-[x,y]=\phi([a^{{}^{\prime}},b^{{}^{\prime}}])}\{\hat{r}_{A}(a^{{}^{\prime}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(b^{{}^{\prime}})\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{A}(a)\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(b)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle t<\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])

which is a contradiction. The other case
\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(-[x,y])>\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(x)\wedge\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(y)
can be proved similarly. Therefore,
\phi(A)
is an anti-CIF ideal of
V^{{}^{\prime}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\phi:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
be a surjective anti-homomorphism of lie-superalgebras. If
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
are anti-CIF ideals of
V
, then the CIF set
\phi(A+B)=\phi(A)+\phi(B)
of
V^{\prime}
is also an anti-CIF ideal.

Proof.

We already proved in Proposition
4.4
that
\phi(A+B)
is an anti-CIF ideal of
V^{{}^{\prime}}
. Therefore the only thing we need to prove is that
\phi(A+B)=\phi(A)+\phi(B)
. Let
y\in V^{{}^{\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi(A+B)}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=\phi(x)}\{\lambda_{A+B}(x)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=\phi(x)}\{\sup_{x=a+b}\{\lambda_{A}(a)\wedge\lambda_{B}(b)\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=\phi(x)}\{\sup_{x=a+b}\{r_{A}(a)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(a)}\wedge r_{B}(b)e^{i2\pi\omega_{B}(b)}\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=\phi(x)}\{\sup_{x=a+b}\{(r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{A}(a)\wedge\omega_{B}(b))}\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=\phi(a)+\phi(b)}\{(r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{A}(a)\wedge\omega_{B}(b))}\}\ \ (A\ and\ B\ are\ homogenous)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=m+n}\{(\sup_{m=\phi(a)}\{r_{A}(a)\}\wedge\sup_{n=\phi(b)}\{r_{B}(b)\})e^{i2\pi(\sup\limits_{m=\phi(a)}\{\omega_{A}(a)\}\wedge\sup\limits_{n=\phi(b)}\{\omega_{B}(b)\})}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=m+n}\{(r_{\phi(A)}(m)\wedge r_{\phi(B)}(n))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{\phi(A)}(m)\wedge\omega_{\phi(B)}(n))}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=m+n}\{r_{\phi(A)}(m)e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(A)}(m)}\wedge r_{\phi(B)}(n)e^{i2\pi\omega_{\phi(B)}(n)}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y=m+n}\{\lambda_{\phi(A)}(m)\wedge\lambda_{\phi(B)}(n)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi(A)+\phi(B)}(y)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi(A+B)}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=\phi(x)}\{\rho_{A+B}(x)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=\phi(x)}\{\inf_{x=a+b}\{\rho_{A}(a)\vee\rho_{B}(b)\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=\phi(x)}\{\inf_{x=a+b}\{\hat{r}_{A}(a)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(a)}\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{B}(b)}\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=\phi(x)}\{\inf_{x=a+b}\{(\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{\omega}_{B}(b))}\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=\phi(a)+\phi(b)}\{(\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{\omega}_{B}(b))}\}\ \ (A\ and\ B\ are\ homogenous)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=m+n}\{(\inf_{m=\phi(a)}\{\hat{r}_{A}(a)\}\vee\inf_{n=\phi(b)}\{\hat{r}_{B}(b)\})e^{i2\pi(\inf\limits_{m=\phi(a)}\{\hat{\omega}_{A}(a)\}\vee\inf\limits_{n=\phi(b)}\{\hat{\omega}_{B}(b)\})}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=m+n}\{(\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(m)\vee\hat{r}_{\phi(B)}(n))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(m)\vee\hat{\omega}_{\phi(B)}(n))}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=m+n}\{\hat{r}_{\phi(A)}(m)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{\phi(A)}(m)}\vee\hat{r}_{\phi(B)}(n)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{\phi(B)}(n)}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y=m+n}\{\rho_{\phi(A)}(m)\vee\rho_{\phi(B)}(n)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi(A)+\phi(B)}(y).

So,
\phi(A+B)=\phi(A)+\phi(B)
is an anti-CIF ideal of
V^{{}^{\prime}}
.
∎

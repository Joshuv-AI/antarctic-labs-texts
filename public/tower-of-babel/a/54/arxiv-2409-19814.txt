Abstract
. We introduce the notion of the Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number for holomorphic 1-forms relative to a pair ( X , V ) (X,V) of complex analytic subvarieties. When the pair ( X , V ) (X,V) consists of isolated complex analytic hypersurfaces, we prove that the Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number is related to the Bruce-Roberts number, the Tjurina number of the 1-form with respect to V V and the Tjurina number of X X , among other invariants. As an application, we present a quasihomogeneity result for germs of holomorphic foliations in complex dimension two.

1.
Introduction and statement of the results

Let
(X,0)
denote the germ of a complex analytic variety at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
, and let
\omega
be the germ of a holomorphic 1-form with an isolated singularity at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. Let
V
be a germ of a complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

invariant
by
\omega
.
In this paper, we introduce the
Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number
of
\omega
relative to the pair
(X,V)
as

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}},

where
\mathcal{O}_{n}
stands for the local ring of holomorphic functions from
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
to
(\mathbb{C},0)
,
I_{V}
is ideal of germs of holomorphic functions vanishing on
(V,0)
, and
\Theta_{X}
is the module of the so-called
logaritmic vector fields
– as defined by K. Saito
[
28
]
:

\Theta_{X}=\{\xi\in\Theta_{n}:\xi(I_{X})\subseteq I_{X}\}.

Here
I_{X}
is the ideal of germs of holomorphic functions vanishing on
(X,0)
and
\Theta_{n}
is the module of holomorphic vector fields on
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. Geometrically,
\Theta_{X}
represents the
\mathcal{O}_{n}
-submodule of
\Theta_{n}
that are tangent to
(X,0)
along the regular points of
X
.
We recall that
V
is said to be
invariant
by
\omega
if
T_{p}V\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\omega_{p})
for every regular point
p
on
V
.

Note that if
\omega=df
is an exact holomorphic 1-form with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then
V=\{f=0\}
is a complex analytic hypersurface invariant by
\omega
, and consequently
\tau_{BR}(df,X,V)
coincides with the
Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number

\tau_{BR}(f,X)
of
f
along
X
, as defined by Ahmed-Ruas
[
1
]
and Bivià-Ausina–Ruas
[
5
]
. Observe also that
\tau_{BR}(df,\mathbb{C}^{n},V)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\left\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial{x}_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial f}{\partial{x}_{n}},f\right\rangle}=\tau_{0}(V)
is the classical
Tjurina number
of
V
defined by G.N. Tjurina
[
29
]
. The Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number of holomorphic functions has been studied in
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
7
]
.

On the other hand, the
Bruce-Roberts number of
\omega
relative to
(X,0)
was defined in
[
4
]
by the following expression:

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})}.

Analogously,
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)
generalizes
Bruce-Roberts number

\mu_{BR}(f,X)
of a holomorphic function
f
along
X
defined by J.W. Bruce and R.M. Roberts
[
8
]
, and the classical
Milnor number
of
f
defined in
[
23
]
. It follows directly from the definitions that

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)\leq\mu_{BR}(\omega,X).

Therefore, if
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)<\infty
then
\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)<\infty
.

The aim of this paper is to establish connections between the Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number and other indices of 1-forms such as the Bruce-Roberts number of 1-forms itself, the
Tjurina number of a 1-form
with respect to an invariant isolated complex analytic hypersurface and the
Tjurina number
of
X
, (see for instance, Theorem
1
and Proposition
4.1
). As application, we obtain some results for holomorphic foliations in complex dimension two. Indeed, by combining a Mattei’s theorem
[
27
, Théorèm A]
with a corollary of Bivià-Ausina–Kourliouros–Ruas
[
6
, Corollary 3.4]
, we prove a quasihomogeneity result for germs of non-dicritical curve generalized holomorphic foliations with respect to a non-invariant complex analytic curve
X
, (see Theorem
2
). We remark that curve generalized foliations were introduced by Camacho-Lins Neto-Sad in
[
11
]
.

Before we state our results, we define the

Tjurina number
of
\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}(x)dx_{j}
with respect to a germ
V=\{f=0\}
of complex analytic hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
invariant by
\omega
as follows

(1)

\displaystyle\tau_{0}(\omega,V):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n},f\rangle}.

For a holomorphic vector field
v
with an isolated singular on a complex analytic hypersurface
V
, the Tjurina number of
v
with respect to
V
first appears in the work of Gómez-Mont
[
21
]
though he does not use this terminology. To our knowledge, the first authors to explicitly use the term “Tjurina name” in the context of foliations were Cano-Corral-Mol in
[
12
]
. This number also appears in the study of Baum-Bott residues of foliations in
[
24
]
. More recently,
[
17
]
exhibits a relationship between the Milnor and Tjurina number of germs of foliations on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. The above-mentioned definition can be viewed as a natural extension of the Tjurina number for 1-forms on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
to higher dimensions.

In
[
22
]
,
[
15
]
, Ebeling–Gusein-Zade introduced the notion of the
GSV
-index for a 1-form
\omega
with respect to
X
. This index is denoted by
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)
. According to
[
22
]
or
[
16
, Theorem 5.3.35]
, when
X=\phi^{-1}(0)
is an isolated complete intersection singularity with
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k})
(an ICIS, for short), this index can also be defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}^{n},0}}{I_{X}+I_{k+1}\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}},

where
I_{X}
denotes the ideal of germs of holomorphic functions vanishing on
(X,0)
and
I_{k+1}\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}
represents the ideal generated by the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
-minors of the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{1}&\cdots&A_{n}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right).

Now, let
X=\phi^{-1}(0)
be an ICIS at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, defined by
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k})
, and let
V=\{f=0\}
be
an isolated complex hypersurface invariant by
\omega
. Assuming that
X
is not invariant by
\omega
, we introduce the
GSV
-index of
\omega
with respect to the pair
(X,V)
as follows:

(6)

\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{I_{X}+I_{k+1}\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}+\langle f\rangle}.

With this terminology, we can establish our first result:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. Suppose that the pair
(X,V)
are isolated complex analytic hypersurfaces at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
V
invariant by
\omega
and
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Then

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}},

where
\Theta_{X}^{T}
is the submodule of
\Theta_{X}
of trivial vector fields.

We emphasize that
\Theta_{X}^{T}
is defined in
[
26
, Section 2]
, and if
\omega
is a holomorphic 1-form at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
(X,0)
is an ICIS such that
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)<\infty
, then

(7)

\tau_{0}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\Theta_{X}}{\Theta_{X}^{T}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}

by
[
26
, Proposition 4.6]
and
[
25
, Corollary 3.7]
.

As a consequence, we obtain the following Corollary:

Corollary 1
.

Let
X\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
be an isolated complex analytic hypersurface and
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
a function germ such that
\mu_{BR}(f,X)<\infty
. Then

\tau_{BR}(f,X)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(df;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{df(\Theta_{X})\cap\langle f\rangle}{df(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap\langle f\rangle},

where
V=\{f=0\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
.

As an application, motivated by
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
, we present the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a non-dicritical curve generalized foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
X
be a germ of a reduce curve at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
.
Let
V=\{f=0\}
be the reduced equation of the total set of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. If
\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\tau_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,V)
, then
there exists coordinates
(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, with
u(0)=0
,
v(0)=0
,
g(0)\neq 0
and integers
\alpha,\beta
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(u,v)=\sum_{\alpha i+\beta j=d}P_{i,j}u^{i}v^{j},\,\,\,\,P_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C},

g\omega=df+h(\beta vdu-\alpha udv),

and the pair
(f,X)
is relatively quasihomogeneous in these coordinates.

The paper is organized as follows: Section
2
is devoted to establishing known results of commutative algebra that are necessary for the proof of Theorem
1
. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1
, and in Section
4
we present a formula that relates the Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number and the Bruce-Roberts number, following
[
6
]
. Finally, in Section
5
, by combining a Mattei’s theorem
[
27
, Théorèm A]
with the corollary of Bivià-Ausina–Kourliouros–Ruas
[
6
, Corollary 3.4]
, we prove Theorem
2
that can be viewed as a quasihomogeneity result for germs of non-dicritical curve generalized holomorphic foliations with respect to a non-invariant complex curve.

2.
Basic tools

This section is devoted to establishing some known results in commutative algebra and properties of
\Theta_{X}
and
\Theta_{X}^{T}
that we will use in the proof of our main result.
First, we state a technical lemma from
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},g,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, where
f_{i}
and
g
are relatively prime, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Then

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},gp_{1},\ldots,gp_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},g\rangle}.

The following lemma immediately follows from
[
3
, Proposition 2.1 (ii)]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
M_{1},M_{2}
and
M_{3}
are submodules of
M
, with
M_{3}\subset M_{2}
, then

\dfrac{M_{1}+M_{2}}{M_{1}+M_{3}}\cong\dfrac{M_{2}}{M_{3}+(M_{1}\cap M_{2})}.

We also state the following result from
[
26
, Proposition 2.1]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(X,0)
be the ICIS determined by
\phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{k}):(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{k},0)
, then

\Theta_{X}^{T}=I_{k+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}_{n}}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{k}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right)+\left\langle\phi_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x}_{j}},\,\,i=1,\ldots,k,\,\,j=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle,

where the first term in the right-hand side is the submodule of
\Theta_{X}
generated by the
(k+1)
-
minors of the matrix.

Consequently, if
\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}(x)dx_{j}
, Proposition
2.3
implies that

(8)

\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})=I_{k+1}\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}+\left\langle\phi_{i}\cdot A_{j},\,\,i=1,\ldots,k,\,\,j=1,\ldots,n\right\rangle

where
I_{k+1}\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}
is the ideal generated by the
(k+1)
-minors of the matrix given in (
1
).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

For the convenience of the reader, we repeat the statement of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. Suppose that the pair
(X,V)
are isolated complex analytic hypersurfaces at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
V
invariant by
\omega
and
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Then

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}},

where
\Theta_{X}^{T}
is the submodule of
\Theta_{X}
of trivial vector fields.

Proof.

The exactness of the following sequence of
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces

0\longrightarrow\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}{\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}{\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}\longrightarrow 0,

implies that

(9)

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}.

To compute
\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}
, we apply Lemma
2.2
with
M_{1}=I_{V}
,
M_{2}=\omega(\Theta_{X})
, and
M_{3}=\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})
, yielding

(10)

\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}\cong\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+(\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V})}.

From the exact sequence

(11)

0\longrightarrow\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+(\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}\longrightarrow\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}\longrightarrow\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+(\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V})}\longrightarrow 0,

we deduce

(12)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+(\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}.

Now,

(13)

\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+(\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}\cong\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap(\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V})}\cong\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}.

Substituting (
13
) into equation (
12
), we obtain

(14)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}.

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}
. Suppose
X=\{\phi=0\}
,
V=\{f=0\}
, and

\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}(x)dx_{j}
. Then, using (
8
),

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}\omega\\
d\phi\end{pmatrix}+\langle\phi A_{i}\rangle_{1\leq i\leq n}+\langle f\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}n}{\langle\phi A_{1},\ldots,\phi A_{n},\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}A_{k}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}A_{j},f\rangle_{(j,k)\in\Lambda}},

where
\Lambda=\{(j,k):j,k=1,\ldots,n;j\neq k\}
. Since
V
is invariant and
X
is not invariant under
\omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle\phi,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}A_{k}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}A_{j},f\rangle_{(j,k)\in\Lambda}}

\displaystyle+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}n}{\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n},\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}A_{k}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}A_{j},f\rangle_{(j,k)\in\Lambda}},

by Lemma
2.1
. Noting that
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{j}}A_{k}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{k}}A_{j}\in\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\rangle
, and using (
6
), we deduce

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n},f\rangle}.

Thus, by (
1
), we have

(15)

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V).

Finally, from (
9
), (
14
), and (
15
), we conclude

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V)-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}.

Using (
7
), where
\displaystyle\tau_{0}(X)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

We present examples in dimension three and two where Theorem
1
is verified.

Example 3.1
.

Let
X=\{\phi(x,y,z)=x^{3}+yz=0\}
and
V=\{f(x,y,z)=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=0\}
be germs of isolated complex hypersurfaces on
(\mathbb{C}^{3},0)
. Consider

\omega=df+f(zdx+xdy+ydz)=(2x+zf)dx+(2y+xf)dy+(2z+yf)dz.

Clearly
V
is invariant by
\omega
, and since

\displaystyle\omega\wedge d\phi

\displaystyle=[((2x+zf)dx+(2y+xf)dy+(2z+yf)dz)]\wedge(3x^{2}dx+zdy+ydz)

\displaystyle=(2xz-6x^{2}y+(z^{2}-3x^{2})f)dx\wedge dy+(2y^{2}-2z^{2}+(xy-yz)f)dy\wedge dz

\displaystyle+(2xy-6x^{2}+(yz-3x^{2}y)f)dx\wedge dz,

we have
X
is not invariant by
\omega
. Additionally, by
[
5
, Theorem 2.6]
we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{X}=\left\langle z\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}-3x^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y},\ y\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}-3x^{2}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z},\ y\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}-z\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z},\ x\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}+2y\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}+z\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\right\rangle.

Using SINGULAR
[
13
]
to compute the indices, we get
\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=5
,
\tau_{0}(\omega,V)=1
,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)=5
,

(16)

\tau_{0}(X)=2\text{ and }\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}=1.

By (
14
) and (
16
) we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}=1.

Hence, Theorem
1
is satisfied, since

\displaystyle 5=\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}

\displaystyle=5+1-2+1.

Example 3.2
.

Let
X=\{\phi(x,y)=y^{p}-x^{q}=0\}
and
V=\{f(x,y)=xy=0\}
, representing germs of complex analytic curves on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the foliation defined
\omega=\lambda xdy+ydx
, with
\lambda\neq-\displaystyle\frac{p}{q},\ \lambda\neq 1
. Note that
X
is not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, while
V
is invariant by
\mathcal{F}
, as seen from the following calculations:

\displaystyle\omega\wedge d\phi

\displaystyle=(\lambda xdy+ydx)\wedge(py^{p-1}dy-qx^{q-1}dx)=(py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q})\ dx\wedge dy,

\displaystyle\omega\wedge df

\displaystyle=(\lambda xdy+ydx)\wedge(ydx+xdy)=((1-\lambda)xy)\ dx\wedge dy.

Since
\lambda\neq-\displaystyle\frac{p}{q},\ \lambda\neq 1
, we find that
\tau_{0}(\omega,V)=1
and
\tau_{0}(X)=(p-1)(q-1)
. Next, we compute the remaining indices. First, observe that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}=\dfrac{\langle\ (p+\lambda q)xy,\ py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q},xy\ \rangle}{\langle\ y(y^{p}-x^{q}),\ \lambda x(y^{p}-x^{q}),\ py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q},xy\ \rangle}=\dfrac{I}{\langle y^{p+1},x^{q+1}\rangle+I},

where
I=\langle xy,py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q}\rangle
. Since,
y^{p}=\alpha x^{q}
on
I
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, we have:

\displaystyle y^{p+1}=y\cdot y^{p}=y\cdot\alpha x^{q}\in I,

and similarly,
x^{q+1}\in I
. Thus,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})+I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})+I_{V}}=0
.
From equation (
14
), we have:

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})}=\tau_{0}(X)=(p-1)(q-1).

Now, for
\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)
, note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle y^{p}-x^{q},py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q},xy\rangle}.

Rewriting the ideal, we get

\displaystyle\langle y^{p}-x^{q},py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q},xy\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y^{p}-x^{q},p(y^{p}-x^{q})+px^{q}+\lambda qx^{q},xy\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y^{p}-x^{q},(p+\lambda q)x^{q},xy\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y^{p},x^{q},xy\rangle,

therefore

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle y^{p}-x^{q},py^{p}+\lambda qx^{q},xy\rangle}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{2}}{\langle y^{p},x^{q},xy\rangle}=p+q-1.

Finally, let’s compute the Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number. From
[
19
, Example 1]
, we know

\displaystyle\Theta_{X}=\left\langle qy\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+px\frac{\partial}{\partial x},py^{p-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+qx^{q-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right\rangle.

Hence, we have
\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=p+q
. Since

\displaystyle\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p+q-1)+1-(p-1)(q-1)+(p-1)(q-1)

we conclude that Theorem
1
is verified.

4.
The Bruce-Roberts Tjurina number and the Bruce-Roberts number

Let
(X,0)
be a complex analytic subvariety and
\omega
be a germ of a holomorphic 1-form with isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)<\infty
. We consider
V=\{f=0\}
a germ of complex hypersurface invariant by
\omega
. We also denote

\Theta_{V}^{\omega}=\{\delta\in\Theta_{n}:\omega(\delta)\in\langle f\rangle\}
, and by
H_{\omega}=\{\zeta\in\Theta_{n}:\omega(\zeta)=0\}
the submodule of vector fields tangent to
\omega
. Note that
H_{\omega}\subset\Theta_{V}^{\omega}
.
Inspired by Bivià-Ausina–Kourliouros–Ruas
[
6
]
, we define the following numbers

\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\Theta_{n}}{\Theta_{X}+H_{\omega}}

and

\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\Theta_{n}}{\Theta_{X}+\Theta_{V}^{\omega}}

in case where these numbers are finite.
Note that
\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega)\geq\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V)
, and

\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega)-\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\Theta_{V}^{\omega}}{H_{\omega}+(\Theta_{X}\cap\Theta_{V}^{\omega})}

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\omega,X)
be a pair in
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
with
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)<\infty
. Suppose that
V
is a complex hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
invariant by
\omega
. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(\omega)+\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega)

\displaystyle\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{0}(\omega,V)+\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V)

In particular,

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)-\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=\mu_{0}(\omega)-\tau_{0}(\omega,V)+\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega)-\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V).

Proof.

Let
\omega=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}dx_{j}
and set
\langle\omega\rangle=\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\rangle
. Then,
the proof follows from the following exact sequences of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
-modules:

0\longrightarrow\dfrac{\Theta_{n}}{\Theta_{X}+H_{\omega}}{\overset{\cdot\omega}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})}{\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle\omega\rangle}\longrightarrow 0

0\longrightarrow\dfrac{\Theta_{n}}{\Theta_{X}+\Theta^{\omega}_{V}}{\overset{\cdot\omega}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\omega(\Theta_{X})+\langle f\rangle}{\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}}\dfrac{\mathcal{O}_{n}}{\langle\omega\rangle+\langle f\rangle}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\cdot\omega
is the evaluation map and
\pi
is induced by the inclusion
\omega(\Theta_{X})\subseteq\langle\omega\rangle
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain an algebraic characterization of the equality of the Bruce-Roberts Milnor and Tjurina numbers.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
(\omega,X)
be a pair in
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
with
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)<\infty
. Suppose that
V
is a complex hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
invariant by
\omega
. Then the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)=\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)

(2)

\mu_{0}(\omega)=\tau_{0}(\omega,V)
and
\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega)=\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V)
, the last equation is equivalent to

\Theta_{V}^{\omega}=H_{\omega}+\Theta_{X}\cap\Theta_{V}^{\omega}.

When
\omega=df
, for some
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, and
\mu_{BR}(f,X)<\infty
, we recover
[
6
, Corollary 2.2]
.

In the case that
X
is an isolated complex hypersurface at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(\omega,X)
be a pair in
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
with
\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)<\infty
. Suppose that
V
is a complex hypersurface with an isolated singularity at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
invariant by
\omega
. Then

\overline{\mu}_{X}(\omega)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)-\tau_{0}(X)

and

\overline{\tau}_{X}(\omega,V)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap\langle f\rangle}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap\langle f\rangle}

Proof.

It follows from
[
4
, Theorem 1]
and Theorem
1
that

(17)

\mu_{BR}(\omega,X)=\mu_{0}(\omega)+\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,0)-\tau_{0}(X)

(18)

\tau_{BR}(\omega,X,V)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\omega;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\omega,V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap I_{V}}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap I_{V}}.

We conclude the proof by comparing (
17
) and (
18
) with Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Now, we denote by
r_{f}(\omega(\Theta_{X}))
the minimum of
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
f^{r}\in\omega(\Theta_{X})
. If such
r
does not exist, we set then
r_{f}(\omega(\Theta_{X}))=\infty
.

Corollary 4.4

5.
Applications to holomorphic foliations in complex dimension two

In this section, we apply our results to holomorphic foliations on
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
. For a general overview of Foliation theory, we refer the reader to
[
9
]
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\omega=0
be a germ of a singular holomorphic foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Let
V=\{f=0\}
be an
\mathcal{F}
-invariant curve, where
f\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Then, there exists
g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, with
f
relative prime to
g
and
h
respectively, and a holomorphic 1-form
\eta
(see Saito
[
28
]
) such that

g\omega=hdf+f\eta.

The
Gómez-Mont–Seade–Verjovsky index
of the foliation
\mathcal{F}
at the origin with respect to
V
is

\operatorname{GSV}_{0}(\mathcal{F},V)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial{V}}\frac{g}{h}d\left(\frac{h}{g}\right),

where
\partial{V}
is the link of
V
at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. This index was introduced in
[
20
]
but here we
follow the presentation of
[
10
]
.

We use the following result of Gómez-Mont
[
21
]
, (see also
[
17
, Proposition 6.2]
)

(20)

\operatorname{GSV}_{0}(\mathcal{F},V)=\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F},V)-\tau_{0}(V)

to prove the following corollary.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}:\omega=0
be a singular holomorphic foliation at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Suppose that
V=\{f=0\}
is a complex analytic curve at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
invariant by
\omega
. Then

\displaystyle\tau_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,V)-\tau_{BR}(f,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\mathcal{F};X,V,0)-\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(df;X,V,0)+\operatorname{GSV}_{0}(\mathcal{F},V)

\displaystyle+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap\langle f\rangle}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap\langle f\rangle}-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{df(\Theta_{X})\cap\langle f\rangle}{df(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap\langle f\rangle}

Proof.

According to Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
, we have

\tau_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,V)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(\mathcal{F};X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(\mathcal{F},V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{\omega(\Theta_{X})\cap\langle f\rangle}{\omega(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap\langle f\rangle}

\tau_{BR}(f,X)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{GSV}}(df;X,V,0)+\tau_{0}(V)-\tau_{0}(X)+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\dfrac{df(\Theta_{X})\cap\langle f\rangle}{df(\Theta_{X}^{T})\cap\langle f\rangle}

We conclude the proof by subtracting the above equations and using (
20
).
∎

Now we present an application with respect to the notion of
relatively quasihomogeneous
of a germ
f\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
along a variety
X
. This concept was defined by Bivià-Ausina–Kourliouros–Ruas
[
6
]
:

Definition 5.1
.

A pair
(f,X)
in
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
will be called relatively quasihomogeneous
if there exists a vector of positive rational numbers

w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{+}
, a
system of coordinates
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and a system of generators
\langle h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\rangle=I_{X}
of the ideal of functions vanishing on
X
, such that

f(x)=\sum_{\langle w,m\rangle=1}a_{m}x^{m},\,\,\,a_{m}\in\mathbb{C}

h_{i}(x)=\sum_{\langle w,m\rangle=d_{i}}b_{m,i}x^{m},\,\,\,b_{m,i}\in\mathbb{C},\,\,\,i=1,\ldots,m

where each
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
is the quasihomogeneous degree of
h_{i}
.

To continue, motivated by
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
, we state and prove our second theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a germ of a non-dicritical curve generalized foliation at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
, and let
X
be a germ of a reduce curve at
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
not invariant by
\mathcal{F}
.
Let
V=\{f=0\}
be the reduced equation of the total set of separatrices of
\mathcal{F}
. If
\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\tau_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,V)
, then
there exists coordinates
(u,v)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, with
u(0)=0
,
v(0)=0
,
g(0)\neq 0
and integers
\alpha,\beta
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(u,v)=\sum_{\alpha i+\beta j=d}P_{i,j}u^{i}v^{j},\,\,\,\,P_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C},

g\omega=df+h(\beta vdu-\alpha udv),

and the pair
(f,X)
is relatively quasihomogeneous in these coordinates.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be defined by
\omega=Adx+Bdy
. Since
\mu_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X)=\tau_{BR}(\mathcal{F},X,V)
, we have

\mu_{0}(\omega)=\tau_{0}(\omega,V)
and

(21)

\Theta_{V}^{\omega}=H_{\omega}+\Theta_{X}\cap\Theta_{V}^{\omega}

by Corollary
4.2
. The equality
\mu_{0}(\omega)=\tau_{0}(\omega,V)
implies that
f\in\langle A,B\rangle
. According to
[
27
, Théorème A]
, there exists coordinates
(u,v)\in(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
,
g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
, with
u(0)=0
,
v(0)=0
,
g(0)\neq 0
and integers
\alpha,\beta
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(22)

f(u,v)=\sum_{\alpha i+\beta j=d}P_{i,j}u^{i}v^{j}

and

(23)

g\omega=df+h(\beta vdu-\alpha udv).

Let
X_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha u\frac{\partial}{\partial{u}}+\beta v\frac{\partial}{\partial{v}}
. It follows from (
22
) and (
23
) that
X_{\alpha,\beta}\in\Theta_{V}^{\omega}
. Suppose that
X_{\alpha,\beta}\not\in\Theta_{X}
in these coordinates (otherwise, there is noting to prove by
[
6
, Corollary 3.4]
). By (
21
), we get that there exists
\zeta\in H_{\omega}
such that
\delta=X_{\alpha,\beta}-\zeta\in\Theta_{X}\cap\Theta_{V}^{\omega}
. Since
\zeta
is a vector field defining
\mathcal{F}
,
f
is an eigenfunction for
\zeta
, and from (
22
),
f
also is an eigenfunction for
X_{\alpha,\beta}
. Thus,
f
is an eigenfunction for
\delta\in\Theta_{X}
. Finally, we obtain that the pair
(f,X)
is relatively quasihomogeneous in these coordinates by
[
6
, Corollary 3.4]
.
∎

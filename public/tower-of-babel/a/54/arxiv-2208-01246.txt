Finite Theta Correspondence of Almost Characters
Shu-Yen Pan

Abstract
. The theory of almost characters which is closely related to character sheaves is proposed by Lusztig to study the representation theory of finite reductive groups. In this article we show that the decomposition of the Weil character for finite reductive dual pairs ( Sp 2 ​ n , O 2 ​ n ′ ± ) ({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}}) or ( Sp 2 ​ n , SO 2 ​ n ′ + 1 ) ({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1}) with respect to the almost characters is exactly the same as the decomposition with respect to the irreducible characters. As a consequence, the finite theta correspondence on almost characters is established.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
G
be a classical group such as
{\rm GL}_{n},{\rm U}_{n},{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{n}
defined
over a finite field
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
of odd characteristic,
and let
G
denote the group of rational points.
The (complex) space
{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G})
of class functions on
G
is an inner product space with
respect to the inner product

\langle f,f^{\prime}\rangle_{\text{\bf G}}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}f(g)f^{\prime}(g^{-1})

for
f,f^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G})
.
The set of irreducible characters

{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G})=\{\,\rho_{x}\mid x\in X(\text{\bf G})\,\}

of
G
for some index set
X(\text{\bf G})
forms an orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G})
.
Another orthonormal basis

{\mathcal{A}}(\text{\bf G})=\{\,R_{x}\mid x\in X(\text{\bf G})\,\}

whose elements are called
almost characters
and are defined as linear combinations of
irreducible characters is proposed by Lusztig (
cf
.
[
Lus84
]
p.347).
If
G
is a general linear group
{\rm GL}_{n}
or a unitary group
{\rm U}_{n}
,
then
R_{x}=\rho_{x}
for
x\in X(\text{\bf G})
and there is nothing really interesting.
However, if
G
is a symplectic group or an orthogonal group,
the story is very different.
For
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n}
or
{\rm SO}_{2n+1}
,
the precise definition of
{\mathcal{A}}(\text{\bf G})
is given in
[
Wal04
]
§4.
Note that here we regard
{\rm O}^{\pm}
as a “group” and define

{\mathcal{E}}({\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n})={\mathcal{E}}({\rm O}^{+}_{2n})\cup{\mathcal{E}}({\rm O}^{-}_{2n})
,

X({\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n})=X({\rm O}^{+}_{2n})\cup X({\rm O}^{-}_{2n})
,
and then
{\mathcal{V}}({\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n})={\mathcal{V}}({\rm O}^{+}_{2n})\oplus{\mathcal{V}}({\rm O}^{-}_{2n})
.

1.2.

If
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n^{\prime}})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1})

where
\epsilon=+
or
-
is a reductive dual pair
of a symplectic group and an orthogonal group,
let
\omega^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
denote the Weil character of the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})

with respect to a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
.
We can also regard
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})
as a “dual pair”,
and define

\displaystyle\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{+}_{2n^{\prime}}}+\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{-}_{2n^{\prime}}}\in{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})\otimes{\mathcal{V}}({\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1}}

\displaystyle=\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1}}|_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)\times{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1}(q)}\in{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})\otimes{\mathcal{V}}({\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1}).

The decomposition of
\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}}}
(resp.
\omega^{\psi}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1}}
)
with respect to the orthonormal basis
{\mathcal{E}}({\rm Sp}_{2n}\times{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})

(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}({\rm Sp}_{2n}\times{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
) consisting of irreducible characters is known in

[
Pan
]
,
[
Pan19
]
.
The following main result of this article is to show that they have the same decomposition with respect to
the orthonormal basis consisting of almost characters
{\mathcal{A}}({\rm Sp}_{2n}\times{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})

(resp.
{\mathcal{A}}({\rm Sp}_{2n}\times{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
,
and write

\displaystyle\omega^{\psi}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X(\text{\bf G}),\ x^{\prime}\in X(\text{\bf G}^{\prime})}m_{x,x^{\prime}}\rho_{x}\otimes\rho_{x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X(\text{\bf G}),\ x^{\prime}\in X(\text{\bf G}^{\prime})}\widetilde{m}_{x,x^{\prime}}R_{x}\otimes R_{x^{\prime}}

for
m_{x,x^{\prime}},\widetilde{m}_{x,x^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then
m_{x,x^{\prime}}=\widetilde{m}_{x,x^{\prime}}
for any
(x,x^{\prime})\in X(\text{\bf G})\times X(\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
.

It might be a little surprised that the above result does not hold for the dual pair

({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n^{\prime}})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
.
An example is given in Subsection
3.1
.
From this point of view, the dual pairs
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})
and
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})

might be more natural than the usual
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{+}_{2n^{\prime}})
,
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{-}_{2n^{\prime}})
or

({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
.

It is well known that the correspondence for dual pair
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n^{\prime}})
or

({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
is of multiplicity one.
Therefore, the correspondence for the pair
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})
is also of multiplicity one,
i.e., the coefficient
m_{x,x^{\prime}}
in Theorem
1.1
is either
0
or
1
.
Although the correspondence for
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}^{\epsilon}_{2n^{\prime}})
is not of multiplicity one
(
cf
. Example
4.31
), the correspondence for the pair
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
is still of multiplicity one.
This result should be known to the experts, still we provide a proof (
cf
. Proposition
4.30
) for completeness.

A direct consequence of the above theorem is that one can define a correspondence on almost
characters for dual pair
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})

which is exactly the same as the correspondence on irreducible characters.
As an application, we can define the
\Theta
-rank of an almost character
R_{x}

as we did for an irreducible character
\rho_{x}
.
Moreover, the above theorem tells us that
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(R_{x})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{x})

for any
x\in X(\text{\bf G})
for
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n}
or
{\rm SO}_{2n+1}
.

1.3.

Now we briefly explain the idea of our proof.
It is known that the set of irreducible characters

{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G})=\bigcup_{(s)\subset(G^{*})^{0}\,\text{semisimple}}{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G})_{s}

is partition into
Lusztig series
indexed by conjugacy classes of semisimple
elements in the connected component of the dual group.
Elements in
{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G})_{1}
are called
unipotent
and parametrized by “
symbols
”
{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
.
The set of symbols are partitioned into families
{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}=\bigcup_{Z}{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}
indexed by the set of
“
special symbols
”.

For each family of symbols
{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}
, the transition matrix between irreducible characters
and almost characters is known explicitly in
[
Lus84
]
or
[
Wal04
]
.
Then we can adapt the technique from
[
Pan21b
]
to prove the unipotent part of the
equality of Theorem
1.1
:

(1.2)

\omega_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}},1}=\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}}}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}}}}R_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}

(
cf
. Corollary
3.5
).

For a general Lusztig series
{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G})_{s}
, we can define groups

\text{\bf G}^{(0)}(s),\text{\bf G}^{(-)}(s),\text{\bf G}^{(+)}(s)
and a
modified Lusztig correspondence

\Xi_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G})_{s}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(\text{\bf G}^{(0)}(s)\times\text{\bf G}^{(-)}(s)\times\text{\bf G}^{(+)}(s))_{1}

which is a bijection and can be extended linearly to an isometry of inner product spaces

\Xi_{s}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G})_{s}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(0)}(s))_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(-)}(s))_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(+)}(s))_{1}.

Then we can combine several Lusztig series together so that
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}(s),\text{\bf G}^{(+)}(s)

are enlarged to
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}[s],\text{\bf G}^{(+)}[s]
and each of which is of the form

{\rm Sp}_{{\rm even}},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{{\rm even}}
or
{\rm SO}_{{\rm odd}}
.
Then we have an isometry of inner product spaces

\Xi_{[s]}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G})_{[s]}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(0)}[s])_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(-)}[s])_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(+)}[s])_{1}.

Now the almost character structure of
{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(0)}[s])_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(-)}[s])_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G}^{(+)}[s])_{1}

induces the almost character structure of
{\mathcal{V}}(\text{\bf G})_{[s]}
via the isometry
\Xi_{[s]}
.
If particular, if
\Xi_{[s]}(\rho_{x})=\rho_{x^{(0)}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{x}^{(-)}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda_{x}^{(+)}}
,
then
\Xi_{[s]}(R_{x})=R_{x^{(0)}}\otimes R_{\Lambda_{x}^{(-)}}\otimes R_{\Lambda_{x}^{(+)}}
.
Via the isometry
\Xi_{[s]}
, the identity of unipotent class functions in (
1.2
)
can be extend to an identity of all class functions for
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n^{\prime}})

or
({\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n^{\prime}+1})
.
Then Theorem
1.1
is obtained.

2.
Symbols and Unipotent Characters

2.1.
Bi-partitions and symbols

An ordered pair
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\lambda}{\mu}
of two partitions
\lambda,\mu
is called a
bi-partition
.
For a non-negative integer
n
,
let
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(n)
denote the set of bi-partitions of
n
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(n)=\left\{\,\textstyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\lambda}{\mu}\mid|\lambda|+|\mu|=n\,\right\}.

A
symbol

\Lambda
is an ordered pair

\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}

of two finite subsets
A,B
(possibly empty) of non-negative integers whose
elements are always written in decreasing order, i.e.,
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{m_{1}}
and

b_{1}>b_{2}>\cdots>b_{m_{2}}
.
For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
, let
\Lambda^{*}=A
and
\Lambda_{*}=B

denote the first row and the second row of
\Lambda
respectively.
Moreover, we define its
transpose

\Lambda^{\rm t}=\binom{B}{A}
,
its number of entries,
size
,
rank
, and
defect
by:

\displaystyle|\Lambda|

\displaystyle=|A|+|B|,

\displaystyle{\rm size}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=(|A|,|B|),

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}b_{j}-\left\lfloor\biggl(\frac{|A|+|B|-1}{2}\biggr)^{2}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle{\rm def}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=|A|-|B|.

We define an equivalence relation on symbols generated by

\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\sim\binom{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,0}{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,0},

and let
{\mathcal{S}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{S}}_{n,\delta}
) denote the set of equivalence classes of symbols
(resp. equivalences of symbols of rank
n
and defect
\delta
).
It is clear that each equivalence class of symbols contains a unique symbol
\binom{A}{B}
such that

0\not\in A\cap B
.
Such a symbol is called
reduced
.
Unless otherwise specified, a symbol is always assumed to be reduced.
It is not difficult to check that the mapping

(2.1)

\Upsilon\colon\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\mapsto\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a_1-(m_1-1),a_2-(m_1-2),\cdots,a_{m_1-1}-1,a_{m_1}}{b_1-(m_2-1),b_2-(m_2-2),\cdots,b_{m_2-1}-1,b_{m_2}}

gives a bijection
{\mathcal{S}}_{n,1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(n)
and a bijection
{\mathcal{S}}_{n,0}\rightarrow{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(n)
.
Note that
\Upsilon(\Lambda_{1})=\Upsilon(\Lambda_{2})
if
\Lambda_{1}\sim\Lambda_{2}
.

2.2.
Unipotent almost characters of a symplectic group

Define

{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}=\bigcup_{\delta\equiv 1\pmod{4}}{\mathcal{S}}_{n,\delta}=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{4}\,\}

It is known that there exists a parametrization of the unipotent characters
{\mathcal{E}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{1}

of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
by
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}
(
cf
.
[
Lus77
]
).
For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}
, let
\rho_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{1}
denote the irreducible
character parametrized by
\Lambda
.
Then let
{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{1}
denote the subspace spanned by
\{\,\rho_{\Lambda}\mid\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}\,\}
.

A symbol

Z=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m+1}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}}
of rank
n
and defect
1

is called
special
if

a_{1}\geq b_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m}\geq b_{m}\geq a_{m+1}
.
For a special symbol
Z
, let
Z_{\rm I}
denote the subsymbol of “
singles
” in
Z
, i.e.,

Z_{\rm I}=Z\smallsetminus\binom{Z^{*}\cap Z_{*}}{Z^{*}\cap Z_{*}}
.
Then
|Z_{\rm I}|=2d+1
for some non-negative integer
d
,
called the
degree
of
Z
, and denoted by
\deg(Z)=d
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}
denote the set of (reduced) symbols with exactly the same entries of
Z
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}\mid\Lambda^{*}\cup\Lambda_{*}=Z^{*}\cup Z_{*},\ \Lambda^{*}\cap\Lambda_{*}=Z^{*}\cap Z_{*}\,\}.

Then we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{Z\ \text{special},\ {\rm rk}(Z)=n,\ {\rm def}(Z)=1}{\mathcal{S}}_{Z},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{1}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{Z\ \text{special},\ {\rm rk}(Z)=n,\ {\rm def}(Z)=1}{\mathcal{V}}_{Z}

where
{\mathcal{V}}_{Z}={\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{Z}
is the subspace of class functions spanned by

\{\,\rho_{\Lambda}\mid\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}\,\}
.

For a subsymbol
M\subset Z_{\rm I}
,
we define

(2.2)

\Lambda_{M}=(Z\smallsetminus M)\cup M^{\rm t},

i.e.,
\Lambda_{M}
is obtained from
Z
by switching the two rows of
M
.
Clearly,
{\rm rk}(\Lambda_{M})={\rm rk}(Z)
.
Then it is easy to see that

{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}=\{\,\Lambda_{M}\mid M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}\,\},

and
|{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}|=2^{2\deg(Z)}
.
For
\Lambda_{M_{1}},\Lambda_{M_{2}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}
, we define an addition

(2.3)

\Lambda_{M_{1}}+\Lambda_{M_{2}}=\Lambda_{N}\quad\text{where }N=(M_{1}\cup M_{2})\smallsetminus(M_{1}\cap M_{2}).

This gives
{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}
a vector space structure over
{\mathbf{F}}_{2}
with identity element
\Lambda_{\binom{-}{-}}=Z
.
Moreover, we have a symmetric pairing
\langle,\rangle\colon{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}\rightarrow{\mathbf{F}}_{2}
given by

(2.4)

\langle\Lambda_{M_{1}},\Lambda_{M_{2}}\rangle=|M_{1}\cap M_{2}|\pmod{2}.

According to Lusztig, the
unipotent almost character

R_{\Lambda}
of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)

associated to
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}
is defined by

(2.5)

R_{\Lambda}=\frac{1}{2^{\deg(Z)}}\sum_{\Lambda_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}(-1)^{\langle\Lambda,\Lambda_{1}\rangle}\rho_{\Lambda_{1}}\in{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{Z}\subset{\mathcal{V}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{1}.

This means that

\langle R_{\Lambda_{1}},\rho_{\Lambda_{2}}\rangle_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2^{\deg(Z)}}(-1)^{\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle},&\text{if $\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}$ for some special $Z$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular, if
\deg(Z)=0
, then
{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}=\{Z\}
and
R_{Z}=\rho_{Z}
.
The set of unipotent almost characters of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
is denoted by

{\mathcal{A}}({\rm Sp}_{2n})_{1}=\{\,R_{\Lambda}\mid\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}\,\}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Z
be a special symbol of defect
1
.
The set
{\mathcal{A}}_{Z}:=\{\,R_{\Lambda}\mid\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}\,\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{Z}
.

Proof.

Let
M_{1},M_{2}\subset Z_{\rm I}
such that both
|M_{1}|
and
|M_{2}|
are even.
Then by (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\langle R_{\Lambda_{M_{1}}},R_{\Lambda_{M_{2}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2\deg(Z)}}\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\ \text{even}}\;\sum_{M^{\prime}\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M^{\prime}|\ \text{even}}(-1)^{|M\cap M_{2}|+|M^{\prime}\cap M_{2}|}\langle\rho_{\Lambda_{M}},\rho_{\Lambda_{M^{\prime}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2\deg(Z)}}\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\ \text{even}}(-1)^{|M\cap M_{1}|+|M\cap M_{2}|}

If
M_{1}=M_{2}
, then clearly

\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\ \text{even}}(-1)^{|M\cap M_{1}|+|M\cap M_{2}|}=|{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}|=2^{2\deg(Z)}.

Suppose that
M_{1}\neq M_{2}
.
Without loss of generality, may assume that there is an entry
a_{1}\in M_{2}\smallsetminus M_{1}

and another entry
a_{2}\in Z_{\rm I}\smallsetminus M_{2}
(because
M_{2}\subsetneq Z_{\rm I}
).
It is not difficult to see that

\{\,M\mid M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\ \text{even}\,\}=\{\,M,M^{*}\mid M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\ \text{even},\ a_{1}\not\in M\}

where

M^{*}=\begin{cases}M\cup\{a_{1},a_{2}\},&\text{if $a_{2}\not\in M$};\\
(M\cup\{a_{1}\})\smallsetminus\{a_{2}\},&\text{if $a_{2}\in M$}.\end{cases}

Now

|M^{*}\cap M_{1}|+|M^{*}\cap M_{2}|=|M\cap M_{1}|+|M\cap M_{2}|+1

for any
M\subset Z_{\rm I}
such that
|M|
even and
a_{1}\not\in M
.
Hence, if
M_{1}\neq M_{2}
, we have

\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\ \text{even}}(-1)^{|M\cap M_{1}|+|M\cap M_{2}|}=0.

Therefore the lemma is proved.
∎

\widetilde{f}^{\prime}(R^{(1)}_{\Lambda_{M^{\prime}}})=R_{\Lambda_{f^{\prime}(M^{\prime})}}

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
m
be a non-negative integer,
and let
Z=Z_{(m)}
and
Z^{\prime}=Z^{\prime}_{(m+1)}
.
Then

\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{Z,Z^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{Z,Z^{\prime}}}R_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}.

Proof.

By (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{Z,Z^{\prime}}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}}\rho_{\Lambda_{M}}\otimes(\rho_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}}}+\rho_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}\cup\Psi^{\prime}}})=\sqrt{2}\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}}\rho_{\Lambda_{M}}\otimes\rho^{(1)}_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}}}

\displaystyle\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{Z,Z^{\prime}}}R_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}}R_{\Lambda_{M}}\otimes(R_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}}}+R_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}\cup\Psi^{\prime}}})=\sqrt{2}\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}}R_{\Lambda_{M}}\otimes R^{(1)}_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}}}

where
\Psi^{\prime}=\binom{2m+1}{-}
.
Let
\widetilde{f}\colon{\mathcal{V}}_{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{Z}
be the identity map.
Note that now
Z=Z_{(m)}
and
Z^{\prime(1)}=Z^{\prime}_{(m)}
and
by Lemma
3.10
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}\otimes\widetilde{f}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}}\rho_{\Lambda_{M}}\otimes\rho^{(1)}_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{Z,Z^{\prime(1)}}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{Z,Z^{\prime(1)}}}R_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\Lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}\otimes\widetilde{f}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{M\subset Z_{\rm I},\ |M|\text{ even}}R_{\Lambda_{M}}\otimes R^{(1)}_{\Lambda_{M^{\rm t}}}\right).

Because
\widetilde{f}\otimes\widetilde{f}^{\prime}
is an isometry, the lemma is proved.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
Z
be a special symbol of rank
n
and of defect

or
0

Then

\sum_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}R_{\Lambda}\otimes R_{\Lambda}.

Proof.

Write

\sum_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda}=\sum_{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}m_{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}R_{\Lambda_{1}}\otimes R_{\Lambda_{2}}

Then from the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle m_{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}=\biggl\langle\sum_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}\rho_{\Lambda}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda},R_{\Lambda_{1}}\otimes R_{\Lambda_{2}}\biggr\rangle_{\!\text{\bf G}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2\deg(Z)}}\sum_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Z}}(-1)^{\langle\Lambda,\Lambda_{1}\rangle+\langle\Lambda,\Lambda_{2}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}$};\\

Proposition 4.15

For
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
or
{\rm O}^{\pm}_{2n}

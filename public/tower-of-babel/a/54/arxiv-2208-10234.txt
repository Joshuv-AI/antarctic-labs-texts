Time Encoding via Unlimited Sampling: Theory, Algorithms and Hardware Validation
Dorian Florescu and Ayush Bhandari

Abstract
An alternative to conventional uniform sampling is that of time encoding, which converts continuous-time signals into streams of trigger times. This gives rise to Event-Driven Sampling (EDS) models. The data-driven nature of EDS acquisition is advantageous in terms of power consumption and time resolution and is inspired by the information representation in biological nervous systems. If an analog signal is outside a predefined dynamic range, then EDS generates a low density of trigger times, which in turn leads to recovery distortion due to aliasing. In this paper, inspired by the Unlimited Sensing Framework (USF), we propose a new EDS architecture that incorporates a modulo nonlinearity prior to acquisition that we refer to as the modulo EDS or MEDS. In MEDS, the modulo nonlinearity folds high dynamic range inputs into low dynamic range amplitudes, thus avoiding recovery distortion. In particular, we consider the asynchronous sigma-delta modulator (ASDM), previously used for low power analog-to-digital conversion. This novel MEDS based acquisition is enabled by a recent generalization of the modulo nonlinearity called modulo-hysteresis. We design a mathematically guaranteed recovery algorithm for bandlimited inputs based on a sampling rate criterion and provide reconstruction error bounds. We go beyond numerical experiments and also provide a first hardware validation of our approach, thus bridging the gap between theory and practice, while corroborating the conceptual underpinnings of our work.

Bottleneck in the Current EDS Acquisition.
In EDS, the input is encoded into an asynchronous sequence of time events or trigger times. The information content in such a sequence can be characterised by the sampling density, a positive quantity measuring the number of trigger instants per unit time. This leads to an encoding barrier: no information is being transmitted for inputs whose amplitudes are outside the range where the sampling density is positive. The barrier is illustrated for the particular case of the asynchronous sigma-delta modulator based EDS in Fig.
1
. Therefore, the accurate recovery of the input is affected, which requires a minimal sampling density to prevent aliasing
[
13
]
. Thus, in EDS, the input is typically constrained to a predefined dynamic range.

The problem of limited dynamic range is common in traditional encoding schemes such as analog-to-digital converters (ADCs), which clip or saturate the input signals exceeding a predefined threshold.
To overcome this fundamental bottleneck, the Unlimited Sensing Framework (USF)

To address the dynamic range limitation in event-driven sampling schemes, we propose a new encoding model consisting of a modulo-hysteresis nonlinearity in series with an EDS model, which we call modulo EDS or MEDS. In our setting the EDS model is fixed, and the EDS input (modulo output) is guaranteed to satisfy the EDS dynamic range by selecting an appropriate modulo nonlinearity (Fig.

II-A

Model Description

At the initial time point
t{=t_{0}}=0
, the output of the Schmitt trigger is set as
z\left(t\right)=-b
. The signal
z\left(t\right)
retains this state until the first trigger time occurs, as will be explained next. In a vicinity of the
t=0
the integrator output
y(t)
satisfies:

y\left(t\right)=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(g\left(s\right)+b\right)ds,

(1)

where
g\left(t\right)
is the input,
y_{0}
is a constant denoting the integrator’s initial condition, and
b,\delta
are the Schmitt trigger parameters. The ASDM generates output only if
|g\left(t\right)|\leqslant c<b
, which implies that
y\left(t\right)
is strictly increasing (
1
). When
|g\left(t\right)|>c
,
y\left(t\right)
is no longer monotonic, and thus may not cross the threshold
\delta
. This saturates the ASDM, preventing any triggers until the input is back in the ASDM dynamic range (see Fig.
3
). The trigger time
t_{1}
corresponds to
y\left(t_{1}\right)=\delta
, which flips the output of the Schmitt trigger to
b
. This enforces
y\left(t\right)
to be strictly decreasing, and the next trigger time satisfies
y\left(t_{2}\right)=-\delta
.
The sequence of trigger times
\left\{t_{k}\right\}
is directly linked with the input and ASDM parameters via the following recursive equation, also known as the
t-transform

[
1
]

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\left(g\left(s\right)+\left(-1\right)^{k}b\right)ds=\left(-1\right)^{k}2\delta,\quad{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}}.

(2)

We model the link between the trigger times and input via the operator
\left\{t_{k}\right\}=\mathrm{ASDM}_{\delta,b}\left(g\right)
.
The
t
-transform was also studied in the context of other EDS models such as the IF neuron
[
2
]
. By defining
G\left(t\right)\triangleq\int_{0}^{t}g\left(s\right)ds
, it can be shown via induction that
G\left(t_{k}\right)
can be derived directly from (
2
). This creates a strong link between ASDM sampling and nonuniform sampling, and allows using similar analysis and recovery approaches
[
13
]
. A hardware realisation of an ASDM is in Fig.
5
(b).

II-B

Reconstruction from Asynchronous Sigma-Delta Samples

The ASDM input
g(t)
can be perfectly reconstructed
[
2
]
if it is bandlimited
g\in\mathsf{PW}_{\Omega}
and the maximum distance between the ASDM trigger times satisfies the Nyquist rate condition, i.e.,

\left\lvert t_{k+1}-t_{k}\right\rvert<\frac{\pi}{\Omega}.

This condition is sufficient to guarantee recovery from nonuniform samples
G\left(t_{k}\right)

[
13
]
.

In the case of the ASDM, if
\left\lvert g(t)\right\rvert>b
the encoder does not generate any output, meaning that
\left\lvert t_{k+1}-t_{k}\right\rvert<\frac{\pi}{\Omega}
does not always hold. To overcome this problem it is assumed that
g_{\infty}<b
, which , via (
2
), guarantees that
t_{k+1}-t_{k}<\frac{2\delta}{b-g_{\infty}}
. Therefore, the Nyquist rate condition for recovery is guaranteed if

g_{\infty}<b-\frac{2\delta\Omega}{\pi}.

(3)

The input is recovered as
[
1
]

g\left(t\right)=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{m}\mathrm{sinc}_{\Omega}\left(t-\bar{t}_{m}\right),

(4)

where
\bar{t}_{m}=\left(t_{m}+t_{m+1}\right)/{2}
are the midpoints of the intervals between the trigger times. The coefficients
c_{m}
are computed via least squares from

\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g\left(s\right)ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:ttransform})}}{{=}}\left(-1\right)^{k}2\delta-\left(-1\right)^{k}b\left(t_{k+1}-t_{k}\right).

In the context of nonuniform sampling, this procedure is also known as
reconstruction from local averages

[
13
]
.

Intuitively, whenever
\left\lvert g\left(t\right)\right\rvert>b
, the input does not trigger the ASDM (Fig.
1
(b)). This is because the integrator output

is no longer monotonic and might not reach the threshold
\pm b
. This locks in the ASDM output until the threshold is reached. This effect is also observed in hardware, as illustrated in Fig.
3
. As will be shown in Section
V

III-B

The Modulo-Hysteresis Model

The phenomenon of
hysteresis
, present in ADC circuits
[
41
]
, results from a difference between the quantization threshold values when the value is approached from above or below
[
42
]
. This effect is also commonly exploited in circuits such as the Schmitt trigger, which avoids noise induced spurious triggering during ADC conversion. Recently, that effect was exploited in a modulo-hysteresis model and associated hardware implementation
[
23
]
see Fig.
5

We give below the formal definition of modulo-hysteresis.

Definition 1

(Modulo-Hysteresis)

The modulo-hysteresis with threshold
\lambda
and hysteresis parameter
h\in\left[0,\lambda\right)
, denoted
\mathscr{M}_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}=\left[\lambda\ h\right]
, generates an nonuniform sequence
\left\{\tau_{r}\right\}
and an analog function
x\left(t\right)=\mathscr{M}_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}}g\left(t\right),

t\geqslant\tau_{0}
in response to input
g\in\mathsf{PW}_{\Omega}
. The sequence

is computed recursively as

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{t>\tau_{0}|\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}\left(g\left(t\right)+\lambda\right)=0\right\}

\displaystyle\tau_{r+1}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{t>\tau_{r}|\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}\left(g\left(t\right)-g\left(\tau_{r}\right)+hs_{r}\right)=0\right\},

where
s_{r}=\mathrm{sign}\left(g\left(\tau_{r}\right)-g\left(\tau_{r-1}\right)\right),n\geqslant 1
.
The function

returned by the encoder is then given by

x\left(t\right)=g\left(t\right)-\varepsilon_{g}\left(t\right),

(7)

where
\varepsilon_{g}\left(t\right)=2\lambda_{h}\sum\nolimits_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}s_{r}\mathbbmtt{1}_{[\tau_{r},\infty)}\left(t\right)
and
\lambda_{h}\triangleq\lambda-h/2
.

Modulo-hysteresis has the following important property.

Lemma 1

(Minimum Separation between Folds)

Let
\left\{\tau_{r}\right\}_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence of folding times corresponding to encoder
\mathscr{M}_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}=\left[\lambda\ h\right]

\tau_{r+1}-\tau_{r}\geqslant\frac{h^{*}}{\Omega g_{\infty}},\quad h^{*}=\min\left\{h,2\lambda_{h}\right\}.

(8)

Proof.

From Bernšteĭn’s inequality
\|g^{\left(n\right)}\|_{p}\leqslant\Omega^{n}\|g\|_{p}
(cf. pg. 116,
[
43
]
) and Definition
1
, for

,

and

\tau_{r+1}-\tau_{r}\geqslant\frac{\left\lvert g\left(\tau_{r+1}\right)-g\left(\tau_{r}\right)\right\rvert}{\max\left\lvert g^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right\rvert}\geqslant\frac{\min\left\{h,2\lambda-h\right\}}{\Omega\max\left\lvert g\left(t\right)\right\rvert}=\frac{h^{*}}{\Omega g_{\infty}}.

This proves the result.
∎

When the data is sampled with period

Let

. Then

Furthermore, let

IV
Recovery from Modulo Event-Driven Data

Our goal is to recover

from (

\left\{t_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and design parameters

The proposed encoding circuit guarantees the asynchronous sigma-delta modulator input is in a predefined range.
Generally, the EDS input

{\tau_{r}\geqslant 0}

cannot be recovered with the traditional methods for EDS models, which assume that the input is bandlimited, or at least smooth
[
2
,
44
,
3
,
23
]
.

Given samples
X\left(t_{k}\right)=G\left(t_{k}\right)-E\left(t_{k}\right)
, the main challenge is to recover both
G\left(t_{k}\right)
and
E\left(t_{k}\right)
. By applying a generic filter
\mathscr{D}
to the data

for

we get (

\mathscr{D}X\left(t_{k}\right)=\mathscr{D}G\left(t_{k}\right)-\mathscr{D}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)=\mathscr{D}\left(\sum\limits_{m={0}}^{k-1}q_{m}\right).

(13)

The input reconstruction is organised in two stages.
In the first stage,
\mathscr{D}G\left(t_{k}\right)
is interpreted as noise and an estimation
\widetilde{E}_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
of
E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
is computed by thresholding
\mathscr{D}X\left(t_{k}\right)
. This approach was used before to reconstruct modulo folded data in the context of uniform samples
[
40
,
24
,
23
]
. In the second recovery stage, an estimation
\widetilde{G}\left(t_{k}\right)
of
G\left(t_{k}\right)
is computed from the integral form of the modulo-hysteresis input

and

is reconstructed from the estimated samples

, such that the error norm

is bounded.

The section is organised as follows. To accommodate nonuniform samples, we define the nonuniform difference operator
\mathscr{D}^{N}
in Section

. We then describe the first recovery stage, and provide guarantees for computing

in the particular case of one folding time in Section

, and extend it to multiple folding times in Section

. Section

describes the second recovery stage and gives the input reconstruction algorithm and associated error bound.

IV-A

The Nonuniform Difference Operator

Here we formulate a thresholding based recovery for the case of the ASDM model. Given samples
X\left(t_{k}\right)=G\left(t_{k}\right)-E\left(t_{k}\right)
, the main challenge is to recover both
G\left(t_{k}\right)
and
E\left(t_{k}\right)
. We threshold samples
\mathscr{D}X\left(t_{k}\right)
to recover the residual
E_{g}\left(t\right)
, which requires identifying the folding times
\tau_{r}
and signs
s_{r}
(
10
).
Operator
\mathscr{D}
is designed to enhance the effect of
E_{g}
in (
13
). In
[
23
]
the data was processed with the uniform finite difference filter
\Delta^{N}
. Here we recover the input from nonuniform samples and, to this end, we choose
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{D}^{N}
defined recursively the nonuniform difference operator of order
N
,

\mathscr{D}^{N}f\left[k\right]=\frac{\mathscr{D}^{N-1}f\left[k+1\right]-\mathscr{D}^{N-1}f\left[k\right]}{t_{k+N}-t_{k}},\quad N>0,

(14)

where
\mathscr{D}^{0}f\left[k\right]=f\left[k\right]
and
f\left[k\right]
is a generic sequence. Here,
\mathscr{D}^{N}

is an extension of the difference operator
\Delta^{N}
used for uniform samples. In (
13
), the values
\mathscr{D}^{N}\sum_{m={0}}^{k-1}q_{m}
can be computed directly from the measurements. As is the case for USF, here the measurements lead to a mixture of the samples
\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)
and
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
. However, unlike the uniform sampling scenario, values
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
do not lie on an uniform grid. We will show that the separation of
\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)
and
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
is still possible via a thresholding approach. To this end, we provide the following bound, which will be used in our recovery approach
(proof in Section
VI
).

Proposition 1

Let
\mathscr{D}^{N}
be the nonuniform finite difference operator defined in (
14
). Then

\left\lvert\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{1}{\Omega N!}\left(\frac{T_{\mathsf{max}}}{T_{\mathsf{min}}}\Omega e\right)^{N}g_{\infty},

(15)

where
T_{\mathsf{min}},T_{\mathsf{max}}>0
are two constants satisfying
T_{\mathsf{min}}\leqslant t_{k+1}-t_{k}\leqslant T_{\mathsf{max}}
.

This bound is an extension to nonuniform samples of the bound derived in
[
14
]
for
\left\lvert\Delta^{N}g\left(kT\right)\right\rvert
. Specifically, we note that in the limit case where
T_{\mathsf{min}}=T_{\mathsf{max}}=T
, and thus
t_{k}=kT
, we have that

\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:NFD})}}{{=}}\frac{\Delta^{N}G\left(kT\right)}{T^{N}N!}.

According to Bernšteĭn’s we have
\|G^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\Omega G_{\infty}\Rightarrow g_{\infty}\leqslant\Omega G_{\infty}
. Then, in this particular case, (
15
) becomes
\left\lvert\Delta^{N}G\left(kT\right)\right\rvert\leqslant\left(T\Omega e\right)^{N}G_{\infty}
, which is in accordance to the bound derived in
[
14
,
16
]
.

IV-B

The Case of a Single Fold

For simplicity, let us first assume we have only one folding time
\tau_{1}
. Let
K_{1}
be the index of the trigger time preceding the folding time, such that
\tau_{1}\in\left[t_{K_{1}},t_{K_{1}+1}\right)

\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)

Proposition 2

For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, sequence
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
satisfies

\frac{\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)}{s_{1}\left(2\lambda-h\right)}=\mu_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[k\right]p+\beta_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[k\right]\left(1-p\right),\forall N\geqslant 2,

(16)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p=\frac{t_{K_{1}+1}-\tau_{1}}{t_{K_{1}+1}-t_{K_{1}}}\in\left[0,1\right]
and
\mu_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[k\right]
,
\beta_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[k\right]
satisfy

\mu_{K_{1}}^{N+1}\left[k\right]=\frac{\mu_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[{k+1}\right]-\mu_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[{k}\right]}{t_{k+N+1}-t_{k}}\mbox{ and }\beta_{K_{1}}^{N+1}\left[k\right]=\frac{\beta_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[{k+1}\right]-\beta_{K_{1}}^{N}\left[{k}\right]}{t_{k+N+1}-t_{k}},

(17)

for
N\geqslant 2
, where

\mu_{K_{1}}^{2}\left[k\right]=\frac{\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left\{K_{1}-1\right\}}\left(k\right)}{t_{k+2}-t_{k}}\mbox{ and }\beta_{K_{1}}^{2}\left[k\right]=\frac{\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left\{K_{1}\right\}}\left(k\right)}{t_{k+2}-t_{k}}.

Proof.

We compute
E_{g}\left(t\right)
by integrating
\varepsilon_{g}\left(t\right)
defined in (
7
)

E_{g}\left(t\right)=\left(2\lambda-h\right)s_{1}\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left[\tau_{1},\infty\right)}\left(t\right)\cdot\left(t-\tau_{1}\right).

(18)

We evaluate
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
for
N=1,2
as below

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{D}^{1}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)}{s_{1}\left(2\lambda-h\right)}

\displaystyle=p\cdot\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left\{K_{1}\right\}}\left(k\right)+\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left\{K_{1}+1,\dots,\infty\right\}}\left(k\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{D}^{2}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)}{s_{1}\left(2\lambda-h\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{t_{K_{1}+1}-t_{K_{1}-1}}\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left\{K_{1}-1\right\}}\left(k\right)+\frac{1-p}{t_{K_{1}+2}-t_{K_{1}}}\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left\{K_{1}\right\}}\left(k\right)=\mu_{K_{1}}^{2}\left[k\right]p+\beta_{K_{1}}^{2}\left[k\right]\left(1-p\right),

and the result follows via (
14
) and (
17
).
∎

Using (
16
), the non-zero values of
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}
therefore satisfy

\mathrm{supp}\left(\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)\right)\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{N},

(19)

where
\mathbb{S}_{N}=\left\{{K_{1}-N+1},\dots,{K_{1}}\right\}
.
We only have access to
\mathscr{D}^{N}X\left(t_{k}\right)
, which also includes the effect of
\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)
. In the first recovery stage, we compute the support of
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
via thresholding. In the case of uniform sampling, a similar strategy was proposed with a fixed threshold
\Psi^{N}=\frac{\lambda_{h}}{2N}

[
23
]
. In the present case of nonuniform sampling, we can achieve input reconstruction in a more general scenario by allowing a time-varying threshold
\Psi^{N}\left[k\right]
, which depends on the nonuniform trigger times
\left\{t_{k}\right\}
. In order to constrain the effect of
\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)
on the measurements
\mathscr{D}^{N}X\left(t_{k}\right)
, the following lemma (proof in Section

) derives a bound for
\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)
and subsequently computes support of
\mathscr{D}^{N}E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)

Lemma 2

Assume that

\left\lvert\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)\right\rvert<\Psi^{N}\left[k\right],

(20)

where
\Psi^{N}\left[k\right]
is defined as

\Psi^{N}\left[k\right]=\lambda_{h}\cdot\min\left\{\phi_{0,k+N-1},\phi_{0,k+N-2},\phi_{1,k-1},\phi_{1,k}\right\}

and sequences
\phi_{i,l},i=0,1,

l\in\mathbb{N}
, satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{0,l}&=\frac{\left\lvert\mu_{l}^{N}\left[l-N+1\right]\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\beta_{l}^{N}\left[l-N+2\right]\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\mu_{l}^{N}\left[l-N+1\right]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta_{l}^{N}\left[l-N+2\right]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mu_{l}^{N}\left[l-N+2\right]\right\rvert},\\
\phi_{1,l}&=\frac{\left\lvert\mu_{l}^{N}\left[l-1\right]\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\beta_{l}^{N}\left[l\right]\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\mu_{l}^{N}\left[l-1\right]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta_{l}^{N}\left[l\right]\right\rvert+\left\lvert\beta_{l}^{N}\left[l-1\right]\right\rvert},\end{split}

(21)

where
\mu_{l}^{N}\left[k\right]
and
\beta_{l}^{N}\left[k\right]
are given in (
17
). Furthermore, let
\mathcal{K}_{N}=\left\{k_{m},k_{M}\right\}
be such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}k_{m}=\min\mathbb{M}_{N},\ \ k_{M}=\max\mathbb{M}_{N},\\
\mathbb{M}_{N}\triangleq\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ \left\lvert\mathscr{D}^{N}Z\left(t_{k}\right)\right\rvert\geqslant\Psi^{N}\left[k\right]\right\}.\end{split}

(22)

Then
k_{m}\in\left\{K_{1}-N+1,K_{1}-N+2\right\}
and
k_{M}\in\left\{K_{1}-1,K_{1}\right\}
.

can be correctly identified and

Theorem 1

(Estimation of Folding Times)

Let
g\in\mathsf{PW}_{\Omega}
,

\left\{t_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\mathrm{ASDM}_{\delta,b}\left(\mathscr{M}_{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}}g\right)

and assume
\left\lvert\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)\right\rvert<\Psi^{N}\left[k\right],\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{N}=\{k_{m},k_{M}\}
defined as in (
22
), and let
\widetilde{s}_{1}
be the estimation of the folding sign given by

\widetilde{s}_{1}=-\mathrm{sign}\left[\mathscr{D}^{N}X\left(t_{k_{m}}\right)\right].

(23)

Let
T_{\mathsf{min}},T_{\mathsf{max}}>0
be two constants satisfying
T_{\mathsf{min}}\leqslant t_{k+1}-t_{k}\leqslant T_{\mathsf{max}}
. The folding time estimation
\widetilde{\tau}_{1}
is defined as follows.

1.

If
k_{M}-k_{m}=N-1
, let
\widetilde{\tau}_{1}=\frac{t_{k_{M}}+t_{k_{M}+1}}{2}
and thus

\widetilde{s}_{1}=s_{1},\quad\left\lvert\tau_{1}-\widetilde{\tau}_{1}\right\rvert\leqslant T_{\mathsf{max}}/2.

(24)

2.

If

, let
\widetilde{\tau}_{1}=t_{k_{M}+1}
. Then

\widetilde{s}_{1}=s_{1},\quad\left\lvert\tau_{1}-\widetilde{\tau}_{1}\right\rvert\leqslant T_{\mathsf{max}}.

(25)

3.

If
k_{M}-k_{m}=N-3
, then
\widetilde{\tau}_{1}=\frac{t_{k_{M}+1}+t_{k_{M}+2}}{2}
and (
24
) holds.

Given that
t_{k+1}-t_{k}\leqslant\frac{2\delta}{b-g_{\infty}},\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}
, by choosing
T_{\mathsf{max}}=\frac{2\delta}{b-g_{\infty}}
, then
\tau_{1}
can be recovered with any accuracy for a small enough
\delta
given by (
24
,
25
).
We note that in
USF the folding times
\left\{\tau_{r}\right\}
are estimated as

, leading to a similar error as here, bounded by the sampling period
T
.

Lemma 3

(Sufficient Recovery Conditions)

The recovery is possible when either of the conditions 1) and 2) below is true.

1.

The values
\widetilde{s}_{r},\widetilde{\tau}_{r},r=1,\dots,R
can be recovered from
\mathscr{D}^{N}X\left(t_{k}\right)
with an error given by (
24
,
25
) if

\mathrm{S}_{1}
)

\left(\frac{C^{2}}{b-\lambda}2\delta\Omega e\right)^{N-1}g_{\infty}<\frac{\lambda_{h}}{CNe}
.

\mathrm{S}_{2}
)

N\frac{2\delta\Omega g_{\infty}}{b-\lambda}\leqslant h^{*}
,

where
h^{\ast}=\min\left\{h,2\lambda-h\right\}
and
C=\frac{b+\lambda}{b-\lambda}
.

2.

Furthermore,
\mathrm{S}_{1})
and
\mathrm{S}_{2})
are satisfied if

\delta<\frac{\left(b-\lambda\right)h^{*}\kappa}{2N\Omega g_{\infty}}\mbox{ where }\kappa=\min\left\{{1,\frac{{{\lambda_{h}}}}{e^{2}h^{*}C^{2+\frac{1}{N-1}}}}\right\}.

(30)

Proof.

1.

We use the upper bound for
\left\lvert\mathscr{D}^{N}G\left(t_{k}\right)\right\rvert
in Proposition
1
to derive a sufficient condition for (
20
) as

\left(\frac{T_{\mathsf{max}}^{3}}{T_{\mathsf{min}}^{2}}\Omega e\right)^{N}g_{\infty}<\frac{\lambda_{h}}{Ne}\frac{T_{\mathsf{min}}}{T_{\mathsf{max}}}.

(31)

To prove (
31
), note that, in the limit case,
T_{\mathsf{max}}=T_{\mathsf{min}}=T
is always satisfied for large enough
N
if

\frac{T_{\mathsf{max}}^{3}}{T_{\mathsf{min}}^{2}}\Omega e=T\Omega e<1.

The latter is the sampling rate condition for uniform sampling
[
14
]
. Then
\left.{\mathrm{S}}_{1}\right)
follows from (
31
) and
\left.{\mathrm{S}}_{2}\right)
from (
27
) where
T_{\mathsf{min}}
and
T_{\mathsf{max}}
are selected as the following bounds for the intervals between the trigger times
[
1
]

T_{\mathsf{min}}=\frac{2\delta}{b+\lambda}\leqslant\left\lvert t_{k+1}-t_{k}\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{2\delta}{b-\lambda}=T_{\mathsf{max}}.

(32)

2.

From
\left.{\mathrm{S}}_{1}\right)
and
\left.{\mathrm{S}}_{2}\right)
it follows that
2\delta<\min\left\{B_{1}\left(N\right),B_{2}\left(N\right)\right\}
, where

B_{1}\left(N\right)=\frac{b-\lambda}{C^{2}\Omega e}\left(\frac{\lambda_{h}}{g_{\infty}CNe}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\ \text{and}\ B_{2}\left(N\right)=\frac{\left({b-\lambda}\right)h^{*}}{N\Omega g_{\infty}}.

∎

IV-D

Input Reconstruction

Assuming
\left\{\widetilde{\tau}_{r},\widetilde{s}_{r}\right\}
known, the estimation of
E_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
is

\widetilde{E}_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)=2\lambda_{h}\sum\limits_{r=1}^{R}\widetilde{s}_{r}\mathbbmtt{1}_{\left[\widetilde{\tau}_{r},\infty\right)}\left(t_{k}\right)\cdot\left(t_{k}-\widetilde{\tau}_{r}\right).

(33)

Finally,
\widetilde{g}\left(t\right)
is recovered recursively using reconstruction from local averages,

\Delta\widetilde{G}\left(t_{k}\right)=\widetilde{G}\left(t_{k+1}\right)-\widetilde{G}\left(t_{k}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:rec_local_av})}}{{=}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}g\left(s\right)ds,

where
\widetilde{G}\left(t_{k}\right)=X\left(t_{k}\right)+\widetilde{E}_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
. This recovery approach was proposed in the context of irregular sampling in
[
13
]
and adapted for event-driven ASDM sampling in
[
1
]
. In our case, sequence
\widetilde{g}_{n}\left(t\right)
is computed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{g}_{0}\left(t\right)&=\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}\left[{\Delta\widetilde{G}\left(t_{k}\right)}\right]\left(t\right),\\
\widetilde{g}_{n+1}\left(t\right)&=\widetilde{g}_{n}\left(t\right)+\widetilde{g}_{0}\left(t\right)-\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}\left[{\mathcal{L}\widetilde{g}_{n}}\right]\left(t\right),\end{split}

(34)

where,

•

\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}:\ell^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.

•

\mathcal{S}_{\Omega}\left[{a_{k}}\right]\triangleq\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}\cdot\mathrm{sinc}_{\Omega}\left(t-s_{k}\right)
,
s_{k}=\frac{t_{k}+t_{k+1}}{2}
, and,

•

\mathcal{L}:L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2},\left(\mathcal{L}f\right)_{k}=\int\limits_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}f\left(s\right)ds
.

We outline the recovery procedure in Algorithm
1
.

Data:

\lambda,h,\delta,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\{t_{k}\},\Omega>0,N\geqslant 2
.

Result:

\widetilde{g}\left(t\right)

1.

Compute
X_{k}^{N}=\mathscr{D}^{N}\left(\sum\limits_{m={0}}^{k-1}q_{m}\right)
, where
q_{m}=\left(-1\right)^{m}2\delta-\left(-1\right)^{m}b\left(t_{m+1}-t_{m}\right)
.

2.

Compute
R,\ \left\{k_{m}^{r},k_{M}^{r}\right\}_{r=1}^{R}
with (
28
), (
29
).

3.

For
r=1,\dots,R
, compute
\widetilde{s}_{r}
with (
23
) and,

\widetilde{\tau}_{r}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{cases}t_{k_{M}^{r}}+t_{k_{M}^{r}+1}&\mbox{ if }\left(k_{M}^{r}-k_{m}^{r}\right)=N-1\\
2\left(t_{k_{M}^{r}+1}\right)&\mbox{ if }\left(k_{M}^{r}-k_{m}^{r}\right)=N-2\\
t_{k_{M}^{r}+1}+t_{k_{M}^{r}+2}&\mbox{ if }\left(k_{M}^{r}-k_{m}^{r}\right)=N-3\end{cases}.

4.

Compute
\widetilde{E}_{g}\left(t_{k}\right)
using (

VII
Conclusions and Future Work

Summary of Results.
We proposed a new encoding model based on the modulo event-driven sampling (MEDS) pipeline consisting of a modulo-hysteresis nonlinearity in series with an asynchronous sigma-delta modulator (ASDM) model. Our result contributes to the existing efforts of alleviating the dynamic range restrictions of existing EDS sensors. We provide mathematical guarantees for the input reconstruction, and validate the MEDS model with synthetic and hardware experiments. Numerical simulations show that the proposed method can recover inputs with a dynamic range up to
20
times the modulo threshold.

Future Work.
We plan on following up the results in several directions,

1.

The method can be extended to modulo models in series with other event-driven encoders, such as level-crossing encoders and biphasic integrate-and-fire neurons.

2.

The nonuniform finite difference could be replaced by a more general operator for achieving a better recovery.

3.

The current methodology can be extended to inputs in multiple dimensions.

New exciting hardware applications such as event-driven cameras show the promise that this encoding scheme holds in the field of signal processing. Despite this, the sensor dynamic range restriction remains a fundamental bottleneck in the field. Recently, it was shown that a high dynamic range image can be recovered from uniform samples in a single capture
[
22
]
. Given that event-driven signals have an inherently low dynamic range, this line of research represents a natural step in the field that could lead to promising new encoding strategies.

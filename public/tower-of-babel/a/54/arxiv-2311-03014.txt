On real analytic functions on closed subanalytic domains Thanks: Supported by FWF-Project P 32905-N
Armin Rainer

Abstract
. We show that a function f : X → ℝ f:X\to\mathbb{R} defined on a closed uniformly polynomially cuspidal set X X in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} is real analytic if and only if f f is smooth and all its composites with germs of polynomial curves in X X are real analytic. The degree of the polynomial curves needed for this is effectively related to the regularity of the boundary of X X . For instance, if the boundary of X X is locally Lipschitz, then polynomial curves of degree 2 2 suffice. In this Lipschitz case, we also prove that a function f : X → ℝ f:X\to\mathbb{R} is real analytic if and only if all its composites with germs of quadratic polynomial maps in two variables with images in X X are real analytic; here it is not necessary to assume that f f is smooth.

Introduction

In this note, we are interested in Hartogs-type characterizations of real analytic functions.
Let us recall two fundamental results due to Bochnak and Siciak:
let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty open set and
f:U\to\mathbb{R}
any function.

Theorem A

(
[
2
,
16
]
)
.

The function
f
is real analytic if and only if
f
is smooth and the restriction of
f
to each affine line
that meets
U
is real analytic.

Theorem B

(
[
3
,
4
]
)
.

The function
f
is real analytic if and only if the restriction of
f
to each affine
2
-plane that meets
U
is real analytic.

In Theorem
A
, the assumption that
f
is smooth cannot be omitted.
Recently, Bochnak, Kollár, and Kucharz
[
1
]
proved a global version of Theorem
B
:
a function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
on a real analytic manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 3
is real analytic
if
f|_{N}
is real analytic for every real analytic submanifold
N\subseteq M

that is homeomorphic to the
2
-sphere.

We will investigate versions of these results on closed fat subsets
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with cusps
(that
X
is
fat
means that it is contained in the closure of its interior, i.e.,
X=\overline{X^{\circ}}
).
Even if
X
has Lipschitz boundary, we will have to
compose
f
with germs of quadratic polynomial maps
instead of just affine maps.
It will turn out that the maximal degree of the polynomial maps needed to
detect real analyticity is strongly related to the regularity of the boundary (i.e., to the sharpness of the cusps).

A recent result of Kucharz and Kurdyka
[
9
]
(see
C
below)
shows that, for subanalytic functions on real analytic manifolds,
real analyticity can be recognized by restriction to real analytic subsets of dimension one.
We will discuss a variant on suitable closed fat subsets
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

This note is a natural continuation of our papers
[
13
]
and

[
14
]
.
Before we can state the results, some terminology must be introduced.

1.1.
Plot-analytic functions

Let
d,m,n
be positive integers.
Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty.
By an
(m,d)
-plot in
X
we mean the germ
[p]
at
0
of a polynomial map
p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

•

of degree at most
d
, i.e.,

p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\deg p_{i}\leq d
,

•

and image contained in
X
, i.e., there exists a neighborhood
U
of
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

p(U)\subseteq X
.

We will write
p
instead of
[p]
; this slight abuse of notation will
lead to no confusion.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{m,d}(X)
denote the set of all
(m,d)
-plots in
X
.
We will also consider

\mathcal{P}_{m}(X):=\bigcup_{d=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m,d}(X)

and call its elements
polynomial
m
-plots in
X
.

For any function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and any
p\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(X)

it is meaningful to consider the function germ
f\circ p
at
0
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
We define
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m,d}(X)
to be the set of all functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

f\circ p\text{ is a real analytic germ at }0\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\text{ for all }p\in\mathcal{P}_{m,d}(X).

Furthermore, we set

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X):=\bigcap_{d=1}^{\infty}\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m,d}(X).

We call the elements of
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m,d}(X)

(m,d)
-plot-analytic functions
.
Recall that
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is called
arc-analytic
if
f\circ c
is real analytic for each germ
c
of a real analytic arc in
X
.
The arc-analytic functions on
X
form a subset of
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1}(X)
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed.
Let
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}|_{X}
) be the set of all functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}

such that there exist an open neighborhood
U
of
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and a real analytic (resp. smooth) function
F:U\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F|_{X}=f
.
Note that each
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}
is the restriction to
X
of a holomorphic function defined on an open neighborhood of
X
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

1.2.
UPC sets

Let us recall (cf.
[
12
]
) that
a closed set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
uniformly polynomially cuspidal
(UPC)
if there exist positive integers
m,D
and a constant
M>0
such that for each
x\in X
there is a polynomial curve
h_{x}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

of degree at most
D

such that

(1)

h_{x}(0)=x
,

(2)

\operatorname{dist}(h_{x}(t),\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus X)\geq M\,t^{m}
for all
x\in X
and
t\in[0,1]
.

In that case, we say that the UPC set
X
has the
characteristic

(m,D)
. (The constant
M
will not be important for us.)
Let
\operatorname{char}(X)
be the set of all characteristics of
X
.
Note that if
(m,D)\in\operatorname{char}(X)
then
(m,D)+\mathbb{N}^{2}\subseteq\operatorname{char}(X)
.
We define

d(X):=2\min_{(m,D)\in\operatorname{char}(X)}\max\{m,D\}.

Any UPC set
X
is fat.
We say that
X
is
simple
if each
x\in\partial X

has a basis of neighborhoods
\mathscr{U}
such that
U\cap X^{\circ}
is connected for all
U\in\mathscr{U}
.
We shall see in
Example

3.3
that, regarding our results, to be simple is a natural and indispensable condition.

An important class of UPC sets is the collection of all compact fat subanalytic sets
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
see Pawłucki and Plesniak
[
12
]
.

1.3.
Hölder and Lipschitz sets

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
r,h>0
. The set

\Gamma^{\alpha}_{n}(r,h):=\Big\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:|x^{\prime}|<r,\,\big(\tfrac{|x^{\prime}|}{r}\big)^{\alpha}<\tfrac{x_{n}}{h}<1\Big\}

is a
truncated open
\alpha
-cusp
.
By an
\alpha
-set
we mean a closed fat set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
X^{\circ}
has the
uniform
\alpha
-cusp property
:
for each
x\in\partial X
there exist
\epsilon>0
, a truncated open
\alpha
-cusp
\Gamma=\Gamma^{\alpha}_{n}(r,h)
,
and an orthogonal linear map
A\in O(n)
such that
y+A\Gamma\subseteq X^{\circ}
for all

y\in X\cap B(x,\epsilon)
.
A bounded open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has the uniform
\alpha
-cusp property if and only if it has
\alpha
-Hölder boundary; cf.
[
14
, Remark 2.1]
.
We say that
X
is a
Hölder set
if
X
is an
\alpha
-set for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
;

1
-sets are also called
Lipschitz sets
. Note that Hölder sets are always simple; see

[
13
, Proposition 3.9]
.

By definition, any compact
\alpha
-set
X
is a UPC set of characteristic
(\lceil\alpha^{-1}\rceil,1)

so that
d(X)\leq 2\lceil\alpha^{-1}\rceil
,
where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer
m\geq x
.
If
X
is not a
\beta
-set with
\lceil\beta^{-1}\rceil<\lceil\alpha^{-1}\rceil
,
then
d(X)=2\lceil\alpha^{-1}\rceil
. For instance,
we have

d(\overline{\Gamma}^{1/m}_{n}(r,h))=2m

for
the closure
\overline{\Gamma}^{1/m}_{n}(r,h)
of
\Gamma^{1/m}_{n}(r,h)
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.

1.4.
Results

1.4.1.
Smooth plot-analytic functions

The first result extends
A
to simple closed UPC sets
with a precise control of the degree of the required polynomial
1
-plots in terms of
d(X)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a simple closed UPC set and let
d:=d(X)
. Then

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1,d}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\infty}|_{X}=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}.

We shall see in
Example

3.1
that
d=d(X)
is optimal:
in general,

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1,d}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\infty}|_{X}\not\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}
if
d<d(X)
.

If we do not restrict the degree of the polynomial plots, we may infer:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a simple closed set such that for each
z\in\partial X
there exists
a closed UPC set
X_{z}
with
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X
.
Then

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1}(X)\cap\mathcal{C}^{\infty}|_{X}=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}.

For instance, the corollary applies to the compact subset
X=X^{\prime}\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}X_{m}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
where
X_{m}:=\overline{\Gamma}^{1/m}_{2}(\frac{1}{m^{2}},\frac{1}{m})+(2\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{1}{m^{2}},-\frac{1}{m})

and
X^{\prime}=[0,\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}]\times[0,1]
. It is simple, since the cusps
X_{m}
converge to the point
(\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3},0)
.
The spikes
X_{m}
can be replaced by suitable horn-like sets.

1.4.2.
Plot-analytic functions that are not presupposed to be smooth

Next we will discuss extensions of
B
to closed fat sets. In the following, we will not assume that
f
is smooth.

By a
simplex
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we mean the convex hull of any collection of
n+1
affinely independent points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a simple closed set such that for each
z\in\partial X
there exists
a simplex
X_{z}
with
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X
.
Then

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{2,2}(X)=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}.

This is best possible; see
Example

3.2
.
The assumption is fulfilled for all Lipschitz sets.
At this stage, we do not know if there is an analogue of
Theorem

1.3

for Hölder sets or simple fat closed subanalytic sets.
But in dimension two, we can give a fairly complete answer.
Here (for technical reasons) we need to work with

d^{\prime}(X):=2\min_{(m,D)\in\operatorname{char}(X)}m\cdot D.

For all compact Hölder sets
X
we have
d^{\prime}(X)=d(X)
;
in general,
d^{\prime}(X)\geq d(X)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a simple compact fat subanalytic or Hölder set and let
d^{\prime}:=d^{\prime}(X)
.
Then

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{2,d^{\prime}}(X)=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}.

It is likely that in this statement
d^{\prime}=d^{\prime}(X)
is not optimal.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a simple closed set such that for each
z\in\partial X
there exists
a compact fat subanalytic set
X_{z}
with
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X
.
Then

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{2}(X)=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}.

Note that for each Hölder set
X^{\prime}_{z}
with
z\in X^{\prime}_{z}\subseteq X
we may find a
compact fat subanalytic set
X_{z}
with
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{z}
; the converse is not always possible.

1.4.3.
Curve-analytic functions

We give an application to curve-analytic functions on closed fat sets
which extends a recent result of Kucharz and Kurdyka
[
9
]
, see
C
below.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
a function.
We say that
f
is
curve-analytic
if for each real analytic arc
c:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
image contained in
X

there exist
\delta\in(0,\epsilon]
and a real analytic function
F:U\to\mathbb{R}
defined on an open neighborhood
U
of
c(0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
c(t)\in U
and
F(c(t))=f(c(t))
for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
.

Remark 1.6
.

If
X
is a real analytic manifold, then
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is curve-analytic if and only if

f|_{C}
is real analytic
for every locally irreducible real analytic set
C
of dimension
1
in
X
; see
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
.
(That
f|_{C}
is real analytic means that for each
x\in C
there is a neighborhood
U
of
x
in
X
and a real analytic function
F:U\to\mathbb{R}

such that
f|_{C\cap U}=F|_{C\cap U}
.)

Theorem C

(
[
9
]
)
.

A subanalytic function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
on a real analytic manifold
X

is real analytic if and only if it is curve-analytic.

It is an open question, if the assumption that
f
is subanalytic is necessary.

By definition, if
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is curve-analytic, then
f
is arc-analytic, in particular,
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1}(X)
.
Thus, any
smooth
curve-analytic
f:X\to\mathbb{R}

is the restriction of a real analytic function, for all
X
satisfying the assumptions of
Corollary

1.2
.
More interestingly, without presupposing that
f
is smooth, we have:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a simple closed set
such that one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(1)

n
is arbitrary and for each
z\in\partial X
there is a simplex
X_{z}
with
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X
.

(2)

n=2
and for each
z\in\partial X
there is a compact fat subanalytic set
X_{z}
with
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X
.

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be any function
such that
f|_{X_{z}}
is subanalytic for all

X_{z}
appearing in
1
or
2
.
Then
f
is curve-analytic if and only if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}
.

All the results are proved in
Section

2
.
The examples in
Section

3
show that the connection between the degree of polynomial plots
that recognize real analyticity and the regularity of the boundary is optimal
and complement the investigation.

Localization of the problems

Proposition

at

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be the map defined by

Let

Then

is a neighborhood of

F:U\to\mathbb{R}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Proof.

This is a consequence of a theorem of Luna

are the basic invariants of the natural action of the group

on

\mathbb{R}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}

Let us sketch an alternative proof.
Take a smooth function

such that
\chi\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
0
and
\chi\equiv 0

outside

, where

. Then
\tilde{f}:=\chi f
extends by zero to a function defined on all of

Thus, by

7
]
or
[

extends

to
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In particular,

has a smooth extension to a neighborhood of

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If

denotes the Taylor series at

of

(equivalently, of

then

has positive radius of convergence, since

,

converges and defines a real analytic function with the desired properties.

Remark 2.9

An important step in the proof of

is the criterion that an arc-analytic subanalytic function

satisfies the

That means that for every pair of linearly independent vectors

3.
Examples

The first example shows that
in general the degree of polynomial test plots in
Theorem

1.1
is optimal.

Example 3.1
.

Let
D
be a positive integer.
The horn
H=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0\leq x\leq 1,\,x^{D}\leq y\leq 2x^{D}\}

is a UPC set with characteristic
(D,D)
and
d(H)=2D
.
Indeed, every polynomial curve
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
h(0)=0

and
h([0,1])\subseteq H
must be of degree at least
D
and
\operatorname{dist}(h(t),\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus H)\lesssim t^{D}
for
t\in[0,1]
.
Every polynomial
1
-plot in
H
through
0
must be of degree at least
2D
.

The function
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f(x,y):=e^{-1/(x^{2}+y^{2})}
for
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
and
f(0,0):=0

belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}|_{H}
and to
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1,2D-1}(H)
, simply because

f
is real analytic away from
0
and for all
p\in\mathcal{P}_{1,2D-1}(H)
we have
p(0)\neq 0
.

On the other hand,
f\not\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{1,2D}(H)
either by
Theorem

1.1
or by
observing that for
p(t):=(t^{2},t^{2D})
, which lies in
H
for all
t
near
0
, the composite

(f\circ p)(t)=\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{t^{4}(1+t^{4(D-1)})}\Big)

has no analytic extension to
t=0
.

The following easy example shows that
Theorem

1.3
is best possible.

Example 3.2
.

Let
X=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}\geq 0\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n\}
be the first orthant.
A plot
p\in\mathcal{P}_{m,1}(X)
(
m
arbitrary) can satisfy
p(0)=0
only if
p\equiv 0
.
In other words, an arbitrary function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m,1}(X)
is unrestricted at
0
.

The next example shows that the assumption of being simple is essential.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the set
X_{1}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\geq 0,\,x^{\sqrt{2}}\leq y\leq x^{\sqrt{2}}+x^{2}\}
.
Every smooth curve
c
in
X_{1}
must vanish to infinite order on
c^{-1}(0)
; see
[
14
, Example 6.7]
.
Thus
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X_{1})
(
m
arbitrary)
contains elements that do not have a real analytic extension.

Let
X_{2}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x\leq 0\}
be the left half-plane
and consider
X:=X_{1}\cup X_{2}
which is not simple.
But for each point
z
of
\partial X
there exists a simple fat compact subanalytic set
X_{z}
such that
z\in X_{z}\subseteq X
.
Again it is easy to find (even smooth) elements of
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)

that do not admit a real analytic extension.

Note that it was wrongly stated in
[
13
, Corollary 1.17]
that, in a related result, the assumption
that the set is simple is not needed.

There are algebraic sets
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)
(
m
arbitrary) is essentially bigger than
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}|_{X}
:

Example 3.4
.

Consider the algebraic set
X=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x^{3}=y^{2}\}
and the function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\varphi(x,y)=y^{1/3}
.
Then
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)
for all
m\geq 1
. Indeed, let
p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(X)
. The composite
u=\varphi\circ p

satisfies
u^{2}=p_{1}
and
u^{3}=p_{2}
so that
u
is smooth, by a result of Joris
[
8
]
, and in turn real analytic,
by
[
11
, Proposition VI.3.11]
.

In fact, we can completely describe
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)
: for all
m\geq 1
,

\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)=\varphi^{*}\mathcal{C}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}):=\{g\circ\varphi:g\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})\}.

That
\varphi^{*}\mathcal{C}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is contained in
\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)
is an easy consequence of the fact that
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)
.
Conversely, suppose that
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\omega}_{m}(X)
.
Then
g(t):=f(t^{2},t^{3})
defines an element
g\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
g(\varphi(x,y))=f(y^{2/3},y)=f(x,y)
.

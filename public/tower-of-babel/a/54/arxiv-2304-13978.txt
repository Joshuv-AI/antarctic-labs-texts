Applications of Ramanujan’s work on Eisenstein series
Masato Kobayashi

Abstract
. Ramanujan (1916) expressed quotients of certain q q -series as polynomials of the Eisenstein series P , Q , R P,Q,R and derived the famous Ramanujan’s differential equations. We continue this research with the variants of Eisenstein-type series which Hahn (2007) recently introduced. We also prove new formulas of convolution sums for divisor sum functions as subsequent work of Cheng-Williams (2004) and Huard-Ou-Spearman-Williams (2002).

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Eisenstein series

Eisenstein series
are one of the important topics in the number theory;
in particular, they play a significant role in the theory of modular forms, Ramanujan’s theta functions and elliptic functions.
Let us make a definition of these series after preparing several words and symbols. For
n\in\mathbf{N}
and
s\in\mathbf{R}
,
the
divisor sum function
of weight
s
is

\sigma_{s}(n)=\sum_{d|n}d^{s}
.
Often, we write
\sigma_{1}(n)=\sigma(n)
.
In the sequel, we always assume
q,x\in\mathbf{C}
with
|q|<1
and
|x|<1
. Define signed Bernoulli numbers
(B_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by

\displaystyle\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{n!}x^{n}.

The first few values of nonzero
B_{n}
are

B_{0}=1,B_{1}=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2},B_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{6},B_{4}=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{30},B_{6}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{42},B_{8}=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{30},B_{10}=\displaystyle\frac{5}{66}.

Definition 1.1

(Normalized Eisenstein series)
.

E_{2k}(q)=1-\displaystyle\frac{4k}{B_{2k}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{2k-1}(n)q^{n},\quad k\geq 1.

We remark that it is more traditional to regard Eisenstein series as functions of
\tau\in\mathbf{C}
,
\text{Im}(\tau)>0
with
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
.
Customarily, there is particular usage for the first three Eisenstein series as

\displaystyle P(q)

\displaystyle=1-24\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle Q(q)

\displaystyle=E_{4}(q)=1+240\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{3}(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle R(q)

\displaystyle=E_{6}(q)=1-504\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{5}(n)q^{n}.

In the literature, some authors also use symbols
L(q),M(q),N(q)
for
P(q),Q(q),R(q)
, respectively.
Whenever there is no confusion from the context, we abbreviate
P(q),Q(q),R(q)
to
P,Q,R
.

Following Hahn

Definition 2.5

\displaystyle\widetilde{\sigma}_{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|n}(-1)^{d-1}d^{s}.

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}_{s}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d|n}(-1)^{n/d-1}d^{s}.

Often, we write

For convenience, set

Remark 2.6
.

[ 1 ]

These functions are related to Lambert-type series in the following way.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{k^{s}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{s}(n)q^{n}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k-1}k^{s}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{\sigma}_{s}(n)q^{n}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{k^{s}q^{k}}{1+q^{k}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\sigma}_{s}(n)q^{n}.

[ 2 ]

Observe the following properties.

\widetilde{\sigma}_{s}(n)=\sigma_{s}(n)-2^{s+1}\sigma_{s}\left({\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}}\right).

\widehat{\sigma}_{s}(n)=\sigma_{s}(n)-2\sigma_{s}\left({\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}}\right).

\widetilde{\sigma}_{s}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}d^{s}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\text{ even}\end{subarray}}d^{s},\quad\widehat{\sigma}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}d^{s}.

In particular, if
n
is odd, then

\widetilde{\sigma}_{s}(n)=\sigma_{s}(n)=\widehat{\sigma}_{s}(n).

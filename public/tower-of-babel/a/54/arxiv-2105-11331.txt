Abstract
. We study measurable spaces equipped with a σ \sigma -ideal of negligible sets. We find conditions under which they admit a localizable locally determined version – a kind of fiber space that describes locally their directions – defined by a universal property in an appropriate category that we introduce. These methods allow to promote each measure space ( X , 𝒜 , μ ) (X,\mathscr{A},\mu) to a strictly localizable version ( X ^ , 𝒜 ^ , μ ^ ) (\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu}) , so that the dual of 𝐋 1 ​ ( X , 𝒜 , μ ) \mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu) is 𝐋 ∞ ​ ( X ^ , 𝒜 ^ , μ ^ ) \mathbf{L}_{\infty}(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu}) . Corresponding to this duality is a generalized Radon-Nikodým theorem. We also provide a characterization of the strictly localizable version in special cases that include integral geometric measures, when the negligibles are the purely unrectifiable sets in a given dimension.

Foreword

The Radon-Nikodým Theorem does not hold for every measure space

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
.
One way to phrase this precisely is to consider the canonical
embedding

\Upsilon:\mathbf{L}_{\infty}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)\to\mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)^{*}.

The following hold.

(A)

\Upsilon
is injective (this corresponds to the uniqueness
almost everywhere of Radon-Nikodým derivatives) if and only if

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is semi-finite.

(B)

\Upsilon
is surjective (this corresponds to the existence
of Radon-Nikodým derivatives) if and only if the Boolean algebra

\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}
is Dedekind complete (i.e. order
complete as a lattice).

While (A) is classical, see e.g.
[
5
, 243G(a)]
, (B) is
recent and due to the second author, see
[
3
, 4.6]
.
Let us recall the relevant definitions.
Given a measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
, we abbreviate
\mathscr{A}^{f}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{A:\mu(A)<\infty\}
and
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{N:\mu(N)=0\}
.
We say that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is
semi-finite
if every
A\in\mathscr{A}
of infinite measure contains some
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\setminus\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
.
Equivalently,
\mu(A)=\sup\{\mu(F):A\supseteq F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\}
.
We further define the
\sigma
-ideal of
locally
\mu
-null
sets
as follows:
\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{A:A\cap F\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu}\text{ for all }F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\}
.
It is easy to see
[
3
, 4.4]
that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is
semi-finite if and only if
\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}=\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
.
Thus we obtain the following classical criterion,
[
5
, 243G(b)]
.

(C)

\Upsilon
is an isometric isomorphism if and only if

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is semi-finite and the Boolean algebra
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
is Dedekind complete.

Though semi-finiteness is a natural property, Caratheodory’s method
does not always provide it.
For instance, the measure spaces
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{H}^{1}},\mathscr{H}^{1})
and

(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathscr{I}^{1}_{\infty})
are not semi-finite – see

[
8
, 439H]
and
[
4
, 3.3.20]
.
Here,
\mathscr{H}^{1}
is the 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure in the
Euclidean plane
[
4
, 2.10.2]
,
\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{H}^{1}}
is the

\sigma
-algebra consisting of
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-measurable sets in
Caratheodory’s sense,
\mathscr{I}^{1}_{\infty}
is a 1-dimensional integral
geometric measure
[
4
, 2.10.5(1)]
, and
\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the

\sigma
-algebra whose members are the Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Both
\mathscr{H}^{1}(\Gamma)
and
\mathscr{I}^{1}_{\infty}(\Gamma)
coincide with the
usual Euclidean length of
\Gamma
when this is a Lipschitz curve.

It is natural to want to associate, with an arbitrary

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
, an improved version of itself – in a universal way
– ideally one for which the Radon-Nikodým Theorem holds.
This is one of our several achievements in this paper.
It is not difficult to modify slightly the measure
\mu
, keeping the
underlying measurable space
(X,\mathscr{A})
untouched, in order to make it
semi-finite.
Specifically, letting
\mu_{\mathrm{sf}}(A)=\sup\{\mu(A\cap F):F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\}
, for
A\in\mathscr{A}
, one checks that

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu_{\mathrm{sf}})
is semi-finite and that

\mathscr{N}_{\mu_{\mathrm{sf}}}=\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}
.
However, it appears to be a more delicate task to modify

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
in a canonical way in order for
\Upsilon
to become
surjective.

An idea for testing whether
\Upsilon
is surjective is as
follows.
Given
\alpha\in\mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)^{*}
we apply the Radon-Nikodým
Theorem “locally”, as it is valid on each finite measure subspace

(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mu_{F})
,
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
, i.e. we represent by integration
the functional
\alpha\circ\iota_{F}\in\mathbf{L}_{1}(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mu_{F})^{*}
,
where
\iota_{F}\colon\mathbf{L}_{1}(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mu_{F})\to\mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is the obvious map.
This produces a family of Radon-Nikodým derivatives
\langle f_{F}\rangle_{F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}}
.
By the almost everywhere uniqueness of Radon-Nikodým derivatives in
finite measure spaces, this is a
compatible family
in the sense
that
F\cap F^{\prime}\cap\{f_{F}\neq f_{F^{\prime}}\}\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
, for every

F,F^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
.
In order to obtain a globally defined Radon-Nikodým derivative, one
ought to be able to “glue” together the functions of this family.
A
gluing
of
\langle f_{F}\rangle_{F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}}
is, by definition, an

\mathscr{A}
-measurable function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
F\cap\{f\neq f_{F}\}\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
for every
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
.

The question whether such a gluing exists takes us away from the
realm of measure spaces, as it rather pertains to
measurable
spaces with negligibles
, abbreviated MSNs, i.e. triples

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
where
(X,\mathscr{A})
is a measurable space and
\mathscr{N}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is a
\sigma
-ideal.
The notion of compatible family
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}
of

\mathscr{A}_{E}
-measurable functions
E\to\mathbb{R}
subordinated to an arbitrary
collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
readily makes sense in this more
general setting, as does the notion of gluing of such a compatible
family.
That each compatible family of partially defined measurable functions
admits a gluing is equivalent to the Boolean algebra
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}

being Dedekind complete.
In this case we say that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is
localizable
.
Equivalently,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is localizable if and only if every
collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
admits an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum
(see
3.3
for a definition), which corresponds to taking
an actual supremum in the Boolean algebra
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
.
For a proof of these classical equivalences, see
e.g.
[
3
, 3.13]
.
It will be convenient to call
\mathscr{N}
-generating
a collection

\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
that admits
X
as an
\mathscr{N}
-essential
supremum.
For instance, one easily checks that if
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is a
semi-finite measure space, then
\mathscr{A}^{f}
is
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating.

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a localizable MSN,
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}

be
\mathscr{N}
-generating, and
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}
be a compatible
family of partially defined measurable functions.
The problem of gluing this compatible family in our setting is
reminiscent of the fact that, for a topological space
X
, the functor
of continuous functions on open sets is a sheaf.
However, unlike in the case of continuous functions, in order to
define
f
globally, we ought to make choices on the domains
E\cap E^{\prime}
, for
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
, because
f_{E}
and
f_{E^{\prime}}
do not coincide
everywhere there, but merely
almost everywhere
.
In an attempt to avoid the issue, one can replace
\mathscr{E}
with an
almost disjointed refinement of itself, say
\mathscr{F}
.
By this we mean that each member of
\mathscr{F}
is contained in a member
of
\mathscr{E}
, that
\mathscr{F}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating, and that
F\cap F^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
whenever
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
are distinct.
The existence of

follows from Zorn’s Lemma, see
4.9
.
Still,
F\cap F^{\prime}
may not be empty whenever
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
are
distinct and we are again in a position to make choices.
A step further along the road would be to produce from
\mathscr{F}
a
disjointed family
\mathscr{G}
whose union is conegligible.
From classical measure theory, we learn of two situations when this is
doable.
First, in the presence of a lower density of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
(see

10.1
for a definition), and second when
\rmcard\mathscr{F}\leqslant\mathfrak{c}
(see the proof of
7.6
).
In those cases, a gluing exists.
In fact, in the context of measure spaces, the existence of a lower
density yields a somewhat stronger structure than localizability.
In order to state this, we need one more definition.
We say that a measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is
locally
determined
if it is semi-finite and if the following holds:

\forall A\subseteq X:\big[\forall F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}:A\cap F\in\mathscr{A}\big]\Rightarrow A\in\mathscr{A}.

A complete locally determined measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
admits a
lower density if and only if it is strictly localizable, which means,
by definition, that there exists a partition
\mathscr{G}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
of

X
such that
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{A:A\cap G\in\mathscr{A}\text{
for all }G\in\mathscr{G}\}
and
\mu(A)=\sum_{G\in\mathscr{G}}\mu(A\cap G)
, for
A\in\mathscr{A}
.
See
[
7
, 341M]
for a proof.
The existence of a lower density for a strictly localizable measure
space follows from the case of finite measure spaces by gluing, and
the case of finite measure spaces is a consequence of a martingale
convergence theorem.
Even though the notion of a lower density makes sense for MSNs, their
existence does not hold for even the most natural generalization of
finite measure spaces, namely ccc MSNs (satisfying the countable chain
condition,
4.3
and
4.5
), see
[
16
]
.

Both notions of localizability (of an MSN) and local
determination (of a measure space) seem to express in different ways
the fact that “there are enough measurable sets”.
For instance, one easily checks that an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})

\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{P}(X)

2.
Measurable spaces with negligibles

2.1
\sigma
-algebra. —

Let
X
be a set. A
\sigma
-algebra
on
X
is a set
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(X)
such that

(1)

\emptyset\in\mathscr{A}
;

(2)

If
A\in\mathscr{A}
then
X\setminus A\in\mathscr{A}
;

(3)

If
\langle A_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathscr{A}
then

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}\in\mathscr{A}
.

If
\mathscr{A}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
X
then
X\in\mathscr{A}
and

\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}\in\mathscr{A}
whenever
\langle A_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
a sequence in
\mathscr{A}
. Clearly
\{\emptyset,X\}
and
\mathscr{P}(X)
are

\sigma
-algebras on
X
, respectively the coarsest and the finest.
If
\langle\mathscr{A}_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
is a nonempty family of

\sigma
-algebras on
X
then
\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathscr{A}_{i}
is a

\sigma
-algebra on
X
. Thus each
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(X)
is
contained in a coarsest
\sigma
-algebra on
X
which we will denote
by
\sigma(\mathscr{E})
. If
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(X)
and
\mathscr{A}=\sigma(\mathscr{E})
we say that the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}

is
generated
by
\mathscr{E}
. Clearly, if
\mathscr{E}_{1}\subseteq\mathscr{E}_{2}\subseteq\mathscr{P}(X)
then
\sigma(\mathscr{E}_{1})\subseteq\sigma(\mathscr{E}_{2})
. A

measurable space
is a couple
(X,\mathscr{A})
where
X
is a set
and
\mathscr{A}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
X
. In this case, if no
confusion is possible we call
measurable
the members of

\mathscr{A}
.

2.2
Measurable maps. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B})
be measurable spaces and
f\colon X\to Y
. We say that
f
is
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})
-measurable
(or
simply
measurable
if no confusion can occur) if
f^{-1}(B)\in\mathscr{A}
whenever
B\in\mathscr{B}
. Measurable spaces, together with
measurable maps, form a well-defined category, as one can check that
the composition of two measurable maps is measurable.

2.3
\sigma
-ideal. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A})
be a measurable space. A
\sigma
-ideal

\mathscr{N}
of
\mathscr{A}
is a subset of
\mathscr{A}
that satisfies the
following requirements:

(1)

\emptyset\in\mathscr{N}
;

(2)

If
A\in\mathscr{A}
,
N\in\mathscr{N}
and
A\subseteq N
then
A\in\mathscr{N}
;

(3)

If
\langle N_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathscr{N}
, then

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}N_{n}\in\mathscr{N}
.

2.4
Measurable space with negligibles. —

A
measurable space with negligibles
(abbreviated
MSN
) is
a triple
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
where
(X,\mathscr{A})
is a measurable
space and
\mathscr{N}
is a
\sigma
-ideal of
\mathscr{A}
. Given an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
, elements belonging to
\mathscr{N}
are referred to as

\mathscr{N}
-negligible
sets (simply negligible sets if no
confusion can occur). Complements of
\mathscr{N}
-negligible sets
are called
\mathscr{N}
-conegligible
sets (or simply
conegligible sets).

We can associate to any measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
the MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
where
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
is the
\sigma
-ideal

\mathscr{N}_{\mu}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{N:\mu(N)=0\}
. Conversely, any MSN

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
derives from a measure space: it suffices to
consider the measure
\mu\colon\mathscr{A}\to[0,\infty]
that sends negligible sets to
0
and the remaining sets
to
\infty
.

2.5
Saturated MSNs. —

An MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is called
saturated
whenever the
following property holds: For all
N\in\mathscr{N}
, any subset
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
is
\mathscr{A}
-measurable – therefore, in fact,
N^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
.
This
property is of purely technical nature, as an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
that does not have it can be turned into a saturated MSN
(
X,\bar{\mathscr{A}},\bar{\mathscr{N}})
, by setting:

\begin{split}\bar{\mathscr{N}}&=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{\bar{N}:\bar{N}\subseteq N\text{ for some }N\in\mathscr{N}\}\\
\bar{\mathscr{A}}&=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{\bar{A}:A\ominus\bar{A}\in\bar{\mathscr{N}}\text{ for some }A\in\mathscr{A}\}\\
&=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{A\ominus\bar{N}:A\in\mathscr{A}\text{ and }\bar{N}\in\bar{\mathscr{N}}\}.\end{split}

Here,
\ominus
denotes the symmetric difference of sets. We call

(X,\bar{\mathscr{A}},\bar{\mathscr{N}})
the
saturation
of

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
. In case the original MSN corresponds to a measure
space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
, its saturation corresponds to the measure
space usually referred to as the completion of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
. We
will denote the latter by
(X,\bar{\mathscr{A}},\bar{\mu})
.

2.6
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
be two MSNs. We say
that a map
f\colon X\to Y
is
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable
(or simply
measurable
) if

(1)

f
is
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})
-measurable;

(2)

f^{-1}(M)\in\mathscr{N}
for every
M\in\mathscr{M}
.

It is easy to check that measurability in the above sense is
preserved by composition.

2.7
Morphisms of saturated MSNs. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
be two saturated
MSNs. A
morphism
from
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
to
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is an equivalence class of
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable maps under the relation
\sim
of equality
almost everywhere:
f\sim f^{\prime}
whenever
\{f\neq f^{\prime}\}\in\mathscr{N}
.
In order to check that this relation is, indeed, transitive, it is
important to assume that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is saturated for
otherwise we would not know that
\{f\neq f^{\prime\prime}\}\in\mathscr{A}
when

f,f^{\prime\prime}:X\to Y
are both

[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable. Also, in the special
case where
X
is
\mathscr{N}
-negligible and
Y=\emptyset
, we follow
the convention that there is unique morphism from
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
to
(\emptyset,\{\emptyset\},\{\emptyset\})
.

2.8 Lemma
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
,
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
and
(Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})

be MSNs and let
f,f^{\prime}\colon X\to Y
and
g,g^{\prime}\colon Y\to Z
be maps. If

(A)

f,f^{\prime}
are
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable;

(B)

g,g^{\prime}
are
[(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M}),(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})]
-measurable;

(C)

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is saturated;

(D)

f\sim f^{\prime}
,
g\sim g^{\prime}
,

then
g\circ f
,
g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime}
are
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})]
-measurable and
g\circ f\sim g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The first conclusion follows from hypotheses (A) and (B) and
Paragraph
2.6
. The second conclusion is a
consequence of
\{g\circ f\neq g^{\prime}\circ f^{\prime}\}\subseteq\{f\neq f^{\prime}\}\cup f^{-1}(\{g\neq g^{\prime}\})
and hypotheses (A), (C) and (D).
∎

2.9
Category
\mathsf{MSN}
. —

Thanks to the preceding result, there is a notion of composition for
morphisms between saturated MSNs: if
\mathbf{f}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
and
\mathbf{g}\colon(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\to(Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})
are morphisms, we let
\mathbf{g}\circ\mathbf{f}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})
be the equivalence
class of
g\circ f
where
f\in\mathbf{f}
and
g\in\mathbf{g}
.

This allows to define the category
\mathsf{MSN}
whose objects are
saturated MSNs and whose morphisms are described in the
paragraph
2.7
. Additionally, we add the convention
that, for a negligible saturated MSN, i.e. an MSN of the form
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{P}(X))
, there is a unique morphism from
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{P}(X))
to
(\emptyset,\{\emptyset\},\{\emptyset\})
. This way,
negligible saturated MSNs are isomorphic to one another in the
category
\mathsf{MSN}
.

The categorical point of view is rarely considered in measure theory,
mainly due to the lack of a well-behaved notion of morphism between
measure spaces. The category

\mathsf{MSN}
also appears in the work
[
14
]
under the
name
\mathsf{StrictEMS}
. We start to investigate the existence of
limits and colimits in this category.

2.10
subMSN. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN and
Z\in\mathscr{P}(X)
. We define
the
subMSN

(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
, where
\mathscr{A}_{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{A\cap Z:A\in\mathscr{A}\}
and
\mathscr{N}_{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{N\cap Z:N\in\mathscr{N}\}
. Note that in the special case where
Z
is

\mathscr{A}
-measurable, we have
\mathscr{A}_{Z}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{A:A\subseteq Z\}
and likewise
\mathscr{N}_{Z}=\mathscr{N}\cap\{N:N\subseteq Z\}
.

The inclusion map
\iota_{Z}\colon Z\to X
is
[(\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z}),(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})]
-measurable and induces a morphism

\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}
between
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
and
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
.

2.11 Proposition
. —

Let
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
be a pair of
morphisms
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
in the category
\mathsf{MSN}
, represented by
the maps
f\in\mathbf{f}
and
g\in\mathbf{g}
, and set
Z\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{f=g\}
. Then the equalizer of
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
is
[(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z}),\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}]
.

Proof.

As
f\circ\iota_{Z}=g\circ\iota_{Z}
, we have clearly
\mathbf{f}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}=\mathbf{g}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}
. Let

\mathbf{h}\colon(T,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be any other
morphism that satisfies the relation
\mathbf{f}\circ\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{g}\circ\mathbf{h}
and let
h\in\mathbf{h}
. Then
h^{-1}(Z)
is conegligible in

T
. Up to modifying
h
, we can suppose that it has values in

Z
. The restriction
h^{\prime}\colon T\to Z
of
h
is
[(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P}),(\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})]
-measurable and we have
h=\iota_{Z}\circ h^{\prime}
, leading to a factorization
\mathbf{h}=\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}\circ\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
. This factorization is unique, as any morphism
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}

satisfying
\mathbf{h}=\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}\circ\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
must derive from
a map
h^{\prime}\colon T\to Z
that coincides almost everywhere with
h
.
∎

2.12 Proposition
. —

The category
\mathsf{MSN}
has coproducts. Let
\langle(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})\rangle_{i\in I}
be a family of saturated MSNs. Its coproduct
is the MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
whose underlying set is
X=\coprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
, and whose
\sigma
-algebra and

\sigma
-ideal are defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{A:A\cap X_{i}\in\mathscr{A}_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{N}=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{N:N\cap X_{i}\in\mathscr{N}_{i}\text{ for all }i\in I\}.

For
i\in I
, the canonical morphism
\boldsymbol{\iota}_{i}\colon(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is the morphism
induced by the inclusion map
\iota_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X
.

Proof.

Notice that, indeed,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is a saturated MSN. Let
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
be a saturated MSN and
\langle\mathbf{f}_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
be a collection of morphisms from
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
to

(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
, each
\mathbf{f}_{i}
being represented by a
measurable map
f_{i}
. We set
f=\coprod_{i\in I}f_{i}
, the map
such that
f\circ\iota_{i}=f_{i}
for any
i\in I
. It is clear
that
f
is
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable and

\mathbf{f}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{i}=\mathbf{f}_{i}
holds for all

i\in I
. We need to show that
\mathbf{f}
is the unique morphism

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
with this property.

Suppose
\mathbf{g}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is
another morphism, represented by a measurable map
g\colon X\to Y
, for which
\mathbf{g}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{i}=\mathbf{f}_{i}
for
all
i\in I
. Then
f
and
g
coincide almost everywhere on each

X_{i}
, which implies, due to the choice of
\mathscr{N}
, that
f
and
g

are equal almost everywhere and
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{g}
.
∎

2.13 Proposition
. —

The category
\mathsf{MSN}
has countable products. Let
\langle(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})\rangle_{i\in I}
be a countable family of saturated
MSNs. Its product is the MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
, whose underlying
set is the product
X=\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}
, whose
\sigma
-ideal is

\mathscr{N}=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\left\{N:\exists\langle N_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathscr{N}_{i},\quad N\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{-1}(N_{i})\right\},

where
\pi_{i}\colon X\to X_{i}
denotes the projection map, and whose

\sigma

\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathscr{A}_{i}

\mathscr{A}=\left\{A\ominus N:A\in\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathscr{A}_{i}\text{ and }N\in\mathscr{N}\right\}.

For
i\in I
, the projection morphism
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{i}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
is the map induced by
the projection map
\pi_{i}
.

Proof.

First note that the projection maps
\pi_{i}
are
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})]

\mathscr{A}
and

\mathscr{N}
. Let
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
be a saturated MSN and
\langle\mathbf{f}_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
be a collection of morphisms from
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
to
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
, each
\mathbf{f}_{i}

being represented by a measurable map
f_{i}\colon Y\to X_{i}
. We
define
f=\prod_{i\in I}f_{i}\colon Y\to\prod_{i\in I}X_{i}

that assigns

\langle f_{i}(y)\rangle_{i\in I}
. Clearly,
f

is
(\mathscr{B},\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathscr{A}_{i})
-measurable. Moreover, for
any negligible set
N\in\mathscr{N}
, we can find a sequence
\langle N_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
such that
N\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}\pi_{i}^{-1}(N_{i})
. Thus
f^{-1}(N)\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}(\pi_{i}\circ f)^{-1}(N_{i})=\bigcup_{i\in I}f_{i}^{-1}(N_{i})
. As
I
is
countable and
f_{i}^{-1}(N_{i})\in\mathscr{M}
for all
i\in I
, we find
that
f^{-1}(N)\in\mathscr{M}
, which entails that the map
f
is

[(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M}),(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})]
-measurable.

Let
\mathbf{g}\colon(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be another
morphism satisfying the identities
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{i}\circ\mathbf{g}=\mathbf{f}_{i}
for
i\in I
. Let
g\colon Y\to X
be a
representative of
\mathbf{g}
. The coordinate functions
\pi_{i}\circ g

must coincide with
f_{i}
almost everywhere. As there are only
countably many of them, we conclude that
f
and
g
are equal
almost everywhere, that is,
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{g}
.
∎

2.14 Remark
. —

In case
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
are associated with measure spaces
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mu_{i})
,
i=1,2
, the
\sigma
-ideal
\mathscr{N}
considered in the above proposition may not coincide with
\mathscr{N}_{\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}}
. This is the case, for instance, when
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mu_{i})=(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}),\mathscr{L}^{1})
,
i=1,2
, since the diagonal
D=\mathbb{R}^{2}\cap\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})
is
\mathscr{L}^{2}
-negligible but does not belong to
\mathscr{N}
.

3.
Supremum preserving morphisms

3.1
Motivation. —

One of the reasons we were led to introduce MSNs is that the
category of measure spaces and (equivalence classes of) measure
preserving measurable maps does not have good properties at
all. Roughly speaking, this can be attributed to the fact that it
has very few arrows. One way to increase their number is to define
as morphisms
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\nu)
the
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})
-measurable maps
\varphi\colon X\to Y
such that the
pushforward measure
\varphi_{\#}\mu
is absolutely continuous with
respect to
\nu
. If we drop the measures and retain only which sets
have measure zero, we get the notion of
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{N}_{\nu})]
-measurability
of
2.6
. However, doing so, we may introduce some
“irregular” maps. For example, if
\mathscr{A}
is the
\sigma
-algebra
of Lebesgue measurable sets of the real line,
\mathscr{L}^{1}
the Lebesgue
measure and
\nu
the counting measure on
(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{A})
then the
identity map induces a morphism
(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{L}^{1})\to(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{A},\nu)
. But,
\mathscr{L}^{1}
does not really compare to
\nu
,
although it is absolutely continuous with respect to
\nu
. For
instance,
\mathscr{L}^{1}
has no Radon-Nikodým density with respect to

\nu
, not even in the generalized sense of
Section
9
. Forgetting the measures, the morphism of MSNs

(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{L}^{1}})\to(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
is
still somehow inappropriate. To avoid this, we restrict our
attention to the supremum preserving morphisms introduced
below. This will allow us to define a new category
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
of
saturated MSNs with supremum preserving morphisms. Later, we will be
able to define localizable versions of MSNs and similar notions by
means of universal properties to be satisfied in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
.

3.2
Boolean algebras. —

Many of the properties that we will introduce underneath for MSNs
are related to their
Boolean algebra
, defined in the
following way: given an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
, we observe that the

\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
is a Boolean algebra and
\mathscr{N}
is an
ideal of
\mathscr{A}
in the ring-theoretic sense; we then associate to

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
the quotient Boolean algebra
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
.

When we restrict our attention to saturated MSNs, this construction
becomes functorial. Call
\mathsf{Bool}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})=\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
the Boolean algebra of a saturated MSN. Given a
morphism
\mathbf{f}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})

represented by a measurable map
f\colon X\to Y
, we define

\mathsf{Bool}(f)\colon\mathsf{Bool}(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\to\mathsf{Bool}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
that maps the equivalence class of

B\in\mathscr{B}
to the equivalence class of
f^{-1}(B)
. This map is
well-defined because of the
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurability of
f
, it is a morphism of Boolean
algebras, and it does not depend on the representative of

\mathbf{f}
, as one can easily check.

3.3
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN and
\mathscr{E}
be a subcollection of

\mathscr{A}
. We say that
U\in\mathscr{A}
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential
upper bound
of
\mathscr{E}
whenever
E\setminus U\in\mathscr{N}
for all

E\in\mathscr{E}
. Furthermore, a measurable set
S\in\mathscr{A}
is an

\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum
of
\mathscr{E}
whenever

(1)

S
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper bound of
\mathscr{E}
;

(2)

If
S^{\prime}
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper bound of

\mathscr{E}
, then
S\setminus S^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
.

In particular, if
S
,
S^{\prime}
are both
\mathscr{N}
-essential suprema of

\mathscr{E}
, their symmetric difference
S\ominus S^{\prime}
is negligible. In
other words, an essential supremum, when it exists, is unique up to
negligible sets. In fact, it corresponds to a (unique) supremum in

\mathsf{Bool}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
. A collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}

that admits
X
as an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum is called
\mathscr{N}
-generating
. We will use the following repeatedly. If

\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
and
S\in\mathscr{A}
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential
supremum of
\mathscr{E}
, then
\mathscr{E}\cup\{X\setminus S\}
is

\mathscr{N}
-generating.

The next ubiquitous lemma expresses that
\cap
is distributive over
the (partially defined) operation of taking essential suprema. It
implies the following fact, which we will use frequently: If
\mathscr{E}

is
\mathscr{N}
-generating and
A\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
, then
E\cap A\not\in\mathscr{N}
for some
E\in\mathscr{E}
.

3.4 Lemma

(Distributivity Lemma)
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN,
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
be a
collection that has an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum
S
, and
C\in\mathscr{A}
. Then
C\cap S
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
\{C\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
.

Proof.

Condition (1) in Definition
3.3
is met because
C\cap E\setminus C\cap S=C\cap(E\setminus S)\in\mathscr{N}
for
all
E\in\mathscr{E}
. As for (2), we let
S^{\prime}
be an
\mathscr{N}
-essential
upper bound for
\{C\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
. We claim that
S^{\prime\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=S^{\prime}\cup(X\setminus C)
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper bound
for
\mathscr{E}
. Indeed, for any
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we have
E\setminus S^{\prime\prime}=(C\cap E)\setminus S^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
. It follows that
(C\cap S)\setminus S^{\prime}=S\setminus S^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
.
∎

3.5
. —

Note that if
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
are MSNs,
f:X\to Y
is
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable,
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
, and
S\in\mathscr{B}
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential upper
bound of
\mathscr{E}
, then
f^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper
bound of
f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. However, if
S
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential
supremum of
\mathscr{E}
then
f^{-1}(S)
may not be an
\mathscr{N}
-essential
supremum of
f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. Consider, for instance,

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})=(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}),\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{L}^{1}})
,

(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})=(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}),\{\emptyset\})
,
f=\rmid_{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\mathscr{E}=\{\{x\}:x\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Then
\mathbb{R}
is an

\{\emptyset\}
-essential supremum of
\mathscr{E}
,
\emptyset
is an

\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{L}^{1}}
-essential supremum of
\mathscr{E}=f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
, and

\mathbb{R}\setminus\emptyset\not\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{L}^{1}}
.

3.6
. —

There are several objects that we can call
supremum
preserving
. For saturated MSNs
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
, we define

•

A morphism of Boolean algebras
\varphi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{B}

is called supremum preserving if, for any family
\mathfrak{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
that admits a
supremum, the family
\varphi(\mathfrak{E})
admits a supremum and
\varphi(\sup\mathfrak{E})=\sup\varphi(\mathfrak{E})
.

•

A morphism
\mathbf{f}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is called supremum preserving whenever

\mathsf{Bool}(\mathbf{f})
is.

•

An
[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable map
f\colon X\to Y
is called supremum preserving if, for any collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
possessing an
\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum
S
, then

f^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{f^{-1}(E):E\in\mathscr{E}\}
.

For a morphism
\mathbf{f}
represented by
f\in\mathbf{f}
, the
supremum preserving characters of
f
,
\mathbf{f}
and

\mathsf{Bool}(\mathbf{f})
are all equivalent. Also, the
composition of two supremum preserving morphisms is supremum
preserving. We call
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
the subcategory of
\mathsf{MSN}
that
consists of saturated MSNs and supremum preserving morphisms. In the
next proposition, we gather some basic facts about supremum
preserving morphisms and the category
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
.

3.7 Proposition
. —

The following hold.

(A)

Two saturated MSNs are isomorphic in
\mathsf{MSN}
if and only
if they are isomorphic in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
.

(B)

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a saturated MSN and
Z\in\mathscr{A}
. The morphism
\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}\colon(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
induced by the inclusion map

\iota_{Z}\colon Z\to X
is supremum preserving.

(C)

Let
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
be a pair of morphisms in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
, represented
by
f\in\mathbf{f}
and
g\in\mathbf{g}
. If
Z\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{f=g\}
is
\mathscr{A}
-measurable, then
((Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z}),\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z})
is the equalizer of
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
.

(D)

The category
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
has coproducts, which are
preserved by the forgetful functor
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}\to\mathsf{MSN}
.

Proof.

(A) Let
\mathbf{f}
be an isomorphism in
\mathsf{MSN}
. Then

\mathsf{Bool}(\mathbf{f})
is an isomorphism of Boolean
algebras. More specifically, it is an isomorphism of posets and for
this reason it preserves suprema.

(B) This is the content of Lemma
3.4
.

(C) By Proposition
2.11
,
((Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z}),\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z})
is the equalizer of
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
in

\mathsf{MSN}
and by (B) the morphism
\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}
is a morphism of
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
. Let
\mathbf{h}\colon(T,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})

be a supremum preserving morphism that satisfies
\mathbf{f}\circ\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{g}\circ\mathbf{h}
. Recalling the proof of Proposition
2.11
,
there is a representative
h\in\mathbf{h}
with values in
Z
, and its
restriction
h^{\prime}\colon T\to Z
induces the unique morphism
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}

such that
\mathbf{h}=\boldsymbol{\iota}_{Z}\circ\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
. The results
follows from the fact that
h^{\prime}
is easily checked to be supremum
preserving.

(D) Let
\langle(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})\rangle_{i\in I}
be a family of
saturated MSNs,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be their coproduct in the
category
\mathsf{MSN}
, and
\langle\mathbf{f}_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
be a family
of supremum preserving morphisms from
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
to a
saturated
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
, each represented by
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\to Y
. We need to show that
f\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\coprod_{i\in I}f_{i}\colon X\to Y
is supremum preserving. For this, let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{B}

be a collection that has an
\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum
S
. We
observe that
f^{-1}(S)=\coprod_{i\in I}f^{-1}_{i}(S)
is an

\mathscr{M}
-essential upper bound of
f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. Let
U
be a
second
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper bound of
f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. Then
X_{i}\cap U
is an
\mathscr{N}_{i}
-essential upper bound of
\{X_{i}\cap f^{-1}(E):E\in\mathscr{E}\}=f_{i}^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. It follows that

f_{i}^{-1}(S)\setminus(X_{i}\cap U)\in\mathscr{N}_{i}
. As this happens
for all
i\in I
, we get that
f^{-1}(S)\setminus U\in\mathscr{N}
.
∎

4.
Localizable, cccc and strictly localizable MSNs

4.1
Localizable MSN. —

An MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is
localizable
whenever each
collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
admits an
\mathscr{N}
-essential
supremum. Equivalently,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is localizable whenever
its Boolean algebra
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
is
Dedekind complete
, that
is, each subset of
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
has a supremum.

Originally, localizability was introduced by Segal
[
15
]
in
the context of measure spaces. Since then, many minor variations
over the definition in that context have been proposed (see
[
13
]
for
an overview). We will follow the definition
in
[
5
, Chapter 2]
. A measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is
called
localizable
whenever

(1)

it is
semi-finite
, i.e. for all
A\in\mathscr{A}
with
\mu(A)>0
, there is a measurable set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A

such that
0<\mu(A^{\prime})<\infty
;

(2)

the underlying MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is
localizable.

4.2
Semi-finite measure space. —

In the definition of localizable measure space, semi-finiteness
plays on important rôle. Let us rephrase it. Given
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)

a measure space, we abbreviate
\mathscr{A}^{f}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{E:\mu(E)<\infty\}
. We say that
N\in\mathscr{A}
is
locally

\mu
-negligible
whenever
N\cap E\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
for every
E\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
. We let
\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}
be the
\sigma
-ideal
consisting of locally
\mu
-negligible measurable sets. The
following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathscr{N}_{\mu}=\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}
.

(2)

(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is semi-finite.

(3)

\mathscr{A}^{f}
is
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating.

The only non trivial part is
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. If
N\in\mathscr{A}
then
N
is an
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-essential supremum of
\{N\cap F:F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\}
, according to the Distributivity Lemma
3.4
. If also
N\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu,\rmloc}
, then it follows that
N\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
. The notion of locally
\mu
-negligible sets will appear again in
5.2
.

Next we introduce some classes of localizable MSNs that will appear
throughout the paper.

4.3
Countable chain condition. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN. A family
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
is called
almost disjointed
whenever
E\cap E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
for any pair of distinct
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
. The MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is said to have the
countable chain condition

(in short: is
ccc
) whenever an almost disjointed family in

\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
is at most countable.

The previous notions have counterparts in the realm of Boolean
algebras. Given a Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{A}
, a subset
\mathfrak{E}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}
is called
disjointed
whenever
x\wedge y=0
for any
pair of distinct elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{E}
. The Boolean algebra

\mathfrak{A}
has the
countable chain condition
(or: is
ccc
)
whenever each of its disjointed families is at most countable. Of
course, an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is ccc if and only if its Boolean
algebra
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
is. In the following proposition, we show that
being ccc is stronger than localizability. It is related to the
fact, first established in
[
18
]
, that a Dedekind

\sigma
-complete Boolean algebra (that is, a Boolean algebra where
countable collections have suprema) having the countable chain
condition is Dedekind complete.

4.4 Proposition
. —

If an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is ccc and
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is a
collection, then there is a countable subcollection
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
such that
\bigcup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum
of
\mathscr{E}
. In particular,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is localizable.

Proof.

Suppose the existence of a collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
for
which one cannot find a countable subcollection
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
whose union is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
\mathscr{E}
. This
assumption allows us to construct transfinitely a sequence
\langle E_{\alpha}\rangle_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
with values in
\mathscr{E}
such that
for every
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, one has
F_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=E_{\alpha}\setminus\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}E_{\beta}\not\in\mathscr{N}
. But the
disjointed family
\{F_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
contradicts the
fact that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is ccc.
∎

4.5 Proposition
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a finite measure space. Then
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is ccc.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
be an almost disjointed
family. For each positive integer
n
, set
\mathscr{E}_{n}=\mathscr{E}\cap\{E:\mu(E)>n^{-1}\}
. As
\mu(X)\geqslant\mu\left(\bigcup\mathscr{E}_{n}\right)\geqslant n^{-1}\rmcard\mathscr{E}_{n}
, we have that
\mathscr{E}_{n}

is finite. Consequently,
\mathscr{E}
is at most countable.
∎

4.6 Proposition
. —

A coproduct
\coprod_{i\in I}(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
of saturated
localizable MSNs is localizable.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be a collection of measurable sets of a coproduct

\coprod_{i\in I}(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
. For each
i\in I
, the
collection
\mathscr{E}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{X_{i}\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
has an

\mathscr{N}_{i}
-essential supremum
S_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
. We then routinely
check that
S\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\coprod_{i\in I}S_{i}\in\mathscr{A}
is an

\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
\mathscr{E}
.
∎

4.7
Stronger notions of localizability. —

An MSN is called
strictly localizable
if it is isomorphic to a
coproduct of the form
\coprod_{i\in I}(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{\mu_{i}})
, where
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mu_{i})
are complete finite
measure spaces. Examples of strictly localizable MSNs are provided
by MSNs associated to complete
\sigma
-finite measure spaces
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
. Indeed, denoting
\langle X_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
a countable
partition of
X
into measurable subsets of finite
\mu
measure,
one can verify that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is isomorphic to

\coprod_{i\in I}(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{X_{i}},\mathscr{N}_{\mu\>{\vrule height=5.0pt,width=0.4pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,X_{i}})
.

Likewise, we say that an MSN is
cccc
whenever it is isomorphic
to a coproduct of saturated ccc MSNs. We have the chain of
implications

\text{strictly localizable}\implies\text{cccc}\implies\text{localizable}.

The first implication comes from Proposition
4.5
, the
second one from Propositions
4.4

and
4.6
. Examples of non localizable spaces are provided
by the next results.

4.8 Lemma
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a localizable MSN,
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
an almost disjointed family. Then
\rmcard(\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N})\geqslant 2^{\rmcard\mathscr{E}}
.

Proof.

Consider the application
\mathscr{P}(\mathscr{E})\to\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
which maps
each subcollection
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
to the equivalence class
of its
\mathscr{N}
essential supremum. We claim that this map is
injective. Indeed, suppose
\mathscr{E}^{\prime},\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
are
distinct. Call
S^{\prime}
(resp.
S^{\prime\prime}
) an
\mathscr{N}
essential supremum of

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
(resp.
\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}
). Without loss of generality, there is
F\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\setminus\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}
. By Lemma
3.4
,
F\cap S^{\prime}
(resp.
F\cap S^{\prime\prime}
) is an essential supremum of
\{F\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\}
(resp.
\{F\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime\prime}\}
). We deduce that
F\setminus S^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
and, taking the
almost disjointed character of
\mathscr{E}
into account, that
F\cap S^{\prime\prime}\in\mathscr{N}
. This
implies that
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
do not induce the same equivalence class
in
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
.
∎

We will use the following many times.

4.9 Lemma
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN and let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
be
\mathscr{N}
-generating. There exists
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
with the following properties.

(A)

\mathscr{E}
is almost disjointed.

(B)

For each
E\in\mathscr{E}
, there exists
C\in\mathscr{C}
such that
E\subseteq C
.

(C)

\mathscr{E}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating.

Proof.

There is no restriction to assume that
\mathscr{N}\neq\mathscr{A}
; in
particular,
\mathscr{C}\neq\emptyset
. Consider the set
\mathbf{E}
consisting
of those
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
that satisfy conditions
(A) and (B) above, ordered by inclusion. Thus,
\mathbf{E}
is nonempty and
one readily checks that every chain in
\mathbf{E}
possesses a maximal
element. Therefore,
\mathbf{E}
admits a maximal element
\mathscr{E}
, according
to Zorn’s Lemma. We ought to show that
\mathscr{E}
is

\mathscr{N}
-generating. If this were not the case, there would exist an

\mathscr{N}
-essential upper bound
U\in\mathscr{A}
of
\mathscr{E}
such that
X\setminus U\not\in\mathscr{N}
. The latter, together with the fact that

\mathscr{C}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating, implies the existence of
C\in\mathscr{C}

such that
C\cap(X\setminus U)\not\in\mathscr{N}
. Then,
\mathscr{E}\cup\{C\cap(X\setminus U)\}
contradicts the maximality of
\mathscr{E}
.
∎

4.10 Proposition

(
\mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{CH}
)
. —

Let
X
be a Polish space endowed with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra

\mathscr{B}(X)
and
\mu\colon\mathscr{B}(X)\to[0,\infty]
be a
semi-finite Borel measure. Under the Continuum Hypothesis, one has
the following dichotomy: either
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite, or the
MSN
(X,\mathscr{B}(X),\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is not localizable.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be associated with
\mathscr{C}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{B}(X)\cap\{A:\mu(A)<\infty\}
in Lemma
4.9
. Recall
4.2

that
\mathscr{C}
is
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating. If
\mathscr{E}
is countable,
then
\bigcup\mathscr{E}
is measurable and, accordingly, an

\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-essential upper bound of
\mathscr{E}
. Thus
X\setminus\bigcup\mathscr{E}\in\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
, since
\mathscr{E}
is
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating. We
have proven that
\mu
is
\sigma
-finite.

On the other hand, if
\mathscr{E}
is uncountable, the Continuum
Hypothesis guarantees that it has cardinal greater or equal to

\mathfrak{c}
. Assume if possible that
(X,\mathscr{B}(X),\mu)
is
localizable. As the map
\mathscr{B}(X)\to\mathscr{B}(X)/\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
is
onto, we deduce from Lemma
4.8
that
\rmcard\mathscr{B}(X)\geqslant 2^{\mathfrak{c}}>\mathfrak{c}
. However, Borel sets are Suslin,
and Suslin sets are continuous images of closed subsets of a
particular Polish space, the Baire space, see e.g

[
17
, 3.3.18]
. This gives the upper bound
\rmcard\mathscr{B}(X)\leqslant\mathfrak{c}
, contradicting the preceding inequality.
∎

4.11
\mathscr{P}
-version of an MSN. —

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a property associated to MSNs. We suppose that the
property
\mathscr{P}
is
hereditary
: if
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has

\mathscr{P}
then the MSNs
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
also has
\mathscr{P}
for
all
Z\in\mathscr{A}
. “Being strictly localizable”, “being cccc” or
“being localizable” are examples of hereditary properties.

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a saturated MSN. We define a
\mathscr{P}
-version
of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
to be a couple
[(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}),\mathbf{p}]
consisting of a saturated MSN

(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
with the property
\mathscr{P}
and
a supremum preserving morphism
\mathbf{p}\colon(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
satisfying the following
property: For any saturated MSN
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
with the
property
\mathscr{P}
and any supremum preserving morphism
\mathbf{q}\colon(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
, there is a unique supremum
preserving morphism
\mathbf{r}\colon(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\to(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
such that
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{p}\circ\mathbf{r}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!\mathbf{r}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{p}}

By this definition, a
\mathscr{P}
-version must satisfy a universal
property, and as such it is unique up to a unique isomorphism of the
category
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
. More specifically, if
[(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}),\mathbf{p}]
and
[(\hat{X}^{\prime},\hat{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\hat{\mathscr{N}}^{\prime}),\mathbf{p}^{\prime}]
are two
\mathscr{P}
-versions, then we easily check that there is
a unique isomorphism
\mathbf{r}\colon(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})\to(\hat{X}^{\prime},\hat{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\hat{\mathscr{N}}^{\prime})
such that
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\circ\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{p}
.

4.12
Atomic MSNs. —

One of our motivations in this article is to find a universal
construction that transforms an MSN into something with better
localizability properties. As such, it is wise to first have a look
at the not so easy case of MSNs
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
such that all
singletons are
\mathscr{A}
-measurable and
\mathscr{N}=\{\emptyset\}
. We
call such MSNs
atomic
.

In an atomic MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
, it is easy to see that
a subset
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
has an
\{\emptyset\}
-essential
supremum if and only if
\bigcup\mathscr{E}\in\mathscr{A}
, in which case

\bigcup\mathscr{E}
is the
\{\emptyset\}
-essential supremum. Therefore
the MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
is localizable if and only if

\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{P}(X)
. In other words, the non localizability of
(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
can only be due to the lack of measurable
sets; therefore it seems sensible to ask for
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\{\emptyset\})
to be the “localization” of
(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
.

Unfortunately, Proposition
4.13
gives a negative
result. It tells us that the localizable version of an MSN, as
defined in
4.11
, is not the right notion of
“localization”. This issue will be addressed in
Section
5
by introducing a notion of local determination
for MSNs.

4.13 Proposition
. —

Let
X
be an uncountable set,
\mathscr{C}(X)
be its
countable-cocountable
\sigma
-algebra. Let
\boldsymbol{\iota}
be
the morphism
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\{\emptyset\})\to(X,\mathscr{C}(X),\{\emptyset\})
induced by the identity map. Then
[(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\{\emptyset\}),\boldsymbol{\iota}]
is not a localizable version of

(X,\mathscr{C}(X),\{\emptyset\})
.

Proof.

That
\boldsymbol{\iota}
is supremum preserving follows from the
discussion in Paragraph
4.12
. Assume if possible that
((X,\mathscr{P}(X),\{\emptyset\}),\boldsymbol{\iota})
is a localizable
version of
(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
. We will get a contradiction
if we manage to build a localizable saturated MSN
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
and a function
q\colon Y\to X
that is
[(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M}),(\mathscr{C}(X),\{\emptyset\})]
-measurable, supremum preserving, but
not
(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{P}(X))
-measurable.

We choose
Y=X^{2}\times\{0,1\}
. For any subset
B\subseteq Y
,
we call
B[0]
and
B[1]
the subsets defined by

B[i]\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=X^{2}\cap\{(x_{1},x_{2}):(x_{1},x_{2},i)\in B\}\quad\text{for }i\in\{0,1\}

We let
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{P}(Y)\cap\{B:B[0]\ominus B[1]\text{ is
countable}\}
. We claim that
\mathscr{B}
is a
\sigma
-algebra of

Y
. The stability of
\mathscr{B}
under countable unions is a
consequence of the formula

\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}\right)[0]\ominus\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}\right)[1]\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}[0]\ominus B_{n}[1]

that holds for any sequence
\langle B_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of subsets in
Y
,
and
we leave the other points to the reader. Finally, we define the

\sigma
-ideal
\mathscr{M}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{B}\cap\{M:M[0]=\emptyset\}
. Clearly
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is a saturated MSN.

Let us show that
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is localizable. Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
be any collection. We set
A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcup_{E\in\mathscr{E}}E[0]
and
S\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\times\{0,1\}
. The set
S
is

\mathscr{B}
-measurable, because
S[0]=S[1]=A
. For any
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we have
(E\setminus S)[0]=E[0]\setminus S[0]=\emptyset
, meaning that
S
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential upper bound of

\mathscr{E}
. Denoting by
U
another essential upper bound of
\mathscr{E}
,
then
(E\setminus U)[0]=E[0]\setminus U[0]=\emptyset
for all

E\in\mathscr{E}
. It follows that
A\subseteq U[0]
and
(S\setminus U)[0]=S[0]\setminus U[0]=\emptyset
. Thus,
S
is an

\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum, as we wanted.

Now, let
\sigma\colon X\to X
be a bijection of
X
without
fixed points. For example, choose a partition
X=Z\cup Z^{\prime}
into
subsets
Z,Z^{\prime}
that have the same cardinality as
X
, choose a
bijection
f\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
and set
\sigma
so that
\sigma(x)=f(x)
for all
x\in Z
and
\sigma(x)=f^{-1}(x)
for all
x\in Z^{\prime}
. We define the map
q\colon Y\to X
by

\forall(x_{1},x_{2},i)\in Y,\quad q(x_{1},x_{2},i)=\begin{cases}x_{2}&\text{if }i=1\text{ and }x_{2}=\sigma(x_{1})\\
x_{1}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

First we show that
q
is
[(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M}),(\mathscr{C}(X),\{\emptyset\})]
-measurable. It suffices to show that

q^{-1}(\{x\})\in\mathscr{B}
for all
x\in X
. But we have

\displaystyle q^{-1}(\{x\})[0]

\displaystyle=\{x\}\times X

\displaystyle q^{-1}(\{x\})[1]

\displaystyle=\big(\{x\}\times\left(X\setminus\{\sigma(x)\}\right)\big)\cup\{(\sigma^{-1}(x)\,,x)\}

Consequently,
q^{-1}(\{x\})[0]\ominus q^{-1}(\{x\})[1]
has only
two elements. By the definition of
\mathscr{B}
, this ensures the
measurability of
q^{-1}(\{x\})
.

However, we claim that
q
is not
(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{P}(X))
-measurable. To
this end, we will show that
q^{-1}(Z)\not\in\mathscr{B}
. We have

\displaystyle q^{-1}(Z)[0]

\displaystyle=Z\times X

\displaystyle q^{-1}(Z)[1]

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}\in Z,x_{2}\neq\sigma(x_{1})\}\cup\{(\sigma^{-1}(x),x):x\in Z\}

It follows that
q^{-1}(Z)[0]\ominus q^{-1}(Z)[1]=\{(x,\sigma(x)):x\in Z\}\cup\{(\sigma^{-1}(x),x):x\in Z\}
is
uncountable. Thus,
q^{-1}(Z)\not\in\mathscr{B}
.

It only remains to prove that
q
is supremum preserving. Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{C}(X)
be a collection that has an

\{\emptyset\}
-essential supremum
S
. This implies that
S=\bigcup\mathscr{E}
. We wish to prove that
q^{-1}(S)
is an

\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum of
q^{-1}(\mathscr{E})=\{q^{-1}\{x\}:x\in S\}
. First suppose that
\mathscr{E}
consists
only of singletons. Of course,
q^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential
upper bound of
q^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. Let
U
an arbitrary
\mathscr{M}
-essential upper bound of

q^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. For all
x\in S
, we have

q^{-1}\{x\}\setminus U\in\mathscr{M}
, meaning that
\{x\}\times X=(q^{-1}\{x\})[0]\subseteq U[0]
. Thus
S\times X\subseteq U[0]
,
which implies
(q^{-1}(S)\setminus U)[0]=q^{-1}(S)[0]\setminus U[0]=S\times X\setminus U[0]=\emptyset
. It means that

q^{-1}(S)\setminus U\in\mathscr{M}
. Thus, we have shown that

q^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum of
\mathscr{E}
.

Now we turn to the general case, where
\mathscr{E}
need not consist only
of singletons. Let
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}=\{\{x\}:x\in E\in\mathscr{E}\}
. Clearly,

and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
have the same

-essential supremum

. By what precedes,
q^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential

\mathscr{M}
-essential upper

-essential upper bound

of

, as any

is a subset of a member of

q^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
. Therefore,
q^{-1}(S)\setminus U\in\mathscr{M}

showing that
q^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum of

.
∎

4.14
Example of an MSN with no localizable part. —

Consider an MSN of the form
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{K}(X))
, where
X
is
a set of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
and
\mathscr{K}(X)
is the

\sigma
-ideal of countable subsets. There is a bijection
\varphi\colon X\to X\times X
and we can use it to construct an
uncountable family of “horizontal lines”
H_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\varphi^{-1}(X\times\{x\})
indexed by
x\in X
witnessing that

(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{K}(X))
is not ccc. Actually, we can do better
and prove that it is not localizable. Suppose
\{H_{x}:x\in X\}

has a
\mathscr{K}(X)
-essential supremum
S
. For each
x\in X
choose
a point
p_{x}\in S\cap H_{x}
. Then it is easy to see that
U\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=S\setminus\{p_{x}:x\in X\}
is an essential upper bound for the
family of horizontal lines, however
S\setminus U=\{p_{x}:x\in X\}
is not negligible, contradicting that
S
is an essential
supremum.

Observe that the MSN
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{K}(X))
is isomorphic to all
its non negligible subMSNs. In particular, it has no nontrivial ccc
or localizable part, an unpleasant situation that we will rule out
in the next paragraph by introducing the notions of “locally
localizable” and “locally ccc” MSN.

We will prove nonetheless that
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{K}(X))
has a cccc
version, that is disappointingly the trivial MSN
(\emptyset,\{\emptyset\},\{\emptyset\})
(with the only morphism from there
to
(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{K}(X))
). To establish this fact, one needs to
prove that if
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is a cccc MSN and
\mathbf{f}\colon(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\to(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\mathscr{K}(X))
is a supremum
preserving morphism, then
Y\in\mathscr{M}
(actually, the supremum
preserving character of
\mathbf{f}
will not be used). We can
reduce to the case where
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is ccc.

We reproduce an argument due to Ulam
[
19
]
, showing that
there is a family
\langle A_{n,\alpha}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N},\alpha<\omega_{1}}
of subsets of
X
such that

•

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the family
\langle A_{n,\alpha}\rangle_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
is disjointed;

•

for all
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, the union
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n,\alpha}
is conegligible (that is, cocountable).

Any ordinal
\beta<\omega_{1}
is countable, so we can select a
sequence
\langle k_{\alpha,\beta}\rangle_{\alpha<\beta}
of distinct
integers. Let
\langle x_{\beta}\rangle_{\beta<\omega_{1}}
be an enumeration
of all the elements in
X
. Set
A_{n,\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{x_{\beta}:\beta>\alpha\text{ and }k_{\alpha,\beta}=n\}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha<\omega_{1}
. For distinct
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}<\omega_{1}
, there cannot be some
x_{\beta}\in A_{n,\alpha}\cap A_{n,\alpha^{\prime}}
, for otherwise we would have
k_{\alpha,\beta}=k_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}
. In addition, one has
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n,\alpha}=\{x_{\beta}:\beta>\alpha\}
whose complement in
X
is
the countable set
\{x_{\beta}:\beta\leqslant\alpha\}
.

Now, fix a representative
f\in\mathbf{f}
. The family
\langle f^{-1}(A_{n,\alpha})\rangle_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}
being disjointed,
the set
C_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\omega_{1}\cap\{\alpha:f^{-1}(A_{n,\alpha})\not\in\mathscr{M}\}
is countable for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence the
existence of some
\alpha<\omega_{1}\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}C_{n}
. Now we see that
f^{-1}\left(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n,\alpha}\right)
is both negligible and conegligible in
Y
, which can
happen only if
Y\in\mathscr{M}
.

4.15
. —

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a hereditary property associated to MSNs. We say that
an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is
locally
\mathscr{P}
whenever one of
the following equivalent statements holds:

(A)

The collection
\mathscr{A}\cap\{Z:(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})\text{ has the property }\mathscr{P}\}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating;

(B)

for any

there is

and
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})

\mathscr{P}
.

Proof of the equivalence.

(A)
\implies
(B) For
Y\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathscr{N}
, an
application of Lemma

gives that
Y
is an
essential supremum of
\{Y\cap Z:Z\in\mathscr{A}\text{ and }(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})\text{ has the property }\mathscr{P}\}
. Therefore,
there must be some
Z\in\mathscr{A}

and
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
has the property
\mathscr{P}
. The
subset
Y\cap Z\subseteq Y
establishes (B).

(B)
\implies
(A) Clearly,
X
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper

\mathscr{A}\cap\{Z:(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})\text{ has the property }\mathscr{P}\}

S
be another upper

were not negligible, (B) gives the

such
that

and
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})

\mathscr{P}
. But

, which

is an essential upper bound.

For instance, in a semi-finite measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
, any
non negligible set

contains a
measurable subset

of nonzero finite measure. By (B), this
implies that the associated MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is locally
strictly localizable.

We conclude this section with an important property of “local

\mathscr{P}
-versions.

4.16 Proposition
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})

\mathscr{P}

r
has values in

\hat{F}
. Calling its restriction
r^{\prime}\colon Y\to\hat{F}
, we see
that
p_{F}\circ r^{\prime}
and
q
coincide
\mathscr{M}
-almost everywhere. The
induced morphism
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
provides a factorization of
\mathbf{q}
through

(\hat{F},\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{\hat{F}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}_{\hat{F}})
.

To establish the uniqueness of this factorization, we proceed as
follows. For any morphism
\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\mathbf{p}_{F}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{q}
we notice that
\boldsymbol{\iota}_{F}\circ\mathbf{q}=\boldsymbol{\iota}_{F}\circ\mathbf{p}_{F}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{\hat{F}}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
. Since this holds for

\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
we obtain
\mathbf{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{\hat{F}}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime}=\mathbf{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\iota}_{\hat{F}}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
and, by
uniqueness of the factorization relative to the universal property
of
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
,

\boldsymbol{\iota}_{\hat{F}}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\iota}_{\hat{F}}\circ\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}
. Thus,
r^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime\prime}

coincide
\mathscr{M}
-almost everywhere.

(B) If
(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F})
has property
\mathscr{P}
, then obviously

[(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F}),\boldsymbol{\mathrm{id}}]
is a second

\mathscr{P}
-version. From the uniqueness of the
\mathscr{P}
-version, we
obtain a isomorphism
\mathbf{r}\colon(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F})\to(\hat{F},\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{\hat{F}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}_{\hat{F}})
such that

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{id}}=\mathbf{p}_{F}\circ\mathbf{r}
, whence
\mathbf{p}_{F}=\mathbf{r}^{-1}
.
∎

5.
Localizable locally determined MSNs

In order to motivate the main definition in this section, we start with the following result, of which we can think as a way of testing whether an
MSN has a property
\mathscr{P}
. For instance, if each
F\in\mathscr{F}
corresponds to a ccc subMSN, then
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is cccc. The difficulty in applying this proposition stems with both hypotheses: conditions (1) and (2)
will be turned into a definition in
5.2
, whereas condition (3), that
\mathscr{F}
is
disjointed
rather than merely
almost disjointed
, calls for techniques that transform almost disjointed generating families (whose existence, in applications, follows from Lemma
4.9
) into partitions – see the proof of Theorem
7.6
in case
\rmcard\mathscr{E}\leqslant\mathfrak{c}
and the notion of compatible family of densities introduced in Section
10
.

5.1 Proposition
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN and
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
. Assume that

(1)

For every
A\subseteq X
the following holds:

\big[\forall F\in\mathscr{F}:A\cap F\in\mathscr{A}\big]\Rightarrow A\in\mathscr{A};

(2)

For every
N\subseteq X
the following holds:

\big[\forall F\in\mathscr{F}:N\cap F\in\mathscr{N}\big]\Rightarrow N\in\mathscr{N};

(3)

\mathscr{F}
is a partition of
X
.

Then the MSNs
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
\coprod_{F\in\mathscr{F}}(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F})
are isomorphic in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
.

Proof.

We abbreviate
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
for
\coprod_{F\in\mathscr{F}}(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F})
. Since
\mathscr{F}
is a partition of
X
, there is
a canonical bijection
\varphi:X\to Y
. Its inverse
\varphi^{-1}
is

[(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M}),(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})]
-measurable, by definition of
coproduct of MSNs. We now show that
\varphi
is

[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable. Given
B\in\mathscr{B}
, we
note that
\varphi^{-1}(B)=\bigcup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}B\cap F
, whence

\varphi^{-1}(B)\cap F=B\cap F\in\mathscr{A}
for every
F\in\mathscr{F}
,
by definition of
\mathscr{B}
. We infer from hypothesis (1) that

\varphi^{-1}(B)\in\mathscr{A}
. Let
M\in\mathscr{M}
. As above we infer from
the definition of
\mathscr{M}
that
\varphi^{-1}(M)\cap F\in\mathscr{N}
for
every
F\in\mathscr{F}
, whence
\varphi^{-1}(M)\in\mathscr{N}
, in view of
hypothesis (2). In other words,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and

(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
are isomorphic in
\mathsf{MSN}
. The conclusion follows
from Proposition
3.7
(A).
∎

5.2
. —

We borrow the following definition from
[
5
, 211H]
. A measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)

is
locally determined
whenever it is semi-finite and, for every
subset
A\subseteq X
,

\big[\forall E\in\mathscr{A}^{f}:A\cap E\in\mathscr{A}\big]\Rightarrow A\in\mathscr{A},

where, as usual,
\mathscr{A}^{f}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{E:\mu(E)<\infty\}
.

The definition relies on the particular collection
\mathscr{A}^{f}
(which
is
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating, recall
4.2
). This makes sense
because we are dealing with a measure space. It is a rather
good surprise that we can define an analogous notion of locally
determined MSNs, by substituting for
\mathscr{A}^{f}
an arbitrary
generating collection. Namely, a saturated MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})

is called
locally determined
whenever the following holds. For every
\mathscr{N}
-generating collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
and every
A\subseteq X
,

\big[\forall E\in\mathscr{E}:A\cap E\in\mathscr{A}\big]\Rightarrow A\in\mathscr{A}.

An MSN that
is both localizable and locally determined is called
lld
.

The following is useful as well. We say that a saturated MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has
locally determined negligible sets
whenever the following holds. For every
\mathscr{N}
-generating collection
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
and every
N\subseteq X
,

\big[\forall E\in\mathscr{E}:N\cap E\in\mathscr{N}\big]\Rightarrow N\in\mathscr{N}.

We observe that if
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is locally determined, then it has
locally determined negligible sets. Indeed, let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
and

N\subseteq X
be as above, we first infer from the local determinacy of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
that
N\in\mathscr{A}
and, in turn from the Distributivity Lemma
3.4
, that
N
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
\{N\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
. Therefore,
N\in\mathscr{N}
.

Next we prove some elementary properties concerning locally
determined MSNs. In particular, the consistency between both notions
of local determination (for complete semi-finite measure spaces and MSNs) is established in
Proposition
5.3
(F). Here, the semi-finiteness property of a measure space is critical as the following example shows. We consider
\mathscr{H}^{1}
, the 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathscr{A}
the
\sigma
-algebra consisting of
\mathscr{H}^{1}
-measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the sense of Carathéodory. The following hold:

(a)

\forall A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}:\big[\forall F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}:A\cap F\in\mathscr{A}\big]\Rightarrow A\in\mathscr{A}
;

(b)

The measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{H}^{1})
is not semi-finite;

(c)

The (saturated) MSN
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{H}^{1}})
does not have locally determined negligible sets and, in particular, is not locally determined.

For (a), see for instance
[
3
, 6.2]
. For (b), see
[
8
, 439H]
. Now (c) follows for example from
[
3
, 4.4]
. It follows from
4.2
that
\mathscr{A}^{f}
is not
\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{H}^{1}}
-generating.

5.3 Proposition
. —

The following hold.

(A)

Being locally determined is a hereditary property.

(B)

Being locally determined is a property invariant under
isomorphisms in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
.

(C)

A saturated ccc MSN is locally determined.

(D)

A coproduct of locally determined MSNs is locally determined.

(E)

A cccc MSN is locally determined.

(F)

A complete semi-finite measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is
locally determined (as a measure space) if and only if the MSN

(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is locally determined.

Proof.

(A) Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a locally determined MSN and
Z\in\mathscr{A}
. Let
\mathscr{E}
be an
\mathscr{N}_{Z}
-generating family in the subMSN

(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
and
A\subseteq Z
be such that
E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}_{Z}
for any
E\in\mathscr{E}
. The family
\mathscr{E}\cup\{X\setminus Z\}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating in
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}
for all
E\in\mathscr{E}\cup\{X\setminus Z\}
. It follows that

A\in\mathscr{A}
.

(B) Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
be two
saturated MSNs,
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
be two
measurable supremum preserving maps that induce reciprocal
isomorphisms. Assume that
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is locally
determined. Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
be an
\mathscr{M}
-generating
collection and
B\subseteq Y
be such that
E\cap B\in\mathscr{B}
for all

E\in\mathscr{E}
. Then
f^{-1}(E)\cap f^{-1}(B)=f^{-1}(E\cap B)\in\mathscr{A}
. As
f
is supremum preserving,
f^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
is

\mathscr{N}
-generating. And as
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is locally
determined, we infer that
f^{-1}(B)\in\mathscr{A}
. Therefore

g^{-1}(f^{-1}(B))\in\mathscr{B}
. But
B\ominus g^{-1}(f^{-1}(B))\in\mathscr{M}
and as
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is saturated we conclude that
B\in\mathscr{B}
.

(C) Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a saturated MSN,
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
an
\mathscr{N}
-generating family and
A\in\mathscr{P}(X)
be such
that
E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}
for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
. By
Proposition
4.4
, there is a countable subset
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
that is
\mathscr{N}
-generating. Then

A=\left(\bigcup_{E\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}E\cap A\right)\cup\left(A\setminus\bigcup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\right)

Since
A\setminus\bigcup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is
\mathscr{N}
-negligible and
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is saturated, we infer that
A\setminus\bigcup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is

\mathscr{A}
-measurable. Therefore,
A\in\mathscr{A}
.

(D) Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be the coproduct of a family
\langle(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})\rangle_{i\in I}
of locally determined MSNs. It is readily saturated. Let

\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
be an
\mathscr{N}
-generating family and
A\subseteq X
such that
E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}
for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
. For all
i\in I
, the family
\mathscr{E}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{E\cap X_{i}:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
is

\mathscr{N}_{i}
-generating in
(X_{i},\mathscr{A}_{i},\mathscr{N}_{i})
by
Lemma
3.4
. This observation leads to the fact that
A\cap X_{i}\in\mathscr{A}_{i}
for all
i\in I
, in other words,
A\in\mathscr{A}
.

(E) This obviously follows from (C) and (D).

(F) Suppose that the measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is locally
determined. Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
be an
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating
family and
A\subseteq X
be such that
E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}
for all

E\in\mathscr{E}
. Let
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
. By Lemma
3.4
, the
collection
\{F\cap E:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
is
\mathscr{N}_{F}
-generating in

(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},(\mathscr{N}_{\mu})_{F})
and of course
F\cap E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}_{F}
for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
. On top of that,
(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},(\mathscr{N}_{\mu})_{F})
is a ccc MSN by Proposition
4.5
and it is saturated. We
get from (B) above that
A\cap F
is measurable. As this happens
for all
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
, we conclude that
A\in\mathscr{A}
.

Conversely, suppose the MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}_{\mu})
is locally
determined. Owing to the semi-finiteness of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
, the
collection
\mathscr{A}^{f}
is
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating. Then
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
is easily seen to be locally determined: if
A\in\mathscr{P}(X)

satisfies
A\cap F\in\mathscr{A}
for all
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
, then
A\in\mathscr{A}
.
∎

5.4
lld version of an atomic MSN. —

As a first result, we mention that the lld version of an atomic MSN

(X,\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})
is
[(X,\mathscr{P}(X),\{\emptyset\}),\boldsymbol{\iota}]
, where
\boldsymbol{\iota}
is the morphism
induced by the identity map (that
\boldsymbol{\iota}
is
supremum preserving follows from
4.12
). This amounts to
prove that, for any lld MSN
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
, a
[(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M}),(\mathscr{A},\{\emptyset\})]
-measurable supremum preserving map
q\colon Y\to X
is automatically
(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{P}(X))
-measurable. Indeed, let
S\in\mathscr{P}(X)
. Then
q^{-1}(S)\cap q^{-1}\{x\}
is either
q^{-1}\{x\}
or
\emptyset
, hence

q^{-1}(S)\cap q^{-1}\{x\}
is
\mathscr{B}
-measurable for every
x\in X
.
Besides,
q

is supremum preserving, thus the collection
\{q^{-1}\{x\}:x\in X\}
is
\mathscr{M}
-generating. By local determination in
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
we conclude that
q^{-1}(S)\in\mathscr{B}
.

5.5
. —

Call
\mathsf{LLD_{sp}}
the full subcategory of
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
that
consists of lld MSNs, and consider the forgetful functor

\mathsf{Forget}\colon\mathsf{LLD_{sp}}\to\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
. In
categorical terms, an lld version is the coreflection of an MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
along the functor
\mathsf{Forget}
, see

[
1
, Chapter 3]
. In this paper, we do not answer the question whether there exists an lld version for
each saturated MSN. This is equivalent
to the existence of a right adjoint
\mathsf{R}
of

\mathsf{Forget}
. As a matter of fact, if such an adjoint exists,
there would be a natural transformation
\varepsilon\colon\mathsf{Forget}\circ\mathsf{R}\implies\rmid_{\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}}
such
that the pair
[\mathsf{R}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),\varepsilon_{(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})}]
gives the lld version of any saturated MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
.

In search for an abstract proof of the existence of
\mathsf{R}
,
one might think of using Freyd’s Adjunction Theorem,

[
1
, Theorem 3.3.3]
. Following this path, one needs to
establish (setting aside the solution set condition) that:

(A)

The category
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
is cocomplete;

(B)

The forgetful functor
\mathsf{Forget}
preserves small
colimits.

Assertion (A) boils down to showing that
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
has two types of
small colimits: coproducts and coequalizers. The existence of the
former is shown in Proposition
3.7
(D). We do not know
whether coequalizers exist in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
and it is the main
difficulty here.

As for (B), which, regarding the existence of lld versions, is a
necessary condition even if (A) were to be false, we have already
proven in Propositions
4.6
and
5.3
(D) that
coproducts of lld MSNs are lld. Our next goal is
Proposition
5.7
which states that coequalizers of lld MSNs
are lld. Before that, we need to introduce some notation and a
lemma.

For a saturated MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and an arbitrary
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{A:E\cap A\in\mathscr{A}\text{
for all }E\in\mathscr{E}\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{P}(X)\cap\{N:E\cap N\in\mathscr{N}\text{ for all }E\in\mathscr{E}\}.

It is clear that
(X,\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}})
is a saturated
MSN.

5.6 Lemma
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a localizable saturated MSN and
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
an
\mathscr{N}
-generating family. Let

\boldsymbol{\iota}
be the morphism
(X,\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}})\to(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
induced by the identity map on
X
. Then

\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})
is an isomorphism. In
particular,
\boldsymbol{\iota}
is supremum preserving and
(X,\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}})
is localizable.

Proof.

First we make the following observation, to be used later in the
proof:
\mathscr{A}\cap\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}=\mathscr{N}
. Indeed, if
N\in\mathscr{A}
is
such that
E\cap N\in\mathscr{N}
for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
, then by the
distributivity lemma
3.4
, we conclude that
N\in\mathscr{N}
. This proves the inclusion
\mathscr{A}\cap\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}\subseteq\mathscr{N}
, the reciprocal being trivial.

The identity map
\iota\colon X\to X
is
[(\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}),(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})]
-measurable because
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
and
\mathscr{N}\subseteq\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
. Let us show that

\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})\colon\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}\to\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}}/\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
is injective by inspecting its
kernel. Let
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
be a class
represented by
A\in\mathscr{A}
such that

\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})(\boldsymbol{A})=0
, in other
words
A=\iota^{-1}(A)\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
. Then
A\in\mathscr{A}\cap\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}=\mathscr{N}
. This means that
\boldsymbol{A}=0
in
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
. Therefore,
\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})
is
injective.

Now let us show that
\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})
is surjective.
To
this end, let
\boldsymbol{H}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}}/\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
be a
class represented by
H\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathscr{E}}
. We ought to prove
that
\boldsymbol{H}
is in the range of

\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})
. Set
\mathscr{F}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{E\cap H:E\in\mathscr{E}\}
. Note that
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
. The localizability
of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
guarantees that
\mathscr{F}
has an

\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum
S\in\mathscr{A}
. In particular,
E\cap H\setminus S\in\mathscr{N}
for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
, meaning that
H\setminus S\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
.

We also claim that
S\setminus H\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
. Indeed, let
E_{0}\in\mathscr{E}
. Set
S^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=S\setminus(E_{0}\cap S\setminus H)
. We
note that
S^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
. For all
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we have
E\cap H\setminus S^{\prime}=E\cap H\setminus S\in\mathscr{N}
, as
H\setminus S\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
. This means that
S^{\prime}
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential upper
bound of
\mathscr{F}
. It follows that
S\setminus S^{\prime}=E_{0}\cap H\setminus S\in\mathscr{N}
. As
E_{0}\in\mathscr{E}
is arbitrary, we obtain
S\setminus H\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
, as required.

We proved that
H\ominus S\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{E}}
. Calling

\boldsymbol{S}
the equivalence class of
S
in
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{N}
, we
have that
\mathsf{Bool}(\boldsymbol{\iota})(\boldsymbol{S})=\boldsymbol{H}
.
∎

5.7 Proposition
. —

Consider the following diagram in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}
, where
((Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P}),\mathbf{h})
is the coequalizer of
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{h}}

(A)

If
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is
localizable, so is
(Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})
.

(B)

If
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
is lld,
so is
(Z,\mathscr{C},\mathscr{P})
.

Proof.

(A) Let us call
\boldsymbol{2}
the special MSN
(\{0,1\},\mathscr{P}(\{0,1\}),\{\emptyset\})
. First we show the following
intermediate result: for any MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
, there is a
one-to-one correspondence
\Upsilon_{X}
between the Boolean algebra

\mathsf{Bool}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
and the set of morphisms
\rmHom((X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),\boldsymbol{2})
(those are automatically supremum
preserving since the Boolean algebra of
\boldsymbol{2}
is
finite). Given a class
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathsf{Bool}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
, represented by a set
A
, the characteristic function

\mathbbm{1}_{A}\colon X\to\{0,1\}
induces a morphism
\boldsymbol{1_{A}}

which only depends on the equivalence class

\boldsymbol{A}
. Indeed, if
A^{\prime}
is another representative of

\boldsymbol{A}
, then
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{A^{\prime}}
coincide

\mathscr{N}
-almost everywhere. We set
\Upsilon_{X}(\boldsymbol{A})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\boldsymbol{1_{A}}
.

This map is surjective because each morphism
\boldsymbol{\varphi}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to\boldsymbol{2}
is represented by a map

\varphi\in\boldsymbol{\varphi}
which has the form
\varphi=\mathbbm{1}_{\varphi^{-1}(\{1\})}
. It is injective because if
\mathbbm{1}_{A}

coincides with
\mathbbm{1}_{B}
almost everywhere, for measurable sets
A

and
B
, then
A
and
B
yield the same equivalence class in

\mathsf{Bool}(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})

5.10 Corollary
. —

\mathsf{LLD_{sp}}
has equalizers preserved by the forgetful

.

6.
Gluing measurable functions

6.1
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be an MSN and
(Y,\mathscr{B})
a measurable
space. Let
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
be a collection. A
family
subordinated to
\mathscr{E}
is a family of functions
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}
such that:

(1)

f_{E}\colon E\to Y
is
(\mathscr{A}_{E},\mathscr{B})
-measurable
for every
E\in\mathscr{E}
.

We further say that
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}
is
compatible
whenever

(2)

for all pairs
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
one has
E\cap E^{\prime}\cap\{f_{E}\neq f_{E^{\prime}}\}\in\mathscr{N}
.

A
gluing
of a compatible family
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}

subordinated to
\mathscr{E}
is a function
f\colon X\to Y
such that

(3)

f
is
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})
-measurable;

(4)

E\cap\{f\neq f_{E}\}\in\mathscr{N}
for every
E\in\mathscr{E}
.

In this section, we will be mainly concerned about the existence of
gluings, as they will be of use in the construction of the cccc
version of a locally ccc MSN in Section
7
. This turns
out to depend both on the domain and the target space. In case where

(Y,\mathscr{B})
is the real line equipped with its Borel

\sigma
-algebra
(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}))
, we can glue measurable functions
together if
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is localizable. In fact, this
important property is a characterization of localizability. The
interested reader may find a proof of this classical result
expressed in the language of MSNs in
[
3
, Proposition
3.13]
. Only the measurable structure of
(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}))
is involved, thus, the result holds in the more general case where

(Y,\mathscr{B})
is a standard Borel space, see
[
17
, Chapter 3]
.

Many questions arise when we remove the condition that
(Y,\mathscr{B})

is a standard Borel space. In this case, we need some additional
assumptions on
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
. We will focus on two cases:
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is cccc or lld. But first, we prove that a gluing
inherits some of the properties of the functions
f_{E}
.

6.2 Lemma
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a saturated MSN,
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})

an MSN, and
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
an
\mathscr{N}
-generating collection. We let
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}
be
a compatible family of functions subordinated to
\mathscr{E}
and we
assume that:

(1)

for every
E\in\mathscr{E}
, the map
f_{E}
is
[(\mathscr{A}_{E},\mathscr{N}_{E}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable;

(2)

the family
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}
has a gluing
f
.

Then

(A)

the gluing
f
is

[(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N}),(\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})]
-measurable;

(B)

if
f_{E}
is supremum preserving, for every
E\in\mathscr{E}
,
then so is
f
.

Proof.

We start with the following easy observation. For each
E\in\mathscr{E}
and
B\in\mathscr{B}
one has
f_{E}^{-1}(B)\ominus(E\cap f^{-1}(B))\subseteq E\cap\{f_{E}\neq f\}\in\mathscr{N}
.

(A) As the gluing
f
is
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B})
-measurable by definition,
we need only show that
f^{-1}(M)\in\mathscr{N}
for
M\in\mathscr{M}
. Since
f_{E}^{-1}(M)
is
\mathscr{N}
-negligible, the above observation applied with
B=M
ensures that
E\cap f^{-1}(M)\in\mathscr{N}
for any
E\in\mathscr{E}
. We next use Lemma
3.4
to assert that

f^{-1}(M)
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
\{E\cap f^{-1}(M):E\in\mathscr{E}\}
. This forces
f^{-1}(M)
to be
\mathscr{N}
-negligible.

(B) Let
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
be a collection that admits an

\mathscr{M}
-essential supremum
S
. Since
f_{E}
is supremum preserving
for every
E\in\mathscr{E}
,
f_{E}^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum

of
\{f_{E}^{-1}(F):F\in\mathscr{F}\}
and it ensues from the observation above, applied with
B\in\{S\}\cup\mathscr{F}
, that
E\cap f^{-1}(S)
is an
\mathscr{N}
-essential supremum of
\{E\cap f^{-1}(F):F\in\mathscr{F}\}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\quad\quad f^{-1}(S)&=\mathscr{N}\text{-}\rmesssup_{E\in\mathscr{E}}E\cap f^{-1}(S)\intertext{(by Lemma \ref{supDistrib})}&=\mathscr{N}\text{-}\rmesssup_{E\in\mathscr{E}}\left(\mathscr{N}\text{-}\rmesssup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}E\cap f^{-1}(F)\right)\intertext{(from what precedes)}&=\mathscr{N}\text{-}\rmesssup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}\left(\mathscr{N}\text{-}\rmesssup_{E\in\mathscr{E}}E\cap f^{-1}(F)\right)\\
&=\mathscr{N}\text{-}\rmesssup_{F\in\mathscr{F}}f^{-1}(F)\intertext{(by Lemma \ref{supDistrib}).}\end{split}

∎

. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a locally determined MSN and
(Y,\mathscr{B})

\mathscr{N}
-generating collection. If a compatible family
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}

Let

be two gluings of
\langle f_{E}\rangle_{E\in\mathscr{E}}

. This happens for any

in the

\mathscr{N}
-generating set
\mathscr{E}
. By local determination and the

3.4
,

.

7.5
. —

It is worth noticing that all the arguments contained in
Theorem
7.1
and Corollary
7.4
remain valid
provided we replace “ccc” by “strictly localizable”, “locally
ccc” by “locally strictly localizable”, and “cccc” by “strictly
localizable”. Summing up, a saturated locally strictly localizable
MSN
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has a strictly localizable version, which is
constructed as the coproduct of subMSNs whose underlying sets
belongs to a family
\mathscr{E}
that satisfies hypotheses (2), (3) of
Theorem
7.1
and

(1’)

(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
is strictly localizable for
every
Z\in\mathscr{C}
.

Since (1’) implies (1) we can apply Theorem
7.1
again to
conclude that the cccc and strictly localizable versions of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
are the same.

As for the existence of lld versions, we have a partial result,
which applies for most locally ccc MSNs that one is likely to
encounter in analysis.

7.6 Theorem
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a saturated MSN with a collection
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
such that

(1)

(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
is ccc for all
Z\in\mathscr{C}
;

(2)

\mathscr{C}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating;

(3)

\rmcard\mathscr{C}\leqslant\mathfrak{c}
.

The following hold.

(A)

If
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has an lld version, then it
coincides with the cccc version.

(B)

If moreover
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z})
is countably separated for all

Z\in\mathscr{C}
, then the lld version exists.

Proof.

(A) Recall
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has a cccc version, according to Corollary

7.4
. Suppose
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has an lld version
[(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}),\mathbf{p}]
. In view of Proposition
5.3
(E), conclusion (A) will be established if we prove that
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
is cccc. To this end, we need to find a
suitable decomposition in
\hat{X}
. Apply Lemma
7.3
to
get an almost disjointed
\mathscr{N}
-generating family
\mathscr{E}
such that

\rmcard\mathscr{E}\leqslant\mathfrak{c}
and
(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
is ccc
for all
Z\in\mathscr{E}
. Choose an injection
c:\mathscr{E}\to\mathbb{R}:Z\mapsto c_{Z}
and
p\in\mathbf{p}
. Let
f_{Z}\colon p^{-1}(Z)\to\mathbb{R}
be the constant map equal to
c_{Z}
; it is readily
(\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{p^{-1}(Z)},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable. The family
\langle f_{Z}\rangle_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}
is obviously compatible, since
\mathscr{E}
is almost disjointed. As
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
is localizable, this family has an
(\hat{\mathscr{A}},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable gluing
f\colon\hat{X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

We now show that
\langle f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}\rangle_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}
is a
partition of
\hat{X}
into ccc measurable pieces. Since
c
is injective, the family
\langle f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}\rangle_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}
is, indeed, a partition of
\hat{X}
and, since
f
is
(\hat{\mathscr{A}},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable,

f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
for all

. As

is a gluing
of

have

for all

. Moreover, since
c
is injective, for
all

distinct from
Z

p^{-1}(Z^{\prime})\cap(f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}\setminus p^{-1}(Z))\subseteq p^{-1}(Z^{\prime})\cap\{f\neq f_{Z^{\prime}}\}\in\hat{\mathscr{N}}
. Also,
p^{-1}(Z)\cap(f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}\setminus p^{-1}(Z))=\emptyset
is clearly negligible. Recalling that
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
is saturated and that
p^{-1}(\mathscr{E})
is
\hat{\mathscr{N}}
-generating (because
\mathscr{E}
is
\mathscr{N}
-generating and

is supremum preserving), one infers from the

that

. Thus
p^{-1}(Z)\ominus f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}\in\hat{\mathscr{N}}
. As
\mathbf{p}
is a local isomorphism, according to Propositions
4.16
(B) and
5.3
(A),
(p^{-1}(Z),\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{p^{-1}(Z)},\hat{\mathscr{N}}_{p^{-1}(Z)})

precedes, so is

(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}(f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\},\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}_{f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}})

is cccc, by definition. It remains to establish that
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
and
(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
are isomorphic in
\mathsf{MSN_{sp}}

5.1

\mathscr{F}=\{f^{-1}\{c_{Z}\}:Z\in\mathscr{E}\}
. Recalling that

is
\hat{\mathscr{N}}
-generating, it ensues from the preceding paragraph that so is
\mathscr{F}
. Since
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
is locally determined (whence, has locally determined negligible sets, recall

),

(B) Apply Lemma
7.3
to
\mathscr{C}
and let

\mathscr{E}
be the family thus obtained. By Theorem

, the MSN
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})

constitute the cccc version of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
. Furthermore,
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}})
is countably separated, by Proposition
6.8
. In

be the inclusion map and

for all

. The family
\langle q_{Z}\rangle_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}
subordinated to

8.
Strictly localizable version of a measure space

8.1 Lemma
. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a measure space and
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}

an
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
-generating collection that is closed under finite
union. Then, for every
A\in\mathscr{A}
, we have
\mu(A)=\sup\left\{\mu(A\cap Z):Z\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
.

Proof.

If
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup\{\mu(A\cap Z):Z\in\mathscr{E}\}
is
infinite, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, select an increasing
sequence
\langle Z_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{n}\mu(A\cap Z_{n})=\alpha
. Set
A^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\cap\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Z_{n}
. Suppose
that
\mu((A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap Z)>0
for some
Z\in\mathscr{E}
. Then

\alpha\geqslant\mu(A\cap(Z_{n}\cup Z))\geqslant\mu(A\cap Z_{n})+\mu((A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap Z)

Letting
n\to\infty
gives a contradiction. So we conclude that

(A\setminus A^{\prime})\cap Z
is negligible for all
Z\in\mathscr{E}
. With
the help of Lemma
3.4
, we obtain that
\mu(A\setminus A^{\prime})=0
. Consequently,
\mu(A)=\mu(A^{\prime})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(A\cap Z_{n})=\alpha
.
∎

8.2
Pushforward of a measure by a morphism. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
be a saturated MSN. A measure
\mu\colon\mathscr{A}\to[0,\infty]
is
absolutely continuous with respect to

\mathscr{N}
whenever
N\in\mathscr{N}
implies
\mu(N)=0
. Let
\mathbf{q}\colon(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})\to(Y,\mathscr{B},\mathscr{M})
a morphism of
saturated MSNs. We define the
pushforward
measure
\mathbf{q}_{\#}\mu\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=q_{\#}\mu
, where
q
is any representative of
\mathbf{q}
. This
definition makes sense, because, for all
q^{\prime}\in\mathbf{q}
and
A\in\mathscr{A}
, we have
\mu(q^{-1}(A)\ominus(q^{\prime})^{-1}(A))=0
, owing to
the absolute continuity of
\mu
. Trivially,
\mathbf{q}_{\#}\mu
is
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathscr{M}
.

8.3
Pre-image Measure. —

Let
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
be a complete semi-finite measure space. To
simplify the notations, we abbreviate
\mathscr{N}_{\mu}
to

\mathscr{N}
. Following the discussion in Paragraph
4.15
,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
is locally ccc. Recording
Corollary
7.4
,
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})
has a cccc version

[(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}),\mathbf{p}]
and we shall show that
there is a unique measure
\hat{\mu}
on
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}})

such that

(1)

\mathscr{N}_{\hat{\mu}}=\hat{\mathscr{N}}
;

(2)

\mathbf{p}_{\#}\hat{\mu}=\mu
.

Such a measure
\hat{\mu}
is referred to as the
pre-image measure
of

\mu
. Moreover, we will show that the measure space
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu})
is strictly localizable; we say that

[(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu}),\mathbf{p}]
is the
strictly
localizable version
of the measure space
(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
.

We start to prove the uniqueness of
\hat{\mu}
. Fix a
representative
p\in\mathbf{p}
. For any
F\in\mathscr{A}
we define
\hat{F}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=p^{-1}(F)
and call
p_{F}\colon\hat{F}\to F
the restriction
of
p
, which induces, as usual, a morphism
\mathbf{p}_{F}\colon(\hat{F},\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{\hat{F}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}_{\hat{F}})\to(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F})
. Call
\mathscr{A}^{f}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{A}\cap\{F:\mu(F)<\infty\}
. A pre-image measure
\hat{\mu}
must satisfy
\mathbf{p}_{F_{\#}}(\hat{\mu}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\hat{F})=\mu\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,F
for every
F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}
. But
(F,\mathscr{A}_{F},\mathscr{N}_{F})
is ccc, so by Proposition
4.16
,
\mathbf{p}_{F}

is an isomorphism, forcing
\hat{\mu}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\hat{F}=(\mathbf{p}_{F}^{-1})_{\#}(\mu\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,F)
to hold. Since
p
is supremum
preserving, the collection
\{\hat{F}:F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\}
admits

\hat{X}
as an
\hat{\mathscr{N}}
essential supremum. By (1) and
Lemma
8.1
we infer that

\hat{\mu}(A)=\sup\left\{\hat{\mu}(A\cap\hat{F}):F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\right\}=\sup\left\{(\mathbf{p}_{F}^{-1})_{\#}(\mu\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,F)(A\cap\hat{F}):F\in\mathscr{A}^{f}\right\}

for all
A\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
, from which the uniqueness of the
pre-image measure follows straightforwardly.

8.4
. —

To deal with the existence of pre-image measures, we will fix a cccc version,
obtained by an application Theorem
7.1
to the family

\mathscr{A}^{f}
defined above. As all cccc versions of
(X,\mathscr{A},\mathscr{N})

are isomorphic, there is no restriction in considering this special
case.

Henceforth we suppose that
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})=\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}(Z,\mathscr{A}_{Z},\mathscr{N}_{Z})
, where
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{f}\setminus\mathscr{N}
is a collection such that (A), (B) and
(C) of Theorem
7.1
hold. We now define
\hat{\mu}
on

(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}})
by

\hat{\mu}\left(\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}A_{Z}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}\mu(A_{Z})

each
A_{Z}
being an arbitrary measurable subset of
Z
. We choose
the representative
p=\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}\iota_{Z}
of
\mathbf{p}
,
each
\iota_{Z}\colon Z\to X
being the inclusion map.

Obviously,
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu})
is a strictly
localizable measure space and
\mathscr{N}_{\hat{\mu}}=\hat{\mathscr{N}}
,
which is condition (1) of Paragraph
8.3
. The next result
gathers some facts about the measure
\hat{\mu}
. In particular,
condition (2) of Paragraph
8.3
is proven in
Proposition
8.5
(B).

8.5 Proposition
. —

With the notations of paragraph
8.4
:

(A)

For all
A\in\mathscr{A}
, one has
\mu(A)=\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}\mu(A\cap Z)
.

(B)

\mathbf{p}_{\#}\hat{\mu}=\mu
;

(C)

For every set
A\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
with
\sigma
-finite

\hat{\mu}
-measure, there is
B\in\mathscr{A}
with
\sigma
-finite

\mu
-measure such that
\hat{\mu}(A\ominus\hat{B})=0
, where

\hat{B}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=p^{-1}(B)
.

Proof.

(A) When
\mathscr{E}\cap\{Z:\mu(A\cap Z)>0\}
is uncountable, the
result follows easily, for there is
\alpha>0
such that

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{E}\cap\{Z:\mu(A\cap Z)>\alpha\}
is
infinite. Taking a countable subset
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}
, then

\mu(A)\geqslant\mu\left(A\cap\bigcup\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\mu(A\cap Z)=\infty

because
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is almost disjointed.

On the other hand, suppose
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{E}\cap\{Z:\mu(A\cap Z)>0\}
is countable and set
A^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=A\setminus\bigcup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
. Then
\mu(A^{\prime}\cap Z)=0
for every
Z\in\mathscr{E}
. By
Lemma
3.4
,
A^{\prime}
is an
\mathscr{N}
essential supremum of

\{A^{\prime}\cap Z:Z\in\mathscr{E}\}
, which forces
A^{\prime}
to be
negligible. Consequently,

\mu(A)=\mu\left(A\cap\bigcup\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}\mu(A\cap Z)=\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}\mu(A\cap Z)

(B) For any
A\in\mathscr{A}
, one has

\mathbf{p}_{\#}\hat{\mu}(A)=\hat{\mu}(p^{-1}(A))=\hat{\mu}\left(\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}A\cap Z\right)=\sum_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}\mu(A\cap Z)

We conclude by means of (A).

(C) Let
A\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
a set of
\sigma
-finite
\hat{\mu}

measure. Writing
A=\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}A_{Z}
, each
A_{Z}
being a
measurable subset of
Z
, the set
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{E}\cap\{Z:\mu(A_{Z})>0\}
must be countable. Define
B\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigcup\{A_{Z}:Z\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\}
. We claim that
A\ominus\hat{B}=\coprod_{Z\in\mathscr{E}}\left(A_{Z}\ominus(B\cap Z)\right)
is negligible,
or, equivalently, all
A_{Z}\ominus(B\cap Z)
are
negligible. Indeed, for
Z\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
, one has
A_{Z}\ominus(B\cap Z)\subseteq\bigcup\{A_{Z^{\prime}}\cap Z:Z^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\text{ and }Z^{\prime}\neq Z\}\in\mathscr{N}
. If
Z\not\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
, then both
A_{Z}
and
B\cap Z
are negligible.
∎

8.6 Proposition
. —

The Banach spaces
\mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
and
\mathbf{L}_{1}(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu})
are isometrically isomorphic.

Proof.

For any
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)
we check that
f\circ p

and
f^{\prime}\circ p
coincide almost everywhere. Thus, the linear map
\varphi\colon\mathbf{L}_{1}(X,\mathscr{A},\mu)\to\mathbf{L}_{1}(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu})
which
assigns to
\mathbf{f}
the equivalence class of
f\circ p
is
well-defined. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|f|d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\{|f|>t\})dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{p}_{\#}\hat{\mu}(\{|f|>t\})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\mu}(\{|f\circ p|>t\})dt=\int_{\hat{X}}|f\circ p|d\hat{\mu},

showing that
\varphi
is an isometry.

Let us show that
\varphi
is onto. Let
\hat{f}
be an integrable
function on
(\hat{X},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mu})
. As
\{\hat{f}\neq 0\}
has
\sigma
-finite
\hat{\mu}
measure,
Proposition
8.5
(B) provides a set
B\in\mathscr{A}
of

\sigma
-finite
\mu
measure such that
\hat{\mu}(\{\hat{f}\neq 0\}\ominus\hat{B})=0
. But
(B,\mathscr{A}_{B},\mathscr{N}_{B})
is strictly
localizable, and by Proposition
4.16
, the morphism
\mathbf{p}_{B}\colon(\hat{B},\hat{\mathscr{A}}_{\hat{B}},\hat{\mathscr{N}}_{\hat{B}})\to(B,\mathscr{A}_{B},\mathscr{N}_{B})
induced by the restriction
p_{B}\colon\hat{B}\to B
of
p
is an isomorphism. We choose
q_{B}\colon B\to\hat{B}
a representative of
\mathbf{p}_{B}^{-1}
and define the map
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
by
f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\hat{f}(q_{B}(x))
for
x\in B
and

f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=0
otherwise. Finally, because
\{\hat{f}\neq f\circ p\}\subseteq(\hat{B}\cap\{x:q_{B}(p(x))\neq x\})\cup(\{\hat{f}\neq 0\}\setminus\hat{B})
, the maps
\hat{f}
and
f\circ p

coincide almost everywhere.
∎

9.
A directional Radon-Nikodým theorem

In this section, we prove an extension of the Radon-Nikodým theorem
for measure spaces that are not necessarily localizable, in connection
with the duality outlined in Corollary
8.7
. So to speak, it
involves a generalized Radon-Nikodým density that also depends on
the direction: as a function, it is defined on the strictly
localizable version.

This result is a slight extension of the Radon-Nikodým theorem that
was discovered independently by McShane
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
and
Zaanen
[
20
]
. Using Fremlin’s version of the Radon-Nikodým
theorem
[
5
, 232E]
in the proof below instead of the
standard one (between measure spaces of finite measure), we are able
to weaken one of the hypotheses in
[
12
]
and ask (2)
instead. But the main difference with
[
12
]
and
[
20
]

is in terms of formulation. In their work, the Radon-Nikodým density
takes the form of a “quasi-function” or a “cross-section”, a
notion that is very close to that of a compatible family of measurable
functions.

-dimensional submanifold passing through
x
. Of course if
M
and
M^{\prime}
belong to the same, classically defined, germ, i.e. if there exists a neighborhood
V
of
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
M\cap V=M^{\prime}\cap V
, then
M\sim_{x}M^{\prime}
. Notwithstanding, the following example illustrates the difference. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let
W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a
k
-dimensional affine subspace containing
x
, and let
C\subseteq W
be closed with empty interior and such that
\Theta^{k}_{*}(\phi_{W}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,C,x)=1
. Choose a
k
-dimensional submanifold
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
of class
C^{1}
“that sticks to
W
exactly along

C
”, that is
W\cap M=C
. It follows that
W\sim_{x}M
, yet
M\cap V\neq W\cap V
, for every neighborhood
V
of
x
. We note, however, that if
M\sim_{x}M^{\prime}
, then
\rmTan(M,x)=\rmTan(M^{\prime},x)
.

The construction here could be repeated by replacing
\mathscr{E}
by
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
,
the collection of all Borel measurable, countably
(\mathscr{H}^{k},k)
-rectifiable subsets
M
of
X
such that
\mathscr{H}^{k}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,M
is locally finite,
and

\Theta^{k}_{*}(\phi_{M},x)=1
for every
x\in M
. The latter does not hold in general for rectifiable sets, unlike the case of (embedded) submanifolds. It is critical when establishing that condition
10.1
(4) holds.

11.2
Integral geometric measure. —

Here, we show that the methods of
11.1
apply, in
fact, to a special measure space. We keep the same notations as in

11.1
and we let
\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}
be the integral
geometric outer measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined in
[
4
, 2.10.5(1)]
or

[
11
, 5.14]
. The measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty})

is not semi-finite (for the case
1=k=m-1
, see

[
4
, 3.3.20]
). Thus, recalling
8.8
, we introduce the
following:

\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(A)=\sup\left\{\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}(A\cap B):B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),B\subseteq A\text{ and }\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}(B)<\infty\right\},

for
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. The measure space
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty})
is semi-finite, and
\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(A)=0
whenever
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is purely
\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}
-infinite, i.e.
A
itself and all its Borel subsets of nonzero measure have infinite measure. We denote by
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})},\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty})
its completion. Our goal is to describe its cccc, lld, and strictly localizable version. The corresponding MSN
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})},\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}})
is readily saturated. We will check conditions (ii) through
(vii) at the beginning of this section.

(ii) We claim that
\mathscr{E}
is

\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
-generating in the MSN

(\mathbb{R}^{m},\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})},\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}})
, where

\mathscr{E}
is as in
11.1
. We know that the collection

\mathscr{A}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\cap\{A:\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(A)<\infty\}
is

\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
-generating, by
4.2
. It
is easy to check that it suffices to establish the following: For
every
A\in\mathscr{A}
there is a sequence
\langle M_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in

\mathscr{E}
such that
A\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}\in\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
. Let
A\in\mathscr{A}
. By definition of
completion of a measure space, there are
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
N\in\mathscr{N}_{\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
, and
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
such that
A=B\ominus N^{\prime}
. Since
\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(N^{\prime})=0
, it suffices to
prove the existence of a sequence
\langle M_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{E}

such that
B\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}\in\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
. Since
\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(B)=\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(B)=\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(A)<\infty
, there
are Borel sets
F
and
N
such that
B=F\cup N
,

\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}(F)<\infty
, and
\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(N)=0
, by

8.8
(3). It follows from the Besicovitch Structure Theorem

[
4
, 3.3.14]
that
F
is
(\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty},k)
-rectifiable. In
particular, there is a sequence
\langle M_{n}\rangle_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathscr{E}

such that
F\setminus\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}\in\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{H}^{k}}\subseteq\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}}
. Since
B\setminus F\in\mathscr{N}_{\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}\subseteq\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
, the proof is complete.

In order
to establish (iii) through (vii), it suffices to observe that for each

M\in\mathscr{E}
the MSNs

(M,\overline{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}_{M},(\mathscr{N}_{\mathrm{pu},k})_{M})
and
(M,\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}_{M},(\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}})_{M})
are the
same. We recall from
11.1
(iii) that the former is
the saturation of
(M,\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})_{M},\mathscr{H}^{k}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,M)
. Let us prove that
the latter has the same property. Let
S\in\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}_{M}
. There are
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
N\in\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}}
such that
S=B\ominus N
. Since
S=S\cap M=(B\cap M)\ominus(M\cap N)
, there is no restriction
to assume that both
B
and
N
are contained in
M
. Therefore, we
ought to show that
\mathscr{H}^{k}(N)=0
. There exists a Borel set
N^{\prime}\subseteq M
containing
N
and such that

\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}(N^{\prime})=0
. We observe that

\check{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,M=\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,M=\mathscr{H}^{k}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,M
, where the second equality follows from
[
4
, 3.2.26]
and
first equality follows from
8.8
(2) and the fact that
M
has

\sigma
-finite
\mathscr{I}^{k}_{\infty}
measure. Thus,
\mathscr{H}^{k}(N^{\prime})=0
and we
are done.

It follows that the germ space

(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{m}},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{N}})
constructed in

11.1
is, in fact, also the cccc and lld version of
the MSN

(\mathbb{R}^{m},\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})},\mathscr{N}_{\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}})
. Furthermore,
if
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}
denotes the pre-image measure of

\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}
along the projection map
p\colon\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then

(\widehat{\mathbb{R}^{m}},\widehat{\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}},{\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty}})

is the strictly localizable version of

(\mathbb{R}^{m},\widetilde{\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})},\tilde{\mathscr{I}}^{k}_{\infty})
.

11.3
Hausdorff measures. —

Here, we briefly comment on why the lower densities set up so far in
this section do not help to describe explicitly the cccc and lld
version of the saturation of the MSN

(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{H}^{k}})
. The main reason is that we would
need to enlarge the collection
\mathscr{E}
for it to be generating, since
there are (much) less
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-negligible sets than there are purely

k
-unrectifiable sets. In doing so we loose (

♣

‣
11.1
),
which was critical for implementing the techniques of the previous
section. In fact, if

is Borel,
\phi_{M}=\mathscr{H}^{k}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,M

is locally finite, and
\Theta^{k}(\phi_{M},x)=1
for
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-almost
every
x\in M
, then
M
is countably
(\mathscr{H}^{k},k)
-rectifiable, see

e.g.

[

. Since we ought to include non

-negligible, purely

\mathscr{N}_{\mathscr{H}^{k}}
-generating family, our only choice is, if possible,
to change the definition of the lower densities
\Theta_{M}
. So far, we
do not know how to construct, in this case, a compatible family of

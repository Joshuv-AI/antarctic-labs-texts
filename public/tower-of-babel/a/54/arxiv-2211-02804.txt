Varieties of unary-determined distributive ℓ \ell -magmas and bunched implication algebras
Natanael Alpay ORCID:0000-0002-1505-7531, Peter Jipsen ORCID:0000-0001-8608-808X, Melissa Sugimoto ORCID:0009-0004-7168-8954

Abstract
. A distributive lattice-ordered magma ( d ​ ℓ d\ell -magma) ( A , ∧ , ∨ , ⋅ ) (A,\wedge,\vee,\cdot) is a distributive lattice with a binary operation ⋅ \cdot that preserves joins in both arguments, and when ⋅ \cdot is associative then ( A , ∨ , ⋅ ) (A,\vee,\cdot) is an idempotent semiring. A d ​ ℓ d\ell -magma with a top ⊤ \top is unary-determined if x ⋅ y = ( x ⋅ ⊤ ∧ y ) x\cdot y=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y) ∨ ( x ∧ ⊤ ⋅ y ) \vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y) . These algebras are term-equivalent to a subvariety of distributive lattices with ⊤ \top and two join-preserving unary operations 𝗉 , 𝗊 \mathsf{p},\mathsf{q} . We obtain simple conditions on 𝗉 , 𝗊 \mathsf{p},\mathsf{q} such that x ⋅ y = ( 𝗉 ​ x ∧ y ) ∨ ( x ∧ 𝗊 ​ y ) x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y) is associative, commutative, idempotent and/or has an identity element. This generalizes previous results on the structure of doubly idempotent semirings and, in the case when the distributive lattice is a Heyting algebra, it provides structural insight into unary-determined algebraic models of bunched implication logic. We also provide Kripke semantics for the algebras under consideration, which leads to more efficient algorithms for constructing finite models. We find all subdirectly irreducible algebras up to cardinality eight in which 𝗉 = 𝗊 \mathsf{p}=\mathsf{q} is a closure operator, as well as all finite unary-determined bunched implication chains and map out the poset of join-irreducible varieties generated by them.

1.
Introduction

Idempotent semirings
(A,\vee,\cdot)
play an important role in several areas of computer science, such as network optimization, formal languages, Kleene algebras and program semantics. In this setting they are often assumed to have constants
0,1
that are the additive and multiplicative identity respectively, with
0
also being an absorbing element.
However semirings are usually only assumed to have two binary operations
+,\cdot
that
are associative such that
+
is also commutative and
\cdot
distributes over
+

from the left and right
[
HW98
]
.
A semiring is (additively) idempotent if
x+x=x
, hence

+
is a (join) semilattice, and
doubly idempotent
if
x\cdot x=x
as well. If

\cdot
is also commutative, then it defines a meet semilattice. The special case when these two semilattices coincide
corresponds exactly to the variety of distributive lattices, which have a well understood
structure theory.

In
[
AJ20
]
a complete structural description was given for finite commutative doubly idempotent semirings where either the multiplicative semilattice is a chain, or the additive semilattice is a Boolean algebra. Here we show that the second description can
be significantly generalized to the setting where the additive semilattice is a distributive
lattice, dropping the assumptions of finiteness, multiplicative commutativity and idempotence
in favor of the algebraic condition
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
for two unary join-preserving operations
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
. While this property is quite restrictive in general, it does hold in all idempotent Boolean magmas and expresses a binary operation in terms of two simpler unary operations. A full structural description of all (finite) idempotent semirings is unlikely, but in the setting of unary-determined idempotent semirings progress is possible.

In Section 2 we provide the needed background and prove a term-equivalence between
a subvariety of top-bounded
d\ell
-magmas and a subvariety of top-bounded distributive lattices with two unary operators. This is then specialized to cases where
\cdot
is
associative, commutative, idempotent or has an identity element.
In the next section we show that when the distributive lattice is a Brouwerian algebra
or Heyting algebra, then
\cdot
is residuated if and only if both
\mathsf{p}
and
\mathsf{q}
are
residuated. This establishes a connection with bunched implication algebras (BI-algebras)
that are the algebraic semantics of bunched implication logic
[
OP99
]
, used in the setting of separation logic for program verification, including reasoning about pointers
[
Rey02
]
and concurrent processes
[
O’H04
]
. Section 4 contains Kripke semantics
for
d\ell
-magmas, called Birkhoff frames, and for the two unary operators
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
. This establishes the connection to the previous results in
[
AJ20
]
and leads to
the main result (Thm.
13
) that preorder forest
P
-frames capture a larger
class of multiplicatively idempotent BI-algebras and doubly idempotent semirings. Although the heap models of BI-algebras used in applications are not (multiplicatively) idempotent, they contain idempotent subalgebras and homomorphic images, hence a characterization of unary-determined idempotent BI-algebras does provide insight into the general case.
In the next section we define weakly conservative
\ell
-magmas and their corresponding frames.
In Section 6 we apply the results from the previous sections to count the number of preorder forest
P
-frames up to isomorphism if their partial order is an antichain and also if it is a chain. Finally in Section 7 we calculate all subdirectly irreducible algebras up to cardinality eight in which
\mathsf{p}
and
\mathsf{q}
are the same closure operator, and map out the poset of join-irreducible varieties generated by them.

2.
A term-equivalence between distributive lattices with operators

A
distributive lattice-ordered magma
, or
d\ell
-magma
, is an algebra
\mathbf{A}=(A,\wedge,\vee,\cdot)
such that

(A,\wedge,\vee)
is a distributive lattice and
\cdot
is a
binary operator
, which in this case means a binary operation that distributes over
\vee
, i.e.,
x\cdot(y\vee z)=x\cdot y\vee x\cdot z
and
(x\vee y)\cdot z=x\cdot z\vee y\cdot z
for all
x,y,z\in A
. Throughout it is assumed that
\cdot
binds more strongly than
\wedge,\vee
, and as usual the lattice order
\leq
is defined by
x\leq y\iff x\wedge y=x

(\iff x\vee y=y)
. If the distributive lattice has a top element
\top
or a bottom element
\bot
then it is called
\top
-bounded
or

\bot
-bounded
, or simply
bounded
if both exist.
A
d\ell
-magma
\mathbf{A}
is
normal
and
\cdot
is a
normal
operation if
A
is
\bot
-bounded and satisfies
x\cdot\bot=\bot=\bot\cdot x
.
Similarly, a unary operation
f
on
A
is an
operator
if it satisfies the identity
f(x\vee y)=fx\vee fy
,
and it is
normal
if
f\bot=\bot
.
For brevity and to reduce the number of nested parentheses, we write function application as
fx
rather than
f(x)
, with the convention that it has priority over
\cdot
hence, e.g.,
fx\cdot y=(f(x))\cdot y
(this convention ensures unique readability).
Note that since operators distribute over
\vee
in each argument, they are order-preserving in each argument. The operation
f

is said to be
inflationary
if
x\leq fx
for all
x\in A
.

A binary operation
\cdot
is said to be
idempotent

if
x\cdot x=x
for all
x\in A
,
commutative
if
x\cdot y=y\cdot x
and
associative
if
(x\cdot y)\cdot z=x\cdot(y\cdot z)
. A
semigroup
is a set with an associative operation, a
monoid
is a semigroup with an identity element denoted by
1
, a
band
is
a semigroup that is also idempotent, and a
semilattice
is a commutative band.
As usual, a semilattice is partially ordered by
x\sqsubseteq y\iff x\cdot y=x
, and in this
case
x\cdot y
is the meet operation with respect to
\sqsubseteq
. We also use this
terminology with the prefix
d\ell
, in which case the magma operation satisfies the
corresponding identities.

A
d\ell
-magma is called
unary-determined
if it is
\top
-bounded and satisfies the identity

x\cdot y=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y).

As examples, we mention that all doubly-idempotent semirings with a Boolean join-semilattice are unary-determined (see Lemma
3
). Complete and atomic versions of such semirings are studied in
[
AJ20
]
, and the results from that paper are generalized here to unary-determined
d\ell
-magmas with algebraic proofs that apply to all members of the variety, while the previous results applied only to complete and atomic algebras.

A
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebra
is a
\top
-bounded distributive lattice with
two unary operators
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
that satisfy

x\wedge\mathsf{p}\top\leq\mathsf{q}x,\quad x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top\leq\mathsf{p}x.

We note that throughout
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
denote unary operations, and they bind more strongly than
\cdot,\wedge,\vee
.
These two (in)equational axioms are needed for
our first result which shows that unary-determined
d\ell
-magmas and
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebras
are term-equivalent. This means that although the two varieties are based on different
sets of fundamental operations (called the signature of each class), each fundamental operation of an algebra in one variety is identical to a term-operation constructed from fundamental operations of an algebra in the other variety (and vice versa). From the point of view of category theory, term-equivalent varieties are model categories of the same Lawvere theory.

Note that the (in)equalities above are satisfied in any
\top
-bounded distributive lattice with inflationary operators
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
since then
\mathsf{p}\top=\top=\mathsf{q}\top
. A
d\ell\mathsf{p}
-algebra
is a
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebra that satisfies the identity
\mathsf{p}x=\mathsf{q}x
.

Although unary-determined
d\ell
-magmas and
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebras seem rather special, they are simpler than general
d\ell
-magmas, yet include interesting idempotent semirings (as reducts).

Theorem 1
.

(1)

Let
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q})
be a
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebra
and define
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
. Then
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\cdot)
is a unary-determined
d\ell
-magma and

\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are given by
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top
and
\mathsf{q}x=\top\cdot x
.

(2)

Let
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\cdot)
be a unary-determined
d\ell
-magma and define
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top
,
\mathsf{q}x=\top\cdot x
. Then
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q})
is a
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebra and
\cdot
is definable from

\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
via
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
.

Proof 2.1
.

(1) Assume
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are unary operators on a
\top
-bounded distributive lattice
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top)
, and
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
. Then

\displaystyle x\cdot(y\vee z)

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge(y\vee z))\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}(y\vee z))

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(\mathsf{p}x\wedge z)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}z)

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)\vee(\mathsf{p}x\wedge z)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}z)

\displaystyle=x\cdot y\vee x\cdot z.

A similar calculation shows that
(x\vee y)\cdot z=x\cdot z\vee y\cdot z
, hence
\cdot
is an operator.

Since
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
satisfy
x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top\leq\mathsf{p}x
, it follows that
x\cdot\!\top=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\top)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top)=\mathsf{p}x\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top)=\mathsf{p}x
, and similarly

\top\!\cdot x=\mathsf{q}x
is implied by
x\wedge\mathsf{p}\top\leq\mathsf{q}x
. Now the identity
x\cdot y=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y)
holds by definition.

(2) Assume
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\cdot)
is a unary-determined
d\ell
-magma, and define
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top
,
\mathsf{q}x=\top\!\cdot y
.
Then
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are unary operators and
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge\top)\vee(x\wedge\top\cdot\top)=\mathsf{p}x\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top)
, hence

x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top\leq\mathsf{p}x
. The inequality
x\wedge\mathsf{p}\top\leq\mathsf{q}x
is proved similarly.
The operation
\cdot
can be recovered from
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
since
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
follows from the identity we assumed.

The preceding theorem shows that unary-determined
d\ell
-magmas and
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebras are “essentially the same”, and we can choose to work with the signature that is preferred in a given situation. The unary operators of
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebras are simpler to handle, while the binary operator
\cdot
is familiar in the semiring setting. Next we examine how standard properties of
\cdot
are captured by identities in the language of
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebras.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q})
be a
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebra
and define
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
.

(1)

The operator
\cdot
is commutative if and only if
\mathsf{p}=\mathsf{q}
.

(2)

If
\mathsf{p}=\mathsf{q}
, then
\cdot
is associative if and only if
\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y))=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y)
.

(3)

The operator
\cdot
is idempotent if and only if
\mathsf{p}
and
\mathsf{q}
are inflationary, if and only if
\mathsf{p}\top=\top=\mathsf{q}\top
.

(4)

If
\cdot
is idempotent, then it is associative if and only if

\displaystyle\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y))

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathsf{q}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y))

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(\mathsf{q}x\wedge\mathsf{q}y).

(5)

The operator
\cdot
has an identity
1
if and only if
\mathsf{p}1{=}\top{=}\mathsf{q}1
and
(\mathsf{p}x\vee\mathsf{q}x)\wedge 1\leq x
.

(6)

If
\cdot
has an identity, then
\cdot
is idempotent.

Proof 2.2
.

(1)
Assuming
x\cdot y=y\cdot x
, we clearly have
x\cdot\!\top=\top\!\cdot x
, hence
\mathsf{p}x=\mathsf{q}x
. The converse makes use
of commutativity of
\wedge
and
\vee
:

x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)=(\mathsf{p}y\wedge x)\vee(y\wedge\mathsf{p}x)=y\cdot x
.

(2)
Assume
\mathsf{p}=\mathsf{q}
. If
\cdot
is associative then
(x\cdot y)\cdot\top=x\cdot(y\cdot\top)
, so by the previous theorem,

\mathsf{p}(x\cdot y)=x\cdot\mathsf{p}y
, which translates to

\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y))=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y)\quad(*).

Conversely, suppose
(*)
holds, and note that
\mathsf{p}(x\cdot y)=\mathsf{p}(y\cdot x)
by (1), hence

\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y))=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y)\vee(\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\quad(**).

It suffices to prove
(x\cdot y)\cdot z\leq x\cdot(y\cdot z)
since then
z\cdot(y\cdot x)\leq(z\cdot y)\cdot x
follows by commutativity. Now

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y))\wedge z]\vee[((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y))\wedge\mathsf{p}z]

\displaystyle=[((\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y))\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]\text{ using $(*)$}

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]

\displaystyle\leq[\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]\vee[x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}z]\vee[x{\wedge}\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y{\wedge}z]

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{p}z]\vee[x\wedge((\mathsf{p}y\wedge\mathsf{p}z)\vee(y\wedge\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}z)\vee(\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}y\wedge z))]

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge((\mathsf{p}y\wedge z)\vee(y\wedge\mathsf{p}z))]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}y\wedge z)\vee(y\wedge\mathsf{p}z))]\text{ using $(**)$}

\displaystyle=x\cdot(y\cdot z).

(3) If
\cdot
is idempotent, then
x=x\cdot x\leq x\cdot\!\top=\mathsf{p}x
and
x\leq\top\!\cdot x=\mathsf{q}x
. Conversely,
if
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are inflationary then
x\cdot x=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge x)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)=x\vee x=x
,
hence
\cdot
is idempotent. For the second equivalence, if
\mathsf{p}\top=\top=\mathsf{q}\top
, then
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are inflationary since they satisfy
x\wedge\mathsf{p}\top\leq\mathsf{q}x
and
x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top\leq\mathsf{p}x
. The reverse implication holds because
x\leq\mathsf{p}x,\mathsf{q}x
implies
\top\leq\mathsf{p}\top,\mathsf{q}\top
.

(4) Assume
\cdot
is idempotent and associative. Then
(\top\!\cdot x)\cdot\top=\top\cdot(x\cdot\!\top)
, hence

\mathsf{q}\mathsf{p}x=\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}x
. Furthermore,
\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}x=\top\!\cdot x\cdot\!\top=\top\cdot x\cdot x\cdot\top=(\mathsf{q}x)\cdot(\mathsf{p}x)=(\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}x\wedge\mathsf{p}x)\vee(\mathsf{q}x\wedge\mathsf{q}\mathsf{p}x)
. By (3)
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are inflationary, so
\mathsf{p}x\leq\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}x
and
\mathsf{q}x\leq\mathsf{q}\mathsf{p}x
. Therefore

\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}x=\mathsf{p}x\vee\mathsf{q}x
. Now we translate
(x\cdot y)\cdot\top=x\cdot(y\cdot\top)
to obtain
\mathsf{p}(x\cdot y)=x\cdot(\mathsf{p}y)
, hence

\displaystyle\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y))=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}\mathsf{p}y)=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge(\mathsf{p}y\vee\mathsf{q}y))

\displaystyle=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)\text{ since $x\leq\mathsf{p}x$ by (3).}

The identity
\mathsf{q}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y))=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(\mathsf{q}x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
has a similar proof.

Conversely, assume the two identities hold. Then using distributivity

\displaystyle(x\cdot y)\cdot z

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y))\wedge z]\vee[((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y))\wedge\mathsf{q}z]

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{q}z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\wedge\mathsf{q}z]

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{q}z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\wedge\mathsf{q}z]\text{ \ since $x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\wedge z\leq x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\wedge\mathsf{q}z$}

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\wedge\mathsf{q}z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\wedge z]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\wedge\mathsf{q}z]

\displaystyle=[\mathsf{p}x\wedge((\mathsf{p}y\wedge z)\vee(y\wedge\mathsf{q}z))]\vee[x\wedge\mathsf{q}((\mathsf{p}y\wedge z)\vee(y\wedge\mathsf{q}z))]=x\cdot(y\cdot z).

(5) Assume
x
has an identity
1
. Then
\mathsf{p}1=1\cdot\top=\top=\top\cdot 1=\mathsf{q}1
and
x=x\cdot 1=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}1)=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1)\vee x
, so
\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1\leq x
and similarly
\mathsf{q}x\wedge 1\leq x
. Therefore
(\mathsf{p}x\vee\mathsf{q}x)\wedge 1=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1)\vee(\mathsf{q}x\wedge 1)\leq x
.

Conversely, suppose
\mathsf{p}1=\top=\mathsf{q}1
and
(\mathsf{p}x\vee\mathsf{q}x)\wedge 1\leq x
. Then
x\cdot 1=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}1)=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1)\vee x=x
since
\mathsf{p}x\wedge 1\leq x
. Likewise
1\cdot x=x
.

(6) This follows from (3) since
x=x\cdot 1\leq x\cdot\!\top=\mathsf{p}x
and
x=1\cdot x\leq\mathsf{q}x
.

Note that if
\mathbf{A}
also has a bottom bound
\bot
, then
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
are normal if and only if
\cdot
is normal, hence the term-equivalence preserves normality.

This term-equivalence is useful since distributive lattices with unary operators are considerably simpler than distributive lattices
with binary operators. In particular, (2) and (4) show that associativity can be replaced by one or two 2-variable identities in this variety. This provides more efficient ways to construct associative operators from a (pair of) unary operator(s) on a distributive lattice.
The variety of
\top
-bounded distributive lattices is obtained as a subvariety of
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-algebras that satisfy
\mathsf{p}x=x=\mathsf{q}x
, or a subvariety of unary determined
d\ell
-magmas that
satisfy
x\cdot y=x\wedge y
.

A
Boolean magma
is a Boolean algebra with a binary operator. The next
lemma shows that if the operator is idempotent, then it is always unary-determined, hence the results in the current paper generalize the theorems about idempotent Boolean nonassociative quantales in
[
AJ20
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Every idempotent Boolean magma
(A,\wedge,\vee,\neg,\bot,\!\top,\cdot)

is unary-determined, i.e., satisfies
x\cdot y=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y)
.

Proof 2.3
.

Idempotence is equivalent to
x\wedge y\leq x\cdot y\leq x\vee y
since

(x\wedge y)\cdot(x\wedge y)\leq x\cdot y\leq(x\vee y)\cdot(x\vee y)
holds in all partially ordered algebras
where
\cdot
is an order-preserving binary operation. The following calculation

\displaystyle x\cdot\!\top\wedge y

\displaystyle=x\cdot(y\vee\neg y)\wedge y=(x\cdot y\wedge y)\vee(x\cdot\neg y\wedge y)

\displaystyle\leq x\cdot y\vee((x\vee\neg y)\wedge y)=x\cdot y\vee(x\wedge y)\vee(\neg y\wedge y)=x\cdot y

and a similar one for
x\wedge\top\!\cdot y\leq x\cdot y
prove that
x\cdot y\geq(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y)
.

Using Boolean negation, the opposite inequality is equivalent to

x\cdot y\wedge\neg(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\leq x\wedge\top\!\cdot y.

By De Morgan’s law it suffices to show
(x\cdot y\wedge\neg(x\cdot\!\top))\vee(x\cdot y\wedge\neg y)\leq x\wedge\top\!\cdot y
. Since
x\cdot y\leq x\cdot\!\top
, the first meet disappears. Next, by idempotence,
x\cdot y\wedge\neg y\leq(x\vee y)\wedge\neg y=(x\wedge\neg y)\vee(y\wedge\neg y)\leq x
and finally
x\cdot y\wedge\neg y\leq x\cdot y\leq\top\!\cdot y
.

3.
BI-algebras from Heyting algebras and residuated unary operations

We now recall some basic definitions about residuated operations, adjoints and residuated lattices. For an overview and additional details we refer to
[
GJKO07
]
.
A
Brouwerian algebra

(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,\top)
is a
\top
-bounded lattice
such that
\to
is the
residual
of
\wedge
, i.e.,

x\wedge y\leq z\quad\iff\quad y\leq x\to z.

Since
\to
is the residual of
\wedge
, we have that
\wedge
is join-preserving, so
the lattice is distributive
[
GJKO07
, Lem. 4.1]
. The
\top
-bound is included as a constant since it always exists
when a meet-operation has a residual:
x\wedge y\leq x
always holds, hence
y\leq(x\to x)=\top
.
A
Heyting algebra
is a bounded Brouwerian algebra with a constant
\bot
denoting the bottom element.

A
dual operator
is an
n
-ary operation on a lattice that preserves meets in each argument.
A
residual
or
upper adjoint
of a unary operation
\mathsf{p}
on a poset
\mathbf{A}=(A,\leq)
is a unary operation
\mathsf{p}^{*}
such that

\mathsf{p}x\leq y\iff x\leq\mathsf{p}^{*}y

for all
x,y\in A
. If
\mathbf{A}
is a lattice, then the existence of a residual guarantees that
\mathsf{p}
is an operator and
\mathsf{p}^{*}
is a dual operator
[
GJKO07
, Lem. 3.5]
. Moreover, if
\mathbf{A}
is bounded, then
\mathsf{p}\bot=\bot
and

\mathsf{p}^{*}\top=\top
.

A binary operation
\cdot
on a poset is
residuated
if there exist a
left residual

\backslash
and a
right residual

/
such that

x\cdot y\leq z\iff y\leq x\backslash z\iff x\leq z/y.

A
residuated
\ell
-magma

(A,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,\backslash,/)
is a lattice with a residuated binary operation. In this case
\cdot
is an operator and
\backslash,/
are dual
operators in the “numerator” argument. The “denominator” arguments of
\backslash,/
map joins to meets, hence they are order reversing. A
residuated Brouwerian-magma
is a residuated
\ell
-magma expanded with
\to,\top
such that
(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,\top)
is a Brouwerian algebra.

A
residuated lattice
is a residuated
\ell
-magma with
\cdot
associative and a constant
1
that is an identity element, i.e.,
(A,\cdot,1)
is a monoid. A
generalized bunched implication algebra
, or GBI-algebra,
\mathbf{A}=(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,\top,\cdot,1,\backslash,/)
is
a
\top
-bounded residuated lattice with a residual
\to
for the meet operation, i.e.,
(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,\top)
is a Brouwerian algebra. A GBI-algebra is called a
bunched implication algebra
(BI-algebra) if
\cdot
is commutative and
\mathbf{A}
also has a bottom element, denoted by the constant
\bot
, hence a BI-algebra has a Heyting algebra reduct. These algebras are the algebraic semantics for
bunched implication logic, which is the propositional part of separation logic, a Hoare logic used for reasoning about memory references in computer programs. In this setting the operation
\cdot
is usually denoted by
*
, the left residual
\backslash
is denoted
\mathbin{-\!\!*}
, and
/
can be omitted since
x/y=y\mathbin{-\!\!*}x
.

Note that the property of being a residual can be expressed by inequalities (
\mathsf{p}^{*}
is a residual of
\mathsf{p}
if and only if
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{p}^{*}x)\leq x\leq\mathsf{p}^{*}(\mathsf{p}x)
for all
x
, and
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{p}^{*}
are order preserving), hence the classes of all Brouwerian algebras, Heyting algebras, residuated
\ell
-magmas, residuated Brouwerian-magmas, residuated lattices, (G)BI-algebras, and pairs of residuated unary maps on a lattice are varieties (see e.g.
[
GJKO07
, Thm. 2.7 and Lem. 3.2]
). Recall also that a
\top
-bounded magma is unary-determined if it satisfies the identity
x\cdot y=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y)
.

We are now ready to prove a result that upgrades the term-equivalence of Theorem
1
to Brouwerian algebras with two pairs of residuated maps and unary-determined residuated Brouwerian-magmas.

Theorem 4
.

(1)

Let
(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,\top,\mathsf{p},\mathsf{p}^{*},\mathsf{q},\mathsf{q}^{*})
be a Brouwerian algebra with unary operators
\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
and their residuals
\mathsf{p}^{*},\mathsf{q}^{*}
such that
x\wedge\mathsf{p}\top\leq\mathsf{q}x
,
x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top\leq\mathsf{p}x
. If we define

x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)
,

x\backslash y=(\mathsf{p}x\to y)\wedge\mathsf{q}^{*}(x\to y)\quad\text{and}\quad x/y=\mathsf{p}^{*}(y\to x)\wedge(\mathsf{q}y\to x),

then
(A,\wedge,\vee,\top,\cdot,\backslash,/)
is a unary-determined residuated Brouwerian-magma
and the unary operations are recovered by
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top
,
\mathsf{p}^{*}x=x/\top
,
\mathsf{q}x=\top\!\cdot x
and
\mathsf{q}^{*}x=\top\backslash x
.

(2)

Let
(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,\top,\cdot,\backslash,/)
be a unary-determined
residuated Brouwerian-magma and define
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top
,
\mathsf{p}^{*}x=x/\top
,

\mathsf{q}x=\top\!\cdot x
and
\mathsf{q}^{*}x=\top\backslash x
. Then
(A,\wedge,\vee,\to,

\top,\mathsf{p},\mathsf{p}^{*},\mathsf{q},\mathsf{q}^{*})
is a Brouwerian algebra with a unary operators

\mathsf{p},\mathsf{q}
and dual operators
\mathsf{p}^{*},\mathsf{q}^{*}
that satisfies
x\wedge\mathsf{p}\top\leq\mathsf{q}x
,

x\wedge\mathsf{q}\top\leq\mathsf{p}x
.

Proof 3.1
.

(1) The following calculation shows that
\cdot
is residuated.

\displaystyle x\cdot y\leq z

\displaystyle\iff(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{q}y)\leq z

\displaystyle\iff\mathsf{p}x\wedge y\leq z\text{ and }x\wedge\mathsf{q}y\leq z

\displaystyle\iff y\leq\mathsf{p}x\to z\text{ and }y\leq\mathsf{q}^{*}(x\to z)

\displaystyle\iff y\leq(\mathsf{p}x\to z)\wedge\mathsf{q}^{*}(x\to z)

hence
x\backslash z=(\mathsf{p}x\to z)\wedge\mathsf{q}^{*}(x\to z)
and similarly
z/y=\mathsf{p}^{*}(y\to z)\wedge(\mathsf{q}y\to z)
. By Theorem

it follows that
\mathsf{p}x=x\cdot\!\top,\mathsf{q}x=\top\!\cdot x
and
x\cdot y=(x\cdot\!\top\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\top\!\cdot y)
. Since
x\cdot\!\top\leq y\iff x\leq y/\top
we obtain
\mathsf{p}^{*}(x)=x/\top
, and similarly
\mathsf{q}^{*}(x)=\top\backslash x
.

(2) Since
\cdot
is residuated it follows that
\mathsf{p}^{*}
and
\mathsf{q}^{*}
are the unary residuals of
\mathsf{p}
,
\mathsf{q}
respectively. The remaining parts hold by Theorem
1
.

Recall that a closure operator
\mathsf{p}
is an order-preserving unary function on a poset such that
x\leq\mathsf{p}x=\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}x
. A bounded

-algebra where

-closure algebra.
If

, so

is a closure operator.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
\mathbf{A}
is a
d\ell\mathsf{p}
-closure algebra and let
x\cdot y=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)
.
Then
\cdot
is associative if and only if

\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\leq\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\vee\mathsf{p}y))
.

Proof 3.2
.

By Lemma

\cdot
is associative if and only if
the identity
\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\vee\mathsf{p}y))=(\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\vee(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)
holds.
This is equivalent to
\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\leq\mathsf{p}((\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\vee(x\vee\mathsf{p}y))

since
x\wedge\mathsf{p}y\leq\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y
,
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{p}x\wedge y)\leq\mathsf{p}\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y=\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y

and similarly
\mathsf{p}(x\wedge\mathsf{p}y)\leq\mathsf{p}x\wedge\mathsf{p}y
.

-algebras, as shown (later) by the algebra

4.
Relational semantics for
d\ell
-magmas

We now briefly recall relational semantics for bounded distributive lattices with
operators and then apply correspondence theory to derive first-order
conditions for the equational properties of the preceding sections.

An element in a lattice is
completely join-irreducible
if it is not the supremum
of all the elements strictly below it. The set of all completely join-irreducible elements of a lattice
A
is denoted by
J(A)
, and it is partially ordered by restricting the order of
A
to
J(A)
.
For example, if
A
is a Boolean lattice, then
J(A)=At(A)
is the antichain of
atoms
, i.e., all elements immediately above the bottom element. The set
M(A)
of completely meet-irreducible elements is defined dually. A lattice is
perfect
if it is complete (i.e., all joins and meets exist) and every element is a join of completely join-irreducibles and a meet of completely meet-irreducibles. For a Boolean algebra, the notion of perfect is equivalent to being
complete
(i.e., joins and meets of all subsets exist) and
atomic
(i.e., every non-bottom element has an atom below it).

Recall that for a poset
\mathbf{W}=(W,\leq)
, a
downset
is a subset
X
such that
y\leq x\in X
implies
y\in X
. As in modal logic,
W
is considered a set of “worlds” or states. We let
D(\mathbf{W})
be the set of all downsets of
\mathbf{W}
, and
(D(\mathbf{W}),\cap,\cup)
the
lattice of downsets
. The collection
D(\mathbf{W})

is a perfect distributive lattice with infinitary meet and join given by (arbitrary) intersections and unions. The following result, due to Birkhoff
[
Bir67
, Thm. III.3.3]
for lattices of finite height, shows that up to isomorphism all perfect distributive lattices arise in this way. The poset
J(D(\mathbf{W}))
contains exactly the principal downsets
\mathord{\downarrow}x=\{y\in W\mid y\leq x\}
.

{thmC}

[
[
DP02
, 10.29]
] For a lattice
A
the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is distributive and perfect.

(2)

A
is isomorphic to the lattice of downsets of a partial order.

Note that the set of upsets of a poset is also a perfect distributive lattice,
and if it is ordered by reverse inclusion then this lattice is isomorphic to
the downset lattice described above.
It is also well known that the maps
J
and
D

are functors for a categorial duality between the category of posets with order-preserving maps and the category of perfect distributive lattices
with complete lattice homomorphisms (i.e., maps that preserve arbitrary joins and meets).

A
complete operator
on a complete lattice is an operation that, in each argument, is completely join-preserving, while a
complete dual operator
is completely meet-preserving (in each argument). A lattice-ordered algebra is called
perfect
if its lattice reduct is perfect and every fundamental operation on it is a complete operator or dual operator.
The duality between the category of perfect distributive lattices and posets extends to the category of perfect distributive lattices with (a fixed signature of) complete operators and dual operators. The corresponding poset category has additional relations of arity
n+1
for each (dual) operator of arity
n
, and the relations have to be upward or downward closed in each argument. For example, a binary relation
Q\subseteq W^{2}
is upward closed in the second argument if
xQy\leq z\implies xQz
. Here
xQy\leq z
is an abbreviation for
xQy
and
y\leq z
.

Perfect distributive lattices with operators, their residuals and dual operators are algebraic models for many
logics, including relevance logic, intuitionistic logic, Hájek’s basic logic, Łukasiewicz
logic and bunched implication logic
[
GNV05
,
GJKO07
]
.
In such an algebra
\mathbf{A}
, a join-preserving binary operation is determined by
a ternary relation
R
on
J(\mathbf{A})
given by

xRyz\iff x\leq yz.

The notation
xRyz
is shorthand for
(x,y,z)\in R
.
For
b,c\in A
the product
bc
is recovered as
\bigvee\{x\in J(\mathbf{A})\mid xRyz
for some
y\leq b
and
z\leq c\}
.

The relational structure
(J(\mathbf{A}),\leq,R)
is an example of a Birkhoff frame. In general, a
Birkhoff frame

[
GJ20
]
is a triple
\mathbf{W}=(W,\leq,R)
where
(W,\leq)
is a poset, and
R\subseteq W^{3}
satisfies the following three properties (downward closure in the 1st, and upward closure in the 2nd and 3rd argument):

\text{(R1)}\ u\leq xRyz\implies uRyz

\text{(R2)}\ xRyz~\&~y\leq v\implies xRvz

\text{(R3)}\ xRyz~\&~z\leq w\implies xRyw
.

A Birkhoff frame
\mathbf{W}
defines the downset algebra
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})=(D(\mathbf{W}),\bigcap,\bigcup,\cdot)
by

Y\cdot Z=\{x\in W\mid xRyz\text{ for some $y\in Y$ and $z\in Z$}\}.

The property (R1) ensures that
Y\cdot Z\in D(\mathbf{W})
.

In relevance logic
[
DR02
]
similar ternary frames are known as Routley-Meyer frames. In that setting upsets are used to recover the distributive lattice-ordered relevance algebra, and this choice implies that
J(A)
with the induced order from
A
is dually isomorphic to
(W,\leq)
. Another difference is that Routley-Meyer frames have a unary relation and axioms to ensure it is a left identity element of the
\cdot
operation.

The duality between perfect
d\ell
-magmas and Birkhoff frames is recalled below. Here we assume that
the binary operation on a complete
d\ell
-magma is a complete operator, i.e., distributes over arbitrary joins in each argument. Such algebras are also known as
nonassociative quantales
or
prequantales

[
Ros90
]
.

{thmC}

[
[
GJ20
]
]

(1)

If
\mathbf{A}
is a perfect
d\ell
-magma and
R\subseteq J(A)^{3}
is defined by
xRyz\Leftrightarrow x\leq yz

then
J(\mathbf{A})=(J(A),\leq,R)
is a Birkhoff frame, and
\mathbf{A}\cong\mathbf{D}(J(\mathbf{A}))
.

(2)

If
\mathbf{W}
is a Birkhoff frame then
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})
is a perfect
d\ell
-magma, and
\mathbf{W}\cong(J(D(\mathbf{W})),\subseteq,R_{\mathord{\downarrow}})
, where
(\mathord{\downarrow}x,\mathord{\downarrow}y,\mathord{\downarrow}z)\in R_{\mathord{\downarrow}}\Leftrightarrow xRyz
.

A ternary relation
R
is called
commutative
if
xRyz\implies xRzy
for all
x,y,z
. The justification for this terminology is provided by the following result.

Lemma 7
.

For any Birkhoff frame
\mathbf{W}
,
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})
is commutative if and only if
R
is commutative.

Lemma 8
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a Birkhoff frame. Then
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})
is idempotent if and only if

xRxx
and
(xRyz\implies x\leq y\text{ or }x\leq z)
for all
x,y,z\in W
.

Proof 4.1
.

Assume
\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})
is idempotent, and let
x\in W
. Then
\mathord{\downarrow}x\cdot\mathord{\downarrow}x=\mathord{\downarrow}x
since
\mathord{\downarrow}x\in D(\mathbf{W})
. From
x\in\mathord{\downarrow}x
we deduce
x\in\mathord{\downarrow}x\cdot\mathord{\downarrow}x
, whence it
follows that
xRyz
for some
y\in\mathord{\downarrow}x,z\in\mathord{\downarrow}x
. Therefore
xRyz
for
y\leq x,z\leq x
, which implies
xRxx
by (R2) and (R3).

Next assume
xRyz
holds. Then
x\in\mathord{\downarrow}\{y,z\}\cdot\mathord{\downarrow}\{y,z\}=\mathord{\downarrow}\{y,z\}
by idempotence.
Hence for some
w\in\{y,z\}
we have
x\leq w
, and it follows that
x\leq y
or
x\leq z
.

For the converse, assume
xRxx
and
(xRyz\implies x\leq y
or
x\leq z)
for all

x,y,z\in W
and let
X\in D(\mathbf{W})
.
From
xRxx
we obtain
X\subseteq X\cdot X
.

For the reverse inclusion, let
x\in X\cdot X
. Then
xRyz
holds for some
y,z\in X
.
By assumption
xRyz
implies
x\leq y
or
x\leq z
. Since
X
is a downset,

x\leq y\implies x\in X
and
x\leq z\implies x\in X
. Hence
X\cdot X=X
.

The previous two results are examples of correspondence theory, since they show that
an equational property on a perfect
d\ell
-magma corresponds to
a first-order condition on its Birkhoff frame.

The relational semantics of a perfect
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-magma is given by a
PQ-frame
, which
is a partially-ordered relational structure
(W,\leq,P,Q)
such that
P,Q
are binary relations
on
W
,
u\leq xPy\leq v\implies uPv
and
u\leq xQy\leq v\implies uQv
.
Relations with this property are called
weakening relations

[
KV16
,
GJ20
]
, and this
is what ensures that if we define

\mathsf{p}(Y)=\{x\mid\exists y(xPy\ \&\ y\in Y)\}

for a downset

Y
, then
\mathsf{p}
is a complete normal join-preserving operator that produces a downset,
and
P
is uniquely determined by
xPy\Leftrightarrow x\in\mathsf{p}(\mathord{\downarrow}y)
.
Similarly, a normal operator
\mathsf{q}
is defined from
Q
, and uniquely determines
Q
.
The residual
\mathsf{p}^{*}
of
\mathsf{p}
is a completely meet-preserving operator, defined
by
\mathsf{p}^{*}(Y)=\{x\mid\forall y(yPx\Rightarrow y\in Y)\}
, and likewise for
\mathsf{q}^{*}
. If
P=Q
then
we omit
Q
and refer to
(W,\leq,P)
simply as a
P
-frame
.

We now list some correspondence results for
d\ell\mathsf{p}\mathsf{q}
-magmas. We begin

Theorem 9
.

(1)

Let
(W,\leq,P,Q)
be a

A significant advantage of

Equational properties from Lemma

Lemma 10

Assume

.

(4) For

and

Recall that a ternary relation

Lemma 12

Let

5.
Weakly conservative perfect
d\ell
-magmas and Birkhoff frames

In this section we explore a special case that arises when the relations
P
and
Q
are
determined from
R
by
xPy\Leftrightarrow xRyx
and
xQy\Leftrightarrow xRxy
, i.e., the existential quantifier from the previous section is instantiated by
z=x
. We first discuss some related algebraic properties.

A binary operation
\cdot
is called
conservative
(or
quasitrivial
) if the output value is always one of the two inputs, i.e., it satisfies
xy=x\text{ or }xy=y\text{ for all }x,y\in A
. Note that this property implies idempotence.

In general a
d\ell
-magma is idempotent if and only if it satisfies
x\wedge y\leq xy\leq x\vee y
, since

x\wedge y=(x\wedge y)(x\wedge y)\leq xy\leq(x\vee y)(x\vee y)=x\vee y,

and conversely, identifying
x,y
we have
x\leq xx\leq x
.

A perfect
\ell
-magma
A
is called
weakly conservative
if it satisfies the formula

xy=x\wedge y\text{ or }xy=x\text{ or }xy=y\text{ or }xy=x\vee y\text{ for all $x,y\in J(A)$}.

So for completely join-irreducible elements
x,y
the product

xy\in\{x\wedge y,x,y,x\vee y\}
.
This is a generalization of conservativity in two ways since there are additional possibilities for
the value of
xy
and the formula only needs to hold for completely join-irreducible elements.

A typical example of a weakly conservative perfect
\ell
-magma is an atomic Boolean algebra with an idempotent binary operation
xy
. In this case the completely join-irreducible elements are the atoms of the Boolean algebra, and for any two atoms
x,y
the interval
[x\wedge y,x\vee y]\subseteq\{x\wedge y,x,y,x\vee y\}
. Since we observed previously that
x\wedge y\leq xy\leq x\vee y
it follows that
xy
can only take on one of the four values
x\wedge y,x,y,x\vee y
.

The notation
x\leq yRzw
is shorthand for
x\leq y
and
yRzw
. We also write
x\leq y,z
as an abbreviation for
x\leq y
and
x\leq z
.
A Birkhoff frame is called
weakly conservative
if it satisfies

xRyz\Leftrightarrow x\leq y,z\text{ or }x\leq yRyz\text{ or }x\leq zRyz.

This terminology is motivated by the following result.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\mathbf{W}
be a Birkhoff frame. Then
D(\mathbf{W})
is weakly conservative if and only if
\mathbf{W}
is weakly conservative.

Proof 5.1
.

We first note that weak conservativity for
D(\mathbf{W})
can be written in conjunctive form as

x\wedge y\leq xy\leq x\vee y\text{ and }(xy\leq x\text{ or }y\leq xy)\text{ and }(xy\leq y\text{ or }x\leq xy).

(1)

Likewise weak conservativity for
\mathbf{W}
in conjunctive form (on right hand side) is

wRxy\Leftrightarrow(w\leq x\text{ or }w\leq y)\text{ and }(w\leq x\text{ or }yRxy)\text{ and }(w\leq y\text{ or }xRxy).

(2)

Now assume (1) and for
w,x,y\in W
assume
wRxy
. To simplify notation, we identify elements of
W
with their principal filters in
D(\mathbf{W})
. Since
w
is join-irreducible and
xy\leq x\vee y
, it follows that
w\leq x
or
w\leq y
. Next, to prove that
w\leq x\text{ or }yRxy
,
assume
w\nleq x
. Then
xy\nleq x
, and again
xy\leq x\vee y
implies
xy\leq y
. The last conjunct is proved similarly.
Suppose now that the right hand side of (2) holds for
w,x,y\in W
. Using the original disjunctive form, there are 3 cases: if
w\leq x,y
then by (1)
w=w\wedge w\leq ww\leq xy
,
hence
wRxy
. If
w\leq x\leq Rxy
then
w\leq x\leq xy
, so again we obtain
wRxy
. The third case is similar.

Conversely, assume (2) holds and let
x,y
be join-irreducibles of
D(\mathbf{W})
. To see that
x\wedge y\leq xy
, let
w
be any join-irreducible such that
w\leq x\wedge y
, in which case
wRxy
follows from the disjunctive form of (2), hence
w\leq xy
. Since weak conservativity of
D(\mathbf{W})
implies idempotence,
xy\leq x\vee y
follows from Lemma
8
. To prove that
xy\leq x
or
y\leq xy
, assume
y\nleq xy
, whence
yRxy
does not hold. For any join-irreducible
w\leq xy
we have
wRxy
, and since (2) implies
w\leq x
or
yRxy
, we conclude
that
xy\leq x
. Finally, the conjunct
xy\leq y
or
x\leq xy
follows by symmetry of
x,y
.

Next we show that in every weakly conservative Birkhoff frame the ternary relation

R
is determined by two binary relations
P,Q
defined by
xPy\Leftrightarrow xRyx
and
xQy\Leftrightarrow xRxy
. This is simpler than the previous definitions with an existential quantifier, but they need not be weakening relations, hence they do not produce a
PQ
-frame. Instead they are axiomatized by the following conditions.

A
PQ-structure
is of the form
(W,\leq,P,Q)
where
(W,\leq)
is a poset and

(P0)
x\leq y\implies xPy

(Q0)
x\leq y\implies xQy

(P1)
x\leq y~\&~xPz\implies x\leq z\text{ or }yPz\quad

(Q1)
x\leq y~\&~xQz\implies x\leq z\text{ or }yQz

(P2)
xPy\leq z\implies xPz

(Q2)
xQy\leq z\implies xQz
.

Note that (P0) and (Q0) together with reflexivity of
\leq
imply that both
P
and
Q
are reflexive.
The following result shows that
PQ
-structures and weakly conservative Birkhoff frames are
definitionally equivalent.
This generalizes an earlier result of
[
AJ20
]
where the partial order
\leq
was assumed to be
=
.

Theorem 16
.

(1)

For a
PQ
-structure
(W,\leq,P,Q)
let
xRyz
be defined by

x\leq y,z\text{ or }x\leq yQz
or
x\leq zPy
. Then
(W,\leq,R)
is a weakly conservative Birkhoff frame and
P,Q
are recovered via

xPy\Leftrightarrow xRyx~\text{ and }~xQy\Leftrightarrow xRxy
.

(2)

For a weakly conservative Birkhoff frame
(W,\leq,R)
define

xPy\Leftrightarrow xRyx
and
xQy\Leftrightarrow xRxy
. Then
(W,\leq,P,Q)
is a
PQ
-structure and

xRyz\Leftrightarrow x\leq y,z\text{ or }x\leq yQz\text{ or }x\leq zPy
.

Proof 5.2
.

(1) Assume
(W,\leq,P,Q)
is a
PQ
-structure and let
R
be defined as above. We need to prove that
R
satisfies (R1), (R2) and (R3).

(R1) Assume
xRyz
and
w\leq x
. Then
wRyz
follows from (the expanded form of)
xRyz
by transitivity of
\leq
.

(R2) Assume
xRyz
and
y\leq w
.
By assumption
x\leq y,z\text{ or }x\leq yQz\text{ or }x\leq zPy
, so we have 3 subcases.
In the first subcase
x\leq y
implies
x\leq w
by transitivity of
\leq
, hence
x\leq w,z
.

In the second subcase
x\leq yQz
. We also have
y\leq w
, hence
x\leq w
.
A substitution instance of (Q1) is
yQz~\&~y\leq w\Rightarrow y\leq z\text{ or }wQz
,
hence
y\leq z\text{ or }wQz
. From the assumption that
x\leq y
it follows that
x\leq z\text{ or }wQz
. Since
x\leq w
holds we obtain
x\leq w,z\text{ or }x\leq wQz
.
Therefore
xRwz
holds.
In the third subcase
x\leq zPy
implies
x\leq zPw
by (P2), and again
xRyz
holds.

(R3) The argument is symmetric to the one for (R2).

To prove that
R
is weakly conservative, we show that
xPy\Leftrightarrow xRyx
and
xQy\Leftrightarrow xRxy
.
Now
xRyx
is equivalent to
x\leq y,x\text{ or }x\leq yQx\text{ or }x\leq xPy
which simplifies to
x\leq y\text{ or }xPy
, and by (P0) this is equivalent to
xPy
.
Similarly
xRxy
is equivalent to
xQy
.

(2) Assume
(W,\leq,R)
is a weakly conservative Birkhoff frame and define
xPy\Leftrightarrow xRyx
and
xQy\Leftrightarrow xRxy
. We show that (P0–P2) hold, and the arguments for (Q0–Q2) are similar.
Note that weak conservativity of
R
is equivalent to
xRyz\Leftrightarrow x\leq y,z\text{ or }x\leq yQz\text{ or }x\leq zPy.

(P0) Assume
x\leq y
. From reflexivity of
\leq
it follows that
x\leq y,x
. This implies that
xRyx
holds, and hence
xPy
.

(P1) Assume
x\leq y
and
xPz
. Then
xRzx
holds, and (R3) implies
xRzy
, or equivalently

x\leq z,y\text{ or }x\leq zQy\text{ or }x\leq yPz
. Since
x\leq y
, this disjunction simplifies
to
x\leq z\text{ or }x\leq zQy\text{ or }yPz
. Since
x\leq z
is a conjunct of the middle part,
the formula simplifies to
x\leq z
or
yPz
.

(P2) Assume
xPy\leq z
. This is equivalent to
xRyx
and
y\leq z
, so by (R2)
xRzx

8.
Conclusion

We showed that unary-determined
d\ell
-magmas have a simple algebraic
structure given by two unary operators and that their relational frames are definitionally equivalent to frames with two binary relations. The complex algebras of these frames are complete distributive lattices with completely distributive operators, hence they have residuals and can be considered Kripke semantics for unary-determined bunched implication algebras and bunched implication logic.
Associativity of the binary operator for idempotent unary-determined algebras can be checked by an identity with 2 rather than 3 variables, and for the frames by a 3-variable universal formula rather than a 6-variable universal-existential formula. All idempotent Boolean magmas are unary-determined, hence these results significantly extend the structural characterization of idempotent atomic Boolean quantales in
[
AJ20
]
and relate them to bunched implication logic. As an application we counted the number of preorder forest
P
-frames with
n
elements for which the partial order is an antichain, as well as the number of linearly ordered preorder
P
-frames. We also found all subdirectly irreducible
d\ell\mathsf{p}
-closure algebras up to cardinality 8, as well as all finite subdirectly irreducible unary-determined BI-chains and showed how the varieties they generate are related to each other by subclass inclusion.

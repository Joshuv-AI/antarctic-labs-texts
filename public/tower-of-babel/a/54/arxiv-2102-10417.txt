Abstract
. A family ℱ \mathcal{F} of elliptic curves defined over number fields is said to be typically bounded in torsion if the torsion subgroups E ​ ( F ) ​ [ tors ] E(F)[\textrm{tors}] of those elliptic curves E / F ∈ ℱ E_{/F}\in\mathcal{F} can be made uniformly bounded after removing from ℱ \mathcal{F} those whose number field degrees lie in a subset of ℤ + \mathbb{Z}^{+} with arbitrarily small upper density. For every number field F F , we prove unconditionally that the family ℰ F \mathcal{E}_{F} of elliptic curves defined over number fields and with F F -rational j j -invariant is typically bounded in torsion. For any integer d ∈ ℤ + d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+} , we also strengthen a result on typically bounding torsion for the family ℰ d \mathcal{E}_{d} of elliptic curves defined over number fields and with degree d d j j -invariant.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Bounds on torsion subgroups

Let
E_{/F}
denote an elliptic curve defined over a number field
F
. Then the Mordell–Weil theorem states that the abelian group
E(F)
of
F
-rational points on
E
is finitely generated. In particular, one has a decomposition

E(F)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r(E,F)}\oplus E(F)[\textrm{tors}]

where
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
is its
torsion subgroup over
F
.
In the present paper we prove results on asymptotic upper bounds for the sizes of these torsion subgroups.

In 1977, Mazur
[
Maz77
]
gave a complete classification of torsion subgroups
E(\mathbb{Q})[\textrm{tors}]
of all elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
. Following this, a combination of work by Kamienny, Kenku and Momose
[
KM88
]

[
Kam92a
]

[
Kam92b
]
lead to a complete classification of elliptic curve torsion subgroups
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
over all quadratic number fields
F
. One consequence of their work is that there are finitely many possibilities for torsion subgroups over all quadratic number fields, suggesting a “uniform bound” on torsion subgroups when the degree of the number field is fixed.

In 1996, Merel proved the “strong uniform boundedness conjecture” suggested previously by work of Kamienny, Kenku and Momose.

Theorem

(
[
Mer96
]
)
.

For each integer
d>0
there exists a constant
B(d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
so that for all number fields
F/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
d
and all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
one has

\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq B(d).

In lieu of Merel’s result, we can ask for the sharpest upper bound
B(d)
, i.e., the largest size of any elliptic curve torsion subgroup over all degree
d
number fields. Let us define a function which records such values: for
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we set

T(d):=\max_{E_{/F},[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d}\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}].

Only a few values of
T(d)
are currently known. Via a complete classification of torsion subgroups over degree
d\leq 3
number fields, one has
T(1)=16

[
Maz77
]
,
T(2)=24

[
KM88
]

[
Kam92a
]

[
Kam92b
]
and
T(3)=28

[
DEvH+21
]
. Lower bounds for
T(d)
are known for
4\leq d\leq 6

[
JKP06
]

[
DS17
]
. We also have explicit upper bounds on
T(d)
which are, unfortunately, greater than exponential in
d

[
Par99
]
.

Let us also define the CM-analogue of
T(d)
,

T_{\textrm{CM}}(d):=\max_{\textrm{CM}~E_{/F},[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d}\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}].

This arithmetic function records the largest size over all CM elliptic curve torsion subgroups over all degree
d
number fields.
One clearly has
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)\leq T(d)<\infty
.

Via a complete classification of CM torsion subgroups over number fields of a fixed degree, many values of
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
are known. For example, if
d\leq 13
then
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
is recorded
[
CCRS14
]
. For all primes
\ell>5
one has
T_{\textrm{CM}}(\ell)=6

[
BCS17
]
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
one has for sufficiently large primes
\ell\gg_{n}0
that
T_{\textrm{CM}}(\ell^{n})=6

[
BCP17
]
. In fact, for any odd
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
is also known
[
BP17
]
.

Bourdon, Clark and Pollack
[
BCP17
]
also treat
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
as an arithmetic function and study its behavior as
d\rightarrow\infty
. As noted above,
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)=6
for infinitely many
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

[
BCS17
]
. In fact, they show that
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)\geq 6
for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, whence the “lower order” of
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
is

\liminf_{d\rightarrow\infty}T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)=6.

Similarly, Clark and Pollack have shown the “upper order” result (Theorem 1.1
[
CP17
]
)

\limsup_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)}{d\log\log d}=\frac{e^{\gamma}\pi}{\sqrt{3}}.

Bourdon, Clark and Pollack
[
BCP17
]
also study the
typical order
of
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
, e.g., its statistical behavior away from certain sets of arbitrarily small upper density.
Recall that the
upper (asymptotic) density
of a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is

\bar{\delta}(S):=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#(S\cap[1,x])}{x}.

They prove the following typical order result on
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)
.

Theorem

(Theorem 1.1.(i)
[
BCP17
]
)
.

For all
\epsilon>0
there exists
B_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
so that

\bar{\delta}(\{d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:T_{\emph{CM}}(d)\geq B_{\epsilon}\})\leq\epsilon.

The above theorem says that for any
\epsilon>0
we choose, by removing some subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
of upper density
\leq\epsilon
, there exists a uniform bound
B_{\epsilon}
on all CM torsion subgroups over all degree
d\not\in S
number fields; writing this out,

\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq B_{\epsilon}

for any CM elliptic curve
E_{/F}
where
F
is a number field whose degree
[F:\mathbb{Q}]\not\in S
. In light of this, we say that the family of CM elliptic curves over number fields is
typically bounded in torsion.

Compare this result to the following, which shows that torsion is decidedly not typically bounded on the family of all elliptic curves over all number fields, which we denote by
\mathcal{A}(1)
.

Theorem

(Theorem 1.7
[
CMP18
]
)
.

For all
B\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
the set

\{d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:T(d)\geq B\}

is cofinite in
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, whence its upper density is equal to 1.

1.2.
Typically bounding torsion for other families

Despite the failure of
\mathcal{A}(1)
to be typically bounded in torsion, we saw that the proper subfamily
\mathcal{A}_{\textrm{CM}}(1)
of CM elliptic curves over number fields is. We will see there are also other familiar subfamilies of
\mathcal{A}(1)
which are typically bounded in torsion.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{{E_{i}}_{/F_{i}}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of elliptic curves
E_{i}
defined over number fields
F_{i}
. From here on out, we assume that any such
\mathcal{F}
is “closed under rational isomorphism”, e.g., if
{E_{1}}_{/F}\in\mathcal{F}
and
{E_{2}}_{/F}
is an elliptic curve with
E_{2}\cong_{F}E_{1}
, then
{E_{2}}_{/F}\in\mathcal{F}
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
typically bounded in torsion
if the torsion subgroups
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
of elliptic curves
E_{/F}\in\mathcal{F}
can be made uniformly bounded after removing those elements whose number field degrees all lie in a certain subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
of arbitrarily small upper density. Formally stated,
\mathcal{F}
is typically bounded in torsion if for all
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
B_{\epsilon}>0
so that the set

\{d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:\exists E_{/F}\in\mathcal{F}~\textrm{so that }[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d~\textrm{and }\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\geq B_{\epsilon}\}

has upper density at most
\epsilon
.

For a family
\mathcal{F}
of elliptic curves, one can define an
\mathcal{F}
-analogue of
T(d)
,

T_{\mathcal{F}}(d):=\max_{E_{/F}\in\mathcal{F},[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d}\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}].

By Merel
[
Mer96
]
one has
T_{\mathcal{F}}(d)<\infty
. We see that
T_{\textrm{CM}}(d)=T_{\mathcal{A}_{\textrm{CM}}(1)}(d)
. It is clear that
\mathcal{F}
is typically bounded in torsion iff for all
\epsilon>0
there exists a constant
B_{\epsilon}>0
so that

\overline{\delta}(\{d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}:T_{\mathcal{F}}(d)\geq B_{\epsilon}\})\leq\epsilon.

Compare this to Theorem 1.1.(i)
[
BCP17
]
above.

One important family of elliptic curves is the “base extension” family of all elliptic curves
E_{/F}
which are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. For example, Lozano-Robledo
[
LR13
]
gives an explicit bound on all primes which divide
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
, and this bound is linear in the degree
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. On the other hand, González-Jiménez and Najman
[
GJN20
]
show that
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]=E(\mathbb{Q})[\textrm{tors}]
when
2,3,5,7\nmid[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.
A (subtly) larger family is that of elliptic curves
E_{/F}
with
\mathbb{Q}
-rational
j
-invariant. For such a family, Propp
[
Pro18
]
provides (conditional) bounds on prime divisors of
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
when
[F:\mathbb{Q}]
is fixed. Clark and Pollack
[
CP18
]
also show that for each
\epsilon>0
there is a constant
C(\epsilon)>0
for which one has polynomial bounds
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq C(\epsilon)[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{\frac{5}{2}+\epsilon}
.

Our primary family of study is a natural generalization of the above. For a fixed number field
F
, we consider the family of elliptic curves with
F
-rational
j
-invariant that are defined over number fields,

\mathcal{E}_{F}:=\{E_{/L}:[L:\mathbb{Q}]<\infty,j(E)\in F\}.

Clark, Milosevic and Pollack study the subfamily
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{F}
of elliptic curves
E_{/L}\in\mathcal{E}_{F}
with
L\supseteq F

[
CMP18
]
.
They show (Theorem 1.8
[
CMP18
]
) that torsion is typically bounded in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{F}
, provided
F
contains no Hilbert class fields of imaginary quadratic fields and that the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) is true. By a brief twisting argument, their proof also shows that
\mathcal{E}_{F}
is typically bounded in torsion when one imposes these conditions.

Our main result is a generalization of Theorem 1.8
[
CMP18
]
: we prove it for
\mathcal{E}_{F}
unconditionally.

Theorem 1
.

For any number field
F
, torsion is typically bounded on the family
\mathcal{E}_{F}
of elliptic curves
E
defined over number fields and whose
j
-invariant
j(E)\in F
.

1.3.
Strategy of the proof

Our proof of Theorem
1
will be broken down into several steps, following
[
CMP18
]
. Let us introduce a
condition P on a family
\mathcal{F}
of elliptic curves (this is condition P2 in
[
CMP18
]
).

P:

There exists a constant
c:=c(\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all primes
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and all
E_{/F}\in\mathcal{F}
, if
E(F)
has a point of order
\ell
then

\ell-1\mid c\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}].

For an integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
let us consider the family
\mathcal{E}_{d}
of elliptic curves over number fields with degree
d

j
-invariant,

\mathcal{E}_{d}:=\{E_{/F}:[F:\mathbb{Q}]<\infty,[\mathbb{Q}(j(E)):\mathbb{Q}]=d\}.

By Theorems 3.2.b and 3.5
[
CMP18
]
any subfamily of a finite union of
\mathcal{E}_{d}
will be typically bounded in torsion if and only if the subfamily satisfies condition P. In particular, from the containment

\mathcal{E}_{F}\subseteq\bigcup_{d=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}_{d},

to prove Theorem
1
it suffices to show that
\mathcal{E}_{F}
satisfies condition P.

Following the proof of Theorem 4.3
[
CMP18
]
, to show that condition P holds for
\mathcal{E}_{F}
it suffices to construct a constant
c:=c(F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for any prime
\ell\gg_{F}0
and any elliptic curve
E_{/F}
with an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny,
one has for all nontrivial
R\in E[\ell]
that

(1)

\ell-1\mid c\cdot[F(R):\mathbb{Q}].

Since
E
has an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, the image
G
of its mod
-\ell
Galois representation is conjugate to a subgroup of upper triangular matrices in
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
.
We will use this fact to compare the orbits of this representation acting on the standard unit vectors, to the orbits of its semisimplification.

First, a careful analysis of the description of our isogeny character from case 1 of Theorem
3
(see the following section) will allow us to relate the semisimplification of
G
to the mod
-\ell
Galois representation of some CM elliptic curve
E^{\prime}_{/F}
with
F
-rational CM. By an orbit-divisibility argument, we will show that (
1
) holds when we replace
E
with
E^{\prime}
, and conclude that such a divisibility holds for
E
with constant
c:=864
. Following this, we will show that the same constant also works when in case 2 of Theorem
3
.

It is worth noting that Lozano-Robledo
[
LR18
]
uses a similar analysis of Theorem
3
to deduce, under certain hypotheses, degree-linear bounds on the order of any rational prime power torsion point. In fact, Theorem 1.9
[
LR18
]
proves (
1
) in the form of an inequality. However, divisibility allows us to apply Theorem 3.2
[
CMP18
]
and conclude that torsion is typically bounded on
\mathcal{E}_{F}
.

1.4.
An additional result

In our proof of Theorem
1
, the constant
c:=864
for which (
1
) holds is independent of the choice of base field
F
. We will use this to improve Theorem 1.10
[
CMP18
]
.

We denote by SI
(d)
the
strong isogeny conjecture in degree
d
, which asserts that there exists a prime
\ell_{0}:=\ell_{0}(d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
so that for all primes
\ell>\ell_{0}
there do not exist non-CM elliptic curves
E_{/F}
with an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny when
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d
. The strong isogeny conjecture SI
(d)
would follow from a stronger conjecture of Serre which has been used to prove other results, see for example Theorem 1.6
[
BELOV19
]
.

Let us denote by LV(
d
) the hypothesis that the set
S_{F}
of primes from Theorem
3
(see the next section)
depends only on
d
for all number fields
F
of degree
d
. One may check that LV(
d
) is a weaker hypothesis than SI(
d
).

Theorem 2
.

Fix an integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. If
LV
(d)
holds, then the family

\mathcal{E}_{d}:=\{E_{/F}:[F:\mathbb{Q}]<\infty,[\mathbb{Q}(j(E)):\mathbb{Q}]=d\}

is typically bounded in torsion.

The proof of Theorem
2
is similar to the proof of Theorem 1.8
[
CMP18
]
, which boils down to the proof of Theorem 4.3
[
CMP18
]
. By Theorem 3.5
[
CMP18
]
, to show that
\mathcal{E}_{d}
is typically bounded in torsion it suffices to show it satisfies condition P. In turn, this reduces to showing that for any prime
\ell\gg_{d}0
one has a constant
c:=c(d)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for any non-CM elliptic curve
{E}_{/\mathbb{Q}(j(E))}
with a
\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, the divisibility

\ell-1\mid c\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(j(E))(R):\mathbb{Q}]

holds for all nontrivial
R\in E[\ell]
. In
[
CMP18
]
, the authors cite SI(

) to exclude this case. But if one assumes LV

instead, then our proof of Theorem

.

1.6.
Definitions and notation

Given an extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
, we let
G_{F}:=\textrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
denote the absolute Galois group of
F
. For an integer
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let us write the mod
-N
cyclotomic character of
F
as

\chi_{N}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

When
N=\ell
is prime, we will write
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}:=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
.

For an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
, let us write its
mod
-N
Galois representation as

\rho_{E,N}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{Aut}(E[N]).

If
\{P,Q\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
-basis for
E[N]
, then when working with explicit matrix representations we may also write

\rho_{E,N,P,Q}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\textrm{GL}_{2}(N),

where
\textrm{GL}_{2}(N):=\textrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
is the general linear group of degree 2 over
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.

Suppose
E_{/F}
has an
F
-rational cyclic
N
-isogeny
C_{N}
, i.e., a one-dimensional
G_{F}
-invariant submodule of
E[N]
. Then one has an
isogeny character
which describes the action of
G_{F}
on
C_{N}
,

r\colon G_{F}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

Given a group homomorphism
f\colon G\rightarrow H
, we sometimes write
\textrm{im}\thinspace f
for its image
f(G)
.

2.
Isogeny Characters

A crucial component of the proof of Theorem
1
is an analysis of the description of our isogeny character from a result of Larson and Vaintrob
[
LV14
]
.

Theorem 3

(Theorem 1
[
LV14
]
)
.

Let
F
be a number field. Then there is a finite set of primes
S_{F}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all primes
\ell\not\in S_{F}
, if
E_{/F}
is an elliptic curve with an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny, then for the corresponding isogeny character
r\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
one of the following holds:

1. There exists a CM elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
defined over
F
such that its CM field
K:=\emph{End}(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is contained in
F
. Furthermore, there exists a character
\psi^{\prime}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}^{\times}
for which we have both similarity

(\rho_{E^{\prime},\ell}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}\overline{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}})(G_{F})\sim\emph{im}\begin{bmatrix}\psi^{\prime}&*\\
0&\chi_{\ell}\psi^{\prime-1}\end{bmatrix}

and equality

r^{12}=\psi^{\prime 12}.

2. GRH fails for
F(\sqrt{-\ell})
, and we have

r^{12}=\chi_{\ell}^{6}.

As per the theory of complex multiplication, the ring class field of an order
\mathcal{O}
in an imaginary quadratic field
K
will equal
K(j(E^{\prime}))
for any
\mathcal{O}
-CM elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
, and all such fields will contain the Hilbert class field of
K
. In particular, for
F
not to contain Hilbert class fields of imaginary quadratic fields is equivalent to there being no CM elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
defined over
F
for which
\textrm{End}(E^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\subseteq F
. For such a field
F
, if one also assumes that GRH is true then Theorem
3
tells us that for
\ell\gg_{F}0
there are no
F
-rational
\ell
-isogenies on any elliptic curve.
It is this observation, coupled with the fact that if
\mathcal{F}
satisfies condition P then one can exclude finitely many primes
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and still have condition P hold for
\mathcal{F}
, which shows us that
\mathcal{E}_{F}
satisfies condition P – this is outlined in Theorem 4.3.b
[
CMP18
]
. In particular, torsion is typically bounded on
\mathcal{E}_{F}
, conditionally.

To prove Theorem
1
we will not assume that GRH is true nor that
F
does not contain the Hilbert class field of any imaginary quadratic field. Instead, we will leverage the extra information from Theorem
3
on our possible isogeny characters to construct a constant
c
for which (
1
) holds for all
\ell\gg_{F}0
and all
E_{/F}
with an
F
-rational
\ell
-isogeny.

3.
Orbits Under the Galois Representation

In our study of the mod
-\ell
Galois representations of an elliptic curve
E_{/F}
, it will help to understand the orbits of the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[G_{F}]
-module
E[\ell]
under various subgroups of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
.

3.1.
The semisimplification of an upper triangular subgroup of
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)

Fix a prime
\ell
, and suppose
G\subseteq\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
is a subgroup of upper triangular matrices. Let us consider its
semisimplification

G^{\textrm{ss}}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell):\exists b\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}~\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G\right\}.

The group
\textrm{GL}_{2}(\ell)
acts on the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-vector space
V
spanned by the column vectors
e_{1}:=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}
and
e_{2}:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
1\end{bmatrix}
via multiplication on the left. Consequently, its subgroups
G
and
G^{\textrm{ss}}
also act on
V
via left multiplication.

Ultimately, we will compare divisibility of the sizes of such orbits from the action of
\textrm{Gal}(F(E[\ell])/F)
on
E[\ell]^{\bullet}:=E[\ell]\smallsetminus\{O\}
.
The following lemma is an important step towards such a comparison.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be prime, and let
G
be an upper triangular subgroup of
\emph{GL}_{2}(\ell)
. Then
G^{\emph{ss}}\subseteq G
or
G
is diagonalizable.

Proof.

We will break the proof into three cases. In the first two cases, we will show containment
G^{\textrm{ss}}\subseteq G
.

Case 1:

G
contains a non-diagonal matrix with repeated eigenvalues. Such a matrix is of the form
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}
with
b\neq 0
. For such a matrix
\gamma
, its power
\gamma^{\ell-1}
equals

(2)

\begin{bmatrix}1&\lambda\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in G

where
\lambda:=(\ell-1)ba^{\ell-2}\neq 0
. It follows that
G
contains the transvection

\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\gamma^{A}\in G,

where
A\in\mathbb{Z}
is taken so that
A\equiv\lambda^{-1}\pmod{\ell}
.
We conclude that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}^{n}\in G.

It follows that
G^{\textrm{ss}}\subseteq G
. Indeed, for any element
\delta:=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G
we check that

\delta\begin{bmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}a&an+b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix},

so taking
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
n\equiv-ba^{-1}\pmod{\ell}
shows that
\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G
.

Case 2:

G
is not commutative. Then choosing any
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in G
with nontrivial commutator, their commutator
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{2}^{-1}
must be of the form
\begin{bmatrix}1&\lambda\\

The following lemma describes the orbits under a diagonal subgroup.

Lemma 5
.

Let

be a subgroup of diagonal matrices, with diagonal characters

and

. Then the number of orbits of the form

(resp.

(resp.

First, we claim that there are
I_{1}:=[\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}:\textrm{im}\thinspace\chi_{1}]
distinct orbits of the form

\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}/\textrm{im}\thinspace\chi_{1}\rightarrow\{\textrm{Orbits }\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(ae_{1}):a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}\},~\overline{a}\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(ae_{1})

is a well-defined bijection:

(1)

Well-defined: if
a=\chi_{1}(\gamma)b
then
\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}ab^{-1}&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}
for some

\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(be_{1})=\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(\gamma be_{1})=\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(ae_{1}).

(2)

Injective: suppose that
\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(ae_{1})=\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}}(be_{1})

\gamma\in G^{\prime}
we have

, whence we have
\chi_{1}(\gamma)=ab^{-1}
, i.e.,

in
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}/\textrm{im}\thinspace\chi_{1}
.

(3)

Surjective: obvious.

We conclude that
e_{1}
contributes

distinct orbits of the form

with

\#\textrm{im}\thinspace\chi_{2}

when

Since the orbits above partition

we conclude that

∎

3.3.
Uniform orbit divisibility

Suppose that a group
G
acts on a finite set
X
. For an element
x\in X
let us use
\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)
to denote its orbit.
Let us say that an integer
M\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

uniformly divides

G
-orbits if there exists an integer
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all
x\in X
one has

M\mid c\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(x).

Clearly, any integer
M>0
will uniformly divide
G
-orbits if we take
c:=M
. The relevance of uniform divisibility comes from
the following lemma, which will allow us to prove (
1
) when we replace the image
G
of our mod
-\ell
Galois representation with a particular subgroup, and then “carry over” this constant
c
to the divisibility (
1
) for
E
. As will be shown in the proof of (
1
), such a constant will not depend on
M:=\ell-1
, nor on
E
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
G
be a group which acts on a finite set
X
on the left, and let
H\subseteq G
be a finite index subgroup.

(1)

If for an integer
M>0
there is a constant
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all
x\in X
we have
M\mid c\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{H}(x)
, then we also have for all
x\in X
the divisibility
M\mid c\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)
.

(2)

If for an integer
M>0
there is a constant
C\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all
x\in X
we have
M\mid C\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)
, then we also have for all
x\in X
the divisibility
M\mid C\cdot[G:H]\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{H}(x)
.

Proof.

Let us write the left coset representatives of
H
as
g_{1}^{-1},\ldots,g_{[G:H]}^{-1}
. Then one has

\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{[G:H]}\mathcal{O}_{H}(g_{i}x).

It follows that for all
x\in X
one has

\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{[G:H]}\#\mathcal{O}_{H}(g_{i}x),

from which the first part follows. The second part follows from the fact that for each
x\in X
one has
\#\mathcal{O}_{G}(x)\mid[G:H]\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{H}(x)
.
∎

(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}
-orbits with constant
864[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. In fact, we will show that for all
v\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle^{\bullet}
one has

\ell-1\mid 36[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot\#\mathcal{O}_{(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}}(v).

By Lemma
5
the sizes of orbits of the form
\mathcal{O}_{(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}}(ae_{1})
and
\mathcal{O}_{(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}}(de_{2})
with
ad\neq 0
are
\#r^{6}(G_{F})
, and the rest of the orbits
\mathcal{O}_{(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}}(ae_{1}+de_{2})
with
ad\neq 0
share the same size as the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}}(e_{1}+e_{2})
. Since the action under
G^{\textrm{ss}}
is scalar, it is clear that
\#\mathcal{O}_{(G^{\textrm{ss}})^{6}}(e_{1}+e_{2})=\#r^{6}(G_{F})
. Therefore, to prove (
1
) with constant
c:=864
it suffices to show that

(9)

\ell-1\mid 864[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot\#r^{6}(G_{F}).

Since
r(G_{F})
is cyclic, we observe that

\#r^{12}(G_{F})=\frac{\#r(G_{F})}{\gcd(12,\#r(G_{F}))}.

Since
\chi_{\ell}(G_{F})
is also cyclic, the image of its sixth power has size

\#\chi_{\ell}^{6}(G_{F})=\frac{\ell-1}{\gcd(6,\ell-1)}.

Since
\chi_{\ell}^{6}=r^{12}
, we compare these sizes and find that

\#\chi_{\ell}(G_{F})\cdot\gcd(12,\#r(G_{F}))=\gcd(6,\#\chi_{\ell}(G_{F}))\cdot\#r(G_{F}),

whence we have

\#\chi_{\ell}(G_{F})\mid 6\cdot\#r(G_{F}).

Since we also have

\#r(G_{F})=\#r^{6}(G_{F})\cdot\gcd(6,\#r(G_{F}))

it follows that

\#\chi_{\ell}(G_{F})\mid 36\cdot\#r^{6}(G_{F}).

Finally, since
\chi_{\ell}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
is surjective, we have
[\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}:\chi_{\ell}(G_{F})]\mid[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, and so we conclude that

\ell-1\mid 36[F:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot\#r^{6}(G_{F}).

This proves (
9
), which concludes our proof of Theorem
1
.

Quantum Curve and the First Painlevé Equation
Kohei IWAKI † and Axel SAENZ ‡

Abstract
We show that the topological recursion for the (semi-classical) spectral curve of the first Painlevé equation P I P_{\rm I} gives a WKB solution for the isomonodromy problem for P I P_{\rm I} . In other words, the isomonodromy system is a quantum curve in the sense of [Dumitrescu O., Mulase M., Lett. Math. Phys. 104 (2014), 635–671, arXiv:1310.6022] and [Dumitrescu O., Mulase M., arXiv:1411.1023].

1
Introduction

Painlevé transcendents
are remarkable
special functions which appear in many areas of
mathematics and physics (e.g.,
[
17
]
). These are solutions
of certain nonlinear ordinary differential equations
known as
Painlevé equations
. These equations were
discovered by Painlevé and Gambier more than 100 years ago
[
34
]
, and solutions have the so-called

Painlevé property
; i.e., any movable singularity must be a pole.
One particular property of the Painlevé equations is
existence of the
Lax pair
; that is,
each Painlevé equation describes an
isomonodromic deformation

of a certain meromorphic linear ordinary differential equation
[
20
,
21
]
. The monodromy data of the linear ODEs
gives a conserved quantity of the Painlevé transcendents.
The
Riemann–Hilbert method
, as well as
exact WKB analysis

are applied to analyze the properties of Painlevé transcendents
[
4
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
36
]
.

On the other hand,
quantum curves
attract both mathematicians
and physicists since they are expected to encode
the information of many quantum topological invariants, such as
Gromov–Witten invariants, quantum knot invariants etc.
These are concieved in physics literature including
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
18
]
.
A quantum curve is an ordinary differential (or difference)
equation containing a formal parameter
\hbar

(which plays the role of the Planck constant),
like a Schrödinger equation. The quantum invariants appear
in the coefficients of the

WKB
(
Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin
)
solution

of the quantum curve.

The Eynard–Orantin’s
topological recursion

introduced in
[
16
]
is closely related to both of
the quantum curves and Painlevé equations (and many other topics).
Topological recursion is a recursive algorithm to
compute the
1/N
-expansion of the correlation functions
and the partition function of matrix models from its

spectral curve
, and it is generalized to any algebraic curve
which may not come from a matrix model.
In this context, quantum curves were first discussed in
[
6
]

for the Airy spectral curve, and generalized to spectral curves
with various backgrounds (see
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
29
]

and the survey article
[
31
]
).
The spectral curves are recovered as the
semi-classical limit

\hbar\rightarrow 0
of the quantum curves.
Moreover, the topological recursion is also closely related to
integrability
[
5
,
7
,
19
]

as is the relationship between matrix models
and integrable systems
[
9
,
28
]
.

The aim of this paper is to relate quantum curves and
the
first Painlevé equation
with a formal parameter
\hbar

\displaystyle P_{\rm I}\colon\ \hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}q}{dt^{2}}=6q^{2}+t.

The (semi-classical) spectral curve for the isomonodormy
system associated with
P_{\rm I}
is given by

\displaystyle y^{2}=4(x-q_{0})^{2}(x+2q_{0}),

(1.1)

where
q_{0}=q_{0}(t)
is an explicit function of
t
.
This is a family of algebraic curves in
(x,y)
-space
parametrized by
t
. (The curve (
1.1
)
appeared in
[
16
, Section 10.6]
as the spectral curve of
(3,2)-minimal model.)
Our main result claims that,
starting from the spectral curve (
1.1
),
its
quantization
through the Eynard–Orantin’s topological
recursion (in the sense of
[
11
,
12
]
)
recovers the
whole
isomonodoromy system for
P_{\rm I}
.

The precise statement of our main theorem is as follows.
Let
W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
be the

Eynard–Orantin differential
of type
(g,n)

defined from the spectral curve (
1.1
)
(see Section
3.1
).
These are meromorphic multi-differential forms,
and
z_{i}
’s are copies of a coordinate on the
spectral curve (
1.1
).

W_{g,n}
’s also depend on
t
since the
spectral curve depends on
t
.
Then, our main result states the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

The following
WKB-type formal series
\psi(x,t,\hbar)
defined by

\displaystyle\psi(x(z),t,\hbar):=\exp\left(\sum_{g\geq 0,n\geq 1}\hbar^{2g-2+n}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int^{z}_{\bar{z}}\cdots\int^{z}_{\bar{z}}W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\right)

(1.2)

satisfies the isomonodromy system associated with
P_{\rm I}
.
Here
x(z)
is an explicit rational function of
z
which appears
in the parametrization of the spectral curve (
1.1
),
and
\bar{z}=-z
.

The above theorem tells us that the isomonodoromy system
associated with
P_{\rm I}
is a quantum curve,
and its particular WKB solution is constructed
by the topological recursion as (
1.2
).
The main differences between our theorem
and previous results on quantum curves are the following:

•

Our quantum curve is a restriction of a certain

partial differential equation
(a holonomic system).

•

There are
infinitely many
\hbar
-correction terms

in the quantum curve, and these correction terms
are essentially given by the
asymptotic expansion

of the solution of
P_{\rm I}
for
\hbar\rightarrow 0
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we briefly review some known facts about
P_{\rm I}
together
with an important result on the WKB analysis of
isomonodromic systems developed by Kawai–Takei
[
25
,
26
]
.
Our main theorem will be formulated in Section
3

after recalling the notion of topological recursion.
We will give a proof of the main results in Section
4
.

Remark 1.2
.

After writing the draft version of this paper,
the authors were informed that B. Eynard also
has the same result which has not been published yet,
but presented in
[
14
]
.
See also
[
15
, Chapter 5]
.

2
The first Painlevé equation and isomonodromy system

Let us consider the
first Painlevé equation

with a formal parameter
\hbar
:

\displaystyle P_{\rm I}\colon\ \hbar^{2}\frac{d^{2}q}{dt^{2}}=6q^{2}+t.

The equation
P_{\rm I}
is obtained from

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\tilde{q}}{d\tilde{t}^{2}}=6\tilde{q}^{2}+\tilde{t}

via the rescaling

\tilde{t}=\hbar^{-4/5}t
,

\tilde{q}=\hbar^{-2/5}q
.
We will regard
\hbar
as a small parameter (i.e., Planck’s constant),
and investigate a particular formal solution
of
P_{\rm I}
which has an
\hbar
-expansion.

2.1
Formal solution of
\boldsymbol{P_{\rm I}}

P_{\rm I}
has the following formal power series solution:

\displaystyle q(t,\hbar)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2n}q_{2n}(t)=q_{0}(t)+\hbar^{2}q_{2}(t)+\hbar^{4}q_{4}(t)+\cdots.

(2.1)

It contains only even order terms of
\hbar
since
P_{\rm I}

is invariant under
\hbar\mapsto-\hbar
.
The leading term
q_{0}=q_{0}(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle 6q_{0}^{2}+t=0,\qquad\text{hence}~q_{0}(t)=\sqrt{-t/6},

(2.2)

and the subleading terms are recursively determined by

\displaystyle q_{2(k+1)}(t)=\frac{1}{12q_{0}(t)}\left(\frac{d^{2}q_{2k}}{dt^{2}}(t)-6\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k+1,\,k_{i}>0}q_{2k_{1}}(t)q_{2k_{2}}(t)\right),\qquad k\geq 1.

(2.3)

As we will see, the coefficients of the formal series appearing in
this paper are multivalued functions of
t
and are defined
on the Riemann surface of
q_{0}
. Thus, in what follows,
we may use
q_{0}
instead of
t
when we express coefficients.

The relation (
2.3
) implies

\displaystyle q_{2k}=c_{2k}q_{0}^{1-5k},\qquad c_{2k}\in{\mathbb{C}}.

It is obvious that the coefficients
q_{2k}(t)
have
a singularity at
q_{0}=0
(i.e.,
t=0
).
This special point is called a
turning point
of
P_{\rm I}

[
25
, Definition 2.1]
(see also
[
26
, Section 4]
).
Throughout the paper, we assume the following:

Assumption 2.1
.

The independent variable
t
of
P_{\rm I}

lies on a domain that doesn’t contain the origin.

Remark 2.2
.

The formal solution (
2.1
) is called a

0
-parameter solution
of
P_{\rm I}
in
[
26
]

since it doesn’t contain free parameters.
More general formal solutions having
one or two free parameters (called 1- or 2-parameter solutions)
are constructed in
[
4
]
for all Painlevé equations
of second order.
See also
[
3
]
for a construction of general formal
solutions of
higher
order Painlevé equations.

Remark 2.3
.

The formal solution (
2.1
) is in fact a
divergent
series.
However,
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that
the formal solution is
Borel summable
when

q_{0}
satisfies
q_{0}\neq 0
and

\arg q_{0}\notin\{\frac{2\ell}{5}\pi\,|\,\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.
The exceptional set is called the
Stokes curve
of
P_{\rm I}
.
(See
[
25
, Definition 2.1]
for the notion of
Stokes curves of Painlevé equations
with a small parameter
\hbar
.)
That is, there exists a function
which is analytic in
\hbar
on a sectorial domain
with the center at the origin (which is also analytic in
t
)
such that (
2.1
) is the asymptotic expansion
of the function for
\hbar\rightarrow 0
in the sector.
The analytic function is called the
Borel sum

of the formal series (
2.1
), and
it gives an analytic solution of
P_{\rm I}

(see
[
8
]
for Borel summation method).
This particular asymptotic solution obtained by
the Borel summation method is called
the
tri-tronquée solution

of
P_{\rm I}
(see
[
22
]
), and the

non-linear Stokes phenomena
on Stokes curves
are analyzed by
[
17
,
24
,
36
]
.

2.2
Isomonodromy system and the
\boldsymbol{\tau}
-function

It is known that
P_{\rm I}
describes the compatibility condition
for the following system of linear PDEs (cf.
[
21
, Appendix C]
):

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial x}=A\Psi,\qquad\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=B\Psi,

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}p&4(x-q)\\
\displaystyle x^{2}+qx+q^{2}+\frac{t}{2}&-p\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}+q&0\end{pmatrix}.

The compatibility condition

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}-\hbar\frac{\partial B}{\partial x}+[A,B]=0

is equivalent to the following Hamiltonian system

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{dq}{dt}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p},\qquad\hbar\frac{dp}{dt}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q},

(2.5)

where the (time-dependent) Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=H(q,p,t):=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}-2q^{3}-tq.

We can easily check that (
2.5
) and
P_{\rm I}
are equivalent.
The above system of linear ODEs
is called the
isomonodromy system
associated with
P_{\rm I}

(see
[
20
,
21
]
).

Let
(q,p)=(q(t,\hbar),p(t,\hbar))
be a formal power series
solution of the Hamiltonian system (
2.5
); that is,

q(t,\hbar)
is the formal solution (
2.1
)
of
P_{\rm I}
, and

\displaystyle p(t,\hbar)=\hbar\frac{dq(t,\hbar)}{dt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2n+1}p_{2n+1}(t).

The corresponding Hamiltonian function is denoted by

\displaystyle\sigma(t,\hbar):=H\big(q(t,\hbar),p(t,\hbar),t\big).

(2.6)

We can check that (
2.6
)
is invariant under
\hbar\mapsto-\hbar
, and hence
it has the following expansion:

\displaystyle\sigma(t,\hbar)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2n}\sigma_{2n}(t).

(2.7)

Definition 2.4

(
[
20
,
32
]
)
.

The
\tau
-function

(corresponding to the formal solution (
2.1
))
of
P_{\rm I}
is defined by

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\log\tau(t,\hbar)=\sigma(t,\hbar)

(2.8)

up to constant.

The
\tau
-function can also be defined in terms of
a solution of (
2.4
)
[
20
]
(see also
Appendix
A
).
The expansion (
2.7
) implies that the
\tau
-function (
2.8
) has an expansion of the form

\displaystyle\log\tau(t,\hbar)=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2}\tau_{2g}(t).

2.3
Spectral curve

In what follows, we assume that the formal solution

(q(t,\hbar),p(t,\hbar))
of (
2.5
)
constructed above is substituted into the coefficients
of the isomonodromy system (
2.4
).
Then, the coefficients of the isomonodromy system has the following

\hbar
-expansions:

\displaystyle A=A_{0}(x,t)+\hbar A_{1}(x,t)+\hbar^{2}A_{2}(x,t)+\cdots,

\displaystyle B=B_{0}(x,t)+\hbar B_{1}(x,t)+\hbar^{2}B_{2}(x,t)+\cdots,

whose top terms are given by

\displaystyle A_{0}(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&4(x-q_{0})\\
\displaystyle x^{2}+q_{0}x+q_{0}^{2}+\frac{t}{2}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad B_{0}(x,t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}+q_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Observe that, since
q_{0}
satisfies (
2.2
),
the algebraic curve defined by

\displaystyle\det(y-A_{0}(x,t))=y^{2}-4(x-q_{0})^{2}(x+2q_{0})=0

(2.9)

has
genus
0
. Actually, this gives a family of algebraic
curves in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}_{(x,y)}

parametrized by
t
. Since we have assumed that

t\neq 0
,
x=q_{0}
and
x=-2q_{0}
are distinct.

Definition 2.5
.

We call the algebraic curve (
2.9
)

the semi-classical spectral curve
, or

the spectral curve

of (the first equation of) the isomonodromy system (
2.4
).

Remark 2.6
.

It is shown in
[
25
, Proposition 1.3]
that, for all (second order)
Painlevé equations with a formal parameter
\hbar
,
the semi-classical spectral curves corresponding to
the same type of formal power series solution
as (
2.1
) have
genus
0
.

Remark 2.7
.

Since we are taking the semi-classical limit
(i.e., top term in
\hbar
-expansion),
our spectral curve (
2.9
) is different from
usual spectral curves for isomonodromic deformation equations
discussed, e.g., in
[
33
,
35
]
.
The spectral curves in the above papers have
higher genus
.
Recently, Nakamura
[
30
]
investigates
the geometry of genus
2
spectral curves which appear
in an
autonomous limit
of the

4th order Painlevé equations
,
and use them to classify the Painlevé equations.
See
[
27
]
for the list of 4th order Painlevé equations.

2.4
WKB analysis of isomonodromy system in scalar form

Denote the unknown vector function of (
2.4
)
by
\Psi={}^{t}(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
.
Then,
\psi=\psi_{1}
satisfies the
following scalar version of isomonodromy system

\displaystyle\left(\left(\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+f\left(\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)+g\right)\psi=0,

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2(x-q)}\left(\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}-p\psi\right),

(2.10)

where

\displaystyle f=f(x,t,\hbar):=-\operatorname{tr}A-\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\log A_{12}=-\hbar\frac{1}{x-q},

\displaystyle g=g(x,t,\hbar):=\det A-\hbar\frac{\partial A_{11}}{\partial x}+\hbar A_{11}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\log A_{12}

\displaystyle\hphantom{g}{}=-\big(4x^{3}+2tx+p^{2}-4q^{3}-2tq\big)+\hbar\frac{p}{x-q}.

The coefficients of
f
and
g
have an
\hbar
-expansion since

q
and
p
are contained in them

\displaystyle f=-\hbar\frac{1}{x-q_{0}}+\hbar^{3}\frac{1}{1728q_{0}^{4}(x-q_{0})^{2}}+\hbar^{5}\frac{49x-51q_{0}}{5971968q_{0}^{9}(x-q_{0})^{3}}+\cdots,

(2.11)

\displaystyle g=-4(x-q_{0})^{2}(x+2q_{0})-\hbar^{2}\frac{x+11q_{0}}{144q_{0}^{2}(x-q_{0})}-\hbar^{4}\frac{7x^{2}+34q_{0}x-53q_{0}^{2}}{248832q_{0}^{7}(x-q_{0})^{2}}+\cdots.

(2.12)

The top term of
g
appears in the defining equation of
the spectral curve (
2.9
),
and its zeros are called
turning points

of the first equation of (
2.10
)
in the WKB analysis.
In particular, under the assumption
t\neq 0
, there is

•

a
simple
turning point at
x=-2q_{0}

which is a branch point
of the spectral curve (
2.9
), and

•

a
double
turning point at
x=q_{0}

which is a singular point of
the spectral curve (
2.9
).

Consider the
Riccati equation

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\left(P^{2}+\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right)+f\hbar P+g=0.

(2.13)

This is equivalent to the first equation
in (
2.10
) by

\displaystyle\psi=\exp\left(\int^{x}Pdx\right),\qquad\text{i.e.},\quad P=\frac{1}{\psi}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}.

Let

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{m-1}P^{(\pm)}_{m}(x,t)

be the formal solutions of (
2.13
) with the top term

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{0}(x,t)=\pm 2(x-q_{0})\sqrt{x+2q_{0}}.

The coefficients
P^{(\pm)}_{m}(x,t)
are recursively determined by

\displaystyle 2P^{(\pm)}_{0}P^{(\pm)}_{m+1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=m+1\\
a,b\geq 1\end{subarray}}P^{(\pm)}_{a}\big(P^{(\pm)}_{b}+f_{b}\big)+\frac{\partial P^{(\pm)}_{m}}{\partial x}+g_{m+1}=0\qquad\text{for}\quad m\geq 0,

(2.14)

where
f_{a}
and
g_{a}
are the coefficient of
\hbar^{a}

in
f
and
g
, respectively. Explicit forms of the first few terms
are given by

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{1}=-\frac{1}{4(x+2q_{0})},\qquad P^{(\pm)}_{2}=\pm\frac{x+17q_{0}}{576q_{0}^{2}(x+2q_{0})^{5/2}},\qquad P^{(\pm)}_{3}=-\frac{2x^{2}+20q_{0}x+77q_{0}^{2}}{6912q_{0}^{4}(x+2q_{0})^{4}},

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{4}=\pm\frac{28x^{4}+500q_{0}x^{3}+3684q_{0}^{2}x^{2}+14273q_{0}^{3}x+27307q_{0}^{4}}{3981312q_{0}^{7}(x+2q_{0})^{11/2}}.

It is obvious from (
2.14
)
that
P^{(\pm)}_{m}(x,t)
are holomorphic except
at the turning points and
x=\infty

(and multivalued for even
m
).
It also follows from the recursion relation (
2.14
) that

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)=\pm\left(\frac{2}{\hbar}x^{3/2}+\frac{t}{2\hbar}x^{-1/2}\mp\frac{1}{4}x^{-1}+\frac{\sigma(t,\hbar)}{2\hbar}x^{-3/2}+O\big(x^{-2}\big)\right)

(2.15)

holds when
x\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 2.8
.

We can check that
P^{(\pm)}_{m}(x,t)
’s
have the following asymptotic expansion for
x\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{0}(x,t)=\pm\left(2x^{3/2}+\frac{t}{2}x^{-1/2}+O\big(x^{-3/2}\big)\right),\qquad P^{(\pm)}_{1}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{4}x^{-1}+O\big(x^{-3/2}\big),

\displaystyle P^{(\pm)}_{m}(x,t)=O\big(x^{-3/2}\big)\qquad\text{for}\quad m\geq 2,

and we have (
2.15
) after summing up

\hbar^{m-1}P^{(\pm)}_{m}(x,t)
. Once you know that
P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)

has an asymptotic expansion in this sense, subleading terms in
(
2.15
) can be computed from
the Riccati equation (
2.13
).

Define

\displaystyle P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar):=\frac{1}{2}\big(P^{(+)}(x,t,\hbar)-P^{(-)}(x,t,\hbar)\big),

\displaystyle P_{\rm even}(x,t,\hbar):=\frac{1}{2}\big(P^{(+)}(x,t,\hbar)+P^{(-)}(x,t,\hbar)\big).

It is easy to check that (cf.
[
26
, Section 2]
)

\displaystyle P_{\rm even}(x,t,\hbar)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\log\frac{\hbar P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)}{2(x-q(t,\hbar))}

(2.16)

and

\displaystyle P_{\rm odd}(\sigma(x),t,\hbar)=-P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)

(2.17)

hold. Here
x
is regarded as a coordinate on the spectral curve,
and
\sigma
is the covering involution for the spectral curve:

P^{(+)}(\sigma(x),t)=-P^{(-)}(x,t)
.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)}{2(x-q(t,\hbar))}=\sqrt{x+2q_{0}}\big(1+O(\hbar)\big),

the right hand-side of (
2.16
)
is the derivative of the formal power series

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\hbar P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)}{2(x-q(t,\hbar))}=-\frac{1}{4}\log(x+2q_{0})+O(\hbar).

Thus the ambiguity of the branch of the logarithm
only appears in the top term, but
we care about the ambiguity since
it doesn’t matter in our computation.

The following theorem was applied in the

transformation theory of Painlevé equations

in
[
25
]
. We will use the fact
in the proof of our main theorem.

Theorem 2.9

(cf.
[
25
, Proposition 1.2 and Theorem 1.1]
)
.

(i)

The formal series
P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)
satisfies

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\hbar P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)-p(t,\hbar)}{2(x-q(t,\hbar))}\right).

(2.18)

In particular,
P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{P_{\rm odd}(x,t,\hbar)}{2(x-q(t,\hbar))}\right).

(2.19)

(ii)

All coefficients of
P^{(\pm)}(x,t,\hbar)
are holomorphic
except at the simple turning point
x=-2q_{0}
and
x=\infty
.
In particular, they are holomorphic at
the double turning point
x=q_{0}
.

Proof.

3
Topological recursion and quantum curve theorem

In this section we review the Eynard–Orantin’s
topological recursion
[
16
]
for our
spectral curve (
2.9
),
and formulate our main theorem.

3.1
Topological recursion

The topological recursion is an algorithm associating some
differential forms
W_{g,n}
and numbers
F_{g}

given the following source data:

•

A plane curve
({\mathcal{C}},x,y)
:
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
compact Riemann surface,
x,y\colon{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

are meromorphic functions.

•

The Bergman kernel
B
:
It is a symmetric differential form on
{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}}

with poles of order 2 along the diagonal, and
satisfying some normalization conditions.

In our case,
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
x,y
are
rational functions which parametrize the spectral curve (
2.9
)

\displaystyle x(z)=z^{2}-2q_{0},\qquad y(z)=2z(z^{2}-3q_{0}).

(3.1)

Here
z
is a coordinate on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
The Bergman kernel is given by

\displaystyle B(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},

since the spectral curve is of genus
0
.
Zeros of
dx
are called
ramification points
of the
spectral curve (
3.1
).
Our spectral curve has only one ramification point at
z=0
.

The topological recursion for our spectral curve (
3.1
)
is formulated as follows (see
[
16
]
for general case):

Definition 3.1

(
[
16
, Definition 4.2]
(see also
[
11
, Section 3]
))
.

The
Eynard–Orantin differential

W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
of type
(g,n)

is a meromorphic
n
-differential on the
n
-times product
of the spectral curve (
3.1
) defined by
the following
topological recursion relation
:

•

for
2g-2+n\leq 0
:

\displaystyle W_{0,1}(z_{1}):=y(z_{1})dx(z_{1})=4z_{1}^{2}\big(z_{1}^{2}-3q_{0}\big)dz_{1},

\displaystyle W_{0,2}(z_{1},z_{2}):=B(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{dz_{1}dz_{2}}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}},

•

for
2g-2+n=1
:

\displaystyle W_{0,3}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_{0}}K(z,z_{1})\big[W_{0,2}(z,z_{2})W_{0,2}(\bar{z},z_{3})+W_{0,2}(z,z_{3})W_{0,2}(\bar{z},z_{2})\big],

\displaystyle W_{1,1}(z_{1}):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_{0}}K(z,z_{1})W_{0,2}(z,\bar{z}),

•

for
2g-2+n\geq 2
:

\displaystyle W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_{0}}K(z,z_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}\times\Biggl[\sum_{j=2}^{n}\big(W_{0,2}(z,z_{j})W_{g,n-1}\big(\bar{z},z_{[\hat{1},\hat{j}]}\big)+W_{0,2}(\bar{z},z_{j})W_{g,n-1}\big(z,z_{[\hat{1},\hat{j}]}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+W_{g-1,n+1}\big(z,\bar{z},z_{[\hat{1}]}\big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
I\sqcup J=[\hat{1}]\end{subarray}}^{\text{stable}}W_{g_{1},|I|+1}(z,z_{I})W_{g_{2},|J|+1}(\bar{z},z_{J})\Biggr].

(3.2)

Here
\gamma_{0}
is a small cycle (in
z
-plane) which encircles
the ramification point
z=0
in the counter-clockwise direction,

\bar{z}=-z
is the conjugate of
z
near the ramification point,
and the
recursion kernel

K(z,z_{1})
is given by

\displaystyle K(z,z_{1})=-\frac{\omega^{\bar{z}-z}(z_{1})}{2(y(z)-y(\bar{z}))dx(z)},\qquad\omega^{\bar{z}-z}(z_{1})=\int^{\bar{z}}_{z}W_{0,2}(\cdot,z_{1}).

Also, we use the index convention

[\hat{j}]=\{1,\dots,n\}{\setminus}\{j\}
and so on.
Lastly, the sum in the third line of (
3.2
) is taken
for indices in the stable range
(i.e., only
W_{g,n}
’s with
2g-2+n\geq 1
appear).

The explicit form of some of Eynard–Orantin differentials
are given as follows

\displaystyle W_{0,3}=\frac{1}{12q_{0}z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}}dz_{1}dz_{2}dz_{3},

\displaystyle W_{0,4}=\frac{z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}z_{4}^{2}+3q_{0}(z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}z_{4}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}z_{4}^{2}z_{1}^{2}+z_{4}^{2}z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2})}{144q_{0}^{3}z_{1}^{4}z_{2}^{4}z_{3}^{4}z_{4}^{4}}dz_{1}dz_{2}dz_{3}dz_{4},

\displaystyle W_{1,1}=\frac{z_{1}^{2}+3q_{0}}{288q_{0}^{2}z_{1}^{4}}dz_{1},

\displaystyle W_{1,2}=\frac{2z_{1}^{4}z_{2}^{4}+6q_{0}(z_{1}^{4}z_{2}^{2}+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{4})+3q_{0}^{2}(5z_{1}^{4}+3z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+5z_{2}^{4})}{3456q_{0}^{4}z_{1}^{6}z_{2}^{6}}dz_{1}dz_{2},

\displaystyle W_{2,1}=\frac{28z_{1}^{8}+84q_{0}z_{1}^{6}+252q_{0}^{2}z_{1}^{4}+609q_{0}^{3}z_{1}^{2}+945q_{0}^{4}}{1990656q_{0}^{7}z_{1}^{10}}dz_{1}.

Eynard–Orantin differentials have
the following properties (see
[
16
]
):

•

As a differential form on each variable

,

W_{g,n}
, for
2g-2+n\geq 1
, is
holomorphic

except for the ramification point
0
and may
have a pole at
0
.

•

W_{g,n}
is

; that is, they are invariant
under any permutation of variables.

•

For
2g-2+n\geq 1
,

is
anti-invariant
under the involution

for each variable:

\displaystyle W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{j},\dots,z_{n})=-W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{j},\dots,z_{n})\qquad\text{for}\quad j=1,\dots,n.

•

W_{g,n}
is also
holomorphic
in

except for

(i.e.,
q_{0}=0
).
There is a formula for the derivative of

with respect to

; see Section

.

3.2
Quantum curve theorem

In this section we describe our main result which claims
that the scalar isomonodromy system (
2.10
)
gives a
quantum curve
.

Definition 3.2
.

For
g\geq 0,n\geq 1
satisfying
2g-2+n\geq 1
,
define
open free energy
of type (
g,n
) by

\displaystyle F_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int^{z_{1}}_{\bar{z}_{1}}\cdots\int^{z_{n}}_{\bar{z}_{n}}W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}).

(3.3)

It follows from the definition that open free energies satisfy

\displaystyle d_{z_{1}}\cdots d_{z_{n}}F_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=W_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}),

\displaystyle F_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{j},\dots,z_{n})=-F_{g,n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{j},\dots,z_{n})\qquad\text{for}\quad j=1,\dots,n.

Explicit computation shows that

\displaystyle F_{0,3}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=-\frac{1}{12q_{0}z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}},

\displaystyle F_{0,4}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})=\frac{z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}z_{4}^{2}+q_{0}\bigl(z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}z_{4}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}z_{4}^{2}z_{1}^{2}+z_{4}^{2}z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}\bigr)}{144q_{0}^{3}z_{1}^{3}z_{2}^{3}z_{3}^{3}z_{4}^{3}},

\displaystyle F_{1,1}(z_{1})=-\frac{z_{1}^{2}+q_{0}}{288q_{0}^{2}z_{1}^{3}},

\displaystyle F_{1,2}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{2z_{1}^{4}z_{2}^{4}+2q_{0}\bigl(z_{1}^{4}z_{2}^{2}+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{4}\bigr)+q_{0}^{2}\bigl(3z_{1}^{4}+z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}+3z_{2}^{4}\bigr)}{3456q_{0}^{4}z_{1}^{5}z_{2}^{5}},

\displaystyle F_{2,1}(z_{1})=-\frac{140z_{1}^{8}+140q_{0}z_{1}^{6}+252q_{0}^{2}z_{1}^{4}+435q_{0}^{3}z_{1}^{2}+525q_{0}^{4}}{9953280q_{0}^{7}z_{1}^{9}}.

We also introduce functions
\{S_{m}(x,t)\}_{m\geq 0}
by

\displaystyle S_{0}(x,t):=\int^{x}_{v}y(z(x^{\prime}))dx^{\prime},\qquad S_{1}(x,t):=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{y(z(x))}{2(x-q_{0})}\right),

and for

\displaystyle S_{m}(x,t):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2g-2+n=m-1\\
g\geq 0,n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{F_{g,n}(z,\dots,z)}{n!}\biggr|_{z=z(x)},

where
z(x)=\sqrt{x+2q_{0}}
is the inverse function of
x(z)
.
After computations we have

\displaystyle S_{0}(x,t)=\frac{4}{5}(x-3q_{0})(x+2q_{0})^{3/2},\qquad S_{1}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{4}\log(x+2q_{0}),

\displaystyle S_{2}(x,t)=-\frac{x+7q_{0}}{288q_{0}^{2}(x+2q_{0})^{3/2}},\qquad S_{3}(x,t)=\frac{2x^{2}+14q_{0}x+35q_{0}^{2}}{6912q_{0}^{4}(x+2q_{0})^{3}},

\displaystyle S_{4}(x,t)=-\frac{140x^{4}+1580q_{0}x^{3}+7476q_{0}^{2}x^{2}+18739q_{0}^{3}x+23499q_{0}^{4}}{9953280q_{0}^{7}(x+2q_{0})^{9/2}}.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

The formal series
\psi(x,t,\hbar)
given by

\displaystyle\psi(x,t,\hbar):=\exp(S(x,t,\hbar)t),

(3.4)

\displaystyle S(x,t,\hbar):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{m-1}S_{m}(x,t)

(3.5)

satisfies both of the differential equations in
scalar-version of the isomonodromy system (
2.10
).
That is, the formal series

given by (
3.5
) satisfies
the following differential equations which are equivalent to (
2.10

\displaystyle\hbar^{2}\left(\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}\right)^{2}+\frac{\partial^{2}S}{\partial x^{2}}\right)=\frac{\hbar}{x-q}\left(\hbar\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}-p\right)+\big(4x^{3}+2tx+p^{2}-4q^{3}-2tq\big),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2(x-q)}\left(\hbar\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}-p\right).

(3.7)

Thus, the
principal specialization

(i.e., setting
z_{i}=z
for all
i=1,\dots,n
)
of the open free energies gives an isomonodromic WKB solution.
Theorem
3.3
implies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}(x,t,\hbar)=P^{(+)}(x,t,\hbar)

(3.8)

holds (under a suitable choice of the branch of
\sqrt{x+2q_{0}}
).
The computational results in Section
2.4

show that (
3.8
)
holds up to
\hbar^{4}
. A full proof of Theorem
3.3

will be given in Section
4
together
with that of Theorem
3.7
below.

.

),

Quantitative equilibrium fluctuations for interacting particle systems
Chenlin Gu, Jean-Christophe Mourrat, Maximilian Nitzschner

Abstract
. We consider a class of interacting particle systems in continuous space of non-gradient type, which are reversible with respect to Poisson point processes with constant density. For these models, a rate of convergence was recently obtained in [ 25 ] for certain finite-volume approximations of the bulk diffusion matrix. Here, we show how to leverage this to obtain quantitative versions of a number of results capturing the large-scale fluctuations of these systems, such as the convergence of two-point correlation functions and the Green–Kubo formula. MSC 2010: 82C22, 35B27, 60K35. Keywords: interacting particle system, quantitative homogenization, equilibrium fluctuation, Green–Kubo formula, density field correlation, current-current correlation.



1.
Introduction

We consider a system of particles
(X_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathbb{N},t\geqslant 0}
evolving on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with local interactions. We take particles to be indistinguishable, so we encode their positions by keeping track of the measure

(1.1)

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\delta_{X_{i}(t)},

an element of the configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
of locally finite measures that are sums of Dirac masses on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote by
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
the law of the Poisson point process with constant intensity
\rho>0
, which is a probability measure on
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, and we write
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}
for the associated expectation. The model we consider is reversible with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
for every
\rho
, and is of non-gradient type. Precisely, its definition is based on the choice of a measurable function
\mathbf{a}_{\circ}
from
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
to the space
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym}}
of
d
-by-
d
symmetric matrices that satisfies the following properties.

•

Uniform ellipticity
: there exists
\Lambda<+\infty
such that for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,

(1.2)

|\xi|^{2}\leqslant\xi\cdot\mathbf{a}_{\circ}(\mu)\xi\leqslant\Lambda|\xi|^{2}.

•

Finite range of dependence
: for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, we have

(1.3)

\mathbf{a}_{\circ}(\mu)=\mathbf{a}_{\circ}(\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}B_{1}).

In (
1.3
) and throughout the paper, for every
r>0
, we write
B_{r}
to denote the open Euclidean ball of radius
r\geqslant 0
centered at the origin, and for every measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and Borel set
A\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we write
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}A
to denote the restriction of the measure
\mu
to
A
, that is, for every Borel set
B\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,

(\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}A)(B)=\mu(A\cap B).

For every
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we write
\tau_{x}
to denote the translation operator on measures, that is, for every Borel set
B\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,

(\tau_{-x}\mu)(B):=\mu(x+B),

and we set, for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
,

(1.4)

\mathbf{a}(\mu,x):=\mathbf{a}_{\circ}(\tau_{-x}\mu).

Roughly speaking, our model is built in such a way that each particle
X_{i}(t)
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, follows a diffusive motion whose diffusion matrix at time
t
is given by

\mathbf{a}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t},X_{i}(t))=\mathbf{a}_{\circ}\big(\tau_{-X_{i}(t)}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}\big)=\mathbf{a}_{\circ}\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\delta_{X_{j}(t)-X_{i}(t)}\bigg).

As will be explained below, one convenient way to give a precise definition of this stochastic process and its associated semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is to relate these to the Dirichlet form

(1.5)

f\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\nabla f\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla f\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right];

we refer to (
2.3
) for a definition of the gradient
\nabla
, and to Section
2.4
for a detailed construction. We denote by
\mathbf{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}
the law of the cloud of particles
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
started from
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}
, and by
\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}
the associated expectation. A classical object of study is the rescaled density fluctuation field

(1.6)

Y_{t}^{N}:=N^{-\frac{d}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{N^{2}t}-\rho m)(N\,\cdot),

where
N\geqslant 1
and
m
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. We postulate that the initial configuration
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}
is sampled according to
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
; since we denote by
\mu
the canonical random variable under
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}
, we implicitly set
\mu=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}
in expressions such that
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}
. One can think of
Y_{t}^{N}
as a (random) distribution over
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, and testing
Y_{t}^{N}
against a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{d}};\mathbb{R})
of compact support results in

(1.7)

Y_{t}^{N}(f)=N^{-\frac{d}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(N^{-1}x)\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{N^{2}t}(x)-\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(N^{-1}x)\,{\mathrm{d}}x\right).

Using the invariance of the measure
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
under the dynamics, one can easily check that for each fixed
t\geqslant 0
, the distribution
Y_{t}^{N}
converges in law under
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}\mathbf{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}
to a white noise over
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
with variance
\rho
as
N
tends to infinity. The space-time correlations are less direct and are governed by the bulk diffusion matrix
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym}}
, whose precise definition is given in (
2.10
). For a discrete class of models analogous to our present setting, it is shown in
[
11
,
23
,
40
]
that, as a space-time distribution,
(Y^{N}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
converges in law to
(Y^{\infty}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
solution to

(1.8)

\partial_{t}Y^{\infty}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla Y^{\infty}_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\rho{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}}\xi_{t}\right),

where
(\xi_{t}(x))_{t\geqslant 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
is a
d
-dimensional space-time white noise over
[0,+\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. In particular, we expect the convergence of two-point density correlation functions, in the sense that for every
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{d}};\mathbb{R})
and
t>s>0
,

(1.9)

\lim_{N\to+\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}[Y^{N}_{t}(f)Y^{N}_{s}(g)]=\mathbb{E}[Y^{\infty}_{t}(f)Y^{\infty}_{s}(g)].

The latter quantity can be rewritten explicitly as

(1.10)

\mathbb{E}[Y^{\infty}_{t}(f)Y^{\infty}_{s}(g)]=\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f(x)\Psi_{t-s}(x-y)g(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y,

where
(\Psi_{t}(x))_{t\geqslant 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
is the heat kernel for the operator
\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla
, that is,

(1.11)

\Psi_{t}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi t)^{{d}}\det{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}}}\exp\left(-\frac{x\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}^{-1}x}{2t}\right).

Our first main result gives a rate of convergence for (
1.9
). We prefer to state it using the unrescaled centered field defined by

(1.12)

Y_{t}:=Y^{1}_{t}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}-\rho m,

and for every
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, we write

(1.13)

Y_{t}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}-\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}m.

Theorem 1.1

(Quantitative asymptotics for two-point functions)
.

There exists an exponent
\beta(d,\Lambda,\rho)>0
and a constant
C(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that for every
f,g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and
t>s>0
, we have

(1.14)

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}\left[Y_{t}(f)Y_{s}(g)\right]-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f(x)\Psi_{t-s}(y-x)g(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y\right|\\
\leqslant C(t-s)^{-\beta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\,\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

By a simple scaling, Theorem
1.1
yields that for every
f,g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, we have

(1.15)

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}[Y^{N}_{t}(f)Y^{N}_{s}(g)]-\mathbb{E}[Y^{\infty}_{t}(f)Y^{\infty}_{s}(g)]\right|\leqslant C(N^{2}(t-s))^{-\beta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\,\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

As another illustration of our results, we show a quantitative version of the Green–Kubo formula. Standard versions of the Green–Kubo formula involve infinite-time and infinite-volume limits. Here we localize the time and space scales, with an estimate of the error. For convenience, we localize in space using boxes of the form

(1.16)

{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}:=\left(-\frac{3^{m}}{2},\frac{3^{m}}{2}\right)^{d},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

The Green–Kubo formula involves an integral of the correlations of the current of the particle density, as for instance in
[
53
, Proposition II.2.1]
. Informally, for every
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, the
\mathbf{E}_{\mu}
-averaged instantaneous current at a point
x\in\supp\mu
in the direction of
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
should be given by

\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\cdot(\mathbf{a}(\mu,x)p)=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\cdot(\mathbf{a}(\mu^{\prime}+\delta_{x},x)p),\qquad\text{ with }\mu^{\prime}:=\mu-\delta_{x}.

This is a continuous analogue of the quantity

j_{l}(\eta)
in
[
53
, (II.2.23)]
, with the correspondence of
(l,\eta,0)
in
[
53
]
to
(p,\mu,x)
in our notation. Since we would like to state a version of the Green–Kubo formula in finite volume, it would be tempting to define the total current over the box
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
as

\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\cdot(\mathbf{a}(\mu,x)p)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x).

However, even if we leave aside the possible difficulties associated with a lack of smoothness of the mapping
x\mapsto\mathbf{a}(\mu^{\prime}+\delta_{x},x)
, this definition fails to capture an important boundary contribution. Instead, we define the total current over the box
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
by duality as follows. We let
\mathscr{L}^{2}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
be the space of square-integrable functions which are measurable with respect to the configuration in
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
; see also (
2.2
). We denote by
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
the space of functions with square-integrable gradients that can be approximated by smooth functions that only depend on
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}K
for some compact set
K\subseteq{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
; see below (
2.5
) for a precise definition. We denote by
\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
the space dual to
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
, with the canonical embedding

\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})\subseteq\mathscr{L}^{2}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})\subseteq\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}),

and by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}),\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}
the duality pairing between
\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
and
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
.
For every
p\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
F_{p,m}\in\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
, the integrated current over
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
, by duality, so that for every
g\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
, we have

(1.17)

\left\langle F_{p,m},g\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}),\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}:=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla g\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right].

As is the case for diffusions on bounded subsets of
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we need to choose a boundary condition in order to define the particle dynamics in finite volume, for instance of Dirichlet or Neumann type. In the context of particle systems, the “Dirichlet”-type boundary condition is understood in the sense provided by the space
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
. Heuristically, with this boundary condition, particles that hit the boundary instantaneously disappear, and some particles also appear spontaneously at the boundary to maintain detailed balance. Since this in effect places the finite-volume dynamics “within a bath of particles at equilibrium”, we feel that this is a more appropriate dynamics for the estimation of the total flux than a Neumann-type boundary condition (which would correspond to dynamics with a conserved number of particles that reflect at the boundary). We denote by
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}^{(m)})_{t\geqslant 0}
the particle dynamics restricted to the box
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
with “Dirichlet” boundary condition, and by
(P_{t}^{(m)})_{t\geqslant 0}
the associated semigroup, so that
P_{t}^{(m)}(f)(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0})=\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}[f(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t}^{(m)})]
; these objects are defined more precisely in
Section
5
. In analogy with
[
53
, (II.2.27)]
(which we think should display a term
x_{\alpha}x_{\beta}
in place of
\delta_{\alpha\beta}x_{\alpha}^{2}
),
[
51
, (1)]
, or
[
44
, (2.2), (2.4), (8.7), (8.9)]
, our qualitative Green–Kubo formula takes the form

(1.18)

\frac{1}{2}p\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}p=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[p\cdot\mathbf{a}_{\circ}(\mu+\delta_{0})p]\\
-\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho|{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}|}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\left\langle F_{p,m},P_{t}^{(m)}(F_{p,m})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1},\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Here we write
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1},\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}}
as a shorthand notation for
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}),\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}
. Our second main result provides quantitative estimates that allow us to localize this formula in space and time. In particular, we can take
\lambda=0
there, in which case the integral of the current correlation becomes the classical case without any localization in time.

Theorem 1.2

(Quantitative Green–Kubo formula)
.

There exist
\alpha(d,\Lambda,\rho)>0
and
C(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in B_{1}
and
\lambda\in[0,+\infty)
, we have

(1.19)

\left|\frac{1}{2}p\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}p-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[p\cdot\mathbf{a}_{\circ}(\mu+\delta_{0})p]+\frac{1}{\rho|{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}|}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\left\langle F_{p,m},P_{t}^{(m)}(F_{p,m})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1},\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\right|\\
\leqslant C\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if }\lambda\in[1,+\infty),\\
\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2(1+\alpha)}}&\text{if }\lambda\in(3^{-2(1+\alpha)m},1),\\
3^{-\alpha m}&\text{if }\lambda\in[0,3^{-2(1+\alpha)m}].\end{array}\right.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Euclidean space

We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geqslant 1
, the standard Euclidean space, and as stated in (
1.16
), we denote by
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
the open hypercube of side-length
3^{m}
centered at the origin. For every
n\leqslant m\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

(2.1)

\mathcal{Z}_{m,n}:=3^{n}{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\cap{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{n}:=3^{n}{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}.

Then we can partition a large cube
{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}
into cubes of a smaller scale as
\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,n}}(z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n})
, up to a set of null Lebesgue measure, and similarly, partition
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
into
\bigcup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n})
, up to a set of null Lebesgue measure.

For any open set
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we write
L^{p}(U)
with
p\geqslant 1
for the classical space of functions
f
for which
|f|^{p}
has a finite Lebesgue integral over
U
, and
H^{k}(U)
with
k\geqslant 1
for the classical Sobolev space of order
k
on
U
. We let
H^{1}_{0}(U)
stand for the closure in
H^{1}(U)
of the set of smooth functions with compact support in
U
. For any non-empty and bounded open set
U
, and
f\in L^{1}(U)
, we also introduce shorthand notation for the Lebesgue integral of
f
, normalized by the Lebesgue measure of
U
, as

\strokedint_{U}f:=\frac{1}{|U|}\int_{U}f.

2.2.
Configuration space

The configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and the Poisson point process
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
are defined at the beginning of the introduction. For a Borel set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
\mathcal{F}_{U}
for the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the mappings
\mu\mapsto\mu(V)
, for all Borel sets
V\subseteq U
, completed with all the
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
-null sets. We also use the shorthand notation
\mathcal{F}
for
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
. The
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
is also the Borel
\sigma
-algebra associated with the topology of vague convergence on
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, as explained for instance in
[
18
, Exercise 5.2 and solution]
.

We introduce several function spaces on
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
that will be used below. On the configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, we define

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{2}(U):=L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\mathcal{F}_{U},\mathbb{P}_{\rho}),

the complete space of the
\mathcal{F}_{U}
-measurable functions with finite second moment under
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
. We write
\mathscr{L}^{2}:=\mathscr{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
as a shorthand notation. It is a Hilbert space and we denote by
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
its associated inner product.

For every sufficiently smooth function
f:\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\to\mathbb{R}
, measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, and
x\in\supp\mu
, the gradient
\nabla f(\mu,x)
is defined by requiring, for every
{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}
, that

(2.3)

\mathrm{e}_{k}\cdot\nabla f(\mu,x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(\mu-\delta_{x}+\delta_{x+h\mathrm{e}_{k}})-f(\mu)}{h},

where
(\mathrm{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{e}_{d})
denotes the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
We define the spaces of smooth functions
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U)
and
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(U)
for an open set
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
as follows. For every open set
V\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and any
\mathcal{F}
-measurable function
f
, suppose the configuration outside of
V
is fixed and the number of particles in
V
is given by
\mu(V)=n\in\mathbb{N}
. This naturally gives rise to a measurable canonical projection
(x_{1},...,x_{n})\mapsto f_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n},\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}V^{c}):=f(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{i}}+\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}V^{c})
, defined on
V^{n}
. We define
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U)
as the space of
\mathcal{F}
-measurable functions
f:\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\to\mathbb{R}
such that for every bounded open set
V\subseteq U
,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the canonical projection
f_{n}(\cdot,\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}V^{c})
is infinitely differentiable on
V^{n}
. The space
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(U)
is the subspace of
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U)
of functions
f
for which there exists a compact set
K\subseteq U
such that
f
is
\mathcal{F}_{K}
-measurable.

We now define
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{P}_{\rho})
, an infinite dimensional analogue of the classical Sobolev space
H^{1}
. For every
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U)
, we introduce the norm

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{P}_{\rho})}:=\left(\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f^{2}(\mu)]+\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{U}|\nabla f(\mu,x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and we define the space
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{P}_{\rho})
as the completion of the set of functions
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(U)
such that
\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{P}_{\rho})}
is finite, with respect to this norm. Since the density
\rho
in this paper is kept fixed, we write
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)
as a shorthand notation for
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U,\mathbb{P}_{\rho})
. We also denote by
\underline{\mathscr{H}}^{1}(U)
a normalized version of this norm which is convenient in order to absorb the diameter, entering as a factor in Poincaré’s inequality (see (
2.6
) below),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\|{f}\right\|_{\underline{\mathscr{H}}^{1}(U)}:=\left(|U|^{-\frac{2}{d}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f^{2}(\mu)]+\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{U}|\nabla f(\mu,x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We stress that functions in
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)
need not be
\mathcal{F}_{U}
-measurable. Indeed, the function
f
can depend on
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}U^{c}
in a relatively arbitrary (measurable) way, as long as
f\in\mathscr{L}^{2}
. If
V\subseteq U
is another open set, then
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)\subseteq\mathscr{H}^{1}(V)
.

We define the space
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)
as the closure in
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)
of the space of functions
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{c}(U)
such that
\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)}
is finite. Notice in particular that, in contrast with functions in
\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)
, a function in
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)
does not depend on
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}U^{c}
. If
V\subseteq U
is another open set, then
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(V)\subseteq\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)
.

We also recall a version of Poincaré’s inequality on the configuration space, which was proved in
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
: there exists a constant
C(d)<+\infty
such that for every bounded open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f\in\mathscr{H}_{0}^{1}(U)
, one has

(2.6)

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[(f-\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[f])^{2}\right]\leqslant C\diam(U)^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{U}|\nabla f|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right],

where
\diam(U)
denotes the Euclidean diameter of
U
.

We denote by
\mathscr{H}^{-1}(U)
the dual space of
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)
, which is the space of all continuous linear forms on
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)
. We denote by
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}(U),\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)}
the duality pairing, and define the norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}(U)}
as

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left\|{g}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}(U)}:=\sup_{f\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U),\left\|{f}\right\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)}\leqslant 1}\left\langle{g,f}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1}(U),\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)}.

2.3.
Homogenization of the bulk diffusion matrix

In this subsection, we recall some results from
[
25
]
, including the definition of the bulk diffusion matrix
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}
and its quantitative convergence from finite-volume approximations
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
and
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
.

For a bounded domain
U\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, vectors
p,q\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, and
\rho>0
, we consider the following optimization problems

(2.8)

\begin{split}\nu(U,p,\rho)&:=\inf_{v\in\ell_{p,U}+\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{\rho|U|}\int_{U}\frac{1}{2}\nabla v\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla v\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right],\\
\nu^{*}(U,q,\rho)&:=\sup_{u\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{\rho|U|}\int_{U}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla u+q\cdot\nabla u\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right],\end{split}

with
\ell_{p,U}=\int_{U}p\,\cdot\,x\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)
. By
[
25
, Proposition 4.1]
, there exist symmetric
(d\times d)
-matrices
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(U,\rho),{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}(U,\rho)
satisfying
{\mathsf{Id}\leqslant{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(U,\rho)\leqslant\Lambda\mathsf{Id}}
and
{\mathsf{Id}\leqslant{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}(U,\rho)\leqslant\Lambda\mathsf{Id}}
such that for every
p,q\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\rho>0
,

(2.9)

\nu(U,p,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}p\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(U,\rho)p,\quad\quad\nu^{\ast}(U,q,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}q\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}^{-1}(U,\rho)q.

The sequence
({\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\rho))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is decreasing in
m
(in the sense that
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\rho)-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m+1},\rho)
is positive semi-definite for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
), and we denote its limit by

(2.10)

{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(\rho):=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\rho).

We also refer to
[
25
, Appendix B]
for equivalent definitions of
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(\rho)
.
The main result of
[
25
]
is a quantification of the speed of convergence in (
2.10
). More precisely, by the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 2.1]
, there exist constants
\alpha=\alpha(d,\Lambda,\rho)>0
and
C=C(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that

(2.11)

|{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\rho)-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(\rho)|+|{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\rho)-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}(\rho)|\leqslant C3^{-\alpha m}.

Concerning the optimization problems in (
2.8
), we write

(2.12)

\begin{split}v(\cdot,U,p)\in\ell_{p,U}+\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U)&\text{ for the minimizer of $\nu(U,p,\rho)$, and}\\
u(\cdot,U,q)\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(U)&\text{ for the maximizer of $\nu^{\ast}(U,q,\rho)$.}\end{split}

As observed in
[
25
, Proposition 4.1]
, these optimizers are unique provided that we also impose that
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[v(\mu,U,p)]=0
and that
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[u(\mu,U,q)|\mu(U),\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}U^{c}]=0
. By
[
25
, (72)]
, the first-order optimality conditions read as

(2.13)

\begin{split}\forall v^{\prime}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(U),\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{U}\nabla v(\cdot,U,p)\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla v^{\prime}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]&=0,\\
\forall u^{\prime}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}(U),\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{U}\nabla u(\cdot,U,q)\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla u^{\prime}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]&=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{U}q\cdot\nabla u^{\prime}{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right].\end{split}

By removing the affine part from
v(\cdot,U,p)
and
u(\cdot,U,q)
, we define the
approximate correctors

(2.14)

\displaystyle\phi_{p,U}:=v(\cdot,U,p)-\ell_{p,U},\qquad\phi^{*}_{p,U}:=u(\cdot,U,{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}(U,\rho)p)-\ell_{p,U}.

Here the definition of
\phi^{*}_{p,U}
is motivated as follows. As seen in
[
25
, (65)]
, we can combine (
2.13
) and (
2.9
) to see that the averaged slope of
u(\cdot,U,q)
is
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}^{-1}(U,\rho)q
:

(2.15)

\displaystyle\forall q^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{\rho|U|}\int_{U}q^{\prime}\cdot\nabla u(\cdot,U,q){\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]=q^{\prime}\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}^{-1}(U,\rho)q.

Therefore, the function
u(\cdot,U,{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}(U,\rho)p)
has an average slope of
p
.

The following lemma subsumes important properties of correctors that will be instrumental in the following sections. Its proof essentially follows from
[
25
, Sections 4 and 5]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1) For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}
with
\dist(z,z^{\prime})>3^{n}
, and
i\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, the quantities
\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}
and
\phi_{e_{i},z^{\prime}+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}
are independent random variables.

(2) There exist constants
\alpha(d,\Lambda,\rho)>0
and
C(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, we have

(2.16)

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{\rho|{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}|}\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|\nabla\phi_{e_{i},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}-\nabla\phi^{*}_{e_{i},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\leqslant C3^{-2\alpha n}.

Proof.

The claim (1) follows directly from observing that
\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}
and
\phi_{e_{i},z^{\prime}+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}
are in
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n})
and
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}(z^{\prime}+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n})
respectively.

Concerning the claim (2), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we introduce the quantity

(2.17)

\begin{split}J({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},p,{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)p)&:=\nu({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},p,\rho)+\nu^{*}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)p,\rho)-p\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)p\\
&=\frac{1}{2}p\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)p-\frac{1}{2}p\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)p.\end{split}

By
[
25
, Proposition 4.2, (86) and (87)]
, we see that upon writing

(2.18)

\begin{split}w(\mu,{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i})&:=u(\mu,{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)e_{i})-v(\mu,{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i})\\
&=\phi_{e_{i},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}-\phi^{*}_{e_{i},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}},\end{split}

the quantity
J
admits the quadratic representation

(2.19)

J({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i},{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)e_{i})=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{\rho|{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}|}\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\frac{1}{2}\nabla w(\mu,{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i})\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla w(\mu,{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right].

Here we remark that our

is different from that in
[
25
, (86)]
up to a term that may only depend on
\mu({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n})
and
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.02773pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=4.30554pt}{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}^{c}
, but this does not change the value of the quadratic form on the right side of (

). Combining (
2.18
), (
2.19
), and (

), we obtain that

(2.20)

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{\rho|{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}|}\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|\nabla\phi_{e_{i},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}-\nabla\phi^{*}_{e_{i},{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\leqslant CJ({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i},{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)e_{i}).

On the other hand, by
[
25
, Section 5.4]
, one knows that

(2.21)

J({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},e_{i},{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}_{\ast}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n},\rho)e_{i})\leqslant C3^{-2\alpha n}.

Upon combining (
2.20
) and (
2.21
), the claim (
2.16
) follows.
∎

2.4.
Semigroup and diffusion on configuration space

In this part, we summarize some properties of the semigroup and the associated diffusion process on the configuration space
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. One possible route to the definition of these objects would be to start by considering initial configurations with a finite number of particles. If
N
particles are involved, the semigroup and stochastic process we aim to define are those associated with a divergence-form operator on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
, and standard methods apply: one can for instance construct the transition probabilities as in
[
6
, Appendix E]
and then construct the stochastic process using the Kolmogorov extension and continuity theorems. Once this is done, one can then seek to define a stochastic process for sufficiently “spread out” initial point clouds (in particular for
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
-almost every configuration) through a limit procedure. This is the method used in
[
38
]
for a slightly different model.

Since the stochastic process will not play a major role in this paper, we prefer to explain a more direct approach using the theory of Dirichlet forms. This approach has been explored in detail in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
36
,
42
,
46
,
50
,
56
]
, and the necessary conditions for the definition of the semigroup and stochastic process are easy to check for our model.

We endow the space
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
with the topology of vague convergence, and define the symmetric form on
\mathscr{L}^{2}
(
=L^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}_{\rho})
) by setting

(2.22)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}):=\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\\
&\displaystyle{\forall u,v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}),\quad\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,v):=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{1}{2}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla v\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right].}\end{aligned}\right.

The symmetric form
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}))
is closed: this means that if a sequence of elements of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}})
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to the norm induced by the symmetric form
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}_{1}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,v)+\left\langle{u,v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
, then this sequence converges to an element of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}})
with respect to this norm (see also
[
22
, Section 1.1]
). We can also verify that
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
is Markovian, since for the unit contraction function
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
as in
[
22
, (1.1.5), Exercise 1.2.1]
, we have that
|\phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant 1
and

\displaystyle\forall u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}),\quad\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}(u),\phi_{\varepsilon}(u))=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{1}{2}|\phi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u)|^{2}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla u\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\leqslant\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,u).

Therefore,
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}))
is a Dirichlet form. By the Riesz representation theorem (see
[
22
, Theorem 1.3.1]
), the Dirichlet form
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}))
defines a unique self-adjoint positive semi-definite operator
-A
on
\mathscr{L}^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}})&=\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{-A}),\\
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,v)&=\left\langle{\sqrt{-A}u,\sqrt{-A}v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}},\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{-A})
denotes the domain of the operator
\sqrt{-A}
. Using the spectral decomposition, the self-adjoint operator
-A
then defines a strongly continuous semigroup
P_{t}:=\exp(tA)
with
t\geqslant 0
on
\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
(see
[
22
, Lemma 1.3.2]
). We will also exploit the close relationship between the semigroup and the resolvent of
-A
, which is the family of operators
(\lambda-A)^{-1}
for
\lambda
ranging in
(0,+\infty)
, and we refer to
[
22
, Section 1.3]
for more on this. We may use the informal notation
\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla
to denote the operator
A
.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
is not locally compact with respect to the vague topology, we construct the associated Markov process using the notion of quasi-regular Dirichlet forms; see
[
41
, Chapter IV.3]
,
[
42
]
, and here we follow the steps in
[
50
, Section 4]
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}
the Dirichlet form with identity diffusion matrix

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}):=\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\\
&\displaystyle{\forall u,v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}),\quad\mathcal{E}(u,v):=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{1}{2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right].}\end{aligned}\right.

Our Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
is comparable to
\mathcal{E}
because of the uniform ellipticity in (
1.2
),

(2.25)

\displaystyle\forall u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}),\qquad\mathcal{E}(u,u)\leqslant\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,u)\leqslant\Lambda\mathcal{E}(u,u).

The quasi-regularity of
\mathcal{E}
is proved in
[
50
, Theorem 4.12]
using the result in
[
42
]
. With the comparison in (
2.25
), the notions of
\mathcal{E}
-nest,
\mathcal{E}
-exceptional, and
\mathcal{E}
-quasi-continuous in
[
50
, Definitions 4.10 and 4.11]
are equivalent to those of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
-nest,
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
-exceptional, and
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
-quasi-continuous, respectively. From this, we obtain the quasi-regularity of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
. The same argument also proves the locality of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
viewing that of
\mathcal{E}
proved in
[
50
, Corollary 4.13]
. As a consequence of
[
41
, Theorems IV.3.5, V.1.5 and V.1.11]
, there exists an
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
-valued Markov process
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
that is canonically associated with the Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
, and we denote by
\mathbf{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}
and
\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}
the probability and expectation associated with this process starting from the configuration
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}
. For every
u\in\mathscr{L}^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}
-quasi every
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, we have that

\displaystyle(P_{t}u)(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0})=\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}[u(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t})].

Moreover,
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
is a reversible measure for
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
.

In this paper, we mostly focus on the properties of the semigroup.

Proposition 2.2

(Elementary properties of the semigroup)
.

For every
u\in\mathscr{L}^{2}
, the function
u_{t}:=P_{t}u\in\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,
t>0
, satisfy the following properties.

(1)

(Energy solution) For every
T>0
, the function
u_{\cdot}(\cdot):[0,T]\times\mathcal{M}_{\delta}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\to\mathbb{R}
is the unique solution in
L^{2}([0,T],\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}))\cap C([0,T],\mathscr{L}^{2})
with
\partial_{t}u_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}))
such that for every
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\forall v\in\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}),\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[u_{t}v]-\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[uv]+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{s},v)\,{\mathrm{d}}s=0.

The function
u_{\cdot}(\cdot)
also satisfies the energy evolution identity

(2.27)

\displaystyle\forall\tau\in[0,t),\qquad\frac{1}{2}\left\|{u_{\tau}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|{u_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{s},u_{s})\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

(2)

(Decay of
\mathscr{L}^{2}
and energy) The mappings
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[(u_{t})^{2}]
and
t\mapsto\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})
are decreasing. Moreover, we have

(2.28)

\displaystyle\forall\tau\in[0,t),\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})\leqslant\frac{1}{2(t-\tau)}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[(u_{\tau})^{2}].

Finally, when
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}})
, we have
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})\xrightarrow{t\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,u)
.

Proof.

Following the classical energy solution theory of the evolution equation, there exists a unique solution
\widetilde{u}_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}))\cap C([0,T],\mathscr{L}^{2})
and
\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}_{t}\in L^{2}([0,T],\mathscr{H}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}}))
; see
[
19
, Sections 7.1.2.b and 7.1.2.c, Theorems 2, 3, 4]
. Moreover, using the definition (
2.4
),
u_{t}=P_{t}u=e^{tA}u
satisfies that for every
v\in\mathscr{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})

\displaystyle\left\langle{u_{t},v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\left\langle{u,v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle{e^{tA}u,v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\left\langle{u,v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle{Ae^{sA}u,v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle{\sqrt{-A}e^{sA}u,\sqrt{-A}v}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{s},v)\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

Then the uniqueness of the solution in (
2.26
) implies that
u_{t}=\widetilde{u}_{t}
. Equation (
2.27
) follows from (
2.26
) by testing with
v=u_{t}
, and it also implies that
t\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[(u_{t})^{2}]
is decreasing.

To prove that
t\mapsto\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})
is also decreasing, we use the spectral measure decomposition for the Dirichlet form

(2.29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})=\left\langle{e^{tA}u,-Ae^{tA}u}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}=\int_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\lambda e^{-2\lambda t}\,{\mathrm{d}}\left\langle{E_{\lambda}u,u}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

Here
E_{\lambda}
is the spectral projection operator with respect to
-A
; see
[
22
, Lemma 1.3.2]
. We see that the quantity above is decreasing with respect to
t
, as announced. We put this property into (
2.27
), which gives

\displaystyle(t-\tau)\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})\leqslant\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{s},u_{s})\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left\|{u_{\tau}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

This shows (
2.28
). When
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,u)=\left\langle{\sqrt{-A}u,\sqrt{-A}u}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}=\int_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\lambda\,{\mathrm{d}}\left\langle{E_{\lambda}u,u}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}<+\infty.

Combining this with an application of the dominated convergence theorem in (
2.29
), we obtain that
\lim_{t\to 0}\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u_{t},u_{t})=\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}(u,u)
.
∎

3.

In this section, we take the Dirichlet problem of elliptic equations as a simple example to explain the two-scale expansion technique on the configuration space. Recall the homogenized matrix
{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}
defined in (
2.10
), where we keep the dependence on
\rho

centered solving

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla U)={F},\qquad-\nabla\cdot({\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla{\overbracket{U}})=F,

in the sense that for all

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}\nabla V\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla U\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}\nabla V\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla{\overbracket{U}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]

We study the homogenization problem in the case when

is a centered linear statistic, meaning that

Here

is a function on Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
rather than the configuration space. This particular case is convenient because its homogenized solution has an explicit expression related to the homogenized solution in Euclidean space.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the condition (
3.4
), the unique solution of (
3.3
) such that
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[{\overbracket{U}}]=0
is

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\overbracket{U}}(\mu)=\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}{\overbracket{u}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu-\rho\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}{\overbracket{u}}\,{\mathrm{d}}m,

with

the unique solution of

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\nabla\cdot({\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla{\overbracket{u}})=f,&\text{ in }{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m},\\
{\overbracket{u}}=0,&\text{ on }\partial{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\overbracket{U}}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
as

. If we test (
3.3
) with some
V\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
, then its left-hand side is

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}\nabla V\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla{\overbracket{U}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\\

Now, with the explicit expression (

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}\nabla V\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla(W-U)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\right|\leqslant C3^{\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}\left\|{V}\right\|_{\underline{\mathscr{H}}^{1}}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}.

Viewing
W
as the sum of
{\overbracket{U}}
and a linear combination of some
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
functions, it also belongs to
\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
. We choose
V=W-U\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})
and use the Poincaré inequality (
2.6
) and the uniform ellipticity condition in (
1.2
) to obtain that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|W-U\|_{\underline{\mathscr{H}}^{1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}\leqslant C3^{\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}.

Step 3: estimate of
\left\|{W-{\overbracket{U}}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
.
The
L^{2}
estimate of
\left\|{W-{\overbracket{U}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
can be done directly

\displaystyle\left\|{W-{\overbracket{U}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,n}}(\partial_{i}{\overbracket{u}})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,n}}(\partial_{i}{\overbracket{u}})^{2}_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\phi^{2}_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C3^{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,n}}(\partial_{i}{\overbracket{u}})^{2}_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|\nabla\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C3^{2n}\left\|{{\overbracket{U}}}\right\|^{2}_{\underline{\mathscr{H}}^{1}({\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m})}.

Here in the second line we use the fact that
\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}
is local function, thus for
\dist(z,z^{\prime})>3^{n}
, the associated correctors are independent; see (1) of Lemma
2.1
. Then we use the basic
\mathscr{H}^{1}
estimate of
{\overbracket{U}}
and Poincaré’s inequality to conclude

(3.18)

\displaystyle 3^{-m}\left\|{W-{\overbracket{U}}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\leqslant C3^{n}\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

Recalling the choice of
n=\lfloor\frac{m}{1+\alpha}\rfloor
, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

4.
Quantitative estimate of two-point function and semigroup

In this section, we use a parabolic two-scale expansion to prove Theorem
1.1
. Recall that
P_{t}
is the parabolic semigroup associated with
(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\mathbf{a}}))
defined in (
2.4
), and let
\overline{P}_{t}
be the one associated to
(\mathcal{E}^{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}))
defined similarly. Theorem
1.1
is also related to the homogenization of the parabolic semigroup.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists an exponent
\beta(d,\Lambda,\rho)>0
and
C(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that for every
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, its associated linear statistic
F(\mu):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu
satisfies, for every
t>0
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\|{(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\leqslant Ct^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Our proof essentially follows the outline of
[
57
]
. In the proof, there are two main ingredients: the two-scale expansion and the regularization by the parabolic semigroup.
Since
(\mathcal{E}^{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}))
defines the diffusion of independent Brownian motions, a very important observation is the explicit formula of
\overline{P}_{t}F(\mu)
for the linear statistic
F(\mu)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu
, which reads as

(4.2)

\displaystyle\overline{P}_{t}F(\mu)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\Psi_{t}\star f\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu,

where we recall that
(\Psi_{t}(x))_{t\geqslant 0,x\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}
is the heat kernel defined in (
1.11
), and
\star
denotes the spatial convolution in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
We use the following classical heat kernel estimates on
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
(see e.g.
[
57
, (1.17)-(1.18)]
): for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{k}(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that for every
g\in L^{2}
and with
g_{t}:=\Psi_{t}\star g
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\forall 0<\tau<t<+\infty,\qquad\int_{\tau}^{t}\left\|{\nabla^{k}g_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{k}\left\|{\nabla^{k-1}g_{\tau}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla^{k}g_{t}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{k}}{(t-\tau)^{k/2}}\left\|{g_{\tau}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

4.1.
Two-scale expansion of parabolic equation

We first give a proof of homogenization in the special case of a rather smooth initial condition, using a two-scale expansion. For any fixed
g\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, we denote its linear statistic by
G:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g\,{\mathrm{d}}m
, and
G_{t}:=P_{t}G
,
\overline{G}_{t}:=\overline{P}_{t}G
. We propose its associated two-scale expansion

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{t}:=\overline{G}_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}g_{t})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is defined in (
2.1
) and
g_{t}:=\Psi_{t}\star g
. Our first estimate also assumes that
g
belongs to
H^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\alpha(d,\Lambda,\rho)>0
be as given by Lemma
2.1
. There exists a constant
C(d,\Lambda,\rho)<+\infty
such that for every
g\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, and
t>0
, its linear statistic and associated two-scale expansion defined above satisfy

(4.5)

\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{t}-\overline{G}_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{t}-G_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leqslant C\left(\left(3^{-\alpha n}+t^{-\frac{1}{8}}\right)\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}+3^{n}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|_{L^{2}}+3^{n}t^{\frac{5}{8}}\left\|{\nabla^{2}g}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Proof.

The part
\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{t}-\overline{G}_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
can be bounded directly

(4.6)

\begin{split}\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{t}-\overline{G}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}&=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}g_{t})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leqslant C\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}g_{t})^{2}_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\phi^{2}_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\right]\\
&\leqslant C3^{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}g_{t})^{2}_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|\nabla\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\\
&\leqslant C3^{2n}\left\|{\nabla g_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\\
&\leqslant C3^{2n}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

In the first three lines, we use the independence of the approximate correctors in (1) of Lemma
2.1
. In the last line, we use the contraction from the convolution
\Psi_{t}
.

The main part of the proof is the estimation of
(\widetilde{G}_{t}-G_{t})
. Using the parabolic equations associated to
G_{s}
and
\overline{G}_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla\right)G_{s}=\left(\partial_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla\right)\overline{G}_{s}=0,

and thus

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla\right)(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})=\left(\partial_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{a}\nabla\right)\widetilde{G}_{s}-\left(\partial_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla\right)\overline{G}_{s}.

We test this with
(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})
and integrate over
[0,t]

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{t}-G_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&\leqslant\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{0}-G_{0}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{m}}\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla\widetilde{G}_{s}-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla\overline{G}_{s})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{s}g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s.\end{split}

It suffices to estimate the three terms on the right-hand side to obtain (
4.5
). The first term can be estimated similarly to
\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{t}-\overline{G}_{t}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
in (
4.6
) as the evolution has not started and
G_{0}=\overline{G}_{0}
, so

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{0}-G_{0}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\leqslant C3^{2n}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

The third term in (
4.7
) only depends on the elementary
\mathscr{L}^{2}
bound, as
\partial_{i}\partial_{s}g_{s}
gains enough decay from (
4.3
)

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{s}g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right|\\
&\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\frac{t^{-\frac{5}{4}}}{2}\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\frac{t^{\frac{5}{4}}}{2}\left\|{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{s}g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}t^{-\frac{5}{4}}\left(\left\|{\overline{G}_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\left\|{G_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\left\|{\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{i}g_{t})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad+t^{\frac{5}{4}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\left\|{\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{i}\partial_{s}g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}t^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\left\|{g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+3^{2n}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+3^{2n}t^{\frac{5}{4}}\left\|{\nabla\partial_{s}g_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\\
&\leqslant C\left(t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left\|{g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+t^{-\frac{1}{4}}3^{2n}\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+3^{2n}t^{\frac{5}{4}}\left\|{\nabla^{2}g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right).\end{split}

Here in the second line, we apply Young’s inequality and choose the weight
t^{\frac{5}{4}}
to better balance each term (it suffices to take

with an exponent
\theta\in(1,2)
). In the third line, we apply the triangle inequality to
\left\|{\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
with the expression (
4.4
) at first, then we apply the independence of the approximate correctors in Lemma
2.1
to the term of type
\left\|{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{s}g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
. From the fourth line to the fifth line, we use (

) and the identity

.

Using the explicit expression of

in (

) can be treated like the elliptic case (

) by the following decomposition

where the two terms are respectively

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})\cdot(\mathbf{a}-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}})(\nabla g_{s}-(\nabla g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s,

\displaystyle\mathbf{II^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}(\partial_{i}g_{s})_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})\cdot\left(\mathbf{a}(e_{i}+\nabla\phi_{e_{i},z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}})-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}e_{i}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s.

For the term
\mathbf{I^{\prime}}
, the main issue is to use the Poincaré inequality to fix the slope, and the proof is similar to (
3.13
)

(4.11)

\begin{split}|\mathbf{I^{\prime}}|&\leqslant C3^{n}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\int_{z+{\mathchoice{\scalebox{1.2}{$\displaystyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\textstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptstyle\square$}}{\scalebox{1.2}{$\scriptscriptstyle\square$}}}_{n}}|\nabla^{2}g_{s}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}m\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=C3^{n}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\nabla^{2}g_{s}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leqslant C3^{n}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s+C3^{2n}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

In the third line, we apply the classical heat kernel decay (
4.3
). In the fourth line, the Young’s inequality is used, and the first term there can be compensated by part of the left-hand side in (

).

For the term
\mathbf{II^{\prime}}
, we apply the flux replacement argument like (

) and (

)

\begin{split}|\mathbf{II^{\prime}}|&\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}3^{-\alpha n}\left\|{\nabla g_{s}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s\\

&\leqslant C3^{-2\alpha n}\left\|{g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s.\end{split}

We apply Young’s inequality in the second line, and the classical heat kernel decay (
4.3
) in the third line. We put (
4.11
), (
4.12
) back to (

) and get

(4.13)

\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})\cdot(\mathbf{a}\nabla\widetilde{G}_{s}-{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla\overline{G}_{s})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\right|\\
\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla(\widetilde{G}_{s}-G_{s})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s+C3^{-2\alpha n}\left\|{g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C3^{2n}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Finally, we put (

), (
4.9
), (
4.13
) back to (

∎

Remark 4.3
.

The result above is already enough for us to prove (
1.15
) with the scaling and a good test function
g\in H^{2}\cap L^{1}
. Let us define

\displaystyle g^{N}(x):=N^{-\frac{d}{2}}g\left(\frac{x}{N}\right),\qquad G^{N}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g{\mathrm{d}}m.

This scaling gives us

\displaystyle\left\|{G^{N}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}=\rho\left\|{g^{N}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\rho\left\|{g}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Then we calculate the density field correlation

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\mathbf{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{0}}\left[Y^{N}_{t}(g)Y^{N}_{0}(g)\right]-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\times{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g(x)\Psi_{t}(x-y)g(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}x{\mathrm{d}}y\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[G^{N}(P_{N^{2}t}-\overline{P}_{N^{2}t})G^{N}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|{G^{N}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\left\|{(P_{N^{2}t}-\overline{P}_{N^{2}t})G^{N}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(3^{-\alpha n}+(N^{2}t)^{-\frac{1}{8}}\right)\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}+3^{n}N^{-1}\left\|{\nabla g}\right\|_{L^{2}}+3^{n}(N^{2}t)^{\frac{5}{8}}N^{-2}\left\|{\nabla^{2}g}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

With the scaling, we gain an extra factor
N^{-1}
for the gradient. It suffices to choose
1\ll 3^{n}\ll N^{\frac{3}{4}}
, for example,
3^{n}\simeq N^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then the error terms vanish when
N\to\infty
with an explicit rate.

4.2.
Regularization effect

In this part, we will show that the diffusive regularization effect of the parabolic semigroup can help weaken the assumption
g\in H^{2}
in Proposition

.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\tau,t>0
, we have the following estimates

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}P_{t}-P_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}\to\mathscr{L}^{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda\tau}{t}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}\overline{P}_{t}-\overline{P}_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}\to\mathscr{L}^{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda\tau}{t}}.

Proof.

Using the parabolic equation (
2.26
) associated to
\overline{P}_{t}
, for any
\widetilde{F}\in\mathscr{L}^{2}

\displaystyle\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}\widetilde{F}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\left\|{\widetilde{F}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\nabla\overline{P}_{s}\widetilde{F}\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla\overline{P}_{s}\widetilde{F}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]\,{\mathrm{d}}s=0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}\widetilde{F}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\|{\widetilde{F}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\tau\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\nabla\widetilde{F}\cdot{\overbracket{\mathbf{a}}}\nabla\widetilde{F}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\|{\widetilde{F}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\Lambda\tau\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla\widetilde{F}|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right].

Here we use the decay of the Dirichlet energy of the parabolic semigroup in Proposition
2.2
. Then we insert
\widetilde{F}=P_{t}F
, and obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\|{P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\Lambda\tau\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}|\nabla P_{t}F|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant\left\|{P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda\tau}{t}\left\|{F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

In the last line, we also use the semigroup property associated to
P_{t}
in (
2.28
). This estimate results in that of
\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}P_{t}-P_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}\to\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\left\|{(\overline{P}_{\tau}P_{t}-P_{t})F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}+\left\|{P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-2\left\|{\overline{P}_{\frac{\tau}{2}}P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\|{P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-2\left(\left\|{P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda\tau}{2t}\left\|{F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\Lambda\tau}{t}\left\|{F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

Here in the second line, we apply
\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\leqslant\left\|{P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
and the estimate (
4.15
) to
\left\|{\overline{P}_{\frac{\tau}{2}}P_{t}F}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
. A similar argument also works for
\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}\overline{P}_{t}-\overline{P}_{t}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}\to\mathscr{L}^{2}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For two functions
f,g
as in the statement, we define

\displaystyle F(\mu):=Y_{0}(f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f\,{\mathrm{d}}m,

\displaystyle G(\mu):=Y_{0}(g)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g\,{\mathrm{d}}m.

As
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
is a stationary measure for the process
(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, it suffices to treat the case
s=0
, and (
1.14
) is equivalent to estimating
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[G(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F]
. We apply the regularization at first. Recall that we denote by
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}
the inner product of
\mathscr{L}^{2}
space

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{G,(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\left\langle{G,\overline{P}_{\tau}(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right|+\left\|{G}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\left\|{\overline{P}_{\tau}(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F-(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\left\langle{G,\overline{P}_{\tau}(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right|+2\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda\tau}{t}}\left\|{G}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

In the second line, we apply Lemma
4.4
to the second term. When
\tau\ll t
, the error paid is very small and it suffices to consider the regularized version. Then we use the self-adjoint property of the operator

\displaystyle\left\langle{G,\overline{P}_{\tau}(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}=\left\langle{(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})(\overline{P}_{\tau}G),F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}.

The term
\overline{P}_{\tau}G
gains some more regularity, and it still has an explicit expression

\displaystyle\overline{P}_{\tau}G(\mu)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g_{\tau}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu-\rho\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g_{\tau}\,{\mathrm{d}}m,

with
g_{\tau}=\Psi_{\tau}\star g\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. Thus, we apply Proposition
4.2
to it

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle{G,\overline{P}_{\tau}(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\|{(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})(\overline{P}_{\tau}G)}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\left\|{F}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(\left(3^{-\alpha n}+t^{-\frac{1}{8}}\right)\left\|{g_{\tau}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+3^{n}\left\|{\nabla g_{\tau}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+3^{n}t^{\frac{5}{8}}\left\|{\nabla^{2}g_{\tau}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\left(3^{-\alpha n}+t^{-\frac{1}{8}}+3^{n}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}+3^{n}t^{\frac{5}{8}}\tau^{-1}\right)\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

From the second line to the third line, we apply the decay of classical heat kernel (
4.3
). Combining all the estimates above, we obtain that

(4.16)

\left|\left\langle{G,(P_{t}-\overline{P}_{t})F}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{L}^{2}}\right|\\
\leqslant C\left(3^{-\alpha n}+t^{-\frac{1}{8}}+3^{n}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}+3^{n}t^{\frac{5}{8}}\tau^{-1}+(\tau/t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

With a choice of mesoscopic scales
1\ll 3^{n}\ll t^{\frac{5}{8}}\ll\tau\ll t
, for example
\tau=t^{\frac{3}{4}},3^{n}\simeq t^{\frac{1}{16}}
we obtain the desired result (
1.14
) with a parameter
\beta=\frac{\min\{\alpha,1\}}{16}
.
∎

Remark 4.5

\begin{split}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\varepsilon}^{T}e^{-\lambda t}\left\langle F_{p,m},P_{t}^{(m)}(F_{p,m})\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1},\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}}\,{\mathrm{d}}t&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\langle{F_{p,m},V_{\lambda,\varepsilon}}\right\rangle_{\mathscr{H}^{-1},\mathscr{H}^{1}_{0}}\\

Step 3: choice of the mesoscopic scale.

•

Regime

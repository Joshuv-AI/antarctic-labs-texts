1.
Introduction

A graph
G
is a finite, at most one-dimensional CW complex. It has
rank

g
if its fundamental group is a free group of rank
g
:
\pi_{1}(G)\cong F_{g}
. Here, graphs shall be
admissible
, that means they do not have vertices of degree
0
or
2
.
Univalent vertices have a special role and are called
legs
(often also
hairs
,
leaves
or
marked points
).
Similarly, we will reserve the name
edge
to 1-cells that are only incident to non-leg vertices.
Legs of graphs are uniquely labeled by integers
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

A
metric graph
is additionally equipped with a length
\ell(e)\geq 0
for each edge
e
such that all edge lengths sum to one,
\sum_{e}\ell(e)=1
.
Fix
g,n
such that
g>0
and
2g-2+n>0
.
The moduli space of graphs,
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
,
is the space of isometry classes of metric graphs of rank
g
with
n
legs, except for graphs that have a cycle in which all edges have length
0
.
It inherits the topology from the metric and by identifying each graph that has an edge
e
of length
0
with the respective graph where
e
is collapsed.

The moduli space of graphs was introduced in
[
CV86
]
,
where it was shown that
\mathcal{MG}_{g,0}
serves as a rational classifying space for

{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
, the outer automorphism group of the free group of rank
g
.
Further,

\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
can be seen as the moduli space of
pure tropical curves

[
ACP15
]
and it is relevant for
Feynman amplitudes
, central objects in quantum field theory
[
Ber20
]
.

A partition
\lambda\vdash n
gives rise to both an irreducible representation
V_{\lambda}
of the symmetric group
\SG_{n}
and a Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda}
, a symmetric polynomial in
\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]^{\SG_{n}}
.
The symmetric group acts on the cohomology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
by permuting the leg-labels.
So,
H^{k}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
is an
\SG_{n}
-representation that we can decompose into irreducibles, i.e. there are integers
c_{g,\lambda}^{k}
such that

\displaystyle H^{k}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})\,\cong\,\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}c_{g,\lambda}^{k}V_{\lambda}.

The multiplicities
c_{g,\lambda}^{k}
are known explicitly if
g\leq 2

[
CHKV16
]
.
The
\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
is the following symmetric polynomial,

(1)

\displaystyle e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})\,=\,\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}c_{g,\lambda}^{k}.

Our first main result is an effective formula for
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
. It is stated as Theorem
2.18
.
Its proof in Section
2
is based on prior work by Vogtmann and the first author
[
BV23
]
.

Analogous formulas exist, e.g., for the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

[
Gor14
]
and for the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of the moduli space of stable tropical curves
[
CFGP23
]
.
The latter moduli space is a compactification of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
and its cohomology injects into the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

[
CGP21
]
.

There is another important invariant of moduli spaces such as
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
, which is in general a rational number: the
virtual
Euler characteristic. It has more convenient properties while studying maps between moduli spaces than the
classical
Euler characteristic, the alternating sum of the Betti numbers. The virtual Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
was studied, for instance, in
[
BV20
]
. The quantitative relation between the virtual and classical Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
is discussed in §
2.8
.

To obtain the classical Euler characteristic,
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
, we replace
s_{\lambda}
with
\dim V_{\lambda}
in eq. (
1
). Another specialization of
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
is the Euler characteristic for the
\SG_{n}
-invariant
part of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
’s cohomology, denoted as
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})
. We obtain it from
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
by setting
s_{(n)}=1
, (which corresponds to the trivial representation), and
s_{\lambda}=0
for all
\lambda\neq(n)
:

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})\,=\,\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})^{\SG_{n}}\,=\,\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}c_{g,(n)}^{k}.

We denote
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}
as the group of homotopy classes of self-homotopy equivalences of a connected graph
G
of rank
g
with
n
legs that fix the legs point-wise. I.e.
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}=\pi_{0}(\mathrm{HE}(G,\mathrm{fix}~\partial G))
. We have
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,0}\cong{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
and
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,1}\cong{\operatorname{Aut}}(F_{g})
. The moduli space
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
is a rational classifying space of
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}
, implying that the rational cohomology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
is the same as that of
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}
(see, e.g.,
[
CHKV16
]
).

Kontsevich made the observation that the homology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
is computed by what he termed the
Lie graph complex

[
Kon93
]
. In Section
2.1
, we utilize a related concept known as the
forested graph complex
, which was introduced in
[
CV03
]
, for the purpose of computing this homology.

In his work, Kontsevich also proposed an
odd
version of the Lie graph complex. This differs from the original graph complex by the notion of the graphs’ orientation. Detailed discussions regarding this are given in Sections
2.1
and
2.3
. He also indicated that, in the absence of legs, this odd graph complex computes the cohomology of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
with coefficients in
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
, the representation of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
that results from the composition of the natural map
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})\rightarrow{\operatorname{GL}}_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
with the determinant.
A formula for this ‘odd’ Euler characteristic, denoted as
e^{\mathrm{odd}}({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\dim H^{k}({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}),\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}})
, was previously presented in
[
BV23
]
. Similar formulas can be obtained for all groups
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}
due to the existence of the natural surjection
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}\rightarrow{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
that comes from forgetting the legs.
The odd Euler characteristic is equivalent to the Euler characteristic of the moduli space of graphs
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
that is fibered by the local system resulting from the
\det
-representation of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{n})
as pointed out in
[
Kon93
]
.
Here, we will prove a formula for the
\SG_{n}
-equivariant odd Euler characteristic, denoted as
e^{\mathrm{odd}}_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
. It is defined analogously to
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
in eq. (
1
) with the coefficients in
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
instead of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Morita, Sakasai, and Suzuki previously calculated the Euler characteristic,
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,0})=e({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))
for
g\leq 11

[
MSS15
]
.
The outcome of recent work of Vogtmann and the first author was the asymptotic formula,
e({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))\sim-e^{-\frac{1}{4}}{\left(g/e\right)^{g}/(g\log g)^{2}}
for large
g
, implying that the total dimension of the cohomology of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
grows super-exponentially with
g

[
BV23
]
. In this work, we enhance this result with explicit computations.

We leverage our effective formulas with the
FORM
programming language by the second author
[
Ver00
]
, for instance, to compute
e({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))
for all
g\leq 100
. The resulting values of
e({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))
and
e^{\mathrm{odd}}({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g}))
for
g\leq 15
were published with
[
BV23
]
.
The ancillary files to the arXiv version of the present article include these numbers for all
g\leq 100
, together with tables of
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
,
e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
,
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})
,
e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})
for all
g+n\leq 60
, and the polynomials
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
,
e_{\SG_{n}}^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
for all
g+n\leq 30
.
Subsets of these data are given in Tables
2
–
5
.
In Section
2.7
, we comment on our implementation of Theorem
2.18
in
FORM
, which is included in the ancillary files.
Based on our computed data, we give some empirical observations on the asymptotic growth rate of the Euler characteristic
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
and its variations for large
g
in Section
2.8
.

Our data in Tables
4
–
5
exhibit an interesting pattern. The
\SG_{n}
-invariant Euler characteristics of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}

stabilize
for large
n
.
The search for an explanation of this pattern led to our second main finding: The
\SG_{n}
-invariant cohomologies
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})^{\SG_{n}}
and
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}})^{\SG_{n}}

stabilize
for large
n
. That means if
n\geq g\geq 2
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\rho}\in\{\mathbb{Q},\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}\}
, then there are isomorphisms
H^{k}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}_{\rho})^{\SG_{n}}\rightarrow H^{k}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n+1};\mathbb{Q}_{\rho})^{\SG_{n}}
for all
k
.
This statement is proven as Theorem
3.1
in Section
3
, where we also comment on an analogy to the cohomology of the braid group.
Theorem
3.1
complements previous stability results for
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
(see
[
CHKV16
]
and the references therein).
Here, in contrast to these previous results, the range of stabilization does not depend on the cohomological degree.

2.
The
\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}

2.1.
Forested graph complexes

In this section we will describe chain complexes that compute the homology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
. These chain complexes are generated by certain graphs.

A subgraph is a subcomplex of a graph that consists of all its vertices, its non-edge 1-cells and a subset of its edges.
A subforest is an acyclic subgraph. A pair
(G,\Phi)
of a graph
G
and a subforest
\Phi\subset G
is
a
forested graph
.
We write
|\Phi|
for the number of edges in the forest
\Phi
.
Figure
1
shows some examples of forested graphs with different rank, forest edge and leg numbers.
The forest edges are drawn thicker and in blue. Legs are drawn as labeled half-edges.

A
(+)
-marking
\sigma_{\Phi}
of
(G,\Phi)
is an ordering of the forest edges, i.e. a bijection
\sigma_{\Phi}:E_{\Phi}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,|\Phi|\}
.
A
(-)
-marking
(\sigma_{\Phi},\sigma_{H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})})
of
(G,\Phi)
is such an ordering of the forest edges
\sigma_{\Phi}

together with a basis for the first homology of the graph, i.e. a bijection

\sigma_{H_{1}}:H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{h_{1}(G)}
, where
h_{1}(G)=\rank H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is the rank of
H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})
or equivalently the rank of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

For given
g,n\geq 0
with
2g-2+n\geq 0
, we define two
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces:

•

\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+}
is generated by tuples
(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})
of a connected admissible forested graph
(G,\Phi)
of rank
g
with
n
legs,
which is

(+)
-marked with
\sigma_{\Phi}
, modulo the relation

\displaystyle(G,\Phi,\pi\circ\sigma_{\Phi})\sim{\operatorname{sign}}(\pi)\cdot(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})

\displaystyle\text{ for all }\pi\in\SG_{|\Phi|},

and modulo isomorphisms of
(+)
-marked forested graphs.

•

\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-}
is generated by tuples
(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi},\sigma_{H_{1}})
of a connected admissible forested graph
(G,\Phi)
of rank
g
with
n
legs,
which is

(-)
-marked with
\sigma_{\Phi},\sigma_{H_{1}}
modulo the relation

\displaystyle(G,\Phi,\pi\circ\sigma_{\Phi},\rho_{H_{1}}\circ\sigma_{H_{1}})\sim{\operatorname{sign}}(\pi)\cdot\det\rho_{H_{1}}\cdot(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})

\displaystyle\text{ for all }\pi\in\SG_{|\Phi|}\text{ and }\rho_{H_{1}}\in{\operatorname{GL}}_{h_{1}(G)}(\mathbb{Z})

and modulo isomorphisms of
(-)
-marked forested graphs.

We will discuss the relations imposed in this definition and how they give rise to two different ways to impose
orientations
on graphs in more detail in Section
2.3
.

In what follows, we will often cover both the
(+)
and
(-)
-cases analogously in the same sentence using the
\pm
-notation.
Notice that the vector spaces
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
are defined for a slightly larger range of pairs
(g,n)
then the moduli spaces
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
. This generalization makes the generating functions for the Euler characteristics in Section
2.6
easier to handle.
There are no (admissible) graphs of rank one without legs, so

\mathcal{F}_{1,0}^{+}=\mathcal{F}_{1,0}^{-}=\emptyset
,
but there is one admissible graph of rank
0
with two legs and no vertices or edges.
It just consists of a 1-cell that connects the legs.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
g,n\geq 0
with
2g-2+n>0
,
the vector spaces
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}

are finite dimensional.

Proof.

Let
G
be a connected admissible graph of rank
g
with
n
legs,
|E_{G}|
edges, and
|V_{G}|
vertices. The Euler characteristic of such a graph equals
|V_{G}|-|E_{G}|=1-g
.
If we exclude the aforementioned graph with two legs and no vertices or edges, then each leg of
G
is incident to a vertex.
Each vertex has at most 3 incident edges or legs, so
3|V_{G}|\leq n+2|E_{G}|
.
It follows that
|E_{G}|\leq 3g-3+n
. There are only finitely many isomorphism classes of graphs of bounded edge number.
Hence, there are also only finitely many isomorphism classes of forested graphs of rank
g
with
n
legs. The relations in Definition
2.1
guarantee that each such isomorphism class contributes at most one generator to
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
.
∎

The vector spaces
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
are graded by the number of edges in the forest. Let

C_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})
be the respective subspace restricted to generators with
k
forest edges.
These spaces form a chain complex, so we will refer to
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
as the
forested graph complex
with
(\pm)
orientation.
We will not explicitly state the boundary maps,
\partial_{k}:C_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})\rightarrow C_{k-1}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})
here (see,
[
CV03
]
), as knowledge of the dimensions of these chain groups suffices for our Euler characteristic considerations.

The following theorem is a consequence of the works of Culler–Vogtmann
[
CV86
]
, Kontsevich
[
Kon93
,
Kon94
]
and Conant–Vogtmann
[
CV03
]
(see in particular
[
CV03
, Sec. 3.1–3.2]
):

Theorem 2.3
.

For
g>0
,
n\geq 0
and
2g-2+n>0
,
the chain complexes
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+}
(
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-}
)
compute the homology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
with trivial coefficients
\mathbb{Q}
(with twisted coefficients
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
).
The
\SG_{n}
-action on
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
(
H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n},\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}})
) descents obviously to
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+}
(
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-}
) by permuting leg-labels.

In what follows, we will use this theorem to prove a formula for

e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
and
e^{\mathrm{odd}}_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
.

2.2.
Equivariant Euler characteristics

Recall that a permutation
\pi\in\SG_{n}
factors uniquely as a product of disjoint cycles.
If the orders of these cycles are
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}
,
then
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
is a partition of
n
called the
cycle type
of
\pi
. For such a permutation
\pi\in\SG_{n}
, we define the
power sum symmetric polynomial
,

p^{\pi}=p_{\lambda_{1}}\cdots p_{\lambda_{\ell}}\in\Lambda_{n}

with
p_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{k}
. The
Fröbenius characteristic
is a symmetric polynomial associated uniquely to a
\SG_{n}
-representation
V
. It is defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{V})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\SG_{n}}\chi_{V}(\pi)p^{\pi},

where
\chi_{V}
is the
character
associated to
V
(see, e.g.,
[
Sta97
, §7.18]
).
The

\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic
of
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
is the alternating sum over the Fröbenius characteristics of
H_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm};\mathbb{Q})
. Note that this is consistent with the definition of
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})
in eq. (
1
) since
\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{V_{\lambda}})=s_{\lambda}
.

As the spaces
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
are finite by Lemma
2.2
,
we can compute the equivariant Euler characteristic
on the chain level:

Proposition 2.4
.

The
\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm}
is given by

\displaystyle e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}),

where
\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})})

is the Fröbenius characteristic of
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
as an
\SG_{n}
-representation,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\SG_{n}}\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}(\pi)p^{\pi},

and
\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}

is the
character
associated to
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
.

Corollary 2.5
.

For
g>0
,
n\geq 0
and
2g-2+n>0
,

\displaystyle e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})

\displaystyle=e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+})

and

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{odd}}_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})

\displaystyle=e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-}).

Proof.

Follows directly from Theorem
2.3
.
∎

The other discussed Euler characteristics of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
can
be obtained by evaluating the polynomials
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})

for certain values of
{\overline{p}}=p_{1},p_{2},\ldots
.
We write
{\overline{p}}={\overline{1}}
for the specification
p_{1}=1
,
p_{2}=p_{3}=\ldots=0

and
{\overline{p}}=\boldsymbol{1}
for the specification
p_{1}=p_{2}=\ldots=1
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For
g>0
,
n\geq 0
and
2g-2+n>0
,

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})

\displaystyle=n!\cdot e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+})|_{{\overline{p}}={\overline{1}}},

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})

\displaystyle=n!\cdot e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-})|_{{\overline{p}}={\overline{1}}},

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})

\displaystyle=e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+})|_{{\overline{p}}=\boldsymbol{1}},

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})

\displaystyle=e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-})|_{{\overline{p}}=\boldsymbol{1}}.

Proof.

Recall the relationship between the character of the

\SG_{n}
-representation
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})
and the symmetric
polynomials
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{\pm})
from
Proposition
2.4
.
Observe that

\chi_{V}(\id)=\dim V
, where
\id
is the trivial permutation.
To verify the first two equations, observe that substituting
p_{1}=1
and
p_{2}=p_{3}=\ldots=0
,
in the formula for

\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})})

of Proposition
2.4

amounts to restricting the sum over
\pi
to
\pi=\id
,
as only the trivial permutation has cycle-type
(1,1,\ldots,1)
.
For the second line recall that

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\SG_{n}}\chi_{V}(\pi)=\dim V^{\SG_{n}},

where
V^{\SG_{n}}
is the
\SG_{n}
-invariant subspace of
V
.
Additionally, use Corollary
2.5
.
∎

To explicitly compute
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
,
we hence need to compute the Fröbenius characteristic of the chain groups and to compute those we need to know the character
\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}
.
To compute these characters, we need a more detailed description of the chain groups
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
that are generated only by

orientable forested graphs
.

2.3.
Orientable forested graphs

Roughly, the relations in Definition
2.1
have the effect that a chosen ordering of the forest edges or a chosen homology basis,
only matters up to its sign or its orientation
. We can therefore think of the generators as
oriented
forested graphs.
Due to the relations, every forested graphs gives rise to at most one generator of
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{+}
or
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}^{-}
.
However, not every forested graph is
orientable
in this way, i.e. gives rise to a generator.
Even though Definition
2.1
only involves connected forested graphs, we will define the notion of orientability here also for disconnected graphs. We will need it later.

Let
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
be the group of automorphisms of the forested graph
(G,\Phi)
.
Each automorphism is required to fix the leg-labels and the forest.
For instance, the graph in Figure
1(a)
has one automorphism of order two that mirrors the graph on the horizontal axis.
The graph in Figure
1(b)
has one automorphism that flips the two doubled edges
and the graph in Figure
1(c)
has no (leg-label-preserving) automorphisms.

Each
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
induces a permutation,
\alpha_{\Phi}:E_{\Phi}\rightarrow E_{\Phi}
on the set of forest edges and an automorphism
on the homology groups
\alpha_{H_{0}}:H_{0}(G,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{0}(G,\mathbb{Z})
and

As connected graphs come with a canonical basis for

The other sign factors capture the signs of the relations in Definition

if the
(\pm)
-marking of the graph

is acted upon using

Definition 2.7

A forested graph

The forested graph in Figure

Proposition 2.8
.

The chain groups
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
are freely generated by isomorphism classes of connected
(\pm)
-orientable forested graphs of rank
g
with
n
legs
and
k
forest edges.

Proof.

Definition
2.1
guarantees that each isomorphism class of a connected forested graph
(G,\Phi)
contributes at most one generator, as all different
(\pm)
-markings of
(G,\Phi)
are related via a permutation
\pi\in\SG_{|\Phi|}
(and
\rho_{H_{1}}\in{\operatorname{GL}}_{h_{1}(G)}(\mathbb{Z})
).

It remains to be proven that
(G,\Phi)
does contribute a free generator if and only if it is
(\pm)
-orientable. We will only prove this for the
(+)
-orientation, as the
(-)
-orientation follows analogously.
Recall that for any isomorphism class of a
(+)
-marked graph
(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})
, Definition
2.1
imposes the relation
(G,\Phi,\pi\circ\sigma_{\Phi})\sim{\operatorname{sign}}(\pi)\cdot(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})
for each
\pi\in\SG_{|\Phi|}
.
As
(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})
is given up to isomorphism, we have
(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi}\circ\alpha_{\Phi})=(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})
for
each
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
with
\alpha_{\Phi}
the permutation that
\alpha
induces on
\Phi
.
If there is an automorphism
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
such that
\alpha_{\Phi}
is
an odd permutation on
\Phi
, then the permutation
\pi:=\sigma_{\Phi}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}\circ\sigma_{\Phi}^{-1}\in\SG_{|\Phi|}
is odd as well and we find
that
(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})=(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi}\circ\alpha_{\Phi})=(G,\Phi,\pi\circ\sigma_{\Phi})\sim-(G,\Phi,\sigma_{\Phi})\sim 0
.
If there is no such automorphism, then for any
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
,

{\operatorname{sign}}(\sigma_{\Phi}\circ\alpha_{\Phi}\circ\sigma_{\Phi}^{-1})=1
. Hence, the sign of a
(+)
-orientable and
(+)
-marked forested graph is fixed by any automorphism which therefore gives a generator.
∎

2.4.
The action of
\SG_{n}
on orientable graphs

The next step for the explicit evaluation of
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
is to quantify the action of
\SG_{n}

that permutes the legs
of the generators of
\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n}
.

Let
{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
be the set of automorphisms of a forested graph
(G,\Phi)
with
n
legs that are
allowed
to permute the leg-labels.
For instance, for the graph in Figure
1(c)
, which has a trivial leg-label-preserving automorphism group,
the group
{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
is generated by the automorphism that mirrors the graph vertically and permutes the
leg-labels by
(12)(34)
.
We have a map
\pi_{G}:{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow\SG_{n}
, given by only looking at the induced permutation on the leg-labels. The kernel of this map is equal to
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
, the group of leg-label-fixing automorphisms of
(G,\Phi)
.

The
\SG_{n}
-action gives a representation
\rho:\SG_{n}\rightarrow{\operatorname{GL}}(C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n}))
.
For a specific
\pi\in\SG_{n}
, the linear map
\rho(\pi)\in{\operatorname{GL}}(C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n}))
replaces each generator of
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
with the respective generator where the leg labels are permuted by
\pi
.
The character of
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
is the composition of
\rho
with the trace,
\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}=\Tr\circ\rho
.
As
\rho(\pi)
maps each generator to a multiple of another generator,
it is sufficient to look at generators that happen to be Eigenvectors of
\rho(\pi)
to compute
\Tr(\rho(\pi))
.
Let
(G,\Phi,\sigma^{\pm})
be a connected
(\pm)
-orientable forested graph with a
(\pm)
-marking
\sigma^{\pm}
corresponding to a generator of
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
.
This generator is an Eigenvector of
\rho(\pi)
if the forested graph
(G,\Phi)
has a non-leg-label-fixing automorphism
\alpha\in{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
such that
\pi_{G}(\alpha)=\pi
.
The following lemma describes the Eigenvalue corresponding to such an Eigenvector. It is either
+1
or
-1
.

Lemma 2.9
.

If, for given
\pi\in\SG_{n}
, the generator
(G,\Phi,\sigma^{\pm})\in C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
is an Eigenvector of
\rho(\pi)
, then
the corresponding Eigenvalue is
\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)
,
where
\alpha
is some representative
\alpha\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.8
,
(G,\Phi)
cannot have a leg-label-fixing automorphism that flips the orientation. However, an automorphism from the larger group
{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
can change the sign of the orientation
(i.e. the sign of the ordering and basis given by the
(\pm)
-marking in Definition
2.1
can be flipped).
The map
\rho(\pi)
does so if
\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)=-1
for a representative of the kernel
\alpha\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)
. As
\alpha\mapsto\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)
gives a group homomorphism
{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and as
\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)=1
for all
\alpha\in\ker\pi_{G}
, it does not matter which representative we pick.
∎

Summing over all such Eigenvalues of
\rho(\pi)
gives the value of

\Tr(\rho(\pi))=\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}(\pi)
:

Corollary 2.10
.

For each
\pi\in\SG_{n}
, the character of
C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
is

\displaystyle\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}(\pi)=\sum\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha_{(G,\Phi,\pi)}),

where we sum over all isomorphism classes of
(\pm)
-orientable forested graphs
(G,\Phi)

with
k=|\Phi|
forest edges,
for which the preimage
\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)\subset{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
is nonempty
(i.e.
(G,\Phi)
has at least one non-leg-label-fixing automorphism that permutes the leg labels by
\pi
),
and
\alpha_{(G,\Phi,\pi)}
is some representative of such an automorphism in
\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)\subset{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
.

To continue, it is convenient to pass to a sum over all connected forested graphs without restrictions on the orientability to get better combinatorial control over the expression:

Corollary 2.11
.

For
g,n\geq 0
with
2g-2+n\geq 0
and
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}(\pi)=\sum_{[G,\Phi]}\frac{1}{|{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)|}\sum_{\alpha\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha),

where we sum over all isomorphism classes of (not necessarily orientable) connected forested graphs
[G,\Phi]
of rank
g
,
n
legs and
k=|\Phi|
forest edges.

Proof.

As
\alpha\mapsto\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)
also gives a map
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
we have by Definition
2.7

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\ker\pi_{G}}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)=\begin{cases}|{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)|&\text{ if $(G,\Phi)$ is $(\pm)$-orientable}\\
0&\text{ else}\end{cases}

The statement follows, as
\sum_{\alpha\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)=\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha^{\prime})\sum_{\alpha\in\ker\pi_{G}}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha),

for any representative
\alpha^{\prime}\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)
and the formula from Corollary
2.10
for
\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})}(\pi)
.
∎

With this we finally obtain our first explicit formula for
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
:

Theorem 2.12
.

For
g,n\geq 0
with
2g-2+n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[G,\Phi]_{U}}\frac{(-1)^{|\Phi|}}{|{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)|}\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)p^{\pi_{G}(\alpha)},

where we sum over all isomorphism classes of connected forested graphs
[G,\Phi]_{U}
of rank
g
with
n

unlabeled
legs.

Proof.

We can plug the statement of Corollary
2.11
into the definition of the Fröbenius characteristic in Proposition
2.4
to get

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in\SG_{n}}p^{\pi}\sum_{[G,\Phi]}\frac{1}{|{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)|}\sum_{\alpha\in\pi_{G}^{-1}(\pi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha).

Next, we can merge the first and the third sum into a summation over the whole group
{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
,
as the preimages of different
\pi\in\SG_{n}
partition
{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{[G,\Phi]}\frac{1}{|{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)|}\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)p^{\pi_{G}(\alpha)}.

Let
L_{S}(G,\Phi)
be the set of isomorphism classes of forested graphs
(G,\Phi)
that only differ by a relabeling of the legs.
Obviously,
\SG_{n}
acts transitively on
L_{S}(G,\Phi)
by permuting the leg-labels. The stabilizer of this action is the image of
\pi_{G}:{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow\SG_{n}
.
By the orbit-stabilizer theorem,
n!=|\SG_{n}|=|\im\pi_{G}||L_{S}(G,\Phi)|
.
By the short exact sequence,
1\rightarrow{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow\im\pi_{G}\rightarrow 1
, we have
|\im\pi_{G}|=|{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)|/|{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)|
. Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}(\chi_{C_{k}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})})=\sum_{[G,\Phi]}\frac{1}{|L_{S}(G,\Phi)||{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)|}\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)p^{\pi_{G}(\alpha)}.

The terms in this sum do not depend on the leg-labeling of the graphs, so we can just sum over non-leg-labeled graphs and remove the
|L_{S}(G,\Phi)|
in the denominator.
∎

2.5.
Disconnected forested graphs

The expression for
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
in Theorem
2.12
involves a sum over
connected
forested graphs without leg-labels. To eventually get an effective generating function for
e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})
, it is convenient to pass to an analogous formula that sums over
disconnected
forested graphs.
Moreover, it is helpful to change from grading the graphs by rank to grading them by their negative Euler characteristic.
For connected graphs, this is just a trivial shift as
the negative Euler characteristic of such graphs fulfills
\rank H_{1}(G,\mathbb{Z})-\rank H_{0}(G,\mathbb{Z})=g-1
.
So, we define for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\geq 0
,

(2)

\displaystyle\ee_{t,n}^{\pm}=\sum_{[G,\Phi]_{U}}\frac{1}{|{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)|}\sum_{\alpha\in{\operatorname{UAut}}(G,\Phi)}\xi^{\pm}(G,\Phi,\alpha)p^{\pi_{G}(\alpha)},

where we sum over all isomorphism classes of (possibly disconnected) forested graphs

of Euler characteristic

There is only a finite number of such graphs, so the sum is finite and
\ee_{t,n}^{\pm}

is a symmetric polynomial in

Let

be the
ring of formal symmetric power series

\widehat{\Lambda}=\lim_{\leftarrow n}\mathbb{Q}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]^{\SG_{n}}

Later in this section we will prove a generating function,

,
for the polynomials
\ee_{t,n}^{\pm}
.
Before that, we explain how we can translate them into our desired
\SG_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristics:

Proposition 2.13
.

We define the following Laurent series over the ring of formal symmetric power series

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar,{\overline{p}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,n\geq 0\\
2g-2+n\geq 0\end{subarray}}e_{\SG_{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{g,n})(\pm\hbar)^{g-1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\pm}(\hbar,{\overline{p}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\ee_{t,n}^{\pm}(\pm\hbar)^{t},

which are elements of
\widehat{\Lambda}((\hbar))
.
Both are related by the
plethystic exponential

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\pm}(\hbar,{\overline{p}})=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar^{k},{\overline{p}}_{[k]})}{k}\right),

where
\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar^{k},{\overline{p}}_{[k]})
denotes the power series
\mathbf{e}^{\pm}
with the substitutions
\hbar\rightarrow\hbar^{k}
and
p_{i}\rightarrow p_{ik}
.

Proof.

The combinatorial argument for
[
BV23
, Proposition 3.2]
applies to
translate between the sum over
connected
forested graphs in Theorem

to a sum over disconnected forested graphs in eq. (
2

The strategy goes back to Pólya
[
P3́7
]
(see also
[
BLL98
, Chapter 4.3]
).
Briefly, each summand

{\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar^{k},{\overline{p}}_{[k]})/k}
in
the exponent of the stated formula
counts pairs consisting of a
k
-tuple of mutually isomorphic forested graphs
and an automorphism that cyclically permutes the different graphs.
Such automorphisms give rise to different sign factors depending on the orientation of the graphs. Accounting for these signs gives the minus signs in front of
\hbar
in the
(-)
-orientation case (see
[
BV23
, Theorem 5.1]
).
∎

We can solve the equation in the statement above for the generating functions
\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar,{\overline{p}})

and therefore obtain the value of

if we know sufficiently many coefficients of

The required inverse transformation of power series is the plethystic logarithm:

Corollary 2.14
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar,{\overline{p}})=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}\log\mathbf{E}^{\pm}(\hbar^{k},{\overline{p}}_{[k]}),

where

is the number-theoretic Möbius function.

Proof.

Let
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots\in\widehat{\Lambda}((\hbar))
such that

converges uniformly in

.
It follows from the definition of the Möbius function,

\sum_{d|n}\mu(d)=0
for all

and
\mu(1)=1
,
that

g_{1}=\sum_{k\geq 1}{\mu(k)}f_{k}/k
.
With

g_{n}=\mathbf{e}^{\pm}(\hbar^{n},{\overline{p}}_{[n]})

and
f_{n}=\log\mathbf{E}^{\pm}(\hbar^{n},{\overline{p}}_{[n]})

, the statement now follows form Proposition
2.13
.
∎

Proof.

Matching all legs of an extended forest in pairs gives a graph with a marked forest and special univalent vertices.
We get this graph by gluing together the legs as described by a matching.
Gluing together two legs creates a new 1-cell between the vertices that are adjacent to the legs and forgets about the legs and the 1-cells that are incident to them.
The special vertices are subsequently promoted to (unlabeled)
legs of the resulting forested graph.
All forested graphs with unlabeled legs can be obtained this way.

2.8.

The

Recall that the notation ‘

By the short exact sequence

The numbers

for

.
Using our data on the Euler characteristics of

to
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
for all

.

.

For fixed
n\geq 0

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})

\displaystyle\sim e^{-\frac{1}{4}}\chi(\Gamma_{g,n})

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n})

\displaystyle\sim e^{\frac{1}{4}}\chi(\Gamma_{g,n})

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})

\displaystyle\sim e^{-\frac{1}{4}}\chi(\Gamma_{g,n})/n!

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})

Proving this conjecture should be feasible by generalizing the analytic argument in
[

, Sec. 4]

3.1.
Analogy to Artin’s braid group

The observed large-
n
stabilization carries similarities with the cohomology of Artin’s
braid group

B_{n}
of
equivalence classes of
n
-braids.

For a graph
G
of rank
g
with
n
legs, let
{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}
be the group of homotopy classes of self-homotopy equivalences of
G
that only fix the legs as a set and not point-wise, i.e.
{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}=\pi_{0}(\mathrm{HE}(G,\partial G)).

By looking only at the action of
{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}
on the leg-labels
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we get a surjective map to the symmetric group
\SG_{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}\rightarrow{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}\rightarrow\SG_{n}\rightarrow 1,

where the ‘pure’ group,
{\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n}
, is the subgroup of
{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}
that fixes the legs point-wise as defined in the introduction.
Analogously, the braid group
B_{n}
maps surjectively to
\SG_{n}
. So,

1\rightarrow P_{n}\rightarrow B_{n}\rightarrow\SG_{n}\rightarrow 1,

where the kernel
P_{n}
is the
pure braid group
.
Whereas the braid groups
B_{n}
exhibit

homological stability
,
i.e. if
n\geq 3
, then
H_{k}(B_{n};\mathbb{Q})\cong H_{k}(B_{n+1};\mathbb{Q})
for all
k

[
Arn69
]
, the pure braid groups
P_{n}
only satisfy representational stability
[
CF13
]
.
For instance,
H^{\bullet}(P_{n};\mathbb{Q})
is an exterior algebra on
\binom{n}{2}
generators modulo a
3
-term relation
[
Arn69
]
and
H^{\bullet}({\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{1,n};\mathbb{Q})
is the even degree part of an exterior algebra on
n-1
generators
[
CHKV16
]
. The numbers of generators of both algebras increase with
n
.
The cohomology of
{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}
is equal to the

\SG_{n}
-invariant cohomology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
. So, analogously to the stabilization of
H^{\bullet}(B_{n})
, Theorem
3.1
implies the large-
n
cohomological stability of
{\operatorname{U\Gamma}}_{g,n}
.

3.2.
The Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

Our proof of Theorem
3.1

makes heavy use of tools from
[
CHKV16
]
.
Following
[
CHKV16
]
, we abbreviate
\mathrm{H}=H^{1}(F_{g})
and think of it as an
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
-module. The action of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
on
\mathrm{H}
factors through the action of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathrm{H}
. The
q-th exterior power
of
\mathrm{H}
is denoted as
\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}
. As
\rank H=g
, the determinant representation is recovered by
\bigwedge\nolimits^{g}\mathrm{H}=\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
. As before, we only work with modules over rational coefficients.

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix
g,n
with
g\geq 2
,
n\geq 0
.
There are two Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences with
E_{2}
pages given by

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}

\displaystyle=H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}\right)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}

\displaystyle=H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}\right)

for

with

and
q\leq\min(g,n)
,
and
E_{2}^{p,q}=\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}=0
for all other values of
p,q
.
Both spectral sequences converge:

E_{2}^{p,q}\Rightarrow H^{p+q}({\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})^{\SG_{n}}
and

\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}\Rightarrow H^{p+q}({\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n};\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}})^{\SG_{n}}
.

The argument works along the lines of Section 3.2 of

[
CHKV16
]
followed by an application of Schur–Weyl duality and the projection to the
\SG_{n}
-invariant cohomology.
A similar argument can be found in
[

, Lemma 4.1]
where it is used to prove representational stability of

.

Proof.

For
g\geq 2
and
n\geq 0
, the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence associated to the group extension

is a first-quadrant spectral sequence with the second page given by

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});H^{q}(F_{g}^{n};\mathbb{Q}))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}({\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
(see
[
CHKV16
, Section 3.2]
).
The virtual cohomological dimension of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{n})
is
2g-3

[

]
,
so if

, then
E_{2}^{p,q}=\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}=0
.
By the Künneth formula, the
\SG_{n}
-module
H^{q}(F_{g}^{n};\mathbb{Q})

vanishes if

and for
0\leq q\leq n
it
is obtained by inducing the
\SG_{q}\times\SG_{n-q}
-module
\mathrm{H}^{\wedge q}\otimes V_{(n-q)}
to
\SG_{n}
, (see
[

, Lemma 3.4]
for details),

H^{q}(F_{g}^{n};\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{Ind}^{\SG_{n}}_{\SG_{q}\times\SG_{n-q}}\left(H^{\wedge q}\otimes V_{(n-q)}\right),

where

is the trivial representation of
\SG_{n-q}
and
\mathrm{H}^{\wedge q}
is
\mathrm{H}^{\otimes q}\otimes V_{(1^{q})}
with

the alternating representation of
\SG_{q}
and
\SG_{q}
acts on

by permuting the entries of the tensor product.

We can set up a similar spectral sequence with coefficients twisted by
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
with second page
\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});H^{q}(F_{g}^{n};\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}({\operatorname{\Gamma}}_{g,n};\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Note that
F_{g}^{n}
acts trivially on
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
. So, applying the Künneth formula to expand

as an
\SG_{n}
-module gives

H^{q}(F_{g}^{n};\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}})=\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\operatorname{Ind}^{\SG_{n}}_{\SG_{q}\times\SG_{n-q}}\left(H^{\wedge q}\otimes V_{(n-q)}\right),

which only differs by the

-action on
\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Schur–Weyl duality gives the irreducible decomposition of

as a module over
{\operatorname{GL}}(\mathrm{H})\times\SG_{q}
(see, e.g.,
[

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\wedge q}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash q}W_{\lambda}\otimes V_{\lambda^{\prime}},

where we sum over all partitions
\lambda
of

g

is the transposed partition. Here,

and

are the irreducible

\SG_{q}
-representations associated to

.

As

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}

\displaystyle=H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\operatorname{Ind}^{\SG_{n}}_{\SG_{q}\times\SG_{n-q}}\left(H^{\wedge q}\otimes V_{(n-q)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash q}H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});W_{\lambda}\right)\otimes\operatorname{Ind}^{\SG_{n}}_{\SG_{q}\times\SG_{n-q}}\left(V_{\lambda^{\prime}}\otimes V_{(n-q)}\right).

If we project to the trivial

\displaystyle(E_{2}^{p,q})^{\SG_{n}}

\displaystyle=H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});W_{(1^{q})}\right).

In the stable regime, the Euler characteristics

This can be explained as another consequence of Proposition
3.2
,
and the following two technical lemmas.

For a graph
G
, the first cohomology
H^{1}(G)=H^{1}(G;\mathbb{Q})
is a representation of its automorphism group
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G)
. Let
H^{1}(G)^{*}
be the corresponding dual representation.

Lemma 3.3
.

H^{1}(G)\cong H^{1}(G)^{*}

as
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G)
-representations.

Proof.

Each graph
G
comes with an
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G)
-invariant
symmetric positive definite bilinear form
H_{1}(G)\otimes H_{1}(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
,
the graph Laplacian
(see, e.g.,
[
Bak11
, 3.1.1]
). Dually, we have an
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G)
-invariant pairing on
H^{1}(G)
giving the stated isomorphism of
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G)
-representations.
∎

Let
\mathrm{H}^{*}
be the
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
-representation dual to
\mathrm{H}

and
e(H^{\bullet}\left(G;M\right))=\sum\limits_{p}(-1)^{p}\dim(H^{p}\left(G;M\right))
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
g\geq 2
and
q\geq 0
,

e\left(H^{\bullet}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}\right)\right)=e\left(H^{\bullet}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}^{*}\right)\right)
.

Proof.

Using standard arguments that relate Culler–Vogtmann outer space to the rational group cohomology of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{n})
, we are looking for a cochain model for the stated cohomologies.
Let
K_{g}
be the
spine of outer space
,
i.e.
K_{g}
is a cubical complex in which each
k
-cube
corresponds to an isomorphism class of a forested graph
(G,\Phi,\mu)

which is equipped with a

, an isomorphism
\mu:\pi_{1}(G)\rightarrow F_{g}
. Each cube is oriented by ordering the edges in
\Phi
.
The group
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
acts on

by composition with the marking.
This action is not free, but it has finite stabilizers.
Most importantly,
K_{g}
is contractible
[
CV86
]
.
Let
M
be
\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}
or
\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}^{*}
, then by standard arguments

H^{\bullet}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});M\right)=H^{\bullet}\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})}(C_{\bullet}(K_{g}),M)\right)

(see, e.g.,
[
Bro82
, Ch. 7]
).
The action of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
on the tuples
(G,\Phi,\mu)
fixes the
isomorphism class of the forested graph and acts transitively on the marking.
The stabilizer of this action is
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})}(C_{\bullet}(K_{g}),M)=\mathrm{Hom}(C_{\bullet}(K_{g}),M)^{{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})}=\bigoplus_{[G,\Phi]}\left({\operatorname{sign}}_{(G,\Phi)}\otimes M\right)^{{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)},

where we take the sum over all isomorphism classes of
forested graphs of rank
g

or equivalently over all
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
-orbits of
isomorphism classes of marked forested graphs of rank
g
.
Here,
{\operatorname{sign}}_{(G,\Phi)}
is the orientation module, the one-dimensional representation of
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
that is given by
\alpha\mapsto{\operatorname{sign}}(\alpha_{\Phi})
with
\alpha_{\Phi}
the permutation that
\alpha\in{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
induces on the forest edges.
The group

{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
acts on
M
via the map
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)\rightarrow{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
that any
chosen marking representative
\pi_{1}(G)\rightarrow F_{g}
induces.
As a
{\operatorname{Aut}}(G,\Phi)
-representation,
\mathrm{H}
is hence
equal to
H^{1}(G)
. Therefore, due to Lemma
3.3
,
we may replace the right-most
M
in the displayed formula above with
either
\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}H^{1}(G)
, or
\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}(H^{1}(G)^{*})\cong(\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}H^{1}(G))^{*}
.
So, in the stated equality, both sides can be computed using cochain complexes with the same generators in each degree. The Euler characteristic only depends on the dimensions of the cochain spaces and not on the differentials.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

If
n\geq g\geq 2
, then
e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})=(-1)^{g}e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})
.

Proof.

There is an isomorphism of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
-representations:

\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}=\bigwedge\nolimits^{g}\mathrm{H}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}\cong\bigwedge\nolimits^{g-q}\mathrm{H}^{*}
.
This can be seen by computing the characters of both

\bigwedge\nolimits^{g}\mathrm{H}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}
and
\bigwedge\nolimits^{g-q}\mathrm{H}^{*}

and by observing that they differ by a multiple of the character of
(\bigwedge\nolimits^{g})^{\otimes 2}
,
which is the same as the trivial representation of
{\operatorname{GL}}_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
, because
\det(h)^{2}=1
for each
h\in{\operatorname{GL}}_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
(see, e.g.,
[
Sta97
, Ch. 7.A.2]
).

Hence, for
n\geq g\geq 2
, we have for the
E_{2}
pages of the spectral sequences in Proposition
3.2
:

\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}\mathrm{H}\right)\cong H^{p}\left({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\bigwedge\nolimits^{g-q}\mathrm{H}^{*}\right).

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
3.4
that
\sum_{p}(-1)^{p}\dim\widetilde{E}_{2}^{p,q}=\sum_{p}(-1)^{p}\dim E_{2}^{p,g-q}
.
So,

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}})=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}\dim E_{2}^{p,q}=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p+g-q}\dim E_{2}^{p,g-q}=(-1)^{g}e^{\mathrm{odd}}(\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}^{\SG_{n}}).\qed

Apart from the (up-to-sign) equality of the Euler characteristics, our argument does not tell us how even and odd
\SG_{n}
-invariant stable
n\rightarrow\infty
cohomology of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
are related.

Moreover, the
E_{2}
page of the spectral sequences in Proposition
3.2
contains the whole (twisted) cohomology of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
(e.g.

E_{2}^{p,0}=H^{p}({\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g});\mathbb{Q})
).
So, we also do not explain why the Euler characteristics of the large-
n
stable
\SG_{n}
-invariant cohomologies of
\mathcal{MG}_{g,n}
are so small
in comparison to the Euler characteristic of
{\operatorname{Out}}(F_{g})
.

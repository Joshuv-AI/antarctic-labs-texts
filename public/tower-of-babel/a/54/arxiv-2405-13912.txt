Matrix Denoising with Doubly Heteroscedastic Noise: Fundamental Limits and Optimal Spectral Methods
Yihan Zhang, Marco Mondelli

Abstract
We study the matrix denoising problem of estimating the singular vectors of a rank- 1 1 signal corrupted by noise with both column and row correlations. Existing works are either unable to pinpoint the exact asymptotic estimation error or, when they do so, the resulting approaches (e.g., based on whitening or singular value shrinkage) remain vastly suboptimal. On top of this, most of the literature has focused on the special case of estimating the left singular vector of the signal when the noise only possesses row correlation (one-sided heteroscedasticity). In contrast, our work establishes the information-theoretic and algorithmic limits of matrix denoising with doubly heteroscedastic noise. We characterize the exact asymptotic minimum mean square error, and design a novel spectral estimator with rigorous optimality guarantees: under a technical condition, it attains positive correlation with the signals whenever information-theoretically possible and, for one-sided heteroscedasticity, it also achieves the Bayes-optimal error. Numerical experiments demonstrate the significant advantage of our theoretically principled method with the state of the art. The proofs draw connections with statistical physics and approximate message passing, departing drastically from standard random matrix theory techniques.

Proof techniques.

We take a completely different route from classical approaches in statistics and random matrix theory (e.g., whitening and shrinkage), and instead exploit tools from statistical physics and the theory of approximate message passing. In particular, the MMSE for the whitened signals

Moreover, for one-sided heteroscedasticity, this MMSE coincides with that for estimating the true signal on the homoscedastic side.
Evaluating the Bayes-optimal estimators requires solving high-dimensional integrals that are computationally intractable. \displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{n}u^{*}{v^{*}}^{\top}+W\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d},

Proof strategy.

To derive the characterizations in
Theorem

4.2
, we write the posterior distribution of
u^{*},v^{*}
given
Y
in a Gibbs form, i.e., its density is the exponential of a Hamiltonian normalized by a partition function.
The interpolation argument relates the log-partition function (also referred to as the ‘free energy’) of the posterior to that of the posteriors of two Gaussian location models.
Since i.i.d. Gaussianity is key to this approach, the challenge is to handle noise covariances.
Our idea is to incorporate the covariances into the priors.
In terms of the Hamiltonian, the model is equivalent to the estimation of the whitened signals
\Xi^{-1/2}u^{*},\Sigma^{-1/2}v^{*}
, whose priors have covariances, in the presence of i.i.d. Gaussian noise.
We then manage to carry out the interpolation argument for the equivalent model and evaluate the free energy of the corresponding Gaussian location models.

Specifically, let us starting by writing down the expression of the posterior distribution after setting up some notation.
For
u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let
\widetilde{u}\coloneqq\Xi^{-1/2}u,\widetilde{v}\coloneqq\Sigma^{-1/2}v
.
Define the densities

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})\coloneqq\sqrt{\det(\Xi)}\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(\Xi^{1/2}\widetilde{u}),\qquad\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{v})\coloneqq\sqrt{\det(\Sigma)}\,\mathrm{d}Q^{\otimes d}(\Sigma^{1/2}\widetilde{v}),

where the determinant factors ensure that the integrals equal
1
.
With
P=Q={\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
, we have
\widetilde{P}={\mathcal{N}}(0_{n},\Xi^{-1}),\widetilde{Q}={\mathcal{N}}(0_{d},\Sigma^{-1})
, and from Bayes’ rule the posterior of

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}P(u,v\,|\,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}(\gamma)}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(u)\,\mathrm{d}Q^{\otimes d}(v),

(4.11)

where the Hamiltonian and the partition function are given respectively by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{n}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z\widetilde{v}+\frac{\gamma}{n}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}-\frac{\gamma}{2n}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}(\gamma)\coloneqq\hskip-3.99994pt\int\hskip-3.99994pt\int\hskip-1.99997pt\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(u)\,\mathrm{d}Q^{\otimes d}(v)=\hskip-3.99994pt\int\hskip-3.99994pt\int\hskip-1.99997pt\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{v}).

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(\gamma)

The free energy, and hence the MMSE, are expected to be sensitive to the priors.
Indeed, this is already the case in the homoscedastic setting
\Xi=I_{n},\Sigma=I_{d}

[
52
]
.
An extension towards general i.i.d. priors is a challenging open problem and,
in fact, without posing additional assumptions on
\Xi,\Sigma
, it is unclear whether a single-letter expression for free energy and MMSE is possible.

At this point, the MMSE can be derived from the above characterization of free energy.
Indeed, let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\gamma>0:{\mathcal{F}}\textnormal{ has a unique maximizer }(q_{u}^{*},q_{v}^{*})\textnormal{ over }{\mathcal{C}}(\gamma,\alpha)\right\}.

The envelope theorem
[
51
, Corollary 4]
ensures that
{\mathcal{D}}(\alpha)
is equal to
{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
up to a countable set.
Using algebraic relations between free energy and MMSE, we prove
Equations

4.7
and
4.8
for all
\gamma\in{\mathcal{D}}(\alpha)
(and, thus, for almost every
\gamma>0
).
Then, using the Nishimori identity (
Proposition

G.4
) and the fact that the ESDs of
\Xi,\Sigma
are upper and lower bounded by constants independent of
n
and

also follows. The formal arguments are contained in
Appendix

5
Spectral estimator

This section introduces a spectral estimator that meets the weak recovery threshold and, for one-sided heteroscedasticity, attains the Bayes-optimal error.
Suppose that the following condition holds

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{4}}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Xi}^{-2}\right]

\displaystyle>1,

(5.1)

which is equivalent to
Equation

4.6
.
Under this condition, the fixed point equations

4.5
have a unique pair of positive solutions
(q_{u}^{*},q_{v}^{*})
. For convenience, we also define the rescalings
\mu^{*}\coloneqq\lambda q_{v}^{*}/\delta,\nu^{*}\coloneqq\lambda q_{u}^{*}
, and the auxiliary quantities

b^{*}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda\nu^{*}+\overline{\Sigma}}\right],\qquad c^{*}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\lambda}{\lambda\mu^{*}+\overline{\Xi}}\right].

(5.2)

Now, we pre-process the data matrix

\displaystyle A^{*}\coloneqq\lambda(\lambda(\mu^{*}+b^{*})I_{n}+\Xi)^{-1/2}\Xi^{-1/2}A\Sigma^{-1/2}(\lambda(\nu^{*}+c^{*})I_{d}+\Sigma)^{-1/2},

from which we obtain the spectral estimators

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{u}&\coloneqq\eta_{u}\sqrt{n}\frac{\Xi^{1/2}(\lambda(\mu^{*}+b^{*})I_{n}+\Xi)^{-1/2}(\lambda\mu^{*}I_{n}+\Xi)u_{1}(A^{*})}{\left\|\Xi^{1/2}(\lambda(\mu^{*}+b^{*})I_{n}+\Xi)^{-1/2}(\lambda\mu^{*}I_{n}+\Xi)u_{1}(A^{*})\right\|_{2}},\end{split}

(5.4a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{v}&\coloneqq\eta_{v}\sqrt{d}\frac{\Sigma^{1/2}(\lambda(\nu^{*}+c^{*})I_{d}+\Sigma)^{-1/2}(\lambda\nu^{*}I_{d}+\Sigma)v_{1}(A^{*})}{\left\|\Sigma^{1/2}(\lambda(\nu^{*}+c^{*})I_{d}+\Sigma)^{-1/2}(\lambda\nu^{*}I_{d}+\Sigma)v_{1}(A^{*})\right\|_{2}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\eta_{u}&\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\mu^{*}}{\lambda\mu^{*}+1}},\qquad\eta_{v}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\nu^{*}}{\lambda\nu^{*}+1}}.\end{split}

Proof strategy.

The design and analysis of the spectral estimator in
Equation

5.4
comprise two steps, detailed in
Appendix

E
. The
first step
is to present an AMP algorithm dubbed Bayes-AMP for matrix denoising with doubly heteroscedastic noise. Specifically, its iterates are updated as

\displaystyle u^{t}

\displaystyle=\Xi^{-1}A\Sigma^{-1}\widetilde{v}^{t}-b_{t}\Xi^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{t-1},\quad\widetilde{u}^{t}=g_{t}^{*}(u^{t}),\quad c_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\tr((\nabla g_{t}^{*}(u^{t}))\Xi^{-1}),

(5.11)

\displaystyle v^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\Sigma^{-1}A^{\top}\Xi^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{t}-c_{t}\Sigma^{-1}\widetilde{v}^{t},\quad\widetilde{v}^{t+1}=f_{t+1}^{*}(v^{t+1}),\quad b_{t+1}=\frac{1}{n}\tr((\nabla f_{t+1}^{*}(v^{t+1}))\Sigma^{-1}),

where
\nabla
denotes the Jacobian matrix, and the functions
g_{t}^{*},f_{t+1}^{*}
are specified below in
Equation

5.12
. As common in AMP algorithms, the iterates
Equation

5.11
are accompanied with a state evolution which accurately tracks their behavior via a simple deterministic recursion: the joint empirical distribution of
(u^{*},v^{*},u^{t},v^{t+1})
converges to the random variables
(U^{*},V^{*},U_{t},V_{t+1})
, see
Proposition

E.1
for a formal statement and the recursive description of the laws of such random variables. Then, the name ‘Bayes-AMP’ is motivated by the fact that
g_{t}^{*},f_{t+1}^{*}
are the posterior-mean denoisers given by

\displaystyle g_{t}^{*}(u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[U^{*}\,|\,U_{t}=u\right],\qquad f_{t+1}^{*}(v)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[V^{*}\,|\,V_{t+1}=v\right].

(5.12)

Remarkably, Bayes-AMP operates on
\Xi^{-1}A\Sigma^{-1}
, as opposed to the widely adopted ansatz of considering the whitened matrix
\Xi^{-1/2}A\Sigma^{-1/2}
. The advantage of operating on
\Xi^{-1}A\Sigma^{-1}
is that the fixed point of the corresponding state evolution matches the extremizers of the free energy in
Equation

4.5
. This would
not
be the case if Bayes-AMP used the whitening
\Xi^{-1/2}A\Sigma^{-1/2}
.
Indeed, one can repeat the analysis of an AMP that operates on
\Xi^{-1/2}A\Sigma^{-1/2}
. The fixed point equations of the resulting state evolution do not match the information-theoretically optimal one in
Equation

4.5
. In particular, the weak recovery threshold coming out of this approach is strictly larger than the optimal one in
Equation

4.6
, as long as at least one of
\Xi,\Sigma
is not a multiple of the identity. Since these derivations led to suboptimal results, the details were left out from the paper.

The design of Bayes-AMP and the proof of its state evolution follow a two-step reduction detailed in
Appendix

F
.
Using a change of variables, we show in
Section

F.2
that Bayes-AMP can be realized by an auxiliary AMP with non-separable denoising functions (meaning that
g_{t},f_{t+1}
cannot be written as univariate functions applied component-wise) operating on
\Xi^{-1/2}A\Sigma^{-1/2}=\frac{\lambda}{n}\widetilde{u}^{*}(\widetilde{v}^{*})^{\top}+\widetilde{W}
.
Then, in
Section

F.1
we simulate the auxiliary AMP using a standard AMP operating on the i.i.d. Gaussian matrix
\widetilde{W}
, whose state evolution has been established in
[
13
,
34
]
.

However, Bayes-AMP by itself is not a practical algorithm since it needs a warm start, i.e., an initialization that achieves non-trivial error. Thus, the
second step
is to design a spectral estimator that solves the fixed point equation of Bayes-AMP, which turns out to be an eigen-equation for
A^{*}
.

To offer the readers an intuition on how the spectral estimators arise from Bayes-AMP,
we now heuristically derive the form
Equation

5.3
of
A^{*}
and the expression
Equation

5.4
of the spectral estimator. To do so, we note that the large-

limits of
c_{t},b_{t+1}
coincide with the auxiliary quantities
c^{*},b^{*}
defined in
Equation

5.2
. Furthermore, when the priors of
u^{*},v^{*}
are Gaussian,
Equation

5.12
reduces to

g_{t}^{*}(u)=\lambda(\lambda\mu^{*}\Xi^{-1}+I_{n})^{-1}u,\qquad f_{t+1}^{*}(v)=\lambda(\lambda\nu^{*}\Sigma^{-1}+I_{d})^{-1}v,

where we recall that
\mu^{*}=\lambda q_{v}^{*}/\delta
and
\nu^{*}=\lambda q_{u}^{*}
are rescalings of the non-trivial solution
(q_{u}^{*},q_{v}^{*})
of
Equation

4.5

the fixed points of the iteration

5.11
, after some manipulations we have

\mathfrak{g}(\Xi)u=A^{*}\mathfrak{f}(\Sigma)v,\qquad\mathfrak{f}(\Sigma)v={A^{*}}^{\top}\mathfrak{g}(\Xi)u,

where
A^{*}
is given in
Equation

5.3
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(\Xi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\lambda}(\lambda(\mu^{*}+b^{*})I_{n}+\Xi)^{1/2}(\lambda\mu^{*}I_{n}+\Xi)^{-1}\Xi^{1/2},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\lambda}(\lambda(\nu^{*}+c^{*})I_{d}+\Sigma)^{1/2}(\lambda\nu^{*}I_{d}+\Sigma)^{-1}\Sigma^{1/2}.

6
Concluding remarks

In this work, we establish information-theoretic limits and propose an efficient spectral method with optimality guarantees, for matrix estimation with doubly heteroscedastic noise.
On the one hand, under Gaussian priors, we give a rigorous characterization of the MMSE; on the other hand, we present a spectral estimator that
(i)
achieves the information-theoretic weak recovery threshold, and
(ii)
is Bayes-optimal for the estimation of one of the signals, when the noise is heteroscedastic only on the other side. While our analysis focuses on rank-1 estimation, we expect that all results admit proper extensions to rank-
r
signals, where
r
is a constant independent on
n,d
.

The design and analysis of the spectral estimator draws connections with approximate message passing and, along the way, we introduce a Bayes-AMP algorithm which could be of independent interest. In this paper, we employ Bayes-AMP solely as a proof technique. However, one could use the spectral method designed here as an initialization of Bayes-AMP itself, after suitably correcting its iterates. This strategy has been successfully carried out for i.i.d. Gaussian noise in
[
56
]
and for rotationally invariant noise in
[
55
,
81
]
. Bayes-AMP is well equipped to exploit signal priors more informative than the Gaussian one, and AMP algorithms are known to achieve the information-theoretically optimal estimation error for low-rank matrix inference
[
56
,
6
]
. Nevertheless, we point out two obstacles towards doing so in the presence of doubly heteroscedastic noise. First, for general priors, establishing the information-theoretic limits remains a challenging open problem, and it is unclear whether a low-dimensional characterization of the free energy (and, hence, of the MMSE) is possible. Second, even for Gaussian priors, Bayes-AMP reduces to the proposed spectral estimator, which is not Bayes-optimal for the general case of doubly heteroscedastic noise.

Finally, the proposed spectral estimator makes non-trivial use of the covariances
\Xi,\Sigma
, which are assumed to be known.
When such matrices possess additional structure –
e.g., they are sparse
[
21
]
, their inverses are sparse
[
19
]
or they are circulant or Toeplitz
[
75
]
– their consistent estimation is possible, see also the survey
[
20
]
. However, in general,
\Xi,\Sigma
cannot be consistently estimated from the data when
n
and
d
grow proportionally. Thus, a challenging open problem is to construct estimators that retain comparable performance without knowing the noise covariances.
The paper
[
33
]
addresses the challenge of unknown covariances by considering a modified model where one additionally observes an independent copy of noise. The statistician can then estimate the covariance from the noise-only observation and use it as a surrogate of the true covariance for estimating the signals from the spiked model. It is possible to derive similar results in the doubly heteroskedastic setting considered in our paper.
If the covariances are completely unknown, then our model (with Gaussian priors) is equivalent to a spiked matrix model with a certain bi-rotationally invariant noise. This problem is expected to exhibit rather different behaviors than when covariances are known, see
[
7
,
29
]
for recent progress on understanding the statistical and computational limits for such models.

Appendix A
Proof of
Proposition

4.1

We eliminate
q_{u}
and write a fixed point equation only involving
q_{v}
:

\displaystyle q_{v}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}}{\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1}\right].

Denote the RHS by
f(q_{v})
.
Recall that we are only interested in non-negative solutions
(q_{u},q_{v})
.
So let us restrict attention on
f
to the domain
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.
We have
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(q_{u})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\gamma\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}}{\left(\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1\right)^{2}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\left(\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1\right)^{2}}\right]>0,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\alpha\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Xi}^{-2}\right],

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(q_{v})

\displaystyle=-2\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\left(\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1\right)^{2}}\right]^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\gamma^{2}\overline{\Sigma}^{-3}}{\left(\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1\right)^{3}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\gamma\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}}{\left(\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-2}}{\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1\right)^{2}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\alpha^{2}\gamma^{2}\overline{\Xi}^{-3}}{\left(\alpha\gamma q_{v}\overline{\Xi}^{-1}+1\right)^{3}}\right]\right)<0,

\displaystyle\lim_{q_{v}\to\infty}f(q_{v})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Xi}^{-1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}}{\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Xi}^{-1}\right]\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1}\right]\in(0,\infty).

It then becomes evident that a non-trivial fixed point
q_{v}>0
exists if and only if
f^{\prime}(0)>1
and in this case, the non-trivial fixed point is unique.

Finally, by the first equation in
Equation

4.5
, there is a non-trivial fixed point
q_{u}
if and only if there is a non-trivial fixed point
q_{v}
, which completes the proof.

Appendix B
Auxiliary Gaussian channel

We formally introduce here the auxiliary model mentioned in
Section

4
.
Consider a Gaussian channel with blocklength
n
, input
x^{*}
, output
Y
, anisotropic Gaussian noise
\Sigma^{1/2}Z
and SNR
\gamma
:

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma}x^{*}+\Sigma^{1/2}Z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

(B.1)

where

\displaystyle(x^{*},Z)

\displaystyle\sim P^{\otimes n}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(0_{n},I_{n}).

By similar derivations as in
Section

4
, the posterior distribution of
x^{*}
given
Y
can be written as

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}P(x\,|\,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{n}(\gamma)}\exp\left(H_{n}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(x),

where the Hamiltonian and the partition function are

\displaystyle H_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\gamma{x^{*}}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}x+\sqrt{\gamma}Z^{\top}\Sigma^{-1/2}x-\frac{\gamma}{2}x^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}x,

\displaystyle Z_{n}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left(H_{n}(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(x).

Define the free energy as

\displaystyle F_{n}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\log Z_{n}(\gamma)\right].

With
P={\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
,
Z_{n}(\gamma)
becomes a Gaussian integral that can be computed as below using
Proposition

G.1
:

\displaystyle Z_{n}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(\gamma\Sigma^{-1}+I_{n})}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma\Sigma^{-1}x^{*}+\sqrt{\gamma}\Sigma^{-1/2}Z\right)^{\top}\left(\gamma\Sigma^{-1}+I_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\gamma\Sigma^{-1}x^{*}+\sqrt{\gamma}\Sigma^{-1/2}Z\right)\right).

Therefore, by
Proposition

G.2
,

\displaystyle\plim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(\gamma\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Sigma}^{-2}\left(\gamma\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1\right)^{-1}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\gamma\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}+1\right)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1+\gamma\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}\right)\right]\right).

(B.2)

The above functional is nothing but
\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(\gamma)
introduced in
Equation

4.15
which will play an important role in characterizing the free energy of the original model
Equation

4.1
.

Appendix C
Proof of
Theorem

4.4

Before diving into the proof, we make further notation adjustments for the ease of applying the interpolation argument.
Specifically, we will henceforth assume
\gamma=1
by incorporating the actual value of
\gamma
into the prior distributions
P,Q
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}Q(x)=1.

This is obviously equivalent to the previous setting.
So we can drop the dependence on
\gamma
and write
\mmse_{n},{\mathcal{Z}}_{n},{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
for
\mmse_{n}(\gamma),{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}(\gamma),{\mathcal{F}}_{n}(\gamma)
defined in
Equations

4.2
,
4.13
and
4.14
.

We will also assume that
\Xi,\Sigma
are diagonal.
This is without loss of generality since the Gaussianity of
P,Q,\widetilde{W}
ensures that both the prior distributions and the noise matrix are rotationally invariant.
Furthermore, we truncate
P,Q
so that they are supported on
[-K,K]
for a constant
K>0
.
The approximation error in the free energy due to truncation can be made arbitrarily small if
K
is sufficiently large, since the free energy is pseudo-Lipschitz in the prior distribution with respect to the Wasserstein-
2
metric.

The proof follows an interpolation argument
[
10
,
52
,
53
]
with suitable modifications to take care of the noise heteroscedasticity featured by the covariances
\Xi,\Sigma
.
To start with, define the interpolating models:

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}{u}^{*}{v^{*}}^{\top}+\Xi^{1/2}Z\Sigma^{1/2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d},

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{u}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\alpha q_{1}(t)}{u}^{*}+\Xi^{1/2}Z^{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{v}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{q_{2}(t)}{v}^{*}+\Sigma^{1/2}Z^{v}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t)\geq 0
are to be determined and

\displaystyle(u^{*},v^{*},Z,Z^{u},Z^{v})

\displaystyle\sim P^{\otimes n}\otimes Q^{\otimes d}\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(0_{nd},I_{nd})\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(0_{n},I_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(0_{d},I_{d}).

(C.1)

By definition,
Y_{0}=Y
is the model that we would like to understand, and
Y_{t}^{u},Y_{t}^{v}
are instances of Gaussian channels in
Equation

B.1
whose free energy we have already understood (see
Equation

B.2
).
The idea is that
Y_{t}
serves as a path parametrized by
t\in[0,1]
from the original model
Y
to the target models
(Y_{1}^{u},Y_{1}^{v})
.
The crux of the interpolation argument lies in showing that
Y_{t}
and
(Y_{t}^{u},Y_{t}^{v})
are equivalent (at the level of free energy) along the path.

To study the interpolating models
(Y_{t},Y_{t}^{u},Y_{t}^{v})
, define the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1},q_{2})&\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z\widetilde{v}+\frac{1-t}{n}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}-\frac{1-t}{2n}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\phantom{=}~+\alpha q_{1}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+\sqrt{\alpha q_{1}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}-\frac{\alpha q_{1}}{2}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\phantom{=}~+q_{2}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}+\sqrt{q_{2}}\widetilde{v}^{\top}Z^{v}-\frac{q_{2}}{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

(C.2)

Then the posterior distribution of
({u}^{*},{v}^{*})
given
(Y_{t},Y_{t}^{u},Y_{t}^{v})
is

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}P({u},{v}\,|\,Y_{t},Y_{t}^{u},Y_{t}^{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v;q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(u)\,\mathrm{d}Q^{\otimes d}(v).

(C.3)

Let the partition function be

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v;q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(u)\,\mathrm{d}Q^{\otimes d}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{v}).

(C.4)

Define the free energy as

\displaystyle f_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\log{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}\right].

(C.5)

The Gibbs bracket
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle_{n,t}
denotes the expectation with respect to the posterior distribution in
Equation

C.3
:

\displaystyle\left\langle g(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\right\rangle_{n,t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}g(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{v}),

(C.6)

for any
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that the expectation exists.
That is,

\displaystyle\left\langle g(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\right\rangle_{n,t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[g(\widetilde{u}^{*},\widetilde{v}^{*})\,|\,Y_{t},Y_{t}^{u},Y_{t}^{v}\right],

where we recall the notation

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}^{*}\coloneqq\Xi^{-1/2}u^{*},\qquad\widetilde{v}^{*}\coloneqq\Sigma^{-1/2}v^{*}.

(C.7)

We will also use the notation
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle_{n}
for the Gibbs bracket with respect to the original posterior
\,\mathrm{d}P(u,v\,|\,Y)
in
Equation

4.11
.

Lemma C.1
.

Consider
f_{n}(t)
defined in
Equation

C.5
with
t\in\{0,1\}
.
Assume that
q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0)
satisfy

\displaystyle q_{1}(0)

\displaystyle\geq 0,\quad q_{2}(0)\geq 0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{1}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}q_{2}(0)=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}_{n}+{\mathcal{O}}(q_{1}(0)+q_{2}(0)),

(C.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha q_{1}(1)\gamma)+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1))+o_{K},

(C.9)

where
\lim_{K\to\infty}o_{K}=0
.

Proof.

To show the first statement
Equation

C.8
, let us control
f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
.
Denoting

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha q_{1}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+\sqrt{\alpha q_{1}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}-\frac{\alpha q_{1}}{2}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}+q_{2}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}+\sqrt{q_{2}}\widetilde{v}^{\top}Z^{v}-\frac{q_{2}}{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}

and recalling the Gibbs bracket notation
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,0}}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left\langle\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right)\right\rangle_{n}\right],

(C.10)

where the outer expectation is over all randomness in
{u}^{*},{v}^{*},Z^{u},Z^{v}
.
The second equality above follows since

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}_{n}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})+{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0)).

We will derive double-sided bounds on
f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
.

To upper bound it, use Jensen’s inequality
\mathbb{E}\left[\log(\cdot)\right]\leq\log\mathbb{E}\left[\cdot\right]
on the partial expectation over
Z^{u},Z^{v}
in
Equation

C.10
:

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{u}^{*},{v}^{*}}\left[\log\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\left\langle\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right)\right\rangle_{n}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{u}^{*},{v}^{*}}\left[\log\left\langle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right)\right]\right\rangle_{n}\right],

where the equality is legit since
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle_{n}
does not depend on
Z^{u},Z^{v}
.
By the Gaussian integral formula (
Proposition

G.1
), the inner expectation equals

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\alpha q_{1}(0)\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+q_{2}(0)\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}\right).

Replacing the Gibbs bracket with
\max
, we obtain an upper bound:

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{u}^{*},{v}^{*}}\left[\log\max_{(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\in\Xi^{-1/2}[-K,K]^{n}\times\Sigma^{-1/2}[-K,K]^{d}}\exp\left(\alpha q_{1}(0)\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+q_{2}(0)\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha q_{1}(0)\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}+q_{2}(0)\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}\frac{d}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\alpha q_{1}(0)K^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Xi})}+\frac{2\alpha q_{2}(0)K^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Sigma})},

(C.11)

where the last inequality holds for all sufficiently large
n
by
Equation

3.3
and
n\asymp d
.

To lower bound
f_{n}-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
, we use Jensen’s inequality again but this time on the Gibbs bracket in
Equation

C.10
:

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right\rangle_{n}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{u}^{*},{v}^{*}}\left[\left\langle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right]\right\rangle_{n}\right].

(C.12)

Since
(Z^{u},Z^{v})\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0_{n},I_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{N}}(0_{d},I_{d})
, the inner expectation equals

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha q_{1}(0)\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}-\frac{\alpha q_{1}(0)}{2}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}+q_{2}(0)\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}-\frac{q_{2}(0)}{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

So

\max_{(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\in\Xi^{-1/2}[-K,K]^{n}\times\Sigma^{-1/2}[-K,K]^{d}}\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right]\right|\\
\leq\frac{3}{2}\alpha q_{1}(0)\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}nK^{2}+\frac{3}{2}q_{2}(0)\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{2}^{-1}dK^{2}.

(C.13)

Using this, we obtain a lower bound on
f_{n}-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
by replacing the Gibbs bracket on the RHS of
Equation

C.12
with
-\max\left|\cdot\right|
:

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)-{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{{u}^{*},{v}^{*}}\left[\max_{(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})\in\Xi^{-1/2}[-K,K]^{n}\times\Sigma^{-1/2}[-K,K]^{d}}\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[{\mathcal{H}}_{n,0}^{\prime}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(0),q_{2}(0))\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3}{2}\alpha q_{1}(0)\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}-\frac{3}{2}q_{2}(0)\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}\frac{d}{n}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{3\alpha q_{1}(0)K^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Xi})}-\frac{3\alpha q_{2}(0)K^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Sigma})},

(C.14)

where the last inequality holds for all sufficiently large
n
by
Equation

3.3
and
n\asymp d
.
Combining
Equations

C.14
and
C.11
gives the first result
Equation

C.8
.

We then prove the second statement
Equation

C.9
.
Since

is pseudo-Lipschitz as a function of the priors, up to a term

that vanishes as

From the definition

of

, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,1}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,1}^{\prime}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v;q_{1}(1),q_{2}(1))\right)\,\mathrm{d}P^{\otimes n}(u)\,\mathrm{d}Q^{\otimes d}(v)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\det(\Xi)}{\gamma^{n}\det(\Xi/\gamma+\alpha q_{1}(1)I_{n})}}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha q_{1}(1)\widetilde{u}^{*}+\sqrt{\alpha q_{1}(1)}Z^{u}\right)^{\top}\left(\Xi/\gamma+\alpha q_{1}(1)I_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(\alpha q_{1}(1)\widetilde{u}^{*}+\sqrt{\alpha q_{1}(1)}Z^{u}\right)\right)

where in the second equality, we use the Gaussian integral formula (

Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\log{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha q_{1}(1)\gamma\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Xi}^{-1}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1+\alpha q_{1}(1)\gamma\overline{\Xi}^{-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{2}(1)\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}\right]-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\log\left(1+q_{2}(1)\overline{\Sigma}^{-1}\right)\right],

verifying the identity

In the second equality, we have used

Lemma C.2

(Free energy variation)
.

For all
t\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle f_{n}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)q_{2}^{\prime}(t)-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}}{n}-q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\right)\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}}{n}-\alpha q_{1}^{\prime}(t)\right)\right\rangle_{n,t}\right].

(C.15)

Proof.

From the definitions
Equations

C.5
and
C.4
, we compute

\displaystyle f_{n}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right)\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{v})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\right\rangle_{n,t}\right].

By the definition
Equation

C.2
, the time derivative of the Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))&=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{(1-t)n}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z\widetilde{v}-\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}+\frac{1}{2n}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\quad+\alpha q_{1}^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{2\sqrt{q_{1}(t)}}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\\
&\quad+q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{q_{2}(t)}}q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\widetilde{v}^{\top}Z^{v}-\frac{1}{2}q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

(C.16)

The expectation of
\left\langle\partial_{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}\right\rangle_{n,t}
can be computed using the Stein’s lemma (
Proposition

G.7
).
Indeed, let us consider the term

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z\widetilde{v}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}_{i}\widetilde{v}_{j}\right\rangle_{n,t}Z_{i,j}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial\left\langle\widetilde{u}_{i}\widetilde{v}_{j}\right\rangle_{n,t}}{\partial Z_{i,j}}\right]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}_{i}^{2}\widetilde{v}_{j}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}_{i}\widetilde{v}_{j}\right\rangle_{n,t}^{2}\right]\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]-\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right],\end{split}

(C.17)

where the last step is by the Nishimori identity (
Proposition

G.4
).
So the first line of
Equation

C.16
upon taken the Gibbs bracket and the expectation becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right].

Similar cancellations happen for the second and third lines of
Equation

C.16
.
Putting them together, we obtain

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]+\frac{\alpha}{2n}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]+\frac{1}{2n}q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right],

which is the same as
Equation

C.15
with the parentheses opened up.
∎

In what follows, our strategy is:

1.

Show that
\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}
is concentrated around its mean
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]
;

2.

Choose
q_{2}(t)
to be the solution to

\displaystyle q_{2}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right].

Once
Items

1
and
2
are done, we then have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\approx f_{n}(0)=f_{n}(1)-\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\approx\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha q_{1}(1)\gamma)+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1))-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\frac{\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}}{n}-q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\right)\left(\frac{\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}}{n}-\alpha q_{1}^{\prime}(t)\right)\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\approx\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha q_{1}(1)\gamma)+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1))-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)q_{2}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the first line above uses
Equation

C.8
in
Lemma

C.1
;
the second line uses
Equation

C.9
in
Lemma

C.1
and
Lemma

C.2
;
the third line uses
Items

1
and
2
.
This will almost lead to the desired characterization of the free energy
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
in
Theorem

4.4
:

\displaystyle\sup_{q_{v}\geq 0}\inf_{q_{u}\geq 0}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{v})+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{u})-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{u}q_{v}.

Consider the function

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\log{\mathcal{Z}}_{n,t}.

Note that
\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})
also depends on
n,u^{*},v^{*},Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}
, and
\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]=f_{n}(t)
where the expectation is over
(u^{*},v^{*},Z,Z^{u},Z^{v})
distributed according to
Equation

C.1
.

Lemma C.3

(Free energy concentration)
.

Fix a constant
M>0
.
There exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
K,M,\alpha,\overline{\Xi},\overline{\Sigma}
such that for any
t\in[0,1]
,
0\leq q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t)\leq M
and sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

Proof.

Fix
u^{*},v^{*}
.
Consider
\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})
as a function of
(Z,Z^{u},Z^{v})
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{(Z,Z^{u},Z^{v})}\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla_{Z}\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla_{Z^{u}}\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\nabla_{Z^{v}}\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\widetilde{u}_{i}\widetilde{v}_{j}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\sqrt{\alpha q_{1}}\widetilde{u}_{i}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\sqrt{q_{2}}\widetilde{v}_{j}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]+\frac{\alpha q_{1}}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]+\frac{q_{2}}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{n}\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{4}+\frac{\alpha}{n}M\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{n}M\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}\leq\frac{C}{n},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\alpha,M,\overline{\Xi},\overline{\Sigma},K
.
The penultimate line is by Cauchy–Schwarz and the last line holds for all sufficiently large
n
by
Equation

3.3
and
n\asymp d
.
Then by the Gaussian Poincaré inequality (
Proposition

G.8
),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\left|\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

(C.18)

The above result holds for any fixed
u^{*},v^{*}
.
We then verify that
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]
has bounded difference as a function of
u^{*},v^{*}
.
We do so by bounding the derivatives of
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]
with respect to
u_{i}^{*},v_{j}^{*}
for any
i\in[n],j\in[d]
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{*}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{*}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};q_{1},q_{2})\right\rangle_{n,t}\right],

(C.19)

and a similar expression holds for the derivative with respect to
v_{j}^{*}
.
Recall the definition of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}
from
Equation

C.2
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{*}_{i}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v;q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t}{n}v^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}v^{*}(\Xi^{-1})_{i,i}u_{i}+\alpha q_{1}(\Xi^{-1})_{i,i}u_{i},

where we have used the fact that
\Xi
is diagonal.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{*}_{i}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v;q_{1},q_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\cdot dK^{2}\left\|\Sigma\right\|_{2}^{-1}\cdot\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K+\alpha q_{1}\cdot\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K\leq C,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\alpha,M,K,\overline{\Xi},\overline{\Sigma}
.
The last inequality holds for all sufficiently large
n
.
A similar bound holds for
\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{*}_{j}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\Xi^{-1/2}u,\Sigma^{-1/2}v;q_{1},q_{2})
.
This, by
Equation

C.19
, implies that
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]
as a function of
(u^{*},v^{*})
satisfies the bounded difference property with
c_{i}=C/n
(see
Proposition

G.9
).
So by
Proposition

G.9
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u^{*},v^{*}}\left[\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u^{*},v^{*}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{Var}}_{u^{*},v^{*}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right]}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}.

(C.20)

Finally using
Equations

C.18
and
C.20
and the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u^{*},v^{*}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\left|\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right|\right]+\left|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{u^{*},v^{*}}\left[\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z,Z^{u},Z^{v}}\left[\phi_{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\right]\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}},

concluding the proof.
∎

Suppose
a,b\geq 0
are constants.
With
q_{1}=s_{n}a^{2},q_{2}=s_{n}b^{2}
, we can write
{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}
in
Equation

C.2
as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};s_{n}a^{2},s_{n}b^{2})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})+{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})+{\mathcal{H}}_{n,b}^{v}(\widetilde{v}),

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,t}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{n}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z\widetilde{v}+\frac{1-t}{n}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}-\frac{1-t}{2n}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\widetilde{vv}\right\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\alpha s_{n}a^{2}\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+a\sqrt{\alpha s_{n}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}-\frac{\alpha s_{n}a^{2}}{2}\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{n,b}^{v}(\widetilde{v})

\displaystyle\coloneqq s_{n}b^{2}\widetilde{v}^{\top}\widetilde{v}^{*}+b\sqrt{s_{n}}\widetilde{v}^{\top}Z^{v}-\frac{s_{n}b^{2}}{2}\left\|\widetilde{v}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Fix
A\geq 2
.
Define

\displaystyle\phi^{u}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\log\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u}),

\displaystyle\phi^{v}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\log\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,b}^{v}(\widetilde{v})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Q}(\widetilde{v}),

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{1/2\leq a\leq A+1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\phi^{u}(a)-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(a)\right]\right|\right],

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{v}(s_{n})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{1/2\leq b\leq A+1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\phi^{v}(b)-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{v}(b)\right]\right|\right],

(C.21)

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u}),

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{v}(\widetilde{v})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,b}^{b}(\widetilde{v}).

Denote by
\left\langle g(u^{*},v^{*})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}
the conditional expectation of
g(u^{*},v^{*})
given

\displaystyle Y_{t},\quad Y_{t}^{u}(a)\coloneqq a\sqrt{\alpha s_{n}}u^{*}+\Xi^{1/2}Z^{u},\quad Y_{t}^{v}(b)\coloneqq b\sqrt{s_{n}}v^{*}+\Sigma^{1/2}Z^{v},

(C.22)

where the expectation is with respect to the distribution in
Equation

C.1
.

Corollary C.4
.

Let
s_{n}=n^{-1/32}
and
A\leq\sqrt{M/s_{n}}-1/2
for a constant
M>0
independent of
n
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
K,\alpha,\overline{\Xi}
such that for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s_{n}\sqrt{n}}.

Proof.

Note that if
b=0,t=1
, it holds that
{\mathcal{H}}_{n,1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v};s_{n}a^{2},0)={\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})
and
\phi_{1}(s_{n}a^{2},0)=s_{n}\phi^{u}(a)
.
The conclusion then follows immediately from
Lemma

C.3
since by the assumption on
A
,
q_{1}=s_{n}a^{2}\in[0,M]
for any
1/2\leq a\leq A+1/2
.
∎

Lemma C.5
.

Let
s_{n}=n^{-1/32}
.
For all
A\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A-1}\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{ns_{n}}}+\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})}\right),

where
C>0
only depends on
\alpha,\overline{\Xi},K
.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\\
&\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].\end{split}

(C.23)

We will bound the two terms on the RHS separately.
We first bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A-1}\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{A-1}\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a\right)^{1/2}.

(C.24)

The first two derivatives of
\phi^{u}
are

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)&=\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial a}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\exp\left({\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{u})}=\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b},\end{split}

(C.25a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\phi^{u})^{\prime\prime}(a)&=\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\left[\left\langle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial a}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial a}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}^{2}+\left\langle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a^{2}}{\mathcal{H}}_{n,a}^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\\
&=ns_{n}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}^{2}\right]+\frac{\alpha}{n}\left[2\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].\end{split}

(C.25b)

Since there exists
C>0
depending only on
\alpha,\overline{\Xi},K
such that for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\alpha}{n}\left[2\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\alpha K^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Xi})}\eqqcolon C,

(C.26)

the second result
Equation

C.25b
above implies that for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\left((\phi^{u})^{\prime\prime}(a)+C\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{ns_{n}}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(A)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(1)\right]+C(A-1)\right).

(C.27)

To proceed, we compute for any
a
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right],

(C.28)

which is by
Equation

C.25a
.
By definition,

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(2\alpha a\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}+\sqrt{\alpha/s_{n}}\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}-\alpha a\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right),

whose expectation is therefore given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{n\sqrt{s_{n}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]-\frac{\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

(C.29)

Using Stein’s lemma (
Proposition

G.7
), the middle term is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{n\sqrt{s_{n}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}Z^{u}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{n\sqrt{s_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{u}_{i}\left\langle\widetilde{u}_{i}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{n\sqrt{s_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial Z^{u}_{i}}\left\langle\widetilde{u}_{i}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{a\alpha}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]-\frac{a\alpha}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right],

(C.30)

and

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq aC,

(C.31)

where
C
depends only on
\alpha,\overline{\Xi},K
.

Using the last inequality, we can further upper bound the RHS of
Equation

C.27
by
\frac{CA}{ns_{n}}
for some
C
depending only on
\alpha,\overline{\Xi},K
.
Putting this back to
Equation

C.24
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A-1}\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{CA}{ns_{n}(A-1)}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2C}{ns_{n}}},

(C.32)

since
A\geq 2
.

Now it remains to bound the second term on the RHS of
Equation

C.23
which can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)-\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)\right]\right|\right]

(C.33)

using
Equation

C.25a
.
To further bound the RHS, consider the following two functions

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto\phi^{u}(a)+\frac{2\alpha K^{2}a^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Xi})},\quad a\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(a)\right]+\frac{2\alpha K^{2}a^{2}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Xi})}.

They are both differentiable and convex for all sufficiently large
n
since their second derivatives are non-negative by
Equations

C.25b
and
C.26
.
Applying
Proposition

G.10
with the above two functions, taking the expectation and using the triangle inequality, we have that for any
1\leq a\leq A,0<a^{\prime}\leq 1/2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)-\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)\right]\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a+a^{\prime})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a-a^{\prime})\right]+3\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})/a^{\prime}+\frac{8\alpha K^{2}a^{\prime}}{\inf\supp(\overline{\Xi})}.

(C.34)

Then

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a+a^{\prime})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a-a^{\prime})\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(A+a^{\prime})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(1+a^{\prime})\right]\right)-\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(A-a^{\prime})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(1-a^{\prime})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(A+a^{\prime})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(A-a^{\prime})\right]\right)-\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(1+a^{\prime})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\phi^{u}(1-a^{\prime})\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-a^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(A+a)\right]\,\mathrm{d}a-\int_{-a^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(1+a)\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq 4a^{\prime}(A+a^{\prime})C\leq 8a^{\prime}AC,

where the last step is by
Equations

C.28
and
C.31
, and
C
depends only on
\alpha,\overline{\Xi},K
.
Using this in
Equation

C.34
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)-\mathbb{E}\left[(\phi^{u})^{\prime}(a)\right]\right|\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq CA\left(a^{\prime}+\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})/a^{\prime}\right),

and the RHS is minimized by
a^{\prime}=\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})}
which lies in the interval
(0,1/2]
for all sufficiently large
n
due to
Corollary

C.4
.
Using this result in
Equation

C.33
and integrating over
a
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A-1}\int_{1}^{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CA}{A-1}\cdot 2\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})}\leq{4C}\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})},

(C.35)

since
A\geq 2
.

Finally, combining
Equations

C.32
,
C.35
and
C.23
proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma C.6
.

There exists
C>0
depending only on
\alpha,K,\overline{\Xi}
such that for any
A\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A-1}\int_{1}^{A}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{ns_{n}}}+\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})}\right),

where
\widetilde{u}^{(1)}=\Xi^{-1/2}u^{(1)},\widetilde{u}^{(2)}=\Xi^{-1/2}u^{(2)}
with
u^{(1)},u^{(2)}
being two i.i.d. copies from the conditional law of
u^{*},v^{*}
given
Equation

C.22
.

Proof.

Since
\supp(P),\supp(Q),\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}
are all bounded, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|

(C.36)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

(C.37)

On the other hand, let us compute
Equation

C.36
on the LHS of the above inequality.
Recall from
Equation

C.30
in the proof of
Lemma

C.5
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]=\frac{\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

(C.38)

Similar to
Equation

C.29
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]=\frac{2\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\\
+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{n\sqrt{s_{n}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}Z^{u}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]-\frac{\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}^{(1)}\right\|_{2}^{2}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

(C.39)

Using Stein’s lemma and following similar derivations leading to
Equation

C.30
, the second term on the RHS equals

\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{n\sqrt{s_{n}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}Z^{u}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]=\frac{a\alpha}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left\|\widetilde{u}^{(1)}\right\|_{2}^{2}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\\
-\frac{2a\alpha}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(3)}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]+\frac{a\alpha}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

(C.40)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha a}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

(C.41)

Putting
Equations

C.38
and
C.41
together and using Nishimori identity (
Proposition

G.4
), we have that
Equation

C.36
equals

\frac{\alpha a}{n^{2}}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]^{2}\right|\\
=\frac{\alpha a}{n^{2}}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|.

(C.42)

So by the inequality
Equation

C.37
, for any
a\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\|\Xi\right\|_{2}^{-1}K^{2}}{\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left|\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\Upsilon^{u}(\widetilde{u})\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right]\right|\right\rangle_{n,a,b}\right].

Integrating over
a\in[1,A]
and invoking
Lemma

C.5
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma C.7

(Overlap concentration)
.

Let
R_{1},R_{2}\colon[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be two continuous bounded functions such that their partial derivatives with respect to the second and third arguments are continuous and non-negative.
Let
s_{n}=n^{-1/32}
.
For
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[1,2]^{2}
, slightly abusing notation, let
q_{1}(\cdot,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}),q_{2}(\cdot,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
be the unique solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q_{1}(0)=s_{n}\varepsilon_{1}\\
q_{2}(0)=s_{n}\varepsilon_{2}\end{cases},\qquad\begin{cases}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)=R_{1}(t,q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\\
q_{2}^{\prime}(t)=R_{2}(t,q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t))\end{cases}.

(C.43)

Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
K
,
\alpha
,
\left\|R_{1}\right\|_{\infty}
,
\left\|R_{2}\right\|_{\infty}
,
\overline{\Xi}
such that for every
t\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}\leq Cn^{-1/8}.

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of the solution to the Cauchy problem
Equation

C.43
is a direct consequence of the Cauchy–Lipschitz theorem
[
37
, Theorem 3.1, Chapter V]
.
For any
t\in[0,1]
, the function
Q_{t}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=(q_{1}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}),q_{2}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism.
Its Jacobian determinant is given by the Liouville formula
[
37
, Corollary 3.1, Chapter V]
and can be lower bounded as

\displaystyle J(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\det(\nabla Q_{t}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))

\displaystyle=s_{n}^{2}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{2}R_{1}(s,Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{3}R_{2}(s,Q_{s}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\geq s_{n}^{2},

(C.44)

since the partial derivatives are non-negative by assumptions.

We then view the RHS below as a function of
q_{1},q_{2}
and denote it by

\displaystyle V(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right].

(C.45)

Denote
\Omega_{t}=Q_{t}([1,2]^{2})/s_{n}
and
M\coloneqq\max\left\{\left\|R_{1}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|R_{2}\right\|_{\infty}\right\}+2
.
Since
R_{1},R_{2}\geq 0
by assumptions,
q_{1},q_{2}
are non-decreasing in
t
by
Equation

C.43
.
So for
i\in\{1,2\}
, and any
t\in[0,1]
and
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[1,2]^{2}
,

\displaystyle q_{i}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})/s_{n}

\displaystyle\geq q_{i}(0,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})/s_{n}=\varepsilon_{i}\geq 1,

\displaystyle q_{i}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})/s_{n}

\displaystyle\leq q_{i}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})/s_{n}=s_{n}^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}q_{i}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\,\mathrm{d}t+s_{n}^{-1}q_{i}(0,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\leq s_{n}^{-1}(\left\|R_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+2).

We obtain the relation
\Omega_{t}\subset[1,M/s_{n}]^{2}
for any
t\in[0,1]
.

Next, using the change of variable
(r_{1},r_{2})=Q_{t}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})/s_{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}V(q_{1}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}),q_{2}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{V(s_{n}r_{1},s_{n}r_{2})s_{n}^{2}}{J(Q_{t}^{-1}(s_{n}r_{1},s_{n}r_{2}))}\,\mathrm{d}(r_{1},r_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{1}^{M/s_{n}}\int_{1}^{M/s_{n}}V(s_{n}r_{1},s_{n}r_{2})\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}\,\mathrm{d}r_{2},

(C.46)

where the last step is by
Equation

C.44
.
Further applying the change of variable
r_{1}=a^{2}
, we have that for all
r_{2}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{1}^{M/s_{n}}V(s_{n}r_{1},s_{n}r_{2})\,\mathrm{d}r_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M/s_{n}}}V(s_{n}a^{2},s_{n}r_{2})2a\,\mathrm{d}a\leq\frac{2\sqrt{M/s_{n}}}{n^{2}}\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M/s_{n}}}V(s_{n}a^{2},s_{n}r_{2})\,\mathrm{d}a.

(C.47)

Recalling
V
from
Equation

C.45
, we recognize that

\displaystyle V(s_{n}a^{2},s_{n}r_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,a,\sqrt{r_{2}}}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,\sqrt{r_{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left((\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle(\widetilde{u}^{(1)})^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{(2)}\right\rangle_{n,a,\sqrt{r_{2}}}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,a,\sqrt{r_{2}}}\right],

where
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle_{n,a,b}
is defined in
Equation

C.22
and the second equality is by Nishimori identity (
Proposition

G.4
).
Since
\sqrt{M/s_{n}}\geq 2
for all sufficiently large
n
, applying
Lemma

C.6
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M/s_{n}}-1}\int_{1}^{\sqrt{M/s_{n}}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}V(s_{n}a^{2},s_{n}r_{2})\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{ns_{n}}}+\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})}\right),

where
C>0
depends only on
\alpha,K,\overline{\Xi}
and
\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})
is given in
Equation

C.21
with
A=\sqrt{M/s_{n}}
.
Using this back in
Equation

C.47
and then in
Equation

C.46
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\left(\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2C(M/s_{n})^{2}(1/\sqrt{ns_{n}}+\sqrt{\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})}).

Corollary

C.4
guarantees that
\xi_{n}^{u}(s_{n})\leq\frac{C}{s_{n}\sqrt{n}}
for some
C>0
depending only on
\alpha,K,\overline{\Xi}
.
By the choice of
s_{n}
, we can finally upper bound (up to a positive constant depending only on
\alpha,K,\overline{\Xi},M
) the RHS above by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s_{n}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{ns_{n}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{n}\sqrt{n}}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{s_{n}^{2.5}}\cdot\frac{2}{n^{1/4}}=2\cdot n^{-11/64}\leq 2\cdot n^{-1/8},

for all sufficiently large
n
, which completes the proof.
∎

Recalling
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle_{n,t}
defined in
Equation

C.6
, let us identify
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]
as a function of
(t,q_{1},q_{2})
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right]

\displaystyle=\Delta(t,q_{1},q_{2}).

(C.48)

Note that
\Delta
is continuous, non-negative (by Nishimori identity) on
[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
and bounded by
K^{2}
.
Its partial derivatives with respect to the second and third arguments are continuous and non-negative, since the correlation between
\widetilde{u}^{*}
and
\left\langle\widetilde{u}\right\rangle_{n,t}
is a non-decreasing function of the SNRs
q_{1},q_{2}
.

Lemma C.8

(Fundamental sum rule)
.

In the setting of
Lemma

C.7
, for
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[1,2]^{2}
, let
q_{1}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}),q_{2}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
be the solution to
Equation

C.43
with
R_{2}=\Delta
defined in
Equation

C.48
.
Then we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{1}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})q_{2}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}+o(1).

Proof.

Fix
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[1,2]^{2}
.
By the choice
R_{2}=\Delta
,

\displaystyle q_{2}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta(t,q_{1}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}),q_{2}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))=\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\widetilde{u}^{\top}\widetilde{u}^{*}\right\rangle_{n,t}\right].

Plugging this into
Equation

C.15
, integrating over
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in[1,2]^{2}
and applying
Lemma

C.7
, we have

\displaystyle f_{n}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t)q_{2}^{\prime}(t)+o(1),

where
o(1)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, uniformly over
t
.
By
Lemma

C.1
, we conclude

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}f_{n}(0)\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}+o(1)=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\left(f_{n}(1)-\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{1}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{1}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})q_{2}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}+o(1),

as desired.
Here the first and last equalities are by
Equations

C.8
and
C.9
, respectively.
∎

Finally, we prove a pair of matching upper and lower bounds, completing the proof of
Theorem

4.4
.

Lemma C.9

(Lower bound)
.

Let
s_{n}=n^{-1/32}
and
R_{2}=\Delta
.
Then

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{q_{v}\geq 0}\inf_{q_{u}\geq 0}{\mathcal{F}}(q_{u},q_{v}).

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
q_{v}\geq 0
.
Let
R_{1}=q_{v}
.
Then
q_{1}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=s_{n}\varepsilon_{1}+tq_{v}
and
Lemma

C.8
gives

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma(s_{n}\varepsilon_{1}+q_{v}))+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{v}q_{2}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\int_{1}^{2}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{v})+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{v}q_{2}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{1}\,\mathrm{d}\varepsilon_{2}+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{q_{2}\geq 0}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{v})+\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}(q_{2})-\frac{\alpha}{2}q_{v}q_{2}+o(1)=\inf_{q_{u}\geq 0}{\mathcal{F}}(q_{u},q_{v})+o(1),

where the second line holds since
\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}
is Lipschitz and
q_{2}(0,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})=s_{n}\varepsilon_{2}=o(1)
.
This completes the proof since the above lower bound holds for all
q_{v}\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma C.10

(Upper bound)
.

Let
s_{n}=n^{-1/32}
and
R_{2}=\Delta
.
Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{q_{v}\geq 0}\inf_{q_{u}\geq 0}{\mathcal{F}}(q_{u},q_{v}).

Proof.

We apply
Lemma

C.8
with

\displaystyle R_{1}(t,q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=2\alpha\psi_{\overline{\Sigma}}^{\prime}\left(\Delta(t,q_{1},q_{2})\right).

(C.49)

Since
\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}
is Lipschitz and convex,

\displaystyle\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{1}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))

\displaystyle=\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}\left(\alpha\gamma(q_{1}(1,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})-q_{1}(0,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))\right)+o(1)=\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}\left(\alpha\gamma\int_{0}^{1}q_{1}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\,\mathrm{d}t\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{\overline{\Xi}}(\alpha\gamma q_{1}^{\prime}(t,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}))\,\mathrm{d}t+o(1),

and similarly

Let

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{{\overline{\Sigma}^{*}}^{2}}{(\beta(\alpha)-c(\alpha)\overline{\Sigma}^{*})^{2}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{{\overline{\Xi}^{*}}^{2}}{(\alpha-\overline{\Xi}^{*})^{2}}\right]

Finally, denote

(E.26)

The characterization of

, which is the same as in
[

]
.

(A3)

For any

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\downarrow\sup\supp(\overline{\Xi}^{*})}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\overline{\Xi}^{*}}{\alpha-\overline{\Xi}^{*}}\right]=\infty.

Proposition G.6

(Chain rule of derivatives)

The proof follows from elementary applications of the chain rule for derivatives.
Writing
A=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&\cdots&a_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
and
f=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}&\cdots&f_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f_{j}(Ax)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f_{j}\left(\left\langle a_{1},x\right\rangle,\cdots,\left\langle a_{n},x\right\rangle\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k}f_{j}(Ax)\frac{\partial\left\langle a_{k},x\right\rangle}{\partial x_{i}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{k,i}\partial_{k}f_{j}(\widetilde{x}),

where
\partial_{k}f_{j}
denotes the partial derivative of
f_{j}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with respect to its
k
-th argument.
Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(Af(Ax))_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}f_{j}(Ax)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{i,j}A_{k,i}\partial_{k}f_{j}(\widetilde{x}),

as claimed.
∎

Proposition G.7

[
68
]
)
.

Proposition G.9

)
.

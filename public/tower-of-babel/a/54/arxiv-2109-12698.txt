The Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction of admissible representations of affine Lie algebras
Gurbir Dhillon

Abstract
. Fix an affine Lie algebra 𝔤 ^ κ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa} with associated principal affine W-algebra 𝒲 κ \mathcal{W}_{\kappa} . A basic conjecture of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto asserts that Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction sends admissible 𝔤 ^ κ \widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa} -modules to zero or cohomological shifts of minimal series 𝒲 κ \mathcal{W}_{\kappa} -modules. We prove this conjecture and a natural generalization to the spectrally flowed Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction functors. This extends the previous results of Arakawa for the minus reduction.



1.
Introduction

1.1.

In the seminal work
[
FF90a
]
Feigin and Frenkel introduced the affine W-algebras into mathematics. These algebras and their representation theory have been intensively studied in the ensuing three decades and have fundamental relations to integrable hierarchies, moduli spaces associated to algebraic curves, and the geometric Langlands program, cf.
[
Ara17
]
,
[
FBZ04
]
,
[
Fre07a
]
and references therein.

In this paper we settle a basic conjecture from
[
FF90a
]
, or more precisely a refinement due to Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto
[
FKW92
]
. In the remainder of the introduction we review some of the history and context of the conjecture; the reader may wish to skip directly to Section
2
where we state our results.

1.2.

The method by which Feigin and Frenkel produced the W-algebras is cohomological in nature, and takes as its input a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with an adjoint-invariant bilinear form
\kappa
. Let us write
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
for the affine Lie algebra associated to
\mathfrak{g}
and
\kappa
, which is a central extension of the loop algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\!(t)\!)
:

0\rightarrow k\cdot\mathbf{1}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(\!(t)\!)\rightarrow 0.

By the construction of the associated W-algebra, which we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
, one has a functor of Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction between the derived categories of modules

(1.1)

\Psi^{+}:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}.

Let us mention two basic properties of this functor. First, it sends the vacuum Kac–Moody vertex algebra
\mathbb{V}_{\kappa}
, concentrated in cohomological degree zero, to
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
, concentrated in cohomological degree zero. Second, it is constructed via conformal field theoretic techniques. As a consequence, the functor
factorizes
. That is, for any smooth algebraic curve
X
, Equation (
1.1
) naturally extends to a functor between the versions of the appearing categories of chiral algebra modules defined over the Ran space of
X
, cf.
[
BD04
]
.

1.3.

In the final pages of
[
FF90a
]
, Feigin and Frenkel proposed a conjectural property of Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction. Kac and Wakimoto had discovered for any positive rational level
\kappa
a family of irreducible highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules, the admissible modules, distinguished by the modularity properties of their characters
[
KW88
]
,
[
KW89
]
. Feigin and Frenkel predicted that Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction should send admissible modules of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
to zero or irreducible minimal series representations of
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
. The latter had attracted interest in both mathematics and physics due to their appearance in rational conformal field theory, cf.
[
BS93
]
.

In particular, by an analysis of Euler characteristics, combined with the character formula for admissible modules due to Kac–Wakimoto, the
conjecture would imply formulae for the
q
-characters of the minimal series, which was the subject of forthcoming joint work of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto
[
FKW92
]
.

Example 1.3.1
.

Let us specialize the preceding discussion to the simplest case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Here, the algebra
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
is a Virasoro vacuum algebra, whose central charge varies with
\kappa
, and its minimal series are the celebrated minimal models of chiral conformal field theory
[
BPZ84
]
. As Feigin and Frenkel emphasize in
[
FF90a
]
, the correspondence with admissible
\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-modules had already been proposed and studied in the physical literature
[
KPZ88
]
,
[
MP90
]
, and proven by the authors themselves in
[
FF90b
]
.

1.4.

In the classic paper
[
FKW92
]
, Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto performed a comprehensive analysis of the resulting
q
-characters and formulated a series of conjectures based on their findings. Combining these with the Verlinde formula, they obtained a conjectural calculation of the fusion rules for the minimal series. We now recall two important features of their work.

A first basic discovery of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto was that, beyond the case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction could not simply send an admissible module, concentrated in cohomological degree zero, to a minimal series representation, concentrated in cohomological degree zero. Instead, to account for certain signs encountered in their analysis of characters, they conjectured that admissible modules are sent to zero or cohomological shifts of minimal series. Moreover, they gave for each admissible module a prediction for whether its reduction vanished, and if not the exact cohomological shift and the highest weight for the obtained minimal series representation. These conjectures intricately depend on the level
\kappa
and the highest weight of the admissible module. They have been since regarded as the definitive formulation of the prediction announced by Feigin–Frenkel on Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction.

A second basic contribution of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto was their introduction of another functor

(1.2)

\Psi^{-}:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

which they called the minus Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction. In the case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, this had been previously considered by Feigin–Frenkel
[
FF90b
]
. This functor, which is a variant of the usual, i.e. plus, Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction, is less immediate to construct using conformal field theoretic techniques. Relatedly, it no longer exchanges the vacuum vertex algebras, and does not extend to a factorizable functor.

However, a crucial realization of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto was that the minus reduction should again send admissible modules to zero or minimal series, and in a drastically simpler fashion than the plus reduction. More precisely, they conjectured that the reduction of an admissible series representation would not vanish if and only if its highest weight, considered as a weight of the abstract Cartan of the finite dimensional algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, was antidominant. They further conjectured that the resulting
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
-module would always be a minimal series representation, concentrated in cohomological degree zero.

The findings of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto, and in particular their conjectures on the plus and minus reductions, have proven highly influential. They continue to guide developments in the subject, e.g. the emerging connections between minimal models and the quantum Langlands correspondence. We note that for the latter connection, and more generally questions of a global nature involving conformal blocks, the factorizability of the plus reduction plays an important role.

1.5.

After both conjectures lay open for over a decade, a breakthrough was made by Arakawa. In the tour-de-force works
[
Ara04
]
,
[
Ara07
]
, he completely proved the conjecture for the minus reduction, and verified many cases of the conjecture for the plus reduction. His work moreover yielded character formulae for every simple highest weight
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
-module, extending the formulae for minimal series conjectured by Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto. We remark that an alternative proof of the conjecture for the minus reduction was given by the author and Raskin recently in the works
[
Ras21
]
,
[
DR20
]
.

1.6.

The decade following Arakawa’s work has seen tremendous progress in the study of W-algebras. Notably, many of the main conjectures from the early days of the subject, e.g. the relation with coset models and the rationality of minimal quotients, have been fully resolved
[
Ara15
]
,
[
ACL19
]
. However, to our knowledge, no further cases of Frenkel--Kac--Wakimoto’s conjecture on the plus reduction were obtained.
1
1

1

However, some cases of the analogous assertions for other spectrally flowed reduction functors may be found in the interesting work
[
ACF20
]
.

The basic added difficulty for the conjecture on plus reduction can be understood immediately from its statement. Namely, the absence of cohomological shifts in the conjecture for the minus reduction underlies the fact that the functor is
t
-exact on all of the Category
\mathscr{O}
of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules. This was known for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
by the work of Feigin–Frenkel
[
FF90b
]
, and was proven in general by Arakawa
[
Ara04
]
. This
t
-exactness plays a crucial role in the argument of Arakawa for the minus reduction, and that of the author and Raskin. An analogous
t
-exactness holds for the plus reduction applied to certain blocks of Category
\mathscr{O}
, which were exactly the cases settled by Arakawa. Beyond these cases, the plus reduction is not
t
-exact, so one needs a further idea to proceed.

1.7.

In this paper, we prove Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto’s conjecture for the plus reduction, as well as a natural generalization to arbitrary dominant weights and spectrally flowed reduction functors.

2.
Statement of Results

In this section, after introducing the necessary notation and preliminaries, we state our main Theorem
2.5.1
.

2.1.

Fix an arbitrary level
\kappa
, and let
L_{\lambda}
be an irreducible highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-module which is dominant. That is, we suppose its Verma cover
M_{\lambda}
is projective in the Category
\mathscr{O}
. In particular
L_{\lambda}
could be any admissible module.

2.2.

Fix in addition an element
\check{\mu}
of the coweight lattice of the finite Cartan. One may associate to it the spectrally flowed Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction functor

(2.1)

\Psi^{\check{\mu}}:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

cf. Section
4
for the definition, which is slightly nonstandard if
\check{\mu}
is not antidominant. For now, it suffices to say that if we write
\check{\rho}
for one half of the sum of the positive finite coroots, then the functor
\Psi^{\check{\rho}}
equals the plus reduction, and the functor
\Psi^{0}
equals the minus reduction.

2.3.

Our main theorem calculates the spectrally flowed reductions

\Psi^{\check{\mu}}(L_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}.

If we write
G
for the simple algebraic group of adjoint type with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and
LG
for its algebraic loop group, then as we explain in Section
4
this calculation is equivalent to computing, for all
g\in LG
, the plus reduction of the
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}
-conjugated simple modules

\Psi^{+}\big(\hskip-1.42262pt\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(L_{\lambda})\big).

The answer involves the following Coxeter-theoretic combinatorics.

Let us denote the finite Weyl group, the extended affine Weyl group, and the integral Weyl group of
L_{\lambda}
by
W_{f},W,
and
W_{\lambda},
respectively. In particular, we have the tautological inclusions

W_{f}\subset W\supset W_{\lambda}.

Write
e^{\check{\mu}}
for the element of
W
corresponding to
\check{\mu}
. Let us say that the pair
(\check{\mu},\lambda)
is
good
if the double coset

(2.2)

W_{f}e^{\check{\mu}}W_{\lambda}

is a
W_{f}\times W_{\lambda}
torsor, i.e. the action is simply transitive. In this case one has a unique factorization

(2.3)

e^{\check{\mu}}=w_{f}w_{-}w_{\lambda},

where
w_{f}\in W_{f}
,
w_{-}
is the unique minimal length element of (
2.2
) with respect to the Bruhat order on
W
, and
w_{\lambda}\in W_{\lambda}
. We write
\ell_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})
for the length of
w_{\lambda}
viewed as an element of the Coxeter system
W_{\lambda}
.

2.4.

Finally, we need some notation. Write
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
for the dual of the finite Cartan. Recall that the combinatorics of highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules is governed by the level
\kappa
dot action of
W
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, which we denote by

W\times\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*},\quad\quad(w,\lambda)\mapsto w\lambda,

cf. Section
3.5.2
for further discussion of this action. To discuss highest weights for
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
-modules, we denote the Harish–Chandra projection by

\pi:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f},

and for an infinitesimal character

\chi\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f}

we write
L_{\chi}
for the corresponding simple highest weight
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
-module, cf. Section
3.5.4
for our precise normalization.

2.5.

With these preparations, we can state our main result.

Theorem 2.5.1
.

Let
\check{\mu}
and
\lambda
be as in Sections
2.2
and
2.1
, respectively. If the pair
(\check{\mu},\lambda)
is good, let
w_{-}
and
w_{\lambda}
be as in Equation (
2.3
). Then the corresponding spectrally flowed reduction is given by

\Psi^{\check{\mu}}(L_{\lambda})\simeq\begin{cases}L_{\pi(w_{-}\lambda)}[\ell_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda})]&\text{if $(\check{\mu},\lambda)$ is good},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We would like to make two comments about the theorem.

Remark 2.5.2
.

Let us take
\check{\mu}=\check{\rho}
, so that we are considering the plus reduction, and let us further suppose that
\lambda
is a principal admissible weight in the sense of Kac–Wakimoto
[
KW89
]
. This case of Theorem
2.5.1
confirms Conjecture
\operatorname{3.4}_{+}
of Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto
[
FKW92
]
.

More precisely, (i) whether the reduction vanishes is exactly as they conjectured. Further, (ii) when the reduction is nonvanishing we get the irreducible
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
-module that they conjectured concentrated in a single cohomological degree. Finally, (iii) the exact cohomological degree corrects their conjecture. Namely, we find a shift by the length of
w_{\lambda}
rather than minus its semi-infinite length; these differ in examples already for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
. Note however that this does not affect the parity of the cohomological degree, and in particular is consistent with their Euler characteristic calculations.

Remark 2.5.3
.

After stating Conjecture
\operatorname{3.4}_{+}
, Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto outline in Conjectures 3.5.1 and 3.5.2 a conjecture on the cohomology of the plus reduction of a 2-sided resolution of
L_{\lambda}
by Wakimoto modules. Their Conjecture 3.5.1 on the existence of two sided resolutions was settled by Arakawa
[
Ara14
]
, and Conjecture 3.5.2 then follows by Theorem
2.5.1
.

2.6.

The method by which we prove Theorem
2.5.1
, which we will indicate in the next subsection, also yields tight bounds on the cohomological amplitude of any
\Psi^{\check{\mu}}
when restricted to a block of Category
\mathscr{O}
. The right exactness of the functors follows from work of Frenkel–Gaitsgory
[
FG06
]
. Our lower bounds on the nonvanishing cohomology are to the best of our knowledge new save for the case of the minus reduction, where they reduce to its
t
-exactness.

For example, for the block containing
L_{\lambda}
as above, we show the functor has cohomological amplitude at most
[-\ell_{\lambda}(w_{\lambda}),0]
. That is, the reduction of
L_{\lambda}
, when nonzero, is concentrated in the most negative possible degree. The case of a general block is similar and is presented in Theorem
5.5.1
below.

2.7.

Finally, let us describe the idea of the proof. Our starting point is the fact that the various reductions
\Psi^{\check{\mu}}
, when restricted to Category
\mathscr{O}
, differ from the minus reduction
\Psi^{-}
by precomposition with intertwining, i.e. twisting, functors.
2
2

2

The twisting functors were constructed geometrically by Feigin–Frenkel in their fundamental work
[
FF90c
]
, and algebraically by Arkhipov in
[
Ark04
]
. They also appear in the earlier work of Beilinson–Bernstein
[
BB83
]
under the name intertwining functors.
Equivalently, writing
\mathring{I}
for the prounipotent radical of
I
, they differ by convolution with costandard objects in the universal affine Hecke category

(2.4)

D_{\kappa}(\mathring{I}\backslash LG/\mathring{I}).

Here universal refers to the fact that we impose no restrictions on the monodromies of the D-modules along the orbits of the finite Cartan
I/\mathring{I}
under left and right multiplication. This relation with intertwining functors is an old observation going back at least to the work of Frenkel–Gaitsgory
[
FG06
]
(and, after applying the sheaves-functions dictionary, far earlier still to the study of principal series representations of finite and
p
-adic Chevalley groups).

In our approach to the conjecture we make essential use of the fact that, upon restriction to a fixed block
\mathscr{O}_{\chi}
of Category
\mathscr{O}
, the relevant intertwining functors descend to costandard objects in an affine Hecke category with right monodromy fixed by
\chi
. As such, this carries an action by the bi
\chi
-monodromic affine Hecke category

(2.5)

D_{\kappa}(\mathring{I}\backslash LG/I,\chi)\circlearrowleft D_{\kappa}(I,\chi\backslash LG/I,\chi).

This action categorifies the right action of the integral Weyl group on the extended affine Weyl group

W\circlearrowleft W_{\chi}.

Practically, this means that the way in which costandard objects in (
2.5
) convolve with one another depends finely upon
\chi
. The upshot for us is that these convolutions satisfy identities which do not descend from the universal case (
2.4
).

To prove Theorem
2.5.1
we lift the factorization

e^{\check{\mu}}=w_{f}\circ w_{-}\circ w_{\chi}

of Equation (
2.3
) to such an identity of costandard objects in (
2.5
). That is, we write in Section
4
a spectrally flowed reduction functor as the minus reduction precomposed with two intertwining functors:

(2.6)

\Psi^{\check{\mu}}\simeq\Psi^{-}\circ j_{w_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}\circ j_{w_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}.

We then track in Section
5
how each step in the factorization transforms dominant highest weight modules in
\mathscr{O}_{\chi}
and find that it produces the behavior anticipated by Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto. Namely, the functors
\Psi^{-}
and
j_{w_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}
of Equation (
2.6
) are
t
-exact and behave simply on the abelian categories of modules; for
\Psi^{-}
this is exactly Arakawa’s theorem. However, the first term
j_{w_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}
is typically far from
t
-exact, and it is in general difficult to calculate its action on a given object. However, on ‘maximally integrable’ objects, such as dominant highest weight modules, it reduces to a cohomological shift of the identity functor, which yields the theorem.

Remark 2.7.1
.

The cases of the plus reduction previously obtained by Arakawa
[
Ara07
]
are exactly the cases when
w_{\chi}
is length zero. The latter condition is equivalent to
j_{w_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}
being
t
-exact, and for this reason the arguments for the minus reduction, where one checks that (co)standard objects are sent to (co)standard objects, and deduce an assertion for arbitrary simples, carry over
mutatis mutandis
.

In short, we find the combinatorics of the Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto conjecture for the plus reduction in the combinatorics of twisted affine Hecke categories.

2.8.

The paper is organized as follows. We gather in Section 3 some preliminary material on loop groups, W-algebras, categorical representation theory, Whittaker sheaves, and Hecke categories.

In Section
4
we relate the spectrally flowed reduction functors to Whittaker sheaves on the affine flag manifold and obtain the factorization of Equation (
2.6
).

Finally, in Section
5
we apply this to obtain the main Theorem
2.5.1
.

3.2.1.

In this paper, we only need the following simple case of semi-infinite cohomology. Suppose

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}(L\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{k},M)\in\operatorname{Vect},

5
5

5

To set up some of the formalism we use and to construct some of the appearing categories, one has to work systematically with dg-categories. Recall that

\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}(L\mathfrak{u},\tilde{\mathfrak{k}},M)\otimes\operatorname{rel.det}(\tilde{\mathfrak{k}},\mathfrak{k})^{*}\simeq C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}(L\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{k},M),

where in the appearing relative determinant line both

and
\tilde{\mathfrak{k}}
lie in cohomological degree -1.

3.2.2.

Fix a generic additive character
\psi^{-}:N^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}
. Associated to our coordinate

is an induced additive character

(3.2)

LN^{-}\xrightarrow{L\psi^{-}}L\mathbb{G}_{a}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Res}(-)}\mathbb{G}_{a},

where, at the level of
k
-points,
\operatorname{Res}(-)
sends a formal power series

f(t)\in k(\!(t)\!)\simeq L\mathbb{G}_{a}(k)

to its coefficient of
t^{-1}
. We again use
\psi^{-}
to denote the composition (
3.2
), and we write
k_{\psi^{-}}
for the induced one dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
and
L\mathfrak{n}^{-}
. With this, by definition the underlying vector space of
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
is the complex

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}(L\mathfrak{n}^{-},L^{+}\mathfrak{n}^{-},\mathbb{V}_{\kappa}\otimes k_{\psi^{-}}),

which is concentrated cohomologically in degree zero.

3.3.
Categories of smooth modules

3.3.1.

For a dg-category
\mathscr{C}
equipped with a
t
-structure we denote by
\mathscr{C}^{+}
the full subcategory of bounded below objects. Similarly, for an integer
n
we denote by
\mathscr{C}^{\leqslant n}
and
\mathscr{C}^{\geqslant n}
the full subcategories of objects concentrated in degrees at most
n

n

t
-structure, i.e. the abelian category of objects in cohomological degree zero, by
\mathscr{C}^{\heartsuit}
.

3.3.2.

For the affine Lie algebra, consider its abelian category
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{\heartsuit}
of smooth modules on which
\mathbf{1}
acts via the identity. We denote by
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
its associated renormalized derived category, as introduced by Frenkel–Gaitsgory

-structure and a canonical

-exact functor to the naive unbounded derived category

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\operatorname{D}(\hskip 1.42262pt\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{\heartsuit}),

which is an equivalence on bounded below categories. So, the only difference between the two lies in cohomological degree

3.3.3.

For the affine W-algebra, consider its abelian category
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{\heartsuit}
of smooth modules. We denote by
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
its renormalized derived category, as introduced by Raskin

3.4.
The plus and minus reductions

The plus reduction below was introduced by Feigin–Frenkel in
[
FF90a
]
. The minus reduction was studied for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
by Feigin–Frenkel
[
FF90b
]
, and was introduced in general by Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto
[
FKW92
]
.

3.4.1.

From the definition of the affine W-algebra, semi-infinite cohomology defines a functor

(3.3)

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}\big(L\mathfrak{n}^{-},L^{+}\mathfrak{n}^{-},(-)\otimes k_{\psi}^{-}\hskip 1.42262pt\big):\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}.

On bounded below objects, the composition

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{+}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\operatorname{D}(\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{\heartsuit})

agrees with standard construction via Feigin’s semi-infinite cochain complex
[
Fei84
]
. Following Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto, we call (
3.3
) the plus reduction. We denote it by
\Psi^{+}
.

3.4.2.

Fix in addition a generic additive character
\psi
of the opposite unipotent subalgebra
N
. Associated to our coordinate
t
is an additive character

LN\xrightarrow{L\psi}L\mathbb{G}_{a}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Res}(t\cdot-)}\mathbb{G}_{a},

where, at the level of
k
-points,
\operatorname{Res}(t\cdot-)
sends a formal power series to its coefficient of
1
. We denote by
k_{\psi}
the associated one dimensional representation of
L\mathfrak{n}
.

Let us write
L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n}
for the first congruence subalgebra of
L^{+}\mathfrak{n}
, so that

L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n}\simeq\mathfrak{n}\otimes t\cdot k[\hskip-0.56905pt[t]\hskip-0.56905pt].

Consider the associated semi-infinite cohomology.

(3.4)

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}\big(L\mathfrak{n},L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n},(-)\otimes k_{\psi}\big):\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect}.

3.4.3.

The functor (
3.4
) lifts to a functor to
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
as follows. Recalling that
G
is adjoint, it follows there is a unique coset representative
\dot{w}_{\circ}
for the longest element
w_{\circ}
of
W_{f}
satisfying

\operatorname{Ad}_{\dot{w}_{\circ}}(\psi)=\psi^{-},

i.e. that exchanges the additive characters of
N
and
N^{-}
under conjugation.

Write
\check{\rho}
for one half of the sum of the positive coroots of
G
. Passing to the loop groups, it follows that the element
x:=\dot{w}_{\circ}t^{\check{\rho}}
satisfies

\operatorname{Ad}_{x}(\psi)=\psi^{-},

i.e. it exchanges the additive characters of
LN
and
LN^{-}
under conjugation.

Using this and the canonical isomorphism

\displaystyle C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}\big(L\mathfrak{n},L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n},(-)\otimes k_{\psi}\big)

\displaystyle\simeq C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}\big(L\mathfrak{n}^{-},\operatorname{Ad}_{x}L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n},\operatorname{Ad}_{x}(-)\otimes k_{\psi^{-}}\hskip 1.42262pt\big)

\displaystyle\simeq C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}\big(L\mathfrak{n}^{-},L^{+}\mathfrak{n}^{-},\operatorname{Ad}_{x}(-)\otimes k_{\psi^{-}}\hskip 1.42262pt\big)\otimes\ell,

where
\ell
is the cohomologically graded line

\displaystyle\ell

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{rel.det}(\operatorname{Ad}_{x}L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n},L^{+}\mathfrak{n}^{-}),

we thereby obtain a functor

C^{\frac{\infty}{2}+*}\big(L\mathfrak{n},L^{(1)}\mathfrak{n},(-)\otimes k_{\psi}\big):\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}.

Following Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto, we call this the minus reduction. We denote it by
\Psi^{-}
.

3.5.
Highest weight modules and the minus reduction

3.5.1.

Recall that we fixed a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
, and that we write
I
for the associated Iwahori subgroup of
LG
. For an element
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we denote the corresponding one dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{t}
by
k_{\lambda}
and the corresponding Verma module of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
by

M_{\lambda}:=\operatorname{pind}_{\mathfrak{t}}^{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}}k_{\lambda}.

We denote the simple quotient of
M_{\lambda}
by
L_{\lambda}
.

3.5.2.

Recall that the combinatorics of such highest modules are controlled by the level
\kappa
dot action of
W
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, which we denote by

W\times\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*},\quad\quad(w,\mu)\mapsto w\mu.

Explicitly,
W_{f}
acts by the usual dot action of the finite Weyl group, and a coweight
\check{\mu}\in\check{\Lambda}
acts by translation by

(\kappa_{c}-\kappa)(\check{\mu})\in\mathfrak{t}^{*},

where
\kappa_{c}
denotes the critical level. In this formula, we have used the restriction of
(\kappa_{c}-\kappa)
from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{t}
, and the induced map

(\kappa_{c}-\kappa):\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}.

3.5.3.

We also recall that to any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
one associates its integral Weyl group
W_{\lambda}
. To define this, write
\check{\Phi}^{+}
for the positive real affine coroots, i.e. the positive real coroots of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
. For
\check{\alpha}\in\check{\Phi}^{+}
, consider the dual real root
\alpha
of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
, and write
\alpha_{c}
for its classical part, i.e. associated root of
\mathfrak{g}
. There is unique affine linear functional on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, which we denote again by
\check{\alpha}
, such that the corresponding reflection
s_{\check{\alpha}}
in
W
acts through the level
\kappa
dot action by

s_{\check{\alpha}}\mu=\mu-\langle\mu,\check{\alpha}\rangle\alpha_{c}.

With this, we may associate to
\lambda
its positive integral coroots

\check{\Phi}_{\lambda}^{+}:=\{\check{\alpha}\in\check{\Phi}^{+}:\langle\check{\alpha},\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

By definition,
W_{\lambda}
is the subgroup of
W
generated by the reflections
s_{\check{\alpha}},
for
\check{\alpha}\in\check{\Phi}^{+}_{\lambda}
. Moreover, if we call an element
\check{\alpha}\in\check{\Phi}^{+}_{\lambda}
indecomposable if it cannot be written as a sum of two other elements of
\check{\Phi}^{+}_{\lambda}
, then the reflections in
W_{\lambda}
associated to the indecomposable integral coroots exhibit
W_{\lambda}
as a Coxeter system. Note that any simple reflection of
W
contained in
W_{\lambda}
is necessarily simple in
W_{\lambda}
, but a simple reflection of
W_{\lambda}
need not be simple in
W
.

3.5.4.

We next review the associated categories of highest weight modules. Let us write
\Lambda
for the the character lattice of
T
, i.e. the root lattice. Recall the canonical bijection between character sheaves on
T
and
\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\Lambda
, which associates to a character sheaf the monodromy of the associated local system.

For a point
\chi\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\Lambda
, we denote by the same letter the associated character sheaf on
I
, and by

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}

the category of
(I,\chi)
-equivariant
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules. Plainly, this is compactly generated the Verma modules

M_{\lambda},\quad\text{for }\lambda\in\chi+\Lambda,

and is a renormalization of the corresponding piece of (cocompleted, derived) Category
\mathscr{O}
. For more discussion, the reader may see for example
[
Gai15
]
, Section 5 of
[
CD19
]
, or Section 9 of
[
Ras20
]
.

3.5.5.

We would like to recall the behavior of the minus reduction on
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}
. To do so, let us write
\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f}
for the invariant theory quotient of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
by the dot action of
W_{f}
. We normalize its identification with the spectrum of the Zhu algebra

\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{Zhu}(\mathcal{W}_{\kappa})\simeq\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f}

as in
[
Dhi21
]
. For an infinitesimal character
\Theta\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f}(k)
, we denote the corresponding Verma module for
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
by
M_{\Theta}
and its simple quotient by
L_{\Theta}
.

The following fundamental theorem of Arakawa settled the Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto conjecture for the minus reduction
[
Ara04
]
,
[
Ara07
]
, for an alternative proof cf.
[
Ras21
]
,
[
DR20
]
. To state it, denote the Harish-Chandra projection by

\pi:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f},

and recall that a weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is said to be finite antidominant if for every finite positive coroot
\check{\alpha}
one has

\langle\check{\alpha},\lambda\rangle\notin\mathbb{Z}^{>0},

where we incorporate a
\rho
-shift into the pairing as in Section
3.5.3
.

Theorem 3.5.6
.

The minus reduction

\Psi^{-}:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}

is
t
-exact. Moreover, for any
\lambda\in\chi+\Lambda
there are canonical isomorphisms

\Psi^{-}(M_{\lambda})\simeq M_{\pi(\lambda)},

and

\Psi^{-}(L_{\lambda})\simeq\begin{cases}L_{\pi(\lambda)}&\text{if $\lambda$ is finite antidominant},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

That is, the minus reduction sends Verma modules to Verma modules and simple modules to simple modules or zero.

3.6.1.

(which, along with

Some references for the ensuing material are as follows. For generalities on dg-categories, one may consult Section 1 of

[

3.6.2.

Consider the
(\infty,2)

-linear dg-categories
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
. We refer to objects of it as dg-categories, and 1-morphisms in it, i.e.
k
-linear quasi-functors in the sense of
[
Dri04
]
which commute with colimits, as functors.

\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
is a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category with respect to the Lurie tensor product.
6
6

6

Using the identification between the arising internal Homs and the morphisms in
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
, one can alternatively work only with the
(\infty,1)
-category associated to
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
, i.e. discard non-invertible 2-morphisms.
The monoidal unit is the category
\operatorname{Vect}
. We denote the underlying binary product of two dg-categories
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
by
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{D}
, and for objects
c
of
\mathscr{C}
and
d
of
\mathscr{D}
we denote by
c\boxtimes d
the corresponding ‘external product’ object of
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{D}
.

Suppose
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
are compactly generated, which is the case for all dg-categories considered in this paper. Concretely,
\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{D}
is then compactly generated by external products of compact objects from each factor, and the complexes of homomorphisms between such objects are simply the tensor product

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}\otimes\mathscr{D}}(c_{1}\boxtimes d_{1},c_{2}\boxtimes d_{2})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(c_{1},c_{2})\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathscr{D}}(d_{1},d_{2})

with the natural compositions.

The category of
\kappa
-twisted D-modules on the loop group, which we denote by
D_{\kappa}(LG)
, is naturally an algebra object of
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
. The underlying binary product is given by convolution, i.e. pushforward along the multiplication map

LG\times LG\rightarrow LG.

We denote its
(\infty,2)
-category of left modules by
D_{\kappa}(LG)\operatorname{-mod}
. Plainly, an object of
D_{\kappa}(LG)\operatorname{-mod}
is a dg-category
\mathscr{C}
equipped with an action

D_{\kappa}(LG)\otimes\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}

which is unital and associative up to coherent homotopy, and a 1-morphism between representations is a functor between the underlying dg-categories equipped with a datum of
D_{\kappa}(LG)
-equivariance.

The main example of interest for us is the canonical
D_{\kappa}(LG)
-module structure on
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
, see for example
[
Ras20
]
. To get a feel for this action, we note that for an element
g\in LG
, convolution with the corresponding delta function

\delta_{g}\star-:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}\simeq\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}

is induced by the adjoint action of
g
on
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
.

3.7.
Invariant vectors and representations

Given a
D_{\kappa}(LG)
-module
\mathscr{C}
, one can form its categories of twisted Iwahori and Whittaker invariant objects

\mathscr{C}^{I,\chi}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{C}^{LN,\psi}.

In our example of interest, these will exactly be the highest weight modules
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}
of Section
3.5.4
, and the category
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}
of Section
3.3.3
, respectively. We now review this in more detail.

3.7.1.

We begin with the general construction of invariants. So, consider an ind-affine placid group ind-scheme
H
equipped with a character sheaf
\phi
, see e.g.
[
Ras15
]
for a discussion of placidity.

Remark 3.7.2
.

The following technical point may be safely ignored by the reader. The category of D-modules on a placid ind-scheme
X
which we denote by
D(X)
is denoted in
[
Ras15
]
and
[
Ber17
]
by
D^{*}(X)
, and comes with functoriality for
*
-pushforwards. There is a dual category
D^{!}(X)
which comes with functoriality for
!
-pullbacks. The latter is symmetric monoidal under
!
-tensor product, and canonically acts on
D^{*}(X)
by
!
-tensor product. In particular, when we speak of a character sheaf
\phi
, it canonically is an object of
D^{!}(X)
. However, a choice of a dimension theory on
X
, which always exists in our cases of interest, identifies
D^{!}(X)
and
D(X)
.

Associated to the character sheaf
\phi
is a
D(H)
-module
\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi}
. Explicitly, its underlying dg-category is
\operatorname{Vect}
, and the action map is of the form

(3.5)

D(H)\otimes\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi}\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi},\quad\quad M\boxtimes V\mapsto\Gamma_{\operatorname{dR}}(H,M\overset{!}{\otimes}\phi)\otimes V,

where in the right-hand expression
\overset{!}{\otimes}
is the
!
-tensor product
D(X)\otimes D^{!}(X)\rightarrow D(X)
, cf. Remark
3.7.2
, and the outer tensor product is of
k
-vector spaces. For any
D(H)
-module
\mathscr{C}
, one may accordingly form its invariants and coinvariants

\mathscr{C}^{H,\phi}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{D(H)\operatorname{-mod}}(\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi},\mathscr{C})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{C}_{H,\phi}:=\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi}\underset{D(H)}{\otimes}\mathscr{C}.

Recalling the tautological equivalence

D(H)\otimes\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi}\simeq D(H),

the action map (
3.5
) underlies a
D(H)
-equivariant functor
D(H)\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi}
, which induces canonical ‘forgetting’ and ‘inserting’ maps

\operatorname{Oblv}:\mathscr{C}^{H,\phi}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{ins}:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}_{H,\phi}.

3.7.3.

Suppose further now that
H
is a group scheme. In this case, the map
D(H)\rightarrow\operatorname{Vect}_{\phi}
admits a left adjoint (i.e.,
D(H)
contains a constant sheaf) and hence one obtains an ‘averaging’ adjunction

\operatorname{Oblv}:\mathscr{C}^{H,\phi}\leftrightarrows\mathscr{C}:\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{H,\phi}.

Moreover,
\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{H,\phi}
canonically factors through through
\operatorname{ins}
, and if the prounipotent radical of
H
is of finite codimension the resulting map is an equivalence

\mathscr{C}_{H,\phi}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{C}^{H,\phi}.

We freely pass between invariants and coinvariants for such groups in what follows.

Let us take
H
to be the Iwahori
I
. The twisting
\kappa
is canonically trivialized on
L^{+}G
, and we use the induced trivialization on its subgroup
I
going forwards. In particular, any
D_{\kappa}(LG)
-module
\mathscr{C}
carries an action of
D(I)
. With this, for an element
\chi\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\Lambda
we may form the twisted invariants
\mathscr{C}^{I,\chi}
. Specializing to

\mathscr{C}=\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

the obtained category
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}
canonically identifies with the renormalized derived category of Harish-Chandra modules discussed in Section
3.5.4
, cf. the references therein for the details. We note in passing that a similar statement holds for any compact open subgroup
H
of
LG
.

3.7.4.

The contents of Section
3.7.3
admit an analogue for the Whittaker invariants, despite
LN
being an ind-scheme. Recall the generic additive character

\psi:LN\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}

of Section
3.4.2
. We denote by the same letter the character sheaf
\psi^{!}(\operatorname{exp}(z))
, where
\operatorname{exp}(z)
is the character sheaf on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
whose underlying D-module is the exponential D-module placed in cohomological degree -1.

The twisting
\kappa
is canonically trivial on
LN
, and so to a
D_{\kappa}(LG)
-module
\mathscr{C}
one may form its Whittaker (co)invariants. It will be convenient for us to use the canonical equivalence

\mathscr{C}_{LN,\psi}\simeq\mathscr{C}^{LN,\psi},

conjectured by Gaitsgory, and proven by Beraldo for
GL_{n}
and in general by Raskin
[
Ber17
]
,
[
Ras21
]
.

We denote by
\operatorname{Av}_{*}^{\psi,r}
the renormalized averaging functor, i.e. the composition

\mathscr{C}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ins}}\mathscr{C}_{LN,\psi}\simeq\mathscr{C}^{LN,\psi}.

It is given by convolution with an object, which we call the (twisted) renormalized constant sheaf

k^{\psi,r}\in D(LN)^{LN,\psi}\subset D_{\kappa}(LG)^{LN,\psi}.

3.7.5.

Let us recall an explicit presentation of
k^{\psi,r}
. To do so, first consider any ind-scheme of the form

A\simeq\varinjlim_{\alpha}A_{\alpha},

where each
A_{\alpha}
is a pro-finite dimensional affine space and each transition map
A_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow A_{\beta}
is the closed embedding of an open affine subspace of finite codimension (here, the word open means with respect to the pro-topology on
A_{\beta}
). Each
A_{\alpha}
, as with any placid scheme, carries a canonical constant sheaf
k(A_{\alpha})
. Namely, consider any presentation of
A_{\alpha}
as a cofiltered limit

A_{\alpha}\simeq\varprojlim_{\gamma}A_{\alpha}^{\gamma},

where the
A_{\alpha}^{\gamma}
are finite dimensional affine spaces, and the transition maps are smooth surjections. It follows that the
*
-pushforwards
D(A_{\alpha}^{\gamma})\rightarrow D(A_{\alpha}^{\gamma^{\prime}})
admit left adjoints, i.e.
*
-pullbacks. We have by definition that

D(A_{\alpha})\simeq\varinjlim_{\gamma}D(A_{\alpha}^{\gamma}),

i.e. its category of D-modules is the colimit in
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
of those on its finite dimensional quotients under
*
-pullback. In particular, the insertion of the constant sheaf from any step in the colimit is the sought-for constant sheaf
k(A_{\alpha})
.

For any fixed index
\alpha_{\circ}
, there is an associated renormalized constant sheaf
k(A,A_{\alpha_{\circ}})
on all of
A
.
To describe this, note that by our assumptions the
*
-pushforwards
D(A_{\alpha})\rightarrow D(A_{\beta})
admit right adjoints, i.e.
!
-pullbacks. With this,
k(A,A_{\alpha_{\circ}})
is determined uniquely up to unique isomorphism by the properties that its
!
-restriction to any
A_{\beta}
is isomorphic to a cohomological shift of a constant sheaf, and its
!
-restriction to
A_{\alpha_{\circ}}
is
k(A_{\alpha_{\circ}})
. Explicitly, we have by definition

D(A)\simeq\varprojlim_{\alpha}D(A_{\alpha}),

i.e. its category of D-modules is the cofiltered inverse limit in
\operatorname{DGCat}_{\operatorname{cont}}
of those on open profinite dimensional subspaces under
!
-pullback. Hence, the existence follows from the fact that such
!
-pullbacks send, by our assumption on the maps
A_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow A_{\beta}
, constants sheaves to shifts of constant sheaves.

Remark 3.7.6
.

As the notation suggests, the objects
k(A,A_{\alpha_{\circ}})
are semi-infinite in nature, appearing like a constant sheaf along the pro-directions and a dualizing sheaf along the ind-directions. Informally, they may be thought of as halfway between the dualizing sheaf and the constant sheaf, which do not exist as objects of
D(A)
. In particular, the various
k(A,A_{\alpha})
agree up to tensoring by cohomologically graded lines.

Let us apply this discussion to
A=LN
. We then have, in the conventions of the present paper, that the kernel for renormalized averaging is

(3.6)

k^{\psi,r}\simeq k(LN,L^{+}N)\overset{!}{\otimes}\psi.

3.7.7.

It will also be convenient to use the affine Skryabin equivalence

Remark 3.7.8

In the alternative proof of Arakawa’s Theorem

4.
Spectral flow and intertwining functors

The contents of this section are as follows. In the first half, we consider the conjugations of Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction by arbitrary elements of
LG

In the second half, we provide a canonical factorization of each spectrally flowed reduction applied to a block of highest weight modules. These depend on the block

[

5.
The Frenkel–Kac–Wakimoto conjecture

In this section, we calculate the spectrally flowed reductions of certain irreducible highest weight modules. The structure is as follows. Bearing in mind the factorization of Corollary
4.6.1
, we first establish some preliminary assertions about the behavior of the appearing intertwining functors. With these in hand, the main result is obtained in Theorem
5.6.1
.

5.1.

Let us begin by establishing notation. Suppose that
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is a dominant weight at level
\kappa
. That is, we assume the Verma module
M_{\lambda}
is projective in the Category
\mathscr{O}
of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
-modules. By the theorem of Kac–Kazhdan on singular vectors
[
KK79
]
, this is equivalent to the condition that
\kappa
is noncritical and for every positive real affine coroot
\check{\alpha}
one has

\langle\hskip 0.85358pt\check{\alpha},\lambda\rangle\notin\mathbb{Z}^{<0},

cf. Section
3.5.3
for a discussion of the appearing pairing.

We would like to calculate the spectrally flowed Drinfeld–Sokolov reductions of the corresponding irreducible module

\Psi^{\dot{w}}(L_{\lambda}),\quad\quad\text{for }w\in{}^{f}W,

where
\Psi^{\dot{w}}
is as in Section
4.4
.

5.2.

To do so, write
\chi
for the character sheaf on
I
associated to the root lattice coset
\lambda+\Lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\Lambda
, so that

L_{\lambda}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}.

Recall from Corollary
4.6.1
the associated factorization

(5.1)

\Psi^{\dot{w}}\simeq\Psi^{-}\circ j_{{w}_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}\circ j_{{w}_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}.

We now collect some facts about the behavior of the first steps, i.e. the intertwining functors.

5.3.

It will be convenient to begin with the middle factor
j_{{w}_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}
. Recalling it is clean, we will control it as follows.

Proposition 5.3.1
.

Fix a character sheaf
\phi\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\Lambda
and an element
w\in W
. Suppose that the associated intertwining integral

j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}

is clean. Then the corresponding convolution functor

j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\phi}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,w\phi}

is
t
-exact, and for any
\mu\in\phi+\Lambda
there are isomorphisms

(5.2)

j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{\mu}\simeq M_{w\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptL_{\mu}\simeq L_{w\mu}.

Remark 5.3.2
.

It formally follows that the convolution also exchanges the contragredient Verma modules.

Proof of Proposition
5.3.1
.

We first reduce the assertion to the case where
w
is a simple reflection. If
w
has length zero, the assertion is clear. If
w
has length greater than one, then
w>ws
for some simple reflection
s
, and we have a corresponding factorization

(5.3)

j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526ptj_{ws,*}^{w\phi,s\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}.

We claim that both factors of the right-hand side are again clean. We first show this for
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
. Indeed, if not, we would have
s\phi=\phi
and a surjection
j_{s,*}^{\phi,\phi}\twoheadrightarrow j_{e,*}^{\phi,\phi}
. By convolving this with
j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}
, by the right exactness of convolution with costandard objects we would obtain a surjection

j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}\twoheadrightarrow j_{ws,*}^{w\phi,\phi},

which would contradict the cleanness of
j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}
.

To proceed, we will use the following basic lemma.

Lemma 5.3.3
.

Suppose
s
is a simple reflection of
W,
and
\phi
is a character sheaf such that the object
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
is clean. Then for any
w\in W
there are isomorphisms

(5.4)

j_{w,!}^{w\phi,s\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s,!}^{s\phi,\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526ptj_{ws,!}^{w\phi,\phi}\quad\text{and}\quad j_{w,*}^{w\phi,s\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526ptj_{ws,*}^{w\phi,\phi}

That is, convolution with
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
sends (co)standard objects to (co)standard objects.

Remark 5.3.4
.

This lemma appears,
mutatis mutandis
, in the study of finite twisted Hecke categories performed in
[
LY20
]
, and likely earlier as well. A similar analysis of the affine twisted Hecke categories, which refines certain results proved in the remainder of the present paper, as well as its applications, e.g. to the tamely ramified local quantum Langlands correspondence, will appear in forthcoming work of the author and Y. W. Li, Z. Yun, and X. Zhu.

Proof of Lemma
5.3.3
.

If
w<ws
, then the both assertions of Equation (
5.4
) are well known to hold without assumptions of cleaness, and follow from the fact that

\mathring{I}wI\overset{I}{\times}IsI\rightarrow\mathring{I}wsI

is an isomorphism. The claims for
w>ws
then follow from the fact that
-\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
is inverse to
-\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s,!}^{\phi,s\phi}
, and the cleanness of the latter.
∎

With the lemma in hand, the asserted cleanness of
j_{ws,*}^{w\phi,s\phi}
follows from (
5.3
).

Iterating the factorization (
5.3
), it follows that
j_{w,*}^{w\phi,\phi}
may be written as a convolution of clean costandard objects associated to simple reflections and a length zero costandard object. As the claims of the proposition are compatible with composition, this completes the reduction to the case of
w
being a simple reflection
s
.

In this case, write
P
for the corresponding (minimal) standard parahoric subgroup of
LG
. We first argue that for any
\mu\in\phi+\Lambda
one has

(5.5)

j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptM_{\mu}\simeq M_{s\mu}.

To see this, write
M
and
\mathfrak{m}
for the corresponding Levi factors of
LG
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
, respectively, and note these are of semisimple rank one. By the
D(P)
-equivariance of parabolic induction

\operatorname{pind}:\mathfrak{m}\operatorname{-mod}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

see for example Section 2.5.4 of
[
CD21
]
, the assertion (
5.5
) reduces to the analogous assertion for
\mathfrak{m}
. To see the latter, write
B^{s}
for the opposite Borel of
P
containing
T
, and
N^{s}
for its prounipotent radical. If we pick any lift
\dot{s}
of
s
to
N_{M}(T)
, we may identify
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
with the composition

(5.6)

\mathfrak{m}\operatorname{-mod}^{B,\phi}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Av}_{N^{s},*}[1]}\mathfrak{m}\operatorname{-mod}^{B^{s},s\phi}\xrightarrow{\delta_{\dot{s}}}\mathfrak{m}\operatorname{-mod}^{B,s\phi}.

The remainder follows from the highest weight representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Briefly, by our assumptions on
\phi
and
s\phi
, the appearing categories are semisimple, so one is reduced to a straightforward verification at the level of characters.

It remains to prove the
t
-exactness of
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
and the interchanging of simple modules. But recall that an object
N
of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,s\phi}
lies in cohomological degrees
\geqslant 0
if and only if

\operatorname{Hom}(M_{\nu},N)\xrightarrow{\sim}\tau^{\geqslant 0}\operatorname{Hom}(M_{\nu},N)

\text{for all $\nu$ in $s\phi+\Lambda$}.

Therefore, the claimed
t
-exactness of
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
follows from (
5.5
). Moreover, as
j_{s,*}^{s\phi,\phi}
is further an equivalence, it also exchanges the simple quotients of the Verma modules, as desired. ∎

5.4.

We next gather some facts which will be of use in controlling the final intertwining integral on the right-hand side of (
5.1
), namely
j_{w_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}
.

Recall that
W_{\chi}
is a Coxeter group, and in particular carries a distinguished set of simple reflections and a Bruhat order. We will need the following assertion.

Proposition 5.4.1
.

Fix elements
y,s\in W_{\chi}
such that
s
is a simple reflection of
W_{\chi}
and
y<ys
. There exists an isomorphism of costandard objects

j_{ys,*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526ptj_{y,*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s,*}^{\chi,\chi}.

We emphasize that the appearing simple reflection
s
of
W_{\chi}
typically is not a simple reflection of the ambient group
W
. This renders the proposition not entirely geometrically obvious, as the underlying convolution of Schubert varieties will typically not be an isomorphism. Before giving the proof, we note the following useful consequence.

Corollary 5.4.2
.

For an element
y
of
W_{\chi}
, fix a reduced expression

y=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{\ell}}.

There exists a corresponding isomorphism of costandard objects

j_{\dot{y},*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s_{i_{1}},*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s_{i_{2}},*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526pt\cdots\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{s_{i_{\ell}},*}^{\chi,\chi}.

Proof of Proposition
5.4.1
.

We will reduce to the case of
s
being also a simple reflection of
W
using the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4.3
.

If
s
is a simple reflection of
W_{\chi}
, then there exists elements
z
and
t
in
W
such that the following hold.

(1)

The element
t
is a simple reflection of
W
.

(2)

One has the equality
s=ztz^{-1}
.

(3)

The action of
z
on the real affine coroots restricts to an isomorphism

(5.7)

z:\check{\Phi}_{\chi}^{+}\xrightarrow{\sim}\check{\Phi}_{z\chi}^{+}.

In particular, conjugation by
z
yields an isomorphism of Coxeter systems

z:W_{\chi}\xrightarrow{\sim}W_{z\chi}.

Proof.

If the coroot
\check{\alpha}
corresponding to
s
is simple in
\check{\Phi}^{+}
we are done. If not, there exists a simple reflection
u
of
W
such that

(5.8)

\check{\alpha}>u(\check{\alpha})>0.

Since by assumption
\check{\alpha}
is a simple coroot in
\check{\Phi}^{+}_{\chi}
, it follows that
u
does not lie in
W_{\chi}
, and in particular we have that acting by
u
restricts to an isomorphism

u:\check{\Phi}^{+}_{\chi}\xrightarrow{\sim}\check{\Phi}^{+}_{u\chi}.

This reduces the assertions of the lemma for the pair
(\chi,s)
to those for
(u\chi,usu^{-1})
. By Equation (
5.8
) and the fact that there are only finitely many positive coroots less than
\check{\alpha}
, iterating this argument must bring us to a simple coroot of
\check{\Phi}^{+}
, as desired. ∎

Let
s
be as in the statement of the proposition, and let
t
and
z
be as in the statement of Lemma
5.4.3
. By the proof of the lemma, in combination with Lemma
5.3.3
, it follows that the objects

j_{z,*}^{\chi,z\chi}\quad\text{and}\quad j_{z^{-1},*}^{z\chi,z}

are clean. In particular, the conjugation monoidal equivalence

j_{z,*}^{\chi,z\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526pt-\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{z^{-1},*}^{z\chi,\chi}:D(I,z\chi\backslash LG/I,z\chi)\simeq D(I,\chi\backslash LG/I,\chi)

reduces the assertion of the proposition to the case where the simple reflection
s
of
W_{\chi}
is a simple reflection of
W
, which is standard, cf. Lemma
5.3.3
.
∎

Since we will use it later, we explicitly record one fact developed in the proof of Proposition
5.4.1
. Namely, the following lifts Lemma
5.4.3
, particularly statement (2) therein, to the intertwining functors.

Lemma 5.4.4
.

Let
s,t,z
be as in Lemma
5.4.3
. Then there is an isomorphism of costandard objects

j_{s,*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526ptj_{z,*}^{\chi,z\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{t,*}^{z\chi,z\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptj_{z^{-1},*}^{z\chi,\chi}.

That is, up to conjugation by a clean object, any costandard object associated to a simple reflection of
W_{\chi}
is a costandard object associated to a simple reflection of
W
.

5.5.

In this subsection, which may be skipped by the reader, we use what we have developed so far to obtain bounds on the cohomological amplitude of the spectrally flowed reduction functors. To our knowledge, these are new already for the plus reduction.

Let us collect two pieces of notation. First, we denote the standard length function on
W_{\chi}
with respect to its Coxeter generators by

\ell_{\chi}:W_{\chi}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\geqslant 0}.

Second, let
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
be dg-categories equipped with
t
-structures and a functor

F:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathscr{D}.

Fix integers
a
and
b
with
a\leqslant b
. Recall that

F
is said to have cohomological amplitude at most
[a,b]
if
F[b]
is right exact and
F[a]
is left exact, i.e.

F(\mathscr{C}^{\leqslant 0})\subset\mathscr{D}^{\leqslant b}\quad\text{and}\quad F(\mathscr{C}^{\geqslant 0})\subset\mathscr{D}^{\geqslant a}.

In particular, for any object
\xi
in the heart of the
t
-structure on
\mathscr{C}
,
F(\xi)
has nonzero cohomology, with respect to the truncation functors on
\mathscr{D}
, in at most cohomological degrees
a\leqslant i\leqslant b
.

Theorem 5.5.1
.

Let
w,\chi,w_{\chi}
be as in Proposition
4.5.3
. Then the spectrally flowed reduction functor

\Psi^{w}:\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}

has cohomological amplitude at most

(5.9)

[-\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi}),0].

Remark 5.5.2
.

The bounds (
5.9
) are essentially optimal. On the one hand, the upper bound is always obtained; indeed it follows from Corollary
4.6.1
that
\Psi^{w}
sends Verma modules to twisted Verma modules concentrated in degree zero. On the other hand, we will see momentarily that reductions of dominant irreducibles, when nonzero, are concentrated in degree
-\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi})
.

Proof of Theorem
5.5.1
.

In view of Corollary
4.6.1
, we equivalently must show that the composition

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}\xrightarrow{j_{{w}_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}}\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}\xrightarrow{j_{{w}_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}}\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,w_{-}\chi}\xrightarrow{\Psi^{-}}\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod},

has cohomological amplitude at most
[-\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi}),0]
. Recall that the middle and rightmost functors are
t
-exact, see Proposition
5.3.1
and Theorem
3.5.6
, respectively. Therefore, it is enough to show that
j_{{w}_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}
has cohomological amplitude at most

[-\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi}),0].

To see this, by Corollary
5.4.2
it suffices to show that for a simple reflection
s
of
W_{\chi}
the intertwining integral
j_{s,*}^{\chi,\chi}
has cohomological amplitude at most

[-1,0].

By Lemma
5.4.4
and Proposition
5.3.1
, we may assume that
s
is also a simple reflection of
W
. However, this final case is clear, for example by the analog of Equation (
5.6
). ∎

5.6.

To state our main theorem, we now introduce one final piece of notation, and then review several others.

Let us write
D_{\kappa}
for the set of dominant weights at level
\kappa
. This carries an action of
W
, which we denote by

W\times D_{\kappa}\rightarrow D_{\kappa},\quad\quad(w,\lambda)\mapsto w*\lambda,

defined as follows. Given a pair
(w,\lambda)
, the orbit of
w\lambda
under its integral Weyl group contains a unique dominant weight
w\lambda^{+}
, and we set

w*\lambda:=w\lambda^{+}.

It is straightforward that this defines an action.

For the statement of the following theorem we recall that
\pi
denotes the Harish-Chandra projection

\pi:\mathfrak{t}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{*}/\hskip-3.1298pt/W_{f},

and that a weight
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is said to be finite antidominant if for every finite positive coroot
\check{\alpha}
one has

\langle\check{\alpha},\lambda\rangle\notin\mathbb{Z}^{>0},

where the appearing pairing incorporates a
\rho
-shift as in Section
3.5.3
. Finally, we refer to Section
2.2
for our normalization of highest weights for
\mathcal{W}_{\kappa}
-modules. We are now ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 5.6.1
.

Fix an element
w\in{}^{f}W
and a dominant irreducible module

L_{\lambda}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}\operatorname{-mod}^{I,\chi}.

Let
w_{\chi}
be as in Proposition
4.5.3
. Then the corresponding spectrally flowed Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction is given by

\Psi^{\dot{w}}(L_{\lambda})\simeq\begin{cases}L_{\pi(w*\lambda)}[\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi})]&\text{if $w*\lambda$ is finite antidominant,}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We will proceed step by step along the factorization of Corollary
4.6.1
, namely

\Psi^{w}\simeq\Psi^{-}\circ j_{w_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}\circ j_{w_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}.

To begin, we claim that one has an isomorphism

(5.10)

j_{w_{\chi},*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptL_{\lambda}\simeq L_{\lambda}[\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi})].

To see this, by Corollary
5.4.2
it is enough to show that for a simple reflection
s
of
W_{\chi}
one has

j_{s,*}^{\chi,\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptL_{\lambda}\simeq L_{\lambda}[1].

By Lemma
5.4.4
and Proposition
5.3.1
, we may further assume that
s
is a simple reflection of
W
. In this case, let
P
be the corresponding parahoric subgroup of
LG
, and note that
\chi
extends uniquely to a character sheaf on
P
, which we denote by the same letter.

As
L_{\lambda}
is
(P,\chi)
-equivariant, it suffices to see that for any
D_{\kappa}(LG)
-module
\mathscr{C}
the composition

(5.11)

\mathscr{C}^{P,\chi}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Oblv}}\mathscr{C}^{I,\chi}\xrightarrow{j_{s,*}^{\chi,\chi}}\mathscr{C}^{I,\chi}

is equivalent to

(5.12)

\mathscr{C}^{P,\chi}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Oblv}[1]}\mathscr{C}^{I,\chi}.

However, it is straightforward to see by a calculation on the Levi factor of
P
, which is of semisimple rank one, that both (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) are given by convolution with the same object of

D_{\kappa}(I,\chi\backslash LG/P,\chi),

as desired. This completes the proof of (
5.10
).

To proceed, recall that
j_{w_{-},*}^{w_{-}\chi,\chi}
is clean. By Proposition
5.3.1
, it therefore follows that

j_{w_{-},*}^{w_{-},\chi}\hskip 2.84526pt\overset{I}{\star}\hskip 2.84526ptL_{\lambda}[\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi})]\simeq L_{w_{-}\lambda}[\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi})].

Finally, applying the minus reduction
\Psi^{-}
to
L_{w_{-}\lambda}[\ell_{\chi}(w_{\chi})]
yields the statement of the theorem.
∎

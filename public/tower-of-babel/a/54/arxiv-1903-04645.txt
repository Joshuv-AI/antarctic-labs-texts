Syzygy Filtrations of Cyclic Nakayama Algebras
Emre SEN

Abstract
. For any cyclic Nakayama algebra Λ \Lambda , we construct syzygy filtered algebra 𝜺 ⁡ ( Λ ) \bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda) which corresponds to various syzygy modules as the name suggests. We prove that the category of modules over the syzygy filtered algebra 𝜺 ⁡ ( Λ ) \bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda) is equivalent to the wide subcategory cogenerated by projective-injective modules of the original algebra Λ \Lambda along with other categorical equivalences. In terms of this new algebra, we interpret the following homological invariants of Λ \Lambda : left and right finitistic dimension, left and right φ \varphi -dimension, Gorenstein dimension, dominant dimension and their upper bounds. For all of them, we obtain a unified upper bound 2 ​ r 2r where r r is the number of relations defining the algebra Λ \Lambda . We show that the left finitistic and φ \varphi -dimensions are equal to the right finitistic and φ \varphi -dimensions respectively, as well as the difference between φ \varphi -dimension and the finitistic dimension is at most one. Furthermore, we recover various seemingly unrelated results in a uniform way.

Introduction

For an artin algebra

\mathbb{K}

A

[

It turns out that this new homological tool has applications. For instance, an algebra is selfinjective if and only if

[
LM18
]

[
ES17
]

An artin algebra is called Nakayama algebra if each left or right indecomposable projective module has a unique composition series. Nakayama algebras are either linear or cyclic depending on the underlying quiver.

2.1.
Nakayama algebras via relations

Before introducing the system of relations independently, first we want to discuss its relationship with Kupisch series which is more common tool for the community. The direct approach was given in
[
Sen21
]
, but it brings its own burden, which is the cyclic ordering of particular simple and projective modules. To keep all the relevant data, intricate usage of indices is necessary and it is not easy to digest at first. To avoid these complications, it is convenient for us to interpret Kupisch series in terms of the structures of the modules. To perform this, the first step we take is the notion of minimal projective module.

Definition 2.7
.

An indecomposable projective
\Lambda

\Lambda

\topp\rad P_{i}\cong\topp P_{i+1}

2.4
, either
\rad P_{i}
is the quotient of
P_{i+1}
or vice versa. Because
\ell(\rad P_{i})=\ell(P_{i})-1=c_{i}-1<c_{i}<c_{i+1}=\ell(P_{i+1})
, the latter is not possible. We get the onto map
f:P_{i+1}\rightarrow\rad P_{i}
. The short exact sequence

(2.5)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\ker f\rightarrow P_{i+1}\rightarrow\rad P_{i}\rightarrow 0

and lemma
2.5
imply that
\topp\ker f\cong\tau\soc\rad P_{i}\cong\tau\soc P_{i}
and
\ell(\ker f)=c_{i+1}-c_{i}+1=d
. If we apply Auslander-Reiten translate
\tau^{d-1}
to the isomorphism
\tau\soc P_{i}\cong\topp\ker f
, then we get
\tau^{d}\soc P_{i}\cong\tau^{d-1}\topp\ker f
. Since the length of
\ker f
is
d
, we obtain
\tau^{d-1}\topp\ker f\cong\soc\ker f\cong\soc P_{i+1}
. Hence
\tau^{d}\soc P_{i}\cong\soc P_{i+1}
.

ii)

If
P_{i}
was not minimal projective, then
\rad P_{i}
would be projective module. However the sequence
2.5
shows that
\rad P_{i}
is a proper quotient of a projective module. Projective modules cannot be quotient of another module. So,
P_{i}
is minimal projective.

iii)

It is enough to prove that the injective envelope
I
of
\tau\soc P_{i}
is the quotient of
P_{i+1}
. First of all, this proves that
P_{i+1}
is an injective module because non-projective but injective modules has to be quotients of projective-injectives by the dual of lemma
2.6
. Secondly, when
I
is an injective quotient, then the sequence of surjective maps

(2.6)

\displaystyle P_{i+1}\rightarrow I\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{P_{i+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\soc P_{i+1}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{P_{i+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\soc P_{i+1}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{P_{i+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\soc P_{i+1}}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{P_{i+1}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\soc P_{i+1}}$}}}\right)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow I=I(\tau\soc P_{i})

shows that each module are injective because
I
is injective quotient of all of them.

Notice that
\soc\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\soc I}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\soc I}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\soc I}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\soc I}$}}}\right)\cong\soc\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}\right)\cong S=\soc P_{i}
. Relying on the uniseriality lemma
2.3
, we get three cases:

Case i)

P_{i}\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}
is not possible because a projective module cannot be a quotient of an injective by
2.6
.

Case ii)

P_{i}\subset{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{S}$}}}
is impossible because a projective module cannot be a subquotient of an injective by
2.6
.

Case iii)

We conclude that
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}
is a proper submodule of
P_{i}
.

Now there are two possibilities, either
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}
is isomorphic to
\rad P_{i}
or it is a proper submodule of
\rad P_{i}
. We will show that the latter is impossible. If we suppose
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}
is a proper submodule of
\rad P_{i}
, we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\ell\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}\right)<\ell(\rad P_{i})=c_{i}-1.

On the other hand, the exact sequence
2.5
gives

(2.8)

\displaystyle\ell\left(\rad P_{i}\right)=\ell(P_{i+1})-\ell(\ker f)

Combining
2.7
and
2.8
we get

(2.9)

\displaystyle\ell\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{P}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{P}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{P}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{P}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}\right)<\ell(\rad P_{i})=\ell(P_{i+1})-\ell(\ker f).

If we substitute the lengths
\ell(P_{i})=c_{i}
,
\ell(P_{i+1})=c_{i+1}
,
\ell(\ker f)=d
(see
2.4
) to
2.9
, then

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}c_{i}-1<c_{i+1}-d\\
c_{i}-1<c_{i+1}-\left(c_{i+1}-c_{i}+1\right)\\
-1<-1\end{gathered}

which is not a true statement. Therefore
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\tau S}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\tau S}$}}}
has to be isomorphic to the radical of
P_{i}
, and by the sequence
2.5
,
P_{i+1}
is the projective cover of
I
, which makes
P_{i+1}
projective-injective. Moreover, this makes all the modules we considered in
2.6
injective. Their socles are
\tau\soc P_{i}\cong\soc I,\tau^{2}\soc P_{i},\ldots,\tau^{d-1}\soc P_{i},\tau^{d}\soc P_{i}\cong\soc P_{i+1}
. There are
d-1=c_{i+1}-c_{i}
proper injective quotients of
P_{i+1}
.

∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\Lambda
be given by the Kupisch series
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N})
. Projective modules
P_{i+1},P_{i+2}\ldots,P_{k},\ldots,P_{j-1},P_{j}
have isomorphic socle
S
and
\soc P_{i}\ncong S
,
\soc P_{j+1}\ncong S
if and only if
c_{i}\leq c_{i+1}
and
c_{j}\leq c_{j+1}
and
c_{k}>c_{k+1}
for all
i+1\geq k\geq j
.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
. If projective modules
P_{k}
have isomorphic socle
S
when
i+1\leq k\leq j
, by proposition
2.8
we get
c_{k}>c_{k+1}
. Because
\soc P_{j}\ncong S
,
P_{j+1}
is not a submodule of
P_{j}
which makes
P_{j}
minimal projective. Therefore, proposition
2.8
implies
c_{j}\leq c_{j+1}
. Similarly,
\soc P_{i}\ncong S
implies that
P_{i+1}
is not submodule of
P_{i}
, hence
P_{i}
is minimal projective and we get
c_{i}\leq c_{i+1}
by the same proposition
2.8
.

(\Leftarrow)

c_{j}\leq c_{j+1}
and
c_{i}\leq c_{i+1}
imply that
P_{j}
and
P_{i}
are minimal projectives. The condition
c_{k}>c_{k+1}
for
i+1\leq k\leq j
imply none of the projective modules from
P_{i+1}
to
P_{j-1}
are minimal. In other words, each
P_{k}
is isomorphic to (higher) radical of
P_{i+1}
, i.e.
P_{k}\cong\rad^{m}P_{i+1}
for some
m
. Therefore they have isomorphic socle.
∎

Definition 2.13
.

The collection of projective modules having isomorphic socle is called
class of projective modules
.

To find the relations, for each class of projective modules, we need to know the minimal projective and projective-injective of the class to express all the projective modules in it. By proposition
2.12
, the information of projective-injective module of each class can be obtained from minimal projective of the previous class. Therefore, the important data is the relative positions and the length of minimal projectives. If we know the top and the length of a minimal projective, it produces the relation starting at the index of the top with the same length. We order each relation according to their relative position in the Kupisch series which describes classes of projective modules.
Let
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N})
be a Kupisch series and
(c_{i_{1}},c_{i_{2}},\ldots,c_{i_{r}})
be a subsequence of it such that each
c_{i_{m}}
is the length of minimal projective
P_{i_{m}}
. Then the relations defining the algebra are

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\alpha_{i_{1}+c_{i_{1}}-1}\circ\alpha_{i_{1}+c_{i_{1}}-2}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{i_{1}+1}\circ\alpha_{i_{1}}=0\\
\alpha_{i_{2}+c_{i_{2}}-1}\circ\alpha_{i_{2}+c_{i_{2}}-2}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{i_{2}+1}\circ\alpha_{i_{2}}=0\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{i_{r}+c_{i_{r}}-1}\circ\alpha_{i_{r}+c_{i_{r}}-2}\circ\cdots\circ\alpha_{i_{r}+1}\circ\alpha_{i_{r}}=0\end{gathered}

The converse is also true. We start with a system of relations which gives the index of minimal projectives. And the ordering of the relations gives us the ordering of the minimal projectives in the Kupisch series.

For our purposes, the socle of a projective module carries more information than the length of a projective module. Therefore, from now on we use relations (see
2.2
below) to define any cyclic Nakayama algebra.

2.4.
Other realizations of the base set

Here, we establish the link between the base set and particular syzygy modules.

Lemma 2.19
.

Elements of the base set cannot be projective-injective modules.

Proof.

Assume that projective-injective module
P_{i}
is an element of the base set. Therefore
\topp P_{i}\cong S_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\Lambda)
which implies that
\tau^{-1}S_{i}\cong S_{i-1}
is an element of the socle set
{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda)
. Because algebra is cyclic,
\ell(P_{i-1})\geq 2
and there is a map

which induces the short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\ker f\rightarrow P_{i}\rightarrow\rad P_{i-1}\rightarrow 0.

If
\ker f
is trivial, then
P_{i}\cong\rad P_{i-1}
, which makes
P_{i}
proper submodule of
P_{i-1}
. This violates the projective-injectivity of
P_{i}
.

If
\ker f
is nontrivial, then
\soc\ker f\cong\soc P_{i}
and
\topp\ker f\cong\tau\soc\rad P_{i-1}\cong\tau\soc P_{i}
by lemma
2.5
. Therefore the top of
\ker f
is an element of the top set
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\Lambda)
, socle of
\ker f
is an element of
{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda)
and
\ker f\subset P_{i}
. Therefore
P_{i}
is not the shortest module (see def.
2.15
). This shows that
P_{i}
cannot be an element of the base set
{\mathcal{B}}(\Lambda)
.
∎

3.1.
Results about
\varphi
-dimension

Let
A
be an artin algebra.
Let
K_{0}
be the abelian group generated by all symbols
\left[X\right]
, where
X
is a finitely generated
A
-module, modulo the relations

i)

\left[A_{1}\right]=\left[A_{2}\right]+\left[A_{3}\right]
if
A_{1}\cong A_{2}\oplus A_{3}

ii)

\left[P\right]=0
if
P
is projective.

Then
K_{0}
is the free abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes of indecomposable finitely generated non-projective
A
-modules. For any finitely generated
A
-module
M
, let
L\left[M\right]:=\left[\Omega M\right]
where
\Omega M
is the first syzygy of
M
. Since
\Omega
commutes with direct sums and takes projective modules to zero this gives a homomorphism
L:K_{0}\rightarrow K_{0}
. For every finitely generated
A
-module
M
, let
\left\langle\add M\right\rangle
denote the subgroup of
K_{0}
generated by additive generator of
M
, i.e. all the indecomposable summands of
M
.
\langle\add M\rangle
is a free abelian group because it is a subgroup of free abelian group
K_{0}
.
In other words, if
M=\oplus_{i=1}^{m}{M_{i}}^{n_{i}}
then
\left\langle\add M\right\rangle=\left\langle\{[M_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{m}\right\rangle
and
L^{t}(\left\langle\add M\right\rangle)=\left\langle\{[\Omega^{t}M_{i}]\}_{i=1}^{m}\right\rangle
.

Definition 3.1
.

For a given module
M
in
\modd A
,
\varphi
-dimension of

is

\displaystyle\varphi(M):=\min\left\{t\,|\operatorname{rank}\left(L^{t}\langle\add M\rangle\right)=\operatorname{rank}\left(L^{t+j}\langle\add M\rangle\right)\text{ for}\,j\geq 1\right\}.

Notice that
\varphi(M)
is finite for each module
M
, since the rank has to become stable at some step. For example, if projective dimension of
M
is finite, then
\varphi(M)=p\dim(M)
.

Definition 3.2
.

If
A
is an artin algebra, then
\varphi
-dimension of
A
is

\varphi\dim A:=\sup\{\varphi(M)\ |\ M\in\modd\text{A}\}.

We collect some statements about
\varphi
-dimension, proofs and other properties can be found in
[
Sen21
]
.

Remark 3.3

i)

Let

be an artin algebra of finite representation type. Let
\{M_{1},\dots,M_{m}\}

\Lambda
-modules. Then
\varphi\dim(A)=\varphi(\oplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{i})
.

ii)

If global dimension of algebra

\findim A=\sup\{\pdim X\ |\ \pdim X<\infty,X\in\text{mod-}A\}

We recall the result about small values of

-dimension as stated in

Theorem 3.6
.

If
\Lambda
is a cyclic Nakayama algebra, then
\varphi\dim\Lambda=\varphi\dim\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)+2
provided that
\varphi\dim\Lambda\geq 2
and
\gldim\Lambda=\infty

Proof.

For any indecomposable
\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)
-module

{\mathcal{B}}(\Lambda)
-filtered
\Lambda
-module

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)}(X)=\varphi_{\Lambda}(\Delta X).

\displaystyle\varphi(M)=2+\varphi(\Omega^{2}(M))=2+\varphi(\Delta X)=2+\varphi_{\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)}(X).

\displaystyle\varphi\dim\Lambda

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\varphi(M)|\,M\in\modd\Lambda\right\}

Corollary 3.7

\displaystyle\varphi\dim\Lambda=2d+\varphi\dim\bm{\varepsilon}^{d}(\Lambda)

Theorem 3.9

[

Assume that global dimension of cyclic Nakayama algebra

-dimension of

3.2.
Results about finitistic dimension

First we state and prove results regarding possible small values of the finitistic dimension and
\varphi
-dimension. Then we state the reduction method for the finitistic dimension.
We recall definition of the finitistic dimension of artin algebra A,

\displaystyle\findim A:=\sup\left\{\pdim M|\,M\,\text{is an}\,A\text{-module with}\,\,\pdim M<\infty\right\}.

We start with the following observation.

Lemma 3.10
.

If
\varphi\dim\Lambda=2
, then
\varphi\dim\Lambda-\findim\Lambda\leq 1
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi\dim\Lambda=2
, by the remark
2.47

\Lambda
is not selfinjective and there exists a projective module

which is not minimal. Therefore, the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\rad P\rightarrow P\rightarrow\topp P\rightarrow 0

implies that
\pdim\topp P\geq 1
and as a result
\findim\Lambda\geq 1
.
On the other hand,
\findim\Lambda\leq\varphi\dim\Lambda
which is stated in the remark
3.3
part
ii)
, we conclude that
\varphi\dim\Lambda-\findim\Lambda\leq 1
.
∎

Now we give the proof of the statement
iii)
of Theorem
A
.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
\Lambda
is a cyclic Nakayama algebra, then

\findim\Lambda=\findim\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)+2
, provided that
\findim\Lambda\geq 2
and
\gldim\Lambda=\infty
.

Proof.

Both of
\Lambda
and the syzygy filtered algebra are finite representation type, so there exists
\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)
-module

such that
\pdim X=\findim\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)
. If

is the corresponding module i.e.

, then

by proposition
2.42
. Therefore we get

(3.1)

\displaystyle\findim\Lambda

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\pdim M|\,M\in\modd\Lambda,\,\pdim M<\infty\right\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\pdim\Omega^{2}(M)|\,M\in\modd\Lambda,\,\pdim M<\infty\right\}+2.

Any

has

-filtration by proposition

which implies

\displaystyle\findim\Lambda

\displaystyle=2+\sup\left\{\pdim\Delta X|\,\Delta X\in Filt({\mathcal{B}}(\Lambda)),\,\pdim\Delta X<\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=2+\sup\left\{\pdim X|\,X\in\modd\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda),\,\pdim X<\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=2+\findim\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)

We want to analyze the case of

. The reduction above implies that

. This forces that all the simple

.
∎

We placed the condition on the lower bound of the finitistic dimension, otherwise we could not pass from

to

Corollary 3.12
.

If
\Lambda

\displaystyle\findim\Lambda=2d+\findim\bm{\varepsilon}^{d}(\Lambda)

, the syzygy filtered algebra is cyclic Nakayama algebra and of infinite global dimension. Therefore we can apply propositions

∎

Corollary 3.13

If
\Lambda
is cyclic Nakayama algebra of infinite global dimension, then there exists

\displaystyle 1\leq\findim\bm{\varepsilon}^{d}(\Lambda)\leq 2.

By Theorem

\bm{\varepsilon}^{d+1}(\Lambda)

Now we can prove the statement

of Theorem

Theorem 3.14
.

If
\Lambda
is a cyclic Nakayama algebra then
\varphi\dim\Lambda-\findim\Lambda\leq 1

Proof.

If global dimension of

. So, we assume that global dimension is infinite.

By Theorem

, there exists

3.3.
Results about Gorenstein dimension

We begin with definition of Gorenstein dimension. By using duality and representation type of Nakayama algebras, instead of injective dimension of
\Lambda
, we prefer to study projective dimensions of injective modules. Then we state and prove results regarding Gorenstein homological properties.

Definition 3.15
.

If projective resolution of injective modules are finite, algebra is called
Gorenstein
. Supremum of projective dimensions of injective modules is called
Gorenstein dimension
, and denoted by
\gordim
. The formulation is

\displaystyle\gordim\Lambda=\sup\left\{\pdim I|\,I\in\modd\Lambda\,\text{is injective}\right\}<\infty.

First, we need possible small values of Gorenstein dimension. Then we show the reduction method.

Lemma 3.16
.

If
\Lambda
is cyclic Nakayama algebra which is Gorenstein, then Gorenstein dimension cannot be one.

Proof.

If
\Lambda
is selfinjective, by definition,
\gordim\Lambda=0
. If
\Lambda
is not selfinjective, there exists a projective-injective module

such that its quotient is injective

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\soc PI\rightarrow PI\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0

is exact. Since
\Lambda
is cyclic, there is no simple projective module. Therefore
\pdim I=1+\pdim\soc PI\geq 2
which implies
\gordim\Lambda\neq 1
.

3.4.
Results about dominant dimension

We study dominant dimension in terms of the syzygy filtered algebras.

Definition 3.27
.

[
ARS97
]
Dominant dimension of algebra
A
is:

\displaystyle\domdim A=\sup\left\{m|\,I_{i}\,\text{is projective for }i=0,1,\ldots m\right\}+1

where
0\rightarrow\,_{A}A\rightarrow I_{0}\rightarrow I_{1}\ldots
is the minimal injective resolution of

. Dominant dimension of semisimple algebra is set to be infinity. We can define dominant dimension of any module

by

\displaystyle\domdim M=\sup\left\{m|\,I_{i}\,\text{is projective for }i=0,1,\ldots m\right\}+1

where
0\rightarrow_{A}M\rightarrow I_{0}\rightarrow I_{1}\ldots
is the minimal injective resolution of

. If we restrict

to projective modules we get the following characterization of dominant dimension

(3.6)

\displaystyle\domdim A=\sup\left\{\domdim P\,|\,P\text{ is projective non-injective }A\,\text{module}\right\}.

It is convenient for us to study projective dimensions of injective modules, because for a Nakayama algebra
\Lambda
,
\domdim\Lambda=\domdim\Lambda^{op}
holds. Therefore the statement
3.6

is equivalent to

(3.7)

\displaystyle\domdim A=\sup\left\{\domdim I\,|\,I\text{ is injective non-projective }A\,\text{module}\right\}.

We always use the characterization of dominant dimension
3.7
in the proofs.

We begin with a lemma which shows that the first syzygy of an injective module has to be a submodule of an element of the base set.

Lemma 3.28

If

\Lambda
-module, then

of the base set

Let

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(I)\rightarrow P_{i}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\ell(P_{i})-\ell(I)=\ell(\Omega^{1}(I))\neq 0,

because

is not projective.

On the other hand,

is minimal injective by the corollary

. Moreover by the result

,

If we subtract

Proposition 4.5

4.3.
Applications of the splitting results

Based on the syzygy filtration construction, we give the proof of the Madsen’s result
[
Mad05
]
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Global dimension of
\Lambda
is infinite if and only if there is no simple module of even projective dimension.

Proof.

By propositions
4.7
,
4.11
and
4.12
if there exists a simple module with even projective dimension, then we reach linear Nakayama algebra, so global dimension is finite.
Assume that there is no simple module with even projective dimension. Therefore there is no simple projective module in
\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)
which makes it cyclic and
\operatorname{rank}\Lambda\geq\operatorname{rank}\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)
. Furthermore there is no simple module in
\modd\bm{\varepsilon}(\Lambda)
with even projective dimension, otherwise we can lift the resolution to
\modd\Lambda
and get
\pdim S
even, not possible by the assumption. We can construct
\bm{\varepsilon}^{2}(\Lambda)
, and the same arguments are true, so there is no simple module of even projective dimension, and therefore there is no simple projective module. In each step we get nontrivial cyclic algebras
\bm{\varepsilon}^{k}(\Lambda)
. On the other hand in each step we get
\operatorname{rank}\bm{\varepsilon}^{k}(\Lambda)\geq\operatorname{rank}\bm{\varepsilon}^{k+1}(\Lambda)
. Since the rank cannot reach to zero, each algebra is nontrivial,
\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{rank}\bm{\varepsilon}^{i}(\Lambda)
has to stabilize, which means that we reach selfinjective algebra, so global dimension of
\Lambda
is infinite.
∎

Theorem 4.14
.

If global dimension of cyclic Nakayama algebra
\Lambda
is finite, then there exists
m
such that
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m}(\Lambda)
is not cyclic, and

\displaystyle\gldim\Lambda\leq 2m+r_{m-1}-C

where
r_{m-1}
is the number of relations of the cyclic algebra
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m-1}(\Lambda)
and
C
is the number of connected components of
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

By proposition
4.13
, global dimension of
\Lambda
is finite if and only if there exists at least one simple module
S
with even projective dimension. Therefore we can use propositions
4.12
or
4.9
depending on the conditions on the other simple modules.
Assume that
\Lambda
satisfies the conditions in proposition
4.12
. Therefore there exists
m
such that
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m-1}(\Lambda)
is cyclic,
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m}(\Lambda)
is not cyclic and

\displaystyle\gldim\Lambda=2m+\gldim\bm{\varepsilon}^{m}(\Lambda).

On the other hand, the rank of
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m}(\Lambda)
is the number of relations of the cyclic algebra
\bm{\varepsilon}^{m-1}(\Lambda)
which is denoted by
r_{m-1}
. We recall a well-known result on linear Nakayama algebras (or more generally epresentation directed algebras), global dimension of linear Nakayama algebra

\displaystyle=2m+\gldim\bm{\varepsilon}^{m}(\Lambda)

Now we assume that

Remark 4.15

We give another proof of the main theorem of

Theorem 4.16
.

[
MM18
]

Let
\Lambda
be a Nakayama algebra with a simple module
S
of even projective dimension. Choose
m
minimal such that a simple
\Lambda

\Lambda
is bounded by

where

is the number of vertices of
\Lambda

Proof.

By the result
4.14
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle\gldim\Lambda\leq 2m+r_{m-1}-C.

We need to find the possible minimal value of

and maximal value of

. It is clear that
C=1
is the minimum. Let

denote the number of relations of higher syzygy filtered algebras

4.4.

Proposition

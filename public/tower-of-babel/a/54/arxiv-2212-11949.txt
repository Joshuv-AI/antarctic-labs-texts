On a new class of 2-orthogonal polynomials, II : The integral representations
Khalfa DOUAK, Pascal MARONI Laboratoire Jacques-Louis Lions, Sorbonne Université-CNRS,Boite courrier 187, 75252, Paris Cedex 05, France, Email: khalfa.douak@gmail.com and pascalmaroni@orange.fr

Abstract
A new class of 2-orthogonal polynomials satisfying orthogonality conditions with respect to a pair of linear functionals ( u 0 , u 1 ) (u_{0},u_{1}) was presented in Douak K & Maroni P [On a new class of 2-orthogonal polynomials, I: the recurrence relations and some properties. Integral Transforms Spec Funct. 2021;32(2):134-153]. Six worthwhile special cases were pointed out there. Here we are precisely interesting with the integral representation problem for the functionals associated to these polynomials in each case. The focus will be on the matrix differential equation ( 𝚽 ​ 𝐔 ) ′ + 𝚿 ​ 𝐔 = 0 \big({\bf\Phi U}\big)^{\prime}+{\bf\Psi U}=0 , where 𝚽 {\bf\Phi} , 𝚿 {\bf\Psi} are 2 × 2 2\times 2 polynomial matrices and 𝐔 {\bf U} stands for the vector ( u 0 , u 1 ) t {{}^{t}}(u_{0},u_{1}) . We first establish the differential systems satisfied by the functionals u 0 u_{0} and u 1 u_{1} and then, depending on the case, we show that they admit integral representation via weight functions supported on the real line or on positive real line and defined in terms of various special functions. In order for certain integral representations to exist, addition of Dirac mass is necessary.

Introduction and Motivation

The present paper represents a continuation of our earlier work
[
1
]
where we gave a new class of classical 2-orthogonal polynomials,
we have achieved it for a matter of completeness. The reader is therefore invited to take note of the principal outcomes presented therein before reading this part.
Our main purpose here is to look for an integral representation of both functionals
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
with respect to which the 2-orthogonality conditions hold. The primary objective is to seek a pair of weight functions

and

supported on (intervals of) the real axis.
These weights turn out to satisfy specific linear differential equations of order less than or equal to
2

In discussing these equations and in trying to solve them, we will restrict ourselves to imposing some conditions on the free parameters to find the desired solutions.
Recall in passing that, there are many
2
-orthogonal polynomials for which the associated weights are not known or are not unique but has a characteristic third order standard recurrence relation.

For the convenience of the reader we follow the same notations used in Part I.

By
{\mathscr{P}}
we denote the vector space of polynomials of one variable with complex coefficients and we let
{\mathscr{P}}^{\prime}
its algebraic dual. The duality brackets between
{\mathscr{P}}
and
{\mathscr{P}}^{\prime}
is denoted by
\bigl<.\,,\,.\bigr>
. For a sequence of monic polynomials

defined by

, where

Let us now introduce the 2-orthogonal polynomials sequence (2-OPS)
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
previously investigated in
[
1
]
.
These polynomials have been obtained by working only on the system satisfied by their recurrence coefficients whose the expressions
are explicitly determined. They satisfy the third-order recurrence relation

\displaystyle P_{n+3}(x)=(x-\beta_{n+2})P_{n+2}(x)-\alpha_{n+2}P_{n+1}(x)-\gamma_{n+1}P_{n}(x)\,,\;n\geqslant 0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle P_{0}(x)=1\,,\;P_{1}(x)=x-\beta_{0}\ ,\;P_{2}(x)=(x-\beta_{1})P_{1}(x)-\alpha_{1},

(1.2)

with the recurrence coefficients are given, for

\displaystyle\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon_{n}r-2s)n-\frac{1}{2}(1-\varepsilon_{n})r+\beta_{0},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(n+1)\Bigl[(r^{2}+s^{2})n+(1-\varepsilon_{n})rs+\alpha_{1}\Bigr],

(1.4)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n+1}

\displaystyle=(n+2)(n+1)\Bigl[\varepsilon_{n}(r-\varepsilon_{n}s)^{2}rn-\frac{1}{2}(1-\varepsilon_{n})\big((r+s)^{2}+2\alpha_{1}\big)r+\gamma\Bigr].

(1.5)

A remarkable character of these polynomials is that they possess the Hahn’s property

This means that the polynomials

In this regard, we have established that they satisfy the third-order recurrence relation

\displaystyle Q_{n+3}(x)=(x-\tilde{\beta}_{n+2})Q_{n+2}(x)-\tilde{\alpha}_{n+2}Q_{n+1}(x)-\tilde{\gamma}_{n+1}Q_{n}(x)\,,\;n\geqslant 0,

\displaystyle Q_{0}(x)=1\,,\;Q_{1}(x)=x-\tilde{\beta}_{0}\;,\;\;Q_{2}(x)=(x-\tilde{\beta}_{1})Q_{1}(x)-\tilde{\alpha}_{1},

\displaystyle=n\Bigl[(r^{2}+s^{2})n+(1-\varepsilon_{n})rs+\alpha_{1}\Bigr],\;n\geqslant 1,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{n}

\displaystyle=(n+1)n\Bigl[\varepsilon_{n}(r-\varepsilon_{n}s)^{2}rn-\frac{1}{2}(1-\varepsilon_{n})\big((r+s)^{2}+2\alpha_{1}\big)r+\gamma\Bigr],\;n\geqslant 1.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In the next section we remind some special functions and useful operations we need in the sequel.
Section 3 is devoted to the fundamental characterization of the classical 2-orthogonal polynomials via the matrix differential equation (Theorem 3.1).
In the fourth section, we first expose the outline of the procedure used to look for integral representation for the pair of functionals

and

via weight functions denoted

and

In that regard, depending on the case and under certain conditions, we show that the obtained 2-OPS are associated with weights involving certain special functions as Airy function, Gauss-Airy function, modified Bessel function or Tricomi function.
In three subcases, however, we obtain that the resulting polynomials are 2-orthogonal with respect to a pair of weights
(w_{0},w_{1})

is given as a combination of

2.2
Airy functions

The Airy functions of the first and second kind denoted

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}-zy=0.

The original Airy function of the first kind with a real argument is defined by the Airy integral

\displaystyle{\rm Ai}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\!\cos\Big(xt+\frac{t^{3}}{3}\Big)dt,\;x\geqslant 0.

Another function called Gauss-Airy function and presented in

Incomplete gamma functions

The incomplete gamma function

and its complementary incomplete gamma function
\Gamma(a,x)
are usually defined via the integrals
[
5
, Sec. 8]

\displaystyle\gamma(a,x)=\int^{x}_{0}\!\!t^{a-1}e^{-t}dt\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma(a,x)=\int^{+\infty}_{x}\!\!t^{a-1}e^{-t}dt\ ,\;x\geqslant 0\;,\;a>0.

Clearly,
\gamma(a,0)=0
and

\displaystyle\gamma(a,x)+\Gamma(a,x)=\Gamma(a).

For

\displaystyle\gamma(a,cx)=c^{a}\int_{0}^{x}\!\!t^{a-1}e^{-ct}dt\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma(a,cx)=c^{a}\int^{+\infty}_{x}\!\!t^{a-1}e^{-ct}dt.

(2.27)

Likewise, we have an analogous formula to (

)

\displaystyle\gamma(a,cx)+\Gamma(a,cx)=\Gamma(a).

(2.28)

Finally, the asymptotic series representation for the function
{\Gamma}(a,x)
as

is

\displaystyle{\Gamma}(a,x)\sim\Gamma(a)x^{a-1}e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{\Gamma(a-k)\ x^{k}},\quad a>0.

(2.29)

2.5
Error functions

The error function
{\rm erf}(x)
and its complementary error function denoted
{\rm erfc}(x)
are, respectively, given by the indefinite integrals
[

, Sec. 7]

\displaystyle{\rm erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}\!\!e^{-t^{2}}dt\ \;\hbox{and}\ \;{\rm erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{x}^{+\infty}\!\!e^{-t^{2}}dt,\;{\rm for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.30)

Since both functions are interrelated via

{\rm erfc}(x)

{\rm erf}(x)

For example, we see that
{\rm erf}(0)=0

{\rm erfc}(0)=1

For large positive argument,

\displaystyle{\rm erfc}(x)\sim\frac{e^{-x^{2}}}{x\sqrt{\pi}},\quad\hbox{as}\ x\to+\infty.

For any linear functional

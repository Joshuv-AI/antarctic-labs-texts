Introduction

Let
H
be the Hilbert transform, corresponding to the Fourier multiplier
i\operatorname{sgn}\xi
, so that the operator
D=-\partial_{x}H
is the positive operator with Fourier multiplier
|\xi|
. (For further elaboration on notational conventions, see §

\text{(BO)}\qquad\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}(-H\partial_{x}u-\frac{1}{2}u^{2})

with
u
real-valued, on
\mathbb{R}
. The equation (BO) is a model for 1D long internal waves in a stratified fluid, introduced by Benjamin
[
2

and Ono
[
36

\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}(\pm H\partial_{x}u\pm\frac{1}{2}u^{2})
, and hence we do not have a distinction between “focusing" or “defocusing" problems for this equation. Moreover, (BO) also satisfies translational invariance in space and has the scaling invariance, for
\lambda>0

u\text{ solves (BO)}\implies u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t)\text{ solves (BO)}

(BO) is completely integrable, so it enjoys infinitely many conserved quantities
[
4
]
, the first three of which are

M_{0}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int u^{2}\,,\quad E_{0}(u)=-\frac{1}{2}\int uHu_{x}-\frac{1}{6}\int u^{3},

E_{1}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int u^{2}_{x}+\frac{3}{8}\int u^{2}Hu_{x}-\frac{1}{16}\int u^{4}

Tao
[
44
]
proved local well-posedness of (BO) in
H_{x}^{1}

[

[

[

In this paper, our interest is in soliton dynamics. Amick & Toland
[
1
]
and Frank & Lenzmann
[
11
]
showed that there is a unique (up to translations) nontrivial
L^{\infty}
solution to

Q-HQ^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{2}=0

given by

Q(y)=\frac{4}{1+y^{2}}

For any
c>0
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, taking
Q_{a,c}(x)=cQ(c(x-a))
we have

(1.1)

cQ_{a,c}-HQ_{a,c}^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{2}_{a,c}=0

Then

u(x,t)=Q_{ct,c}(x)=cQ(c(x-ct))

solves (BO) and we call it the
single soliton
solution to distinguish it from the exact multi-soliton solutions
[
6
]
arising from the completely integrable structure. The (BO) soliton is only decaying at infinity at power rate unlike for the famous Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) model, where the soliton enjoys exponential decay.

From the physical standpoint, it is of interest to consider the effects of perturbations of the equation on the dynamics of solitons. For example, Matsuno
[
30
,
31
]
derived a higher-order BO equation

\partial_{t}u+4uu_{x}+Hu_{xx}=\epsilon f(u,u_{x},u_{xx},u_{xxx})

where the right side is a specific nonlinear function, and carried out a heuristic multiscale analysis of the effect of this perturbation on the dynamics of multisolitons. This equation considered in
[
30
,
31
]
describes the unidirectional motion of interfacial waves in a two-layer fluid system, and provides motivation to consider the mathematical theory of Hamiltonian perturbations of (BO), for which we consider the following model case.

\text{(pBO)}\qquad\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}(-H\partial_{x}u+Vu-\frac{1}{2}u^{2})

with
slowly varying
potential

(1.2)

V(x)=W(hx)\,,\qquad W\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\text{ and }0<h\ll 1

The well-posedness of (pBO) in
H^{1}
can be proved by adapting the gauge-transform method of Tao
[
44
]
. The Hamiltonian has been perturbed to

E(u)=E_{0}(u)+\frac{1}{2}\int Vu^{2}

(pBO) is of the form
\partial_{t}u=JE^{\prime}(u)
, where
J=\partial_{x}
.

Our main result (Theorem
1.1

h

\frac{1}{2}\leq\bar{C}(s)\leq 2

Let

so that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\bar{c}}=h\bar{c}W^{\prime}(h\bar{a})\\
&\dot{\bar{a}}=\bar{c}-W(h\bar{a})\end{aligned}\right.

with initial condition
(\bar{a}(0),\bar{c}(0))=(0,1)
. Now let us introduce the
exact trajectory

(\hat{A}(s),\hat{C}(s))

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\hat{C}}=\hat{C}W^{\prime}(\hat{A})+\frac{1}{2}\hat{C}^{-1}h^{2}W^{\prime\prime\prime}(\hat{A})\\
&\dot{\hat{A}}=\hat{C}-W(\hat{A})+\frac{1}{2}\hat{C}^{-2}h^{2}W^{\prime\prime}(\hat{A})\end{aligned}\right.

with initial condition
(\hat{A}(0),\hat{C}(0))=(0,1)
, which is an
h

\hat{a}(t)=h^{-1}\hat{A}(ht)\,,\qquad\hat{c}(t)=\hat{C}(ht)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{\hat{c}}=h\hat{c}W^{\prime}(h\hat{a})+\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}^{-1}h^{3}W^{\prime\prime\prime}(h\hat{a})\\
&\dot{\hat{a}}=\hat{c}-W(h\hat{a})+\frac{1}{2}\hat{c}^{-2}h^{2}W^{\prime\prime}(h\hat{a})\end{aligned}\right.

By elementary ODE perturbation (Lemma

|\hat{A}-\bar{A}|\lesssim h^{2}e^{\mu s}\,,\qquad|\hat{C}-\bar{C}|\lesssim h^{2}e^{\mu s}

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(exact effective dynamics for (pBO))
.

Given a potential
W\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
(as in (
1.2

\kappa\geq 1
,

\|u_{0}(x)-Q_{0,1}(x)\|_{H_{x}^{1/2}}\leq h^{3/2}

\|u(x,t)-Q_{\hat{a}(t),\hat{c}(t)}(x)\|_{H_{x}^{1/2}}\leq\kappa h^{3/2}e^{\mu ht}

0\leq t\leq T_{0}=h^{-1}\min(\frac{1}{4}\mu^{-1}\ln h^{-1},S_{0})

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies upon an adaptation of a
local virial
estimate in Kenig & Martel
[
20
]
. Let

\mathcal{L}=-H\partial_{y}+1-Q

be the linearized operator and we consider
v
solving

(1.10)

\partial_{t}v=\mathbb{P}v+\partial_{y}\mathcal{L}v+\partial_{y}f

with

(1.11)

\mathbb{P}v:=\frac{\langle v,\mathcal{L}\partial_{y}^{2}Q\rangle}{\|\partial_{y}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\partial_{y}Q.

where
f=f(y,t)
is a forcing function. We will assume that
v
satisfies the
nonsymplectic
orthogonality conditions

(1.12)

\langle v,Q\rangle=0\,,\qquad\langle v,Q^{\prime}\rangle=0

For any
\gamma>0
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(1.13)

g_{\gamma,y_{0}}(y)=\gamma^{-1}\arctan(\gamma(y-y_{0}))

so that

g_{\gamma,y_{0}}^{\prime}(y)=\frac{1}{1+\gamma^{2}(y-y_{0})^{2}}=\langle\gamma(y-y_{0})\rangle^{-2}

is a spatial localization factor with scale
\gamma>0
and center
y_{0}
.

Define the operator

\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}:=1+\gamma\partial_{y}

and let
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}
be the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol
(1+i\gamma\xi)^{-1}
. The operator
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}
will be used in the analysis to conjugate our equation to a dual equation. We remark that if
f
is a real-valued function, then
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}f
is also real-valued. Furthermore, we remark that
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{L}
is a pseudodifferential operator of order
0
.

Theorem 1.2

(local virial estimate for linearized Benjamin-Ono)
.

There exists
0<\gamma_{0}\ll 1
such that for all
0<\gamma\leq\gamma_{0}
, for any time length
T>0
, for any spatial center
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and for any solution
v
to (
1.10
) satisfying (
1.12
), we have

(1.14)

\|\langle D_{y}\rangle^{1/2}((g_{\gamma,y_{0}}^{\prime})^{1/2}v)\|_{L_{[0,T]}^{2}L_{y}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{\gamma}\|v\|_{L_{[0,T]}^{\infty}L_{y}^{2}}^{2}+G_{\gamma}(f,v)

where

(1.15)

G_{\gamma}(f,v)=\int_{0}^{T}\int g_{\gamma,y_{0}}\,v\,\partial_{y}f\,dydt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{y}g_{\gamma,0}(\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{L}v)(\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{L}\partial_{y}f)\,dy\,dt

Importantly, the implicit constant in (
1.14
) is independent of
T
,
y_{0}
.

Notice that this is a local smoothing type estimate, where
\frac{1}{2}
derivative is gained after localizing in space and averaging in time. Such an estimate can be proved for the linearization around
0
using the Fourier representation of the propagator and Plancherel’s theorem.

A weaker version of this estimate (estimate (3.7) in
[

]
) is proved in Kenig & Martel
[

as part of their Theorem 3 or ‘linear Liouville’ result (starting on p. 923). We have isolated and refined this estimate, and to do so we use still essentially follow their method of passing to a dual problem and implementing a positive commutator argument. But to obtain our version of the estimate, we use a slightly different transformation and corresponding dual problem, prove and employ additional commutator estimates, avoid using a uniform spatial decay hypothesis (as in (3.6) of
[
20
]
), and also invoke an extra spectral estimate.

proceeds as follows. Setting

The parameter

In Proposition

The method of deriving and applying a local virial estimate for the linearized equation in the setting of nonlinear dispersive PDE to achieve rigidity results on soliton dynamics was introduced about 20 years ago as a “nonlinear Liouville theorem” in the case of the

-critical gKdV by Martel & Merle
[
28
]
. The method of converting from
v
to a dual problem for
\psi
was introduced by Martel
[
27
]
in the gKdV context, where the transformation
\psi=\mathcal{L}v
is used. The addition of the regularization operator was used by Kenig & Martel
[
20
]
in their treatment of asymptotic stability for the Benjamin-Ono equation. They used
\psi=(1-\delta\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{L}v
while we use
\psi=\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{L}v
, since the explicit kernel of the operator
\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}
facilitates the proof of some commutator estimates that we use to convert the estimate for
\psi
back to an estimate for
v
. This method of using a regularized transformation was also applied by Farah, Holmer, Roudenko, & Yang
[
10
]
in the study of blow-up of the

critical 2D Zakharov-Kuznetsov (ZK) equation, and a different method of handling regularity issues was recently developed in the context of asymptotic stability for solitary waves of the 3D ZK equation by the same authors in
[
9
]
.

2.

We fix a convention for the Fourier transform (in dimension

\hat{f}(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\xi}f(x)\,dx\,,\qquad\check{g}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int e^{ix\xi}g(\xi)\,d\xi

Hf(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{pv}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy=-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{pv}\frac{1}{x}*f

Hence

\widehat{Hf}(\xi)=i(\operatorname{sgn}\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)

The fractional derivative operator
D^{s}
is defined as
\widehat{D^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\hat{f}(\xi)
, and thus
-H\partial_{x}=D
.

The soliton profile
Q(x)
is defined explicitly by the formula

(2.1)

Q(x)=\frac{4}{1+x^{2}}

We have the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}\frac{1}{x-y}=-\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\frac{1}{y-x}+\frac{x}{1+x^{2}}\frac{1}{1+y^{2}}+\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}\frac{y}{1+y^{2}}

and hence (since first and third term integrate to zero)

(2.2)

HQ=-\frac{4}{\pi}\frac{x}{1+x^{2}}\int\frac{dy}{1+y^{2}}=\frac{-4x}{1+x^{2}}=-xQ

From this, and the easily confirmed identity (direct computation)

(2.3)

xQ^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}Q^{2}-2Q

we obtain that
Q
solves the soliton profile equation

(2.4)

Q-HQ^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}Q^{2}=0

Amick & Toland
[
1
]
showed that
Q(x)
is the unique solution to (
2.4
) such that
Q(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. For soliton dynamics problems, we introduce the modulation parameters of translation
a
and scale
c
and define

Q_{a,c}=cQ(c(x-a))

so that
Q=Q_{0,1}
. Note that from (
2.4
),
Q_{a,c}
solves the equation

(2.5)

cQ_{a,c}-HQ^{\prime}_{a,c}-\tfrac{1}{2}Q_{a,c}^{2}=0

The operator corresponding to linearization of (
2.5
) at
c=1
,
a=0
is

(2.6)

\mathcal{L}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{def}}}{{=}}I-H\partial_{x}-Q

We also define a rescaled version of
\mathcal{L}
:

\mathcal{L}_{c}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{def}}}{{=}}c-H\partial_{x}-cQ(cx),

whose properties are basically the same as for
\mathcal{L}
.

By differentiating (
2.5
) with respect to
a
, and evaluating at
c=1
,
a=0
, we obtain

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}Q^{\prime}=0

By differentiating (
2.5
) with respect to
c
, and evaluating at
c=1
,
a=0
, we obtain

(2.8)

\mathcal{L}(xQ)^{\prime}=-Q

From (
2.4
), we can deduce

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}Q=-\tfrac{1}{2}Q^{2}

By (
2.3
), it follows that (
2.9
) converts to

(2.10)

\mathcal{L}Q=-(xQ)^{\prime}-Q

We can use (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) to locate two important eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}
, although the complete spectral picture is provided by Proposition
3.1
below. From (
2.8
) and (
2.10
), for any constants
\alpha
and
\beta
,

\mathcal{L}(\alpha Q+\beta(xQ)^{\prime})=-(\alpha+\beta)Q-\alpha(xQ)^{\prime}

To find eigenfunctions, we find
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\implies\frac{5}{4}\alpha^{2}=(\beta+\frac{\alpha}{2})^{2}\implies\beta=\frac{\pm\sqrt{5}-1}{2}\alpha

Substituting, we obtain

\mathcal{L}e_{\pm}=\lambda_{\pm}e_{\pm}

where

(2.11)

e_{\pm}=Q+\frac{\mp\sqrt{5}-1}{2}(xQ)^{\prime}\,,\qquad\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{\pm\sqrt{5}-1}{2}

Note that both
Q
and
(xQ)^{\prime}
are even functions, so that
e_{\pm}
are even as well.

Lemma 3.2

(angle lemma)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is a self-adjoint operator with eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
and corresponding eigenspace spanned by
e_{1}
with
\|e_{1}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Let
P_{1}f=\langle f,e_{1}\rangle e_{1}
be the corresponding orthogonal projection. Assume that
(I-P_{1})\mathcal{L}
has spectrum bounded below by
\mu_{\perp}
, with
\mu_{\perp}>\mu_{1}
. Suppose that
f
is some other function such that
\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1
and
0\leq\beta\leq\pi
is defined by
\cos\beta=\langle f,e_{1}\rangle
. Then if
v
is satisfies
\langle v,f\rangle=0
, we have

\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle\geq(\mu_{\perp}-(\mu_{\perp}-\mu_{1})\sin^{2}\beta)\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Proof.

It suffices to assume that
\|v\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Decompose
v
and
f
into their orthogonal projection onto
e_{1}
and its orthocomplement:

v=(\cos\alpha)e_{1}+v_{\perp}\,,\qquad\|v_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}=\sin\alpha

f=(\cos\beta)e_{1}+f_{\perp}\,,\qquad\|f_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}=\sin\beta

for
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq\pi
.
Then

0=\langle v,f\rangle=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\langle v_{\perp},f_{\perp}\rangle

from which it follows that

|\cos\alpha\cos\beta|=|\langle v_{\perp},f_{\perp}\rangle|\leq\|v_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sin\alpha\sin\beta

Taking the square yields

\cos^{2}\alpha(1-\sin^{2}\beta)\leq(1-\cos^{2}\alpha)\sin^{2}\beta

and from this it follows that
|\cos\alpha|\leq\sin\beta
. Now

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}v,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\cos^{2}\alpha+\langle\mathcal{L}v_{\perp},v_{\perp}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}\cos^{2}\alpha+\mu_{\perp}\sin^{2}\alpha

\displaystyle=\mu_{\perp}-(\mu_{\perp}-\mu_{1})\cos^{2}\alpha

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{\perp}-(\mu_{\perp}-\mu_{1})\sin^{2}\beta

∎

We will prove spectral estimates for
v
satisfying the orthogonality conditions (
1.12
). For the proof of Theorem
1.2
given in §

(by (

where we have used

(which is (
2.8

1.12

, and

(3.1)

\langle z,Q^{\prime}\rangle=0\,,\qquad\langle z,(yQ)^{\prime}\rangle=0

As mentioned, for the proof of Proposition
5.2

\psi=\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{L}v
, for
\gamma>0
small. Since

\displaystyle=\langle z,[(\mathcal{D}^{*}_{\gamma})^{-1}-I]Q^{\prime}\rangle=\gamma\langle z,(\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{*})^{-1}Q^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\gamma\langle\psi,Q^{\prime\prime}\rangle

and hence

Proposition 3.3
.

(1)

For

(1)

4.

We state and prove as necessary a few commutator estimates that will be needed in the computations in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
0<\gamma\leq 1
, all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\langle y\rangle^{-\alpha}
is
L^{2}\to L^{2}
bounded with operator norm independent of
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
k(y)=e^{-y}\mathbf{1}_{y>0}
. Then
\hat{k}(\xi)=(1+i\xi)^{-1}
. Then the kernel of the operator
\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}^{-1}\langle y\rangle^{-\alpha}
is

K(y,y^{\prime})=\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\langle y^{\prime}\rangle^{-\alpha}\gamma^{-1}k(\gamma^{-1}(y-y^{\prime}))

By duality, it suffices to restrict to
\alpha\geq 0
. We apply Schur’s test, as follows. Using that
\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\lesssim\langle y-y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}+\langle y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}
,

|K(y,y^{\prime})|\lesssim(\langle y-y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}\langle y^{\prime}\rangle^{-\alpha}+1)\gamma^{-1}k(\gamma^{-1}(y-y^{\prime}))

Using that
\langle y-y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}\leq\langle\gamma^{-1}(y-y^{\prime})\rangle^{\alpha}
and
\langle y^{\prime}\rangle^{-\alpha}\leq 1
for the first term,

|K(y,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\langle\gamma^{-1}(y-y^{\prime})\rangle^{\alpha}\gamma^{-1}k(\gamma^{-1}(y-y^{\prime})\rangle

From this it follows that

\int_{y^{\prime}}|K(y,y^{\prime})|\,dy^{\prime}\lesssim\int_{z}\langle z\rangle^{\alpha}|k(z)|\,dz<\infty

and similarly for
\int_{y}|K(y,y^{\prime})|\,dy
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(fractional Leibniz rule)
.

Suppose
0<\alpha<1
,
0\leq\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha
with
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha
, and
1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
, then

\|D^{\alpha}(fh)-fD^{\alpha}h-hD^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|D^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|D^{\alpha_{2}}h\|_{L^{p_{2}}}

Proof.

See, for example,
[
22
, Theorem A.8]
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For each
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
,

(4.1)

\|D^{1/2}(fh)-fD^{1/2}h\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\epsilon}\|\partial_{y}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\epsilon}\|\langle D\rangle^{1/2}h\|_{L^{2}}

Moreover,

(4.2)

\|D^{1/2}(fh)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\partial_{y}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\epsilon}\|\partial_{y}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\epsilon})\|\langle D\rangle^{1/2}h\|_{L^{2}}

The implicit constant diverges as
\epsilon\searrow 0
or as
\epsilon\nearrow\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

By applying Lemma
4.2
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
,
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
,
\alpha_{2}=0
,
p=2
,
p_{1}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}
,
p_{2}=\frac{2}{1-2\epsilon}
, and applying the Hölder inequality on the term

, we obtain

\|D^{1/2}(fh)-fD^{1/2}h\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|D^{1/2}f\|_{L^{1/\epsilon}}\|h\|_{L^{2/(1-2\epsilon)}}

Since Lemma
4.2
is not available for

(where

) or

(where

), the above estimate has a constant that diverges as

or
\epsilon\nearrow\frac{1}{2}
. By Sobolev embedding

\|D^{1/2}(fh)-fD^{1/2}h\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\|D^{1-\epsilon}f\|_{L^{2}}\|D^{\epsilon}h\|_{L^{2}}

Since Sobolev embedding for the second term fails for
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}
, the above estimate has a constant that diverges as
\epsilon\nearrow\frac{1}{2}
.
Gagliardo-Nirenberg (Cauchy-Schwarz on the Fourier side) then yields (
4.1
), and (
4.2
) follows from (

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\partial_{y}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For
0<\gamma\leq 1

\langle\gamma y\rangle\Big(D\langle\gamma y\rangle^{-1}-\langle\gamma y\rangle^{-1}D\Big)

.

First, we claim that it suffices to show that

D(1+i\gamma y)^{-1}-(1+i\gamma y)^{-1}D

\gamma y\Big(D(1+i\gamma y)^{-1}-(1+i\gamma y)^{-1}D\Big)

\lesssim\gamma
. To show this, first note that (
4.5
) and (

) combined give that

\langle\gamma y\rangle\Big(D(1+i\gamma y)^{-1}-(1+i\gamma y)^{-1}D\Big)

. Rewrite (

\frac{\langle\gamma y\rangle}{1+i\gamma y}\Big(\frac{1+i\gamma y}{\langle\gamma y\rangle}D-D\frac{1+i\gamma y}{\langle\gamma y\rangle}\Big)

H^{1/4}\to L^{2}
bounded with operator norm
\lesssim\gamma
. Since the operator of multiplication by

L^{2}\to L^{2}
unitary, it suffices to show

4.4

We prove (

) and (

|\xi|T_{\gamma}-T_{\gamma}|\xi|

\lesssim\gamma

\gamma\partial_{\xi}[k_{\gamma}(\xi-\eta)(|\xi|-|\eta|)]

The kernel corresponding to the operator (

Hence both operators are

Proof.

The operator has the representation

I=(\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}H\partial_{y}\langle y\rangle^{-\alpha}f)(y)=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\int_{|y^{\prime}|>\epsilon}\frac{1}{y^{\prime}}\partial_{y^{\prime}}\left[\frac{\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}}{\langle y-y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}}f(y-y^{\prime})\right]\,dy^{\prime}

Let
\chi(y^{\prime})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be an even nonnegative smooth compactly supported function with
\chi(y^{\prime})=1
on
|y^{\prime}|\leq 1
. Then we break
I=I_{-}+I_{+}
into an inner piece
I_{-}
and outer piece
I_{+}
by inserting
\chi(y^{\prime})
and
1-\chi(y^{\prime})
respectively. For the inner piece
I_{-}
, we distribute
\partial_{y^{\prime}}
to obtain

I_{-}=-\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\int_{|y^{\prime}|>\epsilon}\frac{\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\chi(y^{\prime})}{y^{\prime}}g(y-y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}

where

g(z)=\partial_{z}[\langle z\rangle^{-\alpha}f(z)]

By the oddness of the inner kernel, we can reexpress as

I_{-}=\int_{y^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\chi(y^{\prime})}{y^{\prime}}[g(y+y^{\prime})-g(y-y^{\prime})]\,dy^{\prime}

By the mean-value theorem, for each
y
there exists
z_{0}=z_{0}(y^{\prime})
such that
-y^{\prime}<z_{0}<y^{\prime}
with

g(y+y^{\prime})-g(y-y^{\prime})=2y^{\prime}g^{\prime}(y+z_{0}(y^{\prime}))

Substituting,

I_{-}=2\int_{y^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}\chi(y^{\prime})g^{\prime}(y+z_{0}(y^{\prime}))\,dy^{\prime}

Note that

|g^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\langle z\rangle^{-\alpha}[\langle z\rangle^{-2}|f(z)|+\langle z\rangle^{-1}|f^{\prime}(z)|+|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|]

Since
y^{\prime}
is confined to the compact support of
\chi
, it follows that
\langle y+z_{0}(y^{\prime})\rangle^{-\alpha}\sim\langle y\rangle^{-\alpha}
, and hence

|I_{-}|\lesssim\|\langle z\rangle^{-2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\langle z\rangle^{-1}f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}

For the outer piece
I_{+}
, we have, by integration by parts

I_{+}=\int_{y^{\prime}}\zeta(y^{\prime})\frac{\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}}{\langle y-y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}}f(y-y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}

with

\zeta(y^{\prime})=\left(\frac{\chi(y^{\prime})-1}{y^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{\chi^{\prime}(y^{\prime})}{y^{\prime}}+\frac{1-\chi(y^{\prime})}{(y^{\prime})^{2}}

which satisfies
|\zeta(y^{\prime})|\lesssim\langle y^{\prime}\rangle^{-2}
. Thus

|I_{+}|\lesssim\int_{y^{\prime}}K(y,y^{\prime})|f(y-y^{\prime})|\,dy^{\prime}

where

K(y,y^{\prime})=\frac{\langle y\rangle^{\alpha}}{\langle y^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\langle y-y^{\prime}\rangle^{\alpha}}

For
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
, we have
\int K(y,y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}\lesssim 1
, so

|I_{+}|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

For any functions
g
and
F
, and any
k\geq 0
,

(4.25)

\|H(gF)-gHF\|_{L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim_{k}\|g\|_{H^{k+1}}\|F\|_{H^{-k}}

Proof.

First, we observe that it suffices to assume that
\hat{F}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 4
. Let
\chi(\xi)
be a smooth function so that
\chi(\xi)=1
on
-1\leq|\xi|\leq 1
and
\operatorname{supp}\chi\subset(-2,2)
. Let
\widehat{P_{\textnormal{lo}}F}(\xi)=\chi(\xi/4)\hat{F}(\xi)
and
P_{\textnormal{hi}}=I-P_{\textnormal{lo}}
. Decompose

F=F_{\textnormal{lo}}+F_{\textnormal{hi}}\,,\qquad\text{where }F_{\textnormal{lo}}=P_{\textnormal{lo}}F\,,\quad F_{\textnormal{hi}}=P_{\textnormal{hi}}F

Then

H(gF)-gHF=H(gF_{\textnormal{lo}})-gHF_{\textnormal{lo}}+[H(gF_{\textnormal{hi}})-gHF_{\textnormal{hi}}]

We note that it is straightforward to obtain the bound (
4.25
) for the first two terms:

\|H(gF_{\textnormal{lo}})\|_{L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim\|gF_{\textnormal{lo}}\|_{L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{y}^{\infty}}\|F_{\textnormal{lo}}\|_{L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H_{y}^{1}}\|F\|_{H_{y}^{-k}}

and very similarly for
gHF_{\textnormal{lo}}
. Thus it suffices to prove the bound for
H(gF_{\textnormal{hi}})-gHF_{\textnormal{hi}}
, i.e. it suffices to assume that
\hat{F}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 4
.

Next, we observe that it suffices to assume that
\hat{g}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 1
. To see this,
redefine

\widehat{P_{\textnormal{lo}}g}(\xi)=\chi(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi)
and
P_{\textnormal{hi}}=I-P_{\textnormal{lo}}
(recall that in the argument above,
\chi(\xi)
was replaced with
\chi(\xi/4)
). Decompose

g=g_{\textnormal{lo}}+g_{\textnormal{hi}}\,,\qquad\text{where }g_{\textnormal{lo}}=P_{\textnormal{lo}}g\,,\quad g_{\textnormal{hi}}=P_{\textnormal{hi}}g

Then

H(gF)-gHF=[H(g_{\textnormal{lo}}F)-g_{\textnormal{lo}}HF]+[H(g_{\textnormal{hi}}F)-g_{\textnormal{hi}}HF]

where we can assume that
\hat{F}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 4
, and we know that
\hat{g}_{\textnormal{lo}}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\leq 2
. In the first term, decompose
F=F_{-}+F_{+}

where
F_{-}
is the projection of
F
onto negative frequencies, and
F_{+}
is the projection of
F
onto positive frequencies. Then

H(gF)-gHF=[H(g_{\textnormal{lo}}F_{-})-g_{\textnormal{lo}}HF_{-}]+[H(g_{\textnormal{lo}}F_{+})-g_{\textnormal{lo}}HF_{+}]+[H(g_{\textnormal{hi}}F)-g_{\textnormal{hi}}HF]

Noting that
HF_{-}=F_{-}
, and moreover due to the frequency supports,
H(g_{\textnormal{lo}}F_{-})=g_{\textnormal{lo}}F_{-}
, the first term is zero. Likewise, the second term is zero, leaving only to estimate
H(g_{\textnormal{hi}}F)-g_{\textnormal{hi}}HF
. Thus we have shown that it suffices to assume that
\hat{g}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 1
.

Now we complete the proof assuming that
\hat{g}(\xi)
is supported
|\xi|\geq 1
and
\hat{F}(\xi)
is supported in
|\xi|\geq 1
.
Apply a Littlewood-Paley decomposition

g=\sum_{N}P_{N}g\,,\qquad F=\sum_{M}P_{M}F

where the sums are taken over dyads
|N|\geq 1
and
|M|\geq 1
, respectively. Then

(4.26)

H(gF)-gHF=\sum_{M,N}[H(P_{N}g\,P_{M}F)-P_{N}g\,HP_{M}F]

Split the set of all
(M,N)
into two subclasses. The first subclass
S
consists of those
(M,N)
for which
|N|\geq|M|/4
, and the second subclass
S^{c}
consists of those
(M,N)
for which
|N|<|M|/4
. Note that for any
(M,N)\in S^{c}
, the sign of
M
is the same as the sign of
M+N
, and thus

H(P_{N}g\,P_{M}F)-P_{N}g\,HP_{M}F=0

since either
H
reduces to
+I
in both terms, or
H
reduces to
-I
in both terms.
It follows that the sum in (
4.26
) is only over
(M,N)\in S
. For
(M,N)\in S
we can transfer any number of derivatives from
F
to
g
:

\displaystyle\|H(P_{N}g\,P_{M}F)\|_{L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{N}g\,P_{M}F\|_{L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{k+\frac{1}{4}}\|P_{N}g\|_{L_{y}^{\infty}}M^{-k-\frac{1}{4}}\|P_{M}F\|_{L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{k+\frac{3}{4}}\|P_{N}g\|_{L_{y}^{2}}M^{-k-\frac{1}{4}}\|P_{M}F\|_{L_{y}^{2}}

by Bernstein

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{4}}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|g\|_{H^{k+1}}\|F\|_{H^{k}}

Similarly, for
(M,N)\in S

\|P_{N}g\,HP_{M}F\|_{L_{y}^{2}}\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{4}}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|g\|_{H^{k+1}}\|F\|_{H^{k}}

Thus, returning to (
4.26
), we have

\displaystyle\|H(gF)-gHF\|_{L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{(M,N)\in S_{1}}\|H(P_{N}g\,P_{M}F)-P_{N}g\,HP_{M}F\|_{L_{y}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{M,N}N^{-\frac{1}{4}}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right)\|g\|_{H^{k+1}}\|F\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{k+1}}\|F\|_{H^{k}}

as claimed.

∎

The proof combines two key steps covered in Proposition

From the formula (

Now, from the fact that

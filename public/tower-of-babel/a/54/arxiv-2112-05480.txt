Abstract
We consider structured optimization problems defined in terms of the sum of a smooth and convex function and a proper, lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.), convex (typically nonsmooth) function in reflexive variable exponent Lebesgue spaces L p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( Ω ) {L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)} .Due to their intrinsic space-variant properties, such spaces can be naturally used as solution spaces and combined with space-variant functionals for the solution of ill-posed inverse problems. For this purpose, we propose and analyze two instances (primal and dual) of proximal gradient algorithms in L p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( Ω ) {L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)} , where the proximal step, rather than depending on the natural (non-separable) L p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( Ω ) {L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)} norm, is defined in terms of its modular function, which, thanks to its separability, allows for the efficient computation of algorithmic iterates. Convergence in function values is proved for both algorithms, with convergence rates depending on problem/space smoothness. To show the effectiveness of the proposed modeling, some numerical tests highlighting the flexibility of the space L p ⁡ ( ⋅ ) ​ ( Ω ) {L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)} are shown for exemplar deconvolution and mixed noise removal problems. Finally, a numerical verification of the convergence speed and computational costs of both algorithms in comparison with analogous ones defined in standard L p ​ ( Ω ) {L^{p}(\Omega)} spaces is presented.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in\mathbb{N},\ d\geq 1
be a Lebesgue measurable subset with positive measure and let
p(\cdot):\Omega\longrightarrow[1,+\infty]
be a Lebesgue measurable function. The variable exponent Lebesgue space
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
is defined in terms of the function
p(\cdot)
which, differently from conventional
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
spaces, acts as pointwise variable exponent, inducing a specific shift-variant norm. In this work, we will study a proximal-gradient (a.k.a. forward-backward) splitting algorithm to solve composite optimization problems in the form

\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\phi(x):=f(x)+g(x)

(P)

where
f:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a proper, convex, and Gateaux differentiable function while
g:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.), proper, convex, and possibly nonsmooth. Optimization problems of this kind often arise in the context of variational regularization of inverse problems such as, e.g., the minimization of Tikhonov-like functionals, where
f
denotes a data-fidelity term while
g
stands for the regularization or penalty term, see, e.g.
[
7
]
for a review.

Variable exponent Lebesgue spaces
{L^{p(\cdot)}}
are (non-Hilbertian) Banach spaces, endowed with space-variant geometrical properties which are useful in adaptive regularization techniques, see, e.g.,
[
3
,
5
,
17
,
21
]
. Under suitable choices of the exponent function
p(\cdot)
, having
{L^{p(\cdot)}}
as solution space allows for a better recovery of sparse solutions, avoiding oversmoothing reconstruction artifacts of usual regularization models formulated in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, a property that was already observed in previous works (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
) in the context of iterative regularization methods (with no penalty).

Forward-backward splitting algorithms are a common strategy used to solve problems in the form (
P
). Initially proposed in
[
8
,
10
,
20
]
in Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
for functions
f
with
\mathcal{L}
-Lipschitz gradient, they are defined by the following updating rule for
k\geq 0

x^{k+1}=\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{2}\|x-(x^{k}-\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k}))\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\tau_{k}g(x),

where
\tau_{k}>0
is a suitably chosen sequence of step sizes. Such schemes enable one to exploit the differentiability of the smooth function
f
in a forward step defined in terms of the gradient of
f

x^{k}\mapsto x^{k}-\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})
which is decoupled from the backward step defined in terms of the proximal operator associated to
g
.

The generalization of this algorithm to a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
is not straightforward since the element
\nabla f(x^{k})\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
cannot be identified as an element of
\mathcal{X}
anymore due to the lack of Riesz isomorphism. As a consequence, the forward step cannot be performed directly on
x^{k}\in\mathcal{X}
. As a remedy, we need to introduce the so-called duality maps, which link primal and dual spaces and allow us to perform the forward gradient step either in the dual or in the primal space. The intrinsic nonlinearity of duality mappings, however, introduces new challenges in the definition of a backward step in terms of the proximal operator of
g
. In
[
4
,
18
,
19
]
, forward-backward algorithms have been proposed to solve minimization problems in the form (
P
) and defined in a reflexive, strictly convex and smooth Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, by means of suitably defined notions of duality mappings and proximal operators.

In particular, in
[
4
]
Bredies introduces the following iterative procedure for smooth functions
f
with
(\texttt{p}-1)
-Hölder continuous gradient
\nabla f
on bounded sets with
1<\texttt{p}\leq 2
with constant
K>0
and step-sizes chosen as
0<\bar{\tau}\leq\tau_{k}\leq\frac{\texttt{p}(1-\delta)}{K}
, with
0<\delta<1
:

x^{k+1}\in\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|x-x^{k}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}+\tau_{k}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x\right\rangle+\tau_{k}g(x)\,,

(1)

where
\left\langle u^{*},x\right\rangle=\left\langle x,u^{*}\right\rangle=u^{*}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the duality pairing, for
u^{*}\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Notice that, for
g\equiv 0
, the updating rule (
1
) can be also written as

0\in\partial\Big(\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|\cdot-x^{k}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}+\tau_{k}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),\cdot\right\rangle\Big)(x^{k+1}),

where, for a convex function
h:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\partial h(w)
denotes the subdifferential of
h
evaluated at
z
. By linearity, this leads to the following equivalent inclusions:

\displaystyle 0\in\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k+1}-x^{k})+\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x^{k+1}\in x^{k}-\tau_{k}\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}^{\texttt{p}^{\prime}}(\nabla f(x^{k})),

(2)

where
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{X}^{*}}
,
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}=\partial\Big(\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}\Big)
, is the
p
-duality map of the (primal) space
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X^{*}}}^{\texttt{p}^{\prime}}:{\mathcal{X}^{*}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}\,
is its inverse, that is, the
\texttt{p}^{\prime}
-duality map of the dual space
\mathcal{X^{*}}
, with
\texttt{p}^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate of
p

[
9
]
.
Since
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X^{*}}}^{\texttt{p}^{\prime}}(\nabla f(x^{k}))\in\mathcal{X}
, the latter inclusion shows that (
1
) reduces to a gradient descent step performed in the primal space
\mathcal{X}
. It can thus be seen a generalization of the
primal method
described in
[
26
]
to solve inverse problems
Ax=y\in\mathcal{Y}
, with
A:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
linear operator between two Banach spaces
\mathcal{X}\ni x
and
\mathcal{Y}
, via the minimization of the residual functional
f:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as
f(x)=\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|Ax-y\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\texttt{p}}
.
Such interpretation is not obvious from (
1
), where forward and backward steps are defined as one single minimization problem, so that they cannot be distinguished. Moreover, it shows the tight link between proximal-gradient schemes and regularization theory in Banach spaces
[
25
,
26
]
, notions that relate to the fields of optimization/convex analysis and functional analysis, respectively.

In
[
18
]
, the authors considered a different forward-backward splitting algorithm in Banach spaces, where the proximal step depends on Bregman distance as follows

x^{k+1}=\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{1}{\texttt{p}^{\prime}}\|x^{k}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}+\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}-\left\langle\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k}),x\right\rangle+\tau_{k}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x\right\rangle+\tau_{k}g(x),

(3)

with
f
as above and suitably chosen step-sizes (see
[
18
]
). In this second case, the algorithm requires the computation of the forward step in the dual space. Similarly as before, for
g\equiv 0
we notice that the updating rule (
3
) can now be written as

0\in\partial\Big(\frac{1}{\texttt{p}^{\prime}}\|x^{k}\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}+\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}-\left\langle\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k}),\cdot\right\rangle+\tau_{k}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),\cdot\right\rangle\Big)(x^{k+1}),

so that we recover a generalization of the so-called dual method
[
26
]

\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k+1})\in\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k})-\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})\qquad x^{k+1}=\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X^{*}}}^{\texttt{p}^{\prime}}\Big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k+1})\Big),

(4)

introduced in
[
25
]
for
f(x)=\frac{1}{\texttt{p}}\|Ax-y\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{\texttt{p}}
, where, differently from (
1
), the forward step is now computed in
\mathcal{X}^{*}
, since we have
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{p}}(x^{k}),\nabla f(x^{k})\in\mathcal{X^{*}}
.

Algorithms (
1
) and (
3
) have been proposed for reflexive, strictly convex and smooth Banach spaces, hence they can a priori be applied to solve (
P
) in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
whenever
1<p(t)<+\infty\text{ a.e. in }\Omega
. However, the definitions of
\|\cdot\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}
and of duality mappings make their use impracticable in real applications, since both are not separable, differently from what happens, e.g., in
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
spaces.
To overcome this issue, we propose here to replace the role of the norm naturally appearing in the definition of the proximal operator by the modular functions defined by

\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(t)}|x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x):=\int_{\Omega}|x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t\quad\forall\ x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

Due to their additive separability, they allow efficient computations of proximal points.

On these grounds, inspired by
[
4
]
we study an iterative proximal-gradient algorithm whose iteration for
k\geq 0
reads as

x^{k+1}=\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x-x^{k})+\tau_{k}\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x\rangle+\tau_{k}g(x)

for a proper choice of the sequence
\tau_{k}>0
.
Following
[
18
]
, we then propose another proximal-gradient algorithm whose update reads for
k\geq 0
as

x^{k+1}=\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x)-\langle\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}),x\rangle+\tau_{k}\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x\rangle+\tau_{k}g(x),

where
\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}
stands for the gradient of the modular
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}
.
For both algorithms, we prove convergence in function values and provide an interpretation as primal and dual algorithms, respectively. We next show how the intrinsic space-variant properties of the underlying space
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
correspond to the use of adaptive nonstandard thresholding functions which turn out to be a flexible tool in the solution of exemplar deconvolution and denoising problems, as we show in the numerical section. Finally, a numerical verification of the algorithmic convergence rates, in comparison with standard Hilbert and
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
-type algorithms is performed.

Preliminaries on variable exponent Lebesgue spaces

We introduce in this section some definitions, basic rationales and key concepts of
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
spaces. For a thorough survey of these spaces, see
[
13
,
11
]
.

2.1
Modular and Luxemburg norm

Let the set of all possible exponents be
\mathcal{P}(\Omega):=\{p(\cdot):\Omega\longrightarrow[1,+\infty]\ |\ p(\cdot)\text{ is Lebesgue measurable}\}
. Given
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, we denote the essential infimum and essential supremum of
p(\cdot)
by

p_{-}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\ inf}_{u\in\Omega}p(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{+}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\ sup}_{u\in\Omega}p(u).

For the sake of simplicity, we assume in the following
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<+\infty
(for the general case, see
[
13
]
). We will denote by
\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
the set of all Lebesgue measurable functions
x:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. In order to characterize spaces
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, it is first necessary to introduce the key concept of modular function.

Definition 2.1
.

Given an exponent
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
with
p_{+}<+\infty
, the function
\rho_{p(\cdot)}:\mathcal{F}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
defined by

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)=\int_{\Omega}|x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t

(5)

is called modular associated to the exponent function
p(\cdot)
. An alternative definition of modular function consists in considering
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}:\mathcal{F}(\Omega)\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
defined by

\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(t)}|x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t.

(6)

We will refer to (
5
) and (
6
) as modular functions, as needed. Notice that
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)
is the generalization of the
p
-power
\|x\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|x(t)|^{p}\mathrm{d}t
in
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
with constant exponent
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Similarly,
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x)
generalizes the quantity
\frac{1}{p}\|x\|_{p}^{p}
. The modular allows us to characterize the variable exponent space
{L^{p(\cdot)}}
and to define its norm, in the general framework of the Luxemburg norms of Orlicz spaces
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

The space
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
is the set of functions
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
such that

\rho_{p(\cdot)}\Big(\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)\leq 1,

for some
\lambda>0
. For any
x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, we define
\|\cdot\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{p(\cdot)}\Big(\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)\leq 1\right\}.

(7)

Theorem 2.1
.

[
13
]

The function
\|\cdot\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}
defined in (
7
) is a norm on
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
and the space
\Big({L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)},\|\cdot\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\Big)
is a Banach space.

It is interesting to point out that if
p(\cdot)\equiv p\in(1,+\infty)
, the classical notion of norm
\|x\|_{p}
in
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
can be retrieved. Indeed,
\rho_{p(\cdot)}\Big(\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)=\rho_{p}\Big(\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\rho_{p}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{p}}\|x\|_{p}^{p}

so that the infimum in (
7
) is equal to
\|x\|_{p}
. We remark that the computation of the
p
-radical of the integral is necessary to ensure the homogeneity property
\|\alpha x\|_{p}=|\alpha|\|x\|_{p}
, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. With a variable exponent, such computation is obviously not possible and in turn the one-dimensional (1D) minimization problem (
7
) has to be solved. However, the quantity (
7
) can be bounded by the
p_{-}
and
p_{+}
radicals of the modular. At a certain extent, one can thus think of the norm as
\tilde{p}
-radical (which depends on
x
) of the modular with
p_{-}\leq\tilde{p}\leq p_{+}
, as stated by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 3.2.5, Lemma 3.4.2]

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
with
p_{+}<+\infty
.

(i)

If
\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}>1
, then
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)^{1/p_{+}}\leq\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)^{1/p_{-}}.

(ii)

If
0<\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq 1
, then
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)^{1/p_{-}}\leq\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)^{1/p_{+}}.

(iii)

\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}<1
and
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)<1
are equivalent.

(iv)

\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}=1
and
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)=1
are equivalent.

If
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<+\infty
, then the following useful properties hold true.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.4.7, Theorem 3.4.9]

[
15
, Lemma 1]

Given
p(\cdot)
such that
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<+\infty
, then
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
is reflexive, uniformly convex and smooth.

2.2
Dual space, duality mappings, separability

In
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
spaces, the isometric isomorphism between the space
\Big({L^{p}(\Omega)}\Big)^{*}
and
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, with
p^{\prime}
being the Hölder conjugate of
p
, has a key role in devising regularization algorithms
[
26
]
. In
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
spaces, such isomorphism does not hold true, as we briefly summarize in this subsection. For a comprehensive description of these arguments, see
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional.
G
is bounded if

\sup_{u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)},\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq 1}|G(u)|<+\infty.

The dual space of
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
can thus be defined as the set

{{(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega))^{*}}}=\{G:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\ :\ G\ \text{is linear and bounded}\}\,,

which is a Banach space with the norm

\|G\|_{{(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega))^{*}}}:=\sup_{u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)},\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq 1}|G(u)|.

For
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<+\infty
, the Hölder conjugate of
p(\cdot)
is
a Lebesgue measurable function
p^{\prime}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
such that

\frac{1}{p(t)}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(t)}=1
a.e. in
\Omega
.
For any
z\in{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, it can be shown that there exists a unique
G\in{{(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega))^{*}}}
such that

G(u)=\int_{\Omega}z(t)u(t)\mathrm{d}t\qquad\forall\ u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

Thus, we can denote unambiguously
G
as
G_{z}
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
13
, Definition 2.7.1]

The associate space of
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, denoted by
{{\mathcal{A}(L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega))}}
, is the space of functions
z\in{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
such that

\|z\|^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}:=\sup_{u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)},\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq 1}\penalty\ \int_{\Omega}|z(t)||u(t)|\mathrm{d}t<+\infty.

(8)

It can be proved that
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}
is a norm on
{{\mathcal{A}(L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega))}}
. Note that since

\displaystyle\|G_{z}\|_{{(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega))^{*}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq 1}|G_{z}(u)|=\sup_{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}|z(t)||u(t)|\mathrm{d}t=\|z\|^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\,,

(9)

(
8
) is finite if and only if the linear operator
G_{z}
is bounded. Thus, there exists an isometric embedding between
{{\mathcal{A}(L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega))}}
and
{{(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega))^{*}}}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
13
, Corollary 3.2.14]

For all
z\in{{\mathcal{A}(L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega))}}
, there holds

\frac{1}{2}\|z\|_{{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}}}\leq\|z\|^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\leq 2\|z\|_{{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}}}\,,

and the bounds are optimal.

Remark 2.1
.

From the proposition above combined with (
9
), it follows that
{{(L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega))^{*}}}
and
{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
are not isometrically isomorphic.

A key role in the definition of iterative schemes in Banach space
\mathcal{X}
is played by duality maps, which associate an element of
\mathcal{X}
with a specific element of its dual
\mathcal{X}^{*}
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
9
]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
r>1
. Then the duality map
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{r}}
with gauge function
t\mapsto t^{r-1}
is the operator
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{r}}:\mathcal{X}\to 2^{\mathcal{X}^{*}}
such that

\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{r}}(x)=\big\{x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}^{*}\mid x^{*}(x)=\left<x^{*},x\right>=\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|x^{*}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{*}},\,\|x^{*}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{r-1}\big\}\quad\forall x\in X.

If
\mathcal{X}
is smooth, then the duality map is single valued, that is
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{r}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}^{*}
. In addition, if
\mathcal{X}
is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, by virtue of the Riesz theorem, the duality map reduces to the identity operator for
r=2
, that is
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\texttt{2}}(x)=x
, where the isometric isomorphism between
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{*}
has been implicitly considered.

In general, the following result gives a more intuitive interpretation of the important role played by duality mappings in the definition of minimization schemes.

Theorem 2.3

((Asplund)
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space, and let
r>1
. The
r
-duality map
\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{r}}
is the subdifferential of the convex functional

\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{\texttt{r}}=\partial h=\partial\left(\frac{1}{r}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{r}\right)\,.

Thanks to this Theorem, the duality maps are thus monotone operators.

In
[

]

\frac{1}{r}\|\cdot\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{r}

, hence its Gateaux derivative, the
r
-duality map
\mathbf{J}_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{r}

[
14
,
15
]

Proposition 2.2
.

For each

and for any

(10)

If

\mathbf{J}_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{r}

\langle\mathbf{J}_{p}^{r}(x),u\rangle=\|x\|_{p}^{r-p}\int_{\Omega}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}\big(x(t)\big)|x(t)|^{p-1}u(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
x,u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, with
p_{+}<+\infty
, the functions
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(\cdot)
and
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(\cdot)
are Gateaux differentiable, with derivatives

\langle\mathbf{J}_{\rho_{p(\cdot)}}(x),u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}p(t)\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x(t))|x(t)|^{p(t)-1}u(t)\mathrm{d}t

(11)

\langle\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x),u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x(t))|x(t)|^{p(t)-1}u(t)\mathrm{d}t.

(12)

Proof.

Let
x,u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
. The Gateaux derivative of
\rho_{p(\cdot)}
at
x
along direction
u
is given by

\lim_{t\to 0}\penalty\ \frac{\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x+tu)-\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x)}{t}=\Big[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x+tu)\Big]_{t=0}.

First, note that

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x+tu)=\int_{\Omega}|x(s)+tu(s)|^{p(s)}\mathrm{d}s<+\infty.

Indeed
x,u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, then
x+tu\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
and consequently
\|x+tu\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}<+\infty
by definition of
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
space. Since
p_{+}<+\infty
and by Lemma
2.1
,
\|x+tu\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}<+\infty
implies
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x+tu)<+\infty
. We can thus compute the partial derivative of
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x+tu)
with respect to
t
by “differentiating under the integral sign.” To do so, we first need to verify the regularity of the integrand function. Let
f:\Omega\times(-1,1)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by

f(s,t):=|x(s)+tu(s)|^{p(s)},\quad s\in\Omega,\quad t\in(-1,1).

By direct computations, we obtain

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(s,t)=p(s)|x(s)+tu(s)|^{p(s)-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}\Big(x(s)+tu(s)\Big)u(s)

and, since
|t|<1
,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(s,t)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq p_{+}|x(s)+tu(s)|^{p(s)-1}|u(s)|\leq p_{+}|x(s)+tu(s)|^{p(s)-1}(|u(s)|+|x(s)|)

\displaystyle\leq p_{+}(|u(s)|+|x(s)|)^{p(s)}=:g(s),

with
g(s)
integrable. We conclude the proof thanks to the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We stress that although
\mathbf{J}_{\rho_{p(\cdot)}}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}
are not duality mappings, we adopt nonetheless the same notation for consistency.

It is interesting to observe the following property of the modular function and its gradient. By direct computations, it follows that for any
x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
there holds

\langle\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x),x\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x(t))\,|x(t)|^{p(t)-1}x(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x).

(13)

This is the analogue of a general property of duality mappings in Banach spaces. Indeed, by Definition
2.5
, if
\mathcal{X}
is a smooth Banach space, then for any
r>1
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
there holds
\langle\mathbf{J}_{\mathcal{X}}^{r}(x),x\rangle=\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|x^{*}\|_{\mathcal{X}^{*}}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{r-1}=\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{r}.

As better explained in Section
5
, it is handy having functionals and operators defined in Banach spaces that are
separable
, that is, their global computation can be decomposed into the sum of low-dimensional functionals. To make this property more precise, we consider the following definition of domain additive separability.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a functional Banach space on
\Omega
. An operator
S:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{*}
or a functional
S:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is domain additively separable, if, for any finite family of Lebesgue measurable subsets
\Big(\Omega_{i}\Big)_{i=1}^{n}
of
\Omega
such that
\mathring{\Omega}_{i}\cap\mathring{\Omega}_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\not=j
, and
\Omega=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\Omega_{i}}
, there holds

S(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}S\left(\chi_{i}\,x\right)

for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, where
\chi_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
is the characteristic function of
\Omega_{i}
, that is
\chi_{i}(t)=1
for
t\in\Omega_{i}
, and

\chi_{i}(t)=0
for
t\not\in\Omega_{i}
.

In the following, domain additive separability will often be referred to simply as separability. It is quite evident that in conventional
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
spaces, the norm functional
\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}
, as well as the operator
\mathbf{J}_{p}^{p}(\cdot)
, are domain additively separable, since, for any suitable family of subsets
\Big(\Omega_{i}\Big)_{i=1}^{n}
, there holds
\|x\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\chi_{i}x\|_{p}^{p}
and
\langle\mathbf{J}_{p}^{p}(x),u\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\mathbf{J}_{p}^{p}(\chi_{i}x),u\rangle=\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{J}_{p}^{p}(\chi_{i}\,x),u\rangle
.
On the contrary, norms and duality maps in variable exponent spaces are not separable.

Lemma 2.2
.

The norm and the duality mapping in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
are not domain additively separable in the sense of Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

It is quite evident that the Luxemburg norm (
7
) requires the solution of a 1D minimization problem on the entire domain
\Omega
, which, in general, cannot be divided into the solutions on single sets of the partition, that is,

\|x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\not=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\chi_{i}\,x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\,
.
As the duality mapping is concerned, the two norms in the denominators of
\mathbf{J}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
(
10
) show that its computation cannot be decomposed into the computation of
n
integrals involving only the restriction of the function
x
onto single sets of the partition, or, in other words,
\mathbf{J}_{{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}^{r}(x)\not=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{J}_{{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}^{r}(\chi_{i}\,x)\,
.
∎

The modular functions introduced in Definition
5
as well as their gradients turn out instead to satisfy the separability property.

Lemma 2.3
.

The modular functions in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
and their gradients are domain additively separable, in the sense of Definition
2.6
.

Proof.

We consider the modular function
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(t)}|x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t
defined in (
6
) (for (
5
), the proof is similar). By direct computation, by the linearity property of the integral w.r.t. the integration domain, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\frac{1}{p(t)}|x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(t)}|\chi_{i}(t)x(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(\chi_{i}x).

Similarly, for
\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}
, we can write

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x),u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x(t))|x(t)|^{p(t)-1}u(t)\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x(t))|x(t)|^{p(t)-1}u(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x(t))|\chi_{i}(t)\,x(t)|^{p(t)-1}u(t)\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\chi_{i}\,x),u\rangle=\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\chi_{i}\,x),u\rangle

which concludes the proof.
∎

3
A modular-proximal gradient algorithm

In this section, we propose and analyze an iterative procedure to solve the minimization problem (
P
).
We set
\bar{\phi}:=\inf_{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\phi(x)
, and define Sol(P) as
\text{Sol(P)}:=\{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}:\phi(x)=\bar{\phi}\}\neq\emptyset
. We consider the following two assumptions.

Assumption 3.1
.

The exponent function
p(\cdot)
is such that
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 2
.

Assumption 3.2
.

\nabla f:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}^{*}
is
(\textit{{p}}-1)
Hölder-continuous with exponent
p_{+}\leq\textit{{p}}\leq 2
and constant
K>0
, i.e.:

\|\nabla f(u)-\nabla f(v)\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}\leq K\|u-v\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{\textit{{p}}-1}\qquad\forall\ u,v\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

The first modular-proximal gradient algorithm we propose is reported in Algorithm
1
, as pseudocode.

Algorithm 1

Modular-proximal gradient algorithm in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
spaces

Parameters:

\rho\in(0,1)
,
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k}
s.t.

0<\bar{\tau}\leq\tau_{k}\leq\frac{\textit{{p}}(1-\delta)}{K}\hskip 18.49988pt\text{with}\ 0<\delta<1.

(14)

Initialization:
Start with
x^{0}\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
.

FOR
k=0,1,\ldots
REPEAT

FOR
i=0,1,\ldots
REPEAT

1.

Set
\tau_{k}=\rho^{i}\tau_{k}
.

2.

Compute the next iterate as:

x^{k+1}=\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(x-x^{k})+\tau_{k}\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x\rangle+\tau_{k}g(x).

(15)

UNTIL

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})<1

UNTIL
convergence

The inner loop is needed to select at each
k
-th iteration a sufficiently small step-size
\tau_{k}
such that
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})<1
, which is required in the following convergence analysis as we will see in the proofs of Proposition
20
and Lemma
3.2
. It should be thought as a backtracking-like procedure affecting more the first algorithmic iterations where the quantity
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})
is likely to be large.

We start our analysis discussing the well-definition of the step (
15
), e.g. the existence and uniqueness of the minimizer of the functional defined by (
15
).

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
,
v^{*}\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}^{*}
and
\tau>0
, the problem

\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(u-x)+\tau\langle v^{*},u\rangle+\tau g(u)

(16)

has a unique solution.

Proof.

Let
\tau>0
. Note that when
\|u-x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}>1
, by Lemma
2.1
(
i
) there holds:

\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(u-x)\geq\frac{1}{p_{+}}\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u-x)\geq\frac{1}{p_{+}}\|u-x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{p_{-}}.

Let now
\bar{x}\in\text{Sol(P)}
. The optimality condition reads:
0\in\nabla f(\bar{x})+\partial g(\bar{x})
or, equivalently,
\bar{\omega}:=-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in\partial g(\bar{x})
. By definition of subdifferential, there holds
g(u)\geq g(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{\omega},u-\bar{x}\rangle=g(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{\omega},u\rangle-\langle\bar{\omega},\bar{x}\rangle
for all
u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
. By combining such inequality with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}(u-x)+\tau\langle v^{*},u\rangle+\tau g(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{p_{+}}\|u-x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{p_{-}}+\tau\langle v^{*}+\bar{\omega},u\rangle+\tau g(\bar{x})-\tau\langle\bar{\omega},\bar{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\Big[\frac{1}{p_{+}}\frac{\|u-x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{p_{-}}}{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}}+\tau\frac{\langle v^{*}+\bar{\omega},u\rangle}{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}}+\frac{\tau g(\bar{x})-\tau\langle\bar{\omega},\bar{x}\rangle}{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\Big[\frac{1}{p_{+}}\frac{\|u-x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{p_{-}}}{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}}-\tau\|v^{*}+\bar{\omega}\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}+\tau\frac{g(\bar{x})-\langle\bar{\omega},\bar{x}\rangle}{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}}\Big]\geq L\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}

for some
L>0
and all
u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
such that
\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}
is large enough. Note, in particular, that
\frac{1}{p_{+}}\frac{\|u-x\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{p_{-}}}{\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}}\rightarrow+\infty
as
\|u\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\rightarrow+\infty
since
p_{-}>1
and
x
is fixed.
Hence, the functional in (
16
) is coercive. Moreover, it is convex, proper and l.s.c. in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, which, by Theorem
2.2
, is reflexive: thus at least one solution exists. Moreover, since
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}\leq 2
, the functional is strictly convex, and hence the solution is unique.
∎

Clearly, the convergence analysis of Algorithm
1
is related to the study of fixed points of iterations (
16
). More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

The solutions of (
P
) coincide with the fixed points of the iteration defined by Algorithm
1
.

Proof.

Suppose that for some
k\geq 0

x^{k}\in\text{Sol(P)}
. Then, by optimality,
-\nabla f(x^{k})\in\partial g(x^{k})
. Note that
u
solves (
15
) if and only if the following inclusion holds

-\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})\in\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(u-x^{k})+\tau_{k}\partial g(u)\,,

where
\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}
is the derivative of
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}
, according to Proposition
2.3
. Clearly,
u=x^{k}
is a solution of (
15
), hence
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
, so
x^{k}
is a fixed point of the iteration process.

Conversely, suppose now
x^{k}=x^{k+1}\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, then

-\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})\in\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k+1}-x^{k})+\tau_{k}\partial g(x^{k+1})=\tau_{k}\partial g(x^{k+1})=\tau_{k}\partial g(x^{k}),

meaning that
x^{k}
is optimal, that is,
x^{k}\in\text{Sol(P)}
.
∎

3.1
Convergence analysis

We provide here a detailed convergence analysis of Algorithm
1
in order to provide an insight on its convergence speed in function values. Our analysis is inspired by the one conducted in
[
4
]
, although it relies on properties related to the modular function
\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}
rather than to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}
. As such, it relies on different arguments, as we will make precise in the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, the following inequality holds true:

\left\langle\frac{\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}},u-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\leq g(u)-g(x^{k+1})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),u-x^{k+1}\right\rangle.

(17)

Moreover, we denote by
D(x^{k})
the quantity

D(x^{k}):=g(x^{k})-g(x^{k+1})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle.

(18)

and have that

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\leq\tau_{k}D(x^{k}).

(19)

Proof.

Note that
x^{k+1}
solves (
15
) if and only if

\displaystyle 0\in\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k+1}-x^{k})+\tau_{k}\nabla f(x^{k})+\tau_{k}\partial g(x^{k+1})\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}}-\nabla f(x^{k})\in\partial g(x^{k+1}).

By definition of subdifferential, we thus have that, for all
u\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
,

\left\langle\frac{\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}}-\nabla f(x^{k}),u-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\leq g(u)-g(x^{k+1}),

which, by rearranging, coincides with (
17
).
Choosing now
u=x^{k}
above and recalling (
18
), we get

\left\langle\frac{\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}},x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\leq D(x^{k}).

Applying now (
13
) entails

\left\langle\frac{\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}},x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle=\frac{\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}},

by which (
19
) follows directly.
∎

The following proposition shows that the iteration scheme (
15
) leads to a descent of the functional
\phi
of our minimization problem (
P
). This will be crucial for the following convergence analysis.

Proposition 3.3
.

For every
k\geq 0
, if
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})<1
, then the iteration defined by Algorithm
1
satisfies

\phi(x^{k+1})\leq\phi(x^{k})-\Big(1-\frac{K\tau_{k}}{\textit{{p}}}\Big)D(x^{k}).

(20)

Proof.

By (
18
), we have

\phi(x^{k})-\phi(x^{k+1})=D(x^{k})+f(x^{k})-f(x^{k+1})-\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle.

(21)

For the last three terms, we notice that we can write

\displaystyle f(x^{k})-f(x^{k+1})-\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle

(22)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}+t(x^{k+1}-x^{k}))-\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

By applying now the
(\texttt{p}-1)
-Hölder continuity of
\nabla f
(Assumption
3.2
), we can provide an estimate of the absolute value of the right-hand side of (
22
), since

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}+t(x^{k+1}-x^{k}))-\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|\nabla f(x^{k}+t(x^{k+1}-x^{k}))-\nabla f(x^{k})\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}K\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{\textit{{p}}}t^{\textit{{p}}-1}\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{K}{\textit{{p}}}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{\textit{{p}}}

Since we have
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})<1
by construction, by Lemma
2.1
(
iii
) there holds
\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}<1
and
\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}<\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})^{1/p_{+}}\leq\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})^{1/\textit{{p}}}
by Lemma
2.1
(
ii
). Hence, by (
19
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}+t(x^{k+1}-x^{k}))-\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{\textit{{p}}}\|x^{k}-x^{k+1}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}^{\textit{{p}}}\leq\frac{K}{\textit{{p}}}\rho_{p(\cdot)}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\leq\frac{K\tau_{k}}{\textit{{p}}}D(x^{k})\,,

which concludes the proof by combining this with (
21
) and (
22
).
∎

For each
k\geq 1
, let us now define for simplicity the
k
-th residual
r_{k}:=\phi(x^{k})-\bar{\phi}\,.

Note that
r_{k}\geq 0
by definition. We can thus rewrite (
20
) as

r_{k}-r_{k+1}\geq\Big(1-\frac{K\tau_{k}}{\textit{{p}}}\Big)D(x^{k}).

(23)

Thanks to the bounds on the step-sizes
\tau_{k}
, there holds
r_{k}-r_{k+1}\geq 0
, hence the descent of the functional
\phi=f+g
is guaranteed.

Consider now the conjugate exponent
p^{\prime}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
. Since
1<p(\cdot)\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{p(t)}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}(t)}=1\ a.e.
, there holds
2\leq p^{\prime}(\cdot)<+\infty
and

(p^{\prime})_{-}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\ inf}_{t\in\Omega}p^{\prime}(t)=(p_{+})^{\prime}\qquad(p^{\prime})_{+}:=\operatornamewithlimits{ess\ sup}_{t\in\Omega}p^{\prime}(t)=(p_{-})^{\prime}

where by
(p_{+})^{\prime}
we denote the conjugate of
p_{+}
and by
(p_{-})^{\prime}
the conjugate of
p_{-}
. The following lemma shows that by assuming the boundedness of the sequence
(x_{k})_{k}
, an estimate of the right-hand side of (
23
) depending on
(p_{-})^{\prime}
can be found.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
(x^{k})_{k}
is bounded, then
r_{k}-r_{k+1}\geq c_{0}r_{k}^{(p_{-})^{\prime}}
with
c_{0}>0
.

Proof.

Since
(x^{k})_{k}
is bounded, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that for all
k\geq 1

\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\leq C_{1}
for some
\bar{x}\in\text{Sol(P)}
. The convexity of
f
as well as (
17
) with
u=\bar{x}
gives

\displaystyle r_{k}

\displaystyle=f(x^{k})+g(x^{k})-f(\bar{x})-g(\bar{x})\leq g(x^{k})-g(\bar{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-\bar{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{k+1}\right\rangle+g(x^{k})-g(\bar{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-\bar{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=D(x^{k})+g(x^{k+1})-g(\bar{x})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k}),x^{k+1}-\bar{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq D(x^{k})+\left\langle\frac{\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})}{\tau_{k}},x^{k+1}-\bar{x}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq D(x^{k})+\tau_{k}^{-1}\|\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}\|x^{k+1}-\bar{x}\|_{{L^{p(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle\leq D(x^{k})+\tau_{k}^{-1}\|\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}C_{1}.

(24)

Recalling now that
\langle\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(u),v\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(u(t))|u(t)|^{p(t)-1}v(t)\mathrm{d}t
for any
u,v\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
,
by(
9
) and Proposition
2.1
we have

\|\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(u)\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}=\|\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(u)|u|^{p(\cdot)-1}\|^{\prime}_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\leq 2\|\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(u)|u|^{p(\cdot)-1}\|_{{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}}}.

Observe now that the following equality holds true:

\displaystyle\rho_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(u)|u|^{p(\cdot)-1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(u(t))|u(t)|^{p(t)-1}\Big)^{p^{\prime}(t)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(u(t))|u(t)|\Big)^{p(t)}=\int_{\Omega}|u(t)|^{p(t)}\mathrm{d}t=\rho_{p(\cdot)}(u).

Hence it follows that

\rho_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}\Big(\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})|x^{k}-x^{k+1}|^{p(\cdot)-1}\Big)<1,
since by construction we have

which can now be evaluated in

\displaystyle r_{k}

\displaystyle\leq D(x^{k})+\tau_{k}^{-1}\|\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})\|_{({L^{p(\cdot)}})^{*}}C_{1}

\displaystyle\leq D(x^{k})+\tau_{k}^{-1}C_{1}2\|\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})|x^{k}-x^{k+1}|^{p(\cdot)-1}\|_{{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle\leq D(x^{k})+\tau_{k}^{-1}C_{1}2\Big(\rho_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x^{k}-x^{k+1})|x^{k}-x^{k+1}|^{p(\cdot)-1})\Big)^{1/(p_{-})^{\prime}}

so that, by (

The step-size constraints (

\displaystyle r_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r_{k}-r_{k+1}}{\delta}+2C_{1}\bar{\tau}^{-\frac{(p_{-})^{\prime}-1}{(p_{-})^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{r_{k}-r_{k+1}}{\delta}\right)^{1/(p_{-})^{\prime}}.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

We say that
h
is convex of power-type

if it is convex of power-type
r
in
\hat{u}
for all
\hat{u}\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
.

Proposition 3.6

If

Proof.

First, note that

Suppose now by contradiction that there exists

A Bregmanized modular-proximal gradient algorithm

In this section, we introduce a different modular-proximal gradient algorithm solving (
P
) where the proximal step is defined in terms of a modular Bregman-like distance. Our study is here inspired by the analysis carried out in
[
18
]
where an analogous algorithm is studied for a general Banach space
\mathcal{X}
. Similarly as above, we start this section by stating the required assumptions.

Assumption 4.1
.

\nabla f:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}^{*}
is
(\textit{{p}}-1)
Hölder-continuous with

Assumption 4.2
.

There exists

Algorithm 2

Bregmanized modular-proximal gradient algorithm in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
spaces

Parameters:

\{\tau_{k}\}_{k}
s.t.

(27)

Initialization:
Start with

FOR

(28)

UNTIL

The pseudocode of the proposed algorithm is reported in Algorithm

Assumption
4.2
links the geometrical properties of the space

with the Hölder smoothness properties of

The optimization problem (

Definition 4.1

Given

\mathcal{D}_{h}(x,y):=h(x)-h(y)-\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\qquad\forall x,y\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}.

We now observe that the updating rule in Algorithm

We report now the analogue of Proposition

Proposition 4.1
.

The problem

In order to interpret (

[

Definition 4.2
.

Given

(30)

where

denotes the Bregman-like distance associated to

.

Note that the minimum in (

is a strictly convex function. Moreover, the unique point

0\in\partial h(\pi_{h}(x^{*}))+\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\pi_{h}(x^{*}))-x^{*}\iff x^{*}\in\partial h(\pi_{h}(x^{*}))+\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\pi_{h}(x^{*})).

(31)

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\gamma>0
, there holds
\bar{x}\in\text{Sol(P)}

\bar{x}=\pi_{\gamma g}\big(\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\bar{x})-\gamma\nabla f(\bar{x})\big)

Proof.

Since

solves (

0\in\nabla f(\bar{x})+\partial g(\bar{x})\quad\iff\quad 0\in\gamma\nabla f(\bar{x})-\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\bar{x})+\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\bar{x})+\gamma\partial g(\bar{x})\,,

By (

\bar{x}=\pi_{\gamma g}\big(\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(\bar{x})-\gamma\nabla f(\bar{x})\big)

∎

Remark 4.1
.

The iteration (

Sparse reconstruction models: thresholding functions and primal-dual interpretation

In this section, we consider an exemplar sparse reconstruction model used in a variety of signal/image inverse problems and discuss the application of the algorithms presented in this paper for the computation of its numerical solution. Given a Lebesgue measurable map
p(\cdot):\Omega\longrightarrow(1,2]
, we consider a bounded linear operator
A:{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow L^{p_{+}}(\Omega)
and an observation
y\in L^{p_{+}}(\Omega),\penalty\ p_{+}\leq 2
. For
\lambda>0
, we aim to minimize the Tikhonov-like functional

\operatornamewithlimits{arg\ min}_{x\in{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}}\penalty\ \frac{1}{p_{+}}\|Ax-y\|_{p_{+}}^{p_{+}}+\lambda\|x\|_{1}

In agreement with Assumption
3.2
for Algorithm
1
and with Assumption
4.1
for Algorithm
2
, we now need to study the Hölder continuity of

.

[

p\in\left\{1.2,1.4,1.6,1.8,2\right\}
.

As discussed in the previous sections, a direct application of the proximal-gradient algorithm in Banach spaces studied in
[
4
]
to the
\ell^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
scenario would have been rendered more challenging due to the nonseparability of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p(\cdot)}}
and of the corresponding
r
-duality map, given by

\mathbf{J}_{\ell^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{r}(x)=\frac{1}{{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{p_{j}|x_{j}|^{p_{j}}}{\|x\|_{\ell^{p(\cdot)}}^{p_{j}}}}}\Big({\frac{1}{\|x\|_{\ell^{p(\cdot)}}^{{p_{i}}-{r}}}{p_{i}}|x_{i}|^{{p_{i}}-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x_{i})\Big)_{i=1,\cdots,n}}.

We can similarly focus on Algorithm
2
, about which, we remark, Assumption
4.1
holds with
\texttt{p}=p_{+}
. Assumption
4.2
is hard to verify in practice, although numerical tests show that it is not that challenging to find a step-size for which convergence is guaranteed. Proceeding similarly as above, we exploit again the separability of the modular appearing in the computation of the
k
-th iteration (
28
) of Algorithm
2
, which leads to the computation of the minimizers of the following
1
D function

\Psi^{\text{Alg.D}}_{x,s,t,p}(u)=\frac{1}{p}|u|^{p}-{|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)}u+{s}u+t|u|

where
x=(x^{k})_{i},\ s=\tau_{k}\sigma^{k}_{i},\ t=\tau_{k}\lambda,\ p=p_{i}
and Alg.D stands for dual algorithm as explained in Section
5.1
. Such minimizers are given by
\Big(\partial\Psi^{\text{Alg.D}}_{x,s,t,p}\Big)^{-1}(0)
and correspond to the following thresholding function:

T^{\text{Alg.D}}(x,s,t,p)=\begin{cases}(|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)-s-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}&\text{if }\,|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)>s+t\\
-(s-t-|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}&\text{if }\,|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)<s-t\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(36)

Figure
1(a)
shows a comparison between the thresholding functions (
35
) and (
36
) with the classical soft-thresholding function

T^{\text{ISTA}}(x,s,t)=\begin{cases}x-s-t&\text{if }\,x>s+t\\
x-s+t&\text{if }\,x<s-t\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(37)

which corresponds to the minimization of the
1
D function
\Psi^{\text{ISTA}}_{x,s,t}(u)=\frac{1}{2}(u-x+s)^{2}+t|u|
appearing in the solution of the
\ell_{2}-\ell_{1}
LASSO optimization problem in the Hilbert space
\ell_{2}
by means of the standard ISTA algorithm
[
12
]
.
It is interesting to point out that differently from the ISTA thresholding function (
37
), both thresholding functions (
35
) and (
36
) are no longer symmetrical with respect to the vertical line
x=s
. Moreover,
T^{\text{Alg.P}}(\cdot,s,t,p)
remains linear in
x
similarly to
T^{\text{ISTA}}(\cdot,s,t)
, as shown in Figure
1(b)
for some exemplar values of
p
, while the thresholding function
T^{\text{Alg.D}}
is, in general, nonlinear, as shown in Figure
1
. Note that for
p=2
, both
T^{\text{Alg.P}}(\cdot,s,t,2)
and
T^{\text{Alg.D}}(\cdot,s,t,2)
coincide with the standard soft-thresholding
T^{\text{ISTA}}(\cdot,s,t)
operator.

5.1
Interpretation as primal and dual algorithms

In this section, we analyze the relationship between the thresholding functions (
35
) and (
36
) of algorithms
1
and
2
, respectively, and the primal and dual iterative methods described, e.g., in
[
26
]
, similarly to the analogous considerations sketched in Section
1
for the algorithms in
[
4
]
and
[
18
]
in the case
g\equiv 0
. In order to extend our considerations to the nonsmooth case, we need to provide an analytical expression of duality mappings in
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
spaces, which, for better readability, will be done in the discrete setting, that is in
\ell^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

As a first remark, notice that given a fixed
p
and
r
such that
1<p,r<+\infty
, the
r
-duality mapping of
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be defined in terms of componentwise maps
\mathbf{j}_{p}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\mathbf{j}_{p}(t):=|t|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(t)\,,

as follows

\;\mathbf{J}_{\ell^{p}}^{r}(x)=\|x\|_{p}^{r-p}\left(\mathbf{j}_{p}(x_{i})\right)_{i=1,\ldots,n}.

Thanks to the isometric isomorphism between
\left(\ell^{p}\right)^{*}
and
\ell^{p^{\prime}}
, the inverse of such duality mapping is
\left(\mathbf{J}_{\ell^{p}}^{r}\right)^{-1}=\mathbf{J}_{(\ell^{p})^{*}}^{r^{\prime}}=\mathbf{J}_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}
, which can be explicitly written as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{p^{\prime}}^{r^{\prime}-p^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}(x_{i})\right)_{i=1,\ldots,n}=\|x\|_{p^{\prime}}^{r^{\prime}-p^{\prime}}\Big(|x_{i}|^{p^{\prime}-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x_{i})\Big)_{i=1,\cdots,n}

\displaystyle=\|x\|_{p^{\prime}}^{r^{\prime}-p^{\prime}}\Big(|x_{i}|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x_{i})\Big)_{i=1,\cdots,n}.

Comparing now the thresholding function
T^{\text{Alg.P}}(\cdot,s,t,p)
in (
35
) with the above formulas, we observe that we can rewrite it in terms of the componentwise maps
\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}
associated to the duality map
\mathbf{J}_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}
as:

T^{\text{Alg.P}}(x,s,t,p)=\begin{cases}x-\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}(s+t)&\text{if }x>\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(s+t)|s+t|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\\
x-\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}(s-t)&\text{if }x<\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(s-t)|s-t|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(38)

This new formulation is helpful to better show that as the forward gradient step is computed in the primal space: the pointwise map
\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}
is required to associate the element
s+t
of the dual space to an element in the primal space, thus making the computation well defined. In agreement with what has been discussed in the introduction, see (
2
), we can thus interpret this proximal-gradient iteration as a
primal
algorithmic iteration.

As far as the thresholding function
T^{\text{Alg.D}}(\cdot,s,t,p)
in
(
36
) is concerned, we notice that we can proceed similarly and rewrite it as

\displaystyle T^{\text{Alg.D}}(x,s,t,p)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(\mathbf{j}_{p}(x)-s-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}&\text{if }\,|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)>s+t\\
-(s-t-\mathbf{j}_{p}(x))^{\frac{1}{p-1}}&\text{if }\,|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)<s-t\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{j}_{p}(x)-s-t)&\text{if }\,|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)>s+t\\
-\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}(s-t-\mathbf{j}_{p}(x))&\text{if }\,|x|^{p-1}\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x)<s-t\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(39)

We can observe that both pointwise maps
\mathbf{j}_{p}
and
\mathbf{j}_{p^{\prime}}
associated to the duality maps
\mathbf{J}^{r}_{p}
and
\mathbf{J}^{r^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}}
, respectively, play a role here as both a primal-to-dual map is required to define the forward step in the dual space and a dual-to-primal one is needed to compute the backward step, analogously to what we have seen before. In agreement with what has been observed in the introduction, see (
4
), we thus interpret such iteration as a
dual
proximal-gradient step.

Such considerations can now be similarly applied to our modular-based variable exponent case, where the role of the primal-to-dual map
\mathbf{J}_{p}^{r}
is replaced by the map
\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}
, whose expression in
\ell^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
reads

\mathbf{J}_{\bar{\rho}_{p(\cdot)}}(x)=\left(\operatornamewithlimits{sign}(x_{i})|x_{i}|^{p_{i}-1}\right)_{i=1\ldots,n}=\left(\mathbf{j}_{p_{i}}(x_{i})\right)_{i=1\ldots,n},

and depends on the pointwise mappings
\mathbf{j}_{p_{i}}(\cdot)
defined above. Algorithm
1
should thus be interpreted as a
primal
proximal-gradient algorithm with pointwise thresholding function (
38
), while Algorithm
2
should be understood as a
dual
proximal-gradient algorithm with pointwise thresholding function (
5.1
).

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we present two possible ways of generalizing proximal-gradient algorithms to solve structured minimization problems in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
. Due to the difficulties introduced by working in a Banach space setting (i.e., the lack of Riesz isometric isomorphism and the need for duality mappings) and the further lack of separability of the norm in
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
, standard strategies cannot be applied here. Thus the use of alternative iterative schemes based on the separable modular function is required. Two algorithms are presented, a primal one (Algorithm
1
) inspired by the one studied in
[
4
]
for problems in
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, and a dual one (Algorithm
2
), analogous to the one studied in
[
18
]
.
We provide a detailed convergence analysis for Algorithm
1
showing the descent of the functional and convergence in function values with rate
\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\Big)^{p_{-}-1}
, with
p_{-}
being the essential infimum of the exponent map
p(\cdot)
. For Algorithm
2
a modular Bregman-like distance is used in a dual update fashion and analogous convergence rates are shown in function values. Several numerical results are reported to compare the use of the adaptive
{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
space with Hilbert and
L^{p}(\Omega)
spaces on some exemplar signal deconvolution and signal/image mixed noise removal problems. Finally, a numerical verification on the speed of convergence and on the computational efficiency of the proposed algorithms is given, showing that
primal
algorithms have slower convergence in comparison with
dual
ones.

Future work shall address the crucial question of how the map
p(\cdot)
shall be chosen in order to adapt to local signal/image prior contents. Moreover, it would be interesting to find a strategy to incorporate extrapolation/acceleration in order to improve convergence speed which, in practice, is particularly slow for primal algorithms.

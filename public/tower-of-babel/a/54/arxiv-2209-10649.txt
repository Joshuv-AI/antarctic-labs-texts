Remarks on Villadsen algebras
George A. Elliott, Chun Guang Li, Zhuang Niu

Abstract
. It is shown that certain unital simple C*-algebras constructed by Villadsen in [ 29 ] are classified by the K 0 \mathrm{K}_{0} -group together with the radius of comparison.



1.
Introduction

Villadsen algebras (of the first type) were constructed in
[
29
]
as examples of simple unital C*-algebras which have perforation in their ordered
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group. This class of C*-algebras lies outside the scope of the current classification theorem (
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
3
]
), as Villadsen algebras do not absorb the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
tensorially. Indeed, a Villadsen type algebra was constructed in
[
28
]
which has the same value of the Elliott invariant as an AI algebra, but is not itself isomorphic to that AI algebra.

Each Villadsen algebra is an inductive limit of homogeneous C*-algebras with connecting maps induced by coordinate projections together with a small portion of point evaluations (see Section
2
). In this note, we shall first show that, with different point-evaluation sets, the resulting algebras are classified by the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-multiplicity and the radius of comparison of Toms (
[
27
]
; Definition
3.1
below):

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
X
be a connected finite-dimensional solid space (see Definition
3.3
). Let
A_{E}
and
A_{F}
be Villadsen algebras (see Section
2
) with point-evaluation sets
E
and
F
respectively (but with the same connected space
X
and the same numbers and multiplicities of the coordinate projections
(c_{i})
,
(s_{i_{1}},...,s_{i,c_{i}})
).
Then
A_{E}\cong A_{F}
if, and only if,

\rho(\mathrm{K}_{0}(A_{E}))=\rho(\mathrm{K}_{0}(A_{F}))\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathrm{rc}(A_{E})=\mathrm{rc}(A_{F}),

where
\rho_{A_{E}}
and
\rho_{A_{F}}
are the unique states of the order-unit groups
\mathrm{K}_{0}(A_{E})
and
\mathrm{K}_{0}(A_{F})
, respectively, and
\mathrm{rc}(\cdot)
denotes the radius of comparison.

Moreover, if the fixed seed space
X
is further assumed to be K-contractible (that is,
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathrm{C}(X))=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathrm{K}_{1}(\mathrm{C}(X))=\{0\}
), then the algebras can be classified by the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group together with the radius of comparison even if the numbers and the multiplicities of the coordinate projections and the numbers of point evaluations are arbitrary:

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
7.9
)
.

Let
X
be a connected finite-dimensional solid space which is K-contractible. Let

A:=A(X^{p},(n^{(A)}_{i}),(k^{(A)}_{i}),E^{(A)})\quad\textrm{and}\quad B:=B(X^{q},(n^{(B)}_{i}),(k^{(B)}_{i}),F^{(B)})

be Villadsen algebras with non-zero radius of comparison, where
p,q=1,2,...
.
Then

A\cong B

if, and only if,

\mathrm{K}_{0}(A)\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(B)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathrm{rc}(A)=\mathrm{rc}(B).

Note that this theorem covers the example constructed in
[
28
]
.

One might compare the Villadsen algebras with the UHF algebras of
[
13
]
and
[
5
]
, and the present classification results with the classification of the unital UHF algebras, or, for that matter, of their non-unital hereditary subalgebras in
[
5
]
. The non-unital version of the Villadsen algebras and their classification will be considered in a forthcoming paper.

We hope that our result might shed some light on the possibility of classifying more general non-
\mathcal{Z}
-stable C*-algebras, for instance, general simple A(S)H algebras with diagonal maps (
[
9
]
and
[
1
]
), or general simple transformation group C*-algebras.

2.
The Villadsen algebra
A(X,(n_{i}),(k_{i}),E)

Let
X
be a connected metrizable compact space, let
(c_{i})
and
(k_{i})
be two sequences of non-zero natural numbers, and let

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}E_{1}:=\{x_{1,1},...,x_{1,k_{1}}\}\subseteq X,\\
E_{2}:=\{x_{2,1},...,x_{2,k_{2}}\}\subseteq X^{c_{1}},\\
...\\
E_{i}:=\{x_{i,1},...,x_{i,k_{i}}\}\subseteq X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}},\\
...\end{array}\right.

be a sequence of finite subsets such that for each
i=1,2,...
, the set

\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{s=1}^{c_{i+1}\cdots c_{i+j-1}}\pi_{s}(E_{i+j})

is dense in
X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}
, where
\pi_{s}
are the coordinate projections.

Construct the (generalized) Villadsen algebra as the inductive limit of the sequence

(2.1)

\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}}(\mathrm{C}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})}(\mathrm{C}(X^{c_{1}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})(n_{2}+k_{2})}(\mathrm{C}(X^{c_{1}c_{2}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots,}

where the seed for the
i
th-stage map,

\phi_{i}:\mathrm{C}(X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}})\to\mathrm{M}_{n_{i}+k_{i}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}c_{i}})),

is defined by

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathrm{diag}\{\underbrace{\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{1},...,f\circ\pi_{1}}_{s_{i,1}},...,\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{c_{i}},...,f\circ\pi_{c_{i}}}_{s_{i,c_{i}}}}_{n_{i}},\underbrace{f(x_{i,1}),...,f(x_{i,k_{i}})}_{k_{i}}\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\{\underbrace{\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{1},...,f\circ\pi_{1}}_{s_{i,1}},...,\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{c_{i}},...,f\circ\pi_{c_{i}}}_{s_{i,c_{i}}}}_{n_{i}},f(E_{i})\},

where
s_{i,1},...,s_{i,c_{i}}\geq 1
are natural numbers, and
n_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{c_{i}}s_{i,j}
.

A direct calculation shows that the composed map

\phi_{i,i+j}:\mathrm{C}(X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}})\to\mathrm{M}_{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j-1}+k_{i+j-1})}(\mathrm{C}(X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdots c_{i+j-1}}))

is equal (up to a permutation) to

f\mapsto\mathrm{diag}\{\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{1},...,f\circ\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j-1}}}_{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j-1}},\underbrace{f(x_{i,1}),...,f(x_{i,k_{i}}),}_{k_{i}[(n_{i+1}+k_{i+1})\cdots(n_{i+j-1}+k_{i+j-1})]}\underbrace{f(\cdot),...,f(\cdot)}_{\cdots},...,\},

i.e.,

\displaystyle f\mapsto\mathrm{diag}\{\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{1},...,f\circ\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j-1}}}_{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j-1}},f(E_{i})1_{(n_{i+1}+k_{i+1})\cdots(n_{i+j-1}+k_{i+j-1})},

\displaystyle(f(\pi_{1}(E_{i+1})),...,f(\pi_{n_{i}}(E_{i+1})))1_{(n_{i+2}+k_{i+2})\cdots(n_{i+j-1}+k_{i+j-1})},...,\}.

So, it can be described as

\mathrm{diag}\{\underbrace{f\circ\pi_{1},...,f\circ\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j-1}}}_{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j-1}},\textrm{point evaluations}\}.

We shall choose
c_{i}
,
s_{i,1},...,s_{i,c_{i}}
(hence
n_{i}
), and
k_{i}
in such a way

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}=\lim_{j\to\infty}(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}+k_{i}})\cdots(\frac{n_{i+j}}{n_{i+j}+k_{i+j}})\neq 0.

In other words, we require

(2.2)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}(\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i}+k_{i}})\cdots(\frac{n_{i+j}}{n_{i+j}+k_{i+j}})=1.

Denote the inductive limit algebra by

A(X,(n_{i}),(k_{i}),E).

In what follows, we shall show, with a mild assumption on
X
(see Definition
3.3
), that this algebra, which is always simple, is independent of the choice of points in the point-evaluation set
E
(if the number of them at each stage is kept the same; otherwise, it is classified by the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group together with the radius of comparison). In the case that
X
is contractible, we shall show that this C*-algebra is classified by the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group and the radius of comparison, and also, if the latter is zero, the trace simplex (Theorem
7.1
). (Even allowing different numbers of coordinate projections and multiplicities, and different numbers of point evaluations.)

Remark 2.1
.

If
c_{i}=1
,
i=1,2,...
, then
A(X,(n_{i}),(k_{i}),E)
is the C*-algebra constructed by Goodearl in
[
17
]
with real rank not equal to zero. On the other hand, if
s_{i,j}=1
,
i=1,2,...,

j=1,...,c_{i}
, then
A(X,(n_{i}),(k_{i}),E)
is the C*-algebra constructed by Villadsen in
[
29
]
.

3.
Mean dimension and radius of comparison

In this section, let us calculate the mean dimension (as formulated in
[
21
]
) and radius of comparison (as formulated in
[
27
]
) of the Goodearl–Villadsen algebras
A(X,(n_{i}),(k_{i}),E)
.

First, recall

Definition 3.1

(Definition 6.1 of
[
27
]
)
.

Let
A
be a C*-algebra. Denote by
\mathrm{M}_{n}(A)
the C*-algebra of
n\times n
matrices over
A
. Regard
\mathrm{M}_{n}(A)
as the upper-left corner of
\mathrm{M}_{n+1}(A)
, and consider the union,

\mathrm{M}_{\infty}(A)=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{M}_{n}(A),

the algebra of all finite matrices over
A
.

The radius of comparison of a unital C*-algebra
A
, denoted by
\mathrm{rc}(A)
, is the infimum of the set of real numbers
r>0
such that if
a,b\in(\mathrm{M}_{\infty}(A))^{+}
satisfy

\mathrm{d}_{\tau}(a)+r<\mathrm{d}_{\tau}(b),\quad\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A),

then
a\precsim b
, where
\mathrm{T}(A)
is the simplex of tracial states. (In
[
27
]
, the radius of comparison is defined in terms of quasitraces instead of traces; but since all the algebras considered in this paper are nuclear, by
[
18
]
(see also
[
2
]
in the nuclear case), any quasitrace is actually a trace.)

We also have the following remark on vector bundles:

Remark 3.2
.

Assume a (complex) vector bundle
E
over a compact metrizable space
X
has non-zero Chern class
\mathrm{c}_{n}(E)\in\mathrm{H}^{n}(X)
. Then the trivial sub-bundles of
E
have rank at most
\mathrm{rank}(E)-n/2
, as, if there is a trivial sub-bundle
F
of rank
r>\mathrm{rank}(E)-n/2
, then

\mathrm{c}(E)=\mathrm{c}(F^{c}\oplus F)=\mathrm{c}(F^{c})\mathrm{c}(F)=\mathrm{c}(F^{c}),

but, since
\mathrm{rank}(F^{c})=\mathrm{rank}(E)-\mathrm{rank}(F)<n/2,
we have
\mathrm{c}_{n}(F^{c})=0
, and hence
\mathrm{c}_{n}(E)=\mathrm{c}_{n}(F^{c})=0
, which contradicts the assumption.

Definition 3.3
.

Let us call a metrizable compact space
X
solid if it contains a Euclidean ball of dimension
\mathrm{dim}(X)
when
\mathrm{dim}(X)
is finite; when
\mathrm{dim}(X)=\infty
,
X
solid will mean that
X
contains a Euclidean ball of arbitrarily large dimension.

Note that all finite CW-complexes are solid. The Hawaiian earring and the Hilbert cube are also solid. Not all compact metrizable spaces are solid, as there are such
X
with
\mathrm{dim}(X\times X)<2\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)
which implies that
X
cannot be solid.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a metrizable compact space. With
A=A(X,(n_{i}),(k_{i}),E)
, one has

(3.1)

\mathrm{mdim}(A)\leq\frac{\dim(X)}{n_{0}}\cdot\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})},

where
\infty\cdot 0=0
and
\mathrm{mdim}(\cdot)
is the mean dimension of an AH system introduced in
[
21
]
.

Moreover, if the metrizable compact space
X
is solid, then equality holds in (
3.1
), and the radius of comparison of
A
,
\mathrm{rc}(A)
, is equal to
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{mdim}(A)
.

Proof.

Let us first prove (
3.1
). Consider the
i
th stage,
\mathrm{M}_{m_{i}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{d_{i}}))
, where
m_{i}=n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i-1}+k_{i-1})
and
d_{i}=c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}
(note that all coordinate projections appear, i.e.,
s_{i,1},...,s_{i,c_{i}}\geq 1
,
i=1,2,...
), and let
\alpha
be a finite open cover of
X^{d_{i}}
. Since the pull-back of
\alpha
by any constant map has degree zero and
\mathcal{D}(\alpha)\leq\mathrm{dim}(X^{d_{i}})\leq c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)
, we have

\mathcal{D}(\phi_{i,j}(\alpha))\leq c_{i}\cdots c_{j}\mathcal{\cdot}\mathcal{D}(\alpha)\leq c_{1}\cdots c_{j}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X),

where
\mathcal{D}(\cdot)
denotes the degree of an open cover,
and then

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{D}(\phi_{i,j}(\alpha))}{m_{j+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{j}\mathcal{\cdot}\mathcal{D}(\alpha)}{n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j}+k_{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{D}(\alpha)}{n_{0}}\cdot\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{j}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j}+k_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}\cdot\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{j}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j}+k_{j})},

where
\infty\cdot 0=0
in the case that
\mathrm{dim}(X)=\infty
. Passing to the limit as
i\to\infty
, we obtain (
3.1
) (with
i
in the place of
j
). In particular, by
[
21
]
, we have

(3.2)

\mathrm{rc}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{mdim}(A)\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}\cdot\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})}.

Now, assume
X
is solid (i.e., it contains a Euclidean ball of dimension
\mathrm{dim}(X)
, if
\mathrm{dim}(X)<\infty
, and of arbitrary dimension otherwise), and let us show that

(3.3)

\mathrm{rc}(A)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}\cdot\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})}.

Together with (
3.2
), we will then have

\mathrm{rc}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{mdim}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\dim(X)}{n_{0}}\cdot\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})}.

Set

\gamma=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})}.

Since (
3.3
) holds trivially if
\gamma=0
(as
\infty\cdot 0=0
), let us assume that
\gamma\neq 0
in the rest of the proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{dim}(X)<\infty
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary for the time being.
Choose
i
sufficiently large that

\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)-2}{2n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i-1}+k_{i-1})}>\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}-\varepsilon

and

\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{2n_{0}}\left(\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i-1}+k_{i-1})}-\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{j-1}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})}\right)<\varepsilon,\quad j>i.

Since
X
contains a Euclidean ball of dimension
\mathrm{dim}(X)
, the space
X^{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}}
contains a Euclidian ball of dimension
c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)
, and hence, if
i
is large enough, it contains a
d
-dimensional sphere
S
, where

c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)-2\leq d\leq c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)-1

and
d
is non-zero and even.

Pick a (complex) vector bundle
E
over
S
such that
\mathrm{rank}(E)=d/2
and
e:=\mathrm{c}_{d}(E)\in\mathrm{H}^{d}(S)
is non-zero, where
\mathrm{c}_{d}
is the
d
th Chern class. (Such a vector bundle exists, as, otherwise, the Chern class of every vector bundle would be trivial, then the Chern character is trivial, and it would not induce a rational isomorphism between the K-group and the cohomology group of the sphere
S
.) Note that the total Chern class of
E
is
1+e
. Denote by
p
the corresponding projection in
\mathrm{M}_{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(S))
, and extend
p
to a positive element of
\mathrm{M}_{\infty}(\mathrm{C}(X^{d_{i}}))
such that
\mathrm{rank}(p(x))\geq d/2
,
x\in X^{d_{i}}
. Denote this element still by
p
.

Note that

\mathrm{d}_{\tau}(p)\geq\frac{d}{2n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i-1}+k_{i-1})}\geq\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}\cdot\mathrm{dim}(X)-2}{2n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i-1}+k_{i-1})}>\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}-\varepsilon.

Consider the element
\phi_{i,\infty}(p)\in A
. For each
j>i
, the restriction of
\phi_{i,j}(p)\in\mathrm{M}_{m_{j}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{d_{j}}))
to
S\times\cdots\times S\subseteq X^{d_{j}}
is a projection which corresponds to the vector bundle

E_{j}:=(\bigoplus_{s_{1}}\pi_{1}^{*}(E))\oplus\cdots\oplus(\bigoplus_{s_{c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}}}\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}}^{*}(E))\oplus\theta_{j},

where
\theta_{j}
is a trivial bundle. Then the total Chern class of
E_{j}
is

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{*}(1+c_{d})^{s_{1}}\pi_{2}^{*}(1+c_{d})^{s_{2}}\cdots\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}}^{*}(1+c_{d})^{s_{c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{*}(1+s_{1}e)\pi_{2}^{*}(1+s_{2}e)\cdots\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}}^{*}(1+s_{c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}}e),

and, by the Künneth Theorem, it is non-zero at degree
dc_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}
. Hence (see Remark
3.2
), any trivial sub-bundle of
E_{j}
has rank at most

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}(E_{j})-\frac{1}{2}dc_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}(E)(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})-\frac{1}{2}dc_{i}\cdots c_{j-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}((n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})-c_{i}\cdots c_{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{2}(c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})-c_{1}\cdots c_{j-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{2n_{0}}(\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i-1}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i-1}+k_{i-1})}-\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{j-1}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})})n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1}).

Let
r\in A
be a trivial projection with
2\varepsilon<\mathrm{d}_{\tau}(r)<3\varepsilon
. Then

\mathrm{d}_{\tau}(r)+(\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}-4\varepsilon)<\mathrm{d}_{\tau}(p),\quad\tau\in\mathrm{T}(A).

But the rank of the vector bundle of
r
at the stage
j
is at least

2\varepsilon n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1})>\varepsilon n_{0}(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{j-1}+k_{j-1}),

which implies that
r
is not Cuntz subequivalent to
p
, and therefore,

\mathrm{rc}(A)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}-4\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, this implies
\mathrm{rc}(A)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)}{n_{0}}
.

If
X
is infinite-dimensional (recall still
\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})}=\gamma\neq 0
), then the argument above (choose
d
arbitrarily large) shows that
\mathrm{rc}(A)
is arbitrarily large, and hence
\mathrm{rc}(A)=\infty
. So, (
3.3
) always holds, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For any
r\in[0,+\infty]
, there is a Villadsen algebra
A
such that
\mathrm{rc}(A)=r
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
r\in(0,+\infty)
. Pick a natural number
d
such that
2r<d
, and consider
s:=2r/d\in(0,1)
. Then pick a sequence of rational numbers
p_{i}/q_{i}\in(0,1)
,
i=1,2,...
, such that

\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}}\cdot\frac{p_{2}}{q_{2}}\cdots=s.

Write

n_{i}=p_{i}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad k_{i}=q_{i}-p_{i},\quad i=1,2,...,

and we have

(\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}+k_{1}})(\frac{n_{2}}{n_{2}+k_{2}})\cdots=s.

Let
A
be a Villadsen algebra associated with
(n_{i})
and
(k_{i})
(and
c_{i}=n_{i}
,
i=1,2,...
,
n_{0}=1
) with the seed space
X=[0,1]^{d}
, which is solid. Then it follows from Theorem
3.4
that

\mathrm{rc}=\frac{1}{2}d(\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}+k_{1}})(\frac{n_{2}}{n_{2}+k_{2}})\cdots=\frac{1}{2}ds=r.

If
r=+\infty
, then one can construct a Villadsen algebra with the seed space
X=[0,1]^{\infty}
and with the sequences
(n_{i})
,
(k_{i})
as above. Then the resulting algebra has
\mathrm{rc}(A)=+\infty
.

If
r=0
, then one can construct a Villadsen algebra with the seed space
X
being the Cantor set and with the sequences
(n_{i})
,
(k_{i})
as above. Then the resulting algebra has
\mathrm{rc}(A)=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

If
\mathrm{rc}(A)>0
, then

(3.4)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}(\frac{c_{i}}{n_{i}})\cdots(\frac{c_{i+j}}{n_{i+j}})=1

and

(3.5)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{|\{s_{k}:s_{k}=1,k=1,...,c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}\}|}{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}}=1,

where

\phi_{i,j+1}=\mathrm{diag}\{\underbrace{\underbrace{\pi_{1}^{*},...,\pi_{1}^{*}}_{s_{1}},...,\underbrace{\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}^{*},...,\pi_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}^{*}}_{s_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}}}_{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}},\ \mathrm{point\ evaluations}\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{rc}(A)>0
, we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{c_{1}\cdots c_{i}}{(n_{1}+k_{1})\cdots(n_{i}+k_{i})}>0,

and hence

\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}=1.

Comparing this with (
2.2
) (and since both limits are non-zero), we have (
3.4
):

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}}{\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}}{\displaystyle\frac{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}}{(n_{i}+k_{i})\cdots(n_{i+j}+k_{i+j})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}(\frac{c_{i}}{n_{i}})\cdots(\frac{c_{i+j}}{n_{i+j}}).

As for (
3.5
), note that
n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}
, and hence

\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{n_{i}\cdots n_{i+j}}=\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}}\leq\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{(c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}-b_{i,j})+2b_{i,j}}=\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}+b_{i,j}}\leq 1,

where

b_{i,j}:=|\{s_{k}:s_{k}>1,k=1,...,c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}\}|.

Together with (
3.4
), this yields

1=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}+b_{i,j}}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{1+\frac{b_{i,j}}{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}};

therefore (note that
s_{k}
,
k=1,...,c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}
, are non-zero),

1=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}-b_{i,j}}{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{|\{s_{k}:s_{k}=1,k=1,...,c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}\}|}{c_{i}\cdots c_{i+j}}.

Using (
3.4
) again, one obtains (
3.5
).
∎

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
X
be a K-contractible solid metrizable compact space which is finite dimensional. Let

A:=A(X^{p},(n^{(A)}_{i}),(k^{(A)}_{i}),E^{(A)})\quad\textrm{and}\quad B:=B(X^{q},(n^{(B)}_{i}),(k^{(B)}_{i}),F^{(B)})

be Villadsen algebras (with coordinate projections of arbitrary multiplicity).
Then
A\cong B

if, and only if,

\mathrm{K}_{0}(A)\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(B),\quad\mathrm{T}(A)\cong\mathrm{T}(B)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathrm{rc}(A)=\mathrm{rc}(B).

Moreover, if
\mathrm{rc}(A)\neq 0
(or
\mathrm{rc}(B)\neq 0
), then
\mathrm{T}(A)
(or
\mathrm{T}(B)
) is redundant in the invariant; that is,
A\cong B

if, and only if,

\mathrm{K}_{0}(A)\cong\mathrm{K}_{0}(B)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathrm{rc}(A)=\mathrm{rc}(B).

Proof.

Consider the inductive limit construction

\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}^{(A)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{p}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{m_{1}^{(A)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{pd^{(A)}_{1}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{m_{2}^{(A)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{pd^{(A)}_{2}}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A.}

Tensor it with
\mathrm{M}_{pq}(\mathbb{C})
and add a new first map, which is induced by coordinate projections with multiplicity one (but no point evaluation yet), to obtain the new construction

\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{qn_{0}^{(A)}}(\mathrm{C}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pq}(\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}^{(A)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{p})))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pq}(\mathrm{M}_{m_{1}^{(A)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{pd^{(A)}_{1}})))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pq}(A).}

Similarly, also consider
B
, and consider the new inductive limit construction

\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pn_{0}^{(B)}}(\mathrm{C}(X))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pq}(\mathrm{M}_{n_{0}^{(B)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{q}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pq}(\mathrm{M}_{m_{1}^{(B)}}(\mathrm{C}(X^{qd^{(B)}_{1}})))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{pq}(B).}

Then collapse the first two maps so that there are point evaluations in all connecting maps of the new sequences.

Since
\mathrm{rc}(A)=\mathrm{rc}(B)
, we have
\mathrm{rc}(\mathrm{M}_{pq}(A))=\mathrm{rc}(\mathrm{M}_{pq}(B))
, and
by Theorem
7.1
,
\mathrm{M}_{pq}(A)\cong\mathrm{M}_{pq}(B)
; denote this algebra by
C
. Note that
[e_{A}]_{0}=[e_{B}]_{0}
in
\mathrm{K}_{0}(C)
, where
e_{A}
and
e_{B}
are the images of
1_{A}
and
1_{B}
in the upper left corner of the matrix algebras respectively. Since
A
and
B
have stable rank one (
[
9
]
),
C
has stable rank one, and hence cancellation of projections. This implies that
e_{A}
is Murray-von Neumann equivalent to
e_{B}
inside
C
, and therefore
A\cong B
, as asserted.
∎

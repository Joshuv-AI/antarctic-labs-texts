Abstract
The rational Dunkl operators are commuting differential-reflection operators on the Euclidean space ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} associated with a root system, that contain some non-local refection terms, and the associated Hardy space is defined by means of the Riesz transforms with respect to the Dunkl operators. The aim of the paper is to prove that its dual can be realized by a class of functions on ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , denoted by BMC κ {\text{BMC}}_{\kappa} in the text, that consists of the underlying functions of a certain type of weighted Carleson measures. Our method is “purely analytic” and does not depend on the atomic decomposition. As a corollary we obtain the Fefferman-Stein decomposition of functions in BMC κ {\text{BMC}}_{\kappa} . 2020 MS Classification: 42B30, 42B20 (Primary), 51F15 (Secondary) Key Words and Phrases: Hardy space; Dunkl operator; Riesz transform; Carleson measure, weighted BMO space

Introduction and main results

Let
R
be a root system in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
normalized so that
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2
for
\alpha\in R
and with
R_{+}
a fixed positive subsystem, and
G
the finite reflection group generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in R
), where
\sigma_{\alpha}(x)=x-\langle\alpha,x\rangle\alpha
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stands for the usual inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For a nonnegative multiplicity function
\kappa
defined on
R
(invariant under
G
), the (rational) Dunkl operators
D_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq d)
are defined by (cf.
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle D_{j}=\partial_{j}+\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\alpha_{j}\frac{1-\sigma_{\alpha}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle},

(1)

where
\sigma_{\alpha}f=f\circ\sigma_{\alpha}
and
\alpha_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq d
) is the
j
th coordinate of
\alpha
.
It is proved in
[
11
]
that the Dunkl operators generate a commutative algebra and are anti-symmetry associated to the measure

\displaystyle d\omega_{\kappa}=W_{\kappa}\,dx,\qquad{\rm where}\quad W_{\kappa}(x)=\prod_{\alpha\in R_{+}}|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|^{2\kappa(\alpha)}.

The weight
W_{\kappa}
is
G
-invariant and positive homogeneous of degree
2|\kappa|
, so that the homogeneous dimension of
d\omega_{\kappa}
is

\displaystyle N=2|\kappa|+d,\qquad\hbox{where}\quad|\kappa|=\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}k(\alpha).

It is also noted that
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
equipped with the Euclidean distance
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mapsto|x-y|
and with the measure
\omega_{\kappa}
,
is a space of homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
8
]
.
For
0<p<\infty
, the space
L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such
that
\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}<+\infty
, where
\|f\|^{p}_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f|^{p}\,d\omega_{\kappa}
and
c_{\kappa}^{-1}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}d\omega_{\kappa}
.
L_{\kappa}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is identical to the usual
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

A typical example is
G=S_{d}
(the type
A_{d-1}
case), the symmetric group in
d
elements, a root system of which is given by
R=\{\pm(e_{i}-e_{j}):\,1\leq i<j\leq d\}
. Since
S_{d}
has only one orbit in
R
, a multiplicity function
\kappa
is constant. The Dunkl operators for a given
\kappa\geq 0
have the form

\displaystyle D_{j}=\partial_{j}+\kappa\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{1-\sigma_{ij}}{x_{j}-x_{i}},\qquad j=1,2,\cdots,d,

where
\sigma_{ij}(\dots,x_{i},\dots,x_{j},\dots)=(\dots,x_{j},\dots,x_{i},\dots)
.

In the general setting, the Laplacian
\Delta_{\kappa}
associated to a root system
R
and a multiplicity function
\kappa
, called the
\kappa
-Laplacian, is defined by
\Delta_{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{j}^{2}
, which can be explicitly expressed by (cf.
[
10
]
)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\kappa}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{j}^{2}+2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\left(\frac{\langle\alpha,\nabla\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-\frac{1-\sigma_{\alpha}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}\right),

where
\nabla
is the usual gradient operator on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Note that the operator
-\Delta_{\kappa}
is well defined on
{\mathscr{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(the Schwartz space), symmetric and positive on
L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and has a unique positive self-adjoint extension (cf.
[
2
]
).

We focus on the “analytical” presentation of related theories, because in Dunkl’s setting there are the relevant Fourier transform, the generalized translation, Green’s formula, etc, and we expect that in this way one can better clarify the treatment of some problems, and make more in-depth and extensive research, or in other words, extend the theory of harmonic analysis as far as possible to the Dunkl setting. For example, in a recent work

a Lusin-type area integral is defined by

\displaystyle\left(S_{a,h}u\right)(x)=\left(\iint_{\Gamma_{a}^{h}(0)}\left[\tau_{x}(\Delta_{\tilde{\kappa}}u^{2})\right](x_{0},t)\,x_{0}^{1-d-2|\kappa|}\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\right)^{1/2}

{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
.

In the present paper we take up the
\kappa
-Riesz transforms
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq d
) and the Hardy spaces
H_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
from the so-called analytic point of view. These transforms are treated in conjunction with the associated conjugate Poisson integrals, which allows us to borrow some conclusions about the generalized harmonic functions and yields a better understanding of them. We give a direct proof of the boundedness of the
\kappa
-Riesz transforms from
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to the space
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of functions of bounded mean oscillation with respect to the measure
d\omega_{\kappa}
. The major portion of the the paper is to prove that the dual of the Hardy spaces
H_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
can be realized by a class of functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, denoted by
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, that consists of the underlying functions of a certain type of Carleson measures associated with
d\omega_{\kappa}
.
The proof is based on the original ideas of Fefferman and Stein
[
24
]
and does not depend on the atomic decomposition. As a corollary we obtain the Fefferman-Stein decomposition of functions in
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Our argument naturally implies that
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a subclass of
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and the converse of this inclusion is a consequence of the atomic decomposition in
[
22
]
.

in the Dunkl setting, denoted by

, and the “real” Hardy space

are contained also in Section 3. That the

into the space

2.1
The Dunkl kernel and the Dunkl transform

As in the first section, for a root system
R
let
G
be the associated reflection group and
\kappa
a given nonnegative multiplicity function. As shown in
[
12
]
, there exists a unique degree-of-homogeneity-preserving linear
isomorphism
V_{\kappa}
on polynomials, which intertwines the associated commutative algebra of Dunkl operators and the algebra of usual partial differential operators, namely,

D_{j}V_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}\partial_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq d
.
V_{\kappa}
commutes with the group action of
G
. (For a thorough analysis on
V_{\kappa}
with general
\kappa
, see
[
14
]
.)
It was proved in
[
45
]
that
V_{\kappa}
is positive on the space of polynomials, and moreover, for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
there exists a unique (Borel) probability measure
\mu^{\kappa}_{x}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

V_{\kappa}f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)\,d\mu^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi),

where
\mu^{\kappa}_{x}
is of compact support with

\displaystyle\hbox{supp}\,\mu^{\kappa}_{x}\subseteq\hbox{co}\,\{\sigma(x):\,\,\sigma\in G\},

(2)

the convex hull of the orbit of
x
under
G
.
The intertwining operator
V_{\kappa}
has an extension to
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and establishes a homeomorphism of this space (see
[
57
,
58
]
). For
\delta>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
£¬ we have the distribution estimate (see
[
31
]
)

\displaystyle\int_{\langle x,\xi\rangle>|x|^{2}-\delta^{2}}\,d\mu^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi)\asymp\frac{\delta^{2|\kappa|+d}}{|{\mathbb{B}}(x,\delta)|_{\kappa}},

which reveals the behavior of the representing measure
\mu^{\kappa}_{x}
near the point
x
.

Associated with
G
and
\kappa
, the Dunkl kernel is defined by

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(x,z)=V_{\kappa}\left(e^{\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\right)(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{\langle\xi,z\rangle}\,d\mu^{\kappa}_{x}(\xi),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,\,z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}.

According to
[
42
]
, for fixed
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
,
x\mapsto E_{\kappa}(x,z)
may
be characterized as the unique analytic solution of the system

\displaystyle D^{x}_{j}E_{\kappa}(x,z)=z_{j}E_{\kappa}(x,z),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,\,\,j=1,\dots,d,

(3)

with the initial value
E_{\kappa}(0,z)=1
; and
E_{\kappa}
has a unique holomorphic extension to
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and is symmetric in its arguments (see
[
30
,
42
]
). Furthermore (cf.
[
30
,
12
,
45
]
), for
z,w\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\sigma\in G
, one has

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(\lambda z,w)=E_{\kappa}(z,\lambda w),\qquad E_{\kappa}(\sigma(z),\sigma(w))=E_{\kappa}(z,w),

(4)

and for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, and all multi-indices
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d})
,

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{z}^{\nu}E_{\kappa}(x,z)\right|\leq|x|^{|\nu|}\max_{\sigma\in G}e^{{\rm Re}\,\langle\sigma(x),z\rangle}.

(5)

For
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, its Dunkl transform is defined by

\displaystyle\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)E_{\kappa}(-i\xi,x)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x),\qquad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The Dunkl transform
{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}
commutes with the
G
-action, and shares many of the important properties with the
usual Fourier transform (see
[
13
,
30
,
45
,
48
]
), part of which are listed as follows.

Proposition 2.1

(i)
If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then
{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}f\in C_{0}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and
\|{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}f\|_{C_{0}}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}}
.

(ii)

(Inversion)
If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

such that
{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then

f(x)=[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)](-x)
.

(iii)
For
f\in{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, we have
[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(D_{j}f)](\xi)=i\xi_{j}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(\xi)
,
[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(x_{j}f)](\xi)=i[D_{j}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)](\xi)
for

and

is a homeomorphism of

(iv)

(product formula)
For

.

(v)

(Plancherel)
There exists a unique extension of

{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}
to

with

\|{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}=\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{2}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}^{-1}f=({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(-\cdot)
.

(vi)
If

is radial, then its Dunkl transform

is also radial.

(vii)
If
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cup L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

and

[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(f(a\cdot))](\xi)=|a|^{-2|\kappa|-d}({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f)(a^{-1}\xi)

\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
a\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
.

(7)

3.1

In what follows, associated to the reflection group

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the multiplicity function

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tilde{\kappa}}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\partial_{j}^{2}+2\sum_{\alpha\in R_{+}}\kappa(\alpha)\left(\frac{\langle\alpha,\nabla\rangle}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-\frac{1-\sigma_{\alpha}}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}\right),

is the gradient operator on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

Since the multiplicity value associated to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is zero, the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\otimes G
-invariance of the domain
\Omega
in
[
41

and Proposition
2.12
can be relaxed to the

-invariance. We reformulate the Green formulas in
[
41
, Theorem 4.11]
and
[

, Proposition 3.8]
as follows.

Let

be a bounded, regular, and

Proposition 3.7

\displaystyle\frac{|t_{j}-t_{j}^{\prime}|}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}}\left[\frac{x_{0}+d(x,t)}{x_{0}^{2}+|x-t|^{2}}+\frac{|x_{j}-t_{j}^{\prime}|}{x_{0}^{2}+|x-\tilde{t}|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{x_{0}+|x-t|}\frac{1}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}}.

Again by letting
x_{0}\rightarrow 0+
, the estimate in (
42
) is proved for
d(x,t)\geq 2|t-t^{\prime}|
.

Corollary 4.6

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{d(x,t)>2|t-t^{\prime}|}|K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t^{\prime})|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\leq C,\qquad t,t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(45)

Proof.

Let us first consider the
\kappa
-Riesz kernels
K_{j}(x,t)
(
1\leq j\leq d
) themselves. Applying (
42
) and in view of that (see
[
32
, (32)]
)

\displaystyle\left|B(x,d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}\asymp\left|B(t,d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa},

(46)

we have, for
t\neq t^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\int_{d(x,t)>2|t-t^{\prime}|}|K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x,t^{\prime})|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell}|t-t^{\prime}|<d(x,t)\leq 2^{\ell+1}|t-t^{\prime}|}\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|}{|x-t|}\frac{1}{\left|B(t,d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\frac{1}{\left|B(t,2^{\ell}|t-t^{\prime}|)\right|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B(t,2^{\ell+1}|t-t^{\prime}|))}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x),

(47)

where

{\mathcal{O}}(B(x^{\prime},r))=\{x:\,d(x,x^{\prime})<r\}

denotes the
G
-orbit of the ball
B(x^{\prime},r)
. It follows immediately that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{d(x,t)>2|t-t^{\prime}|}|K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x,t^{\prime})|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\leq C,\qquad t,t^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

(48)

We now turn to the associated estimate for
K_{j}^{\epsilon}
. Assume that
d(x,t)>2|t-t^{\prime}|
. It is obvious that
|d(x,t)-d(x,t^{\prime})|\leq|t-t^{\prime}|
, and so
d(x,t)/2\leq d(x,t^{\prime})\leq 2d(x,t)
.

If
d(x,t)\leq\epsilon
and
d(x,t^{\prime})\leq\epsilon
, then
K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)=K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t^{\prime})=0
; if
d(x,t)>\epsilon
and
d(x,t^{\prime})>\epsilon
, then
K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t^{\prime})=K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x,t^{\prime})
; if
d(x,t)>\epsilon
and
d(x,t^{\prime})\leq\epsilon
, then
K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t^{\prime})=K_{j}(x,t)
and
\epsilon<d(x,t)\leq 2d(x,t^{\prime})\leq 2\epsilon
; and similarly, if
d(x,t)\leq\epsilon
and
d(x,t^{\prime})>\epsilon
, then
K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t^{\prime})=-K_{j}(x,t^{\prime})
and
\epsilon<d(x,t^{\prime})\leq 2d(x,t)\leq 2\epsilon
.
Thus
\int_{d(x,t)>2|t-t^{\prime}|}|K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t^{\prime})|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)
is dominated by

\displaystyle\int_{d(x,t)>2|t-t^{\prime}|}|K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x,t^{\prime})|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{\epsilon<d(x,t)\leq 2\epsilon}|K_{j}(x,t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)+\int_{\epsilon<d(x,t^{\prime})\leq 2\epsilon}|K_{j}(x,t^{\prime})|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

From (
48
) the first term above is bounded by a constant; while, by use of (
41
) and (
46
), the second term is controlled by a multiple of

\displaystyle\int_{\epsilon<d(x,t)\leq 2\epsilon}\frac{|x_{j}-t_{j}|}{\left|B(t,d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}}\frac{d(x,t)}{|x-t|^{2}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{\left|B(t,\epsilon)\right|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B(t,2\epsilon))}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\lesssim 1;

and the same estimate holds for the third term. Summing these concludes (
45
).

We are now in a position to consider the operators
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}
, for
0<\epsilon<N<\infty
, defined by

\displaystyle({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f)(x)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)K_{j}^{\epsilon,N}(x,t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(49)

where

K_{j}^{\epsilon,N}(x,t)=K_{j}^{\epsilon}(x,t)-K_{j}^{N}(x,t).

Lemma 4.7

There exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\epsilon,N
and
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f\|_{*,\kappa}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(50)

Proof.

We fix a ball
B=B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})
and decompose
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f\chi_{{\mathcal{O}}(3B)}
and
f_{2}=f\chi_{[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}}
.

By Corollary
3.15
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}
is bounded from
L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to itself with a bound of operator norms independent of
\epsilon,N
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{B}|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{1}|d\omega_{\kappa}\leq|B|_{\kappa}^{1/2}\left(\int_{B}|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{1}|^{2}d\omega_{\kappa}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|B|_{\kappa}^{1/2}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(3B)}|f_{1}|^{2}d\omega_{\kappa}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim|B|_{\kappa}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(51)

Passing to
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{2}
, for
x\in B
we have

\displaystyle\left|({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{2})(x)-({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{2})(x^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f_{2}(t)||K_{j}^{\epsilon,N}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon,N}(x^{\prime},t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}}|K_{j}^{\epsilon,N}(x,t)-K_{j}^{\epsilon,N}(x^{\prime},t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

It is noted that
K_{j}(x,t)=-K_{j}(t,x)
by (
31
), and then by Corollary
4.6
,

\displaystyle\left|({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{2})(x)-({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{2})(x^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad x\in B.

(52)

Combining (
4
) and (
52
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{B}|({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f)(x)-({\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f_{2})(x^{\prime})|d\omega_{\kappa}\lesssim|B|_{\kappa}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Therefore
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f\in{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}
, and (
50
) holds.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Assume
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and fix a ball
B=B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})
. Note that
|d(x,t)-d(x^{\prime},t)|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\delta
for
x\in B
.

It follows from (
39
) that, for
x\in B
,

\displaystyle\left|(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x)-(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|K_{j}^{N}(x,t)-K_{j}^{N}(x^{\prime},t)\right|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

(53)

Adopting closely the argument in the proof of Corollary
4.6
, the integral above is controlled by

\displaystyle\int_{d(x,t)>N}|K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x^{\prime},t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{N<d(x,t)\leq N+\delta}|K_{j}(x,t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)+\int_{N<d(x^{\prime},t)\leq N+\delta}|K_{j}(x^{\prime},t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

(54)

We consider the case for
N\geq 2(|x^{\prime}|+\delta)
. The first term in (
4
), after a similar treatment in (
4
), is bounded by a multiple of

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{\ell-1}N<d(x,t)\leq 2^{\ell}N}\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{|x-t|}\frac{1}{\left|B(x,d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\lesssim\frac{\delta}{N}.

(55)

As for the second term in (
4
), using (
41
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{N<d(x,t)\leq N+\delta}|K_{j}(x,t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B(x,N)\right|_{\kappa}}\int_{N<d(x,t)\leq N+\delta}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B(x/N,1)\right|_{\kappa}}\int_{1<d(x/N,t)\leq 1+\delta/N}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t);

and since
B(x/N,1)\supseteq B(x^{\prime}/N,1/2)
and
|d(x^{\prime}/N,t)-d(x/N,t)|\leq\delta/N
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{N<d(x,t)\leq N+\delta}|K_{j}(x,t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\lesssim\frac{1}{\left|B(x^{\prime}/N,1/2)\right|_{\kappa}}\int_{1-\delta/N<d(x^{\prime}/N,t)\leq 1+2\delta/N}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

The same estimate holds for the third term in (
4
). Inserting them and (
55
) into (
4
), and then (
53
), asserts that
(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x)-(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x^{\prime})\rightarrow 0
uniformly for
x\in B
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

Since, from (
39
) and (
49
),

\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon}f-\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f={\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon,N}f,

by Lemma
4.7
and (
38
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|_{\kappa}}\int_{B}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon}f-\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f-(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon}f)_{B}+(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)_{B}\right|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}.

(56)

But, with
a=(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x^{\prime})
,

(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x)-(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)_{B}=(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f)(x)-a-[\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{N}f-a]_{B}

tends to zero uniformly for
x\in B
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, as just proved. Thus from (
56
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|_{\kappa}}\int_{B}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon}f-(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}^{\epsilon}f)_{B}\right|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}.

Finally letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0+
, Proposition
4.3
asserts
|B|_{\kappa}^{-1}\int_{B}\left|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f-(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f)_{B}\right|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
. Thus, in view of (
38
),
\|\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\|_{*,\kappa}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and the proof of Theorem
4.4
is completed.

5
The associated Carleson measures

As usual, for a ball
B=B_{r}(x^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the associated tent
T(B)
is the closed set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
given by

T(B)=\{(x_{0},x):\,|x-x^{\prime}|\leq r-x_{0}\}.

Definition 5.1

A Borel measure
d\nu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
is called a
\kappa
-Carleson measure if there exists some
c>0
such that for all balls
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\iint_{T(B)}|d\nu|\leq c|B|_{\kappa};

and
\|d\nu\|_{\kappa}:=\sup_{B}|B|_{\kappa}^{-1}\iint_{T(B)}|d\nu|
is named the
\kappa
-Carleson norm of
d\nu
.

As in the classical case, the property underlying the definition of
\kappa
-Carleson measures is not limited to balls. Indeed we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

If
d\nu
is a
\kappa
-Carleson measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
and
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is open, then

\iint_{\widehat{E}}|d\nu|\lesssim\|d\nu\|_{\kappa}|E|_{\kappa},

where

\widehat{E}=\{(r,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}:\,\,B_{r}(x)\subset E\}
.

The proof of the lemma is a straightforward adaptation of that of
[
16
, Lemma 9.4]
.
A
\kappa
-Carleson measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
can also be characterized as a measure
d\nu
for which the
\kappa
-Poisson integral defines a bounded operator from
L_{\kappa}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+},|d\nu|)
for
1<p<\infty
.

Theorem 5.3

Assume that
d\nu
is a Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
.

(i)
If
d\nu
is a
\kappa
-Carleson measure, then for every
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}}|(Pf)(x_{0},x)|^{p}\,|d\nu|\lesssim\|d\nu\|_{\kappa}\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}^{p},\qquad f\in L^{p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

(ii)
If for some
p\in[1,\infty)
, there holds
\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}}|(Pf)(x_{0},x)|^{p}\,|d\nu|\leq c\|f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}^{p}
for
f\in L^{p}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, where
c>0
is independent of
f
, then
d\nu
is a
\kappa
-Carleson measure.

Proof.

First suppose that
d\nu
is a
\kappa
-Carleson measure.
Let
E_{\lambda}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:(P^{*}_{1}f)(x)>\lambda\}
. It then follows that
\{(x_{0},x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}:\,|(Pf)(x_{0},x)|>\lambda\}\subset\widehat{E_{\lambda}}
,
and by Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}}|(Pf)(x_{0},x)|^{p}\,|d\nu|

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}\iint_{\{(x_{0},x):\,|(Pf)(x_{0},x)|>\lambda\}}|d\nu|\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim p\|d\nu\|_{\kappa}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|E_{\lambda}|_{\kappa}d\lambda,

that is identical with
\|d\nu\|_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(P^{*}_{1}f)(x)^{p}d\omega_{\kappa}
. Part (i) is immediate by Proposition
3.4
.

Conversely, suppose that
d\nu
satisfies the condition in part (ii). For a ball
B=B_{r}(x^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and for
(x_{0},x)\in T(B)
, using the fact
B_{x_{0}}(x)\subset B
and the first inequality in (
43
) we have

\displaystyle(P\chi_{B})(x_{0},x)\geq c_{\kappa}\int_{B_{x_{0}}(x)}(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\gtrsim\int_{B_{x_{0}}(x)}\frac{x_{0}}{x_{0}+|x-t|}\frac{d\omega_{\kappa}(t)}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+|x-t|)\right|_{\kappa}},

which implies
(P\chi_{B})(x_{0},x)\gtrsim 1
for
(x_{0},x)\in T(B)
. Consequently

\displaystyle\iint_{T(B)}|d\nu|\lesssim\iint_{T(B)}|(P\chi_{B})(x_{0},x)|^{p}|d\nu|\lesssim\|\chi_{B}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{p}}^{p}=|B|_{\kappa}.

Hence
d\nu
is a
\kappa
-Carleson measure.

We now introduce a class of functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in terms of
\kappa
-Carleson measures.

Definition 5.4

A function
\varphi\in L_{\kappa,{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is said to be in
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if it satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|\varphi(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{2|\kappa|+d+1}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)<\infty;

(57)

(ii)

d\nu_{\kappa,\varphi}(x_{0},x)=x_{0}|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)|^{2}\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

is a
\kappa
-Carleson measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}
, where
\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}=(\partial_{0},D_{1},\dots,D_{d})
is the
\tilde{\kappa}
-gradient,
and
u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)
is the
\kappa
-Poisson integral of
\varphi
.

Moreover the norm of
\varphi
in
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is defined by
\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}=\|d\nu_{\kappa,\varphi}\|_{\kappa}^{1/2}
.

Proposition 5.5

A bounded function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
belongs to
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

For a fixed ball
B=B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\iint_{T(B)}d\nu_{\kappa,\varphi\chi_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}}(x_{0},x)\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}}x_{0}|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi\chi_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}}(x_{0},x)|^{2}\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(x);

(58)

and since for
f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cup L_{\kappa}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, by (
3
) and (
18
),

\displaystyle\left[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(\partial_{x_{0}}u_{f}(x_{0},\cdot))\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=-|\xi|e^{-x_{0}|\xi|}\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi),

(59)

\displaystyle\left[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(D_{j}u_{f}(x_{0},\cdot))\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=i\xi_{j}e^{-x_{0}|\xi|}\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi),\quad j=1,\cdots,d,

(60)

the Plancherel formula asserts that the integral on the right hand side of (
58
) is equal to
2^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\varphi\chi_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}|^{2}d\omega_{\kappa}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|B|_{\kappa}
.

Nevertheless, from (
16
),

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi\chi_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}}(x_{0},x)|\leq c_{\kappa}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]\right|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),

(61)

and by
[
5
, Proposition 5.1]
(also
[
32
, (32)]
),

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]\right|\lesssim\frac{(x_{0}+d(t,x))^{-1}}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(t,x))\right|_{\kappa}}.

(62)

Since for
(x_{0},x)\in T(B)
and
t\in[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}
,
d(t,x)\geq d(t,x^{\prime})/2\geq\delta
, it follows from (
61
) and (
62
) that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi\chi_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}\frac{d\omega_{\kappa}(t)}{d(t,x)\left|B(x,d(t,x))\right|_{\kappa}}\lesssim\frac{\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta}.

(63)

Thus

\iint_{T(B)}d\nu_{\kappa,\varphi\chi_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}}(x_{0},x)\lesssim\left(\frac{\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\delta}\right)^{2}\iint_{T(B)}x_{0}\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|B|_{\kappa},

so that
\iint_{T(B)}d\nu_{\kappa,\varphi}(x_{0},x)\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}|B|_{\kappa}
, as desired.

Note that, for the symbol “
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}
”, “BM” means “bounded mean” since, at least, a function
\varphi
with the property
\sup_{A>0}A^{-d-2|\kappa|}\int_{|x|\leq A}|\varphi|d\omega_{\kappa}<\infty
satisfies condition (i), and “C” certainly indicates that it is connected with Carleson measures as stated in condition (ii).

At this stage we shall prove an analog of Theorem
4.4
for the space
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, that is necessary in characterizing the dual of the Hardy space
H_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in the next section.

Theorem 5.6

The
\kappa
-Riesz transform
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
defined by (
39
) and (
40
) is bounded from
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, where
j=1,\cdots,d
.

If the multiplicity function
\kappa
is identical to zero, i.e.,
\kappa(\alpha)=0
for all
\alpha\in R
, it is well known that
{\text{BMC}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})={\text{BMO}}_{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(see
[
24
, Theorem 3]
); but for general nonnegative
\kappa
, a partial relation of the two classes
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a consequence of the duality of
H_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to be proved in the next section. It is uncertain whether there was a direct proof of the converse of this inclusion.

In proving Theorem
5.6
, we shall need an estimate of the
\tilde{\kappa}
-gradient of the conjugate
\kappa
-Poisson kernels
(\tau_{x}Q^{(j)}_{x_{0}})(-t)
(
j=1,2,\cdots,d
) given below.

Lemma 5.7

The conjugate
\kappa
-Poisson kernels
(\tau_{x}Q^{(j)}_{x_{0}})(-t)
(
j=1,2,\cdots,d
) satisfy

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}Q^{(j)}_{x_{0}})(-t)]\right|\lesssim\frac{(x_{0}+d(t,x))^{-1}}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(t,x))\right|_{\kappa}}.

(64)

Proof.

Define the function
U_{x_{0}}(x,t)
by

\displaystyle U_{x_{0}}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x_{0}^{2}}{4v}}\,h_{v}(x,t)\,\frac{dv}{v^{3/2}}=\frac{1}{x_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}h_{x_{0}^{2}/4u}(x,t)\,\frac{du}{\sqrt{u}},

(65)

where
h_{v}(x,t)
is the heat kernel associated to the Dunkl operators given by (
25
).

We are going to show, for
i=1,2,\cdots,d
,

\displaystyle\left|D_{i}^{x}\left[U_{x_{0}}(x,t)\right]\right|\lesssim\frac{(x_{0}+d(x,t))^{-1}}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}}\frac{|x_{i}-t_{i}|}{x_{0}^{2}+|x-t|^{2}}.

(66)

From (
27
) and (
65
),

\displaystyle D_{i}^{x}\left[U_{x_{0}}(x,t)\right]=\frac{2(t_{i}-x_{i})}{x_{0}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}h_{x_{0}^{2}/4u}(x,t)\sqrt{u}\,du,

(67)

and making use of
[

, Theorem 4.2]
in our case, we give a variant of the
\kappa
-subharmonic inequality in
[
5
, Theorem 6.3]
.
Similarly to (
81
), for
q>0
and
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tilde{\kappa}}(|U_{F}|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2}=\frac{q}{(|U_{F}|^{2}+\epsilon)^{1-q/2}}\left(V+\sum_{j=0}^{d}|\partial_{j}U_{F}|^{2}+\frac{q-2}{|U_{F}|^{2}+\epsilon}\sum_{j=0}^{d}\langle\partial_{j}U_{F},U_{F}\rangle^{2}\right),

and choose
q\in(0,1)
satisfying (
83
) (see
[
5
, (6.12)]
), independent of
F
and
\epsilon
, to conclude

\Delta_{\tilde{\kappa}}(|U_{F}|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2}\geq 0.

For
\delta,\epsilon>0
let
f_{\delta,\epsilon}(x)=(|U_{F}(\delta,x)|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2}
and set
u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)=(Pf_{\delta,\epsilon})(x_{0},x)
, the
\kappa
-Poisson integral of
f_{\delta,\epsilon}
.
For
F\in{\mathscr{H}}_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+})
, we have
|U_{F}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim(1+x_{0}+|x|)^{-2|\kappa|-d-2}
, and
u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)
is a continuous extension to
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}}
of
f_{\delta,\epsilon}(x)
.
Consider the function

V_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{0},x)=(|U_{F}(\delta+x_{0},x)|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2}-u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x).

Obviously
V_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{0},x)
is continuous on
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}}
and
\Delta_{\tilde{\kappa}}V_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{0},x)\geq 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}
; moreover
V_{\delta,\epsilon}(0,x)=0
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

For given
\eta>0
, choose
M>0
such that

\displaystyle|U_{F}(x_{0},x)|<\eta\qquad\hbox{whenever}\quad|(x_{0},x)|\geq M.

(88)

It then follows that
|f_{\delta,\epsilon}(x)|\lesssim\eta+\epsilon
for
|x|\geq M
.

Now for
|(x_{0},x)|\geq 2M
, from (
16
) and (
43
) we have

\displaystyle|u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim\eta+\epsilon+\int_{|t|\leq M}\frac{d\omega_{\kappa}(t)}{\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(x,t))\right|_{\kappa}};

but
x_{0}+d(x,t)\geq x_{0}+|x|-|t|\geq|(x_{0},x)|-|t|\geq|(x_{0},x)|/2
for
|t|\leq M
, so that

\displaystyle|u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim\eta+\epsilon+\left(\frac{M}{|(x_{0},x)|}\right)^{2|\kappa|+d}.

Choose
M^{\prime}>2M
so that
(M/M^{\prime})^{2|\kappa|+d}<\eta
, and then for
|(x_{0},x)|\geq M^{\prime}
,
|u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim\eta+\epsilon
.
From this and (
88
) we get

\displaystyle|V_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim\eta+\epsilon\qquad\hbox{whenever}\quad|(x_{0},x)|\geq M^{\prime}.

We apply the maximum principle
[
44
, Theorem 4.2]
to
V_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{0},x)
on the upper half ball
=B^{+}((0,0),M^{\prime})
, and then obtain
|V_{\delta,\epsilon}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim\eta+\epsilon
for all
(x_{0},x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}
. Letting
\eta\rightarrow 0+
gives

\displaystyle(|U_{F}(\delta+x_{0},x)|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2}-u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)\lesssim\epsilon\qquad\hbox{for all}\quad(x_{0},x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}.

(89)

It is easy to see that
f_{\delta,\epsilon}(x)=(|U_{F}(\delta,x)|^{2}+\epsilon)^{q/2}
converges to
|U_{F}(\delta,x)|^{q}
uniformly for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0+
, and so

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}u_{f_{\delta,\epsilon}}(x_{0},x)=u_{|U_{F}(\delta,\cdot)|^{q}}(x_{0},x)
for
(x_{0},x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}
. This and (
89
) lead to

\displaystyle|U_{F}(\delta+x_{0},x)|^{q}\leq u_{|U_{F}(\delta,\cdot)|^{q}}(x_{0},x)\qquad\hbox{for all}\quad(x_{0},x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}.

(90)

Since
|U_{F}(\delta,\cdot)|^{q}\in L^{1/q}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
1/q>1
and
\sup_{\delta>0}\||U_{F}(\delta,\cdot)|^{q}\|_{L^{1/q}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\kappa}^{1}}^{q}
.
The *-weak compactness of
L^{1/q}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
implies
that, there exists a sequence
\delta_{n}\rightarrow 0+
such that

U_{F}(\delta_{n},\cdot)
converges *-weakly to a nonnegative function
g\in L^{1/q}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Thus from (
90
) we get

\displaystyle|U_{F}(x_{0},x)|^{q}\leq u_{g}(x_{0},x)\qquad\hbox{for all}\quad(x_{0},x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}.

Now since
\|u_{|U_{F}(\delta_{n},\cdot)|^{q}}(x_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{1/q}_{\kappa}}\leq\||U_{F}(\delta_{n},\cdot)|^{q}\|_{L^{1/q}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\kappa}^{1}}^{q}
, Fatou’s lemma asserts that
\|u_{g}(x_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{1/q}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\kappa}^{1}}^{q}
, and letting
x_{0}\rightarrow 0+
gives
\|g\|_{L^{1/q}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\kappa}^{1}}^{q}
.

Finally from (
87
) we have

\displaystyle J_{3}\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}}x_{0}u_{g}(x_{0},x)^{1/q}|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)dx_{0},

and so, by Theorems
5.3
,
J_{3}\lesssim\|d\nu_{\kappa,\varphi}\|_{\kappa}\|g\|_{L^{1/q}_{\kappa}}^{1/q}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}^{2}\|f\|_{H_{\kappa}^{1}}
. Substituting this into (
86
) proves (
84
), and then, the proof of the lemma is completed.

Lemma 6.4

If
f\in H_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\varphi\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)\varphi(x)d\omega_{\kappa}(x)=2\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}}x_{0}\big\langle\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f}(x_{0},x),\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)\big\rangle d\omega_{\kappa}(x)dx_{0},

(91)

where
u_{f}(x_{0},x)
and
u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)
are the
\kappa
-Poisson integrals of
f
and
\varphi
respectively.

Proof.

From (
57
) it is obvious that the integral on the left hand side of (
91
) converges absolutely, and so does the one on the right hand side by Lemma
6.3
.

For
x_{0}>0
we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t)\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)

which is legitimate on account of (
43
) and (
44
). By use of (
62
),

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]\right|\lesssim\left(1+\frac{1+|x|}{x_{0}}\right)^{2|\kappa|+d+1}\frac{1}{(1+|t|)^{2|\kappa|+d+1}},

so that, in view of (
57
),

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\varphi(t)|\left|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]\right|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\lesssim\left(1+\frac{1+|x|}{x_{0}}\right)^{2|\kappa|+d+1}.

Moreover for
f\in H_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1+x_{0}+|x|)^{2|\kappa|+d+1}\left|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f}(x_{0},x)\right|d\omega_{\kappa}(x)
is finite.
Now Fubini’s theorem yields

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\langle\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f}(x_{0},x),\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}(x_{0},x)\big\rangle d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big\langle\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f}(x_{0},x),\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]\big\rangle d\omega_{\kappa}(x)d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

(92)

By Proposition
2.1
(iii) and (
17
), the Dunkl transform of
x\mapsto\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}^{(x_{0},x)}[(\tau_{x}P_{x_{0}})(-t)]
is

e^{-x_{0}|\xi|}E_{\kappa}(-it,\xi)(-|\xi|,i\xi_{1},\cdots,i\xi_{d}),

and by (
59
) and (
60
),
\left[{\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}(\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f}(x_{0},\cdot))\right](\xi)=e^{-x_{0}|\xi|}\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi)(-|\xi|,i\xi_{1},\cdots,i\xi_{d})
.
Thus the Plancherel formula (Propositions
2.1
(v)) implies that the inner integral on the right hand side of (
6
) becomes

\displaystyle 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}e^{-2x_{0}|\xi|}\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi)E_{\kappa}(it,\xi)d\omega_{\kappa}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}c_{\kappa}^{-1}\partial_{x_{0}}^{2}[u_{f}(2x_{0},t)].

(93)

In addition, by Lemma
6.3
the integral on the right hand side of (
91
) converges absolutely, and then, Fubini’s theorem again gives

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}}x_{0}\big\langle\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f},\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}\big\rangle\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t)\partial_{x_{0}}^{2}[u_{f}(2x_{0},t)]\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)dx_{0}.

Noting that
|\Delta_{\kappa}^{t}[u_{f}(2x_{0},t)]|\lesssim(1+x_{0}+|t|)^{-2|\kappa|-d-3}
and in view of (
57
), further changing order of the integration above is permitted, and hence

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{1+d}}x_{0}\big\langle\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f},\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{\varphi}\big\rangle d\omega_{\kappa}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}x_{0}\partial_{x_{0}}^{2}[u_{f}(2x_{0},t)]\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

(94)

Finally, since

it follows from (

) that

\int_{0}^{\infty}x_{0}\partial_{x_{0}}^{2}[u_{f}(2x_{0},t)]\,dx_{0}=f(t),

and inserting this into (

) finishes the proof of (

).

Theorem 6.5

If
\varphi\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then the linear functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)\varphi(x)d\omega_{\kappa}(x),

(95)

initially defined for
f\in H_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, has a unique bounded extension to
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
\|{\mathcal{L}}\|\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}
, where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\varphi
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that, for
f\in H_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)\varphi(x)d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\Big|\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\|f\|_{H_{\kappa}^{1}},

but that is direct consequence of Lemmas
6.3
and
6.4
. The proof is completed.

Theorem 6.6

If
\mathcal{L}
is a continuous linear functional on
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then there exists a unique element
\varphi\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
so that
\mathcal{L}
can be realized by (
95
),
at least for
f\in H_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\leq C^{\prime}\|{\mathcal{L}}\|
, where the constant
C^{\prime}>0
is independent of
\mathcal{L}
.
Moreover, such a function
\varphi\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
can be expressed as

\varphi=\varphi_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\varphi_{j},

where
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\varphi_{d}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proof.

As in
[
24
, p. 145]
, we regard
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as a closed subspace of the direct sum
X:=L^{1}_{\kappa}\oplus L^{1}_{\kappa}\oplus\cdots\oplus L^{1}_{\kappa}
of
d+1
copies of
L^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. If we set

X=\{(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{d}):\,\,f_{j}\in L^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\,\,j=0,1,\cdots d\}
with the norm
\|(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})\|=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}_{\kappa}}
, it is known that the dual of
X
is equivalent to
L^{\infty}\oplus L^{\infty}\oplus\cdots\oplus L^{\infty}
. We consider the subspace
X_{0}
of
X
given by

X_{0}=\{(f,{\mathcal{R}}_{1}f,\cdots,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}f):\,\,f\in L^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\in L^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\,\,j=1,\cdots d\}.

It is easy to see that
X_{0}
is a closed subspace of
X
, and there is a natural isometric mapping of
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to
X_{0}
, that is,
f\mapsto(f,{\mathcal{R}}_{1}f,\cdots,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}f)
. Thus if
\mathcal{L}
is a continuous linear functional on
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the one on
X_{0}
identified by it, by Hahn-Banach theorem, extends to a continuous linear functional on
X
, denoted by
\mathcal{L}
too. Therefore, there exist
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,\varphi_{d}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

{\mathcal{L}}(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{j}\varphi_{j}d\omega_{\kappa},\qquad(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{d})\in X,

and
\|{\mathcal{L}}\|\asymp\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
In particular, such a representation holds if
(f,{\mathcal{R}}_{1}f,\cdots,{\mathcal{R}}_{d}f)\in X_{0}
, or equivalently, for
f\in H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

{\mathcal{L}}f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{0}f\,d\omega_{\kappa}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{j}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\,d\omega_{\kappa}
.
If, furthermore, we apply this to
f\in H_{\kappa,0}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and then appeal to Lemma
5.8
, to get

{\mathcal{L}}f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f\left(\varphi_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\varphi_{j}\right)\,d\omega_{\kappa}.

According to Theorem
5.6
, the function
\varphi=\varphi_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\varphi_{j}
is in
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\asymp\|{\mathcal{L}}\|
. The uniqueness is obvious, and the proof of the theorem is completed.

Finally we summarize the conclusions in the above theorems as follows.

Theorem 6.7

The dual of
H_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and the norm of a continuous linear functional
\mathcal{L}
on
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, realized by (
95
), is equivalent to the norm of the representing function

\varphi\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Theorem 6.8

A function
\varphi\in L_{\kappa,{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is in
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if and only if it has the representation

\varphi=\varphi_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\varphi_{j},\qquad\hbox{where}\quad\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{d}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d});

and moreover,
\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\asymp\sum_{j=0}^{d}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

7
Remarks on the spaces
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

7.1
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

By Theorems
4.4
,
6.7
and
6.8
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 7.1

The space
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is complete, and is a subclass of
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
; and moreover, for
\varphi\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\|\varphi\|_{*,\kappa}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}
.

In order to find more connections between the two spaces, we introduce a variant of the weighted
BMO
space according to the
G
-orbits of balls in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Definition 7.2

The class
{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
consists of functions
f\in L_{\kappa,{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
satisfying

\displaystyle\|f\|_{*,G}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{O}}(B)|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|f-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|\,d\omega_{\kappa}<\infty,

where the supremum ranges over all balls
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}=|{\mathcal{O}}(B)|_{\kappa}^{-1}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}f\,d\omega_{\kappa}
.

As usual,
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
represents an element of the quotient space of
{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
modulo constants.
We have the following proposition, whose proof is similar to that of Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition 7.3

There exists some
C>0
such that for all
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and for
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B_{\delta}(x^{\prime}))}|}{(\delta+d(x,x^{\prime}))|B_{\delta+d(x,x^{\prime})}(x^{\prime})|_{\kappa}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\leq\frac{C}{\delta}\|f\|_{*,G}.

The analog of the John-Nirenberg inequality is

\displaystyle\left|\left\{x\in B:|f(x)-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|>\lambda\right\}\right|_{\kappa}\leq c_{1}e^{-c_{0}\lambda/\|f\|_{*,G}}|B|_{\kappa},\qquad\lambda>0,

for
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and all balls
B
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where the constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
are independent of
f
and
B
.
A direct consequence of this inequality is that, for
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{*,G}\asymp\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{O}}(B)|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|f-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}\right)^{1/2}.

The next proposition indicates partial relations of
{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proposition 7.4

(i)
The inclusions

\displaystyle{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subseteq{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subseteq{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(96)

hold, and for
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\|f\|_{*,\kappa}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,G}
;

(ii)
if
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is
G
-invariant, then
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\|f\|_{*,\kappa}\asymp\|f\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\asymp\|f\|_{*,G}
.

Proof.

(i) By Corollary
7.1
it remains to show the first inclusion in (
96
). For
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and for a fixed ball
B=B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
write
f=f_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}+f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=(f-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)})\chi_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}
and
f_{2}=(f-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)})\chi_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}
.
Similarly to the first part of the proof of Proposition
5.5
, one has

\displaystyle\iint_{T(B)}d\nu_{\kappa,f_{1}}(x_{0},x)\leq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1+d}_{+}}x_{0}|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f_{1}}(x_{0},x)|^{2}\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f_{1}|^{2}d\omega_{\kappa}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,G}^{2}|B|_{\kappa}.

From (
16
) and (
62
) we get

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f_{2}}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}}\frac{|f(t)-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}|}{(x_{0}+d(t,x))\left|B(x,x_{0}+d(t,x))\right|_{\kappa}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t);

and since for
(x_{0},x)\in T(B)
and
t\in[{\mathcal{O}}(2B)]^{c}
,
d(t,x)\geq d(t,x^{\prime})/2\geq\delta
, it follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\tilde{\kappa}}u_{f_{2}}(x_{0},x)|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{|f(t)-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(2B)}|}{(\delta+d(t,x^{\prime}))\left|B(x,\delta+d(t,x^{\prime}))\right|_{\kappa}}\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta}\|f\|_{*,G}

by Proposition
7.3
. Thus

\iint_{T(B)}d\nu_{\kappa,f_{2}}(x_{0},x)\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{*,G}^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\iint_{T(B)}x_{0}\,dx_{0}d\omega_{\kappa}(x)\lesssim\|f\|_{*,G}^{2}|B|_{\kappa}.

Combining this with the contribution of
f_{1}
concludes
f\in{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\|f\|_{*,C_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,G}
.

(ii) If
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is
G
-invariant, for a ball
B=B_{\delta}(x^{\prime})
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{O}}(B)|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|f-f_{B}|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq\frac{1}{|B|_{\kappa}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{\sigma(B)}|f-f_{B}|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq|G|\|f\|_{*,\kappa},

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|{\mathcal{O}}(B)|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|f-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq\frac{2}{|{\mathcal{O}}(B)|_{\kappa}}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B)}|f-f_{B}|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq 2|G|\|f\|_{*,\kappa}.

Thus
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\|f\|_{*,G}\leq 2|G|\|f\|_{*,\kappa}
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

7.2
An example in the rank-one case

Now we give an example in the rank-one case, with
G={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
, that belongs to
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
, but not to
{\text{BMO}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
. In this case
d\omega_{\kappa}(x)=|x|^{2\kappa}dx
, and
B_{r}(x^{\prime})=(x^{\prime}-r,x^{\prime}+r)
for
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
and
r>0
; the single
\kappa
-Riesz transform is called the
\kappa
-Hilbert transform in
[
35
]
and here we denote it by
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
or
{\mathcal{R}}
.
We need the following lemma.

Lemma 7.5

If
f\in{\text{BMO}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
is odd on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
, then
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
.

Proof.

For
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
and
r>0
, one has
\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B_{r}(x^{\prime}))}f\,d\omega_{\kappa}=0
and so

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B_{r}(x^{\prime}))}|f-f_{{\mathcal{O}}(B_{r}(x^{\prime}))}|\,d\omega_{\kappa}=\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(B_{r}(x^{\prime}))}|f|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\geq\int_{B_{r}(x^{\prime})}|f|\,d\omega_{\kappa}.

Thus

|B_{r}(x^{\prime})|_{\kappa}^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(x^{\prime})}|f|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\lesssim\|f\|_{*,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}
, and the Lebesgue differentiation theorem asserts that for almost every
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
,
|f(x^{\prime})|\lesssim\|f\|_{*,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}
. The proof is completed.

For
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
of compact support, by Proposition
4.3
and (
39
) we have

(\tilde{\mathcal{R}}f)(x)=({\mathcal{R}}f)(x)-a

where
a=c_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{1}}f(t)K^{1}(0,t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)
. Since such an
f
is in
L^{2}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
, by
[
35
, Theorem 6.3]
its
\kappa
-Hilbert transform
({\mathcal{R}}f)(x)
can realized by

\displaystyle({\mathcal{R}}f)(x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}c_{\kappa}\int_{|t-x|>\epsilon}f(t)K(x,t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),

(97)

where, in the present case,
K(x,t)
can be represented as

\displaystyle K(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\kappa}|x+t|^{-2\kappa}}{x-t}\,{}_{2}\!F_{1}\Big[{\kappa,\kappa\atop 2\kappa+1};\frac{4xt}{|x+t|^{2}}\Big],\ \ \ \ \ \hbox{if}\ xt>0,\,t\neq x,

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{\kappa}(x-t)}{(|x|+|t|)^{2\kappa+2}}\,{}_{2}\!F_{1}\Big[{\kappa,\kappa+1\atop 2\kappa+1};\frac{4|xt|}{(|x|+|t|)^{2}}\Big],\ \ \ \ \ \hbox{if}\ xt<0,\,t\neq-x.

Here
m_{\kappa}=2^{\kappa+1/2}\Gamma(\kappa+1)/\sqrt{\pi}
, and
{}_{2}\!F_{1}[a,b;c;t]
is the Gauss hypergeometric function.

Now we consider the function
\varphi_{0}(x)=({\mathcal{R}}\chi_{[-1,1]})(x)
. It is an odd function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
and in
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
by Theorem
4.4
. When
x>1
, from (
97
) we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(x)=c_{\kappa}\int_{-1}^{1}K(x,t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\geq c_{\kappa}\int_{1/2}^{1}K(x,t)\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t),

and for
1/2<t<1
, from the first formula of
K(x,t)
above we have

\displaystyle K(x,t)\geq\frac{m_{\kappa}}{(x+t)^{2\kappa}(x-t)}\geq\frac{m_{\kappa}}{(x+1)^{2\kappa}(x-t)}.

Thus for
x>1
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(x)\geq\frac{c_{\kappa}m_{\kappa}}{(x+1)^{2\kappa}}\int_{1/2}^{1}\frac{t^{2\kappa}}{x-t}\,dt\geq\frac{c_{\kappa}m_{\kappa}}{2^{2\kappa}(x+1)^{2\kappa}}\log\left(\frac{x-1/2}{x-1}\right),

which shows that
\varphi_{0}
is unbounded on
{\mathbb{R}}^{1}
. Therefore
\varphi_{0}\notin{\text{BMO}}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{1})
by Lemma
7.5
.

7.3
The atomic decomposition of
G-
invariant functions in
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

It is well known that the atomic characterization of the usual Hardy space
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is implied by the duality of
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
{\text{BMO}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. This idea belongs to C. Fefferman. Here we consider the atomic decomposition of
G-
invariant functions in
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
according to this kind of thoughts. Such a decomposition of general functions in
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
has been proved in
[
22
]
by a different approach, see the description in Section 1.

Let us recall the concept of atoms associated with the space
({\mathbb{R}}^{d},|\cdot|,\omega_{\kappa})
of homogeneous type based upon Coifman and Weiss
[
8
]
. For
1<q\leq\infty
, a function
a\in L_{\kappa,{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is said to be a
(1,q)
-atom if there is a ball
B
such that

(i)
{\rm supp}\,a\subset B
;

(ii)
\|a\|_{L^{q}_{\kappa}}\leq|B|_{\kappa}^{q^{-1}-1}
;

(iii)
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}a\,d\omega_{\kappa}=0
.

The atomic Hardy space
H^{(1,q)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
consists of those functions
f
, for which there exist a sequence
\{\lambda_{\ell}\}
of complex numbers and a sequence
\{a_{\ell}\}
of
(1,q)
-atoms such that

f=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}a_{\ell}
and
\sum_{\ell}|\lambda_{\ell}|<\infty
. The norm of
f\in H^{(1,q)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is defined by

\|f\|_{H^{(1,q)}_{\kappa}}=\inf\left\{\sum\nolimits_{\ell}|\lambda_{\ell}|\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all possible representations as above.

By
[
8
, pp. 592-3, Theorems A and B]
we have the following two theorems.

Theorem 7.6

For
1<q<\infty
,
H^{(1,q)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=H^{(1,\infty)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and the norms
\|\cdot\|_{H^{(1,q)}_{\kappa}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{(1,\infty)}_{\kappa}}
are equivalent.

It is useful to choose the space
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}}
to work with because of the Fourier structure of the Dunkl setting.

Theorem 7.7

The space
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is the dual of
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in the following sense:

(i)
for each
\varphi\in{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the linear functional

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}f=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ell}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}a_{\ell}\varphi\,d\omega_{\kappa},\qquad f=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}a_{\ell}\in H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

is well-defined and continuous on
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, whose norm is equivalent to
\|\varphi\|_{*,\kappa}
;

(ii)
each continuous linear functional
\mathcal{L}
on
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
has this form.

Proposition 7.8

The space
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is contained in
H_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and for
f\in H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}}
. Moreover
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is closed in
H_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

We present a proof below by means of the
\kappa
-Riesz transforms
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq d
).

Proof.

We first show that, for every
(1,2)
-atom
a
,
\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}}\lesssim 1
.
Indeed, for
a\in L^{2}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a
is well defined, and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(3B)}|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\right)^{2}\leq|{\mathcal{O}}(3B)|_{\kappa}\int_{{\mathcal{O}}(3B)}|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}\lesssim|B|_{\kappa}\int_{B}|a|^{2}\,d\omega_{\kappa}\leq 1.

(98)

Furthermore, if
x^{B}
denotes the center of the ball
B
,

\displaystyle\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a|\,d\omega_{\kappa}=c_{\kappa}\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}}\left|\int_{B}a(t)(K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x,x^{B}))\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\right|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{B}|a(t)|\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}}|(K_{j}(x,t)-K_{j}(x,x^{B}))|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(x)d\omega_{\kappa}(t).

It is easy to see that for
t\in B
,
[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}\subseteq\{x:\,d(x,t)>2|t-x^{B}|\}
. Using (
48
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{[{\mathcal{O}}(3B)]^{c}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a|\,d\omega_{\kappa}\lesssim\int_{B}|a(t)|\,d\omega_{\kappa}(t)\lesssim 1.

(99)

Combining (
98
) and (
99
) proves
\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}}\lesssim 1
.

In general, if
f=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}a_{\ell}\in H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
f_{n}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\lambda_{\ell}a_{\ell}
, then
\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{1}_{\kappa}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore,
since for
m>n
,

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{m}-{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{\kappa}}\leq\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}|\lambda_{\ell}|\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}a_{\ell}\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{m}|\lambda_{\ell}|,

it follows that there exists some
g\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{n}-g\|_{L^{1}_{\kappa}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We also have
\|g\|_{L^{1}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\sum_{\ell}|\lambda_{\ell}|
.

By Proposition
2.1
(i), for
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we have
|[{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}({\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{n})](\xi)-({\mathcal{R}}_{j}g)(\xi)|\leq\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{n}-g\|_{L^{1}_{\kappa}}

and
|[{{\mathscr{F}}}_{\kappa}({\mathcal{R}}_{j}f_{n})](\xi)+i(\xi_{j}/|\xi|)\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi)|\leq\|f_{n}-f\|_{L_{\kappa}^{1}}
, which shows
({\mathcal{R}}_{j}g)(\xi)=-i(\xi_{j}/|\xi|)\left({\mathscr{F}}_{\kappa}f\right)(\xi)
. According to Definition
3.16
,
g={\mathcal{R}}_{j}f\in L_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, so that
f\in H_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Moreover

\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\sum_{\ell}|\lambda_{\ell}|
, and hence
\|f\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}}
. The last assertion of the proposition follows from a standard argument (see
[
25
, pp. 257-8]
).

We denote the
G
-invariant subspaces of
{\text{BMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\text{BMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\text{BMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
H^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
by
{\text{IBMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\text{IBMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
{\text{IBMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
IH^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
respectively. Based on what we have known, the following conclusions are valid.

Theorem 7.9

(i)

IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is closed in
IH_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
;

(ii)

IH^{1}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{*}={\text{IBMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
;

(iii)

IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{*}={\text{IBMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
;

(iv)

{\text{IBMO}}_{G}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})={\text{IBMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})={\text{IBMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Theorem 7.10

The two spaces
IH_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
are identical, and the norms
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}_{\kappa}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}}
are equivalent.

Indeed, since the dual of the quotient space
IH_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})/IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is isometrically isomorphic to (see
[
19
, Theorem 7.2]
, for example)

IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{\bot}=\left\{{\mathcal{L}}\in IH_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{*}:\,\,{\mathcal{L}}f=0\,\,\hbox{for all}\,\,f\in IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\right\},

the unique function
\varphi\in{\text{IBMC}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})={\text{IBMO}}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
associated to
{\mathcal{L}}\in IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{\bot}
satisfies
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f\varphi\,d\omega_{\kappa}=0
for all
f\in IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
But by Theorem
7.7
, this implies that
\varphi=0
, so that
{\mathcal{L}}=0
. Therefore
(IH_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})/IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))^{*}=\{0\}
, and then
IH^{(1,2)}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=IH_{\kappa}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with equivalent norms.

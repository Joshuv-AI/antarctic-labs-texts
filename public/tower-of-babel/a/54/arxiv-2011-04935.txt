Azumaya Loci Of The Quantum Euclidean 2 ​ n 2n -Space.
Snehashis Mukherjee

Abstract
. This article delves into the Azumaya loci of quantum euclidean 2n-space, providing a comprehensive exploration. We introduce a significant class of maximal-dimensional simple modules associated with this algebra. Moreover, we establish a proof asserting that the maximal-dimensional simple modules are exactly the ones we have constructed. The computation of the algebra’s center, coupled with the necessary and sufficient conditions derived for maximal-dimensional simple modules, enables us to determine the Azumaya locus of the algebra.

Let

\displaystyle y_{i}y_{j}

\displaystyle=q^{-1}y_{j}y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ i<j.

\displaystyle=q^{-1}y_{j}x_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ i\neq j.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall relevant information about the Polynomial Identity algebra and we prove that
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})
is a PI algebra which enables us to comment on the
\mathbb{K}
-dimension of simple modules over
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})

A ring
R
is said to be a Polynomial Identity (PI) ring if
R
satisfies some monic polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\rangle
i.e.,
f(r_{1},\ldots,r_{k})=0
for all
r_{i}\in R
. The minimal degree of a PI ring
R
is the least degree of all monic polynomial identities for
R
. PI rings cover a large class of rings including commutative rings. Commutative rings satisfy the polynomial identity
x_{1}x_{2}-x_{2}x_{1}
and therefore have minimal degree
2
. Let us recall a result which provides a sufficient condition for a ring to be PI.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
18
, Corollary 13.1.13]
)

If
R
is a ring that is a finitely generated module over a commutative subring, then
R
is a PI ring.

We now define the PI degree of prime PI-algebras. This definition will suffice because the algebra covered in this article is prime. We now recall one of the fundamental results from the Polynomial Identity theory.

Theorem 2.1

(Posner’s Theorem
[
18
, Theorem 13.6.5]
)
.

Let
A
be a prime PI ring with centre
Z(A)
and minimal
degree
d
. Let
S=Z(A)\setminus\{0\}
,
Q=AS^{-1}
and
F=Z(A)S^{-1}
. Then
Q
is a central simple algebra with centre
F
and
\dime_{F}(Q)=(\frac{d}{2})^{2}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The PI degree of a prime PI ring
A
with minimal degree
d
is
\pideg(A)=\frac{d}{2}
.

The following result provides an important link between the PI degree of a prime affine PI algebra over an algebraically closed field and the

-dimension of its irreducible representations (cf.

Proposition 2.2

Let

Proposition 2.3
.

[

Let

Suppose that

\pideg(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbb{K}^{n}))=\pideg(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{q}}\left(\mathbb{(K^{*})}^{n}\right)=\sqrt{h}.

It is well known that a skew-symmetric matrix over

h_{i}\mid h_{i+1}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\},~~\forall\ \ 1\leq i\leq s-1

(each occurs twice) are called the invariant factors of

(2,2,\ldots,2,2,0,0,\ldots,0)
where the first
2p
many coordinates are
2
.

Since the rank of a skew-symmetric matrix is even, we have
\text{rank}~H\leq 2n-4
. So from
Corollary
2.0.1
, we have
\pideg(A_{p})=\pideg(\mathcal{O}_{H}(\mathbb{K}^{2n}))\leq m^{n-2}
. Hence
\pideg\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})/\langle\omega_{p}\rangle\leq m^{n-2}
.

Now we can establish the equality of the above
by constructing a simple
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})/\langle\omega_{p}\rangle
-module with
\mathbb{K}
-dimension
m^{n-2}
.

Let
(\underline{\alpha},\underline{\lambda},\underline{\gamma}):=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\in\mathbb{K}^{2n+3}
be such that all the scalar except
\alpha_{p+1},\lambda_{p}
are non zero. Given such
(\underline{\alpha},\underline{\lambda},\underline{\gamma})
, let

be the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space with basis consisting of

\{e(\underline{a})~|~\underline{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}),~0\leq a_{i}\leq m-1,~1\leq i\leq n~\text{with}~i\neq 1,p+1,a_{1}=a_{p+1}=0\}.

Let
e_{\pm i}
denote the vector
(0,\cdots,{\pm 1},\cdots,0)
whose
i
-th coordinate is
\pm 1
and the remaining are zero. Define the
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})
-module structure on the
\mathbb{K}
-space

by the action of each generator on the basis vectors as follows:

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})X_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i+1\end{subarray}}^{n}q^{-a_{j}}e(\underline{a})

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})Y_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{1}}{1-q^{-2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq i+1\end{subarray}}^{n}q^{-a_{j}}e(\underline{a})

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})X_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq p+1\end{subarray}}^{k-1}q^{a_{j}}~e(\underline{a}+e_{k})\ \ \text{for $k\neq p+1$}

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})Y_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq p+1\end{subarray}}^{k-1}q^{-a_{j}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=k+1\\
j\neq p+1\end{subarray}}^{n}q^{-2a_{j}}\ \frac{\lambda_{k}-q^{-2a_{k}}\lambda_{k-1}}{1-q^{-2}}e(\underline{a}+e_{-k})\ \ \text{for $k\neq p+1$}

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})X_{p+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{p}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{p-1}q^{a_{j}}\ q^{(a_{p}+1)}\ \gamma_{2}\ (q^{-2}-1)\ \lambda_{p-1}^{-1}\ e(\underline{a}+e_{p})

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})Y_{p+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{p}^{-1}\prod_{j=p+2}^{n}q^{-a_{j}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=2\\
j\neq p,p+1\end{subarray}}^{n}q^{-a_{j}}q^{-(a_{p}-1)}\ \gamma_{3}\ e(\underline{a}+e_{-p})

It can be easily verified this is
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})
-module with
M(\underline{\alpha},\underline{\lambda},\underline{\gamma})\omega_{p}=0
. Hence

is a simple
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})/\langle\omega_{p}\rangle
-module with
\mathbb{K}
-dimension
m^{n-2}
. Thus
\pideg\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})/\langle\omega_{p}\rangle=m^{n-2}
.
∎

4.
Maximal Dimensional Simple Modules.

Notations:

Let

\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})

I:=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}:\alpha_{i}=0\},\ \ J:=\{j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}:\beta_{j}=0\}

Also

Let us start by assuming

\Omega

-module.
Then the following two cases arise.

Case I:

\mathbf{I=\emptyset}
. It is notable that the elements
x_{1}
,
\omega_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
in
\mathcal{O}_{q}(\mathfrak{o}\mathbb{K}^{2n})

with

for

and
vx_{1}=\gamma_{1}v
. Since
N
is
\Omega
-torsionfree, for each

\lambda_{i}
and
\gamma_{1}
are nonzero scalars. Let

be the subspace of the vector space

where

where
\underline{a}=(0,a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})
where
0\leq a_{i}\leq m-1
. We will show that these vectors span a submodule. In fact, after some direct calculation, we get the following results. For
i=1
, we have

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}\prod_{j=2}^{n}q^{-a_{j}}e(\underline{a})

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})y_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{1}(1-q^{-2})^{-1}\prod_{j=2}^{n}q^{-a_{j}}e(\underline{a})

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}~q^{a_{j}}~e(\underline{a}+e_{i})\ \ \text{for $2\leq i\leq n$}

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})y_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}q^{-a_{j}}\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}q^{-2a_{j}}~\frac{\lambda_{i}-q^{-2a_{i}}\lambda_{i-1}}{1-q^{-2}}e(\underline{a}+e_{-i})\ \ \text{for $a_{i}\neq 0$.}

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})y_{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}^{-1}\prod_{j=2}^{i-1}q^{-a_{j}}\prod_{j=i+1}^{n}q^{-2a_{j}}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1})}{(1-q^{-2})}e(\underline{a}+e_{-i})\ \ \text{for $a_{i}=0$.}

Therefore, owing to simpleness of
N
,
N=N_{1}
. Now the following result deciphers the dimension of
N
.

Theorem 4.1
.

N
has dimension
m^{n-1}
.

Proof.

We use the principle of induction to show that all the vectors
e(0,a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})
for
0\leq a_{i}\leq m-1
are linearly independent. Let the result be true for any such
k
vectors. Let
S:=\{e\left(0,a_{2}^{(i)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(i)}\right):i=1,\cdots,k+1\}
be a set of
k+1
such vectors. Suppose

p:=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\zeta_{i}~e\left(0,a_{2}^{(i)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(i)}\right)=0

for some
\zeta_{i}\in\mathbb{K}
. Now,
e\left(0,a_{2}^{(k)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(k)}\right)\neq e\left(0,a_{2}^{(k+1)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(k+1)}\right)
.
We can choose the largest index
r
such that
a_{r}^{(k)}\neq a_{r}^{(k+1)}
in
\mathbb{Z}/{m\mathbb{Z}}
. Now the vectors
e\left(0,a_{2}^{(k)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(k)}\right)
and
e\left(0,a_{2}^{(k+1)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(k+1)}\right)
are eigenvectors of
x_{r}y_{r}
associated with the eigenvalues

q^{-2(a_{n}^{(k)}+\cdots+a_{r+1}^{(k)})}(1-q^{-2})^{-1}(\lambda_{r}-q^{-2a_{r}^{(k)}-2}\lambda_{r-1})=\nu_{k}(say)

and

q^{-2(a_{n}^{(k+1)}+\cdots+a_{r+1}^{(k+1)})}(1-q^{-2})^{-1}(\lambda_{r}-q^{-2a_{r}^{(k+1)}-2}\lambda_{r-1})=\nu_{k+1}(say)

respectively. We claim that
\nu_{k}\neq\nu_{k+1}
. Indeed,

\nu_{k}=\nu_{k+1}\implies q^{-2(a_{r}^{(k)}-a_{r}^{(k+1)})}=1\implies m\mid\left(a_{r}^{(k)}-a_{r}^{(k+1)}\right),

which is a contradiction.

Now

\begin{array}[]{ll}0&=px_{r}y_{r}-\nu_{k+1}p\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}(\nu_{i}-\nu_{k+1})~e\left(0,a_{2}^{(i)},\cdots,a_{n}^{(i)}\right)\end{array}

Using induction hypothesis,

\zeta_{i}(\nu_{i}-\nu_{k+1})=0
for all
i=1,\cdots,k
.

As
\nu_{k}\neq\nu_{k+1}
,
\zeta_{k}=0
.
Putting
\zeta_{k}=0
in (4.1) and using induction hypothesis we have
\zeta_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\cdots,k+1
.

Hence the result.
∎

Now the question is whether there really exists such a module. The answer is yes. Actually, one can easily verify the module defining rules. In order to do that we need the following checks for
1\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq a_{i}\leq{m-1}

(4.1)

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})y_{i}y_{j}=q^{-1}~e(\underline{a})y_{j}y_{i},\ \ \ \forall i<j

(4.2)

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{i}y_{j}=q^{-1}e(\underline{a})y_{j}x_{i},\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ i\neq j.

(4.3)

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{i}x_{j}=qe(\underline{a})x_{j}x_{i},\ \ \forall\ \ \ i<j.

(4.4)

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})x_{i}y_{i}=e(\underline{a})(y_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{l<i}(1-q^{-2})y_{l}x_{l}),\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ i=1,\cdots,n.

The relations (
4.1
) - (
4.3
) are easy to show.
For relation (
4.4
) we have the following calculation. As for
i=1
the calculations are trivial, we only compute for

Now for the right hand side we have,

\displaystyle e(\underline{a})\left(y_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{l<i}(1-q^{-2})y_{l}x_{l}\right)

\displaystyle=e(\underline{a})y_{i}x_{i}+e(a_{2},\cdots,a_{n})\left(\sum_{l<i}(1-q^{-2})y_{l}x_{l}\right)

\displaystyle=e(\underline{a})y_{i}x_{i}+e(\underline{a})\omega_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\left[q^{-2(a_{n}+\cdots+a_{i+1})}\left(1-q^{-2}\right)^{-1}(\lambda_{i}-q^{-2a_{i}}\lambda_{i-1})+\lambda_{i-1}~q^{-2(a_{n}+\cdots+a_{i})}\right]~e(\underline{a})

\displaystyle=q^{-2(a_{n}+\cdots+a_{i+1})}\left(1-q^{-2}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda_{i}-q^{-2a_{i}}\lambda_{i-1}+q^{-2a_{i}}\lambda_{i-1}-q^{-2a_{i}-2}\lambda_{i-1}\right)~e(\underline{a})

\displaystyle=q^{-2(a_{n}+\cdots+a_{i+1})}\left(1-q^{-2}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda_{i}-q^{-2a_{i}-2}\lambda_{i-1}\right)~e(\underline{a}).

5.

In this section, we compute the center of

Theorem 5.1
.

The center
Z_{n}
is the subalgebra generated by
x_{1}^{a}y_{1}^{b}
where
a,b\in\{1,\cdots,m-1\}
such that
a+b=m
,
x_{i}^{m},y_{i}^{m},i=1,\cdots,m

Proof.

Let us use induction on
n=
number of generators.

For
n=2
, let
z=\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}
be an element of the center, where
\underline{a}=(a_{1},a_{2}),\underline{b}=(b_{1},b_{2})
are tuples of non-negative integers. We will show that if

Now

\displaystyle zx_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}\right)x_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\left[x_{2}y_{2}^{b_{2}}-\frac{1-q^{2b_{2}}}{1-q^{2}}\omega_{1}y_{2}^{b_{2}-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{(a_{2}+1)}y_{2}^{b_{2}}-\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}q^{-2a_{2}}\frac{1-q^{2b_{2}}}{1-q^{2}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\omega_{1}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}y_{2}^{(b_{2}-1)}

Again

\displaystyle x_{2}z

\displaystyle=x_{2}\left(\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}q^{{}_{(}a_{1}+b_{1}))}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}x_{2}^{(a_{2}+1)}y_{2}^{b_{2}}

\displaystyle q^{-(a_{1}+b_{1})}c_{\underline{a}-e_{2},\underline{b}}

\displaystyle\left[q^{-(a_{1}+b_{1})}-1\right]c_{\underline{a}-e_{2},\underline{b}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1-q^{2(b_{2}+1)}}{1-q^{2}}q^{-2a_{2}}c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}+e_{2}}\omega_{1}\text{ for $a_{2}\geq 1,b_{2}\geq 0$}

\displaystyle\left[q^{-(a_{1}+b_{1})}-1\right]c_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}

Theorem 5.2
.

The following formulas hold in

\displaystyle\omega_{i}^{m}

\displaystyle={\left(1-q^{-2}\right)}^{m}\sum_{l\leq i}y_{l}^{m}x_{l}^{m}.

Assume the result holds for

Now

\displaystyle\omega_{N}

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}+{(1-q^{-2})}y_{N}x_{N}.

\displaystyle\omega_{N}^{m}

\displaystyle={\left[\omega_{N-1}+{(1-q^{-2})}y_{N}x_{N}\right]}^{m}.

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}^{m}+\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}c(l,m)y_{N}^{l}x_{N}^{l}+{(1-q^{-2})}^{m}y_{N}^{m}x_{n}^{m}.

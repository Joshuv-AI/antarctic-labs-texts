Normality of minimal log canonical centers of threefolds in mixed and positive characteristic
Emelie Arvidsson and Quentin Posva

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove the normality of minimal log canonical centers on threefold pairs which residue fields are perfect of residue characteristics
p\neq 2,3
and
5
. We also show that the union of all log canonical centers on threefold pairs with standard coefficients are seminormal provided that the residue characteristic is large enough. We provide an example of a non-seminormal log canonical center on a threefold in characteristic
3
, and give sufficient conditions to construct similar examples.

1.
Introduction

Log canonical centers play a central role in the birational geometry of varieties. The worst singularities of a log canonical variety are concentrated at its log canonical centers, and the understanding of those is oftentimes a key step to the understanding of the whole variety. In particular, the cohomological and topological properties of log canonical centers are worth investigating.

General properties of log canonical centers are well-understood in characteristic
0
: see for example
[
25
, §4.3]
. It is known that in characteristic
0
log canonical centers are always seminormal and that the minimal ones are actually normal. These properties can be established in two steps. First, one studies the log canonical centers of dlt pairs, which are particularly simple
[
25
, §4.2]
. Then one relates the log canonical centers on lc pairs to the ones on dlt pairs, using dlt resolutions and some vanishing theorems.

The situation in positive characteristic and mixed characteristic is more complicated and not as well-understood. In particular, some crucial vanishing theorems are simply false. Without restriction on the characteristics, even log canonical centers on dlt pairs need not be well-behaved
[
10
,
7
]
. While the surface theory is quite similar in equicharacteristics
0
and
p>0
and in mixed characteristic
[
25
, §2.2, §3.3]
, already for threefolds significant differences appear.

Let us give a summary of the current understanding of log canonical centers in positive and mixed characteristics.

\circ

Plt centers on threefolds.
Normality in characteristic
>5
is shown in
[
16
, Theorem 3.11, Proposition 4.1]
. Without restriction on the characteristic, plt centers on threefolds are known to be normal up to universal homeomorphism
[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also
[
15
, §3.2]
). This is the best we can hope for in general, since in
[
10
]
an example of non-normal plt center on a threefold in characteristic
2
is given.

\circ

Plt centers in arbitrary dimensions.
For every prime
p>0
we have examples of non-normal plt centers in characteristic
p
and dimension
2p+2

[
7
]
.

\circ

Minimal log canonical centers on threefolds.

Normality up to universal homeomorphism in mixed characteristic different from
2,3
and
5
follows from
[
14
]
. For minimal log canonical centers on semi-log canonical threefolds in characteristic
>5
, this is also proved in
[
29
, Theorem 7]
. Minimal log–canonical centers of dlt–pairs are normal in equicharacteristic
>5
, as well as in mixed characteristic of residue characteristic
>5

[
6
, Theorem 3.1]

One could hope that in a fixed dimension, log canonical centers satisfy (semi-)normality properties after bounding from below the residue characteristics. This hope falls short: recently Kollár
[
26
]
constructed in every positive characteristic examples of non-weakly normal log canonical centers on fourfold pairs. Still, the question remains open for unions of log canonical centers on threefolds. The main result of this note is the normality of minimal ones in mixed and positive characteristics different from
2,3
and
5
:

Theorem 1

(
Theorem 3.1.2
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log canonical threefold whose closed points have perfect residue fields of characteristic different from
2,3
and
5
. Then the minimal log canonical centers of
(X,\Delta)
are normal.

If a minimal lc center is a surface, then the pair is plt around that surface and normality follows from the aforementioned works. So the only case left to study is when the minimal lc center is a curve, say
C\subset X
. We follow the strategy used in characteristic
0
. Indeed, when the residue characteristics are greater than five, threefold dlt singularities have all the desired properties. We would like to descend these properties from dlt modifications to log canonical pairs. In order to do so we will need some vanishing theorem. The sufficient vanishing theorem is established in
[
5
]
. More precisely, given a threefold log canonical singularity
(X,\Delta)
with minimal log canonical center a curve
C
, by
[
5
, Prop. 14]
there exists a dlt model
g\colon Y\to X
with exceptional divisor
E
contracting to
C
, such that
E
is weakly normal and
R^{1}g_{*}\mathcal{O}(-E)=0
. Combining this vanishing with a factorization of
E\to C
through the normalization of
C
, whose existence is guaranteed by
Section 2.3
, we obtain that
C
is normal.

Building on
[
5
, Prop. 15]
and on the recent work of Kawakami on Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing on Calabi–Yau surfaces
[
21
]
, we are also able to prove some semi-normality property, provided that the characteristic is large enough:

Theorem 2

(
Theorem 3.3.1
)
.

There exists an integer
p_{0}
such that if
\Delta
has standard coefficients and
(X,\Delta)
is a log canonical threefold whose closed points have perfect residue fields of characteristic
p>p_{0}
,
then the union of all the log canonical centers of
(X,\Delta)
is semi-normal.

Whether an arbitrary union of log canonical centers is semi-normal is not a formal consequence of this theorem, even in characteristic
0
(see for example the proof given in
[
24
, Sections 4.3-4.4]
).

Also, for threefold pairs whose reduced boundary is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier with index not divisible by any of the residue characteristics, it is possible to show that the boundary is demi-normal as long as the residue characteristics at closed points are different from
2,3
and
5
. This can be obtained by combining the main theorem of
[
4
]
and cyclic covers (see also
[
5
, Cor. 23]
). We include it here in
Section 3.2
for completeness.

Let us now discuss counter-examples to (semi)-normality properties.
The only counter-example currently known to normality of plt centers for threefolds, obtained in
[
10
]
, stems from a failure of Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing on some del Pezzo surface. After taking a cone over that surface, the authors obtain a plt pair whose boundary is not
S_{2}
at the vertex. Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing holds on del Pezzo surfaces in characteristic
>5
by
[
3
]
, but we have counter-examples in characteristic
3

[
8
]
and in characteristic
5
by
[
3
]
. (See also
[
27
]
for the relation between Kawamata–Viewheg vanishing and liftability to characteristic
0
for Picard rank one del Pezzo surfaces). Thus one can wonder if it is possible to produce non-normal plt centers on some cones over these surfaces, following the method of
[
10
]
.
Unfortunately, a sole instance of Kawamata–Viewheg vanishing failure is not sufficient to produce such an example. We were unable to find the right configuration of ample divisor and boundary for the del Pezzo surface in characteristic
5
described in
[
3
]
: actually, our calculations led us to believe that no example of non-normal plt center can be produced by taking an appropriate cone over that surface.

For the characteristic
3
del Pezzo surface constructed in
[
8
]
, we find a configuration leading to a non-normal cone boundary. However the cone pair we obtain has a minimal lc center at the vertex, so we do not get a plt pair. Nonetheless we can show that the cone divisor is not seminormal at the vertex, contrarily to what would happen in characteristic
0
(see
[
25
, 4.20.(3)]
).

Theorem 3
.

[
Section 4
]
There exists a threefold lc pair
(X,E)
with reduced boundary over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
3
, such that
E
is not
S_{2}
nor seminormal.

In the example we construct, the reduced boundary
E
contains every log canonical center of the pair
(X,E)
(see
Section 4
). So this shows that
Theorem 2
can fail in characteristic
3
.

In the last section of this note (
Section 5
), we give two sets of sufficient conditions on a Picard rank one variety
X
(of any dimension) for the existence of a non-normal cone boundary over
X
. We believe these conditions to be of independent interest. Indeed, the calculations of
[
10
]
are quite involved, rely on a precise local analysis of the relative cone, and require the vanishing of many cohomology groups. In particular, it is not immediate to adapt their method to a potential new example. By contrast, our conditions are short and only concern the global geometry of
X
: in one case a single failure of Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing for a nef divisor, in the other a failure of Kawamata–Viehweg for an anti-ample divisor and the existence of a boundary with small numerical class. Our counterexample in characteristic
3
is obtained by using one of these conditions.

Let us also mention that examples of pathological behaviour of threefold singularities in positive characteristic are often constructed by considering cones constructed from
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier polarizations (see
[
10
,
8
,
3
]
). The birational singularities of cones with respect to Cartier divisors are well-understood (see for example
[
25
, 3.1]
). However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge the case of
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier polarizations in arbitrary characteristic has not received a similar treatment in the literature, although this construction is well-known to the experts. For the convenience of the reader we gather in
Section 2.5.1
some foundational results about singularities of cones, and more precise statements about three-dimensional cones are given in
Section 2.5.2
.

Lastly, as far as threefolds in positive characteristic are concerned, our results leave us with the following interrogations:

Question 1.0.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a threefold pair over a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
.

(a)

If
p=5
, are plt centers normal? What about minimal lc centers?

(b)

If
p\geq 5
, are unions of lc centers seminormal? or even weakly normal? What about union of slc centers?

(c)

Are Cartier boundaries demi–normal in characteristics
\leq 5
? I.e., does
Section 3.2
fail in characteristic
p\leq 5
?

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Conventions and notations

We will work with schemes that are not necessarily defined over a field, but we keep most of the usual terminology.

Throughout this article we work over an affine base
B
which is excellent, of pure finite dimension, and admits a dualizing complex. Unless stated otherwise, all the schemes appearing in this note are essentially of finite type over
B
.

By a
variety
we shall mean a separated reduced equidimensional excellent scheme over
B
. Note that a variety in our sense might be reducible, even disconnected. A
curve
(resp. a
surface
, resp. a
threefold
) is a variety of absolute dimension one (resp. two, resp. three). In particular the dimension shall always mean the absolute dimension and not the relative dimension over
B
.

Let
X
be a variety and
\mathcal{F}
a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
S_{i}
if
\depth_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}\mathcal{F}_{x}\geq\min\{i,\dim\mathcal{F}_{x}\}
for every
x\in X
.

If
X
is a reduced Noetherian scheme, its
normalization
is defined to be its relative normalization along the structural morphism
\bigsqcup_{\eta}\Spec(k(\eta))\to X
where
\eta
runs through the generic points of
X
. Recall that
X
is normal if and only if it is regular in codimension one and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is
S_{2}
.

If
X
is a variety, we define
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors and
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors the usual way. We usually use these notions when
X
is (demi-)normal.

A scheme
X
essentially of finite type over
B
has a dualizing complex
\omega_{X}^{\bullet}
and a dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X}:=h^{-i}\left(\omega_{X}^{\bullet}\right)
where
i:=\max\{j\mid h^{-j}\left(\omega_{X}^{\bullet}\right)\neq 0\}
. See
[
9
, §2.1]
for more details. If
X
is a variety and
\omega_{X}
is invertible in codimension one, it defines a canonical divisor
K_{X}
.

A
pair
is the data
(X,\Delta)
where
X
is a demi-normal variety,
\Delta
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with coefficients in
[0,1]
such that no component of
\Delta
is contained in
\Sing(X)
, and satisfying that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. If
f\colon Y\to X
is a proper birational morphism from a normal variety, then there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta_{Y}
on
Y
such that

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}=f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)

and
\Delta_{Y}
is uniquely defined if we assume
f_{*}K_{Y}=K_{X}
, which we always do. Running through all such
f\colon Y\to X
, we define the singularity of the pair
(X,\Delta)
(e.g.
lc
,
klt
) the usual way as in
[
22
]
. See
[
9
, §2.5]
for details.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair. We say that a reduced closed subscheme
Z\subset X
is an
lc center
of
(X,\Delta)
if there exists a proper birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
and an
f
-exceptional prime divisor
E\subset Y
such that
\coeff_{E}\Delta_{Y}=1
and
f(E)=Z
.

We refer to
[
9
, §2.5]
for the various notions of positivity (ampleness, nefness, etc.) for
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on proper
B
-schemes.

2.2.
Universal homeomorphisms

Recall that a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
is a
universal homeomorphism
if for every
T\to Y
, the base-change
f_{T}\colon X_{T}\to T
is an homeomorphism. Extensions of rings inducing universal homeomorphisms have the following algebraic property:

Proposition 2.2.1

(
[
32
, 0CNE]
)
.

Let
A\subset B
be an extension of rings. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

the induced morphism
\Spec B\to\Spec A
is a universal homeomorphism;

(b)

every finite subset of
B
is contained in a sub-extension
A[b_{1},\dots,b_{n}]\subset B
such that for every
i=1,\dots,n
we have either:

\circ

b_{i}^{2},b_{i}^{3}\in A[b_{1},\dots,b_{i-1}]
, or

\circ

there exists a prime
p>0
such that
b_{i}^{p},pb_{i}\in A[b_{1},\dots,b_{i-1}]
.

Next we recall some sufficient conditions on a locally Noetherian scheme
X
for its normalization morphism to be a universal homeomorphism.

Definition 2.2.2

(
[
32
, 0BPZ]
)
.

A local ring
\mathcal{O}
is called
geometrically unibranch
if
\mathcal{O}_{\text{red}}
is a domain, its integral closure
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
in
\Frac(\mathcal{O}_{\text{red}})
is local, and the extension of residue fields
k(\mathcal{O})\subset k(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
is purely inseparable.

A scheme
X
is called geometrically unibranch if every local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is geometrically unibranch.

Lemma 2.2.3

(
[
32
, 0BQ4]
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme, and
x\in X
a point. Assume that: if
(x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime})\to(x\in X)
is any étale neighbourhood, then
X^{\prime}
is irreducible at
x^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is geometrically unibranch.

Lemma 2.2.4
.

Let
X
be an excellent geometrically unibranch scheme. Then the normalization morphism
X^{\nu}\to X
is a universal homeomorphism.

The excellent hypothesis implies that of local Noetherianity, and in this context the normalization is defined: see
[
32
, 035E]
.

Proof.

By
[
32
, 0C1S]
the normalization
\nu\colon X^{\nu}\to X
is universally bijective. Since
\nu
is surjective
[
32
, 035Q]
, and moreover universally closed by the excellence hypothesis, we obtain that
\nu
is a universal homeomorphism.
∎

2.3.
Weak normality and semi-normality

We now recall the definition of semi-normal and weakly normal schemes. These notions originally appeared in
[
2
,
1
,
33
]
. We follow the more recent treatment given in
[
23
, I.7]
.

Definition 2.3.1

(
[
23
, I.7.2.1]
)
.

Let
X
be an excellent reduced scheme with normalization
\nu\colon X^{\nu}\to X
. A scheme
X^{\prime}
together with a morphism
\varphi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is called the
weak normalization
(resp. the
semi-normalization
) of
X
if the following properties hold:

(a)

the normalization
X^{\nu}\to X
factors through
\varphi
,

(b)

\varphi
is finite surjective, and an isomorphism over the generic points of
X
,

(c)

\varphi
is an homeomorphism, and for any
x\in X
with unique preimage
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
, the field extension
k(x)\subset k(x^{\prime})
is purely inseparable (resp. an isomorphism),

(d)

if
\varphi^{\prime}\colon Y\to X
also satisfies the above properties, then there is a factorization

\varphi\colon X^{\prime}\longrightarrow Y\overset{\varphi^{\prime}}{\longrightarrow}X.

We say that
X
is
weakly normal
(resp.
semi-normal
) if
\varphi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is the identity morphism.

Let us also recall the notion of demi-normality: it will appear in
Section 3.2
.

Definition 2.3.2
.

A reduced Noetherian scheme
X
is
demi-normal
if it is
S_{2}
and for every codimension one point
\eta\in X
, the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}
is either regular or a nodal singularity (see
[
25
, 1.41]
).

See
[
28
, §2.3.1]
for the relations between demi-normality, semi-normality and weak normality.

Lemma 2.3.3

(
[
23
, I.7.2.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a reduced excellent scheme. Then
X
has a weak normalization and a semi-normalization. They coincide if
X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme.

An example of weakly normal schemes that will be relevant in this note is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta+E)
be an lc pair, where the residue fields of
X
have characteristics
\neq 2
, and where
E
is a (possibly reducible) divisor with coefficients
1
. Assume that
E
is
S_{2}
. Then
E
is weakly normal.

Proof.

By the classification of lc singularities on surfaces
[
25
, 2.31]
, we see that
E
has at worse nodal singularities in codimension
1
. Since
E
is also
S_{2}
, it is demi-normal. Now demi-normal schemes in char
\neq 2
are weakly normal
[
28
, 2.3.8]
.
∎

Let us next give an algebraic description of weak normality. We stick to reduced excellent rings for simplicity.

Proposition 2.3.5

(
[
34
, §1]
)
.

Let
A
be a reduced excellent ring with normalization
\bar{A}
.

(a)

A
is semi-normal if and only if: for every
x\in\bar{A}
, the fact that
x^{2},x^{3}\in A
implies that
x\in A
.

(b)

A
is weakly normal if and only if:
A
is semi-normal, and for every
x\in\bar{A}
the fact that
x^{p},px\in A
for some prime
p>0
implies that
x\in A
.

Notice the similarity between the algebraic conditions for semi-normality and weak normality given above, and the algebraic description of universal homeomorphisms given in

. We take advantage of this similarity in order to obtain the following factorization result:

Proposition 2.3.6
.

Let

5.

To conclude this note, we describe two sets of conditions that are sufficient to obtain examples of cone pairs with non-(semi-)normal reduced boundary. In both cases it boils down to a single cohomology statement on the base of the cone. Our criteria are independent of the dimension.

Let us fix the notations. The assumption on the Picard rank is not strictly speaking necessary, but it makes our conditions easier to state.

Notation 5.0.1
.

We let

be a projective normal variety of Picard rank one over an algebraically closed field and

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier

-divisor on

is lc and that

. So

is a pair
[
8
, 2.4]
. If we additionally assume inversion of adjunction in the form of

then it follows that this pair is actually lc by

. Below we give two sets of sufficient conditions for

to be non-

and non-seminormal at the vertex.

5.2.
Failure of Kawamata–Viehweg for an anti-ample divisor

The second set of sufficient conditions grew out of our efforts to find an alternative to
Section 5.1
.

Proposition 5.2.1
.

Situation as in
Section 5
. Assume that there exists an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor
A
with the following properties:

(a)

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}(-A))\neq 0
, and

(b)

A-(\ind-1)(E)\cdot E
is ample, where
\ind(E)
is the Cartier index of
E
.

Then there exist
d>0
and
0\leq i\leq\ind(E)-1
such that
B=dA-iE
is ample and the cone boundary
E_{\aC(X,B)}
is not
S_{2}
nor seminormal.

The second condition means that
E
is numerically small compared to
A
. For the del Pezzo surface studied in
[
3
]
this criterion cannot be applied, since the ample
A
belongs to the smallest effective numerical class.

The proof goes as follows. First, consider the cone pair
(\aC(X,A),E_{\aC(X,A)})
. By the first condition the cone
\aC(X,A)
is not
S_{3}
at the vertex point (see
[
8
, 4.2]
). By
[
8
, 2.4]

\ind(E_{\aC(X,A)})=\ind(E)=m

E
is Cartier then
E_{\aC(X,A)}
is Cartier and we obtain that
E_{\aC(X,A)}
cannot be
S_{2}
. By
Section 2.5
and
Section 2.5
we deduce that
E_{\aC(X,A)}
is not seminormal either. Thus we may assume that
m>1
for the rest of the proof.

Denote by
D
the divisor
E_{\aC(X,A)}
localized at the vertex
v
. We have an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}(mD)\cong\mathcal{O}_{\aC(X,A),v}=\mathcal{O}
. This isomorphism gives a structure of
\mathcal{O}
-algebra to
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{O}(-iD)

\pi\colon X^{\prime}=\Spec_{\mathcal{O}}\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{O}(-iD)\longrightarrow\Spec\mathcal{O}

be the induced finite morphism (see
[
22
, 2.52]

X^{\prime}
is an integral
S_{2}+G_{1}
semi-local scheme. Denote by
D^{\prime}
the divisorial pullback of
D

\pi^{[*]}\mathcal{O}(D)=\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(D^{\prime})

[
22
, 2.53]

\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(D^{\prime})

We claim that the reduced subscheme
D^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
cannot be
S_{2}

X^{\prime}
would be
S_{3}
at its closed points by

S_{3}
everywhere. By
[

, 5.4]

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{O}(-iD)

S_{3}

\mathcal{O}
-module. But
\mathcal{O}
is a direct summand, so
\mathcal{O}

S_{3}
. In other words
\aC(X,A)

S_{3}
at its vertex, which we know not to be the case.

\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}

S_{2}

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}(D^{\prime})
as
\mathcal{O}
-module. Let us identify
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D^{\prime}}

and for ease of notation we write
\mathcal{B}_{i}
the cokernel. Then
\mathcal{B}_{i}

1

\mathcal{O}_{E_{\aC(X,A)},v}

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}(D^{\prime})

S_{2}

\mathcal{O}

Next we want to a description of the non-zero local cohomology group
H^{1}_{v}(\mathcal{B}_{i})

\aC(X,A)

\displaystyle H^{0}\left(\mathcal{O}(jE_{\aC(X,A)}\right)

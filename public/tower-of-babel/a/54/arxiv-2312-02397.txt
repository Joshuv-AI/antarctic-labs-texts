Regular Sets of Lines in Rank 3 Polar Spaces
Ferdinand Ihringer, Morgan Rodgers

Abstract
There are 6 families of finite polar spaces of rank 3 3 . The set of lines in a rank 3 3 polar space form a rank 5 5 association scheme. We determine the regular sets of minimal size in several of these polar spaces, and describe some interesting examples. We also give a new family of Cameron–Liebler sets of generators in the polar spaces O + ​ ( 10 , q ) O^{+}(10,q) when q = 3 h q=3^{h} using a regular set of lines in O ⁡ ( 7 , q ) O(7,q) .

3
General Examples

Here we list several examples of subsets of lines in rank 3 polar spaces
which lie in a proper subset of the common eigenspaces
of the association scheme.
In many cases, these are either regular sets in all the graphs of the association scheme,
or at least regular with respect to some relation;
in any case, they give us important information about the regular sets through the use of Lemma
2.1
.

3.1
Combinatorial Designs

The group
G
of a finite classical polar space
{\mathcal{P}}
of rank
d

acts generously transitively on each set of totally isotropic subspaces of fixed dimension.
Writing
\Omega_{a}
for the set of isotropic
a
-spaces,
we can consider the
G
-orbits on
\Omega_{a}\times\Omega_{b}
.
These orbits are given precisely by the sets

R_{a,b}^{s,k}=\{(\pi_{a},\pi_{b})\mid\dim(\pi_{a}\cap\pi_{b})=s,\ \dim(\langle\pi_{a},\pi_{b}\cap\pi_{a}^{\perp}\rangle)=k\},

with
0\leq s\leq\min{(a,b)}
,
\max{(a,b)}\leq k\leq\min{(d,a+b-s)}
.
Following
[
36
]
, we say a set
Y\subset\Omega_{a}
is
a
combinatorial design with respect to the
b
-spaces of
{\mathcal{P}}

if, for each such orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
, we have that

|\{\pi_{a}\in Y\mid(\pi_{a},\pi_{b})\in{\mathcal{O}}\}|
is constant for all
\pi_{b}\in\Omega_{b}
.

Therefore if
Y
is a combinatorial design with respect to
b
-spaces of
{\mathcal{P}}
,
then every
b
-space is incident with a constant number of elements of
Y

(we just apply the definition to the relation
R_{a,b}^{a,b}
, respectively
R_{a,b}^{b,a}
, according to whether
a\leq b
or
a>b
).
This property is in fact enough to characterize combinatorial designs in finite polar spaces;
we give the following theorem specialized only to our context of sets of lines in rank 3 polar spaces to avoid giving needless definitions, but a more general version can be found in
[
52
]
.
Also note that Schmidt’s recent research program on combinatorial designs in polar spaces resulted
in a flurry of activities on combinatorial designs in polar spaces
[
18
,
37
,
53
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

In a rank
3
polar space with parameter

,
a set
Y
of lines is a combinatorial design with respect to points
if and only if every point of
{\mathcal{P}}
lies on precisely
m
elements of
S
,
which occurs if and only if
\chi_{Y}\in\langle\bm{j}\rangle+V_{20}+V_{21}

In this case we have that

|Y|=m\frac{(q^{e+2}+1)(q^{2}+q+1)}{(q+1)}.

Theorem 3.2

In a rank

a set
Y
of lines is a combinatorial design with respect to generators

which occurs if and only if
\chi_{Y}\in\langle\bm{j}\rangle+V_{11}+V_{21}

|Y|=m(q^{e+1}+1)(q^{e+2}+1).

5
Extremal Sets with Forbidden Relations

The famous ratio bound (also called Delsarte-Hoffman bound) states that
a coclique
Y
in a regular graph on
n
vertices, degree
k
,
and smallest eigenvalue
\lambda
has at most size
n/(1-k/\lambda)
.
In our case, we can apply it to cocliques in any graph defined by a relation
(or union of relations) in our association scheme.
If
|Y|=n/(1-k/\lambda)
, then

\chi_{Y}\in\langle\bm{j}\rangle+W
, where
W
is the eigenspace of
\lambda
(for instance see Proposition 3.7.2 in
[
6
]
).
Now consider the graph
\Gamma
with adjacency defined as the union
of relations
R_{i}
with
i\in{\mathcal{I}}
for some subset of relations
{\mathcal{I}}
.
Then, more generally, Delsarte’s linear programming bound
(see Proposition 2.5.2 in
[
6
]
)
states that if
Y
has inner distribution
a
, then

{(aQ)}_{j}\geq 0

for all
j
, where
j
runs over the indices of the eigenspaces
E_{j}
.
If we set
a_{i}=0
for all
i\in{\mathcal{I}}

and we maximize for
\sum a_{i}
,
then this gives us a bound on the size of a coclique in
\Gamma
.
Schrijver showed that a weighted version of the Delsarte-Hoffman
bound, also known as Lovász bound, is equivalent to Delsarte’s LP bound
[
43
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{J}}
be the set of indices of those eigenspaces
V_{j}
for which

{(aQ)}_{j}=0
for all optimal solution of the linear program stated in the above.
Then an optimal solution satisfies
\chi_{Y}\in\langle\bm{j}\rangle+W
, where
W=\bigcup_{j\not\in{\mathcal{J}}}V_{j}
.

Let us briefly discuss the possible families of lines for which some
subset of relations is forbidden. Also note that we have a constant
number of variables, a constant number of inequalities,
and that the solution of the linear program is necessarily a polynomial in
q
.
Hence, determining the optimal solution for each case is a (small)
finite problem and can done relatively quickly by computer.

forbidden: For a family of lines

in
{\mathcal{P}}
with no two in a plane,
the ratio bound shows

with equality if

Two-weight Codes & SRGs

It was noted in
[
45
]
(among other things) that for

{\mathcal{P}}\in\{\mathrm{Sp}(2d,q),U(2d+1,q),O^{-}(2d+2,q)\}
naturally embedded
in an ambient (
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
) vector space

the collection of points lying on an element points of an
1
-system give
a two-weight code, that is, the hyperplanes of
V
have two intersection sizes
with respect to this set of points.
Similar observations can be found in
[
2
]
for the strongly
regular graph of the points of
{\mathcal{P}}
.
Here we discuss these results more generally, in the context of rank 3 polar spaces.
Note that in the general result
m
is not necessarily an
integer for
e\neq 2
, but we chose this parameterization for the
sake of readability.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
Y
be a family of pairwise non-intersecting lines in

{\mathcal{P}}\in\{\mathrm{Sp}(6,q),U(7,q),O^{-}(8,q)\}

with
\chi_{Y}\in V_{10}^{\perp}
.
Write
|Y|=m(q^{e+2}+1)
and let
H
be a hyperplane of
V
.
If
H
is a nondegenerate hyperplane or a degenerate hyperplane
with its radical not in
Y
, then
H
contains precisely

m(q+1)(q^{e+1}+1)

points which lie on a line of
Y
, while if
H
is a degenerate hyperplane with its radical in
Y
,
then
H
contains precisely

m(q+1)(q^{e+1}+1)-q^{e+1}

points which lie on a line of
Y
.

Proof.

A hyperplane
H
of
V
intersects
{\mathcal{P}}
in a degenerate polar space
or in a rank
3
polar space with parameter
e-1
.
Let
Z
denote the lines of
H
in
{\mathcal{P}}
.
We will use Lemma
2.1
.

We saw in §
3.6
that if
H
is a rank
3
polar space
(that is,
H
is nondegenerate hyperplane),
then
\chi_{Z}\in\langle\bm{j}\rangle+V_{10}
. We find

|Z|=(q^{e}+1)(q^{e+1}+1)(q^{2}+q+1).

Hence,
H
contains
m(q^{e}+1)
lines of
Y

while it intersects all the other lines of
Y
in a point.

Now let
H
be a degenerate hyperplane with radical
H^{\perp}
.
Similarly,
we saw for the
\tilde{Y}
in §
3.3
that

\chi_{\tilde{Y}}\in\langle\bm{j}\rangle+V_{10}
and

|\chi_{\tilde{Y}}|=(q^{e}+1)(q^{e+1}+1)(q^{2}+q+1)=|Z|
.
Hence, with
Y^{\prime}
as in §
3.3
denoting the set
of lines through
H^{\perp}
,

\chi_{Z}^{T}\chi_{\tilde{Y}}=|Z\cap\tilde{Y}|+q^{e}|Z\cap Y^{\prime}|.

This shows the claims for
H
being degenerate.
∎

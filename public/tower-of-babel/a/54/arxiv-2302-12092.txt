Hopf-Like Bifurcation in a Wave Equation at a Removable Singularity
Nemanja Kosovalić, Brian Pigott

Abstract
. It is shown that a one-dimensional damped wave equation with an odd time derivative nonlinearity exhibits small amplitude bifurcating time periodic solutions, when the bifurcation parameter is the linear damping coefficient is positive and accumulates to zero. The upshot is that the singularity of the linearized operator at criticality which stems from the well known small divisor problem for the wave operator, is entirely removed without the need to exclude parameters via Diophantine conditions, nor the use of accelerated convergence schemes. Only the contraction mapping principle is used.

1.
Introduction

Time periodic solutions of nonlinear wave equations, have
been a large focus of research spanning the last fifty years,
with major interest in non-dissipative systems, or dissipative systems
subject to a periodic forcing. In the last thirty years, the emphasis
of many works addressing the bifurcation of time periodic solutions of nonlinear wave equations
is the resolution of small divisor problems. For instance see

[
6
,
5
]
and

[
4
,
3
]
, to name a few. In this paper, we consider the problem of bifurcating small amplitude time periodic solutions for nonlinear wave equations when the (positive) linear damping coefficient is used as the bifurcation parameter, and is allowed to be
arbitrarily small
. In order for nontrivial time periodic solutions to persist, we consider a nonlinear negative damping as in, for instance, the investigation of the Beam equation in
[
9
]
, to balance out the positive linear damping. The linearized problem at criticality (when the damping coefficient is zero) naturally contains a singularity owing to the small divisor problem stemming from the wave operator. The upshot of this work is that the singularity can be entirely removed
without
the use of techniques such as Nash-Moser iteration, nor the exclusion of parameters via Diophantine conditions. In order to remove the singularity, we exploit three critical features of our problem: (i) An asymptotic expansion for the lower bound of the damping parameter in terms of amplitude, (ii) the fact a certain integral vanishes (see Lemma
4.1
), and (iii) the regularizing effect of Laplacian damping. This allows us to solve the infinite dimensional part of the problem using merely the contraction mapping principle. Our work is motivated by
[
2
]
which proves Lyapunov Center Theorems for various nonlinear wave equations using the contraction mapping principle but differs in two ways: We have an explicit bifurcation parameter, so we are really proving a Hopf Bifurcation Theorem and not a Lyapunov Center Theorem, and we do not require any Diophantine conditions to restrict parameters.

We consider the following damped nonlinear wave equation with Dirichlet boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u-\alpha\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{2}u+mu=\Big(\partial_{t}u\Big)^{2p+1},&t\in\mathbf{R},x\in\mathbf{T}=\mathbf{R}/2\pi\mathbf{Z},\\
u(t,0)=u(t,\pi)=0.\end{array}\right.

(1)

In (
1
)
p\geq 1
is taken to be an integer,
\alpha>0
and
m>0
is irrational.

Before stating the main results we make some clarifying remarks concerning our choices of the nonlinearity, damping, and the question of the stability of the obtained periodic solutions:

(1)

Nonlinearity
- Due to the presence of positive linear damping, in order for non-trivial periodic solutions to persist, we need the nonlinearity to restore energy to the system, and the odd time derivative nonlinearity is the simplest autonomous mechanism which achieves this. See the energy argument in
[
9
]
for details. Such velocity dependent nonlinearities arise in the phenomena of self-excited vibrations; see the introduction of
[
9
]
and the references therein. In fact, our results can be extended to include higher order polynomial perturbations on top of the present nonlinearity, which do not contain any derivatives. On a related note the Lemma
4.1
which concerns the vanishing of a certain integral, is really just another way of stating that monomial expansions of trigonometric functions have finitely many nonzero Fourier modes, and has nothing special to do with our choice of nonlinearity.

(2)

Damping
- The choice of Laplacian damping (sometimes called Kelvin-Voigt damping) in this work is used to facilitate removing the singularity. It is
not
clear if our results can be obtained for non-Laplacian linear damping using the same techniques. On the other hand, our choice of damping naturally arises in modelling the vibrations of viscoelastic materials, as an internal friction of the vibrating material. See e.g. the introduction of
[
1
]
for a discussion of damping mechanisms in wave equations.

(3)

Stability
- We do
not
obtain any results concerning the stability of the periodic solutions here, but can comment on the direction of bifurcation. Although the solutions we obtain persist for arbitrarily small positive values of the damping coefficient, a change of variable
t\to-t
allows us to get solutions for negative values of the damping coefficient for the nonlinearity
-1\cdot(\partial_{t}u)^{2p+1}
. Provided the principle of exchange of stability holds (see Section I.12 of the monograph
[
8
]
), then we expect the latter branch of periodic solutions to be stable.

4.1.
Solution of the Range Equation

The Implicit Function Theorem is not applicable as in the usual Hopf Bifurcation Theorem because when the damping
\alpha=0
there is a genuine small divisor problem since the numbers
k^{2}+m-\omega^{2}n^{2}
accumulate to zero. The novelty of this work is to show that this singularity can be removed owing to a certain integral vanishing (see Lemma
4.1
), in addition to having a suitable lower bound for
\alpha
in terms of
\rho
(see Assumption
2
), and also exploiting the regularizing effect of the Laplacian damping. The removal of this singularity invokes neither Diophantine conditions on the temporal frequency
\omega
, nor any Nash-Moser iteration. We can accomplish this by solving the range equation using the contraction mapping principle. This is a surprising fact since the solutions we will find have damping parameter which accumulates to
\alpha=0
.

Note that (
18
) can be written as

v=L_{\omega,\alpha}^{-1}\Pi_{V^{s-1}}\omega^{2p+1}\Big(\partial_{t}[\rho\cos(t)\sin(x)+v]\Big)^{2p+1}.

We begin with some Lemmas we will need to solve the range equation (

Lemma 4.1
.

There is an integer
K_{p}\geq 1
such that for each

\int_{\mathbf{T}}\sin^{2p+1}(x)\sin(kx)dx=0.

Proof.

Using the formula

\sin^{2p+1}(x)\sin(kx)=i2^{-2p-2}(e^{-ikx}-e^{ikx})i^{2p+1}(e^{-ix}-e^{ix})^{2p+1}

as well as the Binomial Theorem to expand the term

as a sum of exponentials multiplied by constants, it is not difficult to see that we obtain the desired result as long as
k>2p+1
since the exponents in the exponentials do not vanish, and using periodic boundary conditions.
∎

In what follows we will often use the notation
\Pi_{k>K_{p}}
to denote the projection onto Fourier modes for which

Proof.

Hence

\frac{n^{2}}{(\omega^{2}n^{2}-(k^{2}+m))^{2}}\leq 1.

On the other hand if
n\neq 0
and

n^{2}<\left(\frac{K^{2}+1+m}{W_{0}^{2}}\right)|n|

hence by Assumption
1
since
W_{0}>1
,

|n|<\left(\frac{K^{2}+1+m}{W_{0}^{2}}\right)<K^{2}+1+m.

It follows both
|n|,k
are bounded above by constants which only depend on
K,m,p
; that is
1\leq|n|,k\leq A(K,m,p)
for some

. In this case we can choose

small enough so that

|\omega^{2}-(1+m)|<\min_{|n|,k\leq A(K,m,p)}\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{k^{2}+m}{n^{2}}-(1+m)\right|

Then it is easily seen that

|\omega^{2}n^{2}-(k^{2}+m)|\geq\min_{|n|,k\leq A(K,m,p)}\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{k^{2}+m}{n^{2}}-(1+m)\right|>0,

where the last part is positive since

are bounded, above by a constant depending only on
m,p
.
Lastly, the statement concerning the quantities

follows because

is bounded and we just showed the denominators are bounded away from zero.
∎

4.2.

To solve the bifurcation equation (

\displaystyle-\alpha

\displaystyle(m+1-\omega^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{2p+1}}{\rho}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{2}}\Big(\partial_{t}[\rho\cos(t)\sin(x)+v]\Big)^{2p+1}\cos(t)\sin(x)dtdx.

Proposition 4.5

Let

4.3
. Let
\rho>0
be sufficiently small; in particular we require that
\rho
satisfy the inequality (
36
) below. Then the bifurcation equation (
34
) has a solution
\alpha(\omega,\rho)\gtrsim\rho^{2p}>0
, where the implicit constant depends only on
k_{0},m,p
. Furthermore, for fixed
\rho>0
sufficiently small, the map

[W_{0},W_{1}]\ni\omega\mapsto\alpha(\omega,\rho)\in(0,\infty)

is continuous. The bifurcation equation (
35
) has a solution
\omega(\rho)\in[W_{0},W_{1}]
. Finally, we have that
\alpha\downarrow 0
and
\omega^{2}\to\omega_{1}^{2}=m+1
as
\rho\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

Beginning with (
34
) we let

\mathcal{B}(\alpha):=\omega^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p}-\frac{\omega^{2p}}{\rho}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{2}}F(\rho,t,x)\sin(t)\sin(x)dtdx.

Using the fact that
\theta>0
along with Lemma
4.6
we have that

\mathcal{B}(\alpha)\leq|\mathcal{B}(\alpha)|\leq W_{1}^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p}+C_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p}\rho^{2p+1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\geq W_{0}^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p}-C_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p}\rho^{2p+1}

\displaystyle=\rho^{2p}(W_{0}^{2p}\theta-C_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p}\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}W_{0}^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p},

where the last inequality holds provided

0<\rho\leq\frac{1}{2C_{k_{0},m,p}}\left(\frac{W_{0}}{W_{1}}\right)^{2p}\theta.

(36)

Hence provided
\rho
satisfies (
36
), we see that

\mathcal{B}(\alpha)\in I=\left[\frac{1}{2}W_{0}^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p},W_{1}^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p}+C_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p}\rho^{2p+1}\right],

for some constant
C_{k_{0},m,p}>0
depending only on
k_{0},m,p
. We conclude that
\mathcal{B}(I)\subset I
.

Remark 2
.

Assumption
2
implies that the domain of
\mathcal{B}
is
[\frac{1}{4}W_{0}^{2p}\theta\rho^{2p},\infty)\supset I
. Had we changed the exponent in
\rho
in Assumption
2
, we would not necessarily be able to conclude that
I
is contained in the domain of
\mathcal{B}
and hence would not get a fixed point from these estimates.

We will show that actually
\mathcal{B}
is a contraction on
I
. To see this by Proposition
4.4
we have (using that in particular
\|v(\alpha)\|_{V^{s}}\leq\rho^{2}
)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{B(\alpha)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}\binom{2p+1}{j}\left\|(-\rho\sin(t)\sin(x))^{2p+1-j}j(v(\alpha)_{t})^{j-1}\partial_{\alpha}v(\alpha)_{t}\right\|_{V^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho}\sum_{j=1}^{2p+1}\binom{2p+1}{j}\rho^{2p+1-j}j(\rho^{2})^{j-1}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\rho^{2p-\frac{1}{2}},

so it follows that
\left|\partial_{\alpha}\mathcal{B(\alpha)}\right|
can be made smaller than
\frac{1}{2}
as long as
\rho\in(0,1)
is small enough depending only on
p,m,k_{0}
meaning
\mathcal{B}
is a contraction. This grants us a unique fixed point for
\mathcal{B}
on
I
.
Finally we insert the solution
\alpha\in I
of (
34
) into the bifurcation equation (
35
). For convenience we will solve the latter for
\omega^{2}
so we define

\mathcal{G}(\xi):=m+1-\frac{\xi^{\frac{2p+1}{2}}}{\rho}\int_{\mathbf{T}^{2}}F(\rho,t,x)\cos(t)\sin(x)dtdx,

where
\xi=\omega^{2}
. As above we note that if
\xi\in[W_{0}^{2},W_{1}^{2}]
then using
4.6

\mathcal{G}(\xi)\leq|\mathcal{G}(\xi)|\leq m+1+D_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p+1}\rho^{2p+1}\leq W_{1}^{2}

if

0<\rho^{2p+1}\lesssim\frac{W_{1}^{2}-(m+1)}{W_{1}^{2p+1}},

(37)

where we note that the right hand side of the above inequality is positive since
W_{1}^{2}>m+1
.
Furthermore,

\mathcal{G}(\xi)\geq m+1-D_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p+1}\rho^{2p+1}\geq W_{0}^{2}

if

0<\rho^{2p+1}\leq\frac{m+1-W_{0}^{2}}{D_{k_{0},m,p}W_{1}^{2p+1}},

(38)

for some constant
D_{k_{0},m,p}>0
. Note again that the right hand side above is positive since
m+1>W_{0}^{2}
. The continuity of
\mathcal{G}
on
[W_{0}^{2},W_{1}^{2}]
follows from the fact that
\alpha(\rho,\omega)
is continuous in
\omega
, which is a consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{B}
is a contraction (see Corollary 5.2.2 of
[
7
]
), and we conclude the existence a fixed point of
\mathcal{G}
and hence a solution
\omega(\rho)=\sqrt{\xi}\in[W_{0},W_{1}]
of (
35
) by the Brouwer fixed point theorem.

The final statement concerning the limits is clear for the bounds we obtained here and their dependence on
\rho
.
∎

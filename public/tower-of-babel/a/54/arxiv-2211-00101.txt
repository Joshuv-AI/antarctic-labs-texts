A General Decomposition Method for a Convex Problem Related to Total Variation Minimization
Stephan Hilb, Andreas Langer

Abstract
We consider sequential and parallel decomposition methods for a dual problem of a general total variation minimization problem with applications in several image processing tasks, like image inpainting, estimation of optical flow and reconstruction of missing wavelet coefficients. The convergence of these methods to a solution of the global problem is analysed in a Hilbert space setting and a convergence rate is provided. Thereby, these convergence result hold not only for exact local minimization but also if the subproblems are just solved approximately. As a concrete example of an approximate local solution process a surrogate technique is presented and analysed. Further, the obtained convergence rate is compared with related results in the literature and shown to be in agreement with or even improve upon them. Numerical experiments are presented to support the theoretical findings and to show the performance of the proposed decomposition algorithms in image inpainting, optical flow estimation and wavelet inpainting tasks.

Introduction

The dimensionality of images has been tremendously increased in recent years due to the improvement of hardware. In order to further post-process such large-scale data in a distributed parallel or memory-constrained setting, decomposition methods may be used, which split the original problem into a sequence of smaller subproblems that can be solved independently of each other while still approaching the original solution by means of an iterative algorithm. One
particular approach are domain decomposition algorithms
DolJolNat
;
QuaVal
;
TosWid
which subdivide the
problem domain. Typical examples of post-processing images include the removal of noise (denoising), the completion of missing data (inpainting) and the analysis of the data, as the computation of the optical flow in image sequences. In such applications one is usually interested in solutions in which edges are preserved. The total variation (TV) is well-know to promote discontinuities and hence is widely used in image processing tasks. Thereby one may consider the following regularized TV-model, cf.
HinKun

\inf_{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{m}\cap BV(\Omega)^{m}}\tfrac{1}{2}\|Tu-g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}|Du|,

The crucial difficulty of deriving decomposition methods for total variation minimization problems lies in the fact that the total variation is non-differentiable and non-additive with respect to a disjoint splitting of the domain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|D(u_{\mid_{\Omega_{1}}}+u_{\mid_{\Omega_{2}}})|

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{1}}|D(u_{\mid_{\Omega_{1}}})|+\int_{\Omega_{2}}|D(u_{\mid_{\Omega_{2}}})|

A failure of a decomposition method for total variation minimization has been reported in

6
Surrogate Technique

A surrogate iteration substitutes minimization of one functional for
minimization of different, simpler functionals at the cost of an additional
iterative process.
In particular one can substitute the minimization problem
\inf_{p\in K}\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda p-T^{*}g\|_{B^{-1}}^{2}
by the iteration

\displaystyle\inf_{p^{n+1}\in K}

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda p^{n+1}-f^{n}\|_{W}^{2},\qquad f^{n}=\Lambda p^{n}-\tfrac{1}{\tau}B^{-1}(\Lambda p^{n}-T^{*}g),

producing iterates
(p^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for some initialization
p^{0}\in V
that
converge to the same minimizer, provided
\tau\in(\|B^{-1}\|,\infty)
.
Though its properties have been studied extensively in e.g.
mairal2013optimization
, we will analyze it as a nested subalgorithm of
our decomposition scheme for approximate minimization following the
notion from
Definition

3.1
.
The main motivation for the surrogate technique in our case is to rid the local
problems from the dependence on the potentially costly operator
B^{-1}
.

To that end for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we introduce an auxiliary functional
\mathcal{D}_{i}^{s,n}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{s,n}(v_{i},w_{i}):=\mathcal{D}(p_{i-1}^{n}+(v_{i}-\theta_{i}p^{n}))+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}-w_{i})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

with
\tau>\|B^{-1}\|
for
v_{i},w_{i}\in\theta_{i}K
and
i=1,\ldots,M
, whereby
\|u\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}:=\langle u,(\tau I-B^{-1})u\rangle_{W}
for
u\in W
.

Algorithm 3

Surrogate approximation

1:

N_{\text{sur}}\in\mathbb{N}
,

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
i\in\{1,\dotsc,M\}
,

p^{n}\in K
,
p_{i-1}^{n}\in K

2:

\tilde{v}_{i}^{n}\in\theta_{i}K

3:

v_{i}^{n,0}=\theta_{i}p_{i-1}^{n}

4:

for

\ell=0,1,\ldots,N_{\text{sur}}-1

do

5:

v_{i}^{n,\ell+1}\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{v_{i}\in\theta_{i}K}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{s,n}(v_{i},v_{i}^{n,\ell})

6:

end

for

7:

\tilde{v}_{i}^{n}=v_{i}^{n,N_{\text{sur}}}

We note that the subproblems in

3
can be written as

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{i}\in\theta_{i}K}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{s,n}(v_{i},v_{i}^{n,\ell})

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{i}\in\theta_{i}K}\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(p_{i-1}^{n}+(v_{i}-\theta_{i}p^{n}))-T^{*}g\|_{B^{-1}}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}-v_{i}^{n,\ell})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{i}\in\theta_{i}K}\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda v_{i}-f_{i}^{n}\|_{W}^{2},

where

.
The dependence on the operator
B^{-1}
has thereby been
moved into the preparation of fixed data

for every subproblem, while

.

Lemma 6.1
.

Using notation and assumptions from
Algorithm

3
the functional
\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}:V_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v):=\mathcal{D}(p_{i-1}^{n}-\theta_{i}p^{n}+v),

has quadratic growth in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v)-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(\hat{v})\geq\tfrac{1}{2\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|}\|\Lambda(v-\hat{v})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

for any minimizer
\hat{v}\in\theta_{i}K
of
\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}
.

Proof.

Using
Lemma

4.5
and optimality of
\hat{v}\in\theta_{i}K
we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v)-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(\hat{v})

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(p_{i-1}^{n}+(\hat{v}-\theta_{i}p^{n})),v-\hat{v}_{i}\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\|v-\hat{v}\|_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(v-\hat{v})\|_{B^{-1}}^{2}.

Further noting that
\tau I-B^{-1}
is positive definite, since
\tau>\|B^{-1}\|
,

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(v-\hat{v})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}\leq\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|\Lambda(v-\hat{v})\|_{W}^{2}\leq\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|\|\Lambda(v-\hat{v})\|_{B^{-1}}^{2}.

Combining both inequalities yields the statement.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Using notation and assumptions from
Algorithm

3
and
Lemma

6.1
the surrogate iterates
(v_{i}^{n,\ell})_{\ell}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell})-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell+1})

\displaystyle\geq\eta(\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell})-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(\hat{v}_{i}^{n}))

\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

,

\displaystyle\eta=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{4\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|}&\text{if $\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}$}\\
1-\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{i}^{s,n}(v,w)=\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v)+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(w-v)\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell+1})+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}^{n,\ell}-v_{i}^{n,\ell+1})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathcal{D}_{i}^{s,n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell+1},v_{i}^{n,\ell})

\displaystyle\quad=\min_{v_{i}\in\theta_{i}K}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i})+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}^{n,\ell}-v_{i})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\min_{\mu\in[0,1]}\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}((1-\mu)v_{i}^{n,\ell}+\mu\hat{v}_{i}^{n})+\tfrac{\mu^{2}}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}^{n,\ell}-\hat{v}_{i}^{n})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\min_{\mu\in[0,1]}(1-\mu)\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell})+\mu\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(\hat{v}_{i}^{n})+\tfrac{\mu^{2}}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}^{n,\ell}-\hat{v}_{i}^{n})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2},

where we searched for the minimum along the line

,
\mu\in[0,1]
, and used convexity afterwards.
After reordering we use the quadratic growth property from

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell})-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell+1})-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}^{n,\ell}-v_{i}^{n,\ell+1})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\max_{\mu\in[0,1]}\mu(\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell})-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(\hat{v}_{i}^{n}))-\tfrac{\mu^{2}}{2}\|\Lambda(v_{i}^{n,\ell}-\hat{v}_{i}^{n})\|_{\tau I-B^{-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\max_{\mu\in[0,1]}\big(\mu-\mu^{2}\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|\big)(\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(v_{i}^{n,\ell})-\mathcal{D}_{i}^{n}(\hat{v}_{i}^{n})).

Discarding the last term on the left-hand side and evaluating the maximum
optimally at

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{4\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|}&\text{if $\|\tau I-B^{-1}\|\|B\|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}$}\\

∎

9
Conclusion

We have seen that it is possible to improve the domain decomposition
convergence rate results from
ChaTaiWanYan
by making use of different
proof techniques from alternating minimization.
Since as in
ChaTaiWanYan

Algorithm

2
has a slight
advantage over
Algorithm

1
in terms of iteration count, it suggests that
there is still room for improvement of
\alpha
in
Theorem

4.2
in the
sequential case.

We could easily apply
Algorithms

1
and
2
to a wider range of local
image processing tasks, namely inpainting and optical flow estimation, while
global operators could only be decomposed by means of the surrogate technique,
which incurred an additional cost.
When considering the total number of iterations of the inner subalgorithm,

Algorithms

1
and
2
did not differ substantially in terms of convergence
speed from the global one, i.e. not decomposing the problem, which suggests a
minor overhead of the decomposition method.
A runtime improvement is only to be expected by parallel execution of the
subproblems which we managed to verify in a parallel implementation.
Using the decomposition methods in a memory-constrained computing environment
is expected to be possible.

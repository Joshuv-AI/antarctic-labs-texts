On the compactness of the Weyl operator in 𝒮 ω \mathcal{S}_{\omega}
Vicente Asensio, Chiara Boiti, David Jornet, Alessandro Oliaro

Abstract
. We characterize, using time-frequency analysis, the continuity and compactness of the Weyl operator in global classes of ultradifferentiable functions 𝒮 ω \mathcal{S}_{\omega} , for weight functions ω \omega in the sense of Braun, Meise and Taylor. As a consequence, we give results about the compactness of the localization operator in 𝒮 ω \mathcal{S}_{\omega} , in relation with the spaces of ω \omega -multipliers and ω \omega -convolutors of 𝒮 ω \mathcal{S}_{\omega} . Moreover, we provide several examples that complement our investigation.

Primary 47B07, 42B35; Secondary 42C20, 46F12;

1.
Introduction

In 1988, Daubechies
[
22
]
introduced localization operators to localize a signal both in time and frequency, although a particular case had already been considered by Berezin in 1971. They are also known in some cases as Toeplitz operators or anti-Wick operators. These operators were traditionally defined on
L^{2}
or on some Sobolev spaces, but they can be defined on more general spaces, called modulation spaces. The modulation spaces
M^{p,q}

(1\leq p,q\leq\infty)
were introduced by Feichtinger as appropriate classes of functions or distributions to discuss certain problems related to time-frequency analysis. For
p=q=2
, we get
M^{p,q}=L^{2}
and the appropriate weighted versions of the modulation spaces produce Sobolev spaces. Several authors have studied the compactness of localization operators when acting on modulation spaces. In
[
23
]
, the authors characterized the symbols
F
in the modulation class
M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with the property that the localization operator with symbol
F
and windows
\varphi,\psi
, denoted by
L^{F}_{\varphi,\psi}
, is a compact operator when acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every pair of windows
\varphi,\psi
in the Schwartz class. Boggiatto
[
10
]
proved that each
F\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
vanishing at infinity defines a compact localization operator on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. However, there exists a compact localization operator whose symbol
F
is a bounded function with constant modulus, thus not vanishing at infinity
[
24
]
. Boundedness and Schatten-class conditions of localization operators were investigated in
[
19
,
20
]
. Boiti and De Martino
[
16
]
gave a sufficient condition for the compactness of the localization operator in modulation spaces with exponential weights, thus extending partially the work in
[
23
]
.
Bastianoni, Cordero and Nicola studied in
[
8
]
properties of eigenfunctions of compact localization operators.

One of earliest forms of pseudodifferential operator arises in quantum mechanics in 1931 introduced by Weyl as a rule that associates a self-adjoint operator
a^{w}(x,D)
with a real-valued symbol
a(x,\xi)
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Localization operators can be also represented as this type of pseudodifferential operators, the so-called Weyl operators. Sjöstrand
[
33
]
showed that if the symbol belongs to the modulation class
M^{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
(called Sjöstrand class), then the corresponding Weyl operator is continuous on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Continuity of Weyl operators on modulation classes was studied by Gröchenig, Heil, Toft and Czaja, among others
[
21
,
27
,
34
]
. A very important tool is the study of the continuity of the Wigner transform between different classes of modulation spaces
[
34
]
. In particular, if the symbol belongs to the Sjöstrand class and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, then the corresponding Weyl operator is bounded on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
26
, 14.5.2]
and, if the symbol can be approximated in
M^{\infty,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
by functions in the Schwartz class, then the corresponding Weyl operator is compact on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see, for instance,
[
9
]
). In
[
24
]
, the authors proved that, if the symbol is a tempered distribution and, for some
1<p,q<\infty
, the corresponding Weyl operator is compact on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of the symbol vanishes at
infinity, that is, it belongs to
M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and it is the limit in
M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
of a sequence of functions in the Schwartz class. Moreover, when the symbol belongs to the Sjöstrand class, the authors characterized when the Weyl operator is compact for every
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
.

In this work, we characterize the continuity and compactness of the Weyl operator when defined in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, modulated by a weight function
\omega
in the sense of Braun, Meise and Taylor
[
17
]
. We use purely techniques from time-frequency analysis and give the characterization in terms of the STFT of the symbol of the operator. In our setting, the importance of the class
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is twofold. On the one hand, we obtain a scale of classes including, for particular selections of
\omega
, the Schwartz class, or, for instance, many Gelfand-Shilov spaces. On the other hand, these classes appear in a natural way in time-frequency analysis in relation with modulation spaces with exponential weights, since they are the intersection of modulation spaces with exponential weights. Therefore, several problems related with these classes can be studied with time-frequency analysis. We also give some consequences to the compactness of localization operators on
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and obtain relations with the spaces of
\omega
-multipliers and
\omega
-convolutors in the sense of
[
2
,
3
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we give the necessary notation, definitions and known results used in the following sections. In Section
3
we characterize the spaces of multipliers and convolutors introduced in
[
2
,
3
]
in terms of the STFT, thus extending the previous work in
[
7
]
. Since a particular case of the Weyl operator is the multiplication operator, we study in Section
4
the compactness of the multiplication operator on
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In Section
5
we characterize the compactness of the Weyl operator on
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and provide several examples. Finally, in Section
6
, we give consequences for the study of the compactness of the localization operator in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and give a concrete example of a compact localization operator whose symbol is not an
\omega
-convolutor. We also obtain some further results about the compactness of the localization operator in related spaces.

2.
Preliminaries

We consider weight functions in the sense of Braun, Meise, and Taylor
[
17
]
:

Definition 2.1
.

A non-quasianalytic weight function
\omega:[0,+\infty[\to[0,+\infty[
is an increasing and continuous function satisfying:

(\alpha)

There exists
L\geq 1
such that
\omega(2t)\leq L\omega(t)+L
,
t\geq 0
;

(\beta)

\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{1+t^{2}}dt<+\infty
;

(\gamma)

\log(t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\to\infty
;

(\delta)

The function
\varphi_{\omega}(t)=\omega(e^{t})
is convex.

A weight function, or simply, weight, is extended to
\mathbb{C}^{d}
in the following way:
\omega(\xi)=\omega(|\xi|)
,
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, where
|\xi|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
.
The
Young conjugate
\varphi^{\ast}_{\omega}:[0,+\infty[\to[0,+\infty[
of
\varphi_{\omega}
is given by
\varphi^{\ast}_{\omega}(t)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\varphi_{\omega}(s)\}
.

In the following we will consider also subadditive weights, i.e. weights satisfying

(\alpha^{\prime})\qquad\qquad\omega(s+t)\leq\omega(s)+\omega(t),\quad s,t\geq 0.

We observe that
(\alpha^{\prime})
is stronger than
(\alpha)
.

The
Fourier transform
of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is denoted by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-it\cdot\xi}f(t)dt,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then, the inverse of the Fourier transform is given by

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}f(\xi)d\xi,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

When
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, we can also consider
partial Fourier transforms
. For example, the second partial Fourier transform is denoted by

\mathcal{F}_{2}(f)(x,\xi):=\mathcal{F}_{t\mapsto\xi}(f)(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-it\cdot\xi}f(x,t)dt,\qquad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},

and the inverse of the partial Fourier transform is given analogously. The definitions of Fourier and partial Fourier transform can be extended in a standard way to more general spaces of functions or distributions.

Definition 2.2
.

For a weight function
\omega
, we define the space
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as those
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that (
f,\widehat{f}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and) for all
\lambda>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{\alpha}f(x)|e^{\lambda\omega(x)}<+\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi)|e^{\lambda\omega(\xi)}<+\infty.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the strong dual space of
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We refer to
[
11
,
13
]
for equivalent characterizations of

\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
If
\omega(t)=\log(1+t)
, it is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e. the Schwartz class.

Now, we recall some basic notation from time-frequency analysis that we will use in the paper. The
translation, modulation
, and
phase-shift operators
are denoted, for
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, by

T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x),\qquad\ M_{\xi}f(t)=e^{it\cdot\xi}f(t),\qquad\ \Pi(z)f(t)=M_{\xi}T_{x}f(t)=e^{it\cdot\xi}f(t-x).

Given
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
, that we will call
window
, the
short-time Fourier transform
of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(or simply STFT) is denoted, for
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, by

V_{\psi}f(z):=\langle f,\Pi(z)\psi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(y)\overline{\psi(y-x)}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}dy.

Here the bracket
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is consistent with the inner product in
L^{2}
, since we consider distributions as conjugate-linear functionals.

By
[
28
, Theorem 2.7]
, given any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if, for any
\lambda>0
,

p_{\lambda}(f):=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|V_{\psi}f(z)|e^{\lambda\omega(z)}<+\infty.

Moreover, the family of seminorms
\{p_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
defines a fundamental system of seminorms for
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
12
, Proposition 2.10]
(see
[
26
, Corollary 11.2.6]
for polynomial weights). See also
[
5
, Theorem 9]
. If
\mathcal{I}f(t)=f(-t)
denotes the reflection operator of any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
cross-Wigner transform
\Wig(g,f)
of two functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is denoted, for
z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, by

(2.1)

\Wig(g,f)(z):=2^{d}e^{2ix\cdot\xi}\langle g,\Pi(2z)\mathcal{I}f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x+y/2)\overline{f(x-y/2)}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}dy.

If
g,f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then we have
\Wig(g,f)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
since
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is invariant under partial Fourier transform. We write
\Wig f:=\Wig(f,f)
.

We also introduce, following
[
18
]
(cf.
[
11
]
), the
Wigner-like transform of
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
by

(2.2)

\Wig[f](z):=\mathcal{F}_{2}(\mathcal{T}f)(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x+y/2,x-y/2)e^{-iy\cdot\xi}dy,\qquad z=(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},

where

(2.3)

\mathcal{T}f(x,y)=f(x+y/2,x-y/2)\qquad\qquad\mathcal{T}^{-1}f(x,y)=f\Big(\frac{x+y}{2},x-y\Big).

We observe that
\Wig[f]
is invertible in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
since
\mathcal{T}
is a linear invertible change of variables, and the inverse is given by
\Wig^{-1}[f]=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(f)
.

For
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the
Weyl operator

a^{w}(x,D)
applied to
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the distribution defined by

(2.4)

\langle a^{w}(x,D)f,g\rangle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle a,\Wig(g,f)\rangle,\qquad g\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

So, we have that
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The Weyl operator can be interpreted as

(2.5)

a^{w}(x,D)f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{i(x-s)\cdot\xi}a\Big(\frac{x+s}{2},\xi\Big)f(s)dsd\xi,\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
and
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
, if
a(x,\xi)
is a function satisfying
[
6
, Definition 3.1]
, then the operator (
2.5
) is continuous from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself; see also
[
4
, Section 3]
.

The
localization operator
with symbol
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and windows
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is defined as the composition of the following three operators:

(2.6)

L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}=V^{\ast}_{\gamma}\circ{\mathcal{M}}_{a}\circ V_{\psi},

where
V_{\psi}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is the short-time Fourier transform operator with respect to the window
\psi
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is the
multiplication operator
by the ultradistribution
a\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, i.e.
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}(f):=af,
for
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, and
V^{\ast}_{\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the adjoint of the short-time Fourier transform with respect to the window
\gamma
. It is obvious that, if
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
, then, by the continuity of the STFT in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
12
, Prop. 2.9]
, the localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is linear and continuous (see also, for example,
[
19
, Section 1]
, and the references therein).
For
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the localization operator can be seen as a Weyl operator via the formula (see, for instance
[
19
]
, and the references therein)

(2.7)

L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}=(a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi))^{w}(x,D),

that is, the
Weyl symbol
of
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}
is given by

a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi).

If
E
and
F
are two Hausdorff locally convex spaces and
T:E\to F
is a linear operator, we say that
T
is
compact
(
bounded
) if there exists a
0
-neighbourhood
U
in
E
such that
T(U)
is relatively compact (bounded) in
F
. Observe that any compact or bounded linear operator
T:E\to F
is also continuous. When
E=F=\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, since this space is Fréchet-Montel, any bounded linear operator
T:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is also compact. Our aim is to characterize the compactness of the Weyl operator
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is equivalent to characterize the boundedness of such operator.

In the following of this section, we consider a subadditive weight function
\omega
and the spaces
L^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, for
1\leq p,q<+\infty
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, of measurable functions
F:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(x,\xi)|^{p}e^{\lambda p\omega(x,\xi)}dx\Big)^{q/p}d\xi\Big)^{1/q}<+\infty,

with standard extensions when
p=+\infty
or
q=+\infty
.
For
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, we write
L^{p}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for
L^{p,p}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. For instance:

L^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})=\Big\{F\ \text{measurable on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2d}\ :\ \esup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}|F(x,\xi)|e^{\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}<+\infty\Big\}.

Then we denote the corresponding modulation spaces by

M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ :\ V_{\psi}f\in L^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\big\},

endowed with the norm
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert V_{\psi}f\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
. The modulation spaces do not depend on the choice of the windows
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
, since different windows give equivalent norms
[
12
, (3.13)]
. We refer to
[
12
, Section 3]
for more information on modulation spaces in this context.

The following lemma is crucial to characterize the boundedness of the Weyl operator in this setting. The proof is straightforward and can be found in
[
1
, Lemma 25]
(see also
[
31
, Remark 4.3]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X:=\proj_{m}X_{m}
and
Y:=\proj_{m}Y_{m}
be Fréchet spaces such that
X=\cap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}X_{m}
with each
(X_{m},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m})
a Banach space and
Y=\cap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}Y_{m}
with each
(Y_{m},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{m})
a Banach space. Moreover, assume that
X
is dense in
X_{m}
and
X_{m+1}\subseteq X_{m}
with continuous inclusion for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
Y_{m+1}\subseteq Y_{m}
with continuous inclusion for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
T:X\to Y
be a linear operator. We have:

(i)

T:X\to Y
is continuous if and only if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
T
has a unique continuous linear extension
T_{m,n}:X_{m}\to Y_{n}
.

(ii)

T:X\to Y
is bounded if and only if there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
T
has a unique continuous linear extension
T_{k_{0},m}:X_{k_{0}}\to Y_{m}
.

The modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are Banach spaces (
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p,q\leq+\infty
) (see
[
26
, Theorem 11.3.5]
for the case of polynomial weights; the proof is similar in the present context). We also know that (see
[
12
, Remark 3.6]
and
[
13
, Theorem
2.5(h)^{\prime}
]
)

\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{\lambda>0}M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\bigcap_{\lambda>0}M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

We have that
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a dense subspace of
M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1\leq p,q<+\infty
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

[
12
, Proposition 3.9]
. It is easy to check that the inclusion
M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous for all
\lambda>\mu
and
1\leq p,q\leq+\infty
. Hence, the last intersections for
\lambda>0
can be replaced by the intersections for
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since we can describe the space
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with fundamental systems of seminorms with
L^{p,q}
-norms of the STFT (
[
12
, Proposition 2.10]
and
[
13
, Theorem 2.5]
) we have

\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\proj_{m\in\mathbb{N}}M^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Now we easily deduce the following result from Lemma
2.3
:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\omega
be subadditive and
T:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a linear operator, and let
1\leq p,q<+\infty
and
1\leq\bar{p},\bar{q}\leq+\infty
.

(i)

T
is continuous if and only if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that the operator has a unique continuous linear extension
T_{n,m}:M^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{\bar{p},\bar{q}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(ii)

T
is compact if and only if there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
the operator has a unique continuous linear extension
T_{k_{0},m}:M^{p,q}_{k_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{\bar{p},\bar{q}}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

If
1\leq p,q<+\infty
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, then the dual space of
M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\prime}=M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
12
, Theorem 3.8]
,
where
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
. Moreover,
given
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
,
the duality is given by

\langle f,h\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}V_{\psi}f(z)\overline{V_{\psi}h(z)}dz,

for
f\in M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
h\in M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Example 2.5
.

When
a\equiv 1
, then (see for example
[
12
, (2.22)]
)
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}=(2\pi)^{d}\langle\gamma,\psi\rangle I
, where
I
is the identity operator on
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is continuous. Hence, if
\langle\gamma,\psi\rangle\neq 0
,
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}
cannot be compact.

3.
Multipliers and convolutors

We recall the following definition from
[
2
, Definition 3.1]
:

Definition 3.1
.

A function
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
\omega
-slowly increasing
, or simply, an
\omega
-multiplier
if and only if for all
\lambda>0
there exist
C_{\lambda},\mu_{\lambda}>0
such that

(3.1)

|D^{\alpha}a(x)|\leq C_{\lambda}e^{\lambda\varphi^{\ast}_{\omega}\big(\frac{|\alpha|}{\lambda}\big)}e^{\mu_{\lambda}\omega(x)},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We denote
O_{M}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the space of all
\omega
-multipliers.

In
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
it is proved that a function
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an
\omega
-multiplier if and only if

4.
Compactness of the multiplication operator

A particular case of Weyl operator is the multiplication operator (see Section
5
below). In this section, we show that such an operator is compact only when it is the multiplication by zero. First, we recall that the
spectrum
of a linear operator
T:E\to E
defined on a Hausdorff locally convex space
E
is the set

\sigma(T;E):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\ \lambda\cdot I-T\ \text{is not invertible}\}.

Theorem 4.1
.

The multiplication operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if
a=0
.

Proof.

We consider
a\in O_{M}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(if not,
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
is not continuous, nor compact by
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
). In particular,
a
is continuous. First, we check that the image of
a
is contained in the spectrum of the multiplication operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e.

(4.1)

a(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq\sigma({\mathcal{M}}_{a};\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Indeed, fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, for each function
g
in the image of
\{a(x)I-{\mathcal{M}}_{a}\}
, we have
g(x)=a(x)g(x)-a(x)g(x)=0
. Since there exist
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
f(x)\neq 0
, we obtain that
a(x)I-{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
cannot be surjective. Therefore
a(x)\in\sigma({\mathcal{M}}_{a};\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

If
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}
is compact, then by
[
29
, Ch. 5, Part 2, Th. 4]
, the set
\sigma({\mathcal{M}}_{a};\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is either finite or the closure of a sequence that tends to zero. We claim that the function
a
is constant. If not, fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
|a|
is always positive in
\overline{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}
. If
m<M
are the minimum and maximum value attained by
|a|
in
\overline{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}
at
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the function

g(\lambda)=|a|((1-\lambda)x_{1}+\lambda x_{2}),\ \ 0\leq\lambda\leq 1,

satisfies
g([0,1])=[m,M]
(by the intermediate value theorem), which is contained in the image of the function
|a|
. This is a contradiction with the inclusion (
4.1
). Hence, the multiplication operator becomes the multiplication by a complex number, and is compact only in the case that this complex number is zero.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

The multiplication operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{a}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if
a=0
.

Given
a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
convolution operator

{\mathcal{C}}_{a}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined by
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}(f):=a\ast f
, for each
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is well defined and continuous. Moreover, if
\log(t)=o(\omega(t))
as
t\to\infty
, the converse also holds
[
3
, Theorem 5.3]
. Regarding compactness, we have

Theorem 4.3
.

Given
a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the convolution operator
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if
a=0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a topological isomorphism in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is compact if and only if
\mathcal{F}\circ{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
is also compact. Since
a\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see, for instance, the comments after Theorem 3.2 in
[
3
]
), by
[
25
, Kap. I, §6, Satz 6.10]
, we have
\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{C}}_{a}(f))={\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{F}(a)}(\widehat{f})
for every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, the conclusion follows by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

\langle a^{w}(x)f,g\rangle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle a\otimes 1,\Wig(g,f)\rangle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle a(x),\int\Wig(g,f)(x,\xi)d\xi\rangle.

From the fact that
\int\widehat{F}(\xi)d\xi=\int e^{i\xi\cdot 0}\widehat{F}(\xi)d\xi=(2\pi)^{d}F(0)
for
F\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we obtain

\int\Wig(g,f)(x,\xi)d\xi=\int\mathcal{F}_{t\mapsto\xi}\left(g\Big(x+\frac{t}{2}\Big)\overline{f\Big(x-\frac{t}{2}\Big)}\right)d\xi=(2\pi)^{d}g(x)\overline{f(x)}.

Therefore,

\langle a^{w}(x)f,g\rangle=\langle a(x),g(x)\overline{f(x)}\rangle=\langle a(x)f(x),g(x)\rangle,

which shows that
a^{w}(x)={\mathcal{M}}_{a}
.

So, in this case, the operator
a^{w}(x):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous if and only if
a\in O_{M}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and, by Theorem
4.1
,

a^{w}(x):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if
a=0.

(b) Similarly, if we take
a=a(\xi)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it is easy to see that

a^{w}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)\ast f.

Indeed

\displaystyle\langle a^{w}(D)f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle 1\otimes a,\Wig(g,f)\rangle=\langle 1\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a),\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{2}(\Wig(g,f))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 1_{x}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)(t),g(x+t/2)\overline{f(x-t/2)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)(t),\int g(x+t/2)\overline{f(x-t/2)}dx\rangle=\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)(t),\int g(s)\overline{f(s-t)}ds\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)(t),g\ast\overline{\mathcal{I}f(t)}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)\ast f,g\rangle.

This shows, by Theorem
4.3
, that

a^{w}(D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if
a=0

and, for weights satisfying
\log(t)=o(\omega(t))
,
t\to\infty
, we have, using
[
3
, Theorem 6.1]
, that
a^{w}(D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous if and only if
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a)\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
a\in O_{M}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

In general, the continuity of the Weyl operator in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is not characterized by the

-multipliers:

Example 5.4
.

(a)
Consider

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle\delta_{x}\otimes a_{2},\Wig(g,f)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{x}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a_{2})(t),g(x+t/2)\overline{f(x-t/2)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a_{2})(t),g(t/2)\overline{f(-t/2)}\rangle

\displaystyle=2^{d}\langle\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a_{2})(2s),g(s)\overline{\mathcal{I}f(s)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 2^{d}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(a_{2})(2s)\mathcal{I}f(s),g(s)\rangle.

Therefore,

So, since

\displaystyle=\langle a_{1}(x),\int g(x+t/2)\overline{f(x-t/2)}dt\rangle

is not an
\omega

(b)

\displaystyle=f(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{1}(F)(y+2\xi,\xi)

Since now we deal with modulation spaces with exponential weights that depend on weight functions, we assume that the weight functions
\omega
are subadditive. In order to characterize the continuity and compactness of the Weyl operator in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
via Proposition
2.4
, we first give a version of
[
35
, Prop. 4.7]
(see also
[
36
]
) for modulation spaces with exponential weights.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\omega
be a subadditive weight function and assume that
T
is a linear and continuous map from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
into
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
and fix
\lambda,\mu>0
. The operator
T
extends (uniquely) to a continuous mapping from
M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
into
M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
if and only if there exists
K\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})
such that

K\in M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}}),

that is, given
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})\setminus\{0\}
,

(5.1)

\esup_{(x,y,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d_{2}+2d_{1}}}|V_{\psi}K(x,y,\xi,\eta)|e^{\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}e^{-\mu\omega(y,\eta)}<+\infty,

and satisfying

(5.2)

(Tf)(x)=\langle K(x,\cdot),\overline{f}\rangle,\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}).

Proof.

First, we assume that there exists the extension of
T:M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\to M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is nuclear (see, e.g.
[
14
,
15
]
), there is (a unique)
K\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})
satisfying (
5.2
). We show that the estimate (
5.1
) holds.
From the continuity of the extension of
T
, it follows that there exists
C>0
such that

(5.3)

|\langle K,g\otimes\overline{f}\rangle|=|\langle Tf,g\rangle|\leq\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})},

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
. Consider
\psi=g\otimes\overline{f}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})
. We replace
f
and
g
in (
5.3
) with

f_{y,\eta}(t):=\Pi(y,-\eta)f(t)=e^{-it\cdot\eta}f(t-y)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{x,\xi}(t):=\Pi(x,\xi)g(t)=e^{it\cdot\xi}g(t-x)

and we have

\displaystyle\langle K,g_{x,\xi}\otimes\overline{f_{y,\eta}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int K(s,t)e^{-is\cdot\xi}\overline{g(s-x)}e^{-it\cdot\eta}f(t-y)dsdt

\displaystyle=\int K(s,t)\overline{\psi(s-x,t-y)}e^{-i(s,t)\cdot(\xi,\eta)}dsdt=V_{\psi}K(x,y,\xi,\eta).

Therefore, from (
5.3
), we obtain

(5.4)

|V_{\psi}K(x,y,\xi,\eta)|\leq C\left\lVert f_{y,\eta}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}\left\lVert g_{x,\xi}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}.

Now, we estimate the right-hand side of (
5.4
). First, we compute,
for
\psi_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle V_{\psi_{1}}f_{y,\eta}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}e^{-it\cdot\eta}f(t-y)\overline{\psi_{1}(t-u)}e^{-it\cdot v}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}}e^{-it\cdot(v+\eta)}f(t-y)\overline{\psi_{1}(t-u)}dt

\displaystyle=e^{-iy\cdot(v+\eta)}V_{\psi_{1}}f(u-y,v+\eta),\qquad(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d_{1}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\lVert f_{y,\eta}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d_{1}}}|V_{\psi_{1}}f(u-y,v+\eta)|e^{\mu\omega(u,v)}dudv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d_{1}}}|V_{\psi_{1}}f(u,v)|e^{\mu\omega(u+y,v-\eta)}dudv.

Since
\mu>0
, then we estimate
\mu\omega(u+y,v-\eta)\leq\mu\omega(u,v)+\mu\omega(y,\eta)
, so we have

\left\lVert f_{y,\eta}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}\leq e^{\mu\omega(y,\eta)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}.

Thus, as
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

(5.5)

\left\lVert f_{y,\eta}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}\leq C_{1}e^{\mu\omega(y,\eta)}.

We proceed similarly for
g_{x,\xi}
: given
\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})\setminus\{0\}
, one can see

\displaystyle V_{\psi_{2}}g_{x,\xi}(u,v)=e^{-ix\cdot(v-\xi)}V_{\psi_{2}}g(u-x,v-\xi),\qquad(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d_{2}}.

So, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert g_{x,\xi}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}}}|V_{\psi_{2}}g(u,v)|e^{-\lambda\omega(u+x,v+\xi)}dudv.

For
\lambda>0
, we have
-\lambda\omega(u+x,v+\xi)\leq-\lambda\omega(x,\xi)+\lambda\omega(u,v)
. Thus, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

(5.6)

\left\lVert g_{x,\xi}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\leq e^{-\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\leq C_{2}e^{-\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}.

Substituting (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) into formula (
5.4
), we find
C^{\prime}>0
such that

|V_{\psi}K(x,y,\xi,\eta)|\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}e^{\mu\omega(y,\eta)},

and hence
K\in M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})
.

Since
M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
is a Banach space and the inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})\hookrightarrow M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
is dense and continuous, the equality
\langle K,g\otimes\overline{f}\rangle=\langle Tf,g\rangle
in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
can be extended to the modulation spaces
M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
(see for instance the comment in the proof of
[
26
, Theorem 14.4.1]
).

Assume now that the kernel
K
of
T
satisfies (
5.1
). We have

\displaystyle|\langle Tf,g\rangle|=|\langle K,g\otimes\overline{f}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})}\left\lVert g\otimes\overline{f}\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{(-\lambda)\otimes\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})},

for all
g\in M^{1}_{-\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
. Then,
Tf\in M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
and

\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})}\leq\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
. Hence,
T
extends to a continuous map from
M^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
to
M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}})
, and

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\op}\leq\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}+d_{1}})}.

The uniqueness of the extension follows from the density of
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
in
M^{1}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
.
∎

Example 5.7
.

Observe first that the Wigner transform can be defined for
f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\Wig(g,f)=\mathcal{F}_{2}\mathcal{T}(g\otimes\overline{f}),

where
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the second partial Fourier transform,
\mathcal{T}
is the (unitary) change of variables (
2.3
), and both complex conjugate and tensor product are defined on distributions. Then, a corresponding Moyal formula holds in the frame of ultradistributions; indeed, for
h,k\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
it is known that
\Wig(k,h)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, and we have

(5.8)

\langle\Wig(g,f),\Wig(k,h)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{F}_{2}\mathcal{T}(g\otimes\overline{f}),\mathcal{F}_{2}\mathcal{T}(k\otimes\overline{h})\rangle=\langle g\otimes\overline{f},k\otimes\overline{h}\rangle=\langle g,k\rangle\overline{\langle f,h\rangle},

where the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
indicates here the (conjugate-linear) duality product
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}
-
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}
. Now let
a=\Wig(g,f)
for some
f,g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
h,k\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we then have by (
5.8
):

\langle a^{w}(x,D)h,k\rangle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle\Wig(g,f),\Wig(k,h)\rangle=(2\pi)^{-d}\langle g,k\rangle\overline{\langle f,h\rangle}=\langle(2\pi)^{-d}\overline{\langle f,h\rangle}g,k\rangle

that means that

a^{w}(x,D)h=(2\pi)^{-d}\overline{\langle f,h\rangle}g.

Then,
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since this operator has rank one, it is compact.

By Example
5.7
, the function
a\in O_{M}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
given in Example
5.4
(b)
cannot be represented as
\Wig(g,f)
for

. On the other hand, if
b(x,\xi)=e^{2ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)
with

, then
b^{w}(x,D):\,\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is well defined, continuous, and compact. In fact,

b=\Wig(\psi,1)

for some suitable
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, since

\Wig(\psi,1)(x,\xi)=\int\psi(x+y/2)e^{-iy\cdot\xi}dy=2^{d}\int\psi(s)e^{-2i(s-x)\cdot\xi}ds=2^{d}e^{2ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\psi}(2\xi).

Similarly, if we consider
c(x,\xi)=e^{-2ix\cdot\xi}g(x)
, for some

\Wig(\delta,\gamma)(x,\xi)=2^{d}e^{2ix\cdot\xi}V_{\mathcal{I}\gamma}\delta(2x,2\xi)=2^{d}e^{2ix\cdot\xi}\overline{\mathcal{I}\gamma(-2x)}=2^{d}e^{2ix\cdot\xi}\overline{\gamma(2x)},

and so

\Wig(\gamma,\delta)(x,\xi)=\overline{\Wig(\delta,\gamma)(x,\xi)}=2^{d}e^{-2ix\cdot\xi}\gamma(2x).

Now, we characterize the compactness of the Weyl operator via the STFT of the symbol. We need some preparation. Fixed
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\setminus\{0\}
, we set
\Psi:=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(\psi)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. We check that (compare it with the formula in the proof of
[
35
, Prop. 4.8]
)

(5.9)

V_{\Psi}K(x-y/2,x+y/2,\xi+\eta/2,-\xi+\eta/2)=(2\pi)^{-d}e^{iy\cdot\xi}V_{\psi}a(x,\xi,\eta,y).

To show (
5.9
), we see what happens when commuting the phase-shift operator and
\mathcal{T}^{-1}
, where the operator
\mathcal{T}
is given in (
2.3
):

Lemma 5.8
.

For every
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\Pi(x-y/2,x+y/2,\xi+\eta/2,-\xi+\eta/2)\mathcal{T}^{-1}\psi=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\Pi(x,-y,\eta,\xi)\psi,\qquad x,y,\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\Pi(x-y/2,x+y/2,\xi+\eta/2,-\xi+\eta/2)\mathcal{T}^{-1}\psi(u,v)

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{i((\xi+\eta/2)\cdot u+(-\xi+\eta/2)\cdot v)}\mathcal{T}^{-1}\psi(u-x+y/2,v-x-y/2)

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{i\big(\frac{u+v}{2}\cdot\eta+(u-v)\cdot\xi\big)}\psi\Big(\frac{u+v}{2}-x,(u-v)-(-y)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad=\Pi(x,-y,\eta,\xi)\psi\Big(\frac{u+v}{2},u-v\Big),

which gives the result.
∎

We also use the next formula that relates the behaviour of the short-time Fourier transform of the second partial Fourier transform of
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
to that of
a
(compare it to
[
26
, (3.6)]
).

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\setminus\{0\}
. We have

V_{\psi}a(x,-y,\eta,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d}e^{iy\cdot\xi}V_{\mathcal{F}_{2}(\psi)}\mathcal{F}_{2}(a)(x,\xi,\eta,y),\qquad x,\xi,\eta,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that, for
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\displaystyle T_{(x_{1},x_{2})}\mathcal{F}_{2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{2}(M_{(0,x_{2})}T_{(x_{1},0)}\psi),

\displaystyle M_{(y_{1},y_{2})}\mathcal{F}_{2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{2}(M_{(y_{1},0)}T_{(0,-y_{2})}\psi),

\displaystyle\Pi(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})\mathcal{F}_{2}(\psi)

\displaystyle=e^{ix_{2}\cdot y_{2}}\mathcal{F}_{2}(\Pi(x_{1},-y_{2},y_{1},x_{2})\psi).

Then, by the definition of the STFT,

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{F}_{2}(\psi)}\mathcal{F}_{2}(a)(x,\xi,\eta,y)

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{F}_{2}(a),\Pi(x,\xi,\eta,y)\mathcal{F}_{2}(\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-iy\cdot\xi}\langle\mathcal{F}_{2}(a),\mathcal{F}_{2}(\Pi(x,-y,\eta,\xi)\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}\langle a,\Pi(x,-y,\eta,\xi)\psi\rangle,

from which we obtain the result.
∎

For
\Psi:=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(\psi)
, by (
5.7
) and
Lemma
5.8
we have that

\displaystyle V_{\Psi}K(x-y/2,x+y/2,\xi+\eta/2,-\xi+\eta/2)

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle K,\Pi(x-y/2,x+y/2,\xi+\eta/2,-\xi+\eta/2)\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle\mathcal{T}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(a),\mathcal{T}^{-1}\Pi(x,-y,\eta,\xi)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{2}(\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(a),\Pi(x,-y,\eta,\xi)\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{2}(\psi)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=V_{\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{2}(\psi)}\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(a)(x,-y,\eta,\xi).

Hence, by Lemma
5.9
(replacing
\psi
and
a
by
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(\psi)
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}^{-1}(a)
), we see that the equality in (
5.9
) holds and therefore by Propositions
5.5
and
2.4
(since
V_{\psi}a
of any
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous
[
28
, Theorem 2.4]
, we can replace the essential supremum by the supremum in the result below):

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\omega
be a subadditive weight function,
0\neq\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, and
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

(i)

The Weyl operator acts
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if for every
\lambda>0
, there exists
\mu>0
such that

(5.10)

\sup_{x,\xi,\eta,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V_{\psi}a(x,\xi,\eta,y)|e^{\lambda\omega(x-y/2,\xi+\eta/2)}e^{-\mu\omega(x+y/2,-\xi+\eta/2)}<+\infty.

(ii)

The Weyl operator
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if there exists
\mu>0
such that for every
\lambda>0
, (
5.10
) is satisfied.

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
\omega
be a subadditive weight function and let
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. If the Weyl operator
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact, then
a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

Proof.

Fix
0\neq\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. By Theorem
5.10
(ii)
, there exists
\mu>0
such that for every
\lambda>0
there exists
C_{\lambda}>0
such that

|V_{\psi}a(x,\xi,\eta,y)|\leq C_{\lambda}e^{-(\lambda+\mu)\omega(x-y/2,\xi+\eta/2)}e^{\mu\omega(x+y/2,-\xi+\eta/2)},\ \ x,\xi,\eta,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Now, since

-(\lambda+\mu)\omega(x-y/2,\xi+\eta/2))\leq-(\lambda+\mu)\omega(x,\xi)+(\lambda+\mu)\omega(-y/2,\eta/2)

and
\mu\omega(x+y/2,-\xi+\eta/2)\leq\mu\omega(x,\xi)+\mu\omega(y/2,\eta/2)
, we obtain

|V_{\psi}a(x,\xi,\eta,y)|\leq C_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}e^{(\lambda+2\mu)\omega(y,\eta)},\ \ x,\xi,\eta,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The result then follows by Theorem
3.3
.
∎

We note that the converse of Corollary
5.11
does not hold. In fact, the function in Example
5.4
(b)
is an
\omega
-convolutor. For
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,

\displaystyle V_{\psi}a(x,\xi,\eta,y)

\displaystyle=\int e^{-it\eta}e^{-isy}e^{-2its}f(s)\overline{\psi(t-x,s-\xi)}dtds

\displaystyle=\int e^{-i(t+x)\eta}e^{-isy}e^{-2i(t+x)s}f(s)\overline{\psi(t,s-\xi)}dtds

\displaystyle=e^{-ix\eta}\int e^{-is(y+2x)}e^{-it(\eta+2s)}f(s)\overline{\psi(t,s-\xi)}dtds

\displaystyle=e^{-ix\eta}\int e^{-is(y+2x)}f(s)\mathcal{F}_{1}\big(\overline{\psi}\big)(\eta+2s,s-\xi)ds.

Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, we have that

f(X)\mathcal{F}_{1}\big(\overline{\psi}\big)(Y,Z)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{3d}_{(X,Y,Z)}).

Since the change of variables

X=s,Y=\eta+2s,Z=s-\xi

is invertible, we have

f(s)\mathcal{F}_{1}\big(\overline{\psi}\big)(\eta+2s,s-\xi)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{3d}_{(s,\eta,\xi)}).

Therefore,

\mathcal{F}_{s\mapsto w}\Big(f(s)\mathcal{F}_{1}\big(\overline{\psi}\big)(\eta+2s,s-\xi)\Big)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{3d}_{(w,\eta,\xi)}),

and (with
w=y+2x
) for every
\lambda>0
there exists
C_{\lambda}>0
such that

|V_{\psi}a(x,\xi,\eta,y)|\leq C_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda\omega(y+2x)}e^{-\lambda\omega(\xi)}e^{-\lambda\omega(\eta)}\leq C_{\lambda}e^{-\lambda\omega(x,\xi)}e^{\lambda\omega(\eta,y)}.

6.
Applications and further results

In this section we apply our previous study to the compactness of the localization operator when acting from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself, and we also provide some examples and remarks. First, we observe that if
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is linear and continuous, so the localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is well defined if and only if it is continuous (Lemma
5.1
). By (
2.7
) and Theorems
5.6
and
5.10
, we obtain immediately the following characterization:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\omega
be a subadditive weight function. Let
\psi,\gamma,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-zero windows and
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

(i)

The localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is well defined (and continuous) if and only if for every
\lambda>0
there exists
\mu>0
such that
\Wig^{-1}[a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi)]\in M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
if and only if for every
\lambda>0
, there exists
\mu>0
such that

(6.1)

\esup_{x,\xi,\eta,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V_{\varphi}(a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi))(x,\xi,\eta,y)|e^{\lambda\omega(x-y/2,\xi+\eta/2)}e^{-\mu\omega(x+y/2,-\xi+\eta/2)}<+\infty.

(ii)

The localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact if and only if there exists
\mu>0
such that
\Wig^{-1}[a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi)]\in\bigcap_{\lambda>0}M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
if and only if there exists
\mu>0
such that for every
\lambda>0
, (
6.1
) is satisfied.

Then we have:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\omega
be a subadditive weight function and
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Consider the following conditions:

(i)

The Weyl operator
a^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact.

(ii)

a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

(iii)

The localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We have
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
follows from Corollary
5.11
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: If
a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then
a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for any
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and hence
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}=(a\ast\Wig(\gamma,\psi))^{w}(x,D):\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Example 6.3
.

We have seen in Section
5
that
b(x,\xi)=e^{2ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)
, being
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, coincides with
\Wig(\psi,1)
for some
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Therefore, using
[
26
, Lemma 14.5.1(b)]

(proved with the duality product
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}
-
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}
), we have, for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,

\displaystyle V_{\Wig\gamma}(\Wig(\psi,1))(x,y,\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d/2}e^{-iy\cdot\eta}\overline{V_{\gamma}\psi(x+\eta/2,y-\xi/2)}V_{\gamma}1(x-\eta/2,y+\xi/2)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d/2}e^{-iy\cdot\eta}\overline{V_{\gamma}\psi(x+\eta/2,y-\xi/2)}e^{-i(x-\eta/2)\cdot(y+\xi/2)}\overline{\widehat{\gamma}(-y-\xi/2)}.

Since
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, we have that for all
\lambda>0
there exists
C>0
such that

|V_{\Wig\gamma}(\Wig(\psi,1))(x,y,\xi,\eta)|\leq Ce^{-\lambda\omega(x+\eta/2,y-\xi/2)}\leq Ce^{-\lambda\omega(x,y)}e^{\lambda\omega(\xi,\eta)}.

Hence, by Theorem
3.3
, we have
b\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. We can proceed similarly for
c(x,\xi)=e^{-2ix\cdot\xi}g(x)
, for some
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and show that
c\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Hence,
L^{b}_{\psi,\gamma}
and
L^{c}_{\psi,\gamma}
are compact when acting from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by Theorem
6.2
.

Remark 6.4
.

If
a(x,\xi)=e^{-2ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)
for some
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the symbol considered in Example
5.4
(b)
, we see that

a
is an
\omega
-convolutor. Indeed, for
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we consider
\psi:=\psi_{1}\otimes\psi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. We have for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2}),\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
that

\displaystyle V_{\psi}a(z,\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}a(x,\xi)\overline{\psi(x-z_{1},\xi-z_{2})}e^{-i(x,\xi)\cdot(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{-2ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)\overline{\psi_{1}(x-z_{1})}\overline{\psi_{2}(\xi-z_{2})}e^{-i(x\cdot\eta_{1}+\xi\cdot\eta_{2})}dxd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\eta_{1}}\overline{\psi_{1}(x-z_{1})}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\xi)\overline{\psi_{2}(\xi-z_{2})}e^{-i\xi\cdot(2x+\eta_{2})}d\xi\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\eta_{1}}\overline{\psi_{1}(x-z_{1})}V_{\psi_{2}}f(z_{2},2x+\eta_{2})dx.

Now assume that there exist
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
b>0
such that
\omega(t)\geq c+b\log(1+t)
. Since
\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
\mu>0
there exists
C_{\mu}>0
such that

|\overline{\psi_{1}(x-z_{1})}|\leq C_{\mu}e^{-\mu\omega(x-z_{1})}\leq C_{\mu}e^{-\mu\omega(z_{1})}e^{\mu\omega(x)}

and as
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists
C^{\prime}_{\mu}>0
such that

\displaystyle|V_{\psi_{2}}f(z_{2},2x+\eta_{2})|\leq C^{\prime}_{\mu}e^{-2(\mu+(d+1)/b)\omega(z_{2},2x+\eta_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{\mu}e^{-\mu\omega(z_{2})}e^{-(\mu+(d+1)/b)\omega(2x+\eta_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{\mu}e^{-\mu\omega(z_{2})}e^{-(\mu+(d+1)/b)\omega(x)}e^{(\mu+(d+1)/b)\omega(\eta_{2})}.

Thus, we obtain that for all
\mu>0
there exists
\lambda_{\mu}=\mu+(d+1)/b>0
such that

|V_{\psi}a(z,\eta)|\leq C_{\mu}C^{\prime}_{\mu}e^{-\mu(\omega(z_{1})+\omega(z_{2}))}e^{\lambda_{\mu}\omega(\eta_{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{d+1}{b}\omega(x)}dx.

The integral converges and hence, by Theorem
3.3
, we obtain that
a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

It follows, by Theorem
6.2
, that
the localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
However, we have also seen in Example
5.4
(b)
that the corresponding Weyl operator
a^{w}(x,D)
, defined on
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, does not take values in

\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. So (ii) cannot imply (i) in Theorem
6.2
.

On the other hand, that (iii) implies (ii) is also far to be true. In fact, we first observe that there is the following relation between the Wigner-like transform and the cross-Wigner transform: given two functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one has

\Wig[f\otimes\overline{g}]=\Wig(f,g),

where we denote
(f\otimes\overline{g})(x,y):=f(x)\cdot\overline{g(y)}
, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.
Now, we consider the projective tensor product
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that, as
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is metrizable and barrelled, it is also barrelled
[
30
, 15.6.6]
. Moreover, it is dense in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Since the Wigner-like transform is an isomorphism
from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
to
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and

A:=\text{span}\big\{\Wig(\varphi,\psi)\,:\,\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big\}

coincides with
\Wig[\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})]
, we deduce that
A
is a dense and barrelled subspace of
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Being
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
a nuclear space
[
14
,
15
]
, the induced topology in
A
from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is the same as the topology of
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, any ultradistribution
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
satisfies
a\in(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
whenever
a*\Wig(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for every
\psi,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In fact, for
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the convolution operator
\mathcal{C}_{a}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is obviously continuous. Its restriction to
A
,
\mathcal{C}_{a}|_{A}:A\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, has closed graph. Since
A
is barrelled with the induced topology from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
,
\mathcal{C}_{a}|_{A}
is also continuous. Finally, as
A
is dense in
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, its extension to
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is continuous and coincides with
\mathcal{C}_{a}
, which shows that
a
is an
\omega
-convolutor. In particular, we have that
\Wig^{-1}[a*\Wig(\varphi,\psi)]\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
for every
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is, in view of Theorem
6.1
, a stronger condition than

\text{Wig}^{-1}[a*\Wig(\varphi,\psi)]\in\bigcap_{\lambda>0}M^{\infty}_{\lambda\otimes(-\mu)}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),

for some
\mu>0
and every
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. So, (iii) cannot imply (ii) in Theorem
6.2
. Indeed, the following example gives a compact localization operator whose symbol is not an
\omega
-convolutor for some concrete windows.

Example 6.5
.

We recall the following notation from
[
26
]
, but adapted to our definition of Fourier transform. Given two functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we denote the
cross-ambiguity function
of
f
and
g
by

(6.2)

A(f,g)(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\big(t+\frac{x}{2}\big)\overline{g\big(t-\frac{x}{2}\big)}e^{-it\cdot\xi}dt=e^{\frac{i}{2}x\cdot\xi}V_{g}f(x,\xi).

We also denote by
\mathcal{U}
the
rotation operator

\mathcal{U}F(x,\xi)=F(\xi,-x)
of a function
F
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, and by
\mathcal{I}
the reflection operator introduced in Section
2
. Assume now that
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
. Using
[
26
, Lemma 4.3.4]
and the properties of the Fourier transform, we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\Wig(f,g)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}{\mathcal{F}\mathcal{U}A(f,g)}(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{2d}\mathcal{I}\,\mathcal{U}A(f,g)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2d}A(f,g)(-\xi,x)=(2\pi)^{2d}e^{-\frac{i}{2}x\cdot\xi}V_{g}f(-\xi,x)

On the other hand, by
[
26
, Lemma 4.3.1]
, with our definition of Fourier transform, we have

\Wig(f,g)(x,\xi)=2^{d}e^{2ix\cdot\xi}V_{\mathcal{I}g}f(2x,2\xi).

Hence, we obtain

\Wig(f,g)\Big(-\frac{\xi}{2},\frac{x}{2}\Big)=2^{d}e^{-\frac{i}{2}x\cdot\xi}V_{\mathcal{I}g}f(-\xi,x).

Consequently

(6.4)

V_{g}f(-\xi,x)=2^{-d}e^{\frac{i}{2}x\cdot\xi}\Wig(f,\mathcal{I}g)\Big(-\frac{\xi}{2},\frac{x}{2}\Big).

So, combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we deduce

(6.5)

\mathcal{F}\Wig(f,g)(x,\xi)=(\sqrt{2}\pi)^{2d}\Wig(f,\mathcal{I}g)\Big(-\frac{\xi}{2},\frac{x}{2}\Big).

Suppose that
d=1
and take now
f=\mathcal{I}g
and assume
\supp(f)\subseteq[a,b]
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
. As in
[
26
, Lemma 4.3.5]
, we deduce that

\mathcal{F}\Wig(f,\mathcal{I}f)(x,\xi)=(\sqrt{2}\pi)^{2d}\Wig(f,f)(-\xi/2,x/2)=0,\ \text{for}\ \xi\notin[-2b,-2a].

Now, we consider
a\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\widehat{a}(x,\xi)=1
for every
\xi\in[-2b,-2a]
and zero otherwise. In particular,
\widehat{a}\notin O_{M}^{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, and hence
a\notin(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Then

a\ast\Wig(f,\mathcal{I}f)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{a}\cdot\mathcal{F}\Wig(f,\mathcal{I}f))=\Wig(f,\mathcal{I}f)\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

So
L^{a}_{f,\mathcal{I}f}:\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
is compact.

Fernández and Galbis showed (
[
23
, Theorem 3.15]
and
[
24
, Theorem 4.7]
) that, for
a\in M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}
is compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
) for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if, given
0\neq g_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, for every
R>0
, the following holds:

(6.6)

\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\sup_{|\eta|\leq R}|V_{g_{0}}a(z,\eta)|=0.

By Theorem
3.3
, if
a\in(O_{C})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then it satisfies (
6.6
). Thus:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq+\infty
. If
a\in M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\cap(O_{C})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then the localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}
is compact for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when acting from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself and when acting from
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself.

We can also generalize Theorem
6.6
for every subadditive weight function
\omega
when
1\leq p,q<+\infty
. With minor modifications in the proof of
[
16
, Lemma 0.6]
, we obtain
[
16
, Theorem 0.7]
under a weaker assumption (in fact, observe that the exponential in the limit of the next result does not depend on the variable
\eta
):

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\omega
be subadditive and
1\leq p,q<+\infty
. Fix
\lambda\geq 0
. Given
0\neq g_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if
a\in M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
satisfies, for every
R>0
,

\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\sup_{|\eta|\leq R}|V_{g}a(z,\eta)|e^{\lambda\omega(z)}=0,

then
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}:M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compact for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

As a consequence, we obtain

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
\omega
be subadditive and
1\leq p,q<+\infty
. Fix
\lambda\geq 0
. If
a\in M^{\infty}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\cap(O_{C}^{\omega})^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, then the localization operator
L^{a}_{\psi,\gamma}
is compact for every
\psi,\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when acting from
\mathcal{S}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself and when acting from
M^{p,q}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into itself.

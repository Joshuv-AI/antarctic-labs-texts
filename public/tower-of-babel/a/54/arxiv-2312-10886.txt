Unconventional s -Wave Pairing with Point-Node–Like Gap Structure in UTe 2
Shingo Haruna 1, Takuji Nomura 2, and Hirono Kaneyasu 3

Abstract
We explore the pairing state and gap structure of UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe_{2}} using a six-orbital model which we call the f f - d d - p p model. Our model accurately reproduces the quasi-two-dimensional Fermi surfaces consistent with recent de Haas–van Alphen oscillation measurements and the ( 0 , ± π , 0 ) (0,\pm\pi,0) antiferromagnetic spin fluctuations observed by neutron scattering. We incorporate on-site Coulomb repulsion for f f electrons and solve the linearized Eliashberg equation within the third-order perturbation theory to investigate the superconducting symmetry in UTe 2 \mathrm{UTe_{2}} . The most likely state is found to be an s s -wave state with a highly anisotropic superconducting gap structure that exhibits a point-node–like behavior of the specific heat at low temperatures.



Since the discovery of superconductivity in
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
with a critical temperature
T_{c}=1.6
K
[
1

2
]
, this compound has been considered as a promising candidate for spin-triplet superconductors, mainly based on the following observations:
The upper critical field
H_{c2}
appears to exceed the Pauli-limiting field
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5

a

T_{c}

[
6
]
.
The uniform magnetic susceptibility along the
a
-axis exhibits a Curie–Weiss-like behavior, which suggests that
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is near ferromagnetic instability
[
1
]
.
Owing to this magnetic susceptibility behavior,
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
is considered as a paramagnetic end member of U-based ferromagnetic superconductors
[
7
]
.
In addition, multiple superconducting phases have been discerned under magnetic fields and pressure
[
8
,

,

,

,

,
13
,
14

,
5

.
However, recent NMR Knight shift measurements using high-quality samples (
T_{c}=2.1

[
16
]
.
Moreover, neutron scattering experiments have observed anti-ferromagnetic fluctuations at
\bm{Q}\approx(0,\pm\pi,0)

[
17
,
18
]
.
Furthermore, under high pressure, where a magnetic order phase is realized, the anti-ferromagnetic signals near
\bm{Q}\approx(0,\pm\pi,0)
are observed by the neutron scattering measurements
[
19
]
.
These recent observations provide a new perspective that is markedly distinct from that in the early stage.

In terms of the band structure of
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
, density functional theory (DFT) calculations with Coulomb interaction
U
(DFT+U, DFT+DMFT) accurately reproduce the metallic state, although DFT calculations without
U
predict an insulating state with a band gap at the Fermi level
[
20
,
21
,
22

DFT+U and DFT+DMFT calculations reproduce quasi-two-dimensional Fermi surfaces, consistent with angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) experiments
[
23
,
24
]
and de Haas–van Alphen (dHvA) effect
[
25

DFT+U calculations for

[
26
]
, which is consistent with the intensities reported by ARPES
[
23
,
24
]
.
However, this Fermi pocket is absent in DFT+U calculations for
U=2.0

[
25

.

Previous theoretical studies indicate that the ferromagnetic fluctuations or the anti-ferromagnetic ones at
\bm{Q}
lead to the spin-triplet pairing states
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
.
In an early theoretical approach to superconductivity, possible pairing symmetry was analyzed by solving the linearized Eliashberg equation within the random phase approximation (RPA) for a six-orbital periodic Anderson model (PAM)
[
26
]
.
The most possible state at ambient pressure was determined to be the spin-triplet
\mathrm{A_{u}}
or
\mathrm{B_{3u}}
state mediated by ferromagnetic fluctuations.
More recently, a mixed-dimensional PAM has been proposed as a minimal model for
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
that reproduces the observed Fermi surfaces and accounts for both the anti-ferromagnetic and ferromagnetic fluctuations
[
27
]
.
The analysis based on the linearized Eliashberg equation in this model suggests that spin-triplet states are possible pairing states even in the presence of anti-ferromagnetic fluctuations.
Moreover, another theoretical study revealed that Ising-like magnetic fluctuations lead to the spin-triplet superconductivity for a two-orbital model with a cylindrical Fermi surface
[
28
]
.

In contrast to the previous studies, we use the third-order perturbation theory (TOPT) to investigate the possibility of a pairing mechanism that is not associated with the magnetic fluctuations.
In TOPT, the effective pairing interaction is evaluated perturbatively to third order in the on-site Coulomb interaction.
While the RPA mechanism adequately takes into account the contributions from the exchange of strong magnetic fluctuations,
TOPT can include the contributions of third-order vertex correction terms absent in RPA
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
.
In contrast to the previous RPA calculations, we solve the linearized Eliashberg equation within TOPT and obtain the spin-singlet pairing state in line with the observed reduction in the NMR Knight shift.
The obtained orbital state is a highly anisotropic
s
-wave state which leads to point-node–like behavior of the specific heat in qualitative agreement with experimental observations.

Here, we introduce a model, which includes two uranium sites and two tellurium sites within a unit cell, with the same parametrization as the previously proposed PAM
[
26
]
.
Although the setting of the hopping parameters is the same as those in Ref.
[
26
]
, numerical values in our model are different.
The model proposed herein is called the “
f
-
d
-
p
model”, since, in our view, the term “Anderson model” should be reserved for cases without direct hoppings between the impurity-like localized
f
orbitals.
The 5
f
and 6

p

[
26
]
.
The Hamiltonian of the

-

-

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{I}
, where
\hat{H}_{0}
and
\hat{H}_{I}
are the tight-binding Hamiltonian and the on-site Coulomb interaction between

The parameters of the tight-binding Hamiltonian are determined to reproduce the band structure given by GGA+U calculation using WIEN2k
[
S1
]
, which includes the atomic spin-orbit coupling.
Detailed information regarding the tight-binding model is available in the supplemental material
[

]
.

Next, we focus on the spin susceptibility, which is given as follows:

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}(Q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}\sigma^{\mu}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{4}}\chi^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(Q)\sigma^{\nu}_{\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}},

(1)

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(Q)

\displaystyle=-\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k}\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\zeta_{3}\zeta_{1}}(k)\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\zeta_{4}\zeta_{2}}(k+Q),

(2)

where
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(k)
denotes the thermal Green’s function for non-interacting particles, and
\sigma^{\mu}
represents the
\mu
component of the Pauli matrices.

\zeta_{i}\equiv(l_{i},\sigma_{i})
is the combined index for orbital (
l_{i}
) and spin (
\sigma_{i}
).
The four-dimensional momentum arguments are
k\equiv(\bm{k},i\omega_{n})
and
Q=(\bm{Q},i\Omega_{n})
, where
\omega_{n}\equiv(2n+1)\pi T
and
\Omega_{n}\equiv 2n\pi T
are the fermionic and bosonic Matsubara frequencies, respectively.
Fig.
1

\chi^{(0)}(\bm{Q})=\chi^{(0)}(\bm{Q},i\Omega_{n}=0)
in the
Q_{z}=0

The peaks at
\bm{Q}\approx(0,\pm\pi,0)
are consistent with neutron scattering experiments
[
17
,
18
]
.
The
(0,\pm\pi,0)
anti-ferromagnetic peaks are likely due to the nesting between regions with rich
f

\beta

To investigate possible pairing states in
\mathrm{UTe_{2}}
, we solve the linearized Eliashberg equation, expressed as:

\displaystyle\lambda\Delta_{a}(k)=-\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k^{\prime}a^{\prime}}V^{S/T}_{aa^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})\left|\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{a^{\prime}}(k^{\prime})\right|^{2}\Delta_{a^{\prime}}(k^{\prime}),

(3)

where
\lambda
and
\Delta_{a}(k)
represent the eigenvalue and anomalous self-energy, respectively.

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{a}(k)
is the thermal Green’s function for the diagonalized band
a
, and
V^{S/T}_{aa^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})
is the effective pairing interaction, which represents the pair scattering amplitude from
(k^{\prime},-k^{\prime})
to
(k,-k)
for singlet/triplet channels.
In TOPT, we evaluate the effective interaction to third order in on-site Coulomb repulsion
U
for
f
electrons, as presented diagramatically in Fig.
2
.
While the diagrams in Fig.
2
(a) – (c) are incorporated in the interactions considered also within the RPA, the diagrams in Fig.
2
(d) – (g), referred to as vertex correction terms, are not included in the RPA calculations.
The vertex correction terms can provide momentum-dependent contributions which are not mediated by magnetic fluctuations, despite TOPT being a finite-order approximation.
The momentum dependency of the eigenfunction
\Delta_{a}(k)
determines the pairing symmetry and superconducting gap structure.
The superconducting transition point is determined when the maximum eigenvalue reaches unity.
Therefore, comparing the magnitudes of eigenvalues enables us to identify the most likely pairing state.

In our study, we calculate the specific heat below

within the BCS approximation to study the relation between the point-node–like gap structure and the

We assume that the gap function is expressed by the product of the momentum and temperature dependent parts,
\Delta_{a}(\bm{k},T)=\Delta_{a}(\bm{k})\Delta(T)

\Delta_{a}(\bm{k})
is determined above.

The numerical results of the specific heat are depicted in Fig.

The calculated specific heat exhibits a

This temperature dependency is in qualitative agreement with experimental results

In principle, the specific heat exhibits an exponential behavior at low temperatures for the fully gapped superconducting state.
However, our calculated results demonstrate this exponential behavior of the specific heat only at extremely low temperatures, making it impossible to observe in experiments.

Finally, we discuss the cause of the unconventional anisotropic
s
-wave pairing, by explicitly giving the momentum dependency of the pairing interaction.
The pairing interaction
V^{S/T}_{aa^{\prime}}(\bm{k}~i\pi T,\bm{k}^{\prime}~i\pi T)
represents the pair scattering amplitude from
(\bm{k}^{\prime},-\bm{k}^{\prime})
on band
a^{\prime}
to
(\bm{k},-\bm{k})
on band
a
for singlet/triplet channels.
Figure
5
illustrates the momentum dependency of the pairing interaction
V^{S}_{\beta\beta}(\bm{k}~i\pi T,\bm{k}^{\prime}~i\pi T)
, where the
\bm{k}^{\prime}
is fixed on the
\beta
Fermi surface as shown by the blue arrow.
While the effective pairing interaction is strongly repulsive around
\bm{k}\approx\bm{k}^{\prime}
, it is attractive around
\bm{k}\approx-\bm{k}^{\prime}
(Fig.
5
(a)).
This attractive interaction enables the linearized Eliashberg equation to have a non-trivial solution without sign change in the anomalous self-energy.
The attraction is caused by the vertex corrections of Fig.
2
(f) and (g), as shown in Fig.
5
(b), where the
\bm{k}
-dependency of the vertex corrections is extracted.
Figure
5
(b) indicates that the vertex corrections yield the attractive interaction in a wide region of the momentum space, which is strongly attractive particularly at
\bm{k}\approx-\bm{k}^{\prime}
.
This attractive interaction gives rise to the highly anisotropic
s
-wave pairing state without sign change in the order parameter.
To investigate why the
s
-wave pairing state is favored despite of the strong on-site Coulomb repulsion, we calculate the orbital-resolved pairing amplitude defined as
\mathcal{F}_{l_{1}l_{2}}(\bm{k},\tau)\equiv\expectationvalue{c_{\bm{k} l_1}(\tau) c_{-\bm{k} l_2}}
.
The calculated intra-site
\mathcal{F}_{ff}(\bm{k},i\pi T)
and inter-site pairing amplitude
\mathcal{F}_{ff^{\prime}}(\bm{k},i\pi T)
on the Fermi surfaces in the
k_{z}=0
plane are illustrated in Fig.
6
(a) and (b), respectively.
As shown in Fig.
3
(c), the superconducting gap has the maximum value on the
\beta
surface in the
k_{z}=0
plane.
Around the
\bm{k}
point where the gap takes the maximum value, the inter-site pairing amplitude also takes the maximum value as depicted in Fig.
6
(b).
On the other hand, the intra-site pairing amplitude becomes small in the maximally gapped region, although it does not negligibly vanish there.
Due to this inter-site pairing mechanism, Cooper pairs can be formed in the
s
-wave channel while suppressing the on-site Coulomb energy.
Next, we discuss the origin of the point-node–like structure.
The pairing interaction on the
\alpha
-bands is illustrated in Fig.
7
.
In Fig.
7
(a), the momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
is fixed at the largely gapped point and, the negative, i.e., attractive scattering amplitude is seen, which allows the linearized Eliashberg equation to have non-trivial solutions.
However, when the momentum
\bm{k}^{\prime}
is fixed at the nodal point, the scattering amplitude is very small on the Fermi surface as shown in Fig.
7
(b) and it suggests that the nodal points neither contribute to the positive nor negative pair scattering amplitude.
This means that the electrons around the nodal points do not participate in pair-formation scattering.

In summary, we have discussed the most likely superconducting state, gap structure and momentum dependence of the pairing interaction using the
f
-
d
-
p
model.
Although the parameterization of the
f
-
d
-
p
model is the same as that of the periodic Anderson model
[
26
]
, the numerical values of the parameters are different.
Thus, the
f
-
d
-
p
model reproduces the quasi-two-dimensional Fermi surfaces and anti-ferromagnetic fluctuations in good agreement with dHvA
[
25
]
and neutron scattering
[
18
,
17
]
experiments, respectively.
To investigate the superconductivity, we have solved the linearized Eliashberg equation within TOPT.
Although our investigation was restricted to the case without magnetic fields under ambient pressure, we have obtained the highly anisotropic
s
-wave state with point-node–like gap structure, which yields the
T^{3}
behavior of the specific heat at low temperatures in qualitative agreement with experiments
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
,
45
]
.
The attractive component of the pairing interaction, which leads to this unconventional anisotropic
s
-wave pairing, arises from the third-order vertex corrections, although the RPA-like contributions of magnetic fluctuations are included up to third order.
The spin-singlet states align with the recent NMR Knight shift measurements
[
16
]
.
Our results suggest that the possibility of spin-singlet pairing states should not be entirely dismissed yet, particularly in zero-field, and further investigation and verification of the pairing state are required.

\hat{c}_{\bm{k}\sigma}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}c_{\bm{k}f\sigma}&c_{\bm{k}d\sigma}&c_{\bm{k}f^{\prime}\sigma}&c_{\bm{k}d^{\prime}\sigma}&c_{\bm{k}p\sigma}&c_{\bm{k}p^{\prime}\sigma}\end{pmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}
.

c_{\bm{k}l\sigma}
and
c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}l\sigma}
represent the annihilation and creation operators for each orbital
l
and spin
\sigma
, respectively.
The momentum dependency of the Hamiltonian
H_{0}(\bm{k})
is formulated as

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bm{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{ff}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{fd}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{ff^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{fd^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{fp}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{fp^{\prime}}(\bm{k})\\
&\varepsilon_{dd}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{df^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{dd^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{dp}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{dp^{\prime}}(\bm{k})\\
&&\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}f^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}d^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}p}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\bm{k})\\
&&&\varepsilon_{d^{\prime}d^{\prime}}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{d^{\prime}p}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{d^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\bm{k})\\
&H.c.&&&\varepsilon_{pp}(\bm{k})&\varepsilon_{pp^{\prime}}(\bm{k})\\
&&&&&\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix},

(5)

where
\varepsilon_{ij}(\bm{k})
is the Hamiltonian element associated with the electron hopping from orbital
j
to orbital
i
.
Matrix elements
\varepsilon_{ij}(\bm{k})
for U–U hoppings are given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ff(dd)}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}f^{\prime}(d^{\prime}d^{\prime})}(\bm{k})=\varepsilon_{0}^{f(d)}+t_{2}^{f(d)}\Big(e^{-ik_{x}a}+e^{ik_{x}a}\Big),

(6)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{fd}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}d^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=t_{2}^{fd}\Big(e^{-ik_{x}a}-e^{ik_{x}a}\Big),

(7)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{ff^{\prime}(dd^{\prime})}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{f(d)}e^{-ik_{z}c/4}+t_{3}^{f(d)}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/2)}+e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/2)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/2)}+e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/2)}\Big]e^{ik_{z}c/4},

(8)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{fd^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{fd}e^{-ik_{z}c/4}+t_{3}^{fd}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/2)}+e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/2)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/2)}-e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/2)}\Big]e^{ik_{z}c/4},

(9)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{df^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{1}^{fd}e^{-ik_{z}c/4}-t_{3}^{fd}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/2)}+e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/2)}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/2)}-e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/2)}\Big]e^{ik_{z}c/4},

(10)

where
\varepsilon_{0}^{f(d)}
is the one-particle energy of the
f\;(d)
orbital.
Matrix elements for the hybridization between the U and Te2 sites are given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{fp}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{4}^{fp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}-e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(11)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{dp}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{4}^{dp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}+e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(12)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{fp^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-t_{4}^{fp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}-e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(13)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{dp^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-t_{4}^{dp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}+e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4-k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(14)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}p}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{4}^{fp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}-e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(15)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{d^{\prime}p}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{4}^{dp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}+e^{i(k_{x}a/2+k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(16)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{f^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-t_{4}^{fp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}-e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}\Big],

(17)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{d^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=-t_{4}^{dp}\Big[e^{i(-k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}+e^{i(k_{x}a/2-k_{y}b/4+k_{z}c/8)}\Big].

(18)

Finally,
\varepsilon_{ij}(\bm{k})
for Te2–Te2 hoppings are given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{pp}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}p^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=\varepsilon_{0}^{p},

(19)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{pp^{\prime}}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=t_{5}^{p}\Big[e^{-ik_{y}b/2}+e^{ik_{y}b/2}\Big].

(20)

III. Pairing Interaction

In our formulation, the pairing interaction
V^{S/T}_{aa^{\prime}}
in the band representation is related to the pairing interaction
V^{S/T}_{l_{1}l_{2},l_{3}l_{4}}
in the orbital representation as follows
[
S9
,
S10
]
:

\displaystyle V^{S/T}_{aa^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})=\sum_{l_{1}l_{2}l_{3}l_{4}}U^{*}_{l_{1}a}(\bm{k})U^{*}_{l_{2}a}(-\bm{k})V^{S/T}_{l_{1}l_{2},l_{3}l_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})U_{l_{3}a^{\prime}}(-\bm{k}^{\prime})U_{l_{4}a^{\prime}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}),

(21)

where
V^{S/T}_{aa^{\prime}}(k,k^{\prime})
is the pairing interaction for spin-singlet/triplet channels, and
l_{i}
and
a
are orbital and band indices, respectively.

U_{la}(\bm{k})
denotes the elements of the diagonalization matrix of the tight-binding Hamiltonian, that is,

\displaystyle c_{\bm{k}l\sigma}=\sum\limits_{a}U_{la}(\bm{k})c_{\bm{k}a\sigma}.

(22)

c_{\bm{k}l\sigma}
and
c_{\bm{k}a\sigma}
are the annihilation operators in the orbital and band representation, respectively.
The pairing interaction
V^{S}_{l_{1}l_{2},l_{3}l_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})
and
V^{T}_{l_{1}l_{2},l_{3}l_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})
are given by

\displaystyle V^{S}_{l_{1}l_{2},l_{3}l_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})\left(i\sigma^{y}\right)^{\dagger}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\left(i\sigma^{y}\right)_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}},

(23)

\displaystyle V^{T}_{l_{1}l_{2},l_{3}l_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})\delta_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}}V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})\left(i\sigma^{\mu}\sigma^{y}\right)^{\dagger}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\left(i\sigma^{\nu}\sigma^{y}\right)_{\sigma_{3}\sigma_{4}},

(24)

where
\zeta_{i}
denotes the orbital-spin combined index defined as
\zeta_{i}\equiv(l_{i},\sigma_{i})
.

The effective pairing interaction is perturbatively expanded to third order as follows:

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(a)}(k,k^{\prime})+V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(b)}(k,k^{\prime})+V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(c)}(k,k^{\prime})+V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(d)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~+V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(e)}(k,k^{\prime})+V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(f)}(k,k^{\prime})+V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(g)}(k,k^{\prime}).

(25)

The diagrammatic representations of
V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(a)}(k,k^{\prime}),\;\ldots,\;V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(g)}(k,k^{\prime})
are shown in Fig. 2 of the main text.
Each term of Eq. (
25
) is analytically given by

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(a)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}},

(26)

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(b)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{\{\xi_{i}\}}\chi^{(0)}_{\xi_{2}\xi_{4},\xi_{1}\xi_{3}}(k-k^{\prime})\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\zeta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\xi_{4}},

(27)

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(c)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\{\xi_{i}\}}\sum\limits_{\{\eta_{i}\}}\chi^{(0)}_{\xi_{2}\xi_{4},\xi_{1}\xi_{3}}(k-k^{\prime})\chi^{(0)}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{4},\eta_{1}\eta_{3}}(k-k^{\prime})

\displaystyle~~~\times\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\xi_{2},\xi_{3}\zeta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\eta_{2},\eta_{3}\xi_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\eta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\eta_{4}},

(28)

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(d)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k_{1}}\sum\limits_{\{\xi_{i}\}}\sum\limits_{\{\eta_{i}\}}\chi^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{3},\xi_{4}\xi_{2}}(-k+k_{1})\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{1}}(k_{1})

\displaystyle~~~\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{4}\eta_{3}}(k_{1}-k+k^{\prime})\Gamma^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\eta_{3},\xi_{2}\zeta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\xi_{3},\eta_{2}\xi_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\eta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\eta_{4}},

(29)

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(e)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k_{1}}\sum\limits_{\{\xi_{i}\}}\sum\limits_{\{\eta_{i}\}}\chi^{(0)}_{\xi_{2}\xi_{4},\xi_{3}\xi_{1}}(k+k_{1})\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(k_{1})

\displaystyle~~~\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}(k_{1}+k-k^{\prime})\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\eta_{2},\eta_{3}\zeta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\eta_{4}\xi_{2},\zeta_{3}\xi_{1}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\xi_{4}\zeta_{2},\xi_{3}\eta_{1}},

(30)

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(f)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k_{1}}\sum\limits_{\{\xi_{i}\}}\sum\limits_{\{\eta_{i}\}}\phi^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{2},\xi_{4}\xi_{3}}(k+k_{1})\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}(k_{1})

\displaystyle~~~\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}(k_{1}+k-k^{\prime})\Gamma^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{2},\eta_{3}\zeta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\eta_{2},\xi_{3}\xi_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\eta_{4}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\eta_{1}},

(31)

\displaystyle V_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}^{(g)}(k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k_{1}}\sum\limits_{\{\xi_{i}\}}\sum\limits_{\{\eta_{i}\}}\phi^{(0)}_{\xi_{2}\xi_{1},\xi_{3}\xi_{4}}(-k+k_{1})\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{2}\eta_{1}}(k_{1})

\displaystyle~~~\times\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\eta_{4}\eta_{3}}(k_{1}-k+k^{\prime})\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\eta_{3},\eta_{2}\zeta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{2},\zeta_{3}\eta_{4}}\Gamma^{(0)}_{\eta_{1}\zeta_{2},\xi_{3}\xi_{1}},

(32)

with

\displaystyle\chi^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(Q)

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k}\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\zeta_{3}\zeta_{1}}(k)\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\zeta_{4}\zeta_{2}}(k+Q),

(33)

\displaystyle\phi^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(Q)

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{T}{N}\sum\limits_{k}\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\zeta_{3}\zeta_{1}}(k)\mathcal{G}^{(0)}_{\zeta_{4}\zeta_{2}}(Q-k),

(34)

where
N
is the number of
\bm{k}
-mesh points and the summation with
\{\xi_{i}\}
or
\{\eta_{i}\}
denotes that with respect to the orbital and spin states.
The argument
Q
represents the four-dimensional momentum
(\bm{Q},i\Omega_{n})
, where
\Omega_{n}\equiv 2n\pi T
is the bosonic Matsubara frequencies.
The factor of
1/2
in Eq. (
31
) and (
32
) is necessary to avoid double counting of equivalent diagrams.
When these expressions are used in the Eliashberg equation Eq. (3) of the main text,
V^{(a)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})
has to be multiplied by a factor of
1/2
to avoid double counting of the first order
V^{(a)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}(k,k^{\prime})
.

\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}
is the antisymmetrized bare vertex given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(0)}_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}=I_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}-I_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{4}\zeta_{3}},

(35)

where
I_{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}}
is represented as follows:

\displaystyle I_{(l_{1}\sigma_{1})(l_{2}\sigma_{2}),(l_{3}\sigma_{3})(l_{4}\sigma_{4})}=\begin{cases}U_{l}&(l_{1}=l_{2}=l_{3}=l_{4}=l,\;\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{4}\neq\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{3})\\
0&(\mathrm{otherwise})\end{cases}.

(36)

In the
f
-
d
-
p
model, as the on-site Coulomb repulsion is set only at the
f
orbital,
U_{l}
is given by

\displaystyle U_{l}=\begin{cases}U&(\mathrm{for}\;l=f,f^{\prime})\\
0&(\mathrm{otherwise})\end{cases},

(37)

where the parameter
U
is set to 1.75 eV or 1.50 eV in this study.

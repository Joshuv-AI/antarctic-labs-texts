Flight Structure Optimization of Modular Reconfigurable UAVs
Yao Su, Ziyuan Jiao, Zeyu Zhang, Jingwen Zhang, Hang Li, Meng Wang, Hangxin Liu

Abstract
This paper presents a ga ( ga ) designed to reconfigure a large group of modular uav , each with different weights and inertia parameters, into an over-actuated flight structure with improved dynamic properties. Previous research efforts either utilized expert knowledge to design flight structures for a specific task or relied on enumeration-based algorithms that required extensive computation to find an optimal one. However, both approaches encounter challenges in accommodating the heterogeneity among modules. Our ga addresses these challenges by incorporating the complexities of over-actuation and dynamic properties into its formulation. Additionally, we employ a tree representation and a vector representation to describe flight structures, facilitating efficient crossover operations and fitness evaluations within the ga framework, respectively. Using cubic modular quadcopters capable of functioning as omni-directional thrust generators, we validate that the proposed approach can (i) adeptly identify suboptimal configurations ensuring over-actuation while ensuring trajectory tracking accuracy and (ii) significantly reduce computational costs compared to traditional enumeration-based methods.

III-C

Flight Structure Dynamics

Given a flight structure, its translational dynamics can be described as
[
31
]
:

M\,\boldsymbol{\ddot{X}}_{\mathcal{S}}=\prescript{W}{S}{\boldsymbol{R}}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prescript{S}{i}{\boldsymbol{R}}\,T_{i}\boldsymbol{\hat{z}})+M\,g\boldsymbol{\hat{z}},

(4)

where
\boldsymbol{\ddot{X}}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the linear acceleration of the flight structure,
g
is the gravitational acceleration,
\prescript{S}{i}{\boldsymbol{R}}
is a function of tilting and twisting angles (
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
) of
i
th module, and
T_{i}
refers to the magnitude of thrust generated by
i
th module,
\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}=\left[0,\,0,\,1\right]^{\mathsf{T}}
.

Its rotational dynamics can be described as
[
32
]
:

\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}\,\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}}=-\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathcal{S}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\boldsymbol{d}_{i}\times\prescript{S}{i}{\boldsymbol{R}}\,T_{i}\boldsymbol{\hat{z}}),

(5)

where
\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the total inertia matrix of the structure:

\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{J}_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\begin{bmatrix}y_{i}^{2}&-x_{i}y_{i}&0\\
-x_{i}y_{i}&x_{i}^{2}&0\\
0&0&x_{i}^{2}+y_{i}^{2}\end{bmatrix},

(6)

with
\boldsymbol{J}_{i}=Diag(J_{ix},\,J_{iy},\,J_{iz})
is the inertia matrix of each the module,
\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the angular acceleration of the structure.

Taking together, the complete dynamics model of the flight structure is:

\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\ddot{X}}_{\mathcal{S}}\\
\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{\frac{1}{M}}\prescript{W}{S}{\boldsymbol{R}}&0\\
0&\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{u}+\begin{bmatrix}g\boldsymbol{\hat{z}}\\
-\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathcal{S}})\\
\end{bmatrix},

(7)

where
\boldsymbol{u}
is the 6-
dof
wrench generated by all modules with

\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{u}&=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{u}_{X}\\
\boldsymbol{u}_{\Omega}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prescript{S}{i}{\boldsymbol{R}}\,T_{i}\boldsymbol{\hat{z}}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\boldsymbol{d}_{i}\times\prescript{S}{i}{\boldsymbol{R}}\,T_{i}\boldsymbol{\hat{z}})\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{J}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})\\
\boldsymbol{J}_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})\end{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{T},\\
\boldsymbol{\alpha}&=[\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}]^{\mathsf{T}},\boldsymbol{\beta}=[\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}]^{\mathsf{T}},\boldsymbol{T}=[T_{1},\cdots,T_{n}]^{\mathsf{T}}.\end{aligned}

(8)

Of note, due to the passive gimbal mechanism design and the low-level control of each module eliminating rotating torques in the z-axis
[
20
]
, there is
no rotation-induced torque
of propellers pass to the mainframe. Therefore, in the simplified dynamics model
Eq.

7
, the input of each module is only considered as a 3-axis force vector. Besides, the total inertia matrix is approximated as a constant matrix.

III-D

Force Decomposition-based Analysis

Utilizing the force decomposition method, we can transform the nonlinear relationship in
Eq.

8
to a linear one by defining
\boldsymbol{F}
as an intermediate variable
[
26
,
20

\boldsymbol{F}=\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}\\
\vdots\\
F_{n}\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{3n\times 1},F_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\sin\beta_{i}\\
-\sin\alpha_{i}\cos\beta_{i}\\
\cos\alpha_{i}\cos\beta_{i}\\
\end{bmatrix}T_{i},

(9)

Then, the 6-
dof
wrench
\boldsymbol{u}
can be rewritten as:

\boldsymbol{u}^{d}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{J}_{X}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})\\
\boldsymbol{J}_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})\end{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{T}=\boldsymbol{WF},

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 3n}
is a constant allocation matrix with full row rank. Treating
\boldsymbol{F}
as inputs to the system, its dynamics can be analyzed from a linear perspective. The real inputs tilting angle
\alpha_{i}
, twisting angle
\beta_{i}
, and thrust force
T_{i}
of each module are computed from
\boldsymbol{F}
using inverse kinematics.

IV
Flight Structure Optimization

To find the optimal flight structure that is over-actuated and dynamically optimized, we first isolate the configuration-related factors from the dynamics equations (allocation matrix
\boldsymbol{W}
and total inertia matrix
\boldsymbol{J}_{s}

IV-A

Dynamic Property Evaluation

The allocation matrix
\boldsymbol{W}
is derived as
[
12
]
:

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{W}=\boldsymbol{\bar{P}}\boldsymbol{\bar{R}}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{I}_{3}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{I}_{3}\\
\boldsymbol{\hat{d}}_{1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{\hat{d}}_{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\prescript{S}{1}{\boldsymbol{R}}&\cdots&0\\
&\ddots&\\
0&\cdots&\prescript{S}{n}{\boldsymbol{R}}\end{bmatrix},\end{split}

(11)

where
\boldsymbol{\bar{P}}\in\mathbb{R}^{6\times 3n}
,

\boldsymbol{\bar{R}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3n\times 3n}
,

\boldsymbol{\hat{d}}_{i}
is the skew symmetric matrix of
\boldsymbol{d}_{i}
. From the formulation of
\boldsymbol{\bar{P}}
, we can easily find that the translational dynamics (the first three rows of
\boldsymbol{W}
) are independent of the configuration. Therefore, we only focus on the rotational dynamics (the last three rows of
\boldsymbol{W}
) in this optimization problem. Besides, the total inertia
\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}
is also related to the structure configuration (see
Eq.

6
) which is not neglectable. Therefore, we define a matrix
\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}
as:

\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\hat{D}}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\hat{d}}_{1}&\cdots&\boldsymbol{\hat{d}}_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

(12)

Calculating the required thrust energy index
\lVert\boldsymbol{T}\rVert^{2}
and neglecting
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathcal{S}}\times\boldsymbol{J}_{\mathcal{S}}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{\mathcal{S}}
, we have:

\begin{split}\lVert\boldsymbol{T}\rVert^{2}=\lVert\boldsymbol{F}\rVert^{2}&=\boldsymbol{u}_{\Omega}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\hat{D}}^{\dagger T}\boldsymbol{\bar{R}}\boldsymbol{\bar{R}}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\hat{D}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{u}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\mathsf{T}}\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}^{\dagger T}\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}}\\
&\leq{\sigma}_{\text{max}}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}^{\dagger})^{2}\lVert\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}}\rVert^{2}\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\dot{\Omega}}_{\mathcal{S}},\end{split}

(13)

where
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
is the Moore–Penrose inverse of a matrix, and
{\sigma}_{\text{max}}(\cdot)
is the maximum singular value of a matrix.

Based on the above analysis, we formulate the flight structure evaluation function as :

\operatorname*{argmax}_{\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}}\quad-\lambda_{1}\text{cond}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}})-\lambda_{2}{\sigma}_{\text{max}}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}^{\dagger})^{2},

(14)

where
\text{cond}(\cdot)
is the condition number of a matrix. The left half of the objective function
-\text{cond}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}})
considers the controllability where the full-actuation constraint is implicitly included (
\text{cond}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}})=\text{Inf}
if
\text{rank}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}})<3
); the right half
-{\sigma}_{\text{max}}(\boldsymbol{\bar{D}}^{\dagger})^{2}
characterizes the thrust minimization criteria (
Eq.

13
),
\lambda_{1-2}
are the weighting parameters.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we devised a novel optimization method aimed at finding efficient flight structures for modular UAV systems. This method utilized genetic algorithms (GA) to address an energy-minimizing objective function while satisfying over-actuation constraints. By representing the configuration as a tree structure for customized crossover operations and as a position vector for fitness evaluation, the
ga
effectively tackled the optimization problem with significantly reduced computational costs compared to existing emulation-based algorithms for flight structure optimization. Based on our customized modular UAV system, simulation studies illustrated (i) the algorithm’s ability to identify efficient flight structures for a large number of modules, (ii) the superior dynamic performance of the resulted flight structure in tracking challenging trajectories, and (iii) the improvement in computational efficiency. Looking ahead, our future plan includes conducting experiments with physical platforms and further enhancing computational capabilities to enable online reconfiguration based on varying task constraints.

Logarithmic or algebraic: roughening of an active Kardar-Parisi-Zhang surface
Debayan Jana, Astik Haldar, Abhik Basu

Abstract
The Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation sets the universality class for growing and roughening of nonequilibrium surfaces without any conservation law and nonlocal effects. We argue here that the KPZ equation can be generalised by including a symmetry-permitted nonlocal nonlinear term of active origin that is of the same order as the one included in the KPZ equation. Including this term, the 2D active KPZ equation is stable in some parameter regimes, in which the interface conformation fluctuations exhibit sub-logarithmic or super-logarithmic roughness, with nonuniversal exponents, giving positional generalised quasi-long-ranged order. For other parameter choices, the model is unstable, suggesting a perturbatively inaccessible algebraically rough interface or positional short-ranged order. Our model should serve as a paradigmatic nonlocal growth equation.

In summary, we have proposed and studied an “active KPZ” equation, having a surface velocity
v_{p}
depending nonlocally on the surface gradients. Surprisingly, we find stable surfaces with positional generalised QLRO or generalised logarithmic roughness with
nonuniversal exponents
for wide-ranging choices of the model parameters, unlike the 2D KPZ equation. Physically, this is due to the competition between the nonlocal and local nonlinear terms and the lack of their renormalisation. At
d>2
, similar choices for the same parameters can suppress the KPZ roughening transitions, resulting into only smooth surfaces. Heuristically, a nonlocal part in
v_{p}
means a local large fluctuation can generate a propulsion not just
locally
, but
over large scales
, which when sufficiently strong can suppress local variations in
v_{p}
due to the local KPZ-nonlinear term. This in turn has the effect of reducing surface fluctuations. For other parameter choices, a KPZ-like perturbatively inaccessible rough phase is speculated. This may be explored by mode-coupling methods
Bhattacharjee 1998
. In that parameter space, the roughening transition survives at
d>2
, but with significantly different scaling properties, again with nonuniversal exponents. We hope our studies here will provide further impetus to study nonlocal effects on similar nonequilibrium surface dynamics models, e.g., the conserved KPZ
Sun et al. 1989
;
Caballero et al. 2018
and the
|{\bf q}|
KPZ
Besse et al. 2023
equations.

Appendix A
Active KPZ equation

We first construct the appropriate equation in terms of the vector field
{\bf v}({\bf r},t)\equiv{\bm{\nabla}}h
. Field
\bf v
has a definite physical interpretation: for an active membrane
\bf v
is the fluctuation of the local normal to the membrane surface
Chaikin and Lubensky 2000
. Noting that the Burgers velocity
\bf v
is a conserved vector field, it must follow a generic conservation law of the form

\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial t}=-\nabla_{j}J_{ij},

(9)

where
J_{ij}
is the velocity current.
In general
J_{ij}
can be decomposed as the sum of a symmetric part
J_{ij}^{s}=J_{ji}^{s}
and an antisymmetric part
J_{ij}^{a}=-J_{ji}^{a}
:
J_{ij}=J_{ij}^{s}+J_{ij}^{a}
. Since
\bf v
is purely irrolational, it can be expressed, via the Helmholtz theorem
Bladel 1958
;
Koenigsberger 1906
, solely in terms of its divergence, i.e.,
{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot{\bf v}\equiv{\cal D}
, which follows the equation

\frac{\partial{\cal D}}{\partial t}=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}J_{ij}=-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}J_{ij}^{s}.

(10)

Thus,
J_{ij}^{a}
plays no role in the dynamics of
\cal D
and hence of
\bf v
. Without any loss of generality, we then set
J_{ij}^{a}=0
. We further express
J_{ij}=J_{ij}^{s}
as

J_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}\mu_{j}+\partial_{j}\mu_{i}),

(11)

where
{\bm{\mu}}
is the “chemical potential” vector, which in general can have irrolational and solenoidal parts. The latter part does not contribute to the dynamics of
{\cal D}
and hence of
\bf v
, and hence can be set to zero, leaving
\bm{\mu}
purely irrolational. In that case,

\frac{\partial{\bf v}}{\partial t}=-\nabla^{2}{\bm{\mu}}.

(12)

The Burgers equation is given by

\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial t}=\nu\nabla^{2}v_{i}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\nabla_{i}v^{2}+f_{i}.

(13)

Therefore, in the linearised Burgers equation (
\lambda=0
),
{\bm{\mu}}=-\nu{\bf v}
, a local quantity, whereas for the Burgers equation

{\bm{\mu}}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}\nabla^{-2}{\bm{\nabla}}{v^{2}}
, a nonlocal quantity.

Now consistent with the conservation law form of the Burgers equation,
\mu_{i}
can be generalised from its form for the Burgers equation: It also admits, at the same order, a second contribution of the form
\sim\nabla_{j}(v_{i}v_{j})/\nabla^{2}
. Indeed, including this term the most general equation of
\bf v
retaining only up to the lowest order in nonlinearities and spatial gradients that now includes a chiral contribution is of the form

\frac{\partial{v_{i}}}{\partial t}=\nu\nabla^{2}{v_{i}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}{\nabla_{i}}v^{2}+\lambda_{1}\nabla_{j}(v_{i}v_{j})+\lambda_{2}\nabla_{j}e_{jm}(v_{i}v_{m})+f_{i}.

(14)

Both the additional nonlinear terms are nonlocal contributions to
\mu
.
Equation (
14
) reduces to the well-known Burgers equation
Forster et al. 1977
when
\lambda_{1}=0=\lambda_{2}
. Further, the tensor
e_{ij}
is the 2D totally antisymmetric matrix, with
e_{11}=0=e_{22}
and
e_{12}=1=-e_{21}
. Thus, the
\lambda_{2}
-term in Eq. (
14
) is the chiral contribution. Now, further demanding that
{\bf v}
is fully irrotational with
{\bf v}={\bm{\nabla}}h
, the “superfluid velocity” of an XY model, or the deviation of the local normal of an interface in the Monge gauge
Nelson et al. 1989
, we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\nu\nabla^{2}h+\frac{\lambda}{2}(\bm{\nabla}h)^{2}+\lambda_{1}Q_{ij}({\bf r})(\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{j}h)

(15)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}Q_{ij}({\bf r})e_{jm}(\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{m}h)+\eta,

where formally
Q_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}/\nabla^{2}
and is to be understood in terms of its Fourier transform.

Appendix B
Galilean invariance

Under the transformation
x_{i}^{\prime}\rightarrow x_{i}-(\lambda+2\lambda_{1})c_{i}t-\lambda_{2}e_{ij}c_{j}t
and
t^{\prime}\rightarrow t
, where
i
and
j
can take values
1
or
2
we have,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+(\lambda+2\lambda_{1})\textbf{c}\cdot\bm{\nabla}-\lambda_{2}e_{ij}c_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},

(16)

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}^{\prime}\rightarrow\bm{\nabla}.

(17)

Using these we show below that if the height function
h({\bf x},t)
transforms as
h^{\prime}({\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rightarrow h({\bf x},t)+\textbf{c}\cdot\textbf{x}
, then Eq. (
15
) is invariant. Considering RHS of Eq. (
15
), the first term transforms as,

\displaystyle\nu\nabla^{\prime 2}h^{\prime}\rightarrow\nu\nabla^{2}h.

(18)

Second term transforms as,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}(\bm{\nabla}^{\prime}h^{\prime})^{2}\rightarrow\frac{\lambda}{2}(\bm{\nabla}h)^{2}+\lambda\bm{\nabla}h\cdot\textbf{c}+\frac{\lambda}{2}c^{2}.

(19)

Third term transforms as,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\frac{\nabla^{\prime}_{i}\nabla^{\prime}_{j}}{\nabla^{\prime 2}}(\nabla^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}\nabla^{\prime}_{j}h^{\prime})\rightarrow\lambda_{1}\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}(\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{j}h)+2\lambda_{1}\textbf{c}\cdot\bm{\nabla}h.

(20)

Chiral term transforms as,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\frac{\nabla^{\prime}_{i}\nabla^{\prime}_{j}}{\nabla^{\prime 2}}e_{jm}(\nabla^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}\nabla^{\prime}_{m}h^{\prime})\rightarrow

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}}{\nabla^{2}}e_{jm}(\nabla_{i}h\nabla_{m}h)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}e_{ij}c_{i}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{j}}.

(21)

Noise term remains invariant under this transformation. Thus after the transformation extra terms in RHS are

\displaystyle\lambda\bm{\nabla}h\cdot\textbf{c}+\frac{\lambda}{2}c^{2}+2\lambda_{1}\textbf{c}\cdot\bm{\nabla}h-\lambda_{2}e_{ij}c_{i}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{j}}.

(22)

Similarly LHS of Eq. (
15
) transforms as,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h^{\prime}}{\partial t^{\prime}}\rightarrow\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}+(\lambda+2\lambda_{1})\textbf{c}\cdot\bm{\nabla}h-\lambda_{2}e_{ij}c_{i}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{j}}+(\lambda+2\lambda_{1})c^{2}.

(23)

Comparing Eq. (
22
) and Eq. (
23
) we see that Eq. (
15
) is invariant.

C.5
RG flow equations and scaling

After averaging over fields with higher momentum new action contains fields with upper cut off momentum
\frac{\Lambda}{b}
. Since we want to describe the same system with new action, so we have to rescale the space, time and fields so the upper cutoff momentum becomes
\Lambda
.
Rescaling of space and time give
{\bf q}\to{\bf q}^{\prime}=b{\bf q}\implies x^{\prime}=\frac{x}{b}
and
\omega\to\omega^{\prime}=b^{z}\omega\implies t^{\prime}=\frac{t}{b^{z}}
. Furthermore, rescaling of the fields
h
and
\hat{h}
give

\displaystyle h^{<}({\bf q},\omega)=\xi h({\bf q}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hat{h}^{<}({\bf q},\omega)=\hat{\xi}\hat{h}({\bf q}^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle h^{<}(x,t)=\xi_{R}h(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

Now let
\xi_{R}=b^{\chi}
, where
\chi
is the roughness exponent of the surface. Since there is no correction to
\int_{x,t}\hat{h}\partial_{t}\,h
we impose the condition that coefficient of
\int_{x,t}\hat{h}\partial_{t}h
remains unity under rescaling. Using these we find

\displaystyle D^{\prime}=D^{<}b^{z-d-2\chi}

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{<}b^{z-2}

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda b^{\chi+z-2}.

Here,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
scale same as
\lambda
. Since there are no relevant one-loop corrections to
\lambda,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda^{<}=\lambda
etc. Furthermore,

D^{<}=D\Bigr[1+\Bigl(\frac{\lambda^{2}D}{4\nu^{3}}+\frac{3\lambda_{1}^{2}D}{8\nu^{3}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}^{2}D}{8\nu^{3}}+\frac{\lambda\lambda_{1}D}{2\nu^{3}}\Bigl)\int_{\frac{\Lambda}{b}}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{2}q}{q^{2}}\Bigr]

and

\nu^{<}=\nu\Bigr[1+\Bigl(\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}D}{2\nu^{3}}+\frac{5}{8}\frac{\lambda\lambda_{1}D}{\nu^{3}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}^{2}D}{8\nu^{3}}\Bigl)\int_{\frac{\Lambda}{b}}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{2}q}{q^{2}}\Bigr]

. We set
b=e^{\delta l}\approx 1+\delta l
and define dimensionless coupling constants by
g=\frac{\lambda^{2}D}{\nu^{3}}\frac{1}{2\pi}
,
\gamma_{1}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda}
and
\gamma_{2}=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda}
to obtain the RG flow equations in the main text.

In dimensions greater than 2 (where
\gamma_{2}=0
), we set
d=2+\epsilon
with
\epsilon>0
. We aim to determine the fixed point and the corresponding scaling exponents accurately up to
O(\epsilon)
only. To achieve this, it suffices to substitute
d=2
in the one-loop integrals. For example, in a general dimension where
d>2
, the RG flow equation governing the parameter
g
is expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dl}=

\displaystyle g\Biggr[2-d+g\biggl\{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{3}{d(d+2)}\gamma_{1}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{1}}{d}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{2-d}{d}-\frac{3}{d}\gamma_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{3\gamma_{1}}{d(d+2)}\biggl(3-\frac{d+2}{2}+\frac{d(d+2)}{2}\biggl)\biggl\}\Biggr]

Upon setting
d=2+\epsilon
, and considering that
g\sim{\cal O}(\epsilon)
at the fixed point, we substitute
d=2
selectively in the contributions coming from the one-loop integral terms containing
d
inside the curly bracket. This yields the simplified form of the RG flow equation:

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dl}=-\epsilon g-{\cal\tilde{A}}(\gamma_{1})g^{2}.

Consequently, this produces
g
correctly to
{\cal O}(\epsilon)
; see, e.g., Ref.
Barabási and Stanley 1995
.

Appendix D
Correlation function

Following Refs.
Haldar et al. 2023a
;
Haldar et al. 2023b
;
Mukherjee and Basu 2022a
, we now calculate the renormalised correlation functions of
h({\bf x})
, defined as

C_{h}^{R}(r)\equiv\langle[h({\bf x})-h({\bf x^{\prime}})]^{2}\rangle_{R},

(50)

where
R
refers to a renormalised quantity.
We start from

\langle h({\bf k})h({\bf-k})\rangle_{R}\approx\frac{D_{0}}{\nu_{0}k^{2}[\ln(\Lambda/k)]^{1-\mu}},

(51)

Expression (
51
) is no longer valid over the wavevector range from 0 to
\Lambda
, rather it is valid between 0 and
\tilde{\Lambda}\ll\Lambda
.
We then obtain,

C_{h}(r)\approx\int_{0}^{\tilde{\Lambda}}\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\left[1-\exp i{\bf k}\cdot({\bf x-x}^{\prime})\right]\frac{D_{0}}{\nu_{0}k^{2}[\ln(\Lambda/k)]^{1-\mu}}.

(52)

Integrating over the angular variable, we get

\displaystyle C_{h}(r)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tilde{\Lambda}}\frac{dq\,D_{0}}{\nu_{0}q[\ln(\Lambda/q)]^{1-\mu}}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta(1-e^{iqr\cos\theta}\right]

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tilde{\Lambda}}\frac{dq\,D_{0}}{\nu_{0}q[\ln(\Lambda/q)]^{1-\mu}}\left[1-J_{0}(qr)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tilde{\Lambda}r}\frac{du\,D_{0}[1-J_{0}(u)]}{\nu_{0}u|\ln(\frac{r\Lambda}{u})|^{1-\mu}},

where
J_{0}(u)
is the Bessel function of order zero. Then

C_{h}(r)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du\,D_{0}[1-J_{0}(u)]}{\nu_{0}u[-\ln u+\ln(1/y)]^{1-\mu}}+\int_{1}^{\tilde{\Lambda}r}\frac{du\,D_{0}}{\nu_{0}u[-\ln u+\ln(\Lambda r)]^{1-\mu}}-\int_{1}^{\tilde{\Lambda}r}\frac{du\,D_{0}J_{0}(u)}{\nu_{0}u[-\ln u+\ln(\Lambda r)]^{1-\mu}}.

(54)

The first and the third terms on the rhs of (
54
) are finite. Since
u_{max}=\tilde{\Lambda}r\ll\Lambda r
, the second contribution on the right may be integrated with the substitution
u=\exp(z)
giving

\int_{1}^{\tilde{\Lambda}\,r}\frac{du}{u[\ln(\Lambda\,r)]^{1-\mu}}=\mu\frac{\ln(\Lambda r)+\ln(\tilde{\Lambda}/\Lambda)}{\ln(\Lambda r)}[\ln(\Lambda r)]^{\mu}\approx\mu[\ln(\Lambda\,r)]^{\mu}

(55)

in the limit of large
r
.
We thus find
C_{h}(r)\approx\mu\frac{D_{0}}{\nu_{0}}|\ln(\Lambda\,r)|^{\mu}
in the limit of large
r
, with the remaining contributions on the right hand side of (
54
) being finite or subleading for large
r
.

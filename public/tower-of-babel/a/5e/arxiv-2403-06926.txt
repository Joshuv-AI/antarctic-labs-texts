Sheaves of ( ∞ , ∞ ) (\infty,\infty) -categories
Zach Goldthorpe

Abstract
We provide a functorial presentation of the ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category of sheaves of ( n , r ) (n,r) -categories for all − 2 ≤ n ≤ ∞ -2\leq n\leq\infty and 0 ≤ r ≤ n + 2 0\leq r\leq n+2 based on complete Segal space objects. In this definition, the equivalences of sheaves of ( ∞ , ∞ ) (\infty,\infty) -categories are defined inductively, so we also provide a localisation at the coinductive equivalences to define the ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category of sheaves of ω \omega -categories as well. We prove that the ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category of sheaves of ( ∞ , ∞ ) (\infty,\infty) -categories, and the ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -category of sheaves of ω \omega -categories both define distributors over the underlying topos of sheaves of spaces. Moreover, we show that these distributors define terminal and initial fixed points with respect to the construction of complete Segal space objects in a distributor. We conclude with a sheafification result: the category of sheaves of ( n , r ) (n,r) -categories over a site C {C} can be presented as a strongly reflective localisation of 𝐅𝐮𝐧 ⁡ ( C op , 𝐂𝐚𝐭 ( n , r ) ) \mathbf{Fun}({C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Cat}_{(n,r)}) , where the localisation functor preserves fibre products over 𝐒𝐡 ⁡ ( C ) \mathbf{Sh}({C}) , with the analogous result also holding for sheaves of ω \omega -categories.

Introduction

A higher category is a generalisation of an ordinary category consisting of objects,
1
-morphisms between objects,
2
-morphisms between
1
-morphisms,
3
-morphisms between
2
-morphisms,
ad infinitum
.
For
-2\leq n\leq\infty
and
0\leq r\leq n+2
, an
(n,r)
-category
is a higher category where

•

parallel
k
-morphisms are equivalent for
k>n
, and

•

k
-morphisms are invertible for
k>r
.

For
r<\infty
, the notion of
(n,r)
-category is unambiguous up to an action of
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{r}
by
[
2
]
.
However, there are several inequivalent notions of
(\infty,\infty)
-category, due to nuances in the concept of invertibility in a purely infinite-dimensional setting.

We have a tower of inclusions

\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,0)}\subseteq\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,1)}\subseteq\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}\subseteq\dots

where each inclusion
\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,r)}\subseteq\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,r+1)}
admits a left adjoint
\pi_{\leq r}
and a right adjoint
\kappa_{\leq r}
.
For an
(\infty,r+1)
-category
{C}
, we obtain the
(\infty,r)
-category
\pi_{\leq r}{C}
by formally inverting the
(r+1)
-morphisms of
{C}
.
On the other hand,
\kappa_{\leq r}{C}
is just the maximal sub-
(\infty,r)
-category of
{C}
, obtained by discarding the non-invertible
(r+1)
-morphisms.
From these adjoints, we obtain two natural constructions of the
(\infty,1)
-category of
(\infty,\infty)
-categories:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,\infty)}

\displaystyle:=\varprojlim\left(\dots\to\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{\leq 1}}\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,1)}\xrightarrow{\kappa_{\leq 0}}\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,0)}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Cat}_{\omega}

\displaystyle:=\varprojlim\left(\dots\to\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,2)}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\leq 1}}\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,1)}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\leq 0}}\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,0)}\right)

Roughly speaking,
\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,\infty)}
consists of higher categories wherein equivalences are defined inductively from the identity
k
-morphisms, whereas
\mathbf{Cat}_{\omega}
consists of higher categories wherein equivalences are defined coinductively; see
[
4
, Remark 3.0.5]
.

There are several approaches to defining higher categories, but they are largely guided by one of the two principles:

•

An
(n+1,r+1)
-category is a category that is suitably
enriched
in
(n,r)
-categories; that is, an
(n+1,r+1)
-category is a suitable category
{C}
such that
\operatorname{Hom}_{C}(x,y)
is an
(n,r)
-category for every
x,y\in{C}
.

•

An
(n+1,r+1)
-category is a suitable category object
internal
to
(n,r)
-categories; that is, an
(n+1,r+1)
-category consists of an
(n,r)
-category
{C}_{0}
of objects, and an
(n,r)
-category
{C}_{1}
of morphisms, such that
({C}_{0},{C}_{1})
satisfies a suitable analogue of the axioms of category theory.

Although enrichment provides a more concise inductive description of higher categories—
(n+1,r+1)
-categories should be precisely the categories enriched in
(n,r)
-categories—a suitably general theory of enrichment proved too difficult to develop without already establishing a sufficiently general theory of higher categories.

On the other hand, a suitable theory of internalisation was more readily available using the tools from abstract homotopy theory.
The caveat is that a general category object
{C}_{\bullet}
in
\mathbf{Cat}_{(n,r)}
has more structure than that of an
(n+1,r+1)
-category.
Between objects in
{C}_{0}
, there are two distinct notions of a morphism between them: there are the objects of
{C}_{1}
, and there are also the morphisms of
{C}_{0}
.

One way to avoid this caveat is to assert that
{C}_{0}
is a set; that is,
{C}_{0}
is a
(0,0)
-category.
We can then think of
{C}_{0}
as the underlying set of objects of the
(n+1,r+1)
-category.
This leads to the notion of higher
Segal categories
, which is developed in detail, for instance, in
[
11
]
.
However, the “underlying set” of objects in an ordinary category is not invariant under equivalence, and this complicates the notion of equivalence between higher Segal categories.
A functor
{C}_{\bullet}\to{D}_{\bullet}
of
(n+1,r+1)
-categories should be an equivalence precisely if it is fully faithful on the
(n,r)
-categories of morphisms, and essentially surjective on the objects.
Since essential surjectivity cannot be tested on the underlying functor
{C}_{0}\to{D}_{0}
, it must be defined by extracting the higher equivalences from the categorical structure of
{C}_{\bullet}
and
{D}_{\bullet}
.

Alternatively, we can instead assert that
{C}_{0}
is an
\infty
-groupoid; that is, all
k
-morphisms in
{C}_{0}
are invertible.
Then,
{C}_{0}
serves as the underlying
n
-groupoid of
{C}_{\bullet}
, which is an invariant of
{C}_{\bullet}
under equivalence.
By the Homotopy Hypothesis, we can therefore view
{C}_{0}
as a space.
This leads to the notion of higher
Segal spaces
.
However, this means that the objects of
{C}_{\bullet}
have two distinct notions of equivalence:

•

Path-connectedness in the space
{C}_{0}
, and

•

Isomorphism from the categorical structure of
{C}_{\bullet}
.

Therefore, we restrict to
complete Segal spaces
, which are those Segal spaces for which these two notions of equivalence coincide.
Although the model for
(n+1,r+1)
-categories becomes more technical, requiring the completeness condition, the resulting equivalences are easier to define: a functor
{C}_{\bullet}\to{D}_{\bullet}
of complete Segal spaces modelling
(n+1,r+1)
-categories is an equivalence precisely if
{C}_{0}\to{D}_{0}
is a homotopy equivalence (giving essential surjectivity), and
{C}_{1}\to{D}_{1}
is an equivalence of
(n,r)
-categories (giving fully faithfulness).

Complete Segal spaces were first defined by Rezk in
[
8
]
.
Barwick then defined
r
-fold iterated complete Segal spaces as a model for
(\infty,r)
-categories in
[
1
]
(see
[
2
, §14]
).
Lurie expanded this construction and axiomatised its iterative nature in
[
7
, §1]
, providing a means to construct complete Segal space objects in any
distributor
.

Intuitively, a distributor
{Y}
is a locally presentable
(\infty,1)
-category with a choice of full subcategory
{X}\subseteq{Y}
consisting of the “spaces.”
Then, the
(\infty,1)
-category
\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
of complete Segal space object in
{Y}
consists of categories
{C}_{\bullet}
internal to
{Y}
such that
{C}_{0}\in{X}
(i.e.,
{C}_{0}
is “space-like”), and
{C}_{\bullet}
is complete.
The axioms of a distributor imply that the subcategory
{X}
of “spaces” is always an
(\infty,1)
-topos; that is, an
(\infty,1)
-category of sheaves of spaces.
In particular,
r
-fold complete Segal spaces over
{X}
define a model for sheaves of
(\infty,r)
-categories.

In this paper, we use the language of complete Segal spaces to define a presentation of the
(\infty,1)
-category of sheaves of
(n,r)
-categories over an
(\infty,1)
-topos
{X}
as a localisation of
r
-fold simplicial objects in
{X}
.
We then show that the
(\infty,1)
-categories of sheaves of
(\infty,\infty)
-categories forms a distributor over
{X}
.
In fact, the category of sheaves of
(\infty,\infty)
-categories over
{X}
is completely characterised by the construction of complete Segal spaces over
{X}
.
More precisely, we construct an
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
of distributors over
{X}
and show that sheaves of
(\infty,\infty)
-categories satisfies a universal property with respect to the construction of complete Segal space objects:

Theorem A

(
Theorem

3.4.3
)
.

The
(\infty,1)
-category of sheaves of
(\infty,\infty)
-categories is the terminal object in the
(\infty,1)
-category
\mathbf{Fix}_{\textsc{css}}^{R}
of distributors
{Y}
over
{X}
with an equivalence
{Y}\xrightarrow{\sim}\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
.

\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
spanned by the complete Segal spaces.

Remark 3.1.11
.

By
[
7
, Theorem 1.2.13]
,
Y_{\bullet}\in\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
is a complete Segal space if and only if
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y
is essentially constant; that is,
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y
lies in the essential image of the diagonal
{X}\subseteq\mathbf{Grpd}({X})

Proposition 3.1.12
.

[
7
, Proposition 1.3.2]

If
{Y}
is an
{X}
-distributor, then the fully faithful diagonal functor
{X}\subseteq\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
exhibits
\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
as an
{X}
-distributor as well.

Remark 3.1.13
.

The truncation functor
\pi_{X}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})\to{X}
, being left adjoint to the diagonal, is given by geometric realisation

On the other hand, the core functor

3.2
The
\infty
-categories of distributors

Definition 3.2.1
.

Fix a topos
{X}
.
Then, define
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{L}
to be the subcategory of
(\widehat{\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}})_{{X}/}
where the objects are
{X}
-distributors
{X}\subseteq{Y}
, and a functor
\psi:{Z}\to{Y}
under
{X}

if and only if

(L1)

\psi
preserves colimits, and

(L2)

\psi
preserves truncation, in that
\pi_{X}\psi\simeq\pi_{X}
.

On the other hand, define
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
to be the subcategory of
(\widehat{\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}})_{{X}/}

{X}
-distributors
{X}\subseteq{Y}

under

(R1)

preserves limits and
\lambda

(R2)

\phi
preserves cores, in that
\kappa_{X}\phi\simeq\kappa_{X}
.

Remark 3.2.2
.

Since
{X}
-distributors are necessarily locally presentable,
(L1)
is equivalent by
[
6
, Corollary 5.5.2.9]
to asserting that
\psi
admits a right adjoint
\phi
.
As truncation is left adjoint to the inclusion of
{X}

(L2)

\phi|_{X}\simeq\operatorname{id}_{X}

Dually,

is likewise equivalent to asserting that
\phi
admits a left adjoint
\psi
.
As core is right adjoint to the inclusion of
{X}

(R2)

\psi
lies under
{X}
; that is
\psi|_{X}\simeq\operatorname{id}_{X}

This demonstrates that the two categories of

Proposition 3.2.3
.

For any topos
{X}
, the functor
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}\to(\widehat{\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}})_{/{X}}
sending an
{X}
-distributor
{Y}
to its core

Proof.

Let
{Y}_{\bullet}:K\to\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
be a small diagram indexed by a simplicial set

K^{\triangleright}\to\widehat{\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}}

K^{\triangleright}
to

3.3
Functoriality of complete Segal spaces

Definition 3.3.1
.

Let
{Y}
be a category with full subcategory
{X}\subseteq{Y}
.
Say that a functor
\phi:{Y}\to{Z}

preserves fibre products over
{X}
if for every fibre product
y^{1}\times_{x}y^{2}
in
{Y}
with
x\in{X}
, the induced map
\phi(y^{1}\times_{x}y^{2})\to\phi(y^{1})\times_{\phi(x)}\phi(y^{2})
is an equivalence in
{Z}
.

If
{X}
has finite limits, and
{X}\subseteq{Y}
preserves these limits, then say that a functor
\phi:{Y}\to{Z}
is
relatively left exact
(relative to
{X}
) if
\phi|:{X}\to{Z}
is left exact, and
\phi
preserves fibre products over
{X}
.

The functorial nature of the construction of complete Segal spaces can be summarised as follows:

Lemma 3.3.2
.

Let
g_{*}:{X}\to{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism of topoi.
Fix an
{X}
-distributor
{Y}
and an
{X}^{\prime}
-distributor
{Y}^{\prime}
, and let
\phi:{Y}\to{Y}^{\prime}
be a functor such that

•

\phi
extends
g_{*}
, in that
\phi|_{X}\simeq g_{*}
,

•

\phi
commutes with cores, in that
\phi\kappa_{X}\simeq\kappa_{{X}^{\prime}}\phi
, and

•

\phi
preserves fibre products over
{X}
.

Then,
\phi_{*}:\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})\to\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y}^{\prime})
restricts to a functor
\phi_{*}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})\to\textsc{css}_{{X}^{\prime}}({Y}^{\prime})
.

Remark 3.3.3
.

In the situation of
Lemma

3.3.2
,
\phi_{*}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})\to\textsc{css}_{{X}^{\prime}}({Y}^{\prime})
acts pointwise on the underlying functors.
Since limits of complete Segal spaces are computed pointwise, this ensures that
\phi_{*}
preserves fibre products over
{X}
.
Likewise,
Remark

3.1.13
ensures that
\phi_{*}
commutes with cores as well.
Therefore,
Lemma

3.3.2
combined with
Proposition

3.1.12
implies an
endofunctorial
nature of the construction of complete Segal spaces.

Proof of
Lemma

3.3.2
.

Since
\phi
is relatively left exact, it follows that
\phi_{*}
restricts to a functor
\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})\to\mathbf{Cat}_{{X}^{\prime}}({Y}^{\prime})
.
Similarly, the left exactness of the left adjoint
g^{*}\dashv g_{*}
induces a commutative square of left adjoints

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Grpd}({X}^{\prime})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Cat}({X}^{\prime})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Grpd}({X})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Cat}({X})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{*}}

Taking right adjoints implies for any
X_{\bullet}\in\mathbf{Cat}({X})
that
(g_{*}X_{\bullet})^{\sim}\simeq g_{*}(X_{\bullet}^{\sim})
.
It therefore follows for any
Y_{\bullet}\in\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(\phi_{*}Y)

\displaystyle\simeq((\kappa_{{X}^{\prime}}\circ\phi)_{*}Y)^{\sim}

(
Remark

3.1.9
)

\displaystyle\simeq((\phi\circ\kappa_{X})_{*}Y)^{\sim}

(
\phi
commutes with cores)

\displaystyle\simeq((g_{*}\circ\kappa_{X})_{*}Y)^{\sim}

(
\phi
extends
g_{*}
)

\displaystyle\simeq g_{*}(\kappa_{{X},*}Y)^{\sim}\simeq g_{*}(\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y)

(
Remark

3.1.9
)

In particular, if
Y_{\bullet}
is a complete Segal space, then
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(\phi_{*}Y)\simeq g_{*}\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y
is essentially constant, ensuring that
\phi_{*}Y_{\bullet}
is a complete Segal space by
Remark

3.1.11
.
∎

We make the functoriality of the construction of complete Segal spaces precise by realising the construction as a functor on the categories of distributors defined in
Section

3.2
.
For this, we need a preliminary lemma:

Lemma 3.3.4
.

Fix a topos
{X}
, and let
{Y}
be an
{X}
-distributor.
Choose
\lambda\gg 0
such that
{Y}
is locally
\lambda
-presentable and the core functor
\kappa_{X}:{Y}\to{X}
is
\lambda
-accessible.
Then,

(i)

\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
is stable under
\lambda
-filtered colimits in
\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})
.

(ii)

\lambda
-filtered colimits in
\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
commute with
\lambda
-small limits.

(iii)

The core functor
\kappa_{X}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})\to{X}
preserves
\lambda
-filtered colimits.

Proof.

This follows the proof of
[
7
, Proposition 1.2.29]

mutatis mutandis
, noting that
\lambda
-filtered colimits commute with
\lambda
-small limits in any locally
\lambda
-presentable category.
∎

Proposition 3.3.5
.

For a topos
{X}
, the construction of complete Segal spaces on
{X}
-distributors defines a functor
\textsc{css}_{X}:\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}\to\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
, where the functor
\phi_{*}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})\to\textsc{css}_{X}({Z})
induced by any
\phi:{Y}\to{Z}
in
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
acts pointwise.

Proof.

Let
\phi:{Y}\to{Z}
be a morphism of
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
.
Then,
Lemma

3.3.2
implies that pointwise application of
\phi
defines a functor
\phi_{*}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})\to\textsc{css}_{X}({Z})
.
Moreover, this functor preserves cores and small limits.

Choose
\lambda\gg 0
so that
\phi:{Y}\to{Z}
is a
\lambda
-accessible functor between locally
\lambda
-presentable categories.
Then,
Lemma

3.3.4
implies that
\lambda
-filtered colimits in
\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
and
\textsc{css}_{X}({Z})
are computed pointwise, and are thus preserved by
\phi_{*}
.
Therefore,
\phi_{*}
is indeed a morphism of
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
.
∎

Remark 3.3.6
.

By
Remark

3.2.2
, the construction of complete Segal spaces on
{X}
-distributors also defines an endofunctor on
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{L}
.
However, the functor
\psi_{!}:\textsc{css}_{X}({Z})\to\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
induced by

does not act pointwise; rather, if

.

Theorem 3.3.7
.

For a topos
{X}
, the functor
\textsc{css}_{X}:\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}\to\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
preserves weakly contractible limits.

Proof.

Let
{Y}_{\bullet}:K\to\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
be a diagram indexed by a weakly contractible simplicial set
K
, and let
{Y}:=\varprojlim_{k}{Y}_{k}
, with canonical projection maps
\varpi_{k}:{Y}\to{Y}_{k}
.
Consider the commutative square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{k}\textsc{css}_{X}({Y}_{k})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{k}\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y}_{k})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

where the limits are computed in
\widehat{\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}}
.
By
Remark

3.2.5
, the limit
{Y}=\varprojlim_{k}{Y}_{k}
can be computed on underlying categories in
\widehat{\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}}
, implying that the bottom arrow in the above diagram is an equivalence.

Remark

3.2.5
also implies that it suffices to show that the top arrow in the above diagram is an equivalence as well.
As all other functors are fully faithful, the top arrow is fully faithful.
Therefore, it remains to show that the top arrow is essentially surjective.

From the commutative diagram, we can identify
\varprojlim_{k}\textsc{css}_{X}({Y}_{k})
with the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})
spanned by those

for which
\varpi_{k}Y:\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{Y}_{k}
lies in
\textsc{css}_{X}({Y}_{k})
for every

.
It suffices to show that any such functor

is already a complete Segal space in
{Y}
.

Certainly
Y_{0}\in{X}
, since
\varpi_{k}Y_{0}\in{X}
for all
k\in K
.
Since
\varpi_{k}
is a morphism of
\mathbf{Dist}_{X}^{R}
, it preserves limits.
In particular, for
n\geq 0
, the canonical map

in
{Y}
descends via
\varpi_{k}
to an equivalence in
{Y}_{k}
for every

, and is therefore also an equivalence.
In particular,
Y_{\bullet}\in\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
.

As
K
is weakly contractible, it is nonempty, so fix some
k_{0}\in K
.
Since
\varpi_{k_{0}}
preserves cores and limits, the proof of

3.3.2
implies that
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(\varpi_{k_{0},*}Y_{\bullet})\simeq\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y
.
Since
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(\varpi_{k_{0},*}Y_{\bullet})
is essentially constant, this proves that

∎

An analogue to

for colimits is likely untrue in general.

3.4
Distributors of sheaves of higher categories

As in
Section

2
, fix a topos
{X}
, presented as a left exact accessible localisation of
{P}({C})
for some small category
{C}
, where the localisation functor is denoted
(-)^{\#}:{P}({C})\to{X}
.
In this section, we aim to use the language of distributors and complete Segal spaces to further study the categories of sheaves of
(n,r)
-categories over
{C}
, particularly when

.

Lemma 3.4.1
.

Let
{Y}
be an
{X}
-distributor.
Then,
Y_{\bullet}\in\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
is a complete Segal space if and only if it is local with respect to
|\mathbb{I}|_{U}^{\#}\to\Delta_{U}[0]
for every

.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y\in\mathbf{Grpd}({X})
satisfies the Segal condition, it is essentially constant if and only if the map
\operatorname{Gp}_{1}Y\to\operatorname{Gp}_{0}Y
is an equivalence, which is equivalent to asserting that the underlying functor
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y:\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}}\to{X}
is local with respect to
\Delta_{U}[1]\to\Delta_{U}[0]
for every
U\in{C}
.
Now, note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{X})}(\Delta_{U}[1],\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{{P}(\mathbf{\Delta})}(\Delta[1],\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y(U))

(
Definition

2.1.4
)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Cat}({S})}(\Delta[1],\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y(U))

(both
\Delta[1]
and
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y(U)
satisfy the Segal condition)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grpd}({S})}(\mathbb{I},\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y(U))

(
\mathbb{I}
is the groupoidification of
\Delta[1]
)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{{P}(\mathbf{\Delta})}(\mathbb{I},\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y(U))

(
\mathbf{Grpd}({S})\subseteq{P}(\mathbf{\Delta})
is fully faithful)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{X})}(|\mathbb{I}|_{U}^{\#},\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y)

(
Definition

2.2.1
)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grpd}({X})}(|\mathbb{I}|_{U}^{\#},\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y)

(both
|\mathbb{I}|_{U}^{\#}
and
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y
are groupoid objects in
{X}
)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})}(|\mathbb{I}|_{U}^{\#},Y_{\bullet})

(

3.1.8
)

and similarly for
\Delta_{U}[0]
.
Therefore,
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y
is essentially constant if and only if
Y_{\bullet}
is local with respect to
|\mathbb{I}|_{U}^{\#}\to\Delta_{U}[0]
for every
U\in{C}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 3.4.2
.

The category
\mathbf{Sh}_{(\infty,r)}({C})
is an
{X}
-distributor for every
0\leq r<\infty
.
Moreover, we have a canonical equivalence
\mathbf{Sh}_{(\infty,r+1)}({C})\simeq\textsc{css}_{X}(\mathbf{Sh}_{(\infty,r)}({C}))

Proof.

Lemma 3.5.1
.

Suppose
K
is sifted in the sense of
[
6
, Definition 5.5.8.1]
.
Then, for all topoi
{X}
, the functor
\varinjlim:\mathbf{Fun}(K,{X})\to{X}
preserves fibre products over
{X}
, where
{X}
is viewed as a full subcategory of
\mathbf{Fun}(K,{X})
spanned by the essentially constant functors.

Proof.

Suppose
F\to x\leftarrow G
is a cospan of functors
K\to{X}
, where
x\in{X}
is viewed as a constant functor.
Since
K
is sifted, the diagonal
K\to K\times K
is cofinal.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\varinjlim_{k\in K}F(k)\right)\times_{x}\left(\varinjlim_{\ell\in K}G(\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\varinjlim_{(k,\ell)\in K\times K}\Big(F(k)\times_{x}G(\ell)\Big)

(colimits are universal in
{X}
)

\displaystyle\simeq\varinjlim_{j\in K}\Big(F(j)\times_{x}G(j)\Big)

(
K\to K\times K
is cofinal)

showing that
\varinjlim:\mathbf{Fun}(K,{X})\to{X}
preserves the fibre product
F\times_{x}G
.
∎

Lemma 3.5.2
.

Fix an
{X}
-distributor
{Y}
.
Suppose a morphism
Y_{\bullet}\to Z_{\bullet}
in
\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
is a Segal equivalence.
Then, for any cospan
Z_{\bullet}\to x\leftarrow Z_{\bullet}^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
with
x\in{X}
, the induced map
Y\times_{x}Z^{\prime}\to Z\times_{x}Z^{\prime}
is a Segal equivalence.

Proof.

The functor
Y\times_{x}Z^{\prime}\to Z\times_{x}Z^{\prime}
is certainly fully faithful in the sense of
(E1)
, since limits commute with limits.
We need to show that the map is also essentially surjective in the sense of
(E2)
; that is,
|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(Y\times_{x}Z^{\prime})|\to|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(Z\times_{x}Z^{\prime})|
is an equivalence in
{X}
.

Since
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}
is a right adjoint that restricts to the identity on
{X}
, we have
\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}(Y\times_{x}Z^{\prime})\simeq(\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y)\times_{x}(\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Z^{\prime})
and likewise for
Z\times_{x}Z^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, since
\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}}
is sifted by
[
6
, Lemma 5.5.8.4]
, geometric realisation also preserves fibre products over
{X}
by
Lemma

3.5.1
.

Therefore,
Y\times_{x}Z^{\prime}\to Z\times_{x}Z^{\prime}
satisfies
(E2)
if and only if the map
|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y|\times_{x}|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Z^{\prime}|\to|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Z|\times_{x}|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Z^{\prime}|
is an equivalence, which follows from the fact that the is the base change of the map
|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Y|\to|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Z|
along
|\operatorname{Gp}_{\bullet}Z^{\prime}|\to x
, which is an equivalence since
Y\to Z
is a Segal equivalence.
∎

Proposition 3.5.3
.

For an
{X}
-distributor
{Y}
, the left adjoint
\operatorname{Seg}_{X}:\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})\to\textsc{css}_{X}({Y})
to inclusion preserves fibre products over
{X}
.

Proof.

Suppose
Y\to x\leftarrow Y^{\prime}
is a cospan in
\mathbf{SS}_{X}({Y})
, where
x\in{X}
.
Since
(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y)\times_{x}(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y^{\prime})
is a limit of complete Segal spaces, it is also a complete Segal space.
Therefore, it suffices to show that the map
Y\times_{x}Y^{\prime}\to(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y)\times_{x}(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y^{\prime})
is a Segal equivalence, as it would then induce an equivalence
\operatorname{Seg}_{X}(Y\times_{x}Y^{\prime})\to(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y)\times_{x}(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y^{\prime})
.

Since
Y\times_{x}Y^{\prime}\to(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y)\times_{x}(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y^{\prime})
factors as

\displaystyle Y\times_{x}Y^{\prime}\to(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y)\times_{x}Y^{\prime}\to(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y)\times_{x}(\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y^{\prime})

the fact that it is a Segal equivalence follows from
Lemma

3.5.2
, since
Y\to\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y
and
Y^{\prime}\to\operatorname{Seg}_{X}Y^{\prime}
are Segal equivalences.
∎

Lemma 3.5.6
.

Let

be a filtered simplicial set, and
({A}^{k}\to{B}^{k})_{k\in K}

Let
{A}\to{B}
denote the colimit of the diagram of cofinal functors.
By Quillen’s Theorem A
[

is weakly contractible.

is in the essential image of the coprojection

.
Since

is cofinal.
Therefore,

\displaystyle{A}\times_{B}{B}_{B/}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\varinjlim_{\ell}{A}^{\ell}\right)\times_{\left(\varinjlim_{\ell}{B}^{\ell}\right)}\left(\varinjlim_{\ell}{B}^{\ell}\right)_{B/}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}{A}^{\ell}\right)\times_{\left(\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}{B}^{\ell}\right)}\left(\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}{B}^{\ell}\right)_{B/}

(cofinality of

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}{A}^{\ell}\right)\times_{\left(\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}{B}^{\ell}\right)}\left(\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}{B}^{\ell}_{B/}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\varinjlim_{k\to\ell}\left({A}^{\ell}\times_{{B}^{\ell}}{B}^{\ell}_{B/}\right)

By assumption, every

Corollary 3.5.7
.

The full subcategory of
\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}
spanned by sifted categories is closed under filtered colimits.

Proposition 3.5.8

For

Lemma 3.5.11

be an

\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{X})
and
\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})
are certainly locally presentable, verifying
(D1)
.
The inclusion

preserves small limits and colimits, and limits and colimits in functor categories are computed pointwise, which proves
(D2)
.
By
[

, Corollary 1.2.5]

\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})

\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{X})
if and only if the following holds:

(
*

are pullback squares.

Therefore,
\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y})
is then a distributor over
\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{X})
, as desired.

Since
\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y}))\simeq\mathbf{Fun}({K}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},{Y}))
and fibre products are computed pointwise in functor categories, we have

\mathbf{Sh}_{(n,r)}({C})
is the localisation of
\mathbf{Sh}_{(\infty,r)}({C})\simeq\mathbf{Fun}({C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,r)})
at the maps
\Sigma^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{k-m}\times\Delta_{U}[0])\to\Sigma^{m}\Delta_{U}[0]
for

,
k>n

U\in{C}

A functor
F:{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,r)}
is local with respect to
\Sigma^{m}(\mathbb{S}^{k-m}\times\Delta_{U}[0])\to\Sigma^{m}\Delta_{U}[0]
if and only if
F(U)\in\mathbf{Cat}_{(\infty,r)}
is local with respect to
\Sigma^{m}\mathbb{S}^{k-m}\to\Sigma^{m}*
.
By
[
3
, Proposition 6.1.7]
, it follows that
F
lies in
\mathbf{Sh}_{(n,r)}({C})
if and only if
F(U)\in\mathbf{Cat}_{(n,r)}
for every
U\in{C}
, as desired.
∎

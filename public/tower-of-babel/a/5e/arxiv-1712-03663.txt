Abstract
. In this paper we are concerned with the following question: if the tensor product of finitely generated modules M M and N N over a local complete intersection domain is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, then must M M or N N be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay? Celebrated results of Auslander, Lichtenbaum, and Huneke and Wiegand, yield affirmative answers to the question when the ring considered has codimension zero or one, but the question is very much open for complete intersection domains that have codimension at least two, even open for those that are one-dimensional, or isolated singularities. Our argument exploits Tor-rigidity and proves the following, which seems to give a new perspective to the aforementioned question: if R R is a complete intersection ring which is an isolated singularity such that dim ( R ) > codim ⁡ ( R ) \dim(R)>\cod(R) , and the tensor product M ⊗ R N M\otimes_{R}N is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, then M M is maximal Cohen-Macaulay if and only if N N is maximal Cohen-Macaulay.

Question 1.1

Let

Conjecture 1.2

Let
R

Conjecture 1.3

Let

Conjecture

. We should also mention that Conjectures

The main purpose of this paper is to make progress pertaining to Conjecture

. Our argument yields:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection with
\dim(R)>\cod(R)
, and let
M,N\in\md R
. Assume
R
is an isolated singularity. If
M\otimes_{R}N
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, then
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
N
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. In other words,
M\otimes_{R}N
cannot be maximal Cohen-Macaulay in case
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay but
N
is not maximal Cohen-Macaulay.

It is easy to see that the condition

is necessary for Theorem

,

and
N=R/(x^{2})R
, then
M\otimes_{R}N\cong M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay, but
N
is not.

In section 2, we state our main result, give a proof for Theorem

Theorem 2.1

Let

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq\depth_{R}(N)

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0

\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0

Theorem

A proof of Corollary

.

It suffices to assume (i) and prove (ii) and (iii) hold; see
[

Assume (i). Then we have

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})=\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}N_{\mathfrak{p}})

Now, by swaping the role of

Example 2.9
.

Let

be the formal power series ring,

. Then

is a complete intersection with
\cod(R)=\dim(R)=2

\widehat{\Ext}^{0}_{R}(M,N)=\widehat{\Ext}^{1}_{R}(M,N)=0

We have, since

Consequently

(
3.6
.1)

0\to N_{j}\overset{y_{j+1}}{\longrightarrow}N_{j}\longrightarrow N_{j+1}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
N
is Tor-rigid over
S
, it follows from (
3.6
.1) and Nakayama’s lemma that
N_{j}
is Tor-rigid over
S
for each
j=1,\ldots,t-c-1
.
Moreover, for each
j=0,1,\ldots,t-c-1
, (
3.6
.1) induces a long exact sequence of the form:

(
3.6
.2)

\cdots\rightarrow\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N_{j})\rightarrow\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N_{j+1})\rightarrow\Ext^{i+1}_{R}(M,N_{j})\rightarrow\cdots.

Setting
j=0
and using our assumption, we see from (
3.6
.2) that
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N_{1})=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,t-1
. By repeating this argument, we conclude:

\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N_{t-c-1})=0\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,c+1.

Notice
\depth_{R}(N_{t-c-1})=c+1
. Therefore, by using
3.5
for the pair
(M,N_{t-c-1})
and for the cases where
n=1
and
v=0
, we conclude
\G-dim_{R}(M)=\sup\{i\mid\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N_{t-c-1})\neq 0\}\leq 0
. Thus, since
M
is not zero, we deduce that
M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay and
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N_{t-c-1})=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Now, proceding inductively and using Nakayama’s lemma, we see from (
3.6
.2) that
\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N)
vanishes for all
i\geq 1
.
∎

We are now ready to give a proof of our main result. Note that, since modules over regular local rings are Tor-rigid
[
2
]
, the conditions in our setup hold when
S
is regular, i.e., when
R
is a complete intersection ring. In particular the result below contains Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Set
t=\depth_{R}(N)
and assume the following conditions hold:

(i)

\depth_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)\geq t
.

(ii)

\length(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))<\infty
for all
i\gg 0
.

(iii)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.

(iv)

If
t\leq c
, assume further that
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,c-t+1
.

Then
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

We start by recalling that
\CI_{R}(N)<\infty
. It follows from
3.3
that
\length(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))<\infty
and
\length(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N))<\infty
, for all
i\geq 1
. Hence, by
3.1
(ii), we have:

(
3.7
.1)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N)=0\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,t.

Suppose
t\geq c+1
. Then (
3.7
.1) and
3.6
imply
\Tr M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay and
\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Thus
M
is totally reflexive, and the vanishing of
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)
for all
i\geq 1
follows from
3.3
(ii).

Next assume
t\leq c
. Then, by assumption, we have
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, where
n=c-t+1
. We will proceed by induction on
n
.

First suppose
n=1
. We have, by
[
3
, 2.21]
, a short exact sequence of modules in
\md R
of the form

(
3.7
.2)

0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0,

where
F
is a free
R
-module and
\Ext^{1}_{R}(L,R)=0
. Since
\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,N)=0
, tensoring (
3.7
.2) with
N
, we obtain the exact sequence:

(
3.7
.3)

0\longrightarrow N\otimes_{R}F\longrightarrow N\otimes_{R}L\longrightarrow N\otimes_{R}M\longrightarrow 0.

It follows from (
3.7
.2) that
\Tor_{1}^{R}(L,N)=\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,N)=0
and
\Tor_{i}^{R}(L,N)=\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)
for all
i\geq 2
. In particular, by (
2.7
), we see:

(
3.7
.4)

\Ext^{1}_{R}(\Tr\Omega L,N)=0.

It follows from (
3.7
.2) that
L_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally reflexive for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp_{R}(L\otimes_{R}N)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. Therefore, since
\length(\Tor_{i}^{R}(L,N))<\infty
for all
i\geq 1
, we observe from
3.3
(ii) that
\length(\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr L,N))<\infty
for all
i\geq 1
. It is easy to see
\Tr L
is stably isomorphic to
\Omega\Tr\Omega L
because
\Ext^{1}_{R}(L,R)=0
. Furthermore, by (
3.7
.3), we have
\depth_{R}(L\otimes_{R}N)\geq\depth_{R}(N)
. Thus
3.1
gives:

(
3.7
.5)

\Ext^{i+1}_{R}(\Tr\Omega L,N)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Omega\Tr\Omega L,N)\cong\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr L,N)=0\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots t.

Consequently, by (
3.7
.4) and (
3.7
.5), we obtain:

(
3.7
.6)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(\Tr\Omega L,N)=0\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,t+1.

We are now in a position to conclude that
\Tor_{i}^{R}(N,M)=0
for all
i\geq 1
by using
3.5
and
3.3
(ii).

Now we consider the case where
n\geq 2
. Set
T=S/(x_{1},\cdots,x_{c-1})
so that
R\cong T/(x_{c})
.
Consider an exact sequence of modules in
\md T
of the form

(
3.7
.7)

0\rightarrow X\rightarrow P\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,

where
P
is a free
T
-module. Tensoring (
3.7
.7) with
N
over
T
, we have the following exact sequence

(
3.7
.8)

0\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{T}(M,N)\rightarrow X\otimes_{T}N\rightarrow P\otimes_{T}N\rightarrow M\otimes_{T}N\rightarrow 0.

The change of rings spectral sequence
[
27
, 11.64]
yields a long exact sequence of the form

\begin{CD}&&&&&&&&\\
&&&&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots\\
\ \ &&&&\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,N)@>{}>{}>\Tor_{2}^{T}(M,N)@>{}>{}>\Tor_{2}^{R}(M,N)@>{}>{}>&\\
\ \ &&&&M\otimes_{R}N@>{}>{}>\Tor_{1}^{T}(M,N)@>{}>{}>\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,N)@>{}>{}>0.&\\
\end{CD}

Since
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, it follows
M\otimes_{R}N\cong\Tor_{1}^{T}(M,N)
and that
\Tor_{i}^{T}(M,N)=0
for all
i=2,\ldots,n
. Hence

\Tor_{i}^{T}(X,N)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. Similarly, it is easy to check that the hypotheses (i), (ii) and (iii) hold for the pair
(X,N)
over the ring
T
. Therefore, by the induction hypothesis, we have
\Tor_{i}^{T}(X,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, i.e.,
\Tor_{i}^{T}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 2
. This implies
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)\cong\Tor_{i+2}^{R}(M,N)
for all
i\geq 1
. Since, by assumption,
\Tor_{1}^{R}(M,N)
and
\Tor_{2}^{R}(M,N)
vanish, the claim follows.
∎

We finish this section by recording a consequence of Theorem
3.7
; cf. Corollary
2.2
. In the following
\cx_{R}(M)
denotes the
complexity
of
M
; see
[
4
]
.

3.8
.

Assume, in our setup, the ring
(S,\mathfrak{n})
is an unramified or equi-characteristic regular local ring, and the regular sequence
\textbf{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{c}
is contained in
\mathfrak{n}^{2}
. Assume further the following conditions hold:

(a)

M\otimes_{R}N
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay.

(b)

\length(\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N))<\infty
for all
i\gg 0
.

(c)

\cx_{R}(M)\neq c
and
\cx_{R}(N)\neq c
.

(d)

If
\dim(R)<\cod(R)
, assume further
\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,\cod(R)-\dim(R)
.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

M
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay.

(ii)

N
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay.

(iii)

\Tor_{i}^{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

(iv)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

(v)

\Ext^{i}_{R}(N,M)=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

Proof.

Proceeding as in the proof of Corollary
2.2
, it suffices to assume (i) and prove (ii) and (iii) hold; see
[
6
, 4.8, 6.1 and 6.3]
. Hence we assume (i) and induct on
c
. If
c=1
, then
M
is free so the result follows. Thus suppose
c\geq 2
.

Notice, for an indeterminate
z
over
S
, the regular local ring
S[z]_{\mathfrak{n}S[z]}
is unramified or equi-characteristic. Hence extending the residue field by using the faithfully flat extension
S\hookrightarrow S[z]_{\mathfrak{n}S[z]}
, we may assume
R
is complete and has infinite residue field.

It follows there exists a regular sequence
\textbf{f}=f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{c}
on
S
such that
(\textbf{f})S=(\textbf{x})S\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{2}
,
R=R_{1}/(f)
and
\cx_{R_{1}}(M)<\cod(R_{1})=c-1
, where
R_{1}=S/(f_{2},\cdots,f_{c})
and
f=f_{1}
; see
[
25
, 1.3]
and also
[
11
, 2.5]
. Repeating this argument, we obtain a regular sequence
\textbf{y}=y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{c}
on
S
such that
(\textbf{y})S=(\textbf{f})S
,
R=R_{c-1}/(y_{1},\ldots,y_{c-1})
and
\cx_{R_{c-1}}(M)<\cod(R_{c-1})=1
, where
R_{c-1}=S/(y_{c})
. Since
\pd_{R_{c-1}}(M)<\infty
, we have that
M
is Tor-rigid as an
R_{c-1}
-module; see
[
22
, 1.9]
and
[
26
, Theorem 3]
. Therefore, for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp(M\otimes_{R}N)-\{\mathfrak{m}\}
, it follows that

\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})=\depth R_{\mathfrak{p}}+\depth_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}N_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
N_{\mathfrak{p}}
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay. Consequently, by swaping the role of
N
with that of
M
, we conclude from Theorem
3.7
that
\Tor^{R}_{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
.
∎

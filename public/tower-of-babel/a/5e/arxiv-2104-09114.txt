Regularity for quasilinear vectorial elliptic systems through an iterative scheme with numerical applications
Lukas Koch

Abstract
We consider an iterative procedure to solve quasilinear elliptic systems with p p -growth. The scheme was first considered by Koshelev in the quadratic case p = 2 p=2 . We present numerical applications as well as applications to higher regularity properties.

1  Introduction

Let
1<p<\infty
and consider an open and bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This paper is concerned with problems of the form: Seek
u\in W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)=g+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\displaystyle\textup{div}\,a(x,\textup{D}u)=

\displaystyle\,\textup{div}\,f\quad\text{ in }\Omega

(1.1)

where
{a\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}}
is a matrix-valued elliptic structure field satisfying controlled
p
-growth conditions,
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
and
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Here
p^{\prime}
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p
and (
1.1
) is to be understood in the sense of distributions.
To be precise, we make the following assumptions on
a
:

(A1)

For every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
,
a(\cdot,z)
is measurable in
\Omega
and, for almost every
x\in\Omega
,
a(x,\cdot)
is continuously differentiable in
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

(A2)

There are
\Lambda_{a}\geq\lambda_{a}>0
and
\mu\geq 0
such that, for almost every
x\in\Omega
and for all
z,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
,

\lambda_{a}(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi|^{2}\leq\partial_{z}a(x,z)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\Lambda_{a}(\mu^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi|^{2}.

(A3)

There is
c>0
, such that, for all
x\in\Omega
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
,
|a(x,z)|\leq c\left(1+|z|^{p-1}\right)
.

For simplicity of presentation, in addition to
\mathrm{\ref{ass:A1}-\ref{ass:A3}}
, we make the further assumption that

\displaystyle\text{For almost every }x\in\Omega\text{ and every }z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},\,\partial_{z}a(x,z)\text{ is symmetric.}

(A4)

Remark 1.1
.

We have chosen to focus on systems of the form
\textup{div}\,a(x,\textup{D}u)=\textup{div}\,f
, however with minor changes to
\mathrm{\ref{ass:A1}-\ref{ass:A3}}
all the theory we develop applies equally to systems of the form
\textup{div}\,a(x,u,\textup{D}u)=\textup{div}\,f
where
a\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.
Further (
A4
) can be dropped if the results of this paper are modified as indicated in Remark
3.3
.

We will only concern ourselves with the vectorial case
N\geq 2
as the theory in the scalar case
N=1
is different and much more can be said.

The study of elliptic systems of the form (
1.1
) is well established with a very long list of important results. For an introduction we refer to
[
19
]
and
[
21
]
as well as the classical
[
31
]
.
It is well known that in general, without stronger structural assumptions than ellipticity and controlled
p
-growth, solutions to (
1.1
) will not be
C^{1,\alpha}
- or even
C^{0,\alpha}
- regular. In fact, problems already arise in the simplest case when
p=2
. An initial counterexample for systems with
a\equiv a(x,z)
(with
n\geq 3
) was developed in
[
12
]
, where a quadratic system was given for which solutions are not Hölder-continuous and, in fact, fail to be bounded. While the
x
-dependence of the system in
[
12
]
is discontinuous, almost at the same time an example was found of a higher-order elliptic operator with analytic coefficients but discontinuous solutions
[
36
]
. We remark at this point that, if
n=2
, solutions to (
1.1
) are Hölder continuous
[
39
]
. When
a\equiv a(x,y,z)
does indeed depend on
y
, it is possible to give examples where
a
is analytic, but solutions to (
1.1
) are nowhere continuous, see
[
45
,
27
]
, building on examples with analytic fields
a
but discontinuous solutions in
[
22
]
. In this set-up, an example of a system in optimal dimensions
n>2
,
N=2
with discontinuous solutions is given in
[
17
]
. However, the lack of regularity occurs already without
x
-dependence of the coefficients. Even when
a\equiv a(z)
is quadratic and analytic,
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions need not hold, if
n>2
, as
[
40
]
shows.

Due to this lack of regularity, the general regularity theory of systems of type (
1.1
) proceeds through notions of partial regularity, that is, regularity outside of a context-dependent small relatively closed set. Since our focus here lies on results holding everywhere in the domain, we refer the reader to
[
19
,
21
,
38
]
for results and references in this direction.

Regularity results holding in the full domain are known only in a few special cases. The radial Uhlenbeck structure
a\equiv a(\left|z\right|)z
, originally developed in
[
48
]
, is well known to guarantee full
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity. In
[
46
]
, the result is generalised to fields of the form
g(z,z)z
, where
g(\cdot,\cdot)
is a symmetric, positive definite bilinear form. In particular, this theory covers the model case of the
p
-Laplace operator
a(x,y,z)=|z|^{p-2}z
.
There now exists a vast literature concerning the regularity theory of (
1.1
) if
a\equiv a(|z|)z
and we refer the reader to
[
20
,
34
,
35
,
33
,
15
,
14
]
for further results and references.

A second direction of everywhere regularity results, in the case where
a\equiv a(z)
, concerns the case where the modulus of continuity of
\partial_{z}a(z)
is not too large compared to the ellipticity constant of
a(z)
.
C^{1,\alpha}_{\textup{loc}}
-regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) is shown in this set-up in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

A further direction, closely related to the results of this paper, are results of Cordes-Nirenberg type. For linear elliptic PDE, the classical Cordes-Nirenberg results
[
41
,
7
]
state that solutions of

\displaystyle a_{ij}(x)\partial_{ij}u(x)=f(x)

(1.2)

have interior
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity, if
f\in L^{\infty}
and
|a_{ij}-\delta_{ij}|<\varepsilon
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. That is,
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity is obtained when the coefficients
\{a_{ij}\}
are sufficiently close to the identity matrix in an appropriate sense. Note that the identity matrix is nothing but the coefficients corresponding to the Laplacian. In
[
6
]
the result is extended to fully non-linear elliptic equations with solutions in the sense of viscosity solutions. A result of a similar spirit is
[
16
]
, where local
BMO
-regularity for extremals of (a suitable relaxed version of)
\textup{dist}_{K}^{2}(\textup{D}u)
is shown. Here
\textup{dist}_{K}(z)
denotes the distance of
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
to the compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
.

We also comment on the case where
a(x,z)=a(x)z+b(x,z)z
and
\|b(x,z)\|\leq c(n)\lambda_{a}
. Here
a(x)
is a positive definite matrix, with smallest eigenvalue uniformly bounded below in
\Omega
by
\lambda_{a}>0
and
c(n)
is a constant depending only on
n
. If
c(n)
is sufficiently small, then bounded solutions of (
1.1
) are
\alpha
-Hölder-continuous
[
49
]
.

In this paper we obtain regularity results for fields
a
that are close to a suitable reference field
b
, see the start of Section
3
for a precise definition of our notion of reference field. Suppose
a,b
satisfy
(A1)
and
(A2)
, and let
A(x,z)=\partial_{z}a(x,z),B(x,z)=\partial_{z}b(x,z)
. Denote by
\lambda_{B^{-1}A}(x,z),\Lambda_{B^{-1}A}(x,z)
the smallest and largest eigenvalue of
B^{-1}(x,z)A(x,z)
, respectively.
We note that then
\lambda_{B^{-1}A}
has a lower bound, and
\Lambda_{B^{-1}A}(x,z)
has an upper bound, that is independent of
x,z
. Denote these bounds by
\lambda_{a,b}
and
\Lambda_{a,b}
, respectively. We set

\displaystyle K_{a,b}=\frac{\Lambda_{a,b}-\lambda_{a,b}}{\lambda_{a,b}+\Lambda_{a,b}}

(1.3)

We will operate in a regime where
K_{a,b}
is sufficiently small and remark that in all cases we are able to make the smallness assumption on
K_{a,b}
explicit. In this regime we are able to transfer existence and regularity results for solutions of the system
\textup{div}\,b(x,\textup{D}u)=\textup{div}\,f
to solutions of the system
\textup{div}\,a(x,\textup{D}u)=\textup{div}\,f
.

The observation of
[
30
]
is that, if
a
is a matrix-valued elliptic structure field satisfying controlled quadratic growth, then considering the scheme

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n+1}=-\Delta u_{n}-\gamma\textup{div}\,a(x,\textup{D}u_{n})+\gamma\textup{div}\,f&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
u_{n+1}=0&\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

with any choice of
u_{0}\in W^{1,2}_{g}(\Omega)
,
gives a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
that converges in
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
to a solution of (
1.1
). The problem is to be understood in the sense of distributions.
[
30
]
further shows convergence of the scheme in appropriate Morrey spaces, obtaining
C^{0,\alpha}
- and
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions to (
1.1
) under (sharp in the case of
C^{0,\alpha}
) smallness assumptions on
K
. We will return to this point shortly.

We extend this result to matrix-valued elliptic structure fields with
p
-growth.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
and
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Suppose
a,b
satisfy
\mathrm{\ref{ass:A1}-\ref{ass:A3}}
and (
A4
). Assume that the problem

\textup{div}\,b(x,\textup{D}u)=\textup{div}\,F\text{ in }\Omega

has a unique solution in
W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)
for any choice of
F\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.
If

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\Lambda_{b}}{\lambda_{b}}K_{a,b}\left(\dfrac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2p}}6^{p-2}(p-1)<1&\quad\text{ for }p\geq 2\\[20.0pt]
\dfrac{\Lambda_{b}}{\lambda_{b}}K_{a,b}\left(\dfrac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{\frac{(p-2)}{4}}\dfrac{2^{2-p}}{p-1}<1&\quad\text{ for }p\leq 2,\end{cases}

then there is
\gamma>0
such that, if

{u_{0}\in W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)}
, and
{u_{n+1}\in W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)}
is inductively defined to be the weak solution of

\displaystyle\textup{div}\,b(x,\textup{D}u_{n+1})=\textup{div}\,(b(x,\textup{D}u_{n})-\gamma a(x,\textup{D}u_{n})+\gamma f)\text{ in }\Omega

(1.4)

in the class
W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)
, then
u_{n}\to u
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Here
u
is the unique solution in
W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
).

Remark 1.2
.

We note that if
p=2
and
b(x,z)=z
, we recover precisely the situation in
[
30
]
.

The iterative scheme (
1.4
) naturally lends itself as a numerical scheme to solve (
1.1
) using finite element approximations. Take
g=0
and suppose
\Omega
is a regular polytope. Consider a family of shape-regular triangulations
\{T_{h}\}_{h\in(0,1]}
of
\Omega
of mesh-size
h
. Denote by
X_{h}
the space of continuous piecewise linear (subordinate to
T_{h}
) functions. Take
u_{0}\in X_{h}
. Define
u_{n+1}^{h}\in X_{h}
inductively as a solution of the problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}b(x,\textup{D}u_{n+1}^{h})\cdot\textup{D}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(b(x,\textup{D}u_{n}^{h})-\gamma a(x,\textup{D}u_{n}^{h})+\gamma f)\cdot\textup{D}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\quad&\forall\phi\in X_{h}\\
u_{n+1}^{h}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(1.5)

Note that due to
\mathrm{\ref{ass:A1}-\ref{ass:A3}}
and the theory of monotone operators (
1.5
) is well-defined. We prove in Section
4
that under the assumptions of Theorem
1
this scheme converges in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to a solution of the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}a(x,\textup{D}u_{h})\cdot\textup{D}\phi=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\textup{D}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\quad&\forall\phi\in X_{h}\\
u_{h}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Under the additional assumption that,

if
F\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then there is
\alpha>0
such that the equation
\textup{div}\,b(x,\textup{D}u)=\textup{div}\,F
has a solution
u
in
W^{1+\alpha,p}(\Omega)
, and, moreover, we have the following estimate for some
c>0
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{1+\alpha,p}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(1+\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{1/(p-1)}\right),

(1.6)

we show that
u_{h}\to u
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
as
h\to 0
where
u\in W^{1,p}_{g}(\Omega)
is the solution of (
1.1
). We note that (
1.6
) is satisfied by many fields of interest, e.g. the
p
-Laplacian
b(x,z)=|z|^{p-2}z
,
[
44
]
.

If
p=2
and
b(x,z)=z
, the proposed numerical scheme falls into the class of iterative linearised Galerkin schemes studied in
[
26
]
. We refer to this reference for an overview and further references regarding such schemes. An advantage of the schemes studied in this paper, and of unified iterative schemes of the form studied in
[
26
]
in general, is that it is possible to show global convergence results at linear rate for a wide class of problems. This should be compared with nonlinear Newton schemes for which convergence can usually only be expected locally, albeit at quadratic rate. An example of a scheme, fitting into the framework of unified iterative schemes, that has been used to study some problems of
p
-Laplace type where
a(x,z)=a(x,|z|)z
and
a(x,|z|)
has
p-2
-growth, is the so-called Kačanov iteration, originally introduced in
[
28
]
, and defined by solving

\displaystyle\textup{div}\,a(x,|\textup{D}u_{n}|)\textup{D}u_{n+1}=\textup{div}\,f.

Versions of this scheme have been studied in
[
50
,
24
,
18
,
13
,
25
]
, but in general have been restricted to the case
p\in[1,2]
. Note that the scheme studied in this paper cannot be used to replace these schemes as it requires the ability to solve (
1.1
) numerically for a reference field. However, given a problem to which a known numerical scheme, such as the Kačanov iteration can be applied, it enables to solve perturbations of the problem, to which the known scheme cannot be applied directly. For example, in the case of the Kačanov scheme, perturbations need not satisfy any structural assumption of the form
a(x,z)=a(x,|z|)z
.

In
[
30
]
the iterative process is used to derive sharp conditions on the dispersion
\Lambda_{a}/\lambda_{a}
of elliptic systems with quadratic growth that guarantee
C^{0,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions. An alternative proof was suggested in
[
32
]
. Further, under additional assumptions, conditions for
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of solutions are presented. In this spirit, we obtain Cordes-Nirenberg type results with regards to Calderón-Zygmund estimates for perturbations of fields of
p
-Laplace type.
We recall a result of
[

]
:

Theorem 2

(Theorem 1.6 in
[
29
]
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

-boundary for some

. Suppose

2  Preliminaries and notation

c
will denote a a positive constant depending only on
\Omega,N,p
that may change from line to line. We write
a\lesssim b
if there is
c>0
depending only on
\Omega,N,p
such that
a\leq cb
.

Throughout
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be a domain.
Let
1\leq p<\infty
. We denote by
L^{p}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
W^{1,p}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces respectively.
VMO(\Omega)
denotes the space of maps with vanishing mean oscillation. Further
p^{\prime}
will denote the Hölder conjugate of
p
. We also employ the standard fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
where
{k\in(0,\infty)}
, whose theory can be found for example in
[
47
]
. We recall in particular the following fact from
[
23
]
: if
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\alpha>0
and
\Omega
is a regular polytope, then for
u\in W^{1+\alpha,p}(\Omega)
the best approximation
v
to
u
in the space of continuous piecewise linear functions subordinate to a shape-regular triangulation of
\Omega
of mesh-size
h
satisfies

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{W^{1,p}_{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim h^{\alpha}\|u\|_{W^{1+\alpha,p}_{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(2.1)

For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r>0
we denote by
B_{r}(z)
the open ball of radius
r
around
z
.

Given vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
|v|
the Euclidean norm and we denote the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
u\cdot v
.

Given a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
we denote by
\|A\|
the operator norm of
A
. If
A
is positive definite, we denote its smallest eigenvalue by
\lambda_{A}
and its largest by
\Lambda_{A}
. In particular when
a\equiv a(x,z)\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
is such that
a(x,\cdot)
is differentiable in
\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
for almost every
x\in\Omega
, we view
\partial_{z}a(x,y,z)
both as a matrix and as a linear form. If
\partial_{z}a(x,y,z)
is positive definite, we denote
\lambda_{a}=\lambda_{A}
,
\Lambda_{a}=\Lambda_{A}
.

We recall the definition of Muckenhoupt weights. For a fixed
1<p<\infty
, we say that a weight
w\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
belongs to
A_{p}
if
w
is locally integrable and there is a constant
C>0
such that, for all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\fint_{B}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\left(\fint_{B}w^{-\frac{q}{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq C<\infty.

Given a weight
w
on
\Omega
, we denote the weighted Lebesgue-spaces by
L^{p}_{w}(\Omega)
.

Given
\mu\geq 0
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
V_{\mu,p}(v)=(\mu^{2}+|v|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}v
. When the choice of
p
is clear from the context we suppress the index and write
V_{\mu}(v)=V_{\mu,p}(v)
.

We recall some well-known tools for dealing with
p
-growth. The following Lemma is standard, but the author has been unable to find a version in the literature with explicit bounds that tend to
1
as

u_{n}\to u

We note that of particular interest is the choice
w(x)=|x|^{\alpha}
which can be used to obtain estimates in the the familiar Morrey spaces and hence through the Morrey-Sobolev embedding allows to obtain continuity statements. Recall the definition of the
L^{q,\theta}
-Morrey-norm:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q,\theta}(\Omega)}=\sup_{0<r<\text{diam}(\Omega),z\in\Omega}r^{\frac{\theta-n}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega)},

where
\theta\in(0,n)
.
We assume that all the assumptions and the notation of Theorem
10
hold and show how to deduce estimates in Morrey spaces.

Fix
z\in\Omega
,
r\in(0,\text{diam}(\Omega))
and choose for
\rho\in(0,\theta)
,

\displaystyle w(x)=\min\left(|x-z|^{-n+\theta-\rho},r^{-n+\theta-\rho}\right).

Then by our previous work,

\displaystyle\|\textup{D}u\|_{L^{q,\theta}(B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega)}^{q}\leq r^{n-\theta+\rho}\|\textup{D}u\|_{L^{q}_{w}(B_{r}(z)\cap\Omega)}^{q}\leq r^{n-\theta+\rho}c(C,p,q)\left(1+\|f\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p-1}}_{w}(\Omega)}^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\right).

It remains to estimate
\|f\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p-1}}_{w}(\Omega)}
. For this we proceed exactly as
[

]
but provide the argument here for the sake of completeness. We will show that

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p-1}}_{w}(\Omega)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{q,\theta}(\Omega)}^{q}r^{-\rho}

It is convenient to introduce
f^{\prime}
where
|f^{\prime}|^{p-2}f^{\prime}=f
.
For
\alpha>0

{E_{\alpha}=\{x\in\Omega\colon|f^{\prime}|>\alpha\}}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p-1}}(\Omega)}^{q}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}=

\displaystyle q\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{q}\int_{E_{\alpha}}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha\int_{0}^{r^{-n+\theta-\rho}}\left|E_{\alpha}\cap B_{\beta^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho}}}(z)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\beta\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r^{n+\theta-\rho}}\left|E_{\alpha}\cap B_{\beta^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho}}}(z)\right|d\beta\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}2^{-i}r^{-n+\theta-\beta}\left|E_{\alpha}\cap B_{r2^{\frac{-i}{-n+\theta-\rho}}}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}r^{-n+\theta-\rho}}\beta\left|E_{\alpha}\cap B_{\beta^{\frac{1}{-n+\beta-\rho}}}(z)\right|\frac{d\beta}{\beta}.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\frac{q}{p-1}}_{w}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq

\displaystyle 2q\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{q}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}r^{-n+\theta-\rho}}\beta\left|E_{\alpha}\cap B_{\beta^{\frac{1}{-n+\theta-\rho}}}(z)\right|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\beta}{\beta}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\alpha}

\displaystyle 2q\|f\|_{L^{q,\theta}(\Omega)}^{q}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}r^{-n+\theta-\rho}}\beta^{1+\frac{n-\theta}{-n+\theta-\rho}}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{q,\theta}(\Omega)}^{q}r^{-\rho}.

This gives the desired result.

Stars disformally coupled to a shift-symmetric scalar field Preprint: RUP-21-13
Tact Ikeda, Aya Iyonaga, Tsutomu Kobayashi

Abstract
We investigate static and spherically symmetric stars disformally coupled to a scalar field. The scalar field is assumed to be shift symmetric, and hence the conformal and disformal factors of the metric coupled to matter are dependent only on the kinetic term of the scalar field. Assuming that the scalar field is linearly dependent on time, we consider a general shift-symmetric scalar-tensor theory and a general form of the matter energy-momentum tensor that allows for the anisotropic pressure and the heat flux in the radial direction. This is a natural starting point in light of how the gravitational field equations and the energy-momentum tensor transform under a disformal transformation. By inspecting the structure of the hydrostatic equilibrium equation in the presence of the derivative-dependent conformal and disformal factors, we show that the energy density and tangential pressure must vanish at the surface of a star. This fact is used to prove the disformal invariance of the surface of a star, which was previously subtle and unclear. We then focus on the shift-symmetric k-essence disformally coupled to matter, and study the interior and exterior metric functions and scalar-field profile in more detail. It is found that there are two branches of the solution depending on the velocity of the scalar field. The disformally-related metric functions of the exterior spacetime are also discussed.

II
Spherically symmetric stars disformally coupled to a scalar field

In this section, we first derive general field equations
in the case where a scalar field is disformally coupled to matter,
and then provide basic equations for the study of spherically symmetric stars
in the presence of a disformally coupled scalar field with shift symmetry.

II.1
Field equations

We start with a general class of scalar-tensor theories
described by the action

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=S_{\rm grav}[g_{\mu\nu},\phi]+S_{\rm m}[\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\psi_{\rm m}]

\displaystyle=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}{\cal L}_{\rm grav}(g_{\mu\nu},\phi)+\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}{\cal L}_{\rm m}(\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\psi_{\rm m}).

(2)

where the matter field
\psi_{\rm m}
is coupled to
the disformally-related metric (
1
).

Varying the action (
2
) with respect to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
,
we obtain the gravitational field equations,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mu\nu}:=\mathcal{G}^{\mu\nu}-T^{\mu\nu}=0,

(3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\rm grav}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}},\quad T^{\mu\nu}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\rm m}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}.

(4)

Varying the action (
2
) with respect to the
scalar field
\phi
, we obtain the scalar-field equation of motion,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\phi}:=\mathcal{F}_{\phi}-T_{\phi}=0,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\rm grav}}{\delta\phi},\quad T_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\rm m}}{\delta\phi}.

(6)

We assume that
S_{\rm grav}
and
S_{\rm m}
are separately
invariant under an infinitesimal coordinate transformation,

x^{\mu}\to x^{\mu}+\xi^{\mu}
.
It then follows the Bianchi identities,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}{\cal E}^{\mu\nu}+{\cal E}_{\phi}\phi^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}+T_{\phi}\phi^{\mu}

\displaystyle=0.

(8)

Equivalently, one may use

\nabla_{\nu}{\cal G}^{\mu\nu}+{\cal F}_{\phi}\phi^{\mu}=0

instead of Eq. (
7
).

Using the fact that
S_{\rm m}
depends on
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi

through the disformally-related metric (
1
),
we write
T_{\phi}
in a more explicit form:

\displaystyle T_{\phi}=\nabla_{\mu}W^{\mu}-Z,

(9)

where

\displaystyle W^{\mu}

\displaystyle=w_{1}T^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\nu}+w_{2}T\phi^{\mu}+w_{3}T^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}\phi^{\mu},

(10)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=z_{1}T+z_{2}T^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta},

(11)

and
T=T_{\mu}^{\mu}
,
with
w_{i}
and
z_{j}
being functions of
\phi
and
X
defined as

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma}{\Omega},

(12)

\displaystyle w_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Omega_{X}}{\Omega}\frac{\Omega-2X\Gamma}{\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X}},

(13)

\displaystyle w_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma_{X}}{\Omega}\frac{\Omega-2X\Gamma}{\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X}},

(14)

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Omega_{\phi}}{\Omega}+\frac{X\Omega_{X}}{\Omega}\frac{\Omega_{\phi}-2X\Gamma_{\phi}}{\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X}}\right),

(15)

\displaystyle z_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Gamma_{\phi}}{\Omega}+\frac{X\Gamma_{X}}{\Omega}\frac{\Omega_{\phi}-2X\Gamma_{\phi}}{\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X}}\right).

(16)

A detailed derivation is presented in Appendix
A

(see also Ref.
Chibana:2019jrf
).
Note that if the coupling functions
\Omega
and
\Gamma

preserve the shift symmetry,
\phi\to\phi+c
, then one has
Z=0
.

II.2
Basic equations for spherically symmetric stars

Hereafter, we focus on shift-symmetric theories,

\phi\to\phi+c
, and require the same symmetry for the coupling functions.

Let us consider a static and spherically symmetric metric,

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}=-e^{\nu(r)}{\rm d}t^{2}+e^{\lambda(r)}{\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}\left({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\varphi^{2}\right).

(17)

Even though the metric is assumed to be static,
the shift symmetry admits a linearly time-dependent configuration of the scalar field,

\displaystyle\phi=\mu t+\psi(r),

(18)

where
\mu
(
\neq 0
) is a constant.

We assume the following general form of the energy-momentum tensor,

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\rho(r)&e^{-\nu}\chi(r)&0&0\\
-e^{-\lambda}\chi(r)&P_{r}(r)&0&0\\
0&0&P_{\perp}(r)&0\\
0&0&0&P_{\perp}(r)\\
\end{array}\right),

where we allow for the off-diagonal component,
\chi(r):=-T_{tr}
,
i.e., the heat flux in the radial direction,
and anisotropic pressure,
P_{r}\neq P_{\perp}
.
Below we will see that in general this off-diagonal component is
necessary if
\mu\neq 0
.

Under the above ansatz, the radial and temporal components of
the Bianchi identities (
7
) read, respectively,

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\nu/2}}{r^{2}}\left(r^{2}e^{\nu/2}{\cal E}_{r}^{r}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{\nu^{\prime}}{2}{\cal E}_{t}^{t}-\frac{2}{r}{\cal E}_{\perp}+{\cal E}_{\phi}\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0,

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}}{r^{2}}\left[r^{2}e^{(\nu+\lambda)/2}{\cal E}_{t}^{r}\right]^{\prime}+{\cal E}_{\phi}\mu

\displaystyle=0,

(24)

where a prime denotes differentiation with respect to
r

and
{\cal E}_{\perp}
stands for the angular components,

{\cal E}_{\perp}:={\cal E}_{\theta}^{\theta}={\cal E}_{\varphi}^{\varphi}
.
These identities show that one may take

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{t}_{t}={\cal G}_{t}^{t}+\rho=0,

(25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{r}_{r}={\cal G}_{r}^{r}-P_{r}=0,

(26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{tr}={\cal G}_{tr}+\chi=0,

(27)

as independent equations, while
{\cal E}_{\perp}=0
and
{\cal E}_{\phi}=0

follow automatically from the Bianchi identities.

We now look into Eq. (
8
). Similarly to Eqs. (
23
)
and (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{r}^{\prime}+\frac{\nu^{\prime}}{2}(\rho+P_{r})+\frac{2}{r}(P_{r}-P_{\perp})+T_{\phi}\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0,

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}}{r^{2}}\left[r^{2}e^{(\nu+\lambda)/2}T_{t}^{r}\right]^{\prime}+T_{\phi}\mu

\displaystyle=0,

(29)

where, by noting that
Z=0
due to the shift symmetry,

\displaystyle T_{\phi}=\frac{e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}}{r^{2}}\left[r^{2}e^{(\nu+\lambda)/2}W^{r}\right]^{\prime}.

(30)

Explicitly, one has

\displaystyle W^{r}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda}\psi^{\prime}\bigl\{w_{1}P_{r}-w_{2}(\rho-P_{r}-2P_{\perp})

\displaystyle\quad+w_{3}\left[\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}\rho+e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}P_{r}\right]\bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu e^{-\nu-\lambda}\left[w_{1}+2w_{3}e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\right]\chi.

(31)

One can immediately integrate Eq. (
29
) to get

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda}\chi-\mu W^{r}=\frac{{\cal C}e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}}{r^{2}},

(32)

where
{\cal C}
is an integration constant.
To fix
{\cal C}
, let us consider the behavior of the left-hand side
at the center. We may assume that

\nu
,
\lambda
,
\psi^{\prime}
,
\rho
,
P_{r}
,
P_{\perp}
, and
\chi

do not diverge as
r\to 0
. Then,
W^{r}
does not diverge
at the center. This observation leads us to set
{\cal C}=0
.
Having shown that, we can solve Eq. (
32
)
for
\chi
to obtain

\displaystyle\chi=\mu\psi^{\prime}h(\nu,\lambda,\psi^{\prime},\rho,P_{r},P_{\perp}),

(33)

where

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle:=\frac{w_{1}P_{r}-w_{2}(\rho-P_{r}-2P_{\perp})}{1-\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}[w_{1}+2w_{3}e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}]}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{w_{3}\left[\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}\rho+e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}P_{r}\right]}{1-\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}[w_{1}+2w_{3}e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}]}.

(34)

In general, there is no reason to impose
h=0
and
hence we have
\chi\neq 0
.

II.3
The surface of a star

We now consider more specifically
the case where
{\cal L}_{\rm grav}
is given by
that of the shift-symmetric Horndeski theory,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm grav}

\displaystyle=G_{2}(X)-G_{3}(X)\Box\phi+G_{4}(X)R

\displaystyle\quad+G_{4X}\left[(\Box\phi)^{2}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi\right].

(35)

We do not consider the “
G_{5}
” terms for simplicity;
they do not lead to no essential difference in the following results.

The goal of this subsection is to discuss the matching conditions
at the surface of a star,
r=r_{s}
, defined by
P_{r}(r_{s})=0
.
For this purpose only the structure of
the highest derivative terms in
{\cal G}_{\mu\nu}

is important.
We have

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{t}^{t}

\displaystyle=g_{1}+g_{2}\lambda^{\prime}+g_{3}\psi^{\prime\prime}=-\rho,

(36)

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{r}^{r}

\displaystyle=g_{4}-g_{2}\nu^{\prime}=P_{r},

(37)

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{tr}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu e^{\lambda}}{2}\left(g_{5}+g_{3}\nu^{\prime}\right)=-\chi,

(38)

where
g_{a}=g_{a}(\nu,\lambda,\psi^{\prime})
(
a=1,2,3,4,5
).
Using Eqs. (
37
) and (
38
) one can eliminate
\nu^{\prime}

to get the equation of the form

\displaystyle\lambda=F_{\lambda}(\nu,\psi^{\prime},P_{r},\chi).

(39)

Equation (
28
) is independent of
the specific form of the gravitational part of the Lagrangian.

We impose that the induced metric
is continuous across the surface of a star,
and hence
\nu
is continuous.
If the matter is minimally coupled to gravity,

\rho
and
P_{\perp}
are allowed to be discontinuous at
r=r_{s}
,
and then it follows from Eqs. (
36
) and (
39
) that

\lambda^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}
may be discontinuous.
The situation becomes more subtle if the matter is non-minimally
coupled to gravity through
X
-dependent
\Omega
and
\Gamma
.
Now, we have

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\supset\frac{e^{-\lambda}\psi^{\prime}\left[-(w_{2}-w_{3}\mu^{2}e^{-\nu})\rho^{\prime}+2w_{2}P_{\perp}^{\prime}\right]}{1-\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}[w_{1}+2w_{3}e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}]},

(40)

which yields the delta functions
\sim\delta(r-r_{s})
.
However, we see that no other terms in Eq. (
28
)
contain the delta functions canceling
T_{\phi}
.
It is therefore required in the present case that

\rho
and
P_{\perp}
are continuous across the surface, i.e.,

\lim_{r\to r_{s}-0}\rho(r)=0
and
\lim_{r\to r_{s}-0}P_{\perp}(r)=0
.
Note that the first derivatives of
\rho
,
P_{r}
, and
P_{\perp}

may be discontinuous at
r=r_{s}
, and so are
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}
,
provided that
g_{2}\lambda^{\prime}+g_{3}\psi^{\prime\prime}
is continuous.
Note also that there is an exceptional case where

\psi^{\prime}(r_{s})=0
, to which the above argument does not apply.

The requirement that
\rho(r_{s})=P_{\perp}(r_{s})=0

in the presence of the
X
-dependent coupling functions
has a direct implication on the disformal invariance of
the notion of the surface of a star.
As discussed in Appendix
B
,
the radial pressure transforms under a disformal transformation as

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{r}=m_{4}\rho+m_{5}P_{r}+m_{6}P_{\perp},

(41)

where the explicit expressions for
m_{4}
,
m_{5}
, and
m_{6}

can be read off from the equations in Appendix
B
.
It is obvious that if
\rho(r_{s})=P_{r}(r_{s})=P_{\perp}(r_{s})=0
then

\widetilde{P}_{r}
vanishes there. Therefore, the notion of
the surface of a star is invariant under a disformal transformation.
By utilizing the consistency of the hydrostatic equation (
28
),
we have thus arrived at the stronger conclusion than
that in Ref.
Minamitsuji:2021rtw
.
Note in passing that
in the aforementioned exceptional case where
\psi^{\prime}(r_{s})=0
,
we have
m_{4}=m_{6}=0
and hence the same conclusion holds.

III
The case of k-essence

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm grav}=\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R+G_{2}(X),

The function

\displaystyle G_{2}(X_{*})=0,\quad G_{2X}(X_{*})=0,

3

3

\displaystyle=-\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\lambda}-re^{-\lambda}\nu^{\prime}\right)-G_{2}=P_{r}.

III.1
Interior solution

Let us begin with the inspection of the interior solution.
We assume that the radial and tangential pressures are
related to

and
P_{\perp}=P_{\perp}(\rho)
.
To determine the metric functions in the stellar interior,
we first solve Eq. (
45
) algebraically to write

\psi^{\prime}=F(\nu,\lambda,\rho)
.
(Obviously,
F=0
in the Branch I.)
Using this, one can express
T_{\phi}
in terms of

\nu,\lambda,\rho
, and their first derivatives.
Thus eliminating
\psi^{\prime}
,
Eqs. (
28
), (
46
), and (
47
)
reduce to three first-order differential equations for
\nu
,
\lambda
, and
\rho
.
Given the boundary conditions at
r=0
, one can integrate these equations
outwards to determine the stellar structure.
The radius of a star,
r_{s}
, is defined by
P_{r}(r_{s})=0
.

We require that
\nu
,
\lambda
,
\psi
,
\rho
,
P_{r}
, and
P_{\perp}

are regular at
r=0
. Then,
near the center, we may expanded these quantities as

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\nu_{c}+\frac{\nu_{2}}{2}r^{2}+\cdots,

(48)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=0+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}r^{2}+\cdots,

(49)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}r+\dots,

(50)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\rho_{c}+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2}r^{2}+\cdots,

(51)

\displaystyle P_{r}

\displaystyle=P_{r,c}+\frac{P_{r,2}}{2}r^{2}+\cdots,

(52)

\displaystyle P_{\perp}

\displaystyle=P_{\perp,c}+\frac{P_{\perp,2}}{2}r^{2}+\cdots.

(53)

Note here that, as can be seen from Eqs. (
46
) and (
47
),
we have
1-e^{-\lambda}={\cal O}(r^{2})
near the center and hence
\lambda(0)=0
.
In contrast,
\nu_{c}
is not determined locally and
one can integrate the equations for any value of
\nu_{c}
.
The choice of
\nu_{c}
is correlated with how one sets the zero of
the gravitational potential. Usually, one tunes
\nu_{c}
so that
the boundary conditions in the stellar exterior are satisfied.

From Eqs. (
48
)–(
50
) we see that

\displaystyle X=X_{c}-\frac{1}{2}(X_{c}\nu_{2}+\psi_{2}^{2})r^{2}+\cdots,

(54)

where

\displaystyle X_{c}:=\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}e^{-\nu_{c}}.

(55)

Therefore, for a function
f
of
X
(such as
w_{1}
), we have

f(X)=f(X_{c})+{\cal O}(r^{2})
near the center.
Now, it is easy to see that
W^{r}={\cal O}(r)
and
T_{\phi}=\;
constant
near the center. This fact and Eq. (
28
) imply that

\displaystyle P_{r}-P_{\perp}={\cal O}(r^{2})\quad\Rightarrow\quad P_{r,c}=P_{\perp,c}\,(=:P_{c}).

(56)

On the basis of the above argument, let us turn back to Eq. (
45
).
It is clear that the Branch I
is always consistent with the boundary conditions at
r=0
.
In particular, in this branch,

\mu
is a free parameter as far as the interior solution is concerned.
In contrast, for a given central density
\rho_{c}
(and a central pressure
according to the equation of state),

\displaystyle\left.h-G_{2X}\right|_{r=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{1}P_{c}-w_{2}(\rho_{c}-3P_{c})+2X_{c}w_{3}\rho_{c}}{1-2X_{c}w_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad-G_{2X}(X_{c})

(57)

is vanishing only if a particular value of
X_{c}
is chosen.
This implies that the Branch II is consistent
only for a particular value of
\mu
, and this particular value is
dependent on the central density
\rho_{c}
of a star.

III.2
Exterior solution

First let us consider the Branch I, in which
the interior solution with
\psi^{\prime}=0
is smoothly connected to
the exterior solution with
\psi^{\prime}=0
.
In this branch one cannot obtain an analytic solution valid
in the entire region of the stellar exterior.
We therefore discuss linearized solutions around the
Minkowski spacetime with
X=X_{*}
:

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle\simeq\nu_{*}+\delta\nu(r)\ll 1,\quad\lambda\simeq\delta\lambda(r)\ll 1,

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}e^{-\nu_{*}}(1-\delta\nu),

(58)

where

\displaystyle\nu_{*}=\ln\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{2X_{*}}\right).

(59)

By taking
\mu=\sqrt{2X_{*}}
, one can set
\nu_{*}=0
.
With this choice,
\mu
is determined solely by theory parameters.

Linearization is justified sufficiently away from the star,
r\gg r_{s}
.
The linearized field equations read

\displaystyle\frac{r\delta\lambda^{\prime}+\delta\lambda}{r^{2}}+q\delta\nu=0,\quad r\delta\nu^{\prime}-\delta\lambda=0,

(60)

where we defined

\displaystyle q:=\frac{2X_{*}^{2}G_{2XX}\left(X_{*}\right)}{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}}.

(61)

The general solution to these equations is given by

\displaystyle\delta\nu

\displaystyle=C_{1}\frac{\cos(\sqrt{q}r)}{r}+C_{2}\frac{\sin(\sqrt{q}r)}{r},

(62)

\displaystyle\delta\lambda

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{q}\left[C_{1}\sin(\sqrt{q}r)-C_{2}\cos(\sqrt{q}r)\right]-\delta\nu,

(63)

for
q>0
and

\displaystyle\delta\nu

\displaystyle=C_{3}\frac{e^{\sqrt{-q}r}}{r}+C_{4}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{-q}r}}{r},

(64)

\displaystyle\delta\lambda

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-q}\left[C_{3}e^{\sqrt{-q}r}-C_{4}e^{-\sqrt{-q}r}\right]-\delta\nu,

(65)

for
q<0
.

The oscillatory behavior for
q>0
is qualitatively similar to
what has been found in
the Newtonian potential in the ghost condensate model
ArkaniHamed:2003uy
.
Suppose that the length scale associated to
q
is very large,
say, the cosmological horizon scale, and consider the metric
in the region
r_{s}\ll r\ll 1/\sqrt{q}
. We then have

\displaystyle\delta\nu\simeq C_{2}\sqrt{q}+\frac{C_{1}}{r},\quad\delta\lambda\simeq-\frac{C_{1}}{r}.

(66)

After fixing
\mu
as
\mu=\sqrt{2X_{*}}
, one can tune
\nu_{c}

so that the boundary condition
C_{2}=0
is satisfied.
The metric functions for
r_{s}\ll r\ll 1/\sqrt{q}
are thus
described by the linearized Schwarzschild solution
with
-C_{1}
being the mass parameter.

In the case of
q<0
, the diverging term can be removed by
imposing the boundary condition
C_{3}=0
.
After fixing
\mu
as
\mu=\sqrt{2X_{*}}
, this can be satisfied
by tuning
\nu_{c}
. Then,
in the region
r_{s}\ll r\ll 1/\sqrt{-q}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta\nu\simeq-C_{4}\sqrt{-q}+\frac{C_{4}}{r},\quad\delta\lambda\simeq-\frac{C_{4}}{r}.

(67)

In this case one can no longer remove the constant part
-C_{4}\sqrt{-q}
,
but this term is physically unimportant.

Next, let us consider the Branch II, in which
the interior solution with
h-G_{2X}=0
is connected to
the exterior solution with
G_{2X}=0
at the surface where
h=0
.
In this branch,
we have
X=X_{*}
in the entire region of the stellar exterior,
and the metric functions take the
Schwarzschild form,

\displaystyle e^{\nu}=e^{\nu_{\infty}}\left(1-\frac{C_{0}}{r}\right),\quad e^{-\lambda}=1-\frac{C_{0}}{r},

(68)

where
\nu_{\infty}
and
C_{0}
are integration constants.
Note that
\nu_{\infty}
has no physical significance and can be set to zero
by shifting
\nu_{c}
.

III.4
Gravity that matter feels

We have thus obtained the profile of the metric functions
\nu
and
\lambda
.
However, since matter is coupled to the disformally-related
metric
\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
,
the actual metric that we observe is
\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
.
Therefore, let us write the line element

\widetilde{{\rm d}s}{}^{2}=\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}{\rm d}\widetilde{x}^{\mu}\widetilde{x}^{\nu}

in the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\rm d}s}{}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(1+2\widetilde{\Phi}){\rm d}\widetilde{\tau}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-2\widetilde{\Psi})[{\rm d}\widetilde{\varrho}^{2}+\widetilde{\varrho}^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\varphi^{2})],

(70)

and evaluate
\widetilde{\Phi}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
in the region
where linearization is valid.

In the Branch I, we have
X\simeq X_{*}(1-\delta\nu)
and hence

\Omega(X)=\Omega_{*}-X_{*}\Omega_{X*}\delta\nu
,
where the quantities with the asterisk are evaluated at
X=X_{*}
.
By performing the coordinate transformation

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}

\displaystyle=(\Omega_{*}-2X_{*}\Gamma_{*})^{1/2}t,

(71)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varrho}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{1/2}_{*}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\int^{r}\frac{\delta\lambda}{r^{\prime}}{\rm d}r^{\prime}\right)r,

(72)

we can recast the disformally-related metric to the form of
Eq. (
70
) and find that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{*}-X_{*}(\Omega_{X*}-2X_{*}\Gamma_{X*})}{2(\Omega_{*}-2X_{*}\Gamma_{*})}\delta\nu,

(73)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int^{r}\frac{\delta\lambda}{r^{\prime}}{\rm d}r^{\prime}+\frac{X_{*}\Omega_{X*}}{2\Omega_{*}}\delta\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{X_{*}\Omega_{X*}}{\Omega_{*}}\right)\delta\nu,

(74)

where we used
\delta\lambda=r\delta\nu^{\prime}
in the last line.
We therefore have

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Psi}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}=\frac{(1+X_{*}\Omega_{X*}/\Omega_{*})(1-2X_{*}\Gamma_{*}/\Omega_{*})}{1-X_{*}(\Omega_{X*}-2X_{*}\Gamma_{X*})/\Omega_{*}}.

(75)

This result can be used to evaluate the deviation from
general relativity. Note that it is easy to find the cases
where
\Omega_{X}
and
\Gamma
are not small, but
\widetilde{\Psi}\simeq\widetilde{\Phi}
.
A simple example is given by

\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega_{0}e^{\beta_{1}X/X_{*}},\quad\Gamma=\frac{\beta_{2}\Omega_{0}}{X_{*}}e^{\beta_{1}X/X_{*}}.

(76)

In this case we see that
\widetilde{\Psi}\simeq\widetilde{\Phi}

for
\beta_{2}\simeq\beta_{1}/(1+2\beta_{1})
.
However, in the case of conformal coupling,
\Gamma=0
,

X_{*}\Omega_{X*}/\Omega
must be sufficiently small.

In the Branch II, the disformally-related metric
cannot take the form of Eq. (
70
) in general.
Instead one needs to consider
the metric with a solid deficit angle
BenAchour:2019fdf
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\rm d}s}{}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(1+2\widetilde{\Phi}){\rm d}\widetilde{\tau}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1-2\widetilde{\Psi})[{\rm d}\widetilde{\varrho}^{2}+s_{0}^{-1}\widetilde{\varrho}^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\varphi^{2})],

(77)

where
s_{0}
is a constant.
To put the metric into this form, we perform the coordinate transformation

\displaystyle{\rm d}\widetilde{\tau}

\displaystyle=(\Omega_{*}-\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*})\left({\rm d}t-\frac{\mu\Gamma\psi^{\prime}}{\Omega e^{\nu}-\mu^{2}\Gamma}{\rm d}r\right),

(78)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varrho}

\displaystyle=s_{0}^{1/2}\Omega_{*}^{1/2}\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\int^{r}\left(\frac{\delta\lambda}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*}}{\Omega_{*}-\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*}}\frac{\delta\nu}{r^{\prime}}\right){\rm d}r^{\prime}\right]r,

(79)

where

\displaystyle s_{0}:=\frac{\Omega_{*}-2X_{*}\Gamma_{*}}{\Omega_{*}-\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*}}.

(80)

It is then found that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Omega_{*}}{\Omega_{*}-\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*}}\delta\nu,

(81)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int^{r}\left(\frac{\delta\lambda}{r^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*}}{\Omega_{*}-\mu^{2}\Gamma_{*}}\frac{\delta\nu}{r^{\prime}}\right){\rm d}r^{\prime}.

(82)

Since
\delta\nu=-\delta\lambda=-C_{0}/r
,
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}=\widetilde{\Psi}\propto\frac{1}{\widetilde{\varrho}}.

(83)

Thus, in the Branch II
the difference from general relativity
can be found in the solid deficit angle
BenAchour:2019fdf
.
Since the deficit angle changes geodesics,
we can measure its effect by observing the light propagation,
e.g., the deflection angle of the light.
Barriola:1989hx
;
Ono:2018jrv
.

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied static and
spherically symmetric stars disformally coupled with a scalar field.
We have been focusing on shift-symmetric theories,
so that the conformal and disformal factors of the metric
that couples to matter are dependent only on

X:=-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi/2
.
The shift symmetry admits the scalar-field configuration
that is dependent linearly on time,

\phi=\mu t+\psi(r)
, where
\mu\,(=\partial_{t}\phi)
is the constant
velocity of the scalar field.
Starting from a general scalar-tensor theory and
a general form of the energy-momentum tensor,
we have overviewed the gravitational field equations and
fluid equations for matter. More specifically, our energy-momentum
tensor allows for the radial heat flux and anisotropic pressure.
Such a general form of the energy-momentum tensor
is implied by the way it transforms under a disformal transformation,
and hence is natural to consider in the present setup.

We have investigated the density and pressure profile
in the vicinity of the surface of a star (defined as the location
at which the radial pressure vanishes,
P_{r}(r_{s})=0
).
It is known that the density
\rho
and tangential pressure
P_{\perp}
may be
discontinuous across the stellar surface as long as
matter is minimally coupled to gravity.
In the presence of
X
-dependent conformal and disformal factors,
however, we have shown that
\rho(r_{s})=P_{\perp}(r_{s})=0

is requried by the structure of the hydrostatic equilibrium equation.
This in turn indicates the disformal invariance of
the surface of a star. This fact was previously subtle and
shown only when certain extra conditions are met
Minamitsuji:2021rtw
.

We have then concentrated on the shift-symmetric k-essence theory
disformally coupled to matter, and
studied the interior and exterior metric functions and scalar-field profile.
We have found that there are two branches of the solution
depending on the value of
\mu
.
One branch, which we call the Branch I,
is characterized by the trivial scalar-field profile,
\psi^{\prime}=0
.
The velocity
\mu
in the Branch I is determined solely by theory parameters.
The other branch, which we call the Branch II,
is characterized by the stealth Schwarzschild geometry with
the constant
X
profile in the exterior region.
The velocity
\mu
in the Branch II is determined by the central density
as well as theory parameters.
The disformally transformed metric that couples to matter
has also been discussed.
We have found in particular
that in the Branch I the standard result of general relativity
can be reproduced if a certain relation between the conformal and
disformal factors is satisfied.

As we have seen in Fig.
2
,
the Branch I solution exhibits an oscillatory behavior
of the metric functions far away from the source,
which may lead to interesting cosmological consequences.
We hope to come back to the study of cosmology in the present setup in the near future.

Appendix A
Derivation of Eqs. (

)–(

)

We start with writing
T^{\mu\nu}
as

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\widetilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\widetilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}}\left(\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}{\cal L}_{\rm m}\right)

\displaystyle={\cal J}_{g}\biggl(\Omega\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{X}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta}\widetilde{T}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{X}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}\widetilde{T}^{\alpha\beta}\biggr),

(84)

where

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}}{\sqrt{-g}}=\Omega^{3/2}\sqrt{\Omega-2X\Gamma},

(85)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}}\left(\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}{\cal L}_{\rm m}\right).

(86)

By contracting Eq. (
84
) with
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}

and solving the resultant equations for
g_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}

and
\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\Omega+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X})T+X\Gamma_{X}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}}{{\cal J}_{g}\Omega(\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X})},

(87)

\displaystyle\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2X^{2}\Omega_{X}T+(\Omega-X\Omega_{X})\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}T^{\mu\nu}}{{\cal J}_{g}\Omega(\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X})}.

(88)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{g}\Omega\widetilde{T}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=T^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\Omega_{X}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}T}{2(\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X})}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\Gamma_{X}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}T^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}}{2(\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X})}.

(89)

Now, we may write

\displaystyle T_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\phi}}{\delta\phi}=\nabla_{\mu}W^{\mu}-Z,

(90)

\displaystyle W^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{\mu}}\left(\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}{\cal L}_{\rm m}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\widetilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\phi_{\mu}}\cdot{\cal J}_{g}\widetilde{T}^{\alpha\beta},

(91)

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\left(\sqrt{-\widetilde{g}}{\cal L}_{\rm m}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\widetilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial\phi}\cdot{\cal J}_{g}\widetilde{T}^{\alpha\beta}.

(92)

Substituting Eq. (
89
) to Eqs. (
91
)
and (

10
)–(

).

and

\widetilde{T}^{\widetilde{t}\widetilde{r}}

33

\displaystyle\widetilde{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{1}\rho+M_{2}P_{r}+M_{3}P_{\perp}}{{\cal J}_{g}\{1-\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}[w_{1}+2w_{3}e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}]\}},

(100)

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{4}\rho+M_{5}P_{r}+M_{6}P_{\perp}}{{\cal J}_{g}\{1-\mu^{2}e^{-\nu}[w_{1}+2w_{3}e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}]\}},

(101)

(102)

where

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X}}\times

\displaystyle+\frac{e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}}{2}\left(1-2e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}\frac{\Gamma}{\Omega}+2e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}X\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}\right)\Omega_{X}\biggr],

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}}{2(\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X})}\times

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-2e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}\frac{\Gamma}{\Omega}+2e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}X\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{\Omega^{2}}\right)\Omega_{X}\biggr],

(104)

\displaystyle M_{3}

\displaystyle=2e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}w_{2}\frac{\Omega-2X\Gamma-2e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\Gamma}{\Omega},

(105)

\displaystyle M_{4}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}(-w_{2}+e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}w_{3}),

(106)

\displaystyle M_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega-2X\Gamma}{\Omega-X\Omega_{X}+2X^{2}\Gamma_{X}}+e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}(w_{2}-e^{-\nu}\mu^{2}w_{3}),

(107)

\displaystyle M_{6}

\displaystyle=2e^{-\lambda}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}w_{2}.

(108)

This result implies that even if the fluid is isotropic in one frame,
it is anisotropic in the other frame, as already
pointed out in Ref.
Minamitsuji:2021rtw
.
By taking
\mu=0
one can reproduce the expressions
presented in Ref.
Minamitsuji:2021rtw

Appendix C
Jump between branches

In Sec.
III
we have found two branches of the solution
on the basis of the
(tr)
-component of the gravitational field equations (
45
).
In this appendix,
we show that a transition from one branch to another can not
occur in the interior region of the star unless
an exceptional fine tuning takes place.
The discussion here is similar to that of Ref.
Lehebel:2017fag
.

Suppose that a solution exhibits a transition from
the Branch I (
\psi^{\prime}=0
) to the Branch II (
h-G_{2X}=0
) at some
r=r_{0}
.
(The transition from the Branch II to the Branch I can be
treated in the same way.)
We assume that
\nu,\lambda,\psi,\rho,P_{r}
, and
P_{\perp}
are
C^{1}
.
We can then expand various quantities around
r_{0}
:

\displaystyle\nu\simeq\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}\left(r-r_{0}\right),\quad\lambda\simeq\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}\left(r-r_{0}\right),

\displaystyle\rho\simeq\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}\left(r-r_{0}\right),\quad P_{r}\simeq P_{r,0}+P_{r,1}\left(r-r_{0}\right),\quad

\displaystyle P_{\perp}\simeq P_{\perp,0}+P_{\perp,1}\left(r-r_{0}\right).

(109)

Since our field equations include functions of
X
,
it should be expanded similarly as

\displaystyle X\simeq X_{0}+X_{1}(r-r_{0}),

(110)

so that for any smooth function
f
of
X
we have
f\simeq f(X_{0})+f^{\prime}(X_{0})X_{1}(r-r_{0})
.
This implies that

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}=0\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad(r\leq r_{0}),

(111)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}\simeq\psi_{1/2}^{\prime}(r-r_{0})^{1/2}\quad(r\geq r_{0}).

(112)

Now it can be seen that Eq. (
28
) contains

\displaystyle T_{\phi}\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}}{r^{2}}\left(r^{2}e^{(\nu-\lambda)/2}\psi^{\prime}h\right)^{\prime}\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\psi^{\prime}\right)^{2}\frac{e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}}{r^{2}}\left(r^{2}e^{(\nu-\lambda)/2}h\right)^{\prime}+\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}e^{\lambda}h,

(113)

and the second term in the last line
shows a discontinuity at
r=r_{0}
. However,
this is not acceptable because
all the other terms in the same equation are
C^{1}
.

One possible loophole is the case where
\psi_{1/2}^{\prime}=0
and
the expansion of
\psi^{\prime}
starts with a higher-order term in
r-r_{0}
.
Another loophole is the case where
h=0\,(=G_{2X})
at
r=r_{0}
.
In both cases, an extreme fine tuning is required to
the theory, the equation of state, and the central density of the star.
Only under such an extreme fine tuning, a jump between
the branches could occur, and hence practically we do not need
to care about the possibility of a branch jump.

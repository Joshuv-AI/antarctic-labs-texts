A Proof of Merca’s Conjectures on Sums of Odd Divisor Functions
Kaya Lakein and Anne Larsen

Abstract
. In a recent paper, Merca posed three conjectures on congruences for specific convolutions of a sum of odd divisor functions with a generating function for generalized m m -gonal numbers. Extending Merca’s work, we complete the proof of these conjectures.



Euler’s partition function
p(n)
is defined by the number of partitions of any nonnegative integer
n
, and its generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{k}},\quad\absolutevalue{q}<1.

The properties of the function
p(n)
, such as its asymptotic behavior and its parity, have been an object of study for a long time. For instance, Ballantine and Merca
[
1
]
recently made a conjecture on when

\sum_{ak+1\text{ is a square}}p(n-k)

is odd,
which was proved by Hong and Zhang
[
2
]
.

The function
p(n)
is linked to the divisor function

\sigma(n):=\sum_{d\mid n}d,

whose generating function is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nq^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

In particular,
p(n)
and
\sigma(n)
satisfy the following convolution identities, which differ only in the values of
p(0)
and
\sigma(0)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}p(n-P_{5}(k))

\displaystyle=\delta_{0,n},\text{ with }p(0)=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\sigma(n-P_{5}(k))

\displaystyle=0,\text{ with }\sigma(0)\text{ replaced by }n,

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta, and
P_{m}(k)
is the
k
th generalized
m
-gonal number

(1)

\displaystyle P_{m}(k):=\left(\frac{m}{2}-1\right)k^{2}-\left(\frac{m}{2}-2\right)k.

Motivated by these identities as well as the fact that the divisor functions
\sigma(n)
and

\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}d,

where
\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n):=0
for
n\leq 0
, have the same parity, Merca recently studied the relationship between
\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)
and the generalized
m
-gonal numbers.
More specifically, he investigated for which positive integers
m
the following congruences hold for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
:

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{m}(k))

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}n\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)&\text{if }n=P_{m}(j),\,j\in\mathbb{Z},\\
0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)&\text{otherwise},\\
\end{cases}

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{5}(k))

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}n\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum)&\text{if }n=P_{5}(j),\,j\in\mathbb{Z},\\
0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum)&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{P_{3}(-k)}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{5}(k))

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{cases}(-1)^{P_{3}(-j)}\cdot n\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum)&\text{if }n=P_{5}(j),\,j\in\mathbb{Z},\\
0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In particular, Merca posed the following conjectures:

Conjecture
.

The following are true:

(i)

The congruence (
2
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
if and only if
m\in\{5,6\}
.

(ii)

The congruence (
3
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
if and only if
m\in\{2,3,6\}
.

(iii)

The congruence (
4
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
if and only if
m\in\{2,4\}
.

Merca showed the if condition for each of these conjectures. Using his work, we obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Merca’s conjectures are true.

Proof.

We begin by proving (ii). Merca showed that (
3
) holds if
m\in\{2,3,6\}

[
3
, Theorem 3]
, hence it suffices to show that if
m\notin\{2,3,6\}
, then there exists some
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
n\neq P_{5}(j)
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and

(5)

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{5}(k))\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum).

Since
\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{5}(k))=0
whenever
n-P_{5}(k)\leq 0
, the sum in (
5
) is in fact finite, and we easily compute
\sum_{k}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(3-P_{5}(k))=6
, where
3\neq P_{5}(j)
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, (
5
) holds unless
6\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum)
. But this is the case only if
m\in\{2,3,6\}
.

Next, we prove (iii). Again, Merca proves that (
4
) holds if
m\in\{2,4\}

[
3
, Theorem 4]
, hence it suffices to show that if
m\notin\{2,4\}
, then there exists some
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
n\neq P_{5}(j)
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and

(6)

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{P_{3}(-k)}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{5}(k))\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum).

We compute
\sum_{k}(-1)^{P_{3}(-k)}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(3-P_{5}(k))=4
, where
3\neq P_{5}(j)
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and so (
6
) holds unless
4\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mum)
. But this is the case only if
m\in\{2,4\}
.

Finally, we prove (i). Since
\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)
is odd if and only if
n
is a square or a twice square (see
[
3
, p. 3]
), we have that

(7)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{2n^{2}}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2).

The
n
th coefficient of

\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(\ell)q^{\ell}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{P_{m}(k)}\Big)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(\ell)q^{\ell+P_{m}(k)}\Big)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{m}(k))\Big)q^{n}

is given by

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{m}(k))
.
On the other hand, the
n
th coefficient of

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q^{\ell^{2}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}q^{2\ell^{2}}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{P_{m}(k)}\Big)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\left(q^{\ell^{2}+P_{m}(k)}+q^{2\ell^{2}+P_{m}(k)}\right)

is given by

a_{m}(n)+b_{m}(n)
, where

\displaystyle a_{m}(n)=\absolutevalue{A_m(n)}

\displaystyle:=\#\{(\ell,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}:\,\ell^{2}+P_{m}(k)=n\},

\displaystyle b_{m}(n)=\absolutevalue{B_m(n)}

\displaystyle:=\#\{(\ell,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\times\mathbb{Z}:\,2\ell^{2}+P_{m}(k)=n\}.

Thus, due to (
7
) we must have

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(n-P_{m}(k))\equiv a_{m}(n)+b_{m}(n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2).

Suppose first that
m\geq 7
. Then we claim that
P_{m}(0)=0,P_{m}(1)=1
, and
P_{m}(k)>3
for all
k\notin\{0,1\}
.
From (
1
), it is clear that
P_{m}(0)=0
and
P_{m}(1)=1
. To see that
P_{m}(k)>3
for all
k\notin\{0,1\}
, note that
since the leading term of
P_{m}(x)
is positive and its vertex is at
0<\frac{m-4}{2m-4}<1
,
we have
P_{m}(k)\geq P_{m}(2)=m\geq 7
for
k\geq 2
and
P_{m}(k)\geq P_{m}(-1)=m-3\geq 4
for
k\leq-1
.

Now, let
n=3
. Then the above shows that
n
is not a generalized
m
-gonal number for
m\geq 7
, and so for (
2
) to hold, we must have
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(3-P_{m}(k))\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
. If
(\ell,k)\in A_{m}(3)
, then
\ell^{2}=3-P_{m}(k)
, so that in particular
\ell^{2}\leq 3
, which forces
\ell=1
. But then we must have
P_{m}(k)=2
, which we have seen to be impossible. Hence,
A_{m}(3)
is empty, and
a_{m}(3)\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
. On the other hand, if
(\ell,k)\in B_{m}(3)
, we must again have
\ell=1
. It follows that
P_{m}(k)=1
, which is the case if and only if
k=1
. Hence
B_{m}(3)=\{(1,1)\}
, and
b_{m}(3)\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
. We conclude that
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(3-P_{m}(k))\equiv a_{m}(3)+b_{m}(3)\equiv 1\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
.

Merca showed that (
2
) holds for
m\in\{5,6\}
, and for
m\in\{1,2\}
, the sum in (
2
) diverges, hence it remains to consider
m\in\{3,4\}
. Suppose first that
m=3
, and note that
P_{3}(k)=\frac{1}{2}(k^{2}+k)
. We have
3=P_{3}(-3)=P_{3}(2)
, so for (
2
) to hold, we must have
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(3-k)\equiv 3\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
. If
(\ell,k)\in A_{3}(3)
, then
\ell=1
and
P_{3}(k)=2
, which is impossible. Hence
A_{3}(3)
is empty. If
(\ell,k)\in B_{3}(3)
, then
\ell=1
and
P_{3}(k)=1
, which is the case if and only if
k\in\{-2,1\}
. Thus
B_{3}(3)=\{(1,-2),(1,1)\}
, and
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(3-k)\equiv a_{3}(3)+b_{3}(3)\equiv 0\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
.

Finally, suppose
m=4
, and note that
P_{4}(k)=k^{2}
. Since
4=P_{4}(2)
, for (
2
) to hold we must have
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(4-k)\equiv 4\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
. If
(\ell,k)\in A_{4}(4)
, then either
\ell=1
and
P_{4}(k)=3
, which is impossible, or
\ell=2
and
P_{4}(k)=0
, which is the case if and only if
k=0
. Thus,
A_{4}(4)=\{(2,0)\}
. On the other hand, if
(\ell,k)\in B_{4}(4)
, then
\ell=1
and
P_{3}(k)=2
, which is impossible. Thus
B_{4}(4)
is empty, and
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{odd}}(4-k)\equiv a_{4}(4)+b_{4}(4)\equiv 1\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu2)
.
∎

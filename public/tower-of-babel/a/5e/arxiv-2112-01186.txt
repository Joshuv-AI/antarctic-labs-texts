Effective intrinsic ergodicity for countable state Markov shifts Thanks: The authors were partially supported by ISF grant 1149/18 and BSF grant 2016105.
René Rühr and Omri Sarig

Abstract
. For strongly positively recurrent countable state Markov shifts, we bound the distance between an invariant measure and the measure of maximal entropy in terms of the difference of their entropies. This extends an earlier result for subshifts of finite type, due to Kadyrov. We provide a similar bound for equilibrium measures of strongly positively recurrent potentials, in terms of the pressure difference. For measures with nearly maximal entropy, we have new, and sharp, bounds. The strong positive recurrence condition is necessary.

Here we will focus on the connection between intrinsic ergodicity and equidistribution of measures with high entropy, in the case of topological Markov shifts.

Consider for example a topologically transitive subshift of finite type
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
. This system has a unique measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{0}

[

]

\mu\mapsto h_{\mu}(\sigma)
is upper semi-continuous in the weak star topology. Together with intrinsic ergodicity, this easily implies that for every sequence of shift invariant probability measures
\mu_{n}

\text{ if $h_{\mu_{n}}(\sigma)\to h_{\mu_{0}}(\sigma)$, then }\mu_{n}\to\mu_{0}\text{ weak star.}

Kadyrov gave a bound for the rate of convergence

]

such that for every shift invariant probability measure
\mu
and for every Hölder continuous function
\psi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
with Hölder exponent
\beta

\left|\int\psi d\mu-\int\psi d\mu_{0}\right|\leq C_{\beta}\|\psi\|_{\beta}\sqrt{h_{\mu_{0}}(\sigma)-h_{\mu}(\sigma)},

(1.2)

where

is the

Quantitative versions of (

They first appeared in the doctoral thesis of F. Polo

for the

[

In this paper we extend the EKP inequality to topologically transitive countable state Markov shifts. The new features here are non-compactness, the possibility of escape of mass to infinity, and “phase transitions”: non-analytic pressure functions.

Of course, the EKP inequality cannot be expected to hold for all countable Markov shifts.
For (
1.1

1.2
) to make sense, we must
at the very least assume that a measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{0}
exists, and that
h_{\mu_{0}}(\sigma)<\infty

A deeper observation, due to S. Ruette, is that even under these additional assumptions, (

It follows from

[

Thus the right context for studying effective intrinsic ergodicity for countable Markov shifts is the class of topologically transitive SPR shifts.

G. Iommi, M. Todd and A. Velozo have studied the semi-continuity properties of the entropy map for countable Markov shifts
[

Our main result is:
All SPR topologically transitive countable state Markov shifts satisfy the EKP inequality (

Our proof is different from Kadyrov’s, and it produces sharper bounds.
We show in Theorem

The measure of maximal entropy

Let us compare Kadyrov’s proof to our proof.

Assume

2.
A review of the theory of topological Markov shifts

2.1.
Topological Markov shifts

Let
\mathfs G
denote a countable directed graph with set of vertices
S
and set of directed edges
E
. If there is an edge from
a
to
b
, we write
a\to b
.
Set

\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G):=\{\underline{x}=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots):x_{i}\in S,x_{i}\to x_{i+1}\text{ for all }i\}.

For every
\underline{x}\neq\underline{y}
in
\Sigma^{+}
, let
t(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\min\{i:x_{i}\neq y_{i}\}
. We equip
\Sigma^{+}
with the metric

d(\underline{x},\underline{y}):=\begin{cases}0&\underline{x}=\underline{y}\\
\exp[-t(\underline{x},\underline{y})]&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Definition 2.1
.

The topological dynamical system
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
given by

\sigma(\underline{x})_{i}=x_{i+1}
is called the one-sided topological Markov shift (TMS) associated to
\mathfs G
. The elements of
S
are called states, and
\sigma
is called the left shift.

When
|S|=\aleph_{0}
, we will also call
\Sigma^{+}
a
countable state Markov shift
.

The sets

[\underline{a}]=[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}]:=\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma^{+}:x_{i}=a_{i}\ (i=0,\ldots,n-1)\}

(\underline{a}\in\bigcup_{n}S^{n})
are called
cylinders
of length
n
. Cylinders of length one are also called
partition sets
.
The cylinders form a basis for the topology, and they generate the Borel
\sigma
-algebra, which we denote by
\mathfs B
.

2.2.
Topological transitivity and topological mixing

We write
a\xrightarrow{n}b
when there is a non-empty cylinder of the form
[a,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1},b]
. In particular
a\xrightarrow{1}b\Leftrightarrow a\to b
. The following simple facts are well-known:

(1)

\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is topologically transitive if and only if
\forall a,b\in S

\exists n
such that
a\xrightarrow{n}b
. Equivalently,
\mathfs G
is
strongly connected
:
\forall(a,b)\in S^{2}
, there is a path from
a
to
b
.

(2)

If
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is topologically transitive, then

p_{a}:=\gcd\{n:a\xrightarrow{n}a\}

(a\in S)

are all equal to the same value
p\geq 1
, and
p
is called the
period
of
\Sigma^{+}
.

(3)

\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing if and only if it is topologically transitive, and its period is equal to one.

(4)

The spectral decomposition:
Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is a topologically transitive TMS with period
p>1
, then we can decompose

\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma_{0}^{+}\uplus\Sigma_{1}^{+}\uplus\cdots\uplus\Sigma_{p-1}^{+}

where
\sigma(\Sigma_{i}^{+})=\Sigma_{i+1\,\mathrm{mod}\,p}^{+}
, and where
\sigma^{p}:\Sigma_{i}^{+}\to\Sigma_{i}^{+}
are all topologically conjugate to a topologically
mixing
countable Markov shift.

Briefly, this is done as follows. There is an equivalence relation on the states of
\Sigma^{+}
given by
a\sim b\Leftrightarrow a=b\text{ or }a\xrightarrow{n}b
for some
n
divisible by
p
. There are
p
equivalence classes
S_{0},\ldots,S_{p-1}
, and
\Sigma_{i}^{+}:=\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma^{+}:x_{0}\in S_{i}\}
. The map
\sigma^{p}:\Sigma_{i}^{+}\to\Sigma_{i}^{+}
is topologically conjugate to the TMS with set of states
\{[a_{0},\ldots,a_{p-1}]:a_{0}\in S_{i}\}\setminus\{\varnothing\}
and edges
[\underline{a}]\to[\underline{b}]
when
a_{p-1}\to b_{0}
, and this TMS is topologically mixing.

The spectral decomposition is a tool for reducing statements on topologically transitive TMS to the topologically mixing case. We will use this tool frequently.

2.3.
Weak Hölder continuity and summable variations

The
n^{\mathrm{th}}
oscillation
(aka the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
variation
) of a function
\phi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is

\textrm{osc}_{n}(\phi):=\sup\{|\phi(\underline{x})-\phi(\underline{y})|:x_{i}=y_{i}\ (i=0,\ldots,n-1)\}.

A function
\phi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous
if
\theta\in(0,1)
and there exists
A>0
such that
\textrm{osc}_{n}(\phi)\leq A\theta^{n}
for all
n\geq 2
.
This condition does not imply that
\phi
is bounded, and the choice
n\geq 2
is done to include all functions of the form
\phi(\underline{x})=\phi(x_{0},x_{1})
. A bounded
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous is Hölder continuous with exponent
\beta:=-\log\theta
with respect to the metric (
2.1
). We define the space of such functions in (
2.8
).

Some of our results hold under the following weaker regularity assumption, called
summable variations
:
\sum_{n\geq 2}\textrm{osc}_{n}(\phi)<\infty
.

2.4.
Pressure and equilibrium measures

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing,
\phi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
has summable variations and let
\phi_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\phi\circ\sigma^{k}
. Given
a\in S
, let

P_{G}(\phi):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(\phi,a),\text{ where }Z_{n}(\phi,a):=\sum_{\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})=\underline{x}}e^{\phi_{n}(\underline{x})}1_{[a]}(\underline{x}).

The limit exists and is independent of
a
, see
[
Sar99
]
.
2
2

2

\phi

It is always bigger than
-\infty

Definition 2.2
.

P_{G}(\phi)
is called the Gurevich pressure of
\phi
. The Gurevich entropy of
\Sigma^{+}
is

\displaystyle h:=P_{G}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\#\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma^{+}:x_{0}=a\ ,\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})=\underline{x}\}
.

Let
\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
denote the collection of all
\sigma
-invariant Borel probability measures on
\Sigma^{+}
, and let
h_{\mu}(\sigma)
denote the metric entropy of
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
.

Gurevich’s variational principle

[
Gur69
]
says that if
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing, then

h:=P_{G}(0)=\sup\left\{h_{\mu}(\sigma):\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\right\}.

Any measure which achieves the supremum is called a
measure of maximal entropy
. Such measures do not always exist, but if they do, and if
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically transitive, then they are unique
[
Gur70
]
. We will describe the structure of the measure of maximal entropy in the following section.

The Gurevich pressure of a general
\phi
with summable variations satisfies a similar variational principle, which we now explain.

A measurable function
\phi
is called
one-sided
\mu
-integrable
if at least one of the integrals
\int_{[\phi>0]}\phi d\mu,\int_{[\phi<0]}\phi d\mu
is finite. Let

\mathfs M_{\phi}(\Sigma^{+}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+}):\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$\phi$ is one-sided $\mu$-integrable, and }\\
\text{$(h_{\mu}(\sigma),\int\phi d\mu)\neq(+\infty,-\infty)$}\end{array}\right\}.

This is the collection of
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
for which the expression

P_{\mu}(\phi):=h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi d\mu,

is well-defined (we allow
P_{\mu}(\phi)=\pm\infty
but forbid
P_{\mu}(\phi)=\infty-\infty
).

In this paper we will be mostly interested in the case when
\Sigma^{+}
has finite Gurevich entropy and
\sup\phi<\infty
. In this case
P_{\mu}(\phi)
is well-defined for all
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
, and
\mathfs M_{\phi}(\Sigma^{+})=\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
.

The
variational principle
for TMS states that if
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing and if
\phi
has summable variations, then

P_{G}(\phi)=\sup\left\{P_{\mu}(\phi):\mu\in\mathfs M_{\phi}(\Sigma^{+})\right\}.

(2.2)

See
[
Sar99
]
for the special case

,

[

A measure
\mu\in\mathfs M_{\phi}(\Sigma^{+})
which achieves the supremum in (

equilibrium measure for the potential

\phi
. The equilibrium measures for the constant potential are the measures of maximal entropy.

So far we have only discussed the topologically mixing case. In the topologically transitive case, with period
p
, we define

P_{G}(\phi):=(1/p)P_{G}(\phi_{p}|_{\Sigma_{0}^{+}}),

where
\phi_{p}:=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\phi\circ\sigma^{i}
, and
\Sigma_{0}^{+}
is some (any) of the components in the spectral decomposition of
\Sigma^{+}
.

With this definition, the variational principle holds, and
m_{0}
is an equilibrium measure for
\phi_{p}|_{\Sigma_{0}^{+}}
if and only if
m:=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}m_{0}\circ\sigma^{-i}
is an equilibrium measure for
\phi
. Furthermore, in this case
m_{0}:=m(\cdot|\Sigma_{0}^{+})
, the conditional measure on
\Sigma^{+}_{0}
. For more details, see the end of the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Existence and structure of equilibrium measures

The topic is intimately related to the eigenvector problem for
Ruelle’s operator

(L_{\phi}f)(\underline{x}):=\sum_{\sigma(\underline{y})=\underline{x}}e^{\phi(\underline{y})}f(\underline{y}).

Fix a state

Suppose

\mathbb{E}_{m_{\phi}}(f|\sigma^{-n}\mathfs B)=\lambda^{-n}(h_{\phi}^{-1}L_{\phi}^{n}(h_{\phi}f))\circ\sigma^{n}\ \ \ m_{\phi}\text{-a.e.}

In the finite alphabet case (when

Example 2 (BIP)

A topologically mixing Markov shift is said to have the

2.6.
Strong positive recurrence

This is a strengthening of the positive recurrence condition, which implies that
L_{\phi}
acts quasi-compactly on a “sufficiently rich” Banach space of functions (see the next section).

We begin with the special case when
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing, and
\phi\equiv 0
.
Fix a state
a
, and let
f_{n}(a)
denote the number of first return loops with length
n
, at
a
:

f_{n}(a):=\#\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma^{+}:\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})=\underline{x},\;x_{0}=a,\;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\neq a\}.

Let
F_{a}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{n}(a)z^{n}
be the associated generating function.

Definition 2.4
.

A topologically mixing TMS
\Sigma^{+}
with finite Gurevich entropy is called strongly positively recurrent (SPR), if for some state
a
,
F_{a}(R_{a})>1
where
R_{a}:=
radius of convergence of
F_{a}(z)
.

It follows from
[
VJ62
]
that (a) SPR implies that
\phi\equiv 0
is positively recurrent; (b) the SPR property is independent of
a
: if
F_{a}(R_{a})>1
for some
a
, then
F_{a}(R_{a})>1
for all
a
; and (c) SPR implies that Parry’s measure is an exponentially mixing Markov chain.

The SPR condition was extended to general potentials with summable variations in
[
Sar01a
]
(see
[
GS98
]
for the special case when
\textrm{osc}_{2}(\phi)=0
, i.e.
\phi
is
Markovian
).

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing, and
a
is a state. The
induced map on
[a]
is the map
\sigma^{\tau_{a}}:[a]^{\prime}\to[a]^{\prime}
, where
[a]^{\prime}:=\{\underline{x}\in[a]:x_{i}=a\text{ infinitely often}\}
, and

\tau_{a}(\underline{x}):=1_{[a]}(\underline{x})\min\{n>0:x_{n}=a\}.

This map has a coding as a full shift: Let
\overline{S}:=\{[a,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},a]:\xi_{i}\neq a\}\setminus\{\varnothing\}
,
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}:=\overline{S}^{{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}}
and define
\pi:\overline{\Sigma}^{+}\to[a]^{\prime}
by

\pi([a,\underline{\xi}_{1},a],[a,\underline{\xi}_{2},a],\ldots):=(a,\underline{\xi}_{1},a,\underline{\xi}_{2},a,\ldots).

Then
\pi^{-1}\circ\sigma^{\tau_{a}}\circ\pi
is the left shift on
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
.
Given a function
\phi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, we let

\overline{\phi}:=(\sum_{k=0}^{\tau_{a}-1}\phi\circ\sigma^{k})\circ\pi.

If
\phi
is weakly Hölder continuous on
\Sigma^{+}
, then
\overline{\phi}
is weakly Hölder continuous on
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose
\phi
is a weakly Hölder continuous potential with finite Gurevich pressure on a topologically mixing TMS, and let
a
be a state.
The discriminant of
\phi
at state
a
is the (possibly infinite) expression

\Delta_{a}[\phi]:=\sup\{P_{G}(\overline{\phi+p}):p\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }P_{G}(\overline{\phi+p})<\infty\}.

We say that
\phi
is strongly positively recurrent (SPR) if
\Delta_{a}[\phi]>0
for some
a
.

The definition extends to
\phi
with summable variations, but some care is needed since
\overline{\phi}
may not have summable variations in this case, see
[
Sar01a
, Lemma 2]
, which guarantees that
P_{G}(\overline{\phi})
is still well defined.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing,
\phi
has summable variations, and
P_{G}(\phi)<\infty
, then
\phi
is strongly positively recurrent if and only if for some state
a
,

P^{*}_{G}(\phi,a):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}^{\ast}(\phi,a)<\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(\phi,a)=P_{G}(\phi).

If this happens for some state, then it happens for all states.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the discriminant theorem of
[
Sar01a
]
.

Example

Consider the special case
\phi\equiv 0
. In this case
\overline{\phi+p}=\overline{p}=p\cdot\tau_{a}\circ\pi
. So for every
\overline{a}:=[a,\underline{\xi},a]\in\overline{S}

Z_{n}(\overline{\phi+p},\overline{a})=\sum_{[a,\underline{\xi}_{1},a],\ldots,[a,\underline{\xi}_{n-1},a]\in\overline{S}}\hskip-14.22636pte^{p(|a\underline{\xi}|+|a\underline{\xi}_{1}|+\cdots+|a\underline{\xi}_{n-1}|)}=e^{p|a\underline{\xi}|}\biggl(\sum_{[a\underline{\eta}a]\in\overline{S}}e^{p|a\underline{\eta}|}\bigg)^{n-1},

\Delta_{a}[0]=\log\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(a)R^{n}_{a}
, where

F_{a}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}f_{n}(a)z^{n}
. So
\phi\equiv 0
is SPR if and only if

.

It is shown in
[

]
that if
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing,
\phi
has summable variations, and

, then: (a) SPR implies positive recurrence; (b) The SPR property is independent of the choice of

\Delta_{a}[\phi]>0
for some

\Delta_{a}[\phi]>0
for all

.
Additionally, in
[

\phi

So far we have only discussed the topologically mixing case. If

is topologically transitive with period

2.7.
SPR and spectral gap

Let
\Sigma^{+}
be a topologically mixing TMS.
A
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous function
\phi
is said to have the
spectral gap property
if there exists a Banach space
(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}})
of functions
f:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
with the following properties:

(a)

(L_{\phi}f)(\underline{x})=\sum_{\sigma(\underline{y})=\underline{x}}e^{\phi(\underline{y})}f(\underline{y})
converges absolutely whenever
f\in\mathcal{L}
, and
\mathcal{L}
contains all the indicators of cylinder sets.

(b)

f\in\mathcal{L}\Rightarrow|f|\in\mathcal{L}
and
\bigl\||f|\bigr\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}}
.

(c)

\mathcal{L}
-convergence implies uniform convergence on cylinders.

(d)

L_{\phi}(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{L}
and
L_{\phi}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
is bounded.

(e)

L_{\phi}=\lambda P+N
where
\lambda=\exp P_{G}(\phi)
,
P,N
are bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{L}
such that
PN=NP=0
,
P^{2}=P
,
\dim\mathrm{Image}(P)=1
, and the spectral radius of
N
is strictly less than
\lambda
.

(f)

For every bounded
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous function
\psi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
L_{\phi+z\psi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{L}
, and
z\mapsto L_{\phi+z\psi}
is analytic on some complex neighborhood of zero.
(See
[
Kat95
, Chapter VII §1]
for the definition of analyticity for families of operators depending on a complex parameter.)

Property (e) says that the spectrum of
L_{\phi}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
consists of a simple eigenvalue
\lambda
and a compact subset of
\{z:|z|<\lambda\}
.
P
is the eigenprojection of
\lambda
. The “spectral gap” is the difference between
|\lambda|
and the spectral radius of
N
.

The paper
[
CS09
]
proves that
a weakly Hölder continuous function
\phi
with finite Gurevich pressure on a topologically mixing TMS has the spectral gap property, if and only if
\phi
is strongly positively recurrent.

The space
\mathcal{L}
constructed there has the following additional property
[
CS09
, p. 650]
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
be the space of
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions,

\mathcal{H}_{\beta}:=\left\{\psi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}:\|\psi\|_{\beta}:=\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\sup_{\underline{x}\neq\underline{y}}\frac{|\psi(\underline{x})-\psi(\underline{y})|}{e^{-\beta t(\underline{x},\underline{y})}}<\infty\right\}.

(2.8)

(g)

If
\theta=e^{-\beta}
, then for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
and
g\in\mathcal{L}
,
fg\in\mathcal{L}
and
\|fg\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq\|f\|_{\beta}\|g\|_{\mathcal{L}}
.

We caution the reader that the SPR property does
not
imply the spectral gap property for topologically transitive TMS with period
p>1
. We may still find a Banach space with (a)–(d) on which
L_{\phi}
acts quasi-compactly, but there will be
p
points in the spectrum with modulus
\exp P_{G}(\phi)
, and not just one as in (e).

3.
The pressure function

Throughout this section, we fix a topologically mixing one-sided countable Markov shift
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
with finite positive Gurevich entropy
h
,
and potentials
\phi,\psi
with summable variations such that
\sup\phi<\infty
and
\|\psi\|_{\infty}<\infty
. It follows that

\mathfs M_{\phi+t\psi}(\Sigma^{+})=\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\text{ for all }t.

Recall the definition of the Gurevich pressure from §
2.4
.

Definition 3.1
.

The pressure function of
\phi
in direction
\psi
is the function

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\ ,\ \mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t):=P_{G}(\phi+t\psi).

By the variational principle (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)

\displaystyle=\sup\{P_{\mu}(\phi)+t\int\psi d\mu:\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\},

where
P_{\mu}(\phi):=h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi d\mu.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma^{+}
be a topologically mixing TMS with finite Gurevich entropy. If
\phi,\psi
have summable variations,
\sup\phi<\infty
and
\|\psi\|_{\infty}<\infty
, then

(1)

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)
is finite, convex, and continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)
has well-defined finite one-sided derivatives

(D^{\pm}\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi})(t):=\lim_{h\to 0^{\pm}}\frac{1}{h}[\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t+h)-\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)].

(3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(D^{\pm}{\mathfrak{p}}_{\phi,\psi})(t)
exist, are equal, and finite. We call their common value
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(+\infty)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}(D^{\pm}{\mathfrak{p}}_{\phi,\psi})(t)
are equal and finite. We call their common value
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(-\infty)
.

(4)

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(+\infty)=\sup\{\int\psi d\mu:\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\}
,
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(-\infty)=\inf\{\int\psi d\mu:\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\}
.

(5)

If
\psi
is not cohomologous to a constant by a continuous transfer function, then
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{\phi,\psi}(-\infty)<{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{\phi,\psi}(+\infty)
.

For subshifts of finite type (or countable Markov shifts with the BIP property), these results are proved in
[
BL98
, §3]
,
[
Mor07
]
. In this case more is true:
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)
is real-analytic on
\mathbb{R}
, and the one-sided derivatives can be replaced by ordinary derivatives.
For general countable Markov shifts we do not necessarily have differentiability on
\mathbb{R}
, but the theorem is saved by standard convexity arguments:

Proof.

Fix some
\nu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
carried by a periodic orbit, then

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)\geq P_{\nu}(\phi+t\psi)>-\infty
, proving that
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)\neq-\infty
on
\mathbb{R}
. Next,

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)<\infty
, because for every
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
,

P_{\mu}(\phi+t\psi)=h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi d\mu+t\int\psi d\mu\leq h+\sup\phi+|t|\|\psi\|_{\infty}<\infty.
Passing to the supremum over
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
gives

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)\leq h+\sup\phi+|t|\|\psi\|_{\infty}<\infty
. So
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}
is finite on
\mathbb{R}
.

To see convexity, we use the identity
P_{G}(\phi+t\psi)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(\phi+t\psi,a)
.
By Hölder’s inequality, if
t=st_{1}+(1-s)t_{2}
with
s\in[0,1]
, then

Z_{n}(\phi+t\psi,a)\leq Z_{n}(\phi+t_{1}\psi,a)^{s}Z_{n}(\phi+t_{2}\psi,a)^{1-s}.

It follows that
t\mapsto\frac{1}{n}\log Z_{n}(\phi+t\psi,a)
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
. Pointwise limits of convex functions are convex, therefore
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
. We proved (1), (2).

To see (3) we use convexity to note that
t\mapsto(D^{\pm}\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi})(t)
is increasing (in the broad sense), and for all
t_{1}<t_{2}

(D^{-}\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi})(t_{1})\leq(D^{+}\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi})(t_{1})\leq(D^{-}\mathfrak{p}_{\psi,\phi})(t_{2})\leq(D^{+}\mathfrak{p}_{\psi,\phi})(t_{2}).

This implies the existence and equality of the limits which define
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(\pm\infty)
.
To see that these quantities are finite, we note that
P_{G}(\phi)-|t|\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)\leq P_{G}(\phi)+|t|\|\psi\|_{\infty}
,
so
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)
is a convex function with asymptotes of finite slope.

We prove (4). Let
p(t):=\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(t)
,
p^{\prime}_{\pm}:=D^{\pm}p
,
p^{\prime}(\pm\infty):=\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{\phi,\psi}(\pm\infty)
. By convexity, for every
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
and
t>0
,

p^{\prime}(\infty)\geq p^{\prime}_{+}(t)\geq\frac{p(t)-p(0)}{t}\geq\frac{(P_{\mu}(\phi)+t\int\psi d\mu)-p(0)}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\int\psi d\mu.

Thus
p^{\prime}(\infty)\geq\sup\{\int\psi d\mu:\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\}
.

Next, we fix
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small and
t>0
so large that

p^{\prime}(+\infty)\leq p^{\prime}_{-}(t)+\varepsilon.

This is possible by (3).
For every
0<\delta<\varepsilon
, we choose a
\sigma
-invariant
m:=m_{t,\delta}
such that
p(t+\delta)\leq P_{m}(\phi)+(t+\delta)\int\psi dm+\delta^{2}
. By the variational principle,
p(t)\geq P_{m}(\phi)+t\int\psi dm
, so

\displaystyle p^{\prime}(\infty)

\displaystyle\leq p^{\prime}_{-}(t)+\varepsilon\leq\frac{p(t+\delta)-p(t)}{\delta}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{[P_{m}(\phi)+(t+\delta)\int\psi dm+\delta^{2}]-[P_{m}(\phi)+t\int\psi dm]}{\delta}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\int\psi dm+\delta+\varepsilon\leq\sup\{\int\psi d\mu\}+2\varepsilon.

The
\sup
runs over all
\sigma
-invariant probabilities.
Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain
p^{\prime}(+\infty)\leq\sup\{\int\psi d\mu:\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})\}
.
This shows (4) for
p^{\prime}(+\infty)
.
The claim for
p^{\prime}(-\infty)
follows immediately using the identity
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,-\psi}(t)=\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}(-t)
.

To see (5), assume the contrary:
p^{\prime}(-\infty)=p^{\prime}(\infty)=c
.
Then (4) tells us that
\int\psi d\mu=c
for all
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
.
Applying this to invariant measures carried by a periodic orbit we find that

\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})=\underline{x}\Rightarrow\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi(\sigma^{k}\underline{x})=cn.

By the Livshits theorem, this implies that
\psi
is cohomologous to
c
via a continuous transfer function.
(The proof of the Livshits Theorem for subshifts of finite type given in
[
Bow75
, Theorem 1.28]
works verbatim in the countable alphabet case.)
∎

5.
The restricted pressure function

Suppose
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is a topologically mixing countable Markov shift with finite Gurevich entropy
h
, and
\phi
is a weakly Hölder continuous function such that
\sup\phi<\infty
. Fix
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
which is not cohomologous to a constant via a continuous transfer function. By Theorem
3.1

\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(-\infty)<\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(+\infty)
, and we can make the following definition:

Definition 5.1
.

The restricted pressure function of
\phi
, constrained on
\psi
,
is

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}:(\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(-\infty),\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}_{\phi,\psi}(+\infty))\to\mathbb{R}
, given by

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a):=\sup\{h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi d\mu:\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+}),\int\psi d\mu=a\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the assumptions above,

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}
is a well-defined finite valued concave function, and
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}
is bounded from above.

Proof.

Since
h<\infty
and
\sup\phi<\infty
,
\mathfs M_{\phi}(\Sigma^{+})=\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
and
h_{\mu}(\sigma)+\int\phi d\mu
is well-defined for every
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
.
By Theorem
3.1
,
a\in({\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{\phi,\psi}(-\infty),{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}_{\phi,\psi}(+\infty))
if and only if
\inf\{\int\psi d\mu\}<a<\sup\{\int\psi d\mu\}
,
therefore there exist two invariant measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
such that
\int\psi d\mu_{1}<a<\int\psi d\mu_{2}
. Then
\int\psi d\mu=a
for some convex combination
\mu
of
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
, and the set

\{\mu:\int\psi d\mu=a\}
is non-empty. So the supremum in the definition of
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)
is over a non-empty set, and
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)
is well-defined.

Let’s choose the measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
more carefully. A standard ergodic decomposition argument shows that we may choose
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
to be ergodic. On countable Markov shifts,
\mu_{i}
generic orbits are limits of periodic orbits, therefore we may choose
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
to be ergodic measures sitting on periodic orbits. For such measures
\int\phi d\mu_{i}>-\infty
, whence
\int\phi d\mu>-\infty
for every convex combination of
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
. Looking at the argument in the previous section, we find that

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)>-\infty.

This function is uniformly bounded above by
h+\sup\phi<\infty.

So
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}
is finite on its domain, and bounded from above.

Concavity is because for all
a_{1},a_{2}
in the domain, for all
0\leq t\leq 1
, and for all
\varepsilon>0
, if
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are invariant measures such that

P_{\mu_{i}}(\phi)\geq\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{i})-\varepsilon,\text{ and }\int\psi d\mu_{i}=a_{i},

then
\mu:=t\mu_{1}+(1-t)\mu_{2}
is an invariant measure such that
\int\psi d\mu=ta_{1}+(1-t)a_{2}
and hence by the affine properties of the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy,

P_{\mu}(\phi)=tP_{\mu_{1}}(\phi)+(1-t)P_{\mu_{2}}(\phi).

So
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(ta_{1}+(1-t)a_{2})\geq P_{\mu}(\phi)\geq t\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{1})+(1-t)\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{2})-\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, the concavity of
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}
follows.

.
We saw that
|t|\leq\frac{\varepsilon\sigma^{2}}{M^{2}}
.
By Theorem

,
\sigma\leq M
,

. By Theorem
3.2

\phi+t\psi

.
Also, by the choice of

a=p^{\prime}(t)=\int\psi dm_{t}
.
Thus

P_{m_{t}}(\phi)=P_{m_{t}}(\phi+t\psi)-\int t\psi dm_{t}=p(t)-\int t\psi dm_{t}=p(t)-tp^{\prime}(t)=p(t)-ta.

For all other
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
such that
\int\psi d\mu=a

So

, proving the claim.

Let
\delta:=\delta_{\theta}(\phi):=\frac{\varepsilon}{2M^{2}}
, and
I_{\psi}:=(a_{0}-\delta\sigma^{4},a_{0}+\delta\sigma^{4})

is uniformly bounded on

When we proved the claim, we mentioned in passing the following fact:

q(a)=p(t)-tp^{\prime}(t)\text{ for the unique $|t|\leq\varepsilon$ such that $p^{\prime}(t)=a$.}

(5.2)

(
5.2
) says that
q:I_{\psi}\to\mathbb{R}
is minus the Legendre transform of the restriction of
p
to
(p^{\prime})^{-1}(I_{\psi})
.
Indeed, we have the identity

.

By Theorem
3.2
,

It follows that

By (

q(p^{\prime}(t))=p(t)-tp^{\prime}(t)
. Repeated differentiation with respect to

q^{\prime}(p^{\prime}(t))=-t,\ q^{\prime\prime}(p^{\prime}(t))=-1/p^{\prime\prime}(t),\ q^{\prime\prime\prime}(p^{\prime}(t))=p^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)/p^{\prime\prime}(t)^{3}.

(5.3)

So

Part (4) also follows from (

,

and

.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
5.2
,
there are
\delta_{\theta}(\phi),H_{\theta}(\phi)>0
such that if
0<\delta\leq\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
,
then the following holds for every
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that

\|\psi\|_{\beta}\leq 1

\sigma_{m}(\psi)\neq 0

,
\sigma:=\sigma_{m}(\psi)
.

(1)

If

e^{-\delta}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\left(a-a_{0}\right)^{2}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{0})-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)\leq e^{\delta}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\left(a-a_{0}\right)^{2}.

(2)

If
|a-a_{0}|>\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)}{H_{\theta}(\phi)}\sigma^{4}
, then

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{0})-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)\geq\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{2}}{8H_{\theta}(\phi)}|a-a_{0}|.

Proof.

Let
\sigma:=\sigma_{m}(\psi),a_{0}:=\int\psi dm
,

q:={\mathfrak{q}}_{\phi,\psi}
,
p:={\mathfrak{p}}_{\phi,\psi}
.
Let
H:=H_{\theta}(\phi)
and
\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
be as in the previous lemma.
Without loss of generality
\delta_{\theta}(\phi)<\frac{1}{3}
and
H>1
(actually, the proof of Lemma
5.2
gives
\delta_{\theta}(\phi)=\frac{\varepsilon}{2M^{2}}<10^{-6}
and
H=8M>800
). Let’s write
A=B\pm C
if
A\in[B-|C|,B+|C|]
.

Suppose
|a-a_{0}|\leq\frac{\delta\sigma^{4}}{H}
where
0<\delta\leq\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
. Then

and we can use the properties listed in Lemma
5.2
.
Taylor’s expansion gives

\displaystyle q(a)=q(a_{0})+q^{\prime}(a_{0})(a-a_{0})+\frac{1}{2}q^{\prime\prime}(a_{0})(a-a_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{6}q^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)(a-a_{0})^{3}

for some

\displaystyle q(a)=q(a_{0})-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}(a-a_{0})^{2}\pm\frac{1}{6}\frac{H}{\sigma^{6}}|a-a_{0}|^{3}

\displaystyle=q(a_{0})-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}(a-a_{0})^{2}\biggl(1\pm\frac{1}{3}\frac{H}{\sigma^{4}}|a-a_{0}|\biggr)=q(a_{0})-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}(a-a_{0})^{2}(1\pm\tfrac{\delta}{3})

\displaystyle=q(a_{0})-e^{\pm\delta}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}(a-a_{0})^{2},

where the last bound is because

,
so
(1-\tfrac{\delta}{3},1+\tfrac{\delta}{3})\subset(e^{-\delta},e^{\delta})

The second part of the corollary is more delicate, because it deals with parts of the domain where we do not know that

is differentiable.

Suppose

with

, and let

,

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(a_{1})=q^{\prime}(a_{0})+q^{\prime\prime}(\xi)(a_{1}-a_{0})\text{ for some }\xi\in[a_{0},a_{1}]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}(a_{1}-a_{0})=-\frac{\delta\sigma^{2}}{4H}.

Although we cannot assume that

Rearranging terms, and recalling that

\displaystyle q(a)

\displaystyle\leq q(a_{1})-\frac{\delta\sigma^{2}}{4H}(a-a_{1})\leq q(a_{0})-\frac{\delta\sigma^{2}}{8H}(a-a_{0}),

where the last inequality uses the inequality

The case

6.
The EKP Inequality for measures with large pressure

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is a topologically transitive countable Markov shift with finite Gurevich entropy.
Let
\phi
be a
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous SPR potential such that
\sup\phi<\infty
.
Let
m
be the unique equilibrium measure of
\phi
. Suppose
e^{-\beta}\leq\theta
, and recall that
(\mathcal{H}_{\beta},\|\cdot\|_{\beta})
denotes the space of
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions of
\Sigma^{+}
, see (
2.8
).

Theorem 6.1
.

There exist
\varepsilon_{\theta}^{\ast}(\phi),C_{\theta}^{\ast}(\phi)>0
such that for every
0\not\equiv\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
,
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{\theta}^{\ast}(\phi)
,
and
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
, if

P_{\mu}(\phi)\geq P_{G}(\phi)-C_{\theta}^{\ast}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\sigma_{m}^{6}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{\beta}^{6}}

then

\left|\int\psi d\mu-\int\psi dm\right|\leq\sqrt{2}e^{\varepsilon}\sigma_{m}(\psi)\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)}.

The bound is sharp in the following sense:
For any
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that
\sigma_{m}(\psi)>0
there exists a sequence of ergodic measures
\nu_{n}\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
such that
P_{\nu_{n}}(\phi)\to P_{G}(\phi)
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\int\psi d\nu_{n}-\int\psi dm\right|}{\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\nu_{n}}(\phi)}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\sqrt{2}\sigma_{m}(\psi).

Remark.
Recall from §
2
that in the special case
\phi\equiv 0
,
m
is the measure of maximal entropy,
P_{G}(\phi)
is the entropy of
m
, and the condition that
\phi
is SPR is the same as the condition that
\Sigma^{+}
is SPR.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is topologically
mixing
.

If
\sigma_{m}(\psi)=0
, then
P_{\mu}(\phi)\geq P_{G}(\phi)-C_{\theta}^{\ast}(\phi)\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\sigma_{m}^{6}(\psi)}{\|\psi\|_{\beta}^{6}}
implies that
P_{\mu}(\phi)=P_{G}(\phi)
, whence by the uniqueness of the equilibrium measure
\mu=m
and the inequality is trivial. So it is enough to consider the case
\sigma_{m}(\psi)\neq 0
.
It is easy to verify that
\sigma_{m}(t\psi)=|t|\sigma_{m}(\psi)
.
This allows us to work with normalized functions
\psi/\|\psi\|_{\beta}
.
Henceforth we assume that
\|\psi\|_{\beta}=1
and
\sigma:=\sigma_{m}(\psi)\neq 0
.
Let
a_{0}:=\int\psi dm
. Let
H_{\theta}(\phi)
and
\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
denote the constants from Corollary
5.1
.
Let

H:=H_{\theta}(\phi)
,
C^{\ast}:=\frac{1}{9H^{2}}.

Fix some
0<\delta<\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
, and
suppose
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
satisfies
P_{\mu}(\phi)\geq P_{G}(\phi)-C^{\ast}\delta^{2}\sigma^{6}
.
Let
a:=\int\psi d\mu
.
By the definition of the restricted pressure,

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)\geq P_{\mu}(\phi).

We claim that
|a-a_{0}|\leq\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{4}}{H}
. Otherwise, by the assumption on
\mu
,

\displaystyle C^{\ast}\delta^{2}_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{6}\geq P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)\geq P_{G}(\phi)-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a),\text{ because }\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)\geq P_{\mu}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{0})-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a),\text{ because $\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{0})=P_{G}(\phi)$ by Lemma \ref{lemma-h}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{2}}{8H}|a-a_{0}|\text{ by the 2nd part of Corollary~\ref{c.h-double-ineq}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{2}}{8H}\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{4}}{H}=\frac{\delta^{2}_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{6}}{8H^{2}}\text{, by the assumption $|a-a_{0}|\geq\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{4}}{H}$.}

But this contradicts the definition of
C^{\ast}
.

So
|a-a_{0}|\leq\frac{\delta_{\theta}(\phi)\sigma^{4}}{H}
, and the first part of Corollary
5.1
gives us

(a-a_{0})^{2}\leq 2\sigma^{2}e^{\delta}(\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{0})-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)).

Taking the square root, and recalling that
a=\int\psi d\mu
,
a_{0}=\int\psi dm
,

\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{0})=P_{G}(\phi)
, and
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)\geq P_{\mu}(\phi)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int\psi d\mu-\int\psi dm\right|\leq e^{\delta}\sqrt{2}\sigma\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a)}\leq e^{\delta}\sqrt{2}\sigma\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)}.

This proves
the first part of the theorem with
\varepsilon^{\ast}_{\theta}(\phi):=\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
.

To see the second part, take
a_{n}\to a_{0}
. When we proved Lemma
5.2
, we saw that if
a_{n}
is sufficiently close to
a_{0}
, then
\exists!t_{n}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,\psi}^{\prime}(t_{n})=a_{n}
, the equilibrium measure
\nu_{n}:=m_{t_{n}}
of
\phi+t_{n}\psi
exists, and
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\psi}(a_{n})=P_{\nu_{n}}(\phi)
.

Repeating the previous argument with
\nu_{n}
replacing
\mu
, but now with the full force of Corollary
5.1
(1), we find that

\sqrt{2}\sigma e^{-\delta_{n}}\leq\frac{\left|\int\psi d\nu_{n}-\int\psi dm\right|}{\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\nu_{n}}(\phi)}}\leq\sqrt{2}\sigma e^{\delta_{n}},

where
\delta_{n}:=\frac{H}{\sigma^{4}}|a_{n}-a_{0}|\to 0
.

This completes the proof in the topologically mixing case. We will now outline the proof in the non-mixing topologically transitive case.
Suppose
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is topologically transitive, with period
p
, and let

\Sigma^{+}=\Sigma_{0}^{+}\uplus\cdots\uplus\Sigma_{p-1}^{+}

be the spectral decomposition from §
2.2
. The assumption that
\phi
is SPR on
\Sigma^{+}
means, by definition, that
\phi_{p}:=\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\phi\circ\sigma^{k}
is SPR with respect to the topologically mixing
\sigma^{p}:\Sigma_{i}^{+}\to\Sigma_{i}^{+}
for some (and then for all)
i=0,\ldots,p-1
.

\Sigma_{i}^{+}=\sigma^{i-j}(\Sigma_{j}^{+})
, therefore for every
\sigma
-invariant measure
\mu
,
\mu(\Sigma_{i}^{+})
are all equal (to
1/p
), and

\mu=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\mu_{i},\text{ where }\mu_{i}:=\mu(\cdot|\Sigma_{i}^{+})
(the conditional measure on
\Sigma_{i}^{+}
).
In addition:

(1)

h_{\mu_{i}}(\sigma^{p})=ph_{\mu}(\sigma)
: Firstly,
\sigma^{j+p-i}:(\Sigma_{i}^{+},\mu_{i})\to(\Sigma_{j}^{+},\mu_{j})
is a factor map for all
i,j
so
h_{\mu_{i}}(\sigma^{p})
are all equal. Secondly, by the affinity of the entropy map,
\frac{1}{p}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}h_{\mu_{i}}(\sigma^{p})=h_{\mu}(\sigma^{p})=ph_{\mu}(\sigma)
.

(2)

\int\phi_{p}d\mu_{i}=p\int\phi d\mu
. (More generally, for any integer multiple of
p
.)

(3)

By (1) and (2),
P_{\mu_{i}}(\phi_{p})=pP_{\mu}(\phi)
.

(4)

By definition,
P_{G}(\phi_{p}|_{\Sigma_{i}^{+}},\sigma^{p})=pP_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}},\sigma)
.

(5)

If
m
is an equilibrium measure of
\phi
, then
m_{i}
is an equilibrium measure of
\phi_{p}
.

(6)

By definition,
\sigma_{m_{i}}^{2}(\phi_{p})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Var_{m_{i}}(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\psi_{p}\circ\sigma^{jp})
.

(7)

\sigma_{m_{0}}(\psi_{p})=\sqrt{p}\sigma_{m}(\psi)
: By (2),
\mathbb{E}_{m_{i}}(\psi_{np})=np\mathbb{E}_{m}(\psi)=\mathbb{E}_{m}(\psi_{np})
, therefore

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{m}(\psi)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{np}\Var_{m}(\psi_{np})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{np^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\Var_{m_{i}}(\psi_{np})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{np^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\Var_{m_{0}}\bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\psi_{p})\circ\sigma^{jp}\circ\sigma^{i}\bigr)=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sigma_{m_{0}}^{2}(\psi_{p}\circ\sigma^{i})=\frac{1}{p}\sigma_{m_{0}}^{2}(\psi_{p}),

where the last equality is because
\psi_{p}\circ\sigma^{i}
is
\sigma^{p}
-cohomologous to
\psi_{p}
.

It is now an easy exercise to deduce the theorem for
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+},\psi,m,\mu
from the theorem for the topologically mixing
\sigma^{p}:\Sigma_{0}^{+}\to\Sigma_{0}^{+}
, with
\psi_{p},m_{0},\mu_{0}
.
∎

The previous result gives the “optimal” form of the EKP inequality for measures with high entropy. Note that the bound
\const\cdot\|\psi\|_{\beta}\sqrt{h-h_{\mu}(\sigma)}
in the original EKP inequality is replaced by

e^{\varepsilon}\sqrt{2}\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi)\sqrt{h-h_{\mu}(\sigma)},

where
\sigma_{\mu_{0}}^{2}(\psi)
is the asymptotic variance of
\psi
with respect to the measure of maximal entropy
\mu_{0}
.
This is better than (
1.2
), because
\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi)\leq M\|\psi\|_{\beta}
(Theorem
3.3
), and because of the following fact.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically transitive, with positive Gurevich entropy. For every
\beta>0
, there is a sequence of
\psi^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that
\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi^{(n)})\neq 0
,
\int\psi^{(n)}d\mu_{0}=0
, and

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi^{(n)})}{\|\psi^{(n)}\|_{\beta}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Proof.

There are two periodic points
\underline{x},\underline{y}
with the same (perhaps non-minimal) period
p
, and with disjoint orbits:
\sigma^{m}(\underline{x})\neq\sigma^{n}(\underline{y})
for all
m,n
.
Otherwise the Gurevich entropy equals zero.

Construct
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\psi(\sigma^{j}(\underline{x}))\neq\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\psi(\sigma^{j}(\underline{y}))
and
\int\psi d\mu_{0}=0
.
Then
\psi
cannot be cohomologous to a constant
(otherwise it would give all periodic points of fixed period the same weight).
By Theorem
3.3
,
\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi)\neq 0
.
In addition,
since
\psi
is clearly non-constant,

\psi\neq\psi\circ\sigma
,
whence
\|\psi-\psi\circ\sigma\|_{\beta}\neq 0
.

Now take
\psi^{(n)}:=\frac{1}{n}\psi+(\psi-\psi\circ\sigma)
.
On the one hand
\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi^{(n)})=\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi/n)=\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi)/n
, a sequence of positive numbers which converges to zero.
On the other hand
\|\psi^{(n)}\|_{\beta}\geq\|\psi-\psi\circ\sigma\|_{\beta}-\|\psi/n\|_{\beta}\to\|\psi-\psi\circ\sigma\|_{\beta}\neq 0
.
∎

We can now explain why we needed the constants
\varepsilon_{\theta}(\phi),M_{\theta}(\phi)
in Theorem
3.2
to be independent of
\psi
.
We do this in the case of main interest
\phi\equiv 0
, when
\mu_{0}
is the measure of maximal entropy. In this case Theorem
6.1
gives a nearly optimal EKP inequality in the regime

h_{\mu}(\sigma)\geq h-\varepsilon^{2}C^{\ast}_{\theta}(0)(\sigma_{\mu_{0}}(\psi)/\|\psi\|_{\beta})^{6}.

In the absence of the uniformity in
\psi
in Theorem
3.2
the best we could have hoped for was to prove this bound in the regime

h_{\mu}(\sigma)\geq h-\varepsilon^{2}C^{\ast}_{\theta}(\psi),

but without further information on the structure of
C^{\ast}_{\theta}(\psi)
.

7.
The EKP inequality for arbitrary measures

Our next result (which reduces in the case of subshifts of finite type and
\phi\equiv 0
to a result of S. Kadyrov
[
Kad15
]
) is an inequality for all
\sigma
-invariant measures, also those with low entropy or pressure.

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is a topologically mixing countable Markov shift with finite and positive Gurevich entropy. Let
\phi
be a
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous SPR potential such that
\sup\phi<\infty
. Let
m
denote the unique equilibrium measure of
\phi
. Fix
\beta
such that
e^{-\beta}\leq\theta
, and let
(\mathcal{H}_{\beta},\|\cdot\|_{\beta})
denote the space of
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions on
\Sigma^{+}
, see (
2.8
).

Lemma 7.1
.

There exist constants
K_{\beta}
,
Q(\phi)>0
such that for every
\sigma
-invariant probability
\mu
there exists some function
A\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that
\int Adm=\int Ad\mu=0
,
\|A\|_{\beta}\leq K_{\beta}
, and
\sigma_{m}(A)>Q(\phi)
.

Proof.

As in Lemma
6.1
, we can find
p\geq 1
and four periodic points
\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z},\underline{w}\in\Sigma^{+}
with period
p
, such that

x_{0}=y_{0}=z_{0}=w_{0}

and so that the orbits of
\underline{x}
,
\underline{y}
,
\underline{z}
,
\underline{w}
are disjoint.
Let
\underline{x}^{\text{p}}:=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{p-1},x_{0})
,

\underline{y}^{\text{p}}:=(y_{0},\ldots,y_{p-1},y_{0})
,

\underline{z}^{\text{p}}:=(z_{0},\ldots,z_{p-1},z_{0})
,

\underline{w}^{\text{p}}:=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{p-1},w_{0})
.
Define

a(\cdot):=1_{[\underline{x}^{\text{p}}]}(\cdot)-m([\underline{x}^{\text{p}}]),\ b(\cdot):=1_{[\underline{z}^{\text{p}}]}(\cdot)-m([\underline{z}^{\text{p}}]).

Recall the notation
\varphi_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi\circ\sigma^{k}
.
Since
\{\sigma^{k}\underline{x}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
\{\sigma^{k}\underline{y}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
\{\sigma^{k}\underline{z}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
\{\sigma^{k}\underline{w}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are disjoint,
the orbit of
\underline{y}
does not enter
[\underline{x}^{\text{p}}]
,
and the orbit of
\underline{w}
does not enter
[\underline{z}^{\text{p}}]
. In particular,
a_{p}(\underline{x})\neq a_{p}(\underline{y})
and
b_{p}(\underline{z})\neq b_{p}(\underline{w})
.

This implies that
a,b
are not cohomologous to constants
(otherwise
a_{p}(\underline{x})=a_{p}(\underline{y})=\const p
for any pair of
p
-periodic orbits).
So
\sigma_{m}^{2}(a),\sigma_{m}^{2}(b)\neq 0
.

By construction,
\int adm=\int bdm=0
.
If
\int ad\mu=0
take
A:=a
.
If
\int bd\mu=0
, take
A:=b
.
Notice that
A
is independent of
\mu
, therefore

\|A\|_{\beta}
,
\sigma_{m}^{2}(A)
are independent of
\mu
,
and the lemma follows.

In the remaining case,

\int ad\mu\neq 0
and
\int bd\mu\neq 0
,
and we define

A:=\frac{a\int bd\mu-b\int ad\mu}{\sqrt{(\int ad\mu)^{2}+(\int bd\mu)^{2}}}.

Clearly
\int Ad\mu=\int Adm=0
, and

\displaystyle\|A\|_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\int ad\mu|+|\int bd\mu|}{\sqrt{(\int ad\mu)^{2}+(\int bd\mu)^{2}}}\max\{\|a\|_{\beta},\|b\|_{\beta}\}\leq 2\max\{\|a\|_{\beta},\|b\|_{\beta}\}=:K_{\beta}.

K_{\beta}
is independent of
\mu
,
and only depends on
\beta
and
\Sigma^{+}
.
To complete the proof,

\sigma_{m}^{2}(A)
from below by a constant
which is independent of
\mu
.

We need for this purpose the function
Q(s,t):=\sigma_{m}^{2}(sa+tb)
.
By Theorem
3.2
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}^{2}(sa+tb)=\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\biggr|_{\tau=0}\mathfrak{p}_{\phi,sa+tb}(\tau)\equiv\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\biggr|_{\tau=0}P_{G}(\phi+\tau sa+\tau tb)

\displaystyle\overset{!}{=}s^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial u^{2}}(0,0)+2st\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial u\partial v}(0,0)+t^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}P}{\partial v^{2}}(0,0),\text{ where }P(u,v):=P_{G}(\phi+ua+vb).

To justify
\overset{!}{=}
, we use Theorem
3.2
(6).
We see that
Q(s,t)
is a quadratic form.

By the definition of the asymptotic variance,
Q(s,t)\geq 0
for all
(s,t)
.
We claim that

is positive definite.

Suppose

, then

\sigma_{m}^{2}(sa+tb)=0
, whence
sa+tb
is cohomologous to a constant. In this case, by Livshits theorem,
sa+tb
gives the same weight to all periodic orbits with the same period. In particular

\displaystyle sa_{p}(\underline{x})+tb_{p}(\underline{x})

\displaystyle=sa_{p}(\underline{y})+tb_{p}(\underline{y})

\displaystyle sa_{p}(\underline{z})+tb_{p}(\underline{z})

\displaystyle=sa_{p}(\underline{w})+tb_{p}(\underline{w}).

Recall that the orbits of
\underline{x}
,
\underline{y}
,
\underline{z}
,
\underline{w}
are disjoint,
so the orbits of
\underline{y}
,
\underline{z}
,
\underline{w}
do not enter

[\underline{x}^{\text{p}}]

whence
a_{p}(\underline{y})=a_{p}(\underline{z})=a_{p}(\underline{w})=-pm([\underline{x}^{\text{p}}])

and the orbits of
\underline{x}
,
\underline{y}
,
\underline{w}

do not enter

On the other hand
a_{p}(\underline{x})=n_{\underline{x}}-pm([\underline{x}^{\text{p}}]),b_{p}(\underline{z})=n_{\underline{z}}-pm([\underline{z}^{\text{p}}])

with

Substituting this above, we obtain
n_{\underline{x}}s=0,n_{\underline{z}}t=0

whence
s=t=0
.
So
Q(s,t)=0\Rightarrow(s,t)=(0,0)
,
and
Q
is positive definite.

Since

is a positive definite quadratic form, there exists

such that

for all
(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}^{2}(A)

\displaystyle=Q\left(\frac{\int bd\mu}{\sqrt{(\int ad\mu)^{2}+(\int bd\mu)^{2}}},\frac{-\int ad\mu}{\sqrt{(\int ad\mu)^{2}+(\int bd\mu)^{2}}}\right)\geq Q_{0}^{2}.

Notice that
Q_{0}
depends only on
a,b
and
\phi
,
and is therefore independent of
\mu
. We let

.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Given
\beta>0

\exists K_{\beta}^{\prime}(\phi)>0
as follows: For every
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
and
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
,
\exists\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that
\int\varphi dm=\int\psi dm
,
\int\varphi d\mu=\int\psi d\mu
,
\|\varphi\|_{\beta}\leq K_{\beta}^{\prime}(\phi)\|\psi\|_{\beta}
and

\frac{\|\varphi\|_{\beta}}{\sigma_{m}(\varphi)}\leq K_{\beta}^{\prime}(\phi).

(7.1)

Proof.

Let
Q:=Q(\phi)
,
K_{\beta}
and
A(\cdot)
be as in the previous lemma.

If
\sigma_{m}(\psi)\geq\frac{1}{3}Q\|\psi\|_{\beta}
,
we take
\varphi:=\psi
, and note that

\frac{\|\varphi\|_{\beta}}{\sigma_{m}(\varphi)}\leq\frac{3}{Q}.

If
\sigma_{m}(\psi)<\frac{1}{3}Q\|\psi\|_{\beta}
,
we take
\varphi:=\psi+\|\psi\|_{\beta}A
.
Then

\int\varphi dm=\int\psi dm
,
\int\varphi d\mu=\int\psi d\mu
,
and
\|\varphi\|_{\beta}\leq(1+\|A\|_{\beta})\|\psi\|_{\beta}\leq(1+K_{\beta})\|\psi\|_{\beta}
.
In addition,

\displaystyle\sigma_{m}^{2}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Var(\varphi_{n}),\text{ where }\varphi_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi\circ\sigma^{k}\text{, }\Var(B):=\int(B-{\textstyle\int}Bdm)^{2}dm

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Var(\psi_{n})+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Var(\|\psi\|_{\beta}A_{n})+2\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Cov}(\psi_{n},\|\psi\|_{\beta}A_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\text{ where }\mathrm{Cov}(B,C):=\int(B-{\textstyle\int}Bdm)(C-{\textstyle\int}Cdm)dm

\displaystyle\geq 0+\|\psi\|_{\beta}^{2}\sigma_{m}^{2}(A)-2\|\psi\|_{\beta}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\Var(\psi_{n})\Var(A_{n})}\text{\ \ \ (Cauchy-Schwarz)}

\displaystyle=\|\psi\|_{\beta}^{2}\sigma_{m}^{2}(A)-2\|\psi\|_{\beta}\sigma_{m}(\psi)\sigma_{m}(A)\ \

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{3}Q^{2}\|\psi\|_{\beta}^{2}\ \ \ (\because\sigma_{m}(A)\geq Q,\ \sigma_{m}(\psi)<\tfrac{1}{3}Q\|\psi\|_{\beta}),

so
\sigma_{m}(\varphi)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}Q\|\psi\|_{\beta}
.

We saw above that

\|\varphi\|_{\beta}\leq(1+K_{\beta})\|\psi\|_{\beta}
. It follows that

\frac{\|\varphi\|_{\beta}}{\sigma_{m}(\varphi)}\leq\sqrt{3}\frac{1+K_{\beta}}{Q}
.
The lemma follows with

.
∎

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is a topologically transitive countable Markov shift with finite Gurevich entropy.
Let
\phi
be a
\theta
-weakly Hölder continuous SPR potential such that
\sup\phi<\infty
, and let
m
be the unique equilibrium measure of
\phi
. If
e^{-\beta}\leq\theta
, then
\exists C_{\theta,\beta}(\phi)>0
such that for every
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
, and every
\mu\in\mathfs M(\Sigma^{+})
,

\left|\int\psi d\mu-\int\psi dm\right|\leq C_{\theta,\beta}(\phi)\|\psi\|_{\beta}\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)}.

Remark.
In the special case
\phi\equiv 0
,
m
is the measure of maximal entropy,
P_{G}(\phi)
is the entropy of
m
, and the inequality becomes (
1.2
).

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, it is sufficient to prove the theorem in the topologically mixing case.

Fix a
\sigma
-invariant measure
\mu
and some
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
.
If
\int\psi d\mu=\int\psi dm=0
then there is nothing to prove so suppose one of these integrals is non-zero.
Let
K:=K_{\beta}^{\prime}(\phi)
be a constant independent of
\mu,\psi
as in the previous lemma, and choose
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that
\|\varphi\|_{\beta}\leq K\|\psi\|_{\beta}
,
\int\psi d\mu=\int\varphi d\mu
,
\int\psi dm=\int\varphi dm
, and

\frac{\|\varphi\|_{\beta}}{\sigma_{m}(\varphi)}\leq K.

Notice that
\|\varphi\|_{\beta}\neq 0
, because at least one of the integrals
\int\varphi d\mu,\int\varphi dm
is non-zero.
So we can normalize
\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}:=\frac{\varphi}{\|\varphi\|_{\beta}}.

Let
\delta:=\delta_{\theta}(\phi)
,
H:=H_{\theta}(\phi)
be the constants in Corollary
5.1
,
and let
M:=M_{\theta}(\phi)
be the constant from Theorem
3.3
. Without loss of generality,
e^{\delta}<\sqrt{2}
.
Set
a:=\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu
,
a_{0}=\int\overline{\varphi}dm
,

\sigma:=\sigma_{m}(\overline{\varphi})
.
If
|a-a_{0}|\leq\frac{\delta\sigma^{4}}{H}
, then

\left|\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu-\int\overline{\varphi}dm\right|\leq e^{\delta}\sqrt{2}\sigma\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\overline{\varphi}}(a_{0})-\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\overline{\varphi}}(a)}\overset{!}{\leq}2M\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)},

where
\overset{!}{\leq}
is because
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\overline{\varphi}}(a_{0})=P_{G}(\phi)
,
\mathfrak{q}_{\phi,\overline{\varphi}}(a)\geq P_{\mu}(\phi)
, and

\sigma_{m}(\overline{\varphi})\leq M\|\overline{\varphi}\|_{\beta}=M
.

Similarly, if
|a-a_{0}|>\frac{\delta\sigma^{4}}{H}
,
then by the 2nd part of Corollary
5.1

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu-\int\overline{\varphi}dm\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{8H}{\delta\sigma^{2}}(P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi))=\frac{4H}{\delta\sigma_{m}^{2}(\overline{\varphi})}(P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi))

\displaystyle=\frac{4H\|\varphi\|_{\beta}^{2}}{\delta\sigma_{m}^{2}(\varphi)}(P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi))\leq\frac{4HK^{2}}{\delta}(P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)).

Since
\|\overline{\varphi}\|_{\beta}=1
,

\frac{1}{2}|\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu-\int\overline{\varphi}dm|\leq 1
,
and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu-\int\overline{\varphi}dm\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left|\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu-\int\overline{\varphi}dm\right|}\leq\sqrt{\frac{4HK^{2}}{\delta}(P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi))},

\left|\int\overline{\varphi}d\mu-\int\overline{\varphi}dm\right|\leq(4K\sqrt{H/\delta})\sqrt{P_{G}(\phi)-P_{\mu}(\phi)}

\beta,\theta

, and

(7.2)

By the choice of

8.
SPR is a necessary condition for the EKP inequality

Let
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
be a topologically transitive TMS associated to a countable directed graph
\mathfs G
, and suppose
\phi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function with summable variations and finite Gurevich pressure.

In this section we prove that if
\mu_{\phi}
satisfies the EKP inequality,
then
\phi
must be strongly positively recurrent. Ruette’s work implies this for
\phi\equiv 0

[
Rue03
]
, and we will extend her argument to general potentials. As in Ruette’s work, the engine of the proof is the following result, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 8.1
.

Suppose
\Sigma^{+}
is topologically mixing. If
\phi
is not SPR, then
\mathfs G
contains a subgraph
\mathfs G^{\prime}
such that
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})\subsetneq\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G)
,
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})\to\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})
is topologically mixing,
\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})}
is not SPR, and
P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})})=P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}})
.

Proof.

The case
\phi\equiv 0
is done in
[
Rue03
]
.

If
\phi
is not SPR, then
\mathfs G
must have infinitely many vertices, otherwise all bounded Hölder continuous potentials are SPR, see
[
Sar01a
]
.

There is no loss of generality in assuming that
\mathfs G
has at most one edge
a\to b
for every ordered pair
(a,b)\in S\times S
, and that every vertex
a
has an outgoing edge
a\to b
in
E
. Otherwise, pass to the graph
\mathfs G^{\ast}
with set of vertices
S^{\ast}:=\{a\in S:[a]\neq\varnothing\}
and set of edges
\{(a,b)\in E:[a,b]\neq\varnothing\}
, then
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\ast})=\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G)
and
\mathfs G^{\ast}
has the required properties.

It follows that if
\mathfs G^{\prime}
is a proper subgraph of
\mathfs G
, then
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})\subsetneq\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G)
.

By our standing assumptions,
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G)
is topologically mixing. Therefore
there exist two closed loops, starting and ending at the same vertex, with co-prime lengths.
The union of these loops defines a finite subgraph
\mathfs G_{core}
of
\mathfs G
, and every strongly connected graph which contains
\mathfs G_{core}
defines a topologically mixing TMS.

We will obtain the graph
\mathfs G^{\prime}
from
\mathfs G
, by removing some edge
a\to b
outside
\mathfs G_{\text{core}}
. We use the following
edge removal procedure
from
[
Rue03
]
.

Let
S_{\text{core}}
denote the set of vertices of
\mathfs G_{\text{core}}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the collection of finite paths
\gamma
with the following properties:

(1)

the first vertex and the last vertex of
\gamma
belong to
S_{\text{core}}
;

(2)

all other vertices are outside
S_{\text{core}}
;

(3)

for every vertex
x_{i}\in S\setminus S_{\text{core}}
of
\gamma=(x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1})
either
(x_{0},\dots,x_{i})
or
(x_{i},\dots,x_{n-1})
is a
\mathfs G
–geodesic.

Since
\sigma
is topologically transitive,
\mathfs G
is strongly connected. Every vertex in
S\setminus S_{\text{core}}
can be connected by forward and backward
\mathfs G
–geodesics to
S_{\text{core}}
. Their union is a path in
\mathcal{E}
. So every vertex in
S_{\text{core}}^{c}
belongs to some path in
\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathcal{E}
is infinite.

All paths in
\mathcal{E}
begin at
S_{\text{core}}
. Since
S_{\text{core}}
is finite and
\mathcal{E}
is infinite, there exist two different paths
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{E}
which begin at the same vertex in
S_{\text{core}}
and end at the same vertex in
S_{\text{core}}
. Let
\gamma
denote the maximal common prefix of
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}
, and let
a
denote the last vertex in
\gamma
.

By construction,
a
has at least two different outgoing edges
a\to b_{i}

(i=0,1)
such that
b_{i}\not\in S_{\text{core}}
.
Divide the outgoing edges from
a
to
S_{core}^{c}
into two arbitrary subsets,

E_{0}
and
E_{1}
, such that
(a,b_{i})\in E_{i}
.

Let
\mathfs G_{i}
be the graph obtained by removing the edges
E_{1-i}
from
\mathfs G
and restricting to the strongly connected component containing
a
.
It is not difficult to see that
\mathfs G_{i}
contain
\mathfs G_{\text{core}}
, so
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})\to\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})
is topologically mixing for
i=1,2
. By the assumption on
\mathfs G
,
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})\subsetneq\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G)
.

If
x_{0}=a,x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n}=a
is a first return loop to
a
, then
x_{i}\neq a
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, therefore edges from
E_{i}
can only appear as the first edge
(x_{0},x_{1})
.

We let
E_{\text{core}}=E_{\text{core}}(a)
be the edges
(a,b)\in\mathfs G
such that
a,b\in S_{core}
(in particular,
E_{\text{core}}=\varnothing
whenever
a\not\in S_{core}
).

Z^{*}_{n}(\phi,a)
splits into the sums,

Z^{*}_{n}(\phi,a)=Z_{n}^{*}(E_{0})+Z_{n}^{*}(E_{1})+Z_{n}^{*}(E_{\text{core}})

(8.1)

where

Z_{n}^{*}(E_{i}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})=\underline{x}\\
(x_{0},x_{1})\in E_{i}\end{subarray}}e^{\phi_{n}(\underline{x})}1_{[\tau_{a}=n]}(\underline{x})\ \ \ (\text{$\tau_{a}$ is defined in Section~\ref{s.eq}})

and

Z_{n}^{*}(E_{\text{core}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{n}(\underline{x})=\underline{x}\\
(x_{0},x_{1})\in E_{\text{core}}\end{subarray}}e^{\phi_{n}(\underline{x})}1_{[\tau_{a}=n]}(\underline{x}).

We claim that

Z_{n}^{*}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})},a)=Z_{n}^{*}(E_{i})+Z_{n}^{*}(E_{\text{core}}).

(8.2)

The non-trivial inequality is (
\geq
). The first return loops at
a
which begin with an edge in
E_{i}
or
E_{\text{core}}
do not contain other edges in
E_{1-i}
, because
a
cannot appear in the middle of a first return loop at
a
. Therefore all such loops must be contained in the irreducible component of
a
in
\mathfs G\setminus E_{1-i}
, whence in
\mathfs G_{i}
. So the loops participating in the sums in the right-hand-side must also appear in the sum on the left-hand-side. As the sums on the right are over disjoint sets,
(\geq)
follows.

Recall that
P^{*}_{G}(\phi,a)=\limsup\frac{1}{n}\log Z^{*}_{n}(\phi,a)
. By (
8.1
) and (
8.2
),

\displaystyle P^{*}_{G}(\phi,a)\leq\limsup\frac{1}{n}\log\bigl(2\max_{i}(Z_{n}^{*}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})},a)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\limsup\frac{1}{n}\log\bigl(\max_{i}(Z_{n}^{*}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})},a)\bigr)\leq\max_{i}P^{*}_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i})},a)\leq P^{*}_{G}(\phi,a).

So
P^{*}_{G}(\phi,a)=P^{*}_{G}(\phi|_{\mathfs G_{i}},a)
for at least one of the two indices
i=0,1
.
Call this index
i_{0}
. Then

P_{G}(\phi)\overset{(1)}{=}P_{G}^{\ast}(\phi,a)=P_{G}^{\ast}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})},a)\overset{(2)}{\leq}P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})})\overset{(3)}{\leq}P_{G}(\phi)
:

\overset{(1)}{=}
follows from Lemma
2.1
and the assumption that
\phi
is not SPR; and

\overset{(2)}{\leq}
and
\overset{(3)}{\leq}
are due to the trivial inequalities
Z_{n}^{\ast}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})},a)\leq Z_{n}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})},a)\leq Z_{n}(\phi,a)
.
So
P_{G}(\phi)=P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})})
as required.

The previous argument also shows that

P_{G}^{\ast}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})},a)=P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})})
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G_{i_{0}})}
is not SPR.
∎

Corollary 8.1
.

Suppose
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}
is a topologically transitive TMS, and
\phi:\Sigma^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a potential with summable variations, finite Gurevich pressure, and an equilibrium measure
\mu_{\phi}
.

(1)

Assume that for every sequence of invariant probability measures
\mu_{n}
,
if
P_{\mu_{n}}(\phi)\to P_{G}(\phi)
, then
\mu_{n}[\underline{a}]\to\mu_{\phi}[\underline{a}]
for all cylinders
[\underline{a}]
.
Then
\phi
must be SPR.

(2)

If
\phi
satisfies the EKP inequality (
1.4
) for some
\beta>0
and all
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
, then
\phi
must be SPR.

Proof.

Clearly, (1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Therefore, it is enough to prove (1).

It is enough to consider the topologically mixing case, because if
\Sigma^{+}
has period
p
, and
\Sigma^{+}=\biguplus_{i=0}^{p-1}\Sigma^{+}_{i}
is the spectral decomposition from §
2.2
, then
\sigma
satisfies

P_{\mu_{n}}(\phi)\to P_{G}(\phi)\Rightarrow\mu_{n}[\underline{a}]\to\mu_{\phi}[\underline{a}]

(8.3)

if and only if the topologically
mixing

\sigma^{p}:\Sigma_{0}^{+}\to\Sigma_{0}^{+}
satisfies (
8.3
). See the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
\mathfs G
denote the graph associated to
\Sigma^{+}
. Assume without loss of generality that
\mathfs G
has at most one edge
a\to b
for every ordered pair of vertices
(a,b)
.

By assumption,
\phi
has an equilibrium measure, whence by
[
BS03
]
,
\phi
is positively recurrent. Suppose by way of contradiction that
\phi
is not SPR. By the previous theorem, there is a proper subgraph
\mathfs G^{\prime}\subset\mathfs G
such that
\sigma:\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})\to\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})
is topologically mixing, and

P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})})=P_{G}(\phi).

By the variational principle, there are invariant probability measures
\mu_{n}
on
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})
such that

P_{\mu_{n}}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})})
.
Since
P_{\mu_{n}}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})})=P_{\mu_{n}}(\phi)
and
P_{G}(\phi|_{\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})})=P_{G}(\phi)
,

P_{\mu_{n}}(\phi)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}P_{G}(\phi)
.

By (
8.3
),

|\mu_{n}[\underline{a}]-\mu_{\phi}[\underline{a}]|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0

for all cylinders
[\underline{a}]
in
\Sigma^{+}
.
But this is false for any cylinder of the form

where

is an edge which appears in
\mathfs G
but not in
\mathfs G^{\prime}
: For this cylinder

for all

, because
\mu_{n}
are supported in
\Sigma^{+}(\mathfs G^{\prime})
. But
\mu_{\phi}[a,b]\neq 0
because equilibrium measures of potentials with summable variations on topologically mixing TMS always have full support, see
[
BS03
]
.
∎

The following generalizes a result of Salama

.
The case of Markovian potentials (

9.
Two sided topological Markov shifts

Suppose
\mathfs G
is a countable directed graph.
The
two-sided
topological Markov shift associated to
\mathfs G
is the dynamical system with the space

\Sigma=\Sigma(\mathfs G):=\{\underline{x}=(\ldots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\ldots):x_{i}\in S,x_{i}\to x_{i+1}\text{ for all }i\},

the action
\sigma(\underline{x})_{i}=x_{i+1}
, and the metric
d(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\exp(-\inf\{|i|:x_{i}\neq y_{i}\})
.

The definitions we gave in §
2
for one-sided shifts of the Gurevich pressure, Gurevich entropy, the spectral decomposition and SPR topological Markov shifts extend verbatim to the two-sided case, after replacing all the
\Sigma^{+}
by
\Sigma
.

The definition of the SPR property for potentials has a similar extension to the two-sided case, except that now to define the discriminant, we need to work with the induced shift on
\overline{\Sigma}:=\overline{S}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, instead of
\overline{\Sigma}^{+}=\overline{S}^{\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
.

With these definitions in place, Theorems
6.1
and
7.1
on the EKP inequality for SPR potentials extend to the two-sided setup without much difficulty.
We explain why.

Any weakly Hölder continuous
\phi:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is cohomologous via a bounded continuous transfer function to a “one-sided” function of the form
\phi^{+}\circ\pi
, where
\phi^{+}
is a weakly Hölder continuous function on
\Sigma^{+}
, and
\pi:\Sigma\to\Sigma^{+}
is the natural projection,
\pi(\underline{x})=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)
,

[
Sin72
]
,
[
Bow75
]
,
[
Dao13
]
.

The pressure function is defined in terms of sums over periodic orbits. Such sums do not change if we change
\phi
by a coboundary. Therefore, it is easy to see that
\phi
is SPR if and only if
\phi^{+}
is SPR, and
P_{G}(\phi)=P_{G}(\phi^{+})
.

If
\mu
is a shift invariant measure on
\Sigma
, then
\mu^{+}:=\mu\circ\pi^{-1}
is a shift invariant probability measure on
\Sigma^{+}
, and it is easy to see that
h_{\mu^{+}}(\sigma)=h_{\mu}(\sigma)
.
In addition, coboundaries with bounded continuous transfer functions are absolutely integrable with zero integral for all shift invariant probability measures, so
\int\phi d\mu=\int\phi^{+}d\mu^{+}
. So
\mu\in\mathfs M_{\phi}(\Sigma)
if and only if
\mu^{+}\in\mathfs M_{\phi^{+}}(\Sigma^{+})
, and in this case

P_{\mu}(\phi)=P_{\mu^{+}}(\phi^{+}).

In particular,
\mu
maximizes
P_{\mu}(\phi)
if and only if
\mu^{+}
maximizes
P_{\mu^{+}}(\phi^{+})
, and therefore
\mu_{\phi}
is the equilibrium measure of
\phi
on
\Sigma
, if and only if
(\mu_{\phi})^{+}
is the equilibrium measure of
\phi^{+}
on
\Sigma^{+}
.
We are therefore at liberty to write

\mu_{\phi}^{+}=\mu_{\phi^{+}}.

It is now a simple matter to see that the EKP inequalities (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) for
\Sigma^{+}
and
\phi^{+}
, imply the EKP inequalities (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) for
\Sigma
and
\phi
,
provided the test function
\psi
belongs to

\mathcal{H}_{\beta}^{+}(\Sigma):=\{\psi^{+}\circ\pi\circ\sigma^{n}:\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(\Sigma^{+}),n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}^{+}(\Sigma)
is dense in the space of
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions on
\Sigma
, (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) follow for all
\beta
-Hölder continuous functions on
\Sigma
.

C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism on a closed smooth surface admits a Hölder continuous symbolic coding by an SPR countable Markov shift, with finite Gurevich entropy. By the results of this paper, such diffeomorphisms satisfy the EKP inequality for all Hölder continuous functions

Starfish lemma via birational quasi-isomorphisms
Dmitriy Voloshyn

Abstract
We study birational quasi-isomorphisms between normal Noetherian domains endowed with cluster structures of geometric type. We prove an analogue of the Starfish lemma that allows one to transfer various cluster and algebraic properties of one variety onto another. In particular, we develop tools for proving that an upper cluster algebra equals the given commutative ring.

Introduction

Cluster algebras were introduced by S. Fomin and A. Zelevinsky in
[
8
]
. The main objective of the theory is to represent the coordinate ring of a complex variety
V
(often arising in Lie theory) in the form
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})=\mathbb{C}[V]
where
\mathcal{C}
is a cluster structure in
\mathbb{C}[V]
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})
is a cluster algebra. More generally, one aims to prove the equality
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})=\mathbb{C}[V]
where
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})
is the upper cluster algebra.

Many examples of cluster algebras are known. Early examples include the Grassmannian
[
22
]
, double Bruhat cells
[
2
]
and simply connected simple complex algebraic groups
[
14
]
. More recent examples include open Richardson varieties
[
19
]
and Bott-Samelson cells
[
23
]
.

The main technique for proving the inclusion
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathbb{C}[V]
(or
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathbb{C}[V]
) is the so-called Starfish lemma (see Proposition
2.9
). The lemma requires verifying certain regularity and coprimality conditions in the initial cluster and its one-step mutations. To show the reverse inclusion
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{C})\supseteq\mathbb{C}[V]
, one typically applies a result on upper bounds (see Proposition
2.7
).

The work of M. Gekhtman, M. Shapiro and A. Vainshtein shows that the coordinate ring of a simply connected simple complex algebraic group
G
can support several cluster structures of different ranks
[
10
,
14
,
16
]
. In general, it is harder to verify the conditions of the Starfish lemma in
\mathbb{C}[G]
for cluster structures of higher ranks. However, as was shown in
[
10
,
11
,
17
,
24
]
, such cluster structures are typically related by a birational quasi-isomorphism.

In this paper, we axiomatize the notion of a birational quasi-isomorphism, and use it to prove an analogue of the Starfish lemma. Specifically, given cluster structures
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathcal{C}
of ranks
\tilde{N}<N
in normal Noetherian domains
\tilde{R}
and
R
, we assume the conditions of the Starfish lemma hold for
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
; then, if there is a birational quasi-isomorphism between
(\mathcal{C},R)
and
(\tilde{\mathcal{C}},\tilde{R})
, we deduce the corresponding conditions for
(\mathcal{C},R)
. In this setting, we only need to verify conditions for the so-called
marked variables
(see Definition
3.2
; these variables are frozen in
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
and cluster in
\mathcal{C}
).

Our results are particularly useful in case there is a third cluster structure
(\hat{\mathcal{C}},\hat{R})
with
\hat{N}<N
that is birationally quasi-isomorphic to
(\mathcal{C},R)
. In this case, the conditions on the marked variables are mild; moreover, if the given rings
R
,
\tilde{R}
and
\hat{R}
are UFD’s, the conditions on the marked variables can be dropped. The results of the paper are applied in our recent work
[
17
]
(see Appendix
A
for an explicit example).

The notion of a quasi-isomorphism was previously studied by C. Fraser in
[
9
]
. The notion that we need for the Starfish lemma (and for covering the existing examples) is different from the Fraser’s notion in the following ways:

1)

Fraser’s definition requires cluster structures
\mathcal{C}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
to have equal rank
\tilde{N}=N
, while allowing arbitrary coefficients (in particular, any numbers of frozen variables). In our case, we require
\tilde{N}<N
, but the total number of variables (cluster plus frozen) in a single extended cluster has to be the same.

2)

In Fraser’s setup, a quasi-isomorphism sends a variable from
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
to a variable from
\mathcal{C}
times a monomial in frozen variables. In our case, the monomial has to be in the marked variables.

3)

Finally, we require the quasi-isomorphism to be an isomorphism between the localizations of
R
and
\tilde{R}
at the marked variables.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the necessary background on cluster algebras. In Section
3
, we review the notion of a quasi-isomorphism adjusted for our purposes. In Section
4
, we state our main results. In Section
5
and Section
6
, we provide proofs of the main results along with results of lesser importance. In Appendix
A
, we illustrate our theory in
\mathbb{C}[\SL_{3}]
.

\bar{f}_{ij}(X):=\begin{cases}\det X_{[i,n-j+i]}^{[j,n]}&1\leq i\leq j\leq 3;\\
\det X_{[i,n]}^{[j,n-i+j]}&1\leq j\leq i\leq 3.\end{cases}

(A.1)

where
\det X_{I}^{J}
for
I,J\subseteq[1,3]
denotes the minor of a matrix
X\in\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
on
I

J
columns. The variables
\bar{f}_{ij}(X)
are regular, irreducible and pairwise coprime, and it is well-known that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(\bar{\mathcal{C}})=\mathbb{C}[\SL_{3}]
(in fact, each matrix entry is a cluster variable).

The initial extended cluster for
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
is given by

\tilde{f}_{ij}(X)=\begin{cases}\bar{f}_{ij}(X)&(i,j)\neq(1,3)\\
\det\begin{bmatrix}x_{13}&x_{21}\\
x_{23}&x_{31}\end{bmatrix}&(i,j)=(1,3).\end{cases}

(A.2)

The pair
(\tilde{\mathcal{C}},\bar{\mathcal{C}})
is related, with the marked variables given by
\bar{f}_{31}
and
\tilde{f}_{31}
. Define a rational map
\mathcal{U}:\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})\dashrightarrow\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
by

\mathcal{U}(X):=\left(I+\frac{x_{21}}{x_{31}}e_{12}\right)\cdot X.

(A.3)

The map
\mathcal{U}
is birational, with the inverse given by

\mathcal{U}^{-1}(X)=\left(I-\frac{x_{21}}{x_{31}}e_{12}\right)\cdot X.

(A.4)

The induced map on localizations
\mathcal{U}^{*}:\mathbb{C}[\SL_{3}][x_{31}^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\SL_{3}][x_{31}^{\pm 1}]
is a birational quasi-isomorphism. According to Proposition
4.5
, the initial cluster of
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
consists of irreducible pairwise coprime regular functions, and in order to claim
\bar{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}})=\mathbb{C}[\SL_{3}]
, one only needs to verify that

•

The mutation
\tilde{f}_{31}^{\prime}
is regular and not divisible by
x_{31}
;

•

Setting
F_{31}:=\{\tilde{f}_{ij}\ |\ (i,j)\neq(3,1)\}\cup\{\tilde{f}_{31}^{\prime}\}
, any regular function
p\in\mathbb{C}[\SL_{3}]
is an element of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{C}}(F_{31})
; that is, a Laurent polynomial in terms of the functions in
F_{31}
.

For the first condition, one can see that

f_{31}^{\prime}=\det\begin{bmatrix}x_{13}&x_{22}\\
x_{23}&x_{32}\end{bmatrix},

Residual regularization path-following methods for linear complementarity problems Journal: Journal of XXXX
Xin-long Luo ∗ \ast, Sen Zhang, Hang Xiao

Abstract
In this article, we consider the residual regularization path-following method with the trust-region updating strategy for the linear complementarity problem. This time-stepping selection based on the trust-region updating strategy overcomes the shortcoming of the line search method, which consumes the unnecessary trial steps in the transient-state phase. In order to improve the robustness of the path-following method, we use the residual regularization parameter to replace the traditional complementarity regularization parameter. Moreover, we prove the global convergence of the new method under the standard assumptions without the traditional assumption condition of the priority to feasibility over complementarity. Numerical results show that the new method is robust and efficient for the linear complementarity problem, especially for the dense cases. And it is more robust and faster than some state-of-the-art solvers such as the built-in subroutines PATH and MILES of the GAMS v28.2 (2019) environment. The computational time of the new method is about 1/3 to 1/10 of that of PATH for the dense linear complementarity problem.

3
Convergence analysis

In this section, we analyze the global convergence of Algorithm
1
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
M_{\upsilon}=M
in the following
analysis. Namely, we do not discriminate between
M_{\upsilon}
and
M
.
Similarly to the analysis techniques of the references

Wright1994
;
Xu1991
;
Zhang1994
, we firstly construct an auxiliary sequence

(u^{k},\,v^{k})
as follows:

\displaystyle u^{k+1}=u^{k}+\alpha_{k}(x^{k}+\Delta x^{k}-u^{k}),

(32)

\displaystyle v^{k+1}=v^{k}+\alpha_{k}(y^{k}+\Delta y^{k}-v^{k}),

(33)

where
(\Delta x^{k},\,\Delta y^{k})
are solved by equations
(
16
)-(
17
) and
(u^{0},\,v^{0})\leq(x^{0},\,y^{0})

satisfies the feasibility
r_{q}(u^{0},\,v^{0})=0
. Then, it is not difficult
to verify

\displaystyle r_{q}(u^{k},\,v^{k})=v^{k}-(Mu^{k}+q)=0,

(34)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}-u^{k+1}=(1-\alpha_{k})(x^{k}-u^{k})\geq 0,

(35)

\displaystyle y^{k+1}-v^{k+1}=(1-\alpha_{k})(y^{k}-v^{k})\geq 0,

(36)

where
\alpha_{k}=\Delta t_{k}/(1+\Delta t_{k})
.

Meanwhile, in order to obtain the global convergence, we need to enforce the
condition specified below. Firstly, we select a constant
\gamma\in(0,\,1)

that satisfies

\displaystyle 0<\gamma\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\{x^{0}_{i}y^{0}_{i}\}}=\frac{(x^{0})^{T}y^{0}+\|r_{q}^{0}\|}{2n\,\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\{x^{0}_{i}y^{0}_{i}\}}.

(37)

Then, we select
\alpha_{k}
such that

\displaystyle x^{k}_{i}(\alpha)y^{k}_{i}(\alpha)\geq\gamma\mu_{k}(\alpha),\;i=1,\,2,\,\ldots,n

(38)

holds for all
\alpha\in(0,\;\alpha_{k}]\subset(0,\,1]
, where

x^{k}(\alpha)
,
y^{k}(\alpha)
,
r_{q}^{k}(\alpha)
and
\mu_{k}(\alpha)
are
defined by

\displaystyle x^{k}(\alpha)=x^{k}+\alpha\Delta x^{k},\;y^{k}(\alpha)=y^{k}+\alpha\Delta y^{k},

(39)

\displaystyle r_{q}^{k}(\alpha)=r_{q}(x^{k}(\alpha),y^{k}(\alpha)),\;\mu_{k}(\alpha)=\frac{(x^{k}(\alpha))^{T}y^{k}(\alpha)+\|r_{q}^{k}(\alpha)\|}{2n}.

(40)

Condition (
38
) is to prevent iterations from prematurely getting too
close to the boundary of the positive quadrant and its restriction on
\gamma

is very mild. In the practice, it can be selected to be very small.

In order to establish the main global convergence of Algorithm
1
,
similarly to the results
Wright1994
;
Zhang1994
, we prove the following
several technique lemmas.

Lemma 1

When
(x^{k}(\alpha),\,y^{k}(\alpha))
satisfies the condition
(
38

By summing two sides of the condition (

\displaystyle(x^{k}(\alpha))^{T}y^{k}(\alpha)\geq n\gamma\mu_{k}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\gamma\left((x^{k}(\alpha))^{T}y^{k}(\alpha)+(1-\alpha)\|r_{q}^{k}\|\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt\geq\frac{1}{2}\gamma(x^{k}(\alpha))^{T}y^{k}(\alpha).

(43)

Consequently, from equation (
43
) and
0<\gamma<1
, we obtain

(x^{k}(\alpha))^{T}y^{k}(\alpha)\geq 0
. By substituting it into the condition
(
38

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{k}\mu_{k}=\alpha x^{k}_{i}y^{k}_{i}+\alpha x^{k}_{i}\Delta y^{k}_{i}+\alpha y^{k}_{i}\Delta x^{k}_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt=(\alpha-2)x^{k}_{i}y^{k}_{i}+y^{k}_{i}x^{k}_{i}(\alpha)+x^{k}_{i}y^{k}_{i}(\alpha),\;i=1,\,2,\ldots,\,n.

(44)

If

From equations (

\displaystyle x^{k}_{i}(\alpha)y^{k}_{i}(\alpha)-\gamma\mu_{k}(\alpha)=x_{i}^{k}y_{i}^{k}+\alpha(x^{k}_{i}\Delta y^{k}_{i}+y^{k}_{i}\Delta x^{k}_{i})+\alpha^{2}\Delta x^{k}_{i}\Delta y^{k}_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{i}^{k}y_{i}^{k}+\alpha(\sigma_{k}\mu_{k}-x^{k}_{i}y^{k}_{i})+\alpha^{2}\Delta x^{k}_{i}\Delta y^{k}_{i}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt-\gamma\mu_{k}-\frac{1}{2n}\gamma\alpha\left(n\sigma_{k}\mu_{k}-(x^{k})^{T}y^{k}-\|r_{q}^{k}\|+\alpha(\Delta x^{k})^{T}\Delta y^{k}\right)

where

. By substituting

and

into

\displaystyle x^{k}_{i}(\alpha)y^{k}_{i}(\alpha)-\gamma\mu_{k}(\alpha)\geq\alpha\left(\sigma_{k}\mu_{k}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)-\alpha\left(1+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left\|\Delta X^{k}\Delta y^{k}\right\|_{\infty}\right).

Therefore, when

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we give the residual regularization path-following method with the
trust-region updating strategy (RPFMTr) for the linear complementarity problem.
The new residual regularization parameter improves the robustness of the path-following
method, in comparison to the traditional complementarity regularization parameter.
Meanwhile, we prove the global convergence of the new method under the standard
assumptions without the traditional assumption condition of the priority to feasibility
over complementarity. Numerical results show that RPFMTr is a robust and efficient
solver for the linear complementarity problem, especially for the dense case.
Furthermore, it is more robust and faster than some state-of-the-art solvers such
as PATH
DF1995
;
PATH
;
FM2000
;
FM2022
and MILES

Mathiesen1985
;
Rutherford1995
;
Rutherford2022
(the built-in subroutines of
the GAMS v28.2 (2019) environment
GAMS
). The computational time of RPFMTr is
about 1/3 to 1/10 of that of PATH for the dense linear complementarity problem.
Therefore, RPFMTr is an alternating solver for the linear complementarity problem
and worth exploring further for the nonlinear complementarity problem.

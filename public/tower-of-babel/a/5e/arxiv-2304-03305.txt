Impact of dimension-eight SMEFT operators in the EWPO and Triple Gauge Couplings analysis in Universal SMEFT Preprint: YITP-SB-2023-04, FERMILAB-PUB-23-134-V,UWThPh 2023-13
Tyler Corbett, Jay Desai, O. J. P. Éboli, M. C. Gonzalez-Garcia, Matheus Martines, Peter Reimitz

Abstract
We perform a complete study of the electroweak precision observables and electroweak gauge boson pair production in terms of the SMEFT up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / Λ 4 ) {\cal O}(1/\Lambda^{4}) under the assumption of universal, C and P conserving new physics. We show that the analysis of data from those two sectors allows us to obtain closed constraints in the relevant parameter space in this scenario. In particular we find that the Large Hadron Collider data can independently constrain the Wilson coefficients of the dimension-six and -eight operators directly contributing to the triple gauge boson vertices. Our results show that the impact of dimension-eight operators in the study of triple gauge couplings is small.



I
Introduction

During the last decade the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) has accumulated
a large amount of data that lead to further tests of the Standard
Model (SM) and the search for Physics beyond the Standard Model
(BSM). Presently there is no smoking gun indication of any extension
of the SM. Therefore, one can assume that there is a mass gap between
the electroweak scale and the BSM scale. In this scenario, the use of
Effective Field Theory (EFT)
Weinberg 1979
;
Georgi 1984
;
Donoghue et al. 2014
as the tool to search for hints of new Physics has
become customary.

The EFT approach is suited for model–independent analyses since it is
based exclusively on the low-energy accessible states and symmetries.
Assuming that the scalar particle observed in 2012
Aad et al. 2012
;
Chatrchyan et al. 2012
belongs to an electroweak doublet, we can
realize the
SU(2)_{L}\otimes U(1)_{Y}
symmetry linearly. The resulting
model is the so-called Standard Model EFT (SMEFT). There have been
many analyses of the LHC data using dimension-six SMEFT; see for
instance
Corbett et al. 2012
;
Corbett et al. 2013
;
Ellis et al. 2015
;
Corbett et al. 2015
;
Butter et al. 2016
;
Baglio et al. 2017
;
Barducci et al. 2018
;
Ellis et al. 2018
;
da Silva Almeida
et al. 2019
;
Brivio et al. 2020
;
Ellis et al. 2021
;
Dawson et al. 2020
;
Ethier et al. 2021
;
Almeida et al. 2021
and references therein. In order to access the
convergence of the
1/\Lambda
expansion, as well as avoid the
appearance of phase space regions where the cross section is
negative
Baglio et al. 2017
, it is important to perform the full
calculation at order
1/\Lambda^{4}
. The consistent calculation at
order
1/\Lambda^{4}
requires the introduction of the contributions
stemming from dimension-eight operators. In the most general scenario,
the number of dimension-eight operators contributing to the present
observables is extremely large
Henning et al. 2017
and that
precludes a complete general analysis including all effects up to
order
1/\Lambda^{4}
. Due to its complexity, the systematic study of
the
{\cal O}(1/\Lambda^{4})
effects is still in its early stages. To
date there have been a few case studies for
Drell-Yan
Alioli et al. 2020
;
Boughezal et al. 2021
;
Boughezal et al. 2022
;
Kim and Martin 2022
;
Allwicher et al. 2023
,
\bar{t}tH

production
Dawson et al. 2021
, the production of electroweak gauge
boson pairs
Degrande and Li 2023
, Higgs boson
processes
Hays et al. 2019
;
Corbett
et al. 2021a
;
Martin and Trott 2022
;
Corbett 2021
;
Hays et al. 2020
, and the electroweak
precision data
Hays et al. 2020
;
Corbett
et al. 2021b
.

With this motivation, we perform a complete study of the electroweak
precision observables (EWPO) and electroweak diboson (EWDB) production
at order
1/\Lambda^{4}
including all relevant dimension-six and
dimension-eight operators
under the assumption of universal New
Physics with conservation of
C
and
P

Wells and Zhang 2016
so
that the EFT contains only bosonic operators after field
redefinitions. In this case, we show that the analysis of existing
data from those two sectors allows one to obtain closed constraints on
the the full relevant parameter space. Furthermore, we argue that it
is still possible to perform the analysis sequentially, obtaining
first the constraints on four effective combinations of Wilson
coefficients using the EWPO, and then apply those bounds to reduce the
number of Wilson coefficients which are relevant for the the diboson
analysis. Besides demonstrating the feasibility of the analysis and
deriving the corresponding bounds, our main result is to show that in
this scenario the impact of dimension-eight operators in our present
determination of the triple gauge couplings (TGC) is small.

This work is organized as follows . The analysis framework employed
is presented in Sec.
II
.
Sections
III
and
IV
contain the results of
the analysis of EWPO and of the EWDB data respectively. In
Sec.
V
we summarize our conclusions. We present in the
appendices the full expressions of the couplings of the electroweak
gauge bosons to fermions and TGC to order
{\cal O}(1/\Lambda^{4})
in this
scenario.

II
Analysis framework

Following
Wells and Zhang 2016
, we consider a theory as universal if
its EFT can be expressed exclusively in terms of bosonic operators via
field redefinitions. We will also assume conservation of
C
and
P
.
The requirement of the EFT to be universal limits the number of
operators that have to be considered and in Ref.
Wells and Zhang 2016

the independent set of dimension-six operators for universal theories
is explicitly worked out in several bases. In this work we use the
Hagiwara, Ishihara, Szalapski, and Zeppenfeld (HISZ) dimension-six
basis
Hagiwara et al. 1993
;
Hagiwara et al. 1997
.
The relevant set of operators left in HISZ basis for universal
theories can be straightforwardly adapted from the results in
Ref.
Wells and Zhang 2016
for the SILH basis
Giudice et al. 2007

taking into account the different choice of two of the bosonic
operators left in the basis. With this, one finds that in the HISZ
basis universal theories are described by 11 bosonic operators and 5
fermionic operators. The 11 bosonic operators are:

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{\Phi,1}=(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\Phi\Phi^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\Phi)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{\Phi,2}=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\left(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi\right)\partial_{\mu}\left(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi\right)\;,

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt{\cal O}_{\Phi^{6}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{3}\;,\;

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{WW}=\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{W}_{\mu\nu}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}\Phi\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{BB}=\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{B}_{\mu\nu}\widehat{B}^{\mu\nu}\Phi\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{BW}=\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{B}_{\mu\nu}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}\Phi\;,\;

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{W}=(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}(D_{\nu}\Phi)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{B}=(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\widehat{B}^{\mu\nu}(D_{\nu}\Phi)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{WWW}={\rm Tr}[\widehat{W}_{\mu}^{\nu}\widehat{W}_{\nu}^{\rho}\widehat{W}_{\rho}^{\mu}]\;,\;

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{GG}=\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi\;G_{\mu\nu}^{a}G^{a\mu\nu}\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{GGG}=g_{s}^{3}f^{abc}{G}_{\mu}^{a\,\nu}{G}_{\nu}^{b\,\rho}{G}_{\rho}^{c\,\mu}\;,

(1)

where
\Phi
stands for the SM Higgs doublet and we have defined

\widehat{B}_{\mu\nu}\equiv i(g^{\prime}/2)B_{\mu\nu}
and

\widehat{W}_{\mu\nu}\equiv i(g/2)\sigma^{a}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}
, with
g_{s}
,

g
and
g^{\prime}
being the
SU(3)_{C}
,
SU(2)_{L}
and
U(1)_{Y}
gauge
couplings, respectively.
\sigma^{a}
stands for the Pauli matrices
while
f^{abc}
are the
SU(3)_{C}
structure constants.

Five four-fermion operators are generated when applying the equations
of motion (EOM) to eliminate bosonic operators involving the square
of derivatives of the gauge strength tensors and four Higgs fields in
total analogy with the SILH basis in Ref.
Wells and Zhang 2016
:

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{y}=|\Phi|^{2}(\Phi_{\alpha}J_{y}^{\alpha}+{\rm h.c.})\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{2y}=J^{\dagger}_{y\alpha}J^{\alpha}_{y}\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{2JW}=\sum_{f,f^{\prime}\in\{Q,L\}}\left(\bar{f}\gamma_{\mu}\frac{\sigma^{a}}{2}f\right)\left(\bar{f^{\prime}}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{\sigma^{a}}{2}f^{\prime}\right)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{2JB}=\sum_{f,f^{\prime}\in\{Q,L,u,d,e\}}\left(Y_{f}\bar{f}\gamma_{\mu}f\right)\left(Y_{f^{\prime}}\bar{f^{\prime}}\gamma^{\mu}f^{\prime}\right)\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{2JG}=\sum_{f,f^{\prime}\in\{Q,u,d\}}\left(\bar{f}\gamma_{\mu}T^{a}f\right)\left(\bar{f^{\prime}}\gamma^{\mu}T^{a}f^{\prime}\right)\;,

(2)

where
Y_{f}
are the hypercharges,
Q
and
L
are the quark and lepton
doublets and
u
,
d
and
e
represent the fermion singlets. In
addition,
T^{a}
are the Gell-Mann matrices,
y_{f}
are the
Yukawa matrices, and

J_{y}^{\alpha}=\bar{u}y_{u}^{\dagger}Q_{\beta}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}+\bar{Q}^{\alpha}y_{d}d+\bar{L}^{\alpha}y_{e}e\;.

For the dimension-eight operators, we will work in the basis
defined in Ref.
Murphy 2020
. For universal theories
the potentially relevant bosonic operators for
our analyses belong to the classes
\Phi^{6}D^{2}
,
X^{3}\Phi^{2}
,

X^{2}\Phi^{4}
, and
X\Phi^{4}D^{2}
with
X
standing for a field
strength tensor. This includes:

\bullet
two operators in the class
\Phi^{6}D^{2}

related to the dimension-six
{\cal O}_{\Phi,1}
and
{\cal O}_{\Phi,2}
:

{\cal O}^{(1)}_{D^{2}\Phi^{6}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\Phi\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad{\cal O}^{(2)}_{D^{2}\Phi^{6}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)(\Phi^{\dagger}\sigma^{I}\Phi)(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\sigma^{I}D^{\mu}\Phi\;,

(3)

\bullet
two
CP
conserving operators in class
X^{3}\Phi^{2}

that contribute to the EWDB analysis are

{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W^{3}\Phi^{2}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi){\rm Tr}[\widehat{W}^{\mu}_{\nu}\widehat{W}^{\nu}_{\rho}\widehat{W}^{\rho}_{\mu}]\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W^{2}B\Phi^{2}}=\frac{g^{3}s_{W}}{8c_{W}}\epsilon^{IJK}\Phi^{\dagger}\sigma^{I}\Phi B^{\mu}_{\nu}W^{J\,\nu}_{\rho}W^{K\,\rho}_{\mu}\;,

(4)

\bullet
two operators in class
X\Phi^{4}D^{2}
contributing to anomalous
TGC are siblings of dimension-six operators
{\cal O}_{B}
and
{\cal O}_{W}

{\cal O}^{(1)}_{B\Phi^{4}D^{2}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\widehat{B}^{\mu\nu}D_{\nu}\Phi\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W\Phi^{4}D^{2}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\hat{W}^{\mu\nu}D_{\nu}\Phi\;,

(5)

\bullet
four operators in the
X^{2}\Phi^{4}

\displaystyle{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W^{2}\Phi^{4}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{W}_{\mu\nu}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}\Phi\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}^{(1)}_{B^{2}\Phi^{4}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}\widehat{B}_{\mu\nu}\widehat{B}^{\mu\nu}\;,

(6)

\displaystyle{\cal O}^{(1)}_{BW\Phi^{4}}=(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{W}_{\mu\nu}\Phi\widehat{B}^{\mu\nu}\;,

\displaystyle{\cal O}^{(3)}_{W^{2}\Phi^{4}}=\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{W}_{\mu\nu}\Phi\Phi^{\dagger}\widehat{W}^{\mu\nu}\Phi\;.

(7)

In addition some dimension-eight fermionic operators will be generated
by the EOM in analogy to the dimension-six case. Presently there there
is no study of the fermionic operators compatible with universal
theories for the dimension-eight basis. So in what follows, we assume
that only four-fermion operators are generated in exchanging a subset of
the purely bosonic operators defining the universal basis for fermionic operators.

It is important to notice that not all operators listed above appear
in the analysis of EWPO and EWDB data even after accounting for their
finite renormalization contribution to the SM parameters. In this
work, we adopt as input parameters

\{\widehat{\alpha}_{\rm em}\;,\;\widehat{G}_{F}\;,\;\widehat{M}_{Z}\}
and consider the following three relations to define
the renormalized parameters

\displaystyle\hat{e}=\sqrt{4\pi\hat{\alpha}_{\rm em}}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{v}^{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\hat{G}_{F}}\;,

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{c}^{2}\hat{s}^{2}=\frac{\pi\widehat{\alpha}_{\rm em}}{\sqrt{2}\,\widehat{G}_{F}\widehat{M}^{2}_{Z}}\;,

where
\hat{s}
(
\hat{c}
) is the sine (cosine) of the weak mixing angle

\hat{\theta}
.

The predictions of SMEFT at order
1/\Lambda^{4}
and the input
parameters in Eq. (
8
) allow us to obtain the SM mixing
angle, electric charge, and the Higgs vev as a function of the input
parameters and some of dimension-six and -eight Wilson coefficients.
In this process the operators
{\cal O}_{WW}
,
{\cal O}_{BB}
,

{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W^{2}\Phi^{4}}
, and
{\cal O}^{(1)}_{B^{2}\Phi^{4}}
induce an
overall renormalization of the
W^{a}
and
B
field wave functions that
can be absorbed by a redefinition of the coupling constants.
Furthermore, the contribution of
{\cal O}^{(1)}_{D^{2}\Phi^{6}}
to the
Higgs vev cancels against its contribution to the renormalization of
the
W^{a}
and
B
field wave functions. Consequently their
coefficients drop out of any of the predictions in the EWPO and EWDB data
(see the appendix for the explicit expressions).

Altogether the effective Lagrangian considered in this work reads:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm eff}=

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{SM}+\frac{f_{WWW}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{WWW}+\frac{f_{W}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{W}+\frac{f_{B}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{B}+\frac{f_{BW}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{BW}+\frac{f_{\Phi,1}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{\Phi,1}+\frac{f_{4F}}{\Lambda^{2}}{\cal O}_{4F}

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{(2)}_{D^{2}\Phi^{6}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(2)}_{D^{2}\Phi^{6}}+\frac{f^{(1)}_{W^{3}\Phi^{2}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W^{3}\Phi^{2}}+\frac{f^{(1)}_{W^{2}B\Phi^{2}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W^{2}B\Phi^{2}}+\frac{f^{(1)}_{B\Phi^{4}D^{2}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(1)}_{B\Phi^{4}D^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{f^{(1)}_{W\Phi^{4}D^{2}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(1)}_{W\Phi^{4}D^{2}}+\frac{f^{(3)}_{W^{2}\Phi^{4}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(3)}_{W^{2}\Phi^{4}}+\frac{f^{(1)}_{BW\Phi^{4}}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}^{(1)}_{BW\Phi^{4}}+\frac{\Delta^{(8)}_{4F}}{\Lambda^{4}}{\cal O}_{4F}^{(8)}\;,

\left[2\langle\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi\rangle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\hat{G}_{F}}\right]_{\rm fermionic}\equiv\frac{\hat{v}^{4}}{\Lambda^{2}}\Delta_{4F}+\frac{\hat{v}^{6}}{\Lambda^{4}}\Delta^{(8)}_{4F}\;.

The predictions for observables at order
1/\Lambda^{4}

1/\Lambda^{2}

{\cal M}^{(6)}

{\cal M}^{(8)}

(11)

Notice that

V
Summary and Conclusions

We have studied the impact of
{\cal O}(1/\Lambda^{4})
corrections in the
EWPO and EWDB data analyses assuming a universal, C and P conserving
new physics scenario. The universality assumption reduces the number of
dimension-eight operators contributing to the processes making a
complete analysis possible. As described in Sec.
II
, in
the HISZ basis for the dimension-six SMEFT the universal theories are
described by 11 bosonic operators and five fermionic operators, the
latter being generated by the application of the EOM in the reduction
of the basis. At dimension eight there are ten potentially relevant
bosonic operators and one expects a fermionic operator generated by
the EOM. Of those, we find that there are six (nine) dimension-six
(-eight) operators contributing to the EWPO and EWDB observables (see
Eq. (
9
)).

The analysis of EWPO involves three dimension-six and four
dimension-eight operators whose Wilson coefficients cannot be
independently bound. However, we find that it is possible to
eliminate the blind directions by redefining three effective
coefficients which are just a shift of the three Wilson coefficients
of the dimension-six operators corrected by their corresponding
dimension-eight siblings – see Eq. (
13
)– and which
contain the sibling dimension-eight contribution to the universal
parameters S, T, and
\Delta G_{F}
. In addition the analysis contains
a purely dimension-eight contribution to the universal parameter
U
.
The fit to EWPO performed in terms of these four parameters results in
strong constraints on
\tilde{f}_{BW}
,
\tilde{f}_{\Phi,1}
,

\tilde{\Delta}_{4F}
, and
f^{(3)}_{W^{2}\Phi^{4}}
; see
Table
1
.

At
{\cal O}(1/\Lambda^{4})
EWDB analysis involves six (seven)
dimension-six (-eight) operators of which three (four) contribute
directly to the TGC while three (three) enter indirectly via the
finite renormalization of the SM parameters. The indirect
contributions can be cast in terms of three effective couplings
bounded by the EWPO (see Appendix
B
) allowing us to
neglect them in the EWDB analysis.

The direct contributions to the TGC can be expressed in
terms of three effective coefficients which are just a shift of the
three Wilson coefficients of the corresponding three dimension-six
operators corrected by their corresponding dimension-eight siblings
(i.e. rescaled by
\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi
); see Eq. (
19
). In
addition there is a genuine dimension-eight contribution to the
difference between the
\lambda_{\gamma}
and
\lambda_{Z}
couplings. We
performed an effective analysis of the EWDB data in terms of these
four coefficients and showed that the bulk of
{\cal O}(1/\Lambda^{4})

impact on the analysis is due to the dimension-six squared
contribution
|{\cal M}^{(6)}|^{2}
; see Fig.
2
and
Table
3
. This is so because of the different
dependence on the partonic center-of-mass energy of dimension-six
squared terms which give a pure quadratic TGC contribution to the
amplitude squared, and the dimension-eight contribution which enters
in the interference with the SM amplitude.

Profiting from the different energy dependence of the dimension-six
squared and the dimension-eight contributions it is possible to
perform an analysis of the EWDB data which allows us to constrain the
seven Wilson coefficients independently. The result of this analysis
is presented in Fig.
3
and
Table
4
. The results show that the bounds on
the three Wilson coefficients of the dimension-six operators are only
slightly looser than in the effective four-parameter analysis, while
the bounds on the four Wilson coefficients of the dimension-eight
operators are all of similar order and all substantially weaker than
those on their dimension-six siblings.

In summary, we have shown that, for the universal scenario, the analysis of the EWPO and
the EWDB data allows us to constrain the full parameter space of
operators up to
{\cal O}(\Lambda^{-4})
. Within the present precision
of EWDB data and with our choice of basis, it is still consistent
to perform the analysis sequentially: first obtain the constraints
on the relevant Wilson coefficients using the EWPO and then apply those
bounds to reduce the number of Wilson coefficients which are relevant for the
the diboson analysis. That said, the LHC continues to accumulate data on
EWDB production, and consequently, we anticipate stronger bounds on the
TGC couplings in the future. At some point, it will be necessary
to perform a combined analysis of EWPO+EWDB data taking into account
the indirect contributions due to the finite renormalization to the TGC
in analogy with study of TGC and possible anomalous fermionic
couplings
Alves et al. 2018
.

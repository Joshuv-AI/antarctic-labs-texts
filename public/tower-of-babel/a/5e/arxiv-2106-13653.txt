Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the algebraic nature of the value of a higher Green function on an orthogonal Shimura variety at a single CM point. This is motivated by a conjecture of Gross and Zagier in the setting of higher Green functions on the product of two modular curves. In the process, we will study analogue of harmonic Maass forms in the setting of Hilbert modular forms, and obtain results concerning the arithmetic of their holomorphic part Fourier coefficients. As a consequence, we answer a question of Zagier in his 1986 ICM proceeding.



To Don Zagier on the occasion of his 70th birthday

1.3.
Ideas

We now describe some ideas and observations that help to overcome the obstacles mentioned in the previous section:

•

For any holomorphic Hilbert cusp form

and

sufficiently large, the product

has a modular preimage under the lowering operator in

•

The generalization of Borcherds’ lift in

•

The embedding trick only needs to work along the diagonal of

\mathcal{O}

The first idea is inspired by Zwegers’ work

Such products are also called “mixed mock-modular forms” in

The Rankin-Cohen differential operator can also be generalized to be applied on such functions (see the differential operator

Using the Siegel-Weil formula, we can relate

to Hilbert Eisenstein series.
By varying the weight, we will show that for any finite set of points in

, there is a Hilbert Eisenstein series that does not vanish on this set (see Lemma

). This observation has its root in the classical work
[

of Rankin and Swinnerton-Dyer on zeros of elliptic Eisenstein series, and leads to the “partition of unity” result in Proposition
3.4
.

By putting these ideas together, we are able to overcome the obstacles and prove Theorem
1.3
.
When it is specialized to the case in Theorem
1.1
, we can combine this result about differences with the result about partial averages in
[
12
]
to complete the proof.

1.4.
Outlook and Organization

To prove Conjecture
1.6
, one needs algebraicity results concerning sums of CM points, in addition to the “difference result” in Theorem
1.3
.
For real quadratic
F
, we have worked out such a “sum result” when

is biquadratic in
[

, which has led to a proof of Conjecture

in this case.
When

, one can try to relate the (in)coherent Eisenstein series to Eisenstein series on

over
F
, and realize them as suitable theta lifts from
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
over
F
.
We plan to pursue this idea in a future work.

The paper is organized as follows.
In sections

and

Results such as Lemma

Putting these together, we give the proofs of Theorems

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

Proof.

By replacing

We first prove the case

s\in\mathbb{Z}_{>s_{0}}
, then there is a bijection
\sigma:I\to J
such that
c_{i}=b_{\sigma(i)}
for all
i\in I
.
In particular,
I
is finite if and only if
J
is finite, in which case they have the same size.

3.
Functions

3.1.
Weil Representation and Theta Function

Let
G:=\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}({\mathrm{SL}}_{2})
and

\Gamma:={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\subset G(\mathbb{R})\subset G(\mathbb{A}).

Also denote

\Gamma_{f}:={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\hat{\mathcal{O}})\subset G(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})
.

For an
F
-quadratic space
(V,Q)
of even dimension, let
\omega=\omega_{\psi}
be the Weil representation of
G(\mathbb{A})
on the space of Schwartz functions
{\mathcal{S}}(V(\mathbb{A}_{F}))={\mathcal{S}}(V(\hat{F}))\otimes\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq d}{\mathcal{S}}(V_{\sigma_{j}})
, where
\psi
is the standard additive character on
F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}
.

At the infinite local place, suppose
(W,Q_{W})
is an
\mathbb{R}
-quadratic space of signature
(p,q)
with
2\mid(p+q)
.
For a point
w
in the symmetric space
\mathbb{D}_{W}
associated to
{\mathrm{SO}}(W)
, we obtain an orthogonal decomposition
W=w^{\perp}\oplus w
and a Schwartz function

(3.1.1)

\phi^{p,q}(w,\lambda):=\exp(-2\pi(Q_{W}(\lambda_{w^{\perp}})+Q_{W}(\lambda_{w})))

in
{\mathcal{S}}(W)
, which is acted on by

{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})

via the Weil representation
\omega_{W}
to produce

(3.1.2)

\phi^{W}(\tau,w,\lambda):=v^{q/2}{\mathbf{e}}(Q(\lambda_{w^{\perp}})\tau+Q(\lambda_{w})\overline{\tau}).

Note that

(3.1.3)

\phi^{W}(\tau,w,\lambda)=\phi^{W}(\tau,\bar{w},\lambda)

as it is independent of the orientation of
w
.
If
q=0
, then the expressions in (
3.1.1
) and (
3.1.2
) are independent of
w
and we omit them from the notation.
In addition, we will also be interested in the following “singular Schwartz function” when
q\geq 1

(3.1.4)

\phi^{W,*}(\tau,w,\lambda):={\mathbf{e}}(Q(\lambda)\tau)\begin{cases}-(-4\pi Q(\lambda_{w}))^{1-q/2}\Gamma(q/2-1,-4\pi Q(\lambda_{w})v),&\lambda\not\in w^{\perp},\\
\mathrm{CT}_{s=0}\frac{v^{s+q/2-1}}{s+q/2-1},&\lambda\in w^{\perp}.\end{cases}

Here
\Gamma(s,x):=\int^{\infty}_{x}t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt
is the incomplete Gamma function.
Direct calculations yield

(3.1.5)

L_{\tau}\phi^{W,*}(\tau,w,\lambda)=\phi^{W}(\tau,w,\lambda).

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}_{W}
and
\lambda\in W
.

To describe the finite local place, let
L\subset V
be an even lattice and
S_{L}\subset{\mathcal{S}}(V(\hat{F}))
the subspace as in (
2.4.3
).
Then
\Gamma
acts on the space
S_{L}
via
\omega
, whose complex conjugate we denote by
\rho_{L}
.
Its explicit values on the generators of
\Gamma
can be found in section 3.2 of
[
11
]
.
Furthermore, it is unitary with respect to the hermitian pairing on
S_{L}

(3.1.6)

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle:=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\phi(\mu)\overline{\psi(\mu)},~\phi,\psi\in S_{L}.

More generally, for lattices
L,M
and
\phi\in S_{M\oplus L},\psi\in S_{M}
, we define

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{L}\in S_{L}
by

(3.1.7)

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{L}(\mu):=\sum_{\nu\in M^{\prime}/M}\phi((\nu,\mu))\overline{\psi(\nu)},~\mu\in L^{\prime}/L.

These pairings are then naturally defined for functions valued in
S_{L}
.

For the rest of the section, suppose
V_{\sigma_{j}}
is positive definite whenever
j\geq 2
.
Then
\mathbb{D}_{V_{\sigma_{j}}}
is
\mathbb{D}
for
j=1
and a point otherwise.
For
{\tau}=(\tau_{j})\in\mathbb{H}^{d},z\in\mathbb{D},h\in H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\phi_{f}\in{\mathcal{S}}(V(\hat{F}))
, we define the Siegel theta function

(3.1.8)

\begin{split}\Theta({\tau},z,h;\phi_{f})&:=\sum_{\lambda\in V(F)}\phi_{f}(h^{-1}\lambda)\phi_{\infty}(\tau,z,\lambda),\\
\phi_{\infty}(\tau,z,\lambda)&:=\phi^{V_{\sigma_{1}}}(\tau_{1},z,\lambda)\bigotimes_{2\leq j\leq d}\phi^{V_{\sigma_{j}}}(\tau_{j},\lambda).\end{split}

Note that
\Theta(\tau,\bar{z},h;\phi_{f})=\Theta(\tau,z,h;\phi_{f})
by (
3.1.3
).
In the variable
{\tau}
, this is a Hilbert modular form of weight

(3.1.9)

{\tilde{\kappa}}={\tilde{\kappa}}(V):=((p_{1}-q_{1})/2,n/2+1\dots,n/2+1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

For any even lattice
L\subset V
, we also denote the associated theta function by

(3.1.10)

\Theta_{L}({\tau},z,h):=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\Theta({\tau},z,h;\phi_{\mu})\phi_{\mu},

which is valued in
S_{L}
.
When
h=1
, we omit it from the notation.
The definition of
\Theta_{L}
implies that

(3.1.11)

\Theta_{L}(\tau,z)=\Theta_{L_{h}}(\tau,z,h),

where
L_{h}
is defined in (
2.4.4
) and
L^{\prime}/L\cong L_{h}^{\prime}/L_{h}
via (
2.4.5
).

If
V_{\sigma_{1}}
is definite, then
\Theta_{L}(\tau,z,h)
is independent of
z
and we write

(3.1.12)

\theta_{L}({\tau},h):=\Theta_{L}({\tau},z,h).

In particular when
L=P
is positive definite and
\mathbb{Z}
-unimodular, this is a scalar-valued, holomorphic Hilbert modular form on
\Gamma_{F}
of parallel weight
n/2+1
.
We denote the graded subring

(3.1.13)

{\mathcal{M}}^{\theta}_{F}:=\mathrm{Span}\{\theta_{P}(\tau):P\in{\mathcal{U}}_{F}^{+}\}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{F}.

For future convenience, we also define

(3.1.14)

\begin{split}\Theta^{*}({\tau},z,h;\phi_{f})&:=\sum_{\lambda\in V(F)}\phi_{f}(h^{-1}\lambda)\phi^{V_{\sigma_{1}},*}(\tau_{1},z,\lambda)\otimes_{2\leq j\leq d}\phi^{V_{\sigma_{j}}}(\tau_{j},\lambda),\\
\Theta^{*}_{L}(\tau,z,h)&:=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}\Theta^{*}({\tau},z,h;\phi_{\mu})\phi_{\mu}.\end{split}

The sums converge absolutely since the singular Schwartz function decays as a Schwartz function and
z^{\perp}\cap L^{\prime}
is contained in a positive definite lattice.
For fixed
(z,h)
, it defines a real-analytic function in
\tau
, which satisfies the analogue of (
3.1.11
) as well as

(3.1.15)

L_{\tau_{1}}\Theta^{*}_{L}(\tau,z,h)=\Theta_{L}(\tau,z,h)

by (
3.1.5
).
When
V_{\sigma_{1}}
is negative definite, we have
q_{1}=n+2
and the function
\theta^{*}_{L}(\tau,h):=\Theta^{*}_{L}(\tau,z,h)
can be written explicitly as

(3.1.16)

\begin{split}\theta^{*}_{L}(\tau,h)&=-\sum_{\lambda\in h(L^{\prime})\cap V,~\lambda\neq 0}{\mathbf{e}}(\mathrm{tr}(Q(\lambda)\tau))(4\pi|Q(\lambda)_{1}|)^{-n/2}\Gamma(n/2,4\pi|Q(\lambda)_{1}|v_{1})\phi_{\lambda}\\
&+\mathrm{CT}_{s=0}\frac{v_{1}^{s+n/2}}{s+n/2}\phi_{0}.\end{split}

Although
\theta^{*}_{L}
is not modular in
\tau
, difference of such functions will become modular after adding suitable holomorphic functions (see Theorem
4.3
).

Remark 3.1
.

When
\dim_{F}V
is odd, all the constructions above still hold by working with metaplectic covers.
As this is not needed for most of the applications, we refrain from introducing more notations and refer the readers to
[
11
]
.

3.2.
Eisenstein Series and Siegel-Weil Formula.

Let

B\subset{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
the standard Borel subgroup, and
I(s,\chi)=\otimes_{v}I(s,\chi_{v}):=\mathrm{Ind}^{{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}_{B(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\chi|\cdot|^{s}
the induced representation of
G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
for
\chi=\chi_{V}
the quadratic Hecke character associated to an
F
-quadratic space
(V,Q)
with signature
((p_{1},q_{1}),(n+2,0),\dots,(n+2,0))
.
For a standard section
\Phi\in I(s,\chi)
,
the Eisenstein series

(3.2.1)

E(g,s,\Phi):=\sum_{\gamma\in B(F)\backslash{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(F)}\Phi(\gamma g,s)

converges absolutely for
\Re(s)\gg 0
and has meromorphic continuation to
s\in\mathbb{C}
.
When
s_{0}:=n/2
, the map
\lambda:{\mathcal{S}}(V(\mathbb{A}_{F}))\to I(s_{0},\chi)
defined by

(3.2.2)

\lambda(\phi)(g)=(\omega(g)\phi)(0)

is
{{\mathrm{SL}}}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
-equivariant, and
\lambda(\phi)
can be extended uniquely to all
s
and produce a standard sections in
I(s,\chi)
.

The infinite part
\otimes_{1\leq j\leq d}I(s,\chi_{\sigma_{j}})
is generated by functions
\Phi^{\kappa}_{\infty}:=\prod_{j}\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa_{j}}
with
\kappa=(\kappa_{j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfying
\kappa_{j}\equiv n/2+1\bmod{2}
, where

\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}([k_{\theta},1])=e^{i\theta/2},~-\pi<\theta\leq\pi

is the image of
\phi^{q+2,q}_{\infty}
defined in (
3.1.1
) under
\lambda
for any
q\in\mathbb{N}

[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
.
For
\phi_{f}\in{\mathcal{S}}(V(\hat{F}))
and
{\tilde{\kappa}}={\tilde{\kappa}}(V)
as in (
3.1.9
),
the Eisenstein series

(3.2.3)

E({\tau},\phi_{f},{\tilde{\kappa}}):=v^{-{\tilde{\kappa}}/2}E(g_{\tau},s_{0},\lambda(\phi_{f})\otimes\Phi^{{\tilde{\kappa}}}_{\infty})

is a Hilbert modular form of weight
{\tilde{\kappa}}
.
For any even lattice
L\subset V
, we have

(3.2.4)

E_{L}({\tau},{\tilde{\kappa}}):=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}E({\tau},\phi_{\mu},{\tilde{\kappa}})\phi_{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{{\tilde{\kappa}},\rho_{L}}.

Suppose
q=2
.
For any compact open
K\subset H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})
stabilizing
\hat{L}
and acting trivially on
\hat{L}^{\prime}/\hat{L}
, we have the Siegel-Weil formula
[
10
, Lemma 4.3]

(3.2.5)

E_{L}({\tau},{\tilde{\kappa}})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(X_{K})}\int_{X_{K}}\Theta_{L}({\tau},z,h)\Omega^{n},~n\geq 1,

where
\Omega
is the Kähler form on
X_{K}
normalized as in
[
11
]
and
\mathrm{vol}(X_{K})=\int_{X_{K}}\Omega^{n}
.
For
n=0
, the Siegel-Weil formula yields

(3.2.6)

E_{L}({\tau},{\tilde{\kappa}})=\frac{2}{\mathrm{vol}(X_{K})}\sum_{[z,h]\in X_{K}}\Theta_{L}({\tau},z,h).

When
q=0
, we have
{\tilde{\kappa}}=\overrightarrow{s_{0}+1}
and the lattice
P=L
is totally positive definite.
The classical Siegel-Weil formula yields

(3.2.7)

\begin{split}E_{P}(\tau)&:=E_{P}(\tau,{\tilde{\kappa}})=c_{K}^{-1}\sum_{h\in H(\mathbb{Q})\backslash H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})/K}\theta_{P}(\tau,h),\\
c_{K}&=\sum_{h\in H(\mathbb{Q})\backslash H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})/K}1\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

Though
\theta_{P^{\ell}}=\theta_{P}^{\ell}
, the Eisenstein series
E_{P^{\ell}}
is almost never the same as
E_{P}^{\ell}
!

Furthermore suppose that
P\in{\mathcal{U}}_{F}^{+}
with rank
2\mathfrak{r}
. Then
E_{P}(\tau)
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{F}^{\theta}
and coincides with the Hecke Eisenstein series for
F
of parallel weight
\mathfrak{r}=n/2+1
.
It can be written as (see section 2 in
[
5
]
)

(3.2.8)

E_{P}(\tau)=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)}c(\beta,\tau)^{-\mathfrak{r}},~c(\beta,\tau):=\begin{cases}A_{\beta}\cdot{\mathrm{Nm}}(\tau+\beta)&\beta\in F,\\
1&\beta=\infty,\end{cases}

for certain non-zero integers
A_{\beta}
depending only on
\beta
.
Note that since
P^{\ell}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}_{F}
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
E_{P^{\ell}}(\tau)\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\theta}_{F}
.
Applying Lemma
2.9
, we can deduce the following results about zeros of theta functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P\in{\mathcal{U}}_{F}^{+}
and
S\subset\mathbb{H}^{d}
a finite set of points.
Then there exists
\ell\geq 1
such that
E_{P^{\ell}}
does not vanish on
S
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{d}
acts transitively on
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, we can write
S=\{\tau_{0},g_{1}\tau_{0},\dots,g_{N}\tau_{0}\}
with
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
and
g_{i}\in{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{d}
, where we set
g_{0}
to be the identity.
Assume that for every
\ell\geq 1
, there exists
0\leq i\leq N
such that
E_{P^{\ell}}(g_{i}\tau_{0})=0
.
Then the function

f_{\ell}(\tau):=\prod_{i=0}^{N}E_{P^{\ell}}(g_{i}\tau)

vanishes at
\tau=\tau_{0}
for all
\ell\geq 1
.
Using the expression (
3.2.8
) we can write

f_{\ell}(\tau)=\sum_{\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{N}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{N}c(\beta_{i},g_{i}\tau)\right)^{-\mathfrak{r}\ell}.

By Remark
2.10
, we have
\prod_{i=0}^{N}c(\beta_{i},g_{i}\tau_{0})=0
for all
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{N}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
, which is clearly a contradiction since
\prod_{i=0}^{N}c(\infty,g_{i}\tau_{0})=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
, there exist
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{F}^{+}
in the same genus such that
\theta_{P_{1}}(\tau_{0})\neq\theta_{P_{2}}(\tau_{0})
.

Proof.

Assume otherwise. Then for any
P\in{\mathcal{U}}_{F}^{+}
, the function
\theta_{P}(\tau,h)
takes the same value at
\tau_{0}
for all
h\in H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})
and
we have

E_{P}(\tau_{0})=c_{K}\sum_{h\in H(\mathbb{Q})\backslash H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})/K}\theta_{P}(\tau_{0},h)=\theta_{P}(\tau_{0})c_{K}\sum_{h\in H(\mathbb{Q})\backslash H(\hat{\mathbb{Q}})/K}1=\theta_{P}(\tau_{0}).

Therefore
E_{P^{\ell}}(\tau_{0})=\theta_{P^{\ell}}(\tau_{0})=\theta_{P}(\tau_{0})^{\ell}=E_{P}(\tau_{0})^{\ell}
for all
\ell\geq 1
and
P\in{\mathcal{U}}_{F}^{+}
.
Using expression (
3.2.8
), we obtain

\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)}c(\beta,\tau_{0})^{-\mathfrak{r}\ell}=E_{P}(\tau_{0})^{\ell},

which contradicts Corollary
2.11
since
c(\beta,\tau_{0})\neq 0
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
.
∎

Proposition 3.4

(Partition of Unity)
.

Let
F
be a totally real field. Then there exist
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\tilde{P}}_{i},P_{1,i},P_{2,i}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}_{F}
with ranks
2{\tilde{\mathfrak{r}}}_{i}
and
2\mathfrak{r}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m

such that
P_{1,i}
and
P_{2,i}
are in the same genus for all
i
and there is no
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
such that
(E_{{\tilde{P}}_{i}}(\theta_{P_{1,i}}-\theta_{P_{2,i}}))^{\Delta}(\tau_{0})=0
for all
i
.

Furthermore, for any
d_{1},\dots,d_{m},e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists elliptic modular forms
g_{i}\in M^{!}_{-({\tilde{\mathfrak{r}}}_{i}d_{i}+\mathfrak{r}_{i}e_{i})d}
with rational Fourier coefficients such

(3.2.9)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}(E^{d_{i}}_{{\tilde{P}}_{i}}\cdot(\theta_{P_{1,i}}-\theta_{P_{2,i}})^{e_{i}})^{\Delta}=1.

Proof.

Start with a point
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
, we can find
P_{1},P_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}_{F}
in the same genus satisfying
(\theta_{P_{1}}-\theta_{P_{2}})^{\Delta}(\tau_{0})\neq 0
by Lemma
3.3
.
If
(\theta_{P_{1}}-\theta_{P_{2}})^{\Delta}
has no zero on
\mathbb{H}
, then we can apply Lemma
3.2
to take
{\tilde{P}}_{1},{\tilde{P}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}_{F}
such that
E_{{\tilde{P}}_{1}}^{\Delta}
and
E_{{\tilde{P}}_{2}}^{\Delta}
do not have common zero on
\mathbb{H}
.
The forms
E_{{\tilde{P}}_{1}}\cdot(\theta_{P_{1}}-\theta_{P_{2}})
and

E_{{\tilde{P}}_{2}}\cdot(\theta_{P_{1}}-\theta_{P_{2}})
satisfy the first claim.

Otherwise, let
\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{m}\in\mathbb{H}
be the zeros of
(\theta_{P_{1}}-\theta_{P_{2}})^{\Delta}
in a fundamental domain
{\mathcal{F}}
of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
.
By Lemma
3.3
, there exists
P_{1,i}
and
P_{2,i}

such that
\theta^{\Delta}_{P_{1,i}}(\tau_{i})\neq\theta^{\Delta}_{P_{2,i}}(\tau_{i})

for all
2\leq i\leq m
.
Let
S\subset{\mathcal{F}}
be the finite set of the zeros of
\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}(\theta_{P_{1,i}}^{\Delta}-\theta_{P_{2,i}}^{\Delta})
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
, we can find
{\tilde{P}}\in{\mathcal{U}}^{+}_{F}
such that
E_{{\tilde{P}}}
does not vanish on
S
.
Let
S^{\prime}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
be the finite set of the zeros of
E_{{\tilde{P}}}^{\Delta}\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}(\theta_{P_{1,i}}^{\Delta}-\theta_{P_{2,i}}^{\Delta})
.
We can apply Lemma
3.2
to find
E_{{\tilde{P}}_{i}}
for
2\leq i\leq m
such that they do not vanish on
S^{\prime}
.
Now the forms
E_{{\tilde{P}}}\cdot(\theta_{P_{1}}-\theta_{P_{2}})
and
E_{{\tilde{P}}_{i}}\cdot(\theta_{P_{1,i}}-\theta_{P_{2,i}})
with
2\leq i\leq m
satisfy the first claim.

To prove the second claim, we can write

\Delta^{-({\tilde{\mathfrak{r}}}_{i}d_{i}+\mathfrak{r}_{i}e_{i})d}(E^{12d_{i}}_{{\tilde{P}}_{i}}(\theta_{P_{1,i}}-\theta_{P_{2,i}})^{12e_{i}})^{\Delta}=A_{i}(j)\in M^{!}_{0}=\mathbb{Q}[j]

with
j=j(\tau)
the
j
-invariant and
A_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
.
The first claim implies
\gcd(A_{1},\dots,A_{m})=1
, i.e. there exists
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}B_{i}(x)A_{i}(x)=1.

Setting
g_{i}:=B_{i}(j)\Delta^{-({\tilde{\mathfrak{r}}}_{i}d_{i}+\mathfrak{r}_{i}e_{i})d}(E^{11d_{i}}_{{\tilde{P}}_{i}}(\theta_{P_{1,i}}-\theta_{P_{2,i}})^{11e_{i}})^{\Delta}

proves the second claim.
∎

3.3.
Higher Green Function

We follow
[
12
]
to recall higher Green function on the Shimura variety
X_{K}
for
F=\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
V/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic space of signature
(n,2)
and
L\subset V
an even lattice with
K
an open compact stabilizing
\hat{L}
.
Also, we denote

(3.3.1)

\sigma_{0}:=\frac{n}{4}-\frac{1}{2}.

For
\mu\in L^{\prime}/L
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}+Q(\mu)
, the automorphic Green function is defined by

(3.3.2)

\Phi_{m,\mu}(z,h,s):=2\frac{\Gamma(s+\sigma_{0})}{\Gamma(2s)}\sum_{\lambda\in h(L_{m,\mu})}\left(\frac{m}{Q(\lambda_{z^{\perp}})}\right)^{s+\sigma_{0}}F\left(s+\sigma_{0},s-\sigma_{0},2s;\frac{m}{Q(\lambda_{z^{\perp}})}\right),

where
F(a,b,c;z)
is the Gauss hypergeometric function
[
1
, Chapter 15]
.
The sum converges normally on
X_{K}\backslash Z(m,\mu)
for
s>\sigma_{0}+1
and defines an eigenfunction of the Laplacian
\Delta
on
\mathbb{D}
, normalized as in
[
7
]
, with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{2}(s-\sigma_{0}-1)(s+\sigma_{0})
.
Furthermore, it has a meromorphic continuation to
s\in\mathbb{C}
with a simple pole at
s=\sigma_{0}+1
, whose constant term is denoted by
\Phi_{m,\mu}(z,h,\sigma_{0}+1)
and the regularized theta lift of Hejhal-Poincaré series of index
(m,\mu)

[
7
]
.

At
s=\sigma_{0}+1+r
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
\Phi_{m,\mu}(z,h,s)
is called a
higher Green function
.
For the unimodular lattice
L=M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
z=Z(z_{1},z_{2})
as in Example
2.5
, we have

(3.3.3)

\Phi_{m}(z,1,r+1)=\frac{2(-1)^{r}}{\Gamma(2r+2)}G_{r+1}^{m}(z_{1},z_{2}),

where
G_{s}^{m}
is defined in (
1.1.2
).
For a harmonic Maass form
f=\sum_{m,\mu}c(m,\mu)q^{-m}\phi_{\mu}+O(1)\in H_{k-2r,\bar{\rho}_{L}}
with
k:=-2\sigma_{0}
, define

(3.3.4)

\Phi_{L}^{r}(z,h,f):=r!\sum_{m>0,~\mu\in L^{\prime}/L}c(m,\mu)m^{r}\Phi_{m,\mu}(z,h,\sigma_{0}+1+r)

to be the associated higher Green function.
Following from the work of Borcherds
[
6
]
and generalization by Bruinier
[
8
]
(also see
[
35
,
12
]
), the function
\Phi_{L}^{r}
has the following integral representation

(3.3.5)

\Phi_{L}^{r}(z,h,f)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\langle\tilde{R}^{r}_{\tau}f(\tau),\overline{\Theta_{L}(\tau,z,h)}\rangle d\mu(\tau)=(-1)^{r}\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\langle f(\tau),\overline{\tilde{R}^{r}_{\tau}\Theta_{L}(\tau,z,h)}\rangle d\mu(\tau),

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
is the truncated fundamental domain of
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\backslash\mathbb{H}
at height
T>1
.

4.
Real-analytic Hilbert Modular Forms and Algebraicity of Pairing

In this section, we will prove the existence of certain real-analytic Hilbert modular forms by generalizing the proof of Theorem 3.7 in
[
13
]
,
and give some results concerning their Fourier coefficients.
The notations
F,D,\mathcal{O},\mathfrak{d},d
are the same as in section
2
.

4.1.
Certain real-analytic Hilbert modular forms

Let
\rho=\rho_{L}
be a Weil representation,

Therefore, we have

\sum_{(z_{0},h)\in Z(W_{\mathbb{Q}})}\Phi_{L}^{r}(z_{0},h,f)=\frac{1}{c^{\prime}}\log|a|

1.3

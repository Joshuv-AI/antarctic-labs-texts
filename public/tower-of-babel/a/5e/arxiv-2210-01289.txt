The boundedness of commutators of sublinear operators on Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable exponent
CHENGLONG FANG, YINGYING WEI AND JING ZHANG

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, the authors first discuss the characterization of Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
with variable exponent via two families of operators. By this characterization, the authors prove that the Lipschitz commutators of sublinear operators is bounded from Herz spaces with variable exponent to Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable exponent. As an application, the corresponding boundedness estimates for the commutators of maximal operator, Riesz potential operator and Calderón-Zygmund operator are established.

Key words
: commutator; sublinear operator; Lipschitz spaces; Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces; variable exponent

Introduction and statement of main result

Given a locally integrable function
b
, the commutator
[b,T]
is defined by

[b,T]f=bTf-T(bf)

for suitable functions
f
. In 1995, Paluszynski
[
14
]
proved that
b
belongs to the Lipschitz spaces if and only if the commutator
[b,T]
is bounded from Lebesgue spaces to Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, where
T
is a Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator. As we know, the Herz spaces is the Lebesgue spaces with power weights
|x|^{\alpha}
. In recent years, the Herz-type spaces have been paid more and more attention; see, for example, (
[
5
]
-
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
-
[
19
]
) and references therein. More recently, replacing Triebel-Lizorkin spaces by Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces in the results of Paluszynski,
Fang and Zhou
[
5
]
obtained that
[b,T]
is bounded from Herz spaces to Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces,
where
b
belongs to the Lipschitz spaces and
T
is a sublinear operators.
Inspired by
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
, in this article,
the authors show the boundedness of Lipschitz commutators of sublinear operators on Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable exponent.
To this ends, the authors discuss the characterizations of the Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable exponent.

To state our main results, we first recall some definitions and notations.

Definition 1.1
.

For
0<\beta<1
, the Lipschitz spaces
\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}
is the space of functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}=\sup_{x,h\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~h\neq 0}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)|}{|h|^{\beta}}<\infty.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\Omega|>0
. For a measurable function
p(\cdot):\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty)
,
the variable exponent Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is defined by

L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega):=\left\{f~is~measurable:\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)<\infty~for~some~constant~\lambda>0\right\},

where

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{p(x)}dx.

The norm of
f
in the variable exponent Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is denoted by

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\}.

Then
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)}
. Denote

p^{-}:=ess\inf\{p(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\quad\text{and}\quad p^{+}:=ess\sup\{p(x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

Throughout the whole article, the set
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
p(\cdot)
satisfying
p^{-}>1
and
p^{+}<\infty
.

Given a locally integrable function
f
, the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
is defined by

\displaystyle Mf(x):=\sup_{r>0}r^{-n}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.1)

Here and in the future,

B(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\}.

Let
\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying the condition that
M
is bounded on
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
It is well known that if
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equivalent to
p^{\prime}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
1/p^{\prime}(\cdot)=1-1/p(\cdot)
.
When
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, Izuki
[
7
]
proved that there exist
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
depending only on
p(\cdot)
and

such that for ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all measurable subsets
S\subset B
,

\frac{\|\chi_{S}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\leq C\left(\frac{|S|}{|B|}\right)^{\delta_{1}},\ \frac{\|\chi_{S}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\leq C\left(\frac{|S|}{|B|}\right)^{\delta_{2}}.

(1.2)

Now we recall the definitions of Herz spaces with variable exponents and Herz Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with variable exponents.
Set
B_{k}=B(0,2^{k})=\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<2^{k}}\}
,
E_{k}=B_{k}\backslash B_{k-1}
and
\chi_{k}=\chi_{E_{k}}
for

.
For

, define

\tilde{\chi}_{m}=\begin{cases}\chi_{E_{m}},\ &m\geq 1,\\
\chi_{B_{0}},\ &m=0.\end{cases}

Definition 1.3
.

Let

,

(i)

The homogeneous Herz spaces with variable exponents

\|f\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left\{\sum^{\infty}_{l=-\infty}(2^{\alpha l}\|f\chi_{l}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{q}\right\}^{1/q}.

(ii)

The non-homogeneous Herz spaces with variable exponents

Definition 1.4
.

Let

,

3.1

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{1}}}\lesssim\sup_{y\in 3Q}|b(y)-b_{3Q}|\int_{3Q}|Tf(y)|dy\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}M(Tf)(x)\quad\text{for all}\ x\in 3Q.

To estimate
\rm{F_{2}}
, we consider the following two cases:
b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}\geq 0
and
b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}<0
for
y\in 3Q
, where

C_{b,f,Q}:=\sup_{y\in 3Q}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(3Q)}\frac{|b(z)-b_{3Q}||f(z)|}{|y-z|^{n}}dz.

Case 1:  Estimation of
\rm{F_{2}}
in the case
b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}\geq 0
with
y\in 3Q
.
By the definition of sublinear operator in (
1.3
),
we deduce

\displaystyle b_{3Q}Tf(y)

\displaystyle\leq T(b_{3Q}f)(y)

\displaystyle\leq T((b_{3Q}-b)(f_{1}+f_{2}))(y)+T(bf)(y)

\displaystyle\leq T((b_{3Q}-b)f_{1})(y)+T((b_{3Q}-b)f_{2})(y)+T(bf)(y)

\displaystyle\leq T((b_{3Q}-b)f_{1})(y)+C_{b,f,Q}+T(bf)(y),

where the last estimate used the condition (
1.4
). So,

\displaystyle 0<b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}\leq T((b_{3Q}-b)f_{1})(y).

This and the fact
C_{b,f,Q}>C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})
by terms of (
3.1
) allow that

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{3Q}|b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}+C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})|dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{3Q}|b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}|dy+\int_{3Q}|C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})|dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{3Q}|T((b-b_{3Q})f_{1})(y)|dy+|3Q|(C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q}))

(3.3)

\displaystyle=:{\rm{F_{21}}+\rm{F_{22}}}.

To estimate
\rm{F_{21}}
, using Hölder’s inequality and the boundedness of
T
on
L^{p(\cdot)}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
we see that for any
x\in 3Q
and for some
t
satisfying
1<t<p^{-},

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{21}}}

\displaystyle\leq|3Q|^{1-1/t}\left(\int_{3Q}|T((b-b_{Q})f_{1})(y)|^{t}dy\right)^{1/t}

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|^{1-1/t}\left(\int_{3Q}|(b-b_{Q})f(y)|^{t}dy\right)^{1/t}

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|\sup_{y\in Q}|b(y)-b_{Q}|\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|^{t}dy\right)^{1/t}

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}(M(|f|^{t}))^{1/t}(x).

To estimate
\rm{F_{22}}
, we need the following well known fact. Let
Q^{\ast}\subset Q
. Then

|b_{Q^{\ast}}-b_{Q}|\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}|Q|^{\beta/n}.

This and
C_{b,f,Q}>C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})>C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})/3>0
imply that

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{22}}}

\displaystyle\leq|3Q|(C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})/3)

\displaystyle=|3Q|\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\int_{3^{k}Q\backslash 3^{k-1}Q}\sup_{y\in 3Q}\left(\frac{1}{(|y-z|)^{n}}-\frac{1}{(|x_{Q}-z|)^{n}}\right)|b(z)-b_{3Q}||f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\int_{3^{k}Q\backslash 3^{k-1}Q}\sup_{y\in 3Q}\frac{|y-x_{Q}|^{n}}{|x_{Q}-z|^{2n}}(|b(z)-b_{3^{k}Q}|+|b_{3^{k}Q}-b_{3Q}|)|f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{|3Q|}{|3^{k}Q|^{2}}|3^{k}Q|^{\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\int_{3^{k}Q}|f(z)|dz

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}3^{(k-1)(\beta-1)}M(f)(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}M(f)(x).

for all
x\in 3^{k}Q
with
k\geq 2
. Therefore, combing the estimates of
\rm{F_{21}}
and
\rm{F_{22}}
, we obtain that

{\rm{F_{2}}}\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}M(f)(x)\quad\text{for all}\ x\in 3Q.

Case 2:  Estimation of
\rm{F_{2}}
in the case
b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}<0
with
y\in 3Q
.

According to the definition of sublinear operator in (
1.3
) and the condition (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle|b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}|

\displaystyle=C_{b,f,Q}+T(bf)(y)-b_{3Q}Tf(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{b,f,Q}+\left(T(bf)(y)-Tf(y)\sup_{Q}b_{3Q}\right)+\left(Tf(y)\sup_{Q}b_{3Q}-b_{3Q}Tf(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{b,f,Q}+\left(T\left(\sup_{Q}|b-b_{3Q}|f\right)(y)\right)+|Tf(y)|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|3Q|}\int_{3Q}b(z)-b_{3Q}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{b,f,Q}+\sup_{Q}\sup_{y\in 3Q}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(3Q)}\frac{|b(z)-b_{3Q}||f(z)|}{|y-z|}dz+|3Q|^{\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}|Tf(y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2\sup_{Q}C_{b,f,Q}+|3Q|^{\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}|Tf(y)|.

This implies that

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{3Q}|b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}+C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})|dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{3Q}|b_{3Q}Tf(y)-T(bf)(y)-C_{b,f,Q}|dy+\int_{3Q}|C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|2\sup_{Q}C_{b,f,Q}+|3Q||3Q|^{\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}|Tf(y)|+|3Q|(C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q}))

\displaystyle\lesssim|3Q|(3\sup_{Q}C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q}))+|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}|Tf(y)|.

(3.4)

where in the second estimate we used the fact
C_{b,f,Q}>C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q})
by terms of (
3.1
).
Note that we repeat the process of estimation of
{\rm{F_{22}}}
and obtain

|3Q|(3\sup_{Q}C_{b,f,Q}-C_{b,f,Q}(x_{Q}))\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}M(f)(x).

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{2}}}\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}(M(f)(x)+|Tf(y)|)\quad\text{for}\ x,y\in 3Q.

This ends the estimate of
Case 2
.

Summarizing all the estimates of
Case 1
and
Case 2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle{\rm{F_{2}}}\lesssim|3Q|^{1+\beta/n}\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\left(M(f)(x)+(M(|f|^{t}))^{1/t}(x)+|Tf(y)|\right)\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in 3Q.

This and the estimate of
{\rm F}_{1}
allow that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{Q}|[b,T]f-([b,T]f)_{Q}|\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\left(M(f)(x)+(M(|f|^{t}))^{1/t}(x)+|Tf(y)|\right)\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in 3Q.

Taking the supremum over all
Q
such that
x\in Q
on both sides, it follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle\|[b,T]f\|_{K^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\left(\|Mf\|_{K^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|(M(|f|^{t}))^{1/t}\|_{K^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|Tf\|_{K^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)

and

\displaystyle\|[b,T]f\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\left(\|Mf\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|(M(|f|^{t}))^{1/t}\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|Tf\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right).

Further, by Lemma 3.2 and the facts that

and

.
For
g\in L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
let
g_{1}=g\chi_{3Q}
and
g_{2}=g-g_{1}
.
From (
3.1
), it follows that there exists
x_{Q}\in Q
such that

\displaystyle C_{b,g,Q}(x_{Q}):=3\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(3Q)}\frac{|b(z)-b_{3Q}||g(z)|}{|x_{Q}-z|^{n-\lambda}}dz\leq\sup_{y\in 3Q}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\backslash(3Q)}\frac{|b(z)-b_{3Q}||g(z)|}{|y-z|^{n-\lambda}}dz=:C_{b,g,Q}.

(4.2)

Using (
2.5
), we repeat the process of (
3
) and obtain

\displaystyle\|[b,T_{\lambda}](g)\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\sim\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{Q}|[b,T_{\lambda}](g)(y)-([b,T_{\lambda}](g))_{Q}|dy\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{Q}|[b,T_{\lambda}](g)(y)-C_{b,g,Q}(x_{Q})|dy\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{3Q}|(b(y)-b_{3Q})Tg(y)|dy\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{3Q}|b_{3Q}Tg(y)-T(gf)(y)-C_{b,g,Q}(x_{Q})|dy\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle=:\rm{J_{1}+J_{2}},

To estimate
\rm{J_{1}}
, by Lemma
3.1
, we deduce that for all
x\in Q
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{Q}|(b(y)-b_{Q})T_{\lambda}(g)(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{\beta/n}}\sup_{y\in Q}|(b(y)-b_{Q})|\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\lambda}(g)(y)|dy\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}M(T_{\lambda}(g))(x).

This and Theorem
4.2
imply that

{\rm{J}}_{1}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|T_{\lambda}(g)\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Now, we consider
{\rm{J}}_{2}
. According to (
3
) and (
3
) in Theorem
1.5
, by Theorem
4.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle{\rm{J}}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n}}\int_{Q}|T_{\lambda}((b-b_{Q})g_{1})(y)|dy\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}}+\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|T_{\lambda}(g)\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{\beta/n}}3C_{b,g,Q}-C_{b,g,Q}(x_{Q})\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n+\lambda/n}}\int_{Q}|T_{\lambda}((b-b_{Q})g_{1})(y)|dy\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left\|\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{\beta/n+\lambda/n}}3C_{b,g,Q}-C_{b,g,Q}(x_{Q})\right\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=:{\rm{J}}_{21}+{\rm{J}}_{22}+\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Below, we estimate
{\rm{J}}_{21}
. Chooser
r
and
\bar{r}
such that
1<r<p_{1}^{-}
and
(1/r-1/\bar{r})=(\lambda/n)
. Such
\bar{r}
exists, since
r<p_{1}^{-}<n/\lambda
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n+\lambda/n}}\int_{Q}|T_{\lambda}((b-b_{Q})g_{1})(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|Q|^{1+\beta/n+\lambda/n}}\|T_{\lambda}((b-b_{Q})g_{1})\|_{\bar{r}}|Q|^{1/\bar{r}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{-1-(\lambda+\beta)/n+1-1/\bar{r}}\|(b-b_{Q})g\|_{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}(M(|g|^{r}))^{1/r}.

So, by the boundedness of
M
from
\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
we obtain
{\rm{J_{21}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
Notice that if
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
, then

\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This implies

{\rm{J}}_{21}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

To show
\rm{J_{22}}
, we repeat this derivation of
\rm{F_{22}}
in Theorem
1.5
and obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{|Q|^{\beta/n+\lambda/n}}3C_{b,g,Q}-C_{b,g,Q}(x_{Q})\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}M(g)(x).

Similar to the finally estimate of
\rm{J_{21}}
, we also have

{\rm{J_{22}}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}}\|g\|_{\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Summarizing the estimates of
{\rm J_{1}}
and
{\rm J_{2}}
, we conclude that (i) of Theorem
1.6
holds.
To verify (ii) of Theorem
1.6
, by (
2.1
) and

K^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq K^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{for all}\ q_{1}\leq q_{2},

we repeat the process of proof of (i) and obtain (ii).
This ends the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
∎

5
Some classical operators

It is worth that we assumed the boundedness of sublinear operators on the variable exponent Lebesgue space in the discussion of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
. In order to ensure that some classical operators satisfy this fact, we introduce the following
\log
-Hölder continuity condition.

Definition 5.1
.

(i)

A function
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is locally
\log
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there exists
c_{1}>0
such that

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\log(e+1/|x-y|)}\quad\text{for all}\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(ii)

A function
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\log
-Hölder continuous at the origin (or has a
\log
decay at the origin) if there exists
c_{2}>0
such that

|p(x)-p(0)|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\log(e+1/|x|)}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The notation
\mathscr{P}^{\log}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is used for all exponents
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is
\log
-Hölder continuous at the origin.

(iii)

A function
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\log
-Hölder continuous at infinity (or has a
\log
decay at infinity)
if there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{c_{3}}{\log(e+|x|)}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
p_{\infty}=\lim_{|x|\to\infty}p(x)
.
The notation
\mathscr{P}^{\log}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is used for all exponents
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

which is
\log
-Hölder continuous at infinity.
By
\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we define the set of all exponents
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is locally
\log
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and has a
\log
decay at infinity.

Obviously, we have
\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset(\mathscr{P}^{\log}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathscr{P}^{\log}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
Note that
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
p^{\prime}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
where
1/p^{\prime}(\cdot)=1-1/p(\cdot)
.
If
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
is bounded on
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(see
[
4
, Theorem 4.3.8]
), which further implies
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Given a locally integrable function
K
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, suppose that the Fourier transform of
K
is bounded,

|K(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla K(x)|\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{n+1}}\quad\text{for all}\ x\neq 0.

Then the classical Calderón-Zygmund singular integral operator
T
is defined by

\displaystyle Tf(x)=K\ast f(x)\quad\text{for all}\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

(5.1)

Based on the above definition, the following lemma comes from
[
2
, Corollary 2.5]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
T
be as in (
5.1
). Then
T
is bounded on
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

By the condition
|K(x)|\leq C/(|x|^{n})
for
x\neq 0,
we deduce that
T
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).
In addition, it is obvious that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).
Thus, the following corollary follows from Theorem
1.5
and Lemma
5.2
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\beta<1
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
satisfy (
1.2
) and
-n\delta_{1}<\alpha<n\delta_{2}
.
Assume that
b\in\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}
and
T
is as in (
5.1
). Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
[b,T]
and
[b,M]
are bounded from
\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\dot{K}^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
[b,T]
and
[b,M]
are bounded from
K^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
K^{\alpha,q}_{p(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For any
0<\lambda<n
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, the Riesz potential operator
I_{\lambda}
is defined by

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}f(x)=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\lambda}}dy.

Then the Riesz potential operator
I_{\lambda}
satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.5
).
The following lemma about the boundedness of
I_{\lambda}
on the variable exponent Lebesgue space comes from
[
4
, Theorem 6.1.9]
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0<\lambda<n
,
p_{1}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
1/p_{2}(\cdot)=1/p_{1}(\cdot)-\lambda/n
.
If
1<p_{1}^{-}\leq p_{1}^{+}<n/\lambda
, then
I_{\lambda}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p_{2}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Correspondingly, for any
0<\lambda<n
, the fractional maximal function
M_{\lambda}
is defined by

\displaystyle M_{\lambda}f(x):=\sup_{r>0}r^{\lambda-n}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy\quad\text{for all}\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

Then the fractional maximal function
M_{\lambda}
also satisfies (
1.3
) and (
1.5
). Moreover, we have

M_{\lambda}(f)\lesssim I_{\lambda}(|f|).

This, along with Theorem
1.6
and Lemma
5.4
, yields the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
,
1<p_{1}^{-}\leq p_{1}^{+}<n/\lambda
and
1/p_{2}(\cdot)=1/p_{1}(\cdot)-\lambda/n
. Suppose that
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in(0,1)
satisfy (
1.2
) and
-n\delta_{1}<\alpha<n\delta_{2}
.
Assume further that
b\in\dot{\Lambda}_{\beta}
with
0<\beta<1
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If
p_{1}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
[b,I_{\lambda}]
and
[b,M_{\lambda}]
are bounded
from
\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to
\dot{K}^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
p_{1}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
[b,I_{\lambda}]
and
[b,M_{\lambda}]
are bounded
from
K^{\alpha,q_{1}}_{p_{1}(\cdot)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

to
K^{\alpha,q_{2}}_{p_{2}(\cdot)}\dot{F}^{\beta}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind Thanks: * is corresponding author
Taekyun Kim 1,∗, Dae San Kim 2,∗, Hye Kyung Kim 3,∗

Abstract
. The multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind, the unsigned multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind, the multi-Lah numbers and the multi-Bernoulli numbers are all defined with the help of the multiple logarithm, and generalize respectively the Stirling numbers of the second kind, the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind, the unsigned Lah numbers and the higher-order Bernoulli numbers . The aim of this paper is to introduce the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind and to find several identities involving those four numbers defined by means of the multiple logarithm and some other special numbers.

11B68; 11B73; 11B83

1.
Introduction

The Stirling number of the second kind
{n\brace r}
counts the number of ways to partition a set with
n
elements into
r
non-empty subsets. These numbers are generalized to the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}
(see (
18
)) which reduce to the Stirling numbers of the second kind for
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1)
. Indeed,
{n\brace 1,1,\cdots,1}={n\brace r}
.

On the other hand, the unsigned Stirling number of the first kind
{n\brack r}
enumerates the number of permutations of a set with
n
elements which are products of
r
disjoint cycles. These numbers are generalized to the unsigned multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind
{n\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}
(see (
28
)) which become the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind for
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1)
. Thus,
{n\brack 1,1,\cdots,1}={n\brack r}
.

The unsigned Lah numbers
L(n,k)
counts the number of ways of a set of
n
elements can be partitioned into
k
nonempty linearly ordered subsets. These numbers are generalized to the multi-Lah numbers
L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)
(see (
8
)) which boil down to the unsigned Lah numbers for
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1)
. Indeed,
L^{(1,1,\dots,1)}(n,r)=L(n,r).

In addition, the multi-Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}
(see (
7
)) generalize the Bernoulli numbers of order
r
. In fact, we see that
B_{n}^{(1,1,\dots,1)}=\frac{1}{r!}(-1)^{n}B_{n}^{(r)}
.

The common feature of those four numbers is that they are all defined with the help of the multiple logarithm
\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(z)
(see (
4
)), which reduce to the poly-logarithm
\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1}}(z)
, for
r=1
.

The aim of this paper is to introduce the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind and to derive several identities involving those four numbers defined by means of the multiple logarithm and some other numbers.
In more detail, in Theorem 3 we derive an identity involving the multi-Bernoulli numbers, the Stirling numbers of the second kind, Bell polynomials and the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind. In Theorem 4, we express the multi-Stirling number of the second kind in terms of the unsigned multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind. We find in Theorem 5 an identity involving the Stirling numbers of the second kind, the multi-Lah numbers, the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind and the Fubini polynomials of order
r
. We deduce in Theorem 6 an identity involving the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind, the signed Stirling numbers of the first kind and the multi-Lah numbers. Finally, in Theorem 7 we show an identity involving the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind, the signed Stirling numbers of the first kind, the Stirling numbers of the second kind and the multi-Bernoulli numbers.
For the rest of this section, we recall the necessary facts that will be needed throughout this paper.

We recall that the falling factorial sequence is given by

(1)

\begin{split}(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ \ (n\geq 1),\quad(\text{see [1-5,7-16,18,19]}).\end{split}

It is well known that the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(2)

\begin{split}x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{5}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

From (
2
), we note that the generating function of the Stirling numbers of the second kind is given by

(3)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad\ (\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{16}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

For integers
k_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq r)
, the multiple logarithm (of index
(k_{1},\ k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})
) is given by

(4)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(t)=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{8,10}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

If
r=1,\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1}}(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m^{k_{1}}}
is the polylogarithm.
By (
4
), we easily get

(5)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}-1}(t),\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{9,10,15}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

From (
5
), we have

(6)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}(t)&=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=m_{r-1}+1}^{\infty}t^{m_{r}}\\
&=\frac{1}{1-t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1}}(t).\end{split}

In 2000, Kim-Kim introduced the multi-Bernoulli numbers of index
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
which are defined by

(7)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{9}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

Note that
B_{n}^{(\overbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}^{r-\text{times}})}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{r!}B_{n}^{(r)}=\frac{B_{n}^{(r)}(r)}{r!}
, where
B_{n}^{(r)}(x)
are the Bernoulli polynomials of order
r
given by

\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\bigg)^{r}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\text{see [9-16]}),\end{split}

and
B_{n}^{(r)}=B_{n}^{(r)}(0)
are the Bernoulli numbers of order
r
.

Recently, Kim-Kim-Kim-Lee-Park introduced the multi-Lah numbers defined by

(8)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-t)^{r}}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{8}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

Note that

(9)

\begin{split}L^{(\overbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}^{r-\text{times}})}(n,r)=L(n,r),\quad(n,r\geq 0),\end{split}

where
L(n,r)
are the unsigned Lah numbers given by

(10)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{r!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{5,8,16}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

Y. Ma et al also studied the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

(11)

\begin{split}(-1)^{r}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(-t)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}S^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{15}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

Note that
S^{(\overbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}^{r-\text{times}})}=S_{1}(n,r)
, where
S_{1}(n,r)
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind given by

(12)

\begin{split}(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{5, 12}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

The unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(13)

\begin{split}\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{5, 11}{}{}]}}),\end{split}

where
\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\ (n\geq 1)
.
From (
12
) and (
13
), we note that

(14)

\begin{split}{n\brack k}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k),\quad(n,k\geq 0),\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{5, 11}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

Now, we recall that the Fubini polynomials of order
r
are given by

(15)

\begin{split}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{12}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

From (
15
), we note that

(16)

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}&=\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+k-1}{k}x^{k}(e^{t}-1)^{k}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\binom{r+k-1}{k}k!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{r+k-1}{k}{n\brace k}k!x^{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Thus, by (
16
), we get

(17)

\begin{split}F_{n}^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}k!\binom{r+k-1}{k}x^{k},\quad(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{12}{}{}]}}).\end{split}

2.
Multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind

In view of (
3
), we may consider the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind defined by

(18)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Now, we observe that

(19)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}&\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{(1-e^{1-e^{t}})^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}m_{r}}\\
&=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\frac{e^{t}e^{1-e^{t}}}{1-e^{1-e^{t}}}\sum_{m_{r}=m_{r-1}+1}^{\infty}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})^{m_{r}}\\
&=e^{t}\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{(1-e^{1-e^{t}})^{m_{r-1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}=e^{t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}}).\end{split}

Thus, by (
19
), we get

(20)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{1,1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=e^{t}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=-e^{t}\log(e^{1-e^{t}})=e^{t}(e^{t}-1).\end{split}

From (
20
), we note that

(21)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{1,1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Li}_{1,1}(1-e^{1-e^{x}})dx=\int_{0}^{t}e^{x}(e^{x}-1)dx=\frac{1}{2!}(e^{t}-1)^{2}.\end{split}

By (
19
) and (
20
), we get

(22)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{1,1,1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})&=\int_{0}^{t}e^{x}\mathrm{Li}_{1,1}(1-e^{1-e^{x}})dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2!}\int_{0}^{t}e^{x}(e^{x}-1)^{2}dx=\frac{1}{3!}(e^{t}-1)^{3}.\end{split}

Continuing this process, we have

(23)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{r-\text{times}}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=\frac{1}{r!}(e^{t}-1)^{r}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace r}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
18
) and (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n,r\geq 0
with
n\geq r
, we have

\begin{split}{n\brace\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{r-\text{times}}}={n\brace r}.\end{split}

From (
19
), we note that

(24)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}&\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})=e^{t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})\\
&=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=r-1}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1}}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r-1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r-1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1}}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
18
), we get

(25)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})&=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}\frac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r-1}^{\infty}{n+1\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
n\geq 0
and
r\geq 0
with
n+1\geq r
, we have

\begin{split}{n+1\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1}=\sum_{m=r-1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r-1}}.\end{split}

Replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (
7
), we get

(26)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{(1-e^{1-e^{t}})^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})}\frac{1}{m!}(e^{t}-1)^{m}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}{n\brace m}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}{n\brace m}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
18
), we get

(27)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{(1-e^{1-e^{t}})^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})\\
&=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+l-1}{l}e^{l(1-e^{t})}\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+l-1}{l}\phi_{j}(-l)\bigg)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n-r}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+l-1}{l}\phi_{j}(-l)\binom{n}{j}{n-j\brace k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\end{split}

where
\phi_{n}(x)
are Bell polynomials given by

\begin{split}e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
26
) and (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
n,r\geq 0
with
n\geq r
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{m=0}^{n}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}{n\brace m}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-r}\binom{r+l-1}{l}\phi_{j}(-l)\binom{n}{j}{n-j\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}.\end{split}

In addition, for
0\leq n<r
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{m=0}^{n}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}{n\brace m}=0.\end{split}

In view of (
11
) and (
14
), we may consider the unsigned multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

(28)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(t)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Note that

(29)

\begin{split}{n\brack\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{r-\text{times}}}={n\brack r},\quad(n,r\geq 0).\end{split}

By replacing
t
by
1-e^{1-e^{t}}
in (
28
), we set

(30)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})&=\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}}\frac{1}{m!}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})^{m}\\
&=\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(-1)^{m}\sum_{l=m}^{\infty}{l\brace m}\frac{1}{l!}(1-e^{t})^{l}\\
&=\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{l}(-1)^{l-m}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}{l\brace m}\bigg)\frac{1}{l!}(e^{t}-1)^{l}\\
&=\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{l}(-1)^{l-m}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}{l\brace m}\bigg)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}{n\brace l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=r}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l}(-1)^{l-m}{n\brace l}{l\brace m}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
18
) and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, we have

\begin{split}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}=\sum_{l=r}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l}(-1)^{l-m}{n\brace l}{l\brace m}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}.\end{split}

Replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (
8
). Then we have

(31)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})}{(2-e^{t})^{r}}&=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(k,r)\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}\\
&=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(k,r)\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=r}^{n}{n\brace k}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(k,r)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
15
), we get

(32)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{(2-e^{t})^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{1-e^{t}})&=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l}^{(r)}(1)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=r}^{n}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\binom{n}{k}F_{n-k}^{(r)}(1)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
31
) and (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{k=r}^{n}{n\brace k}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(k,r)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\binom{n}{k}F_{n-k}^{(r)}(1).\end{split}

Replacing
t
by
\log(1+t)
in (
18
), we get

(33)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})&=\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{1}{m!}(\log(1+t))^{m}\\
&=\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{n}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}S_{1}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
8
), we get

(34)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})&=\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-t)^{r}}(1-t)^{r}\\
&=\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}l^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(m,r)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{r}{l}(-1)^{l}t^{l}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{n}\frac{1}{m!}l^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(m,r)\binom{r}{n-m}(-1)^{n-m}\bigg)t^{n}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
33
) and (
34
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, we have

(35)

\begin{split}\sum_{m=r}^{n}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}S_{1}(n,m)=\sum_{m=r}^{n}\frac{n!}{m!}l^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}(m,r)\binom{r}{n-m}(-1)^{n-m}.\end{split}

Replacing
t
by
\log(1+t)
in (
18
), we get

(36)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})&=\sum_{m=r}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\frac{1}{m!}(\log(1+t))^{m}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=r}^{n}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}S_{1}(n,m)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by (
7
), we get

(37)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})&=\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}(1-e^{-t})^{r}\\
&=\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)r!(-1)^{r}\frac{1}{r!}(e^{-t}-1)^{r}\\
&=\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\bigg)r!(-1)^{r}\sum_{j=r}^{\infty}{j\brace r}(-1)^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\\
&=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=r}^{n}{j\brace r}(-1)^{j-r}r!\binom{n}{j}B_{n-j}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{r})}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
36
) and (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7

Conclusion

With the help of the multiple logarithm we introduced the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind which generalize the Stirling numbers of the second kind. Similarly, the multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind, the multi-Lah numbers and the multi-Bernoulli numbers, which respectively generalize the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind, the unsigned Lah numbers and the higher-order Bernoulli numbers, are defined by means of the multiple logarithm. In this paper, we derived several identities involving those four numbers defined by means of multiple logarithm and some other numbers.

The unsigned Lah numbers found their recent applications to the steganography method in telecommunication

and the perturbative description of chromatic dispersion in optics
[

]
. So it would be an interesting research problem to find some applications of the multi-Lah numbers.

As it has been, we would like to continue to find some special numbers and their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

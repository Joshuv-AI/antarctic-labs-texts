Introduction

For a positive integer
n
, let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group on

[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
A partition
\lambda
of
n

\lambda\vdash n
and
it is written using the exponential notation with multiplicities of parts written as exponents. Thus, if
i
appears
n_{i}
times in

\lambda=1^{n_{1}}2^{n_{2}}3^{n_{3}}\ldots
and
n_{1}+2n_{2}+3n_{3}+\cdots=n.

For
\lambda\vdash n
, let

\chi_{\lambda}
denote the corresponding irreducible character of

\mathfrak{S}_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}
, the set of complex numbers,
(see the textbook by Sagan
[
17
]
as a
reference for the theory of characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}

Let

where
{\mathbb{M}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
represents the set of all
n\times n
matrices with complex entries.
Then, the normalized immanant function of

associated with
\lambda\vdash n
, denoted

is defined as

(1)

\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(A)=\dfrac{1}{\chi_{\lambda}(\mathsf{id})}\sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\chi_{\lambda}(\psi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i,\psi(i)},

where
\chi_{\lambda}(\mathsf{id})
is the dimension of the
irreducible representation indexed by
\lambda
. The expression
\chi_{\lambda}(\mathsf{id})\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(A)

from Equation (
1
) is called the
immanant function of

and it is denoted as
\mathrm{imm}_{\lambda}(A)
.
As
\chi_{1^{n}}(\mathsf{id})=1=\chi_{n}(\mathsf{id})
, we see that

\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{1^{n}}(A)=\det(A)

and
\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{n}(A)=\mathrm{perm}(A)
, where
\det(A)
and
\mathrm{perm}(A)

determinant and the permanent of

Let

be the set of all

positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix over
\mathbb{C}
. Then,
Schur in
[
19

showed that

for all

.
Towards getting the maximum element in this set, a popular conjecture known as the “permanental dominance conjecture” was given by

[
10

\mathrm{perm}(A)=\max\ \{\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(A):\lambda\vdash n\}
for all

.
This is still open. In this paper, we give a proof of
this conjecture for the Laplacian matrix of a
bipartite graph, see Theorem
8
.

For a simple graph
G
with vertex set
[n]
,
its Laplacian matrix

is defined by

L_{G}=D-A(G)
, where

and

Indexed by
\lambda\vdash n
, the Laplacian immanantal polynomial of

,
denoted

is defined as
\phi_{\lambda}(L_{G},x)=\mathrm{imm}_{\lambda}(xI-L_{G})
.
For

, let
b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})
be the coefficient of

(-1)^{r}x^{n-r}
in
\phi_{\lambda}(L_{G},x)
, that is

(2)

\phi_{\lambda}(L_{G},x)=\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^{r}b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})x^{n-r}

Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices with Laplacian matrix
L_{T}
.
Let

and

be the star tree and the path tree on

When

]

(3)

b_{1^{n},r}(L_{S_{n}})\leq b_{1^{n},r}(L_{T})\leq b_{1^{n},r}(L_{P_{n}})\mbox{ for }r=0,1,2,\ldots,n.

2.1.

Towards defining the Kelmans transformation, we need the

2.2.
Generalized graph shift

Inspired by Kelmans transformation and

To define

Definition 3

Let

by moving all neighbours of

It is worth pointing out the notion of vertex orientation that

In this paper, enumeration of vertex orientations will be used to express each
coefficient of the Laplacian immanantal polynomial of a bipartite graph as a sum of
non-negative terms. From
[
3
,
14
]
we recall the following definition of vertex orientation of a graph.

Definition 4
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices.
In a vertex orientation
O

G

pointing away from
v
along one of its incidence edges.
If
\deg(v)
denotes the degree of
v\in G
then there are
\deg(v)
choices of assigning an arrow to
v
in
G
. Such an assignment of arrows to each vertex of

G
is said to be a vertex orientation. Throughout this paper,
O(v)=u

denotes the arrow of
v
assigned on the edge
(v,u)
, away from
v
and
towards
u
. It is illustrated in Figure
4

From the above definition of vertex orientation in a graph
G
, it is easy to check that each edge in
G
can have atmost two arrows on it.
Let
G
be a bipartite graph with
n
vertices and let
O
be a vertex orientation in
G

Since
G
is a bipartite graph, no directed cycle with odd length can

From Section

of a bipartite graph.

Theorem 8

(Lieb’s Conjecture for bipartite graphs)

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with
n
vertices and let

be its Laplacian matrix.
Then for all
\lambda\vdash n

\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(L_{G})\leq\mathrm{perm}(L_{G}).

Proof.

From Lemma

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(L_{G})=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{imm}_{\lambda}(L_{G})}{\chi_{\lambda}(\mathsf{id})}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{PO}_{G}(n)}a_{G}(\mu)\sum_{\nu\in P(n)}\frac{\chi_{\lambda}(\nu)}{\chi_{\lambda}(\mathsf{id})}\binom{\mu}{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mu\in P(n)}a_{G}(\mu)\sum_{\nu\in P(n)}\left|\frac{\chi_{\lambda}(\nu)}{\chi_{\lambda}(\mathsf{id})}\right|\binom{\mu}{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mu\in P(n)}a_{G}(\mu)\sum_{\nu\in P(n)}\binom{\mu}{\nu}=\mathrm{perm}(L_{G}).

The above identity follows from the fact that for each
\lambda\vdash n
and
for each permutation

Hence the proof is complete.

From Lemma

The important point to note
here is a combinatorial interpretation for each coefficient

in the Laplacian immanantal polynomial
\phi_{\lambda}(L_{G},x)

G

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with vertex set
[n]
and
let

L_{G}=(\ell_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be the Laplacian matrix of
G
.
As done in the proof of Lemma
7
in
[
3
]
,
B
-orientations will be used to express the coefficient

as a sum of non-negative terms.
Let
B\subseteq[n]
with
|B|=r
and let
G_{B}
be the subgraph induced by

G
on the set
B
. We orient each vertex
v
of
B
only to one of its neighbor
(which may or may not belong to
B
) in
G
. Thus each

has
\deg(v)
different choices for assigning an arrow to

Such vertex orientations are known as
B
-orientations in

For a fixed

with
|B|=r
, let
\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)
be the set of all
B
-orientations in

which have type
\mu

to be the number of
elements in
\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)
,
i.e.
,
a_{G,B}(\mu)=|\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)|
. Also define

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{G,r}(\mu)=\cup_{B\subseteq[n]\mbox{ with }|B|=r}\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)\mbox{ and }a_{G,r}(\mu)=|\mathcal{O}_{G,r}(\mu)|.
In other words, one can check that
a_{G,r}(\mu)=\sum_{B\subseteq[n]\mbox{ with }|B|=r}|\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)|.

Let
\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)
be the set of all partitions of
n
obtained from
B
-orientations in
G

G
is a bipartite graph, the induced subgraph
G_{B}

is also a bipartite graph, each
\mu\in\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)
has type
\mu=(1^{n_{1}},2^{n_{2}},4^{n_{4}},\ldots)
for some non-negative integers
n_{i}
.
In the proof of the following result, these terms are used to get a combinatorial interpretation for the coefficient
b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})
as a sum of non-negative integers.

Lemma 9
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph on
[n]
with the Laplacian matrix
L_{G}
. Then, for all
\lambda\vdash n
and
for
0\leq r\leq n
,

b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})=\sum_{\mu\in P(n)}a_{G,r}(\mu)\alpha_{\lambda,\mu}.

In particular,
b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})
is a non-negative integer for all
\lambda\vdash n
and for
r=0,1,\ldots,n.

Proof.

For a subset
B\subseteq[n]
with
|B|=r
, define

b_{\lambda,B}(L_{G})=\mathrm{imm}_{\lambda}\left[\begin{matrix}L_{G}[B|B]&0\\
0&I\end{matrix}\right],

where
L_{G}[B|B]
is a submatrix of
L_{G}
induced by the rows and columns
with indices in
B
and
I
is the
(n-r)\times(n-r)
identity matrix.
It is easy to check that
b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})=\sum\limits_{B\subseteq[n]\mbox{ with }|B|=r}b_{\lambda,B}(L_{G}).

We recall that
G
is a bipartite graph and the induced subgraph
G_{B}

is also a bipartite graph.
Thus it is natural to see that a permutation
\psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with cycle type
\mu
contributes in
b_{\lambda,B}(L_{G})
if and only if there is a
B
-orientation
O\in\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)
and
\psi
fixes
[n]-B
.
For a given
\nu=(1^{m_{1}},2^{m_{2}},4^{m_{4}},\ldots)\vdash n
,
define the set

S_{\nu}^{B}:=\{(\psi,O):\prod_{i\in B}\ell_{i,\psi(i)}\neq 0,\mathrm{Type}(\psi)=\nu,\psi
fixes
[n]-B
and
O\in\mathcal{O}_{G,B}(\mu)
with
\mu=(1^{n_{1}},2^{n_{2}},4^{n_{4}},\ldots)\in\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)

such that

n_{i}\geq m_{i}
for all
i\geq 2\}
. For a given
\psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, cycles in
\psi
with size
2
represent bidirected edges in
O
and each cycle of
\psi

with size strictly greater than
2
gives a directed cycle in
O
.
Each even cycle and each fixed point

of

contribute

and
\deg(v)
in
\prod_{i\in B}\ell_{i,\psi(i)}

B
-orientation
O
of type
\mu\in\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)
such that
n_{i}\geq m_{i}
for
all
i\geq 2
. Therefore, the number of elements in
S_{\nu}^{B}
equals
to the sum of
\prod_{i\in B}\ell_{i,\psi(i)}
over all permutations which fix
[n]-B
and
whose cycle type is
\nu
.

We can also enumerate the set
S_{\nu}^{B}
in another way.
Take a
B
-orientation
O
of type

\mu=(1^{n_{1}},2^{n_{2}},4^{n_{4}},\ldots)\in\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)
with

for all

. Therefore we have

ways to pick

\binom{n_{4}}{m_{4}}
ways to pick

-cycles and so on.
So there are
\binom{n_{2}}{m_{2}}\binom{n_{4}}{m_{4}}\cdots=\binom{\mu}{\nu}

ways to construct the ordered pair

. Thus we get

|S_{\nu}^{B}|=\sum_{\mathrm{Type}(\psi)=\nu,\psi\mbox{ fixes }[n]-B}\prod_{i\in B}\ell_{i,\psi(i)}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)}\binom{\mu}{\nu}a_{G,B}(\mu).

By multiplying
\chi_{\lambda}(\nu)
and by summing over all

with
|B|=r
, the above expression gives us the coefficient
b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})
of
(-1)^{r}x^{n-r}
in

involving the term
a_{G,r}(\mu)
as follows

\displaystyle b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\subseteq[n]\mbox{ with }|B|=r}\sum_{\nu\in P(n)}\chi_{\lambda}(\nu)\sum_{\mathrm{Type}(\psi)=\nu,\psi\mbox{ fixes }[n]-B}\prod_{i\in B}\ell_{i,\psi(i)}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\subseteq[n]\mbox{ with }|B|=r}\sum_{\nu\in P(n)}\chi_{\lambda}(\nu)\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{PO}_{G,B}(n)}\binom{\mu}{\nu}a_{G,B}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in P(n)}a_{G,r}(\mu)\sum_{\nu\in P(n)}\chi_{\lambda}(\nu)\binom{\mu}{\nu},

where the last equality follows from exchanging the order of summations.
Now, using (
4
) and Lemma
6
the proof is completed.
∎

From the above proof, we recall that

b_{\lambda,r}(L_{G})=\sum\limits_{B\subseteq[n]\mbox{ with }|B|=r}b_{\lambda,B}(L_{G}),\mbox{ where }b_{\lambda,B}(L_{G})=\mathrm{imm}_{\lambda}\left[\begin{matrix}L_{G}[B|B]&0\\

6.

We refer the reader to the textbooks by

[

]

[
11
]

for background on
symmetric functions. It is well known that

\mathsf{CF}_{n}

the space of class functions from
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
. Further, there is a well
known isometry between these two spaces called the Frobenius characteristic, denoted

[
20
]
for more details).

From Sections

3

5

For each

\mathsf{ch}^{-1}_{s_{\lambda}}

Thus using the Frobenius characteristic map

For

\mathsf{ch}^{-1}_{\tau}=\mathsf{ch}^{-1}(\tau)

\mathbb{C}

\mathrm{GMF}_{\tau}(A)
and it is defined by

(6)

\mathrm{GMF}_{\tau}(A)=\sum_{\psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\mathsf{ch}^{-1}_{\tau}(\psi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i,\psi(i)}.

Using (
6

\displaystyle\mathrm{GMF}_{\tau}(A)=\mathrm{imm}_{\lambda}(A)
.
Inspired by immanantal polynomial, the generalized matrix polynomial of a matrix

\tau

\phi_{\tau}(A,x)=\mathrm{GMF}_{\tau}(xI-A)

In particular, when

and

Theorem 15

For the convenience of the reader, from

operation
thus making our exposition self contained. The important point to note here is a natural extension of monotonicity results on
\mathrm{GTS}_{n}
to all connected graphs under
\mathrm{GGS}
operation and it is used to solve extreme value problems of finding the max-min pair in the set
\Omega_{C_{k}}^{v}(n)
for some graph-theoretical parameters which are monotonic under this transformation.
Let
G
be a graph with adjacency matrix
A(G)
and spectral radius
\sigma(G)
(the largest eigenvalue of
A(G)
).
Since the proof of the following theorem is identical to the result given in
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
,
we give an outline of the proof.

Theorem 19
.

The
\mathrm{GGS}
operation on an arbitrary graph increases the spectral radius.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two arbitrary graphs with
n
vertices such that
G_{2}=\mathrm{GGS}(G_{1})
.
Let
1
and
k
be the recipient and the donor in
G_{1}
, respectively.
Let
\sigma(G_{1})
and
\sigma(G_{2})
be spectral radii of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively. Let
w=(w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{n})^{t}
be the Perron eigenvector of
A(G_{1})
associated to
\sigma(G_{1})
with
||w||=1
.
Recall that if the role of
1
and
k
in
G_{1}
are exchanged then the resulting graph after using
\mathrm{GGS}
transformation is isomorphic to
G_{2}
. Thus without loss of generality, we assume that

w_{1}\geq w_{k}.
Let
Z_{G_{1}}(k)=\{v\in G_{1}:v\in N(k)\mbox{ with }v\neq k-1\}
,
where
N(k)
is the set of neighbours of
k
which doesn’t contain
k
. Then it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\sigma(G_{1})

\displaystyle=w^{t}A(G_{1})w=w^{t}A(G_{2})w-2(w_{1}-w_{k})\sum_{j\in Z_{G_{1}}(k)}w_{j}

\displaystyle\leq w^{t}A(G_{2})w\leq\max_{||z||=1}z^{t}A(G_{2})z=\sigma(G_{2}).

Hence
\sigma(G_{1})\leq\sigma(G_{2})
completes the proof.
∎

The main point to note about the above theorem is that it is also true when
G_{2}=\mathrm{GGS}(G_{1})
and all edges of
G_{1}
as well as of
G_{2}
are replaced by blocks (complete graphs).

Let
G
be a connected graph with
n
vertices. The Wiener Index of
G
, denoted
\mathrm{Wi\_Index}(G)

is defined by
\mathrm{Wi\_Index}(G)=\sum_{i,j\in G}d_{G}(i,j)
, where
d_{G}(i,j)
is the shortest
distance between the vertices
i
and
j
in
G
. Since the proof of the following theorem is similar to the proof given in
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
we sketch our proof for the sake of completeness.

Theorem 20
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two connected graphs with
n
vertices such that

G_{2}=\mathrm{GGS}(G_{1})
. Then
\mathrm{Wi\_Index}(G_{1})\geq\mathrm{Wi\_Index}(G_{2})
.

Proof.

Let
d_{G_{1}}(i,j)
be the shortest distance between
i
and
j
in
G_{1}
and similarly
d_{G_{2}}(u,v)
is defined for
u,v\in G_{2}
. Let
X
and
Y
be two subgraphs of
G_{1}
as given in Figure
2
.
Thus using Figure
2
, it is easy to see that

d_{G_{1}}(i,x)+d_{G_{1}}(k+1-i,x)=d_{G_{2}}(i,x)+d_{G_{2}}(k+1-i,x)\mbox{ for all }x\in X

and

d_{G_{1}}(i,y)+d_{G_{1}}(k+1-i,y)=d_{G_{2}}(i,y)+d_{G_{2}}(k+1-i,y)\mbox{ for all }y\in Y.

Obviously
d_{G_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2})=d_{G_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
and

d_{G_{1}}(y_{1},y_{2})=d_{G_{2}}(y_{1},y_{2})
for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y
. It is also easy to check that

d_{G_{1}}(x,y)=d_{G_{2}}(x,y)+(k-1)\mbox{ for all }x\in X\mbox{ and }y\in Y
and hence

\sum_{x\in X,y\in Y}d_{G_{1}}(x,y)=\sum_{x\in X,y\in Y}d_{G_{2}}(x,y)+(k-1)|X||Y|.

After combining all the above identities, we assert that

\sum_{i,j\in G_{1}}d_{G_{1}}(i,j)\geq\sum_{u,v\in G_{2}}d_{G_{2}}(u,v)
.
Thus
\mathrm{Wi\_Index}(G_{1})\geq\mathrm{Wi\_Index}(G_{2})
completes the proof.
∎

Thus each monotonic result under
\mathrm{GGS}
operation is a natural extension of
\mathrm{GTS}
transformation.
Using the definition of
\mathrm{GGS}

and

be the maximal and the minimal elements in

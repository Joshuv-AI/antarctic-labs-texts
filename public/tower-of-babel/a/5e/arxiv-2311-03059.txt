Maximal Consistent Subsystems of Max-T Fuzzy Relational Equations
Ismaïl Baaj

Abstract
In this article, we study the inconsistency of a system of max − T \max-T fuzzy relational equations of the form A ​ □ T max ​ x = b A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b , where T T is a t-norm among min \min , the product or Lukasiewicz’s t-norm. For an inconsistent max − T \max-T system, we directly construct a canonical maximal consistent subsystem (w.r.t the inclusion order). The main tool used to obtain it is the analytical formula which compute the Chebyshev distance Δ = inf c ∈ 𝒞 ‖ b − c ‖ \Delta=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\| associated to the inconsistent max − T \max-T system, where 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is the set of second members of consistent systems defined with the same matrix A A . Based on the same analytical formula, we give, for an inconsistent max − min \max-\min system, an efficient method to obtain all its consistent subsystems, and we show how to iteratively get all its maximal consistent subsystems.

2.1
T-norms and their associated residual implicators

A triangular-norm (t-norm, see
[
9
]
) is a map
T:[0,1]\times[0,1]\mapsto[0,1]
, which satisfies:

T
is commutative:
T(x,y)=T(y,x)
,

T
is associative:
T(x,T(y,z))=T(T(x,y),z)
,

T
is increasing :
x\leq x^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad y\leq y^{\prime}\,\Longrightarrow\,T(x,y)\leq T(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
,

T
has
1
as neutral element:
T(x,1)=x
.

To the t-norm
T
is associated the residual implicator

{\cal I}_{T}:[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]:(x,y)\mapsto{\cal I}_{T}(x,y)=\sup\{z\in[0,1]\,\mid\,T(x,z)\leq y\}
.

For all
a,b\in[0,1]
, the main properties of the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
associated to a continuous t-norm
T
are:

•

{\cal I}_{T}(a,b)=\max\{z\in[0,1]\,\mid\,T(a,z)\leq b\}
. Therefore,
T(a,{\cal I}_{T}(a,b))\leq b
.

•

{\cal I}_{T}
is left-continuous and decreasing in its first argument as well as right-continuous and increasing in its second argument.

•

For all
z\in[0,1]
, we have:

T(a,z)\leq b\Longleftrightarrow z\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,b).

•

We have

\,b\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,T(a,b))
.

The t-norm
\min
denoted by
T_{M}
, has a residual implicator
\mathcal{I}_{T_{M}}
which is the Gödel implication:

T_{M}(x,y)=\min(x,y)\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{M}}(x,y)=x\underset{G}{\longrightarrow}y=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\leq y\\
\ y&\text{ if }x>y\end{cases}.

(1)

The t-norm defined by the usual product is denoted by
T_{P}
. Its associated residual implicator is the Goguen implication:

T_{P}(x,y)=x\cdot y\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{P}}(x,y)=x\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}y=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\leq y\\
\frac{y}{x}&\text{ if }x>y\end{cases}.

(2)

Łukasiewicz’s t-norm is denoted by
T_{L}
and its associated residual implicator is Łukasiewicz’s implication
{\cal I}_{T_{L}}
:

T_{L}(x,y)=\max(x+y-1,0)={(x+y-1)}^{+}\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{L}}(x,y)=x\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}y=\min(1-x+y,1).

(3)

2.2
Solving systems of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations

A system of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations based on a matrix
A=[a_{ij}]\in[0,1]^{n\times m}
and a column-vector
b=[b_{i}]\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
is of the form:

(S):A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b,

(4)

where
x=[x_{j}]_{1\leq j\leq m}\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
is an unknown vector and the matrix product
\Box_{T}^{\max}
uses the continuous t-norm
T
as the product and
\max
as the addition.

Using the vector

e=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}b,

(5)

where
A^{t}
is the transpose of
A
and the matrix product
\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}
uses the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
(associated to
T
) as the product and
\min
as the addition, we have the following equivalence proved by Sanchez for
\max-\min
composition
[
14
]
, and extended to
\max-T
composition by Pedrycz
[
12
,
13
]
and Miyakoshi and Shimbo
[
11
]
:

A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b\text{ is consistent}\Longleftrightarrow A\Box_{T}^{\max}e=b.

(6)

Example 1
.

We study the following
\max-\min
system denoted
A\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b
where:

A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0.4&0.5&0.7\\
0.7&0.5&0.3&0.5\\
0.2&1&1&0.6\\
0.4&0.5&0.5&0.8\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }b=\begin{bmatrix}0.8\\
0.7\\
0.4\\
0.4\end{bmatrix}.

(7)

We compute the potential greatest solution of the system using the Gödel implication
\rightarrow_{G}
, see (
1
), which is associated to the t-norm
\min
:

e=A^{t}\Box_{\rightarrow_{G}}^{\min}b=\begin{bmatrix}0.8\\
0.4\\
0.4\\
0.4\end{bmatrix}.

We check that:

A\Box_{\min}^{\max}e=\begin{bmatrix}0.8\\
0.7\\
0.4\\
0.4\end{bmatrix}=b.

So the system
A\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b
is consistent.

2.3
Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations

To the matrix
A
and the second member
b
of the system
(S)
of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations, see (
4

c=[c_{i}]\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
such that the system
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c
is consistent:

\mathcal{C}=\{c=[c_{i}]\in{[0,1]}^{n\times 1}\mid A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c\text{ is consistent}\}.

(8)

This set allows us to define the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
(S)
.

Definition 1
.

The Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
(S):A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b
is:

\Delta=\Delta(A,b)=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\|

(9)

where:

\|b-c\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mid b_{i}-c_{i}\mid.

The following result was proven for
\max-\min
system in
[
5
]
and recently extended to
\max-T
systems in
[
2
]
:

\Delta=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\},

(10)

which involves a vector inequality based on the following application:

F:[0,1]^{n\times 1}\longrightarrow[0,1]^{n\times 1}:c=[c_{i}]\mapsto F(c)=A\Box_{T}^{\max}(A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}c)=[F(c)_{i}]

(11)

where:

\forall i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\},\,F(c)_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}T(a_{ij},\min_{1\leq k\leq n}{\cal I}_{T}(a_{kj},c_{k})).

(12)

We remind (see
[
2
,
3
]
) that for any
c\in[0,1]^{n}
, we have the inequalities:

\forall i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}\,,\,F(c)_{i}\leq c_{i}.

(13)

By solving (
10
) in the case of a system of
\max-\min
fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b
, the author of
[
3
]
gave the following analytical formula for computing the Chebyshev distance associated to its second member
b

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max[(b_{i}-a_{ij})^{+},\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\,\sigma_{G}\,(b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k})],

(14)

where

\sigma_{G}(x,y,z)=\min(\frac{(x-z)^{+}}{2},(y-z)^{+}).

(15)

Similarly, for the case of a system of
\max
-product fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b
, the author of
[
2
]
gave the following analytical formula for computing the Chebyshev distance associated to
b
:

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}),

(16)

where

\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)=\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})]

(17)

and

\varphi(u,x,y,z)=\begin{cases}\frac{(x\cdot y-u\cdot z)^{+}}{u+y}&\text{ if }u>0\\
x&\text{ if }u=0\end{cases}.

(18)

The author of
[
2
]
also gave the following analytical formula for computing Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of a system of
\max
-Łukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}x=b
:

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{L}\,(1-a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}),

(19)

where

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)=\min(x,\max(v^{+},\frac{(v+y-z)^{+}}{2}))\text{ with }v=x+u-1.

(20)

From these formulas, the author of
[
2
,
3
]
proved that
F(\overline{b}(\Delta))
is the greatest Chebyshev approximation of the second member
b
, so the system
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=F(\overline{b}(\Delta))
is consistent and
\|F(\overline{b}(\Delta))-b\|=\Delta
. Therefore, the author showed the following important equivalence for
\max-T
systems:

\Delta=0\Longleftrightarrow\text{ The system }(S)\text{ is consistent.}

(21)

Theorem 1
.

Assume that there is at least one equation of index
i
that is solvable independently of the others, i.e., there is a subsystem reduced to a single equation
(S_{\{i\}})
that is consistent.
Then, we have:

1.

The set
N_{c}
is non-empty (
N_{c}\not=\emptyset
).

2.

The system
(S_{N_{c}}):A_{N_{c}}\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b_{N_{c}}
is consistent.

3.

Among the consistent subsystems

(S_{R}):A_{R}\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b_{R}
of the system
(S):A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b
with
R\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
,
the consistent subsystem
(S_{N_{c}}):A_{N_{c}}\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b_{N_{c}}
is maximal in the following sense: any subsystem defined by a strict superset of
N_{c}
is inconsistent.

The third statement of the above theorem means that for any non-empty subset
R\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, we have:

\text{ The system }(S_{R})\text{ is consistent and }N_{c}\subseteq R\Longrightarrow N_{c}=R.

We begin by proving the second statement, and we will then prove the first and third statements
simultaneously
.

Proof.

\bullet
Proof of the second statement (assuming that
N_{c}\not=\emptyset
).

To prove that the subsystem
(S_{N_{c}})

is consistent, we must show that

\Delta_{N_{c}}=0
, see (Lemma
3
), which means that for any
i\in N_{c}
, there is an index
j\in M
such that
\max_{k\in N_{c}}\delta^{T}_{ijk}=0
.

Let an index
i\in N_{c}
. We have:

\delta_{i}=\min_{j\in M}\max_{k\in N}\delta^{T}_{ijk}=0.

We can easily deduce that there is an index
j\in M
such that:

0=\max_{k\in N}\delta^{T}_{ijk}\geq\max_{k\in N_{c}}\delta^{T}_{ijk}.

\bullet
Proof of the first and third statement.

Let
R\subseteq N
be a non-empty set such that
\text{the system }(S_{R})\text{ is consistent and }N_{c}\subseteq R
. We will show the equality
N_{c}=R
.

In particular, if we take
R=\{i\}
and we suppose that the subsystem
(S_{R})
is consistent, then we trivially have
\emptyset\subset R
, thus
N_{c}\not=\emptyset
.

Let us show that
N_{c}=R
by contradiction.

Assume that we have
N_{c}\subset R
. Let an index
i\in R\cap\overline{N_{c}}
.

By (Lemma
4
), we can take an index
j\in M
verifying (

\max_{k\in R}\delta^{T}_{ijk}=0.

Then by (Proposition
1
), we conclude that for any integer
p\geq 1
, we have

, which is a contradiction.

We have proven that
N_{c}=R
.

b
are ordered in ascending order, i.e., we have:

b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\dots\leq b_{n}.

(40)

We reuse (Notation
2
) i.e.,
for any subset
R\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, we form the following
\max-\min
subsystem:
(S_{R}):A_{R}\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b_{R},
where

A_{R}=[a_{ij}]_{i\in R,1\leq j\leq m}\text{ and }b_{R}=[b_{i}]_{i\in R}
.

We compute the Chebyshev distance associated to the subsystem
(S_{R})
by (see (Lemma
3
)):

\Delta_{R}=\max_{i\in R}\delta^{R}_{i}\quad\text{ where }\quad\delta_{i}^{R}=\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\max_{k\in R}\delta^{T_{M}}_{ijk}.

(41)

We assume that each of the equations in the system
(S)
is solvable independently of the others, which means that all subsystems reduced to one equation are consistent:

\text{for all }i\in N,\text{ the system }(S_{\{i\}}):A_{\{i\}}\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b_{i}\text{ is consistent.}

(42)

For all
s\in N
, we associate to the subsystem
(S_{\{1,2,\dots,s\}})
, the following set:

\mathcal{E}^{s}=\bigg\{R\in 2^{\{1,2,\dots,s\}}\,\mid\,(S_{R}):A_{R}\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b_{R}\quad\text{is consistent}\bigg\}.

(43)

The set
\mathcal{E}^{s}
is non-empty since, from (
42
), all singletons
\{1\},\{2\},\cdots,\{s\}
are in
\mathcal{E}^{s}
.

5.2
Preliminary result

The following result allows us to construct, from a consistent subsystem of
(S_{\{1,2,\dots,s\}})
a consistent subsystem with a larger number of equations, if it is possible.

Lemma 7
.

Let
1\leq s<n-1
be fixed and consider a subset of equation indexes
R\in{\cal E}^{s}
which allows us to form a consistent subsystem of
(S_{\{1,2,\dots,s\}})
. For any
k\in N
such that
k>s
, we put the following subset
\widehat{R}_{k}=R\cup\{k\}
and we have (using the formula (
41
)):

1.

\forall i\in R\,,\,\delta^{\widehat{R}_{k}}_{i}=0
,

2.

The subsystem
(S_{\widehat{R}_{k}})
is a consistent subsystem

\Longleftrightarrow\delta^{\widehat{R}_{k}}_{k}=0.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the first statement.

Let
i\in R
, we have
i<k
, so

b_{i}\leq b_{k}
and for all
l\in M
, we have:

\sigma_{G}(b_{i},a_{kl},b_{k})=\min(\dfrac{(b_{i}-b_{k})^{+}}{2},(a_{kl}-b_{k})^{+})=0.

Since
\Delta_{R}=0
, let
j\in M
be such that
\max_{k^{\prime}\in R}\delta^{T_{M}}_{ijk^{\prime}}=0
.

From
k^{\prime}=i\in R
and (Lemma
1
), we deduce
0=\delta^{T_{M}}_{iji}=(b_{i}-a_{ij})^{+}
.

From (
24
) and the inequality
b_{i}\leq b_{k}
, we obtain:

\delta^{T_{M}}_{ijk}=\sigma_{G}(b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k})=0.

Finally, we obtain from (
41
):

\displaystyle\delta^{\widehat{R}_{k}}_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k^{\prime}\in\widehat{R}_{k}}\delta^{T_{M}}_{ijk^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\max(\max_{k^{\prime}\in R}\delta^{T_{M}}_{ijk^{\prime}},\delta^{T_{M}}_{ijk})

\displaystyle=0.

∎

5.3
Method for finding all consistent subsystems of an inconsistent
\max-\min
system

We will now introduce our method, which allows us to build for
1\leq s\leq n-1
the set
\mathcal{E}^{s+1}
from the set
\mathcal{E}^{s}
.
Trivially, we have
\mathcal{E}^{1}=\bigg\{\{1\}\bigg\}
.

For
1\leq s\leq n-1
the set
\mathcal{E}^{s+1}
is given by:

{\cal E}^{s+1}={\cal E}^{s}\cup\bigg\{\{s+1\}\bigg\}\cup\bigg\{\widehat{R}\mid R\in{\cal E}^{s},\widehat{R}=R\cup\{s+1\},\text{ and }\delta^{\widehat{R}}_{s+1}=0\bigg\}.

(45)

The equality (
45
) is easily deduced from (Lemma
7
).

Algorithmically, the main step to deduce
{\cal E}^{s+1}
from
{\cal E}^{s}
consists in computing the
\text{card}({\cal E}^{s})
numbers
\delta^{\widehat{R}}_{s+1}=\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\max_{k\in\widehat{R}}\delta^{T_{M}}_{s+1jk}
. From the set
\mathcal{E}^{n}
we get all the consistent subsystems of the system
(S)
.

Starting from a consistent subsystem
(S_{R})
of the system
(S_{\{1,\dots,s\}})
that
we suppose to be maximal
among the consistent subsystems of the system
(S_{\{1,\dots,s\}})
, we characterize in which case

(S_{R})
is a maximal consistent subsystem of the whole system
(S)
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
1\leq s<n-1
and
R\subseteq\{1,\dots,s\}
. We suppose that the subsystem

(S_{R})
is a maximal consistent subsystem of
(S_{\{1,\dots,s\}})
. Then we have:

(S_{R})\,\,\text{is a maximal consitent subsystem of the system}\,\,(S)\Longleftrightarrow\forall k\in\{s+1,\dots,n\},\,\,\delta^{\widehat{R}_{k}}_{k}>0.

(46)

Proof.

The proof of the implication
\Longrightarrow
follows directly from (Lemma
7
). In fact, for any
k\in\{s+1,\dots,n\}
we have

R\subset\widehat{R}_{k}=R\cup\{k\}
. The maximality of the subsystem
(S_{R})
implies the non-consistency of the subsystem
(S_{\widehat{R}_{k}})
, thus

\delta^{\widehat{R}_{k}}_{k}>0
.

To prove the implication
\Longleftarrow
, let
U\subseteq N
such that the subsystem
(S_{U})
is consistent and
R\subseteq U
, we must prove the equality
R=U
.

By assumption, if
U
satisfies the inclusion
U\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,s\}
, then we conclude that
R=U
.

Let us prove the inclusion
U\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,s\}
by contradiction.

Suppose that there is an index
k\in\{s+1,\dots,n\}\cap U
. Then we have:

R\subset\widehat{R}_{k}\subseteq U,\quad\widehat{R}_{k}=R\cup\{k\}.

From the consistency of the subsystem
(S_{U})
, we deduce that the subsystem
(S_{\widehat{R}_{k}})
is consistent. By applying
(Lemma
7
), we obtain
\delta^{\widehat{R}_{k}}_{k}=0
and then a contradiction.
∎

We can then obtain, iteratively, all the maximal consistent subsystems of the whole inconsistent system. (Example
6
) shows that two maximal consistent subsystems
do not necessarily have the same cardinality
.

Example 6
.

Let the system
A\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b
be defined by:

A=\begin{bmatrix}0.98&0.02&0.10\\
0.80&0.31&0.18\\
0.78&0.38&0.26\\
0.77&0.20&0.85\\
\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }b=\begin{bmatrix}0.13\\
0.28\\
0.54\\
0.70\\
\end{bmatrix}.

We have
b_{1}<b_{2}<b_{3}<b_{4}
and each subsystem reduced to a single equation is consistent i.e., for all
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
,
\text{ the system }(S_{\{i\}}):A_{\{i\}}\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b_{i}\text{ is consistent.}

We start from
\mathcal{E}^{1}=\bigg\{\{1\}\bigg\}
. We have:

•

\mathcal{E}^{2}=\bigg\{\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}\bigg\}
,

•

\mathcal{E}^{3}=\bigg\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{1,2\}\bigg\}
,

•

\mathcal{E}^{4}=\bigg\{\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{4\},\{1,2\},\{1,4\},\{2,4\},\{3,4\},\{1,2,4\}\bigg\}
.

So,
\{3,4\}
and
\{1,2,4\}
are the two subsets of indexes describing the maximal consistent subsystems of the inconsistent
\max-\min
system.

Our method is much faster than checking, using (
6
), the consistency of each of the subsystems
(S_{R})
of
(S)
one by one.

6
Conclusion

In this article, we studied the inconsistency of systems of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations of the form
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b
, where
T
is a t-norm among
\min
, product or Łukasiewicz’s t-norm. By computing the Chebyshev distance
\Delta=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\|

associated to the second member
b
of an inconsistent
\max-T
system where
\mathcal{C}
is the set of second members of consistent systems defined with the same matrix
A
, using the analytical formulas given in
[
2
,
3
]
, we showed in (Theorem
1
) that we can directly find a canonical maximal consistent subsystem of this inconsistent system. We showed that the computational complexity for obtaining this maximal consistent subsystem is the same as that required for computing
\Delta
.

For an inconsistent
\max-\min
system, we introduced a method for efficiently obtaining all the consistent subsystems of this system. The method is based on the formula of
\Delta
for
\max-\min
systems and allows us to obtain, iteratively, all the maximal consistent systems of the considered inconsistent system.
For the moment, we have not attempted to adapt this method to the cases of
\max-
product and
\max-
Łukasiewicz compositions.

This work may be useful for solving inconsistency issues in
\max-T
systems involved in
\max-T
learning methods, such as the paradigm of
[
3
]
for
\max-\min
learning weight matrices according to training data, learning the rule parameters for possibilistic rule-based systems
[
1
,
3
]
or learning associate memories
[
16
]
. It can also be useful for applications based on
\max-T
systems, e.g. spatial analysis
[
6
]
, diagnostic problems
[
8
]
.

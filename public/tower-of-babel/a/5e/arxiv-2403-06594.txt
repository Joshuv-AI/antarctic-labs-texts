1.1.
The Hardy-Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

In this article, our focus is to study the quantitative stability of the following Hardy-Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

(
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma,s}

\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\tfrac{2}{2^{*}(s)}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-\gamma\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\,{\rm d}x,\text{ for all }u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
N\geq 3,\,0<\gamma<\gamma_{H}:=\tfrac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},\,0<s<2,\,2^{*}(s)=\tfrac{2(N-s)}{N-2}

(1.1)

\mu_{\gamma,s}=\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\inf_{u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-\gamma\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\,{\rm d}x}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\tfrac{2}{2^{*}(s)}}}.

1.2.
The Euclidean Sobolev inequality

The classical Sobolev inequality says for
N\geq 3
,

(1.9)

S\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\tfrac{2}{2^{*}}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

holds for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent. The inequality continues to hold for all
u
satisfying
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}<+\infty
and
\mathcal{L}^{N}\big(\{|u|>t\}\big)
for every
t>0
, where
\mathcal{L}^{N}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Here,
S\equiv S(N)>0
is the best constant in the Sobolev inequality defined by

(1.10)

S=S(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\inf_{u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\tfrac{2}{2^{*}}}}.

The inequality (
1.9
) is invariant under the conformal group action comprised of translation, dilation and inversion which causes the non-compactness in the embedding
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\xhookrightarrow{}L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. More precisely, if
U
minimizes (
1.9
), then any nonzero constant scalar multiple times
U[z,\lambda](x)=\lambda^{-\tfrac{N-2}{2}}U(\tfrac{x-z}{\lambda})
also minimizes (
1.9
) for any
\lambda>0
and for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Aubin
[
2
]
, Talenti
[
35
]
computed the explicit value of
S
and they also showed that if
u
is a minimizer for the Sobolev constant then
u
is a nonzero scalar multiple of
U[z,\lambda]
, where
U
is given by (
1.2
).

1.3.
The Quantitative Stability problem and known results

Following Brézis-Lieb
[
7
]
we may ask if
u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
almost gives equality in the above Sobolev inequality (
1.9
), does it imply that
u
is close to the set of all positive minimizers of (
1.9
)? Or in other words, can we measure the discrepancy in the Sobolev inequality

\delta_{Eu}(u):=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-S\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}

for
u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, from below in terms of the
\dot{H}^{1}
-distance from the solution manifold? The answer is affirmative (see Bianchi-Egnell
[
6
]
, also see Rey
[
30
]
). In particular, they proved that

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
)

There exists a positive constant
\alpha>0
(
\alpha
depends only on
N
),

\delta_{Eu}(u)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\geq\alpha\,\dist(u,\tilde{\mathcal{M}}).

The exponent
\frac{1}{2}
is sharp in the sense that the inequality fails if we replace
\frac{1}{2}
by any other exponent strictly bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
as we consider
\delta_{Eu}(u)\to 0
.

Next, consider the associated Euler-Lagrange equation, which is up to a scaling given by

(1.11)

\Delta u+u|u|^{\tfrac{4}{N-2}}=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In this context, the question of stability can be now reformulated as, if
u
almost solves (
1.11
), that is whenever the quantity
\tilde{\Gamma}(u):=\|\Delta u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
is small enough then whether
u
is quantitatively close to Aubin-Talenti bubble (
[
2
,
35
]
) given by,

U[z,\lambda](x):=\Big(N(N-2)\Big)^{\tfrac{N-2}{4}}\frac{\lambda^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}}{\Big(1+\lambda^{2}|x-z|^{2}\Big)^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}},\quad\text{for }\lambda>0,\,z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

which are precisely all the positive solutions of (
1.11
) (
[
26
,
8
]
). Here the question is much more delicate and the answer is "No" in general, unless some extra assumptions are considered. To see this, we consider,

u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
, where,
\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq i,j\leq k}}{{i\neq j}}}|z_{i}-z_{j}|\gg R
for
R>0
very large. Then,

-\Delta u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}-\Delta U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{k}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]^{2^{*}-1}\approx\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]\Big)^{2^{*}-1}=u^{2^{*}-1},

which gives
\tilde{\Gamma}(u)\approx 0
, but clearly
u
is not close to one single bubble
U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
as all the bubbles have same energy
J(U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}])=\frac{1}{N}S^{\tfrac{N}{2}}
, where
J
is the energy functional associated to (
1.11
) and is defined in (
3.1
). Here it is also significant to note that the interaction between the Aubin-Talenti bubbles
U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]
and
U[z_{j},\lambda_{j}]
for
i\neq j
can be estimated as,

\langle U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}],U[z_{j},\lambda_{j}]\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]^{2^{*}-1}U[z_{j},\lambda_{j}]\approx|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{-(N-2)}\ll R^{-(N-2)}=o(1)\text{ as }R\to\infty,

which makes
\tilde{\Gamma}(u)
small enough.
Another delicate issue is when we take out the sign restriction on
u
. Ding
[
17
]
proved that there are infinitely many sign-changing solutions of (
1.11
) with finite energy. So, even if
\tilde{\Gamma}(u)
is small enough,
u
may not be close to the Aubin-Talenti bubble or the sum of the Aubin-Talenti bubbles. In fact, in a seminal work due to Struwe (
[
33
]
, see also
[
34
]
), he proved that if we restrict our attention to non-negative functions on
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the above-mentioned bubbling phenomenon is the only other possibility due to which stability fails.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
33
,
34
]
)

Let
N\geq 3
and
\nu\geq 1
be positive integers. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a non-negative sequence in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\tilde{\Gamma}(u_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and
\{u_{n}\}
satisfies

(\nu-\frac{1}{2})S^{\tfrac{N}{2}}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq(\nu+\frac{1}{2})S^{\tfrac{N}{2}}.

Then there exists a sequence of
\nu
- tuple of points
(z_{1}^{n},\cdots,z_{\nu}^{n})
on
\mathbb{R}^{N\nu}
and a sequence of
\nu
-tuple
(\lambda_{1}^{n},\cdots,\lambda_{\nu}^{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\nu}
of positive real numbers such that

\Bigg\|\nabla\left(u_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}U[z_{i}^{n},\lambda_{i}^{n}]\right)\Bigg\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0\qquad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

Moreover, the Aubin-Talenti bubbles do not interact with each other at the
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
level. More precisely,

\displaystyle\langle U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}],U[z_{j},\lambda_{j}]\rangle_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}U[z_{i},\lambda_{i}]^{2^{*}-1}U[z_{j},\lambda_{j}]\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq i,j\leq\nu}}{{i\neq j}}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{i}^{n}}{\lambda_{j}^{n}},\frac{\lambda_{j}^{n}}{\lambda_{i}^{n}},\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{n}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}}\right)^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Remark 1.1
.

For

\|u\|_{\gamma}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\;{\rm d}x\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

2.
Bianchi-Egnell’s result for the H-S inequality

The following lemma plays a pivotal role in the proof of Theorem
1.4
. This lemma gives a precise picture of how the functions in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which are near to
\mathcal{M}
(defined in (
1.7
)) behave. Here
U_{\gamma,s}
is a minimizer of (
1.1
) and
C_{N,\gamma,s}
is chosen so that
\|U_{\gamma,s}\|_{\gamma}=1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There is a constant
\alpha=\alpha(N,\gamma,s)>0
such that

(2.1)

\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}\geq\alpha\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})^{2}+o\left(\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})^{2}\right),

for all
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})<\|\phi\|_{\gamma}
.

Proof.

\mathcal{M}
is a two-dimensional manifold embedded inside
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{M}\ni(c,\lambda,U_{\gamma,s})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle cU_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Take
\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with

(2.2)

\displaystyle\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{(c,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\|\phi-cU_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}\|_{\gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{(c,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left[\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}+|c|^{2}\|U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-2\langle\phi,cU_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}\rangle_{\gamma}\right]<\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}

(by hypothesis of lemma)

Let
\{(c_{k},\lambda_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence. Define,

v_{k}:=\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}+|c_{k}|^{2}\|U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-2\langle\phi,c_{k}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}\rangle_{\gamma}\to\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})^{2}\,\,\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Again from (
2.2
), it follows that

(2.3)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}v_{k}<\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}

v_{k}
being a quadratic polynomial in
c_{k}
, hence
c_{k}
is bounded and thus converges (up to a subsequence, still denoting by
c_{k}
) to some
c_{0}
as
k\to\infty
. Again
c_{0}>0
, follows from (
2.3
).
Now we have,

\displaystyle\langle\phi,U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}\rangle_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\nabla U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}(x)\,{\rm d}x-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\phi(x)U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}(x)}{|x|^{2}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda_{k}^{\tfrac{N}{2}}\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\nabla U_{\gamma,s}(\lambda_{k}x)\,{\rm d}x-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}\phi(x)U_{\gamma,s}(\lambda_{k}x)}{|x|^{2}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lambda_{k}^{-\tfrac{N}{2}}\nabla\phi(\tfrac{x}{\lambda_{k}})\cdot\nabla U_{\gamma,s}(x)\,{\rm d}x-\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{-\left(\tfrac{N-2}{2}\right)}\phi(\tfrac{x}{\lambda_{k}})U_{\gamma,s}(x)}{|x|^{2}}\,{\rm d}x.

Again from the scale invariance of
\|\cdot\|_{\gamma}
under the conformal transformation we have for all
k
,
\|U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}\|_{\gamma}=\|U_{\gamma,s}\|_{\gamma}
. Thus if
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty,
then
U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{k}}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
k\to\infty
. Then
v_{k}\to\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}+c_{0}^{2}\|U_{\gamma,s}\|_{\gamma}^{2}
which contradicts (
2.3
). Hence,
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
must be bounded and thus
\lambda_{k}\to\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
(up to a subsequence). Now,
\lambda_{0}=0
contradicts (
2.3
). Thus,
\lambda_{0}>0.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth manifold, we must get
(\phi-c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})\perp T_{c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}}\mathcal{M}
. Furthermore,
T_{c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}}\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{span}\left\{U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}},\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}\right\}.
Now consider the operator,

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\lambda_{0}}:=\frac{\left(-\Delta-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}\right)}{\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}}\text{ on }L^{2}\left(\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}},\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Since the weight function,
\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}\in L^{\tfrac{N}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
\lambda>0
, following similar argument as in
[
19
]
(Appendix
A
, Proposition
A.2
),
\left(\frac{(-\Delta-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}})}{\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}}\right)^{-1}
is well-defined and continuous from
L^{2}\left(\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}},\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
into
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Furthermore, it is a compact, self-adjoint operator from
L^{2}\left(\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}},\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
into itself. Hence, its spectrum is discrete. Thus we have the following Rayleigh quotient characterization for eigenvalues

\eta_{m}=R(w_{m}):=\frac{\|w_{m}\|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tfrac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}|w_{m}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x},

where
w_{m}
denotes the
m
-th eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\lambda_{0}}
and further if we consider
V_{m}=\operatorname{span}\{w_{1},\cdots,w_{m}\},
we have the following variational characterization for the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\eta_{m}=\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\in V_{m}}}{{v\neq 0}}}R(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{v\perp V_{m-1}}R(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle W\subseteq\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{{\dim(W)=m}}}\max_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\in W}}{{v\neq 0}}}R(v).

In particular,

\eta_{1}=\min_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}{{v\neq 0}}}R(v).

From the above characterization of eigenvalues, we have,

(2.5)

\eta_{3}\leq\frac{\|w\|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}w^{2}\,{\rm d}x}\qquad\text{for all }w\perp T_{c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}}\mathcal{M},

with equality if and only if
w
is the third eigenfunction. We also have
\eta_{1}=\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}},\,\eta_{2}=(2^{*}(s)-1)\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}
and all eigenvalues are independent of the scaling factor
\lambda>0
. Moreover,

T_{U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}}\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{span}\{U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}\}\bigoplus\operatorname{span}\left\{\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}\right\},

that is the direct sum of the first and second eigenspace of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,\lambda_{0}}
. Since
(\phi-c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})\perp T_{c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}}\mathcal{M},
we can write,

\phi=c_{0}U_{\gamma.s}^{\lambda_{0}}+dv,

where
v\perp T_{U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}}\mathcal{M}
with
\|v\|_{\gamma}=1
and
d=\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})
. Now, using Taylor series expansion around
U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}
yields,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|c_{0}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}+dv|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x+2^{*}(s)d|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2^{*}(s)(2^{*}(s)-1)|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}d^{2}}{2!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}v^{2}\,{\rm d}x+o(d^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)}\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{-\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}+|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}\frac{2^{*}(s)(2^{*}(s)-1)d^{2}}{2!}\frac{1}{\eta_{3}}+o(d^{2}).

Since,
U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}
solves

(2.6)

-\Delta W-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}W=\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}\frac{W^{2^{*}(s)-1}}{|x|^{s}}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

and
v\perp U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}
,

0=\langle U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}},v\rangle_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}})^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{0}}v\,{\rm d}x.

Now, as
\eta_{2}=(2^{*}(s)-1)\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\phi|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x\leq|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)}\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{-\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}+\frac{2^{*}(s)d^{2}}{2!}|c_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{-\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}+o(d^{2}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|c_{0}|^{2}\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{-1}\left(1+\frac{2^{*}(s)d^{2}}{2}\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}+o(d^{2})\right)^{\tfrac{2}{2^{*}(s)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|c_{0}|^{2}\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{-1}\left(1+d^{2}|c_{0}|^{-2}\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}+o(d^{2})\right),

where in the last inequality we have used
(1+t)^{p}\leq 1+pt,
holds for
t>0
and
p\in(0,1)
.
Hence we obtain the desired expression

\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\|\phi\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}^{2}\geq d^{2}\left(1-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}\right)+o(d^{2}).

Thus the lemma holds for
\alpha=\left(1-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}\right)>0
. To see this estimate is sharp we argue as follows.

Take
\phi=U_{\gamma,s}+dv
where
v
is the third eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,1}
and
d
is a small positive number. Then if
d
is small enough we get
\dist(\phi,\mathcal{M})=d
and the closest point on
\mathcal{M}
is
U_{\gamma,s}
. Thus

\eta_{3}=\frac{\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}v^{2}\,{\rm d}x}=\frac{1+d^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}v^{2}\,{\rm d}x}.

It is clear that the last claim holds locally. If we assume that the claim is not true globally for any
d>0
, then there exists a sequence
(c_{m},\lambda_{m})\nrightarrow(1,1)
such that
c_{m}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{m}}\to U_{\gamma,s}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),
which is absurd.

Now the same argument as above yields

\|\phi\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\|\phi\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}^{2}=d^{2}\left(1-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\eta_{3}}\right)+o(d^{2}).

∎

Proof of Theorem (
1.4
).

The sharpness of the theorem follows from the proof of the last part of the previous lemma.

We have the local result from the previous lemma in a region around
\mathcal{M}
. Now we are going to use a Concentration Compactness argument to show that some stability estimate must hold outside this local region also. Since we are working on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can cite this step as a straightforward application of Concentration Compactness as presented by P.L. Lions
[
28
]
or M. Struwe
[
33
]
.

Assume that the Theorem does not hold. Then we would be able to find a sequence
\{u_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\frac{\|u_{m}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}^{2}}{\dist(u_{m},\mathcal{M})^{2}}\to 0\,\,\text{as }m\to\infty.

By homogeneity, we can assume
\|u_{m}\|_{\gamma}=1
and after selecting a subsequence we can also assume that
\dist(u_{m},\mathcal{M})\to\theta\in\left[0,1\right]
. We note that,
\dist(u_{m},\mathcal{M})\leq\|u_{m}\|_{\gamma}=1.

If
\theta=0,
then we get using Lemma (
2.1
),

\frac{\|u_{m}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}^{2}}{\dist(u_{m},\mathcal{M})^{2}}\geq\alpha+o(1)>0,

which contradicts our assumption.

The other possibility is
L>0.
In this case, we must have,
\|u_{m}\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\|u_{m}\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}^{2}\to 0
as
m\to\infty,\,\|u_{m}\|_{\gamma}=1
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Now scaling argument as in Proposition
3.1
(see also Struwe
[
33
]
) yields a sequence
\{\lambda_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
so that
\lambda_{m}^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}U_{\gamma,s}(\lambda_{m}x)\to\pm U_{\gamma,s}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
m\to\infty.
This implies that
\dist(u_{m},\mathcal{M})=\dist(\lambda_{m}^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}u(\lambda_{m}x),\mathcal{M})\to 0
as
m\to\infty.
This gives the desired contradiction.

∎

3.
Profile decomposition for Hardy-Sobolev equation

In the seminal work due to Struwe
[
33
]
, he proved that all critical points at infinity of the functional
J
are induced by limits of sums of Talenti bubbles, where

(3.1)

J(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x,\qquad u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

whose Euler-Lagrange equation is
\Delta u+|u|^{2^{*}-2}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Here we will give an analog of the above result for the critical Hardy-Sobolev inequality.

In this section, we study the Palais-Smale sequences of the problem (
1.8
), where
0<\gamma<\gamma_{H},\,s\in(0,2)
. Define the associated energy functional

(3.2)

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,s}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[|\nabla u|^{2}-\gamma\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right]\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}(s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\gamma}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}(s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x.

We say that the sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a
(PS)
sequence for
I_{\gamma,s}
at level
\beta
if
I_{\gamma,s}(u_{n})\to\beta
and
(I_{\gamma,s})^{\prime}(u_{n})\to 0
in
H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It is easy to see that the weak limit of a
(PS)
sequence solves (
1.8
) except for the positivity.

However, the main difficulty is that the
(PS)
sequence may not converge strongly and hence the weak limit can be zero even if
\beta>0.

The main purpose of this section is to classify
(PS)
sequences of
the functional
I_{\gamma,s}
. Classification of
(PS)

sequences have been done for various problems having a lack of compactness, to quote a few, we cite in the local case
[
5
,
32
]
with Hardy potentials and in
[
33
]
without Hardy potentials.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}
be a Palais-Smale sequence for
I_{\gamma,s}
at level
\beta
. Then up to a subsequence
(still denoted by
u_{n}
)
the following properties hold:
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
m
sequences
\{R_{n}^{k}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

(for
1\leq k\leq m
)
and
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\,u_{n}=u_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(U_{\gamma,s}\right)^{R_{n}^{k}}+o(1),

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\,I^{\prime}_{\gamma,s}(u_{0})=0

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\,R_{n}^{k}\to 0\,\text{ or, }R_{n}^{k}\to\infty,\,\frac{R_{n}^{k+1}}{R_{n}^{k}}\to\infty\text{ as }k\to\infty

\displaystyle(iv)

\displaystyle\,\beta=I_{\gamma,s}(u_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{m}I_{\gamma,s}(U_{\gamma,s}^{R_{n}^{k}})+o(1),

where
o(1)\to 0
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem in several steps.

Step 1:
Using standard arguments it follows that there exists
M>0
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}\leq M.

More precisely, as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\beta+o(1)+o(1)\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle I_{\gamma,s}(u_{n})\,-\,\frac{1}{2^{*}(s)}\prescript{}{H^{-1}}{\big\langle}I^{\prime}_{\gamma,s}(u_{n}),u_{n}{\big\rangle}_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}(s)}\right)\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}^{2}.

As
2^{*}(s)>2
, from the above estimate, it follows that
(u_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Consequently, there exists
u_{0}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that, up to a subsequence, still denoted by
(u_{n})_{n}
,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Moreover, as
\prescript{}{H^{-1}}{\big\langle}I^{\prime}_{\gamma,s}(u_{n}),v{\big\rangle}_{\dot{H}^{1}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for all
v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

(3.3)

-\Delta u_{n}-\gamma\frac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{s}}\longrightarrow 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Step 2:

From (
3.3
), letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get

(3.4)

\langle u_{n},v\rangle_{\gamma}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x\,{\rightarrow}\,0,\qquad\text{for all }v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

As
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows that
\langle u_{n},v\rangle_{\gamma}\to\langle u_{0},v\rangle_{\gamma}
for all
v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Claim 1:
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{0}v}{|x|^{s}}{\rm d}x

for all
v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Indeed,
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

(3.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x=\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x.

On
B_{R}
we are going to use Vitali’s convergence theorem. For that, given any
\varepsilon>0
, we choose
\Omega\subset B_{R}
such that

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}{\rm d}x\bigg)^{\tfrac{1}{2^{*}(s)}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{(M\mu_{\gamma,s}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2^{*}(s)-1}}
. Since
\frac{|v|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}
is in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the above choice makes sense. Therefore using Hölder’s inequality and Hardy-Sobolev’s inequality we get,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-1}|v|}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}{\rm d}x\bigg)^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)-1}{2^{*}(s)}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}{\rm d}x\bigg)^{\tfrac{1}{2^{*}(s)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{\gamma,s}^{-\frac{2^{*}(s)-1}{2}}\|u_{n}\|_{\gamma}^{2^{*}(s)-1}\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}{\rm d}x\bigg)^{\tfrac{1}{2^{*}(s)}}<\varepsilon

Thus
\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}v}{|x|^{s}}
is uniformly integrable in
B_{R}
. Therefore, using Vitali’s convergence theorem, we can pass to the limit in the 1st integral on RHS of (
3.5
).

To estimate the integral now on
B_{R}^{c}
, we first set
v_{n}=u_{n}-u_{0}
.
Then
v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
It is not difficult to see that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists

C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\bigg|\frac{|v_{n}+u_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}(v_{n}+u_{0})}{|x|^{s}}-\frac{|u_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{0}}{|x|^{s}}\bigg|<\varepsilon\frac{|v_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-1}}{|x|^{s}}+C_{\varepsilon}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-1}}{|x|^{s}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\bigg\{\frac{|u_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{n}}{|x|^{s}}-\frac{|u_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}u_{0}}{|x|^{s}}\bigg\}v\;{\rm d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\varepsilon\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}|^{2^{*}(s)-1}|v|}{|x|^{s}}\;{\rm d}x+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{|u_{0}|^{2^{*}(s)-1}|v|}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u^{2^{*}(s)-1}\rho}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x

5.1.
Nondegeneracy and spectral properties of the linearized operator

In this subsection, we collect a few important lemmas which will be needed for the proof related to the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the linearized operator

\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,s}:=\frac{\left(-\Delta-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}\right)}{\tfrac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}}.

We know that if
U_{\gamma,s}
is of the form (
1.15
), where the constant
C_{N,\gamma,s}
is chosen so that
\|U_{\gamma,s}\|_{\gamma}=1
. Then for any
\lambda>0,

U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}U_{\gamma,s}(\lambda x)

also solves (
1.8
). This fact indicates that the solution
U_{\gamma,s}
must degenerate. In other words, the kernel of the linearized operator contains non-trivial elements. In
[
30
]
, the author shows that the degeneracy happens only along a 1-dimensional subspace characterized by

Z_{\gamma,s}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\bigg|_{\lambda=1}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}=\sum_{i}x^{i}\partial_{i}U_{\gamma,s}+\tfrac{N-2}{2}U_{\gamma,s}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Z_{\gamma,s}
solves the eigenvalue problem

(5.1)

-\Delta\phi-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}\phi=(2^{*}(s)-1)\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}\phi\qquad\text{in }\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

and the degeneracy to the solution space to (
5.1
) can occur only along the directions
Z_{\gamma,s}
.

Theorem 5.1

(Theorem
0.1
,
[
31
]
)
.

We assume that
\gamma\geq 0
and that
\gamma+s>0.
Then

K:=\left\{\phi\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,:\,-\Delta\phi-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}\phi=(2^{*}(s)-1)\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}\phi\,\,\text{in }\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}=\mathbb{R}Z_{\gamma,s}.

In other words, the kernel of
\left(-\Delta-\tfrac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}-(2^{*}(s)-1)\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}\right)
is one dimensional.

As a result, we get complete information on the first and second eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenspaces of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,s}
.

We now recall the following results due to Glaudo-Figalli

19

Since

Remark 5.1
.

A spherical harmonic

of order

-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}P_{n}=\lambda_{n}P_{n},

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\eta_{i},\,i=1,\,2,\,3,\,\cdots,
denote the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{\gamma,s}
given in increasing order. Then,
\eta_{1}=\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}
is simple with eigenfunction

and
\eta_{2}=(2^{*}(s)-1)\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}

\lambda

Proof.

Consider the Rayleigh quotient

R_{U_{\gamma,s}}(v)=\frac{\|v\|_{\gamma}^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U_{\gamma,s}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}|v|^{2}\,{\rm d}x}.

If we consider, the transformation
U^{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\tfrac{N-2}{2}}U(\lambda x),
for
\lambda>0
, then

R_{U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}}(v^{\lambda})=R_{U_{\gamma,s}}(v)\,\text{ for every }v\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

The scaling argument above shows that the eigenvalues do not depend on
\lambda
. In particular, we can assume
\lambda=1
, and hence
U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}=U_{\gamma,s}
. Therefore, we would like to solve the eigenvalue problem

-\Delta v-\frac{\gamma}{|x|^{2}}v=\eta\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{|x|^{s}}v\qquad\text{ in }\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Putting
v=R(r)Y(\theta)
and using spherical co-ordinates we get

-\Delta\equiv-\Delta_{r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}},

where
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}.
Therefore, the separation of variables yields,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{r}v-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}v-\frac{\gamma}{r^{2}}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{r^{s}}v

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{N-1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\gamma}{r^{2}}\right)R(r)Y(\theta)+\frac{R(r)}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}Y(\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{|U_{\gamma,s}|^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{r^{s}}R(r)Y(\theta)

\displaystyle\frac{R^{\prime\prime}(r)+\tfrac{N-1}{r}R^{\prime}(r)+\tfrac{\gamma}{r^{2}}R(r)+\eta\tfrac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{r^{s}}R(r)}{r^{-2}R(r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}Y(\theta)}{Y(\theta)}=\mu\text{ (say)}.

Thus we have,

(5.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}Y=\mu Y\qquad\text{ on }\mathbb{S}^{N-1}

(5.3)

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}+\frac{N-1}{r}R^{\prime}+\frac{(\gamma-\mu)}{r^{2}}R+\eta\frac{U_{\gamma,s}^{2^{*}(s)-2}}{r^{s}}R=0,\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Let us begin with (
5.2
). The first eigenvalue is
\mu_{1}=0
and the corresponding eigenfunction is a constant function. The second eigenvalue is
\mu_{2}=(N-1)
and the corresponding eigenspace is
N

The second eigenvalue problem is regular at the origin and infinity. If
\mu=\mu_{1}=0,
then
\eta_{1}^{1}=\mu_{\gamma,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\tfrac{2^{*}(s)}{2}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{T_{\lambda}(U_{\gamma,s})^{\alpha_{1}}T_{\lambda}(U_{\gamma,s})^{\alpha_{2}}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(U_{\gamma,s})^{\alpha_{1}}(U_{\gamma,s})^{\alpha_{2}}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x,\quad\text{ for }\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}>1,\text{with }\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=2^{*}(s),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|T_{\lambda}(U_{\gamma,s})|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|U_{\gamma,s}|^{2^{*}(s)}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}(U_{\gamma,s})\|_{\gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|U_{\gamma,s}\|_{\gamma}^{2},

\displaystyle U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}(U_{\gamma,s}^{1})

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}T_{\lambda}(\partial_{\lambda}U_{\gamma,s}^{1}).

The operator
T_{\lambda}
plays a significant role in the study of Hardy-Sobolev inequality as they do not change the two quantities
\|\phi\|_{\gamma}
and
\|\phi\|_{L^{2^{*}(s)}(\mathbb{R}^{N},|x|^{-s})}
. These symmetries help us to consider
U_{\gamma,s}^{1}
instead of considering generic bubble
U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda}
.

Using the above symmetries it we can easily show the following :

Proposition 5.2
.

For
N\geq 3,
let
U:=U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{1}}
and
V:=U_{\gamma,s}^{\lambda_{2}}
be two bubbles with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}
and define
Q:=Q(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}
. Then for any fixed
\delta>0
we have,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{U(x)^{\theta}V(x)^{\eta}}{|x|^{s}}\,{\rm d}x\approx_{N,\gamma,s}\begin{cases}Q^{\varepsilon\min\{\theta,\,\eta\}}\qquad\text{if }|\theta-\eta|\geq\delta\\
Q^{\varepsilon\tfrac{N-s}{N-2}}\log(\lambda^{-1})\,\,\text{if }\theta=\eta=\frac{2^{*}(s)}{2},\end{cases}

Remark 5.2
.

The above results match the results of Figalli-Glaudo
(
[
19
]
, Lemma
B.1
and Proposition
B.2
)
for the case
\gamma=s=0
.

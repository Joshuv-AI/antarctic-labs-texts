Hyperbolic Anderson equations with general time-independent Gaussian noise: Stratonovich regime
Xia Chen, Yaozhong Hu

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the hyperbolic Anderson equation generated by a time-independent Gaussian noise with two objectives: The solvability and intermittency. First, we prove that Dalang’s condition is necessary and sufficient for existence of the solution. Second, we establish the precise long time and high moment asymptotics for the solution under the usual homogeneity assumption of the covariance of the Gaussian noise. Our approach is fundamentally different from the ones existing in literature. The main contributions in our approach include the representation of Stratonovich moment under Laplace transform via the moments of the Brownian motions in Gaussian potentials and some large deviation skills developed in dealing effectively with the Stratonovich chaos expansion.



Key-words
: Hyperbolic Anderson equation, Dalang’s condition,
rough and critical Gaussian noises, Stratonovich integrability, multiple Stratonovich integrals, Stratonovich expansion, Feynman-Kac’s representation,
Brownian motion, moment asymptotics, intermittency.

60J65, 81U10.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the hyperbolic Anderson equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+\dot{W}(x)u(t,x)\,,\hskip 14.45377pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 7.22743pt\hbox{and}\hskip 7.22743pt\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x)\,,\hskip 14.45377ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{array}\right.

run by a time-independent, mean zero and possibly generalized
Gaussian noise
\dot{W}(x)
with
the covariance function

\displaystyle{\mathop{{\rm Cov\,}}}\big(\dot{W}(x),\dot{W}(y)\big)=\gamma(x-y)\,,\hskip 14.45377ptx,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

(1.4)

As a covariance function the non-negative definiteness of
\gamma(\cdot)
implies that it admits
a spectral measure
\mu(d\xi)
on

\mathbb{R}^{d}
uniquely defined by the relation

\displaystyle\gamma(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu(d\xi)\,,\hskip 21.68121ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

(1.5)

Throughout this work, we assume that
\gamma(\cdot)\geq 0
and
d=1,2,3
.
The system is set up in Stratonovich regime in the sense that the product
in (
1
) is interpreted as the ordinary (instead of Wick) one.
The equation (
1
) will be approximated appropriately by
classical wave equations run by the smoothed
Gaussian noise
\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)
. We shall provide the details of the construction of the solution
in Section
2
.

Our first concern is the condition to ensure the existence of solution. It is often formulated
in terms of the integrability of the spectral measure
\mu(d\xi)
.
In the Skorohod regime, where
the product between

\dot{W}(x)
and
u(t,x)
in (
1
) is understood as Wick product,
the condition (
[
1
, Theorem 1.6]
,
[

, Remark 3.4]
)
that (

) has a unique solution
is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\bigg)^{3/2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty\,.

(1.6)

Back to the Stratonovich regime and still in the time independent setting, Balan (
[
2
]
) recently
proved that in the dimensions
d=1,2
Equation (
1
) has a
solution if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty\,.

(1.7)

In the setting of time-space Gaussian noise, Chen, Deya, Song and Tindel (
[
8
]

1.7
).

Our first main result is to obtain the best condition for the existence of the solution, which is to remove the square root in (

We can also allow the spatial dimension to be three as well.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

u_{0}(x)=1
and
u_{1}(x)=0
in (

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty

Roughly speaking, the system (

regime can be viewed as a randomization of the deterministic wave

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+f(x)u(t,x)\,,\hskip 14.45377pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 7.22743pt\hbox{and}\hskip 7.22743pt\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x)\,,\hskip 14.45377ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{array}\right.

with a deterministic potential function

on

. To this regard,

Our next topic is the ittermittency of the equation (

). More precisely,
our concern is the asymptotic behaviors of the moments

\mathbb{E}u^{p}(t,x)\hskip 7.22743pt\hbox{and}\hskip 7.22743pt\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{p}

To this end, we assume the

\displaystyle\gamma(cx)=c^{-\alpha}\gamma(x)\,,\hskip 14.45377ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,\hskip 7.22743ptc>0

Taking

In addition (Remark 1.4,

2
Stratonovich expansion and approximations

As usual by the Duhammel principle the mathematical definition of the hyperbolic Anderson equation (
1
) will be the following mild form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=u_{0}(t,x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}G(t-s,x-y)u(s,y)ds\bigg]W(dy)\,,

(2.1)

where

(i)

G(t,x)
is the fundamental solution defined by the
deterministic wave equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}G}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta G(t,x)\\
\\
\displaystyle G(0,x)=0\hskip 7.22743pt\hbox{and}\hskip 7.22743pt\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(0,x)=\delta_{0}(x)\,,\hskip 14.45377ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.\end{array}\right.

(ii)

u_{0}(t,x)
is the solution to the deterministic part of the equation (
1
):

u_{0}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G(t,x-y)u_{0}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t,x-y)u_{1}(y)dy\,.

Under the initial condition given in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
,

u_{0}(t,x)\equiv 1
;

(iii)

the stochastic integral on the right hand side of (
2.1
)
is interpreted as Stratonovich one (see discussion below for details).

2.1
Green’s function

The fundamental solution
G(t,x)
associated with (
(i)
) plays a key role in determining the behavior of
the system (
2.1
). Let us recall some basic facts.
Taking Fourier transform in (
(i)
) we get the expression for the
fundamental solution

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t,x)e^{i\xi\cdot x}dx=\frac{\sin(|\xi|t)}{|\xi|}\,,\hskip 14.45377pt(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

(2.5)

in its Fourier transform.
In the dimensions
d=1,2,3
, the fundamental solution
G(t,x)

itself can be expressed explicitly as

\displaystyle G(t,x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}1_{\{|x|\leq t\}}&\hskip 21.68121ptd=1\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1_{\{|x|\leq t\}}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}&\hskip 21.68121ptd=2\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi t}\sigma_{t}(dx)&\hskip 21.68121ptd=3\,,\end{cases}

(2.6)

where
\sigma_{t}(dx)
is the surface measure on the sphere
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3};\hskip 3.61371pt|x|=t\}
.
We limit our attention to
d=1,2,3
in this work because the treatment developed here
requires
G(t,x)\geq 0
. A scaling property we frequently use
(especially in the proof of Theorem
1.2
) is

\displaystyle G(t,x)=t^{-(d-1)}G(1,t^{-1}x)\,,\hskip 14.45377pt(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

(2.7)

2.2
Stratonovich integral

Before giving the definition of the mild solution we need to give a meaning to
the Stratonovich integral appeared in (
2.1
). We shall do this by smoothing the noise as follows

\displaystyle\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\dot{W}(y)p_{\varepsilon}(y-x)dy\,,\hskip 14.45377pt\varepsilon>0,\hskip 7.22743ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

(2.8)

where
p_{\varepsilon}(x)=(2\pi\varepsilon)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2\varepsilon}\right)

is the heat kernel. The covariance of
\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)
is

\mathbb{E}\left[\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)\right]=\gamma_{2\varepsilon}(x-y)\,,

(2.9)

where
\gamma_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma(z)p_{\varepsilon}(x-z)dz
.
Given a random field
\Psi(x)
(
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
) such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(x)\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)dx\in{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})\hskip 14.45377pt\forall\varepsilon>0\,,

We define the Stratonovich integral of
\{\Psi(x),x\in\mathbb{R}\}
as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(x)W(dx)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Delta}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(x)\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x)dx

(2.10)

whenever such limit exists in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
. We can also use the convergence in probability in above definition. But as in most works on SPDE,
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
norm is easier to deal with so that we choose the
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
convergence
throughout this work. Notice that this definition
implicates that
u(t,x)
as a
solution to (
2.1
) is in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. After defining the Stratonovich integral, we can give the following definition about the solution.

Definition 2.1
.

A random field
\{u(t,x)\,,t\geq 0\,,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is called a mild solution
to (
1
) if
\int_{0}^{t}G(t-s,x-y)u(s,y)ds
is well-defined and is
Stratonovich integrable such that (
2.1
) is satisfied.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we shall use the Stratonovich
expansion (see
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
and
references therein for the multiple
Stratonovich integrals).
Formally iterating (
2.1
)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}\big(g_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\big)

(2.11)

with
S_{0}\big(g_{0}(\cdot,t,x)\big)=1
. Here is how the notation

S_{n}\big(g_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\big)

The iteration procedure creates the recurrent relation

S_{n+1}\big(g_{n+1}(\cdot,t,x)\big)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!\left[\int_{0}^{t}G(t-s,x-y)S_{n}\big(g_{n}(\cdot,s,y)\big)ds\right]{W}(dy)\,.

(2.12)

Iterating this relation formally we have

\displaystyle S_{n}\big(g_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\big)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\bigg[\int_{[0,t]_{<}^{n}}d{\bf r}G(t-r_{n},y_{n}-x)\cdots G(r_{2}-r_{1},y_{2}-y_{1})\bigg]W(dx_{1})\cdots W(dx_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\bigg[\int_{[0,t]_{<}^{n}}d{\bf s}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}G(s_{k}-s_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{k-1})\bigg)\bigg]W(dx_{1})\cdots W(dx_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}g_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},t,x)W(dx_{1})\cdots W(dx_{n})\hskip 14.45377pt\hbox{(say)}\,,

where
[0,t]_{<}^{n}:=\left\{(s_{1},\cdots,s_{n})\in[0,t]^{n}\ \ \hbox{satisfying}\ \ 0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{n}<t\right\}
, and the conventions
x_{0}=x
and
s_{0}=0
are adopted and the above second equality follows
from the substitutions
s_{k}=t-r_{n-k+1}
and
x_{k}=y_{n-k+1}-x

(

).

Thus, the notation “
S_{n}\big(g_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\big)
” is reasonably introduced

a
n
-multiple Gaussian integral of the integrand

\displaystyle g_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},t,x)=\int_{[0,t]_{<}^{n}}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}G(s_{k}-s_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{k-1})\bigg)ds_{1}\cdots ds_{n}\,,

(2.14)

(
n=1,2,\cdots
). In Section 3, the Stratonovich
integrability of

shall be rigorously established (see

The above argument gives us the impression that the existence of system (

This will be justified rigorously in Section

The multiple Strtonovich integration is defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let

Then we define the

\displaystyle S_{n}(f):=

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1}\cdots,x_{n})W(dx_{1})\cdots W(dx_{n})

(2.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1}\cdots,x_{n})\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x_{k})\bigg)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}

{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
.

Remark 2.3
.

Along with the set-up of our model, the Stratonovich integrand

is

The following lemma provides a convenient test

Lemma 2.4
.

The

-multiple Stratonovich integral

if and only if the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0^{+}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots x_{n})\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x_{k})\bigg)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots x_{n})\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x_{k})\bigg)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\bigg\}

exists

Proof.

The existence of the limit in (
2.15
) is
another way to say that the family

{\cal Z}_{\varepsilon}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots x_{n})\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x_{k})\bigg)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\,,\quad\varepsilon>0\,,

is a Cauchy sequence in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
as

which is equivalent to the lemma.

\Box

We refer to Theorem
6.2

so that the multiple Stratonovich integral

exists in

The definition
2.2
can be extended to a random field

we deal with a deterministic integrand and demand some effective ways to compute the expectation of multiple Stratonovich

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{2n}g_{k}=\sum_{{\cal D}\in\Pi_{n}}\prod_{(j,k)\in{\cal D}}\mathbb{E}g_{j}g_{k}\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\prod_{k=1}^{2n-1}g_{k}=0\,,\end{array}\right.

where
(g_{1},\cdots,g_{2n})
is a

and

is the set of all pair partitions of

. As a side remark,

Applying (

in the case of deterministic integrand

,
and taking the

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2n-1}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n-1})W(dx_{1})\cdots W(dx_{2n-1})\right]=0

(2.19)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n})W(dx_{1})\cdots W(dx_{2n})\right]

(2.20)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sum_{{\cal D}\in\Pi_{n}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2n}}\bigg(\prod_{(j,k)\in{\cal D}}\gamma(x_{j}-x_{k})\bigg)f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{2n})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{2n}

under the Stratonovich integrability on the left hand sides.
In particular, the expectation of a

-multiple Stratonovich
integral is non-negative if the integrand is non-negative.

Since Dalang’s condition (

for any

3.3
Link to Dalang-Mueller-Tribe’s work

The discussion in this sub-section does not contribute to the proof of the main theorems in this paper. Rather, it
helps the interested reader to better understand the true nature of Stratonovich solution and
provide a new representation to the Laplace transform of the deterministic system (
1
)
for possible future investigation.

Let
N(t)
(
t\geq 0
) be a Poisson process with parameter 1 and
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be the jumping times of
N(t)
with
definition
\tau_{0}=0
. The stochastic process
X_{t}
(
t\geq 0
) is defined as follows: First, the random sequence

\{X_{\tau_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
is a random sequence whose finite-dimensional distribution of
(X_{\tau_{1}},\cdots,X_{\tau_{k}})

has the conditional distribution (conditioning on
\{\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n}\}
)

\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}(\tau_{k}-\tau_{k-1})^{-1}G(\tau_{k}-\tau_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{k-1})\bigg)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\,.

Set
X_{\tau_{0}}=X_{0}=x
. The process
X_{t}
is defined as the linear interpolation of
\{X_{\tau_{k}}\}_{k\geq 0}
.

Dalang, Mueller and Tribe (Theorem 3.2,
[
11
]
) prove that the function

\displaystyle u(t,x)=e^{t}\mathbb{E}_{x}\Bigg[u_{0}(t-\tau_{N(t)},X_{\tau_{N(t)}})\prod_{k=1}^{N(t)}(\tau_{k}-\tau_{k-1})f(X_{\tau_{k}})\Bigg]

(3.28)

solves the wave equation (
1
), where
u_{0}(t,x)
appears in (
2.1
). For the purpose of comparison,
we consider the case when
u_{0}(t,x)=1
and write

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{t}\mathbb{P}\{N(t)=n\}\mathbb{E}_{x}\Bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{n}(\tau_{k}-\tau_{k-1})f(X_{\tau_{k}})\bigg|N(t)=n\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{t^{n}\over n!}\mathbb{E}_{x}\Bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{n}(\tau_{k}-\tau_{k-1})f(X_{\tau_{k}})\bigg|N(t)=n\Bigg]\,.

By the classic fact that conditioning on
\{N(t)=n\}
, the
n
-dimensional vector
(\tau_{1},\cdots,\tau_{n})
is uniformly
distribution on
[0,t]_{<}^{n}
,

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,t]_{<}^{n}}ds_{1}\cdots ds_{n}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}G(s_{k}-s_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{k-1})\bigg)\prod_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}g_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},t,x)\prod_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})

(3.29)

with the convention
s_{0}=0
and
x_{0}=x
. Comparing this with (
(i)
) and (
2.13
) we see the deterministic root of
stochastic model (
1
) in the Stratonovich setting.

Similar to (
3.10
), the same computation leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}g_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},t,x)\prod_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})

(3.30)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\lambda}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}t\Big\}\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}f\big(B(s)\big)ds\bigg]^{n}dt\,.

Summing both sides over
n
, we obtain the following representation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}u(t,x)dt={\lambda\over 2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}t\Big\}\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}f\big(B(s)\big)ds\bigg\}dt

(3.31)

in the sense that finite of one side leads to finite of the other
side, and to the equality.

The classic semi-group theory (see, e.g., Section 4.1,
[
4
]
) claims
an asymptotically linear growth of the logarithmic exponential moment

\log\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}f\big(B(s)\big)ds\bigg\}\hskip 14.45377pt(t\to\infty)

for a class of functions
f
. In this case, the right hand side
of (
3.31
) is finite for large
\lambda
.

On the other hand, the representation (
3.31
) unlikely makes sense
for the stochastic wave equation (
1
). Under the assumption in
Theorem
1.2
, we have (
[
5
]
)

\log\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\dot{W}\big(B(s)\big)ds\bigg\}\sim C(\gamma)t(\log t)^{2\over 4-\alpha}\hskip 14.45377pta.s.

for some constant
C(\gamma)>0
as
t\to\infty
. So (
3.31
) almost surely blows up
for any
\lambda>0
.

]
, therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\big\{Z_{t_{0}}\geq a+b\big\}\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\big\{Z_{t_{0}}\geq a\big\}\mathbb{P}_{0}\big\{Z_{t_{0}}\geq b\big\}

for any
t_{0},a,b>0
. Thus, for any integer
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}^{n}

\displaystyle=(e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t})^{n}\mathbb{E}_{0}\Big({Z_{t}\over e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}}\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=(e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t})^{n}n\int_{0}^{\infty}b^{n-1}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{Z_{t}\geq eb\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}\}db

\displaystyle=(e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t})^{n}\bigg\{n\int_{0}^{1}b^{n-1}db+n\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{Z_{t}\geq eb\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}\}db\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq(e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t})^{n}\bigg\{1+n\int_{1}^{\infty}b^{n-1}\Big(\mathbb{P}_{0}\{Z_{t}\geq e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}\}\Big)^{b-1}db\bigg\}\,.

The claimed bound (
6.3
) follows from the following estimation

\int_{1}^{\infty}b^{n-1}\Big(\mathbb{P}_{0}\{Z_{t}\geq e\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}\}\Big)^{b-1}db\leq e\int_{0}^{\infty}b^{n-1}e^{-b}db=en!

and the bound ((A.6), Appendix,
[
5
]
)

\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}\leq\Big(\mathbb{E}_{0}Z_{t}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\Big(C(t\vee t^{2})\Big)^{1/2}\,.

We now prove (
6.2
). Let
\dot{W}(x)
be a Gaussian noise
independent of
B,B_{1},B_{2}
and having covariance
\gamma(\cdot)
.
Conditioning on the Brownian motions

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\dot{W}\big(B_{1}(s)\big)ds\bigg]\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\dot{W}\big(B_{2}(s)\big)ds\bigg]=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{1}(s)-B_{2}(r)\big)dsdr\,.

In addition, by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\dot{W}\big(B_{1}(s)\big)ds\bigg]\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\dot{W}\big(B_{2}(s)\big)ds\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\dot{W}\big(B_{1}(s)\big)ds\bigg]^{2}\bigg\}^{1/2}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\dot{W}\big(B_{2}(s)\big)ds\bigg]^{2}\bigg\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{1}(s)-B_{1}(r)\big)dsdr\bigg\}^{1/2}\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{2}(s)-B_{2}(r)\big)dsdr\bigg\}^{1/2}\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{1}(s)-B_{2}(r)\big)dsdr\leq

\displaystyle\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{1}(s)-B_{1}(r)\big)dsdr\bigg\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\times\bigg\{\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{2}(s)-B_{2}(r)\big)dsdr\bigg\}^{1/2}\,.

By the independence between
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B_{1}(s)-B_{2}(r)\big)dsdr\bigg]^{n}\leq\bigg\{\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B(s)-B(r)\big)dsdr\bigg]^{n/2}\bigg\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\gamma\big(B(s)-B(r)\big)dsdr\bigg]^{n}\,.

Therefore, (
6.2
) follows from (
6.1
).

\Box

6.2
Hu-Meyer formula

Although Lemma
2.4
gives a way for us
to show the existence of a multiple Stratonovich integral we also need to know what kind
general conditions to impose on
f
so that its multiple Stratonovich
integral
S_{n}(f)
exists, namely the approximation in (
2.15
) has a limit in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
. If the multiple Stratonovich integral
S_{n}(f)
exists in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
, then according to general Itô-Wiener’s chaos expansion theorem it admits a chaos expansion and it is interesting to find this chaos expansion.
For this we shall establish a Hu-Meyer formula along the line of

[
15
,
16
]
. If
f\in{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
is a (generalized) symmetric function of
n
-variables such that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{{\cal H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}:=&\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})f(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\\
&\qquad\times\gamma(x_{1}-y_{1})\cdots\gamma(x_{n}-y_{n})dx_{1}dy_{1}\cdots dx_{n}dy_{n}<\infty\,,\end{split}

(6.4)

then its multiple Itô-Skorohod integral
exists and is denoted by

I_{n}(f)=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\delta W(x_{1})\cdots\delta W(x_{n})\,,

where
\delta W
denotes the Itô-Skorohod stochastic integral.
To precisely define
{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
, we can complete
the set of all symmetric smooth functions
with compact supports under the Hilbert norm defined by (
6.4
). It is well-known that the Hilbert space
{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
contains generalized functions (see e.g.
[
19
]
).

Recall our definition (
2.8
) that
W_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\varepsilon}(x-y)W(dy)=I_{1}(p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot))
.
From
[
14
, Corollary 5.1, Equation 5.3.15]
, it follows that the chaos expansion of
\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x_{k})
is

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n/2}\sum_{i_{1}<j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}<j_{k}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{i_{\ell}}-y)\gamma(y-z)p_{\varepsilon}(x_{j_{\ell}}-z)dydz

(6.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad I_{n-2k}(\Lambda_{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}}\otimes_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-\cdot))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n/2}\sum_{i_{1}<j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}<j_{k}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\gamma_{2\varepsilon}(x_{i_{\ell}}-x_{j_{\ell}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad I_{n-2k}(\Lambda_{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}}\otimes_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-\cdot))\,,

where

(i)

The set of distinct elements

i_{1}<j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}<j_{k}
is a subset of

\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
and the summation
\sum_{i_{1}<j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}<j_{k}}
is over all such distinct pairs;

(ii)

The function
\Lambda_{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}}\otimes_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-\cdot)
is defined as the symmetrization of
the function

\prod_{m\in[1,n]\setminus\{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}\}}p_{\epsilon}(x_{m}-y_{m})

over the variables
\big(y_{m};\hskip 3.61371ptm\in[1,n]\setminus\{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}\}\big)
, i.e.,

\Lambda_{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}}\otimes_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-y_{m})={1\over(n-2k)!}\sum_{\sigma}\prod_{m\in[1,n]\setminus\{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}\}}p_{\epsilon}(x_{m}-y_{\sigma(m)})

where the summation is over all permutations
\sigma
on

[1,n]\setminus\{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}\}
. When
k=0
,
in particular, we follow the natural convention that

\Lambda_{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}}\otimes_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-y_{m})={1\over n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\prod_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-y_{\sigma(m)})\,,

where
\Sigma_{n}
is the permutation group on
\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

(iii)

I_{n-2k}(\cdots)
is the multiple Itô-Wiener (Itô-Skorohod) integral
with the integration variables

\left\{y_{m}\,;\ m\in[1,n]\setminus\{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}\}\right\}
.

With the above chaos expansion
(
6.5
) we see that the chaos expansion of
the approximated Stratonovich integral is

\displaystyle S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{n}\dot{W}_{\varepsilon}(x_{k})\bigg)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}

(6.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n/2}\sum_{i_{1}<j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}<j_{k}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\gamma_{2\varepsilon}(x_{i_{\ell}}-x_{j_{\ell}})\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times I_{n-2k}\Big(\Lambda_{i_{1},j_{1},\cdots,i_{k},j_{k}}\otimes_{m=1}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{m}-\cdot)\Big)dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\,.

By the symmetry of
f
on
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}

and with a combinatorial analysis as in
[
16
]

the above equation can be written

\displaystyle S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n/2}\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}I_{n-2k}\left(\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\gamma_{2\varepsilon}(x_{2\ell-1}-x_{2\ell})\right.

(6.7)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\prod_{j={2k+1}}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}-\cdot))dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\right)\,.

Since the approximated multiple integral can be decomposed to finite sum of multiple Itô-Wiener integrals which are orthogonal, we see that the convergence in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
of (
2.15
) is equivalent to that each of the multiple Itô-Wiener integrals in (
6.7
) converges in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
. Thus, we have the following theorem which is used to justify (
2.22
).

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
f\in{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
be deterministic
and symmetric.
If the trace

\displaystyle{\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f(y_{2k+1},\cdots,y_{n})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\gamma_{2\varepsilon}(x_{2\ell-1}-x_{2\ell})

(6.8)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{j={2k+1}}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}-y_{j}))dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}

exists in
{\cal H}^{\otimes(n-2k)}
for all
k\leq n/2
, then the Stratonovich
integral
S_{n}(f)
exists as an
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
limit of

S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and we have the following Hu-Meyer formula:

S_{n}(f)=\sum_{k\leq n/2}\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}I_{n-2k}({\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f)\,.

(6.9)

Conversely, if

S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)
is a Cauchy sequence in

{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
, then the right hand side of (
6.8
) is a Cauchy sequence in

{\cal H}^{\otimes(n-2k)}
for all
k\leq n/2
, whose limit is denoted by the left hand side of
(
6.8
) and
S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)
converges to
S_{n}(f)
defined by (
6.9
) in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
.
Moreover, if

S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)
converges to
S_{n}(f)
in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
, then
this convergence also takes place in

{\cal L}^{p}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. This means that

S_{n}(f)
is in
{\cal L}^{p}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Remark 6.3
.

It is obvious that if
f
is the symmetrization
of
\hat{f}
, then by the above definition it is easy to verify that

S_{n}(f)=S_{n}(\hat{f})
.

Proof of the theorem.

Denote

g_{n,k,\varepsilon}(y_{2k+1},\cdots,y_{n}):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2n}}f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\gamma_{2\varepsilon}(x_{2\ell-1}-x_{2\ell})\prod_{j={2k+1}}^{n}p_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}-y_{j}))dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\,.

Equation (
6.8
) means
\|g_{n,k,\varepsilon}-{\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f\|_{{\cal H}^{\otimes(n-2k}}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
By the Itô isometry,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|I_{n-2k}(g_{n,k,\varepsilon})&-I_{n-2k}({\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f)|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|I_{n-2k}(g_{n,k,\varepsilon}-{\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f)|^{2}\\
=&(n-2k)!\|g_{n,k,\varepsilon}-{\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f\|_{{\cal H}^{\otimes(n-2k)}}^{2}\to 0\end{split}

(6.10)

by (
6.8
).
Equation (
6.7
) tells that

S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)
converges to

S_{n}(f)
given by (
6.9
).

Now we assume that
S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)
is a Cauchy sequence in
{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{P})
.
With our notation
g_{n,k,\varepsilon}
we can write

\displaystyle S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n/2}\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}I_{n-2k}\left(g_{n,k,\varepsilon}\right)\,.

Thus, by the orthogonality of multiple Itô-Wiener integrals,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)-S_{n,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(f)\right]^{2}=&\sum_{k\leq n/2}\left(\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}\right)^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{n-2k}\left(g_{n,k,\varepsilon}\right)-I_{n-2k}\left(g_{n,k,\varepsilon^{\prime}}\right)\right]^{2}\\
=&\sum_{k\leq n/2}\left(\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}\right)^{2}(n-2k)!\|g_{n,k,\varepsilon}-g_{n,k,\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|_{{\cal H}^{\otimes(n-2k)}}^{2}\,.\end{split}

This can be used to prove the second part of the theorem easily.

Recall that if
F=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n}(f_{n})
is the chaos expansion of
F
, then
the second quantization operator
(e.g.
[
14
]
) of a number
\alpha\in[-1,1]

is defined as

\Gamma(\alpha)F=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{n}I_{n}(f_{n})\,.

Now for any
p>2
, let
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{1}{p-1}}
and let

\displaystyle F_{t,n,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n/2}(1/\alpha)^{n-2k}\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}\left[I_{n-2k}\left(g_{n,k,\varepsilon}\right)-I_{n-2k}({\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f)\right]\,.

(6.11)

Then
by the hypercontractivity inequality (e.g.
[
14
, p. 54, Theorem 3.20]
, we have

\begin{split}\left(\mathbb{E}|S_{n,\varepsilon}(f)\right.&\left.-S_{n}(f)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}=\left(\mathbb{E}|\Gamma(\alpha)F_{t,n,\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}|F_{t,n,\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
=&\left(\mathbb{E}|\sum_{k\leq n/2}(1/\alpha)^{n-2k}\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}\left[I_{n-2k}\left(g_{n,k,\varepsilon}\right)-I_{n-2k}({\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f)\right]|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq&\left(\sum_{k\leq n/2}(1/\alpha)^{2n-4k}\frac{(n!)^{2}}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}((n-2k)!)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{n-2k}\left(g_{n,k,\varepsilon}\right)-I_{n-2k}({\mathop{{\rm Tr\,}}}^{k}f)\right]^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,,\end{split}

which converges to
0
by (
6.10
).
This proves the theorem.

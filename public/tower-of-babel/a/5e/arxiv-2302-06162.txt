Abstract
. In this work, we focus on the global solvability and uniform large deviations for the solutions of stochastic generalized Burgers-Huxley (SGBH) equation perturbed by a small multiplicative white in time and colored in space noise. The SGBH equation has the nonlinearity of polynomial order and noise considered in this work is infinite dimensional with a coefficient having linear growth. First, we prove the existence of a unique local mild solution in the sense of Walsh to SGBH equation with the help of a truncation argument and contraction mapping principle. Then the global solvability results are established by using uniform bounds of the local mild solution, stopping time arguments, tightness properties and Skorokhod’s representation theorem. By using the uniform Laplace principle, we obtain the large deviation principle (LDP) for the law of solutions to SGBH equation by using variational representation methods. Further, we derive the uniform large deviation principle (ULDP) for the law of solutions in two different topologies by using a weak convergence method. First, in the C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , L p ​ ( [ 0 , 1 ] ) ) \mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1])) topology where the uniformity is over L p ​ ( [ 0 , 1 ] ) \mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1]) -bounded sets of initial conditions, and secondly in the C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] × [ 0 , 1 ] ) \mathrm{C}([0,T]\times[0,1]) topology with uniformity being over bounded subsets in the C ⁡ ( [ 0 , 1 ] ) \mathrm{C}([0,1]) -norm. Finally, we consider SGBH equation perturbed by a space-time white noise with bounded noise coefficient and establish the ULDP for the laws of solutions. The results obtained in this work hold true for stochastic Burgers’ as well as Burgers-Huxley equations.



1.
Introduction

Burgers’ equation
(
[
4
]
) is one of the celebrated fundamental partial differential equations and is the simplest model for analyzing combined effect of nonlinear convection and diffusion. Burgers’ equation appears in various areas of applied mathematics such as fluid mechanics, traffic flow, nonlinear acoustics, gas dynamics, etc. The
Burgers-Huxley equation
is a mathematical model that describes the dynamics of a non-linear, one-dimensional wave system that is subject to both deterministic and stochastic influences. It is a combination of the Burgers equation
[
4
]
, which describes the nonlinear behavior of a fluid flow, and the Huxley equation
[
39
]
, which describes the dynamics of an action potential in a nerve or muscle cell. The Burgers-Huxley equation is a good example of convection-diffusion-reaction equations. In the stochastic version of the equation, a random noise is added to the system to account for the influence of external factors such as thermal fluctuations or other sources of randomness.

1.1.
The model

In this work, we consider the following generalized version of Burgers-Huxley equation driven by a random forcing indexed by
\epsilon\in(0,1]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\nu\frac{\partial^{2}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial x^{2}}-\alpha u_{\epsilon}^{\delta}(t,x)\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial x}+\beta u_{\epsilon}(t,x)(1-u_{\epsilon}^{\delta}(t,x))(u_{\epsilon}^{\delta}(t,x)-\gamma)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\epsilon}g(t,x,u_{\epsilon}(t,x))\mathcal{W}(t,x),~~~~~t>0,~x\in(0,1),

where
\nu>0
and
\alpha>0
are the viscosity and convection coefficients, respectively,
\beta>0,\ \delta\geq 1
,
\gamma\geq 1
are parameters and
\epsilon\in(0,1]
is the intensity of the noise, with the Dirichlet boundary condition and initial data

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}(t,0)=u_{\epsilon}(t,1)=0,\ \ t\geq 0,\ \ \text{ and }\ \ u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=u^{0}(x),

respectively. The noise coefficient
g(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
has linear growth and it satisfies a Lipschitz condition in the third variable (see Hypothesis
1.1
below).

In the deterministic case, for
\beta=0
and
\delta=1
, the equation (
1.1
) represents the
classical viscous Burgers equation
and for
\delta=1
, it is called the
Burgers-Huxley equation
. The equation is commonly used in the study of a wide range of physical and biological systems, including fluid dynamics, neural networks, and pattern formation. For
\beta=0
and
\alpha=1
, the equation gives the following generalized Burgers equations

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial t}=\nu\frac{\partial^{2}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial x^{2}}-\alpha u_{\epsilon}^{\delta}(t,x)\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial x}+\sqrt{\epsilon}g(t,x,u_{\epsilon}(t,x))\mathcal{W}(t,x).

which is very famous as a toy model to study the turbulence in fluids and the model has been explored a lot in the literature, for instance see
[
7
,
8
,
15
,
16
,
22
,
24
]
, etc. and references therein. For
\alpha=0
, the equation (
1.1
) gives rise to the following equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\nu\frac{\partial^{2}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)}{\partial x^{2}}+\beta u_{\epsilon}(t,x)+\beta u_{\epsilon}(t,x)(t-u_{\epsilon}^{\delta}(t,x))(u_{\epsilon}^{\delta}(t,x)-\gamma)

(1.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\epsilon}g(t,x,u_{\epsilon}(t,x))\mathcal{W}(t,x),\ \ \text{ for }\ \ (t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,1).

The equation (
1.1
) is known as the
stochastic Huxley equation
and it models the propagation of nerve pulse in nerve fibers and wall motion in liquid crystals (see
[
39

).

1.2.
A short note on the noise
\mathcal{W}(\cdot,\cdot)

Let us now characterize the noise
\mathcal{W}(\cdot,\cdot)
appearing in (
1.1
) on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
(see
[
25
]
). Consider a sequence
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
of independent standard Gaussian random variables on this basis (see Lemma II.2.3,
[
23
]
). If
\{\mathrm{m}_{j}(t)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis in
\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,T]),
then

\beta(t):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{m}_{j}(s)ds}}\right)\xi_{j}

is a standard Brownian motion with covariance
\mathbb{E}[\beta(t)\beta(s)]=(t\wedge s)
.
Consider a sequence
\{\beta_{j}(t)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of independent standard Brownian motions and an orthonormal basis
\{\varphi_{j}(x)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in the space
\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,1])
. Let us define

h_{j}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}\varphi_{j}(y)dy.

Then the process

\mathrm{W}(t,x):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}h_{j}(x)\beta_{j}(t)

is Gaussian with the mean zero and covariance
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{W}(t,x)\mathrm{W}(s,y)]=(t\wedge s)(x\wedge y).
The process
\mathrm{W}
is known as the
Brownian sheet.
The formal term-by-term differentiation of the series in
\beta(t)
suggests a representation

\displaystyle\dot{\beta}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{m}_{k}(t)\xi_{k},

so that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathrm{W}}{\partial t\partial x}(t,x)=\dot{\mathrm{W}}(t,x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\varphi_{j}(x)\dot{\beta}_{j}(t),

and we call the process
\dot{\mathrm{W}},
the
(Gaussian) space-time white noise.
Even though the series diverges, it defines a random generalized function on
\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,T]\times[0,1])
:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathrm{W}}(f)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(t,x)\varphi_{j}(x)\mathrm{d}x}}\right)\mathrm{d}\beta_{j}(t)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}f(t,x)\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}x).

To construct a noise, that is, white in time and colored in space, take a sequence of non-negative numbers
\{q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and define

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathrm{W}^{\mathrm{Q}}}{\partial t\partial x}(t,x)=\dot{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{Q}}(t,x)=\mathcal{W}(t,x)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}q_{j}\varphi_{j}(x)\dot{\beta}_{j}(t),

and we call the process
\dot{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{Q}},
the
(Gaussian) white in time and colored in space noise.
Whenever we consider
\dot{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{Q}}
in the work, we assume that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}q_{j}^{2}<\infty.

Let the family
\{\varphi_{j}(x)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,1])
consisting of the eigenfunctions of the operator
-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. In fact, for
j=1,2,\ldots

\lambda_{j}=j^{2}\pi^{2},\ \varphi_{j}(x)=\sqrt{2}\sin(j\pi x).\

Then an example of a family
\{q_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
1.7
) is
\{\lambda_{j}^{-\eta}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
for
\eta>\frac{1}{4}
, since
\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{j^{4\eta}}<\infty
provided
\eta>\frac{1}{4}
.

\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}([0,1])

1.3.2.
Hypotheses and mild solution

Let us introduce the linear growth and Lipschitz conditions on the noise coefficient.

Hypothesis 1.1
.

The function
g:[0,T]\times[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function, satisfying the following conditions:

(1.8)

\displaystyle|g(t,x,r)|\leq K(1+|r|),\ \ \text{ and }\ \ |g(t,x,r)-g(t,x,s)|\leq L|r-s|,

for all

The

(1.9)

\displaystyle G(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\bigg[e^{-\frac{(y-x-2m)^{2}}{4t}}-e^{-\frac{(y+x-2m)^{2}}{4t}}\bigg],

for all
t\in(0,T]
and

.

(Mild solution)

Literature Survey

The SGBH equation (
1.1
) covers both the models given by equations (
1.3
) and (
1.1
). The global solvability results of deterministic generalized Burgers-Huxley (GBH) equation and numerical studies have been carried out in
[
13
,
19
,
28
]
, etc. and references therein. For the global solvability results of the stochastic counterpart, one can see
[
20
,
21
,
26
,
27
]
, etc. In the works
[
20
,
21
,
26
]
, the noise coefficient
g(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is taken as a bounded function, while in
[
27
]
Burgers-Huxley equation is considered with the
\mathrm{Q}
-Wiener process
having noise coefficient as a Hilbert-Schmidt operator which satisfy a linear growth and Lipschitz conditions.

The
large deviation theory
is one of the most popular research topics in probability theory and it explores the limiting behavior of the probabilities of rare events in the terms of a rate function. Though the topic of large deviations is much older, the modern theories were mainly developed by Donsker and Vardhan
[
10
,
36
]
, and Freidlin and Wentzell
[
12
]
. In this work, we are following the
uniform large deviation principle
(ULDP) introduced by Freidlin and Wentzell which is known as the
Freidlin-Wentzell uniform large deviation principle
(FWULDP) in the honor of their names. It is one of the most popular definitions of ULDP. Specifically, the work
[
12
]
describes the asymptotic behavior of probability of large deviations of the law of the solution to a family of small noise finite-dimensional SDEs, away from its law of large number dynamics. In order to prove ULDP, one can use different types of
uniform Lapalce principles
(ULP) with additional assumptions available in the literature.
In the work
[
3
]
, the authors proved FWULDP for diverse families of infinite-dimensional stochastic dynamical systems by using certain variational representations. They developed the weak convergence method for proving the ULDP by using the ULP. In order to obtain the ULDP for the law of the solution to certain SPDEs, they have provided a sufficient condition, and to verify the condition, one needs some basic qualitative properties of the solution like existence, uniqueness, and tightness of the controlled analogues of the original SPDEs. Adopting the approach established in
[
3
]
, the authors in
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
34
]
, etc. obtained several results in this direction for different types of SPDEs. The author in the work
[
29
]
established the equivalences between four different kinds of definitions of ULDP and ULP.

One of the main concerns in the method provided by
[
3
]
is that it requires the convergence of initial conditions and therefore one cannot use this method directly to prove ULDP for SPDEs having non-compact sets of the initial conditions. For several applications, for example, in the case of the study of exit times of a stochastic process
``u"
from its domain, the large deviations of
``u"
must be uniform over the initial conditions lie in the bounded subsets of the space that are not necessarily compact
[
30
]
. Recently, in the work
[
29
]
, the author introduced a new definition called the
equicontinuous uniform Laplace principle
(EULP) of ULP and showed that the definition of EULP is equivalent to the definition of FWULDP without any compactness assumptions. In order to prove EULP, he came with a sufficient condition under which the law of the solution follows the ULDP with uniformity being over initial conditions that belong to bounded but not necessarily compact sets. In the work
[
31
]
, the authors adopted the methodology from
[
29
]
and established the ULDP for the law of the solution of the stochastic Burgers type equations. Here, they considered the nonlinearity of polynomial growth of any order and the driving noise as a finite-dimensional Wiener process. Later, the author in
[
33
]
proved the ULDP for the law of the solutions to a class of semilinear SPDEs driven by Brownian sheet with bounded noise coefficient. In both works
[
31
]
and
[
33
]
, the uniformity is considered with respect to the initial conditions that are bounded and do not necessarily belongs to a compact set.

1.5.
Objectives and novelties

In this work, we are mainly considering the SGBH system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) consisting of a multiplicative noise which is white in time and colored in space with the coefficient
g(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
having a linear growth with respect to the unknown
u_{\epsilon}
(Hypothesis
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, this type of noise for this model and the following results on this model have not been explored yet.

•

First we prove the existence of a unique local mild solution to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in the sense of Walsh (Definition
1.2
) by using a truncated system and fixed point arguments. Then by using the uniform energy estimates of the local solution, stopping time arguments, tightness properties and Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we prove the global existence of the solution.

•

Next, we move to the LDP part, where we discuss two different types of LDP depending on the availability of the initial conditions.

(1)

The first LDP result is based on a variational representation method developed in
[
3
]
and to use it, we need the initial data over a compact subset.

(2)

If we are given the non-compact sets of initial data, then it is not enough to establish ULP as mentioned in
[
3
]
, and hence for this situation, we borrow ideas from the works
[
29
,
31
]
. We verify the sufficient condition for EULP and hence ULDP in two different topologies: the
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1]))
topology where the uniformity is over
\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1])
-bounded sets of initial conditions (for
p\geq\max\{6,2\delta+1\}
), and secondly in the
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times[0,1])
topology with uniformity being over bounded subsets in the
\mathrm{C}([0,1])
-norm. To prove the ULDP in
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times[0,1])
, we use the embedding
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times[0,1])\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1]))
and Vardhan’s contraction principle
[
35
]
.

•

Finally, we consider SGBH equation perturbed by a space-time white noise with bounded noise coefficient and establish the ULDP for the laws of solutions in the
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1])),
for
p\geq 2\delta+1
and
\mathrm{C}([0,T]\times[0,1])
topologies.

The well-posedess and LDP results obtained in this work hold true for stochastic Burgers as well as Burgers-Huxley equations. The main difference between the works
[
31
,
33
,
34
]
is that none of them considers the infinite dimensional multiplicative time white and space colored noise with coefficient having linear growth.

The main difficulties in proving our results are the presence of a polynomial type of nonlinearity and the infinite-dimensional of noise with unbounded noise coefficient. The polynomial nonlinearites are handled by using Green’s function estimates (see Appendix
A
). We overcame the difficulty of a proper Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-spaces by using the results from
[
37
,
40
]
. Recently, the authors in
[
37
,
40
]
obtained BDG inequality in UMD Banach spaces, which helped us to handle several maximal inequalities. Precisely, the authors in
[
37
]
showed that if
\mathrm{M}
is an
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-bounded martingale,
1<p<\infty
, with
\mathrm{M}_{0}=0
, that takes values in a UMD Banach space
(\mathrm{X},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{X}})
over a measure space
(S,\Sigma,\mu)
, then for all
t\geq 0
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\mathrm{M}_{s}(\sigma)\|_{\mathrm{X}}^{p}}}\right]\leq C_{p,\mathrm{X}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\|[\mathrm{M}(\sigma)]_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathrm{X}}^{p}}}\right],

where the quadratic variation
[\mathrm{M}(\sigma)]_{t}
is considered pointwise in
\sigma\in S
. As
\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1]),

1<p<\infty
is a UMD Banach space, we can use the above BDG inequality in the sequel. We also point out that in the case of space-time white noise with bounded noise coefficient, we are able to prove the ULDP in the
\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1]))
topology for
p\geq 2\delta+1
, as we are not using Lemma
2.3
for the tightness of stochastic convolution.

2.1.
Local solution

In order to obtain a mild solution of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2

\Pi_{R}(\cdot)

\mathrm{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})

(2.1)

\Pi_{R}(r)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\ \ &\text{ if }\ \ |r|\leq R,\\

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{\epsilon,R}(t,x)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\nu\frac{\partial^{2}u_{\epsilon,R}(t,x)}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{\delta+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Pi_{R}(\|u_{\epsilon,R}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}})\mathfrak{p}(u_{\epsilon,R}(t,x))+\beta\Pi_{R}(\|u_{\epsilon,R}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}})\mathfrak{c}(u_{\epsilon,R}(t,x))

\displaystyle\quad+\beta\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,x,y)\Pi_{R}(\|u_{\epsilon,R}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}})\mathfrak{c}(u_{\epsilon,R}(s,y))\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,x,y)\Pi_{R}(\|u_{\epsilon,R}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}})g(s,y,u_{\epsilon,R}(s,y))\mathrm{W}^{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y),\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Let us assume that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon,R}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]\leq C(R,T).

Definition 3.1

(LDP)

Let

(2)

For any closed subset

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon\log\mathbb{P}\big(X_{\epsilon}\in\mathrm{F}\big)\geq-\inf_{x\in\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{I}(x).

In this section, we recall the basic definitions of ULDP (in particular, Freidlin-Wentzell uniform large deviation principle (FWULDP), see

Let
(\mathcal{E},d)
be a Polish space and
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is a set. For any

(cf.
[

denote the level set

\displaystyle\Lambda_{u^{0}}(s_{0}):=\big\{f\in\mathcal{E}:\mathrm{I}_{u^{0}}(\psi)\leq s_{0}\big\},

for

\mathscr{T}
be the collection of subsets of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and
a(\epsilon)
be a function which converges to 0 as
\epsilon\to 0

If

is a random field and
\mathcal{E}
is a function space (will fix later), then
h
-a.s. in
\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{E}

Definition 4.1

(FWULDP)
.

\mathcal{E}

if the following hold:

(1)

For any
\mathrm{A}\in\mathscr{T},\ t_{0}\geq 0,
and

\displaystyle\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\inf_{u^{0}\in\mathrm{A}}\inf_{\psi\in\Lambda_{u^{0}}(t_{0})}\bigg\{a(\epsilon)\log\mathbb{P}\big(d(X_{\epsilon,u^{0}},\psi)<\eta\big)+\mathrm{I}_{u^{0}}(\psi)\bigg\}\geq 0.

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

5.
Bounded Non-linearity with Space-time White Noise

In this section, we define the noise as the derivatives of Brownian sheet
\frac{\partial^{2}\mathrm{W}(\cdot,\cdot)}{\partial t\partial x}:=\mathcal{W}(\cdot,\cdot)
on the probability space

Hypothesis 5.1

The function

(5.22)

\displaystyle|g(t,x,r)|\leq K,\ \ \text{ and }\ \ |g(t,x,r)-g(t,x,s)|\leq L|r-s|,

For the basic definitions of EULP and ULDP, we refer Section
4
. Let us comment on the solvability under the new Hypothesis
5.1
. In this part we are not using the global solvability result obtained in Section
2
, since the noise is space-time white noise. For this part, we recall the following solvability result from

]
under Hypothesis

Theorem 5.2

(Theorem 3.5,
[
20
]

Let us assume that the initial data

Our aim is to prove ULDP under the new Hypothesis

Hence by Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem (see

38

(5.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|u^{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\sup_{\phi\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times[0,1]}|\zeta_{\epsilon,\phi}(t,x)|^{p}\bigg]<\infty.

and

Remark 5.4
.

Let us take

\displaystyle\|u\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{pq}}=\||u|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2p}}^{2}\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{x}|u(x)|^{\frac{q}{2}}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\|u\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{pq}}\leq C_{q}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{x}u(x)|^{2}|u(x)|^{q-2}\mathrm{d}x,

(5.28)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}^{q}\mathrm{d}s\leq C_{q}\int_{0}^{t}\||u(s)|^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\partial_{x}u(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\quad+\beta\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,x,y)\big(\mathfrak{c}(u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s,y))-\mathfrak{c}(u_{0,\phi}(s,y))\big)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,x,y)\big(g(s,y,u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s,y))-g(s,y,u_{0,\phi}(s,y))\big)\phi(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,x,y)g(s,y,u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s,y))\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)

(5.35)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\delta+1}J_{\epsilon}^{1}(t,x)+\beta J_{\epsilon}^{2}(t,x)+J_{\epsilon}^{3}(t,x)+\sqrt{\epsilon}J_{\epsilon}^{4}(t,x).

Now, we consider
J_{\epsilon}^{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
. From (
4.8
)-(
4.10
), we have

(5.36)

\displaystyle\|J_{\epsilon}^{1}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}\leq C\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{2p}}\|u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s)-u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\mathrm{d}s,

where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
.
Using (
4.9
), (
4.2
) and (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|J_{\epsilon}^{2}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}

(5.37)

\displaystyle\leq C\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg\{(t-s)^{-\frac{\delta}{2p}}(1+\delta)(1+\gamma)+\gamma+(t-s)^{-\frac{\delta}{p}}(1+2\delta)\bigg\}\|u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s)-u_{0,\phi}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\mathrm{d}s,

where we have used Hölder’s inequality. Using Lemma 3.4,
[
16
]
, Hypothesis
5.1
(bound of
g
) and (
4.9
), we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|J_{\epsilon}^{3}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(t-s,\cdot,y)g(s,y,u_{\epsilon,\phi}(s,y))\mathrm{W}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\bigg]

(5.38)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,K),

for
p>2
. Combining (
5
)-(
5
) and applying Fubini’s theorem followed by Gronwall’s inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon,\phi}(t)-u_{0,\phi}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\exp\bigg\{C\int_{0}^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[|t-s|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{2p}}+|t-s|^{-\frac{\delta}{2p}}+\gamma+|t-s|^{-\frac{\delta}{p}}}}\right]\mathrm{d}s\bigg\}

(5.39)

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma,p,T,M,K,P)\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}.

For any
\varpi>0
. Using similar calculations as in (
4.2
) and Theorem
5.2
, there exists
P>0
large enough such that

(5.40)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|u^{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{\phi\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,1]}\big[1-\mathbb{P}(\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon})\big]\leq\varpi.

Using (
5
), (
5.40
), and Markov’s inequality, for any
\epsilon\in(0,1],u^{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}([0,1])
and
\phi\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|u^{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{\phi\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon,\phi}(t)-u_{0,\phi}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}>\eta\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|u^{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{\phi\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\big[1-\mathbb{P}(\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon})\big]+\sup_{\|u^{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leq N}\sup_{\phi\in\mathscr{P}_{2}^{M}}\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon,\phi}(t)-u_{0,\phi}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\chi_{\Upsilon_{P}^{\epsilon}}>\eta\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\varpi+\frac{C(\alpha,\beta,\delta,\gamma,p,T,M,P)\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}}{\eta^{p}}.

Due to arbitrary choice of

4.6
).

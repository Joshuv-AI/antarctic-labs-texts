Shortcuts to Adiabaticity for Fast Qubit Readout in Circuit Quantum Electrodynamics
F. A. Cárdenas-López, Xi Chen

Abstract
We propose how to engineer the longitudinal coupling to accelerate the measurement of a qubit longitudinally coupled to a cavity, motivated by the concept of shortcuts to adiabaticity. Different modulations are inversely designed from inverse engineering, counter-diabatic driving and genetic algorithm, for achieving optimally large values of the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) at nanosecond scale. By comparison, we demonstrate that our protocols outperform the usual periodic modulations on the pointer state separation and SNR. Finally, we show a possible implementation considering state-of-the-art circuit quantum electrodynamics architecture, estimating the minimal time allowed for the measurement process.

The most common measurement strategy is dispersive readout consisting in coupling an auxiliary system whose observables depend on the system state. In the past decade, shortcuts to adiabaticity (STA)
PhysRevLett.104.063002
have experienced a huge development, with the various applications of quantum computing and more generally quantum technologies
RevModPhys.91.045001

In this article, we propose STA method for elaborating the modulation of longitudinal coupling between a two-level system with a cavity mode to accelerate the qubit measurement. By using inverse engineering, we obtain large values for the cavity pointer state separation and the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) on a short timescale. Besides, the SNR is exponentially enhanced when the cavity is prepared in a squeezed state. For completeness, we further discuss the measurement process speed-up by counter-diabatic driving, and their possible implementations. Moreover, we use genetic algorithm, a search-based optimization technique, to find optimal or near-optimal modulation. Finally, a feasible experimental model regarding state-of-the-art cQED architecture is considered and the minimal time for the measurement process is estimated with the bound of coupling strength.

II
Longitudinal cavity-qubit interaction

We consider an LC oscillator of frequency
\omega_{r}
longitudinally coupled to a two-level system of frequency
\omega_{q}
with time-dependent coupling strength
g_{z}(t)
described through the Hamiltonian
PhysRevLett.115.203601
(
\hbar=1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}\sigma^{z}+\omega_{r}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}+g_{z}(t)\sigma^{z}(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}+\hat{a}).

(1)

Here
\sigma^{z}
is the
z-
component Pauli matrix describing the two-level system, and
a^{{\dagger}}
(
a
) is the creation (annihilation) operator of the LC oscillator. This Hamiltonian corresponds to a state-dependent displaced oscillator. When the cavity acts as a pointer state, we can perform high-fidelity quantum measurement on the qubit state since the cavity state displaces upwards or downwards on its phase state according to the qubit state. Furthermore, as the longitudinal interaction commutes with the free terms of the Hamiltonian such process is a quantum nondemolition measurement (QND)
PhysRevLett.115.203601
.

We aim to engineer
g_{z}(t)
inversely to accelerate the QND measurement process. For doing so, we propose a solution for the Schödinger equation of
\mathcal{H}
as
|\Psi(x,t)\rangle=e^{-iE_{\rm{LC}}t/\hbar}\mathcal{V}(t)|\varphi(x,t)\rangle|\xi\rangle
, where
E_{\rm{LC}}=\omega_{r}(n+1/2)
and
|\varphi(x,t)\rangle
correspond to the eigenenergies and eigenfunctions of the LC oscillator
\mathcal{H}_{\rm{LC}}=\omega_{r}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}
. Besides,
|\xi\rangle
describes the qubit state. As a consequence,
\mathcal{V}(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}e^{-i\dot{g}_{c}(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}+a)/\omega_{r}^{2}}e^{-g_{c}(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}-a)/\omega_{r}}
is a unitary transformation that eliminates the longitudinal coupling
PhysRevLett.112.150402
, where
\theta(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{g}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}
corresponds to a phase relating the coupling strength
g_{z}(t)
with the auxiliary variable
g_{c}(t)
through the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{g}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{g}^{2}_{c}(t)}{\omega_{r}^{3}}-\frac{g^{2}_{c}(t)}{\omega_{r}}+\frac{2g_{c}(t)g_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}}.

(2)

To guarantee that
|\Psi(x,t)\rangle
corresponds to the exact solution of the time-dependent Schödinger equation, the classical variables must obey the equation of motion, (see Appendix
A

\displaystyle\ddot{g}_{c}(t)+\omega_{r}^{2}[g_{c}(t)-g_{z}(t)]=0,

(3)

which is nothing but the Euler-Lagrange equation from Eq. (
2

3

IV
Comparision to Counter-diabatic driving

An alternative way to accelerate the qubit readout relies upon the counter-diabatic driving
RevModPhys.91.045001
.
Similar to STA for Rabi model
PhysRevLett.126.023602
, the counter-diabatic term for the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) is calculated
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm{CD}}=-i\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}}\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}-a).

(10)

This interaction could be implemented in cQED architecture with a tunable capacitive interaction
Tun_cap_cou
, which has not been easily implemented yet. To circumvent this problem, we utilize the multiple Schrödinger/interaction pictures
PhysRevLett.109.100403
and express the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}+\mathcal{H}_{\rm{CD}}
in a rotating frame, which has different structure, but the same underlying physics. By using
\mathcal{U}(t)=\exp(-i\dot{g}_{z}(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}+a)/\omega_{r}^{2})
, we arrive at the effective Hamiltonian
\bar{\mathcal{H}}=\omega_{q}\sigma^{z}/2+\omega_{r}\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}\hat{a}+\tilde{g}_{z}(t)\sigma^{z}(\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}+\hat{a})
, where
\tilde{g}_{z}(t)=g_{z}(t)+\ddot{g}_{z}(t)/\omega_{r}^{2}
is the effective longitudinal coupling. In this new frame, it only requires a new modulation on the coupling strength
\tilde{g}_{z}(t)
rather than complicated implementation, for instance, in a current experiment on open cQED
AnNC
.

Moreover, such approximate counter-diabatic driving can be implemented by using Floquet engineering (FE)
Seel2017
;
Claeys2019
;
PhysRevResearch.2.013283
;
PhysRevResearch.3.013227
. Here, we add a high-frequency driving with a complex time dependency to emulate the counter-diabatic term only using the operators available on the system Hamiltonian. In what follows we will calculate the FE Hamiltonian such that its dynamics corresponds to the same as
\mathcal{H}_{\rm{CD}}
. Essentially, to obtain the state preparation, up to the phase factor, it is not necessary to include the original Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
. For doing so, we define the Floquet engineering Hamiltonian as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm{FE}}(t)=\Omega\nu\sin(\nu t)(\sigma^{z}+a^{{\dagger}}a)+\lambda(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}+a),~~

(11)

where
\nu\ll\omega_{r}
is an arbitrary frequency, and
\Omega
is a free parameter. Next, we express
\mathcal{H}_{\rm{FE}}
in the rotating frame described by the following unitary transformation

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t)=\exp\bigg[i\Omega\cos\nu t(\sigma^{z}+a^{{\dagger}}a)\bigg].

(12)

In the rotating frame, the effective Hamiltonian
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{FE}}(t)=\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\mathcal{H}_{\rm{FE}}\hat{U}(t)-i\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\dot{\hat{U}}(t)
reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{FE}}=\lambda(t)\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}+a)\hat{U}(t).

(13)

Using the Bakker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, we express the transformed Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm{FE}}(t)=\lambda(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}e^{-i\Omega\cos\nu t}+ae^{i\Omega\cos\nu t}).

(14)

We proceed by calculating the average of the Hamiltonian over the period
T=2\pi/\nu
to get the first term of the Magnus expansion

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{\rm{FE}}(t)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}e^{-i\Omega\cos\nu t}+ae^{i\Omega\cos\nu t})dt.

(15)

To obtain that
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{\rm{FE}}=\mathcal{H}_{\rm{CD}}
we require that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(t)e^{-i\Omega\cos\nu t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}},

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda(t)e^{i\Omega\cos\nu t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}}.

(17)

We proceed by assuming that
\lambda(t)=\sum_{n}C_{n}\cos(n\nu t)
, and we define
\alpha=\nu t
. In this case, the integrals above defined read

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\frac{C_{n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(n\alpha)e^{-i\Omega\cos\alpha}d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}}.

(18)

Notice that this integral looks similar to the definition of the Bessel function

\displaystyle J_{n}(z)=\frac{i^{-n}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{iz\cos\theta}\cos(n\theta)d\theta.

(19)

With this definition Eq. (
17
) and Eq. (
17
) read

\displaystyle\sum_{n}i^{n}C_{n}J_{n}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}},

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{n}i^{n}C_{n}J_{n}(-\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}}.

(21)

These conditions are meet when
n=2m+1
,
\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, for
n=1
we have
\lambda(t)=C_{1}\cos(\nu t)
leading to

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}J_{1}(\Omega)}.

(22)

Finally, the Floquet engineered Hamiltonian takes the following form:

\mathcal{H}_{\rm{FE}}(t)=\Omega\nu\sin(\nu t)(\sigma^{z}+a^{{\dagger}}a)+\frac{\dot{g}_{z}(t)}{\omega_{r}J_{1}(\Omega)}\cos(\nu t)\sigma^{z}(a^{{\dagger}}+a),

(23)

where
\nu\ll\omega_{r}
is an arbitrary frequency,
\Omega
is a free parameter, and
J_{1}
is the Bessel function of the first kind. By implementing the only Floquet Hamiltonian (not including the original Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
), we show the relative distance between the pointer states and the SNR can be significantly enhanced for different values of
\nu
.

Physical implementation

VI.1
Circuit Hamiltonian

We shall shed light on the experimental implementation for a two-level system coupled to an oscillator via longitudinal interaction. The circuit consist in an LC resonator of capacitance

and inductance

connected to the transmon with the gate capacitance

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{g}}{2}(V_{g}-\dot{\psi}_{J})^{2}+\frac{C_{T}}{2}\dot{\psi}_{J}^{2}+\frac{C_{r}}{2}\dot{\psi}_{r}^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{C_{S}}{2}(\dot{\psi}_{J}-\dot{\psi}_{r})^{2}-\frac{\psi_{r}^{2}}{2L_{r}}+E_{J}(\phi_{x})\cos\bigg(\frac{\psi_{J}}{\varphi_{0}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle E_{JS}(\varphi_{x})\cos\bigg(\frac{\psi_{J}-\psi_{r}}{\varphi_{0}}\bigg),

\displaystyle P_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C_{r}+C_{S})\dot{\psi}_{r}-C_{S}\dot{\psi}_{J}.

VII
Conclusion

In summary, the methods of STA, including inverse engineering and counter-diabaticity, have been worked out for designing the longitudinal qubit-cavity coupling to accelerate the qubit measurement. Remarkably, by engineering the modulations, the pointer state separation is significantly enhanced, accompanied by a large SNR. We also see an exponential enhancement when the cavity in a single-mode is prepared in squeezed state. In addition, genetic algorithm are discussed also for the optimization. In the cQED platform, tunable capacitive interaction is required to implement counter-diabatic driving, which makes the inverse-engineering approach more feasible to speed up the measurement process, with the realistic cQED architecture. We estimate an upper bound for the coupling strength that set the low bound for measuring time. Last but not least, we hope our result can be experimentally verified with circuit design implementing longitudinal coupling of superconducting qubits

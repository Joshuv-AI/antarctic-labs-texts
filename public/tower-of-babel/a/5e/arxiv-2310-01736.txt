Exact results for some extremal problems on expansions I
Xizhi Liu, Jialei Song, Long-Tu Yuan

Abstract
The expansion of a graph F F , denoted by F 3 F^{3} , is the 3 3 -graph obtained from F F by adding a new vertex to each edge such that different edges receive different vertices. We establish a stability version of a theorem by Kostochka–Mubayi–Verstraëte [ 31 ] and demonstrate two applications of it by establishing tight upper bounds for large n : n\colon • The maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex 3 3 -graph that does not contain T 3 T^{3} for certain class 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of trees, thereby (partially) sharpening the asymptotic result of Kostochka–Mubayi–Verstraëte. • The minimum number of colors needed to color the complete n n -vertex 3 3 -graph to ensure the existence of a rainbow copy of F 3 F^{3} when F F is a graph obtained from some tree T ∈ 𝒯 T\in\mathcal{T} by adding a new edge, thereby extending anti-Ramsey results on P 2 ​ t 3 P_{2t}^{3} by Gu–Li–Shi and C 2 ​ t 3 C_{2t}^{3} by Tang–Li–Yan. We introduce a framework that utilizes tools from Extremal Set Theory for solving certain generalized Turán problems. More specifically, we establish a parallel of the stability theorem above in generalized Turán problems. Using this stability theorem, we determine, for large n n , the maximum number of triangles in an n n -vertex graph that does not contain the shadow of C k 3 C_{k}^{3} or T 3 T^{3} for T ∈ 𝒯 T\in\mathcal{T} , thus answering a question of Lv et al . on generalized Turán problems. Keywords: hypergraph Turán problem, anti-Ramsey problem, generalized Turán problem, expansion of trees, linear cycles, triple systems, stability.

1.1
Turán problems for the expansion of trees

Determining

Two well-known conjectures in this regard, the Erdős–Sós Conjecture on trees

]

For

is a path, a cycle, or in a special class of trees, is now well-understood due to series works of Füredi
[
15
]
, Füredi–Jiang
[
16
,
17
]

[

]

Using methods significantly different from theirs, Kostochka–Mubyai–Verstraëte
[

30

extended these results to

Theorem 1.1

(Kostochka–Mubyai–Verstraëte
[
31
]
)
.

Suppose that
T

Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,T^{3})=\left(\frac{\sigma(T)-1}{2}+o(1)\right)n^{2}.

It is worth mentioning that the main term in the theorem above comes from the following construction.
Let

be integers. Define the
3
-graph

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(n,t):=\left\{e\in\binom{[n]}{3}\colon|e\cap[t]|\geq 1\right\}.

It is easy to see that

T^{3}
-free for all trees with

\mathrm{ex}(n,T^{3})\geq|\mathcal{S}(n,t)|

\tau(T)\geq t+1
.
Our main result in this subsection is an exact determination of
\mathrm{ex}(n,F^{3})
when
F
is a strongly edge-critical tree, thus sharpening (partially) Theorem

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
T
is a tree satisfying
\sigma(T)=\tau_{\mathrm{ind}}(T)
and containing a critical edge.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,T^{3})\leq|\mathcal{S}(n,\sigma(T)-1)|\quad\text{for all sufficiently large $n$}.

In particular,
T
is a strongly edge-critical tree.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,T^{3})=|\mathcal{S}(n,\sigma(T)-1)|\quad\text{for all sufficiently large $n$}.

Remark.

•

Note that one cannot hope that Theorem
1.2
holds for all trees, as paths of even length would be counterexamples (see
[
29
]
).

•

For

Füredi–Jiang
[
17
]
determined

for all edge-critical trees

is edge-critical if

and there exists an edge

It would be interesting to see if the conclusion of Theorem

Theorem

We say an

1.2
Anti-Ramsey problems

Let
F
be an
r
-graph.
The

\mathrm{ar}(n,F)
of

is the minimum number

such that any surjective map
\chi\colon K_{n}^{r}\to[m]
contains a rainbow copy of
F

F
in which every edge receives a unique value under

It is easy to observe from the definition that for every
r
-graph

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}\left(n,\{F\setminus e\colon e\in F\}\right)+2\leq\mathrm{ar}(n,F)\leq\mathrm{ex}(n,F)+1.

The study of anti-Ramsey problems was initiated by Erdős–Simonovits–Sós
[
9
]
who proved that

for

and sufficiently large

.
There has been lots of progress on this topic since their work and we refer the reader to a survey
[
14
]
by Fujita–Magnant–Ozeki for more details.
In this subsection we are concerned with
\mathrm{ar}(n,F)
when
F
is the expansion of a tree plus one edge.

We say a graph

As another application of the stability theorem (Theorem

Theorem 1.4

In

Let

be two graphs.
We use

The

When

The generalized Turán problem was first considered by Erdős in
[

In
[
2

Similar to the ordinary Turán problem, determining

\chi(Q)
and
\chi(F)

In this subsection, we make some progress in the case

and

is the
\triangle
-blowup (defined below) of a cycle or a tree.

Given two graphs

and

.

\displaystyle G_{1}\mathbin{\;{\rotatebox{90.0}{{$\small\Bowtie$}}}}G_{2}:=\left\{uv\colon u\in V(G_{1}),\ v\in V(G_{2})\right\}\cup G_{1}\cup G_{2}.

T(n)

n

to denote the
n
-vertex graph obtained from

Given integers

let

\displaystyle S(n,t):=K_{t}\mathbin{\;{\rotatebox{90.0}{{$\small\Bowtie$}}}}T(n-t)\quad\text{and}\quad S^{+}(n,t):=K_{t}\mathbin{\;{\rotatebox{90.0}{{$\small\Bowtie$}}}}T^{+}(n-t).

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{bi}}(n,t)

\displaystyle:=\left\{A\colon\text{$S(n,t)$ induces a copy of $K_{3}$ on $A$}\right\},\quad\mathrm{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\mathrm{bi}}(n,t)

\displaystyle:=\left\{A\colon\text{$S^{+}(n,t)$ induces a copy of $K_{3}$ on $A$}\right\}

Algorithm

Cleaning Algorithm

Input:

Output:

Initialization:

\displaystyle i=0,\quad\mathcal{H}^{\ast}:=\left\{e\in\mathcal{H}\colon\Delta_{2}(e)\leq 3k\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\ast}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}|=\sum_{v\in V}|\widehat{L}(v)|,\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{L}(v)\subseteq\binom{N_{G^{\prime\prime}}(v)}{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in V(\mathcal{H}),

in

For every integer

\displaystyle Z_{i}:=\left\{v\in V\colon|\widehat{L}(v)|\geq\frac{n^{2-2^{-i}}}{(\log n)^{2}}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad U_{i}:=V\setminus Z_{i}.

Additionally, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{0}\coloneqq\left\{e\in\mathcal{G}\colon|e\cap Z_{0}|=1\text{ and }e\setminus Z_{0}\in G^{\prime}\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}_{i}\coloneqq\left\{e\in\mathcal{G}_{i-1}\colon|e\cap Z_{i}|=1\right\}

Observe that for all

\displaystyle V\supseteq Z_{0}\supseteq Z_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{i},\quad U_{0}\subseteq U_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_{i}\subseteq V,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{G}\supseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}\supseteq\mathcal{G}_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathcal{G}_{i}.

Claim 3.2
.

We have
|Z_{i}|\leq 2kn^{2^{-i-1}}\log n
and
|\mathcal{G}_{i}|\geq|\mathcal{G}|-\frac{2(i+1)n^{2}}{(\log n)^{2}}

In particular,

\displaystyle\binom{|Z_{k}|}{t}\leq\sqrt{n}\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathcal{G}_{k}|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon_{1}\right)tn^{2}.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on

For the base case
i=0
,
observe from the second part of (
4
) that
for every

, we have

Therefore, by Claim

\displaystyle 2kn\geq 2|G^{\prime\prime}|\geq\sum_{v\in Z_{0}}d_{G^{\prime\prime}}(v)\geq\sum_{v\in Z_{0}}\sqrt{|\widehat{L}(v)|}\geq|Z_{0}|\frac{n^{1/2}}{\log n},

|Z_{0}|\leq 2kn^{1/2}\log n
.
Consequently, the number of edges in

|Z_{0}|n\leq 2kn^{3/2}\log n

Observe that every triple in
\mathcal{G}
with at least two vertices in

) with (at least) one endpoint in
Z_{0}

\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{0}|\geq|\mathcal{G}|-\sum_{v\in V\setminus Z_{0}}|\widehat{L}(v)|-2ktn^{3/2}\log n

\displaystyle\geq|\mathcal{G}|-n\times\frac{n}{(\log n)^{2}}-2ktn^{3/2}\log n

\displaystyle\geq|\mathcal{G}|-\frac{2n^{2}}{(\log n)^{2}}.

Now suppose that

Repeating the argument above to

\displaystyle 2kn\geq 2|G^{\prime\prime}|\geq\sum_{v\in Z_{i+1}}d_{G^{\prime\prime}}(v)\geq\sum_{v\in Z_{i+1}}\sqrt{|\widehat{L}(v)|}\geq|Z_{i+1}|\frac{n^{1-2^{-i-2}}}{\log n},

.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{i+1}|\geq|\mathcal{G}_{i}|-\sum_{v\in Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i+1}}|\widehat{L}(v)|-t|Z_{i+1}|n

\displaystyle\geq|\mathcal{G}_{i}|-|Z_{i}|\times\frac{n^{2-2^{-i-1}}}{(\log n)^{2}}-2ktn^{1+2^{-i-2}}\log n

\displaystyle\geq|\mathcal{G}_{i}|-\frac{2n^{2}}{(\log n)^{2}}\geq|\mathcal{G}|-\frac{2(i+2)n^{2}}{(\log n)^{2}}.

Here,
t|Z_{i+1}|n
is an upper bound for the number of edges in
\mathcal{G}_{i}
with at least two vertices in
Z_{i+1}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}
denote the outputting

Since

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}|\geq\left(\frac{t}{2}-\varepsilon_{2}\right)n^{2}.

contains exactly one vertex in

.
Similar to the proof of Claim

, the number of pairs in

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime\prime}:=\left\{e\in\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime}\colon\text{the pair }\binom{e}{2}\cap\binom{U_{k}}{2}\text{ has codegree exactly $t$ }\right\}

satisfies

\displaystyle|\mathcal{G}_{k}^{\prime\prime}|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon_{3}\right)tn^{2}.

(5)

For every set

\bullet

\bullet
Recall that one of the key ingredients in proofs of generalized Turán theorems is Kruskal–Katona type theorems (Propositions

\bullet
By utilizing a similar framework as presented in this paper, along with methods and results of Füredi

\bullet
Given an

of

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition

Recall the following statement of Proposition

.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose that
T
is a tree and
(I,R)
is a cross-cut pair of

. Then one of the following holds.

(i)

There exists a vertex

such that all but one vertex in

are leaves in

.

(ii)

There exists an edge

which is a pendant edge in

.

In particular, if

is maximum, then

holds.

Proof.

Define a set system
\mathcal{T}
on

by letting

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}:=R\cup\left\{N_{T}(v)\colon v\in I\right\}.

First we claim that
\mathcal{T}
is linear, i.e.
|E_{1}\cap E_{2}|\leq 1
for all distinct pairs
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
.
Indeed, suppose to the contrary that there exists a distinct pair
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
with
|E_{1}\cap E_{2}|\geq 2
. Then from the definition of

,

and

cannot be both contained in

. So, by symmetry, we may assume that

for some

. If

, then
|E_{2}|=2
, which implies that the induced subgraph of

on

is a copy of

, a contradiction. If
E_{2}=N_{T}(v^{\prime})
for some
v^{\prime}\in I\setminus\{v\}
, then the induced subgraph of

on

, where

are arbitrary two distinct vertices, is a copy of

, a contradiction.
This proves that
\mathcal{T}
is linear.

Next, we claim that
\mathcal{T}
is acyclic.
Suppose to the contrary that there exist distinct vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\subseteq U
and distinct edges

such that

(the indices are taken module

).
Define

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\left\{i\in[\ell]\colon E_{i}\in\left\{N_{T}(v)\colon v\in I\right\}\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2}:=[\ell]\setminus I_{1}.

is the vertex in

.

Let

Appendix G
Proof of Proposition
4.1

Recall the following statement of Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition G.1
.

Suppose that
T
is a tree and
F
is obtained from
T
by adding one edge.
Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,F^{3})\leq 3\left(|T|+1\right)n^{2}
holds for sufficiently large

.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tree and
F
be a graph obtained from
T
by adding one edge.
Let
k:=|F|=|T|+1
.
Let
n
be a sufficiently large integer and
\mathcal{H}
be an
n
-vertex
3
-graph with
3kn^{2}
edges.
Let
V:=V(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be a maximum
3k
-full subgraph.
It follows from Lemma
2.18
that
|\mathcal{H}^{\prime}|\geq|\mathcal{H}|-3k\binom{n}{2}\geq 3kn^{2}/2
.
We will show that
F^{3}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

If
F
is a forest, then it follows from the theorem of Kostochka–Mubayi–Verstraëte
[
31
]
(or Theorem
1.3
) that
F^{3}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
.
So we may assume that
F
is not a forest.
Then
F
contains a unique cycle of length
\ell
for some integer
\ell\leq k
.
Let
p:=k-\ell
.
By sequentially removing pendant edges we obtain a sequence of subgraphs

\displaystyle F=:F_{0}\supseteq F_{1}\cdots\supseteq F_{p}\cong C_{\ell},

where
|F_{i}|=|F|-i
for
i\in[0,p]
and
F_{p}
is the unique cycle in
F
.
In other words, for
i\in[0,p-1]
we choose a pendant edge
e_{i}\in F_{i}
and let
F_{i+1}:=F_{i}-e_{i}
.
We will prove by a backward induction on
i
such that
F_{i}^{3}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

The base case
i=p
follows from the theorem of Kostochka–Mubayi–Verstraëte
[
29
]
on linear cycles.
So we may assume that
i\in[p-1]
.
Suppose that there exists an embedding
\psi\colon F_{i}^{3}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
We want to extend
\psi
to be an embedding of
F_{i-1}^{3}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
To achieve this, let
e_{i-1}:=\{u,v\}
denote the edge in
F_{i-1}\setminus F_{i}
and assume that
v
is a leaf in
F_{i-1}
.
Observe from the definition that each
F_{i}
is connected, so there exists
w\in V(F_{i})
such that

is an edge in
F_{i}
.
This implies that
\psi(uw)\in\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is
3k
-full, there exists a vertex
v^{\prime}\in V\setminus\psi(V(F_{i}^{3}))
such that
\{\psi(u),\psi(w),v^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
In particular,
\{\psi(u),v^{\prime}\}\in\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Using the
3k
-fullness again, there exists a vertex
v^{\prime\prime}\in V\setminus\psi(V(F_{i}^{3}))
such that
\{\psi(u),v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Observe that
\psi(F_{i}^{3})\cup\{\{\psi(u),v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\}\}
is a copy of
F_{i-1}^{3}
in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
This completes the proof for the inductive step, and hence, completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

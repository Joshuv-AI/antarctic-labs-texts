Solving the Transmission Problem for Open Wave-Guides, II Outgoing Estimates
Charles L. Epstein

Abstract
The paper continues the analysis, started in [ 1 ] (Part I), of the model open wave-guide problem defined by 2 semi-infinite, rectangular wave-guides meeting along a common perpendicular line. In Part I we reduce the solution of the physical problem to a transmission problem rephrased as a system of integral equations on the common perpendicular line. In this part we show that solutions of the integral equations introduced in Part I have asymptotic expansions, if the data allows it. Using these expansions we show that the solutions to the PDE found in each half space satisfy appropriate outgoing radiation conditions. In Part III we show that these conditions imply uniqueness of the solution to the PDE as well as uniqueness for our system of integral equations.

\left(\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Id}&D\\

The wave-guide parts are given as finite sums

(8)

Assuming that

u^{l,r}_{c}(r\eta)\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}r}}{\sqrt{r}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{l,r}(\eta)}{r^{j}},

(9)

190

The difficulty in proving uniform expansions comes from the fact that the
location of the stationary phase varies with
\eta,
and, in natural
coordinates, tends to the intersection of two orthogonal segments on the contour
of integration. The proof that these expansions extend uniformly uses
complex contour deformations. In some cases there is a fixed smooth curve so
that all the stationary phase points remain in the interior of the curve. In
other cases there is a smooth family of contours so that the stationary phase
lies at the orthogonal intersection of two curve segments. The uniformity of the
expansions then follows from Lemma
4
:

Lemma
.

Let
f(x,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([0,1)\times[0,\delta]),
for a

\delta>0,

F(\theta)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{irx^{2}}f(x,\theta)dx\sim\frac{e^{ir}}{\sqrt{r}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}(\theta)}{r^{\frac{j}{2}}},

(10)

where
a_{j}(\theta)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,\delta]).

These expansions suffice to show that the solutions
u^{l,r}_{c}+u^{l,r}_{g}

satisfy the natural outgoing radiation conditions for this problem, which are

see
[

4

6

Some notation

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},
the Banach space

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R})
consists of continuous functions

such
that

|f|_{\alpha}=\sup\{(1+|x|)^{\alpha}|f(x)|:\>x\in\mathbb{R}\}<\infty.

(11)

We say that a function

has an asymptotic expansion as

r\to\infty,
and write

f(r\eta)\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}r}}{r^{\alpha}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}(\eta)}{r^{j}},

(12)

provided that, for any

\left|f(r\eta)-\frac{e^{ik_{1}r}}{r^{\alpha}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{a_{j}(\eta)}{r^{j}}\right|=o(r^{-(N+\alpha)}),

(13)

and this also holds for
\partial_{r}
-derivatives of
f(r\eta).

Smoothness in
\eta
means that we can differentiate the expansion
w.r.t.
\eta,
and the error terms remain the same order.

2
Outgoing Estimates for
\sigma,
and
\tau

The solution that we obtain in Section 5 of
[
1
]
to the
transmission problem is essentially the limiting absorption solution. So,
de facto
, our solution to the transmission problem should be thought of as
“outgoing,” though whether such a solution exists depends on the data

(g,h).
From the transmission boundary condition, (
4
), it follows that

(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})[u^{l}(0,x_{2})-u^{r}(0,x_{2})]=(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})g(x_{2})\text{
as }\pm x_{2}\to\infty.

(14)

In Part III we show that the outgoing condition requires, among other things
that,
(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})[u^{l}(0,x_{2})-u^{r}(0,x_{2})]=o(|x_{2}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}),
and
therefore for an outgoing solution to exist it is necessary for
(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})g(x_{2})=o(|x_{2}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

As we shall see, if the data comes from wave-guide modes, then the
resultant solutions,
u^{l,r}(r\eta),
do in fact decay as
r\to\infty
and
satisfy the appropriate radiation conditions. In Part III of this series we
state these conditions precisely and show that these conditions
imply uniqueness. As a consequence of the non-compactness of the domain over
which we integrate the layer potentials, these estimates are rather delicate
to prove.

The estimates for
(\sigma,\tau)
are obtained by a bootstrap argument. We
use estimates for the kernel functions defining the operators
C,D

in (
5
) to first show that, if
(g,h)
decrease rapidly enough,
then the solution
(\sigma,\tau)\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
to (
5
), for an
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2},
actually
satisfies the essentially optimal decay estimates:

|\sigma(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\text{ and }|\tau(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(15)

We then show that these functions satisfy symbolic estimates, and finally that
they have asymptotic expansions as
|x_{2}|\to\infty.

The kernel functions for the operators
C,D
are constructed from functions

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2}),

j=0,1,2.
For the convenience of the reader we recall
the estimates proved in
[

]

then

1.

Asymptotically, for

\mathfrak{w}^{[j]}(x_{2},y_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)}}{(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}}\left[M^{\pm,\pm}_{j0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{\pm,\pm}_{jl}}{(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)^{l}}\right],

3.

The kernel

2.1
The basic estimate

We assume that
\operatorname{supp}q_{l,r}\subset[-d,d];
using the relations

\sigma(x_{2})=g(x_{2})-D\tau(x_{2}),\quad\tau(x_{2})=h(x_{2})-C\sigma(x_{2}),

(21)

we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that

(\sigma,\tau),
satisfy (
21
) and the basic estimates

|\tau(x_{2})|\leq\frac{|\tau|_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad|\sigma(x_{2})|\leq\frac{|\sigma|_{\alpha}}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\alpha}},

(22)

for an
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2},
with data satisfying the estimates

\begin{split}|g(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\quad|(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})g(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\text{ as }\pm x_{2}\to\infty,\\
|h(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{3}{2}}},&\quad|(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})h(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{2}}\text{ as }\pm x_{2}\to\infty.\end{split}

(23)

Then
(\sigma,\tau),
satisfy the estimates

|\tau(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M^{\prime}}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\quad|\sigma(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M^{\prime}}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(24)

for a constant
M^{\prime}
depending on
|\sigma|_{\alpha},|\tau|_{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}
and
M.

Remark 1
.

There is a similar result with the somewhat more natural assumption that

|h(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{1+|x_{2}|},

(25)

concluding that
\tau(x_{2})=O(|x_{2}|^{-1}),
but to avoid even more special
cases we do not consider it here.

Proof.

Note that outside a neighborhood of
B_{d}
the kernels
k_{C}(x_{2},y_{2}),k_{D}(x_{2},y_{2})
are infinitely differentiable. Using the estimate
in (
18
) we see that, for
\pm x_{2}\to\infty,
we have

\begin{split}|(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})D\tau(x_{2})|&\leq\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{Mdy_{2}}{|y_{2}|^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{|x_{2}|^{\alpha+1}}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{Mdt}{|t|^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}(1+|t|)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right|\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{|x_{2}|^{\alpha+1}}.\end{split}

(26)

Similarly we can show that, as
\pm x_{2}\to\infty,

\begin{split}|(\partial_{x_{2}}\mp ik_{1})C\sigma(x_{2})|&\leq\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{Mdy_{2}}{|y_{2}|^{\alpha}(|x_{2}|+|y_{2}|)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{|x_{2}|^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{Mdt}{|t|^{\alpha}(1+|t|)^{\frac{5}{2}}}\right|\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{|x_{2}|^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}}.\end{split}

(27)

From these estimates, (
21
), and (
23
), which show that

\partial_{x_{2}}\sigma=\partial_{x_{2}}g-\partial_{x_{2}}D\tau,\quad\partial_{x_{2}}\tau=\partial_{x_{2}}h-\partial_{x_{2}}C\sigma,

(28)

we see that, as

\pm x_{2}\to\infty,
we have the estimates

\begin{split}&|\partial_{x_{2}}\sigma(x_{2})\mp ik_{1}\sigma(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\alpha+1}}\\
&|\partial_{x_{2}}\tau(x_{2})\mp ik_{1}\tau(x_{2})|\leq\frac{M}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{\alpha+\frac{3}{2}}}.\end{split}

(29)

Let
\psi_{\pm}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be monotone and non-negative with

\psi_{\pm}(x)=\begin{cases}&0\text{ for }\pm x<d,\\
&1\text{ for }\pm x>d+\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

(30)

Using integration parts and (
19

\begin{split}\Bigg|\int_{d}^{\infty}k_{D}(x_{2},y_{2})[\partial_{y_{2}}(\psi_{+}\tau)(y_{2})+&ik_{1}\psi_{+}\tau(y_{2})]dy_{2}\Bigg|\\

(31)

A similar argument with
\psi_{-}(x_{2})

The estimates in (

\partial_{x_{2}}(\psi_{\pm}\tau)(x_{2})=(\partial_{x_{2}}\psi_{\pm}(x_{2}))\tau(x_{2})+\psi_{\pm}(x_{2})\partial_{x_{2}}\tau(x_{2})=\\

Using this in (

An elementary estimate shows that the integral on the r.h.s. is

There is a similar estimate arising

As

(35)

from (
34
) and the analogue for

we conclude that

(36)

and therefore (

) imply that

(37)

as well.

2.2
The asymptotic expansion

From the form of the kernels it is reasonable to expect
\sigma
and
\tau
to have asymptotic
expansions of the form

\sigma(x_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{|x_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{N}\frac{a^{\pm}_{l}}{|x_{2}|^{l}}\right]\text{ and }\tau(x_{2})\sim\frac{e^{ik_{1}|x_{2}|}}{|x_{2}|^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{N}\frac{b^{\pm}_{l}}{|x_{2}|^{l}}\right],

(41)

for a choice of
N
largely determined by the data
(g,h).

Choose a non-negative
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}((-d-2\epsilon,d+2\epsilon))

\varphi(x_{2})=1\text{ for }|x_{2}|\leq d+\epsilon;

(42)

define

\begin{split}C_{0}\sigma(x_{2})&=\int_{-d-\epsilon}^{d+\epsilon}k_{C}(x_{2},y_{2})\varphi(y_{2})\sigma(y_{2})dy_{2},\quad C_{1}\sigma=(C-C_{0})\sigma,\\
D_{0}\tau(x_{2})&=\int_{-d-\epsilon}^{d+\epsilon}k_{D}(x_{2},y_{2})\varphi(y_{2})\sigma(y_{2})dy_{2},\quad D_{1}\tau=(D-D_{0})\tau.\end{split}

From (

17
), (

\sigma

\tau

first remove the oscillations from these functions by defining

\widetilde{\sigma}(x_{2})=e^{-ik_{1}|x_{2}|}\sigma(x_{2}),\quad\widetilde{\tau}(x_{2})=e^{-ik_{1}|x_{2}|}\tau(x_{2}).

(44)

We then show that these functions satisfy symbolic estimates

|\partial_{x_{2}}^{l}\widetilde{\sigma}(x_{2})|\leq\frac{C_{l}}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{l+\frac{1}{2}}}\text{ and }|\partial_{x_{2}}^{l}\widetilde{\tau}(x_{2})|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}_{l}}{(1+|x_{2}|)^{l+\frac{3}{2}}}.

Proof.

With

\int_{|y_{2}|>d}e^{-ik_{1}|x_{2}|}k_{D}(x_{2},y_{2})\tau(y_{2})(1-\varphi(y_{2}))dy_{2}.

The estimates for the compactly supported part follow immediately from (

The contour deformations from the previous section can be adapted to prove

the

or
-k_{1}\eta_{1},

It is complicated to prove uniform estimates because the

intersection, then it would be easy to prove uniform estimates, and this can be

We begin with a lemma

Lemma 4
.

Let
f(x,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([0,1)\times[0,\delta]),
for a

\delta>0,
the integral

F(\theta)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{irx^{2}}f(x,\theta)dx\sim\frac{e^{ir}}{\sqrt{r}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{j}(\theta)}{r^{\frac{j}{2}}},

(142)

where
a_{j}(\theta)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,\delta]).

Proof.

For each
N>0
we have the Taylor expansion, in
x,
for
f

f(x,\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{2N}\frac{\partial_{x}^{j}f(0,\theta))}{j!}x^{j}+x^{2N+1}R_{N}(x,\theta),

(143)

where
R_{N}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,1)\times[0,\delta]).
Let

\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}([0,1))
equal
1
on
\operatorname{supp}f(\cdot,\theta),
for

\theta\in[0,\delta].
We can rewrite
F(\theta)
as

F(\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{2N}\frac{\partial_{x}^{j}f(0,\theta))}{j!}\int_{0}^{1}e^{irx^{2}}x^{j}\psi(x)dx+\int_{0}^{1}e^{irx^{2}}x^{2N+1}R_{N}(x,\theta)\psi(x)dx.

(144)

The smoothness of the expansion in
\theta\in[0,\delta]
is
immediate from this formula. Integrating by parts we easily
show that, for any
k\geq 0,

\left|\partial_{\theta}^{k}\left[\int_{0}^{1}e^{irx^{2}}x^{2N+1}R_{N}(x,\theta)\psi(x)dx\right]\right|=O(r^{-(N+2)}),

(145)

uniformly for
\theta\in[0,\delta].
It is a classical result,
proved by integration by parts that, for
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},

\int_{0}^{1}e^{irx^{2}}x^{j}\psi(x)dx=\frac{c_{j}}{r^{\frac{2j+1}{2}}}+O(r^{-N}),\text{ for any }N.

(146)

The lemma follows easily from these observations.

4
Estimates for
\mathcal{W}^{l,r}\tau-\mathcal{W}^{l,r\,^{\prime}}\sigma

We now prove estimates for the perturbation terms

This requires consideration of many special

To prove that the solutions satisfy the

The operator

The guided mode contribution is a finite

We focus on the contributions of the continuous spectrum,

\begin{split}&u_{c0}(x)\overset{d}{=}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}w^{c}_{0+}(x;0,y_{2})\tau(y_{2})dy_{2}\\
&u_{c1}(x)\overset{d}{=}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\partial_{y_{1}}w^{c}_{0+}(x;0,y_{2})\sigma(y_{2})dy_{2}.\end{split}

(187)

The kernel

proved above in Theorem

\partial_{x_{1}}u^{\operatorname{in}}_{l}(0,x_{2})-\partial_{x_{1}}u^{\operatorname{in}}_{r}(0,x_{2}).

We prove the following asymptotic expansions for the perturbation terms.

Theorem 3

Suppose that

(219)

We now consider what happens if

In this case the integral takes a

. As

\pm k_{1},
with the remainder of the integral rapidly decreasing as

x_{1}\to\infty.
The contribution near to

is given by the contour integral

\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\Lambda_{\mu}}\frac{e^{i\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-t^{2}}x_{1}+2idt}\mathfrak{B}(t,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-t^{2}};x_{2})\hat{\tau}_{\pm}(t)\psi(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-t^{2}})}{\mathfrak{W}(\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-t^{2}},t)\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-t^{2}}}dt.

(222)

with the coefficients depending smoothly on

Similar arguments apply to show that

The coefficients are smooth on

We are left to consider

Remark 11
.

In the remainder of the section we consider a variety of functions that satisfy
symbolic estimates. We say that a smooth function,
s_{l}(\xi)
defined for

|\xi|
sufficiently large, is a “symbol of order
l
” if there
are constants so that

\limsup_{|\xi|\to\infty}(1+|\xi|)^{m-l}|\partial_{\xi}^{m}s_{l}(\xi)|\leq C_{m}<\infty.

(239)

In most cases these functions will depend smoothly on additional parameters,
and the constants,
\{C_{m}\},
in these estimates depend uniformly on these
parameters. As we never differentiate with respect to them, we often suppress
the dependence on these parameters.

The function
\mathfrak{D}(\xi,\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}^{2}};x_{2},y_{2})
can be written as a sum of
terms of the form

e^{-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}h(x_{2},y_{2})}s_{l}(x_{2},y_{2};\xi),

(240)

where
i=1
or
2
and is
h(x_{2},y_{2})
is non-negative, and strictly positive if

x_{2}\neq y_{2}.
The functions
s_{l}(x_{2},y_{2};\xi)
are symbols of order

l\in\{0,-1,-2,-3,-4\}.
Note, for example that

e^{(\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{1}})d}
equals
1
plus a symbol of order

-1.
The remainder of the integrand

\frac{\xi^{j}(1-\psi(\xi))}{\mathfrak{W}(\xi,\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}})\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}-\xi^{2}}},

is a symbol of order
j-2.

Hence we need to show that integrals of the form

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ix_{1}\xi}e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}s_{l}(\xi)(1-\psi(\xi))d\xi,

(241)

where
s_{l}
is a symbol of order
l,
are uniformly rapidly decreasing in
x_{1}

as
\lambda\to 0^{+}.
We show this for any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
by
integrating by parts in the most obvious way: for
\lambda>0
and
m\in\mathbb{N},
we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ix_{1}\xi}e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}s_{l}(\xi)(1-\psi(\xi))d\xi=\\
\frac{1}{(-ix_{1})^{m}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{ix_{1}\xi}\partial_{\xi}^{m}\left[e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}s_{l}(\xi)(1-\psi(\xi))\right]d\xi

(242)

If a derivative falls on
1-\psi(\xi),
then the term is obviously rapidly
decreasing, hence we need to consider:

\partial_{\xi}^{m}\left[e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}s_{l}(\xi)\right]=\sum_{p=0}^{m}\left(\begin{matrix}m\\
p\end{matrix}\right)\partial_{\xi}^{m-p}s_{l}(\xi)\cdot\partial_{\xi}^{p}e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}.

(243)

We let
s_{l+p-m}=\partial_{\xi}^{m-p}s_{l}(\xi),
denote a symbol of order
l+p-m.
We use
the following lemma, which is proved using a simple induction argument and the
fact that

\partial_{\xi}\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}=\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}

(244)

is a symbol of order
0
in
\{\xi:\>|\xi|>k_{i}+\epsilon\},
for any
\epsilon>0.

Lemma 5
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\partial_{\xi}^{m}e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}=e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}\times\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{j}s_{j-m}(\xi),

(245)

where
s_{j-m}
is a symbol of order
j-m
in

\{\xi:\>|\xi|>k_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\}.

Applying the lemma and (
243
) we see that

\partial_{\xi}^{m}\left[e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}s_{l}(\xi)\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lambda^{j}s_{l-m+j}(\xi)e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}.

(246)

To complete this analysis we need to estimate

I_{j}(\lambda)=\left|\int\limits_{k_{i}+\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}e^{i\xi x_{1}}\lambda^{j}s_{l-m+j}(\xi)e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}d\xi\right|\text{ for }j\in\{1,\dots,m\},

(247)

for
m>l+1.
If
j=0,
then it is clear that this integral is uniformly bounded
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}.

Using the symbolic estimate

|s_{l-m+j}(\xi)|\leq C(\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2})^{\frac{l-m+j}{2}},\text{ for
}|\xi|>k_{i}+\epsilon,

we see that

I_{j}(\lambda)\leq C\int\limits_{k_{i}+\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\lambda^{j}(\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2})^{\frac{l-m+j}{2}}e^{-\lambda\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}}d\xi\leq C^{\prime}\int\limits_{\sqrt{k_{i}+\frac{1}{4}}}^{\infty}\lambda^{j}w^{l-m+j}e^{-\lambda w}dw,

(248)

where we have let
w=\sqrt{\xi^{2}-k_{i}^{2}}.
If
l-m+j<-1,
then it is clear that
the limit of
I_{j}(\lambda)
is
0
as
\lambda\to 0^{+}.
If
l-m+j=-1,
then

j=m-l-1>0,
and once again it is clear that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}I_{j}(\lambda)=0.
Finally, if
l-m+j\geq 0,
then we let
\lambda w=x
to
obtain

\lambda^{m-l-1}\int\limits_{\lambda k_{i}}^{\infty}x^{l-m+j}e^{-x}dx,

(249)

which again tends to zero as
\lambda\to 0^{+}.
Combining these results shows
that, for all
N>0,
we have the estimate

w^{[j]}_{01+}(x_{1},x_{2};y_{2})=O(x_{1}^{-N}),

(250)

uniformly for bounded
x_{2},y_{2}.

Proposition 2
.

If
x_{2}
lies in a bounded interval, then for bounded
y_{2},
and any
N>0,
we have the
asymptotic expansions, for
j=0,1,2,

w^{[j]}_{0+}(x_{1},x_{2};y_{2})=\frac{e^{ik_{1}x_{1}}}{\sqrt{x_{1}}}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{f_{jk}(x_{2},y_{2})}{x_{1}^{k}}+O(x_{1}^{-(N+1)}),

(251)

where
\{f_{jk}(x_{2},y_{2})\}
are continuous bounded functions of
x_{2},y_{2}.

As a corollary of the proposition we have the asymptotic expansions for

u^{0}_{cj}(x_{1},x_{2}),
with

bounded:

u^{0}_{cj}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{e^{ik_{1}x_{1}}}{\sqrt{x_{1}}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{b_{jk}(x_{2})}{x_{1}^{k}}+O(x_{1}^{-(N+1)})\right].

(252)

Taken together, (
213
), (

), (

),

5
Concluding Remarks

In this paper we have derived refined estimates for the solution, obtained in
Part I, to the transmission problem specified by two open semi-infinite
wave-guides meeting along a common perpendicular line. These estimates show
that the solution satisfies the usual Sommerfeld radiation condition away from
the channels. Within the channels the solution splits cleanly into a
“radiation” part and a wave-guide part. The radiation part satisfies a
standard Sommerfeld radiation condition. The wave-guide parts are sum of terms
of the form
\{e^{-i\xi^{l}_{m}x_{1}}v(x_{2})\}
with
\xi^{l}_{m}>0
in the left half
plane, and
\{e^{i\xi^{r}_{m}x_{1}}v(x_{2})\}
with
\xi^{r}_{m}>0
in the right half
plane. These contributions are therefore also outgoing, in an obvious sense,
but also in the sense first introduced by Isozaki in
[
3
]
.

This clean splitting into a radiation part and a wave-guide cannot be expected
to hold in much generality. Nonetheless in Part III we introduce the formalism
used in
[
6
]
, which gives radiation conditions for very general
open wave guide problems in any dimension. This analysis also shows the
existence of a “Limiting Absorption Solution,” which is shown to
satisfy these radiation conditions. Hence we conclude that the solutions we
have constructed agree with the Limiting Absorption Solution.

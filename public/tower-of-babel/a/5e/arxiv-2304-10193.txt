Spatially homogeneous solutions of vacuum Einstein equations in general dimensions
Yuichiro Sato and Takanao Tsuyuki

Abstract
. We study time-dependent compactification of extra dimensions. We assume that the spacetime is spatially homogeneous, and solve the vacuum Einstein equations without cosmological constant in more than three dimensions. We consider globally hyperbolic spacetimes in which almost abelian Lie groups act on the spaces isometrically and simply transitively. We give left-invariant metrics on the spaces and solve Ricci-flat conditions of the spacetimes. In the four-dimensional case, our solutions correspond to the Bianchi type II solution. By our results and previous studies, all spatially homogeneous solutions whose spaces have zero-dimensional moduli spaces of left-invariant metrics are found. For the simplest solution, we show that each of the spatial dimensions cannot expand or contract simultaneously in the late-time limit.

1.
Introduction

Why the number of the observed spatial dimensions is three?
In standard physics, such as the standard model of particle physics and general relativity, there is no reason that the number has to be three.
Indeed, the extra dimensions have been considered in various theories such as Kaluza-Klein
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, supergravity
[
5
]
, superstring
[
6
]
, and M-theories
[
7
]
(for recent studies on Einstein equations in 10-dimensional supergravity, see
[
8
,
9
]
).
The superstring theories and the M-theory are particularly interesting, since they can predict the number of spatial dimensions as nine and ten, respectively.
If the extra dimensions exist but are undetectably small, why they are much smaller than the others?

We expect that it is the result of the time evolution of the universe.
The evolution can be described by the Einstein equations.
They are difficult to be solved in general, so some assumptions are necessary.
In the previous studies of time-depending extra dimensions, spacetimes have been often assumed to be the products of two spaces of constant curvature
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
There are only two scale factors in such cases.
The advantage of this assumption is that the Einstein equations become simple.
The disadvantage is that the setup seems special and artificial.
We do not make such an assumption in this paper; the scale factors of each direction can evolve differently.
Instead, we assume that the spacetime is spatially homogeneous.
This assumption reduces Einstein equations into ordinary differential equations (ODEs).
The observed universe is homogeneous at a sufficiently large scale, so we expect that the assumption is natural as a first approximation.

Spatially homogeneous but anisotropic spacetimes in four dimensions are known as the Bianchi spacetimes. A Bianchi spacetime is a four-dimensional Lorentzian
manifold that has a spacelike hypersurface on which a Lie group simply transitively acts.
In particular, it is expressed as a product
I\times G
of an open interval
I
, which describes the time, and a connected Lie group
G
, which describes the space.
Three-dimensional Lie groups are classified into types I to IX.
Types I to VII are solvable (see Table.
1
), while types VIII and IX are semisimple.
In addition, in the three-dimensional case, a Lie group is almost abelian if and only if it is solvable
[
14
]
.

In this study, we consider vacuum Einstein equations without the cosmological constant.
In the four-dimensional case, general vacuum solutions have been found only for the types I
[
15
]
, II
[
16
]
and V
[
17
]
(see also
[
18
]
).
These types correspond to
G=\mathbb{R}^{3}
(abelian group),
H_{3}
(Heisenberg group) and
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{H}^{3}
(hyperbolic space), respectively.
The higher dimensional generalization of type I (
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and V (
G=\mathbb{R}\mathrm{H}^{n}
) have been done in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
.
Ref.
[
20
]
also studies other higher-dimensional vacuum solutions including Bianchi type III and VI, but metrics are assumed to be diagonal.
Higher dimensional generalizations of the Bianchi type IX were also studied in
[
21
]
.
The main purpose of this paper is to derive the vacuum solution for the higher dimensional extension of Bianchi type II.
However, the way of the extension is not unique, so we consider the moduli space
\mathfrak{PM}
of left-invariant metrics.

It is known that

\mathbb{R}^{3}

Accidentally or not, these groups correspond to those of the Bianchi types that vacuum solutions are known.
In the higher

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Spatially homogeneous spacetimes

Let
(M,g_{M})
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold, where
g_{M}
(or
ds^{2}
) denotes a Lorentzian metric on
M
.
We call
(M,g_{M})

time-orientable
if there exists a global vector field
V
on
M
such that

g_{M}(V,V)<0.

In this paper, when
(M,g_{M})
is connected and time-orientable, we call it an
(n+1)
-dimensional
spacetime
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be a spacetime,
\Lambda
a constant and
T
a
(0,2)
-type divergence-free symmetric tensor field on
M
.
Then the following equation

(2.1)

\textrm{Ric}-\frac{\textrm{Scal}}{2}g_{M}+\Lambda g_{M}=T

is called the
Einstein equation
.
Moreover,
\Lambda
and
T
are called the
cosmological constant
and the
energy-momentum tensor
, respectively.
In particular, when
T=0,\ \Lambda=0
, the equation (
2.1
) is called the
vacuum
Einstein equation, then
(M,g_{M})
is called a
vacuum solution
.
The spacetime
(M,g_{M})
is a vacuum solution if and only if it is Ricci-flat.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval.
A spacetime
(M,g_{M})
is
globally hyperbolic
if there exist an
n
-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
and a positive function
N
on
M
such that
(M,g_{M})
is isometric to

(2.2)

\left(I\times\Sigma,-N^{2}dt^{2}+h_{t}\right),

where
t
denotes the coordinate of
I
, and
h_{t}
denotes a Riemannian metric on
\Sigma
for all
t\in I
.
Moreover, we call
\Sigma_{t}:=\{t\}\times\Sigma
a
Cauchy hypersurface
for each
t\in I
.

A globally hyperbolic spacetime (
2.2
)
is
spatially homogeneous
if for each
t_{0}\in I
and any two points
x,y\in\Sigma_{t_{0}}
there exists an isometry
f
of
(M,g_{M})

such that
f(t,x)=(t,y)
for all
t\in I
.
Also, a globally hyperbolic spacetime (
2.2
) is
spatially isotropic

if for each
(t_{0},x)\in I\times\Sigma

and any two spacelike vectors
v,w\in T_{x}\Sigma_{t_{0}}

satisfying
g_{M}(v,v)=g_{M}(w,w)

there exists an isometry
f
of
(M,g_{M})
such that

f(t,x)=(t,x)
for all
t\in I
and
f_{\ast}(v)=w
.
By definition, when a globally hyperbolic spacetime is spatially homogeneous, or spatially isotropic, then the function
N
is a positive function on
I
, which is called the
lapse function
of the spacetime.

Unless otherwise noted,
we deal with the case that the Cauchy hypersurface is a connected Lie group
G
,
that is,

(M,g_{M})=\left(I\times G,-N^{2}dt^{2}+g_{G}(t)\right),

where
N
is a positive function on
I
and
g_{G}(t)
is left-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
for all
t\in I
.
By definition, the globally hyperbolic spacetime is
spatially homogeneous but not necessarily spatially isotropic.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra of
G
,

\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
a set of generators of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then we define the
structure constants

C_{ij}^{k}
of
\mathfrak{g}
as

[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{k}X_{k}.

In addition, we define the left-invariant
1
-forms
\{\omega^{1},\ldots,\omega^{n}\}
on
G
as
\omega^{i}(X_{j})=\delta^{i}_{j}
.
Then a left-invariant Riemannian metric
g_{G}
on
G
can be expressed as

g_{G}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}s_{ij}\,\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}

by using a positive-definite symmetric matrix
\left[s_{ij}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
.
Therefore, considering a one-parameter family of metrics
g_{G}(t)
on the spatially homogeneous spacetime is equivalent to choosing a smooth curve

(2.3)

C:I\ni t\mapsto C(t)=\left[s_{ij}(t)\right]\in\textrm{Sym}_{n}^{+}\mathbb{R},

where
\textrm{Sym}_{n}^{+}\mathbb{R}
denotes the set of
n\times n
positive-definite symmetric matrices.

In Section
4
, we show that the metrics for the Lie groups considered in this paper can be diagonalized.
Let
N,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
be positive functions on an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
Consider a spatially homogeneous spacetime with the diagonal Lorentzian metric

(M,g_{M})=\left(I\times G,-N^{2}dt^{2}+a_{1}^{2}(\omega^{1})^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}^{2}(\omega^{n})^{2}\right),

where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are called
scale factors
.
When we give global vector fields on
M
as

X_{0}=\frac{d}{dt},\ X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n},

and set

(2.4)

e_{0}=\frac{1}{N}X_{0},\ e_{1}=\frac{1}{a_{1}}X_{1},\ e_{2}=\frac{1}{a_{2}}X_{2},\ldots,e_{n}=\frac{1}{a_{n}}X_{n},

then
\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
are global orthonormal frame fields of
(M,g_{M})
, that is, it holds that

g_{M}(e_{0},e_{0})=-1,\quad g_{M}(e_{i},e_{i})=1\quad(1\leq i\leq n),\quad\textrm{otherwise}=0.

Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g_{M})
.
By direct computation using the Koszul formula

\displaystyle 2g_{M}(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=X(g_{M}(Y,Z))+Y(g_{M}(Z,X))-Z(g_{M}(X,Y))

\displaystyle\qquad-g_{M}(X,[Y,Z])+g_{M}(Y,[Z,X])+g_{M}(Z,[X,Y]),

we have the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{X_{0}}X_{0}

\displaystyle=X_{0}(\log{N})X_{0},\quad\nabla_{X_{0}}X_{i}=\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{0}=X_{0}(\log{a_{i}})X_{i},

\displaystyle\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{X_{0}(a_{i}^{2})}{N^{2}}\delta_{ij}X_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{C_{kj}^{i}a_{i}^{2}+C_{ki}^{j}a_{j}^{2}+C_{ij}^{k}a_{k}^{2}}{a_{k}^{2}}X_{k}.

Here we define the
(0,4)
-type Riemannian curvature tensor
R
as

R(X,Y,Z,W)=g_{M}(R(X,Y)Z,W)=g_{M}(([\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]-\nabla_{[X,Y]})Z,W).

Then we obtain the following Lemma
2.2
through calculations using Proposition
2.1
and the symmetry of the structure constants

C_{ij}^{k}=-C_{ji}^{k},\quad\sum_{m=1}^{n}(C_{mi}^{l}C_{jk}^{m}+C_{mj}^{l}C_{ki}^{m}+C_{mk}^{l}C_{ij}^{m})=0.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq i,j,k\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle R(X_{0},X_{i},X_{j},X_{0})=

\displaystyle\left[X_{0}(\log{N})X_{0}(\log{a_{i}})-X_{0}^{2}(\log{a_{i}})-\{X_{0}(\log{a_{i}})\}^{2}\right]a_{i}^{2}\delta_{ij},

\displaystyle R(X_{k},X_{i},X_{0},X_{k})=

\displaystyle\left[X_{0}(\log{a_{k}})-X_{0}(\log{a_{i}})\right]C_{ik}^{k}a_{k}^{2},

\displaystyle R(X_{k},X_{i},X_{j},X_{k})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\left[X_{0}(a_{i}^{2})X_{0}(\log{a_{k}})a_{k}^{2}\delta_{ij}-X_{0}(a_{k}^{2})X_{0}(\log{a_{i}})a_{i}^{2}\delta_{ik}\delta_{kj}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left[\frac{2C_{kl}^{k}a_{k}^{2}(C_{lj}^{i}a_{i}^{2}+C_{li}^{j}a_{j}^{2})}{a_{l}^{2}}+\frac{(C_{kl}^{i}a_{i}^{2}+C_{il}^{k}a_{k}^{2})(C_{kl}^{j}a_{j}^{2}+C_{jl}^{k}a_{k}^{2})}{a_{l}^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+\,C_{kl}^{i}C_{jk}^{l}a_{i}^{2}+C_{kl}^{j}C_{ik}^{l}a_{j}^{2}+(C_{il}^{k}C_{kj}^{l}+C_{jl}^{k}C_{ki}^{l})a_{k}^{2}-3C_{ki}^{l}C_{kj}^{l}a_{l}^{2}\right],

where for a function
f
on
I

X_{0}^{2}f:=X_{0}(X_{0}f)=\frac{d^{2}\!f}{dt^{2}}.

Nextly we define the Ricci tensor
\mathrm{Ric}
with respect to
R

of the spatially homogeneous spacetime
(M,g_{M})
as

\textrm{Ric}(X,Y)=-R(e_{0},X,Y,e_{0})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}R(e_{i},X,Y,e_{i}),

where
\{e_{0},e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
denotes an orthonormal frame field and
X
and
Y
are vector fields on
M
.
Then we can determine all components of the Ricci tensor by using the orthonormal frame fields (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(X_{0},X_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\dot{N}}{N}\frac{\dot{a}_{k}}{a_{k}}-\frac{\ddot{a}_{k}}{a_{k}}\right),\quad\mathrm{Ric}(X_{0},X_{i})=\frac{d}{dt}\log{\left[\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{a_{k}}{a_{i}}\right)^{C_{ik}^{k}}\right]},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{N^{2}}\left[\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{\dot{a}_{k}}{a_{k}}-\frac{\dot{N}}{N}\right)\frac{\dot{a}_{i}}{a_{i}}+\frac{\ddot{a}_{i}}{a_{i}}\right]\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\left[2C_{il}^{k}C_{kj}^{l}+2\frac{C_{lk}^{k}(C_{jl}^{i}a_{i}^{2}+C_{il}^{j}a_{j}^{2})}{a_{l}^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{(C_{kl}^{i}a_{i}^{2}+C_{il}^{k}a_{k}^{2})(C_{kl}^{j}a_{j}^{2}+C_{jl}^{k}a_{k}^{2})}{a_{k}^{2}a_{l}^{2}}+\frac{C_{kl}^{i}C_{jk}^{l}a_{i}^{2}+C_{kl}^{j}C_{ik}^{l}a_{j}^{2}-3C_{ki}^{l}C_{kj}^{l}a_{l}^{2}}{a_{k}^{2}}\right],

where for a function
f
on
I

\dot{f}:=X_{0}f=\frac{df}{dt},\quad\ddot{f}:=X_{0}^{2}f=\frac{d^{2}\!f}{dt^{2}}.

By the assumption of spatial homogeneity, the Einstein equations become ODEs.
However, these equations are still difficult to be solved. Even in the four-dimensional case, the general solutions are found only for three cases (see Table

).
All of these cases correspond to almost abelian Lie groups, that we describe below.

2.2.
Almost abelian Lie groups

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an

-dimensional real Lie algebra.

-dimensional abelian ideal

]
.
A connected

-dimensional Lie group

of

By definition, when

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\log{\left[\,\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\frac{a_{k}}{a_{n}}\right)^{A_{kk}}\right]},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(X_{n},X_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n}^{2}}{N^{2}}\left[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\dot{a}_{k}}{a_{k}}-\frac{\dot{N}}{N}\right)\frac{\dot{a}_{n}}{a_{n}}+\frac{\ddot{a}_{n}}{a_{n}}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n-1}\left\{A_{lk}A_{kl}+\left(A_{kl}\right)^{2}\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{a_{l}^{2}}\right\},

Let

4.

The
n

In this section, we prove that it is enough to consider diagonal metrics for these Lie groups.

Let

be a connected Lie group,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra and
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
a set of generators of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then with respect to the generators, we define the automorphism group of

as

\textrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})=\left\{\Phi=\left[\varphi_{j}^{i}\right]\in\textrm{GL}_{n}\mathbb{R}\ \middle|\ \sum_{l=1}^{n}C_{ij}^{l}\varphi_{l}^{k}=\sum_{l,h=1}^{n}\varphi_{i}^{l}\varphi_{j}^{h}C_{lh}^{k}\ (1\leq i,j,k\leq n)\right\},

where

is the structure constants determined from the generators.

For a general spatially homogeneous spacetime

(M,g_{M})=\left(I\times G,-N^{2}dt^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}s_{ij}(t)\,\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}\right),

we consider the following transformation

(4.1)

[\overline{s}_{ij}(t)]=\Phi^{T}[s_{ij}(t)]\Phi\quad(\Phi\in\textrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})).

Then the above spatially homogeneous spacetime
(M,g_{M})
is isometric to

\left(I\times G,-N^{2}dt^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{s}_{ij}(t)\,\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}\right)

as Lorentzian manifolds since
\Phi
preserves the structure constants.

Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
be a certain set of generators of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
We consider the following conditions:
For any positive-definite symmetric matrix
S\in\textrm{Sym}_{n}^{+}\mathbb{R}
,
there exists an automorphism
\Phi\in\textrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

(4.2)

\Phi^{T}S\Phi=\textrm{diag}(\lambda_{1}^{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}^{2}),

where
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
denote positive numbers.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra.
Let a set of generators
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
satisfy
the above condition (
4.2
).
Then for a general spatially homogeneous spacetime

(M,g_{M})=\left(I\times G,-N(t)^{2}dt^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}s_{ij}(t)\,\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}\right),

if it is Ricci-flat,

is locally isometric to
a diagonal spatially homogeneous spacetime

\left(I\times G,-N(t)^{2}dt^{2}+a_{1}(t)^{2}(\omega^{1})^{2}+\cdots+a_{n}(t)^{2}(\omega^{n})^{2}\right),

where
\{\omega^{1},\ldots,\omega^{n}\}
denote

G

with respect to
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
.

Proof.

First, we fix an initial time
t_{0}\in I
.
When we use canonical coordinates of the first kind
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, the Lorentzian metric is

(g_{M})_{(t_{0},e)}=-{N(t_{0})}^{2}dt^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}s_{ij}(t_{0})\,(dx_{i})_{e}\otimes(dx_{j})_{e}

at the point
(t_{0},e)\in I\times G
.
Since the generators of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the above condition (
4.2
),
we have

When we consider

by using the curve (
2.3
),
and let

,
then we have

.
Since

.

(

)

The solution of the Einstein equations is:

(A.14)

\begin{split}ds^{2}=&f(t)\cosh(kt)\left[-c_{0}^{2}e^{2(\text{tr}h)t}dt^{2}+c_{n}^{2}e^{2h_{n}t}(\omega^{n})^{2}\right]\\

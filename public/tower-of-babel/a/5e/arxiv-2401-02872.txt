Weak Coupling Theory of Magic-Angle Twisted Bilayer Graphene
Jihang Zhu, Iacopo Torre, Marco Polini, Allan H. MacDonald

Abstract
Strong correlations occur in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene (MATBG) when the octet of flat moiré minibands centered on charge neutrality (CN) is partially occupied. The octet consists of a single valence band and a single conduction band for each of four degenerate spin-valley flavors. Motivated by the importance of Hartree electrostatic interactions in determining the filling-factor dependent band structure, we use a time-dependent Hartree approximation to gain insight into electronic correlations. We find that the electronic compressibility is dominated by Hartree interactions, that paramagnetic states are stable over a range of density near CN, and that the dependence of energy on flavor polarization is strongly overestimated by mean-field theory.

The Magic-Angle Correlation Problem—

In this Letter we have reported on the first RPA calculation for MATBG.
The RPA weak-coupling approach has the advantage that it accounts for dynamic screening of long-range
Coulomb interactions, but is less reliable than some other methods in accounting for short-distance correlations.
Competing methods often require tight-binding models, which in the case of MATBG
have the disadvantage that they require the introduction of
additional bands
Po et al. 2019
to compensate for fragile topology inherited from the isolated layer Dirac cones.
Our theory establishes the
crucial influence of correlations in
compressible metallic states in expanding
unbroken symmetry regions
in the MATBG phase diagram. The
RPA weak-coupling approach is also relevant for other
moiré materials that exhibit strong
correlations.

Our calculations include 146 remote valence and conduction bands per spin and flavor.
Our calclations are consistent with experimental indications that flavor ferromagnetism
is common in both insulating and metallic states when the MATBG flat bands are partially filled, less likely close to CN, and
more likely at positive filling factors than at negative filling factors.

\nu

II
TBG Hamiltonian

The Hamiltonian of a periodic crystal is

\hat{H}=\hat{T}_{e}+\hat{H}_{e-e}+\hat{H}_{e-b}+\hat{H}_{b-b},

(21)

where
\hat{T}_{e}
is the kinetic energy operator. Instead of the uniform background charge in the Jellium model, a periodic background of the positive charge

n_{b}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{G}}n_{b,\mathbf{G}}e^{i\mathbf{G}\cdot\mathbf{r}}

(22)

is assumed. At CN, the background charge cancels with excess electron charge

n_{b,\mathbf{0}}=\int n_{b}(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}=N.

(23)

Written in electron number density operators
\hat{n}({\mathbf{q}})
and the total number operator
\hat{N}

\begin{split}&\hat{n}({\mathbf{q}})=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{G}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{q}}c_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}},\\
&\hat{N}=\hat{n}(\mathbf{q}=0)=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{G}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}}c_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}},\end{split}

(24)

the interacting Hamiltonians are

\begin{split}&\hat{H}_{e-b}=-e^{2}\int d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\frac{\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})n_{b}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}=-\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{G}}V(\mathbf{G})n_{b,\mathbf{G}}\hat{n}_{-\mathbf{G}},\\
&\hat{H}_{b-b}=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\int d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\frac{n_{b}(\mathbf{r})n_{b}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{G}}V(\mathbf{G})n_{b,\mathbf{G}}n_{b,-\mathbf{G}},\\
&\hat{H}_{e-e}=\frac{e^{2}}{2}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}\frac{1}{|\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}|}=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{G}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\big[\hat{n}({\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}})\hat{n}({\mathbf{-q}-\mathbf{G}})-\hat{N}\big].\end{split}

(25)

Similar to the Jellium model, the
\mathbf{G}=0
terms of
\hat{H}_{e-b}
and
\hat{H}_{b-b}
, and the
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{G}=0
term of
\hat{H}_{e-e}
cancel in the thermodynamic limit
A,N\rightarrow\infty
. The full Hamiltonian in Eq. (
21
) can be written as

\begin{split}\hat{H}&=\hat{T}_{e}+\hat{H}_{e-e}+\hat{V}_{b}\\
&=\hat{T}_{e}+\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{G}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\big[\hat{n}({\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}})\hat{n}({-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G}})-\hat{N}\big]+\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{G}\neq 0}V(\mathbf{G})\Big[-2n_{b,\mathbf{G}}\hat{n}_{-\mathbf{G}}+n_{b,\mathbf{G}}n_{b,-\mathbf{G}}\Big],\end{split}

(26)

where
′
on the summation symbol means
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{G}=0
term is excluded in the momentum summation.

It is straightforward to generalize the electron-electron interacting Hamiltonian in Eq. (
26
) to the TBG case, where two layers and two sublattices degrees of freedom are explicitly included,

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{e-e}&=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g},\alpha,\beta\\
\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}\end{subarray}}V_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})c^{\dagger}_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{g}_{1}-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}}c^{\dagger}_{\beta,\mathbf{k}_{2}+\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g}}c_{\beta,\mathbf{k}_{2}+\mathbf{g}_{2}}c_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}_{1}+\mathbf{g}_{1}},\end{split}

(27)

where
\mathbf{g},\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}
are moiré reciprocal lattice vectors and
\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{q}
are momenta in the first moiré Brillouin zone.
\alpha,\beta
label layers and sublattices. The
′
on top of the summation means the
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{g}=0
term is excluded, which is cancelled by the periodic background of positive charge in the thermodynamic limit.

Alternatively,
\hat{H}_{e-e}
can be expressed in electron number density operators

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{e-e}&=-\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V_{S}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\hat{N}+\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}\\
\alpha,\beta\end{subarray}}V_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}}\hat{n}^{\mathbf{g}_{1}}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\hat{n}^{\mathbf{g}_{2}}_{\beta}(-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}),\end{split}

(28)

where

\begin{split}\hat{N}&=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{g},\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}}c_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}},\\
\hat{n}(\mathbf{q})&=\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}}\hat{n}_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q})=\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{q}}c_{\alpha,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}}.\end{split}

(29)

For isotropic dielectric media, the Coulomb interaction within the same 2D layer (
V_{S}
) and between different 2D layers (
V_{D}
) are respectively

\begin{split}&V_{S}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\epsilon q},\\
&V_{D}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\epsilon q}e^{-qd},\end{split}

(30)

where
\epsilon
is the relative dielectric constant of the surrounding environment and
d
is the distance between adjacent layers.

For anisotropic dielectric media, for example hBN,
\epsilon_{{}_{\rm BN}}=\sqrt{\epsilon_{zz}\epsilon_{\perp}}\approx 4.5
with
\epsilon_{\perp}=6.9
Geick et al. 1966
,
\epsilon_{zz}=3
.

\begin{split}&V_{S}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{q\sqrt{\epsilon_{\perp}\epsilon_{zz}}},\\
&V_{D}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{q\sqrt{\epsilon_{\perp}\epsilon_{zz}}}e^{-qd\sqrt{\epsilon_{\perp}/\epsilon_{zz}}}.\end{split}

(31)

With dual metallic gates that is equidistant from the target 2D system, with distance
d_{m}
, the screened Coulomb potential energy is

V_{sc}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{q\sqrt{\epsilon_{\perp}\epsilon_{zz}}}\tanh\Big(qd_{m}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_{\perp}}{\epsilon_{zz}}}\Big).

(32)

In calculations throughout this paper, the
d
-dependence of the Coulomb potential in Eq. (
31
) is ignored,
i.e.
,

V(\mathbf{q})=V_{S}(\mathbf{q})=V_{D}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{q\epsilon_{{}_{\rm BN}}},

(33)

and the hBN dielectric constant is chosen to be
\epsilon_{{}_{\rm BN}}=5.1
.
Then Eq. (
28
) simplifies to

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{e-e}&=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\Big[\hat{n}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\hat{n}(-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g})-\hat{N}\Big].\end{split}

(34)

III
Self-consistent Hartree approximation in TBG

With approximate SU(4) symmetry, the exchange interaction is only between electrons within the same flavor. The inter-flavor interaction is only through the Hartree potential.
The matrix element of the flavor- and momentum-independent Hartree self-energy is

\Sigma^{H}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}=\frac{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{A}\sum\limits_{\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{2}^{\prime}}V_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\mathbf{g}_{2}-\mathbf{g}_{1})\delta\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{1}^{\prime};\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{2}^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{g}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{2}-\mathbf{g}_{1}},

(35)

where
\delta\boldsymbol{\rho}
is the relative density matrix defined by subtracting the density matrix of the decoupled graphene bilayer at CN

\delta\boldsymbol{\rho}=\boldsymbol{\rho}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0}.

(36)

The flavor-polarization-dependence of the self-energy is implicitly incorporated in the density matrix through the Fermi-Dirac distribution
\Theta(\mu_{f}-\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}})
:

\rho_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}=\sum\limits_{f,n,\mathbf{k}}z^{nf}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{nf}_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\Theta(\mu_{f}-\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}).

(37)

f=1,2,3,4
represent four flavors.
If the inversion symmetry is retained, the diagonal elements of
\Sigma^{H}
are constant and can be set to zero.

For any specific flavor polarization
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{4})
, the Hamiltonian is solved self-consistently for each flavor

H^{f}(\mathbf{k},\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{4})=H^{f}_{0}(\mathbf{k})+\Sigma^{H}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{4}),

(38)

where
H^{f}_{0}(\mathbf{k})
is the single-particle Hamiltonian of flavor
f
.

Self-consistent Hartree band structures and corresponding Fermi surfaces of flavor-symmetry unbroken states at filling factors
\nu\in(-1,1)
are shown in Fig.
5
.

The electrostatic Hartree energy is

\begin{split}E_{H}&=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{f,n,\mathbf{k}}\langle\Psi_{fn\mathbf{k}}|\Sigma^{H}|\Psi_{fn\mathbf{k}}\rangle\Theta(\mu_{f}-\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}})\\
&=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}\\
\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}V_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\mathbf{g}_{2}-\mathbf{g}_{1})\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{2}}\delta\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{1};\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{\mathbf{g}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{2}-\mathbf{g}_{1}},\end{split}

(39)

and is further regularized by subtracting the negative Fermi sea contribution

E_{H}=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}\\
\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}V_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\mathbf{g}_{2}-\mathbf{g}_{1})\delta\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{2}}\delta\rho_{\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{1};\alpha^{\prime},\mathbf{g}^{\prime}_{2}}\delta_{\mathbf{g}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{g}_{2}-\mathbf{g}_{1}}.

(40)

The SCH energy
E_{0}
in the main text is defined to include both the SCH band dispersion and the electrostatic Hartree energy:

\begin{split}E_{0}&=E_{\text{band}}-E_{H}\\
&=\sum\limits_{f,n,\mathbf{k}}\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}\Theta(\mu_{f}-\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}})-E_{H},\end{split}

(41)

where
\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}
are eigenvalues of SCH Hamiltonian Eq. (
38
) and
E_{H}
is subtracted to avoid double-counting of the Coulomb energy.

V
The density response function of TBG

At zero temperature, the matrix elements of the flavor-specified density response function
\tilde{\chi}^{f}
of MATBG can be derived following the Lindhard formula

\begin{split}[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}\frac{\Theta^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\Theta^{f}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}{\omega+\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon^{f}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}+i\eta}\Big[\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}z^{nf}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{mf}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Big]^{*}\sum\limits_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}z^{nf}_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{mf}_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}).\end{split}

(43)

where
\varepsilon
and
z
are quasiparticle eigen-energy and eigenvector of the self-consistent Hartree approximation.
It can be easily proved that Eq. (
43
) satisfies the symmetry

[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\big[[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},-\omega)\big]^{*}.

(44)

It obeys the general property of the response function

\tilde{\chi}_{AB}(\omega)=[\tilde{\chi}_{A^{\dagger}B^{\dagger}}(-\omega)]^{*}.

(45)

The density response functions of opposite valleys with the same electrostatic doping level are related by the spinless time-reversal symmetry

[\tilde{\chi}^{-}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega),

(46)

following reciprocity relations

\tilde{\chi}^{-}_{AB}(\omega)=\tilde{\chi}^{+}_{B^{T}A^{T}}(\omega),

(47)

where
\pm
represent two opposite valleys in TBG.
If the TBG system is flavor unpolarized, the total density response function
\tilde{\chi}
summed over four flavors is time-reversal invariant and satisfies

\begin{split}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)&=2[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+2[\tilde{\chi}^{-}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\\
&=2[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+2[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)\\
&=\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega).\end{split}

(48)

At finite temperatures, the density response function is

\begin{split}[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}&\frac{\Theta^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\Theta^{f}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}{i\omega+\varepsilon^{f}_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon^{f}_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}\Big[\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}z^{nf}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{mf}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Big]^{*}\sum\limits_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}z^{nf}_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{mf}_{\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}).\end{split}

(49)

Matrix
\tilde{\chi}^{f}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)
is Hermitian along the imaginary frequency axis,
i.e.
,

\begin{gathered}\tilde{\chi}^{f}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\dagger}(\mathbf{q},-i\omega),\\
[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=\big[[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},-i\omega)\big]^{*}.\end{gathered}

(50)

\tilde{\chi}^{f}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)
also satisfies

[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}}(-\mathbf{q},-i\omega)=\big[[\tilde{\chi}^{f}]^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},i\omega)\big]^{*}.

(51)

Opposite valleys with the same electrostatic doping level are related by the time-reversal symmetry

[\tilde{\chi}^{-}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}-\mathbf{g}}(-\mathbf{q},i\omega)=\big[[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)\big]^{*}=\big[[\tilde{\chi}^{-}]^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},-i\omega)\big]^{*}.

(52)

For valley unpolarized state, the total proper density response function in Eq. (
3
) satisfies

\begin{gathered}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=2[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)+2[\tilde{\chi}^{-}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=2[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)+2\big[[\tilde{\chi}^{+}]^{\mathbf{g}^{\prime}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)\big]^{*},\end{gathered}

(53)

and therefore the density response function of flavor unpolarized state is Hermitian,
i.e.
,

\tilde{\chi}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=\tilde{\chi}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{q},i\omega).

(54)

We see that symmetries of
\tilde{\chi}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)
are different from those of
\tilde{\chi}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
at zero temperature.

VI
The Coupling-constant integration as an evaluation of the ground state energy

The ground-state energy of an electron system can be easily connected to the density-density linear-response function—for example at the level of the popular RPA — by the integration over the coupling constant theorem (see, for example, Sect. I.8.3 of Ref.
Giuliani and Vignale 2005
).
However, the ground-state electron density of a moiré crystal is inhomogeneous on the moiré superlattice length scale.
The application of the integration over the coupling constant theorem to an inhomogeneous many-body system is not straightforward and presents some subtleties. On the contrary, the Hohenberg-Kohn and Kohn-Sham theorems (see, for example, Chapter 7 of Ref.
Giuliani and Vignale 2005
) of density functional theory are the natural theoretical framework to deal with inhomogeneous many-body electron systems.
This is why we formulate the problem of the calculation of the ground-state energy of twisted bilayer graphene within the framework of Adiabatic Connection Fluctuation and Dissipation Theorem (ACFDT)
4
4

4

See e.g. Ref.[
Görling 2019
] and references therein to earlier work.

and then recover the RPA by taking a suitable limit. This theory applies the integration over the coupling constant theorem between the Kohn-Sham (KS) ground state and the real ground state of the electronic Hamiltonian as detailed in the following.

VI.1
The Adiabatic Connection Fluctuation and Dissipation Theorem

The electronic Hamiltonian can be written as

\hat{\cal H}=\hat{T}_{\rm e}+\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}+\hat{V}_{\rm ext}~,

(55)

where
\hat{T}_{\rm e}
is the kinetic operator,
\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}
is the electron-electron interaction, i.e.

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{\rm e-e}&=\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{G}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\Big[\hat{n}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})\hat{n}(-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G})-\hat{N}\Big],\end{split}

(56)

and
\hat{V}_{\rm ext}
is the crystal potential, i.e.

\hat{V}_{\rm ext}=\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}V_{\rm ext}(\mathbf{r})\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})~.

(57)

In Eq. (
56
),
V({\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}})
is the Fourier transform of the electron-electron interaction potential, evaluated at the wave vector
{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}
, where
{\mathbf{q}}
is in the first BZ and
{\mathbf{G}}
is an arbitrary reciprocal lattice vector. Similarly,
\hat{n}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G})
is the Fourier transform of the ground-state density operator
\hat{n}({\mathbf{r}})
.

Let
|\Phi\rangle
be the ground state of the full Hamiltonian and
n(\mathbf{r})
the associated ground-state density, i.e.

n(\mathbf{r})=\langle\Phi|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})|\Phi\rangle~.

(58)

We now introduce a key auxiliary system, which is described by the so-called
Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian

\hat{\cal H}_{\rm KS}
:

\hat{\cal H}_{\rm KS}=\hat{T}_{\rm e}+\hat{V}_{\rm ext}+\hat{V}_{\rm H}+\hat{V}_{\rm xc}\equiv\hat{T}_{\rm e}+\hat{V}_{\rm KS}~.

(59)

This Hamiltonian is a (self-consistent) one-particle Hamiltonian whose ground state
|\psi_{\rm KS}\rangle
is a Slater determinant of Kohn-Sham orbitals. The fundamental property of this Hamiltonian is that
it yields the exact same density of the full Hamiltonian
(
55
).

We now define a family
\hat{\cal H}_{\lambda}
of Hamiltonians depending on a real dimensionless parameter
\lambda\in[0,1]
:

\hat{\cal H}_{\lambda}=\hat{T}_{\rm e}+\lambda\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}+\hat{V}_{\lambda}~.

(60)

Let
|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
be the normalized ground state of
\hat{\cal H}_{\lambda}
, i.e.
\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle=1~\forall\lambda
.
In Eq. (
60
),
\hat{T}_{\rm e}
and
\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}
have the exact same meaning as above. The key new quantity is
\hat{V}_{\lambda}
, which is a local potential that interpolates between the Kohn-Sham potential in the limit
\lambda=0
, i.e.
\hat{V}_{\lambda=0}=\hat{V}_{\rm KS}
, and the exact, physical crystal potential in the limit
\lambda=1
, i.e.
\hat{V}_{\lambda=1}=\hat{V}_{\rm ext}
. Crucially,
\hat{V}_{\lambda}
varies with
\lambda
in such a way that the correct electronic density is reproduced at every value of
\lambda
, i.e.

n_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r})\equiv\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{n}(\mathbf{r})|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle=n(\mathbf{r})~\forall\lambda~.

(61)

The uniqueness (up to a constant) of
\hat{V}_{\lambda}
can be proved by applying the Hohenberg-Kohn theorem to the electronic Hamiltonian with reduced coupling constant. In the following, we will fix the arbitrary constant by setting the average value of
\hat{V}_{\lambda}
to zero.

The ground state energy at coupling constant
\lambda
is

E(\lambda)=\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{\cal H}_{\lambda}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle~.

(62)

By applying the Hellman-Feynman theorem we obtain

\frac{dE(\lambda)}{d\lambda}=\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle+\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}n(\mathbf{r})\partial_{\lambda}V_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r})~.

(63)

Integrating the previous differential equation between
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=1
we find:

E(1)-E(0)=\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle+\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}n(\mathbf{r})[V_{\rm ext}(\mathbf{r})-V_{\rm KS}(\mathbf{r})]~.

(64)

In writing the previous two equations we made use of the crucial fact that
n_{\lambda}(\mathbf{r})=n(\mathbf{r})
for every
\lambda
in the integration interval.

We now recall that, in DFT, the exact ground-state energy
E
of the system, which in the notation of Eq. (
64
) coincides with
E(1)
, is given by
Giuliani and Vignale 2005

E=E(1)=T_{\rm s}+\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}n(\mathbf{r})V_{\rm ext}(\mathbf{r})+E_{\rm H}+E_{\rm xc}~,

(65)

where
T_{\rm s}
is the non-interacting kinetic energy functional, i.e. the kinetic energy of a non-interacting system whose ground-state density is
n({\mathbf{r}})
,
E_{\rm H}
is the Hartree energy, i.e.

E_{\rm H}=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}\int d^{2}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}V(|{\mathbf{r}}-{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}|)n({\mathbf{r}})n({\mathbf{r}}^{\prime})~,

(66)

and
E_{\rm xc}
is the exchange-correlation energy functional.

On the other hand, the quantity
E(0)
is the average of the Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian over the Kohn-Sham ground-state

E(0)=\sum_{\alpha\in\rm occ.}\epsilon_{\alpha}^{\rm KS}=T_{\rm s}+\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}n(\mathbf{r})V_{\rm KS}(\mathbf{r})~,

(67)

where
\epsilon_{\alpha}^{\rm KS}
are the eigenvalues of the Kohn-Sham equations and the sum runs over the occupied states.

Taking the difference between Eq. (
65
) and Eq. (
67
) and comparing the result with Eq. (
64
) we find the following important result:

E_{\rm H}+E_{\rm xc}=\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle~.

(68)

We can conveniently express the matrix element
\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle
of the interaction Hamiltonian in terms of the density-density response function by using the fluctuation-dissipation theorem
Giuliani and Vignale 2005
. At zero temperature and assuming a non-degenerate ground state, we obtain

\begin{split}\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{n}_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}\hat{n}_{-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G}}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle&=-\frac{\hbar}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{\hat{n}_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}\hat{n}_{-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G}}}(\omega,\lambda)\right]d\omega+\langle\Phi_{\lambda}|\hat{n}_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}|\Phi_{\lambda}\rangle\langle\Phi_{\lambda}|\hat{n}_{-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G}}|\Phi_{\lambda}\rangle\\
&=-\frac{\hbar A}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm Im}\left[\chi_{nn}^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{q},\omega,\lambda)\right]d\omega+n_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}n_{-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G}}~.\end{split}

(69)

Making use of (
69
) and (
56
) we get

\langle\psi_{\lambda}|\hat{\cal H}_{\rm e-e}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}V_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}\left\{\frac{n_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}n_{-\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{G}}}{N}-\frac{\hbar}{n\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega{\rm Im}[\chi^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}_{nn}(\mathbf{q},\omega,\lambda)]-1\right\}.

(70)

The first term is independent of
\lambda
and coincides with the Hartree energy,

E_{\rm H}=\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{G}\neq{\mathbf{0}}}V_{\mathbf{G}}n_{\mathbf{G}}n_{-\mathbf{G}}~.

(71)

We are therefore left with

E_{\rm xc}=\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}V_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}\left\{-\frac{\hbar}{n\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega{\rm Im}[\chi^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}_{nn}(\mathbf{q},\omega,\lambda)]-1\right\}~.

(72)

It can be further shown that the exchange energy can be written as

E_{\rm x}=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{G}}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}V_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}\left\{-\frac{\hbar}{n\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega{\rm Im}[\chi^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}_{\rm KS}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]-1\right\}~,

(73)

where
\chi^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}_{\rm KS}(\mathbf{q},\omega)
is the Kohn-Sham response function. Note that this is the exchange energy calculated on the KS orbitals, which is
different
from the Hartree-Fock exchange.

The difference between
E_{\rm xc}
and
E_{\rm x}
is the correlation energy:

E_{\rm c}=\frac{N}{2}\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\sum_{\mathbf{G}}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}V_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}\left\{-\frac{\hbar}{n\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega{\rm Im}[\chi^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}_{nn}(\mathbf{q},\omega,\lambda)-\chi_{\rm KS}^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)]\right\}~.

(74)

In a crystal, the linear response relation—see Eq. (7.182) in Ref.
Giuliani and Vignale 2005
—relating the full density response at coupling constant
\lambda
to the Kohn-Sham density response reads as following:

\delta n_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}}(\omega)=\sum_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}\chi_{\rm KS}^{\mathbf{G}\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)\left\{V^{\rm ext}_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}(\omega)+\sum_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}}[\lambda V_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}}+f_{\rm xc,L}^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega,\lambda)]\delta n_{\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}}(\omega)\right\},

(75)

where
f_{\rm xc,L}^{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\mathbf{G}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega,\lambda)
is the wave vector and frequency-dependent exchange-correlation kernel
Giuliani and Vignale 2005
evaluate at coupling constant
\lambda
. Treating functions in Eq. (
75
) as matrices with respect to reciprocal lattice vectors indices, we can finally rewrite Eq. (
75
) as

\delta n=\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot\left\{V^{\rm ext}+[\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda)]\cdot\delta n\right\}~.

(76)

Using the same notation, the definition of the full response function at coupling constant
\lambda
reads as following:

\delta n=\chi(\lambda)\cdot V^{\rm ext}~.

(77)

Substituting this definition into Eq. (
76
) yields

\chi(\lambda)=\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot\left\{1+[\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda)]\cdot\chi(\lambda)\right\}~.

(78)

Carrying out some simple algebraic manipulation we finally find

\chi(\lambda)=\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot\{1-[\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda)]\cdot\chi_{\rm KS}\}^{-1}~,

(79)

which can be further rearranged into

\chi(\lambda)-\chi_{\rm KS}=\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot[\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda)]\cdot\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot\{1-[\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda)]\cdot\chi_{\rm KS}\}^{-1}~.

(80)

Substituting into the formula for the correlation energy we get the exact expression

E_{\rm c}=-\frac{\hbar N}{2\pi n}\int_{\rm BZ}\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda{\rm Im}\left\{\left[V\cdot\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot[\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda)]\cdot\chi_{\rm KS}\cdot[1-(\lambda V+f_{\rm xc,L}(\lambda))\cdot\chi_{\rm KS}]^{-1}\right](\mathbf{q},\omega)\right\}.

(81)

The total energy is finally given by

E=\sum_{\alpha\in\rm occ.}\epsilon_{\alpha}^{\rm KS}-\int d^{2}\mathbf{r}n(\mathbf{r})V_{\rm xc}(\mathbf{r})-E_{\rm H}+E_{\rm x}+E_{\rm c}~.

(82)

VI.2
Taking the RPA limit

The RPA is obtained by setting to zero
\hat{V}_{\rm xc}
in the KS equations and
f_{\rm xc,L}
in (
81
). In this limit, the KS equations become the Hartree equations, the KS orbitals become the Hartree orbitals, and the KS response function becomes the Hartree response function.

The coupling-constant integral can then be done analytically, yielding

\begin{split}E_{\rm c,RPA}&=-\frac{\hbar N}{2\pi n}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\lambda\rm Im\Big\{\text{Tr}\big[[V\cdot\chi_{\rm H}]^{2}\cdot[1-\lambda V\cdot\chi_{\rm H}]^{-1}\big](\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big\}\\
&=-\frac{\hbar N}{2\pi n}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\lambda\rm Im\Big\{\text{Tr}\big[(\sqrt{V}\cdot\chi_{\rm H}\cdot\sqrt{V})^{2}\cdot(1-\lambda\sqrt{V}\cdot\chi_{\rm H}\cdot\sqrt{V})^{-1}\big](\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big\}\\
&=\frac{N}{2n}\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\hbar d\omega}{\pi}\text{Im}\Big\{\text{Tr}[\sqrt{V}\cdot\chi_{\rm H}\cdot\sqrt{V}+\ln(1-\sqrt{V}\cdot\chi_{\rm H}\cdot\sqrt{V})](\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big\}.\end{split}

(83)

Setting
\hat{V}_{\rm xc}=0
in (
82
) we obtain the final expression for the RPA (or time-dependent Hartree) ground-state energy of an inhomogeneous system,

E=\sum_{\alpha}^{\rm occ.}\epsilon_{\alpha}^{\rm H}-E_{\rm H}+E_{\rm x}+E_{\rm c,RPA}~.

(84)

Note that the Hartree and exchange energies are now calculated on the Hartree orbitals.
The first two terms in the previous equation coincide with the Hartree expression of the ground-state energy, avoiding double counting of the Coulomb interaction energy.

VII
The exchange-correlation energy of TBG

Following the ACFDT in SM
VI
,
the exchange-correlation energy of TBG is,

\begin{split}E_{xc}&=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\Big[-\frac{1}{\pi n}\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Im}\chi^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},\omega;\lambda)d\omega-1\Big]\\
&=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\Big[-\frac{1}{\pi n}\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\chi^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega;\lambda)d\omega-1\Big]\\
&=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\Big[-\frac{1}{\pi n}\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega;\lambda)d\omega-1\Big].\end{split}

(85)

In the last two expressions above, the integral along the real axis is rotated to the imaginary axis using the contour deformation, which is justified below.

The response function can be expressed in the entire complex plane using the spectral representation,

\chi(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\text{Im}\chi(\omega)}{z-\omega}d\omega.

(86)

In TBG, the density response function satisfies (as in Eq. (
44
))

\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)=\big[\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},-\omega)\big]^{*},

(87)

therefore

\begin{split}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},z)&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},-\omega)}{z+\omega}+\frac{\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)}{z-\omega}\Bigg]d\omega\\
&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{-\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)}{z+\omega}+\frac{\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)}{z-\omega}\Bigg]d\omega,\\
\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},z)&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},-\omega)}{z+\omega}+\frac{\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)}{z-\omega}\Bigg]d\omega\\
&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{-\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)}{z+\omega}+\frac{\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)}{z-\omega}\Bigg]d\omega.\\
\end{split}

(88)

Combine
\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},z)
and
\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},z)
,

\begin{split}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},z)+\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},z)&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{z-\omega}-\frac{1}{z+\omega}\Big)d\omega\\
&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big)\frac{2\omega(z_{1}^{2}-z_{2}^{2}-\omega^{2})-i4z_{1}z_{2}\omega}{(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}-\omega^{2})^{2}+4z_{2}^{2}\omega^{2}}d\omega,\end{split}

(89)

where
z=z_{1}+iz_{2}
. Along the imaginary axis, the imaginary part of Eq. (
89
) vanishes. Therefore the integral along the real axis can be rotated to the imaginary axis:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)+\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},i\omega)\Big)d\omega&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\Big(\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)+\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},i\omega)\Big)d\omega\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Im}\Big(\tilde{\chi}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{q},\omega)+\tilde{\chi}^{-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{g}^{\prime}}(-\mathbf{q},\omega)\Big)d\omega\\
\end{split}

(90)

Return to the xc energy, since only the diagonal in
\mathbf{g}
elements of matrix
\boldsymbol{\chi}(\mathbf{q},i\omega;\lambda)
is relevant in Eq. (
85
), and the Coulomb matrix
\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})
is diagonal in
\mathbf{g}
,
the exchange-correlation energy can also be written in the matrix product form,

\begin{split}E_{xc}&=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q}}\Big[-\frac{1}{\pi n}\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\text{Tr}\big(\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})\boldsymbol{\chi}(\mathbf{q},i\omega;\lambda)\big)-\text{Tr}\big(\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})\big)\Big].\end{split}

(91)

The coupling-constant-dependent density response function in Eq. (
85
) and Eq. (
91
) is approximated, within the RPA, with

\begin{split}\chi(\lambda)&=\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}(1-\lambda V\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}})^{-1}\\
&=\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}+\lambda\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}V\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}(1-\lambda V\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}})^{-1},\end{split}

(92)

where
\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}
is the proper density response function of the self-consistent Hartree approximation. The expression of
\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}
of a specific flavor is shown in Eq. (
49
).

The exchange energy
E_{x}
is the first order contribution in Eq. (
85
) and Eq. (
91
),
i.e.
arising from the first term in Eq. (
92
)

\begin{split}E_{x}&=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\Big[-\frac{1}{\pi n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)d\omega-1\Big]\\
&=\frac{n}{2}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q}}\Big[-\frac{1}{\pi n}\text{Tr}\big(\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}(\mathbf{q})\big)-\text{Tr}\big(\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})\big)\Big].\end{split}

(93)

Using the Lindhard formula, the diagonal elements of
\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}
is

\begin{split}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=&\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}\Bigg(\frac{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}{\omega^{2}+(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})^{2}}-i\frac{\omega}{\omega^{2}+(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})^{2}}\Bigg)(f_{n\mathbf{k}}-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})\Big|\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}z^{n}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Big|^{2}\end{split}

(94)

and its real and imaginary parts are respectively

\begin{split}&\text{Re}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}\frac{(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})(f_{n\mathbf{k}}-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{\omega^{2}+(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})^{2}}\Big|\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}z^{n}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Big|^{2},\\
&\text{Im}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)=-\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}\frac{\omega(f_{n\mathbf{k}}-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{\omega^{2}+(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})^{2}}\Big|\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}z^{n}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Big|^{2}.\end{split}

(95)

\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}
,
z^{n}(\mathbf{k})
are eigen-energies and eigenvectors of the self-consistent Hartree approximation.
Using the integration equality

\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{1}{\omega^{2}+a^{2}}=\frac{1}{a}\arctan\Big(\frac{\omega}{a}\Big)\Big|^{\infty}_{0}=\frac{\pi}{2|a|},

(96)

the frequency integration of the real part of
\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)
can be done analytically:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)d\omega=&\frac{\pi}{2A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}\frac{(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})(f_{n\mathbf{k}}-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})}{|\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|}\langle n\mathbf{k}|e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle.\end{split}

(97)

Rewrite the Fermi-Dirac occupation difference

f_{n\mathbf{k}}-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}=f_{n\mathbf{k}}(1-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})-(1-f_{n\mathbf{k}})f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}

(98)

and it is clear that

\frac{\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}}{|\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}|}=\begin{cases}-1,\text{ if }f_{n\mathbf{k}}=1\text{ and }f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}=0,\\
1,\text{ if }f_{n\mathbf{k}}=0\text{ and }f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}=1.\end{cases}

(99)

Then Eq. (
97
) becomes

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)d\omega=&-\frac{\pi}{2A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}[f_{n\mathbf{k}}(1-f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})+(1-f_{n\mathbf{k}})f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}]\langle n\mathbf{k}|e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle\\
=&-\frac{\pi}{2A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}(f_{n\mathbf{k}}+f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-2f_{n\mathbf{k}}f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}})\langle n\mathbf{k}|e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle.\\
\end{split}

(100)

The first two terms are simply total occupation number

\begin{split}&\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}f_{n\mathbf{k}}\langle n\mathbf{k}|e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle=\sum\limits_{n,\mathbf{k}}f_{n\mathbf{k}}=N,\\
&\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\langle n\mathbf{k}|e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle=\sum\limits_{m,\mathbf{k}}f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}=N.\end{split}

(101)

Equation (
100
) becomes

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{Re}\tilde{\chi}_{{}_{H}}^{\mathbf{g}\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)d\omega=&-\pi n+\frac{\pi}{A}\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}f_{n\mathbf{k}}f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\langle n\mathbf{k}|e^{-i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\rangle\langle m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}|e^{i(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\cdot\mathbf{r}}|n\mathbf{k}\rangle.\\
\end{split}

(102)

Substitute into the exchange energy Eq. (
93
), the
-\pi n
term above cancels exactly with the self-interacting term, and the exchange energy is simply

\begin{split}E_{x}&=-\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}f_{n\mathbf{k}}f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\Big|\sum\limits_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}z^{n}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\Big|^{2}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{n,m,\mathbf{k}}f_{n\mathbf{k}}f_{m\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta,\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}}\bar{\rho}^{n}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\rho^{m}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}).\end{split}

(103)

This exchange energy looks like the HF exchange but it is not since it is calculated over the SCH wavefunctions instead of the self-consistent HF wavefunctions.

On the other hand, the correlation energy is given by higher order contributions in RPA. Use

\text{Tr}(\mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\chi})=\text{Tr}(\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}\boldsymbol{\chi}\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}),

(104)

the correlation energy is

\begin{split}E_{c}&=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q}}\int_{0}^{1}d\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\text{Tr}\big[\lambda(\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}\sqrt{\mathbf{V}})^{2}(1-\lambda\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}\sqrt{\mathbf{V}})^{-1}\big]\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\text{Tr}\big[\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}+\ln(1-\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}\sqrt{\mathbf{V}})\big].\end{split}

(105)

For any diagonalizable and non-singular (invertible) matrix
\mathbf{A}
, its logarithm is

\ln\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{v}(\ln\boldsymbol{\lambda})\mathbf{v}^{-1},

(106)

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}
,

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\mathbf{v}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v},

(107)

and
\mathbf{v}
is the matrix with eigenvectors in each column.
The trace of
\ln\mathbf{A}

\begin{split}\text{Tr}\ln\mathbf{A}&=\text{Tr}\big[\mathbf{v}(\ln\boldsymbol{\lambda})\mathbf{v}^{-1}\big]\\
&=\text{Tr}\big[(\ln\boldsymbol{\lambda})\mathbf{v}^{-1}\mathbf{v}\big]\\
&=\text{Tr}\ln\boldsymbol{\lambda}\\
&=\ln\det\mathbf{A}.\end{split}

(108)

The correlation energy

\begin{split}E_{c}&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\text{Tr}\Big[\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)+\ln\big(1-\sqrt{\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)\sqrt{\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})}\big)\Big]\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\Big[\text{Tr}\big(\mathbf{V}(\mathbf{q})\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)\big)+\sum\limits_{i}\ln\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)\Big],\end{split}

(109)

where
\lambda_{i}(\mathbf{q},i\omega)
is the
i
-th eigenvalue of
(1-\sqrt{\mathbf{V}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{}_{H}}\sqrt{\mathbf{V}})
.

VIII
Exchange energy regularization

The exchange energy in Eq. (
103
) must be regularized to deal with the negative energy sea of the Dirac model. In the main text we denote
E_{x}
as the regularized exchange energy

\begin{split}E_{x}&=-\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}\\
\alpha,\beta\end{subarray}}\big[\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\rho_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})-\bar{\rho}^{0}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\rho^{0}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\big]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}\\
\alpha,\beta\end{subarray}}\big[\delta\bar{\rho}(\mathbf{k})+2\bar{\rho}^{0}(\mathbf{k})\big]_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}\delta\rho_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}).\end{split}

(110)

\rho^{0}
is the density matrix of the charge neutral decoupled bilayer and therefore it’s diagonal in
\mathbf{g}
’s:

\rho^{0}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})=\delta_{\mathbf{g}_{1}\mathbf{g}_{2}}\sum\limits_{n\in v}z^{0}_{n,\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}}(\mathbf{k})\bar{z}^{0}_{n,\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k}).

(111)

Summing over two valence bands from top and bottom layer Dirac cones, the
4\times 4
density matrix with an explicit
\mathbf{g}
is

\rho^{0}_{\mathbf{g};\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k})\equiv\rho^{0}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g})=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-e^{-i(\theta_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{1}}-\theta/2)}&0&0\\
-e^{i(\theta_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{1}}-\theta/2)}&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&-e^{-i(\theta_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{2}}+\theta/2)}\\
0&0&-e^{i(\theta_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{2}}+\theta/2)}&1\end{pmatrix}.

(112)

Therefore, the second part of the second line of Eq. (
110
) is equivalent to

\begin{split}E_{x}^{(2)}&=-\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\in\text{MBZ}\\
\mathbf{k}\in k_{c}\sim 1/a\end{subarray}}V(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{\mathbf{g}_{1},\alpha,\beta}\bar{\rho}^{0}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{g}_{1})\delta\rho_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\end{split}

(113)

Because the diagonal terms of
\rho^{0}
contribute a constant energy shift which is proportional to the occupation number at
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
, only off-diagonal terms of
\rho^{0}
matter and it’s

\begin{split}E_{x}^{(2)}=\frac{2\pi e^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\in\text{MBZ},\mathbf{g}}\Big[&e^{i\xi(\theta_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{1}}-\theta/2)}[\delta\rho_{\mathbf{g};\mathbf{g}}]_{12}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{1}|\ln\frac{k_{c}}{|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{1}|}\\
+&e^{i\xi(\theta_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{2}}+\theta/2)}\delta[\rho_{\mathbf{g};\mathbf{g}}]_{34}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{2}|\ln\frac{k_{c}}{|\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{K}_{2}|}+h.c\Big]\end{split}

(114)

Therefore the regularized exchange energy is

\begin{split}E_{x}&=-\frac{1}{2A}\sum\limits^{\prime}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{g}}V(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{g})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}\\
\alpha,\beta\end{subarray}}\delta\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}}(\mathbf{k})\delta\rho_{\alpha,\mathbf{g}_{1}+\mathbf{g};\beta,\mathbf{g}_{2}+\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})+E^{(2)}_{x}.\end{split}

(115)

XI
Magnetic Anisotropy in SU(4) Ferromagnets

MATBG has
SU(4)
ferromagnetism because of its four degenerate spin-valley flavors.
In the continuum model we employ the
SU(4)
symmetry is reduced to
SU(2)\times SU(2)\times U(1)

by the difference between two valley projected band Hamiltonians, which contribute explicitly to
the model’s
SU(4)
magnetic anisotropy. Valley-exchange and spin-orbit interactions,
which we neglect, also contribute to magnetic anisotropy. In our RPA approach, we neglect
anisotropy by focusing only on flavor-dependent filling factors, the eigenvalues of the spin-valley
density matrix. Indeed our explicit calculations assume that the ferromagnet’s density matrix remains
diagonal in the spin-valley representation because total valley occupation number remains a good quantum
number, simplifying the use of a coupling-constant integral representation of the energy.
There is in fact theoretical
Khalaf et al. 2021
;
Kwan et al. 2021
;
Ledwith et al. 2021
;
Shavit et al. 2021
;
Shavit et al. 2023

and experimental
Oh et al. 2021
;
Hong et al. 2022
;
Yu et al. 2023
;
Nuckolls et al. 2023
work that
intervalley coherence, which breaks valley number symmetry, is present in many MATBG ferromagnets.
Our calculations make no effort to distinguish between ferromagnets that differ only in the
orientation of the spin-valley magnetization and not in its magnitude as characterized
by the differences between spin-valley density-matrix eigenvalues. There is evidence in
recent experiments
Nuckolls et al. 2023
, in
the form of the presence of sample-specific domain walls and vortices in various partial
order parameters, that as in most conventional ferromagnets the energy scale associated with anisotropy
is smaller than the energy scale associated with ordering.

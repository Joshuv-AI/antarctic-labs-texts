A note on the universal supersingular quotients of U ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) U(2,1)
Peng Xu

Abstract
Let G G be the unramified unitary group U ​ ( 2 , 1 ) ​ ( E / F ) U(2,1)(E/F) defined over a non-archimedean local field F F of residue characteristic p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 . In this note, we prove the universal supersingular quotients of G G are not irreducible in general.

1
Introduction

Let
G
be the unramified unitary group
U(2,1)(E/F)
defined over a non-archimedean locally compact field
F
of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
, and let
K
be a maximal compact open subgroup of
G
. For an irreducible smooth
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-representation
\pi
of
G
, and a weight
\sigma
of
K
contained in
\pi
, the space

\text{Hom}_{G}(\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma,\pi)

is a right module over the spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma):=\text{End}_{G}(\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma)
. In
[
Xu18
, Theorem 1.1]
, we proved the above space admits eigenvectors for
\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma)
. As the algebra
\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma)
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[T_{\sigma}]
for some
T_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma)
, the representation
\pi
is a quotient of
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma}-\lambda)
for some
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
. In the case of
\lambda=0
the representation
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
is usually called the universal supersingular quotient of
G
. In this note, we prove the following result for such representations.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Corollary
4.6
, Corollary
4.13
)
Suppose
K
is special but non-hyperspecial, and the size of the residue field of
F
is
p
. For any weight
\sigma
of
K
, the universal supersingular quotient
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
is not irreducible.

We prove Theorem
1.1
by finding a pro-
p
-Iwahori invariant function from the maximal compact induction and showing that its non-zero image in the universal quotient does not generate the representation. We expect the theorem still holds without the restriction (on the group
K
and and the size of the residue field) in the statement; indeed they are only used in Corollary
4.13
.

To the author’s knowledge, the universal supersingular quotients have only been extensively studied for the group
GL_{2}(F)
(
[
Mor12
]
,
[
Mor13
]
,
[
Sch14
]
,
[
Hen19
]
). Besides the definition (
[
AHHV17
]
), very little is known about them in the general case. It is interesting to see whether our approach works for other groups, and our guess is that the function we found (to make the idea work) is very specific to the group itself: for example some quick computation indicates that the analogue of such functions does not exist for the group
GL_{2}(F)
.

2.4
The supersingular universal quotient of
G

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
G
, and let
\sigma
be a weight of
K
contained in
\pi
. By composition, the space

\text{Hom}_{G}(\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma,\pi)

is a right module over the spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma):=\text{End}_{G}(\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma)
. The main result of
[
Xu18
]
is:

Theorem 2.3
.

The space
\text{Hom}_{G}(\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma,\pi)
admits eigenvectors for the spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma)
.

We modify the Hecke operator
T
slightly as follows:

T_{\sigma}=\begin{cases}T,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\textnormal{dim}\sigma>1\\
T,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\textnormal{dim}\sigma=1,\chi_{\sigma}=\chi^{s}_{\sigma}~\chi_{\sigma}\neq\chi\circ\text{det};\\
T+1,~~~~~~~\textnormal{dim}\sigma=1,\chi_{\sigma}=\chi\circ\text{det}.\end{cases}

As the algebra
\mathcal{H}(K,\sigma)
is isomorphic to
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}[T_{\sigma}]
, the representation
\pi
is isomorphic to a quotient of
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma}-\lambda)
, for some
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
. In the case
\lambda=0
, we encounter the so-called universal supersingular quotient of

, as the main theme we will deal with in the present paper.

3
The space
\text{Hom}_{G}(\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma,\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma^{\prime})

Let
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
be two weights of
K
, and denote by
L(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
the space

\text{Hom}_{G}(\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma,\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma^{\prime})
.

The following two lemmas should be known in quite generality, even our arguments here seems different.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}}\notin\{\chi_{\sigma},\chi^{s}_{\sigma}\}
, then the space
L(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
is zero.

Proof.

If the space
L(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
is not zero, any non-zero map in the space sends
f_{0,\sigma}
to a function of the form
\sum_{k}c_{k}f_{k,\sigma^{\prime}}
. By considering the action of
I_{K}
on both sides, we get a contradiction under the assumption of the Lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\sigma
be a weight such that
\chi_{\sigma}\neq\chi^{s}_{\sigma}
. We have

L(\sigma,\sigma^{s})=0

Proof.

As above, any non-zero
G
-map sends
f_{0,\sigma}
to a function of the form
\sum_{k}c_{k}f_{k,\sigma^{s}}
. By considering the action of
I_{K}
on both sides, we see the sum will only have terms in the positive part, that is of the form
\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}f_{k,\sigma^{s}}
.

Now we apply the operator
S_{K}
to
f_{0,\sigma}
. By our assumption on
\sigma
and
(2)
of Proposition
5.6
, we get
S_{K}f_{0,\sigma}=0
. However, by
(1)
of the same Proposition, we have

S_{K}(\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}f_{k,\sigma^{s}})=\sum_{k\geq 1}c_{k}f_{-k,\sigma^{s}}\neq 0

As any
G
-map respects the action of
S_{K}
, that is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

One may rephrase the Lemma as follows. By Frobenius reciprocity, for a weight
\sigma
satisfying
\chi_{\sigma}\neq\chi^{s}_{\sigma}
the maximal compact induction
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma
does not contain the weight
\sigma^{s}
.

The Lemmas above show that the remaining interesting case is
\{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\}=\{1,st\}
, which we deal with in the following part.

Proposition 3.4
.

We have

(1)
. For a non-zero function
f\in\langle f_{-n}\mid n\geq 1\rangle_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}}
, there exists a non-zero
G
-map
S_{f}
from
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}st
to
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1
, characterized by
S_{f}(f_{0})=f
.

(2)
. The map
f\mapsto S_{f}
from
(1)
gives an isomorphism of vector spaces:

\langle f_{-n}\mid n\geq 1\rangle_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}}\cong\textnormal{Hom}_{G}(\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}st,\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1)
.

Proof.

The argument here is motivated by that of
[
Kis09
, Lemma 1.5.5]
. Suppose we are given a non-zero
f\in\langle f_{-n},n\geq 1\rangle_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}}
. The Iwahori group
I_{K}
acts trivially on
f
(as the character
\chi_{\sigma}=1
), so the representation
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1
contains the trivial character of
I_{K}
. By Frobenius reciprocity, we get a
K
-map
\varphi_{f}
from the finite principal series
\text{Ind}^{K}_{I_{K}}1
to
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1
, sending
1_{I_{K}}
to
f
. We claim that
\varphi_{f}
kills the constant function in
\text{Ind}^{K}_{I_{K}}1
. In other words, we have:

\sum_{k\in K/I_{K}}k\cdot f=0.

Note that the following gives a set of representatives for

:

\{Id\}\cup\{[u]\beta_{K}\mid u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}\}
.

So the above sum
\sum_{k\in K/I_{K}}k\cdot f
reads as

f+\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}}u\beta_{K}\cdot f
.

In our earlier notation, the second part is simply

. As

, we see from

of Proposition
5.6
that

,

whence the claim.

In summary, the
K
-map
\varphi_{f}
factors through
\text{Ind}^{K}_{I_{K}}1/1
, i.e., the Steinberg weight

of

G

\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}st
to
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1
, sending

to

, that is the map

as required.

By revising the argument above and using Proposition

, we see the map

is indeed an isomorphism and we get

.
∎

The following is similar but even easier.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have an isomorphism of spaces

\textnormal{Hom}_{G}(\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1,\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}st)\cong\langle f_{n}+f_{-n}\mid n\geq 1\rangle

Proof.

Frobenius reciprocity says the first space is isomorphic to the space of

gives a basis of the latter space.
∎

4
The universal quotient
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
is not irreducible

For a weight
\sigma
of
K
, the representation
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
is usually called the universal supersingular quotient of
G
. This is because any supersingular representation of
G
is a quotient of
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
for some
\sigma
. We prove in this part that
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T_{\sigma})
is not irreducible in general.

4.1
The degenerate case

We begin with some simple but important observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\textnormal{dim}~\sigma>1
. If
\overline{f_{1}}
generates
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T)
, then
f_{1}
generates
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma
.

Proof.

Suppose
\overline{f_{1}}
generates
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T)
. Then we have

f_{0}\in\langle f_{1}\rangle_{G}+(T)=\langle f_{1}\rangle_{G}+\langle Tf_{0}\rangle_{G}=\langle f_{1}\rangle_{G}+\langle f_{-1}\rangle_{G}=\langle f_{1}\rangle_{G}+\langle S_{K}f_{1}\rangle_{G}=\langle f_{1}\rangle_{G}
.

Here, we have used Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
5.6
for the second and third equality. This shows it generates
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma
, and the argument is done.
∎

Assume
\sigma=1

Proof.

Suppose
f_{0}+f_{1}
generates
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}1/(T+1)

In the case that

Lemma 4.7
.

The representation

has a linear basis given by

Proof.

\beta_{K}f_{1}=\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}+1}/N_{n_{K}+2}}[u\alpha^{-1},v_{0}]

.

The universal quotient
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T)

.

The representation

is by definition equal to

Corollary 4.13
.

The representation
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T)
is not irreducible.

Proof.

By Frobenious reciprocity, there is a non-zero
G
-map from the maximal compact induction
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma^{s}
to
\text{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T)
, sending the function
f_{0,\sigma^{s}}
to
\overline{f_{1,\sigma}}

with

Remark 4.14
.

We deduce a non-zero

-map from
\textnormal{ind}^{G}_{K}\sigma/(T)
to

and an analogous map of the other direction. However, unlike what happens in the case of

for a totally ramified extension

Remark 4.15
.

We expect the main result still holds without any restriction on the group

and the size of the residue field

. However, the strategy we employ seems insufficient to handle the general case not covered by our main result, that is for a weight

would not vanish in any non-supersingular quotient of

5.1

and

In this section, we study some partial linear operators on a smooth representation
\pi
, especially about their certain invariant properties.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of
G
. We define:

S_{K}:\pi^{N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}}\rightarrow\pi^{N_{n_{K}}}
,

v\mapsto\sum_{u\in N_{n_{K}}/N_{n_{K}+1}}~u\beta_{K}v
.

S_{-}:\pi^{N_{n_{K}}}\rightarrow\pi^{N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}}
,

v\mapsto\sum_{u^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}/N^{\prime}_{m_{K}+1}}~u^{\prime}\beta_{K}\alpha^{-1}v

It is simple to check both
S_{K}
and
S_{-}
are well-defined. We summarize the main properties of
S_{K}
and
S_{-}
as follows:

Proposition 5.2
.

We have:

(1)
. Let
h\in H_{1}=I_{1,K}\cap H
. Then
S_{K}(hv)=h^{s}\cdot S_{K}v
, for
v\in\pi^{N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}}
, and
S_{-}(hv)=h^{s}\cdot S_{-}v
, , for
v\in\pi^{N_{n_{K}}}
, where
h^{s}
is short for
\beta_{K}h\beta_{K}
.

(2)
. If
v
is fixed by
I_{1,K}
, the same is true for
S_{K}\cdot v
and
S_{-}\cdot v
.

Proof.

For
(1)
, we note that the group
H_{1}
acts on
\pi^{N_{n_{K}}}
and
\pi^{N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}}
, as it normalizes
N_{n_{K}}
and
N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}
. The statement then follows from the definitions.

For
(2)
, we need some preparation, and we sort them out as two lemmas:

Lemma 5.3
.

For
u^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}_{m_{K}},u\in N_{n_{K}}
, we have:

(1)
. The following identity

u^{\prime}u=u_{1}hu^{\prime}_{1}

holds for a unique
u_{1}\in N_{n_{K}},h\in H_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}\in N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}
.

(2)
. When
u
goes through
N_{n_{K}+l}/N_{n_{K}+m}
, the element
u_{1}
also goes through
N_{n_{K}+l}/N_{n_{K}+m}
, for any
m>l\geq 0
.

Proof.

The uniqueness statement is clear, and only the existence needs to be proved.

Assume
u=n(x_{1},y_{1})\in N,u^{\prime}\in n^{\prime}(x,y)\in N^{\prime}
. Then, if
1+xx_{1}+\overline{yy_{1}}\in E^{\times}
, we have

u^{\prime}u=u_{1}hu^{\prime}_{1}
,

where
hu^{\prime}_{1}
is the following lower triangular matrix:

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1+xx_{1}+\overline{yy_{1}}}&0&0\\
\frac{x-\overline{x_{1}y}}{1+\overline{xx_{1}}+yy_{1}}&\frac{1+xx_{1}+\overline{yy_{1}}}{1+\overline{xx_{1}}+yy_{1}}&0\\
y&yx_{1}-\bar{x}&1+\overline{xx_{1}}+yy_{1}\end{pmatrix},

and
u_{1}=n(x_{2},y_{2})\in N

x_{2},y_{2}
are given by:

x_{2}=\frac{x_{1}-\overline{y_{1}x}}{1+xx_{1}+\overline{yy_{1}}},y_{2}=\frac{y_{1}}{1+\overline{xx_{1}}+yy_{1}}.

Under our assumption that
u^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}
and
u\in N_{n_{K}}
, the condition
1+xx_{1}+\overline{yy_{1}}\in 1+\mathfrak{p}_{E}\subset E^{\times}
holds automatically. The existence is established.

We continue to prove
(2)
. We start by the following observation: from the formula of
y_{2}
given in the argument of
(1)
, we see

y_{2}=y_{1}+
higher valuation terms,

as
u=n(x_{1},y_{1})\in N_{n_{K}},u^{\prime}=n^{\prime}(x,y)\in N^{\prime}_{m_{K}}
. That is to say:

u\in N_{n_{K}+m}\Leftrightarrow u_{1}\in N_{n_{K}+m},\forall m\geq 0
.

Assume now for another

, we have a decomposition
u^{\prime}w=u_{2}b^{\prime\prime}
for
u_{2}\in N_{n_{K}}
and
b^{\prime\prime}\in B^{\prime}
. We have to prove:

u_{2}\in u_{1}N_{n_{K}+m}
implies
w\in uN_{n_{K}+m}
.

Write
u^{-1}_{1}u_{2}
as
u_{3}
. A little algebraic transform gives:

w=u\cdot b^{\prime-1}u_{3}b^{\prime\prime}

We need to check that the element

, denoted by

, lies in

. The element

where the right hand side is a decomposition of

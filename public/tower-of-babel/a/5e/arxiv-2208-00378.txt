On local representation densities of hermitian forms and special cycles II
Sungyoon Cho

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that there are certain relations among representation densities and provide an efficient way to compute representation densities by using these relations. As an application, we compute some arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles on unitary Shimura varieties and propose a conjecture on these.



1.
Introduction

In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, Kudla and Rapoport made a precise conjectural formula for the arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles on unitary Shimura varieties with hyperspecial level structure. This is called the Kudla-Rapoport conjecture. This is proved by Li and Zhang in 2019
[
8
]
. In
[
4
]
, we made a variant of the Kudla-Rapoport conjecture in the case that the unitay Shimura variety has minuscule parahoric level structures. This conjectural formula predicts a relation between the arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles on unitay Rapoport-Zink spaces and the derivative of some representation densities of hermitian forms. To prove this conjecture, one may hope to compute the arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles directly and compare these with the analytic side. However, there is currently no way to compute the geometric side directly except some low dimensional cases. For this reason, we hope to get an idea from the analytic side of the conjecture since it is at least possible to compute.

The analytic side of the Kudla-Rapoport conjecture is related to representation densities of hermitian forms. Therefore, we would like to test our geometric guesses by computing some representation densities. Let
p
be an odd prime and let
F
be an unramified extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with ring of integers
O_{F}
and residue field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
E
be a quadratic unramified extension of
F
with ring of integers
O_{E}
. For
n\times n
hermitian matrices
A
and
B
, we define the representation density
\alpha(A,B)
by

\alpha(A,B)=\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}(q^{-d})^{n(2m-n)}|\{X\in M_{m,n}(O_{E}/\pi^{d}O_{E})|A[X]\equiv B(\Mod\pi^{d})\}|.

We have an explicit formula for these representation densities in
[
5
]
, however, the actual computation is very complicated.
For example, for
A=A_{40}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p^{4}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
and
B=A_{42}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}p^{4}&0\\
0&p^{2}\end{array}\right)
, we used several pages to compute that
\alpha(A_{40},A_{42})=q^{6}(1+\dfrac{1}{q})^{2}
with the above formula. Therefore, we need a more efficient way to compute representation densities.

In the present paper, we prove that there are certain relations among representation densities and by using this, we give an efficient way to compute representation densities. We also apply this method to compute some arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles and then give a conjecture on these.

We now describe our results in more detail. We define the set of hermitian matrices
X_{n}(E)=\{X\in GL_{n}(E)|^{t}X^{*}=X\}
, and
X_{n}(O_{E})=X_{n}(E)\cap M_{n,n}(O_{E})
. Let
\Lambda_{n}^{+}
be the set of
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{n})
such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n}\geq 0
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
, we write
A_{\lambda}
for
\diag(p^{\lambda_{1}},\dots,p^{\lambda_{n}})
. Then, the complete set of representatives of
GL_{n}(O_{E})
-equivalence classes in
X_{n}(O_{E})
is given by
\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}|A_{\lambda}\}
.

Let
\Lambda_{n,s}^{+}
be the subset of
\Lambda_{n}^{+}
such that

\Lambda_{n,s}^{+}=\{\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}\text{ }|\text{ }\xi_{n-s}\geq 1,\xi_{n-s+1}=\dots=\xi_{n}=0\}.

For
\xi\in\Lambda_{n,s}^{+}
, we define
\xi_{a,b}^{+}
as

\xi_{a,b}^{+}:=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n-s},\overset{a}{\overbrace{2,\dots,2}},\overset{b}{\overbrace{1,\dots,1}},\overset{s-a-b}{\overbrace{0,\dots,0}})

For integers
1\leq s\leq n
,
0\leq i\leq s
, we define
d_{n,s,i}
by the coefficients of the polynomial

\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{i=0}^{s}d_{n,s,i}X^{i}\\
\\
=(1-(-q)^{-n}X)(1-(-q)^{-n+1}X)\dots(1-(-q)^{-n+s-1}X)\end{array}

Then, our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Theorem
2.5
)
Let
s
and
n
be integers such that
1\leq s\leq n
, and let
\xi\in\Lambda_{n,s}^{+}
. Then, for any
B\in X_{n}(O_{E})
such that
\pi^{-1}B
is also integral (i.e., the representative of
B
is
A_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}
such that
\lambda\geq(1,\dots,1)
), we have the following equality.

\begin{array}[]{l}{{\sum_{i=0}^{s}d_{n,s,i}\alpha(A_{\xi_{0,i}^{+}},B)-\dfrac{(-1)^{s}}{(-q)^{ns}}\sum_{i=0}^{s}d_{n,s,i}\alpha(A_{\xi_{i,s-i}^{+}},B)=0}}.\end{array}

For example, when
n=2
, the theorem implies that for any
B
such that
\pi^{-1}B
is integral and
\lambda\geq 1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha(A_{\lambda 0},B)=\dfrac{1}{q^{4}}\alpha(A_{\lambda 2},B)\end{array}

Therefore, we can simply compute that

\alpha(A_{40},A_{42})=\dfrac{1}{q^{4}}\alpha(A_{42},A_{42})=q^{4}\alpha(A_{20},A_{20})=q^{4}(1+\dfrac{1}{q})\alpha(A_{2},A_{2})=q^{6}(1+\dfrac{1}{q})^{2}.

Our theorem is true for arbitrary
n
. Therefore, this can be applied to compute any representation densities.

When we apply the theorem to compute representation densities, it is better to use normalized representation densities and a normalized version of the theorem. Also, the Cho-Yamauchi’s formula for the analytic part of the Kudla-Rapoport conjecture (
[
8
, Theorem 3.5.1]
,
[
3
, Theorem 4.7]
) is formulated in terms of normalized representation densities.

For
A,B\in X_{n}(O_{E})
, we define the normalized representation density
A(B)
as

A(B):=\dfrac{\alpha(A,B)}{\alpha(A,A)}.

Then this has the following reductions.

Proposition 1.2
.

(Proposition
2.14
)
For
\xi,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n-1}^{+}
, we consider

in

. Then, we have

A_{\xi,0}(A_{\lambda,0})=A_{\xi}(A_{\lambda}).

Proposition 1.3

For

Then, we have

A_{\xi}(A_{\lambda})=A_{\xi-1}(A_{\lambda-1}).

Also, Theorem

gives the following corollaries when

These corollaries can be used to compute normalized representation densities. The most important advantage of this method is that we can reduce the computations of representation densities for
B
to the ones for
\pi^{-1}B
. For example, consider

. By Corollary

\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{300}(A_{832})&=q^{2}(q+1)A_{311}(A_{832})-q^{5}A_{322}(A_{832})\\

&=q^{4}(q+1)^{2}A_{211}(A_{721})-q^{5}A_{10}(A_{61})\\

&=\{q^{4}(q+1)^{2}-q^{5}\}A_{1}(A_{5})\\
&=q^{6}+q^{5}+q^{4}.\end{array}

Another important advantage of this method is that we do not need to compute all representation densities separately: when we compute a representation density, we can use other representation densities. We refer to Example
3.4
for this.

In Section

Our computational method allows us to test some geometric guesses on the arithmetic intersection numbers of special cycles. In Example

2.1.
Representation densities

In this subsection, we will recall the definition of representation densities and their formulas.

We fix a prime

We define the set of hermitian matrices

\begin{array}[]{l}X_{n}(E)=\{X\in GL_{n}(E)|^{t}X^{*}=X\}.\end{array}

We define

For

Definition 2.1
.

For

\alpha(A,B)=\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}(q^{-d})^{n(2m-n)}|\{X\in M_{m,n}(O_{E}/\pi^{d}O_{E})|A[X]\equiv B(\Mod\pi^{d})\}|.

Now, we recall an explicit formula for the representation densities from
[
5

For each

\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{\mu}:=(\mu_{1}+1,\dots,\mu_{n}+1);\\

\\

For

\lambda

for all

For nonnegative integers

\left[\begin{array}[]{l}u\\

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[

) For

and

Lemma 2.10
.

{\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
l-j\end{array}\middle]\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}l-j\\
l-k\end{array}\middle]=\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
k\end{array}\right]{\prod}_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+(-q)^{l-j}).

Proof.

First, note that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
l-j\end{array}\middle]\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}l-j\\
l-k\end{array}\right]&=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-j}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\\
\\
&\times\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{l-j}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-j}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{l-k}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}&=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{l-k}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{k-j}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}&=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{l-k}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\\
&\times\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{k-j}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}&=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
k\end{array}\middle]\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\end{array}

Therefore, it suffices to show that

(2.2.5)

{\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]={\prod}_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+(-q)^{l-j}).

We prove (
2.2.5
) by induction on
k
.
When
k=1
, we have that

{\sum}_{j=0}^{1}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
j\end{array}\right]=1+(-q)^{l}.

Therefore, (
2.2.5
) is true for
k=1
.
Now, assume that (
2.2.5
) is true for
k
.

Note that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\right]&=\dfrac{\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1-(-q)^{-i})\prod_{i=1}^{k+1-j}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\\
\\
&=\dfrac{(1-(-q)^{-k-1})}{(1-(-q)^{-k-1+j})}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\end{array}

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{l}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]=\dfrac{((-q)^{-k-1+j}-(-q)^{-k-1})}{(1-(-q)^{-k-1+j})}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\\
=(-q)^{-k-1+j}\dfrac{(1-(-q)^{-j})}{(1-(-q)^{-k-1+j})}\dfrac{(1-(-q)^{-k+j-1})}{(1-(-q)^{-j})}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j-1\end{array}\right]\\
=(-q)^{-k-1+j}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j-1\end{array}\right]\end{array}

This implies that

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum}_{j=0}^{k+1}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\middle]-{\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\\
={\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\middle]-\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\}+(-q)^{(2l-k)(k+1)/2}.\end{array}

Since
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
0\end{array}\middle]=\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
0\end{array}\right]=1
, we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\middle]-\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\}+(-q)^{(2l-k)(k+1)/2}\\
={\sum}_{j=1}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\middle]-\middle[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\}+(-q)^{(2l-k)(k+1)/2}\\
={\sum}_{j=1}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}(-q)^{-k-1+j}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j-1\end{array}\right]+(-q)^{(2l-k)(k+1)/2}\\
={\sum}_{j=0}^{k-1}(-q)^{(2l-j)(j+1)/2}(-q)^{-k+j}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]+(-q)^{(2l-k)(k+1)/2}\\
={\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l-j)(j+1)/2}(-q)^{-k+j}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\\
=(-q)^{l-k}{\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k\\
j\end{array}\right]\\
\end{array}

Therefore, we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum}_{j=0}^{k+1}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\

j\end{array}\right].\end{array}

By our inductive hypothesis, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum}_{j=0}^{k+1}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}k+1\\
j\end{array}\right]=(1+(-q)^{l-k}){\prod}_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+(-q)^{l-j}).\end{array}

This finishes the proof of (
2.2.5
) and hence the proof of the lemma.

∎

Lemma 2.11
.

We have that

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum}_{k=0}^{l}(-1)^{k}(-q)^{(l-s)k-k(k+1)/2}{\sum}_{j=0}^{k}(-q)^{(2l+1-j)j/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\

l-k\end{array}\right]\\
\times\{{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+k+i})-(-1)^{s}(-q)^{s(-l+k)}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+i})\}=0.\end{array}

Proof.

By Lemma
2.10
, we need to show that

\begin{array}[]{l}{\sum}_{k=0}^{l}(-1)^{k}(-q)^{(l-s)k-k(k+1)/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
k\end{array}\right]{\prod}_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+(-q)^{l-j}).\\
\times\{{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+k+i})-(-1)^{s}(-q)^{s(-l+k)}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+i})\}=0.\end{array}

Let

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{sk}:=(-1)^{k}(-q)^{(l-s)k-k(k+1)/2}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
k\end{array}\right]{\prod}_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+(-q)^{l-j}).\\
\times\{{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+k+i})-(-1)^{s}(-q)^{s(-l+k)}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+i})\}\end{array}

Since
\left[\begin{array}[]{c}l\\
k\end{array}\right]=\dfrac{\prod_{i=l-k+1}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-i})}
and

we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{sk}&=\dfrac{\prod_{i=l-k+1}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-2i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\\
&\times{{\{}}(-1)^{k}(-q)^{2lk-k^{2}-sk}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+k+i})\\
&-(-1)^{s+k}(-q)^{2lk-k^{2}-sl}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+i}){{\}}}.\end{array}

Now, we want to prove that
\sum_{k=0}^{l}\alpha_{sk}=0
.

Assume that
l<s
. Then,
\alpha_{sk}
is zero for all
k
, and hence
\sum_{k=0}^{l}\alpha_{sk}=0
.

Now, assume that
l\geq s
.

For
0\leq t\leq l
, we write
B_{t,1}
and
B_{t,2}

\begin{array}[]{l}B_{t,1}:=(-1)^{t}(-q)^{2lt-t^{2}-st}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i});\\
B_{t,2}:=-(-1)^{s+t}(-q)^{2lt-t^{2}-sl}{\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+i}).\end{array}

Also, for
0\leq t\leq l
,
0\leq k\leq s-1
, we write
L_{t,k}
and
R_{t,k}
for

\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{t,k}:=&(-1)^{t-k}(-q)^{l(2t-k)-t(t+s-k)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\prod_{i=1}^{s-k-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i}),\\
\\
R_{t,k}:=&(-1)^{t-k}(-q)^{l(2t-k-1)-t(t+s-k-1)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\prod_{i=0}^{s-k-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i}).\\
\end{array}

Here, we choose the following convention. The term
\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})
is
1
if
k=0
, and the term
\prod_{i=1}^{s-k-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i})
is

if

.

Claim 2.12
.

For
0\leq t\leq l
, and
0\leq k\leq s-1
we have the followings.

(1)

\alpha_{s0}=(1-(-q)^{-2l})\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{0,k}

(2)

(3)

For

(4)

R_{t+1,0}+B_{t+1,1}=L_{t+1,0}(1-(-q)^{-2l+2t+2})

(5)

\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{t,k}(1-(-q)^{-t-1})+B_{t+1,1}+B_{t+1,2}=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{t+1,k}(1-(-q)^{-2l+2t+2})

Proof of Claim
2.12
.

(1)

Note that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{s0}&=(1-(-1)^{s}(-q)^{-sl}){\prod}_{i=0}^{s-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+i})\\

This finishes the proof of (1).

(2)

We have that

\begin{array}[]{l}L_{t,s-1}(1-(-q)^{-t-1})+B_{t+1,2}\\

This finishes the proof of (2).

(3)

We have that

\begin{array}[]{l}L_{t,s-r}(1-(-q)^{-t-1})+R_{t+1,s-r+1}\\

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i})(1-(-q)^{-t-1})\\

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\prod_{i=0}^{r-2}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+1+i})\\

\\
=-(-q)^{-t+r-2}(1+(-q)^{-l+t+1})(-1)^{t-s+r}(-q)^{l(2t-s+r)-(t+1)(t+r-1)}\\
(-q)^{l-r+1}(-q)^{-l+r-1}\prod_{i=1}^{s-r}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i})\\

\quad\quad\quad\quad\prod_{i=1}^{s-r}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i})\\
\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{l}=(1-(-q)^{-2l+2t+2})(-1)^{t-s+r}(-q)^{l(2t-s+r+1)-(t+1)(t+r)}\\
\quad\quad\quad\prod_{i=1}^{s-r+1}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})\prod_{i=2}^{r-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i})\\

-(1-(-q)^{-2l+2t+2})(-1)^{t-s+r}(-q)^{l(2t-s+r+1)-(t+1)(t+r)}\\
\quad\quad\quad\prod_{i=1}^{s-r}(1-(-q)^{-l+s-i})\prod_{i=2}^{r-1}(1-(-q)^{-l+t+i})\\

\\
+(-1)^{t-s+r+1}(-q)^{l(2t-s+r+1)-(t+1)(t+r)}\\

(4)

(5)

This is obvious from (1), (2), (3), and (4).

∎

Now, by Claim
2.12
(1), we have that
\alpha_{s0}=(1-(-q)^{-2l})\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{0,k}
. Also, by definition, we have that

(5) we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{s0}+\alpha_{s1}&=\dfrac{(1-(-q)^{-2l})}{(1-(-q)^{-1})}\{\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{0,k}(1-(-q)^{-1})+B_{1,1}+B_{1,2}\}\\
\\
&=\dfrac{(1-(-q)^{-2l})(1-(-q)^{-2l+2})}{(1-(-q)^{-1})}\{\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{1,k}\}\end{array}

Now, we claim that for any

,

(2.2.6)

\sum_{i=0}^{t}\alpha_{si}=\dfrac{\prod_{i=0}^{t}(1-(-q)^{-2l+2i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{t,k}.

We prove this by induction on

. Assume that (
2.2.6
) is true for
t
, then we have

\sum_{i=0}^{t+1}\alpha_{si}=\dfrac{\prod_{i=0}^{t}(1-(-q)^{-2l+2i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t+1}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\{\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{t,k}(1-(-q)^{-t-1})+B_{t+1,1}+B_{t+1,2}\}.

By Claim
2.12
(5), we have

\sum_{i=0}^{t+1}\alpha_{si}=\dfrac{\prod_{i=0}^{t+1}(1-(-q)^{-2l+2i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{t+1}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\{\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{t+1,k}\}.

This finishes the proof of(
2.2.6
).

Therefore, we see that

\sum_{i=0}^{l}\alpha_{si}=\dfrac{\prod_{i=0}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-2l+2i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}(1-(-q)^{-i})}\{\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}L_{l,k}\}=0,

and this completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem 2.5.

This follows from (

) and Lemma

2.3.

When we apply Theorem

and compute some representation densities.

Definition 2.13
.

For
A,B\in X_{n}(O_{E})
, we define
A(B)
by

A(B):=\dfrac{\alpha(A,B)}{\alpha(A,A)}.

These normalized representation densities have the following reduction properties.

Proposition 2.14
.

For
\xi,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n-1}^{+}
, we consider
(\xi,0),(\lambda,0)
in
\Lambda_{n}^{+}
. Then, we have

A_{\xi,0}(A_{\lambda,0})=A_{\xi}(A_{\lambda}).

Proof.

By
[
6
, Corollary 9.12]
, we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha(A_{\xi,0},A_{\lambda,0})=\alpha(A_{\xi,0},A_{0})\alpha(A_{\xi},A_{\lambda}),\\
\alpha(A_{\xi,0},A_{\xi,0})=\alpha(A_{\xi,0},A_{0})\alpha(A_{\xi},A_{\xi}).\end{array}

Therefore, we have

A_{\xi,0}(A_{\lambda,0})=A_{\xi}(A_{\lambda}).

∎

Proposition 2.15
.

For
\xi,\lambda
in
\Lambda_{n}^{+}
, such that
\xi,\lambda\geq(1,1\dots,1)
, we consider

\begin{array}[]{l}\xi-1:=(\xi_{1}-1,\dots,\xi_{n}-1)\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}\\
\lambda-1:=(\lambda_{1}-1,\dots,\lambda_{n}-1)\in\Lambda_{n}^{+}.\end{array}

Then, we have

A_{\xi}(A_{\lambda})=A_{\xi-1}(A_{\lambda-1}).

Proof.

This follows directly from the fact that

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha(A_{\xi},A_{\xi})=q^{n^{2}}\alpha(A_{\xi-1},A_{\xi-1}),\\
\alpha(A_{\xi},A_{\lambda})=q^{n^{2}}\alpha(A_{\xi-1},A_{\lambda-1})\end{array}

∎

.

(20)

A_{0000}(B)=qA_{1100}(B)-(q^{6}-1)A_{1111}(B)-qA_{2211}(B)+q^{6}A_{2222}(B)

=(q+1)(q^{3}+1)A_{1111}(B)-q(q+1)A_{2211}(B)+q^{6}A_{2222}(B)
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
2.22
and Theorem
2.5
.
∎

Remark 2.24
.

The most important advantage of this Corollary
2.23
is that we can reduce the computation of representation densities for
B
to the ones for
\pi^{-1}B
. For example, to compute

, we only need to compute
(q^{3}+1)A_{1000}(\pi^{-1}B)-q^{3}A_{1110}(\pi^{-1}B)

Let

∗

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\overline{S}}}

Example 3.3

[

(3.2.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle{\mathcal{Z}}(x),{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\rangle-\langle{\mathcal{Z}}(\dfrac{x}{\pi}),{\mathcal{Z}}(\dfrac{y}{\pi})\rangle\\

\begin{array}[]{l}=-(q-1)A_{11}(x,y)-(q^{2}-1){\sum}_{\lambda,\kappa\geq 2}A_{\lambda\kappa}(x,y)+{\sum}_{\lambda\geq 2}A_{\lambda 1}(x,y)+{\sum}_{\lambda\geq 2}A_{\lambda 0}(x,y)\\

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle{\mathcal{Z}}(x),{\mathcal{Z}}(y)\rangle-\langle{\mathcal{Z}}(\dfrac{x}{\pi}),{\mathcal{Z}}(\dfrac{y}{\pi})\rangle\\

\begin{array}[]{l}-qA_{11}(x,y)+A_{00}(x,\dfrac{y}{\pi})-qA_{11}(x,\dfrac{y}{\pi})+A_{22}(x,y)\\

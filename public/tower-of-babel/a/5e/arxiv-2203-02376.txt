Abstract

We present a systematic organization of functions and
operators on the fuzzy 2-sphere in terms of string modes,
which are optimally localized in position and momentum space.
This allows to separate the semi-classical and the deep quantum regime
of non-commutative quantum field theory, and exhibits its nonlocal nature.
This organization greatly simplifies the computation
of loop contributions, avoiding oscillatory integrals and
providing the effective action directly in position space.
UV/IR mixing is understood as nonlocality arising from long string modes
in the loops. The method is suited for any quantized symplectic space.

1
Introduction

It is expected on general grounds that the incorporation of gravity into a quantum theory of fundamental interactions will imply some sort of quantum structure of space-time. One approach towards this issue is provided by matrix models, some of which are closely related to string theory
[
1
,
2
]
. In this approach, quantum spaces appear naturally as quantized symplectic spaces, which carry a non-commutative gauge theory arising from the fluctuations within the matrix models; see e.g.
[
3
]
for a review and further literature. Much work has been devoted towards an understanding of these models at the quantum level, which is highly non-trivial due to the UV/IR mixing
[
4
]
. This phenomenon appears quite universally on any non-commutative geometry and
leads to novel types of infrared divergences linked to
ultraviolet divergences in the loops, without any analog in orthodox quantum field theory.

An appropriate and intuitive understanding of this phenomenon is obtained upon realizing that the UV sector of non-commutative fields is completely non-local, and better described by
bi-local string modes which have no analog
in conventional field theory. These modes have the structure

|x\rangle\langle y|
where
|x\rangle,|y\rangle
are coherent states on the quantum space, and behave like open strings connecting
x
and
y

[
5
,
6
]
. Most importantly, they are approximate eigenstates of the (matrix) Laplacian which governs the kinematics of the model,
which allows to compute loop integrals much more efficiently than using the more traditional group-theoretical modes.
It is then easy to recognize that the UV/IR mixing at one loop is a reflection of a simple non-local term in the effective action, which can be computed in few lines for rather generic
quantum spaces
[
5
]
. This also exhibits the pathological nature of generic non-commutative field theories in dimensions larger than 2, and the mild form of UV/IR mixing in the maximally supersymmetrix IKKT model which leads to IIB supergravity interactions in target space
[
5
]
.

In the present paper, we study the properties of string modes in more detail, focusing on the 2-dimensional fuzzy sphere
S^{2}_{N}
.
We provide careful justifications and qualifications for the
approximations which are used in the field-theoretical computations put forward in
[
5
]
. In particular, we derive explicitly the string representation of the propagator, and discuss its properties.
Even though this derivation is specific to the fuzzy sphere, the properties obtained are expected to hold much more generally, and we provide general regularity estimates for string symbols of the propagator.
These results provide a solid basis for future applications of the method in a more general context.
The limitation to
S^{2}_{N}
is mainly to simplify the presentation, and most results should generalize in a straightforward way.

The importance of efficient methods for field theory computations
is hard to overstate. On non-commutative spaces, the only way to proceed so far has been the use of group-theoretical eigenmodes,
i.e. plane waves on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}_{\theta}
and (generalized spherical) harmonics on quantized coadjoint orbits.
In the latter case, this makes the computation of any non-trivial
diagrams beyond the most basic ones extremely hard, and completely intransparent. Even on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}_{\theta}
, the presence of oscillatory integrals in non-planar diagrams is a major problem and also obscures the underlying simple non-local structure. These calculations become much more accessible using the method of string modes.
We argue that computations in noncommutative field theory (NCFT) quite generally decompose into semi-classical contributions with an effective UV cutoff provided by the scale of noncommutativity, and a stringy contribution which is easily computable using the novel methods. For sufficiently large mass, the latter regime is dominant, and is analytically accessible.
In particular, we introduce reduced Feynman rules, which allows to compute and estimate
loop computations in scalar NCFT in a remarkably simple way.

There is another motivation for developing these novel tools: to compute the quantum effective action for the
maximally supersymmetric models related to string theory
[
1
,
2
]
. In these models, the non-local contributions
due to the long string modes are suppressed by SUSY, and the contributions of the short string modes provide a novel and useful tool to compute e.g. the one-loop effective action on non-trivial backgrounds in position space. Such a computation has been given recently in
[
7
]
, and the present paper provides some further background.

This paper is organized as follows.
After a review of
S^{2}_{N}
emphasizing its distinct regimes, we introduce the string symbol of functions in section
2.2
, and establish regularity properties in the UV regime.
In section
3
we introduce the string or crossed representation of operators, and derive an explicit formula. This is then applied to the propagator, which
allows to understand its local and non-local features. A general (off-diagonal) string representation of operators is also defined, which is shown to be regular and non-oscillatory in the UV. This is checked numerically for the propagator,
confirming the non-oscillating behavior.
These results are applied to NCFT in section
4
.

2.2
String modes and semi-classical representation of functions

The oscillating behavior of the coherent state representation in the UV

\displaystyle\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x\\

\displaystyle:=\psi_{x,y}:=|x\rangle\langle y|\qquad\in\mathrm{End}({\cal H})

\displaystyle\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\

A first application of string modes is to obtain a representation of any

\displaystyle{\cal O}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\dim{\cal H})^{2}}{(\rm VolS^{2})^{2}}\!\!\int\limits_{S^{2}\times S^{2}}\!\!\!\!dxdy\,{\cal O}(x,y)|x\rangle\langle y|\ ,

However, it is essentially unique if we require

This property will be extremely useful in NCFT, because

\displaystyle\|{\cal O}\|_{\rm HS}^{2}={\rm Tr}({\cal O}^{\dagger}{\cal O})=1\ ,

\displaystyle\boxed{\ |\nabla{\cal O}(x,y)|\leq\frac{1}{L_{\rm NC}}\ .\ }

(2.49)

To show this,
we first observe that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mu}|x\rangle\|^{2}=\big(\partial_{\mu}\langle x|\big)\partial_{\mu}|x\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}\langle x|y\rangle\big|_{y=0}=\frac{1}{L_{\rm NC}^{2}}\ ,

using the explicit form of

\displaystyle|\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}{\cal O}(x,y)|=|\langle x|{\cal O}\partial_{\mu}|y\rangle|\leq\|{\cal O}^{\dagger}|x\rangle\|\|\partial_{\mu}|y\rangle\|\leq\frac{1}{L_{\rm NC}}\ ,

\displaystyle\|{\cal O}^{\dagger}|x\rangle\|^{2}=\langle x|{\cal O}{\cal O}^{\dagger}|x\rangle\leq\|{\cal O}\|_{\rm HS}^{2}=1\ ,

\displaystyle\big|\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}\langle x|y\rangle\big|\lesssim\frac{1}{L_{\rm NC}}\ .

(2.53)

The reason for this mild behavior of the string symbol is of course

This may seem inconsistent with the fact that the diagonal symbol

In this sense, the extreme UV wavelengths are indeed smoothed out on
quantum spaces, however at the expense of long-range non-locality
mediated by the string modes.

To proceed,

The

and linear momentum determined by

On the other hand, the
long string modes
for
|x-y|>L_{\rm NC}

are completely non-local and non-commutative.
They provide the appropriate description of the UV or deep quantum
regime of NCFT.

2.3
Short string modes as localized wave-packets on
S^{2}

Consider first the semi-classical regime and its description in terms of

We claim that the following identification

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}y+\frac{k}{2}\\

on

defines an isometry from short string modes on

\displaystyle\psi_{\tilde{k};y}(x)

(2.56)

To justify this identification

8

8

On the Moyal-Weyl quantum plane

A simple Gaussian integration gives

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\tilde{k};a},\psi_{\tilde{l};b}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{i}{2}(\tilde{l}-\tilde{k})(a+b)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|a-b|_{g}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}|k-l|_{g}^{2}}\ ,

which is consistent with the inner product (

\displaystyle\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}a+\frac{k}{2}\\

\displaystyle\approx\ e^{-\frac{1}{4}|a-b+\frac{1}{2}(k-l)|_{g}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}|a-b-\frac{1}{2}(k-l)|_{g}^{2}}\ e^{i\varphi}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}|a-b|_{g}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}|k-l|_{g}^{2}}\,e^{i\varphi}\ .

(2.62)

The phase factor is recovered in the gauge (

\varphi
is the difference of
the symplectic triangles
(a+\frac{k}{2},b+\frac{l}{2},0)

and

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{1}{2}(a+\frac{k}{2})(\tilde{b}+\frac{\tilde{l}}{2})-\frac{1}{2}(a-\frac{k}{2})(\tilde{b}-\frac{\tilde{l}}{2})=\frac{1}{2}(a\tilde{l}-b\tilde{k})\ .

(2.63)

Taking into account the phase factor

in (

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{1}{2}(a\tilde{l}-b\tilde{k}-a\tilde{k}+b\tilde{l})=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)(\tilde{l}-\tilde{k})\ ,

consistent with (

The momentum assignment will be clarified further

.

\displaystyle\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\

differs slightly from the isometric identification (

).
This indicates that the short string states are outside of (but bordering on) the
semi-classical regime, which

\displaystyle|x\rangle\langle y||y\rangle\langle z|=|x\rangle\langle z|\

(2.69)

which are not reproduced by their symbols

9

Of course this could be reconciled in terms of a star product on the classical space of functions.
Here we simply wish to point out that they are not quite in the semi-classical regime.
.
The reason is that the size of the wave-packet coincides with the uncertainty scale
L_{\rm NC}

Semi-classical wavefunctions should accordingly be realized
as superpositions of short string states such as

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}^{(L)}_{\tilde{k};y}

\displaystyle\sim\int\limits_{S^{2}}dz\,e^{-|y-z|^{2}/L^{2}}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}z+\frac{k}{2}\\
z-\frac{k}{2}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)\ ,

corresponding to a Gaussian wave packet of size

\tilde{k}

On the other hand, the short string modes with

Fuzzy delta-function as short string mode.

Consider the string mode

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{p}:=\left|{}^{p}_{p}\right)=\sum_{l}a_{l}\,\hat{Y}^{l}_{0}

(2.72)

at the north pole
p\in S^{2}
. According to the above discussion, this should be
interpreted as optimally localized wavepacket at
p
without linear momentum,
i.e. as fuzzy delta-function at
p
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\big(\hat{\delta}_{p}\hat{Y}^{l}_{m}\big)=\langle p|\hat{Y}^{l}_{m}|p\rangle=\frac{1}{c_{l}}Y^{l}_{m}(p)

(2.73)

using (
2.27
), and similarly for any point on
S^{2}
.
The coefficients

are obtained using (

)

\displaystyle a_{l}

\displaystyle=\langle p|\hat{Y}^{l}_{0}|p\rangle=\frac{1}{c_{l}}\,Y^{l}_{0}(p)=\frac{1}{c_{l}}\,\sqrt{\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}}\ \sim\ \sqrt{\frac{2l+1}{N}}e^{-\frac{l^{2}}{2N}}\ .

(2.74)

As a consistency check, we compute

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(\hat{\delta}_{p}\hat{\delta}_{p})=\sum_{l}a_{l}^{2}=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi c_{l}^{2}}\sim\frac{2N^{2}}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dx\frac{x}{c_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle=2N\int_{0}^{1}dx\,xe^{-Nx^{2}}=1\ ,

(2.75)

using the asymptotic formula (
2.35

Note that the function

provides a cutoff at

,
so that only modes in the IR sector

contribute, as expected.
In particular, the optimally localized fuzzy
(delta-like) function at the north pole is

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{p}=\left|{}^{p}_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{N-1}\,\sqrt{\frac{2l+1}{N}}e^{-\frac{l^{2}}{2N}}\hat{Y}^{l}_{0}\ \approx\ \sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\sqrt{\frac{2l+1}{N}}\,\hat{Y}^{l}_{0}\ ,

(2.76)

with classical counterpart

\displaystyle\delta_{p,N}(x)

\displaystyle:=\ \sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\sqrt{\frac{2l+1}{N}}\,Y^{l}_{0}(\vartheta,\varphi)\approx\sqrt{N}e^{-Nx^{2}}\ .

(2.77)

This is indeed the truncation of the exact delta-function on
S^{2}

at
{l=l_{\rm NC}=\sqrt{N}}

Fundamental solution on
S^{2}_{N}
.

In particular, the fuzzy analog of the fundamental solution of
(\Box+m^{2})

centered at the north pole is

\displaystyle{\cal G}_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Box+m^{2}}\left|{}^{p}_{p}\right):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\sqrt{2l+1}}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\hat{Y}^{l}_{0}\ ,

(2.78)

which satisfies

\displaystyle(\Box+m^{2}){\cal G}_{p}

\displaystyle=\hat{\delta}_{p}\ \qquad\in\mathrm{End}({\cal H})\ .

(2.79)

We can construct the corresponding fuzzy 2-point function
G_{N}(x,y)
as follows. We first compute

\displaystyle G_{N}(x,p):=\left({}^{x}_{x}\right|\frac{1}{\Box+m^{2}}\left|{}^{p}_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\sqrt{2l+1}}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\frac{1}{c_{l}}Y^{l}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\frac{2l+1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)\ .

(2.80)

Thanks to the symmetry of the sphere, the general formula will have the same form, with
\vartheta
understood as the angle between the points
x
and
y

x
. Thus

\displaystyle G_{N}(x,y):=\left({}^{x}_{x}\right|\frac{1}{\Box+m^{2}}\left|{}^{y}_{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\frac{2l+1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)\

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{N}\ln(|x-y|^{2})\ ,\qquad m^{2}\ll l^{2}_{\rm NC}

(2.81)

which is a regularization of the fundamental solution
for distances greater than
L_{NC}
.
This becomes more transparent
noting that

\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|
amounts to the equivariant symbol map (
2.25

\displaystyle(\Box_{x}+m^{2})G_{N}(x,y)=(\Box_{x}+m^{2})\left({}^{x}_{x}\right|\frac{1}{\Box+m^{2}}\left|{}^{y}_{y}\right)

\displaystyle=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
y\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)=\frac{4\pi}{N}\delta_{N}(x,y)\ .

In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\left({}^{p}_{p}\right|\frac{1}{\Box+m^{2}}\left|{}^{p}_{p}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l=0}^{\sqrt{N}}\frac{2l+1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{N}\ln(N)

(2.86)

as long as

2.81
) for

Note that the large

2.4
Kinematical properties of string modes

In the context of matrix models, differential operators are realized in terms of
commutators i.e. derivations.
For the fuzzy sphere, consider
the following derivative operators acting on
\mathrm{End}({\cal H})
:

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{a}\,\Phi

\displaystyle:=[X^{a},\Phi],\qquad\Phi\in\mathrm{End}({\cal H})

\displaystyle\Box\,\Phi

\displaystyle:=R_{N}^{2}\,{\cal P}^{a}{\cal P}_{a}\Phi\ ,

(2.87)

where the factor
R_{N}^{2}
is inserted for consistency with (
2.14
).
These can be viewed as quantized differential operators on
S^{2}
.
If
\Phi
is a (quantized) function on
S^{2}
in the semi-classical regime
i.e. with wavelength greater than
L_{\rm NC}
, then
{\cal P}^{a}\sim i\{x^{a},.\}
is adequately interpreted as derivative operator:

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{a}\,\Phi

\displaystyle=[X^{a},\Phi]=i\theta^{a\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Phi,\qquad\theta^{a\mu}=\{x^{a},y^{\mu}\}

(2.88)

in local coordinates
y^{\mu}
.
However if
\Phi
is outside of the semi-classical regime,

{\cal P}^{a}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{End}({\cal H}))

should be viewed as a
non-local
operator.
This non-locality plays a crucial role in NCFT, due to virtual modes propagating in the loops. It is manifest
by considering their matrix elements w.r.t. string modes

\displaystyle\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|{\cal P}^{a}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)={\bf x^{a}}(x)-{\bf x^{a}}(y)\approx x^{a}-y^{a}\ .

Hence the string modes
\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)
have “matrix momentum”

{\cal P}=x-y
, which confirms the
identifcation (
2.3
) with semi-classical
wavepackets on the tangent space
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\theta}
with wave-number
\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}
.
The general matrix elements

\displaystyle\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|{\cal P}^{a}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)

\displaystyle\approx({\bf x^{a}}(x)-{\bf x^{a}}(y))\langle x|x^{\prime}\rangle\langle y^{\prime}|y\rangle

are approximately diagonal.
This confirms previous observations
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
in noncommutative field theory,
which now acquire a precise mathematical realization.

Similarly for the Laplacian, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{N}^{-2}\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|\Box\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|{\cal P}^{a}{\cal P}_{a}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)

\displaystyle=\langle x|X^{a}X_{a}|x^{\prime}\rangle\langle y^{\prime}|y\rangle+\langle x|x^{\prime}\rangle\langle y^{\prime}|X^{a}X_{a}|y\rangle-2\langle x|X^{a}|x^{\prime}\rangle\langle y^{\prime}|X_{a}|y\rangle

\displaystyle\approx E_{xy}\,\langle x|x^{\prime}\rangle\langle y^{\prime}|y\rangle\

(2.105)

to a very good approximation, where

\displaystyle E_{xy}

\displaystyle=(\vec{\bf x}(x)-\vec{\bf x}(y))^{2}+2L_{\rm NC}^{2}

(2.106)

is the energy of a string mode, which is given by its length square
plus the intrinsic quantum length scale.

The propagator.

Since the Laplacian is almost diagonal on the string modes, we can expect the following approximate formula for the propagator
in the string basis

\displaystyle\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|(\Box+m^{2})^{-1}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\prime}\\
y^{\prime}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)\approx\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G_{\Lambda}(x,x^{\prime})&\quad x=y\neq x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\\[4.30554pt]
\tilde{G}(x,y)&\quad x=x^{\prime}\neq y=y^{\prime}\\[4.30554pt]
0&\quad\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\ .

Here

\displaystyle G_{\Lambda}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle:=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|(\Box+m^{2})^{-1}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\prime}\\
x^{\prime}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)\approx\frac{4\pi}{N}G(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle(\Box+m^{2})G_{\Lambda}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x^{\prime}\\
x^{\prime}\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)=\frac{4\pi}{N}\delta_{N}(x,x^{\prime})

is a regularization of the
commutative
Greens function
G(x,y)

for
\Box+m^{2}
with UV cutoff
\Lambda
, while

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|(\Box+m^{2})^{-1}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
y\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)\approx\frac{1}{R_{N}^{2}|x-y|^{2}+m^{2}}\

is the UV contribution to the propagator which arises from the string modes (
3.2.1
).
Note that both are well-defined function, in contrast to the classical Greens function
G(x,y)
which is a distribution.
These two functions should merge at coincident points,

\displaystyle G_{\Lambda}(x,x)\approx\tilde{G}(x,x)\approx\frac{1}{m^{2}+N}\ .

(2.126)

This is consistent with (
2.86
)
up to a factor
\ln(N)
, which is missed by the string approximation.
This supports the validity of the approximation (
2.4
)
in the nonlocal regime
|x-y|>L_{NC}
.
The following section is devoted to refinement and justification the string representation
of the propagator.

3
Operators on
S^{2}_{N}
and string representations

In analogy to the coherent state realization of functions
{\Phi\in\mathrm{End}({\cal H})}

discussed above,
operators
{\cal O}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{End}({\cal H}))

on fuzzy spaces – such as the propagator – can be realized in terms of string modes.
However, there are now two very different possibilities: First, there is a ”local“ realization in terms of local string modes,
which is analogous to the standard representation of operators via an
integral kernel.
Second, there is an entirely new representation
in terms of non-local string modes, which can be viewed as a crossed version
of the local one. This version has no classical analog, and it will
be extremely useful for the propagator and loop computations in NCFT.
This is the main focus of the present paper.

Moreover in both cases, one should distinguish between
a minimal, ”diagonal“ realization which is in general highly oscillatory,
and an off-diagonal representation
which is typically much better behaved, notably for the propagator.

3.1
Operator kernel and local representation

We start with the representation of operators which corresponds to the classical
integral kernel of operators.
Consider an operator on fuzzy
S^{2}_{N}

\displaystyle{\cal O}:\quad\mathrm{End}({\cal H})\to\mathrm{End}({\cal H})\ ,

(3.1)

or equivalently
{{\cal O}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{End}({\cal H}))}
.
We can decompose
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{End}({\cal H}))
as
SO(3)_{L}\times SO(3)_{R}
module as

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{End}({\cal H}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{End}({\cal H}_{L})\otimes\mathrm{End}({\cal H}_{R})^{*}

\displaystyle\cong((N)_{L}\otimes(N^{*})_{L})\otimes((N)_{R}\otimes(N)_{R}^{*})

\displaystyle\cong(\oplus_{l}(l)_{L})\otimes(\oplus_{l}(l)_{R})

(3.2)

into irreps of
SO(3)_{L}\times SO(3)_{R}
. This is isomorphic to the decomposition of
the space of functions on
S^{2}_{L}\times S^{2}_{R}
under
SO(3)_{L}\times SO(3)_{R}

\displaystyle L^{2}(S^{2}_{L}\times S^{2}_{R})

\displaystyle\cong(\oplus_{l}(l)_{L})\otimes(\oplus_{l}(l)_{R})\ ,

(3.3)

truncated at
l_{\rm max}
. Therefore there is a unique intertwiner which respects the normalization given by the integral and trace, respectively.
Relaxing the isometry requirement, we can define such an intertwiner
analogous to the coherent state quantization (
2.24
) as
follows

\displaystyle\boxed{\ \begin{aligned} {\cal K}:\quad L^{2}(S_{L}^{2}\times S_{R}^{2})&\to End(End({\cal H}))\\
k(x,y)&\mapsto\int\limits_{S^{2}\times S^{2}}dxdy\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)k(x,y)\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
y\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|\ .\end{aligned}\ }

(3.4)

This map is surjective because (
2.24
) is,
and we denote the function
k(x,y)
as

local kernel
of
{\cal O}
.
Since
\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)
is the noncommutative analog of a delta-function,

k(x,y)
is analogous to the standard integral kernel of an operator.
Conversely, consider the
following expectation value of operators for local string modes
at different locations:

\displaystyle\boxed{\ {\cal O}_{N}(x,y):=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|{\cal O}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
y\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)\ .\ }

In view of the inner products (
2.2
),
this provides an approximate inverse to the kernel map (
3.4
),

\displaystyle{\cal K}\big({\cal O}_{N}(x,y)\big)\approx{\cal O}\ ,\qquad\big({\cal K}(k(x,y))\big)_{N}\approx k(x,y)

(3.9)

provided (!) the functions are sufficiently smooth, i.e. not oscillating at scales
shorter than
L_{\rm NC}
. Otherwise, this representation of
{\cal O}
is
exact but misleading.
For example, the propagator is represented by the kernel
k(x,y)
as follows

\displaystyle(\Box+m^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum\frac{1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\hat{Y}^{l}_{m}\otimes\hat{Y}^{l\dagger}_{m}

\displaystyle={\cal K}\Big(\sum\frac{1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}c_{l}^{2}Y^{l}_{m}(x)Y^{l*}_{m}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle={\cal K}\Big(\sum\frac{1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}c_{l}^{2}P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)\Big)

\displaystyle=:{\cal K}(k(x,y))\ ,

(3.10)

where
x\cdot y=\cos\vartheta
,
using the spherical harmonics addition theorem (
A.1
).
Even though this formula is exact, there is a problem, because
the
c_{l}
blow up for

l\gg l_{\rm NC}
(
2.35
). Therefore
k(x,y)
is highly
oscillatory, and very different from its classical cousin

\displaystyle G(x,y)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)\ ,

(3.11)

which is the fundamental solution of

\displaystyle(\Box_{x}+m^{2})G(x,y)

\displaystyle=\delta_{y}(x)\ .

(3.12)

In contrast,
the desired UV truncation
is recovered by the local string symbol:

\displaystyle G_{N}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\big({\scriptsize\mkern-6.0mu\begin{array}[]{c}x\\
x\end{array}\mkern-6.0mu}\big|\frac{1}{\Box+m^{2}}\big|\mkern-6.0mu{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}y\\
y\end{array}}\mkern-6.0mu\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\frac{1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}\frac{1}{c_{l}^{2}}Y^{l}_{m}(x)Y^{l*}_{m}(y)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{l_{\rm NC}}\frac{2l+1}{l(l+1)+m^{2}}P_{l}(\cos\vartheta)\

(3.17)

for
m^{2}\ll l_{\rm NC}^{2}
in agreement with (
2.81
),
using (
2.27
) and the spherical harmonics addition theorem (
A.1
).
We will see that the symbols are typically better behaved in the UV
than the operator kernel.
As noted in the previous section, the large
N
limit of fuzzy fundamental solution
G_{N}(x,y)
differs from the classical
G(x,y)
by a factor

\displaystyle G_{N}(x,y)\to\frac{4\pi}{N}G(x,y)\ .

(3.18)

The above example of the propagator illustrates the non-classical nature of
the local operator kernel in the UV regime:
Even though this representation always exists, it may be highly
singular. This is reminiscent of the
coherent state quantization map discussed in section
2.1
, and
we will encounter
a similar phenomenon in section
3.2.1
. This singular behavior will be resolved in section
3.3
, similarly to the
IR representation of functions in (
2.46
).

3.2
String kernel, string representation and crossing

Now we discuss a different representation of operators in terms of
non-local string modes, which is particularly useful in the UV regime.
This is a crossed version of the representation discussed in the previous
section. For some operators such as the propagator, this
turns out again to be singular, which will be cured in section
3.3
.

The key to the string representation of operators is to consider a different
action of
SO(3)_{L}\times SO(3)_{R}
in the identification (
3.2
):

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{End}({\cal H}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{End}({\cal H})\otimes\mathrm{End}({\cal H})^{*}

\displaystyle\cong((N)_{L}\otimes(N^{*})_{R})\otimes((N)_{R}\otimes(N)_{L}^{*})\ ,

(3.19)

where
SO(3)_{L}\times SO(3)_{R}
acts as indicated.
Using the coherent state representation (
2.24
) of
\mathrm{End}({\cal H})
, we can again identify this decomposition
with functions on
S^{2}\times S^{2}

4
Loop computations and (non)locality in NCFT

After the above discussion and construction of the string representation of the operators on the fuzzy sphere, most importantly the propagator, we are in a prime position to discuss the formulation of the scalar field theory on the fuzzy sphere. This is the simplest non-trivial setting, in which these notions apply and yield some important results.

The real scalar field theory on the fuzzy sphere is given by a suitable adaption of the standard commutative action for the scalar field

\displaystyle S=\int dx\,\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi(x)\Box\phi(x)+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi(x)^{2}+\frac{g}{4!}\phi(x)^{4}\right)\ ,

(4.1)

where we have chosen the quartic interaction. In the
fuzzy case, the real function
\phi
is replaced by a hermitian matrix
\Phi\in\mathrm{End}({\cal H})
, the integral is an appropriately rescaled trace, and the Laplacian is the double commutator with the three
SU(2)
generators defining the sphere (
2.4
). Then the fuzzy action is given by

\displaystyle S=\frac{4\pi}{N}{\rm tr}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Phi[J^{a}_{(N)},[J_{a(N)},\Phi]]+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\Phi^{2}+\frac{g}{4!}\Phi^{4}\right)\ .

(4.2)

The quantization of this model is defined in terms of a matrix ”path” integral over
the space of hermitian matrices

\displaystyle\Phi=(\phi^{i}_{j})\quad\in{\cal A}:=\mathrm{End}({\cal H})\ ,

(4.3)

denoting the algebra of functions on the fuzzy sphere with
{\cal A}
for better readability.
Then
the correlators or

-point functions are obtained as usual from a generating function

\displaystyle Z[{\cal J}]=\int_{\cal A}D\Phi\,e^{-S[\Phi]+{\rm tr}\Phi{\cal J}}=e^{-W[{\cal J}]}\ .

(4.4)

The perturbative
expansion of a correlator is given by the sum of contractions of the interaction vertices with the propagator, which is viewed more abstractly as an element in
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal A}^{*}\cong End({\cal A})

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{i}_{j}\,{\phi^{*}}^{k}_{l}\rangle_{0}\quad\in{\cal A}\otimes{\cal A}^{*}

(4.5)

represented by a double line
starting at
({}^{k}_{l})
and ending at
({}^{i}_{j})
.
The quartic product in the interaction is however no longer invariant under arbitrary permutations of the external legs, but only under cyclic permutations.
It is well-known that this leads to ribbon (Feynman) diagrams, with propagators represented by double lines. The distinction between planar and nonplanar diagrams and their phase factors then leads to the well known phenomenon of the UV/IR mixing
[
4
]
.

The conventional way to evaluate these diagrams
is to express the field in terms of the basis
\hat{Y}^{l}_{m}
(
2.8

The explicit computations using this approach however become very difficult beyond one-loop, since they involve complicated group theoretical factors. Here, we will evaluate these diagrams in a different way in terms of string modes and the string symbol of the propagator.
This is achieved by observing that any ribbon diagram is defined in a basis-independent way as a canonical contraction of propagators (
4.5
) with vertices, which can be viewed as element in
{\cal A}^{\otimes 4}
(using the canonical identification
{\cal A}\cong{\cal A}^{*}
given by the trace).
Representing the propagator in terms of its

As expected, this diagram has no internal variables to sum over. We also see that the non-planar structure of the diagram is manifest in the different structure of the contractions of the external labels of the diagram.

Together, these two diagrams yield a contribution to the one loop effective action, with graph combinatorial factors taken into account:

\displaystyle\frac{g}{3}\left(\frac{N}{4\pi}\right)^{4}\int

\displaystyle dx_{1}dy_{1}dx_{2}dy_{2}\,\phi(x_{1},y_{1})\phi(x_{2},y_{2})\times

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\left\langle y_{2}|x_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle y_{1}|x_{2}\right\rangle\frac{m_{N}^{2}}{N}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle y_{2}|x_{2}\right\rangle\left\langle y_{1}|x_{1}\right\rangle\frac{1}{\frac{N^{2}}{4}|x_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+m^{2}}\bigg]\ .

(4.35)

Let us point out that the first terms is the single trace quadratic term
{\rm tr}(\Phi^{2})
in the matrix model formulation of the fuzzy field theory. This is straightforwardly seen by using
\phi(x,y)=\left\langle x\right|\Phi\left|y\right\rangle
. Similarly, the second is reminiscent of the multitrace terms like
({\rm tr}\,\Phi)^{2}
.

When we consider only the local regime of the theory and the field of the form
\phi(x,y)=\phi(x,x)\left\langle x|y\right\rangle
, denote
\phi(x,x)=\phi(x)
and use (
2.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{g}{3}\frac{1}{4\pi}\int dx\,\phi(x)^{2}m_{N}^{2}+\frac{g}{6}\left(\frac{N}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\int dx\,dy\,\phi(x)\phi(y)\frac{1}{\frac{N^{2}}{4}|x-y|^{2}+m^{2}}\ .

(4.36)

It was observed in
[
5

that these two terms reproduce exactly the standard calculation
in momentum basis
[
4
]
, which involves an oscillatory integral. It was also noted that the first term is of the same form as the mass term in the bare action (
4.20
) and is thus a mass renormalization. The second term is however very different and yields a nonlocal contribution responsible for features such as UV/IR mixing.

It is worth writing down the full effective action up to one loop, which is the sum of (
4.2
) and (
4.35
). Restricting to the
low-energy regime of local functions, this has the form

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}\,=\,

\displaystyle\int dx\,\phi(x)\frac{1}{2}(\Box+m^{2})\phi(x)+\int dx\,\frac{g}{4!}\,\phi(x)^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{g}{3}\frac{1}{4\pi}\int dx\,\phi(x)^{2}m_{N}^{2}+\frac{g}{6}\left(\frac{N}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\int dx\,dy\,\phi(x)\phi(y)\frac{1}{\frac{N^{2}}{4}|x-y|^{2}+m^{2}}\ .

(4.37)

5
Discussion

We have discussed in detail the string representation of the propagator on the fuzzy sphere. It provides an explicit separation of the degrees of freedom in non-commutative field theory into a low-energy semi-classical and almost local regime, and a high-energy, stringy and non-local regime. While the low-energy regime behaves approximately as in standard field theory, the stringy regime displays a very different behavior, which can effectively captured by the methods developed here. It turns out that the stringy regime in noncommutative field theory is very different and in fact much simpler than in ordinary field theory, described by ribbon graphs which are manifestly local and diagonal. The stringy regime turns out to dominate for sufficiently large mass
{m^{2}=O(N)}
, which should allow to obtain analytical control over phase transitions of various models; this is postponed for future work.

An important application of the present framework is to compute the quantum effective action of noncommutative field theory and matrix models directly in position space, thereby avoiding
oscillatory integrals.
We formulate the appropriate Feynman rules for the case of
S^{2}_{N}
, which straightforwardly generalize to more general quantum spaces.
A crucial and novel feature is that the identification (
2.56
) of short string modes as localized wave-packets allows to evaluate the effective action directly in position space,
even on non-trivial backgrounds.
This was recently used to obtain the Einstein-Hilbert action from the one-loop effective action of the maximally supersymmetric IKKT or IIB matrix model
[
7
]
, where the stringy regime is suppressed by SUSY.

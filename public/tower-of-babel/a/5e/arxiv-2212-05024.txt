Decomposable Sparse Tensor on Tensor Regression
Haiyi Mao, Jason Xiaotian Dou

Abstract
Most regularized tensor regression research focuses on tensors predictors with scalars responses or vectors predictors to tensors responses. We consider the sparse low rank tensor on tensor regression where predictors 𝒳 \mathcal{X} and responses 𝒴 \mathcal{Y} are both high-dimensional tensors. By demonstrating that the general inner product ( Raskutti et al. 2019 ) or the contracted product ( Lock 2018 ) on a unit rank tensor can be decomposed into standard inner products and outer products, the problem can be simply transformed into a tensor to scalar regression followed by a tensor decomposition. So we propose a fast solution based on stagewise search composed by contraction part and generation part which are optimized alternatively. We successfully demonstrate that our method can outperform current methods in terms of accuracy, predictors selection by effectively incorporating the structural information.

\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\rangle

4.1
Model Formulation

For a
m
th data point
\mathcal{X}_{m}

\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\ldots\times d_{p}}
and a tensor response
m
th response
\mathcal{Y}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p+1}\times d_{p+2}\times\ldots\times d_{p+q}}
.
\mathcal{F}
is a linear transformation function in tensor space.

\mathcal{Y}_{m}=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{X}_{m})+\varepsilon^{m}

(9)

Where
\varepsilon^{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{p+1}\times d_{p+2}\times\ldots\times d_{p+q}}
is the tensor of noise term whose entries are independent and identically distributed centered Gaussian distribution with zero mean and variance
\sigma^{2}
independent with
\mathcal{X}_{m}
.
Without loss of generality, the intercept is set to zero by centering the response and standardizing the predictors
\sum_{m=1}^{M}{\mathcal{Y}_{m}}=\mathbf{0}
,
\sum_{m=1}^{M}{\mathcal{X}_{m}}=\mathbf{0}
. Then the function
\mathcal{F}
can be formalized as a tensor contracted product. There exists
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times\ldots\times d_{p}\times d_{p+1}\times\ldots\times d_{p+q}}
.
P
denotes as first
p
modes contracted product.

\mathcal{Y}_{m}=\llangle\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{B}\rrangle_{P}+\varepsilon^{m}

(10)

So our goal is to estimate
\mathcal{B}
given the i.i.d samples
\{{\mathcal{X}_{m},\mathcal{Y}_{m}}\}
. In order to reduce the complexity and enforce the interpretability and structural information, we impose the sparse and low rankness constraints on regression. Particularly, we assume
\mathcal{B}
can be CP decomposed,
\mathcal{B}=\sum_{r=1}^{R}w\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}^{r}\circ\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p}^{r}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+1}^{r}\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+q}^{r}
, where
\parallel\boldsymbol{\beta}_{i}^{r}\parallel=1
. Here we call first
p

q
vectors are generation vectors, and

is scalar value indicated the multitude of the coefficient tensor. So the problem (
10
) can be rewritten into (
11

\displaystyle\min_{\mathcal{B}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\parallel\mathcal{Y}_{m}-\llangle\mathcal{B},\mathcal{X}_{m}\rrangle_{P}\parallel_{F}^{2}+\alpha\parallel\mathcal{B}\parallel_{F}^{2}+\lambda\parallel\mathcal{B}\parallel_{1}

(11)

\displaystyle s.t.\;\mathcal{B}=\sum_{r=1}^{R}w\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}^{r}\circ\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p}^{r}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+1}^{r}\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+q}^{r}

Lemma 4.1
.

Given two tensors
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times...\times d_{p}}
and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times...\times d_{p}\times d_{p+1}\times...\times d_{p+q}}
, if
\mathcal{B}
can be CP decomposed into rank
1
vectors,
\mathcal{B}=b\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}\circ\ldots\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+1}\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+q}
. Then the contracted product
\llangle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rrangle_{P}=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}_{P}\rangle\otimes\mathcal{B}_{Q}
where
\mathcal{B}_{P}=b\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}\circ\ldots\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p}
and
\mathcal{B}_{Q}=\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+1}\ldots\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+q}

Proof.

\displaystyle\llangle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rrangle_{P}[i_{p+1},\ldots,i_{p+q}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots i_{p}}\mathcal{A}[i_{1}\ldots i_{p}]\mathcal{B}[i_{1}\ldots i_{p},i_{p+1}\ldots i_{p+q}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots i_{p}}\mathcal{A}[i_{1},\ldots i_{p}][b\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}\circ\ldots\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+1}\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+q}][[i_{1}\ldots i_{p},i_{p+1}\ldots i_{p+q}]]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots i_{p}}\mathcal{A}[i_{1},\ldots i_{p}][\mathcal{B}_{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{Q}][i_{1}\ldots i_{p},i_{p+1}\ldots i_{p+q}]

\displaystyle=(\sum_{p_{1},\ldots p_{d}}\mathcal{A}[i_{1},\ldots i_{p}]\mathcal{B}_{P}[i_{1}\ldots i_{p}])\otimes\mathcal{B}_{Q}[i_{p+1}\ldots i_{p+q}]

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}_{P}\rangle\otimes\mathcal{B}_{Q}[i_{p+1}\ldots i_{p+q}]

∎

(12)

\displaystyle+\alpha\parallel\mathcal{B}_{p}^{r}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{q}^{r}\parallel_{F}^{2}+\lambda\parallel\mathcal{B}_{p}^{r}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{q}^{r}\parallel_{1}

\displaystyle s.t.\;\mathcal{B}_{P}^{r}=w_{p}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{1}^{r}\circ\ldots\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p}^{r};\ \mathcal{B}_{Q}^{r}=w_{q}\circ\boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+1}^{r}\ldots\circ\ \boldsymbol{\beta}_{p+q}^{r}

From previous analysis, the unit rank tensor on tensor regression (

Theorem 5.1
.

For a unit rank coefficient tensor

(sketch)
Firstly fix

such that

. Since
\mathcal{B}_{Q}
is fixed, we can flatten

and
\mathcal{B}_{Q}
to vector space.

are vectorized

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we decompose the tensor on tensor regression for a unit rank into a tensor to scalar regression(contraction part) and a tensor decomposition (generation part). Hence the optimization can be formulated in two sub-problems. Furthermore, inspired by previous work, we introduce the stagewise search based algorithm DST2R to solve sparse tensor on tensor regression. The experiments result demonstrates DST2R benefits from adopting the structural information from predictors and responses. We hope our work will be useful to those looking to deploy tensor on tensor regression models.

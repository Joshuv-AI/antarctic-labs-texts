Medium modifications to jet angularities using SCET with Glauber gluons Preprint: TIFR/TH/23-7
Ankita Budhraja, Rishi Sharma, Balbeer Singh

Abstract
We perform a comprehensive analysis of medium modifications on ungroomed jet angularities, τ a \tau_{a} , within the framework of Soft-Collinear Effective Theory with Glauber gluons (SCET G ). Angularities are a one-parameter family of jet substructure observables with angularity exponent a < 2 a<2 for infrared safety. Variation of the angularity exponent allows to modify the relative weighting of the collinear-to-soft radiations in the jet. In this article, we focus on angularity exponents a < 1 a<1 and provide detailed results for a = − 1 , 0 a=-1,0 , and 0.5 0.5 . Within SCET G , the interaction between jet and medium constituents is comprehended by off-shell Glauber gluons generated from the color gauge fields in the medium. While the medium modifications are incorporated in the jet function via the use of in-medium splitting functions, the soft function remains unmodified for a < 1 a<1 . For all values of a a , we find that in the medium, the distributions are narrower and have a steeper fall compared to the vacuum ones. This redistribution of the ungroomed angularity spectrum is more apparent for a jet with a larger cone size. We also present results for the medium sensitivity towards p T p_{T} of the jet and for a jet initiated in a less central event ( 10 − 30 % 10-30\% centrality). Finally, we provide the ratios of nucleus-nucleus and proton-proton differential angularity distributions for different angularity exponents and for two values of the jet radius parameter.

Introduction

Jets are complex objects that span over a wide range of length scales. Due to their sensitivity to varied length and energy scales, they are useful in probing the properties of the quark-gluon plasma (QGP) formed in high-energy nuclear collision experiments. Over the past decade, experiments at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) have provided measurements of a diverse set of observables characterizing jets in heavy-ion (AA, particularly PbPb) collisions
Connors:2017ptx
.
By comparing the cross-section in PbPb collisions, scaled by the inverse number of binary collisions, to that in proton-proton (pp) collisions, it is possible to quantify the energy loss experienced by partons as they traverse the QGP medium
ATLAS:2012tjt
;
ATLAS:2014ipv
;
ATLAS:2018gwx
;
ALICE:2015mjv
;
ALICE:2019qyj
;
CMS:2016uxf
. This energy loss causes a suppression in the yields of jets in AA collisions, a phenomenon commonly known as jet quenching
Baier:2000mf
;
Wiedemann:2009sh
;
Majumder:2010qh
;
Singh:2022dcr
.
Since the propagation of partons in AA is related to the properties of the
medium — for example its temperature, its size, whether it is strongly or
weakly coupled — this suppression is a probe of the medium.

To fully exploit the power of jet measurements for evaluating medium properties,
differential jet substructure measurements must be considered. These measurements provide information about the distribution of particles within the jet. In recent years, there has been a growing interest in studying such observables in AA due to their potential role in providing pivotal information about the microscopic properties of QGP. In particular, jet substructure observables can be designed to focus on specific regions of the phase space. For instance, some of the substructure observables that have been measured so far include jet
mass
CMS:2018fof
, jet fragmentation
functions
CMS:2012nro
;
CMS:2014jjt
, splitting
functions
ALargeIonColliderExperiment:2021mqf

CMS:2013lhm
;
CMS:2018jco
and
others
ALICE:2019ykw
;
CMS:2020plq
. These substructure measurements provide an additional advantage in disentangling the effects of the initial state, cold nuclear matter (CNM) effects, from jet-medium interactions
Chien:2015hda
.

One family of jet substructure observables of particular interest is called
angularities
Berger:2003iw
. The angularity of
a reconstructed jet in pp (and AA) collisions is defined by

\tau_{a}=\frac{1}{p_{T}}\sum_{i\epsilon J}|{p}_{T}^{\,\,i}|(\Delta{\cal R}_{iJ})^{2-a}\,,~

(1)

where

Variation of the exponent ‘

Thus, one can characterize the effect of a change in the energy-momentum distribution of the jet constituents, expected in AA collisions compared to vacuum,

The phenomenology of angularity distributions in pp collisions has been extensively studied, and both Monte Carlo simulations

To the best of our knowledge, so far, EFTs have not been employed to calculate angularities in AA collisions. This paper presents the first attempt to perform such a calculation.

The essence of the dynamics of an energetic parton traversing
a weakly coupled medium was detailed by
BDMPS-Z
Baier:1996kr
;
Baier:1998kq
;
Zakharov:1997uu
. While passing through the medium, the fast-moving parton as well as the radiated gluon picks up
multiple kicks transverse to their motion by scattering off of medium constituents. As a result of these independent scatterings the effective radiation spectrum gets modified, leading to more energy loss to the medium. This process is dubbed as medium-induced radiation
Baier:2000mf
. For reconstructed jets with radius
R
, this energy loss can be collected as the out-of-cone jet radiation. In view of this, a jet with larger
R
will pump less energy out of the jet cone compared to a narrower one. However, let us keep in mind that a large
R
jet will interact more with the medium constituents, leading to larger modifications. As a result of the above-stated effects—the out-of-cone energy loss and the jet-medium interactions—the angularity distributions, for a given set of jet and medium parameters, are modified. In particular, we will see that the angularity distributions in AA, for a larger jet cone size, are narrower than in pp. Similar
arguments can be made for a strongly coupled
medium
Rajagopal:2016uip
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2016jvj

Numerous approaches

2
Soft-Collinear Effective Theory with Glauber gluons

SCET
Bauer:2000ew
;
Bauer:2002uv
;
Bauer:2000yr
;
Bauer:2001ct
;
Bauer:2001yt
;
Bauer:2002nz
is an effective field theory of QCD designed to separate scales in high-energy scattering processes in order to describe the dynamics of energetic quarks and gluons. It has been successfully applied to study jet production and its subsequent evolution. Moreover, the EFT has been used to compute a large class of jet substructure observables up to the state-of-art next-to-next-to-leading-logarithmic (NNLL) accuracy (see
Larkoski:2017jix
;
Kogler:2018hem
and references therein). Before we discuss the EFT describing jet-medium interactions, we outline the necessary ingredients of the usual SCET theories.

The relevant degrees of freedom described by SCET are the collinear and the soft modes. Partons that are highly boosted along a specific direction constitute the collinear modes. On the other hand, the momentum components of soft modes have a homogeneous scaling. The large momentum component along the collinear direction makes the use of light-cone coordinates more convenient to describe the EFT modes. In these coordinates, any four-momentum
p^{\mu}\equiv(p^{+},p^{-},p_{\perp})
, with
p^{+}=n\cdot p
and
p^{-}=\bar{n}\cdot p
, can be expanded as

p^{\mu}=\frac{n^{\mu}}{2}p^{-}+\frac{\bar{n}^{\mu}}{2}p^{+}+p_{\perp}^{\mu}\,,

(2)

where
n^{\mu},\bar{n}^{\mu}
are light-like vectors such that
n^{2}=\bar{n}^{2}=0
and
n\cdot\bar{n}=2
. For a collinear parton along
\bar{n}
direction, there exists a hierarchy between the various light-cone momentum components as
p^{+}\gg p_{\perp}\gg p^{-}
while for a soft parton, there is no such hierarchy in the components and one has
p^{+}\sim p_{\perp}\sim p^{-}
.
More explicitly, for a jet observable like jet mass (
m_{J}
), in terms of the power counting parameter
\lambda\ll 1
(corresponding to
m_{J}\ll Q
), the momentum scaling of the SCET modes is as follows

p_{n}\sim Q(\lambda^{2},1,\lambda),\hskip 28.45274ptp_{\bar{n}}\sim Q(1,\lambda^{2},\lambda),\hskip 28.45274ptp_{s}\sim Q(\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2})\,,

(3)

where
Q
is the scale of the hard interaction which is typically of the order of
p_{T}
for pp (and AA) collisions.

The exact parametrization of
\lambda
in terms of relevant scales depends on the specific jet substructure observable. For instance, for the observable of our interest, i.e.
angularities
, we take the power counting parameter as
\lambda\sim\tau_{a}^{\frac{1}{2-a}}
. Therefore, in terms of this parametrization, the scaling of the collinear modes holds as above while we take a more general scaling of the soft mode as given in Ref.
Budhraja:2019mcz

p_{s}\sim Q(\lambda^{2-a},\lambda^{2-a},\lambda^{2-a})\,.

(4)

For the case of
a<1
angularities, this soft mode always scales smaller than
{\cal O}(\lambda)
and hence
p_{s}^{2}\ll p_{c}^{2}
. In literature, such soft modes are usually known as the
ultrasoft
modes and the corresponding EFT with these degrees of freedom as SCET
I
. However, for the sake of convenience, we will refer to these as the
soft
modes only.

The gauge invariant collinear quark and gluon fields are built out of the SCET collinear fields (
\xi_{n}
,
A_{n}
) and the collinear Wilson line (
U_{n}
), which read as

\chi_{n}(x)=U^{\dagger}_{n}(x)\xi_{n}(x),\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{B}^{\mu}_{n,\perp}(x)=\frac{1}{g}U_{n}^{\dagger}(x)iD^{\mu}_{n,\perp}U_{n}(x)\,,

(5)

where
D^{\mu}_{n,\perp}
is the transverse component of the collinear covariant derivative
Bauer:2001yt
. The collinear Wilson line is built from the path-ordered exponentiation of the collinear gluons by integrating out the heavy off-shell propagators from the theory and is defined as,

U_{n}(x)=\mathcal{P}\exp\left(-ig\int_{0}^{\infty}dy\bar{n}\cdot A_{n}(x+\bar{n}y)\right)\,,

(6)

where
A_{n}
is collinear gluon field operator. At leading order in
\lambda
, the interaction term between soft gluons and the collinear fields in the SCET Lagrangian is decoupled through a BPS field redefinition
Bauer:2001yt
. Thus, the leading order SCET Lagrangian reads formally as

\mathcal{L}_{\text{SCET}}(\xi_{n},\xi_{\bar{n}},A_{n},A_{\bar{n}},A_{s})=\mathcal{L}_{n}(\xi_{n},A_{n})+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{n}}(\xi_{\bar{n}},A_{\bar{n}})+\mathcal{L}_{s}(A_{s})\,,

(7)

with
A_{s}
being the soft gauge field operator. This leading order SCET Lagrangian with its given degrees of freedom is sufficient to describe a large class of jet substructure observables. However, in order to describe jet dynamics in a medium, one needs additional degrees of freedom that allow interaction between jet and medium constituents.

It is well known that when a jet passes through a medium, it interacts with the medium constituents leading to further modifications of its internal structure
as well as the jet yield.
In the literature, jet-medium interactions
have been modeled by incorporating additional modes that can be thought to be originated from background color gluon fields of the medium and are known as the ‘Glauber modes’.
The corresponding EFT with such extra degrees of freedom is known as SCET
{}_{\text{G}}

Idilbi:2008vm
;
DEramo:2010wup
;
Ovanesyan:2011xy
. Glaubers are non-propagating off-shell degrees of freedom with transverse momentum much larger than their other light-cone momentum components,
k_{\perp}^{2}\gg k^{+}k^{-}
.

A key point here is that the scaling of the Glauber mode depends on the scaling of the source that emits these modes and the interaction of these modes with the collinear ones does not change the collinear scaling. In the presence of the medium, the relevant characteristic scale for the Glauber modes can be of the order of the medium temperature
T
or the Debye screening mass
m_{D}
. Thus, with the power counting written in terms of the medium scales, the momentum scaling of the Glauber gluons is identified. For instance, for a collinear source with momentum scaling
Q(\tilde{\lambda}^{2},1,\tilde{\lambda})
, it scales as
Q(\tilde{\lambda}^{2},\tilde{\lambda}^{2},\tilde{\lambda})

Idilbi:2008vm
; for soft source with scaling
Q(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\lambda})
, Glauber modes scale as
Q(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\lambda}^{2},\tilde{\lambda})
and for a massive (static) source with scaling
Q(1,1,\tilde{\lambda})
, Glauber modes scale as
Q(\tilde{\lambda}^{2},\tilde{\lambda}^{2},\tilde{\lambda})
.
Furthermore, in the static source limit, recoil energy in the medium is negligible.
For our EFT, we then require

\tilde{\lambda}\sim\frac{T}{Q}\lesssim\lambda\sim\tau_{a}^{\frac{1}{2-a}}\ll 1\,,

(8)

to allow for the interactions between jet partons and the medium constituents. In this paper, we will not distinguish between the scales
\lambda
and
\tilde{\lambda}
and refer to both as
\lambda
from here onwards.

In an alternate approach, in Ref.
Vaidya:2020cyi
, the interaction between the soft modes of the medium and collinear modes of the jet has been studied by combining the relevant EFT
Rothstein:2016bsq
with the open quantum system framework. Furthermore, a factorized expression within the Markovian approximation with incoherent interactions for substructure observables has been obtained in
Vaidya:2020lih
. For coherent interactions and related hierarchies, see Ref.
Vaidya:2021mly
. For radiative corrections, see Ref.
Vaidya:2021vxu
.

In the present study, we follow
Ref.
Ovanesyan:2011xy
and assume that the Glauber gluon sources in the
QGP medium are heavy quasi-particles. Therefore, such heavy particles can be
described with the heavy quark effective theory. By extending the framework of SCET to include collinear quark-Glauber and collinear gluon-Glauber interactions,
the Glauber Lagrangian in terms of the source fields can be written as

\mathcal{L}_{G}(\chi_{n},\mathcal{B}_{n},\eta)=\sum_{q,p,p^{\prime}}e^{-i(q+p-p^{\prime})\cdot x}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{\chi}_{n,p^{\prime}}\Gamma^{\nu,a}_{qqG}\not{\bar{n}}\chi_{n,p}-i\Gamma^{\nu\mu\lambda,abc}_{ggG}(\mathcal{B}^{c}_{n,p^{\prime}})_{\lambda}(\mathcal{B}^{b}_{n,p})_{\nu}\right)\bar{\eta}\Gamma^{\delta,a}_{s}\eta\Delta_{\nu\delta}(q),

(9)

where
\Gamma_{qqG}
and
\Gamma_{ggG}
are vertices for quark-quark-Glauber gluon and gluon-gluon-Glauber gluon interactions, respectively (for more details see Refs.
Ovanesyan:2011xy
;
DEramo:2010wup
). Here
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}
are source fields and
\Delta_{\nu\delta}
is the Glauber gluon propagator which is the same as the time-like component of resummed gluon propagator in the static limit
Bellac:2011kqa
. A detailed calculation of the relevant quantities such as medium modified splitting functions has been discussed in Ref.
Ovanesyan:2011xy
utlizing this set-up, therefore we shall not discuss it again in this paper.

4
Jet Angularity Distributions in vacuum

4.1
Jet function

The jet function can be computed from the operator form written in terms of the collinear SCET fields as dictated by Eqs. (
13

14

Alternatively, it has been shown in Ref.
Ritzmann:2014mka

Ritzmann:2014mka

{\cal J}_{i}(\dots{})=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}\,e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\!\epsilon}\,2g_{s}^{2}\,\sum_{i^{1}i^{2}\dots{}i^{n}}\int\prod_{n=1}^{m}\frac{d^{d}k_{n}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\,{\cal P}_{i\to i^{1}i^{2}\dots{}i^{n}}\,\delta(\dots{})\delta(k_{n}^{2}).

(18)

m

m

4.4
Non-perturbative Effects to Soft Function

6
Summary & Outlook

In this paper, we perform a detailed study of medium modifications on ungroomed jet angularities and restrict to the range of
a<1
exponents. Angularities are interesting observables in the sense that one can vary the sensitivity of the observable to collinear radiation in the jet by varying the exponent “
a
”. We present results for angularity exponents
a=-1,\,0
and
0.5
, although the framework holds for any value of “
a
” less than 1. To describe jet-medium interactions, we utilize the framework of SCET
G
that incorporates these interactions via background Glauber fields generated from the color charges in the QGP. Utilizing the collinear-matrix elements and the in-medium splitting functions, we calculate the medium-modified angularity jet function to NLO accuracy. We use nCTEQ15 nuclear PDFs to encapsulate initial state effects. To capture medium effects, we estimate medium parameters within the Glauber model and obtain the differential angularity spectrum.

In order to explore the medium modifications on the jet substructure, we present results for various jet parameters, angularity exponents as well as for an off-central event. By looking at a narrow jet cone size
R=0.2
, we find the differential angularity distribution for
a=0
to be less modified by the medium. Furthermore, from the ratio of the differential distributions for
R=0.2
, we observe little to no modification for all the angularity exponents, with the
a=0.5
being the most modified.

We then investigate the medium effects for a wider jet cone with
R=0.4
. We observe a shift of the distribution towards small
\tau_{a}
for a jet with
40<p_{T}<60
and for exponent
a=0
. This apparent enhancement in the small
\tau_{a}
region is an indication of the narrowing of the distribution. Furthermore, for a high
p_{T}
parton-initiated jet with
80<p_{T}<100
, this shift in the peak is less apparent. This may be because as the jet parton is more energetic it is less affected by the soft medium constituents. We also study the centrality dependence of medium effects by investigating a jet with
40<p_{T}<60
and
R=0.4
. Here, we find the jet to be less modified by the medium in the whole range of the
\tau_{a}
distribution in contrast to that in a central collision. Finally, from the ratios, we find a sizable jet substructure modification for almost all the “
a
” values. We, therefore, conclude that experimental efforts to investigate jet angularities in heavy ion collisions for a larger jet cone size would be more insightful.

We emphasize on the fact that ungroomed angularities, however, are sensitive to the effects of jet selection bias, hadronization effects as well as the non-global logarithms, due to soft radiations at the jet boundary. One simple strategy to minimize the selection bias effect is to use
\gamma/Z
-tagged jet samples for measurement. As the
\gamma/Z
will not interact with the medium constituents, one expects to be able to appropriately identify the parton’s energy that initiated the jet, which then traverses the medium, thereby disentangling medium modifications on the jet from these effects. More recently, a larger emphasis is being laid on the use of
groomed
substructure observables both theoretically and experimentally
ALICE:2022hyz
;
Caucal:2021cfb
;
Mulligan:2020tim
;
Casalderrey-Solana:2019ubu
. Grooming reduces the sensitivity of the observable on soft fragments of the jet, thereby minimizing all the stated effects arising from the boundary of the jet. This motivates the use of groomed angularities for the thermal medium, in order to better understand the medium modifications for a class of jet observables. This will be explored in a future publication.

\displaystyle\vec{D}_{\perp}=\vec{k}_{\perp}-\vec{q}_{\perp},\,\,

(74)

and

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{2}=\frac{\vec{B}_{\perp}^{2}}{p_{0}^{+}x(1-x)},\,\Omega_{1}-\Omega_{3}=\frac{\vec{C}_{\perp}^{2}}{p_{0}^{+}x(1-x)},

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}-\Omega_{3}=\frac{\vec{C}_{\perp}^{2}-\vec{B}_{\perp}^{2}}{p_{0}^{+}x(1-x)},\,\,\Omega_{4}=\frac{\vec{A}_{\perp}^{2}}{p_{0}^{+}x(1-x)},

\displaystyle\Omega_{5}=\frac{\vec{A}_{\perp}^{2}-\vec{D}_{\perp}^{2}}{p_{0}^{+}x(1-x)},

(75)

Appendix B
Plus Distributions

For a generic function
g(x)
which is less singular than
1/x^{2}
as
x\to 0
, the plus distribution with its boundary at
x=x_{0}
is defined as
Ligeti:2008ac

\left[\theta(x)\,g(x)\right]_{+}^{x=x_{0}}=\lim_{{\epsilon}\rightarrow 0}\left[\theta(x-{\epsilon})\,g(x)+\delta(x-{\epsilon})\,\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,g(x^{\prime})\right]\,,

(76)

with the boundary condition

\int_{0}^{x_{0}}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\left[\theta(x)\,g(x)\right]_{+}^{x=x_{0}}=0\,.

(77)

Furthermore, the plus distributions with two distinct boundary conditions can be related to each other as

[\theta(x)\,g(x)]_{+}^{x=x_{0}}=[\theta(x)\,g(x)]_{+}^{x=1}+\delta(x)\int_{x_{0}}^{1}\!\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\,g(x^{\prime})\,.

(78)

Utilizing this identity, in particular, we rewrite this relation for the distributions where one has a boundary at infinity to the other one with a boundary at
x=+1
. This relation has been employed, for instance, in Eq. (
22
) of the main text and reads as

\left[\frac{\theta(x)}{x^{1+\alpha}}\right]_{+}^{\infty}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\delta(x)+\left[\frac{\theta(x)}{x}\right]_{+}\!\!\!-\alpha\left[\frac{\theta(x)\ln x}{x}\right]_{+}\!\!\!+{\cal O}(\alpha^{2})\,.

(79)

Another useful identity that we require is the
rescaling
identity, i.e., any constant parameter
\zeta
(with
\zeta>0
) can be scaled out of the distribution as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta}\left[\frac{\theta(x)}{x/\zeta}\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\theta(x)}{x}\right]_{+}\!\!\!-\ln\zeta\,\delta(x)\,,

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta}\left[\frac{\theta(x)\ln(x/\zeta)}{x/\zeta}\right]_{+}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\theta(x)\ln x}{x}\right]_{+}\!\!\!-\ln\zeta\,\left[\frac{\theta(x)}{x}\right]_{+}\!\!\!+\frac{1}{2}\ln^{2}\!\zeta\,\,\delta(x)\,.

(81)

Appendix C
Measurement function for
1\to 2
splitting

The definition for angularity in
pp
collision is related to the
one in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions as

\tau_{a}=\bigg(\frac{2E_{J}}{p_{T}}\bigg)^{2-a}\tau_{a}^{e^{+}e^{-}}\,,

(82)

where
p_{T}
is the transverse momentum of the jet with respect to the beam direction and
E_{J}
is its total energy. The angularity observable for
e^{+}\,e^{-}
reads as

\tau_{a}^{e^{+}e^{-}}=\frac{1}{2E_{J}}\sum_{i\epsilon J}|\vec{p}_{T}^{\,\,i}|e^{-|\eta_{i}|(1-a)}\,.

(83)

For leading order splitting, i.e.,
l\to q\,k
, the measurement function can be simplified as

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{a}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{2E_{J}}{p_{T}}\bigg)^{2-a}\frac{1}{2E_{J}}\left\{|\vec{k}_{T}|e^{-|\eta|(1-a)}+|\vec{q}_{T}|e^{-|\eta|(1-a)}\right\},

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{2E_{J}}{p_{T}}\bigg)^{2-a}\frac{1}{2E_{J}}\left\{(k^{-})^{1-a/2}(k^{+})^{a/2}+(q^{-})^{1-a/2}(q^{+})^{a/2}\right\},

(84)

with
k^{+}>k^{-}
and
q^{+}>q^{-}
.

Utilizing the on-shell conditions,
k^{-}=k_{\perp}^{2}/k^{+}
,
q^{-}=q_{\perp}^{2}/q^{+}
and writing
k^{+}=xl^{+}
,
q^{+}=(1-x)l^{+}
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}_{a}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{2E_{J}}{p_{T}}\bigg)^{2-a}\frac{1}{2E_{J}}\left\{(l^{+}x)^{a-1}k_{\perp}^{2-a}+(l^{+}(1-x))^{a-1}q_{\perp}^{2-a}\right\},

\displaystyle=p_{T}^{2-a}\left\{x^{a-1}k_{\perp}^{2-a}+(1-x)^{a-1}q_{\perp}^{2-a}\right\}\,,

(85)

where we have used
l^{+}=2E_{J}
in the last equation.

Appendix D
Perturbative results

In this section, we collect some necessary ingredients for our perturbative computations that were left out of the main text.

D.1
Fixed-order computation of the medium jet function

Here we provide a detailed computation of the NLO medium modified angularity jet function for
a<1
, used in Eq. (
51
), following the splitting function approach proposed in Ref.
Ritzmann:2014mka
. The jet algorithm constraint to the jet function is power suppressed in the relevant kinematical regime of
\tau^{1/2-a}\ll R
, i.e.,

\Theta_{\rm alg}\equiv\Theta\left(\omega\,x(1-x)\tan\frac{R}{2}-k_{\perp}\right)\to 1\,.

(86)

Following Ref.
Ritzmann:2014mka
, the one-loop medium modified jet function is then given as

{\cal J}_{i}^{\rm med}(\tau_{a},p_{T},\mu)=\int d\Phi_{2}^{c}\,\sigma_{2}^{c}\delta(\tau_{a}-p_{T}^{2-a}\left\{x^{a-1}k_{\perp}^{2-a}+(1-x)^{a-1}q_{\perp}^{2-a}\right\})\,.

(87)

The two-particle phase space,
d\Phi_{2}^{c}
, appearing in the above equation has the form

d\Phi_{2}^{c}=2(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}(2E_{J})\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\delta(k^{2})\theta(k^{0})\!\int\!\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\delta(q^{2})\theta(q^{0})\delta(2E_{J}-k^{+}-q^{+})\delta^{d-2}(k_{\perp}+q_{\perp}),

(88)

and

\sigma_{2}^{c}=\sum_{j,k}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}\,e^{\gamma_{E}}}{4\pi}\right)^{\!\!\epsilon}2g_{s}^{2}{\cal P}^{\rm med}_{i\to jk}(x,k_{\perp})\,.

(89)

Simplifying the phase space integrals using the delta functions, we obtain a simpler form that reads as

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{i}^{\rm med}(\tau_{a},p_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\sum_{j,k}\int dx\,\frac{dk_{\perp}}{k_{\perp}^{2\epsilon-1}}{\cal P}_{i\to jk}^{\rm med}(x,k_{\perp})

\displaystyle\qquad\times\delta\left(\tau_{a}-p_{T}^{a-2}k_{\perp}^{2-a}(x^{a-1}+(1-x)^{a-1})\right).

(90)

Finally, carrying out the
k_{\perp}
integration using the measurement delta function, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{i}^{\rm med}(\tau_{a},p_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi|2-a|}\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac{1}{\tau_{a}^{\frac{2\epsilon-a}{2-a}}}\sum_{j,k}\int dx\,\left(x^{a-1}+(1-x)^{a-1}\right)^{\frac{2\epsilon-2}{2-a}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times{\cal P}_{i\to jk}^{\rm med}\left(x,\frac{p_{T}\tau^{\frac{1}{2-a}}}{(x^{a-1}+(1-x)^{a-1})^{\frac{1}{2-a}}}\right)\,.

(91)

As discussed earlier, the LPM effect constrains the phase space integration of the above equation rendering the medium modified jet function finite. Therefore, one can take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
. As a result of which there is no extra divergence due to the presence of the medium and the RGEs remain the same as the vacuum case.

D.2
Fixed-order computation of the vacuum jet function

Following the same approach as outlined in the previous section, we obtain a form similar to Eq. (
90
), however, now the in-medium splittings are replaced by vacuum splitting functions.
For a quark-initiated jet, the jet function then reads as

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{q}^{\rm vac}(\tau_{a},p_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\pi}\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int dx\,\frac{dk_{\perp}}{k_{\perp}^{1+2\epsilon}}{\cal P}_{q\to gq}(x)\delta\left(\tau_{a}-p_{T}^{a-2}k_{\perp}^{2-a}(x^{a-1}+(1-x)^{a-1})\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\pi|2-a|}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}\right)^{\!\!\epsilon}\frac{1}{\tau_{a}^{1+\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}}}\int dx\,{\cal P}_{q\to gq}(x)\left(x^{a-1}+(1-x)^{a-1}\right)^{\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}},

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,C_{F}}{\pi|2-a|}\frac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}\right)^{\!\!\epsilon}\frac{1}{\tau_{a}^{1+\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}}}\int dx\,\left(1+\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{a-1}\right)^{\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}}\times

\displaystyle\quad\left(\frac{1+x^{2}}{(1-x)^{1+2\epsilon\frac{1-a}{2-a}}}-\epsilon(1-x)^{1-2\epsilon\frac{1-a}{2-a}}\right)\,,

(92)

where in the second line we have used the delta function constraint to perform integration over
k_{\perp}
and in the last step, we have factored out the term
(1-x)^{-2\epsilon\frac{1-a}{2-a}}
to regulate the divergence at
x\to 1
in the splitting function.

Now, performing the
x
integral by first expanding in
\epsilon
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{q}^{\rm vac}(\tau_{a},p_{T},\mu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,C_{F}}{\pi|2-a|}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\epsilon^{2}\right)\left(1+\epsilon\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\ln^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}\right)\bigg(-\frac{2-a}{2\epsilon}\delta(\tau_{a})+\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{a}}\right]_{+}\!\!\!

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}\left[\frac{\ln\tau_{a}}{\tau_{a}}\right]_{+}\bigg)\times\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\bigg[-\frac{1+x^{2}}{2\epsilon}\frac{2-a}{1-a}\delta(1-x)+\frac{1+x^{2}}{[1-x]_{+}}-\frac{2\epsilon(1-a)}{2-a}(1+x^{2})

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\ln(1-x)}{[1-x]_{+}}-\epsilon(1-x)\bigg]+\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\ln\left[1+\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{a-1}\right]\times\bigg(-\frac{1+x^{2}}{2\epsilon}\frac{2-a}{1-a}\delta(1-x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1+x^{2}}{[1-x]_{+}}-\frac{2\epsilon(1-a)}{2-a}\frac{\ln(1-x)}{[1-x]_{+}}-\epsilon(1-x)\bigg)\Bigg\}\,.

(93)

Note that for
a<1
, the term
x/(1-x)
is raised to a negative power, hence the term in the logarithm is non-singular. Keeping only terms up to
{\cal O}(\epsilon)
in the above expression, we have

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{q}^{\rm vac}(\tau_{a},p_{T},\mu)=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)\,C_{F}}{\pi|2-a|}\bigg(\delta(\tau_{a})\Big(-\frac{2-a}{2\epsilon}-\frac{2-a}{2}\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}-\frac{\epsilon}{4}(2-a)\ln^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\epsilon(2-1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{a}}\right]_{+}+\epsilon\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{a}}\right]_{+}-\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}\left[\frac{\ln\tau_{a}}{\tau_{a}}\right]_{+}\bigg)\times\bigg\{-\frac{2-a}{1-a}\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon\Big(\frac{1}{2}+3\frac{2(1-a)}{2-a}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\pi^{2}\epsilon}{3}\frac{1-a}{2-a}+\frac{2\epsilon}{2-a}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}\ln[x^{1-a}+(1-x)^{1-a}]\bigg\}\,,

(94)

which gives the result quoted in Eq. (
22
). For the gluon-initiated jet, we follow similar steps by taking the contributions from
{\cal P}_{gg}
and
{\cal P}_{qg}
, providing the result given in Eq. (
24
).

D.3
Fourier space solution of jet and soft functions

For the jet and the soft functions, the RGEs have the form of a convolution in the measurement variable
\tau_{a}
, as given in Eqs. (
27
) and (
31
). In order to solve these coupled RGEs, we define a Fourier transform with respect to the observable such that the RGEs become multiplicative in the Fourier space. The Fourier transform of a generic function
F
depending on a variable
x
is defined as

F(y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,e^{-ixy}F(x)\,.

(95)

The RGEs for the jet and soft functions, in the Fourier space, then read as

\displaystyle\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\tilde{\cal J}_{i}(y,p_{T},\mu)=\gamma_{{\cal J}i}(y,p_{T},\mu)\,\tilde{\cal J}_{i}(y,p_{T},\mu)\,,

(96)

\displaystyle\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\tilde{\cal S}_{i}(y,p_{T},\mu)=\gamma_{{\cal S}i}(y,p_{T},\mu)\,\tilde{\cal S}_{i}(y,p_{T},\mu)\,,

(97)

where the relevant anomalous dimensions are given as

\displaystyle\gamma_{{\cal J}i}(y,p_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\cal J}^{i}[\alpha_{s}]\left(\frac{2}{j_{J}}\ln(i\,y\,e^{\gamma_{E}})+\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}}\right)+\gamma_{\cal J}^{i}[\alpha_{s}]\,,

(98)

\displaystyle\gamma_{{\cal S}i}(y,p_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle=-2\,\Gamma_{S}^{i}[\alpha_{s}]\ln\!\left(\frac{\mu R^{1-a}}{p_{T}}(i\,y\,e^{\gamma_{E}})^{1/j_{S}}\right).

(99)

Here
\Gamma
and
\gamma
are the cusp and non-cusp anomalous dimensions. These exhibit an order-by-order
expansion in the strong coupling. To the desired NLL
′
accuracy, we require up to two-loop coefficient of the cusp piece, the one-loop non-cusp anomalous dimension, and two-loops running of the beta function. The expansions of the anomalous dimension pieces as well as the beta function in powers of
\alpha_{s}
are written as

\Gamma[\alpha_{s}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma^{n}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\Big)^{n+1}\,,\,\,\gamma[\alpha_{s}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{n}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\Big)^{n+1}\,,\,\,\beta[\alpha_{s}]=-2\alpha_{s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta^{n}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\Big)^{n+1}\,.

(100)

Up to two loops, the
\beta
-function coefficients that we require are given as (in
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme)

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{4}{3}T_{R}\,N_{f}\,,

(101)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{34}{3}C_{A}^{2}-\left(\frac{20}{3}C_{A}+4\,C_{F}\right)T_{R}\,N_{f}\,.

(102)

Having the anomalous dimensions as given in Eq. (
99
), the solution of the RGEs for the jet and the soft functions, in the Fourier space, reads as

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal J}_{i}(y,p_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\cal J}_{i}(y,p_{T},\mu_{J})\times e^{K_{\gamma_{J}}^{i}+2K_{\Gamma_{J}}^{i}}\left[(i\,y\,e^{\gamma_{E}})^{\frac{2\eta_{\Gamma_{J}}^{i}}{j_{J}}}\left(\frac{\mu_{J}}{p_{T}}\right)^{2\eta_{\Gamma_{J}}^{i}}\right]\,,

(103)

\displaystyle\tilde{\cal S}_{i}(y,p_{T},R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\cal S}_{i}(y,p_{T},R,\mu_{S})\times e^{\!-2K_{\Gamma_{S}}^{i}}\left[(i\,y\,e^{\gamma_{E}})^{-\frac{2\eta_{\Gamma_{S}}^{i}}{j_{S}}}\left(\frac{\mu_{S}R^{1-a}}{p_{T}}\right)^{\!-2\eta_{\Gamma_{S}}^{i}}\right]\,.

(104)

Note that both jet and soft functions are evolved from their corresponding natural scales
\mu_{J,S}
to an arbitrary renormalization scale
\mu
which we choose to be the hard matching scale
\mu_{\cal H}\sim p_{T}R
.

K_{\Gamma}
,
k_{\gamma}
and
\eta_{\Gamma}
are the evolution kernels that depend on the characteristic scale of the relevant dynamics described and the free renormalization scale. For brevity, we have suppressed this scale dependence in Eqs. (
103
) and (
104
).
These evolution kernels to all-orders are defined as

\displaystyle K_{\Gamma}^{i}(\mu_{0},\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}}{\beta(\alpha_{s})}\,\Gamma^{i}[\alpha_{s}]\int_{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha_{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}}{\beta(\alpha_{s}^{\prime})}\,,\quad\eta_{\Gamma}^{i}(\mu_{0},\mu)=\int_{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}}{\beta(\alpha_{s})}\,\Gamma^{i}[\alpha_{s}]\,,

\displaystyle K_{\gamma}(\mu_{0},\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}^{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{s}}{\beta(\alpha_{s})}\,\gamma(\alpha_{s})\,,

(105)

and their explicit expressions up to NLL
′
are

\displaystyle K_{\Gamma}(\mu_{0},\mu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{4\beta_{0}^{2}}\,\biggl\{\frac{4\pi}{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}\,\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}-\ln r\Bigr)+\biggl(\frac{\Gamma_{1}}{\Gamma_{0}}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{0}}\biggr)(1-r+\ln r)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2\beta_{0}}\ln^{2}r\biggr\}\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{\Gamma}(\mu_{0},\mu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2\beta_{0}}\,\biggl[\ln r+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}{4\pi}\,\biggl(\frac{\Gamma_{1}}{\Gamma_{0}}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{0}}\biggr)(r-1)\biggr]\,,

\displaystyle K_{\gamma}(\mu_{0},\mu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2\beta_{0}}\,\ln r\,,

(106)

with
r=\alpha_{s}(\mu)/\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})
.

D.4
Fixed-order hard matching functions

The hard matching functions
{\cal H}_{c\to i}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)
encode in them any radiation with virtuality of the order of
{\cal O}(p_{T}R)
, i.e., radiation that fails to pass the jet algorithm constraint. Up to NLO, it has been shown that they can be obtained by the out-of-jet radiation diagrams for the inclusive jet substructure observables
Kang:2016ehg
. For the
k_{t}
-type jets, the one-loop hard matching functions are given by

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{q\to q^{\prime}}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\delta_{qq^{\prime}}\delta(1-z)+\delta_{qq^{\prime}}\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{2\pi}\bigg\{C_{F}\delta(1-z)\Big(\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-\frac{1}{2}L^{2}-\frac{3}{2}L\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+{\cal P}_{gq}(z)L-2C_{F}(1+z^{2})\left[\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\right]_{+}\!\!\!-C_{F}(1-z)\bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{q\to g}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{2\pi}\bigg\{\Big(L-2\ln(1-z)\Big){\cal P}_{q\bar{q}}(z)-C_{F}z\bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{g\to g}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{2\pi}\bigg\{\delta(1-z)\Big(\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-\frac{C_{A}}{2}L^{2}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}L\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+{\cal P}_{gg}(z)L-\frac{4C_{A}(1-z+z^{2})^{2}}{z}\left[\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\right]_{+}\bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{g\to q}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{2\pi}\bigg\{\Big(L-2\ln(1-z)\Big){\cal P}_{qg}(z)-T_{F}2z(1-z)\bigg\}\,,

(107)

with the logarithm
L
defined as

\displaystyle L=\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{p_{T}^{2}R^{2}}\,.

(108)

In Eq. (
107
), for the purpose of brevity, we have suppressed the jet initiating parton’s index
i
on the splitting functions. However, this can be easily understood by looking at the parton flavor written to the right of the arrow in the subscript of the matching function.

From the knowledge of the one-loop structure of the hard matching functions, we can compute the RGE which takes a DGLAP-like form. In order to solve it, we further decompose the complete one-loop matching function as

\mathcal{H}_{c\rightarrow i}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)=\mathcal{E}_{c}(p_{T}R,\mu)\,\mathcal{C}_{c\rightarrow i}(z,p_{T}R,\mu)\,.

(109)

Here,
\mathcal{E}_{c}
is the non-DGLAP piece and satisfies an RGE of the form

\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\ln\mathcal{E}_{c}(\mu,p_{T}R)=\gamma_{c}(\mu,p_{T}R)\,,

(110)

with the anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{c}
given as

\gamma_{c}=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{F}}{\pi}\left(-L-\frac{3}{2}\right)&\qquad:\qquad\text{for quark jet}\,,\\
\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\pi}\left(-C_{A}L-\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}\right)&\qquad:\qquad\text{for gluon jet}\,.\end{cases}

The function
\mathcal{C}_{c\rightarrow i}
satisfies a DGLAP evolution equation which reads as

\frac{d}{d\ln\mu^{2}}\!\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}_{q\rightarrow g}(\mu,p_{T}R,z)\\
\mathcal{C}_{g\rightarrow g}(\mu,p_{T}R,z)\end{pmatrix}\!=\!\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{4\pi}\!\int_{z}^{1}\frac{dz^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{gq}(z/z^{\prime})&~2N_{f}\mathcal{P}_{q\bar{q}}(z/z^{\prime})\\
\mathcal{P}_{qg}(z/z^{\prime})&~\mathcal{P}_{gg}(z/z^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}_{q\rightarrow g}(\mu,p_{T}R,z^{\prime})\\
\mathcal{C}_{g\rightarrow g}(\mu,p_{T}R,z^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}\!.

(111)

The solution of the hard functions from Eqs. (
110
) and (
111
) when combined with the jet and soft functions allows for the resummation of large logarithms of the measurement observable, leaving only logarithms of the jet radius
R
. These large logarithms can then resummed by evolving the result to the hard function scale. We provide the solution of the RGEs corresponding to the matching function in the section below.

D.5
Renormalization Group evolution for matching functions

{\cal E}_{c}(\mu,p_{T}R)=e^{-K_{\Gamma_{\cal H}}^{i}-K_{\gamma_{\cal H}}^{i}}{\cal E}_{c}(\mu_{\cal H},p_{T}R)\,,

(112)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}^{H}=4\,C_{i}\,,\,\,\Gamma_{1}^{H}=4\,C_{i}\left[\left(\frac{67}{9}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\right)C_{A}-\frac{20}{9}T_{R}N_{f}\right]\,,

\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{C}_{q\rightarrow g}(\mu,p_{T}R,z)\\

(116)

where the eigenvalues

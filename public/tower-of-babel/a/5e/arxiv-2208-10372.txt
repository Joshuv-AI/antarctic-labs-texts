On multiplicities of fibers of Fano fibrations Thanks: Keywords : log Calabi-Yau fibration, Fano fibration, rationally connected Calabi-Yau variety, boundedness.
Guodu Chen and Chuyu Zhou

Abstract
. In this note, we reduce various conjectures in birational geometry, including Shokurov’s conjecture on singularities of the base of log Calabi-Yau fibrations of Fano type and boundedness conjecture for rationally connected Calabi-Yau varieties, to a conjecture on multiplicities of fibers of Fano fibrations over curves.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14E30, 14J17, 14J27, 14J32, 14J45

1.
Introduction

We work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.

As the outcome of minimal model program, log Calabi-Yau fibrations and Mori-Fano fibrations play important roles in the classification of algebraic varieties, and it is natural to ask how the singularities behave on the total space and the base of these fibrations (e.g.
[
Bir18
]
). For Fano varieties, people have recently established many powerful theory including boundedness, complement theory (e.g.
[
Bir19
,
Bir21
]
), and K-moduli theory (e.g.
[
Xu21
]
). For Calabi-Yau varieties, however, many aspects are still mysterious, e.g. boundedness. The following conjecture due to M
c
kernan-Shokurov on the singularities of the base of log Calabi-Yau fibrations of Fano type is still widely open, we refer the readers to
[
Bir16
,
Bir18
,
HJL22
]
for the recent process.

Conjecture 1.1

(M
c
kernan-Shokurov)
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Then there exists a positive real number
\delta
depending only on
d
and
\epsilon
satisfying the following.

Assume that
(X,B)
is a log pair of dimension
d
and
\pi:X\to Z
is a contraction between projective normal varieties such that

(1)

(X,B)
is
\epsilon
-lc,

(2)

X
is of Fano type over
Z
, and

(3)

K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}0
.

Then
B_{Z}\leq 1-\delta
, where
B_{Z}
is the discriminant part of the canonical bundle formula for
(X,B)
over
Z
.

The above conjecture is known in some special cases. For example, if one requires that the coefficients of the boundary
B
are bounded from below, then the general fibers of the log Calabi-Yau fibrations lie in a log bounded family, and this leads to a positive answer to the conjecture (see
[
Bir16
, Theorem 1.4]
). In Birkar’s solution to this case, the key idea is to show that the multiplicities of fibers are bounded. Then it is also natural to ask the following question.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Then there exists a positive integer
m
depending only on
d
and
\epsilon
satisfying the following.

Assume that
(X,B)
is a log pair of dimension
d
and
\pi:X\to C
is a contraction such that

(1)

(X,B)
is
\epsilon
-lc,

(2)

X
is of Fano type over
C
where
C
is a curve, and

(3)

K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R},C}0
.

Then
m_{i}\leq m
for every
i
, where
\pi^{*}z=\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}
,
F_{i}
are the irreducible components of
\pi^{*}z
, and
z\in C
is a closed point.

It is clear that Conjecture
1.2
is a direct corollary of Conjecture
1.1
. As we will see later, Conjecture
1.1
provides a suitable inductive frame to prove boundedness results of some special kind of Calabi-Yau varieties (e.g. Corollary
3.3
).

We recall that a variety
X
is said to be
rationally connected
(RC for short) if any two general points can be connected by the image of a rational curve.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Then the set of rationally connected projective varieties
X
such that
\dim X=d
,
(X,B)
is
\epsilon
-lc and
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
for some boundary
B
, is bounded up to flops.

In Conjecture
1.3
, if we replace the condition
K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
with
-K_{X}-B
being nef, it is also expected that
X
is bounded (e.g.
[
MP04
, Conjecture 3.9]
). This case could be put in Calabi-Yau setting in the generalized sense. Then we naturally ask the following boundedness conjecture for generalized Calabi-Yau varieties.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Then the set of rationally connected projective varieties
X
such that
\dim X=d
,
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is an
\epsilon
-lc g-pair and
K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
for some
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
and b-
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\mathbf{M}
, is bounded in codimension one.

Remark 1.5
.

For all above conjectures, we do not put any assumption on the coefficients of
B
like DCC condition or the coefficients are bounded from below. All these conjectures are stated for pairs with
\mathbb{R}
coefficients, however, up to a perturbation, in the rest of this paper, we always assume that
\epsilon
and the coefficients of
B
are rational.

The purpose of this note is to reduce both Conjecture
1.1
, Conjecture
1.3
and Conjecture
1.4
to Conjecture
1.2
. In Proposition
3.5
, we will further reduce Conjecture
1.2
to a even ‘simpler’ setting which is stated for Fano fibrations (see Conjecture
3.4
). Thus all conjectures stated in this note will be implied by Conjecture
1.2
or Conjecture
3.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

(=Proposition
3.1
)

Conjecture
1.2
in dimension
d
implies Conjecture
1.1
in dimension
d
.

Theorem 1.7
.

(=Proposition
4.6
)

Conjecture
1.2
in dimension
d
implies Conjecture
1.3
in dimension
d
.

Theorem 1.8
.

(=Proposition
4.10
)

Conjecture
1.2
in dimension
d

1.4
in dimension
d
.

In Section 4, we also confirm Conjecture
1.4
for surfaces and threefolds. Moreover, we prove that those surfaces in Conjecture

are bounded. Remark that Conjecture

in dimension

[

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Divisors

Let
\mathbb{F}\in\{\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R}\}
. Let
X
be a normal variety and
\operatorname{WDiv}(X)
the free abelian group of Weil divisors on
X
. Then an
\mathbb{F}
-divisor is defined to be an element of
\operatorname{WDiv}(X)_{\mathbb{F}}:=\operatorname{WDiv}(X)\otimes\mathbb{F}
.

A
b-divisor
on
X
is an element of the projective limit

\textbf{WDiv}(X)=\lim_{Y\to X}\operatorname{WDiv}(Y),

where the limit is taken over all the pushforward homomorphisms
f_{*}:\operatorname{WDiv}(Y)\to\operatorname{WDiv}(X)
induced by proper birational morphisms
f:Y\to X
. In other words, a b-divisor
D
on
X
is a collection of Weil divisors
\textbf{D}_{Y}
on higher models
Y
of
X
that are compatible under pushforward. The divisors
\textbf{D}_{Y}
are called the
traces
of
D
on the birational models
Y.
A b-
\mathbb{F}
-divisor is defined to be an element of
\textbf{WDiv}(X)\otimes\mathbb{F}
.

The
Cartier closure
of an
\mathbb{F}
-Cartier
\mathbb{F}
-divisor
D
on
X
is the b-
\mathbb{F}
-divisor
\overline{D}
with trace
\overline{D}_{Y}=f^{*}D
for any proper birational morphism
f:Y\to X
. A b-
\mathbb{F}
-divisor
D
on
X
is
b-
\mathbb{F}
-Cartier
if
\textbf{D}=\overline{D_{Y}}
where
\overline{D_{Y}}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-Cartier
\mathbb{F}
-divisor on a birational model
Y
of
X
, in this situation, we say
D

descends
to
Y
. We say
D
is
b-nef
if it descends to a model
Y
over
X
, such that
\textbf{D}_{Y}
is nef. We say
D
is
b-abundant
if it descends to a model
Y
over
X
such that
\textbf{D}_{Y}
is abundant, i.e.,
\kappa(Y,\textbf{D}_{Y})=\kappa_{\sigma}(Y,\textbf{D}_{Y})
, see
[
Nak04
, Chapters II,V]
for the definitions.

2.2.
Singularities of g-pairs

We adopt the standard notation and definitions in
[
KM98
]
, and will freely use them.

Here, we discuss very briefly the analogous concepts for generalized pairs, while for further details we refer the readers to
[
BZ16
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
generalized sub-pair
(g-sub-pair for short)
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
consists of an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B
on
X
, and a nef b-
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\mathbf{M}
on
X
, such that
K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier. If
B
is effective, then we call
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
a
generalized pair
(g-pair for short).

Let
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
be a g-(sub-)pair and
f:W\to X
a log resolution of
(X,\operatorname{Supp}B)
to which
\mathbf{M}
descends. We may write

K_{W}+B_{W}+\mathbf{M}_{W}=f^{*}(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X})

for some
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B_{W}
on
W
. Let
E
be a prime divisor on
W
. The
log discrepancy
of
E
with respect to
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is defined as

a(E,X,B+\mathbf{M}):=1-\operatorname{mult}_{E}B_{W}.

We say
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is
\epsilon
-lc
(resp.,
lc, klt
) for some non-negative real number
\epsilon
if
a(E,X,B+\mathbf{M})\geq\epsilon
(resp.,
\geq 0,\ >0
) for any prime divisor
E
over
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
be a g-pair which is
a
-lc for some
a\geq 0
. Assume that
D
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor. The
a
-lc threshold
of
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
with respect to
D
is defined as

\displaystyle a\text{-}\operatorname{lct}(X,B+\mathbf{M};D):=\sup\{t\geq 0\mid

\displaystyle(X,(B+tD)+\mathbf{M})\text{ is $a$-lc}\}.

Definition 2.3
.

We say
\pi:X\to Z
is a
contraction
if
X
and
Z
are normal quasi-projective varieties,
\pi
is a projective morphism, and
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}

(\pi
is not necessarily birational
)
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
PS09
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow Z
be a contraction between normal varieties,
X
is said to be of
Fano type
over
Z
if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

there exists a klt pair
(X,B)
such that
-(K_{X}+B)
is ample over
Z
;

(2)

there exists a klt pair
(X,B^{\prime})
such that
K_{X}+B^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}0
and
-K_{X}
is big over
Z
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is an
\epsilon
-lc g-pair for some positive real number
\epsilon
, such that
\mathbf{M}
is b-abundant. Then there exists a boundary
\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta)
is
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-lc and
\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{R}}B+\mathbf{M}_{X}
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exist a higher model
f:X^{\prime}\to X
such that
\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime}}
is abundant. We may write

K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime}}=f^{*}(K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X})

for some
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
B^{\prime}
. Since
\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime}}
is abundant, we can choose a decomposition
\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}E^{\prime}+A^{\prime}
such that

(1)

E^{\prime}\geq 0
and
A^{\prime}\geq 0
is semi-ample, and

(2)

(X,B^{\prime}+E^{\prime}+A^{\prime})
is
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-sub-lc.

Set
\Delta:=f_{*}(B^{\prime}+E^{\prime}+A^{\prime})
, then
0\leq\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{R}}B+\mathbf{M}_{X}
. Moreover, by the negativity lemma, we see that

f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+E^{\prime}+A^{\prime}.

It follows that
(X,\Delta)
is
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-lc.
∎

2.3.
Canonical bundle formulas

Recall that for a sub-lc g-sub-pair
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
and a contraction
\pi:X\to Z
such that
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is a g-pair over the generic point of
Z
,
\dim Z>0
and
K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}\pi^{*}L_{Z}
for some
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
L_{Z}
on
Z
, there exists a g-sub-pair
(Z,B_{Z}+\mathbf{M}_{\pi})
such that

K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}\pi^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z}+\mathbf{M}_{\pi,Z}),

where
B_{Z}
is defined as

B_{Z}:=\sum_{P}(1-\operatorname{lct}(X/{Z}\ni\eta_{P},B+\mathbf{M};\pi^{*}P))P.

We call
B_{Z}
and
\mathbf{M}_{\pi}
the
discriminant divisor
and
moduli b-divisor
of
the canonical bundle formula
for
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
over
Z
respectively, see
[
Fil20
]
for more details. If
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is an lc (resp., klt) g-pair, then
(Z,B_{Z}+\mathbf{M}_{\pi})
is lc (resp., klt). We may refer the readers to
[
Bir19
,
Fil20
,
HL21
,
JLX22
]
for the recent process towards the canonical bundle formulas.

The following result is well-known
[
Amb05
]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Notation as above. Assume that
\mathbf{M}={\bf 0}
and
(X,B)
is klt. Then
\mathbf{M}_{\pi}
is b-nef and b-abundant.

2.4.
Boundedness

A collection
\mathcal{P}
of projective varieties is said to be
bounded
(resp.,
bounded in codimension one
) if there exists a projective morphism
h:\mathcal{X}\to S
between schemes of finite type such that each
X\in\mathcal{P}
is isomorphic (resp., isomorphic in codimension one) to
\mathcal{X}_{s}
for some closed point
s\in S
.

We say that a collection of projective log pairs
\mathcal{P}
is
log bounded
(resp.,
log bounded in codimension one
) if there is a quasi-projective scheme
\mathcal{X}
, a reduced divisor
\mathcal{B}
on
\mathcal{X}
, and a projective morphism
h:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow S
, where
S
is of finite type and
\mathcal{B}
does not contain any fiber, such that for every
(X,B)\in\mathcal{P}
, there is a closed point
s\in S
and an isomorphism (resp., isomorphism in codimension one)
f:\mathcal{X}_{s}\rightarrow X
such that the support of
\mathcal{B}_{s}:=\mathcal{B}|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
coincides with the support of
f_{*}^{-1}B
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{P}
is a set of (log) Calabi-Yau pairs, we also say
\mathcal{P}
is
(log) bounded modulo flops
when
\mathcal{P}
is (log) bounded in codimension one.

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that
\mathcal{P}
is a bounded family of projective RC varieties. Then there exists a positive integer
n
depending only on
\mathcal{P}
such that for any
X\in\mathcal{P}
and any Cartier divisor
D
on
X
with
D\equiv 0
, we have that
nD\sim 0
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a projective morphism
h:\mathcal{X}\to S
between schemes of finite type such that each
X\in\mathcal{P}
is isomorphic to
X_{s}
for some closed point
s\in S
. Then we can stratify
S
into a union of finitely many constructible subsets
S=\cup_{\alpha}S_{\alpha}
, such that over each
S_{\alpha}
,
\mathcal{X}\times_{S}S_{\alpha}
admits a resolution
\mu_{\alpha}:\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\times_{S}S_{\alpha}
, i.e.,
(\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha},E_{\alpha}:=\operatorname{Ex}(\mu_{\alpha}))
is a simple normal crossing, and each stratum is log smooth over
S_{\alpha}
. In particular,
(\mathcal{Y}_{s},E_{s})\rightarrow X_{s}
is a resolution for each
s\in S_{\alpha}
.

Fix an arbitrary
s\in S_{\alpha}
, and let
U_{\alpha}\subseteq S_{\alpha}
be a contractible analytic neighborhood of
s
. Let
f_{U_{\alpha}}:\mathcal{Y}_{U_{\alpha}}:=f^{-1}(U_{\alpha})\rightarrow U_{\alpha}
be the restriction of
f_{\alpha}:\mathcal{Y}_{\alpha}\to S_{\alpha}
. Since
f_{U_{\alpha}}
is topologically locally trivial and
U_{\alpha}
is contractible, the restriction map
H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{U_{\alpha}},\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism. Hence we see that
H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathbb{Z})
is independent of the choice of
s\in S
. Since
X
is rationally connected,
{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{Y}_{s})\cong H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, as
H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-module, we can find a positive integer
n
which only depends on
H^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\mathbb{Z})
such that if
D_{s}
is a numerically trivial Cartier divisor on
\mathcal{Y}_{s}
, then
nD_{s}\sim 0
. We can conclude that
n
has the required property. This finishes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Assume that
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is a projective
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau g-pair of dimension
d
and
\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is a birational contraction. Suppose that
X^{\prime}
is bounded in codimension one. Then
X
is bounded in codimension one.

Proof.

Replacing
X^{\prime}
, we may assume that
X^{\prime}
is bounded. Denote
B^{\prime}
to be the strict transform of
B
on
X^{\prime}
, then it is obvious that
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+\mathbf{M})
is an
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau g-pair. For any prime divisor
E
on
X
which is exceptional over
X^{\prime},

a(E,X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+\mathbf{M})=a(E,X,B+\mathbf{M})\leq 1
. So we can take a birational morphism
g:X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
extracting all prime divisors on
X
which are exceptional over
X^{\prime}
. In particular,
X^{\prime\prime}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial and isomorphic to
X
in codimension 1 . We may write

K_{X^{\prime\prime}}+B^{\prime\prime}+\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime\prime}}=g^{*}(K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime}}).

Then
(X^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime}+\mathbf{M})
is also an
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau g-pair and
X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is of Fano type. Apply
[
Bir18
, Theorem 2.2]
(see also
[
HJ22
, Theorem 3.1]
) to
X^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
, such
X^{\prime\prime}
belongs to a bounded family, and hence
X
is bounded in codimension one.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Assume that
(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is a projective g-pair and
\pi:X\to Z
is a contraction between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial varieties such that

•

(X,B+\mathbf{M})
is
\epsilon
-lc of dimension
d
,

•

\pi:X\to Z
is a Mori fiber space, and

•

K_{X}+B+\mathbf{M}_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
.

Suppose that
Z
is bounded in codimension one. Then
X
is bounded in codimension one.

Proof.

By assumption,
B+\mathbf{M}_{X}\not\equiv 0
. As
Z
is bounded in codimension one, there exists a projective normal variety
Z^{\prime}
that is bounded, such that
Z^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
Z
in codimension one. By the same arguments as in
[
DCS21
, Lemma 3.4]
, there exists a projective normal variety
X^{\prime}
such that

•

\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is isomorphic in codimension one, and

•

X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
is a Mori fiber space.

Let
B^{\prime}
be the strict transform of
B
on
X^{\prime}
, it is clear that
(X^{\prime},B^{\prime}+\mathbf{M})
is
\epsilon
-lc and
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{M}_{X^{\prime}}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
. Since
Z^{\prime}
is bounded,
X^{\prime}
is bounded according to
[
Bir18
, Theorem 2.2]
. Thus
X
is bounded in codimension one. This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is a small modification of
[
Bir18
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
d
be a positive integer, and
\epsilon,\delta
two positive real numbers. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective varieties. Consider the set
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
consisting of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective pairs
(X,B)
such that

(1)

(X,B)
is
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau of dimension
d
,

(2)

the non-zero coefficients of
B
are
\geq\delta
,

(3)

there is a contraction
\pi:X\to Z
with
Z\in\mathcal{P}
, and

(4)

X\rightarrow Z
factors as a tower of Fano fibrations such that
X_{i}\to X_{i+1}
is a Mori fiber space for each
0\leq i\leq l-1
.

If
\mathcal{P}
is bounded modulo flops, then
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is log bounded modulo flops.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a tower of Fano fibrations

X=:X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{l}:=Z,

i.e., the morphism
\phi_{i}:X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i+1}
is a Mori fiber space for any
0\leq i<l
. As
(X,B)
is
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau, there exists a boundary
B_{i}
on
X_{i}
such that
(X_{i},B_{i})
is klt log Calabi-Yau, and
K_{X_{i-1}}+B_{i-1}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{i-1}^{*}(K_{X_{i}}+B_{i})
for each
1\leq i\leq l
, see
[
Amb05
]
. Note here that
B_{i}\neq 0
for any
1\leq i\leq l-1
, as
\phi_{i}
are Fano fibrations. As
Z\in\mathcal{P}
is bounded modulo flops, there exist a projective normal variety
Z^{\prime}
which is bounded and a small birational contraction
Z\dashrightarrow Z^{\prime}
. Moreover, by
[
DCS21

The following lemma is useful in order to show that if a set of log Calabi-Yau pairs is bounded in codimension one, then the set of certain birational models of them remains bounded in codimension one. The proof is well-known to experts (cf.
[

, Theorem 5.1]
).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\epsilon
be a positive real number. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of projective
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau pairs
(Y,B_{Y})
such that
\operatorname{Supp}B_{Y}
is a prime divisor. Denote
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
to be the set consisting of Calabi-Yau projective varieties
X
such that there exists a birational morphism
h:Y\rightarrow X
, and
h^{*}K_{X}=K_{Y}+B_{Y}
, and
(Y,B_{Y})\in\mathcal{P}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is log bounded modulo flops. Then
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is bounded modulo flops.

Proof.

By assumption
\mathcal{P}
is bounded modulo flops, there is a quasi-projective scheme
\mathcal{Y}
, a prime divisor
\mathcal{B}
that does not contain any fiber of the projective morphism
\mathcal{Y}\to S
, where
S
is a disjoint union of finitely many normal varieties such that for every
(Y,B_{Y})\in\mathcal{P}
, there is a closed point
s\in S
and a small birational contraction
f:\mathcal{Y}_{s}\dashrightarrow Y
such that
\mathcal{B}_{s}:=\mathcal{B}|_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}}=f_{*}^{-1}B_{Y}
. We may assume that the set of those points
s
is dense in
S
. After decomposing
S
, we may also assume that
S
is smooth.

Fix a point
s\in S
corresponding to some
(Y,B_{Y})
,

K_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}}+f_{*}^{-1}B_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}f_{*}^{-1}(K_{Y}+B_{Y})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0

and therefore
(\mathcal{Y}_{s},f_{*}^{-1}B_{Y})
is an
\epsilon
-lc log Calabi-Yau pair. Now consider a log resolution
g:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
of
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B})
and denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}
the reduced sum of strict transform of
\mathcal{B}
and all
g
-exceptional divisors on
\mathcal{W}
. Consider the
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-lc pair
(\mathcal{W},(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}})
.
Up to shrinking
S
, we can suppose that
g_{s^{\prime}}:\mathcal{W}_{s^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{s^{\prime}}
is a log resolution for any
s^{\prime}\in S
. Write

K_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}+B_{s}=g_{s}^{*}(K_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}}+f_{*}^{-1}B_{Y})\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0

where

and
\operatorname{Supp}B_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}:=\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}|_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}
. We have

(K_{\mathcal{W}}+(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}})|_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}=K_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}+(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}-B_{s}.

Note that the support of

coincides with the support of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}
which is precisely the divisors on
\mathcal{W}_{s}
exceptional over

. Hence

has a good minimal model

and thus we can run a

-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor over

to get a relative good minimal model
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
over

by
[
HMX18
, Theorem 1.2]
. Note that
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}_{s}}
is contracted and hence
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{s}
is isomorphic to

in codimension one. Moreover, up to shrinking

, this MMP contracts
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{W}}
only, and

is a good minimal model of

for any

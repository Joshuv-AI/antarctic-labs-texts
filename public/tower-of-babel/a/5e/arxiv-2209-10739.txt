I
1. Derivation of cross-
k
non-adiabatic couplings

From the equ. 1 in the main text, we can derive the cross-
k
(
\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
) non-adiabatic coupling as:

\displaystyle V_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)}{\partial t}\right\rangle

(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}}\left\langle\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\cdot\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)\right\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{q}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}}\left\langle u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\mathbf{R}}e^{i(\mathbf{k^{\prime}-k+q})\cdot\mathbf{r}}\middle|u_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)\right\rangle\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{U}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{R}}(t)e^{i\theta_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}-k}}}

(3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{q}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}}\left\langle u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial H}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\middle|u_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)\right\rangle\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{U}_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}-k},\mathbf{R}}(t)e^{i\theta_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}-k}}}

(4)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{\Delta T\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{q}}}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)}\left\langle u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\Delta H\middle|u_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right\rangle e^{i\theta_{\mathbf{k^{\prime}-k}}}

(5)

where
\mathbf{R}
is the atomic position in the supercell and
\mathbf{q}
is the phonon wavevector. Equ. 1 to equ. 2 is based on the expansion of
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\left<\phi_{i}(t)\middle|H(t)\middle|\phi_{j}(t)\right>=0
(
i\neq j
). Here, we want to emphasize that the inner product between the adiabatic states of different wavevectors in equ. 1 will not vanish even when
\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
. Instead, it contains the information of electron-phonon interactions. The time evolution of eigen function
\left|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\right\rangle
changes not only the periodic part
u_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
(
\left|\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>=\left|u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}
), but also the wavevector
\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
, since the phonon modes will re-modulate the periodicity of electron wavefunctions. Equ. 2 to 3 is based on the approximated expansion of an atom’s trajectory to the phonon modes:
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{q}}}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{U}_{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{R}}(t)e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}e^{i\theta_{\mathbf{q}}}
(phonon mode index is not shown for simplicity). It is apparent that given the term
e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}
, only when
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
, the integral in equ. 3 will not vanish, which also satisfies the momentum conservation. From equ. 4 to 5, we assume that the phonon-mode vectors of different phonon wavevectors are similar, which can be replaced by the MD atomic trajectories
\Delta\mathbf{R}
i.e.
\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{U}_{\mathbf{R}}\Delta T=\Delta H
(
\Delta H\equiv H(t+\Delta T)-H(t)
). Equ. 5 is written with a discrete time in order to evaluate this term on the fly during a MD simulation. Such discrete time expression can also be derived as (
\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
):

\displaystyle V_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)}{\partial t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)}{\Delta\mathbf{R}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\right>-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\right>}{\left(\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right)\Delta\mathbf{R}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{H(t+\Delta T)}{\Delta\mathbf{R}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right>-\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{H(t)}{\Delta\mathbf{R}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right>}{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{1}{\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)}\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\Delta H}{\Delta\mathbf{R}}\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{\mathbf{R}}\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right>

(6)

where
\Delta H\equiv H(t+\Delta T)-H(t)
. It arrives at the above equ. 2. This is also how the cross-
k
matrix element is computed during MD.

II
2.
ab initio
MD calculation details

All the first-principle calculations including structural relaxation, self-consistent field calculation, and
ab initio
MD are performed with the plane-wave pseudopotential DFT package PWmat
Jia et al. 2013a
;
Jia et al. 2013b
with the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) functional
Perdew et al. 1996
. The silicon SG15 pseudopotential
Hamann 2013
with 45 Ryd plane-wave energy cut-off are used to converge the charge density of the bulk silicon. The
ab initio
MD simulations of different supercell sizes at different temperatures are performed with time step 2 fs. During MD, the quantities such as
\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|u_{j\mathbf{k}}(t+\Delta T)\right>
,
\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\Delta H(t)\middle|u_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right>
, and
\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)
are saved to files for the post-processing NAMD. The starting points of trajectories used in NAMD is chosen when the system temperatures are reaching equilibrium for the target temperature.

Assuming each unit cell only contains one atom and we have

such unit cells forming a supercell. The eigen energy evolution can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\Delta E_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\Delta H(t)\middle|\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>

(7)

\displaystyle\Delta H(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\frac{\partial V(t)}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\Delta\mathbf{R}

(8)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathbf{R}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{NM}}\Delta U_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}e^{i\omega_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}t}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}

(9)

where summation in equ. 8 is summed for all atoms in the supercell,
M
is the mass of the atom,
\lambda
and
\mathbf{q}
are the phonon mode and the wavevector of the supercell,
U_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}
is the phonon mode vector, and
\omega_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}
is the phonon energy. Meanwhile, the Bloch state can be expressed as
\left|\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}\right\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left|u_{i\mathbf{k}}\right\rangle e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}
with
\int_{\rm unitcell}u^{\ast}_{i\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r})u_{i\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}=1
. If we plugin equ. 8 and 9 into equ. 7, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta E_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\sum_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}}\left<\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial V(t)}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\Delta U_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}e^{i\omega_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}t}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\middle|\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>

(10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{NM}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{R}}\sum_{\lambda}\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial V(t)}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\middle|u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>\Delta U_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}=\Gamma}e^{i\omega_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}t}

(11)

Here, equ. 10 to equ. 11 needs the momentum conservation with only
\mathbf{q}=\Gamma
phonon mode contributing. By assuming that the Bloch wavefunction periodic part
\left|u_{i\mathbf{k}}\right\rangle
is fully delocalized in all
N
unit cells and are generally the same, and
U_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}=\Gamma}
is also similar for all unit cells. The summation of
\mathbf{R}
in equ. 11 can be replaced by
N
times and we obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta E_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{NM}}\sum_{\lambda}\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial V(t)}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\middle|u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>\Delta U_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}=\Gamma}e^{i\omega_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}t}

(12)

\displaystyle\left<\left|\Delta E_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right|^{2}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{NM}\sum_{\lambda}\left|\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial V(t)}{\partial\mathbf{R}}\middle|u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right>\right|^{2}\left|\Delta U_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}=\Gamma}\right|^{2}

(13)

Meanwhile, the magnitude of phonon mode vector is related to temperature approximately as:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}k_{b}T

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}M\omega^{2}_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}U^{2}_{\lambda,\mathbf{q}}

(14)

\displaystyle\left|U\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\propto\frac{k_{b}T}{M\omega^{2}_{\lambda\mathbf{q}}}

(15)

Therefore, the magnitude of
\sqrt{\left<\left|\Delta E_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\right|^{2}\right>}

V
5. Decoherence induced surface hopping (DISH)

In this work, we implement the DISH method originally proposed in Ref.
5
for our NAMD simulation. The wavefunction of the excited carrier
\left|\psi(t)\right\rangle
follows the Schrödinger’s equation and it is expanded with the adiabatic states (eigen states)
\left|\phi_{i}(t)\right\rangle
. The equation of motion for the coefficients used in the expansion is obtained as the following formula (index of
\mathbf{k}
is not shown). The classical path approximation (CPA) is used, which assumes the ions’ motion is always on the ground state potential energy surface. This is a fair approximation for large systems with heavy atoms and the wavefunctions are delocalized, particularly when we are only interested in the carrier dynamics. By using CPA, the NAMD simulation becomes a post-processing of
ab initio
MD. The overlap between the two eigen states in equ. 19 is computed during the
ab initio
MD and saved for post-processing NAMD.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\left|\psi(t)\right\rangle}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-iH\left|\psi(t)\right\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle\left|\psi(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}c_{i}(t)\left|\phi_{i}(t)\right\rangle

(18)

In the practical implementation, following the previous P-matrix scheme
Zheng and Wang 2019
, the time-dependent Hamiltonian (
H(t)
) within one MD step is obtained by a linear interpolation between the Hamiltonians of two successive MD steps:
H(T_{m})
and
H(T_{m+1})
, where
\Delta T=T_{m+1}-T_{m}
is MD time step set as 2 fs. The Hamiltonians at MD time step
T_{m}
and
T_{m+1}
can be re-built by using the overlap matrix elements computed from the
ab initio
MD and such two Hamiltonians share the same basis (index of
\mathbf{k}
is not shown):

\displaystyle\left<\phi_{i}(T_{m})\middle|H(T_{m}+\Delta T)\middle|\phi_{j}(T_{m})\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left<\phi_{i}(T_{m})\middle|\phi_{k}(T_{m}+\Delta T)\right>\left<\phi_{k}(T_{m}+\Delta T)\middle|\phi_{j}(T_{m})\right>\epsilon_{k}(T_{m}+\Delta T)

(19)

Here, it is necessary to note that the non-adiabatic couplings
V_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}=\left\langle\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\frac{\partial\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t)}{\partial t}\right\rangle\approx\left\langle\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right\rangle\frac{1}{\Delta T}-\delta_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
are not used directly in our NAMD implementation, instead, the overlap matrix
S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\equiv\left\langle\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\phi_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right\rangle
is used in equ. 19 to build Hamiltonians and evolve the wavefunctions. A few diagonalizations (usually less than 100) are performed within one MD step for
H(t)
for a more accurate Hamiltonian. In this case, the eigen states of
each
time step
dt
(
dt
is around 0.5 attosecond) are not chosen as the adiabatic states when evolving the above equ. 17. Instead, we choose the diagonalized eigen states within one MD step for the adiabatic states. At the start of each diagonalization, a basis change of adiabatic states is needed. As demonstrated in Ref.
6
, such implementation can significantly reduce the computational cost, particularly caused by diagonalizations. For our system, we find that 10 diagonalizations within one MD step already converge the calculation.

Between the two diagonalizations, the wavefunciton and the Hamiltonian is written with the same basis (using the eigen states of the diagonalization as the basis). The wavefunction is evolved following the Schrödinger’s equation (equ. 17) with the small time step
dt
as:
c_{i}(t+dt)=c_{i}(t)-i\sum_{j}\mathbf{H}_{ij}(t)c_{j}(t)dt-\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbf{H}(t)\mathbf{H}(t)\right]_{ij}c_{j}(t)dt^{2}
.
c_{i}(t)
is the coefficient of wavefunction expansion on the basis of adiabatic states (equ. 18). Here, it is necessary to note that at least the second order of
dt
in the above expansion is needed to evolve the wavefunction correctly. Meanwhile, a converged
dt
is needed. A
dt=0.5
attosecond to show a converged carrier thermalization process for our system.

6. Decoherence time

DISH method can realize both the decoherence and detailed balance using the wavefunction collapsing method. Following the decoherence process proposed in Ref.
5
, the decoherence time enters our NAMD implementation as a constant time
\tau
and a Poisson distribution of decoherence events with the expectation time-interval of
\tau
is carried out. Here, for the simple bulk silicon system, we use a fixed decoherence time
\tau=15
fs. This decoherence time is obtained following the simple method proposed in Ref.
7
using the energy gap standard deviations. Meanwhile, Ref.
8
also investigate a dynamically adjusted decoherence time between two eigen states for silicon quantum dots. A distribution of
\tau
from 10 fs to 25 fs is obtained with its maximum distribution around 15 fs. SI Fig. 7 indicates the hot carrier thermalization process with different decoherence time (5 fs, 15 fs, and 25 fs) starting from the same initial state. Different decoherence-time cooling processes show relatively small differences.

VII
7. Orthonormalization of overlap matrix elements

The overlap matrix is the key ingredient to build the Hamiltonian (equ. 19) and evolve the wavefunction. During the
ab initio
MD, only the adiabatic states (i.e. eigen states
\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}
) within a finite energy window in which we are interested are selected to compute the overlap matrix elements. Thus, these states can not be used directly to span the Hilbert space of the wavefunction for the excited carriers (
\left|\psi\right\rangle=\sum_{i\mathbf{k}}c_{i\mathbf{k}}\left|\phi_{i\mathbf{k}}\right\rangle
). An orthonormalization is required to enforce the unitarity for the overlap matrix elements. Meanwhile, as illustrated in equ. 3 and 5 of main text, it is possible to have the accidental degeneracy between state
\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)
and state
\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)
during the MD simulation, yielding an artificially large value. It is necessary to re-scale these spurious large cases during the NAMD simulation.

VII.1
7.1 Re-scale overlap matrix elements for accidental degenerate cases

When
\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
, if two states have an accidental degeneracy (
\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\approx\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)
), it is necessary to re-scale these large overlap matrix elements. During the
ab initio
MD, the matrix element
M_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\equiv\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|\Delta H\middle|u_{i\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)\right>
will be computed and saved to files as well as eigen energies
\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)
. Based on the equ. 3 in the main text, the overlap matrix element
S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
(used in equ. 19) is then computed with
M_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
and re-scaled as:

\displaystyle S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{k}}}}\sqrt{\frac{T_{0}}{T}}M_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}{\rm sign}(\Delta\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}})\middle/\left[\Delta\epsilon^{2}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}+\frac{2}{N_{\mathbf{k}}}\frac{T_{0}}{T}\left|M_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\right|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right.,\;\;\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}

(20)

where
\Delta\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is defined as
\epsilon_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}(t+\Delta T)-\epsilon_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)
.

When
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
, the overlap
s_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}\equiv\left<u_{i\mathbf{k}}(t)\middle|u_{j\mathbf{k}}(t+\Delta T)\right>
is computed and saved during the MD simulation following the previous implementations
Kang and Wang 2019
;
Zheng and Wang 2019
. Here, these diagonal-block parts should also subject to the
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{k}}}}\sqrt{\frac{T_{0}}{T}}
factor to be used in DISH, except for the few elements near the diagonal positions whose overlaps
|s_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}|
are close to 1 so as to
S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}
(the same state almost unchanged after
\Delta T
). To realize this property, the following re-scaling is applied as:

\displaystyle S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=s_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}\left[1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathbf{k}}}}\sqrt{\frac{T_{0}}{T}}\right)\left(1-\sin^{4}(\left|s_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}\right|^{2}\frac{\pi}{2})\right)\right],\;\;\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k^{\prime}}

(21)

VII.2
7.2 Orthonormalization procedure given the overlap matrix

With the diagonal (
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
) and off-diagonal (
\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}
) blocks of
S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
ready, an orthonormalization procedure is carried out to unitarize the overlap matrix. First, a standard normalization process is performed. For a given state
i\mathbf{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\frac{\left|S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\right|^{2}}{1+\alpha_{i\mathbf{k}}\left|S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=1

(22)

\alpha_{i\mathbf{k}}
of each state
i\mathbf{k}
can be solved using intersection method. After finding
\alpha_{i\mathbf{k}}
, the normalized
S^{\prime}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is defined as:

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}}{\sqrt{1+\alpha_{i\mathbf{k}}\left|S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}\right|^{2}}}

(23)

Then, following the standard Lowdin symmetric orthogonalization procedure,
S^{\prime}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is further orthogonalized into
\widetilde{V}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
.

VII.3
7.3 Orthonormalization procedure after adding deformation potential

For the accidental degenerate cases, the magnitude of
S^{\rm DP}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
(following equ. 5 in main text but the overlap matrix is computed) is usually very large. The normalization of
S^{\rm tot}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
with the DP directly added to the overlap matrix tends to underestimate the magnitude of the cross-
k
term. Therefore, the following procedure is carried out to add the DP induced non-adiabatic couplings.

Here, the overlap matrix
S^{\rm small}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is built as:

\displaystyle S^{\rm small}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}&\text{diagonal block when $\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k^{\prime}}$}\\
S^{\rm DP}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}&\text{off-diagnal block when $\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}$}\\
\end{cases}

(24)

where
S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}
and
S^{\rm DP}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
are all re-scaled following the above section 7.1 to remove spurious large values. The overlap matrix
S^{\rm small}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is then orthonormalized to obtain
\widetilde{S}^{\rm small}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
following the above section 7.2. Now, we add the cross-
k
overlap matrix elements. A new overlap matrix
S^{\rm new}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is built as:

\displaystyle S^{\rm new}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\widetilde{S}^{\rm small}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}&\text{diaognal block when $\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{k^{\prime}}$}\\
S_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}+\widetilde{S}^{\rm small}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}&\text{off-diagonal block when $\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k^{\prime}}$}\\
\end{cases}

(25)

The
S^{\rm new}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}
is further orthonormalized to obtain
\widetilde{S}^{\rm new}_{i\mathbf{k},j\mathbf{k}}
. The latter is then used in DISH algorithm.

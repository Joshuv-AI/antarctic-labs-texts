Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n\geq 2
is the dimension.
We study the following Dirichlet problems for second order linear elliptic equations in non-divergence and divergence forms:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a^{ij}D_{ij}u+b\cdot Du+cu&=f\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\\
u&=0\quad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} -{\rm div}\left(ADu\right)+\textup{div}\,(ub)+cu&=\textup{div}\,F\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\\
u&=0\,\,\,\,\qquad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Here
A=[a^{ij}]:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}
,
b=(b^{1},\dots,b^{n}):\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
c:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are locally integrable functions.
We also consider the dual problems of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}D_{ij}\left(a^{ij}v\right)-\textup{div}\,(vb)+cv&=\textup{div}\,G\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\\
v&=0\,\,\,\,\qquad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\textup{div}\,(ADv)-b\cdot Dv+cv&=\textup{div}\,G\quad\text{in $\Omega$},\\
v&=0\,\,\,\,\qquad\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

Throughout the paper, we assume that
A=[a^{ij}]
satisfies the following ellipticity and regularity conditions:

(A1)

A(x)
is a symmetric matrix for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(A2)

There exists a constant
0<\delta<1
such that

\delta|\xi|^{2}\leq A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq\delta^{-1}|\xi|^{2}
for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(A3)

The functions
a^{ij}
are of vanishing mean oscillation (VMO) for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Then a complete
L^{p}
-theory has been developed for both problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) when the lower order coefficients
b^{i}
and
c
are sufficiently regular and the domain
\Omega
is smooth. For instance, if
b=0
and
c=0
identically, or more generally if
b,c
are bounded and
c
is nonnegative, then unique solvability in
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) and in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
of (
1.2
) are proved in
[
2
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
20
]
for any
1<p<\infty
.
Recently, an
L^{p}
-theory was extended to more general
b
and
c
in critical Lebesgue or weak Lebesgue spaces. Precisely, it was shown by Krylov
[
21
]
that if
b\in L^{n}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
c\in L^{n}(\Omega)
, and
c\geq 0
, then the problem (
1.1
) is uniquely solvable in
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
for any
1<p<n
; that is, for any
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
1<p<n
, there exists a unique strong solution
u
in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,p}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
). The corresponding
W^{1,p}
-results for (
1.2
) have been proved only for
1<p<n
too. For more details, see the papers
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
23
]
.

It has been well known that an
L^{p}
-theory fails to hold for the limiting case when
p=1
; indeed, there exists
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
such that the Poisson equation

-\Delta u=f\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega

(1.5)

has no solutions in
W_{loc}^{2,1}(\Omega)
. As a simple example, we take an
L^{1}
-function

f(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{n}\left|\log|x|\right|^{1+\varepsilon}}

defined on
\Omega=B_{1}
, the open unit ball centered at the origin, where
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{n-1}{n}
. Then the Poisson equation (
1.5
) has a radial solution
v=v(r)
satisfying

-v^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{n-1}{r}v^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{r^{n}|\log r|^{1+\varepsilon}}

for
0<r<1
. Since

|v^{\prime}(r)|=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{t|\log t|^{1+\varepsilon}}dt\sim\frac{1}{\varepsilon r^{n-1}|\log r|^{\varepsilon}}\quad\mbox{as}\,\,r\to 0,

it follows that
|Dv|\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(B_{\rho})\setminus L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(B_{\rho})
for any
\rho<1
. Now, if
u
is any solution of (
1.5
), then
u-v
is harmonic and so smooth in
\Omega
. Hence any solution
u
of (
1.5
) should satisfy
|Du|\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(B_{\rho})\setminus L^{\frac{n}{n-1}}(B_{\rho})
for any
\rho<1
. This implies, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality (see e.g.
[
12
]
)), that there exist no solutions in
W_{loc}^{2,1}(\Omega)
of (
1.5
). To find a right regularity class for solutions of (
1.5
) with
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, let
w=\Gamma\ast f
be the Newtonian potential of

over
\Omega
, where
\Gamma
denotes the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation (see
[
14
]
). Then since
D\Gamma\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\Gamma\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for any
p<\frac{n}{n-2}
, it follows from Young’s convolution inequality in Lorentz spaces

(see ?THM?
in Section 2) that

belongs to the Sobolev-Lorentz space

W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
. Hence, if
u
is any solution of (
1.5
), then
u\in W_{loc}^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
, that is,
u\in W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega_{0})
for any open set
\Omega_{0}
with
\overline{\Omega_{0}}\subset\Omega
.
It turns out that if
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1}
-domain, then for each

there exists a unique weak solution

of (

). Here

is defined as the space of all functions in

having zero trace on
\partial\Omega
. See Section 2 for more details on the Sobolev-Lorentz spaces

and
W_{0}^{1,p,q}(\Omega)
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.

The first purpose of this paper is to establish existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in
W_{0}^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
of the general elliptic problem (
1.1
) for any data

in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, which of course requires some additional assumptions on the coefficients
A
,
b
, and

.
First of all, in addition to (A1)-(A3), we need to assume at least that

\textup{div}\,A\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mbox{that is},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}a^{ij}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for each}\,\,j=1,\ldots,n,

which allows us to write the equation in (
1.1
) as a divergence-form equation

-\textup{div}\,(ADu)+\tilde{b}\cdot Du+cu=f\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,

(1.6)

where
\tilde{b}=\textup{div}\,A+b
.
Then by a weak solution in
W_{0}^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
) with

belonging to
L^{1}(\Omega)
or more generally

being a distribution on
\Omega
, we mean a function
u\in W_{0}^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\tilde{b}\cdot Du,cu\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega)

and

\int_{\Omega}\left(ADu\cdot D\varphi+\varphi\tilde{b}\cdot Du+cu\varphi\right)dx=\langle f,\varphi\rangle

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Note here that
|Du|\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
and
u\in L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)
for any finite
p\leq\frac{n}{n-2}
, due to the Sobolev embedding theorem. Hence in view of Hölder’s inequality or the duality theorem in Lorentz spaces (see Lemmas
and
), we are forced to impose that

\tilde{b}\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad c\in L^{\frac{n}{2},1}(\Omega)\cap L^{s}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for some}\,\,1<s<\frac{3}{2}.

(1.7)

We shall prove a unique solvability result in
W_{0}^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)
for the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) with
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
under the condition (
1.7
) on
A
,
b
, and
c
.
In fact, this is an easy corollary of the following result for weak solutions of the problems (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) in divergence form.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Assume that

,

c\in L^{\frac{n}{2},1}(\Omega)\cap L^{s}(\Omega)
for some
1<s<\frac{3}{2}
, and
c\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

(i)

For each
F\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique weak solution
u
in
W^{1,n,1}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1.2
). Moreover,

\|u\|_{W^{1,n,1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{n,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C=C(n,\Omega,A,b,c)
.

(ii)

For each
G\in L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique weak solution
v
in
W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1.4
). Moreover,

\|v\|_{W^{1,\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|G\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where

.

Given

, define

. Then

\textup{div}\,F=\Delta(\Gamma*f)=f\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega.

Moreover, by Young’s convolution inequality (?THM?
),

\|F\|_{L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n)\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, as an immediate consequence of Theorem
(ii), we obtain the following result for the problem (
1.1
) with
L^{1}
-data.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
-domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
.
Assume that
\textup{div}\,A+b\in L^{n,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})

.

2
Preliminaries

We begin with introducing some standard notations.

For two quasi-Banach spaces
A
and
B
with
A\subset B
, we write
A\hookrightarrow B
if
A
is continuously embedded into
B
, that is, there is a constant
C
such that
\|u\|_{B}\leq C\|u\|_{A}
for all
u\in A
. Also, if
A\hookrightarrow B
and
B\hookrightarrow A
, we write
A=B
. We denote by
A^{*}
the dual space of
A
. The dual pairing of
A^{*}
and
A
will be denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
without specifying the spaces for the sake of simplicity.

2.1
Lorentz spaces

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space.
For a measurable function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, we define its distribution function
d_{f}
by

d_{f}(\alpha)=\mu\left(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\alpha\}\right)\quad(\alpha>0).

The decreasing rearrangement of
f
is defined on
(0,\infty)
by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\left\{\alpha>0:d_{f}(\alpha)\leq t\right\}.

For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, we define the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(X)
as the set of all measurable functions
f
on
X
such that the quantity

\|f\|_{p,q;X}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}&\text{if}\,\,q<\infty,\\
\quad\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)&\text{if}\,\,q=\infty,\end{cases}

is finite.
Then
L^{p,q}(X)
is a quasi-Banach space under the quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p,q;X}
.
In fact,
\|\cdot\|_{p,q;X}
is normable when
1<p<\infty
(see
[
3
, Section 4.4]
and
[
15
, Section 1.4]
, e.g.).
Moreover,
L^{\infty,q}(X)=\{0\}
for
1\leq q<\infty
and
L^{p,q_{1}}(X)\hookrightarrow L^{p,q_{2}}(X)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\infty
.
If
\mu(X)
is finite, then
L^{p_{1},\infty}(X)\hookrightarrow L^{p_{2},1}(X)
for
1\leq p_{2}<p_{1}\leq\infty
.
It is also well known that
L^{p,p}(X)
coincides with the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(X)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, that is,
\|\cdot\|_{p,p;X}
is equivalent to the
L^{p}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p;X}
.
It is easy to check that

\left\||f|^{\alpha}\right\|_{p,q;X}=\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha p,\alpha q;X}^{\alpha}

(2.1)

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
\alpha\geq\max(1/p,1/q)
.

It is standard (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 5.3.1]
) that a Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(X)
is the real interpolation space of two Lebesgue spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

Let

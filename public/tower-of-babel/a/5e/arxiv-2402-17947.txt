Quantitative asymptotic regularity of the VAM iteration with error terms for m m -accretive operators in Banach spaces
Paulo Firmino, Laurenţiu Leuştean, [2mm] Departamento de Matemática, Faculdade de Ciências, Universidade de Lisboa

Abstract
In this paper we obtain, by using proof mining methods, quantitative results on the asymptotic regularity of the viscosity approximation method (VAM) with error terms for m m -accretive operators in Banach spaces. For concrete instances of the parameter sequences, linear rates are computed by applying a lemma due to Sabach and Shtern. Keywords: Quantitative asymptotic regularity; Viscosity approximation method; m m -accretive operators; Proof mining. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010 : 47H05, 47H09, 47J25.

Introduction

Let
X
be a normed space,
A:X\to 2^{X}
be an accretive operator with a nonempty set
of zeros, and
C\subseteq X
be a nonempty closed convex subset of
X
such that

\overline{\mathrm{dom}A}\subseteq C\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)
for all
\gamma>0
. Xu et al.
[
26
]
studied recently the following iteration:

\mathrm{VAM}\qquad x_{0}=x,\quad x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}x_{n},

(1)

where
x\in C
,
f:C\to C
is an
\alpha
-contraction for
\alpha\in[0,1)
,
(\lambda_{n})
is
a sequence in
(0,\infty)
,
(\alpha_{n})
is a sequence in
[0,1]
, and, for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}
is
the resolvent of order
\lambda_{n}
of
A
.

The VAM iteration is an instance of the viscosity approximation method
applied to resolvents of accretive operators in Banach spaces (see, for example,
[
16
,
21
,
22
,
2
,
19
,
25
]
). If one takes
f(x)=u\in X
in
(
1
), one gets the Halpern-type Proximal point algorithm HPPA, introduced by
Kamimura and Takahashi
[
10
]
and Xu
[
24
]
, a modification of the Proximal point
algorithm that was studied in a series of papers in recent years. Thus, VAM is a viscosity
version of the HPPA.

Xu et al.
[
26
]
proved, in the setting of uniformly convex
and/or uniformly Gâteaux differentiable Banach spaces,
strong convergence results for the VAM iteration towards a zero of
A
, extending results for the HPPA obtained by
Aoyama and Toyoda
[
1
]
.

As it is the case with numerous convergence proofs, an intermediate step is to obtain the asymptotic regularity of the iteration.
Asymptotic regularity was introduced by Browder and Petryshyn
[
5
]
for the Picard iteration and extended to general iterations by Borwein, Reich, and Shafrir
[
3
]
.
By inspecting the proofs from
[
26
]
, one can see that
asymptotic regularity of the VAM iteration holds, under some hypotheses on
(\alpha_{n})
,
(\lambda_{n})
,
in the more general setting of Banach spaces.

In this paper we prove quantitative asymptotic regularity results for the VAMe iteration for
m
-accretive operators, defined by adding error terms to the VAM iteration (see (
5
)). These quantitative results provide uniform rates of
asymptotic regularity,
\left(J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}\right)
-asymptotic regularity and, for all

m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
J_{\lambda_{m}}^{A}
-asymptotic regularity for VAMe.
We compute linear such rates for concrete instances of the parameter sequences
(\alpha_{n})
,

(\lambda_{n})
, as an application of a lemma of Sabach and Shtern
[
23
]
. As VAM and HPPA for
m
-accretive operators are particular cases of our VAMe iteration, we obtain rates for these iterations, too.
Furthermore, as an immediate consequence of our quantitative results, we obtain
qualitative asymptotic regularity results for the VAMe iteration.

The results from the paper are obtained by applying methods of proof mining, a research program
concerned with the extraction, by using proof-theoretic techniques, of new quantitative and
qualitative information from mathematical proofs. We refer to Kohlenbach’s textbook
[
12
]

for details on proof mining and to
[
13
,
14
]
for surveys of recent applications in nonlinear analysis and optimization.
Finally, let us remark that proof mining was applied recently by Kohlenbach and Pinto
[
15
]
to obtain quantitative results, providing rates of metastability, for viscosity approximation methods in
W
-hyperbolic spaces.

Preliminaries

Let
X
be a normed space and
A:X\to 2^{X}
be a set-valued operator on
X
. As usual, we identify the operator
A
with its graph

\mathrm{gra}A=\{(x,y)\in X\times X\mid y\in Ax\}
. Let
\mathrm{dom}A=\{x\in X\mid Ax\neq\emptyset\}
be the domain of
A
and

\mathrm{ran}A=\bigcup_{x\in X}Ax
be the range of
A
. Furthermore, we denote by
\mathrm{zer}A
the set of zeros of
A
, that is

\mathrm{zer}A=\{x\in X\mid 0\in Ax\}
. The definition of the inverse
A^{-1}
of
A
is given through its graph:

\mathrm{gra}A^{-1}=\{(y,x)\in X\times X\mid(x,y)\in\mathrm{gra}A\}=\{(y,x)\in X\times X\mid y\in Ax\}
. If
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
B
is another set-valued operator on
X
, then

\lambda A=\{(x,\lambda y)\mid x\in X,y\in Ax\}
and
A+B=\{(x,y+z)\mid x\in X,y\in Ax,z\in Bx\}
.
For every
\gamma>0
, the resolvent
J_{\gamma}^{A}
of order
\gamma
of
A
is defined by
J_{\gamma}^{A}=(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)^{-1}
, where
\mathrm{Id}

is the identity operator on
X
. One can easily verify that
\mathrm{dom}J_{\gamma}^{A}=\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)
and
\mathrm{ran}J_{\gamma}^{A}=\mathrm{dom}A
.

Let us recall that if
\emptyset\neq C\subseteq X
and
T:C\to X
is a mapping, we denote by
\mathrm{Fix}(T)

the set of fixed points of
T
and
T
is said to be nonexpansive if
\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in X
.

An operator
A
is said to be
accretive

[
4
,
11
]
if for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}A
,
u\in Ax
,
v\in Ay
, and
\gamma>0
,

\|x-y+\gamma(u-v)\|\geq\|x-y\|.

It is well-known that for any accretive operator
A
and for all
\gamma>0
,

J_{\gamma}^{A}:\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)\to\mathrm{dom}A
is a nonexpansive mapping such that
\mathrm{Fix}(J_{\gamma}^{A})=\mathrm{zer}A

(see, for example,
[
9
, Corollary 3.4.1]
and
[
9
, Proposition 6.7.1]
for proofs).

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
A
is an accretive operator.
Let
\lambda,\gamma>0
.

(i)

If
x\in\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\lambda A)
, then
\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}x+\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)J^{A}_{\lambda}x\in\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)
and

\displaystyle J^{A}_{\lambda}x

\displaystyle=J^{A}_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}x+\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)J^{A}_{\lambda}x\right).

(2)

(ii)

For all
x\in\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\lambda A)\cap\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)
,

\displaystyle\|J^{A}_{\gamma}x-J^{A}_{\lambda}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\left|1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right|\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda}x-x\right\|.

(3)

Proof.

For a proof of (
2
) see
[
9
, Proposition 3.4.1]
. (
3
) follows immediately from (
2
) and the fact that
J^{A}_{\gamma}
is nonexpansive:

\displaystyle\|J^{A}_{\gamma}x-J^{A}_{\lambda}x\|

\displaystyle=\left\|J^{A}_{\gamma}x-J^{A}_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}x+\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)J^{A}_{\lambda}x\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}x-\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right)J^{A}_{\lambda}x\right\|=\left|1-\frac{\gamma}{\lambda}\right|\left\|x-J^{A}_{\lambda}x\right\|.

∎

An
m
-accretive
operator is an accretive operator
A
that satisfies
\mathrm{ran}(\mathrm{Id}+\gamma A)=X
for all
\gamma>0
.
It follows that, for an
m
-accretive operator
A
, (
2
) and (
3
) hold for all
x\in X
.

3
Quantitative notions and lemmas

Let us recall the main quantitative notions that will be used in this paper.
Suppose that
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence in a metric space
(X,d)
. A mapping
\varphi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}

is said to be

(i)

a Cauchy modulus of
(a_{n})
if for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
n\geq\varphi(k)
,

d(a_{n+p},a_{n})\leq\frac{1}{k+1}\quad\text{holds for all~}p\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(ii)

a rate of convergence of
(a_{n})
(towards
a\in X
) if for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
n\geq\varphi(k)
,

d(a_{n},a)\leq\frac{1}{k+1}.

Obviously,
(a_{n})
is Cauchy iff
(a_{n})
has a Cauchy modulus, and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=a
iff

(a_{n})
has a rate of convergence towards
a
.

Assume that
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
is a series of nonnegative real numbers and

\left(\tilde{b}_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n}b_{i}\right)
is the sequence of partial sums.
Then a Cauchy modulus of the series is a Cauchy modulus of
\left(\tilde{b}_{n}\right)
.
A rate of divergence of the series is a mapping
\theta:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}b_{i}\geq n
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is clear that

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
diverges iff it has a rate of divergence.

Let
(y_{n})
be a sequence in a metric space
(X,d)
,
\emptyset\neq C\subseteq X
,
T:C\to C
,
(T_{n}:C\to C)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be
a countable family of mappings, and
\Phi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
. We say that

(i)

(y_{n})
is asymptotically regular with rate
\Phi

(
or
\Phi
is a rate of asymptotic regularity of
(y_{n})
)

if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n},y_{n+1})=0
with rate of convergence
\Phi
;

(ii)

(y_{n})
is
T
-asymptotically regular with rate
\Phi

(
or
\Phi
is a rate of
T
-asymptotic regularity of
(y_{n})
)

if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n},Ty_{n})=0
with rate of convergence
\Phi
;

(iii)

(y_{n})
is
(T_{n})
-asymptotically regular with rate
\Phi

(
or
\Phi
is a rate of
(T_{n})
-asymptotic regularity of
(y_{n})
)

if
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n},T_{n}y_{n})=0
with rate of convergence
\Phi
.

Useful lemmas on sequences of real numbers

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(b_{n})
is a sequence in
[0,1]
and
\theta
is a rate of divergence for
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}
, then

\theta(n)\geq n-2
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\theta(n)<n-2
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It follows that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\theta(n)}b_{i}\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-2}b_{i}\leq n-1<n,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

, and

\varphi(k)=\max\{\varphi_{1}(2p(k+1)-1),\varphi_{2}(2q(k+1)-1)\}.

Proof.

Let

and all

\displaystyle c_{n+p}-c_{n}

\displaystyle=p(a_{n+p}-a_{n})+q(b_{n+p}-b_{n})\leq p\cdot\frac{1}{2p(k+1)}+q\cdot\frac{1}{2q(k+1)}=\frac{1}{k+1}.

∎

]

Let

VAM with errors for resolvents of
m
-accretive operators in Banach spaces

Let
X
be a normed space,
A:X\to 2^{X}
be an
m
-accretive operator such that
\mathrm{zer}A\neq\emptyset
,
and
f:X\to X
be an
\alpha
-contraction for
\alpha\in[0,1)
,
that is
\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq\alpha\|x-y\|
for all
x,y\in X
.

We consider the iteration
(x_{n})
defined as follows:

\mathrm{VAMe}\qquad x_{0}=x\in X,\quad x_{n+1}=\alpha_{n}f(x_{n})+(1-\alpha_{n})J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}x_{n}+e_{n},

(5)

where
(\alpha_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence in
[0,1]
,
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence in
(0,\infty)
, and
(e_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is
a sequence in
X
. Hence,
(x_{n})
is obtained from the VAM iteration studied in
[
26
]

by adding error terms
e_{n}
.

For every
z\in\mathrm{zer}A
, let
(K_{z,n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of real numbers defined as follows:

K_{z,0}=\max\left\{\|x-z\|,\frac{\|f(z)-z\|}{1-\alpha}\right\},\quad K_{z,n}=K_{z,0}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\|e_{i}\|\text{~~for all~}n\geq 1.

(6)

Thus,
K_{z,n+1}=K_{z,n}+\|e_{n}\|
for all
n\geq 0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
z\in\mathrm{zer}A
and
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(i)

\|x_{n}-z\|,\|f(x_{n})-z\|\leq K_{z,n}
;

(ii)

\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|\leq 2K_{z,n+1}
;

(iii)

\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-z\right\|\leq K_{z,n}
;

(iv)

\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-x_{n}\right\|,\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-f(x_{n})\right\|\leq 2K_{z,n}
.

Proof.

(i)

We prove the two inequalities simultaneously by induction on
n
.

n=0
:
\|x_{0}-z\|\leq K_{z,0}
follows by (
6
). Furthermore, applying the fact that

f
is an
\alpha
-contraction and (
6
), we get that

\|f(x_{0})-z\|\leq\|f(x_{0})-f(z)\|+\|f(z)-z\|\leq\alpha\|x_{0}-z\|+(1-\alpha)K_{z,0}\leq K_{z,0}.

n\Rightarrow n+1
: We have that

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-z\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\alpha_{n}(f(x_{n})-z)+(1-\alpha_{n})(J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}x_{n}-J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}z)+e_{n}\right\|\quad\text{~~as~}J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}z=z

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{n}\|f(x_{n})-z\|+(1-\alpha_{n})\|x_{n}-z\|+\|e_{n}\|\quad\text{~~as~}J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A}\text{~is nonexpansive}

\displaystyle\leq K_{z,n}+\|e_{n}\|\quad\text{~~by the induction hypothesis}

\displaystyle=K_{z,n+1}.

Moreover,
\|f(x_{n+1})-z\|\leq\alpha\|x_{n+1}-z\|+\|f(z)-z\|\leq K_{z,n+1}
.

(ii)

\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|\leq\|x_{n}-z\|+\|x_{n+1}-z\|\leq K_{z,n}+K_{z,n+1}\leq 2K_{z,n+1}
.

(iii)

\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-z\right\|=\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}z\right\|\leq\|x_{n}-z\|\leq K_{z,n}
.

(iv)

\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-x_{n}\right\|\leq\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-z\right\|+\|x_{n}-z\|\leq 2K_{z,n}
and

\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-f(x_{n})\right\|\leq\left\|J^{A}_{\lambda_{m}}x_{n}-z\right\|+\|f(x_{n})-z\|\leq 2K_{z,n}
.

∎

Quantitative hypotheses on the parameter sequences

We consider the following hypotheses on the parameter sequences

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\frac{2}{(1-\alpha)(n+J)},\qquad\text{where~}J=2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil.

As
(\alpha_{n})
is decreasing, we have that
\alpha_{n}\leq\alpha_{0}=\frac{2}{(1-\alpha)J}\leq 1
. Thus,
\alpha_{n}
is a sequence in

[0,1]
.

5.3.1
A first example

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, consider

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=\lambda>0\text{~and~}e_{n}=0.

Then
(x_{n})
is the VAM iteration with a single mapping

J_{\lambda}^{A}
, which is nonexpansive.
It follows that
(x_{n})
is a particular case of the viscosity
version of the Halpern iteration (where one considers an arbitrary nonexpansive mapping
T

instead of
J_{\lambda}
) introduced by Xu
[
25
]
and studied by Sabach and
Shtern
[
23
]
under the name of sequential averaging method (SAM). As an application of
[
23
, Lemma 3]
,
Sabach and Shtern obtained linear rates of (
T
-)asymptotic regularity for SAM.
Cheval and the second author
[
7
]
applied Lemma
3.4
to compute
such linear rates in the more general setting of
W
-hyperbolic spaces; these rates hold in our setting, too.

Consider the following mappings, defined in
[
7
, Section 3.2, (15), (16)]
, with notations adapted to this paper:

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(k)

\displaystyle=4K_{z}^{*}\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil^{2}(k+1)-2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil,

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}(k)

\displaystyle=\left(4K_{z}^{*}\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil^{2}+4K_{z}^{*}\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil\right)(k+1)-2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil,

where
z\in\mathrm{zer}A
and
K_{z}^{*}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
satisfies (
13
).

Then
(x_{n})
is asymptotically regular with rate
\Phi_{0}
and
J_{\lambda}^{A}
-asymptotically regular with rate
\Psi_{0}
.
As
\lambda_{n}=\lambda
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, obviously
(J_{\lambda_{n}}^{A})
-asymptotic regularity and
J_{\lambda_{m}}^{A}
-asymptotic regularity (for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

coincide with
J_{\lambda}^{A}
-asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
.

5.3.2
A second example

Let us take, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n+J}{n+J-1}\text{~and~}e_{n}=\frac{1}{(n+J)^{2}}e^{*},\qquad\text{where~}e^{*}\in X.

Since
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+J)^{2}}<\frac{1}{J-1}
, it follows that
(H3e_{n})
holds with

\displaystyle E=\left\lceil\frac{\|e^{*}\|}{J-1}\right\rceil
.

Let
z\in\text{zer}A
and
K_{z}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
satisfying

K_{z}\geq\max\left\{\|x-z\|,\frac{\|f(z)-z\|}{1-\alpha}\right\}+\left\lceil\frac{\|e^{*}\|}{J-1}\right\rceil.

(14)

Proposition 5.9
.

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|\leq\frac{3JK_{z}+\|e^{*}\|}{(1-\alpha)(n+J)}.

(15)

Thus,

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(k)

\displaystyle=\left(3JK_{z}+\left\lceil\|e^{*}\|\right\rceil\right)\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil(k+1)-J

\displaystyle=6K_{z}\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil^{2}(k+1)+\left\lceil\|e^{*}\|\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil(k+1)-2\left\lceil\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\rceil

is a linear rate of asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
.(
i
), we have that Lemma
4.1

and (
7
) hold with
K_{z}
defined as above instead of
K_{z,n}
or
K_{z,n+1}
.
Applying (
7
), we get that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-(1-\alpha)\alpha_{n+1})\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|+P_{z},

where

\displaystyle P_{z}

\displaystyle=2K_{z}\left(|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|+(1-\alpha_{n+1})\left|1-\frac{\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|\right)+\|e_{n+1}-e_{n}\|

\displaystyle=|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|\left(2K_{z}\left(1+\frac{(1-\alpha_{n+1})}{|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|}\left|1-\frac{\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|\right)+\frac{\|e_{n+1}-e_{n}\|}{|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|}\right).

As

\displaystyle|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}=\frac{2}{(1-\alpha)(n+J)(n+1+J)},

\displaystyle 1-\alpha_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\alpha)(n+1+J)-2}{(1-\alpha)(n+1+J)},

\displaystyle\left|1-\frac{\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}=\frac{1}{(n+J)^{2}},

we have that

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\alpha_{n+1})}{|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|}\left|1-\frac{\lambda_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\big((1-\alpha)(n+1+J)-2\big)(n+J)}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{(n+J)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+J-1}{2(n+J)}<\frac{1}{2}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{\|e_{n+1}-e_{n}\|}{|\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2(n+J)+1)\|e^{*}\|(1-\alpha)}{2(n+J)(n+J+1)}\leq\frac{\|e^{*}\|}{n+J}\leq\frac{\|e^{*}\|}{J}.

It follows that for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle<(1-(1-\alpha)\alpha_{n+1})\|x_{n+1}-x_{n}\|+(\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+1})\left(3K_{z}+\frac{\|e^{*}\|}{J}\right).

One can easily see that Lemma
3.4
can be applied with

,

,

,

,

to conclude that (
15
) holds and,
as a consequence,
\Phi_{0}
is a rate of asymptotic regularity of
(x_{n})
.
∎

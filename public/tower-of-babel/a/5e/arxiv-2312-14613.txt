Absolute and convective instabilities in a liquid film over a substrate moving against gravity Preprint: APS/123-QED
Fabio Pino, Miguel A. Mendez, Benoit Scheid

Abstract
The drag-out problem for small Reynolds numbers ( Re {\rm Re} ) admits the Landau-Levich-Derjaguin (LLD) solution for small capillary numbers ( Ca {\rm Ca} ), and Derjaguin’s solution for large Ca {\rm Ca} . We investigate whether these solutions are absolutely or convectively unstable, solving the Orr-Sommerfeld eigenvalue problem. For Derjaguin’s solution, we show that the LLD solution is convectively unstable for Ka < 17 {\rm Ka}<17 and absolutely unstable for Ka ≳ 0.15 ​ Re 1.7 {\rm Ka}\gtrsim 0.15\,{\rm Re}^{1.7} for Re > 10 {\rm Re}>10 . For water ( Ka = 3400 {\rm Ka}=3400 ), the LLD solution is always convectively unstable. The absolute instability is observed only when the dip-coated film is additionally fed from above.



I
Introduction

The drag-out problem consists of estimating the thickness of the liquid clinging to a substrate withdrawn from a bath at constant velocity
U_{p}

(
Landau and Levich 1942
;
Derjaguin and Levi 1964
;
Derjaguin 1944
)
. Its solution is central in the dip-coating process, where a protective liquid layer is deposited on a substrate
(
Weinstein and Ruschak 2004
)
.
In most applications, the smoothness of the deposited layer is the principal criterion for quality. Understanding and predicting the evolution of free surface instabilities could ease control and sensing tasks
(
Pier 2003
;
Huerre and Monkewitz 1985
)
.

For falling films over inclined plates, experiments
(
Liu et al. 1993
)
, analytical and numerical analysis
(
Brevdo et al. 1999
)
proved that the so-called hydrodynamic (or Kapitza) instabilities are always convective
(
Kalliadasis et al. 2011
)
. For a liquid film suspended under a horizontal plate, instabilities are absolute
(
Sterman-Cohen et al. 2017
)
. Between these extremes, a critical plate inclination angle separating absolute and convective regimes has been identified with experiment
(
Brun et al. 2015
)
, simplified model, Direct Numerical Simulations (DNS)
(
Scheid et al. 2016
)
and linear stability of the Navier-Stokes equations
(
Pino et al. 2024
)
for a large range of Reynolds numbers.

Similarly, in the case of a liquid film over a substrate moving against gravity, perturbations on a sufficiently thin film should be convected upwards, while those in a sufficiently thick film should be convected downwards, like in falling films. There must, therefore, be a window of intermediate thicknesses for which perturbations might propagate upwards and downwards, reminiscent of absolute instability. Determining whether instability is absolute or convective can be crucial for industrial applications.

To the author’s knowledge, the existence of a convective to absolute threshold in a vertical liquid film over a moving substrate has never been explored. In this work, we identify this threshold for a wide range of liquid properties and operating conditions by solving the Orr-Sommerfeld eigenvalue problem for a 2D liquid film.

II
Problem Set and Film Regimes

The problem set is illustrated in Figure
1
with the relevant parameters. A liquid film of thickness
h(x,t)
evolves on a vertical substrate rising at a velocity
U_{p}
. The liquid has density
\rho
dynamic viscosity
\mu
, kinematic viscosity
\nu=\mu/\rho
and surface tension at the gas-liquid interface
\sigma
.

Depending on the liquid properties and the velocity
U_{p}
, different liquid film regimes exist
(
Groenveld 1970
)
. In dimensionless form, the film thickness
\hat{h}=h/[h]
with
[h]=(\mu U_{p}/\rho g)^{1/2}
depends on the Reynolds number
{\rm Re}=U_{p}[h]/\nu=(U_{p}^{3}/g\nu)^{1/2}
, and the capillary number
{\rm Ca}=\mu U_{p}/\sigma
. These numbers can be linked by the Kapitza number
{\rm Ka}={\rm Re}^{2/3}/{\rm Ca}=\sigma/(\rho g^{1/3}\nu^{4/3})
which only dependents on the fluid properties.

In the limit of
{\rm Ca}\rightarrow 0
and perfectly wetting conditions, the Landau-Levich-Derjaguin (LLD) solution gives the steady flat film thickness (
\hat{h}=\overline{h}
) given by
\overline{h}=0.9458{\rm Ca}^{1/6}

(
Derjaguin 1943
;
Landau and Levich 1942
)
. An extension of the LLD solution to account for gravity was found by Wilson
(
Wilson 1982
)
who obtained
\bar{h}=0.9458{\rm Ca}^{1/6}-0.3889{\rm Ca}^{1/2}
; this was shown to be valid up to
{\rm Ca}\sim O(10^{-1})
(
Krechetnikov and Homsy 2006
)
.
For the same
U_{p}
,
Snoeijer et al. 2008
found a one-parameter family of film solutions in the range
\bar{h}\in[\sqrt{3}-0.688{\rm Ca}^{1/6},\sqrt{3}]
, appearing for partially wetting fluids just above the critical substrate speed at which the film starts to cling
(
Snoeijer et al. 2006
)
. Note that
\bar{h}=\sqrt{3}
corresponds to the transition at which the mean flow rate changes sign and is thus directed downwards, as for falling films
(
Kalliadasis et al. 2011
)
.

In the limit of
{\rm Ca}\rightarrow\infty
, several authors have shown that
\bar{h}
reaches a plateau, in line with the maximum flux theory from
Derjaguin 1944
, which predicts
\bar{h}=1
.
Groenveld 1970
derived the upper limit
\bar{h}=0.66
, while experimental data in
Spiers et al. 1974
shows that this should be at
\bar{h}\approx 0.8
. In this work, we determine if these solutions for small and large
{\rm Ca}
are absolutely or convectively unstable. We compute the threshold between absolute and convective instability in the parameter space
{\rm Re}-{\rm Ka}
for
\bar{h}=1
, and in the space
\bar{h}-{\rm Re}
for
{\rm Ka}=3400
(water) and
{\rm Ka}=4
(corn oil).

V
Conclusions

Our study first demonstrates that a liquid film over a substrate moving against gravity is always unstable, even though the growth rate in the LLD limit is generally too small for the perturbations to be seen in practice. Second, it demonstrates the existence of a window of absolute instability that should be appealing for further foundational and applied research in coating processes involving moving substrates.

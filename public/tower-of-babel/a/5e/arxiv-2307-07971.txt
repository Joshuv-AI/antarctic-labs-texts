Extreme inequalities of general L p L_{p} μ \mu -projection body and general L p L_{p} μ \mu -centroid body Thanks: Corresponding author: Gangyi Chen
Chao Li, Gangyi Chen

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the concept of general L p L_{p} projection body and general L p L_{p} centroid body of general measures with positive homogeneity density function, and prove the corresponding extreme inequalities. Meanwhile, we also study their measure comparison problem and monotone inequalities.

2.
Preliminaries

Below we will list some basic facts of convex geometry.

2.1.
L_{p}
-Minkowski combination and
L_{p}
-radial combination

A convex body
K\in\mathscr{K}^{n}
, is uniquely determined by its support function,
h(K,\cdot)=h_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which is defined by

(3)

\displaystyle h(K,x)=\max\{x\cdot y:y\in K\},\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
x\cdot y
denotes the standard inner product of
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is also clear from the definition that
h(K,u)\leq h(L,u)
for
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, if and only if
K\subseteq L
.

For
K,L\in\mathscr{K}_{o}^{n}
,
p\geq 1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
(not both zero), the
L_{p}
-Minkowski combination,
\alpha\cdot K+_{p}\beta\cdot L
, of
K
and
L
is defined by (see
[
38
]
)

(4)

\displaystyle h(\alpha\cdot K+_{p}\beta\cdot L,\cdot)^{p}=\alpha h(K,\cdot)^{p}+\beta h(L,\cdot)^{p},

where
``+_{p}"
denotes the
L_{p}
-sum and
\alpha\cdot K=\alpha^{1/p}K
is the
L_{p}
-Minkowski scalar multiplication. If
p=1
, (
4
) is just the classical Minkowski linear combination.

If
K
is a compact star-shaped set (about the
origin) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then its radial function,

\rho_{K}=\rho(K,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, is defined by

(5)

\displaystyle\rho(K,x)=\max\{c\geq 0:cx\in K\},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

If
\rho_{K}
is positive and continuous,
K
will be called a star body (with respect to the origin). Obviously, if
\alpha>0
, then
\rho_{\alpha K}=\alpha\rho_{K}
.

By (2.3), we easily see that if
K\subseteq L
, then

(6)

\displaystyle\rho(K,\cdot)\leq\rho(L,\cdot),

with equality if and only if
K=L
.

If
K\in\mathscr{K}^{n}_{o}
, the polar body
K^{\ast}
, of
K
is defined by

(7)

\displaystyle K^{\ast}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot y\leq 1,y\in K\}.

From (
3
), (
5
) and (
7
), it follows that if
K\in\mathscr{K}^{n}_{o}
, then

(8)

\displaystyle\rho(K^{\ast},\cdot)=\frac{1}{h(K,\cdot)},\quad\quad\quad h(K^{*},\cdot)=\frac{1}{\rho(K,\cdot)}.

If
K,L\in\mathscr{S}^{n}_{o}
,
p\neq 0
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
(not both zero), the
L_{p}
-radial combination,
\alpha\circ K\widetilde{+}_{p}\beta\circ L\in\mathscr{S}^{n}_{o}
, of
K
and
L
is defined by (see
[
38
]
)

(9)

\displaystyle\rho(\alpha\circ K\widetilde{+}_{p}\beta\circ L,\cdot)^{p}=\alpha\rho(K,\cdot)^{p}+\beta\rho(L,\cdot)^{p},

where
\widetilde{+}_{p}
is called the
L_{p}
-radial addition and
\alpha\circ K=\alpha^{1/p}K
is the
L_{p}
-radial scalar multiplication. If
p=1
, then
\alpha\circ K\widetilde{+}\beta\circ L
is the classical radial combination. If
p\geq 0
, then
\alpha\circ K\widetilde{+}_{-p}\beta\circ L
is called
L_{p}
-harmonic radial combination.

From (
4
), (
8
) and (
9
), for
K,L\in\mathscr{K}_{o}^{n}
,
p\geq 1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
(not both zero), then

(10)

\displaystyle(\alpha\cdot K+_{p}\beta\cdot L)^{*}=\alpha\circ K^{*}\widetilde{+}_{-p}\beta\circ L^{*}.

-measure

A measurable function

A Borel measure
\mu

\mathbb{R}^{n}

For convenience, we call the measure

(

Let
\mu

The nonsymmetric

cosine transform of

[

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

\mathcal{C}_{p}^{+}\nu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

L_{p}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{p}^{+}\nu)(u)=c_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\cdot v)_{+}^{p}d\nu(v),\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

where
(u\cdot v)_{+}=\max\{u\cdot v,0\}
, and

\displaystyle c_{n,p}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\mbox{$\frac{n+p}{2}$}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+p}{2}\right)}.

According to the nonsymmetric
L_{p}
cosine transform, we give the following concept of nonsymmetric
L_{p}

\mu
-projection body by: Let
\mu
satisfy the homogeneity condition
(
I
)
,
K\in\mathscr{K}^{n}_{\mathrm{o}}
and

, then the nonsymmetric
L_{p}

\mu
-projection body
\Pi_{\mu,p}^{+}K
of

, is the convex body defined by

\displaystyle h(\Pi_{\mu,p}^{+}K,\cdot)^{p}=\mathcal{C}_{p}^{+}S_{\mu,p}(K,\cdot),

i.e., for
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\displaystyle h(\Pi_{\mu,p}^{+}K,u)^{p}=c_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\cdot v)_{+}^{p}dS_{\mu,p}(K,v).

Let
(u\cdot v)_{-}=((-u)\cdot v)_{+}=\max\{(-u)\cdot v,0\}
. For
K\in\mathscr{K}^{n}_{\mathrm{o}}
and
p\geq 1
, we define
\Pi_{\mu,p}^{-}K

\displaystyle h(\Pi_{\mu,p}^{-}K,u)^{p}=c_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\cdot v)_{-}^{p}dS_{\mu,p}(K,v),

for
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle h(-\Pi_{\mu,p}^{+}K,u)^{p}

\displaystyle=h(\Pi_{\mu,p}^{+}K,-u)^{p}

\displaystyle=c_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(-u\cdot v)_{+}^{p}dS_{\mu,p}(K,v)

\displaystyle=c_{n,p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(u\cdot v)_{-}^{p}dS_{\mu,p}(K,v)

\displaystyle=h(\Pi_{\mu,p}^{-}K,u)^{p},

that is,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu,p}^{-}K=-\Pi_{\mu,p}^{+}K.

Let

Using the polar coordinates, it is easy to verify that for all

Similarly, we also define

\displaystyle h(\Gamma_{\mu,p}^{-}K,u)^{p}=\frac{2}{c_{n,p}\mu(K)}\int_{K}(u\cdot x)_{-}^{p}d\mu(x),\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Obviously, for
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

\displaystyle h(-\Gamma_{\mu,p}^{+}K,u)^{p}

\displaystyle=h(\Gamma_{\mu,p}^{+}K,-u)^{p}=\frac{2}{c_{n,p}\mu(K)}\int_{K}((-u)\cdot x)_{+}^{p}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{c_{n,p}\mu(K)}\int_{K}(u\cdot x)_{-}^{p}d\mu(x)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu,p}^{-}K=-\Gamma_{\mu,p}^{+}K.

In 2006, Ludwig

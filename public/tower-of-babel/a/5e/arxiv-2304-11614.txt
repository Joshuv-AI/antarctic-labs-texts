On Some Series Involving Harmonic and Skew-Harmonic Numbers
Vincent Nguyen

Abstract
In this paper, we evaluate in closed form several different series involving the harmonic numbers and skew-harmonic numbers. We consider two classes of series involving these sequences. One class of series involves the product of the n n th harmonic or skew-harmonic number and a tail. We provide the solution to two open problems concerning these harmonic series with tails from Alina Sîntămărian and Ovidiu Furdui’s book Sharpening Mathematical Analysis Skills. The other class of series is the Hardy series, which involves a logarithm and the Euler-Mascheroni constant being subtracted from the n n th harmonic number.



1
Introduction

The harmonic numbers are defined as
H_{n}=1+\frac{1}{2}+\ldots+\frac{1}{n}
. Its generalization is defined as
H_{n}^{(p)}=1+\frac{1}{2^{p}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{n^{p}}
. The generalized harmonic numbers are useful in evaluating some of our series in closed form. The alternating analogue of the harmonic numbers, skew-harmonic numbers, are defined to be
\overline{H}_{n}=1-\frac{1}{2}+\ldots+\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}
. Leonhard Euler famously derived

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{n}}{n^{k}}=\left(1+\frac{k}{2}\right)\zeta(k+1)-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{k-2}\zeta(k-n)\zeta(n+1)\quad k\in\{2,3,\ldots\},

where
\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}
is the Riemann zeta function (see
[
1
, p. 807]
,
[
26
, 25.2]
). The result can be found in
[
7
]
and proved in
[
23
, pp. 47-49]
. Other formulae for Euler sums have been derived, one of which we will use from
[
15
, Theorem 7.2, p. 33]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{n^{q}}=&\frac{(1-(-1)^{p})\zeta(p)\eta(q)+\eta(p+q)}{2}\\
&+\sum_{j+2k=p}\binom{q+j-1}{q-1}(-1)^{j+1}\eta(q+j)\eta(2k)\\
&+(-1)^{p}\sum_{i+2k=q}\binom{p+i-1}{p-1}\zeta(p+i)\eta(2k),\quad p+q\text{ is odd}\end{split}

(1.1)

where
\eta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^{s}}
denotes the Dirichlet eta function (see
[

, p. 807]
).

Series involving the Riemann zeta function can be found in

H_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{n}}{1-x}\,dx,\quad n\in\{1,2,\ldots\}.

Theorem 2.3
.

For all

, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{H}_{n}\left(e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right)=ye^{y}\left[\Ein(2y)-\Ein(y)\right]-\cosh(y)+1.

We can easily check that the series evaluates to
0
for

. For now let us assume

. We begin by inserting the integral representation of the skew-harmonic numbers
2.2
and interchange the sum and integral:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{H}_{n}\left(e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-(-x)^{n}}{1+x}\left(e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right)\,dx

It is known via the Lagrange error bound that

\left|e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right|\leq\frac{e^{t}|y|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

for some arbitrary positive

such that

]
. Since

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right)x^{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{y}-e^{xy}}{1-x}&\text{if }x\neq 1\\
ye^{y}&\text{if }x=1\end{cases}

(see
[

) and

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}(1-(-x)^{n})\left(e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}|1-(-x)^{n}|\left|e^{y}-1-\frac{y}{1!}-\frac{y^{2}}{2!}-\ldots-\frac{y^{n}}{n!}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|y|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{t}(e^{|y|}-1-|y|)

for integers
n\geq 1
, real numbers
x
such that

, all real numbers
y
and some arbitrary positive

such that

, we may justify interchanging the sum and integral via Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem (see
[

, pp. 188-189]
). Thus, the series can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(ye^{y}-\frac{e^{y}-e^{-xy}}{1+x}\right)\frac{dx}{1+x}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{-e^{y}+ye^{y}(1+x)+e^{-xy}}{(1+x)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{y}}{2}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ye^{y}(1+x)+e^{-xy}}{(1+x)^{2}}\,dx.

Integrating by parts gives us

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{y}}{2}+1-\frac{e^{-y}}{2}+y\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{y}-e^{-xy}}{1+x}\,dx=

\displaystyle 1-\cosh(y)+y\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{y}-e^{-xy}}{1+x}\,dx.

Since

, which yields

\displaystyle 1-\cosh(y)+ye^{y}\int_{y}^{2y}\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}\,dx=

\displaystyle ye^{y}\left[\Ein(2y)-\Ein(y)\right]-\cosh(y)+1.

We used the fact that

. Thus, the expression is valid for all real

∎

Theorem 3.1
.

For
k\in\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
and
x>0
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\left(H_{n}-\log\sqrt[k]{(n+x-1)^{\overline{k}}}-\gamma\right)=\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{k}\log\left[\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{x+k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}\right]

where
z^{\overline{k}}=z(z+1)\ldots(z+k-1)
denotes the Pochhammer symbol (see
[
26
, 5.2(iii)]
).

We begin by applying Abel’s summation formula (
1.2
) to our summation with
a_{n}=(-1)^{n}
and
b_{n}=H_{n}-\log\sqrt[k]{(n+x-1)^{\overline{k}}}-\gamma
to get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1+(-1)^{n}}{2}\left(H_{n+1}-\log\sqrt[k]{(n+x)^{\overline{k}}}-\gamma\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-(-1)^{n}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}+\log\sqrt[k]{\frac{n+x-1}{n+x+k-1}}\right).

It is easy to check that the limit evaluates to
0
. This leaves us with the infinite series:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1,3,5,\ldots}\frac{1-(-1)^{n}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n+1}+\log\sqrt[k]{\frac{n+x-1}{n+x+k-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2n}+\log\sqrt[k]{\frac{2n+x-2}{2n+x+k-2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\sqrt[k]{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n+x-2}{2n+x+k-2}e^{k/(2n)}}.

It is known that

\Gamma(z+1)=e^{-\gamma z}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{z}{n}\right)^{-1}e^{z/n},\quad z\notin\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\},

which follows from Weierstrass’ definition of the gamma function (see
[
13
, p. 176]
) and the functional equation
z\Gamma(z)=\Gamma(z+1)
(see
[
1
, p. 256]
). Using this equality, we finally get

\displaystyle\log\sqrt[k]{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2n+x-2}{2n+x+k-2}e^{\frac{k}{2n}}}

\displaystyle=\log\sqrt[k]{\frac{e^{-\gamma\left(\frac{x+k-2}{2}\right)}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x+k-2}{2n}\right)^{-1}e^{\frac{x+k-2}{2n}}}{e^{-\gamma\left(\frac{x-2}{2}\right)}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{x-2}{2n}\right)^{-1}e^{\frac{x-2}{2n}}}\cdot e^{\frac{k\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2}+\frac{1}{k}\log\left[\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{x+k}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)}\right].

∎

Further Research

The interested reader may consider a generalization to the series
S_{2}
:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{H}_{n}}{n}\left(\zeta(k)-1-\frac{1}{2^{k}}-\ldots\frac{1}{n^{k}}\right),\quad n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}.

One may also consider other analogues to the Hardy series. For example, one may study the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}n^{k}(H_{n}-\log(n)-\gamma-\sigma_{k}(n)),\quad k\in\{0,1,\ldots\}

where
\sigma_{k}(n)
is the correctional term that ensures the convergence of the series.

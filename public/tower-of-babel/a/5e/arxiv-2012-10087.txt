The antimagic orientation problems for graphs obtained by some graph operations
Eranda Dhananjaya, Wei-Tian Li

Abstract
A simple graph G G is said to admit an antimagic orientation if there exist an orientation on the edges of G G and a bijection from E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) to { 1 , 2 , … , | E ⁡ ( G ) | } \{1,2,\ldots,|E(G)|\} such that the vertex sums of vertices are pairwise distinct, where the vertex sum of a vertex is defined to be the sum of the labels of the in-edges minus that of the out-edges incident to the vertex. It was conjectured by Hefetz, Mütze, and Schwartz [ 8 ] in 2010 that every connected simple graph admits an antimagic orientation. In this paper, we prove that the Mycielski construction and the corona product for graphs with some conditions yield graphs satisfying the above conjecture.



Keywords:
antimagic labeling, antimagic orientation, Euler circuit, Mycielski construction, corona product.

1
Introduction

In this paper, unless we particularly mention, all graphs are simple.
Most of the notation and terminology follows
[
20
]
.
For a graph
G
,
an antimagic labeling
of
G
is a bijection from the edge set
E(G)
to the set
\{1,2,\ldots,|E(G)|\}
such that the vertex sums are pairwise distinct, where the vertex sum of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is defined as the sum of the labels of all edges incident to
v
. It was conjectured by Hartsfield and Ringel
[
9
]
in 90’s that every connected graph other than
K_{2}
has an antimagic labeling. The conjecture has been verified for various graphs, but is still wide open in general. Some remarkable classes of graphs are dense graphs
[
1
]
, regular graphs
[
2
]
, and trees with at most one vertex of degree two
[
11
]
. For more results of the original antimagic problems, we refer a comprehensive survey
[
5
]
to the readers.

The study of antimagic labeling on directed graphs began much later.
It was initiated by Hefetz, Mütze, and Schwartz
[
8
]
in 2010.
For a directed graph
G
, the vertex sum of a vertex
v
is defined as
the sum of the labels of all edges entering
v
minus the sum of the labels of all edges leaving
v
.
As before, we say
G
is antimagic if the vertex sums are pairwise distinct.
Hefetz et al. pointed out that the directed
C_{3}
and
P_{3}
are not antimagic. On the other hand, it is not hard to show among all of the directed graphs whose underlying graph is
P_{3}
(or
C_{3}
), there exists a directed graph that is antimagic. So they proposed two questions:

Question 1.1

Is every connected directed graph with at least 4 vertices antimagic?

Conjecture 1.2

Every connected graph admits an antimagic orientation.

While Question
1.1
seems to be very difficult, Conjecture
1.2
has been studied intensively recently.
We say an undirected graph
G

admits an antimagic orientation
, if there exist an orientation
D
on
E(G)
and a bijection
\tau
from
E(G)
to
\{1,2,\ldots,|E(G)|\}
such that the vertex sums
s_{(D,\tau)}(v)
, defined earlier in this paragraph, of all vertices are pairwise distinct.

There are several families of graphs proved to satisfy Conjecture
1.2
by different groups of researchers:

K_{n}
with
n\geq 4
,
S_{n}
with
n\geq 3
,
W_{n}
with
n\geq 3
, the
(2d+1)
-regular graphs with
d\geq 0
by Hefetz et al
[
8
]
;
the
2d
-regular graphs with
d\geq 2
by Li, Song, Wang, Yang, and Zhang
[
10
]
, by Yang
[
21
]
, and by Song and Hao
[
17
]
;
the biregular graphs by Shan and Yu
[
15
]
;
the Halin graphs by Yu, Chang, and Zhou
[
23
]
;
the caterpillars by Lozano
[
12
]
;
the lobsters by Gao and Shan
[
6
]
;
and the complete
k
-ary trees by Song and Hao
[
16
]
.
In addition to the above special graphs,
Yang, Carlson, Owens, Perry, Singgih, Song, Zhang, and Zhang
[
22
]
, proved that every connected graph with at least
n\geq 9
vertices and maximum degree at least
n-5
admits an antimagic labeling, and
Song, Yang, and Zhang
[
18
]
proved that every graph
G
with independent number at most 4 or least
|V(G)|/2
admits an antimagic orientation.
Some of the above results are also true for disconnected graphs.

Let
G
be a graph with
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
. The
Mycielski construction
of
G
,
M(G)
is a new graph with
V(M(G))=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\cup\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\cup\{w\}
and
E(M(G))=E(G)\cup\{v_{i}u_{j}\mid\mbox{ if }v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}\cup\{wu_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
. Moreover, we call
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
the image vertices of
V(G)
.
An example is
M(K_{2})=C_{5}
.
This graph operation was introduced by Mycielski
[
13
]
to construct the graphs having the large chromatic number but the small clique number.
Given two graphs
G
and
H
, the
corona product
of
G
and
H
, denoted as
G\odot H
, is the graph obtained by taking one copy of
G
as the
center graph
,
|V(G)|
copies of
H
as the
outer graphs
, and join the each vertex of
G
to every vertex of a copy of
H
. This graph product was introduced by Frucht and Harary
[
4
]
in 1970.
In general, the product is non-commutative since one can see that
K_{1}\odot K_{2}
is
K_{3}
but
K_{2}\odot K_{1}
is
P_{4}
.
The research of the antimagic problems for the corona product of graphs can be found in
[
3
,
7
,
19
]
.

In this paper, we study the antimagic orientation problem for the Mycielski construction and also the corona product of graphs. We manage to prove that some graphs obtained by the graph operations mentioned above satisfying Conjecture
1.2
.

The organizations of the paper are the following.
In next section, we discuss a technical lemma which is used frequently for constructing the antimagic orientations in recent papers.
The result on the Mycielski constructions is presented in Section 3, while the result on the corona products is presented in Section 4.

The vertex sums induced by the Euler circuits

A
closed walk
in a multiple graph
G
is an
Euler circuit
if it traverses every edge in
G
exactly once. The following theorem is classical:

Theorem 2.1
(Euler, 1736)

A connected multiple graph has an Euler circuit if and only if the degree of every vertex is even.

Using the Euler circuit, one can estimate the vertex sum of each vertex induced by the circuit. The lemma below plays an important role in proving our theorems and has appeared in several recent papers (e.g.
Lemma 2.2 in
[
18
]
and Lemma 7 in
[
14
]
). For the sake of completeness, we present the proof in the paper. Let
[a,b]:=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\}
.

Lemma 2.2

Let
p\geq 0
be an integer, and
G
be a graph with
|E(G)|=m\geq 1
. Either we can find an orientation
D
of
G
and a bijection
\tau:E(G)\rightarrow[p+1,,p+m]
such that for any
v\in V(G)
,

-(p+m)+\lfloor d_{G}(v)/2\rfloor\leq s_{(D,\tau)}(v)\leq p+m+\lfloor d_{G}(v)/2\rfloor,

(1)

or an orientation
D^{\prime}
and a bijection
\tau^{\prime}
such that for any
v\in V(G)
,

-(p+m)-\lfloor d_{G}(v)/2\rfloor\leq s_{(D^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})}\left(v\right)\leq p+m-\lfloor d_{G}(v)/2\rfloor.

(2)

Proof
.
It suffices to prove the lemma for connected graphs.
Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
V_{1}=\{v\in V(G)\mid d_{G}(v)\mbox{ is odd}\}
.
By the handshaking theorem, we see that
|V_{1}|
is even. If
V_{1}\neq\varnothing
, let
V_{1}=\{v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\ldots,v_{i_{2t}}\}
.
Define
G^{*}=G
when
V_{1}=\varnothing
, else
G^{*}
is obtained by adding
t
new edges
e^{*}_{1},e^{*}_{2},\ldots,e^{*}_{t}
to
G
, where

e^{*}_{j}
is an edge connecting
v_{i_{j}}
and
v_{i_{j+t}}
.
Thus,
d_{G^{*}}(v_{i})
is even for every vertex
v_{i}\in G^{*}
.
Note that
G^{*}
could be a multiple graph.

Pick an Euler circuit
C
in
G^{*}
with the conditions:
If
G
contains an even degree vertex, we choose the even degree vertex as the initial vertex of
C
. Otherwise, every vertex in
V(G)
has an odd degree and is incident to exactly one
e^{*}_{i}
in
G^{*}
. We choose any vertex as the initial vertex of
C
and the edge
e^{*}_{i}
incident to it as the first edge of
C
. Suppose

C=u_{1},e_{1},u_{2},e_{2},\ldots,u_{m+t},e_{m+t},u_{1},

where the
u_{i}
’s are vertices in
V(G)
which may have repetitions, and

e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m+t}
are exactly the edges in
E(G^{*})
.

Let
e_{i_{1}},e_{i_{2}},\ldots,e_{i_{m}}
in
C

be the edges in
E(G)
.
By orienting each edge a direction the same as it is in
C
, we obtain an orientation
D
of
G
.
Observe that
|d^{+}_{D}(v_{i})-d^{-}_{D}(v_{i})|\leq 1
. Define
\tau:E(G)\rightarrow[p+1,p+m]
with
\tau(e_{i_{j}})=p+m+1-j
for all
1\leq j\leq m
.
If two edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
incident to
v_{i}
appears consecutively in
C
, then
\tau(e)+\tau(e^{\prime})
contributes one to the vertex sum of
v_{i}
. Thereby we calculate the vertex sums as follows.
For any vertex
v_{i}
with
d^{+}_{D}(v_{i})=d^{-}_{D}(v_{i})=d_{G}(v_{i})/2
,

s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-m+d_{G}(v_{i})/2&\mbox{if }v_{i}\mbox{ is incident to both the edges}\\
&\mbox{labeled with }p+m\mbox{ and }p+1;\\
d_{G}(v_{i})/2&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

For any vertex
v_{i}
with
d^{+}_{D}(v_{i})-d^{-}_{D}(v_{i})=1
,
let
e_{v_{i}}
be the edge leaving
v_{i}
, whose predecessor in
C
is some
e^{*}_{j}
. We have

s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=-\tau(e_{v_{i}})+(d_{G}(v_{i})-1)/2

Similarly, for any vertex
v_{i}
with
d^{+}_{D}(v_{i})-d^{-}_{D}(v_{i})=-1
, we have

s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=\tau(e_{v_{i}})+(d_{G}(v_{i})-1)/2

where
e_{v_{i}}
is the edge entering
v_{i}
, whose successor in
C
is some
e^{*}_{j}
.
Now the lower and upper bounds of Inequality (
1
) are obtained from the vertex sum of a vertex of odd degree.

Let
\tau^{\prime}=\tau
and
D^{\prime}
be the orientation obtained by reversing the direction of each edge in
D
. By the similar arguments, we see that
\tau^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime}
satisfy Inequality (
2
).

\square

3
Mycielski construction of graphs

Conventionally, the vertex sum of an isolated vertex is defined to be zero. Thus, a graph with more than one isolated vertex does not admit an antimagic orientation.
Also, an isolated vertex of a graph is still an isolated vertex of its Mycielski construction. So we consider the graphs containing at most one isolated vertex in the following the theorem.

Theorem 3.1

Let
G
be a graph with at most one isolated vertex. Then the Mycielski construction of
G
,
M(G)

admits an antimagic orientation.

Proof
.
First if
G
contains exactly an isolated vertex, then one can show the independence number of
M(G)
is
(|V(M(G))|+1)/2
and one of the maximum independent set is the set consisting of the isolated vertex and all image vertices. So the existence of the antimagic orientation follows the result in
[
18
]
.

Now suppose that
G
contains no isolated vertex.
Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
Define
V(M(G))=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\cup\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\cup\{w\}
and
E(M(G))=E(G)\cup\{v_{i}u_{j}\mid\mbox{ if }v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)\}\cup\{wu_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
.
Let
|E(G)|=m
. Then
|E(M(G))|=3m+n
. We partition the label set
[1,3m+n]
into
[1,m]
,
[m+1,3m-n]
,
[3m-n+1,3m]
, and
[3m+1,3m+n]
.

First apply Lemma
2.2
to obtain an orientation
D_{1}
on
E(G)
and a bijection
\tau
from
E(G)
to
[1,m]
so that
s_{(D_{1},\tau)}\left(v\right)\leq m+(d_{G}(v_{i})-1)/2
for each
v_{i}\in V(G)
.
We extend the orientation
D_{1}
to
D
on
E(M(G))
.
Since every edge in
E(M(G))\setminus E(G)
is incident to some image vertex
u_{i}
,
we define the direction of it to be entering the
u_{i}
’s.
For each vertex
v_{i}
, we select an edge in
E(M(G))\setminus E(G)
incident to it as
e_{v_{i}}
.
We arbitrarily label the edges of the form
v_{i}u_{j}
, except for the
e_{v_{i}}
’s, with labels in
[m+1,3m-n]
.
Let
s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})
be the partial (lacking of the label of
e_{v_{i}}
) vertex sum of
v_{i}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume

s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{1})\leq s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{2})\leq\cdots\leq s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{n}).

Then we label
e_{v_{i}}
with
3m+1-i
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Since
e_{v_{i}}
is leaving
v_{i}
, we have
s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})-3m-1+i
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
which implies
s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})\neq s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{j})
for
i\neq j
.
Finally, for the image vertices
u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}
,
suppose we have
the partial (lacking of the label of
wu_{i}
) vertex sums satisfying the following:

s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{1}})\leq s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{2}})\leq\cdots\leq s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{n}}).

Then we label
wu_{i_{j}}
with
3m+j
for
1\leq j\leq n
.
Since
wu_{i_{j}}
is entering
u_{i_{j}}
, we have
s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{j}})=s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{j}})+3m+j
for
1\leq j\leq n
,
which implies
s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{j}})\neq s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i_{k}})
for
j\neq k
.

Our orientation
D
gives
s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{i})>0
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Let us see
s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})<0
for
1\leq i\leq n
. By the fact that every edge of the form
v_{i}u_{j}
is leaving
v_{i}
and by
Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s_{(D_{1},\tau)}(v_{i})-d_{G}(v_{i})(m+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m+\lfloor d_{G}(v_{i})/2\rfloor-d_{G}(v_{i})(m+1)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0.

It remains to show
s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})\neq s_{(D,\tau)}(w)
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
For each
v_{i}
, there are
d_{G}(v_{i})
edges leaving
v_{i}
and entering the image vertices, and one of them is
e_{v_{i}}
. The largest possible sum of the labels of these edges is
(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{G}(v_{i})-1}3m-n+1-j)+3m
.
Thus,

\displaystyle s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle s_{(D_{1},\tau)}(v_{i})-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{G}(v_{i})-1}3m-n+1-j\right)-3m

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-m+\lfloor d_{G}(v_{i})/2\rfloor-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}3m-n+1-j\right)-3m

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}3m+j=s_{(D,\tau)}(w).

Therefore the proof is completed.

\square

4
Corona product of graphs

In this section, we investigate the corona product of graphs.
Let us denote the center and outer graphs of a corona product by
G
and
H
.
The number of vertices and edges of
G
are denoted as
n
and
m
, and the numbers of vertices and edges of
H
are denoted as
k
and
\ell
.
Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and

V(H)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots u_{k}\}
.
Denote the
i^{th}
copy of
H
as
H^{(i)}
and the

i^{th}
copy of
u_{j}
in
H^{(i)}
as
u_{j}^{(i)}
.
We will give the conditions to the outer graphs.

Theorem 4.1

Let
G
and
H
be the center and outer graphs of a corona product. Suppose that
H
contains an even degree vertex. Then

G\odot H
admits an antimagic orientation.

Proof
.
We construct the orientation of
D
and the bijection
\tau
for
G\odot H
by three steps.
Since
|E(G\odot H)|=m+n\ell+nk
, we partition the label set
[1,m+n\ell+nk]
into three sets
[1,m]
,
[m+1,m+n\ell]
, and
[m+n\ell+1,m+n\ell+nk]
.

First, apply the constructive method in Lemma
2.2
to obtain an orientation
D_{G}
of
G
and a bijection
\tau_{G}
from
E(G)
to
[1,m]

such that
s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i})
satisfies

s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i})\geq-m+\lfloor d_{G}(v_{i})/2\rfloor.

For the outer graph
H
, since
H
contains an even degree vertex, we also apply Lemma
2.2
to obtain an orientation
D_{H}
of
H
and a bijection
\tau_{H}
from
E(H)
to
[1,\ell]
so that

s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})\leq\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor.

Next, for
1\leq i\leq n
, orient every
H^{(i)}
the same as
D_{H}
and define
\tau_{H^{(i)}}
from
E(H^{(i)})
to
[m+(i-1)\ell+1,m+i\ell]
by letting

\tau_{H^{(i)}}(e^{(i)})=\tau_{H}(e)+m+(i-1)\ell
, where
e^{(i)}
is the copy of
e
in
H^{(i)}
.
Note that if
u_{j}
is an even degree vertex in
H
, then for
1\leq i\leq n
, we have

s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{j}^{(i)})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ell-d_{H}(u_{j})/2,&\mbox{if }u_{j}\mbox{ is the initial vertex of the}\\
&\mbox{Euler circuit traversing }H^{*};\\
-d_{H}(u_{j})/2,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

which is a constant with respect to
i
.
On the other hand, if
u_{j}^{(i)}
is an odd degree vertex in
H^{(i)}
, then

s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{j}^{(i)})\leq m+i\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor

for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
M=\{v_{i}u_{j}^{(i)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq k\}
be the set of edges connecting
G
and the
H^{(i)}
’s. We give the direction of every edge in
M
from
H^{(i)}
to
G
and call this orientation
D_{M}
.
Then define

\tau_{M}
from
M
to
[m+n\ell+1,m+n(k+\ell)]
as follows.
First arrange the vertices
u_{j}^{(i)}
’s
as
u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})},u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})},\ldots,u_{j_{nk}}^{(i_{nk})}
,
satisfying

s_{(D_{H^{(i_{1})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{1})}})}(u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})})\geq s_{(D_{H^{(i_{2})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{2})}})}(u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})})\geq\cdots\geq s_{(D_{H^{(i_{nk})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{nk})}})}(u_{j_{nk}}^{(i_{nk})}).

Then label the edges
v_{i_{1}}u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})},v_{i_{2}}u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})},\ldots,v_{i_{nk}}u_{j_{nk}}^{(i_{nk})}
with the labels
m+n\ell+1,m+n\ell+2,\ldots,m+n\ell+nk
accordingly.

Now, let
D
be the orientation of
G\odot H
such that
D|_{G}=D_{G}
,
D|_{M}=D_{M}
, and
D|_{H_{i}}=D_{H_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and

\tau
be the bijection from
E(G\odot H)
to
[1,m+n\ell+nk]
such that
\tau|_{G}=\tau_{G}
,
\tau|_{M}=\tau_{M}
, and
\tau|_{H_{i}}=\tau_{H_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
We show that the vertex sums

s_{D,\tau}(v)
for
v\in V(G\odot H)
are pairwise distinct.
Since each edge of
M
is directed from
H_{i}
to
G
, we have

s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{j}^{(i)})=s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{j}^{(i)})-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{j}^{(j)})\leq m+i\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor-(m+n\ell+1)<0

for each
u_{j}^{(i)}
. Moreover, our labeling method for
v_{i}u_{j}^{(i)}
’s gives

s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})})>s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})})>\cdots>s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{j_{nk}}^{(i_{nk})}).

On the other hand, for each
v_{i}
, we have

s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{j}^{(i)})\geq-m+\lfloor d_{G}(v_{i})/2\rfloor+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(m+n\ell+j)\\
>0.

However, it is possible that
s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{j})
for some
i\neq j
at this moment.
Recall that
s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{j}^{(i)})
is a constant for
1\leq j\leq n
if
d_{H}(u_{j})
is even.
Suppose that
u_{1}
is an even degree vertex in
H
.
Assume that

s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i)})=s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i})+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{j}^{(i)})

is non-decreasing in
i
. Then we permute the labels of
v_{1}u_{1}^{(1)},v_{2}u_{1}^{(2)},\ldots,v_{n}u_{1}^{(n)}
in an increasing order. Thus, the new vertex sums of
v_{i}
’s are all distinct. The vertex sums of
u_{1}^{(i)}
’s also permuted but are still all distinct. Then the proof is completed.

\square

By the handshaking theorem, we have the following corollary of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.2

Let
G
and
H
be the center and outer graphs of a corona product. Suppose that
H
contains an odd number of vertices. Then
G\odot H
admits an antimagic orientation.

Theorem 4.3

Let
G
and
H
be the center and outer graphs of a corona product. If
|V(G)|\leq|E(H)|
, then
G\odot H
admits an antimagic orientation.

Proof
.
By Theorem
4.1
, it suffices to prove the statement with the assumption that the degree of each vertex in
H
is odd.
We can further assume that the number of vertices of
H
,
k
, is even by Corollary
4.2
.

For
k=2
, either
H=2K_{1}
or
H=K_{2}
. The former graph contains the even degree vertices, so consider
H=K_{2}
. Thereby
G=K_{1}
since
n\leq\ell=1
. We have
G\odot H=K_{3}
which admits an antimagic orientation.

Suppose
k\geq 4
. We partition the set

[1,m+n\ell+nk]
into four sets

[1,n]
,
[n+1,n+m]
,

[n+m+1,m+n(\ell+1)]
, and

[m+n(\ell+1)+1,m+n\ell+nk]
.
First apply Lemma
2.2
to construct the orientation
D_{G}
and the bijection
\tau_{G}
from
E(G)
to
[n+1,n+m]
satisfying

s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i})\geq-(m+n)-\lfloor d_{G}(v_{i})/2\rfloor,

and the orientation
D_{H}
and the bijection
\tau_{H}
from
E(H)
to
[1,\ell]
satisfying

s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})\leq\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor.

Then imitate the proof of Theorem
4.1
to obtain
D_{H^{(i)}}
and
\tau_{H^{(i)}}
with

s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{j}^{(i)})\leq n+m+i\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor.

Recall that for each
u_{j}\in V(H)
, its vertex sum
s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})
is equal to

\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor\mbox{ or }-\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor,

where
e_{u_{j}}
is an edge incident to
u_{j}
whose definition is given in Lemma
2.2
.
We claim that there exists some
u_{j}
with

s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})=\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor>0.

Since the total vertex sum
\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})=0
, we must have positive
s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})
for some
u_{j}
unless
s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})=0
for every
u_{j}
.
Therefore, if the claim is not true, then

s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})=0
for every
u_{j}
.
By the pigeonhole principle, there exist two vertices
u_{j}
and
u_{j^{\prime}}
that have the same degree with

\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor=\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j^{\prime}}})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j^{\prime}})/2\rfloor=0.

Thus,
\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})=\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}^{\prime}})
and hence

e_{u_{j}}=e_{u_{j}^{\prime}}
. However, if
e_{u_{j}}=e_{u_{j}^{\prime}}
, then the label of
e_{u_{j}}
should contribute
\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})
and
-\tau_{H}(e_{u_{j}})
to the two endpoints of
e_{u_{j}}
, respectively. This contradicts our assumption. As a consequence, we must have some
u_{j}
with positive
s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{j})
and the claim is proved.

Now let
u_{1}
be a vertex such that

s_{(D_{H},\tau_{H})}(u_{1})>0
and

U=\{u_{1}^{(i)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
.
We define the orientation
D_{M}
by directing all the edges in
M
from
H^{(i)}
to
G
. To define
\tau_{M}
, first arrange the vertices
u_{j}^{(i)}
’s for
1\leq i\leq n
and
2\leq j\leq k

as
u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})},u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})},\ldots,u_{j_{n(k-1)}}^{(i_{n(k-1)})}

such that

s_{(D_{H^{(i_{1})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{1})}})}(u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})})\geq s_{(D_{H^{(i_{2})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{2})}})}(u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})})\geq\cdots\geq s_{(D_{H^{(i_{n(k-1)})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{n(k-1)})}})}(u_{j_{n(k-1)}}^{(i_{n(k-1)})}).

Then label the edges
v_{i_{1}}u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})},v_{i_{2}}u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})},\ldots,v_{i_{n(k-1)}}u_{j_{n(k-1)}}^{(i_{n(k-1)})}
with the labels
m+n(\ell+1)+1,m+n(\ell+1)+2,\ldots,m+n(k+\ell)
accordingly.
Now for each vertex
v_{i}
, we consider the partial vertex sum
s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i})=s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i})+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{j}^{(i)})
. Arrange the
v_{i}
’s so that

s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{1}})\leq s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{2}})\leq\cdots\leq s^{\prime}_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{n}}).

Then label the edges
v_{i_{1}}u_{1}^{({i_{1}})},v_{i_{2}}u_{1}^{({i_{2}})},\ldots,v_{i_{n}}u_{1}^{({i_{n}})}

with the labels
1,2,\ldots,n
accordingly to complete defining
\tau_{M}
.

The vertex sums
s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{j}^{(i)})
’s for
1\leq i\leq n
and
2\leq j\leq k
are all distinct since

s_{(D_{H^{(i_{1})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{1})}})}(u_{j_{1}}^{(i_{1})})>s_{(D_{H^{(i_{2})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{2})}})}(u_{j_{2}}^{(i_{2})})>\cdots>s_{(D_{H^{(i_{n(k-1)})}},\tau_{H^{(i_{n(k-1)})}})}(u_{j_{n(k-1)}}^{(i_{n(k-1)})})

by our labeling method. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{j}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{j}^{(i)})-\tau(v_{i}u_{j}^{(i)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n+m+i\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{j})/2\rfloor-[m+n(\ell+1)+1]<0.

Analogously, for the vertices in
V(G)
, we have
s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{1}})<s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{2}})<\cdots<s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{n}})
, and when
k\geq 4
,

\displaystyle s_{(D,\tau)}(v_{i_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{(D_{G},\tau_{G})}(v_{i_{1}})+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\tau_{M}(v_{i_{1}}u_{j}^{({i_{1}})})+1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-(n+m)-\lfloor d_{G}(v_{i_{1}})/2\rfloor+\sum_{j=2}^{k}\tau_{M}(v_{i_{1}}u_{j}^{({i_{1}})})+1

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle-(n+m)-m/2+\sum_{j=2}^{4}[m+n(\ell+1)+j-1]

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle m+n(\ell+1)+1.

For each vertex in
U
, we have

\displaystyle s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{1}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{1}^{(i)})-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{H^{(i)}}(e_{u_{1}}^{(i)})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{1})/2\rfloor-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i)})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\tau_{H}(e_{u_{1}})+n+m+(i-1)\ell-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{1})/2\rfloor-n

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0.

On the other hand, for
1\leq i\leq n

s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{1}^{(i)})=\tau_{H^{(i)}}(e_{u_{1}}^{(i)})-\lfloor d_{H}(u_{1})/2\rfloor-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i)})<\tau_{H^{(i)}}(e_{u_{1}}^{(i)})\leq m+n+n\ell.

Hence the vertex sum of each vertex in
U
is different to that of any vertex not in
U
.
To finish the proof, we verify that all vertices in
U
have distinct vertex sums. For
i\neq i^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle|s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{1}^{(i)})-s_{(D,\tau)}(u_{1}^{(i^{\prime})})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left[s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{1}^{(i)})-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i)})\right]-\left[s_{(D_{H^{(i^{\prime})}},\tau_{H^{(i^{\prime})}})}(u_{1}^{(i^{\prime})})-\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i^{\prime})})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|s_{(D_{H^{(i)}},\tau_{H^{(i)}})}(u_{1}^{(i)})-s_{(D_{H^{(i^{\prime})}},\tau_{H^{(i^{\prime})}})}(u_{1}^{(i^{\prime})})|-|\tau_{M}(v_{i}u_{1}^{(i)})-\tau_{M}(v_{i^{\prime}}u_{1}^{(i^{\prime})})|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|(i-i^{\prime})\ell|-(n-1)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0

by the assumption
\ell\geq n
. So the proof is completed.

\square

Measures of finite energy in pluripotential theory: a synthetic approach
Sébastien Boucksom and Mattias Jonsson

Abstract
. We introduce a synthetic approach to global pluripotential theory, covering in particular the case of a compact Kähler manifold and that of a projective Berkovich space over a non-Archimedean field. We define and study the space of measures of finite energy, introduce twisted energy and free energy functionals thereon, and show that coercivity of these functionals is an open condition with respect to the polarization.



Introduction

Pluripotential theory on compact Kähler spaces is by now a very well developed subject, with key applications to Kähler geometry. Generalizing classical concepts from potential theory, measures and potentials of finite energy play a central role in this theory, see for instance
[
GZ07
,
BBGZ13
,
Berm13
,
DN15
,
DGL21
]
. In parallel, a non-Archimedean version of pluripotential theory has also emerged, taking place on projective Berkovich spaces
[
Berk90
]
over a (complete) non-Archimedean field. Initially motivated by Arakelov geometry
[
Zha95
,
Cha06
]
, it has found various other applications, including degenerations of Calabi–Yau manifolds
[
Y.Li20
]
and the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture
[
BBJ21
,
C.Li22
,
BoJ22b
]
.

These two versions of pluripotential theory bear many similarities, and can be formulated in a quite parallel way. The main purpose of the present article is to introduce a synthetic formalism covering in particular these two cases, and use it to extend some of the main results of
[
BoJ22a
]
and
[
BoJ22b
]
(that were taking place on projective Berkovich spaces over a trivially valued field, and applied to the study of K-stability). More specifically, we

•

define and study the space of measures of finite energy;

•

introduce the
twisted energy
and
free energy
functionals on the latter space, whose composition with the Monge–Ampère operator respectively recover the Donaldson J-functional and the Mabuchi K-energy in the Kähler case, and their analogues in the non-Archimedean case;

•

show that coercivity of the free energy is an open condition with respect to the Kähler class.

The emphasis in this paper is on
measures
of finite energy, as opposed to
potentials
of finite energy, that we do not seek to investigate here (see for instance
[
BFJ15
,
BoJ22a
,
Reb21
,
DXZ23
]
in the non-Archimedean context).

Assume that

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)=\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\mu_{\omega})\quad\text{where}\quad\mu_{\omega}:=V_{\omega}^{-1}\omega^{n}=\MA_{\omega}(0).

Twisted energy, free energy, and coercivity

Assuming from now on the orthogonality property, we next investigate the dependence on
\omega
of the space
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and the energy functional
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. To this end, we require the
submean value property
, i.e. the existence of
C\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\sup\varphi\leq\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}+C

for all
\omega
-psh test functions
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. We show that this condition is independent of
\omega
, and that it is equivalent to the irreducibility of
X
when the latter is a compact Kähler or projective Berkovich space (see §
1.5
).

Theorem B
.

Assume that the submean value property holds. Then:

•

the topological space
\mathcal{M}^{1}=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
is independent of
\omega
;

•

for any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
, there exists a unique continuous functional
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)

for any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
; furthermore,
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)
satisfies Hölder estimates with respect to
\omega
.

The strategy of proof of Theorem B again globally follows the same lines as the trivially valued case treated in
[
BoJ22a
,
BoJ22b
]
. However, in the latter case the submean value inequality is actually an equality, i.e. one can take the constant
C
above to be
0

We call
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)
the
\theta
-twisted energy
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
. It provides an analogue of the
Donaldson J-functional
on the level of measures, and its relevance comes from its relation to the
Mabuchi K-energy
, when
X
is a compact Kähler manifold or a smooth projective Berkovich space. In these two cases, the choice of a (smooth or PL) metric
\rho
on the canonical bundle
K_{X}
defines an
entropy
functional
\Ent\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. In line with
[
Berm13
]
, we then define the

\effe_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by setting

\effe_{\omega}(\mu):=\Ent(\mu)-\Ent(\mu_{\omega})+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu),

where
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
denotes the curvature of
\rho
. The free energy so defined is independent of the choice of
\rho
, and its composition with the Monge–Ampère operator coincides with the
Mabuchi K-energy

\mab_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}
. As a consequence of Theorem B, we then show:

Theorem C
.

Assume
X
is a compact Kähler manifold or smooth projective Berkovich space over a non-Archimedean field, and consider the
coercivity threshold

\sigma(X,\omega):=\sup\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid\effe_{\omega}\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+A\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Then
\sigma(X,\omega)
is a continuous function of
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
.

This result actually holds in much greater generality, for the twisted coercivity threshold of any given functional on
\mathcal{M}^{1}
(see Theorem
5.5
).

In the trivially valued case, the free energy
\effe_{\omega}(\mu)
coincides with the invariant
\beta_{\omega}(\mu)
defined and studied in
[
BoJ22b
]
, and
\sigma(X,\omega)
with the
divisorial stability threshold
of
(X,\omega)
, which is positive iff
(X,\omega)
is
divisorially stable
(a strengthening of uniform K-stability, conjecturally equivalent to it, cf. §
5.2.2
). Specializing to the case of Dirac measures

recovers the notion of
valuative stability

[

,
Liu23
]
, which in the Fano case is equivalent to K-stability
[
Fuj19a
,
Li17
]
.

In the case of a compact Kähler manifold, we have
\sigma(X,\omega)>0
iff
[\omega]
contains a unique constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metric, as a consequence of
[

]
and
[

,

Structure of the paper

The article is organized as follows.

•

Section
1
introduces the synthetic pluripotential theoretic formalism, including the energy pairing and the submean value property, and establishes basic properties of the Dirichlet functional.

•

Section
2
studies the space of measures of finite energy. It introduces the orthogonality property, and proves Theorem A (cf. §
2.6
).

•

Assuming the submean value property, Section
3
establishes the first part of Theorem B, along with various further estimates for the energy.

•

Section
4
is devoted to the twisted energy, which is proved to compute the directional derivatives of the energy, concluding the proof of Theorem B.

•

Section
5
studies the (twisted) coercivity threshold of a functional, and proves that it depends continuously on the cohomology classes. This is then applied to the free energy, yielding Theorem C.

•

Finally, Appendix A establishes the relevant estimates needed for the Dirichlet functional in a simple general setting, while Appendix B studies the orthogonality property on compact Kähler spaces.

1.
A synthetic pluripotential theoretic formalism

In this section, we introduce the general setup considered in this paper, which is designed to cover in a synthetic manner the case of a compact Kähler space and that of a projective Berkovich space over a non-Archimedean field.

1.1.
Notation and terminology

•

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\lesssim y
or
x=O(y)
mean in this paper

for a constant
C_{n}>0
only depending on a given integer

x\approx y
if

and
y\lesssim x
.

•

Recall that any real vector space

admits a
finest vector space topology
, generated by its finite dimensional subspaces, i.e. a subset

is open (or closed) iff, for each finite dimensional subspace

,

is open (resp. closed) in the canonical vector space topology of

. This topology is not locally convex as soon as the dimension of

is uncountable.

•

Consider a partially ordered

-vector space
(V,\geq)
. We shall say for brevity that the partial order is

if
V_{+}:=\{x\in V\mid x\geq 0\}
spans

, and is closed in the finest vector space topology of
V
.

•

In this paper, a
quasi-metric
on a set

is a function
\delta\colon Z\times Z\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
that

–

is
quasi-symmetric
, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\delta(x,y)\leq\delta(y,x)\leq C\delta(x,y);

–

satisfies the
quasi-triangle inequality
, i.e. there exists
C>0
such that

\delta(x,y)\leq C(\delta(x,z)+\delta(z,y))

(or, equivalently,
\delta(x,y)\leq C^{\prime}\max\{\delta(x,z),\delta(z,y)\}
for some other constant
C^{\prime}>0
);

–

separates points
, i.e.
\delta(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow x=y
.

Note that
\delta^{\varepsilon}
is then also a quasi-metric for any
\varepsilon>0
. A quasi-metric space
(Z,\delta)
comes with a Hausdorff topology, and even a uniform structure. In particular, Cauchy sequences and completeness make sense for
(Z,\delta)
. Such uniform structures have a countable basis of entourages, and are thus metrizable, by general theory. In fact, there exists a metric
d
on
X
and
A>0
and constants
\varepsilon>0
only depending on
C
such that the quasi-metric
\delta^{\varepsilon}
satisfies
A^{-1}\delta^{\varepsilon}\leq d\leq A\delta^{\varepsilon}
(see
[
Fri37
,
AIN98
,
PS09
]
, thanks to Prakhar Gupta for hinting at those references).

Test functions and closed

Throughout this paper, we work with a compact Hausdorff topological space

•

\int\varphi\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi\wedge\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n-1}=\int\psi\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\wedge\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n-1}

for all

, while seminegativity requires

\int\varphi\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\wedge\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n-1}\leq 0

.

Definition 1.1
.

For any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}

. We say that

For all

\mathcal{D}_{\theta}+\mathcal{D}_{\theta^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\theta+\theta^{\prime}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}=t\mathcal{D}_{\theta}.

In particular,

, and

\theta\leq\theta^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\theta^{\prime}}.

Since

Constant functions on

1.2.1.
The Kähler case

The above formalism is primarily inspired by the case of a compact Kähler complex analytic space

For all

\int\varphi\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi\wedge\Theta=\int\psi\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\wedge\Theta=-\int\mathrm{d}\varphi\wedge\mathrm{d^{c}}\psi\wedge\Theta

Remark 1.3

More generally, fix a Kähler form

S
of the valuation ring. More precisely, we have

\mathcal{D}\simeq\varinjlim_{\mathcal{X}}\VCar(\mathcal{X})_{\mathbb{R}},

where
\VCar(\mathcal{X})_{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by Cartier divisors on
\mathcal{X}
that are vertical, i.e. supported on the special fiber. The same description applies in the trivially valued case as well, if a model is now understood as a
test configuration

\mathcal{X}\to S:=\mathbb{A}^{1}
(see
[
BHJ17
, §6.1]
,
[
BoJ22a
, §2.2]
).

In the nontrivially valued case, the space
\mathcal{Z}
is defined by setting

(1.4)

\mathcal{Z}:=\varinjlim_{\mathcal{X}}\Num(\mathcal{X}/S),

where
\Num(\mathcal{X}/S)
denotes the (finite dimensional) vector space of relative numerical classes (see
[
BFJ16a
, §4.2]
,
[
GM16
, §4]
, the definition being inspired by
[
BGS95
]
). A form
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
is thus represented by a numerical class
\theta_{\mathcal{X}}\in\Num(\mathcal{X}/S)
for some model
\mathcal{X}
, called a
determination
of
\theta
, two such classes being identified if they coincide after pulling back to some higher model, and we write
\theta\geq 0
if
\theta_{\mathcal{X}}
is (relatively) nef for some (hence any) determination
\mathcal{X}
of
\theta
.

The measure
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}
associated to a tuple of forms
\theta_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}
is a finite linear combination of Dirac masses at divisorial points, whose coefficients can be described in terms of intersection numbers computed on models.

The linear map
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{Z}
takes a vertical divisor
D\in\VCar(\mathcal{X})_{\mathbb{R}}
to its numerical class in
\Num(\mathcal{X}/S)
, and the seminegativity condition (
1.2
) follows the local Hodge index theorem of Yuan–Zhang
[
YZ17
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Again, the same discussion applies to the trivially valued case as well, using test configurations instead of models. In that case, pulling back numerical classes on
X
to the product test configuration further yields an injection

(1.5)

\Num(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}.

Note that only forms lying in
\Num(X)
were considered in
[
BoJ22a
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the non-Archimedean case, one could also work with smooth functions and
(1,1)
-forms in the sense of
[
CD12
]
(see also
[
GK15
,
GK17
,
GJR21
]
), but this will not be considered in the present paper.

Remark 1.5
.

More generally, test configurations for an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold (as in
[
SD18
,
DeR17
]
) can also be approached using the above formalism. This is the topic of forthcoming work of Pietro Piccione.

Remark 1.6
.

Another setting where the above formalism applies is that of tropical toric pluripotential theory as in
[
BGJK21
]
.

1.3.
Bott–Chern cohomology and positive classes

Definition 1.7
.

We define the
Bott–Chern cohomology space
as

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X):=\mathcal{Z}/\mathrm{dd^{c}}\mathcal{D},

and denote by
\theta\mapsto[\theta]
the quotient map
\mathcal{Z}\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
. The
positive cone

\Pos(X)\subset\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)

is defined as the interior of the image of
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
.

Here the interior is taken with respect to the finest vector space topology (see §
1.1
). Concretely, a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
belongs to
\Pos(X)
if, for any
\beta\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
,
\alpha+t\beta
lies in the image of
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
small enough.

Since it is assumed that
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
spans
\mathcal{Z}
, its image in
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
is a convex cone that generates
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
. As a consequence, the positive cone
\Pos(X)
is non-empty as soon as
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
is finite dimensional.

Remark 1.8
.

The image
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
in
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
is not closed in general. This happens already in the complex projective case, see
[
DPS94
, Example 1.7]
.

Since
\mathrm{dd^{c}}
vanishes on
\mathbb{R}\subset\mathcal{D}
, (
1.1
) yields

\int\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\wedge\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n-1}=\int\varphi\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}1\wedge\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n-1}=0

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
and
\theta_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}
. As a result,
(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})\mapsto\int\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}
descends to a symmetric
n
-linear pairing

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\quad(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\mapsto\alpha_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha_{n},

which we call the
intersection pairing
.

Lemma 1.9
.

For all classes
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\Pos(X)
we have
\alpha_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha_{n}>0
.

Proof.

By assumption, the measure
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}
is nonzero for some tuple
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
generates
\mathcal{Z}
, we can further assume
\theta_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
for all
i
. Then
[\theta_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}]=\int\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}>0
. Now we can find
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
such that
\alpha_{i}-\varepsilon[\theta_{i}]\in\Pos(X)
for all
i
, and hence
\alpha_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha_{n}\geq\varepsilon^{n}[\theta_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}]>0
.
∎

Definition 1.10
.

For each
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
and
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
we set

\|\theta\|_{\omega}:=\inf\{C\geq 0\mid\pm\theta\leq C\omega\}\in[0,+\infty],

and we say that
\theta
is
\omega
-bounded
if
\|\theta\|_{\omega}<\infty
.

The set

\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}:=\{\theta\in\mathcal{Z}\mid\|\theta\|_{\omega}<\infty\}

of
\omega
-bounded forms is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{Z}
, on which
\|\cdot\|_{\omega}
defines a norm.

Remark 1.11
.

In general,
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
is a strict subspace of
\mathcal{Z}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}=\mathcal{Z}
iff
\omega
lies in the interior of
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
in the finest vector space topology of
\mathcal{Z}
, which is empty in the non-Archimedean case (see §
1.3.2
below).

We can now characterize positive classes as follows:

Proposition 1.12
.

A class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
lies in
\Pos(X)
iff, for each finite dimensional subspace
V\subset\mathcal{Z}
,
\alpha
admits a representative
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
such that
V\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

Assume
\alpha\in\Pos(X)
, and pick a basis
(\theta_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}
of a finite dimensional vector space
V\subset\mathcal{Z}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
spans
\mathcal{Z}
, for each
i
we can write
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}^{+}-\theta_{i}^{-}
with
\theta_{i}^{\pm}\geq 0
. Since
\alpha\in\Pos(X)
we have
\alpha-\varepsilon[\theta_{i}^{\pm}]\in\Pos(X)
for all
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, and we can thus find
\varepsilon>0
and
\omega_{i}^{\pm}\in\alpha
such that
\omega_{i}^{\pm}-\varepsilon\theta_{i}^{\pm}\geq 0
for all
i
. Now set

\omega:=\tfrac{1}{2r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\omega_{i}^{+}+\omega_{i}^{-})\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}.

Then
[\omega]=\alpha
, and for each
i
we have
\omega\geq\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2r}\theta_{i}^{\pm}\geq\pm\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2r}\theta_{i}
, and hence
\theta_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, i.e.
V\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
. This proves the ‘only if’ part, and the converse is clear.
∎

Corollary 1.13
.

Pick
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
such that
[\theta]\in\Pos(X)
. Then any
f\in\mathcal{D}
can be written as
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
with
f^{\pm}\in\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}=t\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
for some
t>0
. In particular,
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
spans
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.12
, we can find
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
and
C>0
such that
-\mathrm{dd^{c}}f\leq t(\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi)
. Thus
f^{+}:=\varphi+t\psi
lies in
\mathcal{D}_{t\theta}
, and the result follows with
f^{-}:=t\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{C\theta}
.
∎

Following
[
Tho63
]
, we define the
Thompson distance
between
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
as

(1.6)

\dT(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\inf\{\delta\geq 0\mid e^{-\delta}\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq e^{\delta}\omega\}\in[0,+\infty].

We say that
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are
commensurable
if
\dT(\omega,\omega^{\prime})<\infty
. Note that this holds iff
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega^{\prime}}
. Commensurability is an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
. The linear subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
only depends on the commensurability class of
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
, and so does the equivalence class of the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\omega}
.

The next result is readily checked, and left to the reader.

Lemma 1.14
.

The commensurability class of any
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
forms an open convex cone in the normed vector space
(\mathcal{Z}_{\omega},\|\cdot\|_{\omega})
, whose topology is defined by the restriction of the Thompson metric.

We conclude this section with the following useful fact, that will be put to use in §
3
.

Proposition 1.15
.

Each finite subset of
\Pos(X)
can be represented by commensurable forms in
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
.

Proof.

We first claim that any two
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\Pos(X)
admit commensurable representatives. Indeed, setting
\alpha\sim\alpha^{\prime}
when
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
admit commensurable representatives defines an equivalence relation on
\Pos(X)
, and it is thus enough that each equivalence class intersects the line segment
[\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}]
in an open set. Pick
\beta\in[\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}]
, and choose representatives
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
of
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}
. By Proposition
1.12
, we can find a representative
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
of
\beta
such that
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
. By Lemma
1.14
, the commensurability class of
\omega
intersects
[\theta,\omega]
and
[\omega,\theta^{\prime}]
along neighborhood of
\omega
, and it follows, as desired, that the equivalence class of
\beta
intersects
[\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}]
along a neighborhood of
\beta
.

Now pick
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\Pos(X)
. By the first part of the proof, for each
i=2,\dots,r
we can find two commensurable forms
\omega_{1,i},\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
such that
[\omega_{1,i}]=\alpha_{1}
and
[\omega_{i}]=\alpha_{i}
. Then
\omega_{1}:=\tfrac{1}{r-1}\sum_{i=2}^{r}\omega_{1,i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
represents
\alpha_{1}
, and is commensurable to
\omega_{i}
for all
i
since commensurability classes are convex cones.
∎

1.3.1.
The Kähler case

As in §
1.2.1
, assume that
X
is a compact Kähler space. When
X
is nonsingular,
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
coincides with the
(1,1)
-part of the Hodge decomposition of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
. In general, denote by
\mathcal{PH}_{X}
the sheaf of germs of
pluriharmonic functions
, i.e. the kernel of the sheaf morphism
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{X}\to\Omega^{1,1}_{X}
. The existence of partitions of unity implies that the sheaf
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{X}
is soft, and the cohomology long exact sequence associated to the short exact sequence of sheaves

0\to\mathcal{PH}_{X}\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{X}\to\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}_{X}\to 0

thus yields

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{1}(X,\mathcal{PH}_{X}).

We do not know whether this is always finite dimensional, but this holds at least when
X
is normal, as a consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{PH}_{X}
then coincides with the sheaf
\Re\mathcal{O}_{X}
of real parts of holomorphic functions, see
[
BG13
, §4.6.1]
.

The positive cone
\Pos(X)
is compatible with the usual definition, i.e.
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
lies in
\Pos(X)
iff
\alpha
can be represented by a Kähler form. Indeed, given any Kähler form
\omega
, a class
\alpha\in\Pos(X)
can be represented by a form
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
such that
\theta-\varepsilon\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
for some
0<\varepsilon<\ll 1
, so that
\theta
is a Kähler form. In particular, Kähler forms constitute a single commensurability class that maps onto
\Pos(X)
.

1.3.2.
The non-Archimedean case

Assume, as §
1.2.2
, that
X
is a projective Berkovich space over a non-Archimedean field. By definition, any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
is represented by a numerical class on some model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
, whose restriction to the generic fiber defines a numerical class
\{\theta\}\in\Num(X)
on (the projective variety underlying)
X
. This induces a surjective map

(1.7)

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\Num(X),

which is an isomorphism when
X
is smooth and
k
is discretely valued of residue characteristic
0

[
BFJ16a
, Theorem 4.3]
, or when
X
is normal and
k
is algebraically closed
[
Jell16
, Theorem 4.2.7]
). It is also an isomorphism when
k
is trivially valued, with inverse provided by pulling back classes in
\Num(X)
to the trivial test configuration
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}=X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

We claim that
\Pos(X)
coincides with the preimage of the ample cone of
X
under (
1.7
), and that
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
has empty interior in the finest vector space topology of
\mathcal{Z}
(so that Kähler forms do not admit an analogue in the non-Archimedean case).

To see this, we say as in
[
BFJ16a
, §5.1]
that a form
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
is
\mathcal{X}
-positive
for a given model/test configuration
\mathcal{X}
if it is represented by a (relatively) ample class in
\Num(\mathcal{X}/S)
. Note that all
\mathcal{X}
-positive forms are commensurable. As observed in
[
BFJ16a
, Proposition 5.2]
(see also
[
GM16
, Proposition 4.14]
,
[
BoJ22a
, Lemma 3.11]
), any
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
whose image in
\Num(X)
under (
1.7
) is ample can be represented by an
\mathcal{X}
-positive form
\omega
for any sufficiently high model
\mathcal{X}
. Now
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
contains all forms determined on
\mathcal{X}
, but does not contain any
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
-positive form for a model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
strictly dominating
\mathcal{X}
. The claim easily follows.

1.4.
The energy pairing

Mimicking the properties of induced metrics on Deligne pairings (see for instance
[
BE21
, Theorem 8.16]
), and generalizing
[
SD18
, §2.2]
(for a compact Kähler manifold) and
[
BoJ22a
, §3.2]
(for a projective Berkovich space over a trivially valued field), we introduce:

Proposition-Definition 1.16
.

The
energy pairing

(\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{D})^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}\quad((\theta_{0},\varphi_{0}),\dots,(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n}))\mapsto(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n}),

is defined as the unique
(n+1)
-linear symmetric map such that

(1.8)

(0,\varphi_{0})\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})=\int\varphi_{0}\,(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n})

and

(1.9)

(\theta_{0},0)\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},0)=0.

It further satisfies

(1.10)

(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0}+c_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n}+c_{n})=(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})+\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}[\theta_{0}]\cdot\ldots\cdot\widehat{[\theta_{i}]}\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}]

for all
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and

(1.11)

\left(\theta_{0}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau_{0},\varphi_{0}\right)\cdot\ldots\cdot\left(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau_{n},\varphi_{n}\right)=(\theta_{0},\tau_{0}+\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\tau_{n}+\varphi_{n})-(\theta_{0},\tau_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\tau_{n}).

for all
\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Using multilinearity, symmetry and (
1.8
), (
1.9
), we necessarily have

(1.12)

(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\int\varphi_{i}\,\theta_{0}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{i-1}\wedge(\theta_{i+1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{i+1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n}).

This proves uniqueness. To show existence, the only nontrivial part is to check that the right-hand side of (
1.12
) is a symmetric function of the
(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})
. It suffices to see invariance under transpositions, which is an easy consequence of the integration-by-parts formula (
1.1
) (compare
[
SD18
, Proposition 2.3]
).

To see (
1.10
), we may assume
c_{i}=0
for
i>0
, and the result is then a direct consequence of (
1.8
).

Finally, pick
\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
, and set

F(\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n}):=(\theta_{0},\tau_{0}+\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\tau_{n}+\varphi_{n})-(\theta_{0}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau_{n},\varphi_{n}).

By (
1.8
), we have

(0,\varphi_{0})\cdot(\theta_{1},\tau_{1}+\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\tau_{n}+\varphi_{n})-(0,\varphi_{0})\cdot(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau_{n},\varphi_{n})=0.

This implies that
F(\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{n})
is independent of
\varphi_{0}
, and hence equal to
F(0,\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n})
. Applying the same argument successively to
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
, we end up with
F(\varphi_{0},\dots,\varphi_{0})=F(0,\dots,0)=(\theta_{0},\tau_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\tau_{n})
,
which proves (
1.11
).
∎

By (
1.8
), the seminegativity property (
1.2
) translates into

(1.13)

(0,\varphi)^{2}\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n-1},\varphi_{n-1})\leq 0

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
and
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{i}}
.

As in
[
BoJ22a
, §3.2]
, we further note the following straightforward monotonicity properties:

Proposition 1.17
.

For all
\theta_{i},\theta^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\varphi_{i},\varphi^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{i}}
, we have

(1.14)

\varphi_{i}\leq\varphi^{\prime}_{i}\text{ for all }i\Longrightarrow(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})\leq(\theta_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi^{\prime}_{n});

(1.15)

\varphi_{i}\leq 0\text{ and }0\leq\theta_{i}\leq\theta^{\prime}_{i}\text{ for all }i\Longrightarrow(\theta^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta^{\prime}_{n},\varphi_{n})\leq(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n}).

Combining (
1.14
) and (
1.10
), we infer the Lipschitz property

(1.16)

\left|(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})-(\theta_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi^{\prime}_{n})\right|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sup|\varphi_{i}-\varphi^{\prime}_{i}|[\theta_{0}]\cdot\ldots\cdot\widehat{[\theta_{i}]}\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}]

for all
\varphi_{i},\varphi^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta_{i}}
.

Following
[
BoJ22a
, §7.2]
, we next establish a lower bound for the energy pairing, which will play a crucial role in §
3.2
below.

Theorem 1.18
.

Assume
\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
are commensurable, and set
\delta:=\max_{i,j}\dT(\omega_{i},\omega_{j})
. If
0\geq\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, then

0\geq(\omega_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\omega_{n},\varphi_{n})\gtrsim e^{O(\delta)}\min_{i}(\omega_{i},\varphi_{i})^{n+1}.

Here
\dT
denotes the Thompson metric (
1.6
), and the implicit constant in
O(\delta)
only depends on
n
(see §
1.1
).

Lemma 1.19
.

For any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
,
\varphi\mapsto(\theta,\varphi)^{n+1}
is concave on
\mathcal{D}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

This is a formal consequence of (
1.13
), see Appendix
A
.
∎

Lemma 1.20
.

Pick
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
and
t\geq 1
such that
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq t\omega
. For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}
such that
\varphi\leq 0
, we then have

0\geq(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n+1}\geq t^{n}(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}.

Proof.

By (
1.15
) we have

0\geq(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n+1}\geq(t\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}=t^{n+1}(\omega,t^{-1}\varphi)^{n+1}.

Since
t^{-1}\in[0,1]
, concavity of the energy (Lemma
1.19
) yields

(\omega,t^{-1}\varphi)^{n+1}\geq t^{-1}(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 1.21
.

Pick
\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{r}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
and
0\geq\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{i}}
for
i=0,\dots,r
. Assume also given
t\geq 1
such that
\omega_{i}\leq t\omega_{j}
for all
i,j
. Then

(1.17)

(\sum_{i}\omega_{i},\sum_{i}\varphi_{i})^{n+1}\geq C_{r,n}t^{rn}\sum_{i}(\omega_{i},\varphi_{i})^{n+1}

with
C_{r,n}:=\left(2^{r}r!\right)^{n}
.

Proof.

Assume first
r=1
. Set
\omega:=\frac{t}{1+t}(\omega_{0}+\omega_{1})
, and observe that
\omega_{0}\leq\omega\leq t\omega_{0}
,
\omega_{1}\leq\omega\leq t\omega_{1}
, and hence
\tfrac{1}{2}(\omega_{0}+\omega_{1})\leq\omega\leq\tfrac{t}{2}(\omega_{0}+\omega_{1})
. Thus

\left(\omega_{0}+\omega_{1},\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1}\right)^{n+1}=2^{n+1}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}),\tfrac{1}{2}(\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1})\right)^{n+1}\\
\geq 2^{n+1}\left(\omega,\tfrac{1}{2}(\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1})\right)^{n+1}\geq 2^{n}\left((\omega,\varphi_{0})^{n+1}+(\omega,\varphi_{1})^{n+1}\right)\\
\geq(2t)^{n}\left((\omega_{0},\varphi_{0})^{n+1}+(\omega_{1},\varphi_{1})^{n+1}\right),

where the first inequality holds by (
1.15
), the second one by Lemma
1.19
, and the third one by Lemma
1.20
.

Assume now
r\geq 2
, and set
\omega^{\prime}_{0}:=\sum_{i>0}\omega_{i}
,
\varphi^{\prime}_{0}:=\sum_{i>0}\varphi_{i}
. Since
(rt)^{-1}\omega_{0}\leq\omega^{\prime}_{0}\leq rt\omega_{0}
, the first part of the proof yields

(\sum_{i}\omega_{i},\sum_{i}\varphi_{i})^{n+1}=(\omega_{0}+\omega^{\prime}_{0},\varphi_{0}+\varphi^{\prime}_{0})^{n+1}\geq(2rt)^{n}\left((\omega_{0},\varphi_{0})^{n+1}+(\omega^{\prime}_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})^{n+1}\right)

By induction, we have on the other hand

(\omega^{\prime}_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})^{n+1}\geq C_{r-1,n}t^{(r-1)n}\sum_{i>0}(\omega_{i},\varphi_{i})^{n+1},

The result follows, since
(\omega_{0},\varphi_{0})^{n+1}\leq 0
and

C_{r,n}t^{rn}=(2rt)^{n}C_{r-1,n}\geq(2rt)^{n}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.18
.

Expanding out
(\omega_{0}+\dots+\omega_{n},\varphi_{0}+\dots+\varphi_{n})^{n+1}
yields

(n+1)!(\omega_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\omega_{n},\varphi_{n})\geq(\omega_{0}+\dots+\omega_{n},\varphi_{0}+\dots+\varphi_{n})^{n+1},

and we conclude by Lemma
1.21
with

.
∎

1.5.
The Monge–Ampère operator and the submean value property

Pick
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
. We define its
volume
as

V_{\omega}:=\int\omega^{n}=[\omega]^{n},

which is positive by Lemma
1.9
, and introduce the probability measure

\mu_{\omega}:=V_{\omega}^{-1}\omega^{n}.

Definition 1.22
.

The
Monge–Ampère operator

\MA_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{M}
is defined by setting

\MA_{\omega}(\varphi):=V_{\omega}^{-1}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

Equivalently,

(1.18)

\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\mu_{\omega_{\varphi}}.

By (
1.16
), the
Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality

(1.19)

\left|\int\tau\left(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)-\MA_{\omega}(\psi)\right)\right|\leq n\sup|\varphi-\psi|

is satisfied for all
\varphi,\psi,\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.

We next consider the quantity

(1.20)

T_{\omega}:=\sup\left\{\sup\varphi-\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\right\}\in[0,+\infty].

Thus
T_{\omega}<\infty
iff there exists
C>0
such that the submean value inequality

(1.21)

\sup\varphi\leq\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}+C

holds for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.

Proposition 1.23
.

If
T_{\omega}<\infty
, then
T_{\omega^{\prime}}<\infty
for any
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega^{\prime}]\in\Pos(X)
.

Definition 1.24
.

We say that the
submean value property
holds if
T_{\omega}<\infty
for some (hence any)
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
.

When
X
is a compact Kähler or projective Berkovich space, this property holds iff
X
is irreducible (see Theorem
1.26
below).

Lemma 1.25
.

Pick
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
commensurable to
\omega
. Then

(1.22)

T_{\omega_{\tau}}\leq T_{\omega}+(n+2)\sup|\tau|

and

(1.23)

T_{\omega^{\prime}}\leq e^{O(\delta)}T_{\omega}

with
\delta:=\dT(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
.

Recall that, in this paper, the implicit constant in
O
only depends on
n
(see §
1.1
).

Proof.

Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
. Then
\varphi+\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and the Chern–Levine–Nirenberg inequality (
1.19
) yields

\varphi+\tau\leq\int(\varphi+\tau)\MA_{\omega}(0)+T_{\omega}\leq\int(\varphi+\tau)\MA_{\omega}(\tau)+n\sup|\tau|+T_{\omega}.

This proves (
1.22
), in view of (
1.18
).

For each
t>0
we have
\mathcal{D}_{t\omega}=t\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and hence

. Further,
\omega^{\prime}\leq\omega
implies
\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and

and hence

. These properties imply (
1.23
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.23
.

The condition

1.6.
Monge–Ampère energy and the Dirichlet functional

From now on we fix
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
.

Definition 1.28
.

The
Monge–Ampère energy

\en_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(1.24)

\en_{\omega}(\varphi):=\frac{(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}}{(n+1)V_{\omega}}.

For all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}
we have

(1.25)

\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega}(\psi)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}V_{\omega}^{-1}\int(\varphi-\psi)\,\omega_{\varphi}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-j}.

By (
1.8
), this amounts indeed to the basic identity

(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}-(\omega,\psi)^{n+1}=(0,\varphi-\psi)\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{n}(\omega,\varphi)^{j}\cdot(\omega,\psi)^{n-j}.

Assume now
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. By (
1.25
), we then have

(1.26)

\varphi\leq\psi\Longrightarrow\en_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\en_{\omega}(\psi).

Further,
\en_{\omega}((1-t)\varphi+t\psi)
is a polynomial function of
t\in[0,1]
, with

(1.27)

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\en_{\omega}((1-t)\varphi+t\psi)=\int(\psi-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi).

This characterizes
\en_{\omega}
as the unique primitive of the Monge–Ampère operator that vanishes at
0\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. By Lemma
1.19
,
\en_{\omega}
is concave on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, which translates into

(1.28)

\en_{\omega}(\psi)\leq\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\int(\psi-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)

for all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.

Lemma 1.29
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
, we have

(1.29)

\en_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)=\en_{\omega}(\varphi+\tau)-\en_{\omega}(\tau).

Further,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}\Longleftrightarrow\varphi+\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.

Proof.

The first point follows from (
1.11
). Further,
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
iff
\omega+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\tau+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\geq 0
, which is also equivalent to
\varphi+\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
.
∎

Definition 1.30
.

We define the
Dirichlet functional

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\times\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by setting

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi):=\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega}(\psi)+\int(\psi-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi).

By concavity of
\en_{\omega}
,
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)
is a convex function of
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Note also that

(1.30)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi,\varphi)=\int(\varphi-\psi)(\MA_{\omega}(\psi)-\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)).

By (
A.4
), we more explicitly have

(1.31)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)=V_{\omega}^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{j+1}{n+1}\int(\varphi-\psi)\,\mathrm{dd^{c}}(\psi-\varphi)\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-1-j}.

We simply write

(1.32)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi):=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(0,\varphi)=\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}-\en_{\omega}(\varphi).

Example 1.31
.

When
n=1
, we have

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi,\varphi)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int(\varphi-\psi)\mathrm{dd^{c}}(\psi-\varphi),

which recovers the usual expression for the Dirichlet functional on a Riemann surface.

For each
R>0
we set

(1.33)

\mathcal{D}_{\omega,R}:=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\mid\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq R\}.

Lemma 1.32
.

For each
R>0
,
\mathcal{D}_{\omega,R}
is a convex subset of
\mathcal{D}
that generates it.

Proof.

By convexity of
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(0,\cdot)
,
\mathcal{D}_{\omega,R}
is convex. Since
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
spans
\mathcal{D}
(see Corollary
1.13
), to see that
\mathcal{D}_{\omega,R}
spans it suffices to show that any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
satisfies
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(t\varphi)\leq R
for
0<t\ll 1
, which holds since
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(t\varphi)
is a polynomial function of
t
with
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(0)=0
.
∎

We may now collect the fundamental properties of the Dirichlet functional in the next result.

Theorem 1.33
.

For all
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime},\psi,\psi^{\prime},\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
t\in[0,1]
, the following holds:

•

quasi-symmetry:

(1.34)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi,\varphi);

•

quasi-triangle inequality:

(1.35)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\tau)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau,\psi);

•

quadratic estimate:

(1.36)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,(1-t)\varphi+t\psi)\lesssim t^{2}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi);

•

uniform concavity:

\en_{\omega}((1-t)\varphi+t\psi)-[(1-t)\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+t\en_{\omega}(\psi)]\gtrsim t(1-t)\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi).

For all
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime},\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,R}
, we further have the following Hölder estimates:

(1.37)

\left|\int(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})\left(\MA_{\omega}(\psi)-\MA_{\omega}(\psi^{\prime})\right)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})^{\alpha}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi,\psi^{\prime})^{1/2}R^{1/2-\alpha};

and

(1.38)

\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\max\{\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}),\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi,\psi^{\prime})\}^{\alpha}R^{1-\alpha},

where
\alpha:=2^{-n}
.

Proof.

In view of (
1.13
), this is a direct consequence of Theorem
A.3
applied to

•

the vector space
V:=\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{D}
with projection
\pi\colon V\to\mathcal{Z}
onto the first factor;

•

the convex cone
P:=\{(\theta,\varphi)\in V\mid\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\theta}\}
;

•

the homogeneous polynomial
F\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
F(\theta,\varphi):=-\frac{(\theta,\varphi)^{n+1}}{(n+1)V_{\omega}}
.

∎

\mu=\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}
with
\theta_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
need not be of finite energy with respect to
\omega
in general (see however Theorem
3.4
below):

Example 2.7
.

Let
X
be either a compact Kähler or projective Berkovich space. For each irreducible component
Y
of
X
and each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
, we then have

(2.11)

\mu(Y)=\frac{[\omega|_{Y}]^{n}}{[\omega]^{n}}.

Indeed, this is proved in
[
BoJ22a
, Corollary 9.13]
in the trivially valued case, and the proof can be adapted to the general case. Now (
2.11
) fails in general for
\mu=\mu_{\omega^{\prime}}
with
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
such that
[\omega]\neq[\omega^{\prime}]\in\Pos(X)
, and hence
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega^{\prime}}\neq\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
.

By (
2.3
), we have, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
,

\int\varphi\,(\mu_{\omega}-\mu)\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu).

The following converse will come in handy.

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies

S:=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,R}}\int\varphi(\mu_{\omega}-\mu)<\infty

for some
R>0
. Then
\mu
has finite energy, and

(2.12)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\lesssim S(1+R^{-1}S).

Proof.

Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and set
J:=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)
. By (
1.36
), we have
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(t\varphi)\lesssim t^{2}J
for any
t\in[0,1]
, and we can thus choose

1\leq a\lesssim 1+(R^{-1}J)^{1/2}

such that
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(a^{-1}\varphi)\leq R
. By assumption, we then have
\int a^{-1}\varphi\,(\mu_{\omega}-\mu)\leq S
, and hence

\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,\mu=\int\varphi\,(\mu_{\omega}-\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq aS-J

\lesssim S+SR^{-1/2}J^{1/2}-J\leq S+\tfrac{1}{4}S^{2}R^{-1},

where the last inequality follows from the elementary estimate
\sup_{y\geq 0}(xy^{1/2}-y)=x^{2}/4
for any
x\geq 0
. Taking the supremum over
\varphi
yields (
2.12
).
∎

2.3.
Legendre transform of the energy

Here we compute the Legendre transform of the convex functional
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\cdot,0)\colon\mathcal{M}\to[0,+\infty]
.

Definition 2.9
.

For any
f\in\Cz(X)
we set

\ten_{\omega}(f):=\sup_{f\geq\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}}\en_{\omega}(\varphi).

By monotonicity of
\en_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
(see (
1.26
)), the functional
\ten_{\omega}\colon\Cz(X)\to\mathbb{R}
so defined restricts to
\en_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Like the latter,
\ten_{\omega}
is further concave, monotone increasing, and equivariant with respect to translation, i.e.

\ten_{\omega}(f+c)=\ten_{\omega}(f)+c\text{ for }c\in\mathbb{R}.

Proposition 2.10
.

For all
f\in\Cz(X)
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
we have

(2.13)

\ten_{\omega}(f)=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}}\{\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\nu)+\int f\,\nu\};\quad\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)=\sup_{g\in\Cz(X)}\{\ten_{\omega}(g)-\int g\,\mu\}.

Proof.

Define the (convex) Legendre transform
\ten_{\omega}^{\vee}\colon\Cz(X)^{\vee}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
as the right-hand side of (
2.13
), i.e.

\ten_{\omega}^{\vee}(\mu):=\sup_{g\in\Cz(X)}\{\ten_{\omega}(g)-\int f\,\mu\}.

Since
\ten_{\omega}
is increasing and equivariant, it is straightforward to see that
\ten_{\omega}^{\vee}(\mu)<\infty
implies
\mu\geq 0
and
\int\mu=1
, i.e.
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
(compare
[
BoJ22a
, Proposition 9.8]
). By Legendre duality, the result is thus equivalent to
\ten_{\omega}^{\vee}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
. Since
\ten_{\omega}
restricts to
\en_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, we trivially have
\ten_{\omega}^{\vee}(\mu)\geq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
. Conversely, pick
f\in\Cz(X)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
with
\varphi\leq f
. Then

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\geq\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,\mu\geq\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int f\,\mu,

where the first and second inequality respectively follow from (
2.2
) and
\varphi\leq f
. Taking the supremum over
\varphi
and then over
f
yields
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\geq\ten_{\omega}(f)-\int f\,\mu
and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\geq\ten_{\omega}^{\vee}(\mu)
.
∎

2.4.
Orthogonality and differentiability

Definition 2.11
.

We say that
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}

admits maxima
if, for all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
f\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\max\{\varphi,\psi\}<f
pointwise on
X
, there exists
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
with
\max\{\varphi,\psi\}\leq\tau<f
.

This equivalently means that, for any
f\in\mathcal{D}
, the poset

\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}:=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\mid\varphi<f\}

is inductive. We can then consider limits of nets indexed by
\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}
. For instance, note that

(2.14)

\ten_{\omega}(f)=\lim_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}}\en_{\omega}(\varphi).

Example 2.12
.

If
X
is a compact Kähler space, then
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
admits maxima: take
\tau:=\widetilde{\max}(\varphi,\psi)
for an appropriate regularized max function
\widetilde{\max}
.

Example 2.13
.

If
X
is a projective Berkovich space, then
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
also admits maxima, since
\mathbb{Q}
-PL functions in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
are dense in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and stable under max.

Remark 2.14
.

While we will not pursue this direction here, one can introduce as in
[
BFJ16a
,
BE21
,
BoJ22a
]
the space
\PSH(\omega)
of
\omega
-psh functions
\varphi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
, defined as usc functions that can be obtained as pointwise limits of decreasing nets in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and such that
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}>-\infty
. Then
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
admits maxima iff
\PSH(\omega)
(or, equivalently, the subspace
\CPSH(\omega):=\PSH(\omega)\cap\Cz(X)
of continuous
\omega
-phs functions) is stable under max.

Definition 2.15
.

We say that
\omega
has the
orthogonality property
if
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
admits maxima and

(2.15)

\lim_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}}\int(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=0

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}
.

Explicitly, this means for that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
such that
\varphi_{0}<f
and
\int(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\varepsilon
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
with
\varphi_{0}\leq\varphi<f
.

Remark 2.16
.

The orthogonality property for
\omega
only depends on
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
.
Indeed, for any
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
f\in\mathcal{D}
we have

(2.16)

\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau},<f}\Longleftrightarrow\varphi+\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f+\tau}

This implies that
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
has finite maxima iff
\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
does, and similarly for the orthogonality property, using

\MA_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi+\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad\int(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)=\int\left((f+\tau)-(\varphi+\tau)\right)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi+\tau).

Example 2.17
.

Assume
X
is a compact Kähler space. Conjecturally, the orthogonality property always holds. This is known when
X
is nonsingular, or
X
is projective and
[\omega]\in\Amp(X)
, see Appendix
B
for a more detailed discussion.

Remark 2.18
.

More generally, the orthogonality property also holds in the setting of Remark
1.3
(assuming
X
is a compact Kähler manifold). This follows indeed from
[
LN15
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Recall from Corollary
1.13
that any test function
f\in\mathcal{D}
can be written as

(2.17)

f=f^{+}-f^{-},\quad f^{\pm}\in\mathcal{D}_{C\omega}

for some
C=C(f)>0
. In line with
[
BFJ15
, §7]
, we show:

Proposition 2.19
.

Assume
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
admits maxima. The following properties are then equivalent:

(i)

\omega
has the orthogonality property;

(ii)

for any
f\in\mathcal{D}
written as (
2.17
) for a given
C>0
, we have

(2.18)

\left|\ten_{\omega}(\varphi+f)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int f\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim C\sup|f|

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
;

(iii)

in the setting of (ii), we have

(2.19)

\left|\ten_{\omega}(\varphi+tf)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-t\int f\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim Ct^{2}\sup|f|

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Assume (i). Write
f\in\mathcal{D}
as in (
2.17
), and pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. For any
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<\varphi+\varepsilon f}
, (
1.28
) yields

\int(\psi-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\psi)\leq\en_{\omega}(\psi)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\int(\psi-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\int f\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi).

By (
1.19
), we also have
\left|\int f(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)-\MA_{\omega}(\psi))\right|\leq 2nC\sup|\varphi-\psi|
, and we infer

(2.20)

\left|\en_{\omega}(\psi)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int f\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\leq\int\left((\varphi+f)-\psi\right)\MA_{\omega}(\psi)+2nC\sup|\varphi-\psi|.

Now
\lim_{\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<\varphi+f}}\en_{\omega}(\psi)=\ten_{\omega}(\varphi+f)
(see (
2.14
)), while orthogonality yields

\lim_{\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<\varphi+f}}\int\left((\varphi+f)-\psi\right)\MA_{\omega}(\psi)=0.

Further, any
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<\varphi+f}
large enough is greater than
\varphi-\sup|f|\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<\varphi+f}
, and hence satisfies
\sup|\varphi-\psi|\leq\sup|f|
. As a result, (
2.20
) implies

\left|\ten_{\omega}(\varphi+f)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int f\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\leq 2nC\sup|f|,

which shows (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Next, (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii), since
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
with
f\in\mathcal{D}_{C\omega}
implies
tf=\mathrm{sgn}(t)(|t|f^{+}-|t|f^{-})
with
|t|f^{\pm}\in\mathcal{D}_{C|t|\omega}
.

Finally, assume (iii), and pick
f\in\mathcal{D}
. We need to show that

L:=\limsup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}}\int(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\geq 0

vanishes. Write
f
as in (
2.17
) for some
C>0
. Pick also
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}
, and set
g:=f-\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
. For any
t\in[0,1]
we have
\varphi+tg=(1-t)\varphi+tf\leq f
, and hence
\ten_{\omega}(\varphi+tg)\leq\ten_{\omega}(f)
. On the other hand, since
f-\varphi=f^{+}-(f^{-}+\varphi)
with
f^{+},<f^{-}+\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{(C+1)\omega}
, (iii) yields a constant
A>0
only depending on
\varphi
such that

0\leq t\int(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\ten_{\omega}(\varphi+tg)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+t^{2}A\sup|g|\leq\ten_{\omega}(f)-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+t^{2}A\sup|g|.

Since any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}
large enough is greater then
f^{+}-\sup f^{-}
, it satisfies

0\leq g=f-\varphi\leq\sup f^{-}-f^{-}\leq B

with
B
only depending on
f
. We infer
0\leq tL\leq t^{2}AB
. Dividing by
t>0
and letting
t\to 0_{+}
yields, as desired,
L=0
. This proves (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i).
∎

.

Assume

BE21
, Theorem A]

2.5.
Maximizing sequences

Definition 2.21
.

We say that a sequence
(\psi_{i})
in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
is
maximizing for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
if it computes the energy of
\mu
(
2.2
),
i.e.
\en_{\omega}(\psi_{i})-\int\psi_{i}\,\mu\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
.

Equivalently,
(\psi_{i})
is maximizing for
\mu
iff
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{i})\to 0
, see (
2.4
).

Example 2.22
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
the constant sequence
\psi_{i}=\varphi
is maximizing for
\mu=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
(see (
2.5
)).

As a key consequence of Proposition
2.19
, we show:

Theorem 2.23
.

Assume
\omega
has the orthogonality property. Pick
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and a maximizing sequence
\psi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Then the measures
\mu_{i}:=\MA_{\omega}(\psi_{i})
converge strongly to
\mu
in
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
,
i.e.
\mu_{i}\to\mu
weakly and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{i})\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
. In particular, the image of the Monge–Ampère operator

\MA_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}

is dense in the strong topology.

Proof.

Pick
f\in\mathcal{D}
, and choose
C>0
such that
f=f^{+}-f^{-}
with
f^{\pm}\in\mathcal{D}_{C\omega}
. Since we assume orthogonality, Proposition
2.19
yields
A>0
such that

\left|\ten_{\omega}(\psi_{i}+tf)-\en_{\omega}(\psi_{i})-t\int f\,\mu_{i}\right|\leq At^{2}\sup|f|

for all
i
and
t>0
. By Proposition
2.10
and (
2.4
), we have, on the other hand,

\ten_{\omega}(\psi_{i}+tf)\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+\int(\psi_{i}+tf)\mu=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{i})+\en_{\omega}(\psi_{i})+t\int f\,\mu.

Combining these estimates, we get

t\int f\,\mu_{i}\leq t\int f\,\mu+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{i})+At^{2}.

Since
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{i})\to 0
, we infer

t\limsup_{i}\int f\,\mu_{i}\leq t\int f\,\mu+At^{2}.

Dividing by
t
and letting
t\to 0_{+}
yields
\limsup_{i}\int f\,\mu_{i}\leq\int f\,\mu
. Replacing
f
with
-f
, we get
\lim_{i}\int f\,\mu_{i}=\int f\,\mu
. By density of
\mathcal{D}
in
\Cz(X)
, this shows
\mu_{i}\to\mu
weakly.

For each
i
we have
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{i})=\en_{\omega}(\psi_{i})-\int\psi_{i}\,\mu_{i}
(see (
2.5
)), and
\en_{\omega}(\psi_{i})-\int\psi_{i}\,\mu\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
, since
(\psi_{i})
is maximizing for
\mu
. It only remains to prove
\int\psi_{i}\,(\mu_{i}-\mu)\to 0
. Since
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi_{i})
is bounded (see (
2.7
), (
1.37
) yields
C>0
such that

\left|\int\psi_{i}\,(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j})\right|\lesssim C\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi_{i},\psi_{j})^{\alpha}

for all
i,j
, and hence

\left|\int\psi_{i}\,(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j})\right|\lesssim C\max\{\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{i}),\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{j})\}^{\alpha},

by (
2.7
). Since
\mu_{j}\to\mu
weakly and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{j})\to 0
as
j\to\infty
, we infer

\left|\int\psi_{i}\,(\mu_{i}-\mu)\right|\lesssim C\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi_{i})^{\alpha},

and we conclude, as desired, that the left-hand side tends to
0
as
i\to\infty
.
∎

2.6.
The Dirichlet quasi-metric

From now on,
we assume that the orthogonality property holds
for
\omega
. Recall from Examples
2.17
and
2.20
, that this is the case if
X
is a compact Kähler manifold, and for any projective Berkovich space.

Theorem 2.24
.

There exists a unique continuous functional

\delta_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}\times\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

such that

(2.21)

\delta_{\omega}(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi),\MA_{\omega}(\psi))=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)

for all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Furthermore:

(i)

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}

we have

(2.22)

\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\MA_{\omega}(\psi))=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu,\psi),\quad\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\mu_{\omega})=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu);

(ii)

\delta_{\omega}
is a quasi-metric: for all

we have

(2.23)

\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)=0\Leftrightarrow\mu=\nu,\quad\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)\approx\delta_{\omega}(\nu,\mu),\quad\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)\lesssim\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\rho)+\delta_{\omega}(\rho,\nu);

(iii)

the quasi-metric
\delta_{\omega}
satisfies the Hölder continuity property

(2.24)

\left|\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)-\delta_{\omega}(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\max\{\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}),\delta_{\omega}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\}^{\alpha}R^{1-\alpha}

for all
R>0
and
\mu,\mu^{\prime},\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega,R}
, with

(iv)

for all

and

,

\left|\int(\varphi-\psi)(\mu-\nu)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)^{\alpha}\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)^{1/2}R^{1/2-\alpha}.

We call
\delta_{\omega}
the

of
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}

For all

)). In the general case, pick a maximizing sequence
(\psi_{i})
for
\nu
, and set
\nu_{i}:=\MA_{\omega}(\psi_{i})
. By (
2.24
), we have
\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu_{i})\to\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)
uniformly for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega,R}
, and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.26
.

We already know that
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
only depends on
[\omega]
(see Proposition
2.6
). Pick
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
,
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}=\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega_{\tau}}
, and choose maximizing sequences
(\varphi_{i})
,
(\psi_{i})
in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
for
\mu,\nu
, so that
\delta_{\omega_{\tau}}(\mu,\nu)=\lim_{i}\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i})
. By (
2.8
),
(\varphi_{i}+\tau)
and
(\psi_{i}+\tau)
are maximizing sequences in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
for
\mu,\nu
, and hence
\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)=\lim_{i}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i})
. Now (
1.39
) yields
\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi_{i},\psi_{i})=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i}+\tau,\psi_{i}+\tau)
, which proves
\delta_{\omega_{\tau}}(\mu,\nu)=\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)
and proves that
\delta_{\omega}
only depends on
[\omega]
.

We next show that the topology of
(\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega},\delta_{\omega})
is the strong topology, i.e. a net
(\mu_{i})
converges strongly to
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
iff
\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu)\to 0
. When the latter holds, (
2.25
) implies
\mu_{i}\to\mu
weakly (since
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
spans the dense subspace
\mathcal{D}
of
\Cz(X)
), while (
2.24
) yields
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{i})=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{i},0)\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
. Thus
\mu_{i}\to\mu
strongly, and the converse holds by strong continuity of
\delta_{\omega}
.

Finally, consider a Cauchy net
(\mu_{i})
in
(\mathcal{M}^{1},\delta_{\omega})
. Then
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{i})=\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu_{\omega})
is eventually bounded. By weak compactness of
\mathcal{M}
, we may assume, after passing to a subnet, that
(\mu_{i})
admits a weak limit
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
. Since
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}
is weakly lsc on
\mathcal{M}
, we get
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\leq\liminf_{i}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{i})<+\infty
, i.e.
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
. It remains to show
\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu)\to 0
. To see this, pick
\varepsilon>0
and
i_{0}
such that
\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})\leq\varepsilon
for all
i,j\geq i_{0}
. Since
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{j})
is bounded and
\mu_{j}\to\mu
weakly, Lemma
2.27
yields
\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu)\leq\liminf_{j}\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu_{j})\leq\varepsilon
, and we are done.
∎

To conclude this section, we show:

Proposition 2.28
.

For each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
,
\delta_{\omega}(\cdot,\nu)\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is strictly convex, and we further have the uniform convexity estimate

(1-t)\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\nu)+t\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{1},\nu)-\delta_{\omega}((1-t)\mu_{0}+t\mu_{1},\nu)\gtrsim t(1-t)\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

for all
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

By density of the image of
\MA_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and continuity of
\delta_{\omega}
, we may assume without loss

\nu=\MA_{\omega}(\psi)
with
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and hence
\delta_{\omega}(\cdot,\nu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\cdot,\psi)
. Set

J_{t}:=(1-t)\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\psi)+t\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{1},\psi),\quad\mu_{t}:=(1-t)\mu_{0}+t\mu_{1},

and pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Applying (
2.4
) to
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
yields

J_{t}=\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega}(\psi)+\int(\psi-\varphi)\mu_{t}+(1-t)\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\varphi)+t\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{1},\varphi),

and hence

J_{t}-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\en_{\omega}(\psi)+\int(\varphi-\psi)\mu_{t}\geq t(1-t)(\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\psi)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{1},\psi)),

using the elementary estimate
(1-t)a+tb\geq t(1-t)(a+b)
for
a,b\geq 0
(see for instance
[
BoJ22a
, Lemma 7.29]
).
By (
2.23
), this implies

J_{t}-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\en_{\omega}(\psi)+\int(\varphi-\psi)\mu_{t}\gtrsim t(1-t)\delta_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}),

and taking the infimum over
\varphi
shows
J_{t}-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{t},\psi)\gtrsim t(1-t)\dd_{\omega}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

2.7.
An equivalent metric on
\mathcal{M}^{1}

As recalled in §
1.1
, the quasi-metric space
(\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega},\delta_{\omega})
is metrizable, by general theory. Here we introduce a concrete metric that define the strong topology of
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
. Recall from (
1.33
) that

\mathcal{D}_{\omega,1}=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\mid\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq 1\}.

Proposition 2.29
.

Setting

(2.29)

\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,1}}\left|\int\varphi(\mu-\nu)\right|

yields a complete metric on
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
that defines the strong topology. Furthermore:

(i)

the metric
\dd_{\omega}
and the Dirichlet quasi-metric
\delta_{\omega}
share the same bounded sets;

(ii)

they are Hölder equivalent on bounded sets,
i.e.

(2.30)

\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)\lesssim\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)^{1/2}R^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)\lesssim\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)R^{1/2}

for all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega,R}
with
R\geq 1
;

(iii)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
we have

(2.31)

\left|\int\varphi\,(\mu-\nu)\right|\lesssim(\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)^{1/2}+1)\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\mu).

Proof of Proposition
2.29
.

Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Since
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(a^{-1}\varphi)\lesssim a^{-2}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)
for
a\geq 1
(see (
1.36
)), we can choose
1\leq a\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)^{1/2}+1
such that
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(a^{-1}\varphi)\leq 1
. Then
\left|\int a^{-1}\varphi\,(\mu-\nu)\right|\leq\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)
, which proves (
2.31
).

The first part of (
2.30
) is a direct consequence of (
2.25
). It shows, in particular, that
\dd_{\omega}
is finite valued. It is also clear that
\dd_{\omega}
is symmetric, vanishes on the diagonal, and satisfies the triangle inequality. Since
\mathcal{D}_{\omega,1}
spans the dense subspace
\mathcal{D}
of
\Cz(X)
(see Lemma
1.32
),
\dd_{\omega}
further separates points, and hence defines a metric on
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
.

The first part of (
2.30
) also shows that
\mu_{i}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
implies
\dd_{\omega}(\mu_{i},\mu)\to 0
, by continuity of
\delta_{\omega}
, and it follows that the metric
\dd_{\omega}
is continuous. By density of the image of
\MA_{\omega}
, it is thus enough to show the second half of (
2.30
) when
\mu=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
and
\nu=\MA_{\omega}(\psi)
with
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Then
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\leq R
and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi)\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\nu)\leq R
(see (
2.6
)), while

\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)\leq\int(\varphi-\psi)(\mu-\nu),

by (
2.21
) and (
1.30
). Using (
2.31
), we get the second half of (
2.30
). Next pick
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and set
R:=\max\{1,\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\mu_{\omega})\}
and
S:=\max\{1,\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\mu_{\omega})\}
. Applying (
2.30
) to
\nu=\mu_{\omega}
yields

S\lesssim R
, and also
R\lesssim SR^{1/2}
, i.e.
R\lesssim S^{2}
. This proves (i) and (ii). Since
\delta_{\omega}
defines the strong topology of
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
and is complete (see Theorem
2.26
), the same therefore holds, as desired, for
\dd_{\omega}
.
∎

By Theorem
2.26
, the quasi-metric space
(\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega},\delta_{\omega})
only depends on
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
. Here we show:

Lemma 2.30
.

For each
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
we have
\dd_{\omega_{\tau}}\lesssim(\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau)^{1/2}+1)\dd_{\omega}
. In particular, the Lipschitz equivalence class of the metric space
(\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega},\dd_{\omega})
only depends on
[\omega]
.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
, (
1.39
) yields
\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(0,\varphi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau,\varphi+\tau)
.
When
\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)\leq 1
, the quasi-triangle inequality (
1.35
) thus yields
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi+\tau)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(0,\varphi+\tau)\lesssim 1+J
with
J:=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(0,\tau)
. By (
2.31
) we infer

\left|\int(\varphi+\tau)(\mu-\nu)\right|\lesssim(1+J^{1/2})\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu),\quad\left|\int\tau(\mu-\nu)\right|\lesssim(1+J^{1/2})\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu).

Thus
\left|\int\varphi(\mu-\nu)\right|\lesssim(1+J^{1/2})\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)
. Taking the supremum over
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
such that
\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)\leq 1
yields the result.
∎

In what follows we consider

with
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)

1.24
), and use it to investigate the dependence of
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
on
\omega
and establish a Hölder continuity estimate for the energy pairing.

Recall that the standing assumptions hold when

is a compact connected Kähler manifold, or any irreducible projective Berkovich space (see Theorem

3.1.
Lipschitz estimates for the energy

Recall from §
2.7
the metric
\dd_{\omega}
, which defines the strong topology of
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
. As a first key consequence of the submean value property, we show:

Theorem 3.1
.

The Lipschitz equivalence class of the metric space
(\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega},\dd_{\omega})
is independent of
\omega
.

In particular, the topological space
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{\omega}
is independent of
\omega
(see Proposition
2.29
), and will henceforth simply by denoted by
\mathcal{M}^{1}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
is commensurable to
\omega
, with
[\omega^{\prime}]\in\Pos(X)
. For all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}
we then have

(3.1)

\dd_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu,\nu)\leq e^{O(\delta)}(1+T_{\omega})^{1/2}\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)

with
\delta:=\dT(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Pick any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}
such that
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)=\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega^{\prime}}-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\leq 1
. We need to show

(3.2)

\left|\int\varphi(\mu-\nu)\right|\leq e^{O(\delta)}(1+T_{\omega})^{1/2}\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu).

By translation invariance of
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, we may assume wlog
\sup\varphi=0
. Then

-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\leq 1+T_{\omega^{\prime}}\leq 1+e^{O(\delta)}T_{\omega},

by (
1.23
). On the other hand,

0\geq e^{(n+1)\delta}(\omega,e^{-\delta}\varphi)^{n+1}=(e^{\delta}\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}\geq e^{O(\delta)}(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n+1},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
1.20
, since
\omega^{\prime}\leq e^{\delta}\omega\leq e^{2\delta}\omega^{\prime}
. Dividing by
(n+1)V_{\omega}=e^{O(\delta)}(n+1)V_{\omega^{\prime}}
, we get

0\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(e^{-\delta}\varphi)\leq-\en_{\omega}(e^{-\delta}\varphi)\leq-e^{O(\delta)}\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\leq e^{O(\delta)}(1+T_{\omega}).

By (
2.31
) this implies

\left|\int e^{-\delta}\varphi(\mu-\nu)\right|\leq e^{O(\delta)}(1+T_{\omega})^{1/2}\dd_{\omega}(\mu,\nu),

and hence (
3.2
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We argue as in the proof of Proposition
1.23
. On the one hand, the Lipschitz equivalence class only depends on the positive class
[\omega]
, by Lemma
2.30
. On the other hand, it only depends on the commensurability class of

, and we conclude since any two positive classes admit commensurable representatives, by Proposition

.

3.2.
Mixed Monge–Ampère measures

It will be convenient to introduce, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
, the quantities

(3.3)

\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi):=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)+T_{\omega},\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu):=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+T_{\omega}.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
we have

(3.4)

0\leq\sup\varphi-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi);

(3.5)

\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi)\approx\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)).

Proof.

By (
1.32
), we have

\sup\varphi-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)+(\sup\varphi-\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega})\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)+T_{\omega}.

This yields (
3.4
), while (
3.5
) is a direct consequence of (
2.6
).
∎

We next establish a key energy estimate for mixed Monge–Ampère measures.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
, pick
\omega_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega_{i}]\in\Pos(X)
and
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{i}}
, and set

(3.6)

\mu:=([\omega_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\omega_{n}])^{-1}(\omega_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\omega_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n})\in\mathcal{M}.

Then
\mu
lies in
\mathcal{M}^{1}
, and satisfies:

(i)

if each
\omega_{i}
is commensurable to
\omega
, then

\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\lesssim e^{O(\delta)}\max_{i}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega_{i}}(\varphi_{i})

with
\delta:=\max_{i}\dT(\omega_{i},\omega)
;

(ii)

in the general case,

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\lesssim C(\max_{i}\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{i}}(\varphi_{i})+1),

where
C>0
only depends on
\omega
and the
\omega_{i}
.

Proof.

Assume first that each
\omega_{i}
is commensurable to
\omega
. Set

J:=\max_{i}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega_{i}}(\varphi_{i}),\quad V:=V_{\omega},\quad V_{i}:=V_{\omega_{i}},

and observe that

(3.7)

e^{-n\delta}V\leq V_{i}\leq e^{n\delta}V,\quad e^{-n\delta}V\leq[\omega_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\omega_{n}]\leq e^{n\delta}V.

Since
\mu
is unchanged when the
\varphi_{i}
’s are translated by constants, we may assume without loss that
\sup\varphi_{i}=0
. Then

0\geq(\omega_{i},\varphi_{i})^{n+1}=(n+1)V_{i}\en_{\omega_{i}}(\varphi_{i})\geq-(n+1)V_{i}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega_{i}}(\varphi_{i}),

by (
3.4
), and hence

(3.8)

0\geq(\omega_{i},\varphi_{i})^{n+1}\gtrsim-e^{O(\delta)}VJ,

using (
3.7
). Now pick
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
such that
\sup\psi=0
, and set
R:=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi)
. On the one hand,

(3.9)

0\geq(\omega,\psi)^{n+1}=(n+1)V\en_{\omega}(\psi)=-(n+1)VR.

On the other hand, using (
3.7
) again, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq e^{-n\delta}V\int\psi\,\mu\geq\int\psi\,(\omega_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\omega_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n})

\displaystyle=(\omega,\psi)\cdot(\omega_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\omega_{n},\varphi_{n})-(\omega,0)\cdot(\omega_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\omega_{n},\varphi_{n})

\displaystyle\geq(\omega,\psi)\cdot(\omega_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\omega_{n},\varphi_{n})\gtrsim e^{O(\delta)}\min\{(\omega,\psi)^{n+1},\min_{i}(\omega_{i},\varphi_{i})^{n+1}\},

by (
1.15
) and Theorem
1.18
. Combined with (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), this yields

\int\psi(\mu_{\omega}-\mu)\leq-\int\psi\,\mu\lesssim e^{O(\delta)}(R+J).

By Lemma
2.8
, we infer,

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\lesssim e^{O(\delta)}\inf_{R>0}(R+J)\left(1+R^{-1}(R+J)\right)\leq e^{O(\delta)}J,

which concludes the proof of (i) (using (
1.23
)).

We now consider the general case. By Proposition
1.15
, we can choose
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{i}}
such that
\omega^{\prime}:=\omega_{\tau}
and
\omega^{\prime}_{i}:=\omega_{i,\tau_{i}}
are commensurable for all
i
. Then

\mu=([\omega^{\prime}_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\omega^{\prime}_{n}])^{-1}(\omega^{\prime}_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi^{\prime}_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\omega^{\prime}_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n})

with
\varphi^{\prime}_{i}:=\varphi_{i}-\tau_{i}
, and hence

\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)\lesssim\max_{i}(\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime}_{i})+T_{\omega^{\prime}_{i}}),

by Theorem
3.4
. By (
2.9
), we have
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)+C
and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime}_{i})\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i})+C
with
C>0
independent of
\mu
, and (ii) follows.
∎

As a consequence, we get the following Lipschitz estimate for the energy:

Pick
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}

[\omega^{\prime}]\in\Pos(X)
, and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
.

(i)

If

\omega
, then

\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)\approx e^{O(\delta)}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)

with

.

(ii)

In the general case, there exists
C>0
only depending on
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
such that

\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)\leq C(\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+1).

We refer to (
4.21
) below for a more precise estimate when
\delta
is small.

Proof.

Assume first
\omega^{\prime}
commensurable. Pick a maximizing sequence
(\varphi_{j})
for
\mu
in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, and set
\mu_{j}:=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi_{j})
. Then
\mu_{j}\to\mu
strongly in
\mathcal{M}^{1}
(see Theorem
2.23
), and hence
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{j})\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)
and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu_{j})\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)
. For each

we have
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{j})\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu_{j})
(see (

)). Theorem
3.4
thus yields

\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu_{j})\lesssim e^{O(\delta)}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu_{j})
, and (i) follows.

In the general case, we can choose
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
and
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}
such that
\omega_{\tau}
and
\omega_{\tau^{\prime}}
are commensurable (see Proposition
1.15
). By (

), we then have

and

3.3.
Hölder continuity of the energy pairing

Recall from §
1.3
that

\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}=\left\{\theta\in\mathcal{Z}\mid\|\theta\|_{\omega}<\infty\right\}.

Using the above results, we establish a general Hölder continuity property for the energy pairing.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
i=0,\dots,n
, pick
\theta_{i},\theta^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and
\varphi_{i},\varphi^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, normalized by
\int\varphi_{i}\,\mu_{\omega}=\int\varphi^{\prime}_{i}\,\mu_{\omega}=0
. Then

\left|(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})-(\theta^{\prime}_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta^{\prime}_{n},\varphi^{\prime}_{n})\right|\lesssim A\left(\max_{i}\|\theta_{i}-\theta^{\prime}_{i}\|_{\omega}J+\max_{i}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i},\varphi^{\prime}_{i})^{\alpha}J^{1-\alpha}\right).

with
\alpha:=2^{-n}
and

A:=V_{\omega}\prod_{i}\left(1+\|\theta_{i}\|_{\omega}+\|\theta^{\prime}_{i}\|_{\omega}\right),\quad J:=\max_{i}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i}).

In particular,

(3.10)

\left|(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})\right|\lesssim V_{\omega}\prod_{i}\left(1+\|\theta_{i}\|_{\omega}\right)\max_{i}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i}).

Proof.

Assume first
\theta_{i}=\theta^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i
. By symmetry of the energy pairing, we may assume
\varphi_{i}=\varphi^{\prime}_{i}
for
i\geq 1
. For
i=1,\dots,n
set
t_{i}:=1+\|\theta_{i}\|_{\omega}
. Then
\|t_{i}^{-1}\theta_{i}\|_{\omega}\leq 1
, and

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(t_{i}^{-1}\varphi_{i})\leq t_{i}^{-1}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i})\leq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{i}),

by convexity of
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. By homogeneity, we may thus assume
\|\theta_{i}\|_{\omega}\leq 1
for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Thus
-\omega\leq\theta_{i}\leq\omega
, and hence
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}^{+}-\theta_{i}^{-}
where
\theta_{i}^{+}:=\theta_{i}+2\omega
and
\theta_{i}^{-}:=2\omega
both satisfy
\omega\leq\theta_{i}^{\pm}\leq 3\omega
. By multilinearity, we may finally assume
\omega\leq\theta_{i}\leq 3\omega
. By Proposition
1.17
(i), it then suffices to show

(3.11)

\left|\int(\varphi_{0}-\varphi^{\prime}_{0})(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n})\right|\leq V_{\omega}\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})^{\alpha}J^{1-\alpha}.

Since
\omega\leq\theta_{i}\leq 3\omega
, Theorem
3.4
shows that

\mu:=([\theta_{0}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}])^{-1}(\theta_{1}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(\theta_{n}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{n})\in\mathcal{M}

satisfies
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\lesssim J
. By (
2.25
), we infer

\left|\int(\varphi_{0}-\psi_{0})(\mu-\mu_{\omega})\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})^{\alpha}J^{1-\alpha},

which yields (
3.11
) since
\int(\varphi_{0}-\psi_{0})\mu_{\omega}=0
and
[\theta_{0}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}]\lesssim V_{\omega}
.

In the general case, we may again assume
(\theta_{i},\varphi_{i})=(\theta^{\prime}_{i},\varphi^{\prime}_{i})
for
i\geq 1
, by symmetry and multilinearity of the energy pairing. Then

(\theta_{0},\varphi_{0})\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})-(\theta^{\prime}_{0},\varphi^{\prime}_{0})\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})

=(\theta_{0}-\theta^{\prime}_{0},0)\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})+(0,\varphi_{0}-\varphi^{\prime}_{0})\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n}),

where the last term has already been estimated by (
3.11
). We are thus reduced to showing

\left|(\theta_{0},0)\cdot(\theta_{1},\varphi_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},\varphi_{n})\right|\lesssim AJ\|\theta_{0}\|_{\omega}.

By homogeneity we may further assume
\|\theta_{0}\|_{\omega}=1
, and the desired estimate now follows from the first step of the proof applied to
\varphi^{\prime}_{i}=0
, using
(\theta_{0},0)\cdot\ldots\cdot(\theta_{n},0)=0
.
∎

4.
Twisted energy and differentiability

As in §
3
, we assume that the orthogonality and submean properties hold, and recall that this is satisfied when
X
is a compact connected Kähler manifold or any irreducible projective Berkovich space. We fix
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
. In this section we introduce and study the twisted energy of a measure, and show that it computes certain directional derivatives of the energy.

4.1.
The twisted energy of a measure

In view of (
1.24
), the directional derivative

(4.1)

\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi):=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\en_{\omega+t\theta}(\varphi)

is well-defined for any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
, and given by

(4.2)

\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)=\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-V_{\omega}^{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi),

where

(4.3)

\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi):=V_{\omega}^{-1}(\theta,0)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}

and

V_{\omega}^{\theta}:=nV_{\omega}^{-1}[\theta]\cdot[\omega]^{n-1}.

Note that
\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)
is a linear function of
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
, and

(4.4)

\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi+c)=\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)+cV_{\omega}^{\theta}\text{ for }c\in\mathbb{R},

while
\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}
is translation invariant. For all
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}
, we further have

(4.5)

\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\psi)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}V_{\omega}^{-1}\int(\varphi-\psi)\theta\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\psi}^{n-1-j}.

Example 4.1
.

By (
1.24
), we have
\en_{(1+t)\omega}((1+t)\varphi)=(1+t)\en_{\omega}(\varphi)
. By (
1.27
), this implies

\nabla_{\omega}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\int\varphi\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\en_{\omega}(\varphi)
, and hence

(4.6)

\nabla_{\omega}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)=\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,0)\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(0,\varphi)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi).

Example 4.2
.

For each
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, (
1.29
) yields
\en_{\omega+t\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi}(\varphi)=\en_{\omega}(\varphi+t\psi)-\en_{\omega}(t\psi)
, and using again (
1.27
) we get

(4.7)

\nabla_{\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)=\int\psi(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)-\mu_{\omega}).

Example 4.3
.

For any
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, we similarly have
\en_{\omega_{\tau}+t\theta}(\varphi)=\en_{\omega+t\theta}(\varphi+\tau)-\en_{\omega+t\theta}(\tau)
and

(4.8)

\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi)=\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi+\tau)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\tau).

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, the following holds:

(i)

if
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, then

(4.9)

|\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\psi)|\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)^{\alpha}\max\{\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi),\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\psi)\}^{1-\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega}

with
\alpha:=2^{-n}
;

(ii)

in the general case, there exist
C>0
only depending on
\omega
and
\theta
such that

(4.10)

|\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\psi)|\leq C\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)^{\alpha}(\max\{\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi),\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi)\}+1)^{1-\alpha}.

Proof.

By translation invariance of
\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}
, we may assume
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}=\int\psi\,\mu_{\omega}=0
.
When
\theta
lies in
\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, Theorem
3.6
applied to (
1.24
) and (
4.3
) yields

\left|\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega}(\psi)\right|\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\psi)\right|\leq C(1+\|\theta\|_{\omega})

with
C:=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)^{\alpha}\max\{\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi),\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\psi)\}^{1-\alpha}
. By homogeneity of
\en^{\theta}_{\omega}
with respect to
\theta
, we may replace
1+\|\theta\|_{\omega}
with
\|\theta\|_{\omega}
in the last estimate, and (i) now follows from (
4.2
) together with
|V_{\omega}^{\theta}|\leq n\|\theta\|_{\omega}
.

In the general case, pick
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
such that
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{\tau}}
(see Proposition
1.12
). Given
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, we then have
\varphi-\tau,\psi-\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega_{\tau}}
, and (
4.8
), (
1.39
) yield

\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi-\tau)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega_{\tau}}(\psi-\tau)=\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\psi),\quad\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi-\tau,\psi-\tau)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi),

\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\varphi-\tau)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(0,\varphi-\tau)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau,\varphi)\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau),\quad\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}(\psi-\tau)\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\psi)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\tau).

By (i) we thus get

|\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\psi)|\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)^{\alpha}\left(\max\{\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi),\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\psi)\}+\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\tau)\right)^{1-\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega_{\tau}},

which proves (ii).
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
, there exists a unique strongly continuous functional

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, the
\theta
-twisted energy
, such that

(4.11)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi))=\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. For all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
, we also have:

(i)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)
is a linear function of
\theta
;

(ii)

if
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, then

(4.12)

|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\nu)|\lesssim\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)^{\alpha}\max\{\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu),\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\nu)\}^{1-\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega};

with
\alpha:=2^{-n}
, and hence

(4.13)

|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)|\lesssim\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\|\theta\|_{\omega};

(iii)

in the general case, there exist
C>0
only depending on
\omega
and
\theta
such that

(4.14)

|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\nu)|\leq C\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)^{\alpha}(\max\{\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu),\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\nu)\}+1)^{1-\alpha},

and hence

(4.15)

|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)|\leq C(\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+1).

Proof.

Assume
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
satisfy
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\MA_{\omega}(\psi)
. Then
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\varphi,\psi)=0
, and hence
\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)=\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\psi)
, by (
4.10
). As a result, there exists a unique function
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}
on the image of
\MA_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}
such that (
4.11
) is satisfied. For all
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
in the image of
\MA_{\omega}
, it further follows from (
4.10
) that (
4.14
) holds. This shows that
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}
is uniformly continuous on a dense subspace of the quasi-metric space
(\mathcal{M}^{1},\delta_{\omega})
, and hence admits a unique continuous extension
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
. Finally, (i) holds by linearity of
\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}
with respect to
\theta
,(ii) and (iii) follow, by continuity, from Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Using (
4.11
) (with
\varphi=0
), (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we further get, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
,
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
and
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
,

(4.16)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu_{\omega})=0;

(4.17)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\omega}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu);

(4.18)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi}(\mu)=\int\psi(\mu-\mu_{\omega});

(4.19)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}^{\theta}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)+c

with
c\in\mathbb{R}
uniquely determined by (
4.16
), i.e.
c=\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}^{\theta}(\mu_{\omega})=-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu_{\omega_{\tau}})
.

4.2.
Hölder continuity of the twisted energy

The following estimates will be the key ingredients for the continuity of coercivity thresholds (see Theorem
5.5
below).

Theorem 4.6
.

Pick
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
\delta:=\dT(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\leq 1
and
[\omega^{\prime}]\in\Pos(X)
. For all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
, we then have

(4.20)

\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(\mu)\right|\lesssim\left(\delta^{a}\|\theta\|_{\omega}+\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega}\right)\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)

and

(4.21)

\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)=(1+O(\delta^{\alpha}))\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu).

Lemma 4.7
.

Assume
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq e^{\delta}\omega
with
\delta\in[0,2]
. Pick
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}
, and set
\mu:=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
,
\mu^{\prime}:=\MA_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)
. Then:

(4.22)

|\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)|\lesssim\delta\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\|\theta\|_{\omega};

(4.23)

\max\{\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu),\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu^{\prime})\}\lesssim\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu);

(4.24)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\lesssim\delta\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu).

Proof.

All three estimates are invariant under translation of
\varphi
by a constant, and we shall rely on a different normalization for each of them. We first normalize
\varphi
by
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}=0
. By homogeneity, we may further assume
\|\theta\|_{\omega}=1
. Since

\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\varphi)=V_{\omega}^{-1}(\theta,0)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}-V_{\omega^{\prime}}^{-1}(\theta,0)\cdot(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n}

with
V_{\omega^{\prime}}/V_{\omega}=1+O(\delta)
, Theorem
3.6
yields

\left|\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim\|\omega^{\prime}-\omega\|_{\omega}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\lesssim\delta\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu).

We similarly get
\left|\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim\delta\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)
, and (
4.22
) follows, using the trivial estimate

V_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}=V_{\omega}^{\theta}+O(\delta).

Next, we normalize
\varphi
by
\sup\varphi=0
. By Lemma
1.20
, we then have

\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu^{\prime})\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)=\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega^{\prime}}-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\leq-\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\lesssim-\en_{\omega}(\varphi)\leq\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu).

On the other hand, Corollary
3.5
yields
\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)\lesssim\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)
, and (
4.23
) follows.

Finally, we normalize
\varphi
by
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega^{\prime}}=0
. Pick a maximizing sequence
(\psi_{i})
in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}
for
\mu
, also normalized by
\int\psi_{i}\,\mu_{\omega^{\prime}}=0
, and set
\mu_{i}:=\MA_{\omega^{\prime}}(\psi_{i})
. By Theorem
2.23
,
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
is the limit of

\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu_{i},\mu^{\prime})=\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\psi_{i},\varphi)\approx\int(\psi_{i}-\varphi)(\mu^{\prime}-\mu_{i})=\int(\psi_{i}-\varphi)(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)+o(1),

where the last two points hold by (
1.30
) and (
2.25
), respectively.
Now

\displaystyle\int(\psi_{i}-\varphi)(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)

\displaystyle=\left[V_{\omega^{\prime}}^{-1}(0,\psi_{i})\cdot(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n}-V_{\omega}^{-1}(0,\psi_{i})\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[V_{\omega^{\prime}}^{-1}(0,\varphi)\cdot(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n}-V_{\omega}^{-1}(0,\varphi)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}\right],

where

\left|V_{\omega^{\prime}}^{-1}(0,\psi_{i})\cdot(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n}-V_{\omega}^{-1}(0,\psi_{i})\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}\right|\lesssim\|\omega^{\prime}-\omega\|_{\omega^{\prime}}\max\{\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi),\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\psi_{i})\}

and

\left|V_{\omega^{\prime}}^{-1}(0,\varphi)\cdot(\omega^{\prime},\varphi)^{n}-V_{\omega}^{-1}(0,\varphi)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}\right|\lesssim\|\omega^{\prime}-\omega\|_{\omega^{\prime}}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi),

by Theorem
3.6
. Since
\|\omega^{\prime}-\omega\|_{\omega^{\prime}}\lesssim\delta
,
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu^{\prime})
and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\psi_{i})\approx\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu_{i})\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)
, this proves (
4.24
), thanks to (
4.23
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

Note first that
\omega^{\prime\prime}:=e^{-\delta}\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq e^{\delta}\omega
, and hence

\omega^{\prime\prime}\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq e^{2\delta}\omega^{\prime\prime},\quad\omega^{\prime\prime}\leq\omega\leq e^{\delta}\omega^{\prime\prime}.

Arguing successively with
\omega^{\prime\prime},\omega^{\prime}
, and with
\omega^{\prime\prime},\omega
, and relying on Corollary
3.5
, it is thus enough to prove the result when
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}\leq e^{\delta}\omega
, which we henceforth assume. By density of the image of
\MA_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}
and strong continuity of
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}
and
\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}
(recall that the strong topology of
\mathcal{M}^{1}
is independent of
\omega
), we may assume
\mu=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
with
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\omega^{\prime}}
. As in Lemma
4.7
, set

\mu^{\prime}:=\MA_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi)
. By (
4.11
), we have

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)=\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi),\quad\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\mu^{\prime})=\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega^{\prime}}(\varphi),

and (
4.22
) thus yields

(4.25)

\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\mu^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\delta\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\|\theta\|_{\omega}.

On the other hand, (
4.12
), implies

(4.26)

\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\mu^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\delta^{\alpha}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\|\theta\|_{\omega},

thanks to (
4.23
) and (
4.24
). Finally, (
4.13
) yields

(4.27)

\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(\mu)\right|=\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta-\theta^{\prime}}(\mu)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega^{\prime}},

and summing up (
4.25
), (
4.26
) and (
4.27
) yields (
4.20
). Applying the latter estimate with
\theta:=\omega
,
\theta^{\prime}:=\omega^{\prime}
, we get

(4.28)

\left|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}(\mu)\right|\lesssim\delta^{\alpha}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu),

in view of (
4.17
). Since we also have
T_{\omega^{\prime}}=(1+O(\delta))T_{\omega}
(see (
1.23
)), (
4.21
) follows.
∎

4.3.
Differentiability of the energy

Theorem 4.8
.

For any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)

If we do not assume
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, the condition
\omega+t\theta\geq 0
will fail in general, but one can still make sense of
\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)
, see Remark
2.4
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Pick
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
normalized by
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}=0
.Then

\left|\en_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi),\quad\left|\int\varphi\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi),\quad\left|\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim\|\theta\|_{\omega}\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\varphi).

Proof.

We have

V_{\omega}(n+1)\en_{\omega}(\varphi)=(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1},\quad V_{\omega}\int\varphi\,\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=(0,\varphi)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n},\quad V_{\omega}\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)=(\theta,0)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n},

and the estimates thus follow from (
3.10
) (and homogeneity in
\theta
).
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Pick
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
such that
\|\theta\|_{\omega}<1
and
[\omega+\theta]\in\Pos(X)
. For all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
we then have

\mathrm{J}_{\omega+\theta}(\mu)\geq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-O(\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)\|\theta\|_{\omega}^{2}).

Recall that, in this paper, the implicit constant in
O
only depends on
n
.

Proof.

Set
J:=\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)
and
\varepsilon:=\|\theta\|_{\omega}
. If
\varepsilon=0
, then
\theta=0
and the result is clear. We may thus assume
\varepsilon>0
and write
\theta=\varepsilon\tilde{\theta}
with
\|\tilde{\theta}\|_{\omega}=1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

By density of the image of
\MA_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{M}^{1}
, it is enough to prove the result when
\mu=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
with
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, which we normalize by
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\omega}=0
. By (
2.5
), (
4.11
) and (
4.2
), we then have

(4.29)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)=\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,\mu,\quad\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)=\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-V_{\omega}^{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi).

Note that

(4.30)

(1-\varepsilon)\omega\leq\omega+\theta\leq(1+\varepsilon)\omega\leq(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\omega,

and hence

(1-\varepsilon)\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{(1-\varepsilon)\omega}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\omega+\theta}.

By (
2.2
), this yields

(4.31)

\mathrm{J}_{\omega+\theta}(\mu)\geq\en_{\omega+\theta}\left((1-\varepsilon)\varphi\right)-(1-\varepsilon)\int\varphi\,\mu.

\displaystyle(n+1)V_{\omega+\theta}\en_{\omega+\theta}\left((1-\varepsilon)\varphi)\right)

\displaystyle=(\omega+\theta,(1-\varepsilon)\varphi)^{n+1}=\left((\omega,\varphi)+(\theta,-\varepsilon\varphi)\right)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1}+(n+1)\left[(\theta,0)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}-\varepsilon(0,\varphi)\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n}\right]+\varepsilon^{2}a(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=(n+1)V_{\omega}\left[\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-\varepsilon\int\varphi\,\mu\right]+\varepsilon^{2}a(\varepsilon)

with

a(\varepsilon):=\sum_{j=2}^{n+1}{n+1\choose j}\varepsilon^{j-2}(\tilde{\theta},-\varphi)^{j}\cdot(\omega,\varphi)^{n+1-j}.

Since
\|\tilde{\theta}\|_{\omega}=1
, (
3.10
) yields
|a(\varepsilon)|\lesssim V_{\omega}J
. Combining this with

V_{\omega+\theta}=[\omega+\theta]^{n}=[\omega]^{n}+n[\theta]\cdot[\omega]^{n-1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}[\omega]^{n})=V_{\omega}\left(1+V_{\omega}^{\theta}+O(\varepsilon^{2})\right),

and

\left|V_{\omega}^{\theta}\right|\lesssim\varepsilon,\quad\left|\en_{\omega}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim J,\quad\left|\int\varphi\,\mu\right|\leq J,\quad\left|\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)\right|\lesssim\varepsilon J

(see Lemma
4.9
), we infer

\displaystyle\en_{\omega+\theta}((1-\varepsilon)\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-V_{\omega}^{\theta}+O(\varepsilon^{2})\right)\left(\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\en^{\theta}(\varphi)-\varepsilon\int\varphi\,\mu-O(\varepsilon^{2}J)\right)

\displaystyle=\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+\en_{\omega}^{\theta}(\varphi)-V_{\omega}^{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)-\varepsilon\int\varphi\,\mu-O(\varepsilon^{2}J).

Injecting this in (
4.31
) and using (
4.29
), we get, as desired,

\mathrm{J}_{\omega+\theta}(\mu)\geq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-O(\varepsilon^{2}J).

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.8
.

Assume first
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
. Set
J:=\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}(\mu)
. By Lemma
4.10
we then have

\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)\geq\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+t\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)-O(t^{2}\|\theta\|_{\omega}J).

For
|t|\ll 1
, we also have
\delta(\omega+t\theta,\omega)\leq 1
, and hence
\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)\lesssim J
, by Corollary
3.5
. Reversing the roles of
\omega
and
\omega+t\theta
, Lemma
4.10
thus yields

\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\geq\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)-t\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}^{\theta}(\mu)-O(t^{2}\|\theta\|_{\omega}J).

By Theorem
4.6
,
\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}^{\theta}(\mu)\to\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)
as
t\to 0
, and we conclude, as desired,

\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu+t\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu)+o(t).

as
t\to 0
.

In the general case, pick
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
such that
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{\tau}}
(see Proposition
1.12
). Then (
2.9
) yields

\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}+t\theta}(\mu)-\int\tau\,\mu+\en_{\omega+t\theta}(\tau),

see Remark
2.4
. We infer

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathrm{J}_{\omega+t\theta}(\mu)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}^{\theta}(\mu)+\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}(\tau)=\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}^{\theta}(\mu)+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu_{\omega_{\tau}})=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}(\mu),

where the first equality follows from the first step of the proof and (
4.2
), the second one from (
4.11
), and the third from (
4.19
).
∎

5.
Coercivity thresholds and free energy

As in §
4
, we assume that the orthogonality and submean value properties hold. We then establish a general continuity result for coercivity thresholds, and apply this to the free energy, which induces the Mabuchi K-energy on potentials.

5.1.
Continuity of coercivity thresholds

Fix for the moment
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
, and consider an arbitrary functional
F\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the
coercivity threshold
of
F
as

(5.1)

\sigma_{\omega}(F):=\sup\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid F\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+A\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\in[-\infty,+\infty].

We say that
F
is
coercive
if
\sigma_{\omega}(F)>0
,
i.e.
F\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+A
for some
\sigma>0
and
A\in\mathbb{R}
.

By Corollary
3.5
(ii), the condition that
F
is coercive (resp.
\sigma_{\omega}(F)=\pm\infty
) is independent of
\omega
.

Recall from (
2.22
) that the energy of any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
coincides with the quasi-distance to the base point
\mu_{\omega}
, i.e.
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)=\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\mu_{\omega})
. As a result, the coercivity threshold measures the linear growth of
F
with respect to quasi-metric
\delta_{\omega}
. As we next show, any other base point can be used in place of
\mu_{\omega}
(something that could fail for a general quasi-metric).

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
F\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
we have

(5.2)

\sigma_{\omega}(F)=\sup\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid F\geq\sigma\delta_{\omega}(\cdot,\nu)+A\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

In particular,
\sigma_{\omega}(F)
only depends on
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
.

Proof.

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
, (
2.24
) yields

\left|\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)-\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)\right|\lesssim\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\nu)^{\alpha}\max\{\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu),\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\nu)\}^{1-\alpha}.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we can thus find
C_{\varepsilon}>0
(depending on
\nu
) such that

(1-\varepsilon)\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)-C_{\varepsilon}\leq\delta_{\omega}(\mu,\nu)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\mathrm{J}_{\omega}(\mu)+C_{\varepsilon}

for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
. This implies (
5.2
), and the last point follows, since the Dirichlet quasi-metric
\delta_{\omega}
only depends on
[\omega]
(see Theorem
2.26
).
∎

Definition 5.3
.

For each
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
, we introduce the
twisted coercivity threshold

(5.3)

\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F):=\sigma_{\omega}(F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}).

We refer to §
5.2
below for a discussion of the concrete case we have in mind.

Lemma 5.4
.

The following holds:

(i)

\sigma_{\omega}^{0}(F)=\sigma_{\omega}(F)
, and
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta+t\omega}(F)=\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)+t
for all
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(ii)

we have
\sigma_{\omega}(F)\in\mathbb{R}
(resp.
\sigma_{\omega}(F)=\pm\infty
) iff
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)\in\mathbb{R}
(resp.
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)=\pm\infty
) for all
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

The first point is a direct consequence of (
4.17
). For any
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
, (
4.15
) yields a constant
C>0
such that
|\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}|\leq C(\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+1)
. This implies

\sigma_{\omega}(F)-C\leq\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)\leq\sigma_{\omega}(F)+C,

and (iii) follows.
∎

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.5
.

For any functional
F\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, the twisted coercivity threshold
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)
is a continuous function of
([\omega],[\theta])\in\Pos(X)\times\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
.

Continuity is understood with respect to the finest vector space topology of
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
, i.e. for
[\omega]
and
[\theta]
constrained in any given finite dimensional subspace.

Lemma 5.6
.

There exists
\delta_{n}>0
only depending on
n
such that, for all
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
\delta:=\dT(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\leq\delta_{n}
and all
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\omega}
, we have

(5.4)

\sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(F)\geq(1+O(\delta^{\alpha}))\left[\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)+O(\delta^{\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega}+\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega})\right].

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}=\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+T_{\omega}
, we can replace
\mathrm{J}_{\omega}
with
\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}
in (
5.1
), and hence

(5.5)

\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)=\sup\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}+A\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Pick
\sigma,A\in\mathbb{R}
such that
F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}+A
on
\mathcal{M}^{1}
. By (
4.20
) we get

\displaystyle F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}+O(\delta^{\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega}+\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega})\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}

\displaystyle\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}+A+O(\delta^{\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega}+\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega})\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega}

\displaystyle\geq(1+O(\delta^{\alpha}))\left[\sigma+O(\delta^{\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega}+\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega}\right]\mathrm{J}^{+}_{\omega^{\prime}}+A,

using (
4.21
), and hence
\sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}^{\theta^{\prime}}(F)\geq(1+O(\delta^{\alpha}))\left[\sigma+O(\delta^{\alpha}\|\theta\|_{\omega}+\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\|_{\omega}\right]
. Choosing
\delta_{n}>0
such that
1+O(\delta^{\alpha})\geq 0
for
\delta\leq\delta_{n}
and taking the supremum over
\sigma
yields (
5.4
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

We first show that
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)
only depends on the classes
[\omega]\in\Pos(X)
and
[\theta]\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
. For each
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, we have

\sigma_{\omega_{\tau}}^{\theta}(F)=\sigma_{\omega_{\tau}}(F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega_{\tau}}^{\theta})=\sigma_{\omega_{\tau}}(F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta})=\sigma_{\omega}(F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta})=\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F),

where the second equality follows from (
4.19
), and the third from Lemma
5.2
. This proves that
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)
only depends on
[\omega]
. On the other hand, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
, (
4.18
) yields

F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}-C\leq F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi}\leq F+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}+C

with
C:=2\sup|\psi|
. This implies
\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)=\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi}(F)
, which thus only depends on
[\theta]
.

As noted above, Corollary
3.5
(ii) shows that the condition
\sigma_{\omega}(F)=\pm\infty
is independent of
\omega
, and in that case the result trivially holds, since all twisted thresholds are then equal to
\pm\infty
, by Lemma
5.4
(ii).

Assume now that the twisted thresholds are finite valued. Pick
\omega_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
with
[\omega_{0}]\in\Pos(X)
,
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}
. Choose a finite dimensional subspace
W\subset\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
containing
[\omega_{0}]
,
[\theta_{0}]
, and a finite dimensional subspace
V\subset\mathcal{Z}
containing
\theta_{0}
, and whose image in
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{BC}}(X)
contains
W
. Since
\sigma_{\omega_{0}}^{\theta_{0}}(F)
only depends on
[\omega_{0}]
, we can assume wlog
V\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{0}}
(see Proposition
1.12
). After enlarging
V
, we may further assume that it contains
\omega_{0}
. For each
\omega,\theta\in V\subset\mathcal{Z}_{\omega_{0}}
with
\omega
close enough to
\omega_{0}
, (
5.5
) yields

|\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)-\sigma_{\omega_{0}}^{\theta_{0}}(F)|\lesssim\delta^{\alpha}\left[\sigma_{\omega_{0}}^{\theta_{0}}(F)+O(\delta^{\alpha}\|\theta_{0}\|_{\omega_{0}}+\|\theta-\theta_{0}\|_{\omega_{0}})\right]

with
\delta=\dT(\omega,\omega_{0})
. By Lemma
1.14
, this shows that the restriction of
(\omega,\theta)\mapsto\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(F)
to
V
is continuous at
(\omega_{0},\theta_{0})
, and the result follows, since the image of
V
contains
W
.
∎

5.2.
Free energy vs. Mabuchi K-energy

5.2.1.
The Kähler case

Consider first a compact connected Kähler manifold
X
. Any smooth metric
\rho
on
K_{X}
induces a volume form
\mu_{\rho}
, and hence a
(relative) entropy
functional

(5.6)

\Ent_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},

such that

\Ent_{\rho}(\mu):=\int\log\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu_{\rho}}\right)\mu

if
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{\rho}
, and
\Ent_{\rho}(\mu)=+\infty
otherwise. Note that

(5.7)

\Ent_{\rho^{\prime}}(\mu)=\Ent_{\rho}(\mu)+\int(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\,\mu

for any other metric
\rho^{\prime}
on
K_{X}
. As is well-known, the relative entropy can also be written as the Legendre transform

\Ent_{\rho}(\mu):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{D}}\left(\int f\,\mu-\log\int e^{f}\mu_{\rho}\right)

which shows that (
5.6
) is convex and lsc. In particular, the restriction
\Ent_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is lsc in the strong topology. While it is not continuous, we nevertheless have (see
[
BDL17
, Theorem 4.7]
):

Lemma 5.7
.

For each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{i})
in
\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
such that
\mu_{i}:=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi_{i})
satisfies
\mu_{i}\to\mu
strongly in
\mathcal{M}^{1}
and
\Ent_{\rho}(\mu_{i})\to\Ent_{\rho}(\mu)
.

Extending the terminology of
[
Berm13
]
, we introduce:

Definition 5.8
.

We define the
free energy

\effe_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by setting

(5.8)

\effe_{\omega}(\mu):=\Ent_{\rho}(\mu)-\Ent_{\rho}(\mu_{\omega})+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta_{\rho}}(\mu),

where
\theta_{\rho}\in\mathcal{Z}
denotes the curvature of
\rho
.

As the notation suggests,
\effe_{\omega}
is independent of the choice of
\rho
; this follows indeed from (
5.7
) combined with (
4.18
). Furthermore, (
4.16
) and (
4.19
) show that
\effe_{\omega}
only depends on the Kähler class
[\omega]
, up to an additive constant uniquely determined by the normalization
\effe_{\omega}(\mu_{\omega})=0
.

The
raison d’être
of the free energy is that its composition with the Monge–Ampère operator coincides with the
Mabuchi K-energy

\mab_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e. we have

(5.9)

\effe_{\omega}(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi))=\mab_{\omega}(\varphi)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
. Indeed, in view of (
4.11
), (
5.9
) is equivalent to the well-known
Chen–Tian formula
for the K-energy, which can be written as

(5.10)

\mab_{\omega}(\varphi)=\Ent_{\rho}(\MA_{\omega}(\varphi))+\nabla_{\theta_{\rho}}\en_{\omega}(\varphi)+c

in the present formalism, for a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
determined by the normalization
\mab_{\omega}^{\theta}(0)=0
, i.e.
c=-\Ent_{\rho}(\mu_{\omega})
.

Definition 5.9
.

Define the
coercivity threshold
of
(X,\omega)
as
\sigma(X,\omega):=\sigma_{\omega}(\effe_{\omega})
.

Theorem 5.10
.

The coercivity threshold of
(X,\omega)
is a continuous function of the Kähler class
[\omega]
, and it satisfies

\sigma(X,\omega)=\sup\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mab_{\omega}\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+A\text{ on }\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

By
[
CC21
]
,
\sigma(X,\omega)>0
iff there exists a unique constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metric in
[\omega]
. In particular, we recover the fact, originally proved in
[
LS94
]
, that the set of Kähler classes of
X
that contain a unique cscK metric is open.

Proof.

Pick a smooth metric
\rho
on
K_{X}
. By (
5.8
), we have
\sigma(X,\omega)=\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta_{\rho}}(\Ent_{\rho})
, and the first point thus follows from Theorem
5.5
. As to the second point, it is a simple consequence of (
5.9
) and Lemma
5.7
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Given
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}
, one can more generally consider the
\theta
-twisted free energy

\effe_{\omega}^{\theta}:=\effe_{\omega}+\mathrm{J}_{\omega}^{\theta}
, whose composition with the Monge–Ampère operator coincides with the
\theta
-twisted K-energy
\mab_{\omega}^{\theta}=\mab_{\omega}+\nabla_{\theta}\en_{\omega}
(see
[
BDL17
,
CC21
]
). Again, Theorem
5.5
shows that the coercivity threshold

\sigma^{\theta}(X,\omega):=\sigma_{\omega}(\effe_{\omega}^{\theta})=\sigma_{\omega}^{\theta}(\Ent_{\rho})

is a continuous function of
[\omega]
and
[\theta]
, while Lemma
5.7
shows that

\sigma^{\theta}(X,\omega):=\sup\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mab_{\omega}^{\theta}\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+A\text{ on }\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

as considered for instance in
[
SD20
]
.

5.2.2.
The non-Archimedean case

Consider now a smooth, irreducible projective Berkovich space
X
over a non-Archimedean field
k
of characteristic
0
. Pick a PL metric
\rho
on
K_{X}
, and define the associated
non-Archimedean entropy

\Ent_{\rho}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by setting

\Ent_{\rho}(\mu):=\int(\mathrm{A}_{X}-\rho)\,\mu.

Here
\mathrm{A}_{X}
denotes the Temkin metric (see
[
Tem16
]
and also
[
Ste19
]
) on
K_{X}
, and
\mathrm{A}_{X}-\rho\colon X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is the corresponding lsc function, using additive notation for metrics (see
[
BoJ17
, §5.7]
). Again,
\Ent_{\rho}
is convex and lsc, and (
5.7
) holds for any other choice of PL metric
\rho^{\prime}
on
K_{X}
. See also
[
Ino22
]
for a related notion in the trivially valued case.

As above, one can then define the (non-Archimedean)
free energy

\effe_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{M}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
by (
5.8
), with
\theta_{\rho}\in\mathcal{Z}
the curvature of
\rho
. Its composition with the Monge–Ampère operator coincides with the (non-Archimedean)
Mabuchi K-energy

\mab_{\omega}\colon\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by (
5.10
).

However, the major difference in the non-Archimedean case is that the analogue of Lemma
5.7
is only a conjecture (compare
[
BoJ18a
, Conjecture 2.5]
and
[
C.Li22
, Conjecture 4.4]
). Explicitly:

Conjecture 5.12
.

For each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{1}
, there exists a sequence of
\omega
-psh PL functions
(\varphi_{i})
such that
\mu_{i}:=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi_{i})
satisfies
\mu_{i}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{M}^{1}
and
\Ent_{\rho}(\mu_{i})\to\Ent_{\rho}(\mu)
.

As a consequence, the coercivity threshold
\sigma(X,\omega):=\sigma_{\omega}(\effe_{\omega})
only satisfies

(5.11)

\sigma(X,\omega)\leq\sup\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}\mid\mab_{\omega}\geq\sigma\mathrm{J}_{\omega}+A\text{ on }\mathcal{D}_{\omega}\text{ for some }A\in\mathbb{R}\},

and equality holds if Conjecture
5.12
is valid.

These definitions are especially relevant when
k
is trivially valued and
\omega
lies in
\Amp(X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{+}
(see (
1.5
)). Indeed, the free energy
\effe_{\omega}(\mu)
then coincides with the invariant
\beta_{\omega}(\mu)
introduced and studied in
[
BoJ22b
]
; see also
[
DL23
,
Liu23
]
. By homogeneity with respect to the action of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\sigma(X,\omega)=\sigma_{\omega}(\effe_{\omega})
is further equal to the
divisorial stability threshold

\sigma_{\mathrm{div}}(X,\omega)
, which is positive iff
(X,\omega)
is
divisorially stable
.

On the other hand, the right-hand side of (
5.11
) coincides, by definition, with the
K-stability threshold

\sigma_{K}(X,\omega)
, which is positive iff
(X,\omega)
is
uniformly K-stable
in the sense of
[
Der16
,
BHJ17
]
). Thus divisorial stability implies uniform K-stability, and the converse holds if Conjecture
5.12
is satisfied.

Remark 5.13
.

Assume that
(X,L)
is a polarized smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
, and pick a Kähler form
\omega\in c_{1}(L)
. Using
[
SD18
]
and
[
C.Li22
]
, one can then show that the above thresholds satisfy

\sigma_{\mathrm{div}}(X,L)\leq\sigma(X,\omega)\leq\sigma_{K}(X,L).

Conjecture
5.12
would further yield
\sigma_{K}(X,L)=\sigma_{\mathrm{div}}(X,L)
, and hence conclude the proof of the ‘uniform version’ of the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture, as noted in
[
C.Li22
,
BoJ22b
]
.

Appendix A
Convexity estimates

We consider the following data:

•

a surjective map
\pi\colon V\to\Theta
of
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces, with fibers
V_{\theta}=\pi^{-1}(\theta)
;

•

a homogeneous polynomial
F\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
of degree
n+1
,
n\geq 1
, with associated symmetric multilinear map

V^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}\quad(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto x_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n},

i.e.
F(x)=x^{n+1}
;

•

a convex cone
P\subset V
such that

(A.1)

x^{2}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in V_{0}\text{ and }x_{i}\in P.

Example A.1
.

The main example that we have in mind is the negative of the energy pairing in §
1.4
, where
V=\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{D}
with its projection to
\Theta=\mathcal{Z}
, and
P=\{(\theta,\varphi)\in V\mid\theta_{\varphi}\geq 0\}
. Another example is given by the negative of intersection pairing on a flat projective scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Our goal is to use (
A.1
) and the resulting
Cauchy-Schwartz
inequality

(A.2)

(x\cdot y\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n})^{2}\leq(x^{2}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n})(y^{2}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n})

for all
x,y\in V_{0}
and
x_{i}\in P
to derive various inequalities and convexity statements.

For each
x\in V
we define the linear form
F^{\prime}(x)\in V^{\vee}
by

\langle F^{\prime}(x),y\rangle:=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}F(x+ty)=(n+1)x^{n}\cdot y,

and we set for all
x,y\in V

(A.3)

\delta(x,y):=F(x)-F(y)-\langle F^{\prime}(y),x-y\rangle.

A simple computation yields

(A.4)

\delta(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(j+1)(x-y)^{2}\cdot y^{j}\cdot x^{n-1-j}.

In what follows, we fix
\theta\in\Theta
and set
P_{\theta}:=V_{\theta}\cap P
.

Lemma A.2
.

We have
\delta(x,y)\geq 0
for
x,y\in P_{\theta}
. Moreover,
F
is convex on
P_{\theta}
, and for every
y\in P_{\theta}
we have that
x\mapsto\delta(x,y)
is convex.

Proof.

By (
A.1
) and (
A.4
), we have
\delta(x,y)\geq 0
for
x,y\in P_{\theta}
, and this implies that
F
is convex on
P_{\theta}
; it then follows from (
A.3
) that
x\mapsto\delta(x,y)
is convex.
∎

Theorem A.3
.

For all
x,y,z\in P_{\theta}
and
t\in[0,1]
, the following holds:

•

quasi-symmetry
:

\delta(x,y)\approx\delta(y,x);

•

quasi-triangle inequality
:

\delta(x,z)\lesssim\delta(x,y)+\delta(y,z);

•

quadratic estimate
:

\delta(x,(1-t)x+ty)\lesssim t^{2}\delta(x,y);

•

uniform convexity
:

[(1-t)F(x)+tF(y)]-F((1-t)x+ty)\gtrsim t(1-t)\delta(x,y).

For any base point
x_{*}\in P_{\theta}
and
x_{i},y_{i},z_{j}\in P_{\theta}
, the following
Hölder estimates
further hold:

(A.5)

|(x_{0}-y_{0})\cdot(x_{1}-y_{1})\cdot z_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{n}|\lesssim\delta(x_{0},y_{0})^{\alpha}\delta(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha}M^{1-2\alpha};

(A.6)

\left|\langle F^{\prime}(x_{0})-F^{\prime}(y_{0}),x_{1}-y_{1}\rangle\right|\lesssim\delta(x_{0},y_{0})^{1/2}\delta(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha}M^{1/2-\alpha};

(A.7)

\left|\delta(x_{0},x_{1})-\delta(y_{0},y_{1})\right|\lesssim\max\{\delta(x_{0},y_{0}),\delta(x_{1},y_{1})\}^{\alpha}M^{1-\alpha};

with
\alpha:=2^{-n}\in(0,1/2]
and
M=\max_{\xi}\delta(\xi,x_{*})
, where in each case
\xi
ranges over the elements of
P_{\theta}
appearing in the left-hand side of the inequality.

The strategy to get these types of Hölder estimates goes back to
[
BBGZ13
,
BBEGZ19
,
BoJ22a
]
, building on an original idea of
[
Blo03
]
. In the rest of this section we prove Theorem
A.3
, largely following
[
BoJ22a
, §3.3]
.

Given
x,y\in P_{\theta}
, we set

d(x,y):=\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}(x-y)^{2}\cdot y^{j}\cdot x^{n-1-j}.

Using (
A.4
) it is then clear that

(A.8)

\delta(x,y)\approx\delta(y,x)\approx d(x,y)\approx(x-y)^{2}\cdot(\tfrac{1}{2}(x+y))^{n-1}.

To prove the inequality
\delta(x,(1-t)x+ty)\lesssim t^{2}\delta(x,y)
it suffices to prove the corresponding inequality
d(x,(1-t)x+ty)\lesssim t^{2}d(x,y)
. But

d(x,(1-t)x+ty)=t^{2}\max_{0\leq j\leq n-1}(x-y)^{2}\cdot((1-t)x+ty)^{j}\cdot x^{n-1-j}\lesssim t^{2}d(x,y)

using multilinearity and the binomial theorem.

Next we prove what is essentially a special case of (
A.5
).

Lemma A.4
.

If
x,y,z\in P_{\theta}
, then

(x-y)^{2}\cdot z^{n-1}\lesssim d(x,y)^{2\alpha}\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\}^{1-2\alpha}.

Proof.

Set
w:=\frac{1}{2}(x+y)
,
A:=d(x,y)
,
B:=\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\}
, and, for
0\leq j\leq n-1
,

b_{j}:=(x-y)^{2}\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-1-j}.

Then
b_{0}\approx A
, and our goal is to show
b_{n-1}\lesssim A^{2\alpha}B^{1-2\alpha}
. By the triangle inequality for the seminorm
v\mapsto\sqrt{v^{2}\cdot z^{n-1}}
on
V_{0}
, we have

b_{n-1}\leq(\sqrt{(x-z)^{2}\cdot z^{n-1}}+\sqrt{(y-z)^{2}\cdot z^{n-1}})^{2}\leq 4B.

If
A\geq B
, then
b_{n-1}\leq 4B\leq 4A^{2\alpha}B^{1-2\alpha}
and we are done, so we may assume
A\leq B
. In this case, we show by induction that

b_{j}\lesssim A^{2^{-j}}B^{1-2^{-j}}

for
0\leq j\leq n-1
. The case
j=0
is clear, so suppose
0\leq j\leq n-2
, and note that

\displaystyle b_{j+1}-b_{j}

\displaystyle=(x-y)^{2}\cdot(z-w)\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-2-j}

\displaystyle=(x-y)\cdot(z-w)\cdot x\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-2-j}-(x-y)\cdot(z-w)\cdot y\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-2-j}.

Here we can use the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to estimate the last two terms. For example:

|(x-y)\cdot(z-w)\cdot x\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-2-j}|^{2}\\
\leq((x-y)^{2}\cdot x\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-2-j})((z-w)^{2}\cdot x\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-2-j}).

Using that
2w-x=y\in P
, we can bound the first factor by
2b_{j}
, and the second factor by
2(z-w)^{2}\cdot z^{j}\cdot w^{n-1-j}\leq 2d(z,w)
. Adding the two terms, we get

b_{j+1}-b_{j}\leq 4\sqrt{b_{j}}\sqrt{d(z,w)}
.
Now
d(z,w)\approx\delta(w,z)\leq\max\{\delta(x,z),d(y,z)\}\approx B
using the convexity of
\delta(\cdot,z)
, see Lemma
A.2
. All in all, this yields

b_{j+1}-b_{j}\lesssim\sqrt{b_{j}B}.

for
0\leq j\leq n-2
. Using the induction hypothesis
b_{j}\lesssim A^{2^{-j}}B^{1-2^{-j}}
, we get

b_{j+1}\lesssim b_{j}+\sqrt{Bb_{j}}\lesssim A^{2^{-j}}B^{1-2^{-j}}+A^{2^{-j-1}}B^{1-2^{-j-1}}\leq 2A^{2^{-j-1}}B^{1-2^{-j-1}},

where the last inequality follows from our assumption that
A\leq B
. We are done.
∎

Using Lemma
A.4
, we can now prove the quasi-triangle inequality for
\delta
, or equivalently for
d
. Fix
x,y,z\in P_{\theta}
, and set
w:=\frac{1}{2}(x+y)
. Then

d(x,y)\approx(x-y)^{2}\cdot w^{n-1}\lesssim(x-z)^{2}\cdot w^{n-1}+(y-z)^{2}\cdot w^{n-1}\\
\lesssim\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\})^{2\alpha}\max\{d(x,w),d(y,w),d(z,w)\}^{1-2\alpha},

by the triangle inequality for the norm
v\mapsto\sqrt{v^{2}\cdot w^{n-1}}
and by Lemma
A.4
. As noted above, the convexity of
\delta(\cdot,z)\approx d(\cdot,z)
gives
d(z,w)\lesssim\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\}
, as well as
d(x,w),d(y,w)\lesssim d(x,y)
. Thus

d(x,y)\lesssim\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\})^{2\alpha}\max\{d(x,y),d(x,z),d(y,z)\}^{1-2\alpha},

which easily implies
d(x,y)\leq\max\{d(x,z),d(y,z)\}
, as desired.

Next we prove (
A.5
) in general. By the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality we may assume
x_{0}=x_{1}
and
y_{0}=y_{1}
. We may also assume
n\geq 2
, or else we are done by (
A.8
). Set
z:=\tfrac{1}{n-1}(z_{2}+\dots+z_{n})
. Then

(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\cdot z_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{n}\lesssim(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\cdot z^{n-1}\lesssim d(x_{1},y_{1})^{2\alpha}\max\{d(x_{1},z),d(y_{1},z)\}^{1-2\alpha}\\
\approx\delta(x_{1},y_{1})^{2\alpha}\max\{\delta(x_{1},z),\delta(y_{1},z)\}^{1-2\alpha}.

By the quasi-triangle inequality we have
\delta(x_{1},z)\lesssim\max\{\delta(x_{1},x_{*}),\delta(z,x_{*})\}
, and by quasi-symmetry and Lemma
A.2
we have
\delta(z,x_{*})\lesssim\max_{i\geq 2}\delta(z_{i},x_{*})
. A similar estimate for
\delta(y_{1},z)
completes the proof of (
A.5
).

Next we prove (
A.6
), which is equivalent to

|(x_{0}^{n}-y_{0}^{n})\cdot(x_{1}-y_{1})|\leq\delta(x_{0},y_{0})^{1/2}\delta(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha}M^{1/2-\alpha}.

By the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, we have

|(x_{0}^{n}-y_{0}^{n})\cdot(x_{1}-y_{1})|^{2}=|(x_{0}-y_{0})(x_{1}-y_{1})\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{0}^{j}y_{0}^{n-1-j}|^{2}\\
\leq\left((x_{0}-y_{0})^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{0}^{j}y_{0}^{n-1-j}\right)\left((x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}x_{0}^{j}y_{0}^{n-1-j}\right)

Here the first factor on the right is
\approx\delta(x_{0},y_{0})
, whereas the second factor can be bounded above using (
A.5
).

It only remains to prove (
A.7
).
By the quasi-triangle inequality for
\delta
, it suffices to consider the case when
x_{0}=y_{0}
or
x_{1}=y_{1}
. Now

\displaystyle\delta(x_{0},x_{1})-\delta(x_{0},y_{1})

\displaystyle=n(x_{1}^{n+1}-y_{1}^{n+1})-(n+1)x_{0}(x_{1}^{n}-y_{1}^{n})

\displaystyle=(x_{1}-y_{1})\sum_{j,k}(x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{n-j}-x_{0}x_{1}^{k}y_{1}^{n-k-1}).

If
j\leq k
, then

x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{n-j}-x_{0}x_{1}^{k}y_{1}^{n-k-1}=x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{n-k-1}(y_{1}^{k-j+1}-x_{0}^{k-j+1})+x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{n-k-1}x_{0}(x_{0}^{k-j}-x_{1}^{k-j}),

and by factoring each term of the right-hand side we see from (
A.5
) that

|(x_{1}-y_{1})(x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{n-k}-x_{0}x_{1}^{j}y_{1}^{n-k-1})|\lesssim\delta(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha}M^{1-\alpha}.

The case when
j>k
is handled in a similar way, and adding all the terms yields

|\delta(x_{0},x_{1})-\delta(x_{0},y_{1})|\lesssim\delta(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha}M^{1-\alpha}
.

A similar argument shows that
|\delta(x_{0},x_{1})-\delta(y_{0},x_{1})|\lesssim\delta(x_{0},y_{0})^{\alpha}M^{1-\alpha}
, and completes the proof.

Appendix B
Regularization and orthogonality on Kähler spaces

By relying on a variant of the classical Richberg regularization technique, it was proved in
[
BK07
]
that any
\omega
-psh function on a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
can be written as the limit of a decreasing sequence of smooth
\omega
-psh functions. It is natural to hope that this holds in the singular case as well:

Conjecture B.1
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler space. Then any
\omega
-psh function
\varphi
on
X
can be written as the pointwise limit of a decreasing sequence
(\varphi_{i})
of smooth
\omega
-psh functions.

Note that the conclusion only depends on the Kähler class
[\omega]
. Besides the case
X
is nonsingular, mentioned above, we have:

Example B.2
.

Conjecture
B.1
holds if
X
is projective and
[\omega]
lies in the ample cone,
i.e.
the open convex cone generated by classes of ample line bundles on
X
. This is a consequence of
[
CGZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler space for which Conjecture
B.1
holds. Then
[\omega]
has the orthogonality property (cf. Definition
2.15
).

Proof.

Pick a resolution of singularities
\pi\colon Y\to X
, and set
\theta:=\pi^{\star}\omega\geq 0
. Since
\int\theta^{n}=\int\omega^{n}>0
, the
(1,1)
-class
[\theta]
is semipositive and big. For any
g\in\Cz(Y)
, consider the
\theta
-psh envelope

\env_{\theta}(g):=\sup\{\psi\in\PSH(Y,\theta)\mid\psi\leq g\}.

As is well-known,
\env_{\theta}(\gamma)
is
\theta
-psh, and
\MA_{\theta}(\env_{\theta}(g))
is supported in
\{\env_{\theta}(g)=g\}
, i.e.

(B.1)

\int_{Y}(g-\env_{\theta}(g))\MA_{\theta}(\env_{\theta}(g))=0.

For any
f\in\Cz(X)
, we next claim that
\env_{\theta}(\pi^{\star}f)
is the limit in
\mathcal{E}^{1}(Y,\theta)
of the increasing net
\{\pi^{\star}\varphi\}_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}}
. Assume this for the moment. By continuity of Monge–Ampère integrals along increasing nets in
\mathcal{E}^{1}(Y,\theta)
, and using

\int_{X}(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\int_{Y}(\pi^{\star}f-\pi^{\star}\varphi)\MA_{\theta}(\pi^{\star}\varphi),

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega}
, we infer

(B.2)

\lim_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}}\int_{X}(f-\varphi)\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\int_{Y}\left(\pi^{\star}f-\env_{\theta}(\pi^{\star}f)\right)\MA_{\theta}(\env_{\theta}(\pi^{\star}f))=0,

by (
B.1
).

To prove the claim, note first that Conjecture
B.1
implies that the increasing net
\{\varphi\}_{\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}}
converges pointwise to

\env_{\omega}(f):=\sup\{\psi\in\PSH(X,\omega)\mid\psi\leq f\}.

Indeed, given
\delta>0
and a function
\psi\in\PSH(X,\omega)
with
\psi\leq f
, Conjecture
B.1
and a Dini-type argument guarantees the existence of a function
\psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{\omega,<f}
with
\psi\leq\psi^{\prime}<f+\delta
. The claim is thus equivalent to the statement that
\pi^{\star}\env_{\omega}(f)
coincides a.e. with
\varphi:=\env_{\theta}(\pi^{\star}f)
. To prove this, pick
\tau\in\PSH(X,\omega)
with
\tau\leq f
and
\{\tau=-\infty\}=X_{\sing}
(compare proof of Lemma
1.27
). Since
\psi_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)\varphi+\varepsilon\pi^{\star}\tau
is
\pi^{\star}\omega
-psh outside
\pi^{-1}(X_{\sing})
, and
\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv-\infty
on the latter, we have
\psi_{\varepsilon}=\pi^{\star}\varphi_{\varepsilon}
for a unique
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in\PSH(X,\omega)
(see
[
Dem85
, Théorème 1.10]
). Since
\pi^{\star}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leq\pi^{\star}f
, and hence
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leq f
, we have
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leq\env_{\omega}(f)
. Thus

(1-\varepsilon)\varphi+\varepsilon\pi^{\star}\tau=\pi^{\star}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\leq\pi^{\star}\env_{\omega}(f)\leq\varphi,

and hence
\pi^{\star}\env_{\omega}(f)=\env_{\theta}(\pi^{\star}f)
on
Y\setminus\pi^{-1}(X_{\sing})
.
∎

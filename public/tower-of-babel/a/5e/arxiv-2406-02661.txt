A new standard for the logarithmic accuracy of parton showers
Melissa van Beekveld, Mrinal Dasgupta, Basem Kamal El-Menoufi, Silvia Ferrario Ravasio, Keith Hamilton, Jack Helliwell, Alexander Karlberg, Pier Francesco Monni, Gavin P. Salam, Ludovic Scyboz, Alba Soto-Ontoso, Gregory Soyez

Abstract
We report on a major milestone in the construction of logarithmically accurate final-state parton showers, achieving next-to-next-to-leading-logarithmic (NNLL) accuracy for the wide class of observables known as event shapes. The key to this advance lies in the identification of the relation between critical NNLL analytic resummation ingredients and their parton-shower counterparts. Our analytic discussion is supplemented with numerical tests of the logarithmic accuracy of three shower variants for more than a dozen distinct event-shape observables in Z → q ​ q ¯ Z\to q\bar{q} and Higgs → g ​ g \to gg decays. The NNLL terms are phenomenologically sizeable, as illustrated in comparisons to data.

One way of viewing parton showers is as automated and
immensely flexible tools for resumming those logarithms, thus
correctly reproducing the corresponding physics.

The accuracy of resummations is usually classified based on terms with

subleading powers of logarithms at each order in the coupling (next-to-leading
logarithms or NLL, NNLL, etc.).

considerably more powerful tools for analysing and interpreting

colliders.

One of the key observations of this Letter is that as long as a
parton shower correctly generates double-soft emissions in the
soft-collinear region (as required for NNLL handling of event shapes’
sensitivity to multiple emissions),

between the

This is in analogy with the fact that including the correct

without the real double-soft contribution.

.1
Demonstration of relation between
\Delta K
and drift

To help understand why Eq. (
5
) holds, we
choose here to focus on the “non-Abelian” or “correlated-emission”
channels, which for a quark emitter correspond to the terms involving

C_{F}n_{f}
and
C_{F}C_{A}
colour factors.
In general, in the soft limit, we define the correlated contribution
as being the part of the matrix element that remains after subtraction
of the double independent-emission contribution, which in the case of
a quark emitter corresponds to the
C_{F}^{2}
component of the matrix
element.

The starting point is a definition for
\Delta K_{1}(y_{\tilde{\imath}})
, to be
understood with a renormalisation scale of
\mu=k_{t{\tilde{\imath}}}
,

K_{1}+\Delta K_{1}(y_{\tilde{\imath}})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{\imath}}}\left(\mathcal{V}_{{\tilde{\imath}}}+\int d\Phi_{ij|{\tilde{\imath}}}^{\text{{ps}}}\,\mathcal{R}_{ij}\right).

(13)

It is convenient to define

R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{\imath}}}\left(\mathcal{V}_{{\tilde{\imath}}}\,\delta(y_{\tilde{\imath}}-y_{d})+\int d\Phi_{ij|{\tilde{\imath}}}^{\text{{ps}}}\,\mathcal{R}_{ij}\,\delta(y_{d}(i,j)-y_{d})\right),

(14)

where
y_{d}(i,j)
denotes the effective rapidity of the
i,j

descendants, as produced by the shower.
In the correlated-emission channels, it is to be taken as the rapidity of

i+j
.
The discussion can be adapted to the independent-emission channel by instead taking

y_{d}
to be the rapidity of either
i
or
j
, choosing the one with
the larger shower ordering variable (
v_{\text{{ps}}}=k_{t}e^{-\beta_{\text{{ps}}}|y|}
).

It is useful to introduce a few properties of
R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})
.
Firstly, a trivial rewriting of Eq. (
13
) is that

K_{1}+\Delta K_{1}(y_{\tilde{\imath}})=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dy_{d}\,R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})\,.

(15)

For the integral to converge,
R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})
must vanish
sufficiently fast when
|y_{d}-y_{\tilde{\imath}}|
is large,

R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})\to 0\,,\qquad\text{for }|y_{d}-y_{\tilde{\imath}}|\gg 1\,,

(16)

which can be seen as a consequence of the fact that for
large rapidity separations between
i
and
j
, the shower reduces to
independent emission (cf. the PanScales
conditions
[
5
]
).
A second property of
R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})
is that

K_{1}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dy_{{\tilde{\imath}}}\,R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})\,,

(17)

independently of
y_{d}
.
This corresponds to the statement that if one integrates over

y_{{\tilde{\imath}}}
, the shower will generate the correct rate of
soft-parton pairs for any
y_{d}
— it is an essential property of a
shower that has the correct double-soft contributions as in
Ref.
[
29
]
.
At large rapidities (but where the particles are still soft),

R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})
becomes a function of just
y_{d}-y_{{\tilde{\imath}}}
.
Denoting that function as
\bar{R}
, we have

\displaystyle R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})

\displaystyle\to\bar{R}(y_{d}-y_{\tilde{\imath}}),\qquad\text{for }y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d}\gg 1\,,

(18a)

\displaystyle R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})

\displaystyle\to\bar{R}(y_{\tilde{\imath}}-y_{d}),\qquad\text{for }-y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},-y_{d}\gg 1\,.

(18b)

This then implies that
\Delta K_{1}(y_{\tilde{\imath}})
tends to zero at large

|y_{\tilde{\imath}}|
, because Eqs. (
15
) and
(
17
) become equivalent.

Now let us verify Eq. (
5
), which can be written as the
large-
Y
limit of

\displaystyle\Delta K_{1}^{\text{int}}(Y)=\int_{-Y}^{+Y}dy_{\tilde{\imath}}\,\Delta K_{1}(y_{\tilde{\imath}})

\displaystyle=\int_{-Y}^{+Y}dy_{\tilde{\imath}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dy_{d}\,R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})-\int_{-Y}^{+Y}dy_{d}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dy_{\tilde{\imath}}\,R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})\,,

(19)

where, on the right-hand side, the last term is obtained by writing

2YK_{1}
in terms of Eq. (
17
) for the factor of

K_{1}
and an integral over
y_{d}
for the factor
2Y
.
Noting that the integration region where both rapidities are in the
range
-Y
to
Y
cancels between the two terms, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta K_{1}^{\text{int}}(Y)=\int_{-Y}^{+Y}dy_{\tilde{\imath}}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}dy_{d}\,R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})-\int_{-Y}^{+Y}dy_{d}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}dy_{\tilde{\imath}}\,R(y_{{\tilde{\imath}}},y_{d})\,,

(20)

where the factor of
1/2
accounts for the fact that we consider just
the positive infinite rapidity range (the negative infinite rapidity
range gives identical results, notably since

R(y_{\tilde{\imath}},y_{d})=R(-y_{\tilde{\imath}},-y_{d})
).
Next, we make use of the property that the integrand is dominated by a
region where
y_{\tilde{\imath}}
and
y_{d}
are not too different (cf. Eq. (
16
)), change variables to

\Delta_{y}\equiv y_{d}-y_{\tilde{\imath}}
and
\bar{y}=y_{\tilde{\imath}}-Y
, take
the limit
Y\to\infty
and use Eq. (
18a
) so as to write

\frac{1}{2}\Delta K_{1}^{\text{int}}\equiv\lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\Delta K_{1}^{\text{int}}(Y)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}d\Delta_{y}\int_{-\Delta_{y}}^{0}d\bar{y}\,\bar{R}(\Delta_{y})-\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\Delta_{y}\int_{0}^{-\Delta_{y}}d\bar{y}\,\bar{R}(\Delta_{y})\,.

(21)

The
d\bar{y}
integrations can be performed trivially and assembling
both terms, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\Delta K_{1}^{\text{int}}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\Delta_{y}\Delta_{y}\,\bar{R}(\Delta_{y})=\lim_{{\tilde{\imath}}\to\text{{sc}}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{\imath}}}\int d\Phi_{ij|{\tilde{\imath}}}^{\text{{ps}}}\,\mathcal{R}_{ij}\times(y_{d}-y_{\tilde{\imath}})\,,

(22)

which coincides with Eq. (
5
).

Overview of gluon case

In the gluon case, the splitting probability is given by Eq. (7.2) of

The

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{s}(k_{t})}{2\pi}\mathcal{P}_{g}(\zeta,\theta)\equiv\frac{\alpha_{s}(k_{t})}{2\pi}\left\{\frac{2C_{A}}{1-\zeta}\!\left(1+\frac{\alpha_{s}(k_{t})}{2\pi}K_{1}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{1}^{g}(\zeta)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(k_{t})}{2\pi}\left[\mathcal{B}_{2}^{g}(\zeta)+2\mathcal{B}_{1}^{g}(\zeta)\beta_{0}\ln(1-\zeta)\right]\right\},

with
\mu=k_{t}\equiv(1-\zeta)\theta E

First, we have taken into account that, as in the quark case, the

.2.3
Drift contributions

Concentrating once again on the correlated-emission channels, let us
schematically write
B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z)
as

K_{1}+B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z)\equiv\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}}\left(\mathcal{V}_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}+\int d\Phi_{ijk|{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}^{\text{{nlo}}}\,\mathcal{R}_{ijk}\right),

(36)

where in the quark branching channel
{\tilde{\imath}}
is a gluon,
{\tilde{k}}

is a quark,

and we assume
the same scale choice
\mu=zE\theta
as above.
This is the extension of Eq. (
13

region noting that (a) in the soft-collinear region,
\Delta K_{1}(y)
and

B_{2}(z)
both go to zero and (b) the “Born” starting point is a collinear

{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}
splitting, rather than just a soft
{\tilde{\imath}}

emission.
In the correlated-emission channels, the
d\Phi_{ijk|{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}^{\text{{nlo}}}

phase space map is organised such that the energy fraction carried by
the

{\tilde{\imath}}

E_{i}+E_{j}=E_{\tilde{\imath}}
, where
{\tilde{\imath}}
is the emitted gluon (the
calculation also preserves
\theta_{ij,k}=\theta_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}
).
2
2

2

In the independent-emission channel, we would instead have
E_{i}=E_{\tilde{\imath}}
and

\theta_{i,jk}=\theta_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}
.

Let us now suppose that we had a parton shower with tree-level
1\to 3

splittings and 1-loop
1\to 2
splittings.
That shower would implicitly involve a
B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)

In what follows, we will work out the relation between the integral of

B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)
and that of
B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z)
and observe that this
difference depends only on the behaviour of the shower in the soft
collinear region, where the triple-collinear structure reduces to the
double-soft structure.
This will be useful, because event-shape NNLL accuracy will be
sensitive just to the integral of
B_{2}(z)
, through its impact on
the Sudakov form factor.
Thus, even if our actual shower does not have the full triple-collinear
structure, the integral of
B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)
that we determine for an
imaginary shower that does have that structure will be sufficient to
achieve NNLL event-shape accuracy in the actual shower.
3
3

3

An
analogous statement holds for the large-angle region, where global
NNLL event-shape accuracy could have been obtained without the full
large-angle double-soft structure, as long as the double-soft structure is still correct in
the soft-collinear limit and the shower has the correct integral of

\Delta K_{1}(y)
.
We explicitly tested this by modifying
\Delta K_{1}(y)
to be some
function proportional to
e^{-|y|}
with the constraint that its
integral should be correct.
This gave event shape results that were consistent with NNLL accuracy.

The
B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)
function can be expressed as

K_{1}+B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)\equiv\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}}\left(\mathcal{V}_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}+\int d\Phi_{ijk|{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}^{\text{{ps}}}\,\mathcal{R}_{ijk}\right),

(37)

where
d\Phi_{ijk|{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}^{\text{{ps}}}
reflects the parton-shower
kinematic map and associated partitioning of phase space.
In particular the shower map will not in general have
E_{i}+E_{j}=E_{\tilde{\imath}}
or
\theta_{ij,k}=\theta_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}
.
Next, defining
B_{2}^{\text{drift}}=B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)-B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z)
so as to
obtain Eq. (
26
), we have

2C_{F}B_{2}^{\text{int,drift}}\equiv\int_{0}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)B_{2}^{\text{drift}}(z)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}dz\,P_{gq}(z)\left[B_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}(z)-B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z)\right].

(38)

It is now convenient to introduce a representation of the integrand of
Eq. (
36
) that is differential in the energy
fraction
z_{d}
of the descendants (e.g.
z_{d}=z_{i}+z_{j}
in the non-Abelian channel)

K_{1}+B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z)=\int dz_{d}R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z,z_{d})\,,\qquad R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z_{\tilde{\imath}},z_{d})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}}\left(\mathcal{V}_{{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}\delta(z_{d}-z_{\tilde{\imath}})+\int d\Phi_{ijk|{\tilde{\imath}}{\tilde{k}}}^{\text{{nlo}}}\,\mathcal{R}_{ijk}\delta(z_{d}-z_{d}(i,j))\right)\,.

(39)

For the NLO phase-space map discussed above
R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z,z_{d})

is equal to
(K_{1}+B_{2}^{\text{{nlo}}}(z))\delta(z-z_{d})
.
One can also define an analogue for the parton shower,
R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})
,
which will have a more complex structure.
We can rewrite Eq. (
38
) as

2C_{F}B_{2}^{\text{int,drift}}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}dz_{d}\int_{0}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)\left[R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})-R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z,z_{d})\right]\Theta(z-\epsilon)\,.

(40)

Within our assumption that the shower has the correct triple collinear
(and double-collinear virtual) content,
the total amount of radiation at a given

z_{d}
will be reproduced by the parton shower, i.e.

\int_{0}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})=\int_{0}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z,z_{d})\,.

(41)

Furthermore, as in the case of section
.1
, any
sensible shower that satisfies the PanScales conditions will have
the property that the integral over
R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})
is dominated by the region
of
z\sim z_{d}
.
For finite
z_{d}
, the
\Theta(z-\epsilon)
term in
Eq. (
40
) is irrelevant.
Therefore if we place an upper limit on the
z_{d}
integral,

z_{d}\ll\epsilon_{d}
, with
\epsilon\ll\epsilon_{d}\ll 1
, the
result will be unchanged,

2C_{F}B_{2}^{\text{int,drift}}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{d}\to 0\\
\epsilon/\epsilon_{d}\to 0\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{d}}dz_{d}\int_{0}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)\left[R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})-R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z,z_{d})\right]\Theta(z-\epsilon)\,.

(42)

Given that the integral now just involves a region of small
z
and

z_{d}
, i.e. we are in the soft-collinear limit,
we can use the property that aside from an overall
1/z_{d}
factor,

R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})
is a function just of the ratio of
z_{d}/z

\displaystyle R^{\text{{ps}}}(z,z_{d})

\displaystyle\overset{z,z_{d}\ll 1}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{z_{d}}\bar{R}^{\text{{ps}}}(z_{d}/z)\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle R^{\text{{nlo}}}(z,z_{d})

\displaystyle\overset{z,z_{d}\ll 1}{\longrightarrow}\frac{K_{1}}{z_{d}}\delta(z_{d}/z-1)\,.

(43b)

In this limit, we can also use the soft limit of
P_{gq}(z)
as

z\sim z_{d}\ll 1
, allowing us to write

\displaystyle 2C_{F}B_{2}^{\text{int,drift}}

\displaystyle=2C_{F}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{d}\to 0\\
\epsilon/\epsilon_{d}\to 0\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{d}}\frac{dz_{d}}{z_{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\left[\bar{R}^{\text{{ps}}}(z_{d}/z)-K_{1}\delta(z_{d}/z-1)\right]\Theta(z-\epsilon)

(44a)

\displaystyle=2C_{F}\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon_{d}\to 0\\
\epsilon/\epsilon_{d}\to 0\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\ln\frac{\epsilon_{d}}{\xi\epsilon}\left[\bar{R}^{\text{{ps}}}(\xi)-K_{1}\delta(\xi-1)\right]\,.

(44b)

To get the second line, we have replaced
z\to z_{d}/\xi
, used the fact
that
R^{\text{{ps}}}(\xi)
vanishes for
\xi\to 0
to replace the lower limit
of the
\xi
integrand with
0
, and exchanged the order of
z_{d}
and

\xi
integrations.

Next, exploiting the fact that
\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi/\xi\bar{R}^{\text{{ps}}}(\xi)=K_{1}
, we can see that the
\ln\epsilon_{d}/\epsilon
contribution vanishes,
leaving

2C_{F}B_{2}^{\text{int,drift}}=2C_{F}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\ln\frac{1}{\xi}\,\bar{R}^{\text{{ps}}}(\xi)\equiv-2C_{F}\langle\Delta_{\ln z}\rangle\,,

(45)

which is the basis for Eq. (
6
).

Eq. (
7
) expresses the
\langle\Delta_{\ln z}\rangle
in terms of

\langle\Delta_{y}\rangle
and
\langle\Delta_{\ln m_{t}}\rangle
.
The relation between
\langle\Delta_{\ln m_{t}}\rangle
and
\langle\Delta_{\ln k_{t}}\rangle
is
given by

\langle\Delta_{\ln m_{t}}\rangle-\langle\Delta_{\ln k_{t}}\rangle=\int d\Phi^{\text{{ps}}}_{ij|{\tilde{\imath}}}\,\mathcal{R}(i,j)\,\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{m_{t,ij}^{2}}{k_{t,ij}^{2}}\,.

(46)

Note that aside from the phase space map, the integrand does not
depend on
{\tilde{\imath}}
.
Accordingly, as long as the phase space map covers the full
double-soft region, which it does for any soft collinear
{\tilde{\imath}}
, we
are free to replace the parton-shower phase space map with an analytic one that
preserves
k_{t}
and rapidity.
One can then deduce the result for
Eq. (
46
) from the corresponding expressions in
the literature, e.g. Eqs. (3.7)–(3.12) of
Ref.
[
58
]
,

\langle\Delta_{\ln m_{t}}\rangle-\langle\Delta_{\ln k_{t}}\rangle=-\beta_{0}\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\,.

(47)

We have also verified this numerically for each of our shower maps.

.3
Multiple emission contribution

In our definition, the function
\mathcal{F}
in Eq. (
1
)
accounts for the difference between applying a separate condition

\Theta(V(k_{\tilde{\imath}})<v)
on each primary emission
{\tilde{\imath}}
, versus
applying a single condition
\Theta(V(\{k_{i}\})<v)
on the full set
of emissions after branching.
It starts at order
\alpha_{s}^{2}
and its NNLL component (starting from

\alpha_{s}^{2}L
) differs from the corresponding
\delta\mathcal{F}
contribution
which appears at NNLL accuracy in ARES
[
58
]
, in that
the latter also includes certain order
\alpha_{s}
terms (cf. Eq. (2.45) of
Ref.
[
58
]
).
Let us start by defining a primary inclusive emission density

d\rho_{k}=2\frac{dk_{t}}{k_{t}}\,dzP_{gq}(z)\,\frac{\alpha_{\text{eff}}}{2\pi}\Theta(z>k_{t})\,,

(48)

which allows us to write the Sudakov factor from
Eq. (
1
) as

S(v)=\exp\left[-2\int d\rho_{k}\,M(k)\Theta(V(k)>v)\right].

(49)

The factor of two in the Sudakov reflects the presence of two
hemispheres.
The matching factor
M(k)
will be irrelevant in the remainder of this
section because it tends to
1
for
k_{t}\to 0
, but we will need it
later in section
.4
.
Up to NLL, one way of writing
\mathcal{F}
is as

\mathcal{F}_{\text{{nll}}}=\frac{S(\epsilon v)}{S(v)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{{\tilde{\imath}}=1}^{n}\left[2\int d\rho_{k_{\tilde{\imath}}}\,\Theta(V(k_{\tilde{\imath}})>\epsilon v)\right]\Theta(V(k_{\tilde{1}},\ldots,k_{\tilde{n}})<v),

(50)

where
S(\epsilon v)
exponentiates the virtual corrections for no
emission down to scale
\epsilon v
, the denominator
S(v)
simply
divides out the Sudakov factor already included in Eq. (
1
),
while the sum and product account for the real emission of any number

n
of primary particles
{\tilde{\imath}}
at scales above
\epsilon v
.
The final
\Theta
-function represents the constraint on the emissions
from the requirement for the event-shape observable to have a value
less than
v
.
The parameter
\epsilon
is to be taken small, such that
\ln\epsilon

is kept finite or, equivalently,
v\ll\epsilon\ll 1
, so that one does not need to
resum
\ln\epsilon
-enhanced terms, for example in the ratio

S(\epsilon v)/S(v)
.

Now let us extend Eq. (
50
) to NNLL.
In the various manipulations along the way, we will discard
contributions that are beyond NNLL (i.e. that are
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n-2}
or
higher).
We start by writing

\mathcal{F}_{\text{{nnll}}}=\frac{S(\epsilon v)}{S(v)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{{\tilde{\imath}}=1}^{n}\left[2\int d\rho_{k_{\tilde{\imath}}}\left(u_{\tilde{\imath}}+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\int d\Phi_{i_{a}i_{b}|{\tilde{\imath}}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{i_{a}i_{b}}}{\mathcal{B}_{{\tilde{\imath}}}}\left[u_{i_{a}}u_{i_{b}}-u_{\tilde{\imath}}\right]\right)\Theta(V(k_{\tilde{\imath}})>\epsilon v)\right]\times\\
\times\Theta(V(\{k_{1}\},\{k_{2}\}\ldots,\{k_{n}\})<v)\bigg\},

(51)

where the
u_{\tilde{\imath}}
notation, inspired by generating-functionals,
means that in the second line,
\{k_{i}\}
is just
k_{\tilde{\imath}}
, while
u_{i_{a}}u_{i_{b}}

means that
\{k_{i}\}
is to be understood as
k_{i_{a}},k_{i_{b}}
, i.e.

\displaystyle u_{{\tilde{\imath}}}\Theta(V(\left\{k_{1}\right\},\dots,\left\{k_{i}\right\},\dots,\left\{k_{n}\right\})<v)

\displaystyle\equiv\Theta(V(\left\{k_{1}\right\},\dots,k_{\tilde{\imath}},\dots,\left\{k_{n}\right\})<v)\,,

(52a)

\displaystyle\quad u_{i_{a}}u_{i_{b}}\Theta(V(\left\{k_{1}\right\},\dots,\left\{k_{i}\right\},\dots,\left\{k_{n}\right\})<v)

\displaystyle\equiv\Theta(V(\left\{k_{1}\right\},\dots,k_{i_{a}},k_{i_{b}},\dots,\left\{k_{n}\right\})<v)\,.

(52b)

Note that the
{\tilde{\imath}}\to i_{a}i_{b}
branching is unitary in the
soft-collinear region that dominates
\mathcal{F}
.
The individual emission constraint

\Theta(V(k_{\tilde{\imath}})>\epsilon v)
preserves this unitarity, and so
one should similarly constrain just
V(k)>\epsilon v
in the definition of
S(\epsilon v)
of Eq. (
51
).

Next, we take advantage of freedom in how to define the unresolved
emissions (e.g.
V(k_{\tilde{\imath}})<\epsilon v
), as
long as we use consistent definitions of unresolved in the Sudakov and
real emission contributions.
In particular, we choose to place the resolution condition on final
(
i_{a},i_{b}
) particles rather than the (potentially) intermediate

{\tilde{\imath}}
particle, which gives

\mathcal{F}_{\text{{nnll}}}=\frac{\bar{S}(\epsilon v)}{S(v)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{{\tilde{\imath}}=1}^{n}\left[2\int d\rho_{k_{\tilde{\imath}}}\left(u_{\tilde{\imath}}+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\int d\Phi_{i_{a}i_{b}|{\tilde{\imath}}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{i_{a}i_{b}}}{\mathcal{B}_{{\tilde{\imath}}}}\left[u_{i_{a}}u_{i_{b}}-u_{\tilde{\imath}}\right]\right)\Theta(V(k_{i_{ab}})>\epsilon v)\right]\times\\
\times\Theta(V(\{k_{1}\},\{k_{2}\}\ldots,\{k_{n}\})<v)\bigg\}.

(53)

Relative to Eq. (
51
) there are two changes.
Firstly, at the end of the square bracket on the first line,

\Theta(V(k_{{\tilde{\imath}}})>\epsilon v)
has been replaced by

\Theta(V(k_{i_{ab}})>\epsilon v)
, where
k_{i_{ab}}
is defined to
be
k_{\tilde{\imath}}
for the
u_{\tilde{\imath}}
contribution, while for the

u_{i_{a}}u_{i_{b}}
contribution it is equal to a massless momentum with
the same transverse components and rapidity as

k_{i_{a}}+k_{i_{b}}
.
The shower and resummation have different maps from
{\tilde{\imath}}
to

i_{a}i_{b}
.
However, both maps have the property that if one integrates over all
possible
{\tilde{\imath}}
, one obtains identical final distributions of

i_{a}i_{b}
.
Since the
\Theta(V(k_{i_{ab}})>\epsilon v)
condition is a
constraint on those final momenta, not on the (sometimes) intermediate

{\tilde{\imath}}
momenta, the sum and product in Eq. (
53
)
will be the same in the shower and in the resummation (at least up to
NNLL).
The second change to note in Eq. (
53
) relative to
Eq. (
51
) is that
S(\epsilon v)
has been substituted
with
\bar{S}(\epsilon v)
, defined as

\bar{S}(\epsilon v)=\exp\left[-2\int d\rho_{k}\left(\Theta(V(k)>\epsilon v)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\int d\Phi_{ab|k}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{ab}}{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\left[\Theta(V(k_{ab})>\epsilon v)-\Theta(V(k)>\epsilon v)\right]\right)\right].

(54)

As compared to the
S(\epsilon v)
of Eq. (
49
), the
\Theta(V(k)>\epsilon v)
factor
has been replaced by conditions that are specified in terms of final
momenta, either
k
when there was no secondary branching or
k_{ab}

when there was a branching (with
k_{ab}
defined in analogy to

k_{i_{ab}}
above).
This modification is necessary in order for the exponentiated virtual
corrections in
\bar{S}(\epsilon v)
to exactly match the phase space
in the real sum and product of Eq. (
53
), as required by
unitarity.

Since the sum-product contribution in Eq. (
53
) is
the same between the parton shower and the resummation, it will cancel
in the ratio
\mathcal{F}^{\text{{ps}}}/\mathcal{F}^{\text{resum}}
, leaving us with

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\text{{ps}}}}{\mathcal{F}^{\text{resum}}}=\frac{\bar{S}^{\text{{ps}}}(\epsilon v)}{\bar{S}^{\text{resum}}(\epsilon v)}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-2\int d\rho_{k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\int d\Phi_{ab|k}^{\text{{ps}}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{ab}}{\mathcal{B}_{k}}[\Theta(V(k_{ab})>\epsilon v)-\Theta(V(k)>\epsilon v)]\right)\right].

(55)

To reach that result, it is useful to note that in

\bar{S}^{\text{resum}}
, the map
d\Phi_{ab|k}^{\text{resum}}
has the property that

V(k_{ab})
and
V(k)
are identical, giving

\bar{S}^{\text{resum}}(\epsilon v)=S(\epsilon v)
.
Our next step is to write
d\rho_{k}
explicitly in the
soft-collinear limit as
4C_{F}dk_{t}/k_{t}dy\alpha_{s}(k_{t})/2\pi
and to use

V(k)\propto k_{t}\exp[-\beta_{\text{obs}}y]
.
We then perform the
k_{t}
integration within the limits set by the
difference of
\Theta
-functions in
Eq. (
55
).
In doing so, it is convenient to approximate the scale of the coupling
as
k_{t}\sim ve^{\beta_{\text{obs}}y}
, which is legitimate up to and
including NNLL when focusing on the ratio of
\mathcal{F}^{\text{{ps}}}/\mathcal{F}^{\text{resum}}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{F}^{\text{{ps}}}}{\mathcal{F}^{\text{resum}}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-8C_{F}\int_{0}^{\ln 1/v_{\text{hc}}}\!\!\!\!dy\int\frac{dk_{t}}{k_{t}}\delta\!\left(\ln\frac{k_{t}}{ve^{\beta_{\text{obs}}y}}\right)\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(k_{t})}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int d\Phi_{ab|k}^{\text{{ps}}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{ab}}{\mathcal{B}_{k}}\left(\ln\frac{k_{t,ab}}{k_{t}}-\beta_{\text{obs}}(y_{ab}-y)\right)\right]

(56a)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{8C_{F}}{\beta_{\text{obs}}}T_{2}(v,v_{\text{hc}})\left[\langle\Delta_{\ln k_{t}}\rangle-\beta_{\text{obs}}\langle\Delta_{y}\rangle\right],

(56b)

where we have ignored any
\epsilon
dependence (since we ignore

\ln\epsilon
contributions) and in the second line we have
transformed the
dy
integral into a
1/\beta_{\text{obs}}dk_{t}/k_{t}
integral
so as to obtain the
T_{2}(v,v_{\text{hc}})
function.
The final result corresponds to Eq. (
11
).
Note that for
\beta_{\text{obs}}=0
, the result is to be understood as the

\beta_{\text{obs}}\to 0
limit of Eq. (
56b
), with

1/\beta_{\text{obs}}T_{2}(v,v_{\text{hc}})\to(\frac{\alpha_{s}(v)}{2\pi})^{2}\ln 1/v
.

.4
Equivalence with NNLL resummation

.4.1
Relation to the ARES approach

In the previous sections, we have given analytic arguments showing (i)
how we can incorporate into the shower the hard-collinear

\mathcal{B}_{2}(z)
computed analytically in
Refs.
[
67
,
68
]
, (ii) how the
various drift contributions emerge in the parton shower
Sudakov and ultimately compensate for the effects of the shower’s
double-soft map close to the observable
boundary, cf. Eq. (
12
).
In this section, we show that these ingredients, together with 3-loop
running coupling, the CMW scheme (
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
) and full
double-soft matrix-element corrections are sufficient for the shower
to achieve NNLL accuracy, for which we take ARES
[
58
]

as our reference.
4
4

4

The discussion below is presented at full colour
accuracy.
Our shower double-soft corrections are currently implemented only at
leading-colour accuracy, consequently the NNLL terms in the shower
are also only leading-colour accurate.
Were they to be upgraded to full colour, we would expect to achieve
full-colour NNLL accuracy, at least for processes with two coloured
Born legs.

Our starting point is the shower result, Eq. (
1
).
Since the
\langle\Delta_{\ln k_{t}}\rangle
and
\langle\Delta_{y}\rangle
contributions all cancel,
cf. Eq. (
12
) and
sections
.1
,
.2.3
and

.3
,
we will leave them out in the rest of this section, or equivalently
work as if they were zero.
We then write Eq. (
1
) using the shorthand
Eq. (
49
)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\textsc{ps}}_{\textsc{nnll}}(v)=\mathcal{F}^{\textsc{ps}}_{\textsc{nnll}}(v)\,S^{\textsc{ps}}(v)\,.

(57)

It is useful to write the Sudakov form factor
S^{\text{{ps}}}
separated
into terms of different logarithmic order

\ln S^{\text{{ps}}}=(\ln S)_{\text{{ll}}}+(\ln S)_{\text{{nll}}}+(\ln S)_{\text{{nnll}}}+\ldots

(58)

We can write the individual orders as integrals either over
k_{t}
and

z
(as in Eq. (
1
) and in typical resummations), or
as an integral over the shower ordering variable
v_{\text{{ps}}}
and
z
.
The latter gives slightly more complicated expressions, but helps
connect with the actual shower algorithm.
On a first pass, readers may wish to set
\beta_{\text{{ps}}}=0
, in which case

v_{\text{{ps}}}\equiv k_{t}
.
One can also check explicitly that any dependence on
\beta_{\text{{ps}}}
cancels
separately at each logarithmic order.
We take an observable that in the soft or collinear limit behaves as

V(k)=f(z)g(y)k_{t}e^{-\beta_{\text{obs}}y}
with
f(z)\to 1
for
z\to 0
and

g(y)\to 1
for
y\to\infty
.
Using
z=k_{t}e^{y}
, we can write

k_{t}=z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\textsc{ps}}^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}},\qquad V(k)=f(z)\,g(y)\,V_{\text{sc}}(k)\,,\quad\text{ with }\quad V_{\text{sc}}(k)=z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}-\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}\,.

(59)

At LL,
P_{gq}(z)
can be approximated as
2C_{F}/z
, the 3-jet
matrix-element correction factor
M(k)
can be ignored, and the
observable can be approximated by its soft-collinear limit, yielding

(\ln S)_{\text{{ll}}}\equiv-\frac{4}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv_{\text{{ps}}}}{v_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{v_{\text{{ps}}}}^{1}dz\frac{\alpha_{s}(z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}})}{2\pi}\frac{2C_{F}}{z}\,\Theta(z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}-\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}>v)\,.

(60)

Note that we choose to include
3
-loop running for the coupling even
in the LL contribution. Strictly speaking, this introduces subleading
contributions in
(\ln S)_{\text{{ll}}}
, but it simplifies the expressions for
the higher-order contributions, without compromising any of the
arguments of this section.
At NLL, we have

\displaystyle(\ln S)_{\text{{nll}}}\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{4K_{1}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv_{\text{{ps}}}}{v_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{v_{\text{{ps}}}}^{1}dz\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}})}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{2C_{F}}{z}\,\Theta(z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}-\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}>v)

\displaystyle-\frac{4B_{1}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv_{\textsc{ps}}}{v_{\textsc{ps}}}\frac{\alpha_{s}(v_{\textsc{ps}}^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}})}{2\pi}\,\Theta(v_{\textsc{ps}}^{\frac{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}>v),

(61)

where we have included the
K_{1}
NLL contribution and introduced

B_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}dz\mathcal{B}_{1}(1-z)
.
In the second line, we have substituted
z=1
in the argument of
\alpha_{s}

and the observable so as to have purely
\alpha_{s}^{n}L^{n}
terms (aside from
the higher-loop running coupling contributions).
The independence of
(\ln S)_{\text{{ll}}}
and
(\ln S)_{\text{{nll}}}

of
\beta_{\text{{ps}}}
can be verified, e.g. by changing variables from
v_{\text{{ps}}}

to
v_{\text{obs}}\equiv V_{\text{sc}}(k)
.

The NNLL contributions can be written as

\displaystyle(\ln S)_{\text{{nnll}}}\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{4K_{2}^{\text{resum}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv_{\text{{ps}}}}{v_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{v_{\text{{ps}}}}^{1}dz\frac{\alpha_{s}^{3}(z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}})}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{2C_{F}}{z}\,\Theta(z^{\frac{\beta_{\text{{ps}}}-\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}v_{\text{{ps}}}^{\frac{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}>v)

\displaystyle-\frac{4\tilde{B}_{2}^{\text{{ps}}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dv_{\textsc{ps}}}{v_{\textsc{ps}}}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(v_{\textsc{ps}}^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}})}{(2\pi)^{2}}\,\Theta(v_{\textsc{ps}}^{\frac{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}}>v)+H_{1}^{\text{{ps}}}\frac{\alpha_{s}(Q)}{2\pi}+2C_{1,\text{hc}}^{\text{{ps}}}\frac{\alpha_{s}(v^{\frac{1}{1+\beta_{\text{obs}}}})}{2\pi}+C_{1,\text{wa}}^{\text{{ps}}}\frac{\alpha_{s}(v)}{2\pi}\,.

(62)

The
K_{2}^{\text{resum}}
coefficient has
already been introduced, and we elaborate on
the others below.
Note that each of the integrals is independent of
\beta_{\text{{ps}}}
, but some of the
coefficients multiplying them depend on
\beta_{\text{{ps}}}
.
Below, we will verify that this
\beta_{\text{{ps}}}
dependence disappears
when summing over all contributions.

Let us start with
H_{1}^{\text{{ps}}}
, which involves the
3
-jet matrix element
correction factor
M(k)
,

\displaystyle H_{1}^{\text{{ps}}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left[\frac{4}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\frac{dv_{\text{{ps}}}}{v_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{v_{\text{{ps}}}}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)M(k)-\frac{4}{1+\beta_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\frac{dv_{\text{{ps}}}}{v_{\text{{ps}}}}\int_{v_{\text{{ps}}}}^{1}dzP_{gq}(z)\right]

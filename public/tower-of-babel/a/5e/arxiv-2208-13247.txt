On the corank of the fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve over a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension
Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let p p be an odd prime and F ∞ F_{\infty} be a ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extension of a number field F F . Given an elliptic curve E E over F F , we study the structure of the fine Selmer group over F ∞ F_{\infty} . It is shown that under certain conditions, the fine Selmer group is a cofinitely generated module over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} and furthermore, we obtain an upper bound for its corank (i.e., the λ \lambda -invariant), in terms of various local and global invariants.



1.
Introduction

The variation of arithmetic objects in families of number fields of interest is a central theme in number theory. Iwasawa studied the variation of class numbers of number fields in certain infinite towers of number field extensions. In greater detail, let
p
be a prime number and
F
be a number field. Set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to denote the ring of
p
-adic integers, defined to be the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower
consists of a family of Galois extensions
\{F_{n}\}
of
F
, satisfying the containments

F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{n}\subset F_{n+1}\subset\dots,

and such that
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{n}/F)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. The infinite Galois extension
F_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\cup_{n\geq 1}F_{n}
is referred to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, since the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\infty}/F)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Let
e_{n}
be the highest power of
p
dividing the class number of
F_{n}
. Iwasawa showed that there are integers
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all sufficiently large values of
n
, we have that
e_{n}=p^{n}\mu+n\lambda+\nu
(cf.
[
Iwa73
]
or
[
Was97
, Chapter 13]
). Let
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
be the
p
-primary roots of unity in
\bar{F}
; the
cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
is the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
contained in
F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. It is conjectured by Iwasawa that for the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
, the
\mu
-invariant vanishes. In other words, Iwasawa conjectured that over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
, the
p
-primary part of the Hilbert class group is cofinitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. The corank of this
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module is the
\lambda
-invariant.

Mazur (cf.
[
Maz72
]
) initiated the Iwasawa theory of abelian varieties with good ordinary reduction. The original motivation was to study the growth of Selmer groups, Tate-Shafarevich groups and Mordell–Weil ranks of an abelian variety in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of number fields. The Selmer groups in question are defined by local conditions at each prime, defined in terms of certain local Kummer maps. These Selmer groups capture various arithmetic properties of the elliptic curves or abelian varieties in question. A closer analogue with the Hilbert class groups holds for the
fine Selmer group
, which is the subgroup defined by
strict
local conditions at all primes (cf. Definition
2.1
). The fine Selmer group appeared in various guises in work of Perrin-Riou (cf.
[
PR93
,
PR95
]
), Rubin (cf.
[
Rub00
]
) and Kato (cf.
[
K+04
]
). Their algebraic properties were systematically studied by Coates and Sujatha in
[
CS05
]
, with an emphasis on the vanishing of the
\mu
-invariant. Indeed, it is conjectured that the fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of a number field is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module (cf. Conjecture A in
loc. cit.
). In recent work (cf.
[
DRS22
]
), it it shown that this conjecture is indeed satisfied over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, for various Galois representations of interest, provided certain additional conditions are satisfied.

When the
\mu
-invariant is known to vanish, the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-corank of the fine Selmer group is equal to the
\lambda
-invariant. The main focus of this paper is to shed light on the
\lambda
-invariant of the fine Selmer group of an elliptic curve over a general
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. For the classical Selmer groups of elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction, the
\lambda
-invariant over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension can be computed since there are efficient algorithms to compute the
p
-adic L-function
[
Ste07
]
. This calculation is possible since it the
main conjecture
is known to hold in this context, thanks to the seminal work of Skinner and Urban
[
SU14
]
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-corank of the fine Selmer group is shrouded in mystery since there are not many satisfactory computational tools to obtain a precise description of the characteristic ideal except for certain trivial cases. It should be noted here that one possible techniques used in obtaining some insight into the
\lambda
-invariant is via the
Euler characteristic formula
for the leading term of the characteristic series (cf.
[
Wut07
, section 6]
). If the formula for the leading term is a
p
-adic unit, then, under further hypotheses, the
\lambda
-invariant may be exactly computed to be equal to
\operatorname{max}\{0,r-1\}
, where
r
is the Mordell-Weil rank of the elliptic curve in question. Such ideas are used in studying distribution questions for the Iwasawa invariants of the fine Selmer groups of elliptic curves (cf.
[
RS21
]
). However, this technique does not provide any upper bound on the
\lambda
-invariant, when the leading term is not a
p
-adic unit. The upper bounds obtained in this paper can be expressed in terms of various local and global invariants. Theorem
4.6
describes and upper bound which holds in generality. The formula is better understood in a special case in which the global invariants in our formula are shown to vanish, see Theorem
4.9
. We provide a concrete example to better clarify the main results of the paper.

Preliminary Notions

In this section, we introduce relevant notation and recall the definition and basic properties of fine Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves. Let
F
be a number field. Throughout,
p
will be an odd prime number. Fix an algebraic closure
\bar{F}
of
F
. For each prime
v
of
F
, set
F_{v}
to denote the completion of
F
at
v
, and let
\bar{F}_{v}
be a choice of algebraic closure of
F_{v}
. Fix an embedding
\iota_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{F}\hookrightarrow\bar{F}_{v}
for each prime
v
of
F
. Given a field
\mathcal{F}
contained in
\bar{F}
, let
\operatorname{G}_{\mathcal{F}}
denote the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{F}/\mathcal{F})
.

Let
F_{\infty}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
of
F
, i.e.,
F_{\infty}
is an infinite Galois extension of
F
for which
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\infty}/F)
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we set
F_{n}
to be the extension of
F
such that
[F_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F]=p^{n}
. Note that
F_{n}
is a Galois extension of
F
and that
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{n}/F)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
E_{/F}
be an elliptic curve with good reduction at all primes
v|p
of
F
. Let
S
be the set of primes
v
of
F
such that either
v|p
or such that
E
has bad reduction at
v
. Let
S_{p}
be the set of primes of
F
that lie above
p
. Given a set of primes
\Sigma
of
F
and an algebraic extension
\mathcal{F}/F
, let
\Sigma(\mathcal{F})
be the set of primes of
\mathcal{F}
that lie above the set
\Sigma
. Given a prime
v
of
F
, set
v(\mathcal{F})
to be the set of primes of
\mathcal{F}
that lie above
v
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an extension of
F
contained in
\bar{F}
. For a prime
w
in
\mathcal{F}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{w}
be the completion of
\mathcal{F}
at
w
. For ease of notation,
H^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{w},\cdot)
(resp.
H^{i}(\mathcal{F}_{w},\cdot)
) will be used as a shorthand for
H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathcal{F}},\cdot)
(resp.
H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathcal{F}_{w}},\cdot)
). Given a prime
v
of
F
, we set
\mathcal{H}_{v}\left(\mathcal{F},E[p^{\infty}]\right)
to denote the following (possibly infinite) product

\mathcal{H}_{v}\left(\mathcal{F},E[p^{\infty}]\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{w\in v(\mathcal{F})}H^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{w},E[p^{\infty}]).

Note that all primes
v\notin S_{p}
are unramified in any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\infty}
. Therefore,
F_{\infty}
is contained in
F_{S}
. Let
F_{S}
denote the maximal extension of
F
contained in
\bar{F}
in which all the primes
v\notin S
are unramified. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is contained in
F_{S}
.
The
p
-primary fine Selmer group of
E
over
\mathcal{F}
is defined as follows

(2.1)

R\left(E/\mathcal{F}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(F_{S}/\mathcal{F},E[p^{\infty}])\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}\mathcal{H}_{v}\left(\mathcal{F},E[p^{\infty}]\right)\right\},

where the map in question is the natural map induced by restriction to
\mathcal{F}_{w}
as
w
ranges over the set
S(\mathcal{F})
.

We set
\Gamma
to denote the Galois group
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}\left(F_{\infty}/F\right)
. The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
is the completed group algebra
\Lambda=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
. Let
\gamma
be a topological generator of
\Gamma
, and set
T
to denote
(\gamma-1)
. We identify the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
. A polynomial
f(T)\in\Lambda
is said to be
distinguished
if it is a nonconstant monic polynomial and all of its non-leading coefficients are divisible by
p
.

We introduce the Iwasawa invariants associated to a finitely generated module over
\Lambda
. A map of
\Lambda
-modules
M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
is said to be a
pseudo-isomorphism
if its kernel and cokernel are both finite. Let
M
be a finitely generated module over
\Lambda
. According to the structure theorem of
\Lambda
-modules
[
Was97
, Chapter 13]
, there is a pseudoisomorphism of the form

M\longrightarrow\Lambda^{r}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{\mu_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/\left(f_{j}(T)^{\lambda_{j}}\right)\right),

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the rank,
\mu_{i}
are positive integers and
f_{j}(T)
are irreducible distinguished polynomials. The
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants are defined as follows

\mu(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mu_{i},\text{ and }\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\lambda_{j}\operatorname{deg}f_{j},

where we set
\mu(M)=0
(resp.
\lambda(M)=0
) if
s=0
(resp.
t=0
).
Denote the
\mu
(resp.
\lambda
-invariant) of
R(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
by
\mu(E/F_{\infty})
(resp.
\lambda(E/F_{\infty})
). We note that when
F_{\infty}/F
is the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, it is conjectured that
R(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module and
\mu(E/F_{\infty})=0
, cf.
[
CS05
, Conjecture A]
.

We are interested in the following question.

Question 2.1
.

Under what conditions can it be shown that the dual fine Selmer group
R(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu(E/F_{\infty})=0
? Furthermore, assuming that these conditions are satisfied, how well can one characterize the
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda(E/F_{\infty})
?

That
R(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is a finitely generated as a
\Lambda
-module is well known, and follows from a standard argument that involves an application of Nakayama’s lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

With respect to notation above, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

R(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu(E/F_{\infty})=0
,

(2)

R(E/F_{\infty})[p]
is finite.

Furthermore, if the above conditions are satisfied, then,

\lambda(E/F_{\infty})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E/F_{\infty})[p]\right).

Proof.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. It is an easy consequence of the structure theory that
M
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu(M)=0
if and only if
M
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. Let
M
be the dual fine Selmer group
R(E/F_{\infty})^{\vee}
, we find therefore that
M
is a torsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu(M)=0
if and only if
M/pM
is finite. Note that the dual of
R(E/F_{\infty})[p]
is
M/pM
, and hence the two conditions above are equivalent. Furthermore,
\lambda(M)
is equal to the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-rank of
M
, and hence,
\lambda(M)\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}M/pM
. Therefore, we find that
\lambda(E/F_{\infty})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E/F_{\infty})[p]\right)
.
∎

Given a
\Lambda
-module
M
, we say that
M
is cofinitely generated (resp. cotorsion) as a
\Lambda
-module if the Pontryagin dual
M^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}\left(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
is finitely generated (resp. torsion) as a
\Lambda
-module.

3.
The residual fine Selmer group

Recall that
F
is a number field and
E
is an elliptic curve defined over
F
, and
p
is an odd prime. Denote by
E[p]
the
p
-torsion subgroup of
E(\bar{F})
and note that as an abelian group,
E[p]
is isomorphic to
\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{2}
. We define an analogue of the fine Selmer group associated with the residual representation on
E[p]
.

Recall that
S
consists of the set of primes
v\nmid p
at which
E
has bad reduction and the primes
v\mid p
. For
v\in S
, set
\mathcal{H}_{v}(F_{\infty},E[p])
to be the (possibly infinite) product
\prod_{w\in v(F_{\infty})}H^{1}(F_{\infty,w},E[p])
. The
residual fine Selmer group
is defined as follows

R(E[p]/F_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(F_{S}/F_{\infty},E[p])\xrightarrow{\Phi}\bigoplus_{v\in S}\mathcal{H}_{v}\left(F_{\infty},E[p]\right)\right\}.

The map
\Phi
is the natural map obtained by restriction to
F_{\infty,w}
as
w
ranges over the primes in
S(F_{\infty})
. Consider the Kummer sequence

0\rightarrow E[p]\rightarrow E[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow E[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

Given a prime
v\in S
, let
h_{v}
be the map

h_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{H}_{v}(F_{\infty},E[p])\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{v}(F_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])[p]

which is the product of the natural maps induced from the Kummer sequence

h_{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}(F_{\infty,w},E[p])\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{\infty,w},E[p^{\infty}])[p],

as
w
ranges over
v(F_{\infty})
.
Let
h
be the direct sum of the maps
h_{v}
, as
v
ranges over
S

h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bigoplus_{v\in S}\mathcal{H}_{v}(F_{\infty},E[p])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}\mathcal{H}_{v}(F_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])[p].

From the Kummer sequence, we have the following diagram

(3.1)

In the above diagram, the map
g
is surjective with kernel isomorphic to
\frac{H^{0}(F_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])}{pH^{0}(F_{\infty},E[p^{\infty}])}
. The vertical map
h^{\prime}
is the restriction of the map
h
to
\operatorname{im}(\Phi)
. By an application of the Snake lemma, we have the following exact sequence

(3.2)

0\rightarrow\operatorname{ker}\Psi\rightarrow\operatorname{ker}g\rightarrow\operatorname{ker}h^{\prime}\rightarrow\operatorname{cok}\Psi\rightarrow 0.

Note that for
v\in S
, the kernel of
h_{v}
is the product of kernels of
h_{w}
as
w
ranges over the primes of
F_{\infty}
that lie above
v
.

Proposition 3.1
.

With respect to notation above, assume that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

\operatorname{ker}(h)
is finite,

(2)

R(E[p]/F_{\infty})
is finite.

Then, the fine Selmer group
R(E/F_{\infty})
is a cotorsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu(E/F_{\infty})=0
. Furthermore, we have that

\lambda(E/F_{\infty})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E[p]/F_{\infty})\right)+\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(\operatorname{ker}(h)\right).

Proof.

First, we show that
R(E/F_{\infty})
is a cotorsion
\Lambda
-module and
\mu(E/F_{\infty})=0
. According to Lemma
2.2
,
R(E/F_{\infty})
is a cotorsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu=0
if and only if
R(E/F_{\infty})[p]
is finite. Consider the map

\Psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R(E[p]/F_{\infty})\rightarrow R(E/F_{\infty})[p],

(cf. (
3.1
)) and we observe that it suffices to show that
\operatorname{ker}\Psi
and
\operatorname{cok}\Psi
are finite. Referring to the exact sequence (
3.2
), we deduce that
\operatorname{ker}\Psi
is finite since
\operatorname{ker}(g)
is finite. Since it is assumed that
\operatorname{ker}(h)
is finite, it follows that
\operatorname{cok}\Psi
is finite as well. Therefore, we have shown that
R(E/F_{\infty})[p]
is finite, and therefore deduce that
R(E/F_{\infty})
is a cotorsion
\Lambda
-module and
\mu(E/F_{\infty})=0
.

Note that it also follows from Lemma
2.2
that
\lambda(E/F_{\infty})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E/F_{\infty})[p]\right)
. Hence, we arrive at the bounds

\begin{split}\lambda(E/F_{\infty})&\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E/F_{\infty})[p]\right)\\
&\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E[p]/F_{\infty})\right)+\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(\operatorname{cok}\Psi\right)\\
&\leq\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(R(E[p]/F_{\infty})\right)+\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left(\operatorname{ker}(h)\right),\end{split}

and thus, this completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
S_{0}
be the set of primes
v\in S
at which
both
of the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

E
has bad reduction at
v
.

(2)

If
p\geq 5
and
\mu_{p}
is contained in
F_{v}
, then,
E
has split multiplicative reduction at
v
.

Assumption 3.3
.

Assume that each prime
v\in S_{0}\cup S_{p}
is finitely decomposed in
F_{\infty}
.

Since all primes of
F
are finitely decomposed in its cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, the above assumption is always satisfies when
F_{\infty}
is the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
F
.
Note that since
E
is stipulated to have good reduction at all primes
v\in S
, we find that
S_{0}
is a subset of
S\backslash S_{p}
. Given
v\in S_{p}
, set
\delta_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}&2\text{ if }E(F_{v})[p]\neq 0;\\
&0\text{ if }E(F_{v})[p]=0.\end{cases}
For any prime
v\in S\cup S_{p}
, let
g_{v}
be the number of primes
w\in v(F_{\infty})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

It follows from Assumption
3.3
that
\operatorname{ker}(h)
is finite, and moreover,

\operatorname{dim}\left(\operatorname{ker}(h)\right)\leq\sum_{v\in S_{0}}2g_{v}+\sum_{v\in S_{p}}\delta_{v}g_{v}.

Proof.

Let
v\in S
and let
w
be a prime of
F_{\infty}
that lies above
v
. From the Kummer sequence, we find that the kernel of
h_{w}
is identified with
E(F_{\infty,w})[p^{\infty}]\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Note that in particular, the dimension of
\operatorname{ker}h_{w}
is at most
2
. There are three cases to consider.

(1)

If
v\in S_{0}
, then,
\operatorname{dim}\left(\operatorname{ker}(h_{w})\right)
is at most
2
. Thus,
\operatorname{dim}\left(\operatorname{ker}(h_{v})\right)
is at most
2g_{v}
.

(2)

If
v\in S\backslash(S_{0}\cup S_{p})
, then,
\mu_{p}
is contained in
F_{v}
and
E
has either nonsplit multiplicative reduction or additive reduction at
v
. Since
v\nmid p
, it is unramified in any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension, in particular, unramified in
F_{\infty}
. Thus, the elliptic curve
E
has either non-split multiplicative reduction or additive reduction at every prime
w
of
F_{\infty}
that lies above
v
. Then, it follows from
[
HM99
, Proposition 5.1. (iii)]
that
E(F_{\infty,w})[p^{\infty}]=0
for all primes
w\in v(F_{\infty})
. As a result, the kernel of
h_{v}
is
0
in this case.

(3)

Finally, consider the case when
v\in S_{p}
. There are two subcases to consider.

(a)

First, assume that
E(F_{v})[p]=0
. Then, since
F_{\infty,w}/F_{v}
is a pro-
p
extension, it follows that
E(F_{\infty,w})[p^{\infty}]=0
(cf.
[
NSW13
, Proposition 1.6.12]
). Therefore, the kernel of
h_{v}
is
0
in this case.

(b)

On the other hand, if
E(F_{v})[p]\neq 0
, then, we do still have the bound
\operatorname{dim}\left(\operatorname{ker}h_{w}\right)\leq 2
for each prime
w
of
F_{\infty}
that lies above
w
.

From the above case decomposition, we find that
\operatorname{dim}h\leq\sum_{v\in S_{0}}2g_{v}+\sum_{v\in S_{p}}\delta_{v}g_{v}
. Since
g_{v}
is assumed to be finite for each
v\in S_{0}\cup S_{p}
, the sum is finite.

∎

4.
Main results and their proofs

In this section, we state and prove the main results of the paper. We illustrate the bounds obtained via a concrete example at the end of the section.

Given an algebraic extension
\mathcal{F}
, let
H(\mathcal{F})
(resp.
A(\mathcal{F})

Definition 4.1

Denote by

The following assumption is a special case of Iwasawa’s classical

Assumption 4.2

Assume that

is torsion

Lemma 4.3

With respect to notation above, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

is satisfied,

(2)

Y

Furthermore, if the above equivalent conditions are satisfied, then,

It is an easy consequence of the structure theory of finitely generated Iwasawa modules that Assumption

It follows from Lemma

\mathfrak{Y}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\lambda(\mathfrak{Y})}\oplus A,

Spike Variations for Stochastic Volterra Integral Equations
Tianxiao Wang, Jiongmin Yong

Abstract
Spike variation technique plays a crucial role in deriving Pontryagin’s type maximum principle of optimal controls for differential equations of several types, including ordinary differential equations (ODEs), partial differential equations (PDEs), and stochastic differentia equations (SDEs), when the control domains are not assumed to be convex. This technique also applies to (deterministic forward) Volterra intrgral equations (FVIEs). It is natural to expect that such a technique could be extended to the case of (forward) stochastic Volterra integral equations (FSVIEs). However, by mimicking the case of SDEs, one encounters an essential difficulty of handling an involved quadratic term. To overcome the difficulty, we introduce an auxiliary process for which one can use Itô’s formula, and adopt a trick used in linear-quadratic stochastic optimal control problems. Then a suitable representation of the above-mentioned quadratic form is obtained, and the second order adjoint equations are derived. Consequently, the maximum principle of Pontryagin type is established. Some relevant extensions are investigated as well.



Keywords.
Backward stochastic Volterra integral equations, spike variation, second-order adjoint equations, non-convex control domain.

In the current paper, we are going to develop a spike variation technique for FSVIEs. The main ideas can be described as follows. Recall that in the study of linear-quadratic (LQ, for short) optimal controls, people could derive the Riccati equation by applying the Itô’s formula to the map
t\mapsto X(t)^{\top}P(t)X(t)
with
P(\cdot)
being a symmetric matrix valued process which characterizes the quadratic value function of the problem. It turns out that this idea can be adopted to our current problem. Together with the introduction of a suitable auxiliary process, we are able to represent the quadratic form involved
X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
in a desired fashion. Consequently, the second-order adjoint equation will be derived leading to the maximum principle. Moreover, we will go a little further. Recall that in 2007, Mou–Yong (
[
23
]
) established a variational formula for an SDE state equation and the possibly vector-valued cost/payoff functionals. Those results are applicable to multi-objective problems and multi-person differential games of SDEs. The proof of such a variational formula (for SDEs) is almost the same as that for single-objective optimal control problem. Inspired by this, in the current paper, for FSVIEs, we will do the same thing, so that it will lead to necessary conditions for the Nash equilibria of multi-person dynamic games for FSVIEs. By letting the number of players go to infinite, and under some structure conditions, it is expected to obtain the necessary conditions for the equilibria of the mean-field games governed by FSVIEs, which includes the standard SDE case. We hope to report more details in our forthcoming publications.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some preliminary notations and assumptions. In Section 3, we present the variations of the state and the cost functional under the spike variation of the control process, whose detailed and lengthy proof will be given in Section 7. In Section 4, we give a representation of the involved quadratic functional by introducing a system of BSVIEs, called the
second order adjoint equations
. In Section 5, we prove the well-posedness of the aforementioned second order adjoint equations. We present the Pontryagin’s type maximum principle and some extensions to multi-person dynamic games for FSVIEs in Section 6. Finally, some concluding remarks are collected in Section 8.

Variations of the State and the Cost Functional

In this section, we are going to present the variations of the state process and the cost functional under the spike variation of the control process. For Problem (C), such a result will lead to the first order optimality condition in the form of variational inequality.

To state the main result of this section, let
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
be any fixed state-control pair of the state equation, which could be optimal, in particular. Choose any
u\in U
,
\tau\in[0,T)

\varepsilon>0

(3.1)

This is called a
spike variation
of
\bar{u}(\cdot)
in the direction of
u

[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]
. Let
X^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
be the state process corresponding to
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
. Our goal is to obtain expansions of
X^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-\bar{X}(\cdot)
and

\varepsilon

To begin, we introduce the following abbreviations:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle b_{x}(t,s)=b_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad b^{i}_{xx}(t,s)=b^{i}_{xx}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sigma_{x}(t,s)=\sigma_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad\sigma^{i}_{xx}(t,s)=\sigma^{i}_{xx}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle g_{x}(s)=g_{x}(s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\qquad g_{xx}(s)=g_{xx}(s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle h_{x}=h_{x}(\bar{X}(T)),\qquad h_{xx}=h_{xx}(\bar{X}(T)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\delta b(t,s)=b(t,s,\bar{X}(s),u)-b(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\delta\sigma(t,s)=\sigma(t,s,\bar{X}(s),u)-\sigma(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)=\sigma_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),u)-\sigma_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s)).\end{array}

With the above notations, we introduce the following variational equations:

X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)\negthinspace=\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{t}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)ds+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{t}\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)+\delta\sigma(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\big]dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T],

(3.2)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(b_{x}(t,s)X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)+{1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}+\delta b(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)+{1\over 2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}+\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\Big)dW(s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad t\in[0,T],\end{array}

(3.3)

where

b_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{\top}b^{1}_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)\\
\vdots\\
X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{\top}b^{n}_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)\end{pmatrix},

and
\sigma_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}
is similar. Under (H1), the above variational equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) have unique strong solutions
X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
and
X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
. We also introduce the following first order adjoint equations:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\eta(t)=h_{x}^{\top}-\int_{t}^{T}\zeta(s)dW(s),\qquad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Y(t)=g_{x}(t)^{\top}+b_{x}(T,t)^{\top}h_{x}^{\top}+\sigma_{x}(T,t)^{\top}\zeta(t)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(b_{x}(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)+\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{\top}Z(s,t)\Big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

The first equation of the above is the simplest BSDE which is just a martingale representation. The second is a Type-II BSVIE which has a unique adapted M-solution (
[
36
]
). It is clear that the solutions
(\eta(\cdot),\zeta(\cdot))
and
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
of (
3.4
) are only depending on the pair
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
(and independent of the spike variation of the control). Finally, let us define the following Hamiltonian:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle H(s,x,u,\eta(s),\zeta(s),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,s))\negthinspace=\negthinspace\mathop{\langle}\eta(s),b(T,s,x,u)\mathop{\rangle}\negthinspace+\negthinspace\mathop{\langle}\zeta(s),\sigma(T,s,x,u)\mathop{\rangle}\negthinspace+g(s,x,u)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;+\mathbb{E}_{s}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{s}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big(\negthinspace\negthinspace\mathop{\langle}Y(t),b(t,s,x,u)\mathop{\rangle}\negthinspace+\negthinspace\mathop{\langle}Z(t,s),\sigma(t,s,x,u)\mathop{\rangle}\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big)dt.\end{array}

(3.5)

Hereafter,
\mathbb{E}_{s}=\mathbb{E}[\,\cdot\,|{\cal F}_{s}]
is the conditional expectation operator. Then the main result of this section can be stated as follows.

Theorem 3.1

Let
(H1)–(H2)
hold. Then for any
k\geqslant 1
,

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{k}=O(\varepsilon^{k\over 2}),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}=O(\varepsilon^{k\over 2}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}=O(\varepsilon^{k}),\qquad\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}=O(\varepsilon^{k}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}=o(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

Moreover,

J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(\bar{u}(\cdot))=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\Delta H(s)ds+{1\over 2}\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)+o(\varepsilon),

(3.7)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Delta H(s)=H(s,\bar{X}(s),u,\eta(s),\zeta(s),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,s))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-H(s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s),\eta(s),\zeta(s),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,s)),\end{array}

(3.8)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}\Big[X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(T)^{\top}h_{xx}(\bar{X}(T))X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(T)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{\top}H_{xx}(s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s),\eta(s),\zeta(s),Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,s))X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\equiv\mathbb{E}\Big[X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(T)^{\top}h_{xx}X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(T)+\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{\top}H_{xx}(s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)ds\Big].\end{array}

(3.9)

The above is essentially shown in
[
32
]
with the main idea of
[
25
]
(see also
[
37
]
). For convenience of the readers, we provide a detailed proof in Section 7. We point out that in the last estimate of (
3.6
),
2
cannot be replaced by “any
k\geqslant 1
”.

4
Representation of the Quadratic Form.

Recalling (
3.2
) (suppressing the superscript
\varepsilon
in
X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X_{1}(t)=0,\qquad\qquad t\in[0,\tau],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X_{1}(t)=\int_{\tau}^{t}b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)ds+\int_{\tau}^{t}\big[\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\big]dW(s),\quad t\in[\tau,T],\end{array}

where

\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\equiv\delta\sigma(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s).

Thus,
X_{1}(\cdot)
is the image of
\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
under a linear operator, i.e.,

X_{1}(t)=\Phi[\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\cdot\,,\cdot)](t),\qquad\qquad t\in[\tau,T],

for some operator
\Phi
. Let

X_{1}(T)=\Phi[\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\cdot\,,\cdot)](T)\equiv\widehat{\Phi}[\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\cdot\,,\cdot)].

Consequently, by misuse the notation, we roughly have

\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)=\mathop{\langle}\big(\widehat{\Phi}^{*}h_{xx}\widehat{\Phi}+\Phi^{*}H_{xx}\Phi\big)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon},\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}\mathop{\rangle}.

(4.1)

In the above, the dependence of
\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)
on
\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
is not directly enough, due to the fact that the operator
\Phi
is complicated. Therefore, (
4.1
) is not an integral of a bilinear form in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The purpose of this section is to obtain a more direct form of
\mathscr{E}(\cdot)
in terms of
\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, whose procedure leads to the second adjoint equation.
To express the main idea, let us first look at the following simple calculation. Such an idea comes from the standard study of LQ problems.

Consider the following linear SDE:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle dX(s)=\big[AX(s)+b(s)\big]ds+\big[CX(s)+\sigma(s)\big]dW(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(t)=x,\end{array}\right.

with suitable matrices
A,C
and processes
b(\cdot),\sigma(\cdot)
. Also, we are given the following quadratic functional

J=\mathbb{E}\Big[X(T)^{\top}GX(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(X(s)^{\top}QX(s)+2q(s)^{\top}X(s)\Big)ds\Big],

with suitable
G,Q
, and
q(\cdot)
. We want to absorb the first term on the right-hand side of the above into the second one. To this end, we let
(P(\cdot),\Lambda(\cdot))
be the adapted solution to the following BSDE (for symmetric matrix valued processes):

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle dP(t)=-\Gamma(t)dt+\Lambda(t)dW(t),\qquad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P(T)=G,\end{array}\right.

with
\Gamma(\cdot)
undetermined (symmetric matrix valued process). Then by Itô’s formula (suppressing
s
), we have

d(PX)=\Big(-\Gamma X+P(AX+b)+\Lambda(CX+\sigma)\Big)ds+\Big(\Lambda X+P(CX+\sigma)\Big)dW.

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d(X^{\top}PX)=\Big[(X^{\top}A^{\top}+b^{\top})PX+X^{\top}(-\Gamma X+PAX+Pb+\Lambda CX+\Lambda\sigma)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(X^{\top}C^{\top}+\sigma^{\top})(\Lambda X+PCX+P\sigma)\Big]ds+\{\cdots\}dW(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\Big[X^{\top}\Big(-\Gamma+PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+\Lambda C+C^{\top}\Lambda\Big)X^{\top}+X^{\top}\Big(Pb+C^{\top}P\sigma+\Lambda\sigma)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(b^{\top}P+\sigma^{\top}PC+D^{\top}\Lambda\Big)X+\sigma^{\top}P\sigma\Big]ds+\{\cdots\}dW(s).\end{array}

Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle J=\mathbb{E}\Big[X(T)^{\top}GX(T)+\int_{t}^{T}\Big(X(s)^{\top}QX(s)+2q(s)^{\top}X(s)\Big)ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big[X(s)^{\top}\Big(-\Gamma+PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+\Lambda C+C^{\top}\Lambda+Q\Big)X(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2\Big(b^{\top}P+\sigma^{\top}PC+\sigma^{\top}\Lambda+q^{\top}\Big)X+\sigma^{\top}P\sigma\Big]ds+x^{\top}P(t)x.\end{array}

We see that the term
X(T)^{\top}GX(T)
has been absorbed. Further, it is easy to see that to elimnate the term
X(s)^{\top}(\cdots)X(s)
under the integral, we may choose

\Gamma=PA+A^{\top}P+C^{\top}PC+\Lambda C+C^{\top}\Lambda+Q,

so that one has

J=x^{\top}P(t)x+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big[2\Big(Pb+C^{\top}P\sigma+\Lambda\sigma+q\Big)^{\top}X+\sigma^{\top}P\sigma\Big]ds.

Such an idea will play a crucial role below.

Note that the above could not apply directly to
\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)
as
t\mapsto X_{1}(t)
does not satisfy an SDE, and therefore, the Itô’s formula could not apply. To overcome this difficulty, we introduce an auxiliary process
{\cal X}_{1}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
as follows

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)\negthinspace=\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace b_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)ds\negthinspace+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}(s)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\big]dW(s),\quad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant t\leqslant T.\end{array}

(4.2)

Similar form also appeared in
[
31
]
. Note that
r\mapsto{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)
satisfies an SDE on
[\tau,t]
, and thus the Itô’s formula can be used. Moreover, for any
t\in[\tau,T]
, by the second condition in (
3.6
), one has

\sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{r\in[\tau,t]}|{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)|^{p}\Big]=O(\varepsilon^{p\over 2}).

(4.3)

Further,
it is clear that

{\cal X}_{1}(r,r)=X_{1}(r),\qquad r\in[\tau,T].

Thus,

\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}\Big[{\cal X}_{1}(T,T)^{\top}h_{xx}{\cal X}_{1}(T,T)+\int_{0}^{T}{\cal X}_{1}(s,s)^{\top}H_{xx}(s){\cal X}_{1}(s,s)ds\Big].

(4.4)

We now treat the terms in
\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)
. To this end, we first carry out a general calculation which will be used several times below.

Let
\Theta:\Delta_{*}[\tau,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a process such that for each
t\in[\tau,T]
,
\Theta(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\tau,t;

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. For each
(t,s)\in\Delta_{*}[\tau,T]
, applying the martingale representation theorem to the random variable
\Theta(t,s)
, one can find a unique
\Lambda(t,s,\cdot)
such that

\Pi(t,s,r)\equiv\mathbb{E}_{r}[\Theta(t,s)]=\Theta(t,s)-\int_{r}^{s}\Lambda(t,s,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(4.5)

Then, applying Itô’s formula to the map
r\mapsto\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)
, we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\big[\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)\big]=\Big[\Big(\Pi(t,s,r)b_{x}(s,r)+\Lambda(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)\Big)X_{1}(r)+\Lambda(t,s,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\Big]dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big[\Lambda(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)+\Pi(t,s,r)\Big(\sigma_{x}(s,r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\Big)\Big]dW(r).\end{array}

Further, applying the Itô’s formula to the map
r\mapsto{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)
yields

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle d\big[{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)\big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}b_{x}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Big(\Pi(t,s,r)b_{x}(s,r)+\Lambda(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)\Big)X_{1}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(r,s)^{\top}\Lambda(t,s,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)+\Big(X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,r)^{\top}+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,r)^{\top}\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad\cdot\Big(\Lambda(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)+\Pi(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)X_{1}(r)+\Pi(t,s,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\Big)\Big]dr+\Gamma(t,s,r)dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r)+\sigma_{x}(t,r)^{\top}\Lambda(t,s,r)\Big){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Big(\Pi(t,s,r)b_{x}(s,r)+\Lambda(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)\Big)X_{1}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,r)^{\top}\Big(\Lambda(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)+\Pi(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)X_{1}(r)\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\Big({\cal X}_{1}(r,s)^{\top}\Lambda(t,s,r)+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r)\Big)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\Big]dr+\Gamma(t,s,r)dW(r),\end{array}

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Gamma(t,s,r)=\Big(X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,r)^{\top}+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,r)^{\top}\Big)\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Big[\Lambda(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)+\Pi(t,s,r)\Big(\sigma_{x}(s,r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\Big)\Big].\end{array}

(4.6)

By the integrability of
(\Pi,\Lambda)
,
X_{1}
,
{\cal X}_{1}
, and (H1), we see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,r)^{\top}\Big(\Lambda(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)+\Pi(t,s,r)\sigma_{x}(s,r)X_{1}(r)\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\Big({\cal X}_{1}(r,s)^{\top}\Lambda(t,s,r)+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r)\Big)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,r)\Big]dr=o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

(4.7)

Thus, (noting
\tau\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T
, and
{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)
and
{\cal X}_{1}(s,r)
being
{\cal F}_{r}
-measurable)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Theta(t,s){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[{\cal X}_{1}(t,r)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,r){\cal X}_{1}(s,r)\big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,\theta)+\sigma_{x}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda(t,s,\theta)\Big){\cal X}_{1}(s,\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Big(\Pi(t,s,\theta)b_{x}(s,\theta)+\Lambda(t,s,\theta)\sigma_{x}(s,\theta)\Big)X_{1}(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,\theta)\sigma_{x}(s,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Pi(t,s,\theta)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,\theta)\Big]d\theta+o(\varepsilon)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Theta(t,s)+\sigma_{x}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda(t,s,\theta)\Big){\cal X}_{1}(s,\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Big(\Theta(t,s)b_{x}(s,\theta)+\Lambda(t,s,\theta)\sigma_{x}(s,\theta)\Big)X_{1}(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Theta(t,s)\sigma_{x}(s,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,\theta)^{\top}\Theta(t,s)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(s,\theta)\Big]d\theta+o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

(4.8)

In the above, to get rid of the Itô’s integral of
\Gamma(t,s,\cdot)
under the expectation, some standard localization arguments via stopping times have been used. We point out that the arguments of Itô’s formula on
{\cal X}_{1}
also appeared in
[
31
]
. Now, we are ready to look at the terms in
\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)
(see (
3.9
)).

Step 1. Treatment of the first term in
\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)
.

Take
t=s=T
and
\Theta(T,T)=h_{xx}
in (
4.5
). Denote the adapted solution of (
4.5
) by

(\Pi(T,T,r),\Lambda(T,T,r))\equiv(P_{1}(r),Q_{1}(r)),\qquad r\in[\tau,T].

Then (
4.5
) reads

P_{1}(r)=h_{xx}-\int_{r}^{T}Q_{1}(\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad r\in[\tau,T].

(4.9)

By the uniqueness,
P_{1}(\cdot)
and
Q_{1}(\cdot)
take values in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Making use of (
3.6
) and (H1), one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|X_{1}(s)\big|\big|P_{1}(s)\big|\big|\delta\sigma(T,s)\big|ds\\
\displaystyle\leqslant\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|X_{1}(s)\big|^{4}ds\Big]^{1\over 4}\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|\delta\sigma(T,s)\big|^{4}ds\Big]^{1\over 4}\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|P_{1}(s)\big|^{2}ds\Big]^{1\over 2}=o(\varepsilon),\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|{\cal X}_{1}(T,s)\big|\big|Q_{1}(s)\big|\big|\delta\sigma(T,s)\big|ds\\
\displaystyle\leqslant\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|{\cal X}_{1}(T,s)\big|^{4}ds\Big]^{1\over 4}\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|\delta\sigma(T,s)\big|^{4}ds\Big]^{1\over 4}\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\big|Q_{1}(s)\big|^{2}ds\Big]^{1\over 2}=o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

Therefore, the corresponding (
4.7
) follows. On the other hand, in the current case, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Gamma(T,T,r)={\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}Q_{1}(r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\Big(\sigma_{x}(T,r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\Big)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big(X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}\Big)P_{1}(r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r).\end{array}

Again, by a localization argument, we can get rid of the Itô’s integral of
\Gamma(T,T,\cdot)
under expectation to obtain the following, which is a particular case of (
4.8
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}(T)^{\top}h_{xx}X_{1}(T)\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[{\cal X}_{1}(T,T)^{\top}P_{1}(T){\cal X}_{1}(T,T)\big]\\
\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)+\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}Q_{1}(r)\Big){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}\Big(P_{1}(r)b_{x}(T,r)+Q_{1}(r)\sigma_{x}(T,r)\Big)X_{1}(r)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\sigma_{x}(T,r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\Big]dr+o(\varepsilon)\\
\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{1}(r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}F_{1}(r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(r)^{\top}G_{1}(r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\Big]dr+o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

(4.10)

Here,

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle F_{1}(r)=b_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)+\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}Q_{1}(r),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle G_{1}(r)=\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\sigma_{x}(T,r).\end{array}\right.

(4.11)

Note that both
F_{1}(r)
and
G_{1}(r)
are
{\cal F}_{r}
-measurable and independent of
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
. Consequently, at the end of this step, one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{1}(r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}F_{1}(r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\negthinspace\Big(H_{xx}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace G_{1}(r)\Big)X_{1}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}\negthinspace P_{1}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\Big]dr\negthinspace+\negthinspace o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

(4.12)

We see that, with the above step, the term
\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}(T)^{\top}h_{xx}X_{1}(T)\big]
is absorbed. However, the new term of the form
X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{1}(r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)
and its transpose appear under integral. This will be handled by the next step.

Step 2. Treatment of the term
X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{1}(r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)
and its transpose.

Take
t=T
in (
4.5
) and let
\Theta(T,s)=\Theta_{2}(T,r)
be undetermined (which is
{\cal F}_{s}
-measurable). By martingale representation, one has

\Theta_{2}(T,s)=\mathbb{E}_{r}[\Theta_{2}(T,s)]+\int_{r}^{s}\Lambda_{2}(T,s,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T.

(4.13)

In this case, (
4.8
) reads

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)X_{1}(r)\big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)+\sigma_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda_{2}(T,r,\theta)\Big){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Big(\Theta_{2}(T,r)b_{x}(r,\theta)+\Lambda_{2}(T,r,\theta)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)\Big)X_{1}(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)\Big]d\theta\negthinspace+\negthinspace o(\varepsilon)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}F_{2}(r,\theta){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)+{\cal X}_{1}(T,\theta)^{\top}\widetilde{F}_{2}(r,\theta)^{\top}X_{1}(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{1\over 2}X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}G_{2}(r,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)\Big]d\theta+o(\varepsilon),\end{array}

(4.14)

where

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle F_{2}(r,\theta)=b_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)+\sigma_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda_{2}(T,r,\theta),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{2}(r,\theta)=b_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)^{\top}+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda_{2}(T,r,\theta)^{\top},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle G_{2}(r,\theta)=\sigma_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(T,\theta).\end{array}\right.

(4.15)

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\big[{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)X_{1}(r)+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)^{\top}{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)\big]dr\\
\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}F_{2}(r,\theta){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)+{\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)F_{2}(r,\theta)^{\top}X_{1}(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(T,\theta)^{\top}\widetilde{F}_{2}(r,\theta)^{\top}X_{1}(\theta)+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\widetilde{F}_{2}(r,\theta){\cal X}_{1}(T,\theta)+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}G_{2}(r,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,r)^{\top}\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,\theta)\Big]d\theta dr+o(\varepsilon)\\
\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{r}^{T}\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{2}(\theta,r){\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)F_{2}(\theta,r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}\widetilde{F}_{2}(\theta,r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\widetilde{F}_{2}(\theta,r){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)+X_{1}(r)^{\top}G_{2}(\theta,r)X_{1}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,\theta)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,\theta)^{\top}\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\Big]d\theta dr+o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

Consequently, (
4.12
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)\equiv\mathbb{E}\Big[X_{1}(T)^{\top}h_{xx}X_{1}(T)+\int_{\tau}^{T}X_{1}(r)^{\top}H_{xx}(r)X_{1}(r)dr\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Big(F_{1}(r)-\Theta_{2}(T,r)^{\top}+\int_{r}^{T}\widetilde{F}_{2}(\theta,r)d\theta\Big){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+{\cal X}_{1}(T,r)^{\top}\Big(F_{1}(r)^{\top}-\Theta_{2}(T,r)+\int_{r}^{T}\widetilde{F}_{2}(\theta,r)^{\top}d\theta\Big)X_{1}(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{r}^{T}\big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{2}(\theta,r){\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)^{\top}F_{2}(\theta,r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)\big]d\theta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Big(H_{xx}(r)+G_{1}(r)+\int_{r}^{T}G_{2}(\theta,r)d\theta\Big)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,\theta)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)^{\top}\Theta_{2}(T,\theta)^{\top}\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\big]d\theta\Big]dr\negthinspace+\negthinspace o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

Thus, in order to eliminate the term of the form
X_{1}(r)^{\top}(\cdots){\cal X}_{1}(T,r)
and its transpose, we let

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Theta_{2}(T,r)=F_{1}(r)^{\top}+\mathbb{E}_{r}\int_{r}^{T}\widetilde{F}_{2}(\theta,r)^{\top}d\theta\\
\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[P_{1}(r)b_{x}(T,r)+Q_{1}(r)\sigma_{x}(T,r)+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big(\Theta_{2}(T,\theta)b_{x}(\theta,r)+\Lambda_{2}(T,\theta,r)\sigma_{x}(\theta,r)\Big)d\theta\Big].\end{array}

Now, by denoting

P_{2}(r)=\Theta_{2}(T,r)^{\top},\qquad Q_{2}(\theta,r)=\Lambda_{2}(T,\theta,r)^{\top},\qquad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant\theta\leqslant T,

we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P_{2}(r)=\mathbb{E}_{r}\Big[b_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)+\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}Q_{1}(r)+\int_{r}^{T}\Big(b_{x}(\theta,r)^{\top}P_{2}(\theta)+\sigma_{x}(\theta,r)^{\top}Q_{2}(\theta,r)\Big)d\theta\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad=b_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)+\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}Q_{1}(r)+\int_{r}^{T}\Big(b_{x}(\theta,r)^{\top}P_{2}(\theta)+\sigma_{x}(\theta,r)^{\top}Q_{2}(\theta,r)\Big)d\theta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{r}^{T}\widetilde{Q}_{2}(r,\theta)dW(\theta),\end{array}

for some
\widetilde{Q}_{2}(r,\theta)
defined on
\Delta^{*}[\tau,T]
, or for
\tau\leqslant r\leqslant\theta\leqslant T
, with a suitable measurability and integrability. At the same time, (
4.13
) reads

P_{2}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{r}[P_{2}(s)]+\int_{r}^{s}Q_{2}(s,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T,

with
Q_{2}(s,\theta)
defined on
\Delta_{*}[t,T]
, or for
\tau\leqslant\theta\leqslant s\leqslant T
. We now extend
Q_{2}(s,\theta)
from
\Delta_{*}[\tau,T]
to
[\tau,T]^{2}
by

Q_{2}(s,\theta)=\widetilde{Q}_{2}(s,\theta),\qquad\tau\leqslant s\leqslant\theta\leqslant T.

Then
(P_{2}(\cdot),Q_{2}(\cdot\,,\cdot))
is the unique adapted M-solution to the following Type-II BSVIE:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P_{2}(r)\negthinspace=\negthinspace b_{x}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\sigma_{x}(T,r)^{\top}Q_{1}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big(b_{x}(\theta,r)^{\top}P_{2}(\theta)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\sigma_{x}(\theta,r)^{\top}Q_{2}(\theta,r)\Big)d\theta\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\int_{r}^{T}Q_{2}(r,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad r\in[\tau,T],\end{array}

(4.16)

which is independent of spike variation
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
of
\bar{u}(\cdot)
. Having the above result, we obtain:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)=\negthinspace\mathbb{E}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Big(H_{xx}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace G_{1}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace G_{2}(\theta,r)d\theta\Big)X_{1}(r)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{r}^{T}\Big(X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{2}(\theta,r){\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)^{\top}F_{2}(\theta,r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)\Big)d\theta\Big]dr\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\Big[\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}P_{1}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{r}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big(\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)^{\top}P_{2}(\theta)^{\top}\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)^{\top}P_{2}(\theta)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(T,r)\Big)d\theta\Big]dr+o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

(4.17)

We see by (
4.15
) that

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle F_{2}(r,\theta)=b_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}P_{2}(r)^{\top}+\sigma_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}Q_{2}(r,\theta),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle G_{2}(r,\theta)=\sigma_{x}(T,\theta)^{\top}P_{2}(r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}P_{2}(r)\sigma_{x}(T,\theta).\end{array}\right.

(4.18)

Now, in
\mathscr{E}(\varepsilon)
(see (
4.17
)), the terms of the forms
X_{1}(r)^{\top}(\cdots)X_{1}(r)
and
X_{1}(r)^{\top}(\cdots){\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)
, together with its transpose, need to be handled. In the next step, we will get rid of those terms simultaneously.

Step 3. Treatment of the terms
X_{1}(r)^{\top}(\cdots)X_{1}(r)
,
{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)^{\top}(\cdots)X_{1}(r)
, and its transpose.

To treat
X_{1}(r)^{\top}(\cdots)X_{1}(r)
, we take
t=s
in (
4.5
) with
\Theta(s,s)=\Theta_{3}(s)
taking values in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, undetermined. Again, by martingale representation theorem, we have a unique
\Lambda_{3}(s,s,\cdot)
such that

\Theta_{3}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{r}[\Theta_{3}(s)]+\int_{r}^{s}\Lambda_{3}(s,s,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T.

(4.19)

Since
\Theta_{3}(s)
is symmetric, so is
\Lambda_{3}(s,s,r)
. Now, (
4.8
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)X_{1}(r)\big]=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda_{3}(r,r,\theta)\Big){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Big(\Theta_{3}(r)b_{x}(r,\theta)+\Lambda_{3}(r,r,\theta)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)\Big)X_{1}(\theta)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)\Big]d\theta+o(\varepsilon)\\
\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}F_{3}(r,\theta){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)+{\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)^{\top}F_{3}(r,\theta)^{\top}X_{1}(\theta)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}G_{3}(r,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)\Big]d\theta+o(\varepsilon),\end{array}

where

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle F_{3}(r,\theta)=b_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Lambda_{3}(r,r,\theta),\\
\displaystyle G_{3}(r,\theta)=\sigma_{x}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta).\end{array}\right.

(4.20)

Then integrate it over
[\tau,T]
, one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[X_{1}(r)^{\negthinspace\top}\negthinspace\Theta_{3}(r)X_{1}(r)\big]dr\negthinspace=\negthinspace\mathbb{E}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big[X_{1}(\theta)^{\negthinspace\top}\negthinspace F_{3}(r,\theta){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)\negthinspace+\negthinspace{\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta)^{\negthinspace\top}\negthinspace F_{3}(r,\theta)^{\negthinspace\top}\negthinspace X_{1}(\theta)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta)^{\top}G_{3}(r,\theta)X_{1}(\theta)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta)\Big]d\theta dr+o(\varepsilon)\\
\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{T}\int_{r}^{T}\Big[X_{1}(r)^{\top}F_{3}(\theta,r){\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)+{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)^{\top}F_{3}(\theta,r)^{\top}X_{1}(r)\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(r)^{\top}G_{3}(\theta,r)X_{1}(r)+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)^{\top}\Theta_{3}(\theta)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,r)\Big]d\theta dr+o(\varepsilon).\end{array}

(4.21)

Next, for the term
{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)^{\top}(\cdots)X_{1}(r)
, we let
\Theta(t,s)=\Theta_{4}(t,s)
be undetermined and by martingale representation theorem,

\Theta_{4}(t,s)=\mathbb{E}_{r}[\Theta_{4}(t,s)]+\int_{r}^{s}\Lambda_{4}(t,s,\theta)dW(\theta),\qquad\tau\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant t\leqslant T.

Then, (
4.8
) reads

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,r)^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)X_{1}(r)\big]\\
\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Big(b_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)+\sigma_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Lambda_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})\Big){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta^{\prime})\\
\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Big(\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)b_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})+\Lambda_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})\sigma_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})\Big)X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})\\
\displaystyle\qquad+X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\sigma_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta^{\prime})\Big]d\theta^{\prime}+o(\varepsilon)\\
\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau}^{r}\Big[X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})^{\top}F_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime}){\cal X}_{1}(r,\theta^{\prime})+{\cal X}_{1}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\widetilde{F}_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})\\
\displaystyle\qquad+{1\over 2}X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})^{\top}G_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})X_{1}(\theta^{\prime})+\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)\delta\sigma^{\varepsilon}(r,\theta^{\prime})\Big]d\theta^{\prime}+o(\varepsilon),\end{array}

where (note that
\tau\leqslant\theta^{\prime}\leqslant r\leqslant\theta\leqslant T
)

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle F_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})=b_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)+\sigma_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Lambda_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime}),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})=b_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)^{\top}+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Lambda_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})^{\top},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle G_{4}(\theta,r,\theta^{\prime})=\sigma_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)\sigma_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})+\sigma_{x}(r,\theta^{\prime})^{\top}\Theta_{4}(\theta,r)^{\top}\sigma_{x}(\theta,\theta^{\prime}).\end{array}\right.

(4.22)

F(r,t)=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[b_{x}(T,t)^{\top}\negthinspace P_{2}(r)^{\top}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace\sigma_{x}(T,t)^{\top}\negthinspace Q_{2}(r,t)^{\top}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace b_{x}(r,t)^{\top}\negthinspace P_{3}(r)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\sigma_{x}(r,t)^{\top}Q_{3}(r,t)\Big].

(5.2)

The following gives the wellposedness for (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.1

Let
b_{x}(\cdot\,,\cdot),\sigma_{x}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
be bounded and
F(\cdot\,,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta_{*}[\tau,T]))
. Then (
5.1
) admits a unique solution
P(\cdot\,,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta_{*}[\tau,T]))
. In addition, for any
\rho\in[\tau,T]
, the following estimate holds:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}|P(r,t)|^{2}dtdr\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}|F(r,t)|^{2}dtdr,\end{array}

(5.3)

where
K
only depends on
\|b_{x}\|_{\infty}
,
\|\sigma_{x}\|_{\infty}
and
T
, but not on
\rho
.

Proof.
Let
\rho\in[\tau,T]
be fixed. For any
p(\cdot\,,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta_{*}[\rho,T]))
, we look at the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P(r,t)=F(r,t)+\int_{r}^{T}\big[b_{x}(s,t)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}p(s,r)^{\top}+\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{\top}q(s,r,t)^{\top}\big]ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{r}\big[b_{x}(s,t)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}p(r,s)+\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{\top}q(r,s,t)\big]ds,\ \ r\geqslant t,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}p(r,t)=p(r,t)-\int_{\theta}^{t}q(r,t,s)dW(s),\qquad\rho\leqslant\theta\leqslant t\leqslant r\leqslant T.\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

By the first equality in (
5.4
), we have (for any
\beta>0
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|P(r,t)|^{2}dtdr\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|F(r,t)|^{2}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+K\big\|b_{x}(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}\big|p(r,s)\big|ds\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|p(s,r)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big\}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad+K\big\|\sigma_{x}(\cdot\,,\cdot)\big\|^{2}_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}\big|q(r,s,t)\big|ds\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|q(s,r,t)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big\}dtdr.\end{array}

(5.5)

We now estimate the right-hand side of (
5.5
). For the second term, by Fubini theorem, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}\big|p(r,s)\big|ds\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|p(s,r)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big\}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}e^{-\beta s}ds\Big)\Big(\int_{t}^{r}e^{\beta s}\big|p(r,s)\big|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{-\beta s}ds\Big)\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{\beta s}\big|p(s,r)\big|^{2}ds\Big)\Big\}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\int_{t}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}{e^{-\beta t}-e^{-\beta r}\over\beta}e^{\beta s}|p(r,s)|^{2}dsdtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\int_{r}^{T}e^{\beta(r+t)}{e^{-\beta r}-e^{-\beta T}\over\beta}e^{\beta s}|p(s,r)|^{2}dsdtdr\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle={1\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\int_{\rho}^{s}\Big(e^{\beta(r+s)}-e^{\beta(t+s)}\Big)|p(r,s)|^{2}dtdsdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{1\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{r}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\Big(e^{\beta(t+s)}-e^{\beta(r+s+t-T)}\Big)|p(s,r)|^{2}dtdsdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle={1\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\int_{\rho}^{s}\Big(e^{\beta(r+s)}-e^{\beta(t+s)}\Big)|p(r,s)|^{2}dtdsdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{1\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{s}\int_{\rho}^{r}\Big(e^{\beta(t+s)}-e^{\beta(r+s+t-T)}\Big)|p(s,r)|^{2}dtdrds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant{2T\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+s)}|p(r,s)|^{2}dsdr.\end{array}

For the third term on the right-hand side of (
5.5
), similarly, by Fubini theorem,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}\big|q(r,s,t)\big|ds\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|q(s,r,t)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big\}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}e^{-\beta s}ds\Big)\Big(\int_{t}^{r}e^{\beta s}\big|q(r,s,t)\big|^{2}ds\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{-\beta s}ds\Big)\Big(\int_{r}^{T}e^{\beta s}\big|q(s,r,t)\big|^{2}ds\Big)\Big\}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle={1\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\int_{\rho}^{s}\Big(e^{\beta(r+s)}-e^{\beta(t+s)}\Big)|q(r,s,t)|^{2}dtdsdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+{1\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{s}\int_{\rho}^{r}\Big(e^{\beta(t+s)}-e^{\beta(r+s+t-T)}\Big)|q(s,r,t)|^{2}dtdrds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant{2\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}\int_{\rho}^{s}e^{\beta(r+s)}\big|q(r,s,t)\big|^{2}dtdsdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle={2\over\beta}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+s)}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{\rho}^{s}q(r,s,t)dW(t)\Big|^{2}dsdr\leqslant{2\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+s)}|p(r,s)|^{2}dsdr.\end{array}

To sum up the above arguments, with any
\rho\in[\tau,T]
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|P(r,t)|^{2}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|F(r,t)|^{2}dtdr+\frac{K}{\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|p(r,t)|^{2}dtdr<\infty.\end{array}

(5.6)

Hence

P(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta_{*}[\rho,T]))
, and one can define a map
\Xi
from

L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta_{*}[\rho,T]))
to itself as
\Xi(p)=P
.

Suppose
\bar{p}
,
\widetilde{p}
are two elements in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(\Delta_{*}[\rho,T]))
, and
(\bar{q},\widetilde{q})
is defined similarly as the second equality in (
5.4
). By the previous arguments, we have
\bar{P}=\Xi(\bar{p}),

\widetilde{P}=\Xi(\widetilde{p})
, and define
\bar{Q}
,
\widetilde{Q}
accordingly. Also let

\widehat{p}=p-\widetilde{p},\quad\widehat{q}=q-\widetilde{q},\quad\widehat{P}=P-\widetilde{P},\quad\widehat{Q}=Q-\widetilde{Q}.

Then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\widehat{P}(r,t)=\int_{r}^{T}\big[b_{x}(s,t)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}\widehat{p}(s,r)^{\top}+\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{\top}\widehat{q}(s,r,t)^{\top}\big]ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{r}\big[b_{x}(s,t)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}\widehat{p}(r,s)+\sigma_{x}(s,t)^{\top}\widehat{q}(r,s,t)\big]ds,\ \ r\geqslant t,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\widehat{p}(r,t)=\widehat{p}(r,t)-\int_{\theta}^{t}\widehat{q}(r,t,s)dW(s),\ \ \rho\leqslant\theta\leqslant t\leqslant r\leqslant T.\end{array}\right.

(5.7)

By the first equality in (
5.7
), for any
\rho\in[\tau,T]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|\widehat{P}(r,t)|^{2}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}\big|\widehat{p}(r,s)\big|ds\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|\widehat{p}(s,r)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big\}dtdr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+K\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}\Big\{\Big(\int_{t}^{r}\big|\widehat{q}(r,s,t)\big|ds\Big)^{2}+\Big(\int_{r}^{T}\big|\widehat{q}(s,r,t)\big|ds\Big)^{2}\Big\}dtdr.\end{array}

(5.8)

Similar as the estimation of (

), we have
for any
\rho\in[\tau,T]
,

\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|\widehat{P}(r,t)|^{2}dtdr\leqslant{K\over\beta}\mathbb{E}\int_{\rho}^{T}\int_{\rho}^{r}e^{\beta(r+t)}|\widehat{p}(r,t)|^{2}dtdr.

Therefore, by choosing

on

, as well as the above conclusion (
5.3
).

\displaystyle\leqslant\negthinspace K\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\negthinspace\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{k}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace\Big(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[1+|\bar{X}(s)|\big]ds\Big)^{k}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace+\Big(\negthinspace\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{4}ds\Big)^{k\over 2}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace\Big(\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{k\over 2}\Big]\\[8.53581pt]
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\leqslant\negthinspace\negthinspace K\mathbb{E}\Big[\negthinspace\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2k}ds\negthinspace+\negthinspace\varepsilon^{k}\negthinspace\negthinspace+\negthinspace\varepsilon^{k-1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}ds\negthinspace+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{2k}ds\negthinspace+\negthinspace\varepsilon^{k-2\over 2}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\varepsilon}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}ds\Big]\negthinspace\leqslant\negthinspace\negthinspace K\varepsilon^{k}.\end{array}

This proves the forth relation in (
3.6
). Next, we need to have a further expansion for the coefficients of the state equation. For
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, we observe

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\widetilde{b}_{x}^{i}(t,s)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}b^{i}_{x}\big(t,s,\bar{X}(s)+\alpha[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)],u^{\varepsilon}(s)\big)d\alpha\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(b^{i}_{x}\big(t,s,\bar{X}(s)+\alpha[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)],u^{\varepsilon}(s)\big)-b^{i}_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),u^{\varepsilon}(s))\Big)d\alpha\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+b^{i}_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s))+\big[b^{i}_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),u)-b^{i}_{x}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s))\big]{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\alpha b^{i}_{xx}\big(t,s,\bar{X}(s)+\alpha\beta[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)],u^{\varepsilon}(s)\big)d\beta d\alpha\Big)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\qquad+b_{x}^{i}(t,s)+\delta b^{i}_{x}(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=\Big[{1\over 2}b^{i}_{xx}(t,s)+{1\over 2}\delta b^{i}_{xx}(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+\Delta^{2}_{\varepsilon}b^{i}_{xx}(t,s)\Big][X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\qquad+b_{x}^{i}(t,s)+\delta b^{i}_{x}(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s),\end{array}

where
b_{xx}^{i}(t,s)
and
\delta b_{x}(t,s)
are as in (
7.1
), and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\Delta^{2}_{\varepsilon}b^{i}_{xx}(t,s)\negthinspace=\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{1}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\alpha\Big[b^{i}_{xx}\big(t,s,\bar{X}(s)\negthinspace+\negthinspace\alpha\beta[X^{\varepsilon}(s)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\bar{X}(s)],u^{\varepsilon}(s)\big)\negthinspace-\negthinspace b^{i}_{xx}(t,s,\bar{X}(s),u^{\varepsilon}(s))\Big]d\beta d\alpha.\end{array}

Combining the above, we see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle b(t,s,X^{\varepsilon}(s),u^{\varepsilon}(s))-b(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+{1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}+\delta b(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\delta b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+{1\over 2}\delta b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\equiv b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+{1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}+\delta b(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+R_{\varepsilon}^{b}(t,s),\end{array}

(7.9)

where

b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{\top}b^{1}_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]\\
\vdots\\
[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{\top}b^{n}_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]\end{pmatrix}.

The terms
\delta b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}
and
\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}
are defined similarly, and we have denoted

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}^{b}(t,s)=\Big(\delta b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+{1\over 2}\delta b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}.\end{array}

(7.10)

Likewise, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\sigma(t,s,X^{\varepsilon}(s),u^{\varepsilon}(s))-\sigma(t,s,\bar{X}(s),\bar{u}(s))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+{1\over 2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\delta\sigma(t,s)+\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s),\end{array}

(7.11)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)={1\over 2}\delta\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2},\end{array}

(7.12)

with
\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}
and so on being similarly defined as above. Therefore, we may rewrite (
7.6
) as follows

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)\\
\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)]+{1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\delta b(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+R_{\varepsilon}^{b}(t,s)\Big)ds\\
\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)]+{1\over 2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\negthinspace+\negthinspace R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Big)dW(s).\end{array}

(7.13)

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Big[b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+{1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)\Big([X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}\Big)+R^{b}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Big]ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)]+{1\over 2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)\Big([X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)]{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Big]dW(s).\end{array}

Consequently, by the stability estimate of FSVIEs, we have (for
2\leqslant k\leqslant{p\over 2}
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X(t)-\bar{X}(t)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{k}\\
\displaystyle\leqslant K\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\Big|{1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)\big([X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}\big)+R^{b}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Big|ds\Big)^{k}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\Big|{1\over 2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)\big([X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}\big)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)]{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\Big|^{2}ds\Big)^{k\over 2}\Big]\\
\displaystyle\leqslant\negthinspace K\negthinspace\negthinspace\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\negthinspace\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big(|X^{\varepsilon}(s)\negthinspace-\negthinspace\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)+X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)|^{k}+|R^{b}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}\Big)ds\Big]\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Big[\int_{0}^{t}\Big(|X(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)+X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)|^{k}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)|^{k}{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+|R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}\Big)ds\Big]\Big\}\\
\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big\{\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big[|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}\Big(|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{k}+|X^{\varepsilon}_{1}(s)|^{k}\Big)\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)|^{k}\\
\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{t\in]0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|R^{b}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}ds+\sup_{t\in]0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}ds\Big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big\{\varepsilon^{3k\over 2}+\varepsilon^{k+1}+\sup_{t\in]0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|R^{b}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}ds+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}ds\Big\}.\end{array}

(7.14)

For the last two terms on the right-hand side of above, we note that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|R^{b}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\Big|\Big(\delta b_{x}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]+{1\over 2}\delta b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}b_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\Big|^{k}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{k}+|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2k}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\Delta^{2}_{\varepsilon}b_{xx}(t,s)|^{k}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2k}ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[\varepsilon^{k+2\over 2}+\varepsilon^{k}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[K\land\rho\big(|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|\big)\big]^{2k}\Big)^{1\over 2}ds\Big]=K\big[\varepsilon^{k+2\over 2}+o(\varepsilon^{k})\big].\end{array}

In the above,
\rho(\cdot)
is the modulus of continiuity for the map
x\mapsto b_{xx}(t,s,x,u)
. Similarly,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|R^{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}(t,s)|^{k}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\Big|\Big({1\over 2}\delta\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)+\Delta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)[X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)]^{2}\Big|^{k}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2k}\Big){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)ds+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\Delta^{2}_{\varepsilon}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)|^{k}|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|^{2k}ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leqslant K\Big[\varepsilon^{k+1}+\varepsilon^{k}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[K\land\rho\big(|X^{\varepsilon}(s)-\bar{X}(s)|\big)\big]^{2k}\Big)^{1\over 2}ds\Big]=K\big[\varepsilon^{k+1}+o(\varepsilon^{k})\big]=o(\varepsilon^{k}).\end{array}

Hence,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X(t)-\bar{X}(t)-X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)-X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{k}\leqslant K\big[o(\varepsilon^{k})+\varepsilon^{k+2\over 2}\big].

(7.15)

For
k=2
, the above gives the last relation in (
3.6
). It also shows that for
k>2
, the above might not gives
o(\varepsilon^{k})
.

Step 4. Variation of the cost functional.

We now look at the variation of the cost functional. Note that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t)+X_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)\\

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\Big({1\over 2}b_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}+\delta b(t,s){\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\Big)ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{0}^{t}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\Big({1\over 2}\sigma_{xx}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)^{2}+\big[\delta\sigma(t,s)+\delta\sigma_{x}(t,s)X_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)\big]{\bf 1}_{[\tau,\tau+\varepsilon]}(s)\Big)dW(s),\quad t\in[0,T].\end{array}

8
Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we have developed a spike variation technique for optimal controls of FSVIEs. The main contribution is the derivation of the second-order adjoint equation which is a little different from a standard BSVIE. Thus the well-posedness of such a BSVIE is a part of novelty. We have used a trick used in the derivation of Riccatti equation in (stochastic) linear-quadratic optimal control, together with the introduction of the auxiliary process so that the Itô’s formula can be used.

As an extension, we have seen that for multi-person dynamic games of FSVIEs, one can obtain the corresponding maximum principle for the Nash equilibria. It is natural to ask what happens when
N\to\infty
? Under certain structure conditions, this will be related to the mean-field dynamic games of FSVIEs. The relevant results will be reported in our forthcoming work in the near future.

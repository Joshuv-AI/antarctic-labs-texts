Abstract
. The time asymptotic stability for one-dimensional relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations is studied. We show that the composite waves of viscous shock and rarefaction are asymptotically nonlinear stable with both small wave strength and small initial perturbations. Moreover, as the relaxation parameter goes to zero, the solutions of relaxed system are shown to converge globally in time to that of classical system. The methods are based on relative entropy, the a a -contraction with shifts theory and basic energy estimates. Keywords : Relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations; asymptotic stability; relaxation limit; composite waves; relative entropy; energy estimates



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the system of one-dimensional isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations with Maxwell’s constitutive relations. The system can be described as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+(\rho u^{2})_{x}+p(\rho)_{x}=\Pi_{x},\\
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)(\Pi_{t}+u\Pi_{x})+\Pi=\mu u_{x},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\rho
,
u
,
\Pi
denote fluid density, velocity and stress, respectively.
\mu>0
is the viscosity constant.
The pressure
p
is assumed to satisfy the usual
\gamma
-law,
p(\rho)=A\rho^{\gamma}
where
\gamma>1
denotes the adiabatic index and
A
is any positive constant.
Without loss of generality, we assume
A=1
in the sequel.

The constitutive relation
\eqref{1.1}_{3}
was proposed by Maxwell in
[
27
]
, in order to describe the relation of stress tensor and velocity gradient for a non-simple fluid. The relaxation parameter
\tilde{\tau}=\tilde{\tau}(\rho)
describe the time lag in response of the stress tensor to velocity gradient. We note that even in simple fluid, water for example, the
time lag
exists, but it is very small ranging from 1 ps to 1 ns, see
[
26
,
32
]
. However, Pelton et al.
[
30
]
showed that such a
time lag
cannot be neglected, even for simple fluids, in the experiments of high-frequency (20GHZ) vibration of nano-scale mechanical devices immersed in water-glycerol mixtures. It was shown that, see also
[
2
]
, equation
\eqref{eq1}_{3}
provides a general formalism with which to characterize the fluid-structure interaction of nano-scale mechanical devies vibrating in simple fluids.

For simplicity, we assume
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)=\tau\rho
with
\tau
being a positive constant. Therefore, the equation
\eqref{1.1}_{3}
reduces to

\displaystyle\tau\rho(\Pi_{t}+u\Pi_{x})+\Pi=\mu u_{x}.

(1.2)

The constitutive equation (
1.2
) was proposed firstly by Freistühler
[
5
,
6
]
from the point of view of conservation laws. Indeed, under the above assumption, the equation (
1.2
) is conservative by use of mass equation
\eqref{1.1}_{1}
and thus weak solutions are easily to defined. For convenience, we rewrite the system (
1.1
) with
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)=\tau\rho
in Lagrangian coordinates as follows:

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p_{x}=\Pi_{x},\\
\tau\Pi_{t}+v\Pi=\mu u_{x},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
v=\frac{1}{\rho}
denotes the specific volume per unit mass.

We are interested in the Cauchy problem to system (
1.3
) for the functions

\displaystyle(v,u,\Pi):[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

with initial conditions

\displaystyle(v,u,\Pi)(0,x)=(v_{0},u_{0},\Pi_{0})(x)\rightarrow(v_{\pm},u_{\pm},0)\quad(x\rightarrow\pm\infty).

(1.4)

The large-time behavior of solutions to system (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) is closely
related to the Riemann problem of the associated
p
-system

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0,\end{cases}

(1.5)

with the Riemann initial data:

(v,u)(t=0,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad x<0,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad x>0.\end{cases}

(1.6)

If
\tau=0
, the system (
1.3
) reduces to classical compressible isentropic Navier-Stokes equations:

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\mu\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x}.\end{cases}

(1.7)

For system (
1.7
), the asymptotic behavior of solutions with various initial data have been widely studied, see
[
7
,
13
,
14
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. For shock profile initial data, Matsumura and Nishihara
[
21
]
, Goodman
[
8
]
first established the stability of the traveling wave under the condition that the initial disturbance is suitably small and of zero constant component by using anti-derivative method. Using energy methods, Matsumura and Nishihara
[
22
,
23
]
established the stability of rarefaction waves. Note that the standard anti-derivative methods, used to study the stability of viscous shocks, are not compatible to the energy method used for the stability of rarefactions. Recently, M.-J. Kang, A. F. Vasseur, Y. Wang
[
18
]
showed that the composite waves of viscous shock and rarefaction is still stable by using the method of relative entropy and the
a
-contraction with shifts theory, which was first initiated by Bresch and Desjardins in
[
1
]
and was developed by
[
16
,
20
]
. The aim of this paper is to extend this result to the case of relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.3
).

One should note that it is not obvious that the results which hold for the classical systems also hold for the relaxed system. Indeed, and for example, Hu and Wang
[
9
]
and Hu, Racke and Wang
[
10
]
showed that, solutions may blow up in finite time with some large initial data for the relaxed system; while ,for classical compressible Navier-Stokes system, solutions were shown to exist globally with arbitrary large initial data (away from vacuum) , see
[
19
]
. A similar qualitative change was observed before for certain thermoelastic systems, where the non-relaxed system is exponentially stable, while the relaxed one is not, see Quintanilla and Racke resp. Fernández Sare and Muñoz Rivera
[
31
,
3
]
for plates, and Fernández Sare and Racke
[
4
]
for Timoshenko beams.

For the case of relaxed compressible Navier-Stoeks equations, the time-asymptotic stability of single viscous shock wave or composite of two rarefaction waves have already been studied. For
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)
being a constant, Hu-Wang
[
11
]
and Hu-Wang
[
12
]
established the linear stability of the viscous shock wave and nonlinear stability of rarefaction waves, respectively. For
\tilde{\tau}=\tau\rho
, by checking Majda’s condition on the Lopatinski determinant and Zumbrun’s Evans function condition
[
24
,
25
,
29
]
, Freistühler
[
5
]
get the nonlinear stability of the viscous shock waves for system (
1.1
) with shock profile initial data.

Motivated by the methods used in
[
18
]
, we studied the time asymptotic stability for composite waves of the superposition of a viscous shock and a rarefaction to system (
1.3
)-(
1.4
). Note that the B-D entropy used in
[
18
]
, which is essential to prove the
a
-contraction property, is not available for the studied system (
1.3
). Meanwhile, the dissipation structure of relaxed system (
1.3
) is much weaker than that of classical system (
1.5
), thus energy estimates may have new challenges. Therefore, new methods and ideas should be developed to overcome such difficulties. Here are our strategies. Instead of using B-D entropy, by using of the special hyperbolic structure of the relaxed system and the relative entropy quantities with weight function and shifts, we first established the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
estimates of
(v-\widetilde{v},u-\widetilde{u},S-\widetilde{S})
and weighted
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
estimates of
(v-\widetilde{v},S-\widetilde{S})
(see Lemma
4.1
). Unlike that in
[
18
]
, the dissipation estimates of the derivative of
(v-\widetilde{v})
can not obtained for system (
1.3
) due to the essentially different structure of the two system. Indeed, the change of variables based on B-D entropy is not available for the relaxed system and thus the
a
-contraction property could be violated. However, we can recover the estimates of derivative of
(v-\widetilde{v})
by carefully doing higher energy estimates. We remark that, compared to the estimates given in
[
18
]
, the
H^{2}
estimates of the solutions are needed to close the energy.

Our main theorem are stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the relaxation parameter
\tau
satisfy

\tau\leq\min\{\inf\limits_{\widetilde{v}^{S}\in[v_{m},v_{+}]}\frac{\mu}{|\sigma^{2}+p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}^{S})|},1\}.

For a given constant state
(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,
there exist constants
\delta_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the following holds true.

For any
(v_{m},u_{m})\in S_{2}(v_{+},u_{+})
and
(v_{-},u_{-})\in R_{1}(v_{m},u_{m})
such that

|v_{+}-v_{m}|+|v_{m}-v_{-}|\leq\delta_{0}.

Denote
(v^{r},u^{r})(\frac{x}{t})
the 1-rarefaction solution to (
1.5
) with end states
(v_{-},u_{-})
and
(v_{m},u_{m})
, and
(\widetilde{v}^{S},\widetilde{u}^{S},\widetilde{S}^{S})(x-\sigma t)
the 2-viscous shock solution (defined in (
2.3
)) with end states
(v_{m},u_{m},0)
and
(v_{+},u_{+},0)
.
Let
(v_{0},u_{0},S_{0})
be any initial data such that

\sum\limits_{\pm}\left(\|(v_{0}-v_{\pm},u_{0}-u_{\pm})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|((v_{0})_{x},(u_{0})_{x})\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\sqrt{\tau}\|\Pi_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\varepsilon_{0},

(1.8)

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=-\mathbb{R}_{-}=(0,+\infty)
.

Then, the initial value problem (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) has a unique global-in-time solution (v,u,S). Moreover, there exist an absolutely continuous shift

(defined in (

)) such that

\displaystyle v(t,x)-\left(v^{r}(\frac{x}{t})+\widetilde{v}^{S}(x-\sigma t-X(t))-v_{m}\right)\in C(0,+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

(1.9)

\displaystyle u(t,x)-\left(u^{r}(\frac{x}{t})+\widetilde{u}^{S}(x-\sigma t-X(t))-u_{m}\right)\in C(0,+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle\Pi(t,x)-\left(\widetilde{\Pi}^{S}(x-\sigma t-X(t))+\mu\frac{\left(u^{r}(\frac{x}{t})\right)_{x}}{v^{r}(\frac{x}{t})}\right)\in C(0,+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})).

In addition, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\Big|v(t,x)-\left(v^{r}(x/t)+\widetilde{v}^{S}(x-\sigma t-X(t))-v_{m}\right),

(1.10)

\displaystyle u(t,x)-\left(u^{r}(x/t)+\widetilde{u}^{S}(x-\sigma t-X(t))-u_{m}\right),

\displaystyle\Pi(t,x)-\left(\widetilde{\Pi}^{S}(x-\sigma t-X(t))+\mu\frac{\left(u^{r}(\frac{x}{t})\right)_{x}}{v^{r}(\frac{x}{t})}\right)\Big|\rightarrow 0,

and

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}|\dot{X}(t)|=0.

(1.11)

Remark 1.1
.

If we let

, the results in Theorem
1.1
holds true in the case of a single viscous shock. Therefore, we provide a different method to show the nonlinear stability of viscous shocks compare to that in

]

Remark 1.2

According to (

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{{t}\rightarrow{+\infty}}\frac{X(t)}{t}=0.

This shows that the shift function

The notation
R_{1}(v_{m},u_{m})
and

denotes the usual 1-rarefaction wave solution and 2-shock wave solution of Riemann problem for

-system (
1.5

Furthermore, based on the uniform estimates of error terms, we have the following convergence theorem.

Let

2.1.
Relative quantities

We first introduce some relative quantities. For any function
F
defined on
R^{+}
, we define the associated relative quantity for

F(v|w)=F(v)-F(w)-F^{\prime}(w)(v-w).

For the pressure function

, and the potential energy

[
17
,
18
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For given constants
\gamma>1
, and
v_{-}>0
, there exist constants
C,\delta_{\ast}>0
, such that the following holds true.

1)

For any
v,w
such that
0<w<2v_{-},0<v<3v_{-}
,

|v-w|^{2}\leq CH(v|w),

|v-w|^{2}\leq Cp(v|w).

2)

For any
v,w>v_{-}/2

|p(v)-p(w)|\leq C|v-w|.

3)

For any
0<\delta<\delta_{\ast}
, and for any
(v,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
satisfying
|p(v)-p(w)|<\delta
, and
|p(w)-p(v_{-})|<\delta
, the following holds true:

p(v|w)\leq\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma}\frac{1}{p(w)}+c\delta\right)|p(v)-p(w)|^{2},

H(v|w)\geq\frac{p(w)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}|p(v)-p(w)|^{2}-\frac{1+\gamma}{3\gamma^{2}}p(w)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-2}(p(v)-p(w))^{3},

H(v|w)\leq\left(\frac{p(w)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}-1}}{2\gamma}+c\delta\right)|p(v)-p(w)|^{2}.

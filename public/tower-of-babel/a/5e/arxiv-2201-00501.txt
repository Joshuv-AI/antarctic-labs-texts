1.
Introduction

For positive integers
a,c
and
n
with
\gcd(a,c)=1
, define
P_{a,c}(n):=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(ak^{2}+c)
. The question of determining whether the sequence
\{P_{a,c}(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
contains infinitely many squares has been extensively studied. In 2008, Amdeberhan, Medina, and Moll
[
1
]
conjectured that
P_{1,1}(n)
is not a square for
n\geq 4
and
P_{4,1}(n)
is not a square for
n\geq 1
. In addition, they computationally verified that
P_{1,1}(n)
is not a square for
n\leq 10^{3200}
. Shortly, Cilleruelo
[
3
]
proved the conjecture for
P_{1,1}(n)
and showed that
P_{1,1}(n)
is square only for
n=3
. Fang
[
4
]
settled the conjecture for
P_{4,1}(n)
. Yang, Togbé, and He
[
9
]
found all positive integer solutions to the equation
P_{a,c}(n)=y^{l}
for coprime integers
a,c\in\{1,\ldots,20\}
and
l\geq 2
. Yin, Tan, and Luo
[
10
]
obtained the
p
-adic valuation of
P_{1,21}(n)
for all primes
p
and showed that
P_{1,21}(n)
is not a square for all
n\geq 1
. Chen, Wang and Hu
[
2
]
proved that
P_{1,23}(n)
is a square only for
n=3
.

Ho
[
6
]
studied the sequence
\{P_{1,m^{2}}(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
for
m\geq 1
and proved that if
m
has divisors of the form
4q+1
and
N=\max\{m,10^{8}\}
, then
P_{1,m^{2}}(n)
is not a square for all
n\geq N
. Recently, Zhang and Niu
[
11
]
generalized Ho’s result by showing that there is a positive integer
N_{q}
depending on
q
such that
P_{1,q}(n)
is not a square for all
n\geq N_{q}
. Motivated by their work, we consider the problem of finding squares of the form
P_{2,l}(n)
for a positive odd integer
l
. In this paper, we apply Cilleruelo’s technique to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
l
be a positive odd integer. Then there exists a positive integer
N_{l}
depending on
l
such that for all
n\geq N_{l}
,
P_{2,l}(n)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(2k^{2}+l)
is not a square.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we show that for certain values of
l
, there are finitely many squares of the form
P_{2,l}(n)
for some integer
n\geq 1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

P_{2,1}(n)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(2k^{2}+1)
is not a square for all
n\geq 1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

P_{2,3}(n)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(2k^{2}+3)
is not a square for all
n\geq 1
.

Corollary 1.4
.

P_{2,7}(n)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}(2k^{2}+7)
is a square only for
n=1
.

We organize the paper as follows. In Section 2, we present several preliminary lemmas needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In Sections 3 and 4, we prove Theorem
1.1
by working on two separate cases:
l\geq 3
and
l=1
. In Sections 5, 6 and 7, we prove Corollaries
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.4
using Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we list some preliminary results that are used in the proofs of our main theorem. Throughout this section, we denote a prime by
p
and a positive odd integer by
l
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n>\sqrt{l/2}
be such that
P_{2,l}(n)
is a square and let
p
be a prime divisor of
P_{2,l}(n)
. Then
p<2n
.

Proof.

Since
P_{2,l}(n)
is a square and
p\mid P_{2,l}(n)
, we have
p^{2}\mid P_{2,l}(n)
. We consider two cases:

(1)

Suppose
p^{2}\mid 2k^{2}+l
for some
1\leq k\leq n
. Then
p\leq\sqrt{2k^{2}+l}\leq\sqrt{2n^{2}+l}<2n
since
l<2n^{2}
.

(2)

Suppose
p\mid 2k^{2}+l
and
p\mid 2l^{2}+1
for all
1\leq k<l\leq n
. Then
p\mid 2(l^{2}-k^{2})=2(l-k)(l+k)
. Since
l
is odd,
P_{2,l}(n)
is odd, so
p\nmid 2
. Thus, we see that
either
p\mid l-k
or
p\mid l+k
, which implies that
p\leq\max\{l-k,l+k\}<2n
.

∎

The above lemma implies that if
n>\sqrt{l/2}
, then
P_{2,l}(n)=\prod_{p<2n}p^{\alpha_{p}}
, where
p
is odd and

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}=\sum_{j\leq\log(2k^{2}+l)/\log p}\#\{1\leq k\leq n:p^{j}\mid 2k^{2}+l\}.

In particular, we have
\alpha_{2}=0
. Now, observe that for
1\leq k\leq n
,

2k^{2}+l=k^{\log(2k^{2}+l)/\log k}>k^{\log(2n^{2}+l)/\log n}

where the last inequality follows from the fact that for
l\geq 1
, the function
f_{l}(x):=\log(2x^{2}+l)/\log x
is decreasing on
(1,+\infty)
. Setting
\lambda=\log(2n^{2}+l)/\log n
, we have

P_{2,l}(n)>(n!)^{\lambda}
and writing
n!=\prod_{p\leq n}p^{\beta_{p}}
, where

(2)

\displaystyle\beta_{p}=\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}\#\{1\leq k\leq n:p^{j}\mid k\}=\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rfloor,

we deduce that

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq n}\beta_{p}\log p\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{p<2n}\alpha_{p}\log p.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime with
p\nmid l
and let
j>0
. Then the congruence
2x^{2}\equiv-l\pmod{p^{j}}
has
1+\left(\frac{-2l}{p}\right)
solutions.

Proof.

This follows from rewriting the congruence as
(2x)^{2}\equiv-2l\pmod{p^{j}}
and applying
[
7
, Thm. 5.1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime with
p\nmid l
.

(1)

If
\left(\frac{-2l}{p}\right)=-1
, then
\alpha_{p}=0
.

(2)

If
\left(\frac{-2l}{p}\right)=1
, then
\frac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}-\beta_{p}\leq\frac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{\log p}
.

Proof.

(1)

If
\left(\frac{-2l}{p}\right)=-1
, then by Lemma
2.2
, the congruence
2x^{2}+l\equiv 0\pmod{p^{j}}
has no solutions for
j>0
. Thus,
p^{j}\nmid 2k^{2}+l
for all integers
j,k\geq 1
and
\alpha_{p}=0
.

(2)

If
\left(\frac{-2l}{p}\right)=1
, then by Lemma
2.2
, the congruence
2x^{2}+l\equiv 0\pmod{p^{j}}
has at most two solutions in an interval of length
p^{j}
for each
j>0
. From (
1
), we deduce that

(4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}\leq\sum_{j\leq\log(2k^{2}+l)/\log p}2\left\lceil\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rceil\leq\sum_{j\leq\log(2k^{2}+l)/\log p}\lambda\left\lceil\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rceil

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}-\beta_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}\left(\left\lceil\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rfloor\right)+\sum_{\log n/\log p<j\leq\log(2n^{2}+l)/\log p}\left\lceil\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}1+\sum_{\log n/\log p<j\leq\log(2n^{2}+l)/\log p}1=\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{\log p}.

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
p^{s}
is the largest power of an odd prime
p
dividing
l\geq 3
. Then
\alpha_{p}\leq\gamma_{l,p}(n)
, where

\displaystyle\gamma_{l,p}(n)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n}{2}\left(\dfrac{3p-2-p^{-s}}{(p-1)^{2}}-\dfrac{s+3}{p^{s}(p-1)}+\dfrac{4}{p^{s/2}(p-1)}\right)-\dfrac{2np^{s/2}}{(p-1)(2n^{2}+l)}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{s(s+5)}{4}+2p^{s/2}\left(\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{\log p}-s\right).

Proof.

Write
l=p^{s}q
, where
p\nmid q
. We consider the number of solutions to the congruence
2x^{2}\equiv-p^{s}q\pmod{p^{j}}
with
j>0
, which is equivalent to
(2x)^{2}\equiv-2p^{s}q\pmod{p^{j}}
. We work on two cases:

(1)

Suppose
s\geq j
. Then
2x\equiv p^{\lceil j/2\rceil},p^{\lceil j/2\rceil+1},\ldots,p^{j}\pmod{p^{j}}
, so there are exactly
j-\lceil j/2\rceil+1
solutions in this case.

(2)

Suppose
s<j
. Then
(2x)^{2}=mp^{j}-2p^{s}q=p^{s}(mp^{j-s}-2q)
for some positive integer
m
. Since
p
does not divide
mp^{j-s}-2q
, we see that
s
is even. Write
2x=p^{s/2}r
, where
x\in\{0,\ldots,p^{j}-1\}
and
r\in\{0,\ldots,p^{j-s/2}-1\}
. The congruence now becomes
r^{2}\equiv-2q\pmod{p^{j-s}}
, and in view of Lemma
2.2
, it has at most two solutions contained in each interval of length
p^{j-s}
. Thus, the congruence has at most
2p^{j-s/2}/p^{j-s}=2p^{s/2}
solutions contained in each interval of length
p^{j}
.

Combining these cases, we see that

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq s}\left(j-\left\lceil\dfrac{j}{2}\right\rceil+1\right)\left\lceil\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rceil+\sum_{s<j\leq\log(2n^{2}+l)/\log p}2p^{s/2}\left\lceil\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq s}\left(j-\left\lceil\dfrac{j}{2}\right\rceil+1\right)\left(\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}+1\right)+\sum_{s<j\leq\log(2n^{2}+l)/\log p}2p^{s/2}\left(\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq s}\left(\dfrac{j}{2}+1\right)\left(\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}+1\right)+2p^{s/2}\left(\dfrac{n(\frac{1}{p^{s+1}}-\frac{1}{p(2n^{2}+l)})}{1-\frac{1}{p}}+\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{\log p}-s\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n}{2}\sum_{j\leq s}\dfrac{j+2}{p^{j}}+\dfrac{s(s+5)}{4}+\dfrac{2n}{p^{s/2}(p-1)}-\dfrac{2np^{s/2}}{(p-1)(2n^{2}+l)}+2p^{s/2}\left(\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{\log p}-s\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{l,p}(n).

∎

Lemma 2.5
.

For all positive integers
n
, we have
\sum_{n<p<2n}\log p\leq n\log 4
.

Proof.

Observe that for primes
p
with
n<p<2n
,
p
appears exactly once in the prime factorization of
\binom{2n}{n}
. Thus, by binomial theorem, we get
\prod_{n<p<2n}p\leq\binom{2n}{n}\leq 4^{n}
, which is equivalent to the desired inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\pi(n)
be the number of primes at most
n
. Then for all positive integers
n
, we have
\pi(n)\leq 2\log 4\frac{n}{\log n}+\sqrt{n}
.

Proof.

From
[
5
, p. 459, (22.4.2)]
, we have

\pi(n)\leq n^{1-\lambda}+\dfrac{1}{(1-\lambda)\log n}\sum_{p\leq n}\log p

for all
n\geq 1
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Setting
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
and using
\prod_{p<n}p\leq 4^{n}
(see
[
8
, Thm. 1.4]
), the desired inequality follows.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for odd
l\geq 3

We now give the proof of Theorem
1.1
for odd
l\geq 3
as follows. Suppose
n>\sqrt{l/2}
and define the set
\mathcal{S}=\{p:p\text{ is an odd prime with }(-\frac{2l}{p})=1\}
. Then inequality (
3
) becomes

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq n}\beta_{p}\log p\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}\log p+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}\log p+\sum_{n<p<2n}\dfrac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}\log p.

Write
l=\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
, where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}
are odd primes. By Lemma
2.3
, we have

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\beta_{p}\log p\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\log(2n^{2}+l)+\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{p_{i}}\log p_{i}+\sum_{n<p<2n}\dfrac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}\log p.

Observe that if
p>n
, then
\alpha_{p}\leq\lambda
from (
4
), so applying Lemma
2.5
in (
6
) yields

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\beta_{p}\log p\leq\log(2n^{2}+l)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}1+\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{p_{i}}\log p_{i}+n\log 4.

On the other hand, if
p\leq n
, then from (
2
) we have

\displaystyle\beta_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}\left\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}\right\rfloor\geq\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}\left(\dfrac{n}{p^{j}}-1\right)\geq n\sum_{j\leq\log n/\log p}\dfrac{1}{p^{j}}-\dfrac{\log n}{\log p}

\displaystyle\geq n\left(\dfrac{1-\frac{1}{n}}{1-\frac{1}{p}}-1\right)-\frac{\log n}{\log p}=\dfrac{n-p}{p-1}-\dfrac{\log n}{\log p}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{n-1}{p-1}-\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{\log p}.

Thus, from (
7
) we get

(8)

\displaystyle(n-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}\leq\log(2n^{2}+l)\pi(n)+\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{p_{i}}\log p_{i}+n\log 4

and applying Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}\leq\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{n-1}\left(2\log 4\dfrac{n}{\log n}+\sqrt{n}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dfrac{\alpha_{p_{i}}\log p_{i}}{\lambda(n-1)}+\dfrac{n\log 4}{n-1}.

Finally, using Lemma
2.4
, we arrive at

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\notin\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}\leq\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+l)}{n-1}\left(2\log 4\dfrac{n}{\log n}+\sqrt{n}\right)+\dfrac{\log n}{\log(2n^{2}+l)}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\dfrac{\gamma_{l,p_{i}}(n)\log p_{i}}{n-1}+\dfrac{n\log 4}{n-1}.

As
n
grows sufficiently large, the right-hand side of (
10
) approaches the limit

(11)

\displaystyle 10\log 2+\dfrac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\dfrac{3p_{i}-2-p_{i}^{-e_{i}}}{(p_{i}-1)^{2}}-\dfrac{e_{i}+3}{p_{i}^{e_{i}}(p_{i}-1)}+\dfrac{4}{p_{i}^{e_{i}/2}(p_{i}-1)}\right)\log p_{i},

while the left-hand side becomes unbounded since the prime number theorem implies that

\sum_{p\leq n}\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}=\log n-\gamma+o(1)

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant (see
[
8
]
). Thus, there exists a positive integer
N_{l}
such that
\sum_{p\leq n,p\notin\mathcal{S}}(\log p)/(p-1)
is greater than (
11
) for all
n\geq N_{l}
. Hence, we conclude that
P_{2,l}(n)
is not a square for all
n\geq N_{l}
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for
l=1

We now give the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
l=1
as follows. Suppose
P_{2,1}(n)
is a square for some
n\geq 1
and let
p
be a prime divisor of
P_{2,1}(n)
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have
p<2n
. Since
p
divides
2k^{2}+1
for some
1\leq k\leq n
, we have
(-\frac{2}{p})=1
, so that
p\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}
. Thus, we have

P_{2,1}(n)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<2n\\
p\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}p^{\alpha_{p}}

and in view of
P_{2,1}(n)>(n!)^{\lambda}
, we see that

(12)

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq n}\beta_{p}\log p\leq\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<2n\\
p\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{p}\log p.

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\not\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}\beta_{p}\log p

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}\log(2n^{2}+1)+\sum_{n<p<2n}\dfrac{\alpha_{p}}{\lambda}\log p.

Note that if
p>n
, then
\alpha_{p}\leq\lambda
from (
4
) and if
p\leq n
, then

\beta_{p}\geq\dfrac{n-1}{p-1}-\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+1)}{\log p}.

Using the above bounds for
\alpha_{p}
and
\beta_{p}
and Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
to (
13
), we obtain

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\not\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}\leq\dfrac{\log(2n^{2}+1)}{n-1}\left(2\log 4\dfrac{n}{\log n}+\sqrt{n}\right)+\dfrac{n\log 4}{n-1}.

As
n
tends to infinity, the left-hand side of (
14
) becomes unbounded, while the right-hand side approaches to
10\log 2
. Since the smallest positive integer
n
for which

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq n\\
p\not\equiv 1,3\pmod{8}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}>10\log 2

is
n=706310
, we take
N_{1}:=706310
so that
P_{2,1}(n)
is not a square for
n\geq N_{1}
.

We now apply Theorem

to prove Corollary

.

We know from the previous section that

is not a square for

. Since

and
P_{2,1}(2)=27

is not a square for

Since

.

Since

is a prime and the next value of

for which

divides

is

, we see that
P_{2,1}(n)
is not a square for

.

•

Since
2\cdot 21^{2}+1=883
is a prime and the next value of

for which

divides
2k^{2}+1
is

, we see that

.

•

Since

for which

divides

.

•

Since

is a prime and the next value of

for which
131
divides
2k^{2}+3
is

, we see that
P_{2,3}(n)
is not a square for
8\leq n\leq 122
.

•

Since
2\cdot 37^{2}+3=2741

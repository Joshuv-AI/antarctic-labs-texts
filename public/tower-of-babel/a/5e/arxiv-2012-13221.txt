Abstract

We are interested in cell properties of translations in affine Weyl groups. We find out some translations in the second lowest two-sided cell of an affine Weyl group and use the translations to formulate a refinement of Jianyi Shi’s conjecture on the number of left cells in the second lowest two-sided cell. We verify the refinement for an affine Weyl group of type
\tilde{A}_{n-1}
and type
\tilde{G}_{2}
.

Keywords:
affine Weyl group, extended affine Weyl groups, two-sided cell, left cell, the second lowest two-sided cell, translations.

Introduction

Kazhdan-Lusztig cells play an important role in Kazhdan-Lusztig theory. In the theory an interesting topic is the study of cells in affine Weyl groups. Cell decomposition in affine Weyl groups has drawn much attention. But the picture is far more from clear. All translations in an affine Weyl group form a normal subgroup of the affine Weyl group and the normal subgroup has finite index in the affine Weyl group. This suggests that cell properties of translations in affine Weyl groups should be interesting. In
[
20
]
, Nanhua Xi obtained some cell properties of the translations and used the properties to prove that each left cell of an affine Weyl group has only finitely many connected components.

We are interested in cell properties of translations in affine Weyl groups. A specific question is to find out translations in a given two-sided cell and in a given left cell of an affine Weyl groups. In this paper we find out some translations in the second lowest two-sided cell of an affine Weyl group (Theorems 4.5, 4.8, 4.12, 4.14, 4.17) and use the translations to formulate a refinement of Jianyi Shi’s conjecture on the number of left cells in the second lowest two-sided cell (Conjecture 5.2). {1},\quad&{1\leq i<j=n}\end{cases};

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{n-1},\ \gamma^{\vee}_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda_{n-1},\ \frac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\rangle=\begin{cases}{1},\quad&{1\leq k\leq n-1}\\
{0},\quad&{k=n}\end{cases}.

Thus,

\displaystyle l(x_{n-1}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\langle\lambda_{n-1},\ \alpha^{\vee}_{ij}\rangle|+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\langle\lambda_{n-1},\ \beta^{\vee}_{ij}\rangle|+\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}|\langle\lambda_{n-1},\ \gamma^{\vee}_{k}\rangle|

\displaystyle=n-1+(2\cdot\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}+n-1)+n-1=n^{2}-1.

Formula (a) is proved. Formula (b) follows from
l(x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{K})=l(x_{n-1}^{-1})+l(w_{K})
.

Now we are able to show

, which is equivalent to show
l(x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{I}s_{0})=n^{2}-2+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
.

By definition,
s_{0}=t_{-\alpha_{0}}s_{\alpha_{0}}
, where
-\alpha_{0}=\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}=\lambda_{2}
is the highest short root. Thus

x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{K}s_{0}=x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{K}t_{\lambda_{2}}s_{\alpha_{0}}=x_{n-1}^{-1}t_{w_{K}(\lambda_{2})}w_{K}s_{\alpha_{0}}.

We compute
w_{K}(\lambda_{2})
. Notice that
s_{i}(\lambda_{2})=\lambda_{2}
if
i\neq 2
. Set
L=\{1,\ 3,\ \cdots,\ n-1\}
,
w_{L}
is the longest element of

. We have

w_{K}=s_{n-2}s_{n-3}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\cdots s_{3}s_{2}w_{L},\quad w_{L}(\lambda_{2})=\lambda_{2}.

Then

\displaystyle w_{K}(\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=s_{n-2}s_{n-3}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\cdots s_{3}s_{2}(\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=s_{n-2}s_{n-3}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}(\lambda_{2}-\alpha_{2}-\cdots-\alpha_{n-1})

\displaystyle=s_{n-2}s_{n-3}\cdots s_{2}s_{1}(\lambda_{2}-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-\cdots-\alpha_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}-\alpha_{1}-2\alpha_{2}-\cdots-2\alpha_{n-2}-\alpha_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\cdots+2\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{1}-2\alpha_{2}-\cdots-2\alpha_{n-2}-\alpha_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}=\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}+2\varepsilon_{n}=\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}.

Therefore, by the length formula (1) of Iwahori-Matsumoto, we have

\displaystyle\ \qquad l(x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{I}s_{0})=l(x_{n-1}^{-1}t_{\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}}w_{I}s_{\alpha_{0}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in{\Phi^{+}}\\
s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\alpha)\in{\Phi^{+}}\end{subarray}}|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in{\Phi^{+}}\\
s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\alpha)\in{\Phi^{-}}\end{subarray}}|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|.

We need to figure out the positive roots mapped by
s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}
into
\Phi^{+}
or
\Phi^{-}
respectively in detail.

We write
\alpha>0
if
\alpha
is a positive root and write
\alpha<0
if
\alpha
is a negative root.
Assume
1\leq i<j\leq n,\ 1\leq k\leq n
, it is easy to get

\displaystyle s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\alpha_{ij})

\displaystyle=s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})=s_{\alpha_{0}}(\varepsilon_{j}-\varepsilon_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{\varepsilon_{j}-\varepsilon_{i}<0},\quad&{i,\ j\not\in\{1,\ 2\}}\\
{\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{1}<0},\quad&{(i,\ j)=(1,\ 2)}\\
{\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{j}>0},\quad&{i=1,\ j>2}\\
{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{j}>0},\quad&{i=2,\ j>2}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\beta_{ij})

\displaystyle=s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j})=s_{\alpha_{0}}(\varepsilon_{n-i+1}+\varepsilon_{n-j+1})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{\varepsilon_{n-i+1}+\varepsilon_{n-j+1}>0},\quad&{i,\ j\not\in\{n-1,\ n\}}\\
{-\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}<0},\quad&{(i,\ j)=(n-1,\ n)}\\
{-\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{n-i+1}<0},\quad&{1\leq i\leq n-2,\ j=n}\\
{-\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{n-i+1}<0},\quad&{1\leq i\leq n-2,\ j=n-1}.\end{cases}

\displaystyle s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(\gamma_{k})

\displaystyle=s_{\alpha_{0}}w_{I}(2\varepsilon_{k})=s_{\alpha_{0}}(2\varepsilon_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{2\varepsilon_{n-k+1}>0},\quad&{1\leq k\leq n-2}\\
{-2\varepsilon_{2}<0},\quad&{k=n}\\
{-2\varepsilon_{1}<0},\quad&{k=n-1}.\end{cases}

Now we compute
|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle|
and

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|
in accordance with

or

{1},\quad&{j=n-1}\\
{2},\quad&{j=n}.\end{cases}

Thus, these positive roots contribute 3 to the sum in the length formula.

(2) When
\alpha=\alpha_{2j}=\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{j},\ \ j\geq 3
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle|=\begin{cases}{0},\quad&{1\leq j\leq n-2}\\
{1},\quad&{j=n-1}\\
{2},\quad&{j=n}.\end{cases}

Thus, these positive roots contribute 3 to the sum in the length formula.

(3) When
\alpha=\beta_{ij}=\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j},\ \ i,j\not\in\{n-1,\ n\}
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle|=2.

Thus, these positive roots contribute

to the sum in the length formula.

(4) When
\alpha=\gamma_{k}=2\varepsilon_{k},\ \ k\neq n-1,\ n
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle|=1.

n-2
to the sum in the length formula.

(2) When
\alpha=\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|=1.

Thus, this positive root contributes 1 to the sum in the length formula.

(3) When
\alpha=\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|=0.

Thus, this positive root contributes 0 to the sum in the length formula.

(4) When
\alpha=\beta_{in}=\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n-2
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|=1.

Thus, these positive roots contribute
n-2
to the sum in the length formula.

(5) When
\alpha=\beta_{i,n-1}=\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{n-1},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n-2
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|=2.

Thus, these positive roots contribute

to the sum in the length formula.

(6) When
\alpha=\gamma_{n}=2\varepsilon_{n}
, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|=0.

Thus, this positive root contributes 0 to the sum in the length formula.

(7) When

, we have

|\langle-\lambda_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha^{\vee}\rangle-1|=1.

Thus, this positive root contributes 1 to the sum in the length formula.

Add up all the numbers in Case 1 and Case 2, finally we get:

\displaystyle l(x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{I}s_{0})=

\displaystyle 3+3+(n-2)(n-3)+(n-2)

\displaystyle+5n-18+\frac{(n-4)(n-5)}{2}+1+0+(n-2)+2(n-2)+0+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}-2+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}=l(x_{n-1}^{-1}w_{I})-1.

which is exactly what we need, and the theorem follows.∎

Solutions in ℤ ⁡ [ i ] \mathbb{Z}[i] of A 5 + B 5 = C 5 ± 1 A^{5}+B^{5}=C^{5}\pm 1
D. FOSSE, MSc. Physics

Abstract




Introduction

In
[
1
]
, it is shown how we can derive infinitely many integral solutions of
A^{3}+B^{3}=C^{3}\pm 1
starting from Ramanujan’s identity:

{(a^{2}+7ab-9b^{2})}^{3}+{(2a^{2}-4ab+12b^{2})}^{3}={(2a^{2}+10b^{2})}^{3}+{(a^{2}-9ab-b^{2})}^{3}

We will do something similar but for the equation
A^{5}+B^{5}=C^{5}\pm 1
; the price to pay will be that the proposed solutions are Gaussian integers and not natural integers. With
i^{2}=-1
, we consider:

g(x):=(x^{2}+2ax-2a^{2})^{5}+(ix^{2}-2ax+2ia^{2})^{5}

One gets directly
g(x)=g(-x)
so
g
is even and this means:

(x^{2}+2ax-2a^{2})^{5}+(ix^{2}-2ax+2ia^{2})^{5}=(x^{2}-2ax-2a^{2})^{5}+(ix^{2}+2ax+2ia^{2})^{5}

(1)

With
(a,x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, one gets Gaussian integers solutions of
A^{5}+B^{5}=C^{5}+D^{5}
which is a good starting point.

Using classical linear recurrence relations

Let’s consider the following linear recurrence,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
:

F_{n+2}=-2F_{n+1}+2F_{n}\qquad F_{0}:=0,\,F_{1}:=1

(2)

This can be solved using classical linear algebra:

F_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\left(\left(-1+\sqrt{3}\right)^{n}-\left(-1-\sqrt{3}\right)^{n}\right)\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

(3)

From this, we compute by direct calculation:

\displaystyle F_{n+1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{6}\left(\left(2+\sqrt{3}\right)^{n+1}+\left(2-\sqrt{3}\right)^{n+1}+2(-1)^{n}\right)

\displaystyle F_{n+1}F_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\left(\left(-1+\sqrt{3}\right)^{2n+1}+\left(-1-\sqrt{3}\right)^{2n+1}-(-2)^{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle F_{n+2}F_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n}}{6}\left(\left(2+\sqrt{3}\right)^{n+1}+\left(2-\sqrt{3}\right)^{n+1}+4(-1)^{n+1}\right)

From this, we get, in particular,

F_{n+1}^{2}-F_{n}F_{n+2}=2^{n}(-1)^{n}

(4)

Let’s take
F_{n+1}:=x
and
F_{n}:=a
and replace this in equation (
1
), one gets:

\displaystyle A_{n}:=

\displaystyle F_{n+1}^{2}-2F_{n+1}F_{n}-2F_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{3}\left((1+\sqrt{3})(2+\sqrt{3})^{n}+(1-\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})^{n}+(-1)^{n}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle B_{n}:=

\displaystyle iF_{n+1}^{2}+2F_{n+1}F_{n}+2iF_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{6}\left((-1+\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})^{n}-(1+\sqrt{3})(2+\sqrt{3})^{n}+2(-1)^{n}\right)+i\frac{2^{n}}{6}\left((3-\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})^{n}+(3+\sqrt{3})(2+\sqrt{3})^{n}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle C_{n}:=

\displaystyle iF_{n+1}^{2}-2F_{n+1}F_{n}+2iF_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{6}\left((1+\sqrt{3})(2+\sqrt{3})^{n}+(1-\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})^{n}-2(-1)^{n}\right)+i\frac{2^{n}}{6}\left((3-\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})^{n}+(3+\sqrt{3})(2+\sqrt{3})^{n}\right)

(7)

\displaystyle d_{n}:=

\displaystyle F_{n+1}^{2}+2F_{n+1}F_{n}-2F_{n}^{2}

Thus, equation (
1
) becomes
A_{n}^{5}+B_{n}^{5}=C_{n}^{5}+d_{n}^{5}
. Now,

\displaystyle d_{n}=F_{n+1}^{2}+2F_{n+1}F_{n}-2F_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{n+1}^{2}-F_{n}\left(-2F_{n+1}+2F_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=F_{n+1}^{2}-F_{n}F_{n+2}\qquad\textnormal{using equation \eqref{eq-2}}

\displaystyle=2^{n}(-1)^{n}\qquad\textnormal{using equation \eqref{eq-4}}

With
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
|z|<1
, it’s easy enough to calculate the following, from equations
(5)
,
(6)
and
(7)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{4z^{2}+1}{(2z+1)(4z^{2}-8z+1)}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{-4z}{(2z+1)(4z^{2}-8z+1)}+i\left(\frac{1-2z}{4z^{2}-8z+1}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{4z}{(2z+1)(4z^{2}-8z+1)}+i\left(\frac{1-2z}{4z^{2}-8z+1}\right)

And this satisfies
A_{n}^{5}+B_{n}^{5}=C_{n}^{5}+2^{5n}(-1)^{n}\implies\left(\frac{A_{n}}{2^{n}}\right)^{5}+\left(\frac{B_{n}}{2^{n}}\right)^{5}=\left(\frac{C_{n}}{2^{n}}\right)^{5}+(-1)^{n}
. Let’s take
x:=2z
and
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{n}:=\frac{A_{n}}{2^{n}}
,
b_{n}:=\frac{B_{n}}{2^{n}}
and
c_{n}:=\frac{C_{n}}{2^{n}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}+1}{(x+1)(x^{2}-4x+1)}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}b_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2x}{(x+1)(x^{2}-4x+1)}+i\left(\frac{1-x}{x^{2}-4x+1}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2x}{(x+1)(x^{2}-4x+1)}+i\left(\frac{1-x}{x^{2}-4x+1}\right)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle a_{n}^{5}+b_{n}^{5}=c_{n}^{5}+(-1)^{n}

For example,

\displaystyle n:=1\implies

\displaystyle 3^{5}+(-2+3i)^{5}=(2+3i)^{5}-1

\displaystyle n:=2\implies

\displaystyle 13^{5}+(-6+11i)^{5}=(6+11i)^{5}+1

\displaystyle n:=3\implies

\displaystyle 47^{5}+(-24+41i)^{5}=(24+41i)^{5}-1

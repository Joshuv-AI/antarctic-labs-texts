Random Reshuffling with Momentum: Complexity Bounds and Last-iterate Convergence
Junwen Qiu, Bohao Ma, Andre Milzarek

Abstract
Random reshuffling with momentum ( RRM \mathrm{RRM} ) corresponds to the SGD \mathrm{SGD} optimizer with the momentum option enabled, as found in many machine learning libraries such as PyTorch and TensorFlow. Despite its widespread use, the convergence properties of RRM \mathrm{RRM} do not seem to be well understood. This work establishes new complexity bounds and asymptotic convergence guarantees for popular versions of RRM \mathrm{RRM} using stochastic heavy-ball momentum, Nesterov acceleration, and mini-batches in a general nonconvex setting. In particular, we prove that the base variant of RRM \mathrm{RRM} achieves the complexity 𝒪 ( n − 1 / 3 ( ( 1 − β n ) T ) − 2 / 3 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{-1/3}((1-\beta^{n})T)^{-2/3}) , where n n denotes the number of samples, β ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \beta\in[0,1) is a momentum parameter, and T T is the total number of epochs. On the asymptotic side, we show that every accumulation point of the iterates { x k } k \{x^{k}\}_{k} generated by RRM \mathrm{RRM} is a stationary point of the problem. For definable objectives—a broad and common class of functions including, e.g., semialgebraic, globally subanalytic, and log-exp functions—we strengthen this subsequential result to last-iterate convergence to a single stationary point. Moreover, improved asymptotic complexity bounds are presented that are based on the additional geometric properties of definable functions.

Introduction

Many minimization and learning tasks can be formulated as finite-sum problems
[
bottou2018optimization
]
, where the goal is to minimize the sum of a potentially large number of component functions:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}~f(x):=\frac{1}{n}\;{\sum}_{i=1}^{n}\,f_{i}(x).

(1)

Here, each function
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i\in[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
, is assumed to be continuously differentiable. Stochastic gradient methods leverage stochastic approximation techniques and are popular approaches for this class of finite-sum problems,
[
robbins1951stochastic
,
chung1954stochastic
,
tseng1998incremental
,
ghadimi2012optimal
,
gurbu2019
]
. Recent research has shown that the use of
without-replacement sampling schemes
in stochastic algorithms can have a positive impact on convergence and performance
[
bottou2009curiously
,
bottou2012
,
gurbu2019
,
mishchenko2020random
]
. Such sampling strategies are widely applied in practice and are commonly referred to as random reshuffling (
\mathrm{RR}
). In fact, the basic stochastic gradient descent method (
\mathrm{SGD}
) is implemented using random reshuffling when solving large-scale machine learning problems; see
[
bottou2012
,
tseng1998incremental
]
and the documentation in Scikit-learn
1
1

1

https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/sgd.html
and TensorFlow
2
,
[
tensorflow2015
]
. A popular modification of
\mathrm{RR}
is to incorporate
momentum
in each iteration
[
polyak1964some
,
nesterov1983method
,
werbos1974beyond
,
rumelhart1986learning
,
sutskever2013importance
]
. In the following, we present the core loop of random reshuffling with momentum (
\mathrm{RRM}
). A more formal introduction of the algorithm (with mini-batches) can be found in
. Let
\pi^{k}=(\pi_{1}^{k},\dots,\pi_{n}^{k})
denote a random permutation of the numbers
\{1,\dots,n\}
in the
k
-th epoch. Then, the update of
\mathrm{RRM}
is given by:

\begin{aligned} \text{$k$-th outer loop\,/}&\\
\text{\,$k$-th epoch}\end{aligned}\;\;\left[\;\begin{aligned} &\text{Set}\;y_{0}=\tilde{x}^{k}\;\;\text{and}\;\;y_{1}=x^{k}\\
&\textbf{for}\;i=1,\ldots,n\;\textbf{do}\\
&\qquad y_{i+1}=y_{i}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f_{\pi^{k}_{i}}(y_{i}+\lambda(y_{i}-y_{i-1}))+\beta(y_{i}-y_{i-1})\\[2.84526pt]
&\text{Set}\;\tilde{x}^{k+1}=y_{n}\;\;\text{and}\;\;x^{k+1}=y_{n+1}.\end{aligned}\right.

A key motivation for this work stems from the observation that
\mathrm{RRM}
is a standard optimizer included in prominent machine learning libraries such as TensorFlow
[
tensorflow2015
]
and PyTorch
[
paszke2019pytorch
]
. For example, the code snippet shown below from TensorFlow

,
\lambda=0
:

\texttt{tf.keras.optimizers.SGD(learning\_rate = 0.1, momentum = 0.9)}.

(2)

This specific variant of
\mathrm{RRM}

\mathrm{RRHB}
). By selecting
‘nesterov = True’
in (

\mathrm{RRM}

\alpha_{k}=0.1

).

1.1
Related work

Originally developed by Werbos
[
werbos1974beyond
]
and Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams
[
rumelhart1986learning
]
for neural network training,
\mathrm{RRM}
has found widespread application in solving finite-sum problems
[
rumelhart1986learning
,
sutskever2013importance
,
tseng1998incremental
,
yu2019linear
]
. However, the convergence behavior of
\mathrm{RRM}
is less understood compared to
\mathrm{RR}
and
\mathrm{SGDM}
3
3

3

\mathrm{SGDM}
refers to the stochastic momentum variant(s) of
\mathrm{SGD}
, where the stochastic gradient information is generated via
with-replacement
sampling schemes. In contrast to
\mathrm{RR}
-type methods (and
\mathrm{RRM}
), such stochastic gradients are then unbiased estimators of the full gradient.
. The challenges arise from two main aspects. Firstly, unlike
\mathrm{SGD}
, the stochastic gradients in
\mathrm{RRM}
follow a
without-replacement
sampling strategy and are not unbiased estimators of the true gradient. Secondly, the momentum steps in
\mathrm{RRM}
tend to accumulate the stochastic errors from previous iterations, thus complicating the overall analysis; we refer to
for more detailed discussions.

Despite these challenges, there has been some important progress in understanding the convergence properties of
\mathrm{RRM}
. In
[
tseng1998incremental
]
, Tseng studied an incremental gradient method with heavy-ball momentum (
\mathrm{IGM}
) which can be seen as a deterministic analogue of
\mathrm{RRHB}
. Tseng showed that accumulation points of a subsequence of iterates generated by
\mathrm{IGM}
(equipped with an adaptive descent mechanism) are stationary points if
\beta<{0.5}^{1/n}
. Tran et al.
[
tran2021smg
, Theorem 3]
derived the following complexity result for
\mathrm{RRHB}
under a bounded gradient assumption:

{\min}_{k=1,\ldots,T}\;\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}((1-\beta^{n})^{-1}[T^{-2/3}+\beta^{n}]),

(3)

if the same (
shuffle-once
) permutation
\pi^{k}\equiv\pi
is used for all
k
. This complexity bound requires the momentum parameter
\beta
to be sufficiently small to achieve a desired accuracy and it does
not
tend to
0
if
T\to\infty
. To our knowledge, the bound (
3
) seems to be the only available complexity result for
\mathrm{RRM}
; other existing bounds only apply to modified variants of
\mathrm{RRM}

[
tran2021smg
,
tran2022nesterov
]
.
In
[
tran2021smg
]
, Tran et al. proposed a shuffling momentum-type gradient method (
\mathrm{SMG}
) that achieves the improved complexity
\min_{k=1,\ldots,T}\;\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|^{2}]=\mathcal{O}((1-\beta)^{-1}n^{-1/3}T^{-2/3})
. Except for the additional factor
(1-\beta)^{-1}
, this bound exactly matches the iteration complexity of
\mathrm{RR}
, cf.
[
mishchenko2020random
]
and Table
.

\mathrm{SMG}
is a modified version of
\mathrm{RRHB}
that employs a fixed momentum term throughout each outer loop (epoch). This update differs from conventional momentum methods used in practice. Specifically, the momentum component in
\mathrm{SMG}
operates at the epoch level rather than the (inner) iterate level, and it is computed as the averaged gradient of all component functions evaluated during the preceding epoch. In the convex setting, Tran et al.
[
tran2022nesterov
]
established convergence guarantees for the function value sequence for a variant of
\mathrm{RRNAG}
with analogous structural modifications. Very recently, Liang and Xu
[
JMLR:v26:24-1243
]
have analyzed the last-iterate convergence properties of
\mathrm{SMG}
. Specifically, they establish last-iterate convergence,
x^{k}\to x^{*}
, of
\mathrm{SMG}
under the assumption that the objective function is coercive and satisfies a variant of the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality. In addition, explicit convergence rates are shown under the stronger Łojasiewicz inequality. The work
[
JMLR:v26:24-1243
]
extends the theoretical analysis of
\mathrm{RR}
developed in
[
li2023convergence
]
to the shuffling momentum-type method
\mathrm{SMG}
.

Finally, in a recent study, Josz and Lai
[
josz2023global
]
established stability guarantees for
\mathrm{RRM}
in a general nonsmooth setting if the objective function is tame and coercive. Josz and Lai proved that the trajectories of
\mathrm{RRM}
can be approximated by subgradient trajectories and the iterates will eventually remain within a neighborhood of the set of stationary points.

Strictly ergodic distal models and a new approach to the Host-Kra factors
Yonatan Gutman, Zhengxing Lian

Abstract
Cocycles are a key object in Antolín Camarena and Szegedy’s theory of nilspaces. We introduce variants of these cocycles, named nilcycles , enabling us to give conditions which guarantee that an ergodic group extension of a strictly ergodic distal system admits a strictly ergodic distal topological model. In particular we show that if the base space is a dynamical nilspace then a dynamical nilspace topological model may be chosen for the extension. This approach combined with a structure theorem of Gutman, Manners and Varjú applied to the ergodic group extensions between successive Host-Kra characteristic factors gives a new proof that these factors are inverse limit of nilsystems for ℤ k \mathbb{Z}^{k} -actions.

3.1
Continuous measure disintegration (CMD) maps

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\pi:B\rightarrow C
be a
continuous and surjective map between two compact metric spaces. A
collection (system) of Borel probability measures on
B
,
\mu_{\pi}^{\bullet}=\{\mu_{\pi}^{c}\}_{c\in C}
,
is called a
continuous system of measures for
\pi

if

1.

For every
c\in C
,
\mu_{\pi}^{c}
is supported in
\pi^{-1}(c)
.

2.

For every continuous function
f:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
the function

\mu_{\pi}^{(\cdot)}(f):C\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by
\mu_{\pi}^{c}(f):=\int_{B}f(b)d\mu_{\pi}^{c}(b)

is continuous; i.e. the map
C\rightarrow\mathcal{M}(B)
:
c\rightarrow\mu_{\pi}^{c}

is continuous.

Suppose in addition that
\{\mu_{\pi}^{c}\}_{c\in C}
is a measure disintegration of some measure
\mu
on
B
(see Theorem
2.3
), then we say that
\pi:(B,\mu)\rightarrow(C,\pi_{*}\mu)
is a
continuous measure disintegration (CMD) map
.

Remark 3.12
.

Note that
\pi:(B,\mu)\rightarrow(C,\pi_{*}\mu)
may have more than one continuous system of measures which is a measure disintegration. However if
\mu_{\pi}^{(\cdot)}
and
\hat{\mu}_{\pi}^{(\cdot)}
are continuous systems of measures such that
\mu_{\pi}^{x}=\hat{\mu}_{\pi}^{x}
for
x\in C^{\prime}
for a dense subset
C^{\prime}\subset C
, then by continuity
\mu_{\pi}^{(\cdot)}=\hat{\mu}_{\pi}^{(\cdot)}
. In particular, if
\pi_{*}\mu
has full support, then
\pi
has only one measure disintegration which is a continuous system of measures by the a.s. uniqueness of measure disintegration.

Definition 3.13
.

[
Gla75
, Section 3]
Let
(\pi,\tilde{\pi}):(X,G)\rightarrow(Y,H)
be a factor map. A collection (system) of Borel probability measures
\{\lambda_{y}\}_{y\in Y}
on
X
is called a
continuous equivariant measure section
if

1.

The map
\lambda:Y\rightarrow\mathcal{M}(X)
given by
y\rightarrow\lambda_{y}
is continuous.

2.

For each
y\in Y
,
\lambda_{y}(\pi^{-1}(y))=1
.

3.

For each
y\in Y
and
g\in G
,
\lambda_{\tilde{\pi}(g)y}=g_{*}\lambda_{y}
.

Note that in particular it follows that
\{\lambda_{y}\}_{y\in Y}
is a continuous system of measures for
\pi
.

.

[
Gla75

Let

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\pi:(X,G)\rightarrow(Y,H)

be a distal factor map and assume
(X,G)
is uniquely ergodic. Then
(Y,H)
is uniquely ergodic.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.15
, there exists a continuous equivariant measure section
\{\lambda_{y}\}_{y\in Y}
w.r.t.
\pi
. Assume that
(Y,H)
has two possibly distinct ergodic measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
. Let
\mu
be the unique ergodic measure of
(X,G)
. From Lemma
3.14
, as
(X,G)
is uniquely ergodic,

\mu=\int_{Y}\lambda_{y}d\nu_{1}(y)=\int_{Y}\lambda_{y}d\nu_{2}(y).

For any measurable function
F:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
notice that

\int_{X}F(\pi(x))d\mu(x)=\int_{Y}\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}F(\pi(x))d\lambda_{y}(x)d\nu_{1}(y)=\int_{Y}\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}F(\pi(x))d\lambda_{y}(x)d\nu_{2}(y),

and
\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}F(\pi(x))d\lambda_{y}(x)=F(y)
. Therefore one has that
\int_{Y}F(y)d\nu_{1}(y)=\int_{Y}F(y)d\nu_{2}(y)
for any measurable function

\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}
.
∎

. Thus as
g
is
\mathcal{U}
-measurable,
there exists a function
g^{\prime}:Y^{[k]}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
for
\nu^{[k+1]}
-a.e.
(c_{1},c_{2})
,
g(c_{1},c_{2})=g^{\prime}(c_{2})
. Notice that for any
F\in\mathcal{B}(Y^{[k]})
,

\nu^{[k+1]}(D\times F)=\int_{Y^{[k+1]}}1_{D\times Y^{[k]}}1_{Y^{[k]}\times F}d\nu^{[k+1]}=\int_{Y^{[k+1]}}\mathbb{E}(1_{D\times Y^{[k]}}1_{Y^{[k]}\times F}|\mathcal{U})d\nu^{[k+1]}.

As
Y^{[k]}\times F\in\mathcal{U}
,
1_{Y^{[k]}\times F}(c_{1},c_{2})=1_{F}(c_{2})
and
(\pi_{U})_{*}\nu^{[k+1]}=\nu^{[k]}
, by Equation (
3
),

\begin{array}[]{l}\nu^{[k+1]}(D\times F)=\int_{Y^{[k+1]}}g1_{Y^{[k]}\times F}d\nu^{[k+1]}\\
=\int_{Y^{[k+1]}}(g^{\prime}1_{F})(c_{2})d\nu^{[k+1]}(c_{1},c_{2})=\int g^{\prime}1_{F}d\nu^{[k]}.\end{array}

By the definition of
\nu^{[k+1]}
and Equation (
2
) and (
3
),

\begin{array}[]{l}\nu^{[k+1]}(D\times F)=\int_{Y^{[k]}}\mathbb{E}(1_{D}|\mathcal{I}_{G}^{[k]})\mathbb{E}(1_{F}|\mathcal{I}_{G}^{[k]})d\nu^{[k]}\\

We conclude

\int_{Y^{[k]}}g^{\prime}1_{F}d\nu^{[k]}=\int_{Y^{[k]}}\mathbb{E}(1_{D}|\mathcal{I}_{G}^{[k]})1_{F}d\nu^{[k]}.

this implies

g^{\prime}(c)=\mathbb{E}(1_{D}|\mathcal{I}_{G}^{[k]})(c)\text{ for }\nu^{[k]}\text{-a.e. }c.

By a similar argument to the one applied to

above, one may find a measurable function

. It holds that

for
\nu^{[k]}
-a.e.
c
.
Now we consider

\begin{array}[]{l}\pi_{E*}\lambda(D\times F)=\int 1_{\pi_{E}^{-1}(D\times F)}d\lambda=\int 1_{D\times Y^{[k]}}(c_{1})1_{Y^{[k]}\times F}(c_{2})d\lambda(c_{1},c_{2}).\end{array}

Recall that
\lambda
is a conditional product, therefore

\begin{array}[]{l}\int 1_{D\times Y^{[k]}}1_{Y^{[k]}\times F}d\lambda=\int g^{\prime}(c)f^{\prime}(c)d\nu^{[k]}=\int\mathbb{E}(1_{D}|\mathcal{I}_{G}^{[k]})(c)\mathbb{E}(1_{F}|\mathcal{I}_{G}^{[k]})(c)d\nu^{[k]}(c)\\
=\int 1_{D}(c_{1})1_{F}(c_{2})\nu^{[k+1]}(c_{1},c_{2})=\nu^{[k+1]}(D\times F),\end{array}

which gives
\pi_{E*}\lambda(D\times F)=\nu^{[k+1]}(D\times F)
.

Cassini, d’Ocagne, and Vajda identities for n n -step Fibonacci numbers
Milan Janjić

Abstract
. Results of this paper concern n n -determinants which we defined in the paper [ 2 ] . In the paper [ 1 ] , 2 2 -determinants are considered. In this paper, we extend results from [ 1 ] on n n -determinants by proving that Cassini, d’Ocagne, Catalan and Vajda identities may be extended to hold for n n -step Fibonacci numbers.

1.
Introduction

We restate a particular case of a result proved in

[
2
]
.
Let
n
and
r
be positive integers. We consider the following

n+r-1
by
r
matrix
P
:

P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
1&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&1&\cdots&1&0\\
-1&1&\cdots&1&1\\
0&-1&\cdots&1&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&1\\
0&0&\cdots&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.

We note that in each column we have exactly
n
ones.
We connect the matrix
P
with recursively given sequence of
vector-columns in the following way: Let
A=(A_{1}|A_{2}|\ldots|A_{n})
be a square matrix
of order
n
, where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
are columns of
A.
We define a block matrix

A_{n+r}
of
n
rows and
n+r
columns in the following way: First
n
columns of

A_{n+r}
are
A_{i},(1,2,\ldots,n)
, and

(1)

A_{n+j}=\sum_{i=j}^{n+j-1}A_{i},\;(j=1,2,\ldots,r).

For a sequence
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{r}<n+r
of positive
integers we let
M=M(\widehat{j_{1}},\widehat{j_{2}},\ldots,\widehat{j_{r}})
denote the
minor of
A_{n+r}
of order
n
, obtained by deleting columns

j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r}
of
A_{n+r}.

Note that the last column of
A_{n+r}
can not be deleted.
The sign of
M
is defined as

{\rm sgn}(M)=(-1)^{nr+j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{r}+\frac{(r-1)r}{2}}.

We let
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(j_{1},\ldots,j_{r})
denote the sub-matrix of order
r
,
laying in rows
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r}
of
P.

Denote by
\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n}\}
the
numbers of columns of
B_{n+r}
in which the minor
M
lies. We have
i_{n}=r+n
, since the last column of
A_{n,n+r}
can not be deleted. We have

\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n-1}\}\cup\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r}\}=\{1,2,\ldots,n+r-1\}.

It follows that
M=M(\widehat{j_{1}},\widehat{j_{2}},\ldots,\widehat{j_{r}})=M(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{n-1},n+r).

and
{\rm sgn}(M)=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+i_{1}+i_{2}+\ldots,i_{n-1}}
.
In
[
2
]
the following result is proved:

Proposition 1
.

Let
1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r}<r+n
be a sequence of positive
integers. Then,

(2)

M({i_{1}},\ldots,i_{n-1},r+n)={\rm sgn}(M)\cdot\det Q(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r})\cdot\det A.

We stress a particular case of this result, when
i_{k}=k,(k=1,2,\ldots,n-1)
.
In this case, we have
j_{1}=n,j_{2}=n+1,\ldots,j_{r}=n+r-1
, so that the matrix
Q_{r}

has the following form

Q_{r}(n,\ldots,n+r-1)=\begin{vmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
-1&1&\cdots&1&1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&-1&\cdots&1&1&1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&-1&1&1\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&-1&1\end{vmatrix}.

For
r=1,2,\ldots
, we define
\det Q_{r}=F^{(n)}_{r}
.
It follows that

F^{(n)}_{1}=1,F^{(n)}_{2}=2,\ldots,F^{(n)}_{n}=2^{n-1},

and
r>n
we have

F^{(n)}_{r}=F^{(n)}_{r-1}+F^{(n)}_{r-2}+\cdots+F^{(n)}_{r-n}.

Hence, the sequence
\det Q_{1},\det Q_{2},\ldots
consists of Fibonacci
n
-step numbers.
It is obvious that
{\rm sgn}(M)=1
. Using equation (
2
), we obtain

Proposition 2
.

[Generalized d’Ocagne identity] The following equation holds

M(1,\ldots,n-1,r+n-1)=F^{(n)}_{r}\cdot\det A.

We see that the expression
\frac{M(1,\ldots,n-1,r+n-1)}{\det A}
does not depend on the matrix
A
with
\det A\not=0
. In other words, the following result holds:

Proposition 3
.

Assume that
A
and
B
are regular matrices of order
n
, and let
A_{n+r}
and
B_{n+r}
be formed according the same role described above.
Let
M
and
N
are corresponding minors. Then

\frac{M(1,\ldots,n-1,r+n-1)}{\det A}=\frac{N(1,\ldots,n-1,r+n-1)}{\det B}.

Our next goal is to extend Cassini identity of Fibonacci numbers on
n
-step
Fibonacci numbers.

2.
Cassini Identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers

We note that cases
n=2
and
n=3
are already considered in
[
2
]
.

For the matrix
A
, we take the following upper triangular matrix:

A=\begin{pmatrix}F^{(n)}_{1}&F^{(n)}_{2}&F^{(n)}_{3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{n-1}&F^{(n)}_{n}\\
0&F^{(n)}_{1}&F^{(n)}_{2}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{n-2}&F^{(n)}_{n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&F^{(n)}_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence,
\det A=1
. We next take
i_{k}=r+k,(i=1,2,\ldots,n-1)
. Since

Q_{r}(1,2,\ldots,r)
is a lower triangular matrix having ones on the main diagonal, we
have
\det Q_{r}=1
. Also,
M=M(r+1,r+2,\ldots,r+n)
, and
{\rm sgn}M=(-1)^{(n-1)r}
.
We thus obtain

Proposition 4

(Cassini identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers)
.

(3)

\begin{vmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{r+2}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n}\\
F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+1}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{(n-1)r}.

Remark 1
.

This result is known. It is obtained in
[
3
]
using induction.
Also a number of particular identities is given in this paper.

Corollary 1
.

In the case
n=2
, equation (
3
) becomes Cassini identity for
Fibonacci numbers

\begin{vmatrix}F_{r+1}&F_{r+2}\\
F_{r}&F_{r+1}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{r}.

Corollary 2
.

In the case
n=3
, we obtain Cassini identity for Tribonacci
numbers
T_{1}=1,T_{2}=1,T_{3}=2,\ldots
:

\begin{vmatrix}T_{r+1}&T_{r+2}&T_{r+3}\\
T_{r}&T_{r+1}&T_{r+2}\\
T_{r-1}&T_{r}&T_{r+1}\end{vmatrix}=1.

3.
d’Ocagne identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers

We next take that
A=\begin{pmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{r+2}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n}\\
F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+1}\end{pmatrix}
. For
s>0
, we form the matrix
A_{n+s}
, such that columns
n+i,(i=n+1,\ldots,s)
are
sums of preceding
n
columns. We calculate

M=M(1,2,\ldots,s-1,s+n)
. In this case, we have

{\rm sgn}(M)=(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1+2+\ldots+n-1}=1
. Using Proposition
2
, we obtain

Proposition 5

(D’Ocagne identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers)
.

The following formula holds

(4)

\begin{vmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{r+2}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-1}&F^{(n)}_{r+n+s-1}\\
F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-2}&F^{(n)}_{r+n+s-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+s}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{(n-1)r}\cdot F^{(n)}_{s}.

Example 1
.

For
n=2
, (
4
) becomes d’Ocagne identity for Fibonacci
numbers:

\begin{vmatrix}F_{r+1}&F_{r+s+1}\\
F_{r}&F_{r+s}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{r}\cdot F_{s}.

Example 2
.

For
n=3
, from (
4
) we obtain d’Ocagne identity for
Tribonacci numbers:

\begin{vmatrix}T_{r+1}&T_{r+2}&T_{r+s+2}\\
T_{r}&T_{r+1}&T_{r+s+1}\\
T_{r-1}&T_{r}&T_{r+s}\end{vmatrix}=T_{s}.

4.
Vajda identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers

In the determinant on the right hand side of the equation (
4
) sums of
elements in rows are no more
n
-step Fibonacci numbers. But sums of element in
columns are. So we consider the following matrix

(5)

B=\begin{pmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{r}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}\\
F^{(n)}_{r+2}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r-n+4}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r+n-1}&F^{(n)}_{r+n-2}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r-1}&F^{(n)}_{r}\\
F^{(n)}_{r+n+s-1}&F^{(n)}_{r+n+s-2}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+s-1}&F^{(n)}_{r+s}\end{pmatrix},

which is simple the transpose of
A
. Hence,
\det B=(-1)^{(n-1)r}\cdot F^{(n)}_{s}
.
Permuting columns
1
and
n
,
2
and
n-1
and so on, the matrix
B
becomes

(6)

C=\begin{pmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}\\
F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+4}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{r+2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-2}&F^{(n)}_{r+n-1}\\
F^{(n)}_{r+s}&F^{(n)}_{r+s+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n+s-2}&F^{(n)}_{r+n+s-1}\end{pmatrix}.

For this we need
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
permutation. It follows that

\det C=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\cdot\det B.

Applying (
4
), we obtain

Proposition 6

(Vajda identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers)
.

The following formula holds

\begin{vmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{p+r}\\
F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+4}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{p+r+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-2}&F^{(n)}_{p+r+n-2}\\
F^{(n)}_{q+r}&F^{(n)}_{q+r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{q+r+n-2}&F^{(n)}_{p+q+r+n-2}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{(n-1)r+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\cdot F^{(n)}_{p}\cdot F^{(n)}_{q}

As a particular case, for
p=q
, we obtain

Corollary 3

(Catalan identity for
n
-step Fibonacci numbers)
.

The following formula holds

\begin{vmatrix}F^{(n)}_{r-n+2}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{p+r}\\
F^{(n)}_{r-n+3}&F^{(n)}_{r-n+4}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&F^{(n)}_{p+r+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
F^{(n)}_{r}&F^{(n)}_{r+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+n-2}&F^{(n)}_{p+r+n-2}\\
F^{(n)}_{r+p}&F^{(n)}_{r+p+1}&\cdots&F^{(n)}_{r+p+s-2}&F^{(n)}_{2p+r+n-2}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{(n-1)r+\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\cdot[F^{(n)}_{p}]^{2}.

Example 3
.

In the case
n=2
, we obtain the standard Vajda and Catalan identities for Fibonacci numbers.
We have

Identity 1

(Vajda identity)
.

The following formula holds

\begin{vmatrix}F_{r}&F_{p+r}\\
F_{r+q}&F_{p+r+q}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{r+1}\cdot F_{p}\cdot F_{q}.

We also have

Identity 2

(Catalan identity)
.

The following formula holds

\begin{vmatrix}F_{r-p}&F_{r}\\
F_{r}&F_{p+r}\end{vmatrix}=(-1)^{r-p}\cdot F_{p}^{2}.

Example 4

(Tribonacci numbers)
.

In the case
n=3
, we obtain the following
identities for Tribonacci numbers
T_{1}=1,T_{2}=2,T_{3}=4,T_{5}=7,\ldots

Identity 3

(Vajda identity for Tribonacci numbers)
.

The following formula holds

\begin{vmatrix}T_{r-1}&T_{r}&T_{p+r}\\
T_{r}&T_{r+1}&T_{p+r+1}\\
T_{r+q}&T_{r+q+1}&T_{p+r+q+1}\end{vmatrix}=-T_{p}\cdot T_{q}.

For
p=q
we have

Identity 4

(Catalan identity for Tribonacci numbers)
.

We have

\begin{vmatrix}T_{r-1}&T_{r}&T_{p+r}\\
T_{r}&T_{r+1}&T_{p+r+1}\\
T_{r+p}&T_{r+p+1}&T_{2p+r+1}\end{vmatrix}=-T_{p}^{2}.

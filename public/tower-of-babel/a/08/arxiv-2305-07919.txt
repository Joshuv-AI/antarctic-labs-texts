High-dimensional monitoring and the emergence of realism via multiple observers
Alexandre C. Orthey Jr, Pedro R. Dieguez, Owidiusz Makuta, Remigiusz Augusiak

Abstract
Correlation is the basic mechanism of every measurement model, as one never accesses the measured system directly. Instead, correlations are created, codifying information about the measurable property into the environment. Here, we address the problem of the emergence of physical reality from the quantum world by introducing a model that interpolates between weak and strong non-selective measurements for qudits. By utilizing Heisenberg-Weyl operators, our model suggests that independently of the interaction intensity between the system and the environment, full information about the observable of interest can always be obtained by making the system interact with many environmental qudits, following a Quantum Darwinism (QD) framework.

Introduction

Quantum theory gives a prominent role to the notion of measurements. They are the basic ingredients in several quantum technologies such as measurement-based quantum computation
Briegel et al. 2009
;
Raussendorf et al. 2016
, thermal devices fueled by measurements

;

;
Bresque et al. 2021
;

;

;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
and on the understanding of the quantum-to-classical transition
Zurek 2003
;
Zurek 2009
. In particular, the emergence of objective reality has been investigated with the framework of QD
Zurek 2009
;
Touil et al. 2022
through the process of redundancy, where multiple copies of information about the quantum system are created in its environment, and from the closely related Spectrum Broadcast Structure
Korbicz et al. 2014
;
Horodecki et al. 2015
;
Korbicz 2021
.

Generalized measurements that can interpolate between weak and strong (projective) non-selective regimes were employed to investigate the role of measurements in the emergence of realism from the quantum substratum
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
, as quantified by the informational measure known as
quantum irrealism

Bilobran and Angelo 2015
;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
;
Mancino et al. 2018
.
The quantum irrealism measure is based on the contextual realism hypothesis introduced in
Bilobran and Angelo 2015
which generalizes the notion of EPR elements of reality
Einstein et al. 1935
by stating that for quantum systems, a measured property becomes well-defined after a projective measurement of some discrete spectrum observable,
even when one does not have access to the specific measurement result

Bilobran and Angelo 2015
;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
;
Orthey Jr. and Angelo 2022
. In other words, incoherent mixtures of all possible outcomes have realism for the measured observable. Realism was investigated employing monitoring with continuous variable measurement systems
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
, which showed to have a complementary relation with the available information of a quantum system
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
. The information-realism complementarity suggests that the establishment of realism for some observable is grounded on the encoding of information about it.

Figure 1:
A bipartite system
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
with arbitrary dimension is monitored by a collection of environmental subsystems
\{\mathcal{E}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, with each interaction represented by a unitary evolution acting on the joint system. The interaction with the environment establishes not just the realism associated with the context
\{A,\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\}
, but also the proliferation of redundant information about the system in small portions of the environment; a key process in QD. This proliferation of redundant information is associated with the emergence of objective reality as several observers will agree on their outcomes after accessing the information about the system that is encoded in the environment.

Here, we investigate the emergence of realism via monitoring modeled by discrete quantum systems with higher dimensions. We identify that a large qudit-environmental system is sufficient for the establishment of realism for all the range of measurement strengths, corroborating the results obtained with continuous variable monitoring
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
. Moreover, we introduce a consistent interpolation model for weak to strong measurements within high-dimensional systems. First, we discuss the qubit regime and address its limitations and direct generalizations. To address the qudit regime, we model the interaction between the system and the environment through
generalized observables

, described by the Fourier transform of POVMs. Our model allows for the control of the disturbance on the measured quantum system, therefore, allowing an interpolation from a weak to strong projective action
Oreshkov and Brun 2005
;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
. This interpolation regime was experimentally investigated for qubits employing a trapped-ion platform
Pan et al. 2020
, and with photonic weak measurements to investigate the information-realism complementarity
Mancino et al. 2018
. In the noiseless regime, our model reproduces the “perfect record” case from Zurek’s work
Zurek 2009

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Irrealism of observables

The quantum realism argument can be formalized as follows. Let
A=\sum_{a}aA_{a}
be a
d
-output observable acting on
\mathcal{H_{A}}
, where
A_{a}={|{a}\rangle}\!{\langle{a}|}
are projectors satisfying
\sum_{a}A_{a}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Also, let

\Phi_{A}(\varrho)=\sum_{a}(A_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}})\varrho(A_{a}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}})

(1)

be the non-selective projective measurement of the observable
A
. Let us consider a bipartite system
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
acting over
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{AB}}
and an observable
A
acting on
\mathcal{H_{A}}
. We say that
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
has a realism defined for
A

iff

\Phi_{A}(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}})=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(2)

States that already have realism defined for some observable are invariant to a non-selective (or non-revealed) projective measurement of the same observable, i.e.
\Phi_{A}(\Phi_{A}(\rho))=\Phi_{A}(\rho)
. Subsequently, the
irreality
of
A
given
\rho

\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)\coloneqq\min_{\varrho}S\big(\rho||\Phi_{A}(\varrho)\big)=S\big(\Phi_{A}(\rho)\big)-S(\rho)

(3)

proved to be a faithful quantifier of
A
-realism violations for a given state
\rho

Bilobran and Angelo 2015
;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
;
Orthey Jr. and Angelo 2022
, where
S(\rho||\sigma)=\Tr[\rho(\log_{2}{\rho}-\log_{2}{\sigma})\big]
stands for the relative entropy and
S(\rho)\coloneqq-\text{Tr}\big(\rho\log_{2}{\rho}\big)
is the von Neumann entropy. In addition, we can decompose irreality as local coherence plus non-optimized quantum discord, i.e.

\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)=\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})+D_{A}(\rho)

(4)

where
\mathcal{C}(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})=\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\Tr_{\mathcal{B}}\rho)
,
D_{A}(\rho)=I_{\mathcal{A:B}}(\rho)-I_{\mathcal{A:B}}(\Phi_{A}(\rho))
, and
I_{\mathcal{A:B}}=S(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})+S(\rho_{\mathcal{B}})-S(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}})
is the quantum mutual information. Consequently, correlations between parties
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
prevent the existence of elements of physical reality for observables on both parties, that is, any positive value for
D_{A(B)}(\rho)
implies non-null irrealism
\mathfrak{I}_{A(B)}(\rho)
.

The irrealism measure was theoretically employed to study a series of foundational problems
Gomes and Angelo 2018
;
Rudnicki 2018
;
Orthey Jr. and Angelo 2019
;
Gomes and Angelo 2019
;
Fucci and Angelo 2019
;
Freire and Angelo 2019
;
Costa and Angelo 2020
;
Lustosa et al. 2020
;
Orthey Jr. and Angelo 2022
;
Gomes et al. 2022
;
Engelbert and Angelo 2023
;
Paiva et al. 2023
;
Caetano and Angelo 2024
;
Orthey Jr and Streltsov 2024
. Moreover, some experimental reports include: a nuclear magnetic resonance experiment to probe the robustness of the wave-particle duality in a quantum-controlled interferometer
Dieguez et al. 2022
, photonic
Mancino et al. 2018
and superconducting qubits
Basso and Maziero 2022
to investigate the emergence of realism upon
monitoring maps

Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
;
Dieguez and Angelo 2018b
, and the role of distant operations in the erasure of physical reality in an optical quantum eraser experiment
Araújo et al. 2024
. Monitoring maps
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
are defined as

\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\varrho)\coloneqq(1-\epsilon)\rho+\epsilon\Phi_{A}(\varrho),

(5)

that is, they describe an interaction that produces as an output the interpolation between weak and strong non-selective measurement regimes for the measured system. Noteworthy,
Molitor et al. 2024
analyzes the implementation of monitoring maps for incompatible observables in superpositions of causal orders, employing a quantum switch influenced by an environment modeled through a collisional approach.

II.2
Information-realism complementarity

Consider the amount of quantum accessible information of a generic quantum state
\rho
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H_{S}}
with dimension
d
as

I(\rho)\coloneqq\ln{d}-S(\rho).

(6)

Following Ref.
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
, wherein a complementarity relation between information and the degree of irrealism for some discrete observable was introduced, we can check that the subsequent application of
n
monitoring maps over a state that is already a state of reality for
A
does not change the state of affairs, that is

\displaystyle[\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}]^{n}\big(\Phi_{A}(\rho)\big)

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon)^{n}\Phi_{A}(\rho)+\big[1-(1-\epsilon)^{n}\big]\Phi_{A}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{A}(\rho)=\Phi_{A}\big([\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}]^{n}(\rho)\big).

(7)

The above also proves the hierarchy of the map
\Phi_{A}
over
[\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}]^{n}
. Interestingly, this shows that the map
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}
commutes with the map
\Phi_{A}
for all intensities
\epsilon
. Employing entropy concavity and the non-negativity of the irrealism measure
\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)
, we can evaluate the difference in the irrealism given monitoring of the same observable

\displaystyle\Delta\mathfrak{I}_{A}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)-\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\rho))

\displaystyle=S\left(\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\rho)\right)-S(\rho)\geqslant\epsilon\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho),

(8)

with equality for
\epsilon=0
. Introducing the amount of remaining accessible information after some monitoring of a generic observable
A
as

\Delta I_{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq I(\rho)-I(\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\rho)),

(9)

we see that

\Delta I_{\mathcal{S}}=S\left(\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}(\rho)\right)-S(\rho)=\Delta\mathfrak{I}_{A}

(10)

which means that the irrealism of the observable
A
for the preparation
\rho
quantifies the amount of remaining accessible information that still can be extracted after a weak non-selective measurement of
A
. It is important to note that either when
\epsilon=1
or when
\lim_{n\to+\infty}[\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}]^{n}
for infinitely successive applications,
\mathfrak{I}_{A}(\rho)=\Delta I_{\mathcal{S}}
, which implies emergence of realism for this observable.

Moreover, one can prove that the information flow between the system
\mathcal{H_{S}}
and the total environment
\mathcal{H_{E}}
when we impose a global unitary dynamics that reproduces the monitoring of
A
is

\Delta\mathfrak{I}_{A}=\Delta(I_{\mathcal{S:E}}+I_{\mathcal{E}}),

(11)

where
I_{\mathcal{S:E}}(\rho)=S(\rho_{\mathcal{S}})+S(\rho_{\mathcal{E}})-S(\rho)
stands for the quantum mutual information. In other words, because the environment
\mathcal{E}
gets information about
A
, this observable increases its realism degree. In the limit of strong non-selective measurements, irrealism goes to zero as well as the accessible information about this observable.

II.3
Qubit case

Let us describe the monitoring map from the point of view of the global interaction between the system and the environment. The simplest model describing a measurement procedure is constituted by a single CNOT gate. Suppose that our system of interest
\mathcal{S}
is composed of only one arbitrary qubit in the state
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}
, but the environment
\mathcal{E}
is known to be
\rho_{\mathcal{E}}={|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}
. A CNOT gate
Nielsen and Chuang 2010

U_{\textrm{CNOT}}={|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathbbm{1}+{|{1}\rangle}\!{\langle{1}|}\otimes\sigma_{x}

(12)

keeps the qubit in the environment in the same state if the state of the system is
{|{0}\rangle}
and flips it if the state is
{|{1}\rangle}
. If the system lies in a superposition, such a superposition will be extended to the environment in the form of entanglement. Indeed, the global state
\Omega\coloneqq\rho_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}
evolves to

\Omega^{\prime}\coloneqq U\Omega U^{\dagger}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}{\langle{i}|}\rho_{\mathcal{S}}{|{j}\rangle}\ {|{i}\rangle}\!{\langle{j}|}\otimes{|{i}\rangle}\!{\langle{j}|}.

(13)

If we discard the information in the environment by tracing it out, the resulting operation over the system is a non-selective measurement in the form of

\Tr_{\mathcal{E}}\Omega^{\prime}=\sum_{i\,=\,0}^{1}{\langle{i}|}\rho_{\mathcal{S}}{|{i}\rangle}\ {|{i}\rangle}\!{\langle{i}|}=\Phi_{\sigma_{z}}(\rho_{\mathcal{S}}),

(14)

where
\sigma_{z}={|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}-{|{1}\rangle}\!{\langle{1}|}
. The map
\Phi_{\sigma_{z}}
means that a measurement in the
z
direction was realized, but the outcome was not recorded; a sufficient condition for the establishment of realism for observable
\sigma_{z}
. Since (
13
) is symmetric, we have that
\Tr_{\mathcal{S}}\Omega^{\prime}=\Tr_{\mathcal{E}}\Omega^{\prime}
, and thus the probability
p_{i}=\Tr(\ket{i}\bra{i}\rho_\mc{S})
is now encoded in the environment. In a practical situation, this is not always the case, as the CNOT gate cannot be perfectly implemented due to operational errors. Thus, only partial information regarding the observable can be retrieved; which, however, can be circumvented by interacting the system with more qubits in the environment to create redundant accessible information, following the QD framework.

One way to model a noisy CNOT gate for qubits was explored in
Touil et al. 2022
by simply replacing
\sigma_{x}
operator in
U_{\textrm{CNOT}}
with the unitary
\sigma_{\theta}
, which is defined as a linear combination
\sigma_{\theta}\coloneqq\cos\theta\sigma_{x}+\sin\theta\sigma_{z}
. In this work, we also show that by controlling the interaction intensity between the system and the single environmental qubit through the parameter
\theta
, we can interpolate between weak and strong projective non-selective measurements. In detail, a noisy
U_{\textrm{CNOT}}
with
\sigma_{\theta}
results in a monitoring map
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}
after tracing out the environment (see Appendix
A
). However, since this procedure only applies to qubit systems, it is likely to be not a good model for more complex systems. By restricting ourselves to only two dimensions we may miss certain effects which appear only for high-dimensional systems. Therefore, our motivation is to find such a model for arbitrary local dimensions. Consequently, we need to find a unitary operation acting on qudits that can be adjusted to interpolate between weak and strong non-selective measurement regimes.

II.4
Generalized observables

One way to generalize
U_{\textrm{CNOT}}
for qudits (including the noisy regime) is to replace the Pauli operators with the Heisenberg-Weyl operators

Z=\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}\omega^{k}{|{k}\rangle}\!{\langle{k}|},\qquad X=\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}{|{k+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{k}|},

(15)

where
{|{d}\rangle}\equiv{|{0}\rangle}
,
\omega=e^{2\pi\mathbbm{i}/d}
is the complex root of unity, and
d=\dim\mathcal{H}
is the dimension of the Hilbert space. It can be verified that
X^{\dagger}X=Z^{\dagger}Z=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
X^{d}=Z^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and
Z^{l}X^{k}=\omega^{lk}X^{k}Z^{l}
. Also, when
d=2
, then
Z
and
X
are the usual
\sigma_{z}
and
\sigma_{x}
Pauli matrices, respectively. Thus,
\sigma_{\theta}
becomes
\cos\theta X+\sin\theta Z\eqqcolon\overline{\sigma}_{\theta}
. Because we have
d
outcomes, we need to take the powers of
\overline{\sigma}_{\theta}
to codify the information about the outcome in different states of the environment:

U_{\textrm{noisy CNOT}}={|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}\otimes\mathbbm{1}+{|{1}\rangle}\!{\langle{1}|}\otimes\overline{\sigma}_{\theta}+{|{2}\rangle}\!{\langle{2}|}\otimes\overline{\sigma}_{\theta}^{2}+\ldots.

(16)

However, the above operator cannot always be used because it does not represent a unitary evolution in general. The reason why is because
\overline{\sigma}_{\theta}
is not unitary for
d\geqslant 3
. In fact, it is impossible to obtain a unitary operator as a linear combination of only
X
and
Z
for
d\geqslant 3

Kaniewski et al. 2019
;
Santos et al. 2022
. Nonetheless, it is known that one can always find a linear combination of operators in the form
\{XZ^{k}\}
or
\{ZX^{k}\}
for
k\,=\,0,\ldots,d-1
that results in a unitary operator
Bandyopadhyay et al. 2002
.

Although
Z
contains complex eigenvalues, it can fully characterize a valid observable. More generally, consider a
d
-outcome quantum measurement
M=\{M_{a}\}
which is defined by positive-semidefinite operators
M_{a}
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, such that
\dim\mathcal{H}=d
and
\sum_{a}M_{a}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
. The measurement
M
is simply a POVM, but if
M_{a}M_{a^{\prime}}=\delta_{aa^{\prime}}M_{a}
, then
M
is a projective measurement. For qudits, it is convenient to use generalized observables by taking a discrete Fourier transform of
M
given by

T^{(i)}=\sum_{a\,=\,0}^{d-1}\omega^{ia}M_{a}.

(17)

Immediately,
T^{(0)}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{H}}
. By performing the inverse Fourier transform on the generalized observables
T^{(i)}
, one can recover the measurement
M
with

M_{a}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i\,=\,0}^{d-1}\omega^{-ia}T^{(i)}.

(18)

Therefore, the generalized observables
T^{(i)}
fully characterize the measurement
M
. Also, the POVM
\{M_{a}\}
is a projective measurement
iff

T\coloneqq T^{(1)}
is a unitary matrix. In that case,
T
is called the
unitary observable
of
\{M_{a}\}
and
T^{(i)}=T^{i}

Kaniewski et al. 2019
.

The goal now is to find a unitary observable
T
, in replacement to
\overline{\sigma}_{\theta}
, that appropriately codifies the information regarding a given observable of interest into the environment, such that, the resulting operation over the system is a monitoring map (
5
).

III
Results

Let
\mathcal{H=H_{S}}\otimes\mathcal{H_{E}}
, where
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{E}
stand for system and environment, respectively, such that
\mathcal{H_{S}}=\mathcal{H_{A}\otimes H_{B}}
and
\mathcal{H_{E}}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
, with party
\mathcal{A}
containing one qudit and party
\mathcal{E}
containing
n
qudits (see Fig.
1
). The fragment
\mathcal{F}_{m}
of the environment
\mathcal{E}
is constituted by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}_{m}}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
, with
m\leqslant n
. For our purposes,
\dim\mathcal{H_{A}}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}\eqqcolon d
for all
i
.

We propose a noisy CNOT gate
U_{\mathcal{SE}_{i}}
that acts over
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{AE}_{i}}
, which correlates one qudit of the system with qudit
i
in the environment, and it is defined as

U_{\mathcal{SE}_{i}}\coloneqq\sum_{j\,=\,0}^{d-1}P_{j}\otimes T^{j},

(19)

where
P_{j}=A_{j}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
is the projector acting on subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{S}}
and
T
is an operator given by

T\coloneqq\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}ZX^{k},

(20)

where
\alpha_{k}
are coefficients such that
\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}=1
. For
T
to be a unitary observable (see previous section), the following conditions must hold

\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{l\,=\,0}^{d-1}\omega^{lk}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{l}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\phi_{l}=0,

(21)

where, now,
T
is specified by a set of
d-1
phases
\{\phi_{l}\}
(see Appendix
B
). Operator
T
does not depend on the choice of
A
since
A
is an arbitrary measurement and
T
is just a way to encode information about the system in the environment. We have chosen the set
\{ZX^{k}\}
instead of
\{XZ^{k}\}
because we need non-zero terms in the main diagonal of our operator.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose that every qudit in the environment is in the state
{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}
. If one chooses to use operators
\{XZ^{k}\}
, it would require to prepare every environmental qudit in a Fourier transform of
{|{0}\rangle}
in order to obtain the monitoring map. Therefore, we proceed with
\{ZX^{k}\}
. Let us begin with the interaction between the system and the first qudit in the environment given by
\rho_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}^{\prime}\coloneqq U_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}\rho_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}U_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}^{\dagger}
, where
\rho_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\otimes{|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}
. From (
19
), we get

\Tr_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\rho_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}^{\prime}=\sum_{i,j\,=\,0}^{d-1}P_{i}\>\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\>P_{j}\>{\langle{0}|}(T^{j})^{\dagger}T^{i}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}.

(22)

After some algebra (see Appendix
B
), it is possible to verify that

{\langle{0}|}(T^{j})^{\dagger}T^{i}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=0}^{[i-j]_{d}-1}\phi_{[q+m]_{d}}\right),

(23)

where
[a]_{d}\coloneqq a\mod d
, for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, if
[i-j]_{d}=0
in (
23
), then
{\langle{0}|}(T^{j})^{\dagger}T^{i}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}=1
.

A closer look at the monitoring map (
5
) allows us to write

\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon=1-\eta}(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}})=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}P_{i}\>\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\>P_{j}\left[\eta+(1-\eta)\delta_{i,j}\right],

(24)

where
\eta\in[0,1]
acts as the noise of the monitoring, i.e., the complement of the monitoring intensity. Since we require that

\Tr_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}\rho_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon=1-\eta}(\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}),

(25)

we can conclude from Eqs. (
22
) to (
25
) that

\eta=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\cos\phi_{q},

(26)

such that, the phases
\{\phi_{q}\}_{q=0}^{d-1}
must satisfy:

\displaystyle\eta\geqslant 0;

(27a)

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\sin\left(\sum_{m=0}^{p}\phi_{[q+m]_{d}}\right)=0,

(27b)

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\cos\left(\sum_{m=0}^{p}\phi_{[q+m]_{d}}\right)=\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\cos\left(\sum_{m=0}^{p^{\prime}}\phi_{[q+m]_{d}}\right),

(27c)

for every
p,p^{\prime}
satisfying
0\leqslant p<p^{\prime}\leqslant d-2
(see Appendix
B
). Although solving the above transcendental equations in full generality turns out to be difficult, it is possible to find a particular analytical solution for any
d
given by
\phi_{0}=\theta
,
\phi_{1}=-\theta
, and
\phi_{k\geqslant 2}=0
. This solution for the particular case of
d=3
gives rise to a noise intensity
\eta=(1+2\cos\theta)/3
. In order to find other and more nontrivial solutions, one can employ numerics. In fact, in our numerical exploration for dimensions
d=4,5,6,7
such solutions exist for any noise rate
\eta\in[0,1]
, which are distinct from the analytical one we just mentioned.

Operator (
19
) is indeed quite general. Because of the Stinespring dilation theorem
Kretschmann et al. 2008
;
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
, the unitary operator that provides the monitoring map
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{\epsilon}
, after the partial trace, must be unique, up to local isometries. For all practical purposes, we have
\eta>0
, since we are always going to find the presence of noise in any interaction. Therefore, even if the access to the information present in the environment by a macroscopic observer occurs through a strong projective measurement, such an act does not extract the complete information regarding the observable of interest in the system. However, in the noiseless regime when
\eta=0
, our model reproduces the perfect record case
{|{k}\rangle}_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}\to{|{k}\rangle}_{\mathcal{S}}\otimes{|{k}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}
from Zurek’s work
Zurek 2009
, as we are going to see further.

The natural question to ask now is how to codify full information about an observable (
\epsilon\to 1
) into the environmental qudits by using gate (
19
)? We are going to show that to obtain full information about the observable we need a bigger environment, i.e. more qudits. Consider the global unitary that performs the interaction between the system with the entire environment
U\coloneqq U_{\mathcal{SE}_{n}}\ldots U_{\mathcal{SE}_{1}}
. The initial global state of the system+environment is given by
\Omega\coloneqq\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}{|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}
. After the unitary evolution
\Omega^{\prime}\coloneqq U\Omega U^{\dagger}
, we can write

Immediately, from (

IV
Conclusion

Irrealism can be framed as a resource
Costa and Angelo 2020
and has an intimate connection with the concept of information
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
;
Orthey Jr. and Angelo 2022
. Monitoring is identified as a realistic operation, meaning that in general the irrealism of the context
\{A,\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\}
is destroyed by
A
-monitorings
Dieguez and Angelo 2018a
via the flow of information between the system and the auxiliary system (that we called environment). We filled a gap in such connections by providing an explicit unitary interaction between qudits to derive interpolations from weak to strong non-selective measurements. The most important conclusion of our analysis is that, for high-dimensional discrete monitoring, a state of reality for a given observable can be reached regardless of the intensity of the interaction between the system and the environment, as long as the latter is composed of a sufficiently large number of particles. In addition, from (
26
), the noise rate
\eta
is still highly dependent on parameters
\{\phi_{q}\}
even when
d\to\infty
. For instance, if
\min_{q}\cos\phi_{q}=Q
, for some
Q>0
, then
\eta>Q
.

Following the QD framework, if the system manages to disseminate redundant information about an observable to many particles in the environment, such a system will move towards a state of objectivity. In a nutshell, equation (
32
) means that objectivity from QD implies
A
-reality states from quantum realism for discrete and high-dimensional monitoring. Other connections can be made by exploring this new relation, for instance, it would be worth analyzing the volume of system solutions (
27
) for different values of
\eta
. That could allow us to determine, whether it is easier to find unitaries that lead to weak or, instead, strong monitoring maps, and how the dimension of the system influences that process. Still, another possibility for further research is the time needed to probe the system via interaction with the environment. In addition, one could also explore the continuous limit of our model and investigate the connection between quantum Darwinism and non-Markovianity similarly to what is done in
Oliveira et al. 2019
.

Appendix B
Properties of operator
T

One identity of great importance is the following:

\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}\omega^{(a-b)k}=d\delta_{a,b},

(44)

where
a
and
b
are any integers and

\delta_{a,b}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\textrm{for }a=b\mod d;\\
0&\textrm{for }a\neq b\mod d.\end{cases}

(45)

Now, consider the unitary observable
T
given by

T\coloneqq\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}ZX^{k},\qquad\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{l\,=\,0}^{d-1}\omega^{lk}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{l}},

(46)

with condition
\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\phi_{l}=0
. Let us prove three essential properties of the operator
T
.

Proposition 1
.

The operator
T
is unitary.

Proof.

To make the calculations simpler, we can apply a unitary transformation

V\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i,j}\omega^{ij}{|{i}\rangle}\!{\langle{j}|}

(47)

to
T
such that
VTV^{\dagger}\eqqcolon\overline{T}
. Indeed,
T
is unitary
iff

\overline{T}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}XZ^{-k}

(48)

is unitary.

Explicitly,

\overline{T}\overline{T}^{\dagger}=\left(\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}ZX^{k}\right)\left(\sum_{q\,=\,0}^{d-1}\alpha_{q}^{*}X^{-q}Z^{-1}\right).

(49)

By performing the products, we obtain

\overline{T}\overline{T}^{\dagger}=\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,q\,=\,0\\
k\neq q\end{subarray}}^{d-1}\alpha_{k}\alpha_{q}^{*}XZ^{q-k}X^{-1}.

(50)

Let us start by developing the first sum in the r.h.s. of the above expression by using identity (
44
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}|\alpha_{k}|^{2}&=\sum_{k\,=\,0}^{d-1}\dfrac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{k(l^{\prime}-l)}e^{\mathbbm{i}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l^{\prime}})}\\
&=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}e^{\mathbbm{i}(\phi_{l}-\phi_{l^{\prime}})}=1.\end{split}

(51)

For simplicity, let us denote
n\coloneqq q-k
and rewrite (
50
) in the following form

\overline{T}\overline{T}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}+\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{q\,=\,0}^{d-1}\alpha_{q-n}\alpha_{q}^{*}XZ^{n}X^{-1}.

(52)

The remaining sum in the r.h.s. of (
52
) will vanish. Indeed, by implementing identity (
44
) multiple times, we obtain

\sum_{q\,=\,0}^{d-1}\alpha_{q-n}\alpha_{q}^{*}=\dfrac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{q\,=\,0}^{d-1}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{q(m_{2}-m_{1})}e^{m_{1}n}e^{\mathbbm{i}(\phi_{m_{1}}-\phi_{m_{2}})}=0.

(53)

∎

Proposition 2
.

Given an integer
j
, the operator
T
satisfies

{\langle{0}|}T^{j}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=0}^{[j]_{d}-1}\phi_{[q+m]_{d}}\right),

(54)

where

represents

{\langle{0}|}T^{j}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}=1

Proof.

From definition (

Since

(56)

Now, we can suppose that
j>1
and take expected value
{\langle{0}|}T^{j}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}

(57)

The only terms that will have a contribution to

which results in

\kappa\,=\,0\end{subarray}}^{d-1}\left(\prod_{l\,=\,0}^{j-1}\omega^{-lk_{l}}\alpha_{k_{l}}\right).

(59)

By condition (
58
), we can write

{\langle{0}|}T^{j}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{j-1}\,=\,0}^{d-1}\left(\alpha_{-(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{j-1})}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\omega^{-lk_{l}}\alpha_{k_{l}}\right).

(60)

From (
46
), we have:

\alpha_{k_{l}}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{q^{\prime}k_{l}}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q^{\prime}}};

(61)

\alpha_{-(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{j-1})}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-q(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{j-1})}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q}}.

(62)

By implementing the above equations to (

\displaystyle{\langle{0}|}T^{j}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{j-1}=0}^{d-1}\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\Bigg[\omega^{-q(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{j-1})}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{k_{l}(q^{\prime}-l)}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q^{\prime}}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{d^{j}}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{j-1}=0}^{d-1}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}\left[e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\left(\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{k_{l}(q^{\prime}-q-l)}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q^{\prime}}}\right)\right].

(63)

From identity (
44
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\langle{0}|}T^{j}{|{0}\rangle}_{\mathcal{E}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\delta_{0,q^{\prime}-q-l}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q^{\prime}}},

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q}}\prod_{l=1}^{j-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{q+l}}.

(64)

After we develop the product in the above equation, we obtain the desired result

(65)

\Tr_{\mathcal{E}}\Omega^{\prime}=\sum_{i\,=\,0}^{d-1}P_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}P_{i}+\eta^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\,=\,0\\

\displaystyle\Tr_{\mathcal{E}}\Omega^{\prime}

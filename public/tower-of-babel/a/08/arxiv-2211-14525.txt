Calculus rules for proximal ε ​ - \varepsilon\text{-} subdifferentials and inexact proximity operators for weakly convex functions
Ewa Bednarczuk, Giovanni Bruccola, Gabriele Scrivanti, The Hung Tran

Abstract
We investigate proximal ε ​ - \varepsilon\text{-} subdifferentials and derive sum rules that hold for weakly convex function, by incorporating the corresponding moduli of weak convexity into the respective formulas. As an application, we analyse inexact proximity operators for weakly convex functions in terms of proximal ε ​ - \varepsilon\text{-} subdifferentials and the related notion of criticality.



KEYWORDS: weakly convex functions, criticality, proximal operator, inexactness, inexact proximal operator, sum rule for proximal
\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferentials

Introduction

Proximal operators are a fundamental tool in constructing algorithms for solving large-scale convex optimisation problems
[
10
]
. Responding to the need for solving optimisation problems with convex objectives which do not fall into the class of typical convex objective functions appearing in data analysis (see
e.g.
the webpage
[
9
]
), a number of inexact (approximate) proximal operators have been introduced, see
e.g.

[
28
,
31
,
20
]
.

It is our aim to investigate inexact proximal operators for a class of functions which is larger than the one of convex functions: in the present work, we focus on weakly convex functions, which have been appearing in current models in data science problems in a rapidly growing number.
Examples of weakly convex functions appearing in data analysis can be found in
[
6
,
11
]
and in the references therein. The growing interest in the use of this class of function in many fields of applications suggested the necessity of a careful analysis of their properties in terms of subdifferentials and proximal operators, which is the core of the present work.

Weak convexity

Weak convexity can be considered as a special case of the general notions of
\gamma
-paraconvexity and
\alpha(\cdot)
-paraconvexity that were studied by, among others, Jourani and Rolewicz
[
16
,
23
,
26
]
. For a general characterisation in Hilbert spaces, weakly convex functions can be expressed as the difference between a convex function and a quadratic function. This class includes all the convex functions and all the smooth (but not necessarily convex) functions with a Lipschitz continuous gradient, together with many other interesting non-convex functions.

Proximal Subdifferentials

It has already been observed that the concept of subdifferential which is particularly suitable when defining criticality for weakly convex functions is that of
proximal subdifferential
(see

e.g.

[
13
,
12
]
). There exists a vast literature devoted to proximal subdifferentials, see
e.g.
in the finite dimensional case, the monograph by Rockafellar and Wets
[
21
]
, in Hilbert spaces the work by Bernard and Thibault
[
5
]
. In these monographs and papers, the proximal subdifferential at a given
x_{0}
is defined locally, in the sense that
there exists a neighbourhood
V
of
x_{0}
and a constant
C\geq 0
, such that for every
x\in V
,

\langle x^{*},x-x_{0}\rangle\leq f(x)-f(x_{0})+C\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}.

(1)

In our developments we make use of a property that holds in the class of paraconvex functions, called
globalisation property
. Precisely, in the class of paraconvex functions, if inequality (
1
) holds in a neighbourhood of
x_{0}
, then it holds globally over the whole space for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
with
the same constant
C
(see Prop.
1
and Def.
3
). This property allows us to treat the proximal subdifferential as a global tool - exactly as the convex subdifferential is treated with respect to convex functions - and deduce results in analogy to the convex setting.
More on the
globalisation property
can be found in

[
24
]
.

We base our analysis on the more general notion of proximal
\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferentials, which represents a useful tool allowing to take into account inexactness and perturbations in optimisation algorithms.

Before focusing on the class of weakly convex functions, we introduce a more general notion of
{\gamma\text{-paraconvexity}}

{(\gamma,C)\text{-subdifferential}}

Definition 1

(
\gamma
-Paraconvexity
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed vector space. A function

is said to be
\gamma
-paraconvex

\gamma>0
, if there exists a positive constant

such that for
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}^{2})
, the following inequality holds:

f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)+C\lambda(1-\lambda)\|x-y\|^{\gamma}.

(2)

When
\alpha:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a non-decreasing function with

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\alpha(t)}{t}=\penalty\ 0
,
a function
{f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}
is called
\alpha(\cdot)
-paraconvex
if there exists
a constant
C>0
such that for
\lambda\in[0,1]

\displaystyle(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{X}^{2})\hskip 20.00003ptf(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)+C\min\{\lambda,1-\lambda\}\alpha(\|x-y\|)

This class has been introduced by Rolewicz under the name of

-strongly paraconvex functions and investigated in a series of papers by Jourani
[
15
,
16
]
and Rolewicz
[
23
,
26

. When

\alpha(\cdot)
-paraconvexity coincides with the one of
\gamma
-paraconvexity, (see
[
25
, Lemma 5]
). In Hilbert spaces, when

\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\alpha(t)}{t^{2}}<+\infty,

then a

-paraconvex function is a difference of a convex and a quadratic function and is called

(see
[
26
]
).

Definition 2

(
(\gamma,C)-
Subdifferential

[
16

)

Let
\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{X}

\gamma>0

(\forall x\in V)\hskip 10.00002pt\langle x^{*},x-x_{0}\rangle\leq f(x)-f(x_{0})+C\|x-x_{0}\|^{\gamma}.

(4)

The set of all
(\gamma,C)-

.

Let

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt(\forall x\in\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle x^{*}x\leq||x|-1|-1+2|x|.

Let

\displaystyle\partial_{(\gamma,C)}f(x_{0}):=\left\{x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}^{*}\,|\,\ \langle x^{*},x-x_{0}\rangle\leq\right.

\displaystyle\left.f(x)-f(x_{0})+C\|x-x_{0}\|^{\gamma}\,\forall\ x\in\mathcal{X}\right\}.

Definition 3

(Global Proximal Subdifferential)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed vector space. Let
{f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}
and let
x_{0}\in\text{dom}\,f
. Then the

of

at
x_{0}
with constant

is defined as the set

\begin{split}\partial_{(2,C)}f(x_{0}):=\{x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}^{*}\,|\,f(x)\geq f(x_{0})\\
+\langle x^{*},x-x_{0}\rangle-C\|x-x_{0}\|^{2},\;\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\}.\\
\end{split}

(7)

In view of (

When investigating inexact proximal points, the following concept of proximal

Definition 4

(Global proximal
\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferentials)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}

\begin{split}\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,C)}f(x_{0})=\{v\in\mathcal{X}\,|\,f(x)-f(x_{0})\geq\\
\langle v,x-x_{0}\rangle-C\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}-\varepsilon\ \ \forall\ x\in\mathcal{X}\}.\end{split}

(8)

Clearly, for every
\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon

\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,C)}f(x_{0})\subseteq\partial^{\,\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{(2,C^{\prime})}f(x_{0}).

(9)

For any set-valued mapping

\text{dom}\,M:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\,|\,M(x)\neq\emptyset\},

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space. Let
{f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}
be a proper lower semicontinuous and
\rho\text{-}
weakly convex function with
\rho\geq 0
. Then for every
\varepsilon\geq 0

\text{dom}\,\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}f=\text{dom}\,\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{0}(f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2})

(12)

and

\text{dom}\,\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}f\subset\text{dom}\,f.

(13)

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0

\text{dom}\,\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}f=\text{dom}\,f.

(14)

Proof.

We start by showing that for any
{x_{0}\in\text{dom}\,\partial_{0}^{\,\varepsilon}(f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2})}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we have that

\partial_{0}^{\,\varepsilon}(f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2})(x_{0})-\rho x_{0}=\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}f(x_{0}).

(15)

Indeed, for any

and

\displaystyle f(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|x\|^{2}-f(x_{0})-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x_{0}\|^{2}\geq\langle v,x-x_{0}\rangle-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\iff f(x)-f(x_{0})\geq

\displaystyle\langle v-\rho x_{0},x-x_{0}\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}-\varepsilon

which is equivalent to the fact that
{v-\rho x_{0}\in\partial_{(2,{\rho}/{2})}^{\,\varepsilon}f(x_{0})}
and proves (
15

Hence,
{\text{dom}\,\partial_{0}^{\,\varepsilon}(f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2})=\text{dom}\,\partial_{(2,{\rho}/{2})}^{\,\varepsilon}f}
.
Since
\partial_{0}^{\,\varepsilon}
corresponds to the

subdifferential for convex functions and function

Calculus rules

In the literature, there exist numerous results providing calculus rules for the Fréchet, the limiting and the proximal subdifferentials, see
e.g.

[
16
,
17
,
19
,
18
,
30

The main result of the present section is stated in Theo.

\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferentials (in the sense of Def.

The proposed result allows us to extend the sum rule in

– proved for exact proximal subdifferentials in normed spaces – to proximal

subdifferentials in Hilbert spaces: the interesting aspect of such rule is that it allows us to trace the modulus of proximal subdifferentiability.

The following notion of
\rho

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space. Let
{f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}
be a proper function. For every
\rho\geq 0
the function
(h)_{\rho}^{*}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[-\infty,+\infty]

(f)^{*}_{\rho}(u):=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{-\frac{\rho}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\langle u,y\rangle-f(y)\right\}

(25)

is called
\rho
-
conjugate
of
f
at

(when

We recall the following result, which is an important fact in view of the proof of Theo.

Theorem 1

(
[
22
, Theo. 3]
)

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space. Let

contains a point at which either

Then, for all

\begin{split}&(a)\quad\left(f_{0}+f_{1}\right)^{*}(s)=\min_{\underset{s=p_{0}+p_{1}}{p_{0},p_{1}\in\mathcal{X}}}\big\{f_{0}^{*}(p_{0})+f_{1}^{*}(p_{1})\big\}\\
&(b)\quad\partial_{0}\left(f_{0}+f_{1}\right)(x)=\partial_{0}f_{0}(x)+\partial_{0}f_{1}(x)\end{split}

(26)

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space.
For
i=0,1

{f_{i}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}

\rho_{i}

\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}

(f_{0}+f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}}(s)=(f_{0})^{*}_{\rho_{0}}(p_{0})+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}(p_{1}).

(27)

Proof.

We have that
\overline{f}_{0}(\cdot)=f_{0}(\cdot)+\rho_{0}/2\|\cdot\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overline{f}_{i}^{*}(\cdot)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\langle\cdot,y\rangle-\overline{f}_{i}(y)\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{\langle\cdot,y\rangle-f_{i}(y)-\frac{\rho_{i}}{2}\|y\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=(f_{i})_{\rho_{i}}^{*}(\cdot)

so

(\overline{f}_{0}+\overline{f}_{1})^{*}(s)=(f_{0})^{*}_{\rho_{0}}(p_{0})+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}(p_{1})

(30)

and in conclusion

({f}_{0}+{f}_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}}(s)=(f_{0})^{*}_{\rho_{0}}(p_{0})+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}(p_{1}).

(31)

, which are formulated for convex functions and convex subdifferentials. An important aspect of our result – which will be used below in the analysis of proximal operators – is that it allows us to trace the modulus of proximal subdifferentiability (as related to the modulus of weak convexity of the functions involved). Indeed, a useful consequence of this is that whenever a weakly convex function
f
is expressed as the sum of a convex function
f_{0}
and a weakly-convex function
f_{1}
, the proximal subdifferential of
f
corresponds to a (Minkowski) sum of the convex subdifferential of
f_{0}
and of the proximal subdifferential of
f_{1}
. This will be exploited in the analysis of proximal operators in Section
4

Theorem 2

(Sum Rule for
\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferential)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space.
For
i=0,1
, let function
f_{i}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper lower semicontinuous and
\rho_{i}
-weakly convex on
\mathcal{X}
with
\rho_{i}\geq 0
. Then, for all
x\in\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}
and for all
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
we have

\partial_{(2,\rho_{0}/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{0}}f_{0}(x)+\partial_{(2,\rho_{1}/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}f_{1}(x)\subseteq\partial_{(2,{\rho}/{2})}^{\,\varepsilon}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)

(32)

for all
\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}
and for all
\rho\geq\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}
.
The equality

\begin{split}&\partial_{(2,(\rho_{0}+\rho_{1})/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)=\\
&\bigcup_{\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\,|\,\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon}\partial_{(2,\rho_{0}/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{0}}f_{0}(x)+\partial_{(2,\rho_{1}/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}f_{1}(x)\end{split}

(33)

holds when
\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}
contains a point at which either
f_{0}+{\rho_{0}}/2\|\cdot\|^{2}
or
f_{1}(\cdot)+{\rho_{1}}/2\|\cdot\|^{2}
is continuous.

Proof.

For
x\in\mathcal{X}
, if
{w\in\partial_{(2,\rho_{0}/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{0}}f_{0}(x)}
,
{v\in\partial_{(2,\rho_{1}/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}f_{1}(x)}
, then it is clear that
w+v\in\partial_{(2,(\rho_{0}+\rho_{1})/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)
. Hence the inclusion (
32
) is satisfied.

To prove the equality in (
33
), let us consider
{x\in\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}}
and
{u\in\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)}
, where
{\rho=\rho_{0}+\rho_{1}}
. By
[
4
, Theo. 2.4.ii, Eq. (5)]
we have

\begin{split}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)+(f_{0}+f_{1})^{*}_{\rho}(u+\rho x)\\
\leq-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\langle u+\rho x,x\rangle+\varepsilon\end{split}

(34)

The inequality in (
34
) implies that
{u+\rho x\in\text{dom}\,(f_{0}+f_{1})^{*}_{\rho}}
. By applying Prop.
3
, there exist two elements
{p_{0},p_{1}\in\mathcal{X}}
such that
{u+\rho x=p_{0}+p_{1}}
and

(f_{0}+f_{1})^{*}_{\rho}(u+\rho x)=f^{*}_{\rho_{0}}(p_{0})+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}(p_{1})

(35)

so that (
34
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)+(f_{0})^{*}_{\rho_{0}}(p_{0})+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}(p_{1})\\
\leq-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\langle p_{0}+p_{1},x\rangle+\varepsilon\end{split}

(36)

for all
x\in\text{dom}\,f_{0}\cap\text{dom}\,f_{1}
.
We now define the following values

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}:=f_{0}(x)+(f_{0})_{\rho_{0}}^{*}(p_{0})-\langle p_{0},x\rangle+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\|x\|^{2}\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt

(37)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}:=f_{1}(x)+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}(p_{1})-\langle p_{1},x\rangle+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\|x\|^{2}\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt

(38)

which are positive in view of the definition of
\rho
-conjugate.
Notice that (
37
) and (
38
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\varepsilon_{0}=f_{0}(x)+(f_{0})^{*}_{\rho_{0}}((p_{0}-\rho_{0}x)+\rho_{0}x)\\
-\langle(p_{0}-\rho_{0}x)+\rho_{0}x,x\rangle+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\|x\|^{2}\end{split}

(39)

\begin{split}\varepsilon_{1}=f_{1}(x)+(f_{1})^{*}_{\rho_{1}}((p_{1}-\rho_{1}x)+\rho_{1}x)\\
-\langle(p_{1}-\rho_{1}x)+\rho_{1}x,x\rangle+\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\|x\|^{2}\end{split}

(40)

from which, by applying
[
4
, Theo. 2.4.ii, Eq. (5)]
in a similar fashion as in (
34
), we obtain

(p_{0}-\rho_{0}x)\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon_{0}}_{(2,\rho_{0}/2)}f_{0}(x)

(41)

(p_{1}-\rho_{1}x)\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}_{(2,\rho_{1}/2)}f_{1}(x)

(42)

which completes the proof.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space.
Let
{f_{0}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}
be proper, convex and differentiable with a
L_{0}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient on the whole space
\mathcal{X}
. Let
{f_{1}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]}
be proper and
\rho\text{-}

\mathcal{X}
with

. Then, we have the following inclusion

\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2)}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)\subset\nabla(f_{0})(x)+\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+L_{0}/2)}f_{1}(x)

(43)

for all
x\in\text{dom}\,f_{1}
such that
\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2)}f_{1}(x)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

In view of
[
3
, Lemma 2.64]
, both
f_{0}
and
-f_{0}
are proximally
L_{0}
-subdifferentiable on
\mathcal{X}
.
Precisely, for
f_{0}
we have that for every
x,y\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle(-f_{0})(y)-(-f_{0})(x)\geq

(44)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\langle\nabla(-f_{0})(x)\ |\ y-x\rangle-\frac{L_{0}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}.

(45)

Let us choose
x\in\text{dom}\,f_{1}
. If
{\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)=\emptyset}
, nothing needs to be proved. Otherwise, we take
{v\in\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon}(f_{0}+f_{1})(x)}
. For every
y\in\mathcal{X}
,
by adding

(f_{1}+f_{0})(y)-(f_{1}+f_{0})(x)\geq\langle v\ |\ y-x\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}-\varepsilon

(46)

and (
44
) we get

\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2)}(f_{1}+f_{0})(x)+\nabla(-f_{0})(x)\subset\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+L_{0}/2)}f_{1}(x)

i.e.

\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2)}(f_{1}+f_{0})(x)\subset\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+L_{0}/2)}f_{1}(x)+\nabla(f_{0})(x).

(48)

4
inexact proximal maps

In general settings, the computation of the proximal map needs to be addressed as an independent optimisation problem.
Some practical examples involves
non-convex
\ell_{p}
-seminorms (
i.e.

p\in(0,1)
) or the convex
\ell_{p}
-norms (
i.e.

p\geq 1
), unless
p
takes some specific values
[
8
]
.
Another example is given by the combination of a sparsity-promoting functions with a non-orthogonal linear operator, as in the case of the popular discrete Total Variation functional
[
27
]
(and its non-convex modifications), which has been extensively used in the context of image and signal processing. In these cases, at each point, the proximal map is defined up to a certain degree of accuracy and in the framework of proximal algorithms, it is important to carry out a convergence analysis that takes this fact into account. In order to do so, we consider the concept of
\varepsilon\text{-}
solution for an optimisation problem (see Def.
5
) and the related notion of
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal point.

Definition 8

(
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal point)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a normed vector space.
Let function
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
be proper and bounded from below and
\alpha>0
. Then for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
and for all
\varepsilon\geq 0
, any
\varepsilon\text{-}
solution to the proximal minimisation problem

\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}{x\in\mathcal{X}}\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{minimize}}\;\;f(x)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|x-y\|^{2},

(49)

is said to be a
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal point for
f
at
y
with respect to
\alpha
. The set of all
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal points of
f
at
y
with respect to
\alpha
is denoted as

\varepsilon\text{-}\text{prox}_{\alpha f}(y):=\left\{x\in\mathcal{X}\,|\,x\ \text{is\;a\;}\varepsilon\text{-solution\;of\;\eqref{eq:epsmini}}\right\}\\

(50)

In the following result, we provide a relationship between the
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal operator and the
{\varepsilon\text{-proximal}}
subdifferentials of weakly convex function, using the sum rule from Theo.
3
. Specifically, in Prop.
4
, we trace the constant
C
of the
(2,C)
-subdifferential of
f
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space.
Let
{f:\mathcal{X}\to(-\infty,+\infty]}
be a proper, lower semicontinuous
\rho\text{-}
weakly convex function that is bounded from below on
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
\alpha>0
. Then for every
y\in\mathcal{X}
,
x_{\varepsilon}\in\varepsilon\text{-prox}_{\alpha f}(y)
implies

\frac{y-x_{\varepsilon}}{\alpha}\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+1/(2\alpha))}f(x_{\varepsilon}).

(51)

Proof.

By the definition of
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal point and Remark
1
we have

\displaystyle x_{\varepsilon}\in

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\text{-prox}_{\alpha f}(y)

(52)

\displaystyle\implies 0\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2)}\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\cdot-y\|^{2}+f(\cdot)\right)(x_{\varepsilon}).

(53)

The assumptions in Theo.
3
are satisfied since
{f_{0}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\cdot-y\|^{2}}
is differentiable on the whole space and its gradient has a Lipschitz constant
L_{0}=1/\alpha
, hence we also have the inclusion

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\cdot-y\|^{2}\right)(x_{\varepsilon})+\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+1/(2\alpha))}f(x_{\varepsilon})

(54)

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{\varepsilon}-y}{\alpha}+\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+1/(2\alpha))}f(x_{\varepsilon})

(55)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{y-x_{\varepsilon}}{\alpha}\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2+1/(2\alpha))}f(x_{\varepsilon}).

(56)

∎

Remark 7

(
\varepsilon\text{-}

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Hilbert space.
Let
{f:\mathcal{X}\to(-\infty,+\infty]}
be a proper, lower semicontinuous
\rho\text{-}
weakly convex function that is bounded from below on
\mathcal{X}
and let
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
\alpha>0
.
If
x_{\varepsilon}\in\varepsilon\operatorname{-prox}_{\alpha f}(y)
, then there exist
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
with
\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon
and there exists

with
\frac{\|e\|^{2}}{2\alpha}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\frac{y-x_{\varepsilon}-e}{\alpha}\in\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}f(x_{\varepsilon}).

(58)

If
\varepsilon=0
and
1/\alpha>\rho
, we obtain the equivalence.

Proof.

By definition of
\varepsilon\text{-}
proximal point we have

\displaystyle x_{\varepsilon}\in\varepsilon\text{-prox}_{\alpha f}(y)

(59)

\displaystyle\implies 0\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon}_{(2,\rho/2)}\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\cdot-y\|^{2}+f(\cdot)\right)(x_{\varepsilon}).

(60)

We can now apply Theo.
2
, according to which there exist
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
with
\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle 0\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon_{0}}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\cdot-y\|^{2}\right)(x_{\varepsilon})+\partial^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}_{(2,\rho/2)}f(x_{\varepsilon}).

(61)

By applying Remark
7
, we infer that there exists
e\in\mathcal{X}
with
\frac{\|e\|^{2}}{2\alpha}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
such that

\frac{x_{\varepsilon}-y}{\alpha}+\frac{e}{\alpha}\in\partial^{\,\varepsilon_{0}}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|\cdot-y\|^{2}\right)(x_{\varepsilon})

(62)

which implies

\frac{y-x_{\varepsilon}-e}{\alpha}\in\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}^{\,\varepsilon_{1}}f(x_{\varepsilon}).

(63)

For the second part of the statement, we need to show that if
\frac{y-x_{0}}{\alpha}\in\partial_{(2,\rho/2)}f(x_{0})
,
then for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
the following inequality holds

\displaystyle f(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|x_{0}-y\|^{2}\leq f(x)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|x-y\|^{2}.

(64)

By definition of proximal subdifferential we have that for every

f(x)\geq f(x_{0})+\langle\frac{y-x_{0}}{\alpha},x-x_{0}\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}

Since

We use the identity

\displaystyle\left\langle y-x_{0}\,|\,x-x_{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}

(67)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|y-x_{0}\|^{2},

which follows from
[
3
, Lemma 2.12]
. In conclusion, for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
we have

f(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|y-x_{0}\|^{2}\leq f(x)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|y-x\|^{2}.

(68)

5
Conclusions

We discussed inexact proximal
operators (in the sense of Def.
8
) for weakly convex functions defined on Hilbert spaces and their relationships to proximal
\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferentials. We highlighted the main differences and similarities with the fully convex settings.
An important feature of the obtained results is that in Prop.
4
and Prop.
5
we are able to control the moduli of proximal
\varepsilon\text{-}
subdifferentiability of
f
(in relation to the moduli of weak convexity).
Our analysis provides tools to study the convergence of inexact proximal algorithms in which the proximal step is taken on a weakly convex function.

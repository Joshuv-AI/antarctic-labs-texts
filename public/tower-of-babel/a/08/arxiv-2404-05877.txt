Uniform vector-valued pointwise ergodic theorems for operators
Micky Barthmann, Sohail Farhangi

Abstract




Abstract.
We prove a uniform vector-valued Wiener-Wintner Theorem for a class of operators that includes compositions of ergodic Koopman operators with contractive multiplication operators. Our results are new even in the case of complex-valued functions, as they also apply to some non-positive non-contractive operators, and they give new uniform pointwise theorems for ergodic, weakly mixing, and mildly mixing Koopman operators.

Primary 37A25, 47A35; Secondary 28B05, 46M07.

Definition 1.4

Let

Theorem 1.5

Let

-finite measure space, let

be a Banach space, and let

, i.e., any

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\langle T^{n}f,g^{\prime}\rangle\right|=0,

for all

, we have for a.e.

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{n}f(x)\lambda^{n}\right|\right|=0.

Our method of proof is as follows. We first view the sequence
\xi:=(T^{n}f(x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
as a vector in a certain subspace of an ultraproduct that we denote by

, and the spaCb property of

with respect to a left shift operator

on

for some
g^{\prime}\in A_{\mathcal{U}}(E)^{\prime}
. In particular, if
\xi
is weakly mixing with respect to
S_{\mathcal{U}}
, and
g^{\prime}
is compact (reversible) with respect to the adjoint operator

, then
\langle\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle=0
. Lastly, we argue by contradiction that if the convergence in Equation (
11
) was not uniform with respect to
\lambda
, then we could take a sequence of counterexamples that converge to some compact (reversible) functional
g^{\prime}
satisfying
\langle\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0

2.1
Bochner spaces

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and let
E
be a Banach space. A function
s:X\rightarrow E
is called
\mu
-simple function
if
s=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\xi_{n}1_{A_{n}}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\xi_{n}\in E
and
A_{n}\in\mathscr{B}
with
\mu(A_{n})<\infty
. For a
\mu
-simple function

we define

\int_{X}sd\mu=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu(A_{n})\xi_{n}.

A function
f:X\rightarrow E
is called
strongly
\mu
-
measurable
if the exists a sequence
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
\mu
-simple functions converging to
f

\mu
-almost everywhere, which is equivalent to
f
having a separable range. Moreover a strongly
\mu
-measurable
f:X\rightarrow E
is called

if there exists a sequence
(s_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
\mu
-simple functions such as

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}\|s_{n}-f\|d\mu=0.

This gives the notion of the
Bochner integral
for a Bochner integrable
f
, defined via

\int_{X}fd\mu=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}s_{n}d\mu.

Notice that a strongly
\mu
-measurable
f:X\rightarrow E
is Bochner integrable if and only if

\int_{X}\|f\|d\mu<\infty.

Being equal
\mu
-almost everywhere gives an equivalence relation
\sim_{\mu}
on the set of all strongly
\mu
-measurable functions from
X
to
E
. For
1\leq p<\infty
the vector space
L^{p}(X,\mu;E)
is defined as set of all equivalence classes of strongly
\mu
-measurable functions

such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{E}}:=\left(\int_{X}\|f\|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Moreover the vector space
L^{\infty}(X,\mu;E)
is defined as set of all equivalence classes of strongly
\mu
-measurable functions
f:X\rightarrow E
such that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{E}}:=\esssup_{x\in X}\|f(x)\|<\infty.

For any
p\in[1,\infty]
the spaces
(L^{p}(X,\mu;E),\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}_{E}})
are complete. The following theorem is a generalization of Birkhoff’s Pointwise Ergodic Theorem for Bochner spaces.

(
[

)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(\varphi^{n}x)=f^{*}(x),

(13)

where convergence takes place pointwise in the strong topology of

.

2.3
Mixing properties of operators

Let
E
be a Banach space and
T
a bounded linear operator on
E
. We call
T

weakly almost periodic
if for any
\xi\in E
the set
\{T^{n}\xi\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is weakly pre-compact. The operator
T
is called
mean ergodic
if

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{n}\xi

converges in norm for all
\xi\in E
. The convergence induces a decomposition of the space as
E=\text{fix}(T)\oplus\overline{(I-T)E}
, where
\text{fix}(T):=\{\xi\in E\ |\ T\xi=\xi\}
. It is well known that any power bounded operator on a reflexive Banach space is weakly almost periodic, and that any weakly almost periodic operator on a Banach space is mean ergodic (see
[
12
, Chapter 8.4]
). Moreover it is worthwhile mentioning that if
T
a positive and power-bounded operator on a scalar-valued
L^{1}
-space, then mean ergodicity is equivalent to weak almost periodicity (see
[
16
]
, Theorem 3.1.11).

Another classical decomposition that is satisfied by weakly almost periodic operators is the Jacobs-deLeeuw-Glicksberg Decomposition (see
[
12
, Chapter 16.3]
), which says that
E=E_{rev}\oplus E_{aws}
, where

\displaystyle E_{rev}

\displaystyle=\overline{\text{lin}_{\mathbb{C}}}\{\xi\in E\ |\ T\xi=\lambda\xi\text{ for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\},

\displaystyle E_{aws}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in E\ |\ T^{n_{j}}\xi\overset{w}{\rightarrow}0\text{ for some }(n_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi\in E\ |\ \lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\langle T^{n}\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle|=0\ \forall\ g^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}\right\}.

The spaces
E_{rev}
and
E_{aws}
are known as the reversible part and the almost weakly stable part of
E
respectively.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
E
be a Banach space, let
\xi\in E
, and let
T
be a bounded linear operator on
E
. We call
\xi

(i)

ergodic
(with respect to
T
) if for any
g^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle T^{n}\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle=0.

(14)

(ii)

weakly mixing
(with respect to
T
) if for any
g^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\langle T^{n}\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle\right|=0.

(15)

(iii)

mildly mixing
(with respect to
T
) if for any
g^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}
we have

\mathcal{IP}^{*}-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle T^{n}\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle=0.

(16)

(iv)

strongly mixing
(with respect to
T
) if for any

we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle T^{n}\xi,g^{\prime}\rangle=0.

(17)

The operator
T
is

if every
\eta\in E
is ergodic / weakly mixing / mildly mixing / strongly mixing with respect to

.

Remark 2.8

A weakly mixing element

2.4
Strongly Pointwise absolutely Cesàro bounded (spaCb) operators

We begin with two results that will not be used later on in this paper, but they show that the spaCb property is strong enough to replace the maximal inequalities that are usually needed in the study of pointwise ergodic theorems.

Examples 2.13
.

We will now look at examples of operators with and without the (s)paCb property. In these examples
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
will be a standard probability space and
E
a reflexive Banach space. In light of Chacon’s Theorem, we will mostly focus on examples that are contractions on
L^{1}(X,\mu;E)
and
L^{\infty}(X,\mu;E)
, which we call
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
contractions.

(i)

The Koopman operator of a measure preserving transformation, or more generally, of a Markoff process, is a
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
contraction. If the measure preserving transformation is ergodic, or the Markoff process is irreducible, then the corresponding Koopman operator is spaCb as a consequence of Birkhoff’s pointwise ergodic theorem in the former case and Hopf’s pointwise ergodic theorem in the latter.

(ii)

If
f\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
satisfies
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, then multiplication operator
M_{f}(g)(x):=f(x)g(x)
is a
L^{1}(X,\mu)-L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
contraction. More generally, if
\mathcal{L}_{1}(E)
denotes the set of linear operators
T
on
E
satisfying
\|T\|\leq 1

that is strongly measurable with respect to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of the strong operator topology
4
4

4

We use this

[
24

tells us in this case that if

is strongly measurable, then

is also strongly measurable.
, the operator
M_{f}(g)(x)=f(x)(g(x))
is a

contraction. However,
M_{f}
will not be a paCb operator if the range of

consists of isometries. To see this, let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu)
be non-atomic, let

be arbitrary and take any

with

and any

and consider
g=\xi\mathbbm{1}_{A}
. We see that for any

C\int_{X}\|g\|d\mu=C\|\xi\|\mu(A)<\|\xi\|=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|g(x)\|=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|(M_{f}^{n}g)(x)\|

(iii)

A composition of

(iv)

Let

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|((M_{f}T_{\varphi})^{n}g)(x)\|\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|g(\varphi^{n}x)\|=\int_{X}\|g\|d\mu,

(v)

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
. The operator

2.5.1
Sequence spaces

We now identify some spaces that arise naturally when considering sequences of the form

with

For a given Banach space

\displaystyle\text{ces}_{\infty}(E):=\left\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}\ |\ \sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|<\infty\right\},\text{ and}

\displaystyle A(E):=\Bigg\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{ces}_{\infty}(E)\ |\ \forall\ \epsilon>0\ \exists\ (e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq E\text{ with }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|e_{n}\|<\infty\text{ and }

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}A(E):=\Big\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{ces}_{\infty}(E)\ |\ }\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}-e_{n}\|<\epsilon\Bigg\}

Then
(\text{ces}_{\infty}(E),\|\cdot\|_{c})
is a normed space with

\|(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{c}:=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|.

It is worth mentioning that the space

is just the Cesàro sequence space ces

that is discussed in

and the references therein. We also observe that the product topology on

is induced by the norm

\|(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\|_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{n}\|.

Lemma 2.15
.

The space
(\text{ces}_{\infty}(E),\|\cdot\|_{c})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

We only need to show that

in

as

. By passing to a subsequence
((x_{n,m_{k}})_{n=1}^{\infty})_{k=1}^{\infty}

for all

, while

for all

. We inductively construct

\displaystyle\|(x_{n,m_{k}}-x_{n,m_{k+1}})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{c}\geq\frac{1}{N_{k+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k+1}}\|x_{n,m_{k}}-x_{n,m_{k+1}}\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{k+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k+1}}\|x_{n,m_{k}}-z_{n}\|-\frac{1}{N_{k+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k+1}}\|z_{n}-x_{n,m_{k+1}}\|>\frac{\epsilon}{2},

which contradicts the fact that

is a Cauchy sequence.
∎

To see that

\displaystyle A(E)=\Bigg\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{ces}_{\infty}(E)\ |\ \forall\ \epsilon>0\ \exists\ (e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq E\text{ with }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|e_{n}\|<\infty\text{ and }

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}A(E):=\Big\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\text{ces}_{\infty}(E)\ |\ }\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}-e_{n}\|<\epsilon\Bigg\}.

2.5.2
Ultraproducts

The goal of this subsection is to define the space

, which is a closed subspace of an ultraproduct of Banach spaces. For a more detailed discussion of the usage of ultraproducts in Banach space theory, the reader is referred to
[

Let
(E_{n},\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a family of Banach spaces and
\mathcal{U}\in\beta\mathbb{N}^{*}

\ell^{\infty}(E_{n})=\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}:\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|_{n}<\infty\}

and its closed subspace

We call the quotient space

the

of

(with respect to
\mathcal{U}
). We denote its elements by

.
The norm on

is given by

\|[(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}]_{\mathcal{U}}^{*}\|^{*}_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{U}-\lim_{n}\|x_{n}\|_{n}.

Now let us fix a Banach space
E
and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
E_{n}:=E^{n}
be given the norm

\|(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\|_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|x_{k}\|.

(33)

We define

i:\text{ces}_{\infty}(E)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}(E_{n}),\ (x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\mapsto(x_{n}(k)_{k\leq n})_{n=1}^{\infty}

with

for all

. Since

is a linear isometry,

is a closed subspace of

. We consider the space

To see that

is naturally identified as a closed subspace of

is a well defined linear injective contraction since

The shift operator

The shift operator
S
is given by

S:A(E)\rightarrow A(E),\ (x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\mapsto(x_{n+1})_{n=1}^{\infty},

and we denote by

the induced map

on

. We first need to show that

is well defined, i.e.,
S
leaves
i(A(E))\cap N_{\mathcal{U}}
invariant. We begin by showing that for

we have
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{-1}\|x_{n}\|=0
. Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary, and let

\mathcal{U}-\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|=0.

Remark 3.6
.

Our next result is a uniform pointwise ergodic theorem for mildly mixing systems. We recall that the notion opposite to mild mixing is rigidity. If
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,\varphi)
is a measure preserving system and
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
is
\lambda
-rigid
if there exists a sequence
(k_{w})_{w=1}^{\infty}
for which
\lim_{w\rightarrow\infty}\|T_{\varphi}^{k_{w}}f-\lambda f\|_{2}=0
. It is a classical fact that if
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
is
\lambda
-rigid and
g\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
is mildly mixing, then
\langle f,g\rangle=0
. Our next theorem is a another manifestation of this fact that is uniform with respect to
\lambda
and with respect to a countable collection of rigidity sequences
\{(k_{w,i})_{w=1}^{\infty}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
.

Remark 3.8
.

While we would also like to prove a uniform pointwise ergodic theorem for strongly mixing operators, we do not do so because it is not clear what the statement of the theorem should be. To better understand this, let us recall that for a measure preserving system
\mathcal{X}:=(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,\varphi)
, the system
\mathcal{X}
is ergodic if and only if it has no nontrivial invariant factor, it is weakly mixing if and only if it has no nontrivial Kronecker factor, and it is mildly mixing if and only if it has no nontrivial rigid factor. Parreau has shown a similar result for strongly mixing systems (cf.
[
30
, Theorem 11]
or
[
36
]
), i.e., there exists a class of system
\mathcal{S}
such that the system
\mathcal{X}
is strongly mixing if and only if it does not contain a factor from the class
\mathcal{S}
. While the existence of the class
\mathcal{S}
can be used to state an abstract pointwise ergodic theorem for strongly mixing systems, a uniform pointwise ergodic theorem would require a more concrete description of some members of
\mathcal{S}
in order to apply our compactness arguments about a sequence of local counter examples converging to a global counter example. However, the class
\mathcal{S}
does not currently have a simple description.

Appendix: Comparison of terms

In this appendix we mention various notions of mixing sequences that have appeared in different parts of the literature. For the sake of presentation, we focus the discussion on variations of ergodic and weakly mixing sequences.

The notion of ergodic, weakly mixing and strongly mixing sequences of vectors in a Hilbert space was introduced by Bergelson and Berend
[
2
]
. Mukhamedov
[
34
]
then extended the defintions of ergodic sequences and weakly mixing sequences to apply to sequences in Banach spaces. It is worth noting that weakly mixing and strongly mixing sequences can only exist in an infinite dimensional Hilbert/Banach space, while our almost mixing sequences can exist in
E=\mathbb{C}
. Later, Moreira, Richter, and Robertson
[
33
, Definition 3.17]
introduced a notion of weak mixing for certain functions
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, which can also be seen as a notion of weakly mixing sequences of complex numbers, as was done by the second author in
[
17
]
. In
[
18
, Chapter 2.2]
the notion of weakly mixing functions
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
was modified to obtain a new notion of weakly mixing sequences of vectors in a Hilbert space. In order to avoid confusion with the notion of weakly mixing sequences of Berend and Bergelson, this new notion was called nearly weakly mixing, and in
[
18
, Chapter 2.6]
it is shown that every weakly mixing sequences of bounded vectors in a Hilbert space is also a nearly weakly mixing sequences, but the converse need not be true. Interestingly, the analogue of a nearly weakly mixing sequence in the case of ergodicity is called completely ergodic sequence since it is always an ergodic sequence, but the converse need not be true. Now another complication must be pointed out to the reader. The mixing sequences of
[
2
]
are bounded sequences, the nearly (completely) mixing sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
[
18
, Chapter 2]
satisfy

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|^{2}<\infty,

(46)

and the almost
p
-mixing (fully ergodic)
sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
[
18
, Chapter 3]
(which extends the work of
[
17
]
) satisfy

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|x_{n}\|<\infty.

(47)

Condition (
46
) is a natural condition to impose when working with Hilbert spaces, and condition (
47
) is a natural condition to impose when working with sequences of the form
(f(\varphi^{n}x))_{n=1}^{\infty}
with

. This necessity to work with different classes of sequences is what necessitated different definitions of mixing sequences between Chapter 2 and 3 of

]

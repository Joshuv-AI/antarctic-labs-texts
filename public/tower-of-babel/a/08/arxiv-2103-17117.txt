Generalized Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-function as a hypergeometric solution of the BKP hierarchy
Alexander Alexandrov

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that the generalized Brézin–Gross–Witten tau-function is a hypergeometric solution of the BKP hierarchy with simple weight generating function. We claim that it describes a spin version of the strictly monotone Hurwitz numbers. A family of the hypergeometric tau-functions of the BKP hierarchy, corresponding to the rational weight generating functions, is investigated. In particular, the cut-and-join operators are constructed, and the explicit description of the BKP Sato Grassmannian points is derived. Representatives of this family can be associated with interesting families of spin Hurwitz numbers including a spin version of the monotone Hurwitz numbers.

1.
Introduction

Spin Hurwitz numbers were introduced by Eskin, Okounkov, and Pandharipande
[
EOP08
]
. Analogously to the ordinary Hurwitz numbers, they count the ramified coverings, but this time of the surfaces with spin structure. This is an interesting class of enumerative geometry invariants, closely related to the fundamental structures in representation theory and mathematical physics. In particular, the relation between spin Hurwitz numbers and Schur Q-functions was noticed by Gunningham
[
Gun16
]
. On the representation theory side the spin Hurwitz numbers are related to the characters of the Sergeev group.

Generating functions of the weighted double spin Hurwitz numbers were identified with the tau-functions of the 2-component BKP integrable hierarchy by Mironov, Morozov and Natanzon
[
MMN
]
. Corresponding class of the BKP tau-functions, so-called
hypergeometric
tau-functions was introduced and investigated by Orlov
[
Orl03
]
. In this paper, we introduce and investigate an infinite parametric family of the hypergeometric tau-functions of the 2-component BKP hierarchy. This family is associated with the rational weight generating functions.

The motivation for the investigation of this family is two-fold. On the one hand, the simplest representative of this family is given by the generalized Brézin–Gross–Witten (BGW) tau-function. Relation between the generalized BGW tau-function and a hypergeometric solution of the BKP hierarchy was conjectured in
[
Ale21a
]
, and its proof is one of the main results of this paper. On the other hand, we claim that this family contains several interesting examples of the generating functions of the weighted spin Hurwitz numbers, including the natural spin analogs of the monotone, strictly monotone, and Bousquet-Mélou-Schaeffer Hurwitz numbers. In particular, the generalized BGW tau-function can be associated with the generating function of the strictly monotone spin Hurwitz numbers. Ordinary strictly monotone Hurwitz numbers describe Grothendieck’s dessins d’enfants and hypermaps, and the spin case should also be related to the interesting invariants of enumerative geometry and combinatorics.

For the ordinary Hurwitz numbers, the rational weight generating functions can be obtained from the combination of the Schur functions with a particular choice of the variables. In the spin case, the correspondence is more complicated and will be considered elsewhere. Therefore, we do not discuss the detailed interpretation of the obtained generating functions in terms of spin Hurwitz numbers and relations between these numbers and intersection theory invariants. Instead in this paper, we focus on the integrable properties of this family. For this purpose we use the standard ingredients of the theory of integrable hierarchies including the free fermions, vertex operators, and the Sato Grassmannian. However, for the BKP and KP hierarchies, corresponding to the spin and ordinary Hurwitz numbers respectively, these ingredients are different, because of the difference between the underlying symmetry algebras,
\go(\infty)
and
\gl(\infty)
.

In particular, we construct the basis for the corresponding points for the BKP Sato Grassmannian and derive associated Kac–Schwarz operators. Moreover, we construct the cut-and-join operators, which, for the polynomial weight generating functions, are the finite degree differential operators. The Kac–Schwarz description allows us to construct the quantum spectral curves and to derive the linear constraints for the tau-functions. We expect that these ingredients will be useful for further investigation of the spin Hurwitz numbers. In particular, they should be useful for the
construction of the Chekhov–Eynard–Orantin topological recursion. The description obtained in this paper is the spin analog of the description for the ordinary Hurwitz numbers, considered in
[
ALS16
,
AMMN14
,
KZ15
,
Zog15
]
.

A few days after the current paper was posted on the arXiv, a new paper
[
LY22
]
appeared with an alternative proof of Theorem
4
via an action of the Virasoro operators on the Schur Q-functions.

2.1.
Neutral fermions

Let
\phi_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be the neutral free fermions satisfying the canonical anticommutation relations

(2.1)

\left\{\phi_{k},\phi_{m}\right\}=(-1)^{k}\delta_{k+m,0}.

Note that
\phi_{0}^{2}=1/2
. These relations define the Clifford algebra as the associative algebra.

For the vacuum
\left|0\right>
and the co-vacuum
\left<0\right|
vectors, satisfying

(2.2)

\phi_{m}\left|0\right>=0,\,\,\,\,\,\left<0\right|\phi_{-m}=0,\,\,\,\,\,m<0

the elements
\phi_{k_{1}}\phi_{k_{2}}\dots\phi_{k_{m}}\left|0\right>
with
k_{1}>k_{2}>\dots>k_{m}\geq 0
form a basis of a version of infinite wedge space (or neutral fermion Fock space)
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}
,

(2.3)

{\mathcal{F}}_{B}=\sppan\left\{\phi_{k_{1}}\phi_{k_{2}}\dots\phi_{k_{m}}\left|0\right>\ |\ k_{1}>k_{2}>\dots>k_{m}\geq 0\right\},

and its dual

(2.4)

{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{*}=\sppan\left\{\left<0\right|\phi_{k_{m}}\dots\phi_{k_{2}}\phi_{k_{1}}\ |\ k_{1}<k_{2}<\dots<k_{m}\leq 0\right\}.

The space
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}
splits into two subspaces

(2.5)

{\mathcal{F}}_{B}={\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{0}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{1},

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{0}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{1}
denote the subspaces with even and odd numbers of generators respectively. The same decomposition exists for
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{*}
.

There is a nondegenerate bilinear pairing
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}\times{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{*}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
, and the pairing of
\left<U\right|\in{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{*}
and
\left|V\right>\in{\mathcal{F}}_{B}
is denoted by
\left<U|V\right>
with

(2.6)

\left<0|0\right>=1.

The
vacuum expectation value
of the Clifford algebra element
a
is a pairing of
\left<0\right|
and
a\left|0\right>
which is denoted by
\left<0\right|a\left|0\right>
. It is uniquely defined by the anticommutation relations (
2.1
), property (
2.2
), and a relation

(2.7)

\left<0\right|\phi_{0}\left|0\right>=0.

In particular, if
a
is an odd element of the Clifford algebra, then
\left<0\right|a\left|0\right>=0
. It is easy to see that the bases in (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are orthogonal.
Let us focus on the space
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{0}
. The basis can be labelled by strict partitions
\lambda\in\DP
in the following way:

(2.8)

\left|\lambda\right>=\begin{cases}\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\dots\phi_{\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}}\left|0\right>&\mathrm{for}\,\,\,\ell(\lambda)=0\mod 2,\\
\sqrt{2}\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\dots\phi_{\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}}\phi_{0}\left|0\right>&\mathrm{for}\,\,\,\ell(\lambda)=1\mod 2.\end{cases}

For the dual space
{\mathcal{F}}_{B}^{0*}
we have a similar basis. From the anticommutation relations we have:

Proposition 2.1
.

\forall

\lambda,\mu\in\DP

(2.9)

\left<\lambda|\mu\right>=(-1)^{|\lambda|}\delta_{\lambda,\mu}.

It is easy to see that

(2.10)

\left<0\right|\phi_{k}\phi_{m}\left|0\right>=\delta_{k+m,0}H[m],

where

(2.11)

H[m]=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{0}&\mathrm{for}\quad m<0,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}&\mathrm{for}\quad m=0,\\
\displaystyle{(-1)^{m}}\,\,\,\,\,\,&\mathrm{for}\quad m>0.\end{cases}

Bilinear combinations of neutral fermions
\phi_{k}\phi_{m}
satisfy the commutation relations of the Lie algebra
\go(\infty)
. Let
\left(E_{i,j}\right)_{k,l}=\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,l}
be the standard basis of the matrix units
\left.\left\{E_{{i,j}}\right|i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}
. Then
\phi_{k}\phi_{-m}
corresponds
[
DJKM82
]
to

(2.12)

F_{k,m}=(-1)^{m}E_{k,m}-(-1)^{k}E_{-m,-k}

with the commutation relations

(2.13)

\left[F_{a,b},F_{c,d}\right]=(-1)^{b}\delta_{b,c}F_{a,d}-(-1)^{a}\delta_{a+c,0}F_{-b,d}+(-1)^{b}\delta_{b+d,0}F_{c,-a}-(-1)^{a}\delta_{a,d}F_{c,b}.

For the bilinear combinations of neutral fermions we introduce the
normal ordering
by
:\mathrel{\phi_{k}\phi_{m}}:=\phi_{k}\phi_{m}-\left<0\right|\phi_{k}\phi_{m}\left|0\right>
. It is skew-symmetric

(2.14)

:\mathrel{\phi_{k}\phi_{m}}:=-:\mathrel{\phi_{m}\phi_{k}}:,

in particular,
:\mathrel{\phi_{k}\phi_{k}}:=0
.
The normal ordered quadratic combinations of neutral fermions satisfy the commutation relations of a central extension of the algebra
\go(\infty)

(2.15)

[:\mathrel{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}:,:\mathrel{\phi_{c}\phi_{d}}:]=(-1)^{b}\delta_{b+c,0}:\mathrel{\phi_{a}\phi_{d}}:-(-1)^{a}\delta_{a+c,0}:\mathrel{\phi_{b}\phi_{d}}:\\
+(-1)^{b}\delta_{b+d,0}:\mathrel{\phi_{c}\phi_{a}}:-(-)^{a}\delta_{a+d,0}:\mathrel{\phi_{c}\phi_{b}}:+(\delta_{c,b}\delta_{a,d}-\delta_{a-c,0}\delta_{b-d,0})((-1)^{a}H[b]-(-1)^{b}H[a]),

where
H[a]
is given by (
2.11
).

Let us consider the generating function

(2.16)

\phi(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi_{k}z^{k}.

It satisfies the anticommutation relation

(2.17)

\left\{\phi(z),\phi(w)\right\}=\delta(z+w).

Here we introduce the delta-function

(2.18)

\delta(z-w)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)^{k}.

It satisfies

(2.19)

\delta(z-w)f(z)=\delta(z-w)f(w)

for any formal series
f(z)\in{\mathbb{C}}[\![z,z^{-1}]\!]
, and can be represented as

(2.20)

2\delta(z+w)=\frac{z-w}{\underline{z}+w}-\frac{z-w}{\underline{w}+z}.

Here
\frac{1}{\underline{z}-w}
denotes the Laurent series expansion in the
region
|z|>|w|
,

(2.21)

\frac{1}{\underline{z}-w}:=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)^{k}.

Quadratic combinations of these generating functions generate a Lie algebra with the following commutation relations

(2.22)

\left[\phi_{1}(z_{1})\phi(w_{1}),\phi(z_{2})\phi(w_{2})\right]=\delta(w_{1}+z_{2})\phi(z_{1})\phi(w_{2})-\delta(z_{1}+z_{2})\phi(w_{1})\phi(w_{2})\\
+\delta(w_{1}+w_{2})\phi(z_{2})\phi(z_{1})-\delta(z_{1}+w_{2})\phi(z_{2})\phi(w_{1}).

For the normal ordered operator we have

(2.23)

\phi(z)\phi(w)=:\mathrel{\phi(z)\phi(w)}:+\frac{1}{2}\frac{z-w}{\underline{z}+w}.

2.2.
Vertex operators

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}
we introduce the
bosonic
operators

(2.24)

J_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(-1)^{m+1}:\mathrel{\phi_{m}\phi_{-m-k}}:

satisfying a commutation relation of the Heisenberg algebra

(2.25)

\left[J_{k},J_{m}\right]=\frac{k}{2}\delta_{k+m,0}.

From (
2.2
) we have

(2.26)

J_{m}\left|0\right>=0,\,\,\,\,\,\left<0\right|J_{-m}=0,\,\,\,\,\,m>0.

Let us consider the
vertex operator
for the BKP hierarchy introduced in
[
DJKM82
]
,

(2.27)

\widehat{V}_{B}(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}z^{k}t_{k}\right)\exp\left(-2\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}\frac{1}{kz^{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\right).

These operators satisfy the anticommutation relations

(2.28)

\left\{\widehat{V}_{B}(z),\widehat{V}_{B}(w)\right\}=2\delta(z+w)

similar to (
2.17
).

It is convenient to introduce the generating functions of the bosonic operators

(2.29)

J_{+}({\bf t})=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}t_{k}J_{k},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,J_{-}({\bf s})=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}s_{k}J_{-k}.

Then one has

(2.30)

\begin{split}\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}&=2\left<0\right|\phi_{0}e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}\phi(z),\\
\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\left<0\right|\phi_{0}e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}&=\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}\phi(z).\end{split}

Let us consider a bilinear combination of the vertex operators
\widehat{V}_{B}
,

(2.31)

\widehat{V}_{B}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\widehat{V}_{B}(w).

Using the anticommutation relation (
2.28
) it is easy to show that the vertex operators
\widehat{V}_{B}(z,w)
satisfy the commutation relation, equivalent to the one described by (
2.22
) for the bilinear combinations
\phi(z)\phi(w)
,

(2.32)

\left[\widehat{V}_{B}(z_{1},w_{1}),\widehat{V}_{B}(z_{2},w_{2})\right]=\delta(w_{1}+z_{2})\widehat{V}_{B}(z_{1},w_{2})\\
-\delta(z_{1}+z_{2})\widehat{V}_{B}(w_{1},w_{2})+\delta(w_{1}+w_{2})\widehat{V}_{B}(z_{2},z_{1})-\delta(z_{1}+w_{2})\widehat{V}_{B}(z_{2},w_{1}).

These vertex operators, dependent on two parameters, can be represented as

(2.33)

\widehat{V}_{B}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{z-w}{\underline{z}+w}\exp\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}(z^{k}+w^{k})t_{k}\right)\exp\left(-2\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{kz^{k}}+\frac{1}{kw^{k}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\right).

It is also convenient to consider a regularized version of the vertex operator, corresponding to
:\mathrel{\phi(z)\phi(-w)}:

(2.34)

{\widehat{Y}}_{B}(z,w)=\widehat{V}_{B}(z,w)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z-w}{\underline{z}+w},

or, equivalently

(2.35)

{\widehat{Y}}_{B}(z,w)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\widehat{V}_{B}(w)-\frac{z-w}{\underline{z}+w}\right).

This expression is regular at
z=-w
, moreover, it is antisymmetric with respect to the permutation of
z
and
w
,

(2.36)

{\widehat{Y}}_{B}(z,w)=-{\widehat{Y}}_{B}(w,z).

From (
2.30
) it follows that

(2.37)

{\widehat{Y}}_{B}(z,w)\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}=\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}:\mathrel{\phi(z)\phi(w)}:.

Lemma 3.4

For

Proof.

By definition

4.
Orthogonal Sato Grassmannian

In this section we briefly summarize some properties of the Sato Grassmannian. It describes the space of solutions of the KP hierarchy
[
SS83
]
and its BKP version. We describe the action of the algebra
w_{1+\infty}^{B}
on the BKP Sato Grassmannian and clarify the difference between the points of the KP and BKP Sato Grassmannians for the solutions of the KdV hierarchy.

4.1.
Symmetries of the BKP Sato Grassmannian

Let us consider the space
H=H_{+}\oplus H_{-}
, where the subspaces

(4.1)

H_{-}=z^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[\![z^{-1}]\!]

and

(4.2)

H_{+}={\mathbb{C}}[z]

are generated by negative and nonnegative powers of
z
respectively. Then the Sato Grassmannian
\rm{Gr}
consists of all closed linear spaces
\mathcal{W}\in H
, which are compatible with
H_{+}
. Namely, an orthogonal projection
\pi_{+}:\mathcal{W}\to H_{+}
should be a Fredholm operator, i.e. both the kernel
{\rm ker}\,\pi_{+}\in\mathcal{W}
and the
cokernel
{\rm coker}\,\pi_{+}\in H_{+}
should be finite-dimensional vector spaces.
The Grassmannian
\Gr
consists of components
\Gr^{(k)}
, parametrized by an index of the operator
\pi_{+}
. Below we use only the component
\Gr^{(0)}
; other components have an equivalent description. The big cell
\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
of
\Gr^{(0)}
is defined by the constraint
{\rm ker}\,\pi_{+}={\rm coker}\,\pi_{+}=0
. We call
\Gr^{(0)}_{+}
the
Sato Grassmannian
for simplicity.

A point of the Sato Grassmannian
{\mathcal{W}}\in\rm{Gr}^{(0)}_{+}
can be described by an
admissible basis

(4.3)

{\mathcal{W}}=\sppan_{\mathbb{C}}\{\Phi_{1}^{\mathcal{W}},\Phi_{2}^{\mathcal{W}},\Phi_{3}^{\mathcal{W}},\dots\}.

Let

(4.4)

\Psi={\mathcal{W}}\cap 1+z^{-1}{\mathbb{C}}[\![z^{-1}]\!].

be the
wave function
.

The BKP hierarchy can be represented in terms of tau-function
\tau({\bf t})
by the Hirota bilinear identity

(4.5)

\res_{z}z^{-1}e^{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}z^{k}(t_{k}-t^{\prime}_{k})}\tau({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])\tau({\bf t^{\prime}}+2[z^{-1}])=\tau({\bf t})\tau({\bf t^{\prime}}).

Here
{\bf t}
and
{\bf t^{\prime}}
are two independent sets of variables.
For

f(z)\in H
and
g(z)\in H
put

(4.6)

(f(z),g(z))_{B}:=\res_{z}z^{-1}f(z)g(-z).

Note that
(\ ,\ )_{B}
is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear pairing on

H
. For
{\mathcal{W}}\in\Gr^{(0)}
we denote by
{\mathcal{W}}^{\bot_{B}}
the orthogonal compliment of
{\mathcal{W}}
with respect to
(\ ,\ )_{B}
.
The BKP (or orthogonal) Sato Grassmannian
\Gr^{(0)}_{B}
is the subspace of
\Gr^{(0)}
such that
{\mathcal{W}}^{\bot_{B}}\subset{\mathcal{W}}
and the quotient
{\mathcal{W}}/{\mathcal{W}}^{\bot_{B}}
is one dimensional and is generated by
\Psi
. Similarly to the KP case, one can consider the big cell of the BKP Sato Grassmannian,
\Gr^{(0)}_{B+}=\Gr^{(0)}_{+}\cap\Gr^{(0)}_{B}
.
There exists a bijection between the points of the BKP Sato Grassmannian
{\mathcal{W}}\in\Gr^{(0)}_{B+}
and the BKP tau-functions with
\tau({\bf 0})=1
.
Below we focus on
\Gr^{(0)}_{B+}
, and put
\tau({\bf 0})=1
for simplicity.

Tau-functions of the BKP hierarchy are given by the vacuum expectation values of neutral fermions

(4.7)

\tau_{G}({\bf t})=\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}G\left|0\right>.

They are labeled by the group element of the centrally extended
\go(\infty)
algebra,

(4.8)

G=\exp\left(\sum_{k,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{km}:\mathrel{\phi_{k}\phi_{m}}:\right).

The wave function is equal to the principal specialization of the BKP tau-function

(4.9)

\Psi(z)=\tau(-2[z^{-1}])=\tau(2[-z^{-1}]),

because the BKP tau-functions depend only on odd times. The
Baker–Akhiezer function

(4.10)

\Psi^{B}(z,{\bf t})=e^{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}t_{k}z^{k}}\frac{\tau({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])}{\tau({\bf t})}

generates the corresponding point of the BKP Sato Grassmannian through the
{\bf t}
series expansion

(4.11)

\Psi(z,{\bf t})\in{\mathcal{W}}.

Let us label the Baker–Akhiezer function, associated with the tau-function (
4.7
), by
G
. Then, from (
2.30
) we have

(4.12)

\Psi_{G}^{B}(z,{\bf t})=\frac{2\left<0\right|\phi_{0}e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}\phi(z)G\left|0\right>}{\tau_{G}({\bf t})}.

For some
{\mathtt{a}}\in w_{1+\infty}^{B}
let us consider

(4.13)

\tilde{G}=e^{W_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}G,

where
W_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}
is given by (
3.17
). We assume that
{\mathtt{a}}
and
G
are such that both
\tau_{G}
and
\tau_{\tilde{G}}
are well defined.
Then from (
2.30
) for the associated wave function we have

(4.14)

\begin{split}\Psi_{\tilde{G}}^{B}(z,{\bf t})&=\frac{\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}e^{{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}G\left|0\right>}{\tau_{\tilde{G}}({\bf t})}\\
&=\frac{\widehat{V}_{B}(z)e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}G\left|0\right>}{\tau_{\tilde{G}}({\bf t})}.\end{split}

From Corollary
3.2
we have the operator identity

(4.15)

\widehat{V}_{B}(z)e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}=e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\left(e^{-\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\widehat{V}_{B}(z)e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\right)=e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\left(e^{{\mathtt{a}}_{z}}\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\right),

hence

(4.16)

\begin{split}\Psi^{B}_{\tilde{G}}(z,{\bf t})&=\frac{e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}e^{{\mathtt{a}}_{z}}\widehat{V}_{B}(z)\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\bf t})}G\left|0\right>}{\tau_{\tilde{G}}({\bf t})}\\
&=\frac{e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\tau_{G}({\bf t})e^{a_{z}}\Psi^{B}_{G}(z,{\bf t})}{\tau_{\tilde{G}}({\bf t})}.\end{split}

Inverting the operator
e^{\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}
we have

(4.17)

e^{{\mathtt{a}}_{z}}\Psi^{B}_{G}(z,{\bf t})=\frac{e^{-\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\tau_{\tilde{G}}({\bf t})\Psi^{B}_{\tilde{G}}(z,{\bf t})}{\tau_{G}({\bf t})}.

Since
\Psi^{B}_{\tilde{G}}(z,{\bf t})\in{\mathcal{W}}_{\tilde{G}}
, we also have

(4.18)

\frac{e^{-\widehat{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}\tau_{\tilde{G}}({\bf t})\Psi^{B}_{\tilde{G}}(z,{\bf t})}{\tau_{G}({\bf t})}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{\tilde{G}}.

Therefore,

(4.19)

e^{{\mathtt{a}}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}_{G}\subset{\mathcal{W}}_{\tilde{G}},

and inverse is also true

(4.20)

e^{-{\mathtt{a}}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}_{\tilde{G}}\subset{\mathcal{W}}_{G}.

Then the following lemma justifies Definition
1
:

Lemma 4.1
.

For
{\mathtt{a}}\in w_{1+\infty}^{B}
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{G}\in\Gr^{(0)}_{B}

(4.21)

e^{{\mathtt{a}}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}_{G}={\mathcal{W}}_{e^{{W}_{\mathtt{a}}^{B}}G}.

4.2.
KdV vs BKP

In
[
Ale21b
]
we prove that after a simple redefinition of times any tau-function of the KdV hierarchy solves the BKP hierarchy:

Theorem 1

(
[
Ale21b
]
)
.

For any KdV tau-function

(4.22)

\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t})=\tau_{\KdV}({\bf t}/2)

is a tau-function of the BKP hierarchy.

The KdV hierarchy is a 2-reduction of the KP hierarchy.
Let us consider a tau-function of KdV hierarchy
\tau_{\KdV}
and the point
{\mathcal{W}}^{\KdV}\in\Gr^{(0)}
associated to it as a tau-function of the KP hierarchy. If we consider the same tau-function as a tau-function of the BKP hierarchy, in general it defines another point of the same Sato Grassmannian
{\mathcal{W}}^{\BKP}\in\Gr^{(0)}_{B}\subset\Gr^{(0)}
. The reason is that the
Baker–Akhiezer functions of two hierarchies have different form.

Indeed, as a tau-function of the KP hierarchy,
\tau_{\KdV}
defines the
Baker–Akhiezer function by

(4.23)

\Psi^{\KdV}(z,{\bf t})=e^{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}t_{k}z^{k}}\frac{\tau_{\KdV}({\bf t}-[z^{-1}])}{\tau_{\KdV}(\bf t)},

where we put
t_{2k}=0
for
k>0
.
If we represent it in terms of the corresponding BKP tau-function using Theorem
1
, we get

(4.24)

\Psi^{\KdV}(z,{\bf t}/2)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}t_{k}z^{k}}\frac{\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])}{\tau_{\BKP}(\bf t)}.

The Baker–Akhiezer function of the same tau-function, considered in the context of the BKP hierarchy, is

(4.25)

\Psi^{\BKP}(z,{\bf t})=e^{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}t_{k}z^{k}}\frac{\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])}{\tau_{\BKP}(\bf t)}.

If
{\mathcal{W}}^{\BKP}\in\Gr^{(0)}_{B+}
then at
{\bf t}={\bf 0}
these two Baker–Akhiezer functions coincide

(4.26)

\Phi_{1}^{\KdV}(z)=\Phi_{1}^{\BKP}(z)=\tau_{\BKP}(-2[z^{-1}])=:\Psi(z).

However, already the next basis vectors are different:

(4.27)

\begin{split}\Phi_{2}^{\KdV}(z)&=\left.2\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\Psi^{\KdV}(z,{\bf t}/2)\right|_{{\bf t}={\bf 0}}\\
&=z\Psi(z)+\left.2\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])\right|_{{\bf t}={\bf 0}}-2\Psi(z)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t})\right|_{{\bf t}={\bf 0}},\end{split}

and

(4.28)

\begin{split}\Phi_{2}^{\BKP}(z)&=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\Psi^{\BKP}(z,{\bf t}/2)\right|_{{\bf t}={\bf 0}}\\
&=z\Psi(z)+\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t}-2[z^{-1}])\right|_{{\bf t}={\bf 0}}-\Psi(z)\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}\tau_{\BKP}({\bf t})\right|_{{\bf t}={\bf 0}}.\end{split}

Moreover, it is clear that this difference, in general, cannot be compensated by
\Psi
. Hence, in general,

(4.29)

{\mathcal{W}}^{\KdV}\neq{\mathcal{W}}^{\BKP}.

Therefore, the Kac–Schwarz algebras of a KdV tau-function, considered in the frameworks of the KP and BKP hierarchies, are different.

5.
Generalized Brézin–Gross–Witten model

5.1.
Intersection theory

Denote by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
the Deligne–Mumford compactification of the moduli space of all compact Riemann surfaces of genus
g
with
n
distinct marked points. It is a non-singular complex orbifold of dimension
3g-3+n
. It is empty unless the stability condition

(5.1)

\displaystyle 2g-2+n>0

is satisfied.

For each marking index
i
consider the cotangent line bundle
{\mathbb{L}}_{i}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, whose fiber over a point
[\Sigma,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the complex cotangent space
T_{z_{i}}^{*}\Sigma
of
\Sigma
at
z_{i}
. Let
\psi_{i}\in H^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n},\mathbb{Q})
denote the first Chern class of
{\mathbb{L}}_{i}
. We consider the intersection numbers

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left<\tau_{a_{1}}\tau_{a_{2}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}\right>_{g}:=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\psi_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}.

The integral on the right-hand side of (
5.2
) vanishes unless the stability condition (
5.1
) is satisfied, all
a_{i}
are non-negative integers, and the dimension constraint

(5.3)

3g-3+n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}

holds true. Let
T_{i}
,
i\geq 0
, be formal variables and let

(5.4)

\tau_{KW}:=\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2+n}F_{g,n}\right),

where

(5.5)

F_{g,n}:=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\geq 0}\left<\tau_{a_{1}}\tau_{a_{2}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}\right>_{g}\frac{\prod T_{a_{i}}}{n!}.

Witten’s conjecture
[
Wit91
]
, proved by Kontsevich
[
Kon92
]
, states that the partition function
\tau_{KW}
becomes a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy after the change of variables
T_{n}=(2n+1)!!t_{2n+1}
. This is the Kontsevich-Witten tau-function.

In this paper we will focus on a different version of intersection theory on the moduli spaces, which is also governed by the KdV hierarchy, and was recently introduced by Norbury
[
Nor17
]
.
Namely, he introduced
\Theta
-classes,
\Theta_{g,n}\in H^{4g-4+2n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
, and described their intersections with the
\psi
-classes

(5.6)

\left<\tau_{a_{1}}\tau_{a_{2}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}\right>_{g}^{\Theta}=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Theta_{g,n}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\psi_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}.

Again, the integral on the right-hand side vanishes unless the stability condition (
5.1
) is satisfied, all
a_{i}
are non-negative integers, and the dimension constraint

(5.7)

g-1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}

holds true.
Consider the generating function of the
intersection numbers of
\Theta
-classes and
\psi
-classes

(5.8)

F^{\Theta}_{g,n}=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\geq 0}\frac{\prod T_{a_{i}}}{n!}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\Theta_{g,n}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\psi_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}},

then it was conjectured by Norbury
[
Nor17
]
, that it provides a direct analog of the Kontsevich–Witten tau-function. Recently this conjecture was proven by Chidambaram, Garcia-Failde, and Giacchetto.

Theorem

(
[
CGFG22
]
)
.

The generating function

(5.9)

\tau_{\Theta}=\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{2g-2+n}F_{g,n}^{\Theta}\right)

becomes a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy after the change of variables
T_{n}=(2n+1)!!t_{2n+1}
.

Moreover,
\tau_{\Theta}
is nothing but a tau-function of the Brézin–Gross–Witten (BGW) model

(5.10)

\tau_{\Theta}=\tau_{BGW}.

We refer the reader to
[
Nor17
,
Nor20
,
CGFG22
]
for a detailed presentation.

The BGW tau-function can be described by a matrix model. This matrix model description has a natural one-parameter deformation, which we will consider below. Let
\Lambda={\rm diag}\,(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{M})
be a diagonal matrix.
For any function

5.3.
Schur Q-functions and BKP hierarchy

Schur Q-functions were introduced by Schur
[
Sch11
]
for the description of the projective representations of the symmetric groups.
These functions are labeled by strict partitions

. The definition and description of the Schur Q-functions can be found in Section 3.8 of Macdonald’s book
[
Mac95
]

In many aspects the Schur Q-functions are similar to the ordinary Schur functions.
For example, let us mention the
Cauchy formula

(5.31)

\sum_{\lambda\in\DP}\frac{Q_{\lambda}({\bf t})Q_{\lambda}({\bf s})}{2^{\ell(\lambda)}}=\exp\left(2\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}kt_{k}{s}_{k}\right),

[

Q_{\lambda}(\delta_{k,1})=2^{|\lambda|}\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{j}!}\prod_{k<m}\frac{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{m}}.

Schur Q-functions are closely related to the BKP hierarchy. Let us consider the class of hypergeometric solutions of the BKP hierarchy introduced by Orlov
[
Orl03
]
. It was shown by Mironov, Morozov, and Natanzon
[

that the hypergeometric solutions can be interpreted as the generating functions of the spin Hurwitz numbers. Hypergeometric solutions of the 2-component BKP hierarchy are given by the following sums over strict partitions

\begin{split}\tau({\bf t},{\bf s})=\sum_{\lambda\in\DP}2^{-\ell(\lambda)}r_{\lambda}Q_{\lambda}({\bf t}/2)Q_{\lambda}({\bf s}/2),\end{split}

where

(5.34)

r_{\lambda}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}r(1)r(2)\dots r(\lambda_{j})

for some function

, which we call the
weight generating function
.

Remark 5.4
.

Let us note that if

for some

, then the sum in (

) is finite and contains only partitions with

.

Relation between Schur Q-functions and BKP hierarchy, in particular, is described by the following result of You:

Theorem 3

(
[

]
)

For the states (

\sigma_{B}^{0}(\left|\lambda\right>)=2^{-\ell(\lambda)/2}Q_{\lambda}({\bf t}/2).

Schur Q-functions are the polynomial solutions of the BKP hierarchy.

s_{k}
do not vanish, then the sum
\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}{s}_{k}\widehat{W}_{{\mathtt{w}}({\bf u})^{k}}
is finite, and for the finite number of parameters
{\bf u}
the operator
\widehat{W}_{{\mathtt{w}}({\bf u})^{k}}
is a finite degree differential operator in variables
{\bf t}
.

Consider the case
s_{j}=\delta_{j,1}\hbar/2
. It corresponds to the single weighted spin Hurwitz numbers. Then the sum over partitions (
6.6
) can be expanded as

(6.14)

\tau_{a,0}({\bf t},\delta_{j,1}\hbar/2,{\bf u},{\bf 0})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{k}\tau_{a,0}^{(k)}({\bf t},{\bf u}),

where

(6.15)

\tau_{a,0}^{(k)}({\bf t},{\bf u})=\sum_{\lambda\in\DP,|\lambda|=k}r_{\lambda}^{(u_{1})}r_{\lambda}^{(u_{2})}\dots r_{\lambda}^{(u_{a})}\frac{Q_{\lambda}({\bf t}/2)Q_{\lambda}(\delta_{j,1})}{4^{|\lambda|}2^{-\ell(\lambda)}}.

Then the cut-and-join operator provides the recursion in degree of the ramified covering:

(6.16)

\tau_{a,0}^{(k)}({\bf t},{\bf u})=-\frac{1}{2k}\widehat{W}_{{\mathtt{w}}({\bf u})}\cdot\tau_{a,0}^{(k-1)}({\bf t},{\bf u}).

We have a similar expansion for
s_{j}=\delta_{j,k}\hbar/2
with
k>1
, it corresponds to the case of orbifold spin Hurwitz numbers.

For the simplest case
a=1
,
s_{j}=\delta_{j,1}\hbar/2
and
u_{1}=N
this operator reduces the the cut-and-join operator (
5.16
) for the generalized BGW tau-function.

6.2.
Sato Grassmannian and Kac–Schwarz operators

Let

(6.17)

{\mathtt{G}}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{a}{\mathtt{O}}_{B}(u_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{b}{\mathtt{O}}_{B}(w_{j})}e^{-\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}{s}_{k}z^{-k}}

be the group operators of the
w_{1+\infty}^{B}
algebra.
The point of the Sato Grassmannian, corresponding to the BKP tau-function (
6.1
) is given by

(6.18)

{\mathcal{W}}={\mathtt{G}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}_{0},

where
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}=\{1,z,z^{2},z^{3}\dots\}
is a point of the Grassmannian for the trivial BKP tau-function
\tau({\bf t})=1
.

The normalized basis vectors for the tau-function (
6.1
) are given by

(6.19)

\Phi_{k}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{b}\Gamma(k-1/2+w_{j})\Gamma(k-1/2-w_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{a}\Gamma(k-1/2+u_{j})\Gamma(k-1/2-u_{j})}{\mathtt{G}}\cdot z^{k-1}.

Let us introduce the operators

(6.20)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{p}}&:={\mathtt{G}}\,\partial_{z}\,{\mathtt{G}}^{-1},\\
{\mathtt{q}}&:={\mathtt{G}}\,z\,{\mathtt{G}}^{-1}.\end{split}

These are the Kac-Schwarz operators

(6.21)

{\mathtt{p}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}\subset{\mathcal{W}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,{\mathtt{q}}\cdot{\mathcal{W}}\subset{\mathcal{W}},

satisfying the canonical commutation relation

(6.22)

\left[{\mathtt{p}},{\mathtt{q}}\right]={\mathtt{1}}.

Let us introduce

(6.23)

{\mathtt{R}}_{m}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{b}((D+1/2-m)^{2}-w_{j}^{2})}{\prod_{j=1}^{a}((D+1/2-m)^{2}-u_{j}^{2})}.

For operators (
6.20
) we have

(6.24)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{q}}&=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{a}{\mathtt{O}}_{B}(u_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{b}{\mathtt{O}}_{B}(w_{j})}\,z\,\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{b}{\mathtt{O}}_{B}(w_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{a}{\mathtt{O}}_{B}(u_{j})}\\
&=z\,{\mathtt{R}}_{0}\end{split}

and

(6.25)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{p}}&=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{a}((D+1/2)^{2}-u_{j}^{2})}{\prod_{j=1}^{b}((D+1/2)^{2}-w_{j}^{2})}\partial_{z}-\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}k{s}_{k}{\mathtt{q}}^{-k-1}\\
&={\mathtt{R}}_{0}^{-1}\partial_{z}-\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}k{s}_{k}z^{-k-1}\prod_{m=1}^{k+1}{\mathtt{R}}_{m}^{-1}.\end{split}

By construction for the wave function
\Psi=\Phi_{1}
from (
6.19
) we have

(6.26)

{\mathtt{p}}\cdot\Psi(z)=0.

However, it is more convenient to introduce the operator

(6.27)

{\mathtt{c}}={\mathtt{q}}{\mathtt{p}}=D-\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}k{s}_{k}{\mathtt{q}}^{-k}

which also annihilates the wave function,
{\mathtt{c}}\cdot\Psi=0
.
Let us consider the case when
s_{\ell}\neq 0
for some finite odd
\ell
and
s_{k}=0
for
k>\ell
. If
b=0
, then the operator
{\mathtt{c}}
is a finite order differential operator with coefficients in
{\mathbb{C}}[z,z^{-1}]
. If
b>0
we introduce

(6.28)

{\mathtt{K}}=\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{b}((D+r-1/2)^{2}-w_{j}^{2}).

Then for the operator
{\mathtt{A}}:={\mathtt{K}}{\mathtt{c}}
we have

(6.29)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{A}}=D{\mathtt{K}}-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}k{s}_{k}z^{-k}\prod_{r=1}^{\ell-k}\prod_{j=1}^{b}((D+r-1/2)^{2}-w_{j}^{2})\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}\prod_{j=1}^{a}((D-m-1/2)^{2}-u_{j}^{2}).\end{split}

It is a polynomial of
z^{-1}
and
D
, which annihilates the wave function

(6.30)

{\mathtt{A}}\cdot\Psi(z)=0.

We can interpret it as the
quantum spectral curve
. For
{\mathtt{K}}\neq 1
this operator, in general, is not a Kac-Schwarz operator.

Conjecture 6.1
.

The
n
-point functions for the tau-function (
6.1
) can be given by the topological recursion, associated with the semi-classical limit of the quantum spectral curve (
6.29
).

6.3.
Examples

The case of the generalized BGW tau-function, considered in Section
5
, corresponds to

a=1
,
b=0
,
s_{j}=\delta_{j,1}\hbar/2
. In this case

(6.31)

{\mathtt{R}}_{m}=\frac{1}{(D+1/2-m)^{2}-u^{2}}.

The Kac–Schwarz operators, introduced in the previous section, are

(6.32)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{p}}&=((D+1/2)^{2}-u^{2})\partial_{z}-\frac{\hbar}{2}z^{-2}{\mathtt{R}}_{1}^{-1}{\mathtt{R}}_{2}^{-1},\\
{\mathtt{q}}&=z\frac{1}{(D+1/2)^{2}-u^{2}},\end{split}

the operator

(6.33)

{\mathtt{c}}=D-\frac{\hbar}{2}z^{-1}((D-1/2)^{2}-u^{2})

coincides with the quantum spectral curve operator
{\mathtt{A}}
. The quantum spectral curve equation in this case is closely related to the Bessel equation.

We expect, that the spin Hurwitz numbers interpretation of the case with
a=0
,
b=1
is particularly interesting. Namely, this case can be associated with a spin version of the monotone Hurwitz numbers. Let us consider it in more detail for
s_{j}=\delta_{j,1}\hbar/2
. Then

(6.34)

{\mathtt{R}}_{m}=(D+1/2-m)^{2}-w^{2},

and the Kac–Schwarz operators are

(6.35)

\begin{split}{\mathtt{p}}&=\frac{1}{(D+1/2)^{2}-w^{2}}\partial_{z}-\frac{\hbar}{2}z^{-2}{\mathtt{R}}_{1}^{-1}{\mathtt{R}}_{2}^{-1},\\
{\mathtt{q}}&=z((D+1/2)^{2}-w^{2}),\end{split}

and

(6.36)

{\mathtt{c}}=D-\frac{\hbar}{2}z^{-1}\frac{1}{(D-1/2)^{2}-w^{2}}.

Then the quantum spectral curve is given by the third order differential operator

(6.37)

{\mathtt{A}}=((D-1/2)^{2}-w^{2})D-\frac{\hbar}{2}z^{-1}.

6.4.
Constraints

We expect that the tau-functions (
6.1
) satisfy the Virasoro and W-constraints, analogous to ones obtained in
[
KZ15
,
AMMN14
]
. Some of these constraints can be derived using the Kac-Schwarz approach for BKP hierarchy. However, not all of them can be derived this way.

The illustrative example here is the generalized BGW tau-function, considered in the previous section. This model is completely described by the Virasoro constraints, derived in
[
Ale18
]
using the Kac-Schwarz description of the KP hierarchy. However, these constraints do not appear in the BKP picture. Hence, for the generalized BGW tau-function, the KP and BKP pictures are complimentary to each other: the Virasoro constraints are natural symmetries of the KP hierarchy, while the cut-and-join operator is an element of the BKP symmetry group. We expect that such interplay between different integrable structures should be common for the enumerative geometry tau-functions.

The simplest constraint for the generalzed BGW model, which can be obtained from the BKP approach, is given by

(6.38)

\widehat{W}^{B}_{{\mathtt{c}}}\cdot\tau_{BGW}({\bf t}/2,u)=\mu\tau_{BGW}({\bf t}/2,u)

where
{\mathtt{c}}
is given by (
6.33
), and
\mu
is some eigenvalue. From the consideration of this equation at
{\bf t}={\bf 0}
it is easy to see that
\mu=0
. The operator

(6.39)

\widehat{W}^{B}_{{\mathtt{c}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\widehat{W}_{0,1}^{B}+\frac{\hbar}{4}\widehat{W}_{-1,2}^{B}+\hbar\left(\frac{1}{16}-\frac{u^{2}}{4}\right)\widehat{W}_{-1,0}^{B}

describes the cut-and-join equation. Indeed, comparing this operator with (
5.16
), we see that (
6.38
) reduces to

(6.40)

\frac{1}{2}\widehat{W}_{0,1}^{B}\cdot\tau_{BGW}({\bf t}/2,u)=\hbar\widehat{W}_{0}(u)\cdot\tau_{BGW}({\bf t}/2,u).

Since
\widehat{W}_{0,1}^{B}=\widehat{L}_{0}^{B}=2\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}kt_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}
, we have the cut-and-join equation

(6.41)

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\odd}^{+}}kt_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{k}}\tau_{BGW}({\bf t}/2,u)=\hbar\widehat{W}_{0}(u)\cdot\tau_{BGW}({\bf t}/2,u)

with the unique solution normalized by
\tau_{BGW}({\bf 0},u)=1
. This solution is given by (
5.13
).

Abstract
. This is the first in a series of papers [ KMX21c , KMX , KMX21b , KMX21a ] on geometric mapping theory in Carnot groups – and more generally equiregular manifolds – in which we prove a number of new structural results for Sobolev (in particular quasisymmetric) mappings, establishing (partial) rigidity or (partial) regularity theorems, depending on the context.



1.
Introduction

Background and overview

Quasiconformal mappings between domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
have been heavily studied since the 1930s. They initially arose in Teichmuller theory, and over time found applications in a number of areas of mathematics, including Kleinian groups, complex dynamics, PDE, rigidity of lattices, and geometric group theory. Quasiconformal mappings in non-Euclidean settings first appeared around 1970 in the work of Mostow on rigidity of lattices in Lie groups, and the topic gained momentum in the 80s and 90s due to the convergence of developments from several directions, including Gromov’s notion of hyperbolicity, fundamental work of Pansu on quasi-isometries of symmetric spaces, advances in the analytical theory of quasiconformal homeomorphisms, and the explosion of interest in analysis on metric spaces (see for instance
[
Gro87
,
Pan89
,
IM93
,
MM95
,
HK98
,
Ast98
,
Bon06
,
Kle06
,
Hei07
]
). A new phenomenon in the non-Euclidean setting is rigidity
[
Pan89
]
: in some situations quasiconformal mappings turn out to be very restricted, in sharp contrast to mappings in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which are flexible and come in infinite dimensional families. A heuristic explanation for rigidity is that in the case of smooth mappings quasiconformality is equivalent to a first order system which is formally overdetermined except in very special cases. However, the limited regularity of quasiconformal homeomorphisms has posed a major obstacle to converting this heuristic into theorems: since quasiconformal homeomorphisms are not a priori smooth, the standard technique for exploiting overdetermined conditions – repeated differentiation – is not applicable. For this reason, rigidity is only known in some special cases where the regularity problem has been successfully overcome or circumvented
[
Pan89
,
Cap99
,
CC06
,
Xie15
]
.
Regularity issues aside, the general picture remains unclear even for smooth quasiconformal homeomorphisms, because the precise implications of the condition depend in a subtle way on the structure of the Carnot group
G
; see below for further discussion.

In a series of papers
[
KMX21c
,
KMX
,
KMX21b
,
KMSXb
,
KMSXa
,
KMX21a
]
we establish a number of rigidity and regularity theorems for quasiconformal homeomorphisms, and more generally, Sobolev mappings. A key technical result is a generalization to Carnot groups of the fact that in Euclidean space, pullback of differential forms by Sobolev mappings commutes with exterior differentiation, under suitable assumptions on the Sobolev exponent
[
Res89
]
. We consider Sobolev (in particular quasiconformal) mappings
f:G\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
between open subsets of Carnot groups, and define the Pansu pullback
f_{P}^{*}\omega
of a smooth differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{*}(U^{\prime})
using the (approximate) Pansu differential. Although Pansu pullback does not commute with exterior differentiation in general, even for smooth Sobolev mappings (see Lemma
4.1
), it turns out that a partial analog does hold: for some closed forms
\omega
, certain components of the distributional exterior derivative of
f_{P}^{*}\omega
are zero. This result provides new constraints on mappings which can be exploited in different ways depending on the context – to exclude oscillatory behavior of the Pansu differential, to prove regularity, or to show that auxiliary geometric objects satisfy a PDE. We note that Dairbekov and Vodopyanov, motivated by Reshetnyak’s work and applications to quasiregular mappings, considered Pansu pullback and showed that it commutes with exterior derivative in special cases
[
Dai99
,
Vod99
,
Vod07b
]
.

Although the initial motivation for this work arose from geometric mapping theory and geometric group theory, in spirit the phenomena and methods have much in common with the analytical side of geometric mapping theory, and with the literature on rigidity and oscillatory solutions to PDEs, see for instance

[
Res89
,
IM93
,
Vod07b
,
Nas54
,
Tar79
,
Mur81
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

We first discuss mappings and product structure. We remark that the problem of determining to what extent a mapping

[

We observe that bilipschitz homeomorphisms need not respect product structure in Carnot groups: if

is a Carnot group then for any nonconstant Lipschitz map

the shear

defines a bilipschitz homeomorphism

which does not respect the product structure. Another type of example comes from products of the form
G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
G_{1}\simeq K
,
G_{2}\simeq K\times K
for some Carnot group

(x_{1},(x_{2},x_{3}))\mapsto(x_{3},(x_{1},x_{2}))
does not respect the given product structure. Our first result asserts that nondegenerate Sobolev mappings between products must respect the product structure once these two types of examples are excluded. Let
\{G_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
,
\{G_{j}^{\prime}\}_{1\leq j\leq n^{\prime}}
be collections of Carnot groups, where each
G_{i}
,
G_{j}^{\prime}
is nonabelian and does not admit a nontrivial decomposition as a product of Carnot groups.
Let

,

.

Let

Theorem 1.7

Our next result, which is a key ingredient in the rigidity theorems above, concerns the interaction between Sobolev mappings and differential forms. We recall that pullback of smooth
k
-forms by a

-mapping

commutes with the exterior derivative, i.e.

(1.15)

df^{*}\omega=f^{*}d\omega

for all
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
; here the exterior derivative on the left hand side is interpreted distributionally. To our knowledge this was first observed by Reshetnyak, who used it in his approach to quasiconformal and quasiregular mappings
[
Res89
,
Res67
]
; it has many other applications to geometric mapping theory in Euclidean space. Now let
f:G\supset U\rightarrow G^{\prime}
be a

mapping where
G
,
G^{\prime}
are Carnot groups and
U
is an open subset. Since

need not be a Sobolev mapping with respect to the Euclidean metrics (
1.15
) is not applicable. On the other hand we may define the

of a smooth

to be the

-form with measurable coefficients

given by

f_{P}^{*}\omega(p)=(D_{P}f(p))^{*}\omega(f(p))\,;

here we are viewing the Pansu differential as a graded homomorphism
D_{P}f(p):\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
, and identifying tangent spaces of

,

with their respective Lie algebras via left translation.
It turns out that unlike ordinary pullback, Pansu pullback does not commute with the exterior derivative, even for smooth contact diffeomorphisms (see Lemma

). Nonetheless, there is a partial generalization of (

) to Carnot groups. To state this, we will need the notion of the weight of a differential form; the weight

is defined using its decomposition with respect to the diagonalizable action of the Carnot scaling on

Theorem 1.16

(Pullback theorem)
.

Let

,

be Carnot groups where

Let

-mapping between open subsets, for some

. Suppose

and

are smooth forms such that

\operatorname{wt}(\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(d\eta)\leq-\nu\,,\quad\operatorname{wt}(d\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(\eta)\leq-\nu\,,

then

(1.17)

\int_{U}(f_{P}^{*}\omega)\wedge\eta+(-1)^{k}\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge d\eta=0\,.

In particular, if
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(U^{\prime})
and

are closed and

Theorem

Discussion of proofs

We begin with Theorem
1.1
, and for simplicity we take
U=G=G^{\prime}=\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
; see Section

for the detailed proof.

Consider a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping

for some

, such that the Pansu differential
D_{P}f(x)
is an isomorphism for a.e.

. Since the Pansu differential
D_{P}f(x):\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}
is a graded isomorphism of Carnot groups, it is easy to see that
D_{P}f(x)
must respect the direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
, i.e. there is a permutation
\sigma_{x}:\{1,2\}\rightarrow\{1,2\}
such that

(1.19)

D_{P}f(x)(x_{1},x_{2})=(h_{1}(x_{\sigma_{x}^{-1}(1)}),h_{2}(x_{\sigma_{x}^{-1}(2)}))

for some automorphisms
h_{1},h_{2}:\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
. If the permutation
\sigma_{x}
is independent of
x
, then one may readily integrate (
1.19
) to deduce that
f
itself is a product as in (
1.2
). If
D_{P}f(x)
varied continuously with
x
, it would follow immediately that the permutation
\sigma_{p}
is constant since it takes values in a discrete set; however
D_{P}f(x)
varies only measurably with
x
. To appreciate the delicacy of this issue, consider the Lipschitz folding map
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined by

F(x)=\begin{cases}x\quad\text{if}\quad x\in H_{+}\\
r_{\ell}(x)\quad\text{if}\quad x\in H_{-}\,,\end{cases}

where
H_{\pm}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
are the two halfplanes bounded by the line
\ell:=\{(x,x)|x\in\mathbb{R}\}
, and
r_{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
is reflection across the line
\ell
. The derivative
DF(x):\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
preserves the direct sum decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, but

is not a product mapping. We are able to exclude this kind of behavior in the permutation

from (

) using the Pullback Theorem and a short argument with differential forms.

We now turn to Theorem
1.16
.

The proof of (
1.15
) in the Euclidean case follows from a straightforward mollification argument, so it is natural to try to use mollification to prove analogous results in the Carnot group setting. The standard approach to mollifying a map
f:G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
would be as follows. Let
\sigma_{1}
be a symmetric compactly supported smooth probability measure on
G
. For
\rho\in(0,\infty)
,
x\in G
, let
\sigma_{\rho}:=(\delta_{\rho})_{*}\sigma_{1}
be the pushforward of
\sigma_{1}
under the Carnot dilation
\delta_{\rho}
, and
\sigma_{x,\rho}:=(\ell_{x})_{*}\sigma_{\rho}
be the pushforward of
\sigma_{\rho}
under left translation by
x
. One defines the mollified mapping
f_{\rho}:G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
by letting
f_{\rho}(x)
to be the average of the pushforward measure
f_{*}(\sigma_{x,\rho})
, where the average is defined by treating
G^{\prime}
as a linear space, for instance by identifying
G^{\prime}
with its Lie algebra via the exponential map. Unfortunately, such a mollification introduces uncontrollable error terms, a fact which was pointed out in
[
Vod07b
]
(however, see
[
Dai99
]
for a proof of some analytical results using standard mollification in the special case of the Heisenberg group). Instead of using standard averaging to define
f_{\rho}(x)
we use a center of mass construction due to Buser-Karcher which is compatible with left translation and Carnot dilation
[
BK81
]
. This avoids both the uncontrollable error terms and the limitation to forms of codegree
1
faced by the method of
[
Vod07b
]
.
The main consequence is the following approximation theorem, which is the central technical result in this paper:

Theorem 1.20
.

Let
G
,
G^{\prime}
be Carnot groups where
G
has dimension
N
and homogeneous dimension
\nu
.
Let
f:G\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
be a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping between open subsets, for some
p>\nu
. Then there is a family of smooth mappings
\{f_{\rho}:U_{\rho}\rightarrow G^{\prime}\}_{\rho>0}
with the following properties:

•

U_{\rho}\subset U
is an open subset such that every compact subset
K\subset U
is contained in
U_{\rho}
when
\rho
is sufficiently small;

•

f_{\rho}\rightarrow f
locally uniformly;

•

If
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(U^{\prime})
,
\eta\in\Omega^{N-k}_{c}(U)
are smooth differential forms whose weights satisfy
\operatorname{wt}(\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(\eta)\leq-\nu
, then

f_{\rho}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta\,,

and, in particular

\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\int_{U}f_{\rho}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta\,.

Theorem
1.16
follows immediately from Theorem
1.20
by Stokes’ theorem. As discussed in Remark
1.18
there is
an extension of this result to
p\leq\nu
, see
[
KMX
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some background material and fix notation and conventions.

2.1.
Carnot groups

We recommend the survey
[
LD17
]
for a discussion of the material here.

Without explicit mention we
will equip all Lie groups with Haar measure. As is customary, we will conflate two standard definitions of the Lie algebra of a Lie group
G
– the tangent space at the identity
T_{e}G
and the space of left invariant vector fields; likewise we will conflate a linear subspace of
\mathfrak{g}
with the corresponding left invariant subbundle of the tangent bundle
TG
. We will typically use the same notation for a Lie group homomorphism
G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
and the induced homomorphism of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Carnot group
is a simply connected Lie group
G
together with a decomposition of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
into linear subspaces

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus V_{s}

such that:

•

[V_{i},V_{j}]\subset V_{i+j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq s
.

•

The
horizontal space (or first layer)

V_{1}
generates
\mathfrak{g}
.

A graded homomorphism between Carnot groups
(G,\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j})
,
(G^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\oplus_{k=1}^{s^{\prime}}V_{k}^{\prime})
is a Lie group homomorphism
G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
such that the induced homomorphism of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
carries
V_{j}
into
V^{\prime}_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq s
. We will use
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
to denote the group of graded automorphisms of a Carnot group.

Properties of a Carnot group
(G,\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j}V_{j})
:

•

G
is nilpotent, and
V_{j+1}=[V_{1},V_{j}]
for all
1\leq j\leq s
.

•

There is a
1
-parameter group of Carnot group automorphisms
\{\delta_{r}:G\rightarrow G\}
such that
\delta_{r}
scales
V_{j}
by the factor
r^{j}
. We refer to these automorphisms as (Carnot) dilations.

•

Without explicit mention, we will assume that the first layer
V_{1}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is equipped with an inner product, and extend this to a left invariant Riemannian metric on the left invariant subbundle of
TG
determined by
V_{1}
.

•

The Carnot-Caratheodory distance
d_{CC}
between
x_{0},x_{1}\in G
is the infimal length of a
C^{1}
-path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow G
with
\gamma(i)=x_{i}
,
\gamma^{\prime}(t)\in V_{1}
for all
t\in[0,1]
, and the length is taken with respect to the given Riemannian metric on
V_{1}
.

•

The topology induced by the metric
d_{CC}
is the same as the topology coming from
G
as a manifold.

•

d_{CC}
is invariant under left translation, and scaled by dilations:

d_{CC}(\delta_{r}(x),\delta_{r}(x^{\prime}))=rd_{CC}(x,x^{\prime})\,.

•

The
homogeneous dimension
of
G
is
\nu:=\sum_{j}j\dim V_{j}
; this is the Hausdorff dimension of
(G,d_{CC})
, and there is a constant
C\neq 0
such that for every
x\in G
,
r\in[0,\infty)
, the
\nu
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the
r
-ball satisfies:

\mathcal{H}^{\nu}(B(x,r))=Cr^{\nu}\,.

Definition 2.2
.

The
n^{th}
real Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{n}
is the Carnot group with graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
V_{2}=\mathbb{R}
, and the bracket
X,Y\in V_{1}
is given by
[X,Y]=-\omega(X,Y)\in V_{2}
; here
\omega=\sum_{i}dx_{2i-1}\wedge dx_{2i}
is the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

The
n^{th}
complex Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the complexification of
\mathbb{H}_{n}
; its graded Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathfrak{h}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}
; more concretely, it has the grading
\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}=V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus V_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
, where
V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}=V_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}^{2n}
,
V_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}=V_{2}\otimes\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{C}
, and for
X,Y\in V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}
the bracket is given by
[X,Y]=-\omega^{\mathbb{C}}(X,Y)\in\mathbb{C}=V_{2}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\omega^{\mathbb{C}}
is the extension of
\omega
to a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear form
V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\times V_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

2.2.
Sobolev mappings

Starting from the mid-90s the theory of Sobolev spaces in metric measure spaces advanced rapidly, so that basic results were established in great generality. However in this paper we only need results in the Carnot group case, where they were (almost all) already known, and in most cases technically simpler to establish. We refer the reader to
[
Pan89
,
KR95
,
MM95
,
Hei01
,
HK00
,
Vod07b
]
.

Let
G
,
G^{\prime}
be Carnot groups, where
G
has homogeneous dimension
\nu
and grading
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j}V_{j}
.

Definition 2.3
.

If
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
U\subset G
is open, then
W^{1,q}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
is the set of elements
u\in L^{q}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
such that for every horizontal left invariant vector field
X\in V_{1}
, distributional directional derivative
Xu
belongs to
L^{q}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
. We let
|D_{h}u|\in L^{q}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
be the pointwise supremum of the directional derivatives
Xu
, where
X\in V_{1}
and
|X|=1
.

We remark that a variety of other definitions of Sobolev spaces have been developed (see for instance
[
Haj96
,
Che99
,
Sha00
]
) and they are all equivalent to Definition
2.3
in the case of Carnot groups equipped with Haar measure.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
GN96
, Theorem 1.11]

There is a constant
C_{q}<\infty
with the following property. If
x\in G
,
r>0
,
u\in W^{1,q}(B(x,r))
with
q>\nu

then
u
has a continuous representative, and it satisfies

(2.5)

|u(y_{1})-u(y_{2})|\leq C_{q}r^{\frac{\nu}{s}}d(y_{1},y_{2})^{1-\frac{\nu}{q}}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|D_{h}u|^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in B(x,r)
, where
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int
denotes the average.

Proof.

Indeed it follows from
[
GN96
, Theorem 1.11 and (3.19)]
that the assertion holds for
x=e
and
r=1
. The general case
follows by left-translation and scaling.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
U\subset G
is open.
Then a measurable mapping
f:U\rightarrow G^{\prime}
is in
W^{1,q}_{\operatorname{loc}}
if there exists
g\in L^{q}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)
such that
for every
z\in G^{\prime}
, the composition
d_{z}\circ f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belongs to
W^{1,q}_{\operatorname{loc}}(U)

and its horizontal differential satisfies
|D_{h}(d_{z}\circ f)|\leq g
almost everywhere.
Here
d_{z}:=d_{CC}(z,\cdot)
denotes the distance function from
z
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
q>\nu
.
There is a constant
C_{q}
with the following property.
If
f\in W^{1,q}(B(x,r);G^{\prime})
and if
g
is as in Definition
2.6

then
f
has a continuous representative and it satisfies

(2.8)

d_{CC}(f(y),f(x))\leq C_{q}\,r\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|g|^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

Proof.

The main point is to show that
f
has a continuous representative.
The estimate (
2.8
) then
follows from Lemma
2.4
and
Definition
2.6
since
d_{CC}(f(y),f(x))=(d_{f(x)}\circ f)(y)
.

To see that
f
has a continuous representative, let
D
be a countable dense subset
D\subset G^{\prime}
.
Then there exists a null set
N\subset U
such that (
2.5
) holds for the composition
d_{z}\circ f
for every
z\in D

and every
y_{1},y_{2}\in B(x,r)\setminus N
. In particular, the collection
\{d_{z}\circ f\}_{z\in D}
is uniformly Hölder continuous on
B(x,r)\setminus N
.
Since
B(x,r)\setminus N
is dense in
B(x,r)
and
G^{\prime}
is complete, there
exists a unique continuous extension
\bar{f}:B(x,r)\to G^{\prime}
of

f\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{B\setminus N}
which agrees with
f
almost everywhere in
B
.
∎

Let
U\subset G
be open, let
q>\nu
and let
f\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(U;G^{\prime})
. Then
it follows from Lemma
2.7
that
f
has a continuous representative. To see this, write
U
as a countable union of open balls

B_{i}
such that their closure
\overline{B}_{i}
are contained in
U
. By Lemma

there exist continuous functions
\overline{f}_{i}:B_{i}\to G^{\prime}

such that
\overline{f}_{i}=f
a.e. in

. Since the functions

are continuous it follows that
\overline{f}_{i}=\overline{f}_{j}
on

if

.
Thus there exists a continuous function
\overline{f}:U\to G^{\prime}
with
f\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{B_{i}}=\overline{f}_{i}
. In particular
\overline{f}=f
almost everywhere.

Fix a mapping
f:G\supset U\rightarrow G^{\prime}
, where
U\subset G
is open. For all

,

, define

f_{x}=\ell_{f(x)^{-1}}\circ f\circ\ell_{x}\,,\qquad f_{x,r}=\delta_{r^{-1}}\circ f_{x}\circ\delta_{r}\,,

where
\ell_{h}

denote left translation by

.

such that

. We refer to

, as well as the associated homomorphism of graded Lie algebras

of

at

. We use

to denote both objects

,

Theorem 2.10
.

(
[
Vod03
, Corollary 1]
; see also
[

,

If

is a

2.3.
Quasiconformal and quasisymmetric mappings

We let
G
,
G^{\prime}
denote Carnot groups as before, where
G
has homogeneous dimension
\nu>1
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a homeomorphism between metric spaces, and
\eta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a homeomorphism. For
x\in X
, let

H_{f}(x):=\limsup_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sup\{d(f(x),f(y))\;|\;d(x,y)\leq r\}}{\inf\{d(f(x),f(y))\;|\;d(x,y)\geq r\}}\,,

and
H_{f}:=\sup_{x}H_{f}(x)
.
Then
f
is
quasiconformal
if
H_{f}<\infty
, and
f
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric
if for any distinct points
p,x,y\in X
,

\frac{d(f(p),f(x))}{d(f(p),f(y))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d(p,x)}{d(p,y)}\right)\,.

If
f
is an
\eta
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism then its inverse is an
\tilde{\eta}
-quasisymmetric homeomorphism
with
\tilde{\eta}(t)=\frac{1}{\eta^{-1}(t^{-1})}
, and if
f
is an
\eta_{f}
quasisymmetric homeomorphism and

is an
\eta_{g}
quasisymmetric homeomorphism
then
f\circ g
is an
\eta_{f}\circ\eta_{g}
quasisymmetric homeomorphism, see
[
Hei01
, Proposition 10.6]
.

We will need the following properties of quasiconformal and quasisymmetric homeomorphisms:

Theorem 2.12

(See
[
HK95
,
HK98
,
Hei01
]
)
.

Let
f:G\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
be a homeomorphism. Then

(1)

If
f
is quasiconformal, then it is locally
\eta
-quasisymmetric for some
\eta=\eta(H_{f})
, i.e. for every

-quasisymmetric homeomorphism.

(2)

Every global quasiconformal homeomorphism

is
\eta=\eta(H_{f})
-quasisymmetric.

(3)

If
f
is quasiconformal, then

for some

(4)

If

For (1) and (2) see Remark 1.8(b) and Theorem 1.7 in

]
.

[

]

is a homeomorphism, so is

for every

. If

is a point of Pansu differentiability, then

uniformly on compact sets, and therefore its local degree near

is constant for small

, which is the sign of the determinant of

.

3.
Differential forms on Carnot groups

The purpose of this section is to discuss two key notions for the de Rham complex on Carnot groups: the weight of a differential form (which reflects the action of the dilation map
\delta_{r}
) and the Pansu pullback; this sets the stage for the Pullback Theorem in the next section, which asserts that the distributional exterior derivative commutes with Pansu pullback, but only if the form which is pulled back and the test form satisfy certain restriction on their weights.

Let
G
be a Carnot group of step
s
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
We retain the notation from before:

•

N
and

4.
The Pullback Theorem

In this section we present the key technical result in this paper – the Pullback Theorem – and deduce several corollaries.

We begin by pointing out that in general, Pansu pullback does not commute with the exterior derivative, even for smooth contact diffeomorphisms.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{H}\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{H}
be a smooth contact diffeomorphism between open subsets of the Heisenberg group. If for some
k\in\{0,1,2\}
the Pansu pullback
f_{P}^{*}:\Omega^{k}(U^{\prime})\rightarrow\Omega^{k}(U)
commutes with the exterior derivative, then
f
preserves the foliation by cosets of the center, i.e.
Df(V_{2})=V_{2}
.

Proof.

We will show that for every
u\in\Omega^{0}(U^{\prime})
such that

X_{3}u=0
we have
X_{3}(u\circ f)=0
. This implies
Df(V_{2})=V_{2}
.

Take
k=0
. Then since
f_{P}^{*}u:=u\circ f
, by assumption

X_{3}(u\circ f)=(d(u\circ f))(X_{3})=(f_{P}^{*}du)(X_{3})=0

because
D_{P}f(x)(X_{3})\in V_{2}
since
D_{P}f(x)
is a graded homomorphism.

Now suppose
k=1
. Let
\omega:=du
. Then
d\omega=0
, so by assumption we have
df_{P}^{*}\omega=0
. Hence
f_{P}^{*}\omega=d\hat{u}
for some
\hat{u}\in\Omega^{0}(U)
with
X_{3}\hat{u}=0
. We have
d\hat{u}\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{V_{1}}=d(u\circ f)\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{V_{1}}
because Pansu pullback agrees with ordinary pullback on
V_{1}
. It follows that
d(\hat{u}-u\circ f)\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{V_{1}}=0
, which implies that
\hat{u}-u\circ f
is constant. Therefore
X_{3}(u\circ f)=X_{3}\hat{u}=0
.

Take
k=2
. Let
\omega:=u\,\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2}
, so
d\omega=(X_{3}u)\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{3}=0
. Then
f_{P}^{*}\omega=(u\circ f)\,\alpha\,\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2}
, where
\alpha:=\det[D_{P}f\mbox{\Large$|$\normalsize}_{V_{1}}]
(which is nowhere zero since
f
is a diffeomorphism), so by assumption we have

0=df_{P}^{*}\omega=[X_{3}((u\circ f)\alpha)]\,\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{2}\wedge\theta_{3}\,

and therefore
(X_{3}\alpha)\cdot(u\circ f)+\alpha\,(X_{3}(u\circ f))=0
. This also holds if we replace
u
by
u+c
for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
, forcing
X_{3}(u\circ f)=0
.
∎

Theorem 4.2

(Pullback theorem)
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be Carnot groups, where
G
has dimension
N
and homogeneous dimension
\nu
, and suppose
f:G\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
is a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
mapping between open sets for some
p>\nu
.
Suppose
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(U^{\prime})
is a continuous form with continuous distributional exterior derivative
d\omega
, suppose
\eta\in\Omega^{N-k-1}_{c}(U)
is smooth, and

(4.3)

\operatorname{wt}(\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(d\eta)\leq-\nu\,,\quad\operatorname{wt}(d\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(\eta)\leq-\nu\,.

Then

(4.4)

\int_{U}(f_{P}^{*}d\omega)\wedge\eta+(-1)^{k}\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge d\eta=0\,.

If one of the forms in (
4.3
) vanishes, then we interpret its weight as

, so that the corresponding inequality holds.

Note that by the Sobolev embedding theorem for Carnot groups, a

mapping is continuous when

, see Lemma

.

We will prove Theorem

by a new approximation of

by smooth maps

which respects the structure of the Carnot group.
The approximation is introduced in Section
5
and the proof of Theorem
4.2

is carried out in Section

We will usually use the following special case of Theorem

:

Theorem 4.5

(Pullback Theorem (special case))
.

Let

and

be as in Theorem

.
Suppose

and that
\alpha
and
\beta
are closed left invariant forms
which satisfy

\deg\alpha+\deg\beta=N-1\quad\hbox{and}\quad\operatorname{wt}(\alpha)+\operatorname{wt}(\beta)\leq-\nu+1.

\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}(\alpha)\wedge d(\varphi\beta)=0.

Proof.

Letting

, we have

. Thus

. Hence the assertion follows from

∎

For a closed form

5.

Lemma 7.3
.

Suppose

For subsets

. Note that the center of

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}

We define a linear subspace

. Note that

For

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\oplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{I}(\Phi(V_{1i}))=

\displaystyle\oplus_{i}\oplus_{j}\mathcal{I}(\pi_{j}(\Phi(V_{1i})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oplus_{j}\oplus_{i}\mathcal{I}(\pi_{j}(\Phi(V_{1i})))\,.

, there is a unique

for

.
Since
\phi
is a graded isomorphism,

We now give a sketch of an alternative approach to rigidity of products.

8.1.

We first recall some facts and notation pertaining to complex structures and complexification (see

Lemma 8.9
.

Assume that
G
is a Carnot group with homogeneous dimension
\nu
.
Assume that

is in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(U,\mathbb{C})
with

. Then for every horizontal
left invariant vectorfield
X
the distributional derivative
Xu
agrees with
D_{P}uX
, i.e.

\int_{G}D_{P}u(x)X(x)\,\,\eta(x)\,d\mu(x)=-\int_{G}u\,(X\eta)\,d\mu

for any
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
and define

\omega(t)=\int_{G}u(x\exp tX)\eta(x)\,d\mu(x).

Since

is a.e. Pansu differentiable by Theorem

, we have

t^{-1}[u(x\exp tX)-u(x)]\to D_{P}u(x)X

as

for a.e.

Moreover by the Sobolev embedding theorem (see Lemma

) this difference quotient
is bounded by the local maximal function of the horizontal derivative and hence by
a fixed

function (cf. (
6.8
) and (
6.9
)). Thus the dominated convergence theorem implies that

\omega^{\prime}(0)=\int_{G}D_{P}u(x)X(x)\,\,\eta(x)\,d\mu(x).

Using the change of variables

and the bi-invariance of

Differentiation with respect to

Comparing the two expressions for

8.3.
Global quasiconformal homeomorphisms of complexified Carnot groups

We now study global quasiconformal homeomorphisms of complexified Carnot groups.
For simplicity we first state the result for the first complex Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem 8.11
.

(1)

Any
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric, where
\eta=\eta(K)
.

(2)

Any
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}

L

L

(3)

If

(4)

Any holomorphic qc homeomorphism
\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}
is a composition of a left translation and a complex graded automorphism.

(5)

The group of qc homeomorphisms
\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{\mathbb{C}}
is generated by left translations, complex graded automorphisms, and complex conjugation.

In the proof we will need a lemma characterizing affine maps.

Definition 8.12
.

Let
\Phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map. A
C^{1}
map
f:G\rightarrow G
is a
\Phi
-map
if
Df(p)(X)=\Phi(X)(f(p))
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
p\in G
. Here we are identifying tangent spaces with the Lie algebra via left translation.

Lemma 8.13
.

Let
f:G\rightarrow G
be a
\Phi
-map. Then
f
is affine, i.e. a composition of a left translations with a homomorphism. In particular
\Phi
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

Note that
\Phi
-maps are preserved by left translation: if
f
is a
\Phi
-map, then so is
\ell_{g_{1}}\circ f\circ\ell_{g_{2}}
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
. Also, if two
\Phi
-maps
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
agree at some point, then they coincide. To see this, let
W=\{g\in G\mid f_{1}(g)=f_{2}(g)\}
. Then
W
is closed, and if
g
lies in
W
, then so does the integral curve

of the left invariant vector field
X
, by uniqueness of integral curves of the smooth vector field
\Phi(X)
. Hence
W
is also open, and therefore
W=G
by the connectedness of
G
.

Suppose
f
is an
\Phi
-map with
f(e)=e
. Then for every
g\in G

(f\circ\ell_{g})(e)=f(g)=f(g)\cdot f(e)=(\ell_{f(g)}\circ f)(e)\,,

so
f\circ\ell_{g}\equiv\ell_{f(g)}\circ f
. Hence for all

so

is a homomorphism of Lie groups, and
\Phi
is its derivative.
∎

8.4.
Flexibility of the complex Heisenberg groups

We exhibit an abundance of global contact diffeomorphisms
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and in particular, an abundance of local contact diffeomorphisms. Note that the local assertion follows from the work of Ottazzi-Warhurst, since the complex Heisenberg groups are nonrigid in the sense of
[

We let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{2n+1}
be a graded basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{2n}\in V_{1}
,
X_{2n+1}\in V_{2}
, and
[X_{j},X_{j+n}]=-X_{2n+1}

\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{2n+1}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{H}_{n})

. Then

descends to a left invariant

\overline{d\theta_{2n+1}}
on the abelianization
\mathbb{H}_{n}/[\mathbb{H}_{n},\mathbb{H}_{n}]

(\mathbb{H}_{n}/[\mathbb{H}_{n},\mathbb{H}_{n}],\overline{d\theta_{2n+1}})
with
(\mathbb{R}^{2n},\omega_{n})
, where
\omega_{n}=\sum_{j}dx_{j}\wedge dx_{j+n}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
is the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Now consider the complexification of
\mathbb{H}_{n}
, i.e. the complex Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
, whose Lie algebra we identify with the complexification
\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
\{X_{j},iX_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq 2n+1}
is a graded basis for
\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{R}

\{\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq 2n+1}
be the dual basis. Now
\{\zeta_{j}:=\alpha_{j}+i\beta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq 2n+1}
is the corresponding basis of left invariant
(1,0)
-forms, and
d\zeta_{2n+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{j}\wedge\zeta_{j+n}
. The form
d\zeta_{2n+1}
descends to a form
\widehat{d\zeta_{2n+1}}
on the abelianization
and we may identify
(\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}/[\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}],\widehat{\zeta_{2n+1}})

(\mathbb{C}^{2n},\omega_{n}^{\mathbb{C}})

\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.

Lemma 8.15
.

Pick
n\geq 1
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{H}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
\pi:\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n}
denote the abelianization homomorphisms.

(1)

If
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic diffeomorphism, i.e.
f^{*}\omega_{n}=\omega_{n}
, then there is a contact diffeomorphism
\hat{\phi}:\mathbb{H}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{n}
lifting
\phi
, i.e.
\pi\circ\hat{\phi}=\phi\circ\pi
. Morever
\hat{\phi}
is unique up to post-composition with central translation.

(2)

If
\phi:\mathbb{C}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n}
is a diffeomorphism which preserves
\omega_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
, then there is a contact diffeomorphism
\hat{\phi}:\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
lifting
\phi
, i.e.
\pi\circ\hat{\phi}=\phi\circ\pi
. Morever
\hat{\phi}
is unique up to post-composition with central translation.

Proof.

The homomorphisms
\pi:\mathbb{H}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
,
\pi:\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n}
define principal bundles with structure groups
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
respectively. Also,
\theta_{2n+1}
,
\theta_{2n+1}^{\mathbb{C}}
are connection

\omega_{n}
,
\omega_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
are the curvature forms on the two bundles, respectively. Hence (1) and (2) follow from Lemma

.
∎

We recall a standard fact about connections on principal bundles with abelian structure group:

Lemma 8.16
.

Pick
k\geq 1
. For
j\in\{1,2\}
let
\pi_{j}:P_{j}\rightarrow B_{j}
be a principal
\mathbb{R}^{k}
-bundle with connection form
\theta_{j}\in\Omega^{1}(P_{j};\mathbb{R}^{k})
and curvature form
\omega_{j}\in\Omega^{2}(B_{j};\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Assume that
H^{1}(B_{1},\mathbb{R})=\{0\}
.

If
\phi:B_{1}\rightarrow B_{2}
is a smooth map such that
\phi^{*}\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}
, then there is a lift
\hat{\phi}:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
to a connection preserving principal bundle mapping; moreover if
B_{1}
is connected then
\hat{\phi}
is unique up to (composition with) the action of the structure group
P_{1}\curvearrowleft\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Proof.

By replacing
\pi_{2}:P_{2}\rightarrow B_{2}
with the pullback bundle
\phi^{*}\pi_{2}:\phi^{*}P_{2}\rightarrow B_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
with the pullback connection
\phi^{*}\theta_{2}
, we may assume that
B_{2}=B_{1}
and
\phi=\operatorname{id}_{B_{1}}
. There is a principal bundle equivalence
\psi:P_{1}\rightarrow P_{2}
over
\operatorname{id}_{B_{1}}
because
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is contractible. Since
\psi^{*}\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{1}
are both connection forms for
\pi_{1}:P_{1}\rightarrow B_{1}
, the difference
\psi^{*}\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}
descends to a
1
-form
\bar{\theta}\in\Omega^{1}(B_{1};\mathbb{R}^{k})
, where
d\bar{\theta}=\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}=0
. Given a smooth map

, we may compose
\psi
with the corresponding shear, i.e.
\psi^{\prime}(p)=\psi(p)\cdot u(\pi_{1}(p))
. Then
(\psi^{\prime})^{*}\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}
descends to
\bar{\theta}^{\prime}=\bar{\theta}+du
. Using the fact that

, the closed form

is exact, i.e. we may choose

such that

, and

is unique up to a constant function since

is connected.
∎

We may readily produce symplectic shears as follows.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function which depends only on the first
n
-coordinates, i.e.
\partial_{j}u=0
for
n+1\leq j\leq 2n
. Let
X_{u}
be the Hamiltonian vector field of
u
, i.e.
\omega_{n}(X_{u},\cdot)=du(\cdot)
. Then
X_{u}
is tangent to the vertical
n
-planes and invariant under every translation
x\mapsto x+v
for
v\in\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and is therefore complete. The time
1
map
\Phi^{1}:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
of the Hamiltonian flow is a symplectic diffeomorphism.

Similarly, if
u:\mathbb{C}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2n}
is a holomorphic function which depends only on the first
n
-coordinates, then the time
1
map of the associated holomorphic Hamiltonian vector field is a symplectic biholomorphic mapping with respect to the holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}dz_{j}\wedge dz_{j+n}
.

Appendix A
Equiregular manifolds

In this appendix we will discuss versions of the Approximation Theorem and Pullback Theorems for Sobolev mappings between equiregular subriemannian manifolds. We will use (a variation on) the setup from Gromov’s paper, see
[
Gro96
, Section 1.4]
,
[
Vod07a
]
. We refer the reader to these papers for more detail.

We will be using subriemannian manifolds with an adapted frame. These are tuples
(M,V_{1},g,\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}\})
where:

•

M
is a smooth
N
-manifold.

•

V_{1}\subset TM
is an equiregular subbundle, so we have a filtration of
TM
by smooth subbundles

V_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq V_{s}=TM

where
V_{j}
is spanned by brackets of smooth sections of
V_{1}
and
V_{j-1}
for
j\geq 2
, and
s\geq 1
.

•

g
is a smooth Riemannian metric.

•

X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
is a smooth
g
-orthonormal frame.

•

There is a “degree” function

\deg:\{1,\ldots,N\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,s\}

such that if
W_{j}
is the subbundle spanned by
\{X_{i}\mid\deg i=j\}
, then
V_{j}:=\oplus^{\perp}_{i\leq j}W_{i}=\operatorname{span}\{X_{i}\mid\deg i\leq j\}
.

In addition we will use the following objects which are associated with the above structure:

•

The coframe
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
dual to
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
.

•

For
r\in(0,\infty)
, the anisotropically rescaled frame
\{X_{j}^{r}\}
where
X_{j}^{r}:=r^{\deg j}X_{j}
.

•

For every
x\in M
, the graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{x}:=\oplus_{j}(V_{j})_{x}/(V_{j-1})_{x}
with bracket induced by the Lie bracket. We identify
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
with
T_{x}M
using the isomorphism

(A.1)

(W_{j})_{x}\simeq(V_{j})_{x}/(V_{j-1})_{x}

induced by the inclusion
(W_{j})_{x}\rightarrow(V_{j})_{x}
.

•

The associated Carnot-Caratheodory metric
d_{CC}
, with Hausdorff dimension
\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{s}j\dim W_{j}
.

•

The (non-Riemannian) connection
\nabla
on
TM
for which the
X_{j}
s are parallel vector fields.

•

The exponential map
\exp:TM\supset U\rightarrow M
associated with
\nabla
, which sends
v\in TM
to the
\gamma_{v}(1)
, where
\gamma_{v}
is the
\nabla
-geodesic with initial velocity
v
.

A key ingredient will be Gromov’s blow-up theorem for subriemannian geometries, which gives a quantitative comparison between the subriemannian structure at a small scale near
x\in M
, and the nilpotent tangent cone
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
.

Theorem A.2

(
[
Gro96
, Section 1.4]
,
[
Vod07a
]
)
.

If
U\subset M
is an open subset with compact closure in
M
, then there exists
\rho_{0}>0
, and for
x\in U
a family of smooth embeddings

\{\delta_{\rho}^{x}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\supset B(0,r_{\rho})\rightarrow M\}_{\rho<\rho_{0}}

and smooth vector fields
\{\hat{X}_{j}^{x,\rho}\}_{1\leq j\leq N}
on
B(0,r_{\rho})
, for
\rho\in[0,\rho_{0})
, where:

(1)

r_{\rho}\rightarrow\infty
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
.

(2)

The family
\{\delta_{\rho}^{x}\}
depends continuously on
x
and
\rho
in the
C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-topology.

(3)

\delta_{\rho}^{x}(0)=x

(4)

(\delta_{\rho}^{x})^{*}X_{j}^{\rho}=\hat{X}_{j}^{x,\rho}
.

(5)

\hat{X}_{j}^{x,\rho}\rightarrow\hat{X}_{j}^{x,0}
in
C^{k}_{\operatorname{loc}}
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
, uniformly on compact subsets in
x
, for every
k
.

(6)

The vector fields
\{\hat{X}_{j}^{x,0}\}
span a graded Lie algebra of vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with grading given by degree

\oplus_{j}\operatorname{span}\{\hat{X}_{i}^{x,0}\mid\deg i=j\}\,;

moreover there is a graded isomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
to
\operatorname{span}\{\hat{X}_{j}^{x,0}\}
which sends
X_{j}(x)\mapsto\hat{X}_{j}^{x,0}
for
\deg j=1
.

(7)

The vector fields
\{\hat{X}_{j}^{x,0}\}
are given by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula (and in particular are polynomial vector fields); equivalently, the exponential map associated with
\{\hat{X}_{j}^{x,0}\}
is the identity map
\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(8)

If
\hat{g}^{x}_{\rho}
is the Riemannian metric on
B(0,r_{\rho})
for which
\{\hat{X}^{x,\rho}_{j}\}
is orthonormal, and
\hat{d}_{CC}^{x,\rho}
is the corresponding Carnot-Caratheodory metric, then
\hat{d}_{CC}^{x,\rho}\rightarrow\hat{d}_{CC}^{x,0}
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
uniformly on compact subsets, uniformly in
x
.

The weight of a subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
is defined to be
\operatorname{wt}I:=-\sum_{i\in I}\deg i
. A differential form
\alpha=\sum_{I}a_{I}\theta_{I}
has weight
w
(weight
\leq w
) if
a_{I}=0
unless
\operatorname{wt}(I)=w
(
\operatorname{wt}I\leq w
); here
\theta_{I}:=\Lambda_{i\in I}\theta_{i}
.

Next we define the center of mass for probability measures whose support has small diameter, imitating the construction for Carnot groups.

Let
K\subset M
be a compact subset, and let
r_{K}>0
be such that for every
x\in K
, the
\nabla
-exponential map
\exp_{x}
is defined on
B_{g}(0,r_{K})\subset T_{x}M
and is a diffeomorphism onto its image, with inverse
\log_{x}
. If
\nu
is a compactly supported probability measure on
M
, and for some
x\in K
we have
\operatorname{spt}(\nu)\subset\exp_{x}(B_{g}(0,r_{K}))
, then
(\log_{x})_{*}\nu
is a well-defined probability measure on
T_{x}M
, and we define
C_{\nu}(x)
to be its center of mass, i.e.

C_{\nu}(x):=\int_{M}\log_{x}\;d\nu=\int_{T_{x}M}y\;d((\log_{x})_{*}\nu)(y)\,.

We have:

Lemma A.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{K}^{\prime}=r_{K}^{\prime}(\varepsilon,K)\in(0,r_{K})
such that if
\operatorname{spt}\nu\subset K
,
d(\operatorname{spt}\nu,M\setminus K)>\varepsilon
, and
\operatorname{diam}_{g}(\operatorname{spt}\nu)<r_{K}^{\prime}
then the set

\{x\in K\mid\operatorname{spt}\nu\subset B(x,10r_{K}^{\prime})\}\,,

contains a unique element
\operatorname{com}(\nu)
such that
C_{\nu}(\operatorname{com}(\nu))=0
.

By working locally in charts and rescaling, or by using Theorem
A.2
, Lemma
A.3
reduces to a perturbation of the Carnot group case; then the argument from Lemma
5.1
, which is robust under perturbation, may be applied.

Now let
(M^{\prime},V_{1}^{\prime},g^{\prime},\{X_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,X_{N^{\prime}}^{\prime}\})
be a second subriemannian manifold; we will denote the associated data and objects with primes. Let
f:M\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}
be a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping between open subsets, where
p
is strictly larger than the homogeneous dimension of
(M,V_{1})
. Here
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mappings are defined as in the Carnot group case, except that instead of using horizontal left invariant vector fields to define the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(M)
, one uses linear combinations of the vector fields
\{X_{i}\mid\deg i=1\}
.

Next we define a mollification procedure. Choose a smooth, rotationally invariant probability measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
\operatorname{spt}\sigma\subset B(0,1)
, and for every
x\in M
,
\rho\in(0,\infty)
we let
\sigma_{x,\rho}
be the pushforward of
\sigma
under the linear isomorphism
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow T_{x}M
which sends (the standard basis vector)
e_{j}
to
X_{j}^{\rho}(x)=\rho^{\deg j}X_{j}(x)
. If
K_{0}\subset U
is a compact subset, then taking
K_{1}
compact with
K_{0}\subset\operatorname{Int}K_{1}\subset U
, and
K_{1}^{\prime}:=f(K_{1})\subset U^{\prime}
, there is a
\rho_{0}>0
such that if
\rho<\rho_{0}
then applying Lemma
A.3
with
K:=K_{1}^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(f,\rho_{0})
, the measure
f_{*}\sigma_{x,\rho}
will have a well-defined center of mass for every
x\in K_{0}
; we define
f_{\rho}(x):=\operatorname{com}(f_{*}\sigma_{x,\rho})
.

We define the Pansu pullback of a differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{*}(U^{\prime})
by
f_{P}^{*}\omega(x):=(D_{P}f)(x)^{*}\omega(f(x))
, where

D_{P}f(x):T_{x}M\simeq\mathfrak{g}_{x}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}_{f(x)}\simeq T_{f(x)}M^{\prime}

is the Pansu differential, which for a.e.
x\in U
is a graded homomorphism between graded Lie algebras
[
Vod07a
]
.

With these definitions, we have:

Theorem A.4

(Approximation Theorem for equiregular manifolds with an adapted frame)
.

Let
f:M\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}
be a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
-mapping for some
p>\nu
.
Suppose
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(U^{\prime})
,
\eta\in\Omega^{N-k}_{c}(U)
are forms with continuous coefficients, such that
\operatorname{wt}(\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(\eta)\leq-\nu
. Then

(A.5)

f_{\rho}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta

where
f_{\rho}
is the mollified map defined above.
Since
\eta
has compact support, it follows that

(A.6)

\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta=\lim_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\int_{U}f_{\rho}^{*}\omega\wedge\eta\,.

We only give a brief indication of the proof because it is very similar to the Carnot group case. To prove Theorem
A.4
, we use the Dominated Convergence Theorem, following the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

To verify pointwise convergence, one uses the mappings

\hat{f}^{x,\rho}:=(\delta_{\rho}^{{}^{\prime}f(x)})^{-1}\circ f\circ\delta_{\rho}^{x}\,,

where
\delta_{\rho}^{x}
,
\delta_{\rho}^{{}^{\prime}f(x)}
are obtained by applying Theorem
A.2
to
M
and
M^{\prime}
, respectively.
By
[
Vod07a
]
if
x\in M
is a point of differentiability of
f
, then
\hat{f}^{x,\rho}\rightarrow D_{P}f(x)
uniformly on compact sets, where
D_{P}f(x):\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N^{\prime}}
; combining this with Theorem
A.2
, one obtains pointwise convergence as in the Carnot group case.

Letting
U
be an open set with compact closure containing
\operatorname{spt}\eta
, we use the same integrable majorant as in the Carnot group case (see (
6.8
)). The proofs of integrability and majorization follow along the same lines, using Theorem
A.2
to get the estimate (

Using this local approximation theorem we can easily extend the pullback theorem to equiregular manifolds.
In fact, for the validity of the pullback theorem we do not need global adapted frames. We say that a tuple
(M,V_{1},g)
is
an equiregular subriemannian manifold if

•

M
is a smooth
N
-manifold.

•

V_{1}\subset TM
is an equiregular subbundle, so we have a filtration of
TM
by smooth subbundles

\{0\}=V_{0}\subsetneq V_{1}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq V_{s}=TM

where
V_{j}
is spanned by brackets of smooth sections of
V_{1}
and
V_{j-1}
for
j\geq 2
, and
s\geq 1
.

•

g
is a smooth Riemannian metric.

As before the homogeneous dimension of
M
is defined by
\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{s}j\dim(V_{j}/V_{j-1})
.
For each
x\in M
there exists an open neighbourhood
U_{x}
such that
(U_{x},V_{1}|_{U_{x}},g|_{U_{x}})
in addition admits an
adapted frame as above.

For a continuous differential form
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
we define a global notion of weight as follows.
For a one-form
\omega\in\Omega^{1}
with
\omega|_{V_{1}}\equiv 0
and
\omega\not\equiv 0
we define

(A.7)

\operatorname{wt}(\omega):=-\max\{j:\omega|_{V_{j}}\equiv 0\}-1.

If
\omega|_{V_{1}}\not\equiv 0
we set
\operatorname{wt}(\omega)=-1
.
We then define

(A.8)

\Omega^{k,\leq w}(M):=\operatorname{span}\{\omega_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\omega_{k}:\omega_{j}\in\Omega^{1}(M),\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{wt}(\omega_{j})\leq w\}

and for
\omega\not\equiv 0
we set

(A.9)

\operatorname{wt}(\omega)=\operatorname{wt}(\omega,M):=\min\{w:\omega\in\Omega^{k,\leq w}(M)\}.

It is easy to see that if
U\subset M
admits an adapted frame then

\operatorname{wt}(\omega,U)
agrees with the definition of weight given above which was based on the adapted frame.
In particular the weight is independent of the choice of frame.

Theorem A.10

(Pullback theorem for equiregular manifolds)
.

Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be equiregular manifolds, where
M
has dimension
N
and homogeneous dimension
\nu
, and suppose
f:M\supset U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\subset M^{\prime}
is a
W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}
mapping between open sets for some
p>\nu
.
Suppose
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(U^{\prime})
is a continuous form with continuous distributional exterior derivative
d\omega
, suppose
\eta\in\Omega^{N-k-1}_{c}(U)
is smooth, and

(A.11)

\operatorname{wt}(\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(d\eta)\leq-\nu\,,\quad\operatorname{wt}(d\omega)+\operatorname{wt}(\eta)\leq-\nu\,.

Then

(A.12)

\int_{U}(f_{P}^{*}d\omega)\wedge\eta+(-1)^{k}\int_{U}f_{P}^{*}\omega\wedge d\eta=0\,.

Here we use the convention that an inequality in (
A.11
) holds if at least one of the forms involved vanishes.

Proof.

Since the proof is analogous to the proof for Carnot groups we just provide a sketch of the argument.
The main point is to localize. By a partition of unity it suffices to show that for each
x\in U
there exists an open neighborhood
U_{x}
such that
(
4.4
) holds for all smooth
\eta\in\Omega^{N-k-1}_{c}(U_{x})
which satisfy (
A.11
).
Since
p>\nu
the map
f
is continuous. Thus we may choose
U_{x}
so small that there exist adapted frames in
U_{x}
and
U^{\prime}_{f(x)}\supset f(U_{x})
.
Let
\tilde{U}\subset U_{x}
be open and compactly contained in
U_{x}
such that
\eta
vanishes outside
\tilde{U}
.
For sufficiently small
\rho>0
we have
f_{\rho}(\tilde{U})\subset U^{\prime}_{f(x)}
and thus
df_{\rho}^{*}\omega=f_{\rho}^{*}d\omega
in the sense of distributions. The identity
(
A.12
) now follows from
Theorem
A.4
by taking
\rho\to 0
.
∎

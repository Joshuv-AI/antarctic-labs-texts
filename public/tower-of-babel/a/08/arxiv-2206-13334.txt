Butler’s method applied to 𝑍 p [ C p × C p ] \Z_{p}[C_{p}\times C_{p}] -permutation modules
John W. MacQuarrie, Marlon Stefano

Abstract
Let G G be a finite p p -group with normal subgroup N N of order p p . The first author and Zalesskii have previously given a characterization of permutation modules for 𝑍 p G \Z_{p}G in terms of modules for G / N G/N , but the necessity of their conditions was not known. We apply a correspondence due to Butler to demonstrate the necessity of the conditions, by exhibiting highly non-trivial counterexamples to the claim that if both the N N -invariants and the N N -coinvariants of a given lattice U U are permutation modules, then so is U U .

In

Simple examples are given in
[

]
showing that we cannot remove the conditions of Part 1. It was however left open whether Condition 2 is redundant. That is, whether

is a permutation module if, and only if, both

We believe that the methods used to construct these examples are interesting in their own right, utilizing in a new way a wonderful correspondence due to Butler
[

,

]
, wherein he associates to a lattice what he calls a “diagram”. For Butler,
the idea was to analyze the

of lattices for a given abelian
p
-group, using the correspondence to compare it with a category that is better understood. The novelty of our approach is to use Butler’s correspondence to analyze the structure of a

-lattices

for which

,
U^{N}
and

are permutation modules. Using these characterizations, we find a diagram whose corresponding lattice

has the properties that both

and

are permutation modules, but
U
is not. In the future, we believe that analyses of this type will yield further insights in the study of permutation modules.

Getting a diagram from a reduced lattice:

•

Theorem 4.1
.

The reduced lattice

\displaystyle e_{0}\cdot u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\widehat{G}u=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\widehat{K}\widehat{H}u=\frac{1}{p}\widehat{K}u

\displaystyle e_{H}\cdot u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{2}}(p\widehat{H}-\widehat{K}\widehat{H})u=\frac{1}{p}(p\widehat{1}-\widehat{K})u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{2}}(p\widehat{K}-\widehat{H}\widehat{K})u=\frac{1}{p^{2}}(p\widehat{1}-\widehat{H})\widehat{K}u=\frac{1}{p^{2}}(p\widehat{1}-p\widehat{1})\widehat{K}u=0.

g\in G
. Since
U_{N}
is a lattice, Lemma
2.1
says that
(U_{N})_{C}=U/I_{G}U
is a lattice if, and only if,
U_{N}
is a permutation module. Suppose that
U_{N}
is a permutation module and fix
u=\sumop\displaylimits_{H}x_{H}\in U\cap\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{H}F_{(H)}
. By Lemma
2.2
,
(p\cdot 1-\widehat{K})u=\sumop\displaylimits_{H\neq K}px_{H}\in I_{G}U
, and hence, summing over the subgroups of order
p
we get that
\sumop\displaylimits_{H}p^{p}x_{H}=p^{p}u\in I_{G}U
, and hence
u\in I_{G}U
since
U/I_{G}U
is a lattice. On the other hand, the right hand submodule is pure in
U
because
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{H}F_{(H)}
is a summand of
F
, so if the sets are equal then
I_{G}U
is pure in
U
and
U_{N}
is a permutation module.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

Lemma
4.7
is false without the condition that
U_{N}
be a lattice: the given equality applies for the
\Z_{2}[C_{2}\times C_{2}]
-lattice
U
whose diagram has
V=\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{2},V_{(0)}=\langle(1,1)\rangle,V_{(N)}=0,V_{(C)}=\langle(1,0)\rangle
and
V_{(\langle nc\rangle)}=\langle(0,1)\rangle
. Here
U_{N}
is not a lattice.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
U
be a reduced lattice such that
U_{N}
is a lattice. Then
U_{N}
is a permutation module if, and only if

V_{(H)}\cap\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq H}V_{(K)}\subseteq I_{G}V_{(H)}
for every subgroup
H
of order
p
.

Proof.

Suppose that
U_{N}
is a permutation module. Given an element
\overline{x_{H}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq H}\overline{x_{K}}
in
V_{(H)}\cap\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq H}V_{(K)}
,
then
x_{H}-\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq H}x_{K}\in U\cap\Big(\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{H}F_{(H)}\Big)
, hence in
I_{G}U\subseteq I_{G}F
by Lemma
4.7
. Thus
x_{H}\in I_{G}F_{(H)}
and hence
\overline{x_{H}}\in I_{G}V_{(H)}
.

Suppose now that
V_{(H)}\cap\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq H}V_{(K)}\subseteq I_{G}V_{(H)}
for every
H
. By Lemma
4.7
, to prove that
U_{N}
is a permutation module, it is enough to consider an element
u\in U\cap\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{K}F_{(K)}
and check that it is in
I_{G}U
. The given inclusion implies that
U\cap(\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{K}F_{(K)})\subseteq I_{G}(\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{K}F_{(K)})
, so we can write

u=(1-c)y_{N}-(1-n)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N}y_{K},

so that
(1-c)\overline{y_{N}}=(1-n)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N}\overline{y_{K}}
. But by the definition of a diagram, we have
V=V_{(0)}+\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N}V_{(K)}
and so we can write
\overline{y_{N}}=\overline{x_{0}}+\overline{x_{C}}+\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}\overline{x_{K}}
, and hence

(1-c)\overline{y_{N}}=(1-c)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}\overline{x_{K}}.

Thus the element
w:=(1-c)y_{N}-(1-c)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}x_{K}
is in
U
, and indeed in
I_{C}U
because
y_{N}-\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}x_{K}-x_{0}-x_{C}\in U
and

w=(1-c)(y_{N}-x_{0}-x_{C}-\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}x_{K}).

On the other hand, since

(1-n)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N}\overline{y_{K}}=(1-c)\overline{y_{N}}=(1-c)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}\overline{x_{K}},

the element
w^{\prime}:=(1-n)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N}y_{K}-(1-c)\sumop\displaylimits_{K\neq N,C}x_{K}\in U\cap\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{K\neq N}F_{(K)}
, so
w^{\prime}\in I_{N}U
by Lemma
4.4
since
U_{N}
is a lattice. It follows that
u=w-w^{\prime}\in I_{C}U+I_{N}U=I_{G}U
, as required.
∎

5

If

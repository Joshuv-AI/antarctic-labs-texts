Translating Solitons to Flows by Powers of The Gaussian Curvature in Riemannian Products
Ronaldo Freire de Lima

Abstract
. We consider translating solitons to flows by positive powers α \alpha of the Gaussian curvature — called K α K^{\alpha} -flows — in Riemannian products M × ℝ . M\times\mathbb{R}. We prove that, when M M is the Euclidean space ℝ n , \mathbb{R}^{n}, the sphere 𝕊 n , \mathbb{S}^{n}, or one of the hyperbolic spaces ℍ 𝔽 m , \mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}, there exist complete rotational translating solitons to K α K^{\alpha} -flow in M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R} for certain values of α . \alpha.

1.
Introduction

Over the last decades, the subject of extrinsic curvature flows in Riemannian manifolds
has experienced a significant development. Along this time,
special attention has been given to
mean curvature and Gaussian curvature flows in Euclidean space,
resulting in achievements such as the proof
of short time existence of solutions, and
of their convergence (after rescaling)
to round spheres. (For a thorough account of these and other extrinsic flows, we refer
to the recent book
[
3
]
.)

Likewise, flows by powers
of the Gaussian curvature, which we call
K^{\alpha}
-
flows
, have been
considered by many authors, most notably B. Andrews
(see, e.g.,

[
2
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
16
,
20
]
).
Among other reasons, the interest in
K^{\alpha}
-flows comes from the fact that
they naturally relate to a wide range of research fields,
varying from image processing to affine geometry and geometric analysis (cf.
[
1
]
).

In the context of extrinsic curvature flows in Euclidean space, there are special hypersurfaces,
called
translating solitons
, which have the distinguished property of moving
by translation under such a flow. In fact, the construction and classification of complete
translating solitons constitutes a major problem in this theory (see, e.g.,

[
10
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
).
On this regard, we point out that
J. Urbas
[
20
]
established, for all
\alpha\in(0,1/2],

the existence of complete rotational translating solitons to the
K^{\alpha}
-flow in Euclidean space.

More generally, translating solitons can be defined in Riemannian products
M\times\mathbb{R},
where

M
is an arbitrary Riemannian manifold. In
[
17
]
,
assuming
M
complete with non-positive sectional curvature and rotationally invariant
metric, Lira and Martín obtained a one-parameter family of rotational translating
solitons to the mean curvature flow in
M\times\mathbb{R}.

Also, in
[
14
,
15
]
,
results of the same nature were obtained in the context of Lorentzian products.
On the other hand, to our knowledge, there are no works
on translating solitons to
K^{\alpha}
-flows in products
M\times\mathbb{R}.

In this paper, we prove existence of complete rotational
translating solitons to the

K^{\alpha}
-flow in
M\times\mathbb{R}
(for certain positive values of
\alpha
)
when
M
is the Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{n},
the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n},
or one of the hyperbolic spaces

\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
(rank-one symmetric spaces of noncompact type). In particular,
we recover the aforementioned result of Urbas, giving it a new proof.

We remark that the translating solitons we construct here are all
bowl-type graphs of radial functions. In the Euclidean and hyperbolic cases,
these graphs are entire, whereas
in the spherical case they project onto an open hemisphere
B
of
\mathbb{S}^{n},

being asymptotic to the half-cylinder
\partial B\times[0,+\infty).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we fix notation regarding hypersurfaces of Riemannian products
M\times\mathbb{R}.
In Section

3
, we discuss graphs defined on families of parallel hypersurfaces,
setting some formulae. In Section
4
, we introduce the

K^{\alpha}
-flow in
M\times\mathbb{R}
and establish a key lemma. Finally, in
Section
5
, we state and prove our main result.

2.
Preliminaries

Given a Riemannian manifold
M^{n},

n\geq 2,
consider the Riemannian product

M\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with its standard metric

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{M}+dt^{2},

and denote its Levi-Civita connection by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu.

Let
\varSigma^{n}
be an oriented hypersurface of
M\times\mathbb{R}.
Set

N
for its unit normal field and
A
for its shape operator with respect to

N,
so that

AX=-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X}N,\,\,X\in T\varSigma,

where
T\varSigma
stands for the tangent bundle of
\varSigma
.
The principal curvatures of
\varSigma,
that is,
the eigenvalues of the shape operator
A,
will be denoted by
k_{1}\,,\dots,k_{n}
.
In this setting, the
Gaussian curvature
of
\varSigma
is the function
K:\varSigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

K:=\det A=k_{1}\dots k_{n}\,.

We denote by
\partial_{t}
the gradient of the
projection
\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}}
of
M\times\mathbb{R}
on the factor
\mathbb{R}.

Given a hypersurface
\varSigma\subset M\times\mathbb{R},
its

height function

\xi
and its
angle function

\varTheta

are defined by the following identities:

\xi(x):=\pi_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathbb{R}}|_{\varSigma}\quad\text{and}\quad\varTheta(x):=\langle N(x),\partial_{t}\rangle,\,\,x\in\varSigma.

We shall denote the gradient of
\xi
on
\varSigma
by
T,
so that

(1)

T=\partial_{t}-\varTheta N.

Given
t\in\mathbb{R},
the set
P_{t}:=M\times\{t\}
is called a
horizontal hyperplane

of
M\times\mathbb{R}.
Horizontal hyperplanes are all isometric to
M
and totally geodesic in

M\times\mathbb{R}.
In this context,
we call a transversal intersection
\varSigma_{t}:=\varSigma\mathrel{\text{\vbox{\halign{#\cr\smash{$-$}\crcr$\pitchfork$\crcr}}}}P_{t}
a
horizontal section

of
\varSigma.
Any horizontal section
\varSigma_{t}
is a hypersurface of
P_{t}
.
So, at any point
x\in\varSigma_{t}\subset\varSigma,
the tangent space
T_{x}\varSigma
of
\varSigma
at
x
splits
as the orthogonal sum

(2)

T_{x}\varSigma=T_{x}\varSigma_{t}\oplus{\rm Span}\{T\}.

Definition 1
.

A hypersurface
\varSigma\subset M\times\mathbb{R}
is called
rotational
, if there exists
a fixed point
o\in M
such that any connected component of
any horizontal section
\varSigma_{t}
of
\varSigma
is contained in a geodesic sphere of
M\times\{t\}

with center at
o\times\{t\}.

If so, the set
\{o\}\times\mathbb{R}
is called the
axis
of
\varSigma.

Recall that the rank-one symmetric spaces of non-compact type
are the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\,\,\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{m},\,\,\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
and
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{O}}^{2},

m\geq 1,

called
real hyperbolic space, complex hyperbolic space, quaternionic hyperbolic space
and

Cayley hyperbolic plane,
respectively.
We will adopt the unified notation
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
for these spaces with
m=2
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{O}.

The real dimension of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
is
n=m\dim\mathbb{F}.

We remark that the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
are all Einstein–Hadamard manifolds
whose sectional curvatures are negative and pinched. In particular, their
geodesic spheres are all strictly convex (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).

3.
Graphs on Parallel Hypersurfaces

Consider
an isometric immersion

f:M_{0}^{n-1}\rightarrow M^{n}

between two Riemannian manifolds
M_{0}^{n-1}
and
M^{n},

and suppose that there is a neighborhood
\mathscr{U}

of
M_{0}
in
TM_{0}^{\perp}
without focal points of
f,
that is,
the restriction of the normal exponential map
\exp^{\perp}_{M_{0}}:TM_{0}^{\perp}\rightarrow M
to

\mathscr{U}
is a diffeomorphism onto its image. In this case, denoting by

\eta
the unit normal field of
f,
there is an open interval
I\owns 0

such that, for all
p\in M_{0},
the curve

(3)

\gamma_{p}(s)=\exp_{\scriptscriptstyle M}(f(p),s\eta(p)),\,s\in I,

is a well defined geodesic of
M
without conjugate points. Thus,
for all
s\in I,

\begin{array}[]{cccc}f_{s}:&M_{0}&\rightarrow&M\\
&p&\mapsto&\gamma_{p}(s)\end{array}

is an immersion of
M_{0}
into
M,
which is said to be
parallel
to
f.

Observe that, given
p\in M_{0}
, the tangent space
f_{s_{*}}(T_{p}M_{0})
of
f_{s}
at
p
is the parallel transport of
f_{*}(T_{p}M_{0})
along

\gamma_{p}
from
0
to
s.
We also remark that, with the induced metric,
the unit normal
\eta_{s}
of
f_{s}
at
p
is given by

\eta_{s}(p)=\gamma_{p}^{\prime}(s).

Definition 2
.

Let
\phi:I\rightarrow\phi(I)\subset\mathbb{R}
be an increasing diffeomorphism, i.e.,
\phi^{\prime}>0.

With the above notation, we call the set

(4)

\varSigma:=\{(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))\in M\times\mathbb{R}\,;\,p\in M_{0},\,s\in I\},

the
graph
determined by
\{f_{s}\,;\,s\in I\}
and
\phi,
or
(f_{s},\phi)
-
graph
, for short.

For an arbitrary point
x=(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))
of an

(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma,
one has

T_{x}\varSigma=f_{s_{*}}(T_{p}M_{0})\oplus{\rm Span}\,\{\partial_{s}\},\,\,\,\partial_{s}=\eta_{s}+\phi^{\prime}(s)\partial_{t}.

So, a unit normal to
\varSigma
is

(5)

N=\frac{-\phi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}\eta_{s}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}\partial_{t}\,.

In particular, its angle function is

(6)

\varTheta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}\,\cdot

As shown in
[
11
, Theorem 6]
), any
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma

has the
T
-
property
, meaning that

T
is a principal direction at any point of
\varSigma
.
More precisely,

(7)

AT=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}}{(\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}})^{3}}T.

Conversely, any hypersurface of
M\times\mathbb{R}
with non vanishing angle function having
T
as a principal direction
is given locally as an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph.

Given an

(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma,

let
\{X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{n}\}
be
the orthonormal frame of principal directions of
\varSigma

in which
X_{n}=T/\|T\|.
In this case,
for
1\leq i\leq n-1,

the fields
X_{i}
are all horizontal, that is, tangent to
M
(cf. (
2
)).
Therefore, setting

(8)

\varrho:=\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}}}

and considering (
5
), we have, for all
i=1,\dots,n-1,
that

k_{i}=\langle AX_{i},X_{i}\rangle=-\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X_{i}}N,X_{i}\rangle=\varrho\langle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{X_{i}}\eta_{s},X_{i}\rangle=-\varrho k_{i}^{s},

where
k_{i}^{s}
is the
i
-th principal curvature of
f_{s}\,.
Also,
it follows from (
7
)
that
k_{n}=\varrho^{\prime}.
Thus, the principal curvatures of the
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma

at
(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))\in\varSigma
are

(9)

k_{i}=-\varrho(s)k_{i}^{s}(p)\,\,(1\leq i\leq n-1)\quad\text{and}\quad k_{n}=\varrho^{\prime}(s).

In particular, the Gaussian curvature
K
of
\varSigma
at
(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))
is given by

(10)

K=(-\varrho(s))^{n-1}K_{s}(p)\varrho^{\prime}(s)\,,

where
K_{s}
denotes the Gaussian curvature of
the hypersurface
f_{s}
.

We remark that the function
\varrho
defined in (
8
) determines
the function
\phi.

Indeed, it follows from equality (
8
) that

(11)

\phi(s)=\int_{s_{0}}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du+\phi(s_{0}),\,\,\,s_{0},s\in I.

In addition, from (
6
) and (
8
),
the unit normal
N
defined in (
5
) can be
written as
N=-\varrho\eta_{s}+\varTheta\partial_{t}
. Hence, the relation

(12)

\varTheta=\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}}

holds everywhere on any
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma.

In particular, one has
\varrho=\|T\|.

Now, let us assume that the family of parallel hypersurfaces

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in I\}

is
isoparametric
, that is,
for each
s\in I,
any principal curvature
k_{i}^{s}
of
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}
is constant (possibly depending on
i
and
s
).
We shall assume further that each
f_{s}
is strictly convex with
k_{i}^{s}<0.

In this setting, the Gaussian curvature
K_{s}
of each hypersurface
f_{s}
is a (non vanishing) function of
s
alone.
Hence, from (
10
), the same is true for any
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
built on

\mathscr{F},
that is,

(13)

K(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))=(-\varrho(s))^{n-1}\varrho^{\prime}(s)K_{s}\,,\,\,\,(f_{s}(p),\phi(s))\in\varSigma.

It should also be noticed that, by equalities (
9
),
such a graph
\varSigma
is strictly convex.

4.
Translating
K^{\alpha}
-Solitons in
M\times\mathbb{R}

Given a real number
\alpha\neq 0,
we say that an oriented
strictly convex hypersurface
\varSigma
of
M\times\mathbb{R}

moves under

K^{\alpha}
-flow if there exists a one-parameter
family of immersions
F\colon M_{0}\times[0,u_{0})\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R},

u_{0}\leq+\infty,

such that

(14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial F}{\partial u}^{\perp}(p,u)=K^{\alpha}(p,u)N(p,u).\\[4.30554pt]
F(M_{0}\,,0)=\varSigma,\end{array}\right.

where
N(p,u)
is the inward unit normal to the hypersurface
F_{u}:=F(.\,,u)
,

K(p,u)
is the Gaussian curvature
of
F_{u}
with respect to
N_{u}:=N(.\,,u),
and

\frac{\partial F}{\partial u}^{\perp}
denotes the normal component of

\frac{\partial F}{\partial u},
i.e.,

\frac{\partial F}{\partial u}^{\perp}=\left\langle\frac{\partial F}{\partial u},N_{u}\right\rangle N_{u}\,.

In particular, the first equality in (
14
) is equivalent to

(15)

\left\langle\frac{\partial F}{\partial u}(p,u),N(p,u)\right\rangle=K^{\alpha}(p,u).

We call such a map
F
a
K^{\alpha}
-
flow
in
M\times\mathbb{R}.

Denote by
\exp
the exponential map of
M\times\mathbb{R}
and
consider an isometric immersion
F_{0}\colon M_{0}\rightarrow M\times\mathbb{R}
.
Define then the map

F(p,u):=\exp_{F_{0}(p)}(u\partial_{t}),\,\,(p,u)\in M_{0}\times[0,+\infty),

and notice that, for each
u\in(0,+\infty),
the hypersurface
F(M_{0}\,,u)
is nothing
but a vertical translation of
\varSigma:=F(M_{0},0).
Since vertical translations are isometries of

M\times\mathbb{R}
keeping the second factor invariant,
we have that
\varSigma
and
F(M_{0}\,,u)

are congruent with coinciding angle functions and Gaussian curvature, that is,

(16)

\varTheta(p,u)=\varTheta(p,0)\quad\text{and}\quad K(p,u)=K(p,0)\,\,\,\forall(p,u)\in M_{0}\times[0,u_{0}).

Now, differentiating
F
with respect to
u,
we have

(17)

\frac{\partial F}{\partial u}(p,u)=(d\exp_{F_{0}(p)})(u\partial_{t})\partial_{t}=\partial_{t}\,.

From (
16
) and (
17
), we have that

F
satisfies (
15
) if and only if
the equality

\varTheta(p,0)=K^{\alpha}(p,0)

holds on
M_{0}
. This fact motivates the following concept.

Definition 3
.

Given
\alpha>0,
we say that an oriented
strictly convex hypersurface
\varSigma
of the
Riemannian product
M\times\mathbb{R}

is a
translating soliton to the
K^{\alpha}
-curvature flow
(or simply
a
translating
K^{\alpha}
-soliton
) if the equality

K^{\alpha}=\varTheta

holds everywhere on
\varSigma.
(From the above discussion, any such
hypersurface
\varSigma
is the initial data of a
K^{\alpha}
-flow by vertical translations.)

Let us consider now an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma

in
M\times\mathbb{R}
such that the family

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in I\}

is isoparametric with
k_{i}^{s}<0.
In this case,
it follows from (
12
) and (
13
) that

\varSigma
is a translating
K^{\alpha}
-soliton if and only if its associated

\varrho
function satisfies

(18)

(-\varrho(s))^{n-1}\varrho^{\prime}(s)K_{s}=(1-\varrho^{2}(s))^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}},\,\,\,s\in I.

Since
K_{s}
never vanishes on
I,
we have that
(
18
) holds for
\varrho
if and only if

(19)

\uptau^{\prime}(s)=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n(1-\uptau^{\frac{2}{n}})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{K_{s}}\,,\quad\uptau:=\varrho^{n}.

Summarizing, we have the following result.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\varSigma
be an
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph in
M\times\mathbb{R}
whose associated
family

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:M_{0}\rightarrow M\,;\,s\in I\}

of oriented parallel hypersurfaces is isoparametric with each of them having negative principal curvatures.
Then,
\varSigma
is a translating
K^{\alpha}
-soliton if and only if (
19
)
holds for
\uptau:=\varrho^{n},

where
\varrho:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is as in (
8
).

5.
The Main Result

For
n\geq 2
and
m\geq 1,
let
M
be one of the following manifolds, each of them endowed with its canonical
Riemannian metric: Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n},
the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n},
or one of the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}.

Then, define

(20)

\mathcal{R}_{M}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}+\infty&\text{if}&M\neq\mathbb{S}^{n}\\[4.30554pt]
\pi/2&\text{if}&M=\mathbb{S}^{n}\end{array}\right.

and consider a family

(21)

\mathscr{F}:=\{f_{s}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow M\,;\,\,\,s\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{M})\}

of concentric geodesic spheres of
M
indexed by their radiuses, that is,
for a fixed point
o\in M
, and for each
s\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{M}),

f_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
is the (strictly convex) geodesic sphere
S_{s}(o)
of
M
with center at
o
and radius
s.

In accordance to the notation of Section
3
, for each
s\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{M}),

we choose the outward orientation of
f_{s}
,
so that
any principal curvature
k_{i}^{s}
of
f_{s}
is negative
.

For
M=\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m},
the principal curvatures
k_{i}^{s}
of the geodesic spheres
f_{s}\in\mathscr{F}
are:

(22)

\displaystyle k_{1}^{s}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\coth(s/2)\,\,\,\text{with multiplicity}\,\,\,n-p-1.

\displaystyle k_{2}^{s}

\displaystyle=-\coth(s)\,\,\,\text{with multiplicity}\,\,\,p,

where
n=\dim\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m},

p=n-1
for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
p=1
for
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
,
p=3
for
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{K}}^{m}
, and

p=7
for
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{O}}^{2}
(see, e.g.,
[
8
, pgs. 353, 543]
and
[
13
]
).
Thus, the Gaussian curvature
K_{s}
of the geodesic sphere
S_{s}(o)
of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
is given by

K_{s}=(-1)^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\coth(s/2)\right)^{n-p-1}(\coth s)^{p}\,.

In particular, the function

\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{K_{s}}=(2\tanh(s/2))^{n-p-1}(\tanh s)^{p}

is well defined and nonnegative on
[0,+\infty).

As is well known, in the cases
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
M=\mathbb{S}^{n}
one has

\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{K_{s}}=s^{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{K_{s}}=(\tan s)^{n-1},

respectively.

It is easily seen that, in any of these three cases, the equality

(23)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{M}}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{K_{v}}dv=+\infty.

holds.
Finally, set

•

\delta_{n}:=\,\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1/2&\text{if}&n=2.\\[4.30554pt]
\max\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{n-1}\}&\text{if}&n>2.\\[4.30554pt]
\end{array}\right.

•

\mathcal{I}_{M}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}(0,1/2]&\text{if}&M\neq\mathbb{S}^{n}.\\[4.30554pt]
[\delta_{n},1/2]&\text{if}&M=\mathbb{S}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

With this notation, we now state and prove our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
and
\mathcal{I}_{M}\subset(0,1/2]
be as above.
Then, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}_{M},
there exists a complete rotational
strictly convex translating
K^{\alpha}
-soliton in the closed half-space
M\times[0,+\infty),

whose height function is unbounded. In addition, the following assertions hold:

•

For
M\neq\mathbb{S}^{n},

\varSigma
is an entire graph over
M.

•

For
M=\mathbb{S}^{n},

\varSigma
is a graph over an open hemisphere
B\subset\mathbb{S}^{n},

being asymptotic to the half-cylinder
\partial B\times[0,+\infty).

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a family of parallel geodesic spheres
of
M
as in (
21
). We intend to
determine a function
\phi\in C^{\infty}[0,\mathcal{R}_{M})
such that
the corresponding
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
becomes the desired

K^{\alpha}
-soliton. With this purpose, let us consider the equality
(
19
) as an ODE with variable
\uptau,
and rewrite it as

(24)

\frac{d\uptau}{(1-\uptau^{\frac{2}{n}})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n}{K_{s}}ds.

In order to solve (
24
), consider the functions

\Phi\colon[0,1)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi\colon[0,\mathcal{R}_{M})\rightarrow[0,+\infty)

given by

\Phi(\uptau):=\int_{0}^{\uptau}\frac{du}{(1-u^{\frac{2}{n}})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}n}{K_{v}}dv.

It follows from (
23
) that
\Psi
is a diffeomorphism.
Let us show that the same is true for
\Phi.

Indeed,
\Phi
is a
C^{\infty}
function with positive derivative,
which implies that it is a diffeomorphism over its image. So, it remains to prove
that
\Phi([0,1))=[0,+\infty).
To this end, notice first that

1-u^{2/n}\leq 1-u\,\forall u\in[0,1).
Therefore, setting
p:=1/(2\alpha)\geq 1,

one has

\Phi(\uptau)\geq\int_{0}^{\uptau}\frac{du}{(1-u)^{p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\log\left(\frac{1}{1-\uptau}\right)&\text{if}&p=1.\\[8.61108pt]
\frac{(1-\uptau)^{1-p}-1}{p-1}&\text{if}&p>1,\end{array}\right.

which implies that
\Phi(\uptau)\rightarrow+\infty
as

\uptau\rightarrow 1.
Hence,
\Phi([0,1))=[0,+\infty),
as asserted.

Now, we can define
\uptau:[0,\mathcal{R}_{M})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(25)

\uptau(s)=\Phi^{-1}(\Psi(s)),

which is clearly a solution of (
24
) (and so of (
19
)) satisfying

(26)

0=\uptau(0)\leq\uptau(s)<1\,\,\,\,\forall s\in[0,\mathcal{R}_{M})\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{M}}\uptau(s)=1.

Therefore, by Lemma
1
, the corresponding
(f_{s},\phi)
-graph
\varSigma
is a translating
K^{\alpha}
-soliton
in
M\times\mathbb{R}.
Moreover, since
\varrho={\uptau}^{1/n}
, we have from (
11
) (with
s_{0}=0
) and (
26
)
that the function
\phi
is defined in
[0,\mathcal{R}_{M})
and satisfies

\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{M}}\phi^{\prime}(s)=+\infty.

We conclude from the above discussion that, if
M\neq\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
then
\varSigma
is an unbounded entire graph over
M
which is
contained in the closed half-space
M\times[0,+\infty).

In particular,
\varSigma
is complete (Fig.
1
).

If
M=\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then
\varSigma
is a graph over the open hemisphere

B
centered at
o\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
(Fig.
2
).
Thus, to conclude that
\varSigma
is complete and
asymptotic to
\partial B\times[0,+\infty),
we must
prove that
\phi
is unbounded.

For
n=2,
we have from the hypothesis that
\alpha=1/2.
In this case, it is easily checked that

\varrho(s)=\sin s
is the solution of (
18
)
satisfying
\varrho(0)=0.
So, from (
11
),

\phi(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du=\int_{0}^{s}\tan(u)du=\log\left(\frac{1}{\cos s}\right),\,\,\,\,s\in(0,\pi/2),

which implies that
\phi(s)\rightarrow+\infty
as
s\rightarrow\pi/2.

For
n>2,
let us show first
that
\uptau^{\prime}
is bounded in
(0,\pi/2).

Indeed, from (
19
),

\uptau^{\prime}(s)=n(\tan s)^{n-1}(1-(\uptau(s))^{2/n})^{p},\quad p=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\cdot

So, setting

\mu_{1}(s):=n(\tan s)^{n-2}(\sec s)^{2}(1-(\uptau(s))^{2/n})^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{2}(s):=(1-(\uptau(s))^{\frac{2}{n}})\tan s,

a straightforward calculation yields

(27)

\displaystyle\uptau^{\prime\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(s)\left[(n-1)-\frac{2p(\uptau(s))^{\frac{2-n}{n}}(\cos s)^{2}(\tan s)\uptau^{\prime}(s)}{n(1-(\uptau(s))^{\frac{2}{n}})}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(s)\left[(n-1)-\frac{2p(\uptau(s))^{\frac{2-n}{n}}(\sin s)^{2}\uptau^{\prime}(s)}{n\mu_{2}(s)}\right],\,\,\,s\in(0,\pi/2).

Let us suppose, by contradiction, that there exists a sequence
(s_{k})
in

(0,\pi/2)
such that
\uptau^{\prime}(s_{k})\rightarrow+\infty.
We can assume, without loss of generality, that

\uptau^{\prime\prime}(s_{k})>0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Under this assumption, we have from (
27
) that
the sequence
\mu_{2}(s_{k})
is unbounded above.
Otherwise, for a sufficiently large
k,
the expression in the brackets
would be negative for
s=s_{k},
which would give
\uptau^{\prime\prime}(s_{k})<0
— a contradiction.

So, we can assume
\mu_{2}(s_{k})\rightarrow+\infty.

However, from our choice of
\alpha,
we have that
0\leq p-1\leq 1,
which yields

(1-(\uptau(s_{k}))^{2/n})^{p-1}\geq(1-(\uptau(s_{k}))^{2/n}).
Thus,

\frac{\uptau^{\prime}(s_{k})}{\mu_{2}(s_{k})}=n(\tan s_{k})^{n-2}(1-(\uptau(s_{k}))^{2/n})^{p-1}\geq n(\tan s_{k})^{n-3}\mu_{2}(s_{k})\rightarrow+\infty,

which, together with equality (
27
), gives
\uptau^{\prime\prime}(s_{k})<0
for a sufficiently large
k
— again a contradiction.
Therefore,
\uptau^{\prime}
(and so
\varrho^{\prime}
) is bounded in
(0,\pi/2).

Now, let
C>0
be such that
\varrho^{\prime}(s)<C
for all
s\in(0,\pi/2).

Since we are assuming
\alpha(n-1)\geq 1,
equalities (
11
) and (
18
) yield

\phi(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\varrho(u)}{\sqrt{1-\varrho^{2}(u)}}du=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(\tan u)^{\alpha(n-1)}}{(\varrho(u))^{\alpha(n-1)-1}(\varrho^{\prime}(u))^{\alpha}}du\geq\frac{1}{C^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{s}\tan u\,du,

which implies that
\phi(s)\rightarrow+\infty
as
s\rightarrow\pi/2.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We finish by noting that, for a given Riemannian manifold
M,
we have
from Lemma
1
that there exist local
translating
K^{\alpha}
-solitons in

M\times\mathbb{R}
as long as
M
admits families of isoparametric,
strictly convex hypersurfaces. This applies, for instance, to
the hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
and their families of
parallel horospheres (see Proposition-(vi), pg 88, in
[
6
]
), to
the real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and its families
of equidistant hypersurfaces to
a given totally geodesic hyperplane, as well as to

\mathbb{S}^{n}
and any of its many families
of strictly convex isoparametric hypersurfaces (cf.
[
12
]
and the references therein).

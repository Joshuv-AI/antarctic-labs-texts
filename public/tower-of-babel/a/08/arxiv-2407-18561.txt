Abstract.

In this paper, a class of systems of pseudo-parabolic PDEs is considered. These systems (S)
ε
are derived as a pseudo-parabolic dissipation system of Kobayashi–Warren–Carter energy, proposed by [Kobayashi et al., Physica D, 140, 141–150 (2000)], to describe planar grain boundary motion. These systems have been studied in [arXiv:2402.10413], and solvability, uniqueness and strong regularity of the solution have been reported under the setting that the initial data is sufficiently smooth. Meanwhile, in this paper, we impose weaker regularity on the initial data, and work on the weak formulation of the systems. In this light, we set our goal of this paper to prove two Main Theorems, concerned with: the existence and the uniqueness of weak solution to (S)
ε
, and the continuous dependence with respect to the index
\varepsilon
, initial data and forcings.

1
Introduction

Let
N\in\{1,2,3,4\}
be a fixed spatial dimension, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain. When
N\geq 2
,
\Gamma
denotes a smooth boundary of
\Omega
, and
n_{\Gamma}
denotes the outer unit normal on
\Gamma
. Also, let
T>0
be a fixed time constant, and let us set
Q:=(0,T)\times\Omega
,
\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\Gamma
,
H:=L^{2}(\Omega)
, and
V:=H^{1}(\Omega)
.

In this paper, we consider a class of pseudo-parabolic systems, denoted by (S)
ε
for
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle{\rm(S)}_{\varepsilon}\qquad

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\eta-\mathit{\Delta}(\eta+\mu^{2}\partial_{t}\eta)+g(\eta)+\alpha^{\prime}(\eta)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta|^{2}}=u,\ \mbox{ in }Q,\\
&\nabla(\eta+\mu^{2}\partial_{t}\eta)\cdot n_{\Gamma}=0,\ \mbox{ on }\Sigma,\\
&\eta(0)=\eta_{0},\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\alpha_{0}(\eta)\partial_{t}\theta-\diver\Bigl(\alpha(\eta)\frac{\nabla\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta|^{2}}}+\nu^{2}\nabla\partial_{t}\theta\Bigr)=v,\ \mbox{ in }Q,\\
&\Bigl(\alpha(\eta)\frac{\nabla\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta|^{2}}}+\nu^{2}\nabla\partial_{t}\theta\Bigr)\cdot n_{\Gamma}=0,\ \mbox{ on }\Sigma,\\
&\theta(0)=\theta_{0},\ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

The system (S)
ε
is pseudo-parabolic version of KWC-model of planer grain boundary motion, which is proposed by
[
5
,
6
]
. In this system,
\eta
and
\theta
represent
orientation order
and
orientation angle
in a polycrystal, respectively.
g
is a perturbation for the orientation order
\eta
, with a non-negative primitive
G
.
\alpha
and
\alpha_{0}
are positive-valued functions, called mobilities of grain boundary motion.
u,v
are forcing terms. Finally, a pair of functions
[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]
is the initial data of
[\eta,\theta]
.

In this paper, the components of the system (S)
ε
are considered under the following assumptions.

(A0)

\mu>0
,
\nu>0
are fixed positive constants.

(A1)

g:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function with a non-negative primitive
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. In addition,
g
satisfies the following condition:

\varliminf_{\xi\downarrow-\infty}g(\xi)=-\infty\mbox{ and }\varlimsup_{\xi\uparrow\infty}g(\xi)=\infty.

(1.4)

(A2)

\alpha:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
is a
C^{2}
-class convex function, such that
\alpha^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\alpha^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{R}
.
\alpha_{0}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function. Besides, we suppose
\delta_{*}:=\inf\alpha_{0}(\mathbb{R})>0
.

(A3)

u\in L^{\infty}(Q)
, and
v\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
.

(A4)

The initial data
[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]
belong to
[V\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)]\times V
.

The system (S)
ε
is derived from the following energy-dissipation flow:

\displaystyle-\biggl[\begin{gathered}I-\mu^{2}\mathit{\Delta}_{N}\\
\alpha_{0}(\eta(t))I-\nu^{2}\mathit{\Delta}_{N}\end{gathered}\biggr]\biggl[\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}\eta(t)\\
\partial_{t}\theta(t)\end{gathered}\biggr]=\nabla_{[\eta,\theta]}\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\eta(t),\theta(t))+\biggl[\begin{gathered}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{gathered}\biggr]\mbox{ in }[H]^{2},

\displaystyle\mbox{ a.e. }t>0,

(1.11)

where
\mathit{\Delta}_{N}
denotes Laplace operator subject to zero-Neumann boundary condition, and
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is free-energy of grain boundary motion, called
KWC-energy
, and defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}G(\eta)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|D\theta|^{2}}\in[0,\infty].

(1.12)

In this context, the last term is given by:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|D\theta|^{2}}

(1.13)

\displaystyle\quad:=\inf\Biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}\displaystyle\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}\,dx&\parbox{99.58464pt}{$\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset W^{1,1}(\Omega)$ such that $\varphi_{n}\to\theta$ in $L^{1}(\Omega)$ as $n\to\infty$
}\end{array}\Biggr\},

for
[\eta,\theta]\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times BV(\Omega)
, and
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)
.

(1.15)

We note that if
\theta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, then the integral
\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|D\theta|^{2}}
coincides with

\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta)\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta|^{2}}\,dx
.

The system (
1.11
) can be regarded as a generalized version of KWC system, and the case
\mu=\nu=\varepsilon=0
corresponds to the original KWC system. So far, many researchers have worked on mathematical verification of KWC-type systems, and specified that main difficulity of KWC-type systems lies in uniqueness question, because of the the singular flux
\alpha(\eta)\frac{\nabla\theta}{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta|^{2}}}
, and the unknown-dependent mobility
\alpha
and
\alpha_{0}
. In fact, we need suitable regularization for the energy, and simplification for the mobility to obtain uniqueness result (cf.
[
4
,
8
,
2
]
).

Recently, however,
[
3
]
reported the solvability, uniqueness, and regularity result for a KWC system of pseudo-parabolic type with sufficiently smooth initial data, such as
[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]\in[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)]\times H^{2}(\Omega)
, without any change for the settings of free-energy and unknown-dependent mobilities. Consequently, we can say that pseudo-parabolic regularization is an effective way to address the singularity and complex structure in (S)
ε
. In this paper, we focus on unfinished issues on the work
[
3
]
. First, as one of key properties of pseudo-parabolic equations, we cannot expect smoothing effect for initial data in (S)
ε
. In view of this, we will deal with setting up the weak formulation of the system under the weak regularity of initial data, such as
[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]\in[V\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)]\times V
, and work on verifying the well-posedness of the system. Second, looking towards advancement in optimal control problems, we will provide a result for continuous dependence of solution to (S)
ε
, with respect to the change of
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)
, and with respect to the change of initial data and forcings in suitable topology.

Based on the above background, we set the goal of this article to prove the following two Main Theorems.

Main Theorem 1
.

Under the assumptions (A0)–(A4), the system (S)
ε
admits a unique solution
[\eta,\theta]
in the following sense:

(S0)

\eta\in W^{1,2}(0,T;V)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)
, and
\theta\in W^{1,2}(0,T;V)
. In particular, if
\theta_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
v\equiv 0
, then
\theta\in L^{\infty}(Q)
.

(S1)

\eta
solves the following variational identity:

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\eta(t)+g(\eta(t))+\alpha^{\prime}(\eta(t))\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta(t)|^{2}},\varphi)_{H}

(1.16)

\displaystyle\quad+(\nabla(\eta+\mu^{2}\partial_{t}\eta)(t),\nabla\varphi)_{[H]^{N}}=(u(t),\varphi)_{H},

(1.17)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\mbox{ for any }\varphi\in V,\mbox{ and a.e. }t\in(0,T).

(1.18)

(S2)

\theta
solves the following variational inequality:

\displaystyle\bigl((\alpha_{0}(\eta)\partial_{t}\theta)(t),\theta(t)-\psi\bigr)_{H}+\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta(t))\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\theta(t)|^{2}}\,dx

(1.19)

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{2}(\nabla\partial_{t}\theta(t),\nabla(\theta(t)-\psi))_{[H]^{N}}

(1.20)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta(t))\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}}\,dx+(v(t),\theta(t)-\psi)_{H},

(1.21)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\mbox{ for any }\psi\in V,\mbox{ and a.e. }t\in(0,T).

(1.22)

(S3)

[\eta,\theta]
fulfills the following energy-inequality:

\displaystyle C_{0}\int_{s}^{t}\Bigl(|\partial_{t}\eta(r)|_{V}^{2}+|\partial_{t}\theta(r)|_{V}^{2}\Bigr)\,dr+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\eta(t),\theta(t))

(1.23)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\eta(s),\theta(s))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\biggl(|u(r)|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta_{*}}|v(r)|_{H}^{2}\biggr)\,dr,

(1.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mbox{ for any }0\leq s\leq t\leq T.

(1.25)

where
C_{0}=\min\{\frac{1}{4},\mu^{2},\frac{\delta_{*}}{2},\nu^{2}\}
.

(S4)

[\eta(0),\theta(0)]=[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]
in
[H]^{2}
.

Main Theorem 2
.

Let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset[0,\infty)
,
\{[\eta_{0,n},\theta_{0,n}]\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset[V\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)]\times V
and
\{[u_{n},v_{n}]\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset L^{\infty}(Q)\times L^{2}(0,T;H)
be sequences satisfying the following conditions:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bullet\ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\eta_{0,n}|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty,\mbox{ and }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|u_{n}|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}<\infty,\\
&\bullet\ \begin{gathered}\varepsilon_{n}\to\varepsilon,\,\eta_{0,n}\to\eta_{0},\,\theta_{0,n}\to\theta_{0}\mbox{ in }V,\mbox{ and }\\
u_{n}\to u,\,v_{n}\to v\mbox{ weakly in }[L^{2}(0,T;H)]^{2},\end{gathered}\end{aligned}\right.\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

(1.26)

Let
[\eta,\theta]
be the unique solution to (S)
ε
, corresponding to the initial data
[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]
and the forcings
[u,v]
, and let
[\eta_{n},\theta_{n}]
be the unique solution to (S)
{}_{\varepsilon_{n}}
, for the initial data
[\eta_{0,n},\theta_{0,n}]
and the forcings
[u_{n},v_{n}]
, for any
n=1,2,3,\dots
. Then, we can obtain the following convergences as

:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta_{n}\to\eta\mbox{ in }C([0,T];H),\,L^{2}(0,T;V),\mbox{ weakly in }W^{1,2}(0,T;V)\\
&\qquad\quad\mbox{ and weakly-$*$ in }L^{\infty}(Q),\\
&\theta_{n}\to\theta\mbox{ in }C([0,T];H)\mbox{ and weakly in }W^{1,2}(0,T;V),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.27)

and

\eta_{n}(t)\to\eta(t),\,\theta_{n}(t)\to\theta(t)\mbox{ in }V\mbox{ as }n\to\infty,\ \mbox{ for all }t\in[0,T].

(1.28)

Preliminaries

We first prescribe notations and known results used in this paper.

Specific notations.

We define
r\vee s:=\max\{r,s\}
and
r\wedge s:=\min\{r,s\}
for all
r,s\in[-\infty,\infty]
. We denote by
[\cdot]^{+},\,[\cdot]^{-}

Next, to simplify the notations, we set
\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)}
by letting:

\gamma_{\varepsilon}:y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\gamma_{\varepsilon}(y):=\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|y|^{2}}\in[0,\infty).

As is easily seen that
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
is convex and non-expansive function, and for
0\leq\alpha^{\circ}\in L^{2}(\Omega)

{\bm{w}}\in[H]^{N}\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\alpha^{\circ}\gamma_{\varepsilon}({\bm{w}})\,dx\in[0,\infty).

Also, for any
\varepsilon_{0}\in[0,\infty)

\gamma_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\gamma_{\varepsilon_{0}}

\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}
.

Finally, we use the following fact(

[

a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\,\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfy:

\varliminf_{n\to\infty}a_{n}\geq a,\ \varliminf_{n\to\infty}b_{n}\geq b,\mbox{ and }\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}(a_{n}+b_{n})\leq a+b.

(2.3)

Then,
a_{n}\to a
and
b_{n}\to b
as
n\to\infty
.

\tau>0

(2.5)

Here, the following estimates can be obtained by use of Young’s and Hölder’s inequality for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(\partial_{t}z_{\tau}(r),\overline{z}_{\tau}(r))_{X}\,dr

\displaystyle\overline{\zeta}_{\tau}\to\zeta\mbox{ and }\underline{\zeta}_{\tau}\to\zeta\mbox{ in $L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty);X)$, as $\tau\downarrow 0$.}

(2.8b)

Proof of Main Theorem 1

\displaystyle\leq 0,\ \mbox{ for a.e. }t\in(0,T),

(3.86)

\displaystyle{g(M^{*})\geq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|u_{n}|_{L^{\infty}(Q)},\ g(-M^{*})\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}-|u_{n}|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}}.

(3.87)

and applying the same argument in Main Theorem 1, we obtain
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|\eta_{n}|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\leq M^{*}
. Additionally, thanks to Main Theorem 1, the sequence
\{[\eta_{n},\theta_{n}]\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfies the following energy-inequality for
n=1,2,3,\dots
:

\displaystyle C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl(|\partial_{t}\eta_{n}(r)|_{V}^{2}+|\partial_{t}\theta_{n}(r)|_{V}^{2}\bigr)\,dr+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\eta_{n}(T),\theta_{n}(T))

(3.88)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\eta_{0,n},\theta_{0,n})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl(|u_{n}(r)|_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{\delta_{*}}|v_{n}(r)|_{H}^{2}\bigr)\,dr.

(3.89)

Now, using the assumption (
1.26
), energy-inequality (
3.89
) and
L^{\infty}
-boundedness of
\eta_{n}
, we can derive the following boundedness:

•

\{\eta_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
: bounded in
W^{1,2}(0,T;V)
and in
L^{\infty}(Q)
,

•

\{\theta_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
: bounded in
W^{1,2}(0,T;V)
,

Using Aubin’s type compactness theory, we can find a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}\subset\{n\}
;
n_{k}\uparrow\infty
as
k\to\infty
, and a pair of functions
[\bar{\eta},\bar{\theta}]\in[W^{1,2}(0,T;V)\cap L^{\infty}(Q)]\times W^{1,2}(0,T;V)
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta_{n_{k}}\to\bar{\eta}\mbox{ in }C([0,T];H),\mbox{ weakly in }W^{1,2}(0,T;V),\\
&\quad\mbox{ and weakly-$*$ in }L^{\infty}(Q),\\
&\theta_{n_{k}}\to\bar{\theta}\mbox{ in }C([0,T];H),\mbox{ weakly in }W^{1,2}(0,T;V),\end{aligned}\right.\mbox{ as }k\to\infty.

(3.90)

In particular, by (
1.26
), we see that:

[\bar{\eta}(0),\bar{\theta}(0)]=\lim_{k\to\infty}[\eta_{n_{k}}(0),\theta_{n_{k}}(0)]=\lim_{k\to\infty}[\eta_{0,n_{k}},\theta_{0,n_{k}}]=[\eta_{0},\theta_{0}]\mbox{ in }[H]^{2}.

(3.91)

Besides, we have the following convergence:

\displaystyle\eta_{n_{k}}(t)\to\bar{\eta}(t),\theta_{n_{k}}(t)\to\bar{\theta}(t)\mbox{ in }H\mbox{ and weakly in }V,\ \mbox{for any }t\in[0,T].

(3.92)

Now, we derive additional convergence of
\eta_{n_{k}}
and
\theta_{n_{k}}
, and confirm that the pair
[\bar{\eta},\bar{\theta}]
coincides the (unique) solution to (S)
ε
. First, for any
k=1,2,3,\dots
, let us set
\varphi=(\eta_{n_{k}}-\bar{\eta})(t)
in (S1),
\psi=\bar{\theta}
in (S2), and integrate the both side over
(0,t)
.
Then, applying similar arguments in (
3.36
)–(
3.53
), we can derive additional convergences as
k\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\bullet\,\eta_{n_{k}}\to\bar{\eta}\mbox{ in }L^{2}(0,T;V),

(3.93)

\displaystyle\bullet\,\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla\partial_{t}\eta_{n_{k}}(r),\nabla\eta_{n_{k}})_{[H]^{N}}\,dr\to\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla\partial_{t}\bar{\eta}(r),\nabla\bar{\eta})_{[H]^{N}}\,dr,

(3.94)

\displaystyle\bullet\,\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla\partial_{t}\theta_{n_{k}}(r),\nabla\theta_{n_{k}})_{[H]^{N}}\,dr\to\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla\partial_{t}\bar{\theta}(r),\nabla\bar{\theta})_{[H]^{N}}\,dr,

(3.95)

\displaystyle\bullet\,\eta_{n_{k}}(t)\to\bar{\eta}(t),\,\theta_{n_{k}}(t)\to\bar{\theta}(t)\mbox{ in }V.

(3.96)

Here, the pairs
[\eta_{n_{k}},\theta_{n_{k}}]
satisfy the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\int_{I}((\partial_{t}\eta_{n_{k}}+g(\eta_{n_{k}}))(t),\varphi)_{H}\,dt+\int_{I}(\nabla(\eta_{n_{k}}+\mu^{2}\partial_{t}\eta_{n_{k}})(t),\nabla\varphi)_{[H]^{N}}\,dt

(3.97)

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\alpha^{\prime}(\eta_{n_{k}}(t))\varphi\,\gamma_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}(\nabla\theta_{n_{k}}(t))\,dxdt=\int_{I}(u_{n_{k}}(t),\varphi)_{H}\,dt,

(3.98)

\displaystyle\mbox{ for any }\varphi\in V,

(3.99)

and

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\alpha_{0}(\eta_{n_{k}}(t))\partial_{t}\theta_{n_{k}}(t),\theta_{n_{k}}(t)-\psi)_{H}\,dt

(3.100)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta_{n_{k}}(t))\gamma_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}(\nabla\theta_{n_{k}}(t))\,dxdt

(3.101)

\displaystyle\quad+\nu^{2}\int_{I}(\nabla\partial_{t}\theta_{n_{k}}(t),\nabla(\theta_{n_{k}}(t)-\psi))_{[H]^{N}}\,dt

(3.102)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\eta_{n_{k}}(t))\gamma_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}(\nabla\psi)\,dxdt+\int_{I}(v_{n_{k}}(t),\theta_{n_{k}}(t)-\psi)_{H}\,dt

(3.103)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ for any }\psi\in V.

(3.104)

Then, using (
3.94
) and uniformly convergence of
\gamma_{\varepsilon_{n_{k}}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, letting
k\to\infty
in (
3.101
) yields that:

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\alpha_{0}(\bar{\eta}(t))\partial_{t}\bar{\theta}(t),\bar{\theta}(t)-\psi)_{H}\,dt+\nu^{2}\int_{I}(\nabla\partial_{t}\bar{\theta}(t),\nabla(\bar{\theta}(t)-\psi))_{[H]^{N}}\,dt

(3.105)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\bar{\eta}(t))\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\bar{\theta}(t))\,dxdt

(3.106)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\alpha(\bar{\eta}(t))\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\psi)\,dxdt+\int_{I}(v(t),\bar{\theta}(t)-\psi)_{H}\,dt.

(3.107)

On the other hand, using (
3.90
), (
3.94
), continuous embedding from
H^{1}(\Omega)
to
L^{4}(\Omega)
and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we obtain that as
k\to\infty
in (
3.98
):

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\partial_{t}\bar{\eta}(t)+g(\bar{\eta}(t)),\varphi)_{H}\,dt+\int_{I}(\nabla(\bar{\eta}+\mu^{2}\partial_{t}\bar{\eta})(t),\nabla\varphi)_{H}\,dt

(3.108)

\displaystyle+\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\alpha^{\prime}(\bar{\eta}(t))\varphi\,\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\bar{\theta}(t))\,dxdt=\int_{I}(u(t),\varphi)_{H}\,dt.

(3.109)

From (
3.90
), (
3.91
), (
3.106
) and (
3.108
), the pair
[\bar{\eta},\bar{\theta}]
is a solution to (S)
ε
, and therefore, we see that
[\bar{\eta},\bar{\theta}]
coincides the unique solution
[\eta,\theta]
.

Finally, by the uniqueness of the limit, the convergence (
1.27
) is verified. Thus, we complete the proof of Main Theorem
2
.

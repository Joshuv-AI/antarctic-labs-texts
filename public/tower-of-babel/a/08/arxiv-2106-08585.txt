Quantitative stochastic homogenization of nonlinearly elastic, random laminates
Stefan Neukamm, Mathias Schäffner, Mario Varga

Abstract
In this paper we study quantitative stochastic homogenization of a nonlinearly elastic composite material with a laminate microstructure. We prove that for deformations close to the set of rotations the homogenized stored energy function W hom W_{\hom} is C 3 C^{3} and that W hom W_{\hom} , the stress-tensor D ​ W hom DW_{\hom} , and the tangent-moduli D 2 ​ W hom D^{2}W_{\hom} can be represented with help of stochastic correctors. Furthermore, we study the error of an approximation of these quantities via representative volume elements. More precisely, we consider periodic RVEs obtained by periodizing the distribution of the random material. For materials with a fast decay of correlations on scales larger than a unit scale, we establish error estimates on the random and systematic error of the RVE with optimal scaling in the size of the RVE and with a multiplicative random constant that has exponential moments.

Introduction

A standard model for a nonlinearly elastic, composite material is given by the non-convex energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{O}W(\tfrac{x}{{}_{\varepsilon}},\nabla u)\,dx
, where
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the reference domain occupied by the elastic body,
u:O\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
its deformation, and
W:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
the stored energy function, which encodes the mechanical properties of the material. We are interested in the macroscopic behavior of composites that oscillate on a microscopic scale
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Therefore, we consider the homogenization limit
\varepsilon\to 0
of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
in the framework of
\Gamma
-convergence. The first results in this direction are due to Braides
[
Bra85
]
and Müller
[
Mül87
]
: For periodic composites (that is,
x\mapsto W(x,F)
is periodic for all
F
) and assuming additional growth conditions for
W
(in particular, standard
p
-growth with
1<p<\infty
), they show that
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converges to an energy functional of the form
u\mapsto\int_{O}W_{\hom}(\nabla u)\,dx
with a homogenized stored energy function given by the
multi-cell formula
,

W_{\hom}(F):=\lim\limits_{L\to\infty}W_{\hom,L}(F),\quad W_{\hom,L}(F):=\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Box_{L};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box_{L}}W(x,F+\nabla\varphi)\,dx,

(1)

where
\Box_{L}:=[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2})^{d}
and
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Box_{L};\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of
L
-periodic Sobolev functions. A similar homogenization result and, in particular, formula (
1
) are also valid in the random case, that is, when
x\mapsto W(x,F)
is a stationary and ergodic random field, see
[
DMM86b
,
DMM86a
,
MM94
]
.

The multi-cell formula for the homogenized stored energy function
W_{\hom}
is the main subject of this paper. Its definition invokes a nonconvex minimization problem and an asymptotic limit
L\to\infty
, and, therefore, a priori not much is known about its analytic properties.
W_{\hom}
describes the effective mechanical behavior of the composite. It is especially important to understand the
first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor
and the
tangent modulus
, i.e., the Jacobian
DW_{\hom}(F)
and the Hessian
D^{2}W_{\hom}(F)
.
Therefore, it is natural and relevant to investigate the following questions:

(Q1)

Is
W_{\hom}
twice continuously differentiable?

(Q2)

How can
W_{\hom}(F)
,
DW_{\hom}(F)
and
D^{2}W_{\hom}(F)
be evaluated or approximated?

In this paper we study these questions in the regime of small, but finite strains for random laminates composed of frame indifferent materials with a stress-free, non-degenerate reference state (see Definition
2.1
below). In a nutshell, the main results of this paper can be summarized as follows:

•

(Qualitative results and answer to (Q1)). We show
W_{\hom}\in C^{2}(U)
for an open neighborhood
U
of
\operatorname{SO}(d)
, see Theorem
2.4
(iii). This result is based on a representation of
W_{\hom}(F)
for
F\in U
with help of a sublinear corrector
\varphi_{F}
that solves the nonlinear corrector equation
-{\rm div}DW(x,F+\nabla\varphi_{F})=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see Theorem
2.4
(ii). Since
W
is non-convex, this corrector representation is highly non-trivial and extends a recent results for periodic composites by the first two authors
[
NS18
,
NS19
]
.

•

(Quantitative results and answer to (Q2)). We prove optimal error estimates for a periodic representative volume element approximation of
W_{\hom}(F)
,
DW_{\hom}(F)
and
D^{2}W_{\hom}(F)
for
F
close to
\operatorname{SO}(d)
, see Theorem
2.11
and the discussion at the end of this introduction.

Before we present our results in detail, we discuss previous, related works.

Convex case.
We first note that a complete understanding of (Q1) is currently only available for convex integrands with quadratic growth. Indeed, as shown by Müller
[
Mül87
]
, if
W(x,F)
is periodic in
x
and convex in
F
, the multi-cell formula reduces to a
one-cell formula
that can be represented with help of a
corrector
, i.e.,
W_{\hom}(F)=W_{\hom,1}(F)=\int_{\Box}W(y,F+\nabla\varphi_{F})\,dy
, where the corrector
\varphi_{F}
is a minimizer of the minimization problem in the definition of
W_{\hom,1}(F)
. Furthermore, in
[
GMT93
, Theorem 5.4]
it is shown that, if
W(x,F)
is additionally
C^{2}
in
F
and satisfies a quadratic growth condition in
F
, then
W_{\hom}
is
C^{2}
. The argument exploits the
corrector representation
of
W_{\hom}
and only uses basic energy estimates for the corrector. It extends verbatim to the random, convex case.

To reach regularity beyond
C^{2}
is a nontrivial task and requires improved regularity of the corrector. For convex potentials with quadratic growth, the corrector
\varphi_{F}
can be characterized by a monotone, uniformly elliptic system with quadratic growth and thus, a stronger regularity theory is available; we refer to
[
NS18
]
for
C^{3}
-regularity of the homogenized potential in the periodic, vector-valued case, and
[
FN21
,
AFK20
,
CG21
]
for related result for random, monotone equations.

Non-convex case.

The results mentioned so far do not extend to the case of nonlinear elasticity, since there the stored energy function is necessarily non-convex.
In fact, Müller
[
Mül87
, Theorem 4.3]
provides a counterexample in form of a laminate material that features a buckling instability under compressive loading; in particular, one has
W_{\hom}(F)<W_{\hom,L}(F)
for some
F\not\in\operatorname{SO}(d)
and all
L\in\mathbb{N}
. This shows that we cannot even expect a one-cell formula nor a corrector representation to hold for general deformations
F
. However, a better behaviour can be expected for deformations close to
\operatorname{SO}(d)
for materials with a non-degenerate, stress-free reference state at the identity. Indeed, in
[
MN11
]
the first author and Müller show for periodic composites that
W_{\hom}
admits a quadratic expansion at identity:

W_{\hom}(Id+G)=Q_{\hom}(G)+o(|G|^{2})

(2)

where
Q_{\hom}(G):=\inf_{\varphi\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Box;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{\Box}Q(x,G+\nabla\varphi)\,dx
, and
Q(x,G):=\frac{1}{2}D^{2}W(x,Id)[G,G]
denotes the quadratic term in the expansion of
W
at identity. The argument uses soft properties of the corrector
\varphi_{F}
and appeals to the geometric rigidity estimate of
[
FJM02
]
. An analogous statement holds in the random case, see
[
GN11
]
. Identity (
2
) says that the tangent modulus of
W_{\hom}
at identity is given by the homogenization of the tangent modulus of
W
.

Differentiability for small, but finite strains.
To establish an expansion similar to (
2
) for
F\not\in SO(d)
is nontrivial — and, in fact, not always possible, cf. Müller’s counterexample mentioned above. Therefore, it is natural to focus on the regime of small strains, i.e., when
F
is close to
\operatorname{SO}(d)
. The first differentiability result for
W_{\hom}
away from
\operatorname{SO}(d)
has been recently obtained by the first and second author in
[
NS18
,
NS19
]
for a periodic composite with a “regular” microstructure, e.g., a matrix material with smooth, possibly touching inclusions. It is shown that if
W(x,\cdot)
is
C^{2}
in a neighbourhood of
\operatorname{SO}(d)
, then the homogenized stored energy function
W_{\hom}
is
C^{2}
in a neighbourhood of
\operatorname{SO}(d)
, say in
U_{\rho}:=\big\{F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\,:\,{\rm dist}(F,SO(d))<\rho\,\big\}
for some
\rho>0
. The argument critically relies on the observation that for
F\in U_{\rho}
the multi-cell formula simplifies to a single-cell formula that features a representation with help of a periodic corrector. More precisely, in
[
NS18
,
NS19
]
we prove that for any
F\in U_{\rho}
there exists a periodic corrector
\varphi_{F}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Box;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

W_{\hom}(F)=W_{\hom,1}(F)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box}W(x,F+\nabla\varphi_{F}(x))\,dx.

(3)

Moreover, we obtain a similar representation for the derivatives of
W_{\hom}
, in particular, we show that the tangent modulus admits the respresentation

D^{2}W_{\hom}(F)H\cdot G=\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box}D^{2}W(x,F+\nabla\varphi_{F})(H+\nabla\varphi_{F,H})\cdot G\,dx

for the periodic, linearized corrector
\varphi_{F,H}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Box;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. These corrector representations are the starting point for a quantitative analysis of
W_{\hom}
and minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
; in particular, in
[
NS18
,
NS19
]
we prove error estimates for the nonlinear two-scale expansion and we establish Lipschitz estimates for minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
that are uniform in
\varepsilon>0
. The general strategy of proof for (
3
) relies on a reduction to the convex case based on two major ingredients:

•

A
matching convex lower bound
: Inspired by
[
CDMK06
]
we construct in
[
NS18
, Corollary 2.3]
a strongly convex integrand
V
with quadratic growth such that

\displaystyle W(x,F)+\mu\det F\ \geq V(x,F)\qquad\mbox{for all $F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$,}

\displaystyle W(x,F)+\mu\det F\ =V(x,F)\qquad\mbox{for all }F\in U_{\rho},

(4)

where
\mu>0
and
\rho>0
only depend on
W
.
With help of
V
, convex homogenization theory and the fact that
F\mapsto\det F
is a Null-Lagrangian, we obtain the lower bound
W_{\hom}(F)+\mu\det F\geq V_{\hom}(F)=\int_{\Box}V(x,F+\nabla\varphi_{F})
with a convex corrector
\varphi_{F}
given as the unique (up to a constant) sublinear solution to the monotone corrector problem,

-\nabla\cdot DV(x,F+\nabla\varphi_{F})=0\qquad\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$.}

(5)

•

Global Lipschitz regularity.
By the regularity theory for uniformly elliptic, monotone systems with piecewise constant, periodic coefficients, we establish the following global Lipschitz estimate for the convex corrector

\|{\rm dist}(F+\nabla\varphi_{F},SO(d))\|_{L^{\infty}(\Box)}\leq C{\rm dist}(F,SO(d)),

where for
d>2
we require the right-hand side to satisfy an additional smallness condition, see
[
NS19
, Corollary 1]
. In view of the matching property of
V
(cf. (
4
)), this allows us to deduce the sought for corrector representation (
3
).

Random composites and laminates.
The construction of the matching convex lower bound
V
verbatim extends to the random case, see Lemma
3.2
below. Nevertheless the analysis in
[
NS18
,
NS19
]
is restricted to periodic composites, since periodicity is critically used to obtain the required global Lipschitz estimate for the corrector: Indeed, in
[
NS19
]
we obtain the Lipschitz estimate by combining a small-scale Lipschitz estimate (cf.
[
NS19
, Theorem 4]
) of the form
\|\nabla\varphi_{F}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Box_{1/2})}\leq C\|\nabla\varphi_{F}\|_{L^{2}(\Box)}
with the energy estimate
\|\nabla\varphi_{F}\|_{L^{2}(\Box)}\leq C{\rm dist}(F,SO(d))
for sublinear solutions to (
5
). While the latter is standard in the periodic case, in the random setting such an estimate generally only holds in the weaker form of a
large scale

L^{2}
-estimate: For all
L
larger than a random minimal radius we have
L^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|\nabla\varphi_{F}\|_{L^{2}(\Box_{L})}\leq C{\rm dist}(F,SO(d))
; we refer to
[
FN21
]
where such an estimate has been established for monotone systems. Thus, in the random setting we may only expect the bound

\|{\rm dist}(F+\nabla\varphi_{F},SO(d))\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))}\leq\mathcal{C}(x){\rm dist}(F,SO(d)),

where
\mathcal{C}(x)
is a stationary random field with stretched exponential moments. We note that this Lipschitz estimate is not global, which prevents us to follow the strategy of
[
NS19
]
.

In contrast, for random laminates, as considered in this paper, the situation is better, since then the corrector problems for
\varphi_{F}
and the linearized corrector
\varphi_{F,G}
, simplify to ordinary differential equations, see Theorem
2.4
(ii)(b). This allows us to retrieve global, deterministic Lipschitz estimates by appealing to ODE-arguments, see Lemma
3.9
below.

Quantitative periodic RVEs.
We finally comment on our quantitative analysis of the RVE approximation for
W_{\hom}
,
DW_{\hom}
, and
D^{2}W_{\hom}
. As we explain in detail in the next section, in our paper we consider parametrized models to describe random laminates. More precisely, we denote by
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
a probability space of parameter fields
\omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
where
\mathbb{P}
is stationary and ergodic w.r.t. the shifts
\omega\mapsto\omega(\cdot+z)
,
z\in\mathbb{R}
. We then consider stored energy functions of the form
(x,F)\mapsto W(\omega(x_{d}),F)
, where
x_{d}
denotes the
d
th coordinate of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see Assumption
2.2
. Our qualitative Theorem
2.4
then yields the corrector representation formula

W_{\hom}(F)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box}W(\omega(x_{d}),F+\nabla\varphi_{F})\,dx\Big],

(6)

for
F
sufficiently close to
SO(d)
. A direct evaluation of (
6
) is not possible in practice, since the expression invokes two “infinities”: The domain of the corrector equation (
5
) is unbounded and the probability space has infinite degrees of freedom. Hence, a suitable approximation is required. The
representative volume element
(RVE) method is a well-established procedure for this purpose. In this method, the corrector problem is considered (for a finite number of samples) on a domain of a
finite
size
L
together with suitable boundary conditions. It is an ongoing discussion in the computational mechanics community how to choose the size of the RVE and the appropriate boundary conditions, e.g. see
[
KFG+03
,
SJO21
]
and the references therein. In particular, we note that
[
KFG+03
]
also provides a numerical study of the convergence rate for the RVE-approximation of the tangent modulus of a nonlinear material.

The first analytic convergence results with optimal scaling in
L
for the RVE-approximation have been obtained by Gloria and Otto in
[
GO11
,
GO12
]
for discrete, linear elliptic equations. Periodic-RVEs have been first investigated in
[
GNO15
]
in the discrete setting and recently in
[
FN21
]
in the case of monotone systems. In periodic-RVEs (in the form of
[
GNO15
,
FN21
]
) the original probability measure
\mathbb{P}
is approximated by a stationary, probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{L}
that is supported on
L
-periodic parametrizations. The effect of this approximation is that for
\mathbb{P}_{L}
-almost every realization, the corrector equation simplifies to an equation on a
finite
domain (of size
L
) with
periodic boundary conditions
. These equations are well-posed pathwise and thus for
\mathbb{P}_{L}
-a.e. parameter field
\omega_{L}\in\Omega
the proxy

W_{\hom,L}(\omega_{L},F):=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box_{L}}W(\omega_{L}(x_{d}),F+\nabla\varphi_{F}(\omega_{L},\cdot))\,dx

is well-defined (provided
F\in U_{\rho}
). As already observed in
[
GO11
]
the RVE-error naturally decomposes as

W_{\hom,L}(F)-W_{\hom}(F)=\underbrace{W_{\hom,L}(F)-\mathbb{E}_{L}\Big[W_{\hom,L}(F)\Big]}_{=:{\rm err}_{\rm rand}(L)}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{L}\Big[W_{\hom,L}(F)-W_{\hom}(F)\Big]}_{=:{\rm err}_{\rm syst}(L)},

into a random error (due to random fluctuations w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}_{L}
) and a systematic error due to differences of
\mathbb{P}_{L}
and
\mathbb{P}
. As already observed in
[
GNO15
]
, in order to quantify the error, the assumption of ergodicity needs to be quantified. Moreover, both errors are sensitive to the decay of correlations of the random material, see also
[
GNO20
]
for a detailed analysis on the impact of the decay rate of correlations and the scaling of the sublinear corrector.
In the present paper we study the periodic-RVE approximation under the strongest possible assumption on the decay of correlations, namely, materials that feature a rapid decay of correlations on scales larger than
1
. In the existing literature two different approaches exist to quantify ergodicity: Either by means of mixing conditions as in
[
AS16
,
AKM16
]
or in form of functional inequalities, see
[
NS98
,
GO11
,
GO12
,
GNO20
,
DG20a
,
DG20b
]
. In the present paper we follow the second approach and work with a spectral gap estimate. In essence, we make the following assumptions:

•

Existence of an
L
-periodic approximation
: There exists a shift-invariant probability measure
\mathbb{P}_{L}
that is supported on
L
-periodic fields in
\Omega
and that recovers the distribution of
\mathbb{P}
in
\Box_{L/2}
, see Definition
2.8
for details.

•

Fast decorrelations
:
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
and
(\Omega,\mathbb{P}_{L})
feature
spectral gap estimates
that encode fast decorrelations on scales larger than
1
, see Definition
2.9
.

If
W(\omega,F)
is sufficiently regular in
\omega
and
F
, and for deformations
F
that are sufficiently close to
SO(d)
, our main result then yields estimates on the error of the RVE-approximation of
W_{\hom}(F)
, the stress tensor
DW_{\hom}
and the tangent modulus
D^{2}W_{\hom}
. In particular, for
W_{\hom,L}
we get,

\displaystyle|{\rm err}_{\rm rand}|\,\leq\mathcal{C}\,{\rm dist}^{2}(F,SO(d))L^{-\frac{1}{2}},\qquad|{\rm err}_{\rm syst}(L)|\,\leq C\,{\rm dist}^{2}(F,SO(d))\frac{\ln L}{L},

where
\mathcal{C}
denotes a random constant with
\mathbb{E}_{L}\big[\exp(\tfrac{1}{C}\mathcal{C})\big]\leq 2
, and
C
denotes a deterministic constant, see Theorem
2.11
. These estimates are optimal w.r.t. scaling in
L
and w.r.t. the integrability of
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, they are the first analytical estimates for RVEs for geometrically nonlinear composites. Due to the different scaling of the random and systematic error, our results allows for optimizing the ratio between the number of Monte-Carlo iterations and the size of the RVE, see Corollary
2.12
.

2
Setting and main results

In this section we first introduce the standard setting for the description of stationary random media. Consequently we present the main results about nonconvex integral functionals in the vicinity of rotations: In Theorem
2.4
we argue that the RVE approximations are well-defined and the so-called stochastic one-cell formula holds. Theorem
2.11
describes the precision of the RVE method in terms of the size of the representative elements.

We consider the parameter space
\Omega=\left\{{\omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\quad\text{measurable}}\right\}
equipped with a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{S}
and a probability measure
\mathbb{P}
. The mathematical expectation is denoted by
\mathbb{E}\left[{\cdot}\right]:=\int_{\Omega}\cdot d\mathbb{P}(\omega)
. A sample
\omega\in\Omega
describes the spatially-varying (layered) material properties and for this reason we frequently refer to it as a
configuration
. We assume the following standard assumptions for the random configuration space
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P})
:

(P1)

(
Stationarity
.) For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, the vectors
\left({\omega(x_{1}),...,\omega(x_{n})}\right)
and
\left({\omega(x_{1}+z),...,\omega(x_{n}+z)}\right)
have the same joint distribution for arbitrary
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(P2)

(
Ergodicity
.) For any random variable
\mathcal{F}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
we have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{0}^{R}\mathcal{F}(\omega(\cdot+x))dx=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\right].

We state the assumption on the considered energy density
W
as follows. We first introduce a class of frame-indifferent stored energy functions that are minimized, non-degenerate and smooth at identity, and satisfy the growth condition stated below.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\alpha>0
and

, we denote by
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}^{p}
the class of Borel functions
W:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to[0,+\infty]
which satisfies the following properties:

(W1)

satisfies

\alpha|F|^{p}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq W(F)\quad\mbox{for all $F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$};

(W2)

W
is frame indifferent, i.e.

W(RF)=W(F)\quad\mbox{for all $R\in\operatorname{SO}(d)$, $F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$};

(W3)

F=\operatorname{Id}
is a natural state and
W
is non-degenerate, i.e.
W(\operatorname{Id})=0
and

\displaystyle W(F)

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\operatorname{dist}^{2}(F,\operatorname{SO}(d))\quad\mbox{for all $F\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$;}

(7)

(W4)

W
is

in a neighborhood of
\operatorname{SO}(d)
and

\|W\|_{C^{3}(\overline{U_{\alpha}})}<\frac{1}{\alpha}.

(8)

.

Fix
\alpha>0
and

. We suppose that
W:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to[0,+\infty]
is a Borel function and
W(\omega,\cdot)\in\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}^{p}
for almost every
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that we use the same constant
\alpha
in the growth condition
(W1), the non-degeneracy condition (W3) and the regularity
assumption (W4). The only reason for this is that we want to reduce the number
of parameters invoked in the assumption for

. Let us anticipate
that the region, in which the multi-cell formula reduces to a
single-cell expression, will depend (in a quite implicit way) on
the constants in (W1), (W3) and (W4). Hence, working with the
single parameter

simplifies the presentation. Note that we
have
W^{p}_{\alpha}\subset W^{p}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
for
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
.

We associate with

the following approximation for the homogenized stored energy function

W_{\mathrm{hom},L}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad W_{\mathrm{hom},L}(\omega,F)=\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{per},0}(\Box_{L};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box_{L}}W(\omega(x_{d}),F+\nabla\varphi)dx.

(9)

We state the first main result of this paper as follows. Based on a merely qualitative ergodicity assumption combined the laminated structure of the material, we show that in the vicinity of rotations correctors exist and the homogenized stored energy function is characterized by a stochastic one-cell formula. We also discuss regularity properties of the homogenized stored energy function.

Theorem 2.4

(Corrector representation and regularity of

)
.

Let
\left({\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P}}\right)
satisfy

-

(i)

(Homogenized energy density). The following limit exists and it is deterministic

\displaystyle W_{\hom}(F):=

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}W_{\hom,L}(\omega,F)\qquad\mbox{for $\mathbb{P}$-a.a. $\omega\in\Omega$.}

(ii)

(Corrector). There exists a unique random field
\varphi_{F}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.a.
\omega\in\Omega

,
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\Box}\varphi_{F}(\omega,\cdot)=0

(11)

The homogenized energy density given in (i) satisfies the stochastic one-cell formula

Remark 2.5

(
C^{k}
-regularity)
.

In Theorem
2.4
, we show that
\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lVert W(\omega,\cdot)\right\rVert_{C^{3}(\overline{U_{\alpha}})}<\infty
implies
W_{\mathrm{hom},L}(\omega,\cdot),W_{\mathrm{hom}}\in C^{3}(U_{\overline{\delta}})
. By an inductive argument that exploits the laminate structure, it is also possible to show that
\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}\left\lVert W(\omega,\cdot)\right\rVert_{C^{k}(\overline{U_{\alpha}})}<\infty
implies
W_{\mathrm{hom},L}(\omega,\cdot),W_{\mathrm{hom}}\in C^{k}(U_{\overline{\delta}})
for an arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, explicit formulas for the derivatives in terms of linearized correctors of higher order can be obtained, for example, we have, for
F,G,H,I\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,

\displaystyle D^{3}W_{\mathrm{hom}}(F)IHG=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\Box}D^{3}W(\omega(x_{d}),F+\nabla\varphi_{F})\left({I+\nabla\varphi_{F,I}}\right)\left({H+\nabla\varphi_{F,H}}\right)\cdot(G+\nabla\varphi_{F,G})dx}\right].

(19)

For deterministic, periodic composites, the conclusion of Theorem
2.4
was proven in
[
NS18
,
NS19
]
under various assumptions on the regularity of
x\mapsto W(x,F)
. However, Theorem
2.4
is the first corrector representation result for nonlinearly elastic materials with a random microstructure. Furthermore, the smallness assumption
F\in U_{\bar{\delta}}
in Theorem
2.4
cannot be relaxed as can be seen by Müller’s counterexample
[
Mül87
, Theorem 4.7]
which features a periodic laminate material that undergoes buckling for sufficiently strong compressions.

We remark that Assumption
2.2
does not impose any condition on the growth of

from above;

might even be equal to

for

outside of an open neigbourhood of

. Therefore, it is unclear if the energy functional

homogenizes (in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence). However, if

additionally satisfies

the corresponding

-limit takes the form
u\mapsto\int_{O}\overline{W}_{\mathrm{hom}}(\nabla u)dx
where

\overline{W}_{\mathrm{hom}}(F)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Box_{L};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Box_{L}}W(\omega(x_{d}),F+\nabla\varphi(x))dx.

and

match in the vicinity of rotations.

.

Consider the setting of Theorem

. There exists
{\overline{\overline{\delta}}}={\overline{\overline{\delta}}}(\alpha,d,p)>0
such that

(See Section

Remark 2.7
.

Homogenization of
u\mapsto\int_{O}W(\omega(\frac{x_{d}}{\varepsilon}),\nabla u)dx
in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence has been established under weaker conditions than the upper-growth condition (
20
), e.g., see the classic textbooks
[
BD98
,
JKO94
]
, and more recent works
[
DG16
,
AM11
,
HNS21
,
NSS17
]
. However, to our knowledge all existing results require some control from above or some convexity condition on the set
\{W<\infty\}
that rule out the application to stored energy functions that satisfy physical growth conditions. In contrast, with help of the corrector representation of
W_{\rm hom}
it is possible to prove homogenization in a small-strain regime without imposing an additional growth condition for
W
. More precisely, by adapting the method of
[
NS18
, Theorem 3]
we get the following: Let
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be sufficiently smooth and let
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset g+W_{0}^{1,p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\inf_{g+W_{0}^{1,p}(A)}\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}+o(1)\quad\mbox{where}\quad\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{O}W(\omega(\tfrac{x_{d}}{{}_{\varepsilon}}),\nabla u)-f\cdot u\,dx.

Suppose that the assumptions of Theorem
2.4
are satisfied and assume the smallness condition
\|f\|_{L^{q}(A)}+\|g-{\rm id}\|_{W^{2,q}(A)}\ll 1
for some
q>d
. Then it holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{p}(O)}=0\qquad\mbox{$\mathbb{P}$-a.s.,}

(21)

where
u_{0}
denotes the unique minimizer of the homogenized energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\hom}(u):=\int_{O}W_{\hom}(\nabla u)-f\cdot u\,dx\qquad\mbox{subject to}\quad u-g\in W_{0}^{1,p}(O).

Since in Theorem
2.4
we do not assume any growth conditions from above on
F\mapsto W(\omega,F)
, the convergence (
21
) does not follow from the known homogenization theory.

In the following we discuss an approximation of
W_{\mathrm{hom}}
and its derivatives by periodic RVEs. In contrast to the approximation (
9
), which relies on the introduction of periodic boundary conditions, the periodic RVE that we discuss below is analogous to the one considered in
[
FN21
]
and is based on a periodic approximation of the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P})
:

Definition 2.8

(
L
-periodic approximation of
\mathbb{P}
)
.

Let
L\geq 1
. A probability space
(\Omega_{L}=\Omega,\mathcal{S}_{L}=\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P}_{L})
is called an
L
-periodic approximation of
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P})
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\mathbb{P}_{L}
concentrates on
L
-periodic functions and it is stationary in the sense of
(P1)
.

(ii)

If the random field
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\ni(\omega,x)\mapsto\omega(x)
is distributed according to
\mathbb{P}
and
\Omega_{L}\times\mathbb{R}\ni(\omega_{L},x)\mapsto\omega_{L}(x)
according to
\mathbb{P}_{L}
, then the random fields

\Omega\times B_{\frac{L}{4}}(0)\ni(\omega,x)\mapsto\omega(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\Omega_{L}\times B_{\frac{L}{4}}(0)\ni(\omega_{L},x)\mapsto\omega_{L}(x)

have the same distribution.

In order to quantify the statement in Theorem
2.4
(i) we will make an additional quantitative ergodicity assumption on the probability space and its periodic approximation. This relies on a Malliavin type functional calculus, see
[
DG20b
,
DG20a
]
for a systematic discussion.

Definition 2.9

(Spectral gaps)
.

Let
L\geq 1
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
(\Omega_{L},\mathcal{S}_{L},\mathbb{P}_{L})
its
L
-periodic approximation.

(i)

We say that
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P})
satisfies a spectral gap estimate with constant
\rho>0
if the following holds:
For any random variable
\mathcal{F}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[{|\mathcal{F}-\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathcal{F}}\right]|^{2}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left({\int_{B_{1}(s)}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\omega}\right|}\right)^{2}ds}\right],

where
\int_{B_{1}(s)}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\omega}\right|
denotes

\sup_{\delta\omega}\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{|\mathcal{F}(\omega+t\delta\omega)-\mathcal{F}(\omega)|}{t},

where the supremum is taken over all configurations
\delta\omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
supported in
B_{1}(s)
with
\left\lVert\delta\omega\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
.

(ii)

(Periodic spectral gap.) We say that
(\Omega_{L},\mathcal{S}_{L},\mathbb{P}_{L})
satisfies a periodic spectral gap estimate with constant
\rho>0
if the following holds: For any random variable
\mathcal{F}_{L}:\Omega_{L}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{L}\left[|\mathcal{F}-\mathbb{E}_{L}[\mathcal{F}]|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{L}\left[\int_{0}^{L}\left({\int_{B(s)}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\omega_{L}}\right|}\right)^{2}ds\right],

where
\int_{B(s)}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial\omega_{L}}\right|
denotes

\sup_{\delta\omega_{L}}\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{|\mathcal{F}(\omega_{L}+t\delta\omega_{L})-\mathcal{F}(\omega_{L})|}{t},

where the supremum is taken over all
L
-periodic configurations
\delta\omega_{L}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
supported in
B_{1}(s)+L\mathbb{Z}
and with
\left\lVert\delta\omega_{L}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,L)}\leq 1
.

Remark 2.10

(Example of Gaussian type)
.

It is convenient to give examples of admissible distributions in the class of stationary Gaussian random fields, since the latter are uniquely determined by their covariance functions and allow to apply Malliavin calculus to establish spectral gap inequalities. A specific example is the following: Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
describes a stationary, centered Gaussian random field
\omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with a bounded and compactly supported covariance function
\mathcal{C}(s):={\rm Cov}(\omega(x+s),\omega(s))
satisfying
|\mathcal{C}(s)|=0
for
|s|\geq\ell/2
for some correlation length
\ell>0
. Then
(\Omega,\mathcal{S},\mathbb{P})
satisfies the spectral gap estimate of Definition
2.9
(i), e.g. see
[
DG20b
]
. For
L\geq 4\ell
we may define an
L
-periodic approximation by periodizing the covariance function: Set
\mathcal{C}_{L}(x):=\mathcal{C}(\{x\}_{L})
, where
\{x\}_{L}\in[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2})
is uniquely defined by
x-\{x\}_{L}\in L\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{L}
describe the stationary, centered Gaussian random field with covariance function
\mathcal{C}_{L}
. Then
\mathbb{P}_{L}
is indeed an
L
-periodic approximation of
\mathbb{P}
in the sense of Definition
2.8
and additionally satisfies the spectral gap estimate of Definition
2.9
(ii) with a constant
\rho
that is independent of
L
.

We present our main quantitative result:

(Quantitative RVE approximations)
.

Suppose Assumption
2.2
is satisfied and in addition that

of Definition

(ii).

denotes the corresponding representative volume element approximation defined in (

Then, there exists
c=c(\alpha,p,d,\rho)\in[1,\infty)
such that for all
F\in U_{\overline{\delta}}
with
\overline{\delta}>0
as in Theorem

the following statements hold true:

(i)

(Estimate on random fluctuations).

\displaystyle|W_{\mathrm{hom},L}(\cdot,F)-\mathbb{E}_{L}\left[{W_{\mathrm{hom},L}(F)}\right]|

(22)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{L}\left[{W_{\mathrm{hom},L}(F)}\right]-W_{\mathrm{hom}}(F)|

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}_{L}\left[{DW_{\mathrm{hom},L}(F)}\right]-DW_{\mathrm{hom}}(F)|

\left\{{\omega_{i}}\right\}_{i\in\left\{{1,...,N}\right\}}

and we define the Monte Carlo approximation by

(Monte Carlo approximation)
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem

with

as in Theorem

and for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{L}\left[{|D^{\ell}W_{\mathrm{hom},L,N}(F)-D^{\ell}W_{\rm hom}(F)|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\;\mathrm{dist}^{2-\ell}(F,\operatorname{SO}(d))\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{NL}}+\frac{\log(L)}{L}\biggr).

The proof of Corollary
2.12
is standard and thus omitted here. Note that the total

-error is of order

if we choose

.

3
Deterministic intermediate results

In the following we present the deterministic ingredients of our analysis. In particular, in the first part we recall a matching convex lower bound construction that allows us to relate the nonconvex corrector problem with a convex corrector problem. In the second part we collect some standard results about elliptic equations, and in the third part we establish deterministic Lipschitz estimates for correctors that rely on the laminate structure of the material.

3.1
Reduction to a convex problem

The proof of Theorem
2.4
follows the strategy of
[
NS18
]
. The starting point is the observation that
W\in\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}^{p}
implies the existence of a “matching convex lower bound”. For the precise statement we introduce the following class of strongly convex functions:

Definition 3.1

(Convex energy density)
.

For
\beta>0
we denote by
\mathcal{V}_{\beta}
the set of functions
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfying for all
F,G\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

\displaystyle\beta|F|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\leq V(F)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}(|F|^{2}+1),

\displaystyle|DV(F)[G]|\leq\frac{1}{\beta}(1+|F|)|G|,

\displaystyle\beta|G|^{2}\leq D^{2}V(F)[G,G]\leq\frac{1}{\beta}|G|^{2}.

The following lemma is proven in
[
NS18
]
extending a construction that appeared earlier in the context of discrete energies in
[
CDMK06
,
FT02
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Matching convex lower bound, see
[
NS18
, Corollary 2.3]
)
.

Let
Assumption
2.2
be satisfied. Then there exist
\delta,\mu,\beta>0
(depending on
\alpha,d
and
p
), and a Borel measurable function
V:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying for almost every
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
V(\omega,\cdot)\in\mathcal{V}_{\beta}
,

3.2

We formulate three simple deterministic existence, uniqueness and regularity statements for uniformly elliptic equations.

Definition 3.4

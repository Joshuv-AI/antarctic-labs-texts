Optimal quadrature errors and sampling numbers for Sobolev spaces with logarithmic perturbation on spheres
Jiaxin Geng, Yun Ling, Jiansong Li, Heping Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study optimal quadrature errors, approximation numbers, and sampling numbers in L 2 ​ ( 𝕊 d ) L_{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}) for Sobolev spaces H α , β ​ ( 𝕊 d ) {\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}) with logarithmic perturbation on the unit sphere 𝕊 d {\mathbb{S}}^{d} in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} . First we obtain strong equivalences of the approximation numbers for H α , β ​ ( 𝕊 d ) {\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}) with α > 0 \alpha>0 , which gives a clue to Open problem 3 as posed by Krieg and Vybíral in [ 27 ] . Second, for the optimal quadrature errors for H α , β ​ ( 𝕊 d ) {\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}) , we use the “fooling” function technique to get lower bounds in the case α > d / 2 \alpha>d/2 , and apply Hilbert space structure and Vybíral’s theorem about Schur product theory to obtain lower bounds in the case α = d / 2 , β > 1 / 2 \alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2 of small smoothness, which confirms the conjecture as posed by Grabner and Stepanyukin in [ 14 ] and solves Open problem 2 in [ 27 ] . Finally, we employ the weighted least squares operators and the least squares quadrature rules to obtain approximation theorems and quadrature errors for H α , β ​ ( 𝕊 d ) {\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}) with α > d / 2 \alpha>d/2 or α = d / 2 , β > 1 / 2 \alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2 , which are order optimal. .

1.
Introduction and main results

For
d\geq 2
, denote
{S}^{d}=\{{\bf x}\in{R}^{d+1}|\,\|{\bf x}\|=(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d+1}^{2})^{1/2}=1\}
by the unit sphere.
The integral of a continuous function
f:{{S}}^{d}\to{R}
is denoted by

{\rm INT}(f):=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})\,d\sigma_{d}({\bf x}),

where
\sigma_{d}(\bf x)
is the normalized surface measure on

{{S}}^{d}
(i.e.,
\sigma_{d}({{S}}^{d})=1
). Denote by

L_{p}\equiv L_{p}({{S}}^{d})
the usual Lebesgue space with norm

\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}}
. Then
L_{2}
is a Hilbert space with inner
product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf x})g({\bf x})d\sigma_{d}({\bf x}).

Let
H
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
{{S}}^{d}
with norm

\|\cdot\|_{H}
. We want to approximate
{\rm INT}(f)
from
f\in H

using quadrature formulas

Q_{{\bf c},X_{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}f({\bf x}_{j})

with weights
{\bf c}\in{{R}}^{n}
and knots
X_{n}=\{{{\bf x}_{1}},{{\bf x}_{2}},\cdot\cdot\cdot,{{\bf x}_{n}}\}\subset{{S}}^{d}
.

The worst case error of
Q_{c,X_{n}}
is defined by

e(Q_{{\bf c},X_{n}},H,{\rm INT}):=\sup_{\|f\|_{H}\leq 1}|{\rm INT}(f)-Q_{{\bf c},X_{n}}(f)|,

and the
n
-th optimal quadrature error

e_{n}(H,{\rm INT}):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf c}\in{R}^{n}\\
X_{n}\subset{S}^{d}\end{subarray}}e(Q_{{\bf c},X_{n}},H,{\rm INT})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf c}\in{R}^{n}\\
X_{n}\subset{S}^{d}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\|f\|_{H}\leq 1}|{\rm INT}(f)-Q_{{\bf c},X_{n}}(f)|.

We write

e_{n}(H)=e_{n}(H,{\rm INT}).

We are also interested in approximation numbers and (linear)
sampling numbers in
L_{2}
. We define the approximation numbers

a_{n}(H)
by

a_{n}(H)\equiv a_{n}(H,L_{2}):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{1},...,L_{n}\in H^{{}^{\prime}}\\
g_{1},...,g_{n}\in L_{2}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\|f\|_{H}\leq 1}\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}({f})g_{i}\|_{L_{2}},

and the (linear) sampling numbers
g_{n}(H)
by

g_{n}(H)\equiv g_{n}^{\rm lin}(H,L_{2}):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf x}_{i},...,{\bf x}_{n}\in{{S}}^{d}\\
g_{1},...,g_{n}\in L_{2}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\|f\|_{H}\leq 1}\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f({\bf x}_{i})g_{i}\|_{L_{2}},

where
H^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate space of
H
consisting of all
continuous linear functionals on
H
. The approximation numbers
and the (linear) sampling numbers reflect the errors of the best
possible linear algorithms for
L_{2}
approximation using at most

n
continuous linear functionals on
H
or
n
function values,
respectively. Clearly, we have

(1.1)

a_{n}(H)\leq g_{n}(H)\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ e_{n}(H)\leq g_{n}(H).

By (
1.1
) it
suffices to discuss lower bounds for
e_{n}(H)
and upper bounds for

g_{n}(H)
.

This paper is devoted to discussing the optimal quadrature error
and the sampling numbers for the Sobolev spaces
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
with logarithmic perturbation (see the
definition in Section 2). Note that
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
is
a reproducing kernel Hilbert space if and only if

\alpha>d/2,\beta\in{R}
or
\alpha=d/2,\beta>1/2
. Moreover, this
space is just the Besov space
B_{2,2}^{\Omega}({{S}}^{d})
(see

[
46
,
47
]
) of generalized smoothness with equivalent norms,
where
\Omega(t)=t^{\alpha}(1+(\ln\frac{1}{t})_{+})^{\beta}
for
t>0
,

a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
, see Subsection 2.1.

For the classical Sobolev spaces
{\rm H}^{\alpha}({{S}}^{d})={\rm H}^{\alpha,0}({{S}}^{d})
on
{{S}}^{d}
, upper and lower bounds for the optimal
quadrature error were obtained in
[
3
]
and
[
17
]
,
asymptotics and strong equivalences of the approximation numbers
in
[
23
]
and
[
5
]
, and asymptotics of the sampling numbers
in
[
47
]
. For the Sobolev spaces
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
with logarithmic perturbation, asymptotics
of the approximation numbers were obtained in
[
46
]
for

\alpha>0
, asymptotics of the sampling numbers in
[
47
]
for

\alpha>d/2
. Specifically, it was shown in
[
46
]
that for

\alpha>0
,

(1.2)

a_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp n^{-\alpha/d}\ln^{-\beta}n,

and in
[
47
]
that for
\alpha>d/2
,

(1.3)

g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp n^{-\alpha/d}\ln^{-\beta}n,

where for positive
sequences
\{a_{n}\},\{b_{n}\}
,
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
means that there
is a constant
c>0
independent of
n
such that
a_{n}\leq cb_{n}
for all
n\geq 2
. We write
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
if
b_{n}\lesssim a_{n}
and
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
.

In the case
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
of small smoothness,
Grabner and Stepanyuk obtained in
[
14
]
, among others, upper and lower bounds for the
optimal quadrature errors:

n^{-1/2}\ln^{-\beta}n\lesssim e_{n}({\rm H}^{d/2,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\lesssim n^{-1/2}\ln^{-\beta+1/2}n.

However, there is a logarithmic gap between the upper and lower
bounds. They conjectured in
[
14
]
that the correct order for

e_{n}({\rm H}^{d/2,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
is just the upper bound. Also
Krieg and Vybíral posed Open problem 2 in
[
27
]
, which stated that the existing lower bound can be improved.

There has been an increased interest in the comparison of standard
information given by function values and general linear
information for
L_{2}
approximation problem. We refer to

[
9
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
34
,
41
]

for recent upper bounds and to
[
19
,
20
]
for lower
bounds. Specifically, applying random information,
weighted least squares method, and infinite-dimensional variant
of subsampling strategy, the authors in
[
9
]
obtained that
there are two absolute constants
c
,
C>0
, such that for every
reproducing
kernel Hilbert space
H
and every
n\in{N}
,

(1.4)

g_{n}(H)\leq C\Big(\sum_{k\geq cn}a_{n}(H)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

By (
1.2
) and
(
1.4
) we have for
\beta>1/2
,

(1.5)

g_{n}({\rm H}^{d/2,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\lesssim n^{-1/2}\ln^{-\beta+1/2}n.

This give the upper bound
for
g_{n}({\rm H}^{d/2,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
. However, the algorithm used
in
[
9
]
is not constructive (see
[
9
, Remark 7]
).

For constructive algorithms, it was shown in
[
47
]
that the
filtered hyperinterpolation operators (see the definition in

[
47
]
) are asymptotically optimal linear algorithms for

g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
with
\alpha>d/2
. Lu and Wang showed
in
[
28
]
that the weighted least squares operators (see
Subsection 2.3) are asymptotically optimal linear algorithms for

g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha}({{S}}^{d}))
with
\alpha>d/2
.

In this paper, we investigate the approximation numbers, the
sampling numbers, and the optimal quadrature error for the Sobolev
spaces
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}),\,\alpha>0
with generalized
smoothness. We make special attention to the case

\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
.

First, we give strong equivalences of the
approximation numbers
a_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})),\ \alpha>0

which generalized the result of
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
. Such
result gives a clue to Open problem 3 posed in
[
27
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>0,\beta\in{R}
.
Then

(1.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{\alpha/d}(\ln n)^{\beta}a_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))=\Big(\frac{2}{d\,!}\Big)^{\alpha/d}d^{\beta}.

Remark 1
.

If
\beta=0
, then Theorem
1.1
reduces to
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
. One can rephrase (
1.6
) as strong equivalences

a_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\sim\Big(\frac{2}{d\,!}\Big)^{\alpha/d}d^{\beta}n^{-\alpha}(\ln n)^{-\beta},

as
n\to\infty
for arbitrary fixed
d
and
\alpha>0,\beta\in{R}
. The novelty of Theorem
1.1
is that it gives strong
equivalences of
a_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
and provides
asymptotically optimal constants, for arbitrary fixed
d
and

\alpha>0,\beta\in{R}
.

Next, we discuss lower bounds for
e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))

with
\alpha>d/2
or
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
. In the case of
\alpha>d/2
,
we can use the “fooling” function technique, i.e., for any
quadrature rule
Q_{{\bf c},X_{n}}
, we can construct a fooling
function
f
which vanishes in all nodes
X_{n}
, and satisfies

\|f\|_{H^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})}\lesssim 1,\ \ \ \ \int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf x})d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})\gtrsim n^{-\alpha/d}\ln^{-\beta}n.

See
[
8
,
14
,
44
]
for the details of this
technique. We use such technique in
[
8
]
to obtain the
following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha>d/2
. Then

(1.7)

e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\gtrsim n^{-\alpha/d}\ln^{-\beta}n.

However,
in the case of
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
, the “fooling” function
technique does not give optimal lower bounds (see
[
27
]
).
We exploit the Hilbert space structure
and a variant of the Schur product theory to give optimal lower
bounds for
e_{n}({\rm H}^{d/2,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
with
\beta>1/2
.
Such technique was used in
[
19
,
20
,
27
]
on the
d
dimensional torus
{T}^{d}
. It is very surprising that the above technique seems not to
give optimal lower bounds for
e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
with
\alpha>d/2,\ d\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
. Then

(1.8)

e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\gtrsim n^{-1/2}\ln^{-\beta+1/2}n.

By (
1.1
), (
1.7
), (
1.8
), (
1.3
), and
(
1.5
) we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let

\alpha>d/2
or
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
. Then

\displaystyle e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n^{-\alpha/d}\ln^{-\beta}n,&\alpha>d/2,\\
n^{-1/2}\ln^{-\beta+1/2}n,&\alpha=d/2,\beta>1/2.\end{array}

Remark 2
.

By Corollary
1.4
and (
1.2
) we
obtain for
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
,

(1.11)

\displaystyle n^{-1/2}\ln^{-\beta+1/2}n\asymp e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp a_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\,\ln^{1/2}n.

This
confirms the conjecture in
[
14
]
and solves Open
problem 2 posed in
[
27
]
. Of special interest is the
logarithmic gap between the sampling numbers and the approximation
numbers, which has been shown in
[
20
]
on the torus
{T}
, in
[
27
]
on the
d
-dimensional torus
{T}^{d}
, and is
now extended to the sphere
{{S}}^{d}
.

Finally, we give constructive algorithms to obtain upper bounds
for
g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
with
\alpha>d/2
or

\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
. The algorithms are the weighted least
squares algorithms investigated in
[
15
,
28
]
.

An
L_{2}
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund (MZ) family
\mathcal{X}
on a compact space

\mathcal{M}

is a double-indexed set of points
\{{\bf x}_{n,k},\ n=1,2\dots,\,k=1,2,\dots,l_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{M}
with
a weight
\tau
of positive numbers
\{\tau_{n,k}\}
such that the
sampled
\ell_{2}
norm of the
n
-th layer

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{l_{n}}\tau_{n,k}|Q({\bf x}_{n,k})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
is equivalent to the
L_{2}
norm for all
“polynomials”
Q
of degree at most
n
with uniform constants.
Given an
L_{2}
MZ family on
\mathcal{M}
, Gröchenig in
[
15
]

introduced weighted least squares operators and least squares
quadrature rules, and derived approximation theorems and least
squares quadrature errors for Sobolev spaces. However, due to the
generality of the compact space

\mathcal{M}
, the obtained results are not optimal.
Lu and Wang in
[
28
]
proved that the weighted least
squares operators
L_{N}^{{S}}
and the least squares quadrature
rules
I_{N}^{{S}}
on
{{S}}^{d}
are asymptotical optimal for

g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha}({{S}}^{d}))
and
e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha}({{S}}^{d}))
with

\alpha>d/2
.

The following theorem shows that the weighted least squares
operators
L_{N}^{{S}}
and the least squares quadrature rules

I_{N}^{{S}}
on
{{S}}^{d}
are asymptotical optimal for
g_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
and
e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
with

\alpha>d/2
or
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an

L_{2}
MZ family on
{{S}}^{d}
with associated weight
\tau
, global
condition number
\kappa
, and
l_{N}\asymp N^{d}\asymp n
, and let

L_{N}^{{S}}
be the weighted least squares operator and

I_{N}^{{S}}
be least squares quadrature rule induced by

\mathcal{X}
. We have for all
f\in{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})

with
\alpha>d/2
or
\alpha=d/2,\beta>1/2
,

(1.12)

\left|\int_{{S}^{d}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\sigma_{d}({\bf x})-I_{N}^{{S}}(f)\right|\leq\|f-L_{N}^{{S}}(f)\|_{L_{2}}\leq c(1+\kappa^{1/2})C_{\alpha,\beta}(N)\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})},

where
c>0
is independent of

n
,

\kappa
,
f
, or
\mathcal{X}
, and

C_{\alpha,\beta}(N):=\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N^{-\alpha}\ln^{-\beta}N,&\alpha>d/2,\\
N^{-\alpha}\ln^{-\beta+1/2}N,&\alpha=d/2,\beta>1/2.\end{array}

Remark 3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an

L_{2}
MZ family on
{{S}}^{d}
with
l_{N}\leq n
and
l_{N}\asymp N^{d}\asymp n
. For such
L_{2}
MZ family it follows from (
1.4
) and
(
1.12
) that

g_{n}({{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})})\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})}\leq 1}\|f-L_{N}^{{S}}(f)\|_{2}\asymp g_{n}({{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})})\asymp C_{\alpha,\beta}(N),

which implies that
the weighted least squares operators
L_{N}^{{S}}
are order
optimal linear algorithms in the sense of the sampling numbers.
Also, we have

e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f({\bf x})\,d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})-I_{N}^{{S}}(f)\right|\asymp e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))\asymp C_{\alpha,\beta}(N),

which means that the least squares quadrature rules
I_{N}^{{S}}
are the
order optimal quadrature formulas.

The paper is structured as follows. In Subsection 2.1 we
introduce Sobolev spaces
H^{\alpha,\beta}
with logarithmic
perturbation. Then we delve into lower bounds for optimal
quadrature and positive definiteness and present some auxiliary
lemmas in Subsection 2.2. Definition and properties of the
weighted least squares operators are given in Subsection 2.3.
After that, in Section 3 we give the proofs of Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
. Section 4 is devoted to elaborating
the lower bounds for
e_{n}({\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}))
with

\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
. We give the proof of Theorems
1.5

in Section 5.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sobolev spaces
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
with logarithmic
perturbation

We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{d}
the space of
all spherical harmonics of degree
\ell
on
{{S}}^{d}
. Denote by

\Pi^{d+1}_{m}
the set of spherical polynomials on
\mathbb{S}^{d}

of degree
\leq m
, which is just the set of polynomials of total
degree
\leq m
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
restricted to
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
It is well known (see
[
7
, Corollaries 1.1.4 and 1.1.5]
)
that the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{d}
is

(2.1)

N(d,\ell):={\rm dim}\,\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{d}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&{\rm if}\ \ \ell=0,\\
\frac{(2\ell+d-1)\,(\ell+d-2)!}{(d-1)!\ \ell!},&{\rm if}\ \ \ell=1,2,\dots,\end{array}\right.

and

(2.2)

C(d,m):={\rm dim}\,\Pi_{m}^{d+1}=\frac{(2m+d)(m+d-1)!}{m!\,d!},\ m\in{N}.

It is evident that

N(d,\ell)\asymp\ell^{d-1}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ C(d,m)\asymp m^{d}.

Let

\{Y_{\ell,k}\ |\ k=1,\dots,N(d,\ell)\}

be a collection of
L_{2}
-orthonormal real spherical harmonics of
degree
\ell
(see, e.g.,
[
33
]
). Each spherical harmonic

Y_{\ell,k}
of exact degree
\ell
is an eigenfunction of the
negative Laplace-Beltrami opertor
-\Delta_{0}^{*}
with
eigenvalue

\mathaccent 866{\lambda_{\ell}}=\ell(\ell+d-1).

By the addition formula for spherical harmonic of exact degree

\ell
, we have for
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{{S}}^{d},

(2.3)

\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}Y_{\ell,k}({\bf x})Y_{\ell,k}({\bf y})=\frac{n+\frac{d-1}{2}}{\frac{d-1}{2}}C^{\frac{d-1}{2}}_{n}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})=N(d,\ell)G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}),

where
C_{n}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(t),\ n\geq 0,\,t\in[-1,1]
are the
usual Gegenbauer polynomials defined as in
[
11
, pp. 18-20 and
Proposition 1.4.11]
, and
G_{n},\,n=0,1,\dots,
are the
Gegenbauer polynomials of the index
\frac{d-1}{2}
normalized by

(2.4)

G_{n}(1)=1,\ \ \ C_{n}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(1)\,G_{n}(t)=C_{n}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(t).

We fix
G_{n}
in the sequel.

Let

\{Y_{\ell,k}\ |\ k=1,\dots,N(d,\ell)\}

be a fixed orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{d}
. Then

\{Y_{\ell,k}\ |\ \ell=0,1,2,\dots,\ k=1,\dots,N(d,\ell)\}

is
an orthonormal basis for the Hilbert space
L_{2}({{S}}^{d})
. Thus any

f\in L_{2}({{S}}^{d})
can be expressed by its Fourier (or Laplace)
series

f=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}H^{d}_{\ell}(f)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle Y_{\ell,k}},

where

H_{\ell}^{d}(f)({\bf x})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle Y_{\ell,k}({\bf x})=N(d,\ell)\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf y})G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})d\sigma_{d}({\bf y})

denote the orthogonal projections of
f
onto

\mathcal{H}_{\ell}^{d}
, and

\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf x})Y_{\ell,k}({\bf x})\,d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})

are the Fourier coefficients of

f
. We also have the following Parseval equality,

\|f\|_{L_{2}}=\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}|\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\|H_{\ell}^{d}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Let
S_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\Pi_{n}^{d+1}
.
Then for
f\in L_{1}({{S}}^{d})
,

(2.5)

S_{n}(f)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{j}^{d}(f)=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf y})E_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})d\sigma_{d}({\bf y}),

where

E_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}Y_{\ell,k}({\bf x})Y_{\ell,k}({\bf y})

is the reproducing kernel of

\Pi_{n}^{d+1}
with respect to the inner product of
L_{2}
.

For
f\in L_{p},\ 1\leq p\leq\infty,
we define

E_{n}(f)_{L_{p}}=\inf_{g\in\Pi_{n}^{d+1}}\|f-g\|_{L_{p}}.

It is well known that

(2.6)

E_{n}(f)_{L_{2}}=\|f-S_{n}(f)\|_{L_{2}}.

Let

\Lambda=\{\lambda_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence, and let

T^{\Lambda}
be a multiplier operator on
L_{2}({{S}}^{d})
defined by

T^{\Lambda}(f)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1}_{\ell}H^{d}_{\ell}(f)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-1}_{\ell}{\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle Y_{\ell,k}}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
be
a non-increasing positive sequence with

\lim\limits_{\ell\to\infty}\lambda_{\ell}=0
. We define the multiplier
space
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
by

\displaystyle{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})

\displaystyle:=\Big\{T^{\Lambda}f\,\big|\,f\in L_{2}({{S}}^{d})\ ,\ \|T^{\Lambda}f\,\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}=\|f\|_{L_{2}({{S}}^{d})}<\infty\Big\}

\displaystyle:=\Big\{f\in L_{2}({{S}}^{d})\,\big|\,\|f\,\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}:=\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}|\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}<\infty\Big\}.

Clearly, the multiplier space
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
is a Hilbert space
with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle\,\langle g,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{-2}\langle H_{\ell}^{d}(f),H_{\ell}^{d}(g)\rangle,

and

(2.7)

E_{n}(f)_{L_{2}}=\|f-S_{n}(f)\|_{L_{2}}\leq\lambda_{n}\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}.

Moreover, we remark that the embedding
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})\hookrightarrow C({{S}}^{d})\ ({\rm or}\ L_{\infty}({{S}}^{d}))

is continuous if

(2.8)

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2}_{\ell}N(d,\ell)<\infty,

which ensures
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
with the reproducing kernel

(2.9)

\displaystyle K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},{\bf y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}Y_{\ell,k}({\bf x})Y_{\ell,k}({\bf y})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}N(d,\ell)G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}),

where
G_{\ell}(t),\ t\in[-1,1]
is given in (
2.4
). Indeed, if (
2.8
) holds, then we have
for any

{\bf x}\in{{S}}^{d}
,

\|K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},\cdot)\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\langle K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},\cdot),K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},\cdot)\rangle_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}=K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},{\bf x})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}N(d,\ell)<\infty,

and for
f\in{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
,

\langle f,K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},\cdot)\rangle_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N(d,\ell)}\langle f,Y_{\ell,k}\rangle\lambda_{\ell}^{-2}Y_{\ell,k}({\bf x})=f({\bf x}),

which means that
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert
space with the kernel
K^{\Lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
. It follows from the
Cauchy inequality that for
f\in{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d}),\ {\bf x},{\bf y}\in{{S}}^{d},

\displaystyle|f({\bf x})-f({\bf y})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\langle f,K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},\cdot)-K^{\Lambda}({\bf y},\cdot)\rangle_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}^{2}\ \|K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},\cdot)-K^{\Lambda}({\bf y},\cdot)\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})}^{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{\ell}^{2}N(d,\ell)(1-G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})).

This
implies that
f
is continuous on
{{S}}^{d}
. Hence, the space
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
is continuously embedded in
C({{S}}^{d})
.

For
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
, we set

\tilde{\Lambda}=\{\tilde{\lambda}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty},\ \ \tilde{\lambda}_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}^{2}N(d,\ell),\ \ell=0,1,\dots.

If
(
2.8
) holds, then
\tilde{\Lambda}\in\ell_{1}({N}_{0})
, i.e.,

\|\tilde{\Lambda}\|_{\ell_{1}}\equiv\|\tilde{\Lambda}\|_{\ell_{1}({N}_{0})}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}|\tilde{\lambda}_{\ell}|<\infty,

and the reproducing kernel of
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})
can be written by

K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\lambda}_{\ell}G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}),\ \ {\bf x},{\bf y}\in{{S}}^{d}.

We write

{\rm H}_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}:={\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d}),\ \ \ K_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}({\bf x},{\bf y}):=K^{\Lambda}({\bf x},{\bf y}).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha>0,\,\beta\in{R}
. The Sobolev space
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
with logarithmic
perturbation is defined to be the multiplier space

{\rm H}^{\Lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}}({{S}}^{d})
, where

\Lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}=\{\lambda_{\ell}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\}_{\ell=0}^{\infty}
,
and

\lambda_{\ell}^{(\alpha,\beta)}=(1+\ell(\ell+d-1))^{-\alpha/2}(\ln(3+\ell(\ell+d-1)))^{-\beta}.

If
\beta=0
, then the space
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
reduces
to the Sobolev space
{\rm H}^{\alpha,+}
as defined in

[
5
, Definition 2.2]
. Hence
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
is
the Sobolev space with logarithmic perturbation. Such spaces were
investigated intensively (see
[
10
,
14
,
32
,
47
]
). In the
case
\alpha>d/2
or
\alpha=d/2,\,\beta>1/2
, the space
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
is continuously embedded into the space of
continuous functions
C({{S}}^{d})
, and is a reproducing kernel
Hilbert space.

For
f\in{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d}),\,\alpha>0
, we define

\sigma_{1}(f)=S_{2}(f),\ \ \sigma_{j}(f)=S_{2^{j}}(f)-S_{2^{j-1}}(f),\ \ {\rm for}\ j\geq 2.

Then
\sigma_{j}(f)\in\Pi_{2^{j}}^{d+1}
and

f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}(f)

converges in the
L_{2}
norm.
Note that
\sigma_{j}(f)
satisfies the Bernsetin inequality,
i.e., for any
r>0
,

\|(-\triangle_{0})^{r/2}(\sigma_{j}(f))\|_{L_{2}}\lesssim 2^{jr}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{2}}.

Also, by (
2.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{2}}\leq\|f-S_{2^{j}}(f)\|_{L_{2}}+\|f-S_{2^{j-1}}(f)\|_{L_{2}}\leq 2E_{2^{j-1}}(f)_{L_{2}}.

Now set
\Omega(t)=t^{\alpha}(1+(\ln\frac{1}{t})_{+})^{\beta}
for
t>0
.
Consider
the Besov space
B_{2,2}^{\Omega}({{S}}^{d})
of generalized smoothness (see
[
46
,
47
]
). It
follows from
[
46
, Remark 2]
and the above two inequalities
that

\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{\Omega}({{S}}^{d})}\asymp\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}}{\Omega(2^{-j})^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}.

On the other hand, we have

\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_{\ell}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{-2}\|H_{\ell}^{d}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=:\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}I_{j}.

Here,

I_{1}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2}(\lambda_{\ell}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{-2}\|H_{\ell}^{d}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\asymp\sum_{\ell=0}^{2}\|H_{\ell}^{d}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\|\sigma_{1}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\asymp\frac{\|\sigma_{1}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}}{\Omega(2^{-1})^{2}},

and for
j\geq 2
,

I_{j}:=\sum_{\ell=2^{j-1}+1}^{2^{j}}(\lambda_{\ell}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{-2}\|H_{\ell}^{d}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\asymp 2^{2j\alpha}j^{2\beta}\sum_{\ell=2^{j-1}+1}^{2^{j}}\|H_{\ell}^{d}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\asymp\frac{\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}}{\Omega(2^{-j})^{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})}

\displaystyle\asymp\|f\|_{B_{2,2}^{\Omega}({{S}}^{d})}\asymp\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}}{\Omega(2^{-j})^{2}}\Big)^{1/2}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\asymp\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{2j\alpha}j^{2\beta}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

This means that the space
{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})
is
just the Besov space
B_{2,2}^{\Omega}({{S}}^{d})
(see
[
46
,
47
]
) of
generalized smoothness with above equivalent norms. Here,

\Omega(t)=t^{\alpha}(1+(\ln\frac{1}{t})_{+})^{\beta}
for
t>0
. Hence, from

[
46
,
47
]
) we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\rm H}^{\alpha,\beta}({{S}}^{d})}\asymp\|f\|_{L_{2}}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{2j\alpha}j^{2\beta}E_{2^{j}}(f)_{L_{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

2.2.
Lower bounds for optimal quadrature and positive definiteness

Let
H\equiv H(K)
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
consisting of real-valued functions on
{{S}}^{d}
with reproducing
kernel
K\in C({{S}}^{d}\times{{S}}^{d})
and inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
. That is,
H
is a Hilbert space of
real-valued functions on
{S}^{d}
such that point evaluation

\delta_{\bf x}:\,H(K)\to{R},\ \ \delta_{\bf x}(f):=f({\bf x})

is a continuous functional on
H
for all
{\bf x}\in{S}^{d}
,
and the reproducing kernel
K
satisfies

(i) for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{{S}}^{d}
,
K({\bf x},{\bf y})=K({\bf y},{\bf x})
;

(ii) for all
{\bf x}\in{{S}}^{d}
,
K({\bf x},\cdot)\in H
;

(iii) for all
{\bf x}\in{{S}}^{d}
and
f\in H
,
f({\bf x})=\langle f,K({\bf x},\cdot)\rangle_{H}
.

We refer to
[
1
,
2
]
for basics on RKHSs.

For
h\in H
, we define
S_{h}
the continuous linear functional on

H
by
S_{h}(f)=\langle f,h\rangle_{H}
for
f\in H
. Since
\rm INT
is continuous on
H
, by the Riesz representation theorem,
there exists an
h_{0}\in H
for which

{\rm INT}(f)=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})\,d\sigma_{d}({\bf{x}})=\langle f,h_{0}\rangle_{H}=S_{h_{0}}(f).

We have for

{\bf x}\in{{S}}^{d}
,

h_{0}({\bf x})=\big\langle h_{0},K({\bf x},\cdot)\big\rangle_{H}={\rm INT}(K({\bf x},\cdot))=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}K({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})d\sigma_{d}({\bf y}).

We consider the space
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})={\rm H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
, where
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a positive
sequence,
\tilde{\Lambda}=\{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, and
\tilde{\lambda}_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}N(d,k),\ k=0,1,\dots
. As seen in Subsection 2.1, the space
{\rm H}^{\Lambda}({{S}}^{d})

is RKHS on the sphere if and only if

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2}N(d,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\lambda}_{k}<+\infty.

In this case, the reproduce kernel is

K_{\tilde{\Lambda}}({\bf x},{\bf y}):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathaccent 869{\lambda}_{\ell}G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}),\ {\bf x},{\bf y}\in{S}^{d},

and the representer of the
integration problem INT is

h_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}({\bf x})=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}K_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}({\bf{x}},{\bf{y}})d\sigma_{d}({\bf y})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathaccent 869{\lambda}_{\ell}\int_{{S}^{d}}G_{\ell}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})d\sigma_{d}({\bf y})=\mathaccent 869{\lambda}_{0}=\lambda_{0}^{2},

for all
{\bf{x}}\in{{S}}^{d}
. This means that

{\rm INT}(f)=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf{x}})\,d\sigma_{d}({\bf{x}})=\langle f,h_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}\rangle_{{\rm H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}}=S_{h_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}}(f).

Furthermore, we have

\|h_{\mathaccent 869{\Lambda}}\|_{{\rm H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}}=(\tilde{\lambda}_{0})^{1/2}=\lambda_{0}.

We say that a symmetric matrix
M\in{{R}}^{n\times n}

is positive semi-definite if
\mathbf{c}^{T}M\mathbf{c}\geq 0

for all
\mathbf{c}\in{{R}}^{n}
. If
M,N\in{{R}}^{n\times n}
, we denote by
M\circ N
their Hadamard (Schur)
product, i.e., a matrix with entries
(M\circ N)_{j,k}=M_{j,k}\cdot N_{j,k}
for all
j,k=1,...,n
(see

[
16
]
). Furthermore, the partial ordering
M\succeq N
of
symmetric matrices means that
M-N
is positive semi-definite.
We denote by
{\rm diag}M=(M_{1,1},...,M_{n,n})^{T}
the vector
of diagonal entries of
M
whenever
M\in{{R}}^{n\times n}
.

In order to prove lower bounds for
e_{n}({\rm H}_{\tilde{\Lambda}})
we
need the following lemmas. The first lemma is Schoenberg’s
remarkable result about positive definite functions on the sphere.

Lemma 2.3
.

(See
[
37
,
7
]
.) Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\ell_{1}({N}_{0})
, and
let

f_{\Gamma}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}G_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ K_{\Gamma}({\bf x},{\bf y})=f_{\Gamma}({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y}),

where
G_{j}
are the
normalized Gegenbauer polynomials given in (
2.4
),
t\in[-1,1]
,
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{{S}}^{d}
. Then for all
n\in{N}
and
{\bf x}_{1},{{\bf x}_{2}},\cdot\cdot\cdot,{{\bf x}_{n}}\in{{S}}^{d}
, the matrix

\big(K_{\Gamma}({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})\big)_{1\leq j,k\leq n}

is positive semi-definite if and only if
\Gamma
is
nonnegative.

The second lemma gives a characterization of lower bounds for

e_{n}(H)
via the positive definiteness of certain matrices
involving the reproducing kernel
K
and the representer
h_{0}
of
the integration problem INT.

Lemma 2.4
.

(See
[
19
, Proposition 1]
and
[
20
,
27
]
.)
Let
H
be a RKHS on
{{S}}^{d}
with reproducing kernel

K\in C({{S}}^{d}\times{{S}}^{d})
, and let
{\rm INT}=\langle\cdot,h_{0}\rangle_{H}
for some
h_{0}\in H
,
\alpha>0
. For all
n\in{N}
and
{\bf x}_{1},{{\bf x}_{2}},\cdot\cdot\cdot,{{\bf x}_{n}}\in{{S}}^{d}
, the matrix

\big(K({\bf x}_{j},{\bf x}_{k})-\alpha h_{0}({\bf x}_{j})h_{0}({\bf x}_{k})\big)_{1\leq j,k\leq n}

is positive semi-definite, if and only if

e_{n}^{2}(H)\geq\|h_{0}\|_{H}^{2}-\alpha^{-1}.

The third lemma was obtained by J. Vybíral in
[
43
]
and
shows
that the Schur product of a positive semi-definite matrix with itself
is not only positive semi-definite but also bounded from below by some rank-1 matrix.

Lemma 2.5
.

(See
[
43
, Theorem 1]
.)
Let
M\in{{R}}^{n\times n}
be a positive semi-definite
matrix. Then

M\circ M\succeq\frac{1}{n}({\rm diag}\,M)({\rm diag}\,M)^{T}.

2.3.
Weighted least squares operators and least squares quadrature rules

First we give the definition of
L_{2}
MZ families.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathcal{X}_{n}\}=\{{\bf x}_{n,k},\ n=1,2,\dots,k=1,\dots,l_{n}\}
be a doubly-indexed set of points in
{{S}}^{d}
and
\tau=\{\tau_{n,k}:n=1,2,\dots,\,k=1,\dots,l_{n}\}
be a family of
positive numbers. Then
\mathcal{X}
is called an
L_{2}

Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund (MZ) family with associated weight
\tau
,
if there exist constants
A,B>0
independent of
n
such that

(2.12)

A\|p\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l_{n}}|p({\bf x}_{n,k})|^{2}\tau_{n,k}\leq B\|p\|_{2}^{2}\ \qquad{\rm for\ all}\ p\in\Pi_{n}^{d+1}.

The ratio
\kappa=B/A
is the global condition number of the
L_{2}
MZ family

\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{X}_{n}=\{{\bf x}_{n,k}:k=1,\dots,l_{n}\}
is the
n
th layer of
\mathcal{X}
.

The existence of
L_{2}
MZ families on
{{S}}^{d}
(including the case

\kappa=1
) has been established in
[
31
,
4
,
7
,
29
,
30
]
.
Additionally, necessary or sufficient density conditions for
L_{2}

MZ families on
{{S}}^{d}
have been discussed in
[
30
,
33
]
.

Let

\mathcal{X}
be an
L_{2}
MZ family with associated weight
\tau
.
Given the samples
\{f({\bf x}_{N,k})\}
of a continuous function

f
on
{{S}}^{d}
on the
N
th layer
\mathcal{X}_{N}
, Gröchenig in

[
15
]
introduced the weighted least squares polynomials

L_{N}^{{S}}(f)
to approximate
f
using only these samples,
i.e., the solution of weighted least squares problems:

(2.13)

L_{N}^{{S}}(f)=\arg\min_{p\in\Pi_{N}^{d+1}}\ \sum_{k=1}^{l_{N}}|f({\bf x}_{N,k})-p({\bf x}_{N,k})|^{2}\tau_{N,k}.

This procedure yields a sequence
\{L_{N}^{{S}}(f)\}
of the
best weighted
\ell_{2}
-approximation of the data
\{f({\bf x}_{N,k})\}
by a
spherical polynomial in
\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
for every
f\in C({{S}}^{d})
. We call
L_{N}^{{S}}
the weighted least squares
operator.

It follows from (
2.12
) that for
p\in\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
,
p=0

whenever
p({\bf x}_{N,k})=0
. This means that usually
\mathcal{X}_{N}

contains more than
d_{N}=\dim\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
points, so that it is
not an interpolating set for
\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
. Therefore,

L_{N}^{{S}}(f)
is usually a quasi-interpolant.

We use the weighted discretized inner product

(2.14)

\langle f,g\rangle_{(N)}:=\sum_{k=1}^{l_{N}}f({\bf x}_{N,k})g({\bf x}_{N,k})\tau_{N,k}

and the discretized norm

\|f\|_{(N)}^{2}=\langle f,f\rangle_{(N)}.

We consider the corresponding orthogonal polynomial projection

L_{N}^{{S}}
onto
\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
with respect to the weighted
discretized inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(N)}
,
namely the weighted least squares polynomial
L_{N}^{{S}}(f)

defined by

L_{N}^{{S}}(f)=\arg\min_{p\in\Pi_{N}^{d+1}}\|f-p\|_{(N)}^{2}=\arg\min_{p\in\Pi_{N}^{d+1}}\ \sum_{k=1}^{l_{N}}|f({\bf x}_{N,k})-p({\bf x}_{N,k})|^{2}\tau_{N,k}.

We shall
give a formal expression for
L_{N}^{{S}}(f)
(see

[
28
]
). Let
\varphi_{i},\ i=1,\dots,d_{N}
be an orthonormal
basis of
\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
with respect to the weighted discrete
scalar product (
2.14
), i.e.,

\langle\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j}\rangle_{(N)}=\delta_{i,j}=\Big\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&{\rm if}\ \ i=j,\\
0,&{\rm if}\ \ i\neq j,\end{array}\ \ \ 1\leq i,j\leq d_{N}.

We set

D_{N}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{N}}\varphi_{k}({\bf x})\varphi_{k}({\bf y}).

Clearly,
D_{N}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is the reproducing kernel of
\Pi_{N}^{d+1}

with respect to the weighted discrete scalar product
(
2.14
), and the weighted least squares polynomial

L_{N}^{{S}}(f)
is just the orthogonal projection of
f
onto

\Pi_{N}^{d+1}
with respect to the weighted discrete scalar
product (
2.14
), i. e.,

(2.15)

L_{N}^{{S}}(f)({\bf x})=\langle f,D_{N}(\cdot,{\bf x})\rangle_{(N)}=\sum_{k=1}^{l_{N}}f({\bf x}_{N,k})D_{N}({\bf x}_{N,k},{\bf x})\tau_{N,k}.

Obviously,
L_{N}^{{S}}
is a linear operator satisfying

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|f-L_{N}^{{S}}(f)\|_{(N)}\leq\|f-p\|_{(N)},\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ p\in\Pi_{N}^{d+1}.

Also, Gröchenig in
[
15
]
used the frame theory
to construct
least squares quadrature rules

I_{N}^{{S}}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{l_{N}}w_{N,k}f({\bf x}_{N,k})

induced by an
L_{2}
MZ
family. Lu and Wang in
[
28
]
proved that

w_{N,k}=\tau_{N,k}\int_{{{S}}^{d}}D_{N}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{n,k})d\sigma_{d}({\bf x}),\ \ \ I_{N}^{{S}}(f)=\int_{{{S}}^{d}}L_{N}^{{S}}(f)({\bf x}){d}\sigma_{d}({\bf x}),

and

(2.17)

\left|\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf x}){d}\sigma_{d}({\bf x})-I_{N}^{{S}}(f)\right|\leq\left\|f-L_{N}^{{S}}(f)\right\|_{L_{2}}.

Finally, we give the relation between weighted least squares
polynomials and hyperinterpolation (see
[
28
]
).
Hyperinterpolation
was originally introduced by I. H. Sloan (see
[
38
]
).
It uses the Fourier orthogonal projection of a function which can
be expressed in the form of integrals, but approximates the
integrals used in the expansion by means of a positive quadrature
formula. In recent years, hyperinterpolation and its
generalization on
{{S}}^{d}
has attracted much interest, and a great
number of interesting results
have been obtained (see
[
6
,
13
,
18
,
35
,
36
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
44
,
45
]
).

Assume that

Q_{N}(f)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{l_{N}}\tau_{N,k}f({\bf x}_{N,k}),\ N=1,2,\dots
is a sequence of positive quadrature formulas on

{{S}}^{d}
which are exact for
\Pi_{2N}^{d+1}
, i.e.,
\tau_{N,k}>0
,
and for all
f\in\Pi_{2N}^{d+1}
,

\int_{{{S}}^{d}}f({\bf x})d\sigma_{d}({\bf x})=Q_{N}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{l_{N}}\tau_{N,k}f({\bf x}_{N,k}).

Then
the family
\mathcal{X}=\{{\bf x}_{N,k}\}
with weight
\tau
is an
L_{2}
MZ family with the
constants
A=B=1
and the global condition number
\kappa

is equal to
1
. On the other hand, if the global condition number
\kappa
of an
L_{2}
MZ
family
\mathcal{X}=\{{\bf x}_{N,k}\}
with weight
\tau
is equal to

1
, then
\mathcal{X}
determines a sequence of positive
quadrature formulas
Q_{N}(f)=\frac{1}{A}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{l_{N}}\tau_{N,k}f({\bf x}_{N,k})
on
{{S}}^{d}
which are
exact for
\Pi_{2N}^{d+1}
.

Let
Q_{N}(f)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{l_{N}}\tau_{N,k}f({\bf x}_{N,k}),\ N=1,2,\dots
be a sequence of positive quadrature formulas on

Deformations and Extensions of BiHom-alternative algebras
Taoufik Chtioui , Sami Mabrouk , Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences Gafsa, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques, 18, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to deal with BiHom-alternative algebras which are a generalization of alternative and Hom-alternative algebras, their structure is defined with two commuting multiplicative linear maps. We study cohomology and one-parameter formal deformation theory of left BiHom-alternative algebras. Moreover, we study central and T θ T_{\theta} -extensions of BiHom-alternative algebras and their relationship with cohomology. Finally, we investigate generalized derivations and give some relevant results.

: BiHom-alternative algebra, representation, cohomology, formal deformation,
extension.

Introduction

Algebras of Hom-type have been recently investigated by many authors. They appeared in the physics literature, in the ninetieth, in quantum deformations of some algebras of vector fields, such as the Witt and Virasoro algebras. Hartwig, Larsson and Silvestrov introduced and studied, in

The Hom-analogue of associative, alternative, Jordan and Novikov algebras were introduced in

A categorical approach to Hom-algebras was first investigated in

[

[

In
[
22

, BiHom-pre-Lie algebras and BiHom-Leibniz algebras were defined and studied via Rota-Baxter operators. The BiHom-versions of alternative, pre-alternative, quadri-alternative and Malcev algebras were introduced in
[

and
[

. The authors gave several constructions connecting such structures.

Deformation theory arose essentially from geometry and physics. It is a tool to study a mathematical object by deforming it into a family of the same kind of objects depending on a certain parameter. In the sixties, M. Gerstenhaber introduced algebraic formal deformations for associative algebras in a series of papers (see
[

,
14
,

,
16

In this paper, we introduce representations, cohomology and formal deformation theory of left BiHom-alternative algebras. This concept generalize the classical and Hom-type cases studied by M. Elhamdadi and A. Makhlouf in

In section 2, we define first, second and third coboundary operators and corresponding cohomology groups of left BiHom-alternative algebras.
Section 3 is dedicated to develop a one-parameter formal deformation theory for left BiHom-alternative algebras.
Section 4 is devoted to the study of central and

In the lat section, we provide some results involving generalized derivations on BiHom-alternative algebras.

Definition 1.1
.

A left BiHom-alternative algebra
(
resp. right BiHom-alternative algebra) is a quadruple
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
consisting of a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
A
, a bilinear map
\mu:A\times A\longrightarrow A
and two homomorphisms
\alpha,\beta:A\longrightarrow A
such that
\alpha\beta=\beta\alpha
,
\alpha\mu=\mu\alpha^{\otimes^{2}}
and
\beta\mu=\mu\beta^{\otimes^{2}}
that satisfy the left BiHom-alternative identity, i.e. for all
x,y,z\in A
, one has

as_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta(x),\alpha(y),z)+as_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta(y),\alpha(x),z)=0,

(1.1)

respectively, the right BiHom-alternative identity, i.e. for all
x,y,z\in A
, one has

as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\beta(y),\alpha(z))+as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\beta(z),\alpha(y))=0.

(1.2)

A BiHom-alternative algebra is one which is both a left and a right BiHom-alternative algebra.

Observe that when
\alpha=\beta=Id
the left BiHom-alternative identity
(\ref{sa1})
reduces to the left alternative identity.

Lemma 1.1
.

A quadruple
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is a left BiHom-alternative algebra
(
resp. right BiHom-alternative algebra) if and only if

as_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta(x),\alpha(x),y)=0

respectively

as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\beta(y),\alpha(y))=0

for any
x,y\in A
. (since the characteristic of the field
\mathbb{K}
is not
2
).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
and
(A^{{}^{\prime}},\mu^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha^{{}^{\prime}},\beta^{{}^{\prime}})
be two BiHom-alternative algebras. A homomorphism

is said to be a morphism of BiHom-alternative algebra if the following hold

f\circ\mu=\mu^{{}^{\prime}}\circ(f\otimes f),~~f\circ\alpha=\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}\circ f~~\textrm{and}~~f\circ\beta=\beta^{{}^{\prime}}\circ f.

\displaystyle\psi\ell(x)=\ell(\beta(x))\psi,\ \psi r(x)=r(\beta(x))\psi

(1.4)

\displaystyle\ell(\beta(x)\alpha(x))\psi(v)=\ell(\alpha\beta(x))\ell(\alpha(x))v,

(1.5)

3.1
Definition

Suppose that

is a left BiHom-alternative algebra. Let

be the ring of formal power

is the set of formal power series over

-bilinear map

by

f\bigg(\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}x_{i}t^{i},\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}y_{j}t^{j}\bigg)=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}f(x_{i},y_{j})t^{i+j}.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose that
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is a left BiHom-alternative algebra. A one-parameter
formal deformation of
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is a formal power series

of the form

d_{t}(x,y)=\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}d_{i}(x,y)t^{i}=d_{0}(x,y)+d_{1}(x,y)t+d_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\dots,

where each

is a
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear map
d_{i}:A\times A\rightarrow A
(extended to be
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-bilinear)
and

, such that the following identities must be
satisfied:

\displaystyle d_{t}(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\alpha\circ d_{t}(x,y),

(3.21)

\displaystyle d_{t}(\beta(x),\beta(y))=\beta\circ d_{t}(x,y),

(3.22)

\displaystyle d_{t}(d_{t}(\beta(x),\alpha(y)),\beta(z))-d_{t}(\alpha\beta(x),d_{t}(\alpha(y),z))

\displaystyle+d_{t}(d_{t}(\beta(y),\alpha(x)),\beta(z))-d_{t}(\alpha\beta(y),d_{t}(\alpha(x),z))=0.

(3.23)

Conditions (
3.21

3.23

Note that

is a module over

and

3.3
Equivalent and trivial deformations

In this paragraph, we characterize the equivalent and trivial deformations of left BiHom-alternative algebras.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a left BiHom-alternative algebra. Suppose that
d_{t}(x,y,)=\sum_{i\geq 0}d_{i}(x,y)t^{i}
and
d_{t}^{\prime}(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq 0}d_{i}^{\prime}(x,y)t^{i}
are two one-parameter formal deformations of
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
. They are called equivalent, denoted by
d_{t}\sim d_{t}^{\prime}
, if there is a formal isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-modules

\phi_{t}(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}(x)t^{i}:(A[[t]],d_{t},\alpha,\beta)\longrightarrow(A[[t]],d_{t}^{\prime},\alpha,\beta),

where each
\phi_{i}:A\rightarrow A
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map (extended to be
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear) and
\phi_{0}=Id_{A}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ\phi_{t},\ \phi_{t}\circ\beta=\beta\circ\phi_{t}

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\circ d_{t}(x,y)=d_{t}^{\prime}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)).

When
d_{1}=d_{2}=\cdots=0
,
d_{t}=d_{0}
is said to be the null deformation. A 1-parameter formal deformation
d_{t}
is called trivial if
d_{t}\sim d_{0}
. A left BiHom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is called analytically rigid, if every
1
-parameter formal deformation
d_{t}
is trivial.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
d_{t}(x,y)=\sum_{i>0}d_{i}(x,y)t^{i}

and
d^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq 0}d^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}
be equivalent
1
-parameter formal deformations of a left BiHom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
. Then
d_{1}
and
d^{\prime}_{1}
belong to the
same cohomology class in
H_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(A,A)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi_{t}(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}(x)t^{i}
is the formal
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-module isomorphism
such that
\phi_{t}\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ\phi_{t},\phi_{t}\circ\beta=\beta\circ\phi_{t}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}\Big(\sum_{j>0}d_{j}(x,y)t^{j}\Big)t^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}d^{\prime}_{i}\Big(\sum_{k\geq 0}\phi_{k}(x)t^{k},\sum_{k\geq 0}\phi_{l}(y)t^{l}\Big)t^{i}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\phi_{i}\big(d_{j}(x,y)\big)t^{i+j}=\sum_{i+k+l=n}d^{\prime}_{i}\big(\phi_{k}(x),\phi_{l}(y)\big)t^{i+k+l}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=1}\phi_{i}\big(d_{j}(x,y)\big))t^{i+j}=\sum_{i+k+l=1}d^{\prime}_{i}\big(\phi_{k}(x),\phi_{l}(y)\big)t^{i+k+l}.

Since
\phi_{0}=id
, we obtain

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,y)+\phi_{1}(\mu(x,y))=\mu(\phi_{1}(x),y)+\mu(x,\phi_{1}(y))+d^{\prime}_{1}(x,y).

Then
d_{1}-d^{\prime}_{1}\in B_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(A,A)
. Therefore,
d_{1}
and
d^{\prime}_{1}
are cohomologous.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a left BiHom-alternative algebra such that
H_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(A,A)=0
. Then
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is analytically rigid.

Proof.

Let
d_{t}
be a one-parameter formal deformation of
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
. Suppose that
d_{t}=d_{0}+\sum_{i\geq n}d_{i}t^{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\delta^{2}d_{n}=d_{1}\diamond d_{n-1}+d_{2}\diamond d_{n-2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+d_{n-1}\diamond d_{0}=0,

that is
d_{n}\in Z_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(A,A)=B_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(A,A)
. It follows that there exists
f_{n}\in C_{\alpha,\beta}^{1}(A,A)
such that
d_{n}=\delta^{1}f_{n}
.

Let
\phi_{t}=id_{A}-t^{n}f_{n}:(A[[t]],d^{\prime}_{t},\alpha,\beta)\rightarrow(A[[t]],d_{t},\alpha,\beta)
, where

d^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=\phi_{t}^{-1}d_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))
. Here,
\phi_{t}
is a linear isomorphism
since

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\circ\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{n}^{i}t^{in}=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{n}^{i}t^{in}\circ\phi_{t}=id_{A[[t]]}.

Moreover, we have
\phi_{t}\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ\phi_{t}
and
\phi_{t}\circ\beta=\beta\circ\phi_{t}
.

It is straightforward to prove that
d^{\prime}_{t}
is a one-parameter formal deformation of
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
and
d_{t}\sim d^{\prime}_{t}
. Assume that
d^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq 0}d^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}
and use the fact that
\phi_{t}\circ d^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=d_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))
. Then

\displaystyle(id_{A}-f_{n}t^{n})\Big(\sum_{i\geq 0}d^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}\Big)=\Big(d_{0}+\sum_{i\geq n}d_{i}t^{i}\Big)(x-f_{n}(x)t^{n},y-f_{n}(y)t^{n}),

i.e.,

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{0}(x,y)+\sum_{i\geq 1}d^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}-\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{n}\circ d^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{0}(x,y)-(d_{0}(f_{n}(x),y)+d_{0}(x,f_{n}(y)))t^{n}+d_{0}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y))t^{2n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\geq n}d_{i}(x,y)t^{i}-\sum_{i\geq n}(d_{i}(f_{n}(x),y)+d_{i}(x,f_{n}(y))t^{i+n}+\sum_{i\geq n}d_{i}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y))t^{i+2n}.

Then we have
d_{1}^{\prime}=d_{2}^{\prime}=...=d_{n-1}^{\prime}=0
. Since

, then

\displaystyle d_{n}^{\prime}(x,y)-f_{n}\circ d_{0}(x,y)=-(d_{0}(f_{n}(x),y)+d_{0}(x,f_{n}(y)))+d_{n}(x,y).

Hence
d_{n}^{\prime}=d_{n}-\delta^{1}f_{n}=0
and
d^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=d^{\prime}_{0}+\sum_{i\geq n+1}d^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}
. By induction, this procedure
ends with

, so.,
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is analytically rigid.
∎

4.1

In this section, we construct the dual representation of a representation of a BiHom-alternative algebra
without any additional condition. This is nontrivial and to our knowledge, people needed to add a very
strong condition to obtain a representation on the dual space in former studies which restricts
its development.

Let

for all
x\in A,u\in V,\xi\in V^{*}.

However, in general
(r^{*},\ell^{*})
is not a representation of
A
. Define

by

\ell^{\star}(x)(\xi):=\ell^{*}(\alpha^{-1}\beta^{2}(x))\big((\phi^{-1}\psi^{-1})^{*}(\xi)\big)

(4.27)

and

(4.28)

for all

.

\displaystyle\langle\ell^{\star}(x)(\xi),u\rangle=\langle\xi,\ell(\alpha^{-2}\beta(x))(\phi^{-1}\psi^{-1}(u))\rangle

(4.29)

\displaystyle\langle r^{\star}(x)(\xi),u\rangle=\langle\xi,r(\alpha\beta^{-2}(x))(\phi^{-1}\psi^{-1}(u))\rangle

(4.30)

for all
x\in A,u\in V,\xi\in V^{*}
.

.

(V,\ell,r,\phi,\psi)

For all

In this section, we deal with extensions of BiHom-alternative algebras.

An algebra

A central extension of a BiHom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is an extension in which the annihilator of

contains

Example 4.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-alternative algebra,
V
a vector space and
\omega:A\times A\to V
be a bilinear map. We define the following multiplication on the vector space
direct sum
A\oplus V
by

\displaystyle(x+u)\bullet(y+v)=xy+\omega(x,y),\ \forall\ x,y\in A,\ u,v\in V.

(4.33)

Define the linear maps
\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta}:A\oplus V\to A\oplus V
by

\overline{\alpha}(x+u)=\alpha(x)+u,\quad\overline{\beta}(x+u)=\beta(x)+u,\ \forall x\in A,\ u\in V.

It is clear that
\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}=\overline{\beta}\overline{\alpha}
.
Then
(A\oplus V,\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
is a BiHom-alternative algebra if and only if

\displaystyle\overline{\alpha}\big((x+u)\bullet(y+v)\big)=\big(\overline{\alpha}(x+u)\bullet\overline{\alpha}(y+v)\big),

\displaystyle\overline{\beta}\big((x+u)\bullet(y+v)\big)=\big(\overline{\beta}(x+u)\bullet\overline{\beta}(y+v)\big),

\displaystyle\big(\overline{\beta}(x+u)\bullet\overline{\alpha}(x+u)\big)\bullet\overline{\beta}(y+v)-\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}(x+u)\bullet\big(\overline{\alpha}(x+u)\bullet(y+v)\big)=0,

\displaystyle\big((x+u)\bullet\overline{\beta}(y+v)\big)\bullet\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}(y+v)-\overline{\alpha}(x+u)\bullet\big(\overline{\beta}(y+v)\bullet\overline{\alpha}(y+v)\big)=0,

which are equivalent to

\displaystyle\omega(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))=\omega(\beta(x),\beta(y))=\omega(x,y),

(4.34)

\displaystyle\omega(\beta(x)\alpha(x),\beta(y))=\omega(\alpha\beta(x),\alpha(x)y),

(4.35)

\displaystyle\omega(x\beta(y),\alpha\beta(y))=\omega(\alpha(x),\beta(y)\alpha(y)).

(4.36)

In this case, we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V\xrightarrow{\ \ i\ \ }A\oplus V\xrightarrow{\ \ \pi\ \ }A\longrightarrow 0

ker(\pi)=\{0\}\oplus V\subset Ann(A\oplus V)
. Then the extension is central. It is called the central extension of
A
by
V
via
\omega
.

Note that if we consider
V
as a trivial bimodule of
A
, then
A\oplus V
is a central extension of
A
by
V
via
\omega
if and only if
\omega\in Z^{2}(A;V)
.

The concept of
T_{\theta}
-Extensions was developed first in
[
3
]
. We aim in the sequel to discuss it for BiHom-alternative algebras.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-alternative algebra and
(V,\ell,r,\phi,\psi)
be a bimodule of
A
. Let
\theta:A\times A\to V
be a bilinear map.
Define on the direct sum
A\oplus V
the product

\displaystyle(x+u)\circ(y+v)=xy+\ell(x)v+r(y)u+\theta(x,y).

(4.37)

Then
(A\oplus V,\circ,\alpha+\phi,\beta+\psi)
is a BiHom-alternative algebra if and only if
the following conditions holds

\displaystyle\phi\theta(x,y)=\theta(\alpha(x),\alpha(y)),\ \psi\theta(x,y)=\theta(\beta(x),\beta(y)),

(4.38)

\displaystyle\theta(\beta(x)\alpha(y),\beta(z))+r(\beta(z))\theta(\beta(x),\alpha(y))-\theta(\alpha\beta(x),\alpha(y)z)-\ell(\alpha\beta(x))\theta(\alpha(y),z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta(\beta(y)\alpha(x),\beta(z))+r(\beta(z))\theta(\beta(y),\alpha(x))-\theta(\alpha\beta(y),\alpha(x)z)-\ell(\alpha\beta(y))\theta(\alpha(x),z)=0,

(4.39)

\displaystyle\theta(x\beta(y),\alpha\beta(z))+r(\alpha\beta(z))\theta(x,\beta(y))-\theta(\alpha(x),\beta(y)\alpha(z))-\ell(\alpha(x))\theta(\beta(y),\alpha(z))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta(x\beta(z),\alpha\beta(y))+r(\alpha\beta(y))\theta(x,\beta(z))-\theta(\alpha(x),\beta(z)\alpha(y))-\ell(\alpha(x))\theta(\beta(z),\alpha(y))=0.

(4.40)

This means that
\theta\in Z^{2}(A;V)
.
At theses circumstances,
A\oplus V
is called the
T_{\theta}
-extension of
A
by
V
via
\theta
.

As an application of the previous result, let us consider the dual representation. Let
(V^{*},r^{\star},\ell^{\star},(\phi^{-1})^{*},(\psi^{-1})^{*})
be the dual representation of

(V,\ell,r,\phi,\psi)
. Define a bilinear map
\theta:A\times A\to V^{*}
and consider on the vector space
A\oplus V^{*}
the product

\displaystyle(x+f)\circ(y+g)=xy+r^{\star}(x)g+\ell^{\star}(y)f+\theta(x,y).

(4.41)

Then
(A\oplus V^{*},\circ,\alpha+(\phi^{-1})^{*},\beta+(\psi^{-1})^{*})
is a BiHom-alternative algebra if and only if
the following conditions holds

\displaystyle(\phi^{-1})^{*}\theta(x,y)=\theta(\alpha(x),\alpha(y)),\ (\psi^{-1})^{*}\theta(x,y)=\theta(\beta(x),\beta(y)),

(4.42)

\displaystyle\theta(\beta(x)\alpha(y),\beta(z))+\ell^{\star}(\beta(z))\theta(\beta(x),\alpha(y))-\theta(\alpha\beta(x),\alpha(y)z)-r^{\star}(\alpha\beta(x))\theta(\alpha(y),z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta(\beta(y)\alpha(x),\beta(z))+\ell^{\star}(\beta(z))\theta(\beta(y),\alpha(x))-\theta(\alpha\beta(y),\alpha(x)z)-r^{\star}(\alpha\beta(y))\theta(\alpha(x),z)=0,

(4.43)

\displaystyle\theta(x\beta(y),\alpha\beta(z))+\ell^{\star}(\alpha\beta(z))\theta(x,\beta(y))-\theta(\alpha(x),\beta(y)\alpha(z))-r^{\star}(\alpha(x))\theta(\beta(y),\alpha(z))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\theta(x\beta(z),\alpha\beta(y))+\ell^{\star}(\alpha\beta(y))\theta(x,\beta(z))-\theta(\alpha(x),\beta(z)\alpha(y))-r^{\star}(\alpha(x))\theta(\beta(z),\alpha(y))=0.

(4.44)

Under these considerations,
A\oplus V^{*}
is called the
T^{*}_{\theta}
-extension of
A
by
V^{*}
via
\theta
.

Note that the
T^{*}
-extension corresponds to the case of
\theta=0
in the above construction.

5
Generalized derivations of BiHom-alternative algebras

This section is devoted to investigate generalized derivations of BiHom-alternative algebras. Throughout the sequel
A
denotes a BiHom-alternative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
.

For any integer
k
and
l
, denote by
\alpha^{k}
the
k
-times composition of
\alpha
and by
\beta^{l}
the
l
-times composition of
\beta
, i.e

\alpha^{k}=\underbrace{\alpha\circ\cdots\circ\alpha}_{k-times},\quad\beta^{l}=\underbrace{\beta\circ\cdots\circ\beta}_{l-times}.

Let
U
be the subspace of
End(A)
defined by

U:=\{u\in End(A)\mid u\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ u,\ u\circ\beta=\beta\circ u\}

and let
\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta}:U\to U
be two linear maps defined as follow

\widetilde{\alpha}(u)=\alpha\circ u,\ \widetilde{\beta}(u)=\beta\circ u,\ \forall u\in U.

The space
(U,[\cdot,\cdot],\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta})
is a BiHom-Lie algebra where

[u,v]=u\circ v-v\circ u
for any
u,v\in U
.

Definition 5.1
.

A linear map
D:A\to A
is said to be an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-derivation on
A
, for
k,l\in\mathbb{R}
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

\displaystyle[D,\alpha]=0,\ [D,\beta]=0,

(5.45)

\displaystyle D(xy)=D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y),\ \forall x,y\in A.

(5.46)

We denote the set of all
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-derivations by
Der_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
and

Der(A)=\bigoplus_{k,l\geq 0}Der_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
.

We can easily show that
Der(A)
is equipped with a Lie algebra structure. In fact, for
D\in Der_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
and
D^{\prime}\in Der_{\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}}(A)
, we have
[D,D^{\prime}]\in Der_{\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}}(A)
, where
[D,D^{\prime}]
is the standard commutator defined by
[D,D^{\prime}]=DD^{\prime}-D^{\prime}D
.

It is well known that the BiHom-commutator algebra
A^{-}
of
A
is a BiHom-Malcev algebra and
the BiHom-anti-commutator algebra
A^{+}
of
A
is a BiHom-Jordan algebra. We state the following result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
D
be an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-derivation on
A
then
D
is still an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-derivation on its associated BiHom-Jordan algebra
A^{+}
and on its associated BiHom-Malcev algebra
A^{-}
.

Definition 5.2
.

A linear map
D\in End(A)
is said to be an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-quasi-derivation of
A
if there exists a linear map
D^{\prime}\in End(A)
such that

\displaystyle[D,\alpha]=0,~~[D^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha]=0,[D,\beta]=0,~~[D^{{}^{\prime}},\beta]=0,

\displaystyle D^{{}^{\prime}}(xy)=D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y),~~\forall~~x,y\in A,

and we say that
D
associates with
D^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.3
.

A linear map
D\in End(A)
is said to be an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-generalized derivation of
A
if there exist linear maps
D^{\prime},D^{\prime\prime}\in End(A)
such that

\displaystyle[D,\alpha]=0,~~[D^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha]=0,~~[D^{{}^{\prime\prime}},\alpha]=0,[D,\beta]=0,~~[D^{{}^{\prime}},\beta]=0,~~[D^{{}^{\prime\prime}},\beta]=0,

\displaystyle D^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(xy)=D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D^{{}^{\prime}}(y),~~\forall x,y\in A,

and we also say that
D
associates with
D^{\prime}
and
D"
.

Definition 5.4
.

An
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-generalized derivation
D
of
A
associated with
D^{\prime}
and
D"
is said to be symmetric if (for any
x,y\in A
)

D^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(xy)=D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D^{{}^{\prime}}(y)=D^{\prime}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y).

The sets of generalized derivations and quasi-derivations and symmetric
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-generalized derivations will be denoted by
GDer(A)
,
QDer(A)
and
SGDer(A)
respectively.

It is easy to see that

Der(A)\subset QDer(A)\subset SGDer(A)\subset GDer(A).

Definition 5.5
.

A linear map
\theta\in End(A)
is said to be an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-centroid of
A
if

\displaystyle[\theta,\alpha]=0,\ [\theta,\beta]=0,

(5.47)

\displaystyle\theta(xy)=\theta(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)=\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)\theta(y),~~\forall~~x,y\in A.

(5.48)

The set of
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-centroids of
A
is denoted by
C(A)
.

Definition 5.6
.

A linear map
\theta\in End(A)
is said to be an
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-quasi-centroid of
A
if

\displaystyle[\theta,\alpha]=0,\ [\theta,\beta]=0,

(5.49)

\displaystyle\theta(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)=\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)\theta(y),~~\forall~~x,y\in A.

(5.50)

The set of
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-quasi-centroids of
A
is denoted by
QC(A)
.
It is obvious that
C(A)\subset QC(A)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
D\in Der(A)
and
\theta\in C(A)
. Then

\displaystyle[D,\theta]\in C(A).

Proof.

Assume that
D\in Der_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}},~~\theta\in C_{\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}}(A)
. For arbitrary
x,y\in A
, we have

\displaystyle D\theta(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)

\displaystyle=D(\theta(x)\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y))-\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(\theta(x))D(\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y))

\displaystyle=D(\theta(x)\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y))-\theta(\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x))D(\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y))

\displaystyle=D\theta(xy)-\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)\theta D(y).

(5.51)

and

\displaystyle\theta D(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)

\displaystyle=\theta(D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y))

\displaystyle=\theta D(xy)-\theta(\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y))

\displaystyle=\theta D(xy)-(\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)\theta D(y)).

(5.52)

By making the difference of equations
(
5.51
) and (
5.52
), we get

\displaystyle[D,\theta](xy)=[D,\theta](x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y).

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

C(A)\subseteq QDer(A)
.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in C_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
and
x,y\in A
, then we have

\displaystyle\theta(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)\theta(y)

\displaystyle=\theta(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\theta(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)

\displaystyle=2\theta(xy)=D^{{}^{\prime}}(xy).

Then
\theta\in QDer_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

[QC(A),QC(A)]\subseteq QDer(A)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\theta\in QC_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
and
\theta^{\prime}\in QC_{\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}}(A)
. Then for all
x,y\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\theta(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)=\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)\theta(y),

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(x)\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y)=\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(x)\theta^{\prime}(y).

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle[\theta,\theta^{\prime}](x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta\theta^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}\theta)(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\theta^{\prime}(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)-\theta^{\prime}\theta(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)\theta^{\prime}\theta(y)-\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)\theta\theta^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)[\theta,\theta^{\prime}](y),

which implies that
[\theta,\theta^{\prime}](x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)+\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)[\theta,\theta^{\prime}](y)=0.

Then
[\theta,\theta^{\prime}]\in QDer_{\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}}(A)
.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

The spaces
GDer(A),\ QDer(A)
and
C(A)
are BiHom-subalgebras of
(U,[\cdot,\cdot],\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta})
.

Proof.

For any generalized
\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}
-derivation
D
, it is obvious to see that

D\circ\widetilde{\alpha}=\widetilde{\alpha}\circ D\in GDer_{\alpha^{k+1}\beta^{l}}\ \textrm{and}\ D\circ\widetilde{\beta}=\widetilde{\beta}\circ D\in GDer_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l+1}}.

So that
\widetilde{\alpha}(GDer(A))\subseteq GDer(A)
and
\widetilde{\beta}(GDer(A))\subseteq GDer(A)
.

Let
D_{1}\in GDer_{\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}}(A)
and
D_{2}\in GDer_{\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}}(A)
, then, for
x,y\in A
, we have

\displaystyle D_{1}"(xy)

\displaystyle=D_{1}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D^{\prime}_{1}(y),

(5.53)

\displaystyle D_{2}"(xy)

\displaystyle=D_{2}(x)\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y)+\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(x)D^{\prime}_{2}(y).

(5.54)

Moreover

\displaystyle[D_{1},D_{2}](x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)=

\displaystyle D_{1}D_{2}(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)-D_{2}D_{1}(x)\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{1}"(D_{2}(x)\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y))-\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}D_{2}(x)D_{1}^{\prime}\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(y)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle D_{2}"(D_{1}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y))+\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}D_{1}(x)D_{2}^{\prime}\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{1}"D_{2}"(xy)-D_{1}"(\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(x)D_{2}^{\prime}(y))

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle D_{2}"(\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D_{1}^{\prime}(y))+\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)D_{2}^{\prime}D_{1}^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle D_{2}"D_{1}"(xy)+D_{2}"(\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D_{1}^{\prime}(y))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle D_{1}"(\alpha^{s}\beta^{t}(x)D_{2}^{\prime}(y))-\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)D_{1}^{\prime}D_{2}^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[D_{1}",D_{2}"](xy)-\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}(x)[D_{1}^{\prime},D_{2}^{\prime}](y).

Then
[D_{1},D_{2}]
is a generalized
\alpha^{k+s}\beta^{l+t}
-derivation on
A
. Therefore
GDer(A)
is a BiHom-subalgebra of
(U,[\cdot,\cdot],\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta})
.

Similarly, we can show that
QDer(A)
and
C(A)
are also BiHom-subalgebras of
(U,[\cdot,\cdot],\widetilde{\alpha},\widetilde{\beta})
.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

We have
SGDer(A)=QDer(A)+QC(A)
.

Proof.

Let
D\in SGDer(A)
associated with
D^{\prime}
and
D"
. Then for any
x,y\in A
, we have

D^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(xy)=D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D^{{}^{\prime}}(y)=D^{\prime}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y).

We remark that
D=\frac{D+D^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{D-D^{\prime}}{2}
. So we will prove that

\frac{D+D^{\prime}}{2}\in QDer(A)
and
\frac{D-D^{\prime}}{2}\in CD(A)
. For this, take
x,y\in A
, then

\displaystyle\frac{D+D^{\prime}}{2}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)\frac{D+D^{\prime}}{2}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+D^{\prime}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y)+\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D^{\prime}(y))

\displaystyle=D"(xy),

which implies that
\frac{D+D^{\prime}}{2}\in QDer(A)
.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{D-D^{\prime}}{2}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(D(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y)-D^{\prime}(x)\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D(y)-\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)D^{\prime}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{k}\beta^{l}(x)\frac{D-D^{\prime}}{2}(y),

which means that
\frac{D-D^{\prime}}{2}\in CD(A)
. Hence
D\in QDer(A)+QC(A)
.
Moreover, it is straightforward to prove the
QDer(A)+QC(A)\subset SGD(A)
. Therefore
SGD(A)=QDer(A)+QC(A)
.

∎

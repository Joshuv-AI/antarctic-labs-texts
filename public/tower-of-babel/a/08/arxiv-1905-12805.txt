Regularity of the superstring supermeasure and the superperiod map
Giovanni Felder, David Kazhdan, Alexander Polishchuk

Abstract
. The supermeasure whose integral is the genus g g vacuum amplitude of superstring theory is potentially singular on the locus in the moduli space of supercurves where the corresponding even theta-characteristic has nontrivial sections. We show that the supermeasure is actually regular for g ≤ 11 g\leq 11 . The result relies on the study of the superperiod map. We also show that the minimal power of the classical Schottky ideal that annihilates the image of the superperiod map is equal to g g if g g is odd and is equal to g g or g − 1 g-1 if g g is even.



1.
Introduction

The moduli space of supercurves
{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{S}}_{g}
(of given genus
g
) has been
studied in many papers, including
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
15
]
, motivated by superstring perturbation
theory, see
[
24
]
for a recent account with references to the
original literature. The space
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
superstack of complex dimension
3g-3|2g-2
. The corresponding reduced
stack
{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{bos}}
(where “bos” stands for “bosonic”) is the moduli stack of pairs
(C,L)
, where
C
is a
usual smooth projective curve of genus
g
and
L
is a
theta-characteristic on
C
(also known as spin structure). The space

{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{bos}}
(and hence
{\mathcal{S}}
) has two components, which we call
even

and
odd
, that are distinguished by the condition that
L
is
even or odd.

In the first part of this paper we study the behavior of the
superstring supermeasure on the even component
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
of the moduli
space of supercurves
{\mathcal{S}}
(the supermeasure vanishes on the odd
component). Superstring perturbation theory is well-understood up to
genus 2, see
[
7
,
8
]
. In this case the
supermoduli spaces are canonically projected, so that it is possible
to first integrate the supermeasure over odd variables to get a
measure on the moduli space of spin curves and a description in
terms of classical geometry. In higher genus there is no
projection to the reduced space
[
11
]

but there is also an additional difficulty:
the
supermeasure is a priori only defined on a Zariski open subset of the
moduli space where the corresponding theta-characteristic has no
nontrivial section. E. Witten recently revisited and extended the
study of the supermeasure in
[
25
]
and argued that the
supermeasure actually extends smoothly to the whole moduli space for
low genus, indicating potential difficulties starting at
genus 11.

Our main result is the regularity of this supermeasure
for
g\leq 11
along the locus where the corresponding
theta-characteristic acquires a section (see Theorem
5.2
),
confirming Witten’s expectation.

More precisely, the contribution of genus
g
to the vacuum amplitude
of type II superstring theory is given as the integral of the supermeasure
(section of the Berezinian of the cotangent bundle) over a suitable cycle
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}\times\overline{{\mathcal{S}}^{+}}

(where the bar denotes the complex conjugate space).
This supermeasure can be expressed in
holomorphic terms in terms of the super analogue of the Mumford
isomorphism, constructed in
[
22
]
, see

[
21
]
. The relevant special case of the
Mumford isomorphism is a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Ber}^{5}(R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/{\mathcal{S}}})\cong\operatorname{Ber}(T^{*}_{\mathcal{S}})

of the fifth tensor power of the
Berezinian of the derived direct image
of the relative dualizing sheaf (Berezinian)

\omega_{X/{\mathcal{S}}}
of the universal supercurve
\pi\colon X\to{\mathcal{S}}
with the
Berezinian of the holomorphic cotangent bundle.

The supermeasure is defined on a certain open subset of the product

{\mathcal{S}}^{+}\times\overline{{\mathcal{S}}^{+}}
. In the corresponding reduced space

{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{bos}}^{+}\times\overline{{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{bos}}^{+}}
we have a natural locus of pairs of
curves with theta-characteristics
((C,L),(C^{\prime},L^{\prime}))
such that
C=C^{\prime}
,
which we call the
quasi-diagonal
. The supermeasure can be
defined as a meromorphic section of the holomorphic Berezinian on a
neighborhood of the quasi-diagonal in
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}\times\overline{{\mathcal{S}}^{+}}
. It is
the image of the canonical section of

\operatorname{Ber}^{5}(R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/{\mathcal{S}}})\boxtimes\operatorname{Ber}^{5}(R\pi_{*}\omega_{\overline{X}/\overline{{\mathcal{S}}}})

induced by the
embedding of
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/{\mathcal{S}}}
into the local system

R^{1}\pi_{*}\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{S}}}
, which has a canonical pairing.
The possible problem with the regularity occurs when the higher direct
images
R^{i}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/{\mathcal{S}}}
are not locally free. This happens
near points
(C,L)
such that
H^{0}(C,L)\neq 0
. We show in Theorem

5.2
that the supermeasure extends to a regular section for

g\leq 11
.

Note that if we view
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}\times\overline{{\mathcal{S}}^{+}}
as a real supermanifold
then a section of the holomorphic Berezinian can be viewed as a codimension
6g-6|0
closed integral form on it.
Thus, we have to extend the quasi-diagonal to a sub-supermanifold
\Delta
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}\times\overline{{\mathcal{S}}^{+}}
of real dimension
6g-6|4g-4
.
We can integrate over
\Delta
the restriction of the holomorphic Berezinian provided the supermeasure is regular in its neighborhood. The integral does not depend on a choice of
\Delta
since the integral form is closed
(there are convergence issues at infinity, which we do not consider in this paper).

In the second part of the paper we study the superperiod map at the generic point of the
component
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
.
The superperiod map gives rise to a natural
super-Schottky ideal

{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}
on the period domain,
which is containd in the usual Schottky ideal
{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}
describing the image of the classical period map.
The problem of studying
{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}
was raised in
[
3
, Rem. 6.10]
.
Our second main result concerns the minimal power
d
such that

{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}^{d}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}.

It is easy to see that
d\leq g
.
We prove that for
g
odd, one has
d=g
, while for
g
even,
d\geq g-1
.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we review some
standard facts about supercurves and their moduli. In Section

3
we study the direct image
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
and the higher direct image

R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
of the relative
Berezinian of the universal supercurve and show that they are locally
free on the open set where the even theta-characteristic has no
nontrivial sections and are not locally free away from it, as well as on the component of
moduli space with odd theta-characteristic. In the following Section

5
we study the behaviour of the period matrix as we approach
the divisor of even theta-characteristics with nontrivial
sections. The key idea is to realize the symplectic local system

R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}
, for a family of supercurves

\pi\colon X\to S
, where
\mathbb{C}_{X/S}=\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S}
, as a symplectic reduction of a bigger symplectic
bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
with a Lagrangian subbundle
L_{\mathrm{can}}
whose
reduction is
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
. Away from the divisor of even
theta-characteristics with nontrivial sections, the Lagrangian
subbundle
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
of
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}
is locally the graph of the
period matrix, a map
\Omega\colon\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda
for some
Lagrangian splitting
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}=\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}
. The singularities of
\Omega
are governed by the map

L_{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{V}/L_{\mathrm{can}}
where the Lagrangian
subbundle
L_{\Lambda}\subset\mathcal{V}
projects to
\Lambda
. The
period matrix has a pole on the locus where this map fails to be an
isomorphism. We show that this locus is locally the zero set of a
Pfaffian
f
and that
f\Omega
is regular. This result allows us to
compute the behavior of the supermeasure as we approach the locus,
leading to our regularity result. In the remaining sections we study
the second variation of the period matrix in the odd directions at a
generic point of the moduli space of supercurves with even theta
characteristic. An analytic formula for the second variation was given
by D’Hoker and Phong in
[
6
]
in terms of the Szegö
kernel. We present in Section
6
an alternative approach by
expressing the second variation as a Massey product. We study this
Massey product in Section
7
giving explicit formulas, in
particular for hyperelliptic curves. This formula is then used in
Section
8
to prove our result on the super-Schottky ideal.

Conventions

We work with either complex algebraic or analytic superspaces.
Whenever we work with the sheaf
\C_{X/S}
, we use classical topology and analytic framework.
For a superscheme
S
we denote by
|S|
the underlying topological space and by
S_{\operatorname{bos}}
its bosonization, which is a usual scheme with
the underlying topological space
|S|
and the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}/({\mathcal{O}}_{S}\cdot{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{-})
(the quotient by the ideal generated by odd functions).
By a
curve
we always mean a connected smooth projective curve over
\C
. When considering moduli spaces of curves we
always assume that the genus
g
of a curve is
\geq 2
.
By a
supercurve
we always mean a compact complex supermanifold (or a proper smooth algebraic supervariety)
of dimension
1|1
with a superconformal
structure (see Section
2.1
for details). These are often called
super Riemann surfaces
.
For a family of smooth curves
C/S
(resp., supercurves
X/S
) we denote by
\omega_{C/S}
(resp.,
\omega_{X/S})

the relative dualizing sheaf (given by the relative Berezinian in the supercase).
Since we work in the super-context throughout the paper, we drop
the prefix “super” in
supervector bundles
and
supervector spaces
.

2.
Supercurves

In this section we review some standard material on supercurves (also
called super Riemann surfaces).

2.1.
Berezinian of a supercurve

Let
\pi\colon X\to S
be a smooth proper supercurve over a superscheme
S
. By
definition the tangent sheaf
T_{X/S}
has a locally free

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule
{\mathcal{D}}
of rank
(0,1)
such that the composition
of the canonical projection with the Lie bracket

{\mathcal{D}}\otimes{\mathcal{D}}\to T_{X/S}/{\mathcal{D}}
, which is a map of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules,
is an isomorphism. Thus, we have an exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{D}}\to T_{X/S}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes 2}\to 0

(2.1)

The dual sequence is

0\to({\mathcal{D}}^{\vee})^{\otimes 2}\to\Omega_{X/S}^{1}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}\to 0

(2.2)

and taking Berezinians shows that

{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}\cong\omega_{X/S}:=\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{X/S}^{1})
.

Below we work with sheaves in the classical topology.

The map
\Omega_{X/S}^{1}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\vee}
defines an

\mathcal{O}_{S}
-linear derivation

\delta\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\omega_{X/S}.

The map
\delta
is surjective and its kernel is the sheaf
\mathbb{C}_{X/S}=\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S}
of
functions that are locally constant along the fibers.

Locally there exist fiber coordinates
z,\theta
such that
\mathcal{D}

is spanned by
D=\partial_{\theta}+\theta\partial_{z}
. In these coordinates
the map
\delta
is
f\mapsto[dz|d\theta]D(f)
where
[dz|d\theta]
is
the local section of the Berezinian determined by the basis
dz,d\theta
of
\Omega^{1}_{X/S}
.

The long exact sequence associated with

0\to\mathbb{C}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\rTo{\delta}\omega_{X/S}\to 0

(2.3)

includes

\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}\to 0,

(2.4)

(since
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=0
). Locally we have

R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}\cong H^{i}(|X_{0}|,\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}
,
where
|X_{0}|
is the underlying topological space of a fibre

X_{0}
. Thus,
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\oplus 2g}
and

R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}_{X/S}\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}
. In fact, the latter isomorphism is global, and we have in particular a
surjective trace map

\tau\colon R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}

(2.5)

that plays a role in duality theory (see Sec.
2.8
below).

2.2.
Supercurves over an even base

Assume that
S
is even. Then the decomposition of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
into the even and odd components gives a splitting of
X
:

\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}L=\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus L

where
L
is a relative theta-characteristic on the underlying usual family of curves
C\to S
, with a fixed isomorphism

L\otimes L\to\omega_{C/S}.

(2.6)

The dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X/S}
is
\omega_{C}\oplus L
, with
the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure such that
L
acts by zero
on
\omega_{C}
and its action on
L
is given by the structure map (
2.6
).
The derivation
\delta
is
(d,\mathrm{id})
. The distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
is
the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\delta})
where

\bar{\delta}\colon\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\to\omega_{X/S}

is the map of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules associated with
\delta
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\theta
be a local nowhere vanishing section of
L
, and let
\alpha=\theta^{2}\in\omega_{C/S}

be the image of
\theta\otimes\theta
under the structure map (
2.6
). Then

{\mathcal{D}}=\mathrm{Ker}(\alpha-\theta d\theta),

where we use the natural embedding of
\omega_{C/S}=\Omega_{C/S}
into
\Omega_{X/S}
.

Proof.

The section
\alpha-\theta d\theta
of
\Omega_{X/S}
is nowhere vanishing.
We need to check that it belongs to
\mathrm{Ker}(\bar{\delta})
. By definition
\bar{\delta}
is the map of

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules sending an exact form
d(f\oplus\psi)
to

df\oplus\psi
. Thus,
\bar{\delta}(\theta d\theta)=\theta^{2}
.
On the other hand, since
\alpha\in\Omega_{C/S}
, we have

\bar{\delta}\alpha=\alpha
, and our assertion follows.
∎

2.3.
Local automorphisms

Let
X_{0}\to S_{0}
be a smooth supercurve over an even reduced superscheme

S_{0}
. Let
S_{0}\hookrightarrow S
be a closed embedding into a
superscheme corresponding to the nilpotent ideal
I_{S}
of

\mathcal{O}_{S}
(so that
S_{0}=S_{\mathrm{red}}
). Let

\pi\colon X\to S
be a smooth supercurve such that

X|_{S_{0}}=X\times_{S}S_{0}\cong X_{0}
. The
S
-automorphisms of the
supercurve
X
that induce the identity on
X_{0}
act as the identity
on the underlying topological space of
X
. They are thus sections of
a sheaf
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{X/S}
of unipotent groups. The Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{X/S}
of
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{X/S}
consists of even
superconformal vector fields vanishing on
X_{0}
. It is a sheaf of
nilpotent graded Lie algebras on
X
over
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{S}
.

2.4.
Extensions along odd moduli

Suppose again that a smooth supercurve
X_{0}\to S_{0}
over an even
reduced superscheme is given. Up to isomorphism this is the same as giving
an ordinary curve over
S_{0}
with a theta-characteristic. For each
S

with
S_{\mathrm{red}}=S_{0}
we ask about
smooth supercurves
X\to S
restricting to
X_{0}
over
S_{0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
extension of
X_{0}\to S_{0}
along
S

is a supercurve
X
over
S

equipped with
an isomorphism

X\times_{S_{0}}S\cong X_{0}
as supercurves over
S_{0}
. Two extensions

X,X^{\prime}
along
S
are isomorphic if there is an isomorphism
X\to X^{\prime}

of supercurves over
S
inducing the identity on
X_{0}
.

Working locally on smooth
S
, we may assume that
S=(|S_{0}|,\mathcal{O}_{S})

with
\mathcal{O}_{S}=\mathcal{O}_{S_{0}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\bigwedge W

for some finite dimensional vector space
W
. Then we get an
extension
X\to S
, called the trivial extension,
by setting
X=(|X_{0}|,\mathcal{O}_{X})
and

\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\otimes\bigwedge W
. Any other extension
along
S
is obtained from the trivial extension by twisting by a

1
-cocycle.

Lemma 2.3
.

(cf.
[
15
, Lem. 2.3]
)
Let
X
be an extension of
X_{0}
along
S
. Then the isomorphism
classes of extensions of
X_{0}
along
S
are in natural one to one
correspondence with the first non-abelian cohomology

H^{1}(X,\operatorname{Aut}^{0}_{X/S})
.

Proof.

All extensions are locally isomorphic. Thus, every point
x\in|X_{0}|

has a neighbourhood
U\subset|X_{0}|
so that any extension of

(U,\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}|_{U})
along
S
is isomorphic to

(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{U})
. Hence, for any extension
X^{\prime}
we can find an open
covering
\mathcal{U}=(|U_{i}|)
of
|X_{0}|
with corresponding
supercurves
U_{i}=(|U_{i}|,\mathcal{O}_{X}|_{|U_{i}|})
, and isomorphisms

\varphi_{i}\colon U_{i}\to U_{i}^{\prime}
. On non-empty intersections

|U_{i}|\cap|U_{j}|
we get automorphisms

\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{j}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{X/S}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})
defining a

1
-cocycle with values in
\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{X/S}
. If
\varphi_{i}^{\prime}

are a different choice of local isomorphisms then

\varphi_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i}\circ f_{i}
with
f_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}_{X/S}^{0}

and
\varphi_{ij}
is replaced by the equivalent cocycle

f_{j}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{ij}\circ f_{i}
. Thus, to every extension
X^{\prime}

we associate a well-defined characteristic class

[\varphi_{ij}]\in H^{1}(X,\operatorname{Aut}_{X/S}^{0})
. Conversely,
given a class in
H^{1}(X,\operatorname{Aut}_{X/S}^{0})
represented by
a Čech cocycle
(\varphi_{ij})
we obtain an extension
X^{\prime}
by
gluing
\sqcup U_{i}
over
|U_{i}|\cap|U_{j}|
by the isomorphisms

\varphi_{ij}
. By construction, the characteristic class of
X^{\prime}
is

[\varphi_{ij}]
.
∎

2.5.
Local description of
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}_{X/S}

We give a local description of
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}_{X/S}

(cf.
[
4
]
and
[
24
, Section 2.1.1]
).
We can choose local fibre coordinates
z,\theta
on an open
set
U\subset X
so that the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
defining the supercurve is
spanned by
\partial_{\theta}+\theta\partial_{z}
or, equivalently, is the
kernel of
dz-\theta d\theta
.

We are going to construct elements of
\Gamma(U,\operatorname{Aut}^{0}_{X/S})
of two types.
For
f=f(z)\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U)
, an even function such that

\partial_{\theta}f=0
and
f\equiv z\mod I_{S}
, we define a
superconformal map
S_{f}
by

z\mapsto S_{f}^{*}z=f(z),\quad\theta\mapsto S_{f}^{*}\theta=\sqrt{f^{\prime}(z)}\,\theta,

(2.7)

where
f^{\prime}=\partial_{z}f
.
Note that the square root is well-defined since
f^{\prime}(z)=1+
nilpotent.
On the other hand, for
\varphi=\varphi(z)\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{-}(U)
, an odd function
such that
\partial_{\theta}\varphi=0
, we define
T_{\varphi}
by

z\mapsto T_{\varphi}^{*}z=z+\theta\varphi(z),\quad\theta\mapsto T_{\varphi}^{*}\theta=\theta+\varphi(z)+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\,\theta\,\varphi(z)\,\varphi^{\prime}(z).

(2.8)

Lemma 2.4
.

The maps
S_{f},T_{\varphi}
are in
\Gamma(U,\operatorname{Aut}^{0}_{X/S})

and every element of this group can be uniquely written in the form
S_{f}\circ T_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

A superconformal transformation is an automorphism such
that
g^{*}(dz-\theta d\theta)=\lambda(dz-\theta d\theta)
for some
function
\lambda
. Let
g^{*}z=g_{0}(z)+\theta g_{1}(z)
and

g^{*}\theta=h_{0}(z)+\theta h_{1}(z)
, with
g_{0},h_{1}
even and
g_{1},h_{0}

odd. The condition that
g
reduces to the identity on
X_{0}
is

g_{0}(z)=z+
nilpotent and
h_{1}(z)=1+
nilpotent. Thus
g
is
superconformal if and only if

h_{0}(z)=\frac{g_{1}(z)}{h_{1}(z)},\quad h_{1}(z)^{2}=g^{\prime}_{0}(z)+\frac{g_{1}(z)g_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{g_{0}^{\prime}(z)}.

In this case
\lambda=g_{0}^{\prime}(z)+h_{0}(z)h_{0}^{\prime}(z)+2\theta h_{0}(z)h_{1}^{\prime}(z)
.
Since
g_{0}^{\prime}(z)=1+
nilpotent, we can take the square root:

h_{1}(z)=\pm\sqrt{g_{0}^{\prime}(z)}\left(1+\frac{g_{1}(z)g_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{2g^{\prime}_{0}(z)^{2}}\right)

The condition that
g
restricts to the identity on
X_{0}
selects
the positive sign. This gives

g^{*}z=g_{0}(z)+\theta g_{1}(z),\quad g^{*}\theta=\sqrt{g_{0}^{\prime}(z)}\left(\frac{g_{1}(z)}{g_{0}^{\prime}(z)}+\theta\left(1+\frac{g_{1}(z)g_{1}^{\prime}(z)}{2g^{\prime}_{0}(z)^{2}}\right)\right).

If we set
g_{0}(z)=f(z),g_{1}(z)=f^{\prime}(z)\varphi(z)
, the formulas
simplify slightly:

g^{*}z=f(z)+\theta f^{\prime}(z)\varphi(z)\quad g^{*}\theta=\sqrt{f^{\prime}(z)}\left(\varphi(z)+\theta(1+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(z)\varphi^{\prime}(z))\right).

This reduces to
S_{f}^{*}
for
\varphi=0
and to
T^{*}_{\varphi}
for

f(z)=z
. For general
f,\varphi
we get

g^{*}=T_{\varphi}^{*}\circ S_{f}^{*}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The automorphism
T_{\varphi}

dg
of
\omega_{C}\cong L^{2}
; the result is a section of
L
.

It remains to describe the quadratic form
Q
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
L
be a theta-characteristic on
C\to S
.
There is a unique map of sheaves of vector spaces

w\colon L^{-1}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}L^{-1}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}

such that

(1)

w(a,b)=-(-1)^{p(a)\,p(b)}w(a,b)
,

(2)

w(fa,b)=fw(a,b)+\langle d_{C/S}f,a\otimes b\rangle
,

for all local sections
a,b\in L^{-1}
, of parity
p(a),p(b)
and

f\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
. Here

\langle\;,\;\rangle
is the canonical pairing

\omega_{C}\otimes(L^{-1}\otimes L^{-1})\to\mathcal{O}_{C}
induced
by the structure map
L^{2}\to\omega_{C/S}
.

Proof.

Let
e
be a local basis of
L^{-1}
. Then, for any

f,g\in\mathcal{O}_{C}
,

w(fe,ge)=\langle(df\,g-fdg),e\otimes e\rangle
. This shows
uniqueness. It remains to show that this formula defines a
well-defined map
w
, independent of the choice of
e
. This
follows from the identity

d(hf)\,hg-hg\,d(hf)=h^{2}(fdg-gdf),\quad f,g,h\in\mathcal{O}_{C}.

∎

Remark 2.7
.

Locally,

w(f(z)dz^{-1/2},g(z)dz^{-1/2})=f^{\prime}(z)g(z)-f(z)g^{\prime}(z)

and Lemma
2.6
implies that this expression is well-defined,
independently of the choice of local coordinate
z
.

The quadratic
Q\colon L^{-1}\to\mathcal{O}_{C}
appearing in
T_{\varphi}

is the quadratic form
associated to this bilinear form:

Q(a)=\frac{1}{2}w(a,a).

2.7.
Gluing construction of versal families in odd directions

Let us start with any family
C\to S_{0}
of curves with a relative theta-characteristic
L
and a section

p:S_{0}\to C
. This family defines a split
supercurve
X_{0}=(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus L)
over
S_{0}
.
We want to extend
X_{0}
to a supercurve
X\to S
where

\mathcal{O}_{S}=\mathcal{O}_{S_{0}}\otimes\bigwedge W
with
W=H^{1}(C_{s},L_{s}^{-1})^{\vee}

for some
s\in S_{0}

as in Section
2.4
.

For this we assume in addition that
S_{0}
is affine,
h^{0}(C_{s},L_{s}^{-1})=0
for all
s\in S_{0}
,
and the locally free
{\mathcal{O}}(S_{0})
-module
H^{1}(C,L^{-1})
is trivialized:

H^{1}(C,L^{-1})\simeq W^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}.

The required supercurve over
S
is obtained from the trivial
extension
(|X_{0}|,\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\otimes\bigwedge W)
by twisting by
a class in
H^{1}(X_{0},\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{X_{0}/S})
.
To define this class we cover
C
by two open sets: an affine neighbourhood
U_{0}
of

p(S_{0})\subset C
, and
U_{1}=C\smallsetminus p(S_{0})
. Then a class in

H^{1}(X_{0},\mathrm{Aut}^{0}_{X_{0}/S})
is represented by a cocycle in

\Gamma(U_{0}\cap U_{1},\operatorname{Aut}^{0}_{X_{0}/S})
which we take to be

T_{\varphi}=\exp(\varphi),\quad\varphi=\sum\eta_{i}b_{i},

with
b_{i}\in\Gamma(U_{0}\cap U_{1},L^{-1})
representing a basis of

W^{\vee}\subset H^{1}(C,L^{-1})
and the odd coordinates
(\eta_{i})
forming the dual basis of
W
.
Then the class
[b_{i}]\in H^{1}(C,L^{-1})
is the Kodaira-Spencer class
corresponding to the vector field
\partial/\partial\eta_{i}
.

2.8.
Hodge complex and duality

Recall that the Hodge bundle of an ordinary smooth (proper, flat) family of curves

C\to S
of genus
g
is
\pi_{*}\omega_{C/S}
. It is locally free of rank

g
. The situation is more subtle for supercurves: consider for
example a supercurve over a point with theta-characteristic
L
such
that
\operatorname{dim}\,H^{0}(L)=m>0
.
Then
\pi_{*}\omega_{X}=H^{0}(\omega_{X})=H^{0}(\omega_{C})\oplus H^{0}(L)
has rank

g|m
, which varies from one supercurve to another.
A more adequate object to work with is
the
Hodge complex
of a supercurve
X\to S
defined as the derived direct image

R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
of its relative dualizing sheaf.

Below we will show that over the locus where the underlying theta-characterstic has no nonzero sections,
the sheaf
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}

is locally free of rank
g|0
. On the other hand, we will show that for the universal family, the sheaves

\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
are not locally free near every point where
h^{0}(L)\neq 0
(see Theorem

3.3
).

Recall that for a family of supercurves
\pi:X\to S
one has a canonical
map

\tau:R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}

(see (
2.5
)). Since
R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
has cohomology in degrees
0
and
1
we can
view this map as a morphism in the derived category

R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[-1].

Assume that
S
is affine and the corresponding reduced family

C\to S_{0}
has a marked point
\overline{p}:S_{0}\to C
,
and let
U_{0},U_{1}
be an open covering of
C
, such that
U_{0}
is an affine
neighborhood of
\overline{p}(S_{0})
and
U_{1}
is the complement to
\overline{p}(S_{0})
.
Then we can calculate
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}(S)
as

\operatorname{coker}(\omega_{X/S}(U_{0})\oplus\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{01})).

Thus, we get a canonical (even) residue map

\operatorname{Res}_{\overline{p}}:\omega_{X/S}(U_{01})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S},

which vanishes on the image of
\omega_{X/S}(U_{0})
.
Furthermore, since
\tau
vanishes on the image of
\delta:R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
,
it follows that
\operatorname{Res}_{\overline{p}}
vanishes on
\delta(\omega_{X/S}(U_{01}))
.

We can also replace
U_{01}
be a formal punctured disk around
\overline{p}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Let us consider the (split) formal superdisk
D
and the punctured formal superdisk
D^{\prime}
over
S
, given by the algebras

{\mathcal{O}}_{D}={\mathcal{O}}_{S}[\![z]\!]\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[\![z]\!]\theta,

{\mathcal{O}}_{D^{\prime}}={\mathcal{O}}_{S}(\!(z)\!)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(\!(z)\!)\theta,

where we think of
\theta
as a formal square root
dz^{1/2}
.
We have

\omega_{D}=[dz|d\theta]{\mathcal{O}}_{D},\ \ \omega_{D^{\prime}}=[dz|d\theta]{\mathcal{O}}_{D^{\prime}},

and the derivation
\delta
is given by

\delta:{\mathcal{O}}_{D}\to\omega_{D},{\mathcal{O}}_{D^{\prime}}\to\omega_{D^{\prime}}:f(z)+g(z)\theta\mapsto[dz|d\theta](g(z)+f^{\prime}(z)\theta).

Then the map

\operatorname{Res}:[dz|d\theta]{\mathcal{O}}_{D^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}

is the composition of the projection to
[dz|d\theta]{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(\!(z)\!)\theta
with the usual residue map on
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(\!(z)\!)
.
Note that the fact that
\operatorname{Res}
vanishes on
[dz|d\theta]{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(\!(z)\!)
is a consequence of the fact that it vanishes on the image of
\delta
.

There is an analogue of Grothendieck-Serre duality for families of supercurves in which
\omega_{X/S}

plays a role of a relative dualizing sheaf, see
[
23
, Sec. 2]
(see also
[
20
]
, where the case of
Serre duality on supercurves is worked out in detail).
This duality gives an isomorphism in the derived category of
S
,

R\pi_{*}(V)^{\vee}\simeq R\pi_{*}(V^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X/S})[1],

for any perfect complex
V
over
X
.
A part of this theory is the trace
map

\tau^{\prime}:R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[-1].

We claim that
\tau^{\prime}
coincides with
\tau
up to a sign. Indeed, this follows from the compatibility of
\tau^{\prime}

with the residue map: if
X/S
is equipped with a relative divisor
p\subset X
, where
p\to S
is of relative dimension
0|1
,
then we have an exact triangle

R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}(p)\to\omega_{X/S}(p)|_{p}\rTo{\alpha}R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}[1]

such that the composition of
\alpha
with
\tau^{\prime}
is the residue map

\omega_{X/S}(p)|_{p}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
.

3.
Local freeness and non-freeness

3.1.
Base change formalism

Let
f:X\to S
be a projective map of supervarieties, and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
, flat over
S
.

Assume also that
S
is affine,
S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, where
A
is a supercommutative ring (finitely generated over a field).
Then exactly as in the purely even case (see e.g.,
[
13
, Prop. III.12.2]
) one proves that
there exists a bounded above complex of projective
A
-modules
Q^{\bullet}
, such that
for every finitely generated
A
-module
M
, one has a functorial isomorphism

H^{i}(X,{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{A}M)\simeq H^{i}(Q^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}M)

The study of these cohomology functors as
M\mapsto T^{i}(M)

is parallel to the purely even case (see
[
2
]
for details).

Assume in addition that
A
is local with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.
For every
A
-module
M
there is a natural map

\phi^{i}(M):T^{i}(A)\otimes M\to T^{i}(M).

Let
s
be the closed point of
S
with residue field
k(s)=A/\mathfrak{m}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

(see
[
13
, Sec. III.12]
)
(a) If
\phi^{i}(k(s))
is surjective then
\phi^{i}(M)
is an isomorphism for any
M
.

(b) Assume that
\phi^{i}(k(s))
is surjective. Then
\phi^{i-1}(k(s))
is surjective if and only if
T^{i}(A)
is a free
A
-module.

Note that
T^{i}(A)\simeq H^{0}(S,R^{i}f_{*}{\mathcal{F}})
, so we can use the above Theorem to check local freeness of
R^{i}f_{*}{\mathcal{F}}
.

3.2.
Local freeness for a family of supercurves

Now assume that the fiber dimension of the map between reduced (usual) schemes induced by
f
is
1
.
Then
T^{>1}=0
, so by Theorem
3.1
applied to
i=2
,
\phi^{1}(k(s))
is an isomorphism.
Thus, by Theorem
3.1
(b) applied to
i=1
, if
\phi^{0}(k(s))
is surjective then
R^{1}f_{*}({\mathcal{F}})
is locally free.
Since
\phi^{-1}(k(s))
is always surjective, under the same assumption
f_{*}{\mathcal{F}}
is also locally free.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\pi:X\to S
be a family of supercurves, and let
\overline{\pi}:C\to S_{0}
be the corresponding usual family of
curves with a relative theta-characteristic
L
. Assume that
R\overline{\pi}_{*}L=0
(or equivalently,
H^{*}(L_{s})=0
on every fiber).
Then
R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
,
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
are locally free, and the natural map

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

First, let us apply Theorem
3.1
to the sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.
The assumption on
L
implies that for every point
s\in S
the map

\phi^{0}(k(s)):\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\otimes k(s)\to H^{0}(C_{s},{\mathcal{O}})

is surjective. Hence,
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
are locally free,
and the map
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is an isomorphism.
This implies that locally we have a splitting
R\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\simeq\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[-1]
.

By Grothendieck duality, we have

R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\simeq(R\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}[-1]\simeq(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\oplus R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[-1])^{\vee}[-1].

It follows that
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\simeq(\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{C})^{\vee}
and

\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\simeq(R^{1}\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{C})^{\vee}
are locally free.
∎

3.3.
Local non-freeness

The following result implies that
R^{i}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
is not locally free in the vicinity of any point of the moduli space
where the corresponding theta-characteristic
has non-trivial sections. In particular, for the universal family
\pi:X\to{\mathcal{S}}^{-}
over the component
corresponding to odd theta-characteristics, the sheaves
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}

are not locally free.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\pi:X\to S
be the universal family of supercurves over the moduli stack of supercurves.
Assume that the corresponding theta-characteristic
L_{s}
on
C_{s}
has
h^{0}(C_{s},L_{s})\neq 0
for some
point
s\in S
.
Then the sheaves
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
are not locally free near
s
.

We will use the following general obstacle to local freeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
Q^{\bullet}=[Q^{0}\rTo{f}Q^{1}]
be a two-term complex of vector bundles over a superscheme
S
.
For every point
s\in S
let us define the map

\kappa(s)=\kappa_{Q^{\bullet}}(s):T_{s}S\otimes H^{0}(Q^{\bullet}|_{s})\to H^{1}(Q^{\bullet}|_{s}),

where
T_{s}S
is the Zariski tangent space to
S
at
s
as follows.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be the maximal ideal in the local ring of
s
. Then we have

H^{0}(Q^{\bullet}|_{s})=\ker(Q^{0}/{\mathfrak{m}}Q^{0}\to Q^{1}/{\mathfrak{m}}Q^{1}),

so we have a natural map

H^{0}(Q^{\bullet}|_{s})\to{\mathfrak{m}}Q^{1}/({\mathfrak{m}}f(Q^{0})+{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}Q^{1})\simeq{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\otimes(Q^{1}/f(Q^{0})),

(3.1)

from which
\kappa
is obtained by dualization.

(i) The map
\kappa(s)
depends only on a quasi-isomorphism class of
Q^{\bullet}
.

(ii) Locally near
s
there exists a complex

H^{0}(Q^{\bullet}|_{s})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\rTo{g}H^{1}(Q^{\bullet}|_{s})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S},

quasi-isomorphic to
Q^{\bullet}
,
such that the entries of
g
are in
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
g\mod{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
corresponds to
\kappa(s)
.

(iii) If
\kappa(s)
is nontrivial then
\underline{H}^{1}Q^{\bullet}
is not locally free near
s
.

Proof.

(i) This follows from the naturality of the map (
3.1
)
with respect to chain maps between two-term complexes.

(ii) We can find bases of
Q^{0}|_{s}
and
Q^{1}|_{s}
such that the matrix of
f\mod{\mathfrak{m}}
has form

f\mod{\mathfrak{m}}=\left(\begin{matrix}1_{r}&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right).

Lifting these bases to local bases of
Q^{0}
and
Q^{1}
and then adjusting them appropriately, we can
arrange the matrix of
f
to be of the form

f=\left(\begin{matrix}1_{r}&0\\
0&M\end{matrix}\right)

where
M
has entries in
{\mathfrak{m}}
. But this means that there exists a quasi-isomorphic two-term complex
with the differential given by
M
, and the assertion follows.

(iii) If
\underline{H}^{1}Q^{\bullet}
is locally free near
s
then in a neighborhood of
s
there exists a
two-term complex quasi-isomorphic to
Q^{\bullet}
with zero differential. Hence, by (i),
\kappa(s)=0
.
∎

The crucial part of the proof of Theorem
3.3
is the following calculation of
a component of the obstruction map.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
[Q^{0}\to Q^{1}]
be a resolution of
R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
in a neighborhood of a point
s
of the moduli space
S
. Then
we have decompositions into even and odd components,

H^{i}(\omega_{X_{s}})=H^{i}(\omega_{C_{s}})\oplus H^{i}(L_{s})

and the component

H^{1}(L_{s}^{-1})\otimes H^{0}(\omega_{C_{s}})\to H^{1}(L_{s})

(3.2)

of
\kappa(s)
is given by the cup-product. Furthermore,
the map (
3.2
) is surjective.

Proof.

It is enough to consider the family
\pi:X\to S
over
S=\operatorname{Spec}(\C[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2})
(where
\epsilon
is odd) deforming the supercurve
X_{0}
, with

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}={\mathcal{O}}_{C}\oplus L
over
\operatorname{Spec}(\C)
, where
C=C_{s}
,
L=L_{s}
, associated with an odd tangent vector

v\in H^{1}(C,L^{-1})
.

Let
C=U_{0}\cup U_{1}
be an open affine covering of
C
. Then
R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}

is represented by the complex

[\omega_{X/S}(U_{1})\to\omega_{X/S}(U_{01})/\omega_{X/S}(U_{0})].

Furthermore, we have an identification of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U_{i})
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}(U_{i})[\epsilon]
, so that the two identifications of

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(U_{01})
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}(U_{01})[\epsilon]
differ by an automorphism
T
given by
\exp(\epsilon\widetilde{v})
,
where
\widetilde{v}
is an odd vector field over
U_{01}
representing the class
v\in H^{1}(C,L^{-1})
.
Thus, we can represent
R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
by the complex

[\omega_{X_{0}/S}(U_{1})[\epsilon]\rTo{\alpha\mapsto T(\alpha|_{U_{01}})}\omega_{X_{0}/S}(U_{01})/\omega_{X_{0}/S}(U_{0})].

(3.3)

We claim that the restriction of the induced action of
T
on the Berezinian of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}(U_{01})[\epsilon]
over

\C[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
to
\omega_{C}(U_{01})
is given by

\omega_{C}(U_{01})\to\omega_{C}(U_{01})[\epsilon]\oplus L(U_{01})[\epsilon]:\alpha\mapsto\alpha+\epsilon\langle\widetilde{v},\alpha\rangle.

Indeed, in general this action is given
\alpha\mapsto\alpha+L_{\widetilde{v}}(\alpha)
, where

L_{\widetilde{v}}
is the Lie derivative. Since
\widetilde{v}
is an odd vector field, our claim follows easily from the description
of the differential
\delta
in terms of the splitting
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}={\mathcal{O}}_{C}\oplus L
(see Sec.
2.2
).

Thus, if
\alpha
is a global section of
\omega_{C}
then the differential of the complex (
3.3
)
applied to
\alpha|_{U_{1}}
gives
\epsilon\langle\widetilde{v},\alpha\rangle
, which leads to the required formula for
\kappa(s)
.

To check the surjectivity statement we observe that by Serre duality, the map (
3.2
) is dual
to the map

H^{0}(L_{s})\to\operatorname{Hom}(H^{0}(\omega_{C_{s}}),H^{0}(\omega_{C_{s}}\otimes L_{s})):s\mapsto(\alpha\mapsto\alpha s),

which is clearly injective: for a nonzero
s\in H^{0}(L_{s})
, the corresponding map
H^{0}(\omega_{C_{s}})\to H^{0}(\omega_{C_{s}}\otimes L_{s})

is injective, hence nonzero.
∎

We also need the following algebraic result about free modules over an exterior algebra.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and let
{\mathcal{A}}=\bigwedge^{*}_{R}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}R\theta_{i})
be the exterior algebra with
n

generators
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
over
R
. Let us consider the ideal
{\mathcal{N}}=\bigwedge^{\geq 1}_{R}(R^{n})\subset{\mathcal{A}}
. For an
{\mathcal{A}}
-module

M
, we denote by
M_{\mathcal{N}}
the annihilator of
{\mathcal{N}}
in
M
. Suppose we have a morphism
\phi:M\to M^{\prime}
of
free
{\mathcal{A}}
-modules of finite rank, such that the induced morphism
M_{\mathcal{N}}\to M^{\prime}_{\mathcal{N}}
is an isomorphism.
Then
\phi
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

For a free
{\mathcal{A}}
-module of finite rank
M
, the map induced by the
{\mathcal{A}}
-action,

\theta_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\theta_{n}\cdot:M/{\mathcal{N}}M\to M_{\mathcal{N}}

is an isomorphism. Since
\phi
is compatible with the
{\mathcal{A}}
-action, we deduce that the morphism

M/{\mathcal{N}}M\to M^{\prime}/{\mathcal{N}}M^{\prime}

is an isomorphism. Since
{\mathcal{N}}
is nilpotent, this implies that
\phi
is surjective. Hence,
\phi

is a projection onto a direct summand. Thus, if
K=\ker(\phi)
, we deduce that
K/{\mathcal{N}}K=0
, so
K=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Set
C=C_{s}
,
L=L_{s}
.
By Serre duality, the cup-product map
H^{1}(C,L^{-1})\otimes H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\to H^{1}(C,L)
corresponds
to the cup-product map

H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\otimes H^{0}(C,L)\to H^{0}(C,\omega\otimes L).

(3.4)

The latter map is always nonzero whenever
H^{0}(C,L)\neq 0
.
Thus, using Lemma
3.5
, we see that the obstruction
\kappa(s)
associated with a
resolution of
R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
is nonzero, so
by Lemma
3.4
, the sheaf
R^{1}\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
is not locally free near
s
.

Next, we will deal with the sheaf
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
, replacing
S
by an affine neighborhood of
s
.
The trace map (
2.5
) can be viewed as a morphism in derived category

R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[-1].

More precisely, if
R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}
is represented by a complex of vector bundles
[A\rTo{f}B]
then
\tau
corresponds
to a map
B/f(A)\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
, so we can view it as a chain map
[A\to B]\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[-1]
.
Furthermore, since
\tau
is surjective, there exists a splitting
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to B
of the projection
B\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
,
which we can view as a chain map
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[-1]\to[A\to B]
. Thus, we get a splitting in derived category,

R\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S}\simeq P^{\bullet}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{S}[-1].

Next, by Lemma
3.4
(ii), locally near
s
,
the complex
P^{\bullet}
is quasi-isomorphic to a two-term complex
Q^{\bullet}
of the form

H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\oplus H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\rTo{(f^{-},f^{+})}H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}

(here the space
H^{0}(\omega_{C})
is even, while the spaces
H^{i}(L)
are odd),
where the linear terms of
f^{-}
are given by the map (
3.2
).

Let
S_{0}\subset S
be the reduced subscheme. Then the restriction of
Q^{\bullet}
to
S_{0}
splits
into the direct sum of
H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
and the complex

H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}\rTo{f^{+}|_{S_{0}}}H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}

(3.5)

computing the direct image of the universal theta-characteristic.

Set
{\mathcal{F}}=\underline{H}^{0}Q^{\bullet}=\ker(f^{-},f^{+})
. We want to check that
{\mathcal{F}}
is not locally free.
We distinguish two cases.

Case
h^{0}(L)
is even
. Then the complex (
3.5
) is generically acyclic, so

f^{+}|_{S_{0}}
is generically an isomorphism. In particular,
f^{+}
is injective and the sheaf
\operatorname{coker}(f^{+})
has rank
0
.
It follows that
{\mathcal{F}}
can be identified with the kernel of the morphism

H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\operatorname{coker}(f^{+})

induced by
f^{-}
. In particular,
{\mathcal{F}}
is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-submodule of
H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
.

Locally we have a splitting
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\simeq{\bigwedge}(W)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
, with
{\mathcal{N}}={\bigwedge}^{\geq 1}(W)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
.
Assume that
{\mathcal{F}}
is a locally free
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-module and let us lead this to a contradiction.
Consider the submodule
{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{N}}
consisting of
sections annihilated by
{\mathcal{N}}
.
Since
f^{-}
has entries in
{\mathcal{N}}
, we get that

{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{N}}=H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\bigwedge}^{2g-2}(W)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.6
,
{\mathcal{F}}=H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
.
In other words, the image of
f^{-}:H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}

is contained in the image of
f^{+}:H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
.
Let us consider the following component of
f^{-}
:

f^{-}_{1}:H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\bigwedge}^{2g-3}(W)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}\to H^{1}(L)\otimes{\bigwedge}^{2g-2}(W)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}.

Modulo the maximal ideal we get the linear map

H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes W^{\vee}\simeq H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes H^{1}(L^{-1})\to H^{1}(L)

which is given by the cup-product and is surjective (see Lemma
3.5
). Hence,

H^{1}(L)\otimes{\bigwedge}^{2g-2}(W)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}=(H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{{\mathcal{N}}}
is in the image of
f^{-}
, so it has to be in the image of
f^{+}
.
Since
f^{+}
is injective, it can only be in the image of
(H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{{\mathcal{N}}}
.
But the map

H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}\simeq(H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{{\mathcal{N}}}\to(H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{{\mathcal{N}}}\simeq H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}

induced by
f^{+}|_{S_{0}}
has entries in the maximal ideal, so its image cannot be everything, which is a contradiction.

Case
h^{0}(L)
is odd
. Since the locus of local freeness is open, it is enough to consider the case
h^{0}(L)=1

(since the set of such points is dense in the odd component of the moduli space).
In this case
f^{+}|_{S_{0}}=0
, so
f^{+}
has entries in
{\mathcal{N}}^{2}
.
Hence, we have

{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{N}}=H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}\oplus H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}},

which is a locally free
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
-module of rank
g+1
(we count both odd and even
generators). Let us also consider
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}}
, the annihilator of
{\mathcal{N}}^{2}
in
{\mathcal{F}}
.
We have

{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}}=\ker((H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}}\rTo{f^{-}}(H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{\mathcal{N}})\oplus(H^{0}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S})_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}},

so that

{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}}/{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{N}}\simeq\ker(W^{\vee}\otimes H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}\rTo{f^{-}}H^{1}(L)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}})\oplus W^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}.

It follows that
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}}/{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathcal{N}}
is locally free over
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}
of rank
(g+1)\dim(W)-1
.
But for a free
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-module
{\mathcal{G}}
, we would have

\operatorname{rk}({\mathcal{G}}_{{\mathcal{N}}^{2}}/{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{N}})=\dim(W)\cdot\operatorname{rk}({\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{N}}).

Hence,
{\mathcal{F}}
is not locally free over
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
near
s
.
∎

4.
Symplectic picture

4.1.
Support of a complex of bundles

Let
Q^{\bullet}
be a bounded complex of vector bundles over a superscheme
S
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let us define the quasicoherent sheaf of ideals
{\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet})\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
as
the kernel of the canonical morphism

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\underline{H}^{0}((Q^{\bullet})^{\vee}\otimes Q^{\bullet}).

Equivalently, this ideal consists of
f\in{\mathcal{O}}
such that
the morphism
f\cdot\operatorname{id}
of
Q^{\bullet}
is locally homotopic to zero.

We need some simple properties of this definition,
which in particular imply that
{\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet})
defines a subscheme structure on the support of
Q^{\bullet}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

(i) If complexes
Q^{\bullet}
and
R^{\bullet}
are quasi-isomorphic then
{\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet})={\mathcal{I}}(R^{\bullet})
.
In particular, one has
{\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet})={\mathcal{O}}_{S}
if and only if
Q^{\bullet}
is acyclic.

(ii) One has
{\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet})\subset\cap_{i}\operatorname{Ann}(\underline{H}^{i}Q^{\bullet})
.

(iii) For an open subset
U\subset S
one has
{\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet})|_{U}={\mathcal{I}}(Q^{\bullet}|_{U})
.

(iv) Consider a
2
-term complex
A\rTo{\delta}B
, such that
\delta
is an isomorphism on a Zariski open subset
U\subset S
.
Assume that
S
is affine, and the homomorphism
{\mathcal{O}}(S)\to{\mathcal{O}}(U)
is injective.
Then
f\in H^{0}(S,{\mathcal{I}}(A\to B))
if and only if the morphism
f|_{U}\delta^{-1}:B|_{U}\to A|_{U}
has a regular extension to
S
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the fact that any
quasi-isomorphism between bounded complexes of vector bundles locally becomes a homotopy equivalence.
Parts (ii) and (iii) are straightforward. For part (iv), we observe that a homotopy
h:B\to A
between

f\cdot\operatorname{id}
and
0
satisfies
h\delta=f\operatorname{id}_{A}
, which is equivalent to
h
being an extension of
f\delta^{-1}
.
∎

4.2.
Isotropic intersections/co-intersections

Let
{\mathcal{V}},(\cdot,\cdot)
be a symplectic vector bundle of rank
2m|2n
over an irreducible superscheme
S
,
and let
L_{1},L_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
be a pair of maximal isotropic subbundles of rank
m|n

(this means that
L_{i}
is isotropic and the morphism
{\mathcal{V}}/L_{i}\to L_{i}^{\vee}
induced by
the pairing is an isomorphism).
Then we have a complex of bundles over
S
, concentrated in degrees
0
and
1
,

C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})=C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2}):[L_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}/L_{2}]

that controls the behavior of the intersections
f^{*}L_{1}\cap f^{*}L_{2}=\underline{H}^{0}f^{*}C^{\bullet}
for arbitrary morphisms

f:T\to S
, and of the co-intersection
{\mathcal{V}}/(L_{1}+L_{2})\simeq\underline{H}^{1}C^{\bullet}
.
Note that the symplectic form induces an isomorphism
{\mathcal{V}}/L_{2}\simeq L_{2}^{\vee}
, so we can also write the above complex
as

[L_{1}\to L_{2}^{\vee}],

which in particular shows that
C^{\bullet}(L_{2},L_{1})
is dual to
C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})[-1]
.
On the other hand, we have a natural quasi-isomorphism to
C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})
from
the complex

[L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\to{\mathcal{V}}].

Thus, as an object in the derived category, it is self-dual up to a shift.

We say that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are transversal if the natural map
L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\to{\mathcal{V}}
is an isomorphism.
This is equivalent to exactness of the complex
C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})
.

Proposition 4.3
.

(i) Assume that
L^{\prime}_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
is a maximal isotropic subbundle transversal to both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
.
Let us identify
L^{\prime}_{2}
with
L_{2}^{\vee}
using the pairing between
L_{2}
and
L^{\prime}_{2}
given by the symplectic form.
Then
L_{1}
is the graph of a symmetric morphism

\phi:L_{2}\to L^{\prime}_{2}\simeq L_{2}^{\vee}

(i.e.,
\phi^{*}=\phi
), and the complex
C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})
is isomorphic to

[L_{2}\rTo{\phi}L^{\prime}_{2}].

(ii) Assume that the complex
C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})
is generically exact.
If
n
(the dimension of
L_{i}^{-}
)
is even then locally there exists a maximal isotropic subbundle transversal to both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
.
For any
n
there exists a symmetric representative of
C^{\bullet}(L_{1},L_{2})
,
i.e., there exists a quasi-isomorphic complex of the form

M\rTo{\phi}M^{\vee}

with
\phi^{*}=\phi
. Furthermore, if
n
is even then we can find such
M
of rank
m|n
(same as the rank of
L_{i}
),
while for odd
n
we can find
M
of rank
m|(n+1)
.

Proof.

(i) The first assertion is standard. Using the identification of
L_{2}^{\vee}
with
L^{\prime}_{2}
, we can identify
C^{\bullet}
with

[L_{1}\to L^{\prime}_{2}]
, where the map is given by the projection along
L_{2}
.
Since the projection
L_{1}\to L_{2}
along
L^{\prime}_{2}
is an isomorphism,
we get an isomorphism of complexes

[L_{1}\to L^{\prime}_{2}]\simeq[L_{2}\rTo{\phi}L^{\prime}_{2}]

as claimed.

(ii) Assume first that
n
is even. By part (i), in this case it is enough to check that locally we can choose a maximal isotropic
subbundle
L^{\prime}_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
, transversal to both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
.
Indeed, it is easy to see that one can choose a maximal isotropic subbundle
\widetilde{L}_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
,
complementary to
L_{2}
. Next, for every point
x\in S
, we have

{\mathcal{V}}|_{x}={\mathcal{V}}^{+}_{x}\oplus{\mathcal{V}}^{-}_{x},\ \ L_{i}|_{x}=L_{ix}^{+}\oplus L_{ix}^{-},

where
L_{ix}^{+}
are Lagrangian subspaces of the symplectic vector space
{\mathcal{V}}^{+}|_{x}
and

L_{ix}^{-}
are maximal isotropic subspaces of the orthogonal vector space
{\mathcal{V}}^{-}|_{x}
.
Since generically
L_{1}|_{x}
and
L_{2}|_{x}
have trivial intersection in
{\mathcal{V}}|_{x}
,
it follows that at any point the intersection
L^{-}_{1}|_{x}\cap L^{-}_{2}|_{x}
has even dimension.

Then an easy linear algebra argument (using the fact that
n-\dim L_{1x}^{-}\cap L_{2x}^{-}
is even)
shows that there exists a skew-symmetric map
\psi_{x}^{-}:\widetilde{L}^{-}_{2x}\to L_{2x}^{-}
such that the graph
of
\psi_{x}^{-}
has trivial intersection with
L_{1x}^{-}
. Similarly, there exists a symmetric map

\psi_{x}^{+}:\widetilde{L}^{+}_{2x}\to L_{2x}^{+}
such that the graph of
\psi_{x}^{+}
has trivial intersection with

L_{1x}^{+}
. Now we view
\psi_{x}=(\psi_{x}^{+},\psi_{x}^{-})
as a symmetric map of vector spaces,
extend it to a symmetric morphism of bundles
\psi:\widetilde{L}_{2}\to L_{2}
in a neighborhood of
x
and
define
L^{\prime}_{2}
to be the graph of
\psi
.

Now let us consider the case when
n
is odd. Then we can set
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{V}}\oplus({\mathcal{O}}e_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}e_{2})
,
where
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are odd, and equip
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}

with a symplectic pairing using the pairing on
{\mathcal{V}}
and the standard symmetric pairing on
{\mathcal{O}}e_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}e_{2}
, so that

e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are isotropic and
(e_{1},e_{2})=1
.
Let us also set

L^{\prime}_{1}=L_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}e_{1},\ \ L^{\prime}_{2}=L_{2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}e_{2}.

Then
L^{\prime}_{1}
and
L^{\prime}_{2}
are maximal isotropic in
{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}
, and
the complex
L_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}/L_{2}
is quasi-isomorphic to

[L_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}e_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}/L_{2}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}e_{1}]\simeq[L^{\prime}_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}^{\prime}/L^{\prime}_{2}].

It remains to apply the case of even
n
.
∎

We have the following nice reduction property of the complexes
C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
be a pair of maximal isotropic subbundles in a symplectic bundle of rank
2m|2n
.
Let
M\subset L_{1}
be a subbundle such that the map
L_{2}\to M^{\vee}
, induced by the pairing, is surjective (equivalently,

{\mathcal{V}}=L_{2}+M^{\perp}
).
Then the bundle
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}:=M^{\perp}/M
has an induced symplectic structure and induced maximal isotropic subbundles

\overline{L}_{1}=L_{1}/M
,
\overline{L}_{2}=L_{2}\cap M^{\perp}
in
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}
. Furthermore, there is a natural quasi-isomorphism of
complexes

C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2})\to C^{\bullet}(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}};\overline{L}_{1},\overline{L}_{2}).

Proof.

Note that
\overline{L}_{2}
embeds into
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}
since
L_{2}\cap M\subset(L_{2}+M^{\perp})^{\perp}=0
.
One can easily check that
\overline{L}_{1}
and
\overline{L}_{2}
are maximal isotropic in
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}
.
Now we use a quasi-isomorphism of complexes

[L_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}/L_{2}]\to[L_{1}/M\to{\mathcal{V}}/(M+L_{2})],

and observe that the natural map

\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}/\overline{L}_{2}\simeq M^{\perp}/(M+L_{2}\cap M^{\perp})\to{\mathcal{V}}/(M+L_{2})

is an isomorphism.
∎

4.3.
Determinants

Recall that when a complex of vector bundles of even rank, concentrated in degrees
0
and
1
,

C^{\bullet}=[C^{0}\rTo{d}C^{1}]
, is generically exact then we have a natural section

\theta_{C^{\bullet}}=\det(d)\in\operatorname{Det}(C^{\bullet})^{-1}=\operatorname{Det}(C_{0})^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{Det}(C_{1}),

which vanishes precisely on the locus where
C^{\bullet}
fails to be exact.
Similarly, for an arbitrary complex of (super)vector bundles
C^{\bullet}=[C^{0}\rTo{d}C^{1}]
,
exact over an open subset
U
, there is a natural invertible section

\theta=\theta_{C^{\bullet}}=\operatorname{ber}(d)\in H^{0}(U,\operatorname{Ber}(C^{\bullet})^{-1}).

The key property is that a quasi-isomorphism of complexes
C^{\bullet}\to D^{\bullet}
induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Ber}(C^{\bullet})\rTo{\sim}\operatorname{Ber}(D^{\bullet})
such that
\theta_{C^{\bullet}}
gets identified with
\theta_{D^{\bullet}}
.

Now let
L_{1},L_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
be a pair of maximal isotropic subbundles in a symplectic bundle.
Assume that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are transversal over a nonempty open
U\subset S
.
Then we define

\theta(L_{1},L_{2})\in\operatorname{Ber}(C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2}))^{-1}(U)\simeq\operatorname{Ber}(L_{1})^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(L_{2})^{-1}|_{U}

to be the canonical (even) nonvanishing section corresponding to the exact complex
C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2})|_{U}
.
Equivalently,
\theta(L_{1},L_{2})
is the Berezinian of the isomorphism of bundles

L_{1}|_{U}\to L_{2}^{\vee}|_{U}

induced by the symplectic form. Note that the line bundle
\operatorname{Ber}(C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2}))
has rank
1|0
, so
its even sections are given locally by even functions.

Similarly to the even case, a quasi-isomorphism of exact complexes leads to an isomorphism of their Berezinians compatible
with their canonical sections coming from the Berezinian of the differential.
Thus, in the context of Lemma
4.4
,
the sections
\theta(L_{1},L_{2})
and
\theta(\overline{L}_{1},\overline{L}_{2})
get identified under the isomorphism between the corresponding
line bundles.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
be a pair of maximal isotropic subbundles in a symplectic bundle over
S
,
transversal over some open
j:U\hookrightarrow S
such that
the natural map
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to j_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{U}
is injective.
Assume that for some point
s\in S
,
one has

L_{1s}^{+}\cap L_{2s}^{+}=0.

Then locally near
s
there exists a trivialization of
\operatorname{Ber}(C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2}))
such that
\theta^{-1}(L_{1},L_{2})
corresponds to
f^{2}
, where
f

is a regular function. Furthermore,
f
belongs to the ideal
{\mathcal{I}}(C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}},L_{1},L_{2}))
. In other words,

f\delta^{-1}
is regular near
s
, where
\delta:L_{1}\to{\mathcal{V}}/L_{2}
is the natural map.

Proof.

Locally near
s
we can define a decomposition
L_{1}=L_{1}^{+}\oplus L_{1}^{-}
where
L_{1}^{+}
has rank
m|0

and
L_{2}^{-}
has rank
0|n
.
Let us consider the isotropic subbundle
L_{1}^{+}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
.
Then our assumption implies that in a neighborhood of
s
the morphism
L_{2}\to(L_{1}^{+})^{\vee}
is surjective,
so by Lemma
4.4
, we have a quasi-isomorphism of
C^{\bullet}({\mathcal{V}};L_{1},L_{2})
with

C^{\bullet}(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}},\overline{L}_{1},\overline{L}_{2})
, where
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}=(L_{1}^{+})^{\perp}/L_{1}^{+}
and

\overline{L}_{1}=L_{1}/L_{1}^{+}
has rank
0|n
.
Now by Proposition
4.3
, there exists a quasi-isomorphism

C^{\bullet}(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}},\overline{L}_{1},\overline{L}_{2})\simeq[M\rTo{\phi}M^{\vee}],

with
M
of rank
0|n^{\prime}
and
\phi
selfdual. But this means that actually

\theta^{-1}(L_{1},L_{2})=\operatorname{ber}(\phi)^{-1}
is given by the determinant of a skew-symmetric matrix with entries in
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{+}
.
It remains to set

f=\operatorname{Pf}(\phi),

where
\operatorname{Pf}(?)
denotes the Pfaffian.

For the last assertion, we recall that by Lemma
4.2
(i), one has

{\mathcal{I}}(C({\mathcal{V}},L_{1},L_{2}))\simeq{\mathcal{I}}(M\rTo{\phi}M^{\vee}).

Now the assertion follows from Lemma
4.2
(iv) together with the well known fact
that
\operatorname{Pf}(\phi)\phi^{-1}
is regular (see e.g.,
[
14
, Eq. (10)]
).
∎

We need a simple observation that allows to study
\theta(L_{1},L_{2})
in the presence of a Lagrangian splitting

V=\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
V=\Lambda\oplus\Lambda^{\prime}
be a splitting of a symplectic bundle into Lagrangian subbundles, and let

L_{1},L_{2}\subset V
be Lagrangian subbundles. Assume that the pairs
(L_{1},L_{2})
,
(L_{1},\Lambda)
and
(L_{2},\Lambda)

are transversal, and let

\tau_{i}:\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda,\ i=1,2,

be symmetric (with respect to the duality
\Lambda^{\prime}\simeq\Lambda^{\vee}
) morphisms such that

L_{i}
is the graph of
\tau_{i}
.
Then under the identification
\operatorname{Ber}(\Lambda^{\prime})\simeq\operatorname{Ber}(\Lambda)^{-1}
,
we have equality of sections of
\operatorname{Ber}(L_{1})^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(L_{2})^{-1}
,

\theta(L_{1},L_{2})=\pm\theta(L_{1},\Lambda)\theta(L_{2},\Lambda)\operatorname{ber}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}).

Proof.

By the definition,
\pm\theta(L_{1},L_{2})\theta(L_{1},\Lambda)^{-1}\theta(L_{2},\Lambda)^{-1}
is the Berezinian
of the pairing on
\Lambda^{\prime}
given as the composition

\Lambda^{\prime}\otimes\Lambda^{\prime}\rTo{\alpha_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2}}L_{1}\otimes L_{2}\rTo{(\cdot,\cdot)}{\mathcal{O}},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the symplectic form on
V
, and
\alpha_{i}:\Lambda^{\prime}\to L_{i}
are isomorphisms given by

\alpha_{i}:\Lambda^{\prime}\to L_{i}:\lambda^{\prime}\mapsto\tau_{i}(\lambda^{\prime})+\lambda^{\prime}.

Using the fact that
\tau_{2}
is symmetric, we compute

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1}(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}),\alpha_{2}(\lambda^{\prime}_{2}))=(\tau_{1}(\lambda^{\prime}_{1})+\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\tau_{2}(\lambda^{\prime}_{2})+\lambda^{\prime}_{2})=(\tau_{1}(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}),\lambda^{\prime}_{2})-(\tau_{2}(\lambda^{\prime}_{2}),\lambda^{\prime}_{1})

\displaystyle=((\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}),\lambda^{\prime}_{2}).

This implies our formula.
∎

4.4.
Classical picture for theta-characteristic

We are inspired by the following classical isotropic intersection picture for theta-characteristics (see e.g.,

[
16
]
,
[
12
]
).

Let
C
be a curve with a theta-characteristic
{\mathcal{L}}
, and let
p\in C
be a point,
D
a formal punctured disk around
p
.
Then we have a natural symmetric pairing on
H^{0}(D,{\mathcal{L}})
given by

b(s,t)=\operatorname{Res}_{p}(st),

where we use the isomorphism
{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes 2}\simeq\omega_{C}
to view
st
is a
1
-form on
D
.
This induces a nondegenerate pairing on

V=H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(np)/{\mathcal{L}}(-np))

for any
n>0
, such that the subspace

L_{2}:=H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}/{\mathcal{L}}(-np))\subset V

is maximal isotropic.
Furthermore, for
n
large enough, the map
H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(np))\to V
is injective, and one can check that

L_{1}=H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(np))\subset V

is also maximal isotropic.
Now the complex
C^{\bullet}(V;L_{1},L_{2})=[L_{1}\to V/L_{2}]
can be identified with the complex

H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(np))\to H^{0}(C,{\mathcal{L}}(np)/{\mathcal{L}})

computing the cohomology
H^{*}(C,{\mathcal{L}})
.

This also makes sense for families
(C,{\mathcal{L}})/S
. If we further assume that generically
{\mathcal{L}}
has no nonzero sections
then Proposition
4.3
implies that locally we can find a quasi-isomorphism of
R\pi_{*}{\mathcal{L}}

with a complex of the form

M\rTo{\phi}M^{\vee}

on
S
, with
\phi^{*}=-\phi
. It follows that we can use the Pfaffian of
\phi
as a local equation of the locus where

{\mathcal{L}}
acquires nonzero sections.

Applying this to a universal family over the moduli space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\operatorname{bos}}^{+}
of curves with even theta-characteristics,
we get a canonical structure of an effective Cartier divisor on the locus of
(C,{\mathcal{L}})
with
h^{0}({\mathcal{L}})>0
.
Furthermore, the first order computation in
[
17
, Sec. 1]
implies that this divisor is smooth at all points

5.1.
Complex conjugation and the pairing

For a complex manifold
{\mathcal{X}}
we denote by
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
conjugate complex manifold.
In other words,
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}={\mathcal{X}}
as a
C^{\infty}
-manifold, but the holomorphic functions on
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
are antiholomorphic
functions on
{\mathcal{X}}
. For every local holomorphic function
f
on
{\mathcal{X}}
we can view
\overline{f}
as a holomorphic function on

\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
. It follows that for every holomorphic vector bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
over
{\mathcal{X}}
we have a holomorphic vector bundle

\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}
over
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
(defined by the complex conjugate transition matrices), so that we have
a
\C
-antilinear isomorphism

H^{0}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{V}})\rTo{\sim}H^{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{X}}},\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}):s\mapsto\overline{s}.

More generally, the correspondence
{\mathcal{X}}\mapsto\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
extends to a functor on the category of complex manifolds,
and for a morphism
f:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}
, we have

\overline{f_{*}{\mathcal{V}}}\simeq\overline{f}_{*}(\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}).

Given a holomorphic
n
-form
\eta
on
{\mathcal{X}}
, we can view
\overline{\eta}
as a holomorphic
n
-form on
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
.
Hence,
p_{1}^{*}\eta\wedge p_{2}^{*}\overline{\eta}
is a holomorphic
2n
-form on
{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
,
where
p_{1}:{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
and
p_{2}:{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}\to\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
are the projections.
Now let us consider the diagonal map
\Delta:{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
(which is neither holomorphic nor antiholomorphic).
Then we have

\eta\wedge\overline{\eta}=\Delta^{*}(p_{1}^{*}\eta\wedge p_{2}^{*}\overline{\eta}).

For complex supermanifolds, we cannot define a pointwise conjugation of functions, so we define a complex conjugate formally as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

For a complex supermanifold
{\mathcal{X}}=(|{\mathcal{X}}|,{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}})
of dimension
n|m
we define the
conjugate
complex supermanifold
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
of dimension
n|m
as

(|{\mathcal{X}}|,\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}})
, where
\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}}
is the same sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}
with the complex conjugate
\C
-algebra structure (obtained by composing the standard embedding

\C\to{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{X}}
with the complex conjugation on
\C
).

In local coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},

\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m})
holomorphic functions on
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
form an exterior
algebra in
\theta_{i}
over the ring of antiholomorphic functions in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
. This easily implies that
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
is still a complex supermanifold of dimension
n|m
.

If
\eta
is a holomorphic section of the holomorphic Berezinian on
{\mathcal{X}}

then
p_{1}^{*}\eta\wedge p_{2}^{*}\overline{\eta}
will be a holomorphic section of the holomorphic Berezinian on
{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
.
(Note that if we locally view
{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
as a real analytic supermanifold then

will be an integral form of codimension

, where the complex dimension of

is

.)

Now let
V
be a local system of
\R
-vector spaces of finite rank over
{\mathcal{X}}
with a bilinear pairing

(where we denote by
\R
the corresponding constant sheaf).
Assume that

is a morphism of holomorphic vector bundles on
{\mathcal{X}}
.
Then it induces a morphism
\overline{\iota}:\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}\hookrightarrow V\otimes_{\R}{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}}
of holomorphic vector bundles on
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
.
Hence, on
{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}
we get morphisms

p_{1}^{*}\iota:p_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{V}}\hookrightarrow p_{1}^{-1}V\otimes_{\R}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}},\ \ p_{2}^{*}\overline{\iota}:p_{2}^{*}\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}\hookrightarrow p_{2}^{-1}V\otimes_{\R}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathcal{X}}\times\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}}.

Now we observe that the restrictions of the local systems

7.

7.1.
Relation to a univalued Massey product

First, we observe that the Massey product (
6.5
) can be given as

\alpha\mapsto m_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},\alpha),

where we use the minimal
A_{\infty}
-structure obtained by the homological perturbation (this follows from

[

Next, using the fact that this
A_{\infty}
-structure can be chosen to be cyclic with respect to the Serre duality pairing,
we see that

\langle m_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},\alpha_{1}),\alpha_{2}\rangle=\pm\langle x_{1},m_{3}(x_{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\rangle,

for

,
\alpha_{i}\in H^{0}(\omega_{C})

Using Serre duality we can view the map

(see (

H^{0}(\omega_{C}^{\otimes 2})^{*}\simeq H^{1}(\omega_{C}^{-1})\to H^{0}(\omega_{C})^{*}\otimes H^{0}(\omega_{C})^{*},

dual to the multiplication map

H^{0}(\omega_{C})\otimes H^{0}(\omega_{C})\to H^{0}(\omega_{C}^{\otimes 2}).

Thus, the above cyclicity implies that the dual to the Massey product map (
6.5
)
can be identified with the map

K\to\operatorname{Hom}(H^{1}(L^{-1}),H^{0}(\omega\otimes L)):\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha^{\prime}_{i}\mapsto(x\mapsto\sum_{i}m_{3}(x,\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\prime}_{i})),

where

is the kernel of the multiplication map.

Next, applying the
A_{\infty}
-identity to the elements

\alpha,x,\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\otimes\alpha^{\prime}_{i}
, where
\alpha\in H^{0}(\omega_{C})
, we get

\alpha\cdot\sum_{i}m_{3}(x,\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\prime}_{i})=\sum_{i}m_{3}(\alpha,x,\alpha_{i})\cdot\alpha^{\prime}_{i}.

(7.1)

Note that if we know the left-hand side for all
\alpha\in H^{0}(\omega_{C})
, then this determines the element

\sum_{i}m_{3}(x,\alpha_{i},\alpha^{\prime}_{i})\in H^{0}(\omega_{C}\otimes L)

is injective for

(since

is generated by its global sections).

Thus, our Massey product is uniquely determined by the map

m_{3}:H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\otimes H^{1}(C,L^{-1})\otimes H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\to H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}\otimes L),

(7.2)

which is a univalued Massey product due to the assumption
H^{0}(C,L)=H^{1}(C,L)=0
.

7.2.
Calculation of the Massey product via the triangulated structure

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category,

\alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,B),\beta\in\operatorname{Hom}(B,C[1]),\gamma\in\operatorname{Hom}(C,D)

composable morphisms

A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}C[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma[1]}}{{\longrightarrow}}D[1]

such that
\beta\circ\alpha=0
and
\gamma[1]\circ\beta=0
. Then a
Massey product

\operatorname{MP}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,D)/(\gamma\circ\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)+\operatorname{Hom}(B,D)\circ\alpha)

is defined as follows. The map
\beta
is part of an exact triangle

C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\longrightarrow}}B\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}C[1].

With our assumptions on the compositions of
\alpha,\beta,\gamma

there exist morphisms
\tilde{\alpha}\colon A\to V
and

\tilde{\gamma}\colon V\to D
such that

and

\tilde{\gamma}\circ f=\gamma
. The Massey product is

\tilde{\gamma}\circ\tilde{\alpha}\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,D)
. Any two choices of

\tilde{\alpha}
differ by

with
\sigma\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)
;
the corresponding Massey products differ by
\gamma\circ\sigma
. Similarly, changing the choice of
\tilde{\gamma}
changes the
Massey product by
\tau\circ\alpha
for some
\tau\in\operatorname{Hom}(B,D)
. Thus,
the Massey product is a well-defined element of the quotient of

\operatorname{Hom}(A,D)
by these ambiguities. A particularly simple situation,
which occurs in our case, is when
\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)
and
\operatorname{Hom}(B,D)

vanish. In this case, the compositions always vanish and there is no
ambiguity: the Massey product is a well-defined map

\operatorname{MP}\colon\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(B,C[1])\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(C,D)\to\operatorname{Hom}(A,D).

Now we can apply the above recipe to calculate the Massey product
(

, where

. Note that the compatibility of the Massey products

to

(see
[

for details).
Using this compatibility, we can rewrite (

where

are the lifts of

in the diagram

H^{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{C}(p_{1}+\ldots+p_{g}+(2g-3)p_{0}))
:

(G_{j}),j=1,\ldots,g-1,\ \ (\frac{G_{j}x}{F}),j=1,\ldots,g-1,

where the first
g-1
elements are symmetric with respect to
\tau
, and the last
g-1
elements are antisymmetric.
Thus, we have

\varphi_{\alpha}(G_{j}\frac{dt}{x})=e_{j}^{+}\cdot(\frac{dt}{dx}|_{D}),

\varphi_{\alpha}(\frac{G_{j}x}{F}\cdot\frac{dt}{x})=\frac{x_{j}}{F(b_{j})}e_{j}^{-}\cdot(\frac{dt}{dx}|_{D}).

The basis of
H^{0}(\omega_{C})
is given by
t^{m}\frac{dt}{x}
,
m=0,\ldots,g-1
.
Now we can apply the formula of Corollary
7.4
to some element

\xi=\sum_{i}f_{i}(t)\otimes g_{i}(t)\frac{dt^{\otimes 2}}{x^{2}}\in K\subset H^{0}(\omega_{C})^{\otimes 2},

where
\sum_{i}f_{i}g_{i}=0
.
We have

-m_{3}(\varphi^{\vee}_{\alpha}(e_{j}^{\pm})\otimes\xi)=\frac{\sum_{i}\varphi^{-1}_{\alpha}(e_{j}^{\pm}f_{i}(b_{j})dt/x)g_{i}(t)}{G}.

Hence,

-m_{3}(\varphi^{\vee}_{\alpha}(e_{j}^{+})\otimes\xi)=\frac{\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}G_{j}}{G}\cdot\frac{dt}{x}=\frac{\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}}{G^{\prime}(b_{j})(t-b_{j})}\cdot\frac{dt}{x},

-m_{3}(\varphi^{\vee}_{\alpha}(e_{j}^{-})\otimes\xi)=\frac{F(b_{j})\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}G_{j}x}{x_{j}FG}\cdot\frac{dt}{x}=\frac{F(b_{j})\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}x}{x_{j}G^{\prime}(b_{j})(t-b_{j})F}\cdot\frac{dt}{x},

where we used the identity
(t-b_{j})G_{j}=G/G^{\prime}(b_{j})
.

For applications to the second variation of the superperiod map,
we need to compute the skew-symmetrization of the corresponding maps

m_{3}(?\otimes\xi):H^{1}(L^{-1})\to H^{0}(\omega_{C}\otimes L).

Equivalently, we have to skew-symmetrize the maps

A_{\xi}=\varphi_{\alpha}m_{3}(\varphi^{\vee}_{\alpha}(?)\otimes\xi):V\to V\otimes\chi

with respect to the pairing (
7.6
).

Restricting the right-hand sides of the formulas above to
D
, we get

-A_{\xi}(e_{j}^{+})=\frac{\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g^{\prime}_{i}(b_{j})}{G^{\prime}(b_{j})}e_{j}^{+}+\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}\frac{\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}(b_{j^{\prime}})}{G^{\prime}(b_{j})(b_{j^{\prime}}-b_{j})}e_{j^{\prime}}^{+},

-A_{\xi}(e_{j}^{-})=\frac{\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g^{\prime}_{i}(b_{j})}{G^{\prime}(b_{j})}e_{j}^{-}+\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}\frac{x_{j^{\prime}}F(b_{j})\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}(b_{j^{\prime}})}{x_{j}F(b_{j^{\prime}})G^{\prime}(b_{j})(b_{j^{\prime}}-b_{j})}e_{j^{\prime}}^{-},

Thus,

-\langle e_{k}^{+},A_{\xi}^{*}(e_{j}^{-})\rangle=-\langle A_{\xi}(e_{k}^{+}),e_{j}^{-}\rangle=\begin{cases}\frac{2F(b_{j})\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g^{\prime}_{i}(b_{j})}{x_{j}G^{\prime}(b_{j})^{2}}&j=k\\
\frac{2F(b_{j})\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{k})g_{i}(b_{j})}{x_{j}G^{\prime}(b_{j})G^{\prime}(b_{k})(b_{j}-b_{k})}&j\neq k\end{cases},

and so

-A_{\xi}^{*}(e_{j}^{-})=\frac{\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g^{\prime}_{i}(b_{j})}{G^{\prime}(b_{j})}e_{j}^{-}+\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{x_{k}F(b_{j})\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{k})g_{i}(b_{j})}{x_{j}F(b_{k})G^{\prime}(b_{j})(b_{j}-b_{k})}e_{k}^{-},

(A_{\xi}-A_{\xi}^{*})(e_{j}^{-})=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{x_{k}F(b_{j})}{x_{j}F(b_{k})G^{\prime}(b_{j})(b_{j}-b_{k})}\cdot\bigl(\sum_{i}f_{i}(b_{j})g_{i}(b_{k})+g_{i}(b_{j})f_{i}(b_{k})\bigr)e_{k}^{-}.

(7.7)

Note that the restriction of
A_{\xi}-A_{\xi}^{*}
to
V^{+}
is determined from its restriction to
V^{-}
,
due to duality between
V^{+}
and
V^{-}
.

8.
Application to super-Schottky ideal

8.1.
Estimate on generic rank of the second variation

First, let
C
be a hyperelliptic curve. The results of
[
19
]
imply that certain quadratic
relations between differentials on
C
can be deformed away from the hyperelliptic locus.
We keep the notations of the previous section.

Proposition 8.1
.

For any
(g-3)
-tuple of degree
g-3
polynomials
(H_{1}(t),\ldots,H_{g-3}(t))
, the quadratic relation in
S^{2}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
,

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{g-3}\bigl(H_{i}\frac{dt}{x}\otimes H_{i}t^{2}\frac{dt}{x}+H_{i}t^{2}\frac{dt}{x}\otimes H_{i}\frac{dt}{x}-2H_{i}t\frac{dt}{x}\otimes H_{i}t\frac{dt}{x}\bigr)

(8.1)

can be deformed away from the hyperelliptic locus.

Proof.

According to
[
19
, Lem. 1.1.4]
, the quadratic map

Q:H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}(g-3))\to S^{2}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}):f(t)\mapsto f\frac{dt}{x}\otimes ft^{2}\frac{dt}{x}+ft^{2}\frac{dt}{x}\otimes f\frac{dt}{x}-2ft\frac{dt}{x}\otimes ft\frac{dt}{x}

induces an isomorphism of the space
S^{2}H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}(g-3))
with the space
of quadratic relations between the differentials on
C
. Now, by
[
19
, Thm. 2.2.2]
,
any quadratic relation corresponding to a degenerate quadratic form on
H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},{\mathcal{O}}(g-3))^{*}

can be deformed away from the hyperelliptic locus. This immediately implies our assertion.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

(i) Assume that
g\geq 5
is odd. There exists a non-empty open locus
{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, such that
for
C\in{\mathcal{U}}
and a theta-characteristic
L
over
C
such that
h^{0}(L)=0
, there exists
a quadratic relation between the differentials,
\xi\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})^{\otimes 2}
, such that
the skew-symmetrization of
m_{3}(?,\xi)\in{\bigwedge}^{2}H^{0}(\omega_{C}\otimes L)
is nondegenerate.

(ii) In the case
g\geq 4
is even, the statement is that for a generic
C
there exists
\xi
such that the
skew-symmetrization of
m_{3}(?,\xi)
has rank
\geq 2g-4
.

Proof.

(i) Let us compute the skew-symmetric form associated with the quadratic relation (
8.1
),
which we write in the form
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{g-3}\xi_{i}
, where
\xi_{i}
depends on
H_{i}
as in the right-hand
side of (
8.1
).
We can calculate the matrices of the operators

B(i):=(A_{\xi_{i}}-A^{*}_{\xi_{i}})|_{V^{-}}

using (
7.7
).
Let us consider the rescaled bases of
V^{-}
,

d_{j}:=\frac{x_{j}G^{\prime}(b_{j})}{F(b_{j})}e_{j}^{-},\ \ d^{\prime}_{j}=\frac{x_{j}}{F(b_{j})}e_{j}^{-},j=1,\ldots,g-1.

Then the coefficient of
d^{\prime}_{k}
in
B(\omega_{i},\omega_{j},\omega_{k})(d_{j})
is given by

B(i)_{qp}=\frac{1}{b_{p}-b_{q}}\bigl(H_{i}(b_{p})H_{i}(b_{q})b_{q}^{2}+H_{i}(b_{q})H_{i}(b_{p})b_{p}^{2}-2H_{i}(b_{p})H_{i}(b_{q})b_{p}b_{q}\bigr)=H_{i}(b_{p})H_{i}(b_{q})(b_{p}-b_{q}).

We have
B(i)^{t}=-B(i)
and

\operatorname{im}(B(i))=\langle\sum_{k}H_{i}(b_{k})e_{k},\sum_{k}b_{k}H_{i}(b_{k})e_{k}\rangle.

Now, assuming that
g
is odd, let us consider the relation
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{(g-1)/2}\xi_{i}
, where

H_{1}=1,H_{2}=t^{2},H_{3}=t^{4},\ldots,H_{\frac{g-1}{2}}=t^{g-3}.

Then the vectors spanning images of
B(i)
,
i=1,\ldots,(g-1)/2
, are evaluations of the monomials
1,t,\ldots,t^{g-2}

at
b_{1},\ldots,b_{g-1}
, so they are linearly independent.
This implies that
B=B_{1}+\ldots+B_{(g-1)/2}
is nondegenerate. Hence,
A_{\xi}-A^{*}_{\xi}
is nondegenerate.

Since
(g-1)/2\leq g-3
, by Proposition
8.1
, we can find a family of curves with quadratic relation
\xi
specializing to
the hyperelliptic curve with the above quadratic relation. Furthermore, we can extend it to a family of curves with
theta-characteristics. Thus, we will obtain a non-hyperelliptic curve
(C,L)
with a theta-characteristic
L
such that

h^{0}(L)=0
, and a quadratic relation
\xi
such that the skew-symmetrization of
m_{3}(?,\xi)
is nondegenerate.

It remains to recall a well known fact that for non-hyperelliptic curves the product map

\kappa:S^{2}H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})\to H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}^{\otimes 2})

is surjective, so that spaces
K=\ker(\kappa)
form a vector bundle over the non-hyperelliptic locus in
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.
Thus, a quadratic relation
\xi
with the above property can be found over an open locus in
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
.

(ii) The proof is similar to that of (i), except that we consider
\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{(g-2)/2}\xi_{i}
, where

H_{1}=1,H_{2}=t^{2},\ldots,H_{\frac{g-2}{2}}=t^{g-4}.

Then the matrix
B=B_{1}+\ldots+B_{(g-2)/2}
has rank
g-2
. Hence
A_{\xi}-A^{*}_{\xi}
has rank
2g-4
.
∎

8.2.
Super-Schottky ideal

Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}^{+}\to{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
be the covering of
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}={\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{g}
corresponding to a choice of a symplectic
basis in
H^{1}(C,\Z)
, and let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}\subset\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}^{+}
be the preimage of the open substack

{\mathcal{U}}\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{+}
corresponding to theta-characteristics with trivial
H^{0}
.
Then we have a well defined
superperiod map
,

\operatorname{per}:\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}\to LG_{2g},

where
LG_{2g}
is the Lagrangian Grassmannian of the
2g
-dimensional symplectic vector space over
\C
,
corresponding to the Lagrangian subbundle
\pi_{*}\omega_{X/\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}}
in the trivialized symplectic bundle

R^{1}\pi_{*}\C_{X/\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}}
, where
X\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{U}}}
is the universal supercurve.

We define the
super-Schottky ideal

{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}
on
LG_{2g}
as
the ideal defining the schematic image of
\operatorname{per}
. In other words, a
local function
f
belongs to
{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}
if and only if
\operatorname{per}^{*}f=0
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}\subset LG_{2g}
be the usual Schottky ideal corresponding
to the image of the usual period map
\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}\to LG_{2g}
(where

\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}_{g}
is the covering of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
corresponding to a choice of a
symplectic basis in cohomology). It is clear that

{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}
. Furthermore, for any
f\in{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}
,
the pull-back
\operatorname{per}^{*}f
is even, so it belongs to the square of the
nilradical in the structure sheaf of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}^{+}
. Since there are

2g-2
odd variables on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{S}}}^{+}
, it follows that

{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}^{g}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
d
be the minimal number such that

{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}^{d}\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{s-Sch}
. Then for
g
odd, we have
d=g
,
while for
g
even we have
d\geq g-1
.

Proof.

At a generic point of the classical Schottky locus, for any conormal
vector
\xi
we can find a function
f\in{\mathcal{I}}_{Sch}
with
\xi
as
its leading part. Now Theorem
8.2
implies that

\operatorname{per}^{*}f^{g-1}\neq 0
for odd
g
, while
\operatorname{per}^{*}f^{g-2}\neq 0
for
even
g
.
∎

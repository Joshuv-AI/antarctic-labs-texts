On Direct Integral Expansion for Periodic Block-Operator Jacobi Matrices and Applications
Leonid GOLINSKII † and Anton KUTSENKO ‡

Abstract
We construct a functional model (direct integral expansion) and study the spectra of certain periodic block-operator Jacobi matrices, in particular, of general 2D partial difference operators of the second order. We obtain the upper bound, optimal in a sense, for the Lebesgue measure of their spectra. The examples of the operators for which there are several gaps in the spectrum are given.

Introduction

Consider a block-operator Jacobi matrix on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{F}}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle(Ju)_{q}:=A_{q-1}u_{q-1}+B_{q}u_{q}+A_{q}^{*}u_{q+1},\qquad B_{q}=B_{q}^{*},\qquad q\in{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad u_{q}\in{\mathcal{H}},

(1.1)

where the blocks
\{A_{q},B_{q}\}
are bounded linear operators on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Under the standard assumption
\sup\limits_{q\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(\|A_{q}\|+\|B_{q}\|)<\infty
on the entries, the matrix
J
generates a bounded
and self-adjoint operator
J
on
{\mathcal{F}}
.

We are primarily interested in the case when
{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, and the blocks
B_{q}
,
A_{q}
are themselves Hermitian (not necessarily real symmetric) 1D Jacobi matrices

\displaystyle(Ju)_{q}:=A_{q-1}u_{q-1}+B_{q}u_{q}+A_{q}u_{q+1},\qquad q\in{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad u_{q}=(u_{q}(l))_{l}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}).

(1.2)

The block matrices of this type are known as Jacobi-block-Jacobi matrices. So,

\displaystyle A_{q}=J\bigl(\{a_{q,r}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}},\{\alpha_{q,r}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigr),\qquad B_{q}=J\bigl(\{c_{q,r}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}},\{b_{q,r}\}_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigr),

(1.3)

with real entries
\{a_{q,r}\}

(\{c_{q,r}\})
along the main diagonal, and complex entries
\{\alpha_{q,r}\}

(\{b_{q,r}\})
and their conjugates along the diagonals above and below the main one, respectively.

Such block-operator model arises when we deal with 2D partial difference operators
L
of the second order of the form

\displaystyle(L\widetilde{u})_{ik}:=a_{i-1,k}u_{i-1,k}+a_{i,k}u_{i+1,k}+b_{i,k-1}u_{i,k-1}+b_{i,k}u_{i,k+1}+c_{i,k}u_{i,k},

(1.4)

on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)
. A natural isometry
U_{0}
between
\ell^{2}\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)
and
{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}=(u_{i,k})_{i,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\rightarrow u=U_{0}\widetilde{u}=(u_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\colon\ u_{j}=(\dots,u_{j,-1},u_{j,0},u_{j,1},\dots)^{\prime}

transforms the operator
L
in (
1.4
) into
J:=U_{0}LU_{0}^{-1}
on
{\mathcal{F}}
, where

\displaystyle(Ju)_{j}=A_{j-1}u_{j-1}+B_{j}u_{j}+A_{j}u_{j+1},\qquad j\in{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle A_{j}:=\diag(a_{j,k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}},\qquad B_{j}:=J\bigl(\{c_{j,k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}},\{b_{j,k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigr),

(1.5)

see, e.g.,
[
1
, Section VII.3]
. This is a special case of the model operator
J
(
1.2
) with
\alpha_{j,k}=0
.
In particular, for 2D discrete Schrödinger operators
H
(
a_{j,k}=b_{j,k}=1
) we have

\displaystyle(H\widetilde{u})_{i,k}:=u_{i-1,k}+u_{i+1,k}+u_{i,k-1}+u_{i,k+1}+c_{i,k}u_{i,k},

(1.6)

so

\displaystyle A_{j}=I,\qquad B_{j}=J\bigl(\{c_{j,k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}},\{1\}\bigr),

1D discrete Schrödinger operators.

Let
p_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j=1,2
. A sequence of complex numbers
\{w_{q,r}\}_{q,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is called
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic if

\displaystyle w_{q+k_{1}p_{1},r+k_{2}p_{2}}=w_{q,r},\qquad\forall q,r,k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

The operator
J
in (
1.2
) is called
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic if
\{a_{q,r}\}
,
\{\alpha_{q,r}\}
,
\{c_{q,r}\}
, and
\{b_{q,r}\}
,
q,r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic. Equivalently,

1)

all the blocks
A_{q}
,
B_{q}
are
p_{2}
-periodic 1D Jacobi matrices;

2)

J
is block periodic with period
p_{1}
:
A_{q+p_{1}}=A_{q}
,
B_{q+p_{1}}=B_{q}
.

We say that the partial difference operator
L
(
1.4
) is
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic, if all the coefficients are
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic,
or equivalently,
J
(
1.5
) is
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic.

In Section
2
we recall a direct integral expansion (a functional model) for the
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic operator

J
in (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) and establish the banded structure of its spectrum
\sigma(J)
. In Section
3
we estimate the
Lebesgue measure of
\sigma(J)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p_{1},p_{2}\geq 3
. The Lebesgue measure of the spectrum for the periodic operator
J
in (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) admits the upper bound

\displaystyle|\sigma(J)|\leq\min_{(m,n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}R_{m,n},

(1.7)

where

\displaystyle R_{m,n}:=4\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}}(|b_{j,n}|+2|\alpha_{j,n}|)+4\sum_{k=1}^{p_{2}}(|a_{m,k}|+2|\alpha_{m,k}|)-8|\alpha_{m,n}|.

(1.8)

The sequence
\{R_{m,n}\}
is
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic, so minimum in (
1.7
) is actually taken over the finite set of indices

m=1,\dots,p_{1}
and
n=1,\dots,p_{2}
.

The similar result holds for
\min(p_{1},p_{2})=2
, see Remark
3.1
below.

Note that there is a simple general bound for
|\sigma(J)|
(which has nothing to do with periodicity) based on the fact that
J
is
a three-diagonal block-matrix

\displaystyle|\sigma(J)|\leq 2\|J\|\leq 2(2\max_{n}\|A_{n}\|+\max_{n}\|B_{n}\|)

\displaystyle\hphantom{|\sigma(J)|}{}\leq\max_{n}(8\max_{m}|\alpha_{n,m}|+4\max_{m}|a_{n,m}|)+\max_{n}(4\max_{m}|b_{n,m}|+2\max_{m}|c_{n,m}|).

(1.9)

The point is that certain parameters, such as
c_{n,m}
, which appear in (
1.9
), do not enter (
1.7
). So, once some values of
c_{n,m}

are large enough, bound (
1.7
) is better that (
1.9
). On the other hand, (
1.7
) contains sums of the
entries compared to (
1.9
), which does not.

There is another upper bound for the length of the spectrum, based on Gershgorin’s theorem, which is discussed in Remark
3.3
.

For the 1D scalar,
p
-periodic Jacobi operator
J
the estimates for the spectrum

\displaystyle|\sigma(J)|\leq 4(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{p})^{1/p}

were obtained in
[
7
,
9
]
. Here
a_{n}
are the off-diagonal entries of
J
. Recently the second author
[
11
]
improved this result to

\displaystyle|\sigma(J)|\leq 4\min_{n}a_{n}.

We see that both bounds do not depend on the diagonal entries, so the lack of
c_{m,n}
in (
1.7
) looks reasonable.

Corollary 1.2
.

For a
2D
periodic, partial difference operators of the second order
L
in (
1.4
) the spectral estimate is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|\sigma(L)|\leq\min_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}}|b_{j,n}|+\min_{m}\sum_{k=1}^{p_{2}}|a_{m,k}|.

In particular,
|\sigma(H)|\leq 4(p_{1}+p_{2})
for
2D
periodic, discrete Schrödinger operators
H
(
1.6
).

The estimate
|\sigma(H)|\leq 4(p_{1}+p_{2})
was previously obtained in
[
10
]
.

We complete the paper with examples of 2D discrete Schrödinger operators with explicitly computed spectra, and an operator, which has a maximal number of gaps in its spectrum.

3
Spectral estimates for periodic block-Jacobi operators

In this section we are aimed at proving Theorem
1.1
. By Corollary
2.3
,

\displaystyle\sigma(J)=\sigma(M({\mathcal{S}}))=\bigcup_{k=1}^{p}\Lambda_{k},\qquad\Lambda_{k}=[l_{k},r_{k}]

(3.1)

are the closed intervals, swept by the
k
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})
, arranged in the non-increasing order, as the pair

(x_{1},x_{2})
runs over
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

We are looking for two constant matrices
{\mathcal{S}}_{\pm}
, i.e., independent of
(x_{1},x_{2})
, so that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{-}\leq{\mathcal{S}}\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{+}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lambda_{k}^{-}\leq\lambda_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\lambda_{k}^{+},

where

{\mathcal{S}}_{\pm}

\displaystyle|\sigma(M({\mathcal{S}}))|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{p}(r_{k}-l_{k})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{p}(\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k}^{-})=\tr({\mathcal{S}}_{+}-{\mathcal{S}}_{-}).

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{1}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{n,1}&\alpha_{n,1}&&&&\\

&\overline{\alpha}_{n,2}&a_{n,3}&\alpha_{n,3}&&\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\

so

(3.5)

As the next step toward (

\displaystyle A=A_{+}-A_{-},\qquad|A|=A_{+}+A_{-},\qquad A_{\pm}:=\pm P_{\pm}A\geq 0.

It is clear from this definition that

\displaystyle-|A|\leq A\leq|A|,\qquad-\sum_{j=1}^{N}|A_{j}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}A_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}|A_{j}|.

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\pm}:={\mathcal{S}}_{1}\pm D,\qquad D:=|{\mathcal{S}}_{3}|+|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime}|+|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime\prime}|+|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime\prime\prime}|+\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}-1}|E_{j}|,

\displaystyle|\sigma(J)|=|\sigma(M({\mathcal{S}}))|\leq 2\tr D.

The value on the right side of (

\displaystyle\tr D:=\tr|{\mathcal{S}}_{3}|+\tr|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime}|+\tr|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime\prime}|+\tr|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime\prime\prime}|+\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}-1}\tr|E_{j}|.

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{S}}_{3}|=\diag\big(\big|{\mathcal{B}}_{1}^{2}\big|,\dots,\big|{\mathcal{B}}_{p_{1}}^{2}\big|\big),\qquad\big|{\mathcal{B}}_{j}^{2}\big|=\diag(|b_{j,p_{2}}|,0,\dots,0,|b_{j,p_{2}}|);

\displaystyle\tr|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime}|=2\tr|G_{1}|=2\sum_{i=1}^{p_{2}}|\alpha_{p_{1},i}|,\qquad\tr|{\mathcal{S}}_{4}^{\prime\prime}|=2\tr|G_{1}^{*}|=2\sum_{i=1}^{p_{2}}|\alpha_{p_{1},i}|.

\displaystyle|E_{j}|=\diag\big(0,\dots,0,\big|{\mathcal{A}}_{j}^{2}\big|,\big|{\mathcal{A}}_{j}^{2}\big|,0,\dots,0\big),

\displaystyle\tr|E_{j}|=2\tr|{\mathcal{A}}_{j}^{2}|=4|\alpha_{j,p_{2}}|,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}-1}\tr|E_{j}|=4\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}-1}|\alpha_{j,p_{2}}|.

A combination of the above equalities gives

\displaystyle\tr|D|=2\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}}\bigl(|b_{j,p_{2}}|+2|\alpha_{j,p_{2}}|\bigr)+2\sum_{i=1}^{p_{2}}\bigl(|a_{p_{1},i}|+2|\alpha_{p_{1},i}|\bigr)-4|\alpha_{p_{1},p_{2}}|,

and hence

\displaystyle|\sigma(J)|\leq 2\tr D=4\sum_{j=1}^{p_{1}}\bigl(|b_{j,p_{2}}|+2|\alpha_{j,p_{2}}|\bigr)+4\sum_{i=1}^{p_{2}}\bigl(|a_{p_{1},i}|+2|\alpha_{p_{1},i}|\bigr)-8|\alpha_{p_{1},p_{2}}|.

Example 3.4

4
Examples of explicitly computed spectra

The discrete version of the famous Bethe–Sommerfeld conjecture concerns the structure of the spectrum of periodic, discrete Schrödinger operators
H
(
1.6
) (and their multidimensional analogues). It claims that for small enough potentials
\{c_{ik}\}
such spectrum is a union of at most two closed intervals, with the gap open at the zero energy. Moreover, the spectrum is a single interval as long as at least one number
p_{1}
,
p_{2}

is odd. The result was proved for
d=2
in
[
2
]
, with a partial case for coprime periods in
[
8
]
, and for an arbitrary dimension
d\geq 2
in
[
5
]
. This result contrasts strongly with the one-dimensional case, wherein a generic
p
-periodic operator has the spectrum with
p-1
gaps open.

It turns out that Corollary
2.3
enables one to find the spectra for certain
(2,2)
-periodic discrete Schrödinger operators
H
(
1.6
), with
not necessarily small potentials
. Indeed, by Remark
2.2
, the symbol is now

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{B}}_{1}&\tau(x_{1})I_{2}\\
\tau(-x_{1})I_{2}&{\mathcal{B}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}

with
\tau(x):=1+{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}c_{11}&\tau(x_{2})\\
\tau(-x_{2})&c_{12}\end{bmatrix},\qquad{\mathcal{B}}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}c_{21}&\tau(x_{2})\\
\tau(-x_{2})&c_{22}\end{bmatrix}.

The characteristic polynomial of the symbol is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\lambda)=\det\bigl({\mathcal{S}}(x_{1},x_{2})-\lambda I_{4}\bigr)=\begin{vmatrix}{\mathcal{B}}_{1}-\lambda I_{2}&\tau(x_{1})I_{2}\\
\tau(-x_{1})I_{2}&{\mathcal{B}}_{2}-\lambda I_{2}\end{vmatrix}.

To compute this determinant we apply the Schur formula, which reduces determinants of order
2n
to ones of order
n
(see, e.g.,
[
4
, Section II.5]
). Precisely,
if
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
A_{3}
,
A_{4}
are
n\times n
matrices, and
A_{1}A_{3}=A_{3}A_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}\end{vmatrix}=\det(A_{1}A_{4}-A_{3}A_{2}).

Hence,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\lambda)=\det D(\lambda),\qquad D(\lambda)=\bigl(({\mathcal{B}}_{1}-\lambda I_{2})({\mathcal{B}}_{2}-\lambda I_{2})-|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}I_{2}\bigr).

(4.1)

Example 4.1
.

Let

\displaystyle c_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}c,\qquad i,j=1,2,\qquad c>0.

We find explicitly the spectrum of the corresponding operator
H
(
1.6
):

\displaystyle\sigma(H)=\big[{-}\sqrt{c^{2}+16},-c\big]\cup\big[c,\sqrt{c^{2}+16}\big].

Indeed,

\displaystyle({\mathcal{B}}_{1}-\lambda I_{2})({\mathcal{B}}_{2}-\lambda I_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}c-\lambda&\tau(x_{2})\\
\tau(-x_{2})&-c-\lambda\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-c-\lambda&\tau(x_{2})\\
\tau(-x_{2})&c-\lambda\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\hphantom{({\mathcal{B}}_1-\lambda I_2)({\mathcal{B}}_2-\lambda I_2)}{}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda^{2}-c^{2}+|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}&2\tau(x_{2})(c-\lambda)\\
-2\tau(-x_{2})(c+\lambda)&\lambda^{2}-c^{2}+|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}\end{bmatrix},

and, by (
4.1
),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\big(\lambda^{2}-c^{2}+|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}-|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}\big)^{2}-4|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}\big(\lambda^{2}-c^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\zeta+|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}-|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}\big)^{2}-4|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}\zeta

\displaystyle=\big(\zeta-|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}-|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}\big)^{2}-4|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}|\tau(x_{1})|^{2},\qquad\zeta=\lambda^{2}-c^{2}.

So, the eigenvalues of the symbol are

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sqrt{c^{2}+(|\tau(x_{2})|+|\tau(x_{1})|)^{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sqrt{c^{2}+(|\tau(x_{2})|-|\tau(x_{1})|)^{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}(x_{1},x_{2})=-\lambda_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}),\qquad\lambda_{4}(x_{1},x_{2})=-\lambda_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}).

The spectral bands (
3.1
) are

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=\big[c,\sqrt{c^{2}+16}\big],\qquad\Lambda_{2}=\big[c,\sqrt{c^{2}+4}\big],\qquad\Lambda_{3}=-\Lambda_{2},\qquad\Lambda_{4}=-\Lambda_{1},

as claimed.

Remark 4.2
.

The latter example is a key one in
[
8
]
, wherein it is shown that there is a gap in the spectrum for all
c>0
. For the similar computation of the spectrum for various lattices see
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Example 4.3
.

Consider now a two-parameter family of
(2,2)
-periodic Schrödinger operators with

\displaystyle c_{11}=-c_{22}=:c_{1},\qquad c_{12}=-c_{21}=:c_{2},\qquad c_{1},c_{2}>0.

Denote
\beta(x_{1},x_{2}):=|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}-|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}
, so for
D
in (
4.1
) one has

\displaystyle D(\lambda)=\begin{bmatrix}(\lambda-c_{1})(\lambda+c_{2})+\beta(x_{1},x_{2})&-2\lambda\tau(x_{2})\\
-2\lambda\tau(-x_{2})&(\lambda-c_{2})(\lambda+c_{1})+\beta(x_{1},x_{2})\end{bmatrix}

and hence

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(\lambda)=\big(\lambda^{2}-c_{1}^{2}\big)\big(\lambda^{2}-c_{2}^{2}\big)+2\beta(x_{1},x_{2})\big(\lambda^{2}-c_{1}c_{2}\big)+\beta^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})-4\lambda^{2}|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}.

The roots of this biquadratic polynomial can be found explicitly. For
z=\lambda^{2}
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}(z)={\mathcal{D}}(\lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{2}-z\big(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}+4|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}-2\beta(x_{1},x_{2})\big)+(\beta(x_{1},x_{2})-c_{1}c_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=z^{2}-z\big(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}+2|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}+2|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}\big)+(\beta(x_{1},x_{2})-c_{1}c_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=z^{2}-2Az+B,

where

\displaystyle A:=\frac{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}{2}+|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}+|\tau(x_{2})|^{2},\qquad B:=(\beta(x_{1},x_{2})-c_{1}c_{2})^{2}\geq 0.

The roots
z_{\pm}
of
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
are

\displaystyle z_{\pm}(x_{1},x_{2})=A\pm\sqrt{D},

\displaystyle D=A^{2}-B=\left[\frac{(c_{1}-c_{2})^{2}}{2}+2|\tau(x_{2})|^{2}\right]\left[\frac{(c_{1}+c_{2})^{2}}{2}+2|\tau(x_{1})|^{2}\right]\geq 0.

Hence,
0\leq z_{-}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq z_{+}(x_{1},x_{2})
for each
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=-\lambda_{4}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sqrt{z_{+}(x_{1},x_{2})},\qquad\lambda_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=-\lambda_{3}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sqrt{z_{-}(x_{1},x_{2})}.

We begin with the interior gap at the origin. Clearly, this gap is open if and only if

\displaystyle l_{2}=\min_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}z_{-}(x_{1},x_{2})>0.

The latter is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}B(x_{1},x_{2})=\min_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\bigl(2(\cos x_{1}-\cos x_{2})-c_{1}c_{2}\bigr)^{2}>0.

So, the interior gap at the origin is open if and only if
c_{1}c_{2}>4
.

There are another two gaps (possibly closed), symmetric with respect to the origin, which we refer to as “exterior gaps”.
Such gaps are open if and only if

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}z_{-}(x_{1},x_{2})<\min_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}z_{+}(x_{1},x_{2}).

(4.2)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}z_{+}(x_{1},x_{2})=z_{+}(\pi,\pi)=\frac{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}{2}+\frac{|c_{1}-c_{2}|}{2}=\max\big(c_{1}^{2},c_{2}^{2}\big).

On the other hand, it is a matter of elementary (though lengthy) calculus to check that

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}z_{-}(x_{1},x_{2})=z_{-}(0,\pi)=\frac{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}{2}+4-\frac{|c_{1}-c_{2}|}{2}\sqrt{(c_{1}+c_{2})^{2}+16}.

So, (
4.2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}{2}+4-\frac{|c_{1}-c_{2}|}{2}\sqrt{(c_{1}+c_{2})^{2}+16}<\max\big(c_{1}^{2},c_{2}^{2}\big).

A simple sufficient condition for the exterior gaps to be open is

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}>c_{2}^{2}+8.

(4.3)

To summarize, the conditions (
4.3
) and
c_{1}c_{2}>4
ensure the existence of all three gaps, under conditions (
4.3
) and
c_{1}c_{2}\leq 4

the exterior gaps are open. When
c_{1}=c_{2}+\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon>0
small enough, and
c_{2}>2
only interior gap is open.

We complete with a discrete Schrödinger operator (
1.6
), which has maximal number of gaps in its spectrum.

Example 4.4
.

Let
H
be a
(p_{1},p_{2})
-periodic, discrete Schrödinger operator with the potential

\displaystyle c_{i,j}=\frac{(i-1)p_{2}+j}{\varepsilon},\qquad i=1,\dots,p_{1},\qquad j=1,\dots,p_{2},\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{8}.

The symbol is now

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{B}}_{1}&I_{p_{2}}&&&{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{1}}I_{p_{2}}\\
I_{p_{2}}&{\mathcal{B}}_{2}&I_{p_{2}}&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&I_{p_{2}}&{\mathcal{B}}_{p_{1}-1}&I_{p_{2}}\\
{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{1}}I_{p_{2}}&&&I_{p_{2}}&{\mathcal{B}}_{p_{1}}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(x_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{(n-1)p_{2}+1}{\varepsilon}&1&&&{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{2}}\\
1&\frac{(n-1)p_{2}+2}{\varepsilon}&1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&\frac{np_{2}-1}{\varepsilon}&1\\
{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x_{2}}&&&1&\frac{np_{2}}{\varepsilon}\end{bmatrix},\qquad n=1,2,\dots,p_{1}.

The Gershgorin intervals (see Remark
3.3
) take the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{k}=\left[\frac{k}{\varepsilon}-l,\frac{k}{\varepsilon}+l\right],\qquad k=1,2,\dots,p,\qquad l=3\ {\rm or}\ 4.

In any event, such intervals are disjoint for
\varepsilon<1/8
, and, by Gershgorin’s theorem, each
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
contains exactly one eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(x_{1},x_{2})
of the symbol for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. So, the spectral bands
\Lambda_{k}\subset{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
and, by Corollary
2.3
, there are
p-1
gaps in the spectrum of
H
, as claimed.

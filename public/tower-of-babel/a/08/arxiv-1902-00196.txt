Around finite second-order coherence spaces
Lê Thành Dũng Nguyễn

Abstract
Many applications of denotational semantics, such as higher-order model checking or the complexity of normalization, rely on finite semantics for monomorphic type systems. We exhibit such a finite semantics for a polymorphic purely linear language: more precisely, we show that in Girard’s semantics of second-order linear logic using coherence spaces and normal functors, the denotations of multiplicative-additive formulas are finite. This model is also effective, in the sense that the denotations of formulas and proofs are computable, as we show. We also establish analogous results for a second-order extension of Ehrhard’s hypercoherences; while finiteness holds for the same reason as in coherence spaces, effectivity presents additional difficulties. Finally, we discuss the applications our our work to implicit computational complexity in linear (or affine) logic. In view of these applications, we study cardinality and complexity bounds in our finite semantics.

Some immediate consequences of finite semantics

To illustrate the power of the above theorem, we find it instructive to explain

While

Although

1.2
Concrete models: coherence spaces and hypercoherences

It turns out that a finite semantics of
\textsc{mall}_{2}
has been lying around all along
since the birth of linear logic, though we are not aware of anyone noticing this
fact beforehand. It is none other than Girard’s
coherence
spaces

[
13
]
, which he obtained as a simplification of his previous
work on “qualitative domains” in System F
[
12
]
, discovering
linear logic along the way.

In this model, open types (i.e. formulas with free variables) are represented
as
normal functors
3
3

3

This categorical tool had been used
previously to give the first quantitative semantics of the

\lambda
-calculus
[
14
]
– a work which is arguably one of the main
inspirations for linear logic (linear
\lambda
-terms are interpreted in this
model as monomials of degree 1, hence “linear”), and even differential
linear logic
[
10
]
.
. To prove our finiteness theorem, we introduce a
notion of normal functor of
finite degree
, which is preserved by
\textsc{mall}_{2}
connectives and ensures finiteness. It is also equivalent to an asymptotic

polynomial growth
property.

Furthermore, this semantics is more concrete than its formulation using
category-theoretic machinery could suggest. Thanks to a combinatorial
presentation, we prove that it is an
effective
model: the denotations of
types and terms are computable. Note that historically, effectivity was a major
motivation for using coherence spaces instead of qualitative domains
(see
[
12
, Appendix C]
, especially Remark C.3).

We also study the interpretation of
\textsc{mall}_{2}
in Ehrhard’s

hypercoherences

[
9
]
. Although it was defined as a model of
propositional linear logic, transposing the recipe of coherence spaces gives a
model of second-order linear logic, with finite denotations for
\textsc{mall}_{2}
formulas and proofs. In this semantics, effectivity stumbles upon the same issue
as in qualitative domains: roughly speaking, the presence of “
n
-ary
coherences” for arbitrary
n\geq 2
(while in coherence spaces,
n=2
). We
show that despite this, the hypercoherence model can be made effective for

\textsc{mall}_{2}
, using a notion of “specification by projections”.

Coherence spaces (propositional case)

Recall that a
coherence space
is an undirected (reflexive) graph, i.e. a
pair
X=(\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|},\coh_{X})
of a set
\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}
– the
web
of
X
–
and a symmetric and reflexive relation
\coh_{X}\;\subseteq\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}\times\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}
– its

. A subset
c\subseteq\mathopen{|}A\mathclose{|}
is a
clique
of
A
if its points are pairwise
coherent for
\coh_{A}
; in this case we write
c\sqsubset A
.

The operations
(-)^{\perp},\otimes,\oplus,\oc
are defined on coherence spaces
as follows (cf.
[
15
]
):

•

\mathopen{|}X^{\perp}\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}
and
x\coh_{X^{\perp}}x^{\prime}\iff x\not\coh_{X}x^{\prime}\lor x=x^{\prime}
(complement graph)

•

\mathopen{|}X\otimes Y\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}\times\mathopen{|}Y\mathclose{|}
and
(x,y)\coh_{X\otimes Y}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\iff x\coh_{X}x^{\prime}\land y\coh_{Y}y^{\prime}

•

\mathopen{|}X\oplus Y\mathclose{|}=\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}\uplus\mathopen{|}Y\mathclose{|}
and
z\coh_{A\oplus B}z^{\prime}\iff\exists Z\in\{X,Y\}:(z\in Z\land z^{\prime}\in Z\land z\coh_{Z}z^{\prime})

•

\mathopen{|}\oc X\mathclose{|}=\{\text{cliques of $X$}\}
and
c\coh_{\oc X}c^{\prime}\iff c\cup c^{\prime}\sqsubset X
(“set-based” exponential)

Furthermore, the multiplicative units
1,\bot
are interpreted as the singleton
space, and the additive units
\top,0
as the empty space. This is enough to
define inductively the denotation
\mathopen{\llbracket}A\mathclose{\rrbracket}
of a formula

A proof

•

•

whose morphisms between

2.1
Functors on embeddings and uniform families

In order to interpret second-order quantification, we want to give a first-class
status to the map
\{\text{assignments for variables in $A$}\}\to\{\text{possible values for $\mathopen{\llbracket}A\mathclose{\rrbracket}$}\}
when
A
is an open type (i.e. a
formula with free variables). The first idea that comes to mind is to consider
it as a functor on
\mathsf{CohL}
. But it stumbles on the fact that while the binary
connectives are
covariant
bifunctors on
\mathsf{CohL}
, linear negation is a

contravariant
endofunctor. Instead, Girard’s idea is to work in a
“category of embeddings” (see
[
6
]
) to make negation covariant.

Definition 7
.

An
embedding
of a coherence space
X
into a coherence space
Y
is an
injection
\iota:\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}\to\mathopen{|}Y\mathclose{|}
such that
x\coh_{X}x^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow\iota(x)\coh_{Y}\iota(x^{\prime})
. We write
\iota:X\hookrightarrow Y
.

The category
\mathsf{CohI}
has as objects coherence spaces, and as morphisms the
embeddings.

Proposition 8
.

(-)^{\perp}
is a
covariant
endofunctor of
\mathsf{CohI}
.

Proof.

If
X
is an induced subgraph of
Y
, then
X^{\perp}
is an induced subgraph of

Y^{\perp}
.
∎

Remark 9
.

In the same vein, the graph
\{(x,\iota(x))\mid x\in\mathopen{|}X\mathclose{|}\}
of an
embedding
\iota:X\hookrightarrow Y
is a clique both in
X\multimap Y
and in

X^{\perp}\multimap Y^{\perp}
.

Let us say, provisionally, that functors
F:\mathsf{CohI}^{n}\to\mathsf{CohI}
are our
semantical counterpart of open formulas with
n
variables. A proof of such a
formula should be a family of cliques
c_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\sqsubset F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, “uniform” in some way. The following notion of uniformity
has been called the “mutilation property” by Girard
[

]
:

Definition 10

A family
c_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}\sqsubset F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
is

uniform
if for any embeddings
\iota_{i}:X_{i}\hookrightarrow Y_{i}
(
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
),
c_{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}=F(\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n})^{-1}(c_{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}})
.

Remark 11
.

At this point we have to point out a subtlety of the coherence space model: it
is not a priori obvious that uniformity is closed under composition, in other
words, that the pointwise composition of a uniform clique family for
F\multimap G
with a uniform clique family for
G\multimap H
is uniform for

F\multimap H
. Indeed, taking
n=1
for simplicity, the uniformity
condition for a family

seen as a family of
morphisms is expressed as a diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(\iota)^{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\iota)^{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{X}}

in

, where
j^{+}=\{(x,j(x))\mid x\in A\}\sqsubset A\multimap B

and

for

It turns out that uniform clique families
do
compose, but this issue is
non-trivial and was overlooked in Girard’s papers. A proof is part of the
folklore and often credited to Eugenio Moggi.

Remark 12
.

In the relational semantics of linear logic, in which objects are sets and
morphisms are relations, one could define an analogous notion of uniform
subset family for a functor on the category of injections. But then, for the
uniform families

c_{S}=\{*\}\times S\subseteq\mathopen{\llbracket}1\multimap X\mathclose{\rrbracket}_{X\mapsto S}\qquad c^{\prime}_{S}=S\times\{*\}\subseteq\mathopen{\llbracket}X\multimap 1\mathclose{\rrbracket}_{X\mapsto S}

the composition

is
not uniform
: it is equal to

\{(*,*)\}
if
S\neq\varnothing
, and
\varnothing
if
S=\varnothing
.

This issue with the second-order relational model is known and has been
investigated by A. Bac-Bruasse (whose PhD thesis in French
[
2
]
is the
main reference on the subject to our knowledge), T. Ehrhard and C. Tasson.
What makes composition work in coherence spaces is the domain-theoretic

stability
5
5

5

Stability is indeed a recurring pattern here: the
uniformity condition is reminiscent of Berry’s stable order between

property of morphisms.
This is our reason for working with coherence spaces instead of the simpler
relational model.

2.2
Normal functors

The next goal is to interpret quantifiers. Let us look at the example of the
formula
X\multimap X
, which admits a proof
\pi
such that
\mathopen{\llbracket}\pi\mathclose{\rrbracket}_{X\mapsto S}=\{(s,s)\mid s\in S\}
for any coherence space
S
. This uniform
family should correspond to a clique in some space
\mathopen{\llbracket}\forall X.\,X\multimap X\mathclose{\rrbracket}
. The idea is to take some kind of “patterns with bound
variables” as the points of this coherence space. Typically,
\{(s,s)\mid s\in S\}
should correspond to the single pattern
\langle x\vdash(x,x)\rangle
with a
bound variable

2.3
Ensuring finiteness: normal functors of finite degree

We now come to our technical contributions, having just finished the exposition
of Girard’s model. This section introduces a notion of

of a normal
functor, which will witness the finiteness of the interpretation of
\textsc{mall}_{2}
.

Definition 22

Proposition 23
.

A normal functor

is finite if and only if
\mathrm{NF}(F)
is finite.

Theorem 24

(Finiteness = polynomial growth)
.

Let

is the

For applications such as the one in

Proposition 25

A finite normal functor has finitely many uniform families of cliques.

Proof.

By

23

20
, since

\mathopen{|}\mathrm{Tr}(F)\mathclose{|}\subseteq\mathrm{NF}(F)

∎

To obtain a finite semantics, our goal is therefore to show that inside the

\textsc{mall}_{2}

Proposition 26

If

For any normal functor

Finite normal functors are closed under

We still need to show that if

We are now ready to revisit the example outlined at the start of

The idea is to see the webs

With such a concrete description it becomes easier to see how one can compute

One can then routinely check that the adjunction holds.
We therefore conclude:

Theorem 40
.

Variable hypercoherences and uniform families of cliques form a semantics of
second-order linear logic. Furthermore, by restricting to weakly finite
variable hypercoherences, we obtain a finite semantics of
\textsc{mall}_{2}
.

4.2
Strong finiteness and effectivity

Unfortunately, the model of weakly finite variable hypercoherences is
not
effective
. Let us give an exemple: let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,1\}
be any
function, and

F_{f}(X)=(|X|,\{S\subseteq_{\mathrm{fin}}^{*}|X|\mid f(\mathrm{Card}(S))=1\})

This map on objects can be extended to a functor

which is in fact a weakly finite variable hypercoherence. But if
f
is
uncomputable, then
X\mapsto\Gamma(F_{f}(X))
also is.

We are therefore seeking an additional condition on variable hypercoherences
which would both guarantee effectivity and be preserved by
\textsc{mall}_{2}
connectives.
More precisely, our goal is to exhibit a class of variable hypercoherences
F

such that
\Gamma(F)
can be described canonically by some finite data – just
as
\mathrm{NF}(F)
fulfills this role for
|F|
when

is weakly finite. This is the
purpose of the following defintions.

Definition 41

n

If

All projections are computable, so it suffices to precompute a table encoding

∎

Remark 46
.

Our definition of specification by projections is very restrictive. For

connectives, and that the interpretations of

, whether the valuation

The normal functors

If the

From these propositions, we see that

Strongly finite variable hypercoherences form an effective model of

6
Conclusion

Motivated by applications to implicit complexity, we sought a finite semantics
for
\textsc{mall}_{2}
, and obtained it by proving the finiteness of the pre-existing
model of coherence spaces and normal functors. In retrospect, this is not so
surprising: one advantage of coherence spaces (e.g. over Scott domains), that
had already been stressed early in their history, is their tendency to give
small and legible interpretations to formulas.

Another early observation by Girard was that the existential introduction in
this model has a non-trivial computational contents, subsuming the cut rule –
this was mentioned as being “key to a semantic approach to
computation”
[
13
, p. 57]
. By going from
A[T]
to
\exists X.\,A
,
the information of the witness
T
is compressed into some bounded data, and
this is why the semantics can be finite. Let us reformulate this from the
programming language point of view on existential types as abstract data types:
the cliques of
\mathopen{\llbracket}\exists X.\,A\mathclose{\rrbracket}
keep just enough information about the
cliques of
\mathopen{\llbracket}A[T]\mathclose{\rrbracket}
they originate from to determine their interaction
with the generic (universally typed) programs which might use them.

Relatedly, observe that the syntactic model of propositional
mall
is finite,
and the existential witnesses are the only reason why this is not the case in

\textsc{mall}_{2}
. One could also try to directly implement the above intuitions starting
from the syntax; this will be the subject of an upcoming paper with P. Pistone,
T. Seiller and L. Tortora de Falco.

Aside from finiteness, the present work also investigated in detail the question
of effectivity. Almost no effort is needed in the case of coherence spaces, but
to obtain an effective hypercoherence model of
\textsc{mall}_{2}
, we had to introduce the
idea of specifications by projections.

D.2
Proof of soundness by reduction to
lemma

54

In this section, we fix
\pi:\oc\mathtt{Str}\multimap\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}
in
\textsc{ell}_{2}
, and
prove the converse of the above. The first step is to understand the shape of

\pi
.

Lemma 56
.

Up to commutations,
\pi
is of the form

\hat{\pi}

\vdash\mathtt{Str}[A_{1}]^{\bot},\dots,\mathtt{Str}[A_{n}]^{\bot},\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\vdash\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\dots,\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\oc

\vdash\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\dots,\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\vdash\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\invamp

\vdash\oc\mathtt{Str}\multimap\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

Proof.

A proof of
\vdash\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\dots,\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

necessarily ends either with a structural rule or a promotion.
From this and the invertibility of
\invamp
, one obtains that the
proof, up to commutation, ends with the following sequence
of rules:

\vdash\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\dots,\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\oc

\vdash\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\dots,\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\vdash\wn\mathtt{Str}^{\bot},\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

\invamp

\vdash\oc\mathtt{Str}\multimap\oc\oc\mathtt{Bool}

Now, recall that
\mathtt{Str}^{\bot}=\exists X.\mathtt{Str}[X]^{\bot}
. Moreover,
notice the introduction rule for
\exists
commutes with all other
rules except promotion and the introduction of
\forall
. We only
need to show that the introduction rule for all
\exists
connectives
of the occurrences of
\mathtt{Str}^{\bot}
are not followed by a promotion
or a
\forall
introduction. Ruling out promotion is easy, as all
formulas in the conclusion sequent of a promotion rule have
exponential as principal connectives. Moreover, it is possible to
rule out
\forall
introductions as folllws. If a
\forall
introduction
rule appears in the proof, the
\forall
connective it introduces
is part of an existential witness. But all variables existentially
quantified appear under the scope of an exponential connective,
and therefore can only precede a promotion rule.
∎

Now, a crucial observation is that
the
A_{i}
in the previous lemma can be
taken in
\textsc{mall}_{2}
w.l.o.g.
This is where the
stratification
property
of
\textsc{ell}_{2}
plays a key role.

Lemma 57
.

There is a proof
\pi^{\prime}
whose witnesses
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
are closed
\textsc{mall}_{2}
formulas, and which decides the same language as
\pi
.

Proof.

We define the
truncation at depth 2
of a formula as follows: all subformulas
of the form
!A
(resp.
?A
) at depth 2, i.e. in the scope of two other
nested
!/?
modalities, are replaced by
1
(resp.
\bot
). Note that the
truncation at depth 2 of
!\mathtt{Str}
and
!!\mathtt{Bool}
are themselves.

This operation extends to proofs: any functorial promotion of conclusion

\vdash\wn B_{1},\ldots,\wn B_{m},\oc C
is replaced by the only proof of

\vdash\bot,\ldots,\bot,1
, while contractions and weakenings are replaced
by cuts with
1\vdash 1\otimes 1
and
\vdash 1
. Note that this truncation is
the identity on cut-free proofs of
!!\mathtt{Bool}
and
!\mathtt{Str}
.

One may then check that truncation at depth 2 is compatible with cut-elimination,
which means that one can replace
\pi
by its truncation at depth 2 and still
recognize the same language. Then the
A_{i}
are replaced by their “truncation
at depth 0” which are
\textsc{mall}_{2}
formulas.
∎

Proposition 58
.

\mathcal{L}(\pi)
is regular.

Proof.

By
lemma

54
, the language

\{w\in\{0,1\}^{*}\mid\hat{\pi}(\overline{w}[A_{i}]\otimes\ldots\otimes\overline{w}[A_{n}])\longrightarrow^{*}\mathtt{true}\}

is regular. A examination of the cut-elimination process reveals
that this language is none other than
\mathcal{L}(\pi)
.
∎

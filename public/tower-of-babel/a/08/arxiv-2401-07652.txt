Well-posedness and long-term behaviour for a troposphere wave propagation model
Paul Holst, Jens D.M. Rademacher

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a model recently derived by A. Constantin and R.S. Johnson for nonlinear wave propagation in the troposphere, particularly the ’morning glory’ cloud pattern. We consider the model with natural Dirichlet boundary conditions for the vertical velocity at the top of the troposphere, and thus introduce a new pressure term. This modified system has a structural relation to the 2D primitive equations, for which global well-posedness and the existence of a global attractor are already known. We transfer these results to the modified model, giving proofs that exploit specific features and use standard methods combined with anisotropic Sobolev inequalities. Additionally, we show that the attractor exists only for specific parameter ranges, while for other parameters, we find runaway solutions with unbounded growth over time. u(t,x):=e^{(\alpha-\mu)t}e_{(0,1)}(x)=a_{0,(0,1)}e^{(\alpha-\mu)t}\sin(x_{2})\qquad(t\geqslant 0,\ x\in\Omega),

(2.9)

where
e_{(0,1)}
, defined in (
2.3
), turns out to be the first eigenfunction of
\Delta_{\textnormal{div}}
.
The
L_{2}(\Omega)
-norm of
u
decays exponentially over time if
\mu>\alpha
, remains constant if
\mu=\alpha
(in this case
u
is a
steady state
) and grows exponentially and unboundedly over time with growth rate
\alpha-\mu
if
\mu<\alpha
; we refer to the latter as ‘grow-up’. In the latter case, the solutions of (
1.3
)-(
1.7
) accumulate infinite energy over time, which is clearly unphysical.
In fact, all
e_{(0,\ell_{2})}
,
\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e., all
x_{1}
-independent eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{\textnormal{div}}
generate such linear invariant spaces that grow exponentially for sufficiently large
\alpha>\mu
.
This suggests that in practice, one should be cautious with the choice of
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\mu
.
However, for
\pi|\beta|<\mu
if
\alpha\leqslant 0
and
\alpha+\pi|\beta|<\mu
if
\alpha>0
, we prove the existence of a global attractor in Theorem
2.4
below, so in this case there is no problem of that kind. While for
\beta=K=0
there is a sharp dichotomy between a compact attractor and grow-up, it remains unclear whether there are global bounds for
\alpha\in(\mu-\pi|\beta|,\mu)
.

To formulate this result, we first introduce the semigroup associated with (
2.7
), which can be defined thanks to Theorem
2.3
. Assuming
K\in L_{2,\textnormal{loc}}(0,\infty;L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))
, for any
u_{0}\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
, it follows from Theorem
2.3
that there exists a unique

S(\cdot)u_{0}:=u\in H_{\textnormal{loc}}^{1}(0,\infty;L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\cap C([0,\infty);H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega))\cap L_{2.\textnormal{loc}}(0,\infty;H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega))

(2.10)

satisfying (
2.7
) with respect to
u_{0}
and
K
. Let
M(A)
denote the set of all functions from a set
A
into itself. We have that
S\colon[0,\infty)\to M(H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega))
defines a semigroup, i.e.,
S(0)u_{0}=u_{0}
for all
u_{0}\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
and
S(t+s)=S(t)S(s)
for all
t,s\geqslant 0
.
Moreover, due to (
2.8
), the function
S(t)\colon H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)\to H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
is continuous.

Our second main result, concerning the global attractor is now as follows.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K\in H^{1}(0,\infty;H_{\textnormal{div}}^{-1}(\Omega))\cap C_{b}([0,\infty);L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))
, and assume that
\pi|\beta|<\mu
if
\alpha\leqslant 0
and
\alpha+\pi|\beta|<\mu
if
\alpha>0
. Then
S
possesses a compact global attractor in
H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
.

As mentioned, for
\alpha=\beta=0
, (
1.3
)-(
1.7
) is a subsystem of the 2D primitive equations. Since these are in turn a subsystem of the 3D primitive equations, Theorem
2.4
follows for
\alpha=\beta=0
from known global attractor results for the 3D primitive equations, cf.
[
17
, Thm. 4.3]
.
For general
\alpha
and
\beta
– as for the global existence – we rely on standard methods to prove Theorem
2.4
, highlighting how to exactly handle the additional linear terms
\alpha u
and
\beta v
: we first show the boundedness in time
H^{2}
of solutions (Proposition
4.1
), then use the uniform Gronwall lemma (Lemma
5.3
) to show that
S
possesses an absorbing ball in
H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)
norm (Proposition
4.1
)
and finally infer the existence of the global attractor (Theorem
5.1
).

3
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some preliminary results.

Throughout this paper we denote by
\Delta_{\textnormal{div}}
the Laplacian on
L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
\Delta_{\textnormal{div}}
is an unbounded self-adjoint operator on
L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)
defined by

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\textnormal{div}})

\displaystyle:=\{u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega);\ \Delta u\in L_{2}(\Omega)\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textnormal{div}}u

\displaystyle:=\Delta u.

We observe that
(e_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathcal{Z}}
is an orthonormalbasis of
L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)
constisting of eigenvectors of
-\Delta_{\textnormal{div}}
with corresponding eigenvalue
given by
\lambda_{\ell}:=|\ell|^{2}
.
Furthermore,
e_{\ell}\in C_{\textnormal{div}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\lambda_{\ell}\geqslant 1=\lambda_{(0,1)}
for all
\ell\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Using these facts, yields
the Poincaré inequality

\|u\|_{2}\leqslant\|\nabla u\|_{2}\qquad(u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega))

(3.1)

and the inequality

\|\nabla u\|_{2}\leqslant\|\Delta u\|_{2}\qquad(u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega));

(3.2)

in particular, the mapping
H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\|\Delta u\|_{2}\in[0,\infty)
defines an equivalent norm on
H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus, it is also not too difficult to show that
\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\textnormal{div}})=H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Next we want to prove
estimates for the nonlinearity

B(u,v):=u\partial_{1}v+v_{u}\partial_{2}v\qquad(u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega))

appearing in (
2.7
), that will be needed in Section
4
to derive appropriate a-priori estimates for the proof of Theorem
2.3
. Recall the definition of
v_{u}
from (
2.6
), and
note that
v_{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)\subseteq L_{4}(\Omega)

and thus
B(u,v)\in L_{2}(\Omega)
for all
u,v\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(a) Let
u,v,w\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

\int_{\Omega}|B(u,v)w|\,dx\leqslant M_{B}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}

for some
M_{B}\geqslant 0
independent of
u
,
v
and
w
.

(b) Let
u,v\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)
and
w\in L_{2}(\Omega)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|B(u,v)w|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant M_{B}^{\prime}(\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\partial_{1}v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\partial_{1}\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\mkern 20.0mu+\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2}(\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2}+\|\partial_{1}\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}))\|w\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant M_{B}^{\prime\prime}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|w\|_{2}\leqslant M_{B}^{\prime\prime}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}

for some
M_{B}^{\prime},M_{B}^{\prime\prime}\geqslant 0
independent of
u
,
v
and
w
.

(c) Let
u,v\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then
\int_{\Omega}B(u,v)v\,dx=0
. Moreover, if
u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)
, then

\langle B(u,u),\partial_{2}^{2}u\rangle_{2}=0
.

Remark 3.2
.

The estimate given in Lemma
3.1
(a) is sufficient for our purposes, but we remark that it can be improved, as we shall see in the proof of Lemma
3.1
(a) below; e.g., one even has that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|B(u,v)w|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant c(\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\partial_{1}v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\mkern 20.0mu+\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}^{1/2}(\|w\|_{2}^{1/2}+\|\partial_{1}w\|_{2}^{1/2}))\qquad(u,v,w\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega))

for some
c\geqslant 0
.

In preparation of the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we will now derive three important properties of the term
v_{u}
. The first property is the following rule of integration by parts:

\int_{\Omega}v_{u}\partial_{2}v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{1}u)v\,dx\qquad(u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega),\ v\in H_{\textnormal{hp}}^{1}(\Omega));

(3.3)

Indeed, if
u\in C_{\textnormal{div}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and
v\in C_{\textnormal{hp}}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, the equality (
3.3
) directly follows from the fact that
v_{u}(x_{1},\cdot)\in H_{0}^{1}(0,\pi)
for a.e.
x_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi)
,
\partial_{2}v_{u}=-\partial_{1}u
and integration by parts. The general case is then immediately obtained by approximation.

Furthermore, using Hölder’s inequality, for all
u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
we estimate

\displaystyle\|v_{u}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\biggl(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\biggl(\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime})|\,dx_{2}^{\prime}\biggr)^{2}\,dx_{2}\,dx_{1}\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\biggl(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x_{2}^{\prime})|^{2}\,dx_{2}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\pi}\mathbf{1}_{(-\pi,\pi)}^{2}\,dx_{2}^{\prime}\,dx_{1}\biggr)^{1/2}=\pi\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}

(3.4)

and

\|v_{u}(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\pi)}\leqslant\int_{0}^{\pi}|\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x_{2})|\,dx_{2}\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(-\pi,\pi)}

(3.5)

for a.e.
x_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi)
; these are the second and third property of
v_{u}
, respectively.

For the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we also make use of the Ladyzhenskaya inequality

\|u\|_{4}\leqslant c_{\textnormal{L}}\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1/2}\qquad(u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)),

(3.6)

with some
c_{\textnormal{L}}>0
, which follows from (
3.1
) and
[
1
, Theorem 5.8]
. Moreover, the equality

\|\nabla\partial_{1}u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\partial_{2}u\|_{2}^{2}=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}\qquad(u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega))

(3.7)

will prove to be useful; this directly
follows from integration by parts for
u\in\operatorname{lin}\{e_{\ell};\ \ell\in\mathcal{Z}\}
,
and by approximation for
u\in H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)
.
The next lemma states an anisotropic Sobolev inequality, which has been proven in a similar form in
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
. Our proof follows the same argumentation.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in L_{2}(\Omega)
, and assume that
\partial_{1}u\in L_{2}(\Omega)
. Then

\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\leqslant\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}(\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}+\sqrt{2}\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}^{1/2})\qquad(\text{a.e. }x_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi)).

Proof.

We observe that
((-\pi,\pi)\ni x_{1}\mapsto\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}^{2}\in\mathbb{R})\in H^{1}(-\pi,\pi)\subseteq W_{1}^{1}(-\pi,\pi)
with

\frac{d}{dx_{1}}\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}^{2}=2\int_{0}^{\pi}u(x_{1},x_{2})\partial_{1}u(x_{1},x_{2})\,dx_{2}

for a.e.
x_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi)
. Now, using the fact that

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(-\pi,\pi)}\leqslant\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f|\,dx_{1}+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f^{\prime}|\,dx_{1}\qquad(f\in W_{1}^{1}(-\pi,\pi)),

we have for a.e.
x_{1}^{\prime}\in(-\pi,\pi)
that

\displaystyle\|u(x_{1}^{\prime},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}^{2}\leqslant\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}^{2}\,dx_{1}+\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigg|\frac{d}{dx_{1}}\|u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}^{2}\bigg|\,dx_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u\|_{2}^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}|u||\partial_{1}u|\,dx\leqslant\|u\|_{2}(\|u\|_{2}+2\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}).

∎

With all the above preparations we are now able to prove Lemma
3.1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

In the following proofs of (a)-(c) we assume that
u,v,w\in\operatorname{lin}\{e_{\ell};\ \ell\in\mathcal{Z}\}
and show the asserted equations and inequalities for these functions;
the corresponding equations and inequalities then immediately follow by approximation. (Here one uses parts (a) and (b) for the approximation in part (c)).

(a) By Hölder’s inequality, (
3.6
) and (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(\partial_{1}v)w|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\|u\|_{4}\|\partial_{1}v\|_{2}\|w\|_{4}\leqslant c_{\textnormal{L}}^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\partial_{1}v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{\textnormal{L}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}.

Furthermore, with
f(x_{1}):=\|w(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\ (\text{a.e. }x_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi))
, it follows from (
3.5
) and Lemma
3.3
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v_{u}(\partial_{2}v)w|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|v_{u}(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\pi)}\|\partial_{2}v(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\|w(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\,dx_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\|\partial_{2}v(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}f(x_{1})\,dx_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\pi)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}^{1/2}(\|w\|_{2}^{1/2}+\sqrt{2}\|\partial_{1}w\|_{2}^{1/2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}(1+\sqrt{2})\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}\|\nabla w\|_{2}.

(b) Thanks to (
3.6
), (
3.1
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(\partial_{1}v)w|\,dx\leqslant\|u\|_{4}\|\partial_{1}v\|_{4}\|w\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{\textnormal{L}}^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\partial_{1}v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\partial_{1}\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|w\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{\textnormal{L}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|w\|_{2}.

With
f(x_{1}):=\|\partial_{2}v(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\ (\text{a.e }x_{1}\in(-\pi,\pi))
, and by (
3.5
), Lemma
3.3
, (
3.2
), (
3.1
) and (
3.7
), we further have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v_{u}(\partial_{2}v)w|\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}f(x_{1})\|w(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(0,\pi)}\,dx_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(0,\pi)}\|w\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2}(\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2}+\sqrt{2}\|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2})\|w\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}\|\partial_{1}u\|_{2}\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2}(\|\partial_{2}v\|_{2}^{1/2}+\sqrt{2}\|\partial_{1}\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2})\|w\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\pi}(1+\sqrt{2})\|\nabla u\|_{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{1/2}\|w\|_{2}.

(c) Since
|v|^{2}\in H_{\textnormal{hp}}^{1}(\Omega)
, we can use integration by parts and (
3.3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}B(u,v)v\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u(\partial_{1}v)v+v_{u}(\partial_{2}v)v\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}u\partial_{1}(|v|^{2})+v_{u}\partial_{2}(|v|^{2})\,dx=0.

Moreover, since

, it again follows from integration by parts and (

) that

\displaystyle\langle B(u,u),\partial_{2}^{2}u\rangle_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u\partial_{1}u\partial_{2}^{2}u+v_{u}\partial_{2}u\partial_{2}^{2}u\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{2}u|^{2}\partial_{1}u+u\partial_{1}\partial_{2}u\partial_{2}u-\frac{1}{2}v_{u}\partial_{2}|\partial_{2}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{2}u|^{2}\partial_{1}u+\frac{1}{2}u\partial_{1}|\partial_{2}u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{1}u|\partial_{2}u|^{2}\,dx=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

5
Existence of compact global attractor

In this section we show our second main result Theorem
2.4
, i.e., the dynamics associated with (
2.7
) feature a (compact) global attractor.
For this, let
K\in L_{2,\textnormal{loc}}(0,\infty;L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))
be arbitrary but fixed throughout and
S\colon[0,\infty)\to M(H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega))
from (
2.10
).

Following
[
16
, Def. I.1.2]
, we say that a (compact) set
A\subseteq H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
is a (
compact
)
global attractor
of
S
if

(i)

A
is invariant under
S
, i.e.,
S(t)A=A
for all
t\geqslant 0
, and

(ii)

A
attracts the bounded sets of
H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e., for all bounded
B\subseteq H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
holds

d(S(t)B,A)\to 0\qquad(t\to\infty),

where
d(B_{1},B_{2})
is defined as the semidistance of two sets
B_{1},B_{2}\subseteq H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

d(B_{1},B_{2}):=\sup_{u\in B_{1}}\inf_{v\in B_{2}}\|u-v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

We use the following standard result for showing the existence of an attractor; for the formulation of this result we simplify
[
16
, Thm. I.1.1]
, adapted to our situation.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
H
be a metric space, and
U\colon[0,\infty)\to M(H)
a continuous semigroup. Let
B\subseteq H
an
absorbing set
of
U
, i.e, for any
B_{0}\subseteq H
there exists a
t_{0}\geqslant 0
such that
U(t)B_{0}\subseteq B
. Assume that there exists
t_{1}>0
such that
U(t_{1})B
is compact. Then the
\omega
-limit set

A:=\bigcap_{s\geqslant 0}\overline{\bigcup_{t\geqslant s}U(t)B}

of
B
is a compact global attractor of
U
. Moreover,
A
is the maximal bounded attractor of
U
(for the inclusion relation).

In order to apply Theorem
5.1
in our case, we next
show that
S
has an absorbing set.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
K\in H^{1}(0,\infty;H_{\textnormal{div}}^{-1}(\Omega))\cap C_{b}([0,\infty);L_{2,\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))
, and assume that
\pi|\beta|<\mu
if
\alpha\leqslant 0
and
\alpha+\pi|\beta|<\mu
if
\alpha>0
. Then there exists an
R>0
such that
B_{H_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Omega)}(0,R)
is an absorbing set for
S
in
H_{\textnormal{div}}^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e., for any

there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that

for all

.

For the proof of Proposition
5.2
we use the well-known

, which we state for the sake of completeness;
see
[

Lemma 5.3

.

Let

,

6
Discussion

In
[
8
]
, Constantin and Johnson derived a model for nonlinear wave propagation in the troposphere that is particularly relevant to meteorological phenomena such as the ’morning glory’ cloud formations.
The slightly simplified form (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has been studied from a mathematical viewpoint concerning well-posedness in
[
12
,
2
]
, but global existence for general initial data remains unclear.

In this paper, we introduced and analyzed a modification that is motivated by standard atmospheric modeling practices using Dirichlet boundary condition at the top of the troposphere.
Enforcing this boundary condition naturally introduces an additional pressure term to adjust the flow accordingly.
Introducing pressure and Dirichlet boundary conditions has led us to the modified system (
1.3
)-(
1.7
), which has not been considered before to our knowledge. For
\alpha=\beta=0
it is an exact subsystem of the primitive equation model for the atmosphere, which has motivated us to leverage techniques from this well studied equation.

Our investigation has focused on two central aspects: well-posedness and long-term dynamics of the system. In contrast to the known results for the unmodified system, our global well-posedness result for arbitrary initial data in
H^{1}
for (
1.3
)-(
1.7
), Theorem
2.3
, ensures unique strong solutions that depend continuously on the initial data. This may be seen an indication that top boundary conditions for
v
and an additional pressure term are natural.
Based on this, we have considered the long-term behavior of the system. On the one hand we have pointed out runaway modes that for a large set of parameter values: explicit plane wave solutions exhibit exponential and unbounded growth, somewhat similar to those in
[
5
]
and to standing modes from rigid lid conditions
[
11
]
. On the other hand, for a complementary set of parameter choices, we have shown that the system possesses a compact global attractor.

Following
[
12
,
2
]
we have focussed on the simplified form of the model from
[
8
]
with constant coefficients
\alpha,\beta
and isotropic diffusion. We expect that our proofs can be readily generalised to the case of
x_{2}
-dependent coefficients as in
[
9
]
.

In conclusion, our results offer novel insights into the atmospheric wave model, particularly into long-term behavior, stability and energy dissipation.
We have shown that the parameter space supports qualitatively different dynamics for fixed boundary conditions. It would be interesting to explore this further also for other boundary condition such as those suggested recently in
[
11
]
. This also concerns the relation to the different wave dynamics that has been identified without boundary conditions in
[
8
,
9
]
.

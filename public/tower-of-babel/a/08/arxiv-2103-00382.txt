Applications of the theory of Floer to symmetric spaces
Hanwool Bae, Chi Hong Chow, Naichung Conan Leung

Abstract
. We quantize the problem considered by Bott-Samelson who applied Morse theory to any compact symmetric space G / K G/K and the associated real flag manifold G ℝ / B G_{\mathbb{R}}/B which is a real locus of a complex partial flag variety G ℂ / P σ G_{\mathbb{C}}/P_{\sigma} . We prove that the Pontryagin ring H − ⁣ ∗ ​ ( Ω ​ ( G / K ) ) H_{-*}(\Omega(G/K)) of the based loop space Ω ⁡ ( G / K ) \Omega(G/K) is isomorphic to the Floer cohomology ring H ​ F ∗ ​ ( G ℝ / B , G ℝ / B ) HF^{*}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/B,G_{\mathbb{R}}/B) after localization. When G / K G/K is a Lie group, this is a conjecture of Peterson, proved combinatorially by Lam-Shimozono, in the context of quantum cohomologies of complex flag varieties. Our approach is geometric in nature: we construct a Lagrangian correspondence from T ∗ ​ ( G / K ) T^{*}(G/K) to G ℂ / P σ G_{\mathbb{C}}/P_{\sigma} which geometrically composes with a cotangent fiber to G ℝ / B G_{\mathbb{R}}/B , and compute the linear part of the associated Ma’u-Wehrheim-Woodward’s A ∞ A_{\infty} homomorphism from a Floer model of Ω ⁡ ( G / K ) \Omega(G/K) to C ​ F ∗ ​ ( G ℝ / B , G ℝ / B ) CF^{*}(G_{\mathbb{R}}/B,G_{\mathbb{R}}/B) . The crux is to make use of the geometry of G / K G/K to construct specific perturbation data which enables us to reduce the computations to the case when G / K G/K is a torus.

Remark 1.6
.

The group case of Theorem
1.5
was solved completely by Bott
[
5
]
. In general, the problem about whether or not
H_{*}(\Omega(G/K);\Bbbk)
is finitely generated is non-trivial, since, due to possible existence of divided power structures, an algebra which is additively isomorphic to a finitely generated algebra is not necessarily finitely generated, even over a field (of positive characteristics). For example, consider the cohomology ring of
\Omega S^{3}
. We have, by Serre’s spectral sequence,
H^{*}(\Omega S^{3};\Bbbk)\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\Bbbk\langle x_{i}\rangle
for any
\Bbbk
, where
\deg(x_{i})=2i
. The generators satisfy
x_{i}\cup x_{j}=\frac{(i+j)!}{i!j!}x_{i+j}
. Take
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. By elementary number theory, the last equation implies that
x_{0},\ldots,x_{2^{N}-1}
cannot generate
x_{2^{N}}
multiplicatively for any natural number
N
. We conclude that
H^{*}(\Omega S^{3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is not finitely generated.

The proof of the key lemma uses an action-functional argument. In typical applications of this argument, constant solutions are those which contribute to the leading terms. In our case, however, we have to refine it by establishing a positive lower bound for the energy and showing that it is attained by a unique non-constant pseudoholomorphic quilt.

To achieve this, we first consider the open dense subset
\mathcal{U}\subset T^{*}(G/K)\simeq T(G/K)
consisting of vectors which are tangent to a unique maximal torus in
G/K
. This uniqueness property allows us to construct a well-defined Hamiltonian torus action on
\mathcal{U}
using parallel transport. One can show that
G_{\mathbb{C}}/P_{\sigma}
is a symplectic quotient of the action (Proposition
3.1
).

Next, we complexify this action with respect to the canonical almost complex structure
J_{0}
on
T^{*}(G/K)
. Notice that complex orbits are not the cotangent bundles of maximal tori but open subsets which are roughly
1/|W|
of them, where
W
is the Weyl group. Then the almost complex structures we use for the moduli problems will be small perturbations of
J_{0}
which preserve all complex orbits (Definition
3.23
). A relative version of unique continuation theorem is needed in order to achieve the transversality for all the pseudoholomorphic curves involved (Lemma
A.6
).

Finally, we construct a retraction contracting
\mathcal{U}
onto the coisotropic submanifold which defines the symplectic quotient
G_{\mathbb{C}}/P_{\sigma}
. The aforementioned lower bound is then obtained by applying this retraction to every pseudoholomorphic quilt (Lemma
4.7
). Moreover, this lower bound is attained by those quilts which are contained in one of the complex orbits. Since these orbits lie in the cotangent bundles of maximal tori, it is not difficult to show that there is precisely one such quilt with fixed input data, by passing to the universal cover and applying a version of the Riemann mapping theorem (Proposition
4.4
).

One technical difficulty is to handle those quilts which do not lie inside
\mathcal{U}
, preventing one from applying the above retraction directly. This is done by using the fact that the complement
T^{*}(G/K)\setminus\mathcal{U}
is a stratified subset of codimension at least two (Lemma
2.13
). Two is a magic number for Floer theorists: every pseudoholomorphic curve can be perturbed to one which intersects a given codimension-two submanifold along a discrete set. This measure-zero set does not cause any trouble when one analyses the energy of the curve.

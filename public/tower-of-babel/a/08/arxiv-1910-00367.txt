Eulerian collinear configuration for 3-body problem
Liang Ding, Juan Manuel Sánchez-Cerritos, Jinlong Wei

Abstract




Abstract

For 3-body problem with any given masses
m_{1},\,m_{2},\,m_{3}>0
, there exist only Eulerian collinear central configuration and Lagrangian equilateral-triangle central configuration, and in this paper, for planar 3-body problem,
we prove that there exists another non-collision trajectory
q
, which is not the variational
minimizer of the Lagrangian action on the loop space
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
,
is also an Eulerian collinear central configuration at any instant. Moreover, we do not need the restriction condition on the winding number
deg(q_{i}-q_{j})\neq 0\,(i\neq j)
.

Keywords
Planar 3-body problem; Eulerian collinear configuration;
Mountain pass theorem; Perturbation of Newtonian potential

70F07, 70F15

1
Introduction

For the motion of 3-body problem, it is well-known that Newton’s second law and Newton’s universal gravitation law yield the following equation

\displaystyle m_{i}\ddot{q}_{i}+\sum_{{j\neq i\atop{{1\leqslant j\leqslant 3}}}}m_{i}m_{j}\frac{q_{i}-q_{j}}{|q_{j}-q_{i}|^{3}}=0,\quad q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},\quad k=2\,\,or\,\,3,\,\,i=1,2,3,

(1.1)

and when
k=2
, we call it planar 3-body problem; when
k=3
, we call it spatial 3-body problem.
In this paper, for planar 3-body problem (
1.1
), we attempt to study the non-collision solution with central configuration characteristic, and firstly, we introduce a definition on central configuration.

Definition 1.1.
(
[
Wintner1947
]
) At a given instant
t=t_{0}
, for
N
mass points
m_{i}
with positions
q_{i}(t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

(k=2\,\,or\,\,3,\,i=1,2,\ldots,N)
, a configuration
q(t_{0})=(q_{1}(t_{0}),q_{2}(t_{0}),\ldots,q_{N}(t_{0}))^{T}\in X\setminus\Delta
is called a central configuration if there exists a constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum\limits_{{j\neq i\atop{{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}}}}\frac{m_{j}m_{i}}{|q_{j}(t_{0})-q_{i}(t_{0})|^{3}}(q_{j}(t_{0})-q_{i}(t_{0}))=\lambda m_{i}(q_{i}(t_{0})-c_{0}),\quad i=1,2,\ldots,N,\\
\ \lambda=\frac{V(q(t_{0}))}{I(q(t_{0}))},\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle X\setminus\triangle=\{q(t_{0})=(q_{1}(t_{0}),\ldots,q_{N}(t_{0}))^{T}\in(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{N}\,or\,(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{N}:q_{i}(t_{0})\neq q_{j}(t_{0}),\,when\,i\neq j\}.

c_{0}
,
V
and
I
represent the center of masses, the Newtonian potential and the moment of inertia, respectively, which are given by

\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}m_{i}q_{i}(t_{0})}{\sum\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}m_{i}},\,V(q(t_{0}))=-\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}(t_{0})-q_{j}(t_{0})|}

and

\displaystyle I(q(t_{0}))=\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}m_{i}|q_{i}(t_{0})-c_{0}|^{2},

respectively.

The set of central configurations are invariant
under three classes of transformations: translations, scalings and orthogonal
transformations
[
Wintner1947
]
, and the study of central configurations is a very important subject in celestial mechanics with a long and varied history
[
Moeckel1990
,
Smale1998
]
, and a well-known fact is that finding the relative equilibrium solutions of the classical
N
-body problem and the planar central configurations are equivalent. But the problem on the numbers for central configurations is a very difficult topic
[
Albouy2012
,
Hampton2006
]
, and Smale took it as one of the most important 18 mathematical problems (the sixth one) for the 21st century
[
Smale1998
]
, one of the reasons is that finding the concrete central configurations is a very difficult work
[
Fernandes2017
,
Hampton2005
,
MacMillan1932
,
Perez2007
]
.
For
N=3
, in 1767, Euler
[
E1767
]
found the well-known Euler’s collinear central configuration (Euler’s central configuration for short), and in 1772, Lagrange
[
Lagrange1772
]
proved the existence of the famous equilateral-triangle central configuration (also called Lagrange’s central configuration for short). In fact, for 3-body problem, there are only two kinds of concrete central configurations: Euler’s central configuration and Lagrange’s central configuration
[
Abraham1978
]
, and in this paper, we study one kind of
important concrete central configurations: Euler’s central configuration, i.e. we discuss
(
1.1
) in which
q
can form an Euler’s central configuration at any instant. Before recalling
some elegant works which are relevant for the present paper, we introduce a notation on winding number.

Defiition 1.2.
(
[
Chern1989
]
) Let
C=\{x(t),\,t\in[a,b]\}
be a given oriented closed curve, and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a point not on the curve, then the mapping
\phi:\,C\rightarrow S^{1}
, given by

\displaystyle\phi(x(t))=\frac{x(t)-p}{|x(t)-p|},\,\,t\in[a,\,b],

is defined to be a position mapping of the curve
C
relative to
p
. When the point on
C
goes around the
curve once, its image
\phi(x(t))
will go around a number of times, this number is called the winding number
of the curve
C
relative to
p
, and we denote it by
deg(x)
.

For 2-body problem, in 1977, Gordon
[
Gordon1977
]
proved that the elliptic Keplerian orbit minimize the Lagrangian
action of 2-body problem. For planar 3-body problem, there are elegant works: in 2000, by assuming anti-
T/2
symmetry condition, Long and Zhang
[
Long2000
]
(or
[
Chenciner1998
]
) proved that for any given positive masses
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
, the variational
minimizer of the Lagrangian action in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, is precisely Lagrange’s central configuration at any instant; in 2001, by assuming the winding number
deg\,(q_{i}-q_{j})\neq 0\,(i\neq j)
, Zhang and Zhou
[
Zhang2001
]
(or
[
Venturelli2001
]
) also proved that for any given choice of the three positive masses, the variational
minimizer of the Lagrangian action in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the Lagrange’s central configuration at any instant. For more details in this direction, we refer to
[
Llibre2015
,
Wintner1947
,
Zhang2004
]
.

Note that for 3-body problem, there are only two kinds of concrete central configurations: Euler’s central configuration and Lagrange’s central configuration. We also note that for planar 3-body problem, in 2004, employing a direct variational method with that assumptions that the winding number
deg(q_{i}-q_{j})\neq 0\,(i\neq j)
, and the three bodies are collinear, Zhang and Zhou
[
Zhang2004
]
proved that the minimizer of Lagrangian action
f(q)
on
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
, just forms an Euler’s central configuration at any instant, where the loop space
\Lambda_{1}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{q=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,\mid\,\,q_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,q_{3}(t)-q_{1}(t)=\lambda_{0}(q_{2}(t)-q_{1}(t)),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}q_{i}=0,\,deg(q_{i}-q_{j})\neq 0\,\,for\,\,i\neq j\},

where
m_{i}>0\ (i=1,2,3)
and
T>0
is any given masses and period respectively,
\lambda_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{m_{3}\lambda_{0}^{-2}+m_{2}}{m_{3}\lambda_{0}+m_{2}}-\frac{m_{3}(1-\lambda_{0})^{-2}+m_{1}}{m_{3}(1-\lambda_{0})+m_{1}}=0.

(1.4)

Then an interesting question is proposed:

Question.
For planar 3-body problem (
1.1
), can we use the anti-
T/2
symmetry condition to substitute the winding number condition
deg(q_{i}-q_{j})\neq 0\,(i\neq j)
, to obtain new existence of non-collision trajectory
q
such that the three bodies form an Euler’s central configuration at any instant, and
q
is not the variational
minimizer of the Lagrangian action on
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
?

In this paper, by using a new mountain pass theorem, the perturbation of Newtonian potential
V(q)
, some estimates about the Lagrangian
action, and some other known results, we will give a positive answer to the above question. More precisely, without the winding number condition, we will prove that for planar 3-body problem, beside the variational
minimizer of the Lagrangian action restricted on the loop space
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
, there exists another trajectory
q
, which also just forms an Euler’s central configuration at any instant.

The organization of this paper is as follows: In Section 2, we give the main result. Section 3 is devoted to introducing some useful lemmas, and in Section 4, we prove the main result.

2
Main result

In what follows, for any given positive masses
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
, the configuration space of 3-body problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is described as

\displaystyle G=\{(x_{1},\,x_{2},\,x_{3})\in(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{3},\,\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}x_{i}=0,\,and\,\,x_{i}\neq x_{j}\,\,for\,\,i\neq j\}.

For the periodic
T>0
and the special parameter
\lambda_{0}
in (
1.3
)-(
1.4
), we also define a new loop space without the winding number condition as the following

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{q=(q_{1},\,q_{2},\,q_{3})\in G,\,q_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle q_{3}(t)-q_{1}(t)=\lambda_{0}(q_{2}(t)-q_{1}(t)),q(t+\frac{T}{2})=-q(t)\}.

In this paper, we choose
T
such that

\displaystyle 0<T<\Big(\frac{a(\lambda_{0})}{2b(\lambda_{0})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\pi,

(2.2)

where

\displaystyle\cases{a(\lambda_{0})=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}}{\Big[}m_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}m_{3}\lambda^{2}_{0}+m_{2}m_{3}(1-\lambda_{0})^{2}{\Big]},\cr b(\lambda_{0})=m_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}m_{3}\lambda^{-1}_{0}+m_{2}m_{3}(1-\lambda_{0})^{-1}.}

(2.3)

From
[
Zhang2018
, Pages 159-160]
, we know:

(i)

For any given positive masses
m_{1},\,m_{2},\,m_{3}
, there exists a unique
0<\lambda_{0}<1
satisfying equation (
1.4
);

(ii)

For the
\lambda_{0}
, any instant
t
and the non-collision solution
q=(q_{1},\,q_{2},\,q_{3})
, if the solution
q
satisfies that collinear condition, i.e.
q_{3}(t)-q_{1}(t)=\lambda_{0}(q_{2}(t)-q_{1}(t))
holds, then
q
is just the Euler’s central configuration at any instant.

Remark 2.1

From system (
1.1
), it is easy to see that the non-collision condition
q_{i}\neq q_{j}\,for\,i\neq j
in our new loop space
\Lambda_{2}
is natural.

Remark 2.2

Obviously, combining the above
(ii)
, the periodic and non-collision solution of system (
1.1
) in
\Lambda_{2}
, is Euler’s central configuration at any instant.

Remark 2.3

From system (
1.1
), we define the Lagrangian action on
\Lambda_{2}
:

\displaystyle f(q)=\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}|\dot{q}_{i}|^{2}+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|}\Big]dt.

(2.4)

In order to get the existence of periodic solution for system (
1.1
), a well known technique is to find the critical point of
f(q)
. Sine the new loop space
\Lambda_{2}
is not complete and has special collinear geometry structure with the ratio
\lambda_{0}
,
there is no obvious extension of the critical theory directly to this case. Thus new ideas are
needed to approach non-complete space with this special collinear geometry structure. In this paper, we will use the perturbation of the potential
V(q)=-\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{j}m_{i}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|}
to establish the new existence of non-collision solution for system (
1.1
) such that the three bodies form an Euler’s central configuration at any instant.

We are now in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 2.1

For planar 3-body problem (
1.1
) with any given positive masses
m_{1},\,m_{2},\,m_{3}
, there exists a trajectory
\tilde{q}
in the loop space
\Lambda_{2}
, which is different from the minimizer of
f(q)
on
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
, is also an Euler’s central configuration at any instant.

Remark 2.4

(i) Since
\tilde{q}\in\Lambda_{2}
, we do not need the winding number condition:
deg(q_{i}-q_{j})\neq 0\,(i\neq j)
.

(ii) For planar 3-body problem, by using a direct variational method, Zhang and Zhou
[
Zhang2004
]
proved that the minimizer of
f(q)
on
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
, is just an Euler’s central configuration at any instant. But now for planar 3-body problem, by using
a different philosophy, i.e. a new mountain pass theorem, the perturbation of Newtonian potential
V(q)
, some estimates about the Lagrangian
action and some other known results, we proved that there exists another trajectory
\tilde{q}
such that the three bodies form an Euler’s central configuration at any instant, i.e. the minimizer of
f(q)
on the suitable loop space
\overline{\Lambda_{1}}
, is not the unique solution.

Useful lemmas

In 2019, Ding, Wei and Zhang
[
Ding2019
]
obtained the following extension of the mountain pass theorem.

Lemma 3.1

[
Ding2019
, Theorem 1.4]
Let
X
be a Hilbert space,

\tilde{f}\in C^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
q^{(e)},q^{(e_{1})}\in X
and

r>0
such that
0<\|q^{(e_{1})}\|_{X}<r
and
\|q^{(e)}\|_{X}>r
, and

\tilde{f}(\theta)<\tilde{f}(q^{(e)})=\tilde{f}(q^{(e_{1})})
.
Then, for each small enough
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\hat{q}\in X
such that

(i)

\hat{c}-2\varepsilon\leqslant\tilde{f}(\hat{q})\leqslant\hat{c}+2\varepsilon
;

(ii)

\|\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\hat{q})\|_{X}<2\varepsilon
,

where
\hat{c}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\hat{\Gamma}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\tilde{f}\big(\gamma(t)\big)
and

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}:=\{\gamma\in C\big([0,1],X\big):\gamma(0)=\theta,\gamma(\frac{1}{2})=q^{(e_{1})},\gamma(1)=q^{(e)}\}.

Remark 3.1

Let
c_{0}:=\inf_{\|q\|_{X}=r}\tilde{f}(q)
and let
c_{1}:=\max\{\tilde{f}(q^{(\theta)}),\,\,\tilde{f}(q^{(e)})\}
. For the well-known mountain pass type theorems in references
[
Ambrosetti1973
,
Br1980
,
Chang1993
,
Pucci1985
,
Qi1987
,
Willem1996
]
, we see that
c_{0}\geqslant c_{1}
.
But in Lemma
3.1
, it is independent of
c_{0}
, which implies that Lemma
3.1
holds not only for
c_{0}\geqslant c_{1}
, but also holds for
c_{0}<c_{1}
.

Let
\Lambda_{2}
be given by (
2.1
) and let
\overline{\Lambda_{2}}
be the closure of
\Lambda_{2}
(with the same norm of
\Lambda_{2}
). For
q\in\Lambda_{2}
, since
q_{i}(t+\frac{T}{2})=-q_{i}(t)\,(i=1,2,3)
,
then
\int_{0}^{T}q_{i}(t)dt=0\,\,(i=1,2,3)
. With the help of the Poincaré inequality, for
q\in\Lambda_{2}
the norm in
\Lambda_{2}
can be defined by

\|q\|={\Big[}\int_{0}^{T}{\Big(}\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}|\dot{q}_{i}|^{2}{\Big)}dt{\Big]}^{1/2}.

Employing Lemma
3.1
, we have the following result which also holds without the restriction of
c_{0}\geqslant c_{1}
.

Lemma 3.2

Let
\tilde{f}\in C^{2}(\Lambda_{2},\mathbb{R})
,
q^{(\theta)},\,q^{(e)},q^{(e_{1})}\in\Lambda_{2}

and
r>0
such that
0<\|q^{(\theta)}\|<\|q^{(e_{1})}\|<r
and
\|q^{(e)}\|>r
, and

\tilde{f}(q^{(\theta)})<\tilde{f}(q^{(e)})=\tilde{f}(q^{(e_{1})})
.
Then, for each small enough
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\hat{q}\in\Lambda_{2}
such that

(i)

\hat{c}-2\varepsilon\leqslant\tilde{f}(\hat{q})\leqslant\hat{c}+2\varepsilon
;

(ii)

\|\tilde{f}^{\prime}(\hat{q})\|<2\varepsilon
,

where
\hat{c}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\hat{\Gamma}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\tilde{f}\big(\gamma(t)\big)
and

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}:=\{\gamma\in C\big([0,1],\Lambda_{2}\big):\gamma(0)=q^{(\theta)},\gamma(\frac{1}{2})=q^{(e_{1})},\gamma(1)=q^{(e)}\}.

Proof.
The proof can be followed by Lemma
3.1
. To avoid unnecessary repetition, we only give the frame of the proof.

Step 1.
From the calculations in quantitative deformation lemma
[
Ding2019
, Lemma 2.1]
(also see
[
Ambrosetti1973
,
Ambrosetti1992
]
), it is clearly to find that if we change the Hilbert space
X
into the above loop space
\Lambda_{2}
, the quantitative deformation lemma holds as well, i.e.,

Let
\Lambda_{2}
be defined as (
2.1
), and
\varepsilon
be a small enough positive number. Let

\tilde{f}\in C^{2}(\Lambda_{2},\mathbb{R})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}^{\prime}(q)\|\geqslant 2\varepsilon,\quad\forall\ q\in\tilde{f}^{-1}([s-2\varepsilon,s+2\varepsilon]).

Then there exists
\eta\in

C(\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{2})
, such that

(i)

\eta(q)=q
,
\forall\ q\notin\tilde{f}^{-1}\big([s-2\varepsilon,s+2\varepsilon]\big)\backslash D
, where
D
is any
subset of
\Lambda_{2}
satisfying

D\subset\tilde{f}^{-1}\big([s-\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon^{2},s+\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon^{2}]\big)

(ii)

\eta\big(\tilde{f}^{-1}[s+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2},s+\varepsilon^{2}]\big)\subset\tilde{f}^{-1}\big([s-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon^{2},s-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}]\big)
.

Step 2.
By

, the quantitative deformation lemma
[
Ding2019
, Lemma 2.1]
is true for
\Lambda_{2}

in Lemma
3.1
is adapted to
\Lambda_{2}

\Box

Remark 3.2

In Lemma
3.2
, if
\tilde{f}(q)
satisfies the
(P.S.)
condition in
\Lambda_{2}
, then there exists
\hat{q}\in\overline{\Lambda_{2}}

,

Since planar 3-body system (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following system

\displaystyle m_{i}\ddot{q}_{i}+\nabla_{q_{i}}V(q)=0,\quad q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\ i=1,2,3,

\displaystyle V(q)=-\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|},\ \ \ \ q=(q_{1},\,q_{2},\,q_{3}),

then in order to study the new existence of non-collision solution for planar 3-body system (
1.1
) that the three bodies form an Euler’s central configuration at any instant, firstly we consider the following system, i.e. the perturbation of fixed energy system:

\displaystyle\cases{m_{i}\ddot{q}_{i}+\nabla_{q_{i}}V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q)=0,\quad q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad i=1,2,3,\cr\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leqslant i\leqslant 3}m_{i}|\dot{q}_{i}(t)|^{2}+V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q)=h,}

(3.1)

where
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0

\displaystyle V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q)=-\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|}+\tilde{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{h}\cdot\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|^{2}}.

(3.2)

From
b(\lambda_{0})>0
, we can set
h
such that

-b(\lambda_{0})/2<h<0
. By
\|q\|={\Big[}\int_{0}^{T}{\Big(}\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_{i}|\dot{q}_{i}|^{2}{\Big)}dt{\Big]}^{1/2}
with
q_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we consider a new functional

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|q\|^{2}\int_{0}^{T}{\Big(}h-V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q){\Big)}dt

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|q\|^{2}\int_{0}^{T}{\Big(}h+\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|}-\tilde{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{h}\cdot\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q_{i}-q_{j}|^{2}}{\Big)}dt.

Observing that
\Lambda_{2}\subseteq\{q(t)=(q_{1},\,q_{2},\,q_{3}),\,q_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,q(t+\frac{T}{2})=-q(t),\,q_{i}\neq q_{j}\,\,for\,\,i\neq j\}\subseteq W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{R}^{2})

Lemma 3.3

[
Ambrosetti1992
, Lemma 1]

For any
\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
, let
q_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\in\Lambda_{2}
be such that
\varphi_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{\prime}(q_{\tilde{\varepsilon}})=0

\displaystyle\omega^{2}_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\int_{0}^{T}\nabla V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q_{\tilde{\varepsilon}})q_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}}{\|q_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\|^{2}}>0.

).

Lemma 3.4

If
q^{(n)}\rightharpoonup q
in
\Lambda_{2}
and
q\in\partial\Lambda_{2}
, then
\int_{0}^{T}V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q^{(n)})dt\rightarrow-\infty
.

Proof.
Noting that
q^{(n)}\rightharpoonup q\in\partial\Lambda_{2}\subset W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z},\,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, by Sobolev’s compact embedding theorem (or see
[
Mawhin1989
, Proposition 1.2]
), we know
q^{(n)}\rightarrow q

uniformly on
[0,T]
. Since
q\in\partial\Lambda_{2}
, there exists
t^{\ast}\in[0,T]
,
q_{l}
and
q_{r}
(
l,r\in\{1,2,3\}
) such
that
q_{l}(t^{\ast})-q_{r}(t^{\ast})=0
. Therefore, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|q^{(n)}_{l}(t)-q^{(n)}_{r}(t)|<\tilde{\varepsilon}
for all

t\in[t^{\ast}-\delta,\,t^{\ast}+\delta]
if

is large enough.

By (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}V_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(q^{(n)})dt\leqslant\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{h}\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 3}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{|q^{(n)}_{i}-q^{(n)}_{j}|^{2}}dt\leqslant\int_{t^{\ast}-\delta}^{t^{\ast}+\delta}\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{h}\frac{m_{l}m_{r}}{|q^{(n)}_{l}-q^{(n)}_{r}|^{2}}dt

(3.4)

for
h<0
.

If
q_{l}-q_{r}\equiv 0
for all
t\in[t^{\ast}-\delta,\,t^{\ast}+\delta]
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{t^{\ast}-\delta}^{t^{\ast}+\delta}\frac{m_{l}m_{r}}{|q^{(n)}_{l}(t)-q^{(n)}_{r}(t)|^{2}}dt\rightarrow+\infty,

because
q^{(n)}_{l}-q^{(n)}_{r}\rightarrow 0

uniformly on
[t^{\ast}-\delta,\,t^{\ast}+\delta]
. So we can
assume that

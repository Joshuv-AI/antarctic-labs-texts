A Toeplitz-like operator with rational matrix symbol having poles on the unit circle: Fredholm characteristics
G.J. Groenewald, S. ter Horst, J.J. Jaftha, A.C.M. Ran

Abstract
In a recent paper (Groenewald et al. Complex Anal. Oper. Theory 15:1 (2021)) we considered an unbounded Toeplitz-like operator T Ω T_{\Omega} generated by a rational matrix function Ω \Omega that has poles on the unit circle 𝕋 {\mathbb{T}} of the complex plane. A Wiener-Hopf type factorization was proved and this factorization was used to determine some Fredholm properties of the operator T Ω T_{\Omega} , including the Fredholm index. Due to the lower triangular structure (rather than diagonal) of the middle term in the Wiener-Hopf type factorization and the lack of uniqueness, it is not straightforward to determine the dimension of the kernel of T Ω T_{\Omega} from this factorization, and hence of the co-kernel, even when T Ω T_{\Omega} is Fredholm. In the current paper we provide a formula for the dimension of the kernel of T Ω T_{\Omega} under an additional assumption on the Wiener-Hopf type factorization. In the case that Ω \Omega is a 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrix function, a characterization of the kernel of the middle factor of the Wiener-Hopf type factorization is given and in many cases a formula for the dimension of the kernel is obtained. The characterization of the kernel of the middle factor for the 2 × 2 2\times 2 case is partially extended to the case of matrix functions of arbitrary size.

Introduction

The first work on unbounded Toeplitz operators goes back to the 1950s and 1960s
[
11
,
10
,
13
]
. The topic gained more momentum through a paper of Sarason
[
15
]
, who connected it to truncated Toeplitz operators
[
14
]
. The recent paper
[
1
]
of Böttcher, Garoni, and Serra-Capizzano gives insights in possible applications of Toeplitz operators with unbounded symbols. The current paper is a continuation of our earlier work on unbounded Toeplitz-like operators with rational symbols that are allowed to have poles on the unit circle. While the case of scalar rational symbols with poles on the unit circle has a fairly satisfactory theory by now (see
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
), the case of matrix valued rational symbols is only partly understood. In
[
9
]
we investigated the question when the unbounded Toeplitz-like operator is Fredholm. It transpired that the answer is, as one would expected, that Fredholmness corresponds to the rational matrix symbol having no zeroes on the unit circle. This is the same condition as in the scalar case, and the same condition as in the bounded case (i.e., when there are no poles of the rational matrix symbol on the unit circle). In addition, a formula for the Fredholm index was given in
[
9
]
, which was based on a Wiener-Hopf type factorization (see Theorem
1.1
below). For a more complete picture, one would also want to have formulas or the dimension of the kernel and codimension of the range of the Toeplitz-like operator in case the operator is Fredholm. However, it seems that these are not easily determined from the Wiener-Hopf type factorization.

The goal of the current paper is to remedy that situation, at least partly, in the following manner. We shall identify a condition on the factors in the Wiener-Hopf type factorization that allows us to provide formulas for the dimension of the kernel and the codimension of the range in case the Toeplitz-like operator is Fredholm. In addition, we analyse in detail the case of
2\times 2
matrix functions, in which case we characterize the kernel of the Toeplitz-like operator corresponding to the ‘middle term’ in the Wiener-Hopf type factorization, leading to a formula of the dimension of the kernel of the original Toeplitz-like operator in many cases. Finally, this characterization is partially extended to the general case.

The space of

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Omega})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f\in H^{p}_{m}:&\begin{array}[]{l}\Omega f=h+\eta\textrm{ where }h\in L^{p}_{m}({\mathbb{T}}),\\
\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{T}})\\
\end{array}\\
\end{array}\right\},

(1.1)

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}f={\mathbb{P}}h\textrm{ with }{\mathbb{P}}\textrm{ the Riesz projection of }L^{p}_{m}({\mathbb{T}})\textrm{ onto }H^{p}_{m}.

By the Riesz projection,
{\mathbb{P}}
, we mean the projection of
L^{p}_{m}
onto
H^{p}_{m}
due to M. Riesz, see for example pages 149–153 in
[

[
4
]

Recall that an
m\times m
rational matrix function is called a

if it has no poles in the closed unit disk
{\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}}
and it is said to be a
minus function
if it has no poles outside the open unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
, with the point at infinity included.
In the case where
\Omega(z)
has no poles on the unit circle, it is known that
T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm if and only if
\Omega(z)
has no zeroes on the unit circle. In that case
the function
\Omega(z)
can be factorized as

\Omega(z)=\Omega_{-}(z)D(z)\Omega_{+}(z),

where
\Omega_{-}
and
\Omega_{-}^{-1}
are minus functions,
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{+}^{-1}
are plus functions and
D
is a diagonal matrix with monomial entries on the diagonal:
D(z)={\operatorname{Diag}\,}(z^{\kappa_{j}})_{j=1}^{n}
, where we may arrange the integers
\kappa_{j}
such that they are in decreasing order:
\kappa_{1}\geq\kappa_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\kappa_{n}
, see, e.g.,
[
2
]
. This Wiener-Hopf factorization plays an important role in the study of the Toeplitz operator
T_{\Omega}
because of the following relation between the factorization of the rational matrix function and a factorization of the operator:
T_{\Omega}=T_{\Omega_{-}}T_{D}T_{\Omega_{+}}
, and moreover,
T_{\Omega_{-}}
and
T_{\Omega_{+}}
are invertible, with inverses given by, respectively,
T^{-1}_{\Omega_{-}}=T_{\Omega_{-}^{-1}}
and
T^{-1}_{\Omega_{+}}=T_{\Omega_{+}^{-1}}
. As a consequence, the Fredholm properties of
T_{\Omega}
and
T_{D}
coincide. To be precise, the dimension of the kernel and the codimension of the range of
T_{\Omega}
can be read off from the
Wiener-Hopf indices
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}
as follows:

{\rm dim\ Ker\,}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{\{j\mid\kappa_{j}<0\}}\kappa_{j},\qquad{\rm codim\ Ran\,}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{\{j\mid\kappa_{j}>0\}}\kappa_{j}.

Although the factors
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
in the Wiener-Hopf factorization are not uniquely determined by
\Omega
, the middle factor
D
is, at least in case the diagonal entries are ordered decreasingly, and hence so are the Wiener-Hopf indices.

In Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 of
[
9
]
we considered the case where
\Omega
has poles on the unit circle. The following weaker version of the Wiener-Hopf factorization was proved.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with
\det\Omega\not\equiv 0
. Then

\Omega(z)=z^{-k}\Omega_{-}(z)\Omega_{0}(z)P_{0}(z)\Omega_{+}(z),

(1.2)

for some

,
\Omega_{+},\Omega_{0},\Omega_{-}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
and
P_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{m\times m}
such that

(i)

\Omega_{-}
and
\Omega_{-}^{-1}
are minus functions;

(ii)

\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{+}^{-1}
are plus functions;

(iii)

\Omega_{0}=\operatorname{Diag}_{j=1}^{m}(\phi_{j})
with each
\phi_{j}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}({\mathbb{T}})
having zeroes and poles only on
{\mathbb{T}}
;

(iv)

P_{0}
is lower triangular with
\det(P_{0}(z))=z^{N}
for some integer
N\geq 0
.

In fact, Theorem 1.2 in
[
9
]
, gives more restrictions on the middle factor

\Xi(z):=z^{-k}\Omega_{0}(z)P_{0}(z),

(1.3)

making it possible to have
\Omega_{0}
uniquely determined. Note that the diagonal entries of

have to be of the form
z^{k_{j}}
,

for some integers

.
It was also shown in examples in
[
9
]
, Section 7, that the middle factor is not unique, in particular, the

’s are not unique.

Despite this non-uniqueness, the Wiener-Hopf type factorization is useful in studying the Fredholm properties of the operator
T_{\Omega}
. Indeed, we have that

is Fredholm if and only if
T_{\Xi}
is Fredholm, and their indices coincide, as well as the dimensions of the kernels and codimensions of the range. In the next theorem we summarize the results of Theorem 1.3 and 1.4 in

]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with
\det\Omega\not\equiv 0
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) as in Theorem
1.1
with
\Omega_{0}=\operatorname{Diag}_{j=1}^{m}(s_{j}/q_{j})
with
s_{j},q_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}
co-prime with roots only on
{\mathbb{T}}
and
z^{k_{j}}
the
j
-th diagonal entry of
P_{0}
, for
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Then the following hold

(i)

T_{\Omega}=T_{\Omega_{-}}T_{z^{-k}}T_{\Omega_{0}}T_{P_{0}}T_{\Omega_{+}}
,

(ii)

T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm if and only if
T_{\Omega_{0}}
is Fredholm, which happens exactly when
s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}
are all constant,

(iii)

in case
T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Index}(T_{\Omega})

\displaystyle=mk+\operatorname{Index}(T_{\Omega_{0}})+\operatorname{Index}(T_{P_{0}})

\displaystyle=mk+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\deg(q_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}.

Unfortunately, due to the non-uniqueness and lower triangular form, rather than diagonal, of the middle term
\Xi
, it is not straightforward how the dimension of the kernel and the codimension of the range of
T_{\Omega}
can be determined from the Wiener-Hopf type factorization, which is possible in the case when there are no poles on the unit circle, due to the simpler nature of the Wiener-Hopf factorization in that case.

In this paper we shall determine the dimension of the kernel of
T_{\Omega}
in certain special cases where the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
can be described and its dimension can be computed. Note that in the Wiener-Hopf type factorization, the middle factor
\Xi(z):=z^{-k}\Omega_{0}(z)P_{0}(z)
has the form

\displaystyle\Xi(z)=z^{-k}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{p_{11}(z)}{q_{1}(z)}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\frac{p_{21}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}&\frac{p_{22}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
\frac{p_{m1}(z)}{q_{m}(z)}&\frac{p_{m2}(z)}{q_{m}(z)}&\cdots&\frac{p_{m(m-1)}(z)}{q_{m}(z)}&\frac{p_{mm}(z)}{q_{m}(z)}\end{pmatrix}

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{$p_{ij},q_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}$, $q_{i}$ has zeroes only on ${\mathbb{T}}$, $p_{ii}(z)=z^{k_{i}}$ for $1\leq j\leq i\leq m$}.

The construction in
[

gives that, in addition, it is possible to arrange

\deg(p_{ij})<k_{i},\ 1\leq j\leq i\leq m\quad\mbox{and}\quad q_{i}\mbox{ divides }q_{i+1},\ 1\leq i<m,

Definition 1.3
.

A Wiener-Hopf type factorization (

In the next theorem we state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega(z)\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
be a rational matrix function with poles on the unit circle
{\mathbb{T}}
given by a Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) that is in special form, that is,
\Xi(z)=z^{-k}\Omega_{0}(z)P_{0}(z)

\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k+\deg(q_{j})-k_{j},0).

Furthermore, if in addition
\Omega(z)
has no zeroes on the unit circle, then
T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm, and

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k,0).

In Theorem 1.4, we obtain the dimension of the kernel without assuming Fredholmness, but for the codimension of the range we assume Fredholmness, and then use the formula for the index. However, in the scalar case, when the operator is not Fredholm, we still get that the complement of the closure of the range is finite dimensional. It is unknown to us whether the formula for the codimension of the range of
T_{\Omega}
can also be proved without assuming Fredholmness.

The numbers

\kappa_{j}:=k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k,\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

that appear in the formulas for the dimensions of the kernel and cokernel of the Fredholm operator
T_{\Omega}
in Theorem
1.4
are called the factorization indices of
T_{\Omega}
. Using arguments similar to the proof for the classical case, we show that factorization indices are uniquely determined by

, in Proposition

To illustrate the complications that occur in determining the dimension of the kernel of
T_{\Omega}
, we analyse the case of
2\times 2
matrix functions in detail. In that case, without further assumptions on the matrix function, from a Wiener-Hopf type factorization we give descriptions of the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
, where
\Xi
is the middle factor of the Wiener-Hopf type factorization, and use this description to provide a formula for the dimension of the kernel of
T_{\Omega}
in many cases, and give an upper bound in the one case where we do not obtain a formula for the dimension of
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}
. It is also shown that most of these do not correspond to a Wiener-Hopf type factorization that is in special form. The description of the kernel of

can partially be extended to the general case. However, given the many cases that already appear in the case of

Some analysis of the middle factor
\Xi

In this section we provide some analysis of the Toeplitz-like operator of the middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) of the Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
), specified further as in (
1.4
) and with the conditions (
1.5
) in place. We start with some general results, after which we prove Theorem
1.4
. Before turning to the kernel, we first provide a formula for the domain of

, which can be viewed as the matrix analogue of the first part of Lemma 2.3 of

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on

and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (

in (
1.3

\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})=\left\{f\in H^{p}_{m}\colon\!\!\begin{array}[]{l}\Xi f=z^{-k}h+\eta\mbox{ with $h\in H^{p}_{m},\,\eta\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{T}})$}\\
\mbox{where $\eta_{j}=\frac{r_{j}}{q_{j}}$, $\deg(r_{j})<\deg(q_{j})$}\end{array}\right\}.

(2.1)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof in the scalar case
[
6
, Lemma 2.3]
. We will provide a sketch of the proof for completeness. It is clear from the definition of the domain of
T_{\Xi}
as in (
1.1
), that the right hand-side of (
2.1
) is contained in
\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})
. Hence it remains to prove the reverse inclusion. Assume

is in
\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})
, that is,
\Xi f=g+\eta
for some
g\in L^{p}_{m}
and
\eta\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{T}})
. Consider the
j
-th row of
\Xi f=g+\eta
:

\displaystyle z^{-k}\frac{p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{jj}(z)f_{j}(z)}{q_{j}(z)}=g_{j}(z)+\eta_{j}(z).

(2.2)

Write
q_{j}\eta_{j}=\rho_{j}+r_{j}
for
\rho_{j}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}({\mathbb{T}})
and
r_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\deg(q_{j})-1}
and multiply both sides of (
2.2
) with

. This yields

z^{-k}(p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{jj}(z)f_{j}(z))=q_{j}(z)g_{j}(z)+\rho_{j}(z)+r_{j}(z).

Note that all elements apart from
\rho_{j}
are in

, which means that
\rho_{j}
must be in

too. However,
{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}({\mathbb{T}})\cap L^{p}=\{0\}

. Replacing

p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{jj}(z)f_{j}(z)=z^{k}q_{j}(z)g_{j}(z)+z^{k}r_{j}(z).

Now observe that all elements apart from

,
which gives that
\Xi(z)f(z)=z^{-k}v(z)+\rho(z)
with
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})^{T}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k-1}^{m}

Now consider the
j
-th entries on both sides of the identity
\Xi(z)f(z)=z^{-k}v(z)+\rho(z)
and multiply with
z^{k}q_{j}(z)
. This yields

p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{jj}(z)f_{j}(z)=v_{j}(z)+z^{k}r_{j}(z),\quad\mbox{with}\quad p_{jj}(z)=z^{k_{j}}.

Since
f_{1},\ldots,f_{j-1},p_{j1},\ldots p_{j(j-1)},r_{j}
and
v_{j}
are polynomials, it is clear that
p_{jj}f_{j}
must be a polynomial too. Moreover, since
p_{jj}(z)=z^{k_{j}}
, we have

f_{j}(z)=\frac{z^{k}r_{j}(z)-(p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{j(j-1)}(z)f_{j-1}(z))}{z^{k_{j}}}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}\cap H^{p}.

The fact that
f_{j}\in H^{p}
, means that
f_{j}
cannot have a pole at
0
, so that
z^{k_{j}}
must be a factor of
z^{k}r_{j}(z)-(p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{j(j-1)}(z)f_{j-1}(z))
. That implies that
f_{j}
is in
{\mathcal{P}}
with

\deg(f_{j})\leq\max(\deg(p_{j1}f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(p_{j(j-1)}f_{j-1}),\deg(r_{j})+k)-k_{j}.

Note that
\deg(r_{j})<\deg(q_{j})
and

\displaystyle\deg(p_{ji}f_{i})=\deg(p_{ji})+\deg(f_{i})<k_{j}+\deg(f_{i}).

Hence

\deg(f_{j})<\max(\deg(f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(f_{j-1}),\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j})

as claimed. Thus the first inequality of (
2.3
) holds.

It remains to prove the second inequality of (
2.3
). For
j=1
it follows immediately that this holds, in fact, with equality. For
j>1
it remains to show that

\displaystyle\max(\deg(f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(f_{j-1}))\leq

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\max(\deg(q_{1})-k_{1}-1,\ldots,\deg(q_{j-1})-k_{j-1}-1)+k,

(2.4)

where by assumption the inequality holds if
j
is replaced by
i=1,\ldots,j-1
. Applying the first inequality in (
2.3
) to
f_{j-1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\max(\deg(f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(f_{j-2}),\deg(f_{j-1}))\leq

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\max(\deg(f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(f_{j-2}),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\max(\deg(f_{1})-1,\cdots,\deg(f_{j-2})-1,\deg(q_{j-1})+k-k_{j-1}-1))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\max(\deg(f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(f_{j-2}),\deg(q_{j-1})+k-k_{j-1}-1).

Now simply use the upper bound on
\max(\deg(f_{1}),\ldots,\deg(f_{j-2}))
from the induction hypothesis to conclude that (
2.4
) holds, and hence the second inequality in (
2.3
).
∎

Since the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
is contained in
{\mathcal{P}}^{m}
, it follows that the scalar entries in
f\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
are in the domain of the Toeplitz operators of the scalar entries of
\Xi
, because by
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]

{\mathcal{P}}
is always contained in their domain. As a consequence, we can formulate the kernel condition of
T_{\Xi}
in terms of the Toeplitz-like operators corresponding to the scalar entries of
\Xi
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) in the form (
1.4
) with conditions (
1.5
) in place. For
f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})^{T}\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
we have

0=T_{\Xi}f=\begin{pmatrix}T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{11}}{q_{1}}}f_{1}\\
T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{21}}{q_{2}}}f_{1}+T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{22}}{q_{2}}}f_{2}\\
\vdots\\
T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{m1}}{q_{m}}}f_{1}+\cdots+T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{m(m-1)}}{q_{m}}}f_{m-1}+T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{mm}}{q_{m}}}f_{m}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Since
f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{m})^{T}\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that each
f_{j}
is in
{\mathcal{P}}
and hence the right-hand side of the formula is well defined, because
\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\frac{p_{ij}}{q_{i}}})
contains
{\mathcal{P}}
for all
1\leq i\leq j\leq m
by
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
. That the identity holds is because
H^{p}
,
K^{p}:={\overline{\textup{span}}}\{e^{ikt}\colon k\in\{-1,-2,\ldots\}\}\subset L^{p}
and
{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}({\mathbb{T}})
are vector spaces. Indeed, for the
k
-th entry one needs to inspect

z^{-k}\left(\frac{p_{k1}(z)}{q_{k}(z)}f_{1}(z)+\cdots+\frac{p_{kk}(z)}{q_{k}(z)}f_{k}(z)\right).

However, since for each
j
,
f_{j}
is in the domain of
T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{kj}}{q_{k}}}
, the
j
-th summand
z^{-k}\frac{p_{kj}(z)}{q_{k}(z)}f_{j}(z)
is equal to
g_{j}+h_{j}+\rho_{j}
with
g_{j}\in K^{p}
,
h_{j}\in H^{p}
,
\rho_{j}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}({\mathbb{T}})
all uniquely determined and with
h_{j}=T_{z^{-k}\frac{p_{kj}}{q_{k}}}f_{j}
. The sum itself then also comes in the form of a sum of an element
g_{1}+\cdots+g_{k}\in K^{p}
,
h_{1}+\cdots+h_{k}\in H^{p}
and
\rho_{1}+\cdots+\rho_{k}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}({\mathbb{T}})
and the function
h_{1}+\cdots+h_{k}
corresponds to the
k
-th entry in
T_{\Xi}f
while also corresponding to the
k
-th entry in the right-hand side of the equation. Hence the identity follows.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.4
and determination of the factorization indices

We begin this section with a proof of the first part of Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) in the form (
1.4
) with conditions (
1.5
) in place. Assume that the factorization of
\Omega
is in special form (as in Definition
1.3
). Then

\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}=\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k+\deg(q_{j})-k_{j},0).

In fact,

\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\oplus_{j=1}^{m}{\mathcal{P}}_{k+\deg(q_{j})-k_{j}-1}.

Proof.

Let
\Xi
be the middle factor in the Wiener-Hopf type factorization of
\Omega
, given by (
1.3
)-(
1.4
). By assumption, the conditions of Definition
1.3
hold, in particular, we have (
1.6
). The result follows once we have proved the formula for
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
, which we will prove by induction. More precisely, we will prove by induction that for
f=(f_{1}\ldots,f_{m})^{T}\in\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})
the first
l
entries of
T_{\Xi}f
are zero if and only if
f_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k+\deg(q_{j})-k_{j}-1}
for
j=1,\ldots,l
. To this end, let
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})^{T}\in\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})
, so that we have
\Xi(z)f(z)=h(z)+z^{-k}v(z)+\rho(z)
with
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})^{T}\in H_{m}^{p}
,
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})^{T}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{m}_{k-1}
and
\rho=(r_{1}/q_{1},\ldots,r_{m}/q_{m})^{T}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{T}})
, i.e., with
r_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\deg(q_{j})-1}
, according to Lemma
2.1
. Now assume
l\leq m
such that the claim holds for
j=1,\ldots,l-1
.

First assume that the first
l
entries of
T_{\Xi}f
are zero, i.e.,
h_{1}=\cdots=h_{l}=0
. In particular, since the first
l-1
entries are zero, we know that

\deg(f_{j})<\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,l-1
, by the induction assumption. Multiplying the
l
-th component of
\Xi(z)f(z)=h(z)+z^{-k}v(z)+\rho(z)
with
z^{k}q_{l}(z)
yields

p_{l1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{ll}(z)f_{l}(z)=q_{l}(z)v_{l}(z)+z^{k}r_{l}(z).

(3.1)

Since
\deg(r_{l})<\deg(q_{l})
and
\deg(v_{l})<k
, we have

\deg(q_{l}(z)v_{l}(z)+z^{k}r_{l}(z))<\deg(q_{l})+k.

The induction assumption gives
\deg(f_{j})<\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j}
. Together with the condition (
1.6
), for
j=1,\ldots,l-1
this implies that

\deg(p_{lj}f_{j})=\deg(p_{lj})+\deg(f_{j})<\deg(p_{lj})+\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j}\leq\deg(q_{l})+k.

Thus, the right hand-side and the first
l-1
summands in the left-hand side of (
3.1
) are all polynomials of degree less than
\deg(q_{l})+k
. Then also
p_{ll(z)}f_{l}(z)=z^{k_{l}}f_{l}(z)
must be a polynomial of degree less than
\deg(q_{l})+k
, which implies that
f_{l}\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
\deg(f_{l})<\deg(q_{l})+k-k_{l}
.

Conversely, assume that
\deg(f_{j})<\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,l
. The recursion assumption implies that the first
l-1
entries of
T_{\Xi}f
are zero. Again, multiplying the
l
-th component of
\Xi(z)f(z)=h(z)+z^{-k}v(z)+\rho(z)
with
z^{k}q_{l}(z)
yields

p_{l1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{ll}(z)f_{l}(z)=z^{k}q_{l}(z)h_{l}(z)+q_{l}(z)v(z)+z^{k}r_{l}(z).

(3.2)

Since
p_{ll}(z)=z^{k_{l}}
and
\deg(f_{l})<\deg(q_{l})+k-k_{j}
, we have
\deg(p_{ll}f_{l})<\deg(q_{l})+k
. As above,
\deg(q_{l}(z)v_{l}(z)+z^{k}r_{l}(z))<\deg(q_{l})+k
and from (
1.6
) we again obtain that
\deg(p_{lj}f_{j})<\deg(q_{l})+k
. The identity then implies that
z^{k}q_{l}(z)h_{l}(z)\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
\deg(z^{k}q_{l}h_{l})<\deg(q_{l})+k
. However, that can only happen when
h_{l}=0
, which implies that the
l
-th entry in
T_{\Xi}f
is zero as well. Since the statement holds for
l=1,\ldots,m
, the proof is complete.
∎

In addition to the first claim of Theorem
1.4
, in the above proposition we have also shown that the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
splits as the direct sum of
m
subspaces of
H^{p}
in the case that the factorization of
\Omega
is in special form. This, however, does not imply that the domain of
T_{\Xi}
splits in a similar fashion.

Next we prove the second claim of Theorem
1.4
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) in the form (
1.4
) with conditions (
1.5
) in place. Assume that the factorization of
\Omega
is in special form (as in Definition
1.3
)and that
T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm. Then

\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k+\deg(q_{j})-k_{j},0)

and

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k,0).

Proof.

The formula for
\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}
was already established in Proposition
3.1
. Since
T_{\Omega}
is assumed to be Fredholm, according to Theorem 1.4 in
[
9
]
, the Fredholm index is given by

\operatorname{Index}T_{\Omega}=\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}-\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}T_{\Omega}=km+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\deg(q_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}.

Using the formula for
\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}
from Proposition
3.1
it then follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}T_{\Omega}=\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}-\operatorname{Index}T_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k+\deg(q_{j})-k_{j},0)-(km+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\deg(q_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\max(k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k,0).\qed

The numbers

\kappa_{j}:=k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k,\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

(3.3)

that appear in the formulas for the dimensions of the kernel and cokernel of the Fredholm operator
T_{\Omega}
are called the
factorization indices
of
T_{\Omega}
. Note that we can now write

\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\leq 0}-\kappa_{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}T_{\Omega}=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq 0}\kappa_{j}.

Although the Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) of
\Omega
is far from unique, following an argument on page 590 in
[
5
]
, we can show that factorization indices are uniquely determined by
T_{\Omega}
, provided that we can find a Wiener-Hopf type factorization in special form.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) in the form (
1.4
). Assume that the factorization of
\Omega
is in special form (as in Definition
1.3
) and that
T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm. Let
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{m}
be the factorization indices given by (
3.3
). Then for each integer
\kappa
we have

\displaystyle\#\{j\colon\kappa_{j}=\kappa\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}(T_{z^{1-\kappa}\Omega})-2\,\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}(T_{z^{-\kappa}\Omega})+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}(T_{z^{-\kappa-1}\Omega}).

Proof.

For
\kappa\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, set
\Omega_{\kappa}(z):=z^{-\kappa}\Omega(z)\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
. Since
T_{\Omega}
is Fredholm, by assumption, so is
T_{\Omega_{\kappa}}
, since no zeroes on
{\mathbb{T}}
are introduced. Moreover,
\Omega_{\kappa}
has a Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with the same plus function
\Omega_{+}(z)
and minus function
\Omega_{-}(z)
and with middle term
z^{-\kappa}\Xi(z)
. Note that the Wiener-Hopf type factorization of
\Omega_{\kappa}
is also in special form and has factorization indices

{\widetilde{\kappa}}_{j}=k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k-\kappa,\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Hence, we have

d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa}}):=\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Ran}T_{\Omega_{\kappa}}=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa}\kappa_{j}-\kappa=\sum_{\kappa_{j}>\kappa}\kappa_{j}-\kappa=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1}\kappa_{j}-\kappa.

This implies that

\displaystyle d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa}})-d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa+1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa}\kappa_{j}-\kappa-\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1}\kappa_{j}-\kappa-1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1}\kappa_{j}-\kappa-\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1}\kappa_{j}-\kappa-1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1}1=\#\{j\colon\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1\}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\#\{j\colon\kappa_{j}=\kappa\}

\displaystyle=\#\{j\colon\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa\}-\#\{j\colon\kappa_{j}\geq\kappa+1\}

\displaystyle=(d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa-1}})-d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa}}))-(d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa}})-d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa+1}}))

\displaystyle=d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa-1}})-2d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa}}))+d(T_{\Omega_{\kappa+1}}),

which proves our claim.
∎

4
The
2\times 2
case

In this section we consider the general
2\times 2
case. Hence, let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{2\times 2}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
with a Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) of the form (
1.4
), that is

\Xi(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{11}(z)&0\\
\xi_{21}(z)&\xi_{22}(z)\end{pmatrix}=z^{-k}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{z^{k_{1}}}{q_{1}(z)}&0\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{z^{k_{21}}d_{21}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}&\frac{z^{k_{2}}}{q_{2}(z)}\end{pmatrix},

(4.1)

where we also make the assumption in (
1.5
):

k_{21}+\deg(d_{21})<k_{2},\quad d_{21}(0)\not=0,\quad q_{1}|q_{2}.

(4.2)

We do not assume that the Wiener-Hopf type factorization of
\Omega
is in special form, but we will discuss this case in Corollary
4.4
below.

As explained in the general case,
\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Omega}=\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
, hence it suffices to analyse the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
. Using Corollary
2.3
we can make a few direct observations regarding
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
:

(i)

If
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{11}}=\{0\}
, or, equivalently,
k_{1}\geq\deg(q_{1})+k
, then

\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\{0\}\oplus\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}=\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{11}}\oplus\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}.

(ii)

It is always the case that
\{0\}\oplus\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}\subset\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
. However, if
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}\neq\{0\}
, or, equivalently,
k_{2}<\deg(q_{2})+k
, then
T_{\xi_{22}}
is surjective and with an appropriate choice in a complement of
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}
one can always make the second row zero, so that

\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{11}}\oplus\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}.

If
k_{2}=\deg(q_{2})+k
, then
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{22}}=\{0\}
but
T_{\xi_{22}}
is still surjective and the same argument still leads to the same formula for
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
.

In both cases,
k_{1}\geq\deg(q_{1})+k
and
k_{2}\leq\deg(q_{2})+k
, we obtain

\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\max(\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1},0)+\max(\deg(q_{2})+k-k_{2},0),

in correspondence with the result for the case where
\Xi
has a factorization in special form.

Next we present a general result that expresses the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
in terms of the kernel of a certain compressed Toeplitz matrix. To express the result we make an identification between
{\mathcal{P}}_{l-1}
and
{\mathbb{C}}^{l}
via the following notation:

\vec{g}=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{l-1})^{T}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{l}\mbox{ for }g(z)=g_{0}+g_{1}z+\cdots+g_{l-1}z^{l-1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{l-1}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Xi(z)
be given by (
4.1
) such that (
4.2
) is satisfied. For all
f=(f_{1},f_{2})^{T}\in H^{p}_{2}
the following are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
;

(ii)

f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that

\displaystyle\deg(f_{1})<\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\qquad\deg(d_{21}(z)f_{1}(z)+z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}f_{2}(z))<\deg(q_{2})+k-k_{21};

(iii)

one of the following two cases occurs:

(iii.a)

if
k_{1}\geq\deg(q_{1})+k
or
k_{2}\leq\deg(q_{2})+k
, then
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\deg(f_{1})<\deg(g_{1})+k-k_{1}
and
\deg(f_{2})<\deg(g_{2})+k-k_{2}
;

(iii.b)

if
k_{1}<\deg(q_{1})+k
and
k_{2}>\deg(q_{2})+k
, let
M
and
N
be the compressions of the Toeplitz operator of
d_{21}
obtained by restricting to the first
\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}
columns, in both cases, and selecting rows

\max(\deg(q_{2})+k-k_{21},0)+1\quad\mbox{to}\quad k_{2}-k_{21}\quad\mbox{for $M$},

and rows

k_{2}-k_{21}+1\quad\mbox{to}\quad\max(k_{2}-k_{21},\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}+\deg(d_{21}))\quad\mbox{for $N$},

with
N
to be interpreted as vacuous if the upper bound is less than the lower bound, then
f_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\deg(f_{1})<\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}
and
M\vec{f}_{1}=0
and
f_{2}
is uniquely determined by
\vec{f}_{2}=-N\vec{f}_{1}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that there is a certain dichotomy between the two subcases (iii.a) and (iii.b). In the first there is some freedom in
f_{2}
(provided
k_{2}<\deg(q_{2})
) but the second condition in (ii) does not put any further constraint on
f_{1}
, while in the second subcase
f_{1}
is further constrained by the condition
M\vec{f}_{1}=0
, but
f_{2}
is uniquely determined by the choice of
f_{1}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

First we prove the equivalence of (i) and (ii). Note that, by Lemma
2.2
,
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
is contained in
{\mathcal{P}}^{2}
, so that in both (i) and (ii) we have that
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}
. In particular,
f\in\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})
in both cases, so we may assume this to be the case. Then, by Lemma
2.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Xi(z)f(z)=h(z)+z^{-k}v(z)+\rho(z)\mbox{ with}

\displaystyle h=\begin{pmatrix}h_{1}\\
h_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in H^{p}_{2},\,v=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{2}_{k-1},\,\rho=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{1}\\
\rho_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}/q_{1}\\
r_{2}/q_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}({\mathbb{T}}),

and it follows that
f\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
precisely when
h=0
. Writing out the identity in the first row it follows that we get
h_{1}=0
if and only if
f_{1}\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\xi_{11}}
, that is,
\deg(f_{1})<\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}
. Considering the second row, it follows that
h_{2}=0
if and only if

\frac{z^{k_{12}}d_{21}(z)}{z^{k}q_{2}(z)}f_{1}(z)+\frac{z^{k_{2}}}{z^{k}q_{2}(z)}f_{2}(z)=\frac{v_{2}(z)}{z^{k}}+\frac{r_{2}(z)}{q_{2}(z)}

for some
r_{2},v_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
\deg(r_{2})<\deg(q_{2})
and
\deg(v_{2})<k
. Multiplying by
z^{k}q_{2}(z)
on both sides and factoring out
z^{k_{21}}
on the left side, we see that
h_{1}=0
is equivalent to

z^{k_{21}}(d_{21}(z)f_{1}(z)+z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}f_{2}(z))=q_{2}(z)v_{2}(z)+z^{k}r_{2}(z),

. Note that

are co-prime. Therefore, ranging over

and

. As a result, we see that

if and only if

or, what is the same,

\deg(d_{21}(z)f_{1}(z)+z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}f_{2}(z))<\deg(q_{2})+k-k_{21}.

This proves that (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Next we prove the equivalent of (ii) and (iii). The cases

to the first

. Hence

and
N

is larger than both

and
\deg(q_{2})+k
. Note that after the
\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}+\deg(d_{21})
-th row,

only has zero-rows. Set

. Then
\deg(g)<\deg(d_{21})+\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}
. For

(\vec{f}_{2})_{1}
for the first

\displaystyle\max(k_{2}-k_{21},\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}+\deg(d_{21}))-(k_{2}-k_{21})=

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\max(0,\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}+\deg(d_{21})+k_{21}-k_{2})

entries of

(\vec{f}_{2})_{2}

\overrightarrow{d_{21}(z)f_{1}(z)+z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}f_{2}(z)}=\vec{g}+\overrightarrow{z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}f_{2}(z)}=\begin{pmatrix}L\vec{f}_{1}\\
M\vec{f_{1}}\\
N\vec{f}_{1}+(\vec{f}_{2})_{1}\\

Now it follows that
\deg(d_{21}(z)f_{1}(z)+z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}f_{2}(z))<\deg(q_{2})-k_{21}

N\vec{f}_{1}+(\vec{f}_{2})_{1}=0
and

and
\vec{f}_{2}=(\vec{f}_{2})_{1}=-N\vec{f}_{1}

It follows from statement (iii) that the dimension of

Proof.

As observed in Proposition

). Since

.

Next we prove the claim of case (iii). In addition to the number
j_{1}
defined in the first part of the proof, set

. Note that the assumption in case (i) can be rewritten as

, while case (ii) corresponds to

and

. Moreover, the restrictions in case (iii) can be reformulated as:

j_{2}>0,\,j_{1}>0\quad\mbox{and in addition}\quad\deg(d_{21})\leq j_{2}\mbox{ or }\deg(d_{21})\leq j_{1}

(4.5)

and the subcases as

(iii.a):
j_{1}\geq\deg(d_{21})\geq j_{2}
, (iii.b):
j_{1}\geq\deg(d_{21})\leq j_{2}

Since we assume

j_{1}=\deg(q_{2})+k-k_{21}
.

.

Note further that
j_{2}>0

with

for

,

then there are zeroes in the right upper corner of

\displaystyle\textup{rank}\,M

\displaystyle\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\dim\operatorname{Ker}M=\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}-\textup{rank}\,M

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\max(k_{2}-k_{21}-\deg(d_{21}),\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}-j_{1}-1)

\displaystyle\quad=\min(\deg(d_{21})-j_{2},j_{1}+1)

\displaystyle\quad=\min(\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}+\deg(d_{21})+k_{21}-k_{2},k_{21}-\deg(q_{2})+k+1),

in case (iv).

Now we consider the various cases of Proposition
4.3
. The fact that
j_{2}>\deg(d_{21})
and

, excludes the cases (i) (

j_{1}=0
), (iii.a) (
j_{1}\geq\deg(d_{21})\geq j_{2}>0
), (iii.c) (
j_{1}\leq\deg(d_{21})\leq j_{2}>0
) and (iv) (
j_{1}<\deg(d_{21})>j_{2}
). Hence only case (iii.b) (
j_{1}\geq\deg(d_{21})\leq j_{2}>0
) can occur. The special form condition tells us precisely that
\dim\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}=\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1}
, as claimed.
∎

As can be deduced from Corollary
4.4
, even in the
2\times 2
case, there are many cases where the Wiener-Hopf type factorization is not in special form. However, the middle term
\Xi
of Wiener-Hopf type factorization is not uniquely determined by
\Omega
, and so it may well be that there is another Wiener-Hopf type factorization that is in special form. To conclude this section, we consider the case that for
\Omega
we can find a Wiener-Hopf type factorization where all the numerators are monomial (the monomial case), that is, the middle factor
\Xi
in (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) has
d_{21}
constant, say
d_{21}(z)\equiv 1
. Note that in the case
\deg(d_{21})=0
, both cases in Proposition
4.1
(iii.a) can occur, while from Proposition
4.3
only cases (i), (ii) and (iii.b) can occur.

It turns out that in that case, if the Wiener-Hopf type factorization is in monomial form but not in special form, one can always find a Wiener-Hopf type factorization that is diagonal, and hence in special form.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{2\times 2}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
. Assume that
\Omega
admits a Wiener-Hopf type factorization in monomial form, i.e., with middle term
\Xi
as in (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) such that
\deg(d_{21})=0
. The
\Omega
admits a Wiener-Hopf type factorization in special form.

Proof.

Let
\Xi
as in (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) be the middle factor of a monomial Wiener-Hopf type factorization of
\Omega
, i.e., with
\deg(d_{21})=0
. Note that the Wiener-Hopf type factorization is in special form when

k_{1}-\deg(q_{1})\geq k_{21}-\deg(q_{2}).

If that is the case, then we are done, so we only have to consider the case where
k_{1}-\deg(q_{1})<k_{21}-\deg(q_{2})
. Since
q_{1}
is a factor of
q_{2}
, we can write
q_{2}=q_{1}r_{1}
for some
r_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}
with
\deg(p_{1})=\deg(q_{2})-\deg(q_{1})
. Hence
\deg(p_{1})<k_{21}-k_{1}
. Now set

\Omega_{-}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&p_{1}(z)z^{k_{1}-k_{21}}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Omega_{+}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}-z^{k_{2}-k_{21}}&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\Omega_{+}(z)
is a plus function, with plus function inverse, because
k_{21}<k_{2}
. Moreover,
\Omega_{-}(z)
is a minus function with minus function inverse, since the non-special form assumption makes the non-constant term a strictly proper rational function with poles only at
0
. We have

z^{k}\Omega_{-}(z)\Xi(z)\Omega_{+}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{z^{k_{1}+k_{2}-k_{21}}}{q_{1}(z)}\\
\frac{z^{k_{21}}}{q_{2}(z)}&\frac{z^{k_{2}}}{q_{2}(z)}\end{pmatrix}\Omega_{+}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{z^{k_{1}+k_{2}-k_{21}}}{q_{1}(z)}&0\\
0&\frac{z^{k_{21}}}{q_{2}(z)}\end{pmatrix}.

This shows that
\Omega
also has a Wiener-Hopf type factorization with middle term in diagonal form, which, in particular, is in special form.
∎

5
A characterization of
\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
in the
m\times m
case

In this final section we provide a partial extension of Proposition
4.1
to the general case of
m\times m
matrix functions
\Omega
as well as a recursive procedure to determine the kernel of
T_{\Xi}
, where
\Xi
is the middle factor of a Wiener-Hopf type factorization of
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) of the form (
1.4
) with conditions (
1.5
) in place. Then for any
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})^{T}\in H^{p}_{m}
the following are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
;

(ii)

f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in{\mathcal{P}}
such that for
j=1,\ldots,m
we have

\displaystyle\deg(p_{j1}f_{1}+\cdots+p_{jj}f_{j})<\deg(q_{j})+k.

(5.1)

(iii)

for
j=1,\ldots,m
,
f_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{l_{j}-1}
and
\vec{f}:=(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{m})^{T}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{m}}
is in
\operatorname{Ker}M
, where

l_{j}=\max(\deg(q_{1})-k_{1}-1,\dots,\deg(q_{j-1})-k_{j-1}-1,\deg(q_{j})-k_{j},-k)+k

and

M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&0\\
M_{m1}&\cdots&\cdots&M_{mm}\end{pmatrix}

with for
1\leq i\leq j\leq m
the matrix
M_{ji}
being the compression of the Toeplitz operator of
d_{ji}
by restricting to the first
l_{i}
columns and selecting rows

\deg(q_{j})+k+1\quad\mbox{to}\quad k_{j}+l_{j},

to be interpreted as vacuous in case
k_{j}+l_{j}\leq\deg(q_{j})+k
or
l_{i}=0
.

Proof.

The structure and arguments of the proof are similar to the proof for the
2\times 2
case in Proposition
4.1
. We first prove the equivalence of (i) and (ii). More precisely, we show that in
T_{\Xi}f
, the
j
-th entry is zero precisely when (
5.1
) holds. From Lemma
2.2
we know that
f\in\operatorname{Ker}T_{\Xi}
implies that
f\in{\mathcal{P}}^{m}
, hence we may restrict to the case that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
are polynomials. Since
f\in\operatorname{Dom}(T_{\Xi})
, by Lemma
2.1
, we know that
(\Xi f)(z)=z^{-k}h(z)+\eta(z)
for
h=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})^{T}\in H^{p}_{m}
and
\eta=(r_{1}/q_{1},\ldots,r_{m}/q_{m})^{T}\in{\mathrm{Rat}}_{0}^{m}({\mathbb{T}})
, and
h
and
\eta
are uniquely determined by
f
and
\Xi
. Now fix
j
and consider the
j
-th entry of the identity
(\Xi f)(z)=z^{-k}h(z)+\eta(z)
multiplied on both sides with
z^{k}q_{j}(z)
:

p_{j1}(z)f_{1}(z)+\cdots+p_{jj}(z)f_{j}(z)=q_{j}(z)h(z)+z^{k}r_{j}(z).

(5.2)

Since
{\mathbb{P}}(z^{-k}h(z))=0
if and only if
h\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k-1}
, it follows that the
j
-th entry of
T_{\Xi}f
is zero if and only if (
5.2
) holds with
h\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k-1}
. Since
z^{k}
and
q_{j}
are co-prime, varying
h\in{\mathcal{P}}_{k-1}
and
r_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\deg(q_{j})-1}
, in the right hand side of (
5.2
), any polynomial in
{\mathcal{P}}_{\deg(q_{j})+k-1}
can occur. Hence the
j
-th entry of
T_{\Xi}f
is zero if and only if

p_{j1}f_{1}+\cdots+p_{jj}f_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\deg(q_{j})+k-1},

that is, if and only if (
5.1
) holds. Since
j
is arbitrary, it follows that (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

Next we prove the equivalence of (ii) and (iii). The restrictions on the degrees of
f_{i},\ldots,f_{j}
were proved in Lemma
2.2
. In terms of Toeplitz operators, condition (
5.1
) can be interpreted as saying that

T_{z^{-(\deg(q_{j})+k)}}(T_{d_{j1}}f_{1}+\cdots+T_{d_{jj}}f_{j})=0.

Since
\deg(f_{i})<l_{i}
, in the above identity we can restrict the Toeplitz operator
T_{d_{j1}}
to its first
l_{i}
columns (corresponding to
{\mathcal{P}}_{l_{i}-1}\subset H^{p}
). In the resulting operator, all rows after
\deg(d_{ji})+l_{i}
are zero rows. Note further that for all
i>1
we have
l_{i}\geq l_{i-1}-1
, so that
l_{j}\geq l_{i}-1
for
1\leq i\leq j
, while also
k_{j}>\deg(d_{ij})
. As a consequence, we have
\deg(d_{ji})+l_{i}\leq k_{j}+l_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq j
. Thus, in the restriction of
T_{d_{j1}}
to its first
l_{i}
columns, all rows after
k_{j}+l_{j}
are zero, for
1\leq i\leq j
, hence we may safely remove all rows of
T_{d_{ji}}
after the
(k_{j}+l_{j})
-th row. Applying
T_{z^{-(\deg(q_{j})+k)}}
in the above formula corresponds to removing the first
\deg(q_{j})+k
rows, so that the resulting compression of
T_{ji}
corresponds to
M_{ji}
, We have proved that (
5.1
) is equivalent to
\vec{f}
being in
\operatorname{Ker}\begin{pmatrix}M_{1j}&\cdots&M_{jj}&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}
, so that the equivalence of (ii) and (iii) follows.
∎

In Theorem
5.1
we did not exploit the special form of the diagonal elements of
\Xi
. Moreover, the lower triangular form of
\Xi(z)
and resulting block lower triangular form of
M
in Theorem
5.1
suggests to determine
\operatorname{Ker}M
recursively. For this purpose, decompose the matrix
M_{ji}
for
i<j
as

M_{ji}=\begin{pmatrix}N_{ji}\\
L_{ji}\end{pmatrix},

where
N_{ji}
consist of the first
\max(k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k,0)
rows of
M_{ji}
and
L_{ji}
consists of the remaining rows. If
k_{j}<\deg(q_{j})+k
, then
N_{ji}
is vacuous and
L_{ji}=M_{ji}
. Now define

M_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\
\ \vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&0\\
M_{j1}&\cdots&\cdots&M_{jj}\end{pmatrix},\ R_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&0&\cdots&0\\
\ \vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&&\ddots&0\\
M_{(j-1)1}&\cdots&\cdots&M_{(j-1)(j-1)}\\
N_{j1}&\cdots&\cdots&N_{j(j-1)}\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\Omega\in{\mathrm{Rat}}^{m\times m}
with poles on
{\mathbb{T}}
and Wiener-Hopf type factorization (
1.2
) with middle factor
\Xi
in (
1.3
) of the form (
1.4
) with conditions (
1.5
) in place. Then
\operatorname{Ker}M_{1}=\operatorname{Ker}M_{11}={\mathbb{C}}^{\max(\deg(q_{1})+k-k_{1},0)}
and recursively we determine
\operatorname{Ker}M_{j}
as follows: For
f_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{l_{j}-1}
, for
i=1,\ldots,j
, we have that
\vec{f}=(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{j})
is in
\operatorname{Ker}M_{j}
if and only if

(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{j-1})\in\operatorname{Ker}R_{j}=\operatorname{Ker}M_{j-1}\cap\operatorname{Ker}\begin{pmatrix}N_{j1}&\cdots&N_{j(j-1)}\end{pmatrix}

and

f_{j}=r_{j}+z^{\max(\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j},0)}g_{j}

(5.3)

with
r_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\max(\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j},0)-1}
arbitrarily and
\vec{g}_{j}=-L_{j1}\vec{f}_{1}-\cdots-L_{j(j-1)}\vec{f}_{j-1}
.

Proof.

It is clear that for
\vec{f}
to be in
\operatorname{Ker}M_{j}
we need
(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{j-1})
to be in
\operatorname{Ker}M_{j-1}
. Hence we only have to determine the extra conditions imposed by
\vec{f}
being in the kernel of the last block row of
M_{j}
. Decomposing
M_{jj}
in the same way as
M_{ji}
for
i<j
we obtain that

M_{jj}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
I_{l_{j}}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{ if }\quad k_{j}>\deg(q_{j})+k,

or

M_{jj}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{l_{j}+k_{j}-\deg(q_{j})-k}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{ if }\quad k_{j}\leq\deg(q_{j})+k.

The statement for
j=1
is now obvious. For
j>1
we distinguish between two cases, corresponding to the two forms
M_{jj}
can have.

First assume that
k_{j}\leq\deg(q_{j})+k
. In that case
N_{ji}
is vacuous and
L_{ji}=M_{ji}
for
i<j
, and
M_{jj}
is surjective. Hence
R_{j}=M_{j-1}
so that
\operatorname{Ker}R_{j}=\operatorname{Ker}M_{j-1}
. Moreover,
\vec{f}
is in
\operatorname{Ker}M_{j}
if
(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{j-1})
is in

\operatorname{Ker}M_{j-1}=\operatorname{Ker}R_{j}
and

M_{jj}\vec{f}_{j}=-M_{j1}\vec{f}_{1}-\cdots-M_{j(j-1)}\vec{f}_{j-1}.

Given the form of
M_{jj}
in this case, the above identity corresponds to
f_{j}
being as in (
5.3
) since
M_{ji}=L_{ji}
.

Next we consider the case where
k_{j}>\deg(q_{j})+k
. In this case the last block row of
M_{j}
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}N_{j1}&\cdots&N_{j(j-1)}&0\\
L_{j1}&\cdots&L_{j(j-1)}&I_{l_{j}}\end{pmatrix}.

Hence for
\vec{f}
to be in
\operatorname{Ker}M_{j}
it is necessary and sufficient for
(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{j-1})
to be in the kernel of
\begin{pmatrix}N_{j1}&\cdots&N_{j(j-1)}\end{pmatrix}
and

0=L_{j1}\vec{f}_{1}+\cdots+L_{j(j-1)}\vec{f}_{(j-1)}+\vec{f_{j}}.

This corresponds to (
5.3
) since
\max(\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j},0)=0
and hence
f_{j}=g_{j}
, with
g_{j}
as in the theorem.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The dichotomy observed in Remark
4.2
also appears in the general case. Indeed when recursively determining the kernel of
M
as described in Proposition
5.2
, when investigating the restriction imposed by the
j
-th row, in case
k_{j}\leq\deg(q_{j})+k
no further constraints are added on
f_{1},\ldots,f_{j-1}
and for
f_{j}
there is a freedom added of dimension
\deg(q_{j})+k-k_{j}
, whereas in the case where
k_{j}>\deg(q_{j})+k
,
f_{j}
is completely determined and a further reduction of the dimension is established by the requirement that
(\vec{f}_{1},\ldots,\vec{f}_{j-1})^{T}
is in the kernel of
\begin{pmatrix}N_{j1}&\cdots&N_{j(j-1)}\end{pmatrix}
.
